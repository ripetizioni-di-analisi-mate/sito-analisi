## Exercise 1

A small loudspeaker radiates $P = 0.80\,\mathrm{W}$ of acoustic power uniformly in all directions, and the air absorbs no energy. Determine the intensity and the intensity level at $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$, and the distance at which the level has fallen to $60\,\mathrm{dB}$.

$$
I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2} ,
\qquad
\beta = 10\,\log_{10}\frac{I}{I_0} ,
\qquad
I_0 = 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}
$$
$$
I = \frac{0.80\,\mathrm{W}}{4\pi\,(5.0\,\mathrm{m})^2} = \frac{0.80\,\mathrm{W}}{314\,\mathrm{m^2}} = 2.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}
$$
$$
\beta = 10\,\log_{10}\frac{2.546 \cdot 10^{-3}}{10^{-12}} = 10\,\log_{10}(2.546 \cdot 10^{9}) = 94\,\mathrm{dB}
$$
$$
\beta' = 60\,\mathrm{dB}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I' = I_0\,10^{6} = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}
$$
$$
r' = \sqrt{\frac{P}{4\pi I'}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.80\,\mathrm{W}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}}} = \sqrt{6.37 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m^2}} = 2.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The source radiates uniformly and the medium absorbs no energy, therefore the whole power crosses every sphere centred on the source and the intensity is the power divided by the area $4\pi r^2$.

The level is obtained from the definition of the decibel, and the ratio $\dfrac{I}{I_0}$ is a pure number, as it must be inside a logarithm. A level of $94\,\mathrm{dB}$ at five metres is the order of magnitude of a loud concert.

The last question inverts the definition, giving $I' = I_0\,10^{\beta'/10}$, and then inverts the law of the intensity to obtain the distance. The intensity falls as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$, hence the drop from $94\,\mathrm{dB}$ to $60\,\mathrm{dB}$, a factor $10^{3.4} = 2.5 \cdot 10^3$ in intensity, corresponds to a factor $\sqrt{2.5 \cdot 10^3} = 50$ in distance, in agreement with $\dfrac{252\,\mathrm{m}}{5.0\,\mathrm{m}}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At a given point a machine produces the intensity level $82\,\mathrm{dB}$. Determine the level produced at the same point by two identical machines working together, and the number of such machines needed to reach $92\,\mathrm{dB}$.

$$
\beta_1 = 10\,\log_{10}\frac{I_1}{I_0} ,
\qquad
I_n = n\,I_1
$$
$$
\beta_n = 10\,\log_{10}\frac{n\,I_1}{I_0} = \beta_1 + 10\,\log_{10}n
$$
$$
\beta_2 = 82\,\mathrm{dB} + 10\,\log_{10}2 = 82\,\mathrm{dB} + 3.0\,\mathrm{dB} = 85\,\mathrm{dB}
$$
$$
92\,\mathrm{dB} - 82\,\mathrm{dB} = 10\,\log_{10}n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
n = 10^{1} = 10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The machines are independent sources, therefore the intensities add while the levels do not, and the level of $n$ equal sources exceeds the level of one by $10\log_{10}n$.

Doubling the intensity adds $10\log_{10}2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{dB}$, hence two machines give $85\,\mathrm{dB}$ and not $164\,\mathrm{dB}$. An increase of $10\,\mathrm{dB}$ corresponds to a factor $10$ in intensity, therefore **ten** machines are needed to reach $92\,\mathrm{dB}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A siren emits a sound of frequency $f = 500\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and the speed of sound in the air is $v = 343\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine the frequency received when the source moves towards a stationary observer at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$, when the observer moves towards a stationary source at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and when both approach each other at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
f' = f\;\frac{v + v_o}{v - v_s}
$$
$$
v_s = 30\,\mathrm{m/s},\; v_o = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f' = 500\,\mathrm{Hz} \cdot \frac{343}{313} = 548\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
$$
v_s = 0,\; v_o = 30\,\mathrm{m/s}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f' = 500\,\mathrm{Hz} \cdot \frac{373}{343} = 544\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
$$
v_s = v_o = 30\,\mathrm{m/s}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f' = 500\,\mathrm{Hz} \cdot \frac{373}{313} = 596\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both speeds are counted positive when source and observer approach, therefore the numerator grows and the denominator shrinks, and in all three cases the received frequency is higher than the emitted one.

The first two results differ, $548\,\mathrm{Hz}$ against $544\,\mathrm{Hz}$, although the relative speed is the same. The reason is that the medium fixes a preferred frame: a moving source compresses the wavelength, while a moving observer meets an unchanged wavelength at a different relative speed, and the two mechanisms are **not** equivalent.

In the third case both effects act together, hence the two factors multiply and the shift is almost the sum of the previous ones.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A guitar string is sounded together with a tuning fork of frequency $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and three beats per second are heard. The tension of the string is then increased slightly, and the beat frequency rises to five per second. Determine the frequency of the string before the adjustment, and the relative change of tension needed to tune it to $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

$$
f_{\mathrm{beat}} = |f_1 - f_2| = 3\,\mathrm{Hz}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f_1 = 437\,\mathrm{Hz}
\quad\text{or}\quad
f_1 = 443\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
$$
f_1 \propto \sqrt{F_T}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_T \uparrow \;\Rightarrow\; f_1 \uparrow
$$
$$
f_{\mathrm{beat}} \text{ rises}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f_1 = 443\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
$$
\frac{f_1'}{f_1} = \sqrt{\frac{F_T'}{F_T}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{F_T'}{F_T} = \left(\frac{440}{443}\right)^2 = 0.9865
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta F_T}{F_T} = 0.9865 - 1 = -1.3 \cdot 10^{-2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The beat frequency is the modulus of the difference of the two frequencies, therefore three beats per second leave two possibilities, $437\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $443\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and a single measurement does not decide between them.

The fundamental frequency of a string goes with the square root of the tension, hence increasing the tension raises $f_1$. The beats became more frequent, so the distance from $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$ grew, therefore the string was already **above** the fork and $f_1 = 443\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Had the string been at $437\,\mathrm{Hz}$, the same adjustment would have brought it closer to $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and the beats would have slowed down.

Tuning requires lowering the frequency to $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and the ratio of the tensions is the square of the ratio of the frequencies, hence the tension must be reduced by about $1.3\%$.

</div>
