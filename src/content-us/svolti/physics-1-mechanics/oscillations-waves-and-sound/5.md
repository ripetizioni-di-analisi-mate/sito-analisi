## Exercise 1

A body of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ oscillates on a spring of constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ in a medium that exerts the resistive force $-b\dot{x}$ with $b = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. Determine the damped angular frequency, the period of the oscillation and the time in which the amplitude falls to half of its initial value.

$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} ,
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{b}{2m} ,
\qquad
\omega' = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}
$$
$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{200\,\mathrm{N/m}}{0.50\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{0.80\,\mathrm{kg/s}}{2 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}} = 0.80\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}
$$
$$
\omega' = \sqrt{400 - 0.64}\;\mathrm{rad/s} = \sqrt{399.36}\;\mathrm{rad/s} = 19.98\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
T' = \frac{2\pi}{\omega'} = \frac{2\pi}{19.98\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 0.314\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
A\,e^{-\gamma t} = \frac{A}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = \frac{\ln 2}{\gamma} = \frac{0.693}{0.80\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 0.87\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{f="0.05*exp(-0.8*x)*cos(19.98*x);0.05*exp(-0.8*x);-0.05*exp(-0.8*x)" legenda="x(t);A e^{-\gamma t};-A e^{-\gamma t}" nomi="t,x" segmenti="0.866,-0.06,0.866,0.06" etichette="0.9,0.062,t = 0.87 s" xmin=-0.1 xmax=2.6 ymin=-0.08 ymax=0.092 titolo="Oscillation of the body, with $x$ in metres and $t$ in seconds: at $t = 0.87\,\mathrm{s}$ the envelope has fallen to half its initial value"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The damping constant enters through $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m}$, which has the dimensions of a reciprocal time. Here $\gamma = 0.80\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is far smaller than $\omega_0 = 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, therefore the oscillator is **underdamped** and the motion is an oscillation whose amplitude decays.

The damped angular frequency differs from $\omega_0$ by the term $\gamma^2 = 0.64\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$ subtracted from $\omega_0^2 = 400\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$, hence the shift is of the order of one part in a thousand and the period is practically the undamped one.

The envelope of the motion is $Ae^{-\gamma t}$, therefore the condition on the amplitude gives $e^{-\gamma t} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $t = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\gamma}$. The result does not depend on the initial amplitude, so the amplitude halves again in each further interval of $0.87\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $m = 0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ oscillates on a spring of constant $k = 120\,\mathrm{N/m}$, and its amplitude falls to $\dfrac{1}{e}$ of the initial value in $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Determine the damping constant $b$, the damped angular frequency and the number of oscillations completed in those $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
A\,e^{-\gamma\tau} = \frac{A}{e}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\gamma = \frac{1}{\tau} = \frac{1}{4.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 0.25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}
$$
$$
b = 2m\gamma = 2 \cdot 0.30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 0.15\,\mathrm{kg/s}
$$
$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{120\,\mathrm{N/m}}{0.30\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
\omega' = \sqrt{400 - 0.0625}\;\mathrm{rad/s} = 19.998\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
T' = \frac{2\pi}{19.998\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 0.3142\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
N = \frac{\tau}{T'} = \frac{4.0\,\mathrm{s}}{0.3142\,\mathrm{s}} = 12.7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The amplitude decays as $e^{-\gamma t}$, therefore the time in which it falls by the factor $\dfrac{1}{e}$ is $\tau = \dfrac{1}{\gamma}$, and reading $\gamma$ from the measured $\tau$ gives $b = 2m\gamma$ at once.

The correction to the angular frequency is $\dfrac{\gamma^2}{\omega_0^2} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-4}$, hence $\omega'$ and $\omega_0$ agree to four significant figures and the period may be computed from either.

Dividing the decay time by the period gives the number of oscillations performed while the amplitude falls by the factor $\dfrac{1}{e}$. A large number means **weak damping**, and here the oscillator completes about thirteen cycles before its amplitude drops to $37\%$ of the initial value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The damped oscillator with $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, $k = 800\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $b = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ is driven by the force $F = F_0\cos\omega_d t$ with $F_0 = 20\,\mathrm{N}$. Determine the natural angular frequency, the steady amplitude at resonance and the steady amplitude for $\omega_d = 15\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{800\,\mathrm{N/m}}{2.0\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
A(\omega_d) = \frac{F_0}{\sqrt{(k - m\omega_d^2)^2 + b^2\omega_d^2}}
$$
$$
\omega_d = \omega_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k - m\omega_d^2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A = \frac{F_0}{b\,\omega_0} = \frac{20\,\mathrm{N}}{8.0\,\mathrm{kg/s} \cdot 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 0.125\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
k - m\omega_d^2 = 800\,\mathrm{N/m} - 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 225\,\mathrm{s^{-2}} = 350\,\mathrm{N/m} ,
\qquad
b\,\omega_d = 120\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
A = \frac{20\,\mathrm{N}}{\sqrt{350^2 + 120^2}\;\mathrm{N/m}} = \frac{20\,\mathrm{N}}{370\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 0.054\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After the transient has decayed the body oscillates at the driving frequency $\omega_d$ and not at $\omega_0$, therefore the amplitude is given by the formula for the driven oscillator and depends on $\omega_d$ alone, the initial conditions having been forgotten.

At $\omega_d = \omega_0$ the term $k - m\omega_d^2$ vanishes and only the damping term survives, hence the amplitude reduces to $\dfrac{F_0}{b\omega_0}$. This is the condition of **resonance**, and the amplitude is limited only by the damping: halving $b$ would double it.

For $\omega_d = 15\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ the term $k - m\omega_d^2 = 350\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is much larger than $b\omega_d = 120\,\mathrm{N/m}$, therefore it dominates the denominator and the amplitude falls to $0.054\,\mathrm{m}$, about $43\%$ of the resonant value, for a driving frequency only a quarter below $\omega_0$.

</div>
