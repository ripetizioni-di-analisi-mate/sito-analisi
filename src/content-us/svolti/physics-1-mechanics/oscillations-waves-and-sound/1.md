## Exercise 1

A particle moves along the $x$ axis with
$$
x(t) = 0.12\cos\!\left(8.0\,t + \frac{\pi}{3}\right)\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
with $t$ in seconds. Determine the amplitude, the angular frequency, the period and the frequency, the position and the velocity at $t = 0$, and the maximum values of the speed and of the acceleration.

$$
x(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \varphi)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A = 0.12\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\omega = 8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
\varphi = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{1}{T}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 0.79\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{1}{0.785\,\mathrm{s}} = 1.3\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
$$
x(0) = A\cos\varphi = 0.12\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.500 = 0.060\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v(0) = -\omega A\sin\varphi = -8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.12\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.866 = -0.83\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \omega A = 0.96\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
a_{\max} = \omega^2 A = (8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 \cdot 0.12\,\mathrm{m} = 7.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given function has the form $A\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, therefore the three constants are read off directly: the factor in front of the cosine is the **amplitude**, the coefficient of $t$ inside the cosine is the angular frequency and the additive constant is the phase constant.

The period is the time in which the phase advances by $2\pi$, hence $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$, and the frequency is its reciprocal. The position at $t = 0$ is obtained by substituting $t = 0$, and the velocity is the derivative $v = -\omega A\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$ evaluated at the same instant. The velocity comes out negative, therefore at $t = 0$ the particle is at $0.060\,\mathrm{m}$ and is moving towards the equilibrium position.

The sine and the cosine have modulus at most $1$, hence the largest speed is $\omega A$, reached at $x = 0$, and the largest modulus of the acceleration is $\omega^2 A$, reached at $x = \pm A$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block oscillates with amplitude $A = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and period $T = 0.40\,\mathrm{s}$, and at $t = 0$ it is released from rest at $x = +A$. Write the position as a function of time, compute the velocity and the acceleration at $t = 0.10\,\mathrm{s}$, and find the first instant at which the block passes through $x = 2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{0.40\,\mathrm{s}} = 15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v(0) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x(t) = 0.050\cos(15.7\,t)\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\omega t = 15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{s} = 1.571\,\mathrm{rad} = \frac{\pi}{2}
$$
$$
v = -\omega A\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = -15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{m} = -0.79\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
a = -\omega^2 A\cos\frac{\pi}{2} = 0
$$
$$
\cos\omega t = \frac{x}{A} = \frac{2.5\,\mathrm{cm}}{5.0\,\mathrm{cm}} = 0.500
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega t = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$
$$
t = \frac{\pi}{3\,\omega} = \frac{1.047\,\mathrm{rad}}{15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 0.067\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{f="5*cos(15.708*x)" legenda=0 nomi="t,x" segmenti="0.1,-6.2,0.1,6.2;0.4,-0.35,0.4,0.35;0.8,-0.35,0.8,0.35" etichette="0.112,6.6,t = 0.10 s;0.372,-1.0,0.4 s;0.772,-1.0,0.8 s" xmin=-0.06 xmax=0.95 ymin=-6.6 ymax=7.8 titolo="Position of the block, with $x$ in centimetres and $t$ in seconds: at $t = 0.10\,\mathrm{s}$, a quarter of a period after the release, the block crosses the equilibrium position"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The block starts at rest at the largest displacement, hence $v_0 = 0$ gives $\tan\varphi = 0$ and the motion is described by a pure cosine with $\varphi = 0$. The angular frequency follows from the period alone, since $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$.

The instant $t = 0.10\,\mathrm{s}$ is a quarter of the period, therefore the phase is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the block is passing through the equilibrium position. There the cosine vanishes and the sine has modulus $1$, hence the acceleration is zero and the speed has its largest value $\omega A = 0.79\,\mathrm{m/s}$; the minus sign states that the block is moving towards negative $x$.

For the last question the position is imposed and the phase is extracted from the cosine. The equation $\cos\omega t = 0.500$ has infinitely many solutions, and the **first** passage corresponds to the smallest positive one, $\omega t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, which gives $t = 0.067\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A particle in simple harmonic motion has amplitude $A = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and passes through the equilibrium position with speed $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine the angular frequency, the period, and the speed and the acceleration at $x = 0.040\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
v_{\max} = \omega A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega = \frac{v_{\max}}{A} = \frac{1.2\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.080\,\mathrm{m}} = 15\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{15\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 0.42\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
|v| = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = 15\,\mathrm{rad/s}\,\sqrt{(0.080\,\mathrm{m})^2 - (0.040\,\mathrm{m})^2} = 15 \cdot 0.0693\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a = -\omega^2 x = -(15\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{m} = -9.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The speed is largest where the displacement vanishes, therefore the given speed is $v_{\max} = \omega A$, and dividing it by the amplitude gives the angular frequency without any information about the mass.

The question asks for a speed at a given position and not at a given instant, hence the formula $|v| = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ applies and no phase constant is needed. At half the amplitude the speed is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,v_{\max} \approx 0.87\,v_{\max}$, which confirms the value obtained.

The acceleration at any position is $a = -\omega^2 x$, and its sign is opposite to that of $x$, therefore at $x = +0.040\,\mathrm{m}$ the acceleration points towards the equilibrium position.

</div>
