## Exercise 1

A block of mass $m = 0.250\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a frictionless horizontal surface and is attached to a spring of constant $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$. The block is displaced $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the equilibrium position and released from rest. Determine the angular frequency, the period, the frequency, the maximum speed and the maximum acceleration.

$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{1}{T} ,
\qquad
v_{\max} = \omega A ,
\qquad
a_{\max} = \omega^2 A
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{100\,\mathrm{N/m}}{0.250\,\mathrm{kg}}} = \sqrt{400\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}} = 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 0.314\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{1}{0.314\,\mathrm{s}} = 3.18\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
$$
v_{\max} = 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{m} = 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
a_{\max} = 400\,\mathrm{s^{-2}} \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{m} = 16\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The surface is frictionless and the weight is balanced by the normal force, therefore the resultant force on the block is the spring force alone and the motion is simple harmonic with $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$. The unit works out, since $\dfrac{\mathrm{N/m}}{\mathrm{kg}} = \dfrac{\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}}{\mathrm{m\cdot kg}} = \mathrm{s^{-2}}$.

The block is released from rest at the largest displacement, hence the amplitude is the initial displacement, $A = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The angular frequency does not depend on the amplitude, therefore period and frequency would be the same for any release point, while the maximum speed and the maximum acceleration are **proportional to the amplitude**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $m = 0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs at rest from a vertical spring and stretches it by $4.9\,\mathrm{cm}$. Determine the spring constant and the period of the vertical oscillations about the equilibrium position.

$$
k\,x_{\mathrm{eq}} = m g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k = \frac{m g}{x_{\mathrm{eq}}}
$$
$$
k = \frac{0.60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{0.049\,\mathrm{m}} = \frac{5.89\,\mathrm{N}}{0.049\,\mathrm{m}} = 1.20 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{x_{\mathrm{eq}}}{g}}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.049\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 2\pi \cdot 0.0707\,\mathrm{s} = 0.444\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1.1,2.6 1.1,2.6;0,2.6 0,2.35 0.35,2.2 -0.35,1.9 0.35,1.6 -0.35,1.3 0.35,1.0 -0.35,0.7 0,0.55 0,0.35" poligoni="-0.4,-0.35 0.4,-0.35 0.4,0.35 -0.4,0.35" segmenti="-1.9,0,2.6,0" punti="1.9,0,pieno" vettori="1.9,0,1.9,1.4,k x;1.9,0,1.9,-1.4,m g" etichette="-1.85,0.22,x = 0;1.25,1.6,free-body diagram" xmin=-2.3 xmax=3.6 ymin=-2.0 ymax=3.0 titolo="At the equilibrium position the elastic force $k\,x_{\mathrm{eq}}$ balances the weight, and the oscillations take place about that position"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At equilibrium the body is at rest, therefore the resultant force vanishes and the elastic force balances the weight, which gives $k = \dfrac{mg}{x_{\mathrm{eq}}}$. This is the standard way of measuring a spring constant, since it requires only a known mass and a ruler.

Measuring the displacement from the equilibrium position, the constant terms cancel and the resultant force is again $-ku$, hence the motion is simple harmonic with the same $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ as a horizontal oscillator. Substituting $k = \dfrac{mg}{x_{\mathrm{eq}}}$ in the period gives $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{x_{\mathrm{eq}}}{g}}$, therefore the period is determined by the static elongation alone and the mass **cancels**. A static stretch of $4.9\,\mathrm{cm}$ corresponds to $T = 0.444\,\mathrm{s}$ whatever body produces it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block slides on a frictionless horizontal surface between two springs of constants $k_1 = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $k_2 = 300\,\mathrm{N/m}$, arranged so that both act on it with the same displacement. The measured period is $T = 0.50\,\mathrm{s}$. Determine the mass of the block, and the period the same block would have if the two springs were joined one after the other.

$$
F_x = -k_1x - k_2x = -(k_1 + k_2)\,x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k_{\mathrm{p}} = k_1 + k_2 = 500\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k_{\mathrm{p}}}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m = \frac{k_{\mathrm{p}}\,T^2}{4\pi^2}
$$
$$
m = \frac{500\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.50\,\mathrm{s})^2}{4\pi^2} = \frac{125\,\mathrm{N\cdot s^2/m}}{39.48} = 3.17\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{s}}} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k_{\mathrm{s}} = \frac{k_1k_2}{k_1 + k_2} = \frac{200 \cdot 300}{500}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 120\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
T_{\mathrm{s}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{3.166\,\mathrm{kg}}{120\,\mathrm{N/m}}} = 2\pi \cdot 0.1624\,\mathrm{s} = 1.02\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When both springs undergo the same displacement, their forces add and the block feels a single restoring force with constant $k_1 + k_2$. The motion is therefore simple harmonic, and inverting $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$ gives the mass from the measured period, which is how a mass is determined where a balance would not work.

When the springs are joined one after the other, the same force passes through both and the elongations add, hence $\dfrac{1}{k_{\mathrm{s}}} = \dfrac{1}{k_1} + \dfrac{1}{k_2}$ and the pair is **softer** than either spring alone. The period goes as $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}$, therefore the ratio of the two periods is $\sqrt{\dfrac{500}{120}} = 2.04$, in agreement with $\dfrac{1.02\,\mathrm{s}}{0.50\,\mathrm{s}}$.

</div>
