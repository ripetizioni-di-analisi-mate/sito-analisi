## Exercise 1

A block of mass $m = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ oscillates on a spring of constant $k = 250\,\mathrm{N/m}$ with amplitude $A = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ on a frictionless horizontal surface. Determine the mechanical energy, the maximum speed and the speed at $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 ,
\qquad
E = \frac{1}{2}mv_{\max}^2 ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = E - \frac{1}{2}kx^2
$$
$$
E = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 250\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.060\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.45\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 0.45\,\mathrm{J}}{0.40\,\mathrm{kg}}} = \sqrt{2.25\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
U(0.030\,\mathrm{m}) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 250\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.030\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.1125\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(E - U)}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 0.3375\,\mathrm{J}}{0.40\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 1.30\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{f="125*x^2" legenda=0 nomi="x,U" asintoti="y=0.45" punti="-0.06,0.45,pieno,−A;0.06,0.45,pieno,A" segmenti="0.03,0,0.03,0.45" etichette="-0.082,0.5,E;0.034,0.05,U;0.034,0.3,K" xmin=-0.085 xmax=0.085 ymin=-0.07 ymax=0.6 titolo="Energy diagram for $k = 250\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $A = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, with $U$ and $E$ in joules and $x$ in meters: at $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ the potential energy is $0.11\,\mathrm{J}$ and the kinetic energy is $0.34\,\mathrm{J}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The surface is frictionless and the spring force is conservative, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved and can be computed at the most convenient position. At $x = \pm A$ the block is momentarily at rest, hence the whole energy is elastic potential energy and $E = \dfrac{1}{2}kA^2 = 0.45\,\mathrm{J}$.

At $x = 0$ the potential energy vanishes, hence the whole energy is kinetic and the speed reaches $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At an intermediate position the two terms share the energy, and subtracting the potential energy from the total gives the kinetic energy and then the speed.

The same value follows from $|v| = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ with $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{250}{0.40}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 25\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, which gives $25 \cdot 0.0520\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and confirms the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $m = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ oscillates on a spring of constant $k = 180\,\mathrm{N/m}$ with amplitude $A = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. Determine the positions at which the kinetic energy equals the potential energy, the speed at those positions, and the fraction of the energy that is kinetic at $x = \dfrac{A}{2}$.

$$
E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 180\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.10\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.90\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K = U = \frac{1}{2}E
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{4}kA^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \pm\frac{A}{\sqrt{2}}
$$
$$
x = \pm\frac{0.10\,\mathrm{m}}{1.414} = \pm 0.071\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2K}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 0.45\,\mathrm{J}}{0.80\,\mathrm{kg}}} = \sqrt{1.125\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 1.06\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{U\!\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)}{E} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}k\frac{A^2}{4}}{\frac{1}{2}kA^2} = \frac{1}{4}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{K}{E} = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The condition $K = U$ together with $K + U = E$ gives $U = \dfrac{1}{2}E$, and writing both energies in terms of the positions turns it into $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}kA^2$. The spring constant cancels, therefore the answer $x = \pm\dfrac{A}{\sqrt{2}}$ holds for **every** harmonic oscillator and depends on the amplitude alone.

The speed at those positions follows from $K = \dfrac{1}{2}E = 0.45\,\mathrm{J}$. As a check, $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{180}{0.80}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 15\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ gives $v_{\max} = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the speed at $K = \dfrac{1}{2}E$ is $\dfrac{v_{\max}}{\sqrt{2}} = 1.06\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The potential energy goes with the square of the position, hence at half the amplitude it is a quarter of the total and three quarters of the energy is **kinetic**. The ratio does not involve $m$, $k$ or $A$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a frictionless horizontal surface with speed $v_0 = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and strikes a spring of constant $k = 600\,\mathrm{N/m}$ fixed to a wall. Determine the maximum compression of the spring and the speed of the block when the compression is half of that value.

$$
\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}kA^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A = v_0\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
A = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}\,\sqrt{\frac{1.5\,\mathrm{kg}}{600\,\mathrm{N/m}}} = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.0500\,\mathrm{s} = 0.120\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{600\,\mathrm{N/m}}{1.5\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
|v| = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = 20.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}\,\sqrt{(0.120\,\mathrm{m})^2 - (0.060\,\mathrm{m})^2} = 20.0 \cdot 0.1039\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.08\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From the instant of contact the block is attached to the spring as far as the energy balance is concerned, and the surface is frictionless, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved. The block arrives with kinetic energy $\dfrac{1}{2}mv_0^2 = 4.32\,\mathrm{J}$ and stops when all of it has become elastic potential energy, hence the **maximum compression** plays the part of the amplitude and equals $0.120\,\mathrm{m}$.

For the second question the position is half the amplitude, therefore the formula $|v| = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ applies and gives $2.08\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The same value follows from the ratio $\dfrac{v}{v_0} = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 0.866$, since $0.866 \cdot 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.08\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>
