## Exercise 1

A uniform crate of mass $m = 40\,\mathrm{kg}$, width $w = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ stands on a horizontal floor with coefficient of static friction $\mu_s = 0.50$. A horizontal force $F$ is applied at the upper edge. Determine whether the crate slides or tips as $F$ is increased, and the value of $F$ at which equilibrium ends.

$$
F_{\text{slide}} = \mu_s\,m g
$$
$$
F_{\text{tip}}\,h = m g\,\frac{w}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_{\text{tip}} = \frac{m g\,w}{2h}
$$
$$
F_{\text{slide}} = 0.50 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 196\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_{\text{tip}} = \frac{40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{m}}{2 \cdot 1.20\,\mathrm{m}} = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_{\text{tip}} < F_{\text{slide}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the crate tips at } F = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two different failures of equilibrium are possible, and each has its own condition. Sliding begins when the horizontal equation requires a friction force larger than $\mu_s N = \mu_s m g$, hence at $F_{\text{slide}} = 196\,\mathrm{N}$.

Tipping begins when the torque equation requires the normal force to act outside the base of support. Torques are taken about the far bottom edge, which is the edge the crate would turn about. The applied force acts at the height $h$, hence its moment arm about that edge is $h$; the weight acts along the vertical through the center, hence its moment arm is $\dfrac{w}{2}$. Setting the two torques equal gives $F_{\text{tip}} = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}$.

The smaller of the two values is the one reached first as $F$ grows, therefore the crate **tips** before it slides, at $F = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}$. The comparison is written once for all in the form $\dfrac{w}{2h} < \mu_s$, a condition satisfied when the crate is tall and narrow or the floor is rough. Here $\dfrac{w}{2h} = 0.25$ against $\mu_s = 0.50$.

The force applied lower down changes the conclusion. Applying $F$ at the height $y$ gives $F_{\text{tip}} = \dfrac{m g w}{2y}$, which exceeds $F_{\text{slide}}$ when $y < \dfrac{w}{2\mu_s} = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$, hence a push below half the height makes the crate slide instead.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform block of width $w = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $h = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on a plank whose surface has coefficient of static friction $\mu_s = 0.55$. The plank is slowly tilted. Determine the angle at which the block ceases to be in equilibrium and whether it slides or tips.

$$
N = m g\cos\theta,
\qquad
f = m g\sin\theta
$$
$$
f \le \mu_s N
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tan\theta \le \mu_s
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta_{\text{slide}} = \arctan 0.55 = 28.8^\circ
$$
$$
N\,e = m g\sin\theta\,\frac{h}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e = \frac{h}{2}\tan\theta,
\qquad
e \le \frac{w}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tan\theta \le \frac{w}{h}
$$
$$
\theta_{\text{tip}} = \arctan\frac{0.30}{0.90} = 18.4^\circ
$$
$$
\theta_{\text{tip}} < \theta_{\text{slide}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the block tips at } \theta = 18.4^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axes are taken along the incline and perpendicular to it. The equation perpendicular to the incline gives $N = m g\cos\theta$ and the equation along the incline gives $f = m g\sin\theta$, since the block is in equilibrium. Imposing $f \le \mu_s N$ gives the usual sliding condition $\tan\theta \le \mu_s$.

For the tipping condition the torques are taken about the center of the base. The weight component along the incline, $m g\sin\theta$, is applied at the center of gravity, at the height $\dfrac{h}{2}$ above the base, hence it has the moment arm $\dfrac{h}{2}$. The resultant normal force balances this torque, and it can do so only by acting at a distance $e$ from the center of the base, downhill, with $N e = m g \sin\theta \dfrac{h}{2}$.

The point of application of the normal force cannot leave the base, hence $e \le \dfrac{w}{2}$, and substituting $N = m g\cos\theta$ gives $\tan\theta \le \dfrac{w}{h}$. The mass cancels in both conditions, therefore the behavior depends on the shape and on the surface alone.

The tipping angle $18.4^\circ$ is reached before the sliding angle $28.8^\circ$, hence the block **tips** over its lower edge while friction is still able to hold it. A block with $\dfrac{w}{h} > \mu_s$ would slide first.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform cylinder of mass $m = 20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on a horizontal floor against a step of height $s = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. A horizontal force is applied at the highest point of the cylinder, in the direction of the step. Determine the smallest value of that force for which the cylinder begins to rise over the step.

$$
b = \sqrt{R^2 - (R - s)^2} = \sqrt{(0.25\,\mathrm{m})^2 - (0.15\,\mathrm{m})^2} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
F\,(2R - s) = m g\,b
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F = \frac{m g\,\sqrt{R^2 - (R - s)^2}}{2R - s}
$$
$$
F = \frac{20\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{m}}{0.50\,\mathrm{m} - 0.10\,\mathrm{m}} = \frac{39.24\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{0.40\,\mathrm{m}} = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-0.75,0 0.2,0;0.2,0 0.2,0.1;0.2,0.1 0.95,0.1" curve="0.25*cos(t),0.25+0.25*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0.25,pieno,O;0.2,0.1,pieno,C" vettori="0,0.5,0.47,0.5,F;0,0.25,0,0.04,mg" segmenti="0,0.25,0.2,0.25;0.2,0.1,0.2,0.5" etichette="0.1,0.3,b;0.27,0.31,2R − s" xmin=-0.5 xmax=1.05 ymin=-0.22 ymax=0.78 titolo="The cylinder turns about the corner $C$: the weight has the moment arm $b = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and the applied force the moment arm $2R - s = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

As soon as the cylinder begins to rise, it loses contact with the floor and keeps contact with the corner of the step alone, therefore it turns about that corner. The limiting condition is written by taking torques about the corner $C$, where the unknown reaction of the step is applied and has zero torque.

The moment arm of the weight is the horizontal distance $b$ between the corner and the vertical line through the center. The corner lies at the height $s$ and at the distance $R$ from the center, hence by the Pythagorean theorem $b = \sqrt{R^2 - (R-s)^2} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$.

The moment arm of the horizontal force is the vertical distance between the corner and the line of action of the force, which is applied at the height $2R$. That distance is $2R - s = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. Equating the two torques gives $F = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}$, half the weight of the cylinder.

A lower step gives a smaller $b$ and a larger moment arm for the force, hence a smaller $F$, and for $s \to 0$ the required force tends to zero, and for $s = R$ the moment arm of the weight becomes $R$ and $F = m g$.

</div>
