## Exercise 1

A homogeneous plate of constant thickness has the shape of an L formed by a horizontal rectangle $60\,\mathrm{cm} \times 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a vertical rectangle $20\,\mathrm{cm} \times 40\,\mathrm{cm}$ standing on its left end. Taking the origin at the lower left corner, with $x$ horizontal and $y$ vertical, determine the coordinates of the center of gravity.

$$
A_1 = 60 \cdot 20\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 1200\,\mathrm{cm^2},
\qquad
A_2 = 20 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 800\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$
$$
G_1 = (30,\ 10)\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
G_2 = (10,\ 40)\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{A_1 x_1 + A_2 x_2}{A_1 + A_2},
\qquad
y_G = \frac{A_1 y_1 + A_2 y_2}{A_1 + A_2}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{1200 \cdot 30 + 800 \cdot 10}{2000}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{44000}{2000}\,\mathrm{cm} = 22\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
y_G = \frac{1200 \cdot 10 + 800 \cdot 40}{2000}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{44000}{2000}\,\mathrm{cm} = 22\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body is divided into two rectangles, the horizontal one occupying $0 \le x \le 60$, $0 \le y \le 20$ and the vertical one occupying $0 \le x \le 20$, $20 \le y \le 60$, so that the two parts have no point in common and together they form the plate. Each rectangle has two axes of symmetry, therefore its center of gravity is its geometric center.

The plate is homogeneous and of constant thickness, hence the mass of each part is proportional to its area and the areas may be used in place of the masses in the formula for the **center of gravity of a composite body**. The common factor of density and thickness appears in the numerator and in the denominator, and it cancels.

The result is $G = (22,\ 22)\,\mathrm{cm}$. The two coordinates coincide because the figure is symmetric with respect to the line $y = x$, a symmetry that the data make exact: exchanging $x$ and $y$ turns the horizontal rectangle into the vertical one. The point lies outside the material of the plate, in the notch of the L, which is admissible since the center of gravity is a point of the plane.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous disk of radius $R = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ has a circular hole of radius $r = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$, whose center lies at the distance $d = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center of the disk. Determine the position of the center of gravity of the body.

$$
\text{full disk: } A = \pi R^2 \text{ at } x = 0,
\qquad
\text{hole: } A' = -\pi r^2 \text{ at } x = d
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{\pi R^2 \cdot 0 - \pi r^2 d}{\pi R^2 - \pi r^2} = -\,\frac{r^2 d}{R^2 - r^2}
$$
$$
x_G = -\,\frac{(10\,\mathrm{cm})^2 \cdot 15\,\mathrm{cm}}{(30\,\mathrm{cm})^2 - (10\,\mathrm{cm})^2} = -\,\frac{1500}{800}\,\mathrm{cm} = -1.88\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="30*cos(t),30*sin(t),0,6.29;15+10*cos(t),10*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,vuoto,O;15,0,vuoto,C;-1.875,0,pieno" segmenti="-1.875,0,-9,-8" etichette="-11,-11,G" xmin=-40 xmax=40 ymin=-38 ymax=36 titolo="The center of gravity $G$ of the drilled disk lies on the line $OC$, at $1.88\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center $O$ on the side opposite to the hole; dimensions in $\mathrm{cm}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The body with the hole is written as the superposition of the full disk and a disk of **negative** area occupying the hole, since adding those two mass distributions reproduces the real one. Both parts have a center of gravity at their own geometric center, the full disk at $O$ and the hole at the point $C$ at $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from $O$.

The $x$ axis is taken along the line $OC$, with the origin at $O$ and the positive sense toward the hole. The formula for the composite body applies with $A' = -\pi r^2$, and the factor $\pi$ cancels between numerator and denominator. The $y$ coordinate is zero, because the line $OC$ is an axis of symmetry of the body.

The result is negative, hence $G$ lies on the side opposite to the hole, which agrees with the fact that removing material from one side shifts the center of gravity away from that side. The displacement is small, $1.88\,\mathrm{cm}$, because the hole removes only $\dfrac{r^2}{R^2} = \dfrac{1}{9}$ of the area.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform rod of length $\ell = 80\,\mathrm{cm}$ and mass $m_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ carries a uniform sphere of mass $m_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $\rho = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, welded to one end so that the sphere touches that end. Determine the distance of the center of gravity from the free end of the rod, and the force needed to hold the body horizontal by a single support placed under that point.

$$
x_1 = \frac{\ell}{2} = 40\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
x_2 = \ell + \rho = 86\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 40\,\mathrm{cm} + 5.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 86\,\mathrm{cm}}{7.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{510}{7.0}\,\mathrm{cm} = 72.9\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
N = (m_1 + m_2)\,g = 7.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 68.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,-2 80,-2 80,2 0,2" curve="86+6*cos(t),6*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="40,0,vuoto,G₁;86,0,vuoto,G₂;72.86,0,pieno,G" segmenti="72.86,-14,72.86,14" xmin=-10 xmax=104 ymin=-28 ymax=26 titolo="The center of gravity $G$ lies at $72.9\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the free end, between $G_1$ and $G_2$ and much nearer to the sphere, which carries most of the mass; dimensions in $\mathrm{cm}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is uniform, hence its center of gravity is at its midpoint, at $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the free end. The sphere is uniform, hence its center of gravity is at its center, which lies at $\ell + \rho = 86\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the same end, since the sphere touches the end of the rod. Both points are on the axis of the rod, therefore the problem is one-dimensional.

The formula for the composite body gives $x_G = 72.9\,\mathrm{cm}$, a point inside the sphere. The sphere carries $\dfrac{5}{7}$ of the total mass, hence the center of gravity is much nearer to $G_2$ than to $G_1$, in the ratio $\dfrac{x_G - x_1}{x_2 - x_G} = \dfrac{32.9}{13.1} = 2.5 = \dfrac{m_2}{m_1}$.

A single support placed under $G$ holds the body horizontal, because the weight acts along the vertical through $G$ and its torque about the support vanishes. The vertical equation then gives $N = 68.7\,\mathrm{N}$, equal to the whole weight, since the support is the only other force acting on the body.

</div>
