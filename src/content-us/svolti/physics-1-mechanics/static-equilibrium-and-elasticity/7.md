## Exercise 1

A rubber pad has square faces of side $\ell = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and thickness $h = 0.050\,\mathrm{m}$. Its lower face is glued to a fixed plate, and a force $F_\parallel = 400\,\mathrm{N}$ parallel to the faces is applied to the upper face. The shear modulus of the rubber is $S = 2.5 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Determine the shear stress, the shear strain and the horizontal displacement of the upper face.

$$
A = \ell^2 = (0.20\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
\sigma_s = \frac{F_\parallel}{A},
\qquad
\varepsilon_s = \frac{\sigma_s}{S},
\qquad
x = \varepsilon_s\,h
$$
$$
\sigma_s = \frac{400\,\mathrm{N}}{0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}} = 1.0 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
\varepsilon_s = \frac{1.0 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}}{2.5 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{Pa}} = 4.0 \times 10^{-3},
\qquad
x = 4.0 \times 10^{-3} \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.20\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force is parallel to the faces, therefore it produces a **shear stress**, equal to the force divided by the area of the face on which it acts. The area is that of the square face, $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and not a cross section perpendicular to the force.

The **shear strain** is the ratio of the displacement of the upper face to the thickness of the pad, and Hooke's law in the form $\sigma_s = S\,\varepsilon_s$ gives it from the stress. The displacement then follows as $x = \varepsilon_s h = 0.20\,\mathrm{mm}$.

The shear strain equals $\tan\varphi$, where $\varphi$ is the angle by which the lateral faces tilt; here $\varphi = 0.23^\circ$, so the deformation keeps the body almost in its original shape. Rubber has a shear modulus some four orders of magnitude smaller than steel, which is why a rubber mounting of this size deforms visibly under a force that leaves a steel block unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A volume $V_0 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of water at the surface, of density $\rho_0 = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, is brought to a depth where the pressure exceeds the surface value by $\Delta p = 2.0 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The bulk modulus of water is $B = 2.2 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Determine the change in volume and the density at that depth.

$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V_0} = -\frac{\Delta p}{B} = -\frac{2.0 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{Pa}}{2.2 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}} = -9.09 \times 10^{-3}
$$
$$
\Delta V = -9.09 \times 10^{-3} \cdot 1.00\,\mathrm{m^3} = -9.09 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} = -9.09\,\mathrm{L}
$$
$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V_0 + \Delta V} = \frac{\rho_0 V_0}{V_0\left(1 + \dfrac{\Delta V}{V_0}\right)} = \frac{\rho_0}{1 - \dfrac{\Delta p}{B}}
$$
$$
\rho = \frac{1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}}{1 - 9.091 \times 10^{-3}} = 1.009 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressure acts on the whole surface of the sample and it is the same at every point of it, hence the deformation is a change of volume at constant shape, governed by the **bulk modulus**. The definition $\Delta p = -B\dfrac{\Delta V}{V_0}$ gives the relative change in volume directly, and the minus sign states that the volume decreases.

The mass of the sample does not change while it is carried down, therefore the density is the unchanged mass divided by the new volume. Writing $m = \rho_0 V_0$ and $V = V_0\left(1 + \dfrac{\Delta V}{V_0}\right)$ gives the formula above, in which the volume $V_0$ cancels.

The density increases by $0.9\%$ under a pressure of two hundred atmospheres, which corresponds to a depth of about $2000\,\mathrm{m}$. The change is small, hence treating sea water as incompressible in hydrostatics is a good approximation even at great depth.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An aluminum cube of side $a = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ is placed in a chamber where the pressure is raised by $\Delta p = 5.0 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The bulk modulus of aluminum is $B = 7.0 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Determine the change in volume of the cube and the change in the length of its side.

$$
V_0 = a^3 = (0.20\,\mathrm{m})^3 = 8.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V_0} = -\frac{\Delta p}{B} = -\frac{5.0 \times 10^{7}}{7.0 \times 10^{10}} = -7.14 \times 10^{-4}
$$
$$
\Delta V = -7.143 \times 10^{-4} \cdot 8.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} = -5.71 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3} = -5.71\,\mathrm{cm^3}
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta a}{a} = \frac{1}{3}\,\frac{\Delta V}{V_0} = -2.38 \times 10^{-4}
$$
$$
\Delta a = -2.381 \times 10^{-4} \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{m} = -4.76 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m} = -0.0476\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressure is uniform over the whole surface, hence the cube contracts keeping the shape of a cube, and the relative change in volume follows from the definition of the bulk modulus. The initial volume is $8.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, therefore the loss of volume is $5.71\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.

The relation between the change of a side and the change of the volume comes from $V = a^3$: differentiating gives $dV = 3a^2\,da$, hence $\dfrac{dV}{V} = 3\dfrac{da}{a}$, and for changes as small as these the differentials may be replaced by the finite changes. The side therefore shortens by one third of the relative change in volume, $2.38 \times 10^{-4}$ in relative terms and $0.0476\,\mathrm{mm}$ in absolute terms.

As a check, the side of the compressed cube is $0.199952\,\mathrm{m}$ and its cube is $7.99429 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, which differs from $V_0$ by $-5.71 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, the value already found. The pressure applied is about five hundred atmospheres and it changes the side of the cube by less than a twentieth of a millimeter, which shows how small the compressibility of a metal is.

</div>
