## Exercise 1

A uniform plank of mass $M = 20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on two smooth supports, the first at its left end $A$ and the second at the distance $b = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. A crate of mass $m = 50\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on the plank at the distance $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Determine the force exerted by each support.

$$
\begin{cases}
N_1 + N_2 - (M + m)g = 0 \\[4pt]
N_2\,b - M g\,\dfrac{L}{2} - m g\,a = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
N_2 = \frac{M\,\dfrac{L}{2} + m\,a}{b}\,g ,
\qquad
N_1 = (M + m)g - N_2
$$
$$
N_2 = \frac{20\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m} + 50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{m}}{3.0\,\mathrm{m}} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 294\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
N_1 = 70\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 294.3\,\mathrm{N} = 392\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,-0.08 4,-0.08 4,0.08 0,0.08;0.7,0.08 1.3,0.08 1.3,0.68 0.7,0.68;0,-0.08 -0.3,-0.75 0.3,-0.75;3,-0.08 2.7,-0.75 3.3,-0.75" linee="-0.9,-0.75 4.7,-0.75" vettori="0,0.08,0,1.5,N₁;3,0.08,3,1.5,N₂;2,-0.08,2,-1.35,Mg;1,-0.08,1,-1.0,mg" etichette="0.72,0.92,crate" xmin=-1.2 xmax=5.0 ymin=-1.8 ymax=2.0 titolo="Free-body diagram of the plank: $N_1$ at the end $A$, $N_2$ at $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, the weight $Mg$ at the center and the contact force $mg$ of the crate at $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Four forces act on the plank: the two support forces, the weight of the plank and the force with which the crate presses on it. The supports are smooth, hence each of them exerts a vertical force alone, and the crate rests without being attached, hence it presses with a force equal to its weight. The plank is uniform, so by the **torque of the weight** its weight acts at the center, at $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$.

Torques are taken about $A$, because the line of action of $N_1$ passes through that point and $N_1$ therefore has zero torque about it. The torque equation then contains the single unknown $N_2$, and it gives $N_2 = 294\,\mathrm{N}$. The vertical equation contains both unknowns, and with $N_2$ already known it gives $N_1 = 392\,\mathrm{N}$.

As a check, the torques are taken about the second support: $N_1 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ must balance $Mg \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ taken counterclockwise and $mg \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ taken counterclockwise, that is $1177\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 196.2\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} + 981\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, which is satisfied. The two values are positive, hence both supports push upward, as a support must.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform beam of mass $M = 30\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on two supports placed at its ends. A person of mass $m = 65\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on it. Determine the two support forces when the person is at $d = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from the left support, and the position at which the person must stand so that the left support carries twice the force carried by the right one.

$$
N_2 = \frac{M\,\dfrac{L}{2} + m\,d}{L}\,g ,
\qquad
N_1 = (M + m)g - N_2
$$
$$
N_2 = \frac{30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{m} + 65\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m}}{5.0\,\mathrm{m}} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 338\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
N_1 = 932\,\mathrm{N} - 338.4\,\mathrm{N} = 594\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
N_1 = 2N_2 \\[4pt]
N_1 + N_2 = (M + m)g
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_2 = \frac{(M + m)g}{3}
$$
$$
N_2\,L = M g\,\frac{L}{2} + m g\,x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{\dfrac{(M + m)L}{3} - \dfrac{M L}{2}}{m}
$$
$$
x = \frac{\dfrac{95\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m}}{3} - \dfrac{30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m}}{2}}{65\,\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{158.33\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m} - 75\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m}}{65\,\mathrm{kg}} = 1.28\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first question repeats the scheme of the preceding exercise with the second support at the far end, hence the moment arm of $N_2$ about the left support is the whole length $L$. The person stands nearer to the left support, therefore the left support carries the larger share, $594\,\mathrm{N}$ against $338\,\mathrm{N}$.

In the second question the ratio of the two support forces is given and the position is unknown. The sum of the two forces equals the total weight whatever the position of the person, since the vertical equation does not contain $x$; combining it with $N_1 = 2N_2$ gives $N_2 = \dfrac{(M+m)g}{3} = 311\,\mathrm{N}$, a value fixed by the data alone.

The torque equation about the left support then determines $x$. The person must stand at $1.28\,\mathrm{m}$ from the left support. The value is smaller than the $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ of the first question, which agrees with the fact that moving the person toward a support increases the share carried by that support.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform bar of mass $M = 12\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 2.4\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on two supports, each at the distance $c = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ from one end. A body of mass $m$ hangs from the left end of the bar. Determine the largest value of $m$ for which the bar stays in equilibrium.

$$
N_2 = 0
\quad\text{at the limit},
\qquad
\text{torques about the left support}
$$
$$
m g\,c = M g\left(\frac{L}{2} - c\right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m = M\,\frac{\dfrac{L}{2} - c}{c}
$$
$$
m = 12\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \frac{1.2\,\mathrm{m} - 0.40\,\mathrm{m}}{0.40\,\mathrm{m}} = 12\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.0 = 24\,\mathrm{kg}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The hanging body pulls the left end down and tends to turn the bar about the left support, while the weight of the bar, applied at the center, turns it the other way. The right support can only push upward, hence its force $N_2$ cannot become negative, and the bar is on the point of tipping when $N_2 = 0$.

At that limit three forces act on the bar: the pull $mg$ at the left end, the weight $Mg$ at the center and the force of the left support. Torques are taken about the left support, so that its unknown force disappears. The moment arm of $mg$ is $c = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and the moment arm of $Mg$ is $\dfrac{L}{2} - c = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, and setting the two torques equal gives $m = 24\,\mathrm{kg}$.

For $m < 24\,\mathrm{kg}$ the weight of the bar prevails, the right support is pressed and $N_2 > 0$, hence the bar stays in place. For $m > 24\,\mathrm{kg}$ no solution with $N_2 \ge 0$ exists and the bar **tips** about the left support. The limiting mass is twice the mass of the bar, because the moment arm of the bar is twice that of the load.

</div>
