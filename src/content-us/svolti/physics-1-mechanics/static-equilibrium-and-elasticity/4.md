## Exercise 1

A uniform ladder of mass $M = 15\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ leans against a smooth vertical wall and makes the angle $\theta = 60^\circ$ with a horizontal floor. Determine the force exerted by the wall, the normal force and the friction force exerted by the floor, and the smallest coefficient of static friction for which the ladder stays in place.

$$
\begin{cases}
N_1 - f = 0 \\[4pt]
N_2 - M g = 0 \\[4pt]
N_1 L\sin\theta - M g\,\dfrac{L}{2}\cos\theta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
N_1 = \frac{M g}{2\tan\theta},
\qquad
f = N_1,
\qquad
N_2 = M g,
\qquad
\mu_s \ge \frac{f}{N_2} = \frac{1}{2\tan\theta}
$$
$$
N_1 = \frac{15\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{2 \cdot 1.7321} = 42.5\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
f = 42.5\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
N_2 = 147\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\mu_s \ge \frac{42.48\,\mathrm{N}}{147.2\,\mathrm{N}} = 0.289
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wall is smooth, hence it exerts a force perpendicular to itself, that is horizontal, and no friction. The floor is rough, hence it exerts the normal force $N_2$, vertical, and the friction force $f$, horizontal and directed toward the wall, since the foot of the ladder tends to slide away from it.

Torques are taken about the foot of the ladder, where two of the three unknowns are applied. The force of the wall is horizontal and acts at the height $L\sin\theta$, hence its moment arm about the foot is that height; the weight is vertical and acts at the horizontal distance $\dfrac{L}{2}\cos\theta$ from the foot, hence that distance is its moment arm. The torque equation gives $N_1 = 42.5\,\mathrm{N}$, and the length of the ladder cancels.

The horizontal equation gives $f = N_1$ and the vertical equation gives $N_2 = Mg = 147\,\mathrm{N}$. These are the values that equilibrium requires; the floor can actually supply them only if $f \le \mu_s N_2$, hence equilibrium needs $\mu_s \ge 0.289$. The mass of the ladder cancels in this ratio, therefore the condition depends on the angle alone.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The ladder of the preceding exercise, of mass $M = 15\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$, leans against the same smooth wall at the angle $\theta = 65^\circ$, on a floor with coefficient of static friction $\mu_s = 0.40$. A person of mass $m = 70\,\mathrm{kg}$ climbs it. Determine the distance along the ladder, measured from the foot, at which the ladder begins to slip.

$$
\begin{cases}
N_1 = f \le \mu_s N_2 \\[4pt]
N_2 = (M + m)g \\[4pt]
N_1 L\sin\theta = M g\,\dfrac{L}{2}\cos\theta + m g\,d\cos\theta
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\mu_s (M + m) g L\sin\theta = \left(M\,\frac{L}{2} + m\,d\right) g\cos\theta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = \frac{\mu_s (M + m) L\tan\theta - M\,\dfrac{L}{2}}{m}
$$
$$
\mu_s (M + m) L\tan\theta = 0.40 \cdot 85\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 2.1445 = 364.6\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m}
$$
$$
d = \frac{364.6\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m} - 37.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m}}{70\,\mathrm{kg}} = 4.67\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,-0.3 0,4.8;-0.6,0 4.6,0;2.113,0 0,4.532" punti="1.057,2.266,pieno,G;0.139,4.232,pieno,P" vettori="0,4.532,0.8,4.532,N₁;2.113,0,2.113,1.4,N₂;2.113,0,1.313,0,f;1.057,2.266,1.057,0.9,Mg;0.139,4.232,0.139,2.9,mg" etichette="1.76,0.34,65°" xmin=-0.9 xmax=4.9 ymin=-0.5 ymax=5.0 titolo="The person at $P$, at $4.67\,\mathrm{m}$ from the foot, is the highest position for which friction can still hold the ladder"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The person adds a vertical force $mg$ applied at the point of the ladder reached, whose horizontal distance from the foot is $d\cos\theta$. Both the normal force at the floor and the required friction force change with $d$: the normal force stays equal to the total weight $(M+m)g$, while the force of the wall, and with it the friction force, grows as the person climbs, because the torque to be balanced grows.

The ladder is on the point of slipping when friction reaches its largest possible value, $f = \mu_s N_2$. Substituting this value in the torque equation about the foot leaves $d$ as the only unknown, and solving gives $d = 4.67\,\mathrm{m}$.

The value is smaller than the length $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ of the ladder, hence the person reaches the top only after the ladder has started to slip. Had the result exceeded $L$, the conclusion would have been that the ladder holds over its whole length. A larger angle $\theta$ increases $\tan\theta$ and therefore $d$, which is the reason a ladder is set as steeply as the work allows.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ladder of mass $M = 12\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is not uniform, and its center of gravity lies at $x_G = 1.6\,\mathrm{m}$ from the foot. It leans against a smooth vertical wall, on a floor with coefficient of static friction $\mu_s = 0.35$. Determine the smallest angle with the floor at which the ladder stays in place, and the force exerted by the wall at that angle.

$$
N_1 L\sin\theta = M g\,x_G\cos\theta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_1 = \frac{M g\,x_G}{L\tan\theta}
$$
$$
N_1 = f \le \mu_s N_2 = \mu_s M g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{x_G}{L\tan\theta} \le \mu_s
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tan\theta \ge \frac{x_G}{\mu_s L}
$$
$$
\tan\theta \ge \frac{1.6\,\mathrm{m}}{0.35 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 1.143
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta_{\min} = \arctan 1.143 = 48.8^\circ
$$
$$
N_1 = \mu_s M g = 0.35 \cdot 12\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 41.2\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ladder is not uniform, hence its weight acts at the given point $x_G$ and not at the midpoint. The three equations keep the same form, with the moment arm of the weight equal to $x_G\cos\theta$ instead of $\dfrac{L}{2}\cos\theta$.

The torque equation gives the force of the wall as a function of the angle, and that force decreases as $\theta$ grows, since $\tan\theta$ grows. The friction force must equal it, and friction cannot exceed $\mu_s N_2 = \mu_s M g$, because the normal force at the floor is the whole weight of the ladder. Imposing the inequality and solving for the angle gives $\tan\theta \ge 1.143$, hence $\theta_{\min} = 48.8^\circ$.

The mass cancels once more, therefore the smallest angle depends only on the coefficient of friction and on the ratio $\dfrac{x_G}{L}$. Here $\dfrac{x_G}{L} = 0.40$ is smaller than the value $0.50$ of a uniform ladder, hence this ladder may be set at a smaller angle than a uniform one on the same floor, which requires $\arctan\dfrac{1}{2 \cdot 0.35} = 55.0^\circ$.

At the limiting angle friction is exactly at its largest value, hence the force of the wall is $N_1 = \mu_s M g = 41.2\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>
