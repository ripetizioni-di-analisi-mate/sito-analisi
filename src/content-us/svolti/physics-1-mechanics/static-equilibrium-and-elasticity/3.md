## Exercise 1

A uniform beam of mass $M = 25\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is pinned to a wall at its left end and held horizontal by a cable fastened to its right end, making the angle $\theta = 40^\circ$ with the beam. Determine the tension of the cable and the two components of the force exerted by the pin.

$$
\begin{cases}
H - T\cos\theta = 0 \\[4pt]
V + T\sin\theta - M g = 0 \\[4pt]
T L \sin\theta - M g\,\dfrac{L}{2} = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
T = \frac{M g}{2\sin\theta},
\qquad
H = T\cos\theta = \frac{M g}{2\tan\theta},
\qquad
V = M g - T\sin\theta = \frac{M g}{2}
$$
$$
T = \frac{25\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{2 \cdot 0.6428} = 191\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
H = 190.8\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.7660 = 146\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
V = \frac{245.3\,\mathrm{N}}{2} = 123\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Three forces act on the beam: the weight $Mg$ at the center, the pull of the cable along the cable itself, and the reaction of the pin, whose direction is unknown and which is therefore written through the components $H$ and $V$. The axes are taken with $x$ horizontal away from the wall and $y$ vertical upward.

Torques are taken about the pin, because the two unknown components of its reaction have zero torque about that point. The moment arm of the tension about the pin is $L\sin\theta$, since the perpendicular distance from the pin to the line of action of a force applied at the end at the angle $\theta$ equals $L\sin\theta$. The torque equation thus contains $T$ alone and gives $T = 191\,\mathrm{N}$.

The length $L$ cancels in the torque equation, hence the tension does not depend on the length of the beam but only on its weight and on the angle. The two force equations then give the components of the pin reaction, $H = 146\,\mathrm{N}$ and $V = 123\,\mathrm{N}$, both positive, so the pin pushes the beam away from the wall and upward.

The magnitude of the pin reaction is $\sqrt{H^2 + V^2} = 191\,\mathrm{N}$, equal to the tension, and its angle above the horizontal is $\arctan\dfrac{V}{H} = 40^\circ$, equal to $\theta$. This holds for every value of $\theta$, since $V = T\sin\theta$ and $H = T\cos\theta$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform boom of mass $M = 40\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is pinned to the foot of a vertical wall and rises at the angle $\alpha = 55^\circ$ above the horizontal. A horizontal cable joins the upper end of the boom to the wall, and a load of mass $m = 120\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from that same end. Determine the tension of the cable and the force exerted by the pin.

$$
\begin{cases}
H - T = 0 \\[4pt]
V - M g - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
T L \sin\alpha - M g\,\dfrac{L}{2}\cos\alpha - m g L\cos\alpha = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
T = \left(\frac{M}{2} + m\right) \frac{g}{\tan\alpha},
\qquad
H = T,
\qquad
V = (M + m)\,g
$$
$$
T = \left(20 + 120\right)\mathrm{kg} \cdot \frac{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{1.4281} = 962\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
H = 962\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
V = 160\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.57 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,-0.4 0,3.6;0,3.277 2.294,3.277;2.294,3.277 2.294,2.95" poligoni="0.057,-0.040 2.351,3.237 2.237,3.317 -0.057,0.040;2.17,2.65 2.42,2.65 2.42,2.95 2.17,2.95" punti="0,0,pieno" vettori="2.294,3.277,1.294,3.277,T;1.147,1.638,1.147,0.738,Mg;2.294,2.65,2.294,1.75,mg;0,0,1.0,0,H;0,0,0,1.0,V" etichette="0.82,0.34,55°" xmin=-0.9 xmax=3.9 ymin=-0.7 ymax=3.8 titolo="The horizontal cable holds the upper end of the boom; the pin reaction has the components $H$ and $V$, and the load acts at the upper end"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces on the boom are the weight $Mg$ at its center, the pull $mg$ of the hanging load at the upper end, the horizontal tension $T$ at the same end and the pin reaction at the foot. The load hangs from an ideal cable, therefore it pulls the end of the boom straight down with a force equal to its weight.

Torques are taken about the pin. The tension is horizontal and applied at the height $L\sin\alpha$, hence its moment arm is that height. The two weights are vertical, hence their moment arms are the horizontal distances of their points of application from the pin, namely $\dfrac{L}{2}\cos\alpha$ for the boom and $L\cos\alpha$ for the load. The length $L$ cancels again, and the tension follows as $T = 962\,\mathrm{N}$.

The horizontal equation gives $H = T$, because the tension is the only other horizontal force. The vertical equation gives $V = (M + m)g = 1.57 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$, because the tension has no vertical component. The pin therefore carries the whole weight of boom and load, and in addition a horizontal thrust equal to the tension.

The factor $\dfrac{1}{\tan\alpha}$ shows that lowering the boom increases the tension: at $\alpha = 30^\circ$ the same load would require $T = 2.38 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$, two and a half times as much.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform beam of mass $M = 18\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 2.5\,\mathrm{m}$ is pinned to a wall at its left end and kept horizontal by a cable that joins the point of the beam at $b = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pin to a point of the wall at the height $h = 1.8\,\mathrm{m}$ above the pin. A load of mass $m = 45\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the free end of the beam. Determine the tension of the cable and the components of the pin reaction.

$$
\ell = \sqrt{b^2 + h^2} = \sqrt{(2.0\,\mathrm{m})^2 + (1.8\,\mathrm{m})^2} = 2.691\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\sin\theta = \frac{h}{\ell} = 0.6690,
\qquad
\cos\theta = \frac{b}{\ell} = 0.7433
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
H - T\cos\theta = 0 \\[4pt]
V + T\sin\theta - M g - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
T\sin\theta\,b - M g\,\dfrac{L}{2} - m g L = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
T = \frac{\left(M\,\dfrac{L}{2} + m L\right) g}{b\,\sin\theta},
\qquad
H = T\cos\theta,
\qquad
V = (M + m)g - T\sin\theta
$$
$$
T = \frac{(18 \cdot 1.25 + 45 \cdot 2.5)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{2.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.6690} = \frac{1324\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{1.338\,\mathrm{m}} = 990\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
H = 989.9\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.7433 = 736\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
V = 618.0\,\mathrm{N} - 662.2\,\mathrm{N} = -44.1\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cable is no longer attached at the end of the beam, hence its direction has to be found from the geometry. The cable joins the point $(2.0,\ 0)$ of the beam to the point $(0,\ 1.8)$ of the wall, therefore its length is $2.691\,\mathrm{m}$ and the angle $\theta$ it makes with the beam satisfies $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1.8}{2.691}$ and $\cos\theta = \dfrac{2.0}{2.691}$.

Torques are taken about the pin. The vertical component $T\sin\theta$ of the tension acts at $b = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pin, while the horizontal component passes through the beam itself and therefore has zero moment arm about the pin. The two weights act at $1.25\,\mathrm{m}$ and at $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The torque equation gives $T = 990\,\mathrm{N}$.

The vertical component of the pin reaction comes out **negative**, $V = -44.1\,\mathrm{N}$, which means that the pin pulls the beam downward with a force of $44.1\,\mathrm{N}$, not upward. The reason is that the cable is attached before the end of the beam, so its vertical pull $T\sin\theta = 662\,\mathrm{N}$ exceeds the total weight $618\,\mathrm{N}$; the surplus must be balanced, and only the pin can do it.

A pin can pull as well as push, therefore the result is admissible. A smooth support in place of the pin could not supply a downward force, and the beam would turn about it.

</div>
