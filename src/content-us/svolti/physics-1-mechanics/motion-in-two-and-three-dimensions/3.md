## Exercise 1

A ball is launched from ground level with speed $v_0 = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the angle $\theta_0 = 30^\circ$ above the horizontal, over horizontal ground. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the time of flight, the range, the maximum height and the velocity at the highest point.

$$
v_{0x} = v_0\cos\theta_0 = 20\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \cos 30^\circ = 17.32\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_{0y} = v_0\sin\theta_0 = 10.00\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\,v_0\sin\theta_0}{g} = \frac{2 \cdot 10.00\,\mathrm{m/s}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 2.04\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
R = \frac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta_0}{g} = \frac{(20\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 \cdot \sin 60^\circ}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 35.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H = \frac{v_0^2\sin^2\theta_0}{2g} = \frac{(10.00\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 5.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\vec{v}\left(\frac{T}{2}\right) = 17.3\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The launch and the landing are at the same level, therefore the formulas for the time of flight and for the range apply. The initial velocity is resolved first, since every formula uses its components: the horizontal one is $17.32\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the vertical one is $10.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The vertical motion is a free fall with initial upward velocity $10.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence the ball returns to the launch level after $T = \dfrac{2 v_{0y}}{g} = 2.04\,\mathrm{s}$. The horizontal motion is uniform, so the range is $v_{0x} T = 35.3\,\mathrm{m}$, the same value given by $\dfrac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta_0}{g}$.

The highest point is reached when the vertical component of the velocity vanishes, at half the time of flight, and the height there is $5.10\,\mathrm{m}$. At that instant the velocity is purely **horizontal** and equal to $17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, because the horizontal component never changes while the vertical one has been brought to zero by gravity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ball is kicked from ground level with speed $v_0 = 18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and lands $30\,\mathrm{m}$ away on the same level. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the two possible launch angles and the corresponding times of flight.

$$
R = \frac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta_0}{g}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sin 2\theta_0 = \frac{R\,g}{v_0^2} = \frac{30\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{(18\,\mathrm{m/s})^2} = 0.9083
$$
$$
2\theta_0 = 65.28^\circ
\quad\text{or}\quad
2\theta_0 = 180^\circ - 65.28^\circ = 114.72^\circ
$$
$$
\theta_1 = 32.6^\circ ,
\qquad
\theta_2 = 57.4^\circ
$$
$$
T_1 = \frac{2\,v_0\sin\theta_1}{g} = \frac{2 \cdot 18\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.5396}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 1.98\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
T_2 = \frac{2\,v_0\sin\theta_2}{g} = \frac{2 \cdot 18\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.8422}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 3.09\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The range formula contains the angle only through $\sin 2\theta_0$, hence it is inverted by taking the arcsine. The value $\dfrac{R g}{v_0^2} = 0.9083$ is smaller than $1$, therefore the target is within reach and the equation has solutions.

The sine takes the same value at an angle and at its supplement, so $2\theta_0$ has the two values $65.28^\circ$ and $114.72^\circ$, hence the two launch angles $32.6^\circ$ and $57.4^\circ$. The two angles are **complementary**, since their sum is $90^\circ$, which is the general property of the two launches that reach the same range with the same speed.

The times of flight differ, because they depend on $\sin\theta_0$ and not on $\sin 2\theta_0$: the steeper launch stays in the air $3.09\,\mathrm{s}$ against $1.98\,\mathrm{s}$. As a check, the range computed from the horizontal motion gives $18\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \cos 32.64^\circ \cdot 1.979\,\mathrm{s} = 30.0\,\mathrm{m}$ for the first launch and the same value for the second.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A projectile is launched from ground level with speed $v_0 = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $\theta_0 = 40^\circ$ above the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the height and the velocity at $t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$, and state whether the projectile is rising or falling at that instant.

$$
v_{0x} = 25\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \cos 40^\circ = 19.15\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_{0y} = 25\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sin 40^\circ = 16.07\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
y = v_{0y}\,t - \frac{1}{2}g t^2 = 16.07\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{s} - \frac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (1.5\,\mathrm{s})^2
$$
$$
y = 24.10\,\mathrm{m} - 11.04\,\mathrm{m} = 13.1\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_y = v_{0y} - g t = 16.07\,\mathrm{m/s} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{s} = +1.35\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{(19.15)^2 + (1.35)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 19.2\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{1.355}{19.15} = 0.0708
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 4.05^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The components of the initial velocity are $19.15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ horizontally and $16.07\,\mathrm{m/s}$ vertically. The vertical position at a given instant comes from the equation of the vertical motion, in which the upward displacement $v_{0y}t = 24.10\,\mathrm{m}$ is reduced by the fall $\dfrac{1}{2}g t^2 = 11.04\,\mathrm{m}$, leaving $13.1\,\mathrm{m}$.

The vertical component of the velocity at the same instant is $+1.35\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The sign is positive, therefore the projectile is still **rising**. The same conclusion follows from the time of the highest point, $t_H = \dfrac{v_{0y}}{g} = 1.64\,\mathrm{s}$, which is later than $1.5\,\mathrm{s}$.

The speed is $19.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity makes only $4.05^\circ$ with the horizontal, since the vertical component has nearly been cancelled while the horizontal one is unchanged. The maximum height of this launch is $\dfrac{v_{0y}^2}{2g} = 13.2\,\mathrm{m}$, hence at $t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ the projectile is already within $0.1\,\mathrm{m}$ of the top of its path.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A ball is launched from ground level with speed $v_0 = 22\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $\theta_0 = 55^\circ$ above the horizontal. A wall $10\,\mathrm{m}$ high stands $35\,\mathrm{m}$ from the launch point. Air resistance is negligible. Determine whether the ball passes above the wall and by how much, and determine the vertical component of its velocity there.

$$
v_{0x} = 22\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \cos 55^\circ = 12.62\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_{0y} = 22\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sin 55^\circ = 18.02\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
t_w = \frac{x_w}{v_{0x}} = \frac{35\,\mathrm{m}}{12.619\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 2.774\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
y_w = v_{0y}\,t_w - \frac{1}{2}g t_w^2 = 49.99\,\mathrm{m} - 37.74\,\mathrm{m} = 12.25\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y_w - h_w = 12.25\,\mathrm{m} - 10\,\mathrm{m} = 2.25\,\mathrm{m} > 0
$$
$$
v_y = v_{0y} - g t_w = 18.02\,\mathrm{m/s} - 27.21\,\mathrm{m/s} = -9.19\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,1.4281*t-0.030806*t^2,0,46.36" linee="35,0 35,10" punti="0,0,pieno;35,12.25,pieno;46.36,0,pieno" etichette="29,5,wall" xmin=-4 xmax=51 ymin=-3 ymax=20 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="The ball crosses the vertical line of the wall at the height $12.25\,\mathrm{m}$, hence it passes $2.25\,\mathrm{m}$ above the top"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The question concerns a height at a given horizontal distance, therefore the horizontal motion fixes the instant at which the ball reaches the wall. The horizontal motion is uniform, hence that instant is $t_w = \dfrac{x_w}{v_{0x}} = 2.774\,\mathrm{s}$.

Substituting that time into the equation of the vertical motion gives $y_w = 12.25\,\mathrm{m}$, which exceeds the height of the wall, so the ball **passes above it** with a clearance of $2.25\,\mathrm{m}$. The same height follows from the equation of the path, $y = x\tan\theta_0 - \dfrac{g}{2 v_0^2\cos^2\theta_0}x^2$, evaluated at $x = 35\,\mathrm{m}$, without computing the time.

The vertical component of the velocity at the wall is negative, therefore the ball is already descending when it crosses it. This is consistent with the time of the highest point, $t_H = \dfrac{v_{0y}}{g} = 1.84\,\mathrm{s}$, which is earlier than $2.774\,\mathrm{s}$, and with the range $R = \dfrac{v_0^2\sin 110^\circ}{g} = 46.4\,\mathrm{m}$, which places the wall beyond the top of the path.

</div>
