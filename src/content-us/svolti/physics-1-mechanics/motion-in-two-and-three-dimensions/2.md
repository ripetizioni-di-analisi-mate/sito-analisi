## Exercise 1

A ball rolls off a horizontal table $h = 1.25\,\mathrm{m}$ high with speed $v_0 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and leaves the edge horizontally. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the time of flight, the horizontal distance from the edge of the table to the landing point, and the magnitude and direction of the velocity on landing.

$$
\begin{cases}
x = v_0\,t \\[4pt]
y = h - \dfrac{1}{2}g t^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
v_x = v_0 \\[4pt]
v_y = -g t
\end{cases}
$$
$$
y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.25\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 0.505\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = v_0\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.5048\,\mathrm{s} = 1.51\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_y = -\,9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.5048\,\mathrm{s} = -4.95\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 + v_y^2} = \sqrt{(3.0)^2 + (4.95)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 5.79\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{|v_y|}{v_0} = \frac{4.95}{3.0} = 1.651
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 58.8^\circ
$$

::grafico{curve="t,1.25-0.545*t^2,0,1.5145" vettori="0,1.25,0.45,1.25,v₀;1.5145,0,1.9645,-0.743,v" linee="-0.6,1.25 0,1.25" punti="0,1.25,pieno;1.5145,0,pieno" xmin=-0.7 xmax=2.4 ymin=-1.1 ymax=1.8 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="The ball leaves the table horizontally at $v_0 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and lands $1.51\,\mathrm{m}$ from the edge; the arrows are drawn to scale, $0.15$ units for each $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The ball leaves the table horizontally, hence the initial velocity has no vertical component and the launch angle is zero. After the ball leaves the table its weight is the only force, therefore the acceleration is $g$ downwards and the equations of projectile motion apply, with the origin at the landing level and the launch point at height $h$.

The horizontal and the vertical motions are independent, so the time of flight is fixed by the vertical motion alone. Imposing $y = 0$ in the vertical equation gives $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}} = 0.505\,\mathrm{s}$, which is the time a ball **dropped** from the same height would take. The horizontal motion is uniform, therefore the distance covered in that time is $v_0 t = 1.51\,\mathrm{m}$.

On landing the horizontal component of the velocity is still $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, since no horizontal force acts, while the vertical component has grown to $4.95\,\mathrm{m/s}$ downwards. The two components are perpendicular, hence the speed is the square root of the sum of their squares, $5.79\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the velocity makes an angle of $58.8^\circ$ **below** the horizontal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A projectile is fired horizontally with speed $v_0 = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from the edge of a cliff and strikes the ground $60\,\mathrm{m}$ from the base of the cliff. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the height of the cliff and the speed of the projectile on impact.

$$
\begin{cases}
x = v_0\,t \\[4pt]
h = \dfrac{1}{2}g t^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
t = \frac{x}{v_0} = \frac{60\,\mathrm{m}}{25\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 2.40\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
h = \frac{1}{2}\,g\,\frac{x^2}{v_0^2} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (2.40\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 28.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_y = -\,g t = -\,9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 2.40\,\mathrm{s} = -23.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 + v_y^2} = \sqrt{(25)^2 + (23.54)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 34.3\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The data give a horizontal distance and ask for a vertical one, therefore the time of flight is the quantity that links them. The horizontal motion is uniform, hence the time is obtained from $x = v_0 t$ and equals $2.40\,\mathrm{s}$; this step uses no vertical information.

The vertical motion starts from rest, since the launch is horizontal, hence the drop in that time is $\dfrac{1}{2}g t^2 = 28.3\,\mathrm{m}$, which is the **height of the cliff**. Substituting the expression of the time into the vertical equation gives the symbolic formula $h = \dfrac{g x^2}{2 v_0^2}$, which shows that the height grows with the square of the horizontal distance at fixed launch speed.

The impact speed combines the unchanged horizontal component $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with the vertical component $23.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ acquired during the fall, and gives $34.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. As a check, the vertical component can also be obtained from $v_y^2 = 2 g h = 2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 28.25\,\mathrm{m}$, which gives the same $23.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An airplane flies horizontally at $v_0 = 60\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at an altitude of $300\,\mathrm{m}$ and releases a package, which leaves the airplane with the velocity of the airplane. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the time of fall, the horizontal distance covered by the package, the angle that its velocity makes with the horizontal on impact, and the position of the airplane at the instant of impact.

$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 300\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 7.82\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = v_0\,t = 60\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 7.821\,\mathrm{s} = 469\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_y = -\,g t = -\,9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 7.821\,\mathrm{s} = -76.7\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{|v_y|}{v_0} = \frac{76.72}{60} = 1.279
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 52.0^\circ
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{(60)^2 + (76.72)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 97.4\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The package is released, not thrown, therefore its initial velocity is the velocity of the airplane, horizontal and of magnitude $60\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Once released it is a projectile, hence its acceleration is $g$ downwards and the vertical motion is a free fall from rest that lasts $\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}} = 7.82\,\mathrm{s}$.

During that time the horizontal velocity stays $60\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the package advances $469\,\mathrm{m}$ and lands well ahead of the point of release. The vertical component of the velocity on impact is $76.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$, larger than the horizontal one, therefore the velocity makes $52.0^\circ$ with the horizontal and the speed is $97.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The airplane keeps flying horizontally at $60\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with no horizontal acceleration, exactly like the package, hence in $7.82\,\mathrm{s}$ it also advances $469\,\mathrm{m}$. The airplane is therefore **directly above the package** at the instant of impact, which is the independence of the horizontal and the vertical motions seen from the frame of the airplane.

</div>
