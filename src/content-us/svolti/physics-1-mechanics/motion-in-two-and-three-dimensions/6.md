## Exercise 1

A car starts from rest on a circular track of radius $R = 100\,\mathrm{m}$ and increases its speed at the constant rate $a_{\mathrm{tan}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Determine the speed, the radial acceleration and the magnitude of the total acceleration at $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and the angle that the acceleration makes with the radius.

$$
v = a_{\mathrm{tan}}\,t = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{s} = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{rad}} = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(10\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{100\,\mathrm{m}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a = \sqrt{a_{\mathrm{rad}}^2 + a_{\mathrm{tan}}^2} = \sqrt{(1.0)^2 + (2.0)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\tan\beta = \frac{a_{\mathrm{tan}}}{a_{\mathrm{rad}}} = \frac{2.0}{1.0} = 2.0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta = 63^\circ
$$

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.2832" vettori="1,1.7321,-2.1176,3.5321,v;1,1.7321,0.65,1.1259,aᵣ;1,1.7321,-0.2124,2.4321,aₜ;1,1.7321,-0.5624,1.8259,a" segmenti="-0.2124,2.4321,-0.5624,1.8259;0.65,1.1259,-0.5624,1.8259" punti="1,1.7321,pieno;0,0,pieno,O" xmin=-3.6 xmax=3.6 ymin=-2.8 ymax=3.7 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="The speed increases, hence the tangential component has the sense of the velocity; the total acceleration $\vec{a}$ is the sum of $\vec{a}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ and $\vec{a}_{\mathrm{tan}}$, drawn to scale with $a_{\mathrm{tan}}$ twice $a_{\mathrm{rad}}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The speed grows from rest at a constant rate, therefore the tangential component of the acceleration is the given $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the speed after $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ is $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The radial component depends on the speed reached, hence it is zero at the start and equals $\dfrac{v^2}{R} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at that instant.

The two components are perpendicular, so the magnitude of the total acceleration is the square root of the sum of their squares, $2.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The angle with the radius satisfies $\tan\beta = \dfrac{a_{\mathrm{tan}}}{a_{\mathrm{rad}}}$ and equals $63^\circ$, hence the acceleration is still closer to the tangent than to the radius.

The radial component grows with the square of the speed while the tangential one stays constant, therefore the acceleration turns progressively towards the center as the car speeds up. At $v = 14.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the two components are **equal** and the acceleration makes $45^\circ$ with the radius.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle moves on a circle of radius $R = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with speed $v(t) = (2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2})\,t$. Determine the two components of the acceleration and its magnitude at $t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$, and the angle that the acceleration makes with the radius.

$$
v(1.5\,\mathrm{s}) = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{s} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{tan}} = \frac{dv}{dt} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{rad}} = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(3.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{0.50\,\mathrm{m}} = 18\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a = \sqrt{(18)^2 + (2.0)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s^2} = 18.1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\tan\beta = \frac{2.0}{18} = 0.111
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta = 6.3^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tangential component is the derivative of the speed with respect to time, and the speed is a linear function of time, hence that component is the constant $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At $t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ the speed is $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, therefore the radial component is $\dfrac{v^2}{R} = 18\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The radius is small and the speed is already appreciable, so the radial component is nine times the tangential one. The magnitude of the acceleration is $18.1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, which differs from $a_{\mathrm{rad}}$ by less than $1\%$, and the acceleration makes only $6.3^\circ$ with the radius: the motion is **nearly centripetal**.

In angular quantities the same data give $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R} = 6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{a_{\mathrm{tan}}}{R} = 4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, and the radial component written as $\omega^2 R = 36 \cdot 0.50 = 18\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ gives the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cyclist enters a bend of radius $R = 25\,\mathrm{m}$ at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and slows down uniformly, leaving the bend at $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after covering a quarter of a circle. Determine the tangential acceleration and the magnitude of the total acceleration at the entrance and at the exit of the bend.

$$
s = \frac{1}{4} \cdot 2\pi R = \frac{\pi \cdot 25\,\mathrm{m}}{2} = 39.27\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2\,a_{\mathrm{tan}}\,s
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a_{\mathrm{tan}} = \frac{v_f^2 - v_i^2}{2s} = \frac{36 - 144}{2 \cdot 39.27}\ \mathrm{m/s^2} = -1.375\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{rad},i} = \frac{(12\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{25\,\mathrm{m}} = 5.76\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
\qquad
a_i = \sqrt{(5.76)^2 + (1.375)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s^2} = 5.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{rad},f} = \frac{(6.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{25\,\mathrm{m}} = 1.44\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
\qquad
a_f = \sqrt{(1.44)^2 + (1.375)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.99\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The speed changes uniformly along the path, therefore the motion along the curved path obeys the same relation between speed and distance as a motion along a straight line with constant acceleration, with the arc length in place of the displacement. The quarter of a circle is $39.27\,\mathrm{m}$ long, hence the tangential acceleration is $-1.375\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$; the sign is negative because the speed decreases, so that component is **opposite** to the velocity.

The radial component depends on the current speed, therefore it falls from $5.76\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the entrance to $1.44\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the exit, a reduction by a factor of four since the speed is halved. The total acceleration is $5.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the entrance and $1.99\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the exit.

At the entrance the radial component dominates and the acceleration points nearly towards the center, while at the exit the two components are comparable and the acceleration makes $43.7^\circ$ with the radius. As a check, the time spent in the bend is $\dfrac{6.0 - 12}{-1.375}\ \mathrm{s} = 4.36\,\mathrm{s}$, and the mean speed $9.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ multiplied by that time gives $39.3\,\mathrm{m}$, the length of the arc.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A small ball is fixed to the end of a rigid rod of length $L = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ that turns in a vertical plane about its other end. At the instant at which the rod is horizontal the speed of the ball is $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the only tangential acceleration is the one due to gravity. Determine the two components of the acceleration of the ball and the magnitude and direction of the total acceleration.

$$
a_{\mathrm{rad}} = \frac{v^2}{L} = \frac{(4.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{0.60\,\mathrm{m}} = 26.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{tan}} = g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a = \sqrt{(26.67)^2 + (9.81)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s^2} = 28.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\tan\beta = \frac{9.81}{26.67} = 0.368
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta = 20.2^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is horizontal, therefore the direction of the motion is vertical and the weight is parallel to the velocity. The tangential component of the acceleration is the component of the acceleration due to gravity along the velocity, hence it equals $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, while the rod supplies the force that keeps the ball on the circle.

The radial component depends on the speed and on the radius alone, and equals $\dfrac{v^2}{L} = 26.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed along the rod towards the axis, that is, horizontally at this instant. The two components are perpendicular, so the magnitude of the acceleration is $28.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The acceleration makes $20.2^\circ$ with the rod, hence it is mostly centripetal. The tangential component is the same whichever way the ball is moving at that instant, while the radial component would change with the speed: at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ it would be $6.67\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the angle with the rod would grow to $55.8^\circ$.

</div>
