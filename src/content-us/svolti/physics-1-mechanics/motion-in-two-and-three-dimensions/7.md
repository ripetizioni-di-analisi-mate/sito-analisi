## Exercise 1

A river $d = 120\,\mathrm{m}$ wide flows at $w = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A boat whose speed with respect to the water is $u = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ heads perpendicular to the banks. Determine the velocity of the boat with respect to the ground, the time of the crossing and the distance by which the boat is carried downstream.

$$
\vec{v} = \vec{u} + \vec{w} ,
\qquad
\vec{u} \perp \vec{w}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{u^2 + w^2}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{(5.0)^2 + (2.0)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 5.39\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{w}{u} = \frac{2.0}{5.0} = 0.400
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 21.8^\circ
$$
$$
t = \frac{d}{u} = \frac{120\,\mathrm{m}}{5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 24\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\Delta = w\,t = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 24\,\mathrm{s} = 48\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity of the boat with respect to the ground is the sum of its velocity with respect to the water and of the velocity of the water with respect to the ground. The boat heads perpendicular to the banks, therefore those two vectors are perpendicular and the magnitude of the sum is $5.39\,\mathrm{m/s}$, along a line making $21.8^\circ$ with the perpendicular to the banks.

The component of the velocity across the river is $u = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, since the current has no component in that direction. The crossing time depends on that component alone, hence it is $\dfrac{d}{u} = 24\,\mathrm{s}$, and it would be the same on still water.

During those $24\,\mathrm{s}$ the current carries the boat $48\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream, so the boat reaches the other bank **below** the point directly opposite the start. The two motions are independent, which is why the drift does not affect the crossing time.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the river and the boat of the preceding exercise, determine the direction in which the boat must head in order to land at the point directly opposite the starting point, and the time of the crossing.

$$
\vec{v} = \vec{u} + \vec{w}
\quad\text{with}\quad
\vec{v} \perp \text{banks}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
u\sin\theta = w
$$
$$
\sin\theta = \frac{w}{u} = \frac{2.0}{5.0} = 0.400
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 23.6^\circ
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{u^2 - w^2} = \sqrt{25 - 4}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 4.58\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
t = \frac{d}{v} = \frac{120\,\mathrm{m}}{4.583\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 26.2\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,-2,4.5826,u;-2,4.5826,0,4.5826,w;0,0,0,4.5826,v" linee="-3.8,0 2.2,0" punti="0,0,pieno" etichette="-3.5,-0.45,bank;-0.33,1.5,θ" xmin=-4.2 xmax=2.6 ymin=-1.1 ymax=5.4 titolo="The boat heads upstream by the angle $\theta$, so that the component of $\vec{u}$ along the bank cancels $\vec{w}$ and the resultant $\vec{v}$ is perpendicular to the banks"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The boat lands directly opposite the start when its velocity with respect to the ground is perpendicular to the banks, hence when the component of $\vec{v}$ along the banks vanishes. That component is the sum of $w$ and of the component of $\vec{u}$ along the banks, therefore the boat must head upstream by the angle $\theta$ for which $u\sin\theta = w$.

The condition gives $\sin\theta = 0.400$ and $\theta = 23.6^\circ$ measured from the perpendicular to the banks. The requirement $w < u$ is **satisfied**, so the manoeuvre is possible; with a current faster than the boat no heading would cancel the drift.

The resultant velocity has magnitude $\sqrt{u^2 - w^2} = 4.58\,\mathrm{m/s}$, smaller than $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, because part of the propulsion is spent against the current. The crossing therefore takes $26.2\,\mathrm{s}$ against the $24\,\mathrm{s}$ of the perpendicular heading, which is the price of landing at the opposite point.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An airplane whose speed with respect to the air is $220\,\mathrm{km/h}$ must travel due north. A wind of $60\,\mathrm{km/h}$ blows from the west toward the east. Determine the direction in which the airplane must be headed, its speed with respect to the ground and the time needed to cover $400\,\mathrm{km}$.

$$
\vec{v}_{PG} = \vec{v}_{PA} + \vec{v}_{AG} ,
\qquad
v_{PA} = 220\,\mathrm{km/h} ,
\quad
v_{AG} = 60\,\mathrm{km/h}\ \text{eastwards}
$$
$$
\sin\theta = \frac{v_{AG}}{v_{PA}} = \frac{60}{220} = 0.2727
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 15.8^\circ\ \text{west of north}
$$
$$
v_{PG} = \sqrt{v_{PA}^2 - v_{AG}^2} = \sqrt{48400 - 3600}\ \mathrm{km/h} = 212\,\mathrm{km/h}
$$
$$
t = \frac{400\,\mathrm{km}}{211.66\,\mathrm{km/h}} = 1.89\,\mathrm{h}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity of the airplane with respect to the ground is the sum of its velocity with respect to the air and of the velocity of the air with respect to the ground. The route must be due north, therefore the east component of the sum has to vanish, and since the wind contributes $60\,\mathrm{km/h}$ eastwards the airplane must be headed west of north by the angle for which $v_{PA}\sin\theta = v_{AG}$.

That condition gives $\theta = 15.8^\circ$: the nose of the airplane does not point along the route. The ground speed is the north component of the sum, $\sqrt{v_{PA}^2 - v_{AG}^2} = 212\,\mathrm{km/h}$, **smaller** than the airspeed, because part of the velocity with respect to the air is used to cancel the drift.

The flight of $400\,\mathrm{km}$ therefore takes $1.89\,\mathrm{h}$, about $1\,\mathrm{h}\,53\,\mathrm{min}$, against the $1.82\,\mathrm{h}$ that the same airplane would take in still air. The problem has the same structure as the crossing of a river, with the air in place of the water.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A car $A$ travels east at $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a car $B$ travels north at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along two straight roads. Determine the velocity of $B$ with respect to $A$ and the velocity of $A$ with respect to $B$, giving magnitude and direction of each.

$$
\vec{v}_A = 25\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_B = 15\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_{BA} = \vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A = (-25\,\hat{\imath} + 15\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_{BA} = \sqrt{(25)^2 + (15)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 29.2\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{15}{25} = 0.600
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 31.0^\circ\ \text{north of west}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_{AB} = -\,\vec{v}_{BA} = (25\,\hat{\imath} - 15\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_{AB} = 29.2\,\mathrm{m/s}\ \text{at}\ 31.0^\circ\ \text{south of east}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The frame attached to $A$ moves with constant velocity with respect to the ground, therefore the addition of velocities applies with the ground as the first frame and the car $A$ as the second. Writing $\vec{v}_{BG} = \vec{v}_{BA} + \vec{v}_{AG}$ and solving for the unknown gives $\vec{v}_{BA} = \vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A$, the difference of the two velocities measured from the ground.

Taking the $x$ axis east and the $y$ axis north, the difference has components $-25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence magnitude $29.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and direction $31.0^\circ$ north of west. The driver of $A$ therefore sees $B$ moving toward the **north-west**, although $B$ travels due north with respect to the ground.

Reversing the labels reverses the vector, since $\vec{v}_{AB} = -\vec{v}_{BA}$, so the driver of $B$ sees $A$ moving at the same $29.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ toward the south-east. The two accelerations are equal, both zero here, in agreement with the equality of the accelerations measured in two frames in relative motion with constant velocity.

</div>
