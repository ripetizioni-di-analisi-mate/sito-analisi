## Exercise 1

A car travels along a bend of radius $R = 50\,\mathrm{m}$ at the constant speed $v = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine its centripetal acceleration, its angular velocity and the time it would take to cover a whole circle of that radius.

$$
a_{\mathrm{rad}} = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(20\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{50\,\mathrm{m}} = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{50\,\mathrm{m}} = 0.40\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{0.40\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 15.7\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.2832" vettori="1.4142,1.4142,0.2828,2.5456,v;1.4142,1.4142,0.6364,0.6364,a" segmenti="0,0,1.4142,1.4142" punti="0,0,pieno,O;1.4142,1.4142,pieno" etichette="0.55,0.9,R" xmin=-3 xmax=3.2 ymin=-2.6 ymax=3.0 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="In uniform circular motion the velocity is tangent to the circle and the acceleration has magnitude $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$ and points to the centre"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The speed is constant, therefore the tangential component of the acceleration vanishes and the whole acceleration is the centripetal one, $\dfrac{v^2}{R} = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed towards the centre of the bend. The value is about $0.8\,g$, and the chapter on applications of Newton's laws shows that friction has to supply the corresponding force.

The angular velocity follows from $v = R\omega$, hence $\omega = 0.40\,\mathrm{rad/s}$: the radius joining the car to the centre of the bend sweeps $0.40$ radians each second. The period is the time needed for that radius to sweep $2\pi$ radians, which gives $15.7\,\mathrm{s}$.

As a check, the circle of radius $50\,\mathrm{m}$ is $2\pi R = 314\,\mathrm{m}$ long, and at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ it is covered in $\dfrac{314\,\mathrm{m}}{20\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 15.7\,\mathrm{s}$, the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wheel of radius $R = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ turns at $1200$ revolutions per minute. Determine the angular velocity, the speed of a point on the rim and its centripetal acceleration, and express that acceleration as a multiple of $g$.

$$
f = \frac{1200}{60\,\mathrm{s}} = 20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} ,
\qquad
\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \cdot 20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 125.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v = \omega R = 125.66\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.35\,\mathrm{m} = 44.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{rad}} = \omega^2 R = (125.66\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 \cdot 0.35\,\mathrm{m} = 5.53 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\frac{a_{\mathrm{rad}}}{g} = \frac{5527\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 5.6 \times 10^{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A rate given in revolutions per minute is a frequency, which is converted to $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ by dividing by $60$ and then to an angular velocity by multiplying by $2\pi$, since one revolution corresponds to $2\pi$ radians. The result is $\omega = 125.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

The speed of a point on the rim is $\omega R = 44.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the centripetal acceleration is $\omega^2 R = 5.53 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The form $\omega^2 R$ is the convenient one here, because the data give the rate of rotation and not the speed; the form $\dfrac{v^2}{R} = \dfrac{(43.98)^2}{0.35}$ gives the same number.

That acceleration is about $560$ times $g$, which is why a rotating part at this rate must be balanced and firmly attached. The acceleration grows with the **square** of the angular velocity, hence doubling the rate of rotation would multiply it by four.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point on the equator of the Earth describes a circle of radius $R = 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$ in one day. Determine its angular velocity, its speed and its centripetal acceleration, and compare that acceleration with $g$.

$$
T = 24 \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 8.64 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 7.27 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v = \omega R = 7.272 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m} = 463\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{rad}} = \omega^2 R = (7.272 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 \cdot 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m} = 3.37 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\frac{a_{\mathrm{rad}}}{g} = \frac{0.03369\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 3.4 \times 10^{-3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One revolution takes one day, therefore the period is $8.64 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{s}$ and the angular velocity is $\dfrac{2\pi}{T} = 7.27 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The same angular velocity belongs to every point of the Earth, while the speed $\omega R$ depends on the distance from the axis and is largest at the equator, where it reaches $463\,\mathrm{m/s}$, about $1670\,\mathrm{km/h}$.

The centripetal acceleration is $3.37 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, hence about $0.34\%$ of $g$. The rotation of the Earth therefore changes the measured weight of a body by a few parts in a thousand between the equator and the poles, and it is neglected in the problems of this course.

The smallness of the result comes from the period, not from the radius: the acceleration $\omega^2 R$ contains the square of $\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$, and a period of one day makes $\omega^2$ extremely small even for a radius of thousands of kilometers.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A ball attached to a string of length $L = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ moves in a horizontal circle of that radius with period $T = 0.80\,\mathrm{s}$. Determine the angular velocity, the speed and the centripetal acceleration of the ball. Determine also by what factor the acceleration changes when the speed is doubled at the same radius.

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{0.80\,\mathrm{s}} = 7.85\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v = \omega L = 7.854\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m} = 9.42\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{rad}} = \frac{v^2}{L} = \frac{(9.4248\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{1.2\,\mathrm{m}} = 74.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a'_{\mathrm{rad}} = \frac{(2v)^2}{L} = 4\,\frac{v^2}{L} = 296\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The period gives the angular velocity directly, $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T} = 7.85\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and the radius of the circle is the length of the string, since the circle is horizontal and described about the point where the string is held. The speed is therefore $\omega L = 9.42\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The centripetal acceleration is $74.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, about $7.5$ times $g$. Newton's second law then requires a resultant force of that magnitude per unit mass directed towards the centre, which in this arrangement is supplied by the horizontal component of the tension of the string, as the chapter on applications of Newton's laws develops for the conical pendulum.

The acceleration depends on the **square** of the speed at fixed radius, therefore doubling the speed multiplies it by four and gives $296\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. In terms of the period this corresponds to halving $T$, since $a_{\mathrm{rad}} = \dfrac{4\pi^2 L}{T^2}$.

</div>
