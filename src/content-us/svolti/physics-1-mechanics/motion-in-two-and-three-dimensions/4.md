## Exercise 1

A stone is thrown from the roof of a building $h = 30\,\mathrm{m}$ high with speed $v_0 = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $\theta_0 = 25^\circ$ above the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the time of flight, the horizontal distance from the base of the building to the landing point, the impact speed and the maximum height reached above the ground.

$$
v_{0x} = 15\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \cos 25^\circ = 13.59\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_{0y} = 15\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sin 25^\circ = 6.339\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
y = v_{0y}\,t - \frac{1}{2}g t^2 = -h
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2}g t^2 - v_{0y}\,t - h = 0
$$
$$
t = \frac{v_{0y} + \sqrt{v_{0y}^2 + 2 g h}}{g}
= \frac{6.339 + \sqrt{40.18 + 588.6}}{9.81}\ \mathrm{s}
= \frac{6.339 + 25.076}{9.81}\ \mathrm{s} = 3.20\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = v_{0x}\,t = 13.59\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 3.2023\,\mathrm{s} = 43.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2 g h} = \sqrt{225 + 588.6}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 28.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
H = h + \frac{v_{0y}^2}{2g} = 30\,\mathrm{m} + \frac{(6.339\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 32.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,30+0.46631*t-0.026541*t^2,0,43.53" poligoni="-8,0 0,0 0,30 -8,30" linee="-8,0 48,0" punti="0,30,pieno;43.53,0,pieno" etichette="-7.0,16,30 m" xmin=-11 xmax=50 ymin=-5 ymax=38 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="The stone leaves the roof at $25^\circ$ above the horizontal, rises $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and lands $43.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from the base of the building"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The landing level is $30\,\mathrm{m}$ below the launch level, therefore the formulas for the time of flight on level ground do not apply and the vertical equation has to be solved as a quadratic equation in $t$ with $y = -h$. Of its two roots only the positive one has physical meaning, since the motion starts at $t = 0$.

The positive root is $3.20\,\mathrm{s}$, and the horizontal motion is uniform, hence the horizontal distance is $v_{0x} t = 43.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The impact speed is obtained without the time from the relation $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2 g h$, which holds because the acceleration is constant and only the vertical component of the velocity changes; the result is $28.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The highest point of the path lies above the launch level by $\dfrac{v_{0y}^2}{2g} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, which depends on the vertical component of the initial velocity alone. Measured from the ground the maximum height is therefore $32.0\,\mathrm{m}$, reached $0.65\,\mathrm{s}$ after the launch.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ball is thrown from the edge of a cliff $h = 25\,\mathrm{m}$ high with speed $v_0 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ directed at $35^\circ$ below the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the time of flight, the horizontal distance from the base of the cliff to the landing point, and the angle that the velocity makes with the horizontal on impact.

$$
v_{0x} = 12\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \cos 35^\circ = 9.830\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_{0y} = -\,12\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sin 35^\circ = -6.883\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}g t^2 + |v_{0y}|\,t - h = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = \frac{-|v_{0y}| + \sqrt{v_{0y}^2 + 2 g h}}{g}
$$
$$
t = \frac{-6.883 + \sqrt{47.38 + 490.5}}{9.81}\ \mathrm{s} = \frac{-6.883 + 23.192}{9.81}\ \mathrm{s} = 1.66\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = v_{0x}\,t = 9.830\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 1.6625\,\mathrm{s} = 16.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_y = v_{0y} - g t = -6.883\,\mathrm{m/s} - 16.309\,\mathrm{m/s} = -23.19\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{|v_y|}{v_{0x}} = \frac{23.19}{9.830} = 2.359
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 67.0^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ball is thrown downwards, therefore the vertical component of the initial velocity is negative and the ball never rises above the launch point. The vertical equation with $y = -h$ gives again a quadratic equation, whose positive root is $1.66\,\mathrm{s}$; the flight is shorter than it would be for a horizontal throw from the same height, which would last $2.26\,\mathrm{s}$.

The horizontal motion is uniform with $9.830\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence the ball lands $16.3\,\mathrm{m}$ from the base of the cliff. The vertical component of the velocity on impact is $-23.19\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the velocity makes $67.0^\circ$ **below** the horizontal, a steeper angle than the launch angle because gravity has increased the downward component while the horizontal one is unchanged.

As a check, the impact speed is $\sqrt{(9.830)^2 + (23.19)^2} = 25.19\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the relation $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2 g h$ gives $\sqrt{144 + 490.5} = 25.19\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ball is launched from the ground with speed $v_0 = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $\theta_0 = 60^\circ$ above the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the two instants at which the ball is $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground and the corresponding horizontal distances from the launch point.

$$
v_{0x} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \cos 60^\circ = 10.00\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_{0y} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 17.32\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_{0y}\,t - \frac{1}{2}g t^2 = 8.0\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4.905\,t^2 - 17.32\,t + 8.0 = 0
$$
$$
t = \frac{17.32 \pm \sqrt{300.0 - 156.96}}{9.81}\ \mathrm{s} = \frac{17.32 \pm 11.96}{9.81}\ \mathrm{s}
$$
$$
t_1 = 0.546\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
t_2 = 2.98\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x_1 = 10.00\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.5464\,\mathrm{s} = 5.46\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
x_2 = 10.00\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 2.9848\,\mathrm{s} = 29.8\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The height $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is below the maximum height $\dfrac{v_{0y}^2}{2g} = 15.3\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the ball crosses that level **twice**, once while rising and once while falling, and the quadratic equation in the time has two positive roots.

The two roots are $0.546\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2.98\,\mathrm{s}$, and they are symmetric about the instant of the highest point, $t_H = \dfrac{v_{0y}}{g} = 1.77\,\mathrm{s}$, since the vertical motion with constant acceleration is symmetric in time about its turning point. The corresponding horizontal distances, obtained from the uniform horizontal motion, are $5.46\,\mathrm{m}$ and $29.8\,\mathrm{m}$.

The two abscissas are symmetric about the abscissa of the vertex of the parabola, which is $\dfrac{R}{2} = 17.66\,\mathrm{m}$, since the range of this launch is $R = \dfrac{v_0^2\sin 120^\circ}{g} = 35.3\,\mathrm{m}$. A ledge $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$ high placed at either distance would therefore be reached by the ball.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A projectile is launched from the edge of a cliff $h = 50\,\mathrm{m}$ high with speed $v_0 = 30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $\theta_0 = 40^\circ$ above the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the maximum height reached above the base of the cliff, the time of flight, the horizontal distance from the base to the landing point and the impact speed.

$$
v_{0x} = 30\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \cos 40^\circ = 22.98\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_{0y} = 30\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sin 40^\circ = 19.28\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
H = h + \frac{v_{0y}^2}{2g} = 50\,\mathrm{m} + \frac{(19.284\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 50\,\mathrm{m} + 18.95\,\mathrm{m} = 69.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
t = \frac{v_{0y} + \sqrt{v_{0y}^2 + 2 g h}}{g}
= \frac{19.284 + \sqrt{371.9 + 981.0}}{9.81}\ \mathrm{s}
= \frac{19.284 + 36.781}{9.81}\ \mathrm{s} = 5.72\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = v_{0x}\,t = 22.98\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 5.7151\,\mathrm{s} = 131\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2 g h} = \sqrt{900 + 981.0}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 43.4\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The projectile first rises above the launch point by $\dfrac{v_{0y}^2}{2g} = 18.95\,\mathrm{m}$, reached after $t_H = \dfrac{v_{0y}}{g} = 1.97\,\mathrm{s}$, hence the **maximum height** above the base of the cliff is $69.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The horizontal component of the velocity plays no part in this result.

The flight ends at the base level, $50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the launch point, therefore the time comes from the quadratic equation with $y = -h$ and equals $5.72\,\mathrm{s}$. The horizontal motion is uniform, so the landing point is $131\,\mathrm{m}$ from the base of the cliff.

The impact speed follows from $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2 g h$ and equals $43.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, larger than the launch speed because the projectile has descended below the launch level. The angle of the velocity on impact is obtained from $v_y = v_{0y} - g t = -36.78\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_x = 22.98\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which give $58.0^\circ$ below the horizontal.

</div>
