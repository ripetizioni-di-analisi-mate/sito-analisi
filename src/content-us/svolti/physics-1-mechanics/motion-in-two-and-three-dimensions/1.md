## Exercise 1

The position of a particle moving in the $xy$ plane is
$$
\vec{r}(t) = (3t^2 - 2t)\,\hat{\imath} + (4t - t^2)\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
with $t$ in seconds and the components in meters. Determine the position at $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, the velocity and the acceleration as functions of time, the speed at $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and the average velocity between $t = 0$ and $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\vec{v}(t) = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = (6t - 2)\,\hat{\imath} + (4 - 2t)\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}(t) = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = 6\,\hat{\imath} - 2\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{r}(2.0\,\mathrm{s}) = (3 \cdot 4 - 2 \cdot 2)\,\hat{\imath} + (4 \cdot 2 - 4)\,\hat{\jmath} = (8.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\vec{v}(2.0\,\mathrm{s}) = (12 - 2)\,\hat{\imath} + (4 - 4)\,\hat{\jmath} = 10\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_{\mathrm{av}} = \frac{\vec{r}(2.0\,\mathrm{s}) - \vec{r}(0)}{2.0\,\mathrm{s}}
= \frac{(8.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}}{2.0\,\mathrm{s}}
= (4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}_{\mathrm{av}}| = \sqrt{(4.0)^2 + (2.0)^2}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is the derivative of the position vector, and the unit vectors are constant, therefore each component is differentiated separately. The $x$ component $3t^2 - 2t$ gives $6t - 2$ and the $y$ component $4t - t^2$ gives $4 - 2t$. Differentiating once more gives the acceleration, whose components are the constants $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, hence the acceleration is constant in magnitude, direction and sense.

Substituting $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ into the position gives the point $(8.0\,\mathrm{m},\ 4.0\,\mathrm{m})$, and substituting it into the velocity gives a vector whose $y$ component vanishes. The velocity at that instant is therefore **horizontal**, with magnitude $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since the velocity is tangent to the path, the tangent to the path is horizontal at that instant, and the coordinate $y = 4t - t^2$ has its maximum at $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, therefore the particle is at the highest point of its path.

The average velocity is the displacement divided by the elapsed time. The particle starts at the origin, since $\vec{r}(0) = \vec{0}$, hence the displacement equals the final position and the average velocity is $(4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$, of magnitude $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. That value differs from the instantaneous speed $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the end of the interval, because the speed changes during the motion.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle moves in the $xy$ plane with velocity
$$
\vec{v}(t) = (4.0 - 2.0t)\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
in $\mathrm{m/s}$ with $t$ in seconds, and at $t = 0$ it is at the origin. Determine the position as a function of time, the instant at which the velocity is perpendicular to the acceleration, and the position at that instant.

$$
\vec{a}(t) = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = -2.0\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\vec{r}(t) = \int_0^t \vec{v}(s)\,ds = \left(4.0t - t^2\right)\hat{\imath} + \left(3.0t\right)\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v} \cdot \vec{a} = (4.0 - 2.0t)(-2.0) + 3.0 \cdot 0 = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\vec{r}(2.0\,\mathrm{s}) = (8.0 - 4.0)\,\hat{\imath} + 6.0\,\hat{\jmath} = (4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 6.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\vec{v}(2.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 3.0\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is the derivative of the velocity, and only the $x$ component depends on time, therefore the acceleration is the constant vector $-2.0\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed along the negative $x$ axis. The position is recovered by integrating the velocity component by component from $0$ to $t$, and the constants of integration vanish because the particle starts at the origin.

Two nonzero vectors are perpendicular exactly when their scalar product vanishes. The acceleration has no $y$ component, hence the scalar product reduces to $-2.0\,(4.0 - 2.0t)$, which vanishes for $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. At that instant the velocity is $3.0\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s}$, parallel to the $y$ axis and therefore **perpendicular** to the acceleration.

The $x$ component of the velocity is positive before $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and negative after, so the particle advances along $x$, reaches $x = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and then moves back, while $y$ grows at the constant rate $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The instant at which the velocity is perpendicular to the acceleration is therefore the instant at which the coordinate $x$ is maximum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A particle moves with position vector
$$
\vec{r}(t) = R\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
R = 3.0\,\mathrm{m} ,
\quad
\omega = 2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
Determine the speed, show that it is constant, determine the acceleration and verify that its magnitude equals $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$. Determine the time of one revolution.

$$
\vec{v}(t) = -R\omega\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
v = R\omega\sqrt{\sin^2(\omega t) + \cos^2(\omega t)} = R\omega = 3.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\vec{a}(t) = -R\omega^2\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} - R\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} = -\omega^2\,\vec{r}(t)
$$
$$
a = R\omega^2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot (2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(6.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{3.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 3.1\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two components satisfy $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$ at every instant, therefore the path is the circle of radius $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ centered at the origin. Differentiating the position gives the velocity, and factoring $R\omega$ out of its magnitude leaves $\sqrt{\sin^2(\omega t) + \cos^2(\omega t)} = 1$, hence the speed is the constant $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the motion is **uniform circular motion**.

Differentiating a second time gives an acceleration equal to the position vector multiplied by $-\omega^2$. A negative multiple of $\vec{r}$ points from the particle toward the origin, therefore the acceleration is directed at every instant toward the center of the circle, and its magnitude is $R\omega^2 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The same value is obtained from $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$, as the formula for the **centripetal acceleration** requires.

The angle $\omega t$ increases by $2\pi$ in the time $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$, which is therefore the period. As a check, the length of the circle is $2\pi R = 18.8\,\mathrm{m}$ and the speed is constant, hence one revolution takes $\dfrac{18.8\,\mathrm{m}}{6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 3.1\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
