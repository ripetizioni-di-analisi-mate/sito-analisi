## Exercise 1

A bicycle wheel of radius $R = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ rolls without slipping while the bicycle travels in a straight line at $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute the angular speed of the wheel, the speed of the point of the tyre that is at the top of the wheel and the number of turns the wheel makes over $d = 100\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \omega R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega = \frac{v_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R}
$$
$$
v_{\mathrm{top}} = 2\,v_{\mathrm{cm}} ,
\qquad
n = \frac{d}{2\pi R}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.35\,\mathrm{m}} = 14\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v_{\mathrm{top}} = 2 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 10\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
n = \frac{100\,\mathrm{m}}{2\pi \cdot 0.35\,\mathrm{m}} = 45\ \text{turns}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wheel rolls without slipping, hence the **rolling condition** $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \omega R$ is satisfied and the angular speed follows from the speed of the axle alone, without any further datum. Substituting gives $\omega = 14\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, that is, about $2.3$ turns per second.

The velocity of a point of the wheel is the sum of the velocity of the centre and of the velocity due to the rotation about the centre. At the top of the wheel these two contributions have the same direction and the same sense, and both have magnitude $v_{\mathrm{cm}}$, therefore the highest point moves at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, twice as fast as the bicycle.

Over one complete turn the circumference unwinds on the ground, so the centre advances by $2\pi R = 2.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Dividing the total path by this length gives $45$ complete turns in $100\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car starts from rest and reaches $v = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $t = 9.0\,\mathrm{s}$ with constant acceleration, on a straight road. Its wheels, of radius $R = 0.31\,\mathrm{m}$, roll without slipping. Compute the acceleration of the car, the angular acceleration of a wheel, the number of revolutions a wheel makes in those $9.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and the speed of the highest point of a wheel at the final instant.

$$
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{v - 0}{t} ,
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{a_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{25\,\mathrm{m/s}}{9.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 2.78\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{2.78\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{0.31\,\mathrm{m}} = 8.96\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
$$
s = \frac{1}{2}\,a_{\mathrm{cm}}\,t^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.778\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (9.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 112.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
n = \frac{s}{2\pi R} = \frac{112.5\,\mathrm{m}}{2\pi \cdot 0.31\,\mathrm{m}} = 57.8\ \text{revolutions}
$$
$$
v_{\mathrm{top}} = 2\,v = 50\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is constant, therefore it equals the change of velocity divided by the time interval, and it is $2.78\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Differentiating the rolling condition once more gives $a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \alpha R$, hence the angular acceleration of the wheel is $8.96\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$.

The motion is uniformly accelerated from rest, so the distance covered is $\dfrac{1}{2}a_{\mathrm{cm}}t^2 = 112.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The rolling condition in the form $s = R\theta$ turns that distance into a rotation angle, and dividing by $2\pi$ gives $57.8$ revolutions.

At the final instant the centre moves at $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence the highest point of the wheel moves at $50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The result does not depend on the acceleration, because the velocity of a point of the wheel is determined by the velocity of the centre at that instant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wheel of radius $R = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ rolls without slipping with centre speed $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute the speed of the point of the rim that is level with the axle and the speed of the point of the rim that is at height $\dfrac{R}{2}$ above the ground.

$$
v_P = \omega\,d_{PC}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\omega = \frac{v_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R} = \frac{3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.40\,\mathrm{m}} = 7.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
d_{PC} = \sqrt{R^2 + R^2} = R\sqrt{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_P = \omega R \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}\,v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 4.2\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
d_{QC} = \sqrt{R^2\sin^2\varphi + R^2(1-\cos\varphi)^2} = R\sqrt{2-2\cos\varphi}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
R\,(1-\cos\varphi) = \frac{R}{2}
$$
$$
\cos\varphi = \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d_{QC} = R\sqrt{2 - 1} = R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_Q = \omega R = v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),1+sin(t),0,6.29" linee="-2.1,0 2.3,0" segmenti="0,0,-0.866,0.5" punti="0,1,pieno;0,0,pieno;-0.866,0.5,pieno;1,1,pieno" vettori="0,1,0.7,1,v;-0.866,0.5,-0.516,1.106,v;1,1,1.7,0.3,√2 v" etichette="-0.52,0.08,R;0.1,-0.32,C;-1.2,0.5,Q;1.08,1.12,P" xmin=-2.3 xmax=2.9 ymin=-0.7 ymax=2.4 titolo="The point $Q$ of the rim at height $\dfrac{R}{2}$ is at distance $R$ from the contact point $C$, hence it moves exactly as fast as the axle, while the point $P$ level with the axle is at distance $R\sqrt{2}$ from $C$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The contact point has zero velocity, therefore at this instant the wheel turns about the contact line, and the speed of any point equals $\omega$ times its distance from the contact point $C$. The angular speed follows from the rolling condition and is $7.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

The point $P$ level with the axle is at the end of a horizontal radius, so its distance from $C$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $R$ and $R$, that is $R\sqrt{2}$. Its speed is therefore $\sqrt{2}\,v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 4.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and its velocity is perpendicular to the segment $CP$, hence inclined at $45^\circ$ below the horizontal.

For the point $Q$ at height $\dfrac{R}{2}$ the angle $\varphi$ measured from the contact point satisfies $R(1-\cos\varphi) = \dfrac{R}{2}$, hence $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Substituting in the distance from $C$ gives $d_{QC} = R$, so $Q$ moves at exactly the speed of the axle, $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Every rim point at that height has the same speed as the centre, whatever the radius and the speed of the wheel.

</div>
