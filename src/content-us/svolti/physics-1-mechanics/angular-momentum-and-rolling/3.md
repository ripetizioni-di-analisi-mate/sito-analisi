## Exercise 1

A particle of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ passes through the point $\vec{r} = (2.0\,\hat{\imath} - 1.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$ with velocity $\vec{v} = (4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute its angular momentum about the origin.

$$
\vec{\ell} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p} ,
\qquad
\vec{p} = m\vec{v}
$$
$$
\vec{p} = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s} = (2.0\,\hat{\imath} + 1.5\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}
$$
$$
\ell_z = x\,p_y - y\,p_x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} - (-1.0\,\mathrm{m}) \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}
$$
$$
\ell_z = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} + 2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}
$$
$$
\vec{\ell} = 5.0\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 vettori="0,0,2,-1,r;2,-1,6,2,v" punti="0,0,pieno,O;2,-1,pieno" xmin=-1.2 xmax=6.8 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.8 titolo="Position and velocity of the particle: the turn from $\vec{r}$ to $\vec{v}$ is counterclockwise, hence $\ell_z > 0$ and $\vec{\ell}$ points out of the plane of the drawing"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The angular momentum about a point is the vector product of the position vector, measured from that point, and the momentum. Both vectors lie in the $xy$ plane, therefore their product is directed along $z$ and only the component $\ell_z = x\,p_y - y\,p_x$ is nonzero.

Substituting the components gives $\ell_z = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$. The sign is positive, hence $\vec{\ell}$ points out of the plane of the drawing and the particle is turning **counterclockwise** as seen from the origin.

The value refers to the origin and to no other point. About the point $(2.0, -1.0)\,\mathrm{m}$, where the particle is, the position vector is zero and the angular momentum vanishes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ball of mass $m = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ is thrown horizontally with speed $v_0 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and moves under gravity alone, with negligible air resistance. Taking the launch point as origin, compute the angular momentum of the ball about that point at $t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ and the torque of the weight at the same instant, and verify that the torque equals the rate of change of the angular momentum.

$$
\begin{cases}
x(t) = v_0\,t \\[4pt]
y(t) = -\dfrac{1}{2}g\,t^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
v_x = v_0 \\[4pt]
v_y = -g\,t
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\ell_z = m\,(x\,v_y - y\,v_x) = m\left(v_0 t \cdot (-g t) + \frac{1}{2}g t^2 \cdot v_0\right) = -\frac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,v_0\,t^2
$$
$$
\ell_z = -\frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot (1.5\,\mathrm{s})^2 = -26.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}
$$
$$
\tau_z = x\,F_y - y\,F_x = v_0 t \cdot (-m g) - 0 = -m\,g\,v_0\,t
$$
$$
\tau_z = -0.20\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{s} = -35.3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\frac{d\ell_z}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(-\frac{1}{2}m g v_0 t^2\right) = -m\,g\,v_0\,t = \tau_z
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ball is a projectile, hence its position and its velocity are those of a uniform motion along $x$ and of a free fall along $y$. Substituting them in $\ell_z = m(x v_y - y v_x)$ and simplifying gives $\ell_z = -\dfrac{1}{2}m g v_0 t^2$, which at $t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ equals $-26.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$. The sign is negative because the ball turns clockwise about the launch point while it falls.

The only force is the weight, $\vec{F} = -mg\,\hat{\jmath}$, applied at the position of the ball. Its torque about the launch point has magnitude $m g x$, the product of the force and its lever arm, which is the horizontal distance of the ball from the launch point; at $t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ that distance is $18\,\mathrm{m}$ and the torque is $-35.3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

Differentiating the expression for $\ell_z$ with respect to time gives exactly $-m g v_0 t$, therefore the relation $\dfrac{d\vec{\ell}}{dt} = \vec{\tau}$ **is verified**. The angular momentum of the ball about the launch point grows in magnitude because the lever arm of the weight grows as the ball advances.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A particle of mass $m = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves with constant velocity of magnitude $v = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the straight line $y = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$, in the sense of increasing $x$. Compute its angular momentum about the origin and about the point $A = (0,\ 3.0\,\mathrm{m})$, and state whether each one changes with time.

$$
\ell_z = m\,(x\,v_y - y\,v_x)
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
v_x = v ,
\qquad
v_y = 0
$$
$$
\ell_z = -m\,v\,y = -0.40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m} = -6.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}
$$
$$
\ell_z^{(A)} = m\left[(x - 0)\,v_y - (y - 3.0\,\mathrm{m})\,v_x\right] = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

About the origin the position vector is $\vec{r} = x\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m}$ and the momentum is horizontal, therefore only the term $-y\,v_x$ survives. The coordinate $x$ does not appear in the result, hence $\ell_z = -6.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$ is **constant**, in agreement with the fact that the particle is free and the torque about any point is zero.

The same conclusion is read from the lever arm: the distance from the origin to the line of motion is $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at every instant, and $\ell = m v\,r_\perp$ depends on that distance alone.

About the point $A$ the particle moves along a line that passes through $A$, so the lever arm is zero and the angular momentum vanishes at every instant. The two values differ because the angular momentum is defined with respect to a chosen point, and *only* comparisons made about the same point are meaningful.

</div>
