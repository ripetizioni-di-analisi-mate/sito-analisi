## Exercise 1

A solid sphere of mass $M = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ rolls without slipping down a plane inclined at $\theta = 20^\circ$. Compute the acceleration of its centre, the static friction at the contact and the smallest coefficient of static friction that makes the motion possible.

$$
\begin{cases}
M g\sin\theta - f = M\,a_{\mathrm{cm}} \\[4pt]
f R = I_{\mathrm{cm}}\,\alpha \\[4pt]
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \alpha R
\end{cases}
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{2}{5}MR^2
$$
$$
f = \frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R^2}\,a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{2}{5}M\,a_{\mathrm{cm}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + \dfrac{2}{5}} = \frac{5}{7}\,g\sin\theta
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{5}{7} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot \sin 20^\circ = \frac{5}{7} \cdot 3.356\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
f = \frac{2}{5} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.397\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.88\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
N = M g\cos\theta = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot \cos 20^\circ = 27.66\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\mu_s \ge \frac{f}{N} = \frac{2.876\,\mathrm{N}}{27.66\,\mathrm{N}} = 0.104
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 8,0 8,2.912" curve="3.658+cos(t),2.3957+sin(t),0,6.29" punti="4,1.456,pieno;3.658,2.3957,pieno" vettori="3.658,2.3957,3.658,0.5957;4,1.456,3.111,3.899;4,1.456,5.222,1.901" etichette="3.88,0.92,Mg;2.76,4.06,N;5.4,2.05,f;1.3,0.21,θ" xmin=-0.6 xmax=9 ymin=-0.9 ymax=4.6 titolo="Free-body diagram of the sphere: the weight acts at the centre, the normal force and the static friction act at the contact point, and the friction is the only force with a torque about the centre"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Three forces act on the sphere: the weight, applied at the centre, the normal force and the static friction, both applied at the contact point. Newton's second law along the slope and the equation of rotation about the centre of mass form a system, closed by the **rolling condition** $a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \alpha R$.

The normal force passes through the centre, hence it has no torque about the centre, and the same holds for the weight, which is applied there. The friction is therefore the only force that makes the sphere turn, and eliminating $\alpha$ gives $f = \dfrac{2}{5}Ma_{\mathrm{cm}}$. Substituting into the first equation gives $a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \dfrac{5}{7}g\sin\theta = 2.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, smaller than the $3.36\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ of a body that slides down the same slope without friction.

The friction needed is $2.88\,\mathrm{N}$, while the surface can supply at most $\mu_s N$ with $N = Mg\cos\theta = 27.66\,\mathrm{N}$. Rolling without slipping is therefore possible provided $\mu_s \ge 0.104$. The ratio does not contain the mass, since both $f$ and $N$ are proportional to $M$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solid cylinder of mass $M = 2.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ is released from rest on a plane inclined at $\theta = 35^\circ$, with coefficients of friction $\mu_s = 0.20$ and $\mu_k = 0.15$. Establish whether it rolls without slipping and compute the acceleration of its centre and its angular acceleration.

$$
\mu_s^{\min} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{2}}{1 + \dfrac{1}{2}}\,\tan\theta = \frac{1}{3}\tan 35^\circ = \frac{0.7002}{3} = 0.233
$$
$$
0.233 > \mu_s = 0.20
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the cylinder slips}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
M g\sin\theta - \mu_k\,M g\cos\theta = M\,a_{\mathrm{cm}} \\[4pt]
\mu_k\,M g\cos\theta \cdot R = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2\,\alpha
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = g\,(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta) = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\,(0.5736 - 0.15 \cdot 0.8192) = 4.42\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{2\,\mu_k\,g\cos\theta}{R} = \frac{2 \cdot 0.15 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.8192}{0.15\,\mathrm{m}} = 16.1\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Rolling without slipping on a slope requires $\mu_s \ge \dfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}\tan\theta$, with $\beta = \dfrac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{MR^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for a solid cylinder. The condition gives $0.233$, a value larger than the coefficient available, therefore the hypothesis of rolling without slipping is **not satisfied** and the cylinder slides while it turns.

Once the body slips, the contact point moves backwards with respect to the plane, so the friction is kinetic, directed up the slope and of known magnitude $\mu_k N = \mu_k Mg\cos\theta$. The two equations of motion are now independent, because the rolling condition no longer holds.

The translation gives $a_{\mathrm{cm}} = g(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta) = 4.42\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the rotation gives $\alpha = 16.1\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. The two results are consistent with slipping, since $\alpha R = 2.41\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is smaller than $a_{\mathrm{cm}}$, hence $v_{\mathrm{cm}}$ grows faster than $\omega R$ and the body slides forward more and more with respect to the surface.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A solid cylinder of mass $M = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on a horizontal floor and is pulled by a horizontal force $F = 12\,\mathrm{N}$ applied to its axle. It rolls without slipping. Compute the acceleration of its centre, the friction at the contact and the smallest coefficient of static friction required.

$$
\begin{cases}
F + f = M\,a_{\mathrm{cm}} \\[4pt]
f R = I_{\mathrm{cm}}\,\alpha_z \\[4pt]
\alpha_z = -\dfrac{a_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R}
\end{cases}
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{2}MR^2
$$
$$
f = -\frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R^2}\,a_{\mathrm{cm}} = -\frac{1}{2}M\,a_{\mathrm{cm}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F = \frac{3}{2}M\,a_{\mathrm{cm}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{2F}{3M}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{2 \cdot 12\,\mathrm{N}}{3 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
\qquad
f = -\frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = -4.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\mu_s \ge \frac{|f|}{M g} = \frac{4.0\,\mathrm{N}}{4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 0.102
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $x$ axis is taken in the sense of $\vec{F}$ and the $z$ axis out of the plane of the motion, so that a wheel advancing towards $+x$ while rolling turns clockwise and has $\alpha_z = -\dfrac{a_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R}$. The force is applied at the axle, hence it has no torque about the centre, and the friction alone makes the cylinder turn.

Solving the system gives $f = -4.0\,\mathrm{N}$: the friction is directed **backwards**, opposite to the applied force. This is what produces the torque that spins the cylinder up, and the acceleration of the centre, $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, is smaller than the $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ that the same force would give to a block of equal mass on a frictionless floor.

The normal force equals the weight, because the applied force is horizontal, therefore the surface can supply at most $\mu_s M g$. Rolling without slipping requires $\mu_s \ge 0.102$; with a smaller coefficient the cylinder would slide forward while turning more slowly than the rolling condition demands.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A bowling ball of radius $R = 0.11\,\mathrm{m}$, treated as a homogeneous solid sphere, is launched along a horizontal lane with speed $v_0 = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and no rotation. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k = 0.080$. Compute the instant at which the ball begins to roll without slipping, the speed it has then and the distance it has covered.

$$
a = -\mu_k\,g ,
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{\mu_k\,M g\,R}{I_{\mathrm{cm}}} = \frac{\mu_k\,M g\,R}{\dfrac{2}{5}MR^2} = \frac{5\,\mu_k\,g}{2R}
$$
$$
v(t) = v_0 - \mu_k\,g\,t ,
\qquad
\omega(t)\,R = \frac{5}{2}\,\mu_k\,g\,t
$$
$$
v_0 - \mu_k g t^{*} = \frac{5}{2}\mu_k g t^{*}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t^{*} = \frac{2\,v_0}{7\,\mu_k\,g}
$$
$$
t^{*} = \frac{2 \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{7 \cdot 0.080 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = \frac{16\,\mathrm{m/s}}{5.494\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 2.91\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
v^{*} = v_0 - \mu_k g\,t^{*} = \frac{5}{7}\,v_0 = \frac{5}{7} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.71\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x^{*} = v_0 t^{*} - \frac{1}{2}\mu_k g\,(t^{*})^2 = \frac{12\,v_0^2}{49\,\mu_k\,g} = \frac{12 \cdot 64\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{49 \cdot 0.7848\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 20.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{f="8-0.7848*x+0*sqrt(2.9125-x)+0*sqrt(x);1.962*x+0*sqrt(2.9125-x)+0*sqrt(x);5.7143+0*sqrt(x-2.9125)" punti="2.9125,5.7143,pieno" segmenti="2.9125,0,2.9125,5.7143" legenda="v_{\mathrm{cm}}(t);\omega(t)\,R;\text{rolling}" xmin=-0.3 xmax=4.2 ymin=-0.6 ymax=9 nomi="t,v" titolo="The speed of the centre decreases while $\omega R$ grows; the two meet at $t^{*} = 2.91\,\mathrm{s}$, where the rolling condition is reached and the motion continues at the constant speed $\dfrac{5}{7}v_0$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At the launch the ball slides forward without turning, hence the contact point moves forward with respect to the lane and the kinetic friction is directed backwards. That friction slows the centre of mass with $a = -\mu_k g$ and at the same time exerts a torque $\mu_k M g R$ about the centre, which spins the ball up with constant angular acceleration $\alpha = \dfrac{5\mu_k g}{2R}$.

While the ball slips the two motions are independent, so $v$ decreases linearly and $\omega R$ grows linearly. The slipping ends at the instant at which the rolling condition $v = \omega R$ **is satisfied**, and solving that equation gives $t^{*} = 2.91\,\mathrm{s}$.

Substituting $t^{*}$ gives $v^{*} = \dfrac{5}{7}v_0 = 5.71\,\mathrm{m/s}$, a result that depends neither on $\mu_k$ nor on the radius: the coefficient changes how long the slipping lasts, not the fraction of the initial speed that survives. From that instant the friction needed to keep the ball rolling on a horizontal lane is zero, therefore the ball continues at constant speed. The distance covered before that instant is $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
