## Exercise 1

A yo-yo is a homogeneous solid cylinder of mass $M = 0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.030\,\mathrm{m}$. A massless inextensible string is wound around the rim, its free end is held fixed, and the yo-yo is released from rest. Compute the acceleration of its center, the tension of the string and the speed of the center after a fall of $h = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
M g - T = M\,a \\[4pt]
T R = I_{\mathrm{cm}}\,\alpha \\[4pt]
a = \alpha R
\end{cases}
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{2}M R^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R^2}\,a = \frac{1}{2}M\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M g = \frac{3}{2}M\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{2}{3}\,g
$$
$$
a = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 6.54\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.15\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 6.54\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 0.49\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2\,a\,h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 6.54\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m}} = 3.2\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
\omega = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{3.235\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.030\,\mathrm{m}} = 108\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two forces act on the yo-yo: the weight, applied at the center, and the tension of the string, applied at the point where the string leaves the rim and directed upward. The string is inextensible and does not slip on the rim, hence the center descends by the same length of string that unwinds, and the condition $a = \alpha R$ is satisfied, exactly as for a body rolling on a surface.

The weight has no torque about the center, so the tension alone produces the rotation, and eliminating $\alpha$ gives $T = \dfrac{1}{2}Ma$. Substituting into the equation of the translation gives $a = \dfrac{2}{3}g = 6.54\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, which is smaller than $g$ because part of the work of the weight becomes energy of rotation.

The tension is $0.49\,\mathrm{N}$, one third of the weight $1.47\,\mathrm{N}$, and neither $a$ nor the ratio $\dfrac{T}{Mg}$ depends on the mass or the radius. The acceleration is constant, hence the motion is uniformly accelerated from rest and the speed after $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is $3.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which corresponds to $\omega = 108\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A real yo-yo has its string wound around a thin axle of radius $r = 0.0050\,\mathrm{m}$, while the body is a solid cylinder of mass $M = 0.12\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.030\,\mathrm{m}$, whose moment of inertia is $I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$. The yo-yo is released from rest. Compute the acceleration of its center and the tension of the string.

$$
\begin{cases}
M g - T = M\,a \\[4pt]
T r = I_{\mathrm{cm}}\,\alpha \\[4pt]
a = \alpha r
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{g}{1 + \dfrac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{M r^2}} = \frac{2\,g\,r^2}{R^2 + 2\,r^2}
$$
$$
\frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{M r^2} = \frac{R^2}{2\,r^2} = \frac{(0.030\,\mathrm{m})^2}{2 \cdot (0.0050\,\mathrm{m})^2} = \frac{9.0 \times 10^{-4}}{5.0 \times 10^{-5}} = 18
$$
$$
a = \frac{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{1 + 18} = 0.516\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = M\,(g - a) = 0.12\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 0.5163)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.12\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is wound on the axle, therefore the lever arm of the tension about the center is $r$ and the constraint between the fall of the center and the rotation is $a = \alpha r$. The moment of inertia is still that of the whole body about its symmetry axis, since the axle and the disk turn together.

Eliminating $T$ and $\alpha$ gives $a = \dfrac{g}{1 + \dfrac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{Mr^2}}$, where the ratio $\dfrac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{Mr^2}$ is $18$: the small radius of the axle makes this ratio large, hence the descent is very slow, $0.516\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, about one twentieth of $g$.

The tension is $1.12\,\mathrm{N}$, close to the weight $1.18\,\mathrm{N}$, because the center accelerates very little and the two forces almost balance. A yo-yo therefore comes down slowly and spins fast, and the ratio $\dfrac{R}{r} = 6$ is what makes $\omega = \dfrac{a t}{r}$ large.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A spool of thread is a homogeneous solid cylinder of mass $M = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, lying on a horizontal floor. The thread leaves the spool at the top of the rim and is pulled horizontally with a force $F = 5.0\,\mathrm{N}$. The spool rolls without slipping. Compute the acceleration of its center, the friction at the contact and the smallest coefficient of static friction required.

$$
\left(I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M R^2\right)\alpha = F \cdot 2R ,
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M R^2 = \frac{3}{2}M R^2
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{2 F R}{\dfrac{3}{2}M R^2} = \frac{4F}{3MR}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \alpha R = \frac{4F}{3M}
$$
$$
a = \frac{4 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{N}}{3 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
F + f = M\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f = M\,a - F = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.333\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 5.0\,\mathrm{N} = +1.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\mu_s \ge \frac{|f|}{M g} = \frac{1.667\,\mathrm{N}}{2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 0.085
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="0.9*cos(t),0.9+0.9*sin(t),0,6.29" linee="-2.4,0 3.4,0" punti="0,0.9,pieno;0,0,pieno" segmenti="0,0.9,0,1.8" vettori="0,1.8,1.7,1.8,F;0,0,0.75,0" etichette="0.14,1.3,R;0.55,-0.3,f;-0.3,-0.3,C" xmin=-2.6 xmax=3.6 ymin=-0.7 ymax=2.7 titolo="The thread leaves the spool at the top, at distance $2R$ from the contact point $C$, hence the torque of $\vec{F}$ about $C$ is $2RF$ and the spool rolls in the sense of $\vec{F}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The contact point is at rest, therefore the spool turns about the contact line at this instant and the equation of rotation can be written about that line, where the unknown friction has zero lever arm. The moment of inertia about the contact line is given by the parallel-axis theorem and equals $\dfrac{3}{2}MR^2$, while the applied force has lever arm $2R$, since the thread leaves the spool at the top.

That equation gives $\alpha = \dfrac{4F}{3MR}$ at once, and the rolling condition gives $a = \dfrac{4F}{3M} = 3.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Newton's second law along the floor then supplies the friction, $f = Ma - F = +1.7\,\mathrm{N}$.

The sign is positive, hence the friction is directed **forward**, in the same sense as $\vec{F}$. The center accelerates more than $\dfrac{F}{M} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, which is what the force alone would produce, because the floor pushes the spool forward as well. The normal force equals the weight, so rolling without slipping needs $\mu_s \ge 0.085$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A spool has outer radius $R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, mass $M = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and moment of inertia $I_{\mathrm{cm}} = 0.045\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about its axis. A cable is wound on the inner axle of radius $r = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and leaves the spool at the bottom of the axle, where it is pulled horizontally with a force $F = 8.0\,\mathrm{N}$. The spool rolls without slipping on its outer radius. Compute the acceleration of its center, the friction at the contact and the smallest coefficient of static friction required.

$$
\left(I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M R^2\right)\alpha = F\,(R - r) ,
\qquad
a = \alpha R
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M R^2 = 0.045\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.20\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.165\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{8.0\,\mathrm{N} \cdot (0.20 - 0.080)\,\mathrm{m}}{0.165\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = \frac{0.96\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{0.165\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = 5.82\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
$$
a = 5.818\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{m} = 1.16\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
f = M\,a - F = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 1.164\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 8.0\,\mathrm{N} = -4.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\mu_s \ge \frac{|f|}{M g} = \frac{4.509\,\mathrm{N}}{3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 0.15
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cable leaves the axle at a height $R - r$ above the floor, therefore its lever arm about the contact point is $R - r = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$ and its torque turns the spool in the sense that makes it roll toward the pull. The equation of rotation about the contact line, with $I_{\mathrm{cm}} + MR^2 = 0.165\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, gives $\alpha = 5.82\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ and then $a = 1.16\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The friction follows from Newton's second law along the floor and is $-4.5\,\mathrm{N}$, hence it is directed **backwards**, opposite to the cable. The spool therefore advances toward the pull but with an acceleration much smaller than $\dfrac{F}{M} = 2.67\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, because the friction holds it back while making it turn.

The comparison with the previous exercise shows that the sense of the friction is decided by the height at which the cable leaves the spool, and not by the force. With the cable at the top the lever arm about the contact point is $2R$, with the cable at the bottom of the axle it is $R-r$, and the friction changes sign between the two cases. Rolling without slipping requires $\mu_s \ge 0.15$.

</div>
