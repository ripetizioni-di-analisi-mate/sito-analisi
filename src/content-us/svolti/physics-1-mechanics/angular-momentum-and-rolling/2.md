## Exercise 1

A solid cylinder of mass $M = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$ is released from rest on a slope and rolls without slipping, descending a height $h = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the speed of its center and its angular speed at the bottom, and the fraction of the kinetic energy that belongs to the rotation.

$$
M g h = \frac{1}{2}M v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{\mathrm{cm}}\,\omega^2 ,
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{2}M R^2 ,
\qquad
\omega = \frac{v_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R}
$$
$$
M g h = \frac{1}{2}\left(M + \frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R^2}\right)v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2 = \frac{3}{4}M v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \sqrt{\frac{4gh}{3}}
$$
$$
v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m}}{3}} = 4.43\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
\omega = \frac{4.429\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.12\,\mathrm{m}} = 36.9\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
\frac{K_{\mathrm{rot}}}{K} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{2}I_{\mathrm{cm}}\omega^2}{\dfrac{3}{4}M v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{4}M v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2}{\dfrac{3}{4}M v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 8,0 8,4" curve="6.132+0.6*cos(t),3.737+0.6*sin(t),0,6.29;1.332+0.6*cos(t),1.337+0.6*sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="6.132,3.737,6.132,1.337;1.332,1.337,6.132,1.337" vettori="0.796,1.069,-0.098,0.622,v" etichette="6.35,2.45,h;1.1,0.17,θ" xmin=-0.8 xmax=8.8 ymin=-0.8 ymax=4.6 titolo="The cylinder descends by $h$ from the solid position to the dashed one, and the work of the weight becomes kinetic energy of translation and of rotation, since neither the normal force nor the static friction does work"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The surface exerts on the cylinder a normal force and a static friction. The normal force is perpendicular to the velocity of the point on which it acts, and the static friction acts on the contact point, which has zero velocity while the body rolls without slipping; therefore neither force does work and the **mechanical energy is conserved**.

The kinetic energy of a rolling body is the sum of the translational part $\dfrac{1}{2}Mv_{\mathrm{cm}}^2$ and of the rotational part $\dfrac{1}{2}I_{\mathrm{cm}}\omega^2$. Using the rolling condition $\omega = \dfrac{v_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R}$ and $I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$ for a solid cylinder, the total kinetic energy becomes $\dfrac{3}{4}Mv_{\mathrm{cm}}^2$, and equating it to the loss of potential energy $Mgh$ gives $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 4.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The mass and the radius cancel in the expression for $v_{\mathrm{cm}}$, hence every solid cylinder released from the same height arrives with the same speed. The rotation carries one third of the kinetic energy, and this ratio also depends on the shape alone, since $\dfrac{K_{\mathrm{rot}}}{K_{\mathrm{transl}}} = \dfrac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{MR^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A hoop, a solid cylinder and a solid sphere, all homogeneous, are released from rest at the same height $h = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ on the same slope and roll without slipping. Compute the speed of the center of each body at the bottom and state the order of arrival.

$$
v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \sqrt{\frac{2gh}{1+\beta}}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\beta = \frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{MR^2}
$$
$$
\beta_{\mathrm{hoop}} = 1 ,
\qquad
\beta_{\mathrm{cyl}} = \frac{1}{2} ,
\qquad
\beta_{\mathrm{sph}} = \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
2gh = 2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m} = 23.54\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{\mathrm{hoop}} &= \sqrt{\frac{23.54\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{2}} = 3.43\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
v_{\mathrm{cyl}} &= \sqrt{\frac{23.54\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{1.5}} = 3.96\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
v_{\mathrm{sph}} &= \sqrt{\frac{23.54\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{1.4}} = 4.10\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each body the mechanical energy is conserved, and the same calculation as in the first exercise gives $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2gh}{1+\beta}}$, where $\beta$ is the ratio between the moment of inertia about the symmetry axis and $MR^2$. Neither the mass nor the radius appears in this formula, therefore the answer depends on the shape alone.

The three values of $\beta$ are $1$ for the hoop, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ for the solid cylinder and $\dfrac{2}{5}$ for the solid sphere. The larger $\beta$ is, the larger the share of the available energy that goes into the rotation, hence the smaller the speed of the center. The **sphere arrives first**, the cylinder second and the hoop last, and this order holds for any slope and any starting height.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A solid sphere rolls without slipping on a horizontal floor at $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and then goes up a slope inclined at $\theta = 25^\circ$, still rolling without slipping. Compute the distance it travels along the slope before stopping, and compare it with the distance traveled by a block launched at the same speed on the same slope, assumed frictionless.

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left(M + \frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R^2}\right)v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2 = M g\,d\sin\theta ,
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{2}{5}MR^2
$$
$$
\frac{7}{10}M v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2 = M g\,d\sin\theta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = \frac{7\,v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2}{10\,g\sin\theta}
$$
$$
d = \frac{7 \cdot (6.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{10 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot \sin 25^\circ} = \frac{252\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{41.46\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 6.08\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}M v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2 = M g\,d_{\mathrm{block}}\sin\theta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d_{\mathrm{block}} = \frac{v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2}{2 g\sin\theta} = \frac{36\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{8.292\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 4.34\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

While the sphere rolls without slipping neither the normal force nor the static friction does work, hence the mechanical energy is conserved along the slope as well. The body stops when all its kinetic energy has become potential energy, and a displacement $d$ along a slope inclined at $\theta$ raises the center by $d\sin\theta$.

With $I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \dfrac{2}{5}MR^2$ the kinetic energy of the rolling sphere is $\dfrac{7}{10}Mv_{\mathrm{cm}}^2$, so the mass cancels and $d = 6.08\,\mathrm{m}$.

A block has no rotation, so its kinetic energy at the same speed is only $\dfrac{1}{2}Mv_{\mathrm{cm}}^2$ and it climbs $4.34\,\mathrm{m}$. The sphere goes $\dfrac{7}{5}$ times farther, because it also carries the energy stored in its rotation, and that energy too must be converted before the body stops.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A homogeneous body of mass $M = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ is released from rest and rolls without slipping down a height $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, reaching the speed $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 3.96\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute its moment of inertia about the symmetry axis and identify the shape of the body.

$$
M g h = \frac{1}{2}\left(M + \frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{R^2}\right)v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta = \frac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{MR^2} = \frac{2gh}{v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2} - 1
$$
$$
\beta = \frac{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.20\,\mathrm{m}}{(3.96\,\mathrm{m/s})^2} - 1 = \frac{23.54\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{15.68\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} - 1 = 1.501 - 1 = 0.50
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = 0.50 \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.080\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 4.8 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mechanical energy is conserved, therefore the loss of potential energy $Mgh$ equals the kinetic energy at the bottom. Solving that equation for $\beta = \dfrac{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}{MR^2}$ isolates the only quantity that depends on the distribution of the mass, and the measured speed gives $\beta = 0.50$.

A value of $\beta$ equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ belongs to a homogeneous **solid cylinder**, for which $I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2 = 4.8 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. A hoop would have given $\beta = 1$ and a speed of $3.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$, a solid sphere $\beta = \dfrac{2}{5}$ and a speed of $4.10\,\mathrm{m/s}$; the measurement separates the three cases without weighing the body or measuring its radius with any precision.

</div>
