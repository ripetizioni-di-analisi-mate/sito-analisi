## Exercise 1

A uniform rod of mass $M = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ lies at rest on a horizontal frictionless table and can turn about a vertical axis through its centre. A bullet of mass $m = 0.010\,\mathrm{kg}$, travelling horizontally at $v = 300\,\mathrm{m/s}$ perpendicular to the rod, strikes one end and remains embedded in it. Compute the angular speed of the rod just after the impact and the kinetic energy lost.

$$
m\,v\,\frac{L}{2} = I_f\,\omega ,
\qquad
I_f = \frac{1}{12}M L^2 + m\left(\frac{L}{2}\right)^2
$$
$$
m\,v\,\frac{L}{2} = 0.010\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 300\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{m} = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}
$$
$$
I_f = \frac{1}{12} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 0.010 \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I_f = 0.1667\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 0.0025\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.1692\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{1.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}}{0.1692\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = 8.87\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2}m\,v^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.010\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (300\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 450\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2}I_f\,\omega^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.1692\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (8.867\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 6.65\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta K = 6.65\,\mathrm{J} - 450\,\mathrm{J} = -443\,\mathrm{J}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,0 2,0" punti="0,0,pieno;2,-1.4,pieno" vettori="2,-1.4,2,-0.35,v" segmenti="0,0.35,2,0.35" etichette="0.95,0.56,d;-0.06,-0.28,O;2.18,-1.42,m" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.9 ymin=-1.9 ymax=1.1 titolo="The table seen from above: the bullet moves perpendicular to the rod, hence its lever arm about the pivot $O$ is $d = \dfrac{L}{2}$ and its angular momentum is $m\,v\,\dfrac{L}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis exerts on the rod a force of unknown magnitude during the impact, therefore the linear momentum of the system is not conserved. That force is applied at the axis, hence its lever arm about the axis is zero and its torque vanishes, so the **angular momentum about the axis is conserved**.

Before the impact only the bullet moves. Its velocity is perpendicular to the rod, so the lever arm about the centre is the distance $\dfrac{L}{2}$ of the point of impact, and that angular momentum is $m v \dfrac{L}{2} = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$.

After the impact the bullet is embedded in the rod, so the two turn together as one rigid body. The moment of inertia is that of the rod about its centre, $\dfrac{1}{12}ML^2$, plus that of the bullet, a point mass at distance $\dfrac{L}{2}$. Dividing the angular momentum by $I_f$ gives $\omega = 8.87\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

Almost all the kinetic energy is lost, because the bullet has a very small mass and a very large speed: the collision is perfectly inelastic, and the final kinetic energy $\dfrac{1}{2}I_f\omega^2$ is small since $I_f$ is dominated by the rod.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform rod of mass $M = 1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ hangs at rest from a horizontal frictionless hinge at its upper end. A ball of putty of mass $m = 0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ moving horizontally at $v = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes the lower end of the rod and sticks to it. Compute the angular speed just after the impact and the largest angle through which the rod swings.

$$
m\,v\,L = I_f\,\omega ,
\qquad
I_f = \frac{1}{3}M L^2 + m L^2
$$
$$
m\,v\,L = 0.30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m} = 0.96\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}
$$
$$
I_f = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 1.2 \cdot 0.64\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 0.30 \cdot 0.64\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.448\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{0.96\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}}{0.448\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = 2.14\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}I_f\,\omega^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.448\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (2.143\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 1.029\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta U = \left(M g\,\frac{L}{2} + m g\,L\right)(1-\cos\varphi) = (4.709 + 2.354)\,\mathrm{J}\,(1-\cos\varphi)
$$
$$
1 - \cos\varphi = \frac{1.029\,\mathrm{J}}{7.063\,\mathrm{J}} = 0.1456
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\cos\varphi = 0.8544
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 31^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The impact is treated first. The hinge exerts an unknown force, so only the angular momentum about the hinge is conserved; before the impact it is that of the putty, whose lever arm is the whole length $L$ of the rod, hence that angular momentum is $m v L = 0.96\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$.

After the impact the rod and the putty turn together about the hinge. The moment of inertia of a rod about an end is $\dfrac{1}{3}ML^2$, and the putty adds $mL^2$, so $I_f = 0.448\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ and $\omega = 2.14\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

The swing that follows is a separate stage, in which no energy is lost: the hinge is frictionless, hence the **mechanical energy is conserved** from the end of the impact to the highest point. When the rod has turned by $\varphi$ the centre of the rod has risen by $\dfrac{L}{2}(1-\cos\varphi)$ and the putty by $L(1-\cos\varphi)$. Setting the kinetic energy $1.03\,\mathrm{J}$ equal to the gain of potential energy gives $\varphi = 31^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous disk of mass $M = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ turns freely about a fixed vertical axis through its centre and is initially at rest. A lump of clay of mass $m = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ moving horizontally at $v = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$, along a direction tangent to the rim, strikes the rim and sticks to it. Compute the angular speed after the impact and the fraction of the kinetic energy that survives.

$$
m\,v\,R = I_f\,\omega ,
\qquad
I_f = \frac{1}{2}M R^2 + m R^2
$$
$$
m\,v\,R = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 15\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.30\,\mathrm{m} = 0.90\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}
$$
$$
I_f = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 0.09\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 0.20 \cdot 0.09\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I_f = 0.225\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 0.018\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.243\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{0.90\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}}{0.243\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = 3.70\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
\frac{K_f}{K_i} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{2}I_f\,\omega^2}{\dfrac{1}{2}m\,v^2} = \frac{m R^2}{I_f} = \frac{0.018\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}}{0.243\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = 0.074
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity of the clay is tangent to the rim, hence its lever arm about the axis is the radius $R$ and its angular momentum is $m v R = 0.90\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$. The axis exerts a force on the disk during the impact, but that force has no torque about the axis, so the **angular momentum about the axis is conserved**.

After the impact the clay turns with the disk, and the moment of inertia is that of the disk, $\dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$, plus that of the clay at the rim, $mR^2$. The result is $\omega = 3.70\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

Writing the kinetic energy as the square of the angular momentum divided by $2I$, before and after the impact, the ratio reduces to $\dfrac{mR^2}{I_f}$, since the angular momentum is the same and the clay alone has $I = mR^2$ before the impact. Only $7.4\%$ of the initial kinetic energy survives, and the rest is dissipated in the deformation of the clay.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A uniform rod of mass $M = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ lies at rest on a horizontal frictionless table, free to move and to turn. A puck of mass $m = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides at $v = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ perpendicular to the rod, strikes it at a distance $d = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ from its centre and sticks to it. Compute the velocity of the centre of mass of the system after the impact and the angular speed about that centre of mass.

$$
x_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{m\,d}{M+m} = \frac{0.40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{m}}{1.9\,\mathrm{kg}} = 0.0842\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
(M+m)\,v_{\mathrm{cm}} = m\,v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{0.40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.9\,\mathrm{kg}} = 1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
m\,v\,(d - x_{\mathrm{cm}}) = I_{\mathrm{cm}}\,\omega
$$
$$
m\,v\,(d - x_{\mathrm{cm}}) = 0.40 \cdot 6.0 \cdot (0.40 - 0.0842)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} = 0.758\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{12}M L^2 + M\,x_{\mathrm{cm}}^2 + m\,(d-x_{\mathrm{cm}})^2
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = 0.1800\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 0.0106\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 0.0399\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.2305\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{0.7579\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}}{0.2305\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = 3.29\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The table is frictionless and no pivot holds the rod, hence the only horizontal forces during the impact are those the two bodies exchange, which are internal. Both the **linear momentum and the angular momentum are conserved**, and the angular momentum is computed about the centre of mass of the final system, which moves with constant velocity.

The linear momentum gives at once $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = 1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$, in the direction of the puck. The centre of mass of the final body lies on the rod at $x_{\mathrm{cm}} = 0.0842\,\mathrm{m}$ from the centre of the rod, on the side of the point of impact.

Before the impact only the puck carries angular momentum about that point, with lever arm $d - x_{\mathrm{cm}} = 0.316\,\mathrm{m}$, hence that angular momentum is $0.758\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$. After the impact the moment of inertia about the same point is obtained with the parallel-axis theorem for the rod and with $m(d-x_{\mathrm{cm}})^2$ for the puck, and dividing gives $\omega = 3.29\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The body therefore translates at $1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$ while turning at $3.29\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about its own centre of mass.

</div>
