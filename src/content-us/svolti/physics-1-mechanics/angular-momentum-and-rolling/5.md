## Exercise 1

A skater turns about a vertical axis at $1.2$ revolutions per second with her arms outstretched, when her moment of inertia about that axis is $I_1 = 4.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. She pulls her arms in and her moment of inertia becomes $I_2 = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. Compute the new rate of rotation, the kinetic energy before and after, and the work done by her muscles. Friction at the skates is negligible.

$$
I_1\,\omega_1 = I_2\,\omega_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega_2 = \frac{I_1}{I_2}\,\omega_1
$$
$$
\omega_1 = 2\pi \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 7.54\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
\omega_2 = \frac{4.5}{1.5} \cdot 7.540\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 22.6\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
f_2 = \frac{\omega_2}{2\pi} = 3.6\ \text{revolutions per second}
$$
$$
K_1 = \frac{1}{2}I_1\,\omega_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (7.540\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 128\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_2 = \frac{1}{2}I_2\,\omega_2^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (22.62\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 384\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W = K_2 - K_1 = 384\,\mathrm{J} - 128\,\mathrm{J} = 256\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The weight of the skater and the reaction of the ice are both vertical, hence their torque about the vertical axis vanishes, and friction at the skates is negligible by hypothesis. The total torque about the axis is therefore zero and the **angular momentum along the axis is conserved**, so $I_1\omega_1 = I_2\omega_2$.

The moment of inertia becomes three times smaller, hence the angular speed becomes three times larger and the skater turns at $3.6$ revolutions per second. The forces the skater exerts on her own arms are internal, so they do not enter this balance whatever their magnitude.

The kinetic energy is not conserved: writing it as $K = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}$ shows that at constant $L$ it is inversely proportional to $I$, hence it becomes three times larger. The increase of $256\,\mathrm{J}$ is the **work done by the muscles** in pulling the arms towards the axis against the outward tendency of the moving arms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A merry-go-round, a homogeneous disk of mass $M = 150\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, turns freely about its vertical axis at $\omega_0 = 0.80\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. A child of mass $m = 40\,\mathrm{kg}$, standing still on the ground, steps onto the rim. Compute the angular speed of the system afterwards and the kinetic energy lost.

$$
I_{\mathrm{disk}} = \frac{1}{2}M R^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 150\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (2.0\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 300\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{child}} = m R^2 = 40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (2.0\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 160\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{disk}}\,\omega_0 = \left(I_{\mathrm{disk}} + I_{\mathrm{child}}\right)\omega
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega = \frac{I_{\mathrm{disk}}}{I_{\mathrm{disk}} + I_{\mathrm{child}}}\,\omega_0
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{300\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}}{460\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 0.52\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
K_0 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 300\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (0.80\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 96\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 460\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (0.5217\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 62.6\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta K = 62.6\,\mathrm{J} - 96\,\mathrm{J} = -33\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is the merry-go-round together with the child. The forces that the two bodies exchange while the child's feet slide on the platform are internal, and the external forces are vertical and applied along the axis, hence the angular momentum about the axis **is conserved**.

Before the child steps on, only the disk contributes, with $I_{\mathrm{disk}}\omega_0$; the child stands on the ground and has zero angular momentum about the axis. Afterwards the two bodies turn together with the same angular speed, and the child at the rim contributes the moment of inertia of a point mass, $mR^2$. Solving gives $\omega = 0.52\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

The kinetic energy falls from $96\,\mathrm{J}$ to $62.6\,\mathrm{J}$. The loss of $33\,\mathrm{J}$ is dissipated by the friction between the child's shoes and the platform while the two reach a common angular speed, exactly as in a perfectly inelastic collision.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A horizontal platform turning freely about its vertical axis has moment of inertia $I_p = 250\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ and radius $R = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. A child of mass $m = 30\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on the rim while the system turns at $\omega_1 = 1.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The child walks to a distance $r = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis. Compute the new angular speed and the work done by the child.

$$
I_1 = I_p + m R^2 = 250\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (2.0\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 370\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I_2 = I_p + m r^2 = 250\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.50\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 257.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\omega_2 = \frac{I_1}{I_2}\,\omega_1 = \frac{370\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}}{257.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 1.44\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
K_1 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 370\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (1.0\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 185\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 257.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (1.437\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 266\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W = K_2 - K_1 = 266\,\mathrm{J} - 185\,\mathrm{J} = 81\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The child and the platform form a single system whose external forces have no torque about the vertical axis, therefore the angular momentum about that axis is conserved while the child walks. The child is treated as a point mass, so the contribution to the moment of inertia is $m$ times the square of the distance from the axis.

Walking inward reduces the total moment of inertia from $370\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ to $257.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, hence the angular speed rises to $1.44\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The platform alone contributes the same $250\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ throughout, and only the position of the child changes.

The kinetic energy grows by $81\,\mathrm{J}$, and this is the **work done by the child** against the force needed to move inward on a turning platform. No external agent supplies this energy, hence the increase comes from the chemical energy of the child's muscles, as in the first exercise.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A disk of moment of inertia $I_1 = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ turns at $\omega_0 = 50\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about a fixed vertical axis without friction. A second disk, of moment of inertia $I_2 = 1.2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ and at rest, is lowered onto the first one along the same axis; friction between the faces brings them to a common angular speed. Compute that angular speed, the kinetic energy lost and the fraction of the initial energy that is lost.

$$
I_1\,\omega_0 = \left(I_1 + I_2\right)\omega
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega = \frac{I_1}{I_1 + I_2}\,\omega_0
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{0.80\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}}{2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} \cdot 50\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
K_0 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (50\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 1.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (20\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 4.0 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\frac{|\Delta K|}{K_0} = \frac{600\,\mathrm{J}}{1000\,\mathrm{J}} = 0.60 = \frac{I_2}{I_1 + I_2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is frictionless and the weights act along the axis, hence the external torque about the axis is zero and the angular momentum is conserved. The forces that the two faces exchange while they slip on each other are internal to the system of the two disks.

Only the first disk carries angular momentum at the start, so $L = I_1\omega_0 = 40\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$, and after the coupling the two disks share the same angular speed, which gives $\omega = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

The kinetic energy falls from $1.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$ to $4.0 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$, and the $600\,\mathrm{J}$ lost heats the two surfaces. Writing $K = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}$ shows that the fraction lost is $\dfrac{I_2}{I_1+I_2}$, hence it depends on the two moments of inertia alone and not on the initial angular speed: the process **is the rotational analogue of a perfectly inelastic collision**, with the moment of inertia in place of the mass.

</div>
