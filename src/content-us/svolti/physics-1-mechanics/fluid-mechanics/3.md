## Exercise 1

An aluminum block of volume $V = 1.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and density $\rho_b = 2.70 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ hangs from a light string and is completely submerged in water, $\rho_f = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, without touching the walls of the vessel. Compute the weight of the block, the buoyant force and the tension in the string.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,5 0,5" linee="0,5.6 0,0 6,0 6,5.6;2.2,1.6 3.8,1.6 3.8,3 2.2,3 2.2,1.6;3,4.9 3,5.6" vettori="2.8,2.3,2.8,0.2,w;3.4,2.3,3.4,4.3,buoyancy;3,3,3,4.9,T" punti="3,2.3,pieno" etichette="0.2,5.25,free surface;0.9,2.3,block" xmin=-0.4 xmax=7.4 ymin=-0.5 ymax=6.1 titolo="A fully submerged body in equilibrium: $T + F_B = w$, hence the scale reads less than the weight"}

$$
\begin{cases}
w = \rho_b\,V\,g \\[4pt]
F_B = \rho_f\,V\,g \\[4pt]
T + F_B - w = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
T = (\rho_b - \rho_f)\,V\,g
$$
$$
w = 2.70 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 1.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 39.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_B = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 1.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 14.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
T = (2.70 \times 10^{3} - 1.00 \times 10^{3})\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 1.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 25.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Three forces act on the block: its weight, the tension of the string and the buoyant force. The block is fully submerged, therefore the volume of displaced fluid equals the volume of the block, and **Archimedes' principle** gives $F_B = \rho_f V g = 14.7\,\mathrm{N}$, the weight of $1.5\,\mathrm{L}$ of water.

The block hangs at rest, hence the resultant of the three forces vanishes and the tension is $T = w - F_B = 25.0\,\mathrm{N}$. The string is light, therefore the tension equals the reading of a scale attached to its upper end, and that reading is the **apparent weight** of the block.

The ratio of the apparent weight to the true weight is $1 - \dfrac{\rho_f}{\rho_b} = 0.63$, a number that depends on the two densities alone and not on the size of the block.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body hangs from a spring scale, which reads $W_1 = 7.84\,\mathrm{N}$ in air and $W_2 = 6.86\,\mathrm{N}$ when the body is completely submerged in water, $\rho_f = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The buoyancy of the air is negligible. Compute the volume and the density of the body.

$$
F_B = W_1 - W_2 = \rho_f\,V\,g
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
V = \frac{W_1 - W_2}{\rho_f\,g}
$$
$$
\rho_b = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{\dfrac{W_1}{g}}{\dfrac{W_1 - W_2}{\rho_f\,g}} = \rho_f\,\frac{W_1}{W_1 - W_2}
$$
$$
V = \frac{7.84\,\mathrm{N} - 6.86\,\mathrm{N}}{1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 9.99 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
\rho_b = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot \frac{7.84\,\mathrm{N}}{0.98\,\mathrm{N}} = 8.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale measures the tension in the string, which in air equals the weight, since the buoyancy of the air is neglected, and in water equals the weight diminished by the buoyant force. The difference of the two readings is therefore the buoyant force, $F_B = 0.98\,\mathrm{N}$.

Archimedes' principle turns that force into a volume, $V = \dfrac{F_B}{\rho_f g} = 9.99 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, which is about $0.100\,\mathrm{L}$. The mass follows from the reading in air, $m = \dfrac{W_1}{g} = 0.799\,\mathrm{kg}$, hence the density is $8.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

Combining the two steps gives $\rho_b = \rho_f\,\dfrac{W_1}{W_1 - W_2}$, a formula in which $g$ has cancelled: the method determines a density from two weighings alone, with no measurement of the shape of the body. This is the procedure attributed to Archimedes for testing the composition of a crown.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A brass cylinder of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and density $\rho_b = 8.50 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ hangs from a spring scale and is completely submerged in oil of density $\rho_f = 8.5 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Compute the reading of the scale. Determine then the smallest density a liquid must have for the cylinder to float in it.

$$
V = \frac{m}{\rho_b},
\qquad
T = m g - \rho_f\,V\,g = m g\left(1 - \frac{\rho_f}{\rho_b}\right)
$$
$$
V = \frac{2.0\,\mathrm{kg}}{8.50 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}} = 2.35 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
T = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\left(1 - \frac{8.5 \times 10^{2}}{8.50 \times 10^{3}}\right) = 19.62\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.900 = 17.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\rho_f\,V\,g \ge m g = \rho_b\,V\,g
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\rho_f \ge 8.50 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume of the cylinder is obtained from its mass and its density, $V = 2.35 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, and the cylinder is fully submerged, hence the displaced volume is the same. The buoyant force is $\rho_f V g = 1.96\,\mathrm{N}$, and the equilibrium of the three forces gives the reading $T = 17.7\,\mathrm{N}$.

The factor $1 - \dfrac{\rho_f}{\rho_b}$ shows that the loss of apparent weight is a fixed fraction of the weight, here $10\%$, set by the ratio of the two densities.

For the cylinder to float, the buoyant force available when it is just fully submerged must at least balance the weight, that is $\rho_f V g \ge \rho_b V g$, hence $\rho_f \ge \rho_b = 8.50 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. No ordinary liquid except mercury, with $1.36 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, satisfies the condition, and brass **floats** in mercury, with the submerged fraction $\dfrac{8.50}{13.6} = 0.625$.

</div>
