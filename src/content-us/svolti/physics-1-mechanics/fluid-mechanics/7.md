## Exercise 1

A large open tank contains water. A small opening of area $a = 1.5\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ is made in the vertical wall at the depth $h = 2.5\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface and at the height $y_0 = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Air resistance is neglected. Compute the speed of the water at the opening, the flow rate through it and the horizontal distance at which the jet reaches the ground.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 2,0 2,3.7 0,3.7" linee="0,4.2 0,0 2,0 2,1.1;2,1.3 2,4.2;-0.5,0 6.2,0" curve="2+7.0036*t,1.2-4.905*t^2,0,0.4946" vettori="2,1.2,3.1,1.2,v" segmenti="0.6,3.7,0.6,1.2;2.35,1.2,2.35,0;2,-0.5,5.46,-0.5" punti="5.46,0,pieno" etichette="0.75,2.4,h = 2.5 m;2.45,0.55,1.2 m;3.2,-0.85,x = 3.46 m" xmin=-0.7 xmax=6.6 ymin=-1.3 ymax=4.6 titolo="Efflux from the opening and the projectile motion of the jet: $v = \sqrt{2gh}$"}

$$
v = \sqrt{2gh},
\qquad
Q = a\,v,
\qquad
y_0 = \frac{1}{2}g t^2,
\qquad
x = v\,t
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{m}} = 7.00\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 1.5 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} \cdot 7.004\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.05 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 1.05\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2\,y_0}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 0.495\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = 7.004\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.4946\,\mathrm{s} = 3.46\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Bernoulli's equation is applied along the streamline that joins a point of the free surface to the opening. Both points are in contact with the atmosphere, hence the pressures are equal and cancel. The tank is large compared with the opening, therefore the continuity equation gives a speed at the free surface negligible compared with the speed at the opening, and the equation reduces to $\rho g h = \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$. **Torricelli's theorem** then gives $v = \sqrt{2gh} = 7.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the speed of a body falling freely from the height $h$.

The flow rate is the product of the area of the opening and that speed, $1.05\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

Once outside, the jet is a projectile with horizontal initial velocity, since air resistance is neglected and the only force acting on it is its weight. The vertical motion gives the time of fall from $y_0 = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$, that is $0.495\,\mathrm{s}$, and the horizontal motion is uniform, hence the jet lands at $x = 3.46\,\mathrm{m}$ from the wall.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Water flows steadily in a pipe. At the section $1$ the diameter is $d_1 = 8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the speed is $v_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the gauge pressure is $p_1 = 1.80 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. At the section $2$ the diameter is $d_2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the pipe has risen by $\Delta y = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the speed and the gauge pressure at the section $2$.

$$
\begin{cases}
A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 \\[4pt]
p_1 + \rho g y_1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2 = p_2 + \rho g y_2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
v_2 = \left(\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^{2} v_1,
\qquad
p_2 = p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_1^2 - v_2^2) - \rho g\,\Delta y
$$
$$
v_2 = \left(\frac{8.0}{4.0}\right)^{2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_1^2 - v_2^2) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot (4.0 - 64)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = -3.00 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
\rho g\,\Delta y = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m} = 4.91 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
p_2 = 1.80 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} - 3.00 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa} - 4.91 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.01 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two unknowns are the speed and the pressure at the section $2$, hence two equations are needed. The continuity equation supplies the first, and it involves the speeds alone. Since the area is smaller by the factor $4$, the speed is larger by the factor $4$ and equals $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

**Bernoulli's equation** supplies the second. The hypotheses are satisfied, since the flow is steady, the water is treated as incompressible and the viscous forces are neglected, and the two sections belong to the same flow tube. Solving it for $p_2$ shows that the pressure drops for two separate reasons: the kinetic term grows by $3.00 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ as the water accelerates, and the gravitational term grows by $4.91 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ as the water rises.

The gauge pressure at the section $2$ is therefore $1.01 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Both contributions are positive here, hence a pipe that narrows while rising gives the largest pressure drop, which is what limits the height a pump can supply.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A Venturi meter is inserted in a horizontal pipe carrying water. The wide section has area $A_1 = 40\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and the throat has area $A_2 = 10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The pressure difference between the two sections is $p_1 - p_2 = 1.2 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Compute the speed in each section and the flow rate.

$$
\begin{cases}
A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 \\[4pt]
p_1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2 = p_2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
p_1 - p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2\left[\left(\frac{A_1}{A_2}\right)^{2} - 1\right]
$$
$$
v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(p_1 - p_2)}{\rho\left[\left(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}\right)^{2} - 1\right]}},
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{A_1}{A_2}\,v_1,
\qquad
Q = A_1 v_1
$$
$$
v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.2 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}}{1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot (16 - 1)}} = \sqrt{1.6\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2 = 4 \cdot 1.265\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.06\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 40 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} \cdot 1.265\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.06 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 5.1\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pipe is horizontal, therefore the two sections lie at the same height and the gravitational terms of Bernoulli's equation cancel. The continuity equation gives $v_2 = \dfrac{A_1}{A_2}v_1 = 4v_1$, and substituting it leaves one equation in $v_1$ alone.

Solving gives $v_1 = 1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the wide section and $v_2 = 5.06\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the throat. The flow rate is the same at both sections, $5.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and it is the quantity the instrument measures: the pressure difference is read on two vertical tubes, whose levels here differ by $\dfrac{p_1 - p_2}{\rho g} = 1.22\,\mathrm{m}$.

The pressure is **lower** where the speed is larger, in agreement with Bernoulli's equation, and the difference grows with the square of the flow rate, so a meter with a narrow throat is sensitive at small flow rates and gives large pressure drops at large ones.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A cylindrical tank of horizontal cross-section $A = 0.80\,\mathrm{m^2}$, open at the top, is filled with water to the height $H = 1.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above a small opening of area $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ in its bottom. Compute the initial flow rate through the opening and the time the tank takes to empty.

$$
v = \sqrt{2gy},
\qquad
Q_0 = a\sqrt{2gH}
$$
$$
A\,\frac{dy}{dt} = -a\sqrt{2gy}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\int_H^0 \frac{dy}{\sqrt{y}} = -\frac{a}{A}\sqrt{2g}\int_0^{t_s} dt
$$
$$
2\sqrt{H} = \frac{a}{A}\sqrt{2g}\,t_s
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_s = \frac{A}{a}\sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}}
$$
$$
Q_0 = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.60\,\mathrm{m}} = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} \cdot 5.60\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.12 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
t_s = \frac{0.80\,\mathrm{m^2}}{2.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}}\sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.60\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 4.0 \times 10^{3} \cdot 0.5711\,\mathrm{s} = 2.28 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The opening is small compared with the tank, therefore the speed of the free surface is negligible compared with the speed at the opening, and Torricelli's theorem gives the efflux speed $\sqrt{2gy}$ when the water stands at the height $y$ above the opening. At the initial height the speed is $5.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the flow rate is $1.12 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

The level falls while the tank empties, hence the efflux speed decreases and the flow rate is not constant. The volume lost by the tank in the time $dt$ is $-A\,dy$, and the volume leaving through the opening is $a\sqrt{2gy}\,dt$, therefore equating the two gives the differential equation written above. Separating the variables and integrating from $y = H$ to $y = 0$ gives the emptying time
$$
t_s = \frac{A}{a}\sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}} = 2.28 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s} ,
$$
that is about $38$ minutes.

The time is twice the one obtained by dividing the volume $AH$ by the initial flow rate, which gives $1.14 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}$. The factor $2$ is the consequence of the decreasing speed, since the average efflux speed over the whole process is half the initial one.

</div>
