## Exercise 1

A diver works at the depth $h = 25\,\mathrm{m}$ in a fresh-water lake, where $\rho = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the atmospheric pressure at the surface is $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 1.013 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The helmet carries a flat circular window of diameter $d = 12\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the air inside the helmet is kept at atmospheric pressure. Compute the gauge pressure and the absolute pressure at that depth, and the net force that the water exerts on the window.

$$
p_{\mathrm{gauge}} = \rho g h,
\qquad
p = p_{\mathrm{atm}} + \rho g h,
\qquad
F = p_{\mathrm{gauge}}\,A
\quad\text{with}\quad
A = \frac{\pi d^2}{4}
$$
$$
p_{\mathrm{gauge}} = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 25\,\mathrm{m} = 2.45 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
p = 1.013 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} + 2.45 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 3.47 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi\,(0.12\,\mathrm{m})^2}{4} = 1.131 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
F = 2.4525 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} \cdot 1.131 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 2.77 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The water is at rest and the lake is open to the atmosphere, hence the pressure at depth $h$ is $p = p_{\mathrm{atm}} + \rho g h$. The term $\rho g h$ is the **gauge pressure**, and at $25\,\mathrm{m}$ it reaches $2.45 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, about $2.4$ atmospheres; adding the atmosphere gives the absolute pressure $3.47 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$.

The window has water on one face and air at atmospheric pressure on the other, therefore the two atmospheric contributions cancel and the net force is produced by the gauge pressure alone. The window is flat and its dimensions are small compared with the depth, hence the pressure is taken as uniform over it and the force is $F = p_{\mathrm{gauge}}A$. The result is $2.77 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$, the weight of a mass of about $280\,\mathrm{kg}$ pressing on a disc of $12\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A vertical rectangular gate of width $w = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ closes the outlet of a reservoir. The water reaches the top edge of the gate and the depth of the gate is $H = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$; the other face of the gate is exposed to the atmosphere. Compute the resultant force of the water on the gate and the depth at which it is applied.

$$
dF = \rho g y \cdot w\,dy
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
F = \int_0^H \rho g\,w\,y\,dy = \frac{1}{2}\rho g\,w\,H^2
$$
$$
y_{\mathrm{cp}} = \frac{1}{F}\int_0^H y\,dF = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^H \rho g\,w\,y^2\,dy}{\dfrac{1}{2}\rho g\,w\,H^2} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{3}H^3}{\dfrac{1}{2}H^2} = \frac{2}{3}H
$$
$$
F = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot (3.0\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 1.77 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
y_{\mathrm{cp}} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressure on the gate grows with the depth, therefore the force is not the product of a single pressure and the area, and the gate is divided into horizontal strips of depth $y$, height $dy$ and area $w\,dy$, over each of which the gauge pressure $\rho g y$ is constant. The force on one strip is $dF = \rho g y\,w\,dy$, and the resultant is the integral of $dF$ from the surface to the bottom of the gate, which gives $F = \dfrac{1}{2}\rho g w H^2 = 1.77 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{N}$.

The same result is read as the product of the area $wH$ and the pressure at mid-depth $\rho g \dfrac{H}{2}$, since the pressure grows linearly with the depth and its average value is the value at the middle.

The line of action is found by requiring that the resultant produce the same torque about the free surface as the distribution it replaces, hence $y_{\mathrm{cp}}F = \displaystyle\int_0^H y\,dF$. The computation gives $y_{\mathrm{cp}} = \dfrac{2}{3}H = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the resultant is applied **below** mid-depth, because the deeper strips carry the larger forces.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A tank with a square horizontal bottom of side $L = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ contains a layer of water of depth $h_2 = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$, with $\rho_2 = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, covered by a layer of oil of thickness $h_1 = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, with $\rho_1 = 8.20 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The two liquids do not mix and the upper surface is open to the atmosphere. Compute the gauge pressure at the oil-water interface and at the bottom, and the force exerted by the liquids on the bottom.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 4,0 4,3.5 0,3.5;0,3.5 4,3.5 4,5 0,5" linee="0,5.6 0,0 4,0 4,5.6;0,3.5 4,3.5" segmenti="4.5,3.5,4.5,5;4.5,0,4.5,3.5" punti="2,3.5,pieno,A;2,0.08,pieno,B" etichette="1.2,4.2,oil;1.1,1.7,water;4.65,4.2,0.15 m;4.65,1.7,0.35 m" xmin=-0.4 xmax=6.4 ymin=-0.5 ymax=6.1 titolo="Two liquids at rest: the gauge pressure at $A$ is $\rho_1 g h_1$, and from $A$ to $B$ it increases by $\rho_2 g h_2$"}

$$
p_A = \rho_1 g h_1,
\qquad
p_B = \rho_1 g h_1 + \rho_2 g h_2,
\qquad
F = p_B\,L^2
$$
$$
p_A = 8.20 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.15\,\mathrm{m} = 1.21 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
p_B = 1.207 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa} + 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.35\,\mathrm{m} = 4.64 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
F = 4.640 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa} \cdot (0.40\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 7.42 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The law of pressure with depth applies separately inside each liquid, since each of them is homogeneous and at rest. Descending through the oil from the free surface to the interface adds $\rho_1 g h_1 = 1.21 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ to the atmospheric pressure, which is the gauge pressure at $A$.

The two liquids are in contact at the interface, hence the pressure there has one value, and that value is the starting point for the descent through the water. Descending the further $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ adds $\rho_2 g h_2 = 3.43 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, and the gauge pressure at the bottom is the sum, $4.64 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$.

The bottom is horizontal, therefore every point of it lies at the same depth and the pressure over it is uniform; the force exerted by the liquids above the atmospheric contribution is $F = p_B L^2 = 7.42 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$. As a check, that force equals the weight of the liquid contained in the tank, $(\rho_1 h_1 + \rho_2 h_2)L^2 g = 7.42 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$, which holds here because the walls are vertical.

</div>
