## Exercise 1

Water flows steadily in a horizontal pipe of inner diameter $d_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ with speed $v_1 = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The pipe narrows to the diameter $d_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Compute the volume flow rate in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and in $\mathrm{L/s}$, the mass flow rate and the speed in the narrow section.

$$
A = \frac{\pi d^2}{4},
\qquad
Q = A_1 v_1,
\qquad
\dot{m} = \rho\,Q,
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{A_1}{A_2}\,v_1 = \left(\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^{2} v_1
$$
$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi\,(5.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^2}{4} = 1.963 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = 1.963 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2} \cdot 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.7 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 4.7\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
\dot{m} = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 4.712 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 4.7\,\mathrm{kg/s}
$$
$$
v_2 = \left(\frac{5.0}{3.0}\right)^{2} \cdot 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.78 \cdot 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.7\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **volume flow rate** is the product of the area of the section and the speed across it, hence $Q = 4.7 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, that is $4.7$ liters every second. Multiplying by the density gives the mass flow rate, $4.7\,\mathrm{kg/s}$, which for water is numerically equal to the flow rate in liters per second.

The flow is steady and water is incompressible, therefore the **continuity equation** applies and the same volume per unit time crosses every section of the pipe. The narrow section has an area smaller by the factor $\left(\dfrac{3.0}{5.0}\right)^2 = 0.36$, hence the speed there is larger by the reciprocal factor and reaches $6.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The speed depends on the square of the diameter, so reducing the diameter by a factor of $2$ would multiply the speed by $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A tap fills a bucket of $10\,\mathrm{L}$ in $25\,\mathrm{s}$ through an opening of diameter $d = 1.4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The stream leaves the tap vertically. Compute the flow rate, the speed at the opening, and the diameter of the stream after it has fallen $h = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, assuming that it stays compact.

$$
Q = \frac{V}{\Delta t},
\qquad
v_1 = \frac{Q}{A_1},
\qquad
v_2 = \sqrt{v_1^2 + 2gh},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{Q}{v_2},
\qquad
d_2 = \sqrt{\frac{4A_2}{\pi}}
$$
$$
Q = \frac{1.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}}{25\,\mathrm{s}} = 4.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi\,(1.4 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^2}{4} = 1.539 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
v_1 = \frac{4.0 \times 10^{-4}}{1.539 \times 10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.60\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2 = \sqrt{(2.598\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 + 2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.30\,\mathrm{m}} = 3.55\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A_2 = \frac{4.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}}{3.555\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 1.125 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
d_2 = \sqrt{\frac{4 \cdot 1.125 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}}{\pi}} = 1.20\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The flow rate is the volume delivered divided by the time, $4.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, and dividing it by the area of the opening gives the speed at which the water leaves, $2.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

Once in the air the water is in free fall, since the pressure is atmospheric all around the stream and viscosity with the air is neglected. The relation between speed and displacement for constant acceleration gives $v_2 = \sqrt{v_1^2 + 2gh} = 3.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after a fall of $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.

The stream carries the same flow rate at every height, therefore the continuity equation requires the area to decrease as the speed increases, and $A_2 = \dfrac{Q}{v_2}$ gives the diameter $1.20\,\mathrm{cm}$. This is why a stream of water **narrows** as it falls, until it breaks into drops when surface tension prevails.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A main pipe of diameter $D = 12\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries water at $v = 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and divides into three identical branches of diameter $d = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ each. Compute the flow rate in the main pipe and the speed in each branch.

$$
Q = \frac{\pi D^2}{4}\,v,
\qquad
Q = 3\,\frac{\pi d^2}{4}\,v_b
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_b = \frac{D^2}{3\,d^2}\,v
$$
$$
Q = \frac{\pi\,(0.12\,\mathrm{m})^2}{4} \cdot 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.131 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2} \cdot 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
v_b = \frac{(0.12\,\mathrm{m})^2}{3\,(0.050\,\mathrm{m})^2} \cdot 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s} = \frac{144}{75} \cdot 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The flow rate in the main pipe is $2.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, that is $20\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The flow is steady and the liquid is incompressible, therefore no volume accumulates at the junction and the flow rate entering equals the sum of the flow rates leaving.

The three branches are identical and carry equal flow rates, hence each of them carries $\dfrac{Q}{3} = 6.8 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, and dividing by the area of a branch gives $v_b = 3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The total area of the three branches is $58.9\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, smaller than the $113\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ of the main pipe, and for that reason the speed in a branch is **larger** than in the main pipe although each branch carries only a third of the water.

</div>
