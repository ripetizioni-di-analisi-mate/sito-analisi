## Exercise 1

A hydraulic lift has a small piston of diameter $d_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a large piston of diameter $d_2 = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$, both at the same height and closing a vessel filled with oil. A car of mass $M = 1.4 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on the large piston. Compute the force to be applied to the small piston, and the distance it must travel to raise the car by $s_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0.8,0 8,0 8,0.8 0.8,0.8;0.8,0.8 1.8,0.8 1.8,1.6 0.8,1.6;4.5,0.8 8,0.8 8,1.6 4.5,1.6" linee="0.8,3.2 0.8,0 8,0 8,3.2;1.8,3.2 1.8,0.8 4.5,0.8 4.5,3.2;0.8,1.6 1.8,1.6 1.8,1.85 0.8,1.85 0.8,1.6;4.5,1.6 8,1.6 8,1.85 4.5,1.85 4.5,1.6;5.4,1.85 7.1,1.85 7.1,2.8 5.4,2.8 5.4,1.85" vettori="1.25,3.3,1.25,2.0,F₁;4.9,2.0,4.9,3.3,F₂" etichette="0.2,1.72,A₁;8.2,1.72,A₂;5.75,2.3,load" xmin=0 xmax=9.4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=3.9 titolo="Hydraulic lift: the pressure is the same on the two pistons, hence $F_1 = \dfrac{A_1}{A_2}F_2 = \left(\dfrac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^2 F_2$"}

$$
\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
F_1 = \frac{A_1}{A_2}\,F_2 = \left(\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^{2} M g
$$
$$
A_1\,s_1 = A_2\,s_2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
s_1 = \frac{A_2}{A_1}\,s_2 = \left(\frac{d_2}{d_1}\right)^{2} s_2
$$
$$
F_1 = \left(\frac{3.0}{30}\right)^{2} \cdot 1.4 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = \frac{1.3734 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}}{100} = 1.4 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
s_1 = 100 \cdot 5.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The oil is enclosed and at rest, and the two pistons are at the same height, therefore the pressure under them has the same value. **Pascal's principle** then gives $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1} = \dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$, and since the areas are proportional to the squares of the diameters, the force is reduced by the factor $\left(\dfrac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{100}$. The weight of the car is $1.37 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}$, hence $1.4 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$ suffice.

The oil is incompressible, therefore the volume swept by one piston equals the volume swept by the other, $A_1 s_1 = A_2 s_2$, and the small piston travels $100$ times the distance, that is $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ for a lift of $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Multiplying the two relations gives $F_1 s_1 = F_2 s_2 = 6.9 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$: the machine delivers exactly the work supplied to it, and what it multiplies is the force alone. In practice the small piston is operated by a lever with a valve, so the stroke is repeated many times.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a hydraulic brake the driver presses the pedal with a force $F_1 = 90\,\mathrm{N}$ on a master cylinder of diameter $d_1 = 1.8\,\mathrm{cm}$. The brake fluid acts on a slave cylinder of diameter $d_2 = 4.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Compute the pressure in the fluid and the force applied to the brake pad.

$$
p = \frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{4F_1}{\pi d_1^2},
\qquad
F_2 = p\,A_2 = \left(\frac{d_2}{d_1}\right)^{2} F_1
$$
$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi\,(1.8 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^2}{4} = 2.545 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
p = \frac{90\,\mathrm{N}}{2.545 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}} = 3.54 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
F_2 = \left(\frac{4.5}{1.8}\right)^{2} \cdot 90\,\mathrm{N} = 6.25 \cdot 90\,\mathrm{N} = 5.6 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force on the pedal acts on the piston of the master cylinder and produces in the fluid the pressure $p = \dfrac{F_1}{A_1} = 3.54 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, about $3.5$ atmospheres. The two cylinders are at nearly the same height, hence the hydrostatic difference $\rho g \Delta h$ is negligible compared with that value and the pressure at the slave cylinder is the same.

The force delivered is the pressure multiplied by the area of the slave piston, and the ratio of the areas is the square of the ratio of the diameters, $6.25$. The pad is therefore pressed with $5.6 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$.

The fluid also transmits the pressure to the other wheels through pipes of any shape, since the transmission does not depend on the path, and this is what makes a hydraulic circuit preferable to a system of rods.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A hydraulic jack is filled with oil of density $\rho = 9.0 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The small piston has area $A_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and the large piston, which carries a load of mass $M = 5.0 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg}$, has area $A_2 = 250\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and stands $\Delta h = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ above the small one. Compute the force to be applied to the small piston, and compare it with the value obtained when the height difference is ignored.

$$
p_2 = \frac{M g}{A_2},
\qquad
p_1 = p_2 + \rho\,g\,\Delta h,
\qquad
F_1 = p_1\,A_1
$$
$$
p_2 = \frac{5.0 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{250 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}} = 1.962 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
p_1 = 1.962 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} + 9.0 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m} = 2.068 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
F_1 = 2.068 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} \cdot 5.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 1.03 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_1' = 1.962 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} \cdot 5.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The large piston supports the load, therefore the pressure of the oil under it is $p_2 = \dfrac{Mg}{A_2} = 1.96 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The two pistons are at different heights, hence the pressures under them differ by the hydrostatic term of the law of pressure with depth. The small piston is the lower one, so its pressure exceeds the other by $\rho g \Delta h = 1.06 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$.

The force required is $F_1 = p_1 A_1 = 1.03 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$, while neglecting the height difference would give $98.1\,\mathrm{N}$, an error of about $5\%$.

The correction grows with the height difference and with the density of the liquid, hence it matters in a tall jack filled with oil and is negligible in the brake circuit of the preceding exercise, where the two cylinders are nearly level.

</div>
