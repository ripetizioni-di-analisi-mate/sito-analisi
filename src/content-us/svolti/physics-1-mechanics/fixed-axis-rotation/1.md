## Exercise 1

A flywheel starts from rest and is brought to $1800\,\mathrm{rev/min}$ in $t = 12\,\mathrm{s}$ with constant angular acceleration. Find the angular acceleration and the number of revolutions made during the start-up.

$$
\omega = 1800\,\frac{\mathrm{rev}}{\mathrm{min}} = 1800 \cdot \frac{2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}}{60\,\mathrm{s}} = 188.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha\,t \\[4pt]
\theta = \omega_0\,t + \dfrac{1}{2}\,\alpha\,t^2
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\omega_0 = 0
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{\omega}{t} ,
\qquad
\theta = \frac{1}{2}\,\alpha\,t^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,\omega\,t
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{188.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}}{12\,\mathrm{s}} = 15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
$$
\theta = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 188.5\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{s} = 1131\,\mathrm{rad} = \frac{1131}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{rev} = 180\,\mathrm{rev}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The data are given in revolutions per minute and the equations of rotation require radians per second, therefore the angular velocity is converted first, with $1\,\mathrm{rev} = 2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$ and $1\,\mathrm{min} = 60\,\mathrm{s}$.

The angular acceleration is constant and the flywheel starts from rest, hence $\omega_0 = 0$ and the **equations of rotation with constant angular acceleration** apply. The first one gives $\alpha = 15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. Substituting $\alpha\,t = \omega$ into the second one leaves $\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}\omega t$, which is the angular displacement written as the average angular velocity times the time. The result is $1131\,\mathrm{rad}$, that is, **180 revolutions**. The same count follows from the average rate of $900\,\mathrm{rev/min}$ maintained for $0.20\,\mathrm{min}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A grinding wheel is turning at $\omega_0 = 24\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ when the motor is switched off. The friction of the bearings stops it after $9.0$ revolutions, with constant angular acceleration. Find the angular acceleration and the time the wheel takes to stop.

$$
\theta = 9.0\,\mathrm{rev} = 9.0 \cdot 2\pi\,\mathrm{rad} = 56.5\,\mathrm{rad}
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\,\alpha\,\theta
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\omega = 0
$$
$$
\alpha = -\,\frac{\omega_0^2}{2\,\theta} ,
\qquad
t = \frac{0 - \omega_0}{\alpha} = \frac{2\,\theta}{\omega_0}
$$
$$
\alpha = -\,\frac{(24\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2}{2 \cdot 56.55\,\mathrm{rad}} = -5.09\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
$$
t = \frac{2 \cdot 56.55\,\mathrm{rad}}{24\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 4.71\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{f="24-5.093*x" area="24-5.093*x;0;0;4.712" punti="0,24,pieno;4.712,0,pieno" xmin=-0.9 xmax=6.2 ymin=-3 ymax=28 nomi="t,ω" titolo="The angular velocity falls linearly from $24\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ to zero in $4.71\,\mathrm{s}$, and the shaded area is the angular displacement $\theta = 56.5\,\mathrm{rad}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The time is not given and the angular displacement is, therefore the **third equation of rotation** is the one to use, since it connects $\omega$, $\omega_0$ and $\theta$ without the time. The wheel stops, hence the final angular velocity is zero and the angular acceleration is $-5.09\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. The sign is negative because the angular acceleration opposes the rotation.

The time then follows from the first equation. Substituting the value of $\alpha$ gives $t = \dfrac{2\theta}{\omega_0} = 4.71\,\mathrm{s}$, a formula that states the angular displacement as the average angular velocity $\dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$ times the time. The graph of $\omega(t)$ is a straight line, and the area of the triangle below it, $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 24\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 4.71\,\mathrm{s} = 56.5\,\mathrm{rad}$, reproduces the given angular displacement.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wheel has angular position $\theta(t) = 2.0 + 3.0\,t - 0.50\,t^3$, with $\theta$ in radians and $t$ in seconds. Find the angular velocity and the angular acceleration as functions of the time, the instant at which the wheel reverses its sense of rotation, and the angular displacement between $t = 0$ and $t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\omega(t) = \frac{d\theta}{dt} = 3.0 - 1.5\,t^2 ,
\qquad
\alpha(t) = \frac{d\omega}{dt} = -3.0\,t
$$
$$
\omega(t_1) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{3.0}{1.5}} = \sqrt{2} = 1.41\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\theta(0) = 2.0\,\mathrm{rad} ,
\qquad
\theta(3.0) = 2.0 + 9.0 - 0.50 \cdot 27 = -2.5\,\mathrm{rad}
$$
$$
\Delta\theta = \theta(3.0) - \theta(0) = -4.5\,\mathrm{rad}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angular position is given as a function of the time, therefore the angular velocity and the angular acceleration are obtained by differentiating once and twice. The angular acceleration $\alpha = -3.0\,t$ is **not constant**, hence the equations of rotation with constant angular acceleration do not apply here and every quantity must be read from the derivatives.

The wheel reverses when its angular velocity vanishes and changes sign. Solving $3.0 - 1.5\,t^2 = 0$ gives $t_1 = 1.41\,\mathrm{s}$ as the **instant of the reversal**; for $t < t_1$ the angular velocity is positive and the wheel turns counterclockwise, for $t > t_1$ it is negative and the wheel turns clockwise.

The angular displacement between two instants is the difference of the two angular positions, and it is $\Delta\theta = -4.5\,\mathrm{rad}$. The value is negative because the clockwise turn after $t_1$ exceeds the counterclockwise turn before it. The angle actually swept is larger, since the wheel advances from $2.0\,\mathrm{rad}$ to $\theta(t_1) = 4.83\,\mathrm{rad}$ and then goes back to $-2.5\,\mathrm{rad}$, for a total swept angle of $2.83 + 7.33 = 10.2\,\mathrm{rad}$.

</div>
