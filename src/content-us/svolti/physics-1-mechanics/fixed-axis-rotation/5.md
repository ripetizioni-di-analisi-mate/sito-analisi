## Exercise 1

A cord is wound around a pulley of mass $M = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$, which turns on a frictionless axle and is treated as a uniform disk. A body of mass $m = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the free end of the cord. The cord is massless and inextensible and does not slip on the rim. Find the acceleration of the body, the angular acceleration of the pulley and the tension of the cord.

$$
\begin{cases}
m g - T = m\,a \\[4pt]
T R = I\,\alpha \\[4pt]
a = \alpha\,R
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
I = \frac{1}{2}\,M R^2
$$
$$
m g - T = m\,a ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{2}\,M\,a
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
a = \frac{m g}{m + \dfrac{M}{2}} ,
\qquad
T = m\,(g - a)
$$
$$
a = \frac{3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{3.0\,\mathrm{kg} + 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{29.43\,\mathrm{N}}{4.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 7.36\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{a}{R} = \frac{7.3575\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{0.12\,\mathrm{m}} = 61.3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} ,
\qquad
T = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 7.3575)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 7.36\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="3+0.6*cos(t),4.6+0.6*sin(t),0,6.29" linee="2.1,5.7 3.9,5.7;3,5.7 3,4.6;3.6,4.6 3.6,2.5" poligoni="3.2,1.9 4,1.9 4,2.5 3.2,2.5" punti="3,4.6,pieno" vettori="3.6,2.5,3.6,3.45,T;3.6,1.9,3.6,0.75,mg" segmenti="3,4.6,3.6,4.6" etichette="3.1,4.85,R;2.8,2.15,m;2.25,4.5,M" xmin=0.4 xmax=6.4 ymin=0.2 ymax=6.2 titolo="The cord pulls the body upwards with the force $T$ and pulls the rim of the pulley with the same $T$ at lever arm $R$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two equations are needed, one for the translation of the body and one for the rotation of the pulley, and a third relation links them.

On the body act its weight, downwards, and the tension of the cord, upwards. Taking downwards as positive, Newton's second law gives $mg - T = ma$.

On the pulley act its weight, the force of the axle and the tension of the cord. The weight and the force of the axle have their lines of action through the axis, hence they give no torque, and the only torque is that of the tension, which is tangent to the rim and therefore has lever arm $R$. **Newton's second law for rotation** gives $TR = I\alpha$, with $I = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$ from the table.

The cord is inextensible and does not slip, therefore the speed of the body equals the speed of a point of the rim, and differentiating that equality gives $a = \alpha R$. Substituting $\alpha = \dfrac{a}{R}$ into the rotational equation leaves $T = \dfrac{1}{2}Ma$, which is the force needed to spin up the pulley.

Eliminating $T$ gives $a = 7.36\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, a value smaller than $g$ because the cord must also set the pulley into rotation. The tension is $7.36\,\mathrm{N}$, smaller than the weight $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$, consistently with a body accelerating downwards.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $m_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lies on a frictionless horizontal table and is connected by a massless inextensible cord to a body of mass $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hanging from the edge. The cord passes over a pulley of mass $M = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$, treated as a uniform disk on a frictionless axle, and does not slip. Find the acceleration of the bodies and the tension on each side of the pulley.

$$
\begin{cases}
T_1 = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
m_2 g - T_2 = m_2\,a \\[4pt]
(T_2 - T_1)\,R = \dfrac{1}{2}\,M R^2\,\dfrac{a}{R}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = \frac{m_2\,g}{m_1 + m_2 + \dfrac{M}{2}} ,
\qquad
T_1 = m_1\,a ,
\qquad
T_2 = m_2\,(g - a)
$$
$$
a = \frac{3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{4.0 + 3.0 + 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{29.43\,\mathrm{N}}{7.5\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.924\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 15.7\,\mathrm{N} ,
\qquad
T_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 3.924)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 17.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pulley has mass, therefore the two sides of the cord carry different tensions, and their difference is what supplies the torque that spins the pulley up.

The table is frictionless, so the only horizontal force on $m_1$ is $T_1$, and Newton's second law gives $T_1 = m_1 a$. On $m_2$ act its weight and $T_2$, which gives $m_2 g - T_2 = m_2 a$. On the pulley the torque is $(T_2 - T_1)R$, since each tension is tangent to the rim, and **Newton's second law for rotation** gives $(T_2 - T_1)R = I\alpha$ with $I = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{a}{R}$.

Adding the three equations written in terms of $a$ removes both tensions and gives $a = 3.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The denominator contains the term $\dfrac{M}{2} = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, which is the mass the pulley adds to the system as far as the acceleration is concerned.

The tensions are then $T_1 = 15.7\,\mathrm{N}$ and $T_2 = 17.7\,\mathrm{N}$. Their difference, $1.96\,\mathrm{N}$, acting at $R = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$, gives the torque $0.157\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, which equals $I\alpha = 0.0032\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot 49.1\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ as expected.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform rod of mass $M = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ turns about a horizontal frictionless axis through one end. The rod is held horizontal and released from rest. Find the angular acceleration at the instant of release and the acceleration of the free end and of the center of mass at that instant.

$$
I = \frac{1}{3}\,M L^2 ,
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{net}} = M g\,\frac{L}{2} ,
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{\tau_{\mathrm{net}}}{I} = \frac{3\,g}{2\,L}
$$
$$
I = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (1.0\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.667\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} ,
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{net}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{m} = 9.81\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{3 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{2 \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 14.7\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
$$
a_{\mathrm{end}} = \alpha\,L = 14.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
\qquad
a_{\mathrm{cm}} = \alpha\,\frac{L}{2} = 7.36\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is at one end, therefore the moment of inertia is $\dfrac{1}{3}ML^2$ from the table. The force exerted by the axis has its line of action through the axis and gives no torque, so the only torque is that of the weight, which acts at the center of mass of the uniform rod, at $\dfrac{L}{2}$ from the axis. The rod is horizontal at the instant of release, hence the weight is perpendicular to the rod and its lever arm is exactly $\dfrac{L}{2}$.

**Newton's second law for rotation** gives $\alpha = \dfrac{3g}{2L} = 14.7\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. The mass cancels, so the angular acceleration depends on the length alone.

The tangential acceleration of a point of the rod is $\alpha r$, and the rod starts from rest, so the radial component vanishes at that instant and the tangential component is the whole acceleration. The free end has $a_{\mathrm{end}} = \alpha L = 14.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, a value **larger than $g$**: a coin resting on the free end loses contact immediately, because the rod falls away faster than the coin does. The center of mass has half that value, $7.36\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, which is smaller than $g$ because the axis holds part of the weight.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A flywheel is a uniform disk of mass $M = 50\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ turning at $300\,\mathrm{rev/min}$. A brake presses on the rim and applies a tangential friction force that brings the flywheel to rest in $8.0\,\mathrm{s}$ with constant angular acceleration. Find the friction force and the number of revolutions made during the braking.

$$
\omega_0 = 300 \cdot \frac{2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}}{60\,\mathrm{s}} = 31.42\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
I = \frac{1}{2}\,M R^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.40\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
|\alpha| = \frac{\omega_0}{t} ,
\qquad
F\,R = I\,|\alpha|
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
F = \frac{I\,\omega_0}{R\,t} ,
\qquad
\theta = \frac{1}{2}\,\omega_0\,t
$$
$$
|\alpha| = \frac{31.42\,\mathrm{rad/s}}{8.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 3.93\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
$$
F = \frac{4.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot 3.927\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}}{0.40\,\mathrm{m}} = 39.3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\theta = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 31.42\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{s} = 125.7\,\mathrm{rad} = 20\,\mathrm{rev}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angular acceleration is constant and the flywheel stops, therefore $|\alpha| = \dfrac{\omega_0}{t} = 3.93\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, where $\omega_0$ has been converted into radians per second first.

The friction force acts tangentially at the rim, hence its lever arm is $R$ and its torque is $FR$, opposed to the rotation. The weight of the flywheel and the force of the axle give no torque about the axis, so **Newton's second law for rotation** gives $FR = I|\alpha|$ and $F = 39.3\,\mathrm{N}$.

The angular displacement follows from the equations of rotation with constant angular acceleration, since the average angular velocity is $\dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$, hence $\theta = 125.7\,\mathrm{rad}$, that is, **20 revolutions**. The torque needed is $15.7\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, which a modest force supplies because it acts at the rim.

</div>
