## Exercise 1

Three small bodies of masses $m_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_3 = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ are fixed to a rod of negligible mass at the positions $x_1 = 0$, $x_2 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x_3 = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the moment of inertia about the axis perpendicular to the rod through $x = 0$, and then about the parallel axis through the center of mass.

$$
I_O = \sum_i m_i\,x_i^2
$$
$$
I_O = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.40\,\mathrm{m})^2 + 1.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (1.0\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.48 + 1.50 = 1.98\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
M = 6.5\,\mathrm{kg} ,
\qquad
x_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{\sum_i m_i x_i}{M} = \frac{1.20 + 1.50}{6.5}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.415\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
I_O = I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M\,x_{\mathrm{cm}}^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = I_O - M\,x_{\mathrm{cm}}^2
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = 1.98\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} - 6.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.4154\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 1.98 - 1.12 = 0.858\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bodies are small compared with their distances from the axis, therefore each of them is treated as a particle and the **moment of inertia** is the sum $\sum_i m_i x_i^2$. The body at $x_1 = 0$ lies on the axis, hence its distance is zero and it contributes nothing, although it does contribute to the total mass.

The second axis passes through the center of mass and is parallel to the first one, so the **parallel-axis theorem** applies with $d = x_{\mathrm{cm}}$. The theorem is used in the reverse direction here, since the known quantity is the moment of inertia about the displaced axis, and the one requested is $I_{\mathrm{cm}} = I_O - M d^2 = 0.858\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$.

The direct sum confirms the value, since $2.0 \cdot (0.4154)^2 + 3.0 \cdot (0.0154)^2 + 1.5 \cdot (0.5846)^2 = 0.858\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. The result is smaller than $I_O$, as the parallel-axis theorem requires, because among all parallel axes the one through the center of mass gives the smallest moment of inertia.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform rod of mass $M = 2.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ rotates about an axis perpendicular to the rod at $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from one end. Find its moment of inertia about that axis.

$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{12}\,M L^2 ,
\qquad
d = \frac{L}{2} - 0.30\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
I = I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M\,d^2
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{12} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (1.20\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.300\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
d = 0.60\,\mathrm{m} - 0.30\,\mathrm{m} = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
I = 0.300\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} + 2.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.30\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.300 + 0.225 = 0.525\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is neither through the center nor through one end, therefore the value cannot be read from the table directly and the **parallel-axis theorem** supplies it. The rod is uniform, hence its center of mass is at its midpoint, $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ from each end, and the distance between the two parallel axes is $d = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.

The table gives $I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \dfrac{1}{12}ML^2 = 0.300\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, and the theorem adds the term $M d^2 = 0.225\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, which gives $I = 0.525\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. The value lies between $\dfrac{1}{12}ML^2 = 0.300\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ for the axis through the center and $\dfrac{1}{3}ML^2 = 1.20\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ for the axis through one end, consistently with the position of the axis between those two points.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform rod of mass $m = 1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a small body of mass $M_s = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ at each end. Find the moment of inertia about the axis perpendicular to the rod through its center, and about the parallel axis through one end.

$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{12}\,m L^2 + 2\,M_s \left(\frac{L}{2}\right)^2
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{12} \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.80\,\mathrm{m})^2 + 2 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.40\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.064 + 0.160 = 0.224\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{end}} = \frac{1}{3}\,m L^2 + M_s \cdot 0 + M_s\,L^2
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{end}} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 0.64\,\mathrm{m^2} + 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 0.64\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.256 + 0.320 = 0.576\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The moment of inertia is a sum over the particles, therefore the moment of inertia of a body made of several parts about one axis is the sum of the moments of inertia of the parts about that same axis. The rod contributes the value from the table, and each small body contributes $M_s r^2$ with $r$ its own distance from the axis.

About the central axis both small bodies are at $\dfrac{L}{2} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, hence they contribute $0.160\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ together, more than twice the contribution of the rod, which carries more than twice their mass. Mass far from the axis weighs much more, since the distance enters squared.

About the axis through one end the rod contributes $\dfrac{1}{3}mL^2$, the body on the axis contributes nothing and the body at the far end contributes $M_s L^2$, which gives $I_{\mathrm{end}} = 0.576\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. The **parallel-axis theorem** confirms the value, since the total mass is $2.2\,\mathrm{kg}$, the center of mass is at the midpoint by symmetry and $0.224 + 2.2 \cdot (0.40)^2 = 0.576\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A uniform disk of radius $R = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and mass $6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ has a circular hole of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ drilled through it, centred at the distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center of the disk. Find the moment of inertia of the drilled disk about the axis through the center of the original disk, perpendicular to its face.

$$
I = I_{\mathrm{full}} - I_{\mathrm{hole}}
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{full}} = \frac{1}{2}\,M_{\mathrm{full}}\,R^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.30\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.270\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
m_{\mathrm{hole}} = M_{\mathrm{full}}\,\frac{\pi \left(\dfrac{R}{2}\right)^2}{\pi R^2} = \frac{6.0\,\mathrm{kg}}{4} = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{hole}} = \frac{1}{2}\,m_{\mathrm{hole}} \left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^2 + m_{\mathrm{hole}} \left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{3}{2}\,m_{\mathrm{hole}} \left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^2
$$
$$
I_{\mathrm{hole}} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.15\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.0506\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I = 0.270\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} - 0.0506\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.219\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="3*cos(t),3*sin(t),0,6.29;1.5+1.5*cos(t),1.5*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno;1.5,0,pieno" segmenti="0,0,1.5,0;0,0,-3,0" etichette="-0.2,-0.55,O;1.6,-0.55,C;0.7,0.25,d;-1.9,0.3,R" xmin=-3.8 xmax=4.0 ymin=-3.5 ymax=3.5 titolo="The hole of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ is centred at $C$, at distance $d = \dfrac{R}{2}$ from the axis through $O$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The drilled disk is the full disk with the material of the hole removed, and the moment of inertia is additive, therefore the moment of inertia of the drilled disk about an axis is the moment of inertia of the full disk minus the moment of inertia of the removed material about that same axis.

The disk is uniform, hence the mass of the removed material is proportional to its area; the hole has half the radius, so one quarter of the area, and $m_{\mathrm{hole}} = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$.

The removed material is itself a uniform disk, and its own center is at $d = \dfrac{R}{2}$ from the axis, so the **parallel-axis theorem** gives its moment of inertia about the axis through $O$ as $\dfrac{1}{2}m_{\mathrm{hole}}\left(\dfrac{R}{2}\right)^2$ about its own center, plus $m_{\mathrm{hole}}\,d^2$ for the displacement.

The difference is $I = 0.219\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, while the remaining mass is $4.5\,\mathrm{kg}$. A full disk of that mass and radius $R$ would have $0.203\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, hence the drilled disk has the larger moment of inertia, because the material has been removed from the region close to the axis.

</div>
