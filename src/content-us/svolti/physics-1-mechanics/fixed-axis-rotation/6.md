## Exercise 1

A flywheel with moment of inertia $I = 12\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ turns at $240\,\mathrm{rev/min}$. Find its kinetic energy and the time for which it can drive a machine that absorbs $500\,\mathrm{W}$, if the flywheel is allowed to come to rest.

$$
\omega = 240 \cdot \frac{2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}}{60\,\mathrm{s}} = 25.13\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}\,I\,\omega^2 ,
\qquad
t = \frac{K}{P}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (25.13\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 3.79 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
t = \frac{3790\,\mathrm{J}}{500\,\mathrm{W}} = 7.58\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rate is converted into radians per second, since the formula for the **kinetic energy of a rotating body** requires $\omega$ in those units.

The kinetic energy is $K = \dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2 = 3.79\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The flywheel is the energy store of the machine, and the whole of this energy is available while the flywheel slows down to rest.

The machine absorbs $500\,\mathrm{W}$, that is, $500\,\mathrm{J}$ each second, therefore the stored energy lasts $t = 7.58\,\mathrm{s}$. The kinetic energy depends on the square of the angular velocity, hence doubling the rate of the same flywheel would store four times the energy and extend the interval to $30.3\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from a cord wound around a pulley of mass $M = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, treated as a uniform disk on a frictionless axle. The body is released from rest and descends $h = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the speed of the body and the angular velocity of the pulley at that moment.

$$
m g h = \frac{1}{2}\,m v^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,I\,\omega^2 ,
\qquad
I = \frac{1}{2}\,M R^2 ,
\qquad
\omega = \frac{v}{R}
$$
$$
m g h = \frac{1}{2}\,v^2 \left(m + \frac{M}{2}\right)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\,m g h}{m + \dfrac{M}{2}}}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m}}{3.5\,\mathrm{kg}}} = \sqrt{16.82\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 4.10\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{4.101\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.15\,\mathrm{m}} = 27.3\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axle is frictionless and the cord does not slip, therefore no energy is dissipated and the **mechanical energy** of the system is conserved. The potential energy lost by the body as it descends is $mgh$, and it reappears as kinetic energy of the body and of the pulley.

The pulley turns about a fixed axis, hence its kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$ with $I = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$. The cord does not slip, so the speed of the body equals the speed of a point of the rim and $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R}$. Substituting both relations leaves one equation in $v$ alone, in which the radius cancels.

The result is $v = 4.10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, against the $5.42\,\mathrm{m/s}$ of a free fall from the same height, because part of the energy is stored in the rotation of the pulley. The angular velocity follows from $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R} = 27.3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform rod of mass $M = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ turns about a horizontal frictionless axis through one end. It is held horizontal and released from rest. Find the angular velocity when the rod passes through the vertical position and the speed of the free end at that instant.

$$
M g\,\frac{L}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\,I\,\omega^2 ,
\qquad
I = \frac{1}{3}\,M L^2
$$
$$
M g\,\frac{L}{2} = \frac{1}{6}\,M L^2 \omega^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{3\,g}{L}} ,
\qquad
v = \omega\,L = \sqrt{3\,g\,L}
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{3 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{0.60\,\mathrm{m}}} = \sqrt{49.05\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}} = 7.00\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v = 7.004\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{m} = 4.20\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is frictionless and the force it exerts is applied at a point that does not move, therefore that force does no work and the mechanical energy is conserved.

The rod is uniform, so its weight acts at the center of mass, which descends by $\dfrac{L}{2}$ while the rod turns from the horizontal to the vertical position. The potential energy lost is $Mg\dfrac{L}{2}$, and the kinetic energy gained is $\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$ with $I = \dfrac{1}{3}ML^2$, since the axis passes through one end.

The mass cancels, therefore the angular velocity at the vertical **does not depend on the mass** of the rod and is $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{3g}{L}} = 7.00\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The free end is at distance $L$ from the axis, hence its speed is $v = \omega L = 4.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A body dropped from the height $\dfrac{L}{2}$ would arrive at $2.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the end of the rod is faster, because the part of the rod near the axis gives up potential energy while taking up little kinetic energy.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A uniform disk of mass $M = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ hangs from a horizontal frictionless axis through a point of its rim, perpendicular to the face of the disk. The disk is held with its center at the same height as the axis and released from rest. Find the angular velocity when the center is directly below the axis and the speed of the center at that instant.

$$
I = \frac{1}{2}\,M R^2 + M R^2 = \frac{3}{2}\,M R^2
$$
$$
M g R = \frac{1}{2}\,I\,\omega^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{2\,M g R}{I}} = \sqrt{\frac{4\,g}{3\,R}}
$$
$$
I = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.20\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.120\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{4 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{3 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{m}}} = \sqrt{65.40\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}} = 8.09\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \omega\,R = 8.087\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{m} = 1.62\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is a point of the rim, at distance $R$ from the center of mass, therefore the **parallel-axis theorem** applies. The moment of inertia about the central axis is $\dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$ from the table and the displacement adds $MR^2$, hence $I = \dfrac{3}{2}MR^2 = 0.120\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$.

The axis is frictionless and the force it exerts does no work, hence the mechanical energy is conserved. The center of mass starts at the height of the axis and ends directly below it, so it descends by exactly $R$ and the potential energy lost is $MgR$.

Equating that loss to $\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$ and substituting $I$ gives $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{4g}{3R}} = 8.09\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, in which the mass cancels. The center of mass is at distance $R$ from the axis, therefore its speed is $v_{\mathrm{cm}} = \omega R = 1.62\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>
