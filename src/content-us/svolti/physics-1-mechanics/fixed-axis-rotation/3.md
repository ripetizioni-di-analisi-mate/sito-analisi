## Exercise 1

A wrench of length $r = 0.28\,\mathrm{m}$ is pivoted on a bolt. A force of magnitude $F = 85\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied at the free end of the handle, making an angle $\varphi = 60^\circ$ with the handle itself. Find the lever arm of the force and the torque about the axis of the bolt.

$$
\ell = r\sin\varphi ,
\qquad
\tau = F\,\ell = r\,F\sin\varphi
$$
$$
\ell = 0.28\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 0.28\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.8660 = 0.242\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\tau = 0.28\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 85\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.8660 = 20.6\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,-0.12 2.8,-0.12 2.8,0.12 0,0.12" vettori="2.8,0,3.65,1.472,F" linee="2.1,-1.2124 4.2,2.4249" segmenti="0,0,2.1,-1.2124" punti="0,0,pieno;2.8,0,pieno" etichette="-0.35,0.3,O;2.72,0.38,A;0.95,-0.95,ℓ;3.1,0.42,60°" xmin=-0.8 xmax=4.8 ymin=-2.0 ymax=2.9 titolo="The lever arm $\ell$ is the distance from the axis $O$ to the line of action of $\vec{F}$, and $\tau = F\ell = 20.6\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The force is applied at distance $r$ from the axis and makes the angle $\varphi$ with the handle, which is the line joining the axis to the point of application. The **lever arm** is the perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of action of the force, hence $\ell = r\sin\varphi = 0.242\,\mathrm{m}$, a length smaller than the handle.

The torque is the product of the magnitude of the force and the lever arm, therefore $\tau = 20.6\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The same force applied perpendicular to the handle would have $\ell = r$ and would give $23.8\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, the largest torque available at that distance, while a force directed along the handle would have $\ell = 0$ and would give no torque at all.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A stepped pulley turns about a fixed horizontal axis. A cord wound on the rim, of radius $R = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$, is pulled tangentially with a force $T_1 = 30\,\mathrm{N}$ that tends to turn the pulley clockwise. A second cord wound on the hub, of radius $r = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, is pulled tangentially with a force $T_2 = 55\,\mathrm{N}$ that tends to turn it counterclockwise. Find the net torque about the axis.

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{net}} = T_2\,r - T_1\,R
$$
$$
\tau_{\mathrm{net}} = 55\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{m} - 30\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{m} = 5.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} - 7.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = -2.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each cord is tangent to the circle on which it is wound, therefore its lever arm is the radius of that circle and its torque is the product of the tension and the radius. The counterclockwise sense is taken as positive, so the torque of $T_2$ is $+5.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ and the torque of $T_1$ is $-7.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

The **net torque** is the algebraic sum of the two, $\tau_{\mathrm{net}} = -2.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, and the negative sign states that the pulley is driven **clockwise**. The larger force produces the smaller torque, because it acts at a lever arm two and a half times shorter. The force exerted by the axle on the pulley has its line of action through the axis, hence it adds nothing to the net torque.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform beam of mass $M = 24\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is hinged to a wall at one end and held horizontal. A load of mass $m = 15\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the beam at $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge, and a cable attached at the far end pulls with tension $T = 420\,\mathrm{N}$ at $40^\circ$ above the beam. Find the net torque about the hinge and the sense in which the beam starts to turn.

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{net}} = T\sin 40^\circ \cdot L - m g\,d - M g\,\frac{L}{2}
$$
$$
T\sin 40^\circ \cdot L = 420\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.6428 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m} = 810\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
m g\,d = 15\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m} = 294\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
M g\,\frac{L}{2} = 24\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m} = 353\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\tau_{\mathrm{net}} = 810\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} - 294\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} - 353\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = +162\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is the hinge and the counterclockwise sense is taken as positive, with the beam along the positive $x$ direction, so that a force pulling the far end upwards gives a positive torque.

The tension is resolved along the beam and perpendicular to it. The component along the beam has its line of action through the hinge, hence it gives no torque, while the perpendicular component $T\sin 40^\circ = 270\,\mathrm{N}$ acts at lever arm $L$ and gives $+810\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

The load hangs at $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge and its weight is vertical, therefore its lever arm is that distance and its torque is $-294\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The beam is uniform, so its weight is the single force $M\vec{g}$ applied at the center of mass, which lies at $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge, and its torque is $-353\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

The sum is $+162\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, therefore the net torque is **counterclockwise** and the cable lifts the beam. A tension of $336\,\mathrm{N}$ would make the net torque vanish and leave the beam in equilibrium, the situation studied in the chapter on static equilibrium and elasticity.

</div>
