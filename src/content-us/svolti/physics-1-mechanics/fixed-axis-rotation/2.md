## Exercise 1

A wheel of radius $R = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ turns at a constant rate of $2.0$ revolutions per second. Find the speed of a point of the rim, its centripetal acceleration and the distance that point travels in $10\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\omega = 2.0\,\frac{\mathrm{rev}}{\mathrm{s}} = 2.0 \cdot 2\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v = \omega\,R ,
\qquad
a_r = \omega^2 R ,
\qquad
s = v\,t
$$
$$
v = 12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.35\,\mathrm{m} = 4.40\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a_r = (12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 \cdot 0.35\,\mathrm{m} = 55.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
s = 4.398\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{s} = 44.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rate is given in revolutions per second and the formulas require radians per second, therefore $\omega = 2\pi \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

The point of the rim is a point of the body at distance $R$ from the axis, hence its speed is $v = \omega R = 4.40\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The rate is constant, so the angular acceleration vanishes and the tangential component of the acceleration vanishes with it; the whole acceleration is the **centripetal** one, $a_r = \omega^2 R = 55.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed towards the axis. Its magnitude is more than five times $g$, which is why a fast wheel must be balanced.

The speed is constant in magnitude, therefore the arc travelled in $10\,\mathrm{s}$ is $s = v\,t = 44.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The same value follows from $s = R\,\theta$ with $\theta = \omega t = 125.7\,\mathrm{rad}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A belt that does not slip connects a driving pulley of radius $r_1 = 8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, turning at $1200\,\mathrm{rev/min}$, to a driven pulley of radius $r_2 = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the speed of the belt and the rate of the driven pulley in revolutions per minute.

$$
\omega_1 = 1200 \cdot \frac{2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}}{60\,\mathrm{s}} = 125.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
v = \omega_1 r_1 = \omega_2 r_2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\omega_2 = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\,\omega_1
$$
$$
v = 125.7\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.080\,\mathrm{m} = 10.1\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\omega_2 = \frac{0.080\,\mathrm{m}}{0.20\,\mathrm{m}} \cdot 125.7\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 50.3\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 480\,\mathrm{rev/min}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="0.8*cos(t),0.8*sin(t),0,6.29;6+2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" linee="-0.16,0.7838 5.6,1.9596;-0.16,-0.7838 5.6,-1.9596" punti="0,0,pieno;6,0,pieno" segmenti="0,0,0,-0.8;6,0,6,-2" etichette="0.12,-0.5,r₁;6.15,-1.2,r₂;2.6,1.9,belt" xmin=-1.6 xmax=8.8 ymin=-2.9 ymax=2.9 titolo="The belt does not slip, hence the rim of each pulley moves at the belt speed and $\omega_1 r_1 = \omega_2 r_2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The belt does not slip on either pulley, therefore each point of the belt moves at the speed of the rim it is in contact with. The rim speed of a pulley is $\omega r$ by the formula for the **speed of a point of the body**, hence the two rim speeds are equal and $\omega_1 r_1 = \omega_2 r_2$.

The belt speed is computed from the driving pulley, $v = \omega_1 r_1 = 10.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the rate of the driven pulley follows from $\omega_2 = \dfrac{v}{r_2} = 50.3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The ratio of the angular velocities is the inverse of the ratio of the radii, so the larger pulley turns more slowly, and $1200\,\mathrm{rev/min} \cdot \dfrac{8.0}{20} = 480\,\mathrm{rev/min}$. The conversion back to revolutions per minute uses the same factor as before, since the ratio is a pure number.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A turntable of radius $R = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ starts from rest and is spun up with constant angular acceleration $\alpha = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. For a point of the rim, find the tangential and the radial components of the acceleration at $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, the magnitude of the acceleration and the angle it makes with the radius.

$$
\omega = \alpha\,t ,
\qquad
a_t = \alpha\,R ,
\qquad
a_r = \omega^2 R ,
\qquad
a = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_r^2} ,
\qquad
\tan\beta = \frac{a_t}{a_r}
$$
$$
\omega = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
a_t = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} \cdot 0.15\,\mathrm{m} = 0.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
\qquad
a_r = (6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 \cdot 0.15\,\mathrm{m} = 5.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a = \sqrt{(0.45)^2 + (5.40)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 5.42\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\beta = \arctan\frac{0.45}{5.40} = 4.8^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angular acceleration is constant and the turntable starts from rest, therefore $\omega = \alpha t = 6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ at the instant considered.

The acceleration of a point of a rotating body has two perpendicular components. The **tangential component** $a_t = \alpha R$ depends on the angular acceleration alone, hence it keeps the value $0.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at every instant of the spin-up. The **radial component** $a_r = \omega^2 R$ depends on the angular velocity, hence it grows as the turntable speeds up, and at $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ it already exceeds the tangential component by a factor of twelve.

The two components are perpendicular, so the magnitude of the acceleration is obtained by the Pythagorean theorem and the angle with the radius by the arctangent of their ratio. The acceleration points almost towards the axis, $\beta = 4.8^\circ$, and the later the instant, the smaller this angle becomes.

</div>
