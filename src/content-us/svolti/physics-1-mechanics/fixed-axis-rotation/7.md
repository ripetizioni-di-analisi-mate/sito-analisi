## Exercise 1

A motor applies a constant torque $\tau = 45\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ to a shaft that turns $30$ revolutions in $12\,\mathrm{s}$ at constant angular velocity against a load. Find the work done by the motor and the power it delivers.

$$
\Delta\theta = 30\,\mathrm{rev} = 30 \cdot 2\pi\,\mathrm{rad} = 188.5\,\mathrm{rad}
$$
$$
W = \tau\,\Delta\theta ,
\qquad
\omega = \frac{\Delta\theta}{t} ,
\qquad
P = \tau\,\omega
$$
$$
W = 45\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \cdot 188.5\,\mathrm{rad} = 8.48 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{188.5\,\mathrm{rad}}{12\,\mathrm{s}} = 15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
P = 45\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \cdot 15.71\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 707\,\mathrm{W}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque is constant, therefore the integral that defines the work reduces to the product $W = \tau\,\Delta\theta$, with the angular displacement expressed in radians. The $30$ revolutions amount to $188.5\,\mathrm{rad}$, hence $W = 8.48\,\mathrm{kJ}$.

The angular velocity is constant, so it equals the angular displacement divided by the time, $15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and the **power delivered by a torque** is $P = \tau\omega = 707\,\mathrm{W}$. The same value follows from the work divided by the time, $\dfrac{8482\,\mathrm{J}}{12\,\mathrm{s}} = 707\,\mathrm{W}$.

The angular velocity is constant, therefore the kinetic energy of the shaft does not change, and by the **work-energy theorem for rotation** the net torque is zero, since the load exerts on the shaft a torque of $45\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ opposed to the motion, and the work of the motor is dissipated in the load.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electric motor is rated at $2.2\,\mathrm{kW}$ and runs at $1500\,\mathrm{rev/min}$. Find the torque it delivers on its shaft, and the torque it would deliver at $750\,\mathrm{rev/min}$ at the same power.

$$
\omega = 1500 \cdot \frac{2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}}{60\,\mathrm{s}} = 157.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
P = \tau\,\omega
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\tau = \frac{P}{\omega}
$$
$$
\tau = \frac{2200\,\mathrm{W}}{157.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 14.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\tau' = \frac{2200\,\mathrm{W}}{78.54\,\mathrm{rad/s}} = 28.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rated power is the work the motor delivers per unit time, and the **power delivered by a torque** is $P = \tau\omega$, therefore the torque is the power divided by the angular velocity, once the rate has been converted into radians per second.

At $1500\,\mathrm{rev/min}$ the torque is $14.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. At half the angular velocity the same power requires twice the torque, $28.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, since the product $\tau\omega$ is fixed. A gearbox exploits this relation, lowering the angular velocity delivered to the load and raising the torque in the same ratio.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A grindstone is a uniform disk of mass $M = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$, free to turn on a frictionless axle and initially at rest. A constant torque $\tau = 6.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ is applied for $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the angular velocity reached, the work done by the torque and the power delivered at the final instant.

$$
I = \frac{1}{2}\,M R^2 ,
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{\tau}{I} ,
\qquad
\omega = \alpha\,t ,
\qquad
\theta = \frac{1}{2}\,\alpha\,t^2 ,
\qquad
W = \tau\,\theta ,
\qquad
P = \tau\,\omega
$$
$$
I = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.25\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.250\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} ,
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{6.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{0.250\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = 24.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
$$
\omega = 24.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{s} = 120\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
\theta = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 24.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} \cdot (5.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 300\,\mathrm{rad}
$$
$$
W = 6.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \cdot 300\,\mathrm{rad} = 1800\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
P = 6.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \cdot 120\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 720\,\mathrm{W}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque is constant and the axle is frictionless, so the net torque is constant and **Newton's second law for rotation** gives the constant angular acceleration $\alpha = \dfrac{\tau}{I} = 24.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$.

The equations of rotation with constant angular acceleration then give $\omega = 120\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\theta = 300\,\mathrm{rad}$ at $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and the work of the constant torque is $W = \tau\theta = 1800\,\mathrm{J}$.

The grindstone starts from rest, therefore the **work-energy theorem for rotation** requires the work to equal the final kinetic energy, and indeed $\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.250\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} \cdot (120\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 = 1800\,\mathrm{J}$.

The power is $P = \tau\omega$, hence it grows with the angular velocity even though the torque is constant, so it is zero at the start and $720\,\mathrm{W}$ at $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Its average over the interval is $\dfrac{1800\,\mathrm{J}}{5.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 360\,\mathrm{W}$, half the final value, as the linear growth of $\omega$ requires.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A wheel with moment of inertia $I = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ turns on a frictionless axle, initially at rest, under the torque $\tau(\theta) = 12 - 2.0\,\theta$, with $\tau$ in $\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ and $\theta$ in radians. Find the work done between $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad}$, the angular velocity reached there, and the largest angular velocity the wheel attains.

$$
W = \int_0^{\theta_1} \tau(\theta)\,d\theta = \int_0^{3.0} (12 - 2.0\,\theta)\,d\theta = \left[12\,\theta - \theta^2\right]_0^{3.0}
$$
$$
W = 36 - 9.0 = 27\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W = \frac{1}{2}\,I\,\omega^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{2\,W}{I}} = \sqrt{\frac{54\,\mathrm{J}}{0.80\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}}} = 8.22\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
\tau(\theta_{\max}) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta_{\max} = 6.0\,\mathrm{rad} ,
\qquad
W_{\max} = \left[12\,\theta - \theta^2\right]_0^{6.0} = 36\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\omega_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{72\,\mathrm{J}}{0.80\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}}} = 9.49\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$

::grafico{f="12-2*x" area="12-2*x;0;0;3" punti="0,12,pieno;6,0,pieno" xmin=-0.9 xmax=7.4 ymin=-3 ymax=14 nomi="θ,τ" titolo="The work done between $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad}$ is the shaded area under the graph of $\tau(\theta)$, and the torque vanishes at $\theta = 6.0\,\mathrm{rad}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque depends on the angular position, therefore the work is the integral $\displaystyle\int \tau\,d\theta$ and not a product. The integrand is a polynomial of the first degree, so the integral is immediate and gives $W = 27\,\mathrm{J}$ over the first $3.0\,\mathrm{rad}$. The same value is the area of the trapezoid under the graph, with parallel sides $12\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ and $6.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ and width $3.0\,\mathrm{rad}$.

The axle is frictionless and the wheel starts from rest, hence the **work-energy theorem for rotation** gives $W = \dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$ and $\omega = 8.22\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

The torque stays positive and the wheel keeps speeding up as long as $\theta < 6.0\,\mathrm{rad}$; beyond that point the torque becomes negative and the wheel slows down. The angular velocity is therefore largest at $\theta_{\max} = 6.0\,\mathrm{rad}$, where the accumulated work is $36\,\mathrm{J}$ and $\omega_{\max} = 9.49\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

</div>
