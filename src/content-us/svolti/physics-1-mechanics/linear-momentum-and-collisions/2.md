## Exercise 1

A railroad car of mass $m_1 = 12\,000\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves at $v_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a straight horizontal track and couples with a car of mass $m_2 = 8\,000\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest. Friction and rolling resistance are negligible during the coupling. Determine the velocity of the pair after the coupling and the kinetic energy lost.

$$
m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2)\,V
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V = \frac{m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$
$$
V = \frac{12\,000\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s} + 0}{20\,000\,\mathrm{kg}} = 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 12\,000\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (3.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 5.40 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)V^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 20\,000\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (1.8\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 3.24 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
|\Delta K| = 5.40 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J} - 3.24 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J} = 2.16 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system formed by the two cars has no horizontal external force during the coupling, since the weights and the normal forces are vertical and balance each other, therefore the total momentum along the track is conserved. The cars move together afterwards, hence they share one final velocity and conservation of momentum alone determines it.

The final velocity is $1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is smaller than the initial one because the same momentum is now carried by a larger mass. The ratio $\dfrac{V}{v_1} = \dfrac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2} = \dfrac{3}{5}$ holds whenever the target is at rest.

The kinetic energy drops from $5.40 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}$ to $3.24 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}$, so $2.16 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}$ are **lost**, that is, $40\%$ of the initial energy. That energy deformed the couplers and warmed them, and the lost fraction equals $\dfrac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$ when the target is initially at rest.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A truck of mass $m_1 = 1\,500\,\mathrm{kg}$ travelling east at $v_1 = 20.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ collides head-on with a car of mass $m_2 = 1\,000\,\mathrm{kg}$ travelling west at $15.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the two vehicles remain locked together. Determine their common velocity immediately after the collision and the fraction of the kinetic energy lost.

$$
V = \frac{m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2}{m_1 + m_2}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
v_1 = +20.0\,\mathrm{m/s},
\quad
v_2 = -15.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
V = \frac{1\,500\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 20.0\,\mathrm{m/s} - 1\,000\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 15.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2\,500\,\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{1.50 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}}{2\,500\,\mathrm{kg}} = +6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1\,500 \cdot 20.0^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1\,000 \cdot 15.0^2 = 4.125 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\,500\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (6.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 4.50 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\frac{|\Delta K|}{K_i} = \frac{4.125 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J} - 4.50 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}}{4.125 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}} = 0.891
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The east direction is taken as positive, therefore the velocity of the car enters with a negative sign. The two momenta subtract and the total momentum of the system is $1.50 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ towards the east, smaller in magnitude than the momentum of the truck alone.

Conservation of momentum gives $V = +6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence the wreck moves **east**, in the sense of the heavier and faster vehicle, at less than a third of the speed of the truck.

The collision is perfectly inelastic, so kinetic energy is not conserved, and the calculation shows that $89.1\%$ of it is lost. The relative velocity of approach, $35.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, is what the formula $|\Delta K| = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\dfrac{m_1m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,(v_1 - v_2)^2$ raises to the second power, and this is the reason why a head-on collision destroys far more energy than a rear-end collision at the same speeds.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two bodies of modelling clay slide in the same sense along a horizontal frictionless rail: the first has mass $m_1 = 0.400\,\mathrm{kg}$ and velocity $v_1 = 2.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the second has mass $m_2 = 0.600\,\mathrm{kg}$ and velocity $v_2 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and moves ahead of the first. The first reaches the second and the two stick together. Determine the final velocity and verify the loss of kinetic energy with the formula that uses the relative velocity.

$$
V = \frac{m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{0.400 \cdot 2.50 + 0.600 \cdot 1.50}{1.000}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.90\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.400 \cdot 2.50^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.600 \cdot 1.50^2 = 1.250\,\mathrm{J} + 0.675\,\mathrm{J} = 1.925\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.000\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (1.90\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 1.805\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
|\Delta K| = 1.925\,\mathrm{J} - 1.805\,\mathrm{J} = 0.120\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
|\Delta K| = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,(v_1 - v_2)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.240\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (1.00\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 0.120\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both velocities are positive here, since the two bodies move in the same sense, therefore the total momentum is the sum of two positive terms and the final velocity $1.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$ lies between the two initial ones. A common velocity outside that range would contradict conservation of momentum.

The direct computation of the two kinetic energies gives a loss of $0.120\,\mathrm{J}$. The formula proved for the perfectly inelastic collision gives the same value from the reduced mass $\dfrac{m_1m_2}{m_1 + m_2} = 0.240\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the relative velocity $v_1 - v_2 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which confirms the result.

The loss depends on the relative velocity alone, hence it is small here although the two bodies are fast: they approach each other slowly. Two bodies moving with the same velocity that meet lose nothing, because there is no deformation.

</div>
