## Exercise 1

A skater of mass $M = 65\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands at rest on ice of negligible friction and throws a ball of mass $m = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ horizontally at $v = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with respect to the ice. Determine the velocity of the skater after the throw.

$$
0 = m\,v + M\,V
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V = -\frac{m}{M}\,v
$$
$$
V = -\frac{4.0\,\mathrm{kg}}{65\,\mathrm{kg}} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = -0.49\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is formed by the skater and the ball. The forces exchanged during the throw are internal, the weights are balanced by the normal forces of the ice and friction is negligible, therefore the horizontal component of the total momentum is conserved.

The system is initially at rest, hence that component is zero before the throw and remains zero after it. The two momenta must then be opposite, which fixes the sense of the motion of the skater.

The skater **recoils** at $0.49\,\mathrm{m/s}$, a speed smaller than that of the ball in the ratio of the masses. The total momentum is zero while the total kinetic energy is not, since the energy came from the muscles of the skater, and an internal source of energy changes $K$ without changing $\vec{P}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rifle of mass $M = 4.5\,\mathrm{kg}$, free to move horizontally, fires a bullet of mass $m = 12\,\mathrm{g}$ with muzzle velocity $v = 650\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine the recoil velocity of the rifle and the ratio of the kinetic energy of the bullet to that of the rifle.

$$
V = -\frac{m}{M}\,v = -\frac{0.012\,\mathrm{kg}}{4.5\,\mathrm{kg}} \cdot 650\,\mathrm{m/s} = -1.73\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
K_b = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.012\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (650\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 2.54 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_r = \frac{1}{2}MV^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (1.733\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 6.76\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\frac{K_b}{K_r} = \frac{\dfrac{p^2}{2m}}{\dfrac{p^2}{2M}} = \frac{M}{m} = \frac{4.5\,\mathrm{kg}}{0.012\,\mathrm{kg}} = 375
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Conservation of the horizontal momentum gives the recoil velocity $1.73\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the sense opposite to the bullet, by the same argument as in the previous exercise.

The two kinetic energies are computed from the two speeds and are far from equal. The relation $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$ gives the reason, because the two bodies carry momenta of equal magnitude, therefore their kinetic energies are inversely proportional to their masses.

The bullet takes $375$ times the energy of the rifle, that is, $99.7\%$ of the total. This is a general property of recoil, and it is what makes the energy of a rifle manageable for the shoulder of the person firing it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body of mass $M = 2.00\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest on a horizontal frictionless plane explodes into two fragments. The first, of mass $m_1 = 0.500\,\mathrm{kg}$, moves along the plane at $v_1 = 6.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine the velocity of the second fragment and the energy released by the explosion.

$$
0 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
m_2 = M - m_1 = 1.50\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
v_2 = -\frac{m_1}{m_2}\,v_1 = -\frac{0.500\,\mathrm{kg}}{1.50\,\mathrm{kg}} \cdot 6.00\,\mathrm{m/s} = -2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
E = K_f - K_i = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2 - 0
$$
$$
E = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.500\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (6.00\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (2.00\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 9.00\,\mathrm{J} + 3.00\,\mathrm{J} = 12.0\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces of the explosion are internal to the body, and the external forces are vertical and balanced, therefore the horizontal momentum is conserved although the interaction is violent and unknown in detail.

The initial momentum is zero, so the two fragments leave with opposite momenta of equal magnitude. The second fragment is three times heavier than the first, hence its speed is one third, that is, $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the opposite sense.

The kinetic energy grows from zero to $12.0\,\mathrm{J}$, and that is the **energy released** by the chemical reaction of the explosive. An explosion and a perfectly inelastic collision are opposite in this respect, since the total momentum is conserved in both, whereas the kinetic energy increases in the first and decreases in the second.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A body of mass $M = 0.400\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides at $v = 12.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a horizontal frictionless plane and bursts into two equal halves. One half is found at rest immediately after the burst. Determine the velocity of the other half and the energy released.

$$
M\,v = \frac{M}{2}\,u_1 + \frac{M}{2}\,u_2
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
u_1 = 0
$$
$$
u_2 = 2v = 24.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
E = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)u_2^2 - \frac{1}{2}Mv^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.200\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (24.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 - \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.400\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (12.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2
$$
$$
E = 57.6\,\mathrm{J} - 28.8\,\mathrm{J} = 28.8\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is isolated along the direction of motion, therefore the momentum $Mv = 4.80\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ before the burst equals the total momentum after it. One half is at rest and carries no momentum, hence the whole of it belongs to the other half.

That half has one half of the total mass, so its velocity is twice the initial one, $24.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the same sense. The result does not depend on how the burst took place, since only the initial and the final states enter the balance.

The kinetic energy rises from $28.8\,\mathrm{J}$ to $57.6\,\mathrm{J}$, therefore the burst released $28.8\,\mathrm{J}$. The energy released happens to equal the initial kinetic energy here, which is a consequence of the chosen data and not a general rule.

</div>
