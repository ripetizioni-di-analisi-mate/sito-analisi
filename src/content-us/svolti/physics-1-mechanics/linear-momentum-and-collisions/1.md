## Exercise 1

A softball of mass $m = 0.190\,\mathrm{kg}$ arrives horizontally at $v_1 = 30.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The bat sends it back along the same line at $v_2 = 42.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the contact lasting $\Delta t = 1.50\,\mathrm{ms}$. Determine the impulse delivered to the ball and the average force exerted by the bat, and compare that force with the weight of the ball.

$$
J_x = \Delta p_x = m\,(v_{2x} - v_{1x}),
\qquad
F_{\mathrm{av},x} = \frac{J_x}{\Delta t}
$$
$$
J_x = 0.190\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left(-42.0\,\mathrm{m/s} - 30.0\,\mathrm{m/s}\right) = -13.7\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}
$$
$$
F_{\mathrm{av},x} = \frac{-13.68\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}}{1.50 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}} = -9.12 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\frac{|F_{\mathrm{av}}|}{mg} = \frac{9.12 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}}{0.190\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = \frac{9.12 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}}{1.86\,\mathrm{N}} \approx 4.9 \cdot 10^{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $x$ axis is taken along the initial velocity of the ball, therefore $v_{1x} = +30.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_{2x} = -42.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, since the ball leaves in the opposite sense. The two velocities enter the calculation with opposite signs, hence the change of momentum has magnitude $m(v_1 + v_2)$ and not $m(v_2 - v_1)$.

The force of the bat varies in an unknown way during the contact, so the **impulse-momentum theorem** is applied instead of Newton's second law at one instant, since the impulse of the resultant force equals the change of momentum, which is read off the two velocities alone. Dividing that impulse by the contact time gives the **average force**, whose sign states that it points opposite to the initial velocity.

The average contact force exceeds the weight of the ball by a factor of about $4.9 \cdot 10^{3}$, therefore the impulse of the weight during the contact is negligible and the computed value is the average force of the bat.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ball of mass $m = 0.250\,\mathrm{kg}$ is released from rest at the height $h_1 = 1.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above a horizontal floor and rebounds to the height $h_2 = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$. The contact with the floor lasts $\Delta t = 15.0\,\mathrm{ms}$. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the coefficient of restitution and the average normal force exerted by the floor during the contact.

$$
v_1 = \sqrt{2gh_1},
\qquad
v_2 = \sqrt{2gh_2},
\qquad
e = \frac{v_2}{v_1} = \sqrt{\frac{h_2}{h_1}}
$$
$$
v_1 = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.60\,\mathrm{m}} = 5.60\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.90\,\mathrm{m}} = 4.20\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
e = \sqrt{\frac{0.90\,\mathrm{m}}{1.60\,\mathrm{m}}} = 0.750
$$
$$
\left(N_{\mathrm{av}} - mg\right)\Delta t = m\,(v_2 + v_1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_{\mathrm{av}} = \frac{m\,(v_1 + v_2)}{\Delta t} + mg
$$
$$
N_{\mathrm{av}} = \frac{0.250\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (5.60 + 4.20)\,\mathrm{m/s}}{15.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}} + 2.45\,\mathrm{N} = 163\,\mathrm{N} + 2.45\,\mathrm{N} = 166\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fall and the rebound take place under the weight alone, therefore mechanical energy is conserved in both of them and the speeds at the floor follow from $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgh$. The ratio of those speeds is the **coefficient of restitution**, which equals $0.750$ and states that the ball leaves the floor at three quarters of the speed with which it arrived.

During the contact the upward axis is chosen, so the velocity changes from $-v_1$ to $+v_2$ and the change of momentum is $m(v_1 + v_2) = 2.45\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ upward. Two forces act in that interval, the normal force of the floor and the weight, hence the impulse-momentum theorem reads $(N_{\mathrm{av}} - mg)\Delta t = m(v_1 + v_2)$.

Solving for the normal force gives $166\,\mathrm{N}$. The weight contributes $2.45\,\mathrm{N}$ of this total, that is, about $1.5\%$, because the contact is short; for a contact ten times longer the same change of momentum would require a proportionally smaller force and the weight would weigh more in the balance.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cart of mass $m = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest on a horizontal frictionless track. A horizontal force grows linearly from zero to $F_{\max} = 12.0\,\mathrm{N}$ in $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and then decreases linearly to zero in the following $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Determine the impulse of the force, the final speed of the cart and the average force.

$$
J = \int_0^{5.0\,\mathrm{s}} F\,dt = \frac{1}{2}\,F_{\max}\,\Delta t
$$
$$
J = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 12.0\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{s} = 30\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}
$$
$$
v = \frac{J}{m} = \frac{30\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}}{6.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
F_{\mathrm{av}} = \frac{J}{\Delta t} = \frac{30\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}}{5.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 6.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{poligoni="0,0 3,12 5,0" asintoti="y=6" nomi="t,F" etichette="5.2,6.5,F av" xmin=-0.4 xmax=6.2 ymin=-1.6 ymax=14.5 titolo="Force on the cart against time, in $\mathrm{N}$ and $\mathrm{s}$: the area of the triangle is the impulse $J = 30\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ and the dashed line is the average force $6.0\,\mathrm{N}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The force is known as a function of time, therefore its impulse is the integral of the force over the interval, which is the area between the graph and the time axis. That area is a triangle of base $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $12.0\,\mathrm{N}$, hence $J = 30\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ follows from the area of a triangle, without computing the integral.

The track is frictionless and horizontal, so the weight and the normal force balance and the applied force is the resultant. The cart starts from rest, therefore the impulse-momentum theorem gives $J = mv - 0$ and the final speed is $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The **average force** is the constant force that would produce the same impulse in the same time, that is, $6.0\,\mathrm{N}$. The rectangle of height $6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ over the interval of $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ has the same area as the triangle, which is what the dashed line in the figure shows.

</div>
