## Exercise 1

A car of mass $m_1 = 1\,200\,\mathrm{kg}$ travelling east at $v_1 = 25.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ collides at an intersection with a van of mass $m_2 = 1\,800\,\mathrm{kg}$ travelling north at $v_2 = 14.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The two vehicles remain locked together. Determine the velocity of the wreck immediately after the collision.

$$
\begin{cases}
m_1 v_1 = (m_1 + m_2)\,V_x \\[4pt]
m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2)\,V_y
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_x = \frac{m_1v_1}{m_1 + m_2},
\qquad
V_y = \frac{m_2v_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$
$$
P_x = 1\,200\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 25.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.00 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},
\qquad
P_y = 1\,800\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 14.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.52 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}
$$
$$
V_x = \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{4}}{3\,000}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 10.0\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
V_y = \frac{2.52 \cdot 10^{4}}{3\,000}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 8.40\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
V = \sqrt{V_x^2 + V_y^2} = \sqrt{(10.0)^2 + (8.40)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 13.1\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\theta = \arctan\frac{V_y}{V_x} = \arctan 0.840 = 40.0^\circ \ \text{north of east}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 nomi="x,y" vettori="0,0,3,0,p₁;0,0,0,2.52,p₂;0,0,3,2.52,P" segmenti="3,0,3,2.52;0,2.52,3,2.52" etichette="0.75,0.28,40.0°" xmin=-0.5 xmax=3.9 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.2 titolo="The momenta before the collision, in units of $10^4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$: their sum $\vec{P}$ makes the angle $40.0^\circ$ with the east direction, and the wreck leaves along $\vec{P}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The axes are taken with $x$ towards the east and $y$ towards the north. Each vehicle moves along one axis, therefore the car contributes only to $P_x$ and the van only to $P_y$.

During the collision the large forces are those exchanged by the vehicles, which are internal, while friction on the road acts over a very short time and delivers a negligible impulse, hence both components of the total momentum are conserved. The two bodies move together afterwards, so one common velocity carries the whole momentum and the two scalar equations determine its components.

The magnitude of the final velocity is $13.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and its direction is $40.0^\circ$ north of east. That direction is the direction of the total momentum before the collision, which the figure shows as the diagonal of the rectangle built on the two momenta; this is how the police reconstruct the speeds of two vehicles from the skid marks of the wreck.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $m_1 = 0.200\,\mathrm{kg}$ moving at $v_1 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the $x$ axis strikes a body of mass $m_2 = 0.300\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest. After the collision the first body moves at $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $30.0^\circ$ above the $x$ axis. Determine the velocity of the second body and the kinetic energy lost.

$$
\begin{cases}
m_1 v_1 = m_1 v_1'\cos 30.0^\circ + m_2 v_{2x}' \\[4pt]
0 = m_1 v_1'\sin 30.0^\circ + m_2 v_{2y}'
\end{cases}
$$
$$
v_{2x}' = \frac{m_1\left(v_1 - v_1'\cos 30.0^\circ\right)}{m_2} = \frac{0.200\,(3.00 - 1.299)}{0.300}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.134\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_{2y}' = -\frac{m_1 v_1'\sin 30.0^\circ}{m_2} = -\frac{0.200 \cdot 0.750}{0.300}\,\mathrm{m/s} = -0.500\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2' = \sqrt{(1.134)^2 + (0.500)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.24\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
\varphi = \arctan\frac{-0.500}{1.134} = -23.8^\circ
$$
$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.200 \cdot 3.00^2 = 0.900\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
K_f = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.200 \cdot 1.50^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.300 \cdot 1.24^2 = 0.455\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
|\Delta K| = 0.900\,\mathrm{J} - 0.455\,\mathrm{J} = 0.445\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $x$ axis is taken along the initial velocity of the projectile, therefore the initial momentum has no $y$ component and the second equation states that the two final $y$ momenta are opposite.

The first body leaves above the $x$ axis, hence the second leaves below it, and the sign of $v_{2y}'$ confirms this. Resolving the known final velocity of the projectile into components and substituting into the two equations gives the components of the unknown velocity directly, without any auxiliary angle.

The magnitude is $1.24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the direction is $23.8^\circ$ below the $x$ axis. The total kinetic energy falls from $0.900\,\mathrm{J}$ to $0.455\,\mathrm{J}$, so this collision is **inelastic** and $49.4\%$ of the energy is lost. Nothing in the data required the collision to be elastic: the two final directions were data of the problem, and the energy balance is a result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A puck of mass $m = 0.170\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides at $v = 4.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a horizontal frictionless surface and collides elastically with an identical puck at rest. After the collision the first puck moves at $35.0^\circ$ from its original direction. Determine the speeds of the two pucks after the collision and the direction of the second.

$$
\vec{v}_1\,' \cdot \vec{v}_2\,' = 0
\qquad\text{(equal masses, elastic collision, target at rest)}
$$
$$
v_1' = v\cos 35.0^\circ = 4.00\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.819 = 3.28\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2' = v\sin 35.0^\circ = 4.00\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.574 = 2.29\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\theta_2 = -\left(90.0^\circ - 35.0^\circ\right) = -55.0^\circ
$$
$$
v_1'^2 + v_2'^2 = (3.277)^2 + (2.294)^2 = 16.0\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = v^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two masses are equal and the collision is elastic with the target at rest, therefore the theorem on the right angle applies and the two final velocities are perpendicular. The second puck leaves at $55.0^\circ$ on the other side of the original direction, since the two angles add to $90.0^\circ$.

With the directions known, the momentum triangle is a right triangle whose hypotenuse is $\vec{v}_1$, hence the two final speeds are the projections $v\cos 35.0^\circ$ and $v\sin 35.0^\circ$, that is, $3.28\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $2.29\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The last line checks the result: the sum of the squares of the final speeds equals the square of the initial speed, which is conservation of kinetic energy for equal masses. The masses cancel in every step, so the answer does not depend on the value $0.170\,\mathrm{kg}$, which only fixes the energies.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A body of mass $M = 3.00\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest on a horizontal frictionless plane explodes into three fragments of equal mass $1.00\,\mathrm{kg}$. The first moves at $4.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the $x$ axis and the second at $3.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the $y$ axis. Determine the velocity of the third fragment and the energy released.

$$
\vec{p}_1 + \vec{p}_2 + \vec{p}_3 = \vec{0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{p}_3 = -\left(\vec{p}_1 + \vec{p}_2\right)
$$
$$
p_{3x} = -1.00\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 4.00\,\mathrm{m/s} = -4.00\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},
\qquad
p_{3y} = -1.00\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.00\,\mathrm{m/s} = -3.00\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}
$$
$$
v_3 = \frac{\sqrt{(4.00)^2 + (3.00)^2}}{1.00}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.00\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\theta_3 = 180^\circ + \arctan\frac{3.00}{4.00} = 180^\circ + 36.9^\circ = 216.9^\circ
$$
$$
E = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.00 \cdot 4.00^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.00 \cdot 3.00^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.00 \cdot 5.00^2 = 8.00 + 4.50 + 12.5 = 25.0\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The explosion is internal and the plane is horizontal and frictionless, therefore the total momentum stays zero and the three momenta add to the zero vector. The third momentum is then the opposite of the sum of the other two, and it is computed component by component.

Both components of $\vec{p}_3$ are negative, hence the third fragment moves into the third quadrant, at $5.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the direction $216.9^\circ$ from the positive $x$ axis. The arctangent alone would return $36.9^\circ$, which is the direction of the opposite vector, so $180^\circ$ is added.

The energy released is the whole final kinetic energy, since the body started at rest, and it amounts to $25.0\,\mathrm{J}$. The third fragment carries the largest share of it, because it must balance the momenta of the other two.

</div>
