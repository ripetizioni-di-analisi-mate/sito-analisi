## Exercise 1

A bullet of mass $m = 9.0\,\mathrm{g}$ is fired horizontally into a block of mass $M = 2.500\,\mathrm{kg}$ hanging at rest from two long vertical cords, and remains embedded in it. The block swings and its center of mass rises by $h = 12.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the speed of the bullet before the impact and the fraction of the kinetic energy lost in the impact.

$$
m\,v = (m + M)\,V
\qquad\text{(collision)}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}(m + M)V^2 = (m + M)\,g\,h
\qquad\text{(swing)}
$$
$$
V = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.120\,\mathrm{m}} = 1.534\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v = \frac{m + M}{m}\,V = \frac{2.509\,\mathrm{kg}}{0.0090\,\mathrm{kg}} \cdot 1.534\,\mathrm{m/s} = 428\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{|\Delta K|}{K_i} = 1 - \frac{m}{m + M} = 1 - \frac{0.0090}{2.509} = 0.9964
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1.2,0 3.2,0;1,0 1,-3" poligoni="0.7,-3.3 1.3,-3.3 1.3,-2.7 0.7,-2.7;2.63,-2.6 3.23,-2.6 3.23,-2.0 2.63,-2.0" segmenti="1,0,2.93,-2.3;1,-3,3.85,-3;2.93,-2.3,3.85,-2.3" vettori="-0.45,-3,0.55,-3,v;3.6,-3,3.6,-2.3,h" etichette="1.5,-3.15,M;-0.5,-2.75,m" xmin=-1.3 xmax=4.4 ymin=-4 ymax=0.6 titolo="The bullet is stopped inside the block, and the block with the bullet rises by $h$; the collision and the swing are two separate stages"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem has two stages that obey different laws, and separating them is what makes it solvable.

During the impact the bullet and the block exchange very large forces over a very short time, therefore the horizontal momentum of the pair is conserved while its kinetic energy is not, since the bullet is stopped by friction inside the wood. The first equation gives the common velocity $V$ immediately after the impact, when the cords are still vertical.

During the swing the tension of the cords is perpendicular to the motion and does no work, and the only other force is the weight, which is conservative, therefore **mechanical energy is conserved** and the kinetic energy at the lowest point equals the potential energy at the highest point. From the rise $h = 12.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ it follows that $V = 1.534\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

Inverting the first equation gives $v = 428\,\mathrm{m/s}$, a result obtained from a measurement of a length alone. The impact destroys $99.64\%$ of the kinetic energy, which is why the energy equation cannot be used across the collision.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m_1 = 0.500\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides at $v_1 = 4.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a horizontal frictionless surface and hits a block of mass $m_2 = 1.500\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest, attached to a spring of stiffness $k = 800\,\mathrm{N/m}$ whose other end is fixed to a wall. The two blocks stick together. Determine the maximum compression of the spring.

$$
m_1 v_1 = (m_1 + m_2)\,V
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V = \frac{0.500\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 4.00\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2.000\,\mathrm{kg}} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)V^2 = \frac{1}{2}k\,x^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = V\sqrt{\frac{m_1 + m_2}{k}}
$$
$$
x = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2.000\,\mathrm{kg}}{800\,\mathrm{N/m}}} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.0500\,\mathrm{s} = 5.00 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The collision is perfectly inelastic, so conservation of momentum alone gives the common velocity $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ of the two blocks immediately after the impact. The spring has no time to compress appreciably during the impact, therefore it exerts no appreciable impulse and does not enter this first stage.

After the impact the surface is frictionless and the spring force is conservative, hence mechanical energy is conserved from that instant onwards. At maximum compression the two blocks are momentarily at rest, so the whole kinetic energy $1.00\,\mathrm{J}$ has become elastic potential energy.

Solving for the compression gives $x = 5.00\,\mathrm{cm}$. Using the initial energy $4.00\,\mathrm{J}$ instead of $1.00\,\mathrm{J}$ would give twice that compression, and it would be wrong, because three quarters of the energy were dissipated in the impact.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A bullet of mass $m = 12\,\mathrm{g}$ moving horizontally at $v = 380\,\mathrm{m/s}$ embeds itself in a block of mass $M = 3.000\,\mathrm{kg}$ resting on a horizontal surface, and the block slides until it stops. The coefficient of kinetic friction between block and surface is $\mu_k = 0.25$. Determine the distance traveled by the block.

$$
m\,v = (m + M)\,V
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V = \frac{0.012\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 380\,\mathrm{m/s}}{3.012\,\mathrm{kg}} = 1.514\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}(m + M)V^2 = \mu_k\,(m + M)\,g\,d
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = \frac{V^2}{2\mu_k g}
$$
$$
d = \frac{(1.514\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = \frac{2.292\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{4.905\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 0.467\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The impact lasts a very short time, therefore the impulse of friction during the impact is negligible and the momentum of the pair is conserved. The common velocity immediately after the impact is $1.514\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

In the second stage the block with the bullet slides with friction. The surface is horizontal, so the normal force equals the weight of the pair and the friction force is $\mu_k(m + M)g$, constant in magnitude and opposite to the motion. The **work-energy theorem** applied between the start and the stop gives $-\mu_k(m + M)gd = 0 - \dfrac{1}{2}(m + M)V^2$.

The total mass cancels, hence the distance depends only on the velocity after the impact and on the coefficient of friction, and it equals $0.467\,\mathrm{m}$. A measurement of that distance determines the speed of the bullet, exactly as the rise of a ballistic pendulum does.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Two spheres hang from cords of the same length $L = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ attached to the same ceiling and touch each other at rest. The first, of mass $m_1 = 0.200\,\mathrm{kg}$, is raised until its cord is horizontal and released; it strikes the second, of mass $m_2 = 0.300\,\mathrm{kg}$, elastically. Air resistance is negligible. Determine the velocities just after the collision and the maximum height reached by each sphere.

$$
\frac{1}{2}m_1v^2 = m_1gL
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{2gL} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.20\,\mathrm{m}} = 4.852\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v = \frac{-0.100}{0.500} \cdot 4.852\,\mathrm{m/s} = -0.970\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2' = \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,v = \frac{0.400}{0.500} \cdot 4.852\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.882\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
h_1 = \frac{v_1'^2}{2g} = \frac{(0.970\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 4.80\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
h_2 = \frac{v_2'^2}{2g} = \frac{(3.882\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 76.8\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem has three stages. In the descent the tension does no work and the weight is conservative, therefore mechanical energy is conserved and the first sphere arrives at the lowest point with $v = \sqrt{2gL} = 4.852\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the cord having fallen through the height $L$.

The collision is elastic and takes place along the horizontal line of the two centers, hence the formulas for the one-dimensional elastic collision with the target at rest apply. The projectile is the lighter body, so it **rebounds** at $0.970\,\mathrm{m/s}$ while the target leaves at $3.882\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

In the two rises mechanical energy is conserved again, each sphere reaching the height $\dfrac{v'^2}{2g}$. The first returns to $4.80\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the second to $76.8\,\mathrm{cm}$. The energy check is satisfied, since the kinetic energy is $2.354\,\mathrm{J}$ before the collision and $0.094\,\mathrm{J} + 2.260\,\mathrm{J} = 2.354\,\mathrm{J}$ after it.

</div>
