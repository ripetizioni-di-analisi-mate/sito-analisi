## Exercise 1

Three particles lie in the $xy$ plane: $m_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $(0, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$, $m_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $(5.0, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $m_3 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $(2.0, 4.0)\,\mathrm{m}$. Determine the position of the center of mass.

$$
x_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{\sum_i m_ix_i}{\sum_i m_i},
\qquad
y_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{\sum_i m_iy_i}{\sum_i m_i}
$$
$$
M = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} + 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} + 5.0\,\mathrm{kg} = 10.0\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
x_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 0 + 2.0 \cdot 5.0 + 5.0 \cdot 2.0}{10.0}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{20.0}{10.0}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 0 + 2.0 \cdot 0 + 5.0 \cdot 4.0}{10.0}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{20.0}{10.0}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The center of mass is the average of the positions weighted by the masses, therefore each coordinate is computed separately with the same denominator, the total mass $10.0\,\mathrm{kg}$.

The result $(2.0,\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{m}$ lies inside the triangle formed by the three particles, and it is pulled toward $m_3$, which is the heaviest. A change of origin would change both coordinates, while the point itself stays where it is, since the definition is a vector relation.

The point is not occupied by any particle, hence the center of mass is a geometric point attached to the system and not a part of it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform rectangular plate measures $60\,\mathrm{cm}$ by $40\,\mathrm{cm}$. A square of side $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ is cut away from one corner. Taking the origin at the opposite corner of the plate, with the $x$ axis along the long side, determine the position of the center of mass of the remaining piece.

$$
x_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{M_{\mathrm{tot}}\,x_{\mathrm{tot}} - M_{\mathrm{cut}}\,x_{\mathrm{cut}}}{M_{\mathrm{tot}} - M_{\mathrm{cut}}}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
M \propto A \ \text{(uniform plate)}
$$
$$
A_{\mathrm{tot}} = 60 \cdot 40 = 2400\,\mathrm{cm^2},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{cut}} = 20 \cdot 20 = 400\,\mathrm{cm^2},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{tot}} - A_{\mathrm{cut}} = 2000\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$
$$
(x_{\mathrm{tot}},\ y_{\mathrm{tot}}) = (30,\ 20)\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
(x_{\mathrm{cut}},\ y_{\mathrm{cut}}) = (50,\ 30)\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
x_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{2400 \cdot 30 - 400 \cdot 50}{2000}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{72\,000 - 20\,000}{2000}\,\mathrm{cm} = 26\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
y_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{2400 \cdot 20 - 400 \cdot 30}{2000}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{48\,000 - 12\,000}{2000}\,\mathrm{cm} = 18\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 60,0 60,20 40,20 40,40 0,40" punti="26,18,pieno,C" segmenti="26,0,26,18;0,18,26,18" etichette="30,-7,60 cm;-10,20,40 cm;50,46,20 cm;70,30,20 cm" xmin=-16 xmax=82 ymin=-13 ymax=52 titolo="The plate with the corner removed: the center of mass $C$ lies at $x_\mathrm{cm} = 26\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $y_\mathrm{cm} = 18\,\mathrm{cm}$, inside the material"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The plate is uniform, therefore the mass of each piece is proportional to its area and the areas can be used in place of the masses, since the constant surface density cancels between numerator and denominator.

The remaining piece is treated as the whole plate together with a piece of **negative mass** placed where the square was removed. The whole plate has its center of mass at its geometric center $(30,\ 20)\,\mathrm{cm}$ by symmetry, and the removed square has its own at $(50,\ 30)\,\mathrm{cm}$, that is, at the center of the corner it occupied.

The weighted average with the negative term gives $(26,\ 18)\,\mathrm{cm}$. Both coordinates are smaller than those of the full plate, because removing material from the corner shifts the center of mass away from that corner.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two bodies move in the $xy$ plane: $m_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ with velocity $\vec{v}_1 = (3.0,\ -1.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $m_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ with velocity $\vec{v}_2 = (-2.0,\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine the total momentum, the velocity of the center of mass and the two terms of König's theorem.

$$
\vec{P} = m_1\vec{v}_1 + m_2\vec{v}_2,
\qquad
\vec{v}_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{\vec{P}}{m_1 + m_2}
$$
$$
P_x = \left[4.0 \cdot 3.0 + 6.0 \cdot (-2.0)\right]\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} = 0,
\qquad
P_y = \left[4.0 \cdot (-1.0) + 6.0 \cdot 2.0\right]\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{(0,\ 8.0)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}}{10.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = (0,\ 0.80)\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left(3.0^2 + 1.0^2\right)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left(2.0^2 + 2.0^2\right)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = 20\,\mathrm{J} + 24\,\mathrm{J} = 44\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_{\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)\,v_{\mathrm{cm}}^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.80\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 3.2\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_{\mathrm{int}} = K - K_{\mathrm{cm}} = 44\,\mathrm{J} - 3.2\,\mathrm{J} = 40.8\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The total momentum is the vector sum of the two momenta, computed component by component. The $x$ components cancel, hence the total momentum points along $y$ and the center of mass moves straight north at $0.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

König's theorem splits the kinetic energy into the part belonging to the motion of the system as a whole, $K_{\mathrm{cm}} = 3.2\,\mathrm{J}$, and the part belonging to the motion of the bodies relative to the center of mass, $K_{\mathrm{int}} = 40.8\,\mathrm{J}$.

The second term is by far the larger one here, since the two bodies move mainly toward each other. If these two bodies were to collide and stick together, momentum conservation would preserve $K_{\mathrm{cm}}$, therefore the energy dissipated would be exactly $40.8\,\mathrm{J}$, and no more.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A boat of mass $M = 200\,\mathrm{kg}$ floats at rest on still water. A person of mass $m = 70\,\mathrm{kg}$ standing at the stern walks $L = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ toward the bow. The resistance of the water is negligible. Determine the displacement of the boat with respect to the water.

$$
\vec{P} = \vec{0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{v}_{\mathrm{cm}} = \vec{0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x_{\mathrm{cm}} \ \text{is constant}
$$
$$
m\,\Delta x_p + M\,\Delta x_b = 0
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\Delta x_p - \Delta x_b = L
$$
$$
m\,(L + \Delta x_b) + M\,\Delta x_b = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta x_b = -\frac{m}{m + M}\,L
$$
$$
\Delta x_b = -\frac{70\,\mathrm{kg}}{270\,\mathrm{kg}} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m} = -0.78\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta x_p = 3.0\,\mathrm{m} - 0.78\,\mathrm{m} = 2.2\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system formed by the person and the boat has no horizontal external force, since the water offers negligible resistance, therefore the total momentum stays zero and the velocity of the center of mass stays zero. A point at rest does not move, hence the center of mass keeps the same position with respect to the water while the person walks.

The displacements with respect to the water are $\Delta x_p$ and $\Delta x_b$, and the condition that the center of mass does not move reads $m\,\Delta x_p + M\,\Delta x_b = 0$. The length walked along the boat is the displacement of the person relative to the boat, which gives the second equation $\Delta x_p - \Delta x_b = L$.

Solving the system gives $\Delta x_b = -0.78\,\mathrm{m}$, so the boat slides $0.78\,\mathrm{m}$ **backwards**, and the person advances $2.2\,\mathrm{m}$ with respect to the water. The two displacements are in inverse ratio to the masses, and their difference is the $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ walked on board.

</div>
