## Exercise 1

Given $\vec{A} = (5.0,\ 2.0,\ 0)$ and $\vec{B} = (-1.0,\ 4.0,\ 0)$, compute the vector product $\vec{A} \times \vec{B}$, the area of the parallelogram with these two sides and the angle between the two vectors.

$$
\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (A_yB_z - A_zB_y)\,\hat{\imath} + (A_zB_x - A_xB_z)\,\hat{\jmath} + (A_xB_y - A_yB_x)\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (2.0 \cdot 0 - 0 \cdot 4.0)\,\hat{\imath} + (0 \cdot (-1.0) - 5.0 \cdot 0)\,\hat{\jmath} + (5.0 \cdot 4.0 - 2.0 \cdot (-1.0))\,\hat{k} = 22\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
S = |\vec{A} \times \vec{B}| = 22
$$
$$
A = \sqrt{29} = 5.385,
\qquad
B = \sqrt{17} = 4.123,
\qquad
\sin\varphi = \frac{|\vec{A} \times \vec{B}|}{A\,B} = \frac{22}{22.20} = 0.9908
$$
$$
\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = 5.0 \cdot (-1.0) + 2.0 \cdot 4.0 = 3.0 > 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 82.2^\circ
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,5,2,A;0,0,-1,4,B" poligoni="0,0 5,2 4,6 -1,4" xmin=-2.4 xmax=6.4 ymin=-1.2 ymax=7 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="The parallelogram with sides $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ has area $|\vec{A} \times \vec{B}| = 22$, and the turn from $\vec{A}$ to $\vec{B}$ is counterclockwise"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both vectors lie in the $xy$ plane, hence their third components vanish and the general formula leaves only the component along $\hat{k}$, which equals $A_xB_y - A_yB_x$. The product is therefore perpendicular to the plane of the two vectors, as the definition requires, and it points along the positive $z$ axis because the number $22$ is positive, hence the turn from $\vec{A}$ to $\vec{B}$ is counterclockwise.

The magnitude of the vector product equals the area of the parallelogram built on the two vectors, therefore that area is $22$. The figure shows the parallelogram, whose fourth vertex is the tip of $\vec{A} + \vec{B} = (4.0,\ 6.0)$.

The angle follows from $|\vec{A} \times \vec{B}| = A\,B\sin\varphi$, which gives $\sin\varphi = 0.9908$. The sine alone leaves two possibilities, $82.2^\circ$ and $97.8^\circ$, so the scalar product settles the question. That product is positive, hence $\cos\varphi > 0$ and the angle is acute, therefore $\varphi = 82.2^\circ$. A further check uses the identity $|\vec{A} \times \vec{B}|^2 + (\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B})^2 = A^2B^2$, which here reads $484 + 9 = 29 \cdot 17 = 493$ and **is satisfied**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $\vec{A} = (2.0,\ -1.0,\ 3.0)$ and $\vec{B} = (1.0,\ 4.0,\ -2.0)$, compute $\vec{A} \times \vec{B}$, verify that the result is perpendicular to both vectors and compute $\vec{B} \times \vec{A}$.

$$
\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (A_yB_z - A_zB_y)\,\hat{\imath} + (A_zB_x - A_xB_z)\,\hat{\jmath} + (A_xB_y - A_yB_x)\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\vec{A} \times \vec{B})_x &= (-1.0)(-2.0) - (3.0)(4.0) = 2.0 - 12.0 = -10.0 \\[6pt]
(\vec{A} \times \vec{B})_y &= (3.0)(1.0) - (2.0)(-2.0) = 3.0 + 4.0 = 7.0 \\[6pt]
(\vec{A} \times \vec{B})_z &= (2.0)(4.0) - (-1.0)(1.0) = 8.0 + 1.0 = 9.0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (-10.0,\ 7.0,\ 9.0),
\qquad
|\vec{A} \times \vec{B}| = \sqrt{100 + 49 + 81} = \sqrt{230} = 15.17
$$
$$
\vec{A} \cdot (\vec{A} \times \vec{B}) = -20.0 - 7.0 + 27.0 = 0,
\qquad
\vec{B} \cdot (\vec{A} \times \vec{B}) = -10.0 + 28.0 - 18.0 = 0
$$
$$
\vec{B} \times \vec{A} = -\,\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (10.0,\ -7.0,\ -9.0)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each component of the vector product is built from the two components of $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ that do not belong to the axis being computed. The component along $\hat{\imath}$ uses the $y$ and $z$ components, the component along $\hat{\jmath}$ uses the $z$ and $x$ components, and the component along $\hat{k}$ uses the $x$ and $y$ components, each pair taken in that cyclic order.

The product is perpendicular to the plane of the two factors by definition, therefore its scalar product with each factor vanishes. Both checks give zero, hence the three components are computed correctly. This test costs two short sums and it catches a sign error at once, which is the usual mistake in the component along $\hat{\jmath}$, where the order of the indices is reversed with respect to the other two.

Reversing the order of the factors reverses the sense of the product, since the fingers of the right hand turn the other way, therefore $\vec{B} \times \vec{A} = -\,\vec{A} \times \vec{B}$. The two products have the same magnitude $15.17$ and opposite senses, hence the vector product is not commutative and the order matters in every formula that uses it, torque among them.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A force of magnitude $F = 180\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied at the end of a wrench of length $r = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$, in the plane perpendicular to the bolt, and makes the angle $\varphi = 55^\circ$ with the wrench. Compute the magnitude of the torque about the bolt, the largest torque the same force could produce and the force needed to reach a torque of $100\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ at the same angle.

$$
\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F},
\qquad
\tau = r\,F\sin\varphi
$$
$$
\tau = 0.45\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 180\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \sin 55^\circ = 81.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \cdot 0.8192 = 66.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\sin\varphi \le 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tau_{\max} = r\,F = 0.45\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 180\,\mathrm{N} = 81.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\qquad (\varphi = 90^\circ)
$$
$$
F' = \frac{\tau'}{r\sin\varphi} = \frac{100\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{0.45\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.8192} = 271\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque of a force about a point is the vector product of the position vector of the point of application and the force, hence its magnitude is $r\,F\sin\varphi$ and its unit is the newton times the meter. The factor $r\sin\varphi$ is the distance from the bolt to the line of action of the force, called the moment arm, therefore only the component of the force perpendicular to the wrench turns the bolt.

With $\varphi = 55^\circ$ the sine is $0.8192$ and the torque is $66.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. Since the sine never exceeds $1$, the same force applied at the same distance produces at most $r\,F = 81.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, and that value is reached when the force is perpendicular to the wrench. Applying the force along the wrench gives $\sin 0^\circ = 0$, hence no torque at all.

The last line solves the same formula for the force. Reaching $100\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ at $55^\circ$ requires $271\,\mathrm{N}$, while a force perpendicular to the wrench would need only $\dfrac{100}{0.45} = 222\,\mathrm{N}$. A longer wrench lowers the required force in the same proportion, which is why the chapters on fixed-axis rotation and on static equilibrium work with the product of force and moment arm rather than with the force alone.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A force $\vec{F} = (50\,\hat{\imath} - 30\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied at the point located by $\vec{r} = (0.20\,\hat{\imath} + 0.10\,\hat{\jmath} - 0.30\,\hat{k})\,\mathrm{m}$ with respect to the origin. Compute the torque of the force about the origin and its magnitude.

$$
\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_x &= r_yF_z - r_zF_y = (0.10)(0) - (-0.30)(-30) = -9.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \\[6pt]
\tau_y &= r_zF_x - r_xF_z = (-0.30)(50) - (0.20)(0) = -15.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \\[6pt]
\tau_z &= r_xF_y - r_yF_x = (0.20)(-30) - (0.10)(50) = -6.0 - 5.0 = -11.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\vec{\tau} = (-9.0\,\hat{\imath} - 15.0\,\hat{\jmath} - 11.0\,\hat{k})\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\tau = \sqrt{(9.0)^2 + (15.0)^2 + (11.0)^2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = \sqrt{427}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 20.7\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force has no component along $z$, hence $F_z = 0$ and two of the three products disappear, yet the torque still has three nonzero components, because the point of application lies off the $xy$ plane. The torque is perpendicular to the plane containing $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{F}$, and that plane is tilted with respect to the coordinate planes.

Each component of the torque is the torque about the corresponding axis through the origin. The component $\tau_z = -11.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ is negative, therefore the force tends to turn the body clockwise about the $z$ axis as seen from the positive $z$ side, by the right-hand rule.

The magnitude is $20.7\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. As a check, the scalar product of the torque with the force is $(-9.0)(50) + (-15.0)(-30) + (-11.0)(0) = -450 + 450 = 0$, and the scalar product with $\vec{r}$ is $(-9.0)(0.20) + (-15.0)(0.10) + (-11.0)(-0.30) = -1.80 - 1.50 + 3.30 = 0$, hence the torque **is perpendicular** to both vectors, as the vector product requires.

</div>
