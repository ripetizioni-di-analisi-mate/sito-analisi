## Exercise 1

A car travels along a highway at the steady speed of $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Express this speed in meters per second and in miles per hour, with $1\,\mathrm{mi} = 1609\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
v = 72\,\frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{h}}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
1\,\mathrm{km} = 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
1\,\mathrm{h} = 3600\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
v = 72 \cdot \frac{10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}}{3600\,\mathrm{s}} = \frac{72\,000}{3600}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 20.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v = 20.0\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}} \cdot \frac{1\,\mathrm{mi}}{1609\,\mathrm{m}} \cdot \frac{3600\,\mathrm{s}}{1\,\mathrm{h}}
= \frac{20.0 \cdot 3600}{1609}\,\mathrm{mi/h} = 44.7\,\mathrm{mi/h}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A speed is a length divided by a time, therefore its unit is a length unit divided by a time unit, and a conversion is performed by replacing each unit separately by its value in the new unit. The kilometer is $10^{3}$ meters and the hour is $3600$ seconds, hence the numerical factor is $\dfrac{10^{3}}{3600}$, which is close to $\dfrac{1}{3.6}$. The speed $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$ therefore becomes $20.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

For the second conversion the factors are arranged so that every unit to be removed appears once in a numerator and once in a denominator. The meter cancels against the meter of $1\,\mathrm{mi} = 1609\,\mathrm{m}$ and the second cancels against the second of $1\,\mathrm{h} = 3600\,\mathrm{s}$, hence only $\mathrm{mi/h}$ survives. The result is $44.7\,\mathrm{mi/h}$. The value in miles per hour is larger than the value in meters per second, since the hour exceeds the second by the factor $3600$ while the mile exceeds the meter by the smaller factor $1609$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Structural steel has density $\rho = 7.85\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Express the density in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and compute the mass of a steel cube whose edge measures $25.0\,\mathrm{mm}$.

$$
\rho = 7.85\,\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\mathrm{cm^3}}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
1\,\mathrm{g} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg},
\qquad
1\,\mathrm{cm^3} = (10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^3 = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
\rho = 7.85 \cdot \frac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}}{10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}} = 7.85 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$
$$
V = \ell^3 = \left(25.0\,\mathrm{mm}\right)^3 = \left(2.50 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}\right)^3 = 1.5625 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
m = \rho\,V = 7.85 \times 10^{3}\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m^3}} \cdot 1.5625 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 0.123\,\mathrm{kg} = 123\,\mathrm{g}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density is a mass divided by a volume, hence its unit contains a mass unit in the numerator and the cube of a length unit in the denominator. The prefix and the symbol of the unit form a single symbol, therefore the exponent $3$ of $\mathrm{cm^3}$ applies to the prefix as well, and $1\,\mathrm{cm^3} = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The two factors $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-6}$ combine into $10^{3}$, hence the numerical value in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is one thousand times the value in $\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

The edge is first written in meters, so that the volume comes out in $\mathrm{m^3}$ and matches the unit of the density. Since $1\,\mathrm{mm} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$, the edge is $2.50 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$ and the volume is $1.5625 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Multiplying the density by the volume, the unit $\mathrm{m^3}$ cancels and the mass is left in kilograms, hence $m = 0.123\,\mathrm{kg}$. Both data carry three significant figures, therefore the result keeps three. A cube of steel $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ on a side weighing about $120\,\mathrm{g}$ is a plausible value, since steel is about eight times denser than water and the same cube of water would hold about $16\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cylindrical tank of volume $15.0\,\mathrm{ft^3}$ is completely filled with water of density $1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Express the volume in cubic meters and in liters, and compute the mass of the water in kilograms and in pounds, with $1\,\mathrm{ft} = 0.3048\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1\,\mathrm{lb} = 0.4536\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
1\,\mathrm{ft^3} = \left(0.3048\,\mathrm{m}\right)^3 = 2.8317 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
V = 15.0\,\mathrm{ft^3} \cdot 2.8317 \times 10^{-2}\,\frac{\mathrm{m^3}}{\mathrm{ft^3}} = 0.4248\,\mathrm{m^3} = 0.425\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
1\,\mathrm{m^3} = 10^{3}\,\mathrm{L}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V = 0.4248\,\mathrm{m^3} \cdot 10^{3}\,\frac{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{m^3}} = 425\,\mathrm{L}
$$
$$
m = \rho\,V = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m^3}} \cdot 0.4248\,\mathrm{m^3} = 425\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
m = 424.8\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \frac{1\,\mathrm{lb}}{0.4536\,\mathrm{kg}} = 936\,\mathrm{lb}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The conversion factor between feet and meters is raised to the third power, since the volume contains the length unit three times. One cubic foot is therefore $2.8317 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, which is close to $\dfrac{1}{35}$ of a cubic meter, and the tank holds $0.425\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

The liter is defined as one cubic decimeter, hence $1\,\mathrm{L} = (10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m})^3 = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and a cubic meter contains one thousand liters. The volume is therefore $425\,\mathrm{L}$. Water has density $1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, so one liter of water has a mass of one kilogram, and the mass in kilograms is numerically equal to the volume in liters, that is $425\,\mathrm{kg}$. The last conversion divides by the mass of one pound in kilograms, which gives $936\,\mathrm{lb}$. The unrounded value $424.8\,\mathrm{kg}$ is carried into the division, because rounding is applied once to the final result.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A wind turbine delivers the average power $P = 1.80\,\mathrm{MW}$. Compute the energy it produces in one day, in joules and in kilowatt-hours, and express the power in base SI units alone.

$$
P = 1.80\,\mathrm{MW} = 1.80 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{W},
\qquad
\Delta t = 1\,\mathrm{d} = 24 \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 8.6400 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
E = P\,\Delta t
$$
$$
E = 1.80 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 8.6400 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.5552 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{J} = 1.56 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
1\,\mathrm{kW\cdot h} = 10^{3}\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 3.60 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E = \frac{1.5552 \times 10^{11}}{3.60 \times 10^{6}}\,\mathrm{kW\cdot h} = 4.32 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kW\cdot h}
$$
$$
1\,\mathrm{W} = 1\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{s}} = 1\,\frac{\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{\mathrm{s}} = 1\,\frac{\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}}{\mathrm{s^3}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P = 1.80 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The prefix mega multiplies the unit by $10^{6}$, hence the power is $1.80 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{W}$. Power is energy divided by time, therefore the energy delivered at constant power is the product $P\,\Delta t$, and the product of a watt and a second is a joule. One day contains $24$ hours of $3600$ seconds each, which is $8.6400 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{s}$, and this number is exact, so it does not limit the significant figures. The energy is $1.56 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{J}$.

The kilowatt-hour is the energy delivered at the power of one kilowatt during one hour, hence it equals $3.60 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}$ and it is a unit of energy, not of power. Dividing the energy in joules by this value gives $4.32 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kW\cdot h}$, a result that the data also give directly as $1.80 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kW} \cdot 24.0\,\mathrm{h}$.

The last line rewrites the watt with base units alone. The newton is $1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$ by the second law, the joule is a newton times a meter, and the watt is a joule per second, therefore $1\,\mathrm{W} = 1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s^3}$. Every derived unit of mechanics can be reduced to the kilogram, the meter and the second in this way, which is what makes a dimensional check possible.

</div>
