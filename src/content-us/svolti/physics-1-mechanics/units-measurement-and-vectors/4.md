## Exercise 1

Decide which of the two relations $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)$ and $v = v_0 + \dfrac{1}{2}a t^2$ is dimensionally homogeneous, where $v$ and $v_0$ are velocities, $a$ an acceleration, $x$ and $x_0$ positions and $t$ a time.

$$
[v] = [L\,T^{-1}],
\qquad
[a] = [L\,T^{-2}],
\qquad
[x] = [L],
\qquad
[t] = [T]
$$
$$
[v^2] = [L^2\,T^{-2}],
\qquad
[v_0^2] = [L^2\,T^{-2}],
\qquad
[a(x - x_0)] = [L\,T^{-2}][L] = [L^2\,T^{-2}]
$$
$$
[v] = [L\,T^{-1}],
\qquad
[v_0] = [L\,T^{-1}],
\qquad
\left[\frac{1}{2}a t^2\right] = [L\,T^{-2}][T^2] = [L]
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every term of the first relation has the dimensions $[L^2\,T^{-2}]$. The factor $2$ is a pure number, hence it does not contribute, and the difference $x - x_0$ has the dimensions of a length because only lengths are subtracted there. The two sides agree and only terms of equal dimensions are added, therefore the first relation **is homogeneous**. The relation between velocity and position in uniformly accelerated motion has exactly this form, as the chapter on motion along a straight line establishes.

In the second relation the first two terms are velocities, while the third has the dimension of a length. Terms of different dimensions are added, therefore the second relation **is not homogeneous** and it is wrong, whatever numbers are substituted into it. The correct relation for the velocity is $v = v_0 + a t$, in which the last term has the dimensions $[L\,T^{-2}][T] = [L\,T^{-1}]$ and agrees with the other two.

Homogeneity is a necessary condition, hence it can reject a relation but it cannot confirm one. The relation $v^2 = v_0^2 + 4a(x - x_0)$ is homogeneous as well, and it is still wrong, because the dimensionless factor is not the right one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The force exerted by a spring stretched by the amount $x$ has magnitude $F = k\,x$. Determine the dimensions of the constant $k$ and its SI unit, then show that the quantity $\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$, with $m$ a mass, is a time and compute its value for $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$.

$$
[F] = [k][x]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
[k] = \frac{[F]}{[x]} = \frac{[M\,L\,T^{-2}]}{[L]} = [M\,T^{-2}]
$$
$$
[k] = [M\,T^{-2}]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k \ \text{is measured in}\ \mathrm{kg/s^2} = \mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
\left[\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}\right] = \sqrt{\frac{[M]}{[M\,T^{-2}]}} = \sqrt{[T^2]} = [T]
$$
$$
\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.50\,\mathrm{kg}}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}}} = \sqrt{2.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s^2}} = 0.050\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The dimensions of $k$ are read off the relation that defines it. A force has the dimensions $[M\,L\,T^{-2}]$ by the second law of Newton, and $x$ is a length, therefore the quotient has the dimensions $[M\,T^{-2}]$. The corresponding SI unit is the $\mathrm{kg/s^2}$, which coincides with the $\mathrm{N/m}$, since $1\,\mathrm{N} = 1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$ and dividing by a meter removes the length.

In the quotient $\dfrac{m}{k}$ the dimension $[M]$ cancels and $[T^{-2}]$ passes to the numerator, hence the quotient has the dimensions $[T^2]$ and its square root is a time. The numerical check uses the same cancellation on the units, where $\dfrac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{N/m}} = \dfrac{\mathrm{kg\cdot m}}{\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}} = \mathrm{s^2}$, therefore the square root comes out in seconds and equals $0.050\,\mathrm{s}$.

A spring and a mass form the only combination of these two quantities that produces a time, so any period associated with that system is a dimensionless multiple of $\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$. The chapter on oscillations shows that the multiple is $2\pi$, hence the period is $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}} = 0.31\,\mathrm{s}$ for these data.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The period $T$ of a simple pendulum is assumed to depend only on the length $\ell$ of the string and on the acceleration of gravity $g$, in the form $T = C\,\ell^{\,a} g^{\,b}$ with $C$ dimensionless. Determine the exponents $a$ and $b$, and compute the period for $\ell = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ taking $C = 2\pi$.

$$
[T] = [\ell]^a [g]^b
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
[T] = [L]^a\,[L\,T^{-2}]^b = [L^{a+b}\,T^{-2b}]
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
a + b = 0 \\[4pt]
-2b = 1
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
b = -\frac{1}{2},
\qquad
a = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
T = C\,\ell^{\frac{1}{2}} g^{-\frac{1}{2}} = C\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g}}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1.00\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 2\pi \cdot 0.3192\,\mathrm{s} = 2.01\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left side is a time, hence it has the dimensions $[T]$ and no length, and the right side must have the same dimensions. Collecting the exponents of $[L]$ and of $[T]$ on the right gives $[L^{a+b}\,T^{-2b}]$, and two expressions have the same dimensions exactly when the exponent of each base quantity agrees, therefore the exponent of $[L]$ is zero and the exponent of $[T]$ is one. That is the system written above, whose solution is $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $b = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The period is therefore proportional to $\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$, hence it grows with the square root of the length and it does **not** depend on the mass of the bob. Dimensional analysis gives this conclusion before any equation of motion is written, because a mass could not be cancelled by $\ell$ and $g$ alone, neither of which contains $[M]$.

The dimensionless constant $C$ stays undetermined, since it has no dimensions and the method says nothing about it. Experiment and the equation of motion of the pendulum, derived in the chapter on oscillations, both give $C = 2\pi$. With that value and $\ell = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the period is $2.01\,\mathrm{s}$, hence a pendulum one meter long takes about one second to swing from one side to the other.

</div>

## Exercise 4

At high speed the magnitude of the drag force on a sphere moving through air is written $F = C\,\rho^{\,a} v^{\,b} r^{\,c}$, where $\rho$ is the density of the air, $v$ the speed of the sphere, $r$ its radius and $C$ a dimensionless constant. Determine the exponents $a$, $b$ and $c$, and state how the force changes when the speed is doubled.

$$
[F] = [M\,L\,T^{-2}],
\qquad
[\rho] = [M\,L^{-3}],
\qquad
[v] = [L\,T^{-1}],
\qquad
[r] = [L]
$$
$$
[M\,L\,T^{-2}] = [M\,L^{-3}]^a\,[L\,T^{-1}]^b\,[L]^c = [M^{a}\,L^{-3a+b+c}\,T^{-b}]
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
a = 1 \\[4pt]
-3a + b + c = 1 \\[4pt]
-b = -2
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = 1,
\qquad
b = 2,
\qquad
c = 2
$$
$$
F = C\,\rho\,v^2 r^2
$$
$$
v \longrightarrow 2v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F \longrightarrow C\,\rho\,(2v)^2 r^2 = 4\,C\,\rho\,v^2 r^2 = 4F
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three base quantities give three equations, one for each exponent of $[M]$, $[L]$ and $[T]$. The density is the only quantity containing a mass, therefore the exponent of $[M]$ on the right is $a$ and the first equation gives $a = 1$ at once. The speed is the only quantity containing a time, hence the exponent of $[T]$ is $-b$ and the third equation gives $b = 2$. Substituting these two values into the equation for $[L]$ gives $-3 + 2 + c = 1$, hence $c = 2$.

The result $F = C\,\rho\,v^2 r^2$ shows that the drag force is proportional to the square of the speed and to the square of the radius, hence to the cross-sectional area of the sphere, since that area is $\pi r^2$. The chapter on applications of Newton's laws uses this dependence to obtain the terminal speed of a falling body, and the chapter on fluid mechanics identifies the constant $C$ with a drag coefficient measured in a wind tunnel.

Because the exponent of the speed is $2$, doubling the speed multiplies the force by $2^2 = 4$. This conclusion follows from the exponents alone and holds whatever the value of the dimensionless constant, which is what makes dimensional analysis useful even when the constant is unknown.

</div>
