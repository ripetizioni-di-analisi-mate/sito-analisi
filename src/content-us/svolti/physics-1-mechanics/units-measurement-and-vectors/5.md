## Exercise 1

Three forces act on the same point of a body and lie in the $xy$ plane: $F_1 = 40\,\mathrm{N}$ along the positive $x$ axis, $F_2 = 30\,\mathrm{N}$ along the positive $y$ axis and $F_3 = 25\,\mathrm{N}$ along the negative $x$ axis. Compute the magnitude and the direction of the resultant.

$$
\vec{R} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3,
\qquad
R_x = F_{1x} + F_{2x} + F_{3x},
\qquad
R_y = F_{1y} + F_{2y} + F_{3y}
$$
$$
R_x = 40\,\mathrm{N} + 0 - 25\,\mathrm{N} = 15\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
R_y = 0 + 30\,\mathrm{N} + 0 = 30\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2} = \sqrt{(15)^2 + (30)^2}\,\mathrm{N} = \sqrt{1125}\,\mathrm{N} = 33.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{R_y}{R_x} = \frac{30}{15} = 2.0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 63.4^\circ
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,4,0,F₁;0,0,0,3;0,0,-2.5,0,F₃;0,0,1.5,3,R" etichette="-0.95,1.9,F₂" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=-3.6 xmax=5 ymin=-0.9 ymax=3.9 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="The three forces and their resultant $\vec{R}$, drawn to the scale of $10\,\mathrm{N}$ per unit, where the two forces along $x$ partly cancel"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two vectors are equal exactly when their components are equal, therefore one vector equation is equivalent to two numerical equations, one per axis. The resultant is obtained by adding the $x$ components among themselves and the $y$ components among themselves, and no trigonometry is needed here because each force lies along an axis.

The force $\vec{F}_3$ points along the negative $x$ axis, hence its $x$ component is negative and equals $-25\,\mathrm{N}$, while its $y$ component is zero. Adding the three $x$ components gives $R_x = 15\,\mathrm{N}$, which is smaller than $F_1$ because $\vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_3$ have opposite senses and partly cancel.

The magnitude of the resultant follows from the Pythagorean theorem and equals $33.5\,\mathrm{N}$. Both components are positive, hence the resultant lies in the first quadrant and the arctangent gives its angle directly, $\theta = 63.4^\circ$. The magnitude of the resultant is smaller than the sum $40 + 30 + 25 = 95\,\mathrm{N}$ of the three magnitudes, as the inequality $|\vec{A} + \vec{B}| \le A + B$ requires.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two forces act on the same point: $\vec{F}_1$ of magnitude $60\,\mathrm{N}$ at $25^\circ$ from the positive $x$ axis and $\vec{F}_2$ of magnitude $45\,\mathrm{N}$ at $115^\circ$ from the same axis. Compute the magnitude and the direction of the resultant.

$$
F_{1x} = 60\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \cos 25^\circ = 54.38\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F_{1y} = 60\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \sin 25^\circ = 25.36\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_{2x} = 45\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \cos 115^\circ = -19.02\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F_{2y} = 45\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \sin 115^\circ = 40.78\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
R_x = 54.38\,\mathrm{N} - 19.02\,\mathrm{N} = 35.36\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
R_y = 25.36\,\mathrm{N} + 40.78\,\mathrm{N} = 66.14\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{(35.36)^2 + (66.14)^2}\,\mathrm{N} = \sqrt{5625}\,\mathrm{N} = 75.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{66.14}{35.36} = 1.871
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 61.9^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each force is first resolved along the axes with the formulas $F\cos\theta$ and $F\sin\theta$. The angle $115^\circ$ lies in the second quadrant, hence its cosine is negative and its sine is positive, therefore $\vec{F}_2$ has a negative $x$ component and a positive $y$ component. Keeping the sign that the trigonometric functions produce is what makes the method automatic, since no separate decision about the sense of each component is required.

Adding the components gives $R_x = 35.36\,\mathrm{N}$ and $R_y = 66.14\,\mathrm{N}$, hence the magnitude $75.0\,\mathrm{N}$ and the angle $61.9^\circ$. Since $R_x$ is positive the resultant lies in the first quadrant and the arctangent is used without correction.

The two forces are perpendicular, because their directions differ by $115^\circ - 25^\circ = 90^\circ$. The magnitude of the resultant can therefore be checked independently with the Pythagorean theorem applied to the magnitudes, $\sqrt{60^2 + 45^2}\,\mathrm{N} = \sqrt{5625}\,\mathrm{N} = 75.0\,\mathrm{N}$, and the direction of the resultant makes with $\vec{F}_1$ the angle $\arctan\dfrac{45}{60} = 36.9^\circ$, hence $25^\circ + 36.9^\circ = 61.9^\circ$ from the $x$ axis. Both checks agree with the result obtained from the components.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point is acted on by $\vec{F}_1$ of magnitude $80\,\mathrm{N}$ at $30^\circ$ and $\vec{F}_2$ of magnitude $50\,\mathrm{N}$ at $140^\circ$ from the positive $x$ axis. Determine the third force $\vec{F}_3$ that keeps the point in equilibrium.

$$
\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 = \vec{0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{F}_3 = -\left(\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2\right)
$$
$$
F_{1x} = 80\,\mathrm{N}\cos 30^\circ = 69.28\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F_{1y} = 80\,\mathrm{N}\sin 30^\circ = 40.00\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_{2x} = 50\,\mathrm{N}\cos 140^\circ = -38.30\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F_{2y} = 50\,\mathrm{N}\sin 140^\circ = 32.14\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_{3x} = -\left(69.28 - 38.30\right)\,\mathrm{N} = -30.98\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F_{3y} = -\left(40.00 + 32.14\right)\,\mathrm{N} = -72.14\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_3 = \sqrt{(30.98)^2 + (72.14)^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 78.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\arctan\!\left(\frac{-72.14}{-30.98}\right) = 66.8^\circ,
\qquad
F_{3x} < 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta_3 = 66.8^\circ + 180^\circ = 246.8^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A point is in equilibrium when the resultant of the forces applied to it vanishes, therefore the third force is the opposite of the sum of the first two. Multiplying a vector by $-1$ reverses its sense and leaves its magnitude unchanged, hence $\vec{F}_3$ has the magnitude of $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2$ and the opposite direction.

The sum of the first two forces has components $30.98\,\mathrm{N}$ and $72.14\,\mathrm{N}$, hence the components of $\vec{F}_3$ are the same numbers with the signs reversed. Both are negative, therefore the equilibrating force lies in the third quadrant, which agrees with the fact that the sum of $\vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_2$ points upwards and to the right.

The magnitude is $78.5\,\mathrm{N}$. Since $F_{3x}$ is negative the arctangent returns the angle of the opposite vector, hence $180^\circ$ is added and the direction is $\theta_3 = 246.8^\circ$. As a check, the resultant of the three forces has components $69.28 - 38.30 - 30.98 = 0$ and $40.00 + 32.14 - 72.14 = 0$, therefore the equilibrium condition **is satisfied** on both axes.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A hiker walks $2.5\,\mathrm{km}$ due east, then $1.8\,\mathrm{km}$ in the direction $40^\circ$ north of east, and finally $3.0\,\mathrm{km}$ due north. Compute the magnitude and the direction of the net displacement from the starting point.

$$
\vec{d} = \vec{d}_1 + \vec{d}_2 + \vec{d}_3
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
x \ \text{towards east},
\quad
y \ \text{towards north}
$$
$$
\vec{d}_1 = (2.5,\ 0)\,\mathrm{km},
\qquad
\vec{d}_3 = (0,\ 3.0)\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
d_{2x} = 1.8\,\mathrm{km}\cos 40^\circ = 1.379\,\mathrm{km},
\qquad
d_{2y} = 1.8\,\mathrm{km}\sin 40^\circ = 1.157\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
d_x = 2.5 + 1.379 + 0 = 3.879\,\mathrm{km},
\qquad
d_y = 0 + 1.157 + 3.0 = 4.157\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
d = \sqrt{(3.879)^2 + (4.157)^2}\,\mathrm{km} = \sqrt{32.33}\,\mathrm{km} = 5.69\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{4.157}{3.879} = 1.072
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 47.0^\circ \ \text{north of east}
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,2.5,0,d₁;2.5,0,3.879,1.157,d₂;3.879,1.157,3.879,4.157,d₃;0,0,3.879,4.157,d" punti="0,0,pieno;3.879,4.157,pieno" xmin=-0.7 xmax=5.4 ymin=-0.7 ymax=4.9 uguale=1 nomi="E,N" titolo="The three legs drawn tip to tail and the net displacement $\vec{d}$ from the starting point, of magnitude $5.69\,\mathrm{km}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Successive displacements add tip to tail, therefore the net displacement is the vector from the starting point to the arrival point and it does not depend on the order in which the legs are walked. Choosing the $x$ axis towards east and the $y$ axis towards north turns the sum into two separate additions of numbers.

The first leg has no north component and the third has no east component, hence only the second leg requires trigonometry. The direction $40^\circ$ north of east means an angle of $40^\circ$ measured from the east direction towards the north, which is the positive $x$ axis, therefore the components are $1.8\cos 40^\circ$ and $1.8\sin 40^\circ$.

The net displacement has magnitude $5.69\,\mathrm{km}$ and direction $47.0^\circ$ north of east. The total distance walked is $2.5 + 1.8 + 3.0 = 7.3\,\mathrm{km}$, hence it is larger than the magnitude of the net displacement, as it must be whenever the path is not a straight line. The hiker returning to the starting point in a straight line would therefore walk $5.69\,\mathrm{km}$ in the direction $47.0^\circ$ south of west.

</div>
