## Exercise 1

A crate of mass $m = 18\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pushed along a horizontal frictionless floor by a constant horizontal force of magnitude $F = 55\,\mathrm{N}$, and it moves $d = 12\,\mathrm{m}$ in the direction of the force. Compute the work done by each force acting on the crate and the total work.

$$
W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = F\,d\cos\varphi
$$
$$
\varphi_F = 0^\circ,
\qquad
\varphi_N = 90^\circ,
\qquad
\varphi_P = 90^\circ
$$
$$
W_F = F\,d = 55\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{m} = 660\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W_N = N\,d\cos 90^\circ = 0,
\qquad
W_P = m g\,d\cos 90^\circ = 0
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = W_F + W_N + W_P = 660\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Three forces act on the crate: the applied force, the normal force of the floor and the weight. The work of each of them is computed from the definition of the **work of a constant force**, in which only the angle between that force and the displacement appears.

The applied force is parallel to the displacement and has the same sense, hence $\varphi_F = 0^\circ$ and $\cos\varphi_F = 1$, so its work is the product $F\,d = 660\,\mathrm{J}$. The normal force and the weight are vertical while the displacement is horizontal, hence $\varphi = 90^\circ$ and $\cos 90^\circ = 0$ for both, so neither of them does any work. The total work is therefore the work of the applied force alone, $660\,\mathrm{J}$.

The mass is not used, since work depends on the force and on the displacement and not on the mass of the body.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A suitcase is pulled along a horizontal floor by a handle that exerts a constant force of magnitude $F = 85\,\mathrm{N}$ directed at $35^\circ$ above the horizontal. The suitcase moves $d = 20\,\mathrm{m}$ horizontally, and the kinetic friction force exerted by the floor has the constant magnitude $f = 60\,\mathrm{N}$. Compute the work done by the handle, the work done by friction and the total work.

$$
W_F = F\,d\cos\theta,
\qquad
W_f = -f\,d,
\qquad
W_N = W_P = 0
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = F\,d\cos\theta - f\,d = (F\cos\theta - f)\,d
$$
$$
W_F = 85\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \cos 35^\circ = 85\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.8192 = 1.39 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W_f = -60\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m} = -1.20 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = 1392.6\,\mathrm{J} - 1200\,\mathrm{J} = 1.93 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force of the handle makes the angle $\theta = 35^\circ$ with the displacement, therefore its work is $F\,d\cos 35^\circ$, and only the horizontal component $F\cos 35^\circ = 69.6\,\mathrm{N}$ contributes. The vertical component lifts part of the weight off the floor and reduces the normal force, yet it does no work, since it is perpendicular to the displacement.

Kinetic friction opposes the sliding, so it points opposite to the displacement and the angle between them is $180^\circ$, hence its work is **negative** and equals $-f\,d$. The weight and the normal force are perpendicular to the displacement and do no work.

The total work is the sum of the three contributions, $1.93 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$. The sum is positive, therefore the suitcase gains kinetic energy and speeds up along the $20\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides down an inclined plane that makes the angle $\theta = 25^\circ$ with the horizontal, covering the distance $L = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ along the surface. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the plane is $\mu_k = 0.20$. Compute the work done by the weight, by the normal force and by friction, and the total work.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,2.798;2.592,1.209 3.408,1.589 3.197,2.042 2.381,1.662" vettori="2.894,1.626,2.894,-0.374,P;2.894,1.626,2.133,3.257,N;2.894,1.626,4.253,2.26,f" linee="0,0 6,2.798" riferimento="6.4,0.3,25,1.2,x,y" etichette="0.72,0.1,25°" xmin=-0.6 xmax=8.2 ymin=-1.3 ymax=3.7 titolo="Free-body diagram of the block sliding down the plane: $\vec{N}$ is perpendicular to the displacement, while $\vec{f}$ points up the plane because the block moves down it"}

$$
\begin{cases}
N - m g\cos\theta = 0 \\[4pt]
f = \mu_k N
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f = \mu_k\,m g\cos\theta
$$
$$
W_P = m g L\sin\theta,
\qquad
W_N = 0,
\qquad
W_f = -\mu_k\,m g L\cos\theta
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = m g L\,(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta)
$$
$$
W_P = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \sin 25^\circ = 74.6\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
f = 0.20 \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot \cos 25^\circ = 10.7\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
W_f = -10.67\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m} = -32.0\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = 74.6\,\mathrm{J} - 32.0\,\mathrm{J} = 42.6\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block moves along the plane, therefore the displacement has magnitude $L$ and lies along the surface. The weight makes the angle $90^\circ - \theta$ with that displacement, hence $\cos(90^\circ - \theta) = \sin\theta$ and the work of the weight is $m g L\sin\theta$, positive because the block goes down. The same value follows from the **work done by the weight**, since the drop in height is $h = L\sin\theta = 1.27\,\mathrm{m}$ and $W_P = m g h$.

The direction perpendicular to the plane is the direction in which the block does not accelerate, so the equation along that direction gives $N = m g\cos\theta$ and the friction force has magnitude $\mu_k m g\cos\theta = 10.7\,\mathrm{N}$. The block slides down, therefore friction points up the plane and its work is negative, $-32.0\,\mathrm{J}$. The normal force is perpendicular to the displacement at every point, hence it does no work.

The total work is $42.6\,\mathrm{J}$, and it is positive because $\tan 25^\circ = 0.466$ exceeds $\mu_k = 0.20$. The block therefore gains kinetic energy while it descends.

</div>
