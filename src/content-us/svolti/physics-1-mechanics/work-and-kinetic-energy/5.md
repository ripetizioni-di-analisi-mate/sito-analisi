## Exercise 1

A box of mass $m = 30\,\mathrm{kg}$, initially at rest, is pushed along a horizontal floor by a constant horizontal force $F = 150\,\mathrm{N}$ for a distance $d = 8.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the box and the floor is $\mu_k = 0.35$. Compute the speed of the box at the end of the stretch.

$$
\begin{cases}
N - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
f = \mu_k N
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f = \mu_k\,m g
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = (F - \mu_k\,m g)\,d = \frac{1}{2}m v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(F - \mu_k\,m g)\,d}{m}}
$$
$$
f = 0.35 \cdot 30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 103\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = (150\,\mathrm{N} - 103.0\,\mathrm{N}) \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{m} = 376\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 376.0\,\mathrm{J}}{30\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 5.01\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The box does not accelerate in the vertical direction, therefore the normal force equals the weight and the kinetic friction force has the magnitude $\mu_k m g = 103\,\mathrm{N}$, constant along the whole stretch.

The applied force does $+1.20 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$ and friction does $-8.24 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$, so the total work is $376\,\mathrm{J}$. The box starts from rest, hence the **work-energy theorem** gives $W_{\text{tot}} = \dfrac{1}{2}m v^2$ and the final speed is $5.01\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The applied force exceeds the friction force, therefore the total work is positive and the box speeds up. Had the applied force been $103\,\mathrm{N}$, the total work would have vanished and the box, once started, would have kept a constant speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 2.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ is released from rest on an inclined plane that makes the angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ with the horizontal, and it slides down the distance $L = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ along the surface. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k = 0.25$. Compute the speed of the block at the end of the descent.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,3.464;2.567,1.482 3.433,1.982 3.183,2.415 2.317,1.915" vettori="2.875,1.915,2.875,-0.185,P;2.875,1.915,1.975,3.474,N;2.875,1.915,4.173,2.665,f" linee="0,0 6,3.464" riferimento="6.6,0.3,30,1.2,x,y" etichette="0.78,0.12,30°" xmin=-0.6 xmax=8.4 ymin=-1.1 ymax=3.9 titolo="The block slides down the plane, hence $\vec{f}$ points up the plane; the component of the weight along the plane is $m g\sin 30^\circ$"}

$$
N = m g\cos\theta,
\qquad
f = \mu_k\,m g\cos\theta
$$
$$
W_P = m g L\sin\theta,
\qquad
W_N = 0,
\qquad
W_f = -\mu_k\,m g L\cos\theta
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = m g L\,(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta) = \frac{1}{2}m v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{2\,g L\,(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta)}
$$
$$
W_P = 2.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.500 = 49.1\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W_f = -0.25 \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.8660 = -21.2\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 27.81\,\mathrm{J}}{2.5\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 4.72\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The weight is resolved along the plane and perpendicular to it. The perpendicular direction is the one in which the block does not accelerate, hence $N = m g\cos\theta$ and the friction force has magnitude $\mu_k m g\cos\theta$.

The weight does positive work $m g L\sin\theta = 49.1\,\mathrm{J}$, since the block descends the height $L\sin\theta = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$; friction does negative work $-21.2\,\mathrm{J}$, since it points up the plane while the block moves down; the normal force does no work. The total work is $27.8\,\mathrm{J}$.

The block starts from rest, so the **work-energy theorem** gives $v = 4.72\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The mass cancels in the formula, therefore the result holds for any block with that coefficient of friction on that plane. Without friction the speed would have been $\sqrt{2 g L\sin\theta} = 6.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A puck of mass $m = 0.17\,\mathrm{kg}$ is launched on a horizontal ice rink with the speed $v_0 = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and stops after travelling $d = 95\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the coefficient of kinetic friction between the puck and the ice.

$$
W_{\text{tot}} = -\mu_k\,m g\,d,
\qquad
\Delta K = 0 - \frac{1}{2}m v_0^2
$$
$$
-\mu_k\,m g\,d = -\frac{1}{2}m v_0^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_k = \frac{v_0^2}{2\,g\,d}
$$
$$
\mu_k = \frac{(8.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 95\,\mathrm{m}} = 0.034
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Friction is the only force doing work, because the weight and the normal force are perpendicular to the horizontal displacement, and its work is $-\mu_k m g\,d$ along the whole stretch.

The puck stops, so the final kinetic energy is zero and the **work-energy theorem** gives the equality above. The mass cancels, hence the coefficient of friction can be obtained from the initial speed and the distance alone, without weighing the puck.

The value $\mu_k = 0.034$ is small, as expected for a puck on ice, and it explains why the puck covers almost one hundred metres before stopping.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A block of mass $m = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is launched up an inclined plane that makes the angle $\theta = 20^\circ$ with the horizontal, with the initial speed $v_0 = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ directed along the plane. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k = 0.30$. Compute the distance travelled along the plane before the block stops.

$$
W_P = -m g L\sin\theta,
\qquad
W_f = -\mu_k\,m g L\cos\theta,
\qquad
W_N = 0
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = -m g L\,(\sin\theta + \mu_k\cos\theta) = 0 - \frac{1}{2}m v_0^2
$$
$$
L = \frac{v_0^2}{2\,g\,(\sin\theta + \mu_k\cos\theta)}
$$
$$
\sin 20^\circ + 0.30\cos 20^\circ = 0.3420 + 0.2819 = 0.6239
$$
$$
L = \frac{(8.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.6239} = \frac{64\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{12.24\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 5.23\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block moves up the plane, therefore both the weight and friction oppose the motion and both do negative work. Friction points down the plane while the block rises, which is the opposite of the previous exercise, where the block was descending.

The two negative works add, and the **work-energy theorem** with zero final kinetic energy gives the distance $L = 5.23\,\mathrm{m}$. The kinetic energy removed is $160\,\mathrm{J}$, of which $87.7\,\mathrm{J}$ is taken by the weight and $72.3\,\mathrm{J}$ by friction.

Without friction the same launch would have carried the block $9.54\,\mathrm{m}$ along the plane, so friction reduces the distance by almost half.

</div>
