## Exercise 1

A body moves along the $x$ axis from $x_1 = 0$ to $x_2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ under a force whose component along the axis is $F_x(x) = a + b\,x$, with $a = 6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ and $b = 2.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Compute the work done by that force.

::grafico{f="6+2*x" area="6+2*x;0;0;4" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5 ymin=-0.9 ymax=16 nomi="x,F" titolo="The force grows from $6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ to $14\,\mathrm{N}$, and the work is the area of the shaded trapezoid"}

$$
W = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} F_x(x)\,dx = \int_{0}^{4.0} (a + b\,x)\,dx
$$
$$
W = \left[ a\,x + \frac{b\,x^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{4.0} = a\,x_2 + \frac{b\,x_2^2}{2}
$$
$$
W = 6.0\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m} + \frac{2.0\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (4.0\,\mathrm{m})^2}{2} = 24\,\mathrm{J} + 16\,\mathrm{J} = 40\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force changes along the path, therefore the definition of work for a constant force does not apply and the **work of a variable force** is computed as the integral of $F_x$ between the two positions.

The integrand is a polynomial of the first degree, so an antiderivative is $a x + \dfrac{b x^2}{2}$, and evaluating it at the two limits gives $40\,\mathrm{J}$.

The same value follows from the area under the graph, which is a trapezoid with parallel sides $F_x(0) = 6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ and $F_x(4.0) = 14\,\mathrm{N}$ and width $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$, hence of area $\dfrac{6.0 + 14}{2}\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m} = 40\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A spring of constant $k = 320\,\mathrm{N/m}$ lies along the $x$ axis with its free end at the origin when it is at its natural length. Compute the work done by the spring force while the free end is moved from $x = 0$ to $x = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, and the work done while it is moved from $x = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ to $x = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
F_x = -k\,x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} (-k\,x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}k\,x_1^2 - \frac{1}{2}k\,x_2^2
$$
$$
W_{0 \to 0.15} = 0 - \frac{1}{2} \cdot 320\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.15\,\mathrm{m})^2 = -3.6\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W_{0.15 \to 0.25} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 320\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot \left[ (0.15\,\mathrm{m})^2 - (0.25\,\mathrm{m})^2 \right] = -6.4\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring force obeys **Hooke's law**, therefore its component is $-k x$ and its work between two deformations is $\dfrac{1}{2}k x_1^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}k x_2^2$.

Both works are negative, because the spring is being stretched and the force points toward the origin while the displacement points away from it. The second stretch is $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ long against $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ for the first, and yet its work is larger in magnitude, since the force to be overcome in the second stretch is larger at every point.

The work done by the agent that stretches the spring slowly is opposite in sign, hence $+3.6\,\mathrm{J}$ and $+6.4\,\mathrm{J}$. The total work of the spring from $0$ to $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ is $-10\,\mathrm{J}$, which is $-\dfrac{1}{2}k\,(0.25\,\mathrm{m})^2$ as expected.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body moves along the $x$ axis from $x_1 = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ to $x_2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ under a force whose component is $F_x(x) = \dfrac{c}{x^2}$, with $c = 8.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2}$. Compute the work done by that force.

$$
W = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} \frac{c}{x^2}\,dx = \left[ -\frac{c}{x} \right]_{x_1}^{x_2} = c\left( \frac{1}{x_1} - \frac{1}{x_2} \right)
$$
$$
W = 8.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{1.0\,\mathrm{m}} - \frac{1}{4.0\,\mathrm{m}} \right) = 8.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2} \cdot 0.75\,\mathrm{m^{-1}} = 6.0\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force decreases as the inverse square of the distance, which is the dependence of the gravitational and of the electric force, and the integral of $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ has the antiderivative $-\dfrac{1}{x}$.

The work is positive because the force points in the sense of the motion over the whole stretch. Its value $6.0\,\mathrm{J}$ is well below the product $F_x(x_1)\,(x_2 - x_1) = 24\,\mathrm{J}$ that a constant force equal to the initial one would give, since the force at $x = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is only $\dfrac{1}{16}$ of the force at $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

The dimensions confirm the result, since $\mathrm{N\cdot m^2} \cdot \mathrm{m^{-1}} = \mathrm{N\cdot m} = \mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A body is pushed along the $x$ axis from $x_1 = 0$ to $x_2 = L = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ by a force whose component is $F_x(x) = F_0\sin\dfrac{\pi x}{L}$, with $F_0 = 20\,\mathrm{N}$. Compute the work done by that force and the constant force that would do the same work over the same distance.

::grafico{f="20*sin(pi*x/2)" area="20*sin(pi*x/2);0;0;2" xmin=-0.3 xmax=2.6 ymin=-1.5 ymax=24 nomi="x,F" titolo="The force vanishes at the two ends and reaches $F_0 = 20\,\mathrm{N}$ at the middle; the work is the shaded area"}

$$
W = \int_{0}^{L} F_0\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx = F_0\left[ -\frac{L}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi x}{L} \right]_{0}^{L}
$$
$$
W = \frac{F_0 L}{\pi}\,(1 - \cos\pi) = \frac{2\,F_0 L}{\pi}
$$
$$
W = \frac{2 \cdot 20\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m}}{\pi} = 25.5\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\overline{F} = \frac{W}{L} = \frac{25.46\,\mathrm{J}}{2.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 12.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument of the sine is dimensionless, as the principle of dimensional homogeneity requires, since $x$ and $L$ are both lengths. An antiderivative of $\sin\dfrac{\pi x}{L}$ is $-\dfrac{L}{\pi}\cos\dfrac{\pi x}{L}$, and evaluating it at the two limits gives $\dfrac{2 F_0 L}{\pi} = 25.5\,\mathrm{J}$.

The force vanishes at both ends and is largest at the middle, so the work is smaller than the product $F_0 L = 40\,\mathrm{J}$ that a force of constant magnitude $F_0$ would do.

The constant force doing the same work over the same distance is $\overline{F} = \dfrac{W}{L} = 12.7\,\mathrm{N}$, which is $\dfrac{2}{\pi}$ of the maximum, the average value of a sine over a half period.

</div>
