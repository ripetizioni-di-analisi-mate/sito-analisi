## Exercise 1

The motor of an elevator raises a cabin of total mass $m = 950\,\mathrm{kg}$ at the constant speed $v = 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute the power delivered by the cable and the work it does in $10\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
T - m g = m\,a
\quad\text{with}\quad
a = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = m g
$$
$$
P = \vec{T} \cdot \vec{v} = T\,v = m g v,
\qquad
W = P\,\Delta t
$$
$$
P = 950\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.68 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{W} = 16.8\,\mathrm{kW}
$$
$$
W = 1.6775 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{s} = 1.68 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cabin rises at constant speed, therefore its acceleration is zero and the tension of the cable equals the weight, $T = m g = 9.32 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$. The tension and the velocity are both directed upwards, hence the angle between them is zero and the **power of a force** is the product $T\,v$.

The power is constant, since neither the force nor the speed changes, so the work done in a time interval is the product of the power and that interval, $1.68 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$ in $10\,\mathrm{s}$.

The same work follows from the definition applied to the rise $h = v\,\Delta t = 18\,\mathrm{m}$, since $W = m g h = 1.68 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cyclist together with the bicycle has mass $m = 80\,\mathrm{kg}$ and delivers the constant power $P = 250\,\mathrm{W}$. On a horizontal road the total resistive force has the constant magnitude $f = 22\,\mathrm{N}$. Compute the speed the cyclist keeps on the horizontal road, and the speed kept on a road that rises at $4.0^\circ$ with the same resistive force.

$$
P = F\,v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \frac{P}{F}
$$
$$
F_{\text{flat}} = f,
\qquad
F_{\text{slope}} = f + m g\sin\theta
$$
$$
v_{\text{flat}} = \frac{250\,\mathrm{W}}{22\,\mathrm{N}} = 11.4\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
m g\sin 4.0^\circ = 80\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.06976 = 54.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
v_{\text{slope}} = \frac{250\,\mathrm{W}}{22\,\mathrm{N} + 54.74\,\mathrm{N}} = \frac{250\,\mathrm{W}}{76.74\,\mathrm{N}} = 3.26\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The speed is constant in both cases, therefore the resultant force vanishes and the propulsive force balances the forces opposing the motion. On the horizontal road the only such force is the resistive force $f$, so the propulsive force is $22\,\mathrm{N}$ and the relation $P = F v$ gives $v = 11.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

On the rising road the component of the weight along the direction of motion is $m g\sin\theta = 54.7\,\mathrm{N}$ and it points backwards, hence the propulsive force becomes $76.7\,\mathrm{N}$. With the same power available the speed drops to $3.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

A slope of $4.0^\circ$ adds a force more than twice the resistive force on the flat, and the speed falls to about one third, which is the usual experience on a climb.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pump raises water at the constant rate of $12\,\mathrm{kg}$ per second to a height of $18\,\mathrm{m}$, delivering the water with negligible speed. Compute the useful power of the pump and the power its motor absorbs, knowing that the efficiency of the machine is $0.75$.

$$
W = m g h
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_{\text{use}} = \frac{dW}{dt} = \frac{dm}{dt}\,g h
$$
$$
P_{\text{abs}} = \frac{P_{\text{use}}}{\eta}
$$
$$
P_{\text{use}} = 12\,\mathrm{kg/s} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 18\,\mathrm{m} = 2.12 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{W}
$$
$$
P_{\text{abs}} = \frac{2118.96\,\mathrm{W}}{0.75} = 2.83 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{W}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In each second a mass of $12\,\mathrm{kg}$ is raised by $18\,\mathrm{m}$, and the water arrives with negligible speed, hence the kinetic energy may be left out of the balance and the work needed in that second is $m g h$ with $m = 12\,\mathrm{kg}$. The useful power is that work divided by one second, which is the rate $\dfrac{dm}{dt}\,g h = 2.12 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{W}$.

The efficiency is the ratio of the useful power to the power absorbed, therefore the motor absorbs $\dfrac{P_{\text{use}}}{\eta} = 2.83 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{W}$. The difference of $7.1 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{W}$ is dissipated in the bearings and in the fluid.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A car of mass $m = 1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ travels on a horizontal road against a constant resistive force of magnitude $f = 500\,\mathrm{N}$ and accelerates with $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Compute the instantaneous power delivered by the propulsive force when the speed is $v = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
F - f = m\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F = m\,a + f
$$
$$
P = F\,v = (m\,a + f)\,v
$$
$$
F = 1200\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} + 500\,\mathrm{N} = 2.90 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
P = 2.90 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 15\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.35 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{W} = 43.5\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The car is accelerating, therefore the resultant force along the road is not zero and Newton's second law gives $F - f = m a$, from which the propulsive force is $2.90 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$. Of this force, $2.40 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$ produces the acceleration and $500\,\mathrm{N}$ balances the resistance.

The propulsive force and the velocity have the same direction and sense, hence $P = F v$, and at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the power is $43.5\,\mathrm{kW}$, which is about $58\,\mathrm{hp}$.

The result is an instantaneous value, since the speed is changing. At $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with the same force, the power required would be $5.80 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{W}$, so a car with a motor of limited power loses acceleration as the speed grows.

</div>
