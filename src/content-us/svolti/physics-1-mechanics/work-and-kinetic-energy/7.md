## Exercise 1

A block of mass $m = 0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pressed against a spring of constant $k = 450\,\mathrm{N/m}$, compressing it by $x = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$, and is then released on a horizontal frictionless surface. Compute the speed of the block at the instant it leaves the spring.

$$
W = \frac{1}{2}k\,x_1^2 - \frac{1}{2}k\,x_2^2
\quad\text{with}\quad
x_1 = -x,\ x_2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W = \frac{1}{2}k\,x^2
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = W = \frac{1}{2}m v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = x\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$
$$
W = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 450\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.12\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 3.24\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
v = 0.12\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{450\,\mathrm{N/m}}{0.60\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 0.12\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 27.39\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 3.29\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block starts at the compression $x$ and leaves the spring when the free end reaches its natural length, hence the deformation goes from $-x$ to $0$ and the **work done by the spring force** is $\dfrac{1}{2}k x^2 = 3.24\,\mathrm{J}$, positive because the spring pushes in the sense of the motion.

The surface is horizontal and frictionless, therefore the weight and the normal force do no work and the total work is the work of the spring alone. The block starts from rest, so the **work-energy theorem** gives $\dfrac{1}{2}m v^2 = W$ and the speed $3.29\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The dimensions of $\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ are $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathrm{N/m}}{\mathrm{kg}}} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, hence the product with a length is a speed, as required.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a horizontal frictionless surface at the speed $v = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and runs into a spring of constant $k = 800\,\mathrm{N/m}$ fixed to a wall. Compute the maximum compression of the spring.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 8.4,0;8.4,0 8.4,2.8;6,0.9 6.15,0.9 6.3,1.3 6.6,0.5 6.9,1.3 7.2,0.5 7.5,1.3 7.8,0.5 8.1,1.3 8.25,0.9 8.4,0.9" poligoni="4.4,0 5.6,0 5.6,1.6 4.4,1.6" vettori="4.6,2.1,6,2.1,v" segmenti="6,0,6,2.6" etichette="5.0,3.0,natural length" xmin=-0.4 xmax=9.8 ymin=-0.9 ymax=3.4 titolo="The block reaches the free end of the spring with speed $v$ and stops when the compression is $x$, after the spring has done the work $-\dfrac{1}{2}k\,x^2$"}

$$
W_{\text{spring}} = -\frac{1}{2}k\,x^2,
\qquad
\Delta K = 0 - \frac{1}{2}m v^2
$$
$$
-\frac{1}{2}k\,x^2 = -\frac{1}{2}m v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = v\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
x = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1.5\,\mathrm{kg}}{800\,\mathrm{N/m}}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.04330\,\mathrm{s} = 0.173\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The compression is largest when the block stops, because up to that instant the spring force opposes the motion and the block keeps slowing down. At that instant the final kinetic energy is zero.

The spring is compressed from $0$ to $-x$, therefore its work is $-\dfrac{1}{2}k x^2$, and the surface is frictionless, so this is the total work. The **work-energy theorem** then gives $\dfrac{1}{2}k x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}m v^2$, hence the maximum compression $x = 0.173\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, $17.3\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The initial kinetic energy is $12\,\mathrm{J}$ and the force the spring exerts at maximum compression is $k x = 139\,\mathrm{N}$, which is almost ten times the weight of the block.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The same block of mass $m = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ arrives at the spring of constant $k = 800\,\mathrm{N/m}$ with the speed $v = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, but the surface under the spring has coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k = 0.25$. Compute the maximum compression of the spring.

$$
W_{\text{tot}} = -\frac{1}{2}k\,x^2 - \mu_k\,m g\,x = 0 - \frac{1}{2}m v^2
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}k\,x^2 + \mu_k\,m g\,x - \frac{1}{2}m v^2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{-\mu_k\,m g + \sqrt{(\mu_k\,m g)^2 + k\,m v^2}}{k}
$$
$$
\mu_k\,m g = 0.25 \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.679\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
k\,m v^2 = 800\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 16\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = 1.92 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N^2}
$$
$$
x = \frac{-3.679\,\mathrm{N} + \sqrt{13.54\,\mathrm{N^2} + 19200\,\mathrm{N^2}}}{800\,\mathrm{N/m}} = \frac{-3.679\,\mathrm{N} + 138.6\,\mathrm{N}}{800\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 0.169\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two forces now do work during the compression: the spring force, with work $-\dfrac{1}{2}k x^2$, and kinetic friction, with work $-\mu_k m g\,x$, since the normal force equals the weight on the horizontal surface. Both works are negative, because both forces oppose the motion of the block.

The block stops at maximum compression, therefore the **work-energy theorem** gives an equation of the second degree in $x$. Only the positive root has physical meaning, since $x$ is a compression, and it gives $x = 0.169\,\mathrm{m}$.

Friction removes $0.620\,\mathrm{J}$ over this distance, which is about one twentieth of the initial kinetic energy, and the compression falls from $17.3\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $16.9\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A spring gun has a spring of constant $k = 1200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ compressed by $x = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and fires vertically upwards a ball of mass $m = 0.050\,\mathrm{kg}$. Air resistance is negligible. Compute the maximum height reached by the ball above its initial position.

$$
W_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}k\,x^2,
\qquad
W_P = -m g H
$$
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = \frac{1}{2}k\,x^2 - m g H = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H = \frac{k\,x^2}{2\,m g}
$$
$$
W_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1200\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.080\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 3.84\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
H = \frac{3.84\,\mathrm{J}}{0.050\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 7.83\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ball starts from rest at the compressed position and is momentarily at rest at the top of its flight, therefore the change in kinetic energy between these two instants is zero and the total work done on the ball vanishes.

Two forces do work during the flight: the spring, which does $\dfrac{1}{2}k x^2 = 3.84\,\mathrm{J}$ while the free end returns to its natural length, and the weight, which does $-m g H$ over the whole rise $H$ measured from the initial position, by the **work done by the weight**. Setting the sum equal to zero gives $H = 7.83\,\mathrm{m}$.

The ball leaves the spring with the speed obtained from $\dfrac{1}{2}k x^2 - m g x = \dfrac{1}{2}m v^2$, which gives $v = 12.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and from that point it rises a further $7.75\,\mathrm{m}$, so that the total rise is again $7.83\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
