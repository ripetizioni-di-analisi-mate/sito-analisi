## Exercise 1

A block of mass $m_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lies on a horizontal table with $\mu_k = 0.20$ and is connected by an ideal string, passing over an ideal pulley at the edge of the table, to a hanging block of mass $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. The system is released from rest. Compute the acceleration and the tension of the string.

$$
\begin{cases}
T - \mu_k\,m_1 g = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
m_2 g - T = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{m_2 - \mu_k m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,g,
\qquad
T = m_2\,(g - a)
$$
$$
a = \frac{3.0\,\mathrm{kg} - 0.20 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}}{7.0\,\mathrm{kg}} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = \frac{2.2}{7.0} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.08\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 3.083)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 20.2\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1,0 4.3,0;4.3,0 4.3,-1;1.9,0.3 4.3,0.3;4.6,0 4.6,-1.3" poligoni="0.7,0 1.9,0 1.9,1 0.7,1;4.2,-1.3 5,-1.3 5,-2.1 4.2,-2.1" curve="4.3+0.3*cos(t),0.3*sin(t),0,6.29" etichette="1.25,0.5,m₁;4.55,-1.7,m₂;2.9,0.6,T;5.0,-0.6,T;0.1,-0.45,table" xmin=-1.6 xmax=6.6 ymin=-3 ymax=2 titolo="Block on the table connected over an ideal pulley to a hanging block: the string is inextensible, hence both blocks have the same acceleration"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible, therefore the two blocks move with accelerations of equal magnitude $a$, and the string is massless and the pulley is ideal, therefore the tension is the same at the two ends. The positive sense is taken towards the motion of each block, that is away from the pulley for the hanging block and towards the pulley for the block on the table.

On the block on the table the vertical equation gives $N = m_1 g = 39.2\,\mathrm{N}$, hence the kinetic friction force is $\mu_k m_1 g = 7.85\,\mathrm{N}$ and it points away from the pulley, opposite to the motion. On the hanging block the forces are its weight and the tension.

Adding the two equations eliminates the tension and gives the acceleration of the system, $3.08\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The system behaves as a single body of mass $7.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ driven by the net external force $m_2 g - \mu_k m_1 g = 21.6\,\mathrm{N}$.

The second equation gives $T = 20.2\,\mathrm{N}$, a value smaller than the weight $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$ of the hanging block, as it has to be while that block accelerates downwards. The system starts from rest only if $m_2 g$ exceeds the largest static friction force, which the data satisfy for any $\mu_s$ below $0.75$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on top of a block of mass $m_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, which lies on a frictionless horizontal floor. The coefficient of static friction between the two blocks is $\mu_s = 0.40$. Compute the largest horizontal force that can be applied to the lower block with the two blocks still moving together.

$$
\text{together:}\quad
F = (m_1 + m_2)\,a
$$
$$
\text{upper block:}\quad
f_s = m_1\,a
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
f_s \le \mu_s\,m_1 g
$$
$$
m_1\,a \le \mu_s\,m_1 g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a_{\max} = \mu_s\,g = 0.40 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
F_{\max} = (m_1 + m_2)\,\mu_s\,g = 7.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.924\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 27.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The applied force acts on the lower block, while the upper block is dragged along. The floor is frictionless, hence the only horizontal force on the pair is the applied force, and treating the two blocks as a single body of mass $7.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives $a = \dfrac{F}{m_1 + m_2}$.

The upper block has no force applied to it directly, therefore the only horizontal force acting on it is the static friction exerted by the lower block, and that force alone must produce its acceleration: $f_s = m_1 a$. The two blocks press on each other with $N = m_1 g = 19.6\,\mathrm{N}$, hence the law of static friction requires $f_s \le \mu_s m_1 g$.

Combining the two relations, the mass $m_1$ cancels and the condition becomes $a \le \mu_s g$, therefore the largest common acceleration is $3.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ whatever the mass of the upper block. The corresponding force is $F_{\max} = 27.5\,\mathrm{N}$, and a larger force makes the lower block **slide** under the upper one.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lies on a plane inclined at $\theta = 30^\circ$, with $\mu_s = 0.20$ and $\mu_k = 0.15$, and is connected by an ideal string over an ideal pulley at the top of the plane to a hanging block of mass $m_2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. Determine whether the system moves and compute the acceleration and the tension.

$$
m_2 g \overset{?}{>} m_1 g\sin\theta + \mu_s\,m_1 g\cos\theta
$$
$$
39.2\,\mathrm{N} > 24.5\,\mathrm{N} + 8.50\,\mathrm{N} = 33.0\,\mathrm{N}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the system moves}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
T - m_1 g\sin\theta - \mu_k\,m_1 g\cos\theta = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
m_2 g - T = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = \frac{m_2 - m_1\sin\theta - \mu_k m_1\cos\theta}{m_1 + m_2}\,g
= \frac{4.0 - 2.50 - 0.6495}{9.0} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 0.927\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = m_2\,(g - a) = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 0.927)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 35.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sense of the motion is decided before the equations are written. If the system were at rest, the string would have to pull the hanging block up with $T = m_2 g = 39.2\,\mathrm{N}$ and hold the block on the plane with the same tension, against $m_1 g\sin\theta = 24.5\,\mathrm{N}$ down the slope. Static friction would have to supply the difference, $14.7\,\mathrm{N}$, while its largest value is $\mu_s m_1 g\cos\theta = 8.50\,\mathrm{N}$. That requirement is not met, therefore the hanging block descends and the block on the plane moves up the slope.

The block on the plane moves up, hence kinetic friction points down the slope, together with the component $m_1 g\sin\theta$ of the weight. The equation perpendicular to the plane gives $N = m_1 g\cos\theta = 42.5\,\mathrm{N}$, and the string is ideal, therefore the two equations share the same tension and the same acceleration.

Adding the two equations eliminates the tension and gives $a = 0.927\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, a small value because the driving weight exceeds the opposing forces by only $8.34\,\mathrm{N}$. The tension $35.5\,\mathrm{N}$ lies between $24.5\,\mathrm{N}$ and $39.2\,\mathrm{N}$, as it has to be while one block accelerates up the plane and the other accelerates downwards.

</div>
