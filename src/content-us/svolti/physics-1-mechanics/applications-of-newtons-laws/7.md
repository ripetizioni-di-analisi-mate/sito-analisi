## Exercise 1

A small body of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the ceiling of a train that accelerates horizontally with $A = 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The string settles at a constant angle $\theta$ with the vertical. Compute that angle and the tension of the string.

$$
\begin{cases}
T\sin\theta - m A = 0 \\[4pt]
T\cos\theta - m g = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tan\theta = \frac{A}{g},
\qquad
T = m\sqrt{g^2 + A^2}
$$
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{1.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 0.1835
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 10.4^\circ
$$
$$
T = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \sqrt{(9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2})^2 + (1.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2})^2} = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.974\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 4.99\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the frame of the train the body is at rest, hence its acceleration measured in that frame is zero. The frame accelerates with $\vec{A}$ with respect to the ground, therefore the inertial force $\vec{F}_{\text{in}} = -m\vec{A}$ is added to the real forces, and it has magnitude $m A = 0.90\,\mathrm{N}$ directed towards the rear of the train.

Three forces then balance in that frame: the weight, the tension and the inertial force. The horizontal equation gives $T\sin\theta = m A$ and the vertical equation gives $T\cos\theta = m g$. Dividing the first by the second eliminates the tension and gives $\tan\theta = \dfrac{A}{g} = 0.1835$, hence the string hangs at $10.4^\circ$ from the vertical, tilted **towards the rear**.

Squaring and adding the two equations gives $T = m\sqrt{g^2 + A^2} = 4.99\,\mathrm{N}$, slightly larger than the weight $4.91\,\mathrm{N}$. The angle does not contain the mass, therefore any object hanging in the same train settles at the same angle, and the device measures the acceleration of the train.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A box of mass $m = 10\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on the flat bed of a truck, with $\mu_s = 0.35$ and $\mu_k = 0.28$. Compute the largest acceleration of the truck with the box still at rest on the bed. Compute also the acceleration of the box relative to the truck when the truck accelerates at $A = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\text{box at rest on the bed:}\quad
f_s = m\,A
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
f_s \le \mu_s\,m g
$$
$$
A_{\max} = \mu_s\,g = 0.35 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.43\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
f_{s,\max} = 34.3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
A = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} > 3.43\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a_{\text{box}} = \mu_k\,g = 0.28 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.75\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a_{\text{rel}} = a_{\text{box}} - A = 2.747\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = -2.25\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bed is horizontal, hence the vertical equation gives $N = m g = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}$. While the box does not slide it has the acceleration of the truck, and the only horizontal force acting on it is the static friction exerted by the bed, therefore $f_s = m A$.

The law of static friction requires $f_s \le \mu_s m g$, hence $A \le \mu_s g = 3.43\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The mass cancels, therefore a heavy box and a light one of the same material begin to slide at the same acceleration of the truck, and the largest friction force is $34.3\,\mathrm{N}$.

At $A = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ the required friction exceeds the available static friction, hence the box slides backwards on the bed and the friction force becomes kinetic, with the fixed value $\mu_k m g = 27.5\,\mathrm{N}$. That force alone acts on the box in the ground frame, therefore its acceleration is $\mu_k g = 2.75\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, smaller than the acceleration of the truck.

The acceleration of the box relative to the truck is the difference of the two accelerations, that is $2.25\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ directed **towards the rear** of the bed. In the frame of the truck the same result follows from the inertial force $m A = 50\,\mathrm{N}$ directed towards the rear, opposed by the kinetic friction of $27.5\,\mathrm{N}$ directed forwards, whose sum is $22.5\,\mathrm{N}$ towards the rear.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A small block of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a horizontal turntable at a distance $r = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis, with $\mu_s = 0.40$. Compute the largest rotation rate, in revolutions per minute, at which the block does not slide, and the friction force at that rate.

$$
f_s = m\,\omega^2 r
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
f_s \le \mu_s\,m g
$$
$$
m\,\omega^2 r \le \mu_s\,m g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_s\,g}{r}}
$$
$$
\omega_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{0.40 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{0.25\,\mathrm{m}}} = \sqrt{15.70\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}} = 3.96\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
n_{\max} = \frac{\omega_{\max}}{2\pi} \cdot 60\,\frac{\mathrm{s}}{\mathrm{min}} = \frac{3.962}{6.283} \cdot 60\,\mathrm{min^{-1}} = 37.8\,\mathrm{rev/min}
$$
$$
f_s = \mu_s\,m g = 0.40 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.96\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block travels a horizontal circle at constant speed together with the turntable, hence its acceleration is directed towards the axis and equals $\omega^2 r$. The turntable is horizontal, therefore the weight and the normal force cancel and the only horizontal force is the static friction exerted by the surface, which must point towards the axis.

Newton's second law along the radius gives $f_s = m\omega^2 r$, and the law of static friction requires $f_s \le \mu_s m g$. The mass cancels, hence the condition becomes $\omega^2 r \le \mu_s g$ and the largest angular speed is $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mu_s g}{r}} = 3.96\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, which is $37.8$ revolutions per minute.

At that rate the friction force has reached its largest value $\mu_s m g = 1.96\,\mathrm{N}$, equal to $m\omega_{\max}^2 r$ as the two expressions require. A block placed farther from the axis slides at a lower rate, since $\omega_{\max}$ falls as $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}}$.

In the frame rotating with the turntable the block is at rest, and the same condition follows from the balance between the inertial force $m\omega^2 r$ directed outwards and the friction force directed inwards.

</div>
