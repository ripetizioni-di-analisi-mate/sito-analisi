## Exercise 1

A block of mass $m = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lies on a plane inclined at $\theta = 20^\circ$, with $\mu_s = 0.45$. Determine whether the block slides and compute the friction force acting on it.

$$
\tan\theta \le \mu_s
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the block stays at rest}
$$
$$
\tan 20^\circ = 0.364 < 0.45
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
f_s - m g\sin\theta = 0 \\[4pt]
N - m g\cos\theta = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f_s = m g\sin\theta,
\qquad
N = m g\cos\theta
$$
$$
f_s = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot \sin 20^\circ = 16.8\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
f_{s,\max} = \mu_s\,m g\cos\theta = 0.45 \cdot 49.05\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \cos 20^\circ = 20.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axes are taken along the plane and perpendicular to it, so that the normal force lies on one axis and the friction force on the other, while the weight splits into $m g\sin\theta$ down the slope and $m g\cos\theta$ into the plane.

The angle of repose for $\mu_s = 0.45$ is $\arctan 0.45 = 24.2^\circ$, and the plane is inclined at $20^\circ$, therefore the condition $\tan\theta \le \mu_s$ is satisfied and the block **stays at rest**. Its acceleration is zero, hence both equilibrium equations hold.

The equation along the plane gives the friction force actually exerted, $f_s = m g\sin\theta = 16.8\,\mathrm{N}$, and the equation perpendicular to the plane gives $N = 46.1\,\mathrm{N}$, from which $f_{s,\max} = \mu_s N = 20.7\,\mathrm{N}$. The required value is smaller than the maximum, which confirms the conclusion. Writing $f_s = \mu_s N = 20.7\,\mathrm{N}$ would contradict equilibrium, since the block would then accelerate up the slope.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 12\,\mathrm{kg}$ is released from rest on a plane inclined at $\theta = 35^\circ$, with $\mu_k = 0.25$. Compute its acceleration and the speed it reaches after sliding $d = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ along the plane.

$$
\begin{cases}
m g\sin\theta - \mu_k N = m\,a \\[4pt]
N - m g\cos\theta = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = g\,(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta)
$$
$$
a = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (\sin 35^\circ - 0.25\cos 35^\circ) = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (0.5736 - 0.2048) = 3.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2\,a\,d} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 3.618\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 4.66\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 8,0 8,5.602" punti="5.7,4.05,pieno" vettori="5.7,4.05,5.7,0.85,P;5.7,4.05,4.197,6.197,N;5.7,4.05,6.237,4.426,f" riferimento="1.3,4.4,35,1.7,x,y" etichette="1.2,0.3,35°" xmin=-0.6 xmax=9.6 ymin=-0.9 ymax=7.3 titolo="Free-body diagram of a block sliding down a plane inclined at $35^\circ$: the friction force points up the slope, opposite to the velocity"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $\tan 35^\circ = 0.700 > 0.25$, the block cannot remain at rest, therefore it slides down and the friction force is kinetic. The velocity points down the slope, hence the friction force points up the slope, opposite to it.

The block does not leave the surface, so the equation perpendicular to the plane gives $N = m g\cos\theta$. Substituting that value into the equation along the plane and dividing by $m$ gives $a = g(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta) = 3.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The mass has cancelled, therefore the result holds for any block of the same materials.

The acceleration is constant, because both $m g \sin\theta$ and $\mu_k m g\cos\theta$ are constant, hence the motion is **uniformly accelerated** from rest and the relation $v^2 = 2 a d$ applies. The speed after $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is therefore $4.66\,\mathrm{m/s}$, against the $5.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$ that a frictionless plane would give.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pushed up a plane inclined at $\theta = 25^\circ$ by a force parallel to the plane, with $\mu_k = 0.30$, and moves at constant speed. Compute the magnitude of the force. Compute also the magnitude of the deceleration of the block if the force is removed while it is still moving up.

$$
\begin{cases}
F - m g\sin\theta - \mu_k N = 0 \\[4pt]
N - m g\cos\theta = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F = m g\,(\sin\theta + \mu_k\cos\theta)
$$
$$
F = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (\sin 25^\circ + 0.30\cos 25^\circ) = 58.86\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.6945 = 40.9\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
-\,m g\sin\theta - \mu_k\,m g\cos\theta = m\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|a| = g\,(\sin\theta + \mu_k\cos\theta)
$$
$$
|a| = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.6945 = 6.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block moves up the plane, hence the friction force points down the plane, in the same sense as the component $m g\sin\theta$ of the weight. The speed is constant, therefore the acceleration is zero and the applied force balances both: $F = m g\sin\theta + \mu_k m g\cos\theta = 40.9\,\mathrm{N}$. Of that force, $24.9\,\mathrm{N}$ balances the weight component and $16.0\,\mathrm{N}$ balances friction.

When the force is removed the block keeps moving up for a while, hence the friction force still points down the plane. Both remaining forces along the plane are therefore directed down the slope, and Newton's second law gives the deceleration $g(\sin\theta + \mu_k\cos\theta) = 6.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Once the block stops, the sense of the impending motion reverses and the friction force reverses with it. Since $\tan 25^\circ = 0.466 > 0.30$, the condition of the angle of repose is not satisfied, therefore the block **slides back down**, with acceleration $g(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta) = 1.48\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

</div>
