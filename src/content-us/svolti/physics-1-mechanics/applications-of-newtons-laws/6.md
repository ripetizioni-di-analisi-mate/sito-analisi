## Exercise 1

A curve of radius $r = 80\,\mathrm{m}$ is banked so that a vehicle travels it at $v = 22\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with no sideways friction. Compute the bank angle and the normal force exerted on a car of mass $m = 1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ traveling at that speed.

$$
\begin{cases}
N\cos\theta - m g = 0 \\[8pt]
N\sin\theta = m\,\dfrac{v^2}{r}
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{g\,r},
\qquad
N = \frac{m g}{\cos\theta}
$$
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{(22\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 80\,\mathrm{m}} = 0.6167
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 31.7^\circ
$$
$$
N = \frac{1200\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{\cos 31.7^\circ} = \frac{11772\,\mathrm{N}}{0.8510} = 1.38 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,3.70" punti="3.6,2.3,pieno" vettori="3.6,2.3,2.146,4.653,N;3.6,2.3,3.6,-0.054,P;3.6,2.3,2.148,2.3,F" etichette="0.95,0.22,θ" xmin=-0.6 xmax=7 ymin=-0.9 ymax=5.2 titolo="Car on a curve banked at $\theta$, with the arrows drawn to scale ($5\,\mathrm{kN}$ per unit): the sum of $\vec{N}$ and $\vec{P}$ is horizontal and points towards the center of the curve"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The road exerts no sideways friction, hence the only forces on the car are its weight, vertical and of magnitude $m g$, and the normal force, perpendicular to the road surface and therefore tilted by $\theta$ from the vertical. The car travels a horizontal circle at constant speed, so its acceleration is horizontal, directed towards the center of the curve and equal to $\dfrac{v^2}{r}$.

The vertical axis is taken upwards and the horizontal axis towards the center. The vertical acceleration is zero, therefore $N\cos\theta = m g$, while the horizontal equation gives $N\sin\theta = m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$. Dividing the second by the first eliminates $N$ and $m$, hence $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{g r}$ and the bank angle is $31.7^\circ$.

The angle does not depend on the mass, therefore the same bank serves cars and lorries, and it is designed for one speed alone. The normal force follows from the vertical equation, $N = \dfrac{m g}{\cos\theta} = 1.38 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$, which exceeds the weight $1.18 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$ by $17\%$, since the road has to supply a horizontal component as well.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A vertical circular loop of a roller coaster has radius $r = 8.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the smallest speed at the top of the loop that keeps the car in contact with the track, and compute the force exerted by the seat on a rider of mass $m = 60\,\mathrm{kg}$ when the car passes the top at $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
N + m g = m\,\frac{v^2}{r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N = m\left(\frac{v^2}{r} - g\right)
$$
$$
N \ge 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_{\min} = \sqrt{g\,r} = \sqrt{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 8.86\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
N = 60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left(\frac{(12\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{8.0\,\mathrm{m}} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\right) = 60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (18.0 - 9.81)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 491\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the top of the loop the center of the circle lies vertically below the rider, hence the radial axis points downwards. The weight points downwards, and the seat is above the rider and can only push downwards, therefore both forces are directed towards the center and Newton's second law along the radius gives $N + m g = m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$.

The seat can push but cannot pull, hence $N \ge 0$ is required, and that condition holds if and only if $\dfrac{v^2}{r} \ge g$. The smallest speed at the top is therefore $\sqrt{g r} = 8.86\,\mathrm{m/s}$, independent of the mass. At that speed the seat exerts no force and the weight alone supplies the net force towards the center.

At $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the required net force is $m\dfrac{v^2}{r} = 1080\,\mathrm{N}$, of which the weight supplies $589\,\mathrm{N}$, hence the seat supplies the remaining $491\,\mathrm{N}$. The rider is **pressed into the seat** at the top, although both the seat and the rider are upside down.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ball of mass $m = 0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to a string of length $r = 0.70\,\mathrm{m}$ and swings in a vertical circle. Compute the tension of the string at the lowest point, where the speed is $v = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the smallest speed at the highest point that keeps the string taut.

$$
\text{lowest point:}\quad
T - m g = m\,\frac{v^2}{r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = m\left(\frac{v^2}{r} + g\right)
$$
$$
T = 0.30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left(\frac{(5.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{0.70\,\mathrm{m}} + 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\right) = 0.30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (35.71 + 9.81)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 13.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\text{highest point:}\quad
T + m g = m\,\frac{v^2}{r},
\qquad
T \ge 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_{\min} = \sqrt{g\,r}
$$
$$
v_{\min} = \sqrt{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.70\,\mathrm{m}} = 2.62\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the lowest point the center of the circle lies vertically above the ball, hence the radial axis points upwards. The tension points upwards, towards the center, and the weight points downwards, therefore the radial equation is $T - m g = m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ and the tension is $13.7\,\mathrm{N}$, more than four times the weight $2.94\,\mathrm{N}$.

At the highest point the center lies below the ball, hence the radial axis points downwards and both the tension and the weight point towards the center. The string is ideal, so it can pull but cannot push, therefore $T \ge 0$ and the smallest speed is $\sqrt{g r} = 2.62\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The tension varies around the circle, with its largest value at the bottom and its smallest at the top. The speed also varies, because the weight has a component along the velocity everywhere except at the top and at the bottom, and the chapter on potential energy and conservation of energy relates the two speeds.

</div>
