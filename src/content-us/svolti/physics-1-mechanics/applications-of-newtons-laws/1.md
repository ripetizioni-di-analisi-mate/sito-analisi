## Exercise 1

A crate of mass $m = 30\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a horizontal floor, with coefficients of friction $\mu_s = 0.50$ and $\mu_k = 0.35$. Determine whether a horizontal push of $F_1 = 120\,\mathrm{N}$ sets the crate in motion, and compute the acceleration produced by a horizontal push of $F_2 = 180\,\mathrm{N}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
F - f = m\,a \\[4pt]
N - m g = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N = m g
$$
$$
f_{s,\max} = \mu_s\,m g = 0.50 \cdot 30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 147\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_1 = 120\,\mathrm{N} < 147\,\mathrm{N}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = 0,
\qquad
f_s = F_1 = 120\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_2 = 180\,\mathrm{N} > 147\,\mathrm{N}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f_k = \mu_k\,m g = 0.35 \cdot 294.3\,\mathrm{N} = 103.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
a = \frac{F_2 - f_k}{m} = \frac{180\,\mathrm{N} - 103.0\,\mathrm{N}}{30\,\mathrm{kg}} = 2.57\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Four forces act on the crate: its weight, the normal force of the floor, the push and the friction force. The push is horizontal, hence it has no vertical component, and the crate does not leave the floor, therefore the equation along the vertical axis gives $N = m g = 294.3\,\mathrm{N}$.

The law of static friction gives the largest tangential force the contact can sustain, $f_{s,\max} = \mu_s N = 147\,\mathrm{N}$. The push of $120\,\mathrm{N}$ is smaller than that value, therefore the crate **stays at rest** and static friction takes the value required by equilibrium, namely $120\,\mathrm{N}$, which is smaller than its maximum.

The push of $180\,\mathrm{N}$ exceeds $147\,\mathrm{N}$, hence the crate slides and the friction force becomes kinetic, with the value $\mu_k N = 103.0\,\mathrm{N}$ fixed by the normal force alone. Newton's second law along the direction of motion then gives the acceleration $2.57\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The friction force drops from $147\,\mathrm{N}$ at the instant before sliding to $103\,\mathrm{N}$ just after, which is why a crate starts moving abruptly.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lies on a horizontal floor with $\mu_k = 0.30$ and is pulled by a rope that exerts a force of magnitude $F = 40\,\mathrm{N}$ directed at $\alpha = 25^\circ$ above the horizontal. Compute the normal force and the acceleration of the block.

$$
\begin{cases}
F\cos\alpha - \mu_k N = m\,a \\[4pt]
N + F\sin\alpha - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
N = m g - F\sin\alpha,
\qquad
a = \frac{F\cos\alpha - \mu_k\,(m g - F\sin\alpha)}{m}
$$
$$
N = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 40\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \sin 25^\circ = 78.48\,\mathrm{N} - 16.91\,\mathrm{N} = 61.6\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
f_k = 0.30 \cdot 61.6\,\mathrm{N} = 18.5\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F\cos\alpha = 40\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \cos 25^\circ = 36.3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
a = \frac{36.3\,\mathrm{N} - 18.5\,\mathrm{N}}{8.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 2.22\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno" vettori="0,0,1.8126,0.8452,F;0,0,-0.9236,0,f;0,0,0,3.0788,N;0,0,0,-3.924,P" segmenti="1.8126,0,1.8126,0.8452" etichette="1.02,0.06,25°" riferimento="-4.6,-2.6,0,1.1,x,y" xmin=-4.6 xmax=4.6 ymin=-4.7 ymax=4.3 titolo="Free-body diagram of the block, with the arrows drawn to scale ($20\,\mathrm{N}$ per unit): the vertical component of $\vec{F}$ reduces the normal force and therefore the friction force"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The rope pulls obliquely, hence its force is resolved along the two axes: the component $F\cos\alpha$ is horizontal and drives the block forward, the component $F\sin\alpha$ is vertical and lifts part of its weight. The block stays on the floor, therefore its vertical acceleration is zero and the equation along the vertical axis gives $N = m g - F\sin\alpha = 61.6\,\mathrm{N}$, a value smaller than the weight $78.5\,\mathrm{N}$.

Kinetic friction is proportional to the normal force, hence the tilt of the rope reduces the friction force to $18.5\,\mathrm{N}$, while a horizontal rope of the same magnitude would give $f_k = 0.30 \cdot 78.48\,\mathrm{N} = 23.5\,\mathrm{N}$. The horizontal equation then gives the acceleration $2.22\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The tilt has two opposite effects, since it reduces the useful component of the force and it reduces the friction force as well. The second effect prevails here, therefore the oblique pull is **more effective** than a horizontal pull of the same magnitude, which would give $a = \dfrac{40\,\mathrm{N} - 23.5\,\mathrm{N}}{8.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 2.06\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A box slides across a horizontal floor with initial speed $v_0 = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and stops after $d = 4.5\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the coefficient of kinetic friction and the distance the box would cover with an initial speed of $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
-\mu_k\,m g = m\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = -\mu_k\,g
$$
$$
v^2 = v_0^2 - 2\,\mu_k\,g\,d
\quad\text{with}\quad
v = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_k = \frac{v_0^2}{2\,g\,d}
$$
$$
\mu_k = \frac{(6.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 4.5\,\mathrm{m}} = 0.41
$$
$$
d' = \frac{v_0'^2}{2\,\mu_k\,g} = \frac{(12\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 0.4077 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 18\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

No horizontal force other than friction acts on the box, hence the equation along the vertical axis gives $N = m g$ and the equation along the direction of motion gives $a = -\mu_k g$. The acceleration is constant, therefore the relation between speed and distance of uniformly accelerated motion applies, with final speed zero.

Solving that relation for $\mu_k$ gives $0.41$, and the mass has cancelled, hence the measurement of $v_0$ and $d$ determines the coefficient without any weighing. The result is consistent with wood sliding on wood.

The stopping distance is proportional to the square of the initial speed, since $d = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2\mu_k g}$ and the other quantities are unchanged. Doubling the initial speed therefore multiplies the distance by four, and the box travels $18\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
