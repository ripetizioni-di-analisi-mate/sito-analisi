## Exercise 1

A stone of mass $m = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ is tied to a string of length $L = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and travels a horizontal circle on a frictionless table, completing $n = 2.0$ revolutions per second. Compute the tension of the string.

$$
T = m\,\frac{v^2}{r} = m\,\omega^2 r
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
r = L,
\quad
\omega = 2\pi n
$$
$$
\omega = 2\pi \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
T = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m} = 50.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The table is frictionless and horizontal, hence the weight of the stone is balanced by the normal force of the table and the only horizontal force is the tension of the string, directed from the stone toward the center of the circle. The speed is constant, therefore the motion is uniform circular motion and the net force equals $m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ pointing toward the center.

The string is inextensible, so the radius equals its length, and the stone completes $2.0$ revolutions per second, hence the angular speed is $\omega = 2\pi n = 12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Writing the net force as $m\omega^2 r$ avoids computing the speed, and the **tension** is $50.5\,\mathrm{N}$.

The tension is about thirteen times the weight $3.92\,\mathrm{N}$ of the stone, which is why a string that holds the stone at rest can break when the stone is whirled.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car of mass $m = 1100\,\mathrm{kg}$ travels a flat horizontal curve of radius $r = 65\,\mathrm{m}$ at the constant speed $v = 18\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute the friction force exerted by the road on the car and the smallest coefficient of static friction that makes the curve possible.

$$
\begin{cases}
f_s = m\,\dfrac{v^2}{r} \\[8pt]
N - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f_s = \frac{1100\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (18\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{65\,\mathrm{m}} = 5.48 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
f_s \le \mu_s\,m g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_{s,\min} = \frac{v^2}{g\,r} = \frac{(18\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 65\,\mathrm{m}} = 0.51
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The road is horizontal, hence the weight and the normal force are both vertical and cancel, so $N = m g = 1.08 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$. The car has no vertical acceleration and travels a circle at constant speed, therefore its acceleration is horizontal, directed toward the center of the curve and equal to $\dfrac{v^2}{r}$.

The tyres do not slide sideways, hence the force that produces that acceleration is static friction, and Newton's second law along the radius gives $f_s = m\dfrac{v^2}{r} = 5.48 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$.

The law of static friction requires $f_s \le \mu_s N$, therefore the curve is possible only if $\mu_s \ge \dfrac{v^2}{g r} = 0.51$. The mass cancels in that ratio, hence the condition is the same for a light and a heavy vehicle. Dry asphalt offers about $0.8$ and satisfies it, while an icy road offers about $0.1$ and does not.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ball of mass $m = 0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from a string of length $L = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ and travels a horizontal circle with the string making a constant angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ with the vertical. Compute the tension of the string, the speed of the ball and the period of the motion.

$$
\begin{cases}
T\cos\theta - m g = 0 \\[8pt]
T\sin\theta = m\,\dfrac{v^2}{r}
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
r = L\sin\theta
$$
$$
T = \frac{m g}{\cos\theta} = \frac{0.25\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{\cos 30^\circ} = 2.83\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
v = \sin\theta\sqrt{\frac{g\,L}{\cos\theta}} = 0.500 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.90\,\mathrm{m}}{0.866}} = 1.60\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\mathcal{T} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L\cos\theta}{g}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.90\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.866}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 1.77\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno" vettori="0,0,-1.415,2.451,T;0,0,0,-2.4525,P;0,0,-1.4159,0,F" segmenti="0,0,0,2.451" etichette="-0.55,1.9,30°" riferimento="2.5,-2.4,0,1,x,y" xmin=-4.3 xmax=4.3 ymin=-3.5 ymax=3.5 titolo="Free-body diagram of the ball, with the arrows drawn to scale ($1\,\mathrm{N}$ per unit): the horizontal component of $\vec{T}$ is the net force $m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ toward the center"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two forces act on the ball, its weight and the tension of the string, and the ball travels a horizontal circle at constant speed. Its acceleration is therefore horizontal, directed toward the vertical axis of the circle, and equal to $\dfrac{v^2}{r}$. The vertical axis is taken upwards and the horizontal axis toward the center.

The vertical acceleration is zero, hence the equation along the vertical gives $T\cos\theta = m g$ and the tension is $2.83\,\mathrm{N}$, larger than the weight $2.45\,\mathrm{N}$ because only part of the tension is vertical. The equation along the horizontal gives $T\sin\theta = m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$, and the radius of the circle is $r = L\sin\theta = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$.

Dividing the second equation by the first eliminates the tension and gives $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{g r}$, from which the speed is $1.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The period is the length of the circle divided by the speed, and substituting $r = L\sin\theta$ gives $\mathcal{T} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L\cos\theta}{g}} = 1.77\,\mathrm{s}$. The mass does not appear in that expression, therefore two balls of different mass on strings of the same length turn with the same period at the same angle.

</div>
