## Exercise 1

Compute the work required to raise a payload of mass $m = 2000\,\mathrm{kg}$ from the surface of the Earth to the altitude $h = 500\,\mathrm{km}$, with the payload at rest at the start and at the end. Compare the result with the value given by the formula $mgh$ with $g = 9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Use $GM_E = 3.984 \times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ and $R_E = 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
W = \Delta U = U(r) - U(R_E) = -\frac{GM_E m}{r} + \frac{GM_E m}{R_E}
= GM_E m\left(\frac{1}{R_E} - \frac{1}{r}\right),
\qquad
r = R_E + h
$$
$$
W = 3.984 \times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2} \cdot 2000\,\mathrm{kg} \left(\frac{1}{6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}} - \frac{1}{6.87 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}}\right)
$$
$$
W = 7.968 \times 10^{17}\,\mathrm{J\cdot m} \cdot 1.143 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}} = 9.10 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
m g h = 2000\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 5.00 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m} = 9.82 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
\frac{mgh}{W} = 1.079
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The payload starts at rest and ends at rest, therefore its kinetic energy does not change and the work of the external agent equals the increase in potential energy. The potential energy of the pair Earth-payload is $-\dfrac{GM_Em}{r}$, hence the work is the difference of two such terms.

The result $9.10 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$ is positive, as it must be, since separating two masses requires work. The elementary estimate $mgh$ gives $9.82 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$, which exceeds the exact value by $7.9\%$, because it treats the field as constant at its surface value while the field actually decreases with altitude.

The altitude $500\,\mathrm{km}$ is about $8\%$ of the radius of the Earth, hence the two expressions already differ appreciably. For an altitude of a few hundred meters the relative difference drops below one part in $10^{4}$, and the formula $mgh$ is the one to use.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body is released from rest at the distance $r_0 = 3R_E$ from the center of the Earth and falls freely. Compute the speed with which it reaches the surface, neglecting air resistance.

$$
\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 - \frac{GM_E m}{r_0} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{GM_E m}{R_E},
\qquad v_0 = 0
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}v^2 = \frac{GM_E}{R_E} - \frac{GM_E}{3R_E} = \frac{2}{3}\,\frac{GM_E}{R_E}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{\frac{4\,GM_E}{3\,R_E}}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{4 \cdot 3.984 \times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}}{3 \cdot 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}}}
= \sqrt{8.339 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 9.13 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Gravitation is the only force doing work, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved between the starting point and the surface. The body starts at rest, hence its initial kinetic energy is zero and the whole initial energy is the potential energy $-\dfrac{GM_Em}{3R_E}$.

Writing the conservation and isolating $v^2$ gives $\dfrac{1}{2}v^2 = \dfrac{GM_E}{R_E} - \dfrac{GM_E}{3R_E}$, where the mass of the body cancels, so the impact speed does not depend on it. The numerical value is $9.13\,\mathrm{km/s}$.

The field is far from constant over a fall of $2R_E$, therefore the formula $v = \sqrt{2gh}$ does not apply here; using it with $h = 2R_E$ would give $1.58 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is too large by $73\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A projectile is fired vertically upward from the surface of the Earth with the speed $v_0 = 5.00\,\mathrm{km/s}$. Compute the maximum distance it reaches from the center of the Earth and the corresponding altitude, and compare the altitude with the value given by the constant-field formula $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2g}$.

$$
\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 - \frac{GM_E m}{R_E} = -\frac{GM_E m}{r_{\max}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{GM_E}{r_{\max}} = \frac{GM_E}{R_E} - \frac{1}{2}v_0^2
$$
$$
\frac{GM_E}{R_E} = \frac{3.984 \times 10^{14}}{6.37 \times 10^{6}}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = 6.254 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2},
\qquad
\frac{1}{2}v_0^2 = 1.250 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}
$$
$$
r_{\max} = \frac{3.984 \times 10^{14}}{5.004 \times 10^{7}}\,\mathrm{m} = 7.96 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_{\max} = r_{\max} - R_E = 1.59 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{v_0^2}{2g} = \frac{(5.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 1.27 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the highest point the velocity vanishes, therefore the kinetic energy there is zero and the whole mechanical energy is potential. Conservation of mechanical energy between the surface and that point gives the relation written above, in which the mass of the projectile cancels.

The quantity $\dfrac{GM_E}{R_E} = 6.254 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ exceeds $\dfrac{1}{2}v_0^2$, hence the right-hand side is positive and a finite $r_{\max}$ exists, which means the motion is **bound**. The projectile rises to $1.59 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$ above the ground and falls back.

The constant-field formula gives $1.27 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$, that is $20\%$ less, because it keeps the field at its surface value along the whole flight while the real field weakens with altitude and allows the projectile to rise farther. The two results converge when $v_0$ is small enough that $h_{\max} \ll R_E$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Three spheres of mass $m = 30\,\mathrm{kg}$ each occupy the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side $a = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the gravitational potential energy of the system and the work required to separate the three spheres to infinite mutual distance.

$$
U = -G\left(\frac{m^2}{a} + \frac{m^2}{a} + \frac{m^2}{a}\right) = -\,\frac{3\,G m^2}{a}
$$
$$
U = -\,\frac{3 \cdot 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2} \cdot (30\,\mathrm{kg})^2}{0.50\,\mathrm{m}} = -3.60 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
W = U_\infty - U = 0 - U = 3.60 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The potential energy of a system of masses is the sum over the **pairs**, since each term $-\dfrac{Gm_im_j}{r_{ij}}$ belongs to one pair of bodies. Three spheres form three pairs, and in an equilateral triangle all three mutual distances equal $a$, therefore the three terms are equal and the sum is $-\dfrac{3Gm^2}{a}$.

The work required to bring the three spheres to infinite mutual distance, leaving them at rest, equals the increase of potential energy. The potential energy at infinite separation is zero by the chosen reference, hence that work is $-U = 3.60 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}$.

The system is bound, as the negative value of $U$ records, and its binding energy is that same $3.60 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}$. Doubling the side would halve both numbers, since $U$ is inversely proportional to the distance and not to its square.

</div>
