## Exercise 1

Mars has mass $M = 6.42 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 3.39 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the gravitational field at its surface and the weight of an astronaut of mass $m = 70\,\mathrm{kg}$ standing on it.

$$
g_0 = \frac{GM}{R^2},
\qquad
P = m\,g_0
$$
$$
g_0 = \frac{6.674 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2} \cdot 6.42 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{kg}}{(3.39 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m})^2}
= \frac{4.285 \times 10^{13}}{1.149 \times 10^{13}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
= 3.73\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
P = 70\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.73\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.6 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mars has spherical symmetry, hence by the **shell theorem** its field at the surface is the field of a particle of mass $M$ at the distance $R$, which gives $g_0 = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$. Substituting the data gives $3.73\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, about $38\%$ of the terrestrial value.

The weight of the astronaut is the force that Mars exerts on them, $P = mg_0 = 2.6 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$, against the $687\,\mathrm{N}$ they would weigh on the Earth. The mass is a property of the body alone, therefore it is the same $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ in both places, while the weight depends on the planet.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the Earth, with $R_E = 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $g_0 = 9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, compute the altitude at which the gravitational field is half its surface value, and the value of the field at the altitude $h = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$.

$$
g(h) = \frac{g_0}{\left(1 + \dfrac{h}{R_E}\right)^{2}}
$$
$$
g(h) = \frac{g_0}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\left(1 + \frac{h}{R_E}\right)^{2} = 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h = R_E\left(\sqrt{2} - 1\right)
$$
$$
h = 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.4142 = 2.64 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.64 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
g(1.00 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}) = \frac{9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{\left(1 + \dfrac{1.00 \times 10^{6}}{6.37 \times 10^{6}}\right)^{2}}
= \frac{9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{(1.157)^{2}} = 7.34\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field at altitude $h$ is the field of a particle of mass $M_E$ at the distance $R_E + h$ from the center, therefore it is obtained from the surface value by dividing by $\left(1 + \dfrac{h}{R_E}\right)^{2}$. Imposing that this quotient equals $\dfrac{g_0}{2}$ gives the equation $\left(1 + \dfrac{h}{R_E}\right)^{2} = 2$, whose positive root is $1 + \dfrac{h}{R_E} = \sqrt{2}$.

The altitude that halves the field is therefore $2.64 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$, which is more than one third of the radius of the Earth. At the much smaller altitude of $1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$ the field is still $7.34\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, that is $75\%$ of the surface value, which shows how **slowly** the field decreases over the altitudes of ordinary satellites.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A planet has radius $R = 5.82 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}$ and surface gravity $g_0 = 10.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Compute its mass and its mean density, and compare the result with the density of water, $1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

$$
g_0 = \frac{GM}{R^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M = \frac{g_0 R^2}{G},
\qquad
\rho = \frac{M}{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3} = \frac{3\,g_0}{4\pi G R}
$$
$$
M = \frac{10.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (5.82 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m})^2}{6.674 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}} = 5.28 \times 10^{26}\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
\rho = \frac{3 \cdot 10.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{4\pi \cdot 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2} \cdot 5.82 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}} = 6.39 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relation $g_0 = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$ contains the three quantities $g_0$, $M$ and $R$, therefore knowing two of them determines the third, and solving for the mass gives $M = \dfrac{g_0R^2}{G} = 5.28 \times 10^{26}\,\mathrm{kg}$, about $88$ times the mass of the Earth.

The mean density is the mass divided by the volume of the sphere. Substituting $M = \dfrac{g_0R^2}{G}$ in $\rho = \dfrac{M}{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3}$ makes the mass disappear and leaves $\rho = \dfrac{3g_0}{4\pi GR}$, a formula that gives the density from the surface gravity and the radius alone.

The result $6.39 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is smaller than the density of water, therefore the planet is **not** made of rock, and a composition of hydrogen and helium is the one consistent with this value. The mean density of the Earth, obtained by the same formula, is $5.51 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Model the Earth as a uniform sphere of radius $R_E = 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$ with surface gravity $g_0 = 9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Compute the gravitational field at the depth $p = 1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$ below the surface, and find the depth at which the field is $80\%$ of the surface value.

$$
g(r) = \frac{GM}{R_E^3}\,r = g_0\,\frac{r}{R_E}
\qquad\text{for } r \le R_E,
\qquad\text{with } r = R_E - p
$$
$$
g = 9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot \frac{6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m} - 1.00 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}}{6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}}
= 9.82\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.8430 = 8.28\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\frac{g}{g_0} = \frac{R_E - p}{R_E} = 0.80
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p = 0.20\,R_E = 1.27 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the depth $p$ the point lies at the distance $r = R_E - p$ from the center. The shells of material above that point contribute nothing to the field, since the point is inside each of them, therefore only the inner sphere of radius $r$ acts, and its mass is the fraction $\dfrac{r^3}{R_E^3}$ of the total. The field inside a uniform sphere is then proportional to $r$, which gives $g = g_0\,\dfrac{r}{R_E}$.

At the depth of $1.00 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$ the field is $8.28\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Comparing with the result of the preceding exercise, the same field is reached at the altitude of about $570\,\mathrm{km}$, hence going down reduces $g$ more slowly than going up, because part of the mass keeps acting from below.

Imposing $\dfrac{g}{g_0} = 0.80$ gives the depth $p = 0.20\,R_E = 1.27 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$. The linear law applies to a sphere of **uniform** density; the real Earth has a denser core, hence its field actually increases slightly down to the core boundary before decreasing.

</div>
