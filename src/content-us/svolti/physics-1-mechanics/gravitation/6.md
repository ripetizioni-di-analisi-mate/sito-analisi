## Exercise 1

A satellite of mass $m = 1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ travels a circular orbit at the altitude $h = 300\,\mathrm{km}$ above the Earth. Compute its speed, its kinetic energy, its potential energy and its mechanical energy. Use $GM_E = 3.984 \times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ and $R_E = 6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
r = R_E + h = 6.67 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{GM_E}{r}},
\qquad
K = \frac{GM_E m}{2r},
\qquad
U = -\frac{GM_E m}{r},
\qquad
E = -\frac{GM_E m}{2r}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{3.984 \times 10^{14}}{6.67 \times 10^{6}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 7.73 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
K = \frac{3.984 \times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2} \cdot 1200\,\mathrm{kg}}{2 \cdot 6.67 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}} = 3.58 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
U = -7.17 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
E = K + U = -3.58 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a circular orbit the gravitational attraction supplies the centripetal force, hence $v^2 = \dfrac{GM_E}{r}$ and the kinetic energy is $K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \dfrac{GM_Em}{2r}$. The potential energy at the same distance is $-\dfrac{GM_Em}{r}$, therefore $U = -2K$ and $E = -K$.

The mechanical energy is **negative**, in agreement with the fact that a circular orbit is a bound motion, and its magnitude $3.58 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the binding energy of the satellite, that is, the energy an external agent must supply to send it to infinite distance with zero speed.

The relation $E = -K$ holds on every circular orbit, hence measuring the orbital speed is enough to know the whole energy balance once the mass is given.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the energy that must be supplied to the same satellite, of mass $m = 1200\,\mathrm{kg}$, to take it from rest on the surface of the Earth to the circular orbit at the altitude $h = 300\,\mathrm{km}$. Neglect the rotation of the Earth and air resistance.

$$
E_i = -\frac{GM_E m}{R_E},
\qquad
E_f = -\frac{GM_E m}{2r},
\qquad
W = E_f - E_i = GM_E m\left(\frac{1}{R_E} - \frac{1}{2r}\right)
$$
$$
\frac{1}{R_E} = 1.570 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}},
\qquad
\frac{1}{2r} = \frac{1}{1.334 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}} = 7.496 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}
$$
$$
W = 3.984 \times 10^{14} \cdot 1200 \cdot \left(1.570 \times 10^{-7} - 7.496 \times 10^{-8}\right)\,\mathrm{J} = 3.92 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the ground the satellite is at rest, therefore its mechanical energy is the potential energy $-\dfrac{GM_Em}{R_E}$ alone. In orbit the energy is $-\dfrac{GM_Em}{2r}$, which includes the kinetic energy needed to stay on the orbit.

The energy to supply is the difference of the two values, $3.92 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$. Of this, $3.58 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the kinetic energy of the orbital motion computed in the preceding exercise, and the remaining $3.4 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work against gravitation during the climb.

Most of the cost of reaching a low orbit is therefore the speed and not the altitude. The rotation of the Earth gives a launch site on the equator a speed of $465\,\mathrm{m/s}$ eastward, which reduces the energy required and is the reason launch sites are placed at low latitudes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A satellite of mass $m = 800\,\mathrm{kg}$ is transferred from the circular orbit of radius $r_1 = 7.00 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$ to the circular orbit of radius $r_2 = 1.40 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the work of the engines, the change in kinetic energy and the change in potential energy.

$$
W = E_2 - E_1 = \frac{GM_E m}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r_1} - \frac{1}{r_2}\right)
$$
$$
W = \frac{3.984 \times 10^{14} \cdot 800}{2}\left(\frac{1}{7.00 \times 10^{6}} - \frac{1}{1.40 \times 10^{7}}\right)\,\mathrm{J}
= 1.594 \times 10^{17} \cdot 7.143 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J} = 1.14 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta K = \frac{GM_E m}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r_2} - \frac{1}{r_1}\right) = -1.14 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta U = GM_E m\left(\frac{1}{r_1} - \frac{1}{r_2}\right) = +2.28 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The work of the engines equals the change in mechanical energy, since gravitation is accounted for by the potential energy. The energy of a circular orbit is $-\dfrac{GM_Em}{2r}$, hence doubling the radius halves the magnitude of the energy and the difference is positive, $1.14 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$.

The kinetic energy **decreases** by the same amount, because $K = \dfrac{GM_Em}{2r}$ also halves, and the speed drops from $7.54\,\mathrm{km/s}$ to $5.33\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The potential energy increases by twice that amount, and the sum of the two changes gives back the work supplied.

A satellite raised to a higher orbit therefore moves more slowly while having more energy, which is a consequence of the relation $U = 2E$ valid on every circular orbit.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A satellite travels a circular orbit of radius $r = 8.00 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$ around the Earth. Compute the increase in speed that would be required, at that distance, to make it escape, and the corresponding extra energy for a mass $m = 500\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
v_{\text{orb}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM_E}{r}},
\qquad
v_f = \sqrt{\frac{2GM_E}{r}} = \sqrt{2}\,v_{\text{orb}},
\qquad
\Delta v = \left(\sqrt{2} - 1\right) v_{\text{orb}}
$$
$$
v_{\text{orb}} = \sqrt{\frac{3.984 \times 10^{14}}{8.00 \times 10^{6}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 7.06 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_f = 9.98 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
\Delta v = 0.4142 \cdot 7.06 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.92 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Delta E = 0 - E = \frac{GM_E m}{2r} = \frac{3.984 \times 10^{14} \cdot 500}{2 \cdot 8.00 \times 10^{6}}\,\mathrm{J} = 1.25 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The escape condition at the distance $r$ requires the mechanical energy to be non-negative, hence $v_f = \sqrt{\dfrac{2GM_E}{r}}$, while the circular orbit at the same distance requires $v_{\text{orb}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM_E}{r}}$. The ratio of the two is $\sqrt{2}$ at **every** distance, therefore the speed must be increased by the factor $\sqrt{2} - 1 = 0.414$.

For the orbit considered the increase is $2.92\,\mathrm{km/s}$ on a speed of $7.06\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The energy to supply is the whole binding energy of the orbit, $\dfrac{GM_Em}{2r} = 1.25 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$, since the final energy is zero.

The increase in speed is the same in relative terms on any circular orbit, while the energy to supply decreases with the radius, because a satellite farther from the Earth is bound more weakly.

</div>
