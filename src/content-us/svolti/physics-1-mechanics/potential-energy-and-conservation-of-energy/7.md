## Exercise 1

A body of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to a spring of constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and slides on a frictionless horizontal surface with mechanical energy $E = 4.0\,\mathrm{J}$. Compute the turning points of the motion, the largest speed reached and the speed at $x = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
U(x) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2,
\qquad
K = E - U(x) \ge 0
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}kx_t^2 = E
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x_t = \pm\sqrt{\frac{2E}{k}} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{J}}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}}} = \pm 0.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{J}}{0.50\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
U(0.10\,\mathrm{m}) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 200\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.10\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 1.0\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(E - U)}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{J}}{0.50\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 3.46\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The kinetic energy is never negative, hence the motion takes place only where $U(x) \le E$. For the spring this condition reads $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2 \le 4.0\,\mathrm{J}$, so the allowed region is the interval between $-0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, and its endpoints are the **turning points**, where the speed is zero and the force pushes the body back towards the origin.

The speed is largest where the potential energy is smallest, that is at $x = 0$, where the whole $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$ is kinetic and $v = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At $x = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ the spring holds $1.0\,\mathrm{J}$, which is one quarter of the total, because the elastic energy grows with the square of the displacement and $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ is half of the amplitude. The remaining $3.0\,\mathrm{J}$ give $v = 3.46\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $m = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves along the $x$ axis with potential energy
$$
U(x) = 3x^2 - x^3 ,
$$
with $U$ in joules and $x$ in meters. The body is released from rest at $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Determine the mechanical energy, the turning points, the allowed region and the largest speed reached.

$$
E = U(1\,\mathrm{m}) = 3\,\mathrm{J} - 1\,\mathrm{J} = 2\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
3x^2 - x^3 = 2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x^3 - 3x^2 + 2 = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(x - 1)\,(x^2 - 2x - 2) = 0
$$
$$
x = 1\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x = 1 \pm \sqrt{3}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x = -0.73\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x = 2.73\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
U_{\min} = U(0) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(E - U_{\min})}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 2\,\mathrm{J}}{1.0\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{f="3*x^2-x^3;2" punti="-0.73,2,pieno;1,2,pieno;0,0,pieno" etichette="3.0,2.5,E" xmin=-1.3 xmax=3.4 ymin=-1.3 ymax=7 nomi="x,U" titolo="Energy diagram for $U(x) = 3x^2 - x^3$: a body released from rest at $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ has $E = 2\,\mathrm{J}$ and moves between $x = -0.73\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$, with its largest speed at $x = 0$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The body starts at rest, hence its mechanical energy is the potential energy at the release position, $E = 2\,\mathrm{J}$. The turning points are the solutions of $U(x) = E$, and since $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ is one of them the cubic factors with $x - 1$, which leaves the quadratic $x^2 - 2x - 2$ and the two roots $1 \pm \sqrt{3}$.

Three positions satisfy $U = E$, therefore the allowed region splits into two parts. The body starts at $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ and cannot reach $x = 2.73\,\mathrm{m}$, because between the two positions the curve rises above $E$, with the maximum $U(2\,\mathrm{m}) = 4\,\mathrm{J}$ acting as a **barrier**. The motion is therefore confined to the interval from $-0.73\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1\,\mathrm{m}$, and the largest speed, $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, is reached at $x = 0$, where the potential energy is smallest on that interval.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to the lower end of a vertical spring of constant $k = 50\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and is released from rest with the spring at its natural length. Let $y$ be measured downwards from the natural length. Compute the equilibrium position, the turning points of the motion and the largest speed of the block.

$$
U(y) = \frac{1}{2}ky^2 - m g y,
\qquad
E = U(0) = 0
$$
$$
\frac{dU}{dy} = k y - m g = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
y_0 = \frac{m g}{k} = \frac{0.40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{50\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 0.0785\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}ky^2 - m g y = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
y = 0,
\qquad
y = \frac{2 m g}{k} = 0.157\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
K_{\max} = -U(y_0) = \frac{m^2g^2}{2k} = \frac{(3.924\,\mathrm{N})^2}{100\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 0.154\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{2K_{\max}}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.308\,\mathrm{J}}{0.40\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 0.877\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two conservative forces act on the block, hence the total potential energy is the sum of the elastic and the gravitational contributions, and the gravitational term is $-mgy$ because $y$ is measured downwards. The block is released from rest at $y = 0$, where both terms vanish, therefore $E = 0$.

The turning points are the positions where $U(y) = 0$, namely $y = 0$ and $y = \dfrac{2mg}{k} = 0.157\,\mathrm{m}$, so the block oscillates between the natural length and twice the equilibrium extension. The equilibrium position $y_0 = 0.0785\,\mathrm{m}$ is the midpoint of that interval and is the position where $U$ is smallest, hence the block passes through it with the largest speed, $0.877\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The block does **not** remain at the equilibrium position, because it arrives there with that speed.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A body of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves along the positive $x$ axis under a force whose potential energy is
$$
U(x) = -\frac{6}{x} ,
$$
with $U$ in joules and $x$ in meters. At $x_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ the body moves away from the origin. Compute the smallest speed for which the body never returns, and the turning point when its speed at $x_0$ is $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
U(x) \to 0
\quad\text{as}\quad
x \to +\infty
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{the body escapes if and only if } E \ge 0
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{6}{x_0} \ge 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v \ge \sqrt{\frac{12}{m\,x_0}} = \sqrt{\frac{12}{2.0 \cdot 2.0}} = 1.73\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
E = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (1.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 - \frac{6\,\mathrm{J\cdot m}}{2.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{J} - 3.0\,\mathrm{J} = -2.0\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
-\frac{6}{x_t} = -2.0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x_t = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The potential energy increases with $x$ and tends to zero at large distance, hence the force is attractive and the body is slowed down as it moves away. The kinetic energy at the distance $x$ is $E - U(x) = E + \dfrac{6}{x}$, which stays positive at every distance *only* when $E \ge 0$. The limiting case $E = 0$ gives the smallest speed for which the body never returns, $1.73\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

With the speed $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the mechanical energy is $-2.0\,\mathrm{J}$, which is negative, therefore the motion is **bound**. The turning point is the distance at which the whole energy is potential, and solving $U(x_t) = E$ gives $x_t = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$: the body moves out to that distance, stops and comes back. The same computation with the potential energy of Newton's law of gravitation gives the escape speed from a planet, as the chapter on gravitation develops.

</div>
