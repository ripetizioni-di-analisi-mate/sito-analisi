## Exercise 1

A body moves along the $x$ axis under a conservative force whose potential energy is
$$
U(x) = 3x^2 - 12x ,
$$
with $U$ in joules and $x$ in meters. Compute the force as a function of $x$, the position of equilibrium and its character, and the value of the force at $x = 0$.

$$
F_x = -\frac{dU}{dx} = -(6x - 12) = 12 - 6x
$$
$$
F_x = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x_0 = 2\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} = 6\,\mathrm{N/m} > 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{stable equilibrium}
$$
$$
U(2\,\mathrm{m}) = 12\,\mathrm{J} - 24\,\mathrm{J} = -12\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
F_x(0) = 12\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force along the axis is the opposite of the derivative of the potential energy, hence differentiating $U$ gives $F_x = 12 - 6x$, measured in newtons when $x$ is in meters. The equilibrium positions are the zeros of the force, that is the stationary points of $U$, and here there is exactly one, at $x_0 = 2\,\mathrm{m}$.

The second derivative is the constant $6\,\mathrm{N/m}$, which is positive, therefore $U$ has a minimum at $x_0$ and the equilibrium is **stable**. The force confirms the classification, since for $x < 2\,\mathrm{m}$ it is positive and pushes the body towards $x_0$, while for $x > 2\,\mathrm{m}$ it is negative and pushes it back. Writing $U = 3(x-2)^2 - 12$ shows that this is the potential energy of a spring of constant $k = 6\,\mathrm{N/m}$ with its natural length at $x_0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body moves along the $x$ axis with potential energy
$$
U(x) = 2x^3 - 9x^2 + 12x ,
$$
with $U$ in joules and $x$ in meters. Determine the equilibrium positions, classify them, and compute the force at $x = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
F_x = -\frac{dU}{dx} = -(6x^2 - 18x + 12) = -6\,(x - 1)(x - 2)
$$
$$
F_x = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x_1 = 1\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x_2 = 2\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} = 12x - 18
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{d^2U}{dx^2}(1\,\mathrm{m}) = -6\,\mathrm{N/m} < 0 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{d^2U}{dx^2}(2\,\mathrm{m}) = 6\,\mathrm{N/m} > 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
U(1\,\mathrm{m}) = 5\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
U(2\,\mathrm{m}) = 4\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
F_x(0.50\,\mathrm{m}) = -6 \cdot (-0.50)(-1.50) = -4.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The derivative of $U$ is a quadratic polynomial with roots $1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2\,\mathrm{m}$, hence there are two equilibrium positions. The second derivative is negative at $x_1$ and positive at $x_2$, therefore $U$ has a local maximum at $x_1$ and a local minimum at $x_2$, so the first equilibrium is **unstable** and the second is **stable**.

The factored form of the force gives its sign without further computation, since $F_x$ is negative outside the interval between the two roots and positive inside it. At $x = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, which lies to the left of both roots, the force is $-4.5\,\mathrm{N}$, directed towards decreasing $x$, hence away from the unstable position $x_1$. A body placed exactly at $x_1$ stays there, while the smallest displacement to either side starts a motion that carries it away.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body moves along the $x$ axis with potential energy
$$
U(x) = x^4 - 8x^2 ,
$$
with $U$ in joules and $x$ in meters. Determine the equilibrium positions, classify them, and compute the energy needed to move the body from the position of lowest potential energy to the position $x = 0$.

$$
F_x = -\frac{dU}{dx} = -(4x^3 - 16x) = -4x\,(x^2 - 4)
$$
$$
F_x = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x = -2\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x = 0,
\qquad
x = 2\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} = 12x^2 - 16
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{d^2U}{dx^2}(0) = -16\,\mathrm{N/m} < 0 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{d^2U}{dx^2}(\pm 2\,\mathrm{m}) = 32\,\mathrm{N/m} > 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
U(\pm 2\,\mathrm{m}) = 16\,\mathrm{J} - 32\,\mathrm{J} = -16\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
U(0) = 0
$$
$$
\Delta U = U(0) - U(2\,\mathrm{m}) = 16\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The potential energy is an even function, hence its graph is symmetric about the vertical axis and the equilibrium positions come in a symmetric pair together with the central one. The second derivative is negative at the origin and positive at $x = \pm 2\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the origin is a position of **unstable** equilibrium and the two outer positions are **stable**, each at the bottom of its own well.

Moving the body from $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ to $x = 0$ raises its potential energy from $-16\,\mathrm{J}$ to $0$, hence an external agent must supply $16\,\mathrm{J}$. A body that has mechanical energy smaller than $0$ cannot cross the origin and stays in the well where it started, while a body with $E > 0$ passes from one well to the other, as the energy diagram of the potential energy curve shows.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Two particles interact along the line joining them, with potential energy
$$
U(x) = \frac{2}{x^2} - \frac{4}{x},
\qquad
x > 0 ,
$$
with $U$ in joules and $x$ in meters. Determine the equilibrium distance and its character, the depth of the potential well, and the force at $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\frac{dU}{dx} = -\frac{4}{x^3} + \frac{4}{x^2} = \frac{4\,(x - 1)}{x^3}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
F_x = \frac{4\,(1 - x)}{x^3}
$$
$$
F_x = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x_0 = 1\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} = \frac{12}{x^4} - \frac{8}{x^3}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{d^2U}{dx^2}(1\,\mathrm{m}) = 4\,\mathrm{N/m} > 0
$$
$$
U(1\,\mathrm{m}) = 2\,\mathrm{J} - 4\,\mathrm{J} = -2\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
F_x(2\,\mathrm{m}) = \frac{4 \cdot (1 - 2)}{8} = -0.50\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two terms of $U$ describe two opposite tendencies, since the term $\dfrac{2}{x^2}$ grows without bound as $x$ decreases and produces a repulsion at short distance, while the term $-\dfrac{4}{x}$ produces an attraction that dominates at large distance. Their sum has a single stationary point, at $x_0 = 1\,\mathrm{m}$, where the second derivative is positive, hence that distance is a position of **stable** equilibrium.

The depth of the well is the difference between the value of $U$ at large distance, which tends to $0$, and its minimum value $-2\,\mathrm{J}$, therefore a body in the well needs $2\,\mathrm{J}$ to separate completely. At $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ the force is negative, hence it is directed towards smaller $x$. The two particles are farther apart than the equilibrium distance, therefore they **attract** each other.

</div>
