## Exercise 1

A block is released from rest at the height $h = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ on a frictionless curved track. At the bottom the track joins a horizontal floor whose coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k = 0.20$. Compute the speed of the block at the bottom of the track and the distance it travels on the floor before stopping.

$$
m g h = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m}} = 5.42\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
-\mu_k m g\,d = 0 - \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = -m g h
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
d = \frac{h}{\mu_k}
$$
$$
d = \frac{1.5\,\mathrm{m}}{0.20} = 7.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="t,1.5*(1-sin(pi*t/4)),0,2" linee="0,0 10,0" punti="0,1.5,pieno;2,0,pieno;9.5,0,pieno" segmenti="0,1.5,-0.5,1.5;-0.5,0,-0.5,1.5;2,0,2,-0.55;9.5,0,9.5,-0.55" etichette="-0.9,0.65,h;5.2,-0.85,d = 7.5 m" xmin=-1.3 xmax=10.5 ymin=-1.3 ymax=2.1 titolo="The curved part is frictionless, hence the block arrives at the floor with $v = \sqrt{2gh}$; on the rough floor friction removes $\mu_k m g$ per unit length and the block stops after $d = \dfrac{h}{\mu_k}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem splits into two stages with different forces. On the curved track the normal force does no work and there is no friction, hence the mechanical energy is conserved and the block arrives at the bottom with $5.42\,\mathrm{m/s}$, whatever the shape of the track.

On the horizontal floor the height does not change, so the whole kinetic energy is removed by friction. Writing the two stages together, the initial potential energy $mgh$ equals the energy dissipated $\mu_k mg\,d$, and the two masses cancel together with $g$. The distance therefore depends only on the drop and on the coefficient of friction, $d = \dfrac{h}{\mu_k} = 7.5\,\mathrm{m}$, and a block released from twice the height would slide twice as far.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pressed against a spring of constant $k = 400\,\mathrm{N/m}$, compressing it by $x = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, and is then released. The block is not attached to the spring and the floor is horizontal with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k = 0.30$. Compute the total distance traveled by the block before it stops.

$$
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \mu_k m g\,d
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
d = \frac{k x^2}{2\mu_k m g}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 400\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.10\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\mu_k m g = 0.30 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.472\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
d = \frac{2.0\,\mathrm{J}}{1.472\,\mathrm{N}} = 1.36\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block starts at rest and ends at rest, hence $\Delta K = 0$, and the floor is horizontal, hence the gravitational potential energy does not change. The energy equation therefore states that the work of friction equals the change of the elastic potential energy, that is $-\mu_k mg\,d = 0 - \dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$.

The friction force has the constant magnitude $1.472\,\mathrm{N}$ during the whole motion, since the normal force is $mg$ both while the spring pushes and afterwards. The distance $d = 1.36\,\mathrm{m}$ is therefore measured from the **release position**, and the first $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ of it are traveled in contact with the spring. Of the $2.0\,\mathrm{J}$ stored, $0.147\,\mathrm{J}$ are already dissipated before the block separates from the spring.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is released from rest on a plane inclined at $\alpha = 35^\circ$ with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k = 0.25$. After sliding $d = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ along the plane it meets a spring of constant $k = 800\,\mathrm{N/m}$ placed along the plane. Friction acts on the whole path, including the part traveled while the spring is being compressed. Compute the maximum compression of the spring.

$$
m g \sin\alpha\,(d + x) - \mu_k m g \cos\alpha\,(d + x) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2
$$
$$
C = m g\,(\sin\alpha - \mu_k\cos\alpha) = 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \cdot (0.5736 - 0.2048) = 3.618\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 - C\,x - C\,d = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
400\,x^2 - 3.618\,x - 4.341 = 0
$$
$$
x = \frac{3.618 + \sqrt{13.09 + 6946}}{800} = \frac{3.618 + 83.42}{800} = 0.109\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block is at rest at the start and at the maximum compression, hence $\Delta K = 0$ and the energy equation states that the work of friction equals the change of the total potential energy. Along the whole path of length $d + x$ the weight releases $mg\sin\alpha\,(d+x)$, friction dissipates $\mu_k mg\cos\alpha\,(d+x)$ and the spring stores $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$.

Collecting the two terms proportional to $d + x$ gives the constant $C = 3.618\,\mathrm{N}$, which is the net force along the plane acting while the block slides, and the balance becomes a **quadratic equation** in the compression. Its positive root is $x = 0.109\,\mathrm{m}$. Without friction the same calculation with $C = mg\sin\alpha = 5.627\,\mathrm{N}$ would give $0.137\,\mathrm{m}$, hence friction reduces the compression by about one fifth.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A spring of constant $k = 1200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is compressed by $x = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and launches a block of mass $m = 0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ along a horizontal floor. The floor is rough over a length $s = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, with $\mu_k = 0.15$, and beyond it the block runs up a frictionless plane inclined at $\beta = 40^\circ$. Compute the speed of the block at the end of the rough part and the height it reaches on the incline.

$$
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1200\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.080\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 3.84\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\mu_k m g\,s = 0.15 \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{m} = 0.441\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = 3.84\,\mathrm{J} - 0.441\,\mathrm{J} = 3.399\,\mathrm{J}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 3.399\,\mathrm{J}}{0.60\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 3.37\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = m g H
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
H = \frac{3.399\,\mathrm{J}}{0.60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 0.577\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\ell = \frac{H}{\sin 40^\circ} = \frac{0.577\,\mathrm{m}}{0.6428} = 0.898\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is followed stage by stage with a single energy balance. The spring supplies $3.84\,\mathrm{J}$; the rough part removes $0.441\,\mathrm{J}$, since the normal force there is $mg$ and the friction force is constant; the remaining $3.399\,\mathrm{J}$ are the kinetic energy at the foot of the incline, which gives $3.37\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

On the incline there is no friction and the normal force does no work, hence the mechanical energy is conserved and the whole kinetic energy becomes gravitational potential energy at the highest point, where the block is momentarily at rest. The rise is $H = 0.577\,\mathrm{m}$, measured vertically, and the distance traveled along the plane is $\dfrac{H}{\sin\beta} = 0.898\,\mathrm{m}$. The angle of the incline affects the distance along the plane and **not** the height reached, because only the height enters the gravitational potential energy.

</div>
