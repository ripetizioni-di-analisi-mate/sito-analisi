## Exercise 1

A pendulum is made of a bob attached to a massless inextensible string of length $L = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The bob is released from rest with the string making the angle $\theta_0 = 30^\circ$ with the vertical. Air resistance is negligible. Compute the height of the bob above the lowest point of the swing and its speed at that lowest point.

$$
h = L - L\cos\theta_0 = L\,(1 - \cos\theta_0)
$$
$$
h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m} \cdot (1 - 0.8660) = 0.161\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2gL\,(1 - \cos\theta_0)}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.161\,\mathrm{m}} = \sqrt{3.154\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 1.78\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1.3,0 1.3,0;0,0 -0.6,-1.039" curve="1.2*sin(t),-1.2*cos(t),-0.62,0.06" punti="0,0,pieno;-0.6,-1.039,pieno;0,-1.2,pieno" segmenti="0,0,0,-1.2;-0.6,-1.039,-1.3,-1.039;-1.3,-1.039,-1.3,-1.2" vettori="0,-1.2,0.95,-1.2,v" etichette="-0.4,-0.78,30°;-1.62,-1.08,h" xmin=-1.95 xmax=1.6 ymin=-1.75 ymax=0.4 titolo="The bob descends the height $h = L\,(1 - \cos\theta_0)$ from the release position to the lowest point, where its speed is $v = \sqrt{2gh}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two forces act on the bob, the weight and the tension of the string. The string is inextensible, hence the bob moves on a circle and the tension stays perpendicular to the velocity at every instant, therefore the tension does no work. Air resistance is negligible, so the weight is the only force that does work and the **mechanical energy is conserved**.

The height of the bob below the pivot is $L\cos\theta$, hence the release position is higher than the lowest point by $L(1-\cos\theta_0) = 0.161\,\mathrm{m}$. The bob starts from rest, so that drop supplies the whole kinetic energy at the bottom, and the speed is $1.78\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The mass of the bob does not appear in the result, therefore the speed depends on the length of the string and on the release angle alone.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The bob of the pendulum of the previous exercise has mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$. Compute the tension of the string at the lowest point of the swing and at the release position.

$$
T - mg = m\,\frac{v^2}{L}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
T = m\left(g + \frac{v^2}{L}\right)
$$
$$
T_{\text{bottom}} = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left(9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} + \frac{3.154\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}}{1.20\,\mathrm{m}}\right) = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 12.44\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 6.22\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
v = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_0 = m g \cos\theta_0 = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.8660 = 4.25\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the lowest point the bob moves on a circle of radius $L$, hence its acceleration is centripetal and points upwards, towards the pivot. Newton's second law along the vertical gives $T - mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{L}$, as the chapter on applications of Newton's laws establishes for circular motion. The speed at that point comes from the energy balance of the previous exercise, therefore the tension is $6.22\,\mathrm{N}$, larger than the weight $4.91\,\mathrm{N}$.

At the release position the speed is zero, hence the centripetal acceleration is zero as well and the component of the weight along the string, $mg\cos\theta_0$, is balanced by the tension alone, which gives $4.25\,\mathrm{N}$. The tension therefore changes during the swing, and this is the reason why it carries no potential energy, since its value depends on the motion, while the work it does is **zero** at every instant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A small ball attached to a massless string of length $L = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is released from rest with the string horizontal. Compute the speed of the ball at the lowest point and its speed when the string makes the angle $60^\circ$ with the vertical.

$$
h_1 = L,
\qquad
h_2 = L - L\cos 60^\circ = L\,(1 - \cos 60^\circ)
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2g\,h}
$$
$$
v_1 = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m}} = \sqrt{15.70\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 3.96\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
h_2 = 0.80\,\mathrm{m} \cdot (1 - 0.500) = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_2 = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{m}} = \sqrt{7.848\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 2.80\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The release position is at the height of the pivot, hence the drop to the lowest point is the whole length of the string, $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, and the speed there is $3.96\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The tension does no work, so the result is the same as for a body falling freely through $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$.

When the string makes $60^\circ$ with the vertical the ball is $L\cos 60^\circ = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ below the pivot, hence it has descended $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ from the release position and its speed is $2.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The drop is half of the total, and the speed is $\sqrt{2}$ times smaller than at the bottom, because the speed grows with the **square root** of the drop.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A small body slides on a frictionless track. It is released from rest at the height $h$ and then runs along a vertical circular loop of radius $R = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the smallest speed the body can have at the top of the loop while staying in contact with the track, and the smallest value of $h$ for which the body completes the loop.

$$
mg + N = m\,\frac{v_{\text{top}}^2}{R},
\qquad
N \ge 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_{\text{top}}^2 \ge gR
$$
$$
v_{\text{top,min}} = \sqrt{gR} = \sqrt{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{m}} = 1.57\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv_{\text{top}}^2 + mg\,(2R)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
h = 2R + \frac{v_{\text{top}}^2}{2g}
$$
$$
h_{\min} = 2R + \frac{gR}{2g} = \frac{5}{2}R = 2.5 \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{m} = 0.625\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 3.8,0;0.3,0.625 1.6,0" curve="2.5+0.25*cos(t),0.25+0.25*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0.3,0.625,pieno;2.5,0.5,pieno" segmenti="0.3,0.625,-0.15,0.625;-0.15,0,-0.15,0.625;2.5,0.5,3.3,0.5;3.3,0,3.3,0.5" etichette="-0.35,0.3,h;3.47,0.22,2R" xmin=-0.7 xmax=3.9 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1 titolo="At the top of the loop the body is $2R$ above the ground; the release height $h = 2.5\,R$ is the smallest one for which the track still pushes on the body at the top"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At the top of the loop the centre of the circle is below the body, hence the centripetal acceleration points downwards and both the weight and the normal force point that way. Newton's second law gives $mg + N = \dfrac{mv_{\text{top}}^2}{R}$. A track can only push, therefore $N \ge 0$, and the limiting case $N = 0$ gives the smallest admissible speed $v_{\text{top}} = \sqrt{gR} = 1.57\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The track is frictionless and the normal force is perpendicular to the velocity, hence the mechanical energy is conserved between the release point and the top of the loop, which is at the height $2R$. Imposing the smallest admissible speed at the top gives $h_{\min} = 2.5\,R = 0.625\,\mathrm{m}$, that is one quarter of the loop diameter above the top of the loop. A body released from a lower height leaves the track before reaching the top, so the energy balance alone is **not sufficient** to decide the question, and the condition $N \ge 0$ is needed as well.

</div>
