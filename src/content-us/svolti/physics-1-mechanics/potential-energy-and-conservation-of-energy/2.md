## Exercise 1

A spring of constant $k = 250\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is compressed by $x = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$ against a block of mass $m = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ resting on a frictionless horizontal surface. The spring is released. Compute the energy stored in the spring and the speed of the block when it leaves the spring.

$$
U_{\text{el}} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 250\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.12\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 1.8\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v = x\,\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$
$$
v = 0.12\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{250\,\mathrm{N/m}}{0.40\,\mathrm{kg}}} = 0.12\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 5,0;0,0 0,1.6;0,0.55 0.2,0.55 0.35,0.85 0.6,0.25 0.85,0.85 1.1,0.25 1.35,0.85 1.5,0.55 1.6,0.55" poligoni="1.6,0 2.4,0 2.4,1.1 1.6,1.1" vettori="2.6,0.55,3.7,0.55,v" etichette="0.75,1.15,k;1.9,1.3,m" xmin=-0.4 xmax=5.2 ymin=-0.6 ymax=2 titolo="The block leaves the spring when the spring returns to its natural length, and at that instant the whole stored energy $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ has become kinetic energy"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The block moves on a horizontal surface, hence its height does not change and the gravitational potential energy stays constant. The surface is frictionless and the normal force is perpendicular to the displacement, therefore the elastic force is the only force that does work, and the **mechanical energy is conserved** with $U = \dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$.

At the start the block is at rest and the spring is compressed by $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$, so the mechanical energy is the stored energy $1.8\,\mathrm{J}$. The block leaves the spring at the natural length, where the elastic potential energy is zero and the whole $1.8\,\mathrm{J}$ is kinetic, which gives $v = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A spring compressed by twice as much would store four times the energy and would give the block twice the speed, since $v$ is proportional to $x$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a frictionless horizontal surface with the speed $v = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and runs into a spring of constant $k = 600\,\mathrm{N/m}$ fixed to a wall. Compute the maximum compression of the spring and the speed of the block when the compression is $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}kx_{\max}^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x_{\max} = v\,\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
x_{\max} = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1.5\,\mathrm{kg}}{600\,\mathrm{N/m}}} = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{s} = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}kx_1^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_1 = \sqrt{v^2 - \frac{k}{m}x_1^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \sqrt{5.76\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} - 400\,\mathrm{s^{-2}} \cdot (0.080\,\mathrm{m})^2} = \sqrt{3.20\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The compression is largest when the block is momentarily at rest, since from that instant the spring pushes the block back. At that position the whole mechanical energy is elastic potential energy, which gives $x_{\max} = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$.

At an intermediate compression $x_1$ the mechanical energy is shared between the two terms, hence the kinetic energy is what the elastic term leaves, $\dfrac{1}{2}mv_1^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}kx_1^2$. With $x_1 = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ the spring holds $1.92\,\mathrm{J}$ of the total $4.32\,\mathrm{J}$, therefore the block still has $2.40\,\mathrm{J}$ of kinetic energy and its speed is $1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vertical spring of constant $k = 320\,\mathrm{N/m}$ stands on the ground. A block of mass $m = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pressed on it until the spring is compressed by $x = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ from its natural length, and is then released. The block is not attached to the spring. Compute the height reached by the block above the position from which it is released.

$$
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = mgh
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
h = \frac{kx^2}{2mg}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 320\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot (0.15\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 3.6\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
h = \frac{3.6\,\mathrm{J}}{0.80\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 0.459\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two conservative forces act on the block, the elastic force and the weight, hence the total potential energy is the sum $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2 + mgy$ and the mechanical energy is conserved. The initial position is taken as the reference level for the height, so that at the start the energy is the stored energy $3.6\,\mathrm{J}$ and the block is at rest.

At the highest point the block is again at rest and is no longer in contact with the spring, therefore the whole $3.6\,\mathrm{J}$ is gravitational potential energy, and the rise is $0.459\,\mathrm{m}$. The height is measured from the release position, hence the block rises $0.459 - 0.15 = 0.309\,\mathrm{m}$ above the natural length of the spring. The block separates from the spring exactly at the natural length, because beyond that point the spring would have to pull the block, and the two are **not attached**.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A block of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is released from rest on a frictionless plane inclined at $\alpha = 30^\circ$. After sliding $d = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ along the plane it reaches a spring of constant $k = 500\,\mathrm{N/m}$ placed along the plane. Compute the maximum compression of the spring.

$$
m g \sin\alpha\,(d + x) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 - m g \sin\alpha\,x - m g \sin\alpha\,d = 0
$$
$$
m g \sin\alpha = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.500 = 9.81\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
250\,x^2 - 9.81\,x - 5.886 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x = \frac{9.81 + \sqrt{96.24 + 5886}}{500} = \frac{9.81 + 77.35}{500}
$$
$$
x_{\max} = 0.174\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane is frictionless and the normal force is perpendicular to the displacement, hence only the weight and the elastic force do work and the mechanical energy is conserved. The block starts from rest and is again at rest at the maximum compression, therefore the kinetic energy is zero at both positions and the whole drop in gravitational potential energy has become elastic potential energy.

From the start to the maximum compression the block travels $d + x$ along the plane, hence it descends the height $(d+x)\sin\alpha$ and the weight does the work $mg\sin\alpha\,(d+x)$. Equating that work to $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ gives a **quadratic equation** in $x$, whose positive root is $0.174\,\mathrm{m}$. The negative root has no physical meaning here, since $x$ is a compression. Neglecting the extra descent during the compression, that is solving $mg\sin\alpha\,d = \dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$, would give $0.153\,\mathrm{m}$, which is about $12\%$ smaller.

</div>
