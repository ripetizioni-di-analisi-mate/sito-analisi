## Exercise 1

A stone is released from rest from the top of a building $h = 45\,\mathrm{m}$ high. Air resistance is negligible. Find the time it takes to reach the ground and its speed on impact. Take $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
v(t) = -g\,t \\[6pt]
y(t) = h - \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{($y$ measured upward from the ground)}
$$
$$
y(t_f) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_f = \sqrt{\frac{2\,h}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 45\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 3.03\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
|v| = \sqrt{2\,g\,h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 45\,\mathrm{m}} = 29.7\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Air resistance is negligible, therefore the stone is in free fall and its acceleration is constant, directed downward and of magnitude $g$. The axis $y$ is chosen upward with the origin on the ground, so the acceleration is $-g$, the initial position is $h$ and the initial velocity is zero.

The instant of arrival is found by setting $y = 0$ in the law of motion, which gives $t_f = \sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}} = 3.03\,\mathrm{s}$. The impact speed follows from the **relation between velocity and position**, $v^2 = 2gh$, which gives $29.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ without using the time.

As a check, the equation of the velocity gives $|v| = g\,t_f = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 3.0289\,\mathrm{s} = 29.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the same value. The mass of the stone never enters the calculation, since $g$ is the same for every body.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ball is thrown vertically upward from the ground with initial speed $v_0 = 22\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with air resistance negligible. Find the maximum height, the total time of flight, and the velocity and the height at $t = 3.5\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
v(t) = v_0 - g\,t \\[6pt]
y(t) = v_0\,t - \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
v = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_{\mathrm{rise}} = \frac{v_0}{g} = 2.24\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
h = \frac{v_0^2}{2\,g} = \frac{(22\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 24.7\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t\left(v_0 - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t\right) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_f = \frac{2\,v_0}{g} = 4.49\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
v(3.5\,\mathrm{s}) = 22\,\mathrm{m/s} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 3.5\,\mathrm{s} = -12.3\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
y(3.5\,\mathrm{s}) = 22\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 3.5\,\mathrm{s} - \frac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (3.5\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 16.9\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{f="22*x-4.905*x^2" xmin=-0.25 xmax=5.0 ymin=-1.5 ymax=27.5 nomi="t,y" punti="2.243,24.67,pieno;3.5,16.91,pieno;4.485,0,pieno" segmenti="2.243,0,2.243,24.67;3.5,0,3.5,16.91" legenda=0 titolo="Height of the ball: the apex is at $t = 2.24\,\mathrm{s}$, at $t = 3.5\,\mathrm{s}$ the ball is at $16.9\,\mathrm{m}$ and is already coming down"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At the highest point the motion changes sense, therefore the velocity vanishes there. Setting $v = 0$ in the equation of the velocity gives the rising time $2.24\,\mathrm{s}$, and the **relation between velocity and position** gives the height $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2g} = 24.7\,\mathrm{m}$ directly.

The ball returns to the ground when $y = 0$ again. The equation $y = 0$ has the roots $t = 0$, which is the throw, and $t_f = \dfrac{2v_0}{g} = 4.49\,\mathrm{s}$, which is twice the rising time, so the descent lasts exactly as long as the ascent.

At $t = 3.5\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity is negative, hence the ball is already **coming down**, and its speed is $12.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its height is $16.9\,\mathrm{m}$, smaller than the maximum, as the figure shows.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ball is thrown vertically downward with initial speed $v_0 = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from a window $h = 20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground, with air resistance negligible. Find the time it takes to reach the ground and the impact speed.

$$
h = v_0\,t + \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2
\qquad\text{(positive axis pointing downward, origin at the window)}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,t^2 + 5.0\,t - 20 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t = \frac{-v_0 + \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2\,g\,h}}{g}
$$
$$
t = \frac{-5.0\,\mathrm{m/s} + \sqrt{(5.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 + 2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m}}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = \frac{15.43\,\mathrm{m/s}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 1.57\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2\,g\,h} = \sqrt{417.4\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 20.4\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is chosen pointing downward with the origin at the window, so the initial velocity and the acceleration are both positive and the ground is at $y = 20\,\mathrm{m}$. With this choice the law of motion is $y = v_0 t + \dfrac{1}{2}g t^2$, and setting $y = h$ gives a quadratic equation in $t$.

The quadratic equation has one positive root and one negative root, since the product of the roots is $-\dfrac{2h}{g} < 0$. The negative root corresponds to an instant before the throw and is discarded, therefore $t = 1.57\,\mathrm{s}$.

The impact speed is obtained from the **relation between velocity and position**, $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2gh$, which gives $20.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The same value follows from $v = v_0 + g t = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s} + 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.5729\,\mathrm{s}$. A ball simply released from the same window would arrive at $19.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the initial push of $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ adds only $0.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to the impact speed, since the relation combines the squares of the two velocities.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A stone is thrown vertically upward with speed $v_0 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from the top of a tower $H = 30\,\mathrm{m}$ high, with air resistance negligible. Find the maximum height reached above the ground, the time it takes to reach the ground at the foot of the tower and the impact speed.

$$
y(t) = H + v_0\,t - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2,
\qquad
v(t) = v_0 - g\,t
\qquad\text{($y$ upward from the ground)}
$$
$$
t_{\mathrm{apex}} = \frac{v_0}{g} = 1.22\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
y_{\max} = H + \frac{v_0^2}{2\,g} = 30\,\mathrm{m} + 7.34\,\mathrm{m} = 37.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y(t_f) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2}\,g\,t_f^2 - v_0\,t_f - H = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_f = \frac{v_0 + \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2\,g\,H}}{g}
$$
$$
t_f = \frac{12\,\mathrm{m/s} + 27.07\,\mathrm{m/s}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 3.98\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
|v_f| = \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2\,g\,H} = \sqrt{732.6\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = 27.1\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The origin is placed at the foot of the tower with the axis upward, hence the initial position is $H = 30\,\mathrm{m}$ and the initial velocity is $+12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The acceleration is $-g$ during the whole flight, including the apex, because the stone is free from the moment it leaves the hand.

The apex is where the velocity vanishes, so it occurs at $t = \dfrac{v_0}{g} = 1.22\,\mathrm{s}$ and the stone has risen $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2g} = 7.34\,\mathrm{m}$ above the launch point, that is $37.3\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground.

Setting $y = 0$ gives a quadratic equation whose positive root is $t_f = 3.98\,\mathrm{s}$. The impact speed follows from the relation between velocity and position applied between the top of the tower and the ground, $v_f^2 = v_0^2 + 2gH$, which gives $27.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The same relation shows that when the stone passes the top of the tower on the way down its speed is again $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, since the position is the same, therefore the last part of the motion is identical to that of a stone **thrown downward** at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from the tower.

</div>
