## Exercise 1

A train leaves a station from rest with constant acceleration $a = 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find its velocity and the distance covered after $t = 15\,\mathrm{s}$, and then the time and the distance needed to reach $108\,\mathrm{km/h}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
v(t) = v_0 + a\,t \\[6pt]
x(t) = x_0 + v_0\,t + \dfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
v_0 = 0,
\quad
x_0 = 0
$$
$$
v = a\,t = 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 15\,\mathrm{s} = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x = \frac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (15\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 90\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_f = 108\,\mathrm{km/h} = 30\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
t_f = \frac{v_f}{a} = \frac{30\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 37.5\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x_f = \frac{v_f^2}{2\,a} = \frac{(30\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 563\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is constant, therefore the **equations of uniformly accelerated motion** apply. The train starts from rest and the origin is placed at the station, hence $v_0 = 0$ and $x_0 = 0$, and the two equations reduce to $v = at$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2}at^2$.

Substituting $t = 15\,\mathrm{s}$ gives $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $x = 90\,\mathrm{m}$. As a check, the average velocity over this interval is $\dfrac{0 + 12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 15\,\mathrm{s} = 90\,\mathrm{m}$.

For the second question the target speed is converted into SI units first, since the acceleration is in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The time follows from $v = at$, and the distance is obtained from the **relation between velocity and position** $v_f^2 = 2 a x_f$, which avoids substituting the time. Using $x = \dfrac{1}{2}at_f^2$ with $t_f = 37.5\,\mathrm{s}$ gives the same $563\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car traveling at $v_0 = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes with constant deceleration and stops after $d = 40\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the magnitude of the acceleration and the duration of the braking. Find then the stopping distance of the same car, with the same deceleration, starting from $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
v^2 = v_0^2 + 2\,a\,d
\quad\text{with}\quad
v = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
a = -\,\frac{v_0^2}{2\,d}
$$
$$
|a| = \frac{(25\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{m}} = 7.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v = v_0 + a\,t
\quad\text{with}\quad
v = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t = \frac{v_0}{|a|} = \frac{25\,\mathrm{m/s}}{7.8125\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 3.2\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
d' = \frac{v_0'^2}{2\,|a|} = \frac{(30\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 7.8125\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 57.6\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{f="25-7.8125*x" area="25-7.8125*x;0;0;3.2" xmin=-0.3 xmax=3.7 ymin=-1.5 ymax=28 nomi="t,v" punti="0,25,pieno;3.2,0,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="Velocity of the car during the braking: the shaded triangle has area $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3.2\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 25\,\mathrm{m/s} = 40\,\mathrm{m}$, which is the stopping distance"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The data are the initial velocity, the final velocity and the distance, and the time is neither given nor asked, therefore the **relation between velocity and position** is the equation to use. Setting $v = 0$ gives $a = -\dfrac{v_0^2}{2d}$, negative because the car moves in the positive sense and slows down, with magnitude $7.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The duration follows from the equation of the velocity with $v = 0$, hence $t = \dfrac{v_0}{|a|} = 3.2\,\mathrm{s}$. The figure shows the same result as an area, since the velocity-time graph is a straight segment from $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to zero and the triangle below it has area $40\,\mathrm{m}$, in agreement with the datum.

In the stopping distance $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2|a|}$ the initial speed appears squared, so a speed larger by the factor $\dfrac{30}{25} = 1.2$ gives a distance larger by $1.2^2 = 1.44$, that is $57.6\,\mathrm{m}$ instead of $40\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A motorcycle with constant acceleration passes a reference mark at $v_0 = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and covers $120\,\mathrm{m}$ in the following $8.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the acceleration and the velocity at the end of that interval.

$$
\Delta x = v_0\,t + \frac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
a = \frac{2\,(\Delta x - v_0\,t)}{t^2}
$$
$$
a = \frac{2\,(120\,\mathrm{m} - 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{s})}{(8.0\,\mathrm{s})^2} = \frac{2 \cdot 56\,\mathrm{m}}{64\,\mathrm{s^2}} = 1.75\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v = v_0 + a\,t = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s} + 1.75\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{s} = 22\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown is the acceleration and the data are the initial velocity, the displacement and the time, therefore the equation of the position is the one that contains exactly these quantities. Solving it for $a$ gives $a = \dfrac{2(\Delta x - v_0 t)}{t^2}$.

The term $v_0 t = 64\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance the motorcycle would have covered at constant velocity, so the remaining $56\,\mathrm{m}$ are due to the acceleration, and $a = 1.75\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The final velocity follows from the equation of the velocity.

As a check, the relation between velocity and position gives $v^2 = (8.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 + 2 \cdot 1.75\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 120\,\mathrm{m} = 484\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, hence $v = 22\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The average velocity $\dfrac{8.0 + 22}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ multiplied by $8.0\,\mathrm{s}$ gives again $120\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A body moves along the $x$ axis with constant acceleration $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and passes the origin at $t = 0$ with velocity $v_0 = -6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the instant at which the body reverses, the position at that instant and the instant at which it passes the origin again.

$$
v(t) = -6.0 + 2.0\,t,
\qquad
x(t) = -6.0\,t + 1.0\,t^2
\qquad\text{(SI units)}
$$
$$
v(t) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_1 = \frac{6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x(t_1) = -6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{s} + 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (3.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = -9.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
x(t) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t\,(t - 6.0) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity and the acceleration have opposite signs at $t = 0$, therefore the body is moving in the negative sense and is slowing down. The speed decreases until the velocity vanishes, which happens at $t_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and after that instant velocity and acceleration are both positive, so the body **moves back** towards the origin with increasing speed.

The position at $t_1$ is $-9.0\,\mathrm{m}$, the farthest point reached on the negative side. Setting $x(t) = 0$ gives the two roots $t = 0$, which is the departure, and $t_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{s}$, which is the return. The return instant is twice the reversal instant, since the motion after the reversal repeats the first part with the velocities reversed.

At $t_2$ the velocity is $-6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} + 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{s} = +6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, equal in magnitude to the initial velocity, in agreement with the relation between velocity and position, which gives the same $v^2$ at the same position.

</div>
