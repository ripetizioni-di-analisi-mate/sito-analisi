## Exercise 1

Two cars travel on the same straight road. Car $A$ is at the origin at $t = 0$ and moves in the positive sense at the constant speed $v_A = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$; car $B$ is at $x_B(0) = 300\,\mathrm{m}$ and moves towards $A$ at the constant speed $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the instant and the position at which they meet.

$$
\begin{cases}
x_A(t) = 15\,t \\[6pt]
x_B(t) = 300 - 25\,t
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{(SI units)}
$$
$$
x_A(t) = x_B(t)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
15\,t = 300 - 25\,t
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
40\,t = 300
$$
$$
t = \frac{300\,\mathrm{m}}{40\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 7.5\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
x = 15\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 7.5\,\mathrm{s} = 112.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both motions are referred to the same axis, to the same origin and to the same initial instant, which is what allows the two laws of motion to be compared. Car $B$ moves in the negative sense, therefore its velocity enters its law of motion with a minus sign.

The two cars meet when they occupy the same position, hence the condition is $x_A(t) = x_B(t)$, which gives one equation of first degree in the single unknown $t$. Its solution is $t = 7.5\,\mathrm{s}$, and substituting it in either law of motion gives $x = 112.5\,\mathrm{m}$.

The coefficient $40\,\mathrm{m/s}$ that appears in the equation is the sum of the two speeds, that is the rate at which the gap between the cars closes. The initial gap of $300\,\mathrm{m}$ divided by this rate gives directly the **meeting time**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bus leaves a stop from rest with constant acceleration $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At that same instant a passenger who is $12\,\mathrm{m}$ behind the bus starts running towards it at the constant speed $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find whether the passenger reaches the bus and, if so, at which instant and after how many meters.

$$
\begin{cases}
x_{\mathrm{bus}}(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2 = 0.50\,t^2 \\[6pt]
x_{\mathrm{p}}(t) = -12 + 6.0\,t
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{(SI units, origin at the stop)}
$$
$$
0.50\,t^2 = -12 + 6.0\,t
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t^2 - 12\,t + 24 = 0
$$
$$
t = \frac{12 \pm \sqrt{144 - 96}}{2} = \frac{12 \pm 6.93}{2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_1 = 2.54\,\mathrm{s},
\quad
t_2 = 9.46\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = 0.50 \cdot (2.536\,\mathrm{s})^2 \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.2\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The origin is placed at the stop and the positive sense is that of the motion, therefore the passenger starts at $-12\,\mathrm{m}$ and the bus at the origin. The bus has constant acceleration and zero initial velocity, so its law of motion is $\dfrac{1}{2}at^2$; the passenger moves at constant velocity, so his law of motion is a first-degree function of the time.

The passenger reaches the bus when the two positions are equal, which gives a quadratic equation. Its discriminant is $144 - 96 = 48 > 0$, hence there are two real roots and the passenger **does reach** the bus, at the first of them, $t_1 = 2.54\,\mathrm{s}$, that is $3.2\,\mathrm{m}$ past the stop.

The second root $t_2 = 9.46\,\mathrm{s}$ is the instant at which the bus, which meanwhile has become faster than the passenger, would catch up with him again. Between the two instants the passenger is ahead of the bus. The velocity of the bus at $t_1$ is $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 2.536\,\mathrm{s} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, smaller than $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is why the passenger is still gaining ground at that instant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A stone is released from rest from a bridge $h = 24\,\mathrm{m}$ above the water. One second later a second stone is thrown vertically downward from the same point. The two stones reach the water at the same instant. Find the initial speed of the second stone. Air resistance is negligible.

$$
h = \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t_1^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2\,h}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 24\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 2.212\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
t_2 = t_1 - 1.00\,\mathrm{s} = 1.212\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
h = v_0\,t_2 + \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t_2^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_0 = \frac{h - \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t_2^2}{t_2}
$$
$$
v_0 = \frac{24\,\mathrm{m} - \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (1.212\,\mathrm{s})^2}{1.212\,\mathrm{s}} = \frac{24\,\mathrm{m} - 7.21\,\mathrm{m}}{1.212\,\mathrm{s}} = 13.9\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is taken pointing downward with the origin on the bridge, so both stones have positive initial velocity and positive acceleration $g$, and the water is at $24\,\mathrm{m}$.

The first stone is released from rest, therefore its fall time follows from $h = \dfrac{1}{2}g t_1^2$ and equals $2.212\,\mathrm{s}$. The second stone leaves one second later and arrives at the same instant, hence it falls for $t_2 = 1.212\,\mathrm{s}$, a shorter time, and this is the link between the two motions.

Writing the law of motion of the second stone with $t = t_2$ and $y = h$ gives an equation of first degree in $v_0$, whose solution is $13.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The term $\dfrac{1}{2}g t_2^2 = 7.21\,\mathrm{m}$ is the part of the fall due to gravity alone, so the initial push must account for the remaining $16.8\,\mathrm{m}$ in $1.212\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
