## Exercise 1

A body starts at the origin from rest and moves along the $x$ axis with acceleration $a(t) = 6.0 - 2.0\,t$, in SI units. Find the velocity and the position as functions of the time, the instant of maximum velocity and the position at that instant, and the velocity at $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
v(t) = v_0 + \int_{0}^{t} a(\tau)\,d\tau = \int_{0}^{t} (6.0 - 2.0\,\tau)\,d\tau = 6.0\,t - t^2
$$
$$
x(t) = x_0 + \int_{0}^{t} v(\tau)\,d\tau = \int_{0}^{t} (6.0\,\tau - \tau^2)\,d\tau = 3.0\,t^2 - \frac{t^3}{3}
$$
$$
a(t) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_m = 3.0\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
v(t_m) = 18\,\mathrm{m/s} - 9.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 9.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x(t_m) = 3.0 \cdot 9.0\,\mathrm{m} - \frac{27}{3}\,\mathrm{m} = 27\,\mathrm{m} - 9.0\,\mathrm{m} = 18\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v(5.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 6.0 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s} - 25\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{f="6*x-x^2" area="6*x-x^2;0;0;3" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6.6 ymin=-1.5 ymax=10.5 nomi="t,v" punti="3,9,pieno;5,5,pieno;6,0,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="Velocity of the body: it grows until $t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$, where the acceleration vanishes, and the shaded area $18\,\mathrm{m}$ is the position reached at that instant"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is not constant, therefore the equations of uniformly accelerated motion do not apply and the velocity is obtained by **integrating the acceleration**. The body starts from rest, hence $v_0 = 0$ and the integral alone gives $v(t) = 6.0\,t - t^2$.

The position is obtained by integrating the velocity just found, with $x_0 = 0$ because the body starts at the origin. Each integration introduces one constant, and the two initial conditions fix them both.

The velocity attains its maximum where its derivative, that is the acceleration, vanishes, which happens at $t_m = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$; the acceleration is positive before that instant and negative after it, therefore $t_m$ is a maximum. The velocity there is $9.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the position is $18\,\mathrm{m}$, which is also the shaded area of the figure.

At $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity has fallen to $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and is still positive, so the body is still advancing; it stops at $t = 6.0\,\mathrm{s}$, where $v = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The velocity of a body moving along the $x$ axis is $v(t) = 4.0\,t - 0.50\,t^2$, in SI units, and at $t = 0$ the body is at $x_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the acceleration as a function of the time and the instant at which it vanishes, the law of motion, and the displacement and the average velocity between $t = 0$ and $t = 6.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt} = 4.0 - t
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
a(t) = 0 \ \text{ for } \ t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x(t) = 2.0 + \int_{0}^{t} (4.0\,\tau - 0.50\,\tau^2)\,d\tau = 2.0 + 2.0\,t^2 - \frac{t^3}{6}
$$
$$
x(6.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 2.0\,\mathrm{m} + 72\,\mathrm{m} - 36\,\mathrm{m} = 38\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta x = 38\,\mathrm{m} - 2.0\,\mathrm{m} = 36\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
v_{\mathrm{av}} = \frac{36\,\mathrm{m}}{6.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is the derivative of the velocity, hence $a(t) = 4.0 - t$, which vanishes at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$. At that instant the velocity attains its maximum value $v(4.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, since the acceleration changes from positive to negative there.

The law of motion is obtained by integrating the velocity, and the constant of integration is fixed by the initial position $x_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, which must be added to the integral.

The velocity $4.0\,t - 0.50\,t^2 = 0.50\,t\,(8.0 - t)$ is positive for $0 < t < 8.0\,\mathrm{s}$, therefore the body never reverses within the interval considered and the **distance travelled coincides with the displacement**, $36\,\mathrm{m}$. The average velocity over the interval is $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, a value between the initial velocity $0$ and the maximum $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, as it must be.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body passes the origin at $t = 0$ with velocity $v_0 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and has acceleration $a(t) = -3.0\,t$, in SI units. Find the instant at which it reverses and the position at that instant, the position and the velocity at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and the distance travelled between $t = 0$ and $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
v(t) = 12 + \int_{0}^{t} (-3.0\,\tau)\,d\tau = 12 - 1.5\,t^2
$$
$$
x(t) = \int_{0}^{t} (12 - 1.5\,\tau^2)\,d\tau = 12\,t - 0.50\,t^3
$$
$$
v(t) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{12}{1.5}} = 2\sqrt{2} = 2.83\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x(t_1) = 12 \cdot 2\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m} - 0.50 \cdot 16\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m} = 16\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m} = 22.6\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v(4.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 12\,\mathrm{m/s} - 24\,\mathrm{m/s} = -12\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
x(4.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 48\,\mathrm{m} - 32\,\mathrm{m} = 16\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
s = x(t_1) + \big[x(t_1) - x(4.0\,\mathrm{s})\big] = 22.63\,\mathrm{m} + 6.63\,\mathrm{m} = 29.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration depends on the time, hence the velocity is obtained by integration starting from the given initial velocity, which gives $v(t) = 12 - 1.5\,t^2$. A second integration with $x_0 = 0$ gives the law of motion.

The velocity vanishes at $t_1 = 2\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{s} = 2.83\,\mathrm{s}$ and is negative afterwards, therefore the body **reverses** at that instant, where it occupies its farthest position $16\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m} = 22.6\,\mathrm{m}$.

At $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the body has already come back to $16\,\mathrm{m}$ and is moving in the negative sense at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The displacement over the whole interval is $16\,\mathrm{m}$, while the distance travelled adds the two stretches, $22.63\,\mathrm{m}$ forward and $6.63\,\mathrm{m}$ backward, that is $29.3\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

The velocity of a body moving along the $x$ axis is $v(t) = 3.0\,t^2 - 12\,t + 9.0$, in SI units, and the body is at the origin at $t = 0$. Find the instants at which the body is at rest, the displacement and the distance travelled between $t = 0$ and $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
v(t) = 3.0\,(t^2 - 4.0\,t + 3.0) = 3.0\,(t - 1.0)(t - 3.0)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s},
\quad
t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x(t) = \int_{0}^{t} \big(3.0\,\tau^2 - 12\,\tau + 9.0\big)\,d\tau = t^3 - 6.0\,t^2 + 9.0\,t
$$
$$
x(0) = 0,
\qquad
x(1.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 4.0\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x(3.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 0,
\qquad
x(4.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta x = x(4.0\,\mathrm{s}) - x(0) = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
s = 4.0\,\mathrm{m} + 4.0\,\mathrm{m} + 4.0\,\mathrm{m} = 12\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is a second-degree function of the time and factors as $3.0\,(t - 1.0)(t - 3.0)$, therefore it vanishes at $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$. It is positive outside the interval between the two roots and negative inside it, so the body advances, then goes back, then advances again.

The law of motion is obtained by integrating the velocity with $x_0 = 0$. Evaluating it at the two instants of rest and at the ends of the interval gives the four positions $0$, $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$, $0$ and $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

The displacement is the difference between the final and the initial position, $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance travelled is instead the sum of the lengths of the three stretches: $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the origin to $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$, then $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ back to the origin, then $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ forward again, that is $12\,\mathrm{m}$ in total. The body passes the origin twice, at $t = 0$ and at $t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
