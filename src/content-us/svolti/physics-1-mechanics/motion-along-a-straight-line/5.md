## Exercise 1

A subway train starts from rest, accelerates with $a_1 = 1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until it reaches $v = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, travels at that speed, and finally brakes with deceleration $a_3 = 1.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until it stops at the next station, $D = 1200\,\mathrm{m}$ away. Find the duration of each stage, the total duration of the trip and the average velocity.

$$
t_1 = \frac{v}{a_1},
\qquad
d_1 = \frac{v^2}{2\,a_1},
\qquad
t_3 = \frac{v}{a_3},
\qquad
d_3 = \frac{v^2}{2\,a_3},
\qquad
d_2 = D - d_1 - d_3,
\qquad
t_2 = \frac{d_2}{v}
$$
$$
t_1 = \frac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 16.7\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
d_1 = \frac{(20\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 166.7\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
t_3 = \frac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 12.5\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
d_3 = \frac{(20\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 1.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 125\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
d_2 = 1200\,\mathrm{m} - 166.7\,\mathrm{m} - 125\,\mathrm{m} = 908.3\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
t_2 = \frac{908.3\,\mathrm{m}}{20\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 45.4\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
t_{\mathrm{tot}} = 16.7\,\mathrm{s} + 45.4\,\mathrm{s} + 12.5\,\mathrm{s} = 74.6\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
v_{\mathrm{av}} = \frac{1200\,\mathrm{m}}{74.6\,\mathrm{s}} = 16.1\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 16.67,20 62.08,20 74.58,0" poligoni="0,0 16.67,20 62.08,20 74.58,0" xmin=-3 xmax=82 ymin=-2 ymax=24 nomi="t,v" punti="16.67,20,pieno;62.08,20,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="Velocity of the train: the area of the trapezoid is the distance between the two stations, $1200\,\mathrm{m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each stage has its own constant acceleration, therefore the **equations of uniformly accelerated motion** are applied one stage at a time, and the final velocity of a stage is the initial velocity of the next one.

The first and the third stage are determined by the cruising speed and by their accelerations alone, since in both the velocity changes between $0$ and $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The relation between velocity and position gives the two distances $166.7\,\mathrm{m}$ and $125\,\mathrm{m}$, and the equation of the velocity gives the two durations.

The middle stage covers what is left of the $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, that is $908.3\,\mathrm{m}$, at constant speed, hence its duration is the distance divided by $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The total duration is the sum of the three, $74.6\,\mathrm{s}$, and the average velocity of the trip is $16.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, smaller than the cruising speed because of the two stages at lower speed.

The figure confirms the result as an area: the trapezoid has parallel sides $74.58\,\mathrm{s}$ and $45.42\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so its area is $\dfrac{(74.58 + 45.42) \cdot 20}{2}\,\mathrm{m} = 1200\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car starts from rest and accelerates with $a_1 = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$, then travels at constant speed for $10\,\mathrm{s}$, and finally brakes uniformly to rest in $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the cruising speed, the distance covered in each stage, the total distance and the average speed.

$$
v = a_1\,t_1 = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{s} = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
d_1 = \frac{1}{2}\,a_1\,t_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (6.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 45\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
d_2 = v\,t_2 = 15\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{s} = 150\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
d_3 = \frac{1}{2}\,v\,t_3 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 15\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} = 30\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
|a_3| = \frac{15\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 3.75\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
d = 45\,\mathrm{m} + 150\,\mathrm{m} + 30\,\mathrm{m} = 225\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\text{average speed} = \frac{225\,\mathrm{m}}{20\,\mathrm{s}} = 11.25\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cruising speed is reached at the end of the first stage, hence it follows from $v = a_1 t_1$. The distance of the first stage is computed with the equation of the position with zero initial velocity, which gives $45\,\mathrm{m}$.

In the second stage the velocity is constant, therefore the distance is the product of the velocity and the duration. In the third stage the velocity goes from $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to zero, so the average velocity of that stage is $\dfrac{15 + 0}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 7.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the distance is $30\,\mathrm{m}$. The deceleration follows from $v = |a_3| t_3$.

The three distances add to $225\,\mathrm{m}$ and the three durations to $20\,\mathrm{s}$, hence the average speed is $11.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The body never reverses, therefore the average speed and the magnitude of the average velocity coincide.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A runner covers $100\,\mathrm{m}$ in $10.4\,\mathrm{s}$. She accelerates uniformly from rest over the first $20\,\mathrm{m}$, reaching her top speed, and then runs the remaining $80\,\mathrm{m}$ at that constant speed. Find the top speed and the acceleration of the first stage.

$$
d_1 = \frac{v_{\max}}{2}\,t_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_1 = \frac{2\,d_1}{v_{\max}},
\qquad
t_2 = \frac{d_2}{v_{\max}}
$$
$$
t_1 + t_2 = \frac{2\,d_1 + d_2}{v_{\max}} = T
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_{\max} = \frac{2\,d_1 + d_2}{T}
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \frac{2 \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m} + 80\,\mathrm{m}}{10.4\,\mathrm{s}} = \frac{120\,\mathrm{m}}{10.4\,\mathrm{s}} = 11.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a = \frac{v_{\max}^2}{2\,d_1} = \frac{(11.538\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m}} = 3.33\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
t_1 = \frac{2 \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m}}{11.538\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 3.47\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
t_2 = \frac{80\,\mathrm{m}}{11.538\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 6.93\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first stage the velocity grows uniformly from zero to $v_{\max}$, therefore the average velocity of that stage is $\dfrac{v_{\max}}{2}$ and the duration is $t_1 = \dfrac{2 d_1}{v_{\max}}$. In the second stage the velocity is constant, hence $t_2 = \dfrac{d_2}{v_{\max}}$.

Both durations contain the same unknown $v_{\max}$ in the denominator, so adding them and imposing the total time gives a single equation of first degree in $v_{\max}$, whose solution is $v_{\max} = \dfrac{2 d_1 + d_2}{T} = 11.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The acceleration follows from the **relation between velocity and position** applied to the first stage, $v_{\max}^2 = 2 a d_1$, which gives $3.33\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. As a check, the two durations add to $3.47\,\mathrm{s} + 6.93\,\mathrm{s} = 10.4\,\mathrm{s}$, the given total time. The average speed over the race is $\dfrac{100\,\mathrm{m}}{10.4\,\mathrm{s}} = 9.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, smaller than the top speed because of the starting stage.

</div>
