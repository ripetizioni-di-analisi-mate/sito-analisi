## Exercise 1

The velocity-time graph of a body moving along the $x$ axis is a straight segment from $v = 0$ at $t = 0$ to $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$, then a horizontal segment up to $t = 10\,\mathrm{s}$, then a straight segment down to $v = 0$ at $t = 14\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the acceleration in each stage, the total displacement and the average velocity.

$$
a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}
\qquad\text{(slope of the velocity-time graph)}
$$
$$
a_1 = \frac{12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
a_2 = 0,
\qquad
a_3 = \frac{-12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4.0\,\mathrm{s}} = -3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\Delta x = \int_{0}^{14\,\mathrm{s}} v(t)\,dt
\qquad\text{(area under the velocity-time graph)}
$$
$$
\Delta x_1 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{m/s} = 24\,\mathrm{m},
\quad
\Delta x_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{m/s} = 72\,\mathrm{m},
\quad
\Delta x_3 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{m/s} = 24\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta x = 24\,\mathrm{m} + 72\,\mathrm{m} + 24\,\mathrm{m} = 120\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
v_{\mathrm{av}} = \frac{120\,\mathrm{m}}{14\,\mathrm{s}} = 8.6\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 4,12 10,12 14,0" poligoni="0,0 4,12 10,12 14,0" xmin=-0.6 xmax=15.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=14.5 nomi="t,v" punti="4,12,pieno;10,12,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="Velocity-time graph: the shaded trapezoid has area $\dfrac{(14 + 6) \cdot 12}{2} = 120\,\mathrm{m}$, which is the displacement"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is the **slope of the velocity-time graph**, therefore it is read stage by stage. In the first stage the velocity grows by $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$, hence $a_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$; in the second stage the graph is horizontal, hence $a_2 = 0$; in the third stage the velocity drops by $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$, hence $a_3 = -3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The displacement is the **area under the velocity-time graph**. The velocity is never negative, so every area is counted positively and the distance traveled coincides with the displacement. The two triangles contribute $24\,\mathrm{m}$ each and the rectangle $72\,\mathrm{m}$, which add to $120\,\mathrm{m}$.

The whole figure is a trapezoid with parallel sides $14\,\mathrm{s}$ and $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, therefore its area is $\dfrac{(14 + 6) \cdot 12}{2}\,\mathrm{m} = 120\,\mathrm{m}$, which confirms the sum. The average velocity is the displacement divided by the total time, that is $8.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, smaller than the cruising velocity $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ because of the two stages at lower speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body moves along the $x$ axis with velocity $v(t) = 8.0 - 2.0\,t$, with $v$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and $t$ in seconds. Find the acceleration, the displacement and the distance traveled between $t = 0$ and $t = 7.0\,\mathrm{s}$, the average velocity and the average speed over that interval.

$$
a = \frac{dv}{dt} = -2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
v(t) = 0 \ \text{ for } \ t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\Delta x_{+} = \int_{0}^{4.0} (8.0 - 2.0\,t)\,dt = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = +16\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta x_{-} = \int_{4.0}^{7.0} (8.0 - 2.0\,t)\,dt = -\,\frac{1}{2} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = -9.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta x = 16\,\mathrm{m} - 9.0\,\mathrm{m} = 7.0\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
s = 16\,\mathrm{m} + 9.0\,\mathrm{m} = 25\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_{\mathrm{av}} = \frac{7.0\,\mathrm{m}}{7.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
\text{average speed} = \frac{25\,\mathrm{m}}{7.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 3.6\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{f="8-2*x" area="8-2*x;0;0;4|0;8-2*x;4;7" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7.8 ymin=-7.5 ymax=9.5 nomi="t,v" punti="4,0,pieno;7,-6,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="The area above the axis is $+16\,\mathrm{m}$ and the area below it is $-9\,\mathrm{m}$: their sum is the displacement, the sum of their magnitudes is the distance traveled"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is a first-degree function of the time, therefore the acceleration, which is its derivative, is the constant $-2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The velocity vanishes at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and changes sign there, hence the body **reverses** at that instant and the interval must be split at it.

On $[0, 4.0\,\mathrm{s}]$ the velocity is positive and the area under the graph is the triangle of base $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, that is $+16\,\mathrm{m}$. On $[4.0\,\mathrm{s}, 7.0\,\mathrm{s}]$ the velocity is negative, the graph lies below the axis and the area counts negatively, giving $-9.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

The displacement is the signed sum of the two areas, $7.0\,\mathrm{m}$, while the distance traveled adds their magnitudes, $25\,\mathrm{m}$. The average velocity uses the displacement and the average speed uses the distance traveled, therefore the second is more than three times the first.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body starts from rest at the origin. Its acceleration is $a = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $0 \le t \le 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$, then $a = 0$ for $3.0\,\mathrm{s} \le t \le 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, then $a = -2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $5.0\,\mathrm{s} \le t \le 9.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the velocity at $t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$, $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $9.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and the position at $t = 9.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\Delta v = \int a(t)\,dt
\qquad\text{(area under the acceleration-time graph)}
$$
$$
v(3.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{s} = 12\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v(5.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v(9.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 12\,\mathrm{m/s} - 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Delta x_1 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{m/s} = 18\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta x_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{m/s} = 24\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta x_3 = \frac{12\,\mathrm{m/s} + 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} = 32\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
x(9.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 18\,\mathrm{m} + 24\,\mathrm{m} + 32\,\mathrm{m} = 74\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,4 3,4 3,0 5,0 5,-2 9,-2" xmin=-0.5 xmax=10 ymin=-3.5 ymax=5.5 nomi="t,a" legenda=0 titolo="Acceleration-time graph: the area between the graph and the axis gives the change in velocity, $+12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the first stage and $-8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the third"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is constant on each stage, therefore the change in velocity over a stage is the product of the acceleration and the duration, which is the **area under the acceleration-time graph** of that stage. The first stage adds $+12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to the initial velocity, which is zero, the second adds nothing, and the third subtracts $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, leaving $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The displacement of each stage is the area under the velocity-time graph of that stage. The first stage gives a triangle of area $18\,\mathrm{m}$, the second a rectangle of area $24\,\mathrm{m}$, and the third a trapezoid whose parallel sides are the velocities at its ends, of area $32\,\mathrm{m}$.

The velocity stays positive throughout, so the three contributions add and the position at $t = 9.0\,\mathrm{s}$ is $74\,\mathrm{m}$. As a check, the equations of uniformly accelerated motion applied to the third stage give $\Delta x_3 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} - \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (4.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 32\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
