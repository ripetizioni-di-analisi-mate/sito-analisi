## Exercise 1

A person of mass $m = 65\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on a scale inside an elevator. Find the reading of the scale when the elevator is at rest, when it accelerates upward with $a_1 = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and when it accelerates downward with $a_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
N - m g = m\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N = m\,(g + a)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_0 &= 65\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 638\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
N_1 &= 65\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 + 1.5)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 735\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
N_2 &= 65\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 2.0)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 508\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A scale measures the force with which the body presses on its plate, and by the **third law** that force has the same magnitude as the normal force $\vec{N}$ with which the plate pushes the person. The frame of the building is inertial, and in it the forces on the person are the weight and the normal force, so with the $y$ axis vertical and upward the second law gives $N = m(g+a)$, which is the formula for the **apparent weight**.

At rest the acceleration vanishes and the reading equals the weight, $638\,\mathrm{N}$. With an upward acceleration the normal force must exceed the weight in order to produce it, hence the reading rises to $735\,\mathrm{N}$. With a downward acceleration the reading falls to $508\,\mathrm{N}$. A scale graduated in kilograms would show $66.3\,\mathrm{kg}$, $65.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $51.8\,\mathrm{kg}$, although the mass of the person has not changed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An elevator cabin with its load has total mass $m = 800\,\mathrm{kg}$ and hangs from a single cable. The cabin is moving upward and slowing down with a deceleration of magnitude $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the tension of the cable. Find also the largest upward acceleration allowed if the cable can support at most $T_{\max} = 12.0\,\mathrm{kN}$.

$$
T - m g = m\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = m\,(g + a)
$$
$$
a = -1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = 800\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 1.2)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 6.89 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
a_{\max} = \frac{T_{\max}}{m} - g = \frac{12.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}}{800\,\mathrm{kg}} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 15.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 5.19\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body chosen is the cabin with its load, on which the weight and the pull of the cable act. The $y$ axis is vertical and upward, therefore the second law gives $T = m(g+a)$ with the same structure as the apparent weight.

The cabin moves upward and slows down, so the velocity points up and its magnitude decreases, hence the acceleration points down and $a = -1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The tension is then $6.89\,\mathrm{kN}$, which is **less** than the weight $7.85\,\mathrm{kN}$, as it must be for a body accelerating downward. Imposing $T \le T_{\max}$ in the same relation and solving for $a$ gives the largest upward acceleration, $5.19\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 2.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from a spring scale attached to the ceiling of an elevator. The scale reads $T = 20.0\,\mathrm{N}$. Find the acceleration of the elevator, with its direction and sense, and state the reading of the scale if the cable broke and the elevator fell freely.

$$
T - m g = m\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{T}{m} - g
$$
$$
a = \frac{20.0\,\mathrm{N}}{2.5\,\mathrm{kg}} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 8.00\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = -1.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a = -g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = m\,(g - g) = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces on the block are the weight $mg = 24.5\,\mathrm{N}$ and the pull of the scale, whose magnitude is the reading $T$. With the $y$ axis vertical and upward, the second law gives $a = \dfrac{T}{m} - g$.

The reading is smaller than the weight, therefore the acceleration comes out negative, that is **directed downward**, with magnitude $1.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The sense of the velocity is not determined by these data, since a downward acceleration belongs both to an elevator that descends and speeds up and to one that rises and slows down.

In free fall the only force on the block would be its weight, hence $a = -g$ and the same relation gives $T = 0$: the block would float beside the scale without stretching it, which is the state called apparent weightlessness.

</div>
