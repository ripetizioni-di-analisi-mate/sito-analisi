## Exercise 1

A crate of mass $m = 15\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pushed along a frictionless horizontal floor by a force of magnitude $F = 80\,\mathrm{N}$ directed at $\theta = 20^\circ$ below the horizontal. Find the acceleration of the crate and the normal force exerted by the floor.

$$
\begin{cases}
F\cos\theta = m\,a \\[4pt]
N - m g - F\sin\theta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = \frac{F\cos\theta}{m},
\qquad
N = m g + F\sin\theta
$$
$$
a = \frac{80\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.9397}{15\,\mathrm{kg}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
N = 15\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} + 80\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.3420 = 147.15\,\mathrm{N} + 27.36\,\mathrm{N} = 175\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno" vettori="0,0,1.504,-0.547,F;0,0,0,-2.943,w;0,0,0,3.49,N" segmenti="0,0,2.4,0" etichette="1.75,-0.2,20°" riferimento="-1.5,-2.4,0,0.8,x,y" xmin=-2.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-3.4 ymax=3.9 titolo="Free-body diagram of the crate, with the arrows to scale at $50\,\mathrm{N}$ per unit: the vertical component of $\vec{F}$ presses the crate down, hence $N > mg$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **free-body diagram** of the crate contains the push $\vec{F}$, the weight and the normal force of the floor. The $x$ axis is horizontal in the sense of the motion and the $y$ axis is vertical and upward, so the push has the components $F\cos\theta$ and $-F\sin\theta$, since it points downward.

The crate slides on the floor, hence $a_y = 0$ and the equation of the $y$ axis gives $N = mg + F\sin\theta = 175\,\mathrm{N}$. This value exceeds the weight $147.2\,\mathrm{N}$, because the push has a downward component that the floor must support as well. Along $x$ the only force is $F\cos\theta$, therefore the acceleration is $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, smaller than the value $\dfrac{F}{m} = 5.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ that a horizontal push of the same magnitude would produce.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $m = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a frictionless horizontal surface. Two horizontal forces act on it: $\vec{F}_1$ of magnitude $30\,\mathrm{N}$ directed along the positive $x$ axis, and $\vec{F}_2$ of magnitude $20\,\mathrm{N}$ making an angle of $120^\circ$ with the positive $x$ axis. Find the magnitude and the direction of the acceleration.

$$
\begin{cases}
F_x = F_1 + F_2\cos 120^\circ \\[4pt]
F_y = F_2\sin 120^\circ
\end{cases}
$$
$$
F_x = 30\,\mathrm{N} - 10\,\mathrm{N} = 20\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F_y = 20\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.8660 = 17.32\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{F_x^2 + F_y^2} = \sqrt{(20)^2 + (17.32)^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 26.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{F_y}{F_x} = \frac{17.32}{20} = 0.866
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 40.9^\circ
$$
$$
a = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m} = \frac{26.5\,\mathrm{N}}{8.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno" vettori="0,0,3,0,F₁;0,0,-1,1.732,F₂;0,0,2,1.732,F net" segmenti="2,1.732,3,0;2,1.732,-1,1.732" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-1.1 ymax=2.6 titolo="The two forces and their resultant, to scale at $10\,\mathrm{N}$ per unit: $F_{\text{net}} = 26.5\,\mathrm{N}$ at $40.9^\circ$ from the $x$ axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two forces lie in the horizontal plane and the surface is frictionless, so the weight and the normal force balance each other and the net force is the sum of $\vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_2$. Forces add as vectors, therefore the sum is obtained by adding the components separately.

The second force makes an obtuse angle with the $x$ axis, hence its $x$ component is negative and reduces the total to $20\,\mathrm{N}$, while its $y$ component is the only force along $y$. The magnitude of the resultant follows from the Pythagorean theorem and its angle from the arctangent, which is applicable here because $F_x > 0$. The **second law** then gives the acceleration $3.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, in the same direction and sense as the resultant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sign of mass $m = 12\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs at rest from a knot held by two cables. The left cable makes the angle $\alpha = 35^\circ$ with the horizontal and the right cable the angle $\beta = 55^\circ$. Find the tension of each cable.

$$
\begin{cases}
T_2\cos\beta - T_1\cos\alpha = 0 \\[4pt]
T_1\sin\alpha + T_2\sin\beta - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
T_1 = \frac{m g\cos\beta}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)},
\qquad
T_2 = \frac{m g\cos\alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)}
$$
$$
m g = 12\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 117.7\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
\sin(\alpha + \beta) = \sin 90^\circ = 1
$$
$$
T_1 = 117.7\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.5736 = 67.5\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
T_2 = 117.7\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.8192 = 96.4\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno" vettori="0,0,-1.106,0.774,T₁;0,0,1.106,1.58,T₂;0,0,0,-2.354,w" segmenti="-1.5,0,1.5,0" etichette="-0.72,0.17,35°;0.62,0.17,55°" xmin=-2.2 xmax=2.2 ymin=-2.9 ymax=2.1 titolo="Free-body diagram of the knot, to scale at $50\,\mathrm{N}$ per unit: the two tensions and the weight of the sign add to zero"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The body chosen is the knot, because the three forces of interest meet there: the pull of each cable, directed along that cable and away from the knot, and the pull of the vertical cable that carries the sign, whose magnitude equals the weight $mg$ since the sign hangs at rest.

The knot is at rest, hence the **first law** gives zero net force, which in components is the system written above. The first equation states that the horizontal components of the two tensions are opposite, and the second that their vertical components together carry the weight. Solving gives $T_1 = 67.5\,\mathrm{N}$ and $T_2 = 96.4\,\mathrm{N}$. The right cable is the steeper one, therefore it carries the larger share of the weight and its tension is the larger of the two.

</div>
