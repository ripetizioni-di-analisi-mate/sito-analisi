## Exercise 1

Two blocks of masses $m_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rest side by side in contact on a frictionless horizontal floor. A horizontal force of magnitude $F = 24\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied to $m_1$ and pushes the pair toward $m_2$. Find the acceleration of the two blocks and the force they exert on each other.

$$
\begin{cases}
F - C = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
C = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2},
\qquad
C = \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,F
$$
$$
a = \frac{24\,\mathrm{N}}{8.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
C = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 6.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-0.3,0 5,0" poligoni="1,0 3,0 3,2.2 1,2.2;3,0 4.4,0 4.4,2.2 3,2.2" vettori="0.1,1,1,1,F;3,1.25,3.9,1.25,on m₂;3,0.6,2.1,0.6,on m₁" etichette="1.9,1.9,m₁;3.65,1.9,m₂" xmin=-0.6 xmax=5.4 ymin=-0.8 ymax=2.9 titolo="The blocks push each other with forces of equal magnitude $C = 6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ and opposite sense, each entering the equation of the other block"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two blocks stay in contact, hence they have the same acceleration $a$. The force they exert on each other is a contact force perpendicular to the common face, so it is horizontal, and by the **third law** its two members have the same magnitude $C$, so the block $m_1$ pushes $m_2$ forward, while $m_2$ pushes $m_1$ backward.

The floor is frictionless and the vertical forces balance on each block, so only the horizontal equations remain. Adding them eliminates $C$ and gives the acceleration of the pair treated as a single body of mass $8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, that is $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The equation of $m_2$ then gives $C = 6.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

The contact force is a quarter of the applied force, because $m_2$ is a quarter of the total mass and the contact force is the only force that accelerates it. Applying the same force to $m_2$ instead, in the opposite sense, would give the same acceleration and a contact force of $18\,\mathrm{N}$, since in that case the pushed block would be $m_1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three blocks of masses $m_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, $m_2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_3 = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lie on a frictionless horizontal table and are joined in a row by two ideal strings, in the order $m_1$, $m_2$, $m_3$. A horizontal force of magnitude $F = 36\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied to $m_1$ along the line of the strings. Find the acceleration of the system and the tension of each string.

$$
F = (m_1 + m_2 + m_3)\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2 + m_3} = \frac{36\,\mathrm{N}}{12.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T_{12} = (m_2 + m_3)\,a,
\qquad
T_{23} = m_3\,a
$$
$$
T_{12} = 10.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 30\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
T_{23} = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 18\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The strings are inextensible, therefore the three blocks have one single acceleration, and the table is frictionless, therefore the only external horizontal force on the system is $F$. Treating the three blocks as a **single body** of mass $12.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives $a = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at once, since the two tensions are internal forces and cancel in the sum of the three equations.

Each tension is then obtained by writing the equation of the part of the system that the string has to pull. The first string pulls $m_2$ and $m_3$, whose total mass is $10.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, hence $T_{12} = 30\,\mathrm{N}$; the second string pulls $m_3$ alone, hence $T_{23} = 18\,\mathrm{N}$.

As a check, the equation of the first block gives $F - T_{12} = 36\,\mathrm{N} - 30\,\mathrm{N} = 6.0\,\mathrm{N}$, which equals $m_1 a = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The tension falls along the row, because each string has less mass left to accelerate.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two blocks of masses $m_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lie on a frictionless horizontal table and are joined by an ideal horizontal string. A force of magnitude $F = 32\,\mathrm{N}$, directed at $\theta = 25^\circ$ above the horizontal, is applied to $m_1$ and pulls the pair away from $m_2$. Find the acceleration of the system, the tension of the string and the normal force on each block.

$$
\begin{cases}
F\cos\theta - T = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
T = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{F\cos\theta}{m_1 + m_2},
\qquad
T = \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,F\cos\theta
$$
$$
a = \frac{32\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.9063}{8.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{29.0\,\mathrm{N}}{8.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3.63\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.625\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 10.9\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
N_1 = m_1 g - F\sin\theta = 49.05\,\mathrm{N} - 13.52\,\mathrm{N} = 35.5\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
N_2 = m_2 g = 29.4\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The applied force is oblique, hence it is resolved first, and the component $F\cos\theta = 29.0\,\mathrm{N}$ lies along the motion while the component $F\sin\theta = 13.5\,\mathrm{N}$ is vertical and upward. Only the horizontal component accelerates the system, therefore the acceleration is $3.63\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the tension, which is the only horizontal force on $m_2$, is $10.9\,\mathrm{N}$.

The vertical equations are written for each block separately, because the vertical component of $\vec{F}$ acts on $m_1$ alone and the string is horizontal, so it transmits nothing along the vertical. On $m_1$ the floor has to support the weight reduced by the upward component, hence $N_1 = 35.5\,\mathrm{N}$; on $m_2$ the only vertical forces are the weight and the normal force, hence $N_2 = 29.4\,\mathrm{N}$.

Both values are positive, therefore the two blocks remain on the table, and the solution is consistent with the assumption $a_y = 0$ used to write the equations.

</div>
