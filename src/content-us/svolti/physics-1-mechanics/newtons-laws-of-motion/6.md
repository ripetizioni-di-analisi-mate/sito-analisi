## Exercise 1

An Atwood machine carries two bodies of masses $m_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at the ends of an ideal string that passes over a fixed ideal pulley. The system is released from rest. Find the acceleration, the tension of the string, the time the heavier body takes to descend $h = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ and the speed it has at that instant.

$$
\begin{cases}
m_1 g - T = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
T - m_2 g = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\,m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g
$$
$$
a = \frac{2.0}{10.0} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
T = \frac{2 \cdot 6.0 \cdot 4.0}{10.0}\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 47.1\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2\,h}{a}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m}}{1.962\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 1.24\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
v = \sqrt{2\,a\,h} = 2.43\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body $m_1$ is the heavier one, therefore it descends while $m_2$ rises, and for each body the positive sense is taken as the sense of its own motion. The string is ideal, hence the same tension $T$ acts on both bodies, and it is inextensible, hence the two accelerations have the same magnitude $a$.

Adding the two equations eliminates $T$ and gives the **Atwood machine** formula $a = 1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, which is one fifth of $g$ because the unbalanced weight is one fifth of the total weight. Substituting that value back gives $T = 47.1\,\mathrm{N}$, a value between the two weights $39.2\,\mathrm{N}$ and $58.9\,\mathrm{N}$, as it must be, since the string pulls $m_2$ upward harder than its own weight in order to raise it, and holds $m_1$ back with less than its weight.

The acceleration is constant and the system starts from rest, therefore the motion is uniformly accelerated, so the descent of $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ takes $1.24\,\mathrm{s}$ and the speed reached is $2.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$. As a check, $a\,t = 1.962\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 1.2366\,\mathrm{s} = 2.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lies on a frictionless horizontal table. An ideal string attached to it runs horizontally to an ideal pulley fixed at the edge of the table and then hangs vertically, carrying a body of mass $m_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. The system is released from rest. Find the acceleration and the tension of the string.

$$
\begin{cases}
T = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
m_2 g - T = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g,
\qquad
T = \frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g
$$
$$
a = \frac{2.0}{7.0} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.803\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 14.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1,3 4.0,3;-0.6,3 -0.6,0.2;1.8,3.5 4.3,3.5;4.6,3.2 4.6,1.4" curve="4.3+0.3*cos(t),3.2+0.3*sin(t),0,6.29" poligoni="0.6,3 1.8,3 1.8,4 0.6,4;4.1,0.4 5.1,0.4 5.1,1.4 4.1,1.4" vettori="1.8,3.75,2.7,3.75,T;4.6,1.4,4.6,2.3,T;4.6,0.4,4.6,-0.5,w₂" etichette="1.2,3.5,m₁;4.6,0.9,m₂" xmin=-1.3 xmax=5.9 ymin=-0.9 ymax=4.4 titolo="The weight of the hanging body is the only external force along the motion, and it has to accelerate the total mass $m_1 + m_2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The pulley is ideal, so the string has one tension $T$ and the direction of the pull changes from horizontal to vertical without any change of magnitude. The string is inextensible, so the block advances along the table by as much as the hanging body descends, and the two accelerations have the same magnitude $a$.

On the block the table is frictionless and the vertical forces balance, therefore the tension is the only horizontal force and the first equation is $T = m_1 a$. On the hanging body the forces are its weight and the tension, which gives the second equation. Adding them eliminates $T$ and gives $m_2 g = (m_1+m_2)a$, which is the equation of the system treated as a **single body**, since the weight of the hanging body is the only external force along the motion and the mass to be accelerated is the whole $7.0\,\mathrm{kg}$.

The results are $a = 2.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $T = 14.0\,\mathrm{N}$. The tension is smaller than the weight $19.6\,\mathrm{N}$ of the hanging body, in agreement with the fact that this body accelerates downward. As a check, the second equation gives $T = m_2(g-a) = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 7.007\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 14.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In an Atwood machine the heavier body has mass $m_1 = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. Released from rest, the system is observed to move with an acceleration of magnitude $a = 1.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the mass of the lighter body and the tension of the string.

$$
a = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m_2\,(g + a) = m_1\,(g - a)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m_2 = m_1\,\frac{g - a}{g + a}
$$
$$
m_2 = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \frac{(9.81 - 1.40)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{(9.81 + 1.40)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \frac{8.41}{11.21} = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
T = m_2\,(g + a) = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 11.21\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 67.3\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The formula of the **Atwood machine** is used here in the opposite direction, since the acceleration is the datum and one of the masses is the unknown. Multiplying both sides by $m_1 + m_2$ and collecting the terms in $m_2$ gives $m_2(g+a) = m_1(g-a)$, hence $m_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$.

The tension follows from the equation of the lighter body, which rises with acceleration $a$, so $T = m_2(g+a) = 67.3\,\mathrm{N}$. As a check, the equation of the heavier body gives $T = m_1(g-a) = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 8.41\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 67.3\,\mathrm{N}$, the same value, and the tension lies between the two weights $58.9\,\mathrm{N}$ and $78.5\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>
