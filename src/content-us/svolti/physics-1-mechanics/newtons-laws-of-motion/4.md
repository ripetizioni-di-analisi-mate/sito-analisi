## Exercise 1

Two blocks of masses $m_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hang one below the other from the ceiling: the upper string joins the ceiling to $m_1$ and the lower string joins $m_1$ to $m_2$. Find the tension of each string when the system is at rest, and when the whole system is raised with an upward acceleration $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
T_1 - T_2 - m_1 g = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
T_2 - m_2 g = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_2 = m_2\,(g + a),
\qquad
T_1 = (m_1 + m_2)\,(g + a)
$$
$$
a = 0:
\qquad
T_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 49.1\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
T_1 = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 78.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
a = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}:
\qquad
T_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 11.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 59.1\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
T_1 = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 11.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 94.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1.2,6 1.2,6;0,6 0,5;0,4 0,3" poligoni="-0.5,4 0.5,4 0.5,5 -0.5,5;-0.5,2 0.5,2 0.5,3 -0.5,3" vettori="1.9,3.4,1.9,4.4,a" etichette="0,4.5,m₁;0,2.5,m₂;0.5,5.5,T₁;0.5,3.5,T₂" xmin=-2.1 xmax=3 ymin=1.4 ymax=6.6 titolo="The upper string carries both blocks, the lower string carries $m_2$ alone, hence $T_1 > T_2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each block is taken separately. On $m_2$ act its weight and the pull $T_2$ of the lower string. On $m_1$ act its weight, the pull $T_1$ of the upper string and the pull of the lower string, which by the **third law** has the same magnitude $T_2$ and is directed downward. The strings are inextensible, therefore both blocks have the same acceleration $a$.

The equation of $m_2$ gives at once $T_2 = m_2(g+a)$. Adding the two equations eliminates $T_2$ and leaves $T_1 = (m_1+m_2)(g+a)$, which is the equation of the system treated as a **single body** of mass $8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$: the upper string carries both blocks, while the lower string carries only the block below it.

At rest each tension equals the weight it supports, $49.1\,\mathrm{N}$ and $78.5\,\mathrm{N}$. With the upward acceleration each tension grows in the ratio $\dfrac{g+a}{g} = 1.204$, hence the values $59.1\,\mathrm{N}$ and $94.5\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A load of mass $m = 40\,\mathrm{kg}$ is raised by a rope that passes over a fixed ideal pulley attached to the ceiling and is pulled downward at the other end. The load rises with the upward acceleration $a = 0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the tension of the rope and the magnitude of the force that the axle of the pulley must support, the two parts of the rope being vertical.

$$
T - m g = m\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = m\,(g + a)
$$
$$
T = 40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 + 0.50)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 10.31\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 412\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_{\text{axle}} = 2\,T = 825\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pulley is ideal, so it changes the direction of the rope without changing the tension, and the rope is ideal, so the same $T$ acts at both ends. The force applied by the person at the free end therefore has the magnitude $T$ as well.

On the load act its weight and the upward pull $T$, hence the second law along the upward vertical gives $T = m(g+a) = 412\,\mathrm{N}$. The value exceeds the weight $392\,\mathrm{N}$, since the load accelerates upward.

The two parts of the rope both pull the pulley downward with the force $T$ and they are parallel, therefore the axle has to balance $2T = 825\,\mathrm{N}$. The support of a pulley carries about twice the load, which is why the ceiling anchor is the part that has to be checked.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three blocks of masses $m_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_3 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hang one below the other from two ideal strings. A vertical force of magnitude $F = 108\,\mathrm{N}$, applied to the topmost block $m_1$, pulls the whole chain upward. Find the acceleration of the chain and the tension of each string.

$$
F - (m_1 + m_2 + m_3)\,g = (m_1 + m_2 + m_3)\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2 + m_3} - g
$$
$$
a = \frac{108\,\mathrm{N}}{9.0\,\mathrm{kg}} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 12.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.19\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T_{12} = (m_2 + m_3)\,(g + a),
\qquad
T_{23} = m_3\,(g + a)
$$
$$
T_{12} = 7.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 12.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 84\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
T_{23} = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 12.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 48\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three blocks are joined by inextensible strings, hence they share one acceleration $a$, and the whole chain can be treated as a **single body** of mass $9.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ subject to the applied force and to the total weight. That equation gives $a = 2.19\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed upward because $F$ exceeds the total weight $88.3\,\mathrm{N}$.

Each tension is then found by cutting the chain at the string in question and writing the equation of the part hanging below it, which is the only part that string has to carry. The upper string carries $m_2$ and $m_3$, hence $T_{12} = 7.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 12.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 84\,\mathrm{N}$; the lower string carries $m_3$ alone, hence $T_{23} = 48\,\mathrm{N}$.

The tension decreases from the top downward, and the same conclusion follows from the equation of $m_1$ as a check: $F - m_1 g - T_{12} = 108\,\mathrm{N} - 19.6\,\mathrm{N} - 84\,\mathrm{N} = 4.4\,\mathrm{N}$, which equals $m_1 a = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.19\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

</div>
