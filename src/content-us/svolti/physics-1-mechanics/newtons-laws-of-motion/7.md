## Exercise 1

A block of mass $m = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is released from rest on a frictionless plane inclined at $\theta = 25^\circ$ to the horizontal. Find the acceleration of the block, the normal force exerted by the plane and the speed of the block after it has slid $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ along the plane.

$$
\begin{cases}
m g\sin\theta = m\,a \\[4pt]
N - m g\cos\theta = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = g\sin\theta,
\qquad
N = m g\cos\theta
$$
$$
a = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.4226 = 4.15\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
N = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.9063 = 35.6\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2\,a\,d} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 4.146\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 4.07\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces on the block are its weight and the normal force of the plane, because the plane is frictionless. The axes are taken along the plane, positive downhill, and perpendicular to it, so the weight splits into $mg\sin\theta$ along the plane and $mg\cos\theta$ perpendicular to it, while the normal force lies entirely along the second axis.

The block stays on the plane, hence the acceleration perpendicular to it is zero and the second equation gives $N = mg\cos\theta = 35.6\,\mathrm{N}$, a value smaller than the weight $39.2\,\mathrm{N}$ because the plane supports only the component perpendicular to its surface. Along the plane the only force is $mg\sin\theta$, therefore $a = g\sin\theta = 4.15\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, which does not depend on the mass.

The acceleration is constant and the block starts from rest, so the relation between speed and distance of uniformly accelerated motion gives $v = 4.07\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is held at rest on a frictionless incline of $\theta = 35^\circ$. Find the tension of the string and the normal force in two cases: when the string is parallel to the incline, and when the string is horizontal.

$$
\text{string parallel to the incline:}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
T - m g\sin\theta = 0 \\[4pt]
N - m g\cos\theta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
T = 78.5\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.5736 = 45.0\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
N = 78.5\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.8192 = 64.3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\text{horizontal string, axes along the horizontal and the vertical:}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
N\sin\theta - T = 0 \\[4pt]
N\cos\theta - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
N = \frac{m g}{\cos\theta} = \frac{78.5\,\mathrm{N}}{0.8192} = 95.8\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
T = m g\tan\theta = 78.5\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.7002 = 55.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first case the axes are taken along the plane and perpendicular to it, because both the string and the normal force lie along those directions. The block is at rest, hence the **first law** gives zero net force along each axis, therefore the string carries the component of the weight along the plane, $T = 45.0\,\mathrm{N}$, and the plane carries the perpendicular component, $N = 64.3\,\mathrm{N}$.

In the second case the string is horizontal, so no axis contains all the forces and the horizontal and vertical axes are the convenient choice. The normal force is perpendicular to the incline, therefore it makes the angle $\theta$ with the vertical and its components are $N\sin\theta$ horizontal and $N\cos\theta$ vertical. The vertical equation gives $N = \dfrac{mg}{\cos\theta} = 95.8\,\mathrm{N}$ and the horizontal equation then gives $T = mg\tan\theta = 55.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

Both the tension and the normal force are **larger** with the horizontal string. The horizontal string presses the block against the plane, since only part of its pull is spent along the plane, so the plane has to push back harder.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a frictionless incline of $\theta = 30^\circ$. An ideal string attached to it runs up the plane, passes over an ideal pulley fixed at the top of the incline and hangs vertically, carrying a body of mass $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. The system is released from rest. Determine the sense of the motion and find the acceleration, the tension of the string and the normal force on the block.

$$
m_2\,g = 29.4\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
m_1\,g\sin\theta = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.500 = 19.6\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
m_2 g - T = m_2\,a \\[4pt]
T - m_1 g\sin\theta = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
N - m_1 g\cos\theta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = \frac{m_2 - m_1\sin\theta}{m_1 + m_2}\,g,
\qquad
T = m_2\,(g - a),
\qquad
N = m_1 g\cos\theta
$$
$$
a = \frac{29.43\,\mathrm{N} - 19.62\,\mathrm{N}}{7.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{9.81\,\mathrm{N}}{7.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 1.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 1.401)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 25.2\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
N = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.866 = 34.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,3.4641;2.9598,2.4017 3.2598,1.8821 2.7402,1.5821 2.4402,2.1017;6.127,1.2 6.927,1.2 6.927,2 6.127,2" linee="0,0 6,0 6,3.4641 0,0;3.1,2.13 6.227,3.942;6.527,3.942 6.527,2" curve="6.227+0.3*cos(t),3.942+0.3*sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="6.527,2,6.527,2.9,T;6.527,1.2,6.527,0.4,w₂" etichette="2.25,1.55,m₁;7.2,1.6,m₂;0.95,0.17,30°" xmin=-0.4 xmax=7.9 ymin=-0.6 ymax=4.7 titolo="The hanging body descends because its weight $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$ exceeds the component $m_1 g\sin\theta = 19.6\,\mathrm{N}$ that pulls the block down the plane"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The sense of the motion is decided before the equations are written, by comparing the two forces that drive the system along the string. The weight of the hanging body, $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$, tends to make the block climb the plane; the component of the block's weight along the plane, $19.6\,\mathrm{N}$, tends to make it slide down. The first exceeds the second, therefore **the hanging body descends** and the block climbs.

With that sense taken as positive for each body, the string gives one tension $T$ and one acceleration $a$. Adding the first two equations eliminates $T$ and gives $a = 1.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, an acceleration far smaller than $g$ because the unbalanced force is only $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$ while the moving mass is $7.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. The first equation then gives $T = 25.2\,\mathrm{N}$, which is smaller than the weight of the hanging body, as it must be for a body accelerating downward.

The third equation concerns the axis perpendicular to the plane, where the block has no acceleration, and the string is parallel to the plane so it does not appear there. Hence $N = m_1 g\cos\theta = 34.0\,\mathrm{N}$. As a check, the second equation gives $T = 19.62\,\mathrm{N} + 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 1.401\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 25.2\,\mathrm{N}$, the same value.

</div>
