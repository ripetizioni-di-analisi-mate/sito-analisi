## Exercise 1

A crate of mass $m = 18\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a frictionless horizontal floor and is pushed by a constant horizontal force of magnitude $F = 45\,\mathrm{N}$. Find the acceleration of the crate, its speed after $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and the distance it covers in that time.

$$
\begin{cases}
F = m\,a \\[4pt]
N - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = \frac{F}{m},
\qquad
v = a\,t,
\qquad
s = \frac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2
$$
$$
a = \frac{45\,\mathrm{N}}{18\,\mathrm{kg}} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{s} = 12.5\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
s = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (5.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 31\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Three forces act on the crate: the weight, the normal force of the floor and the push. The floor is frictionless, therefore the push is the only horizontal force. The $x$ axis is taken horizontal in the sense of the push and the $y$ axis vertical and upward.

The crate stays on the floor, hence $a_y = 0$ and the equation of the $y$ axis gives $N = mg$. Along $x$ the **second law** gives $F = ma$, hence $a = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The force is constant, therefore the acceleration is constant and the motion is uniformly accelerated from rest, so the laws of that motion give $v = 12.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $s = 31\,\mathrm{m}$. As a check, the average speed over the $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ is $\dfrac{0 + 12.5}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $6.25\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{s} = 31.25\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car of mass $m = 1400\,\mathrm{kg}$ travels on a horizontal road at $v_0 = 28\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with constant deceleration, stopping in the distance $d = 64\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the magnitude of the braking force and the duration of the braking.

$$
v^2 = v_0^2 - 2\,a\,d
\quad\text{with}\quad
v = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{v_0^2}{2\,d}
$$
$$
F = m\,a = \frac{m\,v_0^2}{2\,d},
\qquad
t = \frac{v_0}{a}
$$
$$
a = \frac{(28\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 64\,\mathrm{m}} = 6.125\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
F = 1400\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 6.125\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 8.6 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
t = \frac{28\,\mathrm{m/s}}{6.125\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 4.6\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The data of the problem are kinematic and the quantity asked for is a force, therefore the acceleration is obtained first from the kinematics and the force follows from the second law. The deceleration is constant, hence the relation between speed and distance of uniformly accelerated motion applies, and imposing that the final speed be zero gives $a = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2d} = 6.125\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Along the vertical the weight and the normal force balance, so the braking force is the only horizontal force and it is opposite to the velocity. The **second law** then gives $F = ma = 8.6 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$. The duration of the braking is the time in which the speed falls from $28\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to zero with that deceleration, hence $t = 4.6\,\mathrm{s}$. As a check, the average speed is $14\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $14\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 4.571\,\mathrm{s} = 64\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sled of mass $m = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lies at rest on frictionless ice. A constant horizontal net force of magnitude $F = 15\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on it for $t_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and is then removed. Find the speed of the sled at the instant the force is removed and the distance covered in the first $t_2 = 10.0\,\mathrm{s}$ of the motion.

$$
a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{15\,\mathrm{N}}{6.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v = a\,t_1 = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
s_1 = \frac{1}{2}\,a\,t_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (4.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 20\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
s_2 = v\,(t_2 - t_1) = 10\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{s} = 60\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
s = s_1 + s_2 = 80\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion has two phases and each of them is treated separately, since the net force is constant inside each phase and changes at the instant $t_1$. In the first phase the net force is $15\,\mathrm{N}$, hence the **second law** gives the constant acceleration $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the sled starts from rest, so after $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ it has the speed $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and has covered $20\,\mathrm{m}$.

In the second phase the net force is zero, because the ice is frictionless and the applied force has been removed. The **first law** then applies, therefore the sled keeps the velocity it had, and in the remaining $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$ it covers $60\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant speed. The total distance is $80\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
