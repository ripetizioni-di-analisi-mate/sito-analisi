In a hydraulic lift the large piston has $50$ times the area of the small piston. When the small piston is pushed down by $30\,\mathrm{cm}$, by how much does the large piston rise?

- $0.60\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $4.2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1500\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $30\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The liquid is incompressible, therefore the volume leaving the small cylinder enters the large one, $A_1 s_1 = A_2 s_2$, and
$$
s_2 = \frac{A_1}{A_2}\,s_1 = \frac{30\,\mathrm{cm}}{50} = 0.60\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The force is multiplied by $50$ and the displacement is divided by $50$. The value $4.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ divides by $\sqrt{50}$, which would be the ratio of the diameters, and not by the ratio of the areas.
