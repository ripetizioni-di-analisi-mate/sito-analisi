A steel ball hanging from a string is completely submerged in water and lowered from the depth $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ to the depth $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Treating water as incompressible, how does the buoyant force on the ball change?

- It decreases, since more water lies above the ball.
- It increases, since the pressure on the ball is larger.
- It becomes five times larger.
- It does not change.

## Explanation

The buoyant force is $\rho_f\,g\,V$, which contains neither the depth nor the pressure. Going deeper raises the pressure on the top and on the bottom of the ball by the same amount, therefore the difference between the two, which produces the buoyant force, stays the same.
