Let the $y$ axis point upward in a liquid of density $\rho$ at rest in a uniform gravitational field. Which relation does the pressure $p$ satisfy?

- $\dfrac{dp}{dy} = 0$
- $\dfrac{dp}{dy} = -\rho g y$
- $\dfrac{dp}{dy} = -\rho g$
- $\dfrac{dp}{dy} = \rho g$

## Explanation

The equilibrium of a vertical column of liquid of cross-section $A$ and height $\Delta y$ gives $p(y)A - p(y + \Delta y)A = \rho g A\,\Delta y$, hence $\dfrac{dp}{dy} = -\rho g$. The sign is negative because the pressure decreases going up, with the $y$ axis pointing upward. The derivative is constant, since $\rho$ and $g$ are constant, and $p$ itself is a linear function of $y$.
