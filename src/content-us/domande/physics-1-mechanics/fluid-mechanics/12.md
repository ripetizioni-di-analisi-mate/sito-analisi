Three open vessels, one wide, one narrow and one that widens towards the top, contain the same liquid filled to the same height. How do the pressures on their bottoms compare?

- The largest is on the bottom of the vessel that widens towards the top.
- They are equal.
- The largest is on the bottom of the vessel that holds the most liquid.
- The largest is on the bottom of the narrow vessel.

## Explanation

The pressure in a liquid at rest is $p = p_{\mathrm{atm}} + \rho g h$, which depends on the depth alone. The three bottoms lie at the same depth below free surfaces exposed to the same atmosphere, therefore the pressures are equal, although the vessels hold different quantities of liquid.
