Water flows steadily through a horizontal pipe. At section $1$ the speed is $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and at section $2$ it is $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the pressure difference $p_1 - p_2$?

- $8.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.2 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.6 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $-1.6 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The pipe is horizontal, hence $y_1 = y_2$ and **Bernoulli's equation** gives
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.00 \times 10^{3} \cdot (36 - 4.0)\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.6 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The difference is positive, since the pressure is lower where the speed is larger. The value $3.2 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ loses the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $8.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ squares the difference of the speeds instead of taking the difference of the squares.
