A hydraulic press with two pistons at the same height must turn an input force of $40\,\mathrm{N}$ into an output force of $2.56 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$. The large piston has diameter $16\,\mathrm{cm}$. What diameter must the small piston have?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $128\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The pressure is the same on the two pistons, hence $\dfrac{A_2}{A_1} = \dfrac{F_2}{F_1} = 64$. The areas are proportional to the squares of the diameters, therefore the ratio of the diameters is $\sqrt{64} = 8$ and
$$
d_1 = \frac{d_2}{8} = \frac{16\,\mathrm{cm}}{8} = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The value $0.25\,\mathrm{cm}$ divides the diameter by the ratio of the forces without taking the square root, $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the radius of the small piston, and $128\,\mathrm{cm}$ multiplies by $8$ instead of dividing.
