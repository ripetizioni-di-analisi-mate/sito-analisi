Oil of density $8.5 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ flows through a pipe with a volume flow rate of $0.50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the mass flow rate?

- $0.43\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $5.9 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $4.3 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{kg/s}$

## Explanation

Since $1\,\mathrm{L} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, the volume flow rate is $Q = 5.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, and the **mass flow rate** is
$$
\dot{m} = \rho Q = 8.5 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 5.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.43\,\mathrm{kg/s} .
$$
The value $4.3 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ keeps the flow rate in liters, $0.50\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ uses the density of water, and $5.9 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ divides by the density.
