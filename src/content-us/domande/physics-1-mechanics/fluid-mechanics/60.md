When is the flow of a fluid called steady?

- When the velocity has the same value at every point of the fluid.
- When the streamlines are straight lines.
- When the velocity at each fixed point of space does not change with time.
- When each element of fluid moves with constant velocity.

## Explanation

A flow is **steady** when the velocity at each fixed point does not change with time. The velocity may differ from point to point, therefore an element of fluid that moves from a wide section of a pipe into a narrow one speeds up although the flow is steady. Steady flow allows curved streamlines as well.
