A homogeneous body of density $2.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is released from rest while completely submerged in water. Neglecting the drag of the water, what is its initial acceleration?

- $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, downward
- $15\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, downward
- $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, upward
- $9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, downward

## Explanation

The weight $\rho_b g V$ acts downward and the buoyant force $\rho_f g V$ upward, hence Newton's second law gives $(\rho_b - \rho_f)\,g\,V = \rho_b V a$ for the downward acceleration, and
$$
a = \left(1 - \frac{\rho_f}{\rho_b}\right) g = \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 4.9\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The body is denser than water, therefore it sinks. The value $9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ ignores the buoyant force, and $15\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ adds it to the weight.
