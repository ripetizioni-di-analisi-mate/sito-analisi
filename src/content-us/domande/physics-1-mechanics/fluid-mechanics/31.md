A piston closes the top of a container completely filled with an incompressible liquid at rest. Pushing the piston raises the pressure just below it by $2.0 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. By how much does the pressure on the bottom of the container increase?

- By $2.0 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ plus $\rho g h$, where $h$ is the depth of the bottom.
- By less than $2.0 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, since part of the increase is taken up by the walls.
- By $2.0 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
- By zero, since the liquid is incompressible.

## Explanation

By **Pascal's principle** a change of pressure applied to an enclosed incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished to every point, therefore the pressure on the bottom increases by $2.0 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The term $\rho g h$ was already present before the piston was pushed, so it belongs to the pressure on the bottom and not to its change.
