In a hydraulic lift the large piston, which carries the load, stands higher than the small piston. Compared with a lift whose pistons are at the same height, the force needed on the small piston to hold the same load is

- larger, since the pressure at the small piston exceeds the pressure at the large one by $\rho g\,\Delta h$.
- smaller, since the column of fluid between the two levels pushes the load upward.
- the same, since by Pascal's principle the pressure is the same at the two pistons.
- larger by the weight of all the fluid contained in the lift.

## Explanation

The pressure under the large piston is fixed by the load, $p_2 = \dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$. The small piston lies $\Delta h$ lower in the same fluid, therefore $p_1 = p_2 + \rho g\,\Delta h$ and $F_1 = A_1 p_1$ exceeds $A_1 p_2$ by $\rho g\,\Delta h\,A_1$. Pascal's principle concerns the transmission of a *change* of pressure, while the pressure itself still varies with depth.
