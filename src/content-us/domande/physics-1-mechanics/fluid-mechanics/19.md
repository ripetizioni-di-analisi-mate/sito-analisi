On the Moon the gravitational acceleration is one sixth of its value on the Earth. How does the gauge pressure at the depth of $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ in a tank of water on the Moon compare with the gauge pressure at the same depth on the Earth?

- It is six times as large.
- It is one sixth as large.
- It is one thirty-sixth as large.
- It is the same, since the depth and the liquid are the same.

## Explanation

The gauge pressure at depth $h$ is $\rho g h$, which is proportional to $g$. With the same liquid and the same depth, dividing $g$ by $6$ divides the gauge pressure by $6$. The pressure comes from the weight of the liquid above the point, which is why it depends on $g$ and not only on the depth.
