Pascal's principle for an incompressible fluid at rest follows from the law of pressure with depth. Which property of that law makes it follow?

- The pressure has the same value at every point of the fluid.
- The fluid exerts no tangential force on the walls.
- The pressure at a point does not depend on the density of the fluid.
- The difference of pressure between two points depends only on their difference in depth.

## Explanation

The law of pressure with depth fixes $p_2 - p_1 = \rho g (h_2 - h_1)$, a difference that depends on the geometry alone. Raising the pressure at one point by $\Delta p$ therefore raises it by the same $\Delta p$ at every other point, which is **Pascal's principle**. The pressure itself changes with depth and depends on the density, so the two statements about the value of the pressure are false. The absence of tangential forces is a property of a fluid at rest, and not of the law of pressure with depth, and it does not fix how a change of pressure spreads through the fluid.
