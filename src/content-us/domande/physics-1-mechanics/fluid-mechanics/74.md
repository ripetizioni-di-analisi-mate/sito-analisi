Water flows steadily in a pipe. At section $1$ the gauge pressure is $2.00 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and the speed is $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, while section $2$ lies $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ higher and the speed there is $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Treating water as an ideal fluid, what is the gauge pressure at section $2$?

- $1.80 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.90 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.10 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.50 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The atmospheric pressure appears on both sides of **Bernoulli's equation**, hence the equation holds for gauge pressures as well, and
$$
p_2 = p_1 - \rho g\,(y_2 - y_1) - \frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = \left(2.00 \times 10^{5} - 1.96 \times 10^{4} - 3.00 \times 10^{4}\right)\mathrm{Pa} = 1.50 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
Both the rise and the increase of speed lower the pressure. The value $1.80 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ omits the kinetic term, while the other two values invert the sign of one of the two terms.
