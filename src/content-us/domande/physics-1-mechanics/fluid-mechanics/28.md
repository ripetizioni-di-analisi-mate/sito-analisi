An open-tube mercury manometer is connected to a vessel, and the mercury in the open arm stands $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ *below* its level in the arm connected to the vessel. What is the gauge pressure in the vessel?

- $-5.34 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $5.34 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $9.60 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $-5.34 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Here the lower level is the one in the open arm, where the pressure is $p_{\mathrm{atm}}$. Along the other arm that level lies $h$ below the surface in contact with the vessel, therefore $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = p + \rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\,g\,h$ and
$$
p_{\mathrm{gauge}} = -\rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\,g\,h = -1.36 \times 10^{4} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{Pa} = -5.34 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The pressure in the vessel is below the atmospheric pressure. The value $9.60 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is the absolute pressure, and the positive value inverts the sign.
