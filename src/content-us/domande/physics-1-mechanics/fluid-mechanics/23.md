Two mercury barometers at the same place have tubes of inner diameters $0.50\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. How does the height of the mercury column in the wider tube compare with that in the narrower tube?

- It is one quarter as large.
- It is half as large.
- It is twice as large.
- It is the same.

## Explanation

In a barometer the pressure at the top of the column is zero and the pressure at the level of the basin is $p_{\mathrm{atm}}$, therefore $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = \rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\,g\,h$. The height $h = \dfrac{p_{\mathrm{atm}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\,g}$ depends on the atmospheric pressure, on the density and on $g$, and not on the cross-section of the tube, since the pressure in a liquid depends on the depth alone.
