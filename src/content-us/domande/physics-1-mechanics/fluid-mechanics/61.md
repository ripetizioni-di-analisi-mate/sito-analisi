Why can two streamlines of a steady flow never cross?

- At the crossing point the velocity would have two different values.
- The fluid has no viscosity.
- The fluid is incompressible.
- The pressure at the crossing point would be zero.

## Explanation

In a steady flow the velocity at each point is tangent to the streamline through that point. If two streamlines crossed, the crossing point would have two tangents, hence two velocities, which is impossible for a single value of the velocity at a point. The argument uses only the definition of streamline, and neither incompressibility nor viscosity enters it.
