Two sheets of paper hang parallel and close to each other, and air is blown between them. What happens to the sheets?

- They move towards each other, since the pressure of the moving air between them is higher than the pressure outside.
- They move towards each other, since the pressure of the moving air between them is lower than the pressure of the still air outside.
- They do not move, since the pressure is the atmospheric pressure on both sides.
- They move apart, since the blown air pushes them outward.

## Explanation

At practically the same height, **Bernoulli's equation** relates a larger speed to a smaller pressure. The air between the sheets moves fast while the air outside is still, therefore the pressure between the sheets is lower, and the higher pressure outside pushes them together. A higher pressure between the sheets would push them apart.
