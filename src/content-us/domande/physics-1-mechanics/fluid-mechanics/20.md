A U-tube open at both ends contains water, and oil of smaller density is poured into the left arm, where it rests on the water without mixing. At equilibrium, how do the two free surfaces compare?

- The free surface of the water stands higher than that of the oil.
- The two free surfaces are at the same height, since both are at atmospheric pressure.
- The free surface of the oil stands higher than that of the water.
- The surface in the narrower arm stands higher, whichever liquid it belongs to.

## Explanation

The pressures are equal at the level of the oil-water interface, since there the two arms are joined through water. Above that level the oil column of height $h_1$ and the water column of height $h_2$ produce the same pressure, $\rho_{\mathrm{oil}}\,g\,h_1 = \rho_{\mathrm{w}}\,g\,h_2$, therefore $h_1 > h_2$ because $\rho_{\mathrm{oil}} < \rho_{\mathrm{w}}$. Equal pressure at equal height holds only within one connected body of the same fluid, and the width of the arms does not enter.
