A real liquid flows steadily through a long horizontal pipe of constant cross-section. How does the pressure change along the direction of flow?

- It increases.
- It decreases along the first part of the pipe and then stays constant.
- It decreases.
- It stays constant, since the speed and the height do not change.

## Explanation

A real liquid is viscous, and the tangential forces between its layers dissipate mechanical energy. The speed and the height are the same along the pipe, therefore the energy lost appears as a steady drop of the pressure, and the sum $p + \rho g y + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$ decreases along the flow. A constant pressure is the prediction of Bernoulli's equation, which holds for an ideal fluid only.
