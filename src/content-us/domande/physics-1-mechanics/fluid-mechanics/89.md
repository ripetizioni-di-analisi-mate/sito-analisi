A viscous liquid flows steadily in laminar flow along a straight cylindrical pipe. How does its speed vary across a cross-section?

- It is the same at every point of the section.
- It is zero on the axis and grows linearly toward the wall.
- It is zero at the wall and largest on the axis.
- It is largest at the wall and zero on the axis.

## Explanation

A viscous fluid adheres to the walls of the pipe, therefore its speed is zero at the wall. The tangential forces between adjacent layers slow down the layers near the wall more than those far from it, so the speed is largest on the axis. A uniform speed over the section is the assumption made for an ideal fluid.
