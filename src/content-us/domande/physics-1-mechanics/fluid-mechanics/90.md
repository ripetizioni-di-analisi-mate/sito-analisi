A flat plate of area $0.20\,\mathrm{m^2}$ slides at $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a layer of oil of thickness $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and viscosity $8.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$, which lies on a fixed surface. The speed of the oil varies linearly across the layer. What force keeps the plate moving at constant speed?

- $1.6 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The speed varies linearly from $0$ to $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ across $2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$, hence $\dfrac{dv}{dy} = 250\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, and the definition of the **coefficient of viscosity** gives
$$
F = \eta\,A\,\frac{dv}{dy} = 8.0 \times 10^{-2} \cdot 0.20 \cdot 250\,\mathrm{N} = 4.0\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $20\,\mathrm{N}$ omits the area, $4.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$ keeps the thickness in millimeters, and $1.6 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$ multiplies by the thickness instead of dividing.
