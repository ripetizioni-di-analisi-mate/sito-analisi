A tire gauge reads $2.20\,\mathrm{bar}$. Taking $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 1.013 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, what is the absolute pressure of the air in the tire?

- $3.21 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.19 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.21 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.20 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

A tire gauge reports the **gauge pressure**, that is, the excess over the atmosphere. Since $1\,\mathrm{bar} = 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, the gauge pressure is $2.20 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, hence the absolute pressure is
$$
p = p_{\mathrm{gauge}} + p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 2.20 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} + 1.013 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 3.21 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $2.20 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ treats the reading as an absolute pressure, and $1.19 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ subtracts the atmosphere instead of adding it.
