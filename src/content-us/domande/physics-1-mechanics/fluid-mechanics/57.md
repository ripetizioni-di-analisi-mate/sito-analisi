A steel ship floats although steel is about eight times denser than water. Which statement explains it?

- The buoyant force on a steel body exceeds its weight at any depth.
- The average density of the hull together with the air it encloses is smaller than the density of water.
- Steel becomes less dense than water once it is shaped into a hull.
- The pressure of the water is larger under a wide flat bottom than under a solid block of the same mass.

## Explanation

The buoyant force is fixed by the submerged volume, $\rho_f\,g\,V_{\mathrm{sub}}$. A hollow hull encloses a large volume of air, so it can displace a mass of water equal to its own mass before it is fully submerged. In terms of the condition for floating, the body formed by the hull and the air it encloses has an average density smaller than that of water. The density of the steel itself is unchanged.
