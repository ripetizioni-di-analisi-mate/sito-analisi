A hydrometer is a weighted tube of uniform cross-section. It floats upright in fresh water with $12.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ of its length submerged. What length is submerged when it floats in a liquid of density $1.20 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$?

- $8.33\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $14.4\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $12.0\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The buoyant force equals the weight of the hydrometer in both liquids, hence $\rho_1 g A \ell_1 = \rho_2 g A \ell_2$ and the submerged length is inversely proportional to the density,
$$
\ell_2 = \frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\,\ell_1 = \frac{1.00}{1.20} \cdot 12.0\,\mathrm{cm} = 10.0\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The value $14.4\,\mathrm{cm}$ multiplies by the ratio of the densities instead of dividing, and $8.33\,\mathrm{cm}$ divides by its square.
