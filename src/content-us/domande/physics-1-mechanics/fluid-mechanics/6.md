A small flat surface is placed at a point inside a liquid at rest, first horizontal and then vertical. How do the pressures on the surface in the two orientations compare?

- The pressure on the vertical surface is zero, since no liquid lies above it.
- The pressure on the horizontal surface is larger, since the weight of the liquid acts vertically.
- The pressure on the vertical surface is larger, since the liquid pushes sideways on it.
- They are equal.

## Explanation

A fluid at rest sustains no shear stress, therefore the pressure at a point has the same value for every orientation of the surface used to measure it. The weight of the liquid fixes how the pressure varies from point to point, through the depth, and not how it depends on the orientation at a given point.
