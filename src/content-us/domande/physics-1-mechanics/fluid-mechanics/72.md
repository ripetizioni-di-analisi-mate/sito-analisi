Water flows steadily upward through a vertical pipe of constant cross-section. Treating water as an ideal fluid, by how much does the pressure at a section exceed the pressure at a section $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ higher?

- $3.9 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $-3.9 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $0\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The cross-section is constant, hence the **continuity equation** gives the same speed at the two sections, and the kinetic terms cancel in Bernoulli's equation. What remains is
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \rho g\,(y_2 - y_1) = 1.00 \times 10^{3} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{Pa} = 3.9 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa} ,
$$
the same difference as in water at rest. The value $0\,\mathrm{Pa}$ cancels the height terms along with the speeds, and the negative value inverts the sign, as if the pressure grew going up.
