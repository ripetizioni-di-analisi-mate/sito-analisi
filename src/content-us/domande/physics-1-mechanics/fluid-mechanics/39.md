A body is completely submerged in a liquid at rest. The buoyant force acting on it equals

- the weight of the body minus the weight of the liquid it displaces.
- the weight of the column of liquid lying above the body.
- the weight of the body.
- the weight of the liquid that would occupy the volume of the body.

## Explanation

By **Archimedes' principle** the buoyant force has magnitude $F_B = \rho_f\,g\,V_{\mathrm{sub}}$, the weight of the displaced fluid. For a completely submerged body $V_{\mathrm{sub}}$ is the volume of the body. The buoyant force equals the weight of the body only for a body floating in equilibrium, and the difference between the two weights is the apparent weight.
