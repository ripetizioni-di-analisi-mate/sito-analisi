A vertical gate of fixed width holds water whose free surface is level with its top edge. If the height of the gate and the depth of the water are both doubled, by what factor does the resultant force of the water on the gate change?

- It is multiplied by $4$.
- It is multiplied by $8$.
- It is multiplied by $2$.
- It does not change.

## Explanation

The resultant force on a vertical gate of width $w$ holding water of depth $H$ is $F = \dfrac{1}{2}\rho g H^2 w$, which is proportional to $H^2$. Doubling $H$ therefore multiplies the force by $4$, since the area of the gate doubles and the average gauge pressure $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho g H$ doubles as well. The factor $8$ is the one by which the torque about the bottom edge changes.
