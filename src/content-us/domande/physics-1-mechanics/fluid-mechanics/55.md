An ice cube floats in a glass of fresh water. Evaporation is negligible. When the ice has completely melted, the level of the water in the glass

- is lower than before.
- depends on the shape of the ice cube.
- is higher than before.
- is the same as before.

## Explanation

While the ice floats, the volume it displaces is $V_{\mathrm{sub}} = \dfrac{m}{\rho_{\mathrm{w}}}$, by the condition $\rho_{\mathrm{w}}\,g\,V_{\mathrm{sub}} = m g$. After melting, the same mass $m$ becomes liquid water of volume $\dfrac{m}{\rho_{\mathrm{w}}}$, which fills exactly the volume that the ice displaced. Therefore the level does not change, whatever the shape of the cube.
