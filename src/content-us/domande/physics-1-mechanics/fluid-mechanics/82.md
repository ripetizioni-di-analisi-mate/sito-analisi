An ideal fluid flows steadily through a horizontal pipe that narrows to a throat and then widens again. How does the pressure in the throat compare with the pressure in the wide section?

- It is equal, since the pipe is horizontal.
- It is lower or higher depending on the direction of the flow.
- It is lower.
- It is higher.

## Explanation

In the throat the area is smaller, hence by the **continuity equation** the speed is larger. In a horizontal pipe Bernoulli's equation reduces to $p + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = \text{constant}$, therefore the larger speed goes with the lower pressure. The relation involves $v^2$ and the areas only, so reversing the flow leaves the conclusion unchanged.
