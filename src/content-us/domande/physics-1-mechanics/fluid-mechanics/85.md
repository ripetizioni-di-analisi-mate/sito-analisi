A viscous liquid flows slowly in laminar flow through a cylindrical pipe under a fixed pressure difference between its ends. The pipe is replaced by one of the same length and half the radius. What fraction of the original flow rate remains?

- $12.5\%$
- $50\%$
- $25\%$
- $6.25\%$

## Explanation

By **Poiseuille's law** $Q = \dfrac{\pi R^4}{8\eta L}\,(p_1 - p_2)$, the flow rate is proportional to the fourth power of the radius. Halving $R$ multiplies $Q$ by $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{4} = \dfrac{1}{16} = 0.0625$, that is $6.25\%$. The value $25\%$ is the ratio of the areas, which would hold for an ideal fluid at the same speed.
