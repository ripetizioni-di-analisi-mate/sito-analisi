In an ideal hydraulic press the force on the large piston is $100$ times the force on the small piston. How does the work done on the fluid at the small piston compare with the work done by the fluid on the large piston?

- The work on the large piston is $100$ times larger.
- They are equal.
- The work on the large piston is $10\,000$ times larger.
- The work on the large piston is $100$ times smaller.

## Explanation

The incompressibility of the liquid gives $A_1 s_1 = A_2 s_2$, while Pascal's principle gives $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1} = \dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$. Multiplying the two relations gives $F_1 s_1 = F_2 s_2$, therefore the force is multiplied by $100$ while the displacement is divided by $100$, and the work is the same. A machine of this kind multiplies forces and does not create energy.
