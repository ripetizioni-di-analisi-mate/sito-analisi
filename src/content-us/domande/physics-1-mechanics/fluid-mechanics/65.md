Water flows at $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a garden hose of inner diameter $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and leaves through a nozzle of diameter $0.50\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the speed of the water in the nozzle?

- $0.094\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.38\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By the **continuity equation** the speed is inversely proportional to the area, hence to the square of the diameter, and
$$
v_2 = \left(\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^{2} v_1 = \left(\frac{2.0}{0.50}\right)^{2} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s} = 16 \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s} = 24\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the ratio of the diameters without squaring it, and the two smallest values invert the ratio.
