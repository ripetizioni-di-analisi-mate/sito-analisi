A ship sails from a fresh-water river into the sea, where the water is denser. Its load does not change. What happens to the buoyant force on the ship and to its position in the water?

- The buoyant force increases, and the ship rises slightly.
- The buoyant force stays the same, and the ship sinks slightly.
- The buoyant force stays the same, and the ship rises slightly.
- The buoyant force increases, and the submerged volume stays the same.

## Explanation

A floating ship is in equilibrium, so the buoyant force equals its weight in both waters and does not change. From $\rho_f\,g\,V_{\mathrm{sub}} = W$, a larger $\rho_f$ requires a smaller $V_{\mathrm{sub}}$, therefore the ship floats slightly higher in the sea.
