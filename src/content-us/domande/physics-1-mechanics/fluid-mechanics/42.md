A body of density $5.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ hangs from a spring scale and is completely submerged in water, and the scale reads $16\,\mathrm{N}$. What does the scale read when the body hangs in air, whose buoyancy is negligible?

- $80\,\mathrm{N}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $13\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.2\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The reading in water is the **apparent weight** $W_{\mathrm{app}} = \left(1 - \dfrac{\rho_f}{\rho_b}\right) W = \dfrac{4}{5}W$, therefore
$$
W = \frac{W_{\mathrm{app}}}{1 - \dfrac{\rho_f}{\rho_b}} = \frac{16\,\mathrm{N}}{0.80} = 20\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The weight in air exceeds the reading in water by the buoyant force, $4.0\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $13\,\mathrm{N}$ multiplies by $0.80$ instead of dividing, $80\,\mathrm{N}$ multiplies by $\dfrac{\rho_b}{\rho_f}$, and $3.2\,\mathrm{N}$ multiplies by $\dfrac{\rho_f}{\rho_b}$.
