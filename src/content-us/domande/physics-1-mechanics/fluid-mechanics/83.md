Water flows steadily through a horizontal pipe whose cross-sectional area is halved at a throat. The speed in the wide section is $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the pressure in the wide section minus the pressure in the throat?

- $6.8 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.1 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4.5 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.4 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Halving the area doubles the speed, so $v_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by the continuity equation. **Bernoulli's equation** for a horizontal pipe then gives
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.00 \times 10^{3} \cdot (9.0 - 2.25)\,\mathrm{Pa} = 3.4 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $4.5 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ ignores the speed in the wide section, and $1.1 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ squares the difference of the speeds.
