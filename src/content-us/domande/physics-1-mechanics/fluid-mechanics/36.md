A hydraulic jack has pistons at the same height with area ratio $\dfrac{A_2}{A_1} = 100$, and a force of $200\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied to the small piston. The masses of the pistons are negligible. What is the largest mass that the large piston can hold?

- $0.204\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $20.4\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.00 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.04 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

By Pascal's principle the force on the large piston is $F_2 = 100 \cdot 200\,\mathrm{N} = 2.00 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}$, and this force balances the weight $Mg$ of the load, therefore
$$
M = \frac{F_2}{g} = \frac{2.00 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 2.04 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $2.00 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg}$ takes the force in newtons as a mass, and $20.4\,\mathrm{kg}$ leaves out the area ratio.
