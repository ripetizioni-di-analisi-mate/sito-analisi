In fluid mechanics, which property distinguishes a fluid from a solid?

- Its density does not depend on the pressure.
- It always fills the whole volume of its container.
- Its layers exert no tangential forces on each other while it flows.
- It cannot sustain a shear stress while it is at rest.

## Explanation

A **fluid** is a substance that cannot sustain a shear stress at rest, therefore it takes the shape of its container. A density independent of the pressure defines an *incompressible* fluid, and the absence of tangential forces between layers defines an *ideal* fluid. Both are additional hypotheses of a model, satisfied by some fluids only. A liquid takes the shape of the lower part of its container and does not fill the whole volume, so filling the container is a property of gases alone.
