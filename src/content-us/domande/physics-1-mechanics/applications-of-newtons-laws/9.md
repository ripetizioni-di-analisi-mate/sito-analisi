A block of weight $50\,\mathrm{N}$ rests on a horizontal floor and a horizontal force $F$ is applied to it, increasing slowly from zero. The graph shows the friction force $f$ on the block as a function of $F$, both in newtons. What is the coefficient of kinetic friction?

::grafico{linee="0,0 20,20;20,20 20,15;20,15 32,15" segmenti="0,20,20,20;0,15,20,15;20,0,20,15" nomi="F,f" xmin=-3 xmax=34 ymin=-3 ymax=25 legenda=0}

- $0.40$
- $0.75$
- $3.3$
- $0.30$

## Explanation

The block slides once $F$ reaches $20\,\mathrm{N}$, after which the friction force is kinetic and constant, $f_k = 15\,\mathrm{N}$. Since $N = 50\,\mathrm{N}$,
$$
\mu_k = \dfrac{f_k}{N} = \dfrac{15\,\mathrm{N}}{50\,\mathrm{N}} = 0.30 .
$$
The peak of the graph gives $\mu_s = 0.40$ instead.
