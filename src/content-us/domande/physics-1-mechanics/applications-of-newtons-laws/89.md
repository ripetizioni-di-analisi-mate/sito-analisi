In the ground frame, a student draws for a car on a flat curve both the real forces and a force $m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ pointing outwards, and sets the sum equal to $m\vec{a}$. What does this analysis predict?

- That the largest speed is $\sqrt{\mu_s g r}$
- That the largest speed is $\sqrt{2\mu_s g r}$
- That the car can take the curve at any speed
- That the car cannot take the curve at any speed

## Explanation

Adding an outward $m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ to the real forces in an inertial frame makes the radial sum zero for any friction force, so the equation imposes no limit on the speed. This contradicts the condition $v \le \sqrt{\mu_s g r}$. An inertial force belongs only to the equations written in the accelerating frame.
