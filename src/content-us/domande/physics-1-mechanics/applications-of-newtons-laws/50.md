The graph shows the speed $v$, in meters per second, of a small body falling from rest with linear drag, as a function of the time $t$ in seconds. The dashed line is the horizontal asymptote. What is the time constant of the motion?

::grafico{f="4*(1-exp(-x/0.4077))" asintoti="y=4" nomi="t,v" xmin=-0.15 xmax=2.6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=5 legenda=0}

- $0.41\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $39\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The asymptote gives the terminal speed $v_t = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since $v_t = \dfrac{m g}{b}$ and $\tau = \dfrac{m}{b}$, the time constant is
$$
\tau = \dfrac{v_t}{g} = \dfrac{4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 0.41\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
It equals the time the body would take to reach $v_t$ with the constant acceleration $g$ it has at the start.
