A formula derived by a student turns out to be dimensionally homogeneous. What can be concluded?

- It can still be wrong by a dimensionless factor, such as a missing $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- It is certainly correct.
- It is certainly wrong.
- All its numerical coefficients are equal to $1$.

## Explanation

The **principle of dimensional homogeneity** gives a necessary condition: a relation that violates it is wrong, while a relation that satisfies it is only compatible with being correct. Pure numbers such as $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $2\pi$ are dimensionless, so a formula missing one of them keeps the right dimensions and still gives wrong values.
