The momentum of a body is defined as $p = mv$, the product of its mass and its speed. What is its SI unit?

- $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$
- $\mathrm{N/s}$
- $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$
- $\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s^2}$

## Explanation

The unit of a **derived quantity** follows from its defining relation, so the unit of $mv$ is $\mathrm{kg} \cdot \mathrm{m/s} = \mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The unit $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$ is the newton, and $\mathrm{N/s} = \mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^3}$; the unit $\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s^2}$ is the joule, which belongs to $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.
