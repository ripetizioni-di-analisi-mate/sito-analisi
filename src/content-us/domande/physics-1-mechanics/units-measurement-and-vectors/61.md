A vector equation $\vec{A} = \vec{B}$ between two vectors of the $xy$ plane is equivalent to

- two numerical equations, one for each axis.
- one equation between the magnitudes.
- one equation between the sums of the components, $A_x + A_y = B_x + B_y$.
- one equation between the angles with the $x$ axis.

## Explanation

Two vectors are equal exactly when their components are equal, so in the plane $\vec{A} = \vec{B}$ means $A_x = B_x$ and $A_y = B_y$. Equal magnitudes alone allow different directions, equal angles alone allow different magnitudes, and equal sums of the components allow different vectors, such as $(1,\ 2)$ and $(2,\ 1)$. This is how a balance of forces in the plane gives two equations.
