Two vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ are equal when

- they have the same magnitude.
- they have the same magnitude, direction and sense, wherever they are drawn.
- they have the same magnitude and start from the same point.
- they lie on the same line.

## Explanation

A vector is determined by its magnitude, direction and sense, and the point where it is drawn is not part of it. Two vectors with the same magnitude may point in different directions, and two vectors on the same line may have opposite senses. Since a vector can be translated freely, the tip-to-tail construction of the sum is possible.
