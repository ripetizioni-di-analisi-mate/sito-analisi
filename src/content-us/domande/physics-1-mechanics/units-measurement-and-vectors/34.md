Let $A$ be a length, $\omega$ a quantity measured in $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, $v$ a velocity and $t$ a time. Which relation is NOT dimensionally homogeneous?

- $x = A\cos(\omega t)$
- $x = A\sin(v\,t)$
- $x = A + v\,t$
- $x = \dfrac{v}{\omega}\sin(\omega t)$

## Explanation

The argument of $\sin$ and $\cos$ must be dimensionless. In $A\sin(v\,t)$ the argument has $[v\,t] = [L]$, so the relation is not homogeneous. In the other relations $\omega t$ is dimensionless, the terms $A$ and $v\,t$ are both lengths, and $\left[\dfrac{v}{\omega}\right] = [L\,T^{-1}][T] = [L]$.
