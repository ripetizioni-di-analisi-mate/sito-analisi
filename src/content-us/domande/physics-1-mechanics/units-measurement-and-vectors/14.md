A quantity is defined by $Q = \dfrac{F\,t}{m}$, where $F$ is a force, $t$ a time and $m$ a mass. What is the SI unit of $Q$?

- $\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$
- $\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\mathrm{m\cdot s}$

## Explanation

The unit of $Q$ is read off its defining relation. Since $1\,\mathrm{N} = 1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$,
$$
\frac{\mathrm{N} \cdot \mathrm{s}}{\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2} \cdot \mathrm{s}}{\mathrm{kg}} = \mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
therefore $Q$ is a velocity. Forgetting the time gives $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and forgetting the division by the mass gives $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$.
