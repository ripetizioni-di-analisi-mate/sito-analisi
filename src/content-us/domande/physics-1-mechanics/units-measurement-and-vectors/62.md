Two forces $\vec{F}_1 = (12,\ 5)\,\mathrm{N}$ and $\vec{F}_2 = (-4,\ 9)\,\mathrm{N}$ act on a point. Which third force makes the resultant zero?

- $(8,\ 14)\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-8,\ -14)\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-16,\ 4)\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-8,\ 14)\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The resultant is zero when $\vec{F}_3 = -(\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2)$. Adding the components axis by axis gives $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 = (8,\ 14)\,\mathrm{N}$, therefore $\vec{F}_3 = (-8,\ -14)\,\mathrm{N}$. The force $(8,\ 14)\,\mathrm{N}$ doubles the resultant instead of cancelling it.
