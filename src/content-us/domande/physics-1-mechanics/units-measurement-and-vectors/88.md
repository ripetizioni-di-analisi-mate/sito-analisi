A force $\vec{F} = (0,\ -20,\ 0)\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied at the point $\vec{r} = (0.50,\ 0,\ 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the torque $\vec{r} \times \vec{F}$ about the origin?

- $10\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $-10\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $10\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $-10\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$

## Explanation

Both vectors lie in the $xy$ plane, so $\vec{\tau} = (r_xF_y - r_yF_x)\,\hat{k} = (0.50 \cdot (-20) - 0)\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} = -10\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The torque is perpendicular to the plane of $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{F}$, hence it has no $x$ or $y$ component. The negative sign corresponds to a clockwise turn from $\vec{r}$ to $\vec{F}$.
