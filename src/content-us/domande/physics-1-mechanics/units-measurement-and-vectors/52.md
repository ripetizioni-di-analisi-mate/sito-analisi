In the tip-to-tail construction, $\vec{B}$ is drawn from the tip of $\vec{A}$. Which vector is the sum $\vec{A} + \vec{B}$?

- the vector from the tip of $\vec{A}$ to the tail of $\vec{B}$
- the vector from the tip of $\vec{B}$ to the tail of $\vec{A}$
- the vector from the tail of $\vec{A}$ to the tip of $\vec{B}$
- the vector from the tail of $\vec{B}$ to the tip of $\vec{A}$

## Explanation

By the definition of the **sum of two vectors**, the sum runs from the starting point of the first vector to the end point of the second, as for two displacements performed one after the other. The tip of $\vec{A}$ and the tail of $\vec{B}$ coincide in this construction, so a vector between them is zero. The vector from the tip of $\vec{B}$ to the tail of $\vec{A}$ is $-(\vec{A} + \vec{B})$.
