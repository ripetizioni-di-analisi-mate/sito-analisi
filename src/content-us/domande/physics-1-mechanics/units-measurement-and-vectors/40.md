Two students propose $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$ and $t = \dfrac{2h}{g}$ for the fall time from height $h$, with $g$ an acceleration. Which statement is correct?

- Only $\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$ has the dimensions of a time.
- Both expressions have the dimensions of a time.
- Only $\dfrac{2h}{g}$ has the dimensions of a time.
- Neither expression has the dimensions of a time.

## Explanation

Since $\left[\dfrac{h}{g}\right] = \dfrac{[L]}{[L\,T^{-2}]} = [T^2]$, the ratio $\dfrac{2h}{g}$ has the dimensions of the square of a time, and its square root has the dimensions of a time. The second proposal is therefore rejected by dimensions alone, without substituting any number.
