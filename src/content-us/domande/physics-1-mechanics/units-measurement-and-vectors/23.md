A body of mass $m = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves at $v = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How is its kinetic energy $K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ written with the correct significant figures?

- $2 \times 10^{1}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $24.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $48\,\mathrm{J}$
- $24\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The calculation gives $K = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 4.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 24\,\mathrm{J}$. The factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the exponent $2$ are exact numbers and never limit the result, while $m$ and $v$ carry two significant figures each, so $K$ keeps two. Treating the $1$ of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ as a measured digit leads to one significant figure, and dropping the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ doubles the energy.
