The period of a simple pendulum is assumed to have the form $P = C\,\ell^{\,a} g^{\,b}$, where $\ell$ is the length of the string, $g$ the free-fall acceleration and $C$ a dimensionless constant. What are the exponents?

- $a = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $b = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $a = 1$ and $b = -1$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $b = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $b = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The right side has dimensions $[L]^{a}[L\,T^{-2}]^{b} = [L^{a+b}\,T^{-2b}]$, and it must equal $[T]$. Hence $-2b = 1$ and $a + b = 0$, so $b = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, $P = C\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$. The exponents $1$ and $-1$ give $[T^2]$, the square of a time.
