The velocity of a particle changes from $(4.0\,\hat{\imath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $(4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the magnitude of its average acceleration in that interval?

- $0$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The change of velocity is $\Delta\vec{v} = (-4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$, whose magnitude is $\sqrt{32}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.66\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Dividing by $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ gives $2.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$; the speed is the same at both instants, and yet the acceleration is not zero, since the direction of the velocity has changed.
