A particle moves with constant acceleration. In which case is its path a straight line?

- When the initial velocity is zero or parallel to the acceleration
- When the initial velocity is perpendicular to the acceleration
- Only when the initial velocity is zero
- In no case, since a nonzero constant acceleration always bends the path

## Explanation

If $\vec{v}_0$ is zero or parallel to $\vec{a}$, then both terms of $\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0 = \vec{v}_0\,t + \dfrac{1}{2}\,\vec{a}\,t^2$ lie along the same line, and so does the motion. If $\vec{v}_0$ has a component perpendicular to $\vec{a}$, that component gives a uniform motion across the line of $\vec{a}$ and the path is a parabola.
