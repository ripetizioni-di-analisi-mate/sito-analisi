A particle moving with constant acceleration has velocity $(2.0\,\hat{\imath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t = 0$ and velocity $(2.0\,\hat{\imath} + 8.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its acceleration?

- $(0.50\,\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $(2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $(4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $(8.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

From $\vec{v} = \vec{v}_0 + \vec{a}\,t$ the acceleration is $\dfrac{\vec{v} - \vec{v}_0}{t} = \dfrac{(8.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4.0\,\mathrm{s}} = (2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The $x$ component of the velocity has not changed, hence the acceleration has no $x$ component.
