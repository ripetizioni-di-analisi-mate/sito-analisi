A particle has initial velocity $(6.0\,\hat{\imath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and constant acceleration $(-2.0\,\hat{\imath} + 1.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At what instant does the $x$ component of its velocity vanish?

- $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.33\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The $x$ component of the velocity is $v_x = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} - (2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2})\,t$, and the $y$ part of the acceleration does not enter it. It vanishes at $t = \dfrac{6.0}{2.0}\,\mathrm{s} = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$; the instant $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$ is the one at which the coordinate $x$ returns to zero.
