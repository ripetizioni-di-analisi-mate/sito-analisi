A boat heads perpendicular to the banks of a river and keeps the same speed with respect to the water. If the current became faster, how would the crossing time change?

- It would decrease.
- It would increase.
- It would increase until the crossing became impossible.
- It would not change.

## Explanation

The current is parallel to the banks, therefore it adds nothing to the component of the velocity across the river, which stays equal to the speed of the boat with respect to the water. The crossing time is the width divided by that component, so it does not change; a faster current only carries the boat farther downstream.
