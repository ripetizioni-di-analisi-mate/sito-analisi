Two bodies collide elastically along a line. Before the collision $v_1 = +5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = +1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$; after it $v_2' = +4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is $v_1'$?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $+8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $+2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

In an elastic collision along a line the relative velocity reverses, $v_1' - v_2' = -(v_1 - v_2) = -4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Hence $v_1' = v_2' - 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the masses are not needed, since this relation replaces the energy equation.
