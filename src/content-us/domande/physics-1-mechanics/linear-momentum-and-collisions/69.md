A thin rod of length $L$ lies on the $x$ axis from $x = 0$ to $x = L$, and its linear density is $\lambda = cx$, with $c$ a positive constant. Where is its center of mass?

- $x = \dfrac{2L}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{L}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{L}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{3L}{4}$

## Explanation

The mass is $M = \displaystyle\int_0^L cx\,dx = \dfrac{cL^2}{2}$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^L x\,cx\,dx = \dfrac{cL^3}{3}$. Hence $x_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{cL^3}{3} \cdot \dfrac{2}{cL^2} = \dfrac{2L}{3}$, closer to the denser end of the rod.
