In a one-dimensional collision both final velocities are unknown. Why is conservation of momentum alone not sufficient to find them?

- It gives one equation for two unknowns.
- Momentum is not conserved in inelastic collisions.
- The external forces during the collision are too large.
- The kinetic energy must always be conserved as well.

## Explanation

Along one line conservation of momentum is the single equation $m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = m_1v_1' + m_2v_2'$ in the two unknowns $v_1'$ and $v_2'$. The second equation comes from the type of collision: conservation of kinetic energy if it is elastic, $v_1' = v_2'$ if it is perfectly inelastic, or a given coefficient of restitution.
