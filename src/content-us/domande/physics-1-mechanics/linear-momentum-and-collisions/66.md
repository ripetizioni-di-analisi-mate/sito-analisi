A particle of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at $x = 0$ and a particle of mass $6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at $x = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Where is their center of mass?

- $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 12\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The center of mass is the weighted average $x_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{2.0 \cdot 0 + 6.0 \cdot 4.0}{8.0}\,\mathrm{m} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$. It lies closer to the heavier particle, at distances $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the two particles, in inverse ratio to their masses.
