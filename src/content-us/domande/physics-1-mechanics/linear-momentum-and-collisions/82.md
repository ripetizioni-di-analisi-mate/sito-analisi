Two bodies of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ each move along the same line at $+3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $+1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the largest amount of kinetic energy that a collision between them can dissipate?

- $10\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The center of mass moves at $v_{\text{cm}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the term $\dfrac{1}{2}Mv_{\text{cm}}^2 = 8.0\,\mathrm{J}$ of König's theorem cannot change. The total kinetic energy is $9.0\,\mathrm{J} + 1.0\,\mathrm{J} = 10\,\mathrm{J}$, therefore at most $10\,\mathrm{J} - 8.0\,\mathrm{J} = 2.0\,\mathrm{J}$ can be dissipated, as happens in a perfectly inelastic collision.
