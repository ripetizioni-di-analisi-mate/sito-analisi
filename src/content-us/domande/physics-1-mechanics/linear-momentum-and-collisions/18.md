Why do the internal forces of a system not change its total momentum?

- They do no work on the system.
- By Newton's third law they occur in pairs of opposite forces.
- They are much smaller than the external forces.
- They act only for a short time.

## Explanation

The force $\vec{F}_{ij}$ exerted on particle $i$ by particle $j$ and the force $\vec{F}_{ji}$ exerted on $j$ by $i$ satisfy $\vec{F}_{ij} + \vec{F}_{ji} = \vec{0}$. In the sum of the rates of change of the momenta these pairs cancel, therefore only the external forces remain. Internal forces can do work, as in an explosion, and they still leave $\vec{P}$ unchanged.
