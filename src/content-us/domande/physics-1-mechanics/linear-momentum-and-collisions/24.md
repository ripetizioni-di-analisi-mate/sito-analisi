Two blocks collide on a horizontal table with friction. Why can the momentum of the pair be treated as conserved during the collision?

- Friction is an internal force of the pair.
- The impulse of friction during the short contact is negligible.
- The vertical forces balance, hence friction vanishes.
- Friction does no work during the collision.

## Explanation

Friction is external to the pair and has the magnitude of a fraction of the weights, while the contact forces of the collision are much larger and act for a very short time. The impulse of friction over that interval is therefore negligible, and the momentum just before the collision equals the momentum just after it. Over a longer interval, such as the sliding that follows, friction changes the momentum.
