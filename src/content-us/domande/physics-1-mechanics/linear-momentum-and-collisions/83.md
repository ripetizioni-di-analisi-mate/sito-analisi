In a collision between the two bodies of an isolated system, what happens to the term $\dfrac{1}{2}Mv_{\text{cm}}^2$ of König's theorem?

- It is entirely dissipated in a perfectly inelastic collision.
- It increases in an elastic collision.
- It becomes zero after the collision.
- It stays the same in every collision.

## Explanation

Conservation of momentum keeps $\vec{P} = M\vec{v}_{\text{cm}}$ constant, hence $\dfrac{1}{2}Mv_{\text{cm}}^2$ does not change. A collision changes only the kinetic energy relative to the center of mass, and a perfectly inelastic collision removes that part, not the first.
