A uniform thin rod of length $2L$ is bent at its midpoint into a right angle, with one arm from $(0,\ 0)$ to $(L,\ 0)$ and the other from $(0,\ 0)$ to $(0,\ L)$. Where is its center of mass?

- $\left(\dfrac{L}{2},\ \dfrac{L}{2}\right)$
- $\left(\dfrac{L}{3},\ \dfrac{L}{3}\right)$
- $\left(\dfrac{L}{8},\ \dfrac{L}{8}\right)$
- $\left(\dfrac{L}{4},\ \dfrac{L}{4}\right)$

## Explanation

Each arm has half of the mass and its center of mass at its midpoint, $\left(\dfrac{L}{2},\ 0\right)$ and $\left(0,\ \dfrac{L}{2}\right)$. The average of these two points with equal weights is $\left(\dfrac{L}{4},\ \dfrac{L}{4}\right)$, which lies outside the material of the rod.
