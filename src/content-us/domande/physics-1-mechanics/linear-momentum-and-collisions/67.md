Where does the center of mass of two particles lie?

- On the segment joining them, at distances proportional to the masses
- At the midpoint of the segment joining them, whatever the masses
- Outside the segment joining them, beyond the heavier particle
- On the segment joining them, at distances inversely proportional to the masses

## Explanation

With the origin at the first particle and the second at distance $d$, $x_{\text{cm}} = \dfrac{m_2 d}{m_1 + m_2}$, so the distances from the two particles are $\dfrac{m_2 d}{m_1 + m_2}$ and $\dfrac{m_1 d}{m_1 + m_2}$. Their ratio is $\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}$, the inverse of the ratio of the masses, therefore the center of mass is closer to the heavier particle.
