A cart of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moving at $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ hits an identical cart at rest on a straight track. After the collision the first cart moves at $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the second at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, both in the original sense. How is the collision classified?

- Elastic.
- Inelastic, but not perfectly inelastic.
- Perfectly inelastic.
- Impossible, because momentum is not conserved.

## Explanation

The total momentum is $4.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ before and $1.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} + 3.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ after, so the data are consistent. The kinetic energy falls from $8.0\,\mathrm{J}$ to $0.50\,\mathrm{J} + 4.5\,\mathrm{J} = 5.0\,\mathrm{J}$, hence the collision is inelastic, and it is not perfectly inelastic because the two carts move with different velocities.
