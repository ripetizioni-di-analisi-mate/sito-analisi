A shell fired over level ground would land $600\,\mathrm{m}$ from the launch point. At the top of its path, $300\,\mathrm{m}$ from the launch point, it explodes into two fragments: one, with one third of the mass, is left at rest and falls vertically. Air resistance is negligible. How far from the launch point does the other fragment land?

- $900\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1\,200\,\mathrm{m}$
- $600\,\mathrm{m}$
- $750\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The explosion is internal, so the center of mass lands at $600\,\mathrm{m}$, and both fragments reach the ground together because neither has a vertical velocity after the explosion. Then $600\,\mathrm{m} = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 300\,\mathrm{m} + \dfrac{2}{3}\,x_2$, hence $x_2 = 750\,\mathrm{m}$; the value $900\,\mathrm{m}$ holds for two fragments of equal mass.
