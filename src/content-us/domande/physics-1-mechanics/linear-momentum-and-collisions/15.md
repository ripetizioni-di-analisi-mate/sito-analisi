A ball dropped onto a hard floor rebounds, and the contact lasts $2.0\,\mathrm{ms}$. Why is the impulse of the weight during the contact negligible with respect to the impulse of the normal force?

- The weight and the normal force are an action-reaction pair.
- During the contact the normal force is much larger than the weight.
- The weight does no work during the contact.
- The weight is perpendicular to the change of momentum.

## Explanation

Both forces act for the same $2.0\,\mathrm{ms}$, so their impulses are in the ratio of their average magnitudes. The normal force reverses the momentum of the ball in that short interval, therefore it is many times larger than the weight. The weight and the normal force act on the same body, hence they are not an action-reaction pair.
