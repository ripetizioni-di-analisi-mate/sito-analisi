Two skaters, $A$ of mass $2m$ and $B$ of mass $m$, stand at rest on frictionless ice and push each other apart. Which statement about the instant after the push is correct?

- Their speeds are equal, and $A$ has twice the momentum of $B$.
- Their momenta have equal magnitudes, and $A$ has twice the speed of $B$.
- Their momenta have equal magnitudes, and $A$ has half the speed of $B$.
- Their kinetic energies are equal, and $A$ has half the speed of $B$.

## Explanation

The total momentum is zero before and after the push, hence $2m\,v_A = m\,v_B$ in magnitude. Therefore $v_A = \dfrac{v_B}{2}$, and the kinetic energies differ, since $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$ gives $K_B = 2K_A$.
