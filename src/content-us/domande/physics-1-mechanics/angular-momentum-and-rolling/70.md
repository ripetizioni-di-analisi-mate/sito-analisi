A body turning freely about a fixed axis, with no external torque about that axis, reduces its moment of inertia to one third of the initial value. By what factor does its rotational kinetic energy change?

- It does not change
- It is multiplied by $9$
- It is multiplied by $3$
- It is divided by $3$

## Explanation

The angular momentum $L$ is conserved, and the kinetic energy is $K = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}$. With $L$ fixed, $K$ is inversely proportional to $I$, hence it is multiplied by $3$. The factor $9$ is the one by which $\omega^2$ grows, before the division of $I$ by $3$ is taken into account.
