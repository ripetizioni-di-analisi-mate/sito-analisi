The theorem $\dfrac{d\vec{L}_O}{dt} = \vec{\tau}_O^{\,\mathrm{ext}}$ for a system of particles requires a hypothesis on the internal force between any two particles. Which one?

- Equal and opposite, and directed along the line joining the two particles
- Equal and opposite, with any direction
- Perpendicular to the line joining the two particles
- Doing no work on the system

## Explanation

Each pair of internal forces contributes $(\vec{r}_i - \vec{r}_j) \times \vec{F}_{ij}$ to the rate of change of $\vec{L}_O$, which vanishes only if $\vec{F}_{ij}$ is parallel to $\vec{r}_i - \vec{r}_j$. Two equal and opposite forces acting along two distinct parallel lines form a couple, whose torque is not zero.
