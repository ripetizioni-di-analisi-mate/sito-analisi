A disk of moment of inertia $0.012\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ and radius $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is at rest on a fixed frictionless axis through its center. A ball of mass $0.050\,\mathrm{kg}$, moving at $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along a line tangent to the rim, strikes the rim and sticks to it. What is the angular speed just after the impact?

- $6.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $40\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $1.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $5.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The angular momentum of the ball about the axis is $m\,v\,R = 0.050 \cdot 8.0 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} = 0.080\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$, and it is conserved, since the reaction of the axis has no torque about the axis. After the impact the moment of inertia is $I + mR^2 = 0.014\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, hence
$$
\omega = \frac{0.080}{0.014}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 5.7\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
Leaving out the term $mR^2$ of the ball gives $6.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
