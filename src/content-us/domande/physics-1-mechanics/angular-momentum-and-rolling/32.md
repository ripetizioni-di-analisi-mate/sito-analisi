A ball rolls without slipping down an inclined plane, speeding up. In which direction does the static friction on the ball act?

- Down the slope
- Perpendicular to the slope
- Nowhere, since it is zero
- Up the slope

## Explanation

The rotation of the ball speeds up, and among the forces acting on it only the friction at the contact has a torque about the center. A friction directed up the slope, applied at the lowest point, turns the ball in the sense of its rolling. The equation $Mg\sin\theta - f = Ma_{\mathrm{cm}}$ with $a_{\mathrm{cm}} < g\sin\theta$ gives the same conclusion, $f > 0$ up the slope.
