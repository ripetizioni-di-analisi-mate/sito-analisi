A disk of moment of inertia $0.60\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ turns at $20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about a frictionless vertical axis. A second disk, of moment of inertia $0.20\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ and at rest, is dropped onto it along the same axis, and friction brings the two to a common angular speed. What is that angular speed?

- $20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $17\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $15\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The friction between the disks is an internal force, therefore the angular momentum along the axis is conserved, and $I_1\,\omega_0 = (I_1 + I_2)\,\omega$ gives
$$
\omega = \frac{0.60}{0.80} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 15\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
Kinetic energy is not conserved, since friction dissipates part of it, and equating the kinetic energies would give $17\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
