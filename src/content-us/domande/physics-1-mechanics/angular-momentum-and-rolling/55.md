A particle of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the sense of increasing $x$ along the line $y = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. What is its angular momentum about the point $(0,\ 5.0\,\mathrm{m})$?

- $-9.0\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- $+9.0\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- $-6.0\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- $+15\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$

## Explanation

Relative to the point $(0,\ 5.0\,\mathrm{m})$ the particle has $y' = 2.0\,\mathrm{m} - 5.0\,\mathrm{m} = -3.0\,\mathrm{m}$, hence
$$
\ell_z = m\,(x'\,v_y - y'\,v_x) = -1.0 \cdot (-3.0) \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} = +9.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} .
$$
The particle passes below that point moving to the right, which is a counterclockwise motion as seen from it. About the origin the same particle has $-6.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$, because the angular momentum depends on the chosen point.
