A disk of moment of inertia $0.40\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ turns freely at $6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about a vertical axis, carrying a small bead of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis. The bead slides to the axis. What is the new angular speed of the disk?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $14\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $9.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

Initially the moment of inertia is $I_1 = 0.40 + 0.20 \cdot 1.0^2 = 0.60\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, and at the end the bead on the axis adds nothing, so $I_2 = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. Conservation of angular momentum gives
$$
\omega_2 = \frac{I_1}{I_2}\,\omega_1 = \frac{0.60}{0.40} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 9.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
