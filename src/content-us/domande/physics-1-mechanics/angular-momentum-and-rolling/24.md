A ball rolls without slipping down a slope, and static friction acts at the contact. Why is its mechanical energy conserved?

- Static friction is always much smaller than the weight
- Static friction acts on a point whose velocity is zero, so it does no work
- The negative work of friction is balanced by the work of the normal force
- Static friction acts only at the instant of release

## Explanation

The power of a force is the scalar product of the force and the velocity of the point on which it acts. The contact point of a body that rolls without slipping is at rest, therefore static friction does no work, and the normal force, perpendicular to the displacement, does no work either. Only the weight does work, and it is a conservative force.
