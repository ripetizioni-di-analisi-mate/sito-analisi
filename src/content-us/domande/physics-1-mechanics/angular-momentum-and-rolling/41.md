A homogeneous solid cylinder rolls without slipping down an inclined plane. It is replaced by a homogeneous solid cylinder of the same radius and twice the mass. What happens to the static friction at the contact?

- It doubles
- It does not change
- It is halved
- It becomes four times as large

## Explanation

The friction is $f = \dfrac{\beta}{1 + \beta}\,Mg\sin\theta$, which is proportional to the mass, since $\beta = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is the same for both cylinders. The acceleration $\dfrac{g\sin\theta}{1 + \beta}$ does not contain the mass and is the same for both.
