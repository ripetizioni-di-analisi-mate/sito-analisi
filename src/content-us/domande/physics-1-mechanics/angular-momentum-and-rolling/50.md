A planet moves on an elliptical orbit around the Sun, and the gravitational force of the Sun is the only force on it. What can be said of the angular momentum of the planet about the Sun?

- It is constant, because the speed of the planet is constant
- It is constant, because the gravitational force is directed along $\vec{r}$
- It changes, because the distance from the Sun changes
- It is constant, because the gravitational force is perpendicular to $\vec{r}$

## Explanation

The force on the planet points toward the Sun, along $\vec{r}$, hence its torque $\vec{r} \times \vec{F}$ about the Sun vanishes, and $\dfrac{d\vec{\ell}}{dt} = \vec{\tau}$ gives a constant angular momentum. On an ellipse the speed and the distance both change, while $\ell = m\,r\,v\sin\varphi$ stays the same.
