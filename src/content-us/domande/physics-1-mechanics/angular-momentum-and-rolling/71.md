A skater spinning on frictionless ice pulls her arms in, and her kinetic energy increases. Where does the additional energy come from?

- From the work done by the friction of the ice
- From the work done by her muscles in pulling her arms inward
- From the work done by gravity
- From a decrease of her angular momentum

## Explanation

The angular momentum is conserved, so $K = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}$ grows as $I$ decreases. The increase is the work of internal forces, since her muscles pull the arms towards the axis and the arms move in the direction of the pull. Gravity does no work, since her center of mass does not move vertically, and the frictionless ice does no work either.
