A skater spins on frictionless ice about the vertical axis through her body. Why is her angular momentum about that axis conserved?

- No external force acts on the skater
- Her moment of inertia about the axis does not change
- The weight and the reaction of the ice are equal and opposite, so the resultant force is zero
- The weight and the reaction of the ice are vertical, so their torque about the axis is zero

## Explanation

Conservation requires zero external torque about the axis. Both external forces are vertical, that is parallel to the axis, and a force parallel to an axis has no torque about it. A zero resultant force would not be enough by itself, since two equal and opposite forces on different lines form a couple.
