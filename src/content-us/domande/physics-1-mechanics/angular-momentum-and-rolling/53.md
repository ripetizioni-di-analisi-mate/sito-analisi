In the proof of $\dfrac{d\vec{\ell}}{dt} = \vec{\tau}$ for a particle, the term $\dfrac{d\vec{r}}{dt} \times \vec{p}$ vanishes. Why?

- Because $\dfrac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$ and $\vec{p}$ are perpendicular
- Because $\vec{p}$ is constant
- Because $\dfrac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$ and $\vec{p}$ are parallel
- Because $\vec{r}$ is constant

## Explanation

The derivative $\dfrac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$ is the velocity $\vec{v}$, and $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$ has the same direction, hence their vector product is zero. The argument does not require $\vec{p}$ to be constant, since its derivative appears in the other term, where it gives the torque.
