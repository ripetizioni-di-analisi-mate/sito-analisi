A puck on a frictionless horizontal table is tied to a string that passes through a small hole in the table. The puck moves on a circle of radius $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the string is pulled from below until the radius is $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the new speed of the puck?

- $0.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The tension always points towards the hole, so its torque about the hole is zero, while the weight and the normal force balance. The angular momentum $\ell = m\,v\,r$ about the hole is therefore conserved, and
$$
v_2 = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\,v_1 = \frac{0.60}{0.20} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Conserving the kinetic energy would leave the speed unchanged, and $3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the square root of the ratio of the radii.
