A nonzero external force acts on a system, and its line of action always passes through a point $O$ fixed in an inertial frame. No other external force acts. What happens to the angular momentum of the system about $O$?

- It is conserved
- It increases
- It decreases
- It changes direction and keeps its magnitude

## Explanation

A force whose line passes through $O$ has zero lever arm about $O$, hence zero torque. By the **conservation of angular momentum** the angular momentum about $O$ is constant, even though the linear momentum changes, since the resultant force is not zero.
