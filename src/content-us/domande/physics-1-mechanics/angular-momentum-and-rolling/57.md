A particle moves with constant velocity along a straight line that does not pass through the point $O$. How does its angular momentum about $O$ behave as the particle goes past $O$?

- It is largest at the point of closest approach
- It stays constant
- It grows as the particle approaches $O$
- It changes sign after the point of closest approach

## Explanation

The magnitude is $\ell = m\,v\,b$, where the lever arm $b$ is the distance of the line from $O$, the same at every instant. The direction is fixed as well, since the particle keeps turning in the same sense as seen from $O$. This agrees with $\dfrac{d\vec{\ell}}{dt} = \vec{\tau} = \vec{0}$, since no force acts.
