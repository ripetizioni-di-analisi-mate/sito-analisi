A car traveling at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes with constant acceleration of magnitude $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until it stops, and then remains at rest. What distance does it cover in the first $8.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after it starts braking?

- $24.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $120\,\mathrm{m}$
- $75.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $37.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The car stops after $\dfrac{15\,\mathrm{m/s}}{3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, therefore the equations of uniformly accelerated motion hold only up to that instant. The distance is the stopping distance
$$
d = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,|a|} = \dfrac{(15\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 37.5\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
while substituting $t = 8.0\,\mathrm{s}$ in the law of motion would describe a car that moves backward.
