How is the instantaneous velocity of a body at the instant $t$ defined?

- As the average velocity over the whole motion.
- As the limit of the average velocity over intervals that shrink to the instant $t$.
- As the ratio $\dfrac{x(t)}{t}$ of the position to the time.
- As the change in position during the second that follows the instant $t$.

## Explanation

The instantaneous velocity is $v(t) = \displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \dfrac{x(t + \Delta t) - x(t)}{\Delta t}$, that is, the derivative of the law of motion. An average over a finite interval, however short, gives in general a different value.
