A car brakes to rest with constant deceleration. If the initial speed is doubled and the deceleration is unchanged, how does the braking time change?

- It is multiplied by $4$.
- It is doubled.
- It is unchanged.
- It is multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$.

## Explanation

From $v = v_0 + a\,t$ with $v = 0$ the braking time is $t = \dfrac{v_0}{|a|}$, which is proportional to $v_0$. The braking time is therefore doubled, while the braking distance, which grows with $v_0^2$, is multiplied by $4$.
