A motion consists of several stages, each with its own constant acceleration. How are the equations of uniformly accelerated motion applied?

- Once to the whole motion, with the average of the accelerations of the stages.
- Once to the whole motion, with the acceleration of the longest stage.
- To one stage at a time, with each stage starting from rest.
- To one stage at a time, with the final position and velocity of each stage as the initial values of the next.

## Explanation

The equations hold only where the acceleration is constant, hence they are written for each stage separately. The stages are joined by continuity, therefore the position and the velocity at the end of a stage are the initial data of the following one.
