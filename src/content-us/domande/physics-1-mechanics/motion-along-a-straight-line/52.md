A car traveling at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ stops in $18\,\mathrm{m}$ with constant deceleration. With the same deceleration, what is its stopping distance when it travels at $45\,\mathrm{m/s}$?

- $54\,\mathrm{m}$
- $162\,\mathrm{m}$
- $72\,\mathrm{m}$
- $36\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The stopping distance is $d = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,|a|}$, hence it grows with the square of the initial speed. Tripling the speed multiplies the distance by $9$, therefore $d = 9 \cdot 18\,\mathrm{m} = 162\,\mathrm{m}$.
