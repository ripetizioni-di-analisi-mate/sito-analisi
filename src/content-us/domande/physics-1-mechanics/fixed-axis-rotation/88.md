A wheel with moment of inertia $2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ speeds up from $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ to $20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. What work does the net torque do on it?

- $20\,\mathrm{J}$
- $600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $100\,\mathrm{J}$
- $300\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

By the **work-energy theorem for rotation** the work equals the change in kinetic energy,
$$
W = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I\,(\omega_2^2 - \omega_1^2) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot (400 - 100)\,\mathrm{J} = 300\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The kinetic energy depends on $\omega^2$, so the difference of the squares enters, which differs from the square $(\omega_2 - \omega_1)^2 = 100\,\mathrm{rad^2/s^2}$ of the difference.
