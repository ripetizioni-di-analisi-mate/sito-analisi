A body of mass $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ has moment of inertia $0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about an axis at $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from its center of mass. What is its moment of inertia about the parallel axis through the center of mass?

- $0.44\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.75\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $-0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

From the **parallel-axis theorem** $I = I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M d^2$, therefore
$$
I_{\mathrm{cm}} = I - M d^2 = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} - 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.25\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 0.25\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} .
$$
The term $M d^2$ is subtracted, since the axis through the center of mass gives the smallest moment of inertia.
