The same net torque acts on a thin ring and on a uniform solid disk of the same mass and radius, each about its central axis. How do their angular accelerations compare?

- The angular acceleration of the disk is four times that of the ring
- The angular acceleration of the ring is twice that of the disk
- The two angular accelerations are equal
- The angular acceleration of the disk is twice that of the ring

## Explanation

From $\alpha = \dfrac{\tau}{I}$ with the same torque, the angular acceleration is inversely proportional to the moment of inertia. The disk has $I = \dfrac{1}{2}M R^2$ and the ring $I = M R^2$, therefore the disk acquires twice the angular acceleration of the ring.
