Why is a single variable enough to locate a rigid body that rotates about a fixed axis?

- The mutual distances of the particles are fixed, so the position of one point off the axis fixes all the others
- Every point of the body moves with the same speed
- The axis of rotation always passes through the center of mass
- Every point of the body travels on a circle of the same radius

## Explanation

In a rigid body the distances between the particles stay constant, and every point turns about the same axis. Once the angular position of one point off the axis is known, the position of every other point follows, therefore the single angle $\theta$ locates the whole body. Points at different distances from the axis travel on circles of different radii and have different speeds.
