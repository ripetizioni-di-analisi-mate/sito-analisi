Why is the radian a dimensionless unit?

- It is defined as the ratio of an arc length to a radius, two lengths
- It is defined as the ratio of an arc length to the time taken to travel it
- Angles are always measured from a reference line fixed in the body
- It is defined as one three-hundred-sixtieth of a complete turn

## Explanation

The angular position in radians is $\theta = \dfrac{s}{r}$, the ratio of the arc traveled to the radius of the circle. Both are lengths, so their ratio is a pure number. One complete turn corresponds to the arc $2\pi r$, hence to $2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$, while one three-hundred-sixtieth of a turn is the degree.
