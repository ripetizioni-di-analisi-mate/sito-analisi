A thin ring and a uniform solid disk have the same mass and the same radius. Which one has the larger moment of inertia about its central axis perpendicular to its plane?

- The ring, since all its mass lies at the distance $R$ from the axis
- The disk, since its mass is spread over a larger area
- The disk, since part of its mass lies close to the axis
- Neither, since the mass and the radius are the same

## Explanation

The moment of inertia weights each mass with the square of its distance from the axis. In the ring every particle is at the distance $R$, which gives $I = M R^2$, while in the disk part of the mass lies closer to the axis and $I = \dfrac{1}{2}M R^2$.
