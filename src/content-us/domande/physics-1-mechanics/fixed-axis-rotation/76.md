A body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from a cord wound around a pulley of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, treated as a uniform disk on a frictionless axle. The cord is massless and does not slip. What is the angular acceleration of the pulley?

- $98.1\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $65.4\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $0.654\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $49.1\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

The equations $m g - T = m a$ and $T R = \dfrac{1}{2}M R^2\,\alpha$ with $a = \alpha R$ give
$$
a = \dfrac{m g}{m + \dfrac{M}{2}} = \dfrac{2.0 \cdot 9.81}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 6.54\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
\qquad
\alpha = \dfrac{a}{R} = 65.4\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} .
$$
The pulley enters the denominator with half its mass, because its moment of inertia is $\dfrac{1}{2}M R^2$.
