Why does a single value of $\omega$ describe the rotation of every point of a rigid body about a fixed axis?

- All the points move with the same speed
- All the points are at the same distance from the axis
- All the points have the same centripetal acceleration
- All the points turn through the same angle in the same time

## Explanation

The body is rigid, hence when one point turns through $\Delta\theta$ every other point turns through the same $\Delta\theta$ in the same time. The ratio $\dfrac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}$ is therefore common to all the points. Their speeds $v = \omega r$ and centripetal accelerations $\omega^2 r$ grow with the distance $r$ from the axis.
