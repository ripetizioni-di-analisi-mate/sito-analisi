A wheel turns with constant angular velocity $\omega \neq 0$. Which statement describes the acceleration of a point of its rim, at distance $r$ from the axis?

- It points away from the axis and has magnitude $\omega^2 r$
- It is tangent to the rim and has magnitude $\omega^2 r$
- It is zero, since the angular velocity is constant
- It points towards the axis and has magnitude $\omega^2 r$

## Explanation

With constant $\omega$ the angular acceleration is zero, hence the tangential component $a_t = \alpha r$ vanishes. The radial component $a_r = \omega^2 r$ remains, directed towards the axis, because the direction of the velocity keeps changing. This is uniform circular motion.
