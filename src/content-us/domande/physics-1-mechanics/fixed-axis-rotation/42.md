What does the moment of inertia of a body about an axis measure?

- The mass of the body per unit distance from the axis
- How hard it is to change the angular velocity of the body about that axis
- The torque needed to keep the body turning at constant angular velocity
- The distance of the center of mass of the body from the axis

## Explanation

In the law $\tau_{\mathrm{net}} = I\,\alpha$ the moment of inertia plays the role that the mass plays in $F = m a$: the larger $I$, the smaller the angular acceleration produced by a given torque. A body turning at constant angular velocity about a frictionless axis needs no torque at all.
