A wheel speeds up uniformly from $6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ to $18\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ in $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Through what angle does it turn in this time?

- $72\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $48\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $96\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $24\,\mathrm{rad}$

## Explanation

With constant angular acceleration the graph of $\omega(t)$ is a straight line, so the angular displacement is the average of the initial and final angular velocities times the time,
$$
\Delta\theta = \dfrac{\omega_0 + \omega}{2}\,t = \dfrac{6.0 + 18}{2}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} = 48\,\mathrm{rad} .
$$
The same value follows from $\alpha = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ and $\Delta\theta = \omega_0 t + \dfrac{1}{2}\alpha t^2 = 24\,\mathrm{rad} + 24\,\mathrm{rad}$.
