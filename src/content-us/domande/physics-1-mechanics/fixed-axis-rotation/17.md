The graph of $\omega(t)$ for a wheel is a straight line from $\omega = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ at $t = 0$ to $\omega = 2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the angular acceleration?

- $+2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $-0.50\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $-3.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $-2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

The angular acceleration is the slope of the graph of $\omega(t)$, which is constant for a straight line, so
$$
\alpha = \dfrac{\Delta\omega}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} - 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}}{4.0\,\mathrm{s}} = -2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} .
$$
The angular velocity decreases while staying positive, consistently with the negative sign of $\alpha$.
