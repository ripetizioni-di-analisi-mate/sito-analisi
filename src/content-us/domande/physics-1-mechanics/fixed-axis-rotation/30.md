A wheel turning at $4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ speeds up with constant angular acceleration $\alpha = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. What is its angular velocity after it has turned through a further $10\,\mathrm{rad}$?

- $6.78\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $11.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $34.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $8.72\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The time is not involved, therefore
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 + 2\,\alpha\,\Delta\theta} = \sqrt{16 + 2 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = \sqrt{76}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 8.72\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The square root applies to the whole sum, hence it differs from $\omega_0 + \sqrt{2\,\alpha\,\Delta\theta} = 11.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
