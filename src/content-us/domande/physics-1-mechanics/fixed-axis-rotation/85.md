A motor delivers a constant power. If its angular velocity is halved, how does the torque it exerts change?

- It becomes four times as large
- It halves
- It stays the same
- It doubles

## Explanation

From $P = \tau\,\omega$ with constant $P$, the torque is inversely proportional to the angular velocity, $\tau = \dfrac{P}{\omega}$. Halving $\omega$ therefore doubles $\tau$.
