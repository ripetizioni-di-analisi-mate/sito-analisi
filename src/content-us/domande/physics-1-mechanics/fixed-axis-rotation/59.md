Two axes $a$ and $b$ are parallel to an axis through the center of mass of a body of mass $M$, both at distance $d$ from it, on opposite sides. How are the moments of inertia $I_a$ and $I_b$ related?

- $I_a = I_b$
- $I_a = I_b + 4 M d^2$
- $I_a = I_b + M d^2$
- They cannot be compared without the value of $I_{\mathrm{cm}}$

## Explanation

The **parallel-axis theorem** gives $I_a = I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M d^2$ and $I_b = I_{\mathrm{cm}} + M d^2$, since only the distance from the axis through the center of mass enters the formula. The two moments of inertia are therefore equal, whatever the value of $I_{\mathrm{cm}}$.
