A body hangs from a cord wound around a pulley that turns on a frictionless axle. Why does the body descend with a smaller acceleration when the pulley has mass than when its mass is negligible?

- The weight of the pulley adds to the load carried by the cord
- The cord slips on the rim of a pulley with mass
- The cord must exert a torque to spin up the pulley, so the tension is larger and the net force on the body is smaller
- The axle exerts on the pulley a torque that opposes the rotation

## Explanation

The pulley obeys $T R = I\,\alpha$, so a pulley with moment of inertia $I$ requires a tension $T > 0$ to acquire the angular acceleration $\alpha = \dfrac{a}{R}$. The body then has net force $m g - T < m g$, and its acceleration is smaller than $g$. The frictionless axle passes through the axis and exerts no torque about it.
