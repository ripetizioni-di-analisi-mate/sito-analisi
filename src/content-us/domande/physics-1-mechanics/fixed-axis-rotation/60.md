A body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rotates about two axes parallel to an axis through its center of mass, at distances $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from it. By how much does its moment of inertia about the farther axis exceed that about the nearer one?

- $0.080\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.18\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.16\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

Applying the **parallel-axis theorem** to both axes, $I_{\mathrm{cm}}$ cancels in the difference, so
$$
I_2 - I_1 = M\,(d_2^2 - d_1^2) = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (0.09 - 0.01)\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.16\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} .
$$
The difference of the squares differs from the square $(d_2 - d_1)^2$ of the difference.
