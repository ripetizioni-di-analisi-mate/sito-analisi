Why do the internal forces between the particles of a rigid body give no net torque about an axis?

- They are always parallel to the axis of rotation
- They are much smaller than the external forces
- Their lines of action always pass through the axis
- They act in pairs that are equal, opposite and along the same line, so their torques cancel

## Explanation

By Newton's third law the force of one particle on another is equal and opposite to the reaction, and the two forces act along the line joining the particles. They have the same lever arm and opposite senses of rotation, therefore their torques cancel and only the external forces contribute to the net torque.
