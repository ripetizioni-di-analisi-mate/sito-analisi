A uniform solid disk of mass $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ turns on a frictionless axle through its center. A force of $10\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied tangent to its rim. What is the angular acceleration of the disk?

- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

The force is tangent to the rim, hence its torque is $\tau = F R = 5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, and the disk has $I = \dfrac{1}{2}M R^2 = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. By **Newton's second law for rotation**,
$$
\alpha = \dfrac{\tau}{I} = \dfrac{5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}} = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} .
$$
Taking $I = M R^2$, the value of a ring, gives half the result.
