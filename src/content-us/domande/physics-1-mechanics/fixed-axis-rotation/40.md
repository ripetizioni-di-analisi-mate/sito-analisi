A wheel turns counterclockwise and speeds up with constant angular acceleration. How does the radial acceleration of a point of its rim change?

- It increases, since it equals $\omega^2 r$ and $\omega$ increases
- It stays constant, since the angular acceleration is constant
- It stays zero, since the rotation is not uniform
- It decreases, since the tangential acceleration takes its place

## Explanation

The radial component is $a_r = \omega^2 r$, which depends on the angular velocity and not on the angular acceleration. The wheel speeds up, hence $\omega$ grows and so does $a_r$. The constant tangential component $a_t = \alpha r$ adds to it, perpendicular to the radius.
