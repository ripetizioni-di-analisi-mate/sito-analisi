With counterclockwise taken as positive, a wheel turns counterclockwise through $20\,\mathrm{rad}$ while a constant frictional torque of magnitude $0.50\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ acts on it. What work does the frictional torque do?

- $-1.6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-0.025\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{J}$
- $+10\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The frictional torque opposes the rotation, hence $\tau = -0.50\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ while $\Delta\theta = +20\,\mathrm{rad}$. A constant torque does the work
$$
W = \tau\,\Delta\theta = -0.50\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{rad} = -10\,\mathrm{J} ,
$$
negative because it removes kinetic energy from the wheel.
