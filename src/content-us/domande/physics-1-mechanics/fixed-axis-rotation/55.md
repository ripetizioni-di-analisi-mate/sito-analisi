The parallel-axis theorem $I = I_0 + M d^2$ gives the moment of inertia about an axis at distance $d$ from a second, parallel axis. Which axis must the second one be for the formula to hold?

- The axis through the center of mass perpendicular to the first one
- The axis through the point of the body closest to the first one
- Any axis parallel to the first one
- The axis through the center of mass

## Explanation

The proof uses $\displaystyle\int x\,dm = 0$, which holds when the coordinates are measured from the center of mass, so $I_0$ must be $I_{\mathrm{cm}}$ about the parallel axis through the center of mass. Between two parallel axes neither of which passes through the center of mass, the formula must be applied twice, passing through $I_{\mathrm{cm}}$.
