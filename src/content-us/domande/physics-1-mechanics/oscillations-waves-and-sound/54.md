A driven oscillator is at resonance, $\omega_d = \omega_0$. The damping constant $b$ is halved, while $F_0$, $k$ and $m$ are unchanged. How does the steady amplitude change?

- It is halved
- It doubles
- It is multiplied by $4$
- It does not change

## Explanation

At resonance the steady amplitude is $A = \dfrac{F_0}{b\,\omega_0}$, hence it is inversely proportional to $b$, and halving the damping constant doubles it. The damping sets the height of the resonance peak.
