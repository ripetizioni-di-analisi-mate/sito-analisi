An oscillator with natural angular frequency $\omega_0$ is subject to a weak resistive force proportional to the velocity. How does the angular frequency $\omega'$ of its damped oscillations compare with $\omega_0$?

- It depends on the initial amplitude
- It is smaller than $\omega_0$
- It is larger than $\omega_0$
- It is equal to $\omega_0$

## Explanation

The damped angular frequency is $\omega' = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$, and $\gamma > 0$, hence $\omega' < \omega_0$. For weak damping the difference is small, although it is never zero, and it does not depend on the amplitude.
