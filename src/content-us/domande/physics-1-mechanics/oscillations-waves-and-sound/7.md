The position of a particle is $x(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$ with $A > 0$. At $t = 0$ the particle passes through $x = 0$ moving in the positive $x$ direction. What is $\varphi$?

- $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\varphi = 0$
- $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\varphi = \pi$

## Explanation

The condition $x(0) = A\cos\varphi = 0$ requires $\varphi = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The velocity at $t = 0$ is $v_0 = -\omega A\sin\varphi$, and it is positive only if $\sin\varphi < 0$, therefore $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ describes a particle crossing the origin in the negative direction.
