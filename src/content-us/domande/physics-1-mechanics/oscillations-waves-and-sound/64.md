A string is replaced by one with twice the linear mass density, under the same tension. How does the speed of transverse waves change?

- It doubles
- It is multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$
- It is divided by $\sqrt{2}$
- It is halved

## Explanation

The speed $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{F_T}{\mu}}$ is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}$, hence doubling $\mu$ divides it by $\sqrt{2}$. A heavier string has more inertia per unit length, therefore the waves travel more slowly.
