A pendulum has a period of $1.50\,\mathrm{s}$ on Earth. What is its period, for small oscillations, on a planet where $g$ is half of its value on Earth?

- $2.12\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.06\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.750\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.00\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The period is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$, hence halving $g$ multiplies it by $\sqrt{2}$:
$$
T' = 1.50\,\mathrm{s} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2.12\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $3.00\,\mathrm{s}$ omits the square root, and the values $1.06\,\mathrm{s}$ and $0.750\,\mathrm{s}$ treat the period as increasing with $g$.
