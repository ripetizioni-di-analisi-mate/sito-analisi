A body of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ on a spring of constant $50\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is subject to a resistive force $-b\dot{x}$ with $b = 12\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. How does it move after being displaced and released?

- It is underdamped and oscillates with decaying amplitude
- It is critically damped
- It is overdamped and returns to equilibrium without oscillating
- It oscillates with constant amplitude

## Explanation

The parameters are $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m} = 12\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = 10\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Since $\gamma > \omega_0$, the motion is overdamped, a sum of two decaying exponentials. Equivalently, $b = 12\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ exceeds the critical value $2m\omega_0 = 10\,\mathrm{kg/s}$.
