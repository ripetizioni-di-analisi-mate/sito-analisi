A horse pulls a cart, and the cart accelerates forward. How does the magnitude of the force of the horse on the cart compare with that of the force of the cart on the horse?

- The force of the horse on the cart is larger, otherwise the cart could not accelerate.
- They are equal.
- The force of the cart on the horse is larger.
- They are equal only while the cart moves with constant velocity.

## Explanation

The two forces form an **action-reaction pair**, so by **Newton's third law** they have the same magnitude at every instant, whatever the motion. The cart accelerates because its own equation contains only the forward pull of the horse, while the backward pull acts on the horse.
