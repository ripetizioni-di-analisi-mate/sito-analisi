A car of mass $1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ moving at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ stops in $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ with constant deceleration. What is the magnitude of the net force on the car during the braking?

- $4.8\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $24\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The magnitude of the acceleration is $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{5.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. By the **second law** $F = ma = 1200\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 4.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the weight of the car, which is balanced by the normal force of the road.
