A stone of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and a stone of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ fall freely, with the resistance of the air neglected. How do their accelerations compare?

- The heavier stone has four times the acceleration of the lighter one.
- Both stones have the acceleration $g$.
- The lighter stone has four times the acceleration of the heavier one.
- The heavier stone has twice the acceleration of the lighter one.

## Explanation

In free fall the weight is the only force, so the **second law** gives $m\vec{g} = m\vec{a}$, hence $\vec{a} = \vec{g}$. The heavier stone has a larger weight, but also a larger mass to accelerate, and the two effects cancel exactly.
