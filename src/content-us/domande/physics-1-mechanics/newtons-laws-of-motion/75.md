The equations of two blocks joined by an ideal string are added. Why does the tension disappear from the sum?

- The tension is zero while the system accelerates.
- The string pulls the two blocks with forces of equal magnitude and opposite sense along the string.
- The tension is always smaller than the external force.
- The string has negligible mass, so it exerts no force.

## Explanation

The ideal string pulls the two blocks with the same tension $T$, forward on one and backward on the other, so in the sum $+T$ and $-T$ cancel. What remains is $\sum F_{\text{ext}} = \left(\textstyle\sum_i m_i\right)a$, the equation of the **system treated as a single body**.
