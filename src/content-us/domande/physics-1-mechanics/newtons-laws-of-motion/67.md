A block of mass $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ on a frictionless horizontal floor is pulled by a force of $40\,\mathrm{N}$ directed at $60^\circ$ above the horizontal, and the block stays on the floor. What is its acceleration?

- $8.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.51\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Only the horizontal component of the pull accelerates the block, since the floor is frictionless and the vertical forces balance. Hence
$$
a = \frac{F\cos 60^\circ}{m} = \frac{40\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.500}{4.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
Using $\sin 60^\circ$ in place of $\cos 60^\circ$ gives $8.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
