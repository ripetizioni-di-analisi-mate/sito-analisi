A string that breaks above $150\,\mathrm{N}$ is used to lower a block of mass $18\,\mathrm{kg}$ vertically. What is the smallest magnitude of the downward acceleration that keeps the string intact?

- $8.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

With the $y$ axis downward the second law gives $mg - T = ma$, so $T = m(g - a)$. The condition $T \le 150\,\mathrm{N}$ requires
$$
a \ge g - \frac{150\,\mathrm{N}}{18\,\mathrm{kg}} = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 8.33\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The weight $177\,\mathrm{N}$ exceeds $150\,\mathrm{N}$, therefore the block cannot be lowered at constant speed.
