A person of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on a scale graduated in kilograms, calibrated with $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, inside an elevator that accelerates upward at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What does the scale read?

- $60.0\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $47.8\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $72.2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $62.0\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

By the law of **apparent weight** the scale is pressed with $N = m(g+a) = 60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 11.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 709\,\mathrm{N}$. A scale graduated in kilograms divides the force by $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, so it shows $\dfrac{709}{9.81}\,\mathrm{kg} = 72.2\,\mathrm{kg}$, although the mass is still $60\,\mathrm{kg}$.
