Why do the two forces of an action-reaction pair never cancel in the equation of a body?

- They have different magnitudes.
- They act at different instants.
- One of them is always a contact force and the other is not.
- They act on different bodies.

## Explanation

By the **third law** the two forces have equal magnitudes, appear together and are of the same kind. Each of them acts on a different body, so the equation of any single body contains only one member of the pair.
