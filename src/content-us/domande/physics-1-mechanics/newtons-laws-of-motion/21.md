A body of mass $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ lies on a frictionless horizontal table, with $x$ and $y$ axes in the plane of the table. The horizontal forces on it are $12\,\mathrm{N}$ along $+x$, $9.0\,\mathrm{N}$ along $-x$ and $6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ along $+y$. What is $a_x$?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The **second law in components** treats each axis on its own, so only the $x$ components enter: $\sum F_x = 12\,\mathrm{N} - 9.0\,\mathrm{N} = 3.0\,\mathrm{N}$. Hence $a_x = \dfrac{3.0\,\mathrm{N}}{3.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, while the force along $y$ contributes only to $a_y$.
