Two blocks of unequal masses lie on a frictionless horizontal table, joined by an ideal string, and a horizontal force $F$ along the string pulls the pair. How does the tension of the string change when $F$ is applied to the heavier block instead of the lighter one?

- It becomes larger.
- It stays the same, because the force and the total mass are the same.
- It equals $F$ in both cases.
- It becomes smaller.

## Explanation

The tension is $T = \dfrac{m_{\text{pulled}}}{m_1 + m_2}F$, where $m_{\text{pulled}}$ is the mass of the block that the string has to accelerate, the one opposite to the applied force. When $F$ acts on the heavier block, the string pulls the lighter one, so the tension is smaller. The acceleration is the same in both cases.
