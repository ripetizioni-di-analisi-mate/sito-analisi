A force $F$ gives a body $A$ the acceleration $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and a body $B$ the acceleration $12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What acceleration does the same force give the two bodies joined together?

- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $16\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Masses add when bodies are joined, and $m_A = \dfrac{F}{4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}$, $m_B = \dfrac{F}{12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}$. Hence
$$
a = \frac{F}{m_A + m_B} = \frac{1}{\dfrac{1}{4.0} + \dfrac{1}{12}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
$$
smaller than both separate accelerations.
