During a collision, a truck of mass $8000\,\mathrm{kg}$ exerts on a car of mass $1000\,\mathrm{kg}$ a force of magnitude $40\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the truck at that instant, if no other horizontal force acts on it?

- $40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.63\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

By the **third law** the car pushes the truck with a force of the same magnitude, $40\,\mathrm{kN}$. By the **second law** applied to the truck alone,
$$
a = \frac{40 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}}{8000\,\mathrm{kg}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
$$
while the car receives eight times that acceleration.
