When the second law is written in components, the axes are usually chosen with one axis along the acceleration. What is gained by this choice?

- The other axis has zero acceleration, so its equation gives an unknown force directly.
- The second law holds only along the direction of the acceleration.
- Forces perpendicular to the acceleration can then be left out.
- The mass disappears from the equation of the other axis, since the body does not move along it.

## Explanation

The second law holds along every axis. With one axis along the acceleration, the equation of the perpendicular axis has zero on its right-hand side, so it involves only forces and gives at once an unknown such as $N$ or $T$. The perpendicular forces are still written in that equation.
