Two skaters on frictionless ice, of masses $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $40\,\mathrm{kg}$, push each other apart. During the push the $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ skater has an acceleration of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the acceleration of the other skater?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The force on the first skater is $60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 120\,\mathrm{N}$, and by the **third law** the second skater receives a force of the same magnitude. Hence $a = \dfrac{120\,\mathrm{N}}{40\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, larger for the lighter skater.
