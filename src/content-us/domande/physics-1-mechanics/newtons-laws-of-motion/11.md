A bus brakes sharply, and a standing passenger who holds nothing lurches forward relative to the bus. How is this described in the inertial frame of the road?

- The passenger tends to keep the original velocity while the bus slows down.
- A forward force produced by the braking acts on the passenger.
- The passenger accelerates forward with respect to the road.
- The floor of the bus pushes the passenger forward.

## Explanation

In the frame of the road, which is inertial, no forward force acts on the passenger. By **Newton's first law** the passenger tends to keep the velocity the bus had, while the bus slows down, so the passenger moves forward relative to the bus. The apparent forward push appears only in the frame of the bus, which is not inertial.
