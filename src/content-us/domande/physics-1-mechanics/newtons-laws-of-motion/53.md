A block rests on a horizontal floor. In which case is the normal force on the block equal to its weight?

- When the floor has no vertical acceleration and no other vertical force acts on the block
- Always, by the third law
- Only when the floor is frictionless
- Whenever the block moves horizontally, whatever other forces act on it

## Explanation

The vertical equation is $N - mg + F_y = m a_y$, where $F_y$ collects the other vertical forces. Hence $N = mg$ exactly when $a_y = 0$ and $F_y = 0$. The normal force and the weight are not an action-reaction pair, so the **third law** does not make them equal.
