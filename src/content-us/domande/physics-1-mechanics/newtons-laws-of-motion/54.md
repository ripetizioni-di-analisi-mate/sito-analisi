A cart on a frictionless track is pulled by a string of negligible mass, and the cart accelerates. How do the tensions at the two ends of the string compare?

- The tension is larger at the end where the pull is applied.
- They are equal.
- They are equal only if the cart moves with constant velocity.
- The tension is larger at the end attached to the cart.

## Explanation

For any piece of string the second law gives $T_2 - T_1 = \Delta m \cdot a$. The mass of the string is negligible, hence $T_2 = T_1$ even when the string accelerates, so a **massless string** has the same tension at all of its points.
