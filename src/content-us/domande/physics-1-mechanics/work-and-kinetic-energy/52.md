How many joules are there in $2.0\,\mathrm{kW\cdot h}$?

- $7.2 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $7.2 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

One kilowatt hour is $10^3\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 3.6 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}$, therefore
$$
2.0\,\mathrm{kW\cdot h} = 2.0 \cdot 3.6 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J} = 7.2 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Converting the hour into $60\,\mathrm{s}$ instead of $3600\,\mathrm{s}$ gives $1.2 \times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$.
