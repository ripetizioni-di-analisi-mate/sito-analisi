A body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ speeds up from $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the total work done on it?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $34\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-16\,\mathrm{J}$
- $16\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

By the **work-energy theorem**
$$
W_{\text{tot}} = \frac{1}{2}m\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (25 - 9.0)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = 16\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Squaring the change of speed, $(2.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2$, gives the wrong value $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$.
