A block slides on a horizontal frictionless surface and runs into a spring fixed to a wall. If the speed of the block is doubled, how does the maximum compression of the spring change?

- It is multiplied by $4$.
- It is multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$.
- It is doubled.
- It does not change.

## Explanation

At the maximum compression the block is at rest, so by the **work-energy theorem** $-\dfrac{1}{2}k\,x^2 = -\dfrac{1}{2}m v^2$ and $x = v\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$. The compression is proportional to the speed, therefore doubling the speed doubles the compression.
