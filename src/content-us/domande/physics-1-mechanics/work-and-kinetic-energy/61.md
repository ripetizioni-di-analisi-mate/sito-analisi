A constant resultant force acts on a body initially at rest, in the direction of the motion. How does the power delivered by that force depend on the time $t$?

- It is proportional to $t$.
- It is constant.
- It is proportional to $t^2$.
- It decreases as $t$ increases.

## Explanation

The acceleration $a = \dfrac{F}{m}$ is constant, hence the speed starting from rest is $v = a\,t$. The force is parallel to the velocity, so the power is
$$
P = F\,v = \frac{F^2}{m}\,t ,
$$
which is proportional to $t$.
