A problem gives the initial speed of a body, the forces acting on it and the distance it travels, and it asks for the final speed. Why is the work-energy theorem convenient for this problem?

- It relates the speeds and the displacement without containing the time.
- It gives the acceleration at every instant of the motion.
- It holds only when the acceleration is constant, which simplifies the calculation.
- It removes the mass from every problem.

## Explanation

The equality $W_{\text{tot}} = \dfrac{1}{2}m v_2^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}m v_1^2$ contains the speeds and, through the work, the displacement, but not the time. Therefore one scalar equation gives the final speed, whereas Newton's second law requires first the acceleration and then a kinematic relation. The theorem holds also for variable forces.
