A passenger of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ walks towards the front of a train at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the train, which moves at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the track. What is the kinetic energy of the passenger in the frame of the track?

- $4.32 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.12 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.92 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.20 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In the frame of the track the speed of the passenger is $v = 10\,\mathrm{m/s} + 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (12\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 4.32 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The kinetic energy is not additive in the speeds, so adding $\dfrac{1}{2}m\,(10\,\mathrm{m/s})^2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}m\,(2.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2$ gives the wrong value $3.12 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $1.20 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the kinetic energy in the frame of the train.
