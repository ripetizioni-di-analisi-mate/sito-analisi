A constant force $\vec{F} = 4.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on a body that moves along a quarter circle of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ centered at the origin, from $(2.0,\ 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ to $(0,\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{m}$. How much work does the force do?

- $+8.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-8.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $12.6\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

For a constant force the work along any path equals the scalar product of the force with the displacement from the initial to the final point. The displacement is $(-2.0\,\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore
$$
W = 4.0\,\mathrm{N} \cdot (-2.0\,\mathrm{m}) = -8.0\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $12.6\,\mathrm{J}$ is the force times the length of the arc, which treats the force as tangent to the path.
