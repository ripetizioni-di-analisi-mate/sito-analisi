A bullet of mass $0.010\,\mathrm{kg}$ moving at $400\,\mathrm{m/s}$ enters a block of wood and stops after $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the magnitude of the resistive force of the wood, assumed constant?

- $1.6 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.0 \times 10^{1}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $8.0 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $8.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The initial kinetic energy is $K_1 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.010\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (400\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 = 800\,\mathrm{J}$, and the resistive force does $-F\,d$. By the **work-energy theorem** $-F\,d = -K_1$, hence
$$
F = \frac{K_1}{d} = \frac{800\,\mathrm{J}}{0.10\,\mathrm{m}} = 8.0 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
Losing the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the kinetic energy gives $1.6 \times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}$.
