Two identical blocks start from rest at the same height. One falls vertically, the other slides down a frictionless curved ramp, and both stop at the same lower height. How do the works done by the weight on the two blocks compare?

- They are equal.
- The work is larger on the block on the ramp, whose path is longer.
- The work is larger on the falling block, whose path is shorter.
- The work is zero on the block on the ramp, because the ramp holds it.

## Explanation

The work done by the weight is $W = -m g\,(y_2 - y_1)$, and it depends only on the initial and the final height. The two blocks have the same mass and undergo the same drop in height, therefore the weight does the same work on both. The normal force of the ramp is a separate force, and it does no work because it is perpendicular to the path.
