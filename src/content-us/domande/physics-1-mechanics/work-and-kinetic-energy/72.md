A car stops in $18\,\mathrm{m}$ from a speed of $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With the same constant braking force, in what distance does it stop from $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$?

- $40.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $27.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $60.8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By the **work-energy theorem** $-F\,d = -\dfrac{1}{2}m v^2$, so the stopping distance is proportional to the square of the initial speed. The speed is multiplied by $1.5$, therefore
$$
d = 18\,\mathrm{m} \cdot (1.5)^2 = 40.5\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Assuming a distance proportional to the speed gives $27.0\,\mathrm{m}$.
