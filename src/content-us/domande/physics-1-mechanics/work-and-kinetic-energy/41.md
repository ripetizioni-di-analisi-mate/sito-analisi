A child of mass $30\,\mathrm{kg}$ goes down a curved water slide $9.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long, whose end is $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ lower than its top. How much work does the weight do on the child?

- $2.65 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.18 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.20 \times 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.18 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the **work done by the weight**, the work depends only on the drop in height $h$, whatever the path. For a descent
$$
W = m g h = 30\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{m} = 1.18 \times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The length of the slide does not enter the calculation.
