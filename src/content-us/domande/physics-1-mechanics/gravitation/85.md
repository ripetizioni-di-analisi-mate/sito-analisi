How does the ratio $\dfrac{T^2}{a^3}$ for the moons of Jupiter compare with the same ratio for the planets around the Sun?

- It is the same, because the ratio is a universal constant
- It is smaller, because the mass of Jupiter is smaller than the mass of the Sun
- It depends on the mass of each moon
- It is larger, because the mass of Jupiter is smaller than the mass of the Sun

## Explanation

By **Kepler's third law** $\dfrac{T^2}{a^3} = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}$, where $M$ is the central mass. The ratio is inversely proportional to $M$, therefore the smaller mass of Jupiter gives a larger ratio. The mass of the orbiting body does not appear.
