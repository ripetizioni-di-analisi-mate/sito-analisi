Why do all geostationary satellites travel at the same altitude above the equator?

- They all have the same mass
- The gravitational field of the Earth is uniform at that altitude
- Their speed must equal the speed of the ground at the equator
- Their period is fixed at one sidereal day, and the period of a circular orbit determines its radius

## Explanation

A geostationary satellite must complete one revolution in one sidereal day. Since $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM_E}}$, a fixed period gives a single radius, $4.22 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}$, independently of the mass of the satellite. It is the angular speed that matches the rotation of the Earth, not the linear speed.
