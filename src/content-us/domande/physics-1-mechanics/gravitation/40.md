The measured value of $g$ at the surface of the Earth is about $9.78\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the equator and $9.83\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the poles. What causes the difference?

- The rotation of the Earth and its smaller polar radius
- The attraction of the Moon
- The change in the distance between the Earth and the Sun
- A different value of $G$ at different latitudes

## Explanation

A frame fixed to the ground rotates, so at the equator part of the attraction supplies the centripetal acceleration $\omega^2R_E$ and the reading of a scale is reduced. In addition, the polar radius is about $21\,\mathrm{km}$ smaller than the equatorial one, which increases the field at the poles. The constant $G$ is the same everywhere.
