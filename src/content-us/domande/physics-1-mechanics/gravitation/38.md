A straight tunnel is dug through the center of a planet that is a uniform sphere, and a body is dropped into it. Neglecting friction and the rotation of the planet, what is the gravitational force on the body at the distance $r$ from the center?

- Its magnitude is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$ and it points toward the center
- It is constant and equal to the weight at the surface
- Its magnitude is proportional to $r$ and it points toward the center
- It is zero at every point of the tunnel

## Explanation

Inside a uniform sphere the field is $g(r) = \dfrac{GM}{R^3}\,r$, directed toward the center. The force on the body is $m\,g(r)$, proportional to the displacement from the center and opposite to it, which is the force of simple harmonic motion.
