An astronaut on board a station in circular orbit at $400\,\mathrm{km}$ above the Earth stands on a scale, which reads zero. Why?

- The gravitational field is zero at that altitude
- The gravitational field at that altitude is negligible compared with the surface value
- The astronaut and the station have the same acceleration, so the floor exerts no normal force
- The station is outside the atmosphere

## Explanation

At $400\,\mathrm{km}$ the field is still $8.69\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, about $89\%$ of the surface value. The astronaut and the station follow the same orbit with the same acceleration, which does not depend on mass, therefore the floor does not need to push on the astronaut and the scale reads zero.
