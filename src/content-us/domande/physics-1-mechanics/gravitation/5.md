The gravitational constant is measured in $\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$. Which expression gives the same unit in base SI units?

- $\mathrm{m^3\,kg\,s^{-2}}$
- $\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-2}\,s^{-2}}$
- $\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$
- $\mathrm{m^2\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$

## Explanation

Since $1\,\mathrm{N} = 1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$, the unit of $G$ becomes
$$
\mathrm{\frac{kg\cdot m}{s^2}\cdot\frac{m^2}{kg^2}} = \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}} ,
$$
because one power of the kilogram in the denominator cancels the kilogram of the newton.
