The relation $T^2 = a^3$, with $T$ in years and $a$ in astronomical units, gives a wrong period when applied to the Moon. Why?

- The orbit of the Moon is not an ellipse
- The mass of the Moon is too small for Kepler's laws to apply
- Kepler's third law holds only for orbits far from circular
- The constant in Kepler's third law depends on the central mass, and the Moon orbits the Earth, not the Sun

## Explanation

Kepler's third law holds for bodies orbiting the *same* central mass, since $\dfrac{T^2}{a^3} = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}$. The form $T^2 = a^3$ fixes the constant with the Earth's orbit around the Sun, while for the Moon the central mass is the Earth, about $3.3 \times 10^{5}$ times smaller.
