A planet is a uniform sphere of radius $R$. At what distance from its center, inside the planet, is the gravitational field equal to the field at the altitude $h = R$ above its surface?

- $\dfrac{R}{2}$
- $\dfrac{R}{16}$
- $\dfrac{3R}{4}$
- $\dfrac{R}{4}$

## Explanation

At the altitude $h = R$ the distance from the center is $2R$, so the field is $\dfrac{g_0}{4}$. Inside the planet $g = g_0\,\dfrac{r}{R}$, which equals $\dfrac{g_0}{4}$ at $r = \dfrac{R}{4}$. The value $\dfrac{3R}{4}$ is the corresponding depth below the surface, not the distance from the center.
