A skydiver of mass $80\,\mathrm{kg}$ descends $50\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant speed. What work does air resistance do on the skydiver during that descent?

- $+39.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-39.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-4.00\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

The speed is constant, so $\Delta K = 0$ and the energy equation gives $W_{\text{nc}} = \Delta U = -mgh = -80\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 50\,\mathrm{m} = -39.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$. The potential energy lost becomes thermal energy of the skydiver and of the air.
