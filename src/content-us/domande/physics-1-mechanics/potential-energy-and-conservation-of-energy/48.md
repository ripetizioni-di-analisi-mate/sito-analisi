A bead slides along a frictionless wire. At the height $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ its speed is $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is its speed at the top of a bump of the wire, at the height $2.4\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $1.07\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Conservation of mechanical energy gives $v^2 = 9.0\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} - 2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{m} = 1.15\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, hence $v = 1.07\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The bead reaches the top because $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.46\,\mathrm{m}$ exceeds the rise of $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$.
