A block slides on a rough horizontal floor. Its speed drops from $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$. How much farther does it slide before stopping?

- $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Friction removes the same kinetic energy per meter, and the kinetic energy is proportional to $v^2$. Over $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$ the value of $v^2$ drops by $48\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, so the remaining $16\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ is removed in $6.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \dfrac{16}{48} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$.
