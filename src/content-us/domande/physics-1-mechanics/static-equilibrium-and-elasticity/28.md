In the equilibrium of a beam held by a pin and a cable, the tension of the cable comes out as $T = -80\,\mathrm{N}$. What does this result mean?

- The cable would have to push, so the assumed arrangement cannot be in equilibrium
- The tension is $80\,\mathrm{N}$ and the cable pulls in the sense opposite to the one drawn
- The tension is $80\,\mathrm{N}$ and the pin exerts no force
- The tension is $80\,\mathrm{N}$ and the cable pulls in the sense drawn

## Explanation

An ideal cable exerts a force along itself, directed away from the body, so its sense is fixed by the geometry. A negative tension would correspond to a force toward the body, that is a push, which a cable cannot exert. The arrangement is therefore impossible, and a strut, which can push, would be required in place of the cable.
