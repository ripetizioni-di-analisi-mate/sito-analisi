In an equilibrium problem the point about which the torques are computed can be chosen freely. What is the reason?

- The weight of the body acts at its center of gravity
- The net force vanishes, hence the net torque has the same value about every point
- The moment arm of each force is the same about every point
- The torque of a force does not depend on the point about which it is computed

## Explanation

The first equilibrium condition imposes $\sum \vec{F}_i = \vec{0}$. Under that hypothesis the total torque about a second point $O'$ equals the total torque about $O$, since the two differ by $\vec{d} \times \sum \vec{F}_i$. The torque of a single force and its moment arm do change with the point.
