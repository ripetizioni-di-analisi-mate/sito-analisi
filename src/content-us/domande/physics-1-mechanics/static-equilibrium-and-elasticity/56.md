The figure shows a uniform block on a rough incline, with the vertical line through its center of gravity $G$. Friction is large enough to prevent sliding. What happens to the block?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 5,0 5,3.501;1.638,1.147 2.294,1.606 1.146,3.244 0.491,2.785" punti="1.392,2.196,pieno,G" segmenti="1.392,2.196,1.392,0.975" etichette="1.05,0.25,35°" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5.5 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.9 titolo="A uniform block on a rough incline and the vertical line through its center of gravity $G$"}

- It tips over its lower edge
- It tips over its upper edge
- It stays at rest
- It slides down the incline

## Explanation

The resultant of the contact forces can act only at points of the base, and in equilibrium its line of action coincides with the line of action of the weight. The vertical line through $G$ meets the incline below the lower edge of the base, hence no admissible contact force balances the torque of the weight, and the block **tips** over its lower edge.
