The graph shows the stress $\sigma$ as a function of the strain $\varepsilon$ for a metal specimen under tension, with $\sigma$ in units of $10^{8}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $\varepsilon$ in units of $10^{-3}$. What is Young's modulus of the material?

::grafico{nomi="ε,σ" linee="0,0 2,1.4 2.5,1.55 3.2,1.63 4.5,1.68" punti="2,1.4,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,1.4;0,1.4,2,1.4" etichette="-0.35,1.4,1.4" xmin=-0.6 xmax=5 ymin=-0.3 ymax=2.2 titolo="Stress $\sigma$, in units of $10^{8}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, as a function of the strain $\varepsilon$, in units of $10^{-3}$"}

- $7.0 \times 10^{7}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $7.0 \times 10^{13}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $7.0 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.4 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Young's modulus is the slope of the straight part of the graph, $Y = \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}$. At the end of that part $\sigma = 1.4 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $\varepsilon = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}$, hence $Y = \dfrac{1.4 \times 10^{8}}{2.0 \times 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 7.0 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The curved part beyond that point lies above the proportional limit.
