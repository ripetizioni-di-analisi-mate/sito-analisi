A free-body diagram of an extended body carries a single weight arrow $M\vec{g}$ drawn at the center of gravity. What justifies replacing the weights of all the particles with that one force?

- In a uniform field, the total torque of the weights about any point equals the torque of $M\vec{g}$ applied at $G$
- The particles of a rigid body all have the same mass
- The center of gravity is the geometric center of every body
- The internal forces between the particles cancel in pairs

## Explanation

The sum of the weights is $M\vec{g}$ in any case, so the first condition is unaffected. For the second condition the theorem on the **torque of the weight** gives $\sum \vec{r}_i \times m_i\vec{g} = \vec{r}_G \times M\vec{g}$, because $\vec{g}$ is the same for every particle. The single arrow at $G$ therefore produces the same force and the same torque as all the weights together.
