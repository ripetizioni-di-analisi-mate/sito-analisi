A steel wire of length $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and cross section $1.0\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ carries a tension of $200\,\mathrm{N}$, with $Y = 2.0 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. What is its elongation?

- $0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.0020\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $20\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

With $A = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the elongation is $\Delta L = \dfrac{F L_0}{A Y} = \dfrac{200 \cdot 2.0}{1.0 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 2.0 \times 10^{11}}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$, that is $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The initial length multiplies the force in the numerator, since a longer wire stretches more.
