Can the center of gravity of a body lie at a point where there is no material of the body?

- Only if the body is not homogeneous
- Yes, as for a uniform ring, whose center of gravity is at its empty center
- Only if forces other than the weight act on the body
- No, since the weight is applied only where there is material

## Explanation

The center of gravity is a point of space defined by the weighted average $\dfrac{\sum m_i \vec{r}_i}{\sum m_i}$, and nothing requires it to belong to the body. For a uniform ring it lies on every axis of symmetry, hence at the center, where there is no material. The same happens for an L-shaped plate, whose center of gravity falls in the notch.
