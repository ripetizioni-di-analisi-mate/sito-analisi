A steel wire is loaded beyond its proportional limit, and its elongation is computed with $\Delta L = \dfrac{F L_0}{A Y}$. How does the computed value compare with the real elongation?

- It equals the real elongation
- It is smaller than the real elongation
- It equals the permanent elongation that remains after unloading
- It is larger than the real elongation

## Explanation

Beyond the proportional limit the wire keeps elongating while the stress stays nearly constant, so the real strain is much larger than $\dfrac{\sigma}{Y}$. The formula assumes proportionality, therefore it underestimates the elongation in that range.
