A homogeneous disk of radius $R$ has a circular hole of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ whose center lies at the distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center of the disk. Where is the center of gravity of the drilled disk?

- At $\dfrac{R}{6}$ from the center, on the side of the hole
- At $\dfrac{R}{8}$ from the center, on the side opposite to the hole
- At $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center, on the side opposite to the hole
- At $\dfrac{R}{6}$ from the center, on the side opposite to the hole

## Explanation

The drilled disk is the full disk, of area $\pi R^2$ centered at the origin, together with a part of negative area $-\dfrac{\pi R^2}{4}$ centered at $x = \dfrac{R}{2}$. The remaining area is $\dfrac{3\pi R^2}{4}$, hence $x_G = -\dfrac{\pi R^2}{4} \cdot \dfrac{R}{2} \cdot \dfrac{4}{3\pi R^2} = -\dfrac{R}{6}$. The minus sign places $G$ on the side opposite to the hole.
