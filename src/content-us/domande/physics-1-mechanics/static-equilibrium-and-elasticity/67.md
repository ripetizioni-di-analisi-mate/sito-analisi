For what range of stresses does Hooke's law $\sigma = Y\varepsilon$ hold for a solid under tension?

- For every stress below the tensile strength
- Only for compressive stresses
- For every stress, since the law is exact
- Up to the proportional limit of the material

## Explanation

Hooke's law for solids is an experimental law, valid for small deformations up to a stress characteristic of the material, the proportional limit. Beyond it the strain grows faster than the stress, and the tensile strength, reached later, is the stress at which the specimen breaks.
