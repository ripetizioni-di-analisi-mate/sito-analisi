A uniform horizontal beam is pinned to a wall at its left end and held by an inclined cable fastened to its right end. The force exerted by the pin is not directed along the beam. What is the reason?

- A pin can exert only vertical forces
- The pin exerts a torque on the beam
- The weight of the beam is a third force, so the beam is not acted on by two forces only
- The cable pushes the beam toward the wall

## Explanation

The theorem on the **body acted on by two forces** puts both forces along the line joining their points of application, but it requires exactly two forces. The beam carries its weight at the center in addition to the tension and the pin reaction, so the pin reaction has the vertical component $\dfrac{Mg}{2}$ besides the horizontal one.
