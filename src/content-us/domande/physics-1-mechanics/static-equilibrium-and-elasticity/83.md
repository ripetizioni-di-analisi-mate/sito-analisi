Liquid $A$ has twice the compressibility of liquid $B$, and both are subjected to the same pressure increase. How does the relative volume change of $A$ compare with that of $B$?

- Half as large
- Four times as large
- The same
- Twice as large

## Explanation

The compressibility is $\kappa = \dfrac{1}{B}$, so $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V_0} = -\kappa\,\Delta p$. With the same $\Delta p$ the relative volume change is proportional to $\kappa$, and liquid $A$ changes its volume twice as much.
