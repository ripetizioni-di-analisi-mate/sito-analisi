A uniform plank of mass $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on two supports, one at its left end and one $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from that end. A person of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ walks on the overhanging part, beyond the second support. How far beyond the second support can the person go before the plank starts to tip?

- $0.67\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.33\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At the limit the left support no longer presses on the plank, $N_1 = 0$, and the torques about the second support involve only the two weights. The weight of the plank acts at $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the left end, that is $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ on the other side of the support, hence $20\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{m} = 60\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot x$ and $x = 0.33\,\mathrm{m}$. Beyond that point the torque of the person exceeds the torque of the plank.
