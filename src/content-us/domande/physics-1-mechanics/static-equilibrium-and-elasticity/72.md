A steel wire, with $Y = 2.0 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, and an aluminum wire, with $Y = 7.0 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{Pa}$, have the same length and cross section and carry the same tension. How does the elongation of the aluminum wire compare with that of the steel wire?

- About $2.9$ times larger
- The same
- About $1.7$ times larger
- About $2.9$ times smaller

## Explanation

With the same $F$, $L_0$ and $A$, the elongation $\Delta L = \dfrac{F L_0}{A Y}$ is inversely proportional to Young's modulus. The ratio is $\dfrac{2.0 \times 10^{11}}{7.0 \times 10^{10}} = 2.9$, and the aluminum wire, with the smaller modulus, stretches more.
