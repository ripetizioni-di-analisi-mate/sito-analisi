A uniform ladder leans against a smooth wall. On which quantities does the smallest coefficient of static friction at the floor that keeps it in place depend?

- Only on the angle between the ladder and the floor
- On the mass of the ladder and the angle
- On the length of the ladder and the angle
- On the mass and the length of the ladder and the angle

## Explanation

The equilibrium equations give $f = \dfrac{M g}{2 \tan\theta}$ and $N_2 = M g$, so the ratio $\dfrac{f}{N_2} = \dfrac{1}{2 \tan\theta}$ contains neither the mass nor the length. A heavier ladder needs a larger friction force and receives a proportionally larger normal force, while the length cancels from the torque equation.
