Two wires of the same material and the same cross section carry the same tension, and one is twice as long as the other. How do their strains and elongations compare?

- The longer wire has twice the strain and the same elongation
- Equal strains, and the longer wire elongates twice as much
- The longer wire has twice the strain and four times the elongation
- Equal elongations, and the longer wire has half the strain

## Explanation

The stress $\dfrac{F}{A}$ is the same in the two wires, and Hooke's law gives the strain $\dfrac{\sigma}{Y}$, which is therefore the same. The elongation is $\Delta L = \varepsilon L_0$, so it is proportional to the initial length, and the longer wire stretches twice as much.
