A uniform horizontal beam is pinned to a wall at one end and held by a cable that makes the angle $\theta$ with the beam. The cable is first fastened at the free end of the beam, then at its midpoint, with the same angle $\theta$. How does the tension change?

- It becomes four times larger
- It halves
- It doubles
- It stays the same

## Explanation

Torques about the pin give $T\,b\sin\theta = M g\,\dfrac{L}{2}$, where $b$ is the distance of the fastening point from the pin. Moving the fastening point from $b = L$ to $b = \dfrac{L}{2}$ halves the moment arm of the tension, therefore the tension doubles.
