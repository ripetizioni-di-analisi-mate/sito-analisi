## Exercise 1

Compute $e^{xA}$ for $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$.

$$
Y^{(1)}(x)=\frac12 e^{3x}(1,1)+\frac12 e^x(1,-1).
$$

$$
Y^{(2)}(x)=\frac12 e^{3x}(1,1)-\frac12 e^x(1,-1).
$$

$$
e^{xA}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}e^{3x}+e^x&e^{3x}-e^x\\e^{3x}-e^x&e^{3x}+e^x\end{pmatrix}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **columns of the matrix exponential** are the solutions with initial values $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Express each initial vector in the eigenvector basis $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$. Convert the solutions back to standard coordinates and place them side by side.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute $e^{xA}$ for $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}$.

$$
A=2I+N,\qquad N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad N^2=0.
$$

$$
e^{xA}=e^{2x}e^{xN}=e^{2x}(I+xN).
$$

$$
e^{xA}=e^{2x}\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrix $N=A-2I$ is **nilpotent** of index two because $N^2=0$ and $N\ne 0$. In the series for $e^{xN}$, only $I$ and $xN$ remain. The factor $e^{2x}$ comes from the scalar part $2I$, which commutes with $N$.

</div>
