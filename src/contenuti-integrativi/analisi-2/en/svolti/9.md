## Exercise 1

Solve the initial value problem for $Y'=AY$, $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$, $Y(0)=(a,b)$.

$$
Av_+=3v_+,\quad Av_-=v_-,\quad v_\pm=(1,\pm1).
$$

$$
Y(0)=\frac{a+b}{2}v_++\frac{a-b}{2}v_-.
$$

$$
Y(x)=\frac{a+b}{2}e^{3x}(1,1)+\frac{a-b}{2}e^x(1,-1).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply $A$ by each **eigenvector** to find its eigenvalue. The sum and difference of the initial coordinates give the coefficients in the new basis. Multiply each coefficient by the exponential of its eigenvalue.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the initial value problem for $Y'=AY$, $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-2\end{pmatrix}$, $Y(1)=(2,3)$.

$$
y_1'=y_1,\qquad y_2'=-2y_2.
$$

$$
y_1(x)=2e^{x-1},\qquad y_2(x)=3e^{-2(x-1)}.
$$

$$
Y(x)=(2e^{x-1},3e^{-2(x-1)}).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $A$ is diagonal, the equations are independent. The initial value is prescribed at $x=1$, so the exponent uses $x-1$. Substituting $x=1$ gives $(2,3)$.

</div>
