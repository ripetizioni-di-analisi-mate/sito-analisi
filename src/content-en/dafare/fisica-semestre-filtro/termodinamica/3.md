parole: ["Specific heat from an experiment"]

A $0.200\,\mathrm{kg}$ metal sample at $100\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is immersed in $0.300\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The calorimeter, of heat capacity $60\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, also starts at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Final temperature is $25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Assume an adiabatic system, no phase changes, constant capacities and $c_a=4200\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$. Infer the metal's specific heat and state whether neglecting the calorimeter would overestimate or underestimate it.

:::soluzione
Water and calorimeter together absorb
$$
Q=(0.300\cdot4200+60)(25-20)=6600\,\mathrm J.
$$
This equals the magnitude released by the metal. Thus
$$
c_m=\dfrac{6600}{0.200(100-25)}=440\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}.
$$
Omitting the calorimeter would assign only $6300\,\mathrm J$ of released heat to the metal and give $420\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$, an underestimate. This inverse reasoning uses measured final temperature to reconstruct an unknown capacity; it does not assume all solids have the same specific heat. Substituting the result into the signed balance checks that all three contributions sum to zero.
:::
