---
titolo: "When ice remains in the vessel"
teoria: [calore-latente, calorimetria]
parole: ["When ice remains in the vessel"]
---

Mix $0.100\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with $0.0500\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ in an adiabatic vessel of negligible heat capacity. At atmospheric pressure, use $c_a=4200\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$ and $\ell_f=336000\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$. There is no evaporation. Find final temperature and final phase masses, justifying the balance chosen.

:::soluzione
Water can release at most $Q_{\max}=0.100\cdot4200\cdot20=8400\,\mathrm J$ before reaching zero degrees. Melting all the ice would require $16800\,\mathrm J$, so available heat is insufficient. Both phases remain at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$.
$$
m_{\mathrm{melt}}=\dfrac{8400}{336000}=0.0250\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
There is $0.0250\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice left and $0.125\,\mathrm{kg}$ of liquid water. Total mass remains $0.150\,\mathrm{kg}$. Meltwater does not need warming above zero because heat released by the initially warm portion is entirely used in partial melting. Assuming entirely liquid water from the outset would produce a negative final temperature, signalling an inconsistent phase assumption.
:::
