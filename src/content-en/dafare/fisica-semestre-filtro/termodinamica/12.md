---
titolo: "Designing an efficiency limit"
teoria: [secondo-principio]
parole: ["Designing an efficiency limit"]
---

A cyclic engine operates between a cold reservoir at $300\,\mathrm K$ and an adjustable hot reservoir. The desired reversible efficiency limit is at least $40\%$. What is the lowest compatible hot temperature? At that temperature, if absorbed thermal power is $10.0\,\mathrm{kW}$, find maximum mechanical power and rejected heat per second in the reversible limit. Must a real machine provide that power?

:::soluzione
The limit is $\eta_C=1-\dfrac{T_c}{T_h}$. Requiring $\eta_C\ge0.40$ gives
$$
T_h\ge\dfrac{300}{1-0.40}=500\,\mathrm K.
$$
At the threshold, $P_{\max}=0.40\cdot10.0=4.00\,\mathrm{kW}$ and rejected thermal power is $6.00\,\mathrm{kW}$. A real device can have lower efficiency and therefore less power for the same absorbed power. The result sets a limit, not guaranteed performance or operating speed. Temperatures must be absolute: subtracting the Celsius offset before forming their ratio would incorrectly alter the physical limit.
:::
