A closed ideal gas goes from $A$ ($V_A=1.00\,\mathrm L$, $p_A=100\,\mathrm{kPa}$) to $B$ ($V_B=3.00\,\mathrm L$, $p_B=300\,\mathrm{kPa}$). Along path I, pressure first increases at constant volume, then volume increases at $300\,\mathrm{kPa}$; along path II, volume first increases at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$, then pressure increases at constant volume. Work-producing stages are mechanically reversible and only volume work occurs. Given $U_B-U_A=1200\,\mathrm J$, find $W$ and $Q$ for both paths and compare with the graph.

![Two orthogonal paths in the pressure-volume plane from A to B](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/termodinamica/lavoro-en.svg)

:::soluzione
Vertical segments contribute no work. Since $1\,\mathrm{kPa\,L}=1\,\mathrm J$,
$$
W_I=300(3-1)=600\,\mathrm J,\qquad W_{II}=100(3-1)=200\,\mathrm J.
$$
Taking work done as positive, $Q=\Delta U+W$ gives $Q_I=1800\,\mathrm J$ and $Q_{II}=1400\,\mathrm J$. The $400\,\mathrm J$ work difference equals the rectangle's area between paths. Internal energy changes equally because endpoints coincide. The high-pressure path needs more incoming heat to compensate for more outgoing work; heat cannot be assigned a change determined only by states.
:::
