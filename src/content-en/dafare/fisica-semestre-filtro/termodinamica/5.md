---
titolo: "Heat capacity increasing with T"
teoria: [calorimetria]
parole: ["Heat capacity increasing with T"]
---

A body with no phase changes has heat capacity $C(T)=100\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}+(2\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-2}})(T-300\,\mathrm K)$. It starts at $300\,\mathrm K$ and absorbs $2400\,\mathrm J$ along the process for which $C(T)$ is specified, without other energy inputs. Find final temperature and compare it with an estimate using constant $C=100\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$.

:::soluzione
Let $x=T_f-300$, in kelvin. Heat is the integral of capacity:
$$
2400=\int_0^x(100+2u)\,du=100x+x^2.
$$
The equation has roots $x=20$ and $x=-120$. Heating requires $x>0$, so $T_f=320\,\mathrm K$. The negative root would also leave the model's positive-capacity range.

The constant estimate gives $x=24\,\mathrm K$ and $T_f=324\,\mathrm K$, overestimating temperature. Increasing capacity requires more energy for each successive kelvin. Exact average capacity over this interval is $120\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, the mean of $100$ and $140$ because the law is linear; multiplying by $20\,\mathrm K$ returns $2400\,\mathrm J$.
:::
