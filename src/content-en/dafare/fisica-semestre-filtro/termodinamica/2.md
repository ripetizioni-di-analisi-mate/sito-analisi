---
titolo: "A leak detected by a pressure gauge"
teoria: [gas-ideale]
parole: ["A leak detected by a pressure gauge"]
---

A rigid vessel contains a single ideal gas. Before and after a leak, the gas is equilibrated at the same temperature. A gauge initially reads $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and later $140\,\mathrm{kPa}$; atmospheric pressure remains $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Find the fraction of moles lost. Explain why applying the ratio directly to gauge readings gives a different result.

:::soluzione
The equation of state requires absolute pressures $p_i=300\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_f=240\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Since $V$ and $T$ match in both states,
$$
\dfrac{n_f}{n_i}=\dfrac{p_f}{p_i}=\dfrac{240}{300}=0.80,
\qquad \dfrac{n_i-n_f}{n_i}=0.20=20\%.
$$
The ratio of gauge readings would be $0.70$, incorrectly suggesting a $30\%$ loss. The additive atmospheric pressure does not cancel in a ratio. Equal final temperature is essential: if gas were still cooled by escape, part of the pressure fall could be thermal. Here measurements concern equilibrium states at equal temperature, so the absolute pressure fall measures the amount change alone.
:::
