---
titolo: "The disturbance caused by a thermometer"
teoria: [stato-termico, calorimetria]
parole: ["The disturbance caused by a thermometer"]
---

A sample has constant heat capacity $C_s=400\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$ and unknown initial temperature. A thermometer of heat capacity $C_t=20\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, initially at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, contacts the sample in an adiabatic system with no work or phase changes. The final reading is $38\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Find the sample's initial temperature in Celsius and kelvin, and quantify the thermometer's disturbance relative to that initial value.

:::soluzione
The thermometer absorbs $Q_t=C_t(38-20)=360\,\mathrm J$, which must come from the sample. The balance is
$$
400(38-t_i)+20(38-20)=0,
\qquad t_i=38+\dfrac{360}{400}=38.9\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$
Initial absolute temperature is $T_i=312.05\,\mathrm K$. The reading is $0.9\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, or $0.9\,\mathrm K$, below the initial value. No $273.15$ is added to this interval because the offset cancels in differences. The sample was warmer than the thermometer and must have cooled, so the correction's sign is consistent. Final equilibrium guarantees a common temperature, not that the device preserved the initial one.
:::
