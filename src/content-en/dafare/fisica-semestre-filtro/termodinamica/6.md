---
titolo: "Conductivity from an internal temperature"
teoria: [trasmissione-calore]
parole: ["Conductivity from an internal temperature"]
---

Two plane layers in series, each of area $A=0.50\,\mathrm{m^2}$, have outer faces at $50$ and $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The hot-side layer has $L_1=0.020\,\mathrm m$ and $\kappa_1=0.40\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$; the cold-side layer has $L_2=0.030\,\mathrm m$ and unknown conductivity. At steady state, interface temperature is measured as $40\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. With perfect contact and insulated sides, infer transmitted power and $\kappa_2$.

:::soluzione
The first layer directly gives
$$
P=\dfrac{\kappa_1A(50-40)}{L_1}
=\dfrac{0.40\cdot0.50\cdot10}{0.020}=100\,\mathrm W.
$$
The same power crosses the second layer:
$$
\kappa_2=\dfrac{PL_2}{A(40-20)}
=\dfrac{100\cdot0.030}{0.50\cdot20}
=0.30\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}.
$$
Resistances are $0.10$ and $0.20\,\mathrm{K\,W^{-1}}$; their sum multiplied by $100\,\mathrm W$ reproduces the overall $30\,\mathrm K$ difference. Equal power does not mean equal temperature drop. An unknown contact resistance would prevent the same inference from a single interface measurement.
:::
