A particle has $x_0=-1\,\mathrm{m}$ and velocity $v_x(t)=6\,\mathrm{m/s}-(2\,\mathrm{m/s^2})t$ between $0$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$. Sketch the velocity–time graph and find final position, distance travelled and average speed. Do not treat the whole interval as motion in one direction.

:::soluzione
$$
v_x=0\Rightarrow t=3\,\mathrm{s},\qquad v_x(5\,\mathrm{s})=-4\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
$$
A_+=\dfrac12\cdot3\cdot6\,\mathrm{m}=9\,\mathrm{m},\qquad
A_-=-\dfrac12\cdot2\cdot4\,\mathrm{m}=-4\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
$$
x_f=x_0+A_++A_-=4\,\mathrm{m},\qquad
s=9\,\mathrm{m}+4\,\mathrm{m}=13\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
$$
\dfrac{s}{5\,\mathrm{s}}=2.6\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

The graph is a straight line with negative slope, from $6$ to $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The part above the axis contributes positively to displacement, the part below negatively. Maximum position is $x(3\,\mathrm{s})=8\,\mathrm{m}$; the particle then moves back $4\,\mathrm{m}$. Distance adds absolute areas, whereas position also requires $x_0$. Algebraic average velocity would be $\dfrac55=1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, different from the requested average speed.
:::
