---
titolo: "Inferring mass from an impulse"
teoria: ["quantita-di-moto"]
parole: ["momentum"]
---

The horizontal resultant on a body, over $0.10\,\mathrm{s}$, rises linearly from zero to $100\,\mathrm{N}$ in the first $0.05\,\mathrm{s}$, then decreases linearly to zero. Velocity changes from $-2.0$ to $+8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find impulse, average force and mass. Explain why peak force cannot be used over the entire interval.

:::soluzione
$$
J=\dfrac12(0.10\,\mathrm{s})(100\,\mathrm{N})=5\,\mathrm{N\,s},\qquad
\overline F=\dfrac{J}{\Delta t}=50\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
$$
\Delta v=10\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad m=\dfrac{J}{\Delta v}=0.50\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

The force–time graph forms a triangle: its area is impulse. A rectangle as high as the peak would have twice the area and represent a different force. Velocity change retains signs and equals $8-(-2)=10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Impulse divided by velocity change has units $\mathrm{kg}$ and determines mass without needing displacement during the pulse. Average force is half the maximum for this triangular profile; this is not a universal relation for every collision.
:::
