titolo: "Spring compression with friction"
teoria: ["energia-meccanica", "forze"]
parole: ["mechanical energy", "forces"]
---

A $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block contacts an uncompressed ideal horizontal spring at $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Throughout compression it slides on a stationary surface with $\mu_d=0.20$; the spring has $k=100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and remains linear. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the first maximum compression and energy dissipated up to that instant.

:::soluzione
$$
\dfrac12mv_0^2=\dfrac12kx^2+\mu_dmgx
\Rightarrow 8=50x^2+2x\quad\text{(SI)}.
$$
$$
x=\dfrac{\sqrt{401}-1}{50}\,\mathrm{m}\approx0.3805\,\mathrm{m},\qquad
E_d=(2\,\mathrm{N})x\approx0.761\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

At the first maximum compression instantaneous velocity is zero, so initial kinetic energy splits into elastic energy and dissipation. Friction acts over compression distance $x$, not a separately specified distance. The negative root does not describe compression and is rejected. The result is below the frictionless value $v_0\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}=0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, as expected. Approximately $7.239\,\mathrm{J}$ elastic energy plus $0.761\,\mathrm{J}$ dissipated gives $8\,\mathrm{J}$. An instantaneous turning point is not necessarily static equilibrium.
:::
