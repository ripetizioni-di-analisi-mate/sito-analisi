---
titolo: "Natural length from two measurements"
teoria: ["forze", "energia-meccanica"]
parole: ["forces", "mechanical energy"]

An ideal spring in the linear regime has length $22\,\mathrm{cm}$ under tensile force $4.0\,\mathrm{N}$ and $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ under $10\,\mathrm{N}$. Spring mass is negligible. Find spring constant, natural length and elastic energy at the second length, choosing zero energy at natural length.

:::soluzione
$$
F=k(L-L_0),\qquad
k=\dfrac{10-4}{0.25-0.22}\,\mathrm{N/m}=200\,\mathrm{N/m}.
$$
$$
L_0=L_1-\dfrac{F_1}{k}=0.20\,\mathrm{m},\qquad
U_2=\dfrac12k(0.25-0.20)^2\,\mathrm{m^2}=0.25\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

Hooke's law uses extension $L-L_0$, not total length $L$. Subtracting the two equations eliminates unknown $L_0$ and determines $k$ from the force–length slope. Then obtain $L_0$ from one measurement and check with the other: $200(0.25-0.20)=10$ in newtons. Energy uses extension $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$ squared; inserting $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ as extension would give a much larger result inconsistent with the chosen zero.
:::
