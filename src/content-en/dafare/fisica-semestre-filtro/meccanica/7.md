---
titolo: "A scale in a descending lift"
teoria: ["newton", "moti-rettilinei"]
parole: ["Newton laws", "straight-line motion"]
---

A $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ person stands still relative to a lift floor. The lift initially descends at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. For the next second the scale under their feet constantly reads $600\,\mathrm{N}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find acceleration, final velocity and displacement. Is the lift speeding up or slowing down?

:::soluzione
$$
N-mg=ma_y\Rightarrow a_y=\dfrac{600-500}{50}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
$$
v_{yf}=-3+2\cdot1=-1\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad
\Delta y=-3\cdot1+\dfrac12\cdot2\cdot1^2=-2\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

Use the ground frame, assumed inertial, with upwards positive. The scale measures normal force, not directly $mg$: a reading above weight indicates upward acceleration. Initial velocity is downward, however, so the lift slows down while continuing to descend during this second. Its speed falls from $3$ to $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A reading above weight alone would not determine velocity direction: that direction is given here by the initial condition.
:::
