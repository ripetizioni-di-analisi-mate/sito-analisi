A person walks $3\,\mathrm{m}$ east, then $4\,\mathrm{m}$ north and finally $3\,\mathrm{m}$ west. The entire trip lasts $20\,\mathrm{s}$. With east–north axes, find displacement, distance, average velocity and average speed. Explain why the last two results differ.

:::soluzione
$$
\Delta\vec r=(0,4)\,\mathrm{m},\qquad s=3+4+3=10\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
$$
\vec v_{\mathrm m}=(0;0.20)\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad
\dfrac{s}{\Delta t}=0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

Horizontal components cancel in displacement because the two sections have opposite directions; both lengths add to distance. Average velocity points north and has magnitude $0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, whereas average speed is $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Individual section times are unnecessary: total duration and endpoints determine average velocity, while total duration and path length determine average speed. The check $|\Delta\vec r|\le s$ is satisfied; equality would require a straight path without reversals.
:::
