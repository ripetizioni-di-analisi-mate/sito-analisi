A neutral conductor is near a positive rod without contact. In protocol A it is grounded, disconnected from ground, and then the rod is removed. In protocol B, after grounding, the rod is removed while ground remains connected; equilibrium is reached before disconnection. Assume no other sources. Compare residual charges and identify when the conductor can exchange electrons with its surroundings.

:::soluzione
The positive rod favours electron entry from ground. In protocol A, disconnecting ground first retains the electron excess on the conductor: removing the rod changes its distribution but leaves it negative. In protocol B, excess electrons can return to ground during removal; without other sources, the conductor reaches neutrality before isolation. Rod contact is unnecessary. Charge conservation applies to the complete system: the individual conductor is not isolated while grounded. Without geometry data, the magnitude of the negative charge in protocol A cannot be calculated.
:::
