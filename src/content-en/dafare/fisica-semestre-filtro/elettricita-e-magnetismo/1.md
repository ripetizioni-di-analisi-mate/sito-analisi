Two identical small conducting spheres, far from other sources, carry $+6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $-2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. They are connected in a symmetric arrangement, then disconnected. They are placed $0.10\,\mathrm m$ apart between centres, much farther than their radii. Determine final charges, the number of transferred electrons, and their mutual force. Use $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $k_e=9.0\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

:::soluzione
Total charge is $+4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. Identical spheres, symmetry and equal potential give $q_f=+2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ on each. The initially positive sphere gains electrons with charge magnitude $4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, so $N=\dfrac{4.0\cdot10^{-9}}{1.60\cdot10^{-19}}=2.5\cdot10^{10}$. The electrons come from the other sphere, which also becomes positive. After separation, $F=\dfrac{k_eq_f^2}{r^2}=3.6\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$, repulsive. Use final charges rather than pre-contact charges; the stated separation justifies the point-charge approximation.
:::
