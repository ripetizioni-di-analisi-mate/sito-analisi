A horizontal wire segment $0.20\,\mathrm m$ long carries $5.0\,\mathrm A$ rightwards in a uniform inward field of $0.30\,\mathrm T$. It belongs to a powered circuit maintaining current. Find the force and its mechanical power when the segment translates upward at $0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Explain why a nonzero result does not contradict zero magnetic work on an individual charge.

:::soluzione
Wire and field are perpendicular: $F=I\ell B=0.30\,\mathrm N$, upward by the right-hand rule for conventional current. The segment’s mechanical velocity follows the force, so $P_{\mathrm{mecc}}=Fv=0.060\,\mathrm W$. The wire is not one free particle: carriers move relative to and interact with the material, while a source sustains current. The balance includes electric fields, the source and transfers to the lattice. For each charge, $q(\vec v\times\vec B)\cdot\vec v=0$ still holds using its total velocity; the segment velocity alone cannot replace it while ignoring the rest of the circuit.
:::
