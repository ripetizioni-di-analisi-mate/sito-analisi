A parallel-plate capacitor has plate area $A=50\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and separation $d=0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$. A linear dielectric of $\varepsilon_r=2.0$ completely fills the gap. At $100\,\mathrm V$, find capacitance, charge magnitude on one plate, mean field and energy. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F/m}$ and neglect fringing.

:::soluzione
Conversions are $A=5.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $d=5.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m$. Thus $C=\dfrac{\varepsilon_r\varepsilon_0A}{d}=1.77\cdot10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$, about $180\,\mathrm{pF}$. Keeping guard digits, $q=C\Delta V=1.77\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm C$, $E=\dfrac{\Delta V}{d}=2.0\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and $U_C=\dfrac{C(\Delta V)^2}{2}=8.85\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$, about $0.89\,\mu\mathrm J$. The area prefix must be squared: one square centimetre is $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. At fixed voltage, field depends on separation; the dielectric increases the charge required to maintain that voltage.
:::
