---
titolo: "Energy with additional work"
teoria: [potenziale]
parole: ["energy with additional work"]
---

A charge $q=-3.0\,\mu\mathrm C$ moves from $100\,\mathrm V$ to $160\,\mathrm V$ in an electrostatic field. It starts with $K_i=60\,\mu\mathrm J$, and a nonelectric force does work $W_{\mathrm{ext}}=-40\,\mu\mathrm J$ along the path. Determine potential-energy change, electric work and final kinetic energy. Explain why conservation of $K+U$ cannot simply be imposed.

:::soluzione
The difference is $\Delta V=60\,\mathrm V$, so $\Delta U=q\Delta V=-180\,\mu\mathrm J$ and $W_e=+180\,\mu\mathrm J$. The work–kinetic-energy theorem gives $K_f=K_i+W_e+W_{\mathrm{ext}}=60+180-40=200\,\mu\mathrm J$. Charge sign is essential: higher potential here means lower potential energy. The correct balance is $\Delta(K+U)=W_{\mathrm{ext}}$, so mechanical energy decreases by $40\,\mu\mathrm J$. Positive $K_f$ is compatible with reaching the endpoint, without by itself describing every trajectory detail.
:::
