A resistive heater draws a constant $2.0\,\mathrm A$ at $50\,\mathrm V$. Of its electrical energy, $70\%$ reaches $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water with specific heat $4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, without phase change. How long does a $10\,\mathrm K$ water-temperature increase take? Also calculate heater resistance and energy not reaching the water.

:::soluzione
Electrical power is $P=I\Delta V=100\,\mathrm W$ and resistance is $R=\dfrac{\Delta V}{I}=25\,\Omega$. The water requires $Q=mc\Delta T=4200\,\mathrm J$. Useful power is $0.70P=70\,\mathrm W$, so time is $t=\dfrac{4200}{70}=60\,\mathrm s$. Total electrical energy is $6000\,\mathrm J$, of which $1800\,\mathrm J$ does not reach the water in the stated model. Treating all power as useful would underestimate time; efficiency concerns energy transfer and does not automatically reduce measured heater current.
:::
