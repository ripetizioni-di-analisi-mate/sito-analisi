A nonrelativistic ion of mass $3.2\cdot10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and charge $+3.2\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ enters rightwards at $1.5\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ into a uniform inward field of $0.10\,\mathrm T$. Calculate radius and period, describe initial curvature, and explain what changes if charge sign reverses with all other data fixed. No other forces act.

:::soluzione
Force initially points upward, so the circle’s centre lies above the entry point. Radius is $r=\dfrac{mv}{|q|B}=0.15\,\mathrm m$ and period is $T=\dfrac{2\pi m}{|q|B}\simeq6.3\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$. Speed does not change: magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity and does zero work. Reversing charge sign reverses force and curvature; radius and period remain unchanged because they contain charge magnitude. The inward field does not describe the particle’s path, which stays in the page plane.
:::
