A uniform wire of length $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$ and area $A=2.0\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is kept at the same temperature. Two measurements give $(\Delta V,I)=(5.0\,\mathrm V;10\,\mathrm{mA})$ and $(10\,\mathrm V;20\,\mathrm{mA})$. Find resistance and resistivity and discuss what the two experimental points show and do not prove.

:::soluzione
Converting milliamperes, both ratios give $R=\dfrac{5.0}{0.010}=\dfrac{10}{0.020}=500\,\Omega$. For the stated geometry, $\rho_e=\dfrac{RA}{L}=5.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\Omega\,\mathrm m$. Units distinguish resistivity from resistance. The points are consistent with direct proportionality and one line through the origin, but two measurements do not establish behaviour over any interval: more points and experimental uncertainties are needed. Temperature control avoids attributing a heating-induced change to intrinsic nonlinearity.
:::
