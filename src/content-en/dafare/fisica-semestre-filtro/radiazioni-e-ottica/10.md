For a sample containing $3.60\cdot10^6$ nuclei with half-life $10.0\,\mathrm{min}$, calculate the decay constant in SI units and the activity. Use $\ln2\simeq0.693$. Also find the exponential time constant and distinguish it from half-life.

:::soluzione
Use $\mathscr A=\dfrac{N\ln2}{T_{1/2}}$ with $T_{1/2}=600\,\mathrm s$: the unrounded result is $4158\,\mathrm{Bq}$. Using ten without converting minutes gives a per-minute rate incorrectly labelled as becquerels. Omitting or inverting $\ln2$ confuses half-life with the exponential time constant.

The constant is $\lambda_d=\dfrac{0.693}{600}\simeq1.16\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Multiplication by a pure count gives transformations per second. Its reciprocal, about $866\,\mathrm s$, is the time constant rather than half-life: multiplying it by $\ln2$ recovers $600\,\mathrm s$.
:::
