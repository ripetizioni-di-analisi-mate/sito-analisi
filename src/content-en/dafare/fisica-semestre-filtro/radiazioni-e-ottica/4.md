Build the energy budget of a $3.00\,\mathrm{mW}$ monochromatic source operating for $10.0\,\mathrm s$. Each photon carries $3.00\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm J$. Determine the mean emission rate and interval photon count, then recover total energy both by counting photons and by using power.

:::soluzione
Power is $3.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J/s}$: dividing by individual energy gives $\dot N_\gamma=\dfrac P{E_\gamma}=10^{16}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Omitting the milli prefix overestimates the result a thousandfold. Check by multiplying the resulting rate by one photon’s energy to recover power.

In ten seconds, $1.00\cdot10^{17}$ photons are emitted. Their total energy is $0.0300\,\mathrm J$, obtained from either $N_\gamma E_\gamma$ or $P\Delta t$. Both routes must agree because they describe the same energy budget; one counts quanta, the other uses a continuous quantity.
:::
