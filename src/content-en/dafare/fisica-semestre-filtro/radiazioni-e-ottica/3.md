parole: ["Energy at four hundred nanometres"]
---

Compare monochromatic radiation of vacuum wavelength $400\,\mathrm{nm}$ with a target whose ionization threshold is $3.00\,\mathrm{eV}$. Use $hc\simeq1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}$ to find photon energy and any excess above threshold. State what follows about the possibility and probability of a single-photon event.

:::soluzione
The product $hc$ already uses units compatible with nanometres: $E_\gamma=\dfrac{1240}{400}=3.10\,\mathrm{eV}$. No additional wavelength conversion factor is needed. The reciprocal and product of the two numbers do not give the required energy; energy must decrease as wavelength increases.

The photon exceeds the threshold by $0.10\,\mathrm{eV}$ in the stated model. This makes the process energetically accessible but does not guarantee absorption of every photon: interaction probability is unspecified. A longer wavelength would give lower energy, providing a qualitative check.
:::
