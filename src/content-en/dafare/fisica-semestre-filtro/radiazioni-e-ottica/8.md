Reconstruct beta-minus decay of carbon ${}^{14}_{6}\mathrm C$, naming the daughter nucleus, electron and antineutrino. Determine changes in proton and neutron numbers and check charge balance. Explain why this electron is not simply a cloud electron removed from the atom.

:::soluzione
The daughter is nitrogen-14, ${}^{14}_{7}\mathrm N$:
$$
{}^{14}_{6}\mathrm C\longrightarrow{}^{14}_{7}\mathrm N+e^-+\bar\nu_e.
$$
Charge balances as $6=7-1+0$, while nucleon number stays $14$.

A neutron becomes a proton: nucleon number stays $14$ and proton number rises to $7$. The emitted electron is not a nucleon to subtract from $A$. Changing to $Z=5$ would instead correspond to the direction of a beta-plus transformation, if energetically allowed.

Neutrons change from $14-6=8$ to $14-7=7$, while protons rise from six to seven. The beta electron is produced in the nuclear transformation with the antineutrino; it is not simply a cloud electron removed by ionization. The element changes because $Z$ changes.
:::
