parole: ["Daughter of an alpha decay"]
---

Write and balance alpha emission from ${}^{238}_{92}\mathrm U$, identifying the daughter nuclide. Count protons and neutrons separately before and after the transformation and verify that the differences match the emitted particle’s composition.

:::soluzione
The daughter is thorium-234, ${}^{234}_{90}\mathrm{Th}$, and the balanced transformation is
$$
{}^{238}_{92}\mathrm U\longrightarrow{}^{234}_{90}\mathrm{Th}+{}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}.
$$

An alpha particle contains two protons and two neutrons: it removes four nucleons and two units of nuclear charge. Thus $A^{\prime}=238-4=234$ and $Z^{\prime}=92-2=90$. Preserving either number ignores part of the emitted particle; subtracting two rather than four nucleons confuses protons with nucleons.

Initially there are $238-92=146$ neutrons; the daughter has $234-90=144$. The difference is two, exactly the alpha particle’s neutron count. Independent proton and neutron checks together reconstruct the loss of four nucleons.
:::
