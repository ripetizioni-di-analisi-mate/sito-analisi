Design the thickness of a homogeneous attenuator required to pass $12.5\%$ of a monochromatic beam. Its half-value thickness is $0.500\,\mathrm{cm}$ and exponential attenuation is assumed. Show the first three halvings with their transmitted fractions and justify why absolute losses are unequal.

:::soluzione
$12.5\%$ is $\dfrac{1}{8}=(\dfrac{1}{2})^3$, requiring three half-value thicknesses: $x=3\cdot0.500=1.50\,\mathrm{cm}$. Two thicknesses would give one quarter. Thickness cannot be obtained by linearly scaling the fraction of intensity lost: each layer removes the same fraction of what reaches it.

Thicknesses $0.500$, $1.00$ and $1.50\,\mathrm{cm}$ correspond to $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $\dfrac{1}{8}$. The check always uses the power entering each layer. Absolute losses decrease: first half the initial value, then one quarter, then one eighth, without ever making intensity negative.
:::
