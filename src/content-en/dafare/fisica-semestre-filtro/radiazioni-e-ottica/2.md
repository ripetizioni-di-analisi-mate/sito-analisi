A sinusoidal plane wave in vacuum changes electric amplitude from $E_0$ to $3E_0$ while its frequency stays fixed. Derive the change factors for mean intensity and magnetic amplitude. Then explain changes in energy per photon and photon-number rate through the same area.

:::soluzione
Average intensity is proportional to $E_0^2$ under the stated conditions, so $\dfrac{I^{\prime}}{I}=3^2=9$. A factor of three incorrectly treats intensity as linear in amplitude. Unchanged frequency fixes photon energy, not the total energy transported per unit time.

Since $E_0=cB_0$, $B_0$ also triples. However, $E_\gamma=h\nu$ stays unchanged. At equal beam area, increased intensity requires nine times the photon rate: field amplitudes should not be identified with one photon’s energy.
:::
