A single spherical interface has $\gamma=0.020\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and radius $0.10\,\mathrm{mm}$. Find the inside-minus-outside jump; compare it with a long cylindrical interface and a thin spherical soap film at equal radius and tension. State the geometric idealisations.

:::soluzione

$$
\Delta p_{\mathrm{sph}}=\dfrac{2\cdot0.020}{10^{-4}}=400\,\mathrm{Pa},\quad
\Delta p_{\mathrm{cyl}}=200\,\mathrm{Pa},\quad
\Delta p_{\mathrm{soap}}\simeq800\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

Assume equilibrium, uniform tension, negligible gravity and positive curvatures in the inside-minus-outside convention. The sphere has two non-zero curvatures on one interface; the cylinder has one, far from its ends. The film adds two spherical jumps and requires thickness negligible relative to radius. The word “bubble” alone does not determine the formula.

:::
