---
titolo: "A reading in millimetres of mercury"
teoria: [pressione-densita]
parole: ["A reading in millimetres of mercury"]
---

A pressure difference of $120\,\mathrm{mmHg}$ must be reported in pascals and compared with a water column. Use $1\,\mathrm{mmHg}=133.3\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $\rho_a=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the equivalent height and explain why it is not $120\,\mathrm{mm}$ of water.

:::soluzione

$$
\Delta p=120\cdot133.3=15996\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq1.60\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$
$$
h_a=\dfrac{15996}{1000\cdot10}\,\mathrm m=1.5996\,\mathrm m\simeq1.60\,\mathrm m.
$$

The first step is a conventional unit conversion; the second uses a hydrostatic model with the specified gravity. Equal heights of liquids with different densities do not produce equal pressures. Water, much less dense than mercury, requires a taller column. Pressure differences are compared, so atmospheric pressure is unnecessary.

:::
