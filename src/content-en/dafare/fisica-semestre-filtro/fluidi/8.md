parole: ["Narrowing and speed"]
---

Follow an incompressible liquid through a steady conduit without mass loss. The second section has half the first radius. Derive area and mean-speed ratios, then explain why continuity alone cannot determine the pressure difference.

:::soluzione

$$
\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}=\left(\dfrac12\right)^2=\dfrac14,
\qquad A_1\bar v_1=A_2\bar v_2,
\qquad\dfrac{\bar v_2}{\bar v_1}=4.
$$

Equal mass crosses both sections per unit time; with constant density this means equal volumetric flow. Mean speed rises to compensate for smaller area. Pressure additionally requires an energy or momentum balance and information about heights, losses and machinery. Continuity remains valid even when ideal Bernoulli cannot be applied.

:::
