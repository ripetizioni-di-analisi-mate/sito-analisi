A sensor is lowered to $0.50\,\mathrm m$ in water at rest, with $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The surface is at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Predict readings for an absolute sensor and one zeroed at the surface, and justify their difference.

:::soluzione

$$
\Delta p=\rho gh=1000\cdot10\cdot0.50=5000\,\mathrm{Pa}=5\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$
$$
p_{\mathrm{ass}}=100+5=105\,\mathrm{kPa},\qquad
p_{\mathrm{rel}}=5\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$

The liquid supports a taller column at the sensor point, producing a positive increment. The absolute sensor includes pressure already present at the surface; the zeroed sensor measures only the change. Both describe the same physical state with different references. Their difference remains the specified surface pressure as long as it does not change.

:::
