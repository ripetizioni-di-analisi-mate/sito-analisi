---
titolo: Discontinuity at the origin
teoria: [continuita-due-variabili, teorema-delle-restrizioni]
parole: [continuity in two variables, restrictions, discontinuity]
---

Study the continuity on $\mathbb{R}^2$ of the function
$$
f(x, y) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(x + y)^2}{x^2 + y^2} & (x, y) \neq (0, 0) \\[8pt]
1 & (x, y) = (0, 0)
\end{cases}
$$

:::soluzione
Away from the origin $f$ is continuous. At the origin the limit does not exist: along the $x$-axis the restriction is constantly $1$, along the line $y = -x$ it is constantly $0$. Hence $f$ is not continuous at $(0, 0)$, whatever value is assigned there.
:::
