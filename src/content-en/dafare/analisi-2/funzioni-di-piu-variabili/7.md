---
titolo: Limit along a curve
teoria: [teorema-delle-restrizioni]
parole: [limits in two variables, restrictions, cubic curve, non-existence of the limit]
---

Determine whether the limit
$$
\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{x^3 y}{x^6 + y^2} .
$$
exists.

:::soluzione
The limit does not exist: along every line through the origin the restriction tends to $0$, but along the curve $y = x^3$ we have $f(x, x^3) = \dfrac{x^6}{2x^6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so two restrictions produce different limits.
:::
