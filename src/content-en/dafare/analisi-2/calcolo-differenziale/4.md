---
titolo: Differentiability of a piecewise rational function
teoria: [differenziabilita]
parole: [differentiability, definition, limit in two variables, piecewise-defined function]
---

Determine whether the function
$$
f(x, y) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x^3}{x^2 + y^2} & (x, y) \neq (0, 0) \\[10pt]
0 & (x, y) = (0, 0)
\end{cases}
$$
is differentiable at $(0, 0)$.

:::soluzione
The partial derivatives at the origin equal $f_x(0,0) = 1$ and $f_y(0,0) = 0$, while the ratio in the definition is $\dfrac{-x y^2}{(x^2 + y^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$: along the line $y = x$ with $x > 0$ it is constantly $-\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}$ and along the $x$-axis it equals $0$, so the limit does not exist and in particular it is not zero. Therefore $f$ is not differentiable at $(0,0)$.
:::
