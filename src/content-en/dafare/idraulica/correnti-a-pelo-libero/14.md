A rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a depth of $h_1 = 1.60\,\mathrm{m}$. The bed rises by $\Delta z = 0.250\,\mathrm{m}$ at the step shown. Neglecting head loss through the transition, calculate the downstream depth, the change in water-surface elevation and the greatest step the flow can pass.

::schema{nomi=0 acqua="0,0 4,0 4,0.5 9,0.5 9,3.12 0,3.2" solidi="0,-1 9,-1 9,0.5 4,0.5 4,0 0,0" linee="0,3.2 9,3.12" tratteggi="4,0 9.8,0" quote="2.7 0,2.7 3.2,1.60 m,0;9.6 0,9.6 0.5,Δz = 0.250 m,0.5;6.5 0.5,6.5 3.12,h_2,0" frecce="0.5,1.6,1.5,1.6,v_1" riferimento=0 titolo="Upward bed step of $\Delta z = 0.250\,\mathrm{m}$ in a channel of width $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carrying $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

:::soluzione
$E_1 = 1.680\,\mathrm{m}$, $h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ and $E_{\min} = 1.112\,\mathrm{m}$. The flow can pass. The downstream depth is $h_2 = 1.311\,\mathrm{m}$ and the downstream water-surface elevation is $1.561\,\mathrm{m}$, so the free surface drops by $0.039\,\mathrm{m}$. The maximum step height is $\Delta z_{\max} = 0.567\,\mathrm{m}$.
:::
