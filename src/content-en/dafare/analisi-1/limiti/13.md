---
titolo: Solutions of an equation with the intermediate zero theorem
teoria: [teorema-degli-zeri]
parole: [intermediate zero theorem, equation, roots]
---

Prove that the equation
$$
x^3 - 4x + 1 = 0
$$
has three real solutions, and give for each one an interval of length $1$ that contains it.

:::soluzione
Setting $f(x) = x^3 - 4x + 1$, we have $f(-3) = -14$, $f(-2) = 1$, $f(0) = 1$, $f(1) = -2$, $f(2) = 1$. The function is continuous and changes sign on $[-3, -2]$, on $[0, 1]$ and on $[1, 2]$, therefore by the intermediate zero theorem it has a zero in each of these intervals. A third-degree polynomial has at most three real roots, hence the solutions are exactly three.
:::
