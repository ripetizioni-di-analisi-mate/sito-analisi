Read the following passage.

> There is frequent debate over whether to ban cell phones in schools. Supporters of a ban point out that notifications distract students and that many students look at their screens during breaks instead of talking to one another. These concerns are well founded, but a blanket ban would accomplish little.
>
> Students spend many hours a day using phones outside school, and school is precisely where they could learn to use them thoughtfully: to look up information and verify its source, organize their studies, and recognize misleading messages. A clear rule keeping phones off during lessons and allowing them in certain teacher-led activities would be more useful than a ban. Such a rule requires more of teachers than a ban because the activities need planning and supervision. Yet it is the only rule that prepares students for how they will use their phones once they leave school.

Which position does the author support?

- A) A rule keeping phones off during lessons and allowing them in guided activities is preferable to a total ban
- B) Phones should be banned from schools because they distract students
- C) Phones do not distract students
- D) Young people spend too many hours a day on their phones and parents should intervene

:::soluzione
Answer A: A rule keeping phones off during lessons and allowing them in guided activities is preferable to a total ban.
:::
