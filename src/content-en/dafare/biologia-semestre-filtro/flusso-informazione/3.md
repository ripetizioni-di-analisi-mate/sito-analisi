A linear DNA segment 100 kb long is copied from an origin exactly at its centre. Two forks start together and each advances at 1 kb/s, without pauses. How long does copying the entire segment take?

:::soluzione
50 s

Each fork must travel half the segment, so $\dfrac{100}{2}=50\,\mathrm{kb}$. Time is distance divided by speed: $t=\dfrac{50\,\mathrm{kb}}{1\,\mathrm{kb/s}}=50\,\mathrm{s}$. The two paths are traversed simultaneously, so their times are not added. The double helix contains two strands, but fork speed already measures progress along the region being copied.
:::
