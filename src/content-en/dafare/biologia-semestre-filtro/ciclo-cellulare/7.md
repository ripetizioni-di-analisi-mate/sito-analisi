A species has five homologue pairs. Ignoring crossing over, calculate possible maternal/paternal-origin combinations in gametes. Does a single meiotic event necessarily produce all of them?

:::soluzione
There are $2^5=32$ possible combinations, with two independent choices for each of five pairs. One meiosis produces at most four products and therefore does not necessarily realise all combinations. The number 32 describes possibilities across many events. Crossing over, excluded here, would add within-homologue variation without changing the fact that one cell undergoes only two divisions.
:::
