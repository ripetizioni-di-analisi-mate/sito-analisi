A short pulse labels DNA in S-phase cells. After precursor removal, the same labelled cells reach 4C. Explain why persistent labelling does not prove continuing DNA synthesis, and propose a way to distinguish G2 from M.

:::soluzione
Incorporated precursor remains in DNA after the pulse, so the signal records earlier synthesis. Reaching 4C fits completion of S phase, but residual fluorescence does not measure instantaneous synthesis. G2 and M can be distinguished by nuclear organisation and chromosome condensation, potentially with mitotic markers. Labelling tracks a population over time; positivity alone does not identify its current phase.
:::
