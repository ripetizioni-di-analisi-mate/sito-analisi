An analysis system classifies every cell with chromosomes near the spindle centre as “correct”. Explain why this criterion can miss errors and identify two additional observations.

:::soluzione
Central position does not establish that each kinetochore connects to the appropriate pole. Kinetochore attachments and sister biorientation should be inspected; a checkpoint-activity indicator can provide complementary evidence. A chromosome can be near the plate despite a missing or incorrect attachment. Checkpoints are not infallible, so absence of an obvious signal alone does not prove future perfect segregation. A useful criterion combines geometry, attachments and division progression.
:::
