Use the qualitative diagrams to follow heating at one bar, starting from the solid, for water and CO₂. Identify the crossed boundaries. Why can water’s temperature remain nearly constant while heat continues entering during an equilibrium phase change? What diagram information rules out stable liquid CO₂ at one bar?

![Qualitative water and CO2 diagrams: phase regions, triple points and a one-bar isobar](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/atomo-legami-termodinamica/fasi-en.svg)

:::soluzione
Water crosses the **solid–liquid** boundary and, on further heating, the **liquid–vapour** boundary. During coexistence at fixed pressure, heat changes phase proportions rather than temperature: it supplies the transition’s latent heat. CO₂ instead crosses the **solid–gas** boundary and sublimes. Its triple-point pressure is approximately $5.185\,\mathrm{bar}$, above one bar, so the isobar stays below the liquid region. Qualitative positions do not supply exact temperatures to read from the axes.
:::
