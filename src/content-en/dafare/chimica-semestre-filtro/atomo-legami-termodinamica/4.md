A sample contains $18.0\,\mathrm g$ of pure glucose, $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$. Use $M=180\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ and $N_A=6.022\cdot10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$. Calculate glucose moles and molecules, moles of C atoms and moles of H atoms. Distinguish atom counting from chemical decomposition.

:::soluzione
$n=\dfrac{18.0}{180}=0.100\,\mathrm{mol}$ and $N=nN_A\simeq6.02\cdot10^{22}$ molecules. Each molecule contains six C and twelve H atoms, giving **$n(\mathrm C)=0.600\,\mathrm{mol}$** and **$n(\mathrm H)=1.20\,\mathrm{mol}$**. Subscripts multiply the molecular amount rather than the sample mass. This counts atoms already bonded in glucose; it does not assume decomposition into elemental carbon and hydrogen.
:::
