Assess the three sets $(n,l,m_l,m_s)$: $A=(3,2,2,-\dfrac12)$, $B=(3,3,0,+\dfrac12)$ and $C=(2,1,0,0)$. State whether each is allowed and explain. Can a second electron occupy the same orbital as state A? If so, specify its quantum numbers.

:::soluzione
**A is allowed**: $l=2\le n-1$, $m_l=2$ lies between $-2$ and $+2$, and spin projection is permitted. B is forbidden because $n=3$ requires $l\le2$. C is forbidden because $m_s$ must be $\pm\dfrac12$, never zero. A second electron can occupy A’s orbital with $(3,2,2,+\dfrac12)$, sharing the three spatial quantum numbers but having opposite spin as required by Pauli.
:::
