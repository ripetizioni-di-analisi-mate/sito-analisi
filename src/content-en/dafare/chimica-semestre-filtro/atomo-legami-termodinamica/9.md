A gauge reads $80.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ above atmospheric pressure, which remains $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. A fixed ideal-gas amount expands isothermally from $1.50\,\mathrm L$ to $3.00\,\mathrm L$. Calculate initial and final absolute pressures and the final gauge reading. Is a negative reading possible?

:::soluzione
Initial absolute pressure is $p_1=80.0+100=180\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Boyle’s law uses absolute pressures: $p_2=p_1\dfrac{V_1}{V_2}=180\cdot\dfrac{1.50}{3.00}=90.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The gauge therefore reads **$p_2-p_{\mathrm{atm}}=-10.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$**. This negative reading is allowed: the gas is below atmospheric pressure while absolute pressure remains positive. Applying Boyle’s law directly to the gauge value 80.0 would be incorrect.
:::
