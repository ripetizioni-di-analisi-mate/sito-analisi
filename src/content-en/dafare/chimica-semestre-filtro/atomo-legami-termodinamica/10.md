An ideal gas in a rigid vessel is heated from $10.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ to $40.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. If the vessel is sealed and initial absolute pressure is $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$, find final pressure. Then consider a separate experiment in which a valve maintains constant pressure: what fraction of initial moles remains at the same final temperature?

:::soluzione
Temperatures are $T_1=283.15\,\mathrm K$ and $T_2=313.15\,\mathrm K$. In the sealed vessel, $p_2=100\cdot\dfrac{313.15}{283.15}\simeq111\,\mathrm{kPa}$. With the valve holding pressure constant, $n=\dfrac{pV}{RT}$ instead gives **$\dfrac{n_2}{n_1}=\dfrac{283.15}{313.15}\simeq0.904$**. Approximately 90.4% of initial moles remain. Pressure changes in the first experiment; matter leaves in the second. Rigidity fixes V but does not hold both p and n constant.
:::
