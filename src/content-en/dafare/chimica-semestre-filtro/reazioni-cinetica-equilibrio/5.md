A first-order disappearance has a $40\,\mathrm{min}$ half-life with constant $k$ and volume. Without input or a reverse contribution, calculate the fraction remaining after $120\,\mathrm{min}$ and distinguish it from the fraction lost.

:::soluzione
$\dfrac18$.

Three half-lives pass. The remaining fraction is $\left(\dfrac12\right)^3=\dfrac18$ because each halving acts on the residual amount. One quarter describes only two intervals; seven eighths is the fraction that disappeared. Exponential dependence distinguishes this model from linear loss of a fixed amount per minute.

:::
