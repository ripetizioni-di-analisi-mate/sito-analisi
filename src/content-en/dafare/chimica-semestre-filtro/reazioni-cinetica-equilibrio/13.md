Equal volumes containing $[M^+]=2.0\cdot10^{-4}$ and $[X^-]=4.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ are mixed. For MX, $K_{\mathrm{ps}}=1.0\cdot10^{-8}$ at the assigned temperature. Assuming additive volumes and ideality, calculate the ion product immediately after mixing and interpret its comparison with the constant, distinguishing this from the time needed for crystals to appear.

:::soluzione
$Q_{\mathrm{ps}}=2.0\cdot10^{-8}>K_{\mathrm{ps}}$.

Final volume is twice each initial volume, so both concentrations halve. Their product is $(1.0\cdot10^{-4})(2.0\cdot10^{-4})=2.0\cdot10^{-8}$. Eight times ten to the minus eight would ignore both dilutions. Supersaturation indicates favoured precipitation; immediate appearance of visible solid also depends on nucleation.

:::
