Phenol and benzyl alcohol are examined separately in an aqueous buffer at $pH=11$ and 25 °C. Assume dilute solution, unchanged pH and the teaching values $pK_a=10$ and $pK_a=16$, respectively. Calculate each anionic fraction and discuss whether the groups respond identically to base.

:::soluzione
For each acid/base pair, $r=10^{pH-pK_a}$ and the anionic fraction is $\dfrac r{1+r}$. For phenol, $r=10$ and $f=\dfrac{10}{11}\approx0.909$. For benzyl alcohol, $r=10^{-5}$ and $f\approx0.000010$.

Phenol is therefore predominantly phenoxide, whereas benzyl alcohol remains almost entirely neutral. In the latter, CH2 separates O from the ring: its conjugate base lacks phenoxide's conjugation. Charge can influence distribution between water and an organic solvent, but these fractions alone do not determine extraction yield: partition coefficients and volumes would also be needed.
:::
