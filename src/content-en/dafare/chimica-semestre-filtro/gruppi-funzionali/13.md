Compare ethyl propanoate, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COOCH_2CH_3}$, and ethyl 2-methylpropanoate, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CHCOOCH_2CH_3}$, as donors in classical Claisen condensation driven by product deprotonation. Count the usable alpha H atoms, select the substrate meeting the requirement, and explain ethoxide use and final acid work-up.

:::soluzione
In propanoate the alpha carbon is CH2 and bears two H atoms. In 2-methylpropanoate it is CH and bears one. Both can initially lose an alpha H, but only the first retains another H on the donor carbon after C–C bond formation.

This remaining H between the two carbonyls can be removed, stabilizing the beta-keto ester anion and shifting equilibrium. The second substrate does not meet this classical pathway's requirement; this does not establish that every synthetic variant is impossible. Ethoxide matches both esters' OR group and limits formation of different esters by transesterification. Acid work-up restores the neutral beta-keto ester from its anion.
:::
