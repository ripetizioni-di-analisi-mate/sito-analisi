In a crossed-aldol model, consider one addition of a propanone enolate to formaldehyde, followed by protonation, excluding further and side reactions. Write the product and identify the source of its OH-bearing carbon. Then predict the product of alpha-beta dehydration.

:::soluzione
The addition product is $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_2OH}$, 4-hydroxybutan-2-one. The nucleophilic carbon comes from a propanone methyl group; formaldehyde's carbonyl carbon becomes terminal CH2OH. The donor carbonyl remains a ketone.

Eliminating beta OH and alpha H as water gives $\mathrm{CH_3COCH{=}CH_2}$, containing a conjugated double bond. Formaldehyde can accept attack but cannot supply an ordinary enolate: it has no alpha carbon. The balance retains four carbons, three from propanone and one from formaldehyde. Single addition is a selectivity assumption, not a guarantee that every real mixture yields only one product.
:::
