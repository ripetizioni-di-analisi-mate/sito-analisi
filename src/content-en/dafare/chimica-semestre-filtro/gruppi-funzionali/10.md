A mixture contains $0.60\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of Q and $0.40\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of QH2, both neutral forms. Calculate the electrons and protons required by the half-reaction to convert the entire mixture into QH2. In a separate experiment on the initial mixture, how many electrons would be released by converting everything into Q?

:::soluzione
Reduction is $\mathrm Q+2\,\mathrm{H^+}+2e^-\rightarrow\mathrm{QH_2}$. Only Q must react, requiring $1.20\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of electrons and the same amount of protons in the half-reaction. The already reduced $0.40\,\mathrm{mmol}$ does not require two more electrons per molecule.

In the oxidation experiment only QH2 reacts, releasing $0.80\,\mathrm{mmol}$ each of electrons and protons. Both experiments retain $1.00\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of molecules in the pair overall. Net proton consumption by the whole system would also require the other redox partner's half-reaction; no membrane proton-translocation count follows from this calculation.
:::
