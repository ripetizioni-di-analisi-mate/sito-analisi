Methyl propanoate reacts with excess ammonia under conditions suitable for ester ammonolysis. Write the organic balance giving the N-unsubstituted amide and alcohol. Describe the tetrahedral intermediate's fate and explain why the product is not N-methylpropanamide.

:::soluzione
The balance is

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COOCH_3}+\mathrm{NH_3}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CONH_2}+\mathrm{CH_3OH}.
$$

Ammonia attacks the acyl carbon. After appropriate proton transfers, intermediate collapse restores C=O and releases the methoxy portion as methanol. The ethyl group attached to the acyl group stays in place.

The original methyl was attached to O, not N, and ends up in the alcohol. An N-methylamide would require introducing methyl on nitrogen, for example by using methylamine in the corresponding aminolysis. Acyl substitution does not automatically transfer an alkyl group from oxygen to nitrogen.
:::
