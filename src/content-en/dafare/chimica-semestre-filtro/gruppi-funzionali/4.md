1,2-Epoxybutane reacts with ethoxide in ethanol, after which the alkoxide is protonated. Write the constitutional product, distinguish nucleophile oxygen from original ring oxygen, and count every product carbon. The initial configuration is unspecified.

:::soluzione
The product is $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OCH_2CH(OH)CH_2CH_3}$. Ethoxide attacks the less hindered terminal CH2, breaking its bond to epoxide oxygen. Ring oxygen remains on carbon 2 and becomes OH; ethoxide oxygen forms the terminal ether linkage.

There are six carbons: four from the epoxide and two from ethoxide. A four-carbon answer would lose the introduced group; an answer containing only one oxygen would not conserve atoms. Attack occurs at CH2, which is not stereogenic. The neighboring center retains its connectivity, but no single enantiomer can be assigned without the starting configuration.
:::
