Determine pH for $\mathrm{NH_4Cl}$ at $0.0200\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$, identifying the ion responsible for acidity. Use $K_b(\mathrm{NH_3})=1.80\cdot10^{-5}$ and $K_w=10^{-14}$. Assume ideal behaviour and neglect chloride hydrolysis and the autoprotolysis contribution, checking the latter afterwards.

:::soluzione
**1. Set-up.** Identify which salt ion reacts appreciably with water and obtain the acid constant from the conjugate pair's base constant.

**2. Working and check.** Ammonium has $K_a=K_w/K_b=5.556\cdot10^{-10}$. Hydronium concentration is approximately $\sqrt{K_aC}=3.333\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, giving pH $5.48$. The quadratic gives $5.47716$; dissociation is negligible relative to initial concentration. Hydroxide from water is about $3.0\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, less than $0.1\%$ of calculated hydronium: neglecting it does not alter pH rounded to two decimal places. Ammonium, not chloride, causes acidity, so calling every salt solution neutral would miss the relevant reaction.
:::
