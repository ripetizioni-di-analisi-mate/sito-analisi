For $\mathrm{CaC_2O_4(s)\rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+}+C_2O_4^{2-}}$, explain how oxalate protonation affects the ion product and dissolution, distinguishing this from a change in the solubility constant. Start with a saturated solution in equilibrium with the solid present. Assume fixed temperature and neglect other calcium equilibria and activity-coefficient changes.

:::soluzione
**1. Set-up.** Write the ion product using free species only. Follow the effect of anion protonation while keeping the model's other assumptions fixed.

**2. Working and check.** The solubility product involves free calcium and oxalate activities. Converting some $\mathrm{C_2O_4^{2-}}$ into $\mathrm{HC_2O_4^-}$ lowers free-anion activity. The ion product falls below $K_{ps}$, allowing more solid to dissolve until a new equilibrium. $K_{ps}$ remains unchanged at fixed temperature in the adopted model. Total dissolved oxalate must not be confused with its free, unprotonated fraction.
:::
