In the radical model $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}+H_2O_2\rightarrow Fe^{3+}+OH^-+{}^\bullet OH}$, derive iron’s electron transfer and identify the radical produced, distinguishing it from hydroxide ion.

:::soluzione
**1. Set-up.** Check iron's change and the identities of the oxygen species separately. A negative charge is not the symbol for an unpaired electron.

**2. Working and check.** Iron charge changes from $+2$ to $+3$, so iron loses an electron and is oxidised. The products include hydroxyl radical ${}^\bullet\mathrm{OH}$. Hydroxide $\mathrm{OH^-}$ is a different, negatively charged species, while the stated radical is neutral. Its unpaired-electron dot and the hydroxide charge are not interchangeable notation; the hydroperoxyl radical is not a product in the assigned equation.
:::
