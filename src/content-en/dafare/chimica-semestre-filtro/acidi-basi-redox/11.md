For $\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-/HPO_4^{2-}}$, assume $pK_a=7.20$ under the stated conditions. Derive the $\dfrac{[\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}}]}{[\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}]}$ ratio needed for pH $7.40$ in the ideal model, showing how to undo the logarithm and identifying the more abundant form.

:::soluzione
**1. Set-up.** Isolate the logarithm of the base-to-acid ratio, then raise ten to that power. A pH difference is not a concentration difference.

**2. Working and check.** Henderson–Hasselbalch gives $\log_{10}r=\mathrm{pH}-pK_a=0.20$, where $r$ is the base-to-acid ratio. Thus $r=10^{0.20}=1.58489\simeq1.58$. A ratio greater than one agrees with pH above $pK_a$. The pH difference is the logarithm of the ratio, not the ratio itself.
:::
