For $\mathrm{Zn+Cu^{2+}\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu}$ at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$, $E^\circ=1.10\,\mathrm V$. Set up the quotient and electron count, then calculate $E$ with pure metals, $a_{\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}}=0.0010$ and $a_{\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}}=0.10$. Use the Nernst coefficient $0.05916\,\mathrm V$.

:::soluzione
**1. Set-up.** Write the reaction quotient for the stated direction and count transferred electrons. The pure metal phases present have unit activity.

**2. Working and check.** Metallic zinc and copper have unit activity, so $Q=\dfrac{a_{\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}}}{a_{\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}}}=0.010$. The reaction transfers two electrons. Thus $E=1.10-\dfrac{0.05916}{2}\log_{10}(0.010)=1.15916\,\mathrm V$. A quotient below one raises potential above standard. Reversing the quotient changes the sign of the correction; forgetting the electron count doubles its magnitude.
:::
