In triangle $ABC$ the point $D$ lies on side $AB$ with $AD:DB=2:3$ and the point $E$ lies on side $AC$ with $AE:EC=4:5$. Keeping $D$ fixed, in which direction must $E$ be moved along $AC$ so that $DE$ is parallel to $BC$?

- Towards $A$
- Towards $C$
- In no direction, because $DE$ is already parallel to $BC$
- In no direction, because no position of $E$ makes $DE$ parallel to $BC$

## Explanation

By the converse of the theorem on nested triangles, $DE$ is parallel to $BC$ if and only if $\dfrac{AE}{AC}=\dfrac{AD}{AB}$. From $AD:DB=2:3$ it follows that $\dfrac{AD}{AB}=\dfrac{2}{2+3}=\dfrac25$, while from $AE:EC=4:5$ it follows that $\dfrac{AE}{AC}=\dfrac{4}{4+5}=\dfrac49$. Since $\dfrac49=\dfrac{20}{45}$ and $\dfrac25=\dfrac{18}{45}$, the ratio on $AC$ is greater than the one on $AB$, so $DE$ is not parallel to $BC$. To make the two ratios equal, $AE$ must be reduced to $\dfrac25$ of $AC$, so $E$ must be moved towards $A$.
