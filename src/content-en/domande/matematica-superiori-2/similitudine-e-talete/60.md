Two rectangles have sides $4,5$ and $2,16$. The second has twice the perimeter but not four times the area. Which hypothesis is missing for the squared-ratio rule?

- The perimeters must use the same unit.
- Every angle must be right.
- The rectangles must be similar.
- Side lengths must be integers.

## Explanation

The perimeters are $18$ and $36$, while the areas are $20$ and $32$. The shorter sides have ratio $\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the longer sides have ratio $\dfrac{16}{5}$, so there is no common factor. The angles already match, which is insufficient for quadrilaterals. Squaring the perimeter ratio applies only when the shape is preserved.
