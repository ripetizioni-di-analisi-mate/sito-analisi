In two similar triangles, a median is measured in the first and an altitude from the corresponding vertex in the second. Does their ratio determine the scale factor?

- Generally no, because the median and altitude are not corresponding segments.
- Always yes, because they start from corresponding vertices.
- Yes, by taking the square root of the ratio of the two lengths.
- Yes, but only if the median is longer than the altitude.

## Explanation

A similarity maps a median to the corresponding median and an altitude to the corresponding altitude. The median ends at the side’s midpoint, while the altitude meets its line at a right angle. These endpoints may agree, for example from the apex of an isosceles triangle to its base, but the givens do not guarantee that. The scale factor needs a corresponding pair, not just corresponding starting vertices.
