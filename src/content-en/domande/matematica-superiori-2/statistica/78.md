Two classes have widths $4$ and $8$ and counts $12$ and $16$. An incorrect histogram uses counts as heights. What is the ratio of the second count to the first, and the ratio of the second area to the first in the incorrect chart?

- $\dfrac{4}{3},\quad\dfrac{8}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3},\quad\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3},\quad\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $2,\quad\dfrac{8}{3}$

## Explanation

The count ratio is $\dfrac{16}{12}=\dfrac{4}{3}$. With those counts as heights, the areas are $4\cdot12=48$ and $8\cdot16=128$. The visible ratio is $\dfrac{128}{48}=\dfrac{8}{3}$, twice the correct ratio. In a histogram, divide each count by its own class width before setting the heights.
