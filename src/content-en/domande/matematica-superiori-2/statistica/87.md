Two groups of measurements have the same mean. The first group contains $4$ measurements with descriptive variance $9$, the second $12$ measurements with descriptive variance $1$. What is the descriptive standard deviation of the $16$ measurements combined?

- $\sqrt5$
- $2$
- $3$
- $\sqrt3$

## Explanation

Since the two groups have the same mean, the $16$ combined measurements also have that mean, so the deviations of the individual measurements do not change. The descriptive variance is the sum of the squared deviations divided by the number of values, so in the first group the sum of the squared deviations is $4\cdot9=36$. In the same way, in the second group the sum is $12\cdot1=12$. Hence the variance of the combined measurements is $\dfrac{36+12}{16}=3$ and the standard deviation is $\sqrt3$. The value $\sqrt5$ would come from the simple mean of the two variances, which does not take into account the number of measurements in each group.
