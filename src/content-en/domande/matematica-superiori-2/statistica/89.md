A series of $n$ values $x_1,\dots,x_n$ has mean $m$, and the sum of the squared deviations from the mean is $24$. What is the sum of the squares of the differences $x_j-(m+1)$?

- $25$
- $24-n$
- $24+n$
- $24n$

## Explanation

Each difference can be written as $x_j-(m+1)=(x_j-m)-1$, so its square is $(x_j-m)^2-2(x_j-m)+1$. The sum of the terms $(x_j-m)^2$ is $24$ by hypothesis. The sum of the deviations $x_j-m$ is zero, because $m$ is the mean, so the terms $-2(x_j-m)$ contribute nothing in total. Finally the $n$ terms equal to $1$ give $n$, so the required sum is $24+n$. The value $25$ comes from counting the term $1$ only once instead of once for each value.
