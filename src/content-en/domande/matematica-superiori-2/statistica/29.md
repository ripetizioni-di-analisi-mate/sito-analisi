Two histogram classes have widths $2$ and $6$ and the same positive absolute density. How do the counts compare?

- The counts are equal.
- The first has three times as many observations as the second.
- The numerical density is needed to compare the counts.
- The second has three times as many observations as the first.

## Explanation

In a histogram the count is the area. Writing $d$ for the common height, the two areas are $2d$ and $6d$. In the ratio the height cancels because it is positive and the same for both classes. The second class is three times as wide and contains three times as many observations, without needing the value of $d$.
