What is the solution set of the system $\begin{cases}x^2+x+1>0\\x^2-5x+4<0\end{cases}$?

- $(1,4)$
- $\varnothing$
- $\mathbb R$
- $(-\infty,1)\cup(4,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The trinomial $x^2+x+1$ has discriminant $1-4=-3<0$ and a positive coefficient of $x^2$, so it is positive for every real $x$. The first inequality is therefore satisfied by all real numbers and does not restrict the solution set. The trinomial $x^2-5x+4=(x-1)(x-4)$ has a positive coefficient of $x^2$, so it is negative between its roots, that is for $1<x<4$. The solution set of the system is the intersection $\mathbb R\cap(1,4)=(1,4)$. The answer $\varnothing$ comes from confusing a negative discriminant with an inequality that has no solutions.
