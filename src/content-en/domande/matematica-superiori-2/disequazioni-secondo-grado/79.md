What is the real domain of $\sqrt{-(x-3)^2}$?

- $\{3\}$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{3\}$
- $\mathbb R$
- $\varnothing$

## Explanation

The square root requires a nonnegative radicand. The negative of a square is always at most zero, so the square must equal zero. That happens at $x=3$. The square root of zero is defined and equals zero, so $3$ belongs to the domain. At every other point the minus sign makes the radicand strictly negative.
