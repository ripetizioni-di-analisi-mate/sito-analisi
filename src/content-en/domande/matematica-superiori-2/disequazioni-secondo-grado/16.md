A quadratic has a negative leading coefficient and a negative discriminant. Which statement about its sign holds for every real input?

- It is strictly positive
- It is zero at exactly one point
- It is strictly negative
- It changes sign twice

## Explanation

A negative discriminant means the quadratic has no real zeros. In completed-square form the brackets contain a square plus a strictly positive number. The bracketed value is therefore positive even at the vertex. Multiplication by the negative leading coefficient gives a strictly negative value at every horizontal coordinate.
