What is the domain of $\dfrac{\sqrt{x+1}}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$?

- $[-1,2]$
- $(-1,2)$
- $(-2,2)$
- $[-1,2)$

## Explanation

The numerator requires $x+1\ge0$, so $x\ge-1$. The denominator requires $4-x^2>0$, giving $-2<x<2$, because that root must also be nonzero. The intersection is $[-1,2)$. At $-1$ the quotient is defined and equals zero, whereas at $2$ the denominator vanishes. The two endpoints are treated differently for these two reasons.
