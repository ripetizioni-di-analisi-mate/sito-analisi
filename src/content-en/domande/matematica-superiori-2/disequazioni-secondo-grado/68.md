Which condition is equivalent to $\dfrac{x+2}{x-3}\le0$ without dividing by an expression of unknown sign?

- $(x+2)(x-3)\le0$
- $(x+2)(x-3)\ge0$ and $x\ne3$
- $x+2\le0$ and $x\ne3$
- $(x+2)(x-3)\le0$ and $x\ne3$

## Explanation

On the domain $x\ne3$, multiplication by $(x-3)^2$ is valid because the multiplier is strictly positive. The sign remains $\le$ and the fraction becomes the product $(x+2)(x-3)$. The exclusion of $3$ must stay because the product alone would allow equality there. At $-2$ the quotient is defined and gives zero, so equality is allowed.
