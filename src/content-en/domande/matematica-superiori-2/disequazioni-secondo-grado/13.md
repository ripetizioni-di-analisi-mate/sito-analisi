Solve $\dfrac{(x-2)(x+1)}{x-2}\ge0$.

- $[-1,+\infty)$
- $(-1,2)\cup(2,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-1]$
- $[-1,2)\cup(2,+\infty)$

## Explanation

On the domain $x\ne2$, the fraction equals $x+1$. This gives $x\ge-1$ with $2$ removed, since the original fraction excludes $2$ even after simplification.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S&=[-1,+\infty)\setminus\{2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
