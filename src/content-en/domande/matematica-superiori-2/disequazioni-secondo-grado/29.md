What is the solution of $\dfrac{(x-3)^2(x+1)}{x-3}\ge0$?

- $(-\infty,-1]\cup[3,+\infty)$
- $[-1,3)$
- $(-\infty,-1)\cup(3,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-1]\cup(3,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The denominator requires $x\ne3$. At allowed points, one factor $x-3$ cancels, leaving $(x-3)(x+1)$. That product is nonnegative outside the roots and at the roots themselves. The zero $-1$ belongs to the domain and is included. The simplified form has a zero at $3$, but the original fraction still excludes it because the denominator vanishes.
