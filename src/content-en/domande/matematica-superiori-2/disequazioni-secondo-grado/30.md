Solve $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}<1$.

- $(-\infty,-1)$
- $(-\infty,-1]$
- $(-\infty,1)$
- $(2,+\infty)$

## Explanation

On the domain $x\ne2$, cancel the factor $x-2$ to get $x+2$. Subtracting $2$ gives $x<-1$. All those values satisfy the domain restriction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}&=x+2\qquad(x\ne2).
\end{aligned}
$$
