For which real numbers is $\dfrac1{\sqrt{(x+3)^2+2}}$ defined?

- $\mathbb R\setminus\{-3\}$
- $[-3,+\infty)$
- $\mathbb R$
- $\varnothing$

## Explanation

A square root in the denominator requires a strictly positive radicand. The square is always nonnegative and vanishes at $-3$. Even at that point the added $2$ remains, giving radicand $2$. The denominator is therefore at least $\sqrt2$ and is never zero. The condition holds for every real value, including the vertex coordinate.
