For what value of $k$ does the quadratic term in $(k-3)x^2+2x-1\le0$ vanish, and what condition remains?

- $k=3,\ x\ge\dfrac12$
- $k=3,\ x\le\dfrac12$
- $k=0,\ x\le\dfrac12$
- $k=3,\ x\le-\dfrac12$

## Explanation

The coefficient of $x^2$ is $k-3$, which is zero only when $k=3$. Substitution leaves $2x-1\le0$, giving $2x\le1$. Dividing by the positive number $2$ preserves the sign, so $x\le\dfrac12$. The quadratic discriminant is unnecessary in this case because the relation is linear. The boundary makes the left-hand side exactly zero.
