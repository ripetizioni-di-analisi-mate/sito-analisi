For a quadratic $P$, the solution set of $P(x)\le0$ is exactly $[2,5]$. Which form can $P$ have?

- $-2(x-2)(x-5)$
- $2(x-2)^2$
- $2(x+2)(x+5)$
- $2(x-2)(x-5)$

## Explanation

The boundaries $2$ and $5$ are the roots of the quadratic. Between them the linear factors have opposite signs. A positive leading coefficient makes the product negative there. A leading coefficient of $-2$ would make it positive inside. The squared form has only one root, and the form with plus signs has negative roots.
