A fraction has only simple linear factors in its reduced form. Going from $x=-1$ to $x=2$ crosses exactly one zero and one distinct pole, with neither endpoint a zero or pole. How do the signs at the endpoints compare?

- One endpoint must be excluded
- They agree because there are two changes of sign
- Both must be positive
- They are opposite because the pole cancels the zero

## Explanation

At the simple zero, the numerator changes sign. At the simple zero of the denominator, the denominator changes sign. Two reversals restore the original sign, whatever it was.
