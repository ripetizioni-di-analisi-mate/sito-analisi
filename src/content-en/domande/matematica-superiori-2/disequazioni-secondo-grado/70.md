What is the solution of $\dfrac1{x-1}\ge\dfrac1{x+1}$?

- $[-1,1]$
- $(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,+\infty)$
- $(-1,1)$
- $(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,+\infty)$

## Explanation

Both fractions must be defined, so exclude $-1$ and $1$. Subtracting the second from the first gives $\dfrac2{(x-1)(x+1)}$. Its numerator is positive and never zero. The comparison holds where the denominator is positive, outside its roots. The points $-1$ and $1$ remain excluded even with the non-strict sign.
