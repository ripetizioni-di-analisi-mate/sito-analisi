In a sketch of $y=x^2+6x+9$, the curve seems to lie entirely above the axis. Which check settles whether $x^2+6x+9>0$ holds for every real number?

- At the vertex $x=-3$ the expression equals $0$
- The coefficient of $x^2$ is positive
- At $x=0$ the expression is positive
- The values at $x=3$ and $x=-6$ are positive

## Explanation

The trinomial is $(x+3)^2$. Its minimum is zero and occurs at $-3$, so that point must be excluded from the strict inequality.
