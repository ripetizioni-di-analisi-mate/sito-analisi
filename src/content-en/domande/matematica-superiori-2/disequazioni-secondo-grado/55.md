Two quadratics $P$ and $Q$ have roots $-2$ and $5$. The leading coefficient of $P$ is positive and that of $Q$ is negative. Where does $P(x)Q(x)\ge0$ hold?

- $[-2,5]$
- $(-\infty,-2]\cup[5,+\infty)$
- $\{-2,5\}$
- $\varnothing$

## Explanation

The quadratics have the same linear factors and coefficients of opposite signs. Their product is a negative constant times the square of $(x+2)(x-5)$. It is strictly negative when the square is positive and zero when the square vanishes. The comparison therefore allows exactly the common zeros $-2$ and $5$, including both points.
