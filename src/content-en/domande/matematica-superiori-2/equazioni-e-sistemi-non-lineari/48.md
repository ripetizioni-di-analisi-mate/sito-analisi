Under which conditions does $x^4+kx^2+m=0$ have exactly three distinct real roots?

- $m=0,\quad k>0$
- $m=0,\quad k<0$
- $m<0,\quad k=0$
- $m=0,\quad k=0$

## Explanation

An odd number of roots requires zero because nonzero roots come in opposite pairs. Zero requires $m=0$. The auxiliary equation is then $t(t+k)=0$. Adding one distinct pair requires $-k>0$, so $k<0$. With $k>0$, the other root in $t$ is negative, and with $k=0$, it coincides with zero. In both those cases only $x=0$ remains.
