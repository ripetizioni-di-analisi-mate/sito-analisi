You want to find $x^3+y^3$ using only $s=x+y$ and $p=xy$. Which identity lets you do so even when $s=0$?

- $x^3+y^3=s^3-3ps$
- $x^3+y^3=s^3+3ps$
- $x^3+y^3=s^3-3p$
- $x^3+y^3=s^3-p^3$

## Explanation

Besides the two cubes, the expansion of $(x+y)^3$ contains $3x^2y+3xy^2=3xy(x+y)$. Subtracting these terms gives the formula without dividing by $s$.
