How can $x^3-2x^2+x-2=0$ be solved by grouping pairs of terms, without guessing roots?

- $(x-2)(x^2+1)=0$
- $(x-2)(x^2-1)=0$
- $(x+2)(x^2-1)=0$
- $(x-1)(x^2-2)=0$

## Explanation

The first two terms give $x^2(x-2)$ and the last two give $x-2$. Their common factor is therefore $x-2$. Since $x^2+1$ is always positive, the only real root is $2$.
