The equation $\sqrt{x+3}+\sqrt{x-1}=0$ requires two nonnegative terms to sum to zero. What prevents this?

- Two radicals cannot be added
- The two radicands vanish at different points
- The common domain is empty
- Every square root is strictly positive

## Explanation

Both terms would have to vanish at the same point. The first requires $x=-3$ and the second $x=1$. Their common domain, $x\ge1$, contains no value making both zero.
