Before solving $\sqrt x=2-x$, what observation explains why it has at most one solution?

- Both sides increase
- On the domain the root increases and the line decreases
- Squaring always gives a line
- The domain has one point

## Explanation

For $x\ge0$, the square root strictly increases while $2-x$ decreases. If they agree at one point, then the root is greater to its right and smaller to its left. They cannot meet again. In fact, at $x=1$ both sides equal $1$. Solutions of the squared equation do not conflict with this reasoning, since a candidate with the wrong sign is not an intersection.
