How many solutions does $\sqrt{x+4}=|x|$ have?

- $1$
- $0$
- $4$
- $2$

## Explanation

Both sides are nonnegative on the domain $x\ge-4$. Squaring gives $x^2-x-4=0$, with discriminant $17$ and two distinct roots. Every root of that quadratic satisfies $x+4=x^2\ge0$, so it belongs to the domain automatically. The negative root is allowed because the right side is $|x|$. Replacing it by $x$ would change the sign condition and reject that solution.
