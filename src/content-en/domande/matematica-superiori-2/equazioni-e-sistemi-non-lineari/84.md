A graph shows the curves $y=x^2$ and $y=|x|$. Which step finds all their intersections by solving one equation in a **nonnegative** variable?

::grafico{f="x^2;abs(x)" xmin=-2.2 xmax=2.2 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.8 uguale=1 titolo="Heights of the parabola and the absolute-value graph"}

- Set $t=|x|$ and solve $t^2=t$ with $t\ge0$
- Immediately divide by $|x|$ while retaining all points
- Require $x^2=-x$ for every horizontal coordinate
- Replace $|x|$ by $x$ at every horizontal coordinate

## Explanation

For every real $x$, $x^2=|x|^2$. The roots $t=0$ and $t=1$ correspond to $x=0$ and $x=\pm1$. There are three intersections, including the origin.
