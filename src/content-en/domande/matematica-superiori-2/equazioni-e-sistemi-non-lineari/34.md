Which step shows that $\sqrt{x+9}-\sqrt x=4$ has no real solutions?

- The radicand $x+9$ must be negative
- It gives $8\sqrt x=-7$ on domain $x\ge0$
- It gives $8\sqrt x=7$ on domain $x\ge0$
- Radicals with different radicands cannot be subtracted

## Explanation

Isolation gives $\sqrt{x+9}=4+\sqrt x$. The right side is positive for every $x\ge0$, so squaring is reversible. This gives $x+9=x+8\sqrt x+16$, or $8\sqrt x=-7$. The left side is nonnegative, making the equality impossible. Squaring again would erase the incompatible sign and produce an extraneous candidate.
