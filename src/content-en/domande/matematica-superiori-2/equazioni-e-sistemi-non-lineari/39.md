What value solves $\sqrt{(x-1)^2}=x+1$?

- $-1$
- $1$
- $0$
- $2$

## Explanation

The radical is $|x-1|$, so both branches must be considered. For $x\ge1$, equality becomes $x-1=x+1$, which is impossible. For $x<1$, we get $1-x=x+1$, so $x=0$. This value belongs to the branch and makes the right side nonnegative. Substituting in the original gives $\sqrt{(-1)^2}=1$, the required value.
