Why do you check an equation with a radical after squaring?

- $\text{Squaring can introduce extraneous roots}$
- $\text{Squaring always changes the domain to all real numbers}$
- $\text{Every negative root is always valid}$
- $\text{Squaring always removes a valid root}$

## Explanation

Opposite numbers have the same square. An equality that is false because of signs can therefore become true after squaring. Substitution into the original equation removes candidates that fail the domain or sign condition.
