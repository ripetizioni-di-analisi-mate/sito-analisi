To solve $\sqrt{x+4}=x-2$ both sides are squared, giving $x+4=(x-2)^2$. Which condition must be added so that the solutions of the new equation are exactly the solutions of the original one?

- $x\ge2$
- $x\ge-4$
- $x\ne2$
- $x\le2$

## Explanation

The left-hand side $\sqrt{x+4}$ is non-negative, so the original equation also requires $x-2\geq0$, that is $x\geq2$. Every solution of $x+4=(x-2)^2$ makes the radicand equal to a square, so the condition $x+4\geq0$ is already satisfied. If $x\geq2$ also holds, the two sides of the original equation are non-negative and have the same square, so they are equal. For example, the solutions of $x+4=(x-2)^2$ are $x=0$ and $x=5$, and the condition $x\geq2$ keeps only $x=5$, for which $\sqrt9=3=5-2$.
