How many real solutions does $\sqrt{x+4}=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x+4}}$ have?

- $1$
- $2$
- Infinitely many
- $0$

## Explanation

The denominator radical must be defined and nonzero, so $x>-4$. On this domain we can multiply by $\sqrt{x+4}$. The product on the left becomes $x+4$, and the right becomes $x$. The relation $x+4=x$ is impossible. The endpoint $-4$ cannot be added because it would give division by zero in the original form.
