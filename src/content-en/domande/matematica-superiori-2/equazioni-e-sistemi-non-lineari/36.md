Why does $\sqrt{x+4}+\sqrt x=1$ have no real solutions?

- The domain is empty.
- The sum of two radicals is always negative.
- The value $x=0$ is excluded from the domain.
- On the domain, the left side is at least $2$.

## Explanation

The domain is $x\ge0$. The radicand $x+4$ is at least $4$, so $\sqrt{x+4}\ge2$. Since $\sqrt x$ is nonnegative, the sum is at least $2$ and cannot equal $1$.
