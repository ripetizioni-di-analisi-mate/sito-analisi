Given $x+y=6$ and $x^2+y^2=26$, what is $(x-y)^2$?

- $10$
- $4$
- $36$
- $16$

## Explanation

Adding the squares of the sum and difference gives $2(x^2+y^2)$, because the terms $2xy$ and $-2xy$ cancel. Thus $(x-y)^2=2\cdot26-6^2=16$. The value $4$ is the magnitude of the difference rather than its square. For example, $1$ and $5$ satisfy the data and are four units apart in either order.
