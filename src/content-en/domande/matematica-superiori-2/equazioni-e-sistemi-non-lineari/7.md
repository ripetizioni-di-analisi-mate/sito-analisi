Solve $\begin{cases}x+y=15\\x^2+y^2=113\end{cases}$.

- $\{\left(7;\,8\right)\}$
- $\{\left(-7;\,-8\right)\}$
- $\{\left(7;\,8\right);\, \left(8;\,7\right)\}$
- $\varnothing$

## Explanation

Substituting the linear relation into the quadratic equation gives an equation of degree two. Each value of $x$ determines exactly one value of $y$. Check the pairs in the original equations.

$$
y=15-x
$$

$$
x^2+(15-x)^2=113
$$

$$
2(x-7)(x-8)=0
$$
