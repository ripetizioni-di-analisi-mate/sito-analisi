For the system $x+y=5$, $x^2+y^2=13$, you already know that $(2;3)$ is a solution. What guarantees that $(3;2)$ is also a solution, without recalculating?

- Squares always have two distinct roots
- It is enough that the first equation is linear
- Both equations stay the same when $x$ and $y$ are swapped
- Every system of two equations has two solutions

## Explanation

Swapping the entries leaves both $x+y$ and $x^2+y^2$ unchanged. The swapped pair therefore satisfies both constraints. It is a distinct pair because $2\ne3$.
