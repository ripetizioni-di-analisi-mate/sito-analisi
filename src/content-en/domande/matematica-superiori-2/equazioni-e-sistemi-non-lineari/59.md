Which number solves $\dfrac1{\sqrt{2x+5}}=2$, taking the denominator restriction into account?

- $-\dfrac52$
- $-\dfrac{19}{8}$
- $-\dfrac12$
- $\dfrac{19}{8}$

## Explanation

The radicand must be strictly positive, so $x>-\dfrac52$. Multiplying by the denominator and dividing by $2$ gives $\sqrt{2x+5}=\dfrac12$. Squaring gives $2x+5=\dfrac14$, hence $x=-\dfrac{19}{8}$. At that point the radical equals $\dfrac12$ and its reciprocal equals $2$. The boundary $-\dfrac52$ is excluded because it would give division by zero.
