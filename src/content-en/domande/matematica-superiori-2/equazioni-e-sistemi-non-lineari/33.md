In $\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x-2}=3$, isolate $\sqrt{x+1}$. Which interval retains the domain and sign before squaring?

- $[2,11]$
- $[2,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,11]$
- $[2,9]$

## Explanation

The common domain is $x\ge2$. The side opposite the isolated radical is $3-\sqrt{x-2}$, which must be nonnegative. From $\sqrt{x-2}\le3$ we get $x-2\le9$, hence $x\le11$. Keep endpoint $11$ because the right side may be zero. Solving in the interval gives $x=3$, where the original radicals are $2$ and $1$.
