The first equation of a system is $2x+y=7$. Which second equation makes the pair $(2;3)$ the only solution of the system?

- $4x+2y=14$
- $x-y=-1$
- $x-y=1$
- $2x+y=8$

## Explanation

The pair $(2;3)$ satisfies the first equation, because $2\cdot2+3=7$. The second equation must be satisfied by the pair and its coefficients of $x$ and $y$ must not be proportional to those of the first, otherwise the system would have infinitely many solutions or none. The equation $x-y=-1$ is satisfied, because $2-3=-1$, and its coefficients $1$ and $-1$ are not proportional to $2$ and $1$. Therefore the system formed by $2x+y=7$ and $x-y=-1$ has the unique solution $(2;3)$. The equation $4x+2y=14$ is twice the first and would give infinitely many solutions, while $2x+y=8$ would give an inconsistent system.
