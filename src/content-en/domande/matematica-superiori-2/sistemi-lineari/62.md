For which $k$ is the system $\dfrac{x}{k-3}+y=1$, $x+y=3$ defined and uniquely solvable?

- $\mathbb R\setminus\{3\}$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{4\}$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{3,4\}$
- $\{3,4\}$

## Explanation

The denominator first requires $k\ne3$. In the allowed cases both rows have coefficient one on $y$. The first coefficient on $x$ equals the second when $\dfrac1{k-3}=1$, giving $k=4$. There the two constants differ and the system has no solution. In the other cases the lines meet once.
