To eliminate $y$ by adding the rows $2x-4y=6$ and $-x+6y=-1$, by which smallest positive integer factors should you multiply the first and second rows?

- $(2;3)$
- $(1;1)$
- $(3;2)$
- $(6;4)$

## Explanation

The least common multiple of four and six is twelve. To reach that magnitude, multiply $-4$ by three and $6$ by two. This gives $-12$ and $12$, which cancel when the rows are added. Six and four also cancel the terms, but they are larger than the required smallest integer factors.
