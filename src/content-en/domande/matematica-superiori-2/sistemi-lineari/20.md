Adding $x+y=5$ and $x-y=1$ gives $2x=6$. What happens if you keep only two copies of $2x=6$?

- Exactly the original solution is preserved.
- The system becomes inconsistent.
- Only one vertical coordinate is fixed and every horizontal coordinate stays free.
- Solutions are added because the second coordinate becomes free.

## Explanation

The sum keeps the consequence $x=3$, but by itself leaves $y$ free. For example, $(3;0)$ satisfies both copies of the sum and fails $x+y=5$. An equivalent transformation replaces only one row and retains an original row. That row gives $y=2$.
