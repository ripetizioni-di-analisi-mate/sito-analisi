One solution of $x-y=3$ and $x+y=1$ is $(2;-1)$. What happens if you reverse the coordinates?

- Both equations remain true.
- Only the second equation remains true.
- Only the first equation remains true.
- Both equations become false.

## Explanation

Reversing the order gives $(-1;2)$. The sum remains one because exchanging the addends does not change it. The difference becomes $-1-2=-3$, which is different from three. The reversed pair lies on the second line and outside the first, so it does not solve the system.
