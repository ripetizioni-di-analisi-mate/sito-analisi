The two equations of a system solved using Cramer's rule are swapped. What happens to the solution?

- The two coordinates are swapped.
- Both coordinates change sign.
- The first coordinate becomes zero.
- It stays the same.

## Explanation

Swapping the equations swaps the rows of each determinant $D$, $D_x$, and $D_y$. All three change sign, so the ratios $\dfrac{D_x}{D}$ and $\dfrac{D_y}{D}$ keep the same values.
