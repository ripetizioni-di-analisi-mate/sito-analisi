Consider $kx+y=1$ and $x+ky=1$. How do the systems differ for $k=1$ and $k=-1$?

- Infinitely many solutions for $k=1$ and none for $k=-1$.
- No solutions for $k=1$ and infinitely many for $k=-1$.
- Infinitely many solutions in both cases.
- One solution in both cases.

## Explanation

The determinant is $k^2-1$, so it vanishes in both proposed cases. At $k=1$ both equations are copies of $x+y=1$. At $k=-1$ their left sides are negatives of each other, but both constants are one. Adding gives the contradiction $0=2$. The two zero-determinant cases therefore have different outcomes.
