In the system formed by $2x+y=8$ and $x-y=1$, the first equation is kept and the second is replaced by an equation obtained from the two. Which replacement gives a system that is *not* equivalent to the original one?

- The sum of the two equations, $3x=9$
- Twice the first equation, $4x+2y=16$
- The second equation minus the first, $-x-2y=-7$
- Three times the second equation, $3x-3y=3$

## Explanation

The original system has the unique solution $(3;2)$, because adding the equations gives $3x=9$, so $x=3$ and $y=8-2\cdot3=2$. If the new equation contains the second equation multiplied by a nonzero number, then $x-y=1$ can be recovered from it and the first equation, so the system remains equivalent. The sum, the difference and three times the second equation satisfy this condition. Twice the first equation does not contain the second one, so the system reduces to the single equation $2x+y=8$, which has infinitely many solutions. Hence the replacement with $4x+2y=16$ gives a system that is not equivalent to the original one.
