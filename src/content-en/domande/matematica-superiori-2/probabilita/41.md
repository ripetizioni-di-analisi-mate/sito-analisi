What assumption allows us to take the ratio of favorable to possible outcomes?

- $\text{Only two outcomes}$
- $\text{Events that are always independent}$
- $\text{A finite set of equally likely outcomes}$
- $\text{Infinitely many trials}$

## Explanation

In the classical model, each elementary outcome must have the same probability. The outcome space must also be finite. Without equal likelihood, the number of outcomes alone does not determine the probability.

A bag with three green tiles and one yellow tile gives the colors different probabilities, but a uniform draw gives each tile the same probability. The ratio therefore counts tiles rather than colors.
