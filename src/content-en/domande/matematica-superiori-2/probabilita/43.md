How many equally likely elementary outcomes are there when rolling two distinguishable fair dice independently?

- $36$
- $12$
- $11$
- $6$

## Explanation

Each result on the first die can be paired with six results on the second. There are therefore six times six ordered pairs. The possible sums are fewer and have different probabilities.

In particular, $(1;6)$ and $(6;1)$ have the same sum but occupy different places on the grid. Count each diagonal pair, such as $(3;3)$, just once.
