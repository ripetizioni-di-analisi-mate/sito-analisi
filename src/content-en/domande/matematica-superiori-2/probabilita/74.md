With $P(B)>0$, the probabilities are $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac3{10}$ and $P(A)=\dfrac3{10}$. What follows?

- $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive.
- $P(B)=\dfrac3{10}$
- $P(A\cup B)=1$
- $A$ and $B$ are independent.

## Explanation

Multiply both sides of $P(A\mid B)=P(A)$ by $P(B)$. The left side becomes $P(A\cap B)$ and the right side becomes $P(A)P(B)$. This is the defining relation for independence. The condition tells us how the probability of $A$ behaves given $B$ and leaves the numerical value of $P(B)$ undetermined.
