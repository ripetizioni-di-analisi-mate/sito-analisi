What does it mean for two events to be mutually exclusive?

- $\text{They always have equal probability}$
- $\text{They cannot occur together}$
- $\text{They are always independent}$
- $\text{Both are certain}$

## Explanation

Mutually exclusive events have an empty intersection. No outcome belongs to both events. Their probabilities may differ, and mutual exclusivity is a different property from independence.

On a die, “roll a one” and “roll a two” are mutually exclusive. The intersection has weight zero, although the product of the probabilities is $\dfrac1{36}$, so the events are not independent.
