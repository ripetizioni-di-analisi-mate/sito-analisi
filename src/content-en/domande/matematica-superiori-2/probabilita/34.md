Suppose $P(A)=\dfrac35$, $P(B)=\dfrac12$, and $P(A\cup B)=\dfrac45$. What is $P(A\cap B)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{3}{10}$

## Explanation

The union formula gives the overlap as the sum of the two individual probabilities minus the union. Over denominator ten the three values are $6$, $5$, and $8$ tenths. The difference is $\dfrac3{10}$. The overlap is smaller than both $\dfrac35$ and $\dfrac12$, as it must be because it is contained in both events.
