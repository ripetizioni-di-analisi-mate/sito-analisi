Someone claims that two mutually exclusive events have $P(A)=\dfrac34$ and $P(B)=\dfrac23$. What does checking the claim reveal?

- The data are impossible because the sum exceeds one.
- The data are possible because both values are less than one.
- The data require the union to have probability zero.
- The data require the events to be independent too.

## Explanation

For mutually exclusive events the intersection is empty, so their probabilities add directly. Here $\dfrac34+\dfrac23=\dfrac{17}{12}$, which exceeds one. The union is part of the sample space and can have probability at most one. The three conditions cannot hold together.
