For which integers $n$ does the equality $\sqrt[3]{n^3+1}=n+1$ hold?

- Only $n=0$
- All integers
- No integer
- $n=-1$ or $n=0$

## Explanation

The cube root is defined for every real number, and two real numbers are equal if and only if their cubes are equal. Therefore the equality is equivalent to $n^3+1=(n+1)^3$. Since $(n+1)^3=n^3+3n^2+3n+1$, the equation becomes $3n^2+3n=0$, that is $3n(n+1)=0$. A product is zero if and only if one of its factors is zero, so the equality holds for $n=-1$ or $n=0$. Indeed, for $n=-1$ we get $\sqrt[3]{0}=0$.
