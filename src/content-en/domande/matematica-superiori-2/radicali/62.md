The numerator and denominator of $\dfrac1{x+2}$ are both multiplied by $\sqrt x$. What is the natural domain of the new fraction before simplification?

- $[0,+\infty)$
- $(0,+\infty)$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,0\}$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{-2\}$

## Explanation

The new expression is $\dfrac{\sqrt x}{(x+2)\sqrt x}$. The root exists from zero onwards, and the denominator also requires it to be nonzero. This leaves $x>0$. On that interval $x+2$ is positive. For example, the input $x=-1$ is allowed in the original fraction and is lost when multiplying by the root.
