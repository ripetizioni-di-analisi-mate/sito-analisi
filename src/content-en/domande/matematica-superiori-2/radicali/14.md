For which values of $x$ does the real root $\sqrt{1-x^2}$ exist?

- $(-1,1)$
- $[1,+\infty)$
- $[-1,1]$
- $(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The square root requires $1-x^2\ge0$, which is $x^2\le1$. The square is at most one exactly when the distance from $x$ to zero is at most one. This gives the interval $[-1,1]$. Both endpoints make the radicand zero and are allowed. At $x=-2$, for example, the radicand is $-3$ and the root is not real.
