How does $\sqrt{(x+1)^2}-\sqrt{(x-1)^2}$ simplify when $-1<x<1$?

- $-2x$
- $2$
- $2x$
- $0$

## Explanation

The first radical is $|x+1|$ and the second is $|x-1|$. On the given interval, $x+1$ is positive and $x-1$ is negative. This gives $(x+1)-(1-x)=2x$. For example, at $x=\dfrac12$ the two roots equal $\dfrac32$ and $\dfrac12$. Their difference is one, which agrees with the simplified form. Substituting $-x$ swaps the two roots, so the sign of their difference reverses.
