For a nonnegative integer $m$, what condition is necessary and sufficient for $\sqrt m$ to be rational?

- $m$ is even.
- $m$ is greater than $1$.
- $m$ is divisible by $3$.
- $m$ is a perfect square.

## Explanation

If $\sqrt m=\dfrac pq$ in lowest terms, squaring gives $p^2=mq^2$. Because $m$ is an integer, $q^2$ divides $p^2$. The two squares are coprime, so $q=1$ and $m=p^2$. Conversely, a perfect square has an integer root. For example, $9$ meets the condition, while the even number $2$ has an irrational root. The case $m=0$ is included because it is the square of the integer zero.
