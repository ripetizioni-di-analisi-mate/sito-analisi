What is the value of the sum $\sqrt{7+4\sqrt3}+\sqrt{7-4\sqrt3}$?

- $2\sqrt3$
- $\sqrt{14}$
- $1$
- $4$

## Explanation

Since $(2+\sqrt3)^2=4+4\sqrt3+3=7+4\sqrt3$ and $(2-\sqrt3)^2=4-4\sqrt3+3=7-4\sqrt3$, both radicands are squares. The square root of a square is the absolute value of the base, so the two roots equal $|2+\sqrt3|$ and $|2-\sqrt3|$. Since $\sqrt3<2$, both bases are positive and the roots equal $2+\sqrt3$ and $2-\sqrt3$. Therefore the sum equals $4$. The value $\sqrt{14}$ comes from the mistake of adding the radicands under a single root.
