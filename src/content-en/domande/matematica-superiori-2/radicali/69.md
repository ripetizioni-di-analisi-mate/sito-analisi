Treat $\sqrt2+\sqrt3$ as a single term. What is the product of $(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)+\sqrt5$ and its conjugate $(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)-\sqrt5$?

- $0$
- $2\sqrt6$
- $2\sqrt{15}$
- $5+2\sqrt6$

## Explanation

The product is $(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)^2-(\sqrt5)^2$. The first square is $2+3+2\sqrt6$, including the mixed term from the two roots in the group. Subtracting $5$ leaves $2\sqrt6$. If that product is a denominator, one more step is needed. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $\sqrt6$ gives denominator $12$.
