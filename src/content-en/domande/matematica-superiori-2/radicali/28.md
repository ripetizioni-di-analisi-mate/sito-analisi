Which form of $\dfrac1{\sqrt x}$ removes the radical from the denominator and preserves the exact domain?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt x}x$ for $x>0$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt x}x$ for $x\ge0$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt x}{x^2}$ for $x>0$
- $\dfrac1x$ for $x>0$

## Explanation

For the root in the denominator to be real and nonzero, we need $x>0$. On this set, multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt x$. The denominator becomes $(\sqrt x)^2=x$ and the numerator becomes $\sqrt x$. At $x=4$ both forms give $\dfrac12$, and at $x=0$ both have denominator zero. The candidate $\dfrac1x$ would instead give $\dfrac14$ at that same input $x=4$.
