A line of slope $-2$ meets the horizontal axis at $(3;0)$. What is its intercept on the vertical axis?

- $6$
- $-6$
- $3$
- $-2$

## Explanation

The line has slope $-2$, so its slope-intercept equation is $y=-2x+q$, where $q$ is the intercept on the vertical axis. The point $(3;0)$ lies on the line, therefore substituting its coordinates gives $0=-2\cdot3+q$, hence $q=6$. The same value is also found by moving from $(3;0)$ three units to the left, up to the vertical axis. Since the slope is $-2$, during this move the $y$-coordinate increases by $2\cdot3=6$ units, so the line meets the vertical axis at the point $(0;6)$. The answer $-6$ comes from a sign error in solving $0=-6+q$.
