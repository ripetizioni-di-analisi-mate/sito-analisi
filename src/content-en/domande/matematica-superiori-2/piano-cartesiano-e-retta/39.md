Which point lies on the line $2x-3y+4=0$?

- $(1;1)$
- $(0;4)$
- $(2;2)$
- $(-2;0)$

## Explanation

Setting $y=0$ gives $2x+4=0$, so $(-2;0)$ lies on both the line and the horizontal axis. To check the other points, substitute both coordinates. The expression equals $3$ at $(1;1)$, $-8$ at $(0;4)$, and $2$ at $(2;2)$. The equation requires zero, so none of these three points belongs. The nonzero coefficient of $x$ makes the intersection with the horizontal axis unique.
