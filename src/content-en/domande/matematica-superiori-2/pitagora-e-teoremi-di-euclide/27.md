A square has side length $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a rectangle has sides $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Both have perimeter $20\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the difference between the squares of their diagonals?

- $0\,\mathrm{cm}^2$
- $1\,\mathrm{cm}^2$
- $2\,\mathrm{cm}^2$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}^2$

## Explanation

For each figure, the square of the diagonal is the sum of the squares of its adjacent sides. The equal perimeter fixes the sum of the lengths. The sums of the squares, however, differ.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_2^2&=4^2+6^2=52 \\[5pt]
d_1^2&=5^2+5^2=50 \\[5pt]
\left|52-50\right|&=2\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$
