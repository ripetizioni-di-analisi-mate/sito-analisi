Let $ABC$ be a right triangle with the right angle at $A$, and let $H$ be the foot of the altitude to $BC$. The lengths $BH=3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $HC=12\,\mathrm{cm}$ are the projections of the legs. Which equality justifies $AH=6\,\mathrm{cm}$?

- $AH^2=BH\cdot HC$
- $AH^2=BC\cdot BH$
- $AH^2=BH^2+HC^2$
- $AH^2=HC^2-BH^2$

## Explanation

Segment $AH$ is perpendicular to the hypotenuse, so it is the altitude that Euclid’s second theorem applies to. The altitude has square $36$, with positive square root $6$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AH^2&=3\cdot12=36
\end{aligned}
$$
