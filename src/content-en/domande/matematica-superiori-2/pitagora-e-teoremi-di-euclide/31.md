A right triangle has integer side lengths and an even hypotenuse. Could one of its legs be odd?

- Yes, provided the other leg is even.
- Yes, because two odd numbers always sum to a multiple of $4$.
- No. Both leg squares would have to be odd, and their sum would leave remainder $2$ on division by $4$.
- No, because no right triangle has an even hypotenuse.

## Explanation

The hypotenuse squared is a multiple of $4$. If one leg were odd, the other would also have to be odd for the sum to be even. Every odd square has the form $(2k+1)^2=4k(k+1)+1$, so their sum would leave remainder $2$.
