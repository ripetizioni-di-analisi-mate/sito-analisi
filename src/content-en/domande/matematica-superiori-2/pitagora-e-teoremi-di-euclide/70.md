For a right triangle with two positive legs, can a leg have the same length as its projection?

- Yes, when the projections are equal
- Yes, when the leg is the longer one
- Yes, in every right triangle
- No, because the leg would have to equal the hypotenuse

## Explanation

Write $a$ for the leg and $p$ for its projection. If $p=a$, the equality $a^2=cp$ becomes $a^2=ca$. Since $a$ is positive, divide by $a$ to get $a=c$. This leaves zero for the square of the other leg, violating the hypothesis. Even in an isosceles right triangle, each projection is shorter than its leg.
