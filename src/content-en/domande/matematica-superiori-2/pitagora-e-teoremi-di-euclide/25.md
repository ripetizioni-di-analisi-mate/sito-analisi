To find the diagonal of a rectangle with sides $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, a student proposes $\sqrt{6^2-8^2}$. Which step corrects this setup?

- Add $6^2$ and $8^2$.
- Calculate $8^2-6^2$.
- Add $6$ and $8$, then square the sum.
- Multiply $6^2$ by $8^2$.

## Explanation

In the triangle formed by the diagonal and two sides of the rectangle, the diagonal is the hypotenuse. Its square is therefore the sum of the squares of the two sides.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^2&=36+64=100 \\[5pt]
d&=10\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$
