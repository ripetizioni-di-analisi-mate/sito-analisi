Can a right triangle have a hypotenuse of $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a leg of $8\,\mathrm{cm}$?

- No, the square of the other leg would be negative.
- Yes, the other leg would measure $1$ centimetre.
- Yes, the other leg would measure $\sqrt{15}$ centimetres.
- Yes, the other leg would measure $\sqrt{113}$ centimetres.

## Explanation

The Pythagorean theorem requires the square of the unknown leg to be $7^2-8^2$. The difference is negative, and no real square can have that value. The two given lengths are therefore incompatible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^2&=49-64=-15
\end{aligned}
$$
