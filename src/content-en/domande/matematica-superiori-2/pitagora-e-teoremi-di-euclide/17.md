The sides of a triangular board measure $3$, $4$ and $5$ cm to the nearest centimetre. Do the readings prove that the board has a right angle?

- No. Actual sides of $3$, $4$ and $5.1$ cm would give the same readings.
- Yes. Rounding always preserves the relation between squares.
- Yes. The two shorter sides only need to sum to more than the longest.
- No. Integer side lengths cannot form a right triangle.

## Explanation

The converse requires equality between squares of the actual side lengths. In the counterexample $3^2+4^2=25$, while $5.1^2=26.01$.
