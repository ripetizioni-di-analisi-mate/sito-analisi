A right triangle has hypotenuse $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the greatest possible altitude to it?

- $4\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Let $p$ and $q$ be the projections of the legs on the hypotenuse, so $p+q=10$. By Euclid’s second theorem the altitude $h$ to the hypotenuse satisfies $h^2=pq$. The identity $4pq=(p+q)^2-(p-q)^2$ holds and the square $(p-q)^2$ is never negative, therefore

$$
4h^2=4pq=100-(p-q)^2\le100 .
$$

Hence $h^2\le25$, that is $h\le5\,\mathrm{cm}$, and equality holds for $p=q=5$, that is when the triangle is isosceles. The greatest altitude is therefore $5\,\mathrm{cm}$.
