A rhombus has sides of $17$ cm and one diagonal of $30$ cm. What is its area?

- $60$ cm$^2$
- $240$ cm$^2$
- $120$ cm$^2$
- $480$ cm$^2$

## Explanation

The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular and bisect each other, so the rhombus consists of four congruent right triangles. Each of them has the side, $17$ cm long, as its hypotenuse and half of the given diagonal, that is $15$ cm, as one leg. By the Pythagorean theorem the other leg measures $\sqrt{17^2-15^2}=\sqrt{64}=8$ cm, so the second diagonal measures $16$ cm. The area of a rhombus is half the product of its diagonals, so it equals $\dfrac{30\cdot16}{2}=240$ cm$^2$.
