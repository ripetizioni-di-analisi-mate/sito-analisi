Two points on the same side of a straight wall are $3$ and $5$ metres from it. The feet of the perpendiculars from the points to the wall are $6$ metres apart. How long is the shortest route from the first point to the second that touches the wall?

- $8$ m
- $10$ m
- $14$ m
- $\sqrt{40}$ m

## Explanation

Reflect the second point in the wall. For every possible contact point the route keeps its length. In the reflected diagram the shortest route is a straight segment with components $6$ and $3+5=8$ metres. Pythagoras gives $\sqrt{36+64}=10$ metres.
