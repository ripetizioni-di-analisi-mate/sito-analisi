Two points lie on the same side of a straight wall, at $3$ m and at $5$ m from it respectively. The feet of their perpendiculars to the wall are $6$ m apart. What is the minimum length of a path from the first point to the second that touches the wall?

- $8$ m
- $10$ m
- $14$ m
- $\sqrt{40}$ m

## Explanation

Let $A$ and $B$ be the two points and $B'$ the reflection of $B$ in the line of the wall. Every point $T$ of the wall is at the same distance from $B$ and from $B'$, so the path $AT+TB$ has the same length as $AT+TB'$. This sum is smallest when $T$ lies on the segment $AB'$, therefore the shortest path measures $AB'$. The points $A$ and $B'$ lie on opposite sides of the wall, so in the direction perpendicular to the wall they are $3+5=8$ m apart, while along the wall they are $6$ m apart. By the Pythagorean theorem

$$
AB'=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=\sqrt{100}=10\,\mathrm m .
$$

The answer $\sqrt{40}$ m is the direct distance $AB$, which does not touch the wall.
