In a right triangle the two projections onto the hypotenuse are equal. What follows for the altitude to the hypotenuse?

- It is as long as their sum
- It is as long as either projection
- It is twice the length of each projection
- It is half the length of each projection

## Explanation

Setting $p=q$, the second theorem gives $h^2=p^2$. Both lengths are positive, so $h=p=q$. The sum of projections is $2p$, the hypotenuse. Here the altitude's foot is the midpoint and the altitude is as long as possible for the given hypotenuse. The two legs are equal by the first theorem.
