In a right triangle the altitude to the hypotenuse divides the hypotenuse into two segments of $9$ and $16$ cm. In the smaller of the two right triangles obtained in this way, the altitude to its hypotenuse is drawn. What is the length of this new altitude?

- $12$ cm
- $\dfrac{36}{5}$ cm
- $\dfrac{48}{5}$ cm
- $\dfrac{27}{5}$ cm

## Explanation

By Euclid’s second theorem the altitude of the original triangle measures $\sqrt{9\cdot16}=12$ cm. The smaller triangle is right-angled, its legs are the segment of $9$ cm and this altitude of $12$ cm, and its hypotenuse is the shorter leg of the original triangle, which by the Pythagorean theorem measures $\sqrt{9^2+12^2}=15$ cm. The two expressions of its area must agree, so the altitude $h$ to its hypotenuse satisfies $15\,h=9\cdot12$. Therefore

$$
h=\frac{108}{15}=\frac{36}{5}\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

The answer $\dfrac{48}{5}$ cm is the corresponding altitude in the larger triangle, whose legs are $16$ and $12$ cm.
