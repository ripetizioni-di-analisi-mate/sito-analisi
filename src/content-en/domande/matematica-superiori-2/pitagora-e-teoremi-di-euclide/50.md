The points in the figure are $P=(-3;4)$, $Q=(5;-2)$ and $C=(5;4)$. What is the radius of the circle through all three?

::grafico{xmin=-4.2 xmax=5.9 ymin=-3.15 ymax=5.2 punti="-3,4,pieno,P;5,-2,pieno,Q;5,4,pieno,C" segmenti="-3,4,5,4;5,4,5,-2" linee="-3,4 5,-2" titolo="Right angle at $C$ with $P$ and $Q$ on its two arms"}

- $5$
- $10$
- $7$
- $4$

## Explanation

The segment $PC$ is horizontal, because $P$ and $C$ have the same $y$-coordinate, and the segment $CQ$ is vertical, because $C$ and $Q$ have the same $x$-coordinate. Triangle $PCQ$ is therefore right-angled at $C$, and the circle through its vertices has the hypotenuse $PQ$ as a diameter. The legs measure $|5-(-3)|=8$ and $|4-(-2)|=6$, so by the Pythagorean theorem

$$
PQ=\sqrt{8^2+6^2}=\sqrt{100}=10 .
$$

The radius is half the diameter, hence it measures $5$.
