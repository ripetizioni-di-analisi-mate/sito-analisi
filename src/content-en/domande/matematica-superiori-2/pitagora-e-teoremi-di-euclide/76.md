A ladder $5$ m long, leaning against a vertical wall, reaches a point $4$ m above the floor. The same point is to be reached with the foot of the ladder $1$ m further from the wall. How long must the new ladder be?

- $4\sqrt2$ m
- $8$ m
- $\sqrt{17}$ m
- $\sqrt{41}$ m

## Explanation

The ladder, the wall and the floor form a right triangle with the right angle at the foot of the wall. By the Pythagorean theorem the foot of the ladder is $\sqrt{5^2-4^2}=3$ m from the wall. In the new position the foot is $3+1=4$ m from the wall and the height is still $4$ m, so the new ladder is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs of $4$ m, and its length is

$$
\sqrt{4^2+4^2}=\sqrt{32}=4\sqrt2\,\mathrm m .
$$

The answer $8$ m comes from adding the two legs $4+4$ instead of applying the Pythagorean theorem.
