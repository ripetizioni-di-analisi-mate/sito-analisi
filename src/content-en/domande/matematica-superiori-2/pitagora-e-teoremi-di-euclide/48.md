A bar joins the endpoints of two successive sections. Given only the section lengths, what fact justifies adding their squares to obtain the square of the bar length?

- The sections have equal lengths
- The two sections are perpendicular to each other
- The bar touches the endpoints of the sections
- At least one section is horizontal

## Explanation

Adding the squares works when the two sections are legs of the same right triangle. The decisive condition is the right angle at their meeting point. Knowing only their lengths, even if they agree, leaves the included angle and thus the distance between the endpoints undetermined. Being horizontal is a property of just one section.
