Why is $M$, the intersection of a rectangle's diagonals, the centre of the circle through all its vertices?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-4.35 xmax=4.35 ymin=-4.35 ymax=4.35 curve="3.605551275*cos(t),3.605551275*sin(t),0,6.28318530718" linee="-3,-2 3,-2 3,2 -3,2 -3,-2" segmenti="-3,-2,3,2;3,-2,-3,2" punti="-3,-2;3,-2;3,2;-3,2;0,0" etichette="-3.27,-2.35,A;3.25,-2.35,B;3.25,2.35,C;-3.25,2.35,D;0.29,-0.35,M" titolo="The rectangle’s diagonals meet at $M$"}

- The diagonals have equal length and share a midpoint
- The diagonals are always perpendicular
- All four sides have equal length
- $M$ is the midpoint of a side

## Explanation

Each diagonal has midpoint $M$, so both endpoints of each diagonal are the same distance from $M$. The diagonals also have equal length. Their four halves are therefore equal and form radii of one circle. Perpendicular diagonals are unnecessary and are not a property of every rectangle.
