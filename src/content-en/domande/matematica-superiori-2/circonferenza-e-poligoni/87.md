From a square of side $2R$ the disc of radius $R$ with the same centre as the square is cut out. What fraction of the area of the square is left in the four corners?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $1-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $1-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{4-\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The square has area $(2R)^2=4R^2$ and the disc has area $\pi R^2$. The disc lies inside the square, so the part left in the corners has area $4R^2-\pi R^2=(4-\pi)R^2$. The required fraction is the ratio of this area to the area of the square, therefore

$$
\frac{(4-\pi)R^2}{4R^2}=1-\frac{\pi}{4}.
$$

The answer $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is instead the fraction occupied by the disc.
