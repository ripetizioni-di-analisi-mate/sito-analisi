A student proposes a chord of length $4$ cm whose line is $9$ cm from the centre of a circle of radius $9$ cm. Which check decides whether it is possible?

- The length is below the diameter, which suffices
- The chord is shorter than the radius, so its distance equals the radius
- Distance equal to the radius gives a tangent with one common point
- Perpendicularity requires distance zero for every chord

## Explanation

When the distance equals the radius, the foot of the perpendicular is the only common point. A chord of length $4$ cm requires two distinct intersections. The expression under the half-chord square root would be $9^2-9^2=0$. Comparing with the diameter alone checks only one of the two given facts.
