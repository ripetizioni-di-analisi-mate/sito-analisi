A $120^\circ$ arc lies on a circle of radius $4$ cm. Which central angle gives the same arc length on a circle of radius $6$ cm?

- $180^\circ$
- $120^\circ$
- $60^\circ$
- $80^\circ$

## Explanation

The arc length formula shows that keeping the same length in centimetres keeps the product of radius and angular measure fixed. Set $4\cdot120^\circ=6\alpha$, giving $\alpha=80^\circ$. Since the new radius is greater, a smaller fraction of a turn must suffice. An angle of $180^\circ$ would increase both the radius and the fraction.
