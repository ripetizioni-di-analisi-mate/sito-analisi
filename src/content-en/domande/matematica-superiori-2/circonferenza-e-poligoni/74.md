A regular polygon is enlarged, tripling every length. How do its exterior angle and area change?

- Both the angle and the area triple
- The angle triples and the area stays the same
- The angle stays the same and the area becomes nine times as large
- The angle stays the same and the area triples

## Explanation

The exterior angle depends on the side count, which stays the same under enlargement. The area is the product of two lengths, the perimeter and apothem, divided by two. Both triple, so the area is multiplied by nine. The same factor applies to the area of each triangle built from the centre.
