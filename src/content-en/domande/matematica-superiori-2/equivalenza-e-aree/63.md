A rhombus is claimed to have area $40$ and a longer diagonal of length $8$. Which check shows that these data cannot both hold?

- The other diagonal would be $5$, which is too short.
- The diagonals of a rhombus must be congruent.
- The other diagonal would be $10$, which exceeds $8$.
- Diagonals of different lengths cannot be perpendicular.

## Explanation

The formula makes the diagonal product $2\cdot40=80$. If one length is $8$, the other is $80/8=10$. The two lengths could form a rhombus, but $8$ would be the shorter. Alternatively, if neither exceeded $8$, the area would be at most $8\cdot8/2=32$. A positive answer must still be checked against the stated role of the diagonal.
