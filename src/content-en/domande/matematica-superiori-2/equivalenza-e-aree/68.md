Why does any diagonal of a parallelogram divide it into triangles of equal area?

- The diagonal is always perpendicular to the base.
- The two diagonals always have the same length.
- The triangles are congruent: they share the diagonal and their other sides match in opposite pairs.
- Both triangles are always right triangles.

## Explanation

After drawing the diagonal, each triangle contains one side from each pair of opposite sides. The two pairs have matching lengths, and the third side is shared. SSS gives congruence, which implies equal areas. This requires neither a perpendicular diagonal nor right triangles. Drawing the other diagonal gives the same reasoning with the opposite side pairs exchanged.
