A floor $2.5\,\mathrm m\times1.6\,\mathrm m$ has area $4\,\mathrm m^2$. Can four one-metre square tiles cover it without cuts, with their sides parallel to the floor edges?

- No, because the floor sides are not integer multiples of the tile side.
- Yes, because the floor and tiles have equal areas.
- Yes, because every rectangular floor of area $4$ splits into four squares.
- No, because four tiles with side one cover only $2\,\mathrm m^2$.

## Explanation

The four tiles provide enough material by area. With their sides aligned, however, a tile that meets a floor edge along one of its edges contributes one whole metre there. A length of $2.5$ metres cannot be assembled from whole one-metre edges. Boundary compatibility is an extra condition beyond equal total area. The area count alone does not guarantee a tiling.
