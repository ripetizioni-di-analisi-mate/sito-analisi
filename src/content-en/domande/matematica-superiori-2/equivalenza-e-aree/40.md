In trapezoid $ABCD$ the bases are $AB$ and $CD$, and the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ meet at the point $O$. Which pair of triangles certainly has equal areas?

- $AOB$ and $COD$
- $AOB$ and $BOC$
- $AOD$ and $BOC$
- $AOD$ and $COD$

## Explanation

Triangles $ABD$ and $ABC$ have the same base $AB$, and the opposite vertices $D$ and $C$ lie on the line $CD$, parallel to $AB$. The two heights are therefore equal to the distance between the bases of the trapezoid, so triangles $ABD$ and $ABC$ have equal areas. Triangle $ABD$ is made up of $AOB$ and $AOD$, while $ABC$ is made up of $AOB$ and $BOC$. Removing the same part $AOB$ from two figures of equal area leaves figures of equal area, hence triangles $AOD$ and $BOC$ have **equal areas**. Triangles $AOB$ and $COD$ instead have different areas, because their bases $AB$ and $CD$ are different and the height of $AOB$ exceeds that of $COD$ when $AB>CD$.
