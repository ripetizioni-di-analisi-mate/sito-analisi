We seek a real number $x$ that equals its own square. Which observation rules out every number strictly between $0$ and $1$?

- No fraction has a real square
- Multiplying it by itself gives a smaller number
- A fraction squared is always greater than $1$
- The square of a positive number is negative

## Explanation

If $0<x<1$, multiplying $x$ by the positive factor $x<1$ reduces its value. In general $x^2=x$ gives $x(x-1)=0$, with solutions $0$ and $1$.
