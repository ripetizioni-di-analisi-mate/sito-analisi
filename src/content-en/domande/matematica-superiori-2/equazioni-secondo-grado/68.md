Suppose $ax^2+bx+c=0$ has two distinct real roots and $a,b,c>0$. What signs do the roots have?

- Both are negative
- Both are positive
- They have opposite signs
- One is zero

## Explanation

The product $\dfrac ca$ is positive, so the roots are nonzero and have the same sign. The sum $-\dfrac ba$ is negative, so both roots are negative. The stated hypothesis about the roots matters. Positive coefficients alone do not ensure real roots: $x^2+x+1$ has discriminant $-3$. Check existence and distinctness first, then the signs.
