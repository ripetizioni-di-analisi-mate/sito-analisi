A rectangle of area $54\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ has width $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ greater than its height. The standard form is $h^2+3h-54=0$. Why does only one root give valid sides?

- One root gives two negative side lengths
- One root gives a negative area
- One root is double
- One root gives equal side lengths

## Explanation

The factorisation is $(h-6)(h+9)=0$. With $h=6$, the side lengths are the positive values $6$ and $9$. The other root $-9$ gives width $-6$. The product $(-9)(-6)=54$ also satisfies the area equation, but both lengths are outside the problem's domain. The algebraic equation itself has two distinct roots before the measurements are required to be positive.
