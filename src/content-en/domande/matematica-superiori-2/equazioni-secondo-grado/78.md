For $k\ne0$, the equation $kx^2+x-k=0$ always has two distinct real roots. Which argument proves this for every allowed $k$?

- $x=1$ is always a solution
- The coefficient of $x$ is positive
- Every term contains the factor $k$
- The discriminant is $1+4k^2>0$

## Explanation

We have $\Delta=1^2-4k(-k)=1+4k^2$, which is strictly positive. The condition $k\ne0$ ensures that the leading coefficient does not vanish, so the equation remains quadratic.
