How should the solution of $(3x+2)^2=0$ be described?

- Two distinct roots, $-\dfrac23$ and $\dfrac23$
- One distinct root, $-\dfrac23$, of multiplicity two
- One simple root, $-\dfrac23$
- No real roots

## Explanation

A square vanishes when its base is zero. The equation $3x+2=0$ gives $x=-\dfrac23$. Both copies of the factor vanish at the same value, so the root has multiplicity two. The solution set has one element. Expanding gives $9x^2+12x+4$, with discriminant $144-144=0$. The formula gives the same value twice.
