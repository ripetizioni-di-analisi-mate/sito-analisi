Without knowing $b$, what can be said about the roots of $2x^2+bx-3=0$?

- The two roots must be negatives of each other
- There are two real roots with opposite signs
- No $b$ gives real roots
- Zero is always a root

## Explanation

The discriminant is $b^2+24$, strictly positive for any $b$. The product of the roots is $-\dfrac32$, so their signs are opposite. This does not give equal absolute values: the sum is $-\dfrac b2$ and vanishes only when $b=0$. Zero is not a root because the constant is $-3$.
