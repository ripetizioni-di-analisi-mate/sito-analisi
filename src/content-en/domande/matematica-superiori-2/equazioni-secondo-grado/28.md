$P(x)=0$ has a double root $r$, and $P$ is quadratic. What changes when we pass to $-5P(x)=0$?

- The root becomes $-5r$
- The root remains $r$ and is still double
- The root remains $r$ but becomes simple
- The new discriminant is negative

## Explanation

The polynomial has form $a(x-r)^2$ with $a\ne0$. Multiplying by $-5$ changes only the outer factor, giving $-5a(x-r)^2$. The square still vanishes at the same value and the factor still occurs twice. The curve opens the other way, but the point of contact has the same horizontal coordinate. The discriminant is multiplied by $25$ and remains zero.
