The vertex of $y=-2(x+1)^2+3$ is above the horizontal axis. What follows about the zeroes?

- There are no real zeroes
- There is one double zero
- There are two distinct real zeroes
- There are two zeroes, both to the right of $-1$

## Explanation

The coefficient $-2$ makes the parabola open downwards. The vertex $(-1,3)$ is a positive maximum, and far enough from $-1$ the height becomes negative. Algebra gives $(x+1)^2=\dfrac32$, with two distinct values of the group $x+1$. A double zero would require the vertex to lie on the axis. Here the intersections are on either side of the vertex.
