One root of $x^2-4x+1=0$ is $2+\sqrt3$. Which expression gives the other using the sum?

- $-2+\sqrt3$
- $-2-\sqrt3$
- $4-\sqrt3$
- $2-\sqrt3$

## Explanation

The roots sum to $4$, because the linear coefficient is $-4$ and the equation is monic. Thus the other root is $4-(2+\sqrt3)=2-\sqrt3$. The product checks the value: $(2+\sqrt3)(2-\sqrt3)=4-3=1$, the constant term. The radical changes sign, while the shared term stays $2$. The two roots lie on either side of the vertex's horizontal coordinate.
