A classmate is looking for a monic quadratic with integer coefficients, constant term $5$ and a root at $3$. Which check rules it out?

- Every monic polynomial has opposite roots
- No quadratic with integer coefficients can have an odd constant term
- Substituting $3$ would require a linear coefficient of $-14/3$
- $3$ cannot be a root of a monic quadratic

## Explanation

Writing $x^2+bx+5$ and substituting $x=3$ gives $14+3b=0$. The required value of $b$ is not an integer, so the requirements cannot hold together.
