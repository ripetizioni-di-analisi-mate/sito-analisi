Two real roots have sum $6$ and product $9$. What follows from these two facts?

- The roots coincide at $3$
- The roots are distinct and positive
- The roots are $-3$ and $-3$
- One root is zero

## Explanation

The associated monic equation is $t^2-6t+9=0$, or $(t-3)^2=0$. The two roots counted with multiplicity both equal $3$. The formula $(x_1-x_2)^2=6^2-4\cdot9=0$ also shows they cannot be distinct. The sum then fixes their common value. A positive product alone would leave the sign open, but the sum is positive.
