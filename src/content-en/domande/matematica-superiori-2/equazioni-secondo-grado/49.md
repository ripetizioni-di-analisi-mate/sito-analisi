With $a\ne0$, when is zero a double root of $ax^2+bx=0$?

- $b=a$
- $b=0$
- $b=-a$
- $b\ne0$

## Explanation

The factor $x$ always appears once in $x(ax+b)$. For two copies, $ax+b$ must also vanish at $x=0$, requiring $b=0$. This gives $ax^2$. If $b\ne0$, the second root is $-\dfrac ba$, which differs from zero. Thus a nonzero linear term makes zero a simple root.
