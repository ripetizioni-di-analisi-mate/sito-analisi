In the family $x^2+kx+4=0$, replace $k$ by $-k$. If the first equation has a **negative** double root, what happens to that root in the new equation?

- It splits into two opposite roots
- It disappears and there are no real roots
- It stays negative and double
- It becomes positive and remains double

## Explanation

The discriminant $k^2-16$ does not change. With zero discriminant the root is $-k/2$. After replacement it becomes $k/2$, so it has the opposite sign and the same multiplicity.
