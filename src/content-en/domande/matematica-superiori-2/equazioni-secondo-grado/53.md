For which real values of $b$ does $x^2+bx+9=0$ have no real roots?

- $b<6$
- $-6<b<6$
- $b<-6$ or $b>6$
- $-6\le b\le6$

## Explanation

The discriminant is $b^2-36$. For it to be negative, the square of $b$ must be less than $36$, so the absolute value of $b$ must be less than six. This gives $-6<b<6$. The endpoints are excluded because their discriminant is zero. For example, $b=-6$ gives $(x-3)^2=0$, with double root $3$.
