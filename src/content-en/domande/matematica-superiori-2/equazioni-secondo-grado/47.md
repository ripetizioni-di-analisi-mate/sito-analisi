Let $a,b$ be nonzero real numbers with opposite signs. What is the sign of the nonzero root of $ax^2+bx=0$?

- Positive
- Negative
- It depends on which coefficient is negative
- There is no nonzero root

## Explanation

Factoring gives $x(ax+b)=0$. The nonzero root satisfies $ax+b=0$, so it is $-\dfrac ba$. Since $a$ and $b$ have opposite signs, $\dfrac ba$ is negative and its opposite is positive. This also holds when $a$ is negative. For example, the nonzero root of $-3x^2+7x=0$ is $\dfrac73$.
