For which values of $c$ does the pure quadratic $-2x^2+c=0$ have two distinct real roots?

- $c<0$
- $c\ge0$
- $c>0$
- $c\ne0$

## Explanation

Isolating the square gives $x^2=\dfrac c2$. Two distinct roots require a strictly positive right side, so $c>0$. At $c=0$, there is one distinct root, zero. When $c<0$, no real square can reach the required value. The sign of the original coefficient matters when moving the constant and dividing.
