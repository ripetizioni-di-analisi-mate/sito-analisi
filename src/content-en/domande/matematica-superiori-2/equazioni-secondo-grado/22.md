How many positive solutions does $\dfrac{x+1}{x-2}=\dfrac{3}{x(x-2)}$ have?

- $0$
- $2$
- Infinitely many
- $1$

## Explanation

For $x\ne0,2$ we can multiply by $x(x-2)$ to get $x^2+x-3=0$. Its discriminant is $13$ and its root product is $-3$, so one root is positive and one negative. The excluded values are not roots: the polynomial equals $-3$ at $0$ and $3$ at $2$. There is exactly one positive solution, without calculating its value.
