What are the domain $D$ and solution set $S$ of $\dfrac{x^2-x-6}{x-3}=0$?

- $D=\mathbb R,\quad S=\{-2,3\}$
- $D=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2\},\quad S=\{3\}$
- $D=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,3\},\quad S=\varnothing$
- $D=\mathbb R\setminus\{3\},\quad S=\{-2\}$

## Explanation

Only $3$ makes the denominator zero. The numerator factors as $(x-3)(x+2)$, giving candidates $3$ and $-2$. The domain condition removes the first value. At $x=-2$ the numerator is zero and the denominator is $-5$, so the fraction is defined and zero. The other numerator zero is outside the original domain altogether.
