What is the maximum of $f(x, y, z) = \ln x + \ln y + 3\ln z$ on the points of the plane $x + y + z = 5$ with $x > 0$, $y > 0$, $z > 0$?

- $3\ln 3$
- $\ln 3$
- $5\ln\dfrac{5}{3}$
- $3\ln 5$

## Explanation

On the points considered every coordinate is less than $5$, so each logarithm is less than $\ln 5$, and $f$ tends to $-\infty$ when a coordinate tends to $0$. Therefore the maximum exists and is attained at a point with positive coordinates, where the **Lagrange multiplier theorem** holds with $g = x + y + z - 5$. The system gives
$$
\frac{1}{x} = \lambda, \qquad \frac{1}{y} = \lambda, \qquad \frac{3}{z} = \lambda ,
$$
so $y = x$ and $z = 3x$. The constraint becomes $5x = 5$, therefore the only candidate is $(1, 1, 3)$. The **maximum** is thus $f(1, 1, 3) = 0 + 0 + 3\ln 3 = 3\ln 3$.
