All the following functions have a critical point at the origin. For which one is the Hessian matrix at the origin degenerate?

- $f(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2$
- $f(x, y) = x^2 + 2xy + y^2$
- $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3xy + y^2$
- $f(x, y) = x^2 + 2xy + 2y^2$

## Explanation

For a function $ax^2 + bxy + cy^2$ the Hessian matrix is constant and equals
$$
H = \begin{pmatrix} 2a & b \\ b & 2c \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det H = 4ac - b^2 .
$$
For $x^2 + 2xy + y^2$ we have $\det H = 4 - 4 = 0$, so the Hessian is **degenerate**. For the other three functions the determinant equals $3$, $-5$ and $4$ respectively, so it is nonzero. Indeed $x^2 + 2xy + y^2 = (x + y)^2$ vanishes along the whole line $y = -x$.
