A function $f$ of class $C^2$ has a critical point $(x_0, y_0)$ at which $f_{xx} = 4$, $f_{xy} = 2$ and $f_{yy} = 1$. What does the Hessian test say?

- The point is a local minimum point
- The point is a saddle point
- The point is a local maximum point
- The test is inconclusive

## Explanation

The determinant of the Hessian at the point is
$$
\det H = 4 \cdot 1 - 2^2 = 0 ,
$$
so the Hessian has a zero eigenvalue and the **Hessian test** is inconclusive, even though $f_{xx} > 0$. The nature of the point is determined by studying the sign of $f(x, y) - f(x_0, y_0)$ directly. For example, the functions $\dfrac{(2x + y)^2}{2}$ and $\dfrac{(2x + y)^2}{2} - y^4$ both have a critical point at the origin with these second derivatives. For the first one the origin is a minimum point, while for the second one it is a saddle point, because along the line $y = -2x$ the function equals $-y^4$.
