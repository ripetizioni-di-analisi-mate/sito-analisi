Which of the following points is **not** a critical point of $f(x, y) = \sin x \cos y$?

- $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, 0\right)$
- $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$
- $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$
- $\left(\pi, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = \cos x \cos y$ and $f_y = -\sin x \sin y$. At the point $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ we have $f_x = 0$, but
$$
f_y = -\sin\frac{\pi}{2}\,\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = -1 \neq 0 ,
$$
so the point is **not critical**. At the other three points both derivatives vanish: at $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, 0\right)$ because $\cos x = 0$ and $\sin y = 0$, at $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ and at $\left(\pi, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ because $\sin x = 0$ and $\cos y = 0$.
