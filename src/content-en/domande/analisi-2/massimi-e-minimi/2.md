For the function $f(x, y) = x^4 + y^4 - 4xy$, what is the nature of the critical point $(0, 0)$?

- Local minimum point
- Local maximum point
- The Hessian test is inconclusive
- Saddle point

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 4x^3 - 4y$ and $f_y = 4y^3 - 4x$, which are $0$ at the origin, so $(0, 0)$ is a critical point. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 12x^2$, $f_{yy} = 12y^2$ and $f_{xy} = -4$, which at the origin are $0$, $0$ and $-4$. Therefore
$$
\det H(0, 0) = 0 \cdot 0 - (-4)^2 = -16 < 0 .
$$
The determinant is nonzero even though $f_{xx}(0, 0) = 0$, so the criterion applies. Since $\det H < 0$, by the **Hessian test** the origin is a **saddle point**.
