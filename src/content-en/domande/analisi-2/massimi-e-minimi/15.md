The function $f(x, y) = e^{x}\,(x^2 - y^2)$ has two critical points, $(0, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$. Which statement is true?

- $(-2, 0)$ is a local minimum point and $(0, 0)$ is a saddle point
- $(0, 0)$ is a local minimum point and $(-2, 0)$ is a saddle point
- $(-2, 0)$ is a local maximum point and $(0, 0)$ is a saddle point
- $(-2, 0)$ is a local maximum point and $(0, 0)$ is a local minimum point

## Explanation

The first derivatives are $f_x = e^{x}(x^2 - y^2 + 2x)$ and $f_y = -2y\,e^{x}$, so the critical points have $y = 0$ and $x^2 + 2x = 0$, that is $x = 0$ or $x = -2$. The second derivatives are
$$
f_{xx} = e^{x}(x^2 - y^2 + 4x + 2), \qquad f_{xy} = -2y\,e^{x}, \qquad f_{yy} = -2e^{x} .
$$
At $(0, 0)$ we have $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = 0$, $f_{yy} = -2$, so $\det H = -4 < 0$ and the origin is a **saddle point**. At $(-2, 0)$ we have $f_{xx} = -2e^{-2}$, $f_{xy} = 0$, $f_{yy} = -2e^{-2}$, so $\det H = 4e^{-4} > 0$ with $f_{xx} < 0$, therefore $(-2, 0)$ is a **local maximum point**.
