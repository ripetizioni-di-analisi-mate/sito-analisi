For the function $f(x, y) = x^3 + y^3 - 3x - 12y$, which point is a local maximum point?

- $(-1, -2)$
- $(1, 2)$
- $(1, -2)$
- $(-1, 2)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 3x^2 - 3$ and $f_y = 3y^2 - 12$, which vanish for $x = \pm 1$ and $y = \pm 2$, so the critical points are the four points $(\pm 1, \pm 2)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 6x$, $f_{yy} = 6y$ and $f_{xy} = 0$, therefore $\det H = 36xy$. At $(1, -2)$ and at $(-1, 2)$ we have $\det H < 0$, so these two points are saddle points. At $(1, 2)$ we have $\det H > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 6 > 0$, so the point is a local minimum point. At $(-1, -2)$ we have $\det H = 72 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = -6 < 0$, therefore by the **Hessian test** $(-1, -2)$ is a **local maximum point**.
