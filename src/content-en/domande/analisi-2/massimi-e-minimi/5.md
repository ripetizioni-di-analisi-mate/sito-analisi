For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^4 - 2x$, which statement about the critical point $(1, 0)$ is true?

- It is a saddle point
- The Hessian test classifies it as a local minimum
- It is an absolute minimum point
- It is a local maximum point

## Explanation

The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{yy} = 12y^2$ and $f_{xy} = 0$, which at $(1, 0)$ are $2$, $0$ and $0$, so $\det H = 0$ and the **Hessian test** is inconclusive. Completing the square gives
$$
f(x, y) = (x - 1)^2 + y^4 - 1 .
$$
Since $(x - 1)^2 \geq 0$ and $y^4 \geq 0$, we get $f(x, y) \geq -1 = f(1, 0)$ for every $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$. Therefore $(1, 0)$ is an **absolute minimum point**.
