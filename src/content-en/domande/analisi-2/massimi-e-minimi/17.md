For the function $f(x, y) = xy\,(3 - x - y)$, which point is a local maximum point?

- $(0, 0)$
- $(1, 1)$
- $(3, 0)$
- $(0, 3)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = y\,(3 - 2x - y)$ and $f_y = x\,(3 - x - 2y)$. The condition $f_x = 0$ gives $y = 0$ or $2x + y = 3$, the condition $f_y = 0$ gives $x = 0$ or $x + 2y = 3$, and combining the cases gives the critical points $(0, 0)$, $(3, 0)$, $(0, 3)$ and $(1, 1)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = -2y$, $f_{yy} = -2x$ and $f_{xy} = 3 - 2x - 2y$. At $(0, 0)$, $(3, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$ we have $f_{xx} f_{yy} = 0$ and $f_{xy} = \pm 3$, so $\det H = -9 < 0$ and the three points are saddle points. At $(1, 1)$ we have $f_{xx} = f_{yy} = -2$ and $f_{xy} = -1$, so $\det H = 4 - 1 = 3 > 0$ and $f_{xx} < 0$. Therefore, by the **Hessian test**, $(1, 1)$ is a **local maximum point**.
