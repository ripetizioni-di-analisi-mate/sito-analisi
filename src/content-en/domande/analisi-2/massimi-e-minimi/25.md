For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^3$ the origin is a critical point with degenerate Hessian. Which fact proves that the origin is a saddle point?

- The restriction to the $y$-axis, that is, $f(0, y) = y^3$
- The restriction to the $x$-axis, that is, $f(x, 0) = x^2$
- The determinant of the Hessian at the origin, which is $0$
- The restriction to the bisector, that is, $f(x, x) = x^2 + x^3$

## Explanation

The restriction to the $y$-axis is $f(0, y) = y^3$, which is positive for $y > 0$ and negative for $y < 0$. So in every neighborhood of the origin $f$ takes values both greater and smaller than $f(0, 0) = 0$, therefore the origin is a **saddle point**. The restrictions $f(x, 0) = x^2$ and $f(x, x) = x^2 + x^3$ instead have a minimum at $0$, so by themselves they would be compatible with a minimum. The zero determinant only establishes that the Hessian criterion does not decide.
