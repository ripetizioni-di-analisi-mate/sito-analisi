For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 - xy + y^2 + 3y$, what is the only critical point?

- $(1, 2)$
- $(-2, -1)$
- $(-1, -2)$
- $(1, -2)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2x - y$ and $f_y = -x + 2y + 3$, so the critical points solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x - y = 0 \\[4pt]
-x + 2y + 3 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $y = 2x$, and substituting into the second gives $3x + 3 = 0$, that is, $x = -1$. Therefore $y = -2$, and the **only critical point** is $(-1, -2)$.
