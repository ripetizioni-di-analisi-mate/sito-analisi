The function $f(x, y) = x + 2y$ with the constraint $x^2 + y^2 = 5$ has its constrained maximum at the point $(1, 2)$, where it equals $5$. How is the level line $x + 2y = 5$ positioned with respect to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 5$?

- It is perpendicular to the circle at $(1, 2)$
- It is tangent to the circle at $(1, 2)$
- It cuts the circle at two distinct points
- It passes through the center of the circle

## Explanation

At the constrained maximum point, by the **Lagrange multiplier theorem**, the gradients $\nabla f = (1, 2)$ and $\nabla g = (2x, 2y) = (2, 4)$ are parallel. The vector $(1, 2)$ is perpendicular both to the line $x + 2y = 5$ and to the circle at the point $(1, 2)$. Therefore the level line is **tangent** to the circle at $(1, 2)$. Indeed the distance of the center from the line is $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{5}} = \sqrt{5}$, equal to the radius.
