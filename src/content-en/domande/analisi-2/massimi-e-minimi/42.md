What is the maximum of $f(x, y, z) = z$ on the curve defined by the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 4 \\[4pt]
x + y = 2
\end{cases}
$$

- $2$
- $1$
- $\sqrt{3}$
- $\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

With $g = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 4$ and $h = x + y - 2$ the gradients are $\nabla g = (2x, 2y, 2z)$ and $\nabla h = (1, 1, 0)$. They are parallel only if $z = 0$ and $x = y$, and on the plane $x + y = 2$ this happens only at the point $(1, 1, 0)$, which is not on the sphere, so on the curve they are linearly independent. The condition $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g + \mu \nabla h$ gives $0 = 2\lambda x + \mu$, $0 = 2\lambda y + \mu$ and $1 = 2\lambda z$. The third equation gives $\lambda \neq 0$, and subtracting the first two gives $x = y$. The plane then gives $x = y = 1$, and the sphere gives $z^2 = 2$, therefore the candidates are $(1, 1, \pm\sqrt{2})$. Since the curve is compact, by the **extreme value theorem** the maximum exists, and it equals $\sqrt{2}$.
