The point $(x_0, y_0)$ is a constrained maximum point for $f$ with the constraint $g(x, y) = 0$, where $f$ and $g$ are of class $C^1$ and $\nabla g(x_0, y_0) = (2, -1)$. Which of the following vectors can be $\nabla f(x_0, y_0)$?

- $(-4, 2)$
- $(1, 2)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $(-2, -1)$

## Explanation

Since $\nabla g(x_0, y_0) \neq (0, 0)$, the hypothesis of the **Lagrange multiplier theorem** is satisfied, so there exists $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $\nabla f(x_0, y_0) = \lambda\,(2, -1)$. The vector $(-4, 2)$ is obtained with $\lambda = -2$, therefore it can be the gradient of $f$ at the point. The vectors $(1, 2)$, $(2, 1)$ and $(-2, -1)$ have a ratio between the second and the first component different from $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, so they are not multiples of $(2, -1)$. In particular $(1, 2)$ is orthogonal to $\nabla g$, that is, tangent to the constraint curve.
