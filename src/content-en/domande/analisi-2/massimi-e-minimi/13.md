An open box with a square base (no lid) has volume $4\ \mathrm{dm}^3$. What is the height of the box with minimum surface area (base and four walls)?

- $1\ \mathrm{dm}$
- $\sqrt[3]{4}\ \mathrm{dm}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\ \mathrm{dm}$
- $2\ \mathrm{dm}$

## Explanation

With a base of side length $x > 0$ and height $h$, the constraint $x^2 h = 4$ gives $h = \dfrac{4}{x^2}$. The surface area is the base $x^2$ plus the four walls of area $xh$, so
$$
S(x) = x^2 + 4xh = x^2 + \frac{16}{x} .
$$
The derivative $S'(x) = 2x - \dfrac{16}{x^2}$ vanishes for $x^3 = 8$, that is, for $x = 2$. Since $S'$ is negative for $0 < x < 2$ and positive for $x > 2$, the point $x = 2$ is an absolute minimum point on $(0, +\infty)$. The corresponding height is therefore $h = \dfrac{4}{4} = 1\ \mathrm{dm}$.
