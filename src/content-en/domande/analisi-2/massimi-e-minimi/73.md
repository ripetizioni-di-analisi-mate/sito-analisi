Which point of the plane $2x + y - 2z = 6$ is closest to the origin?

- $(2, 2, 0)$
- $\left(\dfrac{4}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, -\dfrac{4}{3}\right)$
- $(2, 1, -1)$
- $\left(\dfrac{4}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{4}{3}\right)$

## Explanation

We minimize the square of the distance $x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ with the constraint $2x + y - 2z = 6$. The Lagrange system gives $2x = 2\lambda$, $2y = \lambda$, $2z = -2\lambda$, so $(x, y, z) = \left(\lambda, \dfrac{\lambda}{2}, -\lambda\right)$. Substituting into the constraint gives $2\lambda + \dfrac{\lambda}{2} + 2\lambda = 6$, that is $\lambda = \dfrac{4}{3}$. The required point is therefore $\left(\dfrac{4}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, -\dfrac{4}{3}\right)$, at distance $2$ from the origin.
