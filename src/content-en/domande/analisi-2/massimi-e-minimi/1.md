For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - 3x$, which point is a local minimum point?

- $(-1, 2)$
- $(2, -1)$
- $(-2, 1)$
- $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, 0\right)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2x + y - 3$ and $f_y = x + 2y$, so the critical points solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + y - 3 = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 2y = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation gives $x = -2y$, and substituting into the first gives $-3y - 3 = 0$, therefore the only critical point is $(2, -1)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{yy} = 2$ and $f_{xy} = 1$, so $\det H = 4 - 1 = 3 > 0$. Since moreover $f_{xx} = 2 > 0$, by the **Hessian criterion** the point $(2, -1)$ is **a local minimum point**.
