For the function $f(x, y) = (x - y)^2 - y^4$, what is the nature of the critical point $(0, 0)$?

- Local minimum point
- Saddle point
- Local maximum point
- Absolute minimum point

## Explanation

At the origin the second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = -2$ and $f_{yy} = 2$, so $\det H = 4 - 4 = 0$ and the Hessian criterion does not decide. We then study the sign of $f(x, y) - f(0, 0) = (x - y)^2 - y^4$ along curves through the origin. On the $x$-axis we have $f(x, 0) = x^2 > 0$ for $x \neq 0$, while on the bisector $y = x$ we have $f(x, x) = -x^4 < 0$ for $x \neq 0$. Therefore in every neighborhood of the origin $f$ takes both signs, and the origin is a **saddle point**.
