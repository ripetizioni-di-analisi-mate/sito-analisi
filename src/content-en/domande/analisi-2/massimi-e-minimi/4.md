For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 y^2$, which set consists exactly of the critical points?

- Only the point $(0, 0)$
- Only the $x$-axis
- The union of the two coordinate axes
- The two bisectors $y = x$ and $y = -x$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2xy^2$ and $f_y = 2x^2 y$, so the critical points solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2xy^2 = 0 \\[4pt]
2x^2 y = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation is satisfied if $x = 0$ or $y = 0$, and the same holds for the second. Therefore the system is satisfied by all points with $x = 0$ and by all points with $y = 0$, that is, by the points of the **two coordinate axes**. For example, at $(3, 0)$ we have $f_x = 0$ and $f_y = 0$, so the critical points do not reduce to the origin.
