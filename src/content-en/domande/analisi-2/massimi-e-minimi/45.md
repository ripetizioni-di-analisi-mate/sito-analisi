What is the minimum distance of the point $(0, 2)$ from the parabola $y = x^2$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{7}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$

## Explanation

We minimize the square of the distance between $(0, 2)$ and the point $(x, x^2)$ of the parabola, which is
$$
q(x) = x^2 + (x^2 - 2)^2 = x^4 - 3x^2 + 4 .
$$
The derivative $q'(x) = 4x^3 - 6x = 2x\,(2x^2 - 3)$ vanishes for $x = 0$ and for $x^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}$. We have $q(0) = 4$, while for $x^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}$ we have $q = \dfrac{9}{4} - \dfrac{9}{2} + 4 = \dfrac{7}{4}$. Since $q(x) \to +\infty$ as $|x| \to +\infty$, the minimum of $q$ is $\dfrac{7}{4}$, so the **minimum distance** is $\sqrt{\dfrac{7}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$.
