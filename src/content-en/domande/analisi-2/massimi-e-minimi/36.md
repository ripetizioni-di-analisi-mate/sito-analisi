What is the minimum of $f(x, y) = 3x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$?

- $4$
- $2$
- $3$
- $1$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and the circle is compact, so by the **extreme value theorem** the minimum exists. With $g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 1$ the gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 2y)$ does not vanish on the constraint, therefore the candidates solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
6x + 2y = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
2x + 6y = 2\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 = 1
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting the first two equations gives $4(x - y) = 2\lambda (x - y)$, so $y = x$ or $\lambda = 2$. If $\lambda = 2$, the first equation becomes $2x + 2y = 0$, that is $y = -x$. With $y = x$ the constraint gives $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $f = \dfrac{3}{2} + 1 + \dfrac{3}{2} = 4$, while with $y = -x$ we get $f = \dfrac{3}{2} - 1 + \dfrac{3}{2} = 2$. Therefore the **constrained minimum** is $2$, attained at $\pm\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$.
