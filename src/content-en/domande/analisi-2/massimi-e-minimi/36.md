What is the minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ with the constraint $x + 2y = 5$?

- $\sqrt{5}$
- $5$
- $25$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x + 2y - 5$ the gradient $\nabla g = (1, 2)$ never vanishes, so the candidates solve the Lagrange system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x = \lambda \\[4pt]
2y = 2\lambda \\[4pt]
x + 2y = 5
\end{cases}
$$
The first two equations give $y = 2x$, and substituting into the constraint gives $5x = 5$, so the only candidate is $(1, 2)$, with $f = 5$. The line is not compact, but along it $f$ tends to $+\infty$ as $|x| \to +\infty$, therefore the minimum exists and is attained at the only candidate. The **constrained minimum** is therefore $5$, which is the square of the distance of the line from the origin.
