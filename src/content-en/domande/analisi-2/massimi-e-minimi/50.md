What is the absolute maximum of $f(x, y) = (x^2 + y^2)\, e^{-(x^2 + y^2)}$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2e}$
- $\dfrac{2}{e^{2}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$

## Explanation

The function depends only on $t = x^2 + y^2$, which takes all the values in $[0, +\infty)$, so it is enough to study $h(t) = t\,e^{-t}$ for $t \geq 0$. The derivative is $h'(t) = (1 - t)\,e^{-t}$, positive for $t < 1$ and negative for $t > 1$. Therefore $h$ has its absolute maximum at $t = 1$, with $h(1) = e^{-1}$. It follows that the **absolute maximum** of $f$ is $\dfrac{1}{e}$, attained at all the points of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$.
