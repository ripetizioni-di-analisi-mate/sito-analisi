For the function $f(x, y) = x$ with the constraint $g(x, y) = (x - 1)^3 - y^2 = 0$, which statement is true?

- The Lagrange system has the only solution $(1, 0)$
- $f$ has no constrained minimum, because the Lagrange system has no solutions
- The constrained minimum is $1$, attained at a point where $\nabla g = (0, 0)$
- The constrained minimum is $0$, attained at the origin

## Explanation

On the constraint we have $(x - 1)^3 = y^2 \geq 0$, so $x \geq 1$, and the point $(1, 0)$ belongs to the constraint. Therefore $f(x, y) = x \geq 1 = f(1, 0)$ on the whole constraint, and the **constrained minimum** is $1$. The Lagrange system consists of $1 = 3\lambda (x - 1)^2$, $0 = -2\lambda y$ and the constraint. The first equation gives $\lambda \neq 0$, so the second gives $y = 0$ and the constraint gives $x = 1$, but then the first equation becomes $1 = 0$, therefore the system has no solutions. The point $(1, 0)$ is a candidate because the gradient $\nabla g = (3(x - 1)^2, -2y)$ vanishes at $(1, 0)$, and the points of the constraint where $\nabla g$ vanishes must be added to the candidates.
