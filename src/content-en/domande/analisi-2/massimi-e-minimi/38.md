What is the maximum of $f(x, y) = x^2 y$ with the constraint $x^2 + y^2 = 3$?

- $\sqrt{2}$
- $3$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 3$ the gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 2y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which does not belong to the constraint, so the candidates solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2xy = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
x^2 = 2\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 = 3
\end{cases}
$$
If $x = 0$, the constraint gives $y = \pm\sqrt{3}$ and $f = 0$. If $x \neq 0$, the first equation gives $\lambda = y$, the second becomes $x^2 = 2y^2$ and the constraint gives $3y^2 = 3$, so $y = \pm 1$ and $x^2 = 2$. At the candidates $(\pm\sqrt{2}, 1)$ we have $f = 2$, at the candidates $(\pm\sqrt{2}, -1)$ we have $f = -2$. Since the circle is compact, by **Weierstrass's theorem** the maximum exists, and the **constrained maximum** is $2$.
