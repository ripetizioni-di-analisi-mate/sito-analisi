For the function $f(x, y) = x^3 - 3xy + y^3$, what is the nature of the critical point $(1, 1)$?

- Saddle point
- Local maximum point
- The Hessian criterion does not decide
- Local minimum point

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 3x^2 - 3y$ and $f_y = 3y^2 - 3x$, which are $0$ at $(1, 1)$, so $(1, 1)$ is a critical point. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 6x$, $f_{yy} = 6y$ and $f_{xy} = -3$, which at $(1, 1)$ are $6$, $6$ and $-3$. Therefore
$$
\det H(1, 1) = 6 \cdot 6 - (-3)^2 = 27 > 0 .
$$
Since moreover $f_{xx}(1, 1) = 6 > 0$, by the **Hessian criterion** the point $(1, 1)$ is a **local minimum point**.
