What is the maximum of $f(x, y) = xy$ with the constraint $x^2 + 4y^2 = 16$?

- $8$
- $4$
- $2$
- $4\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2 - 16$ the gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 8y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which does not belong to the constraint, so the candidates solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
y = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
x = 8\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + 4y^2 = 16
\end{cases}
$$
Multiplying the first equation by $4y$ and the second by $x$ gives $4y^2 = 8\lambda xy = x^2$. Substituting $x^2 = 4y^2$ into the constraint gives $8y^2 = 16$, so $y = \pm\sqrt{2}$ and $x = \pm 2\sqrt{2}$. The product $xy$ equals $4$ when the coordinates have the same sign and $-4$ otherwise. Since the ellipse is compact, by **Weierstrass's theorem** the maximum exists, and the **constrained maximum** is $4$.
