The point $(x, y)$ moves along the ellipse $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 3$. What maximum value does the sum $x + y$ reach?

- $\sqrt{3}$
- $\sqrt{6}$
- $3$
- $2$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - 3$ the Lagrange system is
$$
\begin{cases}
1 = \lambda\,(2x + y) \\[4pt]
1 = \lambda\,(x + 2y) \\[4pt]
x^2 + xy + y^2 = 3
\end{cases}
$$
The first two equations give $\lambda \neq 0$ and $2x + y = x + 2y$, so $x = y$. Substituting into the constraint gives $3x^2 = 3$, that is $x = \pm 1$, and the candidates are $(1, 1)$ with $f = 2$ and $(-1, -1)$ with $f = -2$. The gradient $\nabla g = (2x + y, x + 2y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which is not on the constraint. Since the ellipse is compact, the **constrained maximum** is $2$.
