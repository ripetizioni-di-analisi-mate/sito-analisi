The function $f(x, y) = x y^2$ is considered on the quarter disk $K = \{(x, y) : x \geq 0,\ y \geq 0,\ x^2 + y^2 \leq 4\}$. What is its absolute maximum?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{16\sqrt{3}}{9}$
- $\dfrac{8\sqrt{3}}{9}$
- $\dfrac{16}{9}$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and $K$ is compact, so by the **extreme value theorem** the maximum exists. The system $f_x = y^2 = 0$, $f_y = 2xy = 0$ gives only points with $y = 0$, which are not interior points of $K$, so the maximum lies on the boundary. On the two segments of the axes $f = 0$. On the arc we set $x = 2\cos t$, $y = 2\sin t$ with $t \in \left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$, and we get $h(t) = 8\cos t \sin^2 t$, with
$$
h'(t) = 8\sin t\,(2\cos^2 t - \sin^2 t) .
$$
Inside the interval the derivative vanishes for $\sin^2 t = 2\cos^2 t$, that is $\cos^2 t = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\sin^2 t = \dfrac{2}{3}$, where $h = 8 \cdot \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{16\sqrt{3}}{9}$. Therefore the **absolute maximum** is $\dfrac{16\sqrt{3}}{9}$.
