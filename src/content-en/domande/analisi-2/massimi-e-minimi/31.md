What is the absolute maximum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + 2y^2 - 2x$ on the half-disk $K = \{(x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 4,\ y \geq 0\}$?

::grafico{area="sqrt(4-x^2);0;-2;2" punti="1,0,pieno" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=2.4 uguale=1 titolo="The half-disk $K$ and the point $(1, 0)$, the only critical point of $f$"}

- $9$
- $8$
- $3$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and $K$ is compact, so by the **extreme value theorem** the maximum exists. The only critical point is $(1, 0)$, which lies on the diameter and is therefore not an interior point of $K$. On the diameter $y = 0$, $x \in [-2, 2]$, the restriction is $x^2 - 2x$, which equals $8$ at $x = -2$, $-1$ at $x = 1$ and $0$ at $x = 2$. On the arc $y^2 = 4 - x^2$, so the restriction is
$$
h(x) = x^2 + 2(4 - x^2) - 2x = 8 - 2x - x^2, \qquad x \in [-2, 2] ,
$$
whose derivative $h'(x) = -2 - 2x$ vanishes at $x = -1$, where $h(-1) = 9$. Comparing the values gives the **absolute maximum** $9$, attained at the point $(-1, \sqrt{3})$ of the arc.
