What is the absolute minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 - 2x + y^2 - 4y$ on the rectangle $K = [0, 3] \times [0, 1]$?

- $-5$
- $-3$
- $0$
- $-4$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and $K$ is compact, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute minimum exists. The only critical point is $(1, 2)$, where $f = -5$, but $(1, 2)$ does not belong to $K$, therefore the value $-5$ is not attained on $K$. Completing the squares gives
$$
f(x, y) = (x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 - 5 .
$$
For every $(x, y) \in K$ we have $(x - 1)^2 \geq 0$ and, since $y \leq 1$, also $(y - 2)^2 \geq 1$, so $f(x, y) \geq -4$. The value $-4$ is attained at $(1, 1)$, therefore the **absolute minimum** is $-4$.
