What is the absolute minimum of the function $f(x, y) = e^{x^2 - 2x + y^2}$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $e$
- $e^{-1}$

## Explanation

Completing the square, the exponent becomes
$$
x^2 - 2x + y^2 = (x - 1)^2 + y^2 - 1 ,
$$
which is greater than or equal to $-1$ for every $(x, y)$ and equals $-1$ at $(1, 0)$. Since the exponential function is increasing, it follows that $f(x, y) \geq e^{-1} = f(1, 0)$ for every $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$. Therefore $(1, 0)$ is an absolute minimum point, and the **absolute minimum** is $e^{-1}$.
