A function $f$ of class $C^2$ has a critical point $(x_0, y_0)$ at which $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = 1$ and $f_{yy} = -3$. What is the nature of the point?

- Local minimum point
- Local maximum point
- Saddle point
- The Hessian test is inconclusive

## Explanation

The determinant of the Hessian at the point is
$$
\det H = f_{xx} f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2 = 2 \cdot (-3) - 1^2 = -7 .
$$
Since $\det H$ is the product of the eigenvalues and is negative, the eigenvalues have opposite signs and the Hessian is indefinite. Therefore, by the **Hessian test**, $(x_0, y_0)$ is a **saddle point**. The sign of $f_{xx}$ enters the classification only when $\det H > 0$, so $f_{xx} = 2 > 0$ does not indicate a minimum.
