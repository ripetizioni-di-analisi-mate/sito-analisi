For the function $f(x, y) = xy + \dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y}$, defined for $x \neq 0$ and $y \neq 0$, what is the value of $f$ at its local minimum point?

- $1$
- $2$
- $3$
- $-3$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = y - \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ and $f_y = x - \dfrac{1}{y^2}$. The condition $f_x = 0$ gives $y = \dfrac{1}{x^2}$, and substituting into $f_y = 0$ gives $x = x^4$, that is, $x^3 = 1$ since $x \neq 0$. So the only critical point is $(1, 1)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = \dfrac{2}{x^3}$, $f_{yy} = \dfrac{2}{y^3}$ and $f_{xy} = 1$, which at $(1, 1)$ are $2$, $2$ and $1$, therefore $\det H = 3 > 0$ and $f_{xx} > 0$. By the **Hessian test** the point $(1, 1)$ is a local minimum point, and the value is $f(1, 1) = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.
