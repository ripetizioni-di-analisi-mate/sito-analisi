For the function $f(x, y, z) = x + y + z$ on the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 12$, what is the constrained maximum?

- $2\sqrt{3}$
- $6\sqrt{3}$
- $6$
- $12$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 12$ the gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 2y, 2z)$ does not vanish on the sphere, so the candidates solve $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$. This condition gives $1 = 2\lambda x$, $1 = 2\lambda y$ and $1 = 2\lambda z$, therefore $\lambda \neq 0$ and $x = y = z = \dfrac{1}{2\lambda}$. Substituting into the constraint gives $3x^2 = 12$, so $x = \pm 2$. The candidates are $(2, 2, 2)$, with $f = 6$, and $(-2, -2, -2)$, with $f = -6$. Since $f$ is continuous and the sphere is compact, by the **extreme value theorem** the maximum exists, and the **constrained maximum** is $6$.
