We look for the extrema of $f(x, y) = x + y$ on the open disk $A = \{(x, y) : x^2 + y^2 < 1\}$, without its circle. What do we obtain?

- $f$ has neither a maximum nor a minimum on $A$
- The maximum is $\sqrt{2}$, attained at $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$
- The maximum is $\sqrt{2}$ and the minimum is $-\sqrt{2}$
- The minimum is $-\sqrt{2}$ and the maximum does not exist

## Explanation

Every point of the open disk is an interior point of $A$. If $f$ had an extremum at a point of $A$, by **Fermat's theorem** the gradient would vanish at that point. The gradient, however, equals $\nabla f = (1, 1)$ at every point, so $f$ **has neither a maximum nor a minimum** on $A$. The values $\pm\sqrt{2}$ are reached at the points $\pm\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$ of the circle, which do not belong to $A$.
