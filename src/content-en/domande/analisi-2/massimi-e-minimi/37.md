What is the minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ with the constraint $x^2 + 4y^2 = 4$?

- $4$
- $2$
- $1$
- $0$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2 - 4$ the gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 8y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which does not belong to the constraint, so the candidates solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
2y = 8\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + 4y^2 = 4
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $x = 0$ or $\lambda = 1$. If $\lambda = 1$, the second gives $y = 0$, so $x = \pm 2$ and $f = 4$; if $x = 0$, the constraint gives $y = \pm 1$ and $f = 1$. Since $f$ is continuous and the ellipse is compact, by the **extreme value theorem** the minimum exists, and the **constrained minimum** is $1$, attained at $(0, \pm 1)$.
