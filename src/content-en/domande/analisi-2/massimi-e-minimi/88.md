What is the maximum volume of a right circular cylinder inscribed in a sphere of radius $\sqrt{3}$?

- $4\pi$
- $2\pi$
- $6\pi$
- $\dfrac{4\pi\sqrt{3}}{3}$

## Explanation

Let $r$ be the base radius and $h$ the height of the cylinder; the rims of the bases lie on the sphere, so by the Pythagorean theorem $r^2 + \dfrac{h^2}{4} = 3$. The volume becomes
$$
V(h) = \pi r^2 h = \pi\left(3 - \frac{h^2}{4}\right) h = \pi\left(3h - \frac{h^3}{4}\right), \qquad 0 < h < 2\sqrt{3} .
$$
The derivative $V'(h) = \pi\left(3 - \dfrac{3h^2}{4}\right)$ vanishes at $h = 2$, positive before and negative after. Therefore the maximum is at $h = 2$, with $r^2 = 2$, and the **maximum volume** is $\pi \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 4\pi$.
