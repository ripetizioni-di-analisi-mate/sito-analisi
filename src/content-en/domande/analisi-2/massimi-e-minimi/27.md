For the function $f(x, y) = (y - x^2)(y - 3x^2)$ the origin is a critical point, and the restriction of $f$ to every line through the origin has a minimum at $0$. What is the nature of the origin?

- Local minimum point
- Absolute minimum point
- Local maximum point
- Saddle point

## Explanation

On the parabola $y = 2x^2$ we have
$$
f(x, 2x^2) = x^2 \cdot (-x^2) = -x^4 < 0 \quad \text{for } x \neq 0 ,
$$
while on the $y$-axis we have $f(0, y) = y^2 > 0$ for $y \neq 0$. So in every neighborhood of the origin $f$ takes values both greater and smaller than $f(0, 0) = 0$, therefore the origin is a **saddle point**. The parabola $y = 2x^2$ lies between the parabolas $y = x^2$ and $y = 3x^2$, where the two factors have opposite signs, and near the origin no line stays in that region. For this reason the minimum of every restriction to a line is compatible with a saddle point.
