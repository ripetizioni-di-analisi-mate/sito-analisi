What is the absolute maximum of $f(x, y) = x^2 - xy + y^2$ on the square $[-1, 1] \times [-1, 1]$?

- $3$
- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and the square is compact, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The system $f_x = 2x - y = 0$, $f_y = -x + 2y = 0$ has the only solution $(0, 0)$, where $f = 0$. On the side $x = 1$ the restriction is $g(y) = 1 - y + y^2$ with $y \in [-1, 1]$, which has minimum $\dfrac{3}{4}$ at $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and maximum $g(-1) = 3$; on the side $x = -1$ the restriction $1 + y + y^2$ has maximum $3$, at $y = 1$. Since $f(x, y) = f(y, x)$, the sides $y = \pm 1$ give the same values. The comparison gives the **absolute maximum** $3$, attained at $(1, -1)$ and at $(-1, 1)$.
