What is the absolute maximum of $f(x, y) = 3x + 4y$ on the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$?

- $7$
- $5$
- $4$
- $\sqrt{7}$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and the disk is compact, so by **Weierstrass's theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The gradient $\nabla f = (3, 4)$ never vanishes, therefore there are no interior critical points and the maximum is on the boundary. With $x = \cos t$ and $y = \sin t$ the restriction is $g(t) = 3\cos t + 4\sin t$, whose derivative $g'(t) = -3\sin t + 4\cos t$ vanishes for $\cos t = \pm\dfrac{3}{5}$, $\sin t = \pm\dfrac{4}{5}$ with the same signs. At the point $\left(\dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5}\right)$ we have $f = \dfrac{9}{5} + \dfrac{16}{5} = 5$, while at the opposite point $f = -5$. Hence the **absolute maximum** is $5$.
