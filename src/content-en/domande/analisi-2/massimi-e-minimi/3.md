For the function $f(x, y) = (x - y)^2 + y^3 - 3y$, which point is a local minimum point?

- $(1, 1)$
- $(-1, -1)$
- $(1, -1)$
- $(-1, 1)$

## Explanation

By the chain rule, $f_x = 2(x - y)$ and $f_y = -2(x - y) + 3y^2 - 3$. The condition $f_x = 0$ gives $x = y$, and substituting into $f_y = 0$ gives $3y^2 - 3 = 0$, so the critical points are $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, -1)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = -2$ and $f_{yy} = 2 + 6y$, therefore
$$
\det H = 2(2 + 6y) - (-2)^2 = 12y .
$$
At $(1, 1)$ we have $\det H = 12 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 2 > 0$, so $(1, 1)$ is a **local minimum point**. At $(-1, -1)$, instead, $\det H = -12 < 0$, so $(-1, -1)$ is a saddle point.
