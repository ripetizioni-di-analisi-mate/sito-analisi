What is the absolute maximum of $f(x, y) = xy$ on the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 2$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and the disk is compact, so by **Weierstrass's theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The only critical point is the origin, interior to the disk, where $f = 0$. On the boundary we set $x = \sqrt{2}\cos t$, $y = \sqrt{2}\sin t$ with $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, and the restriction becomes
$$
g(t) = 2\cos t \sin t = \sin 2t ,
$$
which has maximum $1$ for $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and for $t = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$. Comparing the values $0$ and $1$ gives the **absolute maximum** $1$, attained at $(1, 1)$ and at $(-1, -1)$.
