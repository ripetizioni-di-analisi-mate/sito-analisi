Which system gives the candidates for the extrema of $f(x, y, z) = xyz$ with the constraint $x + 2y + 3z = 6$?

- $\begin{cases} yz = 3\lambda \\ xz = 2\lambda \\ xy = \lambda \\ x + 2y + 3z = 6 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} yz = \lambda \\ xz = 2\lambda \\ xy = 3\lambda \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} yz = \lambda \\ xz = 2\lambda \\ xy = 3\lambda \\ x + 2y + 3z = 6 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} yz = \lambda x \\ xz = 2\lambda y \\ xy = 3\lambda z \\ x + 2y + 3z = 6 \end{cases}$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y, z) = x + 2y + 3z - 6$ the gradients are $\nabla f = (yz, xz, xy)$ and $\nabla g = (1, 2, 3)$. The condition $\nabla f = \lambda\,\nabla g$ gives the three equations $yz = \lambda$, $xz = 2\lambda$, $xy = 3\lambda$, to which the equation of the constraint is added. The system therefore has four equations in the four unknowns $x$, $y$, $z$, $\lambda$. Since $\nabla g$ never vanishes, the solutions of this system are all the candidates.
