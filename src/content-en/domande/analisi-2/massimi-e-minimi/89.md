What is the minimum distance between the points of the line $y = x - 4$ and the points of the parabola $y = x^2$?

- $\dfrac{15\sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{15\sqrt{2}}{8}$
- $\dfrac{15}{4}$
- $\dfrac{15}{8}$

## Explanation

The distance of the point $(t, t^2)$ of the parabola from the line $x - y - 4 = 0$ is
$$
d(t) = \frac{|t - t^2 - 4|}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{t^2 - t + 4}{\sqrt{2}} ,
$$
because $t^2 - t + 4 > 0$ for every $t$. The numerator has its minimum at the vertex $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$, where it equals $\dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{2} + 4 = \dfrac{15}{4}$. Therefore the **minimum distance** is $\dfrac{15}{4\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{15\sqrt{2}}{8}$, reached by the point $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{4}\right)$, where the tangent to the parabola is parallel to the line.
