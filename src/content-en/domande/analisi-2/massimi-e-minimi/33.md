For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 - y^2$ on the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$, which statement is true?

- There are no absolute extrema, because the only critical point is a saddle point
- The absolute minimum is $0$, attained at the origin
- The absolute maximum is $1$ and the absolute minimum is $-1$
- The absolute maximum is $1$ and there is no absolute minimum

## Explanation

The function is continuous and the disk is compact, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute maximum and minimum exist. The only critical point is the origin, which is a saddle point with $f = 0$ and remains among the candidates. On the boundary $y^2 = 1 - x^2$, so the restriction is $g(x) = 2x^2 - 1$ with $x \in [-1, 1]$, which takes values from $-1$, for $x = 0$, to $1$, for $x = \pm 1$. Comparing $0$, $-1$ and $1$ gives the **absolute maximum** $1$, at $(\pm 1, 0)$, and the **absolute minimum** $-1$, at $(0, \pm 1)$.
