For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2y$ on the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$, at which point does $f$ attain its absolute maximum?

- $(0, 2)$
- $(0, -2)$
- $(0, 1)$
- $(2, 0)$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and the disk is compact, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The system $f_x = 2x = 0$, $f_y = 2y - 2 = 0$ has the only solution $(0, 1)$, interior to the disk, with $f(0, 1) = -1$. On the boundary $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ holds, so the restriction is $f = 4 - 2y$ with $y \in [-2, 2]$. This function of $y$ is decreasing, therefore on the boundary the largest value is $f(0, -2) = 8$. Comparing $-1$ and $8$ gives the **absolute maximum** $8$, attained at $(0, -2)$.
