Consider $f(x, y) = x^2 - y$ on the region $K = \{(x, y) : x^2 \leq y \leq 1\}$, between the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 1$. Which statement is true?

::grafico{f="x^2" area="1;x^2;-1;1" xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 titolo="The region $K$ between the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 1$"}

- The absolute maximum is $0$, attained only at the origin
- The absolute maximum is $1$ and the absolute minimum is $-1$
- The absolute minimum is $0$ and the absolute maximum is $1$
- The absolute maximum is $0$, attained at all the points of the arc of parabola, and the absolute minimum is $-1$

## Explanation

On $K$ we have $y \geq x^2$, so $f(x, y) = x^2 - y \leq 0$, and equality holds exactly at the points with $y = x^2$. Therefore the **absolute maximum** is $0$, attained at all the points of the arc of parabola with $x \in [-1, 1]$. Moreover $f(x, y) \geq 0 - 1 = -1$, because $x^2 \geq 0$ and $y \leq 1$, and the value $-1$ is reached only for $x = 0$ and $y = 1$. So the **absolute minimum** is $-1$, attained at $(0, 1)$.
