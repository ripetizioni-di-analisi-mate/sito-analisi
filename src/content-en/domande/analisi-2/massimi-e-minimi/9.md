For the function $f(x, y) = 2x + y$ with the constraint $x^2 + y^2 = 20$, what is the constrained maximum?

- $8$
- $10$
- $4\sqrt{5}$
- $2\sqrt{5}$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 20$ the Lagrange system is
$$
\begin{cases}
2 = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
1 = 2\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 = 20
\end{cases}
$$
The first two equations give $\lambda \neq 0$ and $x = 2y$, and substituting into the constraint gives $5y^2 = 20$, so $y = \pm 2$. The candidates are $(4, 2)$, with $f = 10$, and $(-4, -2)$, with $f = -10$. The gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 2y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which does not belong to the constraint. Since $f$ is continuous and the circle is compact, by the **extreme value theorem** the maximum exists, so the **constrained maximum** is $10$, attained at $(4, 2)$.
