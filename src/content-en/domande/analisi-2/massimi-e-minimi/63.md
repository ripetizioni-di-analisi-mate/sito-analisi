On the rhombus $K = \{(x, y) : |x| + |y| \leq 1\}$, boundary included, what is the maximum of $x^2 + y^2$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $1$
- $\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The function is the square of the distance from the origin, so it is largest at the points of $K$ farthest from the origin. The only critical point is the origin, where $f = 0$, therefore the maximum lies on the boundary. On the side $x + y = 1$ with $x \in [0, 1]$ the restriction is $x^2 + (1 - x)^2$, which equals $1$ at the endpoints $x = 0$ and $x = 1$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ at the midpoint; by symmetry the other sides give the same values. So the **absolute maximum** is $1$, attained at the four vertices $(\pm 1, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 1)$.
