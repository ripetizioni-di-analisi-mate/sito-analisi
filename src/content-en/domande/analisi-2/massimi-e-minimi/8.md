For the function $f(x, y) = x + y - xy$ on the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$, $(0, 2)$, boundary included, what is the absolute maximum?

- $1$
- $3$
- $0$
- $2$

## Explanation

The function is continuous and the triangle is compact, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The system $f_x = 1 - y = 0$, $f_y = 1 - x = 0$ has the only solution $(1, 1)$, which lies on the hypotenuse $x + y = 2$ and is therefore not interior. On the legs $y = 0$ and $x = 0$ the restriction is $f = x$ or $f = y$, with values from $0$ to $2$. On the hypotenuse $y = 2 - x$, with $x \in [0, 2]$, the restriction is
$$
g(x) = 2 - x(2 - x) = x^2 - 2x + 2 ,
$$
which equals $2$ at the endpoints of the interval and has minimum $g(1) = 1$. Comparing the values gives the **absolute maximum** $2$, attained at the vertices $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$.
