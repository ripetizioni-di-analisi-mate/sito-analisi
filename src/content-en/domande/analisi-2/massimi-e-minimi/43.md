What is the minimum distance from the origin of the points of the line defined by the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 3 \\[4pt]
x - y = 0
\end{cases}
$$

- $3$
- $1$
- $\sqrt{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

We minimize the square of the distance $x^2 + y^2 + z^2$. Since both constraints can be solved explicitly, we obtain $y = x$ from the second equation and $z = 3 - 2x$ from the first. The square of the distance therefore becomes
$$
q(x) = 2x^2 + (3 - 2x)^2 = 6x^2 - 12x + 9 .
$$
The derivative $q'(x) = 12x - 12$ vanishes only for $x = 1$, and $q$ is an upward-opening parabola, therefore the minimum is $q(1) = 3$, at the point $(1, 1, 1)$. The **minimum distance** is therefore $\sqrt{3}$.
