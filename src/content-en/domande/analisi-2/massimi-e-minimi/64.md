Consider $f(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{x^2 + y^2}$, set equal to $0$ at the origin, on the closed disk $K = \{(x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1\}$. Which hypothesis of the extreme value theorem is not satisfied?

- $f$ is not continuous on $K$
- $K$ is not closed
- $K$ is not bounded
- All the hypotheses are satisfied

## Explanation

The disk $K$ contains its circle and is contained in the square $[-1, 1] \times [-1, 1]$, so it is closed and bounded, that is compact. The function, instead, tends to $+\infty$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$, while it equals $0$ at the origin, therefore it **is not continuous** at $(0, 0)$, which belongs to $K$. The extreme value theorem does not apply, and indeed $f$ has no maximum on $K$, because it is not bounded above.
