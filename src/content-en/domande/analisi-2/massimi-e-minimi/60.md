The critical points of $f(x, y) = x^2 y$ are exactly the points $(0, b)$ of the $y$-axis. Which statement about these points is true?

- They are local minimum points for $b > 0$, local maximum points for $b < 0$, and the origin is a saddle point
- They are all saddle points, because the Hessian is degenerate
- They are all local minimum points, because $f(0, b) = 0$
- They are local minimum points for $b < 0$, local maximum points for $b > 0$, and the origin is a saddle point

## Explanation

At every point $(0, b)$ we have $f(0, b) = 0$, so we study the sign of $f(x, y) = x^2 y$ near the point. If $b > 0$, in a small enough neighborhood $y > 0$, therefore $f(x, y) \geq 0$ and $(0, b)$ is a **local minimum point**, not a strict one. If $b < 0$, near the point $y < 0$, therefore $f(x, y) \leq 0$ and $(0, b)$ is a **local maximum point**. At the origin, instead, $f(x, y)$ takes positive values for $y > 0$ and negative values for $y < 0$, with $x \neq 0$, so the origin is a **saddle point**.
