For the function $f(x, y) = (x + y)^2 + x^4$, what is the nature of the critical point $(0, 0)$?

- Saddle point
- Local maximum point
- Absolute minimum point
- The point is not critical

## Explanation

At the origin the second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = 2$ and $f_{yy} = 2$, so $\det H = 4 - 4 = 0$ and the Hessian criterion does not decide. We then study directly the sign of $f(x, y) - f(0, 0) = (x + y)^2 + x^4$. This expression is the sum of two nonnegative quantities, so $f(x, y) \geq 0 = f(0, 0)$ for every $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$. Therefore the origin is an **absolute minimum point**.
