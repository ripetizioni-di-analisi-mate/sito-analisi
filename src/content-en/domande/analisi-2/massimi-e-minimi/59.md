For the function $f(x, y) = \cos(xy)$ the origin is a critical point with zero Hessian. What is the nature of the origin?

- Local minimum point
- Absolute maximum point
- Saddle point
- It is not a critical point

## Explanation

The first derivatives $f_x = -y\sin(xy)$ and $f_y = -x\sin(xy)$ vanish at the origin, and the second derivatives at the origin are all $0$, so the Hessian criterion is inconclusive. We then study the sign of $f(x, y) - f(0, 0)$. Since $\cos(xy) \leq 1 = f(0, 0)$ for every $(x, y)$, the origin is an **absolute maximum point**, not a strict one because $f = 1$ also along the two axes.
