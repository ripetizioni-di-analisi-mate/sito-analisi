On the plane $x + y + z = 11$ the sum $x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2$ has a minimum value. What is it?

- $121$
- $\dfrac{121}{3}$
- $66$
- $33$

## Explanation

The Lagrange system gives $2x = \lambda$, $4y = \lambda$, $6z = \lambda$, so $x = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}$, $y = \dfrac{\lambda}{4}$, $z = \dfrac{\lambda}{6}$. Substituting into the constraint gives
$$
\frac{\lambda}{2} + \frac{\lambda}{4} + \frac{\lambda}{6} = \frac{11\lambda}{12} = 11 ,
$$
that is $\lambda = 12$, and the candidate is $(6, 3, 2)$, with $f = 36 + 18 + 12 = 66$. The function is a sum of squares with positive coefficients and tends to $+\infty$ as the point moves away on the plane, so the minimum exists and the **constrained minimum** is $66$.
