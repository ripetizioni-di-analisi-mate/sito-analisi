What is the work of the field
$$
F(x, y) = \left( -\frac{y}{x^2 + y^2},\; \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2} \right)
$$
along the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$, traversed once counterclockwise?

- $0$
- $18\pi$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $2\pi$

## Explanation

The field is not defined at the origin, but it is continuous on the trace of $\gamma$, so the line integral of the second kind makes sense.

Let $\gamma$ be the circle. We parametrize it by $\gamma(t) = (3\cos t,\; 3\sin t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, which traverses it counterclockwise. Along the curve $x^2 + y^2 = 9$, therefore $F(\gamma(t)) = \left( -\dfrac{\sin t}{3},\; \dfrac{\cos t}{3} \right)$.

Since $\gamma'(t) = (-3\sin t,\; 3\cos t)$, the dot product equals
$$
F(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t) = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1 .
$$
Hence the work is $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} 1 \, dt = 2\pi$. The value does not depend on the radius, because on the circle of radius $r$ the field is tangent and has magnitude $\dfrac{1}{r}$, while the length is $2\pi r$.
