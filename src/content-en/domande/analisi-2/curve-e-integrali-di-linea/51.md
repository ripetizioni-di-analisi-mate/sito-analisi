For which values of $t \in (0, 2\pi)$ does the tangent vector of the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (2\cos t + \cos 2t,\; 2\sin t - \sin 2t)
$$
vanish?

- $t = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $t = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $t = \pi$
- $t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $t = \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$
- $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $t = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

Differentiating the components gives
$$
\gamma'(t) = \bigl( -2\sin t - 2\sin 2t,\; 2\cos t - 2\cos 2t \bigr).
$$

The second component vanishes if $\cos 2t = \cos t$, that is if $2t = \pm t + 2k\pi$. Hence $t = 2k\pi$ or $t = \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$, and in $(0, 2\pi)$ the values $t = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $t = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ remain.

For $t = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ we have $\sin 2t = \sin \dfrac{4\pi}{3} = -\sin t$, therefore the first component vanishes too; the same happens for $t = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.

Hence $\gamma'(t) = \mathbf{0}$ for $t = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $t = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, and the curve **is not regular** on $[0, 2\pi]$. At $t = \pi$ we have $\gamma'(\pi) = (0, -4) \neq \mathbf{0}$.
