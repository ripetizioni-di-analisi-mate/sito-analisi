What is the length of the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos 3t,\; \sin 3t), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi]?
$$

- $2\pi$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $6\pi$
- $18\pi$

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = (-3\sin 3t,\; 3\cos 3t)$, so $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 3$ for every $t$. By the **length formula** we get $L(\gamma) = \displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} 3 \, dt = 6\pi$.

The trace is the unit circle, whose length is $2\pi$. As $t$ varies in $[0, 2\pi]$ the angle $3t$ varies in $[0, 6\pi]$, therefore the curve traverses the circle three times. The formula measures the path traveled by the parametrization, so the length of the curve is $6\pi$, **three times** the length of the trace.
