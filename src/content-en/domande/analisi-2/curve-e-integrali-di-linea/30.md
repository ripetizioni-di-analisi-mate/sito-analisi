What is the arc length function $s(t)$ of the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (e^t \cos t,\; e^t \sin t,\; e^t), \qquad t \in [0, 1],
$$
measured from the point $\gamma(0)$?

- $s(t) = \sqrt{3}\, e^t$
- $s(t) = \sqrt{3}\,(e^t - 1)$
- $s(t) = \sqrt{2}\,(e^t - 1)$
- $s(t) = 3\,(e^t - 1)$

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = \bigl( e^t(\cos t - \sin t),\; e^t(\sin t + \cos t),\; e^t \bigr)$. The sum of the squares of the first two components equals $2e^{2t}$, so
$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{2e^{2t} + e^{2t}} = \sqrt{3}\, e^t .
$$
The **arc length function** is therefore
$$
s(t) = \int_0^t \sqrt{3}\, e^{\tau} \, d\tau = \sqrt{3}\,(e^t - 1).
$$
We have $s(0) = 0$, consistent with the fact that the length is measured from $\gamma(0)$.
