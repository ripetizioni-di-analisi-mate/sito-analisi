The work of the field $F(x, y) = (y^2,\; x)$ along the curve $\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t)$, $t \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, is equal to which integral?

- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left( \sin^3 t + \cos^2 t \right) dt$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left( -\sin^3 t + \cos^2 t \right) dt$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left( \sin^2 t + \cos t \right) dt$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left( \sin^2 t \cos t - \sin t \cos t \right) dt$

## Explanation

The integrand of the work is $F_1(\gamma(t))\, x'(t) + F_2(\gamma(t))\, y'(t)$. Along the curve $F(\gamma(t)) = (\sin^2 t,\; \cos t)$, and $\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t)$.

We therefore get
$$
F(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t) = \sin^2 t \cdot (-\sin t) + \cos t \cdot \cos t = -\sin^3 t + \cos^2 t ,
$$
so the work is $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left( -\sin^3 t + \cos^2 t \right) dt$. In the integral with $\sin^2 t \cos t - \sin t \cos t$ the first component of the field is multiplied by $y'(t)$ and the second by $x'(t)$, in swapped order.
