Let $\gamma$ be the boundary of the quarter disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$, $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$, traversed once **clockwise**. What is $\displaystyle\int_\gamma y^2 \, dx$?

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=1.5 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,1.5708" linee="0,1 0,0 1,0" punti="0,0,pieno;1,0,pieno;0,1,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="The boundary of the quarter disk: two segments on the axes and an arc of circle"}

- $-\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$

## Explanation

The boundary is piecewise regular, so the integral is the sum of the contributions of the three pieces. The integral is first computed counterclockwise, then the sign is changed.

On the segment of the $x$-axis we have $y = 0$ and on the segment of the $y$-axis we have $dx = 0$, therefore the two contributions are zero.

On the arc $(\cos t,\; \sin t)$, $t \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, traversed counterclockwise, we have $dx = -\sin t \, dt$, so
$$
\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin^2 t \, (-\sin t) \, dt = -\frac{2}{3} .
$$

Counterclockwise the integral equals $-\dfrac{2}{3}$; since the integral of the second kind changes sign with the orientation, clockwise it equals $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
