What is the length of the graph of the function $f(x) = \ln(\cos x)$, $x \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right]$?

- $\ln \sqrt{2}$
- $\ln\left( 1 + \sqrt{2} \right)$
- $\sqrt{2} - 1$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

We have $f'(x) = -\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = -\tan x$, so $1 + f'(x)^2 = 1 + \tan^2 x = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$. On the interval $\left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right]$ the cosine is positive, therefore $\sqrt{1 + f'(x)^2} = \dfrac{1}{\cos x}$.

By the **length formula** for a Cartesian curve, and since an antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{\cos x}$ is $\ln\left| \dfrac{1}{\cos x} + \tan x \right|$, we get
$$
L = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \frac{1}{\cos x} \, dx = \ln\left( \sqrt{2} + 1 \right) - \ln 1 = \ln\left( 1 + \sqrt{2} \right).
$$
