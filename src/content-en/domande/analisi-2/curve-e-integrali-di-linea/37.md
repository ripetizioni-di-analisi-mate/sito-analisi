A mass is distributed on the semicircle
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t), \qquad t \in [0, \pi],
$$
with linear density $\delta(x, y) = y$. Where is the center of mass?

- $\left( 0,\; \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$
- $\left( 0,\; \dfrac{2}{\pi} \right)$
- $\left( 0,\; \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$
- $\left( 0,\; \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$

## Explanation

We have $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 1$, so the mass is
$$
m = \int_0^\pi \sin t \, dt = 2 .
$$

The trace is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis and the density $\delta = y$ takes equal values at symmetric points, therefore $\bar{x} = 0$.

The second coordinate requires the integral of $y\, \delta = y^2$, that is
$$
\int_\gamma y^2 \, ds = \int_0^\pi \sin^2 t \, dt = \frac{\pi}{2} .
$$
Hence $\bar{y} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and the center of mass is $G = \left( 0,\; \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$.
