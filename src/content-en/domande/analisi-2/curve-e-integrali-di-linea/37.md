What is the center of mass of the homogeneous wire lying along the arc of the circle
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t), \qquad t \in \left[ -\frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{\pi}{3} \right]?
$$

- $\left( \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2\pi},\; 0 \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\; 0 \right)$
- $(1,\; 0)$
- $\left( \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi},\; 0 \right)$

## Explanation

The arc is symmetric with respect to the $x$-axis, so the center of mass belongs to the $x$-axis and $\bar{y} = 0$.

We have $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 1$, therefore the length is $L = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and
$$
\int_\gamma x \, ds = \int_{-\frac{\pi}{3}}^{\frac{\pi}{3}} \cos t \, dt = 2\sin \frac{\pi}{3} = \sqrt{3} .
$$
The wire is homogeneous, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{3}{2\pi} \cdot \sqrt{3} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}$. Since $\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2\pi} < 1$, the center of mass $G = \left( \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2\pi},\; 0 \right)$ does *not* belong to the arc.
