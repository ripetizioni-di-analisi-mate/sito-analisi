Along the circle $\gamma(t) = (5\cos t,\; 5\sin t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, one starts from the point $(5, 0)$ and travels an arc of length $\dfrac{5\pi}{2}$ in the direction of increasing $t$. Which point is reached?

- $(0,\; 5)$
- $(-5,\; 0)$
- $(0,\; -5)$
- $\left( \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{2}},\; \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{2}} \right)$

## Explanation

We have $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 5$, so the arc length measured from $\gamma(0) = (5, 0)$ is $s(t) = 5t$.

The arc traveled has length $\dfrac{5\pi}{2}$ when $5t = \dfrac{5\pi}{2}$, that is for $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Therefore one reaches the point $\gamma\!\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = (0,\; 5)$, after a quarter turn counterclockwise.
