Let $\gamma$ be the semicircle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, $y \geq 0$, traversed from the point $(-1, 0)$ to the point $(1, 0)$. What are the values of $\displaystyle\int_\gamma y \, ds$ and of the work of the field $F(x, y) = (-y,\; x)$ along $\gamma$?

- $\displaystyle\int_\gamma y \, ds = 2$ and the work equals $-\pi$
- $\displaystyle\int_\gamma y \, ds = -2$ and the work equals $-\pi$
- $\displaystyle\int_\gamma y \, ds = 2$ and the work equals $\pi$
- $\displaystyle\int_\gamma y \, ds = -2$ and the work equals $\pi$

## Explanation

The curve $\gamma_0(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t)$, $t \in [0, \pi]$, traverses the semicircle from the point $(1, 0)$ to the point $(-1, 0)$, so $\gamma$ is its reversed curve. Along $\gamma_0$ we have $\lVert \gamma_0'(t) \rVert = 1$ and $F(\gamma_0(t)) \cdot \gamma_0'(t) = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$.

It follows that $\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_0} y \, ds = \int_0^\pi \sin t \, dt = 2$ and that the work along $\gamma_0$ equals $\pi$. The integral of the first kind is invariant under a change of direction, therefore $\displaystyle\int_\gamma y \, ds = 2$. The integral of the second kind changes sign on the reversed curve, therefore the work along $\gamma$ equals $-\pi$.
