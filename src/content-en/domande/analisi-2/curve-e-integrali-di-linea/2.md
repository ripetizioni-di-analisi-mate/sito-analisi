For the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (t^3,\; t^6), \qquad t \in [-1, 1],
$$
which statement is true?

- The curve is regular and its trace is the arc of the parabola $y = x^2$ with $x \in [-1, 1]$
- The curve is not regular, it is piecewise regular and its trace is the arc of the parabola $y = x^2$ with $x \in [-1, 1]$
- The curve is not regular, it is piecewise regular and its trace has a cusp at the origin
- The curve is not regular, it is piecewise regular and its trace is the arc of the parabola $y = x^2$ with $x \in [0, 1]$

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = (3t^2,\; 6t^5)$, which vanishes for $t = 0$, an interior point of $[-1, 1]$; hence the curve is **not regular**. At the interior points of the intervals $[-1, 0]$ and $[0, 1]$ the vector $\gamma'(t)$ is nonzero, therefore the curve is **piecewise regular**.

Since $t^6 = (t^3)^2$, every point of the trace satisfies $y = x^2$, and the component $x = t^3$ takes all the values of $[-1, 1]$ as $t$ varies in $[-1, 1]$. The trace is therefore the arc of the parabola $y = x^2$ with $x \in [-1, 1]$. The same arc is the trace of the regular curve $\delta(u) = (u,\; u^2)$, $u \in [-1, 1]$, therefore the trace has no cusps at the origin and the vanishing of $\gamma'(0)$ is a property of the parametrization.
