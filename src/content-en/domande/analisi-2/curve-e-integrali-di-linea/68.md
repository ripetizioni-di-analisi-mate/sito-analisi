Along the quarter circle $\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t)$, $t \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, the function $f(x, y) = xy$ is integrated. What is $\displaystyle\int_\gamma f \, ds$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t)$, so $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 1$.

Substituting the parametrization into the function we obtain
$$
\int_\gamma xy \, ds = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos t \sin t \, dt = \left[ \frac{\sin^2 t}{2} \right]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
