What is the length of the cardioid
$$
\gamma(t) = \bigl( (1 + \cos t)\cos t,\; (1 + \cos t)\sin t \bigr), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi] ?
$$

- $4$
- $0$
- $16$
- $8$

## Explanation

Differentiating the components and simplifying with $\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$ we obtain
$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert^2 = (1 + \cos t)^2 + \sin^2 t = 2 + 2\cos t = 4\cos^2 \frac{t}{2} .
$$

Hence $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 2\left| \cos \dfrac{t}{2} \right|$, with the absolute value, because $\cos \dfrac{t}{2} < 0$ for $t \in (\pi, 2\pi]$.

By the symmetry of the absolute value with respect to $t = \pi$,
$$
L(\gamma) = 2 \int_0^{\pi} 2\cos \frac{t}{2} \, dt = 2 \left[ 4\sin \frac{t}{2} \right]_0^{\pi} = 8 .
$$
Without the absolute value the integral over $[0, 2\pi]$ would give $0$, which cannot be the length of a curve.
