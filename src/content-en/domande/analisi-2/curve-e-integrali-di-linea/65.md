Which of the following curves, defined for $t \in [0, 4\pi]$, is parametrized by arc length, that is, has speed equal to $1$ at every instant?

- $\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; t)$
- $\gamma(t) = \left( \cos \dfrac{t}{2},\; \sin \dfrac{t}{2},\; \dfrac{t}{2} \right)$
- $\gamma(t) = \left( \cos \sqrt{2}\,t,\; \sin \sqrt{2}\,t,\; \sqrt{2}\,t \right)$
- $\gamma(t) = \left( \cos \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{2}},\; \sin \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{2}},\; \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{2}} \right)$

## Explanation

For the helix $\gamma(t) = (\cos kt,\; \sin kt,\; kt)$ with $k > 0$ we have $\gamma'(t) = (-k\sin kt,\; k\cos kt,\; k)$, so
$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{k^2 + k^2} = \sqrt{2}\, k .
$$

The speed equals $1$ if and only if $k = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.

For $k = 1$, $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $k = \sqrt{2}$ one obtains instead the speeds $\sqrt{2}$, $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $2$. Therefore the curve parametrized by arc length is $\left( \cos \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{2}},\; \sin \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{2}},\; \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{2}} \right)$.
