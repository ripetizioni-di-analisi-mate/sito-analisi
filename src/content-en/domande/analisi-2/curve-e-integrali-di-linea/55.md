At which point does the tangent line to the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (t,\; t^2,\; t^3), \qquad t \in [0, 2],
$$
at the point $(1, 1, 1)$ intersect the plane $z = 0$?

- $\left( \dfrac{2}{3},\; \dfrac{1}{3},\; 0 \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{4}{3},\; \dfrac{5}{3},\; 0 \right)$
- $(0,\; 0,\; 0)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2},\; 0,\; 0 \right)$

## Explanation

The point $(1, 1, 1)$ is $\gamma(1)$. Since $\gamma'(t) = (1,\; 2t,\; 3t^2)$, the tangent vector at that point is $\gamma'(1) = (1,\; 2,\; 3)$.

The tangent line passes through $\gamma(1)$ with the direction of $\gamma'(1)$, hence
$$
r(s) = (1,\; 1,\; 1) + s\,(1,\; 2,\; 3) = (1 + s,\; 1 + 2s,\; 1 + 3s), \qquad s \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

The line intersects the plane $z = 0$ when $1 + 3s = 0$, that is, for $s = -\dfrac{1}{3}$. Therefore the intersection point is $r\!\left( -\dfrac{1}{3} \right) = \left( \dfrac{2}{3},\; \dfrac{1}{3},\; 0 \right)$.

The origin is the point where the curve meets the plane $z = 0$, and it does not lie on the tangent line.
