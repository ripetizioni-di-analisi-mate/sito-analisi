A thin rod occupies the segment of the $x$-axis between the points $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$, with linear density $\delta(x, y) = x^2$. What is the center of mass of the rod?

- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2},\; 0 \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{2}{3},\; 0 \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{3}{4},\; 0 \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{3},\; 0 \right)$

## Explanation

With the parametrization $\gamma(t) = (t, 0)$, $t \in [0, 1]$, we have $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 1$.

The mass is
$$
m = \int_0^1 t^2 \, dt = \frac{1}{3} ,
$$
and the moment with respect to the $y$-axis is $\displaystyle\int_0^1 t \cdot t^2 \, dt = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

Hence $\bar{x} = 3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{4}$, while $\bar{y} = 0$ because the rod lies on the $x$-axis. The center of mass is closer to the endpoint $(1, 0)$, where the density is larger.
