A wire lies along the segment $\gamma(t) = (t,\; t)$, $t \in [0, 1]$, with linear density $\delta(x, y) = 1 + x$. Which integral gives the mass of the wire?

- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (1 + t) \, dt$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 2\,(1 + t) \, dt$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\sqrt{2}} (1 + t) \, dt$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \sqrt{2}\,(1 + t) \, dt$

## Explanation

The mass is the integral of the first kind of the density, $m = \displaystyle\int_\gamma \delta \, ds = \int_0^1 \delta(\gamma(t))\, \lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert \, dt$.

Along the segment $x = t$, so $\delta(\gamma(t)) = 1 + t$. Since $\gamma'(t) = (1, 1)$, we have $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{2}$, therefore the mass is $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \sqrt{2}\,(1 + t) \, dt = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The factor $\sqrt{2}$ is the ratio between the length of the segment, equal to $\sqrt{2}$, and the length $1$ of the parameter interval.
