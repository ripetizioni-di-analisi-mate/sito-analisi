A wire lies along the semicircle
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t), \qquad t \in [0, \pi],
$$
with linear density $\delta(x, y) = x^2$. What is the mass of the wire?

- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The mass is the integral of the first kind of the density, $m = \displaystyle\int_\gamma \delta \, ds$. We have $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t} = 1$, and along the curve $\delta(\gamma(t)) = \cos^2 t$.

Since $\cos^2 t = \dfrac{1 + \cos 2t}{2}$, we get
$$
m = \int_0^\pi \cos^2 t \, dt = \int_0^\pi \frac{1 + \cos 2t}{2} \, dt = \frac{\pi}{2} .
$$
The density is nonnegative and vanishes only at the point $(0, 1)$, so the mass is positive.
