A homogeneous wire consists of the semicircle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ with $y \geq 0$ and of the diameter joining the points $(-1, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$. What is the center of mass of the wire?

::grafico{xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-0.5 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,3.1416" linee="-1,0 1,0" punti="-1,0,pieno;1,0,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="The wire: the semicircle of radius $1$ and its diameter"}

- $\left( 0,\; \dfrac{2}{\pi} \right)$
- $\left( 0,\; \dfrac{1}{\pi} \right)$
- $\left( 0,\; \dfrac{4}{\pi + 2} \right)$
- $\left( 0,\; \dfrac{2}{\pi + 2} \right)$

## Explanation

The wire is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis, so the center of mass belongs to the $y$-axis and $\bar{x} = 0$.

The wire is piecewise regular, therefore length and integrals are computed by adding the contributions of the two pieces. The semicircle $(\cos t, \sin t)$, $t \in [0, \pi]$, has length $\pi$ and
$$
\int y \, ds = \int_0^\pi \sin t \, dt = 2 ,
$$
while the diameter has length $2$ and $y = 0$ on it.

The total length is $L = \pi + 2$, hence
$$
\bar{y} = \frac{2 + 0}{\pi + 2} = \frac{2}{\pi + 2} .
$$
The center of mass is therefore $G = \left( 0,\; \dfrac{2}{\pi + 2} \right)$.
