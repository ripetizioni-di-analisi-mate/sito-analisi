## Explanation

We have $f'(x) = \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{2}\, x^{\frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{x}$, so $\sqrt{1 + f'(x)^2} = \sqrt{1 + x}$. By the **length formula** for a Cartesian curve the length is $\displaystyle\int_0^3 \sqrt{1 + x}\; dx$.

The integral is reducible to the immediate integral $\displaystyle\int (1 + x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\, dx = \frac{2}{3}(1 + x)^{\frac{3}{2}} + c$, therefore
$$
L = \frac{2}{3}\left( 4^{\frac{3}{2}} - 1 \right) = \frac{2}{3}\,(8 - 1) = \frac{14}{3}.
$$
