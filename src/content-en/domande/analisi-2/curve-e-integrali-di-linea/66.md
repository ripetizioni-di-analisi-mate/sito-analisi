A point travels along the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (3t,\; 3t^2,\; 2t^3), \qquad t \in [0, 2],
$$
starting from $\gamma(0)$. At which instant does the length of the arc traveled equal $5$?

- $t = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$
- $t = 1$
- $t = \dfrac{5}{3}$
- $t = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{5}{2}}$

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = (3,\; 6t,\; 6t^2)$, so
$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert^2 = 9 + 36t^2 + 36t^4 = \left( 3 + 6t^2 \right)^2 .
$$

Since $3 + 6t^2 > 0$, the speed is $3 + 6t^2$ and the **arc length** is
$$
s(t) = \int_0^t \left( 3 + 6\tau^2 \right) d\tau = 3t + 2t^3 .
$$

The equation $3t + 2t^3 = 5$ has the solution $t = 1$, and it is the only one because $s$ is strictly increasing. The value $t = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ solves instead $s'(t) = 5$, that is, it is the instant at which the speed equals $5$.
