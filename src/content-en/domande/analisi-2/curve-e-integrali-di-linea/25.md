The plane curve
$$
\gamma(t) = \left( t^{2},\; t - \frac{t^{3}}{3} \right), \qquad t \in [0, 1],
$$
joins the origin and the point $\left( 1, \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$. What is its length?

- $\dfrac{28}{15}$
- $\dfrac{5}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

Differentiating the components gives $\gamma'(t) = \left( 2t,\; 1 - t^{2} \right)$. The first component vanishes only at $t = 0$, where the second equals $1$, so the curve is regular. The square of the speed is $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert^2 = 4t^{2} + 1 - 2t^{2} + t^{4} = \left( 1 + t^{2} \right)^{2}$, therefore $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 1 + t^{2}$.

By the **length formula** we then have
$$
L(\gamma) = \int_0^1 \left( 1 + t^{2} \right) dt = \Bigl[ t + \frac{t^{3}}{3} \Bigr]_0^1 = \frac{4}{3} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{28}{15}$ is obtained by integrating $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert^2$ instead of $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert$, the value $\dfrac{5}{3}$ by writing the square root of a sum as the sum of the square roots, that is, $2t + 1 - t^{2}$, and the value $\dfrac{2}{3}$ by integrating only the second component of $\gamma'$.
