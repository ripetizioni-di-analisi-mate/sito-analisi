The curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cosh t,\; \sinh t,\; t), \qquad t \in [0, 1],
$$
has arc length $s(t)$ measured from $\gamma(0)$. What is the parameter $t$ expressed as a function of $s$?

- $t = \ln\left( \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}} + \sqrt{\dfrac{s^2}{2} + 1} \right)$
- $t = \ln\left( \sqrt{2}\, s + \sqrt{2s^2 + 1} \right)$
- $t = \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}}$
- $t = \ln\left( s + \sqrt{s^2 + 1} \right)$

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = (\sinh t,\; \cosh t,\; 1)$ and, by the identity $1 + \sinh^2 t = \cosh^2 t$,
$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{\sinh^2 t + \cosh^2 t + 1} = \sqrt{2}\cosh t .
$$

Hence the arc length is $s(t) = \displaystyle\int_0^t \sqrt{2}\cosh \tau \, d\tau = \sqrt{2}\sinh t$.

From $\sinh t = \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}}$ we obtain $t$ with the inverse hyperbolic sine, $\sinh^{-1} y = \ln\left( y + \sqrt{y^2 + 1} \right)$. Therefore
$$
t = \ln\left( \frac{s}{\sqrt{2}} + \sqrt{\frac{s^2}{2} + 1} \right).
$$
