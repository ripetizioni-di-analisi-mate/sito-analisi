The point $\gamma(t) = (\cosh t,\; \sinh t)$, with $t \in [-1, 1]$, describes the trace of a curve. Which set of the plane is this trace?

- The arc of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ between the points $(\cosh 1, -\sinh 1)$ and $(\cosh 1, \sinh 1)$
- The whole branch of the hyperbola $x^2 - y^2 = 1$ contained in the half-plane $x > 0$
- The arc of the branch of the hyperbola $x^2 - y^2 = 1$ with $x < 0$ between the points $(-\cosh 1, -\sinh 1)$ and $(-\cosh 1, \sinh 1)$
- The arc of the branch of the hyperbola $x^2 - y^2 = 1$ with $x > 0$ between the points $(\cosh 1, -\sinh 1)$ and $(\cosh 1, \sinh 1)$

## Explanation

For every $t$ the identity $\cosh^2 t - \sinh^2 t = 1$ holds, so every point of the trace satisfies $x^2 - y^2 = 1$.

Since $x = \cosh t \geq 1$, the points belong to the branch of the hyperbola contained in the half-plane $x > 0$.

The function $\sinh$ is strictly increasing, therefore $y = \sinh t$ takes all and only the values of $[-\sinh 1, \sinh 1]$. The trace is hence the **arc of the right branch** between the points $(\cosh 1, -\sinh 1)$ and $(\cosh 1, \sinh 1)$, and not the whole branch.
