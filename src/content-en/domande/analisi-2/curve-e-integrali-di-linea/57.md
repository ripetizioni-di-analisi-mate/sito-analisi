The curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t + \cos 2t,\; \sin t + \sin 2t), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi],
$$
passes through the point $(-1, 0)$ for $t = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and for $t = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$. What angle do the tangent vectors at these two instants form?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

Differentiating the components gives $\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t - 2\sin 2t,\; \cos t + 2\cos 2t)$.

For $t = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ we have $\sin t = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\sin 2t = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos t = \cos 2t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, hence $\gamma'\!\left( \dfrac{2\pi}{3} \right) = \left( \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\; -\dfrac{3}{2} \right)$. For $t = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ the sines change sign and the cosines stay the same, therefore $\gamma'\!\left( \dfrac{4\pi}{3} \right) = \left( -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\; -\dfrac{3}{2} \right)$.

The dot product of the two vectors equals $-\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{9}{4} = \dfrac{3}{2}$, and each of them has norm $\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{9}{4}} = \sqrt{3}$. Hence the cosine of the angle is
$$
\cos \theta = \frac{\frac{3}{2}}{\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{2} ,
$$
and the angle between the two tangent vectors is $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
