Let $\gamma(t) = (3\cos t,\; \sin t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, be the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{9} + y^2 = 1$ traversed once. What is $\displaystyle\int_\gamma x y^2 \, ds$?

- $\pi$
- $2\pi$
- $4\pi$
- $0$

## Explanation

By the definition of the line integral of the first kind,
$$
\int_\gamma x y^2 \, ds = \int_0^{2\pi} 3\cos t \sin^2 t \, \sqrt{9\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t} \; dt .
$$

With the substitution $t = \pi - \tau$ the term $\sin^2 t$ and the factor $\sqrt{9\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t}$ stay the same, while $\cos t$ becomes $-\cos \tau$. This substitution corresponds to the symmetry of the ellipse with respect to the $y$-axis, which exchanges $x$ with $-x$ and preserves the arc length element $ds$.

Since the function $x y^2$ takes opposite values at points symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis, the contributions of the two halves of the ellipse cancel out. Therefore the integral equals $0$, even though the integral giving the length of the ellipse cannot be computed with elementary functions.
