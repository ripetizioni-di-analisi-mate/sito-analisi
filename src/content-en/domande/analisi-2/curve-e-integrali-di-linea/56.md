At which points of the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; \cos t), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi),
$$
is the tangent vector parallel to the $xy$-plane?

- $(0,\; 1,\; 0)$ and $(0,\; -1,\; 0)$
- Only $(1,\; 0,\; 1)$
- $(1,\; 0,\; 1)$ and $(-1,\; 0,\; -1)$
- At no point

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t,\; -\sin t)$, which never vanishes, since $\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$.

A nonzero vector is parallel to the $xy$-plane when its third component is zero, so the condition is $\sin t = 0$. In $[0, 2\pi)$ this happens for $t = 0$ and for $t = \pi$.

Therefore the required points are $\gamma(0) = (1,\; 0,\; 1)$ and $\gamma(\pi) = (-1,\; 0,\; -1)$. The points $(0, 1, 0)$ and $(0, -1, 0)$ are those where the curve crosses the plane $z = 0$, and there the tangent vector equals $(\mp 1,\; 0,\; \mp 1)$.
