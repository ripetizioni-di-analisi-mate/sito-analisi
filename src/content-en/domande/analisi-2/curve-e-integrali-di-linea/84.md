The work of the field $F(x, y) = (xy,\; 0)$ from the origin to the point $(1, 1)$ is computed along two paths: the segment $y = x$ and the arc of parabola $y = x^2$. Which statement is true?

::grafico{xmin=-0.3 xmax=1.5 ymin=-0.3 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 curve="t,t,0,1;t,t^2,0,1" punti="0,0,pieno;1,1,pieno" etichette="0.25,0.62,y = x;0.75,0.32,y = x²" legenda=0 titolo="The two paths from the origin to the point $(1, 1)$"}

- Both works equal $\dfrac{1}{3}$, because the paths have the same endpoints
- Along the segment the work equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and along the parabola it equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- Along the segment the work equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and along the parabola it equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- Along the segment the work equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and along the parabola it equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The second component of the field is zero, so the work reduces to $\displaystyle\int_\gamma xy \, dx$.

Along the segment $\gamma_1(t) = (t, t)$, $t \in [0, 1]$, we have $x'(t) = 1$ and the work equals $\displaystyle\int_0^1 t^2 \, dt = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

Along the parabola $\gamma_2(t) = (t, t^2)$, $t \in [0, 1]$, we still have $x'(t) = 1$ and the work equals $\displaystyle\int_0^1 t^3 \, dt = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

The two values are different, so the work of this field **depends on the path** and not only on the endpoints.
