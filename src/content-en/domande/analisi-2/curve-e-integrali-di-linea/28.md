What is the length of the curve $\gamma(t) = (t^2,\; t^3)$, $t \in [0, 1]$?

- $\dfrac{13\sqrt{13}}{27}$
- $\dfrac{13\sqrt{13} - 8}{18}$
- $\dfrac{13\sqrt{13} - 1}{27}$
- $\dfrac{13\sqrt{13} - 8}{27}$

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = (2t,\; 3t^2)$, so $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{4t^2 + 9t^4} = |t|\sqrt{4 + 9t^2}$, which for $t \in [0, 1]$ equals $t\sqrt{4 + 9t^2}$.

The integral reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int t\,(4 + 9t^2)^{\frac{1}{2}} \, dt = \frac{1}{27}(4 + 9t^2)^{\frac{3}{2}} + c$. By the **length formula** we therefore get
$$
L(\gamma) = \frac{1}{27}\left( 13^{\frac{3}{2}} - 4^{\frac{3}{2}} \right) = \frac{13\sqrt{13} - 8}{27} .
$$
