The trace of the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (t,\; |t|), \qquad t \in [-1, 1],
$$
has a corner at the origin. What can be said about the regularity of $\gamma$?

- It is regular, because $\gamma'(t) \neq \mathbf{0}$ for every $t \neq 0$
- It is piecewise regular, with the subdivision $-1 < 0 < 1$
- It is not piecewise regular, because $\gamma$ is not differentiable at $t = 0$
- It is not a curve, because $\gamma$ is not continuous at $t = 0$

## Explanation

The function $|t|$ is continuous but not differentiable at $t = 0$, so $\gamma$ is continuous but not of class $C^1$ on $[-1, 1]$. Therefore $\gamma$ **is not regular**.

On the interval $[-1, 0]$ we have $\gamma(t) = (t, -t)$ and $\gamma'(t) = (1, -1)$; on the interval $[0, 1]$ we have $\gamma(t) = (t, t)$ and $\gamma'(t) = (1, 1)$.

Both restrictions are regular, so the curve is **piecewise regular** with the subdivision $-1 < 0 < 1$. The trace has a corner at the origin, as happens at the junction points of polygonal lines.
