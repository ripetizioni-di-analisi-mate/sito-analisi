Which curve is equivalent to $\gamma(t) = (t,\; t^2)$, $t \in [0, 1]$, and keeps its direction of traversal?

- $\delta(u) = (2u,\; 4u^2)$, $u \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{1}{2} \right]$
- $\delta(u) = \bigl( 1 - u,\; (1 - u)^2 \bigr)$, $u \in [0, 1]$
- $\delta(u) = (u^2,\; u^4)$, $u \in [0, 1]$
- $\delta(u) = (2u,\; 4u^2)$, $u \in [0, 1]$

## Explanation

With $\varphi(u) = 2u$ we have $\delta(u) = \gamma(\varphi(u))$. The function $\varphi$ is of class $C^1$, maps $\left[ 0, \dfrac{1}{2} \right]$ bijectively onto $[0, 1]$ and has $\varphi'(u) = 2 > 0$, so $\delta$ is **equivalent** to $\gamma$ and has the **same orientation**.

The curve $\bigl( 1 - u,\; (1 - u)^2 \bigr)$ corresponds to $\varphi(u) = 1 - u$, with $\varphi' = -1 < 0$, therefore it traverses the trace in the opposite direction. For $(u^2,\; u^4)$ we have $\varphi(u) = u^2$ with $\varphi'(0) = 0$, so $\varphi$ is not a change of parameter. The curve $(2u,\; 4u^2)$ with $u \in [0, 1]$ reaches the point $(2, 4)$ and has a different trace.
