- $y = -2x + \dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$
- $y = -x + \dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $y = -\dfrac{x}{2} + \sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

For $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ we have $\cos t = \sin t = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so the point of tangency is $\gamma\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \left(\sqrt{2},\; \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$. Since $\gamma'(t) = (-2\sin t,\; \cos t)$, the tangent vector at that point is $\gamma'\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \left(-\sqrt{2},\; \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$.

The tangent line has the direction of this vector, therefore its slope is the ratio of the second component to the first, $m = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{-\sqrt{2}} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The line passes through the point of tangency, so
$$
y - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = -\frac{1}{2}\left( x - \sqrt{2} \right),
$$
that is, $y = -\dfrac{x}{2} + \sqrt{2}$.
