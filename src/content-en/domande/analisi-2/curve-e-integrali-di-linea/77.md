A homogeneous wire has the shape of the polygonal line that goes from the point $(0, 2)$ to the origin and then from the origin to the point $(4, 0)$. What is its center of mass?

::grafico{xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=3 uguale=1 linee="0,2 0,0 4,0" punti="0,2,pieno;0,0,pieno;4,0,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="The L-shaped wire: a piece of length $2$ on the $y$-axis and one of length $4$ on the $x$-axis"}

- $\left( 1,\; \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{4}{3},\; \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$
- $(2,\; 1)$
- $\left( \dfrac{4}{3},\; \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$

## Explanation

The wire is piecewise regular, so length and integrals are the sum of the contributions of the two segments.

The vertical piece has length $2$ and $x = 0$ on it, while $\displaystyle\int y \, ds = \int_0^2 y \, dy = 2$. The horizontal piece has length $4$ and $y = 0$ on it, while $\displaystyle\int x \, ds = \int_0^4 x \, dx = 8$.

The total length is $L = 6$, therefore
$$
\bar{x} = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3}, \qquad \bar{y} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
The center of mass $\left( \dfrac{4}{3},\; \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$ is closer to the longer piece and does not belong to the wire.
