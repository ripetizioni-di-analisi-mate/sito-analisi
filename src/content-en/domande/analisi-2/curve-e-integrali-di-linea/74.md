A wire has length $2$. At the point at distance $s$ from one endpoint, with the distance measured along the wire, the linear density equals $\delta = 3s^2$. What is the mass of the wire?

- $12$
- $8$
- $24$
- $6$

## Explanation

The density is given as a function of the arc length parameter, so we use the arc length parametrization $\tilde{\gamma}(s)$, $s \in [0, 2]$, starting from the chosen endpoint.

This parametrization has $\lVert \tilde{\gamma}'(s) \rVert = 1$, therefore the mass is
$$
m = \int_{\tilde{\gamma}} \delta \, ds = \int_0^2 3s^2 \, ds = 8 .
$$

The value $24$ is obtained by multiplying the density at the final endpoint, $\delta = 12$, by the length, as if the density were constant.
