A wire lies along the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, with linear density $\delta(x, y) = 2 + x$. What is the mass of the wire?

- $2\pi$
- $6\pi$
- $4\pi$
- $8\pi$

## Explanation

With the parametrization $\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, we have $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 1$.

The mass is
$$
m = \int_0^{2\pi} (2 + \cos t) \, dt = 4\pi + \int_0^{2\pi} \cos t \, dt = 4\pi ,
$$
since the integral of $\cos t$ over a period is zero.

The contribution of $x$ is zero also by symmetry, because the circle is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis and $x$ takes opposite values at symmetric points. Therefore the mass is $4\pi$, that is, the mass of a homogeneous wire of density $2$.
