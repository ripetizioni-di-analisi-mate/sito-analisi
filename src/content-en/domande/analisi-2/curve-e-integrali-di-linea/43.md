Let $\gamma$ be the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$, traversed once counterclockwise. Which statement about the work of the field $F(x, y) = (x,\; y)$ along $\gamma$ is true?

- The work equals $18\pi$, because $\lVert F \rVert = 3$ on the circle and its length is $6\pi$
- The work is positive, because the field and the velocity vector point in the same direction
- The work is zero, because at every point the field is orthogonal to the tangent vector
- The work depends on the point where the parametrization starts

## Explanation

With $\gamma(t) = (3\cos t,\; 3\sin t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, we have $F(\gamma(t)) = (3\cos t,\; 3\sin t)$ and $\gamma'(t) = (-3\sin t,\; 3\cos t)$. The scalar product equals
$$
F(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t) = -9\cos t \sin t + 9\sin t \cos t = 0
$$
for every $t$, so the field is orthogonal to the tangent vector at every point of the circle.

The integral of the second kind is the integral of the first kind of the tangential component $F \cdot T$, which here is identically zero. Therefore the work is **zero**.
