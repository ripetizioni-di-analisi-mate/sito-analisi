Let $\gamma$ be the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$, traversed once **clockwise** starting from the point $(2, 0)$. What is the work of the field $F(x, y) = (y,\; 0)$ along $\gamma$?

- $6\pi$
- $-6\pi$
- $0$
- $12\pi$

## Explanation

The parametrization $(2\cos t,\; 3\sin t)$ traverses the ellipse counterclockwise. For the clockwise orientation we change the sign of the second component, hence $\gamma(t) = (2\cos t,\; -3\sin t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$. Indeed $\gamma(0) = (2, 0)$ and $\gamma'(0) = (0, -3)$, therefore the point starts moving downward.

We have $F(\gamma(t)) = (-3\sin t,\; 0)$ and $x'(t) = -2\sin t$, hence
$$
F(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t) = 6\sin^2 t .
$$

Since $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} \sin^2 t \, dt = \pi$, the work equals $6\pi$. With the counterclockwise orientation one would obtain $-6\pi$, because the line integral of the second kind changes sign with the orientation.
