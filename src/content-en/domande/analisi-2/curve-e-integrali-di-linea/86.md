Which curve, defined for $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, traverses the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ once **clockwise**?

- $\gamma(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t)$
- $\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; -\sin t)$
- $\gamma(t) = (-\cos t,\; -\sin t)$
- $\gamma(t) = (\cos 2t,\; \sin 2t)$

## Explanation

The orientation is read from the velocity vector at the initial point.

For $\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; -\sin t)$ one starts from $(1, 0)$ with $\gamma'(0) = (0, -1)$, that is downward, so the circle is traversed clockwise. The curve makes only one turn, because the angle $-t$ ranges over an interval of length $2\pi$.

The curve $(-\sin t,\; \cos t)$ starts from $(0, 1)$ with velocity $(-1, 0)$ and the curve $(-\cos t,\; -\sin t)$ starts from $(-1, 0)$ with velocity $(0, -1)$, therefore both traverse the circle counterclockwise. The curve $(\cos 2t,\; \sin 2t)$ turns counterclockwise and makes two turns.
