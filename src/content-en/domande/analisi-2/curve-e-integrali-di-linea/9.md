- $\dfrac{\pi^3}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}\, \pi^3}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}\, \pi^3}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi^3}{3}$

## Explanation

We have $\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t,\; 1)$, so $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + 1} = \sqrt{2}$. Along the curve the third component is $z = t$, therefore the integrand equals $t^2$.

By the definition of the **line integral of the first kind** we obtain
$$
\int_\gamma z^2 \, ds = \sqrt{2} \int_0^\pi t^2 \, dt = \frac{\sqrt{2}\, \pi^3}{3}.
$$
