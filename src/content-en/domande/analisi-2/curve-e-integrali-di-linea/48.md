Which curve has as its trace the graph of the function $y = \ln x$ for $x \in [1, e]$?

- $\gamma(t) = (\ln t,\; t)$, $t \in [1, e]$
- $\gamma(t) = (t,\; \ln t)$, $t \in [1, e]$
- $\gamma(t) = (e^t,\; t)$, $t \in [1, e]$
- $\gamma(t) = (t,\; e^t)$, $t \in [0, 1]$

## Explanation

The graph of a function $f : [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ is the trace of the **Cartesian curve** $\gamma(t) = (t, f(t))$, $t \in [a, b]$.

With $f(x) = \ln x$ and $[a, b] = [1, e]$ we obtain $\gamma(t) = (t,\; \ln t)$, $t \in [1, e]$.

The curve $(\ln t,\; t)$ has as its trace the graph of $y = e^x$ for $x \in [0, 1]$, because $x = \ln t$ gives $t = e^x$. The curve $(e^t,\; t)$ with $t \in [1, e]$ has its trace on the graph of the logarithm, but for $x \in [e, e^e]$.
