- $(1,\; 1)$
- $\left( \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\; \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{\pi},\; \dfrac{1}{\pi} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{2}{\pi},\; \dfrac{2}{\pi} \right)$

## Explanation

The wire is homogeneous, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{1}{L} \displaystyle\int_\gamma x \, ds$, with $L$ the length of the curve. Since $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t} = 1$, the length is $L = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and
$$
\bar{x} = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos t \, dt = \frac{2}{\pi}.
$$
The quarter circle is symmetric with respect to the bisector $y = x$, therefore the center of mass belongs to this line and $\bar{y} = \bar{x}$. The center of mass is therefore $G = \left( \dfrac{2}{\pi},\; \dfrac{2}{\pi} \right)$, which does *not* belong to the arc, since its distance from the origin is $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{\pi} < 1$.
