A point mass travels along the quarter of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ from the point $(1, 0)$ to the point $(0, 1)$ and is subject to the field $F(x, y) = (0,\; -1)$. What is the work done by the field?

- $1$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The path is parametrized by $\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t)$, $t \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, which goes from $(1, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$.

We have $\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t)$, so $F(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t) = -\cos t$.

Therefore the work is
$$
\int_\gamma F \cdot d\gamma = -\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos t \, dt = -1 .
$$
The work is negative because the point moves up, while the field points downward.
