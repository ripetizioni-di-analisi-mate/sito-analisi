A regular curve $\gamma$, a continuous function $f$ and a continuous field $F$ satisfy
$$
\int_\gamma f \, ds = 3 , \qquad \int_\gamma F \cdot d\gamma = 5 .
$$
What are the same integrals on the reversed curve $-\gamma$?

- $-3$ and $-5$
- $-3$ and $5$
- $3$ and $5$
- $3$ and $-5$

## Explanation

The integral of the first kind contains $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert$, which does not change when the orientation is reversed, so $\displaystyle\int_{-\gamma} f \, ds = 3$.

The integral of the second kind contains the vector $\gamma'(t)$, which reverses its direction on the reversed curve, so $\displaystyle\int_{-\gamma} F \cdot d\gamma = -5$.

Therefore on the reversed curve the two integrals equal $3$ and $-5$.
