Let $s(t)$ be the arc length of the curve $\gamma(t) = (t,\; t^2)$, $t \in [0, 2]$, measured from $\gamma(0)$. What is $s'(1)$?

- $\sqrt{3}$
- $5$
- $\sqrt{5}$
- $\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

By definition $s(t) = \displaystyle\int_0^t \lVert \gamma'(\tau) \rVert \, d\tau$.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus $s'(t) = \lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert$, so there is no need to compute the integral.

We have $\gamma'(t) = (1,\; 2t)$, therefore $\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{1 + 4t^2}$ and $s'(1) = \sqrt{5}$.
