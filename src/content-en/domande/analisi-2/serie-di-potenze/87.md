The expansion of $e^{-x^2}$ is integrated term by term on the interval $[0, 1]$ and the first three nonzero terms are added. Which approximate value of $\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{-x^2} \, dx$ is obtained?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{23}{30}$
- $\dfrac{13}{15}$
- $\dfrac{43}{30}$

## Explanation

From the exponential expansion with $x$ replaced by $-x^2$ we have, for every $x$,
$$
e^{-x^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n}}{n!} = 1 - x^2 + \frac{x^4}{2} - \cdots
$$

By **term-by-term integration**
$$
\int_0^1 e^{-x^2} \, dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n! \, (2n+1)} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{10} - \cdots
$$
The first three terms give $1 - \dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{30 - 10 + 3}{30} = \dfrac{23}{30}$. The series has alternating signs and decreasing terms, so the error is less than the next term, $\dfrac{1}{3! \cdot 7} = \dfrac{1}{42}$.
