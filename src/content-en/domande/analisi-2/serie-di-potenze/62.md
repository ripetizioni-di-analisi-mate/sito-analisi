For $|t| < 1$ we have $\dfrac{1}{1 + t} = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n t^n$. Integrating term by term from $0$ to $x$, with $|x| < 1$, which expansion is obtained for $\ln(1 + x)$?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, n \, x^{n-1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, x^{n+1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{n+1}}{n+1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{n}}{n+1}$

## Explanation

Since $\displaystyle\int_0^x \frac{dt}{1 + t} = \ln(1 + x)$, the required expansion is the integral from $0$ to $x$ of the geometric series of ratio $-t$.

By **term-by-term integration** each term is integrated: $\displaystyle\int_0^x (-1)^n t^n \, dt = \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{n+1}}{n+1}$. Hence, for $|x| < 1$,
$$
\ln(1 + x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{n+1}}{n+1} ,
$$
which with the index $k = n + 1$ is the standard expansion $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{k+1} \, x^k}{k}$. The series with $n \, x^{n-1}$ is obtained by differentiating, not by integrating.
