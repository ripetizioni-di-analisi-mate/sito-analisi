Let $T_4(x) = 1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{x^3}{6} + \dfrac{x^4}{24}$ be the Taylor polynomial of degree $4$ of $e^x$ centered at $0$. Which inequality is true?

- $\left| e - T_4(1) \right| \leq \dfrac{e}{120}$
- $\left| e - T_4(1) \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{120}$
- $\left| e - T_4(1) \right| \leq \dfrac{e}{720}$
- $\left| e - T_4(1) \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{720}$

## Explanation

By **Taylor's formula with Lagrange remainder** there exists $c \in (0, 1)$ such that
$$
e - T_4(1) = \frac{e^{c}}{5!} \cdot 1^5 = \frac{e^{c}}{120} .
$$

Since $0 < c < 1$, we have $1 < e^{c} < e$. Hence $\dfrac{1}{120} < e - T_4(1) < \dfrac{e}{120}$, and the inequality with $\dfrac{e}{120}$ is true.

The same chain shows that the error is greater than $\dfrac{1}{120}$, so the estimates with $\dfrac{1}{120}$, with $\dfrac{e}{720}$ and with $\dfrac{1}{720}$, all less than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{120}$, are false.
