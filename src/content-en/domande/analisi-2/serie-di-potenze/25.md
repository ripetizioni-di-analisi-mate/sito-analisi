What is the set of convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n \, (\ln n)^2}$?

- $(-1,\, 1)$
- $[-1,\, 1)$
- $[-1,\, 1]$
- $(-1,\, 1]$

## Explanation

The coefficient is $a_n = \dfrac{1}{n \, (\ln n)^2}$ and
$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{n}{n+1} \cdot \left( \frac{\ln n}{\ln (n+1)} \right)^{\! 2} \to 1 ,
$$
so by the **ratio test for the radius** we have $R = 1$.

At $x = \pm 1$ the absolute value of the general term is $\dfrac{1}{n \, (\ln n)^2}$. The function $\dfrac{1}{x \, (\ln x)^2}$ is positive and decreasing on $[2, +\infty)$ and
$$
\int_2^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x \, (\ln x)^2} = \left[ -\frac{1}{\ln x} \right]_2^{+\infty} = \frac{1}{\ln 2} ,
$$
so by the **integral test** the series of absolute values converges. The series therefore converges absolutely at both endpoints and $E = [-1,\, 1]$.
