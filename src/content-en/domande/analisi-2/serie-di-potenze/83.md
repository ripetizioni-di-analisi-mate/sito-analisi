Let $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^n$ be the Maclaurin series of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + x + x^2}$. What is $a_{10}$?

- $1$
- $-1$
- $0$
- $2$

## Explanation

Since $(1 - x)(1 + x + x^2) = 1 - x^3$, for $|x| < 1$ we have
$$
\frac{1}{1 + x + x^2} = \frac{1 - x}{1 - x^3} = (1 - x) \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} x^{3k} .
$$
The product contains the powers $x^{3k}$ with coefficient $1$ and the powers $x^{3k+1}$ with coefficient $-1$, while the powers $x^{3k+2}$ have coefficient $0$.

Since $10 = 3 \cdot 3 + 1$, the power $x^{10}$ is of the type $x^{3k+1}$, so its coefficient is $-1$. By the **uniqueness of the power series expansion** this is the coefficient of the Maclaurin series of $f$.
