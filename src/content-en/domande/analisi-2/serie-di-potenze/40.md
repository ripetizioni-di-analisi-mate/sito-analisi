What is the coefficient $a_n$ of $x^n$ in the Maclaurin expansion of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{(1 - x)(1 - 2x)}$?

- $2^n - 1$
- $2^{n+1} - 1$
- $2^{n+1} + 1$
- $2^n + 1$

## Explanation

We decompose the function into partial fractions. Imposing $1 = A \, (1 - 2x) + B \, (1 - x)$ and setting $x = 1$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ we find $A = -1$ and $B = 2$, so
$$
f(x) = \frac{2}{1 - 2x} - \frac{1}{1 - x} .
$$
For $|x| < \dfrac{1}{2}$ both geometric series converge, and
$$
f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 2 \cdot 2^n x^n - \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( 2^{n+1} - 1 \right) x^n .
$$
By the **uniqueness of the expansion** the coefficient of $x^n$ is $a_n = 2^{n+1} - 1$; for example $a_0 = 1 = f(0)$.
