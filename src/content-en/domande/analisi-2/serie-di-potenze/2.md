What is the set of convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \, 3^n \, x^n$?

- $(-3,\, 3)$
- $\left[ -\dfrac{1}{3},\, \dfrac{1}{3} \right]$
- $\left[ -\dfrac{1}{3},\, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{3},\, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$

## Explanation

The coefficient is $a_n = n \, 3^n$, so
$$
\frac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|} = \frac{3 \, (n+1)}{n} \to 3 .
$$
By the **ratio test for the radius** we therefore have $R = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

At $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ the general term equals $n \, 3^n \cdot \dfrac{1}{3^n} = n$, and at $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ it equals $(-1)^n \, n$. At both endpoints the absolute value of the general term is $n$, which does not tend to $0$, so by the **necessary condition for convergence** the series does not converge at those points. Consequently $E = \left( -\dfrac{1}{3},\, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$.
