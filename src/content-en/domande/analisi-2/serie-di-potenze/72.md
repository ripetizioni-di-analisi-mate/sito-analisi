What is the Taylor polynomial of degree $4$ of $f(x) = \sin x$ centered at $0$?

- $x - \dfrac{x^3}{6} + \dfrac{x^4}{24}$
- $x - \dfrac{x^3}{6}$
- $x - \dfrac{x^3}{6} + \dfrac{x^5}{120}$
- $x - \dfrac{x^3}{3}$

## Explanation

The Taylor polynomial of degree $4$ is the partial sum of the Maclaurin series up to the power $x^4$. The expansion of the sine is
$$
\sin x = x - \frac{x^3}{6} + \frac{x^5}{120} - \cdots ,
$$
and it contains only odd powers, because $\sin x$ is odd.

The coefficient of $x^4$ is therefore zero, that is $\sin^{(4)}(0) = \sin 0 = 0$, and the term $\dfrac{x^5}{120}$ has degree greater than $4$. Therefore $T_4(x) = x - \dfrac{x^3}{6}$, the same polynomial as $T_3$.
