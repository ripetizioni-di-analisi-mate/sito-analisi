The limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{1}{\sin^2 x} \right)$ has the form $+\infty - \infty$. What is its value?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{6}$

## Explanation

Bringing to a common denominator gives
$$
\frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{1}{\sin^2 x} = \frac{\sin^2 x - x^2}{x^2 \sin^2 x} .
$$
From the expansion $\sin x = x - \dfrac{x^3}{6} + o(x^3)$ one obtains $\sin^2 x = x^2 - \dfrac{x^4}{3} + o(x^4)$, because twice the product of $x$ and $-\dfrac{x^3}{6}$ equals $-\dfrac{x^4}{3}$.

Hence the numerator equals $-\dfrac{x^4}{3} + o(x^4)$ and the denominator equals $x^4 + o(x^4)$. Therefore the limit equals $-\dfrac{1}{3}$.
