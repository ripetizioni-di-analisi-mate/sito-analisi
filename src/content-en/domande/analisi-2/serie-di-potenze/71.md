The Taylor polynomial of degree $3$ of a function $f$, centered at $0$, is $T_3(x) = 2 - x + 4x^3$. What is $f'''(0)$?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $24$
- $12$

## Explanation

The coefficient of $x^k$ in the Taylor polynomial centered at $0$ is $\dfrac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}$. The coefficient of $x^3$ in $T_3$ is $4$, so
$$
\frac{f'''(0)}{3!} = 4 .
$$
Therefore $f'''(0) = 4 \cdot 3! = 24$.

From the other coefficients one also reads $f(0) = 2$, $f'(0) = -1$ and $f''(0) = 0$, because the term in $x^2$ is missing from $T_3$.
