The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^n$ has radius of convergence $2$, and at $x = 2$ it converges absolutely. What happens at $x = -2$?

- The series does not converge
- The series converges, but not absolutely
- It cannot be determined
- The series converges absolutely

## Explanation

At $x = -2$ the absolute value of the general term is
$$
|a_n (-2)^n| = |a_n| \, 2^n = |a_n \, 2^n| ,
$$
that is, it coincides with the absolute value of the general term at $x = 2$.

By hypothesis the series $\sum |a_n \, 2^n|$ converges, so the series of absolute values at $x = -2$ also converges. Therefore at $x = -2$ the series **converges absolutely**.

The hypothesis of *absolute* convergence is necessary: the series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{x^n}{n \, 2^n}$ converges at $x = -2$ but not at $x = 2$.
