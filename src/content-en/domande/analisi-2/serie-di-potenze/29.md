Let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n \, (n+1)}$ for $|x| \leq 1$. What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x)$?

- $1$
- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The ratio between two consecutive coefficients is $\dfrac{n}{n+2} \to 1$, so the radius of convergence is $R = 1$. At $x = 1$ the series is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n \, (n+1)}$, which converges.

Since the series converges at the endpoint $x = 1$, by **Abel's theorem** the limit as $x \to 1^-$ of $f(x)$ is the sum of the series at that point. Since $\dfrac{1}{n \, (n+1)} = \dfrac{1}{n} - \dfrac{1}{n+1}$, the series is telescoping and its $N$-th partial sum is $1 - \dfrac{1}{N+1}$, which tends to $1$. Hence the limit is $1$.
