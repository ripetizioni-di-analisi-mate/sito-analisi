The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (3x - 1)^n$ is a power series. What is its radius of convergence?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $1$
- $3$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

Factoring out the $3$ we have $(3x - 1)^n = 3^n \left( x - \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{n}$, so the series is centered at $x_0 = \dfrac{1}{3}$ with coefficients $a_n = 3^n$.

The ratio between two consecutive coefficients is $3$, therefore by the **ratio test for the radius** we have $R = \dfrac{1}{3}$. The same result follows from the geometric series of ratio $3x - 1$, which converges if and only if $|3x - 1| < 1$, that is $\left| x - \dfrac{1}{3} \right| < \dfrac{1}{3}$.

The open interval of convergence is $\left( 0,\, \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$, which has length $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and radius $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
