For which value of the real parameter $a$ does the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x - x + a x^3}{x^5}$ exist and is finite, and what is its value?

- $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$ and the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{120}$
- $a = -\dfrac{1}{6}$ and the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{120}$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$ and the limit equals $0$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$ and the limit equals $-\dfrac{1}{120}$

## Explanation

From the expansion of the sine up to the fifth order we obtain
$$
\sin x - x + a x^3 = \left( a - \frac{1}{6} \right) x^3 + \frac{x^5}{120} + o(x^5) .
$$

If $a \neq \dfrac{1}{6}$, the quotient by $x^5$ behaves like $\dfrac{a - \frac{1}{6}}{x^2}$, which tends to $\pm\infty$. Hence the limit is finite only for $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$.

For $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$ the numerator equals $\dfrac{x^5}{120} + o(x^5)$, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{120}$.
