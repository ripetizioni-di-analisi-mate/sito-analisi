A power series centered at $x_0 = 1$ has radius of convergence $3$. Which of the following sets can be its set of convergence?

- $[-2,\, 4)$
- $(-3,\, 3)$
- $[-2,\, 1]$
- $(-1,\, 5]$

## Explanation

By the **radius of convergence theorem** the series converges at every $x$ with $|x - 1| < 3$ and does not converge at every $x$ with $|x - 1| > 3$. Hence the set of convergence $E$ satisfies
$$
(-2,\, 4) \subseteq E \subseteq [-2,\, 4] .
$$
The set $[-2,\, 4)$ satisfies these inclusions: it is the open interval with the endpoint $-2$ added, a situation that occurs, for example, with the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x - 1)^n}{n \, 3^n}$.

The set $(-3,\, 3)$ is centered at $0$, the set $[-2,\, 1]$ does not contain the points of $(1, 4)$, and the set $(-1,\, 5]$ does not contain the points of $(-2, -1]$. Therefore none of the three satisfies the inclusions.
