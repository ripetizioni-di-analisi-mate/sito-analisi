What is the Maclaurin expansion of $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x \frac{dt}{1 + t^3}$ for $|x| < 1$?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{3n+1}}{3n+1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{3n+1}}{3n+1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{3n+1}}{n+1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, x^{3n+1}$

## Explanation

The geometric series of ratio $-t^3$ gives, for $|t| < 1$,
$$
\frac{1}{1 + t^3} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, t^{3n} .
$$
This series has radius $1$, so for $|x| < 1$ the theorem on **term-by-term integration** applies on the interval with endpoints $0$ and $x$. Since $\displaystyle\int_0^x t^{3n} \, dt = \frac{x^{3n+1}}{3n+1}$, we have
$$
F(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{3n+1}}{3n+1} , \qquad |x| < 1 .
$$
