For $|x| < 1$ let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}$. Which expression gives $f(x)$?

- $-\ln(1 - x)$
- $\arctan x$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \ln \dfrac{1 - x}{1 + x}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \ln \dfrac{1 + x}{1 - x}$

## Explanation

The series has radius $1$. By **term-by-term differentiation**, for $|x| < 1$
$$
f'(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^{2n} = \frac{1}{1 - x^2} ,
$$
because the resulting series is the geometric series of ratio $x^2$.

Since $f(0) = 0$, we have $f(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x \frac{dt}{1 - t^2}$. With the partial fraction decomposition $\dfrac{1}{1 - t^2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \left( \dfrac{1}{1 - t} + \dfrac{1}{1 + t} \right)$ we obtain
$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{2} \bigl( -\ln(1 - x) + \ln(1 + x) \bigr) = \frac{1}{2} \ln \frac{1 + x}{1 - x} .
$$
The function $\arctan x$ has the expansion with alternating signs, and $-\ln(1 - x)$ the one with all the powers.
