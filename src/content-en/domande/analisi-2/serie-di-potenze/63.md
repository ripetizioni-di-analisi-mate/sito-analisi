Differentiating term by term the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$, the Maclaurin series of which function is obtained?

- $-\cos x$
- $\cos x$
- $\sin x$
- $-\sin x$

## Explanation

The series has radius $+\infty$, so by **term-by-term differentiation** it can be differentiated on all of $\mathbb{R}$. The derivative of the general term is
$$
\frac{(-1)^n \, (2n+1) \, x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!} = \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n}}{(2n)!} ,
$$
because $(2n+1)! = (2n+1) \cdot (2n)!$.

The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$ is the expansion of $\cos x$. This is consistent with the fact that the starting series is the expansion of $\sin x$ and that the derivative of $\sin x$ is $\cos x$.
