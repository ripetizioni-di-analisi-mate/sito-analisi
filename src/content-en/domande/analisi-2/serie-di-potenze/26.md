Which of the following series has set of convergence $(-1,\, 1]$?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{\sqrt{n}}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n \sqrt{n}}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, x^n$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^n}{\sqrt{n}}$

## Explanation

All four series have radius $1$, because the ratio between the absolute values of two consecutive coefficients tends to $1$, so the four series differ in their behavior at the endpoints $x = \pm 1$.

For the series $\sum \dfrac{(-1)^n x^n}{\sqrt{n}}$, at $x = 1$ the general term is $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ and the series converges by the **Leibniz test**. At $x = -1$ the general term is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$, and the generalized harmonic series with exponent $\dfrac{1}{2}$ diverges. Hence this series has set of convergence $(-1,\, 1]$.

The others have different sets. The series $\sum \dfrac{x^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ converges at $x = -1$ and diverges at $x = 1$, so it has $E = [-1,\, 1)$; the series $\sum \dfrac{x^n}{n \sqrt{n}}$ converges absolutely at both endpoints, so it has $E = [-1,\, 1]$; at $x = \pm 1$ the series $\sum (-1)^n x^n$ has a general term of absolute value $1$, so it has $E = (-1,\, 1)$.
