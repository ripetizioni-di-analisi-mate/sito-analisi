What is the Maclaurin expansion of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 - x} + \dfrac{1}{1 + x}$ for $|x| < 1$?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^{2n}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 2 \, x^{n}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 2 \, x^{2n}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 2 \, x^{2n+1}$

## Explanation

For $|x| < 1$ we have the geometric expansions
$$
\frac{1}{1 - x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{1 + x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n x^n .
$$
Adding term by term, the coefficient of $x^n$ is $1 + (-1)^n$, which equals $2$ for even $n$ and $0$ for odd $n$.

Therefore $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 2 \, x^{2n}$. The result is consistent with $f(x) = \dfrac{2}{1 - x^2}$, an even function.
