The power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^n$ has set of convergence $[-2,\, 2)$. Which statement about the differentiated series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \, a_n x^{n-1}$ is certainly true?

- Its set of convergence is $[-2,\, 2)$
- Its radius of convergence is $2$
- Its radius of convergence is $1$
- It converges at $x = -2$

## Explanation

The given series converges at $x = -2$, so its radius satisfies $R \geq 2$; it does not converge at $x = 2$, so $R \leq 2$. Therefore $R = 2$, and by the theorem on **term-by-term differentiation** the differentiated series has the *same* radius $2$.

The behavior at the endpoints, on the other hand, can change. The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n \, 2^n}$ has set of convergence $[-2,\, 2)$, and its differentiated series is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^{n-1}}{2^n}$. At $x = -2$ the absolute value of the general term of this series is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and does not tend to $0$, so the differentiated series does not converge at $x = -2$.
