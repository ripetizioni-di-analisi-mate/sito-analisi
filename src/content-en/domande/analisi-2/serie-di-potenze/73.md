Which of the following functions is **not** analytic at $x_0 = 0$?

- $e^{x^2}$
- $\sin(x^3)$
- $\dfrac{1}{2 - x}$
- $x \, |x|$

## Explanation

A function analytic at $0$ coincides in a neighborhood of $0$ with the sum of a power series, so it is of class $C^{\infty}$ in that neighborhood.

The function $g(x) = x \, |x|$ equals $x^2$ for $x \geq 0$ and $-x^2$ for $x < 0$, so $g'(x) = 2|x|$. The function $2|x|$ is not differentiable at $0$, therefore $g$ is not of class $C^2$ in any neighborhood of $0$ and it is not analytic at $0$.

The other three functions are analytic at $0$: $e^{x^2}$ and $\sin(x^3)$ are obtained by substitution from the standard expansions, valid on all of $\mathbb{R}$, and $\dfrac{1}{2 - x} = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{2^{n+1}}$ for $|x| < 2$.
