- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1) \, (2n+1)!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n+2}}{(2n+2) \, (2n+1)!}$

## Explanation

From the standard expansion $\sin t = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, t^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$, dividing by $t \neq 0$ we obtain
$$
\frac{\sin t}{t} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, t^{2n}}{(2n+1)!} .
$$
At $t = 0$ the series equals $1$, which is the value of the extension, so the equality holds for every $t \in \mathbb{R}$ and the series has radius $+\infty$.

Since the radius is $+\infty$, by the theorem on **term-by-term integration** the series can be integrated term by term from $0$ to $x$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Since $\displaystyle\int_0^x t^{2n} \, dt = \frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}$, we have
$$
F(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1) \, (2n+1)!} , \qquad x \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
