A power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^n$ converges at $x = 3$ and does not converge at $x = -5$. What can be said about its radius of convergence $R$?

- $R = 3$
- $R = 4$
- $3 \leq R \leq 5$
- $3 < R < 5$

## Explanation

The series is centered at $0$. If $R < 3$, the point $x = 3$ would be at a distance greater than $R$ from the center, and by the **radius of convergence theorem** the series would not converge at that point. Since the series converges at $x = 3$, we therefore have $R \geq 3$.

If $R > 5$, the point $x = -5$ would be at distance $5 < R$ from the center and the series would converge at that point. Since the series does not converge at $x = -5$, we have $R \leq 5$.

Both endpoints are possible. The series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{x^n}{n^2 \, 3^n}$ has $R = 3$, converges at $x = 3$ and does not converge at $x = -5$; the series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{x^n}{5^n}$ has $R = 5$ and satisfies the same two conditions. Therefore the only certain information is $3 \leq R \leq 5$.
