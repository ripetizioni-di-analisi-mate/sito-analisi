Which power series expansion centered at $0$ holds for $f(x) = \ln(2 + x)$ when $-2 < x \leq 2$?

- $\ln 2 + \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{n \, 2^n}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{n \, 2^n}$
- $\ln 2 + \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, 2^n \, x^n}{n}$
- $\ln 2 + \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{n}$

## Explanation

Factoring out the $2$ we have $\ln(2 + x) = \ln 2 + \ln \left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{2} \right)$.

The expansion of $\ln(1 + y)$ holds for $-1 < y \leq 1$. With $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ the condition becomes $-2 < x \leq 2$, and
$$
\ln \left( 1 + \frac{x}{2} \right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n} \left( \frac{x}{2} \right)^{n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{n \, 2^n} .
$$
Therefore $\ln(2 + x) = \ln 2 + \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{n \, 2^n}$; the constant term $\ln 2$ is the value $f(0)$.
