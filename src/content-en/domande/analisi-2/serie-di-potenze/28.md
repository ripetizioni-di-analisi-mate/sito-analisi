The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}$ has radius of convergence $1$. On which of the following intervals does the series converge normally?

- $(-1,\, 1)$
- $[-1,\, 0]$
- $\left[ -\dfrac{9}{10},\, \dfrac{9}{10} \right]$
- $[0,\, 1)$

## Explanation

On the interval $\left[ -\dfrac{9}{10}, \dfrac{9}{10} \right]$ we have $\sup \left| \dfrac{x^n}{n} \right| = \dfrac{1}{n} \left( \dfrac{9}{10} \right)^{\! n}$, and the series of these numbers converges by comparison with the geometric series of ratio $\dfrac{9}{10}$. Hence on this interval the convergence is **normal**, as guaranteed by the theorem on **normal convergence on interior compact sets**, because $\dfrac{9}{10} < 1$.

On the other three intervals the supremum of $\left| \dfrac{x^n}{n} \right|$ is $\dfrac{1}{n}$. On $[-1, 0]$ it is attained at $x = -1$, while on $(-1, 1)$ and on $[0, 1)$ it is the limit as $x \to 1^-$. The harmonic series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n}$ diverges, therefore on these intervals the convergence is not normal.
