In the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^{n^2} = 1 + x + x^4 + x^9 + \cdots$ only the powers whose exponent is a perfect square appear. What is its set of convergence?

- $[-1,\, 1]$
- $\{ 0 \}$
- $(-1,\, 1)$
- $[-1,\, 1)$

## Explanation

The sequence of coefficients contains infinitely many zeros, so the ratio test is applied to the terms $u_n = x^{n^2}$ with $x \neq 0$. Since $(n+1)^2 - n^2 = 2n + 1$, we have
$$
\frac{|u_{n+1}|}{|u_n|} = |x|^{2n+1} .
$$

If $|x| < 1$ the ratio tends to $0$ and the series converges absolutely. If $|x| > 1$ the ratio tends to $+\infty$ and the series does not converge, so the radius is $1$.

At $x = 1$ the general term equals $1$, and at $x = -1$ it equals $(-1)^{n^2} = (-1)^n$. In both cases the general term does not tend to $0$, therefore the set of convergence is $(-1,\, 1)$.
