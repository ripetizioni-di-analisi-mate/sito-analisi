What is the set of convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{5^{\,n + (-1)^n}}$?

- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{5},\, \dfrac{1}{5} \right)$
- $[-5,\, 5]$
- $(-5,\, 5)$
- $[-5,\, 5)$

## Explanation

The coefficient is $a_n = 5^{-n - (-1)^n}$, so the ratio $\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = 5^{-1 + 2(-1)^n}$ equals $5$ for even $n$ and $\dfrac{1}{125}$ for odd $n$. The ratio has no limit, therefore the ratio test for the radius does not apply.

The $n$-th root equals $\sqrt[n]{a_n} = 5^{-1 - \frac{(-1)^n}{n}}$, and since $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n} \to 0$ it tends to $\dfrac{1}{5}$. By the **root test for the radius** we therefore have $R = 5$.

At $x = \pm 5$ the absolute value of the general term is $\dfrac{5^n}{5^{\,n + (-1)^n}} = 5^{-(-1)^n}$, which equals $\dfrac{1}{5}$ for even $n$ and $5$ for odd $n$. The general term does not tend to $0$, so the series does not converge at either endpoint and $E = (-5,\, 5)$.
