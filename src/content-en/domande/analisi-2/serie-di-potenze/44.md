What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1) \, 3^n}$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3} \, \pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3} \, \pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The expansion $\arctan x = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}$ holds for $|x| \leq 1$. With $x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ we have $x^{2n+1} = \dfrac{1}{3^n \sqrt{3}}$, so
$$
\arctan \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1) \, 3^n} .
$$
Since $\arctan \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, the sum of the series is $\sqrt{3} \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3} \, \pi}{6}$.
