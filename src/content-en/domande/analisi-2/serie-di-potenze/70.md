Let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^n$ for $|x| < R$, with $R > 0$, and let $f$ be odd, that is $f(-x) = -f(x)$. What can be said about the coefficients?

- All the coefficients of even index are zero
- All the coefficients of odd index are zero
- Only the coefficient $a_0$ is certainly zero
- The radius of convergence is necessarily $+\infty$

## Explanation

For $|x| < R$ we have
$$
f(-x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n a_n x^n
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
-f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-a_n) \, x^n .
$$
The two series have the same sum on an interval centered at $0$, so by the **uniqueness of the power series expansion** they have the same coefficients: $(-1)^n a_n = -a_n$ for every $n$.

For odd $n$ the equality is always true. For even $n$ it becomes $a_n = -a_n$, that is $a_n = 0$. Therefore all the coefficients of even index are zero, as in the expansion of $\sin x$.
