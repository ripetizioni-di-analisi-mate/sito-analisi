Let $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$. What is $f^{(6)}(0)$?

- $720$
- $-1$
- $-720$
- $-\dfrac{1}{720}$

## Explanation

The geometric series of ratio $-x^2$ gives, for $|x| < 1$,
$$
\frac{1}{1 + x^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, x^{2n} = 1 - x^2 + x^4 - x^6 + \cdots
$$
By the **uniqueness of the expansion** this is the Maclaurin series of $f$, so the coefficient of $x^6$, which is $-1$, equals $\dfrac{f^{(6)}(0)}{6!}$. Therefore $f^{(6)}(0) = -6! = -720$.
