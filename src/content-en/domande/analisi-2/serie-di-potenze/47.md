The coefficient of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^3 x^n$ grows like a power of $n$. What is the radius of convergence of the series?

- $3$
- $+\infty$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

The ratio between two consecutive coefficients is
$$
\frac{(n+1)^3}{n^3} = \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^{\! 3} \to 1 ,
$$
so by the **ratio test for the radius** we have $R = 1$.

The exponent $3$ does not affect the radius, because $\sqrt[n]{n^3} \to 1$: a coefficient that is polynomial in $n$ always gives radius $1$. The exponent matters only in the study of the endpoints, where at $x = \pm 1$ the general term has absolute value $n^3$ and the series does not converge.
