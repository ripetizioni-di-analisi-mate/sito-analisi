What is the Maclaurin expansion of the function $f(x) = x \cos(3x)$?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, 3^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n)!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, 9^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{9^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n)!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, 9^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n)!}$

## Explanation

The standard expansion $\cos y = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, y^{2n}}{(2n)!}$ holds for every $y \in \mathbb{R}$. Substituting $y = 3x$ we have $(3x)^{2n} = 9^n x^{2n}$, so
$$
\cos(3x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, 9^n \, x^{2n}}{(2n)!} , \qquad x \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
Multiplying each term by $x$ we obtain $x \cos(3x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, 9^n \, x^{2n+1}}{(2n)!}$, and by the **uniqueness of the expansion** this series is the Maclaurin series of $f$.
