At which points does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ converge absolutely?

- $(-1,\, 1)$
- $(-1,\, 1]$
- $[-1,\, 1)$
- $[-1,\, 1]$

## Explanation

The absolute value of the general term is $\dfrac{|x|^n}{\sqrt{n}}$, and the ratio between two consecutive coefficients is $\dfrac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n+1}} \to 1$, so the radius of convergence is $R = 1$. By the **radius of convergence theorem** the series converges absolutely at every $x$ with $|x| < 1$.

At $x = 1$ and at $x = -1$ the series of absolute values is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$, a generalized harmonic series with exponent $\dfrac{1}{2} \leq 1$, which diverges. Therefore the convergence is not absolute at either endpoint, and the required set is $(-1,\, 1)$.

At $x = 1$ the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ converges by the alternating series test, so the interval of pointwise convergence is $(-1,\, 1]$, which contains one more point than the set of absolute convergence.
