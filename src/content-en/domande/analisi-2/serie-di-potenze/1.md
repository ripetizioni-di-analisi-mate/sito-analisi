What is the set of convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n^2 \, 2^n}$?

- $(-2,\, 2)$
- $[-2,\, 2]$
- $[-2,\, 2)$
- $\left[ -\dfrac{1}{2},\, \dfrac{1}{2} \right]$

## Explanation

The coefficient is $a_n = \dfrac{1}{n^2 \, 2^n}$, so
$$
\frac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{n}{n+1} \right)^{\! 2} \to \frac{1}{2} .
$$
By the **ratio test for the radius** we therefore have $R = 2$, and the series converges absolutely in $(-2, 2)$.

At $x = 2$ and at $x = -2$ the absolute value of the general term equals $\dfrac{2^n}{n^2 \, 2^n} = \dfrac{1}{n^2}$. Since the series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges, the given series **converges absolutely** at both endpoints, so $E = [-2,\, 2]$.
