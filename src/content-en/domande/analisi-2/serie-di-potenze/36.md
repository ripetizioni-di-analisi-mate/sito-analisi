Let $f(x) = e^{-\frac{1}{x^2}}$ for $x \neq 0$, with $f(0) = 0$. The function is of class $C^{\infty}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ and $f^{(n)}(0) = 0$ for every $n$. Which statement is true?

- The Maclaurin series of $f$ has radius of convergence $0$
- The Maclaurin series of $f$ converges for every $x$, but its sum differs from $f(x)$ for every $x \neq 0$
- The Maclaurin series of $f$ coincides with $f$ in a neighborhood of $0$
- For every $x \neq 0$ the remainder $f(x) - T_n(x)$ tends to $0$ as $n \to \infty$

## Explanation

All the coefficients $\dfrac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}$ are zero, so the Maclaurin series of $f$ is the identically zero series. This series converges for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, with radius $+\infty$ and sum $0$.

For $x \neq 0$ we have $f(x) = e^{-\frac{1}{x^2}} > 0$, so the sum of the series differs from $f(x)$ at every point other than $0$, and $f$ **is not analytic** at $0$. For the same reason every Taylor polynomial $T_n$ is zero, therefore the remainder $f(x) - T_n(x) = f(x)$ does not tend to $0$, in agreement with the expandability criterion.
