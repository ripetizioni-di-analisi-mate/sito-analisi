What is the region of validity of the Maclaurin expansion of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{2 + x^3}$, that is, the set of all points at which the series converges to $f(x)$?

- $\left( -\sqrt[3]{2},\, \sqrt[3]{2} \right)$
- $(-2,\, 2)$
- $(-8,\, 8)$
- $\left[ -\sqrt[3]{2},\, \sqrt[3]{2} \right]$

## Explanation

Factoring $2$ out of the denominator we have
$$
\frac{1}{2 + x^3} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{1 - \left( -\dfrac{x^3}{2} \right)} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{3n}}{2^{n+1}} ,
$$
and the geometric series of ratio $-\dfrac{x^3}{2}$ converges if and only if $\left| \dfrac{x^3}{2} \right| < 1$.

The condition $|x|^3 < 2$ is equivalent to $|x| < \sqrt[3]{2}$. At $x = \pm \sqrt[3]{2}$ the absolute value of the general term is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and does not tend to $0$, so the series does not converge at the endpoints and the region of validity is $\left( -\sqrt[3]{2},\, \sqrt[3]{2} \right)$.
