In the Taylor series of $f(x) = \cos x$ centered at $x_0 = \pi$, what is the coefficient of $(x - \pi)^4$?

- $\dfrac{1}{24}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{24}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

We set $y = x - \pi$. By the addition formula, $\cos x = \cos(\pi + y) = -\cos y$.

From the expansion of the cosine, valid on all of $\mathbb{R}$,
$$
\cos x = -\cos y = -1 + \frac{y^2}{2} - \frac{y^4}{24} + \cdots = -1 + \frac{(x - \pi)^2}{2} - \frac{(x - \pi)^4}{24} + \cdots
$$
By the **uniqueness of the power series expansion** this is the Taylor series of $\cos x$ centered at $\pi$, so the coefficient of $(x - \pi)^4$ is $-\dfrac{1}{24}$.

The same value is obtained from the definition: $f^{(4)}(\pi) = \cos \pi = -1$, and $\dfrac{-1}{4!} = -\dfrac{1}{24}$.
