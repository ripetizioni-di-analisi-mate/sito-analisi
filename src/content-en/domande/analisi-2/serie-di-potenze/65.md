For $|x| < 1$ let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, x^{2n}$. Which series is obtained for $f'(x)$ by differentiating term by term?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, n \, x^{2n-1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, 2n \, x^{2n+1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{n+1} \, 2n \, x^{2n-1}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, 2n \, x^{2n-1}$

## Explanation

The term with $n = 0$ is the constant $1$, whose derivative is zero, so the differentiated series starts from $n = 1$. For $n \geq 1$ the derivative of $(-1)^n \, x^{2n}$ is $(-1)^n \, 2n \, x^{2n-1}$.

By **term-by-term differentiation**, for $|x| < 1$
$$
f'(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \, 2n \, x^{2n-1} = -2x + 4x^3 - 6x^5 + \cdots
$$
The result is consistent with $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$, whose derivative $-\dfrac{2x}{(1 + x^2)^2}$ has $-2x$ as the first term of its expansion.
