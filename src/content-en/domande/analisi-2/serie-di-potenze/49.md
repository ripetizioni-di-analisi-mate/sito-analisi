Two exponentials appear in the coefficient of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( 5^n + 2^n \right) x^n$. What is the radius of convergence?

- $5$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{7}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$

## Explanation

Factoring $5^n$ out of the coefficient we have $5^n + 2^n = 5^n \left( 1 + \left( \dfrac{2}{5} \right)^{n} \right)$, and the expression $1 + \left( \dfrac{2}{5} \right)^{n}$ tends to $1$.

Hence
$$
\sqrt[n]{5^n + 2^n} = 5 \sqrt[n]{1 + \left( \frac{2}{5} \right)^{n}} \to 5 ,
$$
and by the **root test for the radius** we have $R = \dfrac{1}{5}$. The term $2^n$ is negligible with respect to $5^n$, so the radius depends only on the larger base.
