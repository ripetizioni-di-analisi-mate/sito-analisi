What is the Maclaurin expansion of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{5 + x}$?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^n}{5^n}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{5^{n+1}}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{5^{n+1}}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^n}{5^{n+1}}$

## Explanation

Factoring $5$ out of the denominator we have
$$
\frac{1}{5 + x} = \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{1 - \left( -\dfrac{x}{5} \right)} .
$$
The geometric series $\sum q^n$ has sum $\dfrac{1}{1 - q}$ for $|q| < 1$, and with $q = -\dfrac{x}{5}$ the condition $|q| < 1$ is equivalent to $|x| < 5$. Hence for $|x| < 5$
$$
\frac{1}{5 + x} = \frac{1}{5} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( -\frac{x}{5} \right)^{\! n} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^n}{5^{n+1}} .
$$
At $x = \pm 5$ the absolute value of the general term equals $\dfrac{1}{5}$ and does not tend to $0$, therefore the region of validity is exactly $(-5,\, 5)$.
