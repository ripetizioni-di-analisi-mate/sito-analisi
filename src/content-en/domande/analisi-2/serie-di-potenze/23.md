What is the radius of convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2n}}{2^n + 3^n}$, in which only the even powers of $x$ appear?

- $3$
- $\sqrt{3}$
- $\sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$

## Explanation

The series is lacunary, so the ratio test for the radius does not apply to its coefficients. We set $t = x^2$ and obtain the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{t^n}{2^n + 3^n}$.

For this series the ratio between two consecutive coefficients is
$$
\frac{2^n + 3^n}{2^{n+1} + 3^{n+1}} = \frac{\left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{n} + 1}{2 \left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{n} + 3} \to \frac{1}{3} ,
$$
so the series in $t$ has radius $3$ and converges for $|t| < 3$. Since $t = x^2$, the condition becomes $x^2 < 3$, that is $|x| < \sqrt{3}$, and the series does not converge for $|x| > \sqrt{3}$. Therefore the radius of convergence is $\sqrt{3}$.
