What is the set of convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n}}{n \, 4^n}$?

- $(-2,\, 2)$
- $[-2,\, 2]$
- $[-2,\, 2)$
- $[-4,\, 4]$

## Explanation

The series contains only the even powers of $x$, so it is **lacunary**. With $t = \dfrac{x^2}{4} \geq 0$ the series becomes $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, t^n}{n}$, which has radius $1$ because the ratio $\dfrac{n}{n+1}$ tends to $1$.

For $t = 1$ the series in $t$ is $\sum \dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$, which converges by the **Leibniz test**. Since $t \geq 0$, the series in $t$ therefore converges if and only if $0 \leq t \leq 1$, that is, $x^2 \leq 4$, that is, $|x| \leq 2$.

The endpoints $x = \pm 2$ both correspond to $t = 1$, so the series converges at both and $E = [-2,\, 2]$.
