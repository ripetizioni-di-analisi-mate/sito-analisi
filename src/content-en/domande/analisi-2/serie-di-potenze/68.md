Let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{3n}}{(3n)!} = 1 + \frac{x^3}{3!} + \frac{x^6}{6!} + \cdots$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Which equation does $f$ satisfy?

- $f' = f$
- $f''' = f$
- $f'' = f$
- $f''' = -f$

## Explanation

The series has radius $+\infty$, so by **term-by-term differentiation** it can be differentiated three times on all of $\mathbb{R}$. The term with $n = 0$ is constant and has zero third derivative.

For $n \geq 1$ the third derivative of $\dfrac{x^{3n}}{(3n)!}$ is
$$
\frac{3n \, (3n - 1)(3n - 2) \, x^{3n-3}}{(3n)!} = \frac{x^{3n-3}}{(3n-3)!} .
$$
Hence $f'''(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^{3(n-1)}}{(3(n-1))!}$, which with the index $k = n - 1$ is again $f(x)$. Therefore $f''' = f$.

The equation $f' = f$ does not hold, because $f'(0) = 0$ while $f(0) = 1$; for the same reason $f'' = f$ does not hold.
