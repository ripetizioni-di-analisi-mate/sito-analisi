The function $f$ is of class $C^{\infty}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ and satisfies $|f^{(n)}(x)| \leq n!$ for every $n \geq 0$ and every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Which conclusion is guaranteed?

- $f$ coincides with its Maclaurin series for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- $f$ coincides with its Maclaurin series at least for $|x| < 1$
- The Maclaurin series of $f$ has radius of convergence $+\infty$
- $f$ coincides with its Maclaurin series only at $x = 0$

## Explanation

By **Taylor's formula with Lagrange remainder**, for every $x$ there exists $c$ between $0$ and $x$ such that
$$
|f(x) - T_n(x)| = \frac{|f^{(n+1)}(c)|}{(n+1)!} \, |x|^{n+1} \leq |x|^{n+1} .
$$
For $|x| < 1$ we have $|x|^{n+1} \to 0$, so the remainder tends to $0$ and, by the **criterion for Taylor series representation**, $f$ coincides with its Maclaurin series.

For $|x| \geq 1$ the estimate gives no information, and the conclusion on all of $\mathbb{R}$ is not guaranteed. The function $\dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$ satisfies the hypothesis, because $|f^{(n)}(x)| \leq \dfrac{n!}{(1 + x^2)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}} \leq n!$, and its Maclaurin series $\displaystyle\sum (-1)^n x^{2n}$ has radius $1$.
