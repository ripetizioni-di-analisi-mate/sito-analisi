As $x \to 0$, what is the order of the infinitesimal $f(x) = x - \ln(1 + x)$ with respect to $x$?

- $1$
- $3$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

From the expansion $\ln(1 + x) = x - \dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{x^3}{3} + o(x^3)$ we obtain
$$
x - \ln(1 + x) = \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3} + o(x^3) .
$$
The first nonzero term is $\dfrac{x^2}{2}$, so $\dfrac{f(x)}{x^2} \to \dfrac{1}{2} \neq 0$.

Therefore $f$ is an infinitesimal of order $2$. The terms of degree $1$ cancel because the series of the logarithm starts with $x$.
