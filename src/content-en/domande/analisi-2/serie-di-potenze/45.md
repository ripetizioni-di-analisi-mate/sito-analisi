What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x - e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}}}{x^4}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{12}$
- $-\dfrac{5}{24}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{12}$

## Explanation

The denominator is $x^4$, so both expansions are needed up to the fourth order. For the cosine, $\cos x = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{x^4}{24} + o(x^4)$.

From the expansion $e^y = 1 + y + \dfrac{y^2}{2} + o(y^2)$ as $y \to 0$, with $y = -\dfrac{x^2}{2}$ one obtains
$$
e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}} = 1 - \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^4}{8} + o(x^4) .
$$
The terms of degree $0$ and $2$ cancel, so the numerator equals $\left( \dfrac{1}{24} - \dfrac{1}{8} \right) x^4 + o(x^4) = -\dfrac{x^4}{12} + o(x^4)$. Therefore the limit equals $-\dfrac{1}{12}$.
