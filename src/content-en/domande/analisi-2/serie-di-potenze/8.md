What is the set of convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n \, x^{2n+1}}{4^n}$?

- $(-4,\, 4)$
- $[-2,\, 2]$
- $(-2,\, 2)$
- $[-2,\, 2)$

## Explanation

The series contains only the odd powers of $x$, so it is **lacunary**. Since $x^{2n+1} = x \, (x^2)^n$, with $t = \dfrac{x^2}{4}$ the series can be written as $x \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \, t^n$.

The series $\sum n \, t^n$ has radius $1$, because the ratio $\dfrac{n+1}{n}$ tends to $1$, so it converges for $|t| < 1$, that is, for $x^2 < 4$, that is, for $|x| < 2$.

At $x = \pm 2$ the general term equals $\dfrac{n \, (\pm 2)^{2n+1}}{4^n} = \pm 2n$, which does not tend to $0$. By the **necessary condition for convergence** the series does not converge at either endpoint, therefore $E = (-2,\, 2)$.
