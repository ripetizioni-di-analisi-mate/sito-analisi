What is the coefficient of $x^2$ in the Maclaurin expansion of $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x}}$?

- $-\dfrac{3}{8}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{8}$
- $\dfrac{3}{8}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

We have $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x}} = (1 + x)^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The expansion is therefore the **binomial series**, in which the coefficient of $x^n$ is $\dbinom{\alpha}{n} = \dfrac{\alpha (\alpha - 1) \cdots (\alpha - n + 1)}{n!}$. For $n = 2$ we obtain
$$
\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{2} = \frac{\left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right) \left( -\dfrac{1}{2} - 1 \right)}{2} = \frac{\left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right) \left( -\dfrac{3}{2} \right)}{2} = \frac{3}{8} .
$$
The product of the two negative factors is positive, therefore the coefficient is $\dfrac{3}{8}$.
