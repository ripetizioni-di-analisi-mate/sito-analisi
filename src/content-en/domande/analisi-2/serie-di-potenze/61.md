At $x = \pm 1$, the endpoints of the open interval of convergence, we study the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n} \, x^n$. Which set of convergence is obtained?

- $[-1,\, 1)$
- $(-1,\, 1)$
- $[-1,\, 1]$
- $(-1,\, 1]$

## Explanation

The ratio between two consecutive coefficients is $\dfrac{\ln(n+1)}{\ln n} \cdot \dfrac{n}{n+1} \to 1$, so the radius of convergence is $R = 1$.

At $x = 1$ the series is $\displaystyle\sum \frac{\ln n}{n}$. For $n \geq 3$ we have $\ln n > 1$, so $\dfrac{\ln n}{n} > \dfrac{1}{n}$, and by the **comparison test** the series diverges.

At $x = -1$ the series is $\displaystyle\sum (-1)^n \frac{\ln n}{n}$. The function $g(t) = \dfrac{\ln t}{t}$ has derivative $\dfrac{1 - \ln t}{t^2} < 0$ for $t > e$, so the sequence $\dfrac{\ln n}{n}$ is decreasing for $n \geq 3$ and tends to $0$. Therefore the series converges by the **alternating series test**, and the set of convergence is $[-1,\, 1)$.
