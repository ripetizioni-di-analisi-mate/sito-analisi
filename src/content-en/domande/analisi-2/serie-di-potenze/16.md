The power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (x - 2)^n$ converges at the point $x = 5$. Which statement is certainly true?

- The series converges absolutely at $x = 0$
- The series converges at $x = -1$
- The series converges at $x = 6$
- The series does not converge at $x = 8$

## Explanation

The series is centered at $x_0 = 2$ and the point $x = 5$ is at distance $3$ from the center. Since the series converges at that point, by the **radius of convergence theorem** the radius satisfies $R \geq 3$.

The point $x = 0$ is at distance $2 < 3 \leq R$ from the center, so the series **converges absolutely** at $x = 0$.

The other statements are not guaranteed. The series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{(-1)^n (x - 2)^n}{n \, 3^n}$ has $R = 3$ and converges at $x = 5$ by the Leibniz test, but at $x = -1$ it becomes the harmonic series, which diverges, and at $x = 6$ it does not converge because $|6 - 2| > R$. The series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{(x - 2)^n}{n!}$ converges at every point, hence also at $x = 8$.
