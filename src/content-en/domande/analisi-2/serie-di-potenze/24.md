Studying the endpoints as well, what set of convergence is found for the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \right) x^n$?

- $(-1,\, 1)$
- $[-1,\, 1)$
- $[-1,\, 1]$
- $(-1,\, 1]$

## Explanation

Since $1 - \cos y = \dfrac{y^2}{2} + o(y^2)$ as $y \to 0$, the coefficient satisfies $1 - \cos \dfrac{1}{n} \sim \dfrac{1}{2n^2}$. Hence the ratio between two consecutive coefficients is asymptotic to $\dfrac{n^2}{(n+1)^2} \to 1$, and the radius of convergence is $R = 1$.

At $x = \pm 1$ the absolute value of the general term is $1 - \cos \dfrac{1}{n}$, positive and asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2n^2}$. The series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{1}{2n^2}$ converges, so by the **limit comparison test** the series of absolute values converges at both endpoints.

Therefore the series converges absolutely at $x = -1$ and at $x = 1$, and the set of convergence is $[-1,\, 1]$.
