What is the set of convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, (x - 3)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$?

- $(2,\, 4]$
- $[2,\, 4)$
- $[2,\, 4]$
- $(2,\, 4)$

## Explanation

The absolute value of the coefficient is $|a_n| = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ and $\dfrac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|} = \sqrt{\dfrac{n}{n+1}} \to 1$, so by the **ratio test for the radius** we have $R = 1$. The series is centered at $x_0 = 3$, therefore the open interval of convergence is $(2,\, 4)$.

At $x = 4$ we have $(x - 3)^n = 1$, so the general term is $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$. Since $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ is decreasing and tends to $0$, the series converges by the **alternating series test**.

At $x = 2$ we have $(x - 3)^n = (-1)^n$, so the general term is $\dfrac{(-1)^{2n}}{\sqrt{n}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$. The p-series with exponent $\dfrac{1}{2} \leq 1$ diverges, therefore $E = (2,\, 4]$.
