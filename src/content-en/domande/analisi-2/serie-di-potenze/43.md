What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n \, 4^n}$?

- $\ln \dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\ln \dfrac{5}{4}$
- $\ln \dfrac{4}{3}$

## Explanation

Replacing $x$ with $-x$ in the expansion of $\ln(1 + x)$ we obtain, for $-1 \leq x < 1$,
$$
\ln(1 - x) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{n \, 4^n} = \dfrac{1}{n} \left( \dfrac{1}{4} \right)^{\! n}$ and the point $x = \dfrac{1}{4}$ belongs to the region of validity, the given series equals $-\ln \left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{4} \right) = -\ln \dfrac{3}{4}$. Hence the sum is $\ln \dfrac{4}{3}$.
