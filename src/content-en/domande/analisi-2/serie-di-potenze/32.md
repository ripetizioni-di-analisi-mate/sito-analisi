The Maclaurin expansion of $\ln(1 + 2x)$ is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, 2^n \, x^n}{n}$. What is its region of validity, that is, the set of all points at which the series converges to $\ln(1 + 2x)$?

- $(-1,\, 1]$
- $\left[ -\dfrac{1}{2},\, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2},\, \dfrac{1}{2} \right]$
- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2},\, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$

## Explanation

The standard expansion $\ln(1 + y) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, y^n}{n}$ holds for $-1 < y \leq 1$. The given expansion is obtained by substituting $y = 2x$, so it holds when $2x$ belongs to the region of the original expansion.

The condition $-1 < 2x \leq 1$ is equivalent to $-\dfrac{1}{2} < x \leq \dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore the region of validity is $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2},\, \dfrac{1}{2} \right]$. The endpoint $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ corresponds to $y = 1$, where the series converges to $\ln 2$, while the endpoint $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ corresponds to $y = -1$, where the series diverges.
