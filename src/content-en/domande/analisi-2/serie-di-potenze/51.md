The radius of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^n$ is $2$. Dividing each coefficient by $n!$ we obtain the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{n!} \, x^n$. What is its radius of convergence?

- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $+\infty$
- $0$

## Explanation

We fix $\rho = 1$, which is less than $2$. By the **radius of convergence theorem** the series $\sum a_n \rho^n$ converges, so its general term is bounded: there exists $M > 0$ such that $|a_n| \leq M$ for every $n$.

Then for every $x$
$$
\left| \frac{a_n}{n!} \, x^n \right| \leq M \, \frac{|x|^n}{n!} ,
$$
and the series $\displaystyle\sum M \, \frac{|x|^n}{n!}$ converges with sum $M e^{|x|}$. By the **comparison test** the second series converges absolutely for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, therefore its radius is $+\infty$.
