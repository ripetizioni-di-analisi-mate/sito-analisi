What is the Maclaurin expansion of the function $f(x) = x \, e^{-2x}$?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^n \, x^{n+1}}{n!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^n \, x^{n}}{n!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^n \, x^{n+1}}{n!}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^n \, x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$

## Explanation

The standard expansion $e^y = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{y^n}{n!}$ holds for every $y \in \mathbb{R}$. Substituting $y = -2x$ we obtain, for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$
e^{-2x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-2x)^n}{n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^n \, x^n}{n!} .
$$
Multiplying each term by $x$ we have
$$
x \, e^{-2x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^n \, x^{n+1}}{n!} , \qquad x \in \mathbb{R} ,
$$
and by the **uniqueness of the expansion** this series is the Maclaurin series of $f$.
