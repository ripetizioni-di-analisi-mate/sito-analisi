Let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{3^n}$ for $|x| < 3$. What is $f'(1)$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{9}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

The series is the geometric series of ratio $\dfrac{x}{3}$, so for $|x| < 3$
$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{x}{3}} = \frac{3}{3 - x} .
$$
Differentiating we obtain $f'(x) = \dfrac{3}{(3 - x)^2}$, therefore $f'(1) = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

The same value is obtained by **term-by-term differentiation**, which applies at the point $x = 1$ because it is interior to $(-3, 3)$. We have $f'(1) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{3^n}$, and this series has sum $\dfrac{3}{4}$.
