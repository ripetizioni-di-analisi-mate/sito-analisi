Let $f(x) = x^2 \sin x$. What is $f^{(7)}(0)$?

- $-42$
- $\dfrac{1}{120}$
- $0$
- $42$

## Explanation

Multiplying the standard expansion of the sine by $x^2$ we obtain, for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$
x^2 \sin x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n+3}}{(2n+1)!} = x^3 - \frac{x^5}{6} + \frac{x^7}{120} - \cdots
$$
By the **uniqueness of the expansion** this is the Maclaurin series of $f$, so the coefficient of $x^7$ is $\dfrac{f^{(7)}(0)}{7!}$. Therefore $\dfrac{f^{(7)}(0)}{7!} = \dfrac{1}{120}$, and $f^{(7)}(0) = \dfrac{5040}{120} = 42$.
