- $\ln 2$
- $\ln \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\ln \dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\ln \dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The standard expansion
$$
\ln(1 + x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{n}
$$
holds for $-1 < x \leq 1$. Since $\dfrac{1}{n \, 2^n} = \dfrac{1}{n} \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\! n}$, the given series is the value of this expansion at $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

The point $\dfrac{1}{2}$ belongs to the region of validity $(-1, 1]$, so the sum of the series is $\ln \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{2} \right) = \ln \dfrac{3}{2}$.
