To study the lacunary series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2n}}{n!}$ the ratio test is applied to its terms $u_n = \dfrac{x^{2n}}{n!}$, with $x \neq 0$ fixed. What does $\dfrac{|u_{n+1}|}{|u_n|}$ tend to as $n \to \infty$?

- $0$
- $x^2$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2}$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The next term is $u_{n+1} = \dfrac{x^{2n+2}}{(n+1)!}$, so
$$
\frac{|u_{n+1}|}{|u_n|} = \frac{x^{2n+2}}{(n+1)!} \cdot \frac{n!}{x^{2n}} = \frac{x^2}{n+1} .
$$
For fixed $x$ the numerator is constant and the denominator tends to $+\infty$, so the ratio tends to $0$.

The limit is less than $1$ for every $x$, therefore by the ratio test the series converges absolutely on all of $\mathbb{R}$ and the radius is $+\infty$. The test is applied to the terms, with $x$ as a parameter, because the sequence of coefficients contains infinitely many zeros.
