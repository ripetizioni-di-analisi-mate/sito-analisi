For $|x| < 1$ let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n^2}$. Which equality holds for every $x$ with $0 < |x| < 1$?

- $f'(x) = -\ln(1 - x)$
- $x \, f'(x) = \dfrac{x}{1 - x}$
- $x \, f'(x) = -\ln(1 - x)$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 - x}$

## Explanation

The series has radius $1$. By **term-by-term differentiation**, for $|x| < 1$
$$
f'(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n \, x^{n-1}}{n^2} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^{n-1}}{n} ,
$$
so, multiplying by $x$, we obtain $x \, f'(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}$.

From the standard expansion of $\ln(1 + x)$ with $x$ replaced by $-x$ we have $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n} = -\ln(1 - x)$ for $|x| < 1$. Therefore $x \, f'(x) = -\ln(1 - x)$.
