- It is differentiable at $(0, 0)$, with differential $(h, k) \mapsto h$
- It is not differentiable at $(0, 0)$, because it is not continuous
- It is not differentiable at $(0, 0)$, although it is continuous and partially differentiable
- It is not differentiable at $(0, 0)$, because the partial derivatives do not exist

## Explanation

Since $x^2 \leq x^2 + y^2$, we have $|f(x, y)| \leq |x|$, so $f$ is continuous at the origin. On the $x$-axis we have $f(t, 0) = t$ and on the $y$-axis we have $f(0, t) = 0$, therefore $f_x(0, 0) = 1$ and $f_y(0, 0) = 0$. The ratio in the definition of differentiability becomes
$$
\frac{\dfrac{h^3}{h^2 + k^2} - h}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}} = -\frac{h k^2}{(h^2 + k^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}} .
$$
Along the line $k = h$ with $h > 0$ the ratio is constantly equal to $-\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}$, while along the $h$-axis it equals $0$, so the limit as $(h, k) \to (0, 0)$ does not exist. Therefore $f$ is **not differentiable** at $(0, 0)$, although it is continuous and partially differentiable at that point.
