The differentiability of $f(x, y) = |x - y|\,(x + y)$ is studied on the bisector $y = x$. Which points of the bisector are points of differentiability?

- At no point of the line
- Only at the origin
- At every point of the line
- At the points $(a, a)$ with $a \geq 0$

## Explanation

At a point $(a, a)$ one has $f(a + t, a) = |t|\,(2a + t)$, so the difference quotient with respect to $x$ tends to $2a$ as $t \to 0^+$ and to $-2a$ as $t \to 0^-$. For $a \neq 0$ the derivative $f_x(a, a)$ does not exist, therefore $f$ is not differentiable at $(a, a)$. At the origin the partial derivatives are zero, because $f(t, 0) = |t|\,t$ and $f(0, t) = |t|\,t$ have zero derivative at $0$. Since $|f(h, k)| = |h^2 - k^2| \leq h^2 + k^2$, the differentiability quotient is bounded by $\sqrt{h^2 + k^2} \to 0$, so $f$ is **differentiable** only at the origin.
