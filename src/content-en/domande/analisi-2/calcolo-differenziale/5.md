- It is differentiable at $(0, 0)$, with zero differential
- It is partially differentiable at $(0, 0)$, but it is not differentiable
- It is not partially differentiable at $(0, 0)$, because $f_y(0, 0)$ does not exist
- It is not differentiable at $(0, 0)$, because it is not continuous

## Explanation

On the axes we have $f(t, 0) = 0$ and $f(0, t) = 0 \cdot |t| = 0$, so $f_x(0, 0) = f_y(0, 0) = 0$. The ratio in the definition of differentiability is therefore $\dfrac{h\,|k|}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}}$. Since $|h|\,|k| \leq \dfrac{h^2 + k^2}{2}$, its absolute value is bounded above by $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}$, which tends to $0$. By the **squeeze theorem** the ratio tends to $0$, so $f$ is **differentiable** at $(0, 0)$ with zero differential.
