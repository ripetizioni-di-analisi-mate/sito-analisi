Is there a function $f$ differentiable at $P$ with $f_x(P) = 1$, $f_y(P) = 1$ and directional derivative equal to $2$ along the unit vector $\mathbf{v} = \left( \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$?

- Yes, because directional derivatives along different directions do not depend on each other
- Yes, provided the partial derivatives of $f$ are not continuous at $P$
- No, because along $\mathbf{v}$ the directional derivative necessarily equals $1$
- No, because along $\mathbf{v}$ the directional derivative necessarily equals $\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

Since $f$ is differentiable at $P$, the **gradient formula** applies to every unit vector, whatever the regularity of the partial derivatives. Along $\mathbf{v}$ one therefore has
$$
D_{\mathbf{v}} f(P) = 1 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 1 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2} .
$$
Hence the derivative along $\mathbf{v}$ cannot equal $2$, and a function with those values **does not exist**.
