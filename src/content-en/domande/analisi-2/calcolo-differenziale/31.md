For the function $f(x, y) = \sqrt[3]{x^3 + y^3}$, what is the directional derivative at the point $(0, 0)$ along the unit vector $\mathbf{v} = \left( \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$?

- $\sqrt{2}$
- $0$
- The directional derivative does not exist
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[6]{2}}$

## Explanation

On the axes we have $f(t, 0) = t$ and $f(0, t) = t$, so $f_x(0, 0) = f_y(0, 0) = 1$. At the points $\left( \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,t,\, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,t \right)$ we have $x^3 + y^3 = 2\left( \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^3 t^3 = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,t^3$, and since $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 2^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ the cube root equals $2^{-\frac{1}{6}}\,t = \dfrac{t}{\sqrt[6]{2}}$. The difference quotient is therefore constantly equal to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[6]{2}}$, and this is the directional derivative. The **gradient formula** would give $1 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 1 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2}$; since the two values are different, $f$ is **not differentiable** at the origin and the formula does not apply.
