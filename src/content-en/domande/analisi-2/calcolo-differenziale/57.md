For the function $f(x, y) = \displaystyle\int_x^y e^{t^2}\, dt$, what is the gradient $\nabla f(x, y)$?

- $\left( e^{x^2},\; e^{y^2} \right)$
- $\left( -2x\, e^{x^2},\; 2y\, e^{y^2} \right)$
- $\left( e^{y^2},\; -e^{x^2} \right)$
- $\left( -e^{x^2},\; e^{y^2} \right)$

## Explanation

With $x$ held constant, $f$ is an integral function of its upper limit $y$, so by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** $f_y = e^{y^2}$. With $y$ held constant one writes $f(x, y) = -\displaystyle\int_y^x e^{t^2}\, dt$, therefore the same theorem gives $f_x = -e^{x^2}$. Hence the gradient is $\left( -e^{x^2},\; e^{y^2} \right)$.
