For the function $f(x, y) = \ln\left( x^2 + y^2 \right)$, what is the gradient at the point $(1, 2)$?

- $\nabla f(1, 2) = \left( \dfrac{1}{5},\, \dfrac{2}{5} \right)$
- $\nabla f(1, 2) = (2,\, 4)$
- $\nabla f(1, 2) = \left( \dfrac{2}{5},\, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$
- $\nabla f(1, 2) = \left( \dfrac{1}{5},\, \dfrac{1}{5} \right)$

## Explanation

The function is the composition of $\ln u$ with $u = x^2 + y^2$, so each partial derivative is $\dfrac{1}{x^2 + y^2}$ multiplied by the partial derivative of $x^2 + y^2$. With $y$ held constant, the derivative of $x^2 + y^2$ with respect to $x$ is $2x$, therefore $f_x = \dfrac{2x}{x^2 + y^2}$; in the same way we obtain $f_y = \dfrac{2y}{x^2 + y^2}$. At the point $(1, 2)$ we have $x^2 + y^2 = 5$, so $f_x(1, 2) = \dfrac{2}{5}$ and $f_y(1, 2) = \dfrac{4}{5}$. The **gradient** is therefore $\left( \dfrac{2}{5},\, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$.
