For which values of $\alpha > 0$ is the function $f(x, y) = |xy|^{\alpha}$ differentiable at $(0, 0)$?

- $\alpha > 0$
- $\alpha \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha > 1$
- $\alpha > \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The function vanishes on the axes, so $f_x(0, 0) = f_y(0, 0) = 0$ and the ratio in the definition of differentiability is $\dfrac{|hk|^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}}$. Setting $\rho = \sqrt{h^2 + k^2}$, we have $|hk| \leq \dfrac{\rho^2}{2}$, therefore the ratio is bounded above by $\dfrac{\rho^{2\alpha - 1}}{2^{\alpha}}$. For $\alpha > \dfrac{1}{2}$ the exponent $2\alpha - 1$ is positive, so by the **comparison theorem** the ratio tends to $0$ and $f$ is differentiable. For $\alpha \leq \dfrac{1}{2}$, along the line $k = h$ the ratio equals $\dfrac{|h|^{2\alpha}}{\sqrt{2}\,|h|}$, which is constantly $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ for $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and tends to $+\infty$ for $\alpha < \dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore in these cases $f$ is **not differentiable** at $(0, 0)$.
