For the function $f(x, y) = e^{x} \sin y$, what is the minimum value of the directional derivative at the point $\left( 0, \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$?

- $-1$
- $0$
- $-\sqrt{2}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = e^{x} \sin y$ and $f_y = e^{x} \cos y$, continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$, so $f$ is differentiable. At the point $\left( 0, \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$ we have $\nabla f = \left( \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$, whose length is $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}} = 1$. By the theorem on the **direction of steepest ascent**, the directional derivative is minimal along the unit vector opposite to the gradient, where it equals $-|\nabla f|$. The minimum value is therefore $-1$.
