A function $f$ is differentiable at the point $P$ and its directional derivatives at $P$ along the unit vectors
$$
\mathbf{v}_1 = \left( \frac{3}{5},\, \frac{4}{5} \right), \qquad \mathbf{v}_2 = \left( -\frac{4}{5},\, \frac{3}{5} \right)
$$
are $D_{\mathbf{v}_1} f(P) = 2$ and $D_{\mathbf{v}_2} f(P) = 1$. What is $\nabla f(P)$?

::grafico{vettori="1,0.6,1.6,1.4,v₁;1,0.6,0.2,1.2,v₂" punti="1,0.6,pieno,P" xmin=-0.3 xmax=2.2 ymin=-0.3 ymax=1.7 uguale=1 titolo="The unit vectors $\mathbf{v}_1$ and $\mathbf{v}_2$ applied at $P$ are orthogonal"}

- $\left( \dfrac{2}{5},\, \dfrac{11}{5} \right)$
- $(2,\, 1)$
- $\left( \dfrac{11}{5},\, \dfrac{2}{5} \right)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{2}{5},\, \dfrac{11}{5} \right)$

## Explanation

By the **gradient formula** $\nabla f(P) \cdot \mathbf{v}_1 = 2$ and $\nabla f(P) \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 = 1$. The unit vectors $\mathbf{v}_1$ and $\mathbf{v}_2$ are orthogonal, so they form an orthonormal basis of the plane and every vector is the sum of its projections onto them. Therefore
$$
\nabla f(P) = 2\, \mathbf{v}_1 + 1\, \mathbf{v}_2 = \left( \frac{6}{5} - \frac{4}{5},\; \frac{8}{5} + \frac{3}{5} \right),
$$
that is, $\nabla f(P) = \left( \dfrac{2}{5},\, \dfrac{11}{5} \right)$.
