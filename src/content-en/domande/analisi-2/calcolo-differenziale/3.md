- $f_x = 2xy \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right), \quad f_y = x^2 \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right)$
- $f_x = \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right), \quad f_y = \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right)$
- $f_x = x^2 \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right), \quad f_y = 2xy \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right)$
- $f_x = 2x \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right), \quad f_y = \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right)$

## Explanation

The function is the composition of $\sin t$ with $t = x^2 y$, so each partial derivative is $\cos\!\left( x^2 y \right)$ multiplied by the partial derivative of $x^2 y$. With $y$ held constant the derivative of $x^2 y$ with respect to $x$ is $2xy$, therefore $f_x = 2xy \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right)$. With $x$ held constant the derivative of $x^2 y$ with respect to $y$ is $x^2$, therefore $f_y = x^2 \cos\!\left( x^2 y \right)$.
