- $T_2(x, y) = y + xy + \dfrac{y^2}{2}$
- $T_2(x, y) = y + \dfrac{xy}{2} - \dfrac{y^2}{2}$
- $T_2(x, y) = y - \dfrac{y^2}{2}$
- $T_2(x, y) = y + xy - \dfrac{y^2}{2}$

## Explanation

We have $f(0, 0) = 0$, $f_x = e^{x} \ln(1 + y)$ and $f_y = \dfrac{e^{x}}{1 + y}$, so $f_x(0, 0) = 0$ and $f_y(0, 0) = 1$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = e^{x} \ln(1 + y)$, $f_{xy} = \dfrac{e^{x}}{1 + y}$ and $f_{yy} = -\dfrac{e^{x}}{(1 + y)^2}$, therefore at the origin they equal $0$, $1$ and $-1$. Since $f$ is of class $C^2$ in the half-plane $y > -1$, which contains the origin, the **second-order Taylor formula** applies and we obtain
$$
T_2(x, y) = y + \frac{1}{2}\bigl( 2xy - y^2 \bigr) = y + xy - \frac{y^2}{2} .
$$
