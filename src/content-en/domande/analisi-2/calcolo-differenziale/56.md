A function $f$ of class $C^2$ has second-order Taylor polynomial centered at $(1, 2)$
$$
T_2(x, y) = 4 + 3(x - 1) - (y - 2) + 2(x - 1)^2 - (x - 1)(y - 2) .
$$
What is the equation of the tangent plane to the graph of $f$ at the point $(1, 2, 4)$?

- $z = 3x - y + 4$
- $z = 3x - y + 3$
- $z = 3x + y - 1$
- $z = 3x - y - 1$

## Explanation

In the Taylor formula the constant term is $f(1, 2)$, and the coefficients of $x - 1$ and $y - 2$ are $f_x(1, 2)$ and $f_y(1, 2)$. From the given polynomial one therefore reads $f(1, 2) = 4$, $f_x(1, 2) = 3$ and $f_y(1, 2) = -1$, while the second-degree terms play no role. Since $f$ is of class $C^2$, it is differentiable, and the tangent plane is formed by the terms of degree at most $1$ of the polynomial:
$$
z = 4 + 3(x - 1) - (y - 2) = 3x - y + 3 .
$$
