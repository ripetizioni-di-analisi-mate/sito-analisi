For the function $f(x, y) = x y^2$, what is the directional derivative at the point $(1, 1)$ in the direction of the vector $\mathbf{w} = (3, 4)$?

- $11$
- $\dfrac{11}{5}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{11}{25}$

## Explanation

The vector $\mathbf{w}$ has length $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, so the unit vector of the direction is $\mathbf{v} = \left( \dfrac{3}{5},\, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$. The partial derivatives are $f_x = y^2$ and $f_y = 2xy$, continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$, therefore $f$ is differentiable and the **gradient formula** applies. Since $\nabla f(1, 1) = (1,\, 2)$, we have
$$
D_{\mathbf{v}} f(1, 1) = 1 \cdot \frac{3}{5} + 2 \cdot \frac{4}{5} = \frac{11}{5} .
$$
