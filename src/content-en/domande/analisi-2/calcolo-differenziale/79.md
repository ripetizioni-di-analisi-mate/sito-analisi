A function $f$ is differentiable at $(1, 2)$, with $f(1, 2) = 3$, and for all real $x$ and $y$
$$
f(x, 2) = x^2 + 2, \qquad f(1, y) = y + 1 .
$$
What is the equation of the tangent plane to the graph of $f$ at the point $(1, 2, 3)$?

- $z = x + 2y - 2$
- $z = 2x + 2y - 3$
- $z = 2x + y - 1$
- $z = 2x + y + 3$

## Explanation

The derivative $f_x(1, 2)$ is the derivative at $x = 1$ of the restriction $x \mapsto f(x, 2) = x^2 + 2$, so it equals $2$. The derivative $f_y(1, 2)$ is the derivative at $y = 2$ of the restriction $y \mapsto f(1, y) = y + 1$, so it equals $1$. Since $f$ is differentiable at $(1, 2)$, the tangent plane is
$$
z = 3 + 2(x - 1) + (y - 2) = 2x + y - 1 .
$$
