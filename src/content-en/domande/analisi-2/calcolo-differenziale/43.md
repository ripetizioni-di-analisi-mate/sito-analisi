What is the second-order Taylor polynomial of $f(x, y) = \cos(x + 2y)$ centered at $(0, 0)$?

- $T_2(x, y) = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2} - 2y^2$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2} - 4xy - 2y^2$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2} - 2xy - 2y^2$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2} - xy - \dfrac{y^2}{2}$

## Explanation

We have $f(0, 0) = 1$; the first derivatives are $f_x = -\sin(x + 2y)$ and $f_y = -2\sin(x + 2y)$, which vanish at the origin. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = -\cos(x + 2y)$, $f_{xy} = -2\cos(x + 2y)$ and $f_{yy} = -4\cos(x + 2y)$, which at the origin equal $-1$, $-2$ and $-4$. The function is of class $C^2$, so by the **second-order Taylor formula**
$$
T_2(x, y) = 1 + \frac{1}{2} \bigl( -x^2 - 4xy - 4y^2 \bigr) = 1 - \frac{x^2}{2} - 2xy - 2y^2 .
$$
The same polynomial is obtained by substituting $u = x + 2y$ into the expansion $\cos u = 1 - \dfrac{u^2}{2} + o\left( u^2 \right)$.
