What is the differential of $f(x, y) = x\,e^{y}$ at the point $(3, 0)$, evaluated at the increment $(h, k)$?

- $3h + k$
- $h + 3k$
- $h + k$
- $3k$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = e^{y}$ and $f_y = x\,e^{y}$, continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$, so $f$ is differentiable by the total differential theorem. At the point $(3, 0)$ they equal $f_x(3, 0) = 1$ and $f_y(3, 0) = 3$. The differential is therefore the linear map $(h, k) \mapsto h + 3k$.
