The second derivatives of $f(x, y, z) = x^2 y + y z^2$ are computed at the point $(1, 1, 1)$. Which Hessian matrix is obtained?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The first derivatives are $f_x = 2xy$, $f_y = x^2 + z^2$ and $f_z = 2yz$. The pure second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2y$, $f_{yy} = 0$ and $f_{zz} = 2y$, while the mixed ones are $f_{xy} = 2x$, $f_{xz} = 0$ and $f_{yz} = 2z$. The function is a polynomial, so by Schwarz's theorem the matrix is symmetric. At the point $(1, 1, 1)$ one obtains
$$
H_f(1, 1, 1) =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 2 & 0 \\
2 & 0 & 2 \\
0 & 2 & 2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
