For the function $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, which statement about the partial derivatives at the point $(0, 0)$ is true?

- Both exist and equal $0$
- Neither of them exists
- Both exist and equal $1$
- $f_x(0, 0)$ exists, while $f_y(0, 0)$ does not exist

## Explanation

On the $x$-axis we have $f(t, 0) = \sqrt{t^2} = |t|$, so the difference quotient of $f_x$ at the origin is $\dfrac{|t|}{t}$. This quotient equals $1$ for $t > 0$ and $-1$ for $t < 0$, therefore its limit as $t \to 0$ does not exist and $f_x(0, 0)$ **does not exist**. Since $f(0, t) = |t|$ as well, the same argument applies to $f_y(0, 0)$. The graph of $f$ is a cone with its vertex at the origin, and the restrictions of $f$ to the two axes have a corner point at $t = 0$.
