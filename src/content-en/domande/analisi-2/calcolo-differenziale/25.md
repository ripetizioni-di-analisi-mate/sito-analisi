The unit vector $\mathbf{v}$ forms an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ with the positive $x$-axis. At what rate does $f(x, y) = x^2 + y$ change starting from the point $(1, 0)$ in the direction of $\mathbf{v}$?

- $\sqrt{3} + \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1 + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$

## Explanation

The unit vector is $\mathbf{v} = \left( \cos \dfrac{\pi}{3},\, \sin \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = \left( \dfrac{1}{2},\, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$. The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2x$ and $f_y = 1$, so $\nabla f(1, 0) = (2,\, 1)$. The function is a polynomial, therefore it is differentiable, and by the **gradient formula**
$$
D_{\mathbf{v}} f(1, 0) = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + 1 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 1 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} .
$$
