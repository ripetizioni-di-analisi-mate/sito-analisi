For the function
$$
f(x, y) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x y^2}{x^2 + y^4} & (x, y) \neq (0, 0) \\[10pt]
0 & (x, y) = (0, 0)
\end{cases}
$$
which statement at the point $(0, 0)$ is true?

- It is differentiable at $(0, 0)$, with zero differential
- It has zero partial derivatives at $(0, 0)$, but it is not continuous, so it is not differentiable
- It is continuous at $(0, 0)$, but the partial derivatives do not exist
- It is continuous and partially differentiable at $(0, 0)$, but it is not differentiable

## Explanation

The function vanishes on the axes, because $f(t, 0) = 0$ and $f(0, t) = 0$, so $f_x(0, 0) = f_y(0, 0) = 0$. Along the parabola $x = y^2$ we have $f(t^2, t) = \dfrac{t^4}{2t^4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $t \neq 0$, while along the $x$-axis the function equals $0$. Therefore the limit of $f$ at $(0, 0)$ does not exist and $f$ is **not continuous** at the origin. Since differentiability implies continuity, $f$ is **not differentiable** at $(0, 0)$, although the partial derivatives exist.
