A point moves along the line $x(t) = 1 + t$, $y(t) = 2 - t$. At which instant $t$ does the function $F(t) = f\bigl( x(t), y(t) \bigr)$, with $f(x, y) = xy$, have zero derivative?

- $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $t = 1$
- $t = 2$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = y$ and $f_y = x$, and the velocity of the point is $\bigl( x'(t), y'(t) \bigr) = (1,\, -1)$. By the **chain rule**
$$
F'(t) = y(t) \cdot 1 + x(t) \cdot (-1) = (2 - t) - (1 + t) = 1 - 2t .
$$
The derivative vanishes for $1 - 2t = 0$, hence at the instant $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
