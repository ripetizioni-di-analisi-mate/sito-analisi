Along the bisector consider $F(t) = f(t, t)$, where $f$ is of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and has at the origin the Hessian matrix
$$
H_f(0, 0) =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
2 & 3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
What is $F''(0)$?

- $6$
- $4$
- $8$
- $16$

## Explanation

By the **chain rule** $F'(t) = f_x(t, t) + f_y(t, t)$. Applying the rule again to each term gives
$$
F''(t) = f_{xx}(t, t) + f_{xy}(t, t) + f_{yx}(t, t) + f_{yy}(t, t) .
$$
By Schwarz's theorem $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$, so $F''(0) = f_{xx}(0,0) + 2 f_{xy}(0,0) + f_{yy}(0,0) = 1 + 4 + 3 = 8$.
