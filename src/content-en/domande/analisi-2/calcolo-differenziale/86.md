The function $f(x, y) = \dfrac{xy\left( x^2 - y^2 \right)}{x^2 + y^2}$, extended by $f(0, 0) = 0$, has first partial derivatives at every point of the plane. What is the mixed derivative $f_{xy}(0, 0) = \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y} f_x \, (0, 0)$?

- $-1$
- $1$
- $0$
- The derivative does not exist

## Explanation

For every $y$ the difference quotient of $f_x$ at the point $(0, y)$ is
$$
\frac{f(t, y) - f(0, y)}{t} = \frac{y\left( t^2 - y^2 \right)}{t^2 + y^2},
$$
which tends to $-y$ as $t \to 0$, so $f_x(0, y) = -y$. Differentiating with respect to $y$ gives $f_{xy}(0, 0) = -1$. By the same computation $f_y(x, 0) = x$, so $f_{yx}(0, 0) = 1$. The mixed derivatives are different, therefore the hypotheses of **Schwarz's theorem** are not satisfied and the mixed derivatives are not continuous at the origin.
