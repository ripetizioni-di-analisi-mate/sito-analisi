At which points of the plane does the partial derivative $f_x$ of the function $f(x, y) = |x|\, y$ exist?

- Only at the points with $x \neq 0$
- At every point of $\mathbb{R}^2$
- At every point except the points $(0, y)$ with $y \neq 0$
- At every point except the origin

## Explanation

At the points with $x \neq 0$ the function $|x|$ is differentiable, so $f_x$ exists. At a point $(0, y_0)$ the difference quotient with respect to $x$ is
$$
\frac{f(t, y_0) - f(0, y_0)}{t} = \frac{|t|}{t}\, y_0 ,
$$
which equals $y_0$ for $t > 0$ and $-y_0$ for $t < 0$. The limit as $t \to 0$ therefore exists only if $y_0 = -y_0$, that is $y_0 = 0$. Hence $f_x$ exists at every point except the points $(0, y)$ with $y \neq 0$; at the origin it equals $0$.
