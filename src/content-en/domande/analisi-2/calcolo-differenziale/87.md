The function $f(x, y) = \sin(x + y) - \sin x - \sin y$ tends to $0$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$. What is its second-order Taylor polynomial centered at the origin?

- $T_2(x, y) = 2xy$
- $T_2(x, y) = -xy$
- $T_2(x, y) = x + y$
- $T_2(x, y) = 0$

## Explanation

By the one-variable Taylor formula $\sin u = u + o\left( u^2 \right)$, because the second derivative of the sine at $0$ is zero. Applying it with $u = x + y$, $u = x$ and $u = y$ gives
$$
f(x, y) = (x + y) - x - y + o\left( x^2 + y^2 \right) = o\left( x^2 + y^2 \right).
$$
The terms of degree at most $2$ are therefore all zero, hence $T_2(x, y) = 0$.
