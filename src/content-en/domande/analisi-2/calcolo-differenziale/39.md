What is the Hessian matrix of $f(x, y) = e^{xy}$ at the point $(1, 0)$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The first derivatives are $f_x = y\,e^{xy}$ and $f_y = x\,e^{xy}$. Differentiating again we obtain $f_{xx} = y^2 e^{xy}$, $f_{yy} = x^2 e^{xy}$ and, by the product rule, $f_{xy} = e^{xy} + xy\,e^{xy}$. At the point $(1, 0)$ we have $e^{xy} = 1$, so $f_{xx} = 0$, $f_{yy} = 1$ and $f_{xy} = f_{yx} = 1$, in agreement with the **Schwarz theorem**. Therefore the **Hessian matrix** at $(1, 0)$ has $0$ and $1$ on the diagonal and $1$ off the diagonal.
