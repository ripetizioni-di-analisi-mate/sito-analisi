The linear approximation of $f(x, y) = e^{x} \sin y$ at the origin is $y$. What estimate of the difference $f(0.1,\, 0.2) - 0.2$ does the second-degree term of the Taylor polynomial centered at $(0, 0)$ give?

- $0.01$
- $0.02$
- $-0.02$
- $0.04$

## Explanation

The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = e^{x} \sin y$, $f_{xy} = e^{x} \cos y$ and $f_{yy} = -e^{x} \sin y$, which at the origin equal $0$, $1$ and $0$. The function is of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, so by the **second-order Taylor formula** the second-degree term is
$$
\frac{1}{2} \bigl( 0 \cdot x^2 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot xy + 0 \cdot y^2 \bigr) = xy .
$$
With $x = 0.1$ and $y = 0.2$ the estimate of the difference is therefore $0.1 \cdot 0.2 = 0.02$. The exact value of the difference is about $0.0196$.
