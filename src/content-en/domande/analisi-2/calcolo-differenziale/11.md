Let $F(s, t) = f\bigl(st,\; s - t\bigr)$, with $f = f(u, v)$ differentiable and $\nabla f(2, 1) = (3, -2)$. What is the value of $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial t}$ at the point $(s, t) = (2, 1)$?

- $4$
- $5$
- $1$
- $8$

## Explanation

The intermediate variables are $u = st$ and $v = s - t$, so $\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t} = s$ and $\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial t} = -1$. By the **chain rule** we have $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial t} = s\,f_u - f_v$. At the point $(s, t) = (2, 1)$ the intermediate variables take the values $(u, v) = (2, 1)$, therefore $f_u = 3$ and $f_v = -2$. It follows that $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial t}(2, 1) = 2 \cdot 3 - (-2) = 8$.
