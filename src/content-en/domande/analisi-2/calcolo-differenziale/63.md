In the second-order Taylor polynomial centered at $(0, 0)$ of the function $f(x, y) = \sin x \cos y$, what is the coefficient of the term $y^2$?

- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The coefficient of $y^2$ in the Taylor formula is $\dfrac{1}{2} f_{yy}(0, 0)$. Differentiating twice with respect to $y$ gives $f_y = -\sin x \sin y$ and $f_{yy} = -\sin x \cos y$, which equals $0$ at $(0, 0)$ because $\sin 0 = 0$. Therefore the coefficient is $0$. The same result follows from the product of the expansions $\sin x = x + o(x^2)$ and $\cos y = 1 - \dfrac{y^2}{2} + o(y^2)$, because the product $-\dfrac{x y^2}{2}$ has degree $3$ and so the second-order polynomial is $x$.
