Let $F(t) = f(\cos t,\, \sin t)$ with $f(x, y) = x^2 y$. What is the value of $F'\!\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$

## Explanation

The function $f$ is a polynomial, so it is differentiable and the **chain rule** applies, with $f_x = 2xy$ and $f_y = x^2$. Since $x'(t) = -\sin t$ and $y'(t) = \cos t$, we have
$$
F'(t) = 2\cos t \sin t \cdot (-\sin t) + \cos^2 t \cdot \cos t = -2\cos t \sin^2 t + \cos^3 t .
$$
For $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ we have $\cos t = \sin t = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and $\left( \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^3 = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$. Therefore $F'\!\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = -2 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$.
