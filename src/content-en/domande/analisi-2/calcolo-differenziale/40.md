For the function $f(x, y) = \ln\left( x + y^2 \right)$, defined for $x + y^2 > 0$, what is the value of $f_{xy}(1, 1)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $-1$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Differentiating with respect to $x$ with $y$ held constant we obtain $f_x = \dfrac{1}{x + y^2} = \left( x + y^2 \right)^{-1}$. By the derivative of a composite function, the derivative of $\left( x + y^2 \right)^{-1}$ with respect to $y$ is $-\left( x + y^2 \right)^{-2} \cdot 2y$, so
$$
f_{xy} = -\frac{2y}{\left( x + y^2 \right)^2} .
$$
At the point $(1, 1)$ we have $x + y^2 = 2$, therefore $f_{xy}(1, 1) = -\dfrac{2}{4} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
