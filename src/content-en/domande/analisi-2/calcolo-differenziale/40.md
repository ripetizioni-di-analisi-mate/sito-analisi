For $f(x, y) = xy$ at the point $(1, 2)$ one writes
$$
f(1 + h,\, 2 + k) = f(1, 2) + f_x(1, 2)\, h + f_y(1, 2)\, k + R(h, k) .
$$
What is the expression of the remainder $R(h, k)$?

- $hk + 2h + k$
- $hk + h - k$
- $hk$
- $0$

## Explanation

Expanding the product gives $f(1 + h,\, 2 + k) = (1 + h)(2 + k) = 2 + 2h + k + hk$. Since $f_x = y$ and $f_y = x$, we have $f(1, 2) = 2$, $f_x(1, 2) = 2$ and $f_y(1, 2) = 1$, so the linear part is $2h + k$. Therefore the remainder is $R(h, k) = hk$. Since $|hk| \leq \dfrac{h^2 + k^2}{2}$, the absolute value of the quotient $\dfrac{R(h, k)}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}}$ is bounded by $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}$, which tends to $0$, so $f$ is **differentiable** at $(1, 2)$.
