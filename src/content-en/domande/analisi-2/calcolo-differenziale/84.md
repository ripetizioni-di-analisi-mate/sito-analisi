For the function $f(x, y) = xy$, at which points $(x_0, y_0, x_0 y_0)$ of the graph does the tangent plane pass through the origin $(0, 0, 0)$?

- Only at the point $(0, 0, 0)$
- At every point of the graph
- At the points with $x_0 = 0$ or $y_0 = 0$
- At the points with $x_0 = y_0$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = y$ and $f_y = x$, so the tangent plane at the point with coordinates $(x_0, y_0)$ is
$$
z = x_0 y_0 + y_0 (x - x_0) + x_0 (y - y_0) = y_0\, x + x_0\, y - x_0 y_0 .
$$
Substituting $x = y = z = 0$ gives the condition $x_0 y_0 = 0$. Therefore the tangent plane passes through the origin at the points with $x_0 = 0$ or $y_0 = 0$, that is, at the points of the graph lying above the coordinate axes.
