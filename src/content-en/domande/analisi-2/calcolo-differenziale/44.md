What is the second-order Taylor polynomial of $f(x, y) = x^2 y$ centered at $(1, 1)$?

- $T_2(x, y) = 1 + 2(x - 1) + (y - 1) + 2(x - 1)^2 + 4(x - 1)(y - 1)$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 + 2(x - 1) + (y - 1) + (x - 1)^2 + (x - 1)(y - 1)$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 + 2x + y + x^2 + 2xy$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 + 2(x - 1) + (y - 1) + (x - 1)^2 + 2(x - 1)(y - 1)$

## Explanation

We have $f(1, 1) = 1$, $f_x = 2xy$ and $f_y = x^2$, so $f_x(1, 1) = 2$ and $f_y(1, 1) = 1$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2y$, $f_{xy} = 2x$ and $f_{yy} = 0$, which at $(1, 1)$ equal $2$, $2$ and $0$. With $h = x - 1$ and $k = y - 1$ the **second-order Taylor formula** gives
$$
T_2 = 1 + 2h + k + \frac{1}{2} \bigl( 2h^2 + 2 \cdot 2\,hk \bigr) = 1 + 2h + k + h^2 + 2hk .
$$
Substituting $h = x - 1$ and $k = y - 1$ we obtain the polynomial in powers of $x - 1$ and $y - 1$.
