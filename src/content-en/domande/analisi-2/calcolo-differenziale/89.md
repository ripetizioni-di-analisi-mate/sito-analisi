The function $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ measures the distance from the origin. At the point $(3, 4)$, what is the sum of the pure second derivatives $f_{xx} + f_{yy}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{25}$

## Explanation

Setting $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, one has $f_x = \dfrac{x}{r}$, and differentiating the quotient with $r_x = \dfrac{x}{r}$ gives
$$
f_{xx} = \frac{r - x \cdot \frac{x}{r}}{r^2} = \frac{r^2 - x^2}{r^3} = \frac{y^2}{r^3} .
$$
In the same way $f_{yy} = \dfrac{x^2}{r^3}$, so $f_{xx} + f_{yy} = \dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{r^3} = \dfrac{1}{r}$. At the point $(3, 4)$ one has $r = 5$, therefore the sum equals $\dfrac{1}{5}$.
