A point moves counterclockwise along the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 2$ and passes through $(1, 1)$. What is the directional derivative at $(1, 1)$ of $f(x, y) = x + 2y$ along the unit vector tangent to the circle in the direction of motion?

::grafico{curve="sqrt(2)*cos(t),sqrt(2)*sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="1,1,0.2929,1.7071,v" punti="1,1,pieno" etichette="1.1,0.8,(1, 1)" legenda="x^2 + y^2 = 2" xmin=-2 xmax=2.2 ymin=-2 ymax=2.2 uguale=1 titolo="The unit vector $\mathbf{v}$ is tangent at $(1, 1)$ to the circle traversed counterclockwise"}

- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\sqrt{5}$

## Explanation

The radius reaching $(1, 1)$ has the direction of $(1, 1)$, and the tangent is perpendicular to the radius. In the counterclockwise direction the tangent unit vector is therefore $\mathbf{v} = \left( -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$. The function is linear, hence differentiable, with $\nabla f = (1,\, 2)$. By the **gradient formula**
$$
D_{\mathbf{v}} f(1, 1) = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} .
$$
