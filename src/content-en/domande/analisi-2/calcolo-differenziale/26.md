Let $g(x, y) = \varphi\left( x^2 + y^2 \right)$, with $\varphi$ differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ and $\varphi'(5) = 3$. What is $\nabla g(1, 2)$?

- $(3,\, 6)$
- $(2,\, 4)$
- $(3,\, 3)$
- $(6,\, 12)$

## Explanation

By the **chain rule** the derivative of $\varphi\left( x^2 + y^2 \right)$ with respect to $x$ is $\varphi'\left( x^2 + y^2 \right) \cdot 2x$, and with respect to $y$ it is $\varphi'\left( x^2 + y^2 \right) \cdot 2y$. At the point $(1, 2)$ one has $x^2 + y^2 = 5$, so $\varphi' = 3$. Therefore
$$
\nabla g(1, 2) = 3 \cdot (2,\, 4) = (6,\, 12) .
$$
