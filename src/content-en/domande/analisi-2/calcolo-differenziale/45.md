Let $f$ be of class $C^2$ with $f(0, 0) = 1$, $\nabla f(0, 0) = (0, 0)$ and
$$
H_f(0, 0) =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 1 \\
1 & -4
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
What is the second-order Taylor polynomial of $f$ centered at $(0, 0)$?

- $T_2(x, y) = 1 + x^2 + xy - 2y^2$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 + 2x^2 + 2xy - 4y^2$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 + x^2 + \dfrac{xy}{2} - 2y^2$
- $T_2(x, y) = 1 + x^2 + 2xy - 2y^2$

## Explanation

Since the gradient at the origin is zero, the first-degree term is zero, so the polynomial is $f(0, 0)$ plus the quadratic form of the Hessian matrix with the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$. From the matrix we read $f_{xx}(0, 0) = 2$, $f_{xy}(0, 0) = 1$ and $f_{yy}(0, 0) = -4$. By the **second-order Taylor formula** we therefore have
$$
T_2(x, y) = 1 + \frac{1}{2} \bigl( 2x^2 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot xy - 4y^2 \bigr) = 1 + x^2 + xy - 2y^2 .
$$
The mixed term has coefficient $f_{xy}(0, 0)$, because the quadratic form contains $2 f_{xy}\, hk$ and the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ halves it.
