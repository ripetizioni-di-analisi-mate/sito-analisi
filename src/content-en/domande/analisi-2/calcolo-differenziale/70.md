Which of the following functions has second-order Taylor polynomial centered at $(0, 0)$ equal to $1 + x + y + xy$?

- $e^{x + y}$
- $(1 + x)\,e^{y}$
- $(1 + x)(1 + \sin y)$
- $(1 + x)\cos y$

## Explanation

By the one-variable Taylor formula $\sin y = y + o\left( y^2 \right)$, because the second derivative of the sine at $0$ is zero. Since $y^2 \leq x^2 + y^2$, we therefore have $(1 + x)(1 + \sin y) = (1 + x)(1 + y) + o\left( x^2 + y^2 \right) = 1 + x + y + xy + o\left( x^2 + y^2 \right)$, and the polynomial is $1 + x + y + xy$. For $(1 + x)\,e^{y}$ the expansion $e^{y} = 1 + y + \dfrac{y^2}{2} + o\left( y^2 \right)$ adds the term $\dfrac{y^2}{2}$. For $e^{x + y}$ the second-degree term is $\dfrac{(x + y)^2}{2}$, which contains $x^2$ and $y^2$, and for $(1 + x)\cos y$ the polynomial is $1 + x - \dfrac{y^2}{2}$, without the term $y$.
