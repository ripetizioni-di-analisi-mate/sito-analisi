The sides of a rectangle change with time. At a certain instant they measure $x = 3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $y = 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and grow at rates $x' = 1\,\mathrm{cm/s}$ and $y' = 2\,\mathrm{cm/s}$. How fast does the diagonal grow at that instant?

- $\dfrac{11}{5}\,\mathrm{cm/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{cm/s}$
- $\dfrac{11}{25}\,\mathrm{cm/s}$
- $11\,\mathrm{cm/s}$

## Explanation

The diagonal is $d = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, with partial derivatives $d_x = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$ and $d_y = \dfrac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$. At the given instant $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = 5$, so $d_x = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $d_y = \dfrac{4}{5}$. By the **chain rule**
$$
d' = \frac{3}{5} \cdot 1 + \frac{4}{5} \cdot 2 = \frac{11}{5} ,
$$
therefore the diagonal grows at $\dfrac{11}{5}\,\mathrm{cm/s}$.
