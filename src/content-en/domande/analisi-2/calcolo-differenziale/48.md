Which of the following functions has the mixed second derivative $f_{xy}$ equal to zero at every point of $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $f(x, y) = e^{x} + \sin y$
- $f(x, y) = xy$
- $f(x, y) = e^{xy}$
- $f(x, y) = x \sin y$

## Explanation

For $f = e^{x} + \sin y$ one has $f_x = e^{x}$, which does not depend on $y$, so $f_{xy} = 0$ at every point. For the other functions one gets $f_{xy} = 1$ for $xy$, $f_{xy} = (1 + xy)\, e^{xy}$ for $e^{xy}$ and $f_{xy} = \cos y$ for $x \sin y$, which are not identically zero. The mixed derivative is zero when $f$ is the **sum** of a function of $x$ alone and a function of $y$ alone.
