At which points is the function $f(x, y) = \sqrt[3]{x^4} + y$ differentiable?

- At every point of $\mathbb{R}^2$
- At every point of $\mathbb{R}^2$ except the line $x = 0$
- At every point of $\mathbb{R}^2$ except the origin
- Only at the points with $x > 0$

## Explanation

For $x \neq 0$ we have $f_x = \dfrac{4}{3}\sqrt[3]{x}$, while at the points $(0, y)$ the derivative is computed from the definition. The difference quotient is $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{t^4}}{t} = \sqrt[3]{t}$, which tends to $0$, so $f_x(0, y) = 0$. Therefore $f_x = \dfrac{4}{3}\sqrt[3]{x}$ at every point, and this function is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$; $f_y = 1$ is continuous as well. By the **total differential theorem** $f$ is therefore **differentiable at every point** of $\mathbb{R}^2$.
