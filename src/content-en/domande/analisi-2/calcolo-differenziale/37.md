Let $F(r, \theta) = f(r\cos\theta,\, r\sin\theta)$ with $f(x, y) = xy$. What is the value of $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial \theta}$ at the point $(r, \theta) = (2, 0)$?

- $4$
- $2$
- $0$
- $8$

## Explanation

The intermediate variables are $x = r\cos\theta$ and $y = r\sin\theta$, with $\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial \theta} = -r\sin\theta$ and $\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial \theta} = r\cos\theta$. Since $f_x = y$ and $f_y = x$, by the **chain rule** we have
$$
\frac{\partial F}{\partial \theta} = y \cdot (-r\sin\theta) + x \cdot r\cos\theta .
$$
At the point $(r, \theta) = (2, 0)$ we have $x = 2$ and $y = 0$, therefore $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial \theta}(2, 0) = 0 + 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 4$. The same value is obtained from $F(r, \theta) = r^2 \sin\theta \cos\theta = \dfrac{r^2}{2} \sin 2\theta$, whose derivative with respect to $\theta$ is $r^2 \cos 2\theta$.
