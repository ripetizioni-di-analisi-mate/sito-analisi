For the function $f(x, y) = \ln \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, defined for $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$, what is the magnitude of the gradient $|\nabla f(x, y)|$?

- $\dfrac{1}{x^2 + y^2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$

## Explanation

Since $\ln \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \ln\left( x^2 + y^2 \right)$, the partial derivatives are
$$
f_x = \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2}, \qquad f_y = \frac{y}{x^2 + y^2}.
$$
The square of the magnitude of the gradient is therefore $\dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{\left( x^2 + y^2 \right)^2} = \dfrac{1}{x^2 + y^2}$. Hence $|\nabla f(x, y)| = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$, that is, the reciprocal of the distance from the origin.
