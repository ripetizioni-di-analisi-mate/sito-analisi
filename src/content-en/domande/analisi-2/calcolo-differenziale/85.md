Let $g(x, y) = f(x + y,\, x - y)$, with $f = f(u, v)$ of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. What is the expression of $g_{xy}$, with the derivatives of $f$ evaluated at the point $(x + y,\, x - y)$?

- $f_{uu} - f_{vv}$
- $f_{uu} + f_{vv}$
- $f_{uu} + 2f_{uv} + f_{vv}$
- $f_{uu} - 2f_{uv} + f_{vv}$

## Explanation

Since $u = x + y$ and $v = x - y$, we have $u_x = v_x = 1$, $u_y = 1$ and $v_y = -1$. By the **chain rule** $g_x = f_u + f_v$. Differentiating with respect to $y$, again with the chain rule, gives
$$
g_{xy} = f_{uu} - f_{uv} + f_{vu} - f_{vv} .
$$
Since $f$ is of class $C^2$, by **Schwarz's theorem** $f_{uv} = f_{vu}$, so the mixed terms cancel and $g_{xy} = f_{uu} - f_{vv}$.
