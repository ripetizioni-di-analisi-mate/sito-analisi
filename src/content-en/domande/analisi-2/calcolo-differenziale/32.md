For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3y^2$, along which unit vectors is the directional derivative at the point $(1, 1)$ equal to zero?

- $\mathbf{v} = \pm \left( \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\, \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{10}} \right)$
- $\mathbf{v} = \pm \left( \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{10}},\, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}} \right)$
- $\mathbf{v} = \pm \left( \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{10}},\, -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}} \right)$
- $\mathbf{v} = \pm \left( \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\, -\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{10}} \right)$

## Explanation

The function is a polynomial, so it is differentiable and by the **gradient formula** $D_{\mathbf{v}} f(1, 1) = \nabla f(1, 1) \cdot \mathbf{v}$. Since $f_x = 2x$ and $f_y = 6y$, we have $\nabla f(1, 1) = (2,\, 6)$. The directional derivative is zero if and only if $2v_1 + 6v_2 = 0$, that is, if $\mathbf{v}$ is orthogonal to $(2,\, 6)$, and the vectors orthogonal to $(2,\, 6)$ are the multiples of $(3, -1)$. Dividing $(3, -1)$ by its length $\sqrt{10}$ we obtain the two unit vectors $\pm \left( \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{10}},\, -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}} \right)$.
