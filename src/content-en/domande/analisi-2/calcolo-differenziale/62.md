For the function $f(x, y) = x^3 + 3xy^2$, at which points is the determinant of the Hessian matrix zero?

- Only at the origin
- On the coordinate axes
- On the lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$
- On the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$

## Explanation

The first derivatives are $f_x = 3x^2 + 3y^2$ and $f_y = 6xy$, so $f_{xx} = 6x$, $f_{xy} = 6y$ and $f_{yy} = 6x$. The determinant of the Hessian matrix is
$$
\det H_f(x, y) = 6x \cdot 6x - (6y)^2 = 36 \left( x^2 - y^2 \right).
$$
Since $x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y)$, the determinant vanishes on the lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$.
