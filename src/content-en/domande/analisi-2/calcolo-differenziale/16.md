For the function $f(x, y) = \arctan \dfrac{y}{x}$, defined for $x \neq 0$, what is the value of $f_x(1, 1)$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$
- $1$

## Explanation

The derivative of $\arctan u$ is $\dfrac{1}{1 + u^2}$ and, with $y$ held constant, the derivative of $u = \dfrac{y}{x}$ with respect to $x$ is $-\dfrac{y}{x^2}$. By the chain rule we therefore have
$$
f_x = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{y^2}{x^2}} \cdot \left( -\frac{y}{x^2} \right) = -\frac{y}{x^2 + y^2} .
$$
At the point $(1, 1)$ we therefore obtain $f_x(1, 1) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
