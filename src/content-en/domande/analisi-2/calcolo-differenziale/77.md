For $x > 0$ and $y > 0$ one has $\ln(xy) = \ln x + \ln y$. Using this identity, what is the second-order Taylor expansion of $\ln(xy)$ at the point $(1, 2)$?

- $\ln 2 + (x - 1) + \dfrac{y - 2}{2} - \dfrac{(x - 1)^2}{2} - \dfrac{(y - 2)^2}{8}$
- $\ln 2 + (x - 1) + \dfrac{y - 2}{2} - \dfrac{(x - 1)^2}{2} - \dfrac{(y - 2)^2}{4}$
- $\ln 2 + (x - 1) + \dfrac{y - 2}{2} - \dfrac{(x - 1)^2}{2} - \dfrac{(y - 2)^2}{8} + \dfrac{(x - 1)(y - 2)}{2}$
- $\ln 2 + (x - 1) + \dfrac{y - 2}{2} - \dfrac{(x - 1)^2}{2} - \dfrac{(y - 2)^2}{2}$

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ and $y > 0$ one has $\ln(xy) = \ln x + \ln y$, so $f_x = \dfrac{1}{x}$, $f_y = \dfrac{1}{y}$, $f_{xx} = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, $f_{yy} = -\dfrac{1}{y^2}$ and $f_{xy} = 0$. At the point $(1, 2)$ they equal $f = \ln 2$, $f_x = 1$, $f_y = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $f_{xx} = -1$ and $f_{yy} = -\dfrac{1}{4}$. With the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in front of the quadratic term the polynomial is
$$
\ln 2 + (x - 1) + \frac{y - 2}{2} - \frac{(x - 1)^2}{2} - \frac{(y - 2)^2}{8} .
$$
