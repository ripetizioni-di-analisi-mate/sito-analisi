Let $w = f(x, y, z) = xy + z$, with $x = t$, $y = t^2$ and $z = t^3$. What is $\dfrac{dw}{dt}$ for $t = 1$?

- $3$
- $5$
- $6$
- $2$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives of $f$ are $f_x = y$, $f_y = x$ and $f_z = 1$, and the derivatives of the intermediate variables are $x' = 1$, $y' = 2t$ and $z' = 3t^2$. By the **chain rule**, extended to all three intermediate variables,
$$
\frac{dw}{dt} = y \cdot 1 + x \cdot 2t + 1 \cdot 3t^2 .
$$
For $t = 1$ one has $x = y = 1$, so $\dfrac{dw}{dt} = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6$.
