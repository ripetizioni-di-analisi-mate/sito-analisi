Let $F(t) = f(\cos t,\, \sin t)$, with $f$ differentiable on $\mathbb{R}^2$. Which expression equals $F'(0)$?

- $f_x(1, 0)$
- $f_x(1, 0) + f_y(1, 0)$
- $f_y(1, 0)$
- $-f_x(1, 0)$

## Explanation

By the **chain rule**
$$
F'(t) = f_x(\cos t, \sin t) \cdot (-\sin t) + f_y(\cos t, \sin t) \cdot \cos t .
$$
For $t = 0$ the point is $(\cos 0, \sin 0) = (1, 0)$, with $\sin 0 = 0$ and $\cos 0 = 1$. The first term vanishes, so $F'(0) = f_y(1, 0)$. The result corresponds to the fact that at $(1, 0)$ the unit circle, traversed counterclockwise, has velocity $(0, 1)$.
