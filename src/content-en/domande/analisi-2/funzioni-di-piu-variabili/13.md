## Explanation

In polar coordinates we have $x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$, so the expression becomes $\dfrac{\sin(3\rho^2)}{\rho^2}$, which depends only on $\rho$. Setting $t = 3\rho^2$, we have $\rho^2 = \dfrac{t}{3}$ and
$$
\frac{\sin(3\rho^2)}{\rho^2} = 3 \cdot \frac{\sin t}{t} .
$$
By the standard limit of the sine, $\dfrac{\sin t}{t} \to 1$ as $t \to 0$, therefore the expression tends to $3$. The limit exists and is finite, so $f$ **can be extended by continuity** by setting $\tilde{f}(0, 0) = 3$.
