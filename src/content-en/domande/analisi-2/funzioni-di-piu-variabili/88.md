Let $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ on the open disk $A = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 < 1 \}$. Which statement is true?

- $f$ has maximum $1$ and minimum $0$ on $A$
- $f$ has neither a maximum nor a minimum on $A$
- $f$ has maximum $1$ and no minimum on $A$
- $f$ has minimum $0$ and no maximum on $A$

## Explanation

At every point of $A$ we have $f(x, y) \geq 0$, and $f(0, 0) = 0$ with $(0, 0) \in A$, so $f$ has **minimum** $0$. On $A$ we have $f(x, y) < 1$, while the points $(t, 0)$ with $t \to 1^-$ give values $t^2$ arbitrarily close to $1$, so the supremum is $1$. The value $1$ is attained only on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, which does not belong to $A$, therefore $f$ **has no maximum**. The extreme value theorem does not apply, because $A$ is not closed.
