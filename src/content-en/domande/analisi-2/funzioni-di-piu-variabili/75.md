In $\mathbb{R}^3$, what is $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y, z) \to (0, 0, 0)} \frac{xyz}{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $1$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

Let $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$ be the distance from the origin. Each component satisfies $|x| \leq r$, $|y| \leq r$ and $|z| \leq r$, so $|xyz| \leq r^3$. Therefore, for $(x, y, z) \neq (0, 0, 0)$,
$$
\left| \frac{xyz}{x^2 + y^2 + z^2} \right| \leq \frac{r^3}{r^2} = r ,
$$
and $r \to 0$. By the **squeeze theorem** the limit equals $0$.
