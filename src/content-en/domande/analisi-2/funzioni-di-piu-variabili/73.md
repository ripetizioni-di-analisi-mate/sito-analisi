The denominator of the function $\dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{|x| + |y|}$ vanishes only at the origin. What does the function tend to as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

Expanding the square gives $(|x| + |y|)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + 2|x||y| \geq x^2 + y^2$, so $|x| + |y| \geq \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. Therefore, for $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$,
$$
0 \leq \frac{x^2 + y^2}{|x| + |y|} \leq \frac{x^2 + y^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} ,
$$
and $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \to 0$. By the **squeeze theorem** the limit equals $0$.
