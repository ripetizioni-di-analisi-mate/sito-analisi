For the function $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x y^2}{x^2 + y^4}$, which statement about the limit as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$ is true?

- The limit equals $0$, because the restriction to every line through the origin tends to $0$
- The limit does not exist, since the value along $x=y^2$ is $\dfrac12$ and the value along $x=0$ is $0$
- The limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, because the restriction to the curve $x = y^2$ is constantly equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The limit equals $0$ by the comparison theorem, because $|f(x, y)| \leq |x|$

## Explanation

Along the line $y = mx$ we have $f(x, mx) = \dfrac{m^2 x^3}{x^2 + m^4 x^4} = \dfrac{m^2 x}{1 + m^4 x^2}$, which tends to $0$, and on the $y$-axis the function equals $0$. Along the curve $x = y^2$, instead, we have
$$
f(y^2, y) = \frac{y^4}{y^4 + y^4} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
Since two restrictions produce different limits, by the **restriction theorem** the limit **does not exist**. The inequality $|f(x, y)| \leq |x|$ is false, because for $x = y^2$ we have $|f(x, y)| = \dfrac{1}{2}$, while $|x| = y^2$ tends to $0$.
