Let $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x^2 + y}{x^2 - y}$, defined for $y \neq x^2$. What is the restriction of $f$ to the parabola $y = 2x^2$, for $x \neq 0$?

- $3$
- $-1$
- $-3$
- $-\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

On the parabola $y = 2x^2$ the numerator becomes $x^2 + 2x^2 = 3x^2$ and the denominator becomes $x^2 - 2x^2 = -x^2$. For $x \neq 0$ the denominator does not vanish, so the parabola without the origin lies in the domain. Therefore
$$
f(x, 2x^2) = \frac{3x^2}{-x^2} = -3 ,
$$
and the restriction is constantly equal to $-3$.
