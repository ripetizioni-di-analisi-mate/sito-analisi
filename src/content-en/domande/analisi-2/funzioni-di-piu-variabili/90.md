Let
$$
f(x, y) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2} & (x, y) \neq (0, 0) \\[8pt]
0 & (x, y) = (0, 0)
\end{cases}
$$
on the closed disk $K = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1 \}$. Which statement is true?

- The extreme value theorem applies, because $f$ is continuous on $K$ and $K$ is compact
- The extreme value theorem does not apply, because $f$ is not continuous at the origin
- The extreme value theorem applies, because $K$ is compact, even without checking the continuity of $f$
- The extreme value theorem does not apply, because $f$ is piecewise defined

## Explanation

Away from the origin $f$ is a quotient of polynomials with nonzero denominator, so it is continuous. Since $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2} \leq 1$, we get $|f(x, y)| \leq |y|$, and $|y| \to 0$; by the **squeeze theorem** the limit at the origin equals $0 = f(0, 0)$, so $f$ is continuous at the origin as well. The disk $K$ is closed and bounded, that is compact. Both hypotheses are satisfied, therefore the **extreme value theorem** applies. The compactness of $K$ alone would not be enough, because the continuity of $f$ is also a hypothesis of the theorem.
