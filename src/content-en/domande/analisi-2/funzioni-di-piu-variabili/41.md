The function $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x^3 - y^3}{x - y}$, defined for $x \neq y$, can be extended continuously to the points of the line $y = x$. What value does the extension take at the point $(1, 1)$?

- $3$
- $2$
- $0$
- $1$

## Explanation

Since $x^3 - y^3 = (x - y)(x^2 + xy + y^2)$, for $x \neq y$ we have $f(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2$. The polynomial $x^2 + xy + y^2$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$, so the limit of $f$ as $(x, y) \to (1, 1)$ is computed by substitution and equals $1 + 1 + 1 = 3$. Therefore the **continuous extension** takes the value $3$ at the point $(1, 1)$.
