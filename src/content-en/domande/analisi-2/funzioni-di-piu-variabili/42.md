Let $a > 0$ and let
$$
f(x, y) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{|x|^a\, y}{x^2 + y^2} & (x, y) \neq (0, 0) \\[8pt]
0 & (x, y) = (0, 0)
\end{cases}
$$
For which values of $a$ is the function $f$ continuous at the origin?

- $a \geq 1$
- $a > 2$
- $a > 0$
- $a > 1$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates we have
$$
|f(\rho\cos\theta, \rho\sin\theta)| = \frac{\rho^{a+1} |\cos\theta|^a\, |\sin\theta|}{\rho^2} = \rho^{a-1} |\cos\theta|^a\, |\sin\theta| \leq \rho^{a-1} .
$$
If $a > 1$ the function $g(\rho) = \rho^{a-1}$ does not depend on $\theta$ and tends to $0$, so by **computing in polar coordinates** the limit equals $0 = f(0, 0)$ and $f$ is continuous. If $0 < a \leq 1$, on the bisector $y = x$ with $x > 0$ we have $f(x, x) = \dfrac{x^{a+1}}{2x^2} = \dfrac{x^{a-1}}{2}$, which tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ for $a = 1$ and to $+\infty$ for $a < 1$. In both cases the restriction does not tend to $0$, therefore $f$ is not continuous at the origin. Hence $f$ is continuous at the origin if and only if $a > 1$.
