Let $A = \{ (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : xy \geq 0 \}$, that is, the union of the closed first and third quadrants. What is the set $\mathring{A}$ of interior points of $A$?

- The set $A$ itself
- The set of points with $xy > 0$
- The union of the two coordinate axes
- The set of points with $x > 0$ and $y > 0$

## Explanation

Let $(x_0, y_0)$ be a point with $x_0 y_0 > 0$. The disk centered at $(x_0, y_0)$ with radius $\min(|x_0|, |y_0|)$ does not meet the axes, so it lies in the same open quadrant as its center, where $xy > 0$. Therefore the disk is contained in $A$ and the point is interior. A point with $x_0 y_0 = 0$ lies on an axis, and every neighborhood of it contains points of the open second or fourth quadrant, where $xy < 0$. Hence the points of the axes are boundary points and are not interior. Consequently $\mathring{A}$ is the set of points with $xy > 0$, that is, the union of the open first and third quadrants.
