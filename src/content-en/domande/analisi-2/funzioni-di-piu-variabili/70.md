The function $\dfrac{x^2}{y}$ is defined for $y \neq 0$, and the origin is an accumulation point of its domain. What is its limit as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$, if it exists?

- $0$
- The limit does not exist
- $+\infty$
- $1$

## Explanation

On the line $y = x$, for $x \neq 0$, the restriction equals $\dfrac{x^2}{x} = x$, which tends to $0$. On the parabola $y = x^2$, for $x \neq 0$, the restriction equals $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2} = 1$. Two restrictions produce different limits, so, by the **restriction theorem**, the limit **does not exist**. The numerator has a higher degree than the denominator, but the denominator vanishes on the whole $x$-axis, and near that axis the ratio takes large values.
