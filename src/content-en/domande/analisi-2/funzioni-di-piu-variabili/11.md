- $f$ is continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$
- $f$ is continuous only away from the origin, because the limit at $(0, 0)$ does not exist
- $f$ is continuous only away from the origin, because the limit at $(0, 0)$ equals $1$
- $f$ is continuous only away from the coordinate axes

## Explanation

Away from the origin $f$ is a quotient of polynomials with nonzero denominator, so it is continuous. At the origin we write $|f(x, y)| = \dfrac{y^2}{x^2 + y^2}\, |y|$. Since $y^2 \leq x^2 + y^2$, the ratio $\dfrac{y^2}{x^2 + y^2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, therefore $|f(x, y)| \leq |y|$. The function $|y|$ tends to $0$, so by the **squeeze theorem** the limit equals $0 = f(0, 0)$. Consequently $f$ is **continuous on all of** $\mathbb{R}^2$.
