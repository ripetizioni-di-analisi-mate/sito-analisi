Among the following subsets of the plane, only one is path-connected. Which one?

- $\{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \neq 1 \}$
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{ (0, 0) \}$
- $\{ (x, y) : xy > 0 \}$
- $\{ (x, y) : |x| > 1 \}$

## Explanation

Two points of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$ can be joined by a segment or, if the segment passes through the origin, by an arc going around it; so the set is **connected**. The set $x^2 + y^2 \neq 1$ consists of the open disk and of the exterior of the circle, and a continuous curve joining them crosses the circle, therefore it is not connected. The set $xy > 0$ is the union of the open first and third quadrants, and a curve joining them crosses an axis. The set $|x| > 1$ is the union of the half-planes $x > 1$ and $x < -1$, and a curve joining them passes through the $y$-axis, where $|x| \leq 1$.
