The level curves of a function $f(x, y)$ are all lines parallel to the line $y = 2x$. Which of the following functions has this property?

- $f(x, y) = x + 2y$
- $f(x, y) = y - 2x$
- $f(x, y) = \dfrac{y}{x}$
- $f(x, y) = 2x + y$

## Explanation

For $f(x, y) = y - 2x$ the level curve $k$ is the line $y = 2x + k$, which has slope $2$ for every $k$; hence all the level curves are **parallel** to $y = 2x$. For $x + 2y$ the level curves are the lines $y = \dfrac{k - x}{2}$, with slope $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. For $2x + y$ they are the lines $y = k - 2x$, with slope $-2$. For $\dfrac{y}{x}$ they are the lines $y = kx$ without the origin, which all pass through the origin and have different slopes.
