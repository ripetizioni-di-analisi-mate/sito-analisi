- $0$
- $e$

- $1$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates we have $x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$, so the expression becomes $\dfrac{e^{\rho^2} - 1}{\rho^2}$, which depends only on $\rho$. Setting $t = \rho^2$, we have $t \to 0^+$ as $\rho \to 0^+$. By the standard limit $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{e^t - 1}{t} = 1$ the expression tends to $1$. Since the expression does not contain $\theta$, the convergence is uniform in all directions, therefore the limit equals $1$.
