- The limit equals $0$
- The limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The limit does not exist
- The limit equals $1$

## Explanation

Along the line $y = mx$ we have
$$
f(x, mx) = \frac{m x^4}{x^6 + m^2 x^2} = \frac{m x^2}{x^4 + m^2} ,
$$
which tends to $0$ as $x \to 0$, and the restriction to the $y$-axis is also constantly equal to $0$. Along the curve $y = x^3$, on the other hand, we have
$$
f(x, x^3) = \frac{x^6}{x^6 + x^6} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
Since two restrictions produce different limits, by the **restriction theorem** the limit **does not exist**.
