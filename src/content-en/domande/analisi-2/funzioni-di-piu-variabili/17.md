Let $A = \{ (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : y > x^2 \}$. Which of the following points is a boundary point of $A$?

- $(0, 1)$
- $(1, 1)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $(-1, 2)$

## Explanation

The function $g(x, y) = y - x^2$ is continuous and $A$ is defined by the strict inequality $g(x, y) > 0$, so every point with $y > x^2$ is an interior point of $A$. For the same reason every point with $y < x^2$ is an exterior point of $A$. Therefore $(0, 1)$ and $(-1, 2)$ are interior points, while $(2, 1)$ is exterior, because $1 < 2^2$. The point $(1, 1)$ lies on the parabola $y = x^2$, and every neighborhood of it contains points $(1, 1 + t)$ of $A$ and points $(1, 1 - t)$ outside $A$, with $t > 0$ small enough. Hence $(1, 1)$ is a **boundary point** of $A$.
