What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 1)} \frac{x^2 (y - 1)}{x^2 + (y - 1)^2}$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

Since $x^2 \leq x^2 + (y - 1)^2$, the ratio $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + (y - 1)^2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$. Therefore
$$
\left| \frac{x^2 (y - 1)}{x^2 + (y - 1)^2} \right| = \frac{x^2}{x^2 + (y - 1)^2}\, |y - 1| \leq |y - 1| .
$$
The function $|y - 1|$ tends to $0$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 1)$, so by the **comparison theorem in two variables** the limit equals $0$.
