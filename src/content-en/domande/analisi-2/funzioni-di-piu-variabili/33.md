As $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$, to which value does the function $\dfrac{x^2 \sin y}{x^2 + y^2}$ tend?

- $0$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

Since $x^2 \leq x^2 + y^2$, the factor $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$. Moreover $|\sin y| \leq |y|$ for every $y$. Hence
$$
\left| \frac{x^2 \sin y}{x^2 + y^2} \right| = \frac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2}\, |\sin y| \leq |y| ,
$$
and $|y| \to 0$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$. Therefore, by the **squeeze theorem**, the limit equals $0$.
