On the line $y = x$ the ratio $\dfrac{\sin x - \sin y}{x - y}$ is not defined. What is its limit as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$?

- $0$
- $2$
- The limit does not exist
- $1$

## Explanation

By the sum-to-product formulas $\sin x - \sin y = 2 \cos\dfrac{x + y}{2} \sin\dfrac{x - y}{2}$. Setting $u = \dfrac{x - y}{2}$, which is different from zero in the domain, we have $x - y = 2u$, so
$$
\frac{\sin x - \sin y}{x - y} = \cos\frac{x + y}{2} \cdot \frac{\sin u}{u} .
$$
As $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$ the first factor tends to $\cos 0 = 1$, since the cosine is continuous, and $u \to 0$, so the second factor tends to $1$ by the **standard limit of the sine**. Therefore the limit equals $1$.
