The function $f(x, y) = e^{-\frac{y^2}{x^2}}$ is defined off the $y$-axis and takes values between $0$ and $1$. How does $f$ behave as $(x, y)$ tends to the origin?

- The limit equals $1$
- The limit equals $0$
- The limit equals $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

On the line $y = mx$, for $x \neq 0$, the exponent equals $-\dfrac{m^2 x^2}{x^2} = -m^2$, so the restriction is constantly equal to $e^{-m^2}$. For $m = 0$, that is on the $x$-axis, the restriction equals $1$; for $m = 1$ it equals $e^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{e}$. Two restrictions produce different limits, therefore, by the **restriction theorem**, the limit **does not exist**.
