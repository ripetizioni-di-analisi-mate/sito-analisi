What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 2)} \frac{\sin(xy)}{x}$?

- $0$
- $2$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Near $(0, 2)$ we have $y > 0$, so for $x \neq 0$ also $xy \neq 0$ and we can write
$$
\frac{\sin(xy)}{x} = y \cdot \frac{\sin(xy)}{xy} .
$$
As $(x, y) \to (0, 2)$ we have $t = xy \to 0$, therefore by the **standard limit of the sine** the factor $\dfrac{\sin t}{t}$ tends to $1$. The factor $y$ tends to $2$, so by the limit of a product the limit equals $2 \cdot 1 = 2$.
