Let $A = \left\{ \left( \dfrac{1}{n}, 0 \right) : n \in \mathbb{N}, \ n \geq 1 \right\}$. Which point is an accumulation point of $A$?

- $(0, 0)$
- $(1, 0)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 0 \right)$
- No point of the plane

## Explanation

Every neighborhood $B((0, 0), r)$ contains the points $\left( \dfrac{1}{n}, 0 \right)$ with $n > \dfrac{1}{r}$, which belong to $A$ and are different from the origin. Hence $(0, 0)$ is an **accumulation point** of $A$, even though it does not belong to $A$. A point $\left( \dfrac{1}{k}, 0 \right)$ of $A$, instead, has a neighborhood with radius smaller than the distance $\dfrac{1}{k} - \dfrac{1}{k+1}$ from the next point, which contains no other points of $A$. Therefore the points $(1, 0)$ and $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 0 \right)$ are **isolated**.
