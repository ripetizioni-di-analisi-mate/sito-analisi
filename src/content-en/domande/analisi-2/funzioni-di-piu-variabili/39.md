For which value of $a$ is the function
$$
f(x, y) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x^2 y^2}{x^2 + y^2} & (x, y) \neq (0, 0) \\[8pt]
a & (x, y) = (0, 0)
\end{cases}
$$
continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $a = 1$
- $a = 0$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- For no value of $a$

## Explanation

Away from the origin $f$ is a quotient of polynomials with nonzero denominator, so it is continuous for every $a$. Since $\dfrac{y^2}{x^2 + y^2} \leq 1$, for $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$ we have $0 \leq f(x, y) \leq x^2$, and $x^2 \to 0$. Therefore, by the **comparison theorem**, the limit of $f$ at the origin equals $0$. The function is continuous at $(0, 0)$ if and only if this limit coincides with $f(0, 0) = a$, so $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$ only for $a = 0$.
