For the function $f(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2 + y^2}$, for which values of $k$ is the level curve $k$ nonempty?

- $k > 0$
- $0 \leq k \leq 1$
- $0 < k < 1$
- $0 < k \leq 1$

## Explanation

The denominator $1 + x^2 + y^2$ is greater than or equal to $1$, so $0 < f(x, y) \leq 1$ for every $(x, y)$. Therefore for $k \leq 0$ and for $k > 1$ the level curve is empty. For $0 < k \leq 1$ the equation $f(x, y) = k$ is equivalent to
$$
x^2 + y^2 = \frac{1}{k} - 1 ,
$$
and the right-hand side is greater than or equal to $0$. Hence the level curve is the circle centered at the origin with radius $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{k} - 1}$, which for $k = 1$ reduces to the point $(0, 0)$. The level curve $k$ is therefore **nonempty** if and only if $0 < k \leq 1$.
