What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{1 - \cos(x^2 + y^2)}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates we have $x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$, so the expression becomes $\dfrac{1 - \cos(\rho^2)}{\rho^4}$, which depends only on $\rho$. Setting $t = \rho^2$, we have $t \to 0^+$ as $\rho \to 0^+$ and the expression becomes $\dfrac{1 - \cos t}{t^2}$. By the standard limit $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos t}{t^2} = \frac{1}{2}$ the expression tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since the expression does not contain $\theta$, the convergence is uniform in all directions, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
