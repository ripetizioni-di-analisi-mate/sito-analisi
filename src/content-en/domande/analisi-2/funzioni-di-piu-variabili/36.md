The function $(1 + xy)^{\frac{1}{x}}$ is defined at the points with $x \neq 0$ and $1 + xy > 0$. What value does it tend to as $(x, y) \to (0, 2)$?

- $e^2$
- $e$
- $1$
- $e^{\frac{1}{2}}$

## Explanation

Near $(0, 2)$ we have $y > 0$, so for $x \neq 0$ also $t = xy$ is different from zero. Since $\dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{y}{t}$, we can write
$$
(1 + xy)^{\frac{1}{x}} = \left[ (1 + t)^{\frac{1}{t}} \right]^{y} .
$$
As $(x, y) \to (0, 2)$ we have $t \to 0$, so by the **standard limit** $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} (1 + t)^{\frac{1}{t}} = e$ the base tends to $e$, while the exponent $y$ tends to $2$. The function $u^{v} = e^{v \ln u}$ is continuous for $u > 0$, therefore the limit equals $e^2$.
