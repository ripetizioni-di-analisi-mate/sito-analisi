What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (2, 1)} \frac{x - 2y}{x^2 - 4y^2}$?

- $0$
- $4$
- $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

At the point $(2, 1)$ the numerator and the denominator both equal $0$, so the limit takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $x^2 - 4y^2 = (x - 2y)(x + 2y)$, at the points of the domain, where $x - 2y \neq 0$ and $x + 2y \neq 0$, we have
$$
\frac{x - 2y}{x^2 - 4y^2} = \frac{1}{x + 2y} .
$$
The function $\dfrac{1}{x + 2y}$ is continuous at $(2, 1)$, so the limit is computed by substitution and equals $\dfrac{1}{2 + 2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
