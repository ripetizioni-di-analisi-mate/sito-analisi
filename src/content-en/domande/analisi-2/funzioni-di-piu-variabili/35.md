What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} x \cos\!\left( \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \right)$?

- $1$
- The limit does not exist
- $+\infty$
- $0$

## Explanation

The factor $\cos\!\left( \dfrac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \right)$ is bounded, because its absolute value is less than or equal to $1$ for every $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$. The factor $x$ tends to $0$, so by the **bounded times infinitesimal** theorem the product tends to $0$. Indeed $|f(x, y)| \leq |x|$, and $|x| \to 0$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$.
