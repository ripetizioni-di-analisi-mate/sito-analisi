The function $x \cos\!\left( \dfrac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \right)$ is defined away from the origin. What does it tend to as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$?

- $1$
- The limit does not exist
- $+\infty$
- $0$

## Explanation

The factor $\cos\!\left( \dfrac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \right)$ is bounded, because its absolute value is less than or equal to $1$ for every $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$. The factor $x$ tends to $0$, so by the theorem on the **product of a bounded function and an infinitesimal function** the product tends to $0$. Indeed $|f(x, y)| \leq |x|$, and $|x| \to 0$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$.
