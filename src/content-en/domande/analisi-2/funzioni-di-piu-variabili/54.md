The denominator of $f(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{x - y}$ vanishes at infinitely many points. What is the natural domain of $f$?

- The half-plane $y < x$
- The plane without the line $y = x$
- The plane without the line $y = -x$
- The plane without the origin

## Explanation

The only condition is that the denominator be different from zero, that is $x - y \neq 0$. This condition is equivalent to $y \neq x$. Hence the domain is the **plane without the line** $y = x$, which includes both the half-plane $y < x$ and the half-plane $y > x$.
