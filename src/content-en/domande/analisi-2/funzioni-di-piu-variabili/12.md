- $f$ is continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$
- $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$ and is not continuous at $(0, 0)$
- $f$ becomes continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$ by setting $f(0, 0) = 2$
- $f$ becomes continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$ by setting $f(0, 0) = 0$

## Explanation

Away from the origin $f$ is a quotient of polynomials with nonzero denominator, so it is continuous. On the $x$-axis we have $f(x, 0) = \dfrac{x^2}{x^2} = 1$, while on the line $y = -x$ we have $f(x, -x) = \dfrac{0}{2x^2} = 0$. Since two restrictions produce different limits, by the **restriction theorem** the limit at the origin does not exist. Therefore $f$ is **not continuous** at $(0, 0)$, and no value assigned at $(0, 0)$ makes it continuous.
