What value is obtained for $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (1, 1)} \frac{\ln(xy)}{xy - 1}$, with the function defined at the points with $xy > 0$ and $xy \neq 1$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $e$
- $2$

## Explanation

As $(x, y) \to (1, 1)$ the product $xy$ tends to $1$, so the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$. Setting $t = xy - 1$, we have $t \to 0$ with $t \neq 0$ in the domain, and $xy = 1 + t$. The expression becomes
$$
\frac{\ln(1 + t)}{t} ,
$$
which tends to $1$ by the **standard limit of the logarithm**. Therefore the limit equals $1$.
