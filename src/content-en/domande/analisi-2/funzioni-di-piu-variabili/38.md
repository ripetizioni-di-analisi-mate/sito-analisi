What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{\ln(1 + x^2 y^2)}{x^2 + y^2}$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The graph of $\ln(1 + t)$ lies below its tangent line at the origin, which is $y = t$, so $0 \leq \ln(1 + t) \leq t$ for every $t \geq 0$. With $t = x^2 y^2$ we obtain
$$
0 \leq \frac{\ln(1 + x^2 y^2)}{x^2 + y^2} \leq \frac{x^2 y^2}{x^2 + y^2} .
$$
Since $y^2 \leq x^2 + y^2$, the ratio $\dfrac{y^2}{x^2 + y^2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, therefore $\dfrac{x^2 y^2}{x^2 + y^2} \leq x^2$. The function $x^2$ tends to $0$, so by the **comparison theorem in two variables** the limit equals $0$.
