What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{x^2 y^2}{x^2 y^2 + (x - y)^2}$?

- The limit does not exist
- $0$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The denominator vanishes only if $xy = 0$ and $x = y$, that is, only at the origin. Along the $x$-axis, that is, for $y = 0$ and $x \neq 0$, the function equals $\dfrac{0}{x^2} = 0$. Along the bisector $y = x$ we have $x - y = 0$, so $f(x, x) = \dfrac{x^4}{x^4} = 1$. Since two restrictions produce different limits, by the **restriction theorem** the limit **does not exist**.
