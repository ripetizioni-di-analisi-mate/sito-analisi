Let $f(x, y) = \dfrac{y^2 \ln x}{(x - 1)^2 + y^2}$, defined for $x > 0$ and $(x, y) \neq (1, 0)$. What is the limit of $f$ as $(x, y) \to (1, 0)$?

- $1$
- The limit does not exist
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Since $y^2 \leq (x - 1)^2 + y^2$, the factor $\dfrac{y^2}{(x - 1)^2 + y^2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, so it is bounded. The logarithm is continuous at $x = 1$, therefore the factor $\ln x$ tends to $\ln 1 = 0$ as $(x, y) \to (1, 0)$. Hence, by the theorem on the **product of a bounded function and an infinitesimal function**, the limit equals $0$.
