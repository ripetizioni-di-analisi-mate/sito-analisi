## Explanation

With $x = \rho \cos\theta$ and $y = \rho \sin\theta$ the numerator becomes $\rho^3 \cos\theta \sin^2\theta$ and the denominator becomes $\rho^2$, so
$$
\left| \frac{x y^2}{x^2 + y^2} \right| = \rho\, |\cos\theta|\, \sin^2\theta \leq \rho ,
$$
since $|\cos\theta| \leq 1$ and $\sin^2\theta \leq 1$. The function $g(\rho) = \rho$ does not depend on $\theta$ and tends to $0$ as $\rho \to 0^+$. Therefore, by **computing in polar coordinates**, the limit equals $0$.
