For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2$, what is the level curve $k = 4$?

- The circle centered at the origin with radius $2$
- The ellipse centered at the origin with semi-axes $4$ along $x$ and $1$ along $y$
- The ellipse centered at the origin with semi-axes $2$ along $x$ and $1$ along $y$
- The ellipse centered at the origin with semi-axes $1$ along $x$ and $2$ along $y$

## Explanation

The level curve $k = 4$ is the set of points that satisfy $x^2 + 4y^2 = 4$. Dividing by $4$ we obtain the equation
$$
\frac{x^2}{4} + y^2 = 1 ,
$$
which describes an ellipse centered at the origin with semi-axes $\sqrt{4} = 2$ along the $x$-axis and $1$ along the $y$-axis. Indeed, for $y = 0$ we find the points $(\pm 2, 0)$ and for $x = 0$ the points $(0, \pm 1)$. Therefore the level curve $k = 4$ is an **ellipse** with semi-axes $2$ along $x$ and $1$ along $y$.
