Let
$$
f(x, y) =
\begin{cases}
1 + xy & y \geq 0 \\[4pt]
e^{x} & y < 0
\end{cases}
$$
On which set is $f$ continuous?

- On all of $\mathbb{R}^2$
- On $\mathbb{R}^2$ minus the $x$-axis
- On $\mathbb{R}^2$ minus the points $(x_0, 0)$ with $x_0 \neq 0$
- On $\mathbb{R}^2$ minus the origin

## Explanation

In the open half-planes $y > 0$ and $y < 0$ the function coincides with $1 + xy$ and with $e^x$, which are continuous, so $f$ is continuous at those points. Let $(x_0, 0)$ be a point of the $x$-axis, where $f(x_0, 0) = 1$. The restriction to the half-line $x = x_0$, $y > 0$ tends to $1$, while the restriction to the half-line $x = x_0$, $y < 0$ is constantly equal to $e^{x_0}$. If $x_0 \neq 0$ then $e^{x_0} \neq 1$, so by the **restriction theorem** the limit does not exist and $f$ is not continuous at $(x_0, 0)$. At the origin, instead, $|f(x, y) - 1|$ is less than or equal to $|xy| + |e^x - 1|$, which tends to $0$, therefore $f$ is continuous at $(0, 0)$.
