Let $T$ be the closed triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$. What is the absolute maximum of $f(x, y) = 2x + y$ on $T$?

- $3$
- $4$
- $7$
- $5$

## Explanation

The triangle is compact and $f$ is continuous, so by the **extreme value theorem** the maximum exists. The side joining $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$ lies on the line $3x + 2y = 6$, therefore $T$ is the set of points with $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$ and $3x + 2y \leq 6$. For these points we have
$$
2x + y = \frac{2}{3}(3x + 2y) - \frac{y}{3} \leq \frac{2}{3} \cdot 6 - \frac{y}{3} \leq 4 ,
$$
since $y \geq 0$. The value $4$ is attained at the vertex $(2, 0)$, so the maximum equals $4$.
