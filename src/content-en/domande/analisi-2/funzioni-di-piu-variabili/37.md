What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{x^2 + y^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 1} - 1}$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $0$

## Explanation

Setting $t = x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$, the expression depends only on $t$, and $t \to 0^+$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$. As $t \to 0^+$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{t + 1} + 1$ we obtain
$$
\frac{t}{\sqrt{t + 1} - 1} = \frac{t \left( \sqrt{t + 1} + 1 \right)}{(t + 1) - 1} = \sqrt{t + 1} + 1 .
$$
The expression $\sqrt{t + 1} + 1$ tends to $2$ as $t \to 0^+$, and it does not depend on $\theta$, therefore the limit equals $2$.
