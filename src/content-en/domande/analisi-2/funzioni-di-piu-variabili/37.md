The function $\dfrac{\sqrt{1 + x + 2y} - 1}{x + 2y}$ is defined at the points with $x + 2y \geq -1$ and $x + 2y \neq 0$. What is its limit as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Setting $t = x + 2y$, we have $t \to 0$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$, with $t \neq 0$ in the domain, and the expression becomes
$$
\frac{\sqrt{1 + t} - 1}{t} = \frac{(1 + t)^{\frac{1}{2}} - 1}{t} .
$$
By the **standard limit** $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{(1 + t)^{\alpha} - 1}{t} = \alpha$ with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, the expression tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
