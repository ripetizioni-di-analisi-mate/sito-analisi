The function $\dfrac{2x^2 + 2y^2 + x^3}{x^2 + y^2}$ is not defined at the origin. What is its limit as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$?

- $0$
- $3$
- $2$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

Separating the terms, for $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$ we have
$$
\frac{2x^2 + 2y^2 + x^3}{x^2 + y^2} = 2 + \frac{x^3}{x^2 + y^2} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2} \leq 1$, we get $\left| \dfrac{x^3}{x^2 + y^2} \right| \leq |x|$, and $|x| \to 0$. Hence the difference between the function and $2$ is bounded by $|x|$, and by the **squeeze theorem** the limit equals $2$.
