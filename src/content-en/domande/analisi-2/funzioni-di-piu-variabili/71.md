At the origin the product $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\, \ln(x^2 + y^2)$ takes the form $0 \cdot \infty$. What is its limit as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$?

- $0$
- $-\infty$
- $1$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

In polar coordinates we have $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = \rho$ and $x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$, so the expression becomes
$$
\rho \ln \rho^2 = 2 \rho \ln \rho ,
$$
which depends only on $\rho$. By the standard limit $\displaystyle\lim_{\rho \to 0^+} \rho \ln \rho = 0$, the expression tends to $0$. Since $\theta$ does not appear, the convergence is uniform in all directions, therefore the limit equals $0$.
