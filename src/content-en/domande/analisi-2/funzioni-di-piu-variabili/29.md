What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (1, 0)} \frac{(x - 1)\, y}{(x - 1)^2 + y^2}$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The limit does not exist
- $1$

## Explanation

Along the line $y = m(x - 1)$, which passes through $(1, 0)$, for $x \neq 1$ we have
$$
f(x, m(x - 1)) = \frac{m (x - 1)^2}{(x - 1)^2 + m^2 (x - 1)^2} = \frac{m}{1 + m^2} .
$$
The restriction is constant and its value depends on $m$: for $m = 0$ it equals $0$, for $m = 1$ it equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since two restrictions produce different limits, by the **restriction theorem** the limit **does not exist**.
