The work of a continuous field $F$ along a regular curve $\gamma$, traversed from the point $A$ to the point $B$, equals $5$. What is the work of $F$ along the same curve traversed from $B$ to $A$?

- $5$
- $-5$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$

## Explanation

The line integral of the second kind **changes sign** when the direction of the curve is reversed. Indeed, if $r : [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}^2$ traverses $\gamma$ from $A$ to $B$, the curve $s(t) = r(a + b - t)$ traverses it from $B$ to $A$ and has derivative $s'(t) = -r'(a + b - t)$. With the substitution $u = a + b - t$ the integral along $s$ becomes the opposite of the integral along $r$. The work from $B$ to $A$ is therefore $-5$. The result does not require the field to be conservative.
