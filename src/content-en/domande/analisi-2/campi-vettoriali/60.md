The field $F = (F_1, F_2)$ is of class $C^1$ and conservative on $\mathbb{R}^2$, and its first component is $F_1(x, y) = x^2 y^3$. What is $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}$?

- $2x y^3$
- $6x y^2$
- $3x^2 y^2$
- $x^2 y^3$

## Explanation

A conservative field of class $C^1$ is irrotational, because the mixed second derivatives of the potential coincide by **Schwarz's theorem**. Hence $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = \dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}$ at every point. Differentiating $F_1 = x^2 y^3$ with respect to $y$ gives $3x^2 y^2$, therefore $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = 3x^2 y^2$. The value $2xy^3$ is the derivative of $F_1$ with respect to $x$, which does not appear in the condition.
