The field $F(x, y, z) = \left( \dfrac{-y}{x^2 + y^2}, \; \dfrac{x}{x^2 + y^2}, \; 0 \right)$ is of class $C^1$ and irrotational on the open set $A$ obtained by removing the $z$-axis from $\mathbb{R}^3$. Which statement is true?

- $F$ is conservative on $A$, because $\operatorname{rot} F = (0, 0, 0)$ at every point of $A$
- $F$ is conservative on $A$, because in space a closed curve can always go around the $z$-axis
- $F$ is not conservative on $A$, because its circulation along $r(t) = (\cos t, \; \sin t, \; 5)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, equals $2\pi$
- Whether $F$ is conservative cannot be decided, because $A$ is not simply connected

## Explanation

Along the curve $r(t) = (\cos t, \sin t, 5)$ one has $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, so $F(r(t)) = (-\sin t, \; \cos t, \; 0)$, and $r'(t) = (-\sin t, \; \cos t, \; 0)$. The dot product equals $\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$, therefore the circulation is $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} 1 \, dt = 2\pi$. Since there is a closed curve in $A$ with nonzero circulation, by the **characterization theorem** the field **is not conservative** on $A$. The open set $A$ is not simply connected, since a circle around the $z$-axis cannot be shrunk to a point while staying in $A$; for this reason irrotationality alone is not enough. Computing one circulation nevertheless settles the question.
