On the upper arc of the circle $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1$, oriented from $(0, 0)$ to $(2, 0)$ through $(1, 1)$, the work of the field $F(x, y) = (y, \; x^2)$ is computed. Which value is obtained?

- $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The cross derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 1$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = 2x$, so the field is not conservative and the work is computed with the definition. The circle has center $(1, 0)$ and radius $1$, and the parametrization $r(t) = (1 + \cos t, \; \sin t)$ passes through $(0, 0)$ for $t = \pi$, through $(1, 1)$ for $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and through $(2, 0)$ for $t = 0$. The arc is therefore traversed with $t$ from $\pi$ to $0$, that is, clockwise. One has $r'(t) = (-\sin t, \; \cos t)$ and $F(r(t)) = \bigl( \sin t, \; (1 + \cos t)^2 \bigr)$, therefore the dot product is $-\sin^2 t + \cos t + 2\cos^2 t + \cos^3 t$. On $[0, \pi]$ the integrals of $\cos t$ and of $\cos^3 t$ are zero, hence
$$
\int_{\pi}^{0} \bigl( -\sin^2 t + 2\cos^2 t \bigr) \, dt = -\int_0^{\pi} \bigl( -\sin^2 t + 2\cos^2 t \bigr) \, dt = -\left( -\frac{\pi}{2} + \pi \right) = -\frac{\pi}{2} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is obtained by traversing the arc with $t$ from $0$ to $\pi$, that is, in the opposite direction.
