What is $\dfrac{1}{2} \displaystyle\oint_{\gamma} (x \, dy - y \, dx)$, where $\gamma$ is the curve $r(t) = (2 \sin t, \; 2 \cos t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$?

- $-4\pi$
- $4\pi$
- $0$
- $-8\pi$

## Explanation

With $x = 2 \sin t$ and $y = 2 \cos t$ one has $dx = 2 \cos t \, dt$ and $dy = -2 \sin t \, dt$, so
$$
x \, dy - y \, dx = (-4 \sin^2 t - 4 \cos^2 t) \, dt = -4 \, dt .
$$
The integral therefore equals $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot (-4) \cdot 2\pi = -4\pi$. The sign is negative because the curve starts at $(0, 2)$ and passes through $(2, 0)$ for $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, that is, it traverses the circle of radius $2$ **clockwise**, and the formula gives the area only for the positively oriented boundary.
