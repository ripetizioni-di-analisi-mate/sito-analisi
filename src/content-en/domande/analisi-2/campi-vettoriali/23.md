A field $F$, continuous on $\mathbb{R}^2$, has work $2$ along the segment from $(0, 0)$ to $(1, 1)$ and work $3$ along the arc of the parabola $y = x^2$ from $(0, 0)$ to $(1, 1)$. Which statement is true?

- $F$ is conservative, because $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected
- The circulation along the closed curve formed by the two arcs is zero
- $F$ is conservative and every potential of it satisfies $U(1, 1) - U(0, 0) = 5$
- $F$ is not conservative on $\mathbb{R}^2$

## Explanation

The two curves have the same endpoints and the same orientation, but the work along them is different. By the **characterization theorem for conservative fields** the work of a conservative field depends only on the endpoints, so $F$ **is not conservative** on $\mathbb{R}^2$. The closed curve that traverses the segment and then the arc of the parabola in the opposite direction has circulation $2 - 3 = -1$, which is nonzero. The simple connectedness of $\mathbb{R}^2$ guarantees conservativeness only for a field that is of class $C^1$ and irrotational, therefore $F$ does not have both of these properties.
