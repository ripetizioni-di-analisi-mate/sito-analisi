The field $F(x, y, z) = \bigl( y e^z, \; x e^z, \; x y e^z \bigr)$ is conservative on $\mathbb{R}^3$. Let $U$ be its potential that equals $0$ at the origin. What is $U(1, 2, \ln 3)$?

- $2$
- $6$
- $2 \ln 3$
- $3$

## Explanation

Integrating $F_1 = y e^z$ with respect to $x$ gives $U = x y e^z + c(y, z)$. Imposing $U_y = x e^z$ gives $c_y(y, z) = 0$, so $c$ depends only on $z$. Imposing $U_z = x y e^z$ gives $c'(z) = 0$, therefore the potentials are $U(x, y, z) = x y e^z + k$. The condition $U(0, 0, 0) = 0$ gives $k = 0$. Hence
$$
U(1, 2, \ln 3) = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot e^{\ln 3} = 6 .
$$
