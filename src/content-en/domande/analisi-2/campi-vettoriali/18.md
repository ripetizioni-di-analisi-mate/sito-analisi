The gravitational field of a mass placed at the origin, in suitable units, is
$$
F(x, y, z) = -\frac{(x, \; y, \; z)}{(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}
$$
on $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{(0, 0, 0)\}$. What is the work of $F$ along a piecewise regular curve, not passing through the origin, from the point $(0, 0, 1)$ to the point $(0, 3, 4)$?

- $-\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{24}{25}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Let $\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$ be the distance from the origin. The function $U = \dfrac{1}{\rho}$ has partial derivatives $U_x = -\dfrac{x}{\rho^3}$, $U_y = -\dfrac{y}{\rho^3}$, $U_z = -\dfrac{z}{\rho^3}$, so $\nabla U = F$ and the field is conservative with potential $U$. The initial point has distance $1$ from the origin and the final point has distance $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$. By the theorem on the **work of a conservative field** the work therefore equals
$$
U(0, 3, 4) - U(0, 0, 1) = \frac{1}{5} - 1 = -\frac{4}{5} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{4}{5}$ is obtained with the potential of the wrong sign, $-\dfrac{1}{\rho}$.
