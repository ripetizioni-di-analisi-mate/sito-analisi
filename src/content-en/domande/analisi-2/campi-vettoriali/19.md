Let $D$ be the region between the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$ and the circle $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1$, that is, $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 9, \; (x - 1)^2 + y^2 \geq 1 \}$. How is the positively oriented boundary of $D$, used in Green's theorem, traversed?

- Both circles counterclockwise
- Both circles clockwise
- $x^2 + y^2 = 9$ counterclockwise and $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1$ clockwise
- $x^2 + y^2 = 9$ clockwise and $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1$ counterclockwise

## Explanation

The disk $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 \leq 1$ has its points with $x \leq 2$, so it is contained in the disk of radius $3$ and $D$ is a domain with a hole. The boundary is **positively oriented** when it is traversed keeping $D$ on its left. On the outer circle the domain lies inside, so it is traversed counterclockwise. On the circle of the hole the domain lies outside, so it is traversed **clockwise**. The position of the hole, not centered at the origin, does not change these orientations.
