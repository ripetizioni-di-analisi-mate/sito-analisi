Along the upper unit semicircle, from $(1, 0)$ to $(-1, 0)$, the work of the rotation field $F(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{-y}{x^2 + y^2}, \; \dfrac{x}{x^2 + y^2} \right)$ equals $\pi$. What is the work along the lower unit semicircle, again from $(1, 0)$ to $(-1, 0)$?

- $-\pi$
- $\pi$
- $0$
- $-2\pi$

## Explanation

The lower semicircle from $(1, 0)$ to $(-1, 0)$ is parametrized by $r(t) = (\cos t, \; \sin t)$, with $t$ from $0$ to $-\pi$. On the unit circle $F(r(t)) = (-\sin t, \; \cos t) = r'(t)$, so the dot product equals $1$ and the work is
$$
\int_0^{-\pi} 1 \, dt = -\pi .
$$
The two works between the same end points are different, consistently with the fact that the field **is not conservative** on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$. Their difference, $\pi - (-\pi) = 2\pi$, is the circulation along the unit circle counterclockwise.
