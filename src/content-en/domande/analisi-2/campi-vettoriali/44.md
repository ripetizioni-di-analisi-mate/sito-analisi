For the field $F(x, y) = \bigl( 2xy, \; x^2 + xy \bigr)$ one integrates $F_1$ with respect to $x$, obtaining $U(x, y) = x^2 y + c(y)$, and imposes $U_y = F_2$. Which statement is true?

- $c'(y) = y$, so the potentials are $x^2 y + \dfrac{y^2}{2} + k$
- $c'(y) = 0$, so the potentials are $x^2 y + k$
- $c'(y) = xy$, so the potentials are $x^2 y + \dfrac{x y^2}{2} + k$
- $c'(y) = xy$, so the field is not conservative

## Explanation

Differentiating $U(x, y) = x^2 y + c(y)$ with respect to $y$ gives $x^2 + c'(y)$, and imposing $U_y = x^2 + xy$ yields $c'(y) = xy$. A function of $y$ alone cannot have derivative $xy$, so no potential exists and the field **is not conservative**. The same result follows from the necessary condition of irrotationality, since $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 2x$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = 2x + y$ do not coincide.
