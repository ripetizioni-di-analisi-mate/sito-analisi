The function $U(x, y) = x^2 y + 3$ is a potential of the field $F$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. Which of the following functions is also a potential of $F$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $V(x, y) = x^2 y - 5$
- $V(x, y) = x^2 y + y$
- $V(x, y) = 2x^2 y + 3$
- $V(x, y) = x^2 y + x$

## Explanation

The field is $F = \nabla U = (2xy, \; x^2)$. On a connected open set two potentials of the same field differ by a constant, so the potentials of $F$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ are the functions $x^2 y + k$ with $k \in \mathbb{R}$. The function $x^2 y - 5$ has this form, with $k = -5$, therefore it is a **potential** of $F$. The other three differ from $U$ by the nonconstant functions $y - 3$, $x^2 y$ and $x - 3$, so they are not potentials of $F$; for example $\nabla (x^2 y + y) = (2xy, \; x^2 + 1)$.
