Which of the following closed curves, contained in $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$, can be continuously deformed to a point while remaining in $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$?

- The circle $(x - 2)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 1$
- The circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$
- The ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + y^2 = 1$
- The boundary of the square with vertices $(\pm 1, \pm 1)$

## Explanation

A closed curve in the plane minus the origin can be shrunk to a point while remaining in the set when the region it encloses does not contain the origin. The circle with center $(2, 2)$ and radius $1$ encloses a disk whose points have distance at least $2\sqrt{2} - 1 > 0$ from the origin, so it shrinks to its center without crossing the origin. The unit circle, the ellipse and the boundary of the square enclose the origin, so they go around the removed point and cannot be shrunk to a point while remaining in the set.
