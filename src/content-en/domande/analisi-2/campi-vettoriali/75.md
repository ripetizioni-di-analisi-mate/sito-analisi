A spiral is parametrized by $r(t) = (t \cos t, \; t \sin t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$. What is the work of the field $F(x, y) = (-y, \; x)$ along the spiral?

- $2\pi^2$
- $\dfrac{8\pi^3}{3}$
- $0$
- $8\pi^3$

## Explanation

The cross derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = -1$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = 1$, so the field is not conservative and the work is computed with the definition. One has $r'(t) = (\cos t - t \sin t, \; \sin t + t \cos t)$ and $F(r(t)) = (-t \sin t, \; t \cos t)$. The dot product equals
$$
-t \sin t \cos t + t^2 \sin^2 t + t \sin t \cos t + t^2 \cos^2 t = t^2 ,
$$
therefore the work is
$$
\int_0^{2\pi} t^2 \, dt = \frac{8\pi^3}{3} .
$$
The value $0$ is obtained by neglecting the terms $-t \sin t$ and $t \cos t$ in the components of $r'(t)$, that is, by differentiating the spiral as if it were a circle.
