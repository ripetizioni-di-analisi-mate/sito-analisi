To find a potential of the conservative field $F(x, y) = \bigl( 3x^2 y, \; x^3 + 4y^3 \bigr)$, a student integrates $F_1$ with respect to $x$, integrates $F_2$ with respect to $y$ and adds the two results, obtaining $2x^3 y + y^4$. Which function is a potential of $F$?

- $U(x, y) = 2x^3 y + y^4$
- $U(x, y) = x^3 y$
- $U(x, y) = x^3 y + y^4$
- $U(x, y) = x^3 y + 4y^3$

## Explanation

The gradient of the function $2x^3 y + y^4$ is $(6x^2 y, \; 2x^3 + 4y^3)$, which differs from $F$, because the term $x^3 y$, which appears in both integrations, has been counted twice. With successive integration, integrating $F_1$ with respect to $x$ gives $U(x, y) = x^3 y + c(y)$. Imposing $U_y = F_2$ gives $x^3 + c'(y) = x^3 + 4y^3$, so $c'(y) = 4y^3$ and therefore $c(y) = y^4 + k$. A potential is thus $U(x, y) = x^3 y + y^4$, whose gradient is $(3x^2 y, \; x^3 + 4y^3) = F$.
