The figure shows the field $F(x, y) = (x, \; y)$ at some points of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$. What is the work of $F$ along the circle traversed once counterclockwise?

::grafico{xmin=-3.4 xmax=3.4 ymin=-3.2 ymax=3.2 uguale=1 unicolore=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="2,0,2.7,0;1.41,1.41,1.91,1.91;0,2,0,2.7;-1.41,1.41,-1.91,1.91;-2,0,-2.7,0;-1.41,-1.41,-1.91,-1.91;0,-2,0,-2.7;1.41,-1.41,1.91,-1.91" titolo="The field $F(x, y) = (x, y)$ at points of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$"}

- $8\pi$
- $4\pi$
- $0$
- $16\pi$

## Explanation

With the parametrization $r(t) = (2\cos t, \; 2\sin t)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, one has $F(r(t)) = (2\cos t, \; 2\sin t)$ and $r'(t) = (-2\sin t, \; 2\cos t)$. The dot product equals $-4\cos t \sin t + 4 \sin t \cos t = 0$ for every $t$. Indeed, at every point the field points along the radius, which is perpendicular to the tangent to the circle. The work is therefore **zero**. The value $8\pi$ is obtained by multiplying the length of the field, $2$, by the length of the circle, as if field and tangent were parallel.
