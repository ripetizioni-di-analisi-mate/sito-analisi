Let $f(x, y, z) = x^2 y z^3 + \sin (xz)$ and let $F = \nabla f$. What is $\operatorname{rot} F$?

- $(0, \; 0, \; 0)$ only at the points with $xz = 0$
- $(2xz^3, \; 0, \; 0)$ at every point
- $(0, \; 0, \; 0)$ at every point
- $(0, \; 3x^2 z^2, \; 0)$ at every point

## Explanation

The function $f$ is of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, so the field $F = \nabla f$ is of class $C^1$ and is conservative with potential $f$. By **Schwarz's theorem** the mixed second derivatives of $f$ coincide, and each component of the curl of $\nabla f$ is the difference of two equal mixed derivatives, for example $f_{zy} - f_{yz}$. Therefore $\operatorname{rot} F = (0, 0, 0)$ at every point, without computing the derivatives of $f$.
