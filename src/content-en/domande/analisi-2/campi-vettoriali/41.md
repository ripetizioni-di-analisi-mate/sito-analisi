Which of the following fields is conservative on $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $F(x, y) = (y, \; -x)$
- $F(x, y) = (xy, \; xy)$
- $F(x, y) = (y^2, \; x^2)$
- $F(x, y) = (x + y, \; x - y)$

## Explanation

The four fields are of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, which is simply connected, so each of them is conservative if and only if it is irrotational. For $(x + y, \; x - y)$ the cross derivatives both equal $1$, therefore the field is **conservative**, with potential $U(x, y) = \dfrac{x^2 - y^2}{2} + xy$. For $(y, -x)$ the cross derivatives are $1$ and $-1$, for $(xy, xy)$ they are $x$ and $y$, for $(y^2, x^2)$ they are $2y$ and $2x$. In these three cases the two derivatives do not coincide on $\mathbb{R}^2$, so the fields are not conservative.
