A point moves along the branch of the hyperbola $xy = 1$ from $(1, 1)$ to $\left( 2, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ under the force $F(x, y) = (y^2, \; x)$. What is the work of $F$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2} + \ln 2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} - \ln 2$
- $\ln 2 - \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The cross derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 2y$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = 1$, so the field is not conservative and the work is computed with the definition. The arc of the hyperbola is the graph of $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ for $1 \leq x \leq 2$, so it is parametrized by $r(t) = \left( t, \; \dfrac{1}{t} \right)$, $t \in [1, 2]$. One has $r'(t) = \left( 1, \; -\dfrac{1}{t^2} \right)$ and $F(r(t)) = \left( \dfrac{1}{t^2}, \; t \right)$, so the dot product is $\dfrac{1}{t^2} - \dfrac{1}{t}$ and the work equals
$$
\int_1^2 \left( \frac{1}{t^2} - \frac{1}{t} \right) dt = \left[ -\frac{1}{t} - \ln t \right]_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} - \ln 2 .
$$
The value $\dfrac{1}{2} + \ln 2$ is obtained by forgetting the minus sign in the second component of $r'(t)$.
