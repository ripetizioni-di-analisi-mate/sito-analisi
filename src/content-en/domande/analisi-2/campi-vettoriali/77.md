A field $F$ of class $C^1$ is irrotational on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$ and its circulation along the unit circle, traversed counterclockwise, is zero. Which statement is true?

- $F$ is conservative on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$
- $F$ is not conservative, because the domain is not simply connected
- $F$ is conservative only on the half-planes that do not contain the origin
- Whether $F$ is conservative cannot be decided without computing other circulations

## Explanation

Let $\gamma$ be a simple closed curve in $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$, traversed counterclockwise. If $\gamma$ does not enclose the origin, the field is irrotational on the enclosed region and by **Green's theorem** the circulation is zero. If $\gamma$ encloses the origin, Green's theorem applied to the region between $\gamma$ and a small circle centered at the origin shows that the two circulations coincide; the same argument shows that all circles centered at the origin have the circulation of the unit circle, that is, $0$. An arbitrary closed curve decomposes into curves of these two kinds, so every circulation is zero. By the **characterization theorem** the field is conservative on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$.
