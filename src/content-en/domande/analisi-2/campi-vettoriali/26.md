What are the potentials on $\mathbb{R}^2$ of the field $F(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{2x}{x^2 + 1} + y, \; x + \cos y \right)$?

- $U(x, y) = \ln (x^2 + 1) + 2xy + \sin y + k$
- $U(x, y) = \ln (x^2 + 1) + xy + \sin y + k$
- $U(x, y) = \arctan x + xy + \sin y + k$
- $U(x, y) = \ln (x^2 + 1) + xy - \sin y + k$

## Explanation

The field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the cross derivatives both equal $1$. Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, the field is **conservative**. The function $\dfrac{2x}{x^2 + 1}$ is the derivative of $\ln (x^2 + 1)$, so integrating $F_1$ with respect to $x$ gives $U(x, y) = \ln (x^2 + 1) + xy + c(y)$. Imposing $U_y = F_2$ gives $x + c'(y) = x + \cos y$, so $c'(y) = \cos y$ and therefore $c(y) = \sin y + k$. The potentials are
$$
U(x, y) = \ln (x^2 + 1) + xy + \sin y + k, \qquad k \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
