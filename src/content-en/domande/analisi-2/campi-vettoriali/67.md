The cardioid $r(t) = \bigl( (1 + \cos t) \cos t, \; (1 + \cos t) \sin t \bigr)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, is traversed counterclockwise. What is the area of the region it encloses?

- $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$
- $3\pi$
- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

We use the formula $\operatorname{area} = \dfrac{1}{2} \displaystyle\oint (x \, dy - y \, dx)$. For a curve of the form $r(t) = (\rho(t) \cos t, \; \rho(t) \sin t)$ a direct computation gives $x \, dy - y \, dx = \rho(t)^2 \, dt$, since the terms with $\rho'(t)$ cancel. With $\rho(t) = 1 + \cos t$ one obtains
$$
\operatorname{area} = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2\pi} (1 + \cos t)^2 \, dt = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2\pi} (1 + 2\cos t + \cos^2 t) \, dt = \frac{1}{2} (2\pi + 0 + \pi) = \frac{3\pi}{2} .
$$
The value $3\pi$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the formula.
