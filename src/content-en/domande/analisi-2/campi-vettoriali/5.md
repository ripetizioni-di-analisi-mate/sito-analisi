## Explanation

The cross derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 1$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = -1$, so the field is not irrotational and therefore it is not conservative. The work is then computed with the definition of the line integral.

The segment from $(1, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$ is parametrized by $r(t) = (1 - t, \, t)$, $t \in [0, 1]$, so that $r'(t) = (-1, 1)$ and $F(r(t)) = (t, \, t - 1)$. The dot product equals $F(r(t)) \cdot r'(t) = -t + (t - 1) = -1$, so the work is
$$
\int_0^1 (-1) \, dt = -1 .
$$
