We want to apply Green's theorem to the field $F(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{1}{x - 2}, \; xy \right)$ on the disk $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1 \}$. Are the hypotheses of the theorem satisfied?

- No, because $F$ is not defined on the line $x = 2$
- No, because $F$ is not conservative
- Yes, because $F$ is of class $C^1$ on an open set containing $D$
- Yes, because $F$ is irrotational

## Explanation

The field is defined and of class $C^1$ on the open half-plane $x < 2$, since the denominator $x - 2$ vanishes only on the line $x = 2$. The disk $D$ is contained in the half-plane, given that its points have $x \leq 1$, and its boundary is a circle, so the hypotheses of **Green's theorem** are satisfied. The theorem does not require the field to be conservative or irrotational; for this field the integrand is $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = y$, and the circulation along the unit circle equals $0$ by the symmetry of the disk with respect to the $x$-axis.
