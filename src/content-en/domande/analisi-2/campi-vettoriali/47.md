For the field $F(x, y) = \bigl( \sin y, \; x \cos y + x^2 \bigr)$, what is $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}$?

- $2x + 2\cos y$
- $-2x$
- $0$
- $2x$

## Explanation

The derivative of the second component with respect to $x$, with $y$ fixed, is $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = \cos y + 2x$. The derivative of the first component with respect to $y$ is $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = \cos y$. The difference therefore equals $\cos y + 2x - \cos y = 2x$. Since the difference is not identically zero, the field is not irrotational and therefore **is not conservative** on $\mathbb{R}^2$.
