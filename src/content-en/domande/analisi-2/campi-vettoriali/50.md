We look for a potential of the field $F(x, y) = \bigl( 3x^2 y + y^2, \; x^3 + 2xy \bigr)$ and, as a first step, integrate the first component with respect to $x$. Which expression is obtained?

- $x^3 y + y^2 + c(y)$
- $6xy + c(y)$
- $x^3 y + x y^2 + c(x)$
- $x^3 y + x y^2 + c(y)$

## Explanation

In the integration with respect to $x$ the variable $y$ is fixed, so $3x^2 y$ has antiderivative $x^3 y$ and $y^2$, constant with respect to $x$, has antiderivative $x y^2$. The integration constant is a **function of $y$ alone**, since the partial derivative with respect to $x$ of a function of $y$ is zero. Therefore
$$
U(x, y) = \int (3x^2 y + y^2) \, dx = x^3 y + x y^2 + c(y) .
$$
The expression $x^3 y + y^2 + c(y)$ treats $y^2$ as if the integration were with respect to $y$, while $c(x)$ does not have zero derivative with respect to $x$.
