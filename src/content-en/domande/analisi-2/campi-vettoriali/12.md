The field $F(x, y, z) = \bigl( 2xy, \; x^2 + z \cos (yz), \; y \cos (yz) \bigr)$ has zero curl on $\mathbb{R}^3$. Which expression describes all its potentials?

- $U(x, y, z) = x^2 y + 2 \sin (yz) + k$
- $U(x, y, z) = x^2 y - \sin (yz) + k$
- $U(x, y, z) = x^2 y + y \sin (yz) + k$
- $U(x, y, z) = x^2 y + \sin (yz) + k$

## Explanation

The field is of class $C^1$ and irrotational on $\mathbb{R}^3$, which is simply connected, so it is conservative. Integrating $F_1 = 2xy$ with respect to $x$ gives $U = x^2 y + c(y, z)$. Imposing $U_y = x^2 + z \cos (yz)$ gives $c_y(y, z) = z \cos (yz)$, so $c(y, z) = \sin (yz) + d(z)$. Finally, imposing $U_z = y \cos (yz)$ gives $y \cos (yz) + d'(z) = y \cos (yz)$, therefore $d$ is constant. The term $\sin (yz)$ appears **only once**, even though its derivatives appear both in $F_2$ and in $F_3$; the potentials are $U(x, y, z) = x^2 y + \sin (yz) + k$ with $k \in \mathbb{R}$.
