What are the potentials on $\mathbb{R}^2$ of the field $F(x, y) = \bigl( 3x^2 + 2y, \; 2x - 5y^4 \bigr)$?

- $U(x, y) = x^3 + 4xy - y^5 + k$
- $U(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy - y^5 + k$
- $U(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy + y^5 + k$
- $U(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy - \dfrac{y^5}{5} + k$

## Explanation

The components are polynomials, so the field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. The cross derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 2$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = 2$, so the field is irrotational on $\mathbb{R}^2$. Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, the theorem on **irrotational fields on a simply connected open set** applies and the field is conservative.

Integrating $F_1$ with respect to $x$ we obtain $U(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy + c(y)$. Imposing $U_y = F_2$ we obtain $2x + c'(y) = 2x - 5y^4$, so $c'(y) = -5y^4$ and therefore $c(y) = -y^5 + k$. The term $2xy$ comes from the first integration and appears only once in the potential. The potentials are
$$
U(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy - y^5 + k, \qquad k \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
