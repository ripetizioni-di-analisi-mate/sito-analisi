The field $F(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{-y}{x^2 + y^2}, \; \dfrac{x}{x^2 + y^2} \right)$ is irrotational on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$. On which of the following open sets is it conservative?

- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$
- $\{ (x, y) : 0 < x^2 + y^2 < 1 \}$
- The half-plane $y < 0$
- $\{ (x, y) : 1 < x^2 + y^2 < 4 \}$

## Explanation

The half-plane $y < 0$ is convex, hence simply connected, and does not contain the origin. The field is of class $C^1$ and irrotational on the half-plane, therefore the theorem on irrotational fields on a simply connected open set applies and the field is **conservative** on the half-plane. Each of the other three open sets contains a circle centered at the origin, and along every circle centered at the origin and traversed counterclockwise the circulation of the field equals $2\pi$. By the characterization theorem, therefore, the field is not conservative on these three open sets.
