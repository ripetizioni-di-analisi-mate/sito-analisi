What are the potentials on $\mathbb{R}^2$ of the field $F(x, y) = \bigl( 2x e^y, \; x^2 e^y - 2y \bigr)$?

- $U(x, y) = x^2 e^y - y^2 + k$
- $U(x, y) = x^2 e^y + y^2 + k$
- $U(x, y) = x^2 e^y + k$
- $U(x, y) = x^2 e^y - 2y + k$

## Explanation

The field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the cross derivatives both equal $2x e^y$, so the field is irrotational on $\mathbb{R}^2$. Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, the theorem on irrotational fields on a simply connected open set applies and the field is **conservative**.

Integrating $F_1 = 2x e^y$ with respect to $x$ we obtain $U(x, y) = x^2 e^y + c(y)$. Imposing $U_y = F_2$ we obtain $x^2 e^y + c'(y) = x^2 e^y - 2y$, so $c'(y) = -2y$. Integrating with respect to $y$ we obtain $c(y) = -y^2 + k$, therefore the potentials are
$$
U(x, y) = x^2 e^y - y^2 + k, \qquad k \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
