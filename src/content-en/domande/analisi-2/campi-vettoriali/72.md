A field $F$ is of class $C^1$ and irrotational on the open set $A = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 > 1 \}$, the exterior of the closed unit disk. Which statement is true?

- $F$ is conservative on $A$, because it is irrotational
- The theorem on irrotational fields on a simply connected open set does not apply on $A$
- $F$ is not conservative on $A$, because $A$ is not simply connected
- $F$ is conservative on $A$, because $A$ is connected

## Explanation

The circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ is contained in $A$ and goes around the removed disk, so it cannot be shrunk to a point while remaining in $A$. Therefore $A$ is connected but **not simply connected**, and the theorem on irrotational fields on a simply connected open set does not apply. With the given information alone one can conclude neither that $F$ is conservative nor that it is not. The field $(x, y)$ is irrotational and conservative on $A$, while the rotation field is irrotational and not conservative on $A$.
