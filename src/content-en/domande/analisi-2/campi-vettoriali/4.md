## Explanation

The field $F = (y^2, \, 2xy)$ is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, its cross derivatives both equal $2y$ and $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, so the field is conservative. Integrating $y^2$ with respect to $x$ we obtain $U(x, y) = xy^2 + c(y)$, and the condition $U_y = 2xy$ requires $c'(y) = 0$, therefore a potential is $U(x, y) = xy^2$.

The curve starts at $r(0) = (0, 0)$ and ends at $r(1) = \left( 1, \sin \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = (1, 1)$. By the theorem on the **work of a conservative field** the work depends only on the endpoints, so it equals
$$
U(1, 1) - U(0, 0) = 1 - 0 = 1 .
$$
