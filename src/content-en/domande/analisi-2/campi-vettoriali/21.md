What are the potentials on $\mathbb{R}^2$ of the field $F(x, y) = \bigl( y e^{xy} + 2x, \; x e^{xy} \bigr)$?

- $U(x, y) = xy \, e^{xy} + x^2 + k$
- $U(x, y) = e^{xy} + x^2 + k$
- $U(x, y) = \dfrac{e^{xy}}{y} + x^2 + k$
- $U(x, y) = e^{xy} + x^2 + y + k$

## Explanation

The field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the cross derivatives both equal $e^{xy} + xy \, e^{xy}$. Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, the field is **conservative**. Integrating $F_2 = x e^{xy}$ with respect to $y$ gives $U(x, y) = e^{xy} + c(x)$, where $c$ is a function of $x$ alone. Imposing $U_x = F_1$ gives $y e^{xy} + c'(x) = y e^{xy} + 2x$, so $c'(x) = 2x$ and therefore $c(x) = x^2 + k$. The potentials are
$$
U(x, y) = e^{xy} + x^2 + k, \qquad k \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
