On the half-plane $y > 0$ consider the field $F(x, y) = \left( \ln y + 1, \; \dfrac{x}{y} \right)$, which is conservative. What are its potentials?

- $U(x, y) = x \ln y + k$
- $U(x, y) = x \ln y + y + k$
- $U(x, y) = x \ln y + x + \ln y + k$
- $U(x, y) = x \ln y + x + k$

## Explanation

The second component is simpler to integrate. Integrating $F_2 = \dfrac{x}{y}$ with respect to $y$, with $x$ fixed and $y > 0$, gives $U(x, y) = x \ln y + c(x)$. Differentiating with respect to $x$ gives $\ln y + c'(x)$, and imposing $U_x = \ln y + 1$ yields $c'(x) = 1$, so $c(x) = x + k$. The half-plane is connected, therefore the potentials are $U(x, y) = x \ln y + x + k$ with $k \in \mathbb{R}$.
