In a cylindrical pipe with axis $z$ and radius $1$ a viscous fluid flows with velocity $v(x, y, z) = \bigl( 0, \; 0, \; 1 - x^2 - y^2 \bigr)$, maximal on the axis and zero at the wall. What is $\operatorname{rot} v$?

- $(0, \; 0, \; 0)$
- $(2y, \; -2x, \; 0)$
- $(-2x, \; -2y, \; 0)$
- $(-2y, \; 2x, \; 0)$

## Explanation

The first two components of $v$ are zero, so only the derivatives of $v_3 = 1 - x^2 - y^2$ remain in the curl. The first component is $\dfrac{\partial v_3}{\partial y} - \dfrac{\partial v_2}{\partial z} = -2y$. The second is $\dfrac{\partial v_1}{\partial z} - \dfrac{\partial v_3}{\partial x} = 0 - (-2x) = 2x$. The third is $\dfrac{\partial v_2}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial v_1}{\partial y} = 0$. Therefore $\operatorname{rot} v = (-2y, 2x, 0)$, which vanishes only on the axis of the pipe. The velocities are all parallel to the $z$-axis, but their length depends on the distance from the axis, and for this reason the field **is not irrotational**.
