The figure shows the field $F(x, y) = (y, \; 0)$, whose arrows are all parallel to the $x$-axis. Which statement is true?

::grafico{xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-2.8 ymax=2.8 uguale=1 unicolore=1 vettori="-2,1,-1.5,1;0,1,0.5,1;2,1,2.5,1;-2,2,-1,2;0,2,1,2;2,2,3,2;-2,-1,-2.5,-1;0,-1,-0.5,-1;2,-1,1.5,-1;-2,-2,-3,-2;0,-2,-1,-2;2,-2,1,-2" titolo="The field $F(x, y) = (y, 0)$: horizontal arrows, pointing right for $y > 0$ and left for $y < 0$, longer moving away from the $x$-axis"}

- The arrows are parallel, so $F$ is irrotational
- $F$ is conservative with potential $U(x, y) = xy$
- $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 0$, because $F_2$ is zero
- $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = -1$, so $F$ is not conservative

## Explanation

One has $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = 0$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 1$, so the difference equals $-1$ at every point. The field is not irrotational, therefore by the necessary condition it **is not conservative** on $\mathbb{R}^2$. Parallel arrows are not enough, because the length of the arrows changes from one horizontal line to another and this variation with respect to $y$ makes the difference of the cross derivatives nonzero. The function $xy$ has gradient $(y, x)$, which is different from $F$.
