An ellipse has equation $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + y^2 = 1$. What is the work of the field $F(x, y) = (2x - y, \; 2y + x)$ along its arc going from $(2, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$ counterclockwise?

- $\pi$
- $\pi - 3$
- $-3$
- $\pi + 3$

## Explanation

The field is the sum of $G = (2x, \; 2y)$ and $H = (-y, \; x)$. The field $G$ is the gradient of $U(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$, so by the theorem on the **work of a conservative field** its work equals $U(0, 1) - U(2, 0) = 1 - 4 = -3$. The field $H$ is not conservative and its work is computed with the parametrization $r(t) = (2\cos t, \; \sin t)$, $t \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$. Since $H(r(t)) = (-\sin t, \; 2\cos t)$ and $r'(t) = (-2\sin t, \; \cos t)$, the dot product equals $2\sin^2 t + 2\cos^2 t = 2$, therefore the work of $H$ is $2 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \pi$. The work of $F$ is the sum of the two works, that is, $\pi - 3$.
