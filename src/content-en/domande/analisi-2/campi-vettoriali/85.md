Consider the circles centered at the origin with radius $R > 0$, traversed counterclockwise. For which value of $R$ is the circulation of the field $F(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{y^3}{3}, \; x - \dfrac{x^3}{3} \right)$ maximal?

- $R = 1$
- $R = \sqrt{2}$
- $R = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $R = 2$

## Explanation

By **Green's theorem** the circulation along the circle of radius $R$ is the integral over the disk of $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 1 - x^2 - y^2$. In polar coordinates one obtains
$$
C(R) = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^R (1 - \rho^2) \rho \, d\rho \, d\theta = 2\pi \left( \frac{R^2}{2} - \frac{R^4}{4} \right) .
$$
The derivative $C'(R) = 2\pi (R - R^3)$ is positive for $0 < R < 1$ and negative for $R > 1$, so the circulation is **maximal** for $R = 1$. The disk of radius $1$ is exactly the region where the integrand is positive; for $R = \sqrt{2}$ the circulation equals $0$.
