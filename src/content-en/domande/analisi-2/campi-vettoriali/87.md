For the field $F(x, y) = (y^2, \; x^2)$ the cross derivatives $\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}$ coincide at the points of the line $y = x$. Which statement is true?

- $F$ is conservative on every open strip containing the line $y = x$
- $F$ is conservative on the line $y = x$
- $F$ is not conservative on any open subset of the plane
- $F$ is conservative on the half-planes $y > x$ and $y < x$

## Explanation

The cross derivatives equal $2y$ and $2x$, so they coincide only at the points with $y = x$. A $C^1$ field that is conservative on an open set $A$ is irrotational at *every* point of $A$. Every open subset of the plane contains a disk, and every disk contains points with $y \neq x$, where the cross derivatives are different. Therefore $F$ **is not conservative** on any open set. The line $y = x$ is not an open set, so the notion of conservative field considered here does not apply to it.
