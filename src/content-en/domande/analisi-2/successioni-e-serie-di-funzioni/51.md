The sequence $f_n(x) = \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{n}\right)$ converges pointwise to $0$ on $\mathbb{R}$. Which statement about uniform convergence is true?

- It is uniform on $\mathbb{R}$, because $\left|\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{n}\right)\right| \leq \dfrac{1}{n}$
- It is not uniform on any interval $[-a, a]$ with $a > 0$
- It is uniform on every interval $[-a, a]$ with $a > 0$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$
- It is uniform on $\mathbb{R}$, because $\left|\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{n}\right)\right| \leq 1$

## Explanation

Since $|\sin t| \leq |t|$, on $[-a, a]$ we have $\left|\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{n}\right)\right| \leq \dfrac{|x|}{n} \leq \dfrac{a}{n}$, so the supremum on $[-a, a]$ tends to $0$ and the convergence is uniform. On $\mathbb{R}$ we choose $x = \dfrac{n \pi}{2}$, where $f_n\left(\dfrac{n \pi}{2}\right) = \sin \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$, so $\sup_{\mathbb{R}} |f_n| = 1$ for every $n$ and the convergence is not uniform. The estimate $\left|\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{n}\right)\right| \leq \dfrac{1}{n}$ is false, because the argument of the sine is $\dfrac{x}{n}$ and $x$ is not bounded.
