What is the sum $f(x)$ of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^2}{\left(1 + x^2\right)^n}$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$?

- $f(x) = 1$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = 1$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- $f(x) = 1 + x^2$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{1 + x^2}$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

At $x = 0$ all terms vanish, so $f(0) = 0$. For $x \neq 0$ the series is $x^2$ times a geometric series with common ratio $q = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2} \in (0, 1)$ starting at $n = 1$, so
$$
f(x) = x^2 \cdot \frac{q}{1 - q} = x^2 \cdot \frac{1}{x^2} = 1 .
$$
The sum is therefore discontinuous at $x = 0$, even though all terms are continuous. By the theorem on the **continuity of the sum** the convergence is not uniform on any interval containing $0$.
