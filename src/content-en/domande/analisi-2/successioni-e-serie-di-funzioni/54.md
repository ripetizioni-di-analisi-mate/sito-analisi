On $[0, 1]$ we study the uniform convergence of $f_n(x) = x^n - x^{2n}$, whose pointwise limit $f = 0$ is continuous. Which statement is true?

- It is uniform on $[0, 1]$, because the limit is continuous
- It is uniform on $[0, 1]$, because $f_n(0) = f_n(1) = 0$ for every $n$
- It is not uniform on any interval $[0, a]$ with $0 < a < 1$
- It is uniform on every interval $[0, a]$ with $0 < a < 1$, but not on $[0, 1]$

## Explanation

Set $t = x^n$, which ranges over $[0, 1]$ as $x$ ranges over $[0, 1]$. The function $t - t^2$ has maximum $\dfrac{1}{4}$ at $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\sup_{[0, 1]} |f_n| = \dfrac{1}{4}$ for every $n$, attained at $x = 2^{-\frac{1}{n}}$. Since the supremum does not tend to $0$, by the **supremum criterion** the convergence is not uniform on $[0, 1]$, even though the limit is continuous. On $[0, a]$ with $0 < a < 1$ we have $0 \leq f_n(x) \leq x^n \leq a^n$, and $a^n \to 0$, therefore the convergence is uniform on every interval of this kind.
