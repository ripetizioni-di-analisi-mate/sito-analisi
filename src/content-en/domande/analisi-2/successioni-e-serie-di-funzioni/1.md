- $f(x) = 1$ for $0 \leq x \leq 1$ and $f(x) = 0$ for $x > 1$
- $f(x) = 1$ for $0 \leq x < 1$, $f(1) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $f(x) = 0$ for $x > 1$
- $f(x) = 0$ for $0 \leq x < 1$, $f(1) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $f(x) = 1$ for $x > 1$
- $f(x) = 1$ for $0 \leq x < 1$ and $f(x) = 0$ for $x \geq 1$

## Explanation

For $0 \leq x < 1$ the geometric sequence $x^n$ tends to $0$, so $f_n(x) \to 1$. For $x = 1$ we have $1^n = 1$ for every $n$, therefore $f_n(1) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $n$ and the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$. For $x > 1$ the sequence $x^n$ tends to $+\infty$, so $f_n(x) \to 0$. The pointwise limit is therefore
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } 0 \leq x < 1 \\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2} & \text{if } x = 1 \\[8pt]
0 & \text{if } x > 1
\end{cases}
$$
