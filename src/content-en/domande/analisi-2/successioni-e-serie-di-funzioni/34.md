What is the set of pointwise convergence of the series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}$?

- $(-1, 1)$
- $[-1, 1)$
- $[-1, 1]$
- $(-1, 1]$

## Explanation

By the **root test** applied to the series of absolute values we have $\sqrt[n]{\dfrac{|x|^n}{n}} = \dfrac{|x|}{\sqrt[n]{n}} \to |x|$, so the series converges absolutely for $|x| < 1$. For $|x| > 1$ the term $\dfrac{|x|^n}{n}$ tends to $+\infty$, therefore the general term does not tend to zero and the series does not converge. For $x = 1$ we obtain the harmonic series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n}$, which diverges. For $x = -1$ we obtain $\sum \dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$, which converges by the **alternating series test**, because $\dfrac{1}{n}$ is decreasing and tends to zero. Hence the set of convergence is $[-1, 1)$.
