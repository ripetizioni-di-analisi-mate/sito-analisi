For which $x \in \mathbb{R}$ does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{1 + x^{2n}}$ converge?

- $x \in (-1, 1)$
- $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- $x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 1\}$
- $x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}$

## Explanation

For $|x| < 1$ we have $\left|\dfrac{x^n}{1 + x^{2n}}\right| \leq |x|^n$, so the series converges absolutely by comparison with the geometric series. For $|x| > 1$, dividing numerator and denominator by $x^{2n}$, we have $\left|\dfrac{x^n}{1 + x^{2n}}\right| \leq \dfrac{1}{|x|^n}$, and in this case too the series converges absolutely. For $x = 1$ the general term equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and for $x = -1$ it equals $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{2}$, so in both cases it does not tend to $0$ and the series does not converge. The set of convergence is therefore $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 1\}$.
