For the series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{2n + x^2}$, what is $\displaystyle\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \left| \frac{(-1)^n}{2n + x^2} \right|$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n + 1}$

## Explanation

For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ we have $2n + x^2 \geq 2n$, with equality at $x = 0$, so $\left| \dfrac{(-1)^n}{2n + x^2} \right| = \dfrac{1}{2n + x^2} \leq \dfrac{1}{2n}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2n}$ is attained at $x = 0$, therefore it is the maximum. The series $\sum \dfrac{1}{2n}$ is half the harmonic series and diverges, so the series **does not converge totally** on $\mathbb{R}$. For every fixed $x$ the sequence $\dfrac{1}{2n + x^2}$ is positive, decreasing and infinitesimal, so by the **Leibniz test** the series converges and the remainder satisfies $|f(x) - s_N(x)| \leq \dfrac{1}{2(N + 1) + x^2} \leq \dfrac{1}{2N + 2}$. Since $\dfrac{1}{2N + 2} \to 0$ and does not depend on $x$, the convergence is nevertheless uniform on $\mathbb{R}$.
