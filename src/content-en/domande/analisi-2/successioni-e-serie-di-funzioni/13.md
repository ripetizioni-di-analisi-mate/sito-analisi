On $[-1, 1]$ consider the sequence $f_n(x) = x^n$. At which points does it converge, and what is the limit?

- It converges on $[-1, 1]$, with limit $0$ on $(-1, 1)$ and $1$ at $x = \pm 1$
- It converges on $[-1, 1]$, with limit $0$ on $[-1, 1)$ and $1$ at $x = 1$
- It converges on $(-1, 1]$, with limit $0$ on $(-1, 1)$ and $1$ at $x = 1$
- It converges only on $[0, 1]$, with limit $0$ on $[0, 1)$ and $1$ at $x = 1$

## Explanation

For $|x| < 1$ the geometric sequence $x^n$ tends to $0$. For $x = 1$ we have $1^n = 1$ for every $n$, so the limit is $1$. For $x = -1$ the values $(-1)^n$ are alternately $-1$ and $1$, so the sequence has no limit. The set of convergence is therefore $(-1, 1]$, with limit $0$ on $(-1, 1)$ and $1$ at the point $x = 1$.
