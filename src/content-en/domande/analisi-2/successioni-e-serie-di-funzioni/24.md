Which of the following sequences of functions converges uniformly on $[0, 1]$?

- $f_n(x) = \dfrac{\sin(n x)}{n}$
- $f_n(x) = x^n$
- $f_n(x) = n x \, e^{-n x}$
- $f_n(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + n x}$

## Explanation

For $f_n(x) = \dfrac{\sin(n x)}{n}$ we have $|f_n(x)| \leq \dfrac{1}{n}$ for every $x$, so $\sup |f_n| \leq \dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$ and by the **sup criterion** the convergence to $0$ is **uniform**. The sequence $x^n$ tends to $0$ on $[0, 1)$ and to $1$ at $x = 1$, while $\dfrac{1}{1 + n x}$ tends to $1$ at $x = 0$ and to $0$ on $(0, 1]$. In both cases the functions are continuous and the pointwise limit is discontinuous, therefore by the theorem on the **continuity of the uniform limit** the convergence is not uniform. Finally, $n x \, e^{-n x}$ tends pointwise to $0$, but its maximum on $[0, 1]$ is at $x = \dfrac{1}{n}$ and equals $\dfrac{1}{e}$ for every $n$, so in this case too the convergence is not uniform.
