Let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{1 + n^2 x^2}$ for $x > 0$. Which statement is true?

- The convergence is normal on $(0, +\infty)$, so $f$ is continuous on $(0, +\infty)$
- The convergence is not normal on $(0, +\infty)$, but it is normal on every $[\delta, +\infty)$ with $\delta > 0$, so $f$ is continuous on $(0, +\infty)$
- The convergence is not normal on any half-line $[\delta, +\infty)$ with $\delta > 0$
- The convergence is normal on every $[\delta, +\infty)$, but $f$ is not continuous on $(0, +\infty)$, because the convergence on $(0, +\infty)$ is not normal

## Explanation

Each term is decreasing on $(0, +\infty)$ and tends to $1$ as $x \to 0^+$, so $\sup_{(0, +\infty)} |f_n| = 1$. The series $\sum 1$ diverges, so the convergence is not normal on $(0, +\infty)$. On $[\delta, +\infty)$, instead, $\sup |f_n| = \dfrac{1}{1 + n^2 \delta^2} \leq \dfrac{1}{\delta^2 n^2}$, and the series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges, so the convergence is normal. Every point $x_0 > 0$ belongs to the half-line $\left[\dfrac{x_0}{2}, +\infty\right)$, on which the sum is continuous by the theorem on the **continuity of the sum**. Therefore $f$ is continuous on all of $(0, +\infty)$.
