The sequence $f_n(x) = \sqrt[n]{x}$ is considered on $[0, 1]$ and on the subinterval $\left[\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right]$. Which statement is true?

- The convergence is uniform on $[0, 1]$
- The convergence is not uniform on $\left[\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right]$, because $\sqrt[n]{\dfrac{1}{2}} < 1$ for every $n$
- The convergence is uniform on $\left[0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$, but not on $\left[\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right]$
- The convergence is uniform on $\left[\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right]$, but not on $[0, 1]$

## Explanation

For $0 < x \leq 1$ we have $\sqrt[n]{x} = e^{\frac{\ln x}{n}} \to 1$, while $f_n(0) = 0$ for every $n$, so the pointwise limit equals $0$ at $x = 0$ and $1$ on $(0, 1]$. Since the $f_n$ are continuous and the limit is discontinuous at $0$, the convergence is not uniform on $[0, 1]$, nor on $\left[0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$. On $\left[\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right]$ the function $1 - \sqrt[n]{x}$ is decreasing, so
$$
\sup_{\left[\frac{1}{2}, 1\right]} \left| \sqrt[n]{x} - 1 \right| = 1 - \sqrt[n]{\frac{1}{2}} \xrightarrow[n \to +\infty]{} 0 ,
$$
therefore on $\left[\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right]$ the convergence is uniform.
