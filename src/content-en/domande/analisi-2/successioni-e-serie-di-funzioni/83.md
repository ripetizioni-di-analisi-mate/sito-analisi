The exponent $n^2$ makes the root test convenient. For which real values of $x$ is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{n + x}{n + 1} \right)^{n^2}$ convergent?

- For $x \leq 1$
- For $|x| < 1$
- For $x < 0$
- For $x < 1$

## Explanation

We apply the **root test** to the series of absolute values. For fixed $x$ we have
$$
\sqrt[n]{\left| \frac{n + x}{n + 1} \right|^{n^2}} = \left| 1 + \frac{x - 1}{n + 1} \right|^{n} \xrightarrow[n \to +\infty]{} e^{x - 1} ,
$$
by the standard limit $\left(1 + \dfrac{a}{n}\right)^n \to e^a$. If $x < 1$ then $e^{x - 1} < 1$, so the series converges absolutely. If $x > 1$ then $e^{x - 1} > 1$, so the general term does not tend to $0$ and the series does not converge. For $x = 1$ all the terms equal $1$, therefore the series diverges, and the set of convergence is $x < 1$.
