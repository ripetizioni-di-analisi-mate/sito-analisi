What is $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!} \right) dx$?

- $e - 1$
- $e$
- $e - \dfrac{5}{2}$
- $e - 2$

## Explanation

On $[0, 1]$ we have $\left| \dfrac{x^n}{n!} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{n!}$ and the series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n!}$ converges, so the series converges totally, hence uniformly, on $[0, 1]$. The terms are continuous, therefore **term-by-term integration** applies and gives
$$
\int_0^1 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!} \, dx = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n + 1)!} = \sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!} .
$$
Since $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!} = e$ and the terms with $k = 0$ and $k = 1$ equal $1$ each, the integral equals $e - 2$.
