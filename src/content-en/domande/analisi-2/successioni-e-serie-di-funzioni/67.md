Consider the functions $f_n(x) = n\left(e^{\frac{x}{n}} - 1\right)$ on $[0, 1]$. To which function $g$ does the sequence of derivatives $f_n'$ converge uniformly on $[0, 1]$?

- $g(x) = 0$
- $g(x) = e^x$
- $g(x) = 1$
- $g(x) = x$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f_n'(x) = e^{\frac{x}{n}}$, and on $[0, 1]$ we have $0 \leq e^{\frac{x}{n}} - 1 \leq e^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \to 0$, so $f_n' \to 1$ uniformly. Moreover $f_n(0) = 0$ for every $n$. By the **differentiation theorem for sequences** $f_n$ converges uniformly to a function $f$ with $f' = 1$ and $f(0) = 0$, that is, to $f(x) = x$, in agreement with the standard limit $\dfrac{e^t - 1}{t} \to 1$.
