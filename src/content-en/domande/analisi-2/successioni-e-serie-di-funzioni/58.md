What value does $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos\left( \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right) x \right) dx$ tend to as $n \to +\infty$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

By the mean value theorem we have $|\cos a - \cos b| \leq |a - b|$, so on $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$
$$
\left| \cos\left( \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right) x \right) - \cos x \right| \leq \frac{x}{n} \leq \frac{\pi}{2n} ,
$$
and the sequence converges uniformly to $\cos x$. The functions are continuous, so **passing to the limit under the integral sign** applies and the limit equals $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos x \, dx = 1$.
