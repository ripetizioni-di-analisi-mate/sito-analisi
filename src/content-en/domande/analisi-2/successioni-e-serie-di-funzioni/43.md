For $x > 0$ let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-n x}}{n}$. What is $f'(x)$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{e^x - 1}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e^x - 1}$
- $-\dfrac{e^x}{e^x - 1}$
- $-\dfrac{e^x}{\left( e^x - 1 \right)^2}$

## Explanation

The terms $\dfrac{e^{-n x}}{n}$ are of class $C^1$ and have derivative $-e^{-n x}$. On an interval $[\delta, b]$ with $0 < \delta < b$ we have $\left| -e^{-n x} \right| \leq e^{-n \delta}$ and the geometric series $\sum e^{-n \delta}$ converges, so the series of derivatives converges totally on $[\delta, b]$; moreover, the given series converges at $x_0 = \delta$. Therefore **term-by-term differentiation** applies on $[\delta, b]$, and since every $x > 0$ belongs to an interval of this kind, the sum of the geometric series with ratio $e^{-x}$ gives
$$
f'(x) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-n x} = -\frac{e^{-x}}{1 - e^{-x}} = -\frac{1}{e^x - 1} .
$$
