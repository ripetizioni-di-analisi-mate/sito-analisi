What is the pointwise limit on $\mathbb{R}$ of the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = \arctan(n x)$?

- $f(x) = 0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x \leq 0$ and $f(x) = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x > 0$
- $f(x) = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x < 0$, $f(0) = 0$ and $f(x) = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x > 0$

## Explanation

For $x = 0$ we have $f_n(0) = \arctan 0 = 0$ for every $n$. For $x > 0$ the argument $n x$ tends to $+\infty$, so $\arctan(n x) \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. For $x < 0$ the argument $n x$ tends to $-\infty$, so $\arctan(n x) \to -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The pointwise limit therefore equals $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x < 0$, $0$ at $x = 0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x > 0$. This limit is discontinuous at $0$, while the $f_n$ are continuous, so by the theorem on the **continuity of the uniform limit** the convergence is not uniform on $\mathbb{R}$.
