What is the pointwise limit on $(0, +\infty)$ of the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = n \left( x^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \right)$?

- $f(x) = \ln x$
- $f(x) = 0$
- $f(x) = x - 1$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

For $x = 1$ we have $f_n(1) = 0$ for every $n$, and $\ln 1 = 0$. For $x \neq 1$ we write $x^{\frac{1}{n}} = e^{\frac{\ln x}{n}}$ and set $t_n = \dfrac{\ln x}{n}$, which is different from $0$ and tends to $0$. Since $n = \dfrac{\ln x}{t_n}$, we have
$$
f_n(x) = n \left( e^{t_n} - 1 \right) = \ln x \cdot \frac{e^{t_n} - 1}{t_n} .
$$
By the **standard limit** $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{e^t - 1}{t} = 1$ the second factor tends to $1$, so $f_n(x) \to \ln x$. Therefore the pointwise limit is $f(x) = \ln x$ on all of $(0, +\infty)$.
