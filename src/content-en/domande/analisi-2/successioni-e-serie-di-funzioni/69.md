Let $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n} \sin\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$. What is $f'(0)$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The derivatives of the terms are $\dfrac{1}{n^2} \cos\left(\dfrac{x}{n}\right)$, and $\left|\dfrac{1}{n^2}\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{n}\right)\right| \leq \dfrac{1}{n^2}$, so the series of derivatives converges normally on $\mathbb{R}$. At $x = 0$ all terms of the given series vanish, so the series converges at that point. By the theorem on **term-by-term differentiation**
$$
f'(0) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} \cos 0 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6} .
$$
