For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = \dfrac{n x}{1 + n^2 x^2}$, which converges pointwise to $f = 0$ on $\mathbb{R}$, on which sets is the convergence uniform?

- On $\mathbb{R}$
- On every half-line $[\delta, +\infty)$ with $\delta > 0$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$
- On $(0, +\infty)$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$
- On no half-line $[\delta, +\infty)$ with $\delta > 0$

## Explanation

The function $f_n$ is odd, so $|f_n|$ is even and it suffices to study $x > 0$. For $x > 0$ the derivative is
$$
f_n'(x) = \frac{n \left( 1 - n^2 x^2 \right)}{\left( 1 + n^2 x^2 \right)^2} ,
$$
therefore $f_n$ is increasing on $\left( 0, \dfrac{1}{n} \right]$, decreasing on $\left[ \dfrac{1}{n}, +\infty \right)$ and has maximum $f_n\!\left( \dfrac{1}{n} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Hence $\sup |f_n| = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $n$, both on $\mathbb{R}$ and on $(0, +\infty)$, and on these sets the convergence **is not uniform**. On $[\delta, +\infty)$, for $n > \dfrac{1}{\delta}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{n} < \delta$, so $f_n$ is decreasing on $[\delta, +\infty)$ and
$$
M_n = f_n(\delta) = \frac{n \delta}{1 + n^2 \delta^2} \xrightarrow[n \to +\infty]{} 0 .
$$
Therefore the convergence is **uniform** on every half-line $[\delta, +\infty)$.
