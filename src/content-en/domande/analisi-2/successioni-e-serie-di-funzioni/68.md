Differentiating $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2 + x^2}$ term by term, which series is obtained for the derivative of the sum?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2x}{\left(n^2 + x^2\right)^2}$
- $-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\left(n^2 + x^2\right)^2}$
- $-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2x}{\left(n^2 + x^2\right)^2}$
- $-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2x}{n^2 + x^2}$

## Explanation

The derivative of the general term is
$$
\frac{d}{dx} \, \frac{1}{n^2 + x^2} = -\frac{2x}{\left(n^2 + x^2\right)^2} .
$$
On an interval $[-a, a]$ we have $\left| \dfrac{2x}{\left(n^2 + x^2\right)^2} \right| \leq \dfrac{2a}{n^4}$, so the series of derivatives converges normally; moreover the given series converges at $x = 0$, where it equals $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$. By the theorem on **term-by-term differentiation** on every $[-a, a]$, and hence on all of $\mathbb{R}$, the derivative of the sum is $-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2x}{\left(n^2 + x^2\right)^2}$.
