On $\mathbb{R}$ we study the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^2}{1 + n^3 x^2}$. Which statement is correct?

- It converges normally only on every interval $[-a, a]$, because $x^2$ is unbounded
- It converges normally on $\mathbb{R}$, with $\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{R}} |f_n| = \dfrac{1}{n^3}$
- It converges normally on $\mathbb{R}$, with $\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{R}} |f_n| = \dfrac{1}{2 n \sqrt{n}}$
- It converges on $\mathbb{R}$, but not normally, because $\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{R}} |f_n| = \dfrac{1}{n}$

## Explanation

We write
$$
\frac{x^2}{1 + n^3 x^2} = \frac{1}{n^3} \cdot \frac{n^3 x^2}{1 + n^3 x^2} ,
$$
and the second factor lies between $0$ and $1$ and tends to $1$ as $|x| \to +\infty$. Hence $\sup_{\mathbb{R}} |f_n| = \dfrac{1}{n^3}$, even though the supremum is not attained. The series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^3}$ converges, so the series **converges normally** on $\mathbb{R}$, although each term contains the unbounded factor $x^2$.
