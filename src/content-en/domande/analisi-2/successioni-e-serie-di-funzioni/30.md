For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = x^n$ on $[0, 1]$, with pointwise limit $f$, which statement is correct?

- The convergence is uniform, so $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 f_n \, dx = \int_0^1 f \, dx = 0$
- The convergence is not uniform, so $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 f_n \, dx \neq \int_0^1 f \, dx$
- The convergence is not uniform and $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 f_n \, dx = 1$
- The convergence is not uniform, but $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 f_n \, dx = \int_0^1 f \, dx = 0$

## Explanation

The pointwise limit equals $0$ on $[0, 1)$ and $1$ at $x = 1$, so it is discontinuous, and since the $f_n$ are continuous the convergence **is not uniform** by the theorem on the continuity of the uniform limit. For every $n$ we have
$$
\int_0^1 x^n \, dx = \frac{1}{n + 1} \xrightarrow[n \to +\infty]{} 0 .
$$
The function $f$ differs from $0$ at a single point, so $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f \, dx = 0$ and the limit of the integrals coincides with the integral of the limit. Therefore uniform convergence, required by **passing to the limit under the integral sign**, is a *sufficient* condition for the interchange, and *not a necessary* one.
