How far, in the sense of the supremum on $[0, +\infty)$, is the function $f_n(x) = \sqrt{x + \dfrac{1}{n}}$ from its pointwise limit $\sqrt{x}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n}$

## Explanation

Rationalizing, for every $x \geq 0$ we have
$$
\sqrt{x + \frac{1}{n}} - \sqrt{x} = \frac{\frac{1}{n}}{\sqrt{x + \frac{1}{n}} + \sqrt{x}} .
$$
The denominator increases with $x$, so the difference is decreasing and the supremum is attained at $x = 0$. Therefore the supremum equals $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$. Since $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \to 0$, the convergence is uniform on $[0, +\infty)$.
