For the geometric series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n$ on $(-1, 1)$, which statement is correct?

- It converges totally on $(-1, 1)$
- It converges uniformly on $(-1, 1)$, but not totally
- It converges totally on every interval $[-a, a]$ with $0 < a < 1$, but not uniformly on $(-1, 1)$
- It converges uniformly on $(-1, 1)$ to the sum $\dfrac{1}{1 + x}$

## Explanation

For $|x| < 1$ the series converges and has sum $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 - x}$. The $N$-th remainder equals
$$
f(x) - s_N(x) = \sum_{n = N+1}^{\infty} x^n = \frac{x^{N+1}}{1 - x} ,
$$
which tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to 1^-$, so $\displaystyle\sup_{(-1, 1)} |f - s_N| = +\infty$ for every $N$ and the convergence **is not uniform** on $(-1, 1)$. On $[-a, a]$ with $0 < a < 1$ we have $|x^n| \leq a^n$ and the geometric series $\sum a^n$ converges, therefore the convergence is **total** on every interval $[-a, a]$. Since every $x \in (-1, 1)$ belongs to an interval $[-a, a]$ of this kind, the sum is continuous on all of $(-1, 1)$.
