The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n x^n$ is considered on the interval $\left[0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$. Which statement is correct?

- It converges normally, with $\sup |f_n| = \dfrac{n}{2^n}$
- It does not converge normally, because the factor $n$ tends to $+\infty$
- It converges normally, with $\sup |f_n| = \dfrac{1}{2^n}$
- It converges normally on $[0, 1)$ as well

## Explanation

On $\left[0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$ the function $n x^n$ is increasing, so $\sup |f_n| = n \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^n = \dfrac{n}{2^n}$. By the **ratio test** the series $\sum \dfrac{n}{2^n}$ converges, because $\dfrac{n + 1}{2^{n+1}} \cdot \dfrac{2^n}{n} \to \dfrac{1}{2} < 1$. Therefore the series **converges normally** on $\left[0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$. On $[0, 1)$, instead, $\sup |f_n| = n$, and the series $\sum n$ diverges, so on $[0, 1)$ the convergence is not normal.
