What can be said about the convergence of $f_n(x) = \dfrac{1}{n x}$ on the half-line $(0, +\infty)$ and on its subsets?

- It is uniform on $(0, +\infty)$
- It is uniform on $(0, 1]$, but not on $[1, +\infty)$
- The sequence does not converge pointwise on $(0, +\infty)$
- It is uniform on $[1, +\infty)$, but not on $(0, +\infty)$

## Explanation

For every fixed $x > 0$ we have $\dfrac{1}{n x} \to 0$, so the pointwise limit is $f = 0$. On $[1, +\infty)$ the function $\dfrac{1}{n x}$ is decreasing, so $\sup_{[1, +\infty)} |f_n| = f_n(1) = \dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$ and the convergence is uniform. On $(0, +\infty)$, as on $(0, 1]$, we have $\dfrac{1}{n x} \to +\infty$ as $x \to 0^+$, so the supremum is $+\infty$ for every $n$ and the convergence is not uniform.
