What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 \frac{n x + 1}{n + x} \, dx$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $0$
- $\ln 2$

## Explanation

For every $x \in [0, 1]$ the integrand tends pointwise to $x$, and
$$
\left| \frac{n x + 1}{n + x} - x \right| = \frac{1 - x^2}{n + x} \leq \frac{1}{n} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, by the sup criterion the convergence is uniform on $[0, 1]$. The functions are continuous on $[0, 1]$, therefore **passing to the limit under the integral sign** is allowed and
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 \frac{n x + 1}{n + x} \, dx = \int_0^1 x \, dx = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
