For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = \dfrac{x}{1 + n x^2}$, which converges pointwise to $f = 0$ on $\mathbb{R}$, what is $M_n = \displaystyle\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} |f_n(x)|$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{n + 1}$

## Explanation

The function $f_n$ is odd, so $|f_n|$ is even and it suffices to study $x \geq 0$, where $f_n \geq 0$. The derivative is
$$
f_n'(x) = \frac{1 - n x^2}{\left( 1 + n x^2 \right)^2} ,
$$
therefore $f_n$ is increasing on $\left[ 0, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \right]$ and decreasing on $\left[ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}, +\infty \right)$. Hence the maximum is
$$
M_n = f_n\!\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \right) = \frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}{1 + 1} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}} .
$$
Since $M_n \to 0$, by the **supremum criterion** the convergence is **uniform** on $\mathbb{R}$.
