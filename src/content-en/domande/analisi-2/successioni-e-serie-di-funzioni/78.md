Consider the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{x^n}$, convergent for $|x| > 1$. What is its sum at the point $x = 3$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{9}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

Differentiating the geometric series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} q^n = \frac{1}{1 - q}$ term by term for $|q| < 1$ and multiplying by $q$ we get
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n q^n = \frac{q}{(1 - q)^2} .
$$
For $x = 3$ the terms are $n \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^n$, so with $q = \dfrac{1}{3}$ the sum equals $\dfrac{\frac{1}{3}}{\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2} = \dfrac{3}{4}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the sum of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^n$, without the factor $n$.
