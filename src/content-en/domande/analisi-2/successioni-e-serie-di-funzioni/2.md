- $f(x) = x$
- $f(x) = 1$
- $f(x) = \ln(1 + x)$
- $f(x) = 0$

## Explanation

For $x = 0$ we have $f_n(0) = n \ln 1 = 0$ for every $n$, so the limit equals $0$. For fixed $x > 0$ we write
$$
n \ln\!\left( 1 + \frac{x}{n} \right) = x \cdot \frac{\ln\!\left( 1 + \frac{x}{n} \right)}{\frac{x}{n}} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{x}{n} \to 0$, by the **standard limit of the logarithm** $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\ln(1 + t)}{t} = 1$ the second factor tends to $1$. Therefore $f_n(x) \to x$ for every $x \geq 0$, and the pointwise limit is $f(x) = x$ on $[0, +\infty)$.
