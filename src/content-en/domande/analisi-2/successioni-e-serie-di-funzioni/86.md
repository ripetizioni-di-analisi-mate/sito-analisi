Where does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-n x}}{n^2}$ converge normally?

- On $\mathbb{R}$
- Only on every half-line $[\delta, +\infty)$ with $\delta > 0$
- On $[0, +\infty)$
- On $(-\infty, 0]$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 0$ we have $0 < e^{-n x} \leq 1$, so $\left|\dfrac{e^{-n x}}{n^2}\right| \leq \dfrac{1}{n^2}$, with equality at $x = 0$. The series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges, so the series **converges normally** on all of $[0, +\infty)$, origin included. For $x < 0$ the term $\dfrac{e^{-n x}}{n^2} = \dfrac{e^{n |x|}}{n^2}$ tends to $+\infty$, so the series does not converge at any point of $(-\infty, 0)$.
