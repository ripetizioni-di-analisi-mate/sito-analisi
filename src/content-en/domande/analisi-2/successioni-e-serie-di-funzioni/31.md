What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 \left( 1 + \frac{x}{n} \right)^n dx$?

- $e$
- $1$
- $e - 1$
- $e + 1$

## Explanation

An antiderivative of $\left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{n} \right)^n$ is $\dfrac{n}{n + 1} \left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{n} \right)^{n + 1}$, so
$$
\int_0^1 \left( 1 + \frac{x}{n} \right)^n dx = \frac{n}{n + 1} \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^{n + 1} - 1 \right] .
$$
By the **standard limit** $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^n \to e$ we also have $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n + 1} \to e$. Since moreover $\dfrac{n}{n + 1} \to 1$, the limit equals $e - 1$. The result coincides with $\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^x \, dx$, since $\left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{n} \right)^n$ tends pointwise to $e^x$.
