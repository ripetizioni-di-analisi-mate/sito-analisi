The series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^x}$ converges pointwise for $x > 1$. On which sets does it converge normally?

- On $(1, +\infty)$
- On every half-line $[a, +\infty)$ with $a > 1$, but not on $(1, +\infty)$
- On $[1, +\infty)$
- On every interval $(1, a]$ with $a > 1$

## Explanation

For $x \geq a > 1$ we have $n^x \geq n^a$, so $\dfrac{1}{n^x} \leq \dfrac{1}{n^a} = M_n$, and the p-series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^a}$ converges because $a > 1$. Therefore the series **converges normally** on every half-line $[a, +\infty)$. On $(1, +\infty)$, and also on every interval $(1, a]$, the supremum of $\dfrac{1}{n^x}$ is the limit as $x \to 1^+$, that is, $\dfrac{1}{n}$, and the harmonic series diverges, so the convergence **is not normal**. On $[1, +\infty)$ the series does not even converge pointwise, because at $x = 1$ we obtain the harmonic series.
