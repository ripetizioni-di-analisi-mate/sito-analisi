Let $S(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2x}{\left(n + x^2\right)^2}$ for $x \in [0, 1]$. What is the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^1 S(x)\, dx$?

- $-1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

On $[0, 1]$ we have $0 \leq \dfrac{2x}{\left(n + x^2\right)^2} \leq \dfrac{2}{n^2}$ and the series $\sum \dfrac{2}{n^2}$ converges, so the series converges normally, hence uniformly, on $[0, 1]$. The terms are continuous, so **term-by-term integration** applies. For every $n$ we have
$$
\int_0^1 \frac{2x}{\left(n + x^2\right)^2}\, dx = \left[ -\frac{1}{n + x^2} \right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n + 1} .
$$
The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n + 1} \right)$ is telescoping, with partial sums $1 - \dfrac{1}{N + 1}$, therefore the integral equals $1$.
