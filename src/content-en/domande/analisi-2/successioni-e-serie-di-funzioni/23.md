For fixed $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the sequence $\left[\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]^{n}$ has limit $f(x)$. What is $f(x)$?

- $1$
- $e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}}$
- $e^{-x^2}$
- $0$ for $x \neq 0$ and $1$ for $x = 0$

## Explanation

For $n$ large enough the argument $\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{n}}$ is close to $0$, so $\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right) > 0$ and we can write
$$
\left[\cos\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]^{n} = e^{\,n \ln \cos\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)} .
$$
Since $\cos t = 1 - \dfrac{t^2}{2} + o\left(t^2\right)$ and $\ln(1 + s) = s + o(s)$ as $t, s \to 0$, with $t = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{n}}$ we get $n \ln \cos\left(\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right) = n \left( -\dfrac{x^2}{2n} + o\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right) \right) \to -\dfrac{x^2}{2}$. Therefore the pointwise limit is $f(x) = e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}}$, which is a continuous function.
