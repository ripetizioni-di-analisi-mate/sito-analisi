For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = n \sin\left( \dfrac{x}{n} \right)$ on $\mathbb{R}$, which statement is correct?

- It converges uniformly to $f(x) = x$ on $\mathbb{R}$
- It converges uniformly to $f = 0$ on $\mathbb{R}$
- It converges uniformly to $f(x) = x$ on every interval $[-a, a]$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$
- It converges uniformly to $f(x) = x$ on every half-line $[a, +\infty)$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

For fixed $x \neq 0$ we have $\dfrac{x}{n} \to 0$, so by the **standard limit** of the sine $n \sin\dfrac{x}{n} = x \cdot \dfrac{\sin\frac{x}{n}}{\frac{x}{n}} \to x$; moreover $f_n(0) = 0$, therefore the pointwise limit is $f(x) = x$. At the point $x = n\pi$ we have $f_n(n\pi) = n \sin \pi = 0$, so $\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{R}} |f_n(x) - x| \geq n\pi$ and the convergence **is not uniform** on $\mathbb{R}$, nor on any half-line $[a, +\infty)$, which contains $n\pi$ for large $n$. On $[-a, a]$ we use the inequality $|\sin t - t| \leq \dfrac{|t|^3}{6}$, which follows from Taylor's formula with the Lagrange remainder, and obtain
$$
|f_n(x) - x| = n \left| \sin\frac{x}{n} - \frac{x}{n} \right| \leq \frac{|x|^3}{6 n^2} \leq \frac{a^3}{6 n^2} \xrightarrow[n \to +\infty]{} 0 .
$$
Therefore the convergence is **uniform** on every interval $[-a, a]$.
