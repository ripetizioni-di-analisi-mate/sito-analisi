Consider the functions $f_n(x) = n \arctan\left(\dfrac{x}{n}\right)$ on $\mathbb{R}$. Which statement is correct?

- The $f_n'$ converge uniformly to $1$ on $\mathbb{R}$, so $f_n \to x$ uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$
- On every interval $[-a, a]$ the $f_n'$ converge uniformly to $1$, and $f_n \to x$ uniformly on $[-a, a]$
- On every interval $[-a, a]$ the $f_n'$ converge uniformly to $1$, but the limit of the $f_n$ is $x + c$ with $c$ undetermined
- The $f_n'$ converge to $0$, so the limit of the $f_n$ is a constant function

## Explanation

The derivative is $f_n'(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + \frac{x^2}{n^2}}$, so
$$
1 - f_n'(x) = \frac{\frac{x^2}{n^2}}{1 + \frac{x^2}{n^2}} \leq \frac{x^2}{n^2} .
$$
On $[-a, a]$ we get $\sup |1 - f_n'| \leq \dfrac{a^2}{n^2} \to 0$, therefore $f_n' \to 1$ uniformly. Moreover $f_n(0) = 0$ for every $n$, so by the **differentiation theorem for sequences** $f_n$ converges uniformly on $[-a, a]$ to a function $f$ with $f' = 1$ and $f(0) = 0$, that is, to $f(x) = x$. On $\mathbb{R}$ the convergence of the derivatives is not uniform, because $f_n'(n) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $n$.
