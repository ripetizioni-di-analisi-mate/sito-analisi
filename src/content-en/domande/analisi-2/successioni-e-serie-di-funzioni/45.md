For the series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(n x)}{n^2}$ on $\mathbb{R}$, which statement is correct?

- The sum is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, but the theorem on term-by-term differentiation does not apply on an interval containing $0$
- The sum is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and the theorem on term-by-term differentiation applies on every interval $[a, b]$
- The sum is not continuous at $x = 0$, because the series of derivatives diverges at $x = 0$
- The series converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$, but not totally

## Explanation

Since $\left| \dfrac{\sin(n x)}{n^2} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ and $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges, the series converges totally on $\mathbb{R}$. The terms are continuous, so by the theorem on the **continuity of the sum** the sum is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. The series of derivatives is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(n x)}{n}$, which at $x = 0$ becomes the harmonic series and diverges. Therefore the series of derivatives does not converge uniformly on any interval containing $0$, and the hypothesis on the derivatives in the theorem on **term-by-term differentiation** is not satisfied.
