What is the sum at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ of the series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

For every fixed $x \neq 0$ the series converges absolutely by the **ratio test**, because the ratio between the absolute values of two consecutive terms is $\dfrac{x^2}{(2n + 1)(2n + 2)} \to 0$. The series is the Taylor expansion of $\cos x$ centered at $0$, which converges to $\cos x$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Hence the sum at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ equals $\cos \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is $\sin \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, that is, the sum of the analogous series with odd powers.
