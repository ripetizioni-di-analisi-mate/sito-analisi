The functions $f_n$ are continuous on $[0, 1]$ and satisfy $\displaystyle\sup_{x \in [0, 1]} \left| f_n(x) - x^2 \right| = \dfrac{1}{n}$. What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 f_n(x)\, dx$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- It cannot be determined without the expression of $f_n$

## Explanation

Since $\sup_{[0, 1]} \left| f_n - x^2 \right| = \dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, by the **supremum criterion** the $f_n$ converge uniformly to $x^2$ on $[0, 1]$. Moreover
$$
\left| \int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx - \int_0^1 x^2\,dx \right| \leq \int_0^1 \left| f_n(x) - x^2 \right| dx \leq \frac{1}{n} ,
$$
so the limit of the integrals equals $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x^2\,dx = \dfrac{1}{3}$. The expression of the $f_n$ is not needed, because the uniform estimate is enough.
