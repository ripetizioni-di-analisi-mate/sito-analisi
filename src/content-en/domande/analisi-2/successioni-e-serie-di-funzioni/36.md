For the series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x}{n^2 + x^2}$, what is $\displaystyle\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \left| \frac{x}{n^2 + x^2} \right|$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n^2}$

## Explanation

The term $f_n(x) = \dfrac{x}{n^2 + x^2}$ is odd, so it suffices to study it for $x \geq 0$. The derivative is
$$
f_n'(x) = \frac{n^2 - x^2}{\left( n^2 + x^2 \right)^2} ,
$$
therefore $f_n$ is increasing on $[0, n]$ and decreasing on $[n, +\infty)$, and the maximum is $f_n(n) = \dfrac{n}{2n^2} = \dfrac{1}{2n}$. The series $\sum \dfrac{1}{2n}$ is half the harmonic series and diverges, so the series **does not converge totally** on $\mathbb{R}$. On every interval $[-a, a]$, instead, we have $|f_n(x)| \leq \dfrac{a}{n^2}$, therefore on $[-a, a]$ the convergence is **total**.
