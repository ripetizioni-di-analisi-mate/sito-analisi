The function $f(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(n x)}{2^n}$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. What is the value of its integral over $[0, \pi]$?

- $\ln 3$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\ln 2$

## Explanation

Since $\left|\dfrac{\sin(n x)}{2^n}\right| \leq \dfrac{1}{2^n}$, the series converges normally on $\mathbb{R}$, hence uniformly on $[0, \pi]$, and by **term-by-term integration** each term is integrated. We have
$$
\int_0^{\pi} \sin(n x)\,dx = \frac{1 - \cos(n\pi)}{n} = \frac{1 - (-1)^n}{n} ,
$$
which equals $\dfrac{2}{n}$ for odd $n$ and $0$ for even $n$. Therefore, setting $n = 2k + 1$,
$$
\int_0^{\pi} f(x)\,dx = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{2}{(2k + 1)\, 2^{2k+1}} = 2 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2k + 1} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2k+1} .
$$
From the expansions of $\ln(1 + t)$ and $\ln(1 - t)$ it follows that $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{t^{2k+1}}{2k + 1} = \frac{1}{2} \ln\frac{1 + t}{1 - t}$ for $|t| < 1$, so with $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$ the integral equals $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \ln 3 = \ln 3$.
