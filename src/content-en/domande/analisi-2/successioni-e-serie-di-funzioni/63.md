Integrating the geometric series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n$ term by term on $\left[0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$, what value is obtained for the integral of its sum over $\left[0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$?

- $1$
- $\ln 2$
- $2$
- $\ln \dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

On $\left[0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$ we have $|x^n| \leq \dfrac{1}{2^n}$ and the series $\sum \dfrac{1}{2^n}$ converges, so the series **converges normally**, hence uniformly. By **term-by-term integration**
$$
\int_0^{\frac{1}{2}} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n \, dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \int_0^{\frac{1}{2}} x^n \, dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n + 1)\, 2^{n+1}} .
$$
The sum of the geometric series is $\dfrac{1}{1 - x}$, so the same integral equals $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{1}{2}} \frac{dx}{1 - x} = \ln 2$, and the numerical series obtained has sum $\ln 2$.
