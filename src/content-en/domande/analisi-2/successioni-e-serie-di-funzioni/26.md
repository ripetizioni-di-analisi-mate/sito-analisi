For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = e^{-n x^2}$ on $\mathbb{R}$, which statement is correct?

- The pointwise limit is $f = 0$ and the convergence is uniform on $\mathbb{R}$
- The pointwise limit equals $1$ at $x = 0$ and $0$ for $x \neq 0$, and the convergence is uniform on $\mathbb{R}$
- The pointwise limit is $f = 0$ and the convergence is not uniform on $\mathbb{R}$
- The pointwise limit equals $1$ at $x = 0$ and $0$ for $x \neq 0$, and the convergence is uniform on every set $\{ |x| \geq \delta \}$ with $\delta > 0$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

For $x = 0$ we have $f_n(0) = 1$ for every $n$. For $x \neq 0$ we have $n x^2 \to +\infty$, so $e^{-n x^2} \to 0$. The pointwise limit is therefore discontinuous at $0$, while the $f_n$ are continuous, so by the theorem on the **continuity of the uniform limit** the convergence **is not uniform** on $\mathbb{R}$. On the set $\{ |x| \geq \delta \}$ the function $e^{-n x^2}$ is largest for $|x| = \delta$, therefore $M_n = e^{-n \delta^2} \to 0$ and on this set the convergence is **uniform**.
