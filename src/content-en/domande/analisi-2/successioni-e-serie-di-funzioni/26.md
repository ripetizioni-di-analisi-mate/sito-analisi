A sequence of functions $(f_n)$ converges pointwise to $f$ on a set $A$, and for every $n$ we have $\displaystyle\sup_{x \in A} |f_n(x) - f(x)| = \dfrac{n}{2n + 1}$. What can be concluded about uniform convergence on $A$?

- It is uniform, because the sequence of suprema is bounded
- It is uniform, because the sequence of suprema is convergent
- Nothing can be concluded without knowing $f$
- It is not uniform, because the supremum tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

By the **supremum criterion**, the convergence is uniform on $A$ if and only if $M_n = \sup_{A} |f_n - f|$ tends to $0$. Here $M_n = \dfrac{n}{2n + 1} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$, and the limit is different from $0$. Hence the convergence **is not uniform** on $A$. The criterion uses only the numerical sequence $M_n$, so there is no need to know the expression of $f$.
