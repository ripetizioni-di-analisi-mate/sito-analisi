For which values of the real parameter $\alpha$ does the sequence $f_n(x) = n^{\alpha}\, x\, e^{-n x}$ converge uniformly to $0$ on $[0, +\infty)$?

- $\alpha \leq 1$
- $\alpha < 2$
- $\alpha < 0$
- $\alpha < 1$

## Explanation

For every $x > 0$ the exponential $e^{-n x}$ tends to $0$ faster than any power $n^{\alpha}$, and $f_n(0) = 0$, so the pointwise limit is $f = 0$ for every $\alpha$. The derivative $f_n'(x) = n^{\alpha} e^{-n x}(1 - n x)$ vanishes at $x = \dfrac{1}{n}$, where $f_n$ has its maximum
$$
M_n = n^{\alpha} \cdot \frac{1}{n} \cdot e^{-1} = \frac{n^{\alpha - 1}}{e} .
$$
By the **supremum criterion** the convergence is uniform if and only if $n^{\alpha - 1} \to 0$, that is, if and only if $\alpha < 1$. For $\alpha = 1$ we have $M_n = \dfrac{1}{e}$ for every $n$, so the convergence is not uniform.
