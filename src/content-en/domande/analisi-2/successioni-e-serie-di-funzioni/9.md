- $1$
- $\ln 2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

For every $x \in [1, 2]$ we have $\dfrac{x}{n} \to 0$, so $\dfrac{e^{\frac{x}{n}}}{x} \to \dfrac{1}{x}$. Since $1 \leq x \leq 2$, on $[1, 2]$ we have
$$
\left| \frac{e^{\frac{x}{n}}}{x} - \frac{1}{x} \right| = \frac{e^{\frac{x}{n}} - 1}{x} \leq e^{\frac{2}{n}} - 1 .
$$
The right-hand side tends to $0$, so by the supremum criterion the convergence is uniform on $[1, 2]$. The functions are continuous on $[1, 2]$, therefore **passing to the limit under the integral sign** is justified and
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_1^2 \frac{e^{\frac{x}{n}}}{x} \, dx = \int_1^2 \frac{1}{x} \, dx = \ln 2 .
$$
