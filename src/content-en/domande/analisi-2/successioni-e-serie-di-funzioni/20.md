Which function is obtained as the pointwise limit on $\mathbb{R}$ of $f_n(x) = \dfrac{n x}{1 + n |x|}$?

- $f(x) = 1$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = -1$ for $x < 0$, $f(0) = 0$ and $f(x) = 1$ for $x > 0$
- $f(x) = -1$ for $x \leq 0$ and $f(x) = 1$ for $x > 0$
- $f(x) = x$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

For $x = 0$ we have $f_n(0) = 0$ for every $n$. For fixed $x \neq 0$, dividing numerator and denominator by $n$ we get
$$
f_n(x) = \frac{x}{\frac{1}{n} + |x|} ,
$$
which tends to $\dfrac{x}{|x|}$. The ratio $\dfrac{x}{|x|}$ equals $1$ for $x > 0$ and $-1$ for $x < 0$. Therefore the pointwise limit equals $-1$ on $(-\infty, 0)$, $0$ at $x = 0$ and $1$ on $(0, +\infty)$.
