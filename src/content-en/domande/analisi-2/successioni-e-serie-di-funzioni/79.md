How does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n e^{-n x}}{\sqrt{n}}$, which converges absolutely for $x > 0$, behave at the point $x = 0$?

- It converges absolutely
- It diverges to $+\infty$
- It converges, but not absolutely
- It does not converge, because the general term does not tend to $0$

## Explanation

At $x = 0$ the series becomes $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$. The sequence $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ is decreasing and tends to $0$, so by the **alternating series test** the series converges. The series of absolute values is $\sum \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$, a $p$-series with exponent $\dfrac{1}{2} \leq 1$, which diverges. Therefore at $x = 0$ the series converges, but not absolutely.
