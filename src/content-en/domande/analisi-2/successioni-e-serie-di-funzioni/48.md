The functions $f_n$ are continuous on $[0, 2]$. Which of the following functions can *not* be their uniform limit on $[0, 2]$?

- $f(x) = |x - 1|$
- $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$
- $f(x) = 0$ for $0 \leq x < 1$ and $f(x) = 1$ for $1 \leq x \leq 2$
- $f(x) = x^2$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the **continuity of the uniform limit**, the uniform limit on $[0, 2]$ of continuous functions is continuous on $[0, 2]$. The functions $|x - 1|$, $\sqrt{x}$ and $x^2$ are continuous on $[0, 2]$, even though $|x - 1|$ is not differentiable at $x = 1$ and $\sqrt{x}$ is not differentiable at $x = 0$. The function equal to $0$ on $[0, 1)$ and $1$ on $[1, 2]$ has a jump at $x = 1$, so it is not continuous. Therefore it cannot be the uniform limit of continuous functions on $[0, 2]$.
