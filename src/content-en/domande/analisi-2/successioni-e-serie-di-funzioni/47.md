On $[0, 1]$ the sequence $f_n(x) = \left(1 + \dfrac{x}{n}\right)^{n}$ converges pointwise to $e^x$. What is $M_n = \displaystyle\sup_{x \in [0, 1]} \left| e^x - f_n(x) \right|$?

- $\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n}\right)^{n} - 1$
- $1 - \dfrac{1}{e} \left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n}\right)^{n}$
- $e - 2$
- $e - \left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n}\right)^{n}$

## Explanation

Since $1 + t \leq e^t$ for every $t$, with $t = \dfrac{x}{n}$ we have $\left(1 + \dfrac{x}{n}\right)^n \leq e^x$, so the difference $g(x) = e^x - f_n(x)$ is nonnegative. Its derivative is
$$
g'(x) = e^x - \left(1 + \frac{x}{n}\right)^{n-1} ,
$$
which is nonnegative, because for $x \geq 0$ we have $\left(1 + \dfrac{x}{n}\right)^{n-1} \leq \left(1 + \dfrac{x}{n}\right)^{n} \leq e^x$. Therefore $g$ is increasing on $[0, 1]$ and the supremum is $g(1) = e - \left(1 + \dfrac{1}{n}\right)^{n}$. By the standard limit defining the number $e$ this value tends to $0$, so the convergence is uniform on $[0, 1]$.
