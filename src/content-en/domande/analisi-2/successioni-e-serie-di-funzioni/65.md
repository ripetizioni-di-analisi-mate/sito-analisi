What is the limit as $n \to +\infty$ of $\displaystyle\int_0^2 \frac{dx}{1 + x^n}$?

- $2$
- $0$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The pointwise limit equals $1$ on $[0, 1)$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ at $x = 1$ and $0$ on $(1, 2]$, so it is discontinuous and the convergence is not uniform on $[0, 2]$; the integral is therefore estimated directly. On $[0, 1]$ we have $0 \leq 1 - \dfrac{1}{1 + x^n} = \dfrac{x^n}{1 + x^n} \leq x^n$, so $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1 + x^n}$ differs from $1$ by at most $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x^n\,dx = \frac{1}{n + 1}$. On $[1, 2]$ we have $0 < \dfrac{1}{1 + x^n} \leq \dfrac{1}{x^n}$, so
$$
0 < \int_1^2 \frac{dx}{1 + x^n} \leq \int_1^2 x^{-n}\,dx = \frac{1 - 2^{1-n}}{n - 1} .
$$
Both estimates tend to $0$, so the limit is $1$, that is, the integral of the pointwise limit.
