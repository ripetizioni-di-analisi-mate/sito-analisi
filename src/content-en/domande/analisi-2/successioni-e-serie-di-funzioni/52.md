The pointwise limit of $f_n(x) = \dfrac{n x^2}{1 + n x^2}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ equals $1$ for $x \neq 0$ and $0$ at $x = 0$. Where is the convergence uniform?

- On $\mathbb{R}$
- On every interval $[-a, a]$ with $a > 0$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$
- On every set $\{ |x| \geq \delta \}$ with $\delta > 0$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$
- On no set $\{ |x| \geq \delta \}$ with $\delta > 0$

## Explanation

The $f_n$ are continuous and the pointwise limit is discontinuous at $x = 0$, so by the theorem on the **continuity of the uniform limit** the convergence is not uniform on $\mathbb{R}$, nor on any interval containing $0$. For $x \neq 0$ we have
$$
|f_n(x) - 1| = \frac{1}{1 + n x^2} ,
$$
which decreases as $|x|$ grows. On $\{ |x| \geq \delta \}$ the supremum is therefore $\dfrac{1}{1 + n \delta^2} \to 0$, so the convergence is uniform on every set of this kind.
