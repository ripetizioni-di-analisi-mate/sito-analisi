To prove that the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(n x)}{n^2 + x^2}$ converges normally on $\mathbb{R}$, which sequence $M_n$ can be used?

- $M_n = \dfrac{1}{n^2}$
- $M_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$
- $M_n = \dfrac{1}{n^2 + x^2}$
- $M_n = \dfrac{1}{x^2}$

## Explanation

For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ we have $|\sin(n x)| \leq 1$ and $n^2 + x^2 \geq n^2$, so $\left| \dfrac{\sin(n x)}{n^2 + x^2} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{n^2}$. The series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges, therefore $M_n = \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ satisfies both conditions of normal convergence. The expressions $\dfrac{1}{n^2 + x^2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ depend on $x$, so they are not numerical sequences. The bound by $\dfrac{1}{n}$ is true, but the harmonic series diverges, so it does not prove normal convergence.
