For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = x^n - x^{2n}$ on $[0, 1]$, which converges pointwise to $f = 0$, what is $M_n = \displaystyle\max_{x \in [0, 1]} |f_n(x)|$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n}$

## Explanation

We set $t = x^n$. As $x$ ranges over $[0, 1]$, $t$ also ranges over $[0, 1]$, and $f_n(x) = t - t^2 \geq 0$. The function $t - t^2$ has its maximum at $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$, where it equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$, so $M_n = \dfrac{1}{4}$ for every $n$, attained at $x = \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{1}{n}}$. Since $M_n$ does not tend to $0$, by the **supremum criterion** the convergence **is not uniform** on $[0, 1]$. Since the pointwise limit $f = 0$ is continuous, this sequence shows that the converse of the theorem on the continuity of the uniform limit does not hold.
