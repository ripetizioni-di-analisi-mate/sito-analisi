Consider $f_n(x) = \dfrac{\ln(1 + n x)}{n}$ on $[0, 1]$, which converges pointwise to $0$. What is the maximum of $f_n$ on $[0, 1]$?

- $\dfrac{\ln 2}{n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{n + 1}$
- $\dfrac{\ln(1 + n)}{n}$
- $\ln(1 + n)$

## Explanation

The derivative $f_n'(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + n x}$ is positive on $[0, 1]$, so $f_n$ is increasing and attains its maximum at the endpoint $x = 1$. The maximum is therefore $f_n(1) = \dfrac{\ln(1 + n)}{n}$. Since the logarithm grows more slowly than $n$, this value tends to $0$, so by the **supremum criterion** the convergence is uniform on $[0, 1]$.
