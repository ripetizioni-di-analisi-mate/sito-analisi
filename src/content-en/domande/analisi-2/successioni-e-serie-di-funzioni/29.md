What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^1 n x^n \, dx$?

- $0$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

For every $n$ we have
$$
\int_0^1 n x^n \, dx = \frac{n}{n + 1} \xrightarrow[n \to +\infty]{} 1 .
$$
For $0 \leq x < 1$ we have $n x^n \to 0$, because the geometric sequence $x^n$ is an infinitesimal of higher order than $\dfrac{1}{n}$, while at $x = 1$ we have $n \cdot 1^n = n \to +\infty$. The limit of the integrals therefore equals $1$, while the pointwise limit equals $0$ on $[0, 1)$ and has zero integral. Therefore in this case the limit and the integral cannot be interchanged. Indeed $\displaystyle\sup_{[0, 1)} n x^n = n$ for every $n$, so the convergence is not uniform on $[0, 1)$.
