Denoting by $\lfloor t \rfloor$ the integer part of $t$, the sequence $f_n(x) = \dfrac{\lfloor n x \rfloor}{n}$ converges pointwise to $f(x) = x$ on $\mathbb{R}$. What is $\displaystyle\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} |f_n(x) - x|$?

- $\dfrac{1}{n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n}$
- $0$
- $1$

## Explanation

By definition of the integer part we have $\lfloor n x \rfloor \leq n x < \lfloor n x \rfloor + 1$. Dividing by $n$ we get $0 \leq x - f_n(x) < \dfrac{1}{n}$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, so the supremum is at most $\dfrac{1}{n}$. For $x = \dfrac{1 - \delta}{n}$ with $0 < \delta < 1$ we have $f_n(x) = 0$ and $x - f_n(x) = \dfrac{1 - \delta}{n}$, which approaches $\dfrac{1}{n}$ as $\delta \to 0^+$. Therefore the supremum equals $\dfrac{1}{n}$, even though it is not attained. Since $\dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, the convergence is uniform on $\mathbb{R}$, although the $f_n$ are discontinuous.
