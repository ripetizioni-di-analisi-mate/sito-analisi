On which set does the series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n^2}$ converge totally?

- On $\mathbb{R}$
- On every interval $[-a, a]$ with $0 < a < 1$, but not on $[-1, 1]$
- On $[-1, 1)$, but not on $[-1, 1]$
- On $[-1, 1]$

## Explanation

For $|x| \leq 1$ we have $\left| \dfrac{x^n}{n^2} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{n^2} = M_n$, and the generalized harmonic series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges, so the series **converges totally** on $[-1, 1]$, endpoints included. For $|x| > 1$ the term $\dfrac{|x|^n}{n^2}$ tends to $+\infty$, therefore the series does not converge at any point with $|x| > 1$. By the **Weierstrass test** the series also converges uniformly on $[-1, 1]$, and its sum is continuous on $[-1, 1]$.
