In the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^2 x}{n}$ the numerator depends only on $x$. What is the set of convergence?

- For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- For $x = k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$
- For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$
- For no $x \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

For fixed $x$ the numerator $\sin^2 x$ does not depend on $n$, so the series is the harmonic series multiplied by the constant $\sin^2 x$. If $\sin^2 x > 0$ the series diverges, because the harmonic series diverges. If $\sin x = 0$ all the terms are zero and the series converges. Since $\sin x = 0$ if and only if $x = k\pi$, the series converges exactly at the points $x = k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.
