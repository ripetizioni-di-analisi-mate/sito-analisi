The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x}{n^2}$ converges at every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, with sum $\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}\, x$. On which sets does it converge normally?

- On every interval $[-a, a]$ with $a > 0$, but not on $\mathbb{R}$
- On $\mathbb{R}$
- Only at the point $x = 0$
- On no interval $[-a, a]$ with $a > 0$

## Explanation

On $[-a, a]$ we have $\sup \left| \dfrac{x}{n^2} \right| = \dfrac{a}{n^2}$, and the series $\sum \dfrac{a}{n^2}$ converges, so the series **converges normally** on $[-a, a]$. On $\mathbb{R}$ we have $\sup \left| \dfrac{x}{n^2} \right| = +\infty$ for every $n$, because $x$ is unbounded, so no numerical sequence $M_n$ bounds the terms on all of $\mathbb{R}$. Therefore the convergence on $\mathbb{R}$ is pointwise, but not normal.
