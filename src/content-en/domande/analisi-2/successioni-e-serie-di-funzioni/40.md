On $[0, +\infty)$ every value $f_n(x) = \dfrac{x}{n}\, e^{-\frac{x}{n}}$ tends to $0$ as $n \to +\infty$. What is $M_n = \displaystyle\sup_{x \geq 0} f_n(x)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{n e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{n}$
- $0$

## Explanation

With the substitution $t = \dfrac{x}{n}$, as $x$ ranges over $[0, +\infty)$ so does $t$, hence $M_n = \sup_{t \geq 0} t\, e^{-t}$. The derivative of $t\, e^{-t}$ is $(1 - t)\, e^{-t}$, positive for $t < 1$ and negative for $t > 1$, so the maximum equals $e^{-1}$ and is attained at $t = 1$, that is, at $x = n$. Since $M_n = \dfrac{1}{e}$ for every $n$, the sequence $M_n$ does not tend to $0$ and by the **supremum criterion** the convergence **is not uniform** on $[0, +\infty)$.
