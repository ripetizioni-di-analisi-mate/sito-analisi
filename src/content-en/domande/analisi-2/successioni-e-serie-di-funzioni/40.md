For the series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x}{n \left( 1 + n x^2 \right)}$, what is $M_n = \displaystyle\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \left| \frac{x}{n \left( 1 + n x^2 \right)} \right|$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2 n^{\frac{3}{2}}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2 n^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2 \sqrt{n}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{n (n + 1)}$

## Explanation

We study $g_n(x) = \dfrac{x}{1 + n x^2}$, which is odd. For $x \geq 0$ the derivative is $g_n'(x) = \dfrac{1 - n x^2}{\left( 1 + n x^2 \right)^2}$, so $g_n$ has its maximum at $x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$, where it equals $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}$. Therefore
$$
M_n = \frac{1}{n} \cdot \frac{1}{2 \sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{2 n^{\frac{3}{2}}} .
$$
The generalized harmonic series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ converges, because the exponent is greater than $1$, so the series **converges totally** on $\mathbb{R}$ and, by the **Weierstrass test**, also uniformly.
