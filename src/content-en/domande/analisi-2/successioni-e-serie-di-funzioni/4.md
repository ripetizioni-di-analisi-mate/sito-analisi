For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = x^2 e^{-n x}$ on $[0, +\infty)$, which converges pointwise to $f = 0$, what is $M_n = \sup_{x \geq 0} |f_n(x)|$?

- $\dfrac{1}{n^2 e}$
- $\dfrac{2}{n^2 e^2}$
- $\dfrac{4}{n^2 e}$
- $\dfrac{4}{n^2 e^2}$

## Explanation

Since $f_n \geq 0$, the value $M_n$ is the supremum of $f_n$. The derivative is
$$
f_n'(x) = 2x \, e^{-n x} - n x^2 e^{-n x} = x \, e^{-n x} (2 - n x) ,
$$
so for $x > 0$ the sign of $f_n'$ coincides with the sign of $2 - n x$. Therefore $f_n$ is increasing on $\left[ 0, \dfrac{2}{n} \right]$ and decreasing on $\left[ \dfrac{2}{n}, +\infty \right)$, and the maximum is
$$
M_n = f_n\!\left( \frac{2}{n} \right) = \frac{4}{n^2} \, e^{-2} = \frac{4}{n^2 e^2} .
$$
Since $M_n \to 0$, by the **supremum criterion** the convergence is uniform on $[0, +\infty)$.
