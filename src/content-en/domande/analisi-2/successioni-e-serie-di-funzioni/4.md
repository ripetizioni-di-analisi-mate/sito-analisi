As $n \to +\infty$, what does the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = \dfrac{x^n}{n!}$ tend to at a generic point $x \in \mathbb{R}$?

- To $e^x$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- To $0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- To $0$ for $|x| \leq 1$, while for $|x| > 1$ the sequence diverges
- To $0$ for $|x| < 1$ and to $1$ for $x = 1$

## Explanation

For $x = 0$ we have $f_n(0) = 0$ for every $n \geq 1$. For fixed $x \neq 0$, the ratio between the absolute values of two consecutive terms is
$$
\frac{|f_{n+1}(x)|}{|f_n(x)|} = \frac{|x|^{n+1}}{(n + 1)!} \cdot \frac{n!}{|x|^n} = \frac{|x|}{n + 1} ,
$$
which tends to $0$. By the **ratio test** for sequences we therefore have $|f_n(x)| \to 0$, so the pointwise limit is $f = 0$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$. The value $e^x$ is the sum of the series $\sum \dfrac{x^n}{n!}$, whereas here the question is about the limit of the single term.
