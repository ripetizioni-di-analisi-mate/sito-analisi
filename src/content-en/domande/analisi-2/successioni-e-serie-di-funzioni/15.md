Which function is the pointwise limit on $[0, \pi]$ of $f_n(x) = (\sin x)^n$?

- $f(x) = 0$ for every $x \in [0, \pi]$
- $f(x) = 1$ on $(0, \pi)$ and $f(0) = f(\pi) = 0$
- $f\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 1$ and $f(x) = 0$ for every other $x \in [0, \pi]$
- $f(x) = \sin x$ for every $x \in [0, \pi]$

## Explanation

On $[0, \pi]$ we have $0 \leq \sin x \leq 1$, and $\sin x = 1$ only for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. If $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the base $\sin x$ lies in $[0, 1)$, so the geometric sequence $(\sin x)^n$ tends to $0$. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ we have $f_n\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 1$ for every $n$, so the limit is $1$. The pointwise limit therefore equals $1$ at the single point $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $0$ elsewhere, and it is discontinuous, while the $f_n$ are continuous.
