What is the pointwise limit on $\mathbb{R}$ of the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = \dfrac{n x + 1}{n x^2 + 2}$?

- $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = x$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

For $x = 0$ we have $f_n(0) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $n$, so the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$. For $x \neq 0$ we divide the numerator and the denominator by $n$ and obtain
$$
f_n(x) = \frac{x + \frac{1}{n}}{x^2 + \frac{2}{n}} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$ and $x^2 \neq 0$, the numerator tends to $x$ and the denominator tends to $x^2$, therefore $f_n(x) \to \dfrac{x}{x^2} = \dfrac{1}{x}$. Hence the pointwise limit is $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, with $f(0) = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
