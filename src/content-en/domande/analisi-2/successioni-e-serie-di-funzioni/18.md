What is the pointwise limit on $\mathbb{R}$ of the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = \sqrt[n]{1 + x^{2n}}$?

- $f(x) = 1$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- $f(x) = 1$ for $|x| \leq 1$ and $f(x) = x^2$ for $|x| > 1$
- $f(x) = 1$ for $|x| \leq 1$ and $f(x) = |x|$ for $|x| > 1$
- $f(x) = 1$ for $|x| < 1$, $f(\pm 1) = 2$ and $f(x) = x^2$ for $|x| > 1$

## Explanation

For $|x| \leq 1$ we have $1 \leq 1 + x^{2n} \leq 2$, so $1 \leq f_n(x) \leq \sqrt[n]{2}$. Since $\sqrt[n]{2} \to 1$, by the **squeeze theorem** $f_n(x) \to 1$. For $|x| > 1$ we factor out $x^{2n}$ inside the root and obtain
$$
f_n(x) = x^2 \sqrt[n]{1 + \frac{1}{x^{2n}}} .
$$
The last root lies between $1$ and $\sqrt[n]{2}$, so it tends to $1$, therefore $f_n(x) \to x^2$. Hence the pointwise limit equals $1$ for $|x| \leq 1$ and $x^2$ for $|x| > 1$.
