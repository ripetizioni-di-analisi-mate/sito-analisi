For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = x \, e^{-\frac{x}{n}}$ on $[0, 1]$, which statement about convergence is correct?

- Converges pointwise to $f(x) = x$, but not uniformly on $[0, 1]$
- Converges uniformly to $f = 0$ on $[0, 1]$
- Converges uniformly to $f(x) = x$ on $[0, 1]$
- Converges pointwise to $f = 0$, but not uniformly on $[0, 1]$

## Explanation

For every fixed $x \in [0, 1]$ we have $\dfrac{x}{n} \to 0$, so $e^{-\frac{x}{n}} \to 1$ and the pointwise limit is $f(x) = x$. The difference equals $|f_n(x) - x| = x \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{x}{n}} \right)$. On $[0, 1]$ we have $x \leq 1$ and, since $e^{-\frac{x}{n}}$ is decreasing in $x$, also $1 - e^{-\frac{x}{n}} \leq 1 - e^{-\frac{1}{n}}$. Therefore
$$
M_n = \sup_{x \in [0, 1]} |f_n(x) - x| \leq 1 - e^{-\frac{1}{n}} \xrightarrow[n \to +\infty]{} 0 ,
$$
and by the **sup criterion** the convergence is **uniform** on $[0, 1]$.
