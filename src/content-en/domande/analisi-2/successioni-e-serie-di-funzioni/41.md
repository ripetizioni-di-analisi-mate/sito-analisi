The product $x^n \ln x$ tends to $0$ as $n \to +\infty$ at every point $x \in (0, 1]$. What is the supremum of $|x^n \ln x|$ on $(0, 1]$?

- $\dfrac{1}{n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e n}$

## Explanation

On $(0, 1]$ we have $\ln x \leq 0$, so $|f_n(x)| = -x^n \ln x$. The derivative of $g(x) = -x^n \ln x$ is
$$
g'(x) = -n x^{n-1} \ln x - x^{n-1} = -x^{n-1} \left( n \ln x + 1 \right) ,
$$
which is positive for $x < e^{-\frac{1}{n}}$ and negative for $x > e^{-\frac{1}{n}}$. Therefore the maximum is attained at $x = e^{-\frac{1}{n}}$ and equals
$$
g\left(e^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right) = -e^{-1} \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{n}\right) = \frac{1}{e n} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{e n} \to 0$, by the **supremum criterion** the convergence to $0$ is uniform on $(0, 1]$.
