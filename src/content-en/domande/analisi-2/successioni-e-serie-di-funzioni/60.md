The functions $f_n(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2 + \frac{x^n}{n}}$ are continuous on $[0, 1]$. What is the limit as $n \to +\infty$ of their integral over $[0, 1]$?

- $\ln 2$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

For every $x \in [0, 1]$ we have $\dfrac{x^n}{n} \to 0$, so the integrand $f_n$ tends to $\dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$. On $[0, 1]$ we get
$$
\left| f_n(x) - \frac{1}{1 + x^2} \right| = \frac{\frac{x^n}{n}}{\left(1 + x^2\right)\left(1 + x^2 + \frac{x^n}{n}\right)} \leq \frac{1}{n} ,
$$
because both factors of the denominator are greater than or equal to $1$ and $x^n \leq 1$. Therefore the convergence is uniform on $[0, 1]$. The $f_n$ are continuous, so by **passing to the limit under the integral sign** the limit equals
$$
\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1 + x^2} = \arctan 1 = \frac{\pi}{4} .
$$
