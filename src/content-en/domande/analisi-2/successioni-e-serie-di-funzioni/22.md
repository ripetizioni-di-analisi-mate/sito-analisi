Consider $f_n(x) = \dfrac{1}{n} \ln\left(1 + e^{n x}\right)$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$. What is its pointwise limit?

- $f(x) = x$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- $f(x) = 0$ for $x < 0$, $f(0) = \ln 2$ and $f(x) = x$ for $x > 0$
- $f(x) = |x|$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- $f(x) = 0$ for $x \leq 0$ and $f(x) = x$ for $x > 0$

## Explanation

For $x < 0$ we have $e^{n x} \to 0$, so $\ln\left(1 + e^{n x}\right) \to 0$, and $f_n(x)$, which is this logarithm divided by $n$, also tends to $0$. For $x = 0$ we get $f_n(0) = \dfrac{\ln 2}{n} \to 0$. For $x > 0$ we factor $e^{n x}$ out of the argument of the logarithm:
$$
f_n(x) = \frac{1}{n} \Bigl( n x + \ln\left(1 + e^{-n x}\right) \Bigr) = x + \frac{\ln\left(1 + e^{-n x}\right)}{n} ,
$$
and the second term tends to $0$, so $f_n(x) \to x$. The pointwise limit is therefore $f(x) = \max\{x, 0\}$, that is, $0$ for $x \leq 0$ and $x$ for $x > 0$.
