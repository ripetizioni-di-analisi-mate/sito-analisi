For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = \dfrac{n}{n + x}$, which converges pointwise to $f = 1$ on $[0, +\infty)$, on which sets is the convergence uniform?

- On $[0, +\infty)$
- On every interval $[0, a]$ with $a > 0$, but not on $[0, +\infty)$
- On no interval $[0, a]$ with $a > 0$
- On every half-line $[a, +\infty)$ with $a > 0$, but not on $[0, +\infty)$

## Explanation

For every $x \geq 0$ we have
$$
|f_n(x) - 1| = \frac{x}{n + x} ,
$$
and this function is increasing in $x$, because its derivative $\dfrac{n}{(n + x)^2}$ is positive. On $[0, +\infty)$ the supremum is the limit as $x \to +\infty$, which equals $1$ for every $n$, so the convergence **is not uniform** on $[0, +\infty)$. For the same reason the convergence is not uniform on any half-line $[a, +\infty)$. On $[0, a]$ the maximum is attained at $x = a$ and equals $M_n = \dfrac{a}{n + a} \to 0$, therefore the convergence is **uniform** on every interval $[0, a]$.
