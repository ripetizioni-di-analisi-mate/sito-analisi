On which set does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (\ln x)^n$, defined for $x > 0$, converge?

- $(0, e)$
- $\left[\dfrac{1}{e}, e\right]$
- $\left(\dfrac{1}{e}, e\right)$
- $\left(\dfrac{1}{e}, e\right]$

## Explanation

For every $x > 0$ the series is geometric with common ratio $\ln x$, so it converges if and only if $|\ln x| < 1$. The condition is equivalent to $-1 < \ln x < 1$, that is, to $\dfrac{1}{e} < x < e$. At the endpoints the ratio equals $-1$ or $1$ and the general term does not tend to $0$, so the series does not converge. The set of convergence is therefore $\left(\dfrac{1}{e}, e\right)$.
