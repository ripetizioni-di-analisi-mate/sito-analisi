- The series converges normally on $[0, +\infty)$
- The series converges normally only on every half-line $[\delta, +\infty)$ with $\delta > 0$
- The series converges pointwise on $[0, +\infty)$, but not uniformly
- The series converges normally only on every interval $[0, a]$ with $a > 0$

## Explanation

The terms $f_n(x) = x^2 e^{-n x}$ are nonnegative. The derivative $f_n'(x) = x \, e^{-n x} (2 - n x)$ is positive on $\left( 0, \dfrac{2}{n} \right)$ and negative on $\left( \dfrac{2}{n}, +\infty \right)$, so
$$
\sup_{[0, +\infty)} |f_n| = f_n\!\left( \frac{2}{n} \right) = \frac{4}{e^2 n^2} .
$$
The series $\sum \dfrac{4}{e^2 n^2}$ converges, because it is a multiple of the p-series with exponent $2$. Therefore the series **converges normally** on all of $[0, +\infty)$, origin included, and hence also uniformly.
