For the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = x^{\frac{1}{n}}$ on $[0, 1]$, which statement is correct?

- The pointwise limit equals $1$ on all of $[0, 1]$ and the convergence is uniform
- The pointwise limit equals $0$ at $x = 0$ and $1$ on $(0, 1]$, and the convergence is uniform
- The pointwise limit equals $0$ at $x = 0$ and $1$ on $(0, 1]$, and the convergence is not uniform
- The pointwise limit equals $0$ on $[0, 1)$ and $1$ at $x = 1$, and the convergence is not uniform

## Explanation

For $x = 0$ we have $f_n(0) = 0$ for every $n$, so the limit equals $0$. For $0 < x \leq 1$ we have $x^{\frac{1}{n}} = e^{\frac{\ln x}{n}}$. Since $\dfrac{\ln x}{n} \to 0$ and the exponential function is continuous, $x^{\frac{1}{n}} \to e^0 = 1$. The pointwise limit therefore equals $0$ at $x = 0$ and $1$ on $(0, 1]$, so it is discontinuous at $0$. Since the $f_n$ are continuous on $[0, 1]$, if the convergence were uniform the limit would be continuous by the theorem on the **continuity of the uniform limit**. Therefore the convergence **is not uniform** on $[0, 1]$.
