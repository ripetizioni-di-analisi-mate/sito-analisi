Consider $f_n(x) = \dfrac{2 n x + 3}{n + x^2}$ with $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Which function $f$ is its pointwise limit?

- $f(x) = \dfrac{2}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = 2x$
- $f(x) = 2x$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 3$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{3}{x^2}$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 3$

## Explanation

For fixed $x$, the quantities $x$ and $x^2$ are constants and the variable is $n$. Dividing numerator and denominator by $n$ we get
$$
f_n(x) = \frac{2x + \frac{3}{n}}{1 + \frac{x^2}{n}} ,
$$
and since $\dfrac{3}{n} \to 0$ and $\dfrac{x^2}{n} \to 0$ the limit is $2x$. The computation also holds for $x = 0$, where $f_n(0) = \dfrac{3}{n} \to 0$. Therefore the pointwise limit is $f(x) = 2x$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$.
