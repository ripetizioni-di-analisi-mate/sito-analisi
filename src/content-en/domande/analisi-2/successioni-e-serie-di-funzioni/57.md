Using the uniform convergence of the integrand on $[0, 2]$, what is the value of $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^2 \frac{n x^2 + x}{n}\, dx$?

- $\dfrac{14}{3}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

We have $f_n(x) = x^2 + \dfrac{x}{n}$, so $|f_n(x) - x^2| = \dfrac{x}{n} \leq \dfrac{2}{n}$ on $[0, 2]$ and the convergence to $x^2$ is uniform. The $f_n$ are continuous, so by **passing to the limit under the integral sign**
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \int_0^2 f_n(x)\,dx = \int_0^2 x^2\,dx = \frac{8}{3} .
$$
