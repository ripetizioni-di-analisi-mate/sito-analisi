The general term $\dfrac{n}{n^2 + x^2}$ tends to $0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Where does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{n^2 + x^2}$ converge?

- At every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- At no point
- Only at $x = 0$
- For $|x| > 1$

## Explanation

For fixed $x$ the terms are positive and
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{\frac{n}{n^2 + x^2}}{\frac{1}{n}} = \lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^2}{n^2 + x^2} = 1 .
$$
By the **limit comparison test** the series therefore behaves like the harmonic series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n}$, which diverges. The computation holds for every $x$, including $x = 0$, where the series is exactly $\sum \dfrac{1}{n}$. Therefore the series does not converge at any point of $\mathbb{R}$.
