We look for an index $N$ that works for all points at once. What is the smallest $N$ such that $\dfrac{1}{n + x^2} < \dfrac{1}{100}$ for every $n \geq N$ and every $x \in \mathbb{R}$?

- $N = 11$
- $N = 101$
- $N = 1$
- $N = 201$

## Explanation

For every $n$ the maximum value of $\dfrac{1}{n + x^2}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ occurs at $x = 0$ and equals $\dfrac{1}{n}$. The inequality therefore holds for every $x$ if and only if $\dfrac{1}{n} < \dfrac{1}{100}$, that is, $n > 100$. Therefore the smallest index is $N = 101$, because for $n = 100$ at $x = 0$ we get equality. The same $N$ works for all points of $\mathbb{R}$, as required by the definition of **uniform convergence** of $\dfrac{1}{n + x^2}$ to $0$.
