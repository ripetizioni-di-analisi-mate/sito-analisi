For the series of functions $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n (1 - x)$ on $[0, 1]$, which statement is correct?

- The sum equals $1$ on $[0, 1]$ and the convergence is uniform on $[0, 1]$
- The sum equals $1$ on $[0, 1)$ and $0$ at $x = 1$, and the convergence is not uniform on $[0, 1]$
- The sum equals $1$ on $[0, 1)$ and $0$ at $x = 1$, and the convergence is uniform on $[0, 1]$
- The sum equals $\dfrac{1}{1 - x}$ on $[0, 1)$ and the convergence is not uniform on $[0, 1]$

## Explanation

By the finite geometric sum, for every $x \in [0, 1]$ the partial sum is
$$
s_N(x) = (1 - x) \sum_{n=0}^{N} x^n = 1 - x^{N+1} .
$$
For $0 \leq x < 1$ we have $x^{N+1} \to 0$, so the sum equals $1$; for $x = 1$ every term is zero, so the sum equals $0$. The sum is therefore discontinuous at $x = 1$, while the terms are continuous. If the convergence were uniform, by the theorem on the **continuity of the sum** the sum would be continuous, so the convergence **is not uniform** on $[0, 1]$.
