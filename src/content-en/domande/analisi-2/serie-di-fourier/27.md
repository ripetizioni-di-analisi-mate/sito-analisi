The sine-only series $\displaystyle\frac{8}{\pi} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin\bigl( (2k+1)x \bigr)}{(2k+1)^3}$ is the Fourier series of the odd $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x(\pi - x)$, $x \in [0, \pi]$. What is the sum of the numerical series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^3}$?

- $\dfrac{\pi^3}{16}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^3}{32}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^3}{8}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{32}$

## Explanation

The odd extension equals $0$ at $x = 0$ and at $x = \pm\pi$, so it is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$; moreover it is piecewise smooth, because on every interval $\bigl( k\pi, (k+1)\pi \bigr)$ it coincides with a polynomial of degree two. By the **pointwise convergence theorem** the series converges to $f(x)$ at every point, in particular at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, where $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = \dfrac{\pi^2}{4}$. Since $\sin\left( (2k+1)\dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = (-1)^k$, we obtain
$$
\frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{8}{\pi} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^3} .
$$
Therefore the sum equals $\dfrac{\pi}{8} \cdot \dfrac{\pi^2}{4} = \dfrac{\pi^3}{32}$.
