Let $f$ be the $2$-periodic extension of $f(x) = |x|$, $x \in [-1, 1]$. Does the Fourier series of $f$ converge uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$?

- No, because $|x|$ is not differentiable at $x = 0$
- No, because the extension has a jump at the points $x = \pm 1$
- Yes, because the extension is continuous and piecewise smooth
- Only on closed intervals that do not contain the points $x = k$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The extension has period $2L = 2$ and the values at the endpoints coincide, since $f(-1) = f(1) = 1$; so $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. Moreover $f$ is piecewise smooth, since on every interval $(k, k+1)$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, it coincides with a polynomial of degree one, and at the points $x = k$ the one-sided limits of $f'$ equal $1$ and $-1$. The hypotheses of the **uniform convergence theorem** are therefore satisfied, and the series **converges uniformly** to $f$ on $\mathbb{R}$. In agreement with the theorem, the coefficients $a_n = \dfrac{2 \bigl( (-1)^n - 1 \bigr)}{n^2 \pi^2}$ satisfy $|a_n| \leq \dfrac{4}{\pi^2 n^2}$, and since the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges, by the **Weierstrass M-test** the Fourier series also converges totally.
