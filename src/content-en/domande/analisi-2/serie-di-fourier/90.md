The $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x$, $x \in [0, 2\pi)$, has Fourier coefficients $a_0 = 2\pi$, $a_n = 0$ and $b_n = -\dfrac{2}{n}$ for $n \geq 1$. Which value of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ follows from Parseval's identity?

- $\dfrac{2\pi^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi^2}{12}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{3}$

## Explanation

The function is bounded, so it is square integrable, and the integral over one period can be computed on $[0, 2\pi]$. The left-hand side of **Parseval's identity** equals $\dfrac{1}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} x^2\, dx = \frac{8\pi^2}{3}$, and the right-hand side equals $\dfrac{a_0^2}{2} + \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n^2 = 2\pi^2 + 4 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$. Hence $4 \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{8\pi^2}{3} - 2\pi^2 = \frac{2\pi^2}{3}$, therefore $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$. The value $\dfrac{2\pi^2}{3}$ is obtained by neglecting the term $\dfrac{a_0^2}{2}$.
