To expand $f(x) = \cos x$, $x \in (0, \pi)$, in a sine-only series, one takes the odd extension of $f$ to $(-\pi, \pi)$ and then its $2\pi$-periodic extension. What is the coefficient $b_2$ of this expansion?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{4}{3\pi}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3\pi}$
- $-\dfrac{8}{3\pi}$

## Explanation

The extension is **odd**, so $a_n = 0$ and $b_n = \dfrac{2}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \cos x \sin(nx)\, dx$. For $n = 2$ the product-to-sum formula gives $\cos x \sin(2x) = \dfrac{\sin(3x) + \sin x}{2}$, therefore
$$
b_2 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \bigl( \sin(3x) + \sin x \bigr)\, dx = \frac{1}{\pi} \left( \frac{2}{3} + 2 \right) = \frac{8}{3\pi} .
$$
The odd extension coincides with $\cos x$ only on $(0, \pi)$ and equals $-\cos(-x)$, that is $-\cos x$, on $(-\pi, 0)$. For this reason the orthogonality relation between $\cos x$ and $\sin(2x)$ on $[-\pi, \pi]$ does not apply and the coefficient is nonzero.
