For the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x^2$, $x \in [0, 2\pi)$, what is the value of the Fourier coefficient $b_1$?

- $-4\pi$
- $0$
- $4\pi$
- $-2\pi$

## Explanation

The extension is not an even function, because for example $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = \dfrac{\pi^2}{4}$, while $f\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = f\left( \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right) = \dfrac{9\pi^2}{4}$; so the proposition on cosine-only series does not apply. Since the integral over one period does not depend on the starting point, we can integrate over $[0, 2\pi]$, and
$$
b_1 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} x^2 \sin x\, dx .
$$
Integrating by parts gives
$$
\int_0^{2\pi} x^2 \sin x\, dx = \bigl[ -x^2 \cos x \bigr]_0^{2\pi} + 2 \int_0^{2\pi} x \cos x\, dx = -4\pi^2 + 0 ,
$$
since $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} x \cos x\, dx = \bigl[ x \sin x + \cos x \bigr]_0^{2\pi} = 0$. Therefore $b_1 = -4\pi$.
