Let $f$ be a $2\pi$-periodic function, continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and piecewise smooth, with Fourier coefficients $a_n$, $b_n$. Which of the following series converges for every function $f$ of this kind?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \bigl( |a_n| + |b_n| \bigr)$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sqrt{|a_n| + |b_n|}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^2 \bigl( |a_n| + |b_n| \bigr)$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigl( |a_n| + |b_n| \bigr)$

## Explanation

By the **uniform convergence theorem** the series $\displaystyle\sum \bigl( |a_n| + |b_n| \bigr)$ converges for every $2\pi$-periodic continuous piecewise smooth function, and the normal convergence of the Fourier series follows from this. The other series may diverge. For the extension of $|x|$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi]$, we have $b_n = 0$, $a_n = 0$ for even $n$ and $|a_n| = \dfrac{4}{\pi n^2}$ for odd $n$. Hence $n |a_n| = \dfrac{4}{\pi n}$ and $\sqrt{|a_n|} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}\, n}$ for odd $n$, and the corresponding series diverge like the series of the reciprocals of the odd numbers; a fortiori the series with the factor $n^2$ diverges.
