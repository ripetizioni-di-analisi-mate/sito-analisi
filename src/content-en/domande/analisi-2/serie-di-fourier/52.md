For a $2\pi$-periodic square-integrable function $f$ only the coefficient $a_0 = 0$ and the integral $\dfrac{1}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)^2\, dx = 2$ are known. Which of the following values is impossible for the Fourier coefficient $b_1$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $-1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

By **Parseval's identity** we have $2 = \dfrac{a_0^2}{2} + \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigl( a_n^2 + b_n^2 \bigr)$, and since $a_0 = 0$ the sum of the squares of the coefficients equals $2$. Every term is nonnegative, so $b_1^2 \leq 2$, that is $|b_1| \leq \sqrt{2}$. Since $\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^2 = \dfrac{9}{4} > 2$, the value $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is impossible. The other three values have square less than $2$ and are possible; for example $f(x) = \sin x + \sin(2x)$ satisfies the conditions and has $b_1 = 1$.
