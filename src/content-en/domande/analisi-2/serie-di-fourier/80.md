Can the sequence $b_n = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$, together with $a_n = 0$ for every $n$, be the sequence of Fourier coefficients of a $2\pi$-periodic function that is square integrable on $[-\pi, \pi]$?

- Yes, because $b_n \to 0$
- Yes, because the series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ converges
- No, because $\displaystyle\sum b_n^2 = \sum \frac{1}{n}$ diverges
- No, because Fourier coefficients are always bounded by $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$

## Explanation

If $f$ is square integrable, by **Parseval's identity** the series $\displaystyle\sum \bigl( a_n^2 + b_n^2 \bigr)$ converges and has sum $\dfrac{1}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)^2\, dx - \frac{a_0^2}{2}$. With $b_n = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ we have $b_n^2 = \dfrac{1}{n}$, and the harmonic series diverges. Therefore $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ **cannot** be the sequence of coefficients of a square-integrable function. The condition $b_n \to 0$ is necessary but not sufficient, and the bound by $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$ is false in general, because the sawtooth wave has $|b_n| = \dfrac{2}{n}$.
