Which of the following integrals is different from zero?

- $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \sin(2x) \cos(2x)\, dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos(2x) \cos(3x)\, dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \sin^2(3x)\, dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \sin x \cos(4x)\, dx$

## Explanation

By the **orthogonality of the trigonometric system** the integral over $[-\pi, \pi]$ of the product of two different functions of the system $1, \cos x, \sin x, \cos(2x), \sin(2x), \dots$ is zero. The pairs $\sin(2x)$ and $\cos(2x)$, $\cos(2x)$ and $\cos(3x)$, $\sin x$ and $\cos(4x)$ consist of different functions of the system, so the three corresponding integrals are zero. The integral of $\sin^2(3x)$, instead, is the integral of the product of $\sin(3x)$ with itself, and for $n = m = 3$ the orthogonality relation gives $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \sin^2(3x)\, dx = \pi$. Therefore the only integral different from zero is the one of $\sin^2(3x)$.
