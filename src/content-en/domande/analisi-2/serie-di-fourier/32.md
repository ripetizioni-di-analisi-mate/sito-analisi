Let $f$ be the $2\pi$-periodic function such that $f(x) = x^2$ for $-\pi < x < \pi$ and $f(\pi) = 0$. What is the sum of the Fourier series of $f$ at $x = \pi$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{2}$
- The series does not converge at $x = \pi$
- $\pi^2$

## Explanation

The function is piecewise smooth, because on $(-\pi, \pi)$ it coincides with $x^2$ and at the endpoints the one-sided limits of $f$ and of $f'$ are finite; so the **pointwise convergence theorem** applies. The left limit at $x = \pi$ is $\pi^2$, and by periodicity the right limit coincides with $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\pi^+} x^2 = \pi^2$. Therefore the sum of the series at $\pi$ is $\dfrac{\pi^2 + \pi^2}{2} = \pi^2$. The value $f(\pi) = 0$ does not change the coefficients, which are integrals, and hence it does not change the series.
