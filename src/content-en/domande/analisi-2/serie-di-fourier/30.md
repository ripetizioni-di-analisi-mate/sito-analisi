Which of the following functions, defined on $[-\pi, \pi)$ and extended $2\pi$-periodically, has a cosine-only Fourier series?

- $x^3 \cos x$
- $x + \cos x$
- $x \sin(2x)$
- $\cos x + \sin(3x)$

## Explanation

For $x \in (-\pi, \pi)$ we have $(-x) \sin(-2x) = x \sin(2x)$, so $x \sin(2x)$ is **even**, and the value at $x = -\pi$ does not change the integrals. By the proposition on cosine-only series, therefore, $b_n = 0$ for every $n \geq 1$. The function $x^3 \cos x$ is odd, therefore its series is a sine-only series. The functions $x + \cos x$ and $\cos x + \sin(3x)$ have coefficients $b_n$ that are not all zero, because the sawtooth $x$ has $b_1 = 2$ and the trigonometric polynomial $\sin(3x)$ has $b_3 = 1$, while $\cos x$ contributes only to the cosines.
