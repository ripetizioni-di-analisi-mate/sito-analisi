Which statement about the trigonometric series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(nx)}{2^n}$ is true?

- It converges only for $x \neq 2k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$
- It converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$ and its sum is continuous
- It converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$, but its sum has a jump at $x = 0$
- It converges at every point, but not uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ we have $\left| \dfrac{\cos(nx)}{2^n} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{2^n}$, and the geometric series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^n}$ converges. Hence by the **Weierstrass M-test** the series converges normally, and therefore uniformly, on $\mathbb{R}$. Every term is a continuous function, and a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, therefore the sum is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and has no jumps. In particular at $x = 0$ the series converges to $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^n} = 1$.
