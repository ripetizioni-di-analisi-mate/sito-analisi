On the interval $[0, \pi]$, which is half a period, what is $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \sin(2x) \cos(3x)\, dx$?

- $-\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{2}{5}$

## Explanation

The integral of $\sin(nx) \cos(mx)$ is zero over the symmetric interval $[-\pi, \pi]$, because the product is odd; on $[0, \pi]$ this argument does not apply. By the product-to-sum formula $\sin(2x) \cos(3x) = \dfrac{\sin(5x) - \sin x}{2}$, hence
$$
\int_0^{\pi} \sin(2x) \cos(3x)\, dx = \frac{1}{2} \left[ -\frac{\cos(5x)}{5} + \cos x \right]_0^{\pi} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{5} - 1 + \frac{1}{5} - 1 \right) = -\frac{4}{5} .
$$
