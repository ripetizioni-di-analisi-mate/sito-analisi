The $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi)$, has a Fourier series that does not converge uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$. Which hypothesis of the uniform convergence theorem is not satisfied?

- Piecewise smoothness
- Continuity on $\mathbb{R}$
- $2\pi$-periodicity
- Integrability on $[-\pi, \pi]$

## Explanation

The uniform convergence theorem requires the function to be $2\pi$-periodic, continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and piecewise smooth. The extension is $2\pi$-periodic by construction and is piecewise smooth, because on $(-\pi, \pi)$ it coincides with $x$. At $x = \pi$ the left limit is $\pi$ and the right limit is $-\pi$, so the extension has a jump and the **continuity** hypothesis is not satisfied. Integrability on $[-\pi, \pi]$ holds, because the function is bounded and has finitely many discontinuities.
