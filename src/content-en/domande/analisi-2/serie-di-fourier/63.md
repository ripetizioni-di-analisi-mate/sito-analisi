In the trigonometric system of period $2$, made of $1$, $\cos(\pi x)$, $\sin(\pi x)$, $\cos(2\pi x)$, $\sin(2\pi x)$, $\dots$, what is $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} \cos^2(3\pi x)\, dx$?

- $\pi$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

With the substitution $t = \pi x$ the interval $[-1, 1]$ becomes $[-\pi, \pi]$ and $dx = \dfrac{dt}{\pi}$, so the integral equals $\dfrac{1}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos^2(3t)\, dt$. By the **orthogonality of the trigonometric system** with $n = m = 3$ the last integral equals $\pi$. Therefore the required integral equals $\dfrac{\pi}{\pi} = 1$.
