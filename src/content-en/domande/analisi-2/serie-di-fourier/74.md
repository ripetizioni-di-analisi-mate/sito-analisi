For which $\alpha > 0$ does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(nx)}{n^{\alpha}}$ converge normally on $\mathbb{R}$?

- $\alpha > 0$
- $\alpha > 1$
- $\alpha \geq 1$
- $\alpha > \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

For every $n$ we have $\displaystyle\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \left| \frac{\sin(nx)}{n^{\alpha}} \right| = \frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}$, because $\sin(nx)$ equals $1$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2n}$. Hence the series converges normally if and only if the generalized harmonic series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}$ converges, that is if and only if $\alpha > 1$. For these values the **Weierstrass M-test** also guarantees uniform convergence. For $\alpha = 1$ the sum of the series is discontinuous at $x = 0$, so the convergence is not even uniform.
