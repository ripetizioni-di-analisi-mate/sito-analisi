The product of $\sin\left( \dfrac{x}{2} \right)$ and $\sin x$ is integrated over $[-\pi, \pi]$. What value is obtained?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{8}{3}$

## Explanation

The function $\sin\left( \dfrac{x}{2} \right)$ has frequency $\dfrac{1}{2}$, which is not an integer, so it does not belong to the system $1, \cos x, \sin x, \cos(2x), \sin(2x), \dots$ and the **orthogonality of the trigonometric system** does not apply. By the product-to-sum formula
$$
\sin\left( \frac{x}{2} \right) \sin x = \frac{1}{2} \left( \cos\frac{x}{2} - \cos\frac{3x}{2} \right) .
$$
Since $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos\frac{x}{2}\, dx = \left[ 2 \sin\frac{x}{2} \right]_{-\pi}^{\pi} = 4$ and $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos\frac{3x}{2}\, dx = \left[ \frac{2}{3} \sin\frac{3x}{2} \right]_{-\pi}^{\pi} = -\frac{4}{3}$, the integral equals $\dfrac{1}{2} \left( 4 + \dfrac{4}{3} \right) = \dfrac{8}{3}$.
