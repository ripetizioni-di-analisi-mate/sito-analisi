Let $f$ be the $2\pi$-periodic extension of
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } -\pi \leq x < 0 \\
1 & \text{if } 0 \leq x < \pi
\end{cases}
$$
Which statement about the Fourier series of $f$ at the point $x = 0$ is true?

- It converges to $1$, the value $f(0)$
- It converges to $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- It converges to $0$
- It does not converge, because $f$ is not continuous at $x = 0$

## Explanation

The function is constant on $(-\pi, 0)$ and on $(0, \pi)$, so it is piecewise smooth and the **pointwise convergence theorem** applies. At $x = 0$ we have $f(0^-) = 0$ and $f(0^+) = 1$, therefore the series **converges** to the mean $\dfrac{0 + 1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. At jump points the sum is the mean of the one-sided limits whatever value is assigned to $f$ at the point, so the value $f(0) = 1$ plays no role.
