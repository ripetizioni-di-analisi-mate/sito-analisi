For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, we have
$$
|\sin x| = \frac{2}{\pi} - \frac{4}{\pi} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(2nx)}{4n^2 - 1} ,
$$
since $|\sin x|$ is continuous and piecewise smooth. What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{4n^2 - 1}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $1$

## Explanation

At $x = 0$ the left-hand side equals $|\sin 0| = 0$ and every cosine equals $1$. Hence
$$
0 = \frac{2}{\pi} - \frac{4}{\pi} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{4n^2 - 1} ,
$$
therefore $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{4n^2 - 1} = \frac{2}{\pi} \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$. The same value follows from the decomposition $\dfrac{1}{4n^2 - 1} = \dfrac{1}{2} \left( \dfrac{1}{2n - 1} - \dfrac{1}{2n + 1} \right)$, which makes the series telescoping.
