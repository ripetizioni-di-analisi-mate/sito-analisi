What is the value of the integral $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \bigl( 1 + 2 \sin x - 3 \cos(2x) \bigr)^2\, dx$?

- $14\pi$
- $17\pi$
- $30\pi$
- $15\pi$

## Explanation

The function $f(x) = 1 + 2 \sin x - 3 \cos(2x)$ is a trigonometric polynomial with coefficients $a_0 = 2$, $b_1 = 2$, $a_2 = -3$ and all the others zero. By **Parseval's identity**
$$
\frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)^2\, dx = \frac{a_0^2}{2} + b_1^2 + a_2^2 = 2 + 4 + 9 = 15 .
$$
Therefore the integral equals $15\pi$. The same result is obtained by expanding the square, because by orthogonality the mixed products have zero integral and what remains are the integrals of the three squares, which equal $2\pi$, $4\pi$ and $9\pi$.
