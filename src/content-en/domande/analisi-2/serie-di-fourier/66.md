For every $N \geq 1$ consider the partial sum $s_N(x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n} \sin(nx)$ of the Fourier series of the sawtooth wave, that is of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi)$. What is $s_N(\pi)$?

- $0$
- $\pi$
- $-\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

Every term contains $\sin(n\pi) = 0$, so $s_N(\pi) = 0$ for every $N$. The result is consistent with the **pointwise convergence theorem**, because at $x = \pi$ the extension has left limit $\pi$ and right limit $-\pi$, and their mean is $0$. The value $\pi$ is the left limit of $f$ at $\pi$, which the partial sums at that point do not reach.
