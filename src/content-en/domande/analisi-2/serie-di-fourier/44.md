The $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = \cos\left( \dfrac{x}{2} \right)$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi]$, has Fourier coefficients $a_0 = \dfrac{4}{\pi}$, $a_n = \dfrac{4\,(-1)^{n+1}}{\pi (4n^2 - 1)}$ for $n \geq 1$ and $b_n = 0$. What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{4n^2 - 1}$?

- $\dfrac{\pi - 4}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi - 2}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi - 2}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The extension is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, because $f(-\pi) = f(\pi) = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$, and it is piecewise smooth; hence by the **pointwise convergence theorem** the series converges to $f(x)$ at every point. At $x = 0$ we have $f(0) = 1$, every cosine equals $1$ and the constant term is $\dfrac{a_0}{2} = \dfrac{2}{\pi}$, therefore
$$
1 = \frac{2}{\pi} + \frac{4}{\pi} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{4n^2 - 1} .
$$
Solving for the series we obtain $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{4n^2 - 1} = \frac{\pi}{4} \left( 1 - \frac{2}{\pi} \right) = \frac{\pi - 2}{4}$.
