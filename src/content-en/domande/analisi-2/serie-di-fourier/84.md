A $2\pi$-periodic integrable function $f$ satisfies $f(x + \pi) = -f(x)$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Which statement about its Fourier coefficients holds for every function of this kind?

- $a_n = b_n = 0$ for every odd $n$
- $a_n = 0$ for every $n \geq 0$
- $b_n = 0$ for every $n \geq 1$
- $a_n = b_n = 0$ for every even $n$

## Explanation

We split the integral defining $a_n$ over $[-\pi, 0]$ and $[0, \pi]$, and in the first one we set $x = t - \pi$. Since $f(t - \pi) = -f(t)$ and $\cos(nt - n\pi) = (-1)^n \cos(nt)$, we obtain
$$
\int_{-\pi}^{0} f(x) \cos(nx)\, dx = \int_{0}^{\pi} \bigl( -f(t) \bigr) (-1)^n \cos(nt)\, dt ,
$$
so $a_n = \dfrac{1 - (-1)^n}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} f(t) \cos(nt)\, dt$. In the same way $b_n = \dfrac{1 - (-1)^n}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} f(t) \sin(nt)\, dt$. For even $n$ the factor $1 - (-1)^n$ is zero, therefore $a_n = b_n = 0$ for every even $n$, including $a_0$. The other statements are false in general: $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ satisfy the condition, and the first has $b_1 = 1$, the second $a_1 = 1$.
