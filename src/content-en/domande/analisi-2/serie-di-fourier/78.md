For which value of $c$ are the functions $\cos(2x) + c$ and $\cos^2 x$ orthogonal on $[-\pi, \pi]$, that is $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \bigl( \cos(2x) + c \bigr) \cos^2 x\, dx = 0$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$
- $0$

## Explanation

Since $\cos^2 x = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\cos(2x)}{2}$, the integrand is $\dfrac{\cos(2x)}{2} + \dfrac{\cos^2(2x)}{2} + \dfrac{c}{2} + \dfrac{c \cos(2x)}{2}$. By the **orthogonality of the trigonometric system** the integrals of $\cos(2x)$ over $[-\pi, \pi]$ are zero and the integral of $\cos^2(2x)$ equals $\pi$, so the integral equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + c\pi$. Therefore the integral is zero for $c = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
