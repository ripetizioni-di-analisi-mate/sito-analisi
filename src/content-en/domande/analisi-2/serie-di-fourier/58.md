The absolute value $|x|$, considered on $[-\pi, \pi]$ and repeated with period $2\pi$, has a corner point at $x = 0$. To which value does its Fourier series converge at that point?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$
- $0$
- The series does not converge, because $f$ is not differentiable at $x = 0$

## Explanation

The extension is piecewise smooth, because on $(-\pi, 0)$ and on $(0, \pi)$ it coincides with a polynomial of degree one and the one-sided limits of $f'$ exist and are finite. Hence the **pointwise convergence theorem** applies. The function is continuous at $x = 0$, where both one-sided limits equal $0$, therefore the series converges to $f(0) = 0$. The theorem requires piecewise smoothness and not differentiability at every point, so the corner point does not prevent convergence; the value $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is the constant term $\dfrac{a_0}{2}$ of the series.
