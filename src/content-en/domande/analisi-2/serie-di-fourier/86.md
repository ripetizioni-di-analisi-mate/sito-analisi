An even trigonometric polynomial of degree $2$, $p(x) = \dfrac{a_0}{2} + a_1 \cos x + a_2 \cos(2x)$, satisfies $p(0) = 3$, $p(\pi) = 1$ and $p\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = 0$. What is $a_2$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $0$
- $-1$

## Explanation

At the points $x = 0$, $x = \pi$ and $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ we have respectively $\cos x = 1, -1, 0$ and $\cos(2x) = 1, 1, -1$, so the three conditions form the system
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{a_0}{2} + a_1 + a_2 = 3 \\[6pt]
\dfrac{a_0}{2} - a_1 + a_2 = 1 \\[6pt]
\dfrac{a_0}{2} - a_2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting the second equation from the first gives $a_1 = 1$, and hence $\dfrac{a_0}{2} + a_2 = 2$. From the third equation $\dfrac{a_0}{2} = a_2$, therefore $a_2 = 1$ and $a_0 = 2$.
