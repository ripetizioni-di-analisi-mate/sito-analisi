Two $2\pi$-periodic square-integrable functions $f$ and $g$ have the same Fourier coefficients $a_n$ and $b_n$ for every $n \geq 1$, while their coefficients $a_0$ satisfy $a_0(f) - a_0(g) = 2$. What is $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \bigl( f(x) - g(x) \bigr)^2\, dx$?

- $4\pi$
- $2\pi$
- $\pi$
- $4$

## Explanation

The Fourier coefficients depend linearly on the function, so $h = f - g$ has $a_0 = 2$ and all other coefficients equal to zero. Since $(f - g)^2 \leq 2f^2 + 2g^2$, the function $h$ is square-integrable too, and by **Parseval's identity**
$$
\frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} h(x)^2\, dx = \frac{a_0^2}{2} = \frac{4}{2} = 2 .
$$
Therefore the integral equals $2\pi$. The value $4\pi$ would be obtained by using $a_0^2$ instead of $\dfrac{a_0^2}{2}$.
