For which of the following functions, defined for $0 < |x| \leq \pi$ and extended $2\pi$-periodically, can Parseval's identity *not* be applied?

- $|x|^{-\frac{1}{4}}$
- $x^2$
- $e^x$
- $|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}$

## Explanation

Parseval's identity requires $f$ to be square integrable on $[-\pi, \pi]$. For $f(x) = |x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ we have $f(x)^2 = \dfrac{1}{|x|}$, and the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \frac{dx}{x}$ diverges, so the hypothesis **is not satisfied**. The function $|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ is nevertheless integrable, therefore its Fourier coefficients exist. For $|x|^{-\frac{1}{4}}$ the square is $|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, whose improper integral over $[-\pi, \pi]$ converges, while $x^2$ and $e^x$ are bounded; in these three cases the hypothesis is satisfied.
