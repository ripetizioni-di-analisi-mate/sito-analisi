The exponential function $e^{-|x|}$, considered on $[-\pi, \pi]$ and repeated with period $2\pi$, has the term $a_1 \cos x$ in its Fourier series. What is $a_1$?

- $\dfrac{2 \bigl( 1 + e^{-\pi} \bigr)}{\pi}$
- $\dfrac{1 + e^{-\pi}}{2\pi}$
- $-\dfrac{1 + e^{-\pi}}{\pi}$
- $\dfrac{1 + e^{-\pi}}{\pi}$

## Explanation

The function is **even**, so $a_1 = \dfrac{2}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} e^{-x} \cos x\, dx$, because $|x| = x$ on $[0, \pi]$. Integrating by parts twice gives the antiderivative $\dfrac{e^{-x} (\sin x - \cos x)}{2}$. Since $\sin 0 = \sin\pi = 0$, $\cos 0 = 1$ and $\cos\pi = -1$, we have
$$
\int_0^{\pi} e^{-x} \cos x\, dx = \left[ \frac{e^{-x} (\sin x - \cos x)}{2} \right]_0^{\pi} = \frac{e^{-\pi}}{2} + \frac{1}{2} .
$$
Therefore $a_1 = \dfrac{2}{\pi} \cdot \dfrac{1 + e^{-\pi}}{2} = \dfrac{1 + e^{-\pi}}{\pi}$.
