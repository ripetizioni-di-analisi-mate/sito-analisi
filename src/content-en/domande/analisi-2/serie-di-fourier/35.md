The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(nx)}{n}$ converges at every point to the $2\pi$-periodic function $g$ with $g(0) = 0$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{\pi - x}{2}$ for $0 < x < 2\pi$. Which statement about the convergence of the series on $\mathbb{R}$ is true?

- It is uniform by the Weierstrass M-test
- It is not uniform, because $g$ is discontinuous at $x = 0$
- It is uniform, because the series converges at every point
- It is not uniform, because the series does not converge at $x = 0$

## Explanation

At $x = 0$ the right limit of $g$ is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and, by periodicity, the left limit is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2\pi^-} \frac{\pi - x}{2} = -\frac{\pi}{2}$, so $g$ has a jump at $0$. The partial sums of the series are trigonometric polynomials, that is continuous functions. A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, therefore the convergence on $\mathbb{R}$ is **not uniform**. The Weierstrass M-test does not apply, because the series of bounds $\displaystyle\sum \frac{1}{n}$ diverges; at $x = 0$ the series converges, because every term is zero.
