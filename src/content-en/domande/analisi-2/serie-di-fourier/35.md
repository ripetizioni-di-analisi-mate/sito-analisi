For which of the following functions, defined on $[-\pi, \pi)$ and extended $2\pi$-periodically, does the Fourier series converge uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$?

- $x$
- $x^3 - \pi^2 x$
- $e^x$
- $x^2 + x$

## Explanation

The four functions have continuous derivative on $(-\pi, \pi)$ with finite one-sided limits, so their extensions are piecewise smooth, and it remains to check continuity at the junction points $\pm\pi$. For $x^3 - \pi^2 x$ the value at $-\pi$ and the limit as $x \to \pi^-$ are both $0$, so the extension is continuous and by the **uniform convergence theorem** the series converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$. For the other three functions the values at the endpoints are different ($-\pi$ and $\pi$; $e^{-\pi}$ and $e^{\pi}$; $\pi^2 - \pi$ and $\pi^2 + \pi$), so the extension has a jump at $\pi$. Since the partial sums are continuous and a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, in these three cases uniform convergence on $\mathbb{R}$ is impossible.
