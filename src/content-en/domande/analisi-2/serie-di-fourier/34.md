The $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi)$, has Fourier series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n} \sin(nx)$. For which $x$ does the equality $x = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n} \sin(nx)$ hold?

- For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- For every $x \in [-\pi, \pi]$
- For every $x \in (-\pi, \pi)$
- For every $x \in [-\pi, \pi)$

## Explanation

The extension is piecewise smooth, so by the **pointwise convergence theorem** the series converges at every point to the mean $\dfrac{f(x^+) + f(x^-)}{2}$. For $-\pi < x < \pi$ the function is continuous and equals $x$, therefore at these points the sum is $x$. At $x = \pm\pi$ the extension has a jump between the values $\pi$ and $-\pi$, so the sum equals $0$, which is different from $\pm\pi$. For $|x| > \pi$ the sum is $f(x)$, which is different from $x$; for example at $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ the sum equals $f\left( \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right) = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Therefore the equality holds exactly for $x \in (-\pi, \pi)$.
