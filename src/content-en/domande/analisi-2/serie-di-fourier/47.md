For which integer $n \geq 0$ is the integral $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos^2(nx)\, dx$ equal to $2\pi$?

- For $n = 1$
- For every $n \geq 1$
- For no $n$
- For $n = 0$

## Explanation

For $n = 0$ we have $\cos(0 \cdot x) = 1$, so the integral equals $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} 1\, dx = 2\pi$. For $n \geq 1$ the **orthogonality of the trigonometric system** with $n = m$ gives $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos^2(nx)\, dx = \pi$. Therefore the integral equals $2\pi$ only for $n = 0$. Because of this difference the constant term of the Fourier series is written $\dfrac{a_0}{2}$, and in this way the formula $a_n = \dfrac{1}{\pi} \displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x) \cos(nx)\, dx$ also holds for $n = 0$.
