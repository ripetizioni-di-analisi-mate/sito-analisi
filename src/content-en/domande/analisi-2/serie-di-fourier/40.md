The $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi)$, has Fourier coefficients $a_n = 0$ and $b_n = \dfrac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$. What is the value of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n^2$?

- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi^3}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4\pi^2}{3}$

## Explanation

The function is square integrable on $[-\pi, \pi]$, so **Parseval's identity** applies. Since all the $a_n$ are zero, the identity becomes
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n^2 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} x^2\, dx = \frac{1}{\pi} \cdot \frac{2\pi^3}{3} = \frac{2\pi^2}{3} .
$$
The result is consistent with $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$, because $b_n^2 = \dfrac{4}{n^2}$ and $4 \cdot \dfrac{\pi^2}{6} = \dfrac{2\pi^2}{3}$.
