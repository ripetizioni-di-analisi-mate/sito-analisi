The Fourier coefficients of the $2\pi$-periodic function $g$, with $g(x) = \dfrac{\pi - x}{2}$ for $0 < x < 2\pi$, are $a_n = 0$ and $b_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$. What is the integral $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} g(x)^2\, dx$?

- $\dfrac{\pi^3}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^3}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi^3}{3}$

## Explanation

The function is bounded, so it is square integrable and by **Parseval's identity**
$$
\frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} g(x)^2\, dx = \frac{a_0^2}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigl( a_n^2 + b_n^2 \bigr) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} ,
$$
because all the $a_n$, including $a_0$, are zero. Since $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$, the integral equals $\pi \cdot \dfrac{\pi^2}{6} = \dfrac{\pi^3}{6}$.
