What is the value of the integral $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} (1 + \cos x)(2 \sin x + 3 \cos x)\, dx$?

- $3\pi$
- $6\pi$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

Expanding the product gives
$$
(1 + \cos x)(2 \sin x + 3 \cos x) = 2 \sin x + 3 \cos x + 2 \sin x \cos x + 3 \cos^2 x .
$$
By the **orthogonality of the trigonometric system** the integrals over $[-\pi, \pi]$ of $\sin x$, of $\cos x$ and of $\sin x \cos x$ are zero. What remains is the integral of $3 \cos^2 x$, and since $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos^2 x\, dx = \pi$, the integral equals $3\pi$.
