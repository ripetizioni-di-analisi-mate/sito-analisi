From the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x^2$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi]$,
$$
\frac{\pi^2}{3} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{4\,(-1)^n}{n^2} \cos(nx),
$$
one wants to derive the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$. At which of the following points does evaluating the series give this sum?

- $x = 0$
- $x = \pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The extension is continuous and piecewise smooth, so the series converges to $x^2$ at every point of $[-\pi, \pi]$. At $x = \pi$ we have $\cos(n\pi) = (-1)^n$, therefore every term becomes $\dfrac{4\,(-1)^n (-1)^n}{n^2} = \dfrac{4}{n^2}$ and the equality $\pi^2 = \dfrac{\pi^2}{3} + 4 \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ gives the sum $\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}$. At $x = 0$ we have $\cos 0 = 1$ and the alternating series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ appears. At $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the terms with odd $n$ vanish and an alternating series remains, while at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ the values $\cos\left( \dfrac{n\pi}{3} \right)$ are $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, $-1$, $1$ and the resulting series has varying signs.
