The $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x^2$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi]$, has Fourier series $\dfrac{\pi^2}{3} + \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{4\,(-1)^n}{n^2} \cos(nx)$. Evaluating it at $x = 0$, what is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^2}$?

- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{12}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{8}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi^2}{12}$

## Explanation

The extension is continuous, because $f(-\pi) = f(\pi) = \pi^2$, and it is piecewise smooth, so by the **pointwise convergence theorem** the series at $x = 0$ has sum $f(0) = 0$. Since $\cos 0 = 1$, we get
$$
0 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + 4 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2} ,
\qquad \text{quindi} \qquad
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2} = -\frac{\pi^2}{12} .
$$
The required series has terms of opposite sign, therefore its sum equals $\dfrac{\pi^2}{12}$.
