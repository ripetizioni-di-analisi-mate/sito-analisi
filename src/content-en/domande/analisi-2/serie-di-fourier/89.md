Which statement about the function $f(x) = \sin x + \sin(\sqrt{2}\, x)$ is true?

- $f$ is not periodic
- $f$ is periodic with period $2\pi$
- $f$ is periodic with period $\sqrt{2}\, \pi$
- $f$ is periodic with period $2\sqrt{2}\, \pi$

## Explanation

If $f$ had a period $T > 0$, then $f''(x) = -\sin x - 2 \sin(\sqrt{2}\, x)$ would also be $T$-periodic. Then $f + f'' = -\sin(\sqrt{2}\, x)$ and $2f + f'' = \sin x$ would be $T$-periodic as well. Hence $T$ would be an integer multiple of $2\pi$ and an integer multiple of $\sqrt{2}\, \pi$, that is $T = 2k\pi = m\sqrt{2}\, \pi$ with positive integers $k$, $m$. It would follow that $\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{2k}{m}$, which is false because $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational. Therefore $f$ is **not periodic**.
