Let $f$ be the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = e^x$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi)$. Which statement about the Fourier series of $f$ is true?

- It converges at every point of $\mathbb{R}$, but not uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$
- It converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$, because $e^x$ is continuous
- It converges to $e^x$ at every point of $[-\pi, \pi]$
- It does not converge at the points $x = \pi + 2k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The extension is piecewise smooth, so by the **pointwise convergence theorem** the series converges at every point of $\mathbb{R}$. At the points $\pi + 2k\pi$ the left limit equals $e^{\pi}$ and the right limit equals $e^{-\pi}$, therefore the extension has a jump and at those points the series converges to $\dfrac{e^{\pi} + e^{-\pi}}{2} = \cosh\pi$, which is different from $e^{\pi}$ and from $e^{-\pi}$. Since the partial sums are continuous functions and a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, the convergence **is not uniform** on $\mathbb{R}$.
