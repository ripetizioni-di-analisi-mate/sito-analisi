For an integer $n \geq 1$, what is the sum $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \sin^2(nx)\, dx + \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos^2(nx)\, dx$?

- $\pi$
- $2\pi$
- $4\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

By the **orthogonality of the trigonometric system** with $n = m$ each of the two integrals equals $\pi$, so the sum equals $2\pi$. The same result follows from $\sin^2(nx) + \cos^2(nx) = 1$, because the integral of $1$ over $[-\pi, \pi]$ is $2\pi$.
