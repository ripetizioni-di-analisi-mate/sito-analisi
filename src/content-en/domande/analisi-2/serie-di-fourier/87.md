Integrating over $[-\pi, \pi]$ the product of the three functions $\sin x$, $\sin(2x)$ and $\cos(3x)$, which value is obtained?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $-\pi$

## Explanation

By the product-to-sum formula $\sin x \sin(2x) = \dfrac{\cos x - \cos(3x)}{2}$, so the integrand is $\dfrac{\cos x \cos(3x) - \cos^2(3x)}{2}$. By the **orthogonality of the trigonometric system** the integral of $\cos x \cos(3x)$ over $[-\pi, \pi]$ is zero and the integral of $\cos^2(3x)$ equals $\pi$. Therefore the integral equals $\dfrac{0 - \pi}{2} = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The integrand is even, because it is the product of two odd functions and an even one, so the integral does not vanish by symmetry.
