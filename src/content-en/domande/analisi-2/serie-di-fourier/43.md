The square wave with period $2$ that equals $-1$ on $(-1, 0)$ and $1$ on $(0, 1)$ has Fourier coefficients $a_n = 0$, $b_n = \dfrac{4}{n\pi}$ for odd $n$ and $b_n = 0$ for even $n$. What does Parseval's identity give for the sum of the reciprocals of the squares of the odd numbers, $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2k+1)^2}$?

- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{8}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{16}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}$

## Explanation

The period is $2L = 2$, so $L = 1$ and the left-hand side of **Parseval's identity** is $\dfrac{1}{L} \displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)^2\, dx = \int_{-1}^{1} 1\, dx = 2$, because $f(x)^2 = 1$ except at $x = 0$. The right-hand side contains only the $b_n$ with odd $n$, hence
$$
2 = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{16}{(2k+1)^2 \pi^2} .
$$
Therefore $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2k+1)^2} = \frac{2\pi^2}{16} = \frac{\pi^2}{8}$. The value $\dfrac{\pi^2}{16}$ would be obtained by dividing the integral by $2L$ instead of $L$, and the value $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$ by using the factor $\dfrac{1}{\pi}$ of period $2\pi$.
