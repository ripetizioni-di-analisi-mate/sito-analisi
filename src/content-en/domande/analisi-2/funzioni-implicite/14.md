The equation $y^2 \ln x + y = 2$ implicitly defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ near the point $(1, 2)$. What is $\varphi'(1)$?

- $\varphi'(1) = 4$
- $\varphi'(1) = -4$
- $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\varphi'(1) = -2$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = y^2 \ln x + y - 2$, which is of class $C^1$ on the half-plane $x > 0$; we have $F(1, 2) = 4 \ln 1 + 2 - 2 = 0$. The partial derivatives are
$$
F_x = \frac{y^2}{x}, \qquad F_y = 2y \ln x + 1 ,
$$
so $F_x(1, 2) = 4$ and $F_y(1, 2) = 1 \neq 0$. Therefore **Dini's theorem** applies and $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{4}{1} = -4$.
