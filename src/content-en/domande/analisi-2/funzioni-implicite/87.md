Two functions $F$ and $G$ of class $C^1$ vanish at the point $P_0 = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$, with $\nabla F(P_0) = (1, 2, 1)$ and $\nabla G(P_0) = (0, 1, 3)$. The system $F = 0$, $G = 0$ defines $y = y(x)$ and $z = z(x)$ near $P_0$. What are $y'(x_0)$ and $z'(x_0)$?

- $y'(x_0) = \dfrac{3}{5}$, $z'(x_0) = -\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $y'(x_0) = -\dfrac{3}{5}$, $z'(x_0) = \dfrac{1}{5}$
- $y'(x_0) = \dfrac{1}{5}$, $z'(x_0) = -\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $y'(x_0) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, $z'(x_0) = 0$

## Explanation

The Jacobian determinant with respect to $(y, z)$ is $\det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = 5 \neq 0$, so the implicit function theorem for systems applies. Differentiating the identities $F(x, y(x), z(x)) = 0$ and $G(x, y(x), z(x)) = 0$ with the chain rule gives, at $x_0$,
$$
\begin{cases}
1 + 2\,y' + z' = 0 \\[4pt]
y' + 3\,z' = 0 .
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation $y' = -3z'$, and substituting into the first gives $1 - 5z' = 0$. Therefore $z'(x_0) = \dfrac{1}{5}$ and $y'(x_0) = -\dfrac{3}{5}$.
