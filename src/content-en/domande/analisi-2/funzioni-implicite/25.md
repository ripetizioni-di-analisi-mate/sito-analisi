How many continuous functions $\varphi : [-1, 1] \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy $\varphi(x)^2 = x^2 - x^4$ for every $x \in [-1, 1]$?

- Four
- One
- Two
- Infinitely many

## Explanation

Since $x^2 - x^4 = x^2 (1 - x^2)$, every solution satisfies $|\varphi(x)| = |x| \sqrt{1 - x^2}$, which vanishes only for $x = -1$, $x = 0$ and $x = 1$. On the interval $(0, 1)$ the continuous function $\varphi$ does not vanish, so by **Bolzano's theorem** it has constant sign; the same holds on the interval $(-1, 0)$. Therefore on each of the two intervals we have $\varphi(x) = |x| \sqrt{1 - x^2}$ or $\varphi(x) = -|x| \sqrt{1 - x^2}$. The two choices are independent, since at $x = 0$ both expressions equal $0$ and the resulting function is continuous in every case. Hence there are $2 \cdot 2 = 4$ continuous functions. The gradient of $F(x, y) = y^2 - x^2 + x^4$ vanishes at the origin, so the origin is a singular point of the curve, where the four arcs meet.
