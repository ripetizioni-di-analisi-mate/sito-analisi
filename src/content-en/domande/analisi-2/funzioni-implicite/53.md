What is the normal line to the curve $x^2 + y^3 = 2$ at the point $(1, 1)$, that is, the line through the point orthogonal to the tangent line?

- $2x + 3y = 5$
- $2x - 3y = -1$
- $3x - 2y = 1$
- $3x + 2y = 5$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + y^3 - 2$, with $F(1, 1) = 0$ and $\nabla F(1, 1) = (2x, 3y^2)\,\big|_{(1,1)} = (2, 3)$. The gradient is orthogonal to the tangent line, so the normal line passes through $(1, 1)$ with the direction of $(2, 3)$. A line with direction $(2, 3)$ has equation $3(x - 1) - 2(y - 1) = 0$, therefore the normal line is
$$
3x - 2y = 1 .
$$
The line $2x + 3y = 5$ is instead the tangent line.
