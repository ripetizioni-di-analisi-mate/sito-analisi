The equation $e^{x+y} - x^2 - y^2 = 1$ implicitly defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ near the point $(0, 0)$. What is the tangent line to the curve at the origin?

- $y = -x$
- $y = x$
- $y = 0$
- $x = 0$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = e^{x+y} - x^2 - y^2 - 1$, which is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(0, 0) = 1 - 1 = 0$. The partial derivatives are $F_x = e^{x+y} - 2x$ and $F_y = e^{x+y} - 2y$, so $F_x(0, 0) = 1$ and $F_y(0, 0) = 1 \neq 0$. By the **implicit function theorem** we then have
$$
\varphi'(0) = -\,\frac{F_x(0, 0)}{F_y(0, 0)} = -1 .
$$
Therefore the tangent line at the origin is $y = -x$.
