Consider $F(x, y) = x - y^3$ at the point $(0, 0)$. Which statement is true?

- Near $(0, 0)$ no function $y = \varphi(x)$ describes the curve $F(x, y) = 0$
- Near $(0, 0)$ the curve is the graph of $y = \sqrt[3]{x}$, which is not differentiable at $x = 0$
- Dini's theorem with respect to $y$ applies and $\varphi'(0) = 0$
- The gradient of $F$ at $(0, 0)$ is zero, so the point is singular

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $F_x = 1$ and $F_y = -3y^2$, so $\nabla F(0, 0) = (1, 0)$ and the point is not singular. Since $F_y(0, 0) = 0$, Dini's theorem with respect to $y$ does not apply. However, the equation $x - y^3 = 0$ can be solved for $y$ for every $x$ and gives $y = \sqrt[3]{x}$, so the curve is the **graph** of this function, which is continuous but not differentiable at $x = 0$. Since $F_x \neq 0$, the curve is also the graph of $x = y^3$, and the tangent line at $(0, 0)$ is vertical.
