The equation $xz + z^3 = y + 1$ implicitly defines a function $z = g(x, y)$ near the point $(1, 1, 1)$. Using the differential of $g$ at $(1, 1)$, which value approximates the increment $g(1.2,\ 1.4) - g(1, 1)$?

- $0.15$
- $-0.05$
- $0.05$
- $0.2$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = xz + z^3 - y - 1$, with $F(1, 1, 1) = 0$, $F_x = z$, $F_y = -1$ and $F_z = x + 3z^2$, so $F_z(1, 1, 1) = 4 \neq 0$. The partial derivatives of $g$ at the point are $g_x(1, 1) = -\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $g_y(1, 1) = -\dfrac{-1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. With the increments $\Delta x = 0.2$ and $\Delta y = 0.4$ the differential equals
$$
g_x(1, 1)\,\Delta x + g_y(1, 1)\,\Delta y = -\frac{0.2}{4} + \frac{0.4}{4} = 0.05 ,
$$
which is the required approximation of the increment.
