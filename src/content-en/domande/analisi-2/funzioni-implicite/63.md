The function $z = g(x, y)$ is implicitly defined by the equation $z\,e^{z} = x + 2y$ near the origin, with $g(0, 0) = 0$. What is the directional derivative of $g$ at $(0, 0)$ in the direction of the unit vector $v = \left(\dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5}\right)$?

- $\dfrac{11}{5}$
- $3$
- $-\dfrac{11}{5}$
- $1$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = z\,e^{z} - x - 2y$, with $F(0, 0, 0) = 0$ and $F_z = (1 + z)\,e^{z}$, so $F_z(0, 0, 0) = 1 \neq 0$ and the implicit function theorem applies with respect to $z$. Since $F_x = -1$ and $F_y = -2$, we have $g_x(0, 0) = 1$ and $g_y(0, 0) = 2$, that is, $\nabla g(0, 0) = (1, 2)$. The function $g$ is of class $C^1$, hence differentiable, and the directional derivative is the dot product of the gradient with the unit vector, that is,
$$
\frac{\partial g}{\partial v}(0, 0) = 1 \cdot \frac{3}{5} + 2 \cdot \frac{4}{5} = \frac{11}{5} .
$$
