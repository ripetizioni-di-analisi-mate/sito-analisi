For the curve $x^2 + 2y^2 = 3$ at the point $(1, 1)$, which vector is orthogonal to the tangent line?

- $(2, -1)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $(1, 1)$
- $(1, 2)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + 2y^2 - 3$; we have $F(1, 1) = 0$ and $\nabla F(1, 1) = (2x, 4y) \,\big|_{(1,1)} = (2, 4)$. The gradient is **orthogonal** to the level curve, hence to the tangent line. The vector $(1, 2)$ is parallel to $(2, 4)$, so it is also orthogonal to the tangent line. Indeed the tangent line $2(x - 1) + 4(y - 1) = 0$, that is, $x + 2y = 3$, has the direction of the vector $(2, -1)$, and $(1, 2) \cdot (2, -1) = 0$. For the other vectors we have $(2, 1) \cdot (2, -1) = 3$ and $(1, 1) \cdot (2, -1) = 1$, while $(2, -1)$ is the direction of the tangent itself.
