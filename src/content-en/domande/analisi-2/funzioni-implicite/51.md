For a function $F$ of class $C^1$ it is known that $F(2, -1) = 0$ and $\nabla F(2, -1) = (3, -6)$. What is the tangent line to the curve $F(x, y) = 0$ at the point $(2, -1)$?

- $2x + y = 3$
- $x - 2y = 4$
- $x + 2y = 0$
- $x - 2y = 0$

## Explanation

Since $\nabla F(2, -1) \neq (0, 0)$, the tangent line to the curve at the point has equation $F_x(2, -1)(x - 2) + F_y(2, -1)(y + 1) = 0$. Substituting the values gives
$$
3(x - 2) - 6(y + 1) = 0 ,
$$
that is, $3x - 6y - 12 = 0$, so $x - 2y = 4$. The gradient $(3, -6)$ is orthogonal to this line, since the line has the direction of the vector $(2, 1)$ and $(3, -6) \cdot (2, 1) = 0$.
