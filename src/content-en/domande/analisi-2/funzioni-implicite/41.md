The system
$$
\begin{cases}
xy + z = 2 \\[4pt]
x + y z^2 = 2
\end{cases}
$$
implicitly defines two functions $y = y(x)$ and $z = z(x)$ near the point $(1, 1, 1)$. What are $y'(1)$ and $z'(1)$?

- $y'(1) = -1$, $z'(1) = 0$
- $y'(1) = 0$, $z'(1) = -1$
- $y'(1) = 1$, $z'(1) = 0$
- $y'(1) = -1$, $z'(1) = 1$

## Explanation

The functions $F(x, y, z) = xy + z - 2$ and $G(x, y, z) = x + y z^2 - 2$ are polynomials, hence of class $C^1$, and the point $(1, 1, 1)$ solves the system. The Jacobian determinant with respect to $(y, z)$ at the point is
$$
\det \begin{pmatrix} x & 1 \\ z^2 & 2yz \end{pmatrix}_{(1, 1, 1)} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = 1 \neq 0 ,
$$
so **Dini's theorem for systems** applies. Differentiating the two equations with respect to $x$ and evaluating at the point we obtain
$$
\begin{cases}
1 + y'(1) + z'(1) = 0 \\[4pt]
1 + y'(1) + 2\,z'(1) = 0 .
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting the first equation from the second we obtain $z'(1) = 0$, so $y'(1) = -1$.
