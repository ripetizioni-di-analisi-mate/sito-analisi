What is the tangent line to the curve $x^2 + y^3 - xy = 1$ at the point $(1, 1)$?

- $x + 2y = 3$
- $2x + y = 3$
- $x - 2y = -1$
- $x + 2y = 0$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + y^3 - xy - 1$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(1, 1) = 1 + 1 - 1 - 1 = 0$, so the point lies on the curve. The partial derivatives are $F_x = 2x - y$ and $F_y = 3y^2 - x$, so $\nabla F(1, 1) = (1, 2) \neq (0, 0)$. The tangent line therefore has equation
$$
1 \cdot (x - 1) + 2\,(y - 1) = 0 ,
$$
that is, $x + 2y = 3$.
