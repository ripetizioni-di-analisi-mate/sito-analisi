Near a point $(x_0, y_0)$ the curve $F(x, y) = 0$, with $F$ of class $C^1$, is both the graph of a function $y = \varphi(x)$ and the graph of a function $x = \psi(y)$, both given by the implicit function theorem, and it is known that $\varphi'(x_0) = -4$. What is $\psi'(y_0)$?

- $\psi'(y_0) = \dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\psi'(y_0) = 4$
- $\psi'(y_0) = -4$
- $\psi'(y_0) = -\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

Since the theorem applies with respect to both variables, at the point we have $F_x \neq 0$ and $F_y \neq 0$. The two derivatives are $\varphi'(x_0) = -\dfrac{F_x}{F_y}$ and $\psi'(y_0) = -\dfrac{F_y}{F_x}$, evaluated at $(x_0, y_0)$, so
$$
\psi'(y_0) = \frac{1}{\varphi'(x_0)} = -\,\frac{1}{4} .
$$
The result reflects the fact that $\psi$ is the inverse of $\varphi$ near the point.
