A surface $F(x, y, z) = 0$, with $F$ of class $C^1$, has at the point $(1, 1, 2)$ the tangent plane $2x - y + 2z = 5$, and near that point it is the graph of a function $z = g(x, y)$. What is $g_y(1, 1)$?

- $g_y(1, 1) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $g_y(1, 1) = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $g_y(1, 1) = -1$
- $g_y(1, 1) = 2$

## Explanation

The normal vector to the tangent plane is $(2, -1, 2)$, and the gradient $\nabla F(1, 1, 2)$ is a nonzero multiple of it, that is, $\nabla F(1, 1, 2) = \lambda\,(2, -1, 2)$ with $\lambda \neq 0$. By the implicit function theorem in three variables
$$
g_y(1, 1) = -\,\frac{F_y}{F_z} = -\,\frac{-\lambda}{2\lambda} = \frac{1}{2} ,
$$
and the value does not depend on $\lambda$. Likewise $g_x(1, 1) = -\dfrac{2\lambda}{2\lambda} = -1$, consistent with the explicit form of the plane $z = -x + \dfrac{1}{2}\,y + \dfrac{5}{2}$.
