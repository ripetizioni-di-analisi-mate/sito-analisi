The surface $z\,e^{xz} = y$ contains the point $(0, 1, 1)$, and near it is the graph of a function $z = g(x, y)$. What are the signs of the partial derivatives $g_x(0, 1)$ and $g_y(0, 1)$?

- $g_x(0, 1) > 0$ and $g_y(0, 1) < 0$
- $g_x(0, 1) > 0$ and $g_y(0, 1) > 0$
- $g_x(0, 1) < 0$ and $g_y(0, 1) > 0$
- $g_x(0, 1) < 0$ and $g_y(0, 1) < 0$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = z\,e^{xz} - y$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, with $F(0, 1, 1) = 1 - 1 = 0$. The partial derivatives are $F_x = z^2 e^{xz}$, $F_y = -1$ and $F_z = (1 + xz)\,e^{xz}$, so at the point $F_x = 1$, $F_y = -1$ and $F_z = 1 \neq 0$. By the implicit function theorem in three variables we then have
$$
g_x(0, 1) = -\,\frac{F_x}{F_z} = -1 ,
\qquad
g_y(0, 1) = -\,\frac{F_y}{F_z} = 1 .
$$
Therefore $g_x(0, 1) < 0$ and $g_y(0, 1) > 0$.
