The equation $\sin(\pi x y) + y = 1$ implicitly defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ near the point $(1, 1)$. What is $\varphi'(1)$?

- $\varphi'(1) = \dfrac{\pi}{\pi - 1}$
- $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{\pi}{\pi - 1}$
- $\varphi'(1) = -1$
- $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{\pi - 1}{\pi}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = \sin(\pi x y) + y - 1$, which is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(1, 1) = \sin \pi + 1 - 1 = 0$. By the chain rule the partial derivatives are
$$
F_x = \pi y \cos(\pi x y), \qquad F_y = \pi x \cos(\pi x y) + 1 ,
$$
so, since $\cos \pi = -1$, we get $F_x(1, 1) = -\pi$ and $F_y(1, 1) = 1 - \pi \neq 0$. Therefore the **implicit function theorem** applies and
$$
\varphi'(1) = -\,\frac{-\pi}{1 - \pi} = -\,\frac{\pi}{\pi - 1} .
$$
