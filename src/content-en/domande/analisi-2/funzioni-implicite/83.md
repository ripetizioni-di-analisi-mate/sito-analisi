A system made of the equation $x + y + z = 3$ and another equation $G(x, y, z) = 0$ defines two functions $y = y(x)$ and $z = z(x)$ near a point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$, and it is known that $y'(x_0) = 2$. What is $z'(x_0)$?

- $z'(x_0) = -2$
- $z'(x_0) = 3$
- $z'(x_0) = -1$
- $z'(x_0) = -3$

## Explanation

The functions $y(x)$ and $z(x)$ satisfy the identity $x + y(x) + z(x) = 3$ for every $x$ near $x_0$. Differentiating with respect to $x$ gives
$$
1 + y'(x) + z'(x) = 0 .
$$
For $x = x_0$ we therefore have $z'(x_0) = -1 - y'(x_0) = -1 - 2 = -3$.
