- $g_x(1, 1) = \dfrac{2}{5}$, $g_y(1, 1) = -\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $g_x(1, 1) = -\dfrac{2}{3}$, $g_y(1, 1) = \dfrac{2}{3}$
- $g_x(1, 1) = -\dfrac{2}{5}$, $g_y(1, 1) = \dfrac{2}{5}$
- $g_x(1, 1) = -\dfrac{2}{5}$, $g_y(1, 1) = -\dfrac{2}{5}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = z^3 + 2z + x^2 - y^2 - 3$, which is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$; we have $F(1, 1, 1) = 1 + 2 + 1 - 1 - 3 = 0$. Since $F_z = 3z^2 + 2$, we have $F_z(1, 1, 1) = 5 \neq 0$, so **Dini's theorem in three variables** applies. The other partial derivatives at the point are $F_x = 2x = 2$ and $F_y = -2y = -2$. Therefore
$$
g_x(1, 1) = -\,\frac{F_x}{F_z} = -\,\frac{2}{5},
\qquad
g_y(1, 1) = -\,\frac{F_y}{F_z} = \frac{2}{5} .
$$
