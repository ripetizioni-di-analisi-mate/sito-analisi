For the equation $x + y + z + \sin(xyz) = 0$ near the point $(0, 0, 0)$, which statement is true?

- Dini's theorem does not apply, because $\nabla F(0, 0, 0) = (0, 0, 0)$
- The surface is the graph of $z = g(x, y)$ with $g_x(0, 0) = g_y(0, 0) = -1$
- The surface is the graph of $z = g(x, y)$ with $g_x(0, 0) = g_y(0, 0) = 1$
- The surface is the graph of $z = g(x, y)$ with $g_x(0, 0) = g_y(0, 0) = 0$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = x + y + z + \sin(xyz)$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$; we have $F(0, 0, 0) = 0$. By the chain rule
$$
F_x = 1 + yz\cos(xyz), \qquad F_y = 1 + xz\cos(xyz), \qquad F_z = 1 + xy\cos(xyz) ,
$$
so $\nabla F(0, 0, 0) = (1, 1, 1)$. Since $F_z(0, 0, 0) = 1 \neq 0$, **Dini's theorem in three variables** applies, and $g_x(0, 0) = -\dfrac{1}{1} = -1$, $g_y(0, 0) = -\dfrac{1}{1} = -1$.
