- $\varphi'(1) = 1$, $\varphi''(1) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = y^3 + y - 2x^2$, which is of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(1, 1) = 0$ and $F_y(1, 1) = 3y^2 + 1 \,\big|_{(1,1)} = 4 \neq 0$, so the **implicit function theorem** applies. Differentiating the identity with respect to $x$, with $y = \varphi(x)$, we obtain $3y^2\,y' + y' - 4x = 0$; at the point $(1, 1)$ this gives $4\,y' - 4 = 0$, therefore $\varphi'(1) = 1$. Differentiating again we obtain
$$
6y\,(y')^2 + 3y^2\,y'' + y'' - 4 = 0 .
$$
At the point, with $y' = 1$, we have $6 + 4\,y'' - 4 = 0$, so $\varphi''(1) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
