For which of the following curves does the implicit function theorem with respect to $y$ apply at *every* point of the curve?

- $y^3 + 2y - e^{x} = 0$
- $y^3 - 3y - x = 0$
- $x^2 + y^2 - 4 = 0$
- $y^2 - x^3 = 0$

## Explanation

The implicit function theorem with respect to $y$ applies at every point of the curve $F(x, y) = 0$ if $F_y$ vanishes at no point of the curve. For $F = y^3 + 2y - e^{x}$ we have $F_y = 3y^2 + 2 > 0$ on the whole plane, so the condition is **satisfied** everywhere. For $F = y^3 - 3y - x$ we have $F_y = 3y^2 - 3$, which vanishes at the points $(-2, 1)$ and $(2, -1)$ of the curve. For $F = x^2 + y^2 - 4$ the derivative $F_y = 2y$ vanishes at the points $(2, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$, and for $F = y^2 - x^3$ it vanishes at the point $(0, 0)$.
