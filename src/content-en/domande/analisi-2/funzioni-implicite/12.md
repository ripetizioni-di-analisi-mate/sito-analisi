- $y'(1) = \dfrac{3}{5}$, $z'(1) = \dfrac{1}{5}$
- $y'(1) = -\dfrac{2}{3}$, $z'(1) = -\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $y'(1) = -\dfrac{3}{5}$, $z'(1) = -\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $y'(1) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, $z'(1) = 0$

## Explanation

The functions $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 6$ and $G(x, y, z) = x + 2y - z - 1$ are polynomials, hence of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$. The point $(1, 1, 2)$ solves the system, since $1 + 1 + 4 = 6$ and $1 + 2 - 2 = 1$. The Jacobian determinant with respect to $(y, z)$ at the point is $\det \begin{pmatrix} 2y & 2z \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}_{(1,1,2)} = -2 - 8 = -10 \neq 0$, so **Dini's theorem for systems** applies. Differentiating the two equations with respect to $x$ and evaluating at the point we obtain
$$
\begin{cases}
2 + 2\,y'(1) + 4\,z'(1) = 0 \\[4pt]
1 + 2\,y'(1) - z'(1) = 0 .
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation it follows that $z'(1) = 1 + 2\,y'(1)$, and substituting into the first we obtain $6 + 10\,y'(1) = 0$. Therefore $y'(1) = -\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $z'(1) = 1 - \dfrac{6}{5} = -\dfrac{1}{5}$.
