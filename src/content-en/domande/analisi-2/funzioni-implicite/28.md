We want to compute the limit
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\varphi(x) + x}{x^2} ,
$$
where $\varphi$ is the function, with $\varphi(0) = 0$, defined near $x_0 = 0$ by the equation $e^{x + y} = 1 + xy$. What is the value of the limit?

- $1$
- $-2$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = e^{x + y} - 1 - xy$, of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, with $F(0, 0) = 0$. The partial derivatives are $F_x = e^{x + y} - y$ and $F_y = e^{x + y} - x$, so $F_x(0, 0) = 1$ and $F_y(0, 0) = 1 \neq 0$. Therefore the implicit function theorem applies, $\varphi$ is of class $C^2$ and $\varphi'(0) = -1$. Differentiating the identity with respect to $x$, with $y = \varphi(x)$, we obtain $e^{x + y}\,(1 + y') - y - x\,y' = 0$. Differentiating again we obtain
$$
e^{x + y}\,(1 + y')^2 + e^{x + y}\,y'' - 2y' - x\,y'' = 0 ,
$$
which at $x = 0$, with $y' = -1$, becomes $\varphi''(0) + 2 = 0$, so $\varphi''(0) = -2$. By Taylor's formula with the Peano remainder we then have $\varphi(x) = -x - x^2 + o(x^2)$ as $x \to 0$, therefore
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\varphi(x) + x}{x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-x^2 + o(x^2)}{x^2} = -1 .
$$
