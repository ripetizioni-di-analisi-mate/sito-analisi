The equation $y^3 + y = x^3 - 3x$ implicitly defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. What is the set of points $x$ where $\varphi'(x) > 0$?

- $(-1, 1)$
- $(1, +\infty)$
- $(0, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, -1) \cup (1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = y^3 + y - x^3 + 3x$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. Since $F_y = 3y^2 + 1 > 0$, the implicit function theorem applies at every point of the curve and
$$
\varphi'(x) = -\,\frac{F_x}{F_y} = \frac{3x^2 - 3}{3y^2 + 1} ,
$$
with $y = \varphi(x)$. The denominator is positive, so $\varphi'(x)$ has the sign of $x^2 - 1$. Therefore $\varphi'(x) > 0$ if and only if $x < -1$ or $x > 1$, and $\varphi$ is **increasing** on each of the two intervals.
