- $\varphi'(1) = 1$
- $\varphi'(1) = -1$
- $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{4}{3}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^3 + y^3 + xy - 3$, which is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. We have $F(1, 1) = 1 + 1 + 1 - 3 = 0$. The partial derivatives are $F_x = 3x^2 + y$ and $F_y = 3y^2 + x$, so $F_x(1, 1) = 4$ and $F_y(1, 1) = 4 \neq 0$. The hypotheses of **Dini's theorem** are therefore satisfied, and
$$
\varphi'(1) = -\,\frac{F_x(1, 1)}{F_y(1, 1)} = -\,\frac{4}{4} = -1 .
$$
