- $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,x + \dfrac{3}{2}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{2}\,x + \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $y = -2x + 3$
- $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,x + \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 y + y^3 - 2$, which is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(1, 1) = 0$, so the point belongs to the curve. The partial derivatives are $F_x = 2xy$ and $F_y = x^2 + 3y^2$, therefore $\nabla F(1, 1) = (2, 4) \neq (0, 0)$. The tangent line therefore has equation
$$
2(x - 1) + 4(y - 1) = 0 .
$$
Dividing by $4$ and solving for $y$ we obtain $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,x + \dfrac{3}{2}$.
