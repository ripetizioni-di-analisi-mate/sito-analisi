For the curve $x^3 + y^2 - 3x = 0$, at which points does the implicit function theorem not apply with respect to the variable $y$?

- $(1, \sqrt{2})$ and $(1, -\sqrt{2})$
- $(0, 0)$, $(\sqrt{3}, 0)$ and $(-\sqrt{3}, 0)$
- $(\sqrt{3}, 0)$ and $(-\sqrt{3}, 0)$
- $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^3 + y^2 - 3x$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, with $F_y = 2y$. The implicit function theorem with respect to $y$ does not apply at the points of the curve where $F_y = 0$, that is, $y = 0$. Substituting $y = 0$ into the equation we obtain $x^3 - 3x = x\,(x^2 - 3) = 0$, so $x = 0$ or $x = \pm\sqrt{3}$. The points are therefore $(0, 0)$, $(\sqrt{3}, 0)$ and $(-\sqrt{3}, 0)$. At these points $F_x = 3x^2 - 3$ equals $-3$, $6$ and $6$ respectively, which are nonzero, so near each point the curve is the graph of a function $x = \psi(y)$, with vertical tangent.
