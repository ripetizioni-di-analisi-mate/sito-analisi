At the points of the curve $y^3 + xy = 5$ where the implicit function theorem with respect to $y$ applies, what is the expression of the derivative of the implicit function $y = \varphi(x)$?

- $\varphi'(x) = -\dfrac{3y^2 + x}{y}$
- $\varphi'(x) = -\dfrac{y}{3y^2 + x}$
- $\varphi'(x) = \dfrac{y}{3y^2 + x}$
- $\varphi'(x) = -\dfrac{y}{3y^2}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = y^3 + xy - 5$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. The partial derivatives are $F_x = y$ and $F_y = 3y^2 + x$, and the theorem applies at the points of the curve where $3y^2 + x \neq 0$. At those points the formula of the implicit function theorem gives
$$
\varphi'(x) = -\,\frac{F_x}{F_y} = -\,\frac{y}{3y^2 + x} ,
$$
with $y = \varphi(x)$. The same result is obtained by differentiating the identity with respect to $x$, that is, $3y^2 y' + y + x y' = 0$, and solving for $y'$.
