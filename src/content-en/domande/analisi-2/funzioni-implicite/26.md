The equation $x^2 + 3y^2 = 12$ implicitly defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ near the point $(3, 1)$. What is $\varphi''(3)$?

- $\varphi''(3) = -\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\varphi''(3) = -\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\varphi''(3) = \dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\varphi''(3) = -1$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + 3y^2 - 12$, of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(3, 1) = 9 + 3 - 12 = 0$ and $F_y(3, 1) = 6y \,\big|_{(3,1)} = 6 \neq 0$, so the **implicit function theorem** applies. Differentiating the identity with respect to $x$ we obtain $2x + 6y\,y' = 0$; at the point this gives $6 + 6y' = 0$, so $\varphi'(3) = -1$. Differentiating again we obtain
$$
2 + 6(y')^2 + 6y\,y'' = 0 .
$$
At the point, with $y' = -1$, we have $2 + 6 + 6y'' = 0$, hence $\varphi''(3) = -\dfrac{8}{6} = -\dfrac{4}{3}$.
