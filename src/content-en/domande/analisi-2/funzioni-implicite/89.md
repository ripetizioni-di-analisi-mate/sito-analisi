For the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 3 \\[4pt]
xyz = 1
\end{cases}
$$
at the point $(1, 1, 1)$, which statement is true?

- The theorem applies only with $(y, z)$ as dependent variables
- The theorem applies with every pair of dependent variables
- The theorem fails to apply only with $(x, y)$ as dependent variables
- All $2 \times 2$ Jacobian determinants are zero, so the implicit function theorem for systems does not apply for any choice of the dependent variables

## Explanation

Let $F = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 3$ and $G = xyz - 1$; the point solves the system, and the Jacobian matrix at the point is
$$
\begin{pmatrix} F_x & F_y & F_z \\ G_x & G_y & G_z \end{pmatrix}_{(1,1,1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The two rows are proportional, so every $2 \times 2$ minor equals $2 - 2 = 0$. Therefore the theorem **does not apply** with any pair of dependent variables. The result reflects the fact that the gradients are parallel, that is, the two surfaces have the same tangent plane $x + y + z = 3$ at the point.
