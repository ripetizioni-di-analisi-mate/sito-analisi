At which points of the ellipsoid $x^2 + 4y^2 + 9z^2 = 36$ does the normal line to the ellipsoid pass through the origin?

- At the six points where the ellipsoid meets the coordinate axes
- At every point of the ellipsoid
- Only at the two points $(6, 0, 0)$ and $(-6, 0, 0)$
- At no point of the ellipsoid

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + 4y^2 + 9z^2 - 36$, with $\nabla F = (2x, 8y, 18z)$, which never vanishes on the ellipsoid. The normal line at a point $P = (x, y, z)$ has the direction of $\nabla F(P)$, so it passes through the origin if and only if $\nabla F(P)$ is parallel to the vector $(x, y, z)$, that is, if there exists $\lambda$ such that
$$
\begin{cases}
2x = \lambda x \\[4pt]
8y = \lambda y \\[4pt]
18z = \lambda z .
\end{cases}
$$
If two coordinates were nonzero, $\lambda$ would have to equal two of the numbers $2$, $8$, $18$, which are distinct; hence at most one of the three coordinates is nonzero. Therefore the required points are those of the ellipsoid lying on the axes, that is, $(\pm 6, 0, 0)$, $(0, \pm 3, 0)$ and $(0, 0, \pm 2)$. On a sphere, by contrast, the normal line passes through the center at every point.
