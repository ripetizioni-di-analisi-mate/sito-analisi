The system
$$
\begin{cases}
u + v^2 = x + y + z \\[4pt]
u^2 + v = xyz + 1
\end{cases}
$$
in the five variables $x, y, z, u, v$ is satisfied at the point $(x, y, z, u, v) = (0, 0, 0, 0, 1)$. What does the implicit function theorem for systems guarantee near this point?

- The system defines $u$ and $v$ as functions of class $C^1$ of $(x, y, z)$
- The system defines $x$, $y$ and $z$ as functions of class $C^1$ of $(u, v)$
- The system defines $u$ and $v$ as functions of the single variable $x$
- The theorem does not apply, because there are fewer equations than variables

## Explanation

Let $F = u + v^2 - x - y - z$ and $G = u^2 + v - xyz - 1$. The Jacobian determinant with respect to $(u, v)$ at the point is
$$
\det \begin{pmatrix} F_u & F_v \\ G_u & G_v \end{pmatrix} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2v \\ 2u & 1 \end{pmatrix}_{(u, v) = (0, 1)} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 1 \neq 0 .
$$
The theorem for $m = 2$ equations in $n + m = 5$ variables therefore applies with $u, v$ as dependent variables, and defines them as $C^1$ functions of the other $n = 3$ variables $(x, y, z)$. Two equations, on the other hand, are not enough to determine three dependent variables.
