The curves $x^2 - y^2 = 3$ and $xy = 2$ both pass through the point $(2, 1)$. What angle do their tangent lines form at that point?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 - y^2 - 3$ and $G(x, y) = xy - 2$, with $\nabla F(2, 1) = (4, -2)$ and $\nabla G(2, 1) = (y, x)\,\big|_{(2,1)} = (1, 2)$. Each gradient is orthogonal to the tangent line of its own curve, so the angle between the tangent lines equals the angle between the two gradients. Since
$$
\nabla F(2, 1) \cdot \nabla G(2, 1) = 4 - 4 = 0 ,
$$
the gradients are orthogonal, therefore the two curves meet at a **right angle**.
