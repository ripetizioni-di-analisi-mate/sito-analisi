For a function $F$ of class $C^1$ it is known that $F(2, 1) = 0$, $F_x(2, 1) = 6$ and $F_y(2, 1) = -3$. The equation $F(x, y) = 0$ defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ near $(2, 1)$. What is $\varphi'(2)$?

- $\varphi'(2) = -2$
- $\varphi'(2) = 2$
- $\varphi'(2) = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\varphi'(2) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The hypotheses of the implicit function theorem are satisfied, since $F(2, 1) = 0$ and $F_y(2, 1) = -3 \neq 0$. The formula of the theorem gives
$$
\varphi'(2) = -\,\frac{F_x(2, 1)}{F_y(2, 1)} = -\,\frac{6}{-3} = 2 .
$$
The value is obtained from the partial derivatives at the point alone, without knowing $F$ or $\varphi$.
