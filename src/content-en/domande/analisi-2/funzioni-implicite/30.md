What is the tangent line to the curve $\ln x + x y^2 = 4$ at the point $(1, 2)$?

- $4x + 5y = 14$
- $5x + 4y = 13$
- $x + 4y = 9$
- $5x - 4y = -3$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = \ln x + x y^2 - 4$, of class $C^1$ on the half-plane $x > 0$; we have $F(1, 2) = 0 + 4 - 4 = 0$. The partial derivatives are
$$
F_x = \frac{1}{x} + y^2, \qquad F_y = 2xy ,
$$
so $\nabla F(1, 2) = (5, 4) \neq (0, 0)$. The tangent line has equation $5(x - 1) + 4(y - 2) = 0$, that is, $5x + 4y = 13$.
