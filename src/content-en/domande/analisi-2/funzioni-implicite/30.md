The equation $y^5 + y + x = 0$ defines, for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, a unique value $y = \varphi(x)$. What is $\varphi'(-2)$?

- $\varphi'(-2) = \dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\varphi'(-2) = -\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\varphi'(-2) = -6$
- $\varphi'(-2) = -\dfrac{1}{5}$

## Explanation

For $x = -2$ the equation becomes $y^5 + y = 2$, which has the solution $y = 1$; it is the only one, since $y \mapsto y^5 + y$ is strictly increasing. Let $F(x, y) = y^5 + y + x$, of class $C^1$, with $F_x = 1$ and $F_y = 5y^4 + 1$, so $F_y(-2, 1) = 6 \neq 0$ and the implicit function theorem applies. Therefore
$$
\varphi'(-2) = -\,\frac{F_x(-2, 1)}{F_y(-2, 1)} = -\,\frac{1}{6} .
$$
