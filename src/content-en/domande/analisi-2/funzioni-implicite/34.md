Let $\varphi$ be the function implicitly defined, near the point $(1, 0)$, by the equation
$$
\int_0^{y} e^{t^2}\,dt = x^2 - 1 .
$$
What is $\varphi'(1)$?

- $\varphi'(1) = -2$
- $\varphi'(1) = 2$
- $\varphi'(1) = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\varphi'(1) = 2e$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = \displaystyle\int_0^{y} e^{t^2}\,dt - x^2 + 1$; we have $F(1, 0) = 0 - 1 + 1 = 0$. By the **fundamental theorem of calculus** $F_y = e^{y^2}$, while $F_x = -2x$; both are continuous, so $F$ is of class $C^1$. At the point $F_y(1, 0) = 1 \neq 0$ and $F_x(1, 0) = -2$, therefore the implicit function theorem applies and
$$
\varphi'(1) = -\,\frac{-2}{1} = 2 .
$$
