The equation $\sin(xz) + yz = 2$ implicitly defines a function $z = g(x, y)$ near the point $(0, 2, 1)$. What is $g_x(0, 2)$?

- $g_x(0, 2) = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $g_x(0, 2) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $g_x(0, 2) = -2$
- $g_x(0, 2) = 0$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = \sin(xz) + yz - 2$, which is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$; we have $F(0, 2, 1) = 0 + 2 - 2 = 0$. By the chain rule $F_x = z \cos(xz)$ and $F_z = x \cos(xz) + y$, so at the point $F_x = 1$ and $F_z = 2 \neq 0$. Therefore the **implicit function theorem in three variables** applies and
$$
g_x(0, 2) = -\,\frac{F_x(0, 2, 1)}{F_z(0, 2, 1)} = -\,\frac{1}{2} .
$$
