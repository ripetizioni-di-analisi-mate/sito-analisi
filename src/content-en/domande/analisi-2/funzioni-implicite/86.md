The system
$$
\begin{cases}
x = r \cos\theta \\[4pt]
y = r \sin\theta
\end{cases}
$$
relates the Cartesian coordinates $(x, y)$ to the polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$. At which points $(x, y, r, \theta)$ satisfying it does the implicit function theorem for systems guarantee that $r$ and $\theta$ are, locally, functions of class $C^1$ of $(x, y)$?

- At all points with $\cos\theta \neq 0$
- At all points
- At all points with $r \neq 0$
- Only at points with $\theta \neq 0$

## Explanation

Let $F = x - r\cos\theta$ and $G = y - r\sin\theta$. The Jacobian determinant with respect to the dependent variables $(r, \theta)$ is
$$
\det \begin{pmatrix} -\cos\theta & r\sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & -r\cos\theta \end{pmatrix} = r\cos^2\theta + r\sin^2\theta = r .
$$
Therefore the theorem applies at all points with $r \neq 0$. For $r = 0$ the point is the origin, where the angle $\theta$ is not determined by $(x, y)$.
