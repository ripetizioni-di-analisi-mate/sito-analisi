The curve $x^2 + y^2 + kxy = 2 + k$ passes through the point $(1, 1)$ for every $k \in \mathbb{R}$. For which values of $k$ are the hypotheses of Dini's theorem with respect to $y$ satisfied at the point $(1, 1)$?

- $k \neq 2$
- $k \neq -2$
- $k \neq 0$
- $k > -2$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 + kxy - 2 - k$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(1, 1) = 2 + k - 2 - k = 0$ for every $k$. The derivative with respect to $y$ is $F_y = 2y + kx$, so $F_y(1, 1) = 2 + k$. The hypotheses of Dini's theorem are therefore **satisfied** if and only if $k \neq -2$. For $k = -2$ the equation becomes $(x - y)^2 = 0$, that is, the line $y = x$, which is nevertheless the graph of a function of $x$.
