The equation $e^{y} + y = 1 + x^3$ defines, for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, a unique function $y = \varphi(x)$, with $\varphi(0) = 0$. Which statement about $x_0 = 0$ is true?

- $x_0 = 0$ is a local maximum point of $\varphi$
- $x_0 = 0$ is a stationary point of $\varphi$ but neither a local maximum nor a local minimum
- $x_0 = 0$ is a local minimum point of $\varphi$
- $x_0 = 0$ is not a stationary point of $\varphi$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = e^{y} + y - 1 - x^3$, with $F_y = e^{y} + 1 > 0$ and $F_x = -3x^2$. The derivative of the implicit function is
$$
\varphi'(x) = \frac{3x^2}{e^{\varphi(x)} + 1} ,
$$
which vanishes only at $x = 0$, so $x_0 = 0$ is a stationary point. Also $\varphi''(0) = -\dfrac{F_{xx}(0, 0)}{F_y(0, 0)} = 0$, therefore the second derivative is inconclusive. Since $\varphi'(x) > 0$ for every $x \neq 0$, the function $\varphi$ is **strictly increasing**, so $x_0 = 0$ is neither a local maximum nor a local minimum.
