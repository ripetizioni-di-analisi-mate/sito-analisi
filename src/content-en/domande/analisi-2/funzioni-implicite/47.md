Which of the following points of the curve $x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2 = 2$ is a point where the implicit function $y = \varphi(x)$ has a local maximum?

- $(1, -1)$
- $(\sqrt{2}, 0)$
- $\left(0, \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\right)$
- $(-1, 1)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2 - 2$, with $F_x = 2x + 2y$ and $F_y = 2x + 6y$. The stationary points of $\varphi$ are the points of the curve with $F_x = 0$, that is, $y = -x$; substituting gives $2x^2 = 2$, so the points are $(1, -1)$ and $(-1, 1)$, while at $(\sqrt{2}, 0)$ and at $\left(0, \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\right)$ we have $F_x \neq 0$. At $(-1, 1)$ we have $F_y = 4$ and $F_{xx} = 2$, so $\varphi''(-1) = -\dfrac{2}{4} = -\dfrac{1}{2} < 0$ and the point is a **local maximum**. At $(1, -1)$ we have $F_y = -4$, so $\varphi''(1) = \dfrac{1}{2} > 0$ and the point is a local minimum.
