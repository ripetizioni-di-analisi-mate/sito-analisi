- $\varphi'(1) = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\varphi'(1) = -2$
- $\varphi'(1) = -1$
- $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x\,e^{y} + y - 1$, which is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. We have $F(1, 0) = 1 \cdot e^{0} + 0 - 1 = 0$. The partial derivatives are $F_x = e^{y}$ and $F_y = x\,e^{y} + 1$, so $F_x(1, 0) = 1$ and $F_y(1, 0) = 2 \neq 0$. Therefore the **implicit function theorem** applies and
$$
\varphi'(1) = -\,\frac{F_x(1, 0)}{F_y(1, 0)} = -\,\frac{1}{2} .
$$
