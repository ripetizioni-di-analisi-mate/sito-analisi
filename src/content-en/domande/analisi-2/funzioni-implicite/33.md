For $x > 0$ and $y > 0$ the equation $x^y = y^x$ is satisfied at the point $(2, 4)$ and defines near it a function $y = \varphi(x)$. What is $\varphi'(2)$?

- $\varphi'(2) = \dfrac{1 - 2\ln 2}{4(1 - \ln 2)}$
- $\varphi'(2) = \dfrac{4(1 - \ln 2)}{2\ln 2 - 1}$
- $\varphi'(2) = \dfrac{2(1 - \ln 2)}{1 - 2\ln 2}$
- $\varphi'(2) = \dfrac{4(1 - \ln 2)}{1 - 2\ln 2}$

## Explanation

Since $x$ and $y$ are positive, the equation is equivalent to $y \ln x = x \ln y$; let $F(x, y) = y \ln x - x \ln y$, of class $C^1$ for $x > 0$, $y > 0$, with $F(2, 4) = 4 \ln 2 - 2 \ln 4 = 0$. The partial derivatives are $F_x = \dfrac{y}{x} - \ln y$ and $F_y = \ln x - \dfrac{x}{y}$, so $F_x(2, 4) = 2 - 2\ln 2$ and $F_y(2, 4) = \ln 2 - \dfrac{1}{2} \neq 0$. Therefore
$$
\varphi'(2) = -\,\frac{2 - 2\ln 2}{\ln 2 - \frac{1}{2}} = \frac{4(1 - \ln 2)}{1 - 2\ln 2} ,
$$
which is negative, since $1 - 2\ln 2 < 0$.
