Near the point $(1, 1)$ the curve $y^3 + xy + x^2 = 3$ is the graph of a function $y = \varphi(x)$. Setting $h(x) = \varphi(x^2)$, what is $h'(1)$?

- $h'(1) = -\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $h'(1) = -\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $h'(1) = \dfrac{3}{2}$
- $h'(1) = -\dfrac{3}{8}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = y^3 + xy + x^2 - 3$, with $F(1, 1) = 0$, $F_x = y + 2x$ and $F_y = 3y^2 + x$. At the point $F_y(1, 1) = 4 \neq 0$ and $F_x(1, 1) = 3$, so the implicit function theorem applies and $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{3}{4}$. By the chain rule $h'(x) = 2x\,\varphi'(x^2)$, and for $x = 1$ we have $x^2 = 1$, therefore
$$
h'(1) = 2 \cdot \left(-\frac{3}{4}\right) = -\,\frac{3}{2} .
$$
