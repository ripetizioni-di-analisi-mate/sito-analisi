At which of the following points of the surface $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2z = 0$ does Dini's theorem in three variables *not* apply with respect to the variable $z$?

- $(1, 0, 1)$
- $(0, 0, 0)$
- $(0, 0, 2)$
- $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, 0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2z$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$; the four points lie on the surface, since $F = 0$ at each of them. Dini's theorem with respect to $z$ applies at the points where $F_z = 2z - 2 \neq 0$, that is, $z \neq 1$. At the points $(0, 0, 0)$, $(0, 0, 2)$ and $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, 0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ the derivative $F_z$ equals $-2$, $2$ and $-1$. At the point $(1, 0, 1)$, instead, $F_z = 0$, so the theorem with respect to $z$ **does not apply**. Since $F_x = 2 \neq 0$ at that point, near it the surface is the graph of a function $x = h(y, z)$, and the tangent plane is $x = 1$.
