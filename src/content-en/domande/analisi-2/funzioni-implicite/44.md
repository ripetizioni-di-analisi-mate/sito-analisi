The equation $e^{y} + y - x^2 = 1$ implicitly defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ near the point $(0, 0)$. Which statement is true?

- $\varphi'(0) = 1$, so $x = 0$ is not a stationary point of $\varphi$
- $x = 0$ is a local maximum point of $\varphi$
- $\varphi'(0) = 0$ and $\varphi''(0) = 0$, so the nature of the point cannot be deduced from the second derivative
- $x = 0$ is a local minimum point of $\varphi$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = e^{y} + y - x^2 - 1$, of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(0, 0) = 1 + 0 - 0 - 1 = 0$ and $F_y(0, 0) = e^{y} + 1 \,\big|_{(0,0)} = 2 \neq 0$, so Dini's theorem applies. Since $F_x = -2x$ vanishes at $(0, 0)$, we have $\varphi'(0) = 0$ and $x = 0$ is a stationary point of $\varphi$. At a stationary point the second derivative equals
$$
\varphi''(0) = -\,\frac{F_{xx}(0, 0)}{F_y(0, 0)} = -\,\frac{-2}{2} = 1 .
$$
Since $\varphi''(0) > 0$, the point $x = 0$ is a **local minimum point** of $\varphi$.
