What is the Taylor polynomial of order $2$, centered at $x_0 = 0$, of the function $y = \varphi(x)$ defined near the origin by the equation $y + \sin y = x^2$?

- $x^2$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{x^2}{2}$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{4}$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = y + \sin y - x^2$, of class $C^2$, with $F(0, 0) = 0$ and $F_y = 1 + \cos y$, so $F_y(0, 0) = 2 \neq 0$ and the implicit function theorem applies. Since $F_x = -2x$ vanishes at $0$, we have $\varphi(0) = 0$ and $\varphi'(0) = 0$. At a stationary point
$$
\varphi''(0) = -\,\frac{F_{xx}(0, 0)}{F_y(0, 0)} = -\,\frac{-2}{2} = 1 .
$$
Therefore the Taylor polynomial of order $2$ is $\varphi(0) + \varphi'(0)\,x + \dfrac{\varphi''(0)}{2}\,x^2 = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$.
