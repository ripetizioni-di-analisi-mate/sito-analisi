For the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 3 \\[4pt]
z = 1
\end{cases}
$$
at the point $(1, 1, 1)$, which pair of variables *cannot* be chosen as the pair of dependent variables when applying Dini's theorem for systems?

- $(y, z)$
- $(x, z)$
- All three pairs can be chosen
- $(x, y)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 3$ and $G(x, y, z) = z - 1$; at the point $(1, 1, 1)$ we have $\nabla F = (2, 2, 2)$ and $\nabla G = (0, 0, 1)$. The Jacobian determinants at the point are
$$
\frac{\partial(F, G)}{\partial(x, y)} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = 0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial(F, G)}{\partial(y, z)} = \frac{\partial(F, G)}{\partial(x, z)} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 2 .
$$
Hence the pair $(x, y)$ **cannot be chosen**, while the other two can. This agrees with the fact that along the intersection curve the variable $z$ is constant, so $x$ and $y$ are not functions of $z$.
