At which points of the ellipsoid $x^2 + 2y^2 + z^2 = 4$ is the tangent plane parallel to the plane $x + 2y + z = 0$?

- Only at $(1, 1, 1)$
- $(1, 1, 1)$ and $(-1, -1, -1)$
- $\pm\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{5}, \dfrac{2\sqrt{10}}{5}, \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{5}\right)$
- $(0, \sqrt{2}, 0)$ and $(0, -\sqrt{2}, 0)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + 2y^2 + z^2 - 4$, with $\nabla F = (2x, 4y, 2z)$. The tangent plane is parallel to the given plane if and only if the gradient is parallel to $(1, 2, 1)$, that is, $(2x, 4y, 2z) = \lambda\,(1, 2, 1)$ for some $\lambda \neq 0$, which gives $x = y = z = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}$. Substituting into the equation of the ellipsoid gives $4x^2 = 4$, so $x = \pm 1$. Therefore the points are $(1, 1, 1)$ and $(-1, -1, -1)$, where the tangent planes are $x + 2y + z = 4$ and $x + 2y + z = -4$.
