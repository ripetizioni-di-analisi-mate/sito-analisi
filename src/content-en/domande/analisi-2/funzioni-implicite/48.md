The function $\varphi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ assigns to every $x$ the unique real solution $y$ of the equation $y^3 + y = x$, that is, it is the inverse of $y \mapsto y^3 + y$. On which set is $\varphi$ convex?

- $[0, +\infty)$
- $\mathbb{R}$
- $(-\infty, 0]$
- $[-1, 1]$

## Explanation

Differentiating the identity $y^3 + y = x$ gives $(3y^2 + 1)\,y' = 1$, so $y' = \dfrac{1}{3y^2 + 1}$. Differentiating again gives
$$
y'' = -\,\frac{6y\,y'}{(3y^2 + 1)^2} = -\,\frac{6y}{(3y^2 + 1)^3} ,
$$
which is positive if and only if $y < 0$. Since $\varphi$ is strictly increasing and $\varphi(0) = 0$, the condition $\varphi(x) < 0$ is equivalent to $x < 0$. Therefore $\varphi$ is **convex** on $(-\infty, 0]$ and concave on $[0, +\infty)$.
