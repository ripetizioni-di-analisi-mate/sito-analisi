The system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1 \\[4pt]
x + y + z = 0
\end{cases}
$$
describes a circle. At which of its points does the implicit function theorem for systems *not* apply with $(y, z)$ as dependent variables?

- $\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}, \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}, \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}\right)$
- $\left(0, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$ and $\left(0, -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$
- $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, 0\right)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, 0\right)$
- The theorem applies at every point of the circle

## Explanation

Let $F = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 1$ and $G = x + y + z$. The Jacobian determinant with respect to $(y, z)$ is
$$
\det \begin{pmatrix} 2y & 2z \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 2(y - z) ,
$$
which vanishes if and only if $y = z$. From the second equation it follows that $x = -2y$, and substituting into the first gives $6y^2 = 1$, so $y = \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}$. Therefore the points are $\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}, \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}, \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{6}\right)$, where the $x$-coordinate on the circle is minimal and maximal. At the points with $y = -z$, on the other hand, $2(y - z) = 4y \neq 0$.
