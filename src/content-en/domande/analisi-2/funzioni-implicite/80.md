For the system
$$
\begin{cases}
xy + z^2 = 2 \\[4pt]
x + y + z = 3
\end{cases}
$$
what is the Jacobian determinant $\dfrac{\partial(F, G)}{\partial(y, z)}$ at the point $(1, 1, 1)$, with $F = xy + z^2 - 2$ and $G = x + y + z - 3$?

- $1$
- $-1$
- $3$
- $-3$

## Explanation

The point $(1, 1, 1)$ solves the system, since $1 + 1 = 2$ and $1 + 1 + 1 = 3$. The partial derivatives with respect to $y$ and $z$ are $F_y = x$, $F_z = 2z$, $G_y = 1$ and $G_z = 1$. Therefore
$$
\frac{\partial(F, G)}{\partial(y, z)}(1, 1, 1) = \det \begin{pmatrix} x & 2z \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}_{(1,1,1)} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 1 - 2 = -1 .
$$
Since the determinant is nonzero, near the point the system defines $y = y(x)$ and $z = z(x)$.
