Consider $F(x, y) = y^3 - x^3$ near the origin. Which statement is true?

- The hypotheses of Dini's theorem are satisfied and $\varphi'(0) = 1$
- The hypotheses of Dini's theorem are not satisfied, so the equation $F(x, y) = 0$ defines no function $y = \varphi(x)$ near the origin
- The hypotheses of Dini's theorem are not satisfied, but the zero set is the graph of $y = x$
- The zero set is the union of the lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $F_x = -3x^2$ and $F_y = 3y^2$, so $F_y(0, 0) = 0$ and the hypotheses of Dini's theorem are not satisfied. On the other hand $y^3 - x^3 = (y - x)(y^2 + xy + x^2)$, and the second factor vanishes only at $(0, 0)$, since
$$
y^2 + xy + x^2 = \left( y + \frac{x}{2} \right)^2 + \frac{3}{4}\,x^2 .
$$
Therefore the zero set is the line $y = x$, that is, the **graph** of the function $\varphi(x) = x$. The condition $F_y \neq 0$ is *sufficient* for the existence of the implicit function, and this case shows that it is not *necessary*.
