Which vector is tangent at the point $(1, 2, 1)$ to the curve of intersection of the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 6$ with the plane $x + y = 3$?

- $(1, -1, 1)$
- $(1, 2, 1)$
- $(1, 1, 0)$
- $(1, 1, -1)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 6$ and $G(x, y, z) = x + y - 3$; the point lies on both surfaces, and $\nabla F(1, 2, 1) = (2, 4, 2)$, $\nabla G = (1, 1, 0)$. The intersection curve is contained in both surfaces, so its tangent vector is orthogonal to both gradients, and a direction of it is given by the **cross product**
$$
\nabla F \times \nabla G = (4 \cdot 0 - 2 \cdot 1,\ 2 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 0,\ 2 \cdot 1 - 4 \cdot 1) = (-2, 2, -2) .
$$
Therefore $(1, -1, 1)$, a multiple of $(-2, 2, -2)$, is tangent to the curve. The vector $(1, 1, -1)$ is not, since $(1, 1, -1) \cdot (2, 4, 2) = 4 \neq 0$.
