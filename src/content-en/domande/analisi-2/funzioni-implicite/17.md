For $F(x, y) = x^2 - y^2 + x^3$, which are the points of the curve $F(x, y) = 0$ at which the gradient of $F$ vanishes?

- Only $(0, 0)$
- $(0, 0)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}, 0\right)$
- Only $\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}, 0\right)$
- Only $(-1, 0)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $F_x = 2x + 3x^2$ and $F_y = -2y$. The gradient vanishes if $y = 0$ and $x\,(2 + 3x) = 0$, that is, at the points $(0, 0)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}, 0\right)$. The first lies on the curve, since $F(0, 0) = 0$; the second does not, since $F\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}, 0\right) = \dfrac{4}{9} - \dfrac{8}{27} = \dfrac{4}{27} \neq 0$. The point $(-1, 0)$ lies on the curve, but $F_x = 1 \neq 0$ there. Therefore the only **singular** point of the curve is $(0, 0)$.
