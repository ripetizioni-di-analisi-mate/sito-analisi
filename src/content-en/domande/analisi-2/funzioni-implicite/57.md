At which points of the hyperbola does the tangent line pass through $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, 0\right)$?

- $(\sqrt{2}, 1)$ and $(\sqrt{2}, -1)$
- $(2, \sqrt{3})$ and $(2, -\sqrt{3})$
- $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$
- $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 - y^2 - 1$, with $\nabla F = (2x, -2y)$. At the point $(a, b)$ of the hyperbola the tangent line is $2a(x - a) - 2b(y - b) = 0$, which, since $a^2 - b^2 = 1$, can be written $a x - b y = 1$. The line passes through $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, 0\right)$ if and only if $\dfrac{a}{2} = 1$, that is, $a = 2$. From the equation of the curve it follows that $b^2 = a^2 - 1 = 3$, so the points are $(2, \sqrt{3})$ and $(2, -\sqrt{3})$.
