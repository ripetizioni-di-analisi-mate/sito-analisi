Consider the curve $x^2 + y^2 - 2y = 0$. At which of the following points does Dini's theorem guarantee that, near the point, the curve is the graph of a function $y = \varphi(x)$?

- $(1, 1)$
- $(-1, 1)$
- $(0, 0)$
- $(1, 0)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2y$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, with $F_y = 2y - 2$. Dini's theorem with respect to $y$ requires the point to lie on the curve and $F_y \neq 0$ at that point. The point $(1, 0)$ does not lie on the curve, since $F(1, 0) = 1 \neq 0$. The points $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, 1)$ lie on the curve, but $F_y = 0$ there, so the theorem does not apply. At the point $(0, 0)$ we have $F(0, 0) = 0$ and $F_y(0, 0) = -2 \neq 0$, so the hypotheses are **satisfied**.
