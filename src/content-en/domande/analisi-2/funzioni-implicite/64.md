A function $F$ of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ vanishes at the point $P_0$, where the three partial derivatives $F_x$, $F_y$, $F_z$ are nonzero. Near $P_0$ the equation $F(x, y, z) = 0$ therefore defines three functions, $z = g(x, y)$, $x = h(y, z)$ and $y = k(x, z)$. What is the product $g_x \, h_y \, k_z$ evaluated at $P_0$?

- $-1$
- $1$
- $0$
- It depends on $F$ and on the point $P_0$

## Explanation

Since $F_z(P_0) \neq 0$, $F_x(P_0) \neq 0$ and $F_y(P_0) \neq 0$, the implicit function theorem in three variables applies with respect to each of the three variables. By the formulas of the theorem, with the roles of the variables exchanged, we have
$$
g_x = -\,\frac{F_x}{F_z}, \qquad h_y = -\,\frac{F_y}{F_x}, \qquad k_z = -\,\frac{F_z}{F_y} ,
$$
all evaluated at $P_0$. In the product each partial derivative appears once in the numerator and once in the denominator, so it cancels, and the product of the three minus signs equals $-1$. Therefore $g_x \, h_y \, k_z = -1$ for every $F$ and at every point where the hypotheses are satisfied.
