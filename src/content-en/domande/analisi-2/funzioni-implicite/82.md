The linear system
$$
\begin{cases}
2y + z = x \\[4pt]
y - z = 2x
\end{cases}
$$
defines $y$ and $z$ as functions of $x$. What are $y'(x)$ and $z'(x)$?

- $y'(x) = -1$, $z'(x) = 1$
- $y'(x) = 1$, $z'(x) = 1$
- $y'(x) = 1$, $z'(x) = -1$
- $y'(x) = \dfrac{1}{3}$, $z'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

The Jacobian determinant with respect to $(y, z)$ is $\det \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -3 \neq 0$, so the system defines $y$ and $z$ as functions of $x$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$. Adding the two equations gives $3y = 3x$, that is, $y = x$, and from the second equation $z = y - 2x = -x$. Therefore
$$
y'(x) = 1, \qquad z'(x) = -1
$$
for every $x$.
