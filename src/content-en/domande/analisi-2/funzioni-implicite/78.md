Along which set does the tangent plane to the surface $z = xy$ at the origin intersect the surface itself?

- Only at the point $(0, 0, 0)$
- Along the line $y = x$, $z = 0$
- Along the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, $z = 0$
- Along the two coordinate axes $x$ and $y$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = xy - z$, with $F(0, 0, 0) = 0$ and $\nabla F = (y, x, -1)$, so $\nabla F(0, 0, 0) = (0, 0, -1)$. The tangent plane at the origin is therefore $-(z - 0) = 0$, that is, the plane $z = 0$. The points of the surface lying on this plane satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
z = xy \\[4pt]
z = 0 ,
\end{cases}
$$
so $xy = 0$, that is, $x = 0$ or $y = 0$. The intersection therefore consists of the two coordinate axes. At the origin the surface lies partly above and partly below its own tangent plane, as happens at a **saddle point**.
