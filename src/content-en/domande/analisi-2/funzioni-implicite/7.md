- Local minimum point, with $\varphi''(1) = 3$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = y^3 + 3y - x^2 + 2x - 5$, which is of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. We have $F(1, 1) = 0$ and $F_y(1, 1) = 3y^2 + 3 \,\big|_{(1,1)} = 6 \neq 0$, so the **implicit function theorem** applies. Since $F_x = -2x + 2$ vanishes at $(1, 1)$, we have $\varphi'(1) = 0$ and $x_0 = 1$ is a stationary point of $\varphi$. At a stationary point the second derivative equals
$$
\varphi''(1) = -\,\frac{F_{xx}(1, 1)}{F_y(1, 1)} = -\,\frac{-2}{6} = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
Since $\varphi''(1) > 0$, the point $x_0 = 1$ is a **local minimum point** of $\varphi$.
