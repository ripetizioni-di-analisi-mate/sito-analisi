What can be said about the zero set of $F(x, y) = x^2 - y^2$ near the origin?

- It is the union of the lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$, and near the origin it is not the graph of any function $y = \varphi(x)$
- It is the line $y = x$, the graph of the function $\varphi(x) = x$
- It is the graph of the function $\varphi(x) = |x|$
- It consists of the single point $(0, 0)$

## Explanation

Since $x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y)$, the zero set is the union of the lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$. Every neighborhood of the origin contains, for the same value $x \neq 0$, the two points $(x, x)$ and $(x, -x)$ of the set, so near the origin the set **is not the graph** of a function of $x$. The graph of $|x|$ is contained in the zero set, but does not cover all of it. Consistently with this, $\nabla F(0, 0) = (2x, -2y) \,\big|_{(0,0)} = (0, 0)$, so the origin is a singular point of the curve.
