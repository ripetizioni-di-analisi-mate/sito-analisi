The equation $x^2 + xy - y^2 = 1$ implicitly defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ near the point $(1, 1)$. What is $\varphi''(1)$?

- $\varphi''(1) = 10$
- $\varphi''(1) = 8$
- $\varphi''(1) = -13$
- $\varphi''(1) = -10$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + xy - y^2 - 1$, which is of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$; we have $F(1, 1) = 0$ and $F_y(1, 1) = x - 2y \,\big|_{(1,1)} = -1 \neq 0$, so **Dini's theorem** applies. Differentiating the identity with respect to $x$, with $y = \varphi(x)$, we obtain $2x + y + x\,y' - 2y\,y' = 0$; at the point $(1, 1)$ this gives $3 - y' = 0$, so $\varphi'(1) = 3$. Differentiating again we obtain
$$
2 + 2y' + x\,y'' - 2(y')^2 - 2y\,y'' = 0 .
$$
At the point, with $y' = 3$, we have $2 + 6 + y'' - 18 - 2y'' = 0$, that is, $-10 - y'' = 0$. Hence $\varphi''(1) = -10$.
