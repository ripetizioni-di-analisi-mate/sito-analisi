For the curve $x^2 + y^3 = 2$, with $y = \varphi(x)$, differentiating the identity with respect to $x$ gives $2x + 3y^2\,y' = 0$. Which equality is obtained by differentiating once more with respect to $x$?

- $2 + 6y\,(y')^2 + 3y^2\,y'' = 0$
- $2 + 3y^2\,y'' = 0$
- $2 + 6y\,y' + 3y^2\,y'' = 0$
- $2 + 6y\,(y')^2 = 0$

## Explanation

The term $3y^2\,y'$ is the product of $3y^2$ and $y'$, both functions of $x$, so it is differentiated with the **product rule**. The derivative of $3y^2$ with respect to $x$ is $6y\,y'$, by the chain rule, and the derivative of $y'$ is $y''$. Therefore the derivative of $3y^2\,y'$ is $6y\,y' \cdot y' + 3y^2\,y'' = 6y\,(y')^2 + 3y^2\,y''$, and differentiating the identity gives
$$
2 + 6y\,(y')^2 + 3y^2\,y'' = 0 .
$$
