The system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 2
\end{cases}
$$
implicitly defines two functions $y = y(x)$ and $z = z(x)$ near the point $(1, 0, -1)$. What are $y'(1)$ and $z'(1)$?

- $y'(1) = 2$, $z'(1) = -1$
- $y'(1) = -1$, $z'(1) = 0$
- $y'(1) = -2$, $z'(1) = 1$
- $y'(1) = 0$, $z'(1) = -1$

## Explanation

The functions $F(x, y, z) = x + y + z$ and $G(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2$ are polynomials, hence of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, and the point $(1, 0, -1)$ solves the system. The Jacobian determinant with respect to $(y, z)$ at the point is
$$
\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2y & 2z \end{pmatrix}_{(1, 0, -1)} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix} = -2 \neq 0 ,
$$
so **Dini's theorem for systems** applies. Differentiating the two equations with respect to $x$ we obtain $1 + y' + z' = 0$ and $2x + 2y\,y' + 2z\,z' = 0$; at the point the second equation becomes $2 - 2z'(1) = 0$. Therefore $z'(1) = 1$ and, from the first equation, $y'(1) = -1 - z'(1) = -2$.
