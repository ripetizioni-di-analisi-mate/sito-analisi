For every $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ let $g(x, y)$ denote the unique real solution $z$ of the equation $z^3 + z = x + y$. Which statement about the partial derivatives of $g$ is true?

- $g_x = g_y = 1$ at every point
- $g_x = -g_y = \dfrac{1}{3z^2 + 1}$ at every point
- $g_x = g_y = -\dfrac{1}{3z^2 + 1}$ at every point
- $g_x = g_y = \dfrac{1}{3z^2 + 1}$ at every point

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = z^3 + z - x - y$, with $F_z = 3z^2 + 1 > 0$, so the implicit function theorem applies at every point. The partial derivatives $F_x$ and $F_y$ are both equal to $-1$, therefore
$$
g_x = -\,\frac{F_x}{F_z} = \frac{1}{3z^2 + 1}
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
g_y = -\,\frac{F_y}{F_z} = \frac{1}{3z^2 + 1} ,
$$
with $z = g(x, y)$. The two derivatives are equal because $g$ depends on $x$ and $y$ only through the sum $x + y$.
