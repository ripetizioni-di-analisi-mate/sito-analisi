Let $\Phi(u, v)$ be a function of class $C^1$, and let $z = g(x, y)$ be the function implicitly defined by the equation
$$
\Phi(x - 2z,\; y - 3z) = 0
$$
near a point of the surface where $2\,\Phi_u + 3\,\Phi_v \neq 0$. Which relation between the partial derivatives of $g$ holds near the point, whatever $\Phi$ is?

- $3\,g_x + 2\,g_y = 1$
- $2\,g_x + 3\,g_y = 0$
- $2\,g_x + 3\,g_y = 1$
- $g_x + g_y = 1$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y, z) = \Phi(x - 2z, y - 3z)$. By the chain rule $F_x = \Phi_u$, $F_y = \Phi_v$ and $F_z = -2\,\Phi_u - 3\,\Phi_v$, so the hypothesis $2\,\Phi_u + 3\,\Phi_v \neq 0$ is equivalent to $F_z \neq 0$ and the implicit function theorem applies with respect to $z$. The partial derivatives of $g$ are therefore
$$
g_x = -\,\frac{F_x}{F_z} = \frac{\Phi_u}{2\,\Phi_u + 3\,\Phi_v},
\qquad
g_y = -\,\frac{F_y}{F_z} = \frac{\Phi_v}{2\,\Phi_u + 3\,\Phi_v} .
$$
Then $2\,g_x + 3\,g_y = \dfrac{2\,\Phi_u + 3\,\Phi_v}{2\,\Phi_u + 3\,\Phi_v} = 1$. The result has a geometric meaning: if $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ lies on the surface, so does every point $(x_0 + 2t, y_0 + 3t, z_0 + t)$, hence the surface contains lines parallel to the vector $(2, 3, 1)$.
