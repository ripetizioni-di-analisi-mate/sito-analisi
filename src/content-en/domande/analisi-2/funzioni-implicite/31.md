The curve $x^2 + 3xy - y^2 = 3$ meets the line $y = x$ at the points $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, -1)$. At each of the two points, what is the derivative of the implicit function $y = \varphi(x)$ defined near the point?

- $-5$ at both points
- $-5$ at $(1, 1)$ and $5$ at $(-1, -1)$
- $-\dfrac{1}{5}$ at both points
- $5$ at both points

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 + 3xy - y^2 - 3$, with $F_x = 2x + 3y$ and $F_y = 3x - 2y$. At the points of the line $y = x$ we have $F_x = 5x$ and $F_y = x$, which is nonzero for $x = \pm 1$, so the implicit function theorem applies at both points. Therefore
$$
\varphi'(x) = -\,\frac{5x}{x} = -5
$$
both at $(1, 1)$ and at $(-1, -1)$. The value is the same because $F_x$ and $F_y$ are homogeneous of degree one, so their ratio is constant along every line through the origin.
