At which points of the curve $x^2 - 2xy + 2y^2 = 4$ is the tangent line vertical?

- $(2, 2)$ and $(-2, -2)$
- $(\sqrt{2}, 2\sqrt{2})$ and $(-\sqrt{2}, -2\sqrt{2})$
- $(2\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2})$ and $(-2\sqrt{2}, -\sqrt{2})$
- $(2, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$

## Explanation

Let $F(x, y) = x^2 - 2xy + 2y^2 - 4$, of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, with $F_x = 2x - 2y$ and $F_y = -2x + 4y$. The tangent line $F_x\,(x - x_0) + F_y\,(y - y_0) = 0$ is vertical at the points of the curve where $F_y = 0$ and $F_x \neq 0$. The condition $-2x + 4y = 0$ gives $x = 2y$, and substituting into the equation of the curve we obtain $4y^2 - 4y^2 + 2y^2 = 4$, so $y = \pm\sqrt{2}$. The points are therefore $(2\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2})$ and $(-2\sqrt{2}, -\sqrt{2})$, where $F_x = 2y$ equals $\pm 2\sqrt{2} \neq 0$.
