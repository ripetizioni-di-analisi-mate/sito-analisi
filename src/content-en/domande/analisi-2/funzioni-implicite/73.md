The sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 3$ and the surface $xyz = 1$ both pass through the point $(1, 1, 1)$. Which statement is true?

- At the point the two surfaces intersect at a right angle
- At the point the two surfaces have the same tangent plane $x + y + z = 3$
- At the point the tangent planes are $x + y + z = 3$ and $x + y + z = 1$
- At the point the tangent planes form an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

For the sphere let $F(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 3$, with $\nabla F(1, 1, 1) = (2, 2, 2)$; for the other surface let $G(x, y, z) = xyz - 1$, with $\nabla G = (yz, xz, xy)$, so $\nabla G(1, 1, 1) = (1, 1, 1)$. The two gradients are parallel, therefore the tangent planes at the point have the same normal vector. Since both pass through $(1, 1, 1)$, they coincide with the plane $(x - 1) + (y - 1) + (z - 1) = 0$, that is, $x + y + z = 3$, therefore the two surfaces are **tangent** at the point.
