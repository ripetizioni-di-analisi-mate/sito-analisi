The equation $xy + y^3 = 2$ implicitly defines a function $y = \varphi(x)$ near the point $(1, 1)$. Near $x_0 = 1$, where does the graph of $\varphi$ lie with respect to its tangent line at $(1, 1)$?

- Below the tangent line, because $\varphi''(1) = -\dfrac{1}{32} < 0$
- Below the tangent line, because $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{1}{4} < 0$
- It crosses the tangent line, because $\varphi''(1) = 0$
- Above the tangent line, because $\varphi''(1) = \dfrac{1}{32} > 0$

## Explanation

Differentiating the identity gives $y + x\,y' + 3y^2\,y' = 0$, which at $(1, 1)$ gives $1 + 4\,y'(1) = 0$, so $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{1}{4}$. Differentiating again gives $2y' + 6y\,(y')^2 + (x + 3y^2)\,y'' = 0$, which at the point becomes
$$
-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{8} + 4\,y''(1) = 0 ,
$$
therefore $\varphi''(1) = \dfrac{1}{32}$. Since $\varphi''(1) > 0$ and $\varphi''$ is continuous, $\varphi$ is convex near $1$, so its graph lies **above the tangent line**. The sign of $\varphi'(1)$ only says that $\varphi$ is decreasing near $1$.
