Near the origin the equation $z + e^{z} = 1 + x + xy$ has a solution $z = g(x, y)$ with $g(0, 0) = 0$, given by the implicit function theorem. What is $g_{xx}(0, 0)$?

- $g_{xx}(0, 0) = \dfrac{1}{8}$
- $g_{xx}(0, 0) = -\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $g_{xx}(0, 0) = -\dfrac{1}{8}$
- $g_{xx}(0, 0) = 0$

## Explanation

The point satisfies the equation, since $0 + 1 = 1$, and the derivative of the left-hand side with respect to $z$ is $1 + e^{z}$, which equals $2$ at the origin. Differentiating the identity with respect to $x$ gives $(1 + e^{z})\,g_x = 1 + y$, so $g_x(0, 0) = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Differentiating again with respect to $x$ gives
$$
e^{z}\,g_x^2 + (1 + e^{z})\,g_{xx} = 0 ,
$$
which at the origin becomes $\dfrac{1}{4} + 2\,g_{xx}(0, 0) = 0$. Therefore $g_{xx}(0, 0) = -\dfrac{1}{8}$.
