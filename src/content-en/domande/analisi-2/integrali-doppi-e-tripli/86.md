A homogeneous lamina $D$ is bounded by the $x$-axis, by the line $x = 1$ and by the arc of the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$ with $0 \leq x \leq 1$. At which point is its center of mass?

- $\left( \dfrac{2}{5},\ \dfrac{1}{4} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{3}{5},\ \dfrac{3}{4} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2},\ \dfrac{3}{8} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{3}{5},\ \dfrac{3}{8} \right)$

## Explanation

The lamina is homogeneous, so the coordinates of the center of mass are the averages of $x$ and of $y$ over $D$. The area is $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \sqrt{x}\, dx = \frac{2}{3}$. Moreover
$$
\iint_D x\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 x \sqrt{x}\, dx = \frac{2}{5} ,
\qquad
\iint_D y\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 \frac{x}{2}\, dx = \frac{1}{4} .
$$
Dividing by the area gives $\bar{x} = \dfrac{2}{5} \cdot \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\bar{y} = \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{3}{8}$. The point $\left( \dfrac{2}{5}, \dfrac{1}{4} \right)$ reports the two integrals without dividing them by the area.
