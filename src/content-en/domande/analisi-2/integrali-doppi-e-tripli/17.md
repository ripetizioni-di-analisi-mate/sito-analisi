Let $Q = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ and $T = \{ (x, y) : 0 \leq y \leq x \leq 1 \}$. Which of the following equalities is true?

- $\displaystyle\iint_Q x e^{y}\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 x\, dx \cdot \int_0^1 e^{y}\, dy$
- $\displaystyle\iint_T x e^{y}\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 x\, dx \cdot \int_0^1 e^{y}\, dy$
- $\displaystyle\iint_Q \left( x + e^{y} \right) dx\, dy = \int_0^1 x\, dx \cdot \int_0^1 e^{y}\, dy$
- $\displaystyle\iint_Q x e^{xy}\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 x\, dx \cdot \int_0^1 e^{y}\, dy$

## Explanation

The function $x e^{y}$ is the product of $g(x) = x$ and $h(y) = e^{y}$ and the domain $Q$ is a rectangle, so the theorem on **functions with separated variables** applies and the equality for $x e^{y}$ on $Q$ is true. Both of its sides equal $\dfrac{e - 1}{2}$. On the triangle $T$ the inner integral in $y$ runs from $0$ to $x$, therefore the integral equals $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x \left( e^{x} - 1 \right) dx = \frac{1}{2}$, which differs from $\dfrac{e - 1}{2}$. The function $x + e^{y}$ is a sum and the function $x e^{xy}$ is not the product of a function of $x$ and a function of $y$, so for these two functions the theorem does not apply. In fact their integrals over $Q$ equal $e - \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $e - 2$.
