Four double integrals over the unit disk $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1 \}$ are compared with zero. Which of them has a value less than zero?

- $\displaystyle\iint_D \left( x^2 + y^2 - \dfrac{1}{2} \right) dx\, dy$
- $\displaystyle\iint_D x y^3 \, dx\, dy$
- $\displaystyle\iint_D \left( 1 - x^2 \right) dx\, dy$
- $\displaystyle\iint_D \left( x^2 + y^2 - 1 \right) dx\, dy$

## Explanation

On $D$ we have $x^2 + y^2 - 1 \leq 0$, with strict inequality inside the disk, so by **monotonicity** the integral of $x^2 + y^2 - 1$ is negative; in polar coordinates it equals $2\pi \displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \rho^3 - \rho \right) d\rho = -\frac{\pi}{2}$. The function $1 - x^2$ is positive inside $D$, therefore its integral is positive. The function $x y^3$ is odd in $x$ and the disk is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis, so its integral is zero. Finally the function $x^2 + y^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}$ changes sign on the disk and in polar coordinates its integral equals $2\pi \displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \rho^3 - \frac{\rho}{2} \right) d\rho = 0$.
