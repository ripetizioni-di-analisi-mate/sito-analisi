The trapezoid $D = \{ (x, y) : x \geq 0, \ y \geq 0, \ 1 \leq x + y \leq 4 \}$ lies between the lines $x + y = 1$ and $x + y = 4$ in the first quadrant. Using the variables $u = x + y$, $v = y$, what is the value of $\displaystyle\iint_D \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + y}}\, dx\, dy$?

- $\dfrac{14}{3}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{7}{3}$
- $\dfrac{28}{3}$

## Explanation

The inverse transformation is $x = u - v$, $y = v$, with Jacobian $\det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 1$, so $dx\, dy = du\, dv$. The condition $1 \leq x + y \leq 4$ becomes $1 \leq u \leq 4$, while $y \geq 0$ and $x \geq 0$ become $v \geq 0$ and $v \leq u$, therefore $D$ becomes the trapezoid $1 \leq u \leq 4$, $0 \leq v \leq u$. By the **change of variables**
$$
\iint_D \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + y}}\, dx\, dy = \int_1^4 \left( \int_0^u \frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}\, dv \right) du = \int_1^4 \sqrt{u}\, du = \frac{2}{3} (8 - 1) = \frac{14}{3} .
$$
The value $2$ forgets the length $u$ of the interval of $v$, while $\dfrac{7}{3}$ and $\dfrac{28}{3}$ use $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $2$ as the Jacobian.
