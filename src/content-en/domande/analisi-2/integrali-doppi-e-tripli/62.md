What is the area of the region enclosed by the cardioid with polar equation $\rho = 1 + \cos\theta$, that is, $D = \{ 0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi, \ 0 \leq \rho \leq 1 + \cos\theta \}$?

::grafico{xmin=-0.7 xmax=2.4 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 curve="(1+cos(t))*cos(t),(1+cos(t))*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="2,0;0,1;0,-1" etichette="2.05,0.18,(2, 0);-0.66,1.12,(0, 1);-0.66,-1.22,(0, −1)" titolo="The cardioid $\rho = 1 + \cos\theta$"}

- $\pi$
- $3\pi$
- $2\pi$
- $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The area is the integral of $1$ over $D$, and in **polar coordinates** the area element is $\rho\, d\rho\, d\theta$:
$$
\operatorname{Area}(D) = \int_0^{2\pi} \left( \int_0^{1 + \cos\theta} \rho \, d\rho \right) d\theta = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2\pi} \left( 1 + 2\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta \right) d\theta .
$$
Over a period the integral of $\cos\theta$ is zero and that of $\cos^2\theta$ equals $\pi$, so the area is $\dfrac{1}{2} (2\pi + \pi) = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$. The value $3\pi$ forgets the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\pi$ forgets the term $\cos^2\theta$ and $2\pi$ integrates $1 + \cos\theta$ without the factor $\rho$.
