- $\left( \dfrac{4}{3\pi},\ \dfrac{4}{3\pi} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{3},\ \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{12},\ \dfrac{\pi}{12} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{3\pi},\ \dfrac{1}{3\pi} \right)$

## Explanation

The lamina is homogeneous, so each coordinate of the center of mass is the integral of that coordinate over $D$ divided by the area of $D$, which equals $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. In **polar coordinates** the quarter disk is the rectangle $0 \leq \rho \leq 1$, $0 \leq \theta \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Since $x = \rho \cos\theta$ and the area element is $\rho \, d\rho \, d\theta$, the integrand becomes $\rho^2 \cos\theta$, therefore
$$
\iint_D x \, dx\, dy = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos\theta \, d\theta \cdot \int_0^1 \rho^2 \, d\rho = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
It follows that $\bar{x} = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{4}{\pi} = \dfrac{4}{3\pi}$. The domain is symmetric with respect to the bisector $y = x$, so $\bar{y} = \bar{x}$ and the center of mass is $\left( \dfrac{4}{3\pi}, \dfrac{4}{3\pi} \right)$.
