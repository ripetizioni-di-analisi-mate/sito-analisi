The function $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$ is unbounded near the origin. What is the value of its integral over the disk $D$ with center at the origin and radius $3$?

- $3\pi$
- $6\pi$
- $18\pi$
- $9\pi$

## Explanation

In **polar coordinates** the disk becomes the rectangle $0 \leq \rho \leq 3$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$ and the function equals $\dfrac{1}{\rho}$. The area element is $\rho\, d\rho\, d\theta$, so the factor $\rho$ cancels with the function:
$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \left( \int_0^3 \frac{1}{\rho} \cdot \rho \, d\rho \right) d\theta = 2\pi \cdot 3 = 6\pi .
$$
The function is unbounded near the origin, but after the change of variables the integrand is the constant $1$ and the integral is finite. The value $18\pi$ takes $9$ as the radius, and $9\pi$ is the area of the disk.
