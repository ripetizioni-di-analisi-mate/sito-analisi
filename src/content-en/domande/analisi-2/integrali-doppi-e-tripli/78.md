Which equation in spherical coordinates $(r, \varphi, \theta)$ describes the cone $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ without its vertex?

- $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

In spherical coordinates $z = r \cos\varphi$ and $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = r \sin\varphi$, because $\sin\varphi \geq 0$ for $\varphi \in [0, \pi]$. For $r > 0$ the equation of the cone therefore becomes $\cos\varphi = \sin\varphi$, which in $[0, \pi]$ has the single solution $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The condition $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ describes the plane $z = 0$, while $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ describes a vertical half-plane containing the $z$-axis.
