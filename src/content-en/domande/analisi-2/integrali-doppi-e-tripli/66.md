What is the absolute value of the Jacobian of the transformation $x = e^u \cos v$, $y = e^u \sin v$?

- $e^u$
- $2e^u$
- $e^{2u} \cos(2v)$
- $e^{2u}$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial u} = e^u \cos v$, $\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial v} = -e^u \sin v$, $\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial u} = e^u \sin v$ and $\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial v} = e^u \cos v$. The **Jacobian** is therefore
$$
J = e^u \cos v \cdot e^u \cos v - \left( -e^u \sin v \right) e^u \sin v = e^{2u} \left( \cos^2 v + \sin^2 v \right) = e^{2u} .
$$
The value $e^{2u} \cos(2v)$ comes from the wrong sign in the second product. The transformation coincides with polar coordinates with $\rho = e^u$, and indeed $e^{2u} = \rho \cdot \dfrac{d\rho}{du}$.
