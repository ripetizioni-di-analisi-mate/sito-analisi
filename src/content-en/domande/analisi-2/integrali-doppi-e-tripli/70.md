Using polar coordinates $x = \rho\cos\theta$, $y = \rho\sin\theta$ on the rectangle $D' = [0, 1] \times [0, 4\pi]$ one gets $\displaystyle\iint_{D'} \rho \, d\rho\, d\theta = 2\pi$, while the image of $D'$ is the unit disk, whose area is $\pi$. Which hypothesis of the change of variables theorem is not satisfied?

- The Jacobian $\rho$ vanishes at interior points of $D'$
- The transformation is not of class $C^1$
- The integrand $1$ is not continuous on the disk
- The transformation is not injective in the interior of $D'$

## Explanation

The angles $\theta$ and $\theta + 2\pi$ give the same point, so for $0 < \theta < 2\pi$ the points $(\rho, \theta)$ and $(\rho, \theta + 2\pi)$, both interior to $D'$, have the same image. Therefore the transformation **is not injective** in the interior of $D'$, and every point of the disk is counted twice, which explains the value $2\pi = 2 \cdot \pi$. The Jacobian $\rho$ vanishes only on the side $\rho = 0$, which belongs to the boundary of $D'$, so the condition $J \neq 0$ in the interior is satisfied. The transformation is of class $C^1$ and the constant $1$ is continuous.
