On the rectangle $R = [0, 1] \times [1, 2]$ the function $\dfrac{1}{(x + y)^2}$ is continuous, because $x + y \geq 1$. What is the value of its double integral over $R$?

- $\ln \dfrac{4}{3}$
- $-\ln \dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\ln \dfrac{27}{16}$
- $\ln 2$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on $R$, because $x + y \geq 1$, so by **Fubini's theorem** we integrate first with respect to $x$ and then with respect to $y$. For fixed $y$ an antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{(x + y)^2}$ with respect to $x$ is $-\dfrac{1}{x + y}$, therefore
$$
\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{(x + y)^2} = \frac{1}{y} - \frac{1}{y + 1} .
$$
Integrating over $[1, 2]$ gives
$$
\Bigl[ \ln y - \ln(y + 1) \Bigr]_1^2 = (\ln 2 - \ln 3) - (0 - \ln 2) = \ln \frac{4}{3} .
$$
The value $-\ln \dfrac{4}{3}$ comes from writing the antiderivative without the minus sign, and $\ln \dfrac{27}{16}$ is the integral of $\dfrac{1}{x + y}$ without the square.
