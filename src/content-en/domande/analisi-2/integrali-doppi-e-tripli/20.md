The triangle $T$ with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ is normal with respect to the $y$-axis. Which description of $T$ is correct?

- $0 \leq y \leq 1$, $\ 0 \leq x \leq 1 - \dfrac{y}{2}$
- $0 \leq y \leq 2$, $\ 0 \leq x \leq 1 - \dfrac{y}{2}$
- $0 \leq y \leq 1$, $\ 0 \leq x \leq 2 - 2y$
- $0 \leq y \leq 1$, $\ 2 - 2y \leq x \leq 2$

## Explanation

The side joining $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ lies on the line $\dfrac{x}{2} + y = 1$, that is, $x = 2 - 2y$. The points of the triangle have y-coordinates between $0$ and $1$, and for each fixed $y$ the horizontal segment starts on the $y$-axis, where $x = 0$, and ends on the slanted side, where $x = 2 - 2y$. Therefore
$$
T = \left\{ (x, y) :
\begin{cases}
0 \leq y \leq 1 \\
0 \leq x \leq 2 - 2y
\end{cases}
\right\} .
$$
The descriptions with $x \leq 1 - \dfrac{y}{2}$ are obtained by exchanging $x$ and $y$ in the equation of the side; for $y = 0$ they give $x \leq 1$, whereas the vertex $(2, 0)$ belongs to $T$.
