- $2\pi \ln 2$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi \ln 2}{2}$
- $\pi \ln 2$

## Explanation

The domain is the disk of radius $1$ and the function depends on $x^2 + y^2$, so we switch to **polar coordinates**. Since $x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$ and the area element is $\rho \, d\rho \, d\theta$, the integral becomes
$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \left( \int_0^1 \frac{\rho}{1 + \rho^2}\, d\rho \right) d\theta = 2\pi \int_0^1 \frac{\rho}{1 + \rho^2}\, d\rho .
$$
The integral in $\rho$ is reducible to the immediate integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(\rho)}{f(\rho)}\, d\rho = \ln |f(\rho)| + c$ with $f(\rho) = 1 + \rho^2$, so it equals $\left[ \dfrac{1}{2} \ln (1 + \rho^2) \right]_0^1 = \dfrac{\ln 2}{2}$. Therefore the double integral equals $2\pi \cdot \dfrac{\ln 2}{2} = \pi \ln 2$.
