Let $T = \{ (x, y) : x \geq 0, \ y \geq 0, \ x + y \leq 1 \}$. Using the variables $u = y - x$, $v = y + x$, what is the value of $\displaystyle\iint_T e^{\frac{y - x}{y + x}}\, dx\, dy$?

- $\dfrac{e - e^{-1}}{2}$
- $e - e^{-1}$
- $\dfrac{e - e^{-1}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{e - 1}{4}$

## Explanation

The determinant of the derivatives of $u$ and $v$ with respect to $x$ and $y$ equals $(-1) \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 1 = -2$, so $dx\, dy = \dfrac{1}{2}\, du\, dv$. The conditions $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$ become $-v \leq u \leq v$, and the condition $x + y \leq 1$ becomes $v \leq 1$, therefore $T$ becomes the triangle $0 \leq v \leq 1$, $-v \leq u \leq v$. For fixed $v$
$$
\int_{-v}^{v} e^{\frac{u}{v}}\, du = v \left( e - e^{-1} \right) ,
$$
so the integral equals $\dfrac{1}{2} \left( e - e^{-1} \right) \displaystyle\int_0^1 v \, dv = \frac{e - e^{-1}}{4}$. The value $\dfrac{e - e^{-1}}{2}$ forgets the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the Jacobian, and $\dfrac{e - 1}{4}$ lets $u$ range only between $0$ and $v$.
