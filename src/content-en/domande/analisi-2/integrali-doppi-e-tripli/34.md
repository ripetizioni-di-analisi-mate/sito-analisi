Let $Q = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$. What is the value of $\displaystyle\iint_Q \frac{x}{x + y}\, dx\, dy$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\ln 2$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

The function is bounded and continuous on $Q$ except at the origin, so it is integrable. The change of variables that swaps $x$ and $y$ has a Jacobian of absolute value $1$ and maps $Q$ onto itself, therefore
$$
\iint_Q \frac{x}{x + y}\, dx\, dy = \iint_Q \frac{y}{x + y}\, dx\, dy .
$$
By **linearity** the sum of the two integrals is the integral of $\dfrac{x + y}{x + y} = 1$, that is, the area of $Q$, which equals $1$. The two integrals are equal and their sum is $1$, so each of them equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
