- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $3\pi$
- $4\pi$
- $6\pi$

## Explanation

With the transformation $x = 3u$, $y = 2v$ the ellipse becomes the unit disk $u^2 + v^2 \leq 1$, the function becomes $u^2 + v^2$ and the Jacobian equals $3 \cdot 2 = 6$. Therefore, by the **change of variables** theorem,
$$
\iint_D \left( \frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{4} \right) dx\, dy = 6 \iint_{u^2 + v^2 \leq 1} \left( u^2 + v^2 \right) du\, dv .
$$
In **polar coordinates** the last integral equals $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 \rho^2 \cdot \rho \, d\rho\, d\theta = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2}$, so the result is $6 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 3\pi$.
