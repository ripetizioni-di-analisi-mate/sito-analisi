Let $f$ be continuous. After changing the order of integration, which integral is equal to $\displaystyle\int_0^2 \left( \int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{1} f(x, y)\, dy \right) dx$?

- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \int_{2y}^{2} f(x, y)\, dx \right) dy$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \int_0^{\frac{y}{2}} f(x, y)\, dx \right) dy$
- $\displaystyle\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{1} \left( \int_0^2 f(x, y)\, dx \right) dy$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \int_0^{2y} f(x, y)\, dx \right) dy$

## Explanation

The limits describe the region $0 \leq x \leq 2$, $\dfrac{x}{2} \leq y \leq 1$, that is, the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(2, 1)$, bounded by the $y$-axis, the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ and the line $y = 1$. The y-coordinates of the points of the triangle range from $0$ to $1$. For fixed $y$ the horizontal segment goes from the $y$-axis, where $x = 0$, to the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$, where $x = 2y$. Therefore the triangle, normal with respect to the $y$-axis, is described by $0 \leq y \leq 1$, $0 \leq x \leq 2y$, and the integral is equal to $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \int_0^{2y} f(x, y)\, dx\, dy$. In the reduction formula the outer variable ranges over a fixed interval, so the expression with limits $\dfrac{x}{2}$ and $1$ in the outer integral does not define a number.
