- $\dfrac{e^2 - 1}{2}$
- $e^2 - 2$
- $\dfrac{e^2 + 1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{e^2 - 3}{2}$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on the rectangle, so by **Fubini's theorem** we can integrate first with respect to $y$ and then with respect to $x$. With $x$ fixed, the inner integral is reducible to the immediate integral $\displaystyle\int e^{t}\, dt = e^{t} + c$ with $t = xy$, therefore
$$
\int_0^2 x\, e^{xy}\, dy = \bigl[ e^{xy} \bigr]_{y=0}^{y=2} = e^{2x} - 1 .
$$
Integrating this result with respect to $x$ we obtain
$$
\int_0^1 \left( e^{2x} - 1 \right) dx = \left[ \frac{e^{2x}}{2} - x \right]_0^1 = \frac{e^2}{2} - 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{e^2 - 3}{2} .
$$
