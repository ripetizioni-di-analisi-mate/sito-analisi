The circular segment $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 2, \ y \geq 1 \}$ is the part of the disk of radius $\sqrt{2}$ lying above the line $y = 1$. Which bounds in polar coordinates describe it?

::grafico{xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-1.7 ymax=2 uguale=1 curve="1.4142*cos(t),1.4142*sin(t),0,6.29" area="sqrt(abs(2-x^2));1;-1;1" segmenti="-1.8,1,1.8,1" punti="-1,1;1,1" etichette="1.25,0.75,y = 1;1.1,1.25,(1, 1);0.35,1.15,D" titolo="The circular segment $D$, the part of the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 2$ above the line $y = 1$"}

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \leq \theta \leq \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, $\ \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta} \leq \rho \leq \sqrt{2}$
- $0 \leq \theta \leq \pi$, $\ 1 \leq \rho \leq \sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \leq \theta \leq \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, $\ 1 \leq \rho \leq \sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \leq \theta \leq \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, $\ 0 \leq \rho \leq \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates the line $y = 1$ has equation $\rho \sin\theta = 1$, that is, $\rho = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}$, and the circle has equation $\rho = \sqrt{2}$. The line cuts the circle at the points $(\pm 1, 1)$, whose angles are $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\theta = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, so $\theta$ ranges over $\left[ \dfrac{\pi}{4}, \dfrac{3\pi}{4} \right]$. For each of these angles the ray from the origin enters $D$ by crossing the line and exits through the circle, therefore $\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta} \leq \rho \leq \sqrt{2}$. The condition $0 \leq \rho \leq \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}$ describes the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, 1)$, which lies below the line.
