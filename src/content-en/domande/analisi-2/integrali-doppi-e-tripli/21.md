Let $f$ be continuous. Which integral is equal to the sum $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \int_0^{y} f(x, y)\, dx \right) dy + \int_1^2 \left( \int_0^{2 - y} f(x, y)\, dx \right) dy$?

- $\displaystyle\int_0^2 \left( \int_x^{2 - x} f(x, y)\, dy \right) dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \int_0^{2 - x} f(x, y)\, dy \right) dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \int_x^{2 - x} f(x, y)\, dy \right) dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( \int_x^{2} f(x, y)\, dy \right) dx$

## Explanation

The first integral describes the region $0 \leq y \leq 1$, $0 \leq x \leq y$ and the second the region $1 \leq y \leq 2$, $0 \leq x \leq 2 - y$. By **additivity** the sum is the integral of $f$ over the union, which is the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(0, 2)$ and $(1, 1)$, bounded by the $y$-axis and by the lines $y = x$ and $y = 2 - x$. The two lines meet at the point $(1, 1)$, so in the triangle $x$ ranges between $0$ and $1$. For fixed $x$ the vertical segment enters through the line $y = x$ and exits through the line $y = 2 - x$, therefore the triangle is normal with respect to the $x$-axis and the sum equals
$$
\int_0^1 \left( \int_x^{2 - x} f(x, y)\, dy \right) dx .
$$
With $x$ up to $2$ the vertical segment would have $x > 2 - x$ for $x > 1$, so that expression does not describe the triangle.
