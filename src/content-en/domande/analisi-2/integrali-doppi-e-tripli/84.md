The rectangular lamina $[0, 1] \times [0, 2]$ has density $\delta(x, y) = 1 + y$, which increases with height. At which point is its center of mass?

- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2},\ 1 \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2},\ \dfrac{7}{6} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2},\ \dfrac{7}{3} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2},\ \dfrac{4}{3} \right)$

## Explanation

The density does not depend on $x$ and the rectangle is symmetric with respect to the line $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The functions to integrate have separated variables on the rectangle, therefore
$$
m = \int_0^1 dx \cdot \int_0^2 (1 + y)\, dy = 4 ,
\qquad
\iint y\, \delta\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 dx \cdot \int_0^2 \left( y + y^2 \right) dy = 2 + \frac{8}{3} = \frac{14}{3} .
$$
Hence $\bar{y} = \dfrac{14}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{7}{6}$, greater than $1$, since the density is higher in the upper part of the lamina. The point $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 1 \right)$ is the center of mass of the homogeneous lamina, $\dfrac{7}{3}$ divides by the area $2$ instead of by the mass, and $\dfrac{4}{3}$ uses the density $y$ instead of $1 + y$.
