Let $C$ be the annulus $1 \leq x^2 + y^2 \leq 9$. Which integral in polar coordinates is equal to $\displaystyle\iint_C x^2\, dx\, dy$?

- $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} \left( \int_1^3 \rho^2 \cos^2\theta \, d\rho \right) d\theta$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} \left( \int_1^9 \rho^3 \cos^2\theta \, d\rho \right) d\theta$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} \left( \int_1^3 \rho^3 \cos^2\theta \, d\rho \right) d\theta$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \left( \int_1^3 \rho^3 \cos^2\theta \, d\rho \right) d\theta$

## Explanation

The annulus has inner radius $1$ and outer radius $3$, because $x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$, and it goes all the way around the origin, so it becomes the rectangle $1 \leq \rho \leq 3$, $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$. The function becomes $x^2 = \rho^2 \cos^2\theta$ and the area element is $\rho\, d\rho\, d\theta$, therefore the integrand in polar coordinates is $\rho^3 \cos^2\theta$. The integral with $\rho^2 \cos^2\theta$ forgets the factor $\rho$ of the Jacobian, the one with $\rho$ up to $9$ confuses the radius with its square, and the one with $\theta$ up to $\pi$ describes only the half of the annulus with $y \geq 0$.
