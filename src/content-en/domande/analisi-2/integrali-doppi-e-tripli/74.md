The solid $E$ lies above the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$ in the plane $z = 0$ and is bounded above by the slanted plane $z = 2 + x$. What is the volume of $E$?

- $3\pi$
- $2\pi + \dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\pi$
- $2\pi$

## Explanation

On the disk $2 + x \geq 1 > 0$, so the solid is $E = \{ (x, y, z) : (x, y) \in D, \ 0 \leq z \leq 2 + x \}$, where $D$ is the unit disk. By **integration by vertical lines** the volume is
$$
\operatorname{Vol}(E) = \iint_D \left( \int_0^{2 + x} dz \right) dx\, dy = \iint_D (2 + x)\, dx\, dy .
$$
The integral of $2$ equals $2\pi$, because the disk has area $\pi$. The function $x$ is odd in $x$ and the disk is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis, therefore its integral is zero and the volume equals $2\pi$. The value $3\pi$ uses the maximum height $3$ over the whole disk, and $2\pi + \dfrac{4}{3}$ integrates $|x|$ instead of $x$.
