What is the value of the double integral $\displaystyle\iint_Q \sin(x + y)\, dx\, dy$, where $Q = \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right] \times \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$?

- $0$
- $2$
- $1$
- $\pi$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on the square, so by **Fubini's theorem** the integral is an iterated integral. For fixed $x$ an antiderivative of $\sin(x + y)$ with respect to $y$ is $-\cos(x + y)$, therefore
$$
\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin(x + y)\, dy = \cos x - \cos\left( x + \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = \cos x + \sin x .
$$
Integrating over $\left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$ gives
$$
\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} (\cos x + \sin x)\, dx = 1 + 1 = 2 .
$$
The function $\sin(x + y)$ is not a product of a function of $x$ and a function of $y$, so the integral cannot be written as a product of two single integrals.
