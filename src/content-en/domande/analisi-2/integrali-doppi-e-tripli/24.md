Let $T$ be the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(1, 0)$ and $(1, 2)$. What is the value of $\displaystyle\iint_T \frac{1}{1 + x^2}\, dx\, dy$?

- $\dfrac{\ln 2}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\ln 2$
- $2 \ln 2$

## Explanation

The triangle lies between the $x$-axis and the line $y = 2x$ for $0 \leq x \leq 1$, so it is the **normal domain** with respect to the $x$-axis
$$
T = \left\{ (x, y) :
\begin{cases}
0 \leq x \leq 1 \\
0 \leq y \leq 2x
\end{cases}
\right\} .
$$
The function does not depend on $y$, therefore the inner integral equals $\displaystyle\int_0^{2x} \frac{1}{1 + x^2}\, dy = \frac{2x}{1 + x^2}$. The integral of this last function reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\, dx = \ln |f(x)| + c$ with $f(x) = 1 + x^2$, so
$$
\iint_T \frac{1}{1 + x^2}\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 \frac{2x}{1 + x^2}\, dx = \bigl[ \ln (1 + x^2) \bigr]_0^1 = \ln 2 .
$$
