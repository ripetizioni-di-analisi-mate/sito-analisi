The transformation $x = u$, $y = v + u^2$ maps the square $[0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ of the $uv$-plane onto the region $D = \{ (x, y) : 0 \leq x \leq 1, \ x^2 \leq y \leq x^2 + 1 \}$. What is the area of $D$?

- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial u} = 1$, $\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial v} = 0$, $\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial u} = 2u$ and $\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial v} = 1$, so the **Jacobian** is
$$
J = \det
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
2u & 1
\end{pmatrix}
= 1 .
$$
The transformation is injective and of class $C^1$, therefore by the **change of variables** theorem the area of $D$ is the integral of $|J| = 1$ over the square, that is, $1$. The same value follows from the reduction formula, since the vertical segment at abscissa $x$ has length $(x^2 + 1) - x^2 = 1$. The value $\dfrac{4}{3}$ is the area under the curve $y = x^2 + 1$, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is the area under the parabola $y = x^2$, and $2$ is obtained by taking $1 + 2u$ as the Jacobian.
