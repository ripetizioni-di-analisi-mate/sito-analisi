The solid $E$ lies between the planes $z = 0$ and $z = 1$, and its cross-section at height $z$ is the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq e^{2z}$. What is the volume of $E$?

- $\pi \left( e^2 - 1 \right)$
- $\dfrac{\pi \left( e^2 - 1 \right)}{2}$
- $\pi (e - 1)$
- $\dfrac{e^2 - 1}{2}$

## Explanation

The cross-section at height $z \in [0, 1]$ is the disk of radius $e^{z}$, so its area is $\pi e^{2z}$. By **integration by slices** the volume is the integral of the areas of the cross-sections:
$$
\operatorname{Vol}(E) = \int_0^1 \pi e^{2z}\, dz = \pi \left[ \frac{e^{2z}}{2} \right]_0^1 = \frac{\pi \left( e^2 - 1 \right)}{2} .
$$
The value $\pi \left( e^2 - 1 \right)$ forgets the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the antiderivative, $\pi (e - 1)$ takes $\pi e^{z}$ as the area of the cross-section, and $\dfrac{e^2 - 1}{2}$ forgets the factor $\pi$.
