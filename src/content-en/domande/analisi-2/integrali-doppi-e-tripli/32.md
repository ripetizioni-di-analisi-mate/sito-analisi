Let $D$ be the quarter disk $\{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1, \ x \geq 0, \ y \geq 0 \}$. What is the value of $\displaystyle\iint_D xy\, dx\, dy$?

- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{8}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

In **polar coordinates** the quarter disk becomes the rectangle $0 \leq \rho \leq 1$, $0 \leq \theta \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Since $xy = \rho^2 \cos\theta \sin\theta$ and the area element is $\rho\, d\rho\, d\theta$, the integrand becomes $\rho^3 \sin\theta \cos\theta$. The integral in $\theta$ equals $\left[ \dfrac{\sin^2\theta}{2} \right]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The limits are constant and the function has separated variables, therefore
$$
\iint_D xy\, dx\, dy = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin\theta \cos\theta \, d\theta \cdot \int_0^1 \rho^3 \, d\rho = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{8} .
$$
