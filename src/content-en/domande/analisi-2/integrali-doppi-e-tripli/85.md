A lamina has the shape of the half-disk $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1, \ y \geq 0 \}$ and density $\delta(x, y) = x^2$. What is its mass?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{16}$

## Explanation

The mass is the integral of the density over $D$. In **polar coordinates** the half-disk becomes the rectangle $0 \leq \rho \leq 1$, $0 \leq \theta \leq \pi$, the density equals $\rho^2 \cos^2\theta$ and the area element is $\rho\, d\rho\, d\theta$, so
$$
m = \int_0^{\pi} \cos^2\theta \, d\theta \cdot \int_0^1 \rho^3 \, d\rho = \frac{\pi}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{\pi}{8} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is the mass of the whole disk with the same density, and $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ is obtained without the factor $\rho$ of the Jacobian.
