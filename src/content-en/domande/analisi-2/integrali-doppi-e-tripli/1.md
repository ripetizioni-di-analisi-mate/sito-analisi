- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The function $y \sin x$ is the product of a factor that depends only on $x$ and a factor that depends only on $y$, and the domain is a rectangle. Therefore, by the theorem on **functions with separated variables**, the double integral is the product of two single integrals
$$
\iint_R y \sin x \, dx\, dy = \int_0^{\pi} \sin x \, dx \cdot \int_0^1 y \, dy .
$$
The first integral equals $\bigl[ -\cos x \bigr]_0^{\pi} = 1 + 1 = 2$ and the second equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the integral equals $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$.
