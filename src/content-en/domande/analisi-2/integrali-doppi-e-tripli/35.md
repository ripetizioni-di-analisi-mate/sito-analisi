Let $D = \{ (x, y) : -1 \leq x \leq 1, \ 0 \leq y \leq 1 - x^2 \}$ and let $D^+$ be the part of $D$ with $x \geq 0$. Which statement about the integral $I = \displaystyle\iint_D (x + y)\, dx\, dy$ is true?

- $I = \displaystyle\iint_D y\, dx\, dy$
- $I = 0$
- $I = \displaystyle\iint_D x\, dx\, dy$
- $I = 2 \displaystyle\iint_{D^+} (x + y)\, dx\, dy$

## Explanation

The domain is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis, since the condition $0 \leq y \leq 1 - x^2$ does not change when $x$ is replaced by $-x$. The function $x$ is odd in $x$, so by the theorem on **odd functions on symmetric domains** its integral over $D$ is zero. By linearity, therefore, $I$ is equal to the integral of $y$ over $D$. The domain is not symmetric with respect to the $x$-axis and $y > 0$ in the interior of $D$, so this integral is positive and equals $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{(1 - x^2)^2}{2}\, dx = \frac{8}{15}$. The function $x + y$ is not even in $x$, therefore the formula with twice the integral over $D^+$ does not apply.
