Let $Q = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ and let $f(x, y) = 1$ if $x \leq y$, $f(x, y) = 0$ if $x > y$. Which statement is true?

- $f$ is integrable on $Q$ and $\displaystyle\iint_Q f\, dx\, dy = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $f$ is not integrable on $Q$, because it is not continuous
- $f$ is integrable on $Q$ and $\displaystyle\iint_Q f\, dx\, dy = 1$
- $f$ is integrable on $Q$ and $\displaystyle\iint_Q f\, dx\, dy = 0$

## Explanation

The function is bounded and its points of discontinuity lie on the segment $y = x$, which is a regular curve. Since a bounded function that is continuous except on finitely many regular curves is **integrable**, $f$ is integrable on $Q$. The function equals $1$ on the triangle $\{ 0 \leq x \leq y \leq 1 \}$ and $0$ on the rest of the square, so the integral is the area of that triangle:
$$
\iint_Q f\, dx\, dy = \int_0^1 \left( \int_0^{y} dx \right) dy = \int_0^1 y \, dy = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
Continuity is a sufficient condition for integrability, not a necessary one.
