Which region is described by the inequality $\sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2} \leq 1$?

- The points with $x^2 + y^2 \geq 3$
- The points with $x^2 + y^2 \leq 3$
- The points with $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$
- The points with $3 \leq x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$

## Explanation

The inequality $\sqrt{F} \leq c$, with $c \geq 0$, is equivalent to $0 \leq F \leq c^2$, because the square root is defined for $F \geq 0$ and is an increasing function. With $F = 4 - x^2 - y^2$ and $c = 1$ we obtain $0 \leq 4 - x^2 - y^2 \leq 1$. The first inequality gives $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$ and the second gives $x^2 + y^2 \geq 3$. The region is therefore the **annulus** lying between the circles centered at the origin with radii $\sqrt{3}$ and $2$, both included. The point $(3, 0)$ satisfies $x^2 + y^2 \geq 3$, but at this point the radicand equals $-5$ and the square root is not defined.
