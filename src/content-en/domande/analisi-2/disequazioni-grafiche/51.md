With respect to the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{9} - \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$, where do the points that satisfy the inequality $\dfrac{x^2}{9} - \dfrac{y^2}{4} < 1$ lie?

- In the two regions inside the branches, branches excluded
- In the region between the two branches, branches excluded
- In the region between the two branches, branches included
- In the two regions inside the branches, branches included

## Explanation

The hyperbola has its center at the origin and its branches to the right and to the left, because the positive term is the one in $x^2$. At the center the left-hand side equals $0$, which is less than $1$. The left-hand side less than $1$ therefore describes the region that contains the center, that is, the **region between the two branches**. The symbol $<$ does not include equality, so the branches are **excluded**.
