Which set is described by the equation $4x^2 - y^2 + 6y - 9 = 0$?

- A hyperbola with center $(0, 3)$ and branches on the right and on the left
- A hyperbola with center $(0, 3)$ and branches at the top and at the bottom
- The two lines $y = 2x + 3$ and $y = -2x + 3$
- The single point $(0, 3)$

## Explanation

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ have opposite signs, so the equation describes a hyperbola or one of its degenerate cases. Since $-y^2 + 6y - 9 = -(y - 3)^2$, the equation can be rewritten as
$$
4x^2 - (y - 3)^2 = 0 .
$$
The right-hand side is zero and the left-hand side is a difference of squares, so the equation is equivalent to $(2x - y + 3)(2x + y - 3) = 0$. A product is zero when at least one of the factors is zero, therefore the set consists of the **two lines** $y = 2x + 3$ and $y = -2x + 3$, which meet at $(0, 3)$. A hyperbola with center $(0, 3)$ is obtained only with a right-hand side different from zero.
