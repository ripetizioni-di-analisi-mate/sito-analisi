At which points of the Cartesian plane is the absolute value $|x + 2|$ greater than $1$?

- On the strip lying between the lines $x = -3$ and $x = -1$, lines excluded
- To the left of the line $x = 1$ and to the right of the line $x = 3$, lines excluded
- Only to the right of the line $x = -1$, line excluded
- To the left of the line $x = -3$ and to the right of the line $x = -1$, lines excluded

## Explanation

The inequality $|x + 2| \leq 1$ is equivalent to $-1 \leq x + 2 \leq 1$, that is, to $-3 \leq x \leq -1$, and describes the vertical strip between the lines $x = -3$ and $x = -1$, lines included. The inequality $|x + 2| > 1$ is satisfied by the points that do not satisfy $|x + 2| \leq 1$, so it describes the points that lie outside this strip. We thus obtain **two half-planes**, the one to the left of the line $x = -3$, where $x + 2 < -1$, and the one to the right of the line $x = -1$, where $x + 2 > 1$. The two lines belong to the strip, so they are excluded.
