In the inequality $\sqrt{y} > x$ the square root is defined only for $y \geq 0$. What is the set of all the solutions?

- The points with $y > x^2$
- The points with $x < 0$ and $y \geq 0$, together with the points with $x \geq 0$ and $y > x^2$
- The points with $x \geq 0$ and $y > x^2$
- The points with $y \geq 0$ and $y < x^2$

## Explanation

The square root is defined for $y \geq 0$ and its value is greater than or equal to zero. If $x < 0$, the inequality is true at every point with $y \geq 0$, because a number greater than or equal to zero is greater than a negative number. If $x \geq 0$, both sides are greater than or equal to zero, so they can be squared preserving the direction of the inequality, and we obtain $y > x^2$. The solutions are therefore the points with $x < 0$ and $y \geq 0$, together with the points with $x \geq 0$ that lie above the parabola $y = x^2$. The point $(-2, 1)$ satisfies the inequality, because $1 > -2$, but it does not satisfy $y > x^2$.
