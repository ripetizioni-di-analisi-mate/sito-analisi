In the inequality $\dfrac{y - 2x}{x^2 + y^2 - 5} \geq 0$ the boundaries are the line $y = 2x$ and the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 5$. Which points of the two curves belong to the solutions?

- The points of the line other than $(1, 2)$ and $(-1, -2)$, and no point of the circle
- All the points of the line, and no point of the circle
- All the points of the line and all the points of the circle
- No point of the line and no point of the circle

## Explanation

On the circle the denominator is zero, so the quotient is not defined and the circle is **excluded**. On the line the numerator is zero, so the quotient equals $0$ at all the points where the denominator is different from zero, and the symbol $\geq$ includes the value $0$. The line meets the circle where $x^2 + 4x^2 = 5$, that is, for $x = \pm 1$, at the points $(1, 2)$ and $(-1, -2)$. At these two points the denominator is zero, therefore the solutions include the points of the line other than $(1, 2)$ and $(-1, -2)$.
