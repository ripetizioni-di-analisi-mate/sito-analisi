Let $g$ be a function continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. Which region does the inequality $g(y) - x < 0$ describe?

- The points to the left of the curve $x = g(y)$, curve excluded
- The points to the right of the curve $x = g(y)$, curve included
- The points below the graph $y = g(x)$, graph excluded
- The points to the right of the curve $x = g(y)$, curve excluded

## Explanation

Moving $x$ to the right-hand side we obtain $g(y) < x$, that is, $x > g(y)$. The variable $x$ is isolated, so the **isolated variable rule** applies. On the horizontal line with $y$-coordinate $y_0$ the point of the curve has $x$-coordinate $g(y_0)$, and a point of the same line with a greater $x$-coordinate lies further to the right. The solutions are therefore the points **to the right** of the curve $x = g(y)$. The symbol is strict, so the curve is excluded.
