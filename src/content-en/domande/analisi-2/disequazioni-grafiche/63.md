Into how many regions does the curve $x^2 - 4y^2 - 2x = 3$ divide the plane?

- Three, because the curve is a hyperbola
- Two, because the curve is an ellipse
- Four, because the curve is a pair of intersecting lines
- Two, because the curve is a parabola

## Explanation

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are $1$ and $-4$, which have opposite signs, so the curve is either a hyperbola or a pair of lines. Completing the square in $x$ gives $(x - 1)^2 - 1 - 4y^2 = 3$, that is,
$$
\frac{(x - 1)^2}{4} - y^2 = 1 .
$$
The right-hand side is different from zero, therefore the curve is a **hyperbola** with center $(1, 0)$ and branches on the right and on the left. A hyperbola consists of two branches, so it divides the plane into **three regions**, namely the region between the two branches and the two regions inside the branches. The pair of intersecting lines is obtained when the right-hand side is zero, as in the equation $x^2 - 4y^2 - 2x = -1$.
