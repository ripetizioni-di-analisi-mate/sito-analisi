Which of the following inequalities has a hyperbola as its boundary?

- $2x^2 + 5y^2 - 4x - 3 \leq 0$
- $2x^2 - 5y^2 - 4x - 3 \leq 0$
- $2x^2 + 2y^2 - 4x - 3 \leq 0$
- $2x^2 - 5y - 4x - 3 \leq 0$

## Explanation

In the equation $Ax^2 + By^2 + Cx + Dy + E = 0$ the curve is recognized from the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$. In $2x^2 - 5y^2 - 4x - 3 = 0$ the two coefficients are $2$ and $-5$, which have opposite signs, so the boundary is a **hyperbola**. Completing the square in $x$ gives $2(x - 1)^2 - 5y^2 = 5$, and the right-hand side is different from zero, therefore the hyperbola is not degenerate. With the coefficients $2$ and $5$, which have the same sign and are different, the boundary is an ellipse, and with the coefficients $2$ and $2$ it is a circle. In the inequality $2x^2 - 5y - 4x - 3 \leq 0$ the term $y^2$ is missing and $y$ appears, so the boundary is a parabola with vertical axis.
