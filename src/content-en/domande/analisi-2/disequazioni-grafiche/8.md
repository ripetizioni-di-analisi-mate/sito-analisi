What is the solution set of the inequality $(x + y - 2)^2 \leq 0$?

- The half-plane $x + y \leq 2$
- No points
- Only the points of the line $x + y = 2$
- All the points of the plane

## Explanation

The square of a real number is greater than or equal to zero, so $(x + y - 2)^2 < 0$ has no solutions. The inequality is therefore satisfied only where $(x + y - 2)^2 = 0$, that is, where $x + y = 2$, and the solutions are **only the points of the line**. A test point in each of the two half-planes confirms this. At $(0, 0)$ the left-hand side equals $(-2)^2 = 4$ and at $(2, 2)$ it equals $2^2 = 4$, so the left-hand side is positive in both half-planes and neither of them contains solutions.
