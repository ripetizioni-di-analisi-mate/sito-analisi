The inequality $\ln(y - x^2) < 0$ is rewritten without the logarithm. Which condition is obtained?

- $x^2 < y < x^2 + 1$
- $y < x^2 + 1$
- $y < x^2$
- $y > x^2 + 1$

## Explanation

The logarithm is defined when its argument is positive, and it is a strictly increasing function with $\ln 1 = 0$. The inequality $\ln F < 0$ is therefore equivalent to $0 < F < 1$. With $F = y - x^2$ we obtain $0 < y - x^2 < 1$, that is, $x^2 < y < x^2 + 1$. The region lies between the parabolas $y = x^2$ and $y = x^2 + 1$, both **excluded**. The condition $y < x^2 + 1$ alone is also satisfied by the point $(0, -1)$, where the argument equals $-1$ and the logarithm is not defined.
