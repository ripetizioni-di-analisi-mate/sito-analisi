The shaded region is bounded by the parabola $y = x^2$ and by the line $y = 1$, both excluded. Which inequality describes it?

::grafico{regione="y>x^2; y<1 | y<x^2; y>1" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="The shaded region"}

- $(y - x^2)(y - 1) > 0$
- $(y - x^2)(y - 1) < 0$
- $\dfrac{y - x^2}{y - 1} \leq 0$
- $\begin{cases} y > x^2 \\ y < 1 \end{cases}$

## Explanation

The shaded region consists of the points above the parabola and below the line, and of the points below the parabola and above the line. At the former we have $y - x^2 > 0$ and $y - 1 < 0$, at the latter $y - x^2 < 0$ and $y - 1 > 0$. In both cases the two factors have opposite signs, so the region is described by $(y - x^2)(y - 1) < 0$, with the boundaries **excluded**. The system formed by $y > x^2$ and $y < 1$ describes only the points above the parabola and below the line. The inequality with the quotient and the symbol $\leq$ also includes the points of the parabola where the denominator is different from zero, because at those points the quotient equals $0$.
