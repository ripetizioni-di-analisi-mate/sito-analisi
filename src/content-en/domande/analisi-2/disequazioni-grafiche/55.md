Completing the squares in the inequality $x^2 - y^2 + 2x + 4y - 7 \geq 0$ shows that the boundary is a hyperbola. Where are its vertices?

- $(-1, 0)$ and $(-1, 4)$
- $(-3, 2)$ and $(1, 2)$
- $(-1, -2)$ and $(3, -2)$
- $(-5, 2)$ and $(3, 2)$

## Explanation

We complete the squares in $x$ and in $y$, since $x^2 + 2x = (x + 1)^2 - 1$ and $-(y^2 - 4y) = -(y - 2)^2 + 4$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 1)^2 - 1 - (y - 2)^2 + 4 - 7 &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
(x + 1)^2 - (y - 2)^2 &\geq 4 \\[6pt]
\frac{(x + 1)^2}{4} - \frac{(y - 2)^2}{4} &\geq 1
\end{aligned}
$$
The center is $(-1, 2)$ and the positive term is the one in $x$, so the branches lie to the right and to the left, with $a = 2$. The vertices are $(-1 \pm 2,\, 2)$, that is, $(-3, 2)$ and $(1, 2)$.
