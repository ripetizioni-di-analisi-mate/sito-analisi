Let $D$ be the region lying between the parabola $x = y^2$ and the line $x + y = 2$. Which expression describes $D$ as a normal domain with respect to the $y$-axis?

- $-2 \leq y \leq 1$, $\ 2 - y \leq x \leq y^2$
- $-2 \leq y \leq 1$, $\ y^2 \leq x \leq 2 - y$
- $-1 \leq y \leq 2$, $\ y^2 \leq x \leq 2 - y$
- $1 \leq y \leq 4$, $\ y^2 \leq x \leq 2 - y$

## Explanation

Isolating $x$, the line becomes $x = 2 - y$. The two curves meet where $y^2 = 2 - y$, that is, $y^2 + y - 2 = 0$, which has the solutions $y = -2$ and $y = 1$. The projection of $D$ onto the $y$-axis is therefore the interval $[-2, 1]$. For $y = 0$ the parabola gives $x = 0$ and the line gives $x = 2$, therefore between the two intersection points the parabola is the curve on the left and the line is the curve on the right. We obtain $-2 \leq y \leq 1$, $y^2 \leq x \leq 2 - y$. The numbers $1$ and $4$ are the $x$-coordinates of the two intersection points, $(1, 1)$ and $(4, -2)$.
