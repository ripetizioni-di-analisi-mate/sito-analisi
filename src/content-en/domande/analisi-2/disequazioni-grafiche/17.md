The double inequality $-3 \leq x - 3y < 6$ describes a strip. Which lines bound it, and which of the two is included?

- $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + 1$ excluded and $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x - 2$ included
- $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x - 1$ included and $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + 2$ excluded
- $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + 1$ included and $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x - 2$ excluded
- $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x - 1$ excluded and $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + 2$ included

## Explanation

The double inequality is equivalent to the system formed by $-3 \leq x - 3y$ and $x - 3y < 6$. From the first we obtain $3y \leq x + 3$, that is, $y \leq \dfrac{1}{3}x + 1$, so the line $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + 1$ bounds the strip from above and is **included**. From the second we obtain $-3y < -x + 6$ and, dividing by $-3$, the direction changes and we get $y > \dfrac{1}{3}x - 2$. The line $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x - 2$ therefore bounds the strip from below and is **excluded**.
