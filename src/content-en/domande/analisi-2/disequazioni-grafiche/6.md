To determine in which region the inequality $x^2 - 2x - 3y < 0$ holds, one computes $F(x, y) = x^2 - 2x - 3y$ at a test point. Which of the following points can be used?

- $(0, 0)$
- $(2, 0)$
- $(3, 1)$
- $(1, 0)$

## Explanation

The test point gives the sign of $F$ in the region it belongs to. On the boundary $F$ equals $0$, so a point of the boundary gives no information on the sign. We have $F(0, 0) = 0$, $F(2, 0) = 4 - 4 = 0$ and $F(3, 1) = 9 - 6 - 3 = 0$, so these three points belong to the parabola $x^2 - 2x - 3y = 0$. On the other hand $F(1, 0) = 1 - 2 = -1$, so $(1, 0)$ lies in one of the two regions and can be used as a **test point**. Since $F(1, 0) < 0$, by the constant sign theorem the inequality holds in the whole region that contains $(1, 0)$, that is, above the parabola.
