The shaded region is bounded by the $y$-axis and by a semicircle with center the origin and radius $3$. Which double inequality describes it?

::grafico{regione="x>=0; x^2+y^2<=9" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 tracce=0 titolo="The shaded region"}

- $0 \leq y \leq \sqrt{9 - x^2}$
- $-\sqrt{9 - y^2} \leq x \leq 0$
- $0 \leq x \leq \sqrt{3 - y^2}$
- $0 \leq x \leq \sqrt{9 - y^2}$

## Explanation

Squaring both sides of $x = \sqrt{9 - y^2}$ gives $x^2 + y^2 = 9$ with $x \geq 0$, so this equation describes the right semicircle of the circle with center the origin and radius $3$. The shaded region consists of the points lying between the $y$-axis, with equation $x = 0$, and this semicircle. On every horizontal line the $x$-coordinate therefore ranges from $0$ to $\sqrt{9 - y^2}$, and the region is $0 \leq x \leq \sqrt{9 - y^2}$. The expression $0 \leq y \leq \sqrt{9 - x^2}$ describes the upper half-disk, bounded by the $x$-axis.
