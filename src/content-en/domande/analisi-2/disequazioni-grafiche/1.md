Which of the following points is a solution of the inequality $x^2 + xy - y > 3$?

- $(1, 3)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $(2, -1)$
- $(-1, 2)$

## Explanation

A point is a solution if its coordinates, substituted into the inequality, make it true. At the point $(2, 1)$ the left-hand side equals $4 + 2 - 1 = 5$, and $5 > 3$ is true, so $(2, 1)$ **is a solution**. At the point $(2, -1)$ the left-hand side equals $4 - 2 + 1 = 3$, so the point belongs to the boundary $x^2 + xy - y = 3$, which with the symbol $>$ is **excluded**. At the points $(1, 3)$ and $(-1, 2)$ the left-hand side equals $1 + 3 - 3 = 1$ and $1 - 2 - 2 = -3$, two numbers less than $3$, so these points are not solutions either.
