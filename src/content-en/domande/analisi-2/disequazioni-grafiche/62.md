For which value of $k$ is the boundary of the inequality $kx^2 + 4y^2 - 8x \leq 9$ a circle?

- $k = 1$
- $k = -4$
- $k = 0$
- $k = 4$

## Explanation

The boundary is the curve $kx^2 + 4y^2 - 8x = 9$. A second-degree equation without the term $xy$ describes a circle only if the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are equal, so $k = 4$. Dividing by $4$ and completing the square in $x$ gives
$$
(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = \frac{13}{4} .
$$
The right-hand side is positive, therefore the curve is the **circle** with center $(1, 0)$ and radius $\dfrac{\sqrt{13}}{2}$. For $k = 1$ the two coefficients have the same sign and are different, so the boundary is an ellipse. For $k = -4$ they have opposite signs and the boundary is a hyperbola, while for $k = 0$ the term $x^2$ is missing and the boundary is a parabola with horizontal axis.
