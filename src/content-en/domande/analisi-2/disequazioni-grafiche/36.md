The inequality $x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 2y + 6 < 0$ is satisfied by the points whose distance from a point $C$ is less than $r$. What are $C$ and $r$?

- $C(3, -1)$ and $r = 2$
- $C(-3, 1)$ and $r = 4$
- $C(-6, 2)$ and $r = 2$
- $C(-3, 1)$ and $r = 2$

## Explanation

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are equal, so the boundary is a circle and we complete the squares.
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)^2 - 9 + (y - 1)^2 - 1 + 6 &< 0 \\[6pt]
(x + 3)^2 + (y - 1)^2 &< 4
\end{aligned}
$$
The left-hand side is the square of the distance from the point $(-3, 1)$, so the solutions are the points whose distance from $C(-3, 1)$ is less than $\sqrt{4} = 2$. They are the **interior points** of the circle with center $C$ and radius $2$.
