For the surface $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl( u\cos v,\; u\sin v,\; u \bigr)$, with $(u, v) \in D = [0, 1] \times [0, 2\pi]$, which statement is true?

- $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v$ does not vanish at any point of $D$
- $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v$ vanishes for $u = 0$, which is an interior point of $D$, so the surface is not regular
- $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v$ vanishes only for $u = 0$, on the boundary of $D$, so the surface is regular
- $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v$ vanishes for $v = 0$ and $v = 2\pi$, so the surface is not regular

## Explanation

The partial derivative vectors are $\mathbf{r}_u = (\cos v,\; \sin v,\; 1)$ and $\mathbf{r}_v = (-u\sin v,\; u\cos v,\; 0)$, so
$$
\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v = \bigl( -u\cos v,\; -u\sin v,\; u \bigr), \qquad \lVert \mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v \rVert = \sqrt{2}\, u .
$$
The norm vanishes if and only if $u = 0$. The points with $u = 0$ form the side $\{0\} \times [0, 2\pi]$ of the rectangle, which lies on the boundary of $D$, and they are all mapped to the vertex of the cone.

At the interior points of $D$ we therefore have $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v \neq \mathbf{0}$; moreover, $\mathbf{r}$ is injective at the interior points, since $u = z$ and $v$ is the angle of the point about the $z$-axis. Therefore the cone is a **regular** surface.
