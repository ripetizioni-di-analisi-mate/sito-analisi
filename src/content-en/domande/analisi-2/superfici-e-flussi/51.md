The cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ with $0 \leq z \leq 1$ is described by $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl( \cos u,\; \sin u,\; v \bigr)$ with $(u, v) \in D = [0, 4\pi] \times [0, 1]$. Which statement about the regularity of this surface is true?

- The surface is regular, because $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v \neq \mathbf{0}$ at every point of $D$
- The surface is not regular, because $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v = \mathbf{0}$ for $v = 0$
- The surface is not regular, because $\mathbf{r}$ is not injective at the interior points of $D$
- The surface is regular, because its trace is a cylinder

## Explanation

The partial derivative vectors are $\mathbf{r}_u = (-\sin u,\; \cos u,\; 0)$ and $\mathbf{r}_v = (0, 0, 1)$, so $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v = (\cos u,\; \sin u,\; 0)$, which has norm $1$ and never vanishes.

The definition of a regular surface also requires $\mathbf{r}$ to be injective at the interior points of $D$. For $0 < u < 2\pi$ and $0 < v < 1$ the interior points $(u, v)$ and $(u + 2\pi, v)$ have the same image, since sine and cosine have period $2\pi$.

Therefore the injectivity hypothesis **is not satisfied** and the surface is not regular. With this parametrization the cylinder is described twice, and the definition of area would give twice the area of the cylinder.
