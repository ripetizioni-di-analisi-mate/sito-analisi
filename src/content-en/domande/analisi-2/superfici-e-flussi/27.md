A homogeneous lamina has the shape of the hemisphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ with $z \geq 0$. What is the height $\bar{z}$ of its center of mass?

- $\dfrac{3}{8}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3\pi}$

## Explanation

For a homogeneous lamina the constant density cancels, so the height of the center of mass is $\bar{z} = \dfrac{1}{\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma)} \displaystyle\iint_\Sigma z \; d\sigma$.

In spherical coordinates with radius $1$ we have $z = \cos\varphi$ and $d\sigma = \sin\varphi \; d\varphi \, d\theta$, and the hemisphere corresponds to $\varphi \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$. Therefore
$$
\iint_\Sigma z \; d\sigma = 2\pi \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos\varphi \sin\varphi \; d\varphi = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \pi .
$$
The area of the hemisphere is $2\pi$, so $\bar{z} = \dfrac{\pi}{2\pi} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{3}{8}$ is the height of the center of mass of the solid half ball.
