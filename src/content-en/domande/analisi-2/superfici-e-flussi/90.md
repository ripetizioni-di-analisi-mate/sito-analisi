Let $\gamma$ be the circle of intersection of the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ with the plane $x + y + z = 0$, traversed counterclockwise as seen from the point $(1, 1, 1)$. What is the circulation of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (y,\; z,\; x)$ along $\gamma$?

- $\sqrt{3}\,\pi$
- $-\sqrt{3}\,\pi$
- $-3\pi$
- $-\pi$

## Explanation

The plane passes through the center of the sphere, so $\gamma$ has radius $1$ and is the boundary of the disk $D$ of radius $1$ contained in the plane. The unit normal to the plane directed toward $(1, 1, 1)$ is $\mathbf{n} = \dfrac{(1, 1, 1)}{\sqrt{3}}$, and the counterclockwise direction as seen from $(1, 1, 1)$ is the one compatible with $\mathbf{n}$. The field is of class $C^1$, therefore **Stokes' theorem** applies.

We have
$$
\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} = \left( \frac{\partial x}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial z}{\partial z},\; \frac{\partial y}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial x}{\partial x},\; \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial y}{\partial y} \right) = (-1,\; -1,\; -1) ,
$$
so $\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = -\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = -\sqrt{3}$, constant on $D$. The circulation is therefore the product of $-\sqrt{3}$ and the area of $D$, that is $-\sqrt{3}\,\pi$.
