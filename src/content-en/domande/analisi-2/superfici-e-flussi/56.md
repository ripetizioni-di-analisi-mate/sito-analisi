At the pole $(0, 0, 2)$ of the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 4$ the vector $\mathbf{r}_\varphi \wedge \mathbf{r}_\theta$ of the parametrization $\mathbf{r}(\varphi, \theta) = \bigl( 2\sin\varphi\cos\theta,\; 2\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\; 2\cos\varphi \bigr)$ is zero. Which statement about the tangent plane to the sphere at $(0, 0, 2)$ is true?

- The tangent plane does not exist, because the normal vector at the pole is zero
- The tangent plane is $z = 0$, because the normal vector at the pole is zero
- The tangent plane exists but is not unique, because at the pole $\theta$ can take any value
- The tangent plane exists and is $z = 2$, as obtained from the Cartesian parametrization $z = \sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}$

## Explanation

The vector $\mathbf{r}_\varphi \wedge \mathbf{r}_\theta$ vanishes at the pole because of a property of the chosen parametrization: all the points with $\varphi = 0$ have the same image $(0, 0, 2)$, which comes from the boundary of the parameter rectangle.

Near the pole the sphere is also the graph of $f(x, y) = \sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}$, which is of class $C^1$ near $(0, 0)$. We have $f_x = -\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}}$ and $f_y = -\dfrac{y}{\sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}}$, which vanish at $(0, 0)$, so for this parametrization $\mathbf{N} = (-f_x, -f_y, 1) = (0, 0, 1)$.

Therefore the tangent plane exists and is the horizontal plane $z = 2$.
