The helicoid $\Sigma$ is parametrized by $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl( u\cos v,\; u\sin v,\; v \bigr)$, with $(u, v) \in [0, 1] \times [0, 2\pi]$, and is oriented so that the unit normal has negative third component at the points off the $z$-axis. What is the flux of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (y,\; -x,\; 0)$ through $\Sigma$?

- $-\pi$
- $\pi$
- $-2\pi$
- $0$

## Explanation

The partial derivative vectors are $\mathbf{r}_u = (\cos v,\; \sin v,\; 0)$ and $\mathbf{r}_v = (-u\sin v,\; u\cos v,\; 1)$, so
$$
\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v = \bigl( \sin v,\; -\cos v,\; u \bigr) .
$$
For $u > 0$ the third component is positive, therefore the parametrization induces the orientation opposite to the chosen one, and the flux is the integral of $\mathbf{F} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v)$ with the sign changed.

On the surface the field is $\mathbf{F} = (u\sin v,\; -u\cos v,\; 0)$, so $\mathbf{F} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v) = u\sin^2 v + u\cos^2 v = u$. We obtain
$$
\Phi = -\int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 u \; du \, dv = -2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{2} = -\pi .
$$
