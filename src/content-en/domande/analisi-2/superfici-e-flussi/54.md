Which line, written in the form $(x, y, z) = \dots$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$, passes through the point $(1, 1, 1)$ and is orthogonal to the graph of $f(x, y) = e^{x - y}$ at that point?

- $(1 + t,\; 1 - t,\; 1 + t)$
- $(1 - t,\; 1 + t,\; 1)$
- $(1 - t,\; 1 + t,\; 1 + t)$
- $(1 + t,\; 1 + t,\; 1 + t)$

## Explanation

The required line has the direction of the normal vector to the graph, $\mathbf{N} = (-f_x,\; -f_y,\; 1)$.

We have $f_x = e^{x - y}$ and $f_y = -e^{x - y}$, so at $(1, 1)$ they equal $f_x = 1$ and $f_y = -1$, and therefore $\mathbf{N} = (-1,\; 1,\; 1)$. Hence the line is $(x, y, z) = (1, 1, 1) + t\,(-1, 1, 1)$, that is $(1 - t,\; 1 + t,\; 1 + t)$.

The line with direction $(1, -1, 1)$ is obtained by forgetting the minus signs in the first two components of $\mathbf{N}$; its direction is not parallel to $\mathbf{N}$, so the line is not orthogonal to the tangent plane.
