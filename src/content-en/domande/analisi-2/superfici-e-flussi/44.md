Let $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( -y + z^2,\; x,\; xyz \bigr)$. What is the flux of $\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F}$ through the hemisphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ with $z \geq 0$, oriented outward?

- $-2\pi$
- $2\pi$
- $4\pi$
- $\pi$

## Explanation

The boundary of the hemisphere is the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ in the plane $z = 0$. At the points with $z > 0$ the outward normal has positive third component, so the compatible orientation of the boundary is counterclockwise as seen from above. The field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, so by **Stokes' theorem** the flux of the curl equals the circulation of $\mathbf{F}$ along the boundary.

With $\mathbf{r}(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; 0)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, we have $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t,\; 0)$ and $\mathbf{r}'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t,\; 0)$, so $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{r}' = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$. Therefore the required flux equals
$$
\iint_\Sigma \operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \; d\sigma = \int_0^{2\pi} 1 \; dt = 2\pi .
$$
