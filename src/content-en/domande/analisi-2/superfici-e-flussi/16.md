The sphere of radius $2$ is parametrized by $\mathbf{r}(\varphi, \theta) = \bigl( 2\sin\varphi\cos\theta,\; 2\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\; 2\cos\varphi \bigr)$, with $(\varphi, \theta) \in [0, \pi] \times [0, 2\pi]$. What is the coordinate line $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$?

- The circle of radius $1$ in the plane $z = \sqrt{3}$
- The circle of radius $2$ in the plane $z = 1$
- The semicircle of the sphere contained in the half-plane $y = \sqrt{3}\, x$, $x \geq 0$
- The circle of radius $\sqrt{3}$ in the plane $z = 1$

## Explanation

For $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ we have $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the coordinate line is
$$
\theta \mapsto \bigl( \sqrt{3}\cos\theta,\; \sqrt{3}\sin\theta,\; 1 \bigr), \qquad \theta \in [0, 2\pi] .
$$
The third component is constant and equal to $1$, while $x^2 + y^2 = 3$. Therefore the coordinate line is the **parallel** of radius $\sqrt{3}$ in the plane $z = 1$, centered at $(0, 0, 1)$. The semicircle in the half-plane $y = \sqrt{3}\, x$, $x \geq 0$, is the meridian obtained by fixing $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
