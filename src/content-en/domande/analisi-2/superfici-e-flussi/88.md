The lateral surface $\Sigma$ of the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ with $0 \leq z \leq 1$ is oriented by the unit normal pointing away from the $z$-axis. The boundary of $\Sigma$ consists of the circle $\gamma_0$ in the plane $z = 0$ and the circle $\gamma_1$ in the plane $z = 1$. Which orientation of the boundary is compatible with that of $\Sigma$?

- $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ both counterclockwise, as seen from above
- $\gamma_0$ counterclockwise and $\gamma_1$ clockwise, as seen from above
- $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ both clockwise, as seen from above
- $\gamma_0$ clockwise and $\gamma_1$ counterclockwise, as seen from above

## Explanation

At the point $(1, 0, 0)$ of $\gamma_0$ the unit normal is $(1, 0, 0)$ and the surface lies above the circle. An observer aligned with $(1, 0, 0)$ who moves in the direction $(0, 1, 0)$ has on their left the direction $(1, 0, 0) \wedge (0, 1, 0) = (0, 0, 1)$, that is, the surface; therefore $\gamma_0$ must be traversed counterclockwise as seen from above.

On $\gamma_1$ the surface lies below the circle, so the compatible direction is the opposite one, clockwise as seen from above.

**Stokes' theorem** with $\mathbf{F} = (-y, x, 0)$ confirms the choice. The curl $(0, 0, 2)$ is tangent to $\Sigma$, so its flux is zero, and indeed the circulations along $\gamma_0$ counterclockwise and along $\gamma_1$ clockwise equal $2\pi$ and $-2\pi$, with zero sum.
