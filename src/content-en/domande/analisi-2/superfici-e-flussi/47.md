The graph of $f(x, y) = x^2 y$ is parametrized by $\mathbf{r}(x, y) = \bigl( x,\; y,\; x^2 y \bigr)$. What is the normal vector $\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{r}_x \wedge \mathbf{r}_y$ at the point corresponding to $(1, 2)$?

- $(4,\; 1,\; 1)$
- $(-4,\; -1,\; 1)$
- $(-2,\; -1,\; 1)$
- $(-1,\; -4,\; 1)$

## Explanation

For a Cartesian surface the normal vector is $\mathbf{N} = (-f_x,\; -f_y,\; 1)$.

We have $f_x = 2xy$ and $f_y = x^2$, so at $(1, 2)$ they equal $f_x(1, 2) = 4$ and $f_y(1, 2) = 1$. Therefore $\mathbf{N} = (-4,\; -1,\; 1)$, which has third component $1$ and points upward.
