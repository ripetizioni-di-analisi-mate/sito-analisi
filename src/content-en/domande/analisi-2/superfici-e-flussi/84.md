The curl of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( xy,\; e^{z},\; \sin x \bigr)$ crosses the whole sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$. What is its outward flux through the sphere?

- $4\pi$
- $-\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $0$
- $2\pi$

## Explanation

The plane $z = 0$ divides the sphere into the hemispheres $\Sigma_1$, with $z \geq 0$, and $\Sigma_2$, with $z \leq 0$, which have the same boundary, the circle $\gamma$ of radius $1$ in the plane $z = 0$. With the outward normal, the compatible orientation of $\gamma$ is counterclockwise as seen from above for $\Sigma_1$ and clockwise as seen from above for $\Sigma_2$.

The field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, so by **Stokes' theorem** the two fluxes of the curl are the circulations of $\mathbf{F}$ along $\gamma$ traversed in the two directions, which are opposite. Therefore the outward flux through the whole sphere equals $0$, without computing the curl. The same result follows from the divergence theorem, since $\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F}) = 0$.
