The hemisphere $\Sigma = \{ x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1,\; z \geq 0 \}$ is oriented by the unit normal pointing out of the ball. What is the flux through $\Sigma$ of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( yz,\; xz,\; 1 + x^2 \bigr)$?

- $0$
- $\pi$
- $-\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

We have $\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F} = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0$, because $yz$ does not depend on $x$, $xz$ does not depend on $y$ and $1 + x^2$ does not depend on $z$.

The hemisphere is not closed; we add the disk $D = \{ x^2 + y^2 \leq 1,\; z = 0 \}$ oriented downward. Then $\Sigma \cup D$ is the boundary of the half ball, oriented by the outward unit normal, and the field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$. By the **divergence theorem** the total outward flux is the triple integral of a zero function, so the flux through $\Sigma$ is the opposite of the flux through $D$ oriented downward.

On $D$ oriented upward we have $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{k} = 1 + x^2$ and $d\sigma = dx \, dy$. Since $\displaystyle\iint_D x^2 \; dx \, dy = \frac{\pi}{4}$, we obtain
$$
\Phi_\Sigma = \iint_D \bigl( 1 + x^2 \bigr) \; dx \, dy = \pi + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{5\pi}{4} .
$$
