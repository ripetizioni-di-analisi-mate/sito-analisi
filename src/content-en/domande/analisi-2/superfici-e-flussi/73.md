Let $f(x, y, z) = x^2 y + z^3$. What is $\operatorname{div}(\nabla f)$?

- $2y + 3z^2$
- $2x + 6z$
- $0$
- $2y + 6z$

## Explanation

The gradient is $\nabla f = \bigl( 2xy,\; x^2,\; 3z^2 \bigr)$.

The **divergence** of the gradient is the sum of the derivatives of each component with respect to the corresponding variable,
$$
\operatorname{div}(\nabla f) = \frac{\partial (2xy)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (x^2)}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial (3z^2)}{\partial z} = 2y + 0 + 6z ,
$$
that is, the sum of the pure second derivatives $f_{xx} + f_{yy} + f_{zz}$. The zero vector is instead the curl of the gradient, by the identity $\operatorname{rot}(\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}$.
