The lateral surface of the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ with $0 \leq z \leq 3$ is parametrized by $\mathbf{r}(t, \theta) = \bigl( \cos\theta,\; \sin\theta,\; t \bigr)$, with $(t, \theta) \in [0, 3] \times [0, 2\pi]$, and is oriented by this parametrization. What is the flux of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (x, y, z)$ through it?

- $6\pi$
- $-6\pi$
- $0$
- $-2\pi$

## Explanation

In the order of the parameters the partial derivative vectors are $\mathbf{r}_t = (0, 0, 1)$ and $\mathbf{r}_\theta = (-\sin\theta,\; \cos\theta,\; 0)$, so
$$
\mathbf{r}_t \wedge \mathbf{r}_\theta = \bigl( 0 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot \cos\theta,\; 1 \cdot (-\sin\theta) - 0 \cdot 0,\; 0 \cdot \cos\theta - 0 \cdot (-\sin\theta) \bigr) = \bigl( -\cos\theta,\; -\sin\theta,\; 0 \bigr) .
$$
This vector points toward the $z$-axis, therefore the induced orientation is the one with the inward normal.

On the surface $\mathbf{F} = (\cos\theta,\; \sin\theta,\; t)$, so $\mathbf{F} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_t \wedge \mathbf{r}_\theta) = -\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = -1$, and
$$
\Phi = \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^3 (-1) \; dt \, d\theta = -6\pi .
$$
