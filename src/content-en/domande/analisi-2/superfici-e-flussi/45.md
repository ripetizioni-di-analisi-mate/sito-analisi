The hemisphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ with $z \leq 0$ is oriented with the outward unit normal, which has negative third component at the points with $z < 0$. Which orientation of the boundary, the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ in the plane $z = 0$, is compatible with this orientation?

- Clockwise as seen from above
- Counterclockwise as seen from above
- Both directions, because the boundary is a circle
- Neither, because the hemisphere is a closed surface

## Explanation

The compatible direction can be identified with Stokes' theorem applied to the field $\mathbf{F} = (-y, x, 0)$, which has $\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} = (0, 0, 2)$.

On the lower hemisphere the outward unit normal is $\mathbf{n} = (x, y, z)$, so $\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = 2z \leq 0$ and the flux of the curl is negative; in spherical coordinates it equals $2 \cdot 2\pi \displaystyle\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi} \cos\varphi \sin\varphi \; d\varphi = -2\pi$.

The circulation of $\mathbf{F}$ along the circle traversed counterclockwise equals $2\pi$ instead. The equality in Stokes' theorem holds only with the compatible orientation, therefore the compatible direction is **clockwise** as seen from above, which gives circulation $-2\pi$.
