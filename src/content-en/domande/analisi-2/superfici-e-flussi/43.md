What is the circulation of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (z, x, y)$ along the boundary of the triangle with vertices $(1, 0, 0)$, $(0, 1, 0)$, $(0, 0, 1)$, traversed from $(1, 0, 0)$ to $(0, 1, 0)$, then to $(0, 0, 1)$ and back to $(1, 0, 0)$?

- $-\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

## Explanation

As the surface we choose the triangle itself, which is the graph of $f(x, y) = 1 - x - y$ over the triangle $T$ with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$. Seen from above, the boundary is traversed from $(1, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$ and then to $(0, 0)$, that is, counterclockwise, so it is oriented compatibly with the upward orientation, whose normal vector is $(-f_x, -f_y, 1) = (1, 1, 1)$.

The field is of class $C^1$, therefore **Stokes' theorem** applies. We compute $\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} = (1 - 0,\; 1 - 0,\; 1 - 0) = (1, 1, 1)$, so $\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} \cdot (1, 1, 1) = 3$ and
$$
\oint \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_T 3 \; dx \, dy = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2} .
$$
