On the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 4$ consider the zone $\Sigma_h$ formed by the points with $h \leq z \leq h + 1$, where $h \in [-2, 1]$. Which statement about the area of $\Sigma_h$ is true?

- It equals $4\pi$ for every $h \in [-2, 1]$
- It is largest for $h = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, for the zone centered on the equator
- It equals $2\pi$ for every $h \in [-2, 1]$
- It tends to $0$ as $h \to 1$, because the zone becomes a cap

## Explanation

In spherical coordinates with radius $2$ we have $z = 2\cos\varphi$ and $d\sigma = 4\sin\varphi \; d\varphi \, d\theta$. Let $\varphi_1 \leq \varphi_2$ be the colatitudes of the planes $z = h + 1$ and $z = h$; then
$$
\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_h) = 2\pi \int_{\varphi_1}^{\varphi_2} 4\sin\varphi \; d\varphi = 8\pi \bigl( \cos\varphi_1 - \cos\varphi_2 \bigr) .
$$
Since $2\cos\varphi_1 = h + 1$ and $2\cos\varphi_2 = h$, we have $\cos\varphi_1 - \cos\varphi_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_h) = 4\pi$ for every $h$.

Therefore the area of a spherical zone depends only on its height, and equals $2\pi R$ times the height; here $2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 4\pi$.
