What is $\displaystyle\iint_\Sigma (x + y + z) \; d\sigma$, where $\Sigma$ is the portion of the plane $x + y + z = 1$ contained in the first octant?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

At the points of $\Sigma$ the integrand is identically equal to $1$, so the surface integral coincides with the area of $\Sigma$.

The surface is the graph of $f(x, y) = 1 - x - y$ over the triangle $T$ with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, which has area $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $f_x = f_y = -1$, the area element is $d\sigma = \sqrt{3} \, dx \, dy$, therefore
$$
\iint_\Sigma (x + y + z) \; d\sigma = \sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} .
$$
