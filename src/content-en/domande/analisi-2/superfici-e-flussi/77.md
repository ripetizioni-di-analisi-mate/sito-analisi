Let $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$ and, for $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, let $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = r^{\alpha}\,(x,\; y,\; z)$, defined for $(x, y, z) \neq (0, 0, 0)$. For which value of $\alpha$ is the field solenoidal?

- $\alpha = -3$
- $\alpha = -2$
- $\alpha = -1$
- $\alpha = 3$

## Explanation

We have $\dfrac{\partial r}{\partial x} = \dfrac{x}{r}$, so by the product rule and the chain rule
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \bigl( r^{\alpha} x \bigr) = r^{\alpha} + \alpha\, r^{\alpha - 1} \cdot \frac{x}{r} \cdot x = r^{\alpha} + \alpha\, r^{\alpha - 2} x^2 .
$$
Adding the three analogous derivatives and using $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = r^2$ we obtain
$$
\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F} = 3 r^{\alpha} + \alpha\, r^{\alpha - 2}\, r^2 = (3 + \alpha)\, r^{\alpha} .
$$
Since $r^{\alpha} > 0$ away from the origin, the divergence is identically zero if and only if $\alpha = -3$. For $\alpha = -3$ we get the field $\dfrac{(x, y, z)}{r^3}$, whose magnitude is $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$.
