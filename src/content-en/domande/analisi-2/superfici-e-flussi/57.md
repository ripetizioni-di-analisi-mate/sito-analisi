A regular surface $\Sigma = \mathbf{r}(D)$ has area $5$. What is the area of the surface parametrized by $\mathbf{s}(u, v) = 3\,\mathbf{r}(u, v) + (1, 2, 0)$, with $(u, v) \in D$?

- $15$
- $5$
- $135$
- $45$

## Explanation

The constant vector $(1, 2, 0)$ has zero derivatives, so $\mathbf{s}_u = 3\,\mathbf{r}_u$ and $\mathbf{s}_v = 3\,\mathbf{r}_v$.

The cross product is linear in each factor, therefore $\mathbf{s}_u \wedge \mathbf{s}_v = 9\, (\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v)$ and $\lVert \mathbf{s}_u \wedge \mathbf{s}_v \rVert = 9\, \lVert \mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v \rVert$.

By the definition of **area** we obtain
$$
\operatorname{Area} = \iint_D 9\, \lVert \mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v \rVert \; du \, dv = 9 \cdot 5 = 45 .
$$
The translation does not change the area, while the dilation of ratio $3$ multiplies it by $3^2$.
