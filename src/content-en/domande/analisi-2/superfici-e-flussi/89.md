The field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \left( -\dfrac{y}{x^2 + y^2},\; \dfrac{x}{x^2 + y^2},\; 0 \right)$, defined off the $z$-axis, has zero curl at every point of its domain. What is its circulation along the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ of the plane $z = 1$, traversed counterclockwise as seen from above?

- $2\pi$
- $0$
- $4\pi$
- $-2\pi$

## Explanation

The disk bounded by the circle contains the point $(0, 0, 1)$ of the $z$-axis, where $\mathbf{F}$ is not defined. The hypothesis of **Stokes' theorem** on the field is not satisfied, so it does not follow from the zero curl that the circulation is zero.

The circulation is computed directly with $\mathbf{r}(t) = (2\cos t,\; 2\sin t,\; 1)$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$. We have $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) = \dfrac{1}{4}\,(-2\sin t,\; 2\cos t,\; 0)$ and $\mathbf{r}'(t) = (-2\sin t,\; 2\cos t,\; 0)$, so
$$
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) = \frac{4\sin^2 t + 4\cos^2 t}{4} = 1 .
$$
Therefore the circulation equals $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} 1 \; dt = 2\pi$, regardless of the radius of the circle.
