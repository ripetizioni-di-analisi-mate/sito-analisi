The cube $[-1, 1]^3$ contains the origin, where the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \dfrac{(x, y, z)}{(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ is not defined. What is the outward flux of $\mathbf{F}$ through the boundary of the cube?

- $4\pi$
- $0$
- $8$
- $2\pi$

## Explanation

The field has the form $r^{\alpha}(x, y, z)$ with $\alpha = -3$, so $\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F} = (3 + \alpha)\, r^{\alpha} = 0$ away from the origin. The origin is interior to the cube and the field is not defined there, therefore the divergence theorem does not apply to the cube.

Remove from the cube the ball centered at the origin with radius $\varepsilon < 1$. The remaining region is a regular domain on which $\mathbf{F}$ is of class $C^1$, so by the **divergence theorem** its outward flux is zero. Therefore the outward flux through the cube equals the flux through the sphere of radius $\varepsilon$ oriented by the normal pointing away from the origin.

On this sphere we have $\mathbf{n} = \dfrac{(x, y, z)}{\varepsilon}$ and $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^2}$, so the flux equals $\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^2} \cdot 4\pi\varepsilon^2 = 4\pi$.
