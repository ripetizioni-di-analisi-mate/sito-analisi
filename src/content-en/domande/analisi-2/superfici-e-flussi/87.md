What is the circulation of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (0,\; z,\; 0)$ along the curve of intersection of the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ with the plane $z = x$, traversed counterclockwise as seen from above?

- $0$
- $-\pi$
- $\sqrt{2}\,\pi$
- $\pi$

## Explanation

The curve is the boundary of the portion $\Sigma$ of the plane $z = x$ whose projection is the disk $D$ of radius $1$. Oriented upward, $\Sigma$ has normal vector $(-f_x, -f_y, 1) = (-1, 0, 1)$ and compatible boundary traversed counterclockwise as seen from above. The field is of class $C^1$, so **Stokes' theorem** applies.

We have
$$
\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} = \left( 0 - \frac{\partial z}{\partial z},\; 0 - 0,\; 0 - 0 \right) = (-1,\; 0,\; 0) ,
$$
so $\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} \cdot (-1, 0, 1) = 1$. By the formula for the flux through a Cartesian surface the circulation is therefore the area of $D$, that is $\pi$.
