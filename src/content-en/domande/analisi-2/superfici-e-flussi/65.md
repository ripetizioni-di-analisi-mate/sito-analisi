What is $\displaystyle\iint_\Sigma \sqrt{1 + x^2 + y^2} \; d\sigma$, where $\Sigma$ is the helicoid $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl( u\cos v,\; u\sin v,\; v \bigr)$ with $(u, v) \in [0, 1] \times [0, 2\pi]$?

- $\dfrac{8\pi}{3}$
- $2\pi$
- $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{16\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The partial derivative vectors are $\mathbf{r}_u = (\cos v,\; \sin v,\; 0)$ and $\mathbf{r}_v = (-u\sin v,\; u\cos v,\; 1)$, so
$$
\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v = \bigl( \sin v,\; -\cos v,\; u \bigr), \qquad \lVert \mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v \rVert = \sqrt{1 + u^2} .
$$
On the surface we have $x^2 + y^2 = u^2$, therefore the function equals $\sqrt{1 + u^2}$ and its product with the norm of the normal vector is $1 + u^2$. We obtain
$$
\iint_\Sigma \sqrt{1 + x^2 + y^2} \; d\sigma = \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 (1 + u^2) \; du \, dv = 2\pi \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{8\pi}{3} .
$$
