- $\dfrac{12\pi}{5}$

## Explanation

The ball $V$ of radius $1$ is a regular domain, its boundary is the sphere oriented with the outward unit normal and the field is of class $C^1$ on all of $\mathbb{R}^3$. The hypotheses are satisfied, so the **divergence theorem** applies and the outward flux is the triple integral of $\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F}$ over $V$.

We have $\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F} = 3x^2 + 3y^2 + 3z^2$, which in spherical coordinates equals $3r^2$. With the Jacobian $r^2 \sin\varphi$ we obtain
$$
\Phi = \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^{\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 3 r^2 \cdot r^2 \sin\varphi \; dr \, d\varphi \, d\theta = 3 \cdot 2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \frac{12\pi}{5} .
$$
