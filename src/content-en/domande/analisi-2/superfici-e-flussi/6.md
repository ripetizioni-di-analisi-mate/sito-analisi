- $4\pi$
- $\dfrac{4\pi}{15}$
- $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ does not change if two coordinates are interchanged, so the surface integrals of $x^2$, $y^2$ and $z^2$ over $\Sigma$ are equal.

By linearity their sum is the integral of $x^2 + y^2 + z^2$, which on $\Sigma$ is identically equal to $1$. Therefore the sum is the area of the sphere of radius $1$, that is, $4\pi$.

Each of the three integrals is then one third of the area,
$$
\iint_\Sigma z^2 \; d\sigma = \frac{4\pi}{3} .
$$
The same value is obtained in spherical coordinates from $2\pi \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \cos^2\varphi \, \sin\varphi \; d\varphi = 2\pi \cdot \frac{2}{3}$.
