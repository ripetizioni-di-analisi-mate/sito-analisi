Which of the following fields $\mathbf{G}$ satisfies $\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{G} = (1,\; 1,\; 1)$ at every point of $\mathbb{R}^3$?

- $\mathbf{G} = (z,\; x,\; y)$
- $\mathbf{G} = (y,\; z,\; x)$
- $\mathbf{G} = (0,\; 0,\; x + y)$
- $\mathbf{G} = (x,\; y,\; z)$

## Explanation

For $\mathbf{G} = (z, x, y)$ each component of the **curl** contains a single nonzero derivative,
$$
\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{G} = \left( \frac{\partial y}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial x}{\partial z},\; \frac{\partial z}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial y}{\partial x},\; \frac{\partial x}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} \right) = (1,\; 1,\; 1) .
$$
The field $(y, z, x)$ has curl $(-1, -1, -1)$, because the nonzero derivatives appear with a minus sign. The field $(0, 0, x + y)$ has curl $(1, -1, 0)$, and the field $(x, y, z)$ is the gradient of $\dfrac{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}{2}$, so its curl is zero.
