For the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ parametrized by $\mathbf{r}(\varphi, \theta) = \bigl( \sin\varphi\cos\theta,\; \sin\varphi\sin\theta,\; \cos\varphi \bigr)$, with $(\varphi, \theta) \in [0, \pi] \times [0, 2\pi]$, which statement about the vector $\mathbf{r}_\varphi \wedge \mathbf{r}_\theta$ is true?

- It equals $-\sin\varphi \; \mathbf{r}(\varphi, \theta)$, so it points into the sphere
- It equals $\mathbf{r}(\varphi, \theta)$, so it has norm $1$ at every point
- It equals $\sin\varphi \; \mathbf{r}(\varphi, \theta)$, so it points outward for $0<\varphi<\pi$ and vanishes at the poles
- It is tangent to the sphere at every point

## Explanation

The partial derivative vectors are $\mathbf{r}_\varphi = (\cos\varphi\cos\theta,\; \cos\varphi\sin\theta,\; -\sin\varphi)$ and $\mathbf{r}_\theta = (-\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\; \sin\varphi\cos\theta,\; 0)$. Their cross product is
$$
\mathbf{r}_\varphi \wedge \mathbf{r}_\theta = \bigl( \sin^2\varphi\cos\theta,\; \sin^2\varphi\sin\theta,\; \sin\varphi\cos\varphi \bigr) = \sin\varphi \; \mathbf{r}(\varphi, \theta) .
$$
At the interior points of the parameter rectangle we have $\sin\varphi > 0$, so $\mathbf{r}_\varphi \wedge \mathbf{r}_\theta$ has the same direction as the position vector $\mathbf{r}$, which points away from the origin. Therefore the parametrization induces the orientation of the **outward unit normal**, and the norm of the vector is $\sin\varphi$.
