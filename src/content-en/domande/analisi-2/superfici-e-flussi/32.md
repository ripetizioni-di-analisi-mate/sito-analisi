What is the flux of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (y, x, z)$ through the portion of the plane $z = x + y$ with $(x, y) \in [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$, oriented upward?

- $0$
- $1$
- $2$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The surface is the graph of $f(x, y) = x + y$, with $f_x = f_y = 1$. On the surface the components of the field are $F_1 = y$, $F_2 = x$ and $F_3 = x + y$, so the integrand of the formula for the **flux through a Cartesian surface** is
$$
-F_1 f_x - F_2 f_y + F_3 = -y - x + (x + y) = 0 .
$$
Therefore the flux is **zero**. The result also has a geometric interpretation: at the points of the plane the field equals $y\,(1, 0, 1) + x\,(0, 1, 1)$, which is a combination of two vectors parallel to the plane, so $\mathbf{F}$ is tangent to the surface and $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = 0$.
