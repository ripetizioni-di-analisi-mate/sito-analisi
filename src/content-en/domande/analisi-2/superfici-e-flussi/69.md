Let $\Sigma$ be the portion of the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ lying in the first octant, $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$, $z \geq 0$, oriented by the unit normal pointing out of the ball. What is the flux of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (x,\; 0,\; 0)$ through $\Sigma$?

- $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$

## Explanation

On the sphere of radius $1$ the outward unit normal is $\mathbf{n} = (x, y, z)$, so $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = x^2$ and the flux is $\displaystyle\iint_\Sigma x^2 \; d\sigma$.

On the whole sphere the integrals of $x^2$, $y^2$ and $z^2$ are equal, because the sphere does not change when two coordinates are swapped, and their sum is the area $4\pi$. Hence the integral of $x^2$ over the whole sphere equals $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.

The function $x^2$ does not change when the sign of a coordinate is changed, and the eight octants are obtained from one another by these sign changes, therefore each of them contributes one eighth of the integral. We obtain
$$
\Phi = \frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{4\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}{6} .
$$
