What is the circulation of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( xy,\; x^2,\; 0 \bigr)$ along the boundary of the square $Q = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ of the plane $z = 0$, traversed counterclockwise as seen from above?

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=1.5 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1.4 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" curve="(abs(t)-abs(t-1)+1)/2-(abs(t-2)-abs(t-2-1)+1)/2,(abs(t-1)-abs(t-1-1)+1)/2-(abs(t-3)-abs(t-3-1)+1)/2,0,4" vettori="0.3,0,0.65,0;1,0.3,1,0.65;0.7,1,0.35,1;0,0.7,0,0.35" titolo="The boundary of the square $Q$ seen from above, traversed counterclockwise"}

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The square, oriented by $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{k}$, has as compatible boundary the boundary traversed counterclockwise as seen from above, and the field is of class $C^1$, so **Stokes' theorem** applies.

The surface is flat, therefore the flux of the curl is the integral of the third component of the curl, as in **Green's formula**,
$$
\oint_{\partial Q} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_Q \left( \frac{\partial (x^2)}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial (xy)}{\partial y} \right) dx \, dy = \iint_Q (2x - x) \; dx \, dy .
$$
The integral of $x$ over the unit square is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the circulation is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
