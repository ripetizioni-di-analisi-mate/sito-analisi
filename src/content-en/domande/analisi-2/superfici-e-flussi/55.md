The tangent plane to the graph of $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 1}$ at the point with coordinates $(x_0, y_0) = (2, 2)$ meets the $z$-axis at one point. What is the height of that point?

- $3$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{17}{3}$
- $-5$

## Explanation

We have $f(2, 2) = \sqrt{9} = 3$. By the chain rule $f_x = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 1}}$ and $f_y = \dfrac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 1}}$, so $f_x(2, 2) = f_y(2, 2) = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

Therefore the tangent plane has equation
$$
z = 3 + \frac{2}{3}(x - 2) + \frac{2}{3}(y - 2) .
$$
The points of the $z$-axis have $x = y = 0$, so the required height is $3 - \dfrac{4}{3} - \dfrac{4}{3} = \dfrac{1}{3}$.
