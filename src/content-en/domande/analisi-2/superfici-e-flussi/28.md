A lamina has the shape of the cone $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ with $0 \leq z \leq 1$ and surface density $\mu(x, y, z) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. What is its mass?

- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $\sqrt{2}\,\pi$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi}{3}$
- $2\sqrt{2}\,\pi$

## Explanation

The mass is the surface integral of the density, $M = \displaystyle\iint_\Sigma \mu \; d\sigma$. The cone is the graph of $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ over the disk of radius $1$, and for $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$ we have $f_x^2 + f_y^2 = 1$, so $d\sigma = \sqrt{2} \, dx \, dy$.

In polar coordinates the density equals $\rho$ and the Jacobian is $\rho$, therefore
$$
M = \sqrt{2} \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 \rho \cdot \rho \; d\rho \, d\theta = \sqrt{2} \cdot 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi}{3} .
$$
