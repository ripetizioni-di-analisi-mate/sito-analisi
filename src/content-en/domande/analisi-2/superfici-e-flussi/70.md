The cone $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ with $0 \leq z \leq 1$ is oriented upward. What is the flux through it of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (x,\; y,\; 0)$?

- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $-\pi$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The surface is the graph of $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ over the disk of radius $1$, and for $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$ we have $f_x = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$ and $f_y = \dfrac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$. Hence the integrand in the formula for the **flux through a Cartesian surface** is
$$
-F_1 f_x - F_2 f_y + F_3 = -\frac{x^2 + y^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} = -\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} ,
$$
which in polar coordinates equals $-\rho$. With the Jacobian $\rho$ we obtain
$$
\Phi = -\int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 \rho^2 \; d\rho \, d\theta = -\frac{2\pi}{3} .
$$
The sign is negative because the field points away from the $z$-axis, while the normal of the upward-oriented cone points toward the axis.
