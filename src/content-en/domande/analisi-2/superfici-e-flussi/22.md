For which of the following functions does the graph over the disk $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1 \}$ have area equal to $\pi$?

- $f(x, y) = x + y$
- $f(x, y) = 3$
- $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$
- $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$

## Explanation

By the formula for the **area of a Cartesian surface**, the area of the graph is $\displaystyle\iint_D \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2} \; dx \, dy$, and the area of the disk $D$ is $\pi$.

The integrand is at least $1$, and it equals $1$ only where $f_x = f_y = 0$. Hence the area of the graph equals $\pi$ if and only if the gradient of $f$ is zero at every point of $D$, that is, if $f$ is constant.

Among the four functions only $f(x, y) = 3$ is constant: its graph is the disk of radius $1$ in the plane $z = 3$, which has area $\pi$. For $x + y$ the integrand equals $\sqrt{3}$ and the area is $\sqrt{3}\,\pi$; for $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ it equals $\sqrt{2}$ and the area is $\sqrt{2}\,\pi$; for $x^2 + y^2$ the area is $\dfrac{\pi}{6} \bigl( 5\sqrt{5} - 1 \bigr)$.
