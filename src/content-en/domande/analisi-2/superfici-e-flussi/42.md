The field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \dfrac{(x, y, z)}{(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$, defined for $(x, y, z) \neq (0, 0, 0)$, has zero divergence at every point of its domain. Which statement about the outward flux of $\mathbf{F}$ through the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ is true?

- The flux equals $4\pi$, and the divergence theorem does not apply to the ball because $\mathbf{F}$ is not defined at the origin
- The flux equals $0$, by the divergence theorem applied to the ball
- The flux equals $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, that is, the volume of the ball
- The flux equals $-4\pi$, because the field points toward the origin

## Explanation

The divergence theorem requires a field of class $C^1$ on an open set containing the ball. The origin belongs to the ball and $\mathbf{F}$ is not defined there, so the hypothesis **is not satisfied** and the theorem does not apply.

The flux is computed directly. On the sphere of radius $1$ we have $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$, so $\mathbf{F} = (x, y, z)$, and the outward unit normal is $\mathbf{n} = (x, y, z)$. Therefore $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = 1$ and the flux is the area of the sphere, $\Phi = 4\pi$.
