For the surface $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl( u + v,\; u - v,\; uv \bigr)$, what is the normal vector $\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v$ at the point corresponding to $(u_0, v_0) = (1, 2)$?

- $(-3,\; -1,\; 2)$
- $(3,\; 1,\; -2)$
- $(3,\; -1,\; -2)$
- $(1,\; 3,\; -2)$

## Explanation

The partial derivative vectors are $\mathbf{r}_u = (1,\; 1,\; v)$ and $\mathbf{r}_v = (1,\; -1,\; u)$, so at $(1, 2)$ they equal $\mathbf{r}_u = (1, 1, 2)$ and $\mathbf{r}_v = (1, -1, 1)$.

The **normal vector** is their cross product,
$$
\mathbf{N} = \bigl( 1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot (-1),\; 2 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 1,\; 1 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 1 \bigr) = (3,\; 1,\; -2) .
$$
The vector $(-3, -1, 2)$ is $\mathbf{r}_v \wedge \mathbf{r}_u$, which has the opposite direction, because the cross product changes sign when the factors are swapped.
