What is the area of the surface generated by a full rotation about the $z$-axis of the arc of the astroid $x = \cos^3 t$, $z = \sin^3 t$, with $t \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, lying in the plane $y = 0$?

::grafico{xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-0.3 ymax=1.3 uguale=1 nomi="x,z" curve="cos(t)^3,sin(t)^3,0,1.5708;-cos(t)^3,sin(t)^3,0,1.5708" punti="1,0,pieno;0,1,pieno" titolo="Cross-section with the plane $y = 0$: the arc of the astroid from $(1, 0, 0)$ to $(0, 0, 1)$ and its mirror image across the $z$-axis"}

- $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$
- $\dfrac{6\pi}{5}$
- $\dfrac{12\pi}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The formula for the **area of a surface of revolution** applies with $g(t) = \cos^3 t \geq 0$ and $h(t) = \sin^3 t$. We have $g'(t) = -3\cos^2 t \sin t$ and $h'(t) = 3\sin^2 t \cos t$, so
$$
\sqrt{g'(t)^2 + h'(t)^2} = 3 \sin t \cos t \sqrt{\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t} = 3 \sin t \cos t ,
$$
since $\sin t$ and $\cos t$ are nonnegative on $\left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$. The square root vanishes only at the endpoints $t = 0$ and $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which do not change the value of the integral.

Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Area} = 2\pi \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos^3 t \cdot 3 \sin t \cos t \; dt = 6\pi \left[ -\frac{\cos^5 t}{5} \right]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \frac{6\pi}{5} .
$$
The integral reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int g^{\alpha}\, g' \; dt = \frac{g^{\alpha + 1}}{\alpha + 1} + c$, with $g(t) = \cos t$ and $\alpha = 4$.
