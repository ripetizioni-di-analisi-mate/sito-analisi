What is $\displaystyle\iint_\Sigma (x + 2) \; d\sigma$, where $\Sigma$ is the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$?

- $4\pi$
- $0$
- $2\pi$
- $8\pi$

## Explanation

By linearity the integral is the sum of $\displaystyle\iint_\Sigma x \; d\sigma$ and $2 \displaystyle\iint_\Sigma d\sigma$.

The sphere is symmetric with respect to the plane $x = 0$, that is, the points $(x, y, z)$ and $(-x, y, z)$ both lie on $\Sigma$, and the function $x$ takes opposite values at them. Hence $\displaystyle\iint_\Sigma x \; d\sigma = 0$.

The second term is twice the area of the sphere of radius $1$, therefore
$$
\iint_\Sigma (x + 2) \; d\sigma = 0 + 2 \cdot 4\pi = 8\pi .
$$
