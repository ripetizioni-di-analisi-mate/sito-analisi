- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \bigl( 17\sqrt{17} - 1 \bigr)$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6} \bigl( 17\sqrt{17} - 1 \bigr)$
- $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} \bigl( 17\sqrt{17} - 1 \bigr)$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6} \bigl( 65\sqrt{65} - 1 \bigr)$

## Explanation

The paraboloid is the graph of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$, and the condition $z \leq 4$ is equivalent to $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$. The base domain is therefore the disk centered at the origin with radius $2$.

Since $f_x = 2x$ and $f_y = 2y$, the formula for the **area of a Cartesian surface** gives the integrand $\sqrt{1 + 4x^2 + 4y^2}$, which in polar coordinates becomes $\sqrt{1 + 4\rho^2}$. With the Jacobian $\rho$ we obtain
$$
\operatorname{Area} = 2\pi \int_0^2 \rho \sqrt{1 + 4\rho^2} \; d\rho = 2\pi \left[ \frac{(1 + 4\rho^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{12} \right]_0^2 = \frac{\pi}{6} \bigl( 17\sqrt{17} - 1 \bigr) .
$$
The integral in $\rho$ is reducible to the immediate integral $\displaystyle\int g^{\alpha}\, g' \; d\rho = \frac{g^{\alpha + 1}}{\alpha + 1} + c$, with $g(\rho) = 1 + 4\rho^2$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
