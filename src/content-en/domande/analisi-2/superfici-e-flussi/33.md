The graph of $f(x, y) = 1 - x^2 - y^2$ is parametrized by $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl( v,\; u,\; 1 - u^2 - v^2 \bigr)$. Which orientation does this parametrization induce?

- Upward, because every Cartesian surface is oriented upward
- Upward, because the third component of $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v$ equals $1$
- Downward, because the third component of $\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v$ equals $-1$
- None, because the parametrization is not regular

## Explanation

The partial derivative vectors are $\mathbf{r}_u = (0,\; 1,\; -2u)$ and $\mathbf{r}_v = (1,\; 0,\; -2v)$, so
$$
\mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v = \bigl( -2v,\; -2u,\; -1 \bigr) .
$$
The vector never vanishes, so the parametrization is regular, and its third component is negative at every point. Therefore the induced orientation is **downward**. The upward orientation is the one induced by $\mathbf{r}(x, y) = (x, y, f(x, y))$; swapping the order of the parameters changes the sign of the cross product.
