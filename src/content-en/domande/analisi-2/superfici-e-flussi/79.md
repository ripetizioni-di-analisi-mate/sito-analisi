The field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( xy,\; yz,\; zx \bigr)$ crosses the boundary of the solid cylinder $V = \{ (x, y, z) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1,\; -1 \leq z \leq 1 \}$. What is its outward flux through $\partial V$?

- $2\pi$
- $6\pi$
- $0$
- $\pi$

## Explanation

The solid cylinder is a regular domain and the field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, so the **divergence theorem** applies. We have $\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F} = y + z + x$.

The cylinder $V$ is symmetric with respect to the three coordinate planes: for example, the points $(x, y, z)$ and $(-x, y, z)$ both belong to $V$, and at them the function $x$ takes opposite values. Therefore the triple integral of $x$ over $V$ is zero, and the same holds for $y$ and for $z$.

Hence the outward flux is
$$
\Phi = \iiint_V (x + y + z) \; dx \, dy \, dz = 0 .
$$
