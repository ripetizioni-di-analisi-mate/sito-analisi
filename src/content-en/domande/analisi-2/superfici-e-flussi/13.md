## Explanation

As the surface with boundary $\gamma$ we choose the disk $D$ of radius $3$ in the plane $z = 0$, oriented with $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{k}$. An observer standing along $\mathbf{k}$ who walks along the circle counterclockwise as seen from above has the disk on their left, so the boundary is oriented compatibly with $D$. Moreover, the field is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$, therefore **Stokes' theorem** applies.

The curl of the field is
$$
\operatorname{curl} \mathbf{F} = \left( \frac{\partial (z^2)}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial (3x)}{\partial z},\; \frac{\partial (2y)}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial (z^2)}{\partial x},\; \frac{\partial (3x)}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial (2y)}{\partial y} \right) = (0,\; 0,\; 1) .
$$
Since $\operatorname{curl} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{k} = 1$, the flux of the curl through $D$ is the area of the disk, so
$$
\oint_\gamma \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \pi \cdot 3^2 = 9\pi .
$$
