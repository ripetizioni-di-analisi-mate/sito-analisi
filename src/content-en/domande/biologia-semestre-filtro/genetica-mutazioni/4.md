Two different inactivating variants of the same gene associated with a recessive trait lie one on the maternal chromosome and one on the paternal chromosome. How is this configuration described?

- Heterozygosity for variants at two different loci.
- Compound heterozygosity with variants in trans.
- Homozygosity for a single variant.
- Two variants in cis on one copy.

## Explanation

The two copies of the same gene carry different alterations, so the individual is a compound heterozygote. Their location on different homologs defines trans phase. If both alterations lay on the same copy, the other copy could remain functional and the recessive consequence would require reassessment.
