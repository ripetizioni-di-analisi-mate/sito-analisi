Five alleles of an autosomal locus exist in a population. How many alleles of that locus does one diploid individual normally carry?

- Two allelic copies, identical or different.
- Four different alleles, two on each homolog.
- Five copies, one for each allele in the population.
- Only one copy, as in a gamete.

## Explanation

Multiple allelism describes variation in the population. A diploid individual has two homologs and normally two copies of the locus, one inherited from each parent. The individual can therefore be homozygous or heterozygous even when the number of possible population alleles exceeds two.
