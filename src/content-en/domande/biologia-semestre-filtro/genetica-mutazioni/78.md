One functional copy of a gene produces enough enzyme for the ordinary phenotype. An inactivating variant in the other copy may then be:

- Dominant-negative despite no interference by the mutant product.
- Gain of function caused by inactivation alone.
- Recessive for that phenotype.
- Haploinsufficient despite sufficient output from the intact copy.

## Explanation

If the intact copy produces enough product to exceed the required functional threshold, the heterozygote can retain the ordinary phenotype. Loss of function is then recessive at that level. The same variant may still reduce quantitatively measured activity; dominance depends on the observed trait.
