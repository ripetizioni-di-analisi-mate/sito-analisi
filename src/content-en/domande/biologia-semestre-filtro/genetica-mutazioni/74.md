A heteroplasmic mother transmits mitochondrial DNA to her children. Why can the children show different manifestations?

- They all receive exactly the same proportion of variant molecules.
- Maternal origin alone imposes equal severity in every child.
- They can receive different proportions of variant molecules and cross different functional thresholds.
- The variant-DNA fraction is fixed at one half, as at a heterozygous autosomal locus.

## Explanation

Segregation of mitochondrial populations can generate different proportions of variant DNA. Tissue distribution and energy demands also influence the effect. Maternal transmission therefore does not guarantee equal severity among children; interpretation must distinguish molecular origin from relative amount.
