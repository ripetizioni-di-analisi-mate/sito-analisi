An individual has two maternal copies and no paternal copy of a region containing imprinted genes. Total copy number is two. Why can expression still be altered?

- Expression of genes normally active only from the paternal copy may be absent.
- Imprinting marks concern only sex chromosomes.
- A copy number of two always guarantees normal expression.
- After fertilization, one maternal copy automatically acquires paternal imprinting.

## Explanation

For imprinted genes, copy origin also matters. If a gene is normally expressed only from the paternal copy, two maternally marked copies may fail to supply its normal activity. A numerical dosage of two therefore does not guarantee functional equivalence of parental origins. After fertilization, the marks are not automatically corrected to compensate for the copies’ identical origin.
