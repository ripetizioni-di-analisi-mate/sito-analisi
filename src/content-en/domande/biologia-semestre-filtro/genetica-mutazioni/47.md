Two crossovers involve the same chromatids in the interval between outer markers A and C. What can happen to the combination of the outer markers alone?

- Every double exchange is counted as two recombinant gametes in a two-locus test.
- Both exchanges become individually recognizable by counting only the outer markers.
- The outer markers must be recombinant after any number of exchanges.
- It can become parental again and escape a two-locus recombinant count.

## Explanation

Two exchanges can change and then restore the association of the outer markers. The product is then classified as parental in an analysis observing only A and C, despite recombination within the interval. This explains why simple two-locus counts underestimate long distances.
