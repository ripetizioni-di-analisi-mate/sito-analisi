DNase removes free DNA from a preparation, but DNA inside intact capsids remains available for transfer. Which explanation is consistent?

- Every DNase-resistant transfer must be conjugation
- Every bacterial DNA molecule is intrinsically DNase-resistant, even when free
- DNase is inactive throughout the preparation
- The capsid limits DNase access to the enclosed DNA

## Explanation

The enzyme can degrade DNA it can access, whereas an intact capsid can protect its contents. DNase-resistant transfer is therefore consistent with transduction when a phage vehicle is established. A DNase test alone cannot identify every mechanism: information about particles and contact between cells is also needed.
