Two amino acids share the same general backbone but have different side chains: one is charged and the other nonpolar. Which property can directly differ?

- The requirement that only the charged one can form peptide bonds
- Obligatory identity of all interactions because the general backbone is shared
- Interaction with water and with other protein groups
- The type of covalent bond joining consecutive residues in the chain

## Explanation

The side chain contributes to amino-acid charge, polarity and interactions. A charged group can interact with water or opposite charges; a nonpolar group tends to associate with other nonpolar groups in water. Both residues can form peptide bonds through their common backbone: a charged side chain is not the sole feature enabling this.
