Covalent attachment of a lipid chain increases a protein’s association with a membrane. Which modification is described?

- Acetylation
- Phosphorylation
- Glycosylation
- Lipidation

## Explanation

The lipid chain introduces a component that can interact with the membrane’s nonpolar environment. Lipidation can therefore contribute to protein localisation and interactions. Glycosylation, acetylation and phosphorylation attach different groups and have context-dependent effects. The finding identifies the modification by the attached group’s nature and its covalent linkage to the target.
