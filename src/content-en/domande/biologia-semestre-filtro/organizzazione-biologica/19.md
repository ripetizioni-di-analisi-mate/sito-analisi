Before antibiotic treatment, a bacterial population contains a small resistant fraction. After treatment, that fraction predominates. Which process is directly consistent?

- Selection of pre-existing variants
- A reduction in total count with identical relative survival in the two groups
- Exclusive elimination of resistant cells with survival of every sensitive cell
- Intentional appearance of the same useful mutation in every exposed cell

## Explanation

Resistance was present before exposure. If resistant bacteria survive and reproduce more than others under the new conditions, their frequency increases. This is natural selection acting on pre-existing variation. The frequency change does not show that the antibiotic produced a specific need-directed mutation in every cell.
