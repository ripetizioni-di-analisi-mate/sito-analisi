A mature human erythrocyte lacks a nucleus. Which observation makes this compatible with cell theory?

- It matures directly from a platelet already present in blood
- It necessarily has a nucleus that continues to replicate
- It forms by haemoglobin self-assembly without a cellular precursor
- It develops through maturation of a cellular precursor

## Explanation

Cell theory concerns the organisation of organisms and the origin of cells from pre-existing cells. During differentiation, some cells lose structures, including the nucleus. A mature erythrocyte develops from a precursor and is therefore not a case of spontaneous generation. Its specialisation does not justify extending absence of a nucleus to all human cells.
