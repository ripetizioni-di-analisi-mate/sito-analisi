A single-stranded DNA virus first forms a double-stranded DNA intermediate. Which requirement for protein production does this step meet in the typical cycle?

- Allowing ribosomes to translate DNA directly
- Replacing the need for mRNA with a second DNA strand
- Providing a template for mRNA transcription
- Producing a capsid without viral gene expression

## Explanation

The route to proteins requires mRNA. In the typical cycle of single-stranded DNA viruses, formation of a double-stranded intermediate allows transcription of the required RNAs. Ribosomes then translate those RNAs. The intermediate does not make the virus autonomous or replace the function of cellular machinery.
