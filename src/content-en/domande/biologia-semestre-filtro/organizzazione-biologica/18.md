Two branches of a phylogenetic tree are rotated around the same node without changing their connections. What necessarily changes?

- Only the graphical arrangement of the branches
- The biological age of the groups
- The number of mutations that occurred
- The groups’ common ancestor

## Explanation

Rotation preserves the connections between nodes and tips, which contain information about relatedness. It changes the order in which tips are drawn without introducing new ancestors. Mutation counts and dates cannot be inferred from rotation; they require data and, where appropriate, an explicit scale. The tree is therefore read through its topology.
