A region of an RNA pairs with a complementary segment of the same molecule to form a stem. Which property is illustrated?

- Complete absence of base complementarity in RNA
- Ability of RNA to fold intramolecularly
- Requirement for a separate second strand for every paired region
- Requirement to convert RNA into DNA before any folding

## Explanation

A single strand can contain complementary sequences that approach one another after folding. This creates paired regions within the same molecule, important for many RNA functions. A stem does not require a separate second RNA. Describing RNA as generally single-stranded must therefore be distinguished from claiming that it lacks structure.
