A mycoplasma cell lacks a wall but has a membrane, ribosomes and genetic material. Which generalisation is contradicted?

- Cells have a plasma membrane
- All bacteria have a peptidoglycan wall
- Genetic material can be transmitted to descendants
- Bacteria can have ribosomes

## Explanation

Mycoplasma provides a counterexample to the universal statement about a wall. It does not contradict the presence of a membrane, ribosomes or hereditary information. When describing a biological group, common traits should be distinguished from traits without exceptions in the model considered. Absence of a wall does not thereby turn the cell into a virion.
