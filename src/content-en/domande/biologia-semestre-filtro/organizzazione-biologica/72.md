An enzyme’s activity increases between two moderate temperatures but collapses after strong heating. Which interpretation is consistent?

- Loss of activity necessarily proves cleavage of every peptide bond
- Every temperature increase must always raise activity without limit
- Temperature affects rate and can also alter enzyme structure
- Temperature can change only equilibrium and not enzyme structure

## Explanation

At moderate temperatures, an increase can favour reaction rates. Stronger heating may, however, disrupt folding and the active site, reducing catalysis. The two effects can therefore produce a curve with a maximum rather than unlimited growth. A structural measurement would be needed to establish denaturation, but the observed behaviour is consistent with it.
