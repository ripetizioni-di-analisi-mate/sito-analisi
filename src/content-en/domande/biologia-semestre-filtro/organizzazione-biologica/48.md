A gene from the ancient endosymbiont was transferred to the nucleus during evolution. Which change can make this transfer functional for the present-day organelle?

- Translation of the nuclear gene exclusively in the matrix without exporting any product
- Retention of the protein only in the nucleus even when its function is mitochondrial
- Nuclear transcription, cytosolic translation and protein import into the mitochondrion
- Obligatory loss of expression of the transferred gene

## Explanation

To continue performing a mitochondrial function, the transferred gene’s product must be synthesised and reach the organelle. Expression of the nuclear gene and targeting signals can meet these requirements. Gene transfer does not eliminate the need for translation and transport. This functional integration explains the interdependence developed during evolution.
