A cellular synthesis uses activated precursors and several enzyme-catalysed steps. Why is describing it only as spontaneous joining of monomers with water loss incomplete?

- It makes mechanism irrelevant because all cellular condensations are spontaneous
- It omits activation steps and energy coupling
- It proves that monomer proximity alone always makes every synthesis favourable
- It implies that an enzyme freely changes equilibrium without coupling

## Explanation

Biopolymer formation may require energy invested in activated precursors and coupled reactions. A formal condensation equation alone does not describe every step of the cellular mechanism. Enzymes enable suitable pathways but do not arbitrarily change thermodynamics. The description must therefore connect product structure with the conditions enabling its synthesis.
