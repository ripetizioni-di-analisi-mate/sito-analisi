A protease cleaves a protein into shorter fragments. Which bonds are directly hydrolysed?

- Hydrogen bonds stabilising the alpha helix
- Phosphodiester bonds
- Hydrophobic interactions between side chains
- Peptide bonds

## Explanation

Amino acids in a protein chain are linked by peptide bonds. A protease catalyses their hydrolysis and thus produces shorter chains. Altering only hydrogen bonds or hydrophobic interactions may change folding without cleaving the covalent backbone. Phosphodiester bonds belong to nucleic-acid backbones, not to the linkage between protein residues.
