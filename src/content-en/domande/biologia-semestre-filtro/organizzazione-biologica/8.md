A mitochondrial protein is encoded by a nuclear gene. Where is its precursor normally synthesised before import into the mitochondrion?

- In the nucleoplasm before mRNA export
- On cytosolic ribosomes
- On mitochondrial ribosomes directly using the nuclear gene’s mRNA
- On rough-endoplasmic-reticulum ribosomes, with entry into its lumen

## Explanation

The nuclear gene is transcribed, and its mRNA available in the cytosol is translated by cytosolic ribosomes. The protein is then targeted to and imported into the organelle. This route explains why endosymbiotic origin is compatible with present-day dependence on the nucleus. Mitochondrial ribosomes synthesise the subset of proteins encoded by the mitochondrial genome.
