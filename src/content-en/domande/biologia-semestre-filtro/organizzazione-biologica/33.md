During Gram staining, two cultures receive the same reagents. One retains the crystal violet–iodine complex after decolourisation. Which structure is consistent with the typical result?

- Complete absence of a wall in the mycoplasma model
- A thick peptidoglycan layer
- A capsule that alone always defines a Gram-positive result
- Thin peptidoglycan with an outer membrane in the Gram-negative model

## Explanation

In the typical Gram-positive result, thick peptidoglycan contributes to retaining the complex during decolourisation. Gram-negative cells lose the initial dye and take up the counterstain. A capsule, if present, does not alone define a Gram-positive result. A wall-less cell lacks the thick layer referred to in this interpretation of the test.
