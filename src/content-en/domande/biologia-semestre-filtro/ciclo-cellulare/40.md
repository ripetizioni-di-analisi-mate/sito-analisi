A cell with one kinetochore attached to both poles enters anaphase. Other attachments are normal, and its checkpoint responds to unattached kinetochores. Which interpretation is most consistent?

- The described kinetochore must have lacked microtubules
- Kinetochore occupancy can allow a merotelic attachment to evade the checkpoint
- Any attachment to both poles guarantees correct biorientation
- The absence of arrest proves that the checkpoint never responds to unattached kinetochores

## Explanation

A single kinetochore with microtubules from both poles has a merotelic attachment: it is occupied, but its geometry is incorrect. This error may fail to sustain a sufficient checkpoint signal. Anaphase entry proves neither correct biorientation nor loss of every checkpoint function; in this case, the system still responds to unattached kinetochores.
