Why is inactivation of both copies not an absolute rule for every tumour suppressor?

- One intact copy always guarantees sufficient activity regardless of the other variant
- Every inactivating variant is compensated by automatic doubling of expression
- Dominant-negative effects and haploinsufficiency exist
- Loss of both copies is required only for activating proto-oncogenes

## Explanation

Loss of both copies matters for many genes, but some variants interfere with normal protein or reduce function below a threshold despite one intact copy. The gene and mechanism must be considered rather than applying one universal rule.
