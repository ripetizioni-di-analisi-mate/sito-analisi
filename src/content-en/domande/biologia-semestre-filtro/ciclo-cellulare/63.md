Why does oogenesis not normally produce four equivalent large functional gametes from one primary oocyte?

- The egg and the first polar body normally receive equal amounts of cytoplasm
- The entire chromosome complement stays in the oocyte, and polar bodies receive only cytoplasm
- The first division is asymmetric, but the second redistributes all cytoplasm equally among four cells
- Cytoplasm is partitioned asymmetrically

## Explanation

The divisions concentrate most cytoplasm in the cell destined to contribute to development; other products are polar bodies. The number of possible nuclei therefore differs from the number of functional gametes with equivalent cellular resources.
