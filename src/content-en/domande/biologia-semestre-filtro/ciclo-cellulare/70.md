A stem cell remains quiescent but retains self-renewal and differentiation capacity when reactivated. Does quiescence contradict stemness?

- No, because every quiescent cell is a stem cell
- Yes; all stem cells must divide continuously
- Yes; every reversible exit from the cycle removes self-renewal capacity
- No; potential and proliferation rate are distinct

## Explanation

The criterion concerns retained functional capacities, not a required division frequency. A stem cell can be quiescent, but the converse does not hold: not every quiescent cell is a stem cell. The defining capacities must be demonstrated.
