A nuclear protein is imported already folded. Which comparison with mitochondrial import of many precursors is correct?

- Nuclear-pore tolerance of folded cargo shows that TOM and TIM also normally import any folded protein.
- A mitochondrial presequence makes folding state and chaperones irrelevant.
- Nuclear transport requires unobserved complete unfolding, identical to that of mitochondrial precursors.
- The nuclear pore can transport folded cargo; many mitochondrial translocases require chains suitable for passage in an extended form.

## Explanation

Passage through the nuclear pore differs from translocation through a channel in a lipid membrane. Many mitochondrial proteins are maintained in an import-competent state by chaperones; nuclear transport can instead accommodate folded proteins and complexes. Destination determines different requirements.
