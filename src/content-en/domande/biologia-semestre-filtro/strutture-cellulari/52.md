A hydrophobic sequence stops chain transfer through the translocon and remains in the bilayer. Which outcome is plausible?

- Complete translocation of the hydrophobic segment into the lumen without membrane insertion.
- Obligatory removal of the hydrophobic segment, releasing an entirely soluble protein.
- Permanent translation arrest at the hydrophobic segment rather than arrest of transfer across the membrane.
- Formation of a transmembrane segment.

## Explanation

A stop-transfer sequence can leave the translocon laterally and enter the membrane. Positions of start and stop signals contribute to final topology. Cargo therefore remains a membrane protein rather than necessarily becoming a soluble secreted protein.
