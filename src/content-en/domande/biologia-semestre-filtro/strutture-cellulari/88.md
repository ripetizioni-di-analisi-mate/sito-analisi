A neutrophil on an adhesive two-dimensional surface detects a chemical gradient and migrates towards its source. Which coordination is required for effective translocation?

- Front protrusion, adhesion and rear retraction.
- Front adhesion and rear retraction without advancement of the leading edge.
- Front protrusion with stable rear adhesions that are not released.
- Uniformly distributed contraction and protrusions without a front-to-rear axis.

## Explanation

A protrusion without effective adhesion or rear release can change shape without moving the whole cell. In the stated adhesive model, chemotaxis requires polarity and coordination between actin, forces and substrate contacts. The gradient orients the process, while cytoskeletal machinery carries out movement.
