A cell is immersed in an initially iso-osmotic solution containing only a rapidly penetrating solute. Why can it swell?

- Solute entry necessarily lowers internal osmolarity before water enters.
- Initially equal osmolarities guarantee that no subsequent fluxes occur.
- The solute enters and water follows, while intracellular nonpenetrating solutes remain.
- The permeant solute indefinitely maintains the same tonic effect as an impermeant solute.

## Explanation

Osmolarity counts particles initially present; tonicity depends on those remaining effectively separated by the membrane. The penetrating solute also distributes inside, where other nonpenetrating solutes remain. Subsequent water entry shows why iso-osmolarity and isotonicity are not equivalent in this case.
