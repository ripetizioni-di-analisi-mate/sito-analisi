What follows from most mitochondrial proteins being encoded in the nucleus?

- Nuclear-encoded proteins enter the matrix mainly in vesicles originating from the reticulum.
- The mitochondrion depends on import of many cytosol-produced proteins.
- All proteins encoded by nuclear genes are translated by mitochondrial ribosomes after mRNA import.
- Nuclear-encoded proteins remain in the cytosol and act at a distance without entering the organelle.

## Explanation

The mitochondrial genome encodes only some required components. Many precursors are therefore translated on cytosolic ribosomes and targeted by signals and translocases. Local DNA and ribosomes support some synthesis without making the organelle independent of the nucleus.
