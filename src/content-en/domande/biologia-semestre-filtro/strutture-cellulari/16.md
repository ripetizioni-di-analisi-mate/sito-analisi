An impermeant extracellular probe binds carbohydrates on an intact plasma membrane. Which structure is it recognizing?

- The hydrophobic glycolipid tails, directly exposed to external fluid.
- The glycocalyx.
- Glycosylated cytosolic domains of transmembrane proteins.
- The carbohydrate heads present in all phospholipids of the outer leaflet.

## Explanation

Carbohydrate portions of glycoproteins and glycolipids form the glycocalyx on the extracellular side. An impermeant probe can recognize them without entering the cell. The cytosolic leaflet and lamina instead require internal access, while lipid tails are hidden in the bilayer’s hydrophobic region.
