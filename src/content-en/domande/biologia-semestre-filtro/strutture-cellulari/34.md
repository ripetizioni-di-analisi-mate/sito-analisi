Conversion of pyruvate to lactate allows glycolysis to continue when mitochondrial NADH reoxidation is insufficient. What is its direct effect?

- It directly produces two extra ATP for each lactate formed.
- It regenerates NAD⁺ by transferring electrons directly to oxygen in the lactate dehydrogenase reaction.
- It regenerates NAD⁺ by oxidizing NADH.
- It consumes NAD⁺ to produce NADH.

## Explanation

Reduction of pyruvate to lactate consumes NADH and restores NAD⁺, needed by the glycolytic glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase reaction. This conversion adds no ATP to the glycolytic gain. Its role is to maintain the oxidized cofactor that allows the pathway to continue.
