SREBP is initially membrane-associated. Which event allows its active domain to act in the nucleus?

- The intact protein remains anchored to the reticulum while its active domain directly binds nuclear DNA.
- Addition of an export signal retains the active domain on Golgi membranes.
- Cleavage causes complete degradation of the active domain, which is required for its transcriptional action.
- Regulated proteolytic processing releases the fragment that is imported.

## Explanation

The membrane-bound precursor must be processed to release the domain capable of reaching the nucleus. After release, the fragment uses the appropriate import system. This example connects proteolytic control with localization without requiring an entire membrane to pass through the pore.
