In the classical transferrin cycle, after iron release in the endosome, what happens to the protein and receptor?

- Transferrin dissociates in the endosome and the receptor is degraded each cycle.
- Both reach lysosomes, where protein and receptor are degraded.
- Transferrin is degraded in the lysosome while the receptor recycles.
- They recycle to the surface, where iron-free transferrin can dissociate.

## Explanation

Endosomal pH favours iron release, while apotransferrin can remain associated with its receptor during recycling. The different surface pH favours dissociation. This route delivers iron without degrading receptor and carrier protein during every cycle.
