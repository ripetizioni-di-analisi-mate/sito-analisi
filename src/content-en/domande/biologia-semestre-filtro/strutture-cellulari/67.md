A trafficking protein binds only membranes enriched in a particular phosphoinositide. Which principle does this illustrate?

- The protein recognizes only luminal lipid heads even when its binding domain is cytosolic.
- Binding depends only on total phosphatidylinositol amount, regardless of phosphate positions.
- The specific phosphoinositide alone supplies all the energy needed for fusion.
- Lipid composition contributes to compartment identity and protein recruitment.

## Explanation

Phosphoinositides can be recognized by specific protein domains. Their distribution therefore helps recruit factors to suitable locations and coordinate trafficking. Membrane identity depends on multiple components, including lipids, Rab proteins and other residents.
