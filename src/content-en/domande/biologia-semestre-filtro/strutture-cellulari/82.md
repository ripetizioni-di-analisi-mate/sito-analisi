Why does axonemal dynein activity bend a cilium instead of simply separating doublets without limit?

- Dynein only assembles new tubulin on the convex side of the cilium.
- Doublet sliding remains completely unconstrained, which alone imposes curvature.
- Periodic shortening of microtubules on one side replaces doublet sliding.
- Structural linkages constrain relative sliding and convert it into bending.

## Explanation

Dyneins generate force between doublets, tending to slide them. Axonemal constraints limit free sliding and coordinate deformation of the whole structure. Bending therefore results from interaction between motors and linkages, not direct microtubule shortening during every beat.
