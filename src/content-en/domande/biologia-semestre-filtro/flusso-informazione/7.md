After transcription stops completely, an mRNA has a constant half-life of 15 minutes. The initial amount is 80 units. How many remain after 45 minutes?

- 40 units
- 0 units
- 10 units
- 20 units

## Explanation

In 45 minutes, $\dfrac{45}{15}=3$ half-lives elapse. Each interval halves the amount present at its start: 80 → 40 → 20 → 10. Equivalently, $80\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^3=10$. Half-life describes fractional loss, so half of the initial amount is not subtracted each time. The result requires the absence of new synthesis specified in the question.
