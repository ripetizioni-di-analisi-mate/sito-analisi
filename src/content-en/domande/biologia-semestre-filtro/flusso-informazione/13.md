A fully coding 24-nucleotide exon is skipped in an mRNA. The junctions preserve the original reading frame and no new stops appear. What is the effect on the translated chain?

- Eight amino acids are missing but every downstream codon is frameshifted.
- Eight amino acids are missing and the downstream sequence remains in the same frame.
- Eight amino acids are added.
- Twenty-four amino acids are missing instead of eight.

## Explanation

Twenty-four nucleotides correspond to $\dfrac{24}{3}=8$ codons. The question states that the junctions preserve the frame and create no stops, so downstream codons continue to be read in the same grouping. The protein loses eight amino acids. This may alter its function, but the information alone does not quantify the biological effect.
