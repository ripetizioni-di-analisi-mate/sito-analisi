A LacI mutant can no longer bind any operator, while CAP-cAMP is active and promoters are normal. Without lactose, what is predicted in the classical model?

- LacI repression is lost even without inducer.
- The mutant maintains repression because inducer is absent, even without DNA binding.
- Repression increases because free LacI directly blocks RNA polymerase without binding DNA.
- CAP-cAMP replaces LacI as the operator repressor.

## Explanation

To repress, LacI must occupy the operator. The described mutant cannot perform this step, so inducer is no longer needed to remove that negative control. CAP-cAMP activation is retained, allowing high transcription. The defect concerns regulatory binding rather than the polymerase catalytic machinery.
