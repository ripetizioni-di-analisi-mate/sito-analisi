cAMP is added to bacteria with an intact lac operon but no lactose or other inducer. Why does this intervention alone not guarantee strong transcription?

- The LacI repressor can remain bound to the operator.
- cAMP is the direct LacI inducer and should be sufficient to remove it.
- cAMP presence necessarily prevents RNA polymerase from binding the promoter.
- CAP-cAMP always represses the promoter when glucose is absent.

## Explanation

The cAMP signal favours positive activation through CAP but does not replace inducer binding to LacI. Without inducer, the repressor can continue hindering transcription by occupying the operator. Expression therefore requires separate consideration of negative and positive control.
