A bacterial RNA polymerase retains catalytic elongation activity but loses the sigma factor suited to a group of promoters. Which step is most directly impaired?

- RNA hairpin formation during intrinsic termination.
- Efficient recognition of those promoters during initiation.
- ATP-dependent movement of Rho along the transcript.
- Nucleotide incorporation during established elongation.

## Explanation

Sigma contributes to promoter sequence recognition and directs polymerase to suitable initiation sites. The question preserves catalytic capacity, so the problem concerns recruitment and initiation at promoters dependent on that sigma factor. An initiation defect can reduce RNA production even when the enzyme can still elongate a chain.
