Which feature distinguishes the typical 5′ cap of many eukaryotic mRNAs from the ordinary nucleotide succession in the chain?

- A modified guanosine attached through the ordinary internal 3′–5′ linkage.
- A methyl group added only to the poly-A tail, without a terminal guanosine.
- A modified guanosine connected through a 5′–5′ linkage.
- A series of adenines added to the 3′ end.

## Explanation

The cap contains a modified guanosine joined by a 5′–5′ linkage to the RNA end. Proteins recognising this structure participate in protection, processing and translation initiation. Ordinary internal chain linkages instead connect the 3′ and 5′ positions of successive nucleotides. The structural difference enables specific functions at the end.
