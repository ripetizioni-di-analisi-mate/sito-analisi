Two opposite forks start from an origin. Can the same parental strand serve as the leading-strand template at one fork and the lagging-strand template at the other?

- No, because the two forks always copy the same region.
- Yes, but only if lagging-strand synthesis becomes 3′ → 5′.
- Yes, because fork movement changes direction relative to the template.
- No, because a parental strand always has the same role throughout a chromosome.

## Explanation

Leading and lagging are defined relative to an individual fork's direction. In a bidirectional bubble, forks move in opposite directions: the template allowing continuous synthesis to the right requires fragmented synthesis to the left. All new chains are still synthesised 5′ → 3′. The role is therefore not a permanent property of the parental strand.
