A splice-site variant causes intron retention in mRNA. The retained intron contains a stop in the reading frame. In a system where that mRNA is translated, which outcome is predicted?

- The ribosome automatically removes the intron before reading its stop.
- The chain can terminate within the retained intron.
- The stop is always recoded as an amino acid because it was in an intron.
- The intron sequence is ignored because ribosomes read only genomic exons.

## Explanation

If the intron remains in the messenger and is traversed by the ribosome, its bases enter the reading frame. The stated stop recruits release factors and can terminate the chain early. The question specifies that the mRNA is translated, avoiding assumptions about which RNA quality-control process dominates in a real system. The ribosome does not replace the spliceosome.
