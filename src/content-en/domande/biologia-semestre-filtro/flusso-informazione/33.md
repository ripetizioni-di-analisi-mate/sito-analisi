A bacterial mRNA contains three coding regions with separate start and stop signals. What can it produce?

- Only one protein, because bacterial RNA can contain only one effective start.
- Always one polypeptide chain made by ignoring the internal stops.
- Three mature mRNAs produced by obligatory spliceosomal division of the transcript.
- Three distinct polypeptide chains from one transcript.

## Explanation

A polycistronic messenger contains several coding units, each potentially initiated and terminated separately. Ribosomes can therefore synthesise distinct proteins from the same RNA. Transcript number does not necessarily equal the number of different proteins produced, and stop codons are not incorporated into the chain.
