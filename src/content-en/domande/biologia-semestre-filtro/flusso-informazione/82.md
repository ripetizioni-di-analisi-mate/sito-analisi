An ATP-dependent chaperone reduces aggregation of a newly synthesised protein. Which description is appropriate?

- It supplies a universal template imposing the same structure on every protein.
- It assists folding by limiting inappropriate interactions.
- Permanently exposes the protein’s hydrophobic surfaces to the solvent.
- It always degrades the substrate into peptides as its only way to reduce aggregates.

## Explanation

Chaperones can recognise exposed surfaces and reduce contacts that would promote aggregation, allowing further folding attempts. ATP supports binding and release cycles in many systems. Assistance does not mean changing the primary sequence and does not guarantee recovery of every severely altered protein.
