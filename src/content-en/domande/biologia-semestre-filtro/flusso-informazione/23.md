At unchanged fork speed, why can multiple active origins reduce the time required to copy a long eukaryotic chromosome?

- Origins allow the lagging strand to grow 3′ → 5′ without primers.
- Different regions are copied simultaneously by multiple forks.
- Segments between origins no longer require replication.
- Every individual polymerase must double its speed at each origin.

## Explanation

Activating more origins divides the chromosome into regions traversed by different forks. Work can proceed in parallel, reducing the maximum distance a single fork must cover before meeting another. The incorporation speed of an individual polymerase need not increase. The benefit also depends on actual activation times and origin distribution.
