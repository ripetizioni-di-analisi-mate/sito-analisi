A bacterium has an inactive lacI gene but a normal operator. A second copy introduces functional LacI expressed normally. What can happen without inducer?

- Repression is restored only if mutated lacI is repaired first.
- The second copy necessarily induces the first even without inducer.
- LacI can act only on the operator of the copy carrying functional lacI.
- Repressor produced from the second copy can bind the first copy’s operator.

## Explanation

LacI is a diffusible trans-acting factor. Once synthesised, it can recognise compatible operators on DNA copies other than the one that enabled its production. Regulatory function can therefore be restored without repairing the mutated lacI gene. This behaviour distinguishes a regulatory protein from a cis-acting DNA element.
