Two non-overlapping genes on the same chromosome are transcribed in opposite directions. What does this imply about their template strands?

- They can use opposite strands of the same double helix.
- They must use the same template strand because they belong to the same chromosome.
- One must synthesise RNA in the 3′ → 5′ direction.
- Both must be controlled by one promoter in the same direction.

## Explanation

For each gene, promoter orientation defines transcription direction and therefore which strand is read 3′ → 5′. Oppositely oriented genes can use the two complementary strands as their respective templates. In both cases, the new RNA chain grows 5′ → 3′ because the template used changes, not the chemistry of synthesis.
