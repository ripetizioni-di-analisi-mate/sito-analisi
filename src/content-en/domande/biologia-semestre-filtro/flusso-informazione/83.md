A polyubiquitinated protein is recognised by the proteasome. What is a typical fate of the substrate and ubiquitin signal?

- All ubiquitin chains necessarily remain attached to the substrate’s final peptides.
- Ubiquitin is removed and the substrate is always released intact and active.
- The substrate is degraded into peptides and ubiquitin can be removed and recycled.
- Substrate and ubiquitin are necessarily degraded together without signal recovery.

## Explanation

Proteasomal processing includes recognition, deubiquitination, unfolding and substrate degradation. Ubiquitin recovery allows the signal to be reused on other proteins. The proteasome produces peptide fragments from the target protein. Deubiquitination and degradation are distinct events: removing the signal does not imply that the substrate is always released intact.
