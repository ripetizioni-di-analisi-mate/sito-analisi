An E3 ligase loses its recognition site for a protein it normally tags for proteasomal degradation, while E1, E2 and the proteasome remain active. What is the most directly expected outcome for that substrate, with other pathways unchanged?

- Its degradation necessarily remains unchanged because E3 only recycles ubiquitin.
- Its ubiquitination necessarily increases because E1 compensates for recognition.
- Degradation necessarily stops for all substrates, including those recognised by other E3 ligases.
- Its ubiquitination may decrease and its stability may increase.

## Explanation

E3 helps select which substrate receives the ubiquitin signal. If recognition of a protein is lost, that pathway may tag it less effectively even with a functioning proteasome. Reduced degradation can increase its stability. The effect is stated for that substrate with other pathways unchanged: other E3s or removal mechanisms could compensate in a real system.
