During splicing of a spliceosomal intron, a looped intermediate is observed. What does it correspond to?

- The double helix formed by the two exons without involving the intron.
- A circle consisting exclusively of the joined exons.
- The lariat formed through the intron branch point.
- The 5′ cap detached from pre-mRNA during capping.

## Explanation

In the first splicing reaction, the intron branch point participates in forming a linkage that produces the lariat. The second reaction joins the exons and releases the intron in that initial form. These are RNA-backbone transformations; they involve neither translation nor peptide bond formation.
