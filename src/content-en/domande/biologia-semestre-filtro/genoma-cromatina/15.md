Which comparison correctly distinguishes sister chromatids from homologous chromosomes?

- Sisters are always one maternal and one paternal copy.
- Sisters arise through replication; homologues carry corresponding maternally and paternally derived loci.
- Homologues are the two strands of one double helix.
- Sisters occupy different loci; homologues are copies of one chromosome produced in S phase.

## Explanation

Replication of a chromosome produces two sisters. A homologous pair already exists before S phase and derives from the two parental sets; its alleles may differ. Corresponding loci and origin from the same replication event are therefore different relationships.
