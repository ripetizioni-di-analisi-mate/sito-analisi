A remodeler moves nucleosomes with ATP but not with a non-hydrolysable analogue. Which interpretation is supported?

- The data support a role for ATP hydrolysis in repositioning.
- Nucleotide binding alone is demonstrated to be sufficient without hydrolysis.
- The analogue demonstrates that remodelling occurs independently of the nucleotide.
- ATP acts only as a precursor incorporated into nucleosomal DNA.

## Explanation

The result is consistent with a role for ATP hydrolysis in the changes to DNA–histone interactions that allow repositioning. To attribute the difference specifically to hydrolysis, it is useful to verify that the analogue reaches and binds the remodeler properly. Lack of activity with an analogue alone does not exclude different binding; it supports the energetic hypothesis without establishing it in isolation.
