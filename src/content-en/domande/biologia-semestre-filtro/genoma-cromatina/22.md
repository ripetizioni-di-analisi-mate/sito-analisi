A neocentromere functions in a region lacking the usual alpha-satellite block. Which conclusion is supported?

- Alpha-satellite sequences are never associated with centromeres.
- Centromere identity also depends on chromatin organisation.
- Alpha-satellite alone is always sufficient to make any site functional.
- Any region containing canonical H3 is already a functional centromere.

## Explanation

A functional centromere lacking the usual alpha-satellite block shows that this block is not necessary in every functional centromere. Appropriate chromatin, including CENP-A, contributes to centromeric identity. This does not deny that usual human centromeres are often rich in alpha-satellite; canonical H3 in a region alone does not demonstrate centromeric function.
