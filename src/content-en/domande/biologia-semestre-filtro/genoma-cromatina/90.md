Which set of findings best distinguishes a new genetic insertion from altered chromatin without a sequence change?

- For insertion, new DNA bases and junctions; for the chromatin state, identical bases with different histone positions or modifications.
- A methylation change always demonstrates that new bases have been inserted.
- In both cases, observing less RNA alone identifies the mechanism.
- An accessibility change alone identifies chromatin, because an insertion cannot influence it.

## Explanation

The discriminating criterion is what changes. An insertion alters sequence and creates new junctions; altered histone position or modification can occur with an identical sequence. Both can influence expression, so measuring RNA alone does not separate the mechanisms.
