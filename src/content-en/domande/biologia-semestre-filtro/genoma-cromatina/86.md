Extracted chromatin is compared with chromatin in intact cells. Why may the observed structures differ?

- DNA length alone determines a single conformation independently of conditions.
- Every regular fibre obtained in vitro must also be the predominant form in vivo.
- Ions alter only image contrast without influencing chromatin interactions.
- Preparation and ionic conditions can alter the observed organisation.

## Explanation

Chromatin organisation depends on interactions influenced by the environment. Extraction, salts and sample treatment may alter structures or make some more visible. An in-vitro structure must therefore be related to its experimental conditions before being treated as a universal description of in-vivo chromatin.
