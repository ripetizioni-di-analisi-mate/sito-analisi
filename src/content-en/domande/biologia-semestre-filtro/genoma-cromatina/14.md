Two globin genes occupy distinct loci and derive from an ancient duplication. What relationship describes them?

- They are orthologous genes defined by a species split rather than duplication.
- They are paralogous members of a gene family.
- They are always identical copies without distinct regulation.
- They are necessarily two alleles of the same locus.

## Explanation

Duplication produces separate loci that may diverge during evolution. Genes related through duplication are paralogues and may belong to one family, such as the globins. Alleles are variants of the same locus; that definition does not apply to the two distinct loci in the question.
