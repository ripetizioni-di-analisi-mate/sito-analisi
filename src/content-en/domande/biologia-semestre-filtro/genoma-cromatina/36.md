Two loci are far apart along the sequence but close together in nuclear space. Which feature can explain the observation?

- Deletion of the intervening DNA is the only way to bring the loci together.
- Necessary adjacency of the two loci in the linear sequence.
- Formation of chromatin loops and three-dimensional contacts.
- An increased distance along DNA must always increase spatial distance.

## Explanation

Linear distance counts the bases between two loci. Folding can bring regions far apart along that coordinate close together in space. A three-dimensional contact therefore does not show that the loci are adjacent in sequence or have become the same locus.
