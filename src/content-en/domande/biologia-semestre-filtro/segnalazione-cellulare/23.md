Removing extracellular calcium destabilises E-cadherin-mediated adhesion. Which interpretation is correct?

- Calcium supports the adhesive conformation of cadherin.
- Calcium serves only as a cytosolic second messenger and does not stabilise the external region.
- Calcium removal demonstrates obligatory loss of every CAM, including calcium-independent ones.
- Adhesion necessarily requires calcium entry through an open channel.

## Explanation

Extracellular calcium stabilises cadherin regions that enable adhesive interactions. The observed loss of binding is consistent with this dependence. It does not follow that all CAMs are calcium-dependent or that the phenomenon requires increased cytosolic calcium through channels. The stated role is extracellular and structural; it is not equivalent to signalling from increased cytosolic calcium.
