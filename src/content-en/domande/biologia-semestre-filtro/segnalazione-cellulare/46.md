cAMP increases in a cell with functional canonical PKA. Which component does it bind to promote catalytic activity?

- PKA regulatory subunits.
- The ATP-binding site of catalytic subunits, directly replacing ATP.
- The nucleotide-binding site of Gαs, replacing the GTP that activates the G protein.
- The PKA protein substrate, as the phosphate group to be transferred in the reaction.

## Explanation

In canonical PKA, regulatory subunits control catalytic subunits. cAMP binding changes this regulation and increases kinase activity. cAMP is an allosteric regulator; it is neither the nucleotide in the Gαs switch nor the kinase's phosphate donor, which is ATP.
