In a simple binding model, nearly all receptors are already occupied. Doubling ligand causes only a small change in occupancy. Which property explains this?

- An affinity increase necessarily caused by every ligand increase.
- Saturability of the receptor-binding sites.
- Reduced total receptor concentration as a requirement for approaching full occupancy.
- Ligand degradation as the sole explanation of every occupancy plateau.

## Explanation

The number of sites is finite. When occupancy is already near its maximum, few sites remain free, so additional ligand can occupy only a small fraction. This concerns occupancy; response magnitude also depends on downstream steps. In the simple model, the curve approaches full occupancy even with constant affinity, receptor number and applied ligand concentration.
