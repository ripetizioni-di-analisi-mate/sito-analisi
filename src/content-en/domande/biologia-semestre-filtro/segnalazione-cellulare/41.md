A Gαs variant binds GTP but hydrolyses it very slowly. If it can still activate its effector, what is expected after a ligand pulse?

- Faster termination because less hydrolysis means less energy available to maintain signalling.
- More prolonged signalling through the effector.
- Unchanged signalling because duration would depend only on receptor occupancy.
- Immediate return to GDP when ligand dissociates, independently of hydrolysis rate.

## Explanation

GTP hydrolysis helps terminate Gα activity. Slowing it prolongs the state capable of stimulating the effector, provided that ability is preserved. The question therefore separates a termination defect from a possible defect in effector activation. Bound GTP represents a regulatory state; its hydrolysis switches the protein off rather than supplying energy proportional to response strength.
