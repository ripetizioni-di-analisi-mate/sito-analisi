A transient rise in cytosolic calcium activates an enzyme through calmodulin. Which event directly connects calcium to the enzyme?

- Calmodulin phosphorylates the target using ATP directly, without a kinase.
- Calcium binds calmodulin and prevents its interaction with every target.
- Calcium binding changes calmodulin conformation and its interaction with the target.
- Calmodulin transfers bound ions to the target as substrates consumed during activation.

## Explanation

Calmodulin is a calcium-binding protein. Binding changes its properties and enables regulation of target proteins. Information about calcium concentration is thus converted into altered enzyme activity without requiring immediate synthesis of a new protein. Calmodulin is a regulatory sensor; its conformational change enables interactions with appropriate targets. It is not itself a kinase and does not consume calcium as a substrate.
