What function do certain phosphorylated tyrosines of an activated RTK serve?

- Recruitment sites for suitable signalling proteins.
- Extracellular sites for direct growth-factor binding.
- Phosphate groups transferred from the receptor to substrates instead of those from ATP.
- Sites directly recognising GTP and performing nucleotide exchange on Ras.

## Explanation

Phosphorylation changes the interaction properties of receptor tails. Proteins with domains that recognise those sites can be recruited near the membrane and other pathway components. The receptor thus organises a signalling complex as well as acting as a kinase. Phosphotyrosines remain in the receptor's intracellular portion and can recruit proteins. They replace neither the extracellular site, ATP used in catalysis nor a GEF such as SOS.
