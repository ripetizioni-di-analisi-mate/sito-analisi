Two tissues contain the same amount of collagen, but their fibres have different orientations. Which conclusion is justified?

- They may resist tension differently along a given direction.
- They must have identical tensile responses because only collagen mass matters.
- Fibre orientation can affect only compression, never tension.
- Their collagen amounts must differ despite the stated equality.

## Explanation

Resistance also depends on fibre orientation relative to the applied force. Equal amounts of collagen do not describe network organisation. The finding therefore allows mechanical differences without requiring differences in total collagen, cell number or hormonal response. An amount measurement does not determine anisotropy: force direction relative to the fibres is additional information.
