During cell migration, selective inhibition of metalloproteinases reduces cleavage of a matrix that obstructs passage. Which explanation directly links the intervention to the result?

- The inhibitor promotes new crosslinks between matrix fibres rather than reducing their degradation.
- Proteolytic matrix remodelling decreases.
- The inhibitor directly reduces integrin affinity rather than acting on matrix-cleaving enzymes.
- The inhibitor reduces new matrix deposition while cleavage of existing matrix continues at the same rate.

## Explanation

Metalloproteinases can cleave matrix components. If migration requires reorganisation of this barrier, lowering their activity can hinder passage. The reasoning uses the specified proteolytic role and does not assign a universal effect on every signalling pathway to the intervention. Metalloproteinases cleave extracellular components; deposition, fibre crosslinking and integrin affinity are distinct processes.
