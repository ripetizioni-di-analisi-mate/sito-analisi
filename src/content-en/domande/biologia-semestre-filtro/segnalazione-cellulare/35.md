A kinase phosphorylates and inhibits enzyme E. A phosphatase removes that exact phosphate, with no other effects in the model. What is expected for E?

- E activity increases.
- E activity remains unchanged because only a kinase can alter its regulatory state.
- E activity decreases because removal of any phosphate would always be inhibitory.
- E remains inactive after phosphate removal because phosphorylation produces irreversible inhibition.

## Explanation

The specified phosphorylation is inhibitory. Removing it relieves that inhibition and favours E activity. The prediction follows from the stated sign of the connection, not from a rule that every phosphatase activates or inhibits every protein. The effect depends on the site: here, the phosphate is inhibitory and is removed. New E molecules are not needed to reverse this modification.
