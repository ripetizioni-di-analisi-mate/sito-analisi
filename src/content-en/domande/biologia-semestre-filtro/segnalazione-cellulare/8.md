In a muscle fibre at a negative resting potential, acetylcholine opens nicotinic receptors and produces a net inward current of positive charge. Which change follows directly?

- Hyperpolarisation, because receptor opening increases internal negativity.
- Unchanged potential, because sodium influx and potassium efflux balance under all conditions.
- A full action potential as an inevitable consequence, even if depolarisation stays below threshold.
- Depolarisation.

## Explanation

A net inward current of positive charge makes the internal potential less negative and therefore depolarises the membrane. This prediction depends on the gradients and initial potential, specified here. The nicotinic receptor is a ligand-gated channel, not a phospholipase. Inward current makes the potential less negative. Local depolarisation alone does not guarantee reaching the threshold for an action potential.
