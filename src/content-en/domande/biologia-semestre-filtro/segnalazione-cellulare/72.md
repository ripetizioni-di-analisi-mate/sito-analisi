Which definition distinguishes a proto-oncogene from its altered oncogenic form?

- A gene absent from every normal cell and present only after transformation.
- A gene defined exclusively by loss of a function restraining proliferation.
- A proto-oncogene is a normal gene that can acquire activity favouring inappropriate growth or survival.
- A gene that is necessarily already oncogenic whenever physiologically expressed.

## Explanation

The term proto-oncogene refers to the gene in its normal function. Certain alterations can produce a form with oncogenic activity. Presence of the normal gene is therefore compatible with healthy cells; the alteration and its effect in context are what matter. Loss of a brake is the typical model for many tumour suppressors; a proto-oncogene can instead normally contribute to growth and survival.
