After phospholipase C cleaves PIP2, why do DAG and IP3 have different cellular distributions?

- DAG is soluble while IP3 retains the hydrophobic chains in the membrane.
- Both necessarily remain in the bilayer because they derive from a phospholipid.
- Both necessarily become soluble after phospholipid cleavage.
- DAG retains the lipid portion, whereas IP3 is soluble.

## Explanation

Cleavage separates a component that remains associated with the bilayer from a soluble polar head group. DAG therefore stays in the membrane, whereas IP3 diffuses in the cytosol. This allows two products of the same reaction to reach targets in different compartments. A common origin does not impose identical solubility: cleavage separates portions with different chemical properties.
