Why can NO act as a local signal between an endothelial cell and a nearby smooth-muscle cell?

- It necessarily remains tethered to the endothelial membrane.
- It crosses membranes only through gap junctions requiring direct contact.
- It is so stable that long-distance endocrine transport is obligatory.
- It can cross membranes and is rapidly transformed in the biological environment.

## Explanation

Diffusibility allows NO to reach a nearby cell; its rapid transformation helps limit its action in space and time. The intracellular target can be soluble guanylyl cyclase. Transfer does not require an adhesive contact between the two membranes. Membrane diffusion and limited lifetime permit local action without a tethered ligand or an intercellular channel.
