An intracellular protein increases an integrin's affinity for its ligand without changing the external ligand. Which direction of regulation does this describe?

- Inside-out regulation.
- Regulation due exclusively to increased external ligand concentration.
- Outside-in, as if the initial intervention acted on the extracellular ligand.
- Obligatory increased integrin number as a synonym for higher affinity.

## Explanation

The intervention begins inside the cell and changes the ability of the extracellular region to bind ligand. This defines inside-out regulation. In the opposite direction, adhesion and external binding modify intracellular events; both directions can coexist in the same system. Affinity, receptor number and ligand concentration are distinct variables; here the first changes through an intracellular intervention.
