Which description of the insulin receptor is correct?

- It is an RTK present as a complex of associated subunits before hormone binding.
- It is necessarily a monomeric RTK until hormone binding, without preassociated subunits.
- It is a GPCR that must always use a heterotrimeric G protein as its sole initial step.
- It is a receptor-channel directly transporting glucose.

## Explanation

The insulin receptor is a preassembled complex with tyrosine-kinase activity. Hormone binding changes its activation. Glucose transport occurs through transporters, including GLUT4 in the specified tissues, rather than through a pore formed by the insulin receptor. Preassembly distinguishes this receptor from the simplification that all RTKs associate only after ligand binding.
