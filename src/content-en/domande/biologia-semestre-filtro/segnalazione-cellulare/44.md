Ras and Gα both belong to proteins that bind guanine nucleotides. Which difference is correct in this context?

- Ras always forms an alpha-beta-gamma trimer; Gα is always isolated as a small GTPase.
- Only Gα can hydrolyse GTP, whereas Ras remains permanently nucleotide-bound.
- Ras is monomeric; Gα is a subunit of a heterotrimeric G protein.
- Ras is activated by directly converting bound GDP to GTP through phosphorylation.

## Explanation

The GDP–GTP cycle is shared, but complex organisation differs. Ras is a small monomeric G protein; Gα belongs to the alpha-beta-gamma system. Sharing the bound nucleotide does not make their structure, upstream receptors or downstream effectors identical. Both use nucleotide exchange and hydrolysis; the requested difference concerns protein organisation, not absence of the cycle in Ras.
