Why can PIP3 promote Akt recruitment near the membrane?

- It diffuses freely through cytosol like IP3 and forms a soluble complex there with Akt.
- It directly supplies the phosphates transferred to Akt, replacing ATP in kinase reactions.
- It is recognised by the Akt catalytic site as a substrate to degrade before recruitment.
- It is recognised by suitable binding domains and remains in the bilayer.

## Explanation

PIP3 is a membrane lipid providing interaction sites for proteins with appropriate domains. Recruitment brings Akt close to other components required for activation. PIP3 is not Akt itself, and recruitment alone does not replace all necessary phosphorylation steps. An Akt PH domain recognises appropriate phosphoinositides. This binding localises the protein; PIP3 is neither a soluble molecule like IP3 nor the kinase's phosphate donor.
