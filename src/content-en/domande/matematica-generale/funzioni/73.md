What is the solution set of the inequality $\ln(2x - 1) < 0$?

- $(-\infty, 1)$
- $(1, +\infty)$
- $(0, 1)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 1 \right)$

## Explanation

The argument of the logarithm must be positive, so $2x - 1 > 0$. Since $0 = \ln 1$ and the natural logarithm is increasing, the inequality $\ln(2x - 1) < \ln 1$ is equivalent to $2x - 1 < 1$. The two conditions must hold together:
$$
\begin{cases}
2x - 1 > 0 \\[4pt]
2x - 1 < 1
\end{cases}
\iff
\frac{1}{2} < x < 1 .
$$
Therefore the solution set is $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 1 \right)$. The set $(-\infty, 1)$ is obtained by forgetting the condition on the argument.
