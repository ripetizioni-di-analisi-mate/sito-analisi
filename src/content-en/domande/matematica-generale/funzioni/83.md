The figure shows the demand line of a good, with the quantity $q$ on the horizontal axis and the price $p$ on the vertical axis; the line passes through the points $(0, 30)$ and $(60, 0)$. What is the demand function $q_d(p)$?

::grafico{f="30-x/2+0*sqrt(x*(60-x))" punti="0,30,pieno;60,0,pieno" xmin=-10 xmax=90 ymin=-5 ymax=45 nomi="q,p" legenda=0}

- $q_d(p) = 30 - \dfrac{p}{2}$
- $q_d(p) = 60 - 2p$
- $q_d(p) = 30 - 2p$
- $q_d(p) = 60 - \dfrac{p}{2}$

## Explanation

In the plane with quantity on the horizontal axis and price on the vertical axis, the line through $(0, 30)$ and $(60, 0)$ has slope $\dfrac{0 - 30}{60 - 0} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and intercept $30$. Hence the inverse demand is $p = 30 - \dfrac{q}{2}$. The demand function gives the quantity as a function of the price, therefore we solve for $q$ and obtain $\dfrac{q}{2} = 30 - p$, that is, $q_d(p) = 60 - 2p$. Indeed $q_d(0) = 60$ and $q_d(30) = 0$, in agreement with the two points of the figure. The function $30 - \dfrac{p}{2}$ is obtained by writing the inverse demand with the letter $p$ in place of $q$.
