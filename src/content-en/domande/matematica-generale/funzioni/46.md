Let $A = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : x^2 < 4\}$ and $B = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : x \geq 1\}$. What is the union $A \cup B$?

- $(-2, +\infty)$
- $[1, 2)$
- $[-2, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, -2) \cup [1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The inequality $x^2 < 4$ holds for $-2 < x < 2$, so $A = (-2, 2)$; the endpoints are excluded, because $(\pm 2)^2 = 4$. The **union** $A \cup B$ consists of the numbers that lie in $A$ or in $B$. The numbers in $(-2, 1)$ lie in $A$ and those in $[1, +\infty)$ lie in $B$, while a number $x \leq -2$ lies in neither set. Therefore $A \cup B = (-2, +\infty)$. The interval $[1, 2)$ is instead the intersection $A \cap B$.
