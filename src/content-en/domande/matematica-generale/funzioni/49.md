How many integers belong to the interval $\left( -\dfrac{5}{2}, \sqrt{10} \, \right]$?

- $5$
- $7$
- $4$
- $6$

## Explanation

Since $-\dfrac{5}{2} = -2.5$ and the left endpoint is excluded, the smallest integer in the interval is $-2$. Since $3^2 = 9 < 10 < 16 = 4^2$, we have $3 < \sqrt{10} < 4$, so the largest integer in the interval is $3$. Therefore the integers in the interval are $-2$, $-1$, $0$, $1$, $2$, $3$, that is, $6$ numbers.
