What is the inverse of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3}$, defined for $x \neq 3$?

- $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{3y + 1}{y - 2}$
- $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{y - 3}{2y + 1}$
- $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{3y - 1}{y - 2}$
- $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{3y + 1}{y + 2}$

## Explanation

To find the inverse we solve the equation $y = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3}$ for $x$. Multiplying by $x - 3$ we get $xy - 3y = 2x + 1$, so $x\,(y - 2) = 3y + 1$. For every $y \neq 2$ this equation has exactly one solution, therefore $f$ is injective, its image is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{2\}$ and
$$
f^{-1}(y) = \frac{3y + 1}{y - 2}, \qquad y \neq 2 .
$$
