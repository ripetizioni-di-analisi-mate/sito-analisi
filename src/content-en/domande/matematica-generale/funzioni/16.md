Let $A = (-\infty, 3]$ and $B = (0, +\infty)$. What is the difference $A \setminus B$?

- $(-\infty, 0)$
- $(0, 3]$
- $(3, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, 0]$

## Explanation

The **difference** $A \setminus B$ consists of the elements of $A$ that do not belong to $B$. A number $x \leq 3$ is not in $B$ when $x \leq 0$, so $A \setminus B = (-\infty, 0]$. The number $0$ belongs to $A$ and does not belong to $B$, because $B$ is open at the endpoint $0$; therefore $0$ lies in the difference. The interval $(0, 3]$ is instead the intersection $A \cap B$, and $(3, +\infty)$ is the difference $B \setminus A$.
