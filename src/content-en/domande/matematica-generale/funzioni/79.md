What is the natural domain of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{e^{x} - 1}$?

- $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}$
- $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$
- $(0, +\infty)$
- $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{e\}$

## Explanation

The exponential imposes no conditions, so the only condition for the **natural domain** is that the denominator be different from zero. The equation $e^{x} - 1 = 0$ is equivalent to $e^{x} = 1 = e^{0}$. The exponential is injective, therefore the only solution is $x = 0$. Hence the domain is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$. The number $1$ is the value of $e^{x}$ to be excluded, which corresponds to $x = 0$.
