What is the natural domain of $f(x) = \sqrt{2^{x} - 4}$?

- $(2, +\infty)$
- $[2, +\infty)$
- $[4, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, 2]$

## Explanation

The square root is defined when the radicand is greater than or equal to $0$, so the domain is the solution set of $2^{x} - 4 \geq 0$. Since $4 = 2^{2}$, the inequality becomes $2^{x} \geq 2^{2}$. The exponential function with base $2$ is increasing, therefore the inequality between the powers is equivalent to the one between the exponents, that is $x \geq 2$. The **natural domain** is thus $[2, +\infty)$, and the endpoint $2$ is included because $f(2) = \sqrt{0} = 0$. The interval $[4, +\infty)$ comes from comparing $x$ with $4$ instead of with the exponent $2$, and $(-\infty, 2]$ from reversing the inequality as for a base less than $1$.
