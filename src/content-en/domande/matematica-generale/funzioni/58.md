What is the inverse of the function $f(x) = x^2 + 2$ restricted to the interval $(-\infty, 0]$?

- $f^{-1}(y) = -\sqrt{y - 2}$, with $y \geq 2$
- $f^{-1}(y) = \sqrt{y - 2}$, with $y \geq 2$
- $f^{-1}(y) = -\sqrt{y + 2}$, with $y \geq -2$
- $f^{-1}(y) = -\sqrt{y - 2}$, with $y \leq 0$

## Explanation

For $x_1 < x_2 \leq 0$ we have $x_1^2 > x_2^2$, so $f$ is decreasing on $(-\infty, 0]$ and is injective. On $(-\infty, 0]$ the square $x^2$ takes all the values in $[0, +\infty)$, therefore the range of $f$ is $[2, +\infty)$. From the equation $y = x^2 + 2$ we get $x^2 = y - 2$, that is, $x = \pm \sqrt{y - 2}$. Since $x \leq 0$, we choose the minus sign. Hence $f^{-1}(y) = -\sqrt{y - 2}$, defined on the range of $f$, that is, for $y \geq 2$.
