The demand and supply of a good are $q_d(p) = a - 2p$ and $q_s(p) = 3p - 20$. The parameter $a$ goes from $80$ to $90$, because at every price consumers demand $10$ more units. What happens to the equilibrium?

- The equilibrium price and quantity both increase
- The equilibrium price increases and the quantity decreases
- The equilibrium price decreases and the quantity increases
- The equilibrium price stays the same and the quantity increases by $10$

## Explanation

The equation $a - 2p = 3p - 20$ gives $p^* = \dfrac{a + 20}{5}$, which increases as $a$ increases. The equilibrium quantity is $q^* = 3p^* - 20$, and it increases together with $p^*$ because supply is increasing. For $a = 80$ we have $p^* = 20$ and $q^* = 40$, for $a = 90$ we have $p^* = 22$ and $q^* = 46$. Hence the equilibrium price and quantity both increase. The quantity increases by $6$ and not by $10$, because the higher price partly reduces the quantity demanded.
