What is the solution set of the inequality $\left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{x} > 9$?

- $x > -2$
- $x < 2$
- $x < -2$
- $x > 2$

## Explanation

Since $9 = \left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{-2}$, the inequality can be written as $\left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{x} > \left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{-2}$. The base $\dfrac{1}{3}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, so the exponential is decreasing, and of two powers the larger one is the one with the smaller exponent. Therefore the inequality is equivalent to $x < -2$. For example, $x = -3$ gives $\left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{-3} = 27 > 9$.
