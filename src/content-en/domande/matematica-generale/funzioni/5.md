- $f^{-1}(y) = 2 + e^{\,y + 1}$, with $y \in \mathbb{R}$
- $f^{-1}(y) = 2 + e^{\,y - 1}$, with $y > 2$
- $f^{-1}(y) = 2 + e^{\,y - 1}$, with $y \in \mathbb{R}$
- $f^{-1}(y) = e^{\,y - 1} - 2$, with $y \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

The function $\ln(x - 2) + 1$ is increasing for $x > 2$, so it is injective and has an inverse. From the equation $y = \ln(x - 2) + 1$ we obtain $\ln(x - 2) = y - 1$. By the definition of the **logarithm**, this equality is equivalent to $x - 2 = e^{\,y - 1}$, therefore $f^{-1}(y) = 2 + e^{\,y - 1}$. The domain of the inverse is the range of $f$. Since $x - 2$ takes every value in $(0, +\infty)$, the logarithm $\ln(x - 2)$ takes every real value, so the inverse is defined for every $y \in \mathbb{R}$.
