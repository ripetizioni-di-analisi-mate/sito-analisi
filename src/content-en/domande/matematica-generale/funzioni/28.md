For which $x$ is the function $f(x) = -x^2 + x + 6$ positive?

- $(-\infty, -2) \cup (3, +\infty)$
- $(-3, 2)$
- $[-2, 3]$
- $(-2, 3)$

## Explanation

The zeros of $f$ are the solutions of $-x^2 + x + 6 = 0$, that is, of $x^2 - x - 6 = 0$. Since $x^2 - x - 6 = (x - 3)(x + 2)$, the zeros are $-2$ and $3$. The coefficient of $x^2$ is $a = -1 < 0$, so $f$ has the sign of $a$ outside the interval $[-2, 3]$ and the opposite sign inside it. Therefore $f$ is positive for $-2 < x < 3$. The endpoints $-2$ and $3$ are excluded because the function equals $0$ there.
