What is the natural domain of $f(x) = \dfrac{\ln x}{\ln x - 1}$?

- $(0, +\infty)$
- $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{e\}$
- $(0, e) \cup (e, +\infty)$
- $(0, 1) \cup (1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The logarithm requires $x > 0$ and the denominator requires $\ln x - 1 \neq 0$, and the two conditions must hold together. The equation $\ln x = 1$ is equivalent to $x = e^{1} = e$, by the definition of **logarithm**. Therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\[4pt]
\ln x \neq 1
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\[4pt]
x \neq e
\end{cases}
$$
and the domain is $(0, e) \cup (e, +\infty)$. The number $1$ belongs to the domain, because it makes the numerator zero and not the denominator, and indeed $f(1) = \dfrac{0}{-1} = 0$.
