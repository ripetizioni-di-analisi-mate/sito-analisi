The inverse demand of a good is $p = 40 - q$ and the inverse supply is $p = 4 + 2q$. The government introduces a tax of $6$ euros on every unit sold, paid by the producers, who therefore ask a price $6$ euros higher for every quantity. What is the new equilibrium price paid by consumers?

- $34$ euros
- $24$ euros
- $30$ euros
- $40$ euros

## Explanation

With the tax the inverse supply becomes $p = 4 + 2q + 6 = 10 + 2q$, while demand stays the same. At equilibrium the price is the same on the two curves, so $40 - q = 10 + 2q$, that is, $3q = 30$ and $q^* = 10$. The price paid by consumers is $p^* = 40 - 10 = 30$ euros. Without the tax the equilibrium would be $q = 12$ and $p = 28$ euros, therefore consumers pay $2$ euros more and not the whole $6$ euros of the tax. The value $24$ euros is the price received by producers net of the tax.
