What is the largest interval on which the function $f(x) = \ln(x^2 - 4x + 5)$ is increasing?

- $(-\infty, 2]$
- $\mathbb{R}$
- $(0, +\infty)$
- $[2, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The argument $g(x) = x^2 - 4x + 5$ has discriminant $\Delta = 16 - 20 < 0$ and $a = 1 > 0$, so it is positive for every $x$ and the domain of $f$ is $\mathbb{R}$. The function $g$ is a parabola with vertex at $x = 2$, decreasing on $(-\infty, 2]$ and increasing on $[2, +\infty)$. Since $\ln$ is increasing, the composite $f = \ln \circ g$ increases where $g$ increases and decreases where $g$ decreases. Therefore the largest interval on which $f$ is increasing is $[2, +\infty)$.
