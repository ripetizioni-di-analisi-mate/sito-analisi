The demand for a good is $q_d(p) = \dfrac{100}{p}$, with $p > 0$, and the supply is $q_s(p) = p - 15$. What are the equilibrium price and quantity?

- $p^* = 5$ euros, $q^* = 20$
- $p^* = 10$ euros, $q^* = 10$
- $p^* = 25$ euros, $q^* = 4$
- $p^* = 20$ euros, $q^* = 5$

## Explanation

At **market equilibrium** the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied, so $\dfrac{100}{p} = p - 15$. Multiplying by $p > 0$ gives $p^2 - 15p - 100 = 0$, that is, $(p - 20)(p + 5) = 0$. The price must be positive, therefore $p^* = 20$ euros. The equilibrium quantity is $q^* = \dfrac{100}{20} = 5$, and indeed also $20 - 15 = 5$. At a price of $25$ euros demand is $4$ and supply is $10$, so that price is not an equilibrium price.
