What is the natural domain of $f(x) = \ln(4 - x^2) + \dfrac{1}{x}$?

- $(-2, 2)$
- $(-2, 0) \cup (0, 2)$
- $[-2, 0) \cup (0, 2]$
- $(-\infty, -2) \cup (2, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The argument of the logarithm must be strictly positive and the denominator must be different from zero, so the following must hold together:
$$
\begin{cases}
4 - x^2 > 0 \\[4pt]
x \neq 0
\end{cases}
$$
The inequality $4 - x^2 > 0$ is equivalent to $x^2 < 4$, which is satisfied for $-2 < x < 2$; the endpoints are excluded, because $\ln 0$ is not defined. Removing $x = 0$ we obtain the domain $(-2, 0) \cup (0, 2)$.
