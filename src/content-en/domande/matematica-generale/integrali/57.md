Compute $\displaystyle\int e^{x} \sin x\,dx$.

- $\dfrac{e^{x} (\sin x - \cos x)}{2} + c$
- $\dfrac{e^{x} (\sin x + \cos x)}{2} + c$
- $e^{x} (\sin x - \cos x) + c$
- $-e^{x} \cos x + c$

## Explanation

Let $I = \displaystyle\int e^{x} \sin x\,dx$. By parts, with $\sin x$ as the factor to differentiate and $e^{x}$ as the factor to integrate, we have
$$
I = e^{x} \sin x - \int e^{x} \cos x\,dx .
$$
Applying **integration by parts** again, with $\cos x$ as the factor to differentiate, we get $\displaystyle\int e^{x} \cos x\,dx = e^{x} \cos x + \int e^{x} \sin x\,dx = e^{x} \cos x + I$. Substituting gives an equation in $I$:
$$
I = e^{x} \sin x - e^{x} \cos x - I ,
$$
so $2I = e^{x} (\sin x - \cos x)$, and therefore $I = \dfrac{e^{x} (\sin x - \cos x)}{2} + c$.
