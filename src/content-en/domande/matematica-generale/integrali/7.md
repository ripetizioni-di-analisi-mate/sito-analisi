- $2 - \ln 3$
- $8 - 2 \ln 5$
- $4 - 2 \ln 3$
- $4 + 2 \ln 3$

## Explanation

We set $t = \sqrt{x}$, that is, $x = t^2$, so $dx = 2t\,dt$. When $x$ goes from $0$ to $4$ the new variable $t$ goes from $0$ to $2$; therefore
$$
\int_0^4 \frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{x}}\,dx = \int_0^2 \frac{2t}{1 + t}\,dt = 2 \int_0^2 \left( 1 - \frac{1}{1 + t} \right) dt .
$$
By the fundamental formula of calculus the last integral equals $2 \Big[ t - \ln (1 + t) \Big]_0^2 = 2\,(2 - \ln 3)$, so the result is $4 - 2 \ln 3$.
