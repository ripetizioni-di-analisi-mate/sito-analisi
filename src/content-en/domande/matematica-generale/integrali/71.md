Which of the following integrals has a negative value?

- $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} x^3\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_{1}^{0} (x - 1)\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2} \left( x^2 - 2x \right) dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi} \sin x\,dx$

## Explanation

For $0 < x < 2$ we have $x^2 - 2x = x\,(x - 2) < 0$, so the integrand is negative on the whole interval, and with the limits in increasing order the integral is **negative**; it equals $\dfrac{8}{3} - 4 = -\dfrac{4}{3}$. The function $x^3$ is odd, therefore its integral over $[-1, 1]$ is $0$. In the integral $\displaystyle\int_{1}^{0} (x - 1)\,dx$ the function is negative on $[0, 1]$, but the limits are reversed, so the value is $-\displaystyle\int_0^1 (x - 1)\,dx = \dfrac{1}{2} > 0$. Finally, $\sin x \geq 0$ on $[0, \pi]$, and its integral equals $2$.
