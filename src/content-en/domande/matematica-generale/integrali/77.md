Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_x^{2x} \dfrac{1}{t}\,dt$ for $x > 0$. Which statement is true?

- $F$ is constant on $(0, +\infty)$ and equals $\ln 2$
- $F$ is increasing on $(0, +\infty)$
- $F$ is decreasing on $(0, +\infty)$
- $F(x) = \ln x$ for every $x > 0$

## Explanation

Let $H(x) = \displaystyle\int_1^{x} \dfrac{1}{t}\,dt$ for $x > 0$. By additivity with respect to the interval we have $F(x) = H(2x) - H(x)$. The function $\dfrac{1}{t}$ is continuous on $(0, +\infty)$, so by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** $H'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$. By the chain rule
$$
F'(x) = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2x} - \frac{1}{x} = 0 \qquad \text{for every } x > 0 ,
$$
therefore $F$ is constant on the interval $(0, +\infty)$. Its value is $F(1) = \displaystyle\int_1^{2} \dfrac{1}{t}\,dt = \ln 2$. Forgetting the factor $2$, the derivative of $2x$, one would get $F'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{2x}$, that is, a decreasing function.
