Compute $\displaystyle\int_1^{e} (\ln x)^2\,dx$.

- $e - 1$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $e + 2$
- $e - 2$

## Explanation

We apply **integration by parts** with $(\ln x)^2$ as the factor to differentiate, whose derivative is $\dfrac{2 \ln x}{x}$, and $1$ as the factor to integrate, whose antiderivative is $x$:
$$
\int_1^{e} (\ln x)^2\,dx = \Big[ x\,(\ln x)^2 \Big]_1^{e} - \int_1^{e} 2 \ln x\,dx .
$$
The first term equals $e \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 0 = e$. An antiderivative of $\ln x$ is $x \ln x - x$, so
$$
\int_1^{e} 2 \ln x\,dx = 2 \Big[ x \ln x - x \Big]_1^{e} = 2 \big( (e - e) - (0 - 1) \big) = 2 .
$$
Therefore the integral equals $e - 2$. The value $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is that of $\displaystyle\int_1^{e} \dfrac{(\ln x)^2}{x}\,dx$, which has a different integrand.
