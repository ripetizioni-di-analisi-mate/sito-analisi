Compute $\displaystyle\int e^{\sqrt{x}}\,dx$ for $x > 0$ with the substitution $t = \sqrt{x}$.

- $2\,e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $2\sqrt{x}\,e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $2 \left( \sqrt{x} - 1 \right) e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $2 \left( \sqrt{x} + 1 \right) e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$

## Explanation

With $t = \sqrt{x}$ we have $x = t^2$ and $dx = 2t\,dt$, so by **integration by substitution**
$$
\int e^{\sqrt{x}}\,dx = \int 2t\,e^{t}\,dt .
$$
The integral in $t$ is computed by parts, with $2t$ as the factor to differentiate and $e^{t}$ as the factor to integrate:
$$
\int 2t\,e^{t}\,dt = 2t\,e^{t} - \int 2\,e^{t}\,dt = 2\,(t - 1)\,e^{t} + c .
$$
Returning to the variable $x$ with $t = \sqrt{x}$ gives $2 \left( \sqrt{x} - 1 \right) e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$. The result $2\,e^{\sqrt{x}}$ would come from forgetting the factor $t$ in the differential $dx = 2t\,dt$.
