- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- The integral diverges

## Explanation

By definition the integral is the limit as $t \to +\infty$ of $\displaystyle\int_0^t x\,e^{-2x}\,dx$. With **integration by parts**, with finite factor $x$ and differential factor $e^{-2x}$, whose antiderivative is $-\dfrac{e^{-2x}}{2}$, we obtain
$$
\int_0^t x\,e^{-2x}\,dx = \left[ -\frac{x\,e^{-2x}}{2} \right]_0^t + \frac{1}{2} \int_0^t e^{-2x}\,dx = -\frac{t\,e^{-2t}}{2} + \frac{1 - e^{-2t}}{4} .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ we have $e^{-2t} \to 0$ and, by the comparison of infinities, $t\,e^{-2t} \to 0$. So the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$; therefore the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$.
