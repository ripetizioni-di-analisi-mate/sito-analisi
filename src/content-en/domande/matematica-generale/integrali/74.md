Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^{x} t\,e^{-t}\,dt$. At which point does the graph of $F$ have an inflection point?

- $x = 0$
- $x = 1$
- $x = 2$
- $F$ has no inflection points

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous, so by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** $F'(x) = x\,e^{-x}$. Differentiating again gives
$$
F''(x) = e^{-x} - x\,e^{-x} = (1 - x)\,e^{-x} .
$$
Since $e^{-x} > 0$, we have $F''(x) > 0$ for $x < 1$ and $F''(x) < 0$ for $x > 1$, therefore $F$ is convex to the left of $1$ and concave to the right, and $x = 1$ is an **inflection point**. At $x = 0$ the derivative $F'$ vanishes and changes from negative to positive values, so $x = 0$ is a local minimum point. The point $x = 2$ is the inflection point of the integrand $x\,e^{-x}$, not of $F$.
