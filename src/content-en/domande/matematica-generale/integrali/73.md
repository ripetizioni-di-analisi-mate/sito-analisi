Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_1^{x} e^{t^2 - 1}\,dt$. What is the tangent line to the graph of $F$ at the point with $x$-coordinate $x = 1$?

- $y = x - 1$
- $y = x$
- $y = 2x - 2$
- $y = e\,(x - 1)$

## Explanation

The tangent line at $x = 1$ is $y = F(1) + F'(1)\,(x - 1)$. The limits of integration coincide for $x = 1$, so $F(1) = 0$. The function $e^{t^2 - 1}$ is continuous, therefore by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** $F'(x) = e^{x^2 - 1}$, and $F'(1) = e^{0} = 1$. The tangent line is then $y = x - 1$. The slope $2$ is the derivative of the integrand at $1$, and the slope $e$ would come from evaluating $e^{x^2}$ instead of $e^{x^2 - 1}$.
