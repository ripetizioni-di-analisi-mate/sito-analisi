Compute $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{(x + 1)^2}{x}\,dx$ for $x > 0$.

- $\dfrac{(x + 1)^3}{3} \ln x + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + x + \ln x + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + 2x + \ln x + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + 2x - \dfrac{1}{x^2} + c$

## Explanation

Expanding the square gives $(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$, so dividing each term by $x$
$$
\frac{(x + 1)^2}{x} = x + 2 + \frac{1}{x} .
$$
By linearity we integrate term by term with the **basic integrals**, and since $x > 0$ the absolute value in the logarithm is omitted:
$$
\int \left( x + 2 + \frac{1}{x} \right) dx = \frac{x^2}{2} + 2x + \ln x + c .
$$
The antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is $\ln x$, while $-\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ is its derivative.
