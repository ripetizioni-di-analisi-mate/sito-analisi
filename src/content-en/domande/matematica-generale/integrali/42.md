The inverse demand for a good is $p_d(q) = 100 - 2q$ and the inverse supply is $p_s(q) = 10 + q$. What is the sum $S_C + S_P$ of the consumer surplus and the producer surplus?

- $900$ euros
- $1350$ euros
- $450$ euros
- $2700$ euros

## Explanation

At equilibrium $100 - 2q = 10 + q$, so $q^* = 30$ and $p^* = 40$. Adding the definitions of the two surpluses gives
$$
S_C + S_P = \int_0^{q^*} \big( p_d(q) - p^* \big)\,dq + \int_0^{q^*} \big( p^* - p_s(q) \big)\,dq = \int_0^{q^*} \big( p_d(q) - p_s(q) \big)\,dq ,
$$
because the terms with $p^*$ cancel. The sum is therefore the area between the demand curve and the supply curve for $0 \leq q \leq 30$, and it equals
$$
S_C + S_P = \int_0^{30} (90 - 3q)\,dq = \left[ 90q - \frac{3q^2}{2} \right]_0^{30} = 2700 - 1350 = 1350
$$
euros. Separately, $S_C = 900$ euros and $S_P = 450$ euros, and their sum is again $1350$ euros.
