Compute $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{x^2}{x^2 - 4}\,dx$.

- $\ln \left\lvert \dfrac{x - 2}{x + 2} \right\rvert + c$
- $x + \dfrac{1}{4} \ln \left\lvert \dfrac{x - 2}{x + 2} \right\rvert + c$
- $x + 4 \ln \left\lvert x^2 - 4 \right\rvert + c$
- $x + \ln \left\lvert \dfrac{x - 2}{x + 2} \right\rvert + c$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator have the same degree, so the division is carried out first. Since $x^2 = \left( x^2 - 4 \right) + 4$, one has $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 - 4} = 1 + \dfrac{4}{x^2 - 4}$. The denominator has roots $2$ and $-2$, and the **partial fraction** decomposition $\dfrac{4}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} = \dfrac{A}{x - 2} + \dfrac{B}{x + 2}$ is equivalent to $4 = A\,(x + 2) + B\,(x - 2)$. With $x = 2$ one obtains $A = 1$, and with $x = -2$ one obtains $B = -1$, therefore
$$
\int \frac{x^2}{x^2 - 4}\,dx = x + \ln \lvert x - 2 \rvert - \ln \lvert x + 2 \rvert + c = x + \ln \left\lvert \frac{x - 2}{x + 2} \right\rvert + c .
$$
The result without the term $x$ comes from forgetting the quotient $1$ of the division, and the coefficient $\dfrac{1}{4}$ from decomposing $\dfrac{1}{x^2 - 4}$ instead of $\dfrac{4}{x^2 - 4}$.
