Compute $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{x^3}{x^2 - 1}\,dx$.

- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + \ln \left\lvert x^2 - 1 \right\rvert + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} - \dfrac{1}{2} \ln \left\lvert x^2 - 1 \right\rvert + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \ln \left\lvert x^2 - 1 \right\rvert + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} \ln \left\lvert x^2 - 1 \right\rvert + c$

## Explanation

The numerator has degree $3$ and the denominator has degree $2$, so we first carry out the division. We have $x^3 = x\left( x^2 - 1 \right) + x$, therefore
$$
\frac{x^3}{x^2 - 1} = x + \frac{x}{x^2 - 1} .
$$
The numerator $x$ is half the derivative of $x^2 - 1$, so the second term reduces to the basic integral $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx = \ln \lvert g(x) \rvert + c$, and we obtain
$$
\int \frac{x^3}{x^2 - 1}\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \ln \left\lvert x^2 - 1 \right\rvert + c .
$$
The result $\dfrac{1}{2} \ln \left\lvert x^2 - 1 \right\rvert + c$ comes from forgetting the quotient $x$ of the division.
