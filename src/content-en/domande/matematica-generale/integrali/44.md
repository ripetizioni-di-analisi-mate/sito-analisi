The marginal cost of a firm is $C'(q) = 50\,e^{0.1\,q}$. What is the additional cost of raising production from $10$ to $20$ units?

- $50 \left( e^2 - e \right)$ euros
- $5 \left( e^2 - e \right)$ euros
- $500 \left( e^2 - e \right)$ euros
- $500\,e$ euros

## Explanation

The additional cost is $C(20) - C(10)$, and by the fundamental formula of calculus it equals the integral of the marginal cost from $10$ to $20$. The integral is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int e^{g(q)}\,g'(q)\,dq = e^{g(q)} + c$ with $g(q) = 0.1\,q$, so an antiderivative of $50\,e^{0.1\,q}$ is $\dfrac{50}{0.1}\,e^{0.1\,q} = 500\,e^{0.1\,q}$. Therefore
$$
\int_{10}^{20} 50\,e^{0.1\,q}\,dq = \Big[ 500\,e^{0.1\,q} \Big]_{10}^{20} = 500 \left( e^2 - e \right) ,
$$
that is, about $2335.39$ euros.
