Compute $\displaystyle\int \cos x \; e^{\sin x}\,dx$.

- $e^{\sin x} \cos x + c$
- $e^{\cos x} + c$
- $-e^{\sin x} + c$
- $e^{\sin x} + c$

## Explanation

The integrand has the form $e^{g(x)}\,g'(x)$ with $g(x) = \sin x$, because $(\sin x)' = \cos x$. By the **basic integrals in composite form** we therefore have
$$
\int e^{\sin x} \cos x\,dx = e^{\sin x} + c .
$$
Indeed, the derivative of $e^{\sin x}$ is $e^{\sin x} \cos x$ by the chain rule. With the minus sign one would get the derivative $-e^{\sin x} \cos x$.
