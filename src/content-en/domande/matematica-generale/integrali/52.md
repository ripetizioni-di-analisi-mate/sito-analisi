Let $F$ be an antiderivative on $\mathbb{R}$ of the continuous function $f$. Which function is an antiderivative of $f(5 - 2x)$?

- $-2F(5 - 2x)$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}F(5 - 2x)$
- $F(5 - 2x)$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}F(5 - 2x)$

## Explanation

Set $t = 5 - 2x$, so $dt = -2\,dx$, that is $dx = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,dt$. With this **substitution** one obtains
$$
\int f(5 - 2x)\,dx = -\frac{1}{2} \int f(t)\,dt = -\frac{1}{2}F(t) + c .
$$
Going back to the variable $x$, an antiderivative of $f(5 - 2x)$ is therefore $-\dfrac{1}{2}F(5 - 2x)$. The result $\dfrac{1}{2}F(5 - 2x)$ comes from neglecting the sign in $dt = -2\,dx$, and $-2F(5 - 2x)$ from multiplying by the coefficient of $x$ instead of dividing by it.
