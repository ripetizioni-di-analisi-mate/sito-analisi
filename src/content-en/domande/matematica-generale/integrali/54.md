Compute the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} \dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1}\,dx$.

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The integral diverges to $+\infty$
- $1$
- $0$

## Explanation

The numerator $x$ is half the derivative of $x^2 + 1$, so the integral reduces to the basic integral $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx = \ln \lvert g(x) \rvert + c$, and for $t > 0$
$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{x}{x^2 + 1}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \Big[ \ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right) \Big]_0^{t} = \frac{1}{2} \ln\left( t^2 + 1 \right) .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ we have $t^2 + 1 \to +\infty$, therefore the logarithm tends to $+\infty$ and the **improper integral diverges** to $+\infty$. The integrand tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$, but for large $x$ it is close to $\dfrac{1}{x}$, whose improper integral diverges. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is that of $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} \dfrac{x}{\left( x^2 + 1 \right)^2}\,dx$, which has a different integrand.
