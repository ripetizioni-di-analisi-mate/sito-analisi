- $x^4 - 9\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2 \ln x + c$
- $x^4 - 4\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2 \ln x + c$
- $x^4 - 4\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} - \dfrac{2}{x^2} + c$
- $x^4 - \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{x}} + 2 \ln x + c$

## Explanation

By the **linearity** of the integral, each term is integrated separately and the coefficients are brought outside the integral. We write $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$, so the power formula gives $\displaystyle\int x^3\,dx = \dfrac{x^4}{4}$ and $\displaystyle\int x^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dx = \dfrac{2}{3}\,x^{\frac{3}{2}}$. Since $x > 0$, we have $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx = \ln x$. Therefore
$$
\int \left( 4x^3 - 6\sqrt{x} + \frac{2}{x} \right) dx = 4 \cdot \frac{x^4}{4} - 6 \cdot \frac{2}{3}\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2 \ln x + c = x^4 - 4\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2 \ln x + c .
$$
