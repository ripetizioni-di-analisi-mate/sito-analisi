Let $f$ be continuous on $[0, 2]$ with $\displaystyle\int_0^{2} f(t)\,dt = 6$. What is $\displaystyle\int_0^{1} f(2x)\,dx$?

- $6$
- $12$
- $2$
- $3$

## Explanation

We apply the substitution $t = 2x$, with $dt = 2\,dx$, that is, $dx = \dfrac{1}{2}\,dt$. As $x$ goes from $0$ to $1$, the variable $t$ goes from $0$ to $2$, so the limits change as well:
$$
\int_0^{1} f(2x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2} f(t)\,dt = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6 = 3 .
$$
The value $12$ comes from multiplying by $2$ instead of dividing, and the value $6$ comes from changing the limits without the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
