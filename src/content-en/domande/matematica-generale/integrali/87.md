What is the area of the unbounded region between the graphs of $y = \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{x^3}$ for $x \geq 1$?

::grafico{f="1/x^2+0*sqrt(x-0.5);1/x^3+0*sqrt(x-0.5)" area="1/x^2;1/x^3;1;9" segmenti="1,0,1,1" xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-1 ymax=2 legenda="y = \dfrac{1}{x^2};y = \dfrac{1}{x^3}" titolo="The graphs of $y = \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{x^3}$ for $x \geq 1$"}

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The region has infinite area

## Explanation

For $x \geq 1$ we have $x^3 \geq x^2$, so $\dfrac{1}{x^2} \geq \dfrac{1}{x^3}$, and the area is the improper integral of the difference. By the **improper integral of powers** with $a = 2$ and $a = 3$
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2 - 1} = 1,
\qquad
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{1}{x^3}\,dx = \frac{1}{3 - 1} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
Both integrals converge, therefore the area is finite and equals $1 - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{3}{2}$ comes from adding the two integrals instead of subtracting them.
