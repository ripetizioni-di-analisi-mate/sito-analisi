The figure shows the graph of the function $f(x) = 2 - x$. Which statement is true for *every* antiderivative $F$ of $f$ on $\mathbb{R}$?

::grafico{f="2-x" punti="2,0,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=4 legenda="y = 2 - x" titolo="The graph of $f(x) = 2 - x$"}

- $F$ is convex on $\mathbb{R}$
- $F$ is concave on $\mathbb{R}$
- $F$ is decreasing on $\mathbb{R}$
- $F$ has a local minimum at $x = 2$

## Explanation

Every antiderivative $F$ satisfies $F'(x) = f(x) = 2 - x$, so $F''(x) = -1 < 0$ for every $x$. Therefore every antiderivative is **concave** on $\mathbb{R}$, and it is not convex. From the graph, $f(x) > 0$ for $x < 2$ and $f(x) < 0$ for $x > 2$, so $F$ is increasing on $(-\infty, 2]$ and decreasing on $[2, +\infty)$. Hence $F$ is not decreasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$, and it has a local maximum at $x = 2$.
