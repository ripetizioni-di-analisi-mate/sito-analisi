The region $R$ is bounded by the graph of $y = \sqrt{x}$, the line $y = x - 2$ and the $x$-axis. Which expression gives the area of $R$?

::grafico{f="sqrt(x);x-2" area="sqrt(x);0;0;2|sqrt(x);x-2;2;4" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=4 legenda="y = \sqrt{x};y = x - 2" titolo="The region bounded by $y = \sqrt{x}$, by $y = x - 2$ and by the $x$-axis"}

- $\displaystyle\int_0^{4} \left( \sqrt{x} - x + 2 \right) dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_2^{4} \left( \sqrt{x} - x + 2 \right) dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{2} \sqrt{x}\,dx + \int_2^{4} \left( \sqrt{x} - x + 2 \right) dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{4} \sqrt{x}\,dx + \int_2^{4} (x - 2)\,dx$

## Explanation

The line meets the $x$-axis at $x = 2$, and it meets the graph of $\sqrt{x}$ where $\sqrt{x} = x - 2$ with $x \geq 2$; squaring gives $x = x^2 - 4x + 4$, that is, $x^2 - 5x + 4 = 0$, and the admissible solution is $x = 4$. For $0 \leq x \leq 2$ the region lies between the graph of $\sqrt{x}$ and the $x$-axis, while for $2 \leq x \leq 4$ it lies between the graph of $\sqrt{x}$ and the line. Therefore the lower boundary changes at $x = 2$, and we split the integral at that point:
$$
A = \int_0^{2} \sqrt{x}\,dx + \int_2^{4} \left( \sqrt{x} - x + 2 \right) dx = \frac{10}{3} .
$$
The integral of $\sqrt{x} - x + 2$ over $[0, 4]$ also counts the triangle between the $x$-axis and the line for $0 \leq x \leq 2$, which does not belong to $R$, and it equals $\dfrac{16}{3}$.
