Which of the following integrals reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx = \ln \lvert g(x) \rvert + c$, without partial fractions?

- $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{2x}{x^2 + x - 6}\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{2x + 1}{x^2 + x - 6}\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{x + 1}{x^2 + x - 6}\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{1}{x^2 + x - 6}\,dx$

## Explanation

The derivative of the denominator is $\left( x^2 + x - 6 \right)' = 2x + 1$. The numerator $2x + 1$ coincides with this derivative, so
$$
\int \frac{2x + 1}{x^2 + x - 6}\,dx = \ln \left\lvert x^2 + x - 6 \right\rvert + c .
$$
The numerators $2x$, $x + 1$ and $1$ are not constant multiples of $2x + 1$, therefore the other integrals require the factorization $x^2 + x - 6 = (x - 2)(x + 3)$ and partial fractions.
