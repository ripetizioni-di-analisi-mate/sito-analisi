Which of the following improper integrals converges?

- $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x}}\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} e^{\frac{x}{2}}\,dx$

## Explanation

By the **improper integral of powers**, $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \dfrac{1}{x^{a}}\,dx$ converges if and only if $a > 1$. We have $\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x}} = \dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ with $a = \dfrac{3}{2} > 1$, so the integral of $\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x}}$ **converges** and equals $\dfrac{1}{a - 1} = 2$. The integrals of $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ and of $\dfrac{1}{x}$ have $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $a = 1$; therefore they diverge. For $x \geq 0$ we have $e^{\frac{x}{2}} \geq 1$, so $\displaystyle\int_0^{t} e^{\frac{x}{2}}\,dx \geq t$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} e^{\frac{x}{2}}\,dx$ diverges as well.
