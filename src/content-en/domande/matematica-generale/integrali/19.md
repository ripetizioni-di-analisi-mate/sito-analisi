Compute $\displaystyle\int_0^{\ln 2} e^{2x}\,dx$.

- $6$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $3$

## Explanation

The integral is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int e^{g(x)}\,g'(x)\,dx = e^{g(x)} + c$ with $g(x) = 2x$, so an antiderivative of $e^{2x}$ is $\dfrac{e^{2x}}{2}$. By the fundamental formula of calculus
$$
\int_0^{\ln 2} e^{2x}\,dx = \left[ \frac{e^{2x}}{2} \right]_0^{\ln 2} = \frac{e^{2 \ln 2} - 1}{2} .
$$
Since $e^{2 \ln 2} = e^{\ln 4} = 4$, the integral equals $\dfrac{4 - 1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
