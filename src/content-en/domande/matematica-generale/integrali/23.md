Compute $\displaystyle\int_0^{1} x^3\,e^{x^2}\,dx$.

- $\dfrac{e - 1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $e - \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Write $x^3\,e^{x^2} = x^2 \cdot x\,e^{x^2}$ and set $t = x^2$. Then $dt = 2x\,dx$, and $t$ goes from $0$ to $1$ as $x$ goes from $0$ to $1$, so
$$
\int_0^{1} x^3\,e^{x^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{1} t\,e^{t}\,dt .
$$
The last integral is computed by parts, with $f(t) = t$ and $g'(t) = e^{t}$:
$$
\int_0^{1} t\,e^{t}\,dt = \Big[ t\,e^{t} \Big]_0^{1} - \int_0^{1} e^{t}\,dt = e - (e - 1) = 1 .
$$
Therefore the integral equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
