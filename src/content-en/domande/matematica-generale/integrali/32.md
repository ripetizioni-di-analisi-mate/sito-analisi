Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^{x^2} e^{-t}\,dt$. What is $F'(x)$?

- $e^{-x^2}$
- $1 - e^{-x^2}$
- $-2x\,e^{-x^2}$
- $2x\,e^{-x^2}$

## Explanation

The function $e^{-t}$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, so by the **fundamental theorem of integral calculus** the function $G(y) = \displaystyle\int_0^{y} e^{-t}\,dt$ has derivative $G'(y) = e^{-y}$. We have $F(x) = G\left( x^2 \right)$; therefore by the chain rule
$$
F'(x) = e^{-x^2} \cdot 2x = 2x\,e^{-x^2} .
$$
