- $\dfrac{\left( x^3 + 1 \right)^5}{5} + c$
- $\dfrac{3 \left( x^3 + 1 \right)^5}{5} + c$
- $\dfrac{x^3 \left( x^3 + 1 \right)^5}{15} + c$
- $\dfrac{\left( x^3 + 1 \right)^5}{15} + c$

## Explanation

The integral can be reduced to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int g(x)^{a}\,g'(x)\,dx = \dfrac{g(x)^{a + 1}}{a + 1} + c$ with $g(x) = x^3 + 1$, $g'(x) = 3x^2$ and $a = 4$. We multiply and divide by $3$, so
$$
\int x^2 \left( x^3 + 1 \right)^4 dx = \frac{1}{3} \int 3x^2 \left( x^3 + 1 \right)^4 dx = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{\left( x^3 + 1 \right)^5}{5} + c = \frac{\left( x^3 + 1 \right)^5}{15} + c .
$$
