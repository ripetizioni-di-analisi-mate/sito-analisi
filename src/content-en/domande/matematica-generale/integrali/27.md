Let $f$ be continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, with $\displaystyle\int_0^5 f(x)\,dx = 7$ and $\displaystyle\int_3^5 f(x)\,dx = 2$. What is $\displaystyle\int_3^0 f(x)\,dx$?

- $5$
- $-5$
- $9$
- $-9$

## Explanation

By **additivity over intervals**, we have
$$
\int_0^5 f(x)\,dx = \int_0^3 f(x)\,dx + \int_3^5 f(x)\,dx ,
$$
so $\displaystyle\int_0^3 f(x)\,dx = 7 - 2 = 5$. Swapping the limits of integration changes the sign of the integral; therefore $\displaystyle\int_3^0 f(x)\,dx = -5$.
