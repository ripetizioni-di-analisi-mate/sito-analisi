Compute $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \dfrac{x^2}{x + 1}\,dx$.

- $\ln 2 + \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\ln 2$
- $\ln 2 - \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\ln 2 - \dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The numerator has higher degree than the denominator, so we divide $x^2$ by $x + 1$. The quotient is $x - 1$ and the remainder is the value of the numerator at $x = -1$, that is, $1$; therefore
$$
\frac{x^2}{x + 1} = x - 1 + \frac{1}{x + 1} .
$$
On $[0, 1]$ we have $x + 1 > 0$, so by the fundamental formula of calculus
$$
\int_0^1 \frac{x^2}{x + 1}\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} - x + \ln (x + 1) \right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{2} - 1 + \ln 2 = \ln 2 - \frac{1}{2} .
$$
