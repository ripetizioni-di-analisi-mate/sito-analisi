Compute the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} \dfrac{1}{(2x + 1)^2}\,dx$.

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The integral diverges

## Explanation

We have $\dfrac{1}{(2x + 1)^2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2\,(2x + 1)^{-2}$, so an antiderivative is $-\dfrac{1}{2\,(2x + 1)}$. By the definition of improper integral
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{1}{(2x + 1)^2}\,dx = \lim_{s \to +\infty} \left[ -\frac{1}{2\,(2x + 1)} \right]_0^{s} = \lim_{s \to +\infty} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2\,(2s + 1)} \right) = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
The limit is finite, so the integral converges and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
