What is the antiderivative $F$ of $f(x) = 6x^2 - 4x + 1$ such that $F(1) = 5$?

- $F(x) = 2x^3 - 2x^2 + x + 5$
- $F(x) = 6x^3 - 4x^2 + x + 2$
- $F(x) = 2x^3 - 2x^2 + x + 4$
- $F(x) = 2x^3 - 4x^2 + x + 6$

## Explanation

By the **linearity** of the integral and the power rule we have $\displaystyle\int 6x^2\,dx = 2x^3$ and $\displaystyle\int 4x\,dx = 2x^2$, so the antiderivatives of $f$ are
$$
F(x) = 2x^3 - 2x^2 + x + c, \qquad c \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
The condition $F(1) = 5$ gives $2 - 2 + 1 + c = 5$, so $c = 4$. Therefore the required antiderivative is $F(x) = 2x^3 - 2x^2 + x + 4$.
