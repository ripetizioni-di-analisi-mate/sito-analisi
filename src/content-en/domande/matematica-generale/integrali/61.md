Compute $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{2x + 1}{(x - 1)^2}\,dx$.

- $2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert - \dfrac{3}{x - 1} + c$
- $2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + \dfrac{3}{x - 1} + c$
- $3 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert - \dfrac{2}{x - 1} + c$
- $\ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert - \dfrac{3}{x - 1} + c$

## Explanation

The denominator has the double root $x = 1$. Writing the numerator as $2x + 1 = 2\,(x - 1) + 3$ gives the **partial fraction** decomposition
$$
\frac{2x + 1}{(x - 1)^2} = \frac{2}{x - 1} + \frac{3}{(x - 1)^2} .
$$
The antiderivatives of $\dfrac{1}{x - 1}$ and $\dfrac{1}{(x - 1)^2}$ are $\ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert$ and $-\dfrac{1}{x - 1}$, so
$$
\int \frac{2x + 1}{(x - 1)^2}\,dx = 2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert - \frac{3}{x - 1} + c .
$$
The minus sign in front of $\dfrac{3}{x - 1}$ comes from the antiderivative of the power $(x - 1)^{-2}$.
