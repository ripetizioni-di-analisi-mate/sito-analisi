- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \dfrac{x^2}{2} + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} \ln x + \dfrac{x^2}{4} + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \dfrac{x^2}{4} + c$
- $x^2 \ln x - \dfrac{x^2}{2} + c$

## Explanation

We apply **integration by parts** with finite factor $f(x) = \ln x$ and differential factor $g'(x) = x$, so $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$. The formula gives
$$
\int x \ln x\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \int \frac{x^2}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \frac{1}{2} \int x\,dx .
$$
Since $\displaystyle\int x\,dx = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$, the result is $\dfrac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \dfrac{x^2}{4} + c$.
