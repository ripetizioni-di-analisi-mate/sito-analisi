For which values of the real parameter $k$ does the integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} e^{(1 - k)\,x}\,dx$ converge?

- $k < 1$
- $k > 0$
- $k > 1$
- $k \geq 1$

## Explanation

We write $e^{(1 - k)\,x} = e^{-(k - 1)\,x}$. By the **improper integral of the exponential**, $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-hx}\,dx$ converges and equals $\dfrac{1}{h}$ if $h > 0$, and diverges if $h \leq 0$. With $h = k - 1$ the integral therefore converges if and only if $k - 1 > 0$, that is, $k > 1$. For $k = 1$ the integrand equals $1$, and the integral from $0$ to $t$ equals $t$, which tends to $+\infty$.
