Let $f$ be a continuous function on $\mathbb{R}$ such that $\displaystyle\int_0^{x} f(t)\,dt = x^2 e^{x}$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. What is $f(1)$?

- $e$
- $3e$
- $2e$
- $e^2$

## Explanation

Since $f$ is continuous, by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** the accumulation function $\displaystyle\int_0^{x} f(t)\,dt$ is differentiable and its derivative is $f(x)$. Hence $f(x)$ is the derivative of $x^2 e^{x}$, and by the product rule
$$
f(x) = 2x\,e^{x} + x^2 e^{x} = \left( x^2 + 2x \right) e^{x} .
$$
Therefore $f(1) = 3e$. The value $e$ is that of the accumulation function at $x = 1$, and the value $2e$ comes from differentiating only the factor $x^2$.
