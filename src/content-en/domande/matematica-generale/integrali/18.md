Compute $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}}\,dx$.

- $2\sqrt{x^2 + 9} + c$
- $\sqrt{x^2 + 9} + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{x^2 + 9} + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \ln\left( x^2 + 9 \right) + c$

## Explanation

Let $g(x) = x^2 + 9$, so $g'(x) = 2x$ and the integrand can be written as
$$
\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} = \frac{1}{2}\,\big( g(x) \big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,g'(x) .
$$
The integral therefore reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \big( g(x) \big)^{a} g'(x)\,dx = \dfrac{\big( g(x) \big)^{a + 1}}{a + 1} + c$ with $a = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $a + 1 = \dfrac{1}{2}$, we get
$$
\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\left( x^2 + 9 \right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{1}{2}} + c = \sqrt{x^2 + 9} + c .
$$
