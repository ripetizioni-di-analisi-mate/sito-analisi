Compute $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{x}{x^2 + 4}\,dx$.

- $\ln\left( x^2 + 4 \right) + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \ln\left( x^2 + 4 \right) + c$
- $2 \ln\left( x^2 + 4 \right) + c$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2\left( x^2 + 4 \right)} + c$

## Explanation

The integral is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx = \ln \lvert g(x) \rvert + c$ with $g(x) = x^2 + 4$ and $g'(x) = 2x$. We multiply and divide by $2$, so
$$
\int \frac{x}{x^2 + 4}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{2x}{x^2 + 4}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \ln\left( x^2 + 4 \right) + c .
$$
The absolute value is omitted because $x^2 + 4 > 0$ for every $x$.
