A business generates a continuous income that grows over time: after $t$ years the intensity of the flow is $f(t) = 100\,t$ euros per year, and the flow continues indefinitely. Under continuous compounding with $\delta = 0.1$, what is the present value of the entire flow?

- $10\,000$ euros
- $1000$ euros
- $100\,000$ euros
- The integral diverges

## Explanation

The **present value** is the improper integral $V = \displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} 100\,t\,e^{-0.1\,t}\,dt$. By parts, with $t$ as the factor to differentiate and $e^{-0.1\,t}$ as the factor to integrate, whose antiderivative is $-10\,e^{-0.1\,t}$, for $T > 0$ we have
$$
\int_0^{T} t\,e^{-0.1\,t}\,dt = \Big[ -10\,t\,e^{-0.1\,t} \Big]_0^{T} + 10 \int_0^{T} e^{-0.1\,t}\,dt = -10\,T\,e^{-0.1\,T} + 100 \left( 1 - e^{-0.1\,T} \right) .
$$
By the comparison of infinities $T\,e^{-0.1\,T} \to 0$ as $T \to +\infty$, so the integral converges to $100$. Therefore $V = 100 \cdot 100 = 10\,000$ euros. The intensity of the flow grows without bound, but the discount factor $e^{-0.1\,t}$ tends to zero faster, and for this reason the integral converges.
