- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + x + \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + x + 2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} - x + 2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + x - 2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + c$

## Explanation

The numerator has higher degree than the denominator, so we divide $x^2 + 1$ by $x - 1$. The quotient is $x + 1$ and the remainder is the value of the numerator at $x = 1$, that is, $2$; therefore
$$
\frac{x^2 + 1}{x - 1} = x + 1 + \frac{2}{x - 1} .
$$
The first term is a polynomial, with antiderivative $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + x$. The second is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx = \ln \lvert g(x) \rvert + c$ with $g(x) = x - 1$, so it has antiderivative $2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert$. The result is $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + x + 2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + c$.
