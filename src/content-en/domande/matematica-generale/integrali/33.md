Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_2^x \dfrac{t - 3}{t^2 + 1}\,dt$. Which statement is true?

- $x = 2$ is a local minimum point of $F$
- $x = 3$ is a local maximum point of $F$
- $x = 3$ is a local minimum point of $F$
- $F$ has no local extrema

## Explanation

The denominator $t^2 + 1$ never vanishes, so the integrand is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. The **fundamental theorem of calculus** therefore gives $F'(x) = \dfrac{x - 3}{x^2 + 1}$. Since the denominator is positive, $F'(x) < 0$ for $x < 3$ and $F'(x) > 0$ for $x > 3$. Thus $F$ decreases on $(-\infty, 3]$ and increases on $[3, +\infty)$, making $x = 3$ a **local minimum point**. The point $x = 2$, where $F$ equals $0$, lies in the interval where $F$ decreases.
