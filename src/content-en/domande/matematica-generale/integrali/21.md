Compute $\displaystyle\int_1^{e} \dfrac{(\ln x)^2}{x}\,dx$.

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{e^3 - 1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

We set $t = \ln x$, so $dt = \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$. When $x$ goes from $1$ to $e$ the new variable $t$ goes from $\ln 1 = 0$ to $\ln e = 1$; therefore
$$
\int_1^{e} \frac{(\ln x)^2}{x}\,dx = \int_0^1 t^2\,dt = \left[ \frac{t^3}{3} \right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
The limits of integration were changed together with the variable, so there is no need to return to the variable $x$.
