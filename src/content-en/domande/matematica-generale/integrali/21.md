The substitution $t = x^2 + 1$ is applied to the integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{\sqrt{3}} \dfrac{x}{\left( x^2 + 1 \right)^2}\,dx$. Which integral in the variable $t$ is obtained?

- $\dfrac{1}{2} \displaystyle\int_1^{4} \dfrac{1}{t^2}\,dt$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \displaystyle\int_0^{\sqrt{3}} \dfrac{1}{t^2}\,dt$
- $\displaystyle\int_1^{4} \dfrac{1}{t^2}\,dt$
- $2 \displaystyle\int_1^{4} \dfrac{1}{t^2}\,dt$

## Explanation

With $t = x^2 + 1$ we have $dt = 2x\,dx$, so $x\,dx = \dfrac{1}{2}\,dt$. As $x$ goes from $0$ to $\sqrt{3}$, the variable $t$ goes from $0^2 + 1 = 1$ to $3 + 1 = 4$, so the limits of integration change as well. We get
$$
\int_0^{\sqrt{3}} \frac{x}{\left( x^2 + 1 \right)^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \int_1^{4} \frac{1}{t^2}\,dt = \frac{1}{2} \left[ -\frac{1}{t} \right]_1^{4} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{8} .
$$

Keeping the limits $0$ and $\sqrt{3}$, which are values of $x$, would give the integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{\sqrt{3}} \dfrac{1}{t^2}\,dt$, which diverges.
