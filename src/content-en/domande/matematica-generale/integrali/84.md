Compute $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \dfrac{1}{x\,(x + 1)}\,dx$.

- $-\ln 2$
- $1$
- The integral diverges
- $\ln 2$

## Explanation

With **partial fractions** we have $\dfrac{1}{x\,(x + 1)} = \dfrac{1}{x} - \dfrac{1}{x + 1}$, so for $t > 1$
$$
\int_1^{t} \frac{1}{x\,(x + 1)}\,dx = \Big[ \ln x - \ln(x + 1) \Big]_1^{t} = \ln \frac{t}{t + 1} - \ln \frac{1}{2} .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ the fraction $\dfrac{t}{t + 1}$ tends to $1$, and its logarithm tends to $0$. Therefore the **improper integral converges** and equals $-\ln \dfrac{1}{2} = \ln 2$. The two integrals of $\dfrac{1}{x}$ and of $\dfrac{1}{x + 1}$ diverge separately, but their difference has a finite limit.
