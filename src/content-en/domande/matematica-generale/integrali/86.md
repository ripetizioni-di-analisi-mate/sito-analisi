Compute the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0} e^{3x}\,dx = \lim_{s \to -\infty} \int_{s}^{0} e^{3x}\,dx$.

- $3$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $1$

## Explanation

An antiderivative of $e^{3x}$ is $\dfrac{e^{3x}}{3}$, so for $s < 0$
$$
\int_{s}^{0} e^{3x}\,dx = \left[ \frac{e^{3x}}{3} \right]_{s}^{0} = \frac{1}{3} - \frac{e^{3s}}{3} .
$$
As $s \to -\infty$ we have $3s \to -\infty$, therefore $e^{3s} \to 0$, and the integral converges to $\dfrac{1}{3}$. The result is positive, because the integrand is positive and the limits are in increasing order. The value $3$ comes from multiplying by $3$ instead of dividing in the antiderivative.
