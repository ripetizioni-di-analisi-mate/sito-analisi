The interval $[0, 2]$ is divided into $4$ equal parts. What is the Riemann sum of $f(x) = x^2$ with the points $\xi_k$ equal to the right endpoints of the subintervals?

- $\dfrac{15}{4}$
- $\dfrac{7}{4}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- $\dfrac{15}{2}$

## Explanation

The width of the subintervals is $\Delta x = \dfrac{2 - 0}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and the right endpoints are $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $1$, $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $2$. The Riemann sum is therefore
$$
S_4 = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{4} + 1 + \frac{9}{4} + 4 \right) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{15}{2} = \frac{15}{4} .
$$
The value is greater than the integral $\displaystyle\int_0^2 x^2\,dx = \dfrac{8}{3}$, because $f$ is increasing on $[0, 2]$ and on each subinterval the rectangle has the maximum height.
