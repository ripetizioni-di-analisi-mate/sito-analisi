Compute $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{3}{x^2 - x - 2}\,dx$.

- $\ln \lvert x + 2 \rvert - \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + c$
- $\ln \lvert x + 1 \rvert - \ln \lvert x - 2 \rvert + c$
- $3 \ln \left\lvert x^2 - x - 2 \right\rvert + c$
- $\ln \lvert x - 2 \rvert - \ln \lvert x + 1 \rvert + c$

## Explanation

The denominator has roots $2$ and $-1$, so $x^2 - x - 2 = (x - 2)(x + 1)$ and we look for $A$ and $B$ such that
$$
\frac{3}{(x - 2)(x + 1)} = \frac{A}{x - 2} + \frac{B}{x + 1}
\iff
3 = A\,(x + 1) + B\,(x - 2) .
$$
With $x = 2$ we get $3 = 3A$, so $A = 1$. With $x = -1$ we get $3 = -3B$, so $B = -1$. Each partial fraction is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx = \ln \lvert g(x) \rvert + c$; therefore the integral equals $\ln \lvert x - 2 \rvert - \ln \lvert x + 1 \rvert + c$.
