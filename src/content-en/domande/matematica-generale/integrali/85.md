Compute $\displaystyle\int_e^{+\infty} \dfrac{1}{x\,(\ln x)^2}\,dx$.

- $1$
- $e$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

With the substitution $u = \ln x$ we have $du = \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$; as $x$ goes from $e$ to $t$, the variable $u$ goes from $1$ to $\ln t$. Hence
$$
\int_e^{t} \frac{1}{x\,(\ln x)^2}\,dx = \int_1^{\ln t} \frac{1}{u^2}\,du = \left[ -\frac{1}{u} \right]_1^{\ln t} = 1 - \frac{1}{\ln t} .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ we have $\ln t \to +\infty$, therefore $\dfrac{1}{\ln t} \to 0$ and the **improper integral** equals $1$. The value $-1$ comes from getting the sign of the antiderivative $-\dfrac{1}{u}$ wrong.
