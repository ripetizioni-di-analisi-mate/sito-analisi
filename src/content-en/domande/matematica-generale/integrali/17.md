Compute $\displaystyle\int \left( 2^x + \dfrac{1}{x^3} \right) dx$ for $x > 0$.

- $2^x \ln 2 - \dfrac{1}{2x^2} + c$
- $\dfrac{2^x}{\ln 2} - \dfrac{1}{4x^4} + c$
- $\dfrac{2^x}{\ln 2} + \dfrac{1}{2x^2} + c$
- $\dfrac{2^x}{\ln 2} - \dfrac{1}{2x^2} + c$

## Explanation

By the **linearity** of the integral we integrate the two terms separately. From the table of basic integrals, with base $a = 2$, we have $\displaystyle\int 2^x\,dx = \dfrac{2^x}{\ln 2} + c$. We write $\dfrac{1}{x^3} = x^{-3}$, so the power rule with exponent $-3$ gives $\dfrac{x^{-2}}{-2} = -\dfrac{1}{2x^2}$. Therefore
$$
\int \left( 2^x + \frac{1}{x^3} \right) dx = \frac{2^x}{\ln 2} - \frac{1}{2x^2} + c .
$$
