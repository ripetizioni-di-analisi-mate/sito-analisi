What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \sum_{k=1}^{n} \dfrac{1}{n} \left( \dfrac{k}{n} \right)^2$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $1$
- $0$

## Explanation

We divide $[0, 1]$ into $n$ equal parts of width $\Delta x = \dfrac{1}{n}$, with partition points $x_k = \dfrac{k}{n}$. The sum is then the **Riemann sum** of $f(x) = x^2$ with the points $\xi_k$ equal to the right endpoints:
$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{n} \left( \frac{k}{n} \right)^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} f(x_k)\,\Delta x .
$$
Since $f$ is continuous, as $n \to +\infty$ the sum tends to the **definite integral**, so the limit equals $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x^2\,dx = \dfrac{1}{3}$. Each term tends to $0$, but the number of terms grows with $n$, therefore the limit is not obtained by adding the limits of the terms.
