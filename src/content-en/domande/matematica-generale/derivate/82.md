The production cost $C(q)$, in euros, is differentiable for $q > 0$, with $C(10) = 500$ and $C(20) = 800$. What does the mean value theorem guarantee?

- $C'(q) = 30$ euros for every $q \in (10, 20)$
- There exists $q \in (10, 20)$ with $C'(q) = 30$ euros
- $C'(15) = 30$ euros
- There exists $q \in (10, 20)$ with $C'(q) = 40$ euros

## Explanation

The function is differentiable for $q > 0$, so it is continuous on $[10, 20]$ and differentiable on $(10, 20)$, and the **mean value theorem** applies. The average slope is $\dfrac{800 - 500}{20 - 10} = 30$ euros per unit. Therefore there exists at least one quantity $q \in (10, 20)$ at which the marginal cost equals $30$ euros. The theorem does not say at which point, and the midpoint $15$ is only one of the possible points. The value $40$ is the average cost $\bar{C}(20)$.
