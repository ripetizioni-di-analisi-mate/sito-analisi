How many solutions does the equation $f'(x) = 0$ have in the interval $(1, 3)$, for $f(x) = (x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3)$?

- Two
- One
- Three
- None

## Explanation

The function $f$ is a polynomial, so it is continuous and differentiable on every interval, and $f(1) = f(2) = f(3) = 0$.

Since $f(1) = f(2)$, Rolle's theorem applies on $[1, 2]$ and guarantees a solution of $f'(x) = 0$ in $(1, 2)$; in the same way, since $f(2) = f(3)$, it guarantees another one in $(2, 3)$.

On the other hand $f'$ is a polynomial of degree two, so it has at most two zeros. Therefore the equation has exactly **two** solutions in $(1, 3)$.
