For $f(x) = e^{-2x}$, which formula gives the $n$-th derivative $f^{(n)}(x)$, for every integer $n \geq 1$?

- $(-2)^{n} e^{-2x}$
- $2^{n} e^{-2x}$
- $-2^{n} e^{-2x}$
- $(-2)^{n} e^{-2nx}$

## Explanation

By the chain rule the derivative of $e^{-2x}$ is $-2\,e^{-2x}$, that is, each differentiation multiplies the function by $-2$. Hence $f'(x) = -2\,e^{-2x}$, $f''(x) = 4\,e^{-2x}$ and $f'''(x) = -8\,e^{-2x}$. Therefore after $n$ differentiations we get $f^{(n)}(x) = (-2)^{n} e^{-2x}$. The formula $-2^{n} e^{-2x}$ always gives a minus sign, while the second derivative is positive.
