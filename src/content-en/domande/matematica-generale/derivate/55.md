Which of the following functions, defined for $x < 0$, has derivative $\dfrac{1}{x}$ at every $x < 0$?

- $-\ln(-x)$
- $\ln(-x)$
- $-\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$
- $\ln\left( x^{2} \right)$

## Explanation

By the chain rule the derivative of $\ln(-x)$ is $\dfrac{1}{-x} \cdot (-1) = \dfrac{1}{x}$. Hence $\ln(-x)$ has the required derivative. The derivative of $-\ln(-x)$ is $-\dfrac{1}{x}$, that of $\ln\left( x^{2} \right)$ is $\dfrac{2x}{x^{2}} = \dfrac{2}{x}$, and that of $-\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$ is $\dfrac{2}{x^{3}}$.
