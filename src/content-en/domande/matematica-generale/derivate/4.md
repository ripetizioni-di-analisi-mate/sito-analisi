- $f'(x) = -\dfrac{3}{(x + 2)^2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{2x + 1}{(x + 2)^2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{3}{x + 2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{3}{(x + 2)^2}$

## Explanation

The function is defined for $x \neq -2$. The numerator $x - 1$ and the denominator $x + 2$ both have derivative $1$, so by the **quotient rule**
$$
f'(x) = \frac{1 \cdot (x + 2) - (x - 1) \cdot 1}{(x + 2)^2} .
$$
The numerator equals $x + 2 - x + 1 = 3$, therefore $f'(x) = \dfrac{3}{(x + 2)^2}$ for every $x \neq -2$. In the formula the term containing the derivative of the numerator comes first; swapping the order of the two terms gives the result with the opposite sign.
