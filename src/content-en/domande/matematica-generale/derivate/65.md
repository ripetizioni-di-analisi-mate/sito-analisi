The production cost, in euros, is $C(q) = q^{2} + 4q + 100$, for $q > 0$. For which quantities is the average cost $\bar{C}(q) = \dfrac{C(q)}{q}$ decreasing?

- For $q > 10$
- For $0 < q < 10$
- For every $q > 0$
- For $0 < q < 100$

## Explanation

We have $\bar{C}'(q) = \dfrac{1}{q} \left( C'(q) - \bar{C}(q) \right)$, so the average cost decreases where the marginal cost is lower than the average cost. The marginal cost is $C'(q) = 2q + 4$ and the average cost is $\bar{C}(q) = q + 4 + \dfrac{100}{q}$. The condition $C'(q) < \bar{C}(q)$ becomes $q < \dfrac{100}{q}$, that is, $q^{2} < 100$. Since $q > 0$, we get $0 < q < 10$. Therefore the average cost decreases for $0 < q < 10$ and increases for $q > 10$. The interval $0 < q < 100$ comes from forgetting that the condition involves $q^{2}$.
