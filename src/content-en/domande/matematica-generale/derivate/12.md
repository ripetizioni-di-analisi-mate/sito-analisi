- $c = \sqrt{\dfrac{26}{3}}$
- $c = 2$
- $c = \dfrac{13}{3}$
- $c = \sqrt{\dfrac{13}{3}}$

## Explanation

The function $x^3$ is continuous on $[1, 3]$ and differentiable on $(1, 3)$, so the hypotheses of the **mean value theorem** are satisfied. The slope of the secant through the endpoints is
$$
\frac{f(3) - f(1)}{3 - 1} = \frac{27 - 1}{2} = 13 .
$$
Since $f'(c) = 3c^2$, the point $c$ solves the equation $3c^2 = 13$, that is, $c^2 = \dfrac{13}{3}$. Of the two solutions $\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{13}{3}}$, only the positive one lies in $(1, 3)$, so $c = \sqrt{\dfrac{13}{3}} \approx 2{,}08$.
