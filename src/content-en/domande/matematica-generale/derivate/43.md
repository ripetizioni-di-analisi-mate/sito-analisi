What is the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} \left( \frac{1}{\ln x} - \frac{1}{x - 1} \right)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $1$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 1$ the function has the form $\infty - \infty$. With a common denominator it becomes
$$
\frac{x - 1 - \ln x}{(x - 1) \ln x} ,
$$
which for $x \to 1$ has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

By l'Hôpital's rule one passes to $\dfrac{1 - \dfrac{1}{x}}{\ln x + \dfrac{x - 1}{x}} = \dfrac{x - 1}{x \ln x + x - 1}$, again of the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

Applying the rule once more gives $\dfrac{1}{\ln x + 1 + 1}$, which tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to 1$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
