What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{x^2}$?

- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $4$
- $1$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ one has $\cos 2x \to 1$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. By the chain rule the derivative of $1 - \cos 2x$ is $2 \sin 2x$, hence **de l'Hôpital's rule** leads to the quotient $\dfrac{2 \sin 2x}{2x}$, still in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Applying the rule a second time gives $\dfrac{4 \cos 2x}{2}$, which tends to $2$ as $x \to 0$. Since this limit exists, the required limit equals $2$.
