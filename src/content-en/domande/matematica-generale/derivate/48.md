At which point of the graph of $f(x) = \ln x$ is the tangent line parallel to the line $y = 2x - 5$?

- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, -\ln 2 \right)$
- $(2, \ln 2)$
- $\left( e^{2}, 2 \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{5}{2}, \ln \dfrac{5}{2} \right)$

## Explanation

Two lines are parallel when they have the same slope, so we look for the point where $f'(x) = 2$. Since $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$, the equation $\dfrac{1}{x} = 2$ gives $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. At this point $f\!\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right) = \ln \dfrac{1}{2} = -\ln 2$, so the point is $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, -\ln 2 \right)$. At the point $(2, \ln 2)$ the slope of the tangent is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and at the point $\left( e^{2}, 2 \right)$ it is $e^{-2}$.
