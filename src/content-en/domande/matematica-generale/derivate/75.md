For $f(x) = |x - 1|$ on the interval $[0, 3]$, which statement about the conclusion of the mean value theorem is true?

- The conclusion holds with $c = 1$
- No point $c \in (0, 3)$ has $f'(c)$ equal to the slope of the secant
- The conclusion holds with $c = \dfrac{3}{2}$
- The conclusion holds for every $c \in (1, 3)$

## Explanation

The slope of the secant through the endpoints is $\dfrac{f(3) - f(0)}{3 - 0} = \dfrac{2 - 1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{3}$. The derivative equals $-1$ for $x < 1$ and $1$ for $x > 1$, and it does not exist at $1$, because the graph has a corner there. Hence no point $c$ has $f'(c) = \dfrac{1}{3}$. The mean value theorem does not apply, because the hypothesis of differentiability on $(0, 3)$ is not satisfied at $1$.
