What is the derivative of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{x - 1}$, for $x \neq 1$?

- $f'(x) = 2x$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{2x - x^2}{(x - 1)^2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{3x^2 - 2x}{(x - 1)^2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 2x}{(x - 1)^2}$

## Explanation

The numerator $x^2$ has derivative $2x$ and the denominator $x - 1$ has derivative $1$, so by the **quotient rule**
$$
f'(x) = \frac{2x\,(x - 1) - x^2 \cdot 1}{(x - 1)^2} .
$$
The numerator equals $2x^2 - 2x - x^2 = x^2 - 2x$, hence $f'(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 2x}{(x - 1)^2}$ for $x \neq 1$. The quotient of the two derivatives, $2x$, is a function different from $f'$.
