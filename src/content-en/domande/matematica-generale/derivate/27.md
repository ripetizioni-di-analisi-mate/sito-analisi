For $f(x) = (x + 1)\sqrt{2x + 1}$, what is $f'(4)$?

- $\dfrac{14}{3}$
- $\dfrac{23}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{5}{3}$

## Explanation

By the chain rule the derivative of $\sqrt{2x + 1}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2x + 1}} \cdot 2 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2x + 1}}$. By the **product rule** we therefore have
$$
f'(x) = \sqrt{2x + 1} + \frac{x + 1}{\sqrt{2x + 1}} .
$$
At $x = 4$ one has $\sqrt{9} = 3$, hence $f'(4) = 3 + \dfrac{5}{3} = \dfrac{14}{3}$. Without the derivative $2$ of the inner function $2x + 1$ the second term becomes $\dfrac{5}{6}$, and we obtain the wrong value $\dfrac{23}{6}$.
