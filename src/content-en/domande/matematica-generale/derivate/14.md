- Decreasing on $(-\infty, 1]$, increasing on $[1, +\infty)$
- Decreasing on $(-\infty, -1]$, increasing on $[-1, +\infty)$
- Increasing on $(-\infty, 1]$, decreasing on $[1, +\infty)$
- Increasing on $(-\infty, 0]$, decreasing on $[0, +\infty)$

## Explanation

By the chain rule $(e^{-x})' = -e^{-x}$, so by the **product rule**
$$
f'(x) = e^{-x} - x\,e^{-x} = (1 - x)\,e^{-x} .
$$
Since $e^{-x} > 0$ for every $x$, the sign of $f'(x)$ is the sign of $1 - x$, which is positive for $x < 1$ and negative for $x > 1$. Therefore, by the sign of the derivative, $f$ is **increasing** on $(-\infty, 1]$ and **decreasing** on $[1, +\infty)$.
