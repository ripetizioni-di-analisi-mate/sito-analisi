What does the ratio $\dfrac{\ln\left( x^{2} + 1 \right)}{\ln x}$ tend to as $x \to +\infty$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $0$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The limit has the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$, so **l'Hôpital's rule** applies. The quotient of the derivatives is
$$
\frac{\dfrac{2x}{x^{2} + 1}}{\dfrac{1}{x}} = \frac{2x^{2}}{x^{2} + 1} ,
$$
which tends to $2$ as $x \to +\infty$. Therefore the limit equals $2$. The same result follows from $\ln\left( x^{2} + 1 \right) = 2 \ln x + \ln\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{x^{2}} \right)$.
