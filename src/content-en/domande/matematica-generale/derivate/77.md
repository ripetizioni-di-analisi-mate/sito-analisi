The function $g(x) = e^{x} - 1 - x$ is studied to compare $e^{x}$ with $1 + x$. Which statement is true?

- $e^{x} \geq 1 + x$ for every real $x$
- The inequality $e^{x} \geq 1 + x$ holds only for $x \geq 0$
- $e^{x} \leq 1 + x$ for every $x \leq 0$
- $e^{x} \leq 1 + x$ for every real $x$

## Explanation

We have $g'(x) = e^{x} - 1$, negative for $x < 0$ and positive for $x > 0$. By the monotonicity test $g$ is decreasing on $(-\infty, 0]$ and increasing on $[0, +\infty)$. Hence $g$ has its minimum at $0$, where $g(0) = 0$. Therefore $g(x) \geq 0$ for every real $x$, that is, $e^{x} \geq 1 + x$, with equality only at $x = 0$. For example, for $x = -2$ we have $e^{-2} \approx 0.14 > 1 + (-2) = -1$, so the inequality also holds for negative $x$.
