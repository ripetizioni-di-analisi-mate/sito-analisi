## Explanation

We have $f(1) = 1 + 3 = 4$ and $f(1 + h) = (1 + h)^2 + 3\,(1 + h) = 4 + 5h + h^2$. The difference quotient between $1$ and $1 + h$, with $h \neq 0$, is therefore
$$
\frac{f(1 + h) - f(1)}{h} = \frac{5h + h^2}{h} = 5 + h .
$$
As $h \to 0$ the difference quotient tends to $5$, so $f$ is **differentiable** at $1$ and $f'(1) = 5$.
