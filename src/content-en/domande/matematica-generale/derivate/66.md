The inverse demand for a good is a differentiable function $p(q)$ with $p'(q) < 0$ for every $q > 0$. The revenue is $R(q) = q\,p(q)$. Which relation holds for every $q > 0$?

- $R'(q) < p(q)$
- $R'(q) = p(q)$
- $R'(q) > p(q)$
- $R'(q) = p'(q)$

## Explanation

By the **product rule** $R'(q) = p(q) + q\,p'(q)$. Since $q > 0$ and $p'(q) < 0$, the term $q\,p'(q)$ is negative. Hence $R'(q) < p(q)$, that is, the marginal revenue is lower than the price. To sell one more unit the price of every unit sold must be lowered, and the term $q\,p'(q)$ measures this loss. The equality $R'(q) = p(q)$ holds when the price is fixed, that is, for $p'(q) = 0$.
