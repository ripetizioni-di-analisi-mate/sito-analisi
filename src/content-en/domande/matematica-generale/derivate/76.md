How many real solutions does the equation $x^{3} + 3x + 1 = 0$ have?

- Three
- None
- Two
- One

## Explanation

Let $f(x) = x^{3} + 3x + 1$. One has $f'(x) = 3x^{2} + 3 > 0$ for every $x$, so $f$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$ and vanishes at most once. Moreover $f(-1) = -3 < 0$ and $f(0) = 1 > 0$. Since $f$ is continuous, by the intermediate zero theorem it vanishes at a point of $(-1, 0)$. Therefore the equation has **exactly one** real solution.
