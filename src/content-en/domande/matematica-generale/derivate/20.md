What is the derivative of $f(x) = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{x}} - \dfrac{1}{x^2}$, for $x > 0$?

- $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} + \dfrac{2}{x^3}$
- $f'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x}} - \dfrac{2}{x^3}$
- $f'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x}} + \dfrac{2}{x^3}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x}} + \dfrac{2}{x^3}$

## Explanation

The function is written as a sum of powers, $f(x) = 2x^{-\frac{1}{2}} - x^{-2}$. By the power rule $\left( x^{-\frac{1}{2}} \right)' = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,x^{-\frac{3}{2}}$ and $\left( x^{-2} \right)' = -2x^{-3}$, so
$$
f'(x) = -x^{-\frac{3}{2}} + 2x^{-3} .
$$
Since $x^{\frac{3}{2}} = x\sqrt{x}$, we obtain $f'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x}} + \dfrac{2}{x^3}$.
