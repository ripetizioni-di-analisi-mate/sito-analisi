At the current price the demand $q(p)$ for a good is positive and has elasticity $\varepsilon = -2.5$. The price rises by $2\%$. By approximately how much does the revenue $R(p) = p\,q(p)$ change?

- $-5\%$
- $+3\%$
- $-3\%$
- $-1.5\%$

## Explanation

By the theorem on elasticity and revenue $R'(p) = q(p)\,\big( 1 + \varepsilon \big)$. By the linear approximation $\Delta R \approx R'(p)\,\Delta p$, so, dividing by $R = p\,q(p)$,
$$
\frac{\Delta R}{R} \approx \frac{q(p)\,(1 + \varepsilon)\,\Delta p}{p\,q(p)} = (1 + \varepsilon)\,\frac{\Delta p}{p} .
$$
With $\varepsilon = -2.5$ and $\dfrac{\Delta p}{p} = 2\%$ we get $\dfrac{\Delta R}{R} \approx -1.5 \cdot 2\% = -3\%$. Therefore the revenue **decreases by about $3\%$**, because demand is elastic. The value $-5\%$ is the percentage change in the quantity demanded, and the value $-1.5\%$ comes from using the factor $1 + \varepsilon$ without multiplying it by the percentage change in the price.
