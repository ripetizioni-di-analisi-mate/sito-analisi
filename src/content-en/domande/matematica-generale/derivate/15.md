For $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$, what is the difference quotient between $2$ and $2 + h$, with $h \neq 0$ and $h > -2$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{2\,(2 + h)}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2\,(2 + h)}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{(2 + h)^2}$

## Explanation

The change in the function is
$$
f(2 + h) - f(2) = \frac{1}{2 + h} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2 - (2 + h)}{2\,(2 + h)} = -\frac{h}{2\,(2 + h)} .
$$
Dividing by the change $h$ in the variable gives the **difference quotient** $-\dfrac{1}{2\,(2 + h)}$, which depends on $h$. As $h \to 0$ the difference quotient tends to $-\dfrac{1}{4}$, which is the derivative $f'(2)$.
