- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{9}{2}$
- $9$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is of the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. By the chain rule the derivative of $e^{3x}$ is $3e^{3x}$, therefore **L'Hôpital's rule** leads to the quotient $\dfrac{3e^{3x} - 3}{2x}$, again of the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Applying the rule a second time gives $\dfrac{9e^{3x}}{2}$, which as $x \to 0$ tends to $\dfrac{9}{2}$. Since this limit exists, the rule applies to both steps, and the required limit equals $\dfrac{9}{2}$.
