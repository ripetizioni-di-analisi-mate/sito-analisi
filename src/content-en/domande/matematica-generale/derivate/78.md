The function $f(x) = x + \dfrac{1}{x}$ is defined for $x \neq 0$. Where is it increasing and where is it decreasing?

- Increasing on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[1, +\infty)$, decreasing on $[-1, 1]$
- Increasing on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[1, +\infty)$, decreasing on $[-1, 0)$ and on $(0, 1]$
- Decreasing on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[1, +\infty)$, increasing on $[-1, 0)$ and on $(0, 1]$
- Increasing on $(-\infty, 0)$ and on $(0, +\infty)$

## Explanation

One has $f'(x) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{x^{2}} = \dfrac{x^{2} - 1}{x^{2}}$, which is positive for $|x| > 1$ and negative for $0 < |x| < 1$. The monotonicity test applies on intervals, and $0$ does not belong to the domain. Hence $f$ is decreasing on $[-1, 0)$ and on $(0, 1]$, separately. The function is not decreasing on $[-1, 1]$, because it is not defined at $0$.
