What is the derivative of $f(x) = x^2 \cos x$?

- $f'(x) = 2x \cos x + x^2 \sin x$
- $f'(x) = -2x \sin x$
- $f'(x) = 2x \cos x - x^2 \sin x$
- $f'(x) = x^2 \cos x - 2x \sin x$

## Explanation

One has $(x^2)' = 2x$ and $(\cos x)' = -\sin x$, so by the **product rule**
$$
f'(x) = 2x \cos x + x^2 \cdot (-\sin x) = 2x \cos x - x^2 \sin x .
$$
The product of the two derivatives, $-2x \sin x$, is a function different from $f'$. The product rule adds the two terms in which one factor at a time is differentiated.
