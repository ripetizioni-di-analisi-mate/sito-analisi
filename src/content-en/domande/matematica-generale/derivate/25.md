The function $f$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ and even, that is, $f(-x) = f(x)$ for every $x$. It is known that $f'(2) = 5$. What is $f'(-2)$?

- $5$
- $0$
- $-5$
- It cannot be determined from these data

## Explanation

Differentiate both sides of the identity $f(-x) = f(x)$. By the chain rule the derivative of $f(-x)$ is $f'(-x) \cdot (-1)$, hence
$$
-f'(-x) = f'(x) \qquad \text{for every } x .
$$
For $x = 2$ one gets $f'(-2) = -f'(2) = -5$. Therefore the derivative of a differentiable even function is an odd function.
