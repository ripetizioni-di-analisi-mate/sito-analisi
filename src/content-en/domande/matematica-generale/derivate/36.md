The demand for a good is $q(p) = 48 - p^2$, for $0 < p < 4\sqrt{3}$. At what price is the revenue $R(p) = p\,q(p)$ stationary?

- $p = 2\sqrt{6}$
- $p = 4\sqrt{3}$
- $p = 4$
- $p = 16$

## Explanation

Revenue is $R(p) = 48p - p^3$, hence $R'(p) = 48 - 3p^2$.

The derivative vanishes for $p^2 = 16$, and in the interval $0 < p < 4\sqrt{3}$ this gives $p = 4$.

The same result follows from the elasticity $\varepsilon(p) = \dfrac{-2p^2}{48 - p^2}$, which at $p = 4$ equals $\dfrac{-32}{32} = -1$, and revenue is stationary precisely where elasticity is unitary. The value $2\sqrt{6}$ is obtained by setting $-2p^2 = -48$, that is, by forgetting the term $-p^2$ in the denominator.
