The demand $q(p)$ for a good is differentiable and positive, and at the current price it has elasticity $\varepsilon = -0{,}6$. What happens to the revenue $R(p) = p\,q(p)$ after a small price increase?

- Revenue decreases
- Revenue stays constant
- Revenue increases
- Revenue increases by $60\%$

## Explanation

By the theorem on elasticity and revenue $R'(p) = q(p)\,\big( 1 + \varepsilon(p) \big)$. With $\varepsilon = -0{,}6$ one has $R'(p) = 0{,}4\,q(p)$, which is positive because $q(p) > 0$. So the derivative of revenue is positive at the current price, and a small price increase makes revenue **increase**. Since $|\varepsilon| < 1$ demand is inelastic, that is, the percentage decrease in quantity is smaller than the percentage increase in price.
