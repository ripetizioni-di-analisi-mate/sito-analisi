What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{x} + 1}{\cos x + x}$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator tends to $2$ and the denominator tends to $1$, so the limit is not in an indeterminate form. The numerator and the denominator are continuous at $0$ and the denominator equals $1$ there, hence the limit equals $\dfrac{e^{0} + 1}{\cos 0 + 0} = 2$. **De l'Hôpital's rule** applies only to the forms $\dfrac{0}{0}$ and $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. The quotient of the derivatives $\dfrac{e^{x}}{1 - \sin x}$ tends to $1$, which is a value different from the limit.
