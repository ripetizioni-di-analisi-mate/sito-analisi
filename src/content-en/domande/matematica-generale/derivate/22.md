For the function $f(x) = \ln\left( 1 + e^{2x} \right)$, what is $f'(0)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $2$
- $\ln 2$

## Explanation

The function is a composite: the logarithm is applied to $1 + e^{2x}$, which contains the exponential of the inner function $2x$. By the **chain rule** the derivative of the logarithm evaluated at $1 + e^{2x}$, that is $\dfrac{1}{1 + e^{2x}}$, is multiplied by the derivative of $1 + e^{2x}$, which is $2e^{2x}$. Hence
$$
f'(x) = \frac{2e^{2x}}{1 + e^{2x}} ,
$$
and at $x = 0$ one obtains $f'(0) = \dfrac{2}{1 + 1} = 1$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ comes from forgetting the derivative $2$ of the inner function $2x$, the value $2$ from forgetting the denominator $1 + e^{2x}$, and $\ln 2$ is the value $f(0)$ of the function.
