The cost of a firm is a differentiable function $C(q)$ of the quantity produced, with marginal cost $C'(100) = 22$ euros. Output depends on time $t$, measured in months, and at the instant when $q(t) = 100$ it grows at the rate $q'(t) = 5$ units per month. At what rate does the cost grow at that instant?

- $22$ euros per month
- $27$ euros per month
- $110$ euros per month
- $4.4$ euros per month

## Explanation

The cost as a function of time is the composite function $C\big( q(t) \big)$. By the **chain rule** its derivative is $C'\big( q(t) \big) \cdot q'(t)$. At the instant considered $q(t) = 100$, so the derivative equals $C'(100) \cdot 5 = 22 \cdot 5 = 110$ euros per month. The value $22$ is the marginal cost, which measures the growth of the cost with respect to quantity. The value $27$ comes from adding the two rates, and the value $4.4$ from dividing the marginal cost by $5$.
