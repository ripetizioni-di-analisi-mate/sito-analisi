The function $f(x) = x^{2}$ on the interval $[1, 3]$ has its minimum at $x = 1$, and $f'(1) = 2$. Which statement is true?

- Fermat's theorem does not apply at $1$, because $1$ is not interior to the interval
- The point $1$ is not a minimum point, because $f'(1) \neq 0$
- Fermat's theorem applies at $1$ and leads to a contradiction
- The function is not differentiable at $1$

## Explanation

One has $f(x) \geq 1 = f(1)$ for every $x \in [1, 3]$, so $1$ is indeed the minimum point. **Fermat's theorem** requires the extremum point to be interior to the interval. The point $1$ is an endpoint of the interval, so the hypothesis is not satisfied and the theorem does not apply. For this reason maxima and minima on a closed interval are sought also at the endpoints of the interval, besides the stationary points.
