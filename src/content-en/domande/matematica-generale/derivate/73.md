The demand for a good is $q(p) = \dfrac{1000}{p + 10}$, for $p > 0$. Which statement is true?

- Demand is inelastic at every price, so a price increase raises the revenue
- Demand is elastic at every price, so a price increase lowers the revenue
- Demand is inelastic for $p < 10$ and elastic for $p > 10$
- Demand is unit elastic at the price $p = 10$

## Explanation

One has $q'(p) = -\dfrac{1000}{(p + 10)^{2}}$, so
$$
\varepsilon(p) = \frac{p\,(p + 10)}{1000} \cdot \left( -\frac{1000}{(p + 10)^{2}} \right) = -\frac{p}{p + 10} .
$$
Since $0 < p < p + 10$, we have $-1 < \varepsilon(p) < 0$ at every price, that is, demand is **inelastic** at every price. By the theorem on elasticity and revenue $R'(p) = q(p)\,\big( 1 + \varepsilon(p) \big) > 0$, so a price increase raises the revenue. At the price $10$ the elasticity equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
