What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\ln x}{x^2 - 1}$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 1$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The derivatives of the numerator and of the denominator are $\dfrac{1}{x}$ and $2x$, and their quotient $\dfrac{1}{2x^2}$ tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to 1$. Since this limit exists, by **de l'Hôpital's rule** the required limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
