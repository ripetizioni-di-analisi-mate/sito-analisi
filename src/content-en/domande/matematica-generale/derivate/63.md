For $f(x) = \ln\left( 1 + x^{2} \right)$, for which $x$ is $f''(x) > 0$?

- For $x > 0$
- For $x < -1$ and for $x > 1$
- For $-1 < x < 1$
- For every real $x$

## Explanation

By the chain rule $f'(x) = \dfrac{2x}{1 + x^{2}}$. By the quotient rule
$$
f''(x) = \frac{2\,(1 + x^{2}) - 2x \cdot 2x}{(1 + x^{2})^{2}} = \frac{2\,(1 - x^{2})}{(1 + x^{2})^{2}} .
$$
The denominator is positive, so $f''(x) > 0$ if and only if $1 - x^{2} > 0$, that is, for $-1 < x < 1$. The set $x > 0$ is the one where $f'(x) > 0$.
