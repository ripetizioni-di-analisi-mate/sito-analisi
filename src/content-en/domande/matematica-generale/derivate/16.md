For which values of $x$ do the functions $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = x^3$ have the same derivative, that is, the tangent lines to the two graphs at the points with abscissa $x$ have the same slope?

- Only for $x = \dfrac{2}{3}$
- For $x = 0$ and for $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$
- For $x = 0$ and for $x = \dfrac{2}{3}$
- For $x = 0$ and for $x = 1$

## Explanation

The slope of the tangent to the graph of $f$ at the point with abscissa $x$ is $f'(x) = 2x$; the slope of the tangent to the graph of $g$ is $g'(x) = 3x^2$.

The two slopes are equal if $3x^2 = 2x$, that is, $x\,(3x - 2) = 0$.

Therefore the tangents have the same slope for $x = 0$ and for $x = \dfrac{2}{3}$. For $x = 1$ the two graphs pass through the same point $(1, 1)$, but the slopes are $2$ and $3$, so they are different.
