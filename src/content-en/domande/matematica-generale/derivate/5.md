- $f'(x) = \dfrac{e^{x}}{x}$
- $f'(x) = e^{x} \left( \ln x + \dfrac{1}{x} \right)$
- $f'(x) = e^{x} \left( \ln x - \dfrac{1}{x} \right)$
- $f'(x) = e^{x} \ln x + \dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

The function is defined for $x > 0$. Since $(e^{x})' = e^{x}$ and $(\ln x)' = \dfrac{1}{x}$, by the **product rule** one has
$$
f'(x) = e^{x} \ln x + e^{x} \cdot \frac{1}{x} .
$$
Factoring out $e^{x}$ gives $f'(x) = e^{x} \left( \ln x + \dfrac{1}{x} \right)$, for $x > 0$.
