The production cost, in euros, is $C(q) = 2000 + 15q + 0.05\,q^2$. What is the marginal cost $C'(50)$, that is, the derivative of the cost at $q = 50$?

- $17.5$ euros
- $57.5$ euros
- $20.05$ euros
- $20$ euros

## Explanation

The marginal cost is the derivative of the cost. Since the derivative of $0.05\,q^2$ is $0.1\,q$, one has $C'(q) = 15 + 0.1\,q$, so $C'(50) = 15 + 5 = 20$ euros. This value approximates the actual cost of the fifty-first unit, which is $C(51) - C(50) = 2895.05 - 2875 = 20.05$ euros. The average cost $\bar{C}(50) = \dfrac{2875}{50} = 57.5$ euros, on the other hand, is the cost per unit of the whole output, and it is a different quantity from the marginal cost.
