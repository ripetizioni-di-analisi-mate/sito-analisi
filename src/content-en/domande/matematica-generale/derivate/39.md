The function $f$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$, with $f'(x) = 0$ for every $x \neq 0$ and $f(1) = 2$. What can be said about $f(-1)$?

- It cannot be determined from these data
- $f(-1) = 2$
- $f(-1) = -2$
- $f(-1) = 0$

## Explanation

The theorem on the sign of the derivative applies on an interval. The set $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ is the union of the two intervals $(-\infty, 0)$ and $(0, +\infty)$, so $f$ is constant on each of them, with constants that may be different.

On $(0, +\infty)$ the constant equals $f(1) = 2$, while no datum fixes the constant on $(-\infty, 0)$. For example the function equal to $2$ for $x > 0$ and to $7$ for $x < 0$ satisfies all the conditions and has $f(-1) = 7$.

Therefore the value $f(-1)$ cannot be determined.
