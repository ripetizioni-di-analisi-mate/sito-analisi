The radius of a circle goes from $10$ cm to $10.1$ cm. By about how much does the area $A(r) = \pi r^{2}$ increase, according to the linear approximation?

- $20\pi$ cm²
- $\pi$ cm²
- $0.01\,\pi$ cm²
- $2\pi$ cm²

## Explanation

By the linear approximation the change in the area is about the **differential** $A'(10) \cdot \Delta r$. One has $A'(r) = 2\pi r$, so $A'(10) = 20\pi$, and $\Delta r = 0.1$. Therefore the area increases by about $20\pi \cdot 0.1 = 2\pi$ cm². The exact value is $\pi\,(10.1^{2} - 10^{2}) = 2.01\,\pi$ cm². The value $20\pi$ is the derivative $A'(10)$, without the factor $\Delta r$.
