For $f(x) = \sin x + \cos x$, what is $f'\!\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$?

- $\sqrt{2}$
- $1$
- $-\sqrt{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Since $(\sin x)' = \cos x$ and $(\cos x)' = -\sin x$, the derivative is $f'(x) = \cos x - \sin x$. At $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ the sine and the cosine both equal $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so $f'\!\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = 0$. The value $\sqrt{2}$ is obtained by taking $\sin x$ as the derivative of $\cos x$, without the minus sign.
