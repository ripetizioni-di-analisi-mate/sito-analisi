For $x > 0$ consider the base-$2$ logarithm, $f(x) = \log_2 x$. Which expression does its derivative have?

- $f'(x) = \dfrac{\ln 2}{x}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x \ln 2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{2}{x}$

## Explanation

By the change of base formula $\log_2 x = \dfrac{\ln x}{\ln 2}$, so $f$ is the product of the constant $\dfrac{1}{\ln 2}$ and $\ln x$. Since the derivative of $\ln x$ is $\dfrac{1}{x}$, we get $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x \ln 2}$. The answer $\dfrac{1}{x}$ holds only for the natural logarithm.
