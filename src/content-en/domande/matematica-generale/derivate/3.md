- $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 2x + 6$
- $f'(x) = 4x$
- $f'(x) = 6x^2 + 2x + 6$
- $f'(x) = -2x^2 + 2x + 6$

## Explanation

One has $(2x - 1)' = 2$ and $(x^2 + 3)' = 2x$, so by the **product rule**
$$
f'(x) = 2\,(x^2 + 3) + (2x - 1) \cdot 2x .
$$
Expanding the products gives $2x^2 + 6 + 4x^2 - 2x$, therefore $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 2x + 6$. The same result is obtained by first expanding the product, $f(x) = 2x^3 - x^2 + 6x - 3$, and differentiating term by term.
