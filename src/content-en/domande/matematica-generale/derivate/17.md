Which of the following functions is continuous at $x_0 = 0$ but not differentiable at $x_0 = 0$?

- $x\,|x|$
- $|x|\,(x + 2)$
- $|x + 1|$
- $x^2 + |x - 1|$

## Explanation

All four functions are continuous at $0$. For $g(x) = |x|\,(x + 2)$ one has $g(0) = 0$, and the difference quotient at $0$ is $\dfrac{|h|\,(h + 2)}{h}$, which equals $h + 2$ for $h > 0$ and $-(h + 2)$ for $h < 0$. The right-hand limit is therefore $2$ and the left-hand limit is $-2$, so $g$ is **not differentiable** at $0$, where the graph has a corner. For $x\,|x|$ the difference quotient is $|h|$, which tends to $0$, so this function is differentiable at $0$. Near $0$ the functions $|x + 1|$ and $x^2 + |x - 1|$ coincide with the polynomials $x + 1$ and $x^2 - x + 1$, hence they are differentiable at $0$.
