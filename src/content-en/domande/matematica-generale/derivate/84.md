The limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} x \ln x$ has the form $0 \cdot (-\infty)$. What is its value?

- $-\infty$
- $1$
- $-1$
- $0$

## Explanation

The limit has the form $0 \cdot (-\infty)$, which is turned into a quotient by writing $x \ln x = \dfrac{\ln x}{\dfrac{1}{x}}$. The quotient has the form $\dfrac{-\infty}{+\infty}$, so **l'Hôpital's rule** applies:
$$
\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \frac{\ln x}{\dfrac{1}{x}} = \lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \frac{\dfrac{1}{x}}{-\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}} = \lim_{x \to 0^{+}} (-x) = 0 .
$$
Therefore the limit equals $0$.
