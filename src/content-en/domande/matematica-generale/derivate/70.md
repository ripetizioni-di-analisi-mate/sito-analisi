The demand $q(p) = 100\,p^{-2} e^{-0.1\,p}$, for $p > 0$, is the product of a power and an exponential. What value does its elasticity $\varepsilon(p) = \dfrac{p}{q(p)}\,q'(p)$ take when $p = 10$?

- $-3$
- $-2$
- $-1$
- $3$

## Explanation

By the product rule
$$
q'(p) = 100 \left( -2\,p^{-3} - 0.1\,p^{-2} \right) e^{-0.1\,p} = q(p) \left( -\frac{2}{p} - 0.1 \right) .
$$
Hence the elasticity is $\varepsilon(p) = \dfrac{p}{q(p)}\,q'(p) = -2 - 0.1\,p$. For $p = 10$ we get $\varepsilon(10) = -2 - 1 = -3$. The value $-2$ is the elasticity of the factor $p^{-2}$ alone, and the value $-1$ that of the exponential factor alone.
