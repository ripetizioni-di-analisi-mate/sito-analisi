Which statement about the function $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$ at the point $x_0 = 0$ is true?

- It is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 0$
- It is not differentiable at $0$ and the graph has a corner at $(0, 0)$
- It is not continuous at $0$
- It is not differentiable at $0$ and the graph has a vertical tangent at $(0, 0)$

## Explanation

The function is continuous at $0$, because $\sqrt[3]{h} \to 0$ as $h \to 0$. The difference quotient at $0$ is
$$
\frac{\sqrt[3]{h} - 0}{h} = \frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{h^{2}}} ,
$$
which tends to $+\infty$ both from the right and from the left. Hence the limit is not finite and $f$ is **not differentiable** at $0$. Since the slopes of the secants tend to $+\infty$ on both sides, the secants tend to the vertical line $x = 0$, which is the tangent at $(0, 0)$. The point is not a corner, because the right-hand and left-hand limits of the difference quotient are equal.
