The inverse demand for a good is $p = 60 - 0.3\,q$. What is the marginal revenue?

- $R'(q) = 60 - 0.3\,q$
- $R'(q) = 60 - 0.6\,q$
- $R'(q) = 60q - 0.3\,q^2$
- $R'(q) = -0.3$

## Explanation

Revenue is price times quantity sold, so $R(q) = p\,q = 60q - 0.3\,q^2$. The marginal revenue is the derivative of revenue, therefore $R'(q) = 60 - 0.6\,q$. The price $60 - 0.3\,q$ coincides with the average revenue $\dfrac{R(q)}{q}$, and for $q > 0$ the marginal revenue is less than the price, because to sell one more unit the price of all units must be lowered. The marginal revenue is zero at $q = 100$.
