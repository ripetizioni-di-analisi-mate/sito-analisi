What is the derivative of $f(x) = 3^{x} + x^{3}$?

- $f'(x) = 3^{x} \ln 3 + 3x^{2}$
- $f'(x) = x\,3^{x - 1} + 3x^{2}$
- $f'(x) = 3^{x} + 3x^{2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{3^{x}}{\ln 3} + 3x^{2}$

## Explanation

The derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives. The table of derivatives of elementary functions gives $(a^{x})' = a^{x} \ln a$, and with $a = 3$ we get $(3^{x})' = 3^{x} \ln 3$. By the power rule $(x^{3})' = 3x^{2}$, so $f'(x) = 3^{x} \ln 3 + 3x^{2}$. The power rule concerns functions with the variable in the base; in $3^{x}$ the variable is in the exponent, hence the exponential formula is used.
