The function $f$ equals $x$ for $x < 0$ and $x + 1$ for $x \geq 0$. Which statement about $f$ at the point $x_0 = 0$ is true?

- It is not differentiable at $0$, because it is not continuous at $0$
- It is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 1$
- It is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 0$
- It is continuous at $0$, but not differentiable at $0$

## Explanation

The limit of $f$ as $x \to 0^-$ is $0$, while $f(0) = 1$, so $f$ is **not continuous** at $0$. A function differentiable at a point is continuous at that point. Therefore $f$ is not differentiable at $0$. Both pieces have slope $1$; however, for $h < 0$ the difference quotient equals $\dfrac{h - 1}{h}$, which tends to $+\infty$ as $h \to 0^-$.
