A household's consumption, in euros, depends on income $Y > 0$ through the function $C(Y) = 100 + 20\sqrt{Y}$. What is the marginal propensity to consume $C'(Y)$ at $Y = 400$?

- $1$
- $0.5$
- $0.025$
- $1.25$

## Explanation

Since the derivative of $\sqrt{Y}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{Y}}$, one has
$$
C'(Y) = 20 \cdot \frac{1}{2\sqrt{Y}} = \frac{10}{\sqrt{Y}} .
$$
For $Y = 400$ one gets $C'(400) = \dfrac{10}{20} = 0.5$. Therefore one extra euro of income raises consumption by about $50$ cents. The value $1.25$ is the average propensity $\dfrac{C(400)}{400} = \dfrac{500}{400}$, which is a different quantity.
