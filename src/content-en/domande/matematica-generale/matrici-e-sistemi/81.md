For which value of the real parameter $k$ is the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x - y + z = 2 \\[4pt]
2x + y - z = 1 \\[4pt]
x + 2y - 2z = k
\end{cases}
$$
consistent, and how many solutions does it have in that case?

- $k = 1$, with $\infty^{1}$ solutions
- $k = -1$, with $\infty^{1}$ solutions
- $k = -1$, with exactly one solution
- $k = -1$, with $\infty^{2}$ solutions

## Explanation

In the coefficient matrix the third row is the second minus the first, because $(2, 1, -1) - (1, -1, 1) = (1, 2, -2)$. The minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 3$ is nonzero, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. Subtracting the second equation from the third and adding the first, the left side vanishes and what remains is $0 = k - 1 + 2 = k + 1$. Therefore for $k \neq -1$ we have $\operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 3$ and the system is inconsistent, while for $k = -1$ we have $\operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 2$. By the **Rouché–Capelli theorem**, for $k = -1$ the system is consistent, and it has $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^{1}$ solutions.
