- $k \neq 0$ and $k \neq 2$
- $k \neq -2$ and $k \neq 0$
- $k \neq 2$
- $k = 0$ or $k = 2$

## Explanation

A square matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero. With **Sarrus's rule**
$$
\det A = (k^2 + 2 + 2) - (k + k + 4) = k^2 - 2k = k\,(k - 2) .
$$
The determinant vanishes at $k = 0$ and $k = 2$, so $A$ is **invertible** for $k \neq 0$ and $k \neq 2$. The factor $k$ gives an excluded value too, since dividing the equation $k^2 - 2k = 0$ by $k$ would lose the root $k = 0$.
