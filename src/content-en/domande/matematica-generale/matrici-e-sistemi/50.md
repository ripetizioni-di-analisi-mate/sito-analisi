The upper triangular matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
are multiplied in this order. Which matrix is the product $AB$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 9 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 10 \\ 0 & 3 & 11 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 10 \\ 0 & 3 & 13 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The product of two upper triangular matrices is upper triangular, and its diagonal contains the products of the corresponding diagonal entries, that is, $2$, $3$ and $-2$. It remains to compute the entries above the diagonal, row of $A$ times column of $B$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(AB)_{12} &= 1 \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot 1 + (-1) \cdot 0 = 2, \\[4pt]
(AB)_{13} &= 1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 4 + (-1) \cdot (-1) = 10, \\[4pt]
(AB)_{23} &= 0 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 4 + 1 \cdot (-1) = 11 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 10 \\ 0 & 3 & 11 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 9 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ is the product $BA$, which differs from $AB$.
