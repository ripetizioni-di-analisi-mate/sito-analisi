An economy with two sectors has technical coefficient matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0.2 & 0.4 \\ 0.3 & 0.1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The final demand of the second sector increases by $1$ unit, while that of the first sector stays the same. By how many units must the output of the first sector increase?

- $0.4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

In the **Leontief model** the output that meets the final demand $d$ is $x = (I_2 - A)^{-1} d$. If $d$ becomes $d + \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$, by the distributive property the output increases by $(I_2 - A)^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$, that is, by the second column of $(I_2 - A)^{-1}$. We have
$$
I_2 - A = \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & -0.4 \\ -0.3 & 0.9 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(I_2 - A) = 0.72 - 0.12 = 0.6 ,
$$
so, by the formula for the inverse of order $2$,
$$
(I_2 - A)^{-1} = \frac{1}{0.6} \begin{pmatrix} 0.9 & 0.4 \\ 0.3 & 0.8 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{3}{2} & \frac{2}{3} \\ \frac{1}{2} & \frac{4}{3} \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Therefore the output of the first sector increases by $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a unit. The coefficient $a_{12} = 0.4$ counts only the direct requirement of the second sector, while $\dfrac{2}{3}$ also includes the inputs needed to produce those inputs.
