A linear system of $3$ equations in $5$ unknowns has $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 3$. How many solutions does it have?

- $\infty^{2}$ solutions
- $\infty^{3}$ solutions
- Exactly one solution
- No solution

## Explanation

Since $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b)$, the system is consistent by the **Rouché–Capelli theorem**. The common value of the ranks is $r = 3$ and there are $n = 5$ unknowns, so $r < n$ and the system has infinitely many solutions. The solutions depend on $n - r = 5 - 3 = 2$ free parameters, so there are $\infty^{2}$ of them. The number $3$ in the exponent is the rank, which counts the unknowns determined by the equations and not the free ones.
