Let $A$ be a square matrix of order $3$ with $\det A = 6$. The matrix $B$ is obtained from $A$ by swapping the first row with the third and multiplying the second row by $3$. What is $\det B$?

- $18$
- $-54$
- $-18$
- $-6$

## Explanation

Swapping two rows changes the sign of the determinant, so after swapping the first and the third row the determinant equals $-6$. Multiplying a single row by $3$ multiplies the determinant by $3$, therefore
$$
\det B = 3 \cdot (-6) = -18 .
$$
The factor $3^{3} = 27$ appears only when all three rows are multiplied by $3$, that is, for the matrix $3A$.
