Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix},
$$
which matrix $X$ satisfies the matrix equation $AX = B$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -3 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 3 \\ 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The determinant of $A$ is $2 - 1 = 1 \neq 0$, so $A$ is invertible and, by the formula for the inverse of order $2$, $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. Multiplying the equation $AX = B$ on the left by $A^{-1}$ gives $X = A^{-1} B$, therefore
$$
X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -3 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The product is not commutative, so $B A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ is a different matrix, which solves the equation $XA = B$.
