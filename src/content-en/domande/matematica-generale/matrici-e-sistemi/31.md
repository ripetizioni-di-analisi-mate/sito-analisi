Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
$$
which matrix is the inverse $(AB)^{-1}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Both matrices have determinant $1$, and the formula for the inverse of order $2$ gives
$$
A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
By the **properties of the inverse** $(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1} A^{-1}$, with the order of the factors reversed, so
$$
(AB)^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The same result is obtained by inverting directly $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, which has determinant $1$. The product $A^{-1} B^{-1}$ equals instead $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, which is the inverse of $BA$.
