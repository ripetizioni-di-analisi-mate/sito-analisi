Which of the following systems is *not* equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 2 \\[4pt]
x - y = 0
\end{cases}
$$

- $\begin{cases} x + y = 2 \\ 2x = 2 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} 2x + 2y = 4 \\ x - y = 0 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} x + y = 2 \\ 2x + 2y = 4 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} x - y = 0 \\ x + y = 2 \end{cases}$

## Explanation

The given system has the unique solution $(1, 1)$. Three of the four listed systems are obtained from the given system through elementary operations, so they are equivalent to it. The system with $2x = 2$ has as second equation the sum of the two equations, the system with $2x + 2y = 4$ and $x - y = 0$ has the first equation multiplied by $2$, and the last one swaps the two equations. The system formed by $x + y = 2$ and $2x + 2y = 4$ has instead lost the equation $x - y = 0$, because its second equation is twice the first. Therefore it has $\infty^{1}$ solutions, for example $(2, 0)$, and it is not equivalent to the given system.
