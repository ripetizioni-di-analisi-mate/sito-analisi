The demand and supply functions of two goods are
$$
q_{d1} = 24 - 3p_1 + p_2, \quad q_{s1} = 2p_1 - 2,
\qquad
q_{d2} = 10 + p_1 - 2p_2, \quad q_{s2} = 3p_2 - 4 .
$$
What are the equilibrium prices $(p_1, p_2)$?

- $(4, 6)$
- $(36, 10)$
- $\left( \dfrac{29}{6}, \dfrac{13}{6} \right)$
- $(6, 4)$

## Explanation

At equilibrium, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied in both markets. Setting $24 - 3p_1 + p_2 = 2p_1 - 2$ and $10 + p_1 - 2p_2 = 3p_2 - 4$ and moving the terms in $p_1$ and $p_2$ to the left gives
$$
\begin{cases}
5p_1 - p_2 = 26 \\[4pt]
-p_1 + 5p_2 = 14
\end{cases}
$$
The determinant of the coefficients is $25 - 1 = 24$, so by **Cramer's rule**
$$
p_1 = \frac{26 \cdot 5 - (-1) \cdot 14}{24} = \frac{144}{24} = 6,
\qquad
p_2 = \frac{5 \cdot 14 - (-1) \cdot 26}{24} = \frac{96}{24} = 4 .
$$
The equilibrium quantities are $q_1 = 2 \cdot 6 - 2 = 10$ and $q_2 = 3 \cdot 4 - 4 = 8$.
