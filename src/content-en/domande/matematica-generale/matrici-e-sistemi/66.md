Let $A$ and $B$ be invertible square matrices of order $2$. Which statement holds for every choice of $A$ and $B$?

- The sum $A + B$ is invertible
- The product $A^{T} B$ is invertible
- The difference $A - B$ is invertible
- The matrix $AB - BA$ is invertible

## Explanation

By **Binet's theorem** and since $\det A^{T} = \det A$,
$$
\det(A^{T} B) = \det A \cdot \det B \neq 0 ,
$$
because $A$ and $B$ are invertible and therefore have nonzero determinant. Hence $A^{T} B$ is always invertible. The other three matrices can be zero. With $A = I_2$ and $B = -I_2$ we get $A + B = O$, and with $A = B = I_2$ we get $A - B = O$ and $AB - BA = O$. The zero matrix has determinant $0$, so it is not invertible.
