In the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}
$$
the entries above the antidiagonal, that is, above the diagonal running from the top-right corner to the bottom-left corner, are zero. Which statement about $\det A$ is true?

- $\det A = 24$, the product of the entries on the antidiagonal
- $\det A = 0$, the product of the entries on the main diagonal
- $\det A = -24$, the negative of the product of the entries on the antidiagonal
- $\det A$ cannot be computed with Sarrus's rule, because $A$ is not triangular

## Explanation

The matrix is not triangular, so the rule for the determinant of a triangular matrix does not apply. **Sarrus's rule** holds for every matrix of order $3$, and for this matrix the three products along the diagonals going down to the right are
$$
0 \cdot 3 \cdot 6 = 0,
\qquad
0 \cdot 1 \cdot 4 = 0,
\qquad
2 \cdot 0 \cdot 5 = 0 ,
$$
while the three products along the diagonals going up to the right are
$$
2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 24,
\qquad
0 \cdot 1 \cdot 5 = 0,
\qquad
0 \cdot 0 \cdot 6 = 0 .
$$
Therefore $\det A = 0 - 24 = -24$, that is, the negative of the product $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4$ of the entries on the antidiagonal, which is subtracted in Sarrus's rule.
