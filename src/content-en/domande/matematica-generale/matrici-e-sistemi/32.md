Which matrix is the inverse of the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} ?
$$

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix is upper triangular with diagonal entries equal to $1$, so $\det A = 1$ and $A$ is invertible. The entry in position $(i, j)$ of the inverse is $\dfrac{A_{ji}}{\det A} = A_{ji}$, therefore one computes the matrix of cofactors and transposes it. The nonzero cofactors off the diagonal are
$$
A_{21} = -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -1,
\qquad
A_{31} = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 1,
\qquad
A_{32} = -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -1 ,
$$
and the diagonal ones equal $1$. After transposition these numbers go to positions $(1, 2)$, $(1, 3)$ and $(2, 3)$, so $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. As a check, the first row of $A$ times the third column of $A^{-1}$ gives $1 - 1 + 0 = 0$.
