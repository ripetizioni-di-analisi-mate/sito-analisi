The matrix
$$
N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is upper triangular with zero main diagonal. Which matrix is $N^{2} = N N$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

In matrix multiplication, row $i$ of $N N$ is the sum of the rows of $N$ multiplied by the entries of row $i$ of $N$. The third row of $N$ is zero, so the third row of $N^{2}$ is also zero. The second row of $N$ is $(0, 0, 3)$ and the third row of $N$ is zero, so the second row of $N^{2}$ is $3$ times the third row of $N$, that is, it is zero. The first row of $N^{2}$ is $1$ times the second row of $N$ plus $2$ times the third:
$$
1 \cdot (0, 0, 3) + 2 \cdot (0, 0, 0) = (0, 0, 3) .
$$
Hence $N^{2}$ has the single entry $3$ in position $(1, 3)$. Squaring each entry would give $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, which is not the matrix product. The zero matrix is instead $N^{3}$.
