Which statement is true for the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y = 3 \\[4pt]
2x + 4y = 6
\end{cases}
$$

- The system is inconsistent, because the two lines are parallel
- The system has the unique solution $(1, 1)$
- The system is indeterminate with $\infty^{1}$ solutions
- The system is indeterminate with $\infty^{2}$ solutions

## Explanation

The second equation is the first multiplied by $2$, constant term included, so the two equations represent the same line. The coefficient matrix and the augmented matrix both have rank $1$ and there are $2$ unknowns, therefore by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is **indeterminate** with $\infty^{2 - 1} = \infty^{1}$ solutions, that is $(3 - 2t,\ t)$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$. The pair $(1, 1)$ is one of these solutions, obtained for $t = 1$. The lines would be parallel and distinct if the constant terms were not in the same ratio as the coefficients.
