The demand functions of two goods are
$$
q_{d1} = 40 - 3p_1 + 2p_2,
\qquad
q_{d2} = 30 + p_1 - 2p_2 .
$$
Which statement is true?

- The goods are complements, because the demand for each good decreases when its own price increases
- The goods are substitutes, because the demand for each good increases when the price of the other increases
- The goods are complements, because the demand for each good increases when the price of the other increases
- It cannot be established whether they are substitutes or complements, because the coefficients $2$ and $1$ of the price of the other good are different

## Explanation

In the demand for the first good the coefficient of $p_2$ is $2 > 0$, so, with $p_1$ fixed, an increase of $1$ euro in the price of the second good increases the demand for the first by $2$ units. In the demand for the second good the coefficient of $p_1$ is $1 > 0$, so the demand for the second good also increases when the price of the first increases. A positive coefficient of the price of the other good indicates that the two goods are **substitutes**. The negative coefficients $-3$ and $-2$ of the own prices describe instead the decreasing behavior of each demand with respect to its own price, which holds both for substitutes and for complements. For the classification what matters is the sign of the coefficients of the price of the other good, which here is positive in both demands.
