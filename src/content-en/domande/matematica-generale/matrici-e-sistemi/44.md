The demand and supply functions of two goods are
$$
q_{d1} = 30 - 2p_1 + p_2, \quad q_{s1} = 3p_1 - 5,
\qquad
q_{d2} = 20 + p_1 - 3p_2, \quad q_{s2} = 2p_2 .
$$
What happens in the two markets at the prices $p_1 = 8$ and $p_2 = 6$?

- In the first market there is an excess supply of $1$ unit, in the second an excess demand of $2$ units
- Both markets are in equilibrium
- There is excess demand in both markets
- In the first market there is an excess demand of $1$ unit, in the second an excess supply of $2$ units

## Explanation

Substitute the prices into the four functions. In the first market the quantity demanded is $q_{d1} = 30 - 16 + 6 = 20$ and the quantity supplied is $q_{s1} = 24 - 5 = 19$; since $20 > 19$, there is an excess demand of $1$ unit. In the second market the quantity demanded is $q_{d2} = 20 + 8 - 18 = 10$ and the quantity supplied is $q_{s2} = 12$; since $10 < 12$, there is an excess supply of $2$ units. Therefore the pair $(8, 6)$ is not the equilibrium. Setting demand equal to supply gives the equations $5p_1 - p_2 = 35$ and $-p_1 + 5p_2 = 20$, whose solution is $\left( \frac{65}{8}, \frac{45}{8} \right)$.
