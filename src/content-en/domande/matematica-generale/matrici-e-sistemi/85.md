For which value of the real parameter $k$ is the system of three equations in two unknowns
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 2 \\[4pt]
x - y = 0 \\[4pt]
2x + k y = 5
\end{cases}
$$
consistent?

::grafico{f="2-x;x" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=4 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The lines $x + y = 2$ and $x - y = 0$"}

- $k = 3$
- $k = 5$
- $k = -3$
- $k = 1$

## Explanation

The first two equations form a system with a unique solution, because $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -2 \neq 0$, and its solution is $(1, 1)$, the point where the two lines meet. The whole system is consistent if and only if the third equation is also satisfied by $(1, 1)$, that is,
$$
2 \cdot 1 + k \cdot 1 = 5
\iff
k = 3 .
$$
For $k = 3$ the third line passes through $(1, 1)$ and the system has the unique solution $(1, 1)$. For $k \neq 3$ the three lines have no common point and the system is inconsistent.
