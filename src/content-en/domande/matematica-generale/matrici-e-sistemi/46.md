Let $v = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ be a column vector and $u = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ a row vector. Which matrix is the product $v\,u$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 6 & 0 & 4 \\ -3 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 6 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 4 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The vector $v$ is a $3 \times 1$ matrix and the vector $u$ is a $1 \times 3$ matrix, so the product $v\,u$ is defined and is a $3 \times 3$ matrix. The entry in position $(i, j)$ is the product of row $i$ of $v$, which contains only the number $v_i$, and column $j$ of $u$, which contains only the number $u_j$. Therefore row $i$ of $v\,u$ is $v_i$ times the vector $u$:
$$
v\,u = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \cdot 1 & 3 \cdot 2 & 3 \cdot (-1) \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 \cdot 1 & 2 \cdot 2 & 2 \cdot (-1) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 6 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 4 & -2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The product in the opposite order, $u\,v = 3 + 0 - 2 = 1$, is instead a $1 \times 1$ matrix.
