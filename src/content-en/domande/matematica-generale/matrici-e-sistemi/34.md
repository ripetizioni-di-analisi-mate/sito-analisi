Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be square matrices of order $2$ such that
$$
AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
AC = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which matrix is $A\,(B - 2C)$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -4 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -3 & 4 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

By the distributive property of the product, $A\,(B - 2C) = AB + A\,(-2C)$. By compatibility with scalars, $A\,(-2C) = -2\,(AC)$, hence
$$
A\,(B - 2C) = AB - 2\,AC
= \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 4 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The result is obtained without knowing $A$, $B$ and $C$.
