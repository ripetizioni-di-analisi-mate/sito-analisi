Let $A$ be a $2 \times 3$ matrix, $B$ a $3 \times 2$ matrix and $C$ a $2 \times 2$ matrix. Which statement is true?

- The product $AC$ is defined and is a $2 \times 2$ matrix
- The product $CB$ is defined and is a $2 \times 3$ matrix
- The product $BC$ is defined and is a $3 \times 2$ matrix
- The product $AB$ is defined and is a $3 \times 3$ matrix

## Explanation

The product $PQ$ is defined when the number of columns of $P$ equals the number of rows of $Q$, and in that case it has the rows of $P$ and the columns of $Q$. The matrix $B$ has $2$ columns and $C$ has $2$ rows, so $BC$ is defined and is a $3 \times 2$ matrix. The product $AC$ is not defined, because $A$ has $3$ columns and $C$ has $2$ rows; for the same reason $CB$ is not defined. The product $AB$ is defined, but it has the $2$ rows of $A$ and the $2$ columns of $B$, so it is a $2 \times 2$ matrix.
