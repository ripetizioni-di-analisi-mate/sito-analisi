For which values of the real parameter $k$ is the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & k + 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 & k^2 - 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}
$$
symmetric?

- $k = 2$
- $k = 2$ and $k = -2$
- $k = -2$
- For no value of $k$

## Explanation

The matrix is symmetric if $a_{ij} = a_{ji}$ for all $i$ and $j$. The entries in positions $(1, 3)$ and $(3, 1)$ are both $2$, so two conditions remain, to be satisfied together
$$
\begin{cases}
k + 1 = 3 \\[4pt]
k^2 - 2 = 2
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $k = 2$, the second gives $k = 2$ or $k = -2$. The value $k = -2$ satisfies the second equation but not the first, because $a_{12} = -1 \neq 3$. Therefore the matrix is **symmetric** only for $k = 2$.
