The matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}
$$
has a $2 \times 2$ block in the top-left corner, the entry $4$ in the bottom-right corner and zeros elsewhere. Which matrix is its inverse?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{4} \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{4} \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{4} \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Multiply $A$ by a matrix $X$ with the same zeros in the third row and in the third column. The product $AX$ has the product of the two $2 \times 2$ blocks in the top-left corner, $4$ times the entry $x_{33}$ in the bottom-right corner and zeros elsewhere in the third row and in the third column. Hence $AX = I_3$ when the block of $X$ is the inverse of the block of $A$ and $x_{33}$ is the inverse of $4$. The block $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $2 - 1 = 1$, and the formula for the inverse of order $2$ gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Since the inverse of $4$ is $\dfrac{1}{4}$, we get
$$
A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{4} \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The check $A A^{-1} = I_3$ is done row by column; for example, the first row of $A$ times the first column of $A^{-1}$ gives $2 - 1 + 0 = 1$.
