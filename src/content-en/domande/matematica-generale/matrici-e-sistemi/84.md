How many solutions does the system of two equations in three unknowns
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x + y + k z = 2
\end{cases}
$$
have, depending on the real parameter $k$?

- For $k = 1$ it has $\infty^{2}$ solutions, for $k \neq 1$ it has $\infty^{1}$ solutions
- For every value of $k$ it has $\infty^{1}$ solutions
- For $k = 1$ it is inconsistent, for $k \neq 1$ it has $\infty^{1}$ solutions
- For $k = 1$ it is inconsistent, for $k \neq 1$ it has exactly one solution

## Explanation

For $k = 1$ the two equations have the same left side $x + y + z$ and different right-hand sides, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$, $\operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 2$ and the system is inconsistent. For $k \neq 1$ the minor of the coefficient matrix formed by the second and third columns is
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & k \end{vmatrix} = k - 1 \neq 0 ,
$$
so $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 2$. There are $3$ unknowns, so by the **Rouché–Capelli theorem** there are $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^{1}$ solutions. With two equations in three unknowns the system never has exactly one solution.
