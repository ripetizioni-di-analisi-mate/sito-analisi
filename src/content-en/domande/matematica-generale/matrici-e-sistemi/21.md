Let $A$ be the square matrix of order $3$ whose entry in position $(i, j)$ is the number
$$
a_{ij} = (i - j)^{2} .
$$
What is $\det A$?

- $0$
- $-8$
- $16$
- $8$

## Explanation

On the main diagonal $i = j$, so $a_{11} = a_{22} = a_{33} = 0$. The entries with $|i - j| = 1$ equal $1$ and those with $|i - j| = 2$ equal $4$, therefore
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 4 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
By **Sarrus's rule**, the three products along the descending diagonals are $0$, $1 \cdot 1 \cdot 4 = 4$ and $4 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = 4$, while the three products along the ascending diagonals are $4 \cdot 0 \cdot 4 = 0$, $1 \cdot 1 \cdot 0 = 0$ and $0 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = 0$. Hence
$$
\det A = (0 + 4 + 4) - (0 + 0 + 0) = 8 .
$$
