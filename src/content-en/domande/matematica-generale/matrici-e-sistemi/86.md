Which classification of the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x + k y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x + y + k z = 1
\end{cases}
$$
depending on the real parameter $k$ is correct?

- For $k \neq 1$ it has a unique solution, for $k = 1$ it has $\infty^{1}$ solutions
- For $k \neq 1$ it has a unique solution, for $k = 1$ it is inconsistent
- For $k \neq \pm 1$ it has a unique solution, for $k = 1$ it has $\infty^{2}$ solutions, for $k = -1$ it is inconsistent
- For $k \neq 1$ it has a unique solution, for $k = 1$ it has $\infty^{2}$ solutions

## Explanation

Subtracting the first row from the second and from the third does not change the determinant, and the coefficient matrix becomes upper triangular:
$$
\det A = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & k - 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & k - 1 \end{vmatrix} = (k - 1)^{2} .
$$
For $k \neq 1$ we have $\det A \neq 0$, so by **Cramer's rule** the system has a unique solution, namely $(1, 0, 0)$. For $k = 1$ the three equations coincide with $x + y + z = 1$, so $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 1$. There are $3$ unknowns, so by the **Rouché–Capelli theorem** there are $\infty^{3 - 1} = \infty^{2}$ solutions.
