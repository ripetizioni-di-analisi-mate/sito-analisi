For which values of the real parameter $k$ is the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & k \\ 0 & k - 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & k + 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
not invertible?

- Only for $k = 1$
- For $k = -1$ and for $k = 2$
- For $k = 0$, for $k = 1$ and for $k = -2$
- For $k = 1$ and for $k = -2$

## Explanation

The matrix is upper triangular, so its determinant is the product of the entries on the main diagonal
$$
\det A = 1 \cdot (k - 1)(k + 2) .
$$
The entry $k$ in position $(1, 3)$ lies above the diagonal and plays no role in the determinant. A square matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is different from zero, and $\det A = 0$ for $k = 1$ and for $k = -2$. Therefore $A$ is **not invertible** exactly for $k = 1$ and for $k = -2$.
