The inverse of a matrix $A$ of order $2$ is
$$
A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which matrix is the inverse of the transpose, $\left( A^{T} \right)^{-1}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\ \frac{1}{6} & \frac{1}{3} \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

By the **properties of the inverse**, if $A$ is invertible then $A^{T}$ is invertible too and $\left( A^{T} \right)^{-1} = \left( A^{-1} \right)^{T}$. Hence $\left( A^{T} \right)^{-1}$ is obtained by transposing the given matrix, that is, by swapping its rows and columns, without computing $A$:
$$
\left( A^{T} \right)^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}^{T} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\ \frac{1}{6} & \frac{1}{3} \end{pmatrix}$ is instead $A^{T}$, since $A = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{6} \\ 0 & \frac{1}{3} \end{pmatrix}$.
