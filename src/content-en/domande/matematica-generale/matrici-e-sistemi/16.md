Consider the following diagonal matrix $D$ and matrix $A$.
$$
D = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Multiplying $A$ on the left by $D$, which matrix $DA$ results?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 4 \\ -3 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 12 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 6 \\ 6 & -1 & -3 \\ 0 & -4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 4 \\ 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Row $i$ of $D$ has $d_{ii}$, in column $i$, as its only nonzero entry. Therefore row $i$ of the product $DA$ is row $i$ of $A$ multiplied by $d_{ii}$. So the first row of $A$ is multiplied by $2$, the second by $-1$ and the third by $3$:
$$
DA = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 4 \\ -3 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 12 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
In the product $AD$, instead, the columns of $A$ are multiplied by the diagonal entries of $D$, and the result is the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 6 \\ 6 & -1 & -3 \\ 0 & -4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
