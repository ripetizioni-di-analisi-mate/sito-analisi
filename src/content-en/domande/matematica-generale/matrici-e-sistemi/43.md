For which values of the real parameter $k$ is the system
$$
\begin{cases}
k x + 2y = 2 \\[4pt]
2x + k y = k
\end{cases}
$$
inconsistent?

- For $k = -2$
- For $k = 2$
- For no value of $k$
- For $k = 2$ and for $k = -2$

## Explanation

The coefficient matrix has determinant $k^2 - 4$, so at $k \neq \pm 2$ the system has a unique solution by Cramer’s rule. At $k = 2$ both equations equal $2x + 2y = 2$, so $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 1$. At $k = -2$ the equations are $-2x + 2y = 2$ and $2x - 2y = -2$, and the second is the first multiplied by $-1$, including the constant term. Hence $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 1$ in this case as well. By the Rouché–Capelli theorem the system has infinitely many solutions both at $k = 2$ and at $k = -2$. The system is consistent for every $k$, so **no value** of $k$ makes it inconsistent.
