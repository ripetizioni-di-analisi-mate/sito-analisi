For which value of the real parameter $k$ does the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} k & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
coincide with its own inverse, that is, $A^{-1} = A$?

- $k = -1$
- $k = 1$ or $k = -1$
- $k = 1$
- For no value of $k$

## Explanation

The condition $A^{-1} = A$ is equivalent to $A^{2} = I_2$, because multiplying $A^{-1} = A$ on the left by $A$ gives $I_2 = A^{2}$, and conversely $A^{2} = I_2$ says that $A$ is its own inverse. Matrix multiplication gives
$$
A^{2} = \begin{pmatrix} k^{2} & k - 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Therefore the following must hold together
$$
\begin{cases}
k^{2} = 1 \\[4pt]
k - 1 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
and the only solution is $k = 1$. For $k = -1$ the first condition is satisfied, but the entry in position $(1, 2)$ of $A^{2}$ is $-2$.
