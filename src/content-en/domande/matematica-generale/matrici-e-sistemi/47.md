Which of the following matrices commutes with $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, that is, satisfies $AX = XA$?

- $X = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $X = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $X = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Let $X = \begin{pmatrix} p & q \\ r & s \end{pmatrix}$. Matrix multiplication gives
$$
AX = \begin{pmatrix} p + r & q + s \\ r & s \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
XA = \begin{pmatrix} p & p + q \\ r & r + s \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Comparing the entries in position $(1, 1)$ gives $r = 0$, and comparing those in position $(1, 2)$ gives $s = p$. Hence the matrices that commute with $A$ are those of the form $\begin{pmatrix} p & q \\ 0 & p \end{pmatrix}$, with equal diagonal entries and a zero below the diagonal. Among the four, only $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has this form: $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has different diagonal entries, while the other two have a nonzero entry below the diagonal.
