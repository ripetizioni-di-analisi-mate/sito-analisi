Let $A$ be a square matrix of order $3$ with $\det A = 7$, and let $b$ be the second column of $A$. What is the solution $(x_1, x_2, x_3)$ of the system $A x = b$?

- $(0, 1, 0)$
- $(0, 7, 0)$
- $\left( 0, \dfrac{1}{7}, 0 \right)$
- $(1, 1, 1)$

## Explanation

Since $\det A \neq 0$, **Cramer's rule** applies and the system has a unique solution. For $j = 2$ the matrix $A_2$ is obtained by replacing the second column of $A$ with $b$, which is already the second column, so $A_2 = A$ and $x_2 = \dfrac{\det A}{\det A} = 1$. The matrices $A_1$ and $A_3$ instead have two columns equal to the second column of $A$, so their determinant is zero and $x_1 = x_3 = 0$. The same result can be read from the product, because $A$ times the vector $(0, 1, 0)$ is exactly the second column of $A$.
