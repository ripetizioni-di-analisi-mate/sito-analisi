The system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x - y + z = 3 \\[4pt]
x + 3z = 1 \\[4pt]
4x + y - 2z = 0
\end{cases}
$$
has a coefficient matrix with nonzero determinant. Which expression gives the unknown $y$ according to Cramer's rule?

- $y = \dfrac{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 0 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}$
- $y = \dfrac{\begin{vmatrix} 3 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}$
- $y = \dfrac{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 & 0 \\ 4 & 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}$
- $y = \dfrac{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 0 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}$

## Explanation

By **Cramer's rule**, each unknown is the quotient of two determinants. The denominator is always $\det A$, where $A$ is the coefficient matrix. The numerator is obtained from $A$ by replacing the column of coefficients of the unknown in question with the column of constant terms. The unknown $y$ is the second one, so the second column of $A$, which is $(-1, 0, 1)$, is replaced by the column $(3, 1, 0)$:
$$
y = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 0 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix}}
= \frac{34}{-19} = -\frac{34}{19} .
$$
The quotient with the first column replaced gives the unknown $x$ instead, while replacing a row with the constant terms does not correspond to any unknown.
