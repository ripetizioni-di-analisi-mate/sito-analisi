Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
$$
which matrix is the transpose $(AB)^{T}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 7 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 7 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The row-by-column multiplication gives
$$
AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + 6 & 0 + 2 \\ 0 + 3 & 0 + 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The transpose has as rows the columns of $AB$, so $(AB)^{T} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. The same result follows from the formula $(AB)^{T} = B^{T} A^{T}$, in which the order of the factors is reversed. The product $A^{T} B^{T}$, with the order of the factors unchanged, equals instead $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 7 \end{pmatrix}$, which is a different matrix.
