For which value of the real parameter $k$ is the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y - z = 2 \\[4pt]
x + k y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
k x + y + z = 1
\end{cases}
$$
inconsistent?

- $k = -3$
- $k = 1$
- $k = 3$
- $k = 1$ or $k = -3$

## Explanation

With Sarrus's rule $\det A = (k + k - 1) - (-k^2 + 1 + 1) = k^2 + 2k - 3 = (k - 1)(k + 3)$. For $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq -3$ the determinant is nonzero, so by Cramer's rule the system has a unique solution. For $k = 1$ the second and the third equation both coincide with $x + y + z = 1$, so $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 2$ and the system has infinitely many solutions. For $k = -3$ the minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -3 \end{vmatrix} = -4$ is nonzero, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. The minor of the augmented matrix formed by the columns of $x$, of $y$ and of the constant terms is
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & -3 & 1 \\ -3 & 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -24 ,
$$
so $\operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 3$. Since the two ranks are different, by the **Rouché–Capelli theorem** the system is **inconsistent** for $k = -3$.
