A firm produces three items. One unit of the first requires $2$ hours in department A, $1$ in department B and $1$ in department C; one unit of the second requires $1$, $3$ and $1$ hours; one unit of the third requires $1$, $2$ and $3$ hours. Departments A, B and C have $80$, $130$ and $80$ hours available. In the production plan that uses all the available hours in full, how many units of the second item are produced?

- $20$
- $10$
- $30$
- $330$

## Explanation

Denoting by $x$, $y$ and $z$ the quantities of the three items, each department gives one equation, and the three equations form the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + y + z = 80 \\[4pt]
x + 3y + 2z = 130 \\[4pt]
x + y + 3z = 80
\end{cases}
$$
With Sarrus's rule $\det A = (18 + 2 + 1) - (3 + 4 + 3) = 11$, so **Cramer's rule** applies. For the second unknown the *second* column of $A$ is replaced with the constant terms, and we obtain
$$
\det A_2 = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 80 & 1 \\ 1 & 130 & 2 \\ 1 & 80 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = (780 + 160 + 80) - (130 + 320 + 240) = 330 .
$$
Therefore $y = \dfrac{330}{11} = 30$, and $30$ units of the second item are produced.
