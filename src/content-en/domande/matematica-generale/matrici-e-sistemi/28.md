Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
$$
which statement is true?

- $\det(A + B) = 0$
- $\det(AB) = -4$
- $\det(BA) = 4$
- $\det(2A) = -4$

## Explanation

The determinants of the two matrices are $\det A = 4 - 6 = -2$ and $\det B = 2 - 0 = 2$. By **Binet's theorem** $\det(AB) = \det A \cdot \det B = -4$, and also $\det(BA) = -4$, although $AB \neq BA$. The determinant is not additive, so $\det(A + B)$ must be computed on the sum matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 4 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, and it equals $15 - 8 = 7$, while $\det A + \det B = 0$. Finally the matrix has order $2$, therefore $\det(2A) = 2^{2} \det A = -8$.
