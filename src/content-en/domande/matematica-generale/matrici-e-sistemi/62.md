Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
$$
for which $\det(AB) = 3$, what is the value of $\det(BA)$?

- $3$
- $9$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The matrix $BA$ is square of order $3$, while $A$ and $B$ are not square, so **Binet's theorem** does not apply and $\det(BA)$ must be computed. Matrix multiplication gives
$$
BA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The third row is the sum of the first two, so $\det(BA) = 0$. The equality $\det(AB) = \det(BA)$, which follows from Binet's theorem, holds for square matrices of the same order.
