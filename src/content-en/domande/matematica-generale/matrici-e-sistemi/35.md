In the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 & 4 & 6 \end{pmatrix}
$$
the minor $M$ formed by the first two rows and the first two columns equals $1$. How many bordered minors of $M$ are there?

- $2$
- $4$
- $1$
- $3$

## Explanation

A bordered minor of $M$ is obtained by adding to $M$ one row and one column of $A$ that $M$ does not already contain. The row to add can only be the third, while the column can be the third or the fourth, so there are $1 \cdot 2 = 2$ bordered minors. The minors of order $3$ of $A$ are instead $4$, one for each choice of three columns. In this matrix the third row is the sum of the first two, therefore both bordered minors are zero and, by the **bordered minors theorem**, $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$.
