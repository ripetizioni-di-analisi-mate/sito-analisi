The circle $x^2 + y^2 = 2$ and the parabola $y = x^2$ intersect at the point $(1, 1)$. Can they be the level curve for level $2$ and the level curve for level $5$ of the same function $f$?

- No, because $f(1, 1)$ would have to equal both $2$ and $5$
- Yes, if $f$ is not continuous at $(1, 1)$
- Yes, if the gradient of $f$ is zero at $(1, 1)$
- Yes, provided that $(1, 1)$ belongs to the domain of $f$

## Explanation

The level curve for level $k$ of $f$ consists of the points in the domain where $f$ equals $k$. If the circle were the level curve for level $2$, we would have $f(1, 1) = 2$; if the parabola were the level curve for level $5$, we would have $f(1, 1) = 5$. A function takes a single value at each point of its domain, so the two equalities cannot hold together. Therefore the answer is **no**, whatever the function, continuous or not, and whatever its gradient. Two level curves for different levels never have points in common.
