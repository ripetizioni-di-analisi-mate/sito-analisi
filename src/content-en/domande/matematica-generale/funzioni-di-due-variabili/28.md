For the function $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x - y}{x + y}$, what is $f_x(1, 1)$?

- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

We differentiate with respect to $x$ using the quotient rule, treating $y$ as a constant. The derivatives of $x - y$ and of $x + y$ with respect to $x$ are both $1$, so
$$
f_x(x, y) = \frac{1 \cdot (x + y) - (x - y) \cdot 1}{(x + y)^2} = \frac{2y}{(x + y)^2} .
$$
At the point $(1, 1)$ we get $f_x(1, 1) = \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
