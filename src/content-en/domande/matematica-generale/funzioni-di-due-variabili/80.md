The curve $x^3 + xy + y^2 = 3$ passes through the point $(1, 1)$, and near that point it is the graph of a differentiable function $y(x)$. What is $y'(1)$?

- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $-4$
- $-\dfrac{4}{3}$

## Explanation

The curve is the level curve $3$ of $f(x, y) = x^3 + xy + y^2$. Along the curve $f(x, y(x)) = 3$, and differentiating with respect to $x$ by the derivative along a curve gives $f_x + f_y\,y'(x) = 0$. We have $f_x = 3x^2 + y$ and $f_y = x + 2y$, so at $(1, 1)$ they equal $f_x = 4$ and $f_y = 3$. Therefore
$$
y'(1) = -\frac{f_x(1, 1)}{f_y(1, 1)} = -\frac{4}{3} .
$$
