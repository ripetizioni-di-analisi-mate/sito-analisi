What is the stationary point of $f(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - 3x$?

- $(-1, 2)$
- $\left( \dfrac{3}{2}, 0 \right)$
- $(2, -1)$
- $(1, 1)$

## Explanation

A point is stationary if the gradient is the zero vector. The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2x + y - 3$ and $f_y = x + 2y$, so the conditions to be satisfied together are
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + y - 3 = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 2y = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation gives $x = -2y$. Substituting into the first gives $-4y + y - 3 = 0$, that is, $y = -1$. Therefore $x = 2$, and the **stationary point** is $(2, -1)$.
