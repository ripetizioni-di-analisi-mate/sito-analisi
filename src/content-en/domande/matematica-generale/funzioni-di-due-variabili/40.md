A consumer has a utility function $U(x, y) = x^2 y$. Using the marginal utility, by approximately how much does utility increase if the quantity $x$ goes from $3$ to $3{,}1$ and the quantity $y = 5$ stays unchanged?

- $0{,}9$
- $30$
- $3$
- $1{,}5$

## Explanation

The **marginal utility** of the first good is $U_x = 2xy$, which at the bundle $(3, 5)$ equals $30$. Using the linear approximation with $dx = 0{,}1$ and $dy = 0$, the change in utility is approximately $U_x\,dx = 30 \cdot 0{,}1 = 3$. The exact value is $U(3{,}1;\ 5) - U(3, 5) = 48{,}05 - 45 = 3{,}05$, very close to the estimate.
