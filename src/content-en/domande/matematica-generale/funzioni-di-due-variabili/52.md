The shaded region in the figure, bounded by the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ and the parabola $y = x^2$, is the natural domain of one of the following functions. The arc of the parabola, drawn as a solid line, belongs to the domain; the circle, drawn dashed, does not. Which function has this domain?

::grafico{f="x^2" curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" area="sqrt(abs(4-x^2));x^2;-1.2496;1.2496" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda="y = x^2;x^2 + y^2 = 4" titolo="The shaded region is bounded by the parabola $y = x^2$ and the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$"}

- $f(x, y) = \ln(4 - x^2 - y^2) + \sqrt{x^2 - y}$
- $f(x, y) = \ln(x^2 + y^2 - 4) + \sqrt{y - x^2}$
- $f(x, y) = \ln(4 - x^2 - y^2) + \sqrt{y - x}$
- $f(x, y) = \ln(4 - x^2 - y^2) + \sqrt{y - x^2}$

## Explanation

The shaded region lies inside the circle of radius $2$, which does not belong to it, so its points satisfy $x^2 + y^2 < 4$, that is, $4 - x^2 - y^2 > 0$. The region also lies above the parabola, which belongs to it, so its points satisfy $y \geq x^2$, that is, $y - x^2 \geq 0$. The logarithm requires a strictly positive argument and the square root an argument greater than or equal to zero, therefore the domain of $\ln(4 - x^2 - y^2) + \sqrt{y - x^2}$ is given exactly by
$$
\begin{cases}
4 - x^2 - y^2 > 0 \\[4pt]
y - x^2 \geq 0
\end{cases}
$$
and it is the region in the figure. With $\sqrt{x^2 - y}$ one would get the part of the disk below the parabola, with $\ln(x^2 + y^2 - 4)$ the part outside the circle, and with $\sqrt{y - x}$ the part of the disk above the line $y = x$.
