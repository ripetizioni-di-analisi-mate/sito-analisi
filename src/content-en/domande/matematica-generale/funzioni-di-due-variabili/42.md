Which statement is true for the production function $Q(K, L) = 6\,K^{\frac{1}{3}} L^{\frac{2}{3}}$, with $K > 0$ and $L > 0$?

- It has constant returns to scale and a marginal product of capital that decreases with $K$
- It has constant returns to scale and a marginal product of capital that increases with $K$
- It has increasing returns to scale and a marginal product of capital that decreases with $K$
- It has decreasing returns to scale and a marginal product of capital that is constant in $K$

## Explanation

The Cobb-Douglas function has degree of homogeneity equal to the sum of its exponents, $\dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3} = 1$, so it has **constant returns to scale**. The marginal product of capital is $Q_K = 2\,K^{-\frac{2}{3}} L^{\frac{2}{3}}$, and its derivative with respect to $K$ is
$$
Q_{KK} = -\frac{4}{3}\,K^{-\frac{5}{3}} L^{\frac{2}{3}} < 0 .
$$
Therefore the marginal product of capital is **decreasing** in $K$.
