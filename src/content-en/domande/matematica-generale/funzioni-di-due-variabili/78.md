The tangent plane to the graph of $f(x, y) = 2x^2 + y^2$ at the point $(1, 1, 3)$ has equation $z = 4x + 2y - 3$. How is the graph of $f$ positioned relative to this plane?

- The graph lies entirely below the plane, which touches it only at $(1, 1, 3)$
- The graph crosses the plane along a line through $(1, 1, 3)$
- The graph lies entirely above the plane, which touches it only at $(1, 1, 3)$
- The graph and the plane share an ellipse centered at $(1, 1, 3)$

## Explanation

We compare the height of the graph with that of the plane at the same point $(x, y)$. The difference is
$$
2x^2 + y^2 - (4x + 2y - 3) = 2x^2 - 4x + 2 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = 2\,(x - 1)^2 + (y - 1)^2 .
$$
This expression is greater than or equal to zero at every point, and it equals zero only for $(x, y) = (1, 1)$. Therefore **the graph lies entirely above the tangent plane, which touches it only at $(1, 1, 3)$**. Consequently, for this function, the linear approximation at $(1, 1)$ always gives a value less than or equal to the exact one.
