As $t \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the point $(2 \cos t, \sin t)$ moves in the plane. How does the value of the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2$ change along the path?

- It increases for $0 < t < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- It stays constant and equal to $4$
- It decreases for $0 < t < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- It varies between $1$ and $4$

## Explanation

Along the path the value of $f$ is $g(t) = f(2 \cos t, \sin t)$, and
$$
g(t) = 4 \cos^2 t + 4 \sin^2 t = 4 .
$$
Therefore the value of $f$ **stays constant and equal to $4$**, and the path is contained in the level curve for level $4$ of $f$, that is, in the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 4$. The same result follows from the derivative along a curve. We have $f_x = 2x$ and $f_y = 8y$, so
$$
g'(t) = 2 \cdot 2 \cos t \cdot (-2 \sin t) + 8 \sin t \cdot \cos t = -8 \sin t \cos t + 8 \sin t \cos t = 0 ,
$$
in agreement with the fact that the gradient is perpendicular to the level curve.
