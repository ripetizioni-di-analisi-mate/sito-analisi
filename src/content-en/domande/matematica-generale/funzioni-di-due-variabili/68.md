For the function $f(x, y) = \ln(x + y^2)$, defined where $x + y^2 > 0$, at which points of the domain is the second derivative $f_{xx}$ negative?

- At no point of the domain
- Only at the points of the domain with $x > 0$
- Only at the points of the domain with $y \neq 0$
- At every point of the domain

## Explanation

Treating $y$ as a constant, we have
$$
f_x(x, y) = \frac{1}{x + y^2},
\qquad
f_{xx}(x, y) = -\frac{1}{(x + y^2)^2} .
$$
In the domain $x + y^2 > 0$, so the denominator $(x + y^2)^2$ is positive. Therefore $f_{xx}$ is negative **at every point of the domain**, including those with $x \leq 0$, such as $(-1, 2)$.
