Which of the following production functions, with $K > 0$ and $L > 0$, has a constant marginal product of labor?

- $Q(K, L) = KL$
- $Q(K, L) = K^{\frac{1}{2}} L^{\frac{1}{2}}$
- $Q(K, L) = K^2 + 3L$
- $Q(K, L) = L^2 + K$

## Explanation

The marginal product of labor is $Q_L$. For $K^2 + 3L$ we have $Q_L = 3$, which is **constant**. For $KL$ we have $Q_L = K$, which changes with capital. For $K^{\frac{1}{2}} L^{\frac{1}{2}}$ we have $Q_L = \dfrac{1}{2}\,K^{\frac{1}{2}} L^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, which depends on $K$ and on $L$. For $L^2 + K$ we have $Q_L = 2L$, which increases with labor.
