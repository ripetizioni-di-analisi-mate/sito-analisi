At which point does the tangent plane to the graph of $f(x, y) = \sqrt{xy}$, with $x > 0$ and $y > 0$, at the point $(4, 1, 2)$ meet the $z$-axis?

- $(0, 0, 2)$
- $(0, 0, 4)$
- $(0, 0, -2)$
- $(0, 0, 0)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = \dfrac{y}{2\sqrt{xy}}$ and $f_y = \dfrac{x}{2\sqrt{xy}}$, so $f_x(4, 1) = \dfrac{1}{4}$ and $f_y(4, 1) = 1$. The tangent plane is therefore
$$
z = 2 + \frac{1}{4}\,(x - 4) + (y - 1) = \frac{x}{4} + y .
$$
For $x = 0$ and $y = 0$ we get $z = 0$, so the plane meets the $z$-axis at the **origin**. This agrees with Euler's theorem, because $f$ is homogeneous of degree $1$ and so the height $f - x_0 f_x - y_0 f_y$ of the plane on the $z$-axis is zero.
