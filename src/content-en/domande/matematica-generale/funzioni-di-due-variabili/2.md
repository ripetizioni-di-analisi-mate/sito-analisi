For the function $f(x, y) = \ln(x^2 + y^2 - 9)$, what kind of set is the natural domain?

- Open and unbounded
- Closed and unbounded
- Open and bounded
- Closed and bounded

## Explanation

The argument of the logarithm must be strictly positive, so the domain is $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 > 9 \}$, that is, the set of points outside the circle centered at the origin with radius $3$. The boundary of $D$ is the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$, whose points do not satisfy the strict inequality; therefore $D$ contains none of its boundary points and is **open**. The domain contains the points $(n, 0)$ for every integer $n > 3$, which lie arbitrarily far from the origin, so $D$ is **not bounded**.
