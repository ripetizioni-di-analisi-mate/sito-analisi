The graph of the function $f(x, y) = x^2 - 2xy + 3y^2$ is intersected with the vertical plane $y = 1$. The resulting cross-section is a parabola. What is its vertex?

- $(-1, 1, 6)$
- $(0, 1, 3)$
- $(1, 1, 2)$
- $(2, 1, 3)$

## Explanation

The points of the graph lying in the plane $y = 1$ have height $z = f(x, 1) = x^2 - 2x + 3$. Completing the square gives
$$
x^2 - 2x + 3 = (x - 1)^2 + 2 ,
$$
so the cross-section is a parabola opening upward, with its lowest point at $x = 1$, where the height equals $2$. Therefore the vertex is **$(1, 1, 2)$**. The points $(0, 1, 3)$ and $(2, 1, 3)$ lie on the parabola, at the same height and symmetric with respect to the vertex, while $(-1, 1, 6)$ comes from getting the sign of the $x$-coordinate of the vertex wrong.
