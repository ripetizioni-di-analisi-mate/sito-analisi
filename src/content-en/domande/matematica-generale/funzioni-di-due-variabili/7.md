For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 e^{y}$, what is the Hessian matrix at the point $(1, 0)$?

- $H(1, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $H(1, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $H(1, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $H(1, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2e & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The first derivatives are $f_x = 2x\,e^{y}$ and $f_y = x^2 e^{y}$. Differentiating again gives $f_{xx} = 2e^{y}$, $f_{xy} = 2x\,e^{y}$, $f_{yx} = 2x\,e^{y}$ and $f_{yy} = x^2 e^{y}$. The mixed derivatives coincide, as guaranteed by **Schwarz's theorem**, whose hypotheses are satisfied because $f_{xy}$ and $f_{yx}$ are continuous on the whole plane. At the point $(1, 0)$ we have $e^{0} = 1$, so $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = f_{yx} = 2$ and $f_{yy} = 1$. The first row of the Hessian matrix contains $f_{xx}$ and $f_{xy}$. The second contains $f_{yx}$ and $f_{yy}$. The matrix is therefore
$$
H(1, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
