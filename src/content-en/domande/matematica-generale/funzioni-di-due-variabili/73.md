A function $f$, defined on the whole plane, has partial derivative $f_x(x, y) > 0$ at every point. Which of the following inequalities is certainly true?

- $f(1, 6) > f(1, 5)$
- $f(2, 5) > f(1, 5)$
- $f(2, 5) > f(1, 4)$
- $f(1, 5) > 0$

## Explanation

Fix $y = 5$ and consider the function of one variable $h(x) = f(x, 5)$. Its derivative is $h'(x) = f_x(x, 5)$, which is positive for every $x$, so $h$ is strictly increasing. Since $2 > 1$, we have $h(2) > h(1)$, that is, **$f(2, 5) > f(1, 5)$**. The other three inequalities compare points with different $y$-coordinates or concern the sign of $f$, and the hypothesis gives no information about these. For example, $f(x, y) = x - y^2$ has $f_x = 1 > 0$, and $f(1, 6) = -35 < f(1, 5) = -24$, $f(2, 5) = -23 < f(1, 4) = -15$ and $f(1, 5) < 0$.
