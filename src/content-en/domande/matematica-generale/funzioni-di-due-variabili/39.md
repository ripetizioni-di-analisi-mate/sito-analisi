For the production function $Q(K, L) = 3\,K^{\frac{1}{3}} L^{\frac{2}{3}}$, what is the marginal rate of substitution $\dfrac{Q_L}{Q_K}$ at the point $K = 8$, $L = 1$?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{16}$
- $2$
- $16$

## Explanation

For the Cobb-Douglas function the marginal productivities are $Q_K = \dfrac{a\,Q}{K}$ and $Q_L = \dfrac{b\,Q}{L}$, so the **marginal rate of substitution** is
$$
\frac{Q_L}{Q_K} = \frac{b}{a} \cdot \frac{K}{L} ,
$$
and the constant $3$ cancels out. With $a = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $b = \dfrac{2}{3}$ we have $\dfrac{b}{a} = 2$. Therefore at the point $K = 8$, $L = 1$ the rate equals $2 \cdot \dfrac{8}{1} = 16$.
