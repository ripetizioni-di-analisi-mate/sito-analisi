For the production function $Q(K, L) = 4\sqrt{KL}$, with $K > 0$ and $L > 0$, what happens to the marginal product of labor if capital increases and labor stays the same?

- It decreases, since $Q_{LL} < 0$
- It stays the same, since $K$ and $L$ are independent variables
- It decreases, since $Q_{LK} < 0$
- It increases, since $Q_{LK} > 0$

## Explanation

The marginal product of labor is $Q_L = \dfrac{2\sqrt{K}}{\sqrt{L}}$, which also depends on $K$. Its derivative with respect to capital is
$$
Q_{LK} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{K}\,\sqrt{L}} > 0 ,
$$
so $Q_L$ **increases** as $K$ grows. The inequality $Q_{LL} < 0$ is true, but it describes how $Q_L$ changes as $L$ grows, not as $K$ grows. The fact that $K$ and $L$ are independent does not prevent $Q_L$ from depending on $K$.
