For the function $f(x, y) = |x| + y$, which statement about the partial derivatives at the point $(0, 0)$ is true?

- $f_x(0, 0) = 0$ and $f_y(0, 0) = 1$
- $f_y(0, 0) = 1$ and $f_x(0, 0)$ does not exist
- $f_x(0, 0) = 1$ and $f_y(0, 0) = 1$
- Neither partial derivative exists

## Explanation

For the derivative with respect to $x$ we keep $y = 0$ and consider the difference quotient
$$
\frac{f(h, 0) - f(0, 0)}{h} = \frac{|h|}{h} ,
$$
which equals $1$ for $h > 0$ and $-1$ for $h < 0$. The quotient has no limit as $h \to 0$, so $f_x(0, 0)$ does not exist. For the derivative with respect to $y$ we have $\dfrac{f(0, h) - f(0, 0)}{h} = \dfrac{h}{h} = 1$, therefore **$f_y(0, 0) = 1$ and $f_x(0, 0)$ does not exist**.
