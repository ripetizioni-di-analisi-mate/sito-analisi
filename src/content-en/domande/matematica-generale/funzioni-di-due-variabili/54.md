The natural domain of the function $f(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{\ln(x - y)}$ is a half-plane with a line removed. Which one?

- The half-plane $y < x$ with the line $y = x$ removed
- The half-plane $y > x$ with the line $y = x + 1$ removed
- The half-plane $y < x$ with the line $y = x - 1$ removed
- The half-plane $y < x$ with the line $y = x + 1$ removed

## Explanation

The logarithm requires $x - y > 0$, and the denominator must be nonzero, that is, $\ln(x - y) \neq 0$. Since the logarithm equals $0$ only at $1$, the second condition is equivalent to $x - y \neq 1$. The conditions to be satisfied together are therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
x - y > 0 \\[4pt]
x - y \neq 1
\end{cases}
$$
Hence the domain is the half-plane **$y < x$ with the line $y = x - 1$ removed**.
