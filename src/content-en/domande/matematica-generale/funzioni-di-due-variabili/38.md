Let $f(x, y) = x\,e^{y}$ and consider the curve $x(t) = t^2$, $y(t) = 2t$. What is $g'(1)$, where $g(t) = f(x(t), y(t))$?

- $4e^2$
- $3e^2$
- $2e^2$
- $4e$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives of $f$ are $f_x = e^{y}$ and $f_y = x\,e^{y}$, and the derivatives of the components of the curve are $x'(t) = 2t$ and $y'(t) = 2$. The partial derivatives of $f$ are continuous on the plane, so $f$ is differentiable, and by the formula for the **derivative along a curve**
$$
g'(t) = e^{y(t)} \cdot 2t + x(t)\,e^{y(t)} \cdot 2 = 2t\,e^{2t} + 2t^2 e^{2t} .
$$
For $t = 1$ the point of the curve is $(1, 2)$, therefore $g'(1) = 2e^2 + 2e^2 = 4e^2$.
