A point moves along the line $x(t) = 1 + t$, $y(t) = 2t$. Let $g(t) = f(x(t), y(t))$ with $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$. For which value of $t$ is $g'(t) = 0$?

- $t = -\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $t = -\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $t = \dfrac{1}{5}$
- $t = -1$

## Explanation

By the derivative along a curve $g'(t) = f_x\,x'(t) + f_y\,y'(t)$, with $f_x = 2x$, $f_y = 2y$, $x'(t) = 1$ and $y'(t) = 2$. Hence
$$
g'(t) = 2\,(1 + t) \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 2t \cdot 2 = 2 + 10t ,
$$
which vanishes for $t = -\dfrac{1}{5}$. The corresponding point, $\left( \dfrac{4}{5}, -\dfrac{2}{5} \right)$, is the point of the line closest to the origin, because $f$ is the square of the distance from the origin. The value $t = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ comes from forgetting the factor $y'(t) = 2$.
