Let $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x^2 y}{x^4 + y^2}$, defined away from the origin. Along every line through the origin, $f$ tends to $0$ as the point tends to $(0, 0)$. Which statement about the limit of $f$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$ is true?

- The limit is $0$, since it is $0$ along every line through the origin
- The limit is $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The limit is $0$, if we set $f(0, 0) = 0$
- The limit does not exist, since along the parabola $y = x^2$ the function equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

At the points of the parabola $y = x^2$ with $x \neq 0$ we have
$$
f(x, x^2) = \frac{x^2 \cdot x^2}{x^4 + x^4} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
Approaching the origin along the parabola therefore gives the value $\dfrac{1}{2}$, while along the lines it gives $0$. Two different curves give two different limits, so the limit **does not exist**. The value assigned at $(0, 0)$ does not change this conclusion, because the limit depends only on the values at points other than $(0, 0)$.
