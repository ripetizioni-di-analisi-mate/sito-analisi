What is the value of $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x y^2}{x^2 + y^4}$ at the points of the parabola $x = y^2$ with $y \neq 0$?

- $0$
- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Substituting $x = y^2$ gives
$$
f(y^2, y) = \frac{y^2 \cdot y^2}{y^4 + y^4} = \frac{y^4}{2y^4} .
$$
Since $y \neq 0$, we can cancel $y^4$, and the function equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$ at every point of the parabola under consideration. Along a line $y = mx$, we get $f(x, mx) = \dfrac{m^2 x}{1 + m^4 x^2}$, which tends to $0$ as $x \to 0$. So approaching the origin along the parabola and along the lines gives different limits, and therefore the limit of $f$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$ **does not exist**.
