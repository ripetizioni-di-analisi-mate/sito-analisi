For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3xy - y^3$, what is the determinant of the Hessian matrix at the point $(1, 1)$?

- $-3$
- $21$
- $-21$
- $-12$

## Explanation

The first derivatives are $f_x = 2x + 3y$ and $f_y = 3x - 3y^2$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = f_{yx} = 3$ and $f_{yy} = -6y$, which at $(1, 1)$ equals $-6$. Hence
$$
H(1, 1) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & -6 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(1, 1) = 2 \cdot (-6) - 3 \cdot 3 = -21 .
$$
