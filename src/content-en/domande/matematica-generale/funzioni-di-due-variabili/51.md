A subset of the plane can be open and closed at the same time. For which of the following sets does this happen?

- The plane $\mathbb{R}^2$
- The disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$
- The half-plane $y > 0$
- The line $y = 0$

## Explanation

Every point of the plane has a neighborhood contained in $\mathbb{R}^2$, so $\mathbb{R}^2$ is open. The set $\mathbb{R}^2$ has no boundary points, because every neighborhood contains only points of $\mathbb{R}^2$; therefore it contains all its boundary points and is also closed. The disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$ is closed and not open, because the points of the circle belong to it and are boundary points. The half-plane $y > 0$ is open and not closed, because it does not contain the points of the $x$-axis, which are boundary points. The line $y = 0$ is closed and not open, because no disk centered at one of its points is contained in the line. Hence the only set that is both open and closed is **$\mathbb{R}^2$**.
