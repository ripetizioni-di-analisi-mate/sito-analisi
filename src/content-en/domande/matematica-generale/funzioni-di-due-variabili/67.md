For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 y + y^3 - 3y$, at which points of the plane is $f_x(x, y) = 0$?

- At the points of the two coordinate axes
- At the origin only
- At the points of the $y$-axis only
- At the points of the lines $y = 1$ and $y = -1$

## Explanation

In computing $f_x$ the variable $y$ is a constant, so the terms $y^3$ and $-3y$ have zero derivative and
$$
f_x(x, y) = 2xy .
$$
The product $2xy$ is zero if and only if $x = 0$ or $y = 0$. Therefore $f_x$ vanishes at the points of the $y$-axis and at the points of the $x$-axis, that is, **on the two coordinate axes**.
