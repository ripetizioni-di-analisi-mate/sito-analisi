Let $f$ be a differentiable function for $x > 0$ and $y > 0$ such that $f(tx, ty) = t^{3} f(x, y)$ for every $t > 0$. Given that $f_x(1, 1) = 2$, what is $f_x(2, 2)$?

- $16$
- $4$
- $2$
- $8$

## Explanation

For every $t > 0$ we have $f(tx, ty) = t^{3} f(x, y)$. Differentiating both sides with respect to $x$, using the chain rule on the left-hand side, gives
$$
t\,f_x(tx, ty) = t^{3} f_x(x, y) ,
$$
that is, $f_x(tx, ty) = t^{2} f_x(x, y)$. Therefore $f_x$ is homogeneous of degree $2$. With $t = 2$ and $(x, y) = (1, 1)$ we get $f_x(2, 2) = 4 \cdot 2 = 8$.
