What is $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (2, 1)} \frac{x^2 - 4y^2}{x - 2y}$?

- $0$
- $-4$
- $4$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

The function is defined at points with $x \neq 2y$, and the point $(2, 1)$ lies on the line $x = 2y$, so it is a boundary point of the domain. At $(2, 1)$ the numerator and the denominator are both $0$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $x^2 - 4y^2 = (x - 2y)(x + 2y)$, at the points of the domain the function coincides with the polynomial $x + 2y$. The polynomial is continuous, hence the limit equals $2 + 2 \cdot 1 = 4$.
