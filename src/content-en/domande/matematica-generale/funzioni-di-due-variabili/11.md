What is the value of $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x^2 - y^2}{x^2 + y^2}$ at the points of the line $y = mx$ with $x \neq 0$?

- $\dfrac{m^2 - 1}{m^2 + 1}$
- $\dfrac{1 - m^2}{1 + m^2}$
- $\dfrac{1 - m}{1 + m}$
- $1 - m^2$

## Explanation

Substituting $y = mx$ gives
$$
f(x, mx) = \frac{x^2 - m^2 x^2}{x^2 + m^2 x^2} = \frac{x^2 (1 - m^2)}{x^2 (1 + m^2)} .
$$
Since $x \neq 0$, the factor $x^2$ can be canceled, and the function takes the value $\dfrac{1 - m^2}{1 + m^2}$ at every point of the line under consideration. This value depends on $m$. For example, it equals $1$ on the $x$-axis and $0$ on the line $y = x$. Hence the restrictions of $f$ to the lines through the origin have different limits, and therefore the **limit** of $f$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$ **does not exist**.
