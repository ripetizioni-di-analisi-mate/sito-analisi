A point starts at $(2, 1)$ with velocity vector $(3, -1)$, along a differentiable curve $(x(t), y(t))$ with $(x(0), y(0)) = (2, 1)$. How does the value of $f(x, y) = x y^2$ along the curve change at time $t = 0$, with $g(t) = f(x(t), y(t))$?

- It increases, since $g'(0) = 7$
- It increases, since $g'(0) = 11$
- It decreases, since $g'(0) = -4$
- It decreases, since $g'(0) = -1$

## Explanation

By the derivative along a curve, $g'(0)$ is the dot product of the gradient of $f$ at $(2, 1)$ with the velocity vector $(x'(0), y'(0)) = (3, -1)$. The partial derivatives are $f_x = y^2$ and $f_y = 2xy$, so $\nabla f(2, 1) = (1, 4)$. Therefore
$$
g'(0) = 1 \cdot 3 + 4 \cdot (-1) = -1 .
$$
The derivative is negative, so at time $t = 0$ the value of $f$ along the curve **decreases**. The value $11$ comes from swapping the two components of the gradient.
