Which of the following functions is differentiable at the point $(0, 0)$?

- $f(x, y) = e^{x} \cos y$
- $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$
- $f(x, y) = |x| + y$
- $f(x, y) = \sqrt[3]{x} + y$

## Explanation

For $e^{x} \cos y$ we have $f_x = e^{x} \cos y$ and $f_y = -e^{x} \sin y$, which are continuous on the whole plane. By the **total differential theorem** the function is therefore differentiable at every point, in particular at $(0, 0)$. The other three functions have no partial derivative with respect to $x$ at the origin, and a function differentiable at a point has both partial derivatives at that point. For $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ and for $|x| + y$ the difference quotient with respect to $x$ is $\dfrac{|h|}{h}$, which has no limit. For $\sqrt[3]{x} + y$ the quotient is $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{h}}{h} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{h^2}}$, which tends to $+\infty$.
