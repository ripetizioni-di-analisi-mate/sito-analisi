For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 y$, in which direction does $f$ increase most rapidly starting from the point $(1, 2)$?

- In the direction of the vector $(4, 1)$
- In the direction of the vector $(1, 4)$
- In the direction of the vector $(-4, -1)$
- In the direction of the vector $(2, 1)$

## Explanation

The gradient, when it is not zero, gives the direction in which the function increases most rapidly. The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2xy$ and $f_y = x^2$, so at the point $(1, 2)$ we have $\nabla f(1, 2) = (4, 1)$. The gradient is not zero, therefore $f$ increases most rapidly in the direction of the vector $(4, 1)$. The opposite vector $(-4, -1)$ gives instead the direction in which $f$ decreases most rapidly.
