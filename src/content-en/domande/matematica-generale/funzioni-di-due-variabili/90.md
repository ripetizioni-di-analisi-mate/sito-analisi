What is the set of points in the plane that are equidistant from $A = (1, 0)$ and $B = (3, 2)$?

- The line $x - y = 1$
- The circle centered at $(2, 1)$ with radius $\sqrt{2}$
- The line $x + y = 3$
- The line $2x + y = 5$

## Explanation

The point $(x, y)$ is equidistant from $A$ and $B$ if and only if the squares of the two distances are equal:
$$
(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = (x - 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 .
$$
Expanding, the terms $x^2$ and $y^2$ cancel and we are left with $-2x + 1 = -6x - 4y + 13$, that is, $4x + 4y = 12$. Therefore the required set is **the line $x + y = 3$**, which passes through the midpoint $(2, 1)$ of the segment $AB$ and is perpendicular to $AB$. The line $x - y = 1$ is the one through $A$ and $B$, and the circle centered at $(2, 1)$ with radius $\sqrt{2}$ is the one having $AB$ as a diameter. The line $2x + y = 5$ passes through the midpoint but is not perpendicular to $AB$.
