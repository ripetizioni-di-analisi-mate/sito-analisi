Using the linear approximation of $f(x, y) = \sqrt{xy}$ at the point $(4, 9)$, which approximate value is obtained for $f(4.04,\ 8.97)$?

- $6.04$
- $6.02$
- $6.03$
- $5.98$

## Explanation

At the point $(4, 9)$ we have $f(4, 9) = \sqrt{36} = 6$. The partial derivatives are $f_x = \dfrac{y}{2\sqrt{xy}}$ and $f_y = \dfrac{x}{2\sqrt{xy}}$, continuous where $xy > 0$, so $f$ is differentiable at $(4, 9)$ and $f_x(4, 9) = \dfrac{9}{12} = \dfrac{3}{4}$, $f_y(4, 9) = \dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac{1}{3}$. The increments are $dx = 0.04$ and $dy = -0.03$, because $y$ decreases. By the **linear approximation**
$$
f(4.04,\ 8.97) \approx 6 + \frac{3}{4} \cdot 0.04 + \frac{1}{3} \cdot (-0.03) = 6 + 0.03 - 0.01 = 6.02 .
$$
