Using the linear approximation of $f(x, y) = \ln(x^2 + y)$ at the point $(1, 0)$, which approximate value is obtained for $f(1{,}02;\ -0{,}01)$?

- $0{,}05$
- $0{,}03$
- $1{,}03$
- $0{,}01$

## Explanation

At the point $(1, 0)$ we have $f(1, 0) = \ln 1 = 0$. The chain rule gives $f_x = \dfrac{2x}{x^2 + y}$ and $f_y = \dfrac{1}{x^2 + y}$, which are continuous where $x^2 + y > 0$. Therefore $f$ is differentiable at $(1, 0)$ and $f_x(1, 0) = 2$, $f_y(1, 0) = 1$. The increments are $dx = 0{,}02$ and $dy = -0{,}01$, because $y$ decreases. Using the **linear approximation**
$$
f(1{,}02;\ -0{,}01) \approx 0 + 2 \cdot 0{,}02 + 1 \cdot (-0{,}01) = 0{,}04 - 0{,}01 = 0{,}03 .
$$
