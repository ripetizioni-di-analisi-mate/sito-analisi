Which of the following functions, defined for $x > 0$ and $y > 0$, is homogeneous?

- $f(x, y) = x^2 + y$
- $f(x, y) = \ln(xy)$
- $f(x, y) = xy + x$
- $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^3 + y^3}$

## Explanation

For $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^3 + y^3}$ and $t > 0$ we have
$$
f(tx, ty) = \sqrt{t^3 (x^3 + y^3)} = t^{\frac{3}{2}} \sqrt{x^3 + y^3} ,
$$
so $f$ is homogeneous of degree $\dfrac{3}{2}$. For $\ln(xy)$ we have $\ln(t^2 xy) = 2 \ln t + \ln(xy)$, which is not a multiple of $\ln(xy)$. The functions $x^2 + y$ and $xy + x$ have terms of different degrees, so they are not homogeneous.
