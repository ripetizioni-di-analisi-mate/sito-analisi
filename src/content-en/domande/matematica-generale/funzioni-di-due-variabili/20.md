What is the natural domain of $f(x, y) = \ln(y - x^2) + \sqrt{1 - y}$?

- $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 < y \leq 1 \}$
- $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 \leq y \leq 1 \}$
- $D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 < y < 1 \}$
- $D = \{ (x, y) : y < x^2, \ y \leq 1 \}$

## Explanation

The logarithm requires a strictly positive argument and the square root a nonnegative argument, so the conditions to be satisfied together are
$$
\begin{cases}
y - x^2 > 0 \\[4pt]
1 - y \geq 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
y > x^2 \\[4pt]
y \leq 1
\end{cases}
$$
The first condition gives the points above the parabola $y = x^2$, with the parabola excluded; the second gives the points below the line $y = 1$, with the line included. The domain is therefore the region between the parabola and the line, that is, $x^2 < y \leq 1$.
