What value does $\dfrac{\sin(x^2 + y^2)}{x^2 + y^2}$ approach as the point $(x, y)$ approaches the origin?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- The limit does not exist
- $1$

## Explanation

The function depends on $x$ and $y$ only through $t = x^2 + y^2$, the square of the distance from the origin. As $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$ we have $t \to 0$ with $t > 0$, from whatever direction the origin is approached. Therefore the limit coincides with
$$
\lim_{t \to 0^+} \frac{\sin t}{t} = 1 ,
$$
which is the standard limit of the sine. Hence the limit equals **$1$**.
