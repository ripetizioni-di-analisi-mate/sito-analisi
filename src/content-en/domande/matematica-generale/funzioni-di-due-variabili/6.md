- $\nabla f(1, 1) = (1, 2)$
- $\nabla f(1, 1) = (1, -1)$
- $\nabla f(1, 1) = (1, 0)$
- $\nabla f(1, 1) = (0, 1)$

## Explanation

Differentiating with respect to $x$ with $y$ constant gives $f_x = \ln y + e^{x - y}$, because the derivative of $x - y$ with respect to $x$ is $1$. Differentiating with respect to $y$ with $x$ constant gives $f_y = \dfrac{x}{y} - e^{x - y}$, because the derivative of $x - y$ with respect to $y$ is $-1$. At the point $(1, 1)$ we have $\ln 1 = 0$ and $e^{0} = 1$, so $f_x(1, 1) = 0 + 1 = 1$ and $f_y(1, 1) = 1 - 1 = 0$. Therefore the **gradient** is $\nabla f(1, 1) = (1, 0)$.
