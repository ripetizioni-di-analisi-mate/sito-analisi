Which of the following equations defines $z$ as a function of $(x, y)$ whose domain is the whole plane $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 1}$
- $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$
- $z^2 = x^2 + y^2$
- $z = \ln(x^2 + y^2)$

## Explanation

A function of two variables assigns exactly one number to each point of its domain. For every $(x, y)$ we have $x^2 + y^2 + 1 \geq 1 > 0$, so the square root is defined and gives a single value; therefore **$z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 1}$** defines a function on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$. The equation $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ has no solution in $z$ for $(x, y) = (2, 0)$ and has two, $z = 1$ and $z = -1$, for $(x, y) = (0, 0)$. The equation $z^2 = x^2 + y^2$ gives two values, $z = 1$ and $z = -1$, for $(x, y) = (1, 0)$. The logarithm $\ln(x^2 + y^2)$ is not defined at the origin, because its argument equals $0$ there.
