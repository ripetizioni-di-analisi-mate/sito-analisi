What is the gradient of $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ at the point $(3, 4)$?

- $\nabla f(3, 4) = \left( \dfrac{3}{10}, \dfrac{2}{5} \right)$
- $\nabla f(3, 4) = \left( \dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$
- $\nabla f(3, 4) = (6, 8)$
- $\nabla f(3, 4) = \left( \dfrac{4}{5}, \dfrac{3}{5} \right)$

## Explanation

By the chain rule, the derivative of $\sqrt{u}$ is $\dfrac{u'}{2\sqrt{u}}$. With $u = x^2 + y^2$ the derivative of $u$ with respect to $x$ is $2x$, so $f_x = \dfrac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$; in the same way $f_y = \dfrac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$. At the point $(3, 4)$ we have $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$. Therefore the **gradient** is $\nabla f(3, 4) = \left( \dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$.
