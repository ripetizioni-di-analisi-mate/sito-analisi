A consumer has a differentiable utility function $U(x, y)$. At the current bundle the marginal utilities are $U_x = 6$ and $U_y = 2$. The quantity of the first good decreases by $0.3$ units. According to the differential, by how much must the quantity of the second good increase for the utility to stay the same?

- $0.1$
- $0.9$
- $1.8$
- $3$

## Explanation

According to the **differential** the change in utility is approximately $U_x\,\Delta x + U_y\,\Delta y$. For the utility to stay the same this change must be zero, so $6\,\Delta x + 2\,\Delta y = 0$. With $\Delta x = -0.3$ one obtains $2\,\Delta y = 1.8$, therefore $\Delta y = 0.9$. The same value is obtained by multiplying the decrease $0.3$ by the marginal rate of substitution $\dfrac{U_x}{U_y} = 3$. The value $0.1$ comes from using the ratio $\dfrac{U_y}{U_x}$ instead of $\dfrac{U_x}{U_y}$, and $1.8$ is the loss of utility $6 \cdot 0.3$, which must still be divided by $U_y = 2$.
