Let $f(x, y) = x + y$ for $y \geq 0$ and $f(x, y) = x^2 - y$ for $y < 0$. At which points of the plane is $f$ continuous?

- At every point except those on the $x$-axis other than $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$
- At every point except those on the $x$-axis
- At every point of the plane
- At every point except $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$

## Explanation

In the open half-plane $y > 0$ the function coincides with the polynomial $x + y$, and in the open half-plane $y < 0$ with the polynomial $x^2 - y$, so it is continuous at every point off the $x$-axis. Now consider a point $(x_0, 0)$ on the $x$-axis, where $f(x_0, 0) = x_0$. Approaching from $y \geq 0$ the values of $f$ tend to $x_0$, and approaching from $y < 0$ they tend to $x_0^2$. Therefore $f$ is continuous at $(x_0, 0)$ if and only if $x_0^2 = x_0$, that is, for $x_0 = 0$ or $x_0 = 1$. The function is thus continuous **at every point except those on the $x$-axis other than $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$**.
