What is $\displaystyle\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2}$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

For $(x, y) \neq (0, 0)$ we have $x^2 \leq x^2 + y^2$, so $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2} \leq 1$. It follows that
$$
\left| \frac{x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2} \right| \leq |y| \leq \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} ,
$$
that is, the absolute value of the function is less than or equal to the distance of the point from the origin. Given $\varepsilon > 0$, we choose $\delta = \varepsilon$, and then $0 < \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} < \delta$ implies $|f(x, y) - 0| < \varepsilon$. Therefore, by the definition of **limit**, the limit equals $0$.
