For the production function $Q(K, L) = 2K + 3L$, with $K > 0$ and $L > 0$, which statement about the marginal rate of technical substitution $\dfrac{Q_L}{Q_K}$ is true?

- It equals $\dfrac{2}{3}$ at every point
- It depends on the ratio $\dfrac{K}{L}$
- It equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$ at every point
- It equals $-\dfrac{3}{2}$ at every point

## Explanation

The marginal products are $Q_K = 2$ and $Q_L = 3$, both constant. The marginal rate of technical substitution is therefore
$$
\frac{Q_L}{Q_K} = \frac{3}{2}
$$
at every point. Indeed the isoquants $2K + 3L = Q_0$ are lines with slope $-\dfrac{3}{2}$ in the $(L, K)$ plane, and the rate is the slope with the opposite sign. The rate depends on $\dfrac{K}{L}$ for Cobb-Douglas functions, which are not linear.
