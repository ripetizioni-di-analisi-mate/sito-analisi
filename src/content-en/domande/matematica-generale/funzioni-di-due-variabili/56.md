For the function $f(x, y) = \dfrac{y}{x}$, defined for $x \neq 0$, what are the level curves?

- The hyperbolas $xy = k$
- The lines $y = x + k$
- The parabolas $y = kx^2$
- The lines $y = kx$ with the origin removed

## Explanation

The level curve $k$ consists of the points of the domain where $\dfrac{y}{x} = k$. For $x \neq 0$ this equality is equivalent to $y = kx$, so the level curve $k$ is the line $y = kx$ without the points with $x = 0$. The only point of that line with $x = 0$ is the origin. Therefore the level curves are **the lines through the origin $y = kx$ with the origin removed**, one for each $k \in \mathbb{R}$; the $y$-axis is not a level curve, because it is not contained in the domain.
