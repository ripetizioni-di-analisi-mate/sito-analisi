On the hyperbola $xy = 1$ the function $f(x, y) = x + y$ is studied with the substitution method. What is the conclusion?

- $(1, 1)$ is a constrained maximum point, with $f = 2$, and $(-1, -1)$ is a constrained minimum point, with $f = -2$
- $f$ has no extrema on the constraint, because $\nabla f = (1, 1)$ never vanishes
- $(1, 1)$ is a constrained minimum point and $(-1, -1)$ is a constrained maximum point, and on the constraint $f$ has neither an absolute maximum nor an absolute minimum
- $(1, 1)$ is the absolute minimum point of $f$ on the constraint, and there are no other constrained extrema

## Explanation

Solving the constraint gives $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ with $x \neq 0$, so we study $\varphi(x) = x + \dfrac{1}{x}$. The derivative $\varphi'(x) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ vanishes for $x = \pm 1$, and the second derivative $\varphi''(x) = \dfrac{2}{x^3}$ equals $2$ at $x = 1$ and $-2$ at $x = -1$. Therefore $(1, 1)$ is a **constrained minimum point**, with $f = 2$, and $(-1, -1)$ is a **constrained maximum point**, with $f = -2$. These extrema are only local, because $\varphi(x)$ tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$ and to $-\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$. The value at the constrained maximum is smaller than the value at the constrained minimum, because the two points lie on two different branches of the hyperbola.
