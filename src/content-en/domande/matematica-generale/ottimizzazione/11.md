A monopolist sells two goods with inverse demands $p_1 = 40 - q_1$ and $p_2 = 60 - 2q_2$ and has cost $C(q_1, q_2) = q_1^2 + q_2^2 + 100$. What is the maximum profit?

- $500$ euros
- $325$ euros
- $400$ euros
- $700$ euros

## Explanation

The revenue is $p_1 q_1 + p_2 q_2$, so the profit is
$$
\pi(q_1, q_2) = (40 - q_1)\,q_1 + (60 - 2q_2)\,q_2 - q_1^2 - q_2^2 - 100 = 40q_1 - 2q_1^2 + 60q_2 - 3q_2^2 - 100 .
$$
The first-order conditions are $\pi_{q_1} = 40 - 4q_1 = 0$ and $\pi_{q_2} = 60 - 6q_2 = 0$, therefore $q_1 = 10$ and $q_2 = 10$. The Hessian is $\begin{pmatrix} -4 & 0 \\ 0 & -6 \end{pmatrix}$, with $\det H = 24 > 0$ and $\pi_{q_1 q_1} = -4 < 0$, so by the **Hessian test** the point is a maximum point. The maximum profit is $\pi(10, 10) = 400 - 200 + 600 - 300 - 100 = 400$ euros.
