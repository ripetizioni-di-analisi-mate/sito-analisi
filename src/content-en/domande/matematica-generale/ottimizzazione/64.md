The function $f(x, y) = (x + y)\,e^{-(x^2 + y^2)}$ has two stationary points, one a local maximum and one a local minimum. What value does it take at the local maximum point?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{e}}$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = \big(1 - 2x(x + y)\big)\,e^{-(x^2 + y^2)}$ and $f_y = \big(1 - 2y(x + y)\big)\,e^{-(x^2 + y^2)}$. The exponential is positive, so the stationary points solve $2x(x + y) = 1$ and $2y(x + y) = 1$. Subtracting the two equations we obtain $2(x - y)(x + y) = 0$, and since $x + y \neq 0$ (otherwise the first equation would give $0 = 1$) we have $x = y$. Then $4x^2 = 1$, and the stationary points are $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$. At $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ the second derivatives are $f_{xx} = f_{yy} = -\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{e}}$ and $f_{xy} = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$, so $\det H = \dfrac{8}{e} > 0$ with $f_{xx} < 0$, and the point is a **local maximum** point. The value is $f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right) = 1 \cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$.
