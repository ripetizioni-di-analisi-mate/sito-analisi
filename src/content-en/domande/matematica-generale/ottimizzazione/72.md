The stationary point of $f(x, y) = x^2 - 4x + y^2$ does not belong to the rectangle $R = [0, 1] \times [-1, 1]$ shown in the figure. What is the absolute minimum of $f$ on $R$?

::grafico{poligoni="0,-1 1,-1 1,1 0,1" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=2 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The rectangle $R = [0, 1] \times [-1, 1]$"}

- $-4$
- $-2$
- $-3$
- $0$

## Explanation

The rectangle is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so the minimum exists by the extreme value theorem. The system $f_x = 2x - 4 = 0$, $f_y = 2y = 0$ gives the point $(2, 0)$, which does not belong to $R$, so there are no interior stationary points and the value $f(2, 0) = -4$ is not a candidate. Completing the square we write $f(x, y) = (x - 2)^2 + y^2 - 4$. On $R$ the term $(x - 2)^2$ is smallest for $x = 1$ and the term $y^2$ is smallest for $y = 0$. Hence the **absolute minimum** is $f(1, 0) = 1 - 4 = -3$, at the point $(1, 0)$ of the right side.
