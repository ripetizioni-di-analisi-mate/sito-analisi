Let $f$ be a function with continuous second partial derivatives and let $(1, 2)$ be a stationary point of $f$, with $f_{xx}(1, 2) = 2$, $f_{xy}(1, 2) = 1$ and $f_{yy}(1, 2) = 4$. Which approximate value does the second-order Taylor formula give for the difference $f(1.1,\ 1.9) - f(1, 2)$?

- $0$
- $0.04$
- $0.025$
- $0.02$

## Explanation

At the stationary point the first derivatives are zero, so the second-order Taylor formula gives
$$
f(1 + h, 2 + k) - f(1, 2) \approx \frac{1}{2}\left( f_{xx}\,h^2 + 2 f_{xy}\,h k + f_{yy}\,k^2 \right) .
$$
Here $h = 0.1$ and $k = -0.1$, therefore $h^2 = k^2 = 0.01$ and $hk = -0.01$. The quadratic form equals $2 \cdot 0.01 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot (-0.01) + 4 \cdot 0.01 = 0.04$. Hence the difference is approximately $\dfrac{0.04}{2} = 0.02$. The value is positive, in agreement with the fact that $\det H = 8 - 1 = 7 > 0$ and $f_{xx} > 0$, that is, $(1, 2)$ is a relative minimum point.
