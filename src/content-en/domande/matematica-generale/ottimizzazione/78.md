For the points of the circle $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 4$ shown in the figure, what is the maximum of $x^2 + y^2$, that is, of the squared distance from the origin?

::grafico{curve="1+2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno,O" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda="(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 4" titolo="The circle with center $(1, 0)$ and radius $2$ and the origin $O$"}

- $9$
- $3$
- $4$
- $5$

## Explanation

Expanding the constraint we obtain $x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 = 4$, that is, $x^2 + y^2 = 3 + 2x$. On the circle the function therefore equals $f = 3 + 2x$, and the coordinate $x$ varies in $[-1, 3]$, because the center is $(1, 0)$ and the radius is $2$. The function $3 + 2x$ is increasing, so the **maximum** is $3 + 2 \cdot 3 = 9$, at the point $(3, 0)$, and the minimum is $1$, at the point $(-1, 0)$. The Lagrange system gives the same two points as candidates, with $\lambda = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $9$ is the square of $3$, the largest distance of the origin from the points of the circle.
