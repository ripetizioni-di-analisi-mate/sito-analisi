The profit of a firm producing two goods is $\pi(q_1, q_2) = 60q_1 + 40q_2 - q_1^2 - 3q_1 q_2 - q_2^2$. Which statement about its only stationary point is true?

- It is a saddle point, so it does not give the maximum profit
- It is a maximum point, because $\pi_{q_1 q_1} < 0$ and $\pi_{q_2 q_2} < 0$
- It is a minimum point
- The Hessian test does not allow a conclusion

## Explanation

The first-order conditions are
$$
\begin{cases}
\pi_{q_1} = 60 - 2q_1 - 3q_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
\pi_{q_2} = 40 - 3q_1 - 2q_2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Multiplying the first equation by $3$ and the second by $2$ and subtracting, we obtain $100 - 5q_2 = 0$, so $q_2 = 20$ and, from the first equation, $q_1 = 0$. The Hessian is $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & -3 \\ -3 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$, with $\det H = 4 - 9 = -5 < 0$, therefore by the **Hessian test** the point $(0, 20)$ is a **saddle point**. The negative sign of $\pi_{q_1 q_1}$ and of $\pi_{q_2 q_2}$ is not enough for a maximum, because the test also requires $\det H > 0$.
