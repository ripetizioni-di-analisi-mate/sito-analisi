The profit of a firm producing two goods is $\pi(q_1, q_2) = 60q_1 + 40q_2 - q_1^2 - 3q_1 q_2 - q_2^2$. Only quantities $q_1,q_2\geq0$ are feasible. How does profit vary near its only stationary point?

- Some nearby feasible quantities give higher profit and others give lower profit
- Profit falls for every sufficiently small feasible change
- Profit rises for every sufficiently small feasible change
- Profit is constant in a feasible neighborhood

## Explanation

The first-order conditions are
$$
\begin{cases}
\pi_{q_1} = 60 - 2q_1 - 3q_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
\pi_{q_2} = 40 - 3q_1 - 2q_2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Multiplying the first equation by $3$ and the second by $2$ and subtracting, we obtain $100 - 5q_2 = 0$, so $q_2 = 20$ and, from the first equation, $q_1 = 0$. The point is on the boundary of the domain. For $0<t<20$, profit at $(t,20-t)$ rises by $t^2$, whereas profit at $(t,20)$ falls by $t^2$. Both pairs are feasible and approach $(0,20)$ as $t$ approaches zero.
