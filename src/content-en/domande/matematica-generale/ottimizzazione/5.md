For the function $f(x, y) = e^{x}\,(x + y^2)$, what is the value of $f$ at its relative minimum point?

- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $-1$
- $-e$

## Explanation

By the product rule we have $f_x = e^{x}\,(x + y^2 + 1)$, while $f_y = 2y\,e^{x}$. Since $e^{x} > 0$, the system $f_x = 0$, $f_y = 0$ is equivalent to $x + y^2 + 1 = 0$ and $y = 0$, so the only stationary point is $(-1, 0)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = e^{x}\,(x + y^2 + 2)$, $f_{xy} = 2y\,e^{x}$ and $f_{yy} = 2e^{x}$, which at $(-1, 0)$ equal $\dfrac{1}{e}$, $0$ and $\dfrac{2}{e}$. Therefore $\det H = \dfrac{2}{e^2} > 0$ and $f_{xx} = \dfrac{1}{e} > 0$, so by the **Hessian test** $(-1, 0)$ is a relative minimum point. The minimum value is $f(-1, 0) = e^{-1} \cdot (-1) = -\dfrac{1}{e}$.
