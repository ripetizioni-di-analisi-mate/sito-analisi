What is the minimum distance of the point $A = (0, 2)$ from the parabola $y = x^2$ shown in the figure?

::grafico{f="x^2" punti="0,2,pieno,A" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=4 uguale=1 legenda="y = x^2" titolo="The parabola $y = x^2$ and the point $A = (0, 2)$"}

- $\dfrac{7}{4}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$

## Explanation

The square root is increasing, so the distance from $A$ is smallest where its square $f(x, y) = x^2 + (y - 2)^2$ is smallest. On the parabola we have $x^2 = y$, so we study $\varphi(y) = y + (y - 2)^2$ with $y \geq 0$. The derivative $\varphi'(y) = 2y - 3$ vanishes for $y = \dfrac{3}{2}$, is negative before and positive after, so $y = \dfrac{3}{2}$ is the minimum point, with $\varphi\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right) = \dfrac{3}{2} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{7}{4}$. The **minimum distance** is therefore $\sqrt{\dfrac{7}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$, at the points $\left(\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$. The vertex $(0, 0)$ is at distance $2$ from $A$, more than $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$.
