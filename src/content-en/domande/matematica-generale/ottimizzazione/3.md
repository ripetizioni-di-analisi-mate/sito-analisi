For the function $f(x, y) = x^3 - 12x + y^2$, which classification of the stationary points is correct?

- $(2, 0)$ is a saddle point and $(-2, 0)$ is a local maximum point
- $(2, 0)$ is a local minimum point and $(-2, 0)$ is a saddle point
- $(2, 0)$ is a local minimum point and $(-2, 0)$ is a local maximum point
- $(2, 0)$ is a local maximum point and $(-2, 0)$ is a local minimum point

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 3x^2 - 12$ and $f_y = 2y$, so the stationary points satisfy $x^2 = 4$ and $y = 0$, that is, they are $(2, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 6x$, $f_{xy} = 0$ and $f_{yy} = 2$, therefore $\det H = 12x$. At $(2, 0)$ we have $\det H = 24 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 12 > 0$, so by the **Hessian test** $(2, 0)$ is a **local minimum point**. At $(-2, 0)$ we have $\det H = -24 < 0$, so $(-2, 0)$ is a **saddle point**, even though $f_{xx} = -12 < 0$.
