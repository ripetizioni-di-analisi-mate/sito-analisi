For a consumer the two goods are perfect substitutes, with utility $U(x, y) = x + 2y$. With $30$ euros and prices of $1$ and $3$ euros, which bundle does the consumer choose, with $x, y \geq 0$?

::grafico{poligoni="0,0 30,0 0,10" xmin=-4 xmax=36 ymin=-2 ymax=12 legenda=0 titolo="The affordable bundles, with $x + 3y \leq 30$, $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$"}

- $(30, 0)$
- $(0, 10)$
- $(15, 5)$
- $(12, 6)$

## Explanation

The utility is increasing in $x$ and in $y$, so the consumer spends the whole income and the optimal bundle lies on the budget line $x + 3y = 30$. On this line $x = 30 - 3y$, with $y \in [0, 10]$, and the utility equals $U = 30 - 3y + 2y = 30 - y$. This function is decreasing in $y$, so the maximum is at $y = 0$, that is, at the bundle $(30, 0)$, with $U = 30$. The optimal bundle is a **corner solution**, and there the condition $\dfrac{U_x}{U_y} = \dfrac{p_1}{p_2}$ is not satisfied, because $\dfrac{1}{2} \neq \dfrac{1}{3}$. Indeed each euro spent on the first good gives $1$ unit of utility, while spent on the second it gives $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
