What is the tangent line at the point $(2, 1)$ to the level curve of $f(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2$ through that point?

- $2x + y = 5$
- $y = 2x - 3$
- $x + 2y = 4$
- $x + 4y = 6$

## Explanation

The gradient $\nabla f = (2x, 8y)$ equals $(4, 8)$ at the point $(2, 1)$, and it is perpendicular to the level curve $x^2 + 4y^2 = 8$ through $(2, 1)$. The tangent line is therefore the line through $(2, 1)$ perpendicular to the vector $(4, 8)$, that is, $4(x - 2) + 8(y - 1) = 0$. Dividing by $4$ we obtain $x - 2 + 2(y - 1) = 0$, that is, $x + 2y = 4$. By the geometric interpretation of Lagrange multipliers, the point $(2, 1)$ is therefore a candidate extremum of $f$ on the constraint $x + 2y = 4$.
