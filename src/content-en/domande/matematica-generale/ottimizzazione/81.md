For a consumer the two goods are perfect complements, and the utility is $U(x, y) = \min\{x, 2y\}$. The prices are $2$ euros for the first good and $1$ euro for the second, and the income is $50$ euros. Which bundle maximizes the utility?

- $(12.5, 25)$
- $(25, 0)$
- $(15, 20)$
- $(20, 10)$

## Explanation

The function $U$ has no partial derivatives at the points of the line $x = 2y$, so the Lagrange multiplier theorem does not apply and we reason directly on the constraint $2x + y = 50$. If $x > 2y$, the utility equals $2y$, and moving part of the spending from the first to the second good increases $2y$. If $x < 2y$, the utility equals $x$, and moving part of the spending from the second to the first good increases $x$. Therefore at the optimal bundle $x = 2y$, and the constraint becomes $4y + y = 50$, that is, $y = 10$ and $x = 20$. The optimal bundle is therefore $(20, 10)$, with $U = 20$, while $(15, 20)$ gives $U = 15$, $(12.5, 25)$ gives $U = 12.5$ and $(25, 0)$ gives $U = 0$.
