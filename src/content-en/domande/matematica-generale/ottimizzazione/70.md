What is the absolute minimum of $f(x, y) = y - x$ on the set $D = \{(x, y) : x^2 \leq y \leq 1\}$ shown in the figure?

::grafico{f="x^2;1" area="1;x^2;-1;1" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=2 uguale=1 legenda="y = x^2;y = 1" titolo="The set $D$ between the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 1$"}

- $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The set $D$ is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so the minimum exists by the extreme value theorem. The gradient $(-1, 1)$ never vanishes, so the minimum lies on the boundary. On the segment $y = 1$, with $x \in [-1, 1]$, the function equals $1 - x$, which has minimum $0$ at $x = 1$. On the arc of the parabola $y = x^2$, with $x \in [-1, 1]$, we study $\varphi(x) = x^2 - x$, whose derivative $2x - 1$ vanishes at $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, with $\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{4}$. Comparing with the values at the vertices, $f(-1, 1) = 2$ and $f(1, 1) = 0$, the **absolute minimum** is $-\dfrac{1}{4}$, at the point $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{4}\right)$.
