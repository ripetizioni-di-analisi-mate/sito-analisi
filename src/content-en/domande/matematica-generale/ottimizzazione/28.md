What is the Hessian matrix of $f(x, y) = x^3 y - 2xy^2$ at the point $(1, 1)$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 6 & -1 \\ -1 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 6 & 3 \\ 3 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 6 & -1 \\ -1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 6 & -1 \\ -1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The first derivatives are $f_x = 3x^2 y - 2y^2$ and $f_y = x^3 - 4xy$. Differentiating again gives $f_{xx} = 6xy$, $f_{yy} = -4x$ and $f_{xy} = f_{yx} = 3x^2 - 4y$, where the two mixed derivatives coincide by Schwarz's theorem. At the point $(1, 1)$ we therefore have $f_{xx} = 6$, $f_{xy} = 3 - 4 = -1$ and $f_{yy} = -4$. Hence the Hessian matrix is
$$
H(1, 1) = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -1 \\ -1 & -4 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
