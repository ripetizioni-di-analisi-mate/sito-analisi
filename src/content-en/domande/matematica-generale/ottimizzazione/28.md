What is the absolute maximum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + 2y^2 - 2x$ on the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$?

- $9$
- $8$
- $0$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The disk is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The system $f_x = 2x - 2 = 0$, $f_y = 4y = 0$ gives the interior stationary point $(1, 0)$, with $f(1, 0) = -1$. On the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ we have $y^2 = 4 - x^2$, so $f = x^2 + 8 - 2x^2 - 2x = -x^2 - 2x + 8$, with $x \in [-2, 2]$. The derivative of this function is $-2x - 2$, which vanishes for $x = -1$, and the values are $9$ for $x = -1$, $8$ for $x = -2$ and $0$ for $x = 2$. The largest of the values found is $9$, therefore the **absolute maximum** is $9$, at the points $(-1, \pm\sqrt{3})$.
