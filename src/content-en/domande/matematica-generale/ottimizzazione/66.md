We look for the line $y = mx + q$ closest to the three points $(0, 1)$, $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 2)$ shown in the figure, in the least squares sense: the parameters $m$ and $q$ minimize the sum of the squared deviations
$$
S(m, q) = (q - 1)^2 + (m + q - 2)^2 + (2m + q - 2)^2 .
$$
Which line is obtained?

::grafico{punti="0,1,pieno;1,2,pieno;2,2,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The three points $(0, 1)$, $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 2)$"}

- $y = \dfrac{1}{2}\,x + \dfrac{7}{6}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{2}\,x + 1$
- $y = x + 1$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{2}\,x + \dfrac{5}{3}$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives of $S$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_m &= 2(m + q - 2) + 4(2m + q - 2) = 10m + 6q - 12, \\[4pt]
S_q &= 2(q - 1) + 2(m + q - 2) + 2(2m + q - 2) = 6m + 6q - 10 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Subtracting the second equation of the system $S_m = 0$, $S_q = 0$ from the first gives $4m - 2 = 0$, so $m = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The second equation then gives $6q = 10 - 3 = 7$, that is, $q = \dfrac{7}{6}$. The Hessian of $S$ is constant, with $S_{mm} = 10$, $S_{mq} = 6$ and $S_{qq} = 6$, therefore $\det H = 60 - 36 = 24 > 0$ and $S_{mm} > 0$, and the stationary point is a **minimum**. The least squares line is therefore $y = \dfrac{1}{2}\,x + \dfrac{7}{6}$, with $S = \dfrac{1}{6}$, while the line $y = \dfrac{1}{2}\,x + 1$, which passes through the first and the third point, gives $S = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
