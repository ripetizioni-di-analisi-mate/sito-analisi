Along the circle parametrized by $\gamma(t) = (\cos t, \sin t)$ we study the function $f(x, y) = x^2 y$, that is, $\varphi(t) = f(\cos t, \sin t)$. What is $\varphi'\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$?

- $\dfrac{5}{8}$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{5}{8}$

## Explanation

By the derivative along a curve we have $\varphi'(t) = \nabla f(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t)$. For $t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ the point is $\gamma\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$, and the tangent vector is $\gamma'\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = \left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, because $\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t, \cos t)$. The gradient is $\nabla f = (2xy, x^2)$, which at the point equals $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{4}\right)$. Hence
$$
\varphi'\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) + \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = -\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{8} = -\frac{5}{8} .
$$
The value is different from $0$, so by Fermat's theorem $t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is not an extremum point of $\varphi$, and the point $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$ is not an extremum of $f$ on the circle.
