What is the absolute maximum of $f(x, y) = xy$ on the triangle $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$, $x + y \leq 4$?

- $8$
- $2$
- $4$
- $0$

## Explanation

The triangle is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The gradient $(y, x)$ vanishes only at the origin, which is a vertex, so there are no interior stationary points. On the sides $x = 0$ and $y = 0$ the function equals $0$. On the side $y = 4 - x$, with $x \in [0, 4]$, one studies $\varphi(x) = x\,(4 - x)$, whose derivative $4 - 2x$ vanishes for $x = 2$, where $\varphi(2) = 4$. The largest of the values found is $4$, therefore the **absolute maximum** is $4$, at the point $(2, 2)$.
