A firm has the production function $Q = \sqrt{LK}$ and pays $9$ euros per unit of labor and $4$ euros per unit of capital. What is the minimum cost of producing $Q = 6$?

- $97$ euros
- $78$ euros
- $72$ euros
- $12\sqrt{6}$ euros

## Explanation

The constraint $\sqrt{LK} = 6$ is equivalent to $LK = 36$, so we minimize $C = 9L + 4K$ with the constraint $LK - 36 = 0$. The Lagrange multiplier equations are
$$
\begin{cases}
9 = \lambda K \\[4pt]
4 = \lambda L \\[4pt]
LK = 36
\end{cases}
$$
Dividing the first equation by the second gives $\dfrac{K}{L} = \dfrac{9}{4}$, that is, $K = \dfrac{9}{4}\,L$, and the constraint becomes $\dfrac{9}{4}\,L^2 = 36$. Therefore $L = 4$, $K = 9$ and the cost is $C = 36 + 36 = 72$ euros. Substituting $K = \dfrac{36}{L}$ we have $C(L) = 9L + \dfrac{144}{L}$ for $L > 0$. Since $C'(L) = 9 - \dfrac{144}{L^2}$ is negative for $0 < L < 4$ and positive for $L > 4$, the point $(4, 9)$ is a **minimum** point on the constraint and the minimum cost is $72$ euros.
