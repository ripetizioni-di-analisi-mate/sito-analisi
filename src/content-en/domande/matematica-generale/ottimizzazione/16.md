The function $f(x, y) = x^4 - 2x^2 + y^2$ has the stationary points $(0, 0)$, $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$. Which statement is true?

- $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$ are relative but not absolute minimum points, $(0, 0)$ is a saddle point
- $(0, 0)$ is an absolute minimum point, $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$ are saddle points
- $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$ are absolute minimum points, $(0, 0)$ is a saddle point
- $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$ are absolute minimum points, $(0, 0)$ is a relative maximum point

## Explanation

The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 12x^2 - 4$, $f_{xy} = 0$ and $f_{yy} = 2$. At $(1, 0)$ and at $(-1, 0)$ we have $\det H = 8 \cdot 2 = 16 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 8 > 0$, so the two points are relative minimum points, with $f(\pm 1, 0) = -1$. At the origin we have $\det H = -4 \cdot 2 = -8 < 0$, so by the **Hessian test** the origin is a saddle point. Completing the square gives $f(x, y) = (x^2 - 1)^2 + y^2 - 1$, which is greater than or equal to $-1$ for every $(x, y)$. Therefore $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$ are **absolute minimum** points.
