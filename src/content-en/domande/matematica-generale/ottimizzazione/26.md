The function $f(x, y) = (y - x^2)(y - 2x^2)$ has the origin as a stationary point, with $\det H(0, 0) = 0$. Which statement is true?

- The origin is a relative minimum point, because it is one along every line through the origin
- The origin is an absolute minimum point
- The origin is a relative maximum point
- The origin is a saddle point

## Explanation

On the $x$-axis we have $f(x, 0) = 2x^4 > 0$ for $x \neq 0$. On the parabola $y = \dfrac{3}{2}\,x^2$, which lies between the parabolas $y = x^2$ and $y = 2x^2$, the two factors have opposite signs, and indeed
$$
f\left(x, \frac{3}{2}\,x^2\right) = \frac{1}{2}\,x^2 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\,x^2\right) = -\frac{x^4}{4} < 0 \qquad \text{for } x \neq 0 .
$$
In every disk centered at the origin there are therefore points where $f > 0 = f(0, 0)$ and points where $f < 0$, so the origin is a **saddle point**. Along every line through the origin the restriction of $f$ has a minimum at the origin, but the definition of relative minimum requires the comparison with all the points of a disk, including those of the parabola $y = \dfrac{3}{2}\,x^2$.
