The point $(1, 2)$ is the minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ on the line $x + 2y = 5$. With $g(x, y) = x + 2y - 5$, what is the multiplier $\lambda$ such that $\nabla f(1, 2) = \lambda\,\nabla g(1, 2)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $4$
- $2$
- $5$

## Explanation

The gradient of $f$ is $\nabla f = (2x, 2y)$, which at the point $(1, 2)$ equals $(2, 4)$. The gradient of $g$ is constant and equals $\nabla g = (1, 2)$. The condition $(2, 4) = \lambda\,(1, 2)$ gives $2 = \lambda$ from the first component and $4 = 2\lambda$ from the second, so $\lambda = 2$. The two gradients are therefore parallel, as the **Lagrange multiplier theorem** states.
