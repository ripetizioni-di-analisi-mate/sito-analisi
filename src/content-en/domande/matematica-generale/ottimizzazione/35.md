To find the extrema of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y$ subject to the constraint $x + y^2 = 1$ we use the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}(x, y, \lambda) = x^2 + y - \lambda\,(x + y^2 - 1)$. Which system is obtained by setting its partial derivatives equal to zero?

- $\begin{cases} 2x = \lambda \\ 1 = \lambda \\ x + y^2 = 1 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} 2x = 2\lambda y \\ 1 = \lambda \\ x + y^2 = 1 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} 2x = \lambda \\ 1 = 2\lambda y \\ x + y^2 = 1 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} 2x = \lambda \\ 0 = 2\lambda y \\ x + y^2 = 1 \end{cases}$

## Explanation

The derivative with respect to $x$ is $\mathcal{L}_x = 2x - \lambda$, because $f_x = 2x$ and $g_x = 1$. The derivative with respect to $y$ is $\mathcal{L}_y = 1 - 2\lambda y$, because $f_y = 1$ and $g_y = 2y$. The derivative with respect to $\lambda$ is $\mathcal{L}_\lambda = -(x + y^2 - 1)$, which vanishes exactly on the constraint. Hence the system consists of the equations $2x = \lambda$, $1 = 2\lambda y$ and $x + y^2 = 1$, to be satisfied simultaneously.
