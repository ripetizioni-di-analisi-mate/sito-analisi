To find the extrema of $f(x, y) = x + y$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, one substitutes $y = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$ and studies $\varphi(x) = x + \sqrt{1 - x^2}$ on $[-1, 1]$. What are the minimum of $\varphi$ and the minimum of $f$ on the circle?

- $-\sqrt{2}$ and $-\sqrt{2}$
- $-1$ and $-1$
- $-1$ and $-\sqrt{2}$
- $0$ and $-\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The derivative $\varphi'(x) = 1 - \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$ vanishes only for $x = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, where $\varphi$ has its maximum $\sqrt{2}$. At the endpoints we have $\varphi(-1) = -1$ and $\varphi(1) = 1$, so the minimum of $\varphi$ is $-1$. The substitution $y = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$, however, describes only the half circle with $y \geq 0$. On the whole circle, with $x = \cos t$ and $y = \sin t$, we have $f = \cos t + \sin t = \sqrt{2}\,\sin\left(t + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$, which has minimum $-\sqrt{2}$ at the point $\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$, where $y < 0$. Therefore the minimum of $\varphi$ is $-1$ and the **minimum** of $f$ on the circle is $-\sqrt{2}$.
