For the function $f(x, y) = x + y$ on the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 20$, what is the maximum?

- $2\sqrt{5}$
- $5$
- $4$
- $-5$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2 - 20$ the Lagrange system is
$$
\begin{cases}
1 = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
1 = 8\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + 4y^2 = 20
\end{cases}
$$
From the first two equations it follows that $\lambda \neq 0$ and $2\lambda x = 8\lambda y$, so $x = 4y$. Substituting into the constraint gives $20y^2 = 20$, therefore $y = \pm 1$ and the candidates are $(4, 1)$, with $f = 5$, and $(-4, -1)$, with $f = -5$. The gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 8y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which does not lie on the constraint. The ellipse is closed and bounded, so by the **Weierstrass theorem** the maximum exists and is attained at one of the two candidates. Therefore the maximum is $5$, at the point $(4, 1)$.
