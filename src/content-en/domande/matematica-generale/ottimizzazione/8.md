For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 - y^2$ on the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$, what is the absolute minimum?

- $0$
- $-2$
- $-4$
- $4$

## Explanation

The disk is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute minimum exists. The partial derivatives $f_x = 2x$ and $f_y = -2y$ vanish only at $(0, 0)$, so the only interior stationary point is the origin, where $f$ equals $0$. On the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ we have $y^2 = 4 - x^2$, so $f = x^2 - (4 - x^2) = 2x^2 - 4$, with $x \in [-2, 2]$. This function equals $-4$ for $x = 0$ and $4$ for $x = \pm 2$. The smallest of the values $0$, $-4$ and $4$ is $-4$, therefore the **absolute minimum** is $-4$, at the points $(0, \pm 2)$.
