Among the rectangles with sides parallel to the axes and all four vertices on the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 4$, like the one in the figure, what is the largest area?

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" poligoni="1.6,0.6 -1.6,0.6 -1.6,-0.6 1.6,-0.6" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=2 uguale=1 legenda="x^2 + 4y^2 = 4" titolo="A rectangle with its vertices on the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 4$"}

- $4$
- $2$
- $8$
- $2\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The rectangle with vertex $(x, y)$ in the first quadrant, with $x > 0$ and $y > 0$, has sides $2x$ and $2y$, so its area is $f(x, y) = 4xy$. With $g(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2 - 4$ the Lagrange system gives $4y = 2\lambda x$ and $4x = 8\lambda y$. The first equation gives $\lambda = \dfrac{2y}{x}$, and substituting into the second we obtain $4x = \dfrac{16y^2}{x}$, that is, $x^2 = 4y^2$. The constraint becomes $8y^2 = 4$, therefore $y = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $x = \sqrt{2}$, with area $4 \cdot \sqrt{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 4$. On the arc of the ellipse in the first quadrant the area tends to $0$ as the vertex approaches one of the axes and the candidate is unique, so the **largest area** is $4$.
