What is the maximum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3y^2$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$?

- $1$
- $3$
- $4$
- $\sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

From the constraint we get $x^2 = 1 - y^2$, with $y \in [-1, 1]$, and with the **substitution method** we obtain $\varphi(y) = 1 - y^2 + 3y^2 = 1 + 2y^2$. Since $0 \leq y^2 \leq 1$, the function $\varphi$ takes values between $1$, for $y = 0$, and $3$, for $y = \pm 1$. Therefore the **constrained maximum** is $3$, at the points $(0, \pm 1)$, and the minimum is $1$, at the points $(\pm 1, 0)$.
