For the function $f(x, y) = x^3 + 3xy - y^3$, which point is a relative minimum point?

- $(0, 0)$
- $(-1, 1)$
- $(1, 1)$
- $(1, -1)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 3x^2 + 3y$ and $f_y = 3x - 3y^2$, so the stationary points solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
y = -x^2 \\[4pt]
x = y^2
\end{cases}
$$
Substituting the first equation into the second gives $x = x^4$, that is, $x\,(x^3 - 1) = 0$, therefore the stationary points are $(0, 0)$ and $(1, -1)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 6x$, $f_{xy} = 3$ and $f_{yy} = -6y$, so $\det H = -36xy - 9$. At $(0, 0)$ we have $\det H = -9 < 0$, so the origin is a saddle point. At $(1, -1)$ we have $\det H = 27 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 6 > 0$, so by the **Hessian test** $(1, -1)$ is a **relative minimum point**.
