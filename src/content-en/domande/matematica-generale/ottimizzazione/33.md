For $f(x, y) = x - y$ on the parabola $y = x^2$, which statement is true?

- $f$ has maximum $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and no minimum
- $f$ has minimum $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and no maximum
- $f$ has maximum $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and no minimum
- $f$ has neither a maximum nor a minimum, because it has no stationary points

## Explanation

Substituting $y = x^2$ we obtain the function of one variable $\varphi(x) = x - x^2$, defined for every real $x$. The derivative $\varphi'(x) = 1 - 2x$ vanishes for $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, it is positive before and negative after, so $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is the absolute maximum point of $\varphi$, with $\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{4}$. Since $\varphi(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, the function has no minimum on the constraint. The gradient $(1, -1)$ of $f$ never vanishes, so $f$ has no free extrema; constrained extrema, instead, are found by comparing only the values on the parabola. Therefore $f$ has **maximum** $\dfrac{1}{4}$, at the point $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{4}\right)$, and no minimum.
