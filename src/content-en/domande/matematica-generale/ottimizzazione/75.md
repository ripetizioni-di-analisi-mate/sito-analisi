As the point $(x, y)$ moves along the curve $y = e^{-x}$, with $x \geq 0$, what is the largest value of the product $f(x, y) = xy$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $\dfrac{2}{e^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2e}$

## Explanation

Substituting $y = e^{-x}$ we study $\varphi(x) = x\,e^{-x}$ for $x \geq 0$. The derivative is $\varphi'(x) = (1 - x)\,e^{-x}$, positive for $0 \leq x < 1$ and negative for $x > 1$, so $\varphi$ has its absolute maximum at $x = 1$. Therefore the **maximum** of $f$ on the curve is $\varphi(1) = \dfrac{1}{e}$, at the point $\left(1, \dfrac{1}{e}\right)$. The value $\dfrac{2}{e^2} = \varphi(2)$ is smaller, because $\varphi$ is decreasing for $x > 1$.
