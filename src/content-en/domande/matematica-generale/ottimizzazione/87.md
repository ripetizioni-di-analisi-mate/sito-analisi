With an income of $10$ euros and a price of $1$ euro for both goods, a consumer chooses the bundle that maximizes $U(x, y) = 2\sqrt{x} + y$, with $x > 0$ and $y \geq 0$. How much does the consumer spend on the first good?

- $5$ euros
- $1$ euro
- $9$ euros
- $4$ euros

## Explanation

The utility is increasing in $x$ and in $y$, so the optimal bundle lies on the budget line $x + y = 10$. Substituting $y = 10 - x$ we study $\varphi(x) = 2\sqrt{x} + 10 - x$ for $0 < x \leq 10$. The derivative $\varphi'(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} - 1$ is positive for $x < 1$ and negative for $x > 1$, so $\varphi$ has its maximum at $x = 1$. The optimal bundle is hence $(1, 9)$, with utility $U = 2 + 9 = 11$, and the spending on the first good is $1$ euro. In this bundle the marginal rate of substitution $\dfrac{U_x}{U_y} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ equals $1$, the ratio of the prices.
