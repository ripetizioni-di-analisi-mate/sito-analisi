The function $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2\,(1 + x)^3$ has the origin as its only stationary point, and at the origin the second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = 0$ and $f_{yy} = 2$. Which statement is true?

- The origin is a relative minimum point, but it is not an absolute minimum point
- The origin is an absolute minimum point, because it is the only stationary point and it is a relative minimum
- The origin is a saddle point
- The origin is an absolute minimum point, because $f(x, 0) = x^2 \geq 0$ and $f(0, y) = y^2 \geq 0$

## Explanation

At the origin we have $\det H = 4 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 2 > 0$, so by the **Hessian test** the origin is a **relative minimum point**, with $f(0, 0) = 0$. For $x = -3$ we have $(1 + x)^3 = -8$, therefore $f(-3, y) = 9 - 8y^2$. In particular $f(-3, 2) = 9 - 32 = -23 < 0 = f(0, 0)$, so the origin is not an absolute minimum point. The sign of $f$ along the two axes is not enough to decide this, because the point $(-3, 2)$ lies on neither axis.
