What is the maximum of $f(x, y) = x^2 y$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 3$?

- $\sqrt{2}$
- $4$
- $2$
- $0$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 3$ the Lagrange system consists of the equations $2xy = 2\lambda x$, $x^2 = 2\lambda y$ and $x^2 + y^2 = 3$. If $x = 0$, the constraint gives $y = \pm\sqrt{3}$, and at these points $f = 0$. If $x \neq 0$, the first equation gives $y = \lambda$, so the second becomes $x^2 = 2y^2$, and the constraint gives $3y^2 = 3$, that is, $y = \pm 1$ and $x = \pm\sqrt{2}$. At these points $f = 2y$, which equals $2$ for $y = 1$ and $-2$ for $y = -1$. The gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 2y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which is not on the constraint. The circle is closed and bounded, therefore the **maximum** is $2$, at the points $(\sqrt{2}, 1)$ and $(-\sqrt{2}, 1)$.
