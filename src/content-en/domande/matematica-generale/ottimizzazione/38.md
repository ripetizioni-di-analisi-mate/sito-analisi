What is the maximum of $f(x, y) = 3x + 4y$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$?

- $5$
- $35$
- $25$
- $7$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 25$ the Lagrange multiplier equations are
$$
\begin{cases}
3 = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
4 = 2\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 = 25
\end{cases}
$$
From the first two equations it follows that $\lambda \neq 0$, $x = \dfrac{3}{2\lambda}$ and $y = \dfrac{4}{2\lambda}$, so $y = \dfrac{4}{3}\,x$. Substituting into the constraint we obtain $\dfrac{25}{9}\,x^2 = 25$, therefore $x = \pm 3$ and the candidates are $(3, 4)$, with $f = 25$, and $(-3, -4)$, with $f = -25$. The gradient $\nabla g = (2x, 2y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which does not lie on the constraint. The circle is closed and bounded, so by the **extreme value theorem** the maximum exists, and it equals $25$, at the point $(3, 4)$.
