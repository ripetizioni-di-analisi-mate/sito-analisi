For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 2y$ on the square $[0, 3] \times [0, 3]$, what is the absolute maximum?

- $3$
- $-2$
- $2$
- $6$

## Explanation

The square is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute maximum and minimum exist. The system $f_x = 2x - 2 = 0$, $f_y = 2y - 2 = 0$ gives the interior stationary point $(1, 1)$, with $f(1, 1) = -2$. On the sides $x = 0$ and $y = 0$ the function becomes $t^2 - 2t$, and on the sides $x = 3$ and $y = 3$ it becomes $t^2 - 2t + 3$, with $t \in [0, 3]$; both have their minimum at $t = 1$ and their maximum at $t = 3$. The candidates are therefore $(1, 1)$, $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 3)$, $(3, 1)$ and the vertices, with values
$$
-2, \quad -1, \quad -1, \quad 2, \quad 2, \qquad f(0, 0) = 0, \quad f(3, 0) = f(0, 3) = 3, \quad f(3, 3) = 6 .
$$
The largest value is $6$, therefore the **absolute maximum** is $6$, at the vertex $(3, 3)$.
