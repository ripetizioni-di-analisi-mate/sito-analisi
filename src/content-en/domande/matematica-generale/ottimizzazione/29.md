Consider the function $f(x, y) = xy$ on the closed first quadrant $Q = \{(x, y) : x \geq 0,\ y \geq 0\}$, which is closed but not bounded. What can be said about its absolute extrema on $Q$?

- The absolute minimum is $0$, attained only at the origin, and there is no absolute maximum
- There are no absolute extrema, because the Weierstrass theorem does not apply
- The absolute minimum and maximum exist by the Weierstrass theorem, because $Q$ is closed
- The absolute minimum is $0$, attained at all points of the two semi-axes, and there is no absolute maximum

## Explanation

The set $Q$ is not bounded, so the Weierstrass theorem does not apply. For $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$ we have $xy \geq 0$, and $f$ equals $0$ at all points with $x = 0$ or $y = 0$. Therefore the **absolute minimum** is $0$, attained at all points of the two semi-axes. Along the bisector we have $f(t, t) = t^2$, which tends to $+\infty$ as $t \to +\infty$, so $f$ has no absolute maximum. The Weierstrass theorem gives a sufficient condition, therefore when it does not apply an absolute extremum may still exist.
