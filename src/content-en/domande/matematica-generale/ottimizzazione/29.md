What is the absolute minimum of $f(x, y) = xy$ on the square $[-1, 2] \times [-1, 2]$?

- $-1$
- $0$
- $-4$
- $-2$

## Explanation

The square is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute minimum exists. The gradient $(y, x)$ vanishes only at the origin, which is interior, with $f(0, 0) = 0$. On each side one of the two variables is constant, so $f$ is a linear function of the other one and attains its maximum and minimum at the endpoints of the side, that is, at the vertices. At the vertices we have
$$
f(-1, -1) = 1, \qquad f(2, 2) = 4, \qquad f(2, -1) = f(-1, 2) = -2 .
$$
The smallest of the values found is $-2$, therefore the **absolute minimum** is $-2$, at the points $(2, -1)$ and $(-1, 2)$.
