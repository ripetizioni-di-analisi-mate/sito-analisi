Among the Lagrange candidates for the extrema of $f(x, y) = x^3 + y^3$ with the constraint $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ there is the point $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$. Which statement about the constrained maximum is true?

- The maximum is $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, at the point $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$
- The maximum is $\sqrt{2}$, at the point $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$
- The maximum is $1$, at the points $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$
- The maximum is $2$, at the point $(1, 1)$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 1$ the first two equations of the Lagrange system are $3x^2 = 2\lambda x$ and $3y^2 = 2\lambda y$. The first gives $x = 0$ or $x = \dfrac{2\lambda}{3}$, and the second gives $y = 0$ or $y = \dfrac{2\lambda}{3}$. This yields the points $(0, \pm 1)$ and $(\pm 1, 0)$, where $f$ equals $\pm 1$, and the points with $x = y$ on the constraint, $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$, where $f = \pm 2 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4} = \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The circle is closed and bounded and $\nabla g$ does not vanish on it, so the **maximum** is $1$, at the points $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$. At the symmetric point $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$ the function equals only $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} < 1$.
