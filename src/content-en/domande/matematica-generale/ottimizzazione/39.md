What is the minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ on the ellipse $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 3$?

- $3$
- $2$
- $6$
- $\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - 3$ the Lagrange multiplier equations are
$$
\begin{cases}
2x = \lambda\,(2x + y) \\[4pt]
2y = \lambda\,(x + 2y) \\[4pt]
x^2 + xy + y^2 = 3
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting the second equation from the first we obtain $2\,(x - y) = \lambda\,(x - y)$, that is, $(x - y)(2 - \lambda) = 0$. If $x = y$, the constraint becomes $3x^2 = 3$, which gives the candidates $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, -1)$, where $f = 2$. If $\lambda = 2$, the first equation becomes $2x = 4x + 2y$, that is, $y = -x$. The constraint then becomes $x^2 = 3$, which gives the candidates $(\sqrt{3}, -\sqrt{3})$ and $(-\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3})$, where $f = 6$. The gradient $\nabla g = (2x + y, x + 2y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which does not lie on the constraint. The ellipse is closed and bounded, so by the **extreme value theorem** the minimum exists, and it equals $2$.
