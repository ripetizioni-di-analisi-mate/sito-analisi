At which point does $f(x, y) = x^2 y$ attain its maximum on the line $x + y = 3$, with $x > 0$ and $y > 0$?

- $(1, 2)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$
- $(0, 3)$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x + y - 3$ the Lagrange system is
$$
\begin{cases}
2xy = \lambda \\[4pt]
x^2 = \lambda \\[4pt]
x + y = 3
\end{cases}
$$
Equating the two expressions of $\lambda$ we obtain $2xy = x^2$, that is, $x\,(x - 2y) = 0$. Since $x > 0$, we have $x = 2y$, and the constraint becomes $3y = 3$, so the candidate is $(2, 1)$, with $f(2, 1) = 4$; the solution $x = 0$ gives the point $(0, 3)$, which does not satisfy the condition $x > 0$. On the constraint we have $\varphi(x) = x^2\,(3 - x)$ for $0 < x < 3$, and $\varphi'(x) = 3x\,(2 - x)$ is positive for $x < 2$ and negative for $x > 2$. Therefore $(2, 1)$ is the **maximum** point.
