The set $Q = \{(x, y) : |x| + |y| \leq 1\}$ shown in the figure is a square with its vertices on the axes. What is the absolute maximum on $Q$ of the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - x$?

::grafico{poligoni="1,0 0,1 -1,0 0,-1" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-2 ymax=2 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The set $Q$ of the points with $|x| + |y| \leq 1$"}

- $1$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$

## Explanation

The set $Q$ is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so the maximum exists by the extreme value theorem. The system $f_x = 2x - 1 = 0$, $f_y = 2y = 0$ gives the point $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, 0\right)$, interior to $Q$, with $f = -\dfrac{1}{4}$. On the two sides with $x \geq 0$ we have $|y| = 1 - x$, so $f = 2x^2 - 3x + 1$ with $x \in [0, 1]$, which has maximum $1$ at $x = 0$. On the two sides with $x \leq 0$ we have $|y| = 1 + x$, so $f = 2x^2 + x + 1$ with $x \in [-1, 0]$, which has maximum $2$ at $x = -1$. The **absolute maximum** is therefore $2$, at the vertex $(-1, 0)$, while at the other vertices $f$ equals $1$ at $(0, \pm 1)$ and $0$ at $(1, 0)$.
