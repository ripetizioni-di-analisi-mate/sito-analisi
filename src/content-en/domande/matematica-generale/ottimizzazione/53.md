The function $f(x, y) = x + 2y$ has gradient $\nabla f = (1, 2)$ at every point. Which statement about its absolute maximum on the square $[0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ is true?

- The absolute maximum does not exist, because $f$ has no stationary points
- The absolute maximum is $2$, at the vertex $(0, 1)$
- The absolute maximum is $3$, at the vertex $(1, 1)$, which is a stationary point
- The absolute maximum is $3$, at the vertex $(1, 1)$, which is not an interior point

## Explanation

The square is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so by the **extreme value theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The gradient $(1, 2)$ never vanishes, so there are no interior stationary points and the maximum lies on the boundary. Since $x \leq 1$ and $y \leq 1$ on the square, we have $f(x, y) \leq 1 + 2 = 3$, with equality only at $(1, 1)$. The vertex $(1, 1)$ is not interior to the square, so Fermat's theorem in two variables does not apply, and the gradient at that point can be different from $(0, 0)$.
