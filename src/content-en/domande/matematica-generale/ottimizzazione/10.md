For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ on the line $2x + y = 5$, what is the minimum?

- $5$
- $\sqrt{5}$
- $\dfrac{25}{4}$
- $\dfrac{50}{9}$

## Explanation

From the constraint we obtain $y = 5 - 2x$, and with the **substitution method** we study
$$
\varphi(x) = x^2 + (5 - 2x)^2 = 5x^2 - 20x + 25,
\qquad
\varphi'(x) = 10x - 20 .
$$
The derivative vanishes at $x = 2$, is negative before and positive after, so $x = 2$ is the absolute minimum point of $\varphi$. For $x = 2$ the constraint gives $y = 1$, therefore the constrained minimum is $f(2, 1) = 4 + 1 = 5$. The number $5$ is the square of the distance of the line from the origin, which equals $\sqrt{5}$.
