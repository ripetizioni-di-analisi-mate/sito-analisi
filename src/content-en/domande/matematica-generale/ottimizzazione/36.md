The point $(2, 2)$ is the maximum of $f(x, y) = xy$ on the line $x + y = 4$. Which statement is true at the point $(2, 2)$?

- The gradient of $f$ is zero
- The gradient of $f$ is parallel to the line $x + y = 4$
- The level curve $xy = 4$ is perpendicular to the line $x + y = 4$
- The level curve $xy = 4$ is tangent to the line $x + y = 4$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = x + y - 4$ we have $\nabla f(2, 2) = (2, 2)$ and $\nabla g = (1, 1)$, so $\nabla f(2, 2) = 2\,\nabla g(2, 2)$ and the gradient of $f$ is different from $(0, 0)$. The gradient of $g$ is perpendicular to the line, therefore $\nabla f(2, 2)$ is perpendicular to the line as well. The gradient of $f$ is also perpendicular to the level curve $xy = 4$ through $(2, 2)$, so at that point the level curve and the line have the same perpendicular line. Therefore the level curve $xy = 4$ is **tangent** to the line $x + y = 4$, as stated by the geometric interpretation of the Lagrange multiplier theorem.
