A consumer has utility $U(x, y) = xy$ and an income of $40$ euros, with prices $p_1 = 1$ and $p_2 = 4$ euros. At the optimal choice, what is the value of the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$, that is, the marginal utility of income?

- $100$
- $20$
- $5$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$

## Explanation

With the budget constraint $x + 4y = 40$ the Lagrange multiplier equations are
$$
\begin{cases}
y = \lambda \\[4pt]
x = 4\lambda \\[4pt]
x + 4y = 40
\end{cases}
$$
The first two equations give $x = 4y$, and the constraint becomes $8y = 40$, so $y = 5$, $x = 20$ and $\lambda = y = 5$. The maximum utility is $U(20, 5) = 100$. With an arbitrary income $m$ the same procedure gives $x = \dfrac{m}{2}$ and $y = \dfrac{m}{8}$, so the maximum utility is $\dfrac{m^2}{16}$, and its derivative at $m = 40$ equals $\dfrac{40}{8} = 5$, which is $\lambda$. Therefore the **marginal utility of income** is $5$.
