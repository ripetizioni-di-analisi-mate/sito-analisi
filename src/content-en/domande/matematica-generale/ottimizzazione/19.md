For the function $f(x, y) = x^2 y$, which statement is true?

- Every point of the $y$-axis is stationary, and the points $(0, y_0)$ with $y_0 > 0$ are relative minimum points
- The only stationary point is the origin, and it is a saddle point
- Every point of the $y$-axis is stationary, and each of them is a saddle point
- Every point of the $x$-axis is stationary, and each of them is a relative minimum point

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2xy$ and $f_y = x^2$. The second one vanishes only for $x = 0$, and then the first one vanishes too, so the stationary points are all the points $(0, y_0)$ of the $y$-axis, where $f$ equals $0$. Let $y_0 > 0$. In the disk centered at $(0, y_0)$ with radius $y_0$ we have $y > 0$, so $f(x, y) = x^2 y \geq 0 = f(0, y_0)$, and the point is a **relative minimum** point. At the origin, instead, for $x \neq 0$ the value $x^2 y$ has the sign of $y$, so in every disk centered at $(0, 0)$ the function takes both positive and negative values, and the origin is a saddle point.
