What maximum value does $f(x, y) = (x^2 + y^2)\,e^{-(x^2 + y^2)}$ reach as $(x, y)$ varies in the plane?

- $1$
- $e$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$

## Explanation

The function depends only on $s = x^2 + y^2 \geq 0$, because $f(x, y) = g(s)$ with $g(s) = s\,e^{-s}$. The derivative $g'(s) = (1 - s)\,e^{-s}$ is positive for $0 \leq s < 1$ and negative for $s > 1$, so $g$ has its absolute maximum at $s = 1$, with $g(1) = \dfrac{1}{e}$. Therefore the **absolute maximum** of $f$ is $\dfrac{1}{e}$, attained at all points of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$. These points are stationary but not isolated, and each of them is a maximum point.
