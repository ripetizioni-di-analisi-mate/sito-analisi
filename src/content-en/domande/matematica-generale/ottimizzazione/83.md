We look for the extrema of $f(x, y) = (x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2$ on the line $x + y = 3$ with Lagrange multipliers, setting $g(x, y) = x + y - 3$. Which statement about the solution of the Lagrange system is true?

- The solution is $(1, 2)$, with $\lambda = 1$
- The solution is $(1, 2)$, with $\lambda = 0$
- The solution is $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$, with $\lambda = 1$
- The system has no solutions, because $\nabla f$ vanishes on the constraint

## Explanation

The Lagrange system is $2(x - 1) = \lambda$, $2(y - 2) = \lambda$, $x + y = 3$. The first two equations give $x - 1 = y - 2$, that is, $y = x + 1$, and the constraint becomes $2x + 1 = 3$. Hence $x = 1$, $y = 2$ and $\lambda = 2\,(1 - 1) = 0$. The multiplier is zero because the point $(1, 2)$, the absolute minimum of $f$ without constraints, already lies on the line $x + y = 3$, and there $\nabla f = (0, 0) = 0 \cdot \nabla g$. Since $f \geq 0$ everywhere and $f(1, 2) = 0$, the point $(1, 2)$ is the **constrained minimum**.
