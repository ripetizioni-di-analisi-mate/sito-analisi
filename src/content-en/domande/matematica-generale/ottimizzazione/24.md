What is the value of $f(x, y) = x^2 - 2xy + \dfrac{y^3}{3}$ at its local minimum point?

- $-\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $-4$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2x - 2y$ and $f_y = -2x + y^2$. The condition $f_x = 0$ gives $x = y$, and substituting into $f_y = 0$ we obtain $y^2 - 2y = 0$, so the stationary points are $(0, 0)$ and $(2, 2)$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = -2$ and $f_{yy} = 2y$, therefore $\det H = 4y - 4$. At the origin $\det H = -4 < 0$, so the origin is a saddle point; at $(2, 2)$ we have $\det H = 4 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 2 > 0$, so by the **Hessian test** $(2, 2)$ is a local minimum point. The minimum value is
$$
f(2, 2) = 4 - 8 + \frac{8}{3} = -\frac{4}{3} .
$$
