Let $f$ and $g$ have continuous partial derivatives and let $P_0$ be a point of the constraint $g(x, y) = 0$ where $\nabla f(P_0) = (2, 1)$ and $\nabla g(P_0) = (1, 2)$. Which statement is true?

- $P_0$ is a constrained maximum point, with $\lambda = 2$
- $P_0$ is neither a constrained maximum nor a constrained minimum point
- $P_0$ is a constrained minimum point, with $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- Without knowing $f$ and $g$ one cannot tell whether $P_0$ is a constrained extremum

## Explanation

Since $\nabla g(P_0) = (1, 2) \neq (0, 0)$, the hypotheses of the **Lagrange multiplier theorem** are satisfied at $P_0$. If $P_0$ were a constrained extremum, there would therefore exist a number $\lambda$ with $(2, 1) = \lambda\,(1, 2)$. The first component gives $\lambda = 2$ and the second gives $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so no such $\lambda$ exists. It follows that $P_0$ **is not a constrained extremum**. Geometrically, at $P_0$ the level curve of $f$ crosses the constraint, because the two gradients, perpendicular to the two curves, are not parallel.
