The function $f(x, y) = \sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}$ is defined on the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$. What are its absolute minimum points?

- All points of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$
- Only the point $(0, 0)$
- Only the points $(2, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$
- No point, because $f$ has no stationary minimum points

## Explanation

The square root is always greater than or equal to $0$, so $f(x, y) \geq 0$ on the whole disk. The value $0$ is obtained exactly when $4 - x^2 - y^2 = 0$, that is, at the points of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$. Therefore **all points of the circle** are absolute minimum points, with value $0$. These points lie on the boundary of the domain and are not interior, so they are not found by solving the system $\nabla f = (0, 0)$. That system gives only the origin, which is the absolute maximum point, with $f(0, 0) = 2$.
