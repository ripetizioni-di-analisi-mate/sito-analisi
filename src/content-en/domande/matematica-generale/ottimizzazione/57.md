At which points is the tangent plane to the graph of $f(x, y) = x^2 y + y^2 - 4y$ horizontal?

- Only at $(0, 2)$
- At $(0, 2)$, $(2, 0)$, $(-2, 0)$ and $(0, 0)$
- Only at $(2, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$
- At $(0, 2)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$

## Explanation

The tangent plane $z = f(x_0, y_0) + f_x(x_0, y_0)\,(x - x_0) + f_y(x_0, y_0)\,(y - y_0)$ is horizontal when $f_x$ and $f_y$ are both zero, that is, at the stationary points. The partial derivatives are $f_x = 2xy$ and $f_y = x^2 + 2y - 4$. The first one vanishes for $x = 0$ or for $y = 0$. If $x = 0$, the second equation gives $2y - 4 = 0$, so $y = 2$. If $y = 0$, it gives $x^2 = 4$, so $x = \pm 2$. The required points are therefore $(0, 2)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$, while at the origin $f_y(0, 0) = -4 \neq 0$.
