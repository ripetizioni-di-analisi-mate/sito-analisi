The exponent of $f(x, y) = e^{x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y}$ is a polynomial of degree two in $x$ and $y$. What minimum value does $f$ take on the plane?

- $-5$
- $e^{-5}$
- $e^{5}$
- $1$

## Explanation

Completing the squares, the exponent can be written as $x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y = (x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 - 5$. The sum of the two squares is greater than or equal to $0$ and equals $0$ only at $(1, -2)$, so the exponent has absolute minimum $-5$ at the point $(1, -2)$. The exponential function is strictly increasing, therefore $f$ has its minimum at the same point as the exponent. The **absolute minimum** of $f$ is hence $e^{-5}$, attained at $(1, -2)$.
