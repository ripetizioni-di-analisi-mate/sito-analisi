A consumer has utility $U(x, y) = x y^2$ and an income of $60$ euros, with prices $p_1 = 2$ and $p_2 = 4$ euros. What is the optimal bundle, with $x, y > 0$?

- $(20, 5)$
- $(10, 10)$
- $\left(15, \dfrac{15}{2}\right)$
- $(30, 0)$

## Explanation

The derivatives of the utility are $U_x = y^2$ and $U_y = 2xy$. At the optimal choice the marginal rate of substitution equals the price ratio, so
$$
\frac{y^2}{2xy} = \frac{2}{4} ,
$$
and since $y > 0$ we obtain $\dfrac{y}{2x} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, $y = x$. The budget constraint $2x + 4y = 60$ becomes $6x = 60$, therefore the optimal bundle is $(10, 10)$, with utility $1000$. On the constraint we have $x = 30 - 2y$, so for $0 < y < 15$ the utility equals $\varphi(y) = (30 - 2y)\,y^2$. Since $\varphi'(y) = 6y\,(10 - y)$ is positive for $0 < y < 10$ and negative for $10 < y < 15$, the point $(10, 10)$ is a **maximum** point on the constraint.
