The function $f(x, y) = \ln x + \ln y - x - 2y$ is defined for $x > 0$ and $y > 0$. What is its stationary point?

- $(1, 2)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $\left(1, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right)$

## Explanation

The partial derivatives are $f_x = \dfrac{1}{x} - 1$ and $f_y = \dfrac{1}{y} - 2$. The system
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{x} - 1 = 0 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{1}{y} - 2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
gives $x = 1$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$, both positive, so the point belongs to the domain. Therefore the only stationary point is $\left(1, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$. Since $f_{xx} = -\dfrac{1}{x^2} < 0$, $f_{yy} = -\dfrac{1}{y^2} < 0$ and $f_{xy} = 0$, the determinant of the Hessian is positive, and the point is a local maximum point.
