A monopolist sells the same good in two separate markets, with inverse demands $p_1 = 70 - q_1$ and $p_2 = 50 - q_2$. The cost depends on total output and is $C = (q_1 + q_2)^2$. Which quantities maximise the profit?

- $q_1 = 5$, $q_2 = 15$
- $q_1 = 17.5$, $q_2 = 12.5$
- $q_1 = 22$, $q_2 = 2$
- $q_1 = 15$, $q_2 = 5$

## Explanation

The profit is $\pi(q_1, q_2) = (70 - q_1)\,q_1 + (50 - q_2)\,q_2 - (q_1 + q_2)^2$. The first-order conditions $\pi_{q_1} = 70 - 2q_1 - 2\,(q_1 + q_2) = 0$ and $\pi_{q_2} = 50 - 2q_2 - 2\,(q_1 + q_2) = 0$ form the system
$$
\begin{cases}
4q_1 + 2q_2 = 70 \\[4pt]
2q_1 + 4q_2 = 50
\end{cases}
$$
whose solution is $q_1 = 15$, $q_2 = 5$. The Hessian is $\begin{pmatrix} -4 & -2 \\ -2 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$, with $\det H = 12 > 0$ and $\pi_{q_1 q_1} = -4 < 0$, so by the **Hessian test** the point is a maximum. At this point the marginal revenues of the two markets, $70 - 30 = 40$ and $50 - 10 = 40$, are equal to the marginal cost $2 \cdot 20 = 40$ euros. The result $q_1 = 17.5$, $q_2 = 12.5$ comes from using the cost $q_1^2 + q_2^2$ instead of $(q_1 + q_2)^2$, and $q_1 = 22$, $q_2 = 2$ from setting the prices, instead of the marginal revenues, equal to the marginal cost.
