A monopolist sells the same good in two separate markets, with inverse demands $p_1 = 50 - q_1$ and $p_2 = 40 - 2q_2$, and has cost $C = 10\,(q_1 + q_2)$. Which prices maximize profit?

- $p_1 = 20$, $p_2 = 7{,}5$
- $p_1 = 30$, $p_2 = 10$
- $p_1 = 10$, $p_2 = 10$
- $p_1 = 30$, $p_2 = 25$

## Explanation

The profit is the revenue $p_1 q_1 + p_2 q_2$ minus the cost, so
$$
\pi(q_1, q_2) = (50 - q_1)\,q_1 + (40 - 2q_2)\,q_2 - 10q_1 - 10q_2 = 40q_1 - q_1^2 + 30q_2 - 2q_2^2 .
$$
The first-order conditions are $\pi_{q_1} = 40 - 2q_1 = 0$ and $\pi_{q_2} = 30 - 4q_2 = 0$, therefore $q_1 = 20$ and $q_2 = \dfrac{15}{2}$. The Hessian is $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 0 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$, with $\det H = 8 > 0$ and $\pi_{q_1 q_1} = -2 < 0$, so by the **Hessian test** the point is a maximum point. Substituting the quantities into the inverse demands we obtain the prices $p_1 = 50 - 20 = 30$ euros and $p_2 = 40 - 15 = 25$ euros.
