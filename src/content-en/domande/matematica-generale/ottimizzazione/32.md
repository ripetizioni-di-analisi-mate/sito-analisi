The constraint is the curve $y = x^3 - x$, written as $g(x, y) = y - x^3 + x = 0$, and the function to be studied on it is $f(x, y) = x + y$. What do the Lagrange system and the study of $f$ on the curve give?

- The Lagrange system has no solutions, so $f$ has no extrema on the constraint
- The origin solves the system with $\lambda = 0$ and is a constrained minimum point
- The origin solves the system with $\lambda = 1$, so it is a constrained maximum or minimum point
- The origin solves the system with $\lambda = 1$, but it is neither a constrained maximum nor a constrained minimum point

## Explanation

The gradients are $\nabla f = (1, 1)$ and $\nabla g = (1 - 3x^2, 1)$, so the Lagrange system is
$$
\begin{cases}
1 = \lambda\,(1 - 3x^2) \\[4pt]
1 = \lambda \\[4pt]
y = x^3 - x
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation gives $\lambda = 1$, the first then gives $3x^2 = 0$, that is, $x = 0$, and the constraint gives $y = 0$. Therefore the only solution is the origin, with $\lambda = 1$, and there $\nabla g = (1, 1) \neq (0, 0)$. On the constraint, however, $f(x, x^3 - x) = x^3$, which is positive for $x > 0$ and negative for $x < 0$. Hence the origin is neither a constrained maximum nor a constrained minimum point, because the Lagrange condition is *necessary* but not sufficient.
