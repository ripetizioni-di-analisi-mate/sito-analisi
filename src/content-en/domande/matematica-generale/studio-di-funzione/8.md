For the function $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2$ on the interval $[-1, 4]$, at which points is the absolute minimum attained?

- Only at $x = 2$
- Only at $x = -1$
- At $x = 0$ and at $x = 2$
- At $x = -1$ and at $x = 2$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on the closed and bounded interval $[-1, 4]$, so by Weierstrass's theorem it has an absolute minimum. The derivative $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x = 3x(x - 2)$ vanishes at $x = 0$ and at $x = 2$, both interior to the interval. By the theorem on the absolute maximum and minimum on a closed interval one compares the values at the endpoints and at the stationary points, which are
$$
f(-1) = -4, \qquad f(0) = 0, \qquad f(2) = -4, \qquad f(4) = 16 .
$$
The smallest value is $-4$, therefore the **absolute minimum** is $-4$ and it is attained both at the endpoint $x = -1$ and at the stationary point $x = 2$.
