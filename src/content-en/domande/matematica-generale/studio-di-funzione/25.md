Which of the following functions is concave on the whole interval $(0, +\infty)$?

- $f(x) = \ln x - x^2$
- $f(x) = x \ln x$
- $f(x) = e^{-x}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

For $f(x) = \ln x - x^2$ we have $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x} - 2x$ and
$$
f''(x) = -\frac{1}{x^2} - 2 ,
$$
which is negative for every $x > 0$. Therefore, by the test on the sign of the second derivative, $f$ is **concave** on $(0, +\infty)$. The other three functions are convex on $(0, +\infty)$, because their second derivatives $\dfrac{1}{x}$, $e^{-x}$ and $\dfrac{2}{x^3}$ are positive for $x > 0$.
