For the function $f(x) = x^3 + 3x^2 - 2$, what is the equation of the tangent line at the inflection point?

- $y = -3x - 3$
- $y = -3x + 3$
- $y = 3x + 3$
- $y = -3x$

## Explanation

The derivatives are $f'(x) = 3x^2 + 6x$ and $f''(x) = 6x + 6 = 6(x + 1)$. The second derivative is negative for $x < -1$ and positive for $x > -1$, so $x = -1$ is the inflection point. We have $f(-1) = -1 + 3 - 2 = 0$ and $f'(-1) = 3 - 6 = -3$, therefore the tangent at the inflection point is
$$
y = f(-1) + f'(-1)\,(x + 1) = -3\,(x + 1) ,
$$
that is, $y = -3x - 3$. The line $y = -3x + 3$ is obtained by writing $x - 1$ in place of $x + 1$.
