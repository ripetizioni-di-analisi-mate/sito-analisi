The demand for a good is $q(p) = 100 - 2p$, with $0 \leq p \leq 50$ and $p$ in euros. At the price $p = 10$ euros, what happens to revenue if the price increases slightly?

- Revenue decreases, because the elasticity is negative
- Revenue decreases, because the elasticity equals $-4$
- Revenue stays constant, because the elasticity equals $-1$
- Revenue increases, because the elasticity equals $-\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

The elasticity of demand is
$$
\varepsilon(p) = \frac{p\,q'(p)}{q(p)} = \frac{-2p}{100 - 2p}, \qquad \varepsilon(10) = \frac{-20}{80} = -\frac{1}{4} .
$$
For revenue $R(p) = p\,q(p)$ we have $R'(p) = q(p)\,\big( 1 + \varepsilon(p) \big)$, so $R'(10) = 80 \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} = 60 > 0$. Since demand is inelastic, that is $|\varepsilon| < 1$, the decrease in the quantity sold is proportionally smaller than the increase in price. Therefore a small price increase makes **revenue increase**. Revenue is maximal where $\varepsilon = -1$, that is for $p = 25$.
