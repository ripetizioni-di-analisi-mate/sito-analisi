Consider the function $g(x) = \ln x - x + 1$, for $x > 0$. Which inequality follows from the study of $g$?

- $\ln x \geq x - 1$ for every $x > 0$
- $\ln x < x - 1$ for every $x > 0$
- $\ln x \leq x - 1$ only for $x \geq 1$
- $\ln x \leq x - 1$ for every $x > 0$

## Explanation

The derivative is $g'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x} - 1 = \dfrac{1 - x}{x}$, positive for $0 < x < 1$ and negative for $x > 1$. By the theorem on functions with a single stationary point, $x = 1$ is the point of **absolute maximum** of $g$ on $(0, +\infty)$, and $g(1) = 0$. So $g(x) \leq 0$ for every $x > 0$, that is $\ln x \leq x - 1$. Equality holds for $x = 1$, therefore the strict inequality is false at that point. Geometrically, the graph of $\ln x$ lies below its tangent $y = x - 1$ at the point $(1, 0)$, consistently with the concavity of $\ln x$.
