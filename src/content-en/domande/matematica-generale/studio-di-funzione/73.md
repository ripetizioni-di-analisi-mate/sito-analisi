For which values of the real parameter $k$ does the equation $x^3 - 3x = k$ have three distinct real solutions?

- $-2 < k < 2$
- $-2 \leq k \leq 2$
- $k < -2$ or $k > 2$
- $k > -2$

## Explanation

The solutions are the $x$-coordinates of the points where the graph of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ meets the horizontal line $y = k$. The derivative $f'(x) = 3\,(x - 1)(x + 1)$ shows that $f$ increases on $(-\infty, -1]$, decreases on $[-1, 1]$ and increases on $[1, +\infty)$, with local maximum $f(-1) = 2$ and local minimum $f(1) = -2$. Moreover $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$ and $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$. By the intermediate zero theorem, if $-2 < k < 2$ the line $y = k$ meets the graph once in each of the three intervals of monotonicity, so there are **three** solutions. For $k = \pm 2$ the line is tangent at the stationary point and there are two distinct solutions, while for $|k| > 2$ there is only one solution.
