What is the minimum distance from the point $P = (4, 0)$ to the points of the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$?

- $\dfrac{15}{4}$
- $2\sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{2}$

## Explanation

The generic point of the curve is $\left( x, \sqrt{x} \right)$, with $x \geq 0$, and the square of its distance from $P$ is
$$
d^2(x) = (x - 4)^2 + x = x^2 - 7x + 16 .
$$
The distance is minimal where its square is minimal, because the square root is increasing. The derivative $2x - 7$ is negative for $x < \dfrac{7}{2}$ and positive for $x > \dfrac{7}{2}$, so the minimum of $d^2$ is at $x = \dfrac{7}{2}$ and equals $\dfrac{49}{4} - \dfrac{49}{2} + 16 = \dfrac{15}{4}$. Therefore the **minimum distance** is $\sqrt{\dfrac{15}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{2}$. The point $(4, 2)$ of the curve, vertically above $P$, is at distance $2$, which is greater.
