A function $f$ is continuous on $[0, 4]$, differentiable on $(0, 4)$, and its derivative vanishes only at $x = 1$. It is known that $f(0) = 3$, $f(1) = 5$ and $f(4) = 7$. What are the absolute maximum and minimum of $f$ on $[0, 4]$?

- Maximum $5$ and minimum $3$
- Maximum $7$ and minimum $5$
- Maximum $7$ and minimum $3$
- They cannot be determined without the expression of $f$

## Explanation

The hypotheses of the theorem on the absolute maximum and minimum on a closed interval are satisfied, because $f$ is continuous on $[0, 4]$ and differentiable on $(0, 4)$. So the maximum and the minimum exist and are attained among the endpoints $0$ and $4$ and the stationary point $1$, the only candidates. The expression of $f$ is not needed; it suffices to compare the values $f(0) = 3$, $f(1) = 5$ and $f(4) = 7$. Therefore the **absolute maximum** is $7$, attained at $x = 4$, and the **absolute minimum** is $3$, attained at $x = 0$.
