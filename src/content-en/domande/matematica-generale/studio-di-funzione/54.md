A function $f$ differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ has derivative $f'(x) = (x - 2)^2\,(x + 1)\,e^{x}$. What are the points of local extremum of $f$?

- $x = -1$, a minimum, and $x = 2$, a maximum
- Only $x = -1$, a local maximum
- Only $x = -1$, a local minimum
- $x = -1$ and $x = 2$, both minima

## Explanation

The factors $(x - 2)^2$ and $e^{x}$ are nonnegative, and $(x - 2)^2$ vanishes only at $x = 2$. So for $x \neq 2$ the sign of $f'$ is that of $x + 1$. Therefore $f'$ is negative for $x < -1$ and positive for $-1 < x < 2$ and for $x > 2$. By the first derivative test, $x = -1$ is a **local minimum point**. At $x = 2$ the derivative vanishes but is positive on both sides, so $x = 2$ is not an extremum, and $f$ has a horizontal inflection point there.
