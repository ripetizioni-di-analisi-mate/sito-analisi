The demand for a good is $q(p) = 300\,e^{-\frac{p}{15}}$, with $p \geq 0$ in euros. Which price maximizes revenue?

- $30$ euros
- $45$ euros
- $5$ euros
- $15$ euros

## Explanation

Revenue as a function of price and its derivative are
$$
R(p) = p\,q(p) = 300\,p\,e^{-\frac{p}{15}},
\qquad
R'(p) = 300\,e^{-\frac{p}{15}} \left( 1 - \frac{p}{15} \right) .
$$
Since $e^{-\frac{p}{15}} > 0$, the derivative is positive for $0 \leq p < 15$ and negative for $p > 15$, so the **maximum revenue** is reached at the price $p = 15$ euros. In agreement with the unit elasticity condition, the elasticity of demand is $\varepsilon(p) = \dfrac{p\,q'(p)}{q(p)} = -\dfrac{p}{15}$, which equals $-1$ exactly for $p = 15$.
