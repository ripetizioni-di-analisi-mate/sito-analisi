A truck travels $400\,\mathrm{km}$ at constant speed $v$, in $\mathrm{km/h}$. Each hour of travel costs $50$ euros for the driver and $0.02\,v^2$ euros for fuel. Which speed minimizes the cost of the trip?

- $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $50\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $25\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $100\,\mathrm{km/h}$

## Explanation

The trip takes $\dfrac{400}{v}$ hours, so the total cost is
$$
C(v) = \frac{400}{v}\left( 50 + 0.02\,v^2 \right) = \frac{20000}{v} + 8v, \qquad v > 0 .
$$
The derivative $C'(v) = 8 - \dfrac{20000}{v^2}$ vanishes for $v^2 = 2500$, that is for $v = 50$, and is negative for $0 < v < 50$ and positive for $v > 50$. By the theorem on functions with a single stationary point, the **minimum cost** is obtained at $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and equals $400 + 400 = 800$ euros. At that speed the driver cost and the fuel cost are equal.
