A monopolist has inverse demand $p(q) = 120 - 3q$ and cost $C(q) = q^2 + 40q + 100$, in euros. Which quantity maximizes profit?

- $q = 16$
- $q = 20$
- $q = 40$
- $q = 10$

## Explanation

Revenue is $R(q) = (120 - 3q)\,q = 120q - 3q^2$, so marginal revenue is $R'(q) = 120 - 6q$, while marginal cost is $C'(q) = 2q + 40$. The first-order condition $R'(q) = C'(q)$ gives $120 - 6q = 2q + 40$, that is, $q = 10$. Since $R''(q) = -6 < 2 = C''(q)$, the second-order condition is satisfied. Moreover $\pi'(q) = 80 - 8q$ is positive for $q < 10$ and negative for $q > 10$, so by the theorem on functions with a single stationary point the maximum is absolute. Therefore the **maximum profit** is obtained with $q = 10$, and equals $\pi(10) = 900 - 600 = 300$ euros. The value $q = 16$ is obtained by setting price equal to marginal cost, as in perfect competition, and $q = 20$ maximizes revenue.
