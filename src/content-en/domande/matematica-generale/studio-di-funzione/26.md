A swimmer is at the point $A$, $3\,\mathrm{km}$ from a straight shore; let $H$ be the point of the shore closest to $A$. The swimmer must reach the point $B$ on the shore, $6\,\mathrm{km}$ from $H$. The swimmer swims at $3\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and runs along the shore at $5\,\mathrm{km/h}$. At what distance from $H$, toward $B$, should the swimmer reach the shore to arrive at $B$ in the least time?

- $0\,\mathrm{km}$
- $3\,\mathrm{km}$
- $6\,\mathrm{km}$
- $\dfrac{9}{4}\,\mathrm{km}$

## Explanation

Let $x$ be the distance from $H$ of the point where the swimmer reaches the shore, with $0 \leq x \leq 6$. The swimming stretch has length $\sqrt{9 + x^2}$ and the running stretch $6 - x$, so the time in hours is
$$
T(x) = \frac{\sqrt{9 + x^2}}{3} + \frac{6 - x}{5},
\qquad
T'(x) = \frac{x}{3\sqrt{9 + x^2}} - \frac{1}{5} .
$$
The condition $T'(x) = 0$ is equivalent to $5x = 3\sqrt{9 + x^2}$, that is $16x^2 = 81$, and therefore $x = \dfrac{9}{4}$. With the closed interval method we compare $T(0) = 2.2$, $T\left( \dfrac{9}{4} \right) = 2$ and $T(6) \approx 2.24$ hours, so the **least time** is obtained by reaching the shore $\dfrac{9}{4}\,\mathrm{km}$ from $H$.
