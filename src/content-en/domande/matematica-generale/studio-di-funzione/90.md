The function $f(x) = e^{x} + e^{-x} - x^2$ has $f'(0) = f''(0) = 0$, so the second derivative test does not classify the point $x = 0$. What does the study of the sign of $f'$ give?

- $f'$ does not change sign, so $x = 0$ is a horizontal inflection point
- $f'$ changes from negative to positive, so $x = 0$ is a local minimum
- $f'$ changes from positive to negative, so $x = 0$ is a local maximum
- $f'$ is negative on all of $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$, so $f$ is decreasing

## Explanation

The first derivative is $f'(x) = e^{x} - e^{-x} - 2x$, and to find its sign we study its derivative
$$
f''(x) = e^{x} + e^{-x} - 2 = \left( e^{\frac{x}{2}} - e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \right)^2 ,
$$
which is positive for every $x \neq 0$. Therefore $f'$ is increasing on $\mathbb{R}$, and since $f'(0) = 0$ we have $f'(x) < 0$ for $x < 0$ and $f'(x) > 0$ for $x > 0$. By the first derivative test $x = 0$ is a **local minimum point**, with $f(0) = 2$. The point is not an inflection point, because $f''$ does not change sign at $0$.
