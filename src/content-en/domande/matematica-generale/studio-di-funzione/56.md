The function $f(x) = (x - 3)\sqrt{x}$ is defined for $x \geq 0$ and has a local minimum at $x = 1$. Is the point $x = 0$, an endpoint of the domain, a point of local extremum?

- No, because $f$ is not differentiable at $x = 0$
- Yes, it is a local minimum point
- No, because $f'$ does not vanish at $x = 0$
- Yes, it is a local maximum point

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ the derivative is
$$
f'(x) = \sqrt{x} + \frac{x - 3}{2\sqrt{x}} = \frac{3\,(x - 1)}{2\sqrt{x}} ,
$$
negative for $0 < x < 1$ and positive for $x > 1$. So $x = 1$ is a local minimum point, with $f(1) = -2$. The point $x = 0$ is an endpoint of the domain, and $f$ decreases on $[0, 1]$, therefore $f(x) \leq f(0) = 0$ for $0 \leq x \leq 1$. By the definition of local maximum, which compares $f(0)$ with the values at points of the domain near $0$, the point $x = 0$ is a **local maximum point**. The endpoints of the domain are among the candidates even if the derivative does not vanish or does not exist there.
