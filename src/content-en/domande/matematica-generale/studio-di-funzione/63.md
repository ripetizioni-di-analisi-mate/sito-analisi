The function $f(x) = x\,(\ln x)^2$, defined for $x > 0$, has two stationary points. Which of the following is the local maximum point?

- $x = 1$
- $x = e^{-2}$
- $x = e^{2}$
- $x = e^{-1}$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = (\ln x)^2 + 2\ln x = \ln x\,(\ln x + 2)$, which vanishes when $\ln x = 0$ and when $\ln x = -2$, that is at $x = 1$ and at $x = e^{-2}$. The second derivative is
$$
f''(x) = \frac{2\ln x}{x} + \frac{2}{x} = \frac{2\,(\ln x + 1)}{x} .
$$
We have $f''(1) = 2 > 0$, so $x = 1$ is a local minimum point. Instead $f''\left( e^{-2} \right) = 2\,(-2 + 1)\,e^{2} = -2e^{2} < 0$, therefore by the **second derivative test** $x = e^{-2}$ is the **local maximum point**, with $f\left( e^{-2} \right) = \dfrac{4}{e^{2}}$. The point $x = e^{-1}$ is the zero of $f''$, that is the inflection point.
