How many real solutions does the equation $x\,e^{x} = -\dfrac{1}{4}$ have?

- None
- One
- Three
- Two

## Explanation

We study $g(x) = x\,e^{x} + \dfrac{1}{4}$ on $\mathbb{R}$. The derivative $g'(x) = (x + 1)\,e^{x}$ is negative for $x < -1$ and positive for $x > -1$, so $g$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty, -1]$ and strictly increasing on $[-1, +\infty)$. The absolute minimum equals $g(-1) = \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{e}$, which is negative because $e < 4$. As $x \to -\infty$ we have $x\,e^{x} \to 0$, so $g(x) \to \dfrac{1}{4} > 0$; as $x \to +\infty$ we have $g(x) \to +\infty$. Therefore, by the intermediate value theorem, $g$ vanishes in each of the two intervals, and by strict monotonicity only once in each. The equation thus has **two solutions**, one less than $-1$ and one between $-1$ and $0$. The limit as $x \to -\infty$ is finite; if it is replaced by $-\infty$, only one solution is counted.
