For the function $f(x) = e^{x} + 4\,e^{-x}$, defined on $\mathbb{R}$, what is the absolute minimum?

- $5$
- $10$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = e^{x} - 4\,e^{-x} = e^{-x}\left( e^{2x} - 4 \right)$. Since $e^{-x} > 0$, the sign of $f'$ is that of $e^{2x} - 4$, negative for $x < \ln 2$ and positive for $x > \ln 2$. By the theorem on functions with a single stationary point, $x = \ln 2$ is therefore the **absolute minimum** point. Since $e^{\ln 2} = 2$ and $e^{-\ln 2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, the minimum equals $f(\ln 2) = 2 + 4 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 4$. The value $5$ is $f(0)$, and $10$ is obtained by replacing $e^{-\ln 2}$ with $2$.
