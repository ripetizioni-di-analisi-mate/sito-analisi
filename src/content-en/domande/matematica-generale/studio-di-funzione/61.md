The second derivative of $f(x) = x^5 - 5x^4$ vanishes at $x = 0$ and at $x = 3$. At which of these points does $f$ have an inflection point?

- At $x = 0$ and at $x = 3$
- Only at $x = 0$
- At neither of them
- Only at $x = 3$

## Explanation

The derivatives are $f'(x) = 5x^4 - 20x^3$ and
$$
f''(x) = 20x^3 - 60x^2 = 20x^2\,(x - 3) .
$$
The second derivative vanishes at $x = 0$ and at $x = 3$, which are the only candidates. The factor $20x^2$ is nonnegative, so for $x \neq 0$ the sign of $f''$ is that of $x - 3$. At $x = 3$ the second derivative changes from negative to positive, therefore $x = 3$ is an **inflection point**. At $x = 0$, instead, $f''$ is negative on both sides, so $x = 0$ is not an inflection point, even though $f''(0) = 0$; it is a local maximum point.
