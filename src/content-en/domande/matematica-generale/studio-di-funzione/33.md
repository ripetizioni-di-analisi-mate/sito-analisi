For the function $f(x) = x + e^{-x}$, which statement about oblique asymptotes is true?

- The line $y = x$ is an oblique asymptote as $x \to \pm\infty$
- The line $y = x + 1$ is an oblique asymptote as $x \to +\infty$
- The line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote as $x \to -\infty$
- The line $y = x$ is an oblique asymptote only as $x \to +\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ we have $e^{-x} \to 0$, so $\dfrac{f(x)}{x} = 1 + \dfrac{e^{-x}}{x} \to 1$ and $f(x) - x = e^{-x} \to 0$. Therefore the line $y = x$ is an **oblique asymptote** as $x \to +\infty$. As $x \to -\infty$, instead, $e^{-x} \to +\infty$ faster than any power of $x$, so $f(x) \to +\infty$ and $\dfrac{f(x)}{x} \to -\infty$. The limit of $\dfrac{f(x)}{x}$ is not finite, therefore as $x \to -\infty$ there is neither an oblique asymptote nor a horizontal asymptote.
