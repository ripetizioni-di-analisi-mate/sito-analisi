How many points of local extremum does the function $f(x) = \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^3$ have?

- $3$
- $1$
- $2$
- $0$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = 3\left( x^2 - 1 \right)^2 \cdot 2x = 6x\,(x - 1)^2(x + 1)^2$, which vanishes at $x = 0$ and at $x = \pm 1$. At $x = \pm 1$ we also have $f''(\pm 1) = 0$, so the second derivative test is inconclusive and we study the sign of $f'$. The factor $(x - 1)^2(x + 1)^2$ is nonnegative, therefore for $x \neq \pm 1$ the sign of $f'$ is that of $x$. The derivative changes sign only at $x = 0$, where it goes from negative to positive, so $x = 0$ is a minimum, with $f(0) = -1$. At $x = \pm 1$ the sign of $f'$ does not change, and those points are horizontal inflection points. The function therefore has **only one** point of local extremum.
