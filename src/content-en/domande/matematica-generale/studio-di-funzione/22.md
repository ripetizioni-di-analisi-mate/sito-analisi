For the function $f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2 + 1$, what is the largest interval on which $f$ is concave?

- $[-\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3}]$
- $[-1, 1]$
- $(-\infty, -1]$
- $[1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The derivatives are $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x$ and $f''(x) = 12x^2 - 12 = 12(x - 1)(x + 1)$. The product $(x - 1)(x + 1)$ is negative for $-1 < x < 1$ and positive for $|x| > 1$. By the theorem on the sign of the second derivative and concavity, $f$ is therefore **concave** on $[-1, 1]$ and convex on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[1, +\infty)$. The interval $[-\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3}]$ is bounded by the stationary points $\pm\sqrt{3}$, that is, by the zeros of $f'$, and is unrelated to the interval of concavity.
