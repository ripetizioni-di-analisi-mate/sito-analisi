Which of the following functions, each considered on its natural domain, has the line $x = 0$ as a vertical asymptote?

- $f(x) = x \ln x$
- $f(x) = e^{\frac{1}{x}}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{e^{x} - 1}{x}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{\ln(1 + x)}{x}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to +\infty$, so $e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to +\infty$. Therefore the line $x = 0$ is a **vertical asymptote** of $e^{\frac{1}{x}}$, from the right; from the left, instead, $\dfrac{1}{x} \to -\infty$ and the function tends to $0$. For the other three functions the limit at $0$ is finite. We have $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} x \ln x = 0$, while by the standard limits $\dfrac{e^{x} - 1}{x} \to 1$ and $\dfrac{\ln(1 + x)}{x} \to 1$, so none of the three has a vertical asymptote at $x = 0$.
