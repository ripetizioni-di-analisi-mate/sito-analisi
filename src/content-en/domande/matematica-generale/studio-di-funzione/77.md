What is the range of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{x^2 + 4}$, defined on $\mathbb{R}$?

- $\left[ -\dfrac{1}{4}, \dfrac{1}{4} \right]$
- $\left[ -\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right]$
- $\left[ 0, \dfrac{1}{4} \right]$
- $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

The derivative is
$$
f'(x) = \frac{x^2 + 4 - 2x^2}{(x^2 + 4)^2} = \frac{4 - x^2}{(x^2 + 4)^2} ,
$$
positive for $-2 < x < 2$ and negative for $|x| > 2$. So $f$ decreases on $(-\infty, -2]$, increases on $[-2, 2]$ and decreases on $[2, +\infty)$, with $f(2) = \dfrac{1}{4}$ and $f(-2) = -\dfrac{1}{4}$. Since $f(x) \to 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, the value $\dfrac{1}{4}$ is the absolute maximum and $-\dfrac{1}{4}$ the absolute minimum. The function is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, therefore by the intermediate value theorem it takes every value in between, and the **range** is $\left[ -\dfrac{1}{4}, \dfrac{1}{4} \right]$.
