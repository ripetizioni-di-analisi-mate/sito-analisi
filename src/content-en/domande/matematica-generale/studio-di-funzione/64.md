A function $f$ twice differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ has $f'(1) = 0$ and $f''(x) > 0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Which statement is true?

- $x = 1$ is a local minimum, but $f$ may take values smaller than $f(1)$
- $x = 1$ may be a horizontal inflection point
- $f$ is increasing on $\mathbb{R}$
- $x = 1$ is the point of absolute minimum of $f$

## Explanation

Since $f''(x) > 0$ on $\mathbb{R}$, the function is **convex** on $\mathbb{R}$, so its graph lies above every tangent line. The tangent at $x = 1$ is horizontal, because $f'(1) = 0$, and has equation $y = f(1)$. Therefore $f(x) \geq f(1)$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, that is $x = 1$ is the point of **absolute minimum**. The point cannot be an inflection point, because $f''$ does not change sign. The function is not increasing on $\mathbb{R}$, because $f'$ is increasing and vanishes at $1$, so $f'(x) < 0$ for $x < 1$.
