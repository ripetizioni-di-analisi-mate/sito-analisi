What is the domain of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{\ln x}$?

- $[0, 1) \cup (1, +\infty)$
- $(1, +\infty)$
- $(0, +\infty)$
- $(0, 1) \cup (1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The domain consists of the real numbers for which the following conditions hold simultaneously
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
x > 0 \\
\ln x \neq 0
\end{cases}
$$
that is, the square root has a nonnegative argument, the logarithm has a positive argument and the denominator does not vanish. The second condition contains the first, and $\ln x = 0$ only for $x = 1$. Therefore the **domain** is $(0, 1) \cup (1, +\infty)$. The point $x = 0$ is excluded by the logarithm, even though the square root is defined at $0$.
