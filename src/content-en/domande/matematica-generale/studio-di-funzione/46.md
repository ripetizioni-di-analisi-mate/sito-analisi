The function $f(x) = x - 3\sqrt[3]{x}$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and not differentiable at $x = 0$. What are its points of local extremum?

- $x = -1$, a maximum, and $x = 0$ and $x = 1$, minima
- $x = -1$, a maximum, and $x = 1$, a minimum
- Only $x = 0$, a minimum
- $x = -1$, a minimum, and $x = 1$, a maximum

## Explanation

For $x \neq 0$ the derivative is
$$
f'(x) = 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{x^2}} ,
$$
which vanishes when $\sqrt[3]{x^2} = 1$, that is at $x = \pm 1$, while $f$ is not differentiable at $x = 0$. The candidates are therefore $-1$, $0$ and $1$. For $|x| > 1$ we have $\sqrt[3]{x^2} > 1$, so $f'(x) > 0$; for $0 < |x| < 1$ we have $\sqrt[3]{x^2} < 1$, so $f'(x) < 0$. Since $f'$ is negative both to the left and to the right of $0$, by the **first derivative test** the point $x = 0$ is not an extremum, even though $f$ is not differentiable at $0$. Therefore $x = -1$ is a local maximum point, with $f(-1) = 2$, and $x = 1$ is a local minimum point, with $f(1) = -2$.
