Among the right triangles with hypotenuse $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, what is the maximum area?

- $25\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $\dfrac{25\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $50\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $25\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm^2}$

## Explanation

Let $x$ be one leg, with $0 < x < 10$; by the Pythagorean theorem the other leg is $\sqrt{100 - x^2}$, so the area is $A(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,x\sqrt{100 - x^2}$. The area is maximal where its square $A^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}\left( 100x^2 - x^4 \right)$ is maximal, and its derivative is
$$
\frac{1}{4}\left( 200x - 4x^3 \right) = x\,\left( 50 - x^2 \right) ,
$$
positive for $0 < x < 5\sqrt{2}$ and negative for $5\sqrt{2} < x < 10$. Therefore the maximum is at $x = 5\sqrt{2}$, where the other leg also equals $\sqrt{100 - 50} = 5\sqrt{2}$. The triangle with maximum area is thus **isosceles**, with area $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 5\sqrt{2} \cdot 5\sqrt{2} = 25\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.
