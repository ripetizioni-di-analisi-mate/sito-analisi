For the function $f(x) = x^3 e^{-x}$, what is the value of the local maximum?

- $\dfrac{27}{e}$
- $\dfrac{9}{e^3}$
- $\dfrac{27}{e^3}$
- $\dfrac{8}{e^2}$

## Explanation

By the product rule, with $\left( e^{-x} \right)' = -e^{-x}$, we have
$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 e^{-x} - x^3 e^{-x} = x^2 (3 - x)\,e^{-x} .
$$
Since $x^2 e^{-x} \geq 0$, the sign of $f'$ is that of $3 - x$ for $x \neq 0$, so $f$ increases on $(-\infty, 3]$ and decreases on $[3, +\infty)$. The point $x = 0$ makes $f'$ vanish but is not an extremum, because $f'$ is positive on both sides. Therefore the only **local maximum** point is $x = 3$, and the maximum equals $f(3) = 27\,e^{-3} = \dfrac{27}{e^3}$.
