The function $f(x) = \dfrac{3e^{x} + 1}{e^{x} + 2}$ has a finite limit both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$. Which horizontal lines are asymptotes of the graph, and on which side?

- $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = 3$ as $x \to -\infty$
- Only $y = 3$, as $x \to \pm\infty$
- $y = 3$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = 3$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = 1$ as $x \to -\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ we have $e^{x} \to +\infty$, and dividing numerator and denominator by $e^{x}$ gives
$$
f(x) = \frac{3 + e^{-x}}{1 + 2e^{-x}} \to 3 .
$$
As $x \to -\infty$, instead, $e^{x} \to 0$, so $f(x) \to \dfrac{0 + 1}{0 + 2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore the function has **two different horizontal asymptotes**, $y = 3$ on the right and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ on the left.
