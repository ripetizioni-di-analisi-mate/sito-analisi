For the function $f(x) = x\,e^{-x}$, defined on $\mathbb{R}$, what is the largest interval on which $f$ is increasing?

- $(-\infty, 1]$
- $[1, +\infty)$
- $[-1, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, -1]$

## Explanation

By the product rule, with $\left( e^{-x} \right)' = -e^{-x}$, one has $f'(x) = e^{-x} - x\,e^{-x} = (1 - x)\,e^{-x}$. Since $e^{-x} > 0$ for every $x$, the sign of $f'$ is that of $1 - x$. Hence $f'(x) > 0$ for $x < 1$ and $f'(x) < 0$ for $x > 1$, so $f$ is **increasing** on $(-\infty, 1]$ and decreasing on $[1, +\infty)$. Every other interval of increase, such as $(-\infty, -1]$, is contained in $(-\infty, 1]$, hence the largest interval of increase is $(-\infty, 1]$.
