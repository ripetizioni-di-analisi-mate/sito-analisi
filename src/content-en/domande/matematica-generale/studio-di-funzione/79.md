The extreme value theorem does not apply to the function $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ on the open interval $(-2, 2)$. What can be said about the maximum and the minimum of $f$ on this interval?

- They do not exist, because the interval is not closed
- Both exist: the maximum is $2$ and the minimum is $-2$
- Only the maximum exists, and it equals $2$
- Only the minimum exists, and it equals $-2$

## Explanation

The extreme value theorem gives a sufficient condition, so the fact that it does not apply does not rule out the existence of a maximum and a minimum. The derivative $f'(x) = 3\,(x - 1)(x + 1)$ shows that $f$ increases on $(-2, -1]$, decreases on $[-1, 1]$ and increases on $[1, 2)$. So on $(-2, 1]$ we have $f(x) \leq f(-1) = 2$, and on $[1, 2)$ the function increases, therefore $f(x) < 2^3 - 3 \cdot 2 = 2$. In the same way on $[-1, 2)$ we have $f(x) \geq f(1) = -2$, and on $(-2, -1]$ we have $f(x) > (-2)^3 + 6 = -2$. Therefore $f(x) \leq 2$ and $f(x) \geq -2$ on all of $(-2, 2)$, and $f$ has **maximum** $2$, at $x = -1$, and **minimum** $-2$, at $x = 1$.
