For the function $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3$, what kind of point is the stationary point $x = 0$?

- A point of local maximum
- A point of local minimum
- A horizontal inflection point
- The kind of point cannot be determined

## Explanation

The derivatives are $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x^2 = 4x^2(x - 3)$ and $f''(x) = 12x^2 - 24x = 12x(x - 2)$. Since $f'(0) = 0$ and $f''(0) = 0$, the **second derivative test** does not apply, so one studies the sign of $f'$ near $0$. The factor $4x^2$ is positive for $x \neq 0$ and the factor $x - 3$ is negative for $x < 3$, so $f'(x) < 0$ both to the left and to the right of $0$. By the first derivative test, $x = 0$ is therefore not an extremum. The second derivative $12x(x - 2)$ is positive for $x < 0$ and negative for $0 < x < 2$, so $f$ is convex to the left of $0$, concave to the right, and $x = 0$ is an inflection point. Since moreover $f'(0) = 0$, the tangent at $0$ is horizontal and the inflection point is **horizontal**.
