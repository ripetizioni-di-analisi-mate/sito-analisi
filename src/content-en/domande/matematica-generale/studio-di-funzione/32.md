For the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x - 1}$, what is the set where $f(x) > 0$?

- $(-\infty, -3) \cup (3, +\infty)$
- $(-3, 1) \cup (3, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, -3) \cup (1, 3)$
- $(3, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The numerator $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$ is positive for $|x| > 3$ and negative for $-3 < x < 3$; the denominator is positive for $x > 1$. The quotient is positive where the two signs agree. For $x < -3$ the numerator is positive and the denominator negative, for $-3 < x < 1$ both are negative, for $1 < x < 3$ the numerator is negative and the denominator positive, for $x > 3$ both are positive. Therefore $f(x) > 0$ for $x \in (-3, 1) \cup (3, +\infty)$. The set $(-\infty, -3) \cup (3, +\infty)$ is where the numerator alone is positive.
