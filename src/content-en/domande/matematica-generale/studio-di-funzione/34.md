For the function $f(x) = 2x^3 - 9x^2 + 12x$ on the interval $[0, 3]$, what is the absolute maximum?

- $9$
- $5$
- $4$
- $0$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on the closed and bounded interval $[0, 3]$, so by Weierstrass's theorem it has an absolute maximum. The derivative $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 18x + 12 = 6(x - 1)(x - 2)$ vanishes at $x = 1$ and at $x = 2$. By the theorem on the absolute maximum and minimum on a closed interval we compare the values
$$
f(0) = 0, \qquad f(1) = 5, \qquad f(2) = 4, \qquad f(3) = 9 .
$$
The largest value is $9$, therefore the **absolute maximum** is $9$, attained at the endpoint $x = 3$. The value $5$ is the relative maximum at $x = 1$, which is not absolute.
