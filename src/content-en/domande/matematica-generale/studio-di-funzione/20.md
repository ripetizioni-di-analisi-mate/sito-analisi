For the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x - 1}{x^2}$, defined for $x \neq 0$, what is the largest interval on which $f$ is increasing?

- $(-\infty, 2]$
- $[2, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, 0)$
- $(0, 2]$

## Explanation

By the quotient rule
$$
f'(x) = \frac{x^2 - 2x\,(x - 1)}{x^4} = \frac{2x - x^2}{x^4} = \frac{2 - x}{x^3} .
$$
For $0 < x < 2$ the numerator and the denominator are positive, so $f'(x) > 0$. For $x > 2$ the numerator is negative, and for $x < 0$ the denominator is negative while the numerator is positive, so in both cases $f'(x) < 0$. Therefore $f$ is **increasing** on $(0, 2]$ and decreasing on $(-\infty, 0)$ and on $[2, +\infty)$. The interval $(-\infty, 2]$ contains $x = 0$, which does not belong to the domain.
