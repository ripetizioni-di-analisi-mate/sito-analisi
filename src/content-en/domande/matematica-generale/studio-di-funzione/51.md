The figure shows the graph of the derivative $f'$ of a function $f$ that is twice differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$; the derivative is $f'(x) = x^2 - 2x - 3$. What is the $x$-coordinate of the inflection point of $f$?

::grafico{f="x^2-2*x-3" punti="1,-4,pieno;-1,0,vuoto;3,0,vuoto" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=14 legenda="y = f'(x)" titolo="Graph of the derivative $f'(x) = x^2 - 2x - 3$"}

- $x = -1$
- $x = 3$
- $f$ has no inflection points
- $x = 1$

## Explanation

The concavity of $f$ depends on the sign of $f''$, that is on the monotonicity of $f'$. The graph of $f'$ is a parabola with vertex at $(1, -4)$, so $f'$ is decreasing on $(-\infty, 1]$ and increasing on $[1, +\infty)$. Indeed $f''(x) = 2x - 2$ is negative for $x < 1$ and positive for $x > 1$. Therefore $f$ is concave on $(-\infty, 1]$ and convex on $[1, +\infty)$, and the **inflection point** is at $x = 1$. The zeros $x = -1$ and $x = 3$ of $f'$ are instead the stationary points of $f$, a local maximum and a local minimum respectively.
