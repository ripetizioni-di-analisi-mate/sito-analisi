For the function $f(x) = x^2 - 4|x|$ on the interval $[-1, 3]$, at which points is the absolute maximum attained?

- At $x = -1$ and at $x = 3$
- Only at $x = 2$
- Only at $x = 0$
- At $x = 0$ and at $x = 2$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on $[-1, 3]$, so by Weierstrass's theorem it has a maximum. For $x > 0$ we have $f(x) = x^2 - 4x$, with $f'(x) = 2x - 4$, which vanishes at $x = 2$. For $x < 0$ we have $f(x) = x^2 + 4x$, with $f'(x) = 2x + 4$, which vanishes at $x = -2$, outside the interval. The point $x = 0$ is interior and $f$ is not differentiable at $0$, because the two derivatives equal $-4$ and $4$. By the theorem on the absolute maximum and minimum on a closed interval we compare $f(-1) = -3$, $f(0) = 0$, $f(2) = -4$ and $f(3) = -3$, therefore the **absolute maximum** is $0$, attained only at $x = 0$.
