For the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{x + 1}$ on the interval $[0, +\infty)$, which statement is true?

- $f$ has minimum $0$ and maximum $1$
- $f$ has minimum $0$ and no maximum
- $f$ has neither a maximum nor a minimum
- $f$ has maximum $1$ and no minimum

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = \dfrac{(x + 1) - x}{(x + 1)^2} = \dfrac{1}{(x + 1)^2} > 0$, so $f$ is increasing on $[0, +\infty)$. Therefore $f(x) \geq f(0) = 0$ for every $x \geq 0$, and $0$ is the **absolute minimum**, attained at the endpoint $x = 0$. Moreover $f(x) < 1$ for every $x$, because the numerator is less than the denominator, and $f(x) \to 1$ as $x \to +\infty$. The value $1$ is never attained, so $f$ has no maximum. The interval is not bounded, therefore the extreme value theorem does not apply and the absence of a maximum does not contradict it.
