The figure shows the graph of $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x$ on the interval $[0, 4]$. Which statement is true?

::grafico{f="x^3-6*x^2+9*x+0*sqrt(x*(4-x))" punti="0,0,pieno;1,4,pieno;3,0,pieno;4,4,pieno" segmenti="1,0,1,4;4,0,4,4" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=5 legenda=0 titolo="Graph of $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x$ for $0 \leq x \leq 4$"}

- The absolute maximum is attained only at $x = 1$
- $x = 3$ is a point of local minimum but not of absolute minimum
- $x = 4$ is a point of absolute maximum, even though $f'(4) \neq 0$
- $x = 0$ is not a minimum point, because $f'(0) \neq 0$

## Explanation

We have $f(1) = 4$, $f(4) = 64 - 96 + 36 = 4$, $f(0) = 0$ and $f(3) = 27 - 54 + 27 = 0$. Since $f(x) \leq 4$ for every $x \in [0, 4]$, the absolute maximum $4$ is attained both at $x = 1$ and at $x = 4$. At $x = 4$ we have $f'(4) = 48 - 48 + 9 = 9 \neq 0$, and this is possible because $4$ is an endpoint of the interval and Fermat's theorem concerns only interior points. In the same way the absolute minimum $0$ is attained at $x = 0$, where $f'(0) = 9$, and at $x = 3$. Therefore $x = 4$ is a point of **absolute maximum**, even though the derivative at $4$ is not zero.
