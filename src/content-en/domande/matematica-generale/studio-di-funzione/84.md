A right triangle has legs of $6\,\mathrm{m}$ and $8\,\mathrm{m}$. A rectangle has two sides on the legs, one vertex at the right-angle vertex and the opposite vertex on the hypotenuse. What is the maximum area of the rectangle?

- $24\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $16\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m^2}$

## Explanation

Place the right-angle vertex at the origin, the $6\,\mathrm{m}$ leg on the $x$-axis and the $8\,\mathrm{m}$ leg on the $y$-axis. The hypotenuse lies on the line $y = 8 - \dfrac{4}{3}x$, so the rectangle with base $x$, with $0 < x < 6$, has height $8 - \dfrac{4}{3}x$ and area
$$
A(x) = 8x - \frac{4}{3}x^2 .
$$
The derivative $A'(x) = 8 - \dfrac{8}{3}x$ vanishes at $x = 3$, is positive before and negative after. Therefore the **maximum area** is obtained with base $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $4\,\mathrm{m}$, and equals $12\,\mathrm{m^2}$, that is half the area of the triangle.
