Among the rectangles inscribed in a circle of radius $5\,\mathrm{cm}$, what is the maximum perimeter?

- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $20\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $40\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The diagonal of an inscribed rectangle is a diameter, so the sides $x$ and $y$ satisfy $x^2 + y^2 = 100$, that is $y = \sqrt{100 - x^2}$ with $0 < x < 10$. The perimeter and its derivative are
$$
P(x) = 2\left( x + \sqrt{100 - x^2} \right),
\qquad
P'(x) = 2\left( 1 - \frac{x}{\sqrt{100 - x^2}} \right) .
$$
The derivative vanishes for $x = \sqrt{100 - x^2}$, that is for $x = 5\sqrt{2}$, it is positive for $0 < x < 5\sqrt{2}$ and negative for $5\sqrt{2} < x < 10$. Therefore, by the theorem on functions with a single stationary point, the **maximum perimeter** is obtained for the square of side $5\sqrt{2}$, and it equals $P\left( 5\sqrt{2} \right) = 20\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the limit of $P(x)$ as $x \to 0^{+}$ and as $x \to 10^{-}$, when the rectangle flattens onto the diameter, and it is not attained. The value $10\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$ is half the perimeter of the square.
