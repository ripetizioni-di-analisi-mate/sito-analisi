For the function $f(x) = 2x^3 + 3x^2 - 36x$, what is the largest interval on which $f$ is decreasing?

- $(-\infty, -3]$
- $[-3, 2]$
- $[-2, 3]$
- $[2, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = 6x^2 + 6x - 36 = 6(x^2 + x - 6) = 6(x + 3)(x - 2)$. The product $(x + 3)(x - 2)$ is negative between the roots and positive outside, so $f'(x) < 0$ for $-3 < x < 2$ and $f'(x) > 0$ for $x < -3$ and for $x > 2$. Therefore $f$ is **decreasing** on $[-3, 2]$ and increasing on $(-\infty, -3]$ and on $[2, +\infty)$. The interval $[-2, 3]$ corresponds to the factorization $(x - 3)(x + 2)$, whose product is $x^2 - x - 6$.
