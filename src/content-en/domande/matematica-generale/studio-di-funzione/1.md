For the function $f(x) = x^3 - 27x + 4$, where does it attain a local minimum?

- $x = -3$
- $x = 3$
- $x = 3\sqrt{3}$
- $x = 0$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 27 = 3(x - 3)(x + 3)$, which vanishes at $x = -3$ and at $x = 3$. The product $(x - 3)(x + 3)$ is negative for $-3 < x < 3$ and positive for $|x| > 3$, so $f$ decreases on $[-3, 3]$ and increases on $[3, +\infty)$. By the **first derivative test**, $x = 3$ is therefore a point of local minimum, with $f(3) = 27 - 81 + 4 = -50$. At $x = -3$, the function has a local maximum because it increases to the left of $-3$ and decreases to the right.
