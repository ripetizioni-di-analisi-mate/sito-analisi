For the function $f(x) = x^2 + k\ln\left( 1 + x^2 \right)$, with $k$ a real parameter, the point $x = 0$ is stationary for every $k$. For which values of $k$ is $x = 0$ a local maximum point?

- $k \leq -1$
- $k < -1$
- $k > -1$
- $k < 0$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = 2x + \dfrac{2kx}{1 + x^2}$, so $f'(0) = 0$, and the second derivative at $0$ equals $f''(0) = 2 + 2k$. By the **second derivative test**, if $k < -1$ then $f''(0) < 0$ and $x = 0$ is a local maximum point, while if $k > -1$ then $f''(0) > 0$ and $x = 0$ is a local minimum point. For $k = -1$ the test is inconclusive, and we study the sign of the derivative
$$
f'(x) = 2x - \frac{2x}{1 + x^2} = \frac{2x^3}{1 + x^2} ,
$$
which is negative for $x < 0$ and positive for $x > 0$. Therefore for $k = -1$ the point $x = 0$ is a minimum, and the required values are exactly $k < -1$.
