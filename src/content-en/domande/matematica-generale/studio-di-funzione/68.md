The function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - 3x + 2}$ is not defined at $x = 1$ and at $x = 2$. Which vertical lines are asymptotes of its graph?

- $x = 1$ and $x = 2$
- Only $x = 1$
- Only $x = 2$
- The function has no vertical asymptotes

## Explanation

The denominator factors as $x^2 - 3x + 2 = (x - 1)(x - 2)$, so the domain is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{1, 2\}$. Since the numerator also factors as $(x - 2)(x + 2)$, for $x \neq 2$ we have
$$
f(x) = \frac{x + 2}{x - 1} .
$$
Therefore $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} f(x) = \dfrac{4}{1} = 4$ is finite, and the line $x = 2$ is not an asymptote. As $x \to 1$ the numerator tends to $3$ and the denominator to $0$, so $f(x) \to \pm\infty$, and the only **vertical asymptote** is $x = 1$.
