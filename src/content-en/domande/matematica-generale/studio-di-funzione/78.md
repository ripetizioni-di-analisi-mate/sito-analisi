What are the points of absolute maximum of the function $f(x) = x^2 e^{-x^2}$, defined on $\mathbb{R}$?

- $x = -1$ and $x = 1$
- Only $x = 1$
- Only $x = 0$
- Only $x = 2$

## Explanation

The function is even, because $f(-x) = (-x)^2 e^{-(-x)^2} = f(x)$, so its graph is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis. The derivative is
$$
f'(x) = 2x\,e^{-x^2} - 2x^3 e^{-x^2} = 2x\,(1 - x)(1 + x)\,e^{-x^2} ,
$$
positive for $x < -1$ and for $0 < x < 1$, negative for $-1 < x < 0$ and for $x > 1$. Therefore $f$ increases on $(-\infty, -1]$, decreases on $[-1, 0]$, increases on $[0, 1]$ and decreases on $[1, +\infty)$. From the monotonicity it follows that $f(x) \leq f(-1)$ for $x \leq 0$ and $f(x) \leq f(1)$ for $x \geq 0$, and $f(-1) = f(1) = \dfrac{1}{e}$. So the **absolute maximum** $\dfrac{1}{e}$ is attained at two points, $x = -1$ and $x = 1$, symmetric with respect to the origin. The point $x = 0$ is instead the point of absolute minimum, with $f(0) = 0$.
