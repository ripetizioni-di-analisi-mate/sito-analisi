For the function $f(x) = \dfrac{2x^2 - 3x}{x + 1}$, what is the oblique asymptote?

- $y = 2x - 5$
- $y = 2x - 3$
- $y = 2x$
- $y = 2x + 5$

## Explanation

Dividing the numerator by the denominator gives $2x^2 - 3x = (x + 1)(2x - 5) + 5$, so
$$
f(x) = 2x - 5 + \frac{5}{x + 1} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{5}{x + 1} \to 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, we have $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = 2$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \bigl( f(x) - 2x \bigr) = -5$. Therefore the line $y = 2x - 5$ is an **oblique asymptote** both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$. The answer $y = 2x - 3$ is obtained by taking the coefficient $-3$ of the numerator as the intercept, without carrying out the division.
