The figure shows the graph of a function $f$ differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$, with a horizontal tangent at the points with $x$-coordinates $-1$ and $3$ and no other stationary point. On which set is $f'(x) < 0$?

::grafico{f="x^3/3-x^2-3*x+4" punti="-1,5.667,pieno;3,-5,pieno" segmenti="-1,0,-1,5.667;3,0,3,-5" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-8 ymax=8 legenda=0 titolo="Graph of $f$, with a horizontal tangent at $x = -1$ and at $x = 3$"}

- $(-1, 3)$
- $(-\infty, -1) \cup (3, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, 1)$
- $(3, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The sign of $f'$ tells whether $f$ increases or decreases, so it is read from the graph where the curve goes down. The graph rises up to the point with $x$-coordinate $-1$, falls between $-1$ and $3$ and then rises again. Since $f'$ vanishes only at $-1$ and at $3$, its sign is constant on each of the three intervals. Therefore $f'(x) > 0$ on $(-\infty, -1)$ and on $(3, +\infty)$, and $f'(x) < 0$ exactly on $(-1, 3)$. The set $(-\infty, 1)$ is where the graph is concave, and it concerns the sign of $f''$.
