The function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x^2 - 2x + 5}$ is defined and positive on all of $\mathbb{R}$. What is the largest value it takes?

- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $1$

## Explanation

We complete the square in the denominator: $x^2 - 2x + 5 = (x - 1)^2 + 4$, which is always greater than or equal to $4$ and equals $4$ only for $x = 1$. Since the denominator is positive, $f$ is larger the smaller the denominator is. So $f(x) \leq \dfrac{1}{4}$ for every $x$, with equality at $x = 1$. The same result follows from the derivative $f'(x) = -\dfrac{2x - 2}{(x^2 - 2x + 5)^2}$, positive for $x < 1$ and negative for $x > 1$. Therefore the **absolute maximum** is $f(1) = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
