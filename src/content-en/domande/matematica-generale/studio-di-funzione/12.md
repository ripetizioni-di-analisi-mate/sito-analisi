A firm in perfect competition sells at the price $p = 29$ euros and has cost $C(q) = \dfrac{q^3}{3} - 4q^2 + 20q + 30$ euros, with $q \geq 0$. What is the maximum profit?

- $162$ euros
- $261$ euros
- $132$ euros
- $-132$ euros

## Explanation

The first-order condition $p = C'(q)$ is
$$
29 = q^2 - 8q + 20
\iff
q^2 - 8q - 9 = 0
\iff
(q - 9)(q + 1) = 0 ,
$$
and the only positive solution is $q = 9$. Since $C''(q) = 2q - 8$ and $C''(9) = 10 > 0$, the second-order condition is satisfied. Moreover the profit is $\pi(q) = 29q - C(q)$, and its derivative $\pi'(q) = 29 - C'(q) = -(q - 9)(q + 1)$ is positive for $0 < q < 9$ and negative for $q > 9$, so at $q = 9$ the profit has its absolute maximum. The cost is $C(9) = 243 - 324 + 180 + 30 = 129$ euros and the revenue is $29 \cdot 9 = 261$ euros, therefore the **maximum profit** is $261 - 129 = 132$ euros.
