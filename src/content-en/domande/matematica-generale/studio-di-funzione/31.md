For the function $f(x) = \dfrac{3x^2 + 1}{x^2 - 4}$, what are all the asymptotes?

- $x = 2$ and $y = 3$
- $x = -2$, $x = 2$ and $y = 0$
- $x = -2$, $x = 2$ and $y = 3$
- $x = -2$, $x = 2$ and $y = -\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

The denominator vanishes for $x = \pm 2$, where the numerator equals $13$, so $f(x) \to \pm\infty$ as $x \to \pm 2$ and the lines $x = -2$ and $x = 2$ are vertical asymptotes. The numerator and the denominator have the same degree, therefore
$$
\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{3x^2 + 1}{x^2 - 4} = 3 ,
$$
and the line $y = 3$ is a **horizontal asymptote** as $x \to \pm\infty$. Since there is a horizontal asymptote, there are no oblique asymptotes. The value $-\dfrac{1}{4}$ is $f(0)$, the $y$-intercept.
