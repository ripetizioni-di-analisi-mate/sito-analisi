For the function $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2$, which statement about the tangent line to the graph at the point with $x = 3$ is true?

- Near $x = 3$ the graph lies above the tangent $y = 9x - 27$
- Near $x = 3$ the graph lies below the tangent $y = 9x - 27$
- The graph crosses the tangent $y = 9x - 27$ at $x = 3$
- Near $x = 3$ the graph lies above the tangent $y = 9x$

## Explanation

We have $f(3) = 27 - 27 = 0$ and $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x$, so $f'(3) = 9$ and the tangent is $y = 9\,(x - 3) = 9x - 27$. The second derivative is $f''(x) = 6x - 6$, positive for $x > 1$, therefore $f$ is convex on $[1, +\infty)$, which contains a neighborhood of $3$. By the definition of convex function, the graph lies **above the tangent**. The computation confirms it, because
$$
f(x) - (9x - 27) = (x - 3)^2\,(x + 3) ,
$$
which is positive for $x > -3$ with $x \neq 3$. The graph crosses the tangent only at an inflection point, and $x = 3$ is not one.
