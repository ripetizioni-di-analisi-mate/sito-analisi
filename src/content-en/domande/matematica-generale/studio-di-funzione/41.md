A firm in perfect competition sells at the price $p = 50$ euros and has cost $C(q) = q^2 + 10q + 30$ euros, with $q \geq 0$; the maximum profit is obtained with $q = 20$. The fixed cost rises from $30$ to $80$ euros and the rest of the cost does not change. What happens to the quantity that maximizes profit?

- It decreases, because the maximum profit decreases by $50$ euros
- It remains $q = 20$, because marginal cost does not change
- It increases, because more must be produced to cover the fixed cost
- It becomes $q = 0$, because it is better not to produce

## Explanation

With the new cost $C(q) = q^2 + 10q + 80$ the marginal cost is still $C'(q) = 2q + 10$, because the fixed cost is a constant and has zero derivative. The first-order condition $p = C'(q)$ gives $50 = 2q + 10$, so $q = 20$, and the second-order condition is satisfied, because $C''(q) = 2 > 0$. The maximum profit becomes $\pi(20) = 1000 - 680 = 320$ euros, greater than $\pi(0) = -80$ euros, therefore the **optimal quantity remains** $q = 20$. The fixed cost lowers the profit by $50$ euros for every $q$ and does not move the maximum point.
