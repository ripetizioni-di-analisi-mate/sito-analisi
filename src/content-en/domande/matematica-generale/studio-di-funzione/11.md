Among the cylinders of volume $54\pi$ cubic centimeters, what radius does the cylinder of minimum total surface area have?

- $3\sqrt[3]{2}$ centimeters
- $6$ centimeters
- $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt[3]{2}}$ centimeters
- $3$ centimeters

## Explanation

Let $r$ be the radius and $h$ the height, in centimeters. From the constraint $\pi r^2 h = 54\pi$ one obtains $h = \dfrac{54}{r^2}$, so the total surface area, the sum of the two bases and the lateral surface, is
$$
S(r) = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{108\pi}{r}, \qquad r > 0 .
$$
The derivative is
$$
S'(r) = 4\pi r - \frac{108\pi}{r^2} = \frac{4\pi\,(r^3 - 27)}{r^2} ,
$$
negative for $0 < r < 3$ and positive for $r > 3$. By the theorem on functions with a single stationary point the absolute minimum is therefore at $r = 3$. The cylinder of **minimum surface area** hence has radius $3$ centimeters, height $6$ centimeters and surface area $54\pi$ square centimeters.
