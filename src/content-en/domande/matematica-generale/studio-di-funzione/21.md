For the function $f(x) = x\,|x - 2|$, defined on $\mathbb{R}$, where does it attain a local minimum?

- $x = 2$
- $x = 1$
- $x = 0$
- $x = 0$ and $x = 2$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 2$ we have $f(x) = x^2 - 2x$, with $f'(x) = 2x - 2 > 0$, so $f$ increases on $[2, +\infty)$. For $x < 2$ we have $f(x) = 2x - x^2$, with $f'(x) = 2 - 2x$, positive for $x < 1$ and negative for $1 < x < 2$. Hence $f$ decreases on $[1, 2]$ and increases on $[2, +\infty)$. The function is continuous at $2$, so by the **first derivative test**, which also holds at points of non-differentiability, $x = 2$ is a point of local minimum, with $f(2) = 0$. The point $x = 1$ is a point of local maximum, and $x = 0$ is not an extremum, because $f$ increases on all of $(-\infty, 1]$.
