For the function $f(x) = \ln\left( x^2 + 4 \right)$, what is the largest interval on which $f$ is convex?

- $(-\infty, -2]$
- $[0, +\infty)$
- $[-2, 2]$
- $[2, +\infty)$

## Explanation

By the chain rule $f'(x) = \dfrac{2x}{x^2 + 4}$, and by the quotient rule
$$
f''(x) = \frac{2\,(x^2 + 4) - 2x \cdot 2x}{(x^2 + 4)^2} = \frac{2\,(4 - x^2)}{(x^2 + 4)^2} .
$$
The denominator is positive, so the sign of $f''$ is that of $4 - x^2$, positive for $-2 < x < 2$ and negative for $|x| > 2$. By the theorem on the sign of the second derivative and concavity, $f$ is therefore **convex** on $[-2, 2]$ and concave on $(-\infty, -2]$ and on $[2, +\infty)$. The interval $[0, +\infty)$ is the one on which $f$ increases.
