For the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 + 9}{x}$, what are the asymptotes?

- $x = 0$ and $y = x$
- $x = 0$ and $y = 1$
- $x = 0$ and $y = x + 9$
- $x = 0$ and $y = 0$

## Explanation

The domain is $x \neq 0$. As $x \to 0^{\pm}$ the numerator tends to $9$ and the denominator to $0$, so $f(x) \to \pm\infty$ and the line $x = 0$ is a vertical asymptote. The numerator has a higher degree than the denominator, therefore $f(x) \to \pm\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$ and there is no horizontal asymptote. Dividing one obtains $f(x) = x + \dfrac{9}{x}$. Since
$$
\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{9}{x^2} \right) = 1 ,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \bigl( f(x) - x \bigr) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{9}{x} = 0 ,
$$
the line $y = x$ is an **oblique asymptote** both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$.
