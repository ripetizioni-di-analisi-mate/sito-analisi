For the function $f(x) = e^{-x^2}$, what are the abscissas of the inflection points?

- $x = \pm\sqrt{2}$
- $x = 0$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

By the chain rule one has $f'(x) = -2x\,e^{-x^2}$, and differentiating again with the product rule one obtains
$$
f''(x) = -2\,e^{-x^2} + 4x^2\,e^{-x^2} = 2\,(2x^2 - 1)\,e^{-x^2} .
$$
Since $e^{-x^2} > 0$, the sign of $f''$ is that of $2x^2 - 1$, which vanishes for $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, for $x = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The polynomial $2x^2 - 1$ is negative between the two roots and positive outside, so $f''$ changes sign at both points. Therefore the **inflections** have abscissas $x = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, with ordinate $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$. The point $x = 0$, instead, is the maximum point, where $f'$ vanishes.
