A firm has total cost $C(q) = q^3 - 9q^2 + 40q + 50$ euros, with $q \geq 0$, and its marginal cost first decreases and then increases. At which quantity does marginal cost reach its lowest value?

- $q = 3$
- $q = 6$
- $q = \dfrac{9}{2}$
- $q = 9$

## Explanation

Marginal cost is $C'(q) = 3q^2 - 18q + 40$, and its derivative is $C''(q) = 6q - 18$. Since $C''(q) < 0$ for $q < 3$ and $C''(q) > 0$ for $q > 3$, marginal cost decreases on $[0, 3]$ and increases on $[3, +\infty)$. Therefore **marginal cost is minimal** at $q = 3$, where it equals $C'(3) = 27 - 54 + 40 = 13$ euros. The point $q = 3$ is also the inflection point of total cost, where $C$ changes from concave to convex. The value $q = \dfrac{9}{2}$ is instead the minimum point of average variable cost $q^2 - 9q + 40$.
