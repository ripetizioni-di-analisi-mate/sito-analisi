For the function $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 2$, what is the largest interval on which $f$ is concave?

- $[2, +\infty)$
- $[0, 4]$
- $(-\infty, 0]$
- $(-\infty, 2]$

## Explanation

The derivatives are $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x$ and $f''(x) = 6x - 12 = 6(x - 2)$. Since $f''(x) < 0$ for $x < 2$, by the test on the sign of the second derivative and concavity the function is **concave** on $(-\infty, 2]$. For $x > 2$ we have $f''(x) > 0$, so $f$ is convex on $[2, +\infty)$. Therefore the largest interval of concavity is $(-\infty, 2]$, which contains $(-\infty, 0]$. The interval $[0, 4]$ is the one where $f' \leq 0$, that is, the one where $f$ decreases.
