At the annual rate of $5\%$ under compound interest, the ordinary perpetuity with payment $R$ is compared with the nondeferred ordinary annuity of $n$ payments equal to $R$. What is the minimum $n$ for which the present value of the annuity of $n$ payments is at least $90\%$ of that of the perpetuity?

- $18$
- $90$
- $3$
- $48$

## Explanation

The present value of the perpetuity is $\dfrac{R}{0.05}$, so the ratio of the two present values is $0.05\,a_{\overline{n}|0.05} = 1 - 1.05^{-n}$. The condition $1 - 1.05^{-n} \geq 0.9$ is equivalent to $1.05^{n} \geq 10$. Taking logarithms gives $n \geq \dfrac{\ln 10}{\ln 1.05} \approx 47.19$, therefore the minimum is $n = 48$. The payments after the forty-eighth are therefore worth, all together, less than $10\%$ of the perpetuity. The value $18$ is $\dfrac{0.9}{0.05}$, that is, the number of payments that without interest would be worth $0.9 \cdot \dfrac{R}{0.05}$.
