An investment with cash flows $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n$ has a single internal rate of return $i^*$. Consider the investment with cash flows $3a_0, 3a_1, \dots, 3a_n$. What is its internal rate of return?

- $3\,i^*$
- $\dfrac{i^*}{3}$
- $(1 + i^*)^{3} - 1$
- $i^*$

## Explanation

The NPV of the new investment is
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{n} 3a_k\,(1 + i)^{-k} = 3\,\operatorname{NPV}(i) ,
$$
where $\operatorname{NPV}(i)$ is the NPV of the original investment. Since $3\,\operatorname{NPV}(i) = 0$ if and only if $\operatorname{NPV}(i) = 0$, the two functions have the same zeros. Therefore the internal rate of return is still $i^*$. For example, $(-1000,\ 600,\ 720)$ and $(-3000,\ 1800,\ 2160)$ both have an IRR of $20\%$.
