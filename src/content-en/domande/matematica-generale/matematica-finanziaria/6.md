## Explanation

The nominal rate $j_4 = 6\%$ with quarterly compounding corresponds to the quarterly rate $i_4 = \dfrac{j_4}{4} = 1{,}5\%$. The effective annual rate is the rate equivalent to $i_4$, that is, $i = (1 + i_4)^{4} - 1$. Therefore
$$
i = 1{,}015^{4} - 1 \approx 0{,}06136 ,
$$
and the effective annual rate is $6{,}136\%$. The result is greater than the nominal rate, as happens for every nominal rate with compounding $k \geq 2$ times a year.
