The same principal is invested for $3$ years in two ways: investment A at the annual rate of $5\%$ under simple interest, investment B at the annual rate of $4.5\%$ under compound interest. Which statement is true?

- The accumulated value of A is larger
- The accumulated value of B is larger, because the term exceeds one year
- The two accumulated values coincide
- The comparison depends on the principal invested

## Explanation

The accumulation factor of A is $1 + 0.05 \cdot 3 = 1.15$. The accumulation factor of B is $1.045^{3} \approx 1.1412$. Since $1.15 > 1.1412$ and both accumulated values are proportional to the principal, the **accumulated value of A is larger** for every principal $C > 0$. The comparison of the two regimes, according to which compound interest gives the larger accumulated value for $t > 1$, holds at the same rate, while here the rates are different.
