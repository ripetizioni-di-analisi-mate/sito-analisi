The investment $(-2000,\ 800,\ 900,\ 700)$, with the cash flows at times $0, 1, 2, 3$, has internal rate of return $i^* \approx 9{,}91\%$. Which statement is true at the discount rate of $12\%$?

- The NPV is positive, so the investment is worthwhile
- The NPV is zero
- The NPV is negative, so the investment is not worthwhile
- The NPV is negative, but the investment is worthwhile because the IRR is positive

## Explanation

The investment has a negative initial cash flow, positive subsequent cash flows and sum of the cash flows $-2000 + 800 + 900 + 700 = 400 > 0$, so the theorem on the existence and uniqueness of the IRR applies. By the theorem $\operatorname{VAN}(i) < 0$ for every $i > i^*$, and since $12\% > 9{,}91\%$ the NPV at $12\%$ is negative. Indeed
$$
\operatorname{VAN}(0{,}12) = -2000 + \frac{800}{1{,}12} + \frac{900}{1{,}12^{2}} + \frac{700}{1{,}12^{3}} \approx -69{,}99 .
$$
Hence at $12\%$ **the investment is not worthwhile**, in agreement with the IRR criterion, since the IRR is smaller than the return available on alternatives.
