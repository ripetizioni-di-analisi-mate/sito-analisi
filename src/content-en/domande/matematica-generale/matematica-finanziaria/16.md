A principal is invested for $3$ years under compound interest, at the rate of $2\%$ in the first year, $4\%$ in the second and $6\%$ in the third. Let $\bar{\imath}$ be the constant annual rate that gives the same accumulated value over the three years. Which statement is true?

- $\bar{\imath}$ is less than $4\%$
- $\bar{\imath}$ is equal to $4\%$
- $\bar{\imath}$ is greater than $4\%$
- $\bar{\imath}$ is equal to $12\%$

## Explanation

The accumulation factor over the three years is $1.02 \cdot 1.04 \cdot 1.06$, so the constant rate satisfies
$$
(1 + \bar{\imath})^{3} = 1.02 \cdot 1.04 \cdot 1.06 .
$$
Since $1.02 \cdot 1.06 = 1.0812$ and $1.04^{2} = 1.0816$, the product of the three factors is less than $1.04^{3}$. Therefore $1 + \bar{\imath} < 1.04$, that is, $\bar{\imath} < 4\%$; the computation gives $\bar{\imath} \approx 3.99\%$. The equivalent rate is the geometric mean of the factors minus $1$, and the geometric mean does not exceed the arithmetic mean $1.04$. The value $12\%$ is the sum of the three rates.
