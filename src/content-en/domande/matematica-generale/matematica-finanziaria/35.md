Investments A and B have cash flows at times $0, 1, \dots, n$; each has an outflow at time $0$ followed by non-negative inflows, not all zero, whose sum exceeds the outflow. The IRR of A is $10\%$ and that of B is $20\%$. Investment A + B has as cash flows the sums of the cash flows of A and B at the same dates. Which statement about the IRR of A + B is true?

- It lies between $10\%$ and $20\%$
- It equals $30\%$
- It is greater than $20\%$
- It equals $15\%$ for every pair of investments with these IRRs

## Explanation

A + B also has an outflow at time $0$ followed by non-negative inflows whose sum exceeds the outflow, so by the existence and uniqueness theorem it has a single IRR $i^*$, and its NPV is positive for $0 \leq i < i^*$ and negative for $i > i^*$. The NPV of A + B is the sum of the NPVs of A and B, because each cash flow is discounted separately. At the rate of $10\%$ the NPV of A is zero and that of B is positive, because $10\%$ is less than the IRR of B, so the NPV of A + B is positive. At the rate of $20\%$ the NPV of A is negative and that of B is zero, so the NPV of A + B is negative. Therefore $10\% < i^* < 20\%$.

The exact value depends on the cash flows. With A $= (-1000,\ 1100)$ and B $= (-1000,\ 1200)$ one gets A + B $= (-2000,\ 2300)$, with IRR $15\%$; with A $= (-1000,\ 1100)$ and B $= (-100,\ 120)$ one gets A + B $= (-1100,\ 1220)$, with IRR $\approx 10.91\%$.
