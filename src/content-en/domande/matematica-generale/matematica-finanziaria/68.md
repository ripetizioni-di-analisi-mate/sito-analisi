A debt of $1000$ euros is due in $2$ years. The creditor agrees to receive $900$ euros today instead of the $1000$ euros. For which annual rates $i$, under compound interest, is the early payment worthwhile for the debtor? (percentages rounded to two decimal places)

- $0 < i < 5.41\%$
- $i > 5.41\%$
- $0 < i < 11.11\%$
- $0 < i < 2.67\%$

## Explanation

Paying today is worthwhile for the debtor if the $900$ euros are less than the present value of the debt, that is, if $900 < 1000\,(1 + i)^{-2}$. The inequality is equivalent to $(1 + i)^{2} < \dfrac{10}{9}$, so $1 + i < \sqrt{\dfrac{10}{9}} \approx 1.0541$. Therefore the early payment is worthwhile for $0 < i < 5.41\%$. For higher rates the $900$ euros invested for two years exceed $1000$ euros, so the debtor is better off paying at maturity. The value $11.11\%$ is $\dfrac{100}{900}$, that is, the discount obtained referred to a single year.
