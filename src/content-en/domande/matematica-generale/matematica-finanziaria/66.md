A creditor can receive $1000$ euros today or $1100$ euros in one year, and compares the two amounts at date $2$, that is, in two years, under simple interest at the annual rate of $10\%$. Which statement is true?

- At date $2$ the $1000$ euros of today are worth more
- At date $2$ the $1100$ euros in one year are worth more
- At date $2$ the two amounts have the same value
- The two amounts have the same value at every date

## Explanation

Under simple interest the value of an amount at date $2$ is obtained with the factor $1 + i\,t$, where $t$ is the time that separates the amount from date $2$. The $1000$ euros of today are worth $1000 \cdot (1 + 0.1 \cdot 2) = 1200$ euros and the $1100$ euros in one year are worth $1100 \cdot (1 + 0.1 \cdot 1) = 1210$ euros, so at date $2$ the $1100$ euros available in one year are worth **more**. At date $1$, instead, the two amounts have the same value, $1100$ euros. Under simple interest equivalence therefore depends on the chosen date, while under compound interest it does not.
