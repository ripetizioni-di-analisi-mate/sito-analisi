A principal of $1000$ euros is invested for $8$ months at the annual rate of $6\%$. Use the factor $(1+i)^t$ for compound interest, with $t$ measured in years. Which comparison with the simple-interest accumulated value is correct?

- The accumulated value under compound interest is larger
- The accumulated value under simple interest is larger
- The two accumulated values coincide
- The comparison depends on the principal invested

## Explanation

By the comparison of the two regimes, if $i > 0$ and $0 < t < 1$ then $(1 + i)^{t} < 1 + i\,t$. Here $t = \dfrac{8}{12} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ of a year, so the duration is shorter than a year and the accumulated value under simple interest is **larger**. Indeed under simple interest one obtains $1000 \left( 1 + 0.06 \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} \right) = 1040$ euros, whereas under compound interest one obtains $1000 \cdot 1.06^{\frac{2}{3}} \approx 1039.61$ euros. The comparison concerns the accumulation factors $1 + i\,t$ and $(1 + i)^{t}$, which are independent of the principal, therefore the conclusion holds for every positive principal.
