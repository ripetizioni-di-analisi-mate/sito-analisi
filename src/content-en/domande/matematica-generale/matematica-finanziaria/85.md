Two investments both cost $1000$ euros today. Investment A returns $1440$ euros in two years, investment B returns $1250$ euros in one year. The figure shows the two NPVs as functions of the valuation rate $i$, expressed as a percentage; the IRR of A is $20\%$ and that of B is $25\%$. At the valuation rate of $10\%$, which investment is chosen with the NPV criterion?

::grafico{f="-1000+1440/(1+x/100)^2+0*sqrt(x);-1000+1250/(1+x/100)+0*sqrt(x)" punti="20,0,vuoto;25,0,vuoto" xmin=-4 xmax=36 ymin=-500 ymax=500 nomi="i,VAN" legenda="\operatorname{VAN}_A;\operatorname{VAN}_B" titolo="NPV of investments A and B as functions of the valuation rate (as a percentage)"}

- B, because its IRR is higher
- B, because its NPV is higher
- The two investments are indifferent
- A, because its NPV is higher

## Explanation

At the rate of $10\%$ the two NPVs are
$$
\operatorname{VAN}_A = -1000 + \frac{1440}{1.1^{2}} \approx 190.08 ,
\qquad
\operatorname{VAN}_B = -1000 + \frac{1250}{1.1} \approx 136.36 .
$$
The NPV criterion prefers the investment with the higher NPV, so **A** is chosen. The two curves cross when $\dfrac{1440}{(1 + i)^{2}} = \dfrac{1250}{1 + i}$, that is, for $1 + i = \dfrac{1440}{1250} = 1.152$, and for rates below $15.2\%$ the NPV of A is higher. The IRR of B is higher, so comparing the IRRs leads to a choice different from that of the NPV.
