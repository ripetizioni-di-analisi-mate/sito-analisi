Under compound interest at a constant annual rate, a principal triples in $10$ years. How many years after the investment is the accumulated value nine times the principal?

- $30$ years
- $90$ years
- $20$ years
- $40$ years

## Explanation

Under compound interest the accumulation factor is $(1 + i)^{t}$, and by hypothesis $(1 + i)^{10} = 3$. Then $(1 + i)^{20} = \left[ (1 + i)^{10} \right]^{2} = 9$, so the accumulated value is nine times the principal after $20$ years. The accumulation factor is multiplied by $3$ every $10$ years, therefore it goes from $1$ to $3$ in the first ten years and from $3$ to $9$ in the next ten. Under simple interest, with $1 + 10\,i = 3$, one would instead have $i = 0.2$, and the accumulated value would be nine times the principal when $1 + 0.2\,t = 9$, that is, after $40$ years.
