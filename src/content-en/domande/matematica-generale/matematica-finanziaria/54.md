An amount $M$ is due in $t$ years, with $0 < t < 1$. The present value with commercial discount at the rate $d$ is compared with the present value with rational discount, that is, discounting under simple interest, at the rate $i = d$, with $0 < d < 1$. Which statement is true?

- Rational discount gives the smaller present value
- The two present values coincide
- Commercial discount gives the smaller present value
- The comparison depends on $t$

## Explanation

The two present values are $M\,(1 - d\,t)$ and $\dfrac{M}{1 + d\,t}$. Since $(1 - d\,t)(1 + d\,t) = 1 - d^{2} t^{2} < 1$ and $1 + d\,t > 0$, one has $1 - d\,t < \dfrac{1}{1 + d\,t}$. Therefore for every $t$ with $0 < t < 1$ **commercial discount** gives the smaller present value, that is, the larger discount. With $M = 5000$ euros, $d = 4\%$ and $t = \dfrac{3}{4}$ one obtains $4850$ euros with commercial discount and about $4854.37$ euros with rational discount.
