A transaction has the cash flows $(-100,\ 230,\ -132)$ at times $0, 1, 2$. For which rates $i > -1$ is the NPV zero?

- Only for $i = 10\%$
- Only for $i = 20\%$
- For $i = 10\%$ and for $i = 20\%$
- For no rate

## Explanation

With $v = (1 + i)^{-1}$ the NPV is $-100 + 230\,v - 132\,v^{2}$, which vanishes if $132\,v^{2} - 230\,v + 100 = 0$. The discriminant is $230^{2} - 4 \cdot 132 \cdot 100 = 52\,900 - 52\,800 = 100$, so $v = \dfrac{230 \pm 10}{264}$, that is, $v = \dfrac{10}{11}$ or $v = \dfrac{5}{6}$. Since $1 + i = \dfrac{1}{v}$, one obtains $i = 10\%$ and $i = 20\%$. The cash flows change sign twice, so the theorem on the existence and uniqueness of the IRR does not apply, and the NPV has **two zeros**.
