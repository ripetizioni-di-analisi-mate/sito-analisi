Under compound interest at the annual rate of $5\%$, after how long does a principal double? (years rounded to two decimal places)

- $20$ years
- $13.86$ years
- $14.21$ years
- $0.07$ years

## Explanation

The principal doubles when $M = 2C$, that is, when $1.05^{t} = 2$. Taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives $t \ln 1.05 = \ln 2$, so
$$
t = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 1.05} \approx \frac{0.69315}{0.04879} \approx 14.21 .
$$
Hence the principal doubles after about $14.21$ years, whatever its amount. Under simple interest it would take $\dfrac{1}{0.05} = 20$ years, and the value $13.86$ is obtained by dividing $\ln 2$ by $0.05$ instead of by $\ln 1.05$.
