An annuity pays $1000$ euros at times $2$, $4$ and $6$, measured in years, as shown in the figure. The annual rate is $i > 0$ under compound interest. Which expression gives the present value of the annuity?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-0.4,0 6.6,0" vettori="2,0,2,1.4,1000;4,0,4,1.4,1000;6,0,6,1.4,1000" unicolore=1 punti="0,0,pieno" etichette="0,-0.45,0;1,-0.45,1;2,-0.45,2;3,-0.45,3;4,-0.45,4;5,-0.45,5;6,-0.45,6;0,0.45,V" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=2 titolo="Payments of $1000$ euros at times $2$, $4$ and $6$; the present value $V$ is measured at time $0$"}

- $1000\,a_{\overline{3}|2i}$
- $1000\,a_{\overline{3}|j}$, with $j = (1 + i)^{2} - 1$
- $1000\,(1 + i)^{-1}\,a_{\overline{3}|i}$
- $1000\,a_{\overline{6}|i}$

## Explanation

The period of the annuity is two years, so the equivalent two-year rate $j$ is needed, defined by $1 + j = (1 + i)^{2}$. With respect to this period the annuity is a nondeferred ordinary annuity of $3$ payments, therefore its present value is $1000\,a_{\overline{3}|j}$. Indeed
$$
1000\,a_{\overline{3}|j} = 1000 \left[ (1 + j)^{-1} + (1 + j)^{-2} + (1 + j)^{-3} \right] = 1000 \left[ (1 + i)^{-2} + (1 + i)^{-4} + (1 + i)^{-6} \right] .
$$
The expression $1000\,a_{\overline{3}|2i}$ uses the proportional two-year rate, which is equivalent only under simple interest. The expression $1000\,(1 + i)^{-1}\,a_{\overline{3}|i}$ corresponds to payments at times $2$, $3$ and $4$.
