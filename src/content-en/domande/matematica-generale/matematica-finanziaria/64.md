Two debts of $1000$ euros are due in $1$ year and in $3$ years. At the annual rate of $10\%$ under compound interest, one looks for the average due date $t^*$, that is, the time at which the single payment of $2000$ euros is equivalent to the two debts. Which statement is true?

- $t^* < 2$ years
- $t^* = 2$ years
- $2 < t^* < 3$ years
- $t^* = 3$ years

## Explanation

The present value of the two debts is $1000 \cdot 1.1^{-1} + 1000 \cdot 1.1^{-3} \approx 909.09 + 751.31 = 1660.40$ euros. The average due date satisfies $2000 \cdot 1.1^{-t^*} = 1660.40$, so
$$
t^* = \frac{\ln 2000 - \ln 1660.40}{\ln 1.1} \approx 1.95 \text{ years} .
$$
Therefore $t^* < 2$ years, that is, the average due date under compound interest comes before the arithmetic mean of the two due dates. The reason is the convexity of the function $1.1^{-t}$, by which $\dfrac{1.1^{-1} + 1.1^{-3}}{2} > 1.1^{-2}$.
