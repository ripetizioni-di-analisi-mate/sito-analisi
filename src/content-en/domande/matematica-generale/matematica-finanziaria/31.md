A perpetuity-immediate pays $600$ euros at the end of every year, at the annual rate of $5\%$ under compound interest. Right after the tenth payment, what are the remaining payments worth?

- $12\,000$ euros
- $6000$ euros
- $19\,546.74$ euros
- $7366.96$ euros

## Explanation

Right after the tenth payment the payments at times $11, 12, \dots$ remain, and at date $10$ they form a perpetuity-immediate with payment $600$ euros. Therefore at date $10$ they are worth
$$
\frac{600}{0.05} = 12\,000 \text{ euros} ,
$$
the same value the perpetuity has today. Every year $12\,000$ euros earn interest of $0.05 \cdot 12\,000 = 600$ euros, equal to the payment made, so the value of the remaining payments stays constant. The value $6000$ euros subtracts the ten payments made from the present value without interest, $19\,546.74$ euros is the accumulated value of $12\,000$ euros after $10$ years, and $7366.96$ euros is the present value of $12\,000$ euros available in $10$ years.
