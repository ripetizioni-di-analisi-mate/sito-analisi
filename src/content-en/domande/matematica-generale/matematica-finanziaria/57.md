The effective annual rate is fixed at $10\%$. How does the equivalent nominal rate $j_k$, compounded $k$ times a year, vary as $k$ increases?

- It decreases and tends to $\ln 1.1$
- It increases and tends to $10\%$
- It stays equal to $10\%$
- It increases without bound

## Explanation

The equivalent periodic rate is $i_k = 1.1^{\frac{1}{k}} - 1$, so $j_k = k\,\left( 1.1^{\frac{1}{k}} - 1 \right)$. One has $j_1 = 10\%$, $j_2 \approx 9.76\%$, $j_4 \approx 9.65\%$ and $j_{12} \approx 9.57\%$, so $j_k$ **decreases**. Setting $x = \dfrac{1}{k}$ gives $j_k = \dfrac{1.1^{x} - 1}{x}$, which by the standard limit $\dfrac{a^{x} - 1}{x} \to \ln a$ tends to $\ln 1.1 \approx 9.53\%$ as $k \to +\infty$. The limit is the force of interest equivalent to the annual rate of $10\%$.
