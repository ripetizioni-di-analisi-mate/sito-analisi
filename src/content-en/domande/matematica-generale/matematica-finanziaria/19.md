Under compound interest at the annual rate $i > 0$, the accumulated value of a principal $C > 0$ is the function $M(t) = C\,(1 + i)^{t}$, with $t \geq 0$ in years. What is the ratio $\dfrac{M'(t)}{M(t)}$?

- $i$
- $t\,\ln(1 + i)$
- $\ln(1 + i)$
- $\dfrac{i}{1 + i}$

## Explanation

Since the derivative of $a^{t}$ is $a^{t}\ln a$, one has $M'(t) = C\,(1 + i)^{t}\ln(1 + i)$. Dividing by $M(t) = C\,(1 + i)^{t}$ gives
$$
\frac{M'(t)}{M(t)} = \ln(1 + i) .
$$
The ratio is therefore constant and equals the force of interest $\delta$ equivalent to the rate $i$. The value $\dfrac{i}{1 + i}$ is the annual discount rate, and $t\,\ln(1 + i)$ is the logarithm of the accumulation factor.
