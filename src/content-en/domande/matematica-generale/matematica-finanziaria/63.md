Compound interest at the annual rate of $5\%$ is compared with continuous compounding with force of interest $\delta = \ln 1.05$. For which terms $t \geq 0$ do the accumulated values of the same principal coincide?

- Only for $t = 1$
- For every $t \geq 0$
- Only for integer $t$
- Only for $t = 0$, because for $t > 0$ continuous compounding gives a larger accumulated value

## Explanation

The accumulated value under continuous compounding is $C\,e^{\delta t}$. Since $e^{\delta} = e^{\ln 1.05} = 1.05$, one has $e^{\delta t} = \left( e^{\delta} \right)^{t} = 1.05^{t}$ for every $t \geq 0$. Hence the two accumulated values coincide **for every term**, and the two regimes are equivalent. Continuous compounding gives a larger accumulated value when the force of interest equals the rate, that is, with $\delta = 5\%$.
