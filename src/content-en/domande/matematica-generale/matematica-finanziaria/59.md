A loan provides $5000$ euros today and requires a repayment of $5500$ euros in one year, that is, it has the cash flows $(5000,\ -5500)$ at times $0$ and $1$. For which valuation rates $i \geq 0$ is its NPV positive?

- For $0 \leq i < 10\%$
- For every $i \geq 0$
- For no $i \geq 0$
- For $i > 10\%$

## Explanation

The net present value of the transaction is
$$
\operatorname{VAN}(i) = 5000 - \frac{5500}{1 + i} .
$$
The NPV is positive if $\dfrac{5500}{1 + i} < 5000$, that is, if $1 + i > 1.1$, so for $i > 10\%$. The IRR of the transaction is $10\%$, but the first cash flow is positive and the second negative, therefore the theorem on the existence and uniqueness of the IRR does not apply, and the NPV is increasing in $i$. For the borrower the transaction is therefore convenient when the rate of the alternatives exceeds its cost, $10\%$.
