Three debts are due within one year, and their average due date is computed with commercial discount at the annual rate $d = 4\%$. What happens to the average due date if the rate $d = 8\%$ is used instead?

- It doubles
- It halves
- It increases, but by less than double
- It stays the same

## Explanation

With commercial discount the average due date $t^*$ satisfies $S\,(1 - d\,t^*) = \sum_h C_h\,(1 - d\,t_h)$, where $S = C_1 + C_2 + C_3$. Since $S = \sum_h C_h$, the condition reduces to $S\,d\,t^* = d \sum_h C_h\,t_h$, and dividing by $S\,d > 0$ gives
$$
t^* = \frac{C_1 t_1 + C_2 t_2 + C_3 t_3}{C_1 + C_2 + C_3} .
$$
The expression contains only the amounts and the due dates, so the average due date **stays the same** for every rate $d$ with all times less than $\dfrac{1}{d}$. With $d = 8\%$ one has $\dfrac{1}{d} = 12.5$ years, therefore the condition is satisfied.
