An amount available in $10$ years has present value $V$ at the annual rate of $5\%$ under compound interest. Which statement is true for the present value $V'$ of the same amount at the annual rate of $10\%$?

- $V' = \dfrac{V}{2}$
- $V' < \dfrac{V}{2}$
- $V' > V$
- $\dfrac{V}{2} < V' < V$

## Explanation

If $M$ is the amount, then $V = M \cdot 1.05^{-10}$ and $V' = M \cdot 1.1^{-10}$, so
$$
\frac{V'}{V} = \left( \frac{1.05}{1.1} \right)^{10} \approx 0.628 .
$$
Since $0.5 < 0.628 < 1$, one has $\dfrac{V}{2} < V' < V$. The present value decreases as the rate increases, and the decrease depends on the ratio of the factors $1.05$ and $1.1$ raised to the term, so doubling the rate does not halve the present value.
