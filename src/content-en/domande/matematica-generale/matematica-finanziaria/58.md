A principal is invested for one year under simple interest, either at the monthly rate of $0.5\%$ or at the annual rate of $6\%$. Which statement is true?

- The monthly rate of $0.5\%$ gives the larger accumulated value
- The two rates are equivalent
- The annual rate of $6\%$ gives the larger accumulated value
- The two rates are equivalent only for terms shorter than one year

## Explanation

A year contains $12$ months, so under simple interest the monthly rate gives the accumulation factor $1 + 0.005 \cdot 12 = 1.06$, equal to the factor $1 + 0.06$ of the annual rate. Therefore the two rates are **equivalent**. For any term of $t$ years the factors are $1 + 0.005 \cdot 12\,t$ and $1 + 0.06\,t$, which coincide, because under simple interest equivalent rates are proportional, $i_k = \dfrac{i}{k}$. Under compound interest, instead, the monthly rate of $0.5\%$ is equivalent to the annual rate $1.005^{12} - 1 \approx 6.17\%$, which is larger than $6\%$.
