Let $i > 0$ be an annual rate and let $\delta = \ln(1 + i)$ be the equivalent force of interest. For which values of $i$ does $\delta < i$ hold?

- Only for $0 < i < 1$
- Only for $i > 1$
- For no value of $i$
- For every $i > 0$

## Explanation

The function $g(i) = i - \ln(1 + i)$ equals $0$ at $i = 0$ and has derivative $g'(i) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{1 + i} = \dfrac{i}{1 + i}$, which is positive for $i > 0$. Hence $g$ is strictly increasing on $[0, +\infty)$, and $g(i) > g(0) = 0$ for every $i > 0$. Therefore $\ln(1 + i) < i$, that is, the inequality $\delta < i$ holds **for every** $i > 0$. For example the rate of $5\%$ corresponds to $\delta = \ln 1.05 \approx 4.879\%$.
