An investment requires $1000$ euros today and returns $400$ euros in one year and $500$ euros in two years. Which statement about its internal rate of return $i^* > -1$ is true?

- It exists and is negative
- It exists and is zero
- It exists and is positive
- It does not exist, because the inflows do not exceed the outflow

## Explanation

With $v = (1 + i)^{-1}$ the NPV is $-1000 + 400\,v + 500\,v^{2}$, and for $i = 0$, that is, $v = 1$, it equals $-100$ euros. Since the inflows are positive, the NPV is decreasing for $i > -1$, so for every $i \geq 0$ it is at most $-100$ euros and is never zero. The equation $500\,v^{2} + 400\,v - 1000 = 0$ has the positive root
$$
v = \frac{-400 + \sqrt{2\,160\,000}}{1000} \approx 1.0697 ,
$$
so $i^* = \dfrac{1}{v} - 1 \approx -6.52\%$. The IRR exists and is negative, because the inflows, $900$ euros in total, do not recover the initial outflow.
