Let $f(x) = x$ and let $g$ be the function equal to $1$ for $x \geq 0$ and $-1$ for $x < 0$. Which statement about the product $h(x) = f(x)\,g(x)$ at $x = 0$ is true?

- $h$ is continuous at $x = 0$, because $h(x) = |x|$
- $h$ has a jump discontinuity of size $2$ at $x = 0$
- $h$ is not continuous at $x = 0$, because $g$ is not continuous at $x = 0$
- $h$ has a removable discontinuity at $x = 0$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 0$ we have $h(x) = x \cdot 1 = x$, and for $x < 0$ we have $h(x) = x \cdot (-1) = -x$, so $h(x) = |x|$ for every $x$. The function $|x|$ has limit $0$ as $x \to 0$ and equals $0$ at $x = 0$, therefore $h$ is continuous at $x = 0$. The function $g$ has a jump of size $2$ at $x = 0$, so the rule on the continuity of a product of continuous functions does not apply. The product is continuous anyway, because $|h(x)| = |x|$ tends to $0$ whatever the sign of $g(x)$.
