Which of the following limits does not exist, either as a finite or as an infinite limit?

- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x^2}$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} e^{-x}$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \sin x$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \ln x$

## Explanation

The function $\sin x$ equals $1$ at the points $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$ and $-1$ at the points $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, which for integer $k$ exist beyond any threshold $N$. So for large $x$ it takes both the value $1$ and the value $-1$, and it neither stays close to any number $L$ nor exceeds every threshold; therefore $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \sin x$ **does not exist**. The other limits exist: $\dfrac{1}{x^2} \to +\infty$, $e^{-x} \to 0$ and $\ln x \to -\infty$.
