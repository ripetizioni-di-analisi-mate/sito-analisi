What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\ln\left( x^{5} \right)}{\sqrt{x}}$?

- $5$
- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$

## Explanation

By the properties of logarithms $\ln\left( x^{5} \right) = 5 \ln x$, and $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$. Hence
$$
\frac{\ln\left( x^{5} \right)}{\sqrt{x}} = 5 \cdot \frac{\ln x}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}} .
$$
By the hierarchy of growth rates, with $\beta = 1$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, the quotient $\dfrac{\ln x}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}$ tends to $0$. Therefore the limit is $5 \cdot 0 = 0$.
