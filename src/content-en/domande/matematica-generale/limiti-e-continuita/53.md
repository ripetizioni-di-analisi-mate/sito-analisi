What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{x\,(x + 1)} \right)$?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $-1$
- $1$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^+$ both terms tend to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$, and similarly as $x \to 0^-$. We write the difference over the common denominator $x\,(x + 1)$:
$$
\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{x\,(x + 1)} = \frac{(x + 1) - 1}{x\,(x + 1)} = \frac{x}{x\,(x + 1)} = \frac{1}{x + 1} \qquad (x \neq 0) .
$$
As $x \to 0$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x + 1} \to 1$, therefore the limit is $1$.
