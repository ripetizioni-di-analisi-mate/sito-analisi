What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\ln\left( x^3 + 1 \right)}{\ln x}$?

- $3$
- $1$
- $0$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Factoring $x^3$ out of the argument of the logarithm and using the properties of logarithms gives
$$
\ln\left( x^3 + 1 \right) = \ln\left[ x^3 \left( 1 + \frac{1}{x^3} \right) \right] = 3 \ln x + \ln\left( 1 + \frac{1}{x^3} \right) .
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{\ln\left( x^3 + 1 \right)}{\ln x} = 3 + \frac{\ln\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{x^3} \right)}{\ln x} .
$$
The numerator of the fraction tends to $\ln 1 = 0$ and its denominator to $+\infty$, so the fraction tends to $0$ and the limit equals $3$.
