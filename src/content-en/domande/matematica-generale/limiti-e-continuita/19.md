What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{x^4 - 3x}{2 - x^3}$?

- $+\infty$
- $-\infty$
- $-1$
- $0$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Factoring out $x^4$ from the numerator and $x^3$ from the denominator gives
$$
\frac{x^4 - 3x}{2 - x^3} = x \cdot \frac{1 - \dfrac{3}{x^3}}{\dfrac{2}{x^3} - 1} .
$$
The second factor tends to $-1$. Since $x \to -\infty$, the product of $x$ and a factor tending to $-1$ tends to $+\infty$, so the limit equals $+\infty$.
