As $x \to 2$ the function $f$ tends to $0$ and takes only positive values in a punctured neighborhood of $2$, while the function $g$ tends to $-3$. What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{g(x)}{f(x)}$?

- $-\infty$
- $+\infty$
- $0$
- It cannot be determined from the data

## Explanation

The quotient is written as a product, $\dfrac{g(x)}{f(x)} = g(x) \cdot \dfrac{1}{f(x)}$. Since $f(x) \to 0$ through positive values, $\dfrac{1}{f(x)} \to +\infty$. The product of a function tending to $-3$ and a function tending to $+\infty$ tends to $-\infty$, by the convention $a \cdot (+\infty) = -\infty$ for $a < 0$. Therefore the limit equals $-\infty$, and the form $\dfrac{-3}{0^{+}}$ is not indeterminate.
