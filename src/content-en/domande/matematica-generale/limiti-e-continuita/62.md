What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2^{x} - 1}{x}$?

- $\ln 2$
- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{\ln 2}$

## Explanation

By the properties of logarithms $2 = e^{\ln 2}$, so $2^{x} = e^{x \ln 2}$. We multiply and divide by $\ln 2$:
$$
\frac{2^{x} - 1}{x} = \frac{e^{x \ln 2} - 1}{x \ln 2} \cdot \ln 2 .
$$
As $x \to 0$ the quantity $x \ln 2$ also tends to $0$, so by the standard limit of the exponential the first factor tends to $1$. Therefore the limit is $\ln 2$.
