We have $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} (3x - 1) = 5$. For $\varepsilon = \dfrac{1}{2}$, what is the largest $\delta > 0$ such that $0 < |x - 2| < \delta$ implies $|(3x - 1) - 5| < \varepsilon$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}$

## Explanation

We have $|(3x - 1) - 5| = |3x - 6| = 3\,|x - 2|$. The inequality $3\,|x - 2| < \dfrac{1}{2}$ is therefore equivalent to $|x - 2| < \dfrac{1}{6}$. Hence the implication holds for every $\delta \leq \dfrac{1}{6}$, and the largest value is $\delta = \dfrac{1}{6}$. The value $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is obtained by multiplying $\varepsilon$ by $3$ instead of dividing it.
