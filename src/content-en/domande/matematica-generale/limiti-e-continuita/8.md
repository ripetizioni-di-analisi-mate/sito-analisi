- $1$
- $e^{\frac{1}{3}}$
- $e^{3}$
- $3e$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$. With the change of variable $t = \dfrac{x}{3}$, which tends to $+\infty$, we have $\dfrac{3}{x} = \dfrac{1}{t}$ and $x = 3t$. Therefore
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{3}{x} \right)^{x} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{3} .
$$
By the **standard limit** $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} = e$ the bracketed power tends to $e$, hence the limit equals $e^{3}$.
