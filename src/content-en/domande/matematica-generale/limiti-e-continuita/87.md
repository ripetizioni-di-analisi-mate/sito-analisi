What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left[ \ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right) - 2 \ln x \right]$?

- $+\infty$
- $0$
- $1$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

Both terms tend to $+\infty$, so the limit has the form $+\infty - \infty$. By the properties of logarithms, with $x > 0$, we have
$$
\ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right) - 2 \ln x = \ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right) - \ln\left( x^2 \right) = \ln \frac{x^2 + 1}{x^2} = \ln\left( 1 + \frac{1}{x^2} \right) .
$$
The argument tends to $1$ and the logarithm is continuous at $1$, therefore the limit is $\ln 1 = 0$.
