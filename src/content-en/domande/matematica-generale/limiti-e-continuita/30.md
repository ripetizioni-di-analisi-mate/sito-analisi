What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{2}{x} \right)^{3x}$?

- $e^{6}$
- $e^{-6}$
- $e^{-\frac{2}{3}}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$. With the change of variable $t = -\dfrac{x}{2}$, which tends to $-\infty$, we have $-\dfrac{2}{x} = \dfrac{1}{t}$ and $3x = -6t$. Therefore
$$
\left( 1 - \frac{2}{x} \right)^{3x} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{-6} .
$$
By the **standard limit** $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to -\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} = e$ the bracketed power tends to $e$, so the limit equals $e^{-6}$.
