The function $f$ is continuous at $x = 0$, while the function $g$ has a jump discontinuity of size $3$ at $x = 0$. Which statement about the sum $f + g$ at $x = 0$ is true?

- $f + g$ is continuous at $x = 0$ if $f(0) = -g(0)$
- $f + g$ has a jump discontinuity of size $3$ at $x = 0$
- $f + g$ has a removable discontinuity at $x = 0$
- $f + g$ has a discontinuity of the second kind at $x = 0$

## Explanation

Let $A$ and $B$ be the left-hand and right-hand limits of $g$ at $0$, which are finite and satisfy $|B - A| = 3$. Since $f$ is continuous at $0$, its left-hand and right-hand limits both equal $f(0)$. By the limit laws, the left-hand limit of $f + g$ is $f(0) + A$ and the right-hand limit is $f(0) + B$. These two limits are finite and differ by $B - A$, so $f + g$ has a jump discontinuity of size $3$ at $0$, whatever $f$ is. In particular the sum is not continuous even when $f(0) = -g(0)$, because the right-hand and left-hand limits remain different.
