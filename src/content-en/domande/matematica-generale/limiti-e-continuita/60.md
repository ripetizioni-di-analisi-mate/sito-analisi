What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{\sin(2x)}$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$
- $10$

## Explanation

Numerator and denominator tend to $0$, so the limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. We multiply and divide by $5x$ and by $2x$:
$$
\frac{\sin(5x)}{\sin(2x)} = \frac{\sin(5x)}{5x} \cdot \frac{2x}{\sin(2x)} \cdot \frac{5x}{2x} .
$$
By the standard limit of the sine the first two factors tend to $1$, and the third equals $\dfrac{5}{2}$ for every $x \neq 0$. Therefore the limit is $\dfrac{5}{2}$.
