What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(4x)}{x^2 + 2x}$?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $x^2 + 2x = x(x + 2)$, for $x \neq 0$ we write
$$
\frac{\sin(4x)}{x(x + 2)} = \frac{\sin(4x)}{4x} \cdot \frac{4}{x + 2} .
$$
By the **standard limit** $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\sin t}{t} = 1$, with $t = 4x$, the first factor tends to $1$, and the second tends to $\dfrac{4}{2} = 2$. Therefore the limit equals $2$. The value $4$ results from leaving out the factor $x + 2$ in the denominator.
