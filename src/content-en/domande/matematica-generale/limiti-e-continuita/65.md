Which of the following functions, positive for $x > 1$, satisfies both conditions
$$
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\ln x}{f(x)} = 0 \\[10pt]
f(x) < x \quad \text{for every } x > 1
\end{cases}
$$

- $f(x) = x$
- $f(x) = x^2$
- $f(x) = \ln x$
- $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$

## Explanation

By the **comparison of infinities** $\dfrac{\ln x}{x^{\alpha}} \to 0$ for every $\alpha > 0$, so the first condition is satisfied by $x$, by $x^2$ and by $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$. For $f(x) = \ln x$ the ratio is constantly $1$, therefore the first condition is not satisfied. For $x > 1$ one has $x^2 > x$, and the function $f(x) = x$ gives the equality $f(x) = x$, so neither of them satisfies the second condition. For $x > 1$ one has instead $\sqrt{x} > 1$, therefore $x = \sqrt{x} \cdot \sqrt{x} > \sqrt{x}$. The required function is thus $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$, which grows faster than the logarithm and more slowly than $x$.
