For $x\to+\infty$ we know $f(x)>0$ and $\dfrac{\ln x}{f(x)}\to0$. Which function shows that this does not necessarily imply $f(x)\geq x$ for all sufficiently large $x$?

- $f(x)=x$
- $f(x)=x^2$
- $f(x)=\ln x$
- $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$

## Explanation

The square root grows faster than the logarithm and more slowly than $x$. It therefore satisfies the hypothesis and violates the proposed conclusion for every $x>1$. A counterexample must meet both conditions.
