What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \sqrt{x}\, \ln x$?

- $-\infty$
- $1$
- $+\infty$
- $0$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^+$ the factor $\sqrt{x}$ tends to $0$ and $\ln x$ tends to $-\infty$, so the limit has the form $0 \cdot \infty$. We set $t = \dfrac{1}{x}$, which tends to $+\infty$; since $\ln x = -\ln t$ and $\sqrt{x} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}$, we obtain
$$
\sqrt{x}\, \ln x = -\frac{\ln t}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}} .
$$
By the hierarchy of growth rates the quotient $\dfrac{\ln t}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}$ tends to $0$, therefore the limit is $0$.
