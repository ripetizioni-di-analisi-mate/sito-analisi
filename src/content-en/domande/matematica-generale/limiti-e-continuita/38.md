At $x = 0$, what type of discontinuity does $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{|x|}$ have?

- Removable
- Jump discontinuity of size $2$
- Jump discontinuity of size $1$
- Of the second kind

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ we have $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$, which tends to $1$ by the standard sine limit. For $x < 0$ we have $|x| = -x$, so $f(x) = -\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$, which tends to $-1$. The right-hand and left-hand limits are finite and unequal, so at $x = 0$ there is a **jump discontinuity** of size $1 - (-1) = 2$.
