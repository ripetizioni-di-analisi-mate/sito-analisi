What type of discontinuity does the function $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{|x|}$ have at $x = 0$?

- Removable
- Jump, of size $2$
- Jump, of size $1$
- Of the second kind

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ we have $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$, which tends to $1$ by the standard limit of the sine. For $x < 0$ we have $|x| = -x$, so $f(x) = -\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$, which tends to $-1$. The right-hand and left-hand limits exist, are finite and are different, therefore at $x = 0$ there is a **jump discontinuity**, of size $1 - (-1) = 2$.
