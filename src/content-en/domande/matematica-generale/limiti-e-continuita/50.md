What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} x^{\frac{1}{x}}$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $e$

## Explanation

The base tends to $+\infty$ and the exponent $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $0$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $\infty^{0}$. For $x > 0$ we write the power as an exponential:
$$
x^{\frac{1}{x}} = e^{\frac{\ln x}{x}} .
$$
By the hierarchy of growth rates, with $\beta = 1$ and $\alpha = 1$, the exponent $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$ tends to $0$. Since the exponential function is continuous at $0$, the limit equals $e^{0} = 1$.
