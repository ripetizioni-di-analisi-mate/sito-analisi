What are the right-hand and left-hand limits as $x \to 0$ of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{\frac{1}{x}}}$?

- Right-hand $1$, left-hand $0$
- Right-hand $\dfrac{1}{2}$, left-hand $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- Right-hand $0$, left-hand $+\infty$
- Right-hand $0$, left-hand $1$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^+$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to +\infty$, so $e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to +\infty$ and the denominator tends to $+\infty$. Therefore the right-hand limit is $0$. As $x \to 0^-$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to -\infty$, so $e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to 0$ and the denominator tends to $1$. Therefore the left-hand limit is $1$, and the function has a jump at $x = 0$.
