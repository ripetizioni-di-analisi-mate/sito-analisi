What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} x \left( e^{\frac{1}{x}} - 1 \right)$?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $e$
- $1$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ the factor $x$ tends to $+\infty$ and $e^{\frac{1}{x}} - 1$ tends to $e^{0} - 1 = 0$, so the limit has the form $0 \cdot \infty$. We set $t = \dfrac{1}{x}$, which tends to $0^+$, and obtain
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} x \left( e^{\frac{1}{x}} - 1 \right) = \lim_{t \to 0^{+}} \frac{e^{t} - 1}{t} .
$$
By the standard limit of the exponential, the last limit equals $1$.
