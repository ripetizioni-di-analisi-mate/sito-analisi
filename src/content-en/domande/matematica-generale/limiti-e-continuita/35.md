What is the right oblique asymptote of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 4x}$?

- $y = x + 2$
- $y = x + 4$
- $y = x$
- $y = 2x$

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ we have $\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} = x\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{x}}$, so $m = \displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = 1$. To compute $q$ we multiply and divide by $\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x$, and we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= \lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x \right) = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{4x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x} \\[6pt]
&= \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{4}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{x}} + 1} = 2 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Both limits are finite and $m \neq 0$, so the line $y = x + 2$ is the right **oblique asymptote**. The value $q = 4$ is obtained by forgetting that the denominator tends to $2$.
