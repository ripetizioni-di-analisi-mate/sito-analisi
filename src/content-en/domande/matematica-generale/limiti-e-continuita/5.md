- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $+\infty$
- $0$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ the numerator and the denominator diverge, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Factoring out $x^3$ in the numerator and $x$ in the denominator we obtain
$$
\frac{x^3 - 2}{5x + 1} = x^2 \cdot \frac{1 - \dfrac{2}{x^3}}{5 + \dfrac{1}{x}} .
$$
The second factor tends to $\dfrac{1}{5}$, which is positive, and $x^2 \to +\infty$. Therefore the limit equals $+\infty$.
