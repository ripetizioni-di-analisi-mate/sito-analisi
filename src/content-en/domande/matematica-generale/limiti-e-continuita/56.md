For $x > 1$ consider the functions $f(x) = 3x + \sqrt{x}$ and $g(x) = 3x - \sqrt{x}$, for which $\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} \to 1$ as $x \to +\infty$. What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \big( f(x) - g(x) \big)$?

- $0$
- $1$
- $+\infty$
- $2$

## Explanation

The difference is $f(x) - g(x) = 3x + \sqrt{x} - 3x + \sqrt{x} = 2\sqrt{x}$. Since $\sqrt{x} \to +\infty$, the difference tends to $+\infty$. The ratio instead tends to $1$, because factoring out $x$ gives $\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \dfrac{3 + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}}{3 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}}$ and $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \to 0$. Therefore the limit of the ratio does not determine the limit of the difference, which tends to $+\infty$ while remaining negligible with respect to $3x$. The value $0$ comes from deducing the limit of the difference from that of the ratio, and the value $1$ from confusing the difference with the ratio.
