- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by $\sqrt{1 + x} + 1$ turns the numerator into the difference of squares $(1 + x) - 1 = x$, and for $x \neq 0$ we obtain
$$
\frac{\sqrt{1 + x} - 1}{x} = \frac{x}{x \left( \sqrt{1 + x} + 1 \right)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x} + 1} .
$$
The last function is continuous at $x = 0$, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1} + 1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
