What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\ln x}{x - 1}$?

- $0$
- $e$
- $+\infty$
- $1$

## Explanation

At $x = 1$ we have $\ln 1 = 0$, so the limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. We set $t = x - 1$, which tends to $0$ as $x \to 1$; then $x = 1 + t$ and
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\ln x}{x - 1} = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\ln(1 + t)}{t} .
$$
By the standard limit of the logarithm, the last limit equals $1$.
