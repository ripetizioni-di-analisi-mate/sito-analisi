What is $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\ln\big[ (1 + h)(1 - h) \big]}{h^2}$?

- $0$
- $1$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

As $h \to 0$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $(1 + h)(1 - h) = 1 - h^2$, set $t = -h^2$, which tends to $0$ as $h \to 0$, and one obtains
$$
\frac{\ln\left( 1 - h^2 \right)}{h^2} = -\frac{\ln(1 + t)}{t} .
$$
By the **standard limit** of the logarithm $\dfrac{\ln(1 + t)}{t} \to 1$, therefore the limit equals $-1$. The value $1$ comes from losing the minus sign of $t = -h^2$, and the value $0$ from approximating $\ln(1 + h)$ by $h$ and $\ln(1 - h)$ by $-h$, two terms that cancel each other.
