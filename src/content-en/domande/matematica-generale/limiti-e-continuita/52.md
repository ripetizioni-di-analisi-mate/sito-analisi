What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( 2^{x} - 3^{x} \right)$?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $-\infty$
- $-1$

## Explanation

Both terms tend to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$. We factor out the term that grows faster, namely $3^{x}$, and obtain
$$
2^{x} - 3^{x} = 3^{x} \left[ \left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{x} - 1 \right] .
$$
Since $0 < \dfrac{2}{3} < 1$, we have $\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{x} \to 0$, so the expression in square brackets tends to $-1$. Therefore the product tends to $-\infty$.
