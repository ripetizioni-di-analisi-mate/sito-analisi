The demand for a good is a continuous function $D(p)$ of its price on the interval $[2, 6]$, with $D(2) = 500$ and $D(6) = 100$. Which statement is guaranteed by the intermediate value theorem?

- $D(4) = 300$
- $D$ is decreasing on $[2, 6]$
- $D(p) \leq 500$ for every $p \in [2, 6]$
- There exists a price $p \in (2, 6)$ such that $D(p) = 300$

## Explanation

The function $D$ is continuous on $[2, 6]$ and the value $300$ lies between $D(6) = 100$ and $D(2) = 500$. The **intermediate value theorem** therefore gives a price $p \in (2, 6)$ such that $D(p) = 300$. The theorem does not say at which point this happens, so the equality $D(4) = 300$ is not guaranteed. The other two statements concern how $D$ behaves between the endpoints. The values $D(2)$ and $D(6)$ alone give no information about that behavior.
