Which of the following limits has an indeterminate form?

- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \frac{\ln x}{x}$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} (x + \ln x)$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} x \ln x$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} x^{\frac{1}{x}}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $x \to 0$ and $\ln x \to -\infty$, so $x \ln x$ is in the form $0 \cdot \infty$, which is **indeterminate**. The quotient $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$ has a numerator tending to $-\infty$ and a denominator tending to $0^{+}$, so it tends to $-\infty$. The sum $x + \ln x$ as $x \to +\infty$ is in the form $+\infty + \infty$, which equals $+\infty$. Finally, $x^{\frac{1}{x}}$ is in the form $0^{+\infty}$, which is a determinate form, and tends to $0$.
