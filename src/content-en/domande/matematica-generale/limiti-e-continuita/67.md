Which statement about the oblique asymptotes of the function $f(x) = x + 2 + e^{-x}$ is true?

- The line $y = x + 2$ is an oblique asymptote both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$
- The line $y = x + 2$ is an oblique asymptote as $x \to +\infty$, and there is no oblique asymptote as $x \to -\infty$
- The line $y = x + 3$ is an oblique asymptote as $x \to +\infty$
- The function has no oblique asymptotes

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ we have $e^{-x} \to 0$, so $f(x) - (x + 2) = e^{-x} \to 0$ and the line $y = x + 2$ is a right oblique asymptote. As $x \to -\infty$ we have $e^{-x} \to +\infty$, and by the hierarchy of growth rates $\dfrac{e^{-x}}{x} \to -\infty$. Therefore $m = \displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = -\infty$, which is not finite, and there is no oblique asymptote on the left.
