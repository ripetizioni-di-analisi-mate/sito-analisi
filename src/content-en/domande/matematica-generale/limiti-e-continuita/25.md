What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x}$?

- $1$
- $0$
- The limit does not exist
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ the function $\sin x$ takes the values $-1$ and $1$ infinitely many times, so it has no limit and the limit laws do not apply to the quotient. Since $|\sin x| \leq 1$, for $x > 0$ we have
$$
\left| \frac{\sin x}{x} - 0 \right| \leq \frac{1}{x} .
$$
Given $\varepsilon > 0$, we have $\dfrac{1}{x} < \varepsilon$ for every $x > \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}$. Therefore the definition of the limit as $x \to +\infty$ is satisfied with $N = \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}$, and the limit equals $0$. The standard limit $\dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 1$ concerns $x \to 0$ instead.
