What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{4x^2 + 3x} - 2x \right)$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $0$
- $3$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$. We multiply and divide by $\sqrt{4x^2 + 3x} + 2x$, so that the numerator becomes $(4x^2 + 3x) - 4x^2 = 3x$. For $x > 0$, we have $\sqrt{4x^2 + 3x} = x\sqrt{4 + \dfrac{3}{x}}$. Dividing by $x$ therefore gives
$$
\sqrt{4x^2 + 3x} - 2x = \frac{3x}{\sqrt{4x^2 + 3x} + 2x} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{4 + \dfrac{3}{x}} + 2} .
$$
The denominator tends to $\sqrt{4} + 2 = 4$, so the limit equals $\dfrac{3}{4}$.
