- $0$
- $6$
- $+\infty$
- $3$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ both terms diverge, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$. Multiplying and dividing by the sum $\sqrt{x^2 + 6x} + x$ turns the numerator into the difference of squares $(x^2 + 6x) - x^2 = 6x$. For $x > 0$, we have $\sqrt{x^2 + 6x} = x \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{6}{x}}$. Dividing the numerator and denominator by $x$ therefore gives
$$
\sqrt{x^2 + 6x} - x = \frac{6x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 6x} + x} = \frac{6}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{6}{x}} + 1} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{6}{x} \to 0$, the denominator tends to $2$, so the limit equals $\dfrac{6}{2} = 3$.
