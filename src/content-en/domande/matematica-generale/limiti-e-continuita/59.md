What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{x + 1}{x - 1} \right)^{x}$?

- $1$
- $e^{2}$
- $e$
- $e^{-2}$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit has the form $1^{\infty}$. We write the base as $\dfrac{x + 1}{x - 1} = 1 + \dfrac{2}{x - 1}$ and set $t = \dfrac{x - 1}{2}$, that is $x = 2t + 1$. Then
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{2}{x - 1} \right)^{x} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{2} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right) .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ the first factor tends to $e^{2}$ by the standard limit of $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{t} \right)^{t}$, and the second tends to $1$. Therefore the limit is $e^{2}$.
