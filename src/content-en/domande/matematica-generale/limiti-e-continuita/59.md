In $\lim_{h\to0}(1+2h)^{1/h}$ a student substitutes $u=2h$ and obtains $e$. What exponent actually appears after the substitution?

- $\dfrac1u$
- $\dfrac2u$
- $\dfrac1{2u}$
- $\dfrac1{u^2}$

## Explanation

From $h=\dfrac u2$ we have $\dfrac1h=\dfrac2u$. The expression is the square of $(1+u)^{1/u}$ and tends to $e^2$. The factor of two in the exponent accounts for the difference.
