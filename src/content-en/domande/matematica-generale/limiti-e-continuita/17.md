What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{-}} e^{\frac{1}{x}}$?

- $+\infty$
- $0$
- $1$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{-}$, the variable $x$ approaches $0$ through negative values, so $\dfrac{1}{x} \to -\infty$. Since $e^{y} \to 0$ as $y \to -\infty$, we obtain $e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to 0$. The value $+\infty$ is the right-hand limit because as $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to +\infty$. The value $-\infty$ is impossible because the exponential is always positive.
