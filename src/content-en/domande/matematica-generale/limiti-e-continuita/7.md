- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $5$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. For $x \neq 0$ we write
$$
\frac{\ln(1 + 5x)}{2x} = \frac{\ln(1 + 5x)}{5x} \cdot \frac{5}{2} .
$$
With the change of variable $t = 5x$, which tends to $0$, the first factor tends to $1$ by the **standard limit of the logarithm** $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\ln(1 + t)}{t} = 1$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{5}{2}$.
