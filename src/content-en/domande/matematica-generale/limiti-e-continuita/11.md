- Vertical $x = -2$, horizontal $y = 3$
- Vertical $x = 2$, horizontal $y = 3$
- Vertical $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$, horizontal $y = 3$
- Vertical $x = -2$, horizontal $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The function is defined for $x \neq -2$. As $x \to -2$ the numerator tends to $-7 \neq 0$ and the denominator tends to $0$, so $f$ tends to $\pm\infty$ and the line $x = -2$ is a **vertical asymptote**. As $x \to \pm\infty$ the numerator and the denominator have the same degree, therefore $f(x) \to \dfrac{3}{1} = 3$ and the line $y = 3$ is a **horizontal asymptote** both on the right and on the left. Since there is a horizontal asymptote, there are no oblique asymptotes. The point $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ is a zero of the numerator, hence the function equals $0$ there.
