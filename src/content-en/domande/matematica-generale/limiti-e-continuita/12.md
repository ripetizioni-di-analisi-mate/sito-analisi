- Vertical asymptote $x = 1$, oblique asymptote $y = 2x$
- Vertical asymptote $x = 1$, oblique asymptote $y = 2x - 2$
- Vertical asymptote $x = 1$, oblique asymptote $y = 2x + 2$
- Vertical asymptote $x = 1$, horizontal asymptote $y = 2$

## Explanation

As $x \to 1$ the numerator tends to $3$ and the denominator to $0$, so the line $x = 1$ is a **vertical asymptote**. The numerator has a higher degree than the denominator, so $f$ diverges as $x \to \pm\infty$ and there are no horizontal asymptotes. For the oblique asymptote we compute
$$
m = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2x^2 + 1}{x\,(x - 1)} = 2,
\qquad
q = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \left( \frac{2x^2 + 1}{x - 1} - 2x \right) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2x + 1}{x - 1} = 2 .
$$
Both limits are finite and $m \neq 0$, hence the line $y = 2x + 2$ is an **oblique asymptote** on both sides.
