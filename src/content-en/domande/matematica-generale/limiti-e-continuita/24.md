What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x - 3}{\sqrt{x + 1} - 2}$?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$
- $0$

## Explanation

At $x = 3$ the numerator and the denominator equal $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. We multiply the numerator and the denominator by $\sqrt{x + 1} + 2$, so that the denominator becomes the difference of squares $(x + 1) - 4 = x - 3$, and for $x \neq 3$ we obtain
$$
\frac{x - 3}{\sqrt{x + 1} - 2} = \frac{(x - 3)\left( \sqrt{x + 1} + 2 \right)}{x - 3} = \sqrt{x + 1} + 2 .
$$
The resulting function is continuous at $x = 3$, so the limit equals $\sqrt{4} + 2 = 4$.
