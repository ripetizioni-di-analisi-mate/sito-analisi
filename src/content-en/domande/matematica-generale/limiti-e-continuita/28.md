What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{3x} - 1}{\ln(1 + 2x)}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $6$
- $1$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. For $x \neq 0$ we write
$$
\frac{e^{3x} - 1}{\ln(1 + 2x)} = \frac{e^{3x} - 1}{3x} \cdot \frac{2x}{\ln(1 + 2x)} \cdot \frac{3}{2} .
$$
By the **standard exponential limit**, with $t = 3x \to 0$, the first factor tends to $1$. By the **standard logarithmic limit**, with $t = 2x \to 0$, the second factor tends to $1$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
