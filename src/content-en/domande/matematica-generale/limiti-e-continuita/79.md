How many real solutions does the equation $x^3 - 4x + 1 = 0$ have?

- $1$
- $3$
- $2$
- $0$

## Explanation

The function $f(x) = x^3 - 4x + 1$ is a polynomial, so it is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. We have $f(-3) = -14$, $f(-2) = 1$, $f(0) = 1$, $f(1) = -2$ and $f(2) = 1$. The sign changes on the intervals $(-3, -2)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 2)$, so by **Bolzano's theorem** there is at least one solution in each of them. A polynomial equation of degree three has at most three real solutions, therefore there are exactly $3$ solutions.
