Each of the following functions is defined by the given formula for $x \neq 0$ and by the given value at $x = 0$. Which one is continuous at $x = 0$?

- $f(x) = \dfrac{|x|}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{|x|}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{e^{x}}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 1$

## Explanation

For $x \neq 0$ we have $\dfrac{x^2}{|x|} = \dfrac{|x|^2}{|x|} = |x|$, which tends to $0 = f(0)$, so this function is **continuous** at $0$. The function $\dfrac{|x|}{x}$ equals $1$ for $x > 0$ and $-1$ for $x < 0$, so it has a jump at $0$. By the standard limit of the sine $\dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 1$, which differs from the assigned value $0$. Finally $\dfrac{e^{x}}{x} \to +\infty$ as $x \to 0^+$, so the limit is not finite.
