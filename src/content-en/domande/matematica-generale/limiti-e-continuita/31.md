A capital of $1000$ euros is invested for $3$ years at an annual rate of $4\%$, compounded $n$ times a year; the accumulated amount is $M(n) = 1000\left( 1 + \dfrac{0.04}{n} \right)^{3n}$. What value does $M(n)$ tend to as $n \to +\infty$?

- $1000 \cdot 1.04^{3}$
- $1000\,e^{0.04}$
- $1000\,e^{3}$
- $1000\,e^{0.12}$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$. With the change of variable $t = \dfrac{n}{0.04}$, which tends to $+\infty$, we have $\dfrac{0.04}{n} = \dfrac{1}{t}$ and $3n = 0.12\,t$, therefore
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{0.04}{n} \right)^{3n} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{0.12} \to e^{0.12}
$$
by the **standard limit** of the number $e$. The accumulated amount therefore tends to $1000\,e^{0.12}$, about $1127.50$ euros, which is the accumulated amount under continuous compounding. The value $1000 \cdot 1.04^{3}$ is the accumulated amount with a single annual compounding, and $1000\,e^{0.04}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $3$ in the exponent.
