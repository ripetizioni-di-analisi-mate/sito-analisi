A capital of $1000$ euros is invested for one year at an annual rate of $5\%$, compounded $n$ times a year; the accumulated amount is $M(n) = 1000\left( 1 + \dfrac{0.05}{n} \right)^{n}$. What value does $M(n)$ tend to as $n \to +\infty$?

- $1050$
- $1000\,e$
- $1000\,e^{20}$
- $1000\,e^{0.05}$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$. With the change of variable $t = \dfrac{n}{0.05}$, which tends to $+\infty$, we have $\dfrac{0.05}{n} = \dfrac{1}{t}$ and $n = 0.05\,t$, therefore
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{0.05}{n} \right)^{n} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{0.05} \to e^{0.05}
$$
by the **standard limit** of the number $e$. The accumulated amount therefore tends to $1000\,e^{0.05}$, about $1051.27$ euros, which is the accumulated amount under continuous compounding. The value $1050$ is the accumulated amount with a single annual compounding.
