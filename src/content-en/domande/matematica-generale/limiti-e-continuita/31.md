An initial investment of $1000$ euros is held for $3$ years at an annual interest rate of $4\%$, compounded $n$ times a year. The accumulated amount is $M(n) = 1000\left( 1 + \dfrac{0{,}04}{n} \right)^{3n}$. What is the limit of $M(n)$ as $n \to +\infty$?

- $1000 \cdot 1{,}04^{3}$
- $1000\,e^{0{,}04}$
- $1000\,e^{3}$
- $1000\,e^{0{,}12}$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$. With the change of variable $t = \dfrac{n}{0{,}04}$, which tends to $+\infty$, we have $\dfrac{0{,}04}{n} = \dfrac{1}{t}$ and $3n = 0{,}12\,t$. Therefore
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{0{,}04}{n} \right)^{3n} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{0{,}12} \to e^{0{,}12}
$$
by the **standard limit** defining $e$. The accumulated amount therefore tends to $1000\,e^{0{,}12}$, about $1127{,}50$ euros, which is the accumulated amount under continuous compounding. The value $1000 \cdot 1{,}04^{3}$ is the accumulated amount with compounding once a year, and $1000\,e^{0{,}04}$ results from omitting the factor $3$ in the exponent.
