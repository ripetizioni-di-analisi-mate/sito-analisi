The profit $\pi(q)$ of a firm is a continuous function of the quantity produced $q$, and its production capacity allows it to produce from $0$ to $100$ units, that is, $q \in [0, 100]$. Which statement is guaranteed by the extreme value theorem?

- $\pi(100) \geq \pi(q)$ for every $q \in [0, 100]$
- There exists $q \in [0, 100]$ such that $\pi(q) > 0$
- There is exactly one quantity $q \in [0, 100]$ that maximizes the profit
- There exists $q^{*} \in [0, 100]$ such that $\pi(q) \leq \pi(q^{*})$ for every $q \in [0, 100]$

## Explanation

The hypotheses of the **extreme value theorem** are satisfied, because $\pi$ is continuous on the closed and bounded interval $[0, 100]$. The theorem guarantees that $\pi$ has a maximum on $[0, 100]$, that is, there exists $q^{*}$ with $\pi(q) \leq \pi(q^{*})$ for every $q$ in the interval. The theorem does not say at which point the maximum is attained, so it does not guarantee that it is $q = 100$, and it does not say that the maximum point is unique. It says nothing about the sign of the values either: an always negative profit, such as $\pi(q) = -q - 50$, still has maximum $-50$ at $q = 0$.
