Which asymptotes does the graph of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{1 + e^{-x}}$ have as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$?

- The line $y = x$ is an oblique asymptote both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$
- The line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote as $x \to +\infty$ and the line $y = x$ is an oblique asymptote as $x \to -\infty$
- The line $y = x$ is an oblique asymptote as $x \to +\infty$ and the line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote as $x \to -\infty$
- The line $y = 1$ is a horizontal asymptote as $x \to +\infty$ and the line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote as $x \to -\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ we have $e^{-x} \to 0$, so $\dfrac{f(x)}{x} = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-x}} \to 1$, hence $m = 1$. Then
$$
f(x) - x = \frac{x - x - x\,e^{-x}}{1 + e^{-x}} = -\frac{x\,e^{-x}}{1 + e^{-x}} \to 0 ,
$$
because $x\,e^{-x} = \dfrac{x}{e^{x}} \to 0$ by the hierarchy of growth rates; so $q = 0$ and the line $y = x$ is an oblique asymptote on the right. As $x \to -\infty$ we multiply numerator and denominator by $e^{x}$ and get $f(x) = \dfrac{x\,e^{x}}{e^{x} + 1}$. With $t = -x$ we have $x\,e^{x} = -\dfrac{t}{e^{t}} \to 0$, while the denominator tends to $1$, so $f(x) \to 0$ and the line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote on the left.
