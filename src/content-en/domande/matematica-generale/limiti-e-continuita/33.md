What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^3 - 2^{x}}{x^3 + 2^{x}}$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $-1$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Dividing the numerator and denominator by the faster-growing term $2^{x}$ gives
$$
\frac{x^3 - 2^{x}}{x^3 + 2^{x}} = \frac{\dfrac{x^3}{2^{x}} - 1}{\dfrac{x^3}{2^{x}} + 1} .
$$
By the **hierarchy of growth rates**, we have $\dfrac{x^3}{2^{x}} \to 0$, so the limit equals $\dfrac{0 - 1}{0 + 1} = -1$. The value $1$ is obtained by neglecting $2^{x}$ compared with $x^3$, that is, by reversing the comparison of the growth rates.
