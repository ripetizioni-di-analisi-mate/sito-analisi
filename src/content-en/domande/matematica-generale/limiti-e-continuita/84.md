What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \sqrt{\frac{9x + 1}{x + 4}}$?

- $9$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $3$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ the argument of the square root is a quotient of polynomials of degree one, and factoring out $x$ gives $\dfrac{9 + \dfrac{1}{x}}{1 + \dfrac{4}{x}} \to 9$. The function $\sqrt{y}$ is continuous at $y = 9$, so the limit of the composition is obtained by taking the square root of the limit of the argument. Therefore the limit is $\sqrt{9} = 3$.
