What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + x} - \sqrt{x^2 - x} \right)$?

- $0$
- $2$
- $1$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

Both roots tend to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$. By **rationalisation** one multiplies and divides by the sum of the two roots, and the numerator becomes $\left( x^2 + x \right) - \left( x^2 - x \right) = 2x$. Factoring out $x$ in the denominator, for $x > 1$ one obtains
$$
\frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2 + x} + \sqrt{x^2 - x}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} + \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{x}}} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{x} \to 0$, the denominator tends to $2$, therefore the limit equals $1$. The value $2$ comes from approximating the sum of the roots by $x$ instead of $2x$, and the value $0$ from treating the two roots as equal because they have the same term $x^2$.
