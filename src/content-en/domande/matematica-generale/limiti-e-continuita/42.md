The function $f$ is continuous on the interval $[0, 1]$, with $f(0) = 0.3$ and $f(1) = 0.6$. Which of the following equations certainly has at least one solution in $[0, 1]$?

- $f(x) = 0$
- $f(x) = x$
- $f(x) = 1$
- $f(x) = x + 1$

## Explanation

The equation $f(x) = x$ is equivalent to $g(x) = 0$, with $g(x) = f(x) - x$. The function $g$ is continuous on $[0, 1]$ as a difference of continuous functions, and $g(0) = 0.3 > 0$, $g(1) = 0.6 - 1 = -0.4 < 0$. The hypotheses of **Bolzano's theorem** are satisfied, so $g$ vanishes at a point of $(0, 1)$, where $f(x) = x$. The other equations have no solutions for the function $f(x) = 0.3 + 0.3x$, which is continuous and takes the given values at the endpoints, because on $[0, 1]$ this function lies between $0.3$ and $0.6$ and stays smaller than $x + 1$.
