The equation $e^{x} + x - 3 = 0$ has exactly one real solution. In which of the following intervals does it lie?

- $(-1, 0)$
- $(0, 1)$
- $(1, 2)$
- $(2, 3)$

## Explanation

Let $f(x) = e^{x} + x - 3$, which is a sum of continuous functions and is therefore continuous on every closed interval. We have $f(0) = 1 + 0 - 3 = -2$ and $f(1) = e - 2$, which is positive because $e > 2$. The function is continuous on $[0, 1]$ and has opposite signs at the endpoints. By **Bolzano's theorem**, it therefore has at least one zero in $(0, 1)$. The real solution is unique and is approximately $0{,}79$.
