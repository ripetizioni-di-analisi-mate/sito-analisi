The total cost of producing $q$ units is $C(q) = q^2 + 10q + 400$, and the average cost is $\bar{C}(q) = \dfrac{C(q)}{q}$ for $q > 0$. Which line is an oblique asymptote of the graph of $\bar{C}$ as $q \to +\infty$?

- $y = q$
- $y = q + 400$
- $y = q + 10$
- $y = 10$

## Explanation

Dividing each term by $q$ gives
$$
\bar{C}(q) = q + 10 + \frac{400}{q} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{400}{q} \to 0$ as $q \to +\infty$, we have $\bar{C}(q) - (q + 10) \to 0$. Therefore the line $y = q + 10$ is the **oblique asymptote** of the graph of the average cost. The term $\dfrac{400}{q}$ comes from the fixed cost. It tends to $+\infty$ as $q \to 0^{+}$, so the line $q = 0$ is a vertical asymptote.
