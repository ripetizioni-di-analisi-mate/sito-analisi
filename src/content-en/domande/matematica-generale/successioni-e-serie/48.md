The sequence $a_n = \dfrac{5n - 2}{n + 1}$, with $n \geq 1$, is strictly increasing and tends to $5$. Which statement about its terms is true?

- Its minimum is $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and its maximum is $5$
- Its minimum is $-2$ and it has no maximum
- It is not bounded above
- Its minimum is $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and it has no maximum

## Explanation

Since the sequence is increasing, every term is greater than or equal to the first one, $a_1 = \dfrac{3}{2}$, so $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is the **minimum**. The value $-2$ would be obtained for $n = 0$, which is not an admissible index.

We can write $a_n = 5 - \dfrac{7}{n + 1}$, and since $\dfrac{7}{n + 1} > 0$ we have $a_n < 5$ for every $n$. Therefore the sequence is bounded above, but the value $5$ is not taken by any term.

Moreover every term is smaller than the next one, so no term is the largest, and the sequence **has no maximum**.
