- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{12}$

## Explanation

The series is a **geometric series** with common ratio $q = \dfrac{1}{3}$, so it converges because $-1 < q < 1$.

The index starts at $k = 2$, so the first term is $\left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{2} = \dfrac{1}{9}$.

Dividing the first term by $1 - q$ gives the sum.
$$
\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{3} \right)^{k} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{9}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{3}} = \frac{1}{9} \cdot \frac{3}{2} = \frac{1}{6} .
$$
