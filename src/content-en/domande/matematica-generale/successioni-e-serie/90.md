Start from a square of side $1$. Joining the midpoints of its sides gives a second square, and repeating the construction gives an infinite sequence of squares. What is the sum of the areas of all the squares, including the first?

- $2$
- $2 + \sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The side of the second square joins the midpoints of two consecutive sides, so by the Pythagorean theorem it measures $\sqrt{\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^2 + \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and its area is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The same construction halves the area at each step, so the areas $1$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{1}{4}$, $\dots$ form a geometric series with first term $1$ and common ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The sum is $\dfrac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = 2$. The value $2 + \sqrt{2}$ comes from using as common ratio the ratio of the sides, $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, instead of the ratio of the areas.
