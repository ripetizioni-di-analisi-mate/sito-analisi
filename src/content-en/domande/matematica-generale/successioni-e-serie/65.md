What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \frac{n^2}{n + 1} - \frac{n^2}{n + 2} \right)$?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $2$
- $1$

## Explanation

Each of the two fractions tends to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$.

Over a common denominator we get
$$
\frac{n^2}{n + 1} - \frac{n^2}{n + 2} = \frac{n^2\,\bigl[(n + 2) - (n + 1)\bigr]}{(n + 1)(n + 2)} = \frac{n^2}{n^2 + 3n + 2} .
$$
Factoring out $n^2$ in the numerator and in the denominator, the quotient becomes $\dfrac{1}{1 + \frac{3}{n} + \frac{2}{n^2}}$, so the limit is $1$.

The value $0$ comes from treating the form $+\infty - \infty$ as if it were $0$.
