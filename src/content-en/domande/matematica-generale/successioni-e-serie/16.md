Which general term, with the index starting from $n = 1$, gives the sequence $\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{3}{4}, \dfrac{5}{6}, \dfrac{7}{8}, \dots$?

- $a_n = \dfrac{2n - 1}{2n}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{2n + 1}{2n + 2}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{n}{n + 1}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{2n - 1}{2n + 1}$

## Explanation

The numerators $1, 3, 5, 7$ are the odd numbers, and the odd number in position $n$ is $2n - 1$. The denominators $2, 4, 6, 8$ are the even numbers, and the even number in position $n$ is $2n$.

So the general term is $a_n = \dfrac{2n - 1}{2n}$, and indeed for $n = 1, 2, 3$ one obtains $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{3}{4}$, $\dfrac{5}{6}$.

The formula $\dfrac{2n + 1}{2n + 2}$ gives $\dfrac{3}{4}$ for $n = 1$, therefore it starts from the second term of the sequence.
