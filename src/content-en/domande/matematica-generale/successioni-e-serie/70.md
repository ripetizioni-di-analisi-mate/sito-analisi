What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^2 + (-1)^n\,n}{n^2 + 1}$?

- $1$
- $0$
- The limit does not exist
- $2$

## Explanation

Dividing numerator and denominator by $n^2$ gives
$$
\frac{n^2 + (-1)^n\,n}{n^2 + 1} = \frac{1 + \frac{(-1)^n}{n}}{1 + \frac{1}{n^2}} .
$$
The distance of $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$ from $0$ is $\dfrac{1}{n}$, which tends to $0$, so $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n} \to 0$ even though it changes sign at every step.

Therefore, by the limit laws, the quotient tends to $\dfrac{1 + 0}{1 + 0} = 1$. The factor $(-1)^n$ multiplies a lower-degree term, and the terms with even index and those with odd index tend to the same value.
