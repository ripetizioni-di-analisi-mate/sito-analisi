The sequence $(a_n)$ is decreasing and satisfies $a_n > 1$ for every $n$. Which statement is certainly true?

- It converges to $1$
- It converges to a number $L \geq 1$
- It converges to $0$
- It diverges to $-\infty$

## Explanation

Since the sequence is decreasing, $a_n \leq a_1$ for every $n$, and by hypothesis $a_n > 1$. Hence $1 < a_n \leq a_1$ for every $n$, that is, the sequence is bounded.

A decreasing bounded sequence converges by the **monotone sequence theorem**, so there is a real number $L$ such that $a_n \to L$. Since all the terms are greater than $1$, the limit cannot be less than $1$, hence $L \geq 1$.

The value of $L$ is not determined by the hypotheses. Indeed $a_n = 1 + \dfrac{1}{n}$ converges to $1$, while $a_n = 2 + \dfrac{1}{n}$ converges to $2$, and both are decreasing with terms greater than $1$.
