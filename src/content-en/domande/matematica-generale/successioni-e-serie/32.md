What is the limit of the geometric progression $4, -6, 9, -\dfrac{27}{2}, \dots$?

- $0$
- The limit does not exist
- $-\infty$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The ratio of two consecutive terms is $\dfrac{-6}{4} = -\dfrac{3}{2}$, so the common ratio is $q = -\dfrac{3}{2}$ and the general term is $a_n = 4\left( -\dfrac{3}{2} \right)^{n-1}$.

Since $q \leq -1$, by the **limit of a geometric sequence** the sequence $q^{\,n}$ is oscillatory. The terms change sign at every step and their absolute value $4\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^{n-1}$ tends to $+\infty$, so the progression has no limit.

The value $0$ comes from confusing the condition $|q| < 1$ with $q < 1$, and the values $\pm\infty$ ignore the change of sign of the terms.
