All the terms of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_k$ are positive. Which statement about its behavior is true?

- It always converges, because the terms are positive
- It always diverges to $+\infty$, because the partial sums increase
- It can be oscillatory, if the general term does not tend to $0$
- It either converges or diverges to $+\infty$

## Explanation

The partial sums satisfy $S_{n+1} - S_n = a_{n+1} > 0$, so the sequence $(S_n)$ is increasing.

By the **monotone sequence theorem** an increasing sequence always has a limit: it converges if it is bounded and diverges to $+\infty$ if it is not. Therefore the series either converges or diverges to $+\infty$, and it cannot be oscillatory.

Both cases occur. The series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^k}$ has positive terms and converges to $1$, while the harmonic series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k}$ has positive terms and diverges to $+\infty$.
