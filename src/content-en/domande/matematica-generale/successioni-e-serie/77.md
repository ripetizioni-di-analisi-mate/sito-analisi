What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k\,(k + 2)}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{1}{k} - \dfrac{1}{k + 2} = \dfrac{2}{k\,(k + 2)}$, the general term can be written as
$$
\frac{1}{k\,(k + 2)} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k + 2} \right) .
$$
In the partial sum each negative term $-\dfrac{1}{k + 2}$ cancels with the positive term two places later, so only $1$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ remain with a plus sign and the last two terms with a minus sign:
$$
S_n = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{n + 1} - \frac{1}{n + 2} \right) .
$$
Therefore $S_n \to \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{3}{4}$. The value $\dfrac{3}{2}$ comes from forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ from keeping only the first term $1$.
