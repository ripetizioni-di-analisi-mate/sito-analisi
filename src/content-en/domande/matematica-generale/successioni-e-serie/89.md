Which of the following limits can be computed directly with the rules for computing limits, without meeting an indeterminate form?

- $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( n^2 - 2^{n} \right)$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^2}{2^{n}}$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} n \cdot \frac{1}{n^2}$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( n^2 + 2^{n} \right)$

## Explanation

Since $n^2 \to +\infty$ and, by the limit of the geometric sequence with $q = 2 > 1$, $2^{n} \to +\infty$, the sum $n^2 + 2^{n}$ takes the form $+\infty + \infty$. This form is not indeterminate, so by the rules for computing limits $n^2 + 2^{n} \to +\infty$.

With the same limits, the difference $n^2 - 2^{n}$ takes the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$ and the quotient $\dfrac{n^2}{2^{n}}$ takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$.

Finally $n \to +\infty$ and $\dfrac{1}{n^2} \to 0$, therefore the product takes the indeterminate form $\infty \cdot 0$. These three limits must be handled case by case, for example by writing $n \cdot \dfrac{1}{n^2} = \dfrac{1}{n}$, which tends to $0$.
