The sequence $(a_n)$ is an arithmetic progression with common difference $d \neq 0$, with all terms different from $0$. Which of the following sequences is certainly a geometric progression?

- $b_n = a_n^{2}$
- $b_n = 2^{a_n}$
- $b_n = 3\,a_n + 1$
- $b_n = \dfrac{1}{a_n}$

## Explanation

For $b_n = 2^{a_n}$ the ratio between two consecutive terms is
$$
\frac{b_{n+1}}{b_n} = 2^{a_{n+1} - a_n} = 2^{d} ,
$$
which does not depend on $n$ and is different from $0$. Therefore $(b_n)$ is a **geometric progression** with common ratio $2^d$.

The other sequences are not geometric in general, and one example is enough to see it. With $a_n = n$ we get $1, 4, 9, \dots$ with ratios $4$ and $\dfrac{9}{4}$, then $4, 7, 10, \dots$ with ratios $\dfrac{7}{4}$ and $\dfrac{10}{7}$, and finally $1, \dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{3}, \dots$ with ratios $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$. The sequence $3\,a_n + 1$ is an arithmetic progression with common difference $3d$.
