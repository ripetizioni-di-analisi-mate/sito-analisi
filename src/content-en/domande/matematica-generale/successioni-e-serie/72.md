What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( n - \sqrt{n} \right)$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $+\infty$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

Both terms tend to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$.

Factoring out $n$ gives
$$
n - \sqrt{n} = n \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \right) .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \to 0$, the second factor tends to $1$, so the product **diverges** to $+\infty$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the limit of $\sqrt{n^2 + n} - n$, in which the two terms have the same order of magnitude.
