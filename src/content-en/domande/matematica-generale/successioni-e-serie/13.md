- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Bringing the fractions to a common denominator gives $\dfrac{1}{k + 1} - \dfrac{1}{k + 2} = \dfrac{(k + 2) - (k + 1)}{(k + 1)(k + 2)} = \dfrac{1}{(k + 1)(k + 2)}$, so the given decomposition holds.

In the partial sum each negative term cancels with the positive term of the next summand, therefore
$$
S_n = \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4} \right) + \dots + \left( \frac{1}{n + 1} - \frac{1}{n + 2} \right) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{n + 2} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{n + 2} \to 0$, the partial sums tend to $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the series **converges** and its sum is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
