What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{\sqrt{4n^2 + n}}{3n - 1}$?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

Under the square root we factor out $n^2$. Since $n > 0$, we have $\sqrt{n^2} = n$, so
$$
\sqrt{4n^2 + n} = n\,\sqrt{4 + \frac{1}{n}} .
$$
Factoring out $n$ in the denominator as well, we get
$$
\frac{\sqrt{4n^2 + n}}{3n - 1} = \frac{\sqrt{4 + \frac{1}{n}}}{3 - \frac{1}{n}} \to \frac{\sqrt{4}}{3} = \frac{2}{3} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{4}{3}$ comes from forgetting the square root on the coefficient $4$.
