What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{2^n + (-3)^n}{4^n}$?

- $0$
- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $+\infty$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

Dividing each addend by $4^n$ gives
$$
\frac{2^n + (-3)^n}{4^n} = \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{n} + \left( -\frac{3}{4} \right)^{n} .
$$
The ratios $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-\dfrac{3}{4}$ lie between $-1$ and $1$, so by the limit of the geometric sequence both powers tend to $0$.

By the sum rule the limit is $0$. The term $\left( -\dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{n}$ changes sign at every step, but its absolute value $\left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{n}$ tends to $0$.
