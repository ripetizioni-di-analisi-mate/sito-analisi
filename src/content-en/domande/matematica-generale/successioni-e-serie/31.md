What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} n\left( \sqrt{n^2 + 4} - n \right)$?

- $2$
- $0$
- $4$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The factor $\sqrt{n^2 + 4} - n$ takes the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$. Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{n^2 + 4} + n$ gives
$$
n\left( \sqrt{n^2 + 4} - n \right) = \frac{n\,\left[ (n^2 + 4) - n^2 \right]}{\sqrt{n^2 + 4} + n} = \frac{4n}{\sqrt{n^2 + 4} + n} .
$$
Factoring out $n$ in the denominator and simplifying, the expression becomes
$$
\frac{4}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{n^2}} + 1} ,
$$
which tends to $\dfrac{4}{1 + 1} = 2$, because $\dfrac{4}{n^2} \to 0$.
