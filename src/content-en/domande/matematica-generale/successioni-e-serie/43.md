What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( -\frac{2}{3} \right)^{k}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-2$
- $2$
- $-\dfrac{2}{5}$

## Explanation

The series is a **geometric series** with common ratio $q = -\dfrac{2}{3}$, which converges because $-1 < q < 1$.

The index starts at $k = 1$, so the first term is $-\dfrac{2}{3}$.

Dividing the first term by $1 - q$ gives the sum.
$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( -\frac{2}{3} \right)^{k} = \frac{-\dfrac{2}{3}}{1 + \dfrac{2}{3}} = -\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{5} = -\frac{2}{5} .
$$
