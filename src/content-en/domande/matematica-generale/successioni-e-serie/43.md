How does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} -2 \left( \frac{5}{4} \right)^{k}$ behave?

- It converges with sum $8$
- It diverges to $+\infty$
- It is oscillatory
- It diverges to $-\infty$

## Explanation

The series is geometric, with first term $a = -2$ and common ratio $q = \dfrac{5}{4}$.

Since $q \geq 1$ and $a < 0$, by the **geometric series** theorem the series diverges to $-\infty$. Indeed the partial sums are
$$
S_n = -2 \cdot \frac{1 - \left( \frac{5}{4} \right)^{n}}{1 - \frac{5}{4}} = 8 - 8 \left( \frac{5}{4} \right)^{n} ,
$$
and $\left( \dfrac{5}{4} \right)^{n} \to +\infty$.

The value $8$ comes from applying the formula $\dfrac{a}{1 - q} = \dfrac{-2}{-\frac{1}{4}}$, which gives the sum only for $-1 < q < 1$. For $q = \dfrac{5}{4}$ this hypothesis is not satisfied.
