The series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_k$, with $a_k \neq 0$ for every $k$, converges with sum $S$. Which of the following series certainly converges?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} (a_k + 1)$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{a_k}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 3\,a_k$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( a_k + \frac{1}{k} \right)$

## Explanation

The partial sums of $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 3\,a_k$ are $3\,S_n$, and since $S_n \to S$, by the product law they tend to $3S$. Therefore this series **converges** with sum $3S$.

Since the given series converges, the necessary condition gives $a_k \to 0$. Therefore $a_k + 1 \to 1$ and $\left| \dfrac{1}{a_k} \right| \to +\infty$, so the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} (a_k + 1)$ and $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{a_k}$ have a general term that does not tend to $0$ and do not converge.

The partial sums of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( a_k + \frac{1}{k} \right)$ are $S_n + \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dots + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)$, the sum of a sequence tending to $S$ and the partial sums of the harmonic series, so they diverge to $+\infty$.
