What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{3n - n^3}{n^2 + 5}$?

- $+\infty$
- $-1$
- $0$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

The numerator has degree $3$ and the denominator degree $2$, and the limit takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$.

Factoring out $n^3$ in the numerator and $n^2$ in the denominator gives
$$
\frac{3n - n^3}{n^2 + 5} = n \cdot \frac{\dfrac{3}{n^2} - 1}{1 + \dfrac{5}{n^2}} .
$$
The fraction on the right tends to $-1$ and the factor $n$ tends to $+\infty$, so by the limit laws the product tends to $-\infty$.

The value $-1$ is the ratio of the leading coefficients, which gives the limit only when the numerator and the denominator have the same degree.
