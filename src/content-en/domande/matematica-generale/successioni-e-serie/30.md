What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{(2n + 1)^2}{3n^2 - n}$?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $4$
- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$

## Explanation

The numerator is $(2n + 1)^2 = 4n^2 + 4n + 1$, so the numerator and the denominator are polynomials of degree two and the limit takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$.

Factoring out $n^2$ gives
$$
\frac{4n^2 + 4n + 1}{3n^2 - n} = \frac{4 + \dfrac{4}{n} + \dfrac{1}{n^2}}{3 - \dfrac{1}{n}} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{4}{n}$, $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{n}$ tend to $0$, by the rules for computing limits the limit is $\dfrac{4}{3}$.
