What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n^2} \right)^{n}$?

- $e$
- $1$
- $+\infty$
- $e^{2}$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$.

Setting $b_n = \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n^2} \right)^{n^2}$, we have $a_n = b_n^{\frac{1}{n}}$, so $\ln a_n = \dfrac{1}{n} \ln b_n$. The numbers $b_n$ are the terms with index $n^2$ of the sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{m} \right)^{m}$, so $b_n \to e$ and $\ln b_n \to 1$.

Consequently $\ln a_n \to 0 \cdot 1 = 0$, and the limit is $e^{0} = 1$. The value $e$ comes from confusing the exponent $n$ with $n^2$.
