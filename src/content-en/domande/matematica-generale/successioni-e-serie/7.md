- $\dfrac{5}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $0$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $+\infty$, so the limit takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$.

Factoring out $n^3$, the highest power, gives
$$
\frac{5n^3 - 2n}{2n^3 + n^2 + 1} = \frac{n^3 \left( 5 - \dfrac{2}{n^2} \right)}{n^3 \left( 2 + \dfrac{1}{n} + \dfrac{1}{n^3} \right)} = \frac{5 - \dfrac{2}{n^2}}{2 + \dfrac{1}{n} + \dfrac{1}{n^3}} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{2}{n^2}$, $\dfrac{1}{n}$, and $\dfrac{1}{n^3}$ tend to $0$, the limit laws give $\dfrac{5}{2}$, the ratio of the coefficients of $n^3$.
