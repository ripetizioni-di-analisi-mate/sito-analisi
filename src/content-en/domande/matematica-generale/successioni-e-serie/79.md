The series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} (-1)^k$ is a geometric series with common ratio $-1$. How does it behave?

- It converges with sum $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- It is oscillatory
- It converges with sum $0$
- It diverges to $-\infty$

## Explanation

The partial sums are $S_1 = -1$, $S_2 = -1 + 1 = 0$, $S_3 = -1$, $S_4 = 0$, and in general $S_n = -1$ for odd $n$ and $S_n = 0$ for even $n$.

Therefore the sequence of partial sums takes the values $-1$ and $0$ alternately and has no limit, so the series is **oscillatory**.

The value $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ comes from applying the formula $\dfrac{a}{1 - q}$ with $a = -1$ and $q = -1$. The formula holds only for $-1 < q < 1$, and for $q = -1$ this hypothesis is not satisfied.
