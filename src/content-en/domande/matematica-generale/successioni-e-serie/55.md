For the partial sums $S_n$ of the harmonic series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k}$, grouping the terms into blocks that end at the powers of $2$ gives the estimate $S_{2^m} \geq 1 + \dfrac{m}{2}$ for every $m \geq 1$. Which of the following inequalities is true?

- $S_{1024} \geq 11$
- $S_{1024} \geq 6$
- $S_{1024} \geq 513$
- $S_{1024} \geq 21$

## Explanation

Since $1024 = 2^{10}$, the estimate with $m = 10$ gives
$$
S_{1024} \geq 1 + \frac{10}{2} = 6 .
$$
Direct computation gives $S_{1024} \approx 7.51$, so the other three inequalities are false.

The value $11$ comes from estimating the sum of each block by $1$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{2}$, the value $513$ from using the index $1024$ in place of the exponent $10$, and $21$ from doubling $m$ instead of halving it. The estimate shows that the partial sums exceed every threshold, but they grow very slowly: to get $S_{2^m} \geq 20$ the estimate requires $m = 38$.
