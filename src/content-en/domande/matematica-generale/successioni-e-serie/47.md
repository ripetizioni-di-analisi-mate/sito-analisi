What is the largest term of the sequence $a_n = \dfrac{n^2}{2^n}$, with $n \geq 1$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{9}{8}$
- $\dfrac{25}{32}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The terms are positive, so to study monotonicity we compare the ratio with $1$:
$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(n + 1)^2}{2^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{2^n}{n^2} = \frac{(n + 1)^2}{2n^2} .
$$
The ratio is greater than $1$ if and only if $(n + 1)^2 > 2n^2$, that is, $n^2 - 2n - 1 < 0$. The roots of $n^2 - 2n - 1$ are $1 \pm \sqrt{2}$, and $1 + \sqrt{2} \approx 2.41$, so the inequality holds for $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ and fails for $n \geq 3$.

Therefore $a_1 < a_2 < a_3$ and $a_3 > a_4 > a_5 > \dots$, and the largest term is $a_3 = \dfrac{9}{8}$. The value $1$ is taken by $a_2$ and by $a_4$, which are smaller than $a_3$.
