The general term of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2k}$ tends to $0$. Does the series converge?

- Yes, with sum $1$
- No, it diverges to $+\infty$
- Yes, because the general term tends to $0$
- Yes, with sum $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The partial sum is
$$
S_n = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + \dots + \frac{1}{2n} = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n} \right) ,
$$
that is, half the partial sum of the harmonic series.

The partial sums of the harmonic series diverge to $+\infty$, so half of them also diverge to $+\infty$, and the series **diverges**. The condition $\dfrac{1}{2k} \to 0$ is satisfied, but it is only necessary for convergence.

The value $1$ is the sum of the geometric series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{k}}$, which has a different general term.
