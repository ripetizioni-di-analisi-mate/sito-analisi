For which real value of $x$ are the numbers $x - 2$, $x + 1$, $x + 7$, in this order, consecutive terms of a geometric progression?

- $x = 3$
- $x = 5$
- $x = 1$
- $x = -5$

## Explanation

Three nonzero numbers are consecutive terms of a geometric progression if the ratio of the second to the first equals the ratio of the third to the second, that is, if
$$
\frac{x + 1}{x - 2} = \frac{x + 7}{x + 1} .
$$
Cross-multiplying gives $(x + 1)^2 = (x - 2)(x + 7)$, that is, $x^2 + 2x + 1 = x^2 + 5x - 14$, so $3x = 15$ and $x = 5$.

For $x = 5$ the numbers are $3$, $6$, $12$, nonzero and with common ratio $2$. For $x = 3$ we get $1$, $4$, $10$, for $x = 1$ we get $-1$, $2$, $8$, and for $x = -5$ we get $-7$, $-4$, $2$, which do not have equal ratios.
