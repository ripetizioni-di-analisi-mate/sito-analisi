- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $0$

## Explanation

Since $2^{2n+1} = 2 \cdot 2^{2n} = 2 \cdot 4^n$, the denominator can be written as $2 \cdot 4^n + 3$.

The numerator and the denominator tend to $+\infty$, so the limit takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Factoring out $4^n$ gives
$$
\frac{4^n - 1}{2 \cdot 4^n + 3} = \frac{1 - \dfrac{1}{4^n}}{2 + \dfrac{3}{4^n}} .
$$
By the limit of the geometric sequence, $\dfrac{1}{4^n} = \left( \dfrac{1}{4} \right)^{n} \to 0$, so the limit is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
