A ball is dropped from a height of $8$ meters and, after each bounce, rises to $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the height from which it fell. Assuming infinitely many bounces, how many meters does the ball travel in total?

- $56$
- $32$
- $48$
- $64$

## Explanation

The descents measure $8$, $6$, $\dfrac{9}{2}$, $\dots$ meters, so they form a geometric series with first term $8$ and common ratio $\dfrac{3}{4}$, whose sum is $\dfrac{8}{1 - \frac{3}{4}} = 32$ meters.

The ascents measure $6$, $\dfrac{9}{2}$, $\dots$ meters, that is, the same distances without the first drop, so their sum is $\dfrac{6}{1 - \frac{3}{4}} = 24$ meters.

The total distance is therefore $32 + 24 = 56$ meters. The value $32$ counts only the descents, and $64$ also counts the first drop twice.
