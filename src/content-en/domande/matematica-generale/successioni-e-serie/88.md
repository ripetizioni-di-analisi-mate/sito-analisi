A stock pays a dividend of $100$ euros one year from now, and each subsequent dividend, paid at the end of each year, is $2\%$ higher than the previous one, forever. At an annual rate of $5\%$, what is the sum today of the present values of all the dividends?

- $2000$ euros
- $1428.57$ euros
- $3333.33$ euros
- $5000$ euros

## Explanation

The dividend of year $k$ is $100 \cdot 1.02^{\,k-1}$ euros, and its present value at $5\%$ is $\dfrac{100 \cdot 1.02^{\,k-1}}{1.05^{\,k}}$.

These values form a geometric series with first term $\dfrac{100}{1.05}$ and common ratio $q = \dfrac{1.02}{1.05}$, which lies between $0$ and $1$, so the series converges. Its sum is
$$
\frac{\frac{100}{1.05}}{1 - \frac{1.02}{1.05}} = \frac{100}{1.05 - 1.02} = \frac{100}{0.03} \approx 3333.33 \text{ euros} .
$$
The value $2000$ euros is that of the constant perpetuity of $100$ euros, which ignores the growth of the dividends.
