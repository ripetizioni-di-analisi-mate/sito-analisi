- $q = 2$, $a_1 = 3$
- $q = -2$, $a_1 = 3$
- $q = -2$, $a_1 = -6$
- $q = -8$, $a_1 = \dfrac{3}{16}$

## Explanation

To go from $a_3$ to $a_6$ one multiplies three times by the common ratio, so $a_6 = a_3\,q^{3}$.

Substituting the data gives $-96 = 12\,q^{3}$, that is, $q^{3} = -8$, and the only real solution is $q = -2$.

Since $a_3 = a_1\,q^{2}$, the first term is
$$
a_1 = \frac{a_3}{q^{2}} = \frac{12}{(-2)^{2}} = \frac{12}{4} = 3 .
$$
The signs of the data are consistent with this result, because for $q < 0$ the terms change sign at every step and $a_3$, $a_6$ have indices of different parity.
