A sequence starts from $a_1 = 1$, and each term is twice the previous one increased by $3$, that is, $a_{n+1} = 2\,a_n + 3$. Which formula gives the term $a_n$ as a function of $n$?

- $a_n = 4n - 3$
- $a_n = 2^{n} - 1$
- $a_n = 2^{n+1} - 3$
- $a_n = 3^{n} - 2$

## Explanation

The number $L$ left unchanged by the rule satisfies $L = 2L + 3$, so $L = -3$. We therefore set $b_n = a_n + 3$.

The rule gives $b_{n+1} = a_{n+1} + 3 = 2\,a_n + 6 = 2\,b_n$, so $(b_n)$ is a geometric progression with common ratio $2$ and first term $b_1 = 1 + 3 = 4$.

Hence $b_n = 4 \cdot 2^{n-1} = 2^{n+1}$ and $a_n = 2^{n+1} - 3$, which gives $1$, $5$, $13$, $29$. The formula $4n - 3$ gives the same first two terms, but $a_3 = 9$ instead of $2 \cdot 5 + 3 = 13$; the formulas $2^{n} - 1$ and $3^{n} - 2$ give $a_2 = 3$ and $a_2 = 7$.
