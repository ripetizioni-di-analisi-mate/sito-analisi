The sequence $(a_n)$ converges to a real number $L$. Which statement is certainly true?

- It is monotonic
- From some index on, all its terms are positive
- It is bounded
- It has a largest term and a smallest term

## Explanation

We apply the definition of limit with $\varepsilon = 1$. There is an index $N$ such that $|a_n - L| < 1$ for $n > N$, so for these indices $|a_n| < |L| + 1$.

The terms with $n \leq N$ are finitely many, so the largest of the numbers $|a_1|, \dots, |a_N|, |L| + 1$ is a number $M$ with $|a_n| \leq M$ for every $n$. Hence every convergent sequence is bounded.

The other statements do not hold in general. The sequence $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges to $0$ and is not monotonic, the sequence $-1 + \dfrac{1}{n}$ converges to $-1$ and has negative terms for $n \geq 2$, and the sequence $\dfrac{1}{n}$ converges to $0$ and has no smallest term.
