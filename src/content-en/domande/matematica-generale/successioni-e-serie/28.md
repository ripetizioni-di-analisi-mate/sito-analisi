What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1 + 2 + 4 + \dots + 2^{n}}{2^{n}}$?

- $2$
- $1$
- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The numerator is the sum of the first $n + 1$ terms of the geometric progression with $a_1 = 1$ and $q = 2$, so it equals
$$
1 + 2 + \dots + 2^{n} = \frac{1 - 2^{n+1}}{1 - 2} = 2^{n+1} - 1 .
$$

Therefore the quotient is
$$
\frac{2^{n+1} - 1}{2^{n}} = 2 - \frac{1}{2^{n}} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{2^{n}} \to 0$, the limit is $2$.

The value $1$ comes from considering only the last addend $2^{n}$ of the numerator, while the sum of the preceding terms, $2^{n} - 1$, has the same order of magnitude.
