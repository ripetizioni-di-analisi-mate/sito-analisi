For which of the following series is the necessary condition for convergence enough to conclude that the series does not converge?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{k}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k\,(k + 1)}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{k}{2k + 1}$

## Explanation

By the necessary condition, if a series converges, its general term tends to $0$.

For the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{k}{2k + 1}$ the general term tends to $\dfrac{1}{2} \neq 0$, so the series **does not converge**.

In the other three series the general term tends to $0$, therefore the condition is satisfied and does not determine the behavior of the series. Indeed the harmonic series diverges, while the other two converge.
