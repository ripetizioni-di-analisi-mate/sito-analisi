What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} n\,\bigl[ \ln(n + 1) - \ln n \bigr]$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $e$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The first factor tends to $+\infty$ and the second tends to $0$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $\infty \cdot 0$.

By the laws of logarithms
$$
n\,\bigl[ \ln(n + 1) - \ln n \bigr] = n \ln \frac{n + 1}{n} = \ln \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^{n} .
$$
Since $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n} \to e$ and the logarithm is a continuous function, the limit is $\ln e = 1$. The continuity of the logarithm is covered in the topic on limits and continuity.
