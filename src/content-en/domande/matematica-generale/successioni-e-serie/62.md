What is the product of the first six terms of the geometric progression $2, 4, 8, 16, \dots$?

- $2^{21}$
- $2^{15}$
- $2^{27}$
- $2^{42}$

## Explanation

The progression has first term $2$ and common ratio $2$, so by the formula for the general term $a_n = 2 \cdot 2^{\,n-1} = 2^{n}$. The first six terms are therefore $2^1, 2^2, \dots, 2^6$.

When powers with the same base are multiplied the exponents are added, so the product equals $2^{1 + 2 + \dots + 6}$. The exponents form an arithmetic progression with common difference $1$, and by the sum formula
$$
1 + 2 + \dots + 6 = \frac{6\,(1 + 6)}{2} = 21 .
$$
Therefore the product equals $2^{21}$. The value $2^{15}$ comes from adding only the exponents $0, 1, \dots, 5$ of the powers of $q$, without the factor $2^6$ due to the first term, $2^{27}$ from multiplying $2^{21}$ by a further factor $2^6$, and $2^{42}$ from doubling the sum of the exponents.
