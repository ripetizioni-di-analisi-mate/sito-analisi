What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^{3n}$?

- $e$
- $1$
- $3e$
- $e^3$

## Explanation

By the properties of powers, $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{3n} = \left[ \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n} \right]^{3}$.

The sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}$ tends to the **number $e$**, by the definition of $e$. The cube is the product of three factors that tend to $e$, so by the product rule the limit is $e^3$.

The base tends to $1$ while the exponent tends to $+\infty$, therefore the limit is computed by reducing it to the one that defines $e$.
