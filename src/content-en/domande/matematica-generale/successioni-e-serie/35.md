A capital of $1$ euro is invested for one year at a nominal annual rate of $100\%$, compounded quarterly, that is, applying one quarter of the annual rate every three months. What is it worth after one year, rounded to the cent?

- $2$ euros
- $16$ euros
- $2.72$ euros
- $2.44$ euros

## Explanation

A year has $4$ quarters, and in each quarter the rate $\dfrac{100\%}{4} = 25\%$ is applied. Every quarter the capital is therefore multiplied by $1.25$.

After four quarters the capital is therefore
$$
1 \cdot \left( 1 + \frac{1}{4} \right)^{4} = 1.25^{4} = 2.44140625 ,
$$
that is, about $2.44$ euros.

The value $2$ euros corresponds to annual compounding, and the value $2.72$ euros is $e$, the limit of $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}$ as $n \to +\infty$, which corresponds to continuous compounding. The value $16$ euros comes from applying the whole annual rate every quarter, that is, computing $2^{4}$.
