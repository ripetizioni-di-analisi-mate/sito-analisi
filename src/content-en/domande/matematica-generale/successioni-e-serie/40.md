Which statement about the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \ln\left( 1 + \frac{1}{k} \right)$ is true?

- It converges, because the general term tends to $0$
- It diverges to $+\infty$, even though the general term tends to $0$
- It converges and its sum is $\ln 2$
- Its partial sums have neither a finite nor an infinite limit

## Explanation

Since $1 + \dfrac{1}{k} = \dfrac{k + 1}{k}$, the logarithm laws give the general term $\ln(k + 1) - \ln k$.

In the partial sum the negative term of each addend cancels with the positive term of the next addend, so
$$
S_n = (\ln 2 - \ln 1) + (\ln 3 - \ln 2) + \dots + \bigl( \ln(n + 1) - \ln n \bigr) = \ln(n + 1) .
$$
Since $\ln(n + 1) \to +\infty$, the series **diverges** to $+\infty$. The general term tends to $\ln 1 = 0$, so the necessary condition is satisfied, but it is not sufficient for convergence.
