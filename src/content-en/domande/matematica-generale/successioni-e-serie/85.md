For $-1 < x < 1$, what is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} x^{2k}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{1 - x^2}$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{1 - x}$
- $\dfrac{x}{1 - x^2}$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{1 - x^2}$

## Explanation

Since $x^{2k} = \left( x^2 \right)^{k}$, the series is a geometric series with common ratio $q = x^2$. The index starts at $k = 1$, so the first term is $x^2$.

For $-1 < x < 1$ we have $0 \leq x^2 < 1$, therefore the common ratio satisfies $-1 < q < 1$ and the series converges. For $x = 0$ all the terms are zero and the sum is $0$; for $x \neq 0$ the first term is nonzero and the sum is the first term divided by $1 - q$, that is,
$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} x^{2k} = \frac{x^2}{1 - x^2} .
$$
This expression also equals $0$ for $x = 0$. The value $\dfrac{1}{1 - x^2}$ is the sum of the series starting at $k = 0$, $\dfrac{x^2}{1 - x}$ uses $x$ instead of $x^2$ as the common ratio, and $\dfrac{x}{1 - x^2}$ uses $x$ as the first term.
