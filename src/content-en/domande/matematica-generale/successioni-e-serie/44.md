A perpetuity pays $600$ euros per year, with the first payment today and the following ones at the beginning of each year. At an annual rate of $4\%$, what is the perpetuity worth today?

- $15\,000$ euros
- $15\,600$ euros
- $624$ euros
- $14\,423{,}08$ euros

## Explanation

A payment of $600$ euros in $k$ years is worth $600 \cdot 1{,}04^{-k}$ today, and the first payment, made today, is worth $600$ euros.

The present value of the perpetuity is therefore the sum of a geometric series with first term $600$ and common ratio $q = \dfrac{1}{1{,}04}$, which converges because $0 < q < 1$. Evaluating the sum gives
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{600}{1{,}04^{k}} = \frac{600}{1 - \dfrac{1}{1{,}04}} = \frac{600 \cdot 1{,}04}{0{,}04} = 15\,600 \text{ euros} .
$$
With the first payment in one year, every payment would arrive one year later, and the value would be $\dfrac{600}{0{,}04} = 15\,000$ euros.
