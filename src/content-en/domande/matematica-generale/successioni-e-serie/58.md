An arithmetic progression has first term $1000$ and common difference $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. What is its limit as $n \to +\infty$?

- $-\infty$
- $0$
- $1000$
- It has no limit

## Explanation

By the formula for the general term $a_n = 1000 - \dfrac{n - 1}{2}$. Given any number $M$, we have $a_n < M$ for $n > 2\,(1000 - M) + 1$, so from some index onward the terms lie below any threshold.

Therefore the progression **diverges** to $-\infty$. At each step the terms decrease by the same amount $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $a_{2001} = 0$ and from $n = 2002$ onward the terms are negative and keep decreasing.
