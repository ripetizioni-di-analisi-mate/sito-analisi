What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{2^{k}} + \frac{1}{3^{k}} \right)$?

- $\dfrac{7}{2}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{5}{6}$

## Explanation

The series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{k}}$ and $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{3^{k}}$ are geometric series with common ratio between $-1$ and $1$, so they converge. The partial sums of the given series are the sum of their partial sums, so by the limit laws the series converges and its sum is the sum of the two sums.

The first one has first term $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and common ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so it equals $\dfrac{\frac{1}{2}}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = 1$. The second one has first term $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and common ratio $\dfrac{1}{3}$, so it equals $\dfrac{\frac{1}{3}}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

The sum of the series is therefore $1 + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{7}{2}$ comes from the formula $\dfrac{1}{1 - q}$, which holds for series starting at $k = 0$.
