What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{k + 1}} \right)$?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $1$
- $1 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

## Explanation

In the partial sum the negative term of each addend cancels with the positive term of the next addend, so
$$
S_n = \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right) + \dots + \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{n + 1}} \right) = 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{n + 1}} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n + 1}} \to 0$, the partial sums tend to $1$. Therefore the series **converges** and its sum is $1$.

The value $1 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ is the first term of the series.
