Two sequences satisfy $a_n \to 2$ and $b_n \to 0$, with $b_n > 0$ for every $n$. What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n}$?

- $0$
- $2$
- $+\infty$
- It depends on the chosen sequences

## Explanation

The limit is not an indeterminate form, because the numerator tends to a number different from $0$.

Since $a_n \to 2$, from some index onward $a_n > 1$, so $\dfrac{a_n}{b_n} > \dfrac{1}{b_n}$. Since $b_n$ is positive and tends to $0$, the reciprocal $\dfrac{1}{b_n}$ exceeds any threshold, so the quotient **diverges** to $+\infty$.

For example, with $a_n = 2$ and $b_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$ the quotient equals $2n$. The sign of $b_n$ is needed: if $b_n$ were negative the quotient would diverge to $-\infty$.
