What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2n} \right)^{6n}$?

- $1$
- $e^{12}$
- $e^{3}$
- $e^{\frac{1}{3}}$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$. Set $m = 2n$, which tends to $+\infty$ together with $n$; since $6n = 3m$, one has
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{1}{2n} \right)^{6n} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{m} \right)^{m} \right]^{3} .
$$
By the theorem on the number $e$ the sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{m} \right)^{m}$ tends to $e$, and therefore its terms with even index also tend to $e$. Hence the limit equals $e^{3}$. The value $e^{12}$ comes from multiplying the coefficients $2$ and $6$ instead of dividing them, and the value $1$ from considering only the base.
