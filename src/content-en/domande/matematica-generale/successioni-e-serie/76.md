What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \frac{n + 2}{n + 1} \right)^{n}$?

- $1$
- $e^{2}$
- $e$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$

## Explanation

The base can be written as $\dfrac{n + 2}{n + 1} = 1 + \dfrac{1}{n + 1}$, so multiplying and dividing by the base gives
$$
\left( \frac{n + 2}{n + 1} \right)^{n} = \frac{\left( 1 + \frac{1}{n + 1} \right)^{n + 1}}{1 + \frac{1}{n + 1}} .
$$
The numerator is the term with index $n + 1$ of the sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{m} \right)^{m}$, so it tends to $e$; the denominator tends to $1$.

By the quotient law the limit is $e$. The value $1$ comes from considering only that the base tends to $1$, and $e^{2}$ from reading the base as $1 + \dfrac{2}{n}$.
