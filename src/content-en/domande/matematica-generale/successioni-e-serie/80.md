What can be said about the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{k} \right)^{k}$?

- It converges, because its terms are bounded
- It converges with sum $e$
- It diverges to $+\infty$, because the general term tends to $e$
- It is oscillatory

## Explanation

By the theorem on the number $e$ the general term tends to $e$, which is different from $0$. Therefore the necessary condition for convergence is not satisfied, and the series does not converge.

The terms are positive, so the sequence of partial sums is strictly increasing. By the monotone sequence theorem an increasing sequence that does not converge diverges to $+\infty$, so the series **diverges** to $+\infty$.

Since the sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{k} \right)^{k}$ is strictly increasing and its first term is $2$, every term is at least $2$, and therefore $S_n \geq 2n$.
