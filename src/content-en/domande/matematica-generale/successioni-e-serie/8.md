- $5$
- $\sqrt{5}$
- $0$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

Both square roots tend to $+\infty$, so the limit takes the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$.

One multiplies and divides by $\sqrt{n + 5} + \sqrt{n}$; since $(A - B)(A + B) = A^2 - B^2$, one obtains
$$
\sqrt{n + 5} - \sqrt{n} = \frac{(n + 5) - n}{\sqrt{n + 5} + \sqrt{n}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{n + 5} + \sqrt{n}} .
$$
The numerator is constant and the denominator tends to $+\infty$, therefore the limit is $0$.
