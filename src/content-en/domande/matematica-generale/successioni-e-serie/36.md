A capital of $2000$ euros is invested for one year at a nominal annual rate of $5\%$, compounded over $n$ equal periods, so after one year it is worth $2000\left( 1 + \dfrac{0.05}{n} \right)^{n}$ euros. What value does the accumulated amount tend to as $n \to +\infty$?

- $2000 \cdot 1.05$ euros
- $2000\,e^{0.05}$ euros
- $2000\,e$ euros
- $2000\,e^{5}$ euros

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{0.05}{n} = \dfrac{1}{20n}$, one can write
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{0.05}{n} \right)^{n} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{20n} \right)^{20n} \right]^{\frac{1}{20}} .
$$
As $n$ grows, $m = 20n$ also tends to $+\infty$, so $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{m} \right)^{m}$ tends to $e$.

Therefore the accumulated amount tends to $2000\,e^{\frac{1}{20}} = 2000\,e^{0.05} \approx 2102.54$ euros, the value under continuous compounding. The value $2000 \cdot 1.05 = 2100$ euros corresponds to a single period, that is, to $n = 1$.
