The partial sums of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^2}$ satisfy $S_n \leq 2 - \dfrac{1}{n}$ for every $n$, and $S_{10} \approx 1.55$. The figure shows the first ten partial sums and the line $y = 2$. Which statement is true?

::grafico{punti="1,1,pieno;2,1.25,pieno;3,1.3611,pieno;4,1.4236,pieno;5,1.4636,pieno;6,1.4914,pieno;7,1.5118,pieno;8,1.5274,pieno;9,1.5398,pieno;10,1.5498,pieno" asintoti="y=2" xmin=-2 xmax=12 ymin=-1 ymax=2.5 nomi="n,Sₙ"}

- The series converges and its sum is less than or equal to $2$
- The series converges and its sum is $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- The series diverges to $+\infty$, like the harmonic series
- The series is oscillatory

## Explanation

The terms are positive, so $S_{n+1} = S_n + \dfrac{1}{(n + 1)^2} > S_n$ and the sequence of partial sums is strictly increasing. Moreover $S_n < 2$ for every $n$, so it is bounded.

By the monotone sequence theorem an increasing bounded sequence converges, so the series **converges** to a sum $S$. Since $S_n < 2$ for every $n$, the limit also satisfies $S \leq 2$.

The sum cannot be $\dfrac{3}{2}$, because the partial sums increase and already $S_{10} > \dfrac{3}{2}$.
