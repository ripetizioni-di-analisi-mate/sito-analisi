What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + n}{n^2}$?

- $0$
- $1$
- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The numerator is the sum of the first $n$ terms of the arithmetic progression with $a_1 = 1$ and $a_n = n$, so it equals $\dfrac{n\,(n + 1)}{2}$.

Therefore
$$
\frac{1 + 2 + \dots + n}{n^2} = \frac{n^2 + n}{2n^2} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2n} \to \frac{1}{2} .
$$
The value $0$ comes from observing that each addend divided by $n^2$ tends to $0$; however, the number of addends grows with $n$, so the limit laws do not apply term by term.
