Which statement about the sequence $a_n = \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}$, with $n \geq 1$, is true?

- It tends to $1$, because the base tends to $1$
- It diverges to $+\infty$, because the exponent tends to $+\infty$
- From some index onward $a_n > e$
- $a_n < e$ for every $n$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent tends to $+\infty$, so the sequence has the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$, and its limit does not follow from the limit laws.

By the theorem on the number $e$ the sequence is strictly increasing and converges to $e$. If for some index $m$ we had $a_m \geq e$, then for $n > m$ we would have $a_n \geq a_{m+1} > a_m \geq e$, and the limit would be at least $a_{m+1}$, which is greater than $e$.

This contradicts $a_n \to e$, so $a_n < e$ for every $n$. For example $a_{1000} \approx 2.7169$.
