Two probes measure pressure at two fixed depths in still water. Each adds its own constant offset to the true pressure. The lower reading minus the upper reading is $14\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The probes swap places, and the lower reading minus the upper reading is now $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$. By how much does the true pressure at the lower point exceed that at the upper point?

- $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $24\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Let $D$ be the true pressure difference and $d$ the difference between the probe offsets in the first arrangement. The first measurement gives $D+d=14\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Swapping the probes reverses the offset contribution, giving $D-d=10\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Adding the measurements gives $2D=24\,\mathrm{kPa}$, so $D=12\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
