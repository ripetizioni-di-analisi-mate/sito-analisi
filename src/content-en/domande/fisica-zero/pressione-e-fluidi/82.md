A rubber duck floats at rest in a bathtub, with part of it above the water. Neglecting the buoyant force of the air, how do the weight of the duck and the buoyant force compare?

- The buoyant force is greater than the weight, because the duck floats
- The weight is greater than the buoyant force, because part of the duck is out of the water
- They have the same magnitude and opposite directions
- They have the same magnitude and the same direction

## Explanation

The duck is acted on only by its weight, pointing downward, and the buoyant force of the water, pointing upward. Since it is at rest, the net force is zero, so the two forces have the same magnitude and opposite directions. The part above the water only shows that the immersed volume is smaller than the total volume.
