A stone's volume is estimated from its loss of apparent weight in water. A bubble of negligible mass clings to the stone. How is the inferred volume of the stone alone affected?

- It is unchanged
- It becomes equal to the bubble volume
- It is underestimated
- It is overestimated

## Explanation

The bubble displaces water along with the stone, increasing buoyancy on the suspended system. Attributing all of it to the stone includes the bubble volume.
