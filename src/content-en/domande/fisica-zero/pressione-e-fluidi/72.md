The anchor of a small boat, hanging from its rope, is lowered into a lake and is completely submerged at a depth of $1\,\mathrm{m}$. It is lowered to $3\,\mathrm{m}$, still without touching the bottom; the water has uniform density. What happens to the buoyant force on the anchor?

- It triples
- It increases, but by less than a factor of three
- It stays the same
- It decreases

## Explanation

The buoyant force is $F_A=\rho_f gV_{\mathrm{imm}}$. The anchor stays completely submerged, so the immersed volume does not change, and the density of the water is uniform. Therefore, the buoyant force stays the same. As the anchor goes deeper, the pressures on all of its faces increase, while the difference between the pressure on its lower parts and that on its upper parts stays the same.
