The hydraulic lift in a garage has a small piston with an area of $10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and a large piston with an area of $500\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, at the same height. A car weighing $12000\,\mathrm{N}$ rests on the large piston. Neglecting the weights of the pistons and friction, what force on the small piston holds the car still?

- $600000\,\mathrm{N}$
- $240\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2400\,\mathrm{N}$
- $24\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By Pascal's principle, the additional pressure is the same on both pistons, so $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1}=\dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$ and $F_1=F_2\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}$. The areas appear as a ratio, so they can be left in square centimeters. This gives $F_1=12000\cdot\dfrac{10}{500}\,\mathrm{N}=240\,\mathrm{N}$.
