A diesel tank $1\,\mathrm{m}$ wide has a vertical transparent tube, $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ wide, on its side, connected to the tank near the bottom. Both the tube and the tank are open to the air at the top. What does the diesel level in the tube show?

- A lower level, because the tube holds less diesel
- The same level as the diesel in the tank
- A higher level, because the tube is narrower
- A level that depends on the width of the tank

## Explanation

The tank and the tube are connected, contain the same liquid at rest, and have their free surfaces at the same atmospheric pressure. Therefore, the diesel reaches the same level in both, even though the volumes they hold are very different. This is why the tube shows the level of the diesel in the tank from the outside.
