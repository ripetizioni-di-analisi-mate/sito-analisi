Three flower vases of different shapes, one flaring outward at the top, one cylindrical, and one narrow at the top, contain still water up to the same height of $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ above the bottom. How do the water pressures on the three bottoms compare?

- Greatest in the flared vase, which holds the most water
- They are equal in the three vases
- Greatest in the vase that is narrow at the top
- Greatest in the cylindrical vase

## Explanation

By Stevin's law, the pressure on the bottom is $p_0+\rho gh$. In all three vases the liquid is the same, atmospheric pressure acts on the surface, and the bottom is $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the surface. Therefore, the pressure on the bottom is the same in the three vases, because neither the shape of the container nor the amount of water appears in the formula.
