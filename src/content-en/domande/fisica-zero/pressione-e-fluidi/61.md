For a hydraulic press, the relation $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1}=\dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$ between the forces on the two pistons is used, neglecting the weights of the pistons and friction and with the same external pressure. Which other condition is one of the assumptions of the relation?

- The two pistons have the same area
- The liquid has the same density as water
- The liquid flows slowly through the connecting pipe
- The pistons are at the same height

## Explanation

The relation follows from Pascal's principle applied to a liquid in equilibrium. If the pistons were at different heights, by Stevin's law the pressures under them would differ by $\rho gh$, so the equality $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1}=\dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$ would not hold. Therefore, the relation requires the pistons to be at the same height, while it requires neither equal areas nor a particular density, and the liquid must be at rest.
