A barber's chair is raised by a small hydraulic press. The pedal pushes with $50\,\mathrm{N}$ on a piston with an area of $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$; the large piston, at the same height, supports the chair and the customer, who together weigh $1000\,\mathrm{N}$. Neglecting friction and the weights of the pistons, what area must the large piston have for equilibrium?

- $0.2\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $200\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $8\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $80\,\mathrm{cm^2}$

## Explanation

By Pascal's principle, $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1}=\dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$, so $A_2=A_1\dfrac{F_2}{F_1}$. The ratio of the forces is $\dfrac{1000}{50}=20$. Therefore, $A_2=4\cdot20\,\mathrm{cm^2}=80\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.
