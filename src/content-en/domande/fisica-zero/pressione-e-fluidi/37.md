A kitchen tray with one sloping wall contains still water with a density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Point $P$ on the sloping wall is $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the free surface measured along the wall, and $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the surface measured vertically. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the relative pressure at $P$?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0.5,2 3.41,-0.6 6,-0.6 6,2" linee="0.05,2.4 3.41,-0.6 6,-0.6 6,2.6" segmenti="2.74,2,2.74,0" punti="2.74,0,pieno,P" etichette="3.4,1.1,20 cm;1.1,0.8,30 cm" xmin=-0.6 xmax=6.6 ymin=-1 ymax=3 titolo="Tray with a sloping wall: $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ along the wall and $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ vertically between the surface and point $P$."}

- $2943\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4905\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1962\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $196200\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

In Stevin's law, the depth $h$ is measured vertically, regardless of the shape of the container. Hence for point $P$ we use $h=20\,\mathrm{cm}=0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, not the distance along the wall. Therefore, $p_{\mathrm{rel}}=1000\cdot9.81\cdot0.20\,\mathrm{Pa}=1962\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
