A cargo ship moves from the fresh water of a river, with a density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, into seawater, with a density of $1030\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, without changing its load. What happens to the immersed part of the hull?

- It increases, so the ship sinks slightly
- It decreases, so the ship rises slightly
- It stays the same, because the weight of the ship does not change
- It decreases, because the ship weighs less in the sea

## Explanation

In both cases the ship floats at rest, so the buoyant force equals the weight, which does not change. The immersed volume is therefore $V_{\mathrm{imm}}=\dfrac{m}{\rho_f}$. Since seawater is denser, the immersed volume is smaller, and the ship rises slightly relative to the water level.
