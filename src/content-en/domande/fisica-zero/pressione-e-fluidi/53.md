The rule of communicating vessels says that the same liquid at rest, with the same pressure on its surfaces, reaches the same level. In which of these cases may the rule fail to describe the water levels well?

- Two buckets connected by a garden hose
- A teapot and its spout
- A container connected to a glass tube as thin as a hair
- A tank with a level indicator two centimeters wide

## Explanation

The rule of equal levels neglects surface tension effects. These effects can be significant in very narrow tubes, so in a tube as thin as a hair the level may differ from that in the container. In the other cases the tubes are wide, and the rule describes the levels well.
