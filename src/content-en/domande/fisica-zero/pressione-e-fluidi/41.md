The wall of a dam is much thicker at the base than at the top. Which physical fact justifies this shape?

- Water pressure is greatest near the surface
- Water pressure depends on the length of the lake behind the dam
- The water exerts only vertical forces on the wall
- Water pressure increases with depth, so the forces on the wall are greater near the bottom

## Explanation

By Stevin's law, the pressure in still water is $p_0+\rho gh$, so it increases with the depth $h$. On a section of wall of given area, the force is the pressure times the area, so deeper sections receive larger forces. For this reason the base of the wall, which bears the largest forces, is built thicker. The length of the lake does not appear in Stevin's law.
