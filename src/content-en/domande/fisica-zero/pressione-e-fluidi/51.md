A U-tube contains water at rest. The left branch is open to the air, while the right branch is closed at the top and contains, above the water, a gas at a pressure higher than atmospheric. How do the water levels in the two branches compare?

- The level is lower in the right branch, the closed one
- The level is lower in the left branch, the open one
- The levels are the same, because the liquid is the same
- The levels depend only on the width of the branches

## Explanation

The rule of equal levels requires the same pressure on both free surfaces, and here this condition is not satisfied. Two points in the water at the same level, near the bottom of the tube, have the same pressure. In the right branch the pressure on the surface is greater, so by Stevin's law a smaller depth below that surface is enough to reach the same value. Therefore, the water surface in the closed branch is lower.
