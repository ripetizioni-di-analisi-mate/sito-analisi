In an aquarium full of still fresh water with a density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, point $A$ is $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the surface and point $B$ is $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the surface. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, by how much does the pressure at $B$ exceed that at $A$?

- $3924\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4905\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $294300\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2943\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

By Stevin's law, $p_A=p_0+\rho g\cdot0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $p_B=p_0+\rho g\cdot0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. Subtracting, the atmospheric pressure $p_0$ cancels and we are left with $p_B-p_A=\rho g\cdot0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore, the difference is $1000\cdot9.81\cdot0.30\,\mathrm{Pa}=2943\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
