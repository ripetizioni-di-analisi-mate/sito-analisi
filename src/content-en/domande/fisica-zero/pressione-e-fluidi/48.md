Some still water always remains in the U-shaped trap under a sink. The branch connected to the sink and the branch connected to the drain are both open to air at the same pressure. How do the water levels in the two branches compare?

- Higher in the branch connected to the sink
- Higher in the branch connected to the drain
- At the same level
- Higher in the wider branch

## Explanation

The two branches of the trap are connected, contain the same water at rest, and have the same pressure on their free surfaces. Two points at the same level in the connected water have the same pressure, so by Stevin's law they are at the same depth below the two surfaces. Therefore, the two free surfaces are at the same level, whatever the width of the branches.
