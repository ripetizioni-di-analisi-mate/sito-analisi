A bricklayer uses a water level, a long transparent tube filled with water and open at both ends. At one end, held against a wall, the water is at rest $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor. The other end is held higher, against the opposite wall of the room. At what height above the floor is the water surface there?

- Higher than $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, because that end is raised
- Lower than $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, because the water runs toward the lower end
- At a height that depends on the length of the tube
- At $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, the same height

## Explanation

The two parts of the tube are communicating vessels with the same liquid at rest, and atmospheric pressure acts on both surfaces. Therefore, the two free surfaces are at the same level, wherever the ends of the tube are held. On the other wall the water is therefore $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor, which is why the water level can transfer a height from one wall to another.
