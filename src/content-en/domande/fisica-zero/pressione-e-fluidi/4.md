Two syringes have their tips sealed with a finger; the first contains only air and the second only water. Both plungers are pushed hard. What happens?

- Both plungers move in by the same distance
- The plunger of the water-filled syringe moves in, while the plunger of the air-filled syringe barely moves
- Neither plunger can move
- The plunger of the air-filled syringe moves in, while the plunger of the water-filled syringe barely moves

## Explanation

The volume of a gas can change considerably when the pressure changes. Therefore, when the plunger is pushed, the air trapped in the first syringe is compressed and the plunger moves in. A liquid, on the other hand, keeps approximately the same volume, so the water in the second syringe is not compressed appreciably and its plunger barely moves.
