The water in a well is at rest, and Stevin's law gives the pressure increase $\rho gh$ at the depth $h$. If $\rho$ is expressed in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $g$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and $h$ in $\mathrm{m}$, in what unit is the product $\rho gh$ obtained?

- $\mathrm{N}$
- $\mathrm{kg/m^2}$
- $\mathrm{N/m^3}$
- $\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The product of the units is
$$
\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m^3}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s^2}}\cdot\mathrm{m}=\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m\cdot s^2}}.
$$
Since $1\,\mathrm{N}=1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$, we have
$$
\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m\cdot s^2}}=\frac{\mathrm{kg\cdot m}}{\mathrm{s^2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathrm{m^2}}=\frac{\mathrm{N}}{\mathrm{m^2}}.
$$
Therefore, $\rho gh$ is measured in $\mathrm{N/m^2}$, that is, in pascals, the same unit as the pressure $p_0$ to which it is added in Stevin's law.
