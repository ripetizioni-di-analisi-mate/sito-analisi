All $200\,\mathrm{mL}$ of water in a tall, narrow glass are poured into a soup plate without spilling a drop. What changes about the water?

- Its volume, while its shape stays that of the glass
- Both its shape and its volume
- Its shape, while its volume stays $200\,\mathrm{mL}$
- Its mass, because the water spreads out in the plate

## Explanation

A liquid flows and takes the shape of its container, so in the plate the water takes the shape of the plate. A liquid, however, keeps approximately the same volume. Therefore, the water still occupies $200\,\mathrm{mL}$, even though its level in the plate is much lower; the mass is unchanged because no water was lost.
