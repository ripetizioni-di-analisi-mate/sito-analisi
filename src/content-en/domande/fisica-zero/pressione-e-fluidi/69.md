An iron cube and an aluminum cube have the same volume, $8\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, and the iron cube has the greater mass. Both hang from strings, completely submerged in water. How do the buoyant forces on the two cubes compare?

- They are equal
- Greater on the iron cube
- Greater on the aluminum cube
- Zero on the iron cube, which would sink without the string

## Explanation

The buoyant force depends on the density of the fluid and the immersed volume, $F_A=\rho_f gV_{\mathrm{imm}}$. The two cubes are in the same liquid and have the same volume, all of it submerged. Therefore, the buoyant forces are equal, because the mass of the body does not appear in the formula.
