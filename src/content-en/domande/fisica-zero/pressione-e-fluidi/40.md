A pot contains water $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ deep. Some salt is dissolved, and the level is brought back to $12\,\mathrm{cm}$; salt water is denser than fresh water. What happens to the relative pressure on the bottom of the pot?

- It decreases, because the salt takes up part of the volume
- It stays the same, because the height of the liquid is the same
- It drops to zero, because dissolved salt exerts no pressure
- It increases, because the density of the liquid is greater

## Explanation

By Stevin's law, the relative pressure on the bottom is $\rho gh$. The height $h$ and the acceleration due to gravity $g$ are unchanged, while the density $\rho$ has increased. Therefore, the relative pressure on the bottom increases.
