A scuba diver with gear and weights occupies a volume of $0.08\,\mathrm{m^3}$ in the sea, where the water has a density of $1030\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. What total mass must he have so that, completely submerged and left at rest, he stays in equilibrium without swimming?

- $808.3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $8.24\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $41.2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $82.4\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

When completely submerged, the diver experiences the buoyant force $F_A=\rho_f gV$. He stays in equilibrium if his weight equals the buoyant force, that is, $mg=\rho_f gV$, so $m=\rho_f V$. Therefore, $m=1030\cdot0.08\,\mathrm{kg}=82.4\,\mathrm{kg}$; with this mass, the diver's average density equals that of seawater.
