An insulated cooler bag holds $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and a large block of ice at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. How much ice melts while the water cools to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ and $L_f=334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$.

- $0.63\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.037\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Cooling from $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, the water releases $0.40\cdot4180\cdot50\,\mathrm{J}=83\,600\,\mathrm{J}$. The bag is insulated, so this energy is absorbed by the ice, which stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and melts. The melted mass is therefore
$$
m=\frac{83\,600\,\mathrm{J}}{334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}}\approx0.25\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
The value $0.63\,\mathrm{kg}$ results from leaving out the mass of the warm water when calculating the energy it releases.
