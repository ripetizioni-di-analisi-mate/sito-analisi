The graph shows the temperature of a sample of ice, initially at $-10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, as a function of the heat absorbed, at standard atmospheric pressure. The horizontal segment starts at $Q=1.68\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and ends at $Q=28.40\,\mathrm{kJ}$. What is the mass of the sample? Use $L_f=334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$.

::grafico{curve="t,((-10+t*10/1.68)-abs(-10+t*10/1.68))/2+((t-28.4)*25/8.36+abs((t-28.4)*25/8.36))/2,0,36.76" legenda=0 punti="1.68,0,pieno;28.4,0,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=40 ymin=-15 ymax=30 nomi="Q,t" titolo="Temperature $t$, in degrees Celsius, as a function of the heat absorbed $Q$, in kilojoules. The horizontal segment runs from $1.68\,\mathrm{kJ}$ to $28.40\,\mathrm{kJ}$"}

- $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.012\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.080\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.11\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The horizontal segment corresponds to melting, during which the temperature stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The heat absorbed along this segment is the difference $28.40-1.68=26.72\,\mathrm{kJ}$, that is, $26\,720\,\mathrm{J}$. Since $Q=mL_f$ during melting, the mass is
$$
m=\frac{26\,720\,\mathrm{J}}{334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}}=0.080\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
The value $0.11\,\mathrm{kg}$ results from using all the heat on the graph, including that of the two warming stages.
