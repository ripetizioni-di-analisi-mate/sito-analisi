A thermos holds $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Then $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of tap water is added, and the mixture settles at $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Neglecting the heat capacity of the thermos, what was the temperature of the added water?

- $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $65\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $130\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The masses are equal and the substance is the same, so the equilibrium temperature is the average of the initial temperatures, that is, $50=\dfrac{80+t}{2}$. Multiplying by $2$ gives $80+t=100$, so $t=20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The hot water cools by $30\,\mathrm{K}$ and the added water warms by $30\,\mathrm{K}$, as the energy balance requires for equal masses. The value $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ confuses the temperature sought with the change of the hot water.
