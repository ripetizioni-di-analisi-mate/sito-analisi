A student mixes equal masses of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and at $80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ in an insulated container. To calculate the final temperature, the student writes the temperatures in kelvins, $293.15\,\mathrm{K}$ and $353.15\,\mathrm{K}$, and obtains $323.15\,\mathrm{K}$. Which statement about this calculation is correct?

- The result is wrong, because the formula holds only in degrees Celsius
- The result corresponds to $323.15\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- The result is correct and corresponds to $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- To convert back to degrees Celsius, $273.15$ must be subtracted twice

## Explanation

The formula for the equilibrium temperature holds when all temperatures are expressed on the same scale, so it also holds in kelvins. With equal masses the result is the average, $\dfrac{293.15+353.15}{2}=323.15$. To convert to degrees Celsius, $273.15$ is subtracted only once, so the final temperature is $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, the same value obtained by working in degrees Celsius.
