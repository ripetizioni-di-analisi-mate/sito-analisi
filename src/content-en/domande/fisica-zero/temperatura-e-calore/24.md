A section of copper water pipe with a mass of $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ absorbs $1560\,\mathrm{J}$, and its temperature rises by $20\,\mathrm{K}$. What is the specific heat capacity of copper?

- $78\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $390\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $6240\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $15.6\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$

## Explanation

From the equation $Q=mc\Delta T$, since $m$ and $\Delta T$ are nonzero,
$$
c=\frac{Q}{m\Delta T}=\frac{1560\,\mathrm{J}}{0.20\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot20\,\mathrm{K}}=390\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}.
$$
The denominator is $4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot K}$, so the specific heat capacity is $1560\div4=390$. The value $78$ results from forgetting to divide by the mass.
