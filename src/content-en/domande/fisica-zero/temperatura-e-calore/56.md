In an insulated cup of negligible heat capacity, cold milk is poured into hot coffee. The milk, $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ with a specific heat capacity of $4000\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, goes from $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. What is the heat $Q$ transferred by the coffee?

- $+32\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-40\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-32\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-8000\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The milk absorbs $Q_{\mathrm{milk}}=0.20\cdot4000\cdot(50-10)\,\mathrm{J}=32\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. Since the cup is insulated and has negligible heat capacity, the sum of the heat transferred by the milk and by the coffee is zero. Therefore, the heat transferred by the coffee is $-32\,000\,\mathrm{J}$, and the negative sign indicates that the coffee releases energy. The mass and initial temperature of the coffee are not needed for this calculation.
