A cast-iron griddle taken off the heat releases $1500\,\mathrm{J}$ to the air in the kitchen, and the air receives all of this energy. With the convention $Q>0$ for heat absorbed by the chosen system, what are the values of $Q$ for the griddle and for the air?

- $Q_{\mathrm{griddle}}=+1500\,\mathrm{J}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{air}}=-1500\,\mathrm{J}$
- $Q_{\mathrm{griddle}}=-1500\,\mathrm{J}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{air}}=-1500\,\mathrm{J}$
- $Q_{\mathrm{griddle}}=-1500\,\mathrm{J}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{air}}=+1500\,\mathrm{J}$
- $Q_{\mathrm{griddle}}=+1500\,\mathrm{J}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{air}}=+1500\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The sign of $Q$ depends on the chosen system. The griddle releases energy, so for the griddle $Q_{\mathrm{griddle}}=-1500\,\mathrm{J}$. The air receives the same energy, so for the air $Q_{\mathrm{air}}=+1500\,\mathrm{J}$. The two values have the same absolute value and opposite signs, because all the energy leaving the griddle enters the air.
