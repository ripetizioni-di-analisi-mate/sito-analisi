Either one kilogram of water or a $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ aluminum heat sink is to be warmed by $10\,\mathrm{K}$. Which of the two requires more heat? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ and $c_{\mathrm{Al}}=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- The water, which requires $41\,800\,\mathrm{J}$ compared with $36\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- The heat sink, because its mass is four times as large
- They require the same heat, because they undergo the same temperature change
- The heat sink, which requires $36\,000\,\mathrm{J}$ compared with $4180\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The heat for each object is calculated with the specific heat equation. For the water $Q=1\cdot4180\cdot10\,\mathrm{J}=41\,800\,\mathrm{J}$, while for the heat sink $Q=4.0\cdot900\cdot10\,\mathrm{J}=36\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore, the water requires more heat. Indeed, the mass of the heat sink is four times as large, but the specific heat capacity of water is more than four times that of aluminum. The value $4180\,\mathrm{J}$ for the water results from forgetting the change of $10\,\mathrm{K}$.
