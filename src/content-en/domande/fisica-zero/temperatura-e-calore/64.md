Ice at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ with a mass of $50\,\mathrm{g}$ is added to warm water. Cooling to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ could supply at most $12\,\mathrm{kJ}$ from the water. With $L_f=334$ kJ/kg and no losses, what remains at equilibrium at standard pressure?

- Water and ice at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
- Liquid water alone at exactly $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
- Water and steam at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
- Liquid water only, above $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

## Explanation

Complete melting would require $0.050\cdot 334=16.7$ kilojoules. The available $12\,\mathrm{kJ}$ melts only some of the ice, so both phases coexist at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
