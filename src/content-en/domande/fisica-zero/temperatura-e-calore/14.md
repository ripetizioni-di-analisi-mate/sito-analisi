On a spring morning, the air goes from $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and a news report says that the temperature "has doubled." What happens to the temperature expressed in kelvins?

- It increases by $10\,\mathrm{K}$, from $283.15\,\mathrm{K}$ to $293.15\,\mathrm{K}$
- It doubles, from $10\,\mathrm{K}$ to $20\,\mathrm{K}$
- It doubles, from $283.15\,\mathrm{K}$ to $566.30\,\mathrm{K}$
- It increases by $283.15\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

The two temperatures in kelvins are $10+273.15=283.15\,\mathrm{K}$ and $20+273.15=293.15\,\mathrm{K}$. Their difference is $10\,\mathrm{K}$, the same change expressed in degrees Celsius. The value in kelvins therefore increases by about $3.5\%$. The doubling of the number in degrees Celsius depends on where the zero of the Celsius scale is placed, so it does not describe a doubling of the temperature of the air.
