A camping stove supplies $100\,000\,\mathrm{J}$, but $20\%$ of this energy is lost to the air. The rest is absorbed by $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water in a small pot of negligible heat capacity. By about how much does the temperature of the water rise? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- $48\,\mathrm{K}$
- $38\,\mathrm{K}$
- $9.6\,\mathrm{K}$
- $19\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

The water absorbs $80\%$ of the energy supplied, that is, $Q=0.80\cdot100\,000\,\mathrm{J}=80\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. The specific heat equation gives
$$
\Delta T=\frac{Q}{mc_a}=\frac{80\,000\,\mathrm{J}}{0.50\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}}\approx38\,\mathrm{K}.
$$
The value $48\,\mathrm{K}$ results from giving the water all the energy supplied, including the losses, while $9.6\,\mathrm{K}$ corresponds to the lost energy alone.
