A freshly cooked hard-boiled egg, with a heat capacity of $200\,\mathrm{J/K}$, is placed in a bowl of cold water with a heat capacity of $2000\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Until equilibrium is reached, the water warms by $7.0\,\mathrm{K}$. Neglecting heat transfer to the bowl and to the air, by how much does the temperature of the egg drop?

- $7.0\,\mathrm{K}$
- $70\,\mathrm{K}$
- $0.70\,\mathrm{K}$
- $10\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

The water absorbs $Q=C\Delta T=2000\,\mathrm{J/K}\cdot7.0\,\mathrm{K}=14\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. Heat transfer to the bowl and to the air is negligible, so the egg releases the same energy, $14\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. The temperature of the egg therefore drops by
$$
\frac{14\,000\,\mathrm{J}}{200\,\mathrm{J/K}}=70\,\mathrm{K}.
$$
The egg has one tenth the heat capacity of the water, so its temperature changes ten times as much. The value $0.70\,\mathrm{K}$ results from swapping the two heat capacities.
