An electric kettle holds $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. How much energy must the water absorb to reach $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, with no losses and before boiling begins? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- $627\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $334\,400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $501\,600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6270\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The water stays liquid, so the **specific heat equation** $Q=mc_a\Delta T$ applies. The temperature change is $\Delta T=100-20=80\,\mathrm{K}$. Therefore,
$$
Q=1.5\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\cdot80\,\mathrm{K}=501\,600\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $627\,000\,\mathrm{J}$ results from using the final temperature instead of the change.
