The graph shows the heat $Q$ absorbed by $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water and by $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ of aluminum as a function of the temperature increase $\Delta T$, with no change of state. The specific heat capacities are $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ and $c_{\mathrm{Al}}=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. Which line refers to the water?

::grafico{f="4.18*x;0.9*x" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=45 nomi="ΔT,Q" legenda=0 etichette="7.2,36,A;8.6,11,B" titolo="Heat $Q$, in kilojoules, absorbed by $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ of each substance as a function of $\Delta T$, in kelvins"}

- Line $B$, because for the same $\Delta T$ it requires less heat
- Line $B$, because for the same $Q$ it warms more
- The lines cannot be assigned without knowing the initial temperatures
- Line $A$, because for the same $\Delta T$ it requires more heat

## Explanation

For a fixed mass, the equation $Q=mc\Delta T$ shows that $Q$ is proportional to $\Delta T$, with constant of proportionality $mc$. On the graph, this constant is the slope of the line. The masses are equal and the specific heat capacity of water is larger, so the line for water has the larger slope. Therefore, water corresponds to line $A$, which at $10\,\mathrm{K}$ requires $41.8\,\mathrm{kJ}$, compared with $9\,\mathrm{kJ}$ for aluminum. The initial temperatures are not needed, because the equation contains only the change.
