A barbecue grill with a heat capacity of $800\,\mathrm{J/K}$, at $60\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, is dipped for cleaning into a tub of water with a heat capacity of $3200\,\mathrm{J/K}$, at $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Neglecting heat transfer to the air, what common temperature do they reach?

- $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $35\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The system is treated as isolated, so the sum of the heat transfers is zero. With the heat capacities in place of the products $mc$, the formula for the equilibrium temperature becomes
$$
t_e=\frac{800\cdot60+3200\cdot10}{800+3200}=\frac{80\,000}{4000}=20 .
$$
The final temperature is therefore $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, closer to that of the water, whose heat capacity is four times as large. The value $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ results from swapping the two heat capacities.
