A $1\,\mathrm{g}$ drop of water warms by $1\,\mathrm{K}$. How much energy does it absorb? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- $4180\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.18\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.418\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4\,180\,000\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The mass must be expressed in kilograms, so $1\,\mathrm{g}=0.001\,\mathrm{kg}$. The specific heat equation gives
$$
Q=0.001\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\cdot1\,\mathrm{K}=4.18\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The specific heat capacity refers to one kilogram, so for one gram the energy is a thousand times smaller. The value $4\,180\,000\,\mathrm{J}$ results from multiplying by $1000$ instead of dividing.
