On the summit of Mont Blanc, where the atmospheric pressure is a little more than half of that at sea level, water boils at about $85\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Which statement explains this fact?

- The boiling point depends on pressure, and the value of $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ applies at standard atmospheric pressure
- At high altitude, water has a lower specific heat capacity
- At high altitude, the thermometer gives wrong readings because the air is cold
- At high altitude, water contains less heat

## Explanation

The boiling point of a liquid depends on pressure. The value of $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ for water refers to standard atmospheric pressure, $101\,325\,\mathrm{Pa}$. On Mont Blanc the pressure is lower, so boiling occurs at a different temperature, about $85\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. A thermometer brought into equilibrium with the water measures this temperature correctly.
