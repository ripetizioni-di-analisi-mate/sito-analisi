The graph shows the heat $Q$ absorbed by a $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ metal block as a function of the temperature increase $\Delta T$, with no change of state. What is the specific heat capacity of the metal?

::grafico{f="1.8*x" xmin=0 xmax=15 ymin=0 ymax=30 nomi="ΔT,Q" legenda=0 punti="10,18,pieno" segmenti="10,0,10,18;0,18,10,18" titolo="Heat $Q$, in kilojoules, absorbed by the $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block as a function of $\Delta T$, in kelvins"}

- $1800\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $3600\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $0.9\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$

## Explanation

The graph shows that $\Delta T=10\,\mathrm{K}$ corresponds to $Q=18\,\mathrm{kJ}=18\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. The specific heat equation then gives
$$
c=\frac{Q}{m\Delta T}=\frac{18\,000\,\mathrm{J}}{2.0\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot10\,\mathrm{K}}=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}.
$$
The value $1800$ is the slope of the line in $\mathrm{J/K}$, that is, the heat capacity of the block; to obtain the specific heat capacity, it must also be divided by the mass.
