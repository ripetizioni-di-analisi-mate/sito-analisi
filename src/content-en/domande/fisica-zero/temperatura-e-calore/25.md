A bucket of sand left in the sun absorbs $48\,000\,\mathrm{J}$, and the sand warms by $15\,\mathrm{K}$. The specific heat capacity of sand is $800\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, and the bucket absorbs a negligible amount of energy. How much sand is in the bucket?

- $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $60\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The sand does not change state, so $Q=mc\Delta T$ applies. The product $c\Delta T$ is nonzero, so we divide by it
$$
m=\frac{Q}{c\Delta T}=\frac{48\,000\,\mathrm{J}}{800\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\cdot15\,\mathrm{K}}=4.0\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
The denominator is $12\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, the energy needed to warm one kilogram of sand by $15\,\mathrm{K}$. The value $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ results from forgetting to divide by the temperature change.
