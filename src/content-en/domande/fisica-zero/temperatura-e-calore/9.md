The water in a pot on the stove goes from $293.15\,\mathrm{K}$ to a temperature of $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. What is its temperature change?

- $80\,\mathrm{K}$
- $-193.15\,\mathrm{K}$
- $373.15\,\mathrm{K}$
- $100\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

To compute a difference, both temperatures must be expressed on the same scale. The initial temperature in degrees Celsius is $293.15-273.15=20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The change is the final temperature minus the initial temperature, so it is $100-20=80$ degrees. Since an interval of one kelvin is the same size as an interval of one degree Celsius, $\Delta T=80\,\mathrm{K}$. The value $-193.15\,\mathrm{K}$ results from subtracting a value in kelvins directly from a value in degrees Celsius.
