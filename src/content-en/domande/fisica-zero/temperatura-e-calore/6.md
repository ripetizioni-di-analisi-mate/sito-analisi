In a garden, four temperatures are measured with the same thermometer, brought to equilibrium each time: the soil in a flowerpot is at $300.15\,\mathrm{K}$, the water in a watering can at $27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, a stone at $24\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and a rubber hose at $296.15\,\mathrm{K}$. Which pair of objects, if brought into contact, is already in thermal equilibrium?

- The soil and the water
- The stone and the hose
- The soil and the stone
- The water and the hose

## Explanation

To compare the readings, they are all expressed on the same scale. The soil is at $300.15-273.15=27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and the hose at $296.15-273.15=23\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Only the soil and the water have the same temperature, $27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. By the **zeroth law of thermodynamics**, two objects that the thermometer finds at the same temperature are in thermal equilibrium with each other; therefore, in contact, the soil and the water have no net energy transfer.
