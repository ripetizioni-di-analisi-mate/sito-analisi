Four quantities are considered for the water in a pot, each written first in degrees Celsius and then in kelvins. Which of the four has the same numerical value on both scales?

- The initial temperature of the water
- The temperature increase during heating
- The boiling point of the water
- The final temperature reached by the water

## Explanation

The two scales have different zero points, so the numerical value of a temperature in kelvins exceeds its value in degrees Celsius by $273.15$. A change is the difference of two temperatures, and in the difference the constant $273.15$ appears twice with opposite signs. Therefore, the temperature increase has the same numerical value in degrees Celsius and in kelvins. For example, heating from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is an increase of $80\,\mathrm{K}$.
