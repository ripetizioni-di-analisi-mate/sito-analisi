On a freezing night, the air in a cellar drops to $-5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Next to the crates of vegetables there is a tub full of mixed water and ice, at standard atmospheric pressure. As long as the tub still contains both water and ice, which statement is correct?

- The water absorbs energy from the air in order to freeze, and the contents of the tub stay at about $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- The water releases energy to the air, and the contents of the tub drop at once to $-5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- The tub exchanges no energy with the air until all the water has frozen
- The water releases energy to the air as it freezes, and the contents of the tub stay at about $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The air is colder than the tub, so the contents of the tub release energy to the air and part of the water freezes. Freezing is the reverse of melting, so the freezing water releases the energy $mL_f$, and for the water $Q=-mL_f$. During a change of state at constant pressure, the temperature stays at the melting point as long as both phases are present. The contents of the tub therefore stay at about $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ as long as there is still liquid water.
