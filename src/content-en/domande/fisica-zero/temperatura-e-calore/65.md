An ice sample starts at $-10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The goal is to calculate the energy required to reach the instant when melting first starts at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Which data, besides the temperatures, are sufficient?

- Only the specific heat capacity of liquid water.
- Only mass and specific latent heat of fusion.
- Mass and specific latent heat of vaporisation.
- Mass and mean specific heat capacity of ice.

## Explanation

Up to the onset of melting the sample remains entirely solid. Its energy gain is therefore its mass times the specific heat capacity of ice times the $10\,\mathrm{K}$ rise.
