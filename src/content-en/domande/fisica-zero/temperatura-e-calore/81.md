At standard atmospheric pressure, two processes are compared: melting $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice already at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and vaporizing $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water already at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. With $L_f=334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and $L_v=2\,260\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, which statement is correct?

- Melting requires more energy, because ice is a solid
- The two processes require the same energy, because the mass is the same
- Neither process requires energy, because the temperature stays constant
- Vaporization requires more energy, $2\,260\,000\,\mathrm{J}$ compared with $334\,000\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

For a change of state at the transition temperature, the heat is $Q=mL$. With $m=1\,\mathrm{kg}$, melting requires $334\,000\,\mathrm{J}$ and vaporization requires $2\,260\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore, vaporization requires much more energy, because the latent heat of vaporization of water is larger than the latent heat of fusion. In both cases the temperature stays constant while the sample absorbs energy.
