An empty cast-iron teapot is rinsed with hot water, and in this way it absorbs $9600\,\mathrm{J}$ while its temperature rises by $12\,\mathrm{K}$. What is the heat capacity of the teapot?

- $115\,200\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $800\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $80\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $9600\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The **heat capacity** of an object is the heat transferred divided by the temperature change, provided the change is nonzero. Therefore,
$$
C=\frac{Q}{\Delta T}=\frac{9600\,\mathrm{J}}{12\,\mathrm{K}}=800\,\mathrm{J/K}.
$$
The mass of the teapot is not needed, because the heat capacity refers to the whole object. The value $115\,200\,\mathrm{J/K}$ results from multiplying instead of dividing.
