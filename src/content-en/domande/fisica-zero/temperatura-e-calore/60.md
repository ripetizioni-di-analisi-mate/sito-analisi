To measure the specific heat capacity of a metal, a small $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ cylinder of that metal, heated to $75\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, is placed in $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ inside an insulated container of negligible heat capacity. At equilibrium the temperature is $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. What is the specific heat capacity of the metal? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- $41\,800\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $279\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $418\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$

## Explanation

The water warms by $5\,\mathrm{K}$ and absorbs $Q_a=0.10\cdot4180\cdot5\,\mathrm{J}=2090\,\mathrm{J}$. The system is isolated, so the metal releases the same energy while cooling by $75-25=50\,\mathrm{K}$. Therefore,
$$
c=\frac{2090\,\mathrm{J}}{0.10\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot50\,\mathrm{K}}=418\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}.
$$
The value $279\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ results from using $75\,\mathrm{K}$, the initial temperature of the metal, instead of its change.
