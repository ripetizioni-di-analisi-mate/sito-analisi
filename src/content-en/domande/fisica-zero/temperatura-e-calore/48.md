A $2.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ metal block has a heat capacity of $2250\,\mathrm{J/K}$. A table lists the specific heat capacities, in $\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, of some metals: aluminum $900$, iron $450$, copper $390$, lead $130$. Which metal could the block be made of?

- Iron
- Aluminum
- Copper
- Lead

## Explanation

For a homogeneous object $C=mc$, so the specific heat capacity of the material is
$$
c=\frac{C}{m}=\frac{2250\,\mathrm{J/K}}{2.5\,\mathrm{kg}}=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}.
$$
This value matches that of aluminum. An iron block of the same mass would instead have a heat capacity of $2.5\cdot450\,\mathrm{J/K}=1125\,\mathrm{J/K}$, which is half as much.
