A bottle of water and a carton of milk are in the same refrigerator. The same thermometer, brought to equilibrium with each liquid, reads $6\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ in both cases. What happens if the bottle is placed against the carton?

- The milk transfers energy to the water, because it is a denser liquid
- The water transfers energy to the milk, because it has a higher specific heat capacity
- There is no net energy transfer, because the two liquids are at the same temperature
- Both liquids cool down to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The thermometer is in thermal equilibrium with both the water and the milk. By the **zeroth law of thermodynamics**, the water and the milk are therefore in thermal equilibrium with each other, so they are at the same temperature. Heat transfer is caused by a temperature difference; since the difference is zero, the net energy transfer between the two containers is zero. Density and specific heat capacity do not determine the direction of the transfer, which depends only on the temperatures.
