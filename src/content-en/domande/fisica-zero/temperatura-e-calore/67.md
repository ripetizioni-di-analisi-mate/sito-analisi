In a camping cooler, a $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ block of ice at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ melts, and the resulting water then warms to $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, at standard atmospheric pressure. How much energy does it absorb in total? Use $L_f=334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- $75\,160\,\mathrm{J}$
- $66\,800\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8360\,\mathrm{J}$
- $58\,440\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The process takes place in two stages, so the two contributions are added. During melting the temperature stays constant and the heat is $mL_f=0.20\cdot334\,000\,\mathrm{J}=66\,800\,\mathrm{J}$. In the subsequent heating the water is liquid and absorbs $mc_a\Delta T=0.20\cdot4180\cdot10\,\mathrm{J}=8360\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore, $Q_{\mathrm{tot}}=66\,800+8360=75\,160\,\mathrm{J}$.
