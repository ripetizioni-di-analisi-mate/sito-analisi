A pot of broth receives $50\,000\,\mathrm{J}$ from the stove and warms by $12\,\mathrm{K}$, starting from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The next day, the same amount of broth starts from $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and again receives $50\,000\,\mathrm{J}$, with no losses and no boiling. By how much does the broth warm on the second day?

- $24\,\mathrm{K}$
- $12\,\mathrm{K}$
- $6\,\mathrm{K}$
- $52\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

The specific heat equation gives $\Delta T=\dfrac{Q}{mc}$. The mass, the specific heat capacity, and the heat received are the same as on the first day, so the temperature increase is also the same, $12\,\mathrm{K}$. The initial temperature does not appear in the formula, provided the broth stays liquid. The broth therefore reaches $40+12=52\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, so the number $52$ is the final temperature reached.
