Four situations are observed in a kitchen. In which of the four can the heat absorbed by the water not be calculated with the equation $Q=mc_a\Delta T$?

- Tap water warming from $15\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ in a small saucepan
- Water in a pitcher cooling in the refrigerator from $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $8\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- Water boiling at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and turning into vapor
- Water in a baby bottle warming from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $37\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The specific heat equation applies when the temperature changes without a change of state. In boiling water the temperature stays at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, so $\Delta T=0$, while the water absorbs heat to turn into vapor. In this case the heat absorbed is calculated with $Q=mL_v$. In the other three situations the water stays liquid and changes temperature, so the equation applies.
