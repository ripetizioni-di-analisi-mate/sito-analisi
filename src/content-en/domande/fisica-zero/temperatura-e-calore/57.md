A tube contains water that is still liquid at $-3\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ at standard atmospheric pressure. A tiny seed crystal starts ice formation, and the temperature rises towards $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The tube is insulated during this short interval. Where does the energy that warms the sample come from?

- From freezing part of the supercooled water.
- From melting all the ice that is forming.
- From a drop in specific heat capacity that creates new energy in the liquid.
- From heat that must have entered through the walls.

## Explanation

The liquid was below its freezing point without having formed ice. The seed starts freezing, which releases energy. That energy warms the sample even though no heat is supplied through the walls.
