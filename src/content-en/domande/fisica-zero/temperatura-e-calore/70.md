A pot of water is boiling at standard atmospheric pressure, and the burner flame is turned up. What happens to the temperature of the water as long as there is liquid water in the pot?

- It rises above $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, more so the higher the flame
- It stays at about $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- It drops, because the water loses vapor
- It rises by a few degrees and then levels off

## Explanation

During boiling at constant pressure, the temperature stays at the boiling point as long as both liquid and vapor are present. At standard atmospheric pressure this temperature is about $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The extra energy supplied by the flame is absorbed in vaporization, so the amount of water turning into vapor in a given time increases, while the temperature does not change.
