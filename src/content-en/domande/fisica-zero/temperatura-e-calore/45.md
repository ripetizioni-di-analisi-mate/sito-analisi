A cast-iron grill pan and an empty aluminum pot have different masses and materials but the same heat capacity of $600\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Each receives $6000\,\mathrm{J}$, with no losses. Which statement is correct?

- The object with the smaller mass warms more
- Both warm by $10\,\mathrm{K}$
- The object with the lower specific heat capacity warms more
- It cannot be determined without knowing the masses and the specific heat capacities

## Explanation

For an object of heat capacity $C$, the temperature change is $\Delta T=\dfrac{Q}{C}$. The heat received and the heat capacity are the same for the two objects, so the changes are also equal, each being $\dfrac{6000}{600}\,\mathrm{K}=10\,\mathrm{K}$. The heat capacity already contains the product $mc$, so the masses and specific heat capacities taken separately are not needed.
