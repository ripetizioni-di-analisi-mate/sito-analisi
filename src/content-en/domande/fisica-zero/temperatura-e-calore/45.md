A data sheet gives $c=0.90$ in joules per gram per kelvin. What value in joules per kilogram per kelvin should be used with the sample mass in kilograms?

- $900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $90\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $0.0009\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$

## Explanation

One kilogram contains a thousand grams. Raising its temperature by one kelvin therefore takes a thousand times $0.90\,\mathrm{J}$, giving $900\,\mathrm{J}$.
