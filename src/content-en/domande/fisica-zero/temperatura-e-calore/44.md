A ceramic mug at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, with a heat capacity of $250\,\mathrm{J/K}$, is filled with boiling herbal tea and reaches $80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. How much heat has the mug absorbed?

- $15\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $20\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $5000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $25\,000\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The mug warms without a change of state, so $Q=C\Delta T$ applies. The temperature change of the mug is $\Delta T=80-20=60\,\mathrm{K}$. Therefore,
$$
Q=250\,\mathrm{J/K}\cdot60\,\mathrm{K}=15\,000\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $25\,000\,\mathrm{J}$ results from multiplying by $100$, the temperature of the tea, instead of by the temperature change of the mug.
