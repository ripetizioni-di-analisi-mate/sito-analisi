A $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ cast-iron pot holds $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water, and the pot and the water always warm together, at the same temperature. The specific heat capacity of cast iron is $500\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, and $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. What is the heat capacity of the pot and water together?

- $5680\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $4180\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $1500\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $16\,720\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The two parts always undergo the same temperature change, so the heat capacity of the whole is the sum of the heat capacities of the parts. The pot has $C_p=3.0\cdot500\,\mathrm{J/K}=1500\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and the water has $C_a=1.0\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/K}=4180\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Therefore, $C=1500+4180=5680\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The value $16\,720\,\mathrm{J/K}$ results from treating the entire $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ as if it were water.
