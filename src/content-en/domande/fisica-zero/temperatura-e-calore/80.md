An ice cube at $-10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is put on a saucer in a room at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and left there for many hours. How does the temperature of the contents of the saucer change over time?

- It rises steadily from $-10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- It rises to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and then stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- It rises to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, stays constant during melting, then rises to $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- It stays at $-10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ until all the ice has melted, then rises to $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The ice is colder than the room, so it absorbs energy and its temperature rises until it reaches $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. At that point melting begins, and as long as ice and water are both present the temperature stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. When all the ice has melted, the water keeps absorbing energy and warms until it reaches thermal equilibrium with the room at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
