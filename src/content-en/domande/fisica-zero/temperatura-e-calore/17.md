A probe initially at $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is placed in a large bath maintained at $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Its reading is rising and reaches $32\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ after five seconds. When does it give a reliable estimate of the bath temperature?

- When it reaches the average of $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
- Immediately, because any probe is always in equilibrium.
- When its reading settles after heat exchange.
- At the first reading different from $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

## Explanation

The probe records its own temperature. While that temperature is rising, the probe is still absorbing energy from the bath and the reading is transient.
