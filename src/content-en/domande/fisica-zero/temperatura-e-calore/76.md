A pot holds $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice and $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water, both at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and the stove supplies $16\,700\,\mathrm{J}$ to the mixture. What happens? Use $L_f=334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and neglect the pot.

- The temperature of the mixture rises to about $4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and the ice does not melt
- All the ice melts and the temperature stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $0.050\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice melts and the temperature rises to about $4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $0.050\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice melts and the temperature stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

As long as ice and water coexist, the temperature stays at the melting point, $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and the heat absorbed goes into the change of state. The mass that melts is $m=\dfrac{Q}{L_f}=\dfrac{16\,700}{334\,000}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.050\,\mathrm{kg}$. This mass is much less than $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, so some ice remains and the temperature does not change. The value of about $4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ results from mistakenly applying $Q=mc_a\Delta T$ to the whole mixture.
