A lab book lists $600\,\mathrm{J/K}$ for the vessel and $900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ for the sample material. What must the second value be multiplied by before the two contributions can be added to give total heat capacity?

- By the heat already supplied in joules.
- By the absolute temperature in kelvins.
- By the sample mass in kilograms.
- By the vessel volume in litres.

## Explanation

The second value is per kilogram. Multiplying it by the sample mass gives heat capacity in joules per kelvin, the same unit as the vessel’s capacity.
