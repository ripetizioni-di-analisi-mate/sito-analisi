A hot-water bottle holds $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $70\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and stays all night in a bedroom whose temperature remains at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. How much energy does the water release to its surroundings until thermal equilibrium is reached? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ and neglect the rubber of the bottle.

- $351\,120\,\mathrm{J}$
- $100\,320\,\mathrm{J}$
- $250\,800\,\mathrm{J}$
- $451\,440\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The temperature of the bedroom stays constant, so the water cools until it reaches **thermal equilibrium** with its surroundings, that is, $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The temperature change of the water is therefore $\Delta T=20-70=-50\,\mathrm{K}$. The specific heat equation gives
$$
Q=1.2\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\cdot(-50\,\mathrm{K})=-250\,800\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The negative sign indicates that the water releases energy, so $250\,800\,\mathrm{J}$ goes to the surroundings. The value $351\,120\,\mathrm{J}$ results from using the initial temperature instead of the change.
