A bathtub holds $30\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of cold water at $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is added. Neglecting heat transfer to the tub and to the air, what temperature does the water reach?

- $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The two samples are the same substance, so the specific heat capacities cancel in the formula for the **equilibrium temperature**, which becomes
$$
t_e=\frac{30\cdot50+10\cdot10}{30+10}=\frac{1600}{40}=40 .
$$
The water therefore reaches $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The result is closer to $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ because the hot water has three times the mass; the value $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, the simple average, would be correct only for equal masses.
