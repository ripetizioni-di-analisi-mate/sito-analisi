In a frying pan, $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of seed oil, initially at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, with a specific heat capacity of $2000\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, is heated. The oil absorbs $60\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. What temperature does it reach?

- $80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $60\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $35\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The specific heat equation gives the temperature change
$$
\Delta T=\frac{Q}{mc}=\frac{60\,000\,\mathrm{J}}{0.50\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot2000\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}}=60\,\mathrm{K}.
$$
The oil absorbs heat, so its temperature rises by $60$ degrees. The final temperature is therefore $20+60=80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The value $60\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ confuses the increase with the temperature reached.
