An iron doorstop has a mass of $4.18\,\mathrm{kg}$, and the specific heat capacity of iron is $450\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. What mass of water has the same heat capacity as the doorstop? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- $4.18\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $38.8\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.108\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.45\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The heat capacity of the doorstop is $C=4.18\cdot450\,\mathrm{J/K}=1881\,\mathrm{J/K}$. A mass of water $m$ has heat capacity $mc_a$, so we set $m\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}=1881\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Therefore,
$$
m=\frac{1881\,\mathrm{J/K}}{4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}}=0.45\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
So $0.45\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water has the same heat capacity as more than four kilograms of iron, because the specific heat capacity of water is much larger.
