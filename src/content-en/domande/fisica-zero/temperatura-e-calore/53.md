A steel nail at $90\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ falls into a bathtub full of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Until equilibrium is reached, which statement about the temperature changes is correct?

- The temperature of the nail changes much more than that of the water, because the water has a much larger heat capacity
- The two temperatures change by the same amount, because the heat released equals the heat absorbed
- The temperature of the water changes more, because water has a higher specific heat capacity
- The final temperature is the average of the two, $55\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

In the energy balance, the heat released by the nail equals, in absolute value, the heat absorbed by the water. For each object $|\Delta T|=\dfrac{|Q|}{C}$, so for the same energy exchanged, the temperature of the object with the smaller heat capacity changes more. The water in the tub has a much larger mass and specific heat capacity than the nail, so its temperature changes very little and the final temperature stays close to $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
