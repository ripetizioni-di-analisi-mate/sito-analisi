A water bottle holding $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water, left in a car in the sun, goes from $288.15\,\mathrm{K}$ to $318.15\,\mathrm{K}$. How much heat has the water absorbed? Use $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- $31\,350\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-31\,350\,\mathrm{J}$
- $125\,400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $332\,467\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The two temperatures are already on the same scale, so the change is $\Delta T=318.15-288.15=30\,\mathrm{K}$. The specific heat equation gives
$$
Q=0.25\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\cdot30\,\mathrm{K}=31\,350\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $332\,467\,\mathrm{J}$ results from using the final temperature in kelvins instead of the change.
