Some vapor at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ escaping from a pot, $0.010\,\mathrm{kg}$ in all, condenses on the cook's hand, and the resulting water then cools to $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. How much energy does it release in total? Use $L_v=2\,260\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$.

- $22\,600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2926\,\mathrm{J}$
- $19\,674\,\mathrm{J}$
- $25\,526\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The process has two stages, both releasing energy. In condensation the vapor releases $mL_v=0.010\cdot2\,260\,000\,\mathrm{J}=22\,600\,\mathrm{J}$ at constant temperature. Then the liquid water cools by $70\,\mathrm{K}$ and releases $mc_a\cdot70\,\mathrm{K}=0.010\cdot4180\cdot70\,\mathrm{J}=2926\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore, the total energy released is $22\,600+2926=25\,526\,\mathrm{J}$, and for the vapor $Q=-25\,526\,\mathrm{J}$. Most of the energy comes from condensation.
