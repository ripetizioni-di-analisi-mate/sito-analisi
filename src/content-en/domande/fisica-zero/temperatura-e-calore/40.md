A steel saucepan has a mass of $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$, and the specific heat capacity of steel is $500\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. What is the heat capacity of the saucepan?

- $417\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0.0024\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $600\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $501.2\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

For a homogeneous object, the heat capacity is the product of mass and specific heat capacity, $C=mc$. Therefore,
$$
C=1.2\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot500\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}=600\,\mathrm{J/K}.
$$
The kilograms cancel, leaving the unit $\mathrm{J/K}$. The value $417\,\mathrm{J/K}$ results from dividing the specific heat capacity by the mass instead of multiplying them.
