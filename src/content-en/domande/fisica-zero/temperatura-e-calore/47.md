The back of a frying pan states that its heat capacity is $500\,\mathrm{J/K}$. What does this figure mean?

- It takes $500\,\mathrm{J}$ to raise the temperature of the whole pan by $1\,\mathrm{K}$
- It takes $500\,\mathrm{J}$ to raise the temperature of one kilogram of the pan's material by $1\,\mathrm{K}$
- The pan contains $500\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat for each kelvin of its temperature
- The pan can reach a maximum temperature of $500\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

Heat capacity is $C=\dfrac{Q}{\Delta T}$ and refers to the whole object. With $C=500\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and $\Delta T=1\,\mathrm{K}$, this gives $Q=500\,\mathrm{J}$. The energy for one kilogram of material is the specific heat capacity instead, and finding it would also require the mass of the pan. Heat describes a transfer of energy, so it cannot be said that the pan contains a certain amount of it.
