A $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ brick in a wall, warmed by the sun during the day, releases $33\,600\,\mathrm{J}$ to the air during the night. The specific heat capacity of the brick is $840\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. What is the change in its temperature?

- $+20\,\mathrm{K}$
- $-40\,\mathrm{K}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{K}$
- $-20\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

The brick releases energy, so for the brick $Q=-33\,600\,\mathrm{J}$. The specific heat equation gives
$$
\Delta T=\frac{Q}{mc}=\frac{-33\,600\,\mathrm{J}}{2.0\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot840\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}}=-20\,\mathrm{K}.
$$
The change is negative, so the temperature of the brick drops by $20\,\mathrm{K}$. The value $-40\,\mathrm{K}$ results from forgetting to divide by the mass.
