An ice pack for a cooler bag, equivalent to $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice, goes from $-15\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $-5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ while remaining solid. The specific heat capacity of ice is $2100\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. How much heat does the ice pack absorb?

- $-8400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $16\,800\,\mathrm{J}$
- $21\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8400\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The ice stays solid, so the specific heat equation applies with the specific heat capacity of ice. The change is $\Delta T=-5-(-15)=10\,\mathrm{K}$, positive because the temperature rises. Therefore,
$$
Q=0.40\cdot2100\cdot10\,\mathrm{J}=8400\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $16\,800\,\mathrm{J}$ results from using a change of $20\,\mathrm{K}$, that is, from adding $15$ and $5$.
