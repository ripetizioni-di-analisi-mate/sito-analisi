A kitchen thermometer with a heat capacity of $10\,\mathrm{J/K}$, initially at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, is dipped into a spoon holding $0.010\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $60\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Heat transfer to the spoon and to the air is negligible, and $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. What temperature does the thermometer read at equilibrium, to the nearest degree?

- $60\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $28\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $52\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The thermometer and the water exchange energy until they reach the same temperature, which is the reading. The heat capacity of the water is $0.010\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/K}=41.8\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Since heat transfer to the surroundings is negligible, the formula for the **equilibrium temperature** applies, with the heat capacities in place of the products $mc$, which gives
$$
t_e=\frac{41.8\cdot60+10\cdot20}{41.8+10}=\frac{2708}{51.8}\approx52 .
$$
The thermometer therefore reads about $52\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The reading is lower than the initial temperature of the water because the heat capacity of the thermometer is not negligible compared with that of the sample.
