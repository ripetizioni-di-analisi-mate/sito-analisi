A sample of ice, initially at $-20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, receives energy at standard atmospheric pressure. The graph shows its temperature as a function of the heat absorbed. What happens to the sample along the horizontal segment?

::grafico{curve="t,((-20+t*20/4.2)-abs(-20+t*20/4.2))/2+((t-37.6)*20/8.36+abs((t-37.6)*20/8.36))/2,0,45.96" legenda=0 punti="4.2,0,pieno;37.6,0,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=50 ymin=-25 ymax=25 nomi="Q,t" titolo="Temperature $t$ of the sample, in degrees Celsius, as a function of the heat absorbed $Q$, in kilojoules"}

- The ice melts and the temperature stays constant at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- The sample stops absorbing energy
- The liquid water warms slowly
- The sample boils and turns into vapor

## Explanation

Along the horizontal segment $Q$ increases, so the sample keeps absorbing heat, while the temperature stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. When a pure substance melts at constant pressure, its temperature stays at the melting point as long as both ice and water are present. Therefore, the horizontal segment corresponds to **melting**. The first sloped segment is the warming of the ice, and the last one is the warming of the liquid water.
