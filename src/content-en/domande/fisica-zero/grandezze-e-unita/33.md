The capacity of a jerrycan is given by the expression $V=2.5\,\mathrm{L}$. What do the letter $V$ and the symbol $\mathrm{L}$ indicate?

- $V$ indicates the quantity, namely the volume, and $\mathrm{L}$ indicates the unit of measurement, namely the liter
- $V$ indicates the unit of measurement and $\mathrm{L}$ indicates the quantity
- $V$ indicates the numerical value and $\mathrm{L}$ indicates the quantity
- $V$ and $\mathrm{L}$ indicate two different units of measurement

## Explanation

In an expression such as $V=2.5\,\mathrm{L}$, the letter to the left of the equals sign indicates the quantity, the number is the numerical value, and the symbol written after the number is the unit of measurement. Here $V$ indicates the internal volume of the jerrycan and $2.5$ is its numerical value. The symbol $\mathrm{L}$ stands for the liter, a unit of volume. Therefore, $V$ indicates **the quantity** and $\mathrm{L}$ indicates **the unit of measurement**.
