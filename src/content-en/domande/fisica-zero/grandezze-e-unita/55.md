A square vegetable garden is enlarged by doubling the length of its sides. What happens to its area?

- It becomes two times larger
- It becomes eight times larger
- It becomes four times larger
- It stays the same

## Explanation

The area of a square with side $\ell$ is $\ell\cdot\ell$. If the side becomes $2\ell$, the area becomes $2\ell\cdot2\ell=4\,\ell\cdot\ell$. The area therefore **becomes four times larger**. For example, a garden with $3\,\mathrm{m}$ sides has an area of $9\,\mathrm{m^2}$, while with $6\,\mathrm{m}$ sides it has an area of $36\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
