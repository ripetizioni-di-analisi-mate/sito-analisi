A graduated cylinder contains $50\,\mathrm{mL}$ of water. A metal key with a mass of $48\,\mathrm{g}$ is completely submerged, and the level rises to $56\,\mathrm{mL}$. What is the density of the key?

- $0.96\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac{6}{7}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $0.125\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The volume of the key is the increase in the reading, that is, $V=(56-50)\,\mathrm{mL}=6\,\mathrm{mL}=6\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Density is the ratio of mass to volume. Therefore, $\rho=\dfrac{48\,\mathrm{g}}{6\,\mathrm{cm^3}}=8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. The final reading also includes the water, so the mass is divided by the increase in the reading.
