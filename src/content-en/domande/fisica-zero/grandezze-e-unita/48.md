An empty plastic water bottle is closed with its cap, so that only air remains inside. How does the average density of the closed bottle compare with the density of the plastic?

- Greater than the density of the plastic
- Equal to the density of the plastic
- Equal to the density of air
- Less than the density of the plastic

## Explanation

The average density is the ratio of the mass to the volume of the whole object, $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$. The mass of the closed bottle is that of the plastic plus that of the air inside, which is very small. The volume occupied by the bottle, however, also includes the space inside, so it is much greater than the volume of the plastic alone. Nearly the same mass divided by a much greater volume gives a smaller ratio. Therefore, the average density of the closed bottle is **less** than that of the plastic, and it remains greater than that of air, because the mass of the plastic is added to the mass of the air.
