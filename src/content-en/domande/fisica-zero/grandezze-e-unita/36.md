A marathon runner finishes the race in $10\,000\,\mathrm{s}$. What is this time in hours, minutes, and seconds?

- $2\,\mathrm{h}$, $77\,\mathrm{min}$, and $0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{h}$, $46\,\mathrm{min}$, and $40\,\mathrm{s}$
- $166\,\mathrm{h}$, $40\,\mathrm{min}$, and $0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1\,\mathrm{h}$, $40\,\mathrm{min}$, and $0\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

One hour contains $3600\,\mathrm{s}$, and $2\cdot3600\,\mathrm{s}=7200\,\mathrm{s}$ is the largest whole number of hours that does not exceed $10\,000\,\mathrm{s}$. This leaves $10\,000\,\mathrm{s}-7200\,\mathrm{s}=2800\,\mathrm{s}$. Since one minute contains $60\,\mathrm{s}$ and $46\cdot60\,\mathrm{s}=2760\,\mathrm{s}$, the $2800\,\mathrm{s}$ correspond to $46\,\mathrm{min}$ and $40\,\mathrm{s}$. Therefore, the runner's time is $2\,\mathrm{h}$, $46\,\mathrm{min}$, and $40\,\mathrm{s}$.
