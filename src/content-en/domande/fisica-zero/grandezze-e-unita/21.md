The package insert of a medicine gives a dose of $500\,\mathrm{mg}$. What does the first letter $\mathrm{m}$ in the symbol $\mathrm{mg}$ indicate?

- The prefix milli, which means one thousandth
- The meter, a unit of length
- The prefix that means one thousand
- The mass of the medicine

## Explanation

The symbol $\mathrm{m}$ on its own stands for the meter, while before another unit symbol it stands for the prefix milli. In $\mathrm{mg}$ the letter $\mathrm{m}$ comes before the symbol for the gram, so it is **the prefix milli** and equals $0.001$. Therefore, $500\,\mathrm{mg}$ is $500$ thousandths of a gram, or $0.5\,\mathrm{g}$. The prefix that means one thousand is kilo, with symbol $\mathrm{k}$.
