In a hardware store catalog, cork panels, assumed to be uniform, are described as having a density of $240\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Which statement is true?

- A block of cork with a mass of $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ has a volume of $240\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- Every cork panel has a mass of $240\,\mathrm{kg}$
- A block of cork with a volume of $240\,\mathrm{m^3}$ has a mass of $1\,\mathrm{kg}$
- A block of cork with a volume of $1\,\mathrm{m^3}$ has a mass of $240\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Density is the ratio $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$, so it gives the mass corresponding to one unit of volume. The unit $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ refers the mass to one cubic meter, so a block of cork with a volume of $1\,\mathrm{m^3}$ has **a mass of $240\,\mathrm{kg}$**. A block with a mass of $1\,\mathrm{kg}$, on the other hand, has a volume of $V=\dfrac{m}{\rho}=\dfrac{1}{240}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, much less than one cubic meter. The mass of a single panel depends on its volume, so the density alone does not determine it.
