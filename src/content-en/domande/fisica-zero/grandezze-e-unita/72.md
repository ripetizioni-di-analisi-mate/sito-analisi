To find a liquid's density you divide the mass of the full beaker by the volume of the liquid alone. How does the result compare with the true density?

- It is exact if grams and millilitres are converted to SI units.
- It is too high because the numerator includes the beaker.
- It is exact because the beaker mass would automatically cancel in the ratio.
- It is too low because the tare should be added to the mass reading.

## Explanation

The correct numerator is the liquid's mass, found by subtracting the tare. Adding the positive mass of the beaker with the same denominator increases the quotient.
