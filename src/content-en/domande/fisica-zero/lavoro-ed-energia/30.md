Over a stretch of track, the net work done by the forces on a moving sled is $-50\,\mathrm{J}$. What happens to the sled's velocity over that stretch?

- Its magnitude increases
- Its magnitude stays the same
- The sled necessarily reverses its direction of motion
- Its magnitude decreases

## Explanation

By the **work–energy theorem**, $K_f-K_i=W_{\mathrm{tot}}=-50\,\mathrm{J}$. So the final kinetic energy is $50\,\mathrm{J}$ less than the initial kinetic energy. Since, for the same mass, a smaller kinetic energy corresponds to a smaller speed, **the magnitude of the velocity decreases**. The theorem concerns the speed and says nothing about the direction of motion.
