A scooter with its rider, of total mass $150\,\mathrm{kg}$, travels at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes to a stop at a traffic light. What is the net work done by the forces on the scooter during braking?

- $10\,800\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-21\,600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1800\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-10\,800\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The initial kinetic energy is
$$
K_i=\frac{1}{2}\cdot150\cdot12^2\,\mathrm{J}=10\,800\,\mathrm{J},
$$
while the final kinetic energy is $K_f=0$, because the scooter stops. By the **work–energy theorem**, $W_{\mathrm{tot}}=K_f-K_i=-10\,800\,\mathrm{J}$. The negative sign indicates that the kinetic energy decreases. This calculation does not require knowing how long the braking takes.
