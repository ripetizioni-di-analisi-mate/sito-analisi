A cyclist reaches the top of a hump at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and coasts without pedaling down to its base, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ lower. Neglecting friction and air resistance, and with $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the cyclist's speed at the base of the hump?

- $10.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $55.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Only gravity does work, so $K_i+U_i=K_f+U_f$. With the zero level at the base of the hump, $\dfrac{1}{2}mv_i^2+mgh=\dfrac{1}{2}mv_f^2$. The mass cancels, and multiplying by $2$ gives
$$
v_f=\sqrt{v_i^2+2gh}=\sqrt{16+2\cdot9.81\cdot2}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx7.43\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $10.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$ would come from adding the speeds instead of the energies.
