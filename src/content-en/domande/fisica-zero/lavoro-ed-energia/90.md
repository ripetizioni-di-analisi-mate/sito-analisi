A light-bulb package reads $60\,\mathrm{W}$, while a physics book shows the formula $W=Fs$. What does the letter W denote in the two cases?

- In both cases it denotes work
- On the package it is the symbol for the watt, in the formula it denotes work
- In both cases it is the symbol for the watt
- On the package it denotes work, in the formula it is the symbol for the watt

## Explanation

The symbol $\mathrm{W}$ written after a number denotes a unit, so $60\,\mathrm{W}$ means sixty watts, that is, a power. In the formula $W=Fs$, by contrast, the letter $W$ is a quantity, the product of a force and a displacement, that is, **work**. Therefore, on the package W is the symbol for the watt, and in the formula it denotes work, which is measured in joules.
