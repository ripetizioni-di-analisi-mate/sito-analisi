On an ice rink, a hockey puck slides $15\,\mathrm{m}$ in a straight line, slowed by a constant kinetic friction force of $0.2\,\mathrm{N}$. How much work does friction do on the puck?

- $3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-75\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-0.013\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Kinetic friction opposes sliding, so its work over a straight path of length $s$ is $W_a=-fs$. With $f=0.2\,\mathrm{N}$ and $s=15\,\mathrm{m}$ we get
$$
W_a=-0.2\cdot15\,\mathrm{J}=-3\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The negative sign indicates that friction removes mechanical energy from the puck.
