A waiter crosses the dining room horizontally at constant speed, through $10\,\mathrm{m}$, holding a tray of weight $15\,\mathrm{N}$ on the palm of one hand. The hand exerts an upward vertical force of $15\,\mathrm{N}$ on the tray. How much work does this force do during the trip?

- $150\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-150\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The tray moves horizontally, while the force of the hand is vertical. A force perpendicular to the displacement does **zero work**. Therefore, the work done by the hand on the tray is $0\,\mathrm{J}$, even though the force is $15\,\mathrm{N}$ and the trip is $10\,\mathrm{m}$ long. The product $15\cdot10=150$ would be the work of a force parallel to the displacement.
