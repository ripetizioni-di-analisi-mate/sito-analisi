A cyclist rides up a hill at constant speed. What can be said about the net work done by the forces on the cyclist and bicycle along the climb?

- It is zero
- It is positive and equal to the work done by the cyclist's muscles
- It is negative and equal to the work done by gravity
- It is positive, because the bicycle goes up

## Explanation

The speed is constant, so the final kinetic energy equals the initial kinetic energy. By the **work–energy theorem**, $W_{\mathrm{tot}}=K_f-K_i=0$. Therefore, the net work is **zero**. The positive work of the pedaling and the negative work of gravity and of the resistive forces are each nonzero, but their sum is zero.
