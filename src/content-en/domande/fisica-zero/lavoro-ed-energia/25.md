A girl of mass $30\,\mathrm{kg}$, scooter included, goes from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by pushing off with one foot. What is the net work done by the forces on the girl and scooter?

- $60\,\mathrm{J}$
- $180\,\mathrm{J}$
- $240\,\mathrm{J}$
- $360\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

By the **work–energy theorem**, the net work equals the change in kinetic energy, $W_{\mathrm{tot}}=K_f-K_i$. The initial energy is $K_i=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot30\cdot2^2\,\mathrm{J}=60\,\mathrm{J}$ and the final energy is $K_f=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot30\cdot4^2\,\mathrm{J}=240\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore,
$$
W_{\mathrm{tot}}=240\,\mathrm{J}-60\,\mathrm{J}=180\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $60\,\mathrm{J}$ would come from squaring the difference of the speeds instead of taking the difference of the squares.
