A passenger of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ sits in a train traveling at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the tracks. What is the passenger's kinetic energy in the frame of reference of the train?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot70\cdot30^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- It is negative
- It is zero
- It equals the value measured by an observer at rest beside the tracks

## Explanation

In the frame of reference of the train, the seated passenger is at rest, so the speed is $v=0$. The kinetic energy $K=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ with $v=0$ is zero, so in the train's frame the kinetic energy is **zero**. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot70\cdot30^2\,\mathrm{J}$ is the kinetic energy in the frame of the tracks, where the passenger moves at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
