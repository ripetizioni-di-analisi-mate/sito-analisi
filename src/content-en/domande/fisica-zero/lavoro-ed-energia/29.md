A goalkeeper catches a ball of mass $0.45\,\mathrm{kg}$ arriving at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, pulling the hands back $0.3\,\mathrm{m}$ while stopping it. Assuming the force of the hands on the ball is constant and neglecting the other forces, what is its magnitude?

- $600\,\mathrm{N}$
- $30\,\mathrm{N}$
- $300\,\mathrm{N}$
- $27\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The initial kinetic energy of the ball is $K_i=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot0.45\cdot20^2\,\mathrm{J}=90\,\mathrm{J}$, and the final kinetic energy is zero. By the **work–energy theorem**, the work done by the force of the hands is $W=-90\,\mathrm{J}$. The force is opposite to the displacement, so $W=-Fs$. Therefore,
$$
F=\frac{90\,\mathrm{J}}{0.3\,\mathrm{m}}=300\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
