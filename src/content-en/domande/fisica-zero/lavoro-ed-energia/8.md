While inflating a tire, a cyclist pushes the pump plunger with a constant force of $80\,\mathrm{N}$ along the direction of motion and moves it down $30\,\mathrm{cm}$. How much work does the force do in one stroke?

- $2400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $267\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $24\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

To get the work in joules, the force must be in newtons and the displacement in meters. Since $1\,\mathrm{cm}=0.01\,\mathrm{m}$, the displacement is $s=0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. The force points in the direction of the displacement, so
$$
W=Fs=80\,\mathrm{N}\cdot0.30\,\mathrm{m}=24\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $2400$ would come from multiplying by $30$ without converting the centimeters.
