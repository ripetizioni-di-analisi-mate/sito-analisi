At a construction site, a truck of mass $10\,000\,\mathrm{kg}$ creeps along at $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, while a motorcycle of mass $200\,\mathrm{kg}$, rider included, passes at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which of the two has more kinetic energy?

- The motorcycle
- The truck
- They have the same kinetic energy
- It cannot be determined without knowing the height

## Explanation

We apply $K=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ to both. For the truck, $K=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot10\,000\cdot1^2\,\mathrm{J}=5000\,\mathrm{J}$. For the motorcycle, $K=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot200\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{J}=10\,000\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore, **the motorcycle** has more kinetic energy, because its speed, ten times larger, enters the formula squared.
