A skateboarder starts from rest at the rim of a half-pipe, $3\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. Neglecting friction and air resistance, and with $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the skateboarder's speed at $1\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom on the way down?

- $7.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $39.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Without friction, the normal force from the ramp is perpendicular to the motion and does zero work, so mechanical energy is conserved. Between the rim and the point at $1\,\mathrm{m}$, the skateboarder descends $2\,\mathrm{m}$, so the potential energy lost, $mg\cdot2\,\mathrm{m}$, becomes kinetic energy. From $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2=mg\cdot2\,\mathrm{m}$ we get
$$
v=\sqrt{2\cdot9.81\cdot2}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx6.26\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $7.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the speed at the bottom, after the full $3\,\mathrm{m}$ descent.
