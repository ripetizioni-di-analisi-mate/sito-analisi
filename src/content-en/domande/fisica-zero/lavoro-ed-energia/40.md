A hiker of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$, backpack included, climbs a mountain trail with an elevation gain of $300\,\mathrm{m}$. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, how much work does gravity do during the climb?

- $-2.06\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.06\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-2.1\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.03\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work done by gravity is $W_P=mg(h_i-h_f)$ and depends only on the change in height. On the way up, the final height exceeds the initial one by $300\,\mathrm{m}$, so $h_i-h_f=-300\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore,
$$
W_P=70\cdot9.81\cdot(-300)\,\mathrm{J}=-206\,010\,\mathrm{J}\approx-2.06\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The sign is negative, because the weight points downward while the hiker goes up.
