A brake exerts a constant force opposing a trolley's velocity. The trolley slows on a straight line until it stops. How does the instantaneous power of the braking force change, taking energy supplied to the motion as positive?

- It is negative while moving and its magnitude falls to zero
- It is negative and keeps the same magnitude even at rest
- It is positive and increases as the trolley slows
- It is zero at all times because the force is constant

## Explanation

While the trolley moves, force and velocity point in opposite directions, so $P=-Fv$, where $F$ and $v$ are magnitudes. Force magnitude is fixed while speed falls to zero, so power magnitude falls as well.
