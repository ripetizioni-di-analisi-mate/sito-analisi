A car brakes to a stop on a level road, and the brakes and tires warm up. Which quantity is conserved during braking, considering the car, the brakes, and the road?

- The total energy, including the increase in internal energy of the brakes, tires, and road
- The mechanical energy of the car
- The kinetic energy of the car
- No form of energy, because the kinetic energy is destroyed

## Explanation

The road is level, so the car's potential energy does not change, while its kinetic energy decreases to zero. Therefore, the car's mechanical energy is not conserved. Friction forces transfer that energy to the brakes, tires, and road, increasing their internal energy. When this transformation is included, **the total energy** is conserved.
