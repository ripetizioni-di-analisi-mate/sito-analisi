A car's gasoline engine has an efficiency of $25\%$. On a short trip, the fuel burned supplies $200\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of energy to it. How much useful mechanical energy does the engine produce?

- $800\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $150\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $50\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

From the definition $\eta=\dfrac{E_{\mathrm{utile}}}{E_{\mathrm{ass}}}$, multiplying by $E_{\mathrm{ass}}$ gives $E_{\mathrm{utile}}=\eta\,E_{\mathrm{ass}}$. An efficiency of $25\%$ corresponds to $\eta=0.25$. Therefore,
$$
E_{\mathrm{utile}}=0.25\cdot200\,\mathrm{kJ}=50\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
The value $150\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is the energy transferred in non-useful forms, mostly as heat.
