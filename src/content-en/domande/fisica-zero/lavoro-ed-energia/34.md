A motorized inflatable boat of mass $500\,\mathrm{kg}$, passengers included, speeds up from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Over the same stretch, water and air resistance do a total work of $-16\,\mathrm{kJ}$, while gravity and the other vertical forces do zero work. How much work does the thrust of the motor do?

- $40\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $24\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $32\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

By the **work–energy theorem**, the net work equals the change in kinetic energy, which is
$$
K_f-K_i=\frac{1}{2}\cdot500\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{J}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot500\cdot2^2\,\mathrm{J}=25\,000\,\mathrm{J}-1000\,\mathrm{J}=24\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
The net work is the sum of the work done by the thrust and the work done by the resistive forces, so $W_{\mathrm{tot}}=W_{\mathrm{thrust}}-16\,\mathrm{kJ}$. Since $W_{\mathrm{tot}}=24\,\mathrm{kJ}$, the thrust of the motor does $W_{\mathrm{thrust}}=24\,\mathrm{kJ}+16\,\mathrm{kJ}=40\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The value $24\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is the net work and would come from ignoring the resistive forces.
