A bead modelled as a particle slides up a straight frictionless track inclined at 30 degrees to the horizontal. It leaves the foot at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How far along the track does it travel before stopping? Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $\sin(30^\circ)=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

- $3.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At the stopping point the height gain is $\dfrac{4^2}{2\cdot10}=0.80$ metres. The height gain is half the distance along the track, so the distance is $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$.
