In a pinball launcher, an ideal spring with constant $400\,\mathrm{N/m}$, compressed by $5\,\mathrm{cm}$, is released and pushes a $0.1\,\mathrm{kg}$ ball along a smooth level stretch. At what speed does the ball leave when the spring returns to its natural length?

- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.24\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.47\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.16\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The elastic energy of the compressed spring is $U_e=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot400\cdot0.05^2\,\mathrm{J}=0.5\,\mathrm{J}$. The stretch is level and smooth, so only the spring force does work and mechanical energy is conserved. Therefore, when the spring returns to its natural length, all the elastic energy has become kinetic energy, $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot0.1\cdot v^2=0.5\,\mathrm{J}$. We get
$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot0.5}{0.1}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=\sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx3.16\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
