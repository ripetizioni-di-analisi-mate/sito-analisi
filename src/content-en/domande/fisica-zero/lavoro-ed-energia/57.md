The return spring of a garden gate, ideal and with constant $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, is already stretched by $0.1\,\mathrm{m}$ when the gate is closed. When the gate is opened slowly, the spring stretches to $0.3\,\mathrm{m}$. How much work does this opening require, neglecting all dissipation?

- $4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $9\,\mathrm{J}$
- $16\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

With slow deformation and no dissipation, the work goes into increasing the spring's elastic energy. At the start $U_e=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot200\cdot0.1^2\,\mathrm{J}=1\,\mathrm{J}$, and at the end $U_e=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot200\cdot0.3^2\,\mathrm{J}=9\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore, the work required is
$$
W=9\,\mathrm{J}-1\,\mathrm{J}=8\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $4\,\mathrm{J}$ would come from using the difference of the extensions, $0.2\,\mathrm{m}$, instead of the difference of the energies.
