On a straight country road, a tractor tows a trailer $1.5\,\mathrm{km}$, exerting a constant force of $2000\,\mathrm{N}$ in the direction of motion. How much work does the towing force do?

- $3.0\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.3\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The displacement must be expressed in meters. Since $1\,\mathrm{km}=1000\,\mathrm{m}$, we have $s=1500\,\mathrm{m}$. The towing force points in the direction of the displacement, so
$$
W=Fs=2000\,\mathrm{N}\cdot1500\,\mathrm{m}=3\,000\,000\,\mathrm{J}=3.0\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $3.0\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ would come from leaving the distance in kilometers.
