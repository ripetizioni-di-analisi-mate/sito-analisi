The shock-absorber spring of a mountain bike, treated as an ideal spring, has spring constant $20\,000\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and is compressed by $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ during a jump. How much elastic potential energy does it store?

- $18\,\mathrm{J}$
- $300\,\mathrm{J}$
- $90\,000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $9\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The compression must be expressed in meters, so $x=0.03\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x^2=0.0009\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The elastic potential energy is $U_e=\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$, so
$$
U_e=\frac{1}{2}\cdot20\,000\cdot0.0009\,\mathrm{J}=9\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $90\,000\,\mathrm{J}$ would come from using $x=3$ without converting the centimeters.
