A sprinter of mass $80\,\mathrm{kg}$ runs at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and over the next few meters the net work done by the forces on him is $1560\,\mathrm{J}$. What speed does he reach?

- $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $11.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $64\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The initial kinetic energy is $K_i=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot80\cdot5^2\,\mathrm{J}=1000\,\mathrm{J}$. By the **work–energy theorem**, $K_f=K_i+W_{\mathrm{tot}}=2560\,\mathrm{J}$. From $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot80\cdot v_f^2=2560$ we get $v_f^2=64\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. Therefore, $v_f=8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $6.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ would come from ignoring the initial kinetic energy.
