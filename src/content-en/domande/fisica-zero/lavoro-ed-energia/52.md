An ideal spring is first stretched by $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and then, starting again from its natural length, compressed by $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. How do the elastic potential energies in the two configurations compare?

- They are equal
- It is larger when the spring is stretched
- It is larger when the spring is compressed
- It is positive when stretched and negative when compressed

## Explanation

In the formula $U_e=\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$, the quantity $x$ is the magnitude of the extension or compression from the natural length. In both configurations $x=0.04\,\mathrm{m}$, and the constant $k$ is the same. Therefore, the two elastic energies are **equal**. Moreover, $x^2$ is never negative, so $U_e$ is not negative for the compressed spring either.
