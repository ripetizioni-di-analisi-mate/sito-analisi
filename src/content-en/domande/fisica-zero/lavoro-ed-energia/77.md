A $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ skier starts from rest and skis down a slope with a drop in height of $20\,\mathrm{m}$, reaching the bottom at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Air resistance is negligible and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How much work does friction from the snow do on the skier during the descent?

- $5.86\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-13.7\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-7.88\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-5.86\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

With the zero level at the bottom of the slope, the initial mechanical energy is $E_i=70\cdot9.81\cdot20\,\mathrm{J}=13\,734\,\mathrm{J}$. At the bottom the energy is all kinetic, $E_f=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot70\cdot15^2\,\mathrm{J}=7875\,\mathrm{J}$. The normal force from the slope is perpendicular to the motion and does zero work, so friction is the only force that does work besides gravity. Therefore, the work done by friction equals the change in mechanical energy,
$$
W_a=E_f-E_i=7875\,\mathrm{J}-13\,734\,\mathrm{J}=-5859\,\mathrm{J}\approx-5.86\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
