On smooth snow, a child pulls a sled forward with a horizontal force of $30\,\mathrm{N}$, while his sister, behind it, holds it back with a horizontal force of $30\,\mathrm{N}$ directed backward. The sled moves forward $20\,\mathrm{m}$. Which statement about the work done by these two forces is true?

- Both forces do $600\,\mathrm{J}$
- Both forces do zero work, because they balance
- The sum of the two amounts of work is $1200\,\mathrm{J}$
- The forward force does $600\,\mathrm{J}$, the backward force $-600\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Each force does its own work, computed from its own direction relative to the displacement. The child's force points in the direction of the displacement, so it does $30\cdot20=600\,\mathrm{J}$. The sister's force is opposite to the displacement, so it does $-600\,\mathrm{J}$. Each amount of work is nonzero, and their sum is zero.
