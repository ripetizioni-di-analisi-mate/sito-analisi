The graph shows the force needed to slowly stretch an elastic tow rope, treated as an ideal spring, as a function of the extension. How much work is needed to stretch it by $3\,\mathrm{m}$ starting from its natural length?

::grafico{f="10*x" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=50 nomi="x,F" punti="3,30" segmenti="3,0,3,30;0,30,3,30" titolo="Elastic rope in the ideal model, with $x$ in meters and $F$ in newtons."}

- $90\,\mathrm{J}$
- $30\,\mathrm{J}$
- $15\,\mathrm{J}$
- $45\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

From the graph, the force increases linearly from $0$ to $30\,\mathrm{N}$ as the extension goes from $0$ to $3\,\mathrm{m}$. The work is the area under the force–extension graph, which here is a triangle. Therefore,
$$
W=\frac{3\cdot30}{2}\,\mathrm{J}=45\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The value $90\,\mathrm{J}$ would come from using the final force over the entire extension, whereas the force increases gradually.
