Two friends of equal mass climb the same flight of stairs at constant speed: the first takes $10\,\mathrm{s}$, the second $20\,\mathrm{s}$. Which statement about the work done against gravity and the average power of the two is true?

- The first does more work, because he climbs faster
- They deliver the same average power, because the change in height is the same
- They do the same work, but the first delivers a greater average power
- The second delivers a greater average power, because he takes longer

## Explanation

The work needed to climb is $mgh$, where $h$ is the height of the flight of stairs. The two friends have the same mass and climb the same height, so they do **the same work**. The average power is $P_m=\dfrac{W}{\Delta t}$, so for the same work it is greater for the one who takes less time. Consequently, the first friend, who climbs in $10\,\mathrm{s}$, delivers a greater average power.
