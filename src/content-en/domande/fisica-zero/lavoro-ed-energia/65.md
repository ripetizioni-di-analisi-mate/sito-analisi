A sled slides down a snowy track. What condition must be satisfied for its mechanical energy to be conserved during the descent?

- The track must be straight
- Friction from the snow and air resistance must be negligible
- The sled's speed must stay constant
- The sled's mass must be small

## Explanation

Mechanical energy is conserved when only gravity and ideal spring forces do work, and any other forces do zero work. The sled is also acted on by friction from the snow and by air resistance, which oppose the motion and do negative work. Therefore, conservation requires **these forces to be negligible**. The shape of the track and the mass do not enter the condition, and the speed of a sled sliding down without friction increases.
