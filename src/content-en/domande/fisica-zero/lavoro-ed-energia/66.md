An ideal toy spring with constant $500\,\mathrm{N/m}$, compressed by $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, launches a $0.05\,\mathrm{kg}$ ball straight up. Air resistance and the change in height while the spring extends are neglected, and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How high does the ball rise?

- $0.41\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The elastic energy of the spring is $U_e=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot500\cdot0.02^2\,\mathrm{J}=0.1\,\mathrm{J}$. Only the spring force and gravity do work, so mechanical energy is conserved. At the highest point the ball is at rest, so all the elastic energy has become gravitational potential energy, $mgh=0.1\,\mathrm{J}$. We get
$$
h=\frac{0.1}{0.05\cdot9.81}\,\mathrm{m}\approx0.20\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
