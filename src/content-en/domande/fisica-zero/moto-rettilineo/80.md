In a field, a fox at position $x=0\,\mathrm{m}$ spots a hare at $x=20\,\mathrm{m}$. At the same instant the fox starts running at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the hare flees at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, in the same direction and along the same line. How long does it take the fox to catch the hare?

- $2\,\mathrm{s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\dfrac{10}{3}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The position equations are $x_F=6t$ for the fox and $x_H=20+4t$ for the hare, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. The fox catches the hare when the two positions coincide, that is, when $6t=20+4t$. Therefore $2t=20$ and $t=10\,\mathrm{s}$. At that instant both are at $x=60\,\mathrm{m}$.
