The position-time graph describes the motion of a camera sliding on a straight rail along the sidelines. During which interval does the camera have the greatest average speed, that is, cover the most meters per second?

::grafico{linee="0,0 2,6 6,10 8,2" punti="0,0;2,6;6,10;8,2" xmin=0 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=11 nomi="t,x" legenda=0 titolo="Position $x$ of the camera, in meters, as a function of time $t$, in seconds."}

- Between $0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$
- Between $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $6\,\mathrm{s}$
- The speed is the same in all three intervals
- Between $6\,\mathrm{s}$ and $8\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

On each stretch, the velocity is the change in position divided by the time interval. Between $0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$ we get $\dfrac{6\,\mathrm{m}}{2\,\mathrm{s}}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, between $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $6\,\mathrm{s}$ we get $\dfrac{4\,\mathrm{m}}{4\,\mathrm{s}}=1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and between $6\,\mathrm{s}$ and $8\,\mathrm{s}$ we get $\dfrac{-8\,\mathrm{m}}{2\,\mathrm{s}}=-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. On each stretch the camera does not change direction, so the average speed is the absolute value of the velocity. Therefore the greatest average speed, $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, occurs between $6\,\mathrm{s}$ and $8\,\mathrm{s}$, and the negative sign only indicates that the camera moves back.
