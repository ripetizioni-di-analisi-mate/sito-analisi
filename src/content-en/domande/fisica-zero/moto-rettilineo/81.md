A boy sets off from home on a bicycle at a constant speed of $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. His mother leaves the same house $2\,\mathrm{min}$ later, on the same straight road and in the same direction, at a constant speed of $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How many seconds after the boy's departure does his mother catch up with him?

- $180\,\mathrm{s}$
- $300\,\mathrm{s}$
- $120\,\mathrm{s}$
- $450\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

We measure time from the boy's departure, so the mother leaves at the instant $t_0=120\,\mathrm{s}$. The position equations are $x_B=3t$ and, for $t\geq120\,\mathrm{s}$, $x_M=5(t-120)$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. The mother catches up with her son when $3t=5(t-120)$, that is, $3t=5t-600$. Therefore $2t=600$ and $t=300\,\mathrm{s}$, an instant after the mother's departure.
