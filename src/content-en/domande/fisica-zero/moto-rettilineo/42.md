On a straight track, the origin is at the starting line and the positive direction points toward the finish. An athlete starts at $x=0\,\mathrm{m}$, runs to $x=60\,\mathrm{m}$, and runs back to $x=20\,\mathrm{m}$. Which statement about the average velocity and the average speed for the whole run is correct?

- The average velocity is negative because the athlete turned back
- They are equal because the athlete always ran along the same line
- Both are positive and the average speed is greater
- Both are positive and the average velocity is greater

## Explanation

The displacement is $\Delta x=20\,\mathrm{m}-0\,\mathrm{m}=20\,\mathrm{m}$, which is positive, so the average velocity is also positive. The distance traveled is $60\,\mathrm{m}+40\,\mathrm{m}=100\,\mathrm{m}$. Both quantities are divided by the same time interval, so the average speed exceeds the average velocity in the same ratio as $100\,\mathrm{m}$ to $20\,\mathrm{m}$. The sign of the average velocity depends only on the overall displacement, which here is positive despite the return.
