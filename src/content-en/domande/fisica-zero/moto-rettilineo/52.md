A ball rolls across a smooth floor toward a wall at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, bounces, and rolls back along the same line, still at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Is the motion as a whole, there and back, uniform motion in a straight line?

- No, because the velocity changes direction and therefore does not stay constant
- Yes, because the ball always covers $2\,\mathrm{m}$ every second
- Yes, because the trajectory lies on a straight line
- No, because the trajectory does not lie on a straight line

## Explanation

In **uniform motion in a straight line** the signed velocity stays constant, so the object undergoes equal displacements in equal times, all in the same direction. With the positive direction toward the wall, the velocity of the ball is $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on the way there and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on the way back. Therefore the velocity changes and the motion as a whole is not uniform, even though the trajectory is straight. The $2\,\mathrm{m}$ covered every second only indicates that the magnitude of the velocity stays the same. The trip there and the trip back, taken separately, are instead two uniform motions.
