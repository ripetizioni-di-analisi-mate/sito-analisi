A cyclist rides up a straight road to a mountain hut at a constant speed of $10\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and comes back down the same road at $40\,\mathrm{km/h}$. What is the cyclist's average speed for the whole round trip?

- $25\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $16\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $0\,\mathrm{km/h}$

## Explanation

Let $d$ be the length of the road. The ride up takes $\dfrac{d}{10\,\mathrm{km/h}}$ and the ride down takes $\dfrac{d}{40\,\mathrm{km/h}}$, so the total time is
$$
\Delta t=\frac{d}{10\,\mathrm{km/h}}+\frac{d}{40\,\mathrm{km/h}}=\frac{5d}{40\,\mathrm{km/h}}=\frac{d}{8\,\mathrm{km/h}}.
$$
The distance traveled is $2d$, so $u_m=\dfrac{2d}{\Delta t}=2\cdot8\,\mathrm{km/h}=16\,\mathrm{km/h}$. The cyclist spends more time going uphill, so the average speed is less than the arithmetic mean $25\,\mathrm{km/h}$.
