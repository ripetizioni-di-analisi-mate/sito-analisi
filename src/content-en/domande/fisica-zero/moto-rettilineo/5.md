A runner starts at the $15\,\mathrm{m}$ mark and moves continuously along a straight line. The average velocity over the first $6\,\mathrm{s}$ is $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What can you guarantee about crossing the zero mark?

- The first crossing happens exactly $3.75\,\mathrm{s}$ after the start.
- The runner reaches zero only at the end of the six seconds.
- The runner crosses it at least once during the six seconds, but the first crossing time cannot be determined.
- The runner could stay on the positive side throughout the six seconds.

## Explanation

The displacement is $(-4)\cdot6=-24$ metres, so the runner ends at $15-24=-9$ metres. Moving continuously from a positive to a negative position requires crossing zero. The average velocity gives the final position but leaves the motion at individual instants undetermined.
