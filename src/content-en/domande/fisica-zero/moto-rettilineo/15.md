An elevator goes up from the ground floor to the fifth floor. On the way up it stops at the third floor to let someone in, and then continues upward. Compared with a trip with no stops, what changes?

- The distance traveled increases and the displacement stays the same
- Neither the displacement nor the distance traveled changes
- The displacement decreases and the distance traveled stays the same
- Both the displacement and the distance traveled increase

## Explanation

The displacement depends only on the initial and final positions, which are the ground floor and the fifth floor in both cases, so it stays the same. During the stop the elevator does not move, so the stop adds no stretch to the path. The elevator always moves upward, so the distance traveled equals the magnitude of the displacement even with the stop. The stop only makes the trip last longer.
