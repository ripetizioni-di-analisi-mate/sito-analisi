A child goes to school along a straight road. The child rides a bike $600\,\mathrm{m}$ in $100\,\mathrm{s}$, then locks the bike and walks another $300\,\mathrm{m}$ in $200\,\mathrm{s}$ in the same direction. What is the child's average speed for the whole trip?

- $3.75\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The total distance is $600\,\mathrm{m}+300\,\mathrm{m}=900\,\mathrm{m}$ and the total time is $100\,\mathrm{s}+200\,\mathrm{s}=300\,\mathrm{s}$. The average speed is the ratio of these two quantities, so
$$
u_m=\frac{900\,\mathrm{m}}{300\,\mathrm{s}}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The arithmetic mean of the speeds on the two legs, $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, is $3.75\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and differs from the average speed, because the child walks for twice as long as he rides.
