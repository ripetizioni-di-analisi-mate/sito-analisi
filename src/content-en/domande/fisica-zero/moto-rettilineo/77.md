The point $(40\,\mathrm{s},\,300\,\mathrm{m})$ lies on the position-time graph of a bus. What can be stated with certainty?

- In the first $40\,\mathrm{s}$ the bus traveled $300\,\mathrm{m}$
- The bus travels at a constant velocity of $7.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- At the instant $40\,\mathrm{s}$ the bus is at position $300\,\mathrm{m}$
- The bus stays at rest for $40\,\mathrm{s}$ at position $300\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Each point on a position-time graph gives the position occupied at a particular instant. The given point therefore says that at the instant $40\,\mathrm{s}$ the bus is at $x=300\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance traveled in the first $40\,\mathrm{s}$ also depends on the position at the instant $0$ and on how the bus moved, which a single point does not provide. For the same reason, a single point does not allow us to calculate a velocity.
