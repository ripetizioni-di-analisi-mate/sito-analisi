A boat is $900\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pier, on the straight course that leads to the pier. With the origin at the pier and the positive direction pointing out to sea, the boat moves at a constant velocity $v=-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does it take to reach the pier?

- $300\,\mathrm{s}$
- $-300\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2700\,\mathrm{s}$
- $30\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The boat starts at $x_0=900\,\mathrm{m}$ and must reach $x=0\,\mathrm{m}$, so the displacement is $-900\,\mathrm{m}$. From the position equation we obtain the time interval
$$
t-t_0=\frac{x-x_0}{v}=\frac{-900\,\mathrm{m}}{-3\,\mathrm{m/s}}=300\,\mathrm{s}.
$$
The ratio of two negative numbers is positive, as a time interval must be.
