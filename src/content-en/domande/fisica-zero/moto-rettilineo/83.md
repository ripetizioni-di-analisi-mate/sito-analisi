On a position-time graph, the lines representing the motion of two trains intersect at the point $(6\,\mathrm{min},\,3\,\mathrm{km})$. What does this point represent?

- At the instant $6\,\mathrm{min}$ the two trains have the same velocity
- At the instant $6\,\mathrm{min}$ both trains are at position $3\,\mathrm{km}$
- After $6\,\mathrm{min}$ the two trains are $3\,\mathrm{km}$ apart
- The two tracks cross $3\,\mathrm{km}$ from the station

## Explanation

Each point on a line of a position-time graph gives the position of a train at an instant. A point common to both lines therefore indicates the same instant and the same position for both trains. Hence at the instant $6\,\mathrm{min}$ both trains are at position $3\,\mathrm{km}$, that is, they meet. The velocities correspond to the slopes of the lines, and two lines that intersect at a single point have different slopes.
