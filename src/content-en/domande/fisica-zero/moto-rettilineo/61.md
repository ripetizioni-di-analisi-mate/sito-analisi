A radar records an instantaneous speed of $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ during a $10\,\mathrm{s}$ journey. Which extra condition would justify multiplying that reading by $10\,\mathrm{s}$ to obtain total distance?

- The speed remains $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ throughout the journey
- The journey starts at the origin
- The radar measures exactly halfway through the trip
- The final position is positive

## Explanation

The instantaneous reading describes one moment. If speed is constant, it equals the average speed, and its product with duration gives distance.
