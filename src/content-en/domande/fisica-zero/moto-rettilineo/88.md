In a time trial, a cyclist starts from the origin when the race clock reads $t_0=5\,\mathrm{s}$ and from then on moves in the positive direction at a constant speed of $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which position equation describes the cyclist's motion for $t\geq5\,\mathrm{s}$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds read on the race clock?

- $x=6t-5$
- $x=6(t-5)$
- $x=6(t+5)$
- $x=6t$

## Explanation

If an object starts moving at the instant $t_0$, its time in motion is $t-t_0$. For the cyclist, $t_0=5\,\mathrm{s}$ and $x_0=0\,\mathrm{m}$, so the position equation $x=x_0+v(t-t_0)$ becomes $x=6(t-5)$. At the instant $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$ this equation gives $x=0$, consistent with the start from the origin.
