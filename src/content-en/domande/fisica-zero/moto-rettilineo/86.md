A scuba diver descends vertically at a constant speed of $0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $40\,\mathrm{s}$, then rises along the same vertical line at $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $30\,\mathrm{s}$. What total distance has the diver traveled?

- $14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $49\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $26\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The **distance traveled** is the sum of the lengths of the individual legs. On each leg the velocity is constant, so the length of the leg is the magnitude of the velocity times the time interval. On the way down the diver covers $0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot40\,\mathrm{s}=20\,\mathrm{m}$, and on the way up $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot30\,\mathrm{s}=6\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore the distance traveled is $20\,\mathrm{m}+6\,\mathrm{m}=26\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $14\,\mathrm{m}$ is instead the magnitude of the displacement, because the ascent brings the diver back up by $6\,\mathrm{m}$.
