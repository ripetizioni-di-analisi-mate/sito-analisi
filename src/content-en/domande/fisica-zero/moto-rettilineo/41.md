A traffic officer walks along a straight sidewalk: he covers $50\,\mathrm{m}$ north in $40\,\mathrm{s}$, then walks back $20\,\mathrm{m}$ south in $20\,\mathrm{s}$. Taking north as the positive direction, what is his average velocity over the whole interval?

- $\dfrac{7}{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.75\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The overall displacement is $\Delta x=50\,\mathrm{m}-20\,\mathrm{m}=30\,\mathrm{m}$ and the total time is $40\,\mathrm{s}+20\,\mathrm{s}=60\,\mathrm{s}$. Therefore
$$
v_m=\frac{30\,\mathrm{m}}{60\,\mathrm{s}}=0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $\dfrac{70\,\mathrm{m}}{60\,\mathrm{s}}=\dfrac{7}{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is instead the average speed, because it uses the distance traveled.
