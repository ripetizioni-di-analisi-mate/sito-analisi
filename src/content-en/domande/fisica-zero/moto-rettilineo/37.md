A car crosses the city along a straight boulevard and covers $3\,\mathrm{km}$ in $6\,\mathrm{min}$, with a few stops at traffic lights. Which statement is certainly true?

- The speedometer read $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$ for the whole trip
- The car never went faster than $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- A car traveling at a constant $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$ would have taken the same time
- The car covered $0.5\,\mathrm{km}$ every minute

## Explanation

Six minutes is one tenth of an hour, so the average velocity is $v_m=\dfrac{3\,\mathrm{km}}{0.1\,\mathrm{h}}=30\,\mathrm{km/h}$. A car that always travels at $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$ covers $3\,\mathrm{km}$ in exactly $0.1\,\mathrm{h}$, so it takes the same time. The average velocity describes the entire interval and does not tell how the car moved during each part. Since the car was stopped at traffic lights and its average velocity is $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$, it went faster than $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$ on some stretch.
