With a hand-operated stopwatch, the uncertainty of each time measurement is $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$. One can time a single swing of a playground swing, which lasts about $2\,\mathrm{s}$, or ten consecutive swings, which last about $20\,\mathrm{s}$. Which measurement has the smaller relative uncertainty?

- The single-swing measurement, with about $1\%$
- They have the same relative uncertainty, because the absolute uncertainty is the same
- The ten-swing measurement, with about $1\%$
- The ten-swing measurement, with about $10\%$

## Explanation

For a single swing the relative uncertainty is $\dfrac{0.2\,\mathrm{s}}{2\,\mathrm{s}}=0.1$, that is, $10\%$. For ten swings it is $\dfrac{0.2\,\mathrm{s}}{20\,\mathrm{s}}=0.01$, that is, $1\%$. The absolute uncertainty is the same, but the measured value is ten times larger. Therefore the ten-swing measurement has the **smaller relative uncertainty**.
