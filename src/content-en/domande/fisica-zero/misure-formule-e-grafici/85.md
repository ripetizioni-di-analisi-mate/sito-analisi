On a graph of monthly rainfall, the marks on the vertical axis are all the same distance apart and are labeled $0$, $10$, $20$, $50$, and $100\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is wrong with this scale?

- The vertical axis should start at $10\,\mathrm{mm}$
- The scale of the vertical axis should be the same as that of the horizontal axis
- Equal intervals on the axis do not correspond to equal numerical increments
- Rainfall cannot be plotted on the vertical axis

## Explanation

On each axis, equal intervals must correspond to equal numerical increments. Between the first three marks the increment is $10\,\mathrm{mm}$, while between the third and fourth it is $30\,\mathrm{mm}$ and between the fourth and fifth it is $50\,\mathrm{mm}$. Therefore equally spaced marks represent different increments, and the graph distorts the data. The two axes, on the other hand, may have different scales.
