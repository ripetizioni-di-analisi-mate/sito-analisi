Stacks of identical one-euro coins are weighed: a stack of $2$ coins, one of $4$, and one of $8$. Which set of masses, in the same order, is consistent with direct proportionality between mass and number of coins?

- $15\,\mathrm{g}$, $30\,\mathrm{g}$, $60\,\mathrm{g}$
- $15\,\mathrm{g}$, $25\,\mathrm{g}$, $45\,\mathrm{g}$
- $60\,\mathrm{g}$, $30\,\mathrm{g}$, $15\,\mathrm{g}$
- $15\,\mathrm{g}$, $30\,\mathrm{g}$, $45\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

In direct proportionality, the ratio of mass to number of coins stays constant. For the set $15\,\mathrm{g}$, $30\,\mathrm{g}$, $60\,\mathrm{g}$ the ratios are $\dfrac{15}{2}=\dfrac{30}{4}=\dfrac{60}{8}=7.5$, that is, $7.5\,\mathrm{g}$ per coin. In the two sets ending with $45\,\mathrm{g}$, the last ratio is $\dfrac{45}{8}\approx5.6$, which differs from the first ratio, $7.5$. In the decreasing set, the mass decreases while the number of coins increases, so the ratio is not constant.
