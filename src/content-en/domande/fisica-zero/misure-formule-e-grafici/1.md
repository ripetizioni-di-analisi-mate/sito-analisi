A food thermometer has two numbered marks, $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Between them there are four unnumbered intermediate marks, all equally spaced. What is the scale division?

- $2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $2.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $0.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The four intermediate marks and the two numbered marks bound five equal intervals. The difference between the numbered values is $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The scale division is the distance between two consecutive marks, so the difference is divided by the number of intervals:
$$
r=\frac{50-40}{5}\,^\circ\mathrm{C}=2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}.
$$
Dividing by the number of intermediate marks would give $2.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, a value that does not correspond to the spacing between two neighboring marks.
