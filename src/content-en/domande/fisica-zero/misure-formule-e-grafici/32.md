The time a microwave oven takes to heat a cup of milk is measured three times: $20\,\mathrm{s}$, $22\,\mathrm{s}$, and $24\,\mathrm{s}$. A fourth measurement gives $22\,\mathrm{s}$. What happens to the mean and the half-range when the fourth reading is added?

- Both stay the same
- The mean changes and the half-range stays the same
- The half-range is halved and the mean stays the same
- Both decrease

## Explanation

With three readings the mean is $\dfrac{20+22+24}{3}\,\mathrm{s}=22\,\mathrm{s}$. With four readings it is $\dfrac{20+22+24+22}{4}\,\mathrm{s}=\dfrac{88}{4}\,\mathrm{s}=22\,\mathrm{s}$, so the mean stays the same. The added reading lies between the minimum $20\,\mathrm{s}$ and the maximum $24\,\mathrm{s}$, so these two values do not change. Consequently the half-range also remains $\dfrac{24-20}{2}\,\mathrm{s}=2\,\mathrm{s}$.
