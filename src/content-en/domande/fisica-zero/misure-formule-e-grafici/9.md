A hair has a diameter of about $0.07\,\mathrm{mm}$. Why can't a ruler marked in millimeters measure it?

- The ruler's range is too small
- The ruler's resolution, $1\,\mathrm{mm}$, is much larger than the diameter
- The ruler is not accurate
- The diameter of a hair is not a length

## Explanation

The resolution of a millimeter ruler is the distance between two consecutive marks, namely $1\,\mathrm{mm}$. The diameter of the hair is about fourteen times smaller than this distance, so the ruler cannot distinguish one edge of the hair from the other. The range of the ruler, by contrast, is much larger than the diameter. Therefore the limitation comes from the **resolution**.
