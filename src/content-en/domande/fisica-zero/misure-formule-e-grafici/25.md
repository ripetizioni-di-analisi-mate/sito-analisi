A jar of yogurt is weighed three times on the same kitchen scale, and all three readings are $250\,\mathrm{g}$. Which statement is correct?

- The mass of the jar is exactly $250\,\mathrm{g}$, with no uncertainty
- The readings coincide, but the scale still has its own measurement limitations
- The scale is certainly accurate
- The mean of the readings cannot be calculated

## Explanation

The three readings are equal, so the half-range is $\dfrac{250-250}{2}\,\mathrm{g}=0\,\mathrm{g}$. This result indicates only that the recorded readings coincide. The scale still has a finite resolution and may have a systematic error, which would affect all readings in the same way. Therefore a half-range of zero does not prove that the mass is exactly $250\,\mathrm{g}$. The mean of the readings is calculated as usual and equals $250\,\mathrm{g}$.
