A spring hanging from the ceiling is $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ long with no weights. With a mass of $300\,\mathrm{g}$ hanging from it, it is $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. The extension is directly proportional to the hanging mass. How long is the spring with $500\,\mathrm{g}$?

- $17\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $25\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $27\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

With $300\,\mathrm{g}$ the extension is $15\,\mathrm{cm}-12\,\mathrm{cm}=3\,\mathrm{cm}$, that is, $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ for every $100\,\mathrm{g}$. The extension is proportional to the mass, so with $500\,\mathrm{g}$ it is $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The length of the spring is the initial length plus the extension:
$$
\ell=12\,\mathrm{cm}+5\,\mathrm{cm}=17\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
The value $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ would result from treating the whole length as proportional to the mass, whereas it starts from $12\,\mathrm{cm}$.
