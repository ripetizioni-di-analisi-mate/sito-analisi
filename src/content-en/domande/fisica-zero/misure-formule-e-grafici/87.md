The graph shows the mass of two aluminum samples as a function of volume: $5.4\,\mathrm{g}$ for $2\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and $10.8\,\mathrm{g}$ for $4\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, on a straight line through the origin. What density of aluminum follows from this?

::grafico{f="2.7*x" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=14 nomi="V,m" punti="2,5.4;4,10.8" titolo="Volume $V$ in cubic centimeters and mass $m$ in grams of aluminum samples."}

- $0.37\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $5.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.35\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The line passes through the origin, so mass and volume are directly proportional and the constant of proportionality is the density, $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$. Using the first point gives
$$
\rho=\frac{5.4\,\mathrm{g}}{2\,\mathrm{cm^3}}=2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3},
$$
and the second point gives the same ratio. The value $0.37\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ would result from dividing the volume by the mass.
