During a race, a timekeeper always starts the stopwatch $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$ after the start and stops it exactly at the finish. In this way, the timekeeper measures the same video-recorded race five times. What effect does the delay have on the readings?

- All readings are $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$ longer
- The half-range of the readings increases by $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$
- The mean of the five readings removes the delay
- All readings are $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$ shorter, and the half-range does not reveal the delay

## Explanation

The stopwatch starts $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$ after the race begins and stops at the finish, so each reading is $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$ shorter than the true duration. The delay is the same in every measurement, so it shifts all readings in the same way. The difference between the largest and smallest readings does not change, so the half-range does not reveal the delay. The mean is also shifted by $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$, because it is calculated from readings that are all shorter.
