The width of a door is measured three times: $80.2\,\mathrm{cm}$, $80.6\,\mathrm{cm}$, and $80.4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Taking the half-range as the uncertainty, how is the result written?

- $(80.4\pm0.4)\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $(80.4\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $(80.2\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $(241.2\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The mean of the three readings is
$$
\overline{\ell}=\frac{80.2+80.6+80.4}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}=80.4\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
The half-range is $s=\dfrac{80.6-80.2}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}=0.2\,\mathrm{cm}$. The problem takes the half-range as the uncertainty, so the result is $(80.4\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $0.4\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the difference between the largest and smallest readings before dividing by two.
