A student measures his own height, his brother's height, and his father's height, and then calculates the mean of the three values as a \"measurement of his height.\" Why does this mean not represent a repeated measurement of his height?

- Three readings are too few to calculate a mean
- The three readings refer to different quantities
- Heights cannot be measured in centimeters
- A mean can be calculated only for time intervals

## Explanation

The mean of repeated measurements summarizes readings of the *same* quantity, under the same conditions and in the same unit. The heights of the brother and the father are quantities different from the student's height. Therefore their mean does not describe the variability of the measurements of his height. A mean of three readings is perfectly possible when the readings concern the same quantity.
