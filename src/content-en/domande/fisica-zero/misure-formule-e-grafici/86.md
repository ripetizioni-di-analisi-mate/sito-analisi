The temperature of the water in a pot on the stove, measured $2$, $4$, and $6$ minutes after the burner is turned on, is $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, as shown in the graph. Assuming the points lie on a straight line even before the first measurement, what was the temperature when the burner was turned on?

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=70 nomi="t,T" punti="2,30;4,40;6,50" titolo="Time $t$ in minutes and water temperature $T$ in degrees Celsius."}

- $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

Between one measurement and the next, $2$ minutes pass and the temperature rises by $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, so it increases by $5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ per minute. From turning on the burner to the first measurement, $2$ minutes pass, so the initial temperature is
$$
30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}-2\cdot5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}=20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}.
$$
The value $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ would result from assuming a line through the origin, which does not pass through the measured points.
