For a cake, $(500\pm5)\,\mathrm{g}$ of flour is weighed and a baking time of $(40\pm2)\,\mathrm{min}$ is measured with a timer, with uncertainties estimated by comparable criteria. Which of the two measurements has the smaller relative uncertainty?

- The baking time, because its absolute uncertainty, $2$, is less than $5$
- Neither, because measurements in different units cannot be compared
- The mass of the flour, with a relative uncertainty of $1\%$
- The baking time, with a relative uncertainty of $5\%$

## Explanation

For the flour the relative uncertainty is
$$
u_r=\frac{5\,\mathrm{g}}{500\,\mathrm{g}}=0.01,
$$
that is, $1\%$. For the baking time it is $\dfrac{2\,\mathrm{min}}{40\,\mathrm{min}}=0.05$, that is, $5\%$. In each ratio the units cancel, so the two relative uncertainties are pure numbers and can be compared even though the quantities are different. Therefore the mass of the flour has the **smaller relative uncertainty**. A direct comparison between $2$ and $5$ is meaningless, because the two numbers are expressed in different units.
