By bicycle, the trip from home to school takes $30\,\mathrm{min}$ at a speed of $12\,\mathrm{km/h}$. On the same route, time and speed are inversely proportional. How long does the trip take at $18\,\mathrm{km/h}$?

- $45\,\mathrm{min}$
- $24\,\mathrm{min}$
- $20\,\mathrm{min}$
- $36\,\mathrm{min}$

## Explanation

The two quantities are inversely proportional, so the product of speed and time remains constant:
$$
12\,\mathrm{km/h}\cdot30\,\mathrm{min}=18\,\mathrm{km/h}\cdot t.
$$
Dividing both sides by $18\,\mathrm{km/h}$ gives $t=\dfrac{12\cdot30}{18}\,\mathrm{min}=20\,\mathrm{min}$. The value $45\,\mathrm{min}$ would result from treating time and speed as directly proportional.
