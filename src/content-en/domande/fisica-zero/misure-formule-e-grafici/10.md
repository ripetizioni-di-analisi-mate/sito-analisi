A cat lying down, about $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, is measured with a tailor's tape measure that has a range of $150\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a smallest division of $1\,\mathrm{mm}$; the cat moves slightly during the measurement. Why can the uncertainty exceed half the smallest division?

- The length of the cat exceeds the range of the tape measure
- Half the smallest division is $1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- An absolute uncertainty cannot be smaller than $1\,\mathrm{cm}$
- The cat does not stay still, and this adds a contribution to the uncertainty

## Explanation

Half the smallest division is $\dfrac{1\,\mathrm{mm}}{2}=0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$, and this rule considers only the reading uncertainty between two marks. A real measurement can have other contributions, due to calibration, to the way the instrument is used, or to the stability of the measured object. The cat moves, so its length changes while the scale is being read. Therefore the uncertainty can exceed $0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$. The range does not limit the measurement, because $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ is less than $150\,\mathrm{cm}$.
