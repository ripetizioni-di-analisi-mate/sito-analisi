A rain gauge measures rainfall in millimeters. Between the numbered marks $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $20\,\mathrm{mm}$, the scale is divided into ten equal intervals, and the collected water reaches the seventh mark after the $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ mark. How much rain has fallen?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.8 xmax=11.2 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.6 linee="0,0 10,0;0,-0.4 0,0.4;1,-0.2 1,0.2;2,-0.2 2,0.2;3,-0.2 3,0.2;4,-0.2 4,0.2;5,-0.2 5,0.2;6,-0.2 6,0.2;7,-0.2 7,0.2;8,-0.2 8,0.2;9,-0.2 9,0.2;10,-0.4 10,0.4" punti="7,0,pieno" etichette="0,-0.85,10;10,-0.85,20;10.7,0.75,mm;7,0.85,level" titolo="Rain gauge scale between the numbered marks $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $20\,\mathrm{mm}$."}

- $7\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $17\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $24\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $13\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

There are ten equal intervals between $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $20\,\mathrm{mm}$, so the scale division is $\dfrac{20-10}{10}\,\mathrm{mm}=1\,\mathrm{mm}$. The water level is seven intervals past the $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ mark. Therefore the reading is
$$
10\,\mathrm{mm}+7\cdot1\,\mathrm{mm}=17\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The value $13\,\mathrm{mm}$ would result from counting the marks downward from $20\,\mathrm{mm}$.
