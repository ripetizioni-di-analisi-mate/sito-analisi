An empty jar has a mass of $150\,\mathrm{g}$ and is filled with $200\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of honey, which has a density of $1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the total mass of the full jar?

- $280\,\mathrm{g}$
- $350\,\mathrm{g}$
- $430\,\mathrm{g}$
- $490\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The mass of the honey is $m_{\text{honey}}=\rho V=1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}\cdot200\,\mathrm{cm^3}=280\,\mathrm{g}$. The total mass is the mass of the jar plus the mass of the honey, so
$$
m=150\,\mathrm{g}+280\,\mathrm{g}=430\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
The value $280\,\mathrm{g}$ is the mass of the honey alone, without the jar.
