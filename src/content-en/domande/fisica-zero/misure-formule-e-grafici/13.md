The outdoor thermometer on a balcony has an analog scale with a smallest division of $1\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and the indicator stops on the $14\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ mark. Considering only the reading uncertainty and taking it to be half the smallest division, how is the measurement written?

- $(14\pm1)\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $(14.0\pm0.1)\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $(14\pm2)\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $(14.0\pm0.5)\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The given rule takes half the smallest division as the uncertainty, so
$$
u=\frac{1\,^\circ\mathrm{C}}{2}=0.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}.
$$
The reading is written to the same decimal place as the uncertainty, that is, to the tenths. Therefore the measurement is $(14.0\pm0.5)\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. An uncertainty of $1\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ would correspond to the whole smallest division, that is, to a rule different from the one stated.
