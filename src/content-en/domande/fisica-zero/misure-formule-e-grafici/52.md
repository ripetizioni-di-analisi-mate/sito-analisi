A digital kitchen scale displays $2.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ with a bag of flour on the pan. Which statement about the final zero of the reading is correct?

- It indicates that the reading is reported to the hundredths of a kilogram
- It has no meaning and can always be removed without losing information
- It indicates an uncertainty of $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$
- It makes the reading have two significant digits

## Explanation

The final zero occupies the hundredths-of-a-kilogram place, so it indicates that the display reports the mass to the hundredths. Writing $2.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ would lose this information about the precision of the reading. Significant digits are counted from the first nonzero digit, so $2.50$ has three. The final zero says nothing about an uncertainty of $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$.
