From the edge of a well, a stone is thrown upward with a velocity of $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$; it then falls back and drops into the well. The axis points upward with its origin at the edge, air resistance is negligible, and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the stone's height after $2\,\mathrm{s}$, if it has not yet reached the bottom?

- $y=+10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $y=-20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $y=-10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $y=30\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The height equation is $y=v_0t-\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$, valid throughout the flight, even after the stone passes the launch point. With $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$ this gives
$$
y=5\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot2\,\mathrm{s}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot(2\,\mathrm{s})^2=10\,\mathrm{m}-20\,\mathrm{m}=-10\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The negative height indicates that the stone is $10\,\mathrm{m}$ below the edge of the well. The value $-20\,\mathrm{m}$ leaves out the term $v_0t$, and $30\,\mathrm{m}$ adds the two terms.
