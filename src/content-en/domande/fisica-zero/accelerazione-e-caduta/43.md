An ambulance is heading south; the axis points north, so its velocity is $v_0=-10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The ambulance brakes and stops after a displacement $\Delta x=-20\,\mathrm{m}$. What is its acceleration, assumed constant?

- $a=-2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $a=+5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $a=-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $a=+2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

In the relation $v^2-v_0^2=2a\Delta x$, velocity, displacement, and acceleration keep their signs relative to the axis. When the ambulance stops, $v=0$, so
$$
a=\frac{0-(-10\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2\cdot(-20\,\mathrm{m})}=\frac{-100}{-40}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=+2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The acceleration is positive and the velocity is negative, so the ambulance slows down. The value $-2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ uses the displacement without its sign.
