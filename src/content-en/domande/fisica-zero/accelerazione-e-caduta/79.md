A pine cone comes off a branch $4\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground and falls from rest with negligible air resistance. A vertical axis pointing upward is chosen, with its origin at the ground. Which equation gives the pine cone's height $y$ during the fall, with $y$ in meters and $t$ in seconds?

- $y=4-\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$
- $y=4+\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$
- $y=\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$
- $y=-4+\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$

## Explanation

With the axis pointing upward and the origin at the ground, the initial position is $y_0=4\,\mathrm{m}$ and the acceleration is $a=-g$, because gravity points downward. The pine cone starts from rest, so the position equation $y=y_0+v_0t+\dfrac{1}{2}at^2$ becomes $y=4-\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$. The height decreases with time, as it must during a fall. The equation with $+\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$ would make the pine cone rise, and the one without the term $4$ would make it start from the ground.
