A crane lifts a load that is at rest until the stopwatch reads $12\,\mathrm{s}$. From that instant, the load rises with a constant acceleration of $0.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its velocity when the stopwatch reads $15\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $16.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The accelerated motion begins at $t_0=12\,\mathrm{s}$, so the velocity equation contains the elapsed time $t-t_0=3\,\mathrm{s}$. The load starts from rest, so
$$
v=0.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot(15\,\mathrm{s}-12\,\mathrm{s})=1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the stopwatch reading, $15\,\mathrm{s}$, as if the acceleration had lasted that long. The value $7.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the starting instant, and $16.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ adds the two instants.
