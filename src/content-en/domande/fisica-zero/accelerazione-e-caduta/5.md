A streetcar travels west along a straight track; the axis points east. In $4\,\mathrm{s}$ its velocity changes from $-12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which statement is true?

- $a_m=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the streetcar's speed increases
- $a_m=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the streetcar is braking
- $a_m=+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the streetcar's speed increases
- $a_m=+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the streetcar is braking

## Explanation

The change in velocity is $-20\,\mathrm{m/s}-(-12\,\mathrm{m/s})=-8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
a_m=\frac{-8\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4\,\mathrm{s}}=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The magnitude of the velocity goes from $12$ to $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the streetcar's speed **increases**. Velocity and acceleration have the same negative sign, which corresponds to an increase in speed. A negative acceleration alone does not indicate braking.
