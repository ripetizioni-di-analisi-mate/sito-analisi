A car traveling at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes to a stop with a constant acceleration of $-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the braking distance?

- $80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $100\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The stopping time is $t_{\mathrm{arresto}}=-\dfrac{v_0}{a}=\dfrac{20}{5}\,\mathrm{s}=4\,\mathrm{s}$. During uniform braking the average velocity is $\dfrac{v_0}{2}=10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
s_{\mathrm{frenata}}=10\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot4\,\mathrm{s}=40\,\mathrm{m},
$$
in agreement with $s_{\mathrm{frenata}}=-\dfrac{v_0^2}{2a}=\dfrac{400}{10}\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $80\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the factor of $2$ in the denominator, that is, it multiplies the initial velocity by the stopping time. The value $100\,\mathrm{m}$ multiplies the initial velocity by the magnitude of the acceleration, and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ is the stopping time written as a distance.
