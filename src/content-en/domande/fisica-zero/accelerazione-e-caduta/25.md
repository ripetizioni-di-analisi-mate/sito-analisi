Along a straight street, a streetcar stop is located at position $x_0=50\,\mathrm{m}$. The streetcar passes it with velocity $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and from there accelerates uniformly with $a=1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its position after $6\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $x=30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x=80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x=98\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x=62\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The position equation is $x=x_0+v_0t+\dfrac{1}{2}at^2$. Substituting the data gives
$$
x=50\,\mathrm{m}+2\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot6\,\mathrm{s}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot(6\,\mathrm{s})^2=50\,\mathrm{m}+12\,\mathrm{m}+18\,\mathrm{m}=80\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The value $30\,\mathrm{m}$ is the displacement, without the initial position. The value $98\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $62\,\mathrm{m}$ leaves out the term due to the acceleration.
