The velocity of a rowboat along a canal varies linearly over $4\,\mathrm{s}$. For which of the following cases is the distance traveled during the $4\,\mathrm{s}$ greater than the magnitude of the displacement?

- From $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- From $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- Constant at $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- From $0$ to $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The distance traveled exceeds the magnitude of the displacement only when the boat reverses direction, that is, when the velocity changes sign. In the case from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the velocity is zero at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$, so the two areas have magnitude $2\,\mathrm{m}$ and opposite signs. The displacement is therefore zero, while the distance is $4\,\mathrm{m}$. In the other three cases the velocity does not change sign, so the distance and the magnitude of the displacement are equal.
