A canoe is moving downstream. Its velocity changes from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with a constant acceleration of $0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How long does this take?

- $2\,\mathrm{s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{s}$
- $16\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Solving the velocity equation for time, with $a$ constant and nonzero, gives $t=\dfrac{v-v_0}{a}$. The increase in velocity is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
t=\frac{4\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}=8\,\mathrm{s}.
$$
The value $2\,\mathrm{s}$ multiplies the increase by the acceleration instead of dividing. The values $12$ and $16\,\mathrm{s}$ divide by $a$ only the final velocity or the sum of the two velocities.
