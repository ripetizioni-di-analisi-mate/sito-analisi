A bocce ball is rolled up a grassy slope, stops, and rolls back down. Its velocity along the slope changes linearly from $+3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $6\,\mathrm{s}$. What distance does it travel during these $6\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity varies linearly, so it is zero halfway through, at $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$. On the velocity–time graph there are two triangles with base $3\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, one above and one below the axis, each with area $\dfrac{3\cdot3}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=4.5\,\mathrm{m}$. For the distance we add the magnitudes of the areas, so the ball travels $4.5\,\mathrm{m}+4.5\,\mathrm{m}=9\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0\,\mathrm{m}$ is the displacement, because the ball returns to its launch point.
