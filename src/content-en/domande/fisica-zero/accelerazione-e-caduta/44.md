A sled released on a flat stretch of snow has velocity $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and slows down with a constant acceleration of $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Applying $v^2-v_0^2=2a\Delta x$ with $\Delta x=10\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $v^2=-4\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. What does this result mean?

- The sled covers $10\,\mathrm{m}$ and arrives with velocity $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- The sled covers $10\,\mathrm{m}$ and arrives with velocity $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- The sled stops after $9\,\mathrm{m}$ and does not reach $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- The sled moves back by $4\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The square of a velocity cannot be negative, so no velocity corresponds to a displacement of $10\,\mathrm{m}$. The stopping distance is found by setting $v=0$:
$$
\Delta x=\frac{0-36}{2\cdot(-2)}\,\mathrm{m}=9\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The sled **stops after $9\,\mathrm{m}$**, and the position at $10\,\mathrm{m}$ is never reached. The velocities $\pm2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ take the square root of $4$ and ignore the minus sign.
