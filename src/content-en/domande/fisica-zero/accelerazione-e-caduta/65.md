A car moving along a straight road brakes uniformly to a stop; the axis points in the direction of motion. What shape does the car's position–time graph have during braking?

- A straight line segment that rises
- An arc that rises less and less steeply and becomes horizontal at the instant the car stops
- An arc that rises more and more steeply
- A straight line segment that descends to the time axis

## Explanation

During braking the velocity is positive, so the position increases and the graph rises. The velocity decreases, so in equal time intervals the car covers shorter and shorter distances and the graph becomes less and less steep. When the car stops the velocity is zero, so the position stops changing and the graph becomes horizontal. By the position equation $x=x_0+v_0t+\dfrac{1}{2}at^2$ with $a<0$, the curve is an arc of a parabola. A rising straight segment describes motion at constant velocity, and an arc that gets steeper describes a car that speeds up; the segment descending to the axis is the velocity–time graph of braking.
