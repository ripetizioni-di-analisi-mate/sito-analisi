A bicycle traveling at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes with a constant acceleration of $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until it stops; then the cyclist puts a foot down and stays at rest. What is the bicycle's velocity at $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$ after braking begins?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

During braking the velocity equation is $v=6-2t$, with $v$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and $t$ in seconds. It is zero at $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$, so braking ends at that instant. At later times the bicycle is at rest, so at $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity is $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ extends the braking equation beyond the stop, where its assumptions no longer hold.
