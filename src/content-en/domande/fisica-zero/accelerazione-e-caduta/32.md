On a highway on-ramp, a car speeds up from $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $5\,\mathrm{s}$ with constant acceleration. How many meters does it travel during this interval?

- $75\,\mathrm{m}$
- $100\,\mathrm{m}$
- $50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $25\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With constant acceleration the velocity varies linearly, so the average velocity is the arithmetic mean of the initial and final values:
$$
v_m=\frac{10\,\mathrm{m/s}+20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2}=15\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The displacement is therefore $15\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot5\,\mathrm{s}=75\,\mathrm{m}$. The values $100$ and $50\,\mathrm{m}$ use only the final velocity or only the initial velocity. The value $25\,\mathrm{m}$ is the area of just the triangle above the rectangle of height $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on the velocity–time graph.
