During practice a basketball is thrown straight up with velocity $v_0$ and caught at the same height. The axis points upward and air resistance is negligible. What shape does the ball's velocity–time graph have during the flight?

- A straight line segment descending from $v_0$ to $-v_0$, always with the same slope
- A segment descending from $v_0$ to zero, then a segment rising back to $v_0$
- An arc of a parabola with its vertex at the instant the ball is at the highest point
- A segment descending from $v_0$ to zero, then a horizontal stretch along the time axis

## Explanation

Throughout the flight the acceleration is $-g$, so the velocity follows the equation $v=v_0-gt$, which is linear in time. The graph is therefore a single straight line segment with slope $-g$. The velocity is zero at the highest point, where the segment crosses the time axis, and then becomes negative during the descent. On returning to the launch height it equals $-v_0$. The segment rising back to $v_0$ represents the magnitude of the velocity, while the parabolic arc is the graph of the height as a function of time.
