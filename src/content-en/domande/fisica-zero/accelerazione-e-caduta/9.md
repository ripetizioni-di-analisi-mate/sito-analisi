A car starts from rest and after $10\,\mathrm{s}$ is moving at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. There is no other information about how its velocity changed during those ten seconds. Which statement follows from the data alone?

- After $5\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity was $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- On average, the velocity increased by $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second
- The acceleration was $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at every instant
- In $10\,\mathrm{s}$ the car traveled $100\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The given data yield the average acceleration $a_m=\dfrac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{10\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. This value describes the overall change, that is, **on average** the velocity increased by $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second. The velocity halfway through, the acceleration at each instant, and the distance traveled depend on how the velocity changed during the motion. All three would hold with constant acceleration, but the problem does not say that the acceleration is constant.
