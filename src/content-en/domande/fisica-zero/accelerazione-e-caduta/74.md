Average acceleration is zero from $0\,\mathrm{s}$ to $4\,\mathrm{s}$ and $+3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ from $0\,\mathrm{s}$ to $2\,\mathrm{s}$. What must the average acceleration be from $2\,\mathrm{s}$ to $4\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Velocity gains $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the first $2\,\mathrm{s}$. Returning to the initial velocity requires losing the same amount in the next $2\,\mathrm{s}$, averaging $-3$ metres per second squared.
