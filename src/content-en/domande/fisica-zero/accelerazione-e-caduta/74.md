A stone falls from rest into a deep ravine; air resistance is negligible and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How does the stone's velocity change while it is falling?

- It increases by $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second
- It increases by $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second
- It stays constant at $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- It doubles each second

## Explanation

In free fall the acceleration is constant and equal to $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ downward. A constant acceleration of $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ means that the velocity increases by $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in each second, as the equation $v=gt$ shows. The velocities after $1$, $2$, and $3\,\mathrm{s}$ are therefore about $9.8$, $19.6$, and $29.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with equal increases rather than doublings. The value $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from the coefficient $\dfrac{1}{2}g$ in the distance equation.
