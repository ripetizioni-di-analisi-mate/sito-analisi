A cyclist riding downhill at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ sees an obstacle. Before applying the brakes, $1\,\mathrm{s}$ passes, during which the velocity stays the same; then the cyclist brakes with a constant acceleration of $-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until stopping. What distance does the cyclist travel from the moment the obstacle is seen?

- $12.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $35\,\mathrm{m}$
- $22.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

During the reaction time the velocity is constant, so the cyclist covers $10\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot1\,\mathrm{s}=10\,\mathrm{m}$. During braking the distance is
$$
s_{\mathrm{frenata}}=-\frac{v_0^2}{2a}=\frac{100}{8}\,\mathrm{m}=12.5\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The motion is always in the same direction, so the total distance is $10\,\mathrm{m}+12.5\,\mathrm{m}=22.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $12.5\,\mathrm{m}$ ignores the reaction time, and $35\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the factor of $2$ in the braking distance.
