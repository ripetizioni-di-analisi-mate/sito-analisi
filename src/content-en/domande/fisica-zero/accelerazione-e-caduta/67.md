On a wet road, a car brakes with an acceleration of smaller magnitude than on a dry road. Starting from the same velocity and braking to a stop, what happens on the wet road?

- The braking distance decreases
- The braking distance increases
- The braking distance stays the same, because the initial velocity is the same
- The stopping time decreases

## Explanation

The braking distance is $s_{\mathrm{frenata}}=-\dfrac{v_0^2}{2a}=\dfrac{v_0^2}{2|a|}$, because the acceleration is negative. For the same $v_0$, a smaller magnitude $|a|$ makes the denominator smaller, so the braking distance **increases**. The stopping time $\dfrac{v_0}{|a|}$ also increases, so the statement that the time decreases is false.
