A truck traveling at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes with constant acceleration and stops after $40\,\mathrm{m}$. What is its velocity when it has covered the first $20\,\mathrm{m}$ of braking?

- $14.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $200\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The axis points in the direction of motion. When the truck stops, $v=0$, so the relation $v^2-v_0^2=2a\Delta x$ gives
$$
a=\frac{0-(20\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2\cdot40\,\mathrm{m}}=-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
After $20\,\mathrm{m}$ we have $v^2=400\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}+2\cdot(-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2})\cdot20\,\mathrm{m}=200\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. The truck is still moving in the positive direction, so $v=\sqrt{200}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx14.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Halfway through the braking distance it is the square of the velocity that has been halved, so the velocity is greater than $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ omits the factor of $2$ in the term $2a\Delta x$, and $200\,\mathrm{m/s}$ omits the square root.
