To decide who starts a match, the referee tosses a coin straight up. The axis points upward, $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and air resistance is negligible. What are the coin's velocity and acceleration at its highest point?

- $v=0$ and $a=0$
- $v=-9.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $a=0$
- $v=0$ and $a=-9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $v=0$ and $a=+9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

At the highest point the coin stops rising and starts to fall, so at that instant its velocity is zero. Gravity keeps acting there too, so the acceleration remains $-g=-9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed downward. If the acceleration were zero, the velocity would stay zero and the coin would remain suspended. A positive sign would indicate an upward acceleration, opposite to gravity.
