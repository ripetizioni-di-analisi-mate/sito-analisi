A city bus is traveling at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes for its stop with a constant acceleration of $-2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How long does it take to stop?

- $37.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.17\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

When the bus stops, its velocity is zero, so $0=v_0+at_{\mathrm{arresto}}$, and therefore $t_{\mathrm{arresto}}=-\dfrac{v_0}{a}$. Substituting gives
$$
t_{\mathrm{arresto}}=-\frac{15\,\mathrm{m/s}}{-2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}=6\,\mathrm{s}.
$$
The value $37.5\,\mathrm{s}$ multiplies velocity and acceleration, and $0.17\,\mathrm{s}$ divides the acceleration by the velocity. The value $3\,\mathrm{s}$ halves the time, as if the average velocity were used.
