A scooter pulls away from a traffic light when its velocity is already $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $3\,\mathrm{s}$ later it is moving at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along a straight road. What is its average acceleration?

- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

**Average acceleration** is the change in velocity divided by the elapsed time. The change in velocity is $10\,\mathrm{m/s}-4\,\mathrm{m/s}=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
a_m=\frac{6\,\mathrm{m/s}}{3\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The value $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is just the change in velocity, not divided by the time. The values $3.3$ and $4.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ divide the final velocity, or the sum of the two velocities, by the time.
