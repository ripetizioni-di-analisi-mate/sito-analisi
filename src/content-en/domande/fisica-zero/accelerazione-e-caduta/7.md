In a track test, a sports car goes from rest to $108\,\mathrm{km/h}$ in $10\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its average acceleration, in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$?

- $10.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $30\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

First convert the velocity to meters per second. One kilometer per hour equals $\dfrac{1000\,\mathrm{m}}{3600\,\mathrm{s}}$, so we divide by $3.6$:
$$
\frac{108}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s}=30\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
Since the car starts from rest, the change in velocity is $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the average acceleration is $\dfrac{30\,\mathrm{m/s}}{10\,\mathrm{s}}=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The value $10.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ divides kilometers per hour by seconds without converting.
