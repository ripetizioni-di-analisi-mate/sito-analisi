The graph shows the height of a volleyball thrown straight up, as a function of time. At what instant is the ball's velocity zero?

::grafico{f="15*x-5*x^2+0*sqrt(x*(3-x))" punti="0,0,pieno;3,0,pieno" legenda=0 xmin=-0.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-1 ymax=13 nomi="t,y" titolo="Height of the volleyball in meters and time in seconds"}

- $t=0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$
- $t=1.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $t=0.75\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The ball rises while the height increases and falls when the height decreases. The velocity is zero at the instant the ball stops rising, which is the highest point of the graph, located at $t=1.5\,\mathrm{s}$, halfway between the launch and the return to the ground. At $t=0$ and at $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ the height is zero, but the ball is moving, upward and downward respectively. The curve is symmetric about the highest point, as happens when ascent and descent take the same time.
