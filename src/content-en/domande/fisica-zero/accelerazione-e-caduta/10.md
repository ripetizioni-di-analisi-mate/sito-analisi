The velocity of a galloping horse on a straight stretch is measured with a radar gun. When the stopwatch reads $2\,\mathrm{s}$ the horse has velocity $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$; when it reads $6\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity is $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the average acceleration between the two instants?

- $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The time interval is the difference between the two stopwatch readings, so $\Delta t=6\,\mathrm{s}-2\,\mathrm{s}=4\,\mathrm{s}$. The change in velocity is $15\,\mathrm{m/s}-3\,\mathrm{m/s}=12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
a_m=\frac{12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4\,\mathrm{s}}=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The value $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ divides the change in velocity by the final stopwatch reading, $6\,\mathrm{s}$, instead of by the elapsed interval. The value $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ divides only the final velocity by $6\,\mathrm{s}$, and $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ adds the two velocities.
