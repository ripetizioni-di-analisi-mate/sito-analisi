An elevator moves downward at a constant $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and, on reaching its floor, stops in $3\,\mathrm{s}$. The vertical axis points upward. What is the average acceleration while it stops?

- $-0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-4.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $+0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

With the axis pointing upward, the descending elevator has velocity $v_i=-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and at the end it is at rest, with $v_f=0$. Therefore
$$
a_m=\frac{0-(-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s})}{3\,\mathrm{s}}=+0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The acceleration is positive because the velocity component goes from a negative value to zero, so it increases. Velocity and acceleration have opposite signs, so the elevator's speed decreases, as in any stop. The value $-0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ comes from assuming that slowing down always means a negative acceleration.
