A funicular car travels down a straight track; the axis points toward the top, so during the descent the velocity is negative. The graph shows the car's velocity between $0$ and $7\,\mathrm{s}$. In which interval is the acceleration positive?

::grafico{f="max(-2*x,-4,-4+2*(x-5))+0*sqrt(x*(7-x))" punti="0,0,pieno;2,-4,pieno;5,-4,pieno;7,0,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,-4;5,0,5,-4" xmin=-1 xmax=8 ymin=-5 ymax=1 nomi="t,v" titolo="Velocity of the car in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and time in seconds"}

- Between $0$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$
- Between $2$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$
- In no interval, because the velocity is never positive
- Between $5$ and $7\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

On a velocity–time graph the acceleration is the slope of each stretch. Between $0$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity goes from $0$ to $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the acceleration is $\dfrac{-4}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Between $2$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity is constant, so the acceleration is zero. Between $5$ and $7\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity goes from $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $0$, so the acceleration is $\dfrac{4}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. In this last stretch velocity and acceleration have opposite signs, and indeed the car slows down to a stop. The sign of the acceleration depends on how the velocity changes, not on the sign of the velocity.
