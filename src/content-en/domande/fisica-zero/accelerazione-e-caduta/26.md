In a warehouse, a crate is released from rest at the top of a chute and slides down with a constant acceleration of $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How long does it take to travel $9\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $4.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.1\,\mathrm{s}$
- $9\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The crate starts from rest, so the displacement is $\Delta x=\dfrac{1}{2}at^2$. Multiplying by $2$ and dividing by $a$ gives
$$
t^2=\frac{2\Delta x}{a}=\frac{2\cdot9\,\mathrm{m}}{2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}=9\,\mathrm{s^2}.
$$
The time is positive, so $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$. The value $9\,\mathrm{s}$ stops at $t^2$ without taking the square root. The value $4.5\,\mathrm{s}$ divides the distance by the acceleration, and $2.1\,\mathrm{s}$ omits the factor of $2$ under the square root.
