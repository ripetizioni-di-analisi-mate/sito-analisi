An elevator starts from rest and goes up: it accelerates uniformly to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $2\,\mathrm{s}$, continues at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $10\,\mathrm{s}$, and then slows down uniformly to a stop in $2\,\mathrm{s}$. How many meters has it risen?

- $28\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $24\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity–time graph is a trapezoid, and the displacement is its area. The two phases with changing velocity are triangles with area $\dfrac{2\cdot2}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=2\,\mathrm{m}$ each. The constant-velocity phase is a rectangle with area $2\cdot10\,\mathrm{m}=20\,\mathrm{m}$. So the elevator rises $2+20+2=24\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $28\,\mathrm{m}$ treats the velocity as $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for all $14\,\mathrm{s}$, and $20\,\mathrm{m}$ counts only the middle phase.
