A trolley starts from rest and accelerates uniformly towards a switch $2\,\mathrm{m}$ away. The switch stays on after the trolley first reaches it. At $1\,\mathrm{s}$ the switch is still off and at $2\,\mathrm{s}$ it is already on. What do these readings guarantee about the acceleration $a$?

- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\le a<4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}<a<1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $a\ge4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $a=\dfrac{16}{9}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

First contact occurs at a time $t$ greater than one second and no later than two seconds. In SI units $2=\dfrac{at^2}{2}$ gives $a=\dfrac{4}{t^2}$. Arrival at two seconds would give the minimum of $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Arrival at one second would give $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, but the first reading rules that out. The exact time between samples is unknown.
