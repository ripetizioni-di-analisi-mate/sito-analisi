A taxi traveling at $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes to a stop with a constant acceleration of $-6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How many meters does it travel in the last second before stopping?

- $3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The stopping time is $t_{\mathrm{arresto}}=-\dfrac{v_0}{a}=\dfrac{18}{6}\,\mathrm{s}=3\,\mathrm{s}$, so the last second runs from $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$ to $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$. The braking distance is $s_{\mathrm{frenata}}=-\dfrac{v_0^2}{2a}=\dfrac{324}{12}\,\mathrm{m}=27\,\mathrm{m}$. At $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$, by the position equation, the taxi has traveled
$$
18\cdot2\,\mathrm{m}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot6\cdot2^2\,\mathrm{m}=36\,\mathrm{m}-12\,\mathrm{m}=24\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
Therefore in the last second it travels $27\,\mathrm{m}-24\,\mathrm{m}=3\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $9\,\mathrm{m}$ splits the braking distance equally among the three seconds, but the velocity decreases and the distances covered get shorter. The value $15\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance covered in the first second.
