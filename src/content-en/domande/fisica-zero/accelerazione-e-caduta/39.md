A bus moving with velocity $v_0$ brakes with constant acceleration $a$ and stops after a distance $\Delta x$; the braking time is not known. The axis points in the direction of motion. Which expression gives the acceleration?

- $a=-\dfrac{v_0}{\Delta x}$
- $a=-\dfrac{2\Delta x}{v_0^2}$
- $a=-\dfrac{v_0^2}{2\Delta x}$
- $a=-\dfrac{v_0^2}{\Delta x}$

## Explanation

Without the time, we use the relation $v^2-v_0^2=2a\Delta x$. When the bus stops, $v=0$, so $-v_0^2=2a\Delta x$, and therefore $a=-\dfrac{v_0^2}{2\Delta x}$. The minus sign is consistent with braking in the positive direction. The expression $-\dfrac{v_0}{\Delta x}$ has units of inverse time, $\mathrm{1/s}$, so it is not an acceleration. The expression $-\dfrac{v_0^2}{\Delta x}$ has the right units but gives twice the correct value.
