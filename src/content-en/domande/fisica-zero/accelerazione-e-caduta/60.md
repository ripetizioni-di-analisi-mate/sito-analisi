A skater glides on the ice at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and slows down uniformly, coming to rest in $8\,\mathrm{s}$. How many meters does the skater travel before stopping?

- $16\,\mathrm{m}$
- $32\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In uniform braking the velocity decreases linearly from $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to zero, so the average velocity is $\dfrac{4\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2}=2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The distance traveled is therefore
$$
s=2\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot8\,\mathrm{s}=16\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
On the velocity–time graph, it is the area of the triangle with base $8\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $32\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the initial velocity as if it stayed constant, and $2\,\mathrm{m}$ is the average velocity written as a distance.
