An acorn comes off a branch and falls from rest; air resistance is negligible and the axis points downward, with $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What shape does the acorn's velocity–time graph have during the fall?

- An arc of a parabola with its vertex at the origin
- A horizontal segment at height $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- A straight line segment starting at $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and descending toward the time axis
- A straight line segment starting at the origin with slope $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The velocity when falling from rest is $v=gt$, which is a linear function of time. The velocity–time graph is therefore a straight line segment through the origin, because the velocity is zero at $t=0$. Its slope is the acceleration, $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The parabolic arc is instead the graph of the distance fallen, $h=\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$, as a function of time.
