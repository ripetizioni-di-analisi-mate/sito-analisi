A cart has velocity $v_0=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at time $t=0$ and constant acceleration $a=+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Computing when the velocity equals $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives $t=-2\,\mathrm{s}$. What does this result mean?

- The cart will reach $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ two seconds after the origin
- The cart is braking, because the time is negative
- The velocity equation does not hold when the acceleration is positive
- The cart had $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ two seconds before the instant chosen as the origin

## Explanation

From $t=\dfrac{v-v_0}{a}$ we get $t=\dfrac{2-6}{2}\,\mathrm{s}=-2\,\mathrm{s}$. A negative time indicates an instant before the chosen time origin. The velocity increases by $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second, so two seconds before $t=0$ it was $6-4=2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. After the origin the velocity is always greater than $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the value $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is never reached again.
