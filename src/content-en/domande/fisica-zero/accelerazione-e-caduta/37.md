A freight train slows down as it approaches a switch: its velocity drops from $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with a constant acceleration of $-0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How many meters does it travel while slowing down?

- $-800\,\mathrm{m}$
- $800\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{m}$
- $400\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The relation $v^2-v_0^2=2a\Delta x$ gives the displacement:
$$
\Delta x=\frac{v^2-v_0^2}{2a}=\frac{100-900}{2\cdot(-0.5)}\,\mathrm{m}=\frac{-800}{-1}\,\mathrm{m}=800\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The train always moves in the same direction, so the distance traveled equals the displacement. The value $1000\,\mathrm{m}$ adds the squares of the velocities, and $400\,\mathrm{m}$ squares the difference of the velocities. The value $-800\,\mathrm{m}$ drops the sign of the acceleration.
