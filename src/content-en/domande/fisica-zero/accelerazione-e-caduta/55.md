A dog runs along a straight path and at time $t=0$ it is at position $x_0=4\,\mathrm{m}$. The graph shows its velocity until it stops. What is its position at $t=4\,\mathrm{s}$?

::grafico{f="min(3,3-1.5*(x-2))+0*sqrt(x*(4-x))" area="min(3,3-1.5*(x-2));0;0;4" punti="0,3,pieno;2,3,pieno;4,0,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,3" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=4 nomi="t,v" titolo="Velocity of the dog in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and time in seconds"}

- $x=9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x=13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x=16\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x=10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The displacement is the area between the graph and the time axis. Between $0$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$ there is a rectangle with area $3\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot2\,\mathrm{s}=6\,\mathrm{m}$, and between $2$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$ a triangle with area $\dfrac{2\,\mathrm{s}\cdot3\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2}=3\,\mathrm{m}$. The displacement is therefore $\Delta x=9\,\mathrm{m}$. The final position is obtained by adding the displacement to the initial position, so $x=4\,\mathrm{m}+9\,\mathrm{m}=13\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $9\,\mathrm{m}$ is the displacement, $16\,\mathrm{m}$ treats the velocity as $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for all $4\,\mathrm{s}$, and $10\,\mathrm{m}$ leaves out the slowing-down stretch.
