A stone is dropped from rest from $20\,\mathrm{m}$ above a lawn, with negligible air resistance and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. A student substitutes $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ into $h=\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$ and gets $45\,\mathrm{m}$. Which statement is correct?

- At $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ the stone has fallen $45\,\mathrm{m}$
- The stone has been on the ground since $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$, so the formula does not describe the instant $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$
- At $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ the stone is $25\,\mathrm{m}$ below the level of the lawn
- At $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ the stone has velocity $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The fall time is $t=\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2\cdot20}{10}}\,\mathrm{s}=2\,\mathrm{s}$. The free-fall model ends at the first contact with the ground, so the equations $h=\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$ and $v=gt$ hold only for $0\leq t\leq2\,\mathrm{s}$. At $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ the stone is at rest on the lawn. The values $45\,\mathrm{m}$ and $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ apply the formulas outside the interval in which their assumptions hold.
