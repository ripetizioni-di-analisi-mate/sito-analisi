A ship approaching port reduces its velocity from $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with a constant acceleration of $-0.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How long does the slowdown last?

- $-20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $46.7\,\mathrm{s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.8\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Solving the velocity equation for time gives $t=\dfrac{v-v_0}{a}$. The change in velocity is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}-10\,\mathrm{m/s}=-6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
t=\frac{-6\,\mathrm{m/s}}{-0.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}=20\,\mathrm{s}.
$$
The numerator and the denominator are both negative, so the time is positive. The value $-20\,\mathrm{s}$ drops the sign of the acceleration, and $46.7\,\mathrm{s}$ adds the two velocities.
