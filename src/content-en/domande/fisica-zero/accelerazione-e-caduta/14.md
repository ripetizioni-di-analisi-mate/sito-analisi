A bobsled is going down an ice track. When timing begins, its velocity is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and from then on it accelerates uniformly with $a=0.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its velocity $5\,\mathrm{s}$ later?

- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By the velocity equation, $v=v_0+at$. In $5\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity increases by $0.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot5\,\mathrm{s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
v=4\,\mathrm{m/s}+4\,\mathrm{m/s}=8\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is just the increase, without the initial velocity. The value $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ halves the increase, and $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ squares the time.
