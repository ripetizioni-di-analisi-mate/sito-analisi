A car brakes to a stop, always with the same constant acceleration, once starting from $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and once from $60\,\mathrm{km/h}$. How does the braking distance change in the second case?

- It becomes twice as large
- It stays the same
- It becomes half as large
- It becomes four times as large

## Explanation

The braking distance is $s_{\mathrm{frenata}}=-\dfrac{v_0^2}{2a}$, so for the same acceleration it is proportional to the square of the initial velocity. Doubling $v_0$ makes its square $2^2=4$ times as large, so the braking distance **quadruples**. The stopping time $-\dfrac{v_0}{a}$, by contrast, only doubles. The answer "twice as large" confuses the distance with the stopping time.
