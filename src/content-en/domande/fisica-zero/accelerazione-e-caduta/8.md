A tennis ball reaches the racket with velocity $-20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and leaves it with velocity $+30\,\mathrm{m/s}$; the axis is positive in the direction in which the ball leaves. The contact lasts $0.01\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the ball's average acceleration during the contact?

- $1000\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-5000\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $500\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5000\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The velocity reverses direction, so the signs matter in the difference:
$$
v_f-v_i=30\,\mathrm{m/s}-(-20\,\mathrm{m/s})=50\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
Dividing by the contact time gives $a_m=\dfrac{50\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.01\,\mathrm{s}}=5000\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The value $1000\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ subtracts the magnitudes of the two velocities and ignores the reversal of motion. The value $500\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ divides by $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$ instead of $0.01\,\mathrm{s}$.
