A coin and a flat sheet of paper are dropped together, from rest, from a table. The sheet reaches the floor long after the coin. Which statement explains the difference?

- The sheet has less mass, and in free fall lighter objects fall more slowly
- The sheet is subject to a smaller value of $g$ than the coin
- The coin starts with a greater initial velocity
- Air resistance on the sheet is not negligible, so the free-fall model does not describe it

## Explanation

The free-fall model applies to an object acted on by gravity alone, with negligible air resistance. For the coin this assumption holds to a good approximation; for a flat sheet the air resistance is large, so the assumption **does not hold**. In free fall the mass does not appear in the equations of motion, and near Earth's surface $g$ is the same for all objects. Both objects start from rest.
