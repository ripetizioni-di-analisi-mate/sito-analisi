A car in reverse starts from rest along an axis whose positive direction is the forward direction. It has constant acceleration $a=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and its displacement is $\Delta x=-4\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the velocity component at the end of this stretch?

- $v=+4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $v=-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $v=-16\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $v=-8\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

From the relation $v^2-v_0^2=2a\Delta x$, with $v_0=0$, we get
$$
v^2=2\cdot(-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2})\cdot(-4\,\mathrm{m})=16\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}.
$$
The square root gives the magnitude of the velocity, $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The sign is set by the direction of motion, which is negative, so $v=-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $+4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives only the magnitude, and $-16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ omits the square root.
