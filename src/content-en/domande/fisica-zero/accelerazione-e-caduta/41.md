A skier goes down a slope with constant acceleration. Over a $15\,\mathrm{m}$ stretch her velocity increases from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is her acceleration?

- $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The time is unknown, so we use $v^2-v_0^2=2a\Delta x$:
$$
a=\frac{(8\,\mathrm{m/s})^2-(2\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2\cdot15\,\mathrm{m}}=\frac{64-4}{30}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The value $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ squares the difference of the velocities, $6^2=36$, instead of subtracting the squares. The value $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ omits the factor of $2$, and $0.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ divides the difference of the velocities by the distance.
