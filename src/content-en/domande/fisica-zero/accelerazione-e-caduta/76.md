From the parapet of a tall tower, a stone is dropped and, $1\,\mathrm{s}$ later, a second stone is dropped from the same point, both from rest, with negligible air resistance and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. While both are still falling, how does the distance between the two stones change?

- It stays constant
- It increases
- It decreases
- It first increases and then decreases

## Explanation

The two stones have the same acceleration $g$, but the first one has been falling one second longer. At every instant its velocity exceeds that of the second by $g\cdot1\,\mathrm{s}=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The first stone therefore always descends faster than the second, and the distance between them **increases**. Equal accelerations keep the *difference in velocity* constant, not the distance.
