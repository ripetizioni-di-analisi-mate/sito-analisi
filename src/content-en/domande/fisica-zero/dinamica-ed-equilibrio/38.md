Two skaters at rest on the ice, of masses $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $75\,\mathrm{kg}$, push each other with their hands; friction is negligible. What is the ratio of the magnitude of the lighter skater's acceleration to that of the heavier skater?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{9}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

By Newton's third law, the forces the two skaters exert on each other have the same magnitude $F$. By Newton's second law, the accelerations have magnitudes $\dfrac{F}{50\,\mathrm{kg}}$ and $\dfrac{F}{75\,\mathrm{kg}}$. So their ratio is
$$
\frac{\dfrac{F}{50\,\mathrm{kg}}}{\dfrac{F}{75\,\mathrm{kg}}}=\frac{75}{50}=\frac{3}{2}.
$$
The lighter skater therefore has the larger acceleration, even though both receive forces of the same magnitude.
