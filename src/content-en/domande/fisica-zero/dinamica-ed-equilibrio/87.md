A train travels along a straight track at constant velocity. A passenger standing in the car drops a coin from her hand, and air resistance is negligible. Where does the coin land relative to the passenger?

- Behind her feet, because the train moves forward while the coin falls
- In front of her feet, because the train pushes the coin forward
- At her feet only if the train is at rest
- At her feet, directly below her hand

## Explanation

Relative to the ground, treated as an inertial frame, at the instant it is released the coin has the same horizontal velocity as the train. During the fall only the weight acts on the coin, and the weight is vertical, so the horizontal net force is zero. By Newton's second law, the horizontal velocity of the coin therefore stays equal to that of the train. The passenger and the coin then move forward together, and the coin lands directly below the hand. The same result follows from the fact that the train at constant velocity is an inertial frame, like the ground.
