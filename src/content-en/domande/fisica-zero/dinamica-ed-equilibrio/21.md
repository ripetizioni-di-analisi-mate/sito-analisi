On an air table, where friction is negligible, a $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ puck receives two horizontal forces perpendicular to each other, one of $3\,\mathrm{N}$ and one of $4\,\mathrm{N}$. The vertical forces balance. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the puck?

- $14\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Take the $x$-axis along the $3\,\mathrm{N}$ force and the $y$-axis along the $4\,\mathrm{N}$ force, so $R_x=3\,\mathrm{N}$ and $R_y=4\,\mathrm{N}$. Since the two components are perpendicular, the magnitude of the net force follows from the Pythagorean theorem,
$$
R=\sqrt{(3\,\mathrm{N})^2+(4\,\mathrm{N})^2}=5\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
By **Newton's second law**, the acceleration has the direction of the net force and magnitude
$$
a=\frac{R}{m}=\frac{5\,\mathrm{N}}{0.5\,\mathrm{kg}}=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The value $14\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ comes from adding the magnitudes of the two forces as if they had the same direction.
