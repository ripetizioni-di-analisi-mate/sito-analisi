In a rear-end collision a truck hits a small car. During the collision, how do the magnitudes of the force of the truck on the car and of the force of the car on the truck compare?

- The force of the truck on the car is larger
- They are equal
- The force of the car on the truck is larger
- They are equal only if the two vehicles have the same mass

## Explanation

The two forces belong to a single interaction, one exerted by the truck on the car and the other by the car on the truck. By Newton's third law, they always have equal magnitudes and opposite directions, whatever the masses. The consequences differ because the car has a smaller mass, so the car undergoes a larger acceleration. The difference lies in the effects, not in the magnitudes of the forces.
