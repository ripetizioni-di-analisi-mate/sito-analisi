With a sharp tug to the right, a tablecloth is pulled out from under a $0.4\,\mathrm{kg}$ plate resting on a horizontal table. While the tablecloth slides under the plate, the coefficient of kinetic friction between them is $\mu_d=0.25$. Using $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the acceleration of the plate relative to the table?

- $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to the left
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to the right
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to the right
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Vertically the plate has zero acceleration, so $N=mg=4\,\mathrm{N}$ and the kinetic friction is $F_d=\mu_dN=0.25\cdot4\,\mathrm{N}=1\,\mathrm{N}$. The tablecloth slides to the right under the plate, so the plate slides to the left relative to the tablecloth. Friction on the plate opposes this relative sliding, so it points to the right and it is the only horizontal force on the plate. By Newton's second law, the plate accelerates to the right with
$$
a=\frac{1\,\mathrm{N}}{0.4\,\mathrm{kg}}=2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The value $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ confuses the acceleration with the frictional force.
