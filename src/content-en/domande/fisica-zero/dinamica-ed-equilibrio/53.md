In an elevator that starts upward with an acceleration of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, a $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ person stands on a scale placed on the floor. Using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the normal force of the scale on the person?

- $826.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $686.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $546.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $70\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Relative to the ground, the person accelerates upward together with the elevator. Taking upward as positive, Newton's second law gives $N-mg=ma$, so
$$
N=m(g+a)=70\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot11.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=826.7\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
The normal force exceeds the weight, $686.7\,\mathrm{N}$, because the net force must point upward.
