An $80\,\mathrm{kg}$ refrigerator is pushed horizontally with $150\,\mathrm{N}$ and stays at rest; the coefficient of static friction is $0.5$. Using $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the static frictional force on the refrigerator?

- $400\,\mathrm{N}$
- $250\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $150\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The refrigerator is at rest, so the horizontal net force is zero and static friction balances the push. Therefore the frictional force is $150\,\mathrm{N}$, opposite to the push. The maximum available is $\mu_sN=0.5\cdot800\,\mathrm{N}=400\,\mathrm{N}$, and since $150\,\mathrm{N}\leq400\,\mathrm{N}$, equilibrium is possible. The value $400\,\mathrm{N}$ is the limit and not the actual frictional force.
