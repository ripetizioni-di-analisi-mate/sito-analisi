A book is slid twice across the same horizontal floor, once at $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and once at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In the kinetic friction model, how do the magnitudes of the frictional force in the two throws compare?

- It is three times larger in the faster throw
- It is larger in the slower throw
- They are zero, because the book is already moving
- They are equal

## Explanation

In the kinetic friction model, the magnitude of friction is $F_d=\mu_dN$. The floor and the book are the same, so $\mu_d$ and $N=mg$ have the same values in both throws. The speed does not appear in the formula, so the frictional force has the same magnitude. The faster throw lasts longer only because the book has more speed to lose.
