A buoy at rest is pushed by the current with $12\,\mathrm{N}$ east and by the wind with $5\,\mathrm{N}$ north, as shown in the figure. In the horizontal plane, only the anchor chain holds it in place. With the $x$-axis pointing east and the $y$-axis pointing north, what are the horizontal components $F_x$ and $F_y$ of the force of the chain?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-0.5,-0.5 0.5,-0.5 0.5,0.5 -0.5,0.5" vettori="0.5,0,4.5,0,12 N;0,0.5,0,2.17,5 N" riferimento="-3,-1.8,0,1.2,x (east),y (north)" xmin=-3.6 xmax=5.4 ymin=-2.2 ymax=3 titolo="Pushes of the current and the wind on the buoy seen from above; one unit in the diagram represents $3\,\mathrm{N}$"}

- $F_x=12\,\mathrm{N}$, $F_y=5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $F_x=-5\,\mathrm{N}$, $F_y=-12\,\mathrm{N}$
- $F_x=-12\,\mathrm{N}$, $F_y=-5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $F_x=-17\,\mathrm{N}$, $F_y=0\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The buoy is in equilibrium, so both components of the net force are zero,
$$
\begin{cases}
12\,\mathrm{N}+F_x=0,\\[4pt]
5\,\mathrm{N}+F_y=0.
\end{cases}
$$
Therefore $F_x=-12\,\mathrm{N}$ and $F_y=-5\,\mathrm{N}$, that is, the chain pulls the buoy toward the west and toward the south. The components cancel separately along each axis, so the magnitudes of the two pushes are not added.
