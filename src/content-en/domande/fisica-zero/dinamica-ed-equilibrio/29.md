A $1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ forklift travels in a straight line at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and, for $5\,\mathrm{s}$, receives a constant net force of $600\,\mathrm{N}$ in the direction of motion. What is its speed at the end of the $5\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By Newton's second law, the acceleration is $a=\dfrac{600\,\mathrm{N}}{1200\,\mathrm{kg}}=0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The net force is constant, so the acceleration is constant and the equation $v=v_0+at$ applies. The forklift was already moving at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
v=2\,\mathrm{m/s}+0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot5\,\mathrm{s}=4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is only the increase in speed, which ignores the initial velocity.
