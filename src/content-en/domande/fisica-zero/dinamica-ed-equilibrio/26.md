For $3\,\mathrm{s}$ a child pulls a $30\,\mathrm{kg}$ sled, initially at rest, across the snow, and the net force on the sled stays equal to $60\,\mathrm{N}$ in the direction of motion. What distance does the sled travel in those $3\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By Newton's second law, the acceleration is $a=\dfrac{60\,\mathrm{N}}{30\,\mathrm{kg}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The acceleration is constant and the sled starts from rest, so the distance follows the equation $s=\dfrac{1}{2}at^2$. Therefore
$$
s=\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot(3\,\mathrm{s})^2=9\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The value $18\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
