A $2000\,\mathrm{kg}$ van brakes in a straight line with constant deceleration and goes from $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $8\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the magnitude of the net force on the van while braking?

- $5000\,\mathrm{N}$
- $40{,}000\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2500\,\mathrm{N}$
- $16{,}000\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The acceleration is constant, so its magnitude is the change in velocity divided by the time,
$$
a=\frac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{8\,\mathrm{s}}=2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
By Newton's second law, the magnitude of the net force is $R=ma=2000\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=5000\,\mathrm{N}$. The net force is opposite to the motion, because the van slows down.
