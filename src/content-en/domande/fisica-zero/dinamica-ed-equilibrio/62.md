A toy locomotive pulls two identical cars of $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ each, coupled in a row, along a horizontal track with an acceleration of $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Friction and the masses of the couplings are negligible. What is the tension in the coupling between the locomotive and the first car?

- $1\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The first coupling pulls both cars, so we treat the two cars together as a single body of mass $1\,\mathrm{kg}$. The only horizontal force on this combined body is the tension in the first coupling, because the coupling between the two cars is internal to it. By Newton's second law,
$$
T=1\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=2\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
The value $1\,\mathrm{N}$ is the tension in the coupling between the two cars, which accelerates only the second car.
