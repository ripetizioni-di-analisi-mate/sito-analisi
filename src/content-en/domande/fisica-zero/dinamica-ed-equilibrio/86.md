To start a piano at rest on the floor moving, a horizontal push slightly above $400\,\mathrm{N}$ is needed; once it is moving, $300\,\mathrm{N}$ is enough to keep it at constant velocity. Which statement about the coefficients of friction is true?

- $\mu_s<\mu_d$
- $\mu_s>\mu_d$
- $\mu_s=\mu_d$
- They cannot be compared without the mass

## Explanation

The piano starts to move when the push exceeds the maximum static friction, so $\mu_sN=400\,\mathrm{N}$. At constant velocity the push balances kinetic friction, so $\mu_dN=300\,\mathrm{N}$. The normal force $N$ is the same in both cases, so the ratio of the coefficients is $\dfrac{400}{300}$ and $\mu_s>\mu_d$. The mass is not needed, because it enters both relations in the same way.
