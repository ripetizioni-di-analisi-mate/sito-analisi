A freight elevator lifts a load at a constant velocity of $0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$; the load hangs from a single vertical cable. How does the tension in the cable compare with the weight of the load?

- It equals the weight
- It is greater than the weight, because the load is rising
- It is less than the weight
- It is zero, because the velocity is constant

## Explanation

The load rises at constant velocity, so its acceleration is zero. The forces on the load are the tension upward and the weight downward, so their sum is zero and $T=mg$. The fact that the load rises concerns its velocity, while the forces determine the acceleration. A tension greater than the weight would be needed only to make the upward speed increase.
