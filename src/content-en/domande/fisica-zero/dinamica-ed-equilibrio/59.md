A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ bucket of water is pulled up from the bottom of a well with a rope, and it accelerates upward at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the tension in the rope?

- $39.05\,\mathrm{N}$
- $59.05\,\mathrm{N}$
- $49.05\,\mathrm{N}$
- $10\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Take the axis upward, so Newton's second law for the bucket gives $T-mg=ma$. Therefore
$$
T=m(g+a)=5\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot(9.81+2)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=59.05\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
The tension exceeds the weight, $49.05\,\mathrm{N}$, because the net force points upward. The value $39.05\,\mathrm{N}$ corresponds to a downward acceleration.
