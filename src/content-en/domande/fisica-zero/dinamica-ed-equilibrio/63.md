A cart moves along a straight track and its velocity $v$, in meters per second, changes with time $t$, in seconds, as shown in the figure. In which time interval is the net force on the cart zero?

::grafico{curve="t,12-t-abs(t-2)-2*abs(t-5),0,6" nomi="t,v" legenda=0 segmenti="2,0,2,4;5,0,5,4" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7 ymin=-0.8 ymax=6 titolo="Velocity of the cart: $t$ in seconds, $v$ in meters per second"}

- Between $0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$
- Between $5\,\mathrm{s}$ and $6\,\mathrm{s}$
- Between $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$
- In no interval, because the cart is always moving

## Explanation

Between $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$ the graph is a horizontal segment, so the velocity stays at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the acceleration is zero. By Newton's second law the net force is zero when the acceleration is zero, so in that interval the forces on the cart balance. Between $0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity increases and between $5\,\mathrm{s}$ and $6\,\mathrm{s}$ it decreases, so in those intervals the net force is nonzero. A moving body therefore has zero net force when its velocity stays constant.
