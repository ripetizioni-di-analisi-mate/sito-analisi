A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ picture hangs at rest from two identical vertical wires, attached symmetrically to the two sides of the frame, so that the two wires have the same tension. Using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the tension in each wire?

- $39.24\,\mathrm{N}$
- $78.48\,\mathrm{N}$
- $19.62\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The weight acts downward on the picture and the two tensions act upward. The picture is at rest, so taking upward as positive gives $2T-mg=0$. Therefore
$$
T=\frac{mg}{2}=\frac{4\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{2}=19.62\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
The value $39.24\,\mathrm{N}$ is the weight of the whole picture, which would be the tension of a single wire. The value $2\,\mathrm{N}$ comes from dividing the mass by two without multiplying it by $g$.
