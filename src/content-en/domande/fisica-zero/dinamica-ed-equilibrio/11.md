A hockey puck slides across an ice rink at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$; friction and air resistance are negligible and the vertical forces balance. What is the speed of the puck after $3\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The vertical forces balance and no horizontal force acts, so the net force on the puck is zero. By **Newton's first law**, in an inertial frame a body with zero net force keeps its velocity. Therefore after $3\,\mathrm{s}$ the puck is still moving at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ corresponds instead to the distance in meters covered in those $3\,\mathrm{s}$.
