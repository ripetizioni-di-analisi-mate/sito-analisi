A $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ crate of fruit is pulled across a horizontal floor with a horizontal force of $50\,\mathrm{N}$ and slides in the direction of the force, as shown in the figure. With $\mu_d=0.3$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the acceleration of the crate?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3.5,-0.5 3.5,-0.5" poligoni="-0.6,-0.5 0.6,-0.5 0.6,0.5 -0.6,0.5" vettori="0.6,0,3.1,0,F = 50 N;-0.6,-0.3,-2.1,-0.3,Fd;0,0.5,0,2.5,N;0,-0.5,0,-2.5,P" xmin=-3.8 xmax=4.4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 titolo="Forces on the crate sliding to the right"}

- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Vertically the crate has zero acceleration, so $N=mg=100\,\mathrm{N}$ and the kinetic friction is $F_d=0.3\cdot100\,\mathrm{N}=30\,\mathrm{N}$, opposite to the sliding. With the axis in the direction of motion, Newton's second law gives
$$
50\,\mathrm{N}-30\,\mathrm{N}=10\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot a.
$$
Therefore $a=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The value $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ comes from adding friction to the pull instead of subtracting it.
