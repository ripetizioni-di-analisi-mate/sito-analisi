A horse pulls a cart and, by Newton's third law, the cart pulls the horse with a force of equal magnitude and opposite direction. Why can the cart accelerate?

- The horse pulls with a force larger than the one it receives from the cart
- The two forces act on different bodies, and on the cart the pull exceeds the resistive forces
- The two forces cancel and the cart moves by inertia
- The force of the cart on the horse appears an instant after the force of the horse

## Explanation

The force of the horse on the cart acts on the cart, while the force of the cart on the horse acts on the horse. The two forces do not appear in the same diagram, so they do not cancel each other. The diagram of the cart contains the pull of the horse and the resistive forces of the road. If the pull exceeds the resistive forces, the net force on the cart points forward, and therefore the cart accelerates.
