Two small metal spheres on insulating stands have charges $+6\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. They are brought into contact and then separated, with no exchange with other bodies. In the end, the first sphere has charge $+1\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. What is the charge of the second?

- $-3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $+5\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $+7\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $+3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$

## Explanation

The spheres exchange charge only with each other, so their total charge is conserved. Initially, the total charge is $+6\,\mathrm{\mu C}-2\,\mathrm{\mu C}=+4\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. At the end the sum must still be $+4\,\mathrm{\mu C}$, so the second sphere has charge $+4\,\mathrm{\mu C}-1\,\mathrm{\mu C}=+3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$.
