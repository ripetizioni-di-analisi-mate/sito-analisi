In the circuit shown, an ideal source powers the resistor $R$, connected between points $P$ and $Q$. The positive terminal of the source is the one marked with the $+$ sign. In which direction does conventional current pass through the resistor?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,1.3;0,1.7 0,3 2,3;3,3 5,3 5,0 0,0;-0.4,1.3 0.4,1.3;-0.2,1.7 0.2,1.7;2,2.7 3,2.7 3,3.3 2,3.3 2,2.7" punti="2,3,pieno,P;3,3,pieno,Q" etichette="-0.8,1.15,+;-0.8,1.85,−;2.35,3.65,R" xmin=-1.3 xmax=5.8 ymin=-0.6 ymax=4.5 titolo="Ideal source with the positive terminal at the bottom and resistor $R$ between points $P$ and $Q$"}

- From $P$ to $Q$
- Alternately in both directions
- It depends on the length of the wires
- From $Q$ to $P$

## Explanation

In the external circuit, conventional current flows from the positive terminal of the source to the negative terminal. The positive terminal is at the bottom, so the current flows down the left wire, to the right along the bottom wire, and up the right wire. It therefore reaches the resistor on the $Q$ side and passes through it from $Q$ to $P$ before returning to the negative terminal.
