In a hair dryer, the heating element is in a section of the circuit with no branches. In the wire entering the element, the current is $4\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the current in the wire leaving it?

- Less than $4\,\mathrm{A}$, because part of it turns into heat
- $0\,\mathrm{A}$, because the charge stays in the element
- $4\,\mathrm{A}$, the same as in the incoming wire
- More than $4\,\mathrm{A}$, because the element supplies energy

## Explanation

In the steady state, the amount of charge entering the element during a time interval equals the amount leaving it during the same interval. In a section with no branches, the current therefore has the same value before and after the component, namely $4\,\mathrm{A}$. The element receives electrical energy and converts it to internal energy, while the charge passing through it stays the same.
