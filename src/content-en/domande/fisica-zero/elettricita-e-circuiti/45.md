When it is on, the tungsten filament of an incandescent bulb reaches a much higher temperature than when it is off. If voltage and current measurements taken with the filament cold and with it lit are compared, which assumption of Ohm's first law is not satisfied?

- Constant voltage of the source
- Constant temperature of the conductor
- Negligible resistance of the wires
- Nonzero current

## Explanation

Ohm's first law holds for an ohmic conductor held at constant temperature over the operating range considered. The filament goes from room temperature to a much higher temperature, so the assumption of **constant temperature** is not satisfied. Therefore the ratio of voltage to current measured with the filament cold can differ from the ratio measured with it lit.
