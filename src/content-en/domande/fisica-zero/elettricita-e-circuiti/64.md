A lamp is on, plugged into a power strip connected to a line that is treated as an ideal constant-voltage source. A fan, connected in parallel with the lamp on the same power strip, is then switched on. What happens to the current in the lamp?

- It decreases, because the fan takes part of it
- It increases
- It becomes zero
- It stays the same

## Explanation

The lamp and the fan are in parallel, so the voltage across the lamp is the same as before. The resistance of the lamp does not change, so the current in the lamp, $I=\dfrac{V}{R}$, **stays the same**. What increases is the total current in the power strip cable, which is the sum of the currents in the two branches.
