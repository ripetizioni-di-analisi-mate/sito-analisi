In the circuit of a toy car, an ideal battery powers two resistors in series, of $3\,\Omega$ and $9\,\Omega$; the $3\,\Omega$ resistor is connected to the positive terminal. The two resistors are swapped. What happens to the current?

- It increases
- It decreases
- It reverses direction
- It stays the same

## Explanation

The equivalent resistance of the series combination is the sum $3\,\Omega+9\,\Omega=12\,\Omega$, which does not depend on the order of the resistors. The ideal battery maintains the same voltage, so the current $I=\dfrac{V}{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}$ **stays the same**. The direction of the current is also unchanged, because it is set by the position of the battery terminals.
