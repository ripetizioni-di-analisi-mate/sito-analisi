Two parallel branches across $12\,\mathrm{V}$ contain, respectively, one $6\,\Omega$ resistor and two $3\,\Omega$ resistors in series. How do the branch currents compare?

- Only the single-resistor branch conducts.
- Current in the two-resistor branch is half as large.
- They are equal at $2\,\mathrm{A}$ each.
- Current in the two-resistor branch is twice as large.

## Explanation

Two $3\,\Omega$ resistors in series give $6\,\Omega$. The branch resistances and voltages match, so each current is $\dfrac{12}{6}=2$ amperes.
