A charge counter reads $5\,\mathrm{C}$ at $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $17\,\mathrm{C}$ at $6\,\mathrm{s}$. Why does dividing $17\,\mathrm{C}$ by $6\,\mathrm{s}$ not necessarily give the mean current between the two readings?

- Average current requires a known resistance.
- The final counter measures energy instead of charge.
- Both increments are needed, giving a mean of $3\,\mathrm{A}$.
- Times in seconds must always be divided by sixty.

## Explanation

Within the stated interval $17-5=12$ coulombs pass in $6-2=4$ seconds. Their ratio is $3\,\mathrm{A}$.
