On a component of a power supply, a current of $1\,\mathrm{A}$ is measured with $2\,\mathrm{V}$ across it, and a current of $1.5\,\mathrm{A}$ with $6\,\mathrm{V}$ across it. Which statement is true?

- The component is ohmic with a resistance of $2\,\Omega$
- The component is ohmic with a resistance of $4\,\Omega$
- The component is ohmic with a resistance of $3\,\Omega$
- The component does not follow Ohm's law over this range

## Explanation

In the first measurement, the ratio of voltage to current is $\dfrac{2\,\mathrm{V}}{1\,\mathrm{A}}=2\,\Omega$; in the second, it is $\dfrac{6\,\mathrm{V}}{1.5\,\mathrm{A}}=4\,\Omega$. The two ratios differ, so the resistance is not constant. Therefore the component is **not an ohmic conductor** over the range considered, and no single resistance value describes both measurements.
