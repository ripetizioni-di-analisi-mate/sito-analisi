The heating element of an electric water heater has a resistance of $23\,\Omega$ and is supplied with $230\,\mathrm{V}$. Taking the resistance to be constant, what power does the water heater absorb?

- $10\,\mathrm{W}$
- $52\,900\,\mathrm{W}$
- $2300\,\mathrm{W}$
- $230\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The voltage and the resistance are known, so we use the form $P_e=\dfrac{V^2}{R}$, which holds for an ohmic resistor. Therefore
$$
P_e=\frac{(230\,\mathrm{V})^2}{23\,\Omega}=\frac{52\,900}{23}\,\mathrm{W}=2300\,\mathrm{W}.
$$
The same result follows from first calculating the current, $I=\dfrac{230\,\mathrm{V}}{23\,\Omega}=10\,\mathrm{A}$, and then $P_e=VI$.
