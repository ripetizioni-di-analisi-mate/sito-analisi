A charger cable contains copper wires wrapped in a plastic sheath and ends in a plug with metal prongs. Which part of the cable acts as an insulator?

- The copper wires
- The plastic sheath
- The metal prongs of the plug
- None, because a cable must conduct in every part

## Explanation

A conductor allows charges to move through the material, while an insulator impedes this movement. The copper and the prongs of the plug are metals, so they are conductors, since electrons can move in metals. The **plastic sheath** is an insulator instead, and by wrapping the wires it keeps the charges from passing to the outside.
