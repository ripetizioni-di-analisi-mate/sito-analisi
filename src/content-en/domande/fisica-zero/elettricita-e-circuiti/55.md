Two identical series resistors are powered by an ideal constant-voltage battery. An ideal switch short-circuits the first when closed. What happens to the power dissipated in the second resistor, which stays in the current path?

- It becomes twice the original value.
- It becomes four times the original value.
- It halves.
- It falls to zero because the switch is closed.

## Explanation

Before closure the two equal resistors share the voltage equally. Afterwards the second receives the full battery voltage. At fixed resistance power is proportional to voltage squared, so it quadruples.
