In a soldering iron, the tip is heated by two resistors connected in series to an ideal source, one of $5\,\Omega$ and the other of $20\,\Omega$. Across which of the two is the voltage larger?

- The $20\,\Omega$ resistor
- The $5\,\Omega$ resistor
- They have the same voltage
- The one connected to the positive terminal

## Explanation

The two resistors are in series, so they carry the same current $I$. By Ohm's law, the voltage across each one is $V=RI$. At the same current, the voltage is larger where the resistance is larger, so the larger voltage is across the $20\,\Omega$ resistor.
