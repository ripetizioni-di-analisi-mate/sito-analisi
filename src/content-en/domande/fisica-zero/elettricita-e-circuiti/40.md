An ohmic resistor is disconnected and measures $0\,\mathrm{V}$ and $0\,\mathrm{A}$. Why do these data alone fail to determine its resistance?

- Ohm’s law requires alternating current.
- The two zero readings prove a resistance of $1\,\Omega$.
- The origin lies on every ohmic resistor’s line.
- The two zero readings prove zero resistance.

## Explanation

The relation $V=RI$ holds with both sides zero for any finite resistance. Finding the slope requires at least one measurement at nonzero current.
