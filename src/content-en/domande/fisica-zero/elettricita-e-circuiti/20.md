A camera battery is labeled with a capacity of $2000\,\mathrm{mAh}$, which is the charge that passes when the battery supplies a constant current of $2000\,\mathrm{mA}$ for one hour. What is this charge in coulombs?

- $7\,200\,000\,\mathrm{C}$
- $7200\,\mathrm{C}$
- $120\,\mathrm{C}$
- $2000\,\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

At constant current, the charge that passes is $q=I\Delta t$, with the current in amperes and the time in seconds. The current is $2000\,\mathrm{mA}=2\,\mathrm{A}$, and one hour lasts $3600\,\mathrm{s}$. Therefore $q=2\,\mathrm{A}\cdot3600\,\mathrm{s}=7200\,\mathrm{C}$. The milliampere-hour is thus a unit of charge, because it is a current multiplied by a time.
