In the lighting circuit of a dollhouse, an ideal $18\,\mathrm{V}$ source powers two resistors in series, of $3\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$. What power does the $6\,\Omega$ resistor absorb?

- $54\,\mathrm{W}$
- $36\,\mathrm{W}$
- $12\,\mathrm{W}$
- $24\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The equivalent resistance of the series combination is $3\,\Omega+6\,\Omega=9\,\Omega$, so the current is $I=\dfrac{18\,\mathrm{V}}{9\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm{A}$. The $6\,\Omega$ resistor carries this current, so it absorbs
$$
P_e=RI^2=6\,\Omega\cdot(2\,\mathrm{A})^2=24\,\mathrm{W}.
$$
The voltage across this resistor is $12\,\mathrm{V}$, and indeed $12\,\mathrm{V}\cdot2\,\mathrm{A}=24\,\mathrm{W}$ as well.
