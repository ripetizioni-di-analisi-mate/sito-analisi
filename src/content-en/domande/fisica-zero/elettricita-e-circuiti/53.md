Two resistors share an intermediate node X. A third branch leaving X carries nonzero current. Why can the first two not be combined as series resistors?

- The two resistors would need equal voltage drops to be in series.
- The third branch prevents the first two from forming a single path carrying one common current.
- The node would have to be at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
- Any two resistors near one node are in parallel.

## Explanation

Series reduction requires an intermediate connection without branching. At X some current flows into the third branch, so the current is redistributed.
