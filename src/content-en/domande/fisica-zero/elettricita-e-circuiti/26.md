A lightning bolt transfers a charge of $15\,\mathrm{C}$ to the ground in $0.5\,\mathrm{ms}$ (milliseconds). What is the average current during the discharge?

- $30\,\mathrm{A}$
- $7.5\,\mathrm{A}$
- $3000\,\mathrm{A}$
- $30\,000\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

The prefix milli means one thousandth, so $0.5\,\mathrm{ms}=0.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}=5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$. The average current is the charge divided by the time. Therefore
$$
I=\frac{15\,\mathrm{C}}{5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}}=30\,000\,\mathrm{A}.
$$
The charge transferred is modest, but it passes in a very short time, which is why the average current is very large.
