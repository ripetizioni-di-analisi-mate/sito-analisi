Two extension cords are used on a construction site. In $10\,\mathrm{s}$, $20\,\mathrm{C}$ pass through a cross section of cord A, while in $20\,\mathrm{s}$, $30\,\mathrm{C}$ pass through a cross section of cord B. In which cord is the average current greater?

- In cord B
- The two currents are equal
- In cord A
- It cannot be determined without knowing the voltage

## Explanation

The average current is the charge divided by the time. In cord A it is $\dfrac{20\,\mathrm{C}}{10\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{A}$, and in cord B it is $\dfrac{30\,\mathrm{C}}{20\,\mathrm{s}}=1.5\,\mathrm{A}$. The current is therefore greater **in cord A**. More charge passes through B, but over twice the time, so the charge per second is smaller.
