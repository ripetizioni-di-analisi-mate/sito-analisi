A resistor is powered by an ideal constant-current source of $1\,\mathrm{A}$. A second identical resistor is connected in parallel. How does the voltage across the pair change?

- It halves.
- It doubles.
- It falls to zero.
- It is unchanged because every source fixes its voltage.

## Explanation

This source fixes total current. The parallel connection halves the equivalent resistance, so it also halves the voltage $IR$.
