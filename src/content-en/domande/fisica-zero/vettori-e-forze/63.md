A uniform spring hangs at rest from the ceiling. Its own weight is appreciable and nothing is attached to its lower end. Imagine dividing it into four sections of equal mass. At which point is the tension supporting the material below greatest?

- Halfway down the spring, because the masses above and below are equal.
- At the free lower end, because it is farthest from the support.
- It is equal everywhere because the four sections belong to one spring.
- At the upper end of the first section, next to the ceiling.

## Explanation

Imagine isolating all the spring material below the point in question. The local tension balances the weight of that material. At the top the support carries all four sections, halfway down it carries two, and at the free end it carries none.
