Two curtain springs, one with $k_1=200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the other with $k_2=400\,\mathrm{N/m}$, are stretched so that each exerts the same spring force of $6\,\mathrm{N}$. Both obey Hooke's law. Which statement is correct?

- The spring with $k_1=200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ stretches more
- The spring with $k_2=400\,\mathrm{N/m}$ stretches more
- The two springs have the same extension, because the force is the same
- It cannot be determined without knowing the natural lengths

## Explanation

By Hooke's law, the extension is $\Delta\ell=\dfrac{F_e}{k}$. For the same force, therefore, the extension is larger for the spring with the smaller spring constant. Indeed, $\Delta\ell_1=\dfrac{6}{200}\,\mathrm{m}=3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\Delta\ell_2=\dfrac{6}{400}\,\mathrm{m}=1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The natural lengths do not enter the calculation of the extension.
