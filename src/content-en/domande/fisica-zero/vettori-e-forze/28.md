Two cyclists leave the same intersection on straight roads. The first rides $4\,\mathrm{km}$ north and then $3\,\mathrm{km}$ east; the second rides $3\,\mathrm{km}$ east first and then $4\,\mathrm{km}$ north. Which statement about their end points is correct?

- They end at different points, both $5\,\mathrm{km}$ from the intersection
- They end at the same point, $5\,\mathrm{km}$ from the intersection
- They end at the same point, $7\,\mathrm{km}$ from the intersection
- The first ends farther from the intersection than the second

## Explanation

For both cyclists the overall displacement consists of $3\,\mathrm{km}$ east and $4\,\mathrm{km}$ north, because the order in which the components are added does not change the result. Hence the two overall displacements are the same vector, and the cyclists end at the **same point**. The distance of that point from the intersection is $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}\,\mathrm{km}=5\,\mathrm{km}$, while $7\,\mathrm{km}$ is the distance each of them traveled.
