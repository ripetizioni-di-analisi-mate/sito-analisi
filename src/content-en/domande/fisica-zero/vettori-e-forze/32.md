A bee leaves its hive and flies at a constant height, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ north and then $2\,\mathrm{m}$ east. Where is it relative to the hive at the end of the two legs?

- To the northeast, $4\,\mathrm{m}$ away
- To the northwest, about $2.8\,\mathrm{m}$ away
- To the east, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ away
- To the northeast, about $2.8\,\mathrm{m}$ away

## Explanation

By the tip-to-tail rule, the sum runs from the hive to the tip of the second displacement, and it consists of $2\,\mathrm{m}$ north and $2\,\mathrm{m}$ east. The two parts are equal, so the sum makes the same angle with north and with east and points **northeast**. The two displacements are perpendicular, so the magnitude of the sum is
$$
R=\sqrt{2^2+2^2}\,\mathrm{m}=\sqrt{8}\,\mathrm{m}\approx2.8\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The value $4\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance traveled.
