The spring of a door stopper, with a natural length of $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, is stretched to $14\,\mathrm{cm}$. To calculate the spring force, a student writes $F_e=k\cdot0.14\,\mathrm{m}$. What mistake has the student made?

- Converting centimeters to meters incorrectly
- Giving the force the wrong sense
- There is no mistake
- Using the final length instead of the extension

## Explanation

Hooke's law involves the **extension** $\Delta\ell=\ell-\ell_0$, that is, the change in length. Here $\Delta\ell=14\,\mathrm{cm}-10\,\mathrm{cm}=4\,\mathrm{cm}=0.04\,\mathrm{m}$. The student used $0.14\,\mathrm{m}$, which is the final length of the spring, while the conversion to meters is correct. The correct expression is therefore $F_e=k\cdot0.04\,\mathrm{m}$.
