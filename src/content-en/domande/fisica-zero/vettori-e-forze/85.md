The spring of a force gauge obeys Hooke's law only for forces up to $10\,\mathrm{N}$, and its spring constant is $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$. A student computes the extension produced by a force of $30\,\mathrm{N}$ using $\Delta\ell=\dfrac{F_e}{k}$ and finds $15\,\mathrm{cm}$. Which statement is correct?

- The result is reliable, because Hooke's law holds for any force
- The result is unreliable, because $30\,\mathrm{N}$ lies outside the range where the law holds
- The result is reliable, but it should be written as $15\,\mathrm{m}$
- The extension is $5\,\mathrm{cm}$, because the limiting value of $10\,\mathrm{N}$ is used in the formula

## Explanation

Hooke's law is based on experiments and holds in the range where the deformation is elastic and proportional to the force. For this spring that range goes up to $10\,\mathrm{N}$, so a force of $30\,\mathrm{N}$ lies outside the **range of validity**. Therefore the calculation $\dfrac{30\,\mathrm{N}}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}}=0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ cannot predict the actual extension.
