The graph shows the magnitude of the spring force of a scale spring as a function of its extension, measured in centimeters. What is the spring constant of the spring?

::grafico{f="0.5*x" xmin=0 xmax=12 ymin=0 ymax=7 punti="8,4,pieno" segmenti="8,0,8,4;0,4,8,4" nomi="Δℓ (cm),F (N)" legenda=0 titolo="Spring force as a function of extension; the highlighted point has $\Delta\ell=8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $F_e=4\,\mathrm{N}$."}

- $50\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$

## Explanation

The graph is a straight line through the origin, so within the measured range the spring obeys Hooke's law. The highlighted point has $\Delta\ell=8\,\mathrm{cm}=0.08\,\mathrm{m}$ and $F_e=4\,\mathrm{N}$. Therefore
$$
k=\frac{4\,\mathrm{N}}{0.08\,\mathrm{m}}=50\,\mathrm{N/m}.
$$
The number $0.5$ is obtained by dividing by the extension in centimeters, and it corresponds to $0.5\,\mathrm{N/cm}$.
