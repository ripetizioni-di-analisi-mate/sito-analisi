A student builds a force gauge from a spring hanging from a stand, a pointer attached to its lower end, and a card next to it on which to mark the scale. How is a scale in newtons obtained?

- The natural length of the spring is measured and the value in centimeters is written next to the pointer
- Objects of known weight are hung one at a time, and for each one the position of the pointer is marked with the value of the weight in newtons
- Objects of different mass are hung and their mass in kilograms is written next to each mark
- Marks $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart are drawn and each interval is given the value $1\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

A force gauge is calibrated by comparing its readings with known forces. The weight of each object is a known force, so each mark made this way corresponds to a definite value in newtons. A length in centimeters and a mass in kilograms are not forces, so they do not give a scale in newtons. Marks $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart labeled $1\,\mathrm{N}$ do not come from any comparison with known forces, so there is no way to know whether each interval really corresponds to $1\,\mathrm{N}$.
