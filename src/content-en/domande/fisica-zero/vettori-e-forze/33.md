A motorboat travels four straight legs on a lake. Which route brings it back exactly to its starting point?

- $3\,\mathrm{km}$ north, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ east, $3\,\mathrm{km}$ north, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ west
- $3\,\mathrm{km}$ north, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ east, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ south, $3\,\mathrm{km}$ west
- $3\,\mathrm{km}$ north, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ east, $3\,\mathrm{km}$ south, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ west
- $3\,\mathrm{km}$ north, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ east, $3\,\mathrm{km}$ south, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ east

## Explanation

The boat returns to its starting point when the sum of the four displacements is the zero vector, that is, when both the sum of the north-south components and the sum of the east-west components are zero. On the route $3\,\mathrm{km}$ north, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ east, $3\,\mathrm{km}$ south, $4\,\mathrm{km}$ west, the north and south legs have opposite components, and so do the east and west legs. On the other routes the boat ends $6\,\mathrm{km}$ north, or $1\,\mathrm{km}$ south and $1\,\mathrm{km}$ east, or $8\,\mathrm{km}$ east of its starting point.
