A ferry undergoes a displacement $\vec{A}$ of $4\,\mathrm{km}$ east and then a displacement $\vec{B}$ of $3\,\mathrm{km}$ north. To construct the sum $\vec{A}+\vec{B}$ on paper with the tip-to-tail rule, the arrow of $\vec{B}$ is drawn starting from the tip of $\vec{A}$. Which features of $\vec{B}$ are preserved in this transfer?

- Only the magnitude, while the direction is lined up with that of $\vec{A}$
- Only the direction and the sense, while the length is adjusted to fit the drawing
- The magnitude, the direction, and the sense
- The magnitude and the direction, while the sense is chosen so as to close the triangle

## Explanation

In the tip-to-tail rule the transferred vector remains the same vector, so it keeps its **magnitude, direction, and sense**. Therefore the arrow of $\vec{B}$ starts at the tip of $\vec{A}$, has the length that represents $3\,\mathrm{km}$ on the chosen scale, and points north. If any of these features were changed, the arrow would represent a different displacement, and the sum would no longer describe the ferry's trip.
