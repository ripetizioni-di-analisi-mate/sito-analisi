At the same length, a spring needs $5\,\mathrm{N}$ during slow loading and $4\,\mathrm{N}$ during slow unloading. The difference exceeds the uncertainty. What does the comparison reveal?

- One stiffness and one natural length can give two forces at the same length.
- The force also depends on the loading history, so one force-length line cannot describe both trials.
- The mean of the two forces exactly reproduces both readings.
- Stiffness must be zero because the length is the same.

## Explanation

Hooke's law with fixed parameters assigns a single force to each length. Two distinct forces at the same length show an effect of the deformation history.
