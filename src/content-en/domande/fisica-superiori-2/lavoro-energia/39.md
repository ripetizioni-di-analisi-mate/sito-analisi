A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ block enters a rough horizontal section $4\,\mathrm m$ long. A constant resistance of magnitude $8\,\mathrm N$ is the only force doing work. What minimum entry speed lets the block reach the end exactly as it stops?

- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Friction removes $8\,\mathrm J$ for each metre of travel. The block stops at the end if it starts with $32\,\mathrm J$ of kinetic energy. For $4\,\mathrm{kg}$, the equation becomes $2v_i^2=32$ in SI units. A lower entry speed would make the block stop earlier. At the threshold, the final kinetic energy is exactly zero.

$$
v_i=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot8\cdot4}{4}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
