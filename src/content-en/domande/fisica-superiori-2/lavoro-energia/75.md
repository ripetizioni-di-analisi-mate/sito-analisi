An object passes two points at the same height under gravity and the reaction of a fixed frictionless track alone. How do the speeds compare at those points?

- They always depend on the local track shape
- The second is always greater
- One must be zero
- They are equal

## Explanation

The reaction is perpendicular to the displacement, so $K+U_g$ is conserved. At the two points, equal heights give equal potential energies. Kinetic energy is the same and, for constant mass, so are the speeds. Along a curved track, the two velocities can point in different directions.
