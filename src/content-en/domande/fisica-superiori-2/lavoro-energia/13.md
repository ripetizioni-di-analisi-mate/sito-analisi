A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart starts at rest and descends a smooth track with a vertical drop of $50\,\mathrm m$. It then travels $10\,\mathrm m$ along a horizontal section against a constant resistance of $40\,\mathrm N$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is its speed at the end of the horizontal section?

- $10\sqrt6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $10\sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $10\sqrt{14}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The descent leaves $1000\,\mathrm J$ of kinetic energy at the start of the horizontal section. The resistance removes $400\,\mathrm J$ by the end. This leaves $600\,\mathrm J$, so the cart completes the section. For the given mass, the numerical value of the kinetic energy equals the squared speed in SI units.

$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{2(1000-400)}{2}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=10\sqrt6\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
