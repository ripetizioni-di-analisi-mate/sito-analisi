An object of mass $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ changes speed from $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $13\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the net work done by the forces?

- $-399\,\mathrm{J}$
- $507\,\mathrm{J}$
- $399\,\mathrm{J}$
- $615\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Use the change in kinetic energy for net work. Energy is $108\,\mathrm{J}$ at the start and $507\,\mathrm{J}$ at the end. Subtract in the order final minus initial. When speed decreases, the change is negative because the final energy is lower. Square each speed separately. Subtracting the two velocities first and then squaring would change the value and could give only a non-negative number.

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_i &= \frac12\cdot 6\cdot 6^2\,\mathrm{J}=108\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
K_f &= \frac12\cdot 6\cdot 13^2\,\mathrm{J}=507\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
W_\mathrm{tot} &= K_f-K_i=399\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$
