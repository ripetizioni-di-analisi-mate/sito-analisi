A motor draws $1000\,\mathrm J$ at $80\%$ efficiency. It transfers all its useful output as work to a $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass that rises by $4.60\,\mathrm m$. Gravity is the only other force doing work. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the net work on the mass?

- $800\,\mathrm J$
- $816\,\mathrm J$
- $616\,\mathrm J$
- $984\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The mass receives $800\,\mathrm J$ of work from the motor because efficiency applies to the input energy. Gravity does $-184\,\mathrm J$ of work because the displacement is upwards. Adding the two contributions gives $616\,\mathrm J$, which equals the increase in kinetic energy. The question concerns work on the mass, so losses inside the motor stay outside its balance.

$$
W_{\mathrm{tot}}=(0.80\cdot1000-4\cdot10\cdot4.60)\,\mathrm J=616\,\mathrm J.
$$
