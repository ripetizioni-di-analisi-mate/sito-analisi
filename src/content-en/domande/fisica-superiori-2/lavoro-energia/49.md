Lowering a load releases $200\,\mathrm J$ of gravitational energy. A store makes $60\%$ of it available for the next lift. The next lift needs $300\,\mathrm J$ of useful work. A motor at $75\%$ efficiency supplies the remaining work. How much energy does the motor draw?

- $240\,\mathrm J$
- $180\,\mathrm J$
- $135\,\mathrm J$
- $400\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The store supplies $120\,\mathrm J$ to the lift. That leaves $180\,\mathrm J$ of useful work to obtain from the motor. The $180\,\mathrm J$ is the motor's output, so it must be divided by the efficiency. The input is $240\,\mathrm J$. The motor efficiency applies only to the remaining work.

$$
E_i=\frac{300-0.60\cdot200}{0.75}\,\mathrm J=240\,\mathrm J.
$$
