A pump lifts $120\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at constant speed to a tank $5.0\,\mathrm m$ higher, in $20\,\mathrm s$. In the same interval the pump draws an average power of $400\,\mathrm W$ from the mains. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the efficiency of the pump?

- $0.15$
- $0.75$
- $1.33$
- $0.075$

## Explanation

The useful work is the increase in gravitational potential energy of the water, $mgh=120\cdot10\cdot5.0\,\mathrm J=6000\,\mathrm J$. In $20\,\mathrm s$ the pump draws $400\cdot20\,\mathrm J=8000\,\mathrm J$. The efficiency is the ratio of the useful energy to the energy drawn in the same interval, so
$$
\eta=\frac{6000}{8000}=0.75.
$$
The value $1.33$ is the inverse ratio, which exceeds one and therefore cannot be an efficiency. The value $0.15$ comes from using the weight $mg$ instead of the work $mgh$.
