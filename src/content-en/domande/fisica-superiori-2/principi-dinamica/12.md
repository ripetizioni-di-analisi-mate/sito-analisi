An ideal spring with spring constant $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is displaced $0.06\,\mathrm{m}$ from its natural length in the linear range. Find the magnitude of the spring force.

- $-12\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6\,\mathrm{N}$
- $24\,\mathrm{N}$
- $12\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Hooke’s law holds in the linear range. The minus sign shows restoring action: force points opposite displacement from natural length. Spring constant times the displacement magnitude gives $12\,\mathrm{N}$. Length units cancel, leaving newtons. On the graph the origin is the natural-length state and P is the stated displacement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_e &= -kx=-200(0.06)\,\mathrm{N}=-12\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
\lvert F_e\rvert &= 12\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
