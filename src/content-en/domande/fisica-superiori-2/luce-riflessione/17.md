Two pulses A and B are emitted at the same instant. In a vacuum, A’s path is $1.2\,\mathrm{km}$ longer than B’s. With $c=3\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, how much later does A arrive?

- $4\,\mathrm{\mu s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{\mu s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{\mu s}$
- $0.004\,\mathrm{\mu s}$

## Explanation

For two pulses emitted together, only the extra segment contributes to the delay. Both travel at the same speed in a vacuum. Convert $1.2\,\mathrm{km}$ to $1200\,\mathrm m$ and divide by $c$ to obtain the extra travel time for pulse A.

$$
\Delta t=\frac{1200}{3\cdot10^8}\,\mathrm s=4\,\mathrm{\mu s}.
$$
