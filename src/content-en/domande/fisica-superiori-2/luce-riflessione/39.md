A concave mirror forms a sharp image of a small source on a screen. The source is then placed where the screen was and the screen where the source was. In the paraxial approximation, what is observed on the screen?

- The image becomes virtual and the screen does not collect it.
- The reflected rays leave parallel to the axis and no image forms.
- A sharp, inverted image forms again.
- A sharp image forms, but it is upright.

## Explanation

Initially the source is at distance $p$ from the mirror and the real image at distance $q$, with $\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{q}=\dfrac{1}{f}$. After the swap the source is at distance $q$. Since the mirror equation is symmetric in $p$ and $q$, the new image forms at distance $p$, that is exactly where the screen now is, so the image on the screen is sharp again. The magnification becomes $M=-\dfrac{p}{q}$, which is still negative, so the image remains inverted. The modulus of $M$ becomes the reciprocal of the initial one, so an image that was reduced is now enlarged.
