A measured time includes a known detector delay in addition to light travel. How is the travel time alone found?

- $t_{\mathrm{travel}}=t_{\mathrm{measured}}+t_{\mathrm{delay}}$
- $t_{\mathrm{travel}}=2t_{\mathrm{measured}}$
- $t_{\mathrm{travel}}=t_{\mathrm{delay}}$
- $t_{\mathrm{travel}}=t_{\mathrm{measured}}-t_{\mathrm{delay}}$

## Explanation

Subtract the contribution that is not due to propagation. Travel time can then be multiplied by speed.

The circuit delay occurs after the pulse reaches the detector. For an echo, convert the corrected time to total path length and then halve that length to find the target distance.
