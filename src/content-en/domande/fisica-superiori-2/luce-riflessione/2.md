A pulse of light covers $1500\,\mathrm m$ in a vacuum. Use $c=3\cdot10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does it take? Choose the value closest to the result.

- $1\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $2.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s}$.

## Explanation

The path is in a vacuum and speed is constant. Time is distance divided by $c$, in metres divided by metres per second.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{d}{c} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(1500\right)}{\left(3\cdot10^{8}\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

The result is five microseconds. An outward and return path to a point at that distance would take twice as long.
