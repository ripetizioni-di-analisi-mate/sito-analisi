In two trials a pulse leaves a source and returns to it after reflection. The reflectors in the two trials are at distances of $450\,\mathrm m$ and $1350\,\mathrm m$ respectively. The paths are in a vacuum and $c=3\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the difference $t$ between the two flight times?

- $3\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$
- $6\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$
- $9\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$
- $12\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$

## Explanation

The second reflector is $900\,\mathrm m$ farther away. The pulse covers that extra distance on both the outward and the return paths. The path is thus $1800\,\mathrm m$ longer and the time increases by $\dfrac{1800}{3\cdot10^8}\,\mathrm s=6\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$. Comparing only the outward paths would halve the difference.
