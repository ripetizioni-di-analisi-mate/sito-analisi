In a horizontal launch without air resistance, from the same height, how does the magnitude of the coefficient of $x^2$ in the trajectory $y(x)$ change as the initial speed increases?

- The $x^2$ coefficient always doubles
- The trajectory becomes exactly horizontal
- The intercept changes but curvature remains fixed
- The magnitude of the $x^2$ coefficient is smaller

## Explanation

The expression $y=h-\frac{g}{2u_0^2}x^2$ shows that the quadratic coefficient is inversely proportional to $u_0^2$.

The coefficient has magnitude $\dfrac{g}{2u_0^2}$. Doubling the horizontal launch speed quadruples the denominator and makes that magnitude one quarter as large. At the same horizontal coordinate, the faster ball took less time and lost less height.
