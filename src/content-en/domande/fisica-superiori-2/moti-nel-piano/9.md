A probe moves at constant velocity. Over $2\,\mathrm s$ it travels from $(29;-14)$ to $(-7;34)$, with coordinates in metres. What is the magnitude of its velocity?

- $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $60\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $42\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Subtract the initial coordinates from the final ones to find displacement $(-36;48)\,\mathrm m$. Dividing both components by two seconds gives $(-18;24)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The velocity is constant, so its magnitude is $\sqrt{18^2+24^2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=30\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $60\,\mathrm m$ is the displacement magnitude and still has to be divided by the time.
