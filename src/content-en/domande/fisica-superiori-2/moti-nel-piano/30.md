A wheel with a single mark on its rim rotates at constant frequency. It is photographed twice per second, at regular intervals, and in every photograph the mark is in the same position. Which of these rotation frequencies is consistent with the observation?

- $1\,\mathrm{Hz}$
- $4\,\mathrm{Hz}$
- $3\,\mathrm{Hz}$
- $5\,\mathrm{Hz}$

## Explanation

Between two consecutive photographs $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm s$ elapses. The mark returns to the same position only if the wheel completes a whole number of revolutions in that interval. At frequency $f$ the number of revolutions in $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm s$ is $\dfrac{f}{2}$, so $f$ must be a multiple of $2\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Among the values offered, the only multiple of $2\,\mathrm{Hz}$ is $4\,\mathrm{Hz}$, which gives two whole revolutions between one photograph and the next. At $1$, $3$ or $5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ the wheel completes a whole number of revolutions plus half a revolution, so the mark would appear alternately in two opposite positions.
