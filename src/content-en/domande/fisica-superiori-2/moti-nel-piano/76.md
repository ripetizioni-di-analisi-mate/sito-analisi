Why can motion at constant speed have acceleration?

- The direction of the velocity vector can change
- Its magnitude necessarily changes
- The total distance is always zero
- The period is infinite

## Explanation

Acceleration measures change of the velocity vector. On a circle each new point has a different tangent.

For example, at opposite points on a circle the velocity vectors point in opposite directions along parallel tangents. Their magnitudes agree, but the vector difference is nonzero. Acceleration describes the change in the vector.
