Only one axis is reversed. How does the magnitude of a fixed vector change?

- It becomes negative
- It doubles
- It is unchanged
- It vanishes

## Explanation

The component along the reversed axis changes sign. Its square is unchanged, so the magnitude is unchanged.

If the $x$ axis is reversed, for example, the pair $(4;-2)$ becomes $(-4;-2)$. The squares of both components remain the same, so the physical length of the vector agrees in the two frames.
