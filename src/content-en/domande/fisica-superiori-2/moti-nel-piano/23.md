A ball launched horizontally from $5\,\mathrm{m}$ lands $6\,\mathrm{m}$ from the vertical through the launch point. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and negligible air, what was its initial speed?

- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

First find the one-second flight time from the height. Then divide the horizontal distance by the time shared by both components.

$$
u_0=\frac{6}{\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot5}{10}}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=6\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

From a height of $5\,\mathrm m$ the flight lasts one second because $\dfrac12gt^2=5\,\mathrm m$ then. The horizontal speed is constant during the flight, so the observed six metres are covered in one second.
