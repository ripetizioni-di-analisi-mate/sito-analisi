An athlete starts from rest, accelerates at $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $4\,\mathrm{s}$, then maintains the attained velocity for $3\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the final velocity?

- $10.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The athlete starts from rest and accelerates for $4\,\mathrm s$. At the end of the first stage the velocity is $1.5\cdot4=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The velocity stays constant during the second stage, so it keeps the same value. The final $3\,\mathrm s$ contribute to the distance travelled. Use that constant velocity to find the displacement in the final stage. Applying the initial acceleration there would make the velocity increase again.
