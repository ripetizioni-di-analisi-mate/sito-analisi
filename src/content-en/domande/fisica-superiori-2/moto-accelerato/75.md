At fixed constant deceleration, how does braking distance change if the initial speed doubles?

- It doubles
- It halves
- It is unchanged
- It quadruples

## Explanation

With a fixed magnitude of deceleration, braking distance is $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2b}$. Doubling the initial speed multiplies the numerator by four and leaves the denominator unchanged. On the velocity-time graph both the starting height and the braking time double, so the triangle's area quadruples. The comparison concerns the braking stage alone. It starts when the model makes the velocity decrease, after any reaction interval.
