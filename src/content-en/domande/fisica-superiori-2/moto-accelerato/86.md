A body moves along a straight line with constant acceleration. Its velocity is $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at time $1\,\mathrm s$ and $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at time $3\,\mathrm s$. What is its velocity at time $6\,\mathrm s$?

- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The acceleration is the slope of the velocity–time graph, so $a=\dfrac{2-(-2)}{3-1}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Between $3\,\mathrm s$ and $6\,\mathrm s$ there are $3\,\mathrm s$, so the velocity increases by $2\cdot3=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
v(6\,\mathrm s)=(2+6)\,\mathrm{m/s}=8\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

The second interval is longer than the first, so the increase in velocity is greater than the $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ of the first interval.
