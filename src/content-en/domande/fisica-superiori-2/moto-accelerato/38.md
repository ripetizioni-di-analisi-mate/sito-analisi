A test track allows $40\,\mathrm{m}$ to stop from $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with no reaction time. What constant acceleration magnitude stops the car exactly at the end?

- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

For braking alone, $v_f=0$ and $\Delta x=40\,\mathrm m$. With $b>0$ the magnitude of deceleration, the relation without time becomes $20^2=2b\cdot40$. Thus $b=5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The signed acceleration is $-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ if the initial direction is positive.
