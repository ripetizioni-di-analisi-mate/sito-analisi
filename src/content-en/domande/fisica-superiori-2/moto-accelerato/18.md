A trolley moves along a straight line with constant non-zero acceleration and reverses its direction of motion at $t=4\,\mathrm s$. What can be stated with certainty about the trolley at $t=3\,\mathrm s$ and $t=5\,\mathrm s$?

- It has the same velocity and different positions.
- It occupies the same position and has opposite velocities.
- It has opposite accelerations.
- It is at rest at both instants.

## Explanation

At $t=4\,\mathrm s$ the velocity is zero, so with constant acceleration $a$ the velocity is $v=a\,(t-4\,\mathrm s)$. It follows that $v(3\,\mathrm s)=-a\cdot1\,\mathrm s$ and $v(5\,\mathrm s)=a\cdot1\,\mathrm s$, that is, the two velocities are opposite. The displacement between $3\,\mathrm s$ and $5\,\mathrm s$ is the signed area under the velocity-time graph, made of two equal triangles, one on each side of the time axis. Since the two areas have the same modulus and opposite signs, the displacement is zero and the trolley occupies the same position at the two instants. The acceleration, on the other hand, is the same at the two instants, because it is constant.
