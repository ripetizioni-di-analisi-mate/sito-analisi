Two trials with the same cart give total stopping distances of $15\,\mathrm m$ at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $50\,\mathrm m$ at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The model is $d=\tau v+kv^2$, with the same $\tau$ and $k$ in both trials. What is the delay $\tau$ before braking begins?

- $0.50\,\mathrm s$
- $0.10\,\mathrm s$
- $1.50\,\mathrm s$
- $2.50\,\mathrm s$

## Explanation

Dividing by $v$ gives $\dfrac{d}{v}=\tau+kv$. The measured ratios are $1.5$ and $2.5\,\mathrm s$. A rise of one second per $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives $k=0.10\,\mathrm{s^2/m}$. From the first trial, $\tau=1.5-0.10\cdot10=0.50\,\mathrm s$.
