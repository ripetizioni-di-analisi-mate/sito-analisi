A car travels along a straight road. Over an interval of $5\,\mathrm{s}$ its average acceleration is $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and at the end of the interval its velocity is $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What was its velocity at the start of the interval?

- $-13\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $17\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The change in velocity is the average acceleration multiplied by the duration, so $\Delta v=(-3)\cdot5=-15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since $\Delta v=v_f-v_i$, the initial velocity is $v_i=v_f-\Delta v$.

$$
v_i=[2-(-15)]\,\mathrm{m/s}=17\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

The average acceleration is negative, so the car slows down from $17$ to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
