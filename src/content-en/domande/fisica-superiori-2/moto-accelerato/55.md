Why does $v_i=v_f$ give only zero average acceleration?

- An intermediate velocity is always negative
- Elapsed time must be zero
- Constant acceleration is always one metre per second squared
- Velocity may change between readings and return to its initial value

## Explanation

The initial and final velocities are equal, so their difference is zero. Between the readings the object can first speed up and then slow down. If it starts at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, reaches $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and returns to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the two changes cancel in the average over the full interval.
