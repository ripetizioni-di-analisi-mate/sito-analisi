A vehicle travels at $35\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with a constant acceleration of magnitude $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Before braking, it maintains its initial velocity through a reaction time of $1\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the total distance before it stops?

- $122.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $157.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $35\,\mathrm{m}$
- $280\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Choose the direction of travel as positive. Velocity decreases by $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second and reaches zero after $7\,\mathrm{s}$. During the reaction the velocity stays at $35\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the graph has a rectangle of area $35\,\mathrm{m}$. Add the braking triangle’s area of $122.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The entire duration includes reaction and braking, while the second phase covers less distance than it would at the initial speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_a &= \frac{v_0}{b}=\frac{35}{5}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 7\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
s_r &= v_0t_r=35\cdot 1\,\mathrm{m}=35\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
s_f &= \frac{35^2}{2\cdot 5}\,\mathrm{m}\simeq 122.5\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{tot}} &= s_r+s_f \simeq 157.5\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
