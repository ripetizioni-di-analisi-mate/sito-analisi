A trolley moves along a straight line with constant acceleration. At $t=0$ it is at $x=4\,\mathrm m$ with velocity $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$; at $t=2\,\mathrm s$ its velocity is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is its position at $t=2\,\mathrm s$?

- $4\,\mathrm m$
- $16\,\mathrm m$
- $0\,\mathrm m$
- $8\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

With constant acceleration the average velocity over an interval is the mean of the velocities at its ends, so it is $\dfrac{-2+6}{2}=2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The displacement over the $2\,\mathrm s$ is therefore $\Delta x=2\cdot2=4\,\mathrm m$. The final position is obtained by adding the displacement to the initial position.

$$
x(2\,\mathrm s)=(4+4)\,\mathrm m=8\,\mathrm m.
$$

The average velocity already accounts for the first part of the motion, in which the trolley moves in the negative direction.
