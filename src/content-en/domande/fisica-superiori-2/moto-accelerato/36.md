A vehicle goes from $22$ to $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm{s}$ of uniform braking, then keeps braking in the same way. How much longer does it take to stop?

- $3\,\mathrm{s}$
- $5.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

During the first $3\,\mathrm s$, the vehicle loses $22-10=12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The magnitude of the constant braking acceleration is therefore $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Losing the remaining $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ takes another $\dfrac{10}{4}=2.5\,\mathrm s$. The required duration starts at the second reading. Braking lasts five and a half seconds in total. The time from the second reading also follows by removing the three seconds that have already elapsed.
