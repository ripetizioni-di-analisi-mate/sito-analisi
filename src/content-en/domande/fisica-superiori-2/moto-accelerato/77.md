The linear braking law would give a negative velocity after rest is reached. How is the stopping model used here?

- Backward motion is always extended
- The braking interval ends at the first zero of velocity
- The negative velocity is replaced with its magnitude as the new velocity
- The first zero is ignored

## Explanation

The linear equation describes braking while the vehicle moves in its initial direction. The first zero of velocity marks rest and ends that interval. If the vehicle remains stationary, use $v=0$ in the next stage. A negative velocity would describe a reversal outside the stopping model.
