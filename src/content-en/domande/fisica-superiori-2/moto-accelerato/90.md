An object starts and ends at the same non-zero speed. At the end it is moving in the opposite direction to its initial motion. What is true of its velocity change?

- The change has magnitude twice the speed
- The change is zero
- The change equals the initial speed
- It depends only on initial position

## Explanation

The two velocities have equal magnitudes and opposite signs. If velocity goes from $+5$ to $-5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the difference is $-10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In general, subtracting the opposite components gives a change whose magnitude is twice their common speed. If the change is from the negative direction to the positive direction, the sign of the velocity change reverses. Its magnitude stays at the value just found.
