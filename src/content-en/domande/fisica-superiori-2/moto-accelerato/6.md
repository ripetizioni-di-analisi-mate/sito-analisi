A vehicle travels at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$. It maintains that velocity during a reaction time of $0.9\,\mathrm{s}$, then brakes with a constant deceleration of magnitude $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the total distance before it stops?

- $90\,\mathrm{m}$
- $117\,\mathrm{m}$
- $27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $207\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Choose the direction of travel as positive. Velocity decreases by $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second and reaches zero after $6\,\mathrm{s}$. During the reaction the velocity stays at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the graph has a rectangle of area $27\,\mathrm{m}$. Add the braking triangle’s area of $90\,\mathrm{m}$. The entire duration includes reaction and braking, while the second phase covers less distance than it would at the initial speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_a &= \frac{v_0}{b}=\frac{30}{5}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 6\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
s_r &= v_0t_r=30\cdot 0.9\,\mathrm{m}=27\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
s_f &= \frac{30^2}{2\cdot 5}\,\mathrm{m}\simeq 90\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{tot}} &= s_r+s_f \simeq 117\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
