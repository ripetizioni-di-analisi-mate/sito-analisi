A closed vessel with a movable piston keeps the sample at normal atmospheric pressure. It contains water and vapour in equilibrium at $100\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Their total mass is $0.08\,\mathrm{kg}$, and $0.03\,\mathrm{kg}$ is already vapour. With $L_v=2260000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, how much heat $Q$ is needed for all the water to become vapour?

- $226000\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $56500\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $113000\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $452000\,\mathrm{J}$.

## Explanation

Some of the mass is already gas, so the heat applies only to the liquid. The remaining $0.08-0.03=0.05$ kilograms must vaporise and are already at boiling point. Multiplying this mass by $2260000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ gives $113000\,\mathrm J$. Using the total mass would also count the vapour already present.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= L m \\[6pt]
&= \left(2.26\cdot10^{6}\right) \left(0.05\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 113000\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$
