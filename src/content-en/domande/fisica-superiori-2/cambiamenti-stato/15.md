A cooler must absorb at least $50\,\mathrm{kJ}$ at the melting temperature. Different masses of water or ice can be put inside, all at zero. With $L_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$, which contents can absorb the required energy while remaining at zero?

- $0.12\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice
- $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of liquid water
- $0.18\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice
- $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice

## Explanation

At the melting temperature, the required storage needs just under $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice, since $\dfrac{50}{334}\simeq0.150$. The $0.18\,\mathrm{kg}$ supply can absorb $60.12\,\mathrm{kJ}$ before melting completely. Water that is already liquid would begin to warm as soon as it receives heat. Compare the initial solid mass with the requirement, because the contents can stay at zero only until the last ice melts.
