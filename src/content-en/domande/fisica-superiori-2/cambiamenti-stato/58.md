Only part of a block is to be melted, and L is multiplied by the full mass. Why is the result too high?

- L must always be divided by the total mass.
- L is zero at constant temperature.
- The mass used must be only the part changing phase.
- Latent heat of melting always equals specific heat capacity.

## Explanation

Latent heat applies to the mass that changes from solid to liquid. If only a fraction of the block melts, find that mass first and then multiply it by $L_f$. Using the total mass would give the heat required to complete melting.
