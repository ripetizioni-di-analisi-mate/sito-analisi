In an insulated vessel of negligible heat capacity, warm water is mixed with ice at zero degrees Celsius. Cooling the original warm water all the way to zero would release $75.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$. Melting all the ice takes $33.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and the total liquid mass after melting is $1\,\mathrm{kg}$. With $c_a=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, what final temperature is reached?

- $10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $18\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $8\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$

## Explanation

The heat that the water could release by cooling to zero exceeds the melting threshold. Their difference is $75.4-33.4=42.0$ in kilojoules. That energy warms all the final water, whose heat capacity is $4200\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Converting the remainder to joules and dividing gives $10\,\mathrm K$ above zero.

$$
T_f=0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C+\frac{(75.4-33.4)\cdot1000}{4200}\,\mathrm K=10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$
