Ice at zero receives $334\,\mathrm W$ for $90\,\mathrm s$. How much ice melts with $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and enough initial ice?

- $0.9\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.09\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.009\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $90\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The transferred power supplies $334\cdot90\,\mathrm J=30060\,\mathrm J$. Since the ice is already at the melting temperature and enough ice is available, the whole transfer can drive the phase change. Divide by $334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ to obtain $0.090\,\mathrm{kg}$ of melted ice. As a check, $0.001\,\mathrm{kg}$ melts each second, which is one gram.

$$
m=\frac{334\cdot90}{334000}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.09\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
