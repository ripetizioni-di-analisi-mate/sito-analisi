To melt $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice already at zero, a heater supplies a constant $600\,\mathrm W$. Throughout the process $150\,\mathrm W$ goes to the surroundings. With $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and negligible heat storage in the apparatus, how long does melting take?

::grafico{f="0.6*x;0.45*x" segmenti="0,200.4,473,200.4" etichette="21,205,200.4 kJ" legenda="\text{Source};\text{To the ice}" xmin=0 xmax=485 ymin=0 ymax=310 nomi="t (s),E (kJ)" titolo="The dashed line marks the useful energy needed to melt all the ice."}

- $334\,\mathrm s$
- $445.333\,\mathrm s$
- $1336\,\mathrm s$
- $267.2\,\mathrm s$

## Explanation

The sample receives only $600-150=450$ joules each second. Melting requires $0.60\cdot334000\,\mathrm J=200400\,\mathrm J$. Dividing by the useful power gives about $445.333\,\mathrm s$. Using the total power would count the share that heats the surroundings and underestimate the time.
