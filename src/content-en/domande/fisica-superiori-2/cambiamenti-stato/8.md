A condenser receives $5\,\mathrm{g/s}$ of saturated water vapour for $10\,\mathrm s$. At normal atmospheric pressure all the vapour condenses at $100\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The water leaves at that temperature. With $L_v=2260000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, what is the magnitude $|Q|$ of the energy released?

- $226000\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $56500\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $452000\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $113000\,\mathrm{J}$.

## Explanation

The mass flow rate multiplied by time gives $50\,\mathrm g$, or $0.05\,\mathrm{kg}$ of vapour condensed. Multiplying by latent heat gives a magnitude of $113000\,\mathrm J$. The water still leaves at $100\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, so the balance covers condensation alone. For the vapour, heat transfer would have negative sign.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|Q| &= L m \\[6pt]
&= \left(2.26\cdot10^{6}\right) \left(0.05\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 113000\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$
