Warm water and ice at zero degrees Celsius are mixed in an insulated vessel of negligible heat capacity. Cooling the warm water down to zero would release exactly the energy needed to melt all the ice. What is the final state?

- Only liquid water at zero.
- Ice and water above zero.
- Only ice at zero.
- Liquid water at ten degrees.

## Explanation

The energy available by cooling to zero melts exactly all the ice. As soon as the last solid portion disappears, only liquid water remains. Since the difference between available energy and the threshold is zero, there is no further contribution to warm the liquid above the melting point.
