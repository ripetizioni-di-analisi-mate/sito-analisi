A vessel with heat capacity $630\,\mathrm{J/K}$ holds $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice. Both the vessel and ice start at zero. With no losses, how much heat $Q$ is needed to melt all the ice and bring both the vessel and the resulting water to $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$? Use $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and $c_a=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.

- $263400\,\mathrm J$
- $213000\,\mathrm J$
- $63000\,\mathrm J$
- $400800\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

Melting requires $200400\,\mathrm J$. After melting, the water needs $50400\,\mathrm J$ and the vessel needs $12600\,\mathrm J$, since both warm by $20\,\mathrm K$. Add the three contributions in the energy balance. The vessel’s heat capacity is already given in joules per kelvin.
