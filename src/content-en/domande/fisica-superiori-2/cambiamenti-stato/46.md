Water releases $40\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The ice at $-8\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ first needs $4\,\mathrm{kJ}$ to reach zero. If some solid remains, what energy went into melting?

- $36\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $44\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $40\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

The energy released by the water supplies two separate stages. First $4\,\mathrm{kJ}$ brings the ice to zero. That leaves $40-4=36$ kilojoules for melting. Since ice remains, those $36\,\mathrm{kJ}$ convert only part of the mass and the final mixture stays at zero.

$$
Q_{\mathrm{fus}}=(40-4)\,\mathrm{kJ}=36\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
