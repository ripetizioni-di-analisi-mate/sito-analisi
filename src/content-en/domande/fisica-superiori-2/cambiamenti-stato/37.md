A heater supplies the same useful power to two samples of different pure substances, each already at its own melting point. Sample A has twice the mass of sample B, and the two melt completely in the same time. What is the ratio $\dfrac{L_A}{L_B}$ of the latent heats of fusion?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $1$
- $4$

## Explanation

With the same useful power $P$ and the same time $t$ the two samples absorb the same energy $Pt$. Since each sample melts completely, the energy absorbed is $m_AL_A$ for A and $m_BL_B$ for B, so $m_AL_A=m_BL_B$. Substituting $m_A=2m_B$ gives $2m_BL_A=m_BL_B$, therefore
$$
\frac{L_A}{L_B}=\frac{m_B}{m_A}=\frac{1}{2}.
$$
The value $2$ is obtained by swapping the masses in the ratio.
