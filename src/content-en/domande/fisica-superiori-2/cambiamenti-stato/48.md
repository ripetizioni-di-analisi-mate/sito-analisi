A liquid is poured without compressing its vessel. What property distinguishes it from a solid that keeps its shape?

- It must fill the entire volume like a gas.
- Its mass is always zero.
- It can take the vessel’s shape at nearly constant volume.
- It retains a rigid shape.

## Explanation

When a liquid is poured, its particles can change position relative to one another. The liquid therefore takes the shape of the part of the vessel it occupies. At similar pressure, average particle spacing changes little, so the volume stays nearly the same. A solid retains its own shape.
