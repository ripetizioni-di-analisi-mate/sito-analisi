A $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ water sample starts at $10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and ends as ice at zero. With $c_a=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and $L_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$, what is the signed heat?

- $75.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-75.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-66.8\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-8.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

The water releases $0.20\cdot4200\cdot10\,\mathrm J=8.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$ to reach zero. It then releases $0.20\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=66.8\,\mathrm{kJ}$ as it freezes. Both transfers leave the sample and their signed total is $-75.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The resulting ice stays at zero. The thermometer reaching zero marks the start of freezing. Determining whether all the water has frozen also requires the energy balance.

$$
Q=-0.20\cdot4200\cdot10\,\mathrm J-0.20\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=-75.2\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
