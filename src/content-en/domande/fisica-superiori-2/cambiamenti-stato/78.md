An insulated container has significant heat capacity and is initially warm along with the water. What changes in the heat that can be released down to zero?

- Always subtract all the water’s heat capacity.
- Include the contribution from the container.
- The melting term changes units.
- Ignore the initial water temperature.

## Explanation

The warm vessel is a second energy source for the ice. If its initial temperature is $T$ above zero and its heat capacity is $C$, it can release $C(T-0)$ by cooling to zero. Add this contribution to that of the water before comparing the total with the melting threshold.
