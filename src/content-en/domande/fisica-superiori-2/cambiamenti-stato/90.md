A sample condenses at $100\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, releasing $22.6\,\mathrm{kJ}$, and then the water releases another $840\,\mathrm J$ as it reaches $80\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Use the two stages to find $c_a$ with $L_v=2260\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. What value follows?

- $840\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $2260\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $42000\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$

## Explanation

The $22.6\,\mathrm{kJ}$ from condensation corresponds to $\dfrac{22.6}{2260}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.010\,\mathrm{kg}$. That same liquid mass then releases $840\,\mathrm J$ as it cools by $20\,\mathrm K$. Divide that energy by the product of mass and temperature change to obtain $4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. The reverse check recovers $840\,\mathrm J$ by multiplying the result by the liquid mass and its fall in temperature.

$$
\begin{aligned}m&=\frac{22.6}{2260}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.010\,\mathrm{kg}\\c_a&=\frac{840}{0.010\cdot20}\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}.\end{aligned}
$$
