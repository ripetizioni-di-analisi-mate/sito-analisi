A mixture of ice and pure water is in equilibrium at normal atmospheric pressure. A small amount of energy is added without melting all the ice. What changes?

- The temperature of all phases necessarily rises.
- The amount of liquid increases.
- Total mass increases.
- It becomes only vapour.

## Explanation

The supplied energy melts some of the ice. Total mass stays the same, so the loss of ice is matched by an equal gain in liquid water. Temperature stays at the melting point because both phases still coexist at the fixed pressure. Raising it requires melting the remaining ice first.
