Mix $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $10\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$ with $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at zero degrees Celsius in an insulated vessel of negligible heat capacity. Use $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. How much ice melts? Choose the value closest to the result.

- $0.125749\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- $0.0314371\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- $0.0628743\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- $0.251497\,\mathrm{kg}$.

## Explanation

The water can release $0.50\cdot4200\cdot10\,\mathrm J=21000\,\mathrm J$ before reaching zero. Melting all the ice would take $167000\,\mathrm J$, so some solid remains at equilibrium. Dividing the available $21000\,\mathrm J$ by $L_f$ gives about $0.0629\,\mathrm{kg}$ of melted ice. The remaining ice keeps the mixture at the melting temperature.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{\mathrm{fusa}} &= \frac{T_a c m_a}{L} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(10\right) \left(4200\right) \left(0.5\right)}{\left(334000\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 0.0628743\,\mathrm{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$
