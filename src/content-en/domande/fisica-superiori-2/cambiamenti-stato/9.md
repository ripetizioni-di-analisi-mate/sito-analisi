A vessel initially contains $0.70\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The ice has already absorbed $33400\,\mathrm J$ since melting began. With $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, how much more useful energy $Q$ must it absorb to melt completely?

- $200400\,\mathrm J$
- $233800\,\mathrm J$
- $33400\,\mathrm J$
- $267200\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

Complete melting requires $0.70\cdot334000\,\mathrm J=233800\,\mathrm J$. Some of that energy has already reached the ice and melted $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$. Subtract the $33400\,\mathrm J$ already supplied. That leaves $200400\,\mathrm J$, enough to melt the $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ that remains solid. The heat already received and the heat still needed both belong to the plateau. Together they give its full width.
