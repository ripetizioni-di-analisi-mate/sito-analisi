In separate vessels $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice melts and $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water vaporises, each at its transition temperature. Use $L_f=334$ and $L_v=2260\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. What is the vaporisation energy minus the melting energy?

- $59\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $393\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $167\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $226\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

Both samples absorb energy as they move to a higher-energy phase under the stated conditions. Melting requires $0.50\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=167\,\mathrm{kJ}$. Vaporisation requires $0.10\cdot2260\,\mathrm{kJ}=226\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The second is $59\,\mathrm{kJ}$ greater even though the vaporised mass is smaller.

$$
Q_v-Q_f=(0.10\cdot2260-0.50\cdot334)\,\mathrm{kJ}=59\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
