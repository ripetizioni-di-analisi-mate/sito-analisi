With $c_g=2100\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and $L_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$, how many kilojoules take $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ from ice at $-25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ to water at zero?

- $33.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $5.25\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $43.9\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $38.65\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

Warming the ice to zero requires $0.10\cdot2100\cdot25\,\mathrm J=5.25\,\mathrm{kJ}$. At that temperature, complete melting adds $0.10\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=33.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The sum is $38.65\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The final water is still at zero, so the balance contains no stage for heating the liquid.

$$
Q=0.10\cdot2100\cdot25\,\mathrm J+0.10\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=38.65\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
