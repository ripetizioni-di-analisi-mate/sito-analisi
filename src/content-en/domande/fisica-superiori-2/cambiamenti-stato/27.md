A heating curve against $Q$ enters its plateau at $6\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and leaves it at $46\,\mathrm{kJ}$. How much energy corresponds to the phase change?

- $46\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $52\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $40\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

The horizontal coordinate records energy already supplied. The first $6\,\mathrm{kJ}$ precedes the phase change. The energy for the transition is the width of the plateau, $46-6=40$ in kilojoules. The vertical coordinate stays constant along that section and gives the transition temperature.

$$
Q_{\mathrm{lat}}=(46-6)\,\mathrm{kJ}=40\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
