A sample of a pure liquid substance cools at constant pressure. During freezing the temperature of the sample stays constant, while the refrigerator removes $600\,\mathrm J$ from it. The work due to the change in volume is negligible. How does the internal energy of the sample change during this stage?

- It decreases by $600\,\mathrm J$.
- It stays constant.
- It increases by $600\,\mathrm J$.
- It starts to decrease only when freezing is complete.

## Explanation

During freezing liquid and solid coexist, and the temperature stays constant while the solid fraction grows. The sample gives $600\,\mathrm J$ to the refrigerator, so the heat exchanged by the sample is $Q=-600\,\mathrm J$. Since the work is negligible, all the energy released comes from the internal energy of the sample. The internal energy therefore decreases by $600\,\mathrm J$, even though the temperature does not change. The answer «It stays constant» comes from confusing internal energy with temperature.
