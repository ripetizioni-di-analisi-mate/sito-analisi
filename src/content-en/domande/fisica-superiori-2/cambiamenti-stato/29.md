A pure liquid cools at constant pressure. During freezing the thermometer reading stays constant while the device removes $600\,\mathrm J$. How does the sample's internal energy change if volume work is negligible?

- It decreases by $600\,\mathrm J$.
- It stays constant.
- It increases by $600\,\mathrm J$.
- It begins to decrease only after freezing is complete.

## Explanation

The thermometer measures temperature, which remains constant while the solid fraction changes. The sample releases energy with negligible work, so its internal energy decreases by the amount released.
