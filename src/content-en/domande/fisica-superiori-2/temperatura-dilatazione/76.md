When a liquid thermometer is heated uniformly, the liquid expands by more than the internal volume up to its original mark. What happens to the reading when the thermometer warms?

- It falls for every temperature rise.
- It stays fixed because the capillary expands.
- It rises because the apparent expansion is positive.
- It changes only if the liquid mass changes.

## Explanation

Part of the expansion is taken up by the growth of the container. The excess pushes liquid into the column.

The level responds to the difference between the expansion of the liquid and the expansion of its container. If both grew by the same fraction, the level would remain fixed relative to the scale. Here the difference moves the meniscus towards higher readings.
