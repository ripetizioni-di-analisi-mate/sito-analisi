Two rods joined end to end are free to expand and both warm by $50\,\mathrm K$. The first is $2\,\mathrm m$ long with $\alpha_1=10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. The second is $4\,\mathrm m$ long. The total extension is $4.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the linear expansion coefficient of the second rod?

- $1.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $1\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $7.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$

## Explanation

The first rod extends by $(10^{-5})\cdot2\cdot50\,\mathrm m=1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The second therefore contributes $4.0-1.0=3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. Dividing its extension in metres by $4\,\mathrm m$ and then by $50\,\mathrm K$ gives $\alpha_2\simeq1.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Subtracting the first extension isolates the second rod’s contribution.
