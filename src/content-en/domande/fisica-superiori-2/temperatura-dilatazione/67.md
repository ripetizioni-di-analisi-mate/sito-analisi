A free isotropic container has a linear expansion coefficient of $3\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. It holds $100\,\mathrm{mL}$ of liquid up to a mark. The liquid’s volume expansion coefficient is $6\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Both warm by $50\,\mathrm K$ at constant pressure. Estimate the apparent expansion.

- $2.55\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $2.85\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $3.45\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

The capacity up to the mark depends on three dimensions, so its volume expansion coefficient is about $9\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. The liquid grows by about $3.0\,\mathrm{mL}$, and the capacity up to the mark grows by $0.45\,\mathrm{mL}$. The excess is therefore $3.0-0.45=2.55\,\mathrm{mL}$. Using the linear coefficient for capacity would underestimate the container’s contribution.
