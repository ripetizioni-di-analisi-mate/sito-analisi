A glass has $2\,\mathrm{mL}$ of spare capacity above the liquid. After warming, the liquid would expand by $3.6\,\mathrm{mL}$ if all of it stayed in the glass. The warm glass holds $0.4\,\mathrm{mL}$ more. What volume overflows, measured at the final temperature?

- $1.6\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $3.2\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

The space available when warm is the original headspace plus the increase in container capacity. The liquid expansion would exceed it and the difference overflows.

$$
V_{\mathrm{fuori}}\simeq(3.6-2-0.4)\,\mathrm{mL}=1.2\,\mathrm{mL}.
$$

The heated container can hold $2.4\,\mathrm{mL}$ more than the initial volume of liquid. The free expansion of $3.6\,\mathrm{mL}$ exceeds that allowance by $1.2\,\mathrm{mL}$. The relevant capacity is the volume up to the rim.
