An isotropic solid’s volume rises from $200$ to $200.6\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ over $50\,\mathrm K$. Find the volume expansion coefficient.

- $2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $6\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $0.0006\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $0.003\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$

## Explanation

The measurement concerns volume. Divide the increase by the initial volume and the temperature interval.

$$
\beta\simeq\frac{0.6}{200\cdot50}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}=6\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}.
$$

The increase of $0.6\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ over $200\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ is a fraction of $0.003$. Since that change occurs over $50\,\mathrm K$, the coefficient gives the fraction per kelvin. Finding the isotropic solid’s linear coefficient would require dividing again by three.
