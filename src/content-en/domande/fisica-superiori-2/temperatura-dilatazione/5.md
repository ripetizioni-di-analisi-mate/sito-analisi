A rod of length $6\,\mathrm m$ expands by $0.0042\,\mathrm m$ when warmed by $50\,\mathrm K$. What is its linear expansion coefficient? Choose the value closest to the result.

- $1.4\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$.
- $2.8\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$.
- $7\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$.
- $0.0007\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$.

## Explanation

Both the temperature and length changes are positive. In the unconstrained expansion model, divide $\Delta L$ by $L_0\Delta T$ to isolate $\alpha$. The ratio of the lengths is dimensionless, so the unit of the coefficient is $\mathrm{K^{-1}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha &= \frac{\Delta L}{\Delta T L} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(0.0042\right)}{\left(50\right) \left(6\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 1.4\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
