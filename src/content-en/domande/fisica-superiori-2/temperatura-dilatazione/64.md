A liquid fills a freely expanding container to its $500\,\mathrm{mL}$ mark. Both warm by $20\,\mathrm K$ at constant pressure and the apparent increase is $5\,\mathrm{mL}$. The container’s volume expansion coefficient is $1.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Estimate the liquid’s coefficient.

- $6.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $3.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $1.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$

## Explanation

The apparent reading depends on the difference $\beta_{\mathrm{liq}}-\beta_{\mathrm{rec}}$. Divide $5\,\mathrm{mL}$ by $500\,\mathrm{mL}$ and by $20\,\mathrm K$ to obtain $5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Adding the container’s coefficient gives $\beta_{\mathrm{liq}}\simeq6.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. The reference capacity increases, so the liquid’s actual coefficient is larger than the apparent coefficient.
