For a fixed mass of liquid water at atmospheric pressure, the volumes at $2\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $6\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ read the same within the instrument’s resolution. Which conclusion is justified?

- The volume remains constant throughout the interval.
- The volume can be smaller at an intermediate point near 4 °C.
- The volume increases continuously between the two readings.
- The density necessarily has a minimum near 4 °C.

## Explanation

The measurements at the endpoints compare only the initial and final volumes. At atmospheric pressure, the volume of liquid water decreases up to about $4\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and then increases. The two readings can therefore agree within the resolution while enclosing a minimum. Detecting it requires a sufficiently precise intermediate reading.
