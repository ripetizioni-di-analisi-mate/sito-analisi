An isotropic block initially occupies $180\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. To let it expand freely in all directions, estimate how much extra space it occupies after uniform heating by $60\,\mathrm K$. Use the first-order model and a linear expansion coefficient of $1.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$.

- $0.162\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $0.324\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $0.486\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $4.86\,\mathrm{cm^3}$

## Explanation

Each dimension increases by the fraction $\alpha\Delta T=9\cdot10^{-4}$. Volume depends on three dimensions, so its first-order relative increase is $3\cdot9\cdot10^{-4}=0.0027$. Multiplying by the initial volume of $180\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ gives $\Delta V\simeq0.486\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. This is an absolute increase in the same unit as the initial volume.
