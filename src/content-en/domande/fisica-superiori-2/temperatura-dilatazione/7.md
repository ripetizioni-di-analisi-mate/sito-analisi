An isotropic solid has volume $180\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and linear expansion coefficient $1.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. It is warmed by $60\,\mathrm K$. Find the increase in volume in the small-deformation approximation. Choose the value closest to the result.

- $0.162\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.
- $0.324\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.
- $0.486\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.
- $0.972\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.

## Explanation

All three dimensions expand in the isotropic solid. Its fractional volume change is therefore about $3\alpha\Delta T$, which we multiply by $V_0$ in cubic centimetres. The volume expansion coefficient is about three times the linear coefficient.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V &= 3 \Delta T V \alpha \\[6pt]
&= 3 \left(60\right) \left(180\right) \left(1.5\cdot10^{-5}\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 0.486\,\mathrm{cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$
