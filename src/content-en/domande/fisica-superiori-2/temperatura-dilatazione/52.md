Why does a factor of $3$ appear to first order in the volume expansion of an isotropic solid?

- Three dimensions contribute the same fractional change to the product.
- The linear coefficient has three units.
- Temperature is cubed in the linear law.
- The three deformations cancel.

## Explanation

Multiplying three factors $1+\varepsilon$ gives three copies of $\varepsilon$ at first order. The remaining terms have higher powers.
