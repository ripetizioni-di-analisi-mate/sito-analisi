A thermometer is brought into thermal equilibrium first with one sample and then with a second sample, and in both cases it shows the same temperature. What can be inferred about the two samples?

- The masses are equal.
- The internal energies are equal.
- The temperatures are equal.
- The materials are the same.

## Explanation

In each comparison the thermometer is in thermal equilibrium with the sample, so it shows the temperature of the sample. The two readings are equal, so the two samples have the same temperature. By the zeroth law of thermodynamics the two samples are then in thermal equilibrium with each other, and if they were placed in contact through a conducting wall the net heat exchange would be zero. Masses, materials and internal energies may differ, because thermal equilibrium concerns temperature only.
