A liquid’s physical volume grows with a slope of $0.12\,\mathrm{mL/K}$, and the capacity up to its initial mark grows with a slope of $0.02\,\mathrm{mL/K}$. What is the slope of the volume change read relative to the mark?

- $0.14\,\mathrm{mL/K}$
- $0.12\,\mathrm{mL/K}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{mL/K}$
- $0.02\,\mathrm{mL/K}$

## Explanation

For each kelvin of warming, the liquid gains $0.12\,\mathrm{mL}$ and the container holds another $0.02$ below the mark. The volume above the mark therefore grows by $0.10\,\mathrm{mL/K}$. At $20\,\mathrm K$, for example, the two increases are $2.4$ and $0.4\,\mathrm{mL}$. Their difference of $2.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ agrees with that slope.
