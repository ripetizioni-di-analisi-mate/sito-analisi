Two rods in series warm by $50\,\mathrm K$. The first is $2\,\mathrm m$ with $\alpha_1=10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$, the second $1\,\mathrm m$ with $\alpha_2=2.4\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. How much does their total length change if free to expand?

- $2.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $1.1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $3.4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Add the two absolute changes, each calculated with its own length and coefficient.

$$
\Delta L_{\mathrm{tot}}\simeq[(10^{-5})\cdot2+(2.4\cdot10^{-5})\cdot1]\cdot50\,\mathrm m=2.2\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$

The first rod contributes $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the second contributes $1.2\,\mathrm{mm}$. The shorter rod extends more because its coefficient is more than twice that of the first. The sections are in series, so both contribute to the total length.
