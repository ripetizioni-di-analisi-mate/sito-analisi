Water absorbs $21\,\mathrm{kJ}$ over $10\,\mathrm K$ without changing phase. What mass is present if $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$?

- $5\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.05\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

First divide 21,000 joules by 10 kelvins to find the water's heat capacity. The result is $2100\,\mathrm{J/K}$ for all the water. A kilogram has a heat capacity of $4200\,\mathrm{J/K}$, so the sample is half a kilogram. Substituting this mass into $mc\Delta T$ gives the original 21 kilojoules.

$$
m=\frac{21000}{4200\cdot10}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.5\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
