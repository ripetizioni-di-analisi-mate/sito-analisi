In an insulated vessel the calorimeter starts at the same temperature as the cool water. What happens if you neglect it when warmer water is added?

- The final temperature is underestimated.
- No result changes.
- The final temperature is overestimated.
- The estimate is below absolute zero.

## Explanation

The calorimeter starts at the same temperature as the cold water, and its walls absorb energy during mixing. Including it increases the heat capacity that must warm up on the cold side. The equilibrium temperature lies closer to the colder starting point. Omitting it assigns some of the released heat to the cold water and raises the estimate.
