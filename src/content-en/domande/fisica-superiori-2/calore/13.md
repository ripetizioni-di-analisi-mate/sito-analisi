A solid with heat capacity $210\,\mathrm{J/K}$ initially at $85\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is immersed in water at $25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The container is insulated and has negligible heat capacity. What is the minimum mass of water giving an equilibrium temperature no higher than $45\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$? Use $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and neglect phase changes.

- $0.1\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- $0.05\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- $0.4\,\mathrm{kg}$.

## Explanation

At the threshold the solid releases $210\cdot40=8400\,\mathrm J$. Each kilogram of water can absorb $4200\cdot20=84000\,\mathrm J$ before reaching $45\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Their ratio is $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$. Adding more water increases the weight of the colder initial temperature in the equilibrium average, so this is the minimum mass.
