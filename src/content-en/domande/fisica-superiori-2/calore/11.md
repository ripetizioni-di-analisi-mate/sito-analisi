An insulated calorimeter contains a liquid at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with heat capacity $200\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The container starts at the same temperature. A solid at $80\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with heat capacity $300\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is immersed in the liquid. The common final temperature is $50\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and there are no phase changes. What is the heat capacity of the container?

- $200\,\mathrm{J/K}$.
- $300\,\mathrm{J/K}$.
- $100\,\mathrm{J/K}$.
- $500\,\mathrm{J/K}$.

## Explanation

The hot body releases $300(80-50)=9000\,\mathrm J$. The liquid and container absorb this heat together and each warms by $30\,\mathrm K$. Therefore the heat capacity of the cold side as a whole is $300\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Subtracting the known heat capacity of the liquid leaves $100\,\mathrm{J/K}$ for the container.
