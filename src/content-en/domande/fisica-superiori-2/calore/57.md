A calorimeter with heat capacity $250\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is at $30\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. A sample with heat capacity $500\,\mathrm{J/K}$ at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is placed in it. While the two reach equilibrium, an electric resistor of negligible heat capacity supplies $9.0\,\mathrm{kJ}$ to the system. The system exchanges no other energy with the surroundings and no phase changes occur. What is the final common temperature?

- $10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $12\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $27\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $22\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$

## Explanation

The system receives energy from outside, so the sum of the heats exchanged by the calorimeter and by the sample equals the energy supplied by the resistor:
$$
250\,(T_f-30)+500\,(T_f-0)=9000.
$$
Solving gives the weighted mean of the initial temperatures plus the term due to the resistor:
$$
T_f=\frac{250\cdot30+500\cdot0}{750}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C+\frac{9000}{750}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=(10+12)\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=22\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$
The value $10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is the equilibrium temperature without the resistor. The value $27\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ comes from using the arithmetic mean $15\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, which holds only for equal heat capacities.
