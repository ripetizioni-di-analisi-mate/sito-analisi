A $250\,\mathrm{J/K}$ calorimeter is at $30\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Add a sample with $C=500\,\mathrm{J/K}$ at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. What temperature does the calorimeter reach with insulation and no phase changes?

- $15\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $30\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$

## Explanation

The calorimeter starts warmer and releases energy to the sample. The sample has twice the heat capacity, so for the same heat transfer it warms by half as much as the calorimeter cools. The initial 30 kelvins therefore split into 20 kelvins of cooling and 10 kelvins of warming.

$$
T_f=\frac{250\cdot30+500\cdot0}{750}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$
