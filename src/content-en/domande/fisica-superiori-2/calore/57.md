Two solids at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $50\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ are placed in an insulated container. They have positive heat capacities, do not react and do not change phase. At equilibrium a sensor reads $55\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ for both. What can be checked without knowing their masses, if the container itself has negligible heat capacity and no work is done?

- The first must have twice the mass of the second.
- The reading must be correct if the materials are identical.
- The final temperature must always be the arithmetic mean.
- Both would have absorbed heat, which is inconsistent with heat exchange between them alone.

## Explanation

Both solids would need to gain internal energy to reach $55\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Since energy lost by one must be gained by the other, the equilibrium temperature must lie between the two initial temperatures.
