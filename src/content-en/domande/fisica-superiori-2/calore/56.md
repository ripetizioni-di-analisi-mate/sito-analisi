A liquid and its calorimeter initially have the same temperature. They absorb $10\,\mathrm{kJ}$ together and reach equilibrium. Their heat capacities are $900$ and $100\,\mathrm{J/K}$. What fraction of the energy goes to the calorimeter?

- $0.11111111$
- $0.1$
- $0.9$
- $0.5$

## Explanation

Both parts start and finish at the same temperature, so the factor $\Delta T$ is common to the two heat transfers. In the ratio of the calorimeter's heat to the total heat, this factor cancels. The calorimeter's fraction of the energy is therefore the same as its fraction of the combined heat capacity.

$$
\frac{Q_{\mathrm{cal}}}{Q_{\mathrm{tot}}}=\frac{100}{900+100}=0.10.
$$
