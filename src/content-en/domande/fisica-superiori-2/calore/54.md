A calorimeter and sample absorb $8400\,\mathrm J$ and warm by $12\,\mathrm K$. The sample alone has $C=520\,\mathrm{J/K}$. What is the calorimeter’s heat capacity?

- $700\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $1220\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $520\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $180\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

Divide the energy absorbed by both parts by their shared increase of 12 kelvins. The result, $700\,\mathrm{J/K}$, is their combined heat capacity. Subtract the sample's contribution to isolate the calorimeter's. As a check, the container absorbs $180\cdot12=2160\,\mathrm J$, and the sample absorbs the remaining 6240 joules.

$$
C_{\mathrm{cal}}=\frac{8400}{12}-520\,\mathrm{J/K}=180\,\mathrm{J/K}.
$$
