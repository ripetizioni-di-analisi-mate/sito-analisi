A layer of the same area and thickness but much higher conductivity is added in series with an insulating layer. Why can it have little effect on steady-state power at fixed external temperatures?

- Its resistance is infinite.
- Its resistance $\dfrac{d}{kA}$ is small compared with the insulation.
- Power never depends on the material.
- Resistances subtract for different materials.

## Explanation

At equal area and thickness, the more conductive layer has a much smaller resistance. If the insulation has $1.00\,\mathrm{K/W}$ and the added layer has $0.01\,\mathrm{K/W}$, the total resistance is $1.01\,\mathrm{K/W}$. The denominator in the ratio of temperature drop to resistance grows only slightly, so the power falls only slightly.
