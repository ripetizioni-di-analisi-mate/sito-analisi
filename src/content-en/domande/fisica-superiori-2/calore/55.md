A liquid of heat capacity $200\,\mathrm{J/K}$ at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and a solid of heat capacity $300\,\mathrm{J/K}$ at $80\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ are placed simultaneously in an insulated calorimeter. Its vessel has heat capacity $100\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and starts at $50\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Nothing changes phase. What is the common final temperature?

- $44\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $65\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $55\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $75\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$

## Explanation

The vessel also warms, starting at its own initial temperature. The insulated balance gives $T_f=\dfrac{200\cdot20+300\cdot80+100\cdot50}{600}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=55\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The liquid and vessel absorb $7000\,\mathrm J$ and $500\,\mathrm J$, respectively. Their sum matches the $7500\,\mathrm J$ released by the solid and checks the balance.
