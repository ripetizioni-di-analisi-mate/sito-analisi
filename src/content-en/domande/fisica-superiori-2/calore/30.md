Two samples of heat capacities $C_1=300$ and $C_2=900\,\mathrm{J/K}$ are brought into contact in an insulated system. The first starts at $80\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and the second at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. What is the magnitude of the heat released by the first before thermal equilibrium is reached?

- $4500\,\mathrm J$
- $13500\,\mathrm J$
- $18000\,\mathrm J$
- $40500\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The final temperature is the heat-capacity-weighted mean. It is $35\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, so the first body cools by 45 kelvins and the second warms by 15. The magnitude of the heat released by the first is its heat capacity times 45 kelvins. The second absorbs the same energy because its heat capacity is three times as large.

$$
\begin{aligned}T_f&=\frac{300\cdot80+900\cdot20}{1200}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=35\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\\|Q_1|&=300(80-35)\,\mathrm J=13500\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$
