In an insulated container of negligible heat capacity, water at $68\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is added to $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $18\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. How much hot water is needed for the mixture to reach equilibrium at $28\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$?

- $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The cold water warms by 10 kelvins and the hot water cools by 40 kelvins. In the insulated container, the heat absorbed by one kilogram of cold water must equal the heat released by the hot water. Each kilogram of hot water would release four times as much, so one quarter of a kilogram is enough.

$$
m_{\mathrm{h}}=1\cdot\frac{28-18}{68-28}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.25\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
