A wall has two side-by-side sections with thermal resistances of $0.50$ and $1.50\,\mathrm{K/W}$. In steady state each section has its inner face at $30\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and its outer face at $6\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Neglecting lateral heat transfer, what is the total rate of heat transfer through the wall?

- $12\,\mathrm{W}$.
- $48\,\mathrm{W}$.
- $32\,\mathrm{W}$.
- $64\,\mathrm{W}$.

## Explanation

The two sections share the same $24\,\mathrm K$ temperature difference, so heat passes through them along independent paths. The first transfers $\dfrac{24}{0.50}\,\mathrm W=48\,\mathrm W$, and the second transfers $\dfrac{24}{1.50}\,\mathrm W=16\,\mathrm W$. Add the powers because both paths carry energy from the inner face to the outer face.
