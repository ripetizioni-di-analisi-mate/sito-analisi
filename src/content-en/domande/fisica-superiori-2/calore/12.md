A sample of mass $0.6\,\mathrm{kg}$ absorbs $1800\,\mathrm J$ and its temperature rises by $15\,\mathrm K$ without changing phase. Determine its specific heat capacity. Choose the value closest to the result.

- $400\,\mathrm{J/(kg K)}$.
- $100\,\mathrm{J/(kg K)}$.
- $800\,\mathrm{J/(kg K)}$.
- $200\,\mathrm{J/(kg K)}$.

## Explanation

The sample stays in the same phase, so the model $Q=mc\Delta T$ applies. Dividing the energy absorbed by the mass and the temperature interval gives a value in joules per kilogram per kelvin.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= \frac{Q}{\Delta T m} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(1800\right)}{\left(15\right) \left(0.6\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 200\,\mathrm{J/(kg K)}
\end{aligned}
$$
