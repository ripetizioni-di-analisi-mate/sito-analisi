A sample has heat capacity $600\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The graph shows two intervals close to the temperature at which the heater is switched off, at $20\,\mathrm s$. The sample’s heat loss to the air can be approximated by the same constant power in both intervals. How much power does the heater deliver to the sample when switched on?

::grafico{linee="0,25 20,29 40,27" punti="0,25,pieno;20,29,pieno;40,27,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=42 ymin=24 ymax=31 nomi="t (s),T (°C)" titolo="The sample warms from 25 °C to 29 °C between 0 s and 20 s. At 40 s it is at 27 °C."}

- $180\,\mathrm W$
- $120\,\mathrm W$
- $60\,\mathrm W$
- $300\,\mathrm W$

## Explanation

After switch-off, the sample cools by $2\,\mathrm K$ in $20\,\mathrm s$, corresponding to a loss of $600\cdot\dfrac{2}{20}\,\mathrm W=60\,\mathrm W$. Before switch-off the stored energy increases at $600\cdot\dfrac{4}{20}\,\mathrm W=120\,\mathrm W$. That is the net power. The heater also covers the $60\,\mathrm W$ loss, so it supplies $180\,\mathrm W$.
