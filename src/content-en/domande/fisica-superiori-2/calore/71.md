In an insulated container a piece of hot copper cools in contact with cold water. Why can the masses alone not be used to weight the initial temperatures?

- The masses must be in kelvins.
- Final temperature uses heat capacities, which also depend on specific heat capacities.
- Copper will absorb heat if it starts hotter.
- Heat capacity is equal for all materials.

## Explanation

Each temperature is weighted by the corresponding heat capacity $mc$. At equal masses, a one-kelvin change in water involves more energy than in copper. Water therefore gets more weight even if the masses match. Mass alone provides the correct weights when the two materials share the same $c$.
