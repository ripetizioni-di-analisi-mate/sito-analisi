An object at $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ cools by $30\,\mathrm K$ while the other warms by $10\,\mathrm K$ on reaching equilibrium. With insulation, what is the ratio of the cool object’s heat capacity to the warm object’s?

- $0.33333333$
- $1$
- $3$
- $9$

## Explanation

The hot body cools by 30 kelvins and the cold body warms by 10. For both transfers to have equal magnitude, the body changing temperature by one third as much needs three times the heat capacity. The ratio requested has the cold body's heat capacity in the numerator, so it equals 3.

$$
\frac{C_{\mathrm c}}{C_{\mathrm h}}=\frac{30}{10}=3.
$$
