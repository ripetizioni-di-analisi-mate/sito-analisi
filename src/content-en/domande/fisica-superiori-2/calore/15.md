A sample starts at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Its heat capacity is $120\,\mathrm{J/K}$ up to $30\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $320\,\mathrm{J/K}$ above that temperature. It absorbs $2800\,\mathrm J$ without a phase change. By how much does its temperature increase?

- $8\,\mathrm K$
- $20\,\mathrm K$
- $15\,\mathrm K$
- $24\,\mathrm K$

## Explanation

The first ten kelvin require $120\cdot10\,\mathrm J=1200\,\mathrm J$. The sample still has $1600\,\mathrm J$ to absorb and is now in the range with the larger heat capacity. At that capacity the remaining $1600\,\mathrm J$ give $\dfrac{1600}{320}\,\mathrm K=5\,\mathrm K$. Add the two temperature rises because they are consecutive parts of the same heating process.
