An energy meter was not zeroed. While heating $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$ of a material, it reads $1600\,\mathrm J$ at $25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $4000\,\mathrm J$ at $37\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The material stays in the same phase and heat losses are negligible. What is its mean specific heat capacity over the interval?

- $200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $320\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $1600\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $800\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$

## Explanation

Subtracting the readings cancels the meter’s initial offset. The sample absorbs $(4000-1600)\,\mathrm J=2400\,\mathrm J$ for a temperature rise of $12\,\mathrm K$. The slope is $200\,\mathrm{J/K}$ for the whole sample. Dividing by $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives $800\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. A single reading would not remove the offset.
