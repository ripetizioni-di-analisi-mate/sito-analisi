A sample of mass $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ absorbs $1600\,\mathrm J$ and its temperature rises by $14\,\mathrm K$ without changing phase. Determine its specific heat capacity. Choose the value closest to the result.

- $457\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
- $114\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
- $914\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
- $229\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.

## Explanation

The sample stays in the same phase, so the model $Q=mc\Delta T$ applies. Dividing the energy absorbed by the mass and the temperature interval gives a value in joules per kilogram per kelvin.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= \frac{Q}{\Delta T m} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(1600\right)}{\left(14\right) \left(0.5\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 229\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}
\end{aligned}
$$
