A test is performed in a calorimeter without heat losses, first with $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ and then with $0.35\,\mathrm{kg}$ of the same material. The slopes of $Q$ against $\Delta T$ for the two systems are $85$ and $185\,\mathrm{J/K}$. What is the specific heat capacity of the material?

- $100\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
- $400\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
- $250\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
- $740\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.

## Explanation

Each slope measures the heat required for a one-kelvin rise of the container and sample together. Since the container is unchanged, subtracting the slopes gives the extra heat per kelvin due to only the additional $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$. Dividing by that mass gives $\dfrac{185-85}{0.35-0.10}\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}=400\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
