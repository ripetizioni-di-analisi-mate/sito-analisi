A $500\,\mathrm W$ heater transfers $80\%$ of its input energy to the water. How long will it take to warm $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water by $35\,\mathrm K$? Use $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and assume that the water stays in the same phase. Choose the value closest to the result.

- $352.8\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $441\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $282.24\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $882\,\mathrm{s}$.

## Explanation

The useful power is the rated power multiplied by the efficiency. First find $Q=mc\Delta T$ for the water, then divide it by the useful power. The quotient in joules per watt is in seconds.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{\Delta T c m}{P \eta} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(35\right) \left(4200\right) \left(1.2\right)}{\left(500\right) \left(0.8\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 441\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$
