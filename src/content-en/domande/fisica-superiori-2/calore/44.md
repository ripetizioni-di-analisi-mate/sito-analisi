A wall of area $2\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and thickness $0.10\,\mathrm m$ conducts $40\,\mathrm W$ across $20\,\mathrm K$. What conductivity follows?

- $1\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$
- $10\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$
- $0.1\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$

## Explanation

For a uniform plane wall, use $P=\dfrac{kA\Delta T}{d}$. Solving for $k$ puts the thickness with the power in the numerator and the area and temperature drop in the denominator. The units reduce to watts per metre per kelvin. The result describes the material, while the measured power also depends on the wall's geometry.

$$
k=\frac{40\cdot0.10}{2\cdot20}\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}=0.10\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}.
$$
