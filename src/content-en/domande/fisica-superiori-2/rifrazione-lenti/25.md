A light ray passes from air ($n_1=1$) into a glass of index $n_2=\sqrt3$ with an angle of incidence of $60^\circ$ to the normal. Part of the light is reflected and part is refracted. What angle do the reflected ray and the refracted ray make with each other?

- $90^\circ$
- $60^\circ$
- $30^\circ$
- $150^\circ$

## Explanation

By Snell's law $\sin\theta_2=\dfrac{n_1}{n_2}\sin60^\circ=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the refracted ray makes $30^\circ$ with the normal. By the law of reflection the reflected ray makes $60^\circ$ with the normal, in the air. The two rays leave the point of incidence on the same side of the normal, one above and one below the surface. Since the two half-lines of the normal form a straight angle, the angle between the reflected and the refracted ray is $180^\circ-60^\circ-30^\circ=90^\circ$. The value $30^\circ$ is the angle of refraction alone, while $60^\circ$ would be obtained by ignoring the deviation of the refracted ray.
