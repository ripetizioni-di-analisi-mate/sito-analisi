A small lamp lies on the bottom of a swimming pool $2.0\,\mathrm m$ deep, filled with water of index $n=\dfrac{4}{3}$. Light leaves the surface only through a circle centred on the vertical above the lamp. What is, approximately, the radius of this circle?

- $2.3\,\mathrm m$
- $1.5\,\mathrm m$
- $2.7\,\mathrm m$
- $1.8\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

A ray reaching the surface with an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle undergoes total internal reflection, so light leaves only where the incidence is less than $\theta_c$. Going from water to air the critical angle satisfies $\sin\theta_c=\dfrac{1}{n}=\dfrac{3}{4}$, so $\cos\theta_c=\sqrt{1-\dfrac{9}{16}}=\dfrac{\sqrt7}{4}$ and $\tan\theta_c=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt7}$. The ray incident at the critical angle reaches the surface at a distance $r$ from the vertical above the lamp, with $\tan\theta_c=\dfrac{r}{h}$. Therefore
$$
r=h\tan\theta_c=2.0\,\mathrm m\cdot\frac{3}{\sqrt7}\approx2.3\,\mathrm m.
$$
The value $1.5\,\mathrm m$ is obtained by using the sine instead of the tangent.
