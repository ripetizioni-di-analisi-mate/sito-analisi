A wavelength in glass is $350\,\mathrm{nm}$ with $n=1.6$. What is its vacuum value?

- $350\,\mathrm{nm}$
- $218.75\,\mathrm{nm}$
- $560\,\mathrm{nm}$
- $480\,\mathrm{nm}$

## Explanation

Light has a higher speed in vacuum at the same frequency. Multiply the wavelength in glass by $1.6$ to get the vacuum wavelength. Check by reversing the calculation. Dividing the vacuum wavelength by the index gives back $350\,\mathrm{nm}$.

$$
\lambda_0=n\lambda=1.6\cdot350\,\mathrm{nm}=560\,\mathrm{nm}.
$$
