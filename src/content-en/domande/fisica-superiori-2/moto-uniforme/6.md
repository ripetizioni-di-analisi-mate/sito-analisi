One cyclist is $70\,\mathrm m$ ahead of another. They travel in the same direction at constant velocities of $7$ and $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, respectively. How long does the second take to catch the first?

- $10\,\mathrm{s}$
- $14\,\mathrm{s}$
- $5.83\,\mathrm{s}$
- $28\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The initial gap is $70\,\mathrm{m}$ and shrinks by $5\,\mathrm{m}$ each second because both cyclists move in the same direction. Their relative speed is therefore the difference of their speeds. At the meeting the first cyclist's lead is zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_I &= \frac{70}{12-7}\,\mathrm{s} = 14\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking the second rider's starting point as the origin, the two coordinates at that time are $70+7\cdot14=168\,\mathrm m$ and $12\cdot14=168\,\mathrm m$.
