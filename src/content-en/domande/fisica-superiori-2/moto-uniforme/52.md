During a thunderstorm a flash of lightning is seen and the thunder is heard $6.0\,\mathrm s$ later. Sound travels through air at a constant speed of $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the time taken by the light is negligible. How far away did the lightning strike?

- $57\,\mathrm m$
- $1020\,\mathrm m$
- $4080\,\mathrm m$
- $2040\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The light of the flash arrives practically at the instant the lightning strikes, so the $6.0\,\mathrm s$ are the travel time of the sound. Sound covers the distance at constant speed, so the distance is the product of speed and time:
$$
d=340\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot6.0\,\mathrm s=2040\,\mathrm m.
$$
The value $1020\,\mathrm m$ would be obtained by halving the time as for an echo, which instead travels the distance twice. The value $57\,\mathrm m$ comes from dividing the speed by the time.
