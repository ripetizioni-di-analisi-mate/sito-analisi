An object moves at $+7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $8\,\mathrm s$ and then at $-6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $6\,\mathrm s$. What is the total displacement?

- $92\,\mathrm{m}$
- $56\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-36\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The displacement on the first leg is $56\,\mathrm{m}$ and on the second it is $-36\,\mathrm{m}$. Their signed sum is $20\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance is the sum of the magnitudes, giving $92\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x_1 &= 7\cdot 8 = 56\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\Delta x_2 &= -6\cdot 6 = -36\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\Delta x &= (56)+(-36) = 20\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The positive result places the endpoint on the positive side of the starting point. The second leg reduces the gain from the first leg by $36\,\mathrm m$.
