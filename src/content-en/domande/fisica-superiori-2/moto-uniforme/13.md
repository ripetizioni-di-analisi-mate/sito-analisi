A bicycle travels $90\,\mathrm m$ at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, stops for $5\,\mathrm s$, then continues in the same direction for another $30\,\mathrm m$ at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the average velocity $v_m$ for the whole trip, including the stop?

- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The two moving times are $15$ and $10\,\mathrm s$. Including the stop gives $30\,\mathrm s$ in total. The displacement is $120\,\mathrm m$ because both legs are in the same direction. Their ratio is $v_m=\dfrac{120}{30}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Using only the moving times would give $4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

Using only the $25\,\mathrm s$ of travel would give $4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the average during motion alone.
