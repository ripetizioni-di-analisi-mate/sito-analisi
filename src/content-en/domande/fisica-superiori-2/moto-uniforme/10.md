A skater starts at $x=-6\,\mathrm m$, reaches $x=8\,\mathrm m$, then returns as far as $x=-4\,\mathrm m$. The direction is constant within each leg. What is the total distance $d$ travelled?

- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $26\,\mathrm{m}$
- $14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The first leg contributes $8-(-6)=14\,\mathrm m$. The second has displacement $-4-8=-12\,\mathrm m$, so it adds $12\,\mathrm m$ to the distance. Adding the magnitudes gives $d=26\,\mathrm m$. Final coordinate minus initial coordinate gives $2\,\mathrm m$, which is the displacement.

The outward and return legs both cross the origin. Their lengths still add.
