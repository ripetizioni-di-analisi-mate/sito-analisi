A cyclist travels $400\,\mathrm{m}$ in a straight line at constant speed $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does it take?

- $4000\,\mathrm{s}$
- $40\,\mathrm{s}$
- $50\,\mathrm{s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Both quantities are already in SI units. At $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the cyclist covers $10\,\mathrm{m}$ each second, so divide the distance by the speed to get $40\,\mathrm{s}$. Multiplying the exact time by $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives the original distance $400\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta t &= \frac{d}{u} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{400}{10}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 40\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$
