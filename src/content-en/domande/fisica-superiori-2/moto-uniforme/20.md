At $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ a position–time line gives $x=-1\,\mathrm{m}$; at $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$ it gives $x=-7\,\mathrm{m}$. If uniform motion holds from $t=0$, what is $x(0)$?

- $-1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The change in position is −6 m in two seconds, so velocity is −3 m/s. Going back from the first reading means adding nine metres.

$$
x(0)=x(3)-v\cdot3=-1-(-3)\cdot3=8\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

Starting at $8\,\mathrm m$, the coordinate falls by $15\,\mathrm m$ in five seconds and reaches $-7\,\mathrm m$. This check uses the second reading and the assumption that the law holds from time zero.
