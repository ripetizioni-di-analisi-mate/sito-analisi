A cart moves in a straight line at constant velocity. At time $2\,\mathrm s$ it is at $x=-3\,\mathrm m$ and at time $6\,\mathrm s$ at $x=9\,\mathrm m$. Which of the following pairs $(t;x)$, with $t$ in seconds and $x$ in metres, describes another position of the same motion?

- $(0;-6)$
- $(4;1)$
- $(9;12)$
- $(5;6)$

## Explanation

In $4\,\mathrm s$ the position increases by $12\,\mathrm m$, so the velocity is $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since the velocity is constant, the position at time $t$ is obtained by starting from $-3\,\mathrm m$ at time $2\,\mathrm s$ and adding the displacement $3(t-2)$.

$$
v=\frac{9-(-3)}{6-2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=3\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad x(5\,\mathrm s)=[-3+3(5-2)]\,\mathrm m=6\,\mathrm m.
$$

The pair $(5;6)$ therefore belongs to the motion. The same law gives $x(0)=-9\,\mathrm m$, $x(4\,\mathrm s)=3\,\mathrm m$ and $x(9\,\mathrm s)=18\,\mathrm m$, so the other pairs are not consistent with the motion.
