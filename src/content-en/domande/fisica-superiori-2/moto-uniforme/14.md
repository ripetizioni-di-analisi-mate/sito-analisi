A runner covers two $24\,\mathrm m$ legs in the same direction with no stops. The velocity on the first is $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What constant velocity $v_2$ on the second gives an average of $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over the whole route?

- $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The total time must be $\dfrac{48}{4}\,\mathrm s=12\,\mathrm s$. The first leg takes $\dfrac{24}{3}\,\mathrm s=8\,\mathrm s$, leaving $4\,\mathrm s$ for the second distance. Thus $v_2=\dfrac{24}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The two legs are equal in length and take different times, so the arithmetic mean of their velocities would be inappropriate.

The result exceeds the target average because the second leg must compensate for the slower first leg.
