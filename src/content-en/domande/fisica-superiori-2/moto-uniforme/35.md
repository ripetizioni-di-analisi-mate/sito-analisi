A trolley is at $x=2\,\mathrm m$ at $t=0$ and at $x=14\,\mathrm m$ at $t=60\,\mathrm s$. Between the two instants it moves along the $x$ axis and may stop or reverse direction. What is the shortest distance travelled consistent with these data?

- $16\,\mathrm m$
- It cannot be determined without knowing the velocity
- $12\,\mathrm m$
- $24\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The displacement between the two instants is $\Delta x=14\,\mathrm m-2\,\mathrm m=12\,\mathrm m$. The distance travelled is the sum of the moduli of the displacements of the individual stretches, so it is never less than the modulus of the overall displacement. A motion always in the same direction, even with stops, covers exactly $12\,\mathrm m$, so the minimum is attained. Each reversal adds twice the stretch covered backwards, and therefore takes the distance beyond $12\,\mathrm m$. The velocity is not needed, because the shortest distance depends only on the two positions.
