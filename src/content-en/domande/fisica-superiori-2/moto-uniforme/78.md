A slower object starts behind a faster one. Both travel in the same direction at positive constant speeds. What happens to the gap?

- It decreases uniformly
- It grows uniformly
- It remains constant
- It vanishes after the gap divided by the sum of speeds

## Explanation

Each second the object in front adds more metres to its position. The difference of the coordinates grows at a rate equal to the velocity difference.

For example, with speeds of $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ ahead and $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ behind, the gap grows by two metres each second. The trailing body therefore stays behind.
