The position $s$ of a body, measured in centimetres, is a function of the time $t$, measured in seconds. At a certain instant $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=12\,\mathrm{cm/s}$. What is the same derivative if position is expressed in metres and time in minutes?

- $720\,\mathrm{m/min}$
- $0.12\,\mathrm{m/min}$
- $7.2\,\mathrm{m/min}$
- $0.002\,\mathrm{m/min}$

## Explanation

Since $1\,\mathrm{cm}=0.01\,\mathrm{m}$, we have $12\,\mathrm{cm/s}=0.12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A minute contains $60$ seconds, so a change of $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$ per second corresponds to a change of $0.12\cdot60\,\mathrm{m}$ per minute. Therefore the derivative equals $7.2\,\mathrm{m/min}$. The value $720\,\mathrm{m/min}$ comes from multiplying by $60$ without converting centimetres to metres.
