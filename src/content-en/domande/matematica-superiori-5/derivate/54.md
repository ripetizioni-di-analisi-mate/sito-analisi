In the figure, the line is tangent to the graph of the differentiable function $f$ at the point with $x$-coordinate $2$. What is $f'(2)$?

::grafico{f="4/x;4-x" punti="2,2,pieno;0,4,pieno;4,0,pieno" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=6 uguale=1 legenda="y = f(x);\text{tangent line}" titolo="The tangent line passes through the points $(0, 4)$, $(2, 2)$ and $(4, 0)$"}

- $1$
- $2$
- $-1$
- $4$

## Explanation

The derivative $f'(2)$ is the slope of the **tangent line** at the point with $x$-coordinate $2$. The line passes through $(0, 4)$ and $(4, 0)$, so its slope is $\dfrac{0 - 4}{4 - 0} = -1$. Hence $f'(2) = -1$. The value $2$ is $f(2)$, the $y$-coordinate of the point of tangency, and the value $4$ is the $y$-intercept of the line.
