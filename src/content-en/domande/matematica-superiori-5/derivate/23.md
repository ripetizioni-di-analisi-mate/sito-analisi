The function $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ is defined on $[0, +\infty)$. What is the limit as $h \to 0^{+}$ of the difference quotient $\dfrac{f(h) - f(0)}{h}$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $+\infty$
- $1$

## Explanation

Since $f(0) = 0$, for $h > 0$ the difference quotient is $\dfrac{\sqrt{h}}{h} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{h}}$. As $h \to 0^{+}$ we have $\sqrt{h} \to 0^{+}$, so the quotient tends to $+\infty$. Therefore $f$ is not differentiable at $0$ and the graph has a vertical tangent at the point $(0, 0)$. The formula $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$ holds only for $x > 0$, that is, at the interior points of the domain.
