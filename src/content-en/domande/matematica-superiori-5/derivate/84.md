At $x=a$, a differentiable function satisfies $f(a)=0$. Which condition guarantees that $|f|$ has derivative zero at $a$?

- $f'(a)>0$
- $f'(a)=1$
- $f'(a)<0$
- $f'(a)=0$

## Explanation

The absolute value of the difference quotient of $|f|$ is $|f(a+h)/h|$. When $f'(a)=0$, this tends to zero, so the signed quotient also tends to zero.
