At which points of the interval $(0, 2\pi)$ is the tangent line to the graph of $f(x) = x + 2\cos x$ horizontal?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $x = \dfrac{11\pi}{6}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The tangent line is horizontal at the points where the derivative vanishes. Since the derivative of $\cos x$ is $-\sin x$, we have $f'(x) = 1 - 2\sin x$. The equation $f'(x) = 0$ becomes $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which on $(0, 2\pi)$ has the solutions $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. The solutions $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$ solve $\sin x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and come from taking $\sin x$ instead of $-\sin x$ as the derivative. The solutions $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$ solve $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
