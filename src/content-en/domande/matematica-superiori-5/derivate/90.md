The function $f(x)=(x-4)^{\frac{1}{3}}$ is defined using the real cube root. What happens to its difference quotient at $4$?

- It approaches $+\infty$ from both sides.
- It approaches $-\infty$ from both sides.
- It approaches zero from both sides.
- Its one-sided limits have opposite signs.

## Explanation

For $h\ne0$ the difference quotient equals $1/|h|^{2/3}$. It approaches $+\infty$ from both sides, so the graph has a vertical tangent.
