The function $f$ is differentiable at $x_0 = 3$ and $f'(3) = 2$. What is the value of $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(3 + 2h) - f(3)}{h}$?

- $4$
- $2$
- $1$
- $6$

## Explanation

For $h \neq 0$ we multiply and divide by $2$:
$$
\frac{f(3 + 2h) - f(3)}{h} = 2 \cdot \frac{f(3 + 2h) - f(3)}{2h} .
$$
Setting $k = 2h$, as $h \to 0$ we have $k \to 0$, so the second factor is the difference quotient $\dfrac{f(3 + k) - f(3)}{k}$, which tends to $f'(3) = 2$. Therefore the limit equals $2 \cdot 2 = 4$.
