For $a > 0$, the tangent line to the graph of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ at the point $\left( a, \dfrac{1}{a} \right)$ forms a triangle with the coordinate axes. What is the area of the triangle?

- $1$
- $2$
- $4$
- $\dfrac{2}{a^2}$

## Explanation

Since $f'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, the tangent at the point with abscissa $a$ is
$$
y = \frac{1}{a} - \frac{1}{a^2}\,(x - a) = \frac{2}{a} - \frac{x}{a^2} .
$$
For $x = 0$ we have $y = \dfrac{2}{a}$, and for $y = 0$ we have $x = 2a$. The triangle therefore has legs of length $2a$ and $\dfrac{2}{a}$, and its area equals $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2a \cdot \dfrac{2}{a} = 2$, whatever $a > 0$ is.
