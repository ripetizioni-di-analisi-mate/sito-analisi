For a point in rectilinear motion the average velocity between the instant $3\,\mathrm{s}$ and the instant $3\,\mathrm{s} + \Delta t$ equals $6 + 2\,\Delta t$, in $\mathrm{m/s}$ with $\Delta t$ in seconds, for every $\Delta t > 0$. What is the instantaneous velocity at the instant $3\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The instantaneous velocity is the limit of the average velocity over shorter and shorter intervals starting at the given instant. So
$$
v(3) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \left(6 + 2\,\Delta t\right) = 6\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the average velocity over the interval of length $1\,\mathrm{s}$, which is not short enough.
