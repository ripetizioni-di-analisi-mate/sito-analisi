Let $f(x)=\dfrac{\sin{\left(2 x + 5 \right)}}{x}$ with $x \neq 0$. What is $f'(x)$?

- $\dfrac{2 x \cos{\left(2 x + 5 \right)} + \sin{\left(2 x + 5 \right)}}{x^{2}}$
- $\dfrac{2 \cos{\left(2 x + 5 \right)}}{x}$
- $\dfrac{x \cos{\left(2 x + 5 \right)} - \sin{\left(2 x + 5 \right)}}{x^{2}}$
- $\dfrac{2 x \cos{\left(2 x + 5 \right)} - \sin{\left(2 x + 5 \right)}}{x^{2}}$

## Explanation

By the chain rule the derivative of the numerator is $2\cos(2x+5)$, while the derivative of the denominator is $1$. The quotient rule therefore gives
$$
f'(x)=\frac{2\cos(2x+5)\cdot x-\sin(2x+5)\cdot 1}{x^2}=\frac{2x\cos(2x+5)-\sin(2x+5)}{x^2} .
$$
