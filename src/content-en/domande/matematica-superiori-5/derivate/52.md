The function $f(x)=\ln{\left(x^{2} + 5 \right)}$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$. Which expression gives $f'(x)$?

- $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} + 5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{x^{2} + 5}$
- $2 x \ln{\left(x^{2} + 5 \right)}$
- $\dfrac{2 x}{\left(x^{2} + 5\right)^{2}}$

## Explanation

By the chain rule the derivative of $\ln g(x)$ is $\dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}$. With $g(x)=x^2+5$ one has $g'(x)=2x$, so $f'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{x^2+5}$.
