The function $f$ is differentiable at $x_0$, while the function $g$ is not differentiable at $x_0$. Which statement is certainly true?

- $f \cdot g$ is not differentiable at $x_0$
- $f + g$ is not differentiable at $x_0$
- $f + g$ is differentiable at $x_0$
- $f \cdot g$ is differentiable at $x_0$

## Explanation

If $f + g$ were differentiable at $x_0$, by the **difference rule** the function $(f + g) - f = g$ would also be differentiable at $x_0$, contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore $f + g$ **is not differentiable** at $x_0$. For the product the conclusion depends on the functions. With $f(x) = x$ and $g(x) = |x|$ at $x_0 = 0$ the product $x\,|x|$ has difference quotient $|h|$, which tends to $0$, so it is differentiable. With $f(x) = 1$ and $g(x) = |x|$ the product is $|x|$, which is not differentiable at $0$.
