Which line passes through the point $(1, 1)$ and is perpendicular to the tangent line to the graph of $f(x) = x^2$ at that point?

- $y = 2x - 1$
- $y = -2x + 3$
- $y = \dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $y = -\dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

Since $f'(x) = 2x$, the tangent line at the point $(1, 1)$ has slope $f'(1) = 2$ and is $y = 2x - 1$. Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if the product of their slopes equals $-1$, so the required line has slope $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. The line with slope $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ through the point $(1, 1)$ is $y = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2}\,(x - 1)$, that is, $y = -\dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{3}{2}$.
