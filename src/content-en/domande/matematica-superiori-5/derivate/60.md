The tangent function is a quotient, $f(x) = \tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$. Which expression does its derivative have, where $\cos x \neq 0$?

- $f'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{\cos^{2} x}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{\cos^{2} x}$
- $f'(x) = -\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sin^{2} x}$

## Explanation

By the **quotient rule**
$$
f'(x) = \frac{\cos x \cdot \cos x - \sin x \cdot (-\sin x)}{\cos^{2} x} = \frac{\cos^{2} x + \sin^{2} x}{\cos^{2} x} .
$$
Since $\cos^{2} x + \sin^{2} x = 1$, we get $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{\cos^{2} x}$. The result $-\dfrac{1}{\cos^{2} x}$ comes from a sign error in the derivative of the cosine, and $-\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}$ is the quotient of the derivatives of the numerator and the denominator.
