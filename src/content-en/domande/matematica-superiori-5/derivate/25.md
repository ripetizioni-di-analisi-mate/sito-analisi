For the function $f(x) = \sqrt{1 - \cos x}$, what is the right derivative $f'_{+}(0)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

## Explanation

Since $f(0) = 0$, for $h > 0$ the difference quotient is $\dfrac{\sqrt{1 - \cos h}}{h}$. For $h > 0$ we have $h = \sqrt{h^2}$, so the quotient can be written as $\sqrt{\dfrac{1 - \cos h}{h^2}}$. By the fundamental limit $\dfrac{1 - \cos h}{h^2} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore $f'_{+}(0) = \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. For $h < 0$ we have $h = -\sqrt{h^2}$ and the left derivative equals $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so $x = 0$ is a corner point.
