Why is the derivative of $f(x)=\dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}$ negative on each of the two intervals in its domain?

- The derivative's numerator reduces to $-7$ and its denominator is a positive square.
- The numerator of the function is always negative.
- The function is negative on both intervals.
- The derivative is constant and equals $-7$.

## Explanation

In the quotient rule the numerator is $2(x-3)-(2x+1)=-7$. The denominator $(x-3)^2$ stays positive at every point in the domain.
