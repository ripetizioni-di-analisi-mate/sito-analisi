Let $A$ and $B$ be events with $P(A\cup B)=\dfrac{5}{6}$ and $P(B)=\dfrac{1}{2}$. What is $P(A^c\mid B^c)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{5}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

Condition $B^c$ restricts attention to outcomes outside $B$, with total probability $1-\dfrac12=\dfrac12$. Among them we need the outcomes also outside $A$. An outcome lies outside $A$ and outside $B$ exactly when it belongs to neither event, that is, when it lies outside $A\cup B$. Therefore $A^c\cap B^c=(A\cup B)^c$, whose probability is $1-\dfrac56=\dfrac16$ (this identity is called De Morgan's law). The ratio $\dfrac{\dfrac16}{\dfrac12}$ equals $\dfrac13$. The probability $\dfrac16$ alone is relative to all outcomes.
