In a model for a four-child family, each child is a boy with probability $1/2$ independently of the others. When finding the chance of at least three boys, which counts do we add?

- Three sequences with three boys and one with four.
- Four sequences with three boys and one with four.
- One sequence with three boys and one with four.
- Four sequences with three boys and four with four.

## Explanation

In a sequence with three boys there are four choices for the position of the one girl. Add the single sequence with four boys. All sixteen sequences are equally likely, so the probability is $5/16$.
