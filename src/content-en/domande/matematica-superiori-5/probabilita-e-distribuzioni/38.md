A random variable is reported to satisfy $E[X]=3$ and $E[X^2]=8$. Which conclusion is correct?

- Some random variable has these two moments, and its variance is $1$.
- Some random variable has these two moments, and its variance is $5$.
- No random variable has these two moments, because the variance would be negative.
- Whether such a random variable exists depends on the signs of the support values.

## Explanation

Variance is the expected value of a square, so it is nonnegative. Substituting the data in the identity $\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-E[X]^2$ gives $8-3^2=-1$. Consequently, no random variable has the two reported moments. Indeed, a random variable with mean $3$ satisfies $E[X^2]\ge9$, with equality only when it equals $3$ with probability $1$.
