Two fair coins are tossed independently. Let $A$ and $B$ denote heads on the first and second coins, respectively, and let $C$ denote different outcomes on the two coins. How are the three events related?

- They are pairwise independent but not mutually independent.
- They are pairwise disjoint.
- They are mutually independent.
- No pair is independent.

## Explanation

Heads–heads, heads–tails, tails–heads and tails–tails are equally likely. Each event contains two outcomes. The intersections $A\cap B$, $A\cap C$ and $B\cap C$ contain heads–heads, heads–tails and tails–heads, respectively. Each therefore has probability $\dfrac14=\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac12$. The triple intersection is empty because two heads are identical. Its probability of zero differs from $\left(\dfrac12\right)^3$, so independence holds only pairwise.
