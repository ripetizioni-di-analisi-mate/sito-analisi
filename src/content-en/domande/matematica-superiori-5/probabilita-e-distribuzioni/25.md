Categories $B_1$ and $B_2$ are disjoint, cover all production and have positive probabilities. The rejection rate is identical and positive in both categories. How does $P(B_1)$ change after learning that the part was rejected?

- It always increases.
- It stays the same.
- It becomes one half.
- It always decreases.

## Explanation

The two categories are disjoint and cover all production, so they form a partition. Let $r>0$ denote their common rejection rate. The overall rate is $rP(B_1)+rP(B_2)=r$ because the two category shares sum to one. Bayes' formula gives $P(B_1\mid\text{deviation})=\dfrac{rP(B_1)}{r}=P(B_1)$. Rejection is equally probable in both categories, so observing it leaves the probability of the source unchanged.
