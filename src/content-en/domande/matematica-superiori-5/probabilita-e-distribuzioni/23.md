Suppose $P(A)=\dfrac{1}{2}$, $P(B)=\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $P(A\cap B)=\dfrac{1}{4}$. Which conclusion is correct?

- They are independent because $P(A\cap B)>0$.
- They are disjoint because $P(A\cap B)<P(B)$.
- They are dependent because $P(A\cap B)\ne P(A)P(B)$.
- They are independent because $P(A\cap B)<P(A)$.

## Explanation

Under independence the probability of the intersection would be $\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac34=\dfrac38$. The given value is $\dfrac14$, so the independence criterion fails. Comparing $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac13$ with $P(A)=\dfrac12$ gives the same result. The positive intersection probability shows that both events can occur together. Sharing outcomes is a different property from the equality of probabilities required for independence.
