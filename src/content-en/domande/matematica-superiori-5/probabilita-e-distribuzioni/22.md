A tile is drawn at random from four blue tiles and six red tiles. Let $B$ be the event “the tile is green.” What does the elementary definition tell us about $P(\text{red}\mid B)$?

- The probability is zero because no green tile is red.
- The conditional probability is undefined because $P(B)=0$.
- The probability equals the unconditional probability of a red tile.
- The probability is one because blue tiles are excluded.

## Explanation

Event $B$ consists of the green tiles, of which there are none, so $P(B)=0$. Also $P(\text{red}\cap B)=0$, since the intersection is contained in $B$. The elementary definition would require the ratio $\dfrac{0}{0}$, which is undefined. Since green cannot be observed, there is no reference group for the conditional probability of drawing a red tile.
