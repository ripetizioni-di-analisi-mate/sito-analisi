One of two coins is chosen at random, each with the same probability: one is fair, the other has heads on both faces. The chosen coin is tossed twice and shows heads both times. What is the probability that the two-headed coin was chosen?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

Once the coin has been chosen, the two tosses are independent. With the fair coin, two heads have probability $\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac12=\dfrac14$; with the two-headed coin, probability $1$. By Bayes' theorem, the posterior probability of the two-headed coin is $\dfrac{\frac12\cdot1}{\frac12\cdot1+\frac12\cdot\frac14}=\dfrac{\frac12}{\frac58}=\dfrac45$. The value $\dfrac23$ is the posterior probability after a single head, $\dfrac{\frac12}{\frac12+\frac14}$. The second head increases it further.
