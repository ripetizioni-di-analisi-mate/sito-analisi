With the mean fixed, $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ is replaced by $N(\mu,4\sigma^2)$, with $\sigma>0$. How does the density height at $\mu$ change?

- It is divided by four.
- It doubles.
- It is halved.
- It stays the same.

## Explanation

At the center of a normal distribution the exponent is zero, so the density at $\mu$ is $\dfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}$. The new variance is four times as large. Its square root, the standard deviation, therefore changes from $\sigma$ to $2\sigma$. Substitution in the denominator gives

$$
\frac{1}{2\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}=\frac12\cdot\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}.
$$

The curve widens and the peak is halved, while the total area remains one.
