The function $f(x)=x^2-2$ is considered only for rational $x$ with $1\le x\le2$. Its endpoint values have opposite signs. Why can it still have no zero in the specified domain?

- The domain does not contain the whole real interval between its endpoints.
- A quadratic polynomial cannot change sign.
- The left endpoint is outside the domain.
- The positive root is smaller than one.

## Explanation

The positive root is $\sqrt2$, which belongs to the real interval but is irrational. Continuity of the restriction to the rationals does not supply the hypotheses of the intermediate value theorem on the whole real interval.
