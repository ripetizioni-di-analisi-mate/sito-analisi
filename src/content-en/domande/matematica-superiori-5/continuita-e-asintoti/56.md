The graph of $f(x) = \dfrac{3e^{x} + 1}{e^{x} + 2}$ has horizontal asymptotes. Which ones, and on which side?

- $y = 3$ both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = 3$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = 3$ as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = 3$ as $x \to +\infty$ and none as $x \to -\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ we have $e^{x} \to +\infty$; dividing the numerator and the denominator by $e^{x}$ we obtain $\dfrac{3 + e^{-x}}{1 + 2e^{-x}}$, which tends to $3$. So $y = 3$ is a horizontal asymptote as $x \to +\infty$.

As $x \to -\infty$ we have $e^{x} \to 0$, therefore the numerator and the denominator tend to $1$ and to $2$. So $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is a horizontal asymptote as $x \to -\infty$.
