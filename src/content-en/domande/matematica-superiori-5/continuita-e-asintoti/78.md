Which statement about the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1}$ and its asymptotes is true?

- The line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote and the graph intersects it at the point $(0, 0)$
- The lines $x = 1$ and $x = -1$ are vertical asymptotes
- The function has no horizontal asymptotes
- The line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote and the graph never intersects it

## Explanation

The denominator $x^2 + 1$ is always positive, so the function is defined on $\mathbb{R}$ and has no vertical asymptotes. The numerator has lower degree than the denominator, therefore $f(x) \to 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$ and the line $y = 0$ is a **horizontal asymptote**. Moreover $f(0) = 0$, so the graph passes through the origin and intersects the asymptote. The definition of asymptote only concerns the behavior as $x \to \pm\infty$, so the graph can intersect a horizontal asymptote.
