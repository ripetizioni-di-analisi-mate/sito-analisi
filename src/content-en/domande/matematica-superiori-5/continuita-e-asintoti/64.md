The figure shows the graph of a function $f$ defined on $\mathbb{R}$: the filled dots mark the values of the function, the hollow dots points that do not belong to the graph. What type of discontinuity does $f$ have at $x = 1$ and at $x = 3$?

::grafico{linee="-1,-1 1,1;1,3 5,7" punti="1,1,vuoto;1,3,pieno;3,5,vuoto;3,1,pieno" segmenti="3,1,3,5" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=8 titolo="The graph of $f$; at $x = 1$ the points $(1, 1)$ hollow and $(1, 3)$ filled, at $x = 3$ the points $(3, 5)$ hollow and $(3, 1)$ filled"}

- At $x = 1$ removable, at $x = 3$ a jump
- A jump at both points
- At $x = 1$ of the second kind, at $x = 3$ removable
- At $x = 1$ a jump, at $x = 3$ removable

## Explanation

At $x = 1$ the left-hand limit is $1$ and the right-hand limit is $3$. The two limits are finite and different, so at $x = 1$ there is a **jump discontinuity** of size $2$. At $x = 3$ the right-hand and left-hand limits both equal $5$, while $f(3) = 1$. The limit exists, is finite and differs from the value of the function, therefore at $x = 3$ the discontinuity is **removable**.
