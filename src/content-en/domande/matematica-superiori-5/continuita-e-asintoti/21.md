A function $f$ is continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}$, and $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = -\infty$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x) = 2$. Which property of $f$ follows?

- $f$ is bounded below
- $f$ takes the value $3$
- $f$ has a maximum on $\mathbb{R}$
- $f$ vanishes at least at one point

## Explanation

Since $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$, by the definition of limit there exists $a$ with $f(a) < 0$. Since $f(x) \to 2 > 0$ as $x \to +\infty$, by the **sign-preservation theorem** there exists $b > a$ with $f(b) > 0$.

The function is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $f(a)\, f(b) < 0$, so by **Bolzano's theorem** there exists $c \in (a, b)$ with $f(c) = 0$.

The other properties are not certain. The function $f(x) = 2 - e^{-x}$ is continuous, has the given limits and takes only values less than $2$, so it has no maximum and does not take the value $3$. Moreover, a function with limit $-\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$ is not bounded below.
