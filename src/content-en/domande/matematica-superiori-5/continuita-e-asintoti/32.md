Let $f(x) = x^{3} + x - k$ with $k \in \mathbb{R}$. For which values of $k$ does the zero theorem, applied on $[0, 1]$, guarantee that the equation $x^{3} + x = k$ has a solution in $(0, 1)$?

- $0 < k < 2$
- $k < 0$ or $k > 2$
- $k > 0$
- $0 \leq k \leq 2$

## Explanation

The function $f$ is a polynomial, so it is continuous on $[0, 1]$. The zero theorem applies if $f(0) \cdot f(1) < 0$.

We have $f(0) = -k$ and $f(1) = 2 - k$, therefore the condition is $-k(2 - k) < 0$, that is $k(2 - k) > 0$. The product $k(2 - k)$ is positive for $0 < k < 2$, so for these values the theorem guarantees a solution in $(0, 1)$. For $k = 0$ and for $k = 2$ the product $f(0) \cdot f(1)$ equals $0$ and the hypothesis of the theorem is not satisfied.
