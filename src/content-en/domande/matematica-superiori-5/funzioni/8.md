For $0<u<v$ and $f(x)=3-2/x$, which difference directly proves that $f$ is increasing?

- $f(v)-f(u)=3(v-u)$
- $f(v)-f(u)=\dfrac{2(u-v)}{uv}>0$
- $f(v)-f(u)=\dfrac{2(v-u)}{uv}>0$
- $f(v)-f(u)=0$

## Explanation

The constants cancel, leaving $2/u-2/v$. Combining fractions gives a positive value because $v>u>0$.
