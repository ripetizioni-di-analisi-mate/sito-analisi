At how many points does the line $y = x - 2$ meet the parabola $y = x^2 - 3x + 2$?

- At two points, $(1, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$
- At no point
- At two points, $(2, 0)$ and $(4, 2)$
- At a single point, $(2, 0)$

## Explanation

At the common points the two curves have the same $y$-coordinate, so the $x$-coordinates solve $x^2 - 3x + 2 = x - 2$, that is, $x^2 - 4x + 4 = 0$. The discriminant of this equation is $\Delta = 16 - 16 = 0$, therefore there is a single solution, $x = 2$, because $x^2 - 4x + 4 = (x - 2)^2$. The corresponding $y$-coordinate is $y = 2 - 2 = 0$. Hence the line meets the parabola only at the point $(2, 0)$, that is, it is tangent to the parabola. The points $(1, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$ are the zeros of the parabola, and the point $(1, 0)$ does not lie on the line.
