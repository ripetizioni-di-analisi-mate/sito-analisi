The functions $f$ and $g$ are defined on $\mathbb{R}$; $f$ is increasing and $g$ is decreasing. Which statement is true for every pair of functions with these properties?

- $g \circ f$ is decreasing
- $f + g$ is increasing
- $f \cdot g$ is decreasing
- $g \circ f$ is increasing

## Explanation

Let $x_1 < x_2$. Since $f$ is increasing, we have $f(x_1) < f(x_2)$. Since $g$ is decreasing, applied to the two numbers $f(x_1) < f(x_2)$ it gives $g(f(x_1)) > g(f(x_2))$, so $g \circ f$ is **decreasing**. The statements about the sum and the product are false in general. With $f(x) = x$ and $g(x) = -2x$ the sum $f + g = -x$ is decreasing. With $f(x) = x$ and $g(x) = -x$ the product $f \cdot g = -x^2$ equals $-1$ at $-1$ and $0$ at $0$, therefore it is not decreasing.
