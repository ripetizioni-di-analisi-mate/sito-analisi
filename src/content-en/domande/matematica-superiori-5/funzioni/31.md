Let $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x^2+1}$ on $\mathbb R$. What is its range?

- $[1,+\infty)$
- $(0,1)$
- $[0,1]$
- $(0,1]$

## Explanation

The denominator is at least $1$ and positive, so $0<f(x)\leq1$. For each $y\in(0,1]$, choose $x=\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{y}-1}$. This gives $f(x)=y$. The upper endpoint $1$ is attained at $x=0$. The lower endpoint $0$ is excluded because the numerator is $1$, even though choosing large $|x|$ makes the quotient arbitrarily small. This accounts for the different brackets in $(0,1]$.
