The figure shows the graph of a function that passes through the points $(-1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 3)$; the dashed line is $y = -1$, which the graph approaches for negative $x$ of large absolute value, without ever reaching it. Which function is represented?

::grafico{f="2^(x+1)-1" asintoti="y=-1" punti="-1,0,pieno;0,1,pieno;1,3,pieno" xmin=-5 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=7 legenda=0}

- $f(x) = 3^{x}$
- $f(x) = 2^{x + 1} - 1$
- $f(x) = 2x + 1$
- $f(x) = x^2 + x + 1$

## Explanation

All four functions pass through $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 3)$, so the third point is needed. At $x = -1$ we have $3^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, $(-1)^2 - 1 + 1 = 1$ and $2 \cdot (-1) + 1 = -1$, while $2^{0} - 1 = 0$. Therefore only $f(x) = 2^{x + 1} - 1$ passes through $(-1, 0)$. The graph is also consistent with the dashed line, because $2^{x + 1} > 0$ implies $f(x) > -1$ for every $x$, and $2^{x + 1}$ becomes as small as we like for negative $x$ of large absolute value.
