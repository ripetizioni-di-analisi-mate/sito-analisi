Let $f(x) = 2x + 1$ and $g(x) = x^2$. For which $x$ does $(f \circ g)(x) = (g \circ f)(x)$ hold?

- For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- Only for $x = 0$
- $x = 0$ and $x = 2$
- $x = 0$ and $x = -2$

## Explanation

The composite $f \circ g$ applies $g$ first and then $f$, so $(f \circ g)(x) = 2x^2 + 1$. The composite $g \circ f$ applies $f$ first and then $g$, so $(g \circ f)(x) = (2x + 1)^2 = 4x^2 + 4x + 1$. The equality $2x^2 + 1 = 4x^2 + 4x + 1$ is equivalent to $2x^2 + 4x = 0$, that is, to $2x\,(x + 2) = 0$. Therefore the two composites agree only for $x = 0$ and for $x = -2$, and they differ at every other point.
