On a graph, horizontal tick marks are spaced $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$ apart and vertical tick marks are spaced $2\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. To go from one point to another, move three tick intervals to the right and three up. What is the slope?

- $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

One tick interval represents different values on the two axes. Three horizontal intervals represent $3\cdot 0.5\,\mathrm{s}=1.5\,\mathrm{s}$, and three vertical intervals represent $3\cdot 2\,\mathrm{m}=6\,\mathrm{m}$. The slope is therefore
$$
\frac{6\,\mathrm{m}}{1.5\,\mathrm{s}}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from counting tick intervals as if the axes had the same scale. The value $\dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the reciprocal ratio, while $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives only the vertical change.
