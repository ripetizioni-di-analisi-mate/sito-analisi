Let $f$ be a function differentiable on an interval $I$, with $|f'(x)|\le\dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $x\in I$, and let $p\in I$ be a point with $f(p)=p$. The sequence defined by $x_{n+1}=f(x_n)$ has all its terms in $I$, and $|x_0-p|\le8$. What is the smallest upper bound for $|x_3-p|$ that holds for every function with these properties?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

The mean value theorem, applied to $f$ on the interval with endpoints $x_n$ and $p$, gives $|f(x_n)-f(p)|\le\dfrac{1}{2}|x_n-p|$. Since $f(p)=p$ and $f(x_n)=x_{n+1}$, it follows that $|x_{n+1}-p|\le\dfrac{1}{2}|x_n-p|$. Applying this inequality three times gives $|x_3-p|\le\dfrac{8}{2^3}=1$. The bound cannot be improved, because with $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{2}$, $p=0$ and $x_0=8$ one gets $x_3=1$.
