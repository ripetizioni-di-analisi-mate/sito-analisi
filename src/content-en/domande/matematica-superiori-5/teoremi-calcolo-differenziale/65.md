In the limit of $(e^x-1)/x^2$ as $x\to0$, one application produces $e^x/(2x)$. Why can one not immediately apply L'Hôpital's rule a second time?

- A second application is always forbidden.
- The denominator is negative on both sides.
- The exponential has no second derivative.
- The numerator now tends to $1$ and the denominator to $0$.

## Explanation

The second step lacks an indeterminate form. The new ratio diverges to $+\infty$ from the right and to $-\infty$ from the left, so there is no common two-sided limit.
