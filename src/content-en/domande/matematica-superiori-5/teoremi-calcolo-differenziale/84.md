As $x\to0^+$, consider $\dfrac{x-\ln(1+x)}{x^2}$. After one application of L'Hôpital's rule, which simplification avoids differentiating again?

- $\dfrac{1-1/(1+x)}{2x}=\dfrac1{2(1+x)}$
- $\dfrac{1-1/(1+x)}{2x}=\dfrac1{2x}$
- $\dfrac{1-1/(1+x)}{2x}=\dfrac1{2+x} +1$
- $\dfrac{1-1/(1+x)}{2x}=0$ for every $x>0$.

## Explanation

The differentiated numerator is $x/(1+x)$. Because $x>0$, cancel the factor $x$ and read off the limit $1/2$.
