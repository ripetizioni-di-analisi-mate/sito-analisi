In proving Cauchy's theorem, write $F=f(b)-f(a)$ and $G=g(b)-g(a)$ for the increments. Which function $H$ necessarily satisfies $H(b)=H(a)$?

- $H(x)=Gf(x)-Fg(x)$
- $H(x)=Ff(x)+Gg(x)$
- $H(x)=f(x)g(x)$
- $H(x)=f(x)+g(x)$

## Explanation

The increment of $Gf-Fg$ is $GF-FG=0$. Applying Rolle's theorem to this combination gives the relation between the two derivatives.
