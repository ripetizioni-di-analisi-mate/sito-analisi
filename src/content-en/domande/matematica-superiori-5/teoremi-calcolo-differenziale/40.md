A vehicle's coordinate along a straight road increases from $10$ km to $100$ km over two hours. It is continuous on the interval and differentiable in its interior. What does the mean value theorem guarantee about its signed velocity?

- It equals $45$ km/h for the whole two hours.
- It equals $90$ km/h exactly halfway along the road.
- It vanishes at least once in the interior.
- It equals $45$ km/h at least once in the interior.

## Explanation

Signed displacement is $100-10=90$ km, so the secant slope on the position-time graph is $90/2=45$ km/h. The mean value theorem guarantees a tangent with that slope.
