Cauchy's mean value theorem assumes $g'(x)\ne0$ throughout the interior. Why must $g(b)-g(a)$ then be nonzero?

- Because $g(a)$ must be zero.
- Because $g$ must be positive.
- Because $g'$ must be constant.
- Equal endpoint values would force a zero of $g'$ by Rolle's theorem.

## Explanation

Under the theorem's continuity and differentiability assumptions, equal endpoint values would make Rolle's theorem applicable to $g$. Its conclusion would contradict the hypothesis on $g'$.
