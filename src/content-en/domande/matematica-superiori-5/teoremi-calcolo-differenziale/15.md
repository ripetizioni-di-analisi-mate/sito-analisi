A function $f$ is differentiable on an interval and $f'$ is strictly increasing. For $a<b<c$ in that interval, how do the slopes of the two consecutive chords compare?

- $\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}>\dfrac{f(c)-f(b)}{c-b}$
- The slopes are equal whenever $b-a=c-b$.
- Their order depends on the sign of $f(b)$.
- $\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}<\dfrac{f(c)-f(b)}{c-b}$

## Explanation

The mean value theorem gives slopes $f'(u)$ with $a<u<b$ and $f'(v)$ with $b<v<c$. Since $u<v$ and the derivative is strictly increasing, $f'(u)<f'(v)$ regardless of the lengths of the intervals.
