Two functions satisfying the hypotheses of the ratio-free form of Cauchy's theorem obey $f(a)=f(b)$ and $g(a)=g(b)$. What does the identity $\Delta f\,g'(c)=\Delta g\,f'(c)$ say in this case?

- Every tangent is horizontal.
- Both derivatives must vanish at the same point.
- The ratio of the increments is $1$.
- Both sides are zero for every interior $c$.

## Explanation

Both increments vanish, reducing the identity to $0=0$. Rolle's theorem gives a zero for each derivative, but the identity alone does not locate a common zero.
