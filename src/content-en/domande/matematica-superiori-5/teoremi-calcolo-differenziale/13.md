To apply l'Hôpital's rule to $\lim_{x\to0}\left(\dfrac1x-\dfrac1{\ln(1+x)}\right)$, which quotient should be formed first?

- $\dfrac{1}{x-\ln(1+x)}$
- $\dfrac{x-\ln(1+x)}{x\ln(1+x)}$
- $\dfrac{\ln(1+x)-x}{x\ln(1+x)}$
- $\dfrac{\ln(1+x)-x}{x+\ln(1+x)}$

## Explanation

The common denominator is the product of the two denominators. The resulting quotient has the form zero over zero. After two differentiations the numerator tends to $-1$ and the denominator to $2$, giving a limit of $-1/2$.
