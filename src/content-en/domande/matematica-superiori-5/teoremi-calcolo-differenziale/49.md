For $f(x)=1/x$, the derivative is negative everywhere it is defined. Why is $f(-1)<f(1)$ compatible with the monotonicity test?

- The derivative at $-1$ is positive.
- The interval from $-1$ to $1$ contains a point outside the domain.
- The derivative at $1$ is positive.
- The test always requires a second derivative.

## Explanation

The negative derivative guarantees decrease on each interval $(-\infty,0)$ and $(0,+\infty)$. The mean value theorem cannot be applied to two inputs on opposite sides of the gap.
