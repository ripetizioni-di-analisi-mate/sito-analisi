The functions $f$ and $g$ are differentiable on an interval $I$ containing $1$, with $g'(x) \neq 0$ and $\dfrac{f'(x)}{g'(x)} = 2$ for every $x$ in $I$. Moreover $f(1) = 3$ and $g(1) = 4$. Which relation do the points $(g(x), f(x))$ satisfy for every $x$ in $I$?

- $f(x)=2g(x)-5$
- $f(x)=2g(x)$
- $f(x)=g(x)+2$
- $g(x)=2f(x)-5$

## Explanation

The equality $\dfrac{f'(x)}{g'(x)} = 2$ gives $f'(x) = 2g'(x)$ for every $x$ in $I$. Therefore the function $f - 2g$ has zero derivative on $I$, and by the theorem on **functions with zero derivative** it is constant on $I$. At $x = 1$ it equals $3 - 2 \cdot 4 = -5$, so $f(x) - 2g(x) = -5$ for every $x$ in $I$. The points $(g(x), f(x))$ therefore satisfy the relation $f(x) = 2g(x) - 5$. The relation $f(x) = 2g(x)$ leaves out the constant, which is determined by the values at $1$.
