Near $a$, the graph of $f$ stays on or above that of $g$ and $f(a)=g(a)$. Both functions are differentiable and $a$ is interior. What follows from Fermat's theorem?

- $f'(a)=g'(a)=0$ necessarily.
- $f'(a)=g'(a)$
- $f'(a)>g'(a)$
- $f$ and $g$ coincide on an interval.

## Explanation

The difference $f-g$ is nonnegative near $a$ and equals zero there. It therefore has an interior minimum. Its derivative $f'(a)-g'(a)$ vanishes, although the two slopes can be nonzero.
