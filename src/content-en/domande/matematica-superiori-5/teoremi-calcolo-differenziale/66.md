As $x\to+\infty$, let $g(x)=x+\sin x$. What prevents the usual hypothesis $g'\ne0$ from holding on an entire final half-line?

- $g'$ vanishes at every odd multiple of $\pi$.
- $g$ tends to zero at infinity.
- Sine is not differentiable at multiples of $\pi$.
- $g'$ is always $2$.

## Explanation

Here $g'(x)=1+\cos x$. At $x=(2k+1)\pi$, cosine equals $-1$, giving derivative zeros arbitrarily far to the right.
