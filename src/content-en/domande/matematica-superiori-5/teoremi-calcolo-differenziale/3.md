The function $f(x)=\sqrt[3]{x^2}$ equals $1$ at both ends of $[-1,1]$. What happens to Rolle's conclusion in this example?

- The derivative is zero at the endpoints.
- The derivative at $0$ is zero because there is a minimum there.
- No interior derivative is zero, and the derivative at $0$ does not exist.
- The derivative is zero throughout the interval.

## Explanation

For $x\ne0$ the derivative is $2/(3\sqrt[3]{x})$ and never vanishes. At zero its one-sided limits are infinite with opposite signs, so interior differentiability fails.
