Set $f(0)=0$ and $f(x)=x^2\sin(1/x)$ elsewhere. The endpoint values on $[-1/\pi,1/\pi]$ are zero. Although $f'$ is discontinuous at zero, which conclusion is correct?

- Rolle's theorem requires $f'$ to be continuous at zero.
- The function has a jump at zero.
- Equal endpoint values imply $f'=0$ everywhere.
- Rolle's theorem applies because $f$ is differentiable at zero as well.

## Explanation

The difference quotient at zero is $h\sin(1/h)$ and tends to zero. Thus the function is continuous on the closed interval and differentiable inside. Rolle's theorem guarantees at least one point with zero derivative.
