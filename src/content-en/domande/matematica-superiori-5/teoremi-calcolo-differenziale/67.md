A function $f$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, differentiable inside, with $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$ and $f'(x)\le1$. What follows for each $x$ in the interval?

- $f'(x)<1$ at some interior point.
- $f(x)=x^2$
- $f(x)<x$ for every $0<x<1$.
- $f(x)=x$

## Explanation

Set $h(x)=x-f(x)$. Since $h'(x)\ge0$, the mean value theorem ensures that $h$ is nondecreasing. Both endpoints have value zero, so it is zero throughout the interval.
