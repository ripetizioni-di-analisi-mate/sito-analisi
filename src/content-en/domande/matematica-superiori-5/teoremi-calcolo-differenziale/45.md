The function $f(x)=x^2$ attains its maximum on $[0,3]$ at $x=3$. Why can the derivative at $3$ be nonzero?

- The point is not in the interior of the interval.
- The first derivative does not exist at that point.
- The maximum value is positive.
- The function is discontinuous at that point.

## Explanation

Fermat's theorem requires the extremum to occur at an interior point of the domain under consideration. In this problem the maximum occurs at the right endpoint of the interval.
