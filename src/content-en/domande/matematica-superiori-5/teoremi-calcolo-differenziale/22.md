Consider $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x + \sin x}{x}$. Which statement is true?

- The limit does not exist, because the quotient of the derivatives has no limit
- The limit equals $1$, even though the quotient of the derivatives has no limit
- The limit equals $2$
- The limit equals $0$

## Explanation

The quotient of the derivatives is $\dfrac{1 + \cos x}{1}$, which oscillates between $0$ and $2$ and has no limit. L'Hôpital's rule requires the limit of the quotient of the derivatives to exist, so here it does not apply and says nothing about the limit. The limit is computed directly. One has $\dfrac{x + \sin x}{x} = 1 + \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$, and $\dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 0$ because $|\sin x| \leq 1$. Therefore the limit equals $1$.
