Suppose $f$ is differentiable in a neighbourhood of $a$ and $f'(a)>0$. Which additional fact suffices to conclude that $f$ is strictly increasing on an entire open interval containing $a$?

- The derivative $f'$ is continuous at $a$.
- The function $f$ is continuous at $a$.
- The value $f(a)$ is positive.
- The point $a$ is positive.

## Explanation

Continuity of $f'$ gives an interval where $f'(x)>f'(a)/2>0$. For every pair of points in that interval, the mean value theorem yields a positive secant slope. Continuity of $f$ already follows from differentiability and does not control the signs of nearby derivatives.
