A function is differentiable at an interior point $x=5$ of its domain and has a local maximum there. What does Fermat's theorem imply?

- $f^\prime(5)>0$
- $f^\prime(5)<0$
- The function is constant on its domain
- $f^\prime(5)=0$

## Explanation

Fermat's theorem concerns a local extremum at an interior point where the derivative exists. Under these hypotheses the derivative is zero at that point. This condition is necessary and by itself does not guarantee an extremum.
