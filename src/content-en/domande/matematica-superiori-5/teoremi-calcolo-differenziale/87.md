As $x\to0$, suppose $f(x)/x\to1$ and $g(x)/x\to1$. What do these facts determine about the limit of $(f(x)-g(x))/x^2$?

- The limit is always one.
- The limit is always zero.
- They do not determine it. It may be any real number.
- The limit is always infinite.

## Explanation

Take $f(x)=x+kx^2$ and $g(x)=x$. Both hypotheses hold for every $k$, while the quotient of the difference is identically $k$. The first-order information therefore cannot fix the result.
