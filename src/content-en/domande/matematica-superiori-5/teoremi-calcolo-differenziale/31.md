A function $f$ has an interior minimum at $a$ and $f'(a)$ exists. Let $g(x)=e^{f(x)}$. What follows at $a$?

- $g'(a)$ always equals $1$.
- $g$ has a maximum and $g'(a)=0$.
- $g$ has a minimum and $g'(a)=0$.
- $g(a)$ is always zero.

## Explanation

The exponential is strictly increasing and preserves the ordering of nearby values. Fermat's theorem gives $f'(a)=0$, hence $g'(a)=e^{f(a)}f'(a)=0$.
