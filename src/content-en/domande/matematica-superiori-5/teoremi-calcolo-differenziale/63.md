As $x\to0$, a student writes $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}=\dfrac{\cos x}{1}$ by L'Hôpital's rule. What is the precise correction?

- Only the second derivatives are equal.
- The ratios have equal limits; their pointwise values need not agree.
- The original ratio has limit zero.
- One must substitute $x=0$ into both ratios.

## Explanation

The hypotheses allow the ratio of derivatives inside a limit. At $x=\pi/2$, for example, the two ratios are $2/\pi$ and $0$, although both tend to $1$ at zero.
