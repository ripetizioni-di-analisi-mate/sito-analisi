Is there a finite number $M$ such that $|\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}|\le M|x-y|$ for all $x,y\in[0,1]$? Which test settles the question?

- Since the range is bounded, $M=2$ works.
- Continuity forces $M=1$.
- Taking $y=0$ and $x>0$ makes the ratio $1/\sqrt{x}$, which is unbounded.
- The square root is increasing, so $M=1/2$ always works.

## Explanation

With one endpoint at zero the ratio of vertical to horizontal change is $\sqrt{x}/x$. Near zero this exceeds every fixed slope, ruling out a uniform bound of that kind.
