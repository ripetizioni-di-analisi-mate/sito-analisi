To evaluate $\lim_{x\to0^+} \dfrac{\ln(1+x)}{\sqrt{x}}$, a student claims the value is $1$ because the numerator is equivalent to $x$. What further comparison is needed?

- $\sqrt{x}$ is equivalent to $x$.
- The ratio $x/\sqrt{x}=\sqrt{x}$ tends to zero.
- The denominator tends to infinity.
- The logarithm tends to $1$.

## Explanation

Factor the ratio as $[\ln(1+x)/x]\sqrt{x}$. The first factor tends to $1$ and the second to zero, so the product tends to zero.
