To study $(-1+x)^{1/x}$ as $x\to0^+$, a student proposes taking the logarithm of its base. What obstruction can already be seen along $x=1/n$ for positive integers $n$?

- For $n>1$ the base is negative and the powers alternate in sign.
- The base always equals $1$.
- All the powers are positive.
- The magnitudes tend to zero.

## Explanation

The expression is $(-1+1/n)^n=(-1)^n(1-1/n)^n$. Its magnitude tends to $e^{-1}$ but its sign alternates, so the two real subsequences have different limits. The real logarithm of the base is undefined.
