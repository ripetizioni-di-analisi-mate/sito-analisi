A twice-differentiable function on $[-1,1]$ passes through $(-1,1)$, $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$. Applying Rolle's theorem twice to $h(x)=f(x)-x^2$ gives which information?

- $f''(c)=2$ for some $c\in(-1,1)$.
- $f''(0)=2$ necessarily.
- $f(x)=x^2$ throughout the interval.
- $f''(x)=0$ for all $x\in(-1,1)$.

## Explanation

The auxiliary function has three zeros. Rolle's theorem gives two distinct zeros of $h'$, one in each adjacent interval. Applying it again gives a zero of $h''=f''-2$ between the first two.
