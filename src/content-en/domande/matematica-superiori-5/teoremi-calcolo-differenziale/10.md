To prove $\ln x\le x-1$ for $x>0$, set $g(x)=x-1-\ln x$. Which fact completes the proof?

- Its absolute maximum is zero at $x=1$.
- Its absolute minimum is zero at $x=1$.
- Its derivative is always positive.
- The function tends to zero as $x\to0^+$.

## Explanation

The derivative $1-1/x$ is negative before one and positive afterwards. The minimum is $g(1)=0$, which also gives the only equality case.
