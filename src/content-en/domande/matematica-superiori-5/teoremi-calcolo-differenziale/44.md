A function $f$ satisfies $|f'(x)|<1$ on an interval. Why can it not have two distinct fixed points there?

- The secant between two fixed points would have slope $1$.
- A fixed point must have a horizontal tangent.
- All values of $f$ must be negative.
- The derivative would have to change sign at every fixed point.

## Explanation

With $f(a)=a$ and $f(b)=b$, the secant slope would be $(b-a)/(b-a)=1$. The mean value theorem would force $f'(c)=1$ at an interior point, contradicting the bound.
