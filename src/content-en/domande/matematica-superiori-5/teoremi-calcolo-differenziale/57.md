Over part of a journey, cumulative distance $s$ increases by $90$ km and fuel consumed $u$ increases by $6$ litres. Both functions are differentiable and $u'(t)>0$. What does Cauchy's mean value theorem guarantee?

- Fuel is consumed at a constant rate.
- Speed is always $15\,\mathrm{km/h}$.
- At some instant $s'(t)/u'(t)=15\,\mathrm{km/L}$.
- Maximum speed is $90\,\mathrm{km/h}$.

## Explanation

At some point Cauchy's theorem equates the derivative ratio to the ratio of total changes. Its units are kilometres per litre because time cancels.
