In the limit $\lim_{x\to0}(x-\sin x)/x^3$, which quotient remains after two applications of l'Hôpital's rule?

- $\dfrac{\cos x}{3x}$
- $\dfrac{1-\cos x}{3x^2}$
- $\dfrac{-\sin x}{6x}$
- $\dfrac{\sin x}{6x}$

## Explanation

The first step gives $(1-\cos x)/(3x^2)$, still of the form zero over zero. The second yields the stated quotient, which approaches $1/6$ by the standard sine limit.
