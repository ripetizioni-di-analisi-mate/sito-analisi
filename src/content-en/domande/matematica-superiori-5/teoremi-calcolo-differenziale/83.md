L'Hôpital's rule applies to $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{1-\cos x}{x^2}$ although the denominator's derivative vanishes at zero. Which observation justifies that step?

- The point must belong to the domain of the ratio.
- Cosine vanishes at zero.
- The original ratio equals $1/2$ everywhere.
- $2x$ is nonzero in the punctured neighbourhood.

## Explanation

The condition is imposed near zero, excluding zero itself. One differentiation reduces the limit to that of $\sin x/(2x)$, which is $1/2$.
