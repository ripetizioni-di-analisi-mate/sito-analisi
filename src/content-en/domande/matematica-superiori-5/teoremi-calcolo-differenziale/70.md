A function is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable inside. Its right derivative at $a$ is negative and its left derivative at $b$ is positive. What is guaranteed even without continuity of the derivative?

- An increasing function on the whole interval.
- An interior absolute maximum with derivative zero.
- A unique interior critical point.
- An interior absolute minimum with derivative zero.

## Explanation

The extreme value theorem ensures an absolute minimum. The signs of the one-sided derivatives provide lower values just inside both endpoints. The minimum is therefore interior, and Fermat's theorem gives zero derivative there.
