For $x>1$, the base of $(1+\sin x/x)^x$ tends to $1$. Which comparison shows that the power has no limit as $x\to+\infty$?

- The exponent stays between $0$ and $1$.
- The base is always negative.
- Along $2k\pi$ and $2k\pi+\pi/2$ the limits are $1$ and $e$.
- The logarithm of the base is always zero.

## Explanation

On the first sequence sine is zero, so the power equals one. On the second it is one, giving the limit $(1+1/x)^x\to e$. The two distinct limits rule out an overall limit.
