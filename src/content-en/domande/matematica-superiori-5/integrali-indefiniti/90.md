On every interval where $\cos x \neq 0$, which expression gives the indefinite integral of $\tan^2 x$?

- $\tan x + x + c$
- $\dfrac{\tan^3 x}{3} + c$
- $-\tan x - x + c$
- $\tan x - x + c$

## Explanation

Since $\sin^2 x = 1 - \cos^2 x$, one has
$$
\tan^2 x = \frac{\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{1 - \cos^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{1}{\cos^2 x} - 1 .
$$
By **linearity** and the table of elementary integrals
$$
\int \tan^2 x\, dx = \int \frac{1}{\cos^2 x}\, dx - \int 1\, dx = \tan x - x + c .
$$
The function $\dfrac{\tan^3 x}{3}$ would come from the formula for $f'(x)\, [f(x)]^{\alpha}$, but the factor $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$ is absent from the integrand.
