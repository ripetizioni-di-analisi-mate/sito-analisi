For $x > 0$, what does $\displaystyle\int x^2 \ln x\,dx$ equal?

- $\dfrac{x^3}{3}\ln x+\dfrac{x^3}{9}+c$
- $\dfrac{x^3}{3}\ln x-\dfrac{x^2}{6}+c$
- $\dfrac{x^3}{3}\ln x-\dfrac{x^3}{9}+c$
- $x^3\ln x-\dfrac{x^3}{3}+c$

## Explanation

Integration by parts is applied with finite factor $f(x) = \ln x$ and differential factor $g'(x) = x^2$, so $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{x^3}{3}$. The formula gives
$$
\int x^2 \ln x\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \int \frac{x^3}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \int \frac{x^2}{3}\,dx .
$$
Since $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{x^2}{3}\,dx = \dfrac{x^3}{9} + c$, the result is $\dfrac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \dfrac{x^3}{9} + c$.
