For $x \neq -2$, what is the indefinite integral of $\dfrac{x^2 + x + 1}{x + 2}$?

- $x-1+3\ln|x+2|+c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2}+x+3\ln|x+2|+c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2}-x+\ln|x+2|+c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2}-x+3\ln|x+2|+c$

## Explanation

The numerator has higher degree than the denominator, so we carry out the division, which gives $x^2 + x + 1 = (x + 2)(x - 1) + 3$. Therefore, for $x \neq -2$,
$$
\frac{x^2 + x + 1}{x + 2} = x - 1 + \frac{3}{x + 2} .
$$
An antiderivative of $x - 1$ is $\dfrac{x^2}{2} - x$ and an antiderivative of $\dfrac{3}{x + 2}$ is $3\ln\lvert x + 2 \rvert$, so the result is $\dfrac{x^2}{2} - x + 3\ln\lvert x + 2 \rvert + c$.
