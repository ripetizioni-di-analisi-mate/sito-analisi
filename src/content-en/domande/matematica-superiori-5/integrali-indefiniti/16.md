Let $f$ be continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, and let $F$ and $G$ be two antiderivatives of $f$ on $\mathbb{R}$ with $F(0) = 2$ and $G(0) = -1$. What is $F(5) - G(5)$?

- $1$
- $3$
- It cannot be determined without knowing $f$
- $-3$

## Explanation

By the theorem on **antiderivatives of the same function**, two antiderivatives of $f$ on the same interval differ by a constant, so $F(x) - G(x) = c$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. With $x = 0$ we get $c = F(0) - G(0) = 2 - (-1) = 3$. Since the difference is the same at every point, $F(5) - G(5) = 3$, whatever $f$ is.
