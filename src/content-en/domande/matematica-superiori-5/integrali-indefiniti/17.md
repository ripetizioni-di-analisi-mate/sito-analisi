On an interval where $\cos x \neq 0$, what is $\displaystyle\int \tan x\,dx$?

- $\ln \lvert \sin x \rvert + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x} + c$
- $\ln \lvert \cos x \rvert + c$
- $-\ln \lvert \cos x \rvert + c$

## Explanation

We write $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$. The derivative of the denominator is $(\cos x)' = -\sin x$, so the numerator is the opposite of the derivative of the denominator:
$$
\int \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\,dx = -\int \frac{-\sin x}{\cos x}\,dx .
$$
The integral on the right has the form $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx$ with $g(x) = \cos x$, therefore it reduces to the standard integral $\ln \lvert g(x) \rvert$. We obtain $\displaystyle\int \tan x\,dx = -\ln \lvert \cos x \rvert + c$. The function $\dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$ is the derivative of $\tan x$.
