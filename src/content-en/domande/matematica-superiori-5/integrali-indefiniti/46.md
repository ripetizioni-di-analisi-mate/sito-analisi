Write $I=\int e^x\sin x\,dx$ and $J=\int e^x\cos x\,dx$. Integration by parts gives, up to constants, $I=e^x\sin x-J$ and $J=e^x\cos x+I$. What allows the calculation to terminate?

- Both integrals are constant.
- The two integrals $I$ and $J$ are equal.
- Substituting for $J$ gives a linear equation with $2I$ on the left.
- After two steps the term containing $I$ vanishes by periodicity.

## Explanation

After substitution we obtain $I=e^x\sin x-e^x\cos x-I$. Moving the repeated integral to the left and dividing by two gives an antiderivative, to which an arbitrary constant is added.
