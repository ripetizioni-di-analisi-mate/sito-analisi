For $x\ne0$, seek an antiderivative of $\dfrac{xe^x-e^x+1}{x^2}$ in the form $F_a(x)=\dfrac{e^x+a}{x}$. Which value of $a$ makes this work?

- $a=-1$
- $a=0$
- $a=1$
- $a=2$

## Explanation

The quotient rule gives $F_a'(x)=(xe^x-e^x-a)/x^2$. The exponential terms already match. To obtain the constant $+1$ in the numerator, we need $-a=1$.
