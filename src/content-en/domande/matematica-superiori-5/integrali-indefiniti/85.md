To integrate $\dfrac{x^2 + 3x}{x + 1}$ we write $\dfrac{x^2 + 3x}{x + 1} = Q(x) + \dfrac{r}{x + 1}$, with $Q$ a polynomial and $r$ a constant. What is $r$?

- $-2$
- $0$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

Multiplying by $x + 1$ gives $x^2 + 3x = Q(x)\,(x + 1) + r$ for every $x$. With $x = -1$ the first term on the right vanishes, so $r$ is the value of the numerator at $-1$:
$$
r = (-1)^2 + 3 \cdot (-1) = 1 - 3 = -2 .
$$
Indeed, the division gives $x^2 + 3x = (x + 2)(x + 1) - 2$, that is, $Q(x) = x + 2$. The value $4$ is that of the numerator at $x = 1$, which is not the root of the denominator.
