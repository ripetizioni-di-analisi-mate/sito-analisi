A student writes $F_c(x)=\dfrac{(x+c)^2}{2}$ as the family of antiderivatives of $x$ on the real line. For which values of $c$ does the proposed expression actually give an antiderivative of $x$?

- For no value of $c$.
- For every real $c$.
- Only $c=1$.
- Only $c=0$.

## Explanation

The derivative is $F_c'(x)=x+c$. It equals the integrand at every input only if $c=0$. Adding the arbitrary constant to the completed antiderivative gives $x^2/2+c$.
