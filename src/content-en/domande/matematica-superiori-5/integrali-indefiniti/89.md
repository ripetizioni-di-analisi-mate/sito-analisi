For $x > 0$, which family of functions is the indefinite integral of $\dfrac{e^{\sqrt{x}}}{\sqrt{x}}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2} e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $2 \sqrt{x}\, e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $2 e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$

## Explanation

The derivative of $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ is $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$, so the integrand equals $2 f'(x)\, e^{f(x)}$. Therefore the integral is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int f'(x)\, e^{f(x)}\, dx = e^{f(x)} + c$, and
$$
\int \frac{e^{\sqrt{x}}}{\sqrt{x}}\, dx = 2 \int \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}\, e^{\sqrt{x}}\, dx = 2 e^{\sqrt{x}} + c .
$$
The family $\dfrac{1}{2} e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$ comes from writing the factor $2$ in the denominator instead of in front of the integral.
