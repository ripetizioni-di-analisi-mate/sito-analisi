A step function equals $-1$ for $x<0$ and $1$ for $x\ge0$. Why does no choice of integration constants give an antiderivative on the whole line?

- The two pieces cannot have the same value at zero.
- A derivative can take only positive values.
- The one-sided slopes at zero remain different.
- An antiderivative must be positive.

## Explanation

The two pieces of a possible antiderivative are lines of slopes $-1$ and $1$. Constants can make their values agree, but the unequal slopes rule out differentiability at zero.
