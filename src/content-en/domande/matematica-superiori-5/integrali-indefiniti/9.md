What is the result of the integral $\displaystyle\int x\,e^{-x}\,dx$?

- $-\dfrac{x^2}{2}e^{-x}+c$
- $-(x+1)e^{-x}+c$
- $-(x-1)e^{-x}+c$
- $(x+1)e^{-x}+c$

## Explanation

Integration by parts is applied with finite factor $f(x) = x$ and differential factor $g'(x) = e^{-x}$, so $f'(x) = 1$ and $g(x) = -e^{-x}$. The formula gives
$$
\int x\,e^{-x}\,dx = -x\,e^{-x} + \int e^{-x}\,dx .
$$
Since an antiderivative of $e^{-x}$ is $-e^{-x}$, the result is $-x\,e^{-x} - e^{-x} + c = -(x + 1)\,e^{-x} + c$.
