Using the substitution $t = \sqrt{2x + 1}$, what is $\displaystyle\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{2x + 1}}\,dx$ for $x > -\dfrac{1}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{x-1}{3}\sqrt{2x+1}+c$
- $x\sqrt{2x+1}+c$
- $\dfrac{x+1}{3}\sqrt{2x+1}+c$
- $\dfrac{(2x+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3}+c$

## Explanation

From $t = \sqrt{2x + 1}$ we get $x = \dfrac{t^2 - 1}{2}$, so $dx = t\,dt$. Substituting gives
$$
\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{2x + 1}}\,dx = \int \frac{t^2 - 1}{2t}\,t\,dt = \frac{1}{2} \int \left( t^2 - 1 \right) dt = \frac{t^3}{6} - \frac{t}{2} + c .
$$
Factoring out $\dfrac{t}{6}$ gives $\dfrac{t}{6}\left( t^2 - 3 \right)$, and since $t^2 = 2x + 1$ we have $t^2 - 3 = 2(x - 1)$. Therefore the result is $\dfrac{x - 1}{3}\sqrt{2x + 1} + c$.
