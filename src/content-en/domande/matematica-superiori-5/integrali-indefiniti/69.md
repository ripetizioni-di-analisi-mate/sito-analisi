Writing $x^3 = x^2 \cdot x$, the integral $\displaystyle\int x^3 e^{x^2}\, dx$ is reduced by a substitution to an integral computed by parts. What is the result?

- $\dfrac{x^2 e^{x^2}}{2} + c$
- $\left( x^2 - 1 \right) e^{x^2} + c$
- $\dfrac{\left( x^2 + 1 \right) e^{x^2}}{2} + c$
- $\dfrac{\left( x^2 - 1 \right) e^{x^2}}{2} + c$

## Explanation

With the **substitution** $t = x^2$ one has $dt = 2x\, dx$, so $x^3\, dx = x^2 \cdot x\, dx = \dfrac{t}{2}\, dt$. The integral therefore becomes $\dfrac{1}{2} \displaystyle\int t\, e^{t}\, dt$.

This is computed by **integration by parts**, with $f(t) = t$ and $g'(t) = e^{t}$:
$$
\int t\, e^{t}\, dt = t\, e^{t} - \int e^{t}\, dt = (t - 1)\, e^{t} + c .
$$
Returning to the variable $x$ with $t = x^2$ one obtains $\dfrac{\left( x^2 - 1 \right) e^{x^2}}{2} + c$.
