What is the indefinite integral $\displaystyle\int \left( 4\cos x - 3\sin x + \frac{1}{1 + x^2} \right) dx$?

- $4\cos x+3\sin x+\arctan x+c$
- $4\sin x-3\cos x+\arctan x+c$
- $4\sin x+3\cos x+\arctan x+c$
- $4\sin x+3\cos x+\ln(1+x^2)+c$

## Explanation

By linearity, the antiderivatives of the three terms are added. An antiderivative of $\cos x$ is $\sin x$, so the first term gives $4\sin x$. Since $(\cos x)' = -\sin x$, an antiderivative of $-\sin x$ is $\cos x$, therefore the second term gives $3\cos x$. The third term is the derivative of $\arctan x$, so the result is $4\sin x + 3\cos x + \arctan x + c$.
