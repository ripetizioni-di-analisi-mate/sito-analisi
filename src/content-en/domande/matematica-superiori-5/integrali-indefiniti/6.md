Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{\sin x}{\cos^3 x}\,dx$ for $\cos x \neq 0$.

- $-\dfrac{1}{2\cos^2x}+c$
- $\dfrac{1}{3\cos^3x}+c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2\cos^2x}+c$
- $\ln|\cos x|+c$

## Explanation

Set $u=\cos x$, so that $du=-\sin x\,dx$. Integrating $-u^{-3}$ gives $\dfrac12u^{-2}+c$ on each interval where the cosine is nonzero.
