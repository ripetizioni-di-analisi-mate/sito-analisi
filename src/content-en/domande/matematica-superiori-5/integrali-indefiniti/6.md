For $\cos x \neq 0$, what is the result of $\displaystyle\int \frac{\sin x}{\cos^3 x}\,dx$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{2\cos^2x}+c$
- $\dfrac{1}{3\cos^3x}+c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2\cos^2x}+c$
- $\ln|\cos x|+c$

## Explanation

With the substitution $u = \cos x$ we have $du = -\sin x\,dx$, so $\sin x\,dx = -du$. The integral becomes
$$
-\int u^{-3}\,du = -\frac{u^{-2}}{-2} + c = \frac{1}{2u^2} + c .
$$
Returning to the variable $x$, the result is $\dfrac{1}{2\cos^2 x} + c$ on every interval where $\cos x \neq 0$.
