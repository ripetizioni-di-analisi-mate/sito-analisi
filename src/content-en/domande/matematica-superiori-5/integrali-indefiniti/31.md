For $x>1$, which substitution removes both the radical and the factor $x$ in $\int\dfrac{dx}{x\sqrt{\ln x}}$, leaving a constant integrand?

- $u=x^2$
- $u=\ln(\ln x)$
- $u=\sqrt{x}$
- $u=\sqrt{\ln x}$

## Explanation

With $u=\sqrt{\ln x}$, we have $du=dx/(2x\sqrt{\ln x})$. The integral is therefore $2\int du=2u+c$.
