For $x > 0$, what is $\displaystyle\int \left( \frac{3}{x} - 4\sqrt[3]{x} \right) dx$?

- $-\dfrac{3}{x^2}-3x^{\frac{4}{3}}+c$
- $3\ln x-3x^{\frac{4}{3}}+c$
- $3\ln x-3x^{\frac{1}{3}}+c$
- $3\ln x-4x^{\frac{4}{3}}+c$

## Explanation

By linearity, the two terms are integrated separately. Since $x > 0$, an antiderivative of $\dfrac{3}{x}$ is $3\ln x$. The cube root is $\sqrt[3]{x} = x^{\frac{1}{3}}$, so an antiderivative of it is $\dfrac{3}{4}x^{\frac{4}{3}}$. The second term therefore gives $-4 \cdot \dfrac{3}{4}x^{\frac{4}{3}} = -3x^{\frac{4}{3}}$, and the result is $3\ln x - 3x^{\frac{4}{3}} + c$.
