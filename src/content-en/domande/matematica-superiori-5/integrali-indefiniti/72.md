For $x>1$ and $p\ne-1$, how must $p$ and $q$ be related for the derivative of $(\ln x)^q$ to be a nonzero multiple of $\dfrac{(\ln x)^p}{x}$?

- $q=-p-1$ for every $p$
- $q=p-1$
- $q=p$
- $q=p+1$

## Explanation

The derivative is $q(\ln x)^{q-1}/x$. Matching powers gives $q-1=p$, and the restriction on $p$ ensures $q\ne0$.
