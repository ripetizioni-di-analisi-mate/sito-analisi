Which expression gives the indefinite integral of $\sin 5x$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{5} \cos 5x + c$
- $-\cos 5x + c$
- $-5 \cos 5x + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{5} \cos 5x + c$

## Explanation

The derivative of the inner function $5x$ is $5$, so one multiplies and divides by $5$:
$$
\int \sin 5x\, dx = \frac{1}{5} \int 5 \sin 5x\, dx = -\frac{1}{5} \cos 5x + c .
$$
The integral $\displaystyle\int 5 \sin 5x\, dx$ is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \sin x\, dx = -\cos x + c$, with $5x$ in place of $x$.
