What are the antiderivatives of the function $\sqrt{e^{x} - 1}$, defined for $x \geq 0$?

- $\dfrac{2}{3} \left( e^x - 1 \right)^{\frac{3}{2}} + c$
- $2 \sqrt{e^x - 1} - 2 \arctan \sqrt{e^x - 1} + c$
- $2 \sqrt{e^x - 1} + 2 \arctan \sqrt{e^x - 1} + c$
- $\sqrt{e^x - 1} - \arctan \sqrt{e^x - 1} + c$

## Explanation

Apply the **inverse substitution** $t = \sqrt{e^x - 1}$, so $e^x = 1 + t^2$ and $x = \ln\left( 1 + t^2 \right)$, hence $dx = \dfrac{2t}{1 + t^2}\, dt$. The integral becomes
$$
\int t \cdot \frac{2t}{1 + t^2}\, dt = 2 \int \frac{t^2}{1 + t^2}\, dt .
$$
Since $\dfrac{t^2}{1 + t^2} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{1 + t^2}$, one obtains $2t - 2 \arctan t + c$. With $t = \sqrt{e^x - 1}$ the result is $2 \sqrt{e^x - 1} - 2 \arctan \sqrt{e^x - 1} + c$.
