What is the fundamental period of the function $f(x) = \sin(2x) + \cos(3x)$?

- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $2\pi$
- $3\pi$

## Explanation

The function $\sin(2x)$ has fundamental period $\pi$ and the function $\cos(3x)$ has fundamental period $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, so $2\pi = 2 \cdot \pi = 3 \cdot \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ is a period of both and therefore also of $f$. If $T > 0$ is a period of $f$, then it is also a period of $f''(x) = -4\sin(2x) - 9\cos(3x)$, hence of the functions $f'' + 4f = -5\cos(3x)$ and $f'' + 9f = 5\sin(2x)$. It follows that $T$ is an integer multiple of both $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\pi$, that is, a multiple of $2\pi$. The fundamental period is therefore $2\pi$. The value $\pi$, for example, is not a period, because $f(x + \pi) = \sin(2x) - \cos(3x)$.
