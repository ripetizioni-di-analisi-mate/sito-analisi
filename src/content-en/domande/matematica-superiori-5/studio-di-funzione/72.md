Among the pairs of positive numbers $x$, $y$ with $\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y} = 1$, what is the smallest possible sum $x + y$?

- $2$
- $1$
- $2\sqrt{2}$
- $4$

## Explanation

With $g(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{y} - 1$ we have $\nabla g = \left(-\dfrac{1}{x^2}, -\dfrac{1}{y^2}\right)$, which is never zero. The Lagrange system gives $1 = -\dfrac{\lambda}{x^2}$ and $1 = -\dfrac{\lambda}{y^2}$, so $x^2 = y^2 = -\lambda$, and with $x, y > 0$ we have $x = y$. The constraint becomes $\dfrac{2}{x} = 1$, therefore $x = y = 2$ and $f(2, 2) = 4$. From the constraint we get $y = \dfrac{x}{x - 1}$ with $x > 1$, and the function $x + \dfrac{x}{x - 1}$ tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to 1^+$ and as $x \to +\infty$. The candidate is unique, so the **minimum** is $4$, at the point $(2, 2)$.
