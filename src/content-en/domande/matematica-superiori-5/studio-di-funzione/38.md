A function $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$ and has the graph shown in the figure, with two absolute minima at $(-1, -1)$ and $(1, -1)$ and a relative maximum at $(0, 0)$. How many solutions does the equation $f(x) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ have?

::grafico{f="x^4-2*x^2" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-1.6 ymax=2 punti="-1,-1,pieno;1,-1,pieno;0,0,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="Graph of $y = f(x)$: minima at $(-1, -1)$ and $(1, -1)$, relative maximum at $(0, 0)$"}

- Four
- Three
- Two
- None

## Explanation

The solutions are the abscissas of the points where the line $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ meets the graph. The value $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ lies between the minimum $-1$ and the relative maximum $0$. On the four intervals of monotonicity $(-\infty, -1]$, $[-1, 0]$, $[0, 1]$ and $[1, +\infty)$ the function is continuous and strictly monotonic, and on each of them it takes the value $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ by the **intermediate value theorem**, exactly once by strict monotonicity. Therefore the equation has **four solutions**.
