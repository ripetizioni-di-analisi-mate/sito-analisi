What is the point of inflection of the graph of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}$?

- The graph has no points of inflection
- $(0, 1)$
- $\left(0, \dfrac{1}{4}\right)$
- $\left(0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = \dfrac{e^{-x}}{(1 + e^{-x})^2}$, which can be written as $f'(x) = f(x)\bigl(1 - f(x)\bigr)$. Differentiating this expression gives
$$
f''(x) = f'(x)\bigl(1 - 2f(x)\bigr) .
$$
Since $f'(x) > 0$ for every $x$, the sign of $f''$ is that of $1 - 2f(x)$, positive where $f(x) < \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, for $x < 0$, and negative for $x > 0$. Hence $f''$ changes sign at $0$ and the graph has a **point of inflection** at $\left(0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, because $f(0) = \dfrac{1}{1 + 1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
