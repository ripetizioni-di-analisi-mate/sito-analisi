A firm's total cost $C(q)$ satisfies $C''(q)>0$ for $q>0$. At some quantity $q_*>0$, the line from the origin to $(q_*,C(q_*))$ is tangent to the graph. What happens to the average cost $A(q)=C(q)/q$ at $q_*$?

- It attains its absolute maximum over $q>0$.
- It must equal zero.
- It attains its absolute minimum over $q>0$.
- It equals the total cost for every $q>0$.

## Explanation

Tangency gives $C'(q_*)=C(q_*)/q_*$. The derivative of $A$ has the sign of $H(q)=qC'(q)-C(q)$, which vanishes at $q_*$. Since $H'(q)=q C''(q)>0$, the derivative of $A$ changes from negative to positive at that point.
