A closed box with a square base must have volume $16\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The material for the base and the lid costs $2$ euros per square meter, the material for the four side walls $1$ euro per square meter. Which base side minimizes the cost?

- $2\sqrt[3]{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\sqrt[3]{4}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $x$ be the base side and $h$ the height, in meters. The volume constraint $x^2 h = 16$ gives $h = \dfrac{16}{x^2}$. Base and lid have total area $2x^2$ and cost $2 \cdot 2x^2$ euros, the walls have area $4xh$ and cost $4xh$ euros, so
$$
C(x) = 4x^2 + 4x \cdot \frac{16}{x^2} = 4x^2 + \frac{64}{x}, \qquad x > 0 .
$$
The derivative $C'(x) = 8x - \dfrac{64}{x^2} = \dfrac{8\,(x^3 - 8)}{x^2}$ is negative for $0 < x < 2$ and positive for $x > 2$. By the theorem on functions with a single stationary point the **minimum cost** is obtained with base side $2\,\mathrm{m}$, height $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and cost $48$ euros. With the two materials at the same price one would obtain the cube, with side $2\sqrt[3]{2}\,\mathrm{m}$.
