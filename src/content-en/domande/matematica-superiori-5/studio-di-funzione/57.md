For the function $f(x) = -x^3 + 6x^2 + 1$, at which point does the tangent line to the graph have the greatest slope?

- $x = 2$
- $x = 0$
- $x = 4$
- $x = -2$

## Explanation

The slope of the tangent at the point with $x$-coordinate $x$ is $f'(x) = -3x^2 + 12x$, so we look for the maximum of $f'$. The derivative of $f'$ is $f''(x) = -6x + 12$, positive for $x < 2$ and negative for $x > 2$. Therefore $f'$ has its maximum at $x = 2$, where the slope equals $f'(2) = -12 + 24 = 12$. The point $x = 2$ is the **inflection point** of $f$, because $f''$ changes sign there. The points $x = 0$ and $x = 4$ are the zeros of $f'$, where the tangent is horizontal.
