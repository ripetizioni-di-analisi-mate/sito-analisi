Where does the function $f(x) = |x| + |x - 2|$, defined on $\mathbb{R}$, attain its absolute minimum?

- Only at $x = 1$
- At every point of the interval $[0, 2]$
- Only at $x = 0$ and at $x = 2$
- Nowhere, because $f$ is not differentiable at $x = 0$ and at $x = 2$

## Explanation

The absolute values are removed by studying the sign of $x$ and of $x - 2$:
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} 2 - 2x & \text{if } x < 0 \\ 2 & \text{if } 0 \leq x \leq 2 \\ 2x - 2 & \text{if } x > 2 \end{cases}
$$
For $x < 0$ we have $2 - 2x > 2$, and for $x > 2$ we have $2x - 2 > 2$, while on $[0, 2]$ the function is constantly equal to $2$. So $f(x) \geq 2$ for every $x$, and equality holds exactly for $0 \leq x \leq 2$. Therefore the **absolute minimum** $2$ is attained at every point of $[0, 2]$, which are infinitely many. The lack of differentiability at $0$ and at $2$ does not prevent the minimum from existing, and the points where $f$ is not differentiable are candidates too.
