The figure shows the graph of the second derivative $f''$ of a function $f$ twice differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$, with $f''(x) = (x + 1)^2(2 - x)$. At which points does $f$ have a point of inflection?

::grafico{f="(x+1)^2*(2-x)" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-6 ymax=6 punti="-1,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" legenda="y = f''(x)" titolo="Graph of the second derivative $f''$, zero at $x = -1$ and at $x = 2$"}

- At $x = -1$ and at $x = 2$
- Only at $x = -1$
- Only at $x = 1$
- Only at $x = 2$

## Explanation

The candidates are the zeros of $f''$, that is, $x = -1$ and $x = 2$, and a candidate is a point of inflection when $f''$ changes sign across it. The factor $(x + 1)^2$ is positive for $x \neq -1$, so near $-1$ the second derivative has the sign of $2 - x$, which is positive on both sides. Therefore $f''$ does not change sign at $-1$, and $x = -1$ **is not a point of inflection**. At $x = 2$ the factor $2 - x$ changes from positive to negative, therefore $f$ changes from convex to concave and $x = 2$ is the only **point of inflection**. The point $x = 1$, where $f''$ has a maximum, concerns the monotonicity of $f''$ and not the concavity of $f$.
