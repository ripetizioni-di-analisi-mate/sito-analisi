A window consists of a rectangle topped by a semicircle whose diameter is the top side of the rectangle. The perimeter of the window is $8\,\mathrm{m}$. Which radius of the semicircle maximizes the area of the window?

- $\dfrac{16}{4 + \pi}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{4}{2 + \pi}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{8}{4 + \pi}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{8}{2 + \pi}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $r$ be the radius and $h$ the height of the rectangle, in meters. The perimeter consists of the base $2r$, the two vertical sides and the semicircle, so $2r + 2h + \pi r = 8$ and $h = \dfrac{8 - 2r - \pi r}{2}$. The area is
$$
A(r) = 2rh + \frac{\pi r^2}{2} = 8r - 2r^2 - \frac{\pi r^2}{2} .
$$
The derivative $A'(r) = 8 - 4r - \pi r$ vanishes for $r = \dfrac{8}{4 + \pi}$, is positive before and negative after, therefore the **maximum area** is obtained with radius $\dfrac{8}{4 + \pi}\,\mathrm{m}$, about $1.12\,\mathrm{m}$. With this radius $h = \dfrac{8}{4 + \pi}$, so the height of the rectangle equals the radius.
