As $x$ tends to $+\infty$, the graph of $f(x) = \ln\!\left(1 + e^{x}\right)$ approaches a line. Which one?

- $y = x$
- $y = 0$
- $y = x + 1$
- $y = x + \ln 2$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ we write $1 + e^{x} = e^{x}\left(1 + e^{-x}\right)$, so
$$
f(x) = x + \ln\!\left(1 + e^{-x}\right) .
$$
It follows that $\dfrac{f(x)}{x} \to 1$, that is, $m = 1$, and $f(x) - x = \ln\!\left(1 + e^{-x}\right) \to \ln 1 = 0$, that is, $q = 0$. Therefore the **oblique asymptote** as $x \to +\infty$ is $y = x$. The line $y = 0$ is instead the horizontal asymptote as $x \to -\infty$, because there $e^{x} \to 0$.
