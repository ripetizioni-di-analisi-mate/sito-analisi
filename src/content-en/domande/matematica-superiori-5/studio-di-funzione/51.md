Three shops are located along a straight road, at kilometres $0$, $2$ and $10$. A warehouse is to be built at kilometre $x$, and the supply cost is proportional to the sum of the squares of the distances from the shops,
$$
S(x) = x^2 + (x - 2)^2 + (x - 10)^2, \qquad x \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
Which value of $x$ minimizes $S$?

- $x = 4$
- $x = 2$
- $x = 5$
- $x = 3$

## Explanation

The derivative is
$$
S'(x) = 2x + 2\,(x - 2) + 2\,(x - 10) = 6\,(x - 4) ,
$$
negative for $x < 4$ and positive for $x > 4$. Therefore, by the theorem on functions with a single stationary point, $x = 4$ is the **absolute minimum point** of $S$, and $S(4) = 16 + 4 + 36 = 56$. The number $4$ is the arithmetic mean of $0$, $2$ and $10$. The value $x = 2$, the position of the middle shop, minimizes the sum of the distances $|x| + |x - 2| + |x - 10|$, while $x = 5$ is the midpoint between $0$ and $10$.
