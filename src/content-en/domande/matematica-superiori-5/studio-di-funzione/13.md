For $f(x)=x^3+3x-5$ the first Newton step from $x_0=2$ gives $x_1=7/5$. How can we check, without knowing the root, that this estimate is still to its right?

- Check only that $7/5<2$.
- Check that $f(7/5)>0$ and use the strict increase of $f$.
- Use $f'(7/5)>0$ without evaluating $f$.
- Observe that $7/5$ is rational.

## Explanation

The derivative $3x^2+3$ is positive everywhere, so the unique root separates negative from positive values. Here $f(7/5)=243/125>0$, placing the estimate to the right of the root.
