The figure shows the graph of a rational function $y = f(x)$, which passes through the origin and has vertical asymptote $x = 1$ and horizontal asymptote $y = 2$. Which can be its expression?

::grafico{f="2*x/(x-1)" xmin=-5 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=8 asintoti="x=1;y=2" punti="0,0,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="Graph of $y = f(x)$ with the asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = 2$ (dashed)"}

- $f(x) = \dfrac{2x}{x + 1}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{2x}{x - 1}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{2x - 2}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{2x^2}{x - 1}$

## Explanation

For $f(x) = \dfrac{2x}{x - 1}$ the denominator vanishes at $x = 1$ while the numerator equals $2$, so $x = 1$ is a vertical asymptote. Numerator and denominator have the same degree, therefore $f(x) \to \dfrac{2}{1} = 2$ as $x \to \pm\infty$ and $y = 2$ is a horizontal asymptote; moreover $f(0) = 0$. The function $\dfrac{2x}{x + 1}$ has its vertical asymptote at $x = -1$, the function $\dfrac{x}{2x - 2}$ has horizontal asymptote $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and the function $\dfrac{2x^2}{x - 1}$ has an oblique asymptote and no horizontal asymptote.
