A point $P=(x,y)$ moves along the circle $x^2+y^2=1$. A student wants to minimise $x+y$ and substitutes $y=\sqrt{1-x^2}$. What minimum does the student find, and what is the minimum on the entire circle?

- $-\sqrt{2}$ in both cases.
- $-1$ on the chosen semicircle and $-\sqrt{2}$ on the whole circle.
- $-1$ in both cases.
- $0$ on the chosen semicircle and $-\sqrt{2}$ on the whole circle.

## Explanation

The positive square root selects the semicircle where $y\geq0$. There $x+y\geq -1$, and $(-1,0)$ attains $-1$. On the whole circle we can write $x+y=\cos t+\sin t=\sqrt{2}\sin(t+\pi/4)$, which attains $-\sqrt{2}$ when both coordinates are equal and negative.
