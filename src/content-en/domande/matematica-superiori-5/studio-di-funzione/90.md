For $x\ne0$ let $f(x)=x+\dfrac1x$. A plotting program joins the samples at $x=-0.1$ and $x=0.1$ with a segment. What makes that segment misleading?

- It connects two branches separated by a vertical asymptote.
- The function is defined at zero from the right only.
- The origin is a local minimum.
- The graph coincides with the $x$-axis between the samples.

## Explanation

The one-sided limits at zero are infinite with opposite signs. The plotted segment crosses a gap between the actual branches and can create a spurious zero.
