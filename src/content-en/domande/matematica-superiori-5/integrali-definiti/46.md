What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{1}{x^3} \int_0^{x} \sin\left( t^2 \right) dt$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $0$
- $3$

## Explanation

The numerator $\displaystyle\int_0^{x} \sin\left( t^2 \right) dt$ tends to $0$ as $x \to 0$, as does the denominator $x^3$, so the limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The function $\sin\left( t^2 \right)$ is continuous, therefore by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** the derivative of the numerator is $\sin\left( x^2 \right)$. By l'Hôpital's rule
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin\left( x^2 \right)}{3x^2} = \frac{1}{3} ,
$$
where we used the standard limit $\displaystyle\lim_{y \to 0} \dfrac{\sin y}{y} = 1$ with $y = x^2$. Hence the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
