A firm has marginal revenue $R'(q) = 100 - 2q$ and marginal cost $C'(q) = 20 + 2q$ in euros per unit produced. By how much does profit increase when output goes from $0$ to the quantity that maximizes profit?

- $800$ euros
- $2400$ euros
- $1200$ euros
- $1600$ euros

## Explanation

Profit is $\pi(q) = R(q) - C(q)$, so marginal profit is $\pi'(q) = R'(q) - C'(q) = 80 - 4q$. We have $\pi'(q) > 0$ for $q < 20$ and $\pi'(q) < 0$ for $q > 20$, therefore profit is maximal at $q = 20$. By the definite integral evaluation formula, the increase in profit is
$$
\pi(20) - \pi(0) = \int_0^{20} (80 - 4q)\,dq = \Big[ 80q - 2q^2 \Big]_0^{20} = 1600 - 800 = 800 \text{ euros} .
$$
In the graph of the two marginal functions it is the area between the marginal revenue line and the marginal cost line for $0 \leq q \leq 20$. The value $1600$ is the increase in revenue, without subtracting the increase in cost.
