What value does the integral of $\sin^2 x$ between $0$ and $\pi$ take?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $0$
- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

By the half-angle formula $\sin^2 x = \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$, so an antiderivative of $\sin^2 x$ is $\dfrac{x}{2} - \dfrac{\sin 2x}{4}$. By the **Newton–Leibniz formula**
$$
\int_0^{\pi} \sin^2 x\, dx = \left[ \frac{x}{2} - \frac{\sin 2x}{4} \right]_0^{\pi} = \frac{\pi}{2} ,
$$
because $\sin 2\pi = \sin 0 = 0$.
