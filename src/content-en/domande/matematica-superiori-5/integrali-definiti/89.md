Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus with the antiderivative $-\dfrac{1}{x - 1}$ one would get
$$
\int_0^{3} \frac{dx}{(x - 1)^{2}} = \left[ -\frac{1}{x - 1} \right]_0^{3} = -\frac{3}{2} .
$$
Which statement about the integral is true?

- The integral diverges to $+\infty$
- The integral converges and equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- The integral converges, because the interval of integration is bounded
- The integral converges and equals $-\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive and unbounded near $1$, which is interior to $[0, 3]$, so $1$ is a critical point and the fundamental theorem does not apply on $[0, 3]$. The integral is split at $1$, and on the piece $[0, 1)$ we have
$$
\int_0^{s} \frac{dx}{(x - 1)^{2}} = \frac{1}{1 - s} - 1 \to +\infty \qquad (s \to 1^{-}) .
$$
Since this piece diverges to $+\infty$ and the integrand is positive, the integral **diverges to** $+\infty$.
