Let $I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\dfrac{\sin x}{\sin x+\cos x}\,dx$. What value of $I$ follows by making the substitution $u=\pi/2-x$ and adding the two integrals?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac12$

## Explanation

The substitution exchanges sine and cosine and, after reversing the limits, preserves the interval. The integrands add to one, so $2I=\int_0^{\pi/2}1\,dx=\pi/2$.
