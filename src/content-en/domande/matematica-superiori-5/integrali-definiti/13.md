What is the value of the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{1}{x^2 + 4}\,dx$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$

## Explanation

One integrates up to $b$ and then takes the limit as $b \to +\infty$. An antiderivative of the integrand is $\dfrac{1}{2}\arctan\dfrac{x}{2}$, which equals $0$ at $x=0$ and tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{2}=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ as $x \to +\infty$. Hence the integral converges and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
