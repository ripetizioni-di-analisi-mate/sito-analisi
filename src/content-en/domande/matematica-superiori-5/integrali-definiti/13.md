Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{1}{x^2 + 4}\,dx$.

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$

## Explanation

Integrate up to $b$, then let $b$ tend to $+\infty$. An antiderivative is $\tfrac12\arctan(x/2)$; it vanishes at zero and tends to $\pi/4$.
