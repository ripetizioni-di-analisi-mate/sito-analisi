The function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ is decreasing and convex on the interval $[1, 5]$. The interval $[1, 5]$ is divided into $n$ equal parts. With which choice of the points $\xi_k$ is the Riemann sum of $f$ greater than $\displaystyle\int_1^5 \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$ for every $n$?

- It depends on the value of $n$
- With the right endpoints of the subintervals
- With the left endpoints of the subintervals
- With the midpoints of the subintervals

## Explanation

Since $f$ is decreasing, on each subinterval $[x_{k-1}, x_k]$ the largest value is $f(x_{k-1})$. The rectangle of height $f(x_{k-1})$ therefore contains the region under the graph on that subinterval, and the **Riemann sum** with the left endpoints is greater than the integral for every $n$. With the right endpoints the height is the smallest value, so the sum is less than the integral. Since $f$ is convex, the graph lies above the tangent line at the midpoint of each subinterval. The tangent line is a linear function, so its integral over the subinterval equals the area of the rectangle whose height is the value of $f$ at the midpoint. Therefore the integral of $f$ exceeds that area, and the sum with the midpoints is also less than the integral. For $n = 2$ the three sums equal $\dfrac{8}{3}$, $\dfrac{16}{15}$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$, while the integral equals $\ln 5 \approx 1.61$.
