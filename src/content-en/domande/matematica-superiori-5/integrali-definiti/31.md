For $0 \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the graphs of $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ enclose, together with the lines $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, a region. What is its area?

- $0$
- $2\sqrt{2} - 2$
- $2$
- $\sqrt{2} - 1$

## Explanation

On $\left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$ one has $\sin x = \cos x$ only for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$; on $\left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right]$ one has $\cos x \geq \sin x$, and on $\left[ \dfrac{\pi}{4}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$ one has $\sin x \geq \cos x$. By **additivity** on each piece one integrates the difference between the larger and the smaller function:
$$
\mathcal{A} = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{4}} (\cos x - \sin x)\, dx + \int_{\frac{\pi}{4}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} (\sin x - \cos x)\, dx = \left( \sqrt{2} - 1 \right) + \left( \sqrt{2} - 1 \right) = 2\sqrt{2} - 2 .
$$
The integral of $\cos x - \sin x$ over the whole interval equals $0$ instead, because the two contributions are opposite.
