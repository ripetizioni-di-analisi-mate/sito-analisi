A container has horizontal cross-sections of area $A(h)=1+h^2$ square decimetres at height $h$ decimetres. The volume up to height $h$ is $V(h)=\int_0^h A(s)\,ds$. How many decimetres per litre does the level rise instantaneously when $h=2$?

- $5\,\mathrm{dm/L}$
- $\dfrac14\,\mathrm{dm/L}$
- $\dfrac3{14}\,\mathrm{dm/L}$
- $\dfrac15\,\mathrm{dm/L}$

## Explanation

One litre is one cubic decimetre. The fundamental theorem gives $dV/dh=A(h)$, and the rate of change of height with respect to volume is its reciprocal $dh/dV=1/A(h)$. At height $2$ the area is $5$, so the level rises at $1/5$ of a decimetre per litre.
