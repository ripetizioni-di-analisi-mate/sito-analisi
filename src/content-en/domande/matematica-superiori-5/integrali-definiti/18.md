Let $f$ be a differentiable function with continuous derivative on $[0, 1]$, with $f(1) = 3$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f(x)\, dx = 2$. What is the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x\, f'(x)\, dx$?

- $1$
- $-1$
- $5$
- $3$

## Explanation

Apply **integration by parts** differentiating the factor $x$ and integrating $f'(x)$, whose antiderivative is $f(x)$:
$$
\int_0^1 x\, f'(x)\, dx = \bigl[ x\, f(x) \bigr]_0^1 - \int_0^1 f(x)\, dx .
$$
The first term equals $1 \cdot f(1) - 0 \cdot f(0) = 3$, and the value of $f(0)$ is not needed because it is multiplied by $0$. Therefore the integral equals $3 - 2 = 1$.
