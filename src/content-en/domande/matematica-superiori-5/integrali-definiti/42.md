In which of the following integrals is the point $0$ a singular point?

- $\displaystyle\int_0^{1} \frac{e^{x} - 1}{x}\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{1} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{1} x \ln x\,dx$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{1} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^{3}}\,dx$

## Explanation

A finite endpoint is a singular point when the integrand is unbounded near it. As $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $\dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 1$, $x \ln x \to 0$ and $\dfrac{e^{x} - 1}{x} \to 1$, so these three integrands extend continuously to $0$ and their integrals are ordinary. On the other hand $1 - \cos x \sim \dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$, therefore $\dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^{3}} \sim \dfrac{1}{2x}$ tends to $+\infty$, and $0$ is a **singular point** of $\displaystyle\int_0^{1} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^{3}}\,dx$.
