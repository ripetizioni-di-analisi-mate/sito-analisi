A function $f$ continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ satisfies $\displaystyle\int_0^x f(t)\, dt = x^2 \cos x$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$. What is the value of $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$?

- $-\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi^2}{4}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{\pi^2}{4}$

## Explanation

The function $f$ is continuous, so by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** the integral function $\displaystyle\int_0^x f(t)\, dt$ is differentiable and its derivative is $f(x)$. Differentiating both sides of the equality with the product rule gives
$$
f(x) = 2x \cos x - x^2 \sin x .
$$
Since $\cos \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$ and $\sin \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$, one has $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = -\dfrac{\pi^2}{4}$.
