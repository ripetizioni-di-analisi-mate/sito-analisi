Let $f$ be continuous on $[0, +\infty)$ and suppose that the integral $\displaystyle\int_{10}^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$ converges. What can be said about $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$?

- It converges
- It converges and has the same value as $\displaystyle\int_{10}^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$
- Its behavior cannot be determined without knowing $f$ on $[0, 10]$
- It converges only if $f$ is positive on $[0, 10]$

## Explanation

For every $t > 10$, by additivity of the integral,
$$
\int_0^{t} f(x)\,dx = \int_0^{10} f(x)\,dx + \int_{10}^{t} f(x)\,dx .
$$
Since $f$ is continuous on $[0, 10]$, the first term is an ordinary integral, that is, a finite number. The second term has a finite limit as $t \to +\infty$, so the left-hand side also has a finite limit and the integral on $[0, +\infty)$ **converges**. Its value differs from that of the integral on $[10, +\infty)$ by $\displaystyle\int_0^{10} f(x)\,dx$, which in general is not zero.
