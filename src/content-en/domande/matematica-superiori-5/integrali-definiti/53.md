A function $f$ continuous on $[1, +\infty)$ satisfies
$$
\int_1^{t} f(x)\,dx = \arctan t - \frac{\pi}{4} \qquad \text{for every } t > 1 .
$$
What is $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- The integral diverges

## Explanation

By definition the improper integral is the limit as $t \to +\infty$ of the integral over $[1, t]$. Since $\arctan t \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ as $t \to +\infty$,
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx = \lim_{t \to +\infty} \left( \arctan t - \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{4} .
$$
Hence the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
