What is the solution of the initial value problem $y'' - y' - 6y = 0$, $y(0) = 0$, $y'(0) = 5$?

- $y=e^{2x}-e^{-3x}$
- $y=e^{3x}+e^{-2x}$
- $y=e^{3x}-e^{-2x}$
- $y=5e^{3x}-5e^{-2x}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation is $r^2-r-6=(r-3)(r+2)=0$, with roots $3$ and $-2$, so the general solution is $y=Ae^{3x}+Be^{-2x}$. The initial conditions give $A+B=0$ and $3A-2B=5$, hence $A=1$ and $B=-1$. The solution is therefore $y=e^{3x}-e^{-2x}$.
