Both $y=0$ and $y=x^3$ satisfy $y'=3|y|^{2/3}$ and pass through $(0,0)$. What follows for this initial value problem?

- There are no differentiable solutions.
- Every solution must be linear.
- There are at least two solutions.
- Only the zero solution exists.

## Explanation

For $y=x^3$ both sides equal $3x^2$; for $y=0$ they both vanish. The functions have the same initial value but differ as soon as $x\ne0$.
