The function $y$ is positive, satisfies the equation $y' = y \ln y$ and equals $e$ at $x = 0$. Which function is it?

- $y = e^{x + 1}$
- $y = e^{e^{x}}$
- $y = e^{x} + e - 1$
- $y = e^{e^{-x}}$

## Explanation

The equation is defined for $y > 0$ and is separable with $h(y) = y \ln y$. Since $h(1) = 0$, the function $y \equiv 1$ is a constant solution, but it does not satisfy $y(0) = e$.

For $y > 0$ and $y \neq 1$ the variables are separated. The integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{dy}{y \ln y}$ reduces to the basic integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(y)}{f(y)}\, dy$ with $f(y) = \ln y$, so
$$
\ln \lvert \ln y \rvert = x + c \quad\implies\quad \ln y = C e^{x}, \qquad C \neq 0 .
$$
The condition $y(0) = e$ gives $\ln e = C$, that is, $C = 1$. Therefore $\ln y = e^{x}$ and the function is $y = e^{e^{x}}$.
