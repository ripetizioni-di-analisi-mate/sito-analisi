At which points $(x_0, y_0)$ of the plane does the local existence and uniqueness theorem apply to the initial value problem $y' = \dfrac{y}{x}$, $y(x_0) = y_0$?

- All points of the plane
- Only the points with $x_0 > 0$
- All points with $y_0 \neq 0$
- All points with $x_0 \neq 0$

## Explanation

Here $f(x, y) = \dfrac{y}{x}$ and $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \dfrac{1}{x}$, both defined and continuous at the points with $x \neq 0$.

If $x_0 \neq 0$, the point $(x_0, y_0)$ lies in an open rectangle contained entirely in the half-plane $x > 0$ or entirely in the half-plane $x < 0$, where the hypotheses of the theorem are satisfied; hence the theorem applies, also for $x_0 < 0$ and for $y_0 = 0$.

If $x_0 = 0$, every open rectangle containing the point contains points where $f$ is not defined, therefore the theorem does not apply. The required set consists of **all points with** $x_0 \neq 0$.
