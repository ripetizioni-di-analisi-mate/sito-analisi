To solve $y'-2y/x=x^3$ with $y(1)=1$ for $x>0$, multiply by $x^{-2}$. Which derivative now represents the left-hand side?

- $(x^2y)'$
- $\dfrac{y'}{x^2}$
- $\left(\dfrac{y}{x^2}\right)'$
- $\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)'$

## Explanation

The product rule gives $(x^{-2}y)'=x^{-2}y'-2x^{-3}y$. The right-hand side becomes $x$, so both sides can be integrated directly.
