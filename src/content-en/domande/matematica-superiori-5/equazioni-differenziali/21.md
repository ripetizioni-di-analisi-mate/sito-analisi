The functions $y_1 = x^2$ and $y_2 = x^2 + e^{-x^3}$ are solutions of the same linear equation $y' + a(x)\, y = b(x)$. Which function is a solution of the associated homogeneous equation $y' + a(x)\, y = 0$?

- $x^2$
- $x^2 e^{-x^3}$
- $2x^2 + e^{-x^3}$
- $e^{-x^3}$

## Explanation

We write the two identities $y_1' + a(x)\, y_1 = b(x)$ and $y_2' + a(x)\, y_2 = b(x)$. Subtracting them side by side gives
$$
(y_2 - y_1)' + a(x)\,(y_2 - y_1) = 0 ,
$$
so the difference $y_2 - y_1 = e^{-x^3}$ solves the associated homogeneous equation. This is the **structure of the general solution**: two solutions of the complete equation differ by a solution of the homogeneous one.

From $e^{-x^3}$ one also obtains $a(x) = 3x^2$, and the function $x^2$ does not solve the homogeneous equation, because $2x + 3x^2 \cdot x^2 \neq 0$.
