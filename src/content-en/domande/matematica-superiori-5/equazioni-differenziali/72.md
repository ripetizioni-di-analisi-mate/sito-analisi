For $x>0$ the function $u(x)=x^2/4$ solves $y'=\sqrt y$. Why is $2u$ not a solution too?

- Doubling $y$ multiplies $\sqrt y$ by $\sqrt2$, whereas the derivative doubles.
- The square root is undefined for $2u>0$.
- Doubling a function leaves its derivative unchanged.
- The function $u$ is constant for $x>0$.

## Explanation

The left-hand side becomes $2u'$ and the right-hand side $\sqrt{2u}=\sqrt2\,u'$. Since $u'>0$ on the interval, the values differ. The equation is nonlinear.
