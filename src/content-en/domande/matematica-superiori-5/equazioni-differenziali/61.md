For $x > 0$ the equation $x^2\, y' + e^{x}\, y = \sin x$ is a first-order linear equation. Written in the form $y' + a(x)\, y = b(x)$, what are the coefficients?

- $a(x) = -\dfrac{e^{x}}{x^2}$, $b(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x^2}$
- $a(x) = \dfrac{e^{x}}{x^2}$, $b(x) = \sin x$
- $a(x) = \dfrac{e^{x}}{x^2}$, $b(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x^2}$
- $a(x) = e^{x}$, $b(x) = \sin x$

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ the coefficient $x^2$ of $y'$ is nonzero, so dividing both sides by $x^2$ gives the equivalent equation
$$
y' + \frac{e^{x}}{x^2}\, y = \frac{\sin x}{x^2} .
$$
Therefore $a(x) = \dfrac{e^{x}}{x^2}$ and $b(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x^2}$, both continuous on $(0, +\infty)$. The sign of $a$ is positive because the term $e^{x}\, y$ is already on the left-hand side.
