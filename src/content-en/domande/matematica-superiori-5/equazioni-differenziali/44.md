The problem $y'=\dfrac1{2y}$, $y(0)=1$ has solution $y=\sqrt{x+1}$ for $x>-1$. Why does the finite value obtained by substituting $x=-1$ in the formula fail to extend the differential-equation solution to that point?

- The square root tends to $+\infty$.
- It gives $y=0$, where the right-hand side of the equation is undefined.
- The solution becomes discontinuous from the right.
- The initial value becomes $y(0)=0$.

## Explanation

As $x\to-1^+$, the value of $y$ tends to zero and the derivative $1/(2\sqrt{x+1})$ grows without bound. The function has a continuous extension to the boundary, where the equation itself is undefined.
