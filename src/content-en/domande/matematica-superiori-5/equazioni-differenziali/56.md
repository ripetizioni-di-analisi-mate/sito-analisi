Which of the following pairs of solutions of the equation $y'' - 4y = 0$ does *not* allow every solution to be written in the form $C_1 y_1 + C_2 y_2$?

- $y_1 = e^{2x}$, $y_2 = 3e^{2x}$
- $y_1 = e^{2x} + e^{-2x}$, $y_2 = e^{2x} - e^{-2x}$
- $y_1 = e^{2x}$, $y_2 = e^{-2x}$
- $y_1 = e^{-2x}$, $y_2 = e^{2x} + e^{-2x}$

## Explanation

The solutions of $y'' - 4y = 0$ form a vector space of dimension $2$, and every solution can be written as $C_1 y_1 + C_2 y_2$ when $y_1$ and $y_2$ are non-proportional solutions.

The functions $e^{2x}$ and $3e^{2x}$ are proportional, so their combinations are only the multiples $C e^{2x}$ and the solution $e^{-2x}$ is not obtained. In the other three pairs the two functions are not proportional, therefore each of them generates all solutions.
