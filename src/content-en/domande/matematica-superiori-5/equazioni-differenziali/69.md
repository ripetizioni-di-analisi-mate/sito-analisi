The law $T'=-k(T-A)$ describes an object's temperature in surroundings at temperature $A$, with $k>0$. If the surroundings instantly change from $20$ to $30$ while $T=25$, what happens to the sign of $T'$?

- It stays positive.
- It changes from negative to positive.
- It stays negative.
- It changes from positive to negative.

## Explanation

Before the change, $T-A=5$ and the object cools. Immediately afterwards $T-A=-5$, so heat flow reverses and $T'>0$.
