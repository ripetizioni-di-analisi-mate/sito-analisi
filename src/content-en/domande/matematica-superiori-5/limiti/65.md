What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} x\, e^{\frac{1}{x}}$?

- $+\infty$
- $0$
- $e$
- $1$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ the factor $x$ tends to $0$ and the factor $e^{\frac{1}{x}}$ tends to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $0 \cdot \infty$. Setting $t = \dfrac{1}{x}$, which tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$, we get
$$
\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} x\, e^{\frac{1}{x}} = \lim_{t \to +\infty} \frac{e^{t}}{t} .
$$
By the **hierarchy of growth rates** the exponential $e^{t}$ grows faster than the power $t$, so $\dfrac{t}{e^{t}} \to 0^{+}$. Therefore the quotient $\dfrac{e^{t}}{t}$ tends to $+\infty$, and the limit equals $+\infty$.
