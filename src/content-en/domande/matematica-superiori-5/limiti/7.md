What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 3x} - \sqrt{x^2 - x} \right)$?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $4$
- $2$

## Explanation

The limit has the form $+\infty - \infty$. Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} + \sqrt{x^2 - x}$ gives
$$
\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} - \sqrt{x^2 - x} = \frac{(x^2 + 3x) - (x^2 - x)}{\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} + \sqrt{x^2 - x}} = \frac{4x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} + \sqrt{x^2 - x}} .
$$
For $x > 0$ we have $\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} = x\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{3}{x}}$ and $\sqrt{x^2 - x} = x\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{x}}$, so the quotient becomes
$$
\frac{4}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{3}{x}} + \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{x}}} .
$$
The denominator tends to $1 + 1 = 2$, hence the limit equals $2$.
