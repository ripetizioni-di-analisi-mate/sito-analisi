How does the power $x^{\frac{1}{x}}$ behave as $x \to +\infty$?

- $+\infty$
- $1$
- $e$
- $0$

## Explanation

The base tends to $+\infty$ and the exponent $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\infty^{0}$.

For $x > 0$ we write the power as an exponential:
$$
x^{\frac{1}{x}} = e^{\frac{\ln x}{x}} .
$$
By the **hierarchy of infinities** we have $\dfrac{\ln x}{x} \to 0$. Since the exponential function is continuous, the limit equals $e^{0} = 1$.
