All that is known about a function $f$ is that $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} f(x) = 7$. Which of the following statements is certainly true?

- There exists $\delta > 0$ such that $f(x) > 6$ for every $x$ with $0 < |x - 2| < \delta$
- $f(2) = 7$
- There exists $\delta > 0$ such that $f(x) < 7$ for every $x$ with $0 < |x - 2| < \delta$
- $f$ is continuous at $x = 2$

## Explanation

In the definition of limit we choose $\varepsilon = 1$. Then there exists $\delta > 0$ such that $|f(x) - 7| < 1$ for $0 < |x - 2| < \delta$, that is $6 < f(x) < 8$, so in particular $f(x) > 6$. The definition excludes the point $x = 2$, therefore the value $f(2)$ may differ from $7$ or not exist, and continuity at $2$ is not guaranteed. The inequality $f(x) < 7$ is not guaranteed either, because $f(x) = 7 + (x - 2)^2$ tends to $7$ and is greater than $7$ for every $x \neq 2$.
