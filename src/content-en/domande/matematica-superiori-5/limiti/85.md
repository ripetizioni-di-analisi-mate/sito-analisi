Let $f(x) = \dfrac{\ln\left(e^{2x} + x\right)}{x}$. What is the limit of $f$ as $x \to +\infty$?

- $+\infty$
- $2$
- $0$
- $1$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$.

In the argument of the logarithm the dominant term is $e^{2x}$, therefore we factor it out and use the logarithm of a product:
$$
\ln\left(e^{2x} + x\right) = \ln\left(e^{2x}\left(1 + x e^{-2x}\right)\right) = 2x + \ln\left(1 + x e^{-2x}\right) .
$$
By the **hierarchy of infinities** we have $x e^{-2x} = \dfrac{x}{e^{2x}} \to 0$, so $\ln\left(1 + x e^{-2x}\right) \to 0$. Dividing by $x$ we obtain $2 + \dfrac{\ln\left(1 + x e^{-2x}\right)}{x}$, which tends to $2$.
