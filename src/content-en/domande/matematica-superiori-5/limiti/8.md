What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin (2x)}{\tan (6x)}$?

- $0$
- $1$
- $\dfrac13$
- $3$

## Explanation

The limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. For $x \neq 0$ near $0$ the quotient can be written as a product of three factors:
$$
\frac{\sin (2x)}{\tan (6x)} = \frac{\sin (2x)}{2x} \cdot \frac{6x}{\tan (6x)} \cdot \frac{2x}{6x} .
$$
By the fundamental limit of the sine and the limit $\dfrac{\tan t}{t} \to 1$ derived from it, the first two factors tend to $1$. Since $\dfrac{2x}{6x} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
