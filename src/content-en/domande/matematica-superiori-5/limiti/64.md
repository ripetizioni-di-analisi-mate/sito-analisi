What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1^{+}} \frac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x^2 - 1}}$?

- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The function is defined for $x^2 - 1 > 0$, that is, for $x < -1$ or $x > 1$, so near $1$ only the right-hand limit makes sense. As $x \to 1^{+}$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$ and $x - 1 = \sqrt{x - 1}\,\sqrt{x - 1}$ for $x > 1$, we have
$$
\frac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x^2 - 1}} = \frac{\sqrt{x - 1}\,\sqrt{x - 1}}{\sqrt{x - 1}\,\sqrt{x + 1}} = \frac{\sqrt{x - 1}}{\sqrt{x + 1}} .
$$
The numerator tends to $0$ and the denominator to $\sqrt{2}$, so the limit equals $0$.
