What is the value of the limit of the ratio of $\ln x$ to $\sqrt{x}$ as $x \to +\infty$?

- $2$
- $0$
- $1$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$.

We have $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$, therefore the ratio is $\dfrac{(\ln x)^{1}}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}$ with exponents $\beta = 1$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, both positive. By the **hierarchy of infinities** the logarithm grows more slowly than every power with positive exponent, so the limit equals $0$.
