What is the limit as $x \to 3$ of the quotient $\dfrac{\sqrt{x + 1} - 2}{\sqrt{x - 2} - 1}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $1$

## Explanation

At $x = 3$ we have $\sqrt{4} - 2 = 0$ and $\sqrt{1} - 1 = 0$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

We rationalize both the numerator and the denominator. Since $(\sqrt{x + 1} - 2)(\sqrt{x + 1} + 2) = x - 3$ and $(\sqrt{x - 2} - 1)(\sqrt{x - 2} + 1) = x - 3$, for $x \neq 3$ we obtain
$$
\frac{\sqrt{x + 1} - 2}{\sqrt{x - 2} - 1} = \frac{x - 3}{\sqrt{x + 1} + 2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x - 2} + 1}{x - 3} = \frac{\sqrt{x - 2} + 1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + 2} .
$$
The last expression is continuous at $3$, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1 + 1}{2 + 2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
