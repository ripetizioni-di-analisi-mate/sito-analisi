Let $f(x) = e^{x} - 1 - \sin x$. What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{f(x)}{x^{2}}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $0$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $0$. In the numerator $e^{x} - 1 \sim x$ and $\sin x \sim x$ appear in a difference whose principal parts cancel, so second-order expansions are needed.

By **Taylor's formula** we have $e^{x} = 1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2} + o(x^2)$ and $\sin x = x + o(x^2)$, therefore
$$
e^{x} - 1 - \sin x = \frac{x^2}{2} + o(x^2) .
$$
Dividing by $x^2$ we obtain $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{o(x^2)}{x^2}$, so the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
