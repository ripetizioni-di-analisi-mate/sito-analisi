Why is it incorrect to deduce $\lim_{x\to0}\sin x/x=0$ from continuity of the sine?

- The quotient rule for limits was applied when the denominator tends to zero.
- The denominator changes sign only on the right.
- The sine is discontinuous at zero.
- The numerator tends to one.

## Explanation

Substitution produces the form $0/0$. The quotient rule requires a nonzero limit in the denominator. The standard sine limit, in radians, equals $1$.
