What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos (4x)}{x^2}$?

- $16$
- $8$
- $4$
- $2$

## Explanation

The limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Multiplying and dividing by $16 = 4^2$ brings in the limit $\dfrac{1 - \cos t}{t^2} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$ with $t = 4x$:
$$
\frac{1 - \cos (4x)}{x^2} = 16 \cdot \frac{1 - \cos (4x)}{(4x)^2} .
$$
Since $t = 4x$ tends to $0$, the second factor tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the limit equals $16 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 8$.
