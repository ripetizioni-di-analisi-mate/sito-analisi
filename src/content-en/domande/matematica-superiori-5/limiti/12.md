What is the order of the infinitesimal $f(x) = \sqrt{1 + x^2} - 1$ relative to $x$ as $x \to 0$?

- $4$
- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac12$

## Explanation

Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{1 + x^2} + 1$ gives
$$
\sqrt{1 + x^2} - 1 = \frac{(1 + x^2) - 1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2} + 1} = \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1 + x^2} + 1} .
$$
Hence $\dfrac{f(x)}{x^2} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2} + 1}$, which tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to 0$. The limit is finite and nonzero, so $f$ is an infinitesimal of order $2$ relative to $x$.
