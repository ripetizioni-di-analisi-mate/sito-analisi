Measurements show that $f(1/n)\to1$ and $f(-1/n)\to1$. What can be concluded about the limit of $f$ at zero from these data alone?

- It may fail to exist, since only two sequences were checked.
- The two one-sided limits are different.
- It necessarily exists and equals $0$.
- It necessarily exists and equals $1$.

## Explanation

Set $f=1$ at the measured points and $f=0$ elsewhere. This satisfies the data but takes two distinct values arbitrarily close to zero.
