What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{3}{x} \right)^{2x}$?

- $e^{-3}$
- $1$
- $e^{-6}$
- $e^{6}$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit has the form $1^{\infty}$. By the properties of powers,
$$
\left( 1 - \frac{3}{x} \right)^{2x} = \left[ \left( 1 - \frac{3}{x} \right)^{x} \right]^{2} .
$$
By the limit $\left( 1 + \dfrac{k}{x} \right)^{x} \to e^{k}$ with $k = -3$, the expression in square brackets tends to $e^{-3}$. Therefore the limit equals $\left( e^{-3} \right)^{2} = e^{-6}$.
