What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -3} \frac{x^2 + x - 6}{x^2 - 9}$?

- $\dfrac56$
- $\dfrac16$
- $-\dfrac56$
- $\dfrac65$

## Explanation

At $x = -3$ both the numerator and the denominator equal $0$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

Since $x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2)$ and $x^2 - 9 = (x + 3)(x - 3)$, for $x \neq -3$ we have
$$
\frac{x^2 + x - 6}{x^2 - 9} = \frac{x - 2}{x - 3} .
$$
Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{-3 - 2}{-3 - 3} = \dfrac{-5}{-6} = \dfrac{5}{6}$.
