The figure shows the graph of a function $f$ defined on $\mathbb{R}$: the filled dot marks the value $f(1)$, the hollow dot a point that does not belong to the graph. What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)$?

::grafico{legenda=0 f="x^2-2*x+2" punti="1,1,vuoto;1,3,pieno" segmenti="1,0,1,3" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=6 titolo="The graph of $f$; at $x = 1$ the filled dot is the point $(1, 3)$ and the hollow dot is the point $(1, 1)$"}

- The limit does not exist
- $2$
- $1$
- $3$

## Explanation

The definition of limit only involves the values of $x$ with $0 < |x - 1| < \delta$, so the value $f(1) = 3$ plays no role. For $x$ close to $1$, from the right and from the left, the points of the graph approach the point $(1, 1)$. Therefore the right-hand and left-hand limits are both $1$, and $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = 1$. Since the limit differs from $f(1)$, the function has a removable discontinuity at $x = 1$.
