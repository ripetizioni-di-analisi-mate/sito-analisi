The quotient $\dfrac{\cos 2x}{\cos x - \sin x}$ is not defined at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. What is its limit as $x \to \dfrac{\pi}{4}$?

- $\sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $0$
- $1$

## Explanation

At $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ we have $\cos \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$ and $\cos \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \sin \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

By the double-angle formula $\cos 2x = \cos^{2} x - \sin^{2} x$, and the difference of squares factors:
$$
\cos 2x = (\cos x - \sin x)(\cos x + \sin x) .
$$
Near $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, with $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, we have $\cos x - \sin x \neq 0$, therefore we cancel and the quotient becomes $\cos x + \sin x$. This function is continuous, so the limit equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2}$.
