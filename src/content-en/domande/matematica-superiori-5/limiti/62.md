The difference $\ln(2x + 1) - \ln x$ is in the form $+\infty - \infty$ as $x \to +\infty$. What is its limit?

- $\ln 3$
- $\ln 2$
- $+\infty$
- $0$

## Explanation

Both logarithms tend to $+\infty$, so the difference is in the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$.

By the logarithm of a quotient, for $x > 0$ we have
$$
\ln(2x + 1) - \ln x = \ln \frac{2x + 1}{x} = \ln\left(2 + \frac{1}{x}\right) .
$$
The argument tends to $2$ and the logarithm is continuous, therefore the limit equals $\ln 2$.
