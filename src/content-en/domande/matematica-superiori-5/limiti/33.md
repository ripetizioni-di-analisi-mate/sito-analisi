What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \left( 1 + \sin x \right)^{\frac{1}{x}}$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $e$
- $e^{-1}$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to infinity, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$.

We write the power as an exponential:
$$
\left( 1 + \sin x \right)^{\frac{1}{x}} = e^{\frac{\ln(1 + \sin x)}{x}} .
$$
Since $\sin x \to 0$, by the standard limit of the logarithm $\ln(1 + \sin x) \sim \sin x$, and by the standard limit of the sine $\sin x \sim x$. So the exponent tends to $1$ and, by the continuity of the exponential, the limit equals $e$.
