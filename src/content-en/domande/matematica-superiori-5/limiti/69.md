The present value of $n$ annual payments of $600$ euros at the end of each year, at the annual rate of $4\%$, is $V(n) = 600 \cdot \dfrac{1 - (1.04)^{-n}}{0.04}$ euros. What value does $V(n)$ tend to as $n \to +\infty$, that is, what is the present value of the perpetuity?

- $15{,}000$ euros
- $24$ euros
- $+\infty$
- $1{,}500$ euros

## Explanation

Since $1.04 > 1$, we have $(1.04)^{n} \to +\infty$, so $(1.04)^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{(1.04)^{n}} \to 0$. Therefore the numerator $1 - (1.04)^{-n}$ tends to $1$, and
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} V(n) = \frac{600}{0.04} = 15{,}000 .
$$
The sum of the payments is infinite, but their present value has a finite limit, because payments far in the future are discounted by factors that tend to $0$.
