For which exponent $\alpha > 0$ is the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x^{3} + x}}{x^{\alpha}}$ finite and nonzero?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$
- $3$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

The required exponent is the order of infinity of $\sqrt{x^{3} + x}$ with respect to $x$.

Factoring out $x^3$ under the root, for $x > 0$ we have
$$
\sqrt{x^{3} + x} = x^{\frac{3}{2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x^{2}}} .
$$
The second factor tends to $1$, so the quotient $\dfrac{\sqrt{x^{3} + x}}{x^{\alpha}}$ has limit $1$ for $\alpha = \dfrac{3}{2}$. For $\alpha < \dfrac{3}{2}$ the limit is $+\infty$ and for $\alpha > \dfrac{3}{2}$ it is $0$, therefore the required exponent is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
