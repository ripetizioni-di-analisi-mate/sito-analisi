The functions $\ln x$, $x^{0.1}$, $x^{5}$ and $(1.1)^{x}$ all tend to $+\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$. Which list orders them from slowest to fastest, in the sense that the quotient of each one by the next tends to $0$?

- $\ln x$, $x^{0.1}$, $(1.1)^{x}$, $x^{5}$
- $\ln x$, $x^{0.1}$, $x^{5}$, $(1.1)^{x}$
- $x^{0.1}$, $\ln x$, $(1.1)^{x}$, $x^{5}$
- $x^{0.1}$, $\ln x$, $x^{5}$, $(1.1)^{x}$

## Explanation

By the **hierarchy of growth rates**, with $\beta = 1$ and $\alpha = 0.1$, we have $\dfrac{\ln x}{x^{0.1}} \to 0$, so $\ln x$ comes before $x^{0.1}$ even though the exponent $0.1$ is small. Since $\dfrac{x^{0.1}}{x^{5}} = \dfrac{1}{x^{4.9}} \to 0$, the power $x^{0.1}$ comes before $x^{5}$. Finally the base $1.1$ is greater than $1$, so $\dfrac{x^{5}}{(1.1)^{x}} \to 0$ and the exponential is the fastest. Therefore the order is $\ln x$, $x^{0.1}$, $x^{5}$, $(1.1)^{x}$.
