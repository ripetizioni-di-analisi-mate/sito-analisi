As $x \to +\infty$ we have $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x = \dfrac{c}{x} + o\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$ for a suitable constant $c$. What is $c$?

- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $1$

## Explanation

The difference is in the form $+\infty - \infty$ and tends to $0$, so we look for its principal part.

Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} + x$ we obtain
$$
\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} + x} .
$$
For $x > 0$ the denominator is $x \left( \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}} + 1 \right)$, which is asymptotic to $2x$. Therefore $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x \sim \dfrac{1}{2x}$, that is $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x = \dfrac{1}{2x} + o\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$, and so $c = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
