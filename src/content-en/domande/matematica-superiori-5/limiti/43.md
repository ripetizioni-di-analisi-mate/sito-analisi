Let $\lfloor x \rfloor$ denote the integer part of $x$, that is, the greatest integer less than or equal to $x$. What is the limit of $\dfrac{\lfloor x \rfloor}{x}$ as $x \to +\infty$?

- $0$
- The limit does not exist
- $+\infty$
- $1$

## Explanation

By the definition of the integer part, $x - 1 < \lfloor x \rfloor \leq x$ for every $x$. For $x > 0$ we divide the three members by $x$ and obtain
$$
1 - \frac{1}{x} < \frac{\lfloor x \rfloor}{x} \leq 1 .
$$
The functions $1 - \dfrac{1}{x}$ and $1$ both tend to $1$ as $x \to +\infty$. Therefore, by the **squeeze theorem**, $\dfrac{\lfloor x \rfloor}{x}$ also tends to $1$.

At the integer points $x = k$ the quotient has jumps of size $\dfrac{1}{k}$, and this size tends to $0$ as $k \to +\infty$.
