What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}{x}$?

- $1$
- $-\infty$
- $-1$
- $0$

## Explanation

Factoring $x^2$ out of the square root gives $\sqrt{x^2 + 1} = \sqrt{x^2}\,\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}} = |x|\,\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}}$. Since $x < 0$, we have $|x| = -x$, so
$$
\frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}{x} = \frac{-x\,\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}}}{x} = -\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x^2}} .
$$
As $x \to -\infty$ the square root tends to $1$, therefore the limit is $-1$.
