The function $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x^3 + 3x^2} - x$ has a finite limit as $x \to +\infty$. Which one?

- $0$
- $3$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The cube root and $x$ tend to $+\infty$, so the difference is in the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$.

Factoring out $x^3$ under the root we obtain
$$
\sqrt[3]{x^3 + 3x^2} - x = x \left( \sqrt[3]{1 + \frac{3}{x}} - 1 \right) = \frac{\left(1 + \dfrac{3}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} - 1}{\dfrac{1}{x}} .
$$
With $t = \dfrac{1}{x} \to 0$ the quotient becomes $\dfrac{(1 + 3t)^{\frac{1}{3}} - 1}{t}$. By the standard limit $(1 + y)^{\alpha} - 1 \sim \alpha y$ with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $y = 3t$, the numerator is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 3t = t$, therefore the limit equals $1$.
