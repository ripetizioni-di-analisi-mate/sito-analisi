For which value of the real parameter $k$ does $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{k x^3 - x + 2}{5 - 2x^3} = 3$ hold?

- $k=6$
- $k=-\dfrac32$
- $k=-6$
- $k=3$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator are polynomials of degree $3$, so the limit as $x \to +\infty$ is the ratio of the leading coefficients. Indeed, dividing the numerator and the denominator by $x^3$ gives
$$
\frac{k x^3 - x + 2}{5 - 2x^3} = \frac{k - \frac{1}{x^2} + \frac{2}{x^3}}{\frac{5}{x^3} - 2} ,
$$
which tends to $-\dfrac{k}{2}$. The condition $-\dfrac{k}{2} = 3$ therefore gives $k = -6$.
