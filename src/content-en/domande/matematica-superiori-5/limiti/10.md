What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{4x} - 1}{\ln (1 + 2x)}$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac12$
- $4$
- $2$

## Explanation

The limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. For $x \neq 0$ near $0$ the quotient can be written as a product of three factors:
$$
\frac{e^{4x} - 1}{\ln (1 + 2x)} = \frac{e^{4x} - 1}{4x} \cdot \frac{2x}{\ln (1 + 2x)} \cdot \frac{4x}{2x} .
$$
By the limits derived from the fundamental limit of $e$, the first two factors tend to $1$. Since $\dfrac{4x}{2x} = 2$, the limit equals $2$.
