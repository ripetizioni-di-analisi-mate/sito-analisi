A firm sells each unit of its product for $50$ euros, and the total cost of producing $q$ units is $C(q) = q^2 + 10q + 300$ euros. What does the profit $\pi(q) = R(q) - C(q)$ tend to as $q \to +\infty$?

- $-300$
- $+\infty$
- $40$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

The revenue is $R(q) = 50q$, so the profit is
$$
\pi(q) = 50q - \left( q^2 + 10q + 300 \right) = -q^2 + 40q - 300 .
$$
As $q \to +\infty$ the term $-q^2$ tends to $-\infty$ and the term $40q$ to $+\infty$, so the limit has the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$. Factoring out the highest power gives
$$
\pi(q) = q^2 \left( -1 + \frac{40}{q} - \frac{300}{q^2} \right) .
$$
The first factor tends to $+\infty$ and the second to $-1$, hence $\pi(q) \to -\infty$. For large quantities the cost, which grows like $q^2$, exceeds the revenue, which grows like $q$.
