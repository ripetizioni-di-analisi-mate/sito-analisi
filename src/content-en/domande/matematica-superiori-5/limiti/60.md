Consider the set $A = \left\{ \dfrac{1}{n} : n \in \mathbb{N}, \ n \geq 1 \right\}$ of the reciprocals of the positive integers. Which set do its accumulation points form?

- $\emptyset$
- $A \cup \{0\}$
- $[0, 1]$
- $\{0\}$

## Explanation

A point $x_0$ is an accumulation point of $A$ if every neighborhood of $x_0$ contains at least one point of $A$ different from $x_0$.

The point $0$ is an accumulation point, because for every $r > 0$ there exists $n$ with $\dfrac{1}{n} < r$, so the neighborhood $(-r, r)$ contains the point $\dfrac{1}{n}$ of $A$. A point $\dfrac{1}{n}$ of $A$ is not an accumulation point, because the nearest points of $A$ are $\dfrac{1}{n + 1}$ and $\dfrac{1}{n - 1}$, therefore a neighborhood of radius smaller than $\dfrac{1}{n} - \dfrac{1}{n + 1}$ contains no other point of $A$. In the same way a point $x_0 \neq 0$ that does not belong to $A$ has a neighborhood with no points of $A$, so the required set is $\{0\}$.
