The tip of a pole is at the point $P = (1, 2, 4)$ and the sun's rays have direction $\vec{d} = (1, 1, -2)$. At which point of the ground $z = 0$ does the shadow of the tip fall?

- $(1, 2, 0)$
- $(3, 4, 0)$
- $(5, 6, 0)$
- $(2, 3, 0)$

## Explanation

The shadow of the tip is the point where the ray through $P$ meets the ground. The ray is the line $P + t\,\vec{d} = (1 + t,\ 2 + t,\ 4 - 2t)$, and the condition $z = 0$ gives $4 - 2t = 0$, so $t = 2$. The shadow point is therefore $(1 + 2,\ 2 + 2,\ 0) = (3, 4, 0)$. The point $(1, 2, 0)$ is the orthogonal projection of $P$ onto the ground, which coincides with the shadow only when the rays are vertical, that is, parallel to $(0, 0, 1)$.
