Which set of points is described by the equations $x = 1 + t$, $y = 2t$, $z = 3 - t$ when the parameter ranges over the interval $t \in [0, 1]$?

- The line through $(1, 0, 3)$ and $(2, 2, 2)$
- The half-line starting at $(1, 0, 3)$ and passing through $(2, 2, 2)$
- The segment with endpoints $(1, 0, 3)$ and $(2, 2, 2)$
- The segment with endpoints $(1, 0, 3)$ and $(1, 2, -1)$

## Explanation

As $t$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, the equations describe the line through $A = (1, 0, 3)$ with direction vector $\vec{v} = (1, 2, -1)$. For $t = 0$ one obtains the point $A$, and for $t = 1$ the point $B = A + \vec{v} = (2, 2, 2)$. Since the point $A + t\,\vec{v}$ with $0 \leq t \leq 1$ lies between $A$ and $B$, the set described is the **segment** with endpoints $(1, 0, 3)$ and $(2, 2, 2)$. The point $(1, 2, -1)$ has the coordinates of the direction vector and does not lie on the line, because the first equation gives $t = 0$ and hence $y = 0 \neq 2$.
