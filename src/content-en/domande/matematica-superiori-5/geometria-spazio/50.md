The point $P = (1, 1, 2)$ lies on the line $r : x = t$, $y = t$, $z = 2t$. How many lines pass through $P$, meet $r$ and are perpendicular to $r$?

- Exactly one
- None
- Infinitely many, and they form the plane $x + y + 2z - 6 = 0$
- Infinitely many, and they form the plane $x + y + 2z = 0$

## Explanation

Since $P$ lies on $r$, every line through $P$ meets $r$ at the point $P$. A line through $P$ is perpendicular to $r$ if and only if its direction vector is orthogonal to $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 2)$. The lines through $P$ with direction vector orthogonal to $\vec{v}$ are infinitely many, and they are exactly the lines through $P$ contained in the plane through $P$ with normal vector $\vec{v}$. This plane has equation $(x - 1) + (y - 1) + 2(z - 2) = 0$, that is, $x + y + 2z - 6 = 0$. The plane $x + y + 2z = 0$ has the same normal vector but does not contain $P$. If $P$ did not lie on $r$, the required line would be unique.
