What is the relative position of the planes $\pi_1 : x + y + z = 1$, $\pi_2 : x - y + z = 3$ and $\pi_3 : 2x + 2z = 4$?

- The three planes have a single point in common
- The three planes intersect pairwise in three distinct parallel lines
- The three planes have a line in common
- Two of the three planes are parallel

## Explanation

The points common to the three planes are the solutions of the system of the three equations. In the coefficient matrix the third row $(2, 0, 2)$ is the sum of the first two, so the matrix has rank $2$ and the three planes do not have a single common point. The constant terms also satisfy $1 + 3 = 4$, therefore the equation of $\pi_3$ is the sum of the equations of $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ and the augmented matrix has rank $2$. By the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system then has $\infty^1$ solutions, and the three planes **have a line in common**, that is, $\pi_3$ belongs to the pencil of planes whose axis is the line $\pi_1 \cap \pi_2$. The three distinct parallel lines would occur with an augmented matrix of rank $3$. No pair of planes is parallel, because the normal vectors $(1, 1, 1)$, $(1, -1, 1)$, $(2, 0, 2)$ are pairwise non-proportional.
