The triangle with vertices $A = (1, 0, 1)$, $B = (3, 1, 2)$, $C = (1, 2, 3)$ is projected orthogonally onto the plane $z = 0$. What is the area of the projected triangle?

- $4$
- $2$
- $2\sqrt{2}$
- $4\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The orthogonal projection onto the plane $z = 0$ sets the third coordinate to zero, so the projected triangle has vertices $A' = (1, 0, 0)$, $B' = (3, 1, 0)$, $C' = (1, 2, 0)$. We have $\overrightarrow{A'B'} = (2, 1, 0)$ and $\overrightarrow{A'C'} = (0, 2, 0)$, therefore
$$
\overrightarrow{A'B'} \times \overrightarrow{A'C'} = (0, 0, 4) .
$$
The area of a triangle is half the norm of the cross product of two sides, so the area of the projected triangle is $\dfrac{4}{2} = 2$. The value $4$ is the norm of the cross product without the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The triangle $ABC$ has $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = (0, -4, 4)$, of norm $4\sqrt{2}$, so $2\sqrt{2}$ is the area of $ABC$, which is larger than the area of its projection.
