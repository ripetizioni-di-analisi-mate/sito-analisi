What set do the points of space equidistant from $A = (2, 0, 0)$, $B = (0, 2, 0)$ and $C = (0, 0, 0)$ form?

::grafico{punti="2,0,pieno,A;0,2,pieno,B;0,0,pieno,C" linee="2,0 0,2 0,0 2,0" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=3 uguale=1 titolo="The points $A$, $B$, $C$ in the plane $z = 0$"}

- Only the point $(1, 1, 0)$
- The line $x + y = 2$, $z = 0$
- The line $x = 1$, $y = 1$
- The plane $x = y$

## Explanation

The points equidistant from $A$ and $C$ form the plane through the midpoint $(1, 0, 0)$ orthogonal to $\overrightarrow{CA} = (2, 0, 0)$, that is, the plane $x = 1$. In the same way the points equidistant from $B$ and $C$ form the plane $y = 1$. The points equidistant from $A$, $B$ and $C$ satisfy both conditions, so they form the line
$$
\begin{cases}
x = 1 \\[4pt]
y = 1
\end{cases}
$$
perpendicular to the plane $z = 0$ of the three points. Indeed every point $(1, 1, z)$ is at distance $\sqrt{2 + z^2}$ from each of the three points. The point $(1, 1, 0)$ is the circumcenter of the triangle $ABC$ and is the only point of the line lying in the plane $z = 0$. The plane $x = y$ consists of the points equidistant from $A$ and $B$, which in general are not equidistant from $C$.
