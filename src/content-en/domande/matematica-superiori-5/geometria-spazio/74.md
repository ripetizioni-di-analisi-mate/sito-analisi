The line $r$ has equations $x - y = 0$, $z = 1$, and the point $Q = (2, 2, 1)$ belongs to $r$. How many planes of the pencil with axis $r$ pass through $Q$?

- All the planes of the pencil
- Only one, the plane $x - y = 0$
- Only one, the plane $z = 1$
- None

## Explanation

By the definition of a proper pencil, every plane of the pencil with axis $r$ contains the line $r$. Since $Q$ belongs to $r$, the point $Q$ therefore belongs to every plane of the pencil. Indeed, substituting $Q$ into the equation $\lambda\,(x - y) + \mu\,(z - 1) = 0$ gives $0 = 0$ for every pair $(\lambda, \mu)$, so the condition of passing through $Q$ does not determine $\lambda$ and $\mu$. Passing through a point singles out one plane of the pencil when the point does not lie on the axis.
