Let $\vec{u} \neq \vec{0}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ be vectors in space such that $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = \vec{u} \cdot \vec{w}$. Which statement is certainly true?

- $\vec{v} = \vec{w}$
- $\|\vec{v}\| = \|\vec{w}\|$
- $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{w}$ are parallel
- $\vec{v} - \vec{w}$ is orthogonal to $\vec{u}$

## Explanation

By the linearity of the dot product the hypothesis is equivalent to $\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} - \vec{w}) = 0$, therefore $\vec{v} - \vec{w}$ is **orthogonal** to $\vec{u}$. The dot product does not allow one to cancel $\vec{u}$, because the vectors orthogonal to $\vec{u}$ are not only the zero vector. For example, with $\vec{u} = (1, 0, 0)$, $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 0)$ and $\vec{w} = (1, 0, 2)$ one has $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = \vec{u} \cdot \vec{w} = 1$. In this example $\vec{v} \neq \vec{w}$, the two vectors are not parallel and their magnitudes are $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{5}$.
