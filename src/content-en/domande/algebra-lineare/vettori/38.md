For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ are the vectors of the plane $\vec{u} = (k, 3)$ and $\vec{v} = (4, k + 4)$ parallel?

- For $k = 2$ and for $k = -6$
- For $k = -2$ and for $k = 6$
- Only for $k = 2$
- For $k = 0$ and for $k = -4$

## Explanation

The two vectors are nonzero for every $k$. By the **parallelism criterion** in the plane they are parallel if and only if $k\,(k + 4) - 3 \cdot 4 = 0$, that is, $k^2 + 4k - 12 = 0$. The trinomial factors as $(k + 6)(k - 2)$, therefore the vectors are parallel for $k = 2$ and for $k = -6$. For $k = 2$ one has $\vec{v} = (4, 6) = 2\,\vec{u}$, and for $k = -6$ one has $\vec{v} = (4, -2) = -\dfrac{2}{3}\,\vec{u}$.
