What kind of triangle has vertices $A = (1, 1, 1)$, $B = (3, 2, 3)$, $C = (3, -1, 0)$?

- Right and isosceles
- Equilateral
- Right and scalene
- Obtuse and isosceles

## Explanation

One has $\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 1, 2)$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = (2, -2, -1)$ and $\overrightarrow{BC} = (0, -3, -3)$. Therefore the side lengths are
$$
AB = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3, \qquad AC = \sqrt{4 + 4 + 1} = 3, \qquad BC = \sqrt{9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{2} .
$$
Since $AB = AC$, the triangle is isosceles. Moreover $AB^2 + AC^2 = 18 = BC^2$, so by the converse of the **Pythagorean theorem** the triangle has a right angle at $A$, as confirmed by $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC} = 4 - 2 - 2 = 0$.
