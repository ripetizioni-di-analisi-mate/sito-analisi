The vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are nonzero, not parallel and have the same magnitude. Which statement is true for every choice of vectors with these properties?

- $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are orthogonal
- $\vec{u} + \vec{v}$ and $\vec{u} - \vec{v}$ are orthogonal
- $\|\vec{u} + \vec{v}\| = \|\vec{u} - \vec{v}\|$
- $\vec{u} + \vec{v}$ and $\vec{u} - \vec{v}$ are parallel

## Explanation

By the linearity and symmetry of the dot product the mixed terms cancel, therefore
$$
(\vec{u} + \vec{v}) \cdot (\vec{u} - \vec{v}) = \|\vec{u}\|^2 - \|\vec{v}\|^2 = 0 ,
$$
so $\vec{u} + \vec{v}$ and $\vec{u} - \vec{v}$ are **orthogonal**. Geometrically they are the diagonals of the rhombus with sides $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$. The other statements do not hold in general, as the pair $\vec{u} = (1, 0)$, $\vec{v} = \left( \dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$ shows. For this pair $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = \dfrac{3}{5} \neq 0$ and $\|\vec{u} + \vec{v}\|^2 = \dfrac{16}{5}$ is different from $\|\vec{u} - \vec{v}\|^2 = \dfrac{4}{5}$; moreover $\vec{u} + \vec{v} = \left( \dfrac{8}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$ is not a multiple of $\vec{u} - \vec{v} = \left( \dfrac{2}{5}, -\dfrac{4}{5} \right)$.
