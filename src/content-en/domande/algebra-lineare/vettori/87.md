Let $A = (0, 0, 0)$, $B = (1, 0, 0)$, $C = (0, 1, 0)$ and, for $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $D_t = (t, 2t, 3)$. How does the volume of the tetrahedron $ABCD_t$ vary as $t$ varies?

- It is constant and equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- It is constant and equal to $3$
- It increases as $|t|$ increases
- It is zero for $t = 0$

## Explanation

The volume of the tetrahedron is $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of the absolute value of the scalar triple product of $\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 0, 0)$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = (0, 1, 0)$ and $\overrightarrow{AD_t} = (t, 2t, 3)$. Expanding the determinant along the first row one obtains
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ t & 2t & 3 \end{vmatrix} = 3 ,
$$
so the volume is $\dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $t$. Geometrically, the points $D_t$ lie on a line contained in the plane $z = 3$, which is parallel to the plane $z = 0$ of the base $ABC$, therefore the height relative to the base is always $3$. The value $3$ is the volume of the parallelepiped built on the three edges.
