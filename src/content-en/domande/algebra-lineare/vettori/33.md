What is the cross product $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ of the vectors $\vec{u} = (1, 2, 3)$ and $\vec{v} = (0, 1, -1)$?

- $(-5, 1, 1)$
- $(-5, -1, 1)$
- $(5, -1, -1)$
- $(0, 2, -3)$

## Explanation

By the formula for the **cross product**
$$
\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \big( 2 \cdot (-1) - 3 \cdot 1,\ \ 3 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot (-1),\ \ 1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 0 \big) = (-5, 1, 1) .
$$
The vector $(5, -1, -1)$ is $\vec{v} \times \vec{u}$, which by anticommutativity is the opposite of $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$. The result is orthogonal to $\vec{u}$ and to $\vec{v}$, since $-5 + 2 + 3 = 0$ and $0 + 1 - 1 = 0$.
