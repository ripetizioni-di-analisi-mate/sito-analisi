Let $\vec{i}$, $\vec{j}$, $\vec{k}$ be the unit vectors of the axes of an orthogonal Cartesian coordinate system $Oxyz$. Which vectors $\vec{v}$ satisfy $\vec{v} \times \vec{k} = \vec{i}$?

- All and only the vectors $(0, 1, c)$ with $c \in \mathbb{R}$
- Only the vector $\vec{j} = (0, 1, 0)$
- All and only the vectors $(0, -1, c)$ with $c \in \mathbb{R}$
- All and only the vectors $(1, 0, c)$ with $c \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

Let $\vec{v} = (v_1, v_2, v_3)$. The formula in components with $\vec{k} = (0, 0, 1)$ gives
$$
\vec{v} \times \vec{k} = \left( v_2 \cdot 1 - v_3 \cdot 0,\ \ v_3 \cdot 0 - v_1 \cdot 1,\ \ v_1 \cdot 0 - v_2 \cdot 0 \right) = (v_2, -v_1, 0) .
$$
Therefore the equation $\vec{v} \times \vec{k} = (1, 0, 0)$ is equivalent to $v_2 = 1$ and $v_1 = 0$, while $v_3$ does not appear. Hence the solutions are all the vectors $(0, 1, c)$ with $c \in \mathbb{R}$. The component along $\vec{k}$ remains free because $\vec{k} \times \vec{k} = \vec{0}$, and the vector $\vec{j}$ is only the solution with $c = 0$. The vectors $(0, -1, c)$ instead solve $\vec{k} \times \vec{v} = \vec{i}$, with the factors swapped.
