What is the height of the tetrahedron with vertices $A = (1, 0, 0)$, $B = (0, 1, 0)$, $C = (0, 0, 1)$, $D = (1, 1, 1)$ relative to the face $ABC$, that is, the distance of $D$ from the plane of $A$, $B$, $C$?

- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{9}$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4\sqrt{3}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

With $\overrightarrow{AB} = (-1, 1, 0)$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = (-1, 0, 1)$, $\overrightarrow{AD} = (0, 1, 1)$ the scalar triple product equals
$$
\begin{vmatrix} -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -1\,(0 - 1) - 1\,(-1 - 0) + 0 = 2 ,
$$
so the volume of the tetrahedron is $V = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$. Since $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = (1, 1, 1)$, the area of the face is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The volume of a pyramid is one third of base area times height, therefore
$$
h = \frac{3V}{\mathcal{A}_{ABC}} = 1 \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{9}$ is obtained by dividing the volume by the area without the factor $3$.
