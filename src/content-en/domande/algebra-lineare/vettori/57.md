Which of the following vectors is *not* a linear combination of $\vec{u} = (1, 2, 0)$ and $\vec{v} = (0, 1, 1)$?

- $(1, 3, 1)$
- $(1, 2, 1)$
- $(2, 3, -1)$
- $(1, 1, -1)$

## Explanation

A linear combination of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ is
$$
a\,\vec{u} + b\,\vec{v} = (a,\ 2a + b,\ b) ,
$$
so the first component determines $a$ and the third determines $b$. Therefore a vector $(x, y, z)$ is a combination of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ if and only if $y = 2x + z$. For $(1, 2, 1)$ one has $2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3 \neq 2$, so this vector is **not** a linear combination of the two. The others satisfy the condition: $(1, 3, 1) = \vec{u} + \vec{v}$, $(2, 3, -1) = 2\vec{u} - \vec{v}$ and $(1, 1, -1) = \vec{u} - \vec{v}$.
