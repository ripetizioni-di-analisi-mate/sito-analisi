The vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ have magnitudes $\|\vec{u}\| = 2$ and $\|\vec{v}\| = 1$ and dot product $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 1$. What is $(\vec{u} + 2\vec{v}) \cdot (\vec{u} - \vec{v})$?

- $2$
- $3$
- $1$
- $5$

## Explanation

By the linearity and the symmetry of the dot product, the product expands like a product of binomials:
$$
(\vec{u} + 2\vec{v}) \cdot (\vec{u} - \vec{v}) = \vec{u} \cdot \vec{u} - \vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} + 2\,\vec{v} \cdot \vec{u} - 2\,\vec{v} \cdot \vec{v} = \|\vec{u}\|^2 + \vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} - 2\,\|\vec{v}\|^2 .
$$
Substituting the data gives $4 + 1 - 2 = 3$. The value $2$ is obtained by neglecting the two mixed terms.
