Which vector $\vec{x}$ of the plane satisfies both $\vec{x} \cdot (1, 2) = 4$ and $\vec{x} \cdot (3, -1) = 5$?

- $\left( \dfrac{6}{5}, \dfrac{7}{5} \right)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $(4, 5)$
- $(0, 2)$

## Explanation

Set $\vec{x} = (x_1, x_2)$. Writing the two dot products in components, the conditions become the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x_1 + 2x_2 = 4 \\[4pt]
3x_1 - x_2 = 5
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation $x_2 = 3x_1 - 5$, and substituting into the first gives $7x_1 - 10 = 4$, therefore $x_1 = 2$ and $x_2 = 1$. Hence $\vec{x} = (2, 1)$, and indeed $(2, 1) \cdot (1, 2) = 4$ and $(2, 1) \cdot (3, -1) = 5$. The vector $(0, 2)$ satisfies only the first condition, while $(4, 5)$ has as components the two given values of the dot products.
