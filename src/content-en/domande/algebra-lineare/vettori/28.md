Let $\vec{i}$, $\vec{j}$, $\vec{k}$ be the unit vectors of the axes of an orthogonal Cartesian coordinate system $Oxyz$. What is $(\vec{i} \times \vec{j}) \times \vec{j}$?

- $\vec{i}$
- $\vec{0}$
- $-\vec{i}$
- $\vec{k}$

## Explanation

For the products of the unit vectors along the axes, we have $\vec{i} \times \vec{j} = \vec{k}$, therefore $(\vec{i} \times \vec{j}) \times \vec{j} = \vec{k} \times \vec{j}$. Since $\vec{j} \times \vec{k} = \vec{i}$, by **anticommutativity** one has $\vec{k} \times \vec{j} = -\vec{i}$. Hence the result is $-\vec{i}$. The vector $\vec{0}$ is instead the value of $\vec{i} \times (\vec{j} \times \vec{j})$, since $\vec{j} \times \vec{j} = \vec{0}$, and the two results are different because the cross product is not associative.
