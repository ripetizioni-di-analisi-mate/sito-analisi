- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

The dot product in components is $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 0 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot (-2) + (-1) \cdot 1 = -3$. The magnitudes are $\|\vec{u}\| = \sqrt{2}$ and $\|\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 1} = \sqrt{6}$, therefore
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{-3}{\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{6}} = \frac{-3}{2\sqrt{3}} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} .
$$
Since $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ and the cosine is negative, the angle is obtuse, and the only angle in $[0, \pi]$ with this cosine is $\theta = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.
