The vectors $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ in space have scalar triple product $\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) = 3$. What is the volume of the parallelepiped whose edges are the vectors $\vec{u} + \vec{v}$, $\vec{v} + \vec{w}$, $\vec{w} + \vec{u}$?

- $3$
- $0$
- $6$
- $12$

## Explanation

By the linearity and anticommutativity of the cross product, and since $\vec{w} \times \vec{w} = \vec{0}$, one has
$$
(\vec{v} + \vec{w}) \times (\vec{w} + \vec{u}) = \vec{v} \times \vec{w} + \vec{v} \times \vec{u} + \vec{w} \times \vec{u} .
$$
Taking the dot product with $\vec{u} + \vec{v}$ one obtains six scalar triple products, and all those in which a vector appears twice are zero, because the cross product is orthogonal to each of its factors. What remains is $\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w})$ and $\vec{v} \cdot (\vec{w} \times \vec{u})$, which are equal because the scalar triple product does not change under cyclic permutations. Therefore the new scalar triple product equals $2 \cdot 3 = 6$ and the volume is $6$. The value $0$ is the volume one would obtain with the edges $\vec{u} - \vec{v}$, $\vec{v} - \vec{w}$, $\vec{w} - \vec{u}$, which have zero sum and are coplanar.
