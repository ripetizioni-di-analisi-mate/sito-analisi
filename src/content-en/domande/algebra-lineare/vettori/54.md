A vector $\vec{v}$ in the plane has magnitude $4$ and forms an angle of $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ with the unit vector $\vec{i}$ of the $x$-axis, measured counterclockwise starting from $\vec{i}$. What are the components of $\vec{v}$?

- $\left( -2, 2\sqrt{3} \right)$
- $\left( -2\sqrt{3}, 2 \right)$
- $\left( 2, 2\sqrt{3} \right)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$

## Explanation

The unit vector of $\vec{v}$ has magnitude $1$ and forms the angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ with the $x$-axis, so it has components $\left( \cos\dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3} \right) = \left( -\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$. The vector $\vec{v}$ is the product of its magnitude and its unit vector, therefore
$$
\vec{v} = 4 \left( -\frac{1}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) = \left( -2, 2\sqrt{3} \right) .
$$
The vector $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$ is only the unit vector, $\left( -2\sqrt{3}, 2 \right)$ swaps cosine and sine, and $\left( 2, 2\sqrt{3} \right)$ corresponds to the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
