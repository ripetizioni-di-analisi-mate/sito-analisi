The vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ of space are nonzero and not parallel, and $\vec{w} = \vec{u} \times (\vec{u} \times \vec{v})$. Which statement about $\vec{w}$ is true for every choice of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ with these properties?

- $\vec{w}$ is orthogonal to $\vec{u}$ and coplanar with $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$
- $\vec{w}$ is parallel to $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$
- $\vec{w}$ is the zero vector
- $\vec{w}$ is parallel to $\vec{u}$

## Explanation

Set $\vec{n} = \vec{u} \times \vec{v}$, which is different from $\vec{0}$ because $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are not parallel, and is orthogonal to the plane of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$. The vector $\vec{w} = \vec{u} \times \vec{n}$ is orthogonal both to $\vec{u}$ and to $\vec{n}$. Since $\vec{w}$ is orthogonal to $\vec{n}$, it lies in the plane of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$, therefore it is coplanar with the two vectors. Moreover $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{n}$ are orthogonal and nonzero, so $\|\vec{w}\| = \|\vec{u}\|\,\|\vec{n}\| \neq 0$. A nonzero vector orthogonal to $\vec{u}$ cannot be parallel to $\vec{u}$, and a nonzero vector orthogonal to $\vec{n}$ cannot be parallel to $\vec{n}$.
