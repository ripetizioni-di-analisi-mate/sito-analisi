At which point does the line through $A = (1, 2, 3)$ and $B = (3, 1, 1)$ meet the coordinate plane $z = 0$?

- $\left( -2, \dfrac{7}{2}, 0 \right)$
- $\left( 4, \dfrac{1}{2}, 0 \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{7}{3}, \dfrac{4}{3}, 0 \right)$
- $(3, 1, 0)$

## Explanation

The points of the line $AB$ are $P = A + t\,(B - A)$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$. Since $B - A = (2, -1, -2)$, one has
$$
P = (1 + 2t,\ 2 - t,\ 3 - 2t) .
$$
The point lies on the plane $z = 0$ if $3 - 2t = 0$, that is for $t = \dfrac{3}{2}$, therefore $P = \left( 4, \dfrac{1}{2}, 0 \right)$. The point $\left( -2, \dfrac{7}{2}, 0 \right)$ corresponds to $t = -\dfrac{3}{2}$, whose third coordinate equals $6$, while $(3, 1, 0)$ is the projection of $B$ onto the plane and does not lie on the line.
