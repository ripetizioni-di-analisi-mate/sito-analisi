Let $\vec{u} = (1, 1, 0)$ and $\vec{v} = (0, 1, 1)$. What is the volume of the parallelepiped whose edges are the vectors $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$?

- $0$
- $3$
- $\sqrt{3}$
- $9$

## Explanation

One has $\vec{n} = \vec{u} \times \vec{v} = (1, -1, 1)$. The volume is the absolute value of the scalar triple product, and by cyclic permutations
$$
\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{n}) = \vec{n} \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) = \vec{n} \cdot \vec{n} = \|\vec{n}\|^2 = 3 .
$$
Therefore the volume is $3$. The same result is obtained as base area times height. The base with sides $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ has area $\|\vec{n}\| = \sqrt{3}$, and the edge $\vec{n}$ is perpendicular to the base, so the height is $\|\vec{n}\| = \sqrt{3}$. The value $\sqrt{3}$ is the base area alone.
