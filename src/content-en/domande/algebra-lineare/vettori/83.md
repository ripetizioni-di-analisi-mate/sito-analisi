The points $A = (2, 1, 0)$, $B = (0, 3, -2)$, $C = (3, 0, 1)$ lie on the same line. Which statement about their position is true?

- $A$ lies between $B$ and $C$
- $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$
- $C$ lies between $A$ and $B$
- The three points coincide in pairs

## Explanation

One has $\overrightarrow{AB} = (-2, 2, -2)$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = (1, -1, 1)$, so $\overrightarrow{AC} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,\overrightarrow{AB}$. The coefficient is negative, so $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ have opposite orientations and the points $B$ and $C$ lie on opposite sides of $A$ on the line. It follows that $A$ **lies between** $B$ and $C$, with $AC$ half as long as $AB$. If the coefficient had been positive, $B$ and $C$ would have been on the same side of $A$.
