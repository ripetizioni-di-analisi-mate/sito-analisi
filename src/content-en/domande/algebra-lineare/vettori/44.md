The vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ in space have cross product $\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = (2, -1, 2)$. As the unit vector $\vec{w}$ varies, what is the maximum volume of the parallelepiped with edges $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$, and for how many unit vectors $\vec{w}$ is it attained?

- Volume $3$, for two unit vectors
- Volume $9$, for a single unit vector
- Volume $3$, for a single unit vector
- Volume $9$, for two unit vectors

## Explanation

The volume of the parallelepiped is the absolute value of the scalar triple product, that is, $|(\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) \cdot \vec{w}|$. Since $\|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\| = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3$ and $\|\vec{w}\| = 1$, we have $(\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) \cdot \vec{w} = 3 \cos\vartheta$, where $\vartheta$ is the angle between $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ and $\vec{w}$. Hence the volume equals $3\,|\cos\vartheta| \leq 3$, and the value $3$ is attained for $\vartheta = 0$ and for $\vartheta = \pi$. The corresponding unit vectors are $\vec{w} = \pm \dfrac{1}{3}\,(2, -1, 2)$, therefore the maximum volume is $3$ and it is attained for **two unit vectors**. The value $9$ is $\|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\|^2$.
