The diagonals of a parallelogram in space, taken with an orientation, are the vectors $\vec{p} = (3, 1, 0)$ and $\vec{q} = (1, -1, 2)$. What is the area of the parallelogram?

- $2\sqrt{14}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{2}$
- $\sqrt{14}$
- $28$

## Explanation

Let $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ be the sides issuing from one vertex, with $\vec{p} = \vec{u} + \vec{v}$ and $\vec{q} = \vec{u} - \vec{v}$. By the linearity and anticommutativity of the cross product, and since $\vec{u} \times \vec{u} = \vec{v} \times \vec{v} = \vec{0}$, one has
$$
\vec{p} \times \vec{q} = -\vec{u} \times \vec{v} + \vec{v} \times \vec{u} = -2\,\vec{u} \times \vec{v} .
$$
Therefore the area $\|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\|$ is half of $\|\vec{p} \times \vec{q}\|$. Since $\vec{p} \times \vec{q} = (2, -6, -4)$ has magnitude $\sqrt{56} = 2\sqrt{14}$, the area is $\sqrt{14}$. The value $2\sqrt{14}$ is the area of the parallelogram whose sides are the two diagonals, and $\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{2}$ is the area of one of the two triangles into which a diagonal divides the parallelogram.
