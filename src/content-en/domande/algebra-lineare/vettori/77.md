A force $\vec{F} = (0, 1, 3)$, in newtons, is applied at the point with position vector $\vec{r} = (1, 2, 0)$, in meters. What is the moment of the force about the origin, that is $\vec{M} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$?

- $(-6, 3, -1)$
- $(6, -3, 1)$
- $(6, 3, 1)$
- $(0, 2, 0)$

## Explanation

With the formula for the cross product in components one obtains
$$
\vec{r} \times \vec{F} = \big( 2 \cdot 3 - 0 \cdot 1,\ \ 0 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot 3,\ \ 1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 0 \big) = (6, -3, 1) ,
$$
therefore the moment is $(6, -3, 1)$, in newton meters. The vector $(-6, 3, -1)$ is $\vec{F} \times \vec{r}$, which by anticommutativity has opposite orientation. The vector $(6, 3, 1)$ comes from omitting the minus sign of the second component, and $(0, 2, 0)$ from multiplying the components one by one.
