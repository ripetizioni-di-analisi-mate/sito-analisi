The vectors $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ of space are nonzero and coplanar, and $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are not parallel. Which statement is true?

- $\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \vec{0}$
- $\vec{w}$ is a linear combination of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$
- $\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) \neq 0$
- $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ are pairwise orthogonal

## Explanation

Applied at the same point, $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are not parallel and therefore determine a unique plane, which also contains $\vec{w}$. Decomposing $\vec{w}$ along the directions of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ with the parallelogram rule, there exist $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}$ with $\vec{w} = \alpha\vec{u} + \beta\vec{v}$. Hence $\vec{w}$ is a **linear combination** of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$. By the **coplanarity criterion** the scalar triple product is zero, and by the **parallelism criterion** $\vec{u} \times \vec{v} \neq \vec{0}$. Finally, three nonzero pairwise orthogonal vectors do not lie in a plane, because the third one is parallel to the cross product of the first two.
