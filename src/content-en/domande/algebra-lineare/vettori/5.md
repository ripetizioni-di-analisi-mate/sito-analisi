## Explanation

One has $\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 0, 1)$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = (0, 1, 1)$ and $\overrightarrow{BC} = (-1, 1, 0)$, and the three vectors have magnitude $\sqrt{2}$. The angle $\alpha$ at $A$ is the angle between $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$, both leaving from $A$. Since $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC} = 1$, one has $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The three sides have the same length $\sqrt{2}$, so the triangle is **equilateral** and the other two angles also equal $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
