The vectors $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ in space have scalar triple product $\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) = 5$. What is $(2\vec{w}) \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{u})$?

- $10$
- $-5$
- $5$
- $-10$

## Explanation

By the linearity of the dot product one has $(2\vec{w}) \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{u}) = 2\,\big( \vec{w} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{u}) \big)$. The scalar triple product $\vec{w} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{u})$ is the determinant with rows $\vec{w}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{u}$, which is obtained from the determinant with rows $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ by swapping the first and the third row. Swapping two rows changes the sign of the determinant, therefore $\vec{w} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{u}) = -5$. Hence the required value is $2 \cdot (-5) = -10$.
