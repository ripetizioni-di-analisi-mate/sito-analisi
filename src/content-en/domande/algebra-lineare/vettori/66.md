Consider the vectors $\vec{u} = (k, 1, -2)$ and $\vec{v} = (k, k, 1)$, with $k \in \mathbb{R}$. For which $k$ is the angle between the two vectors acute?

- For $-2 < k < 1$
- For $k < -1$ or $k > 2$
- Only for $k > 1$
- For $k < -2$ or $k > 1$

## Explanation

The two vectors are nonzero for every $k$, because $\vec{u}$ has third component $-2$ and $\vec{v}$ has third component $1$. Moreover they are never parallel, since $\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = (2k + 1,\ -3k,\ k^2 - k)$ has its first two components equal to zero together for no value of $k$. Therefore the angle is acute if and only if the dot product is positive:
$$
\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = k^2 + k - 2 = (k + 2)(k - 1) > 0 .
$$
The inequality is satisfied for values outside the roots $-2$ and $1$, so the angle is acute for $k < -2$ or $k > 1$. For $-2 < k < 1$ the dot product is negative and the angle is obtuse.
