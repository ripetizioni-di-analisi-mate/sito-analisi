A vector $\vec{v} \neq \vec{0}$ in space forms an angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ with the unit vector $\vec{i}$ of the $x$-axis and an angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ with the unit vector $\vec{j}$ of the $y$-axis. Which values can the cosine of the angle $\gamma$ that $\vec{v}$ forms with the unit vector $\vec{k}$ of the $z$-axis take?

- Only $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\pm\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\pm\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Let $\vec{e} = \dfrac{\vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|}$ be the unit vector of $\vec{v}$. Since $\vec{i}$ has magnitude $1$, the cosine of the angle between $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{i}$ is $\vec{e} \cdot \vec{i}$, that is, the first component of $\vec{e}$; the same holds for the other two. Therefore
$$
\vec{e} = \left( \cos\frac{\pi}{3},\ \cos\frac{\pi}{4},\ \cos\gamma \right) = \left( \frac{1}{2},\ \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\ \cos\gamma \right) .
$$
The unit vector has magnitude $1$, so $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{2} + \cos^2\gamma = 1$ and $\cos^2\gamma = \dfrac{1}{4}$. It follows that $\cos\gamma = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the two signs correspond to two different vectors, with positive or negative third component.
