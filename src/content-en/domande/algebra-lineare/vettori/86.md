Consider the vectors $\vec{u} = (1, 0, 0)$, $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 0)$, $\vec{w} = (0, 1, -1)$. Which statement is true?

- The triple $(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w})$ is right-handed and the parallelepiped with edges $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ has volume $1$
- The triple $(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w})$ is left-handed and the parallelepiped with edges $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ has volume $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- The three vectors are coplanar
- The triple $(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w})$ is left-handed and the parallelepiped with edges $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$ has volume $1$

## Explanation

The scalar triple product is the determinant of the components, expanded along the first row:
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = 1 \cdot \big( 1 \cdot (-1) - 0 \cdot 1 \big) = -1 .
$$
The scalar triple product is not zero, so the vectors are not coplanar, and the volume of the parallelepiped is $|-1| = 1$. The triple $(\vec{i}, \vec{j}, \vec{k})$ is right-handed and has scalar triple product $\vec{i} \cdot (\vec{j} \times \vec{k}) = 1 > 0$, therefore a negative scalar triple product indicates a **left-handed** triple. The value $\dfrac{1}{6}$ is the volume of the tetrahedron built on the three vectors.
