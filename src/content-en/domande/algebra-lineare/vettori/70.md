The square $ABCD$ in the plane has its vertices, in this order, arranged counterclockwise, with $A = (1, 1)$ and $B = (4, 2)$. What are the coordinates of the vertex $D$?

- $(2, -2)$
- $(3, 5)$
- $(-1, 3)$
- $(0, 4)$

## Explanation

One has $\overrightarrow{AB} = (3, 1)$. The side $\overrightarrow{AD}$ has the same length as $\overrightarrow{AB}$, is orthogonal to it and, since the vertices follow one another counterclockwise, is obtained by rotating $\overrightarrow{AB}$ through a right angle counterclockwise. In the plane, the counterclockwise rotation through a right angle takes the vector $(a, b)$ to the vector $(-b, a)$, which has the same magnitude and zero dot product with $(a, b)$. Therefore $\overrightarrow{AD} = (-1, 3)$ and
$$
D = A + \overrightarrow{AD} = (1 - 1,\ 1 + 3) = (0, 4) .
$$
The point $(2, -2)$ is obtained by rotating clockwise, $(3, 5)$ is the vertex $C = B + \overrightarrow{AD}$, and $(-1, 3)$ is the vector $\overrightarrow{AD}$, which still has to be applied at $A$.
