A river has parallel banks and is $40\ \text{m}$ wide; the current has speed $3\ \text{m/s}$ parallel to the banks. A boat keeps its bow perpendicular to the banks and has speed $4\ \text{m/s}$ relative to the water. When it reaches the other bank, how many meters has it moved in the direction of the current with respect to its starting point?

- $24\ \text{m}$
- $30\ \text{m}$
- $50\ \text{m}$
- $\dfrac{160}{3}\ \text{m}$

## Explanation

Choose a coordinate system with the $x$-axis along the current and the $y$-axis perpendicular to the banks. The velocity of the boat relative to the bank is the sum of its velocity relative to the water and the velocity of the current, therefore it equals $(0, 4) + (3, 0) = (3, 4)$ in meters per second. The $y$-component is $4\ \text{m/s}$, so the boat crosses the $40\ \text{m}$ in $10\ \text{s}$. In the same time the $x$-component, which equals $3\ \text{m/s}$, moves it $3 \cdot 10 = 30\ \text{m}$ in the direction of the current. The value $50\ \text{m}$ is the length of the total displacement, that is, the magnitude of $(30, 40)$.
