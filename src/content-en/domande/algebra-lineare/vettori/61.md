Let $\vec{u} = (1, 2, 2)$ and $\vec{v} = (0, 0, 5)$. What is the unit vector of the bisector of the angle formed by $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$, that is, the unit vector that forms the same angle with the two vectors and lies in their plane?

- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{54}}\,(1, 2, 7)$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{30}}\,(1, 2, 5)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{5}{3} \right)$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,(1, 2, -1)$

## Explanation

The unit vectors $\dfrac{\vec{u}}{\|\vec{u}\|}$ and $\dfrac{\vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|}$ have the same length, so their sum is the diagonal of a rhombus and bisects the angle between them. Since $\|\vec{u}\| = 3$ and $\|\vec{v}\| = 5$, one has
$$
\frac{\vec{u}}{3} + \frac{\vec{v}}{5} = \left( \frac{1}{3}, \frac{2}{3}, \frac{2}{3} \right) + (0, 0, 1) = \frac{1}{3}\,(1, 2, 5) .
$$
The vector $(1, 2, 5)$ has magnitude $\sqrt{30}$, so the required unit vector is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{30}}\,(1, 2, 5)$. The vector $\vec{u} + \vec{v} = (1, 2, 7)$ does not point along the bisector, because $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ have different magnitudes, and $\left( \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{5}{3} \right)$ has the right direction but magnitude $\dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{3} \neq 1$.
