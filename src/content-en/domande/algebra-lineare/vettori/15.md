The nonzero vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ satisfy $\|\vec{u} + \vec{v}\| = \|\vec{u}\| + \|\vec{v}\|$. Which statement is true?

- $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ have the same direction and the same orientation
- $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are orthogonal
- $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ have the same direction and opposite orientations
- $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ have the same magnitude

## Explanation

Squaring both sides and expanding the left-hand side by the linearity of the dot product gives
$$
\|\vec{u}\|^2 + 2\,\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} + \|\vec{v}\|^2 = \|\vec{u}\|^2 + 2\,\|\vec{u}\|\,\|\vec{v}\| + \|\vec{v}\|^2 ,
$$
therefore $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = \|\vec{u}\|\,\|\vec{v}\|$. Since $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = \|\vec{u}\|\,\|\vec{v}\|\cos\theta$ and the magnitudes are positive, it follows that $\cos\theta = 1$, that is, $\theta = 0$. Hence $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ have the **same direction and the same orientation**. For orthogonal vectors one would instead have $\|\vec{u} + \vec{v}\|^2 = \|\vec{u}\|^2 + \|\vec{v}\|^2$, which is less than $\left(\|\vec{u}\| + \|\vec{v}\|\right)^2$.
