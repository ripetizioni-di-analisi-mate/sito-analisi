The vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are nonzero and not parallel. For which $h, k \in \mathbb{R}$ does $(k - 1)\,\vec{u} + (k + h)\,\vec{v} = 2\vec{u} - \vec{v}$ hold?

- $k = 3$, $h = -2$
- $k = 1$, $h = -2$
- $k = 3$, $h = -4$
- For infinitely many pairs $(h, k)$

## Explanation

The equality is equivalent to $(k - 3)\,\vec{u} + (k + h + 1)\,\vec{v} = \vec{0}$. If the coefficient of $\vec{u}$ were nonzero, $\vec{u}$ would be a multiple of $\vec{v}$, and the two vectors would be parallel; therefore $k - 3 = 0$. Then $(k + h + 1)\,\vec{v} = \vec{0}$ remains with $\vec{v} \neq \vec{0}$, so also $k + h + 1 = 0$. One obtains the system
$$
\begin{cases}
k - 1 = 2 \\[4pt]
k + h = -1
\end{cases}
$$
which has the unique solution $k = 3$, $h = -4$. The pair $k = 3$, $h = -2$ is obtained by setting $k + h$ equal to $1$, that is, by neglecting the sign of the coefficient of $\vec{v}$.
