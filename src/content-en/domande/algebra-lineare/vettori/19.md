In the triangle $ABC$ the point $P$ lies on the side $BC$ and its distance from $B$ is twice its distance from $C$. How is $\overrightarrow{AP}$ written as a linear combination of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$?

- $\overrightarrow{AP} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,\overrightarrow{AB} + \dfrac{1}{3}\,\overrightarrow{AC}$
- $\overrightarrow{AP} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\overrightarrow{AB} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,\overrightarrow{AC}$
- $\overrightarrow{AP} = \overrightarrow{AB} + \dfrac{2}{3}\,\overrightarrow{AC}$
- $\overrightarrow{AP} = \dfrac{1}{3}\,\overrightarrow{AB} + \dfrac{2}{3}\,\overrightarrow{AC}$

## Explanation

Since $P$ lies on the segment $BC$ and $BP$ is twice $PC$, the segment $BP$ is two thirds of $BC$, therefore $\overrightarrow{BP} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,\overrightarrow{BC}$. By the head-to-tail rule $\overrightarrow{AP} = \overrightarrow{AB} + \overrightarrow{BP}$, and $\overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{AC} - \overrightarrow{AB}$. It follows that
$$
\overrightarrow{AP} = \overrightarrow{AB} + \frac{2}{3}\left( \overrightarrow{AC} - \overrightarrow{AB} \right) = \frac{1}{3}\,\overrightarrow{AB} + \frac{2}{3}\,\overrightarrow{AC} .
$$
The larger coefficient belongs to $\overrightarrow{AC}$, because $P$ is closer to $C$ than to $B$.
