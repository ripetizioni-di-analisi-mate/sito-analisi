What is the cosine of the acute angle formed by two diagonals of a cube, that is, by two segments joining opposite vertices through the center?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Choose the cube of side $1$ with a vertex at the origin and the edges on the axes. The diagonal from the origin to $(1, 1, 1)$ has direction $\vec{d}_1 = (1, 1, 1)$, and the one from $(1, 0, 0)$ to $(0, 1, 1)$ has direction $\vec{d}_2 = (-1, 1, 1)$. Since $\vec{d}_1 \cdot \vec{d}_2 = -1 + 1 + 1 = 1$ and $\|\vec{d}_1\| = \|\vec{d}_2\| = \sqrt{3}$, one has
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
The cosine is positive, so $\theta$ is the acute angle. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ is the cosine of the angle between a diagonal and an edge, for instance between $(1, 1, 1)$ and $(1, 0, 0)$.
