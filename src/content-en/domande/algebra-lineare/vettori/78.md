Let $\vec{u} = (1, 2, 3)$, $\vec{v} = (4, 5, 6)$ and $\vec{w} = \vec{u} \times \vec{v}$. What is $\vec{w} \cdot (2\vec{u} - 3\vec{v})$?

- $132$
- $-366$
- $-132$
- $0$

## Explanation

The cross product $\vec{w} = \vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ is orthogonal to both $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$. By the linearity of the dot product one then has
$$
\vec{w} \cdot (2\vec{u} - 3\vec{v}) = 2\,\vec{w} \cdot \vec{u} - 3\,\vec{w} \cdot \vec{v} = 0 .
$$
The explicit computation confirms the result, because $\vec{w} = (-3, 6, -3)$ and $2\vec{u} - 3\vec{v} = (-10, -11, -12)$ give $30 - 66 + 36 = 0$. The value $132$ is obtained by omitting the minus sign in the second component of $\vec{w}$, that is, by using $(-3, -6, -3)$.
