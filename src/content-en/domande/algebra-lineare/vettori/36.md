The vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ of space satisfy both $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 0$ and $\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \vec{0}$. Which statement is true?

- $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are parallel and nonzero
- $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are orthogonal and nonzero
- $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ have the same magnitude
- At least one of the two vectors is zero

## Explanation

By **Lagrange's identity**
$$
\|\vec{u}\|^2\,\|\vec{v}\|^2 = \|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\|^2 + (\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v})^2 = 0 + 0 = 0 ,
$$
therefore $\|\vec{u}\| = 0$ or $\|\vec{v}\| = 0$. Hence at least one of the two vectors is zero. In terms of angles, for nonzero vectors the first condition gives $\cos\theta = 0$ and the second gives $\sin\theta = 0$, and no angle has both sine and cosine equal to zero. For $\vec{u} = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{v} = (1, 2, 2)$ both conditions hold and the magnitudes are different.
