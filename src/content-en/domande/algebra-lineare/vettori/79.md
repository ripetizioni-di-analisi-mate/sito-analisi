In the figure the vectors $\vec{u} = (2, 1, 0)$ and $\vec{v} = (-1, 3, 0)$ of the $xy$-plane are drawn applied at the origin. Which statement about $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ is true?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,2,1,u;0,0,-1,3,v" xmin=-1.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.6 uguale=1 titolo="The vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ in the $xy$-plane"}

- It has the direction of the $z$-axis and the same orientation as $\vec{k}$
- It has the direction of the $z$-axis and orientation opposite to $\vec{k}$
- It lies in the $xy$-plane
- It is the zero vector

## Explanation

The first two components of $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ contain a factor equal to the third component of $\vec{u}$ or of $\vec{v}$, which is zero, therefore
$$
\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \big( 0,\ 0,\ 2 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot (-1) \big) = (0, 0, 7) .
$$
The product is therefore $7\,\vec{k}$, which has the direction of the $z$-axis and the same orientation as $\vec{k}$. The result is consistent with the right-hand rule, because in the figure $\vec{v}$ is obtained by rotating $\vec{u}$ counterclockwise by an angle less than $\pi$, just as $\vec{j}$ is obtained from $\vec{i}$. The product is not zero, because $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are not parallel.
