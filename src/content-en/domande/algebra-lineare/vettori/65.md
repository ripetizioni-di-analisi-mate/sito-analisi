What is the acute angle formed by the lines $y = 2x$ and $y = -3x$ in the plane?

- $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$

## Explanation

The line $y = 2x$ contains the point $(1, 2)$, so it has the direction of $\vec{u} = (1, 2)$; in the same way $y = -3x$ has the direction of $\vec{v} = (1, -3)$. One has $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 1 - 6 = -5$, $\|\vec{u}\| = \sqrt{5}$ and $\|\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{10}$, so
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{-5}{\sqrt{5}\,\sqrt{10}} = -\frac{5}{5\sqrt{2}} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} ,
$$
and the angle between the two vectors is $\theta = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$. Two intersecting lines form two pairs of angles, $\theta$ and $\pi - \theta$, therefore the acute angle between the lines is $\pi - \dfrac{3\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The value $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ is the angle between the chosen direction vectors, which is obtuse.
