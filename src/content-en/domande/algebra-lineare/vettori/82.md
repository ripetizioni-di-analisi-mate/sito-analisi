The vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are nonzero and not parallel. For which $a \in \mathbb{R}$ are the vectors $2\vec{u} + 3\vec{v}$ and $a\,\vec{u} - 6\vec{v}$ parallel?

- $a = 4$
- $a = -9$
- $a = 9$
- $a = -4$

## Explanation

The vector $2\vec{u} + 3\vec{v}$ is not zero, so the two vectors are parallel if and only if there exists $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $a\,\vec{u} - 6\vec{v} = \lambda\,(2\vec{u} + 3\vec{v})$. Since $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are not parallel, the coefficients of a linear combination of them are unique, therefore they can be equated:
$$
\begin{cases}
a = 2\lambda \\[4pt]
-6 = 3\lambda
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation gives $\lambda = -2$, hence $a = -4$. With this value $-4\,\vec{u} - 6\vec{v} = -2\,(2\vec{u} + 3\vec{v})$. The value $a = 4$ is obtained by neglecting the sign of $-6$, that is, with $\lambda = 2$.
