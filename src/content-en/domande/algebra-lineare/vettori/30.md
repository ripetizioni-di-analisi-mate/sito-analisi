Given $\vec{u} = (1, 2, 3)$ and $\vec{v} = (1, 0, 1)$, for which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the vector $\vec{u} + k\,\vec{v}$ orthogonal to $\vec{v}$?

- $k = 2$
- $k = -4$
- $k = -2$
- $k = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

By the linearity of the dot product one has $(\vec{u} + k\,\vec{v}) \cdot \vec{v} = \vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} + k\,\|\vec{v}\|^2$. Since $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 1 + 0 + 3 = 4$ and $\|\vec{v}\|^2 = 2$, the orthogonality condition is $4 + 2k = 0$. Therefore $k = -2$. With this value $\vec{u} - 2\vec{v} = (-1, 2, 1)$ is the component of $\vec{u}$ orthogonal to $\vec{v}$.
