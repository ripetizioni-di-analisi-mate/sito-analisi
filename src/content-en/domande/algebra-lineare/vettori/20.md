For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does the vector $\vec{v} = (k, 2, 2k)$ have magnitude $3$?

- Only for $k = 1$
- For $k = \pm 1$
- For $k = \pm\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{3}}$
- For no value of $k$

## Explanation

The square of the magnitude is $\|\vec{v}\|^2 = k^2 + 4 + (2k)^2 = 5k^2 + 4$. Since the magnitude is nonnegative, the condition $\|\vec{v}\| = 3$ is equivalent to $\|\vec{v}\|^2 = 9$, that is, to $5k^2 + 4 = 9$. Therefore $k^2 = 1$, and the required values are $k = 1$ and $k = -1$. The value $\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{3}}$ is obtained by writing $2k^2$ in place of $(2k)^2$.
