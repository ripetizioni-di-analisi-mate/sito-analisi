The vector $\vec{v} = (1, -2, 3)$ is applied at the point $P = (2, 0, -1)$. What is the point $Q$ such that $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \vec{v}$?

- $Q = (1, 2, -4)$
- $Q = (-1, -2, 4)$
- $Q = (3, -2, -4)$
- $Q = (3, -2, 2)$

## Explanation

By the formula for the vector between two points, the components of $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ are the differences between the coordinates of $Q$ and those of $P$. The condition $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \vec{v}$ is therefore equivalent to saying that each coordinate of $Q$ is the sum of the coordinate of $P$ and the corresponding component of $\vec{v}$. Hence $Q = (2 + 1,\ 0 - 2,\ -1 + 3) = (3, -2, 2)$. The point $(1, 2, -4)$ is obtained by subtracting $\vec{v}$ from $P$, therefore it is the point $R$ such that $\overrightarrow{RP} = \vec{v}$.
