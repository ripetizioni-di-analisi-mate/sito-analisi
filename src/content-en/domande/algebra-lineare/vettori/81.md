What can be said about the quadrilateral with vertices, in order, $A = (0, 0, 0)$, $B = (4, 2, 0)$, $C = (3, 3, 1)$, $D = (1, 2, 1)$?

- It is a parallelogram
- It is a trapezoid, with only the sides $AB$ and $DC$ parallel
- It has the sides $AD$ and $BC$ parallel, while $AB$ and $DC$ are not parallel
- The four points are not coplanar

## Explanation

One has $\overrightarrow{AB} = (4, 2, 0)$ and $\overrightarrow{DC} = (2, 1, 0)$, so $\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\,\overrightarrow{DC}$ and the sides $AB$ and $DC$ are **parallel** but of different length. The other two sides have $\overrightarrow{AD} = (1, 2, 1)$ and $\overrightarrow{BC} = (-1, 1, 1)$, whose cross product $(1, -2, 3)$ is not zero, so by the **parallelism criterion** they are not parallel. Two parallel sides determine a plane that contains the four vertices, therefore the points are coplanar. So the quadrilateral is a trapezoid and not a parallelogram.
