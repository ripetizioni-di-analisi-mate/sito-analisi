Three forces applied at the same material point are in equilibrium, that is, their sum is zero. The first two are $\vec{F}_1 = (3, -2, 1)$ and $\vec{F}_2 = (-1, 4, 2)$, in newtons. What is the third force $\vec{F}_3$?

- $(2, 2, 3)$
- $(-4, 6, 1)$
- $(-2, -2, -3)$
- $(4, -6, -1)$

## Explanation

The equilibrium condition is $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 = \vec{0}$, therefore $\vec{F}_3 = -\left(\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2\right)$. Adding component by component one has $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 = (2, 2, 3)$, so
$$
\vec{F}_3 = (-2, -2, -3) .
$$
The vector $(2, 2, 3)$ is the resultant of the first two forces, that is, the opposite of the required force; $(-4, 6, 1)$ and $(4, -6, -1)$ are the differences $\vec{F}_2 - \vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_1 - \vec{F}_2$.
