In the plane let $\vec{u} = (2, 1)$ and $\vec{v} = (1, 3)$. What is the reflection of $\vec{v}$ across the line through the origin with the direction of $\vec{u}$?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,2,1,u;0,0,1,3,v" f="x/2" xmin=-1.6 xmax=4.2 ymin=-1.6 ymax=3.6 uguale=1 titolo="The vector $\vec{v}$ and the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ with the direction of $\vec{u}$"}

- $(3, -1)$
- $(-3, 1)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $(1, -3)$

## Explanation

Decompose $\vec{v}$ into a part along the line and an orthogonal part. Since $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{u} = 5$ and $\|\vec{u}\|^2 = 5$, the orthogonal projection is
$$
\operatorname{pr}_{\vec{u}}(\vec{v}) = \frac{5}{5}\,\vec{u} = (2, 1), \qquad \vec{v}_{\perp} = \vec{v} - (2, 1) = (-1, 2) .
$$
The reflection across the line leaves the part along the line unchanged and changes the sign of the orthogonal part, therefore the reflected vector is
$$
(2, 1) - (-1, 2) = (3, -1) .
$$
The vector $(-3, 1)$ is the opposite of the reflected vector, and $(1, -3)$ is the reflection across the $x$-axis.
