- $\pm\,(2, 1, 2)$
- $\pm\left( \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$
- $\pm\left( \dfrac{2}{3}, -\dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$
- $\pm\left( \dfrac{2}{9}, \dfrac{1}{9}, \dfrac{2}{9} \right)$

## Explanation

By the formula for the **cross product**
$$
\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \big( (-2) \cdot 0 - (-1)(-2),\ \ (-1) \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 0,\ \ 2 \cdot (-2) - (-2) \cdot 1 \big) = (-2, -1, -2) .
$$
The cross product is different from $\vec{0}$, therefore $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are not parallel, and the vectors orthogonal to both are the multiples of $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$. The magnitude of $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ is $\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3$, so the required unit vectors are $\pm \dfrac{1}{3}\,(-2, -1, -2)$, that is, $\pm\left( \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$.
