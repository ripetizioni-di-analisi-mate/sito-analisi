- Only for $k = 1$
- For $k = 1$ and for $k = -2$
- Only for $k = -2$

## Explanation

By the **coplanarity criterion** the three vectors are coplanar if and only if their scalar triple product is zero. Expanding along the first row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & k \\
1 & k & 1 \\
k & 1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
&= (k - 1) - (1 - k) + k\,(1 - k^2) \\[6pt]
&= (k - 1)(2 - k - k^2) = -(k - 1)^2 (k + 2) .
\end{aligned}
$$
The scalar triple product vanishes for $k = 1$ and for $k = -2$, therefore the vectors are **coplanar** for these two values.
