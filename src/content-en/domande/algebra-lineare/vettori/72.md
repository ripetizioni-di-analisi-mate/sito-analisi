The vectors $\vec{e}_1 = \left( \dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$ and $\vec{e}_2 = \left( -\dfrac{4}{5}, \dfrac{3}{5} \right)$ form an orthonormal basis of the plane. What are the coefficients $a$, $b$ such that $(1, 2) = a\,\vec{e}_1 + b\,\vec{e}_2$?

- $a = \dfrac{2}{5}$, $b = \dfrac{11}{5}$
- $a = 11$, $b = 2$
- $a = \dfrac{11}{5}$, $b = \dfrac{2}{5}$
- $a = \dfrac{11}{25}$, $b = \dfrac{2}{25}$

## Explanation

Take the dot product of the equality $(1, 2) = a\,\vec{e}_1 + b\,\vec{e}_2$ with $\vec{e}_1$. Since $\vec{e}_1 \cdot \vec{e}_1 = 1$ and $\vec{e}_2 \cdot \vec{e}_1 = 0$, one obtains $a = (1, 2) \cdot \vec{e}_1$, and in the same way $b = (1, 2) \cdot \vec{e}_2$. Therefore
$$
a = \frac{3}{5} + \frac{8}{5} = \frac{11}{5}, \qquad b = -\frac{4}{5} + \frac{6}{5} = \frac{2}{5} .
$$
The check $\dfrac{11}{5}\,\vec{e}_1 + \dfrac{2}{5}\,\vec{e}_2 = \left( \dfrac{33 - 8}{25}, \dfrac{44 + 6}{25} \right) = (1, 2)$ confirms the result. The values $11$ and $2$ are obtained by using $(3, 4)$ and $(-4, 3)$, which do not have magnitude $1$.
