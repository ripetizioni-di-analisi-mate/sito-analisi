For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ are the points $A = (1, 2, -1)$, $B = (3, 1, 1)$, $C = (7, k, 5)$ collinear?

- $k = 1$
- $k = -3$
- $k = 5$
- $k = -1$

## Explanation

The three points are collinear if and only if $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ are parallel. One has $\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, -1, 2)$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = (6, k - 2, 6)$. The first and the third component of $\overrightarrow{AC}$ are three times those of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, therefore the vectors are parallel only if $\overrightarrow{AC} = 3\,\overrightarrow{AB}$, that is, if $k - 2 = -3$. Hence the points are **collinear** for $k = -1$.
