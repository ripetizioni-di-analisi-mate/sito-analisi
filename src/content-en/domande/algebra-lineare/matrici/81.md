The permutation matrix
$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$
permutes the rows of every matrix it multiplies on the left. How is its inverse $P^{-1}$ written?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Row $i$ of $PA$ is the product of row $i$ of $P$ and $A$, so $P$ sends the rows $R_1, R_2, R_3$ of a matrix to the rows $R_3, R_1, R_2$. The inverse must bring every row back to its place, that is, send $R_1, R_2, R_3$ to $R_2, R_3, R_1$, and this is the matrix $P^T$. Indeed
$$
P P^T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = I_3,
$$
therefore, by the theorem on right and left inverses, $P^{-1} = P^T$. The matrix $P$ is not its own inverse, since $P^2 = P^T \neq I_3$.
