Let
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which of the following vectors $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ satisfies $Ax = 0$?

- $(1, -1, -1)$
- $(1, 1, 3)$
- $(1, -2, 0)$
- $(1, -1, 1)$

## Explanation

The product $Ax$ is the vector of $\mathbb{R}^2$ whose components are the products of the two rows of $A$ and $x$. For $x = (1, -1, -1)$ we get
$$
Ax = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2 + 1 \\ 2 - 1 - 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},
$$
so this vector satisfies $Ax = 0$. In other words, the first column of $A$ minus the second minus the third gives the zero vector. The vector $(1, 1, 3)$ makes only the product with the first row vanish, since $Ax = (0, 6)$, and $(1, -2, 0)$ only the one with the second row, since $Ax = (-3, 0)$.
