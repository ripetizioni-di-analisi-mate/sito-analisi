The matrix $A \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ is written as $A = P D P^{-1}$ with
$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad D = \operatorname{diag}(2, -1), \qquad P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which matrix is $A^3$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 8 & 9 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 8 & -9 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 8 & -27 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\operatorname{diag}(8, -1)$

## Explanation

By the associative property, in the product of three factors equal to $A$ the inner factors $P^{-1}P$ equal $I_2$:
$$
A^3 = P D (P^{-1} P) D (P^{-1} P) D P^{-1} = P D^3 P^{-1} .
$$
By the theorem on powers of diagonal matrices $D^3 = \operatorname{diag}(8, -1)$. Hence $P D^3 = \begin{pmatrix} 8 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ and
$$
A^3 = \begin{pmatrix} 8 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 & -9 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Swapping $P$ and $P^{-1}$ gives $\begin{pmatrix} 8 & 9 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$, and the matrix with $-27$ is obtained by cubing the individual entries of $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$.
