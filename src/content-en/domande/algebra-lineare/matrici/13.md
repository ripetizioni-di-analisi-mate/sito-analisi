The matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
satisfies $A^2 - 4A + 3I_2 = O$. Which matrix is $A^{-1}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

From the identity we get $4A - A^2 = 3I_2$, that is, $A(4I_2 - A) = 3I_2$. Since $A$ commutes with $I_2$, we also have $(4I_2 - A)A = 3I_2$, so $A$ is invertible and $A^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{3}(4I_2 - A)$. We have $4I_2 - A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, therefore
$$
A^{-1} = \frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The matrix $4I_2 - A$ without the factor $\dfrac{1}{3}$, multiplied by $A$, gives $3I_2$.
