The matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ -1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is multiplied on the left by the diagonal matrix $D = \operatorname{diag}(2, -1, 3)$. What does the product $DA$ give?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & -4 \\ -3 & 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 12 \\ -2 & -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 4 \\ -1 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 6 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & -4 \\ -2 & 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Row $i$ of $DA$ is the product of row $i$ of $D$ and $A$. Row $i$ of $D$ has $d_i$ in position $i$ as its only nonzero entry, so row $i$ of $DA$ is $d_i$ times row $i$ of $A$. Therefore the first row of $A$ is multiplied by $2$, the second by $-1$ and the third by $3$:
$$
DA = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & -4 \\ -3 & 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Multiplying by $D$ on the right multiplies the columns instead, and gives $AD = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 12 \\ -2 & -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
