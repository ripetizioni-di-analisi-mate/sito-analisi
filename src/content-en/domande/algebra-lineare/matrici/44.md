In a region with two cities, every year $10\%$ of the inhabitants of city $1$ move to city $2$ and $20\%$ of the inhabitants of city $2$ move to city $1$; the others stay. If the vector $x = (x_1, x_2)$ contains the inhabitants of the two cities, after one year they are $Px$, with
$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 0.9 & 0.2 \\ 0.1 & 0.8 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Today the inhabitants are $(200, 800)$. How many will there be two years from now?

- $(438, 562)$
- $(340, 660)$
- $(302, 596)$
- $(194, 514)$

## Explanation

After one year the inhabitants are $Px$, and after two years they are $P(Px) = P^2 x$. First we compute
$$
Px = \begin{pmatrix} 0.9 \cdot 200 + 0.2 \cdot 800 \\ 0.1 \cdot 200 + 0.8 \cdot 800 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 340 \\ 660 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Applying $P$ again gives
$$
P \begin{pmatrix} 340 \\ 660 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 306 + 132 \\ 34 + 528 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 438 \\ 562 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The vector $(340, 660)$ describes the situation after only one year. The vector $(302, 596)$ is obtained by using $P^T$ instead of $P$, and $(194, 514)$ by squaring every entry of $P$ instead of computing $P^2$; in both cases the total number of inhabitants does not stay $1000$.
