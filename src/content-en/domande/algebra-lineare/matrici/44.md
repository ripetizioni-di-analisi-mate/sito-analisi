Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
$$
which matrix $X$ satisfies $XA = B$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix $A$ is invertible with $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, since the product of the two matrices is $I_2$. Multiplying both sides of $XA = B$ on the right by $A^{-1}$ gives $X = BA^{-1}$, so
$$
X = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The matrix $A^{-1}B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ solves the equation $AX = B$; the two solutions are different because the product is not commutative.
