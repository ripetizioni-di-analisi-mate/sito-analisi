Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad C = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 5 & -1 \end{pmatrix},
$$
for which real numbers $h$, $k$ do we have $C = hA + kB$?

- $h = 3$, $k = 2$
- $h = 2$, $k = 3$
- $h = 2$, $k = -3$
- $h = 5$, $k = -1$

## Explanation

Performing the operations entry by entry gives
$$
hA + kB = \begin{pmatrix} h & k \\ h + k & h - k \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The equality with $C$ is equivalent to four equalities between numbers. Comparing the entries in positions $(1, 1)$ and $(1, 2)$ gives $h = 2$ and $k = 3$. With these values $h + k = 5$ and $h - k = -1$, so the other two entries also coincide with those of $C$, and $C = 2A + 3B$. The values $h = 5$, $k = -1$ are the entries of the second row of $C$ read as coefficients.
