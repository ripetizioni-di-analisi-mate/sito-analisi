For the $2 \times 3$ matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
there exist matrices $B \in M_{3,2}(\mathbb{R})$ with $AB = I_2$. Which of the following matrices is one of them?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Column $k$ of $AB$ is $A$ times column $k$ of $B$, that is, the combination of the columns $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 1)$ of $A$ with coefficients the entries of column $k$ of $B$. With $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ the columns of $AB$ are the first and the second column of $A$, so $AB = I_2$. In the product in the opposite order, instead,
$$
BA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \neq I_3 ,
$$
so for non-square matrices $BA = I$ does not follow from $AB = I$. With $B = A^T$ we get $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, and with the other two matrices the product is $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
