## Explanation

With $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - kR_1$, and then with $R_3 \to R_3 + R_2$, we obtain
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & k \\ 0 & k - 1 & 1 - k \\ 0 & 0 & (1 - k)(2 + k) \end{pmatrix} .
$$
For $k = 1$ the second and the third rows are zero, so there is only one pivot and $A_1$ is not invertible. For $k \neq 1$ there are three pivots if and only if $(1 - k)(2 + k) \neq 0$, that is, if and only if $k \neq -2$. By the **Gauss-Jordan theorem** $A_k$ is therefore invertible if and only if $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq -2$.
