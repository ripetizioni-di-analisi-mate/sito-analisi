The matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
can be written as $A = I_3 + N$, where $N$ has the entries just above the diagonal equal to $1$ and all other entries $0$. Which matrix is $A^4$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 16 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix $N^2$ has a single nonzero entry, equal to $1$ in position $(1, 3)$, and $N^3 = O$. Since $I_3$ commutes with $N$, the **binomial formula** applies, and the terms with $N^3$ and $N^4$ are zero; hence
$$
A^4 = I_3 + 4N + 6N^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The coefficient $6$ is the binomial coefficient $\binom{4}{2}$. The second matrix is obtained by neglecting the term $6N^2$, the third by using $4^2$ instead of $\binom{4}{2}$, and the last by raising the individual entries of $A$ to the fourth power.
