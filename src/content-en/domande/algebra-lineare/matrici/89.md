Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ with rows $R_1, R_2, R_3$. During the reduction of $\left( A \mid I_3 \right)$ using only elementary row operations, the row
$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -2 & 1 \end{array}\right)
$$
appears. Which statement is true for every matrix $A$ with this property?

- $A$ is not invertible and $C_1 - 2C_2 + C_3$ is the zero column, where $C_1, C_2, C_3$ are the columns of $A$
- $A$ is not invertible and $R_1 - 2R_2 + R_3$ is the zero row
- $A$ is not invertible and $R_3$ is the zero row
- $A$ is invertible and $(1 \ \ {-2} \ \ 1)$ is the third row of $A^{-1}$

## Explanation

Let $P$ be the product of the elementary matrices of the operations performed up to that point. By the theorem on elementary matrices the matrix obtained is $\left( PA \mid P \right)$, so each of its rows consists of the product of a row of $P$ and $A$, followed by that same row of $P$. The row considered says that $(1 \ \ {-2} \ \ 1)\, A$ is the zero row, that is, $R_1 - 2R_2 + R_3 = 0$. The matrix $PA$ has a zero row and is not invertible, therefore $A$ is not invertible either, since otherwise $PA$ would be a product of invertible matrices. With the rows $R_1 = (1, 0, 0)$, $R_2 = (1, 1, 0)$, $R_3 = (1, 2, 0)$ the relation holds, while $R_3$ is not zero and $C_1 - 2C_2 + C_3 = (1, -1, -3)$.
