The matrix
$$
A = \frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ -2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
satisfies $AA^T = I_3$. Which matrix is $A^{-1}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & -2 \\ 2 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ -2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & -2 \\ 2 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 \\ -2 & -2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrices $A$ and $A^T$ are square and $AA^T = I_3$. By the theorem on right and left inverses, therefore, $A$ is invertible and $A^{-1} = A^T$. The transpose is obtained by exchanging rows and columns, so
$$
A^{-1} = A^T = \frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & -2 \\ 2 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The matrix $A$ is not symmetric, hence $A^{-1}$ is different from $A$. The matrix without the factor $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is $3A^T$, and the matrix with all signs changed is $-A^T$.
