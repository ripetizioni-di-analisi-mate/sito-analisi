Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be a matrix with columns $A^1$, $A^2$, $A^3$. Which vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ satisfies $Ax = 2A^1 - A^3$ for every such matrix $A$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

For every $x = (x_1, x_2, x_3)$ the product $Ax$ is the linear combination $x_1 A^1 + x_2 A^2 + x_3 A^3$ of the columns of $A$. The equality $Ax = 2A^1 - A^3$ therefore holds for $x_1 = 2$, $x_2 = 0$, $x_3 = -1$. For $A = I_3$ the columns are the vectors of the standard basis and the equality becomes $x = (2, 0, -1)$, therefore no other vector satisfies it for every $A$.
