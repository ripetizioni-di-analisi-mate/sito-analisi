Let $A \in M_{3,2}(\mathbb{R})$ be a matrix such that $A^T A = O$. Which statement is true?

- $A$ can be nonzero, provided its second column is the opposite of the first
- $A$ has a zero row, but it can be different from $O$
- $A$ is the zero matrix
- $A A^T$ is different from $O$

## Explanation

The entry in position $(j, j)$ of $A^T A$ is the product of row $j$ of $A^T$, that is, column $j$ of $A$, and column $j$ of $A$. Hence
$$
(A^T A)_{jj} = a_{1j}^2 + a_{2j}^2 + a_{3j}^2 .
$$
Since $A^T A = O$, this sum of squares is zero for $j = 1, 2$, therefore every entry $a_{ij}$ is zero and $A$ is the **zero matrix**. Consequently $A A^T = O$ as well. With the column $c = (1, 2, 3)$ and the second column $-c$ the diagonal entries of $A^T A$ equal $14$, so opposite columns are not enough.
