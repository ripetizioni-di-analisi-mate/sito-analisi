To a matrix $A \in M_{2,n}(\mathbb{R})$ we first apply the operation $R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1$ and then, to the matrix obtained, the swap $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$. The result is a matrix $B$. Which sequence of elementary operations, applied to $B$, gives back $A$ for every such matrix?

- First $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$, then $R_2 \to R_2 + 3R_1$
- First $R_2 \to R_2 + 3R_1$, then $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$
- First $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$, then $R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1$
- First $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$, then $R_1 \to R_1 + 3R_2$

## Explanation

Let $a_1, a_2$ be the rows of $A$. The first operation gives the rows $a_1$ and $a_2 - 3a_1$, and the swap gives $B$ with rows $a_2 - 3a_1$ and $a_1$. To return to $A$ the operations are undone in reverse order, each with its own inverse operation. The swap $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$ brings back the rows $a_1$ and $a_2 - 3a_1$. Then $R_2 \to R_2 + 3R_1$ undoes $R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1$, so the second row becomes again $a_2 - 3a_1 + 3a_1 = a_2$. With the inverse operations in the original order the first row becomes $3a_2 - 8a_1$, and after the swap with $R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1$ the second row becomes $a_2 - 6a_1$.
