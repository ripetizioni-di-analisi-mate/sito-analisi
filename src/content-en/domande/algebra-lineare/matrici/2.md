- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 3 & 0 \\ 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 4 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

By the properties of the sum and of multiplication by a scalar, the equation $3X + 2A^T = B$ is equivalent to $3X = B - 2A^T$, so $X = \dfrac{1}{3}\left( B - 2A^T \right)$. The transpose of $A$ has as its columns the rows of $A$, and we obtain
$$
A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B - 2A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 3 & 0 \\ 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Dividing every entry by $3$ gives $X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. The matrix $X$ is $3 \times 2$, like $B$ and $A^T$, therefore a $2 \times 3$ matrix cannot solve the equation.
