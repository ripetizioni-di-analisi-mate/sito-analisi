Let $u = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $v = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. Which matrix is $u v^T$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 6 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 4 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 6 & 0 & 4 \\ -3 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\operatorname{diag}(3, 0, -2)$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 6 & 0 & 4 \\ 3 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix $u$ is $3 \times 1$ and $v^T$ is $1 \times 3$, so the product $u v^T$ is defined and is $3 \times 3$. The entry in position $(i, k)$ is the product of row $i$ of $u$, that is the number $u_i$, and column $k$ of $v^T$, that is the number $v_k$. Therefore row $i$ of $u v^T$ is $u_i$ times the row vector $(3 \ \ 0 \ \ 2)$:
$$
u v^T = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 6 & 0 & 4 \\ -3 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The product in the opposite order $u^T v = 3 + 0 - 2 = 1$ is instead a number, and the matrix $v u^T$ is the transpose of $u v^T$.
