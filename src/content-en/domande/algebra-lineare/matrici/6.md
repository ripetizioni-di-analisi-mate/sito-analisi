- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The first pivot is the entry $1$ in position $(1, 1)$. The operations $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$ give two rows equal to $(0 \ \ 0 \ \ 1 \ \ 2)$, so the second column is zero below the first row and the second pivot is the entry $1$ of the third column. The operation $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$ makes the third row zero, and we obtain
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
This matrix is already reduced, since the pivots equal $1$ and above the pivot of the third column there is $0$. The entry $2$ in position $(1, 2)$ remains, because the second column contains no pivot.
