Let
$$
N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which matrix is $N^2$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 4 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The first column and the third row of $N$ are zero, so the first column and the third row of $N^2$ are also zero. Of the remaining entries, $(N^2)_{13}$ is the product of the first row $(0 \ \ 2 \ \ 1)$ by the third column $(1, 3, 0)$ and equals $2 \cdot 3 = 6$, while $(N^2)_{12}$, $(N^2)_{22}$ and $(N^2)_{23}$ equal $0$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 4 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ contains the squares of the individual entries, which do not coincide with the row-by-column product. The matrix $N$ is nilpotent, and its first zero power is $N^3$.
