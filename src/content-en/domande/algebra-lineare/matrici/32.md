In $N=\begin{pmatrix}0&2&a\\0&0&3\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}$ only the entry $a$ at position $(1,3)$ changes. Why does the entry at $(1,3)$ of $N^2$ remain equal to $6$?

- Squaring a matrix removes every off-diagonal entry.
- The entry at $(1,3)$ of every power must equal $a$.
- A matrix with zero diagonal always has square zero.
- The only chain of two nonzero factors passes through index $2$.

## Explanation

In the row-by-column sum, intermediate indices one and three introduce a zero diagonal entry. Only $N_{12}N_{23}=2\cdot3=6$ survives. The direct entry $N_{13}$ on its own does not form a chain of two factors.
