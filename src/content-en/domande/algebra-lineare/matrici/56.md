The matrices $X, Y \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
X + Y = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 2 \\ 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix} \\[12pt]
X - Y = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 4 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
\end{cases}
$$
Which matrix is $Y$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ -4 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -2 \\ 2 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Let $A$ and $B$ be the matrices on the right-hand sides of the two equations. Subtracting the second equation from the first gives $2Y = A - B$, so
$$
Y = \frac{1}{2}(A - B) = \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ -4 & 6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Adding the two equations gives instead $X = \dfrac{1}{2}(A + B) = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$. The step is legitimate because addition and scalar multiplication have the same properties as the operations on numbers.
