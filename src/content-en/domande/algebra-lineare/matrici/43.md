Let
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which matrix is $A^6$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 6 & 6 \\ 6 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 64 & 64 \\ 64 & 64 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 32 & 32 \\ 32 & 32 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The row-by-column product gives $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = 2A$, which leads to the conjecture $A^k = 2^{k-1} A$ for every integer $k \geq 1$. The formula holds for $k = 1$; if it holds for $k$, then $A^{k+1} = A^k A = 2^{k-1} A^2 = 2^k A$, so by **induction** it holds for every $k \geq 1$. Therefore $A^6 = 2^5 A = \begin{pmatrix} 32 & 32 \\ 32 & 32 \end{pmatrix}$. The value $64 = 2^6$ is obtained with the exponent $k$ instead of $k - 1$. Raising the individual entries to the sixth power gives $A$ again, while the power of a matrix is the row-by-column product of six factors equal to $A$.
