Let
$$
P = \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad x = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which vector is $P^{100} x$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2^{99}} \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The row-by-column product gives $P^2 = \dfrac{1}{4} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = P$. If $P^n = P$, then $P^{n+1} = P^n P = P^2 = P$, so by **induction** $P^n = P$ for every $n \geq 1$. Therefore $P^{100} x = Px$, and both components of $Px$ are equal to the mean $\dfrac{2 + 8}{2} = 5$, that is, $P^{100} x = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$. The vector $\begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the vector with the factor $\dfrac{1}{2^{99}}$ by writing $P^{100} = \dfrac{1}{2^{100}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, that is, by raising only the numerical factor to the hundredth power.
