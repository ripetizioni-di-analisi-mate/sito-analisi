Which matrix is the inverse of $D = \operatorname{diag}\!\left( 2, -1, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$?

- $\operatorname{diag}\!\left( -2, 1, -\dfrac{1}{3} \right)$
- $\operatorname{diag}\!\left( -\dfrac{1}{2}, 1, -3 \right)$
- $\operatorname{diag}\!\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, -1, 3 \right)$
- $\operatorname{diag}\!\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 1, 3 \right)$

## Explanation

The diagonal entries of $D$ are all nonzero, so $D$ is invertible and its inverse is the diagonal matrix of the reciprocals. The reciprocal of $2$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, that of $-1$ is $-1$ and that of $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is $3$, therefore
$$
D^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}\!\left( \frac{1}{2}, -1, 3 \right) .
$$
Indeed the product of two diagonal matrices is the diagonal matrix of the products, and $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = (-1)(-1) = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 3 = 1$. The matrix $\operatorname{diag}\!\left( -2, 1, -\dfrac{1}{3} \right)$ is the opposite of $D$.
