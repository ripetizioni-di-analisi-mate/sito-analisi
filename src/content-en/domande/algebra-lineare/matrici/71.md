An invertible matrix $A \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ has inverse
$$
A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which matrix is $(A^T)^{-1}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ -1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

By the properties of the inverse matrix, if $A$ is invertible then $A^T$ is invertible too, and $(A^T)^{-1} = (A^{-1})^T$. It is therefore enough to transpose the given matrix:
$$
(A^T)^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The formula follows from $A^T (A^{-1})^T = (A^{-1} A)^T = I_2^T = I_2$. The matrix $A^{-1}$ itself is the inverse of $A$, and it coincides with the inverse of $A^T$ only if $A^{-1}$ is symmetric.
