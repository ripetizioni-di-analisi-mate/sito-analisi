Which of the following matrices is invertible?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ is upper triangular with nonzero diagonal entries, so it has two pivots and by the **Gauss-Jordan theorem** it is invertible. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has a zero column, and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has zero square, so it is a zero divisor; by the sufficient conditions for non-invertibility neither of the two is invertible. In $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ the first row is twice the second, therefore the operation $R_1 \to R_1 - 2R_2$ produces a zero row and only one pivot remains.
