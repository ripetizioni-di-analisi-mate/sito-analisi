Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
$$
which matrix $X \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ satisfies $AX + X = B$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 8 \\ 2 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Since $X = I_2 X$, by the distributive property $AX + X = (A + I_2)X$. The matrix $A + I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ is triangular with nonzero diagonal entries, so it is invertible, with
$$
(A + I_2)^{-1} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Multiplying on the left by this matrix gives
$$
X = (A + I_2)^{-1} B = \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ is $B(A + I_2)^{-1}$, that is, the product in the wrong order. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 8 \\ 2 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$ is obtained by adding $1$ to every entry of $A$ instead of adding $I_2$.
