Which of the following matrices is skew-symmetric?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 & -1 \\ 2 & 0 & 3 \\ -1 & 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 & 3 \\ 1 & -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 & -1 \\ -2 & 0 & 3 \\ 1 & -3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 & -1 \\ -2 & 0 & 3 \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

A matrix is skew-symmetric if $a_{ij} = -a_{ji}$ for all $i, j$. Setting $i = j$ gives $a_{ii} = -a_{ii}$, so the diagonal entries of a skew-symmetric matrix are zero, and the matrix with diagonal $(1, 1, 1)$ is ruled out. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 & -1 \\ -2 & 0 & 3 \\ 1 & -3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has zero diagonal and satisfies $a_{21} = -a_{12}$, $a_{31} = -a_{13}$, $a_{32} = -a_{23}$, therefore it is **skew-symmetric**. In the other two matrices with zero diagonal we have $a_{12} = a_{21} = 2$ or $a_{23} = a_{32} = 3$.
