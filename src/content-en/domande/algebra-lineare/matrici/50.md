For which real values of $a$, $b$, $c$ is the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a + 1 & c \\ 3 & 0 & 2b - 1 \\ 2 & b & 5 \end{pmatrix}
$$
symmetric?

- $a = 3$, $b = 1$, $c = 2$
- $a = 2$, $b = 1$, $c = 2$
- $a = 2$, $b = 1$, $c = 3$
- $a = 2$, $b = \dfrac{1}{3}$, $c = 2$

## Explanation

The matrix is symmetric if the entries in positions mirrored across the diagonal coincide, that is, if
$$
\begin{cases}
a_{12} = a_{21} \\[4pt]
a_{13} = a_{31} \\[4pt]
a_{23} = a_{32}
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
\begin{cases}
a + 1 = 3 \\[4pt]
c = 2 \\[4pt]
2b - 1 = b
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $a = 2$ and the third gives $b = 1$. Therefore $A$ is symmetric for $a = 2$, $b = 1$, $c = 2$. The value $b = \dfrac{1}{3}$ solves $2b - 1 = -b$, which is the skew-symmetry condition on that pair of entries.
