The matrix
$$
N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$
satisfies $N^3 = O$. Which matrix is the inverse of $I_3 - N$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & -5 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 8 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Since $N$ commutes with $I_3$, we have $(I_3 - N)(I_3 + N + N^2) = I_3 - N^3$. Since $N^3 = O$, the product equals $I_3$, so the inverse of $I_3 - N$ is $I_3 + N + N^2$. The only nonzero entry of $N^2$ is the one in position $(1, 3)$, which equals $1 \cdot 3 = 3$. Therefore
$$
(I_3 - N)^{-1} = I_3 + N + N^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The matrix $I_3 + N$ is obtained by neglecting $N^2$, and the entry $8$ is obtained by computing the entry of $N^2$ in position $(1, 3)$ as $2 \cdot 3$.
