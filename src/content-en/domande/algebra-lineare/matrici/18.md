Given the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
$$
which matrix $X$ satisfies $3X - A = X + 2B$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & \dfrac{1}{2} \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & \dfrac{1}{2} \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & \dfrac{1}{4} \\ -\dfrac{1}{2} & \dfrac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ -2 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

By the properties of the sum and of multiplication by a scalar, subtracting $X$ from both sides and adding $A$ to both sides gives $2X = A + 2B$. We have $A + 2B = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ -2 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$, so
$$
X = \frac{1}{2}(A + 2B) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & \frac{1}{2} \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & \dfrac{1}{2} \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}(A - 2B)$, which corresponds to a sign error on $2B$.
