An airline connects four cities $1, 2, 3, 4$. In the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$
the entry $a_{ij}$ equals $1$ if there is a direct flight from city $i$ to city $j$ and $0$ otherwise. How many itineraries with exactly one stopover go from city $1$ to city $4$?

- $3$
- $1$
- $0$
- $2$

## Explanation

An itinerary with one stopover from $1$ to $4$ goes through an intermediate city $k$ and uses the flights $1 \to k$ and $k \to 4$, so it contributes the product $a_{1k}\, a_{k4} = 1$. The number of such itineraries is therefore
$$
(A^2)_{14} = \sum_{k=1}^{4} a_{1k}\, a_{k4} = 0 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 0 = 2,
$$
that is, the product of the first row of $A$ and the fourth column. The two itineraries go through cities $2$ and $3$. The value $1$ counts the direct flight $a_{14}$, and $0$ is the entry $(A^2)_{41}$, which counts the itineraries in the opposite direction.
