Let
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
How many solutions $X \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ does the equation $AX = B$ have?

- No solution
- Exactly one solution
- Infinitely many solutions, depending on one parameter
- Infinitely many solutions, depending on two parameters

## Explanation

Column $k$ of $AX$ is $A$ times column $k$ of $X$, so the equation is equivalent to two systems, one for each column of $B$. The second column $(p, q)$ of $X$ must satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
p + 2q = 0 \\[4pt]
2p + 4q = 1
\end{cases}
$$
Multiplying the first equation by $2$ gives $2p + 4q = 0$, which contradicts the second, so this system has no solutions. Therefore the equation $AX = B$ **has no solutions**, even though the system for the first column has infinitely many. The matrix $A$ is not invertible, and for this reason the formula $X = A^{-1} B$ does not apply.
