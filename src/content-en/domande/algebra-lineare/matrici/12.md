- $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & a \end{pmatrix}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ -b & a \end{pmatrix}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ -b & -a \end{pmatrix}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & -a \end{pmatrix}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

We write $X = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ and compute the two products
$$
AX = \begin{pmatrix} c & d \\ -a & -b \end{pmatrix}, \qquad XA = \begin{pmatrix} -b & a \\ -d & c \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The equality $AX = XA$ is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases} c = -b \\ d = a \\ -a = -d \\ -b = c \end{cases}
$$
so $c = -b$ and $d = a$, with $a$ and $b$ arbitrary. The matrices that commute with $A$ are therefore exactly those of the form $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ -b & a \end{pmatrix}$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & a \end{pmatrix}$ satisfies the condition $c = -b$ only for $b = 0$.
