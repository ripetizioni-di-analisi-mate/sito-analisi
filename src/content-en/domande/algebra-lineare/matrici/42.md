Let
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which matrix is $A^{-1}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & -6 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ 6 & -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix $A$ is already in row echelon form with pivots equal to $1$, so in the Gauss-Jordan method it remains to make the entries above the diagonal equal to zero, proceeding from the last column. With $R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_3$ the second row of $\left( A \mid I_3 \right)$ becomes $(0 \ \ 1 \ \ 0 \mid 0 \ \ 1 \ \ {-3})$. With $R_1 \to R_1 - 2R_2$ the first row becomes $(1 \ \ 0 \ \ 0 \mid 1 \ \ {-2} \ \ 6)$, since $0 - 2 \cdot (-3) = 6$. Therefore $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$; the entry $6$ comes from the second operation applied to the already modified second row.
