The matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is invertible. What is the square of its inverse, $A^{-2} = (A^{-1})^2$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -4 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & \dfrac{1}{4} \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The product of $A$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is $I_2$, so by the theorem on right and left inverses $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Therefore
$$
A^{-2} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -4 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The same result is obtained by inverting $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, since $(A^2)^{-1} = (A^{-1})^2$. The inverse is not computed entry by entry, so the matrix with $\dfrac{1}{4}$ is not $A^{-2}$.
