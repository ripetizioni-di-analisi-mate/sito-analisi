The powers of the upper triangular matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
remain upper triangular. Which formula expresses $A^n$ for every integer $n \geq 1$?

- $A^n = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & n \\ 0 & 2^n \end{pmatrix}$
- $A^n = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2^n \\ 0 & 2^n \end{pmatrix}$
- $A^n = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2^n - 1 \\ 0 & 2^n \end{pmatrix}$
- $A^n = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2^{n-1} \\ 0 & 2^n \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The powers of an upper triangular matrix remain upper triangular, with the powers $1$ and $2^n$ on the diagonal. We find $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A^3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 7 \\ 0 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$, which suggests the conjecture $A^n = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2^n - 1 \\ 0 & 2^n \end{pmatrix}$. It holds for $n = 1$; if it holds for $n$, then
$$
A^{n+1} = A^n A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 + 2(2^n - 1) \\ 0 & 2^{n+1} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2^{n+1} - 1 \\ 0 & 2^{n+1} \end{pmatrix},
$$
so by induction it holds for every $n \geq 1$. For $n = 2$ the other formulas give $2$, $4$ and $2$ respectively as the entry in position $(1, 2)$, while the correct value is $3$.
