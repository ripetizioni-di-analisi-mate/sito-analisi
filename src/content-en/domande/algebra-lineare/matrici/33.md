With the Gauss-Jordan method the augmented matrix of a linear system in the unknowns $x, y, z$ is brought to the form
$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 0 & 2 & 3 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}\right) .
$$
How is the solution set written?

- $(x, y, z) = (3 + 2t,\ 1 - t,\ t)$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$
- Only $(x, y, z) = (3, 1, 0)$
- $(x, y, z) = (3 - 2t,\ 1 + t,\ t)$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$
- The system has no solutions

## Explanation

Elementary operations do not change the solutions, so we solve the reduced system $x + 2z = 3$, $y - z = 1$. The last row is zero in the last column too, therefore it corresponds to the equation $0 = 0$ and the system is not inconsistent. The column of $z$ contains no pivot, so $z$ is a free variable and we set $z = t$. From the two equations we get $x = 3 - 2t$ and $y = 1 + t$. Therefore the solutions are $(3 - 2t,\ 1 + t,\ t)$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$, infinitely many; $(3, 1, 0)$ is only the one with $t = 0$.
