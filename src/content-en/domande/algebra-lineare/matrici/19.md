Let $A \in M_{2,3}(\mathbb{R})$, $B \in M_{3,4}(\mathbb{R})$ and $C \in M_{4,2}(\mathbb{R})$. Which of the following products is defined and is a $3 \times 3$ matrix?

- $ABC$
- $CAB$
- $BCA$
- $BAC$

## Explanation

The product of two matrices is defined when the number of columns of the first factor equals the number of rows of the second, and the dimensions follow the pattern $(m \times n) \cdot (n \times p) = m \times p$. For $BCA$ we have $(3 \times 4) \cdot (4 \times 2) = 3 \times 2$ and then $(3 \times 2) \cdot (2 \times 3) = 3 \times 3$. The products $ABC$ and $CAB$ are also defined, but they are $2 \times 2$ and $4 \times 4$ respectively. The product $BA$ is not defined, because $B$ has four columns and $A$ has two rows, so $BAC$ is not defined.
