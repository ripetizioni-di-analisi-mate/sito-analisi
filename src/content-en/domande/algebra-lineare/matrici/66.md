Let $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be a matrix with $A^2 = A$, $A \neq O$ and $A \neq I_n$. Which statement is true?

- $A$ is invertible and $A^{-1} = A$
- $A$ is not invertible
- $A$ is nilpotent
- $A$ is invertible and $A^{-1} = I_n - A$

## Explanation

From the equality $A^2 = A$ we get $A^2 - A = O$, that is, $A(A - I_n) = O$. Since $A \neq I_n$, the matrix $A - I_n$ is different from $O$, so $A$ is a zero divisor. By the sufficient conditions for non-invertibility, therefore, $A$ **is not invertible**. If $A$ were invertible, multiplying $A^2 = A$ on the left by $A^{-1}$ would give $A = I_n$. The matrix is not nilpotent, since $A^k = A \neq O$ for every $k \geq 1$.
