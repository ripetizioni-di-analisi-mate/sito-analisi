In Gauss–Jordan elimination for an invertible matrix $A$ of order three, the first row operation adds a nonzero multiple of one row to another. Its elementary matrix $E$ is mistakenly applied only to the left block of $(A\mid I)$. Subsequent operations act on both blocks until $(I\mid B)$ is reached. Which product always recovers the correct inverse?

- $EB$
- $BE^{-1}$
- $E^{-1}B$
- $BE$

## Explanation

After the first step the blocks are $(EA\mid I)$. The remaining operations multiply on the left by $B$, so $BEA=I$. Thus $A^{-1}=BE$. The order is determined by the side on which row operations act.
