Consider, as the real parameter $k$ varies, the matrix
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 & k \\ -1 & -2 & k - 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
For which values of $k$ does a row echelon form of $A_k$ have exactly two pivots, and in which columns?

- For $k \neq 2$, in columns $1$ and $2$
- For every $k$, in columns $1$ and $3$
- For $k = 2$, in columns $1$ and $3$
- For $k \neq 2$, in columns $1$ and $3$

## Explanation

The operations $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 + R_1$ give
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & k - 2 \\ 0 & 0 & k - 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The second column is zero below the first row, so the second pivot is sought in the third column. If $k \neq 2$, the operation $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$ leads to the row echelon form with pivot $1$ in column $1$ and $k - 2$ in column $3$. If $k = 2$, the last two rows are zero and there is only one pivot. Therefore there are exactly two pivots for $k \neq 2$, in columns $1$ and $3$.
