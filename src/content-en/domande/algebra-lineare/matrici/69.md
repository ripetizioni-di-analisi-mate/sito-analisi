The two columns of the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$
are vectors of $\mathbb{R}^3$. What is obtained by computing the product $A^T A$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 6 & -1 \\ -1 & 10 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 2 & 5 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 & 10 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 6 & 5 \\ 5 & 10 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 10 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix $A^T$ is $2 \times 3$, so $A^T A$ is $2 \times 2$. The entry in position $(i, k)$ of $A^T A$ is the product of row $i$ of $A^T$, that is column $i$ of $A$, and column $k$ of $A$. The columns of $A$ are $(1, 2, -1)$ and $(0, 1, 3)$, therefore
$$
A^T A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + 4 + 1 & 0 + 2 - 3 \\ 0 + 2 - 3 & 0 + 1 + 9 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -1 \\ -1 & 10 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The $3 \times 3$ matrix is $A A^T$, and the result is symmetric like every matrix of the form $A^T A$.
