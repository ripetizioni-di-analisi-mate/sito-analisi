Let $A, B, C \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be such that $AB = AC$. Which of the following assumptions on $A$ guarantees that $B = C$?

- $A \neq O$
- $A$ has all entries nonzero
- $A$ is invertible
- $A$ is symmetric

## Explanation

If $A$ is invertible, multiplying both sides of $AB = AC$ on the left by $A^{-1}$ gives $B = C$. A single example rules out the other three assumptions. The matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is nonzero, symmetric and has all entries nonzero; with $C = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ we have $AC = A = AI_2$, and yet $C \neq I_2$.
