The matrices
$$
R = \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\dfrac{1}{2} \\[8pt] \dfrac{1}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad S = \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{1}{2} & -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[8pt] \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & \dfrac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}
$$
rotate every vector of the plane counterclockwise by $30^\circ$ and by $60^\circ$ respectively. Which matrix is the product $RS$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \\[8pt] \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The entry in position $(1, 1)$ of $RS$ is the product of the first row of $R$ and the first column of $S$, that is, $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} = 0$. In the same way the entry in position $(1, 2)$ is $-\dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{1}{4} = -1$, the one in position $(2, 1)$ is $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{3}{4} = 1$ and the one in position $(2, 2)$ is $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} = 0$. Therefore
$$
RS = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
which sends $(1, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$ and is the counterclockwise rotation by $90^\circ = 30^\circ + 60^\circ$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is the clockwise rotation by $90^\circ$, and the one with all entries equal to $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$ is obtained by multiplying the entries in the same position.
