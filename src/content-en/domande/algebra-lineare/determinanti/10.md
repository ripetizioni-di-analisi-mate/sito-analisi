- $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-2, -1, 1, 2\}$
- $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 1\}$
- $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1, 2\}$
- $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-4, -1, 1, 4\}$

## Explanation

By **Laplace's theorem**, the expansion along the first row gives
$$
\det A_k = k \begin{vmatrix} k & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & k & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & k \end{vmatrix} - 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & k & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & k \end{vmatrix} .
$$
Expanding again along the first row, the two determinants of order $3$ equal $k(k^2 - 4)$ and $k^2 - 4$. Hence $\det A_k = k^2(k^2 - 4) - (k^2 - 4) = (k^2 - 1)(k^2 - 4)$, which vanishes for $k = \pm 1$ and for $k = \pm 2$. Therefore, by the invertibility criterion, $A_k$ is invertible for $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-2, -1, 1, 2\}$.
