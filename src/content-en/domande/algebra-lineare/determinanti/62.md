For $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$, which factorization does the determinant
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ a & b & c \\ a^2 & b^2 & c^2 \end{vmatrix}
$$
have?

- $(a - b)(a - c)(b - c)$
- $(b - a)(c - a)(c - b)$
- $(b - a)(c - a)$
- $(b - a)(c - a)(b + c)$

## Explanation

With the column operations $C_2 \to C_2 - C_1$ and $C_3 \to C_3 - C_1$, which do not change the determinant, the first row becomes $(1, 0, 0)$:
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ a & b - a & c - a \\ a^2 & b^2 - a^2 & c^2 - a^2 \end{vmatrix}
= \begin{vmatrix} b - a & c - a \\ (b - a)(b + a) & (c - a)(c + a) \end{vmatrix} ,
$$
where the equality follows from expansion along the first row. One takes $b - a$ out of the first column and $c - a$ out of the second, and what remains is $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ b + a & c + a \end{vmatrix} = c - b$. Hence the determinant equals $(b - a)(c - a)(c - b)$. The product $(a - b)(a - c)(b - c)$ has three factors of opposite sign, so it is the opposite of the determinant.
