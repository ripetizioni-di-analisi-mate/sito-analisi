For $t \in \mathbb{R}$, what is the inverse of the matrix
$$
R_t = \begin{pmatrix} \cos t & -\sin t \\ \sin t & \cos t \end{pmatrix} ?
$$

- $\begin{pmatrix} -\cos t & \sin t \\ -\sin t & -\cos t \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} \sin t & \cos t \\ \cos t & -\sin t \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} \cos t & \sin t \\ -\sin t & \cos t \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{1}{\cos t} & -\dfrac{1}{\sin t} \\ \dfrac{1}{\sin t} & \dfrac{1}{\cos t} \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The determinant equals $\det R_t = \cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 1$, so $R_t$ is invertible for every $t$. By the **formula for the inverse** of order $2$ one swaps the entries of the main diagonal, changes the sign of the other two and divides by the determinant. One gets
$$
R_t^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos t & \sin t \\ -\sin t & \cos t \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
which is the matrix $R_{-t}$, that is, the rotation by the opposite angle.
