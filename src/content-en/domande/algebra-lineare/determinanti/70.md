Let $A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ with $A$ invertible and $\det B = 0$. Which statement is always true?

- $AB$ is invertible
- $AB$ and $BA$ are not invertible
- $A + B$ is invertible
- $BA$ is invertible and $AB$ is not

## Explanation

By **multiplicativity of the determinant**, $\det (AB) = \det A \cdot \det B = 0$ and $\det (BA) = \det B \cdot \det A = 0$. By the invertibility criterion, therefore, neither $AB$ nor $BA$ is invertible. The sum $A + B$ may or may not be invertible: with $A = I_2$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ one has $\det (A + B) = 2$, while with $B = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ one has $\det (A + B) = 0$.
