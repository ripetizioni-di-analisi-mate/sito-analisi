Which of the following matrices has determinant equal to the product of the entries on its main diagonal?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 5 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 3 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 7 & 2 & 0 \\ -4 & 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

All four matrices have diagonal entries $1, 2, 3$, whose product is $6$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 7 & 2 & 0 \\ -4 & 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ is lower triangular, so by the theorem on the **determinant of a triangular matrix** its determinant is $1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 6$. The others are not triangular and their determinant is different from $6$. Expanding along the second row, the first has determinant $2 \cdot (3 - 20) = -34$; expanding along the third row, the second has determinant $3 \cdot (2 - 6) = -12$; by Sarrus' rule the third has determinant $(6 + 1 + 1) - (2 + 1 + 3) = 2$.
