For $k \in \mathbb{R}$ let
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} k & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & k \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is the maximum value of $\det A_k$ as $k$ varies?

- $2$
- $1$
- $0$
- $4$

## Explanation

Expansion along the first column, which has the entry $a_{31} = 0$, gives
$$
\det A_k = k \begin{vmatrix} 2 & k \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} - 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = k(2 - k) - 0 = -k^2 + 2k .
$$
Completing the square one gets $\det A_k = 1 - (k - 1)^2$. Since $(k - 1)^2 \geq 0$, one has $\det A_k \leq 1$ for every $k$, with equality for $k = 1$. Hence the maximum value of the determinant is $1$. The value $0$ is obtained for $k = 0$ and for $k = 2$, which are the values for which $A_k$ is not invertible.
