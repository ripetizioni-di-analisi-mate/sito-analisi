Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$. What is $\det \left( A^2 - 4 I_2 \right)$?

- $0$
- $-12$
- $-96$
- $96$

## Explanation

Since $A$ and $I_2$ commute, $(A - 2I_2)(A + 2I_2) = A^2 + 2A - 2A - 4I_2 = A^2 - 4I_2$. By the **multiplicativity of the determinant**, therefore, $\det \left( A^2 - 4I_2 \right) = \det (A - 2I_2) \cdot \det (A + 2I_2)$. The two factors are
$$
\begin{vmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = -2 - 6 = -8,
\qquad
\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 3 & 6 \end{vmatrix} = 18 - 6 = 12 .
$$
Therefore $\det \left( A^2 - 4I_2 \right) = -8 \cdot 12 = -96$. The value $(\det A)^2 - \det (4I_2) = 4 - 16 = -12$ is wrong, because the determinant is not additive.
