Let $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be invertible, with all entries integers and with the inverse $A^{-1}$ also having all entries integers. What are the possible values of $\det A$?

- Only $1$
- All nonzero integers
- $-1$, $0$ and $1$
- $1$ and $-1$

## Explanation

The determinant is a sum of products of entries of the matrix, so a matrix with integer entries has an integer determinant. Therefore $\det A$ and $\det (A^{-1})$ are integers. By **multiplicativity of the determinant**, $\det A \cdot \det (A^{-1}) = \det I_n = 1$, so $\det A$ is an integer dividing $1$, that is, $\det A = 1$ or $\det A = -1$. Both values are possible: $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $1$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $-1$, and their inverses have integer entries.
