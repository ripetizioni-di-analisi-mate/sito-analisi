Which of the following polynomials divides the determinant
$$
\begin{vmatrix} a & b & c \\ c & a & b \\ b & c & a \end{vmatrix}
$$
as a polynomial in $a, b, c$?

- $a + b + c$
- $a - b$
- $a + b - c$
- $abc$

## Explanation

Adding the second and third columns to the first column does not change the determinant, and the first column becomes $(a + b + c,\ a + b + c,\ a + b + c)$. By the linearity of the determinant in the first column, the factor $a + b + c$ can be taken out, leaving the determinant of a matrix with first column $(1, 1, 1)$ and polynomial entries. Hence $a + b + c$ **divides the determinant**, which equals $(a + b + c)(a^2 + b^2 + c^2 - ab - bc - ca)$. A polynomial that divides the determinant vanishes only at points where the determinant also vanishes. For $(a, b, c) = (1, 1, 0)$ the determinant equals $2$, while $a - b$ and $abc$ are zero, and for $(a, b, c) = (1, 0, 1)$ the determinant equals $2$, while $a + b - c$ is zero; therefore none of these three polynomials divides the determinant.
