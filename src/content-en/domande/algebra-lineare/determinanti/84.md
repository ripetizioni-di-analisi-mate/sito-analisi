Let $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, $k \neq 1$, and let
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} k & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
For which $k$ does the inverse matrix $A_k^{-1}$ have all entries integers?

- Only for $k = 2$
- For every $k \neq 1$
- For $k = 0$ and $k = 2$
- For $k = -1$ and $k = 3$

## Explanation

The determinant is $\det A_k = k - 1 \neq 0$, so by the **formula for the inverse** of order $2$
$$
A_k^{-1} = \frac{1}{k - 1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & k \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The entry $\dfrac{1}{k - 1}$ is an integer only if the integer $k - 1$ divides $1$, that is, if $k - 1 = 1$ or $k - 1 = -1$. For $k = 2$ the inverse is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and for $k = 0$ it is $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, both with integer entries. Therefore the values are $k = 0$ and $k = 2$.
