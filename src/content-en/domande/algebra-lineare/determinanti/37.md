For the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
what is the entry in position $(2, 1)$ of the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$?

- $\dfrac{6}{13}$
- $-\dfrac{2}{13}$
- $-\dfrac{6}{13}$
- $6$

## Explanation

Expanding along the first row gives $\det A = 1 \cdot (1 - 0) - 2 \cdot (0 - 6) + 0 = 13$. By the **formula for the inverse**, the entry in position $(2, 1)$ of $A^{-1}$ is $\dfrac{C_{12}}{\det A}$, with the indices swapped. Deleting row $1$ and column $2$ leaves the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, with determinant $-6$, and the sign is $(-1)^{1+2} = -1$, so $C_{12} = 6$. Therefore $(A^{-1})_{21} = \dfrac{6}{13}$.
