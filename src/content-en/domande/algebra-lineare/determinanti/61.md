Which of the following matrices is equal to its own inverse?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & \dfrac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

If $A^2 = I_2$, that is, $A \cdot A = I_2$, then by the inverse from a single equality $A$ is invertible and $A^{-1} = A$. Conversely, if $A^{-1} = A$, then $A^2 = A A^{-1} = I_2$. Hence a matrix is equal to its own inverse if and only if its square is $I_2$. Row-by-column multiplication gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^2 = I_2 ,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^2 = -I_2 ,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
and the square of the diagonal matrix with entries $2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ has diagonal entries $4$ and $\dfrac{1}{4}$. Therefore only $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is equal to its own inverse.
