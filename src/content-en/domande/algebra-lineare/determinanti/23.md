Which of the following matrices has zero determinant?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

In the matrix with rows $(1, 2, 3)$, $(2, 1, 0)$, $(3, 3, 3)$ the third row is the sum of the first two. With the operation $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1 - R_2$ the third row becomes zero and the determinant does not change, so the determinant is **zero**. The other three matrices have determinants $-3$, $1$ and $-1$, as can be checked with Sarrus' rule. In the matrix with rows $(1, 2, 3)$ and $(2, 4, 5)$ the second row differs from twice the first only in the last entry, so the two rows are not proportional.
