The determinant of a matrix of order $4$, written out in terms of its entries, is an algebraic sum of products of $4$ entries. How many such products are there?

- $16$
- $24$
- $8$
- $12$

## Explanation

Let $p_n$ be the number of products in the determinant of order $n$. By **Laplace's theorem** along the first row, $\det A = a_{11} C_{11} + \dots + a_{1n} C_{1n}$, and each cofactor is a determinant of order $n - 1$ that contains no entry of the first row. So each term $a_{1j} C_{1j}$ gives $p_{n-1}$ products, and products coming from different terms contain different entries of the first row, therefore $p_n = n \, p_{n-1}$. Since $p_2 = 2$, one gets $p_3 = 6$, in agreement with the six products of Sarrus' rule, and $p_4 = 4 \cdot 6 = 24$.
