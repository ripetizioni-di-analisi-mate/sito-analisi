The line of the plane through the points $P = (1, 2)$ and $Q = (3, 5)$ has equation
$$
\begin{vmatrix} x & y & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 5 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0 .
$$
Which equation is obtained by expanding the determinant?

::grafico{punti="1,2,pieno,P;3,5,pieno,Q" xmin=-1 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1 ymax=6 uguale=1 titolo="The points $P = (1, 2)$ and $Q = (3, 5)$"}

- $3x + 2y - 7 = 0$
- $3x - 2y + 1 = 0$
- $3x - 2y - 1 = 0$
- $2x - 3y + 4 = 0$

## Explanation

One expands along the first row, with signs $+$, $-$, $+$:
$$
x \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 1 \end{vmatrix}
- y \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 \end{vmatrix}
+ \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 5 \end{vmatrix}
= -3x + 2y - 1 .
$$
The equation $-3x + 2y - 1 = 0$, multiplied by $-1$, becomes $3x - 2y + 1 = 0$. The check on the two points confirms the result, since $3 - 4 + 1 = 0$ and $9 - 10 + 1 = 0$. The method works because, for $(x, y) = P$ or $(x, y) = Q$, the matrix has two equal rows and hence zero determinant.
