Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ with $\det A = 4$, and let $C_{23} = 2$ be the cofactor of the entry $a_{23}$. Which equality is certainly true?

- $(A^{-1})_{23} = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $(A^{-1})_{32} = 2$
- $(A^{-1})_{32} = 8$
- $(A^{-1})_{32} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Since $\det A \neq 0$, by the **formula for the inverse** one has $A^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{\det A} (\operatorname{cof} A)^T$, so $(A^{-1})_{ij} = \dfrac{C_{ji}}{\det A}$, with the indices swapped. With $i = 3$ and $j = 2$ one gets $(A^{-1})_{32} = \dfrac{C_{23}}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The entry $(A^{-1})_{23}$ instead equals $\dfrac{C_{32}}{4}$ and depends on $C_{32}$, which is not given. For example $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 3 & 1 & 0 \\ -3 & -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has $\det A = 4$ and $C_{23} = 2$, but $(A^{-1})_{23} = -\dfrac{3}{4}$.
