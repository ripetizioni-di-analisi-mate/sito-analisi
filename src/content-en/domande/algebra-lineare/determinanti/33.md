What is the inverse of the matrix
$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ?
$$

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The rows of $P$ are $e_2^T$, $e_3^T$, $e_1^T$, so the columns of $P^T$ are $e_2$, $e_3$, $e_1$. The entry in position $(i, j)$ of $P P^T$ is the product of row $i$ of $P$ with row $j$ of $P$, which equals $1$ if $i = j$ and $0$ otherwise, because the rows are distinct vectors of the standard basis. Therefore $P P^T = I_3$, and by the inverse from a single equality $P^{-1} = P^T$:
$$
P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The other three matrices, multiplied by $P$, do not give $I_3$: for example $P \cdot P = P^T \neq I_3$.
