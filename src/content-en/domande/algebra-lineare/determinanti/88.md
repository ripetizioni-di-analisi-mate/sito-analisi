For which pairs $(a, b) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ is the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & a \end{pmatrix}$ not invertible?

- Only for $b = a$
- Only for $a = b = 0$
- For $b = a$ or $b = -a$
- For $a = 0$ or $b = 0$

## Explanation

The determinant equals $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$. By the invertibility criterion, the matrix is not invertible if and only if this product is zero, that is, if $a - b = 0$ or $a + b = 0$. Hence the matrix is not invertible for $b = a$ or $b = -a$. For example $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $4 - 4 = 0$.
