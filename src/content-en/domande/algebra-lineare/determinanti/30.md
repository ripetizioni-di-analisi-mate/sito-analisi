Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be a matrix with all entries integer and with $\det A = 2$. Which statement is certainly true?

- $A^{-1}$ has all entries integer
- $2A^{-1}$ has all entries integer
- No entry of $A^{-1}$ is an integer
- $\det \left( 2A^{-1} \right) = 1$

## Explanation

Since $\det A = 2 \neq 0$, by the **formula for the inverse** one has $A^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2} (\operatorname{cof} A)^T$, so $2A^{-1} = (\operatorname{cof} A)^T$. Each cofactor is, up to sign, a determinant of order $2$ of integer entries, so it is an integer. Therefore $2A^{-1}$ has all entries integer. By the determinant of the inverse, $\det \left( A^{-1} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$; since a matrix with integer entries has an integer determinant, $A^{-1}$ has at least one entry that is not an integer. Moreover $\det \left( 2A^{-1} \right) = 2^3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 4$. Finally, for $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ the inverse is $\begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, which has integer entries.
