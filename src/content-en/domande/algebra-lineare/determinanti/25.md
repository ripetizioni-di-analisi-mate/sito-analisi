Which statement is true for every pair of matrices $A, B \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$?

- $\det (AB) = \det (BA)$
- $\det (A + B) = \det A + \det B$
- $\det (2A) = 2 \det A$
- $\det A^T = -\det A$

## Explanation

By **Binet's theorem**, $\det (AB) = \det A \cdot \det B$ and $\det (BA) = \det B \cdot \det A$, and the two products of real numbers coincide. Hence $\det (AB) = \det (BA)$ even when $AB \neq BA$. For a matrix of order $3$, on the other hand, $\det (2A) = 2^3 \det A = 8 \det A$, and moreover $\det A^T = \det A$. With $A = \operatorname{diag}(1, 0, 0)$ and $B = \operatorname{diag}(0, 1, 1)$ one has $\det A + \det B = 0$, while $\det (A + B) = \det I_3 = 1$.
