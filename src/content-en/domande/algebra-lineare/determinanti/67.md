Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be such that $A^2 = A$ and $A \neq I_3$. Which statement is true?

- $\det A = 1$
- $\det A$ can be $0$ or $1$
- $A$ is not invertible
- $A$ is invertible and $A^{-1} = A$

## Explanation

Suppose that $A$ is invertible. Multiplying $A^2 = A$ on the left by $A^{-1}$ gives $A = I_3$, which contradicts the assumption $A \neq I_3$. Hence $A$ **is not invertible** and, by the invertibility criterion, $\det A = 0$. **Multiplicativity of the determinant** gives $(\det A)^2 = \det A$, that is, $\det A = 0$ or $\det A = 1$; the value $1$ is ruled out by the previous argument. An example is $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
