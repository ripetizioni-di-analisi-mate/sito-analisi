Let $A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ with $A$ invertible. Which equality always holds?

- $A^{-1} B A = B$
- $\det \left( A^{-1} B A \right) = \det B$
- $\det \left( A^{-1} B A \right) = \dfrac{\det B}{(\det A)^2}$
- $\det \left( A^{-1} + B \right) = \dfrac{1}{\det A} + \det B$

## Explanation

By **multiplicativity of the determinant** and since $\det (A^{-1}) = \dfrac{1}{\det A}$ one has
$$
\det \left( A^{-1} B A \right) = \frac{1}{\det A} \cdot \det B \cdot \det A = \det B .
$$
The equality between the matrices $A^{-1} B A$ and $B$, instead, does not hold in general: with $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ one gets $A^{-1} B A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \neq B$. The determinant is not additive, so the equality $\det \left( A^{-1} + B \right) = \dfrac{1}{\det A} + \det B$ is false, for example with $A = B = I_2$, where the left-hand side equals $4$ and the right-hand side $2$.
