For $\theta, \varphi \in \mathbb{R}$ let
$$
S = \begin{pmatrix} \cos 2\theta & \sin 2\theta \\ \sin 2\theta & -\cos 2\theta \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
R = \begin{pmatrix} \cos \varphi & -\sin \varphi \\ \sin \varphi & \cos \varphi \end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is $\det (SR)$?

- $1$
- $-1$
- $\cos (2\theta + \varphi)$
- $0$

## Explanation

The two determinants are computed separately. One has $\det S = -\cos^2 2\theta - \sin^2 2\theta = -1$ and $\det R = \cos^2 \varphi + \sin^2 \varphi = 1$, by the identity $\cos^2 \alpha + \sin^2 \alpha = 1$. By **multiplicativity of the determinant** $\det (SR) = \det S \cdot \det R = -1$, for every $\theta$ and every $\varphi$. The matrix $S$ represents a reflection across a line through the origin and $R$ a rotation.
