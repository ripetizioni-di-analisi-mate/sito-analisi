Let $A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ with $B$ different from the zero matrix and with $AB$ equal to the zero matrix $O$. Which statement is always true?

- $\det A = 0$
- $A$ is the zero matrix
- $B$ is not invertible
- $\det A = 0$ and $\det B = 0$

## Explanation

Suppose that $A$ is invertible. Multiplying $AB = O$ on the left by $A^{-1}$ gives $B = A^{-1} O = O$, which contradicts the hypothesis $B \neq O$. Hence $A$ is not invertible and, by the invertibility criterion, $\det A = 0$. The other statements do not always hold. With $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ one has $AB = O$ with $A$ nonzero; with $A = O$ and $B = I_n$ one has $AB = O$ with $B$ invertible.
