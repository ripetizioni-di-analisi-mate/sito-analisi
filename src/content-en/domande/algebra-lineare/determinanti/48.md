The volume of the parallelepiped with three consecutive edges on the vectors $u, v, w$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is the absolute value of the determinant of the matrix with rows $u, v, w$. What is the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices $O = (0, 0, 0)$, $P = (1, 0, 2)$, $Q = (2, 1, 0)$, $R = (0, 3, 1)$?

- $\dfrac{13}{6}$
- $\dfrac{13}{2}$
- $13$
- $\dfrac{13}{3}$

## Explanation

The tetrahedron has the edges leaving $O$ on the vectors $P$, $Q$, $R$, and its volume is $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of the volume of the parallelepiped built on the same vectors. By Sarrus' rule
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \end{vmatrix}
= (1 + 0 + 12) - (0 + 0 + 0) = 13 .
$$
The parallelepiped therefore has volume $13$. Hence the tetrahedron has volume $\dfrac{13}{6}$.
