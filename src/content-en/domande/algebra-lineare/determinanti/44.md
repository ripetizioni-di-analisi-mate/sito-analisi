Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be skew-symmetric, that is, such that $A^T = -A$. Which statement is true?

- $A$ is invertible
- $\det A = -1$
- $A$ is invertible if and only if it is different from the zero matrix
- $A$ is not invertible

## Explanation

By the properties of the determinant, $\det A^T = \det A$ and $\det (-A) = (-1)^3 \det A = -\det A$. The equality $A^T = -A$ therefore gives $\det A = -\det A$, that is, $\det A = 0$. Therefore, by the **invertibility criterion**, $A$ is not invertible. The matrix with $a_{12} = 1$, $a_{21} = -1$ and all other entries equal to zero is skew-symmetric, different from the zero matrix and not invertible.
