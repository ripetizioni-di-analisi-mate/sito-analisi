Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$. What is the solution $x \in \mathbb{R}^2$ of the system $Ax = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$?

- $x = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix}$
- $x = \begin{pmatrix} -7 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $x = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 11 \end{pmatrix}$
- $x = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Since $\det A = 6 - 5 = 1 \neq 0$, by the **invertibility criterion** the matrix $A$ is invertible. Multiplying both sides of $Ax = b$ on the left by $A^{-1}$ gives $x = A^{-1} b$, so the system has exactly one solution. By the **formula for the inverse**
$$
A^{-1} = \frac{1}{1} \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
therefore
$$
x = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 - 2 \\ -5 + 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
