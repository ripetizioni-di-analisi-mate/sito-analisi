Adding $a$, $b$, $c$ to the diagonal entries in positions $(2, 2)$, $(3, 3)$, $(4, 4)$ of the matrix of order $4$ with all entries equal to $1$ gives
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 + a & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 + b & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 + c \end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is $\det A$ for every $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$?

- $a + b + c$
- $(1 + a)(1 + b)(1 + c)$
- $1 + abc$
- $abc$

## Explanation

The operations $R_i \to R_i - R_1$ for $i = 2, 3, 4$ do not change the determinant and turn the last three rows into $(0, a, 0, 0)$, $(0, 0, b, 0)$ and $(0, 0, 0, c)$. The resulting matrix is upper triangular with diagonal $1, a, b, c$. By the theorem on the **determinant of a triangular matrix**, we therefore have $\det A = abc$. The expression $(1 + a)(1 + b)(1 + c)$ is the product of the diagonal entries of the original matrix, which is not triangular.
