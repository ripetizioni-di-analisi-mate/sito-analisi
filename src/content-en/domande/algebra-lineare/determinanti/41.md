For the matrix $A_k = \begin{pmatrix} k & 2 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, the operation $R_2 \to k R_2 - 3 R_1$ produces the matrix $A_k' = \begin{pmatrix} k & 2 \\ 0 & k - 6 \end{pmatrix}$. Which relation holds for every $k \in \mathbb{R}$?

- $\det A_k' = k \det A_k$
- $\det A_k' = \det A_k$
- $\det A_k' = -3 \det A_k$
- $\det A_k' = (k - 3) \det A_k$

## Explanation

The operation is carried out in two steps, first $R_2 \to k R_2$ and then $R_2 \to R_2 - 3 R_1$. The first step multiplies the determinant by $k$ and the second does not change it, so $\det A_k' = k \det A_k$. The direct computation confirms this, since $\det A_k = k - 6$ and $\det A_k' = k(k - 6)$. For $k = 0$ one has $\det A_0' = 0$ while $\det A_0 = -6$, so the root $k = 0$ of $\det A_k'$ is introduced by the operation and $A_0$ is invertible.
