Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be invertible with
$$
A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 5 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is the inverse of the transpose $A^T$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 5 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & -3 & -5 \\ 0 & -4 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & -3 & -4 \\ 0 & -5 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The transpose of a product is the product of the transposes in reverse order, so $A^T (A^{-1})^T = (A^{-1} A)^T = I_3^T = I_3$. By the inverse from a single equality, $(A^T)^{-1} = (A^{-1})^T$. Therefore the inverse of $A^T$ is obtained by swapping the rows and columns of $A^{-1}$:
$$
(A^T)^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
