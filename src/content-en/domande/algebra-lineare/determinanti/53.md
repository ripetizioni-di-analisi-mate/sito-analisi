For $n \geq 1$ let $D_n$ be the determinant of the matrix of order $n$ with $2$ on the main diagonal, $1$ in the positions $(i, i + 1)$ and $(i + 1, i)$ and $0$ elsewhere. For example
$$
D_3 = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} .
$$
Which relation holds for every $n \geq 3$?

- $D_n = 2 D_{n-1} - D_{n-2}$
- $D_n = 2 D_{n-1} + D_{n-2}$
- $D_n = 2 D_{n-1}$
- $D_n = 2 D_{n-1} - 2 D_{n-2}$

## Explanation

One expands along the first row, whose only nonzero entries are $a_{11} = 2$ and $a_{12} = 1$. The minor of $a_{11}$ is the determinant of the same kind of order $n - 1$, so the first term equals $2 D_{n-1}$. The minor of $a_{12}$ has first column $(1, 0, \dots, 0)$, and expanding it along this column leaves the determinant of the same kind of order $n - 2$, that is, $D_{n-2}$. Since $a_{12}$ has sign $(-1)^{1+2} = -1$, the second term equals $-D_{n-2}$. Therefore $D_n = 2 D_{n-1} - D_{n-2}$; with $D_1 = 2$ and $D_2 = 3$ one gets $D_3 = 4$.
