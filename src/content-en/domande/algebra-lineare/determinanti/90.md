For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the matrix
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ k & k + 1 & k + 2 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}
$$
invertible?

- For no value of $k$
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$
- For $k \neq 1$
- For $k \neq 0$

## Explanation

The operation $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$ turns the third row into $(1, 1, 1)$. After this operation, $R_2 \to R_2 - k R_3$ turns the second row into $(0, 1, 2)$. The new second row is the difference between the first row $(1, 2, 3)$ and the third row $(1, 1, 1)$, so the operation $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1 + R_3$ makes it zero. The three operations add multiples of other rows to a row, therefore they do not change the determinant, and $\det A_k = 0$ for every $k$. By the invertibility criterion, $A_k$ **is not invertible for any** value of $k$.
