For the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 5 & 4 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
what is $\det (A^{-1})$?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $-4$

## Explanation

The third column has the only nonzero entry $a_{33} = 4$, with sign $+1$, so $\det A = 4 \cdot (2 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 1) = 4$. Since $\det A \neq 0$, the matrix $A$ is invertible. Therefore, by the theorem on the **determinant of the inverse**, $\det (A^{-1}) = \dfrac{1}{\det A} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
