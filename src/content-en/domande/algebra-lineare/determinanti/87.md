Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ with $\det A = 0$. Which statement is always true?

- $A$ has a zero row
- $A$ has two proportional rows
- $A$ has a zero entry on the main diagonal
- A row of $A$ is a linear combination of the other two

## Explanation

By the property relating a zero determinant to dependent rows, $\det A = 0$ if and only if a row of $A$ is a linear combination of the others. Hence from $\det A = 0$ it follows that **a row of $A$ is a linear combination of the other two**. The other statements do not always hold. The matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
has the third row equal to the sum of the first two, so its determinant is zero, but it has no zero rows, no two proportional rows, and diagonal entries $1$, $1$, $2$.
