For the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
what is the entry in position $(1, 2)$ of the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $-3$
- $-6$
- $-\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

Expanding along the first row gives $\det A = 1 \cdot (6 - 1) - 1 \cdot (3 - 0) + 0 = 2$, so $A$ is invertible. By the **formula for the inverse**, the entry in position $(1, 2)$ of $A^{-1}$ is $\dfrac{C_{21}}{\det A}$, with the indices swapped. The cofactor $C_{21}$ is obtained by deleting row $2$ and column $1$, with sign $(-1)^{2+1} = -1$, so
$$
C_{21} = -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = -3 .
$$
Therefore $(A^{-1})_{12} = -\dfrac{3}{2}$.
