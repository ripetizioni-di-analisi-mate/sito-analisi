What are the real solutions of the equation
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ x & 1 & 0 \\ x^2 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0 ?
$$

- $x = -1$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$
- $x = 1$ and $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $x = -1$ and $x = 3$
- $x = 0$ and $x = -\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

Expansion along the first row, with signs $+$, $-$, $+$, gives
$$
1 \cdot (1 - 0) - 2 \cdot (x - 0) + 3 \cdot (0 - x^2) = 1 - 2x - 3x^2 .
$$
The equation $1 - 2x - 3x^2 = 0$ is equivalent to $3x^2 + 2x - 1 = 0$, that is, to $(3x - 1)(x + 1) = 0$. Hence the solutions are $x = -1$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$.
