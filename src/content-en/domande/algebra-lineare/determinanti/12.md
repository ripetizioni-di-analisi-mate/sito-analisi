- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 7 & 3 \\ 9 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ -3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Since $\det A = 3 - 2 = 1 \neq 0$, the matrix $A$ is invertible and $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Multiplying on the right by $A^{-1}$ both sides of $XA = B$ gives $X = B A^{-1}$; the factor $A^{-1}$ stays on the right, because matrix multiplication is not commutative. The computation gives
$$
X = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ -3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
