Let
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
What are $\det (AB)$ and $\det (BA)$?

- $\det (AB) = -2$ and $\det (BA) = -2$
- $\det (AB) = 0$ and $\det (BA) = 0$
- $\det (AB) = 2$ and $\det (BA) = 0$
- $\det (AB) = -2$ and $\det (BA) = 0$

## Explanation

The row-by-column product gives $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, so $\det (AB) = 2 - 4 = -2$. The matrix $BA$ has order $3$. For $x = (2, 1, -1)^T$ one has $Ax = 0$, so $BAx = 0$ with $x \neq 0$. If $BA$ were invertible, multiplying by $(BA)^{-1}$ would give $x = 0$; therefore $BA$ is not invertible and $\det (BA) = 0$. **Multiplicativity of the determinant** concerns square matrices of the same order and does not apply here, since $A$ and $B$ are not square.
