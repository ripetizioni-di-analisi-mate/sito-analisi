Let
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 3 & 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 5 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 7 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is $\det (AB)$?

- $12$
- $-12$
- $1$
- $36$

## Explanation

The matrix $A$ is lower triangular and $B$ is upper triangular, so the determinant of each is the product of the diagonal entries. One has $\det A = 2 \cdot (-1) \cdot 3 = -6$ and $\det B = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot (-1) = -2$. By **multiplicativity of the determinant** $\det (AB) = (-6) \cdot (-2) = 12$, without computing the product $AB$, which is not triangular.
