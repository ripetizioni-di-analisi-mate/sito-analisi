Let $n \geq 2$ and let $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be the matrix with $a_{i, i+1} = 1$ for $i = 1, \dots, n - 1$, with $a_{n1} = a$ and with all other entries equal to zero. For example, for $n = 3$,
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ a & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is $\det A$ for every $n \geq 2$?

- $a$
- $0$
- $(-1)^n a$
- $(-1)^{n+1} a$

## Explanation

The first column has only one nonzero entry, $a_{n1} = a$, so by **Laplace's theorem** the expansion along the first column gives $\det A = a \cdot (-1)^{n+1} \det A_{n1}$. Deleting the last row and the first column, the entries $a_{i, i+1} = 1$ end up on the main diagonal, therefore $A_{n1} = I_{n-1}$ and $\det A_{n1} = 1$. Hence $\det A = (-1)^{n+1} a$. For example, for $n = 2$ the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ a & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and the determinant equals $-a$.
