What is the inverse of the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} ?
$$

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & -6 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ 6 & -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix is triangular with diagonal entries equal to $1$, so $\det A = 1$. By the **formula for the inverse**, since $\det A = 1$, the entry in position $(i, j)$ of $A^{-1}$ is the cofactor $C_{ji}$. The cofactors on the diagonal equal $1$ and those above the diagonal, $C_{12}$, $C_{13}$, $C_{23}$, are zero, because each of their minors has a zero row or a zero column. For the others,
$$
C_{21} = -\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -2 ,
\qquad
C_{32} = -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = -3 ,
\qquad
C_{31} = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = 6 .
$$
Therefore $A^{-1}$ is upper triangular with first row $(1, -2, 6)$ and second row $(0, 1, -3)$.
