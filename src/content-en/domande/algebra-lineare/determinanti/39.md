For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the matrix
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & k & k \\ 1 & k & k^2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
invertible?

- $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}$
- $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$
- $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0, 1\}$
- $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 0, 1\}$

## Explanation

The operations $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$ do not change the determinant and make the first column zero below $a_{11} = 1$. The expansion along the first column therefore gives
$$
\det A_k = \begin{vmatrix} k - 1 & k - 1 \\ k - 1 & k^2 - 1 \end{vmatrix} .
$$
Since $k^2 - 1 = (k - 1)(k + 1)$, the factor $k - 1$ is taken out of each row, and one obtains $\det A_k = (k - 1)^2 \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & k + 1 \end{vmatrix} = k (k - 1)^2$. Therefore, by the **invertibility criterion**, $A_k$ is invertible for $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0, 1\}$.
