The points $A = (1, 1, 0)$, $B = (2, 0, 1)$, $C = (0, 1, 2)$ and $D = (3, 4, 1)$ are the vertices of a tetrahedron. What is the length of the altitude of the tetrahedron drawn from $D$ to the face $ABC$?

- $14$
- $\dfrac{7}{3}$
- $\sqrt{14}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{2}$

## Explanation

The altitude drawn from $D$ is the distance from $D$ to the plane of the face $ABC$. The vectors $\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, -1, 1)$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = (-1, 0, 2)$ are not parallel, so a normal vector of the plane is
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = \big( (-1) \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 0,\ 1 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 2,\ 1 \cdot 0 - (-1) \cdot (-1) \big) = (-2, -3, -1) ,
$$
which is parallel to $\vec{n} = (2, 3, 1)$. The plane passes through $A$, so it has equation $2(x - 1) + 3(y - 1) + z = 0$, that is, $2x + 3y + z - 5 = 0$. By the formula for the **distance from a point to a plane**, the altitude is
$$
\frac{|2 \cdot 3 + 3 \cdot 4 + 1 - 5|}{\sqrt{4 + 9 + 1}} = \frac{14}{\sqrt{14}} = \sqrt{14} .
$$
The value $14$ is the numerator without the division by $\|\vec{n}\| = \sqrt{14}$. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{2}$ is the area of the face $ABC$, and $\dfrac{7}{3}$ is the volume of the tetrahedron, equal to one third of the product of the base area and the altitude.
