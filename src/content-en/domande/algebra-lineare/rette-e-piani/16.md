Which statement about the points $A = (1, 0, 1)$, $B = (2, 1, 3)$, $C = (4, 3, 7)$ is true?

- They are collinear, so infinitely many planes contain them
- They are not collinear, so they determine a unique plane
- They are collinear, so no plane contains them
- They are collinear, so the plane containing them has normal vector $(1, 1, 2)$

## Explanation

The vectors $\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 1, 2)$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = (3, 3, 6)$ satisfy $\overrightarrow{AC} = 3\,\overrightarrow{AB}$, so they are parallel and the three points lie on the same line. Consequently the cross product $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}$ is the zero vector and does not give a normal vector. Three collinear points belong to all the planes of the pencil whose axis is the line containing them, therefore there are **infinitely many planes** through $A$, $B$, $C$. The vector $(1, 1, 2)$ is a direction vector of this line, and a plane with normal vector $(1, 1, 2)$ meets it at a single point.
