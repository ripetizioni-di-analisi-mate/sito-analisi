Which points of the line $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = t$, $z = 1 - t$ are at distance $1$ from the plane $\pi : 2x - y + 2z = 1$?

- $(1, 0, 1)$ and $(7, 6, -5)$
- $(3, 2, -1)$ and $(5, 4, -3)$
- $(-5, -6, 7)$ and $(13, 12, -11)$
- $(1, 0, 1)$ and $(-5, -6, 7)$

## Explanation

The point of $r$ with parameter $t$ has distance from $\pi$
$$
d = \frac{|2(1 + t) - t + 2(1 - t) - 1|}{\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4}} = \frac{|3 - t|}{3} .
$$
The condition $d = 1$ is equivalent to $|3 - t| = 3$, so $t = 0$ or $t = 6$. The required points are therefore $(1, 0, 1)$ and $(7, 6, -5)$. The pair $(3, 2, -1)$, $(5, 4, -3)$ is obtained by forgetting the denominator, that is, by imposing $|3 - t| = 1$. The pair $(-5, -6, 7)$, $(13, 12, -11)$ is obtained by dividing by $\|\vec{n}\|^2 = 9$ instead of by $\|\vec{n}\| = 3$, and the point $(-5, -6, 7)$ is at distance $3$ from the plane.
