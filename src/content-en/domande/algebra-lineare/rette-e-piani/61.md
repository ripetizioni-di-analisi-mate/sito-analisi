The lines $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2t$, $z = -t$ and $s : x = 2 + u$, $y = 1 + 2u$, $z = 1 - u$ are parallel and distinct. Which plane contains them?

- $x + 2y - z - 1 = 0$
- $x + y + z - 1 = 0$
- $3x + 2y - z - 3 = 0$
- $3x - 2y - z - 3 = 0$

## Explanation

The two lines have the same direction vector $\vec{v} = (1, 2, -1)$, so the cross product of the direction vectors is zero and does not determine the plane. The plane containing $r$ and $s$ also contains the segment joining the point $A = (1, 0, 0)$ of $r$ to the point $B = (2, 1, 1)$ of $s$, therefore it is parallel to $\vec{v}$ and to $\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 1, 1)$. Since these two vectors are not parallel, the plane has normal vector
$$
\vec{n} = \vec{v} \times \overrightarrow{AB} = (3, -2, -1) ,
$$
and, passing through $A$, it has equation $3(x - 1) - 2y - z = 0$, that is, $3x - 2y - z - 3 = 0$. The plane $x + 2y - z - 1 = 0$ has normal vector $\vec{v}$, so it is perpendicular to the lines, and the plane $x + y + z - 1 = 0$ has normal vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$. The plane $3x + 2y - z - 3 = 0$ passes through $A$ but not through $B$, since $6 + 2 - 1 - 3 = 4 \neq 0$.
