Let $r : x = 1$, $y = t$, $z = 0$ and $s : x = u$, $y = 1$, $z = 1$. At which point does the line through $P = (0, 0, 2)$ that meets both $r$ and $s$ meet $r$?

- $(1, 0, 0)$
- $(1, 1, 0)$
- $(1, -2, 0)$
- $(1, 2, 0)$

## Explanation

The required line meets $r$ and passes through $P$, so it lies in the plane $\alpha$ containing $r$ and $P$; in the same way it lies in the plane $\beta$ containing $s$ and $P$. The plane $\alpha$ is parallel to $(0, 1, 0)$ and to the vector $(1, 0, -2)$ from $P$ to the point $(1, 0, 0)$ of $r$, so it has normal vector $(2, 0, 1)$ and equation $2x + z = 2$. The plane $\beta$ is parallel to $(1, 0, 0)$ and to the vector $(0, 1, -1)$ from $P$ to the point $(0, 1, 1)$ of $s$, so it has normal vector $(0, 1, 1)$ and equation $y + z = 2$. The required line is therefore the intersection of the planes $2x + z = 2$ and $y + z = 2$. It meets $r$ where $z = 0$, that is, at the point $(1, 2, 0)$, and it meets $s$ at the point $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 1, 1 \right)$.
