The lines $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2t$, $z = 1 + 2t$ and $s : x = 1 + 4u$, $y = 0$, $z = 1 + 3u$ meet at the point $(1, 0, 1)$. Which vector is a direction vector of one of the two bisectors of the angles formed by $r$ and $s$?

- $(5, 2, 5)$
- $(7, 10, 1)$
- $(17, 10, 19)$
- $(-3, 2, -1)$

## Explanation

The direction vectors $\vec{v} = (1, 2, 2)$ and $\vec{w} = (4, 0, 3)$ have norms $\|\vec{v}\| = 3$ and $\|\vec{w}\| = 5$. Let $\hat{v} = \dfrac{\vec{v}}{3}$ and $\hat{w} = \dfrac{\vec{w}}{5}$ be the corresponding unit vectors. Since $\hat{v} \cdot (\hat{v} + \hat{w}) = 1 + \hat{v} \cdot \hat{w} = \hat{w} \cdot (\hat{v} + \hat{w})$, the vector $\hat{v} + \hat{w}$ forms equal angles with $\vec{v}$ and with $\vec{w}$, and it lies in the plane of the two lines, so it is a direction vector of a bisector. We have
$$
\hat{v} + \hat{w} = \left( \frac{1}{3} + \frac{4}{5},\ \frac{2}{3},\ \frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{5} \right) = \frac{1}{15}\,(17, 10, 19) .
$$
The vector $(5, 2, 5) = \vec{v} + \vec{w}$ does not form equal angles with the two lines, because $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{w}$ have different norms. The other bisector has direction vector $\hat{v} - \hat{w}$, parallel to $(-7, 10, 1)$, while $(7, 10, 1)$ and $(-3, 2, -1) = \vec{v} - \vec{w}$ do not form equal angles with $r$ and $s$.
