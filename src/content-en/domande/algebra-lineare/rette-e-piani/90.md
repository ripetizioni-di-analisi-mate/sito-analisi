Let $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2t$, $z = 3$ and $s_k : x = u$, $y = 1 + ku$, $z = 3 + u$. For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ are the lines perpendicular, and what is their relative position for that value?

- $k = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the lines intersect
- $k = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the lines are skew
- $k = 2$, and the lines are skew
- $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and the lines intersect

## Explanation

The direction vectors are $\vec{v} = (1, 2, 0)$ and $\vec{w} = (1, k, 1)$, so the lines are perpendicular if and only if $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{w} = 1 + 2k = 0$, that is, $k = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. For this value we take $A = (1, 0, 3)$ and $B = (0, 1, 3)$, with $\overrightarrow{AB} = (-1, 1, 0)$, and we have $\vec{v} \times \vec{w} = \left( 2, -1, -\dfrac{5}{2} \right)$. Then
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) = -2 - 1 + 0 = -3 \neq 0 ,
$$
so by the **coplanarity criterion** the lines are **skew**. The perpendicularity of two lines depends only on the direction vectors, therefore it also holds for lines that do not meet.
