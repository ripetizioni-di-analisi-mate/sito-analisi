The lines $r : x = t$, $y = t$, $z = 0$ and $s : x = 1$, $y = u$, $z = 1 + u$ are skew. Which of the following planes contains $r$ and the common perpendicular of $r$ and $s$?

- $x - y + z = 0$
- $2x + y - z - 1 = 0$
- $x - y - 2z + 1 = 0$
- $x - y - 2z = 0$

## Explanation

The common perpendicular has direction vector $\vec{n} = \vec{v} \times \vec{w} = (1, 1, 0) \times (0, 1, 1) = (1, -1, 1)$. The plane containing $r$ and the common perpendicular passes through the point $A = (0, 0, 0)$ of $r$ and is parallel to $\vec{v}$ and to $\vec{n}$, so it has normal vector
$$
\vec{v} \times \vec{n} = (1, 1, 0) \times (1, -1, 1) = (1, -1, -2)
$$
and equation $x - y - 2z = 0$. Indeed the feet of the common perpendicular, $H = \left( \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{1}{3}, 0 \right)$ and $K = \left( 1, -\dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$, both satisfy this equation. The plane $x - y + z = 0$ has normal vector $\vec{n}$, therefore it contains $r$ and is parallel to $s$. The plane $2x + y - z - 1 = 0$, with normal vector $\vec{w} \times \vec{n}$, contains $s$ and the common perpendicular but not $r$.
