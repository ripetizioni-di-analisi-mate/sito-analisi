For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ are the points $A = (1, 0, 2)$, $B = (2, 1, 0)$, $C = (0, 1, 1)$, $D = (3, k, 2)$ coplanar?

- $k = -\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $k = \dfrac{2}{3}$
- $k = -\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $k = 0$

## Explanation

The four points are coplanar if and only if $D$ lies in the plane through $A$, $B$, $C$, that is, if and only if the vectors $\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 1, -2)$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = (-1, 1, -1)$ and $\overrightarrow{AD} = (2, k, 0)$ are coplanar. By the **coplanarity criterion** for three vectors their scalar triple product must be zero, so the determinant
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & -2 \\
-1 & 1 & -1 \\
2 & k & 0
\end{vmatrix}
$$
must vanish. Expanding along the third row gives $2\,(-1 + 2) - k\,(-1 - 2) = 3k + 2$. Therefore the points are coplanar for $k = -\dfrac{2}{3}$.
