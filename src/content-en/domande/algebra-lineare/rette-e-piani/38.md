What is the distance between the parallel lines $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = t$, $z = 0$ and $s : x = t$, $y = 2 + t$, $z = 1$?

- $\sqrt{11}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{22}}{2}$
- $\sqrt{6}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}{2}$

## Explanation

The lines have the same direction vector $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 0)$, and their distance is the distance from a point on $s$ to the line $r$. With $A = (1, 0, 0)$ on $r$ and $B = (0, 2, 1)$ on $s$ we have $\overrightarrow{AB} = (-1, 2, 1)$ and
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \vec{v} = \bigl( 2 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot 1,\; 1 \cdot 1 - (-1) \cdot 0,\; (-1) \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 1 \bigr) = (-1, 1, -3) ,
$$
of norm $\sqrt{11}$. Since $\|\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{2}$, the formula for the **distance from a point to a line** gives
$$
d(r, s) = \frac{\sqrt{11}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{22}}{2} .
$$
