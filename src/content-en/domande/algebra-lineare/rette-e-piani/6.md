## Explanation

From the equations of $s$ one obtains $x = 1 + z$ and $y = -1 - z$, so setting $z = u$ gives $s : x = 1 + u$, $y = -1 - u$, $z = u$, with direction vector $\vec{w} = (1, -1, 1)$. The direction vector of $r$ is $\vec{v} = (2, 1, 1)$, which is not parallel to $\vec{w}$, therefore the lines are not parallel. Substituting the equations of $r$ into the equations of $s$ gives $(1 + 2t) - t = 1$ and $(-1 + t) + t = -1$, both satisfied by $t = 0$. Hence the lines are **intersecting**, and the common point is the point of $r$ with $t = 0$, that is, $(1, -1, 0)$.
