For which value of $k$ do the lines $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2t$, $z = -1 + t$ and $s : x = 2$, $y = 1 + u$, $z = 1 + ku$ intersect?

- $k = 1$
- $k = -1$
- $k = -2$
- For no value of $k$, because the lines are skew for every $k$

## Explanation

The line $r$ passes through $A = (1, 0, -1)$ with direction vector $\vec{v} = (1, 2, 1)$, and the line $s$ passes through $B = (2, 1, 1)$ with direction vector $\vec{w} = (0, 1, k)$, which is not parallel to $\vec{v}$ for any $k$. By the **coplanarity criterion** the lines are coplanar, and hence intersecting, if and only if the determinant of the matrix with rows $\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 1, 2)$, $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{w}$ is zero; this determinant is
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & k \end{vmatrix} = (2k - 1) - k + 2 = k + 1 .
$$
Therefore the lines intersect for $k = -1$, and in that case they meet at the point $(2, 2, 0)$.
