Let $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = t$, $z = 0$ and $s : x = 2 + u$, $y = 2$, $z = 2 - 2u$. Which statement about the position of the two lines and their distance is true?

- The lines are skew and their distance is $3$
- The lines intersect and their distance is $0$
- The lines are skew and their distance is $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- The lines are skew and their distance is $\dfrac{6\sqrt{5}}{5}$

## Explanation

Let $A = (1, 0, 0)$, $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 0)$ and $B = (2, 2, 2)$, $\vec{w} = (1, 0, -2)$. We have $\vec{v} \times \vec{w} = (-2, 2, -1)$ and $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) = (1, 2, 2) \cdot (-2, 2, -1) = 0$, so by the **coplanarity criterion** the lines are coplanar. Since the direction vectors are not parallel, the lines intersect; indeed for $t = 2$ and $u = 1$ both pass through $(3, 2, 0)$. Two lines with a common point therefore have **zero distance**. The value $3$ is the length of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, while $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\dfrac{6\sqrt{5}}{5}$ are the distances from $B$ to $r$ and from $A$ to $s$, which are positive even though the lines meet.
