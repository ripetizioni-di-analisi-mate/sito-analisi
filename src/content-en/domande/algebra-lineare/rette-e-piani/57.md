Which of the following segments meets the plane $\pi : x + 2y - z = 1$?

- The segment with endpoints $(0, 0, 0)$ and $(1, 0, 1)$
- The segment with endpoints $(2, 1, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 0)$
- The segment with endpoints $(0, 1, 0)$ and $(3, 0, 1)$
- The segment with endpoints $(1, 1, 1)$ and $(2, 0, 3)$

## Explanation

Let $f(x, y, z) = x + 2y - z - 1$. The points of the segment with endpoints $A$ and $B$ are $A + t\,(B - A)$ with $0 \leq t \leq 1$, and since $f$ is a polynomial of degree one we have $f(A + t\,(B - A)) = (1 - t)\,f(A) + t\,f(B)$. If $f(A)$ and $f(B)$ are nonzero and have the same sign, this expression has that sign for every $t \in [0, 1]$, so the segment does not meet $\pi$. If they have opposite signs, the expression vanishes for a value of $t$ between $0$ and $1$, therefore the segment meets $\pi$. For $A = (1, 1, 1)$ and $B = (2, 0, 3)$ we have $f(A) = 1$ and $f(B) = -2$, so this segment meets the plane, at the point with $t = \dfrac{1}{3}$, that is, at $\left( \dfrac{4}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{5}{3} \right)$. In the other three segments the values of $f$ at the endpoints are $-1$ and $-1$, $3$ and $1$, $1$ and $1$, so none of them meets $\pi$.
