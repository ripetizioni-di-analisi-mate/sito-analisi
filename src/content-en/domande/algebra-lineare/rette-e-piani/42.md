Let $r$ be the line with Cartesian equations
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
z - 2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Which is the plane that contains $r$ and passes through the point $P = (1, 1, 1)$?

- $x + y - z + 1 = 0$
- $x + y - 1 = 0$
- $x + y + z - 1 = 0$
- $x + y + z - 3 = 0$

## Explanation

The planes containing $r$ are those of the pencil $\lambda\,(x + y - 1) + \mu\,(z - 2) = 0$, with $(\lambda, \mu) \neq (0, 0)$. Substituting the coordinates of $P$ gives $\lambda\,(1 + 1 - 1) + \mu\,(1 - 2) = \lambda - \mu$, so the plane passes through $P$ if and only if $\lambda = \mu$. With $\lambda = \mu = 1$ one obtains the plane $(x + y - 1) + (z - 2) = 0$, that is, $x + y + z - 3 = 0$.
