A light ray with direction vector $\vec{d} = (1, 0, 2)$ hits the plane mirror $\pi : x + y = 1$. What is the direction vector of the reflected ray?

- $(-1, 0, -2)$
- $(2, 1, 2)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}, 2 \right)$
- $(0, -1, 2)$

## Explanation

In the reflection the component of $\vec{d}$ parallel to the mirror stays the same, while the component along the normal vector $\vec{n} = (1, 1, 0)$ changes sign. The component along $\vec{n}$ is
$$
\frac{\vec{d} \cdot \vec{n}}{\|\vec{n}\|^2}\, \vec{n} = \frac{1}{2}\,(1, 1, 0) ,
$$
so the reflected ray has direction vector
$$
\vec{d} - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,(1, 1, 0) = (0, -1, 2) .
$$
The vector $(-1, 0, -2)$ corresponds to the ray going back along the same line. The vector $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}, 2 \right)$ is the projection of $\vec{d}$ onto the mirror, in which the normal component is cancelled instead of changing sign.
