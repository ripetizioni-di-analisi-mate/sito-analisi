For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ do the line $r$ with equations $x + y = 1$, $y + kz = 0$ and the plane $\pi : x + z = 2$ have no points in common?

- $k = -1$
- $k = 1$
- $k = 0$
- $k = 2$

## Explanation

The common points are the solutions of the system of the three equations, whose coefficient matrix has determinant
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & k \\
1 & 0 & 1
\end{vmatrix} = 1 + k .
$$
For $k \neq -1$ the determinant is nonzero, so the system has a unique solution and the line meets the plane at one point. For $k = -1$ the equations of $r$ give $x = 1 - y$ and $z = y$, so every point of $r$ satisfies $x + z = 1$. Since $1 \neq 2$, no point of $r$ satisfies the equation of $\pi$, and the line is **parallel to the plane** with no points in common.
