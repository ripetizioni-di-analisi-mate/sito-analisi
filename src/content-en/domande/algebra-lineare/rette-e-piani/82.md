Let $r$ be the line with equations $x + y - 1 = 0$, $z - 2 = 0$. What is the plane that contains $r$ and is perpendicular to the plane $\pi : x + 2y + z = 0$?

- $x + y + 3z - 7 = 0$
- $x + y - 3z + 5 = 0$
- $3x + 3y + z - 5 = 0$
- $x + y - 1 = 0$

## Explanation

The planes containing $r$ form the **pencil** $\lambda\,(x + y - 1) + \mu\,(z - 2) = 0$, with normal vector $(\lambda, \lambda, \mu)$. The plane is perpendicular to $\pi$ if $(\lambda, \lambda, \mu) \cdot (1, 2, 1) = 3\lambda + \mu = 0$, so one can choose $\lambda = 1$, $\mu = -3$. The required plane is
$$
(x + y - 1) - 3\,(z - 2) = 0 , \qquad \text{that is} \qquad x + y - 3z + 5 = 0 .
$$
The plane $x + y + 3z - 7 = 0$ corresponds to $\mu = 3$, and its normal vector has dot product $6$ with $(1, 2, 1)$. The plane $x + y - 1 = 0$ contains $r$, but its normal vector $(1, 1, 0)$ has dot product $3$ with $(1, 2, 1)$.
