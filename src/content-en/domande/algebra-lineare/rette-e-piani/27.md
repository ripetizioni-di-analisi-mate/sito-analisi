The lines $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = t$, $z = 2t$ and $s : x = 1 + u$, $y = -u$, $z = u$ meet at the point $(1, 0, 0)$. What is the equation of the plane containing them?

- $3x + y - 2z + 3 = 0$
- $x + y + 2z - 1 = 0$
- $3x - y - 2z - 3 = 0$
- $3x + y - 2z - 3 = 0$

## Explanation

The plane containing two intersecting lines is parallel to their direction vectors $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 2)$ and $\vec{w} = (1, -1, 1)$, which are not parallel. Therefore a normal vector of the plane is
$$
\vec{n} = \vec{v} \times \vec{w} = (1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot (-1),\ 2 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 1,\ 1 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 1) = (3, 1, -2) .
$$
The plane passes through the common point $(1, 0, 0)$, so it has equation $3(x - 1) + y - 2z = 0$, that is, $3x + y - 2z - 3 = 0$. The plane $x + y + 2z - 1 = 0$ has $\vec{v}$ as its normal vector and is perpendicular to $r$.
