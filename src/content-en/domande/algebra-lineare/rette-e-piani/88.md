In the cube with opposite vertices $(0, 0, 0)$ and $(1, 1, 1)$, what is the distance between the line of the diagonal through these two vertices and the line of the edge from $(1, 0, 0)$ to $(1, 0, 1)$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$

## Explanation

The diagonal lies on the line through $A = (0, 0, 0)$ with $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 1)$, and the edge on the line through $B = (1, 0, 0)$ with $\vec{w} = (0, 0, 1)$. We have $\vec{v} \times \vec{w} = (1, -1, 0)$ and $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) = 1 \neq 0$, so the lines are skew. By the formula for the **distance between two skew lines**
$$
d = \frac{|\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w})|}{\|\vec{v} \times \vec{w}\|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} .
$$
The value $1$ is the length of $\overrightarrow{AB}$. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$ is the distance from the vertex $B$ to the diagonal, which is greater than the distance between the lines because $B$ is not the point of the edge closest to the diagonal.
