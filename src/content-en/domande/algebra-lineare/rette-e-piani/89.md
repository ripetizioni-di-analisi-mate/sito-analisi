The lines $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = t$, $z = 1$ and $s : x = 0$, $y = 2 + u$, $z = u$ are skew. What is the plane parallel to both lines and equidistant from them?

- $x - y + z = 0$
- $x - y + z - 2 = 0$
- $x - y + z + 2 = 0$
- $x - y + z - 4 = 0$

## Explanation

A plane parallel to $r$ and to $s$ has a normal vector orthogonal to $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 0)$ and to $\vec{w} = (0, 1, 1)$, hence parallel to $\vec{v} \times \vec{w} = (1, -1, 1)$, and it has equation $x - y + z + d = 0$. Every point of $r$ satisfies $x - y + z = 2$, therefore its distance from the plane is $\dfrac{|2 + d|}{\sqrt{3}}$. Every point of $s$ satisfies $x - y + z = -2$, therefore its distance from the plane is $\dfrac{|d - 2|}{\sqrt{3}}$. The two distances are equal for $d = 0$, so the required plane is $x - y + z = 0$, and each line is at distance $\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$ from it, half of the distance $\dfrac{4\sqrt{3}}{3}$ between the lines. The planes $x - y + z - 2 = 0$ and $x - y + z + 2 = 0$ are the ones of the same parallel pencil that contain $r$ and $s$.
