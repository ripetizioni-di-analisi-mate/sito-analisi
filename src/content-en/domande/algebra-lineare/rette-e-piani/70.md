Let $P = (1, 2, 3)$. The point $P'$ is the reflection of $P$ across the plane $x = 0$, and the point $P''$ is the reflection of $P'$ across the plane $y = 0$. With respect to which of the following sets is $P''$ the symmetric point of $P$?

- The origin
- The plane $z = 0$
- The $z$-axis
- The plane $x = y$

## Explanation

The orthogonal projection of $(x, y, z)$ onto the plane $x = 0$ is $(0, y, z)$, so the symmetric point is $(-x, y, z)$; in the same way the symmetric point with respect to the plane $y = 0$ is $(x, -y, z)$. Therefore $P' = (-1, 2, 3)$ and $P'' = (-1, -2, 3)$. The midpoint of the segment $P P''$ is $(0, 0, 3)$, which is the orthogonal projection of $P$ onto the $z$-axis, and $\overrightarrow{P P''} = (-2, -4, 0)$ is orthogonal to the direction vector $(0, 0, 1)$ of the axis. By the definition of symmetric point, $P''$ is therefore the symmetric point of $P$ **with respect to the $z$-axis**. The symmetric points of $P$ with respect to the origin, the plane $z = 0$ and the plane $x = y$ are instead $(-1, -2, -3)$, $(1, 2, -3)$ and $(2, 1, 3)$.
