Let $P = (3, 4, 5)$. What is the maximum of the distance from $P$ to the planes containing the $z$-axis?

::grafico{f="0.5*x" legenda=0 punti="0,0,pieno,O;3,4,pieno,P′" segmenti="3,4,4,2" etichette="4.6,1.9,x − 2y = 0" xmin=-1.5 xmax=6 ymin=-1.5 ymax=5 uguale=1 titolo="View from above, in which the $z$-axis projects to the origin $O$ and $P$ to the point $P' = (3, 4)$; the line is the trace of the plane $x - 2y = 0$, one of the planes containing the $z$-axis"}

- $5$
- $5\sqrt{2}$
- $4$
- $3$

## Explanation

The planes containing the $z$-axis form the pencil $\lambda x + \mu y = 0$, with $(\lambda, \mu) \neq (0, 0)$. The distance from $P$ to the plane of the pencil is
$$
d = \frac{|3\lambda + 4\mu|}{\sqrt{\lambda^2 + \mu^2}} = \frac{|(3, 4) \cdot (\lambda, \mu)|}{\|(\lambda, \mu)\|} .
$$
By the **Cauchy-Schwarz inequality** we have $|(3, 4) \cdot (\lambda, \mu)| \leq 5\,\|(\lambda, \mu)\|$, so $d \leq 5$, with equality when $(\lambda, \mu)$ is parallel to $(3, 4)$, that is, for the plane $3x + 4y = 0$. The maximum is therefore $5$, and it coincides with the distance from $P$ to the $z$-axis. The values $3$ and $4$ are the distances from $P$ to the planes $x = 0$ and $y = 0$, and $5\sqrt{2}$ is the distance from $P$ to the origin.
