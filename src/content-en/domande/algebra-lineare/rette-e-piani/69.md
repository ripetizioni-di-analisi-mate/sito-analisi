Let $r : x = t$, $y = 1 + 2t$, $z = -t$ and $s_k : x = 2 + kt$, $y = t$, $z = 1 + 3t$. For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is there a plane that contains $r$ and is perpendicular to $s_k$?

- $k = 1$
- $k = -1$
- $k = -5$
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

A plane perpendicular to $s_k$ has as normal vector the direction vector $\vec{w} = (k, 1, 3)$ of $s_k$. By the theorem on the relative position of a line and a plane, a plane with normal vector $\vec{w}$ can contain $r$ only if the direction vector $\vec{v} = (1, 2, -1)$ of $r$ is orthogonal to $\vec{w}$. The condition is $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{w} = k + 2 - 3 = 0$, so $k = 1$. In this case the plane is unique, the one through the point $(0, 1, 0)$ of $r$ with normal vector $(1, 1, 3)$, that is, $x + y + 3z - 1 = 0$. For $k \neq 1$ every plane perpendicular to $s_k$ meets $r$ at a single point, therefore a plane with both properties does *not* exist for every $k$.
