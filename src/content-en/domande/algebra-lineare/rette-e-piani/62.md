Which statement about the line $r$ with equations $x - y = 0$, $y - z = 0$ and the line $s$ with equations $x + y = 2$, $z = 0$ is true?

- The lines intersect at the point $(1, 1, 0)$
- The lines are skew
- The lines are parallel and distinct
- The lines intersect at the origin

## Explanation

The four equations form a system in the unknowns $x$, $y$, $z$ with augmented matrix
$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & -1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & 0 \\
1 & 1 & 0 & 2 \\
0 & 0 & 1 & 0
\end{array}\right) .
$$
The coefficient matrix has rank $3$, because the rows $(1, -1, 0)$, $(0, 1, -1)$, $(0, 0, 1)$ are independent; hence the direction vectors of $r$ and $s$ are not parallel. The augmented matrix has determinant $-2 \neq 0$, hence rank $4$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system has no solutions. The lines are not parallel and have no common points, therefore they are **skew**. The point $(1, 1, 0)$ lies on $s$ but not on $r$, and the origin lies on $r$ but not on $s$.
