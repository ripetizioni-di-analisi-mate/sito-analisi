What is the reflection of the line $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2$, $z = t$ across the plane $\pi : x - y = 0$?

- $x = 2,\ y = 1 + t,\ z = t$
- $x = 2 + t,\ y = 1,\ z = t$
- $x = 1 + t,\ y = 2,\ z = -t$
- $x = -2,\ y = -1 + t,\ z = t$

## Explanation

Let $P = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$ and let $\vec{n} = (1, -1, 0)$ be the normal vector of $\pi$. The orthogonal projection of $P$ onto $\pi$ is $H = P - \dfrac{x_0 - y_0}{2}\,\vec{n}$, so the reflection of $P$ is
$$
P' = 2H - P = P - (x_0 - y_0)\,\vec{n} = (y_0, x_0, z_0) .
$$
The reflection across $\pi$ therefore swaps the first two coordinates. The point $(1 + t,\ 2,\ t)$ of $r$ has reflection $(2,\ 1 + t,\ t)$, so the reflected line is $x = 2$, $y = 1 + t$, $z = t$. The line $x = 2 + t$, $y = 1$, $z = t$ passes through the reflection $(2, 1, 0)$ of the point $(1, 2, 0)$, but it has the direction vector $(1, 0, 1)$ of $r$, while the reflection of this vector is $(0, 1, 1)$.
