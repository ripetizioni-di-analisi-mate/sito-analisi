- $x = 1 + t,\ y = 1 + 2t,\ z = -3t$
- $x = 1 + t,\ y = 1 - 2t,\ z = -3t$
- $x = 1 + t,\ y = 1 - 2t,\ z = 3t$
- $x = 1 + t,\ y = 1 + 2t,\ z = -t$

## Explanation

The line lies in both planes, so its direction vector is orthogonal to the normal vectors $\vec{n}_1 = (1, 2, -1)$ and $\vec{n}_2 = (1, -1, 1)$. One can therefore take
$$
\vec{v} = \vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2 = \bigl( 2 \cdot 1 - (-1) \cdot (-1),\; (-1) \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 1,\; 1 \cdot (-1) - 2 \cdot 1 \bigr) = (1, -2, -3) .
$$
To find a point on the line, set $z = 0$. The system formed by $x + 2y = 3$ and $x - y = 0$ gives $x = y = 1$, so the point $(1, 1, 0)$ belongs to the line. The parametric equations are then $x = 1 + t$, $y = 1 - 2t$, $z = -3t$.
