The lines $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2$, $z = -t$ and $s : x = 2u$, $y = u$, $z = 1$ are skew. Which plane contains $r$ and is parallel to $s$?

- $x - 2y + z - 1 = 0$
- $2x + y - 4 = 0$
- $x - 2y + z + 3 = 0$
- $x - 2y - z + 3 = 0$

## Explanation

The required plane contains $r$, so it is parallel to $\vec{v} = (1, 0, -1)$, and it is parallel to $s$, so it is parallel to $\vec{w} = (2, 1, 0)$. Since $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{w}$ are not parallel, a normal vector of the plane is
$$
\vec{n} = \vec{v} \times \vec{w} = (0 \cdot 0 - (-1) \cdot 1,\ (-1) \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 0,\ 1 \cdot 1 - 0 \cdot 2) = (1, -2, 1) .
$$
The plane passes through the point $A = (1, 2, 0)$ of $r$, so it has equation $(x - 1) - 2(y - 2) + z = 0$, that is, $x - 2y + z + 3 = 0$. The plane $x - 2y + z - 1 = 0$ has the same normal vector but passes through the point $(0, 0, 1)$ of $s$, so it contains $s$ and is parallel to $r$. The distance between the two parallel planes is the distance between the skew lines.
