The lines $r : x = t$, $y = t$, $z = 0$ and $s : x = 0$, $y = u$, $z = 1$ are skew. Which of the following vectors is a direction vector of their common perpendicular?

- $(1, 2, 0)$
- $(1, 0, 0)$
- $(0, 0, 1)$
- $(1, -1, 0)$

## Explanation

The common perpendicular is orthogonal to the direction vectors $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 0)$ and $\vec{w} = (0, 1, 0)$, which are not parallel. The cross product
$$
\vec{v} \times \vec{w} = \bigl( 1 \cdot 0 - 0 \cdot 1,\; 0 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot 0,\; 1 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 0 \bigr) = (0, 0, 1)
$$
is orthogonal to both, so it is a direction vector of the common perpendicular. The vector $(1, -1, 0)$ is orthogonal to $\vec{v}$, but its dot product with $\vec{w}$ is $-1$, therefore it is not orthogonal to the line $s$.
