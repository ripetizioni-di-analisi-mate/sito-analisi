At which point do the planes $\pi_1 : x + y = 3$, $\pi_2 : y + z = 5$ and $\pi_3 : x + z = 4$ intersect?

- $(1, 2, 3)$
- $(7, 8, 9)$
- $(2, 1, 4)$
- The planes do not have a single common point, because they intersect in a line

## Explanation

The coefficient matrix of the system formed by the three equations is
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} , \qquad \det A = 1 \cdot (1 - 0) - 1 \cdot (0 - 1) + 0 = 2 \neq 0 .
$$
Hence $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system has a unique solution, that is, the three planes have exactly one common point. Adding the three equations gives $2(x + y + z) = 12$, so $x + y + z = 6$. Subtracting from this equality the equations of $\pi_1$, $\pi_2$ and $\pi_3$ gives $z = 3$, $x = 1$ and $y = 2$, so the common point is $(1, 2, 3)$. The coordinates $(7, 8, 9)$ are obtained by using $x + y + z = 12$ without dividing by $2$. The point $(2, 1, 4)$ lies on $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ but not on $\pi_3$, because $2 + 4 = 6 \neq 4$.
