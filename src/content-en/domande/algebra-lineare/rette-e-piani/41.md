What is the orthogonal projection of the line $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2t$, $z = 3 - t$ onto the plane $z = 0$?

- The line $x = 1 + t,\ y = 2t,\ z = 3$
- The line $x = 1 + t,\ y = 2t,\ z = 0$
- Only the point $(4, 6, 0)$
- The line $x = 1 + t,\ y = 2t,\ z = -t$

## Explanation

The normal vector of the plane $z = 0$ is $(0, 0, 1)$, so the orthogonal projection of the point $(x, y, z)$ onto this plane is $(x, y, 0)$. The direction vector $(1, 2, -1)$ of $r$ is not parallel to $(0, 0, 1)$, therefore $r$ is not perpendicular to the plane and the projections of its points form a line. Setting the third coordinate to zero gives the line $x = 1 + t$, $y = 2t$, $z = 0$, which passes through the point $(4, 6, 0)$ where $r$ meets the plane.
