What is the orthogonal projection of the line $r : x = 1 + 2t$, $y = -1 - t$, $z = 2t$ onto the plane $\pi : 2x - y + 2z = 3$?

- The line $x = 1 + 2t,\ y = -1 - t,\ z = 0$
- The single point $(1, -1, 0)$
- The single point $(3, -2, 2)$
- The line $r$ itself

## Explanation

The direction vector of $r$ is $\vec{v} = (2, -1, 2)$, which coincides with the normal vector $\vec{n}$ of $\pi$. Therefore $r$ is perpendicular to $\pi$, and the orthogonal projection of each of its points onto $\pi$ is the point where $r$ meets the plane. Substituting the equations of $r$ into the equation of $\pi$ gives $2(1 + 2t) + 1 + t + 4t = 3$, that is, $9t = 0$, so $t = 0$. The projection is therefore the single point $(1, -1, 0)$. The point $(3, -2, 2)$ lies on $r$ but not on $\pi$, because $6 + 2 + 4 = 12 \neq 3$.
