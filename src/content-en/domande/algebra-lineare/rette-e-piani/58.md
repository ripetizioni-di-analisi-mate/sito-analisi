Which point of the line $r : x = t$, $y = 1 + t$, $z = 2 - t$ is equidistant from the points $A = (1, 0, 0)$ and $B = (3, 2, 2)$?

- $(-2, -1, 4)$
- $(4, 5, -2)$
- $(1, 2, 1)$
- $(-1, 0, 3)$

## Explanation

The points equidistant from $A$ and $B$ form the plane through the midpoint $M = (2, 1, 1)$ orthogonal to $\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 2, 2)$, that is, the plane $(x - 2) + (y - 1) + (z - 1) = 0$, or $x + y + z = 4$. The point of $r$ with parameter $t$ satisfies this equation if $t + (1 + t) + (2 - t) = 4$, that is, $t + 3 = 4$, so $t = 1$. The required point is therefore $(1, 2, 1)$, and indeed it is at distance $\sqrt{5}$ from both $A$ and $B$. The points $(-2, -1, 4)$ and $(4, 5, -2)$ are obtained by intersecting $r$ with the parallel planes through $A$ and through $B$ instead of through $M$. The point $(-1, 0, 3)$ of $r$ is at distance $\sqrt{13}$ from $A$ and $\sqrt{21}$ from $B$.
