What is the angle between the lines $r : x = t$, $y = 1$, $z = t$ and $s : x = 2 - u$, $y = u$, $z = 0$?

- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The direction vectors are $\vec{v} = (1, 0, 1)$ and $\vec{w} = (-1, 1, 0)$, both of norm $\sqrt{2}$, and their dot product is $-1$. The direction vector is defined up to sign, so the definition of the angle between two lines uses the absolute value of the dot product, and one obtains
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{|\vec{v} \cdot \vec{w}|}{\|\vec{v}\| \, \|\vec{w}\|} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
The angle belongs to the interval $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$, therefore $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The value $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ is the angle between the vectors $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{w}$, the supplement of $\theta$.
