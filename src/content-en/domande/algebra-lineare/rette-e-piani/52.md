Which vector is a direction vector of the line with equations $\dfrac{x - 1}{2} = \dfrac{y + 1}{-1} = \dfrac{z}{-3}$?

- $(2, -1, -3)$
- $(1, -1, 0)$
- $(2, 1, -3)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, -1, -\dfrac{1}{3} \right)$

## Explanation

Setting the common value of the three fractions equal to $t$ gives $x - 1 = 2t$, $y + 1 = -t$, $z = -3t$. So the parametric equations of the line are $x = 1 + 2t$, $y = -1 - t$, $z = -3t$, and the line passes through $(1, -1, 0)$ with direction vector $(2, -1, -3)$. In this form the denominators are therefore the direction numbers. The vector $(1, -1, 0)$ has the coordinates of a point of the line, the vector $(2, 1, -3)$ has the wrong sign in the second component, and $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, -1, -\dfrac{1}{3} \right)$ is obtained by inverting the denominators and is not parallel to $(2, -1, -3)$.
