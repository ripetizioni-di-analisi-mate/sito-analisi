The line $r$ has direction vector $\vec{v} = (1, 2, 2)$. What is the cosine of the angle that $r$ forms with the $z$-axis?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{9}$

## Explanation

The $z$-axis has direction vector $\vec{k} = (0, 0, 1)$ and $\|\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = 3$, so by the definition of the angle between two lines
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{|\vec{v} \cdot \vec{k}|}{\|\vec{v}\|\, \|\vec{k}\|} = \frac{2}{3 \cdot 1} = \frac{2}{3} .
$$
The cosine of the angle with a coordinate axis is therefore the absolute value of the corresponding component of the unit vector $\dfrac{\vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|} = \left( \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$. The value $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is the cosine of the angle with the $x$-axis, $\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$ is the sine of the angle with the $z$-axis, and $\dfrac{2}{9}$ is obtained by dividing by $\|\vec{v}\|^2$ instead of by $\|\vec{v}\|$.
