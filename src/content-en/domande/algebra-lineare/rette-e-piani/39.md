The points $A = (1, 0, 2)$ and $B = (4, 3, 1)$ lie on the same side of the plane $z = 0$. For which point $Q$ of the plane $z = 0$ is the sum $\|\overrightarrow{AQ}\| + \|\overrightarrow{QB}\|$ minimal?

- $\left( \dfrac{5}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}, 0 \right)$
- $(7, 6, 0)$
- $(3, 2, 0)$
- $(1, 0, 0)$

## Explanation

Let $A' = (1, 0, -2)$ be the reflection of $A$ across the plane $z = 0$. For every $Q$ in the plane one has $\|\overrightarrow{AQ}\| = \|\overrightarrow{A'Q}\|$, because the plane is the set of points equidistant from $A$ and $A'$. Hence the sum equals $\|\overrightarrow{A'Q}\| + \|\overrightarrow{QB}\|$, which is minimal when $Q$ lies on the segment $A'B$. Since $A'$ and $B$ are on opposite sides of the plane, the segment meets it. The line $A'B$ has equations $x = 1 + 3t$, $y = 3t$, $z = -2 + 3t$, and the condition $z = 0$ gives $t = \dfrac{2}{3}$. Therefore $Q = (3, 2, 0)$, and the minimum value of the sum is $\|\overrightarrow{A'B}\| = 3\sqrt{3}$. The point $(7, 6, 0)$ is the intersection of the line $AB$ with the plane, which lies outside the segment $AB$.
