For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does the system formed by the equations $x + ky + z = 1$ and $2x + 4y + 2z = 3$ not represent a line?

- $k = 4$
- $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $k = -2$
- $k = 2$

## Explanation

Two linear equations in three unknowns represent a line if and only if the coefficient matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & k & 1 \\ 2 & 4 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
has rank $2$, that is, if the normal vectors $(1, k, 1)$ and $(2, 4, 2)$ are not parallel. Since $(2, 4, 2) = 2\,(1, 2, 1)$, the two vectors are parallel if and only if $k = 2$. For $k = 2$ the augmented matrix has rank $2$, because $3 \neq 2 \cdot 1$, so the two planes are parallel and distinct and the system **has no solutions**. For every other value of $k$ the planes intersect and the system represents a line.
