The lines $r$ and $s$ are both perpendicular to the $z$-axis. Which statement about $r$ and $s$ is true?

- The lines are parallel
- The lines are intersecting
- The lines can be parallel, intersecting or skew
- The lines are skew

## Explanation

A line with direction vector $\vec{v}$ is perpendicular to the $z$-axis if and only if $\vec{v} \cdot (0, 0, 1) = 0$, even when the line and the axis are skew. Hence the direction vectors of $r$ and $s$ have zero third component, and this condition does not determine the relative position of the two lines. The following three examples show that all cases are possible.

- The $x$-axis and the $y$-axis are perpendicular to the $z$-axis and intersect at the origin.
- The $x$-axis and the line $x = t$, $y = 0$, $z = 1$ are perpendicular to the $z$-axis and are parallel, because they have the same direction vector $(1, 0, 0)$.
- The $x$-axis and the line $x = 0$, $y = t$, $z = 1$ are perpendicular to the $z$-axis and are **skew**, because they have no common points (on the first $z = 0$, on the second $z = 1$) and the direction vectors $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 0)$ are not parallel.

In the plane, by contrast, two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel.
