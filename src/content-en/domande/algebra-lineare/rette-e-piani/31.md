Which of the following planes intersects the plane $\pi : x - z = 2$ in a line parallel to the $y$-axis?

- $x + y - z = 0$
- $2x + z = 1$
- $2x - 2z = 1$
- $y = 3$

## Explanation

Two intersecting planes meet in a line whose direction vector is orthogonal to both normal vectors, hence parallel to their cross product. The plane $\pi$ has normal vector $\vec{n}_1 = (1, 0, -1)$, and for the plane $2x + z = 1$, with $\vec{n}_2 = (2, 0, 1)$, one has
$$
\vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2 = (0, -3, 0) ,
$$
which is parallel to the $y$-axis. Therefore the line common to $\pi$ and $2x + z = 1$ is parallel to the $y$-axis. The plane $2x - 2z = 1$ has a normal vector parallel to $\vec{n}_1$ and is parallel to $\pi$, so it does not intersect it. For the planes $x + y - z = 0$ and $y = 3$ the cross product with $\vec{n}_1$ is parallel to $(1, 0, 1)$, which is not parallel to the $y$-axis.
