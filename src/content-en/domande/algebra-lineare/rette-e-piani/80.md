The line $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = t$, $z = 1$ is not perpendicular to the plane $\pi : x + y + z = 0$. Which vector is parallel to its orthogonal projection onto $\pi$?

- $(1, 1, 0)$
- $(1, 1, 1)$
- $(1, 1, -2)$
- $(-1, 1, 0)$

## Explanation

The orthogonal projection maps the direction vector $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 0)$ of $r$ to its component parallel to the plane, which is obtained by subtracting from $\vec{v}$ its component along the normal vector $\vec{n} = (1, 1, 1)$, that is,
$$
\vec{v} - \frac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n}}{\|\vec{n}\|^2}\, \vec{n} = (1, 1, 0) - \frac{2}{3}\,(1, 1, 1) = \left( \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{3}, -\frac{2}{3} \right) .
$$
This vector is parallel to $(1, 1, -2)$, which is orthogonal to $\vec{n}$. The vector $(1, 1, 0)$ is not parallel to $\pi$, since $(1, 1, 0) \cdot \vec{n} = 2$. The vector $(-1, 1, 0) = \vec{n} \times \vec{v}$ is the normal vector of the plane that contains $r$ and is perpendicular to $\pi$, and it is not parallel to the projection.
