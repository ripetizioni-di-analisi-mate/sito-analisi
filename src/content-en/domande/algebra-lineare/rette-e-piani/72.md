The points equidistant from the planes $\pi_1 : x + 2y + 2z = 0$ and $\pi_2 : 2x + y - 2z = 3$ form two planes. Which of the following is one of them?

- $x + y + 1 = 0$
- $x - y - 4z + 3 = 0$
- $x + y - 1 = 0$
- $3x + 3y + 4z - 3 = 0$

## Explanation

The normal vectors $(1, 2, 2)$ and $(2, 1, -2)$ both have norm $3$, so by the formula for the distance from a point to a plane the point $(x, y, z)$ is equidistant from $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ if and only if
$$
|x + 2y + 2z| = |2x + y - 2z - 3| .
$$
The equality holds if the two expressions are equal or opposite. If they are equal we obtain $-x + y + 4z + 3 = 0$, that is, $x - y - 4z - 3 = 0$. If they are opposite their sum is zero, and we obtain $3x + 3y - 3 = 0$, that is, $x + y - 1 = 0$. These are the two **bisector planes** of the angles formed by $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$. The plane $x - y - 4z + 3 = 0$ differs from the first bisector in the sign of the constant term, and the plane $x + y + 1 = 0$ differs from the second.
