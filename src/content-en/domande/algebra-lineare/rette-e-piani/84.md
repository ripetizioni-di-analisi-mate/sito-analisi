Let $r$ be the line with equations $x = 0$, $z = 1$. Which of the following planes contains $r$ and forms an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ with the plane $z = 0$?

- $x + z + 1 = 0$
- $z - 1 = 0$
- $x = 0$
- $x - z + 1 = 0$

## Explanation

The planes containing $r$ form the pencil $\lambda x + \mu\,(z - 1) = 0$, with normal vector $(\lambda, 0, \mu)$. By the definition of the angle between two planes, the angle $\theta$ with the plane $z = 0$, whose normal vector is $(0, 0, 1)$, satisfies
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{|\mu|}{\sqrt{\lambda^2 + \mu^2}} .
$$
The condition $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ is equivalent to $2\mu^2 = \lambda^2 + \mu^2$, that is, $\mu = \pm\lambda$. With $\lambda = 1$ we obtain the two planes $x + z - 1 = 0$ and $x - z + 1 = 0$, and the second is the required plane. The plane $x + z + 1 = 0$ forms the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ but does not contain $r$, because the point $(0, 0, 1)$ of $r$ gives $2 \neq 0$. The planes $z - 1 = 0$ and $x = 0$ contain $r$ and form with $z = 0$ the angles $0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
