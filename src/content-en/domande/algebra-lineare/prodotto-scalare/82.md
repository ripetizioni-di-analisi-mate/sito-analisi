Let
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
0 & 1
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
What are the eigenvalues of $A^T A$?

- $3 + 2\sqrt{2}$ and $3 - 2\sqrt{2}$
- $1$, with algebraic multiplicity $2$
- $1$ and $5$
- $3 + \sqrt{2}$ and $3 - \sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The row-by-column product gives
$$
A^T A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
2 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
2 & 5
\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is symmetric, like every matrix of the form $A^T A$. The trace is $6$ and the determinant is $1$, therefore $p(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 1$. The roots are $\lambda = 3 \pm \sqrt{8} = 3 \pm 2\sqrt{2}$, both real and positive. The double eigenvalue $1$ is that of $A$, and $1$ and $5$ are the diagonal entries of $A^T A$.
