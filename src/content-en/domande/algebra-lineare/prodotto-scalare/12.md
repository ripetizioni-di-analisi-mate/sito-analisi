For the symmetric matrix
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
7 & 6 \\
6 & -2
\end{pmatrix} ,
$$
which orthogonal matrix $P$ satisfies $P^T A P = \begin{pmatrix} 10 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 \end{pmatrix}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^2 - 5\lambda - 50 = (\lambda - 10)(\lambda + 5)$, therefore the eigenvalues are $10$ and $-5$. The system $(A - 10I)x = 0$ reduces to the equation $x - 2y = 0$ and gives the eigenvector $(2, 1)$; the system $(A + 5I)x = 0$ reduces to $2x + y = 0$ and gives the eigenvector $(1, -2)$. The columns of $P$ are these normalized eigenvectors, in the same order as the eigenvalues in the diagonal matrix, therefore $P = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$. Without the factor $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$ the columns are orthogonal but have norm $\sqrt{5}$, and the matrix is not orthogonal.
