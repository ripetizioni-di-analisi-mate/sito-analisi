Let $W$ be the subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$ spanned by $w_1 = (1, 1, 0)$ and $w_2 = (1, -1, 1)$. What is the orthogonal projection of $v = (2, 0, 4)$ onto $W$?

- $(8, -4, 6)$
- $(3, -1, 2)$
- $(-1, 1, 2)$
- $(2, -2, 2)$

## Explanation

The two spanning vectors are orthogonal, because $1 - 1 + 0 = 0$, therefore the projection is the sum of the projections of $v$ onto $w_1$ and onto $w_2$. Since $\langle v, w_1 \rangle = 2$, $\langle w_1, w_1 \rangle = 2$, $\langle v, w_2 \rangle = 6$ and $\langle w_2, w_2 \rangle = 3$, one has
$$
P_W(v) = \frac{2}{2}\,(1, 1, 0) + \frac{6}{3}\,(1, -1, 1) = (3, -1, 2) .
$$
The difference $v - P_W(v) = (-1, 1, 2)$ is orthogonal to both spanning vectors, as the theorem on the orthogonal projection onto a subspace predicts.
