Let
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 1 \\
0 & 3
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which statement is true?

- $A$ is not diagonalizable
- $A$ is diagonalizable, but there is no orthogonal matrix $P$ with $P^T A P$ diagonal
- There is an orthogonal matrix $P$ with $P^T A P$ diagonal, because $A$ has distinct eigenvalues
- There is an orthogonal matrix $P$ with $P^T A P$ diagonal, because $A$ has real eigenvalues

## Explanation

The matrix is triangular, therefore its eigenvalues are $2$ and $3$; since they are distinct, $A$ is **diagonalizable**. By the **spectral theorem** a real matrix is diagonalizable by an orthogonal matrix if and only if it is symmetric, and $A$ is not symmetric, because $a_{12} = 1$ and $a_{21} = 0$.

The computation confirms it: $V_2 = \langle (1, 0) \rangle$ and $V_3 = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$, and the two eigenvectors are not orthogonal, because their inner product equals $1$. Hence there is no orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.
