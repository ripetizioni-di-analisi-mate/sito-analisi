Let $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with $\|u\| = \|v\| = 2$ and $v \neq u$, $v \neq -u$. Which statement is true for every choice of $u$ and $v$ with these properties?

- $u$ and $v$ are orthogonal
- $\|u + v\| = 4$
- $u + v$ and $u - v$ are orthogonal
- $u + v$ and $u - v$ have the same norm

## Explanation

By bilinearity and symmetry one has
$$
\langle u + v, u - v \rangle = \langle u, u \rangle - \langle u, v \rangle + \langle v, u \rangle - \langle v, v \rangle = \|u\|^2 - \|v\|^2 .
$$
Since $\|u\| = \|v\|$, the product is zero, therefore $u + v$ and $u - v$ are **orthogonal**. Indeed, in a rhombus the diagonals are perpendicular.

The other statements depend on $\langle u, v \rangle$. For $u = (2, 0)$ and $v = (1, \sqrt{3})$ one has $\langle u, v \rangle = 2$, hence $u$ and $v$ are not orthogonal, $\|u + v\|^2 = 12$ and $\|u - v\|^2 = 4$. Finally $\|u + v\| = 4$ would require $\langle u, v \rangle = 4 = \|u\|\,\|v\|$, that is, $v = u$ by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, a case excluded by the hypothesis.
