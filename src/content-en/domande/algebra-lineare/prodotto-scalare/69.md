In $\mathbb{R}^3$ let $U$ be the plane $x + y + z = 0$ and $V$ the plane $x - y = 0$. What is the orthogonal complement of $U \cap V$?

- The plane $x + y + z = 0$
- The line $\langle (1, 1, -2) \rangle$
- The plane $x - y = 0$
- The plane $x + y - 2z = 0$

## Explanation

The intersection $U \cap V$ consists of the vectors that satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
x - y = 0
\end{cases}
$$
therefore $y = x$, $z = -2x$, and $U \cap V = \langle (1, 1, -2) \rangle$. The orthogonal complement of a line $\langle a \rangle$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is the plane of the vectors $x$ with $\langle x, a \rangle = 0$, hence $(U \cap V)^\perp$ is the plane $x + y - 2z = 0$. This plane contains the normal vectors $(1, 1, 1)$ and $(1, -1, 0)$ of the two given planes, and in fact it is spanned by them.
