On $\mathbb{R}^2$ consider the inner product $\langle u, v \rangle = 2u_1 v_1 + 3u_2 v_2$. Which pair of vectors is an orthonormal basis with respect to this inner product?

- $\left( \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, 0 \right)$ and $\left( 0, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right)$
- $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 0 \right)$ and $\left( 0, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$
- $\left( \sqrt{2}, 0 \right)$ and $\left( 0, \sqrt{3} \right)$

## Explanation

Two vectors of the form $(a, 0)$ and $(0, b)$ are orthogonal for every $a, b$, because $\langle (a, 0), (0, b) \rangle = 2a \cdot 0 + 3 \cdot 0 \cdot b = 0$. Their squared norms are $\langle (a, 0), (a, 0) \rangle = 2a^2$ and $\langle (0, b), (0, b) \rangle = 3b^2$.

Therefore the two vectors are unit vectors if $2a^2 = 1$ and $3b^2 = 1$, that is, for $a = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $b = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ up to sign. The standard basis is orthogonal but not orthonormal, because its vectors have norm $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$.
