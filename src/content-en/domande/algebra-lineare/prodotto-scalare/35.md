Let $W = \langle (1, 2, -1, 0), (0, 1, 1, 1) \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{R}^4$ and let $v$ be a vector that belongs both to $W$ and to $W^\perp$. What is $\|v\|$?

- $0$
- $\sqrt{6}$
- $\sqrt{3}$
- $3$

## Explanation

Since $v \in W^\perp$, the vector $v$ is orthogonal to every vector of $W$.

Since also $v \in W$, the vector $v$ is orthogonal to itself, that is, $\langle v, v \rangle = 0$.

Then $\|v\|^2 = 0$, and by the positive definiteness of the inner product $v = 0$. Hence $\|v\| = 0$. The same argument holds for every subspace $W$, therefore $W \cap W^\perp = \{ 0 \}$.
