What set do the vectors $v \in \mathbb{R}^3$ form that satisfy
$$
\langle v, (1, 1, 0) \rangle = 0,
\qquad
\langle v, (0, 1, 1) \rangle = 0,
\qquad
\langle v, (1, 0, 1) \rangle = 0 ?
$$

- The line $\langle (1, -1, 1) \rangle$
- The plane $x + y + z = 0$
- Only the zero vector
- The line $\langle (1, 1, 1) \rangle$

## Explanation

The matrix having the three vectors as rows has determinant $2 \neq 0$, therefore the three vectors form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$. Every vector of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a linear combination of this basis, hence, by bilinearity, $v$ is orthogonal to every vector of $\mathbb{R}^3$. In particular $v$ is orthogonal to itself, that is, $\|v\|^2 = 0$, and by positive definiteness $v = 0$.

The vector $(1, -1, 1)$ is orthogonal to the first two vectors, but $\langle (1, -1, 1), (1, 0, 1) \rangle = 2$, therefore it does not satisfy the third condition.
