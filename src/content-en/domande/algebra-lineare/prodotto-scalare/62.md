The orthogonal reflection across the line $r = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$ sends every vector $v$ to the vector $s(v)$ such that the midpoint of $v$ and $s(v)$ is the orthogonal projection of $v$ onto $r$. What is $s(v)$ for $v = (3, 1)$?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,3,1,v" segmenti="-1,-1,4,4" etichette="3.3,3.7,r" xmin=-1.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=4.5 uguale=1 titolo="The vector $v = (3, 1)$ and the line $r = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$, dashed"}

- $(1, 3)$
- $(2, 2)$
- $(-1, -3)$
- $(-3, -1)$

## Explanation

With $u = (1, 1)$ one has $\langle v, u \rangle = 4$ and $\langle u, u \rangle = 2$, therefore the projection of $v$ onto $r$ is $p = 2u = (2, 2)$. The midpoint of $v$ and $s(v)$ is $\dfrac{v + s(v)}{2}$, and the condition $\dfrac{v + s(v)}{2} = p$ gives
$$
s(v) = 2p - v = (4, 4) - (3, 1) = (1, 3) .
$$
The vector $(2, 2)$ is the projection, while $(-1, -3) = v - 2p$ is the reflection of $v$ across the line orthogonal to $r$.
