Let $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with $\|u\| = 3$, $\|v\| = 2$ and $\langle u, v \rangle = -1$. What is $\|u + 2v\|$?

- $5$
- $\sqrt{23}$
- $\sqrt{21}$
- $\sqrt{13}$

## Explanation

By the bilinearity and the symmetry of the scalar product one has
$$
\|u + 2v\|^2 = \langle u + 2v, u + 2v \rangle = \|u\|^2 + 4\langle u, v \rangle + 4\|v\|^2 .
$$
Substituting the data gives $\|u + 2v\|^2 = 9 - 4 + 16 = 21$, therefore $\|u + 2v\| = \sqrt{21}$. The middle term has coefficient $4$, because the cross term $2\langle u, 2v \rangle$ also contains the factor $2$ of the vector $2v$.
