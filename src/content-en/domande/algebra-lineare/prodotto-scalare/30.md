Let $W = \langle (1, 1, 0), (1, 0, 1) \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$. What is the orthogonal projection of $v = (0, 0, 1)$ onto $W$?

- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 0, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{3}, -\dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{4}{3} \right)$

## Explanation

The spanning vectors of $W$ have inner product $1$, therefore they are not orthogonal and the formula with the sum of the projections onto the spanning vectors does not apply. The orthogonal complement of $W$ is the line spanned by $n = (1, -1, -1)$, which is orthogonal to both spanning vectors. Since $\langle v, n \rangle = -1$ and $\langle n, n \rangle = 3$, one has
$$
P_W(v) = v - \operatorname{pr}_n(v) = (0, 0, 1) + \frac{1}{3}\,(1, -1, -1) = \left( \frac{1}{3}, -\frac{1}{3}, \frac{2}{3} \right) .
$$
The vector $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 0, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ is the result of the formula applied to the non-orthogonal spanning vectors, and its difference from $v$ is not orthogonal to $(1, 1, 0)$.
