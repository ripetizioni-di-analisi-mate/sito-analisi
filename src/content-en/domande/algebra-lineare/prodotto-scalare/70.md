In $\mathbb{R}^3$ let $W_1 = \langle (1, 0, 0) \rangle$ and $W_2 = \langle (1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0) \rangle$. Which relation between the orthogonal complements is true?

- $W_2^\perp \subseteq W_1^\perp$
- $W_1^\perp \subseteq W_2^\perp$
- $W_1^\perp = W_2^\perp$
- $W_1^\perp \cap W_2^\perp = \{ 0 \}$

## Explanation

The complement $W_1^\perp$ is the plane $x = 0$, while $W_2^\perp$ is the set of vectors with $x = 0$ and $y = 0$, that is, the line $\langle (0, 0, 1) \rangle$. The line is contained in the plane, therefore $W_2^\perp \subseteq W_1^\perp$.

The same holds in general: since $W_1 \subseteq W_2$, a vector orthogonal to all vectors of $W_2$ is also orthogonal to all vectors of $W_1$. The intersection of the two complements is the line $\langle (0, 0, 1) \rangle$, which contains nonzero vectors.
