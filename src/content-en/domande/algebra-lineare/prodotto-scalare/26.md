Let $w_1, w_2, w_3 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be pairwise orthogonal, with $\|w_1\| = 1$, $\|w_2\| = 2$ and $\|w_3\| = 2$. What is $\|w_1 - w_2 + w_3\|$?

- $1$
- $5$
- $3$
- $9$

## Explanation

The vectors $w_1$, $-w_2$ and $w_3$ are pairwise orthogonal, because changing the sign of $w_2$ only changes the sign of the dot products, which remain zero.

The **Pythagorean theorem**, extended to three pairwise orthogonal vectors, then gives
$$
\|w_1 - w_2 + w_3\|^2 = \|w_1\|^2 + \|w_2\|^2 + \|w_3\|^2 = 1 + 4 + 4 = 9 .
$$
Therefore $\|w_1 - w_2 + w_3\| = 3$.
