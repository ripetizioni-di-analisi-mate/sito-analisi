A symmetric matrix $A \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ has eigenvalue $4$ with eigenvector $(1, 1)$ and eigenvalue $-2$ with eigenvector $(1, -1)$. What is the matrix $A$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 6 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

By the **spectral decomposition** one has $A = 4\, e_1 e_1^T - 2\, e_2 e_2^T$, with $e_1 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 1)$ and $e_2 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, -1)$. Since
$$
e_1 e_1^T = \frac{1}{2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
1 & 1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
e_2 e_2^T = \frac{1}{2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & -1 \\
-1 & 1
\end{pmatrix},
$$
one obtains $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. The product with the eigenvectors confirms the result, since $A\,(1, 1) = (4, 4)$ and $A\,(1, -1) = (-2, 2)$.
