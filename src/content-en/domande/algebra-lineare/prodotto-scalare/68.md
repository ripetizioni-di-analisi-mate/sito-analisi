The Gram-Schmidt process is applied first to the pair $v_1 = (1, 2, 2)$, $v_2 = (3, 1, 0)$ and then to the pair $v_1 = (1, 2, 2)$, $w_2 = (5, 5, 4)$, each time in the given order. What is the relation between the second vector $u_2$ obtained in the first case and the second vector $u_2'$ obtained in the second case, before normalization?

- $u_2' = 2u_2$
- $u_2'$ and $u_2$ are orthogonal
- $u_2' = u_2$
- $u_2' = -u_2$

## Explanation

Observe that $w_2 = v_2 + 2v_1$. The projection onto $v_1$ is linear and $\operatorname{pr}_{v_1}(v_1) = v_1$, therefore $\operatorname{pr}_{v_1}(w_2) = \operatorname{pr}_{v_1}(v_2) + 2v_1$. Hence
$$
u_2' = w_2 - \operatorname{pr}_{v_1}(w_2) = v_2 + 2v_1 - \operatorname{pr}_{v_1}(v_2) - 2v_1 = u_2 .
$$
The direct computation confirms it: $\langle v_2, v_1 \rangle = 5$, $\langle w_2, v_1 \rangle = 23$, $\langle v_1, v_1 \rangle = 9$, and in both cases one obtains $\dfrac{1}{9}\,(22, -1, -10)$. Adding to $v_2$ a multiple of $v_1$ does not change the result of the process.
