The vectors $w_1 = (1, 1, 1)$, $w_2 = (1, -2, 1)$, $w_3 = (1, 0, -1)$ form an orthogonal basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$. What are the coordinates of $v = (2, 1, 0)$ with respect to this basis?

- $(3, 0, 2)$
- $(\sqrt{3}, 0, \sqrt{2})$
- $(2, 1, 0)$
- $(1, 0, 1)$

## Explanation

With respect to an orthogonal basis the $i$-th coordinate of $v$ is $\dfrac{\langle v, w_i \rangle}{\langle w_i, w_i \rangle}$. One obtains
$$
\frac{\langle v, w_1 \rangle}{\langle w_1, w_1 \rangle} = \frac{3}{3} = 1,
\qquad
\frac{\langle v, w_2 \rangle}{\langle w_2, w_2 \rangle} = \frac{0}{6} = 0,
\qquad
\frac{\langle v, w_3 \rangle}{\langle w_3, w_3 \rangle} = \frac{2}{2} = 1 .
$$
Therefore the coordinates are $(1, 0, 1)$, and indeed $w_1 + w_3 = (2, 1, 0)$. The basis is not orthonormal, therefore the inner products alone, $(3, 0, 2)$, do not give the coordinates.
