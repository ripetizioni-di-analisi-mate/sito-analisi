Which of the following sets is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$?

- $\left\{ (1, 0, 1),\ (0, 1, 0),\ (1, 0, -1) \right\}$
- $\left\{ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 0, 1),\ (0, 1, 0),\ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 0, -1) \right\}$
- $\left\{ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 1, 0),\ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(0, 1, 1),\ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 0, 1) \right\}$
- $\left\{ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,(1, 1, 1),\ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, -1, 0) \right\}$

## Explanation

The vectors $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 0, 1)$, $(0, 1, 0)$ and $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 0, -1)$ have norm $1$ and are pairwise orthogonal, because $\langle (1, 0, 1), (1, 0, -1) \rangle = 0$ and $(0, 1, 0)$ is orthogonal to both of the others. Three nonzero pairwise orthogonal vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$ are independent, therefore they form an **orthonormal basis**. The same vectors without the factor $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ form a basis that is only orthogonal, because $(1, 0, 1)$ has norm $\sqrt{2}$. The unit vectors $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 1, 0)$ and $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(0, 1, 1)$ have inner product $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and two vectors alone do not form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$.
