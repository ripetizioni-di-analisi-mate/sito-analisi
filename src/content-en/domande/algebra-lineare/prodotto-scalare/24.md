Which statement is true for the vectors
$$
(1, 1, 0, 0), \qquad (1, -1, 1, 1), \qquad (1, -1, -1, -1), \qquad (0, 0, 1, -1)
$$
of $\mathbb{R}^4$?

- They span a subspace of dimension $3$
- They form an orthogonal basis of $\mathbb{R}^4$
- They form an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^4$
- They are linearly dependent

## Explanation

The six dot products between two distinct vectors are all zero: for example $\langle (1, -1, 1, 1), (1, -1, -1, -1) \rangle = 1 + 1 - 1 - 1 = 0$ and $\langle (1, -1, 1, 1), (0, 0, 1, -1) \rangle = 1 - 1 = 0$.

The four vectors are therefore nonzero and pairwise orthogonal, so they are linearly independent by the theorem on the **independence of orthogonal vectors**.

Since they are four independent vectors of $\mathbb{R}^4$, they form an **orthogonal basis** of $\mathbb{R}^4$, with no need to compute a determinant.

The basis is not orthonormal, because $\|(1, 1, 0, 0)\| = \sqrt{2}$ and $\|(1, -1, 1, 1)\| = 2$.
