Let $u = (2, -1, 3)$ and $v = (a, b, -6)$. For which $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ does $|\langle u, v \rangle| = \|u\|\, \|v\|$ hold?

- $(a, b) = (4, -2)$
- $(a, b) = (9, 0)$
- $(a, b) = (-4, 2)$
- $(a, b) = (-4, -2)$

## Explanation

By the **Cauchy-Schwarz inequality** equality holds if and only if $u$ and $v$ are linearly dependent. Since $u \neq 0$, this is equivalent to $v = t u$ for some $t \in \mathbb{R}$. From the third component one obtains $3t = -6$, therefore $t = -2$ and $v = (-4, 2, -6)$. For $(a, b) = (9, 0)$ the two vectors are orthogonal, and in that case the left-hand side of the equality is zero.
