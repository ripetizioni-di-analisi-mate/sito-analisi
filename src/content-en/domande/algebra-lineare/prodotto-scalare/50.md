What is the maximum value of $2x - y + 2z$ as $(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ varies with $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$?

- $3$
- $5$
- $9$
- $1$

## Explanation

Setting $a = (2, -1, 2)$ and $w = (x, y, z)$, the expression is the inner product $\langle a, w \rangle$, and the condition says that $\|w\| = 1$. By the **Cauchy-Schwarz inequality** one has $\langle a, w \rangle \leq \|a\|\,\|w\| = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3$.

The value $3$ is attained, because for the unit vector $w = \dfrac{1}{3}\,(2, -1, 2)$ one gets $\langle a, w \rangle = \dfrac{4 + 1 + 4}{3} = 3$. Hence the maximum is $3$.
