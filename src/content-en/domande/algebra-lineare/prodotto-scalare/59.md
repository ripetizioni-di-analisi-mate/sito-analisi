Which matrix $P$ satisfies $Pv = \operatorname{pr}_u(v)$ for every $v \in \mathbb{R}^2$, where $\operatorname{pr}_u(v)$ is the orthogonal projection of $v$ onto $u = (1, 2)$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Writing the vectors as columns, $\langle v, u \rangle = u^T v$ and $\langle u, u \rangle = u^T u = 5$, therefore
$$
\operatorname{pr}_u(v) = \frac{u^T v}{5}\, u = \frac{1}{5}\, u\, u^T v .
$$
Hence $P = \dfrac{1}{5}\, u u^T$, and the product of the column $u$ by the row $u^T$ is the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$. As a check, $Pu = u$ and $P(2, -1) = 0$. The matrix $\dfrac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is the one of the projection onto the orthogonal line $\langle (2, -1) \rangle$.
