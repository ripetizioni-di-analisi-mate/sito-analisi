Let
$$
Q = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & -1 \\
1 & 1
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is $Q^4$?

- $-I_2$
- $I_2$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -4 & 0 \\ 0 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix $Q$ is the rotation $R_\theta$ with $\cos\theta = \sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, that is, by the angle $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The direct computation gives
$$
Q^2 = \frac{1}{2}
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -2 \\
2 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -1 \\
1 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
which is the rotation by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Therefore $Q^4 = (Q^2)^2 = -I_2$, the rotation by $\pi$. The matrix $-4I_2$ is obtained by raising to the fourth power the matrix without the factor $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.
