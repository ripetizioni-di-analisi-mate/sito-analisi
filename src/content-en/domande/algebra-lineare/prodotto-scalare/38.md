The orthogonal matrices of order $3$ that are also upper triangular have a particular form. Which one?

- The only one is the matrix $I_3$
- They are diagonal, with diagonal entries equal to $1$ or $-1$
- They all have determinant $1$
- They have at least one zero diagonal entry

## Explanation

Let $Q$ be a matrix with these properties. The columns and the rows of an orthogonal matrix are pairwise orthogonal unit vectors.

The first column of $Q$ is $(q_{11}, 0, 0)$ and has norm $1$, hence $q_{11} = \pm 1$. The first row $(q_{11}, q_{12}, q_{13})$ has norm $1$, therefore $q_{12}^2 + q_{13}^2 = 0$, that is, $q_{12} = q_{13} = 0$.

Then the second column is $(0, q_{22}, 0)$, hence $q_{22} = \pm 1$, and from the norm of the second row it follows that $q_{23} = 0$. Finally the third column is $(0, 0, q_{33})$, therefore $q_{33} = \pm 1$.

Hence $Q$ is diagonal with diagonal entries $\pm 1$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has these properties, is different from $I_3$ and has determinant $-1$.
