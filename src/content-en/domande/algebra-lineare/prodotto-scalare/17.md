Which of the following functions is an inner product on $\mathbb{R}^2$, with $u = (u_1, u_2)$ and $v = (v_1, v_2)$?

- $\langle u, v \rangle = u_1 v_1 - u_2 v_2$
- $\langle u, v \rangle = u_1 v_2 + u_2 v_1$
- $\langle u, v \rangle = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + 1$
- $\langle u, v \rangle = 2u_1 v_1 + 3u_2 v_2$

## Explanation

An inner product is a symmetric, bilinear and positive definite function. The function $2u_1 v_1 + 3u_2 v_2$ is symmetric and bilinear, and $\langle v, v \rangle = 2v_1^2 + 3v_2^2$ is positive for every $v \neq 0$, therefore it is an **inner product**. The function $u_1 v_1 - u_2 v_2$ gives $\langle (0, 1), (0, 1) \rangle = -1$ and the function $u_1 v_2 + u_2 v_1$ gives $\langle (1, -1), (1, -1) \rangle = -2$, therefore neither of the two is positive definite. The function $u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + 1$ is not bilinear, because it equals $1$ when $u$ is the zero vector.
