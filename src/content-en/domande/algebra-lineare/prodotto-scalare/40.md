The matrix $Q = \dfrac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -4 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ is orthogonal. What is the solution of the system $Qx = b$ with $b = (1, 2)$?

- $x = (-1, 2)$
- $x = \left( \dfrac{11}{5}, -\dfrac{2}{5} \right)$
- $x = \left( \dfrac{2}{5}, \dfrac{11}{5} \right)$
- $x = \left( \dfrac{11}{5}, \dfrac{2}{5} \right)$

## Explanation

Since $Q$ is orthogonal, its inverse is its transpose, therefore $x = Q^{-1} b = Q^T b$. One obtains
$$
x = \frac{1}{5}
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & 4 \\
-4 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
2
\end{pmatrix}
= \frac{1}{5}
\begin{pmatrix}
11 \\
2
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
Therefore $x = \left( \dfrac{11}{5}, \dfrac{2}{5} \right)$. The vector $(-1, 2)$ is the product $Qb$.
