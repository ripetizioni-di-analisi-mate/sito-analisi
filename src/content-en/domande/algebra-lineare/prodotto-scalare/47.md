On the space $\mathbb{R}_2[x]$ of polynomials of degree at most $2$ consider the inner product
$$
\langle p, q \rangle = \int_{-1}^{1} p(x)\, q(x)\, dx .
$$
For which $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the polynomial $p(x) = x^2 + k$ orthogonal to the constant polynomial $1$?

- $k = -\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $k = -\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $k = \dfrac{1}{3}$
- $k = -3$

## Explanation

By the definition of the inner product one has
$$
\langle x^2 + k, 1 \rangle = \int_{-1}^{1} \left( x^2 + k \right) dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} + kx \right]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{2}{3} + 2k .
$$
The two polynomials are orthogonal if and only if this number is zero, that is, if $\dfrac{2}{3} + 2k = 0$. Hence $k = -\dfrac{1}{3}$, and the polynomial orthogonal to $1$ is $x^2 - \dfrac{1}{3}$.
