Let $A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be a matrix that is both symmetric and orthogonal. Which equality holds for every matrix with these properties?

- $A = I_n$
- $A^2 = I_n$
- $A^2 = A$
- $A^T = -A$

## Explanation

Since $A$ is orthogonal, $A^T A = I_n$. Since $A$ is symmetric, $A^T = A$, therefore the first equality becomes $A \cdot A = I_n$, that is, $A^2 = I_n$.

The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ is symmetric and orthogonal but different from $I_2$, and for it $A^2 = I_2 \neq A$. Finally $A^T = -A$ together with $A^T = A$ would give $A = 0$, which is not orthogonal.
