Let $W = \{ (x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : x - 2y + z = 0 \}$ and let $e_1 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,(1, 1, 1)$, which belongs to $W$. Which vector $e_2$ makes $\{ e_1, e_2 \}$ an orthonormal basis of $W$?

- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,(1, -2, 1)$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, -1, 0)$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\,(2, 1, 0)$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 0, -1)$

## Explanation

The vector $e_2 = (x, y, z)$ must belong to $W$, be orthogonal to $e_1$ and have norm $1$:
$$
\begin{cases}
x - 2y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
x + y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting the first equation from the second gives $3y = 0$, therefore $y = 0$ and $z = -x$. Hence $e_2$ is a multiple of $(1, 0, -1)$, and normalization gives $e_2 = \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 0, -1)$. The vector $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,(1, -2, 1)$ is orthogonal to $e_1$, but it is normal to the plane and therefore does not belong to $W$.
