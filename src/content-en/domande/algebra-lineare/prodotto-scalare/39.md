Which of the following matrices is orthogonal?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

A matrix is orthogonal if and only if its columns form an orthonormal basis. The columns of the matrix of order $3$ are $\dfrac{1}{3}\,(2, 1, 2)$, $\dfrac{1}{3}\,(-2, 2, 1)$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}\,(1, 2, -2)$; each has norm $\dfrac{\sqrt{9}}{3} = 1$, and they are pairwise orthogonal because $-4 + 2 + 2 = 0$, $2 + 2 - 4 = 0$ and $-2 + 4 - 2 = 0$. Therefore this matrix is **orthogonal**. The matrix with rows $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, 1)$ has orthogonal columns of norm $\sqrt{2}$, and the matrix $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has two equal columns. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $1$, but its second column has norm $\sqrt{5}$.
