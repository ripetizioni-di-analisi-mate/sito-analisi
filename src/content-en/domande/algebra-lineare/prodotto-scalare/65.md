On the space $\mathbb{R}_1[x]$ of polynomials of degree at most $1$ consider the inner product
$$
\langle p, q \rangle = \int_{0}^{1} p(x)\, q(x)\, dx .
$$
The Gram-Schmidt process is applied to the polynomials $p_1(x) = 1$ and $p_2(x) = x$, in the given order. Which polynomial $u_2$ is obtained at the second step, before normalization?

- $x$
- $x - 1$
- $x - \dfrac{1}{3}$
- $x - \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The second step subtracts from $p_2$ its projection onto $u_1 = p_1 = 1$:
$$
u_2 = x - \frac{\langle x, 1 \rangle}{\langle 1, 1 \rangle} \cdot 1 .
$$
Since $\langle x, 1 \rangle = \displaystyle\int_0^1 x \, dx = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\langle 1, 1 \rangle = \displaystyle\int_0^1 dx = 1$, one obtains $u_2 = x - \dfrac{1}{2}$. As a check, $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \left( x - \frac{1}{2} \right) dx = 0$, therefore $u_2$ is orthogonal to $1$. The polynomial $x$ is not, because with this inner product $\langle x, 1 \rangle \neq 0$.
