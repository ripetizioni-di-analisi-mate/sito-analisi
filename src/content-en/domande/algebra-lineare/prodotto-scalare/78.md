What is the inverse of the matrix
$$
M =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 2 \\
2 & 1 & -2 \\
2 & -2 & 1
\end{pmatrix} ?
$$

- $\dfrac{1}{9}\, M$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\, M$
- $M$
- $-\dfrac{1}{9}\, M$

## Explanation

The entry in position $(i, j)$ of $M^T M$ is the inner product of column $i$ and column $j$ of $M$. Each column has squared norm $1 + 4 + 4 = 9$, and the columns are pairwise orthogonal, because $2 + 2 - 4 = 0$, $2 - 4 + 2 = 0$ and $4 - 2 - 2 = 0$. Therefore $M^T M = 9 I_3$, that is, $\left( \dfrac{1}{9}\, M^T \right) M = I_3$.

Hence $M^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{9}\, M^T$, and since $M$ is symmetric one has $M^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{9}\, M$. The matrix $\dfrac{1}{3}\, M$ is orthogonal, but $M$ is not, because its columns have norm $3$.
