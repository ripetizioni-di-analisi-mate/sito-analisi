The angle between a nonzero vector $v$ and a plane $W$ through the origin, with $v \notin W^\perp$, is the angle between $v$ and its orthogonal projection $P_W(v)$. What is the angle between $v = (1, 0, 1)$ and the plane $W$ with equation $x - y = 0$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The orthogonal complement of $W$ is the line spanned by $n = (1, -1, 0)$. Since $\langle v, n \rangle = 1$ and $\langle n, n \rangle = 2$, the projection of $v$ onto $W$ is
$$
P_W(v) = v - \frac{1}{2}\, n = \left( \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}, 1 \right) .
$$
One has $\langle v, P_W(v) \rangle = \dfrac{3}{2}$, $\|v\| = \sqrt{2}$ and $\|P_W(v)\| = \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}$, therefore
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{\dfrac{3}{2}}{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}} = \frac{3}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} .
$$
Hence the angle between $v$ and the plane is $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is the one between $v$ and the normal vector $n$, which is the complementary angle.
