What is the orthogonal complement of the line $W = \langle (1, 2, -1) \rangle$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$?

- The line $\langle (2, -1, 0) \rangle$
- The plane $2x - y = 0$
- The plane $x + 2y - z = 0$
- The line $\langle (1, 2, -1) \rangle$

## Explanation

A vector $(x, y, z)$ is orthogonal to $W$ if and only if it is orthogonal to the spanning vector $(1, 2, -1)$, that is, if $x + 2y - z = 0$. Therefore $W^\perp$ is the plane with equation $x + 2y - z = 0$, in agreement with $\dim W^\perp = 3 - 1 = 2$. The line $\langle (2, -1, 0) \rangle$ is contained in $W^\perp$, but it has dimension $1$.
