The matrix
$$
Q = \frac{1}{3}
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & -1 & 2 \\
2 & 2 & -1 \\
-1 & 2 & 2
\end{pmatrix}
$$
is orthogonal, has determinant $1$ and satisfies $Q\,(1, 1, 1) = (1, 1, 1)$. Hence it represents a rotation of $\mathbb{R}^3$ about the line $\langle (1, 1, 1) \rangle$. What is the measure $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ of the angle of rotation?

- $\arccos \dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

A vector $w$ orthogonal to the axis rotates in the plane orthogonal to the axis, therefore the angle between $w$ and $Qw$ is the angle of rotation. The vector $w = (1, -1, 0)$ is orthogonal to $(1, 1, 1)$, and the row-by-column product gives
$$
Qw = \frac{1}{3}\,(2 + 1,\ 2 - 2,\ -1 - 2) = (1, 0, -1) .
$$
Since $Q$ preserves norms, $\|Qw\| = \|w\| = \sqrt{2}$, and $\langle w, Qw \rangle = 1$. Hence
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{\langle w, Qw \rangle}{\|w\|\, \|Qw\|} = \frac{1}{2} ,
$$
that is, $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The value $\arccos \dfrac{2}{3}$ is the angle between $e_1$ and $Q e_1 = \dfrac{1}{3}\,(2, 2, -1)$, which is not orthogonal to the axis.
