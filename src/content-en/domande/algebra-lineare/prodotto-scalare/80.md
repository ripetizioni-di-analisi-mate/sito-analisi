Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be a symmetric matrix with characteristic polynomial $p_A(\lambda) = -(\lambda - 2)^2 (\lambda + 1)$. What is $\dim V_2$?

- $1$
- $1$ or $2$, depending on the matrix $A$
- $2$
- $3$

## Explanation

By the **spectral theorem** every real symmetric matrix is diagonalizable. By the diagonalizability criterion, then, every eigenvalue has geometric multiplicity equal to its algebraic multiplicity. The eigenvalue $2$ has algebraic multiplicity $2$, therefore $\dim V_2 = 2$.

For a non-symmetric matrix with the same characteristic polynomial the dimension can be $1$: for instance the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ has $V_2 = \langle (1, 0, 0) \rangle$.
