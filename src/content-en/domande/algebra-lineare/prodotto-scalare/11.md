- $(a, b, c) = (2, -2, 1)$
- $(a, b, c) = (-6, 6, -3)$
- $(a, b, c) = \left( -\dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, -\dfrac{1}{3} \right)$
- $(a, b, c) = (-2, 2, -1)$

## Explanation

The columns of an orthogonal matrix form an orthonormal basis, and the first two columns $\dfrac{1}{3}\,(1, 2, 2)$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}\,(2, 1, -2)$ are already orthogonal unit vectors. The third column $\dfrac{1}{3}\,(a, b, c)$ must be orthogonal to the first two and have norm $1$:
$$
\begin{cases}
a + 2b + 2c = 0 \\[4pt]
2a + b - 2c = 0 \\[4pt]
a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 9
\end{cases}
$$
The first two equations give $(a, b, c) = t\,(-2, 2, -1)$, and the third gives $t = 1$ or $t = -1$. For $t = 1$ one obtains $\det A = 1$, while for $t = -1$ one obtains $\det A = -1$, therefore $(a, b, c) = (-2, 2, -1)$.
