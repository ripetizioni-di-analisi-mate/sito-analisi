A symmetric matrix $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ has the eigenvalue $3$, with eigenspace the plane $x + y + z = 0$, and the eigenvalue $-3$, with eigenspace $\langle (1, 1, 1) \rangle$. What is $A\,(1, 0, 0)$?

- $(-1, 2, 2)$
- $(3, 0, 0)$
- $(2, -1, -1)$
- $(1, -2, -2)$

## Explanation

One decomposes $v = (1, 0, 0)$ as the sum of a vector of $V_{-3}$ and a vector of $V_3$, which is the orthogonal complement of $V_{-3}$. The component along $n = (1, 1, 1)$ is $\operatorname{pr}_n(v) = \dfrac{1}{3}\,(1, 1, 1)$, and the component in the plane is $v - \operatorname{pr}_n(v) = \left( \dfrac{2}{3}, -\dfrac{1}{3}, -\dfrac{1}{3} \right)$. By linearity
$$
Av = -3 \cdot \frac{1}{3}\,(1, 1, 1) + 3 \left( \frac{2}{3}, -\frac{1}{3}, -\frac{1}{3} \right) = (-1, -1, -1) + (2, -1, -1) = (1, -2, -2) .
$$
The vector $(-1, 2, 2)$ is obtained by swapping the two eigenvalues.
