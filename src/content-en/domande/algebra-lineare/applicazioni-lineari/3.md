- $\ker f = \mathbb{R}^3$ and $\operatorname{Im} f = \{(0, 0, 0)\}$
- $\ker f = \{(0, 0, 0)\}$ and $\operatorname{Im} f$ is a plane
- $\ker f = \{(0, 0, 0)\}$ and $\operatorname{Im} f = \mathbb{R}^3$
- $\ker f$ is a line and $\operatorname{Im} f = \mathbb{R}^3$

## Explanation

The matrix of $f$ with respect to the standard basis is
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & -2 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 3 \\
1 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
Expanding along the first row gives $\det A = 1 \cdot (1 - 0) + 2 \cdot (0 - 3) = -5$. Since the determinant is nonzero, $f$ is an **isomorphism**. Then $f$ is injective, that is, $\ker f = \{(0, 0, 0)\}$, and it is surjective, that is, $\operatorname{Im} f = \mathbb{R}^3$. The pairs with zero kernel and a plane as image, or with a line as kernel and image $\mathbb{R}^3$, are also ruled out by the **rank-nullity theorem**, because the sum of the dimensions must be $3$.
