Let $A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be similar matrices. Which statement is necessarily true?

- $A + I_n$ is similar to $B$
- $A$ and $B$ have the same entries on the main diagonal
- $AB = BA$
- $A^2$ is similar to $B^2$

## Explanation

Let $B = P^{-1} A P$ with $P$ invertible. Then
$$
B^2 = P^{-1} A P\, P^{-1} A P = P^{-1} A^2 P ,
$$
so $A^2$ is **similar** to $B^2$ with the same matrix $P$. The other statements are not always true. Similar matrices have the same trace, while $\operatorname{tr}(A + I_n) = \operatorname{tr} B + n$, therefore $A + I_n$ is not similar to $B$. With $Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ one has
$$
Q^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
and the two matrices satisfy $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, so they do not commute. Finally $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ are similar through the matrix that swaps the vectors of the standard basis, and they have different diagonals.
