Let $M : \mathbb{R}_1[x] \to \mathbb{R}_2[x]$, $M(p) = x\, p(x)$, and $D : \mathbb{R}_2[x] \to \mathbb{R}_1[x]$, $D(p) = p'$. What is the matrix of $D \circ M$ with respect to the basis $(1, x)$ of $\mathbb{R}_1[x]$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The map $D \circ M$ goes from $\mathbb{R}_1[x]$ to itself and acts by first multiplying by $x$ and then differentiating. On the basis vectors one gets $(D \circ M)(1) = (x)' = 1$ and $(D \circ M)(x) = (x^2)' = 2x$. The coordinates $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$ are the columns of the matrix, so the matrix is diagonal with entries $1$ and $2$. The composition is not the identity, since the derivative of the product $x\, p(x)$ is $p(x) + x\, p'(x)$.
