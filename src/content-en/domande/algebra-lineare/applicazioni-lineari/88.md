Let $f_k$ be the endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$ defined by $f_k(x, y) = (kx + y,\ -4x - ky)$, with $k \in \mathbb{R}$. For which values of $k$ is the composition $f_k \circ f_k$ the zero map?

- Only for $k = 2$
- Only for $k = 0$
- For $k = 2$ and for $k = -2$
- For no value of $k$

## Explanation

The matrix of $f_k$ with respect to the standard basis is $A_k = \begin{pmatrix} k & 1 \\ -4 & -k \end{pmatrix}$. By the **matrix of the composition** theorem, $f_k \circ f_k$ has matrix
$$
A_k^2 = \begin{pmatrix} k^2 - 4 & k - k \\ -4k + 4k & -4 + k^2 \end{pmatrix} = (k^2 - 4)\, I_2 .
$$
Hence $f_k \circ f_k$ is the zero map if and only if $k^2 = 4$, that is, for $k = 2$ and for $k = -2$. For $k = 0$ one gets $A_0^2 = -4 I_2$, therefore $f_0 \circ f_0 = -4\, \operatorname{id}$.
