What is the inverse of the isomorphism $f(x, y) = (2x + y,\ 3x + 2y)$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $f^{-1}(x, y) = (2x - 3y,\ -x + 2y)$
- $f^{-1}(x, y) = (-2x + y,\ 3x - 2y)$
- $f^{-1}(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{x}{2} + y,\ \dfrac{x}{3} + \dfrac{y}{2} \right)$
- $f^{-1}(x, y) = (2x - y,\ -3x + 2y)$

## Explanation

The matrix of $f$ with respect to the standard basis is $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, with $\det A = 4 - 3 = 1$. By the matrix of the inverse map, the matrix of $f^{-1}$ is $A^{-1}$. For a $2 \times 2$ matrix the inverse is obtained by swapping the diagonal entries, changing the sign of the other two and dividing by the determinant, so
$$
A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Therefore $f^{-1}(x, y) = (2x - y,\ -3x + 2y)$.
