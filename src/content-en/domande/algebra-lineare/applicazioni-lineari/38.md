Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $f(x, y) = (x + y,\ x - y)$. What is the matrix of $f$ with respect to the standard basis in the domain and the basis $\mathcal{C} = \big( (1, 1), (1, -1) \big)$ in the codomain?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The columns of the matrix are the coordinates with respect to $\mathcal{C}$ of the images of the standard basis vectors. We have $f(1, 0) = (1, 1)$, which is the first vector of $\mathcal{C}$, so it has coordinates $(1, 0)$. Next, $f(0, 1) = (1, -1)$, which is the second vector of $\mathcal{C}$, so it has coordinates $(0, 1)$. Therefore the matrix is the **identity matrix**, even though $f$ is not the identity. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ is that of $f$ with respect to the standard bases.
