In $\mathbb{R}^2$ consider the bases $\mathcal{B} = \big( (1, 0), (1, 1) \big)$ and $\mathcal{B}' = \big( (1, 2), (0, 1) \big)$. What is the change-of-basis matrix $P = M^{\mathcal{B}'}_{\mathcal{B}}(\operatorname{id})$, which turns coordinates with respect to $\mathcal{B}'$ into coordinates with respect to $\mathcal{B}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The columns of $P$ are the coordinates with respect to $\mathcal{B}$ of the vectors of $\mathcal{B}'$. From $(1, 2) = a\,(1, 0) + b\,(1, 1)$ it follows that $b = 2$ and $a = 1 - b = -1$, so the first column is $(-1, 2)$. From $(0, 1) = a\,(1, 0) + b\,(1, 1)$ it follows that $b = 1$ and $a = -1$, so the second column is $(-1, 1)$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has the vectors of $\mathcal{B}'$ as columns and gives the coordinates with respect to the standard basis, while $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ is the inverse of $P$ and converts coordinates in the opposite direction.
