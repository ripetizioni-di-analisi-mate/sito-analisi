For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the endomorphism $f(x, y, z) = (x + y,\ y + kz,\ kx + z)$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ an isomorphism?

- For $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq -1$
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$
- For $k \neq 0$
- For $k \neq -1$

## Explanation

The matrix of $f$ with respect to the standard basis is
$$
A_k =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & k \\
k & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
Expanding along the first row one obtains $\det A_k = 1 \cdot (1 - 0) - 1 \cdot (0 - k^2) = 1 + k^2$, where the second term $-1 \cdot (0 - k^2) = k^2$ has a positive sign. Since $1 + k^2 \geq 1$ for every $k$, the determinant never vanishes. Therefore $f$ is an **isomorphism** for every $k \in \mathbb{R}$.
