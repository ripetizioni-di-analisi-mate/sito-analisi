Let $T : \mathbb{R}_2[x] \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be the linear map $T(p) = \big( p(0),\ p'(2) \big)$. Which statement is true?

- $T$ is injective and not surjective
- $T$ is surjective and $\ker T = \langle x^2 - 4x \rangle$
- $T$ is surjective and $\ker T = \langle x^2 - 2x \rangle$
- $T$ is not surjective, because $\dim \mathbb{R}_2[x] \neq \dim \mathbb{R}^2$

## Explanation

The images of the vectors of the basis $(1, x, x^2)$ are $T(1) = (1, 0)$, $T(x) = (0, 1)$ and $T(x^2) = (0, 4)$, since the derivative of $x^2$ is $2x$, which equals $4$ at $x = 2$. The vectors $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ span $\mathbb{R}^2$, so $\operatorname{rk} T = 2$ and $T$ is **surjective**. By the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \ker T = 3 - 2 = 1$, therefore $T$ is not injective. A polynomial $p(x) = a + bx + cx^2$ lies in the kernel if and only if $p(0) = a = 0$ and $p'(2) = b + 4c = 0$. With $c = 1$ we get $b = -4$, so $\ker T = \langle x^2 - 4x \rangle$. The polynomial $x^2 - 2x$ has derivative $2x - 2$, which equals $2$ at $x = 2$, therefore it does not belong to the kernel.
