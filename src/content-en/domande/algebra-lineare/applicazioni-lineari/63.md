For the endomorphism $f(x, y) = (x - y,\ x - y)$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$, which statement is true?

- $\ker f \cap \operatorname{Im} f = \{(0, 0)\}$
- $\ker f = \operatorname{Im} f$
- $\operatorname{Im} f = \langle (1, -1) \rangle$
- $f \circ f = f$

## Explanation

One has $f(x, y) = (x - y)\,(1, 1)$, so $\operatorname{Im} f = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$. The kernel is given by $x - y = 0$, so $\ker f = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$, and therefore $\ker f = \operatorname{Im} f$. Consequently the intersection is the line $\langle (1, 1) \rangle$ and is not just the zero vector. Since every vector of the image lies in the kernel, $f(f(v)) = (0, 0)$ for every $v$, that is, $f \circ f$ is the zero map, which is different from $f$.
