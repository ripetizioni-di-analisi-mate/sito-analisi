Let $f$ be an endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^3$ such that $f(e_1) = f(e_2)$, where $(e_1, e_2, e_3)$ is the standard basis. Which statement is necessarily true?

- $f$ is injective and not surjective
- $f$ is surjective and not injective
- $f$ is an isomorphism for a suitable choice of $f(e_3)$
- $f$ is not surjective

## Explanation

By linearity $f(e_1 - e_2) = f(e_1) - f(e_2) = 0$, and $e_1 - e_2$ is not the zero vector, so $\ker f \neq \{0\}$ and $f$ is not injective. For an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional space injectivity and surjectivity are equivalent, by the consequences of the **rank-nullity theorem**. Therefore $f$ **is not surjective**, whatever $f(e_3)$ is, and in particular it is not an isomorphism.
