Let $f$ be the endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^3$ defined by $f(x, y, z) = (y,\ z,\ 0)$. What is the map $f \circ f$?

- $(f \circ f)(x, y, z) = (0, 0, 0)$
- $(f \circ f)(x, y, z) = (y,\ z,\ 0)$
- $(f \circ f)(x, y, z) = (0,\ x,\ y)$
- $(f \circ f)(x, y, z) = (z,\ 0,\ 0)$

## Explanation

One applies $f$ to the vector $f(x, y, z) = (y, z, 0)$. The map $f$ shifts every component one place to the left and puts $0$ in the last one, so
$$
(f \circ f)(x, y, z) = f(y, z, 0) = (z,\ 0,\ 0) .
$$
Applying $f$ a third time gives $f(z, 0, 0) = (0, 0, 0)$, therefore the zero map is $f \circ f \circ f$. The map $(0, x, y)$ shifts the components in the opposite direction and its matrix is the transpose of the matrix of $f$.
