Let $f(x, y) = (x + y,\ 2x + 2y)$ be an endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$ and let $r$ be the line $x + y = 1$. What is the set $f(r) = \{ f(v) : v \in r \}$?

::grafico{f="1-x" xmin=-1.5 xmax=2.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=2.5 uguale=1 etichette="1.9,-0.55,r" titolo="The line $r : x + y = 1$"}

- The point $(1, 2)$
- The line $y = 2x$
- The line $x + y = 1$
- The point $(0, 0)$

## Explanation

One has $f(x, y) = (x + y)\,(1, 2)$, so the image of a vector depends only on the sum $x + y$. For every point of $r$ the sum equals $1$, therefore every point of $r$ has image $(1, 2)$ and $f(r)$ is the **single point** $(1, 2)$. The result has a geometric reading. The direction of $r$ is $(1, -1)$, which spans $\ker f$, and two points of $r$ differ by a vector of the kernel, so they have the same image. The line $y = 2x$ is the image of the whole plane.
