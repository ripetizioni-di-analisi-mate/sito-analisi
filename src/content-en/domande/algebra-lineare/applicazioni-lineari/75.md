Let $f$ be an isomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$ with $f(1, 0) = (2, -1)$. Which of the following vectors cannot be $f(0, 1)$?

- $(4, 2)$
- $(-4, 2)$
- $(-2, -1)$
- $(1, 0)$

## Explanation

An isomorphism is injective, so it sends linearly independent vectors to linearly independent vectors. Since $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ are independent, $f(1, 0)$ and $f(0, 1)$ must be independent too. The vector $(-4, 2) = -2\,(2, -1)$ is proportional to $f(1, 0)$, therefore it cannot be $f(0, 1)$. For the other three vectors the determinant of the matrix with columns $(2, -1)$ and $f(0, 1)$ equals $8$, $-4$ and $1$ respectively, so the matrix is invertible and the corresponding map is an isomorphism.
