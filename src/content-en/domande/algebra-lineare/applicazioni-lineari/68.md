Let $f(x, y) = (x + 2y,\ 2x + 4y)$ be an endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$. Which of the following pairs consists of two distinct vectors with the same image?

- $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 1)$
- $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 2)$
- $(3, 0)$ and $(1, 1)$
- $(1, -2)$ and $(-1, 2)$

## Explanation

By linearity $f(u) = f(v)$ if and only if $f(u - v) = 0$, that is, if and only if $u - v \in \ker f$. The kernel is given by $x + 2y = 0$, so $\ker f = \langle (2, -1) \rangle$. The difference $(3, 0) - (1, 1) = (2, -1)$ lies in the kernel, and indeed $f(3, 0) = f(1, 1) = (3, 6)$. The differences of the other pairs are $(-1, 1)$, $(-1, -2)$ and $(2, -4)$, which are not multiples of $(2, -1)$.
