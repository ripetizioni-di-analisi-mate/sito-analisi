Let $f$ and $g$ be endomorphisms of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $\dim \operatorname{Im} f = \dim \operatorname{Im} g = 2$. Which values can $\dim \operatorname{Im}(g \circ f)$ take?

- Only $2$
- $1$ or $2$
- $0$, $1$ or $2$
- $2$ or $3$

## Explanation

The image of $g \circ f$ is $g(\operatorname{Im} f)$, so its dimension does not exceed $\dim \operatorname{Im} f = 2$. By the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \ker g = 1$. The theorem applied to the restriction of $g$ to the plane $\operatorname{Im} f$ gives $\dim g(\operatorname{Im} f) = 2 - \dim(\ker g \cap \operatorname{Im} f) \geq 2 - 1 = 1$. Both values occur. With $f = g$ both equal to $(x, y, z) \mapsto (x, y, 0)$ one has $g \circ f = f$, of rank $2$. With $f(x, y, z) = (x, y, 0)$ and $g(x, y, z) = (0, y, z)$ one has $(g \circ f)(x, y, z) = (0, y, 0)$, of rank $1$.
