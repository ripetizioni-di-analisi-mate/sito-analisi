For which of the following choices of the spaces $V$ and $W$ does a surjective linear map $f : V \to W$ exist?

- $V = \mathbb{R}^2$ and $W = \mathbb{R}_2[x]$
- $V = \mathbb{R}_2[x]$ and $W = M_2(\mathbb{R})$
- $V = M_2(\mathbb{R})$ and $W = \mathbb{R}_2[x]$
- $V = \mathbb{R}_3[x]$ and $W = \mathbb{R}^5$

## Explanation

If $f$ is surjective, then $\dim \operatorname{Im} f = \dim W$. By the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \operatorname{Im} f = \dim V - \dim \ker f \leq \dim V$, so a necessary condition is $\dim W \leq \dim V$. The dimensions of the four pairs are $2$ and $3$, $3$ and $4$, $4$ and $3$, $4$ and $5$, therefore the condition is satisfied only for $V = M_2(\mathbb{R})$ and $W = \mathbb{R}_2[x]$. In this case the map
$$
f \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = a + bx + cx^2
$$
is linear and surjective, because every polynomial $a + bx + cx^2$ is the image of the matrix with $d = 0$.
