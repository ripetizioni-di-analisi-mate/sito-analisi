Let $f_k$ be the endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with matrix
$$
A_k =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 1 & 1 \\
k & 1 & 1
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad k \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
Which statement is true?

- $f_k$ is an isomorphism for $k \neq 1$
- $f_k$ is an isomorphism for $k \neq 0$
- $f_k$ is not an isomorphism for any value of $k$
- $\dim \ker f_k = 1$ for every $k$

## Explanation

The first two rows of $A_k$ are equal, so $\det A_k = 0$ for every $k$. By the theorem on **isomorphisms and determinant**, $f_k$ is not an isomorphism for any value of $k$. For $k \neq 1$ the third row $(k, 1, 1)$ is not proportional to $(1, 1, 1)$, so the rank is $2$ and $\dim \ker f_k = 1$. For $k = 1$ the three rows are equal, so the rank is $1$ and $\dim \ker f_1 = 2$.
