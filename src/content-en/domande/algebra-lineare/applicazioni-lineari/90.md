For $k \in \mathbb{R}$ let $f_k : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be the linear map
$$
f_k(x, y, z) = (x + y + z,\ x + y + z,\ kx + y + z) .
$$
Which subspace is the intersection of the kernels $\ker f_k$ as $k$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$?

- $\langle (1, 0, -1) \rangle$
- The plane $x + y + z = 0$
- $\langle (0, 1, -1) \rangle$
- $\{ 0 \}$

## Explanation

The first two components of $f_k$ coincide, so $(x, y, z) \in \ker f_k$ if and only if $x + y + z = 0$ and $kx + y + z = 0$. Subtracting the first equation from the second gives $(k - 1)x = 0$. A vector that lies in the kernel of *every* $f_k$ lies in particular in the kernel of $f_0$, therefore $x = 0$, and then the first equation gives $y + z = 0$. Conversely, every vector $(0, t, -t)$ satisfies both equations for every $k$. Hence the intersection of the kernels is the line $\langle (0, 1, -1) \rangle$. The plane $x + y + z = 0$ is the kernel of $f_1$ alone, and the vector $(1, 0, -1)$ belongs to $\ker f_k$ only for $k = 1$.
