Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be linear with $f(1, 1) = (3, 1)$ and $f(1, -1) = (1, -1)$. What is $f(3, 1)$?

- $(5, -1)$
- $(7, 1)$
- $(10, 2)$
- $(5, 3)$

## Explanation

The vectors $(1, 1)$ and $(1, -1)$ are not proportional, so they form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $f$ is determined by the assigned values. One writes $(3, 1) = a\,(1, 1) + b\,(1, -1)$, that is,
$$
\begin{cases}
a + b = 3 \\[4pt]
a - b = 1
\end{cases}
$$
which gives $a = 2$ and $b = 1$. By linearity $f(3, 1) = 2\, f(1, 1) + f(1, -1) = (6, 2) + (1, -1) = (7, 1)$.
