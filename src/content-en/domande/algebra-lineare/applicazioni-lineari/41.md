The linear maps $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ and $g : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ have, with respect to the standard bases, the matrices
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
2 & 1 \\
0 & 1
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
B =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & -1
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is the matrix of $g \circ f$ with respect to the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

By the **formula for the matrix of a composition**, the matrix of $g \circ f$ is $BA$, with the matrix of $f$, which is applied first, on the right. The product of a $2 \times 3$ matrix by a $3 \times 2$ matrix is a $2 \times 2$ matrix, in agreement with $g \circ f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$. Multiplying rows by columns one obtains
$$
BA =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 + 2 + 0 & 0 + 1 + 0 \\
0 + 2 + 0 & 0 + 1 - 1
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The product $AB$, of order $3 \times 3$, is the matrix of $f \circ g$.
