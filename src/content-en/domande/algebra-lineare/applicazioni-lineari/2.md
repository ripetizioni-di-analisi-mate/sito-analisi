- Only for $k = 0$
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$
- Only for $k \neq 0$
- For no value of $k$

## Explanation

For every $k$ one has $f_k(0, 0) = (0, 0)$, so the necessary condition $f(0) = 0$ is satisfied and is not enough to decide. For $k \neq 0$ one compares $f_k(0, 2) = (4k, -2)$ and $2\, f_k(0, 1) = 2\,(k, -1) = (2k, -2)$. Since $4k \neq 2k$, the second condition of the definition is not satisfied and $f_k$ **is not linear**. For $k = 0$ one has $f_0(x, y) = (x,\ 2x - y)$, whose components are polynomials of degree one with no constant term, therefore $f_0$ is **linear**.
