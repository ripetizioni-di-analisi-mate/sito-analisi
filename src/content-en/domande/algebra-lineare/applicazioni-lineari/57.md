Let $a = (1, -1, 2)$ and $b = (2, 1, 0)$, and let $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be the linear map $f(v) = \langle v, a \rangle\, b$, where $\langle v, a \rangle$ is the standard inner product. Which statement is true?

- $\ker f$ is the plane $2x + y = 0$ and $\operatorname{Im} f = \langle (1, -1, 2) \rangle$
- $\ker f = \langle (1, -1, 2) \rangle$ and $\operatorname{Im} f$ is the plane $2x + y = 0$
- $\ker f = \{(0, 0, 0)\}$ and $\operatorname{Im} f = \langle (2, 1, 0) \rangle$
- $\ker f$ is the plane $x - y + 2z = 0$ and $\operatorname{Im} f = \langle (2, 1, 0) \rangle$

## Explanation

Every value $f(v)$ is a multiple of $b$, and $f(a) = \langle a, a \rangle\, b = 6\, b$ is nonzero, so $\operatorname{Im} f = \langle (2, 1, 0) \rangle$. Since $b \neq 0$, one has $f(v) = 0$ if and only if $\langle v, a \rangle = 0$, that is, $x - y + 2z = 0$. Therefore the kernel is the plane of the vectors orthogonal to $a$, in agreement with the **rank-nullity theorem** $3 = 2 + 1$. The statements with $a$ and $b$ swapped describe the map $v \mapsto \langle v, b \rangle\, a$.
