Let $f : U \to V$ and $g : V \to W$ be linear maps such that $g \circ f$ is injective. Which statement is necessarily true?

- $g$ is injective
- $f$ is injective
- $g$ is surjective
- $f$ is surjective

## Explanation

If $f(u) = 0$, then $(g \circ f)(u) = g(0) = 0$, so $u \in \ker(g \circ f) = \{0\}$. Therefore $\ker f = \{0\}$ and $f$ is **injective**. The other statements are not necessarily true. With $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $f(t) = (t, 0)$, and $g : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $g(x, y) = (x, 0)$, one has $(g \circ f)(t) = (t, 0)$, which is injective. In this example $g$ is not injective, since $g(0, 1) = (0, 0)$, $g$ is not surjective, and $f$ is not surjective.
