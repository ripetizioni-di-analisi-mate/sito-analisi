Let $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $f(x, y, z) = (x + y,\ y + z)$. What is the set of vectors $v \in \mathbb{R}^3$ such that $f(v) = (1, 2)$?

- $\{ (-1, 2, 0) + t\,(1, -1, 1) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}$
- $\{ t\,(1, -1, 1) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}$
- $\{ (1, 2, 0) + t\,(1, -1, 1) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}$
- $\{ (-1, 2, 0) + t\,(1, 1, 1) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}$

## Explanation

The equality $f(x, y, z) = (1, 2)$ is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 1 \\[4pt]
y + z = 2
\end{cases}
$$
Setting $z = t$, the second equation gives $y = 2 - t$ and the first gives $x = 1 - y = t - 1$. Therefore the solutions are $(t - 1,\ 2 - t,\ t) = (-1, 2, 0) + t\,(1, -1, 1)$, that is, a particular solution plus the kernel $\ker f = \langle (1, -1, 1) \rangle$. The kernel alone is the solution set of $f(v) = (0, 0)$, and one has $f(1, 2, 0) = (3, 2)$ and $f(1, 1, 1) = (2, 2)$.
