Let $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be linear with
$$
f(1, 1, 0) = (0, 0), \qquad f(0, 1, 1) = (1, 2), \qquad f(1, 0, 0) = (2, 4) .
$$
Which of the following is a basis of $\ker f$?

- $(1, 1, 0)$
- $(1, 1, 0),\ (1, 2, 2)$
- $(1, 1, 0),\ (-1, 2, 2)$
- $(0, 1, 1),\ (1, 0, 0)$

## Explanation

The three vectors form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$, because the determinant of the matrix having them as columns equals $1$, so $\operatorname{Im} f$ is spanned by $(0, 0)$, $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 4)$. These vectors are all multiples of $(1, 2)$, therefore $\dim \operatorname{Im} f = 1$ and by the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \ker f = 3 - 1 = 2$. The vector $(1, 1, 0)$ lies in the kernel. Since $f(1, 0, 0) = 2\, f(0, 1, 1)$, by linearity
$$
f\big( 2\,(0, 1, 1) - (1, 0, 0) \big) = (2, 4) - (2, 4) = (0, 0) ,
$$
so $(-1, 2, 2)$ also lies in the kernel. The two vectors are not proportional, therefore they form a basis of $\ker f$. The vector $(1, 2, 2) = (1, 0, 0) + 2\,(0, 1, 1)$ has image $(4, 8)$.
