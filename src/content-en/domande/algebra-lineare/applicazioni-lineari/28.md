For $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$, $f(x, y) = (x - y,\ 2x - 2y,\ 3x - 3y)$, which statement is true?

- $f$ is injective and not surjective
- $f$ is surjective and not injective
- $f$ is injective and surjective
- $f$ is neither injective nor surjective

## Explanation

One has $f(x, y) = (x - y)\,(1, 2, 3)$, so the image is the line spanned by $(1, 2, 3)$ and the matrix has rank $1$. The rank is less than $3$, the number of rows, therefore $f$ **is not surjective**, like every linear map from $\mathbb{R}^2$ to $\mathbb{R}^3$. The rank is less than $2$, the number of columns, therefore $f$ **is not injective**. Indeed, the nonzero vector $(1, 1)$ belongs to the kernel, since $f(1, 1) = (0, 0, 0)$.
