Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined by $f(x, y) = \sqrt[3]{x^3 + y^3}$. Which statement is true?

- It is linear, because $f(\lambda v) = \lambda f(v)$ for every $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ and every $v \in \mathbb{R}^2$
- It is linear, because $f(0, 0) = 0$
- It is not linear, because $f(1, 0) + f(0, 1) \neq f(1, 1)$
- It is not linear, because $f(-v) \neq -f(v)$ for some $v \in \mathbb{R}^2$

## Explanation

Since $\sqrt[3]{\lambda^3 (x^3 + y^3)} = \lambda \sqrt[3]{x^3 + y^3}$, the equality $f(\lambda v) = \lambda f(v)$ holds for every $\lambda$, and in particular $f(0, 0) = 0$ and $f(-v) = -f(v)$. Linearity, however, also requires additivity. One has $f(1, 0) = 1$ and $f(0, 1) = 1$, while $f(1, 1) = \sqrt[3]{2}$, which is different from $2$. Therefore $f$ **is not linear**, and the conditions $f(0, 0) = 0$ and $f(\lambda v) = \lambda f(v)$ alone are not enough to guarantee linearity.
