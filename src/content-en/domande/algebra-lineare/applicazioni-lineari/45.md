Let $f_k$ be the endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with matrix
$$
A_k =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & k \\
1 & k & 1 \\
k & 1 & 1
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does one have $\dim \ker f_k = 2$?

- $k = -2$
- $k = 1$
- $k = 2$
- $k = -1$

## Explanation

Expanding the determinant one obtains $\det A_k = -k^3 + 3k - 2 = -(k - 1)^2 (k + 2)$, so for $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq -2$ the endomorphism is an isomorphism and $\dim \ker f_k = 0$. For $k = 1$ all rows are equal to $(1, 1, 1)$, so the rank is $1$ and by the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \ker f_1 = 3 - 1 = 2$. For $k = -2$ the first two rows $(1, 1, -2)$ and $(1, -2, 1)$ are not proportional, so the rank is $2$ and $\dim \ker f_{-2} = 1$. Therefore the required value is $k = 1$.
