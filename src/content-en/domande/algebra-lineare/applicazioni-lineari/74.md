Let $N = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Which of the following linear maps $M_2(\mathbb{R}) \to M_2(\mathbb{R})$ is an isomorphism?

- $A \mapsto A^T$
- $A \mapsto A + A^T$
- $A \mapsto (\operatorname{tr} A)\, I_2$
- $A \mapsto A N$

## Explanation

Transposition satisfies $(A^T)^T = A$, so it is bijective and is its own inverse; therefore it is an **isomorphism**. For the other three a nonzero vector of the kernel is enough. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ satisfies $A + A^T = 0$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ has zero trace. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ satisfies $A N = 0$, since the first row of $A N$ is $(1 - 1,\ 1 - 1) = (0, 0)$ and the second is zero. These three maps are not injective, so they are not isomorphisms.
