Let $f_k$ be the endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with matrix
$$
A_k =
\begin{pmatrix}
k & 1 & 1 \\
1 & k & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the endomorphism $f_k$ not an isomorphism?

- Only for $k = 0$
- For $k = 1$ and for $k = -1$
- For $k = 0$ and for $k = -1$
- For $k = 0$ and for $k = 1$

## Explanation

An endomorphism is an isomorphism if and only if the determinant of its matrix is nonzero. Expanding along the first column, whose entries are $k$, $1$, $0$, one obtains
$$
\det A_k = k\,(k - 1) - 1 \cdot (1 - 1) = k\,(k - 1) .
$$
The determinant vanishes for $k = 0$ and for $k = 1$, so $f_k$ is not an isomorphism exactly for these two values. Indeed, for $k = 0$ one has $\ker f_0 = \langle (1, 1, -1) \rangle$, and for $k = 1$ one has $\ker f_1 = \langle (0, 1, -1) \rangle$.
