Let $f(x, y) = (x + y,\ x - y)$ and $g(x, y) = (2x,\ x + y)$. The composition $g \circ f$ is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$. What is its inverse?

- $(g \circ f)^{-1}(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{x - y}{2},\ \dfrac{y}{2} \right)$
- $(g \circ f)^{-1}(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{y}{2},\ \dfrac{y - x}{2} \right)$
- $(g \circ f)^{-1}(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{y}{2},\ \dfrac{x - y}{2} \right)$
- $(g \circ f)^{-1}(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{x + y}{4},\ \dfrac{x - 3y}{4} \right)$

## Explanation

In the composition $f$ is applied first and then $g$, so $(g \circ f)(x, y) = g(x + y,\ x - y) = (2x + 2y,\ 2x)$. Its matrix has determinant $2 \cdot 0 - 2 \cdot 2 = -4 \neq 0$, in agreement with the fact that $g \circ f$ is an isomorphism. To find the inverse one solves the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2a + 2b = x \\[4pt]
2a = y
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation gives $a = \dfrac{y}{2}$, and substituting into the first one obtains $b = \dfrac{x - 2a}{2} = \dfrac{x - y}{2}$.
