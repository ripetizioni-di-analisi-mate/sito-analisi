Which of the following matrices is similar only to itself, that is, satisfies $P^{-1} A P = A$ for every invertible matrix $P \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 3 \\ 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

For the matrix $3 I_2$ one has $P^{-1}\,(3 I_2)\,P = 3\, P^{-1} P = 3 I_2$ for every invertible $P$, so $3 I_2$ is similar **only to itself**. For the others a matrix $P$ giving a different result is enough. With $P = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, which swaps rows and columns, one gets
$$
P^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} P = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
With $Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ one gets
$$
Q^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix} Q = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 6 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
Q^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 3 \\ 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix} Q = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 0 \\ 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
