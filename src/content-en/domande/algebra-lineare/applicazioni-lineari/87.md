Let $T_k : \mathbb{R}_2[x] \to \mathbb{R}_2[x]$ be the endomorphism $T_k(p) = x\, p'(x) + k\, p(x)$, with $k \in \mathbb{R}$. For which values of $k$ is $T_k$ not an isomorphism?

- Only for $k = 0$
- For $k = 0$, $k = -1$ and $k = -2$
- For $k = 0$, $k = 1$ and $k = 2$
- For $k = -1$ and $k = -2$

## Explanation

On the vectors of the basis $(1, x, x^2)$ one has $T_k(1) = k$, $T_k(x) = x + kx = (k + 1)\,x$ and $T_k(x^2) = 2x^2 + kx^2 = (k + 2)\,x^2$. Therefore the matrix of $T_k$ is diagonal with entries $k$, $k + 1$, $k + 2$, and its determinant equals $k\,(k + 1)(k + 2)$. By the theorem on **isomorphisms and determinant**, $T_k$ is not an isomorphism if and only if the determinant is zero, that is, for $k = 0$, $k = -1$ and $k = -2$. For $k = -1$, for example, the polynomial $x$ lies in the kernel, since $T_{-1}(x) = x - x = 0$.
