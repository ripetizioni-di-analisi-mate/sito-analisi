Let $T : \mathbb{R}_2[x] \to \mathbb{R}$ be the linear map $T(p) = p(1)$. Which of the following is a basis of $\ker T$?

- $x - 1$
- $x + 1,\ x^2 + 1$
- $x - 1,\ x^2 - 1$
- $x,\ x^2$

## Explanation

The image of $T$ is $\mathbb{R}$, since $T(1) = 1$, so by the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \ker T = 3 - 1 = 2$. The polynomials $x - 1$ and $x^2 - 1$ vanish at $1$, so they belong to $\ker T$. They have different degrees, therefore they are linearly independent, and two independent vectors in a space of dimension $2$ form a basis of it. The polynomial $x - 1$ alone spans a subspace of dimension $1$, while $x + 1$, $x^2 + 1$, $x$ and $x^2$ take the value $2$ or $1$ at $x = 1$ and do not lie in the kernel.
