Let $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $f(x, y, z) = (x + 2y,\ y - z)$, and let $W = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$. What is the preimage $f^{-1}(W) = \{ v \in \mathbb{R}^3 : f(v) \in W \}$?

- The plane $x + 3y - z = 0$
- The line $\langle (-2, 1, 1) \rangle$
- The plane $x + y + z = 0$
- The line $\langle (1, 0, -1) \rangle$

## Explanation

A vector $(a, b)$ belongs to $W$ if and only if $a = b$. Therefore $v = (x, y, z)$ belongs to $f^{-1}(W)$ if and only if $x + 2y = y - z$, that is $x + y + z = 0$. The preimage is therefore the **plane** $x + y + z = 0$, which contains $\ker f = \langle (-2, 1, 1) \rangle$ and also the vector $(1, 0, -1)$, whose image is $(1, 1)$. The plane $x + 3y - z = 0$ is instead the preimage of the line $\langle (1, -1) \rangle$. Like the kernel, which is the preimage of the zero subspace, the preimage of a subspace is a subspace.
