Let $f : \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be the linear map whose matrix with respect to the standard bases is
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 0 & 1 \\
2 & 4 & 1 & 3 \\
3 & 6 & 1 & 4
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
What is $\dim \ker f$?

- $1$
- $3$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

The third row is the sum of the first two, and the first two rows are not proportional, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. By the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \ker f = \dim \mathbb{R}^4 - \operatorname{rk} A$. The rank is subtracted from the number of columns, $4$, which is the dimension of the domain. Therefore $\dim \ker f = 4 - 2 = 2$.
