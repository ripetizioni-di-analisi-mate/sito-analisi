Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be the rotation by an angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ about the origin, which has matrix
$$
A = \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\sqrt{3} \\ \sqrt{3} & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
with respect to the standard basis. What is the matrix of $f^{-1}$ with respect to the standard basis?

- $\dfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\sqrt{3} \\ \sqrt{3} & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{3} & 1 \\ -1 & \sqrt{3} \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & \sqrt{3} \\ -\sqrt{3} & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \sqrt{3} \\ -\sqrt{3} & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The inverse of the rotation by $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is the rotation by $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, whose matrix has columns $\left( \cos\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right),\ \sin\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) \right) = \left( \dfrac{1}{2},\ -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$ and $\left( \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\ \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$. One checks it with the product
$$
\frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\sqrt{3} \\ \sqrt{3} & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\cdot
\frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \sqrt{3} \\ -\sqrt{3} & 1 \end{pmatrix}
= \frac{1}{4} \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = I_2 ,
$$
so by the **matrix of the inverse map** $A^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \sqrt{3} \\ -\sqrt{3} & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, which is the transpose of $A$. The matrix with $-1$ on the diagonal is the rotation by $-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.
