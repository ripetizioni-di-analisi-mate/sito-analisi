For $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $f(x, y, z) = (x + 2y - z,\ 2x + 4y - 2z)$, which of the following is a basis of $\ker f$?

- $(1, 2, -1)$
- $(2, 1, 0),\ (1, 0, 1)$
- $(-2, 1, 0)$
- $(-2, 1, 0),\ (1, 0, 1)$

## Explanation

The second component is twice the first, so the matrix has rank $1$ and by the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \ker f = 3 - 1 = 2$. The kernel is the plane with equation $x + 2y - z = 0$, that is, $x = -2y + z$. For $(y, z) = (1, 0)$ one obtains $(-2, 1, 0)$ and for $(y, z) = (0, 1)$ one obtains $(1, 0, 1)$, and the two vectors are not proportional. The vector $(1, 2, -1)$ contains the coefficients of the equation and does not belong to the kernel, since $1 + 4 + 1 = 6 \neq 0$.
