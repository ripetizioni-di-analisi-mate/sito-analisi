The endomorphism $f$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$ has matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ with respect to the standard basis. What is the matrix of $f$ with respect to the basis $\mathcal{B}' = \big( (1, 1), (1, -1) \big)$, used in the domain and in the codomain?

- $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The columns of the required matrix are the coordinates with respect to $\mathcal{B}'$ of the images of the vectors of $\mathcal{B}'$. One has $f(1, 1) = (3, 3) = 3\,(1, 1)$ and $f(1, -1) = (-1, 1) = -1 \cdot (1, -1)$. The coordinates are thus $(3, 0)$ and $(0, -1)$, therefore the matrix is diagonal with entries $3$ and $-1$, in this order. The same result is obtained with the change-of-basis formula $P^{-1} A P$, where $P$ has columns $(1, 1)$ and $(1, -1)$.
