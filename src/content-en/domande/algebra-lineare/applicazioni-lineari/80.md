Let $R(x, y) = (-y,\ x)$ be the rotation by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ about the origin and $S(x, y) = (x,\ -y)$ the reflection across the $x$-axis. Which transformation of the plane is $R \circ S$?

- The rotation by $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ about the origin
- The reflection across the line $y = -x$
- The reflection across the line $y = x$
- The rotation by $\pi$ about the origin

## Explanation

In the composition $R \circ S$ one applies $S$ first and then $R$, so
$$
(R \circ S)(x, y) = R(x, -y) = (y,\ x) .
$$
The map $(x, y) \mapsto (y, x)$ fixes the vectors of the line $y = x$ and changes the sign of the vector $(1, -1)$, perpendicular to that line, therefore it is the **reflection across the line** $y = x$. In the opposite order one gets $(S \circ R)(x, y) = S(-y, x) = (-y,\ -x)$, which is the reflection across the line $y = -x$. The rotation by $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is $(x, y) \mapsto (y, -x)$.
