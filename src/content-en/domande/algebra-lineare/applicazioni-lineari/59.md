Let $P : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be the orthogonal projection onto the line $r : y = 3x$. What is $\ker P$?

::grafico{f="3*x" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-2.5 ymax=3.5 uguale=1 etichette="1.25,3.3,r" titolo="The line $r : y = 3x$"}

- The line $y = 3x$
- The line $y = -\dfrac{x}{3}$
- The line $y = \dfrac{x}{3}$
- Only the zero vector

## Explanation

Every $v \in \mathbb{R}^2$ can be written as the sum of a vector of $r$ and a vector perpendicular to $r$, and $P$ keeps the first summand and sends the second to zero. Therefore $P(v) = 0$ if and only if $v$ is perpendicular to $r$. The line $r$ has direction vector $(1, 3)$ and the vector $(3, -1)$ satisfies $\langle (3, -1), (1, 3) \rangle = 0$, so $\ker P = \langle (3, -1) \rangle$, that is, the line $y = -\dfrac{x}{3}$. The line $r$ is the image of $P$; by the **rank-nullity theorem** $\dim \ker P = 2 - 1 = 1$, therefore the kernel is not just the zero vector.
