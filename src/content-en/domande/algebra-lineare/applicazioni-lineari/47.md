Let $a = (1, 2, 3)$ and let $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be the linear map $f(v) = a \times v$, where $\times$ denotes the cross product. What is the matrix of $f$ with respect to the standard basis?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 3 & -2 \\ -3 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -3 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -3 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 3 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

For $v = (x, y, z)$ the cross product is
$$
a \times v = (2z - 3y,\ 3x - z,\ y - 2x) .
$$
The $i$-th row of the matrix contains the coefficients of $x$, $y$, $z$ in the $i$-th component, so the rows are $(0, -3, 2)$, $(3, 0, -1)$ and $(-2, 1, 0)$. The matrix with opposite signs is the matrix of $v \mapsto v \times a$, since $v \times a = -\,a \times v$.
