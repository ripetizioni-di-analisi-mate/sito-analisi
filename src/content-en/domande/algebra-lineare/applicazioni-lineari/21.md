The endomorphism $f$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$ has matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ with respect to the basis $\mathcal{B} = \big( (1, 1), (0, 1) \big)$, used in the domain and in the codomain. What is $f(2, 3)$?

- $(8, 3)$
- $(4, 5)$
- $(4, 1)$
- $(8, 11)$

## Explanation

The associated matrix transforms the coordinates of $v$ into the coordinates of $f(v)$, that is, $[f(v)]_{\mathcal{B}} = A\, [v]_{\mathcal{B}}$. From $(2, 3) = 2\,(1, 1) + 1 \cdot (0, 1)$ one obtains $[v]_{\mathcal{B}} = (2, 1)$. Then $[f(v)]_{\mathcal{B}} = A\, (2, 1)^T = (4, 1)^T$, so $f(v)$ has coordinates $(4, 1)$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$. Therefore $f(2, 3) = 4\,(1, 1) + 1 \cdot (0, 1) = (4, 5)$.
