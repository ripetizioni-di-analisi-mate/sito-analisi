Let $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ and $g : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be linear maps. Which statement is true?

- $g \circ f$ is never an isomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^3$
- $g \circ f$ is an isomorphism if $f$ and $g$ both have rank $2$
- $g \circ f$ is an isomorphism if $g$ is injective
- $f \circ g$ is never an isomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$

## Explanation

Every vector $(g \circ f)(u) = g(f(u))$ belongs to $\operatorname{Im} g$, so $\operatorname{Im}(g \circ f) \subseteq \operatorname{Im} g$. Since $g$ has domain $\mathbb{R}^2$, one has $\dim \operatorname{Im} g \leq 2$, therefore $\dim \operatorname{Im}(g \circ f) \leq 2 < 3$. Then $g \circ f$ is not surjective and **is never an isomorphism** of $\mathbb{R}^3$. The composition $f \circ g$ can instead be an isomorphism, for example with $f(x, y, z) = (x, y)$ and $g(x, y) = (x, y, 0)$, for which $f \circ g$ is the identity of $\mathbb{R}^2$.
