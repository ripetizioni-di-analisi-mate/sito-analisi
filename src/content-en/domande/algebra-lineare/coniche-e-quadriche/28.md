Which of the following changes of coordinates is an orientation-preserving rigid motion?

- $x = \dfrac{5X - 12Y}{13} + 1$, $\ y = \dfrac{12X + 5Y}{13} - 2$
- $x = \dfrac{5X + 12Y}{13} + 1$, $\ y = \dfrac{12X + 5Y}{13} - 2$
- $x = \dfrac{5X + 12Y}{13} + 1$, $\ y = \dfrac{12X - 5Y}{13} - 2$
- $x = 5X - 12Y + 1$, $\ y = 12X + 5Y - 2$

## Explanation

A change of coordinates $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = R \begin{pmatrix} X \\ Y \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} x_0 \\ y_0 \end{pmatrix}$ is an orientation-preserving rigid motion if $R^T R = I_2$ and $\det R = 1$. For $R = \dfrac{1}{13} \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -12 \\ 12 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$ the columns have norm $1$ and are orthogonal, and $\det R = \dfrac{25 + 144}{169} = 1$, so the change with this matrix is an **orientation-preserving rigid motion**. The matrix $\dfrac{1}{13} \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 12 \\ 12 & -5 \end{pmatrix}$ is orthogonal but has determinant $-1$, therefore the corresponding change contains a reflection. In the matrix $\dfrac{1}{13} \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 12 \\ 12 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$ the dot product of the columns equals $\dfrac{120}{169} \neq 0$, and in the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 5 & -12 \\ 12 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$ the columns have norm $13$, so neither of the two is orthogonal.
