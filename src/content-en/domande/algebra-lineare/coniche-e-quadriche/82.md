Which set of points in space is described by the second-degree equation
$$
x^2 + 2xy + y^2 - z^2 = 0 ?
$$

- A cone
- A hyperbolic cylinder
- A pair of intersecting planes
- A hyperbolic paraboloid

## Explanation

The associated matrix has its last row and last column equal to zero, and the block $A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ has its first two rows equal. So $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$, and a quadric with $\operatorname{rk} A \leq 2$ is a pair of planes. Indeed the equation can be written as $(x + y)^2 - z^2 = 0$, that is, $(x + y - z)(x + y + z) = 0$. The planes $x + y - z = 0$ and $x + y + z = 0$ are not parallel, therefore the quadric is a **pair of intersecting planes** meeting along the line $x + y = 0$, $z = 0$. A cone would require $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$.
