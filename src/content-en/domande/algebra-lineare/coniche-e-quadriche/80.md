The quadric
$$
2x^2 + 2xy + 2y^2 + z^2 - 4x - 2y + 2z = 0
$$
has a unique center of symmetry. What are its coordinates?

- $(-1, 0, 1)$
- $(1, 0, -1)$
- $(2, 1, -1)$
- $(1, -1, 0)$

## Explanation

The block of the quadratic terms is $A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, with $\det A_0 = 3 \neq 0$, so the quadric has a unique center. With $b = (-2, -1, 1)^T$ the center solves $A_0 c = -b$, that is,
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + y - 2 = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 2y - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
z + 1 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The third equation gives $z = -1$. Subtracting twice the second equation from the first we obtain $-3y = 0$, so $y = 0$ and $x = 1$. The center is therefore $(1, 0, -1)$.
