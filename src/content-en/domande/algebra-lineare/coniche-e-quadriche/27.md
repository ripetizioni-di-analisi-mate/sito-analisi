What are the asymptotes of the hyperbola
$$
xy - 2x + y - 3 = 0 ?
$$

- $x = 1$ and $y = -2$
- $x = 2$ and $y = -1$
- $x = -1$ and $y = -2$
- $x = -1$ and $y = 2$

## Explanation

We have $a_{12} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $a_{13} = -1$ and $a_{23} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the system of the center is $\dfrac{1}{2}\, y - 1 = 0$, $\dfrac{1}{2}\, x + \dfrac{1}{2} = 0$, and the center is $(-1, 2)$. The second-degree part $xy$ vanishes on the directions $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$, therefore the asymptotes are the horizontal line and the vertical line through the center, that is, $x = -1$ and $y = 2$. This is confirmed by writing $xy - 2x + y - 3 = (x + 1)(y - 2) - 1$.
