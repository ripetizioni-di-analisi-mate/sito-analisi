In space with coordinates $(x, y, z)$, what kind of quadric is
$$
x^2 - 4y^2 + 2x = 0 ?
$$

- A hyperbolic paraboloid
- A hyperbolic cylinder
- A hyperboloid of one sheet
- A cone

## Explanation

The equation does not contain $z$, so the third row of $A_0$ is zero and $\det A_0 = 0$. Completing the square we obtain $(x + 1)^2 - 4y^2 = 1$, which in the plane $z = 0$ is a hyperbola. Every line parallel to the $z$-axis through a point of the hyperbola is contained in the quadric, because $z$ can be chosen freely. Therefore the quadric is a **hyperbolic cylinder**, with $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$.
