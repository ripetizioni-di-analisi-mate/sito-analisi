What are the asymptotes of the hyperbola
$$
x^2 - 2xy - 3y^2 + 2x + 6y - 7 = 0 ?
$$

- $x - y + 1 = 0$ and $x + 3y - 3 = 0$
- $x + y = 0$ and $x - 3y = 0$
- $x + y - 1 = 0$ and $x - 3y + 3 = 0$
- $x + y + 1 = 0$ and $x - 3y - 3 = 0$

## Explanation

The asymptotes pass through the center, which solves the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x - y + 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
-x - 3y + 3 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Adding the two equations we obtain $-4y + 4 = 0$, so $y = 1$, $x = 0$ and the center is $(0, 1)$. The second-degree part factors as $x^2 - 2xy - 3y^2 = (x + y)(x - 3y)$, therefore it vanishes on the directions of the lines $x + y = 0$ and $x - 3y = 0$. The asymptotes are the lines through $(0, 1)$ with these directions, that is, $x + y - 1 = 0$ and $x - 3y + 3 = 0$.
