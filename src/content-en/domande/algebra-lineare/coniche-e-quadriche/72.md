The point $(2, 0)$ lies on the ellipse
$$
2x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2 - 2x + 4y - 4 = 0 .
$$
Which other point certainly lies on it?

- $(-2, 0)$
- $(0, -2)$
- $(0, 2)$
- $(2, -2)$

## Explanation

The center solves the system $2x + y - 1 = 0$, $x + 3y + 2 = 0$. From the first equation $y = 1 - 2x$; substituting into the second we obtain $-5x + 5 = 0$, so the center is $(1, -1)$. The ellipse is symmetric with respect to its center, therefore it contains the point symmetric to $(2, 0)$ with respect to $(1, -1)$, that is, $(2 \cdot 1 - 2, 2 \cdot (-1) - 0) = (0, -2)$. Substitution confirms this, since $12 - 8 - 4 = 0$.
