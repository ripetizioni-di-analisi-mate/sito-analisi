The parabola
$$
x^2 - 4x - 4y + 8 = 0
$$
has its axis parallel to the $y$-axis. Where is its focus?

- $(2, 0)$
- $(3, 1)$
- $(2, 3)$
- $(2, 2)$

## Explanation

Completing the square we have $x^2 - 4x = (x - 2)^2 - 4$, so the equation becomes $(x - 2)^2 = 4(y - 1)$. With the translation $X = x - 2$, $Y = y - 1$ we obtain $X^2 = 4Y$, a parabola with vertex $(2, 1)$ and axis parallel to the $y$-axis, with $2p = 4$. Therefore $p = 2$ and the focus lies on the axis, at distance $\dfrac{p}{2} = 1$ from the vertex on the side where the parabola lies. Since $X^2 = 4Y$ implies $Y \geq 0$, the parabola lies on the side of increasing $y$. The focus is therefore $(2, 2)$.
