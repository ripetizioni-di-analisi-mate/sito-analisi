In the equation
$$
x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 4y + k = 0
$$
the parameter $k \in \mathbb{R}$ appears only in the constant term. For which $k$ does the equation describe a circle of positive radius?

- $k > 5$
- $k \leq 5$
- $k < 5$
- $k < -5$

## Explanation

Completing the squares we have $x^2 + 2x = (x + 1)^2 - 1$ and $y^2 - 4y = (y - 2)^2 - 4$. So the equation becomes $(x + 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 5 - k$. The left-hand side is the square of the distance from the point $(-1, 2)$, therefore we have a circle of positive radius if and only if $5 - k > 0$, that is, $k < 5$. For $k = 5$ the conic contains only the point $(-1, 2)$, and for $k > 5$ it has no real points.
