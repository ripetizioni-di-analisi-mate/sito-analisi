How many points do the hyperbola $x^2 - 4y^2 = 4$ and the line $x - 2y = 1$ have in common?

- None
- Two
- Infinitely many
- One

## Explanation

The quadratic part factors as $(x - 2y)(x + 2y)$, so the asymptotes of the hyperbola are $x - 2y = 0$ and $x + 2y = 0$. The line $x - 2y = 1$ is parallel to the asymptote $x - 2y = 0$ and does not coincide with it. Substituting $x = 1 + 2y$ into the equation of the hyperbola, the terms in $y^2$ cancel and we are left with $1 + 4y = 4$. This first-degree equation has the unique solution $y = \dfrac{3}{4}$, therefore the only common point is $\left( \dfrac{5}{2}, \dfrac{3}{4} \right)$.
