Which of the following conics is congruent to the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + y^2 = 1$, that is, is obtained from it by an orientation-preserving rigid motion?

- $5x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 - 8 = 0$
- $5x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 - 4 = 0$
- $3x^2 - 2xy + 3y^2 - 8 = 0$
- $5x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 + 8 = 0$

## Explanation

Two central conics are congruent if they have the same canonical form, which depends on the eigenvalues of $A_0$ and on the ratio $\dfrac{I_3}{I_2}$. For $5x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 - 8 = 0$ the characteristic polynomial of $A_0$ is $\lambda^2 - 10\lambda + 16 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 8)$, and since there are no linear terms we have $\dfrac{I_3}{I_2} = -8$. So the canonical form is $2X^2 + 8Y^2 = 8$, that is, $\dfrac{X^2}{4} + Y^2 = 1$, and the conic is **congruent** to the given ellipse. With constant term $-4$ we obtain $\dfrac{X^2}{2} + 2Y^2 = 1$, with constant term $+8$ an imaginary ellipse, and $3x^2 - 2xy + 3y^2 - 8 = 0$ has eigenvalues $2$ and $4$, hence semi-axes $2$ and $\sqrt{2}$.
