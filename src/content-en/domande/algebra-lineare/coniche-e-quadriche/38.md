What kind of quadric is
$$
x^2 - 2xz + z^2 - 2y = 0 ?
$$

- An elliptic paraboloid
- A hyperbolic paraboloid
- A cone
- A parabolic cylinder

## Explanation

The associated matrix is
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The third row is the opposite of the first, so $\det A = 0$ and the quadric is degenerate. We have $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ and $\det A_0 = 0$, therefore by the definition of **cones and cylinders** the quadric is a cylinder. The equation can be written as $(x - z)^2 = 2y$, and replacing $(x, y, z)$ with $(x + t, y, z + t)$ the equation does not change. Hence the quadric contains every line with direction $(1, 0, 1)$ through one of its points, and the section with the plane $z = 0$ is the parabola $x^2 = 2y$. The quadric is therefore a **parabolic cylinder**.
