The equation
$$
\frac{x^2}{16} - \frac{y^2}{9} = -1
$$
represents a hyperbola. Where are its vertices?

- $(\pm 4, 0)$
- $(0, \pm 3)$
- $(0, \pm 4)$
- $(\pm 3, 0)$

## Explanation

Changing the sign of both sides, the equation becomes $\dfrac{y^2}{9} - \dfrac{x^2}{16} = 1$, which is a hyperbola with transverse axis on the $y$-axis. For $y = 0$ we would obtain $\dfrac{x^2}{16} = -1$, which has no real solutions, so the hyperbola does not meet the $x$-axis. For $x = 0$ we obtain $y^2 = 9$, therefore the vertices are $(0, \pm 3)$.
