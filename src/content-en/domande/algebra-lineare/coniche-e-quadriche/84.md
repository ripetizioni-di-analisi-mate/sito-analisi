Which surface is described by the equation
$$
2x^2 + y^2 + 2yz + z^2 - 4 = 0 ?
$$

- A circular cylinder of radius $\sqrt{2}$ with axis the line $x = 0$, $y = z$
- A circular cylinder of radius $\sqrt{2}$ with axis the line $x = 0$, $y = -z$
- A circular cylinder of radius $2$ with axis the line $x = 0$, $y = -z$
- A cone with vertex at the origin and axis the line $x = 0$, $y = -z$

## Explanation

The block $A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has two equal rows, so $\det A_0 = 0$. There are no linear terms, therefore the associated matrix is block diagonal with $a_{44} = -4$, and we have $\det A = -4 \det A_0 = 0$ and $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$. A quadric with $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ and $\det A_0 = 0$ is a **cylinder**. The eigenvalues of $A_0$ are $0$, with eigenvector $(0, 1, -1)$, and $2$ twice, with eigenvectors $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 1)$, so the canonical form is $2X^2 + 2Y^2 = 4$, that is, $X^2 + Y^2 = 2$. Therefore the cylinder is circular with radius $\sqrt{2}$, and its axis is the line through the origin with the direction $(0, 1, -1)$ of the eigenvector for $0$, that is, $x = 0$, $y = -z$.
