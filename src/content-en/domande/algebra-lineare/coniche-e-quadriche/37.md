What is the longest semi-axis of the ellipsoid
$$
3x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2 + z^2 - 4 = 0 ?
$$

- $\sqrt{2}$
- $4$
- $2$
- $2\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

There are no first-degree terms, so the center is the origin. The variable $z$ does not appear in the mixed terms, and the block $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ relative to $x$ and $y$ has eigenvalues $2$ and $4$, so the eigenvalues of $A_0$ are $4$, $2$, $1$ and $\det A_0 = 8$. We have $\det A = -4 \cdot 8 = -32$, therefore $\dfrac{\det A}{\det A_0} = -4$ and the canonical form is $4X^2 + 2Y^2 + Z^2 = 4$. Dividing by $4$ we obtain $X^2 + \dfrac{Y^2}{2} + \dfrac{Z^2}{4} = 1$, so the semi-axes are $1$, $\sqrt{2}$ and $2$. The longest semi-axis equals $2$ and corresponds to the smallest eigenvalue.
