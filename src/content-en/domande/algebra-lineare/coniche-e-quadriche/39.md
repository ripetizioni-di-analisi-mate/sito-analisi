A quadric has horizontal sections $(x-1)^2+y^2=4h^2$ in the plane $z=h$ for every $h$. Which feature of these sections distinguishes a cone from a paraboloid of revolution?

- The centres coincide after projection onto the $xy$-plane.
- The nondegenerate horizontal sections are circles.
- The section at the vertex contains a single point.
- The radius is proportional to $|h|$ and sections exist on both sides of the vertex.

## Explanation

The section radius is $2|h|$, so it grows linearly in both directions away from the vertex plane. For a paraboloid of revolution with vertical axis, the squared radius is proportional to the height measured from the vertex, and sections occupy one side only.
