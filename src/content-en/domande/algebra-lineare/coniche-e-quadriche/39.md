What kind of quadric is
$$
x^2 + y^2 - 4z^2 - 2x + 1 = 0 ?
$$

- A hyperboloid of one sheet with center $(1, 0, 0)$
- A hyperboloid of two sheets with center $(1, 0, 0)$
- A cone with vertex $(-1, 0, 0)$
- A cone with vertex $(1, 0, 0)$

## Explanation

Since $x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2$, the equation can be written as $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 - 4z^2 = 0$. The center is $(1, 0, 0)$ and the left-hand side equals $0$ at the center; since the constant term of the canonical form is $\dfrac{\det A}{\det A_0}$, we have $\det A = 0$ and the quadric is degenerate, with $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$. We have $\det A_0 = -4 \neq 0$, therefore the quadric is a **cone** with vertex at the center $(1, 0, 0)$. The cone has real points other than the vertex, because the eigenvalues $1$, $1$, $-4$ do not all have the same sign.
