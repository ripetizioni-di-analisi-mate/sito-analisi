The plane $z = 1$ cuts the quadric
$$
x^2 + 4xy + 4y^2 - 2y - z^2 = 0
$$
along a curve. What conic is this curve in the plane $z = 1$, with coordinates $(x, y)$?

- A pair of parallel lines
- A parabola
- A real ellipse
- A hyperbola

## Explanation

Substituting $z = 1$ we obtain the conic $x^2 + 4xy + 4y^2 - 2y - 1 = 0$, with associated matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 4 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The block of the quadratic terms has $I_2 = 4 - 4 = 0$, because the quadratic part is the square $(x + 2y)^2$. Expanding the determinant along the first row we get $I_3 = 1 \cdot (-4 - 1) - 2 \cdot (-2 - 0) = -1 \neq 0$, so the conic is non-degenerate. By the **classification of non-degenerate conics**, with $I_2 = 0$ the conic is a **parabola**. Parallel lines would occur only with $I_3 = 0$.
