Which of the following points is inside the ellipse
$$
x^2 - xy + y^2 - 1 = 0 ?
$$

- $(1, -1)$
- $\left( 1, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$
- $\left( -1, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$
- $\left( 0, \dfrac{3}{2} \right)$

## Explanation

There are no linear terms, so the center is the origin and a rotation is enough to reach the canonical form. After the rotation the left-hand side becomes $\lambda_1 X^2 + \lambda_2 Y^2 - 1$ with $\lambda_1, \lambda_2 > 0$, therefore a point is inside the ellipse if and only if the left-hand side takes a negative value there. We have $f\left( 1, \dfrac{1}{2} \right) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{4} - 1 = -\dfrac{1}{4} < 0$, so the point $\left( 1, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ is **inside**. At the other three points the left-hand side equals $2$, $\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $\dfrac{5}{4}$, so they are outside.
