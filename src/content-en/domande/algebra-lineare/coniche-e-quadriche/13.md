For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the quadric
$$
x^2 + y^2 + kz^2 + 2z = 0
$$
a hyperboloid of two sheets?

- $k > 0$
- $k < 0$
- $k \leq 0$
- $k \neq 0$

## Explanation

The block of the second-degree terms is the diagonal matrix with entries $1$, $1$, $k$, so the eigenvalues of $A_0$ are $1$, $1$, $k$ and $\det A_0 = k$. Expanding the determinant of the associated matrix we obtain $\det A = -1$ for every $k$. The quadric is a hyperboloid when $\det A_0 \neq 0$ and the eigenvalues do not all have the same sign, that is, for $k < 0$. Since $\det A < 0$, by the theorem on the **recognition of quadrics** the hyperboloid is **of two sheets**. For $k > 0$ we obtain a real ellipsoid and for $k = 0$ an elliptic paraboloid.
