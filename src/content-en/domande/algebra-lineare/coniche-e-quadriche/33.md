For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does the conic
$$
x^2 + 2xy + ky^2 - 2x + 2y = 0
$$
have a unique center lying on the $y$-axis?

- $k = 1$
- $k = -1$
- $k = 0$
- $k = 3$

## Explanation

We have $I_2 = k - 1$, so the conic has a unique center if and only if $k \neq 1$. With $a_{12} = 1$, $a_{13} = -1$ and $a_{23} = 1$, the center solves the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
x + ky + 1 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The center lies on the $y$-axis if $x = 0$; then the first equation gives $y = 1$ and the second becomes $k + 1 = 0$. Hence $k = -1$, and for this value the center is $(0, 1)$. For $k = 0$ the center is $(-1, 2)$ and for $k = 3$ it is $(2, -1)$.
