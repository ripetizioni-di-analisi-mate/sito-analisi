What are the asymptotes of the hyperbola
$$
\frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{y^2}{25} = 1 ?
$$

- $y = \pm \dfrac{2}{5}\, x$
- $y = \pm \dfrac{5}{2}\, x$
- $y = \pm \dfrac{25}{4}\, x$
- $y = \pm \dfrac{4}{25}\, x$

## Explanation

The hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ has asymptotes $y = \pm \dfrac{b}{a}\, x$. Here $a^2 = 4$ and $b^2 = 25$, so $a = 2$ and $b = 5$. Therefore the asymptotes are the lines $y = \pm \dfrac{5}{2}\, x$. The same result is obtained by setting the second-degree part equal to zero, since $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{25} = 0$ is equivalent to $y^2 = \dfrac{25}{4}\, x^2$.
