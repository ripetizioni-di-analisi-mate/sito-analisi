For which values of the real parameter $k$ does the quadric
$$
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2kxy - 2z = 0
$$
have exactly one center?

- $k \neq \pm 1$
- $k \neq 0$
- $k = \pm 1$
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

By the **canonical form of quadrics** the center solves the system $A_0 c = -b$, which has exactly one solution if and only if $\det A_0 \neq 0$. Here
$$
A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & k & 0 \\ k & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
\det A_0 = 1 - k^2 ,
$$
so the center is unique if and only if $k \neq \pm 1$. For $k = 1$ the system for the center becomes $x + y = 0$, $x + y = 0$, $z - 1 = 0$, and its solutions are all the points of the line $x + y = 0$, $z = 1$. For $k = -1$ one obtains in the same way the line $x - y = 0$, $z = 1$.
