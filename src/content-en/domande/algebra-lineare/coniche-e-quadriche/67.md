A conic has orthogonal invariants $I_1 = -3$, $I_2 = 2$ and $I_3 = 5$. What conic is it?

- A real ellipse
- An imaginary ellipse
- A hyperbola
- A parabola

## Explanation

Since $I_3 \neq 0$, the conic is non-degenerate. Since $I_2 > 0$, by the **classification of non-degenerate conics** the conic is an ellipse. The product $I_1 I_3 = -15$ is negative, so the ellipse is **real**. The sign of $I_3$ alone is not enough: with $I_1 = 3$ and the same $I_2$ and $I_3$ the ellipse would be imaginary.
