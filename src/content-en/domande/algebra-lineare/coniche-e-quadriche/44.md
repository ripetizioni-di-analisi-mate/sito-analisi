Let $A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ be the block of the quadratic terms of the conic $x^2 + 4xy - 2y^2 - 6 = 0$. Which of the following is a rotation matrix $R$ such that $R^T A_0 R$ is diagonal?

- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The characteristic polynomial of $A_0$ is $\lambda^2 + \lambda - 6 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda + 3)$, with eigenvectors $(2, 1)$ for $\lambda = 2$ and $(-1, 2)$ for $\lambda = -3$. By the **spectral theorem** the matrix with columns $\dfrac{(2, 1)}{\sqrt{5}}$ and $\dfrac{(-1, 2)}{\sqrt{5}}$ is orthogonal and diagonalizes $A_0$; its determinant equals $\dfrac{4 + 1}{5} = 1$, so it is a rotation. The matrix with second column $\dfrac{(1, -2)}{\sqrt{5}}$ diagonalizes $A_0$ but has determinant $-1$, therefore it is a reflection. The matrix without the factor $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$ has columns of norm $\sqrt{5}$, so it is not orthogonal, and the $45^\circ$ rotation has columns that are not eigenvectors of $A_0$.
