What is the transverse semi-axis, that is, the semi-axis on the axis that contains the vertices, of the hyperbola
$$
4xy - 3y^2 - 4 = 0 ?
$$

- $1$
- $4$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The block $A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 2 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$ has characteristic polynomial $\lambda^2 + 3\lambda - 4 = (\lambda - 1)(\lambda + 4)$, so the eigenvalues are $1$ and $-4$. We have $I_2 = -4$ and $I_3 = 16$, therefore $\dfrac{I_3}{I_2} = -4$. By the **canonical form of central conics** the equation becomes $X^2 - 4Y^2 = 4$, that is, $\dfrac{X^2}{4} - Y^2 = 1$. The vertices lie on the $X$-axis, so the transverse semi-axis equals $2$, while the other semi-axis equals $1$.
