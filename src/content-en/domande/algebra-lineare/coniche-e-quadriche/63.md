The lines $x - 3y + 1 = 0$ and $3x + y - 2 = 0$ are taken as new axes. To which equation does the parabola
$$
(x - 3y + 1)^2 = 10(3x + y - 2)
$$
reduce in this frame?

- $Y^2 = \sqrt{10}\, X$
- $Y^2 = 10X$
- $Y^2 = 2\sqrt{10}\, X$
- $Y^2 = \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{10}\, X$

## Explanation

The lines $x - 3y + 1 = 0$ and $3x + y - 2 = 0$ are perpendicular, since $1 \cdot 3 + (-3) \cdot 1 = 0$, so they are the axis of the parabola and the tangent at the vertex. The signed distances from these lines
$$
Y = \frac{x - 3y + 1}{\sqrt{10}} ,
\qquad
X = \frac{3x + y - 2}{\sqrt{10}}
$$
are orthogonal Cartesian coordinates. Substituting $x - 3y + 1 = \sqrt{10}\, Y$ and $3x + y - 2 = \sqrt{10}\, X$, the equation becomes $10Y^2 = 10\sqrt{10}\, X$, that is, $Y^2 = \sqrt{10}\, X$.
