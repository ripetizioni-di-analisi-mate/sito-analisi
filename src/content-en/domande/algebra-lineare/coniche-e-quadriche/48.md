The figure shows the ellipse
$$
\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1 .
$$
What are its foci?

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),3*sin(t),0,6.29" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="The ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$"}

- $(0, \pm \sqrt{5})$
- $(\pm \sqrt{5}, 0)$
- $(0, \pm \sqrt{13})$
- $(\pm \sqrt{13}, 0)$

## Explanation

The semi-axis on the $x$-axis equals $2$ and the one on the $y$-axis equals $3$, so the major semi-axis lies on the $y$-axis. The foci lie on the major axis, therefore they have the form $(0, \pm c)$. Exchanging the roles of $x$ and $y$ in the canonical form, we have $c^2 = 9 - 4 = 5$. Hence the foci are $(0, \pm \sqrt{5})$.
