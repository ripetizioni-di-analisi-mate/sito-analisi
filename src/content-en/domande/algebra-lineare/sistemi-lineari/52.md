The system formed by $x + 2y = 3$ and $2x - y = 1$ has the unique solution $(1, 1)$. On its augmented matrix the two transformations $R_1 \to R_1 + R_2$ and $R_2 \to R_2 + R_1$ are performed *in the same step*, each using the original rows. How many solutions does the resulting system have?

- The unique solution $(1, 1)$
- No solution
- $\infty^2$ solutions
- $\infty^1$ solutions

## Explanation

Both transformations add the two original rows, so the new first row and the new second row are both equal to $(3, 1 \mid 4)$. The resulting system consists of the equation $3x + y = 4$ written twice, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 1$ and there are $\infty^{2 - 1} = \infty^1$ solutions, namely the points $(t, 4 - 3t)$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$. The new system **is not equivalent** to the original one. Indeed the second transformation uses the row $R_1$, which is modified in the same step, and the theorem on equivalent systems concerns operations performed one after another.
