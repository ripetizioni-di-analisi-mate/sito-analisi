What is the solution of the following system?
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 4 \\[4pt]
2x + 2y + z = 6 \\[4pt]
x + 2y + 3z = 9
\end{cases}
$$

- $(x, y, z) = (2, 0, 2)$
- $(x, y, z) = (1, 2, 1)$
- $(x, y, z) = (1, 1, 2)$
- $(x, y, z) = (3, -1, 2)$

## Explanation

With **Gaussian elimination** one performs $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$:
$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 1 & 4 \\
2 & 2 & 1 & 6 \\
1 & 2 & 3 & 9
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 - R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 1 & 4 \\
0 & 0 & -1 & -2 \\
0 & 1 & 2 & 5
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow{R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 1 & 4 \\
0 & 1 & 2 & 5 \\
0 & 0 & -1 & -2
\end{array}\right)
$$
In the second row the coefficient of $y$ has become zero, so the second and third rows are swapped to obtain an echelon matrix. The last equation $-z = -2$ gives $z = 2$. The second, $y + 2z = 5$, gives $y = 1$, and the first gives $x = 4 - y - z = 1$. Therefore the **unique solution** is $(1, 1, 2)$.
