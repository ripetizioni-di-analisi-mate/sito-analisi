For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x - 3y = 1 \\[4pt]
4x - 6y = k
\end{cases}
$$
have infinitely many solutions?

- $k = 1$
- $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $k = 2$
- $k = -2$

## Explanation

The left-hand side of the second equation is twice that of the first, so the two equations represent parallel lines and $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$. The lines coincide if the constant terms are also in the same ratio, that is, if $k = 2 \cdot 1 = 2$. In this case $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 1$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the solutions are $\infty^{2 - 1} = \infty^1$. For $k \neq 2$ one has $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$, therefore the system is inconsistent.
