For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the solution set of the equation $x + 2y - z = k$, in the unknowns $x, y, z$, a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$?

- $k = 0$
- $k = 1$
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$
- For no value of $k$

## Explanation

A subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$ contains the zero vector, so $(0, 0, 0)$ must satisfy the equation, and this happens only if $k = 0$. For $k = 0$ the equation is homogeneous and its solutions form a **subspace**, because they are closed under addition and under multiplication by a scalar. For $k \neq 0$ the solutions form the plane $x_0 + S_0$, parallel to the subspace $S_0$ of solutions of $x + 2y - z = 0$ and not passing through the origin.
