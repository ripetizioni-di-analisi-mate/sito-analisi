The following system, in the unknowns $x, y$, has a square augmented matrix with $\det (A \mid b) = 0$. Which statement is true?
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 1 \\[4pt]
2x + 2y = 3 \\[4pt]
3x + 3y = 4
\end{cases}
$$

- The system is consistent, because $\det (A \mid b) = 0$
- The system has $\infty^1$ solutions
- The system is inconsistent, because $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ and $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$
- The system has a unique solution

## Explanation

The augmented matrix has equal columns for $x$ and $y$, so $\det (A \mid b) = 0$. The rows of $A$ are $(1, 1)$, $(2, 2)$ and $(3, 3)$, all proportional, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$. In the augmented matrix the minor of the first two rows and of the columns of $y$ and of the constant terms equals $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = 1 \neq 0$, so $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$. Since the two ranks are different, by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is inconsistent. Indeed the three equations represent the parallel and distinct lines $x + y = 1$, $x + y = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and $x + y = \dfrac{4}{3}$. The condition $\det (A \mid b) = 0$ is therefore only necessary for consistency.
