Which statement about the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y = 3 \\[4pt]
2x + 4y = 6
\end{cases}
$$
is true?

- Cramer's rule applies because the system is square, and the unique solution is $(1, 1)$
- Cramer's rule does not apply because $\det A = 0$, and the system is inconsistent
- Cramer's rule does not apply because $\det A = 0$, and the only solution is $(3, 0)$
- Cramer's rule does not apply because $\det A = 0$, and the system has infinitely many solutions

## Explanation

One has $\det A = 1 \cdot 4 - 2 \cdot 2 = 0$, so the hypothesis $\det A \neq 0$ of Cramer's rule is not satisfied. The second equation is twice the first, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 1$. By the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system has $\infty^{2 - 1} = \infty^1$ solutions, that is, $(3 - 2t, \ t)$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$. The pairs $(1, 1)$ and $(3, 0)$ are two of these solutions.
