For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does the matrix
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & k \\
k & 4 & 2k
\end{pmatrix}
$$
have rank $1$?

- $k = 2$
- $k = 0$
- $k = 0$ and $k = 2$
- $k = -2$

## Explanation

The matrix is nonzero, so it has rank $1$ if and only if *all* its minors of order $2$ are zero. The three minors are
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ k & 4 \end{vmatrix} = 4 - 2k, \qquad
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & k \\ k & 2k \end{vmatrix} = k\,(2 - k), \qquad
\begin{vmatrix} 2 & k \\ 4 & 2k \end{vmatrix} = 0 .
$$
The first vanishes only for $k = 2$, and for $k = 2$ the second vanishes as well. Therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ **only for** $k = 2$. For $k = 0$ the second minor is zero, but the first equals $4$, so the rank is $2$.
