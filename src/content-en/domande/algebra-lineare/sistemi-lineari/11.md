- $\{ (3, 0, 0) + t\,(-2, 1, 0) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}$
- $\{ (1, 0, -2) + t\,(-2, 1, 0) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}$
- $\{ (1, 0, -2) + t\,(-2, 1, 1) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}$

## Explanation

With $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ the second equation becomes $3z = -6$, so $z = -2$. The first then becomes $x + 2y = 1$, and setting $y = t$ one has $x = 1 - 2t$. The solutions are therefore
$$
(1 - 2t, \ t, \ -2) = (1, 0, -2) + t\,(-2, 1, 0), \qquad t \in \mathbb{R},
$$
where $(1, 0, -2)$ is a particular solution and $(-2, 1, 0)$ spans the solutions of the associated homogeneous system, as stated by the theorem on the **structure of the solutions**. The triple $(3, 0, 0)$ satisfies only the first equation, while the vectors $(2, 1, 0)$ and $(-2, 1, 1)$ do not solve the associated homogeneous system.
