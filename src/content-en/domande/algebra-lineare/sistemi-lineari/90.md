We want to obtain $10$ liters of a $25\%$ salt solution by mixing a $10\%$ solution and a $30\%$ solution. Letting $x$ and $y$ be the liters of the two solutions, how many liters of the $10\%$ solution are needed?

- $2.5$ liters
- $7.5$ liters
- $5$ liters
- $1.25$ liters

## Explanation

The total volume gives the equation $x + y = 10$. The salt contained in the mixture is $25\%$ of $10$ liters, that is, $2.5$ liters, so $0.1\, x + 0.3\, y = 2.5$, which multiplied by $10$ becomes $x + 3y = 25$. The system formed by $x + y = 10$ and $x + 3y = 25$ has $\det A = 3 - 1 = 2 \neq 0$, therefore by **Cramer's rule**
$$
x = \frac{1}{2}
\begin{vmatrix}
10 & 1 \\
25 & 3
\end{vmatrix}
= \frac{30 - 25}{2} = \frac{5}{2} .
$$
Hence $2.5$ liters of the $10\%$ solution and $7.5$ liters of the $30\%$ solution are needed.
