Which statement about the following system, in the unknowns $x, y, z$, is true as $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies?
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + k z = 1 \\[4pt]
x + k y + z = k
\end{cases}
$$

- The system is consistent for every $k$, with $\infty^2$ solutions for $k = 1$ and $\infty^1$ solutions for $k \neq 1$
- The system is inconsistent for $k = 1$ and has $\infty^1$ solutions for $k \neq 1$
- The system has $\infty^1$ solutions for every $k$
- The system is inconsistent for $k = -1$ and has $\infty^1$ solutions for $k \neq -1$

## Explanation

The minor of the columns of $x$ and $z$ equals $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & k \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 1 - k$. For $k \neq 1$ it is nonzero, so $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$, because the augmented matrix has only two rows; by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** there are $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^1$ solutions. For $k = 1$ both equations coincide with $x + y + z = 1$, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 1$ and there are $\infty^2$ solutions. Hence the system is **consistent for every $k$**. In particular for $k = -1$ the minor equals $2$ and there are $\infty^1$ solutions.
