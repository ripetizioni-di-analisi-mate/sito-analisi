What can be said about the solutions of the following system, in the unknowns $x, y$, as the parameter $k$ varies in $\mathbb{R}$?
$$
\begin{cases}
x + k y = 1 + k \\[4pt]
x - y = 0
\end{cases}
$$

- For every $k$ the unique solution is $(1, 1)$
- For $k = -1$ the system is inconsistent; for $k \neq -1$ the unique solution is $(1, 1)$
- For $k \neq -1$ the unique solution is $(1, 1)$; for $k = -1$ there are $\infty^1$ solutions
- For $k \neq -1$ the unique solution is $(1 + k, \ 1 + k)$; for $k = -1$ there are $\infty^1$ solutions

## Explanation

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & k \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -1 - k$, which vanishes only for $k = -1$. For $k \neq -1$ the system is therefore uniquely solvable; the second equation gives $x = y$, and the first becomes $(1 + k)\, y = 1 + k$, so $y = 1$ and the unique solution is $(1, 1)$, the same for every $k$. For $k = -1$ the first equation becomes $x - y = 0$ and coincides with the second, so $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 1$ and there are $\infty^1$ solutions, namely the pairs $(t, t)$.
