The augmented matrix of a system in the unknowns $x, y, z$, reduced to echelon form, is
$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & -1 & 2 & 1 \\
0 & 3 & 1 & 2 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & h
\end{array}\right)
$$
with $h \in \mathbb{R}$. Which statement is true?

- The system is consistent only for $h = 0$, with a unique solution
- The system is consistent for every $h$, with $\infty^1$ solutions
- The system is consistent only for $h = 0$, with $\infty^1$ solutions
- The system is consistent only for $h \neq 0$, with $\infty^1$ solutions

## Explanation

The third row corresponds to the equation $0 = h$. For $h \neq 0$ this equation has no solutions, so the system is inconsistent. For $h = 0$ the third row is discarded and two pivots remain, in the columns of $x$ and $y$. Therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** there are $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^1$ solutions, with $z$ free.
