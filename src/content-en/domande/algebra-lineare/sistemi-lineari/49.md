What do the three planes in space with equations
$$
x + y + z = 1, \qquad x - y = 0, \qquad 2x + z = 1
$$
have in common?

- A single point
- No point
- A line
- A plane

## Explanation

The points common to the three planes are the solutions of the system formed by the three equations. The third equation is the sum of the first two, both in the coefficients and in the constant terms, so it can be discarded. The first two have non-proportional coefficients, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$. By the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** there are $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^1$ solutions, depending on one parameter. Hence the three planes have **a line** in common, namely the intersection of the planes $x + y + z = 1$ and $x - y = 0$.
