The solution space $S_0$ of a homogeneous system $A x = 0$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ contains the vectors $u = (1, 0, 2)$ and $v = (0, 1, -1)$. Which vector *certainly* belongs to $S_0$?

- $(2, -3, 7)$
- $(1, 1, 2)$
- $(2, 1, 4)$
- $(0, 0, 1)$

## Explanation

The solutions of a homogeneous system form a **subspace** of $\mathbb{R}^3$, so every linear combination $\alpha u + \beta v$ is a solution. The combinations are the triples $(\alpha, \ \beta, \ 2\alpha - \beta)$, that is, the triples $(x, y, z)$ with $z = 2x - y$. The triple $(2, -3, 7)$ satisfies $7 = 4 + 3$; indeed it is $2u - 3v$. For the other triples $2x - y$ equals $1$, $3$ and $0$, different from the third component, so they are not combinations of $u$ and $v$, and the data do not guarantee that they are solutions.
