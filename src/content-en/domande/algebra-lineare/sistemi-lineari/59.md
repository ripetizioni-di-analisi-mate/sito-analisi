How does the rank of the following matrix vary as $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies?
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & k & 1 \\
k & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 1 & k
\end{pmatrix}
$$

- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for $k \neq 1, -2$; $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ for $k = 1$; $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ for $k = -2$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for $k \neq 1, -2$; $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ for $k = 1$; $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ for $k = -2$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for $k \neq 1, -2$; $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ for $k = 1$ and for $k = -2$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for $k \neq -1, 2$; $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ for $k = -1$; $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ for $k = 2$

## Explanation

Expanding along the first row gives
$$
\det A = (k - 1) - k\,(k^2 - 1) + (k - 1) = -(k - 1)^2 (k + 2),
$$
so $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq -2$. For $k = 1$ all rows are equal to $(1, 1, 1)$, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$. For $k = -2$ the minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -3$ is nonzero, and since $\det A = 0$ we have $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$.
