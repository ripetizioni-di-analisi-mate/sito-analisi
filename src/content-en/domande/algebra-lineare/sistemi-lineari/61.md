Consider the skew-symmetric matrix
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & a & b \\
-a & 0 & c \\
-b & -c & 0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
with $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$ not all zero. Which statement about $\operatorname{rk} A$ is true?

- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for every choice of $a, b, c$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ if $a, b, c$ are all nonzero, $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ otherwise
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ if two of $a, b, c$ are zero, $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ otherwise
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ for every choice of $a, b, c$

## Explanation

The rule of Sarrus gives $\det A = 0 + abc - abc - 0 - 0 - 0 = 0$, so $\operatorname{rk} A \leq 2$ for every choice of the parameters. The minor of rows $1$, $2$ and columns $1$, $2$ equals $a^2$, that of rows $1$, $3$ and columns $1$, $3$ equals $b^2$, and that of rows $2$, $3$ and columns $2$, $3$ equals $c^2$. Since $a, b, c$ are not all zero, at least one of these minors is nonzero. Therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ **for every choice** of $a, b, c$; for example with $a = 1$ and $b = c = 0$ the first two rows of the matrix are $(0, 1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0, 0)$, which are not proportional.
