Which triple solves the following system, whose coefficient matrix has $\det A = 18$?
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + y = 1 \\[4pt]
x + 3y + z = 3 \\[4pt]
y + 4z = 1
\end{cases}
$$

- $(x, y, z) = (1, 0, 0)$
- $(x, y, z) = (0, 1, 0)$
- $(x, y, z) = (0, 0, 1)$
- $(x, y, z) = (1, 1, 1)$

## Explanation

Expanding along the first row gives $\det A = 2 \cdot (12 - 1) - 1 \cdot (4 - 0) = 18 \neq 0$, so **Cramer's rule** applies and the solution is unique. The column of constant terms $(1, 3, 1)$ coincides with the second column of $A$. Therefore $A_2 = A$ and $\det A_2 = 18$, while $A_1$ and $A_3$ have two equal columns and $\det A_1 = \det A_3 = 0$. It follows that $x = 0$, $y = \dfrac{18}{18} = 1$ and $z = 0$, that is, the solution is $(0, 1, 0)$, as one checks by substitution.
