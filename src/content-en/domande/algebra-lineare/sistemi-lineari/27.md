For a matrix $A$ with $3$ rows and $4$ columns it is known that the minor formed by rows $1$, $2$ and columns $1$, $2$ is nonzero and that its two bordered minors are zero. Which statement is true?

- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$
- $\operatorname{rk} A \geq 2$, and to conclude one also needs the minors of columns $1, 3, 4$ and $2, 3, 4$
- The rank depends on the other minors of order $2$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$

## Explanation

The given minor is nonzero and has order $2$, so $\operatorname{rk} A \geq 2$. Its bordered minors are $(3 - 2)(4 - 2) = 2$ and they are obtained by adding row $3$ and either column $3$ or column $4$. Since both are zero, the **bordered minors theorem** applies and gives $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. The theorem ensures that, when the bordered minors of a nonzero minor are all zero, the other minors of order $3$ are zero as well, including those of columns $1, 3, 4$ and $2, 3, 4$.
