Which homogeneous system, in the unknowns $x, y, z$, has the line $\langle (1, 1, 1) \rangle$ as its solution set?

- The system formed by the single equation $x + y + z = 0$
- The system formed by the equations $x - y = 0$ and $y + z = 0$
- The system formed by the single equation $x - y = 0$
- The system formed by the equations $x - y = 0$ and $y - z = 0$

## Explanation

The solution space has dimension $1$, so by the theorem on **nontrivial solutions** the coefficient matrix must have rank $3 - 1 = 2$. A single equation gives rank $1$ and a plane of solutions, therefore the two proposals with a single equation are excluded. The equations $x - y = 0$ and $y - z = 0$ have rank $2$ and give $x = y = z$, that is, the solutions $t\,(1, 1, 1)$. The equations $x - y = 0$ and $y + z = 0$ also have rank $2$, but they give the solutions $t\,(1, 1, -1)$.
