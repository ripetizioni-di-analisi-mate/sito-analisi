The figure shows the three lines of the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x - y = 0 \\[4pt]
x + y = 2 \\[4pt]
2y = 1
\end{cases}
$$
in the unknowns $x, y$. Which statement about the ranks is true?

::grafico{f="x;2-x;0.5" etichette="1.3,2.3,x − y = 0;0.2,2.2,x + y = 2;2.4,0.75,2y = 1" xmin=-1.5 xmax=3 ymin=-1.5 ymax=2.5 uguale=1 titolo="The lines $x - y = 0$, $x + y = 2$ and $2y = 1$"}

- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ and $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ and $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ and $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ and $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$

## Explanation

The three lines meet pairwise at the points $(1, 1)$, $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, but there is no point common to all three, so the system is inconsistent. The matrix $A$ has two columns and the minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 2$ is nonzero, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. Since the system is inconsistent, by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) \neq \operatorname{rk} A$, hence $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$. The computation
$$
\det (A \mid b) =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & -1 & 0 \\
1 & 1 & 2 \\
0 & 2 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot (1 - 4) + 1 \cdot (1 - 0) = -2 \neq 0
$$
confirms it.
