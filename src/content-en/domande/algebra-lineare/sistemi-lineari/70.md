Four one-way streets form a ring $A \to B \to C \to D \to A$. The segments $AB$, $BC$, $CD$ and $DA$ carry $x_1$, $x_2$, $x_3$ and $x_4$ vehicles per hour respectively. From outside, $300$ vehicles per hour enter at $A$ and $50$ at $C$, while $100$ vehicles per hour leave towards the outside from $A$, $150$ from $B$ and $100$ from $D$. At each intersection the number of vehicles entering equals the number leaving. If all flows are nonnegative, what is the smallest possible value of $x_1$?

- $200$ vehicles per hour
- $150$ vehicles per hour
- $300$ vehicles per hour
- $50$ vehicles per hour

## Explanation

The balance at the intersections $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ gives the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x_1 - x_4 = 200 \\[4pt]
x_1 - x_2 = 150 \\[4pt]
x_3 - x_2 = 50 \\[4pt]
x_3 - x_4 = 100
\end{cases}
$$
The fourth equation is the first minus the second plus the third, both in the coefficients and in the constant terms, so it is discarded. The first three equations give $x_1 = 200 + x_4$, $x_2 = x_1 - 150 = 50 + x_4$ and $x_3 = x_2 + 50 = 100 + x_4$, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$ and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** there are $\infty^{4 - 3} = \infty^1$ solutions, with $x_4 = t$ free. Since $x_4 = t$ is the smallest of the four flows, all flows are nonnegative if and only if $t \geq 0$. Hence the minimum of $x_1 = 200 + t$ is reached for $t = 0$ and equals $200$ vehicles per hour.
