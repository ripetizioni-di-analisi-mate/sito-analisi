Which of the following matrices is in echelon form?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

A matrix is in echelon form when any zero rows are at the bottom and the pivot of each nonzero row lies in a column strictly to the right of the pivot of the previous row. In the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ the pivots lie in columns $1$, $3$ and $4$, so the matrix **is in echelon form**. In the matrix with the rows $(0, 1, 2, 1)$ and $(0, 3, 0, 4)$ the pivot of the third row lies in column $2$, like that of the second row. In the matrix that starts with the row $(0, 1, 2, 1)$ the pivot of the second row lies to the left of that of the first. In the matrix with the row $(0, 0, 0, 0)$ a zero row precedes a nonzero row.
