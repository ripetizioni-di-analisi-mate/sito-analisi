- $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ for $k = 2$, $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for $k \neq 2$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ for $k = -2$, $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for $k \neq -2$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for every $k \in \mathbb{R}$
- $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ for $k = 2$, $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ for $k \neq 2$

## Explanation

The minor of rows $1$, $2$ and columns $1$, $2$ equals $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -2 \neq 0$, so $\operatorname{rk} A \geq 2$ for every $k$. Its bordered minors are obtained by adding row $3$ and either column $3$ or column $4$:
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 2 \\
1 & -1 & 0 \\
2 & 0 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
= 0,
\qquad
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & k \\
1 & -1 & 1 \\
2 & 0 & 3
\end{vmatrix}
= 2k - 4 .
$$
For $k = 2$ both bordered minors are zero, therefore by the **bordered minors theorem** $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. For $k \neq 2$ the second bordered minor is nonzero, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$.
