Which of the following systems, in the unknowns $x, y$, is equivalent to the system formed by $x + y = 3$ and $x - y = 1$?

- The system formed by $x + y = 3$ and $2x = 2$
- The system formed by $x + y = 3$ and $2x + 2y = 6$
- The system formed by $2x = 4$ and $y = 1$
- The system formed by $x - y = 1$ and $2y = 4$

## Explanation

Two systems are equivalent when they have the same solutions. Adding the two given equations gives $2x = 4$, so $x = 2$, and the first gives $y = 1$; therefore the given system has the unique solution $(2, 1)$. The system formed by $2x = 4$ and $y = 1$ also has the unique solution $(2, 1)$, so it **is equivalent**. The system with $2x = 2$ has the solution $(1, 2)$, the one with $2x + 2y = 6$ has infinitely many solutions and the one with $2y = 4$ has the solution $(3, 2)$.
