While reducing to echelon form a system in the unknowns $x, y, z$ that depends on the parameter $k \in \mathbb{R}$, one obtains the augmented matrix
$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & -1 & 2 \\
0 & 1 & 3 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & k - 2 & k^2 - 4
\end{array}\right) .
$$
Which statement is true?

- For $k \neq 2$ one has $z = k + 2$ and the system has a unique solution; for $k = 2$ the system has $\infty^1$ solutions
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$ one has $z = k + 2$ and the system has a unique solution
- For $k \neq 2$ one has $z = k + 2$ and the system has a unique solution; for $k = 2$ the system is inconsistent
- For $k \neq 2$ one has $z = k - 2$ and the system has a unique solution; for $k = 2$ the system has $\infty^1$ solutions

## Explanation

The last row corresponds to the equation $(k - 2)\, z = k^2 - 4$. For $k \neq 2$ one can divide by $k - 2$, so
$$
z = \frac{k^2 - 4}{k - 2} = \frac{(k - 2)(k + 2)}{k - 2} = k + 2 .
$$
In this case the matrix has three pivots, therefore the system has a unique solution. For $k = 2$ the division is not allowed and the last row becomes $(0 \ 0 \ 0 \mid 0)$, that is, the equation $0 = 0$. Then two pivots remain, in the columns of $x$ and $y$, and the system has $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^1$ solutions with $z$ free.
