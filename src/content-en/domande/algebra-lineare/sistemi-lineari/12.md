For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x + 2y + kz = 2 \\[4pt]
x + ky + 2z = k
\end{cases}
$$
have infinitely many solutions?

- Only for $k = 2$
- Only for $k = 0$
- For $k = -2$ and for $k = 0$
- For $k = 0$ and for $k = 2$

## Explanation

Expanding along the first row gives $\det A = (4 - k^2) - (2 - k) + (k - 2) = -k(k - 2)$. For $k \neq 0$ and $k \neq 2$ the determinant is nonzero, so the system is uniquely solvable. For $k = 2$ the second and third equations coincide, and since $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 1 \neq 0$ one has $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$. For $k = 0$ the sum of the second and third rows of the augmented matrix is $(2 \ \ 2 \ \ 2 \mid 2)$, that is, twice the first, and again $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$. In both cases the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** gives $\infty^1$ solutions.
