For which values of $a \in \mathbb{R}$ does the determinant of the following Vandermonde matrix vanish, that is, are its three columns linearly dependent?
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 2 & a \\
1 & 4 & a^2
\end{pmatrix}
$$

- Only for $a = 2$
- For $a = -1$ and $a = -2$
- For $a = 0$ and $a = 2$
- For $a = 1$ and $a = 2$

## Explanation

A square matrix of order $3$ has rank less than $3$ if and only if its determinant is zero. The operations $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$ do not change the determinant, so
$$
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & a - 1 \\
0 & 3 & a^2 - 1
\end{vmatrix}
= (a^2 - 1) - 3(a - 1) = (a - 1)(a + 1 - 3) = (a - 1)(a - 2) .
$$
Therefore $\det A = 0$ for $a = 1$ and for $a = 2$. In both cases the matrix has two equal columns, the first and the third for $a = 1$, the second and the third for $a = 2$.
