In the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x - y + 2z = 0 \\[4pt]
2x + 3z = k
\end{cases}
$$
the minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix}$, of the first two rows and the columns of $x$ and $y$, is nonzero and $\det A = 0$. By the bordered minors theorem, which determinant is enough to compute in order to establish whether $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$?

- The determinant of the matrix formed by the columns of $x$, of $y$ and of the constant terms
- The determinant of $A$
- The minor of rows $1$, $2$ and the columns of $x$ and of the constant terms
- The minor of rows $2$, $3$ and the columns of $y$ and of the constant terms

## Explanation

The bordered minors of the given minor in the augmented matrix are obtained by adding the third row and one of the remaining columns, that is, the column of $z$ or that of the constant terms. The bordered minor with the column of $z$ is $\det A$, which is zero by hypothesis. Hence only the bordered minor formed by the columns of $x$, of $y$ and of the constant terms remains to be computed. If it is zero, by the **bordered minors theorem** $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$; if it is nonzero, $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$. Minors of order $2$ are not enough, because they say nothing about the minors of order $3$.
