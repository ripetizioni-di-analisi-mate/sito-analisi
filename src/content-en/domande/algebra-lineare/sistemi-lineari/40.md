The triples $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 0)$ are solutions of the same linear system in the unknowns $x, y, z$. Which triple is *certainly* a solution of that system?

- $(1, 1, 0)$
- $(2, -1, 0)$
- $(0, 0, 0)$
- $(1, -1, 0)$

## Explanation

The difference of two solutions, $(0, 1, 0) - (1, 0, 0) = (-1, 1, 0)$, is a solution of the associated homogeneous system. By the theorem on the **structure of the solutions** every triple $(1, 0, 0) + t\,(-1, 1, 0)$ is a solution, and for $t = -1$ one obtains $(2, -1, 0)$. The other triples are solutions only for some systems. The system formed by the single equation $x + y + z = 1$ has $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 0)$ among its solutions, whereas $(1, 1, 0)$, $(0, 0, 0)$ and $(1, -1, 0)$ do not satisfy it.
