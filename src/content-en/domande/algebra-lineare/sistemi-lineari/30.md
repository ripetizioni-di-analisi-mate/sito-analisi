How many solutions does the following system have?
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y - z = 1 \\[4pt]
2x + 2y - 2z = 2 \\[4pt]
x - y + z = 3
\end{cases}
$$

- $\infty^1$ solutions
- $\infty^2$ solutions
- Exactly one solution
- No solutions

## Explanation

The second row of the augmented matrix is twice the first, so the second equation is discarded. The rows $(1 \ \ 1 \ \ {-1} \mid 1)$ and $(1 \ \ {-1} \ \ 1 \mid 3)$ are not proportional, and the minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -2$ is nonzero. Therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the solutions are $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^1$. Indeed, adding the first and third equations gives $x = 2$, and the solutions are $(2, \ t - 1, \ t)$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
