Which statement about the following system, in the unknowns $x, y$, is true?
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 3 \\[4pt]
2x - y = 0 \\[4pt]
3x + y = 5
\end{cases}
$$

- The system is inconsistent, because it has more equations than unknowns
- The system has the unique solution $(1, 2)$
- The system has $\infty^1$ solutions
- The system has the unique solution $(2, 1)$

## Explanation

Adding the first two equations gives $3x = 3$, so $x = 1$, and the first gives $y = 2$. The pair $(1, 2)$ also satisfies the third equation, because $3 \cdot 1 + 2 = 5$. Therefore the system is consistent. Since the first two rows of $A$ are not proportional, $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$, equal to the number of unknowns, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the solution $(1, 2)$ is the **only one**. A system with more equations than unknowns can therefore be consistent.
