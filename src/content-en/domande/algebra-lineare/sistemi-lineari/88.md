The operation $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ transforms the augmented matrix $(A \mid b)$ into $(A' \mid b')$:
$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\
2 & 3 & 1 & 5 \\
1 & 0 & 2 & 1
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
1 & 0 & 2 & 1
\end{array}\right).
$$
Which elementary operation transforms $(A' \mid b')$ back into $(A \mid b)$?

- $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$
- $R_2 \to R_2 + 2R_1$
- $R_1 \to R_1 + 2R_2$
- $R_1 \to R_1 - 2R_2$

## Explanation

With $R_2 \to R_2 + 2R_1$ the second row of $(A' \mid b')$ becomes $(0, 1, -1 \mid 1) + 2\,(1, 1, 1 \mid 2) = (2, 3, 1 \mid 5)$, that is, the second row of $(A \mid b)$, and the other rows remain unchanged. So this operation undoes $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$, because the first row, used by both, does not change. Applying $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ again gives instead the row $(-2, -1, -3 \mid -3)$, different from the original one. The operations $R_1 \to R_1 + 2R_2$ and $R_1 \to R_1 - 2R_2$ change the first row, which is already equal to that of $(A \mid b)$, and leave the second row equal to $(0, 1, -1 \mid 1)$. In general $R_i \to R_i + c\, R_j$ is undone by $R_i \to R_i - c\, R_j$. The proof of the theorem on **equivalent systems** is based on this, because the original system is obtained from the transformed one by an elementary operation.
