Is the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$
diagonalizable?

- No, because $m_g(3) = 1 < m_a(3) = 2$
- No, because it has a repeated eigenvalue
- Yes, because it has three distinct eigenvalues
- Yes, because $m_g(3) = m_a(3) = 2$

## Explanation

The matrix is lower triangular, so $p_A(\lambda) = (1 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda)^2$, with $m_a(1) = 1$ and $m_a(3) = 2$. The matrix
$$
A - 3I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$
has three proportional rows, so it has rank $1$ and $m_g(3) = 3 - 1 = 2$. Since $m_g(3) = m_a(3)$ and the eigenvalue $1$ is simple, by the **diagonalizability criterion** the matrix **is diagonalizable**.
