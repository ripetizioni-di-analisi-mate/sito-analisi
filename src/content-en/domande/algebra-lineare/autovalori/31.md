Let $V$ be the subspace of real functions spanned by $e^{2x}$ and $x e^{2x}$, which has dimension $2$, and let $D : V \to V$ be the endomorphism defined by $D(g) = g'$. Which statement is true?

- $x e^{2x}$ is an eigenvector of $D$ associated with the eigenvalue $2$
- There is a basis of $V$ consisting of eigenvectors of $D$
- $D$ is not invertible, because its only eigenvalue is double
- The eigenvectors of $D$ are the functions $c\, e^{2x}$ with $c \neq 0$

## Explanation

We have $D(e^{2x}) = 2e^{2x}$ and $D(x e^{2x}) = e^{2x} + 2x e^{2x}$, so the matrix associated with $D$ with respect to the basis $\{e^{2x}, x e^{2x}\}$ is
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The matrix is triangular, therefore the only eigenvalue of $D$ is $2$, with $m_a(2) = 2$. The system $(A - 2I_2)X = 0$ reduces to the equation $y = 0$, so its solutions are the coordinate vectors $(c, 0)$. Therefore the eigenvectors of $D$ are the functions $c\, e^{2x}$ with $c \neq 0$, and the function $x e^{2x}$ is not an eigenvector. Since $m_g(2) = 1 < 2 = m_a(2)$, there is no basis of $V$ consisting of eigenvectors of $D$. Finally $\det A = 4 \neq 0$, so $0$ is not an eigenvalue and $D$ is invertible.
