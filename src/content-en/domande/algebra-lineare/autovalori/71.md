Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be a nonzero matrix such that $A^2 = 0$. Which statement is true?

- The only eigenvalue of $A$ is $0$, and $A$ is diagonalizable
- The eigenvalues of $A$ are $0$ and $1$
- The only eigenvalue of $A$ is $0$, and $A$ is not diagonalizable
- The eigenvalues of $A$ are $1$ and $-1$

## Explanation

If $Av = \lambda v$ with $v \neq 0$, then $0 = A^2 v = \lambda^2 v$, so $\lambda = 0$. On the other hand $(\det A)^2 = \det(A^2) = 0$ by Binet's theorem, hence $\det A = 0$ and $0$ is an eigenvalue, the only one. If $A$ were diagonalizable, with $P^{-1}AP = D$, the matrix $D$ would be diagonal with all diagonal entries equal to zero, that is, $D = 0$, and therefore $A = PDP^{-1} = 0$. Since $A \neq 0$, the matrix $A$ **is not diagonalizable**; an example is the matrix with a single nonzero entry, equal to $1$, in position $(1, 3)$.
