For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ k & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ have the eigenvalue $3$?

- $k = 2$
- $k = -2$
- $k = -1$
- $k = 4$

## Explanation

The number $3$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ if and only if it is a root of the characteristic polynomial, that is, if $\det(A - 3I_2) = 0$. We have
$$
\det \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 2 \\ k & -2 \end{pmatrix} = 4 - 2k ,
$$
so the condition is $4 - 2k = 0$, that is, $k = 2$. For $k = 2$ the characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^2 - 2\lambda - 3 = (\lambda - 3)(\lambda + 1)$, and the eigenvalues are $3$ and $-1$.
