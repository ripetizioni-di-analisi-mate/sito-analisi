Let $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be the endomorphism defined by $f(v) = v \times (0, 0, 1)$, where $\times$ denotes the cross product. Which statement is true?

- The eigenvalues are $0$, $1$ and $-1$, and $f$ is diagonalizable
- The only real eigenvalue is $0$, with $V_0$ equal to the plane $z = 0$
- The only real eigenvalue is $0$, with $V_0 = \langle (0, 0, 1) \rangle$, and $f$ is diagonalizable
- The only real eigenvalue is $0$, with $V_0 = \langle (0, 0, 1) \rangle$, and $f$ is not diagonalizable

## Explanation

For $v = (x, y, z)$ we have $(x, y, z) \times (0, 0, 1) = (y, -x, 0)$, so the matrix associated with $f$ with respect to the standard basis is
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Expanding along the third row gives $p_A(\lambda) = -\lambda(\lambda^2 + 1)$, and the factor $\lambda^2 + 1$ has no real roots. Hence the only real eigenvalue is $0$, and $V_0 = \ker f$ consists of the vectors parallel to $(0, 0, 1)$, that is, $V_0 = \langle (0, 0, 1) \rangle$. Since $p_A$ does not have three real roots, by the diagonalizability criterion $f$ **is not diagonalizable**.
