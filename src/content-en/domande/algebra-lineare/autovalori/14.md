Which of the following vectors is an eigenvector of the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$?

- $(3, 2)$
- $(1, 1)$
- $(2, 3)$
- $(1, 2)$

## Explanation

A nonzero vector $v$ is an eigenvector of $A$ if $Av$ is a multiple of $v$. We have $A(2, 3) = (2 + 6,\ 6 + 6) = (8, 12) = 4(2, 3)$, so $(2, 3)$ is an **eigenvector** associated with the eigenvalue $4$. On the other hand, $A(3, 2) = (7, 13)$, $A(1, 1) = (3, 5)$ and $A(1, 2) = (5, 7)$. In each of these three cases the ratios between the corresponding components of $Av$ and $v$ are different, so $Av$ is not a multiple of $v$.
