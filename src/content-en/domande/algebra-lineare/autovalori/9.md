- $k \geq 0$
- $k > 0$ and $k \neq 1$
- Every $k \in \mathbb{R}$
- $k > 0$

## Explanation

Expanding along the third row gives $p_A(\lambda) = (2 - \lambda)\left[(1 - \lambda)^2 - k\right]$. If $k < 0$ the second factor has no real roots, so $A$ is not diagonalizable. If $k = 0$ the eigenvalue $1$ is double and $A - I_3$ has rank $2$, so $m_g(1) = 1 < 2$ and $A$ is not diagonalizable. If $k > 0$ the eigenvalues are $2$ and $1 \pm \sqrt{k}$, distinct for $k \neq 1$; for $k = 1$ the eigenvalue $2$ is double and $A - 2I_3$ has rank $1$, so $m_g(2) = 2 = m_a(2)$. Therefore $A$ is **diagonalizable if and only if** $k > 0$.
