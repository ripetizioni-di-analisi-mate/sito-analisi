Which of the following matrices is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ is triangular with eigenvalues $1$ and $2$, real and distinct, so it is **diagonalizable**. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has characteristic polynomial $\lambda^2 + 2$, which has no real roots. The other two matrices have characteristic polynomial $(\lambda - 1)^2$, and for both of them the matrix obtained by subtracting $I_2$ is nonzero and has rank $1$. Therefore for both $m_g(1) = 1 < 2 = m_a(1)$, and neither of them is diagonalizable.
