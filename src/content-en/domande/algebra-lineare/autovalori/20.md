Let
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Both matrices have $0$ as their only eigenvalue. What are the eigenvalues of $A + B$?

- Only $0$
- $0$ and $1$
- $A + B$ has no real eigenvalues
- $1$ and $-1$

## Explanation

We have
$$
A + B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
so $p_{A+B}(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 1 = (\lambda - 1)(\lambda + 1)$. Therefore the eigenvalues of $A + B$ are $1$ and $-1$. The example shows that the eigenvalues of a sum of matrices are not, in general, obtained by adding the eigenvalues of the summands: here the sum of the eigenvalues of $A$ and $B$ would give only $0$.
