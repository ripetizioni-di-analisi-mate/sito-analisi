Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$. Which vector is an eigenvector of the transpose $A^T$ associated with the eigenvalue $3$?

- $(1, 1)$
- $(1, 0)$
- $(1, -1)$
- $(0, 1)$

## Explanation

Since $\det(A^T - \lambda I_2) = \det\big((A - \lambda I_2)^T\big) = \det(A - \lambda I_2)$, the matrices $A$ and $A^T$ have the same characteristic polynomial, so $A^T$ also has the eigenvalues $1$ and $3$. The eigenvectors, however, must be computed again. We have
$$
A^T - 3I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
and the system $(A^T - 3I_2)v = 0$ reduces to the equation $x = 0$, hence the eigenspace of $A^T$ associated with $3$ is $\langle (0, 1) \rangle$. The vector $(1, 1)$ is an eigenvector of $A$ associated with $3$, but $A^T(1, 1) = (1, 5)$ is not a multiple of it.
