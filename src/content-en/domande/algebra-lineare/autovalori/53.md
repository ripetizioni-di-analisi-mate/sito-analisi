For $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$ let
$$
R_\theta = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}
$$
be the matrix of the rotation by the angle $\theta$ in the plane. For which $\theta$ does $R_\theta$ have real eigenvalues?

- Only for $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$
- Only for $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\theta = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$
- For every $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$
- Only for $\theta = 0$

## Explanation

We have $\operatorname{tr} R_\theta = 2\cos\theta$ and $\det R_\theta = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$, so
$$
p_{R_\theta}(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 2\cos\theta\, \lambda + 1 .
$$
The discriminant is $4\cos^2\theta - 4$, which is negative if $\cos^2\theta < 1$ and zero if $\cos^2\theta = 1$. Hence the roots are real if and only if $\cos\theta = \pm 1$, that is, for $\theta = 0$, with $R_0 = I_2$ and eigenvalue $1$, and for $\theta = \pi$, with $R_\pi = -I_2$ and eigenvalue $-1$. For the other angles no line through the origin is mapped to itself.
