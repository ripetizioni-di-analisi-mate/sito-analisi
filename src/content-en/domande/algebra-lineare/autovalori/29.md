Let $u$, $v$, $w$ be eigenvectors of a matrix $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ associated, respectively, with the eigenvalues $1$, $2$, $3$. Which statement is true?

- $u + v$ is an eigenvector associated with the eigenvalue $3$
- $u$, $v$, $w$ form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$
- $A$ is not invertible
- $u$, $v$, $w$ may be linearly dependent

## Explanation

By the theorem on **eigenvectors associated with distinct eigenvalues**, eigenvectors associated with pairwise distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent. Hence $u$, $v$, $w$ are three independent vectors of $\mathbb{R}^3$, and therefore they form a basis. The vector $u + v$ is not an eigenvector, since $A(u + v) = u + 2v$, and the equality $u + 2v = c(u + v)$ would give $(1 - c)u + (2 - c)v = 0$ with coefficients not both zero, contradicting the independence of $u$ and $v$. Finally $\det A = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 6 \neq 0$, so $A$ is invertible.
