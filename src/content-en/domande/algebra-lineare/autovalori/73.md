The matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & k & 0 \\ k & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
depends on the real parameter $k$. Which statement about its diagonalizability is true?

- It is diagonalizable for every $k$, because it is symmetric
- It is not diagonalizable for $k = \pm 1$, because the eigenvalue $2$ is double
- It is diagonalizable only for $k = 0$
- It is diagonalizable only for $k \neq 0$

## Explanation

For every $k$ the matrix $A$ is **symmetric**, so by the spectral theorem it is diagonalizable for every $k \in \mathbb{R}$. The computation confirms the result. Expanding along the third row gives $p_A(\lambda) = (2 - \lambda)\left[(1 - \lambda)^2 - k^2\right]$, with eigenvalues $2$, $1 + k$, $1 - k$, all real. For $k = \pm 1$ the eigenvalue $2$ is double, and the matrix $A - 2I_3$ has its first two rows opposite and the third one zero, hence rank $1$ and $m_g(2) = 2 = m_a(2)$. For $k = 0$ the matrix is already diagonal.
