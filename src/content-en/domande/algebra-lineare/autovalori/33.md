Let $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $D = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Which matrix $A$ satisfies $P^{-1}AP = D$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The equality $P^{-1}AP = D$ is equivalent to $AP = PD$, so the columns of $P$ are eigenvectors of $A$ and the diagonal entries of $D$ are the corresponding eigenvalues, in the same order. Therefore $A(1, 1) = (3, 3)$ and $A(1, -1) = (1, -1)$. Since $(1, 0) = \dfrac{1}{2}(1, 1) + \dfrac{1}{2}(1, -1)$ and $(0, 1) = \dfrac{1}{2}(1, 1) - \dfrac{1}{2}(1, -1)$, linearity gives $A(1, 0) = (2, 1)$ and $A(0, 1) = (1, 2)$, which are the columns of $A$. Hence $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has the same eigenvectors with the eigenvalues swapped, that is, $(1, 1)$ associated with $1$ and $(1, -1)$ associated with $3$.
