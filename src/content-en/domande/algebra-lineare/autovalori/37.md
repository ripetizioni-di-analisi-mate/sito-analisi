For which $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & k \\ 0 & 1 & k - 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
diagonalizable?

- $k \neq 2$
- $k \neq -1$ and $k \neq 2$
- $k \neq -1$
- Every $k \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

Expanding $\det(A - \lambda I_3)$ along the first row gives
$$
p_A(\lambda) = (2 - \lambda)\left[\lambda^2 - (k - 1)\lambda - k\right] = (2 - \lambda)(\lambda - k)(\lambda + 1) ,
$$
so the eigenvalues $2$, $k$ and $-1$ are real for every $k$. If $k \neq 2$ and $k \neq -1$ the three eigenvalues are distinct, so $A$ is diagonalizable. If $k = 2$ the eigenvalue $2$ is double and $A - 2I_3$ has a zero first row and the other two rows proportional, so it has rank $1$ and $m_g(2) = 2 = m_a(2)$. If $k = -1$ the eigenvalue $-1$ is double and $A + I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank $2$, so $m_g(-1) = 1 < 2 = m_a(-1)$. Therefore $A$ is **diagonalizable if and only if** $k \neq -1$.
