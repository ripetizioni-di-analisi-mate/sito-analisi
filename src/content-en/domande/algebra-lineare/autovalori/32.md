A matrix $A \in M_4(\mathbb{R})$ has $2$ and $5$ as its only real eigenvalues, with $\dim V_2 = 2$ and $\dim V_5 = 1$. Which statement is true?

- $A$ is diagonalizable, because its two eigenvalues are distinct
- $A$ is not diagonalizable, because there is no basis of $\mathbb{R}^4$ consisting of eigenvectors of $A$
- It cannot be determined whether $A$ is diagonalizable without knowing $p_A$
- $A$ is diagonalizable, because $V_2 \cap V_5 = \{0\}$

## Explanation

Every eigenvector of $A$ belongs to $V_2$ or to $V_5$. By the observation on the **union of bases of eigenspaces**, the sum $V_2 + V_5$ is direct and has dimension $\dim V_2 + \dim V_5 = 3$, so all the eigenvectors of $A$ lie in a subspace of dimension $3$ of $\mathbb{R}^4$. Therefore there are no four linearly independent eigenvectors of $A$, and there is no basis of $\mathbb{R}^4$ consisting of eigenvectors. By the theorem on bases of eigenvectors the matrix $A$ **is not diagonalizable**, whatever $p_A$ is. The equality $V_2 \cap V_5 = \{0\}$ holds for two eigenspaces associated with distinct eigenvalues of any matrix, so by itself it gives no information about diagonalizability.
