The matrix $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ has the eigenvalue $2$, with eigenspace $V_2 = \langle (2, -1, 1) \rangle$. The matrix $B \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ commutes with $A$, that is, $AB = BA$, and the vector $B\,(2, -1, 1)$ has first component $6$. Which vector is $B\,(2, -1, 1)$?

- $(6, -1, 1)$
- $(6, 3, -3)$
- $(6, -3, 3)$
- $(6, -2, 2)$

## Explanation

Let $v = (2, -1, 1)$. Since $AB = BA$ and $Av = 2v$, we have $A(Bv) = B(Av) = 2\,Bv$. Hence $Bv$ is either the zero vector or an eigenvector for the eigenvalue $2$, and in both cases it belongs to $V_2$. Therefore $Bv = c\,v$ for some $c \in \mathbb{R}$, and the first component gives $2c = 6$, that is, $c = 3$. It follows that $Bv = 3v = (6, -3, 3)$. The vector $(6, -2, 2)$ combines the given first component with the other two components of $2v$, and none of the other proposed vectors is a multiple of $v$.
