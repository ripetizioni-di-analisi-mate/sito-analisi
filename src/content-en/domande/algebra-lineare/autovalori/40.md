For the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 3 & 6 & 9 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
which statement is true?

- The eigenvalues are $0$ and $14$, with $m_g(0) = 1$
- The eigenvalues are $0$ and $14$, with $m_g(0) = 2$
- The eigenvalues are $1$, $4$ and $9$
- The only eigenvalue is $0$, with $m_g(0) = 2$

## Explanation

The rows of $A$ are multiples of the first, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$. Since $V_0 = \ker A$, we have $m_g(0) = 3 - 1 = 2$, and by the relation between the multiplicities $m_a(0) \geq 2$. Therefore $p_A(\lambda) = -\lambda^2(\lambda - \mu)$ for some number $\mu$, and comparing with the coefficient of $\lambda^2$, which is $\operatorname{tr} A$, gives $\mu = 1 + 4 + 9 = 14$. Hence the eigenvalues are $0$, with $m_g(0) = 2$, and $14$.
