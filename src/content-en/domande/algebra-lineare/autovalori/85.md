A matrix $A \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ satisfies $Av = 3v$ and $Aw = 3w$ for two linearly independent vectors $v$ and $w$. Which matrix is $A$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- It cannot be determined without knowing $v$ and $w$

## Explanation

The vectors $v$ and $w$ belong to the eigenspace $V_3$ and are linearly independent, so $\dim V_3 \geq 2$. Since $V_3$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$, we have $V_3 = \mathbb{R}^2$, that is, $Ax = 3x$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}^2$. In particular the columns of $A$ are $Ae_1 = 3e_1$ and $Ae_2 = 3e_2$, hence $A = 3I_2$, whatever $v$ and $w$ are. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ has the only eigenvalue $3$ but $V_3 = \langle (1, 0) \rangle$, which does not contain two independent vectors.
