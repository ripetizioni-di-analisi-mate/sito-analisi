Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be a matrix in which the sum of the entries of each row equals $4$. Which statement is certainly true?

- $(1, 1, 1)$ is an eigenvector of $A$ associated with the eigenvalue $4$
- $4$ is the only eigenvalue of $A$
- $(1, 1, 1)$ is an eigenvector of $A^T$ associated with the eigenvalue $4$
- $\det A = 4$

## Explanation

The $i$-th component of $A(1, 1, 1)$ is $a_{i1} + a_{i2} + a_{i3}$, that is, the sum of the entries of the $i$-th row. Since each sum equals $4$, we have $A(1, 1, 1) = (4, 4, 4) = 4(1, 1, 1)$, so $(1, 1, 1)$ is an **eigenvector** associated with the eigenvalue $4$. The other statements do not hold for every matrix of this kind. For example
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}
$$
has row sums $4$, also has the eigenvalue $-2$ and has $\det A = -32$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ has row sums $4$, and its transpose maps $(1, 1, 1)$ to $(5, 4, 3)$, which is not a multiple of $(1, 1, 1)$.
