The matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues $1$ and $2$. Which equality between matrices is true?

- $A^2 - 3A + 2I_2 = I_2$
- $A^2 + 3A + 2I_2 = 0$
- $A^2 = 2I_2$
- $A^2 - 3A + 2I_2 = 0$

## Explanation

The eigenvalues $1$ and $2$ are distinct, so $A = PDP^{-1}$ with $D = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, and by the powers of a diagonalizable matrix
$$
A^2 - 3A + 2I_2 = P\,(D^2 - 3D + 2I_2)\,P^{-1} .
$$
The matrix $D^2 - 3D + 2I_2$ is diagonal with entries $1 - 3 + 2 = 0$ and $4 - 6 + 2 = 0$, so it is zero, and therefore $A^2 - 3A + 2I_2 = 0$. The direct computation confirms it, since $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & -6 \\ 3 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$. By the same computation $A^2 + 3A + 2I_2 = P\,(D^2 + 3D + 2I_2)\,P^{-1}$ has eigenvalues $6$ and $12$, so it is not zero.
