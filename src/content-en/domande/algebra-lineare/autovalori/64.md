Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$. Which vectors $v \in \mathbb{R}^2$ satisfy $A^2 v = v$?

- Only the vectors of the line $\langle (1, 0) \rangle$
- Only the vectors of the line $\langle (1, -1) \rangle$
- Only the vectors of the two lines $\langle (1, 0) \rangle$ and $\langle (1, -1) \rangle$
- All vectors of $\mathbb{R}^2$

## Explanation

The matrix is triangular with eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$; the eigenspaces are $V_1 = \langle (1, 0) \rangle$ and, from the equation $2x + 2y = 0$ of the system $(A + I_2)v = 0$, $V_{-1} = \langle (1, -1) \rangle$. Every vector of these two lines satisfies $A^2 v = (\pm 1)^2 v = v$. The set of vectors with $A^2 v = v$ is the kernel of $A^2 - I_2$, so it is a subspace, and it therefore also contains the sum of the two lines, which is all of $\mathbb{R}^2$. Indeed the direct computation gives $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 - 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I_2$. The union of the two lines is not a subspace, because for example $(1, 0) + (1, -1) = (2, -1)$ does not belong to it.
