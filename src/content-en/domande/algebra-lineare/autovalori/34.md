For the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, what is the entry in position $(1, 2)$ of $A^n$, for every integer $n \geq 1$?

- $2^n - 3^n$
- $n \cdot 3^{n-1}$
- $3^n - 2^n$
- $1$

## Explanation

The eigenvalues are $3$ and $2$, with $V_3 = \langle (1, 0) \rangle$ and $V_2 = \langle (1, -1) \rangle$. With $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ we have $P^2 = I_2$, so $P^{-1} = P$ and
$$
\begin{aligned}
A^n &= P \begin{pmatrix} 3^n & 0 \\ 0 & 2^n \end{pmatrix} P = \begin{pmatrix} 3^n & 2^n \\ 0 & -2^n \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= \begin{pmatrix} 3^n & 3^n - 2^n \\ 0 & 2^n \end{pmatrix} .
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore the entry in position $(1, 2)$ is $3^n - 2^n$. For $n = 2$ we obtain $5$, in agreement with the row-by-column product $A \cdot A$.
