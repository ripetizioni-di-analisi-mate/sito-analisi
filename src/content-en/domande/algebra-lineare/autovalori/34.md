A population is divided into young individuals and adults. The vector $x_n = (g_n, a_n)$ of their numbers at step $n$ satisfies $x_{n+1} = L x_n$, with
$$
L = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\[2pt] \dfrac{1}{2} & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
and $g_0$, $a_0$ are positive. For large $n$ the total population is multiplied, at each step, by a factor that tends to a constant value, and the ratio $\dfrac{g_n}{a_n}$ tends to a limit. What are the factor and the limit of the ratio?

- Factor $2$, ratio $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- Factor $1$, ratio $4$
- Factor $2$, ratio $4$
- Factor $2$, ratio $2$

## Explanation

We have $p_L(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - \lambda - 2 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda + 1)$, so the eigenvalues are $2$ and $-1$. The system $(L - 2I_2)v = 0$ gives $g = 4a$, hence $V_2 = \langle (4, 1) \rangle$; similarly $V_{-1} = \langle (-2, 1) \rangle$. Writing $x_0 = c_1 (4, 1) + c_2 (-2, 1)$, we get $c_1 = \dfrac{g_0 + 2a_0}{6} > 0$ and
$$
x_n = c_1\, 2^n (4, 1) + c_2 (-1)^n (-2, 1) .
$$
The second term stays bounded, while the first grows like $2^n$. Therefore for large $n$ the vector $x_n$ is approximately $c_1 2^n (4, 1)$, the population is multiplied by a factor that tends to $2$, and the ratio $\dfrac{g_n}{a_n}$ tends to $4$.
