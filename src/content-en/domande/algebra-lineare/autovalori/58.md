Let $A, B \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ be two matrices such that $AB = BA$, and let $v$ be an eigenvector of $A$ associated with the eigenvalue $\lambda$, with $\dim V_\lambda = 1$. Which statement is certainly true?

- $v$ is an eigenvector of $B$ associated with the same eigenvalue $\lambda$
- $v$ is an eigenvector of $B$
- $A$ and $B$ have the same eigenvalues
- $B$ is a multiple of the identity matrix

## Explanation

Since $AB = BA$, we have $A(Bv) = B(Av) = B(\lambda v) = \lambda Bv$. So $Bv$ is either the zero vector or an eigenvector of $A$ associated with $\lambda$, and in both cases it belongs to $V_\lambda$. Since $V_\lambda = \langle v \rangle$ has dimension $1$, we have $Bv = \mu v$ for some real number $\mu$, hence $v$ is an **eigenvector** of $B$, associated with the eigenvalue $\mu$. The diagonal matrices $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ commute and have the common eigenvector $(1, 0)$, associated with $1$ for $A$ and with $3$ for $B$; their eigenvalues are different and $B$ is not a multiple of the identity.
