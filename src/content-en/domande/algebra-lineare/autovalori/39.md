Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be the endomorphism such that $f(1, 1) = (3, 3)$ and $f(1, -1) = (0, 0)$. Which statement is true?

- The eigenvalues are $3$ and $0$, and $f$ is diagonalizable
- The eigenvalues are $3$ and $0$, and $f$ is not diagonalizable
- The only eigenvalue is $3$, and $f$ is not diagonalizable
- The eigenvalues are $3$ and $-1$, and $f$ is diagonalizable

## Explanation

We have $f(1, 1) = 3(1, 1)$, so $(1, 1)$ is an eigenvector associated with the eigenvalue $3$. We also have $f(1, -1) = 0 \cdot (1, -1)$ with $(1, -1) \neq 0$, so $(1, -1)$ is an eigenvector associated with the eigenvalue $0$. The two vectors are independent, so they form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$ consisting of eigenvectors, and $f$ **is diagonalizable**, with matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ with respect to this basis. The eigenvalue $0$ indicates that $f$ is not invertible, and invertibility is a property independent of diagonalizability.
