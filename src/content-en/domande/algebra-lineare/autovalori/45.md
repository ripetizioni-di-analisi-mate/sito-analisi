Let $f : M_2(\mathbb{R}) \to M_2(\mathbb{R})$ be the endomorphism defined by $f(X) = X^T$. Which statement about the eigenvalues and eigenspaces of $f$ is true?

- The eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$, with $\dim V_1 = 3$ and $\dim V_{-1} = 1$
- The eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$, with $\dim V_1 = 2$ and $\dim V_{-1} = 2$
- The only eigenvalue is $1$, with $\dim V_1 = 4$
- The eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$, with $\dim V_1 = 1$ and $\dim V_{-1} = 3$

## Explanation

If $X^T = \lambda X$ with $X \neq 0$, then $X = (X^T)^T = \lambda X^T = \lambda^2 X$, so $\lambda^2 = 1$ and the only possible eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$. The equality $X^T = X$ characterizes the symmetric matrices $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{pmatrix}$, so $V_1$ has dimension $3$. The equality $X^T = -X$ characterizes the skew-symmetric matrices $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & b \\ -b & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, so $V_{-1}$ has dimension $1$. Since $3 + 1 = 4 = \dim M_2(\mathbb{R})$, the endomorphism $f$ is also diagonalizable.
