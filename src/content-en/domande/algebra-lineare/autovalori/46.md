An endomorphism $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ has associated matrix
$$
M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
with respect to the basis $\mathcal{B} = \big( (1, 0),\ (1, 1) \big)$, both in the domain and in the codomain. Which vector of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is an eigenvector of $f$ associated with the eigenvalue $-1$?

- $(1, -3)$
- $(2, 3)$
- $(1, 0)$
- $(3, 2)$

## Explanation

The eigenvectors of $M$ are the coordinate columns, with respect to $\mathcal{B}$, of the eigenvectors of $f$. The system $(M + I_2)c = 0$, with $M + I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, reduces to the equation $3c_1 + c_2 = 0$, so the coordinates $(1, -3)$ give an eigenvector associated with $-1$. The corresponding vector is
$$
1 \cdot (1, 0) - 3 \cdot (1, 1) = (-2, -3) ,
$$
hence every nonzero multiple of $(2, 3)$ is an **eigenvector** of $f$ associated with $-1$. As a check, the matrix of $f$ with respect to the standard basis is $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ and it maps $(2, 3)$ to $(-2, -3)$. The vector $(1, -3)$ is the coordinate column, and $f(1, -3) = (8, 3)$ is not a multiple of it.
