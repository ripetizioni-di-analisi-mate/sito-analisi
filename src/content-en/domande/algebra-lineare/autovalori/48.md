A sequence of vectors of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is defined by $x_{n+1} = A x_n$ for $n \geq 0$, with
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & \dfrac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix} .
$$
For which initial vectors $x_0$ does the sequence $x_n$ tend to the zero vector?

- For every $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^2$
- Only for $x_0 = 0$
- For the vectors of the line $\langle (3, 2) \rangle$
- For the vectors of the line $\langle (0, 1) \rangle$

## Explanation

The matrix is triangular with distinct eigenvalues $2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The system $\left(A - \dfrac{1}{2} I_2\right)v = 0$ gives $x = 0$, so $V_{1/2} = \langle (0, 1) \rangle$; the system $(A - 2I_2)v = 0$ gives $x = \dfrac{3}{2}y$, so $V_2 = \langle (3, 2) \rangle$. Writing $x_0 = a(3, 2) + b(0, 1)$, we have $x_n = A^n x_0$ and therefore
$$
x_n = a \cdot 2^n (3, 2) + b \cdot \frac{1}{2^n} (0, 1) .
$$
The second term tends to $0$, while the first diverges if $a \neq 0$. Hence $x_n$ tends to the zero vector if and only if $a = 0$, that is, if and only if $x_0$ belongs to the eigenspace $V_{1/2} = \langle (0, 1) \rangle$. On the line $\langle (3, 2) \rangle$ the sequence is $x_n = 2^n x_0$, which diverges for $x_0 \neq 0$.
