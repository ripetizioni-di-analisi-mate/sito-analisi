Let $u$ and $v$ be eigenvectors of a matrix $A \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ associated, respectively, with the eigenvalues $1$ and $3$, and let $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ be not both zero. When is the vector $au + bv$ an eigenvector of $A$?

- For every choice of $a$ and $b$
- If and only if $a = b$
- For no choice of $a$ and $b$
- If and only if $a = 0$ or $b = 0$

## Explanation

By the theorem on eigenvectors associated with distinct eigenvalues, $u$ and $v$ are linearly independent, so $au + bv \neq 0$. We have $A(au + bv) = au + 3bv$, and this vector equals $c(au + bv)$ if and only if $a(1 - c)u + b(3 - c)v = 0$. By the independence of $u$ and $v$ this is equivalent to $a(1 - c) = 0$ and $b(3 - c) = 0$. If $a$ and $b$ were both nonzero, we would have $c = 1$ and $c = 3$, which is impossible; if instead $b = 0$, then $c = 1$ works, and if $a = 0$, then $c = 3$ works. Hence $au + bv$ is an eigenvector **if and only if** $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.
