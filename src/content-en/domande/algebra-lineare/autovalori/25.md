Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and let $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ be the set consisting of the zero vector and all eigenvectors of $A$. Which statement about $S$ is true?

- $S$ is the union of the lines $\langle (1, 0) \rangle$ and $\langle (1, 1) \rangle$, and it is not a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$
- $S = \mathbb{R}^2$, because $V_1 + V_2 = \mathbb{R}^2$
- $S = \{0\}$, because $V_1 \cap V_2 = \{0\}$
- $S$ is the union of the lines $\langle (1, 0) \rangle$ and $\langle (1, 1) \rangle$, and it is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$

## Explanation

The matrix is triangular, so its eigenvalues are $1$ and $2$. The system $(A - I_2)v = 0$ gives $y = 0$, so $V_1 = \langle (1, 0) \rangle$; the system $(A - 2I_2)v = 0$ gives $x = y$, so $V_2 = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$. Every eigenvector lies in one of the two eigenspaces, hence $S = V_1 \cup V_2$. The set $S$ is not closed under addition, since $(1, 0) + (1, 1) = (2, 1)$ and
$$
A \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is not a multiple of $(2, 1)$. Therefore $S$ is not a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$, while each eigenspace is.
