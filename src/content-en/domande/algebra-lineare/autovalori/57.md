Let $A, B \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be two similar matrices, with $B = P^{-1}AP$ for an invertible matrix $P$. Which statement is certainly true?

- $A^2$ and $B^2$ are similar
- $A$ and $B$ have the same eigenvectors
- $A$ and $B$ are diagonalizable
- $A - B$ is the zero matrix

## Explanation

From the equality $B = P^{-1}AP$ it follows that
$$
B^2 = (P^{-1}AP)(P^{-1}AP) = P^{-1}A(PP^{-1})AP = P^{-1}A^2P ,
$$
so $A^2$ and $B^2$ are **similar**, through the same matrix $P$. The other statements do not hold in general. The matrices $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, completed with a $1$ in position $(3, 3)$, are similar through the matrix that swaps the first two coordinates, but they are not diagonalizable, they are different and they have different eigenspaces.
