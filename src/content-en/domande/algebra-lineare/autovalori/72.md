Which of the following conditions on a matrix $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ is sufficient for $A$ to be diagonalizable?

- $\det A \neq 0$
- All the roots of $p_A$ are real
- $A$ is triangular
- $A$ has three distinct real eigenvalues

## Explanation

If $A$ has three distinct real eigenvalues, each has $m_a = 1$ and therefore $m_g = 1$, hence by the theorem on **distinct real eigenvalues** the matrix is diagonalizable. The other conditions are not enough. The matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is triangular, has $\det A = 2 \neq 0$ and has all real eigenvalues; however $A - I_3$ has rank $2$, so $m_g(1) = 1 < 2 = m_a(1)$ and $A$ is not diagonalizable.
