Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$. Which matrix is $A^{20}$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2^{20} & 2^{20} \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

We have $\operatorname{tr} A = 1$ and $\det A = -2 + 2 = 0$, so $p_A(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - \lambda$ and the eigenvalues are $0$ and $1$, distinct. Therefore the matrix is diagonalizable, with $A = PDP^{-1}$ and $D = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Since $0^{20} = 0$ and $1^{20} = 1$, we have $D^{20} = D$, so $A^{20} = PD^{20}P^{-1} = PDP^{-1} = A$. The same result follows from the equality $A^2 = A$, which is checked with a row-by-column product.
