As $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, consider the family of matrices $A_k = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ k & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. There is exactly one value of $k$ for which $A_k$ has a single eigenvalue, counted twice: which one?

- $k = 1$
- $k = 3$
- $k = -1$
- $k = -4$

## Explanation

We have $\operatorname{tr} A_k = 4$ and $\det A_k = 3 - k$, so $p_{A_k}(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 - k$. The polynomial has a double root if and only if the discriminant is zero, that is,
$$
16 - 4(3 - k) = 4 + 4k = 0 ,
$$
hence $k = -1$. For this value $p_{A_k}(\lambda) = (\lambda - 2)^2$ and the double eigenvalue is $2$. For $k > -1$ the eigenvalues are real and distinct, for $k < -1$ they are not real.
