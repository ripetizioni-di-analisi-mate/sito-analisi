For the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} -4 & 3 \\ -6 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, what is the entry in position $(1, 1)$ of $A^n$, for every integer $n \geq 1$?

- $2^{n+1} - (-1)^n$
- $2(-1)^n + 2^n$
- $2(-1)^n - 2^n$
- $(-1)^n - 2^n$

## Explanation

The characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^2 - \lambda - 2 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda + 1)$, so the eigenvalues are $2$ and $-1$, distinct. From $A - 2I_2$ we obtain the equation $-6x + 3y = 0$, so $V_2 = \langle (1, 2) \rangle$; from $A + I_2$ we obtain $-3x + 3y = 0$, so $V_{-1} = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$. With
$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad D^n = \begin{pmatrix} 2^n & 0 \\ 0 & (-1)^n \end{pmatrix}
$$
we have $A^n = PD^nP^{-1}$. The first row of $PD^n$ is $\left(2^n,\ (-1)^n\right)$ and the first column of $P^{-1}$ is $(-1, 2)$, so the entry in position $(1, 1)$ is $-2^n + 2(-1)^n$. For $n = 1$ we recover $-4$.
