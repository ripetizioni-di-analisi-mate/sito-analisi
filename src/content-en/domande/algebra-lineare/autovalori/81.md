Let $A, B \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$, with $A$ invertible. Which statement about the products $AB$ and $BA$ is certainly true?

- $AB$ and $BA$ are similar
- $AB = BA$
- $AB$ and $BA$ have the same eigenvectors
- If $A$ and $B$ are diagonalizable, then $AB$ is diagonalizable as well

## Explanation

Since $A$ is invertible, we can compute
$$
A^{-1}(AB)A = (A^{-1}A)(BA) = BA ,
$$
so $BA$ and $AB$ are **similar** through the matrix $A$, and therefore they have the same characteristic polynomial. The other statements do not hold in general. With $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$, both diagonalizable because they have the distinct eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$, we get $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $BA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$; so $AB \neq BA$, and $AB$ is not diagonalizable, since it has the double eigenvalue $1$ with $m_g(1) = 1$. With $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ the eigenvalue $\sqrt{2}$ has eigenvector $(1, \sqrt{2})$ for $AB$ and $(\sqrt{2}, 1)$ for $BA$.
