Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 4 \\ 4 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$. Which of the following matrices $B$ satisfies $B^2 = A$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{5} & 2 \\ 2 & \sqrt{5} \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The eigenvalues of $A$ are $9$ and $1$, with $V_9 = \langle (1, 1) \rangle$ and $V_1 = \langle (1, -1) \rangle$, so $A = PDP^{-1}$ with $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $D = \begin{pmatrix} 9 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. A matrix $B$ with $B^2 = A$ is obtained as $B = PEP^{-1}$, with $E = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, since $B^2 = PE^2P^{-1} = PDP^{-1} = A$. The matrix $B$ therefore has eigenvector $(1, 1)$ associated with $3$ and eigenvector $(1, -1)$ associated with $1$, and we get $B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. As a check, $B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 4 + 1 & 2 + 2 \\ 2 + 2 & 1 + 4 \end{pmatrix} = A$. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has square $\begin{pmatrix} 5 & -4 \\ -4 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$.
