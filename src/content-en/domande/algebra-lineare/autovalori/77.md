Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ and let $P$ be the matrix with columns $v_1, v_2, v_3$, in this order, such that
$$
P^{-1}AP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which matrix $Q$ satisfies $Q^{-1}AQ = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$?

- $Q$ with columns $v_3, v_1, v_2$
- $Q$ with columns $v_2, v_3, v_1$
- $Q$ with columns $v_3, v_2, v_1$
- $Q = P$, because the diagonalizing matrix is unique

## Explanation

From the equality $P^{-1}AP = D$, which is equivalent to $AP = PD$, it follows that $Av_1 = v_1$, $Av_2 = 2v_2$ and $Av_3 = 3v_3$. The equality $Q^{-1}AQ = D'$ requires the $j$-th column of $Q$ to be an eigenvector associated with the $j$-th diagonal entry of $D'$. So the first column of $Q$ is an eigenvector associated with $3$, the second with $1$ and the third with $2$, and a possible choice is $Q$ with columns $v_3, v_1, v_2$. The columns of $Q$ are those of $P$ in another order, hence they are independent and $Q$ is invertible. The diagonalizing matrix is not unique, since the order of the columns can be changed by reordering $D$ in the same way.
