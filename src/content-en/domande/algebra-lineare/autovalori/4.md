The vector $v = (1, 1, 1)$ is an eigenvector of the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which statement about $A$ is true?

- The eigenvalues are $2$ and $1$, and $A$ is not diagonalizable
- The eigenvalues are $2$ and $1$, and $A$ is diagonalizable
- The eigenvalues are $2$, $3$ and $0$, and $A$ is diagonalizable
- The eigenvalues are $2$ and $-1$, and $A$ is not diagonalizable

## Explanation

We have $Av = (2, 2, 2) = 2v$, so $2$ is an eigenvalue. The characteristic polynomial is $p_A(\lambda) = -\lambda^3 + 5\lambda^2 - 8\lambda + 4$, and since $2$ is one of its roots, dividing by $\lambda - 2$ gives
$$
p_A(\lambda) = -(\lambda - 2)(\lambda^2 - 3\lambda + 2) = -(\lambda - 2)^2(\lambda - 1) .
$$
Hence the eigenvalues are $1$, simple, and $2$, with $m_a(2) = 2$. The matrix
$$
A - 2I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
has its first two rows equal and the third row independent of them, so it has rank $2$ and $m_g(2) = 1 < m_a(2)$. By the **diagonalizability criterion** the matrix $A$ **is not diagonalizable**.
