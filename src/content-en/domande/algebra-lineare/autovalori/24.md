Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be the endomorphism defined by $f(x, y) = (x - y,\ x + y)$. Which statement is true?

- $f$ has the double eigenvalue $1$
- $f$ has no real eigenvalues
- $f$ has the eigenvalues $0$ and $2$
- $f$ has the eigenvalue $1$, with eigenspace the $x$-axis

## Explanation

The matrix associated with $f$ with respect to the standard basis is $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, so
$$
p_A(\lambda) = (1 - \lambda)^2 + 1 = \lambda^2 - 2\lambda + 2 .
$$
The discriminant is $4 - 8 = -4 < 0$, so $p_A$ has no real roots and $f$ **has no real eigenvalues**. The values $0$ and $2$ come from the polynomial $(1 - \lambda)^2 - 1$, that is, from the wrong sign of the product $(-1) \cdot 1$ of the off-diagonal entries. Geometrically, $f$ rotates every vector by the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and multiplies its length by $\sqrt{2}$, so no line through the origin is mapped into itself.
