Let $A \in M_2(\mathbb{R})$ be a matrix such that $A^2 = I_2$ and $\operatorname{tr} A = 0$. What is $\det A$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $2$
- $-1$

## Explanation

From the hypothesis $A^2 = I_2$ it follows that $(A - I_2)(A + I_2) = A^2 - I_2 = 0$. If $A - I_2$ were invertible, multiplying on the left by its inverse would give $A + I_2 = 0$, that is, $A = -I_2$, whose trace is $-2 \neq 0$; hence $\det(A - I_2) = 0$ and $1$ is an eigenvalue. In the same way, if $A + I_2$ were invertible we would have $A = I_2$, whose trace is $2$; hence $-1$ is also an eigenvalue. Therefore the eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$, and $\det A$, which is their product, equals $-1$.
