Let $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ be any matrix. Which statement is true?

- $A$ has at least one real eigenvalue
- $A$ has three real eigenvalues, counted with algebraic multiplicity
- $A$ has at least one real eigenvalue only if it is triangular or symmetric
- $A$ may have no real eigenvalues, like the matrix of a rotation

## Explanation

The characteristic polynomial $p_A(\lambda) = \det(A - \lambda I_3)$ has degree $3$, real coefficients and leading coefficient $-1$. So $p_A(\lambda) \to -\infty$ as $\lambda \to +\infty$ and $p_A(\lambda) \to +\infty$ as $\lambda \to -\infty$. Since $p_A$ is continuous and takes values of opposite sign, by the intermediate zero theorem there exists $\lambda_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ with $p_A(\lambda_0) = 0$, hence $A$ has **at least one real eigenvalue**. The number is not always three, because the matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
has $p_A(\lambda) = (2 - \lambda)(\lambda^2 + 1)$ and therefore the only real eigenvalue $2$. The rotation of the plane by the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ has no real eigenvalues, but its matrix has order $2$.
