For the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
which statement is true?

- $V_1 = \langle (1, 0, 0) \rangle$ and $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$
- $V_1 = \langle (1, 0, 0),\ (0, 1, 1) \rangle$ and $A$ is not diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$
- $V_1 = \langle (1, 0, 0) \rangle$ and $A$ is not diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$
- $V_1 = \langle (0, 1, 0) \rangle$ and $A$ is not diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

Expanding along the first row gives $p_A(\lambda) = (1 - \lambda)\left[(1 - \lambda)^2 + 1\right] = (1 - \lambda)(\lambda^2 - 2\lambda + 2)$. The factor $\lambda^2 - 2\lambda + 2$ has discriminant $4 - 8 = -4 < 0$, so the only real eigenvalue is $1$ and $p_A$ does not have three real roots. By the diagonalizability criterion the matrix $A$ **is not diagonalizable** over $\mathbb{R}$. The eigenspace $V_1$ is the kernel of $A - I_3$, that is, the solution set of the equations $-z = 0$ and $y = 0$, so $V_1 = \langle (1, 0, 0) \rangle$.
