Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. The function $x(t) = e^{3t} v$, with $v \in \mathbb{R}^2$ constant and nonzero, solves the system of differential equations $x'(t) = A\, x(t)$. Which vector can $v$ be?

- $(1, 1)$
- $(1, -1)$
- $(2, 1)$
- $(1, 2)$

## Explanation

Differentiating gives $x'(t) = 3e^{3t} v$, while $A\, x(t) = e^{3t} Av$. Since $e^{3t} \neq 0$, the equality $x'(t) = A\, x(t)$ holds for every $t$ if and only if $Av = 3v$. Hence $v$ must be an eigenvector of $A$ associated with the eigenvalue $3$. We have
$$
A \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad A \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
so $(1, 1)$ satisfies the condition, while $(1, -1)$ is an eigenvector associated with $-1$. Finally, $A(2, 1) = (4, 5)$ and $A(1, 2) = (5, 4)$ are not multiples of the original vectors.
