For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the vector $v = (1, 1)$ an eigenvector of the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & k \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$?

- $k = 4$
- $k = 5$
- $k = -4$
- $k = 3$

## Explanation

We compute $Av = (1 + k,\ 5)$. The vector $v$ is an eigenvector if $Av = \lambda v$ for some number $\lambda$, and since the two components of $v$ are equal, the two components of $Av$ must be equal as well. The second component gives $\lambda = 5$, so we need $1 + k = 5$, that is, $k = 4$. Indeed, for $k = 4$ we get $Av = (5, 5) = 5v$, and the number $5$ is the eigenvalue associated with $v$.
