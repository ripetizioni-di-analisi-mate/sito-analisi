Let $D$ be the endomorphism of the vector space of infinitely differentiable functions on $\mathbb{R}$ defined by $D(g) = g'$. Which statement about the functions $e^{x}$, $e^{2x}$ and $e^{3x}$ is true?

- They are eigenvectors of $D$ associated with the eigenvalue $1$, so they span an eigenspace of dimension $3$
- They are not eigenvectors of $D$, because $D(e^{2x}) = 2e^{2x}$ is different from $e^{2x}$
- They are eigenvectors of $D$ associated with the eigenvalues $1$, $2$ and $3$, so they are linearly independent
- They are eigenvectors of $D$ associated with the eigenvalues $1$, $2$ and $3$, and they are linearly dependent because $e^{3x} = e^{x} \cdot e^{2x}$

## Explanation

We have $D(e^{x}) = e^{x}$, $D(e^{2x}) = 2e^{2x}$ and $D(e^{3x}) = 3e^{3x}$, and none of the three functions is the zero function. So $e^{x}$, $e^{2x}$, $e^{3x}$ are eigenvectors of $D$ associated with the distinct eigenvalues $1$, $2$, $3$. By the theorem on **eigenvectors associated with distinct eigenvalues**, which holds in every vector space, the three functions are linearly independent. The equality $e^{3x} = e^{x} \cdot e^{2x}$ expresses a product of functions, which is not a linear combination, hence it does not give a linear dependence relation.
