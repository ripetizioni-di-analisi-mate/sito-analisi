A matrix $A \in M_3(\mathbb{R})$ has $\operatorname{tr} A = 6$ and $\det A = 6$, and $1$ is one of its eigenvalues. What are the other two roots of the characteristic polynomial of $A$?

- $2$ and $3$
- $-2$ and $-3$
- $1$ and $5$
- $1$ and $6$

## Explanation

For $n = 3$ the leading coefficient of $p_A$ is $-1$, the coefficient of $\lambda^2$ is $\operatorname{tr} A = 6$ and the constant term is $\det A = 6$, so $p_A(\lambda) = -\lambda^3 + 6\lambda^2 + c\lambda + 6$ for some number $c$. Since $1$ is an eigenvalue, $p_A(1) = -1 + 6 + c + 6 = 0$, so $c = -11$. Dividing by $\lambda - 1$ we obtain
$$
p_A(\lambda) = -(\lambda - 1)(\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6) = -(\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 3) .
$$
Hence the other two roots are $2$ and $3$, and indeed $1 + 2 + 3 = 6$ and $1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 6$.
