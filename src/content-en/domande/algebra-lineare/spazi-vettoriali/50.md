Let $W = \{ A \in M_2(\mathbb{R}) : A^2 = O \}$. The set $W$ contains the zero matrix and is closed under scalar multiplication, because $(\lambda A)^2 = \lambda^2 A^2$. Which pair of matrices of $W$ shows that $W$ is not a subspace of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The two matrices $N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $N^T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ have zero square, so they belong to $W$. Their sum is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \neq O .
$$
Therefore $W$ is not closed under addition and, by the **subspace criterion**, it **is not a subspace**. In the other pairs the sum is $3N$, $O$ or $N$, which all have zero square, so those pairs do not provide a counterexample.
