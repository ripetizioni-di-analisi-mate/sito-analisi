- $k \neq -1$
- $k \neq 1$
- $k \neq \pm 1$
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

Three vectors of $\mathbb{R}^3$ are independent if and only if the matrix having them as columns has nonzero determinant. Expanding along the first row gives
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 0 & k \\
k & 1 & 0 \\
0 & k & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot (1 - 0) - 0 + k \cdot (k^2 - 0) = 1 + k^3 .
$$
Since $1 + k^3 = (k + 1)(k^2 - k + 1)$ and the factor $k^2 - k + 1$ has discriminant $-3 < 0$, the determinant vanishes only for $k = -1$. Therefore the vectors are **independent** if and only if $k \neq -1$.
