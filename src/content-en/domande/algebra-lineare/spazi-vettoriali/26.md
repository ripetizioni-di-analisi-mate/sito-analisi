Let $\mathcal{B} = (v_1, v_2, v_3)$ be a basis of a vector space $V$ and let $v$ be the vector with coordinates $(2, -1, 3)$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$. The sequence $\mathcal{B}' = (v_3, \ 2v_1, \ v_2)$ is also a basis of $V$. What are the coordinates of $v$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}'$?

- $(2, -1, 3)$
- $(3, 4, -1)$
- $(3, 1, -1)$
- $(1, -1, 3)$

## Explanation

By hypothesis $v = 2v_1 - v_2 + 3v_3$. We rewrite the combination using the vectors of $\mathcal{B}'$ in their order:
$$
v = 3 \cdot v_3 + 1 \cdot (2v_1) + (-1) \cdot v_2 .
$$
Since coordinates with respect to a basis are unique, the **coordinates** of $v$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}'$ are $(3, 1, -1)$. The coefficient of the second vector is $1$ and not $4$, because the vector $2v_1$ already contains the factor $2$.
