Which polynomial, added to $1 + x$ and $x + x^2$, gives a basis of $\mathbb{R}_2[x]$?

- $1 - x^2$
- $1 + 2x + x^2$
- $2 + x - x^2$
- $1$

## Explanation

With respect to the basis $(1, x, x^2)$ the polynomials $1 + x$, $x + x^2$ and $1$ have coordinates $(1, 1, 0)$, $(0, 1, 1)$ and $(1, 0, 0)$, and
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot (0 - 0) - 0 + 1 \cdot (1 - 0) = 1 \neq 0 .
$$
Hence the three polynomials are independent and, since $\dim \mathbb{R}_2[x] = 3$, they **form a basis**. The other polynomials belong to $\langle 1 + x, \ x + x^2 \rangle$, since $1 - x^2 = (1 + x) - (x + x^2)$, $1 + 2x + x^2 = (1 + x) + (x + x^2)$ and $2 + x - x^2 = 2\,(1 + x) - (x + x^2)$.
