In $\mathbb{R}^3$ let $U = \langle (1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0) \rangle$ and $W_k = \langle (1, 1, k) \rangle$, with $k \in \mathbb{R}$. For which values of $k$ is the union $U \cup W_k$ a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$?

- $k = 1$
- For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$
- For no value of $k$
- $k = 0$

## Explanation

For $k = 0$ the vector $(1, 1, 0) = (1, 0, 0) + (0, 1, 0)$ lies in $U$, so $W_0 \subseteq U$ and $U \cup W_0 = U$, which is a subspace.

For $k \neq 0$ the vectors $(1, 0, 0) \in U$ and $(1, 1, k) \in W_k$ have sum $(2, 1, k)$. This sum does not lie in $U$, whose vectors have zero third component, and it does not lie in $W_k$, because a multiple of $(1, 1, k)$ has equal first two components. Therefore for $k \neq 0$ the union is not closed under addition, and $U \cup W_k$ **is a subspace if and only if $k = 0$**.
