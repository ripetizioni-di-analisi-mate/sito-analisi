In the vector space of functions from $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$ consider $f_1(x) = 1$, $f_2(x) = \cos 2x$ and $f_3(x) = \sin^2 x$, which are linearly dependent. Which dependence relation holds for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$?

- $f_1 + f_2 - 2f_3 = 0$
- $f_1 - f_2 - f_3 = 0$
- $f_1 + f_2 + 2f_3 = 0$
- $f_1 - f_2 - 2f_3 = 0$

## Explanation

The zero vector of this space is the function that is $0$ at every point, so a relation $\lambda_1 f_1 + \lambda_2 f_2 + \lambda_3 f_3 = 0$ must hold *for every* $x$. By the double-angle formula $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$, hence
$$
1 - \cos 2x - 2\sin^2 x = 0 \quad \text{for every } x \in \mathbb{R} .
$$
Therefore **$f_1 - f_2 - 2f_3 = 0$** is a dependence relation, with coefficients $1, -1, -2$ not all zero.

The other three combinations are not the zero function, because at $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$, where $\sin^2 x = 1$ and $\cos 2x = -1$, they take the values $-2$, $1$ and $2$ respectively. The combination $f_1 - f_2 - f_3$ vanishes at $x = 0$, and this is not enough, because the relation must hold at every point.
