Which of the following sets, with the usual addition and scalar multiplication, is a real vector space?

- The polynomials with real coefficients of degree exactly $2$
- The polynomials with real coefficients of degree less than or equal to $2$, including the zero polynomial
- The pairs $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ with $x$ and $y$ integers
- The pairs $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ with $x \geq 0$

## Explanation

The set $\mathbb{R}_2[x]$ of polynomials of degree less than or equal to $2$ is one of the standard vector spaces, with the zero polynomial as zero vector. The polynomials of degree exactly $2$ do not include the zero polynomial, and moreover $(x^2 + x) + (-x^2) = x$ has degree $1$. The pairs of integers are not closed under scalar multiplication, since $\dfrac{1}{2}\,(1, 0) = \left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 0 \right)$. The set of pairs with $x \geq 0$ contains $(1, 0)$ and does not contain its negative $(-1, 0)$.
