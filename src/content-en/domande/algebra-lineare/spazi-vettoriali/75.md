Let $W$ be the plane of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with equation $x - y + z = 0$, which contains the vector $(1, 1, 0)$. Which vector, added to $(1, 1, 0)$, gives a basis of $W$?

- $(1, 0, 0)$
- $(0, 1, 1)$
- $(2, 2, 0)$
- $(1, 0, 1)$

## Explanation

The vector to be added must lie in $W$ and must not be proportional to $(1, 1, 0)$. The vector $(0, 1, 1)$ satisfies $0 - 1 + 1 = 0$, so it belongs to $W$, and it is not a multiple of $(1, 1, 0)$.

Since $\dim W = 3 - 1 = 2$, the two independent vectors $(1, 1, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 1)$ **form a basis of $W$**. The vectors $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(1, 0, 1)$ give $1$ and $2$ in the equation, so they do not lie in $W$, and $(2, 2, 0)$ is proportional to $(1, 1, 0)$.
