For the vectors $v_1 = (1, 2, 3)$, $v_2 = (2, 1, 0)$, $v_3 = (1, -1, -3)$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$, which statement is true?

- $v_3 = v_1 - v_2$
- $v_3 = v_1 + v_2$
- The vectors are linearly independent
- $v_3 = v_2 - v_1$

## Explanation

We compute $v_2 - v_1 = (2 - 1, \ 1 - 2, \ 0 - 3) = (1, -1, -3)$, which coincides with $v_3$. Hence the dependence relation $v_1 - v_2 + v_3 = 0$ holds, and the three vectors are **linearly dependent**. The vector $v_1 - v_2 = (-1, 1, 3)$ is the negative of $v_3$, while $v_1 + v_2 = (3, 3, 3)$ is different from $v_3$.
