Let $B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and let $I$ be the identity matrix of order $2$. Which matrix belongs to the subspace $\langle I, B \rangle$ of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ -3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

A linear combination of $I$ and $B$ has the form
$$
a I + b B = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & a \end{pmatrix} , \qquad a, b \in \mathbb{R} ,
$$
so the matrices of $\langle I, B \rangle$ have equal diagonal entries and equal off-diagonal entries.

The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has this form with $a = 2$ and $b = 3$, therefore it **belongs to $\langle I, B \rangle$**. In the other matrices the off-diagonal entries are different ($3$ and $-3$, or $0$ and $3$) or the diagonal entries are different ($2$ and $-2$), so none of them is a linear combination of $I$ and $B$.
