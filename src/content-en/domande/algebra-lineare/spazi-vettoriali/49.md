The set $\mathbb{C}$ of complex numbers, with the usual sum and multiplication by a real number, is a real vector space. Which sequence is a basis of $\mathbb{C}$ as a real vector space?

- $(1)$
- $(1, -1)$
- $(i, 2i)$
- $(1, i)$

## Explanation

Every complex number can be written as $z = a + bi = a \cdot 1 + b \cdot i$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$, so $1$ and $i$ span $\mathbb{C}$.

The expression is unique, because the real and imaginary parts of $z$ are determined; therefore $1$ and $i$ are also independent, and $(1, i)$ **is a basis**, with $\dim \mathbb{C} = 2$ as a real space. The sequence $(1)$ spans only the real numbers, while $(1, -1)$ and $(i, 2i)$ consist of proportional vectors, hence dependent ones.
