In $\mathbb{R}^3$ let $U$ be the plane with equation $x - y = 0$. Which of the following lines is a complement of $U$?

- $\langle (1, 0, 0) \rangle$
- $\langle (1, 1, 0) \rangle$
- $\langle (0, 0, 1) \rangle$
- $\langle (1, 1, 5) \rangle$

## Explanation

Since $\dim U + \dim \langle w \rangle = 2 + 1 = 3$ for every $w \neq 0$, by the **criterion for complementary subspaces** a line $\langle w \rangle$ is a complement of $U$ if and only if $\langle w \rangle \cap U = \{0\}$, that is, if and only if $w \notin U$. The vectors $(1, 1, 0)$, $(0, 0, 1)$ and $(1, 1, 5)$ satisfy $x - y = 0$, therefore the lines they span are contained in $U$. The vector $(1, 0, 0)$ gives $1 - 0 = 1 \neq 0$, so it does not belong to $U$, and $\mathbb{R}^3 = U \oplus \langle (1, 0, 0) \rangle$.
