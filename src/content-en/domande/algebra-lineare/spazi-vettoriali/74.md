Let $W = \langle p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4 \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{R}_2[x]$ with
$$
p_1 = 1 + x , \qquad p_2 = 2 + 2x , \qquad p_3 = x^2 , \qquad p_4 = 1 + x + x^2 .
$$
Which of the following pairs is **not** a basis of $W$?

- $(p_1, p_2)$
- $(p_1, p_3)$
- $(p_2, p_4)$
- $(p_3, p_4)$

## Explanation

Since $p_2 = 2p_1$ and $p_4 = p_1 + p_3$, by the theorem on the removal of a redundant generator $W = \langle p_1, p_3 \rangle$; the polynomials $p_1$ and $p_3$ are not proportional, so $\dim W = 2$.

By the consequences of the dimension, two independent vectors of $W$ form a basis of $W$. The pairs $(p_1, p_3)$, $(p_2, p_4)$ and $(p_3, p_4)$ consist of non-proportional polynomials, therefore they are bases. The pair $(p_1, p_2)$ consists of proportional polynomials, so it spans only the line $\langle 1 + x \rangle$ and **is not a basis** of $W$.
