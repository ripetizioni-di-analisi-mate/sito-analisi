- $W$ is a subspace, because it contains the zero vector
- $W$ is not a subspace, because it does not contain the zero vector
- $W$ is not a subspace, because it is not closed under addition
- $W$ is not a subspace, because it is not closed under scalar multiplication

## Explanation

The zero vector satisfies $0^2 = 0^2$, so $0 \in W$. If $x^2 = y^2$, then $(\lambda x)^2 = \lambda^2 x^2 = \lambda^2 y^2 = (\lambda y)^2$, therefore $W$ is closed under scalar multiplication. The vectors $(1, 1)$ and $(1, -1)$ lie in $W$, while their sum $(2, 0)$ does not belong to it, since $2^2 = 4 \neq 0$. Hence the condition of closure under addition is not satisfied and, by the **subspace criterion**, $W$ **is not a subspace** of $\mathbb{R}^2$.
