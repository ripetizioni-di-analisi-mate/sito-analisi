For the matrices
$$
A_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} , \quad
A_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} , \quad
A_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} , \quad
A_4 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
which statement is true?

- They form a basis of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$
- They do not form a basis and they span a subspace of dimension $3$
- They do not form a basis and they span a subspace of dimension $2$
- They do not form a basis, because four matrices are not enough to span $M_2(\mathbb{R})$

## Explanation

We have $A_1 + A_4 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = A_2 + A_3$, so the relation $A_1 - A_2 - A_3 + A_4 = O$ holds and the four matrices are dependent. Therefore they do not form a basis, even though $\dim M_2(\mathbb{R}) = 4$. By the **removal of a redundant generator** we have $\langle A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4 \rangle = \langle A_1, A_2, A_3 \rangle$. A combination $aA_1 + bA_2 + cA_3 = \begin{pmatrix} a + c & a + b \\ b & c \end{pmatrix}$ is zero only for $a = b = c = 0$, so $A_1, A_2, A_3$ are independent and the spanned subspace has **dimension $3$**.
