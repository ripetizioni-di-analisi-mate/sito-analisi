In $\mathbb{R}^3$ let $U = \langle (1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0) \rangle$ and $W = \langle (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1) \rangle$. The vector $(1, 1, 1)$ can be written as $u + w$ with $u = (1, 0, 0) \in U$ and $w = (0, 1, 1) \in W$. Which of the following is another way of writing $(1, 1, 1)$ as $u + w$ with $u \in U$ and $w \in W$?

- $u = (1, 1, 0)$, $w = (0, 0, 1)$
- $u = (1, 0, 1)$, $w = (0, 1, 0)$
- $u = (0, 1, 0)$, $w = (1, 0, 1)$
- $u = (1, 1, 1)$, $w = (0, 0, 0)$

## Explanation

The subspace $U$ is the plane $z = 0$ and the subspace $W$ is the plane $x = 0$. The vector $(1, 1, 0)$ has zero third component, so it lies in $U$; the vector $(0, 0, 1)$ has zero first component, so it lies in $W$. Since $(1, 1, 0) + (0, 0, 1) = (1, 1, 1)$, the pair **$u = (1, 1, 0)$, $w = (0, 0, 1)$** is another way of writing it.

In the other pairs one summand does not lie in the required subspace: $(1, 0, 1)$ and $(1, 1, 1)$ do not lie in $U$, because they have $z \neq 0$, and $(1, 0, 1)$ does not lie in $W$, because it has $x \neq 0$.

The decomposition is not unique because $U \cap W = \langle (0, 1, 0) \rangle \neq \{0\}$, so the sum $U + W$ is not direct. For every $t \in \mathbb{R}$ the pair $u = (1, t, 0)$, $w = (0, 1 - t, 1)$ is a decomposition of $(1, 1, 1)$.
