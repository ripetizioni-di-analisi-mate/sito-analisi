Let $W = \langle (1, 2, 1), (2, 4, 2), (1, 3, 2) \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$. Row reducing the matrix having these vectors as columns gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
Which sequence is a basis of $W$?

- $\big( (1, 0, 0), (1, 1, 0) \big)$
- $\big( (1, 2, 1), (1, 3, 2) \big)$
- $\big( (1, 2, 1), (2, 4, 2) \big)$
- $\big( (1, 2, 1), (2, 4, 2), (1, 3, 2) \big)$

## Explanation

The pivots of the echelon matrix are in columns $1$ and $3$, so $\dim W = 2$ and, by the **row reduction method**, the first and third columns of the *original* matrix form a basis of $W$. The basis is therefore $\big( (1, 2, 1), (1, 3, 2) \big)$. The columns $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(1, 1, 0)$ of the echelon matrix do not all lie in $W$, since $W$ has equation $x - y + z = 0$ and $(1, 0, 0)$ does not satisfy it. The vectors $(1, 2, 1)$ and $(2, 4, 2)$ are proportional, and three vectors in a subspace of dimension $2$ are dependent.
