The nonzero vectors of $\mathbb{R}^2$ with both components in $\{0, 1\}$ are $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$. How many ordered bases of $\mathbb{R}^2$ can be formed with these vectors?

- $6$
- $3$
- $9$
- $2$

## Explanation

Since $\dim \mathbb{R}^2 = 2$, a basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is an ordered sequence of two linearly independent vectors. Two distinct vectors chosen among $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$ are not proportional, so every pair of distinct vectors forms a basis.

The ordered pairs of distinct vectors chosen among three are $3 \cdot 2 = 6$, therefore the ordered bases are **$6$**. The number $3$ counts the pairs without taking the order into account, whereas $(v_1, v_2)$ and $(v_2, v_1)$ are different bases, because they give swapped coordinates. The number $9$ also includes the pairs $(v, v)$, made of equal vectors and hence dependent, and the number $2$ counts only the bases formed by $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$.
