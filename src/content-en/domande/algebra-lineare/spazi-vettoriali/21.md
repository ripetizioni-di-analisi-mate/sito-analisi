In $\mathbb{R}^3$ let $v_1 = (1, 0, 0)$, $v_2 = (0, 1, 0)$, $v_3 = (1, 1, 0)$, $v_4 = (0, 0, 1)$. Which statement is true?

- $\langle v_1, v_2, v_3 \rangle = \mathbb{R}^3$
- $\langle v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4 \rangle = \langle v_1, v_2, v_4 \rangle$
- $\langle v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4 \rangle = \langle v_1, v_2, v_3 \rangle$
- $v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4$ are linearly independent

## Explanation

We have $v_3 = v_1 + v_2$, so $v_3$ is a linear combination of the other vectors. By the **removal of a redundant generator** we have $\langle v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4 \rangle = \langle v_1, v_2, v_4 \rangle$, which is $\mathbb{R}^3$. The vectors $v_1, v_2, v_3$ have zero third component, so they span the plane $z = 0$ and not $\mathbb{R}^3$; for the same reason $v_4$ does not belong to the subspace they span. Four vectors of $\mathbb{R}^3$ are always dependent, and here the relation is $v_1 + v_2 - v_3 = 0$.
