Which vector of the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$, added to $v_1 = (1, 1, 0)$ and $v_2 = (1, -1, 0)$, gives a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$?

- Only $e_1$
- Only $e_2$
- Only $e_3$
- Each of the three vectors $e_1$, $e_2$, $e_3$

## Explanation

The vectors $v_1$ and $v_2$ are not proportional and have zero third component, so they span the plane $z = 0$, which contains $e_1 = \dfrac{1}{2}\,(v_1 + v_2)$ and $e_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\,(v_1 - v_2)$. Therefore adding $e_1$ or $e_2$ gives three dependent vectors. The vector $e_3$ does not belong to the plane $z = 0$, so by the **theorem on adding a vector** the vectors $v_1, v_2, e_3$ are independent. Three independent vectors of $\mathbb{R}^3$ form a basis, therefore only $e_3$ extends $v_1, v_2$ to a basis.
