In $\mathbb{R}^3$ consider the line $U = \langle (1, 1, 0) \rangle$ and the plane $W : x - y = 0$. Which statement is true?

- $U + W = \mathbb{R}^3$ and $U \cap W = \{0\}$
- $U + W = W$ and $U \cap W = \{0\}$
- $U + W = W$ and $U \cap W = U$
- $U + W = \mathbb{R}^3$ and $U \cap W = U$

## Explanation

The vector $(1, 1, 0)$ satisfies $1 - 1 = 0$, so it lies in $W$. Since $W$ is a subspace, it contains all multiples of $(1, 1, 0)$, therefore $U \subseteq W$.

From $U \subseteq W$ it follows that $U \cap W = U$. It also follows that $U + W = W$, because every sum $u + w$ with $u \in U \subseteq W$ and $w \in W$ lies in $W$. Hence **$U + W = W$ and $U \cap W = U$**. The dimensions satisfy Grassmann's formula, since $2 = 1 + 2 - 1$.
