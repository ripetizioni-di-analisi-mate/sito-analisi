In $\mathbb{R}^2$ we have $\mathbb{R}^2 = U \oplus W$ with $U = \langle (1, 2) \rangle$ and $W = \langle (1, 0) \rangle$. Writing $v = (4, 6)$ as $u + w$ with $u \in U$ and $w \in W$, what is $u$?

- $(2, 4)$
- $(3, 6)$
- $(4, 8)$
- $(1, 2)$

## Explanation

We look for $a, b$ such that $a\,(1, 2) + b\,(1, 0) = (4, 6)$, that is, $a + b = 4$ and $2a = 6$. The second equation gives $a = 3$, and the first $b = 1$. Hence $u = 3\,(1, 2) = (3, 6)$ and $w = (1, 0)$, and indeed $(3, 6) + (1, 0) = (4, 6)$. Since the sum is direct, by the **uniqueness of the decomposition** this is the only way to write $v$ as $u + w$.
