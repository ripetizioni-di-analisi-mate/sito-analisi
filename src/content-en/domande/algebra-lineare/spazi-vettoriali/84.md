Let $V$ be the set of functions $f : \{1, 2, 3\} \to \mathbb{R}$, with the sum $(f + g)(i) = f(i) + g(i)$ and the scalar multiplication $(\lambda f)(i) = \lambda f(i)$. With these operations $V$ is a real vector space. What is its dimension?

- $1$
- $6$
- $3$
- $V$ is not finite-dimensional

## Explanation

For $j = 1, 2, 3$ let $\delta_j$ be the function equal to $1$ at $j$ and to $0$ at the other two points. Every $f \in V$ satisfies
$$
f = f(1)\,\delta_1 + f(2)\,\delta_2 + f(3)\,\delta_3 ,
$$
because the two sides take the same values at $1$, $2$ and $3$; hence $\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3$ span $V$.

If $\lambda_1 \delta_1 + \lambda_2 \delta_2 + \lambda_3 \delta_3$ is the zero function, its value at $i$ is $\lambda_i = 0$ for every $i$, therefore the three functions are independent. Hence $(\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3)$ is a basis and **$\dim V = 3$**; the function $f$ has coordinates $(f(1), f(2), f(3))$ with respect to this basis.
