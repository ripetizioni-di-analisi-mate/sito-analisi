In $\mathbb{R}^3$ consider the lines $U = \langle (1, 0, 1) \rangle$, $W = \langle (0, 1, 1) \rangle$ and $Z = \langle (1, 1, 2) \rangle$, which pairwise intersect only in the zero vector. What is the dimension of $U + W + Z$?

- $2$
- $3$
- $1$
- $0$

## Explanation

The subspace $U + W + Z$ is spanned by $(1, 0, 1)$, $(0, 1, 1)$ and $(1, 1, 2)$. Since $(1, 1, 2) = (1, 0, 1) + (0, 1, 1)$, by the theorem on the removal of a redundant generator $U + W + Z = \langle (1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1) \rangle$.

The two remaining vectors are not proportional, hence **$\dim (U + W + Z) = 2$** and the three lines lie in the same plane. The fact that the pairwise intersections are zero is not enough to have dimension $1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.
