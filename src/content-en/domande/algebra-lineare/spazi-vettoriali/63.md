The plane $W$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with equation $x + y - z = 0$ has basis $\mathcal{B} = \big( (1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1) \big)$. What are the coordinates of the vector $v = (2, -1, 1)$ of $W$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$?

- $(-1, 2)$
- $(2, -1)$
- $(2, -1, 1)$
- $(2, 1)$

## Explanation

We look for $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ with $a\,(1, 0, 1) + b\,(0, 1, 1) = (2, -1, 1)$, that is, $a = 2$, $b = -1$ and $a + b = 1$. The first two equations give $a = 2$ and $b = -1$, and the third one is satisfied, since $2 - 1 = 1$.

Hence $v = 2\,(1, 0, 1) - (0, 1, 1)$, and the coordinates of $v$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$ are $(2, -1)$. Since $\dim W = 2$, the coordinates with respect to a basis of $W$ are pairs of numbers; the triple $(2, -1, 1)$ consists of the components of $v$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$. The pairs $(-1, 2)$ and $(2, 1)$ give the vectors $(-1, 2, 1)$ and $(2, 1, 3)$, which are different from $v$.
