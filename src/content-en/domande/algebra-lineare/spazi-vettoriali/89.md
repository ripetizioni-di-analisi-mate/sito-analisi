Let $V = U \oplus W$, with $(u_1, u_2)$ a basis of $U$ and $(w_1, w_2, w_3)$ a basis of $W$. Which statement is true?

- $\dim V = 6$
- $u_1 + w_1 \in U$
- $\dim V = 3$
- $(u_1, u_2, w_1, w_2, w_3)$ is a basis of $V$

## Explanation

Since the sum is direct, $U \cap W = \{0\}$, and by Grassmann's formula $\dim V = 2 + 3 - 0 = 5$. By the criterion for complementary subspaces the union of a basis of $U$ and a basis of $W$ is a basis of $V$, hence **$(u_1, u_2, w_1, w_2, w_3)$ is a basis of $V$**.

If $u_1 + w_1$ belonged to $U$, the vector $w_1 = (u_1 + w_1) - u_1$ would lie in $U \cap W = \{0\}$, while $w_1 \neq 0$ because it belongs to a basis.
