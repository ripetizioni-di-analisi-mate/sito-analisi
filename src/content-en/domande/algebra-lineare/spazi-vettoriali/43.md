In $\mathbb{R}^3$ let $U_k = \langle (1, 0, k), (0, 1, 1) \rangle$, with $k \in \mathbb{R}$, and $W = \langle (1, 2, 0) \rangle$. For which values of $k$ does $\mathbb{R}^3 = U_k \oplus W$ hold?

- $k \neq 2$
- $k = -2$
- $k \neq 0$
- $k \neq -2$

## Explanation

By the **criterion for complementary subspaces**, $\mathbb{R}^3 = U_k \oplus W$ if and only if the union of a basis of $U_k$ and a basis of $W$ is a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$. The two generators of $U_k$ are not proportional for any $k$, so they form a basis of $U_k$. The three vectors form a basis if and only if
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 2 \\
k & 1 & 0
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot (0 - 2) - 0 + 1 \cdot (0 - k) = -2 - k \neq 0 .
$$
Therefore $\mathbb{R}^3 = U_k \oplus W$ if and only if $k \neq -2$.
