A subspace $W$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ contains the vectors $(1, 0, 1)$, $(0, 1, 1)$ and $(1, 1, 1)$. Which statement is true?

- $W$ can be a plane, because three vectors can lie in the same plane
- $W$ is the plane with equation $x + y - z = 0$
- $W = \mathbb{R}^3$ only if $W$ also contains the vectors of the standard basis
- $W = \mathbb{R}^3$

## Explanation

The matrix whose columns are the three vectors has determinant
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot (1 - 1) - 0 + 1 \cdot (0 - 1) = -1 \neq 0 ,
$$
so the three vectors are linearly independent. Since $W$ contains three independent vectors, $\dim W \geq 3$. By the theorem on the dimension of a subspace, a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$ of dimension $3$ coincides with $\mathbb{R}^3$, therefore **$W = \mathbb{R}^3$**.

The plane $x + y - z = 0$ contains $(1, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 1, 1)$, and does not contain $(1, 1, 1)$, since $1 + 1 - 1 = 1 \neq 0$.
