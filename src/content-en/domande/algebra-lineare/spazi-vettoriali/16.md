Let $A \in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})$ and let $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ be a nonzero vector. Let $u$ and $v$ be two solutions of the system $A x = b$. Which of the following vectors is certainly a solution of the same system?

- $u + v$
- $u - v$
- $2u - v$
- $3u$

## Explanation

By the linearity of the row-by-column product, $A(\alpha u + \beta v) = \alpha\, Au + \beta\, Av = (\alpha + \beta)\, b$. Since $b \neq 0$, the vector $\alpha u + \beta v$ is a solution of $A x = b$ if and only if $\alpha + \beta = 1$.

For $2u - v$ the sum of the coefficients is $2 - 1 = 1$, so **$A(2u - v) = b$**. For $u + v$ and $3u$ we get $2b$ and $3b$, which are different from $b$. For $u - v$ we get the zero vector, so $u - v$ is a solution of the homogeneous system $A x = 0$ and not of $A x = b$.
