In $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ we have $M_2(\mathbb{R}) = S \oplus K$, where $S$ is the subspace of symmetric matrices and $K$ that of skew-symmetric matrices. Writing $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ as $A_S + A_K$ with $A_S \in S$ and $A_K \in K$, what is $A_S$?

- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 6 \\ 6 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

Since $A_S^T = A_S$ and $A_K^T = -A_K$, transposing $A = A_S + A_K$ gives $A^T = A_S - A_K$. Adding the two equalities gives
$$
A_S = \frac{A + A^T}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 6 & 6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 3 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$
so $A_K = A - A_S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, which is skew-symmetric. Since the sum is direct, by the **uniqueness of the decomposition** this is the only way to write $A$ as the sum of a symmetric matrix and a skew-symmetric one. The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 6 \\ 6 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ is $A + A^T$, without the division by $2$.
