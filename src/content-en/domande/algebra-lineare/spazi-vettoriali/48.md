Let $V$ be a vector space and let $v \in V$ be a nonzero vector. If $\lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{R}$ satisfy $\lambda v = \mu v$, which conclusion is correct?

- $\lambda = \mu = 0$
- $\lambda = -\mu$
- $\lambda = \mu$
- Nothing can be concluded about $\lambda$ and $\mu$

## Explanation

Adding the opposite of $\mu v$ to both sides gives $\lambda v - \mu v = 0$, that is, $(\lambda - \mu)\,v = 0$ by the distributive property.

By the zero-product law, then, $\lambda - \mu = 0$ or $v = 0$. Since $v \neq 0$, it follows that $\lambda - \mu = 0$, hence **$\lambda = \mu$**. The conclusion $\lambda = \mu = 0$ is too strong, because $\lambda = \mu = 1$ also satisfies $\lambda v = \mu v$.
