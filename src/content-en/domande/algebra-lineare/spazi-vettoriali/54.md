In $\mathbb{R}^2$ we want to write the vector $(2, 3)$ as a linear combination $a\,(1, 1) + b\,(1, 2) + c\,(0, 1)$, with $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$. Which statement is true?

- There are infinitely many ways
- There is exactly one way, because the three vectors span $\mathbb{R}^2$
- There is no way, because the three vectors are dependent
- There are exactly three ways, one for each pair of vectors

## Explanation

The equality $a\,(1, 1) + b\,(1, 2) + c\,(0, 1) = (2, 3)$ is equivalent to the system $a + b = 2$, $a + 2b + c = 3$, of two equations in three unknowns.

Subtracting the first equation from the second gives $b = 1 - c$, and hence $a = 1 + c$. Therefore for every $c \in \mathbb{R}$ the triple $(1 + c, \ 1 - c, \ c)$ is a solution, and **there are infinitely many ways**; for example $c = 0$ gives $(1, 1, 0)$ and $c = 1$ gives $(2, 0, 1)$. Uniqueness of the coefficients holds for the vectors of a basis, and three vectors of $\mathbb{R}^2$ are dependent, so they do not form a basis.
