In $\mathbb{R}^4$ consider
$$
U :
\begin{cases}
x_1 + x_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
x_3 + x_4 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
W = \langle (1, 1, 0, 0), \ (0, 0, 1, 1) \rangle .
$$
Which statement is true?

- $\mathbb{R}^4 = U \oplus W$
- $U + W = \mathbb{R}^4$, but the sum is not direct
- The sum is direct, but $U + W \neq \mathbb{R}^4$
- $U \subseteq W$

## Explanation

The generic vector of $W$ is $a\,(1, 1, 0, 0) + b\,(0, 0, 1, 1) = (a, a, b, b)$. Substituting it into the equations of $U$ gives $2a = 0$ and $2b = 0$, so $U \cap W = \{0\}$.

The two equations of $U$ are independent, therefore $\dim U = 4 - 2 = 2$; the generators of $W$ are not proportional, so $\dim W = 2$. Since $U \cap W = \{0\}$ and $2 + 2 = 4$, by the criterion for complementary subspaces **$\mathbb{R}^4 = U \oplus W$**.
