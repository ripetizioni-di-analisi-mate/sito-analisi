For which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ do the vectors $(1, k)$ and $(k, 4)$ form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$?

- $k \neq 2$
- $k \neq 4$
- $k \neq 0$
- $k \neq \pm 2$

## Explanation

Since $\dim \mathbb{R}^2 = 2$, two vectors form a basis if and only if they are independent, that is, if and only if the matrix having them as columns has nonzero determinant. The determinant is
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & k \\
k & 4
\end{vmatrix}
= 4 - k^2 ,
$$
which vanishes for $k = 2$ and for $k = -2$. Therefore the two vectors form a **basis** if and only if $k \neq \pm 2$; for $k = -2$, for example, we have $(-2, 4) = -2\,(1, -2)$.
