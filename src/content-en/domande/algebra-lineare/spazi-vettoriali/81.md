In $\mathbb{R}^2$ let $U = \langle (1, 0) \rangle$. Which lines $W$ through the origin satisfy $\mathbb{R}^2 = U \oplus W$?

- Only one, the line $\langle (0, 1) \rangle$
- All the lines through the origin
- None
- All the lines through the origin other than $U$

## Explanation

Let $W = \langle (a, b) \rangle$ with $(a, b) \neq (0, 0)$. By the **criterion for complementary subspaces**, $\mathbb{R}^2 = U \oplus W$ if and only if $U \cap W = \{0\}$ and $\dim U + \dim W = 2$.

The second condition is satisfied by every line, since $1 + 1 = 2$. The first one is satisfied if and only if $(a, b)$ is not a multiple of $(1, 0)$, that is, if and only if $W \neq U$. Therefore the complements of $U$ are **all the lines through the origin other than $U$**, for example $\langle (0, 1) \rangle$ and $\langle (1, 1) \rangle$, and the complement is not unique. For $W = U$ instead $U \cap W = U \neq \{0\}$.
