Let $v_1, v_2, v_3$ be linearly dependent vectors of a vector space $V$. Which statement is always true?

- One of the three vectors is the zero vector
- Two of the three vectors are proportional
- For every $v_4 \in V$ the vectors $v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4$ are dependent
- $v_1$ is a linear combination of $v_2$ and $v_3$

## Explanation

Since $v_1, v_2, v_3$ are dependent, there is a relation $\lambda_1 v_1 + \lambda_2 v_2 + \lambda_3 v_3 = 0$ with coefficients not all zero. Then $\lambda_1 v_1 + \lambda_2 v_2 + \lambda_3 v_3 + 0 \cdot v_4 = 0$ is a dependence relation for $v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4$, hence **four vectors containing a dependent triple are dependent**.

In $\mathbb{R}^2$ the vectors $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 1)$ are dependent, none of them is zero and no pair is proportional. The vectors $v_1 = (1, 0)$, $v_2 = (0, 1)$, $v_3 = (0, 2)$ are dependent, while $v_1$ is not a linear combination of $v_2$ and $v_3$, which have zero first component.
