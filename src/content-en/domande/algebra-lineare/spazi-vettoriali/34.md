In $\mathbb{R}^3$ consider the lines $U = \langle (1, 0, 3) \rangle$ and $W = \langle (0, 1, -1) \rangle$. Which vector belongs to $U + W$ and does not belong to $U \cup W$?

- $(2, 0, 6)$
- $(0, -3, 3)$
- $(1, 1, 3)$
- $(1, 1, 2)$

## Explanation

The vectors of $U + W$ are the sums $a\,(1, 0, 3) + b\,(0, 1, -1) = (a, \ b, \ 3a - b)$, that is, the vectors $(x, y, z)$ with $z = 3x - y$.

The vector $(1, 1, 2)$ satisfies $2 = 3 - 1$, so it lies in $U + W$. It is a multiple neither of $(1, 0, 3)$ nor of $(0, 1, -1)$, so it does not lie in $U \cup W$. Therefore **$(1, 1, 2)$** is the required vector.

The vectors $(2, 0, 6)$ and $(0, -3, 3)$ lie in $U + W$, but the first lies in $U$ and the second in $W$, so they also belong to $U \cup W$. The vector $(1, 1, 3)$ does not lie in $U + W$, since $3 \neq 3 - 1$.
