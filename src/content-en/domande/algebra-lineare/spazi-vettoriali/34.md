For which value of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ does the subspace $W = \langle (1, 2, 1), (0, 1, 1), (2, k, 0) \rangle$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ have dimension $2$?

- $k = -2$
- $k = 0$
- $k = 4$
- $k = 2$

## Explanation

By the **extraction of a basis**, $\dim W$ is the maximum number of independent vectors among the generators. The first two vectors are not proportional, so $\dim W \geq 2$, and $\dim W = 2$ if and only if the three generators are dependent. The determinant of the matrix having them as columns is
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 0 & 2 \\
2 & 1 & k \\
1 & 1 & 0
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot (0 - k) - 0 + 2 \cdot (2 - 1) = 2 - k ,
$$
which vanishes for $k = 2$. Therefore $\dim W = 2$ if and only if $k = 2$, and in that case $(2, 2, 0) = 2\,(1, 2, 1) - 2\,(0, 1, 1)$.
