In $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ let $S$ be the subspace of symmetric matrices and $T$ that of upper triangular matrices. Which set is $S \cap T$?

- $\{O\}$
- The diagonal matrices
- The multiples of the identity $\lambda I$
- The symmetric matrices

## Explanation

A matrix of $T$ has the form $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & d \end{pmatrix}$. It is symmetric if and only if the entry in position $(1, 2)$ equals the entry in position $(2, 1)$, that is, $b = 0$.

Hence $S \cap T = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & d \end{pmatrix} : a, d \in \mathbb{R} \right\}$, which is **the set of diagonal matrices**, of dimension $2$. The multiples of the identity are only the diagonal matrices with $a = d$, and the diagonal matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ lies in $S \cap T$ without being such a multiple.
