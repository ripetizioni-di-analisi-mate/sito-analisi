Which of the following systems of vectors spans $\mathbb{R}^3$?

- $(1, 0, 0)$, $(0, 1, 0)$, $(1, 1, 0)$, $(2, 1, 0)$
- $(1, 1, 0)$, $(0, 1, 1)$
- $(1, 1, 1)$, $(0, 1, 1)$, $(0, 0, 1)$, $(1, 2, 3)$
- $(1, 2, 0)$, $(0, 1, 1)$, $(1, 3, 1)$

## Explanation

The matrix with columns $(1, 1, 1)$, $(0, 1, 1)$, $(0, 0, 1)$ is triangular with determinant $1 \neq 0$, so the three vectors are independent. Three independent vectors of $\mathbb{R}^3$ form a basis, therefore they span $\mathbb{R}^3$. Adding $(1, 2, 3)$ cannot make the spanned subspace smaller, so the third system is also a system of **generators** of $\mathbb{R}^3$. The vectors of the first system have zero third component, therefore they span only the plane $z = 0$. By the **consequences of the dimension** two vectors do not span $\mathbb{R}^3$, which has dimension $3$. In the last system $(1, 3, 1) = (1, 2, 0) + (0, 1, 1)$, so the three vectors span a plane.
