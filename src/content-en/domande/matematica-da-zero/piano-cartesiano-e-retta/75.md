For what value of $k$ are the lines $y=kx+2$ and $y=-\dfrac{2}{3}x+1$ perpendicular?

- $k=\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $k=-\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $k=\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $k=-\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

The condition for perpendicularity is $k\cdot\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}\right)=-1$. Multiplying by $-\dfrac{3}{2}$ gives $k=\dfrac{3}{2}$. The value $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ would give parallel lines. The value $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is the reciprocal without a sign change and would give a product of $\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}\right)=-\dfrac{4}{9}$, which differs from $-1$.
