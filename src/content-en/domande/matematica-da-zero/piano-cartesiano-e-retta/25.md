Which of the following rules does NOT define a function of $x$, that is, does not assign to every $x$ a single number $y$?

- $y$ is a number satisfying $|y|=x$, for $x\geq 0$
- $y$ is twice $x$
- $y$ is the square of $x$
- $y$ is $x$ increased by $1$

## Explanation

A function assigns to every $x$ one and only one $y$. Under the rule $|y|=x$, the value $x=3$ gives both $y=3$ and $y=-3$, and the points $(3,3)$ and $(3,-3)$ lie on the same vertical line. Each of the other three rules gives just one result for every $x$.
