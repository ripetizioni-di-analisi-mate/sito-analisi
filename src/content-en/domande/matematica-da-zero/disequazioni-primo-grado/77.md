Why can we not solve the inequality $\dfrac{5}{x-2}>1$ simply by multiplying both sides by $x-2$?

- Because $x-2$ is always negative
- Because $x-2$ is not an integer
- Because the numerator $5$ is positive
- Because the sign of $x-2$ depends on $x$, so we do not know whether to preserve or reverse the inequality

## Explanation

The factor $x-2$ is positive for $x>2$ and negative for $x<2$. In the first case the direction would stay the same, and in the second it would reverse. Multiplying without reversing the inequality would give $5>x-2$, that is, $x<7$, and $x=1$ would be a solution. However, when $x=1$ the fraction equals $\dfrac{5}{-1}=-5$, which is not greater than $1$.
