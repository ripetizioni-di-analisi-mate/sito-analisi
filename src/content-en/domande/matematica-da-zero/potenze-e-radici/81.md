What is the sum $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ in simplest form?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $2\sqrt{2}$
- $\sqrt{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

Rationalizing the first term gives $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The sum is then $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2}$. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ counts only one term, $2\sqrt{2}$ omits the division by $2$ and $1$ adds $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}$.
