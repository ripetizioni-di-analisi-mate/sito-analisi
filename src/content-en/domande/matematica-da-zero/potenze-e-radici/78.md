Which fraction results from eliminating the radical from the denominator of $\dfrac{5}{4\sqrt{10}}$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{8}$
- $\dfrac{5\sqrt{10}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{8}$

## Explanation

The radical in the denominator is $\sqrt{10}$, so multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{10}$ to obtain $\dfrac{5\sqrt{10}}{4\cdot10} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{10}}{40} = \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{8}$. The result $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}$ omits the factor $4$ in the denominator, $\dfrac{5\sqrt{10}}{4}$ omits the factor $10$ and $\dfrac{1}{8}$ omits the factor $\sqrt{10}$ in the numerator.
