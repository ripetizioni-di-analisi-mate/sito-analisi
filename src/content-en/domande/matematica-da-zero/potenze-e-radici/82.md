What form does $\dfrac{9}{\sqrt{18}}$ take after removing the radical from the denominator and simplifying?

- $3\sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{9\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{4}$

## Explanation

Since $\sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$, we have $\dfrac{9}{3\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{2}}$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{2}$ gives $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The value $3\sqrt{2}$ omits the division by $2$, $\dfrac{9\sqrt{2}}{2}$ does not simplify $\sqrt{18}$ and $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{4}$ divides by $4$ instead of $2$.
