What is the smallest number greater than $1$ that, when divided by $4$, $6$, and $9$, always leaves remainder $1$?

- $37$
- $25$
- $36$
- $73$

## Explanation

If a number leaves remainder $1$ in each division, subtracting $1$ gives a multiple of $4$, $6$, and $9$. The smallest common multiple other than $0$ is $\operatorname{mcm}(4, 6, 9) = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 = 36$. Therefore the number is $36 + 1 = 37$. The number $73 = 2 \cdot 36 + 1$ also satisfies the condition but is larger.
