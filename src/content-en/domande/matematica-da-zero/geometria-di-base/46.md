The side length of a square doubles. How does its area change?

- It doubles
- It increases by a factor of eight
- It stays the same
- It quadruples

## Explanation

If the side length is $l$, the area is $l^2$. With side length $2l$, the area becomes $(2l)^2 = 4l^2$, or four times as large. For example, a square with side length $3$ cm has area $9\ \text{cm}^2$, and with side length $6$ cm its area is $36\ \text{cm}^2$, which is $4$ times $9$.
