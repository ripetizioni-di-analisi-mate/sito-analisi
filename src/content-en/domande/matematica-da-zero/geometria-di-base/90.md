Which of the following solids has the greatest volume: a cube with edge length $5$ cm, a cylinder with radius $3$ cm and height $5$ cm, a rectangular prism measuring $4$ cm by $5$ cm by $6$ cm, or a rectangular prism measuring $7$ cm by $4$ cm by $4$ cm? Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

- The cube
- The rectangular prism measuring $4$ cm by $5$ cm by $6$ cm
- The cylinder
- The rectangular prism measuring $7$ cm by $4$ cm by $4$ cm

## Explanation

The volumes are $5^3 = 125\ \text{cm}^3$ for the cube, $3.14 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 141.3\ \text{cm}^3$ for the cylinder, and $4 \cdot 5 \cdot 6 = 120\ \text{cm}^3$ and $7 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 = 112\ \text{cm}^3$ for the two rectangular prisms. The cylinder has the greatest volume. The comparison uses volumes calculated with the same approximation for $\pi$.
