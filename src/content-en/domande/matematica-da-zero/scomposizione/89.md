For $x\neq 3$ and $x\neq -3$, which fraction equals the sum $\dfrac{2}{x^2-9}+\dfrac{1}{x+3}$?

- $\dfrac{x+5}{(x+3)(x-3)}$
- $\dfrac{3}{x^2-9}$
- $\dfrac{x-1}{(x+3)(x-3)}$
- $\dfrac{x-1}{x+3}$

## Explanation

The first denominator is $x^2-9=(x+3)(x-3)$ and already contains the second as a factor, so it is the common denominator. Multiplying the second numerator by $x-3$ gives $\dfrac{2+(x-3)}{(x+3)(x-3)}=\dfrac{x-1}{(x+3)(x-3)}$. The result $\dfrac{x+5}{(x+3)(x-3)}$ comes from multiplying by $x+3$ instead of $x-3$.
