To add $\dfrac{1}{x^2-1}$ and $\dfrac{3}{x^2+x}$, use the LCM of the denominators as the common denominator. What is it?

- $(x^2-1)(x^2+x)$
- $x\,(x-1)(x+1)$
- $x\,(x+1)$
- $(x-1)(x+1)$

## Explanation

Factoring gives $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$ and $x^2+x=x\,(x+1)$. The factor $x+1$ is shared and occurs only once, while $x-1$ and $x$ each occur in just one denominator. Therefore, the LCM is $x\,(x-1)(x+1)$. The product of the denominators contains $x+1$ twice and gives a more complicated common denominator.
