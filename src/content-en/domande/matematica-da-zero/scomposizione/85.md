Which expression results from multiplying $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x+1}$ by $\dfrac{3x+3}{x-2}$, with $x\neq -1$ and $x\neq 2$?

- $3\,(x-2)$
- $\dfrac{x+2}{3}$
- $3\,(x+2)(x+1)$
- $3\,(x+2)$

## Explanation

Factoring gives $x^2-4=(x+2)(x-2)$ and $3x+3=3\,(x+1)$. The product becomes $\dfrac{(x+2)(x-2)}{x+1}\cdot\dfrac{3\,(x+1)}{x-2}$. Cancel the factors $x+1$ and $x-2$, leaving $3\,(x+2)$. The factor $3$ remains in the numerator because it comes from the second numerator. For $x=1$ the original product equals $\dfrac{-3}{2}\cdot\dfrac{6}{-1}=9$ and the result equals $3\cdot 3=9$.
