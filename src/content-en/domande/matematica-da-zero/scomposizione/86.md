Dividing $\dfrac{x}{x-1}$ by $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2-1}$, with $x\neq 0$, $x\neq 1$, and $x\neq -1$, gives which quotient?

- $\dfrac{x+1}{x}$
- $\dfrac{x}{x+1}$
- $\dfrac{x-1}{x}$
- $\dfrac{x^3}{(x-1)(x^2-1)}$

## Explanation

Change the quotient into a product using the reciprocal of the second fraction to get $\dfrac{x}{x-1}\cdot\dfrac{(x+1)(x-1)}{x^2}$. Cancel $x-1$ and one factor of $x$, leaving $\dfrac{x+1}{x}$. The result $\dfrac{x}{x+1}$ is the reciprocal of the correct one, while $\dfrac{x^3}{(x-1)(x^2-1)}$ comes from multiplying without inverting the second fraction.
