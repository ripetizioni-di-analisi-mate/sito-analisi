How do you write $\dfrac{x^2-6x+9}{x^2-9}$ after simplification?

- $\dfrac{x-3}{x+3}$
- $\dfrac{x+3}{x-3}$
- $\dfrac{x-6}{x}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The numerator is the square of a binomial, $x^2-6x+9=(x-3)^2$, and the denominator is a difference of squares, $x^2-9=(x+3)(x-3)$. Dividing by the common factor $x-3$ gives $\dfrac{x-3}{x+3}$. The result $\dfrac{x-6}{x}$ comes from canceling the terms $9$, which are not factors.
