Subtract $\dfrac{1}{x+2}$ from $\dfrac{1}{x-2}$, with $x\neq 2$ and $x\neq -2$. Which fraction results?

- $\dfrac{4}{(x-2)(x+2)}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{2x}{(x-2)(x+2)}$
- $-\dfrac{4}{(x-2)(x+2)}$

## Explanation

The common denominator is $(x-2)(x+2)$. The numerator is $(x+2)-(x-2)$, and the minus sign reverses the signs throughout the second numerator, giving $x+2-x+2=4$. Therefore, the difference is $\dfrac{4}{(x-2)(x+2)}$. The result $\dfrac{2x}{(x-2)(x+2)}$ comes from adding, and $0$ comes from a sign error.
