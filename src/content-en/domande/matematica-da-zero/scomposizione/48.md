How do you factor the difference of cubes $x^3-27$?

- $(x-3)(x^2-3x+9)$
- $(x-3)^3$
- $(x-3)(x^2+3x+9)$
- $(x-3)(x+3)^2$

## Explanation

Take $A=x$ and $B=3$, since $3^3=27$. The identity $A^3-B^3=(A-B)(A^2+AB+B^2)$ gives $(x-3)(x^2+3x+9)$. All the signs in the second factor are positive. The cube $(x-3)^3$ also contains lower-degree terms with alternating signs and equals $x^3-9x^2+27x-27$.
