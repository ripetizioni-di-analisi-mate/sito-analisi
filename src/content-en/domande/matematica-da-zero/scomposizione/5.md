The polynomial $x^4-1$ is written as $(x^2-1)(x^2+1)$. Why is this factorization incomplete?

- Because $x^2+1$ factors further as $(x+1)^2$
- Because $x^2-1$ factors further as $(x-1)(x+1)$
- Because the product $(x^2-1)(x^2+1)$ does not equal $x^4-1$
- Because the factors have degree $2$ rather than degree $1$

## Explanation

The product is correct because $(x^2-1)(x^2+1)=x^4-1$. A factorization is complete when all its factors are irreducible. The factor $x^2-1$ is a difference of squares and equals $(x-1)(x+1)$, whereas $x^2+1\geq 1$ shows that the other factor is never zero and is irreducible. The complete factorization is therefore $(x^2+1)(x-1)(x+1)$.
