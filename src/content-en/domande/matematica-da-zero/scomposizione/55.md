The polynomial $x^3+x^2-4x-4$ can be factored first by grouping and then by using another identity. What is its complete factorization?

- $(x+1)(x^2-4)$
- $(x-1)(x+2)(x-2)$
- $(x+1)(x+2)(x-2)$
- $(x+1)(x-2)^2$

## Explanation

Factoring $x^2$ out of the first two terms and $-4$ out of the last two gives $x^2\,(x+1)-4\,(x+1)=(x+1)(x^2-4)$. The factor $x^2-4$ is a difference of squares and equals $(x+2)(x-2)$. Therefore the complete factorization is $(x+1)(x+2)(x-2)$.
