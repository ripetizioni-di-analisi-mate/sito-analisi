What is the complete factorization of $3x^4 - 3x$?

- $3x(x-1)(x^2+x+1)$
- $3x(x-1)(x^2-x+1)$
- $3x(x^3-1)$
- $3x(x-1)^3$

## Explanation

The two terms have the common factor $3x$, so factoring it out gives $3x^4 - 3x = 3x(x^3 - 1)$. Since $1 = 1^3$, the binomial $x^3 - 1$ is a difference of cubes with bases $x$ and $1$. The identity $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$ then gives $x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$. The trinomial $x^2 + x + 1$ is the quadratic factor of a difference of cubes and is therefore irreducible, so the complete factorization is $3x(x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$. The expression $3x(x^3 - 1)$ is equal to the polynomial, but its factor $x^3 - 1$ can still be factored.
