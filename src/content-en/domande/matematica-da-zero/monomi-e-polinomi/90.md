A student writes $\dfrac{9a^2 + 3a}{3a} = 3a$. What is the correct result for $a \neq 0$?

- $3a$
- $3a^2 + 1$
- $3a + 1$
- $3a + 3$

## Explanation

The divisor $3a$ divides both terms. We have $\dfrac{9a^2}{3a} = 3a$ and $\dfrac{3a}{3a} = 1$, so the quotient is $3a + 1$. For $a = 2$ the numerator equals $42$ and $\dfrac{42}{6} = 7$, which is the value of $3a + 1$.
