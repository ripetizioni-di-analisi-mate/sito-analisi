What is the result of calculating $\left[ \left( \dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{1}{6} \right) \cdot 4 - 1 \right] : \dfrac{3}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

The sum in parentheses is $\dfrac{2}{6} + \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Inside the brackets we have $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 - 1 = 2 - 1 = 1$. Finally, $1 : \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. The value $1$ is the result inside the brackets before the division, $\dfrac{4}{3}$ comes from omitting the subtraction of $1$, and $\dfrac{3}{2}$ from multiplying by $\dfrac{3}{2}$ instead of dividing.
