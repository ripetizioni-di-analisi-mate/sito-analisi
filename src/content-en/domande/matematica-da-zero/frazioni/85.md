Evaluate $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^2 + \dfrac{3}{4} : 3$. What is its value?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

First we evaluate the power, $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$, and the division, $\dfrac{3}{4} : 3 = \dfrac{3}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. Then we add, giving $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{3}$ comes from adding the two terms first and then dividing by $3$, while $1$ comes from omitting the division by $3$ and $\dfrac{5}{2}$ from multiplying by $3$.
