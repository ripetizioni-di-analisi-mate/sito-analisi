Of $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, $-\dfrac{2}{3}$, $-\dfrac{3}{4}$, and $-\dfrac{3}{5}$, which number is greatest?

- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{5}$

## Explanation

With the common denominator $60$, the numbers are $-\dfrac{30}{60}$, $-\dfrac{40}{60}$, $-\dfrac{45}{60}$, and $-\dfrac{36}{60}$. Among negative numbers, the greatest is closest to $0$, that is, the one with the least negative numerator, and $-30$ is the greatest of the four numerators. The greatest number is therefore $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. On the number line it lies farther to the right than the others.
