How many admissible solutions does the equation $\dfrac{2}{x-1}-\dfrac{1}{x+1}=\dfrac{4}{x^2-1}$ have?

- None, because the solution $x=1$ is excluded
- One, $x=1$
- One, $x=3$
- One, $x=-1$

## Explanation

Under the condition $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$, we multiply by this product to obtain $2(x+1)-(x-1)=4$, that is, $x+3=4$ and $x=1$. The value $1$ makes the denominator $x-1$ zero, so it is discarded. The equation is inconsistent. The value $3$ results from leaving the sign of $-1$ unchanged when expanding $-(x-1)$.
