The figure shows the lines $y=3x-1$ and $y=3x+2$. What can we say about the equation $3x-1=3x+2$?

::grafico{f="3*x-1;3*x+2" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-8 ymax=8 legenda="y = 3x - 1;y = 3x + 2" titolo="The lines $y = 3x - 1$ and $y = 3x + 2$"}

- It is an identity
- Its only solution is $x=0$
- It is inconsistent
- Its only solution is $x=\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The two lines have the same coefficient of $x$, so they are parallel and do not coincide. They have no points in common, and indeed $3x-1=3x+2$ becomes $0\cdot x=3$. The equation is inconsistent and no number is a solution. Two distinct parallel lines always correspond to an inconsistent equation.
