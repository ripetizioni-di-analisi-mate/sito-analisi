To clear the denominators in $\dfrac{x}{4}-\dfrac{x}{6}=1$, we multiply all terms by the least common multiple. Which equation do we obtain?

- $3x-2x=12$
- $3x-2x=1$
- $4x-6x=12$
- $3x+2x=12$

## Explanation

The least common multiple of $4$ and $6$ is $12$. We have $12\cdot\dfrac{x}{4}=3x$, $12\cdot\dfrac{x}{6}=2x$, and $12\cdot 1=12$. The equation is therefore $3x-2x=12$. The equality $3x-2x=1$ results from neglecting to multiply the term $1$ as well. Checking gives $\dfrac{12}{4}-\dfrac{12}{6}=3-2=1$.
