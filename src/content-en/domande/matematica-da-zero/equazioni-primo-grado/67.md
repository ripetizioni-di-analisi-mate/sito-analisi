Find the domain restrictions for the rational equation $\dfrac{3}{x-2}=\dfrac{6}{x}$ and then solve it. What do you find?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $4$
- $-4$
- $2$

## Explanation

The domain restrictions are $x\neq 2$ and $x\neq 0$. Multiplying by $x(x-2)$ gives $3x=6(x-2)$, that is, $3x=6x-12$. Therefore $12=3x$ and $x=4$. The value $2$ makes a denominator zero and in any case is not a solution. Checking gives $\dfrac{3}{4-2}=\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac{3}{2}$.
