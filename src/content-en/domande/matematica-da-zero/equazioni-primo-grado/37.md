What is the solution of the equation $\dfrac{2x-1}{3}=\dfrac{x+4}{2}$?

- $-2$
- $6$
- $\dfrac{11}{4}$
- $14$

## Explanation

The least common multiple is $6$. Multiplying by $6$ gives $2(2x-1)=3(x+4)$, that is, $4x-2=3x+12$. Therefore $x=14$. The value $-2$ results from moving $3x$ without changing its sign. The right-hand side $\dfrac{x+4}{2}$ becomes $3(x+4)$ and the left-hand side becomes $2(2x-1)$. Checking gives $\dfrac{2\cdot 14-1}{3}=9$ and $\dfrac{14+4}{2}=9$.
