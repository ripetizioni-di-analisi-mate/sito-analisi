What is the solution of the equation $\dfrac{2x-1}{3}=\dfrac{x+4}{2}$?

- $2$
- $6$
- $\dfrac{11}{4}$
- $14$

## Explanation

The least common multiple of the denominators $3$ and $2$ is $6$. Multiplying both sides by $6$, the left-hand side becomes $2(2x-1)$ and the right-hand side becomes $3(x+4)$, so the equation is equivalent to $4x-2=3x+12$. Moving $3x$ to the left-hand side and $-2$ to the right-hand side gives $4x-3x=12+2$, therefore $x=14$. The value $2$ comes from moving $3x$ without changing its sign, the value $\dfrac{11}{4}$ from multiplying each numerator by the denominator of the other fraction, and the value $6$ from multiplying only the term $x$ of the second numerator by $3$.
