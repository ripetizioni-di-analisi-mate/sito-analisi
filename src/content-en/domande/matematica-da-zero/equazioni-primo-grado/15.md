Multiplication by $0$ on both sides of $x+1=5$ gives $0=0$. Why is the resulting equation not equivalent to the original?

- Because multiplying by a number always changes the solutions
- Because $0=0$ is true for every $x$, whereas $x+1=5$ is true only when $x=4$
- Because only positive numbers can be multiplied
- Because the only solution of $x+1=5$ is $x=5$

## Explanation

The second principle applies only to a nonzero number. The only solution of the equation $x+1=5$ is $x=4$. Every number satisfies the equation $0=0$, so it has additional solutions. Multiplication by a nonzero number, including a negative one, preserves the solutions.
