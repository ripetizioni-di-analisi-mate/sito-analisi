To check whether $x=\dfrac{2}{3}$ is a solution of $3x+1=5x-\dfrac{1}{3}$, we evaluate both sides. What is the result?

- The sides equal $3$ and $\dfrac{10}{3}$, so it is not a solution
- The sides equal $2$ and $3$, so it is not a solution
- The sides equal $3$ and $\dfrac{11}{3}$, so it is not a solution
- Both sides equal $3$, so it is a solution

## Explanation

The left-hand side equals $3\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}+1=2+1=3$. The right-hand side equals $5\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{10}{3}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{9}{3}=3$. The two values are equal, so $x=\dfrac{2}{3}$ is a solution. The other results come from an omitted term or an incorrect sign.
