A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the origin $O$ and rotates in the vertical plane; $\vartheta$ is the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise. A torsion spring mounted in the hinge applies to the rod an elastic couple of moment $-\chi\,\vartheta$, with $\chi > 0$, which vanishes when the rod points vertically downward. What is the potential of the active force system, up to an additive constant?

- $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + \dfrac{1}{2}\,\chi\,\vartheta^2$
- $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \chi\,\vartheta^2$
- $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \dfrac{1}{2}\,\chi\,\vartheta^2$
- $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \dfrac{1}{2}\,\chi\,\vartheta^2$

## Explanation

The center of mass of the rod is the midpoint, at height $y_G = -\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, hence the potential of the weight is $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$. The elastic couple has elementary work $-\chi\,\vartheta\,d\vartheta$, which is the differential of the function $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\chi\,\vartheta^2$, therefore the torsion spring is conservative with this potential. Adding the two contributions one obtains
$$
U(\vartheta) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,\chi\,\vartheta^2 .
$$
The derivative $U'(\vartheta)$ contains the term $-\chi\,\vartheta$, which is the moment of the elastic couple and pulls the rod back toward $\vartheta = 0$. The expression with $+\dfrac{1}{2}\,\chi\,\vartheta^2$ contains the potential energy of the spring in place of its potential.
