A sleeve, modeled as a material point of mass $m$, slides without friction on a horizontal guide fixed to the car of an elevator, which goes up with constant velocity $v\,\hat{\jmath}$. The sleeve is at rest relative to the car, and the reaction of the guide is $m g\,\hat{\jmath}$. What are the power of the reaction and its virtual work for a virtual displacement $\delta x\,\hat{\imath}$ along the guide?

- Zero power and zero virtual work
- Power $m g v$ and zero virtual work
- Power $m g v$ and virtual work $m g\,\delta x$
- Power $-m g v$ and zero virtual work

## Explanation

The guide moves with the car, hence the constraint is moving, and the sleeve, at rest relative to the car, has velocity $v\,\hat{\jmath}$. The power of the reaction is therefore
$$
\Pi = m g\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot v\,\hat{\jmath} = m g v ,
$$
which is positive, and indeed in the actual motion it is the reaction of the guide that lifts the sleeve. The virtual displacement is computed with time held fixed, so it is parallel to the guide, and the virtual work of the reaction is $m g\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot \delta x\,\hat{\imath} = 0$. The smooth moving guide is therefore an ideal constraint, although in the actual motion its reaction has nonzero power.
