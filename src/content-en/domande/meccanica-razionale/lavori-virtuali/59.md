A homogeneous square plate, of mass $m$ and side $\ell$, moves freely in the vertical plane $Oxy$. The coordinates $x_G$ and $y_G$ of the center of mass and the angle $\vartheta$ by which the plate is rotated are taken as Lagrangian coordinates. What are the Lagrangian components of the weight?

- $Q_{x_G} = 0$, $Q_{y_G} = -m g$, $Q_\vartheta = 0$
- $Q_{x_G} = 0$, $Q_{y_G} = m g$, $Q_\vartheta = 0$
- $Q_{x_G} = 0$, $Q_{y_G} = -m g$, $Q_\vartheta = -\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$
- $Q_{x_G} = 0$, $Q_{y_G} = -m g\,y_G$, $Q_\vartheta = 0$

## Explanation

The weight is the constant vector $-m g\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at the center of mass, hence the Lagrangian component relative to the coordinate $q_h$ is $Q_h = -m g\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot \dfrac{\partial G}{\partial q_h}$. The position $G - O = x_G\,\hat{\imath} + y_G\,\hat{\jmath}$ does not depend on $\vartheta$, therefore $\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial \vartheta} = \vec{0}$, while $\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial x_G} = \hat{\imath}$ and $\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial y_G} = \hat{\jmath}$. It follows that
$$
Q_{x_G} = 0 ,
\qquad
Q_{y_G} = -m g ,
\qquad
Q_\vartheta = 0 .
$$
The same result is obtained by differentiating the potential $U = -m g\,y_G$. The component $Q_\vartheta$ is zero because the moment of the weight about the center of mass is zero.
