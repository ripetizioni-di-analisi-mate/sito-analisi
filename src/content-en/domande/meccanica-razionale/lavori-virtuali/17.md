A particle travels half a turn of the circle of center $O$ and radius $\ell$. A force of constant magnitude $F$ acts on it, at every instant directed from $O$ towards the particle. What is the work of the force?

- $2\,F\,\ell$
- $\pi\,F\,\ell$
- $-2\,F\,\ell$
- $0$

## Explanation

The infinitesimal displacement of a particle that travels along a circle is tangent to the circle, whereas the given force has the direction of the radius $O P$. The two vectors are therefore orthogonal at every instant and the elementary work $dL = \vec{F} \cdot dP$ is zero along the whole path. Integrating one then obtains $L = 0$. The same result follows from the potential, since the force is central of constant magnitude and admits the potential $U = F\,r$, which on the circle equals $F\,\ell$ and therefore takes the same value in the initial position and in the final one.
