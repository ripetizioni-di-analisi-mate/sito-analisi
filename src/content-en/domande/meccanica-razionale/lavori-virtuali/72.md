A smooth wedge, with its upper face inclined at the angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal, slides on a smooth horizontal floor. A vertical rod of weight $W$, constrained by a smooth vertical guide, rests with its lower end on the inclined face. The horizontal force $F$ acts on the wedge and pushes it under the rod, as in the figure. Which value of $F$ keeps the system in equilibrium?

- $W\sin\alpha$
- $\dfrac{W}{\tan\alpha}$
- $W\tan\alpha$
- $W\cos\alpha$

## Explanation

The system has one degree of freedom, the horizontal displacement $\delta x$ of the wedge in the direction of the force. The inclined face has slope $\tan\alpha$, therefore, as the wedge advances by $\delta x$, the point of the face under the rod rises by $\delta x\tan\alpha$ and the rod, which stays in contact, rises by the same amount. The weight of the wedge does no work, since the wedge moves horizontally, hence
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = F\,\delta x - W\tan\alpha\,\delta x .
$$
The guide and the contacts are smooth, hence ideal, and equilibrium is equivalent to $\delta L^{(a)} = 0$, which gives $F = W\tan\alpha$. For $\alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ one has $\tan\alpha < 1$, and a force smaller than the weight keeps the rod in equilibrium.
