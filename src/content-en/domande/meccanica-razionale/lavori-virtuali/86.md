A material point $P$ of mass $m$ hangs from the fixed point $O$ by an inextensible string of length $\ell$ and lies on the vertical below $O$, with the string taut. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $P$ to the fixed point $C$, placed at distance $\ell$ above $O$. The string can only hold $P$ back, preventing it from moving farther than $\ell$ from $O$. For which values of $k$ is the configuration an equilibrium configuration?

- $k \leq \dfrac{m g}{\ell}$
- $k \geq \dfrac{m g}{2\,\ell}$
- $k \leq \dfrac{m g}{4\,\ell}$
- $k \leq \dfrac{m g}{2\,\ell}$

## Explanation

The string is a unilateral constraint, and the configuration with the taut string is a boundary one, therefore the admissible virtual displacements are those that do not move $P$ away from $O$, that is, the horizontal displacements and the upward ones, with $\delta y \geq 0$. In the configuration $P - C = -2\ell\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence the spring exerts on $P$ the force $-k\,(P - C) = 2\,k\,\ell\,\hat{\jmath}$, directed upward, while the weight is $-m g\,\hat{\jmath}$. The two forces are vertical, so the horizontal displacements give zero work and what remains is
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = (2\,k\,\ell - m g)\,\delta y .
$$
Equilibrium requires $\delta L^{(a)} \leq 0$ for every $\delta y \geq 0$, that is, $k \leq \dfrac{m g}{2\,\ell}$; for larger values the spring lifts the point and the string goes slack. The value $\dfrac{m g}{\ell}$ is obtained by taking $\ell$ instead of $2\ell$ as the length of the spring.
