Two rods $OA$ and $AB$ form a double pendulum: the first one is hinged to the ground at $O$, and the two rods are connected at $A$ by a smooth internal hinge. At the hinge the rod $OA$ exerts the force $\vec{\Phi}$ on the rod $AB$, and the rod $AB$ exerts the force $-\vec{\Phi}$ on the rod $OA$. Which statement holds for the total virtual work of these two forces?

- It is zero, since the two forces are opposite and applied to two points that coincide at $A$ and have the same virtual displacement
- It is zero, since each of the two forces is orthogonal to the virtual displacement of $A$
- It is zero only when the hinge $A$ is at rest
- It is different from zero, since the internal hinge moves together with the rods

## Explanation

The point of $AB$ where $\vec{\Phi}$ is applied and the point of $OA$ where $-\vec{\Phi}$ is applied coincide with the hinge $A$, and the hinge forces them to stay joined, therefore they have the same virtual displacement $\delta A$. The total virtual work is hence
$$
\vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta A + (-\vec{\Phi}) \cdot \delta A = 0
$$
for every virtual displacement of the system. Each of the two forces does the virtual work $\pm\vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta A$, in general different from zero, since $\vec{\Phi}$ can have any direction in the plane. The smooth internal hinge is therefore an ideal constraint, and the reactions that the rods exchange do not appear in the equations of the principle of virtual work.
