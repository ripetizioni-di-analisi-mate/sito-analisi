A plane rigid body is free in its own plane, with no constraints, and it is acted upon by active forces with resultant $\vec{R}^{(a)}$ and resultant moment $M_\Omega^{(a)}\,\hat{k}$ about one of its points $\Omega$. What is the condition $\delta L^{(a)} = 0$ for every virtual displacement of the body equivalent to?

- $\vec{R}^{(a)} = \vec{0}$ and $M_\Omega^{(a)} = 0$
- $\vec{R}^{(a)} = \vec{0}$
- $M_\Omega^{(a)} = 0$
- $\vec{R}^{(a)} \cdot M_\Omega^{(a)}\,\hat{k} = 0$

## Explanation

By the virtual work of a system of forces on a plane rigid body one has
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = \vec{R}^{(a)} \cdot \delta\Omega + M_\Omega^{(a)}\,\delta\vartheta ,
$$
where $\delta\Omega$ is the virtual translation of $\Omega$ and $\delta\vartheta$ the virtual rotation. The body is free, hence $\delta\Omega$ and $\delta\vartheta$ are arbitrary and independent. Choosing $\delta\vartheta = 0$ and any $\delta\Omega$ one obtains $\vec{R}^{(a)} = \vec{0}$, and then choosing $\delta\Omega = \vec{0}$ and $\delta\vartheta \neq 0$ one obtains $M_\Omega^{(a)} = 0$. For the free rigid body the principle of virtual work is therefore equivalent to the **cardinal equations of statics**, one for the translations and one for the rotations. The condition $\vec{R}^{(a)} \cdot M_\Omega^{(a)}\,\hat{k} = 0$ is always satisfied in the plane, because the resultant is orthogonal to $\hat{k}$, therefore it does not characterize equilibrium.
