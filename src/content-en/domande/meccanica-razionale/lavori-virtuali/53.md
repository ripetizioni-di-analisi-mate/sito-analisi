Which of the following forces, applied to a material point of the plane $Oxy$, is not conservative? The constant $c$ is positive.

- $\vec{F} = c\,x\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\vec{F} = c\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$
- $\vec{F} = c\,y\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\vec{F} = c\,(x^2\,\hat{\imath} + y^2\,\hat{\jmath})$

## Explanation

The four forces have components of class $C^1$ in the plane, which is simply connected, hence each of them is conservative if and only if the closedness condition $\dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = \dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial x}$ holds. For $\vec{F} = c\,y\,\hat{\imath}$ one has $\dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = c$ and $\dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial x} = 0$, therefore the condition is not satisfied and the force **is not conservative**. The other three satisfy the condition and admit the potentials $\dfrac{c\,x^2}{2}$, $c\,(x + y)$ and $\dfrac{c}{3}\,(x^3 + y^3)$ respectively. The force $c\,y\,\hat{\imath}$ is horizontal, with an intensity that depends on the height, and along the boundary of the rectangle $[0, a] \times [0, b]$ traveled counterclockwise it does the work $-c\,a\,b$, which is different from zero.
