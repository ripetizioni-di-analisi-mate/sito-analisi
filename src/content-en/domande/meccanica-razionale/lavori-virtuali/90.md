A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at $O$ and rotates in the vertical plane; $\vartheta$ is the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise. Two fixed stops limit the rotation to the interval $0 \leq \vartheta \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and a constant couple of moment $\mathcal{M}$ in the counterclockwise direction acts on the rod, with $\mathcal{M} > \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$. What are the equilibrium configurations?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.36,-1.98" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" poligoni="-0.4,-2.45 -0.1,-2.45 -0.1,-1.9 -0.4,-1.9;1.9,0.1 2.45,0.1 2.45,0.4 1.9,0.4" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.5;0,0 2.5,0" punti="0.68,-0.99,G,baricentro" coppie="1.3 -1.1,1,ℳ" etichette="0.22,-0.75,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged between two stops, acted upon by the weight and by a constant couple"}

- Only $\vartheta = 0$
- Both $\vartheta = 0$ and $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- Only $\vartheta = \arcsin\dfrac{2\,\mathcal{M}}{m g \ell}$
- Only $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The potential is $U(\vartheta) = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + \mathcal{M}\,\vartheta$, hence $U'(\vartheta) = \mathcal{M} - \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$, which is positive on the whole interval because $\mathcal{M} > \dfrac{m g \ell}{2} \geq \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$. In the ordinary configurations, with $0 < \vartheta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, equilibrium would require $U'(\vartheta) = 0$, therefore none of them is an equilibrium configuration; the equation $\sin\vartheta = \dfrac{2\,\mathcal{M}}{m g \ell}$ has no solutions, since the right-hand side exceeds $1$. In the boundary configuration $\vartheta = 0$ the virtual displacements are those with $\delta\vartheta \geq 0$, and the condition $U'(0)\,\delta\vartheta = \mathcal{M}\,\delta\vartheta \leq 0$ is not satisfied. In the boundary configuration $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ only $\delta\vartheta \leq 0$ are admissible, and the condition $U'\!\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)\delta\vartheta \leq 0$ is satisfied, since $U'\!\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) > 0$. The rod is therefore in equilibrium **only** against the upper stop.
