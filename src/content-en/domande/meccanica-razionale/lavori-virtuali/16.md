A disk rotates about its center, which is fixed, with angular velocity of magnitude $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. A couple of moment $3\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ acts on it, in the same direction as the rotation. What is the power of the couple?

- $12\,\mathrm{W}$
- $-12\,\mathrm{W}$
- $6\,\mathrm{W}$
- $0.75\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

A couple of moment $\mathcal{M}\,\hat{k}$ applied to a rigid body that rotates with angular velocity $\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k}$ has power $\Pi = \mathcal{M}\,\dot{\vartheta}$, since its resultant is zero and its moment is the same with respect to every pole. The couple acts in the direction of the rotation, therefore $\mathcal{M}$ and $\dot{\vartheta}$ have the same sign and
$$
\Pi = 3\,\mathrm{N\,m} \cdot 4\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 12\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The power is positive, hence the couple does work on the disk. The value $0.75\,\mathrm{W}$ is obtained by dividing the moment by the angular velocity instead of multiplying.
