A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the origin $O$ and rotates in the vertical plane. The rod is acted upon by the weight, by the force of an ideal spring of constant $k$ connecting $A$ to the fixed point $B = (0, \ell)$, and by a couple of constant moment $\mathcal{M} > 0$ in the counterclockwise direction. The rod makes a full counterclockwise turn and returns to the initial configuration. What is the total work of the active force system?

- $0$
- $-2\pi\,\mathcal{M}$
- $\pi\,\mathcal{M}$
- $2\pi\,\mathcal{M}$

## Explanation

The weight and the elastic force are conservative, with potentials that depend only on the configuration of the rod, therefore their work in a motion that brings the rod back to the initial configuration is zero. The couple has elementary work $\mathcal{M}\,d\vartheta$, where $\vartheta$ is the rotation angle of the rod, hence in a full turn it does the work
$$
L = \int_0^{2\pi} \mathcal{M}\,d\vartheta = 2\pi\,\mathcal{M} ,
$$
which is also the total work. The function $\mathcal{M}\,\vartheta$ is used as the potential of the couple for displacements that do not complete a turn, but after one turn it increases by $2\pi\,\mathcal{M}$ while the configuration is the same, therefore the couple does not admit a potential that is a function of the configuration alone. The value $0$ is obtained by treating the couple as a conservative force over the full turn as well.
