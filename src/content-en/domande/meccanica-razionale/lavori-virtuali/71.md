Two gears, of radii $r$ and $3r$, rotate about their fixed centers and mesh with each other, so that there is no slipping at the contact point. A couple of moment $\mathcal{M}_1$ acts on the small gear. Which couple, applied to the large gear, keeps the system in equilibrium?

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,0.6;2.4,0,1.8" punti="0,0,O_1,centro;2.4,0,O_2,centro" coppie="-0.2 0.95,1,ℳ_1" etichette="-0.2,1.6, " titolo="Two gears of radii $r$ and $3r$ meshing without slipping"}

- $\mathcal{M}_1$, in the same direction as $\mathcal{M}_1$
- $\dfrac{\mathcal{M}_1}{3}$, in the same direction as $\mathcal{M}_1$
- $3\,\mathcal{M}_1$, in the direction opposite to $\mathcal{M}_1$
- $3\,\mathcal{M}_1$, in the same direction as $\mathcal{M}_1$

## Explanation

The system has one degree of freedom. At the contact point the rims of the two gears have the same displacement, and since the centers lie on opposite sides of the contact the virtual rotations have opposite directions and satisfy $r\,\delta\varphi_1 = -3r\,\delta\varphi_2$, that is, $\delta\varphi_2 = -\dfrac{\delta\varphi_1}{3}$. Denoting by $\mathcal{M}_2$ the moment of the couple on the large gear, the virtual work is
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = \mathcal{M}_1\,\delta\varphi_1 + \mathcal{M}_2\,\delta\varphi_2 = \left( \mathcal{M}_1 - \frac{\mathcal{M}_2}{3} \right) \delta\varphi_1 .
$$
Contact without slipping is an ideal constraint, hence equilibrium is equivalent to $\delta L^{(a)} = 0$, which gives $\mathcal{M}_2 = 3\,\mathcal{M}_1$, with the same sign and therefore in the **same direction** as $\mathcal{M}_1$. The couple $\mathcal{M}_1$ tends to rotate the large gear in the direction opposite to its own, and $\mathcal{M}_2$ opposes this rotation.
