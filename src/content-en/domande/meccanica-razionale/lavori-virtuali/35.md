A rod has one end constrained by a smooth sleeve to slide along a fixed straight guide and can rotate about that end. What is the virtual work of the reaction of the sleeve?

- It is zero, since the reaction is orthogonal to the guide whereas the virtual displacement of the end is parallel to it
- It is positive, since the sleeve pushes the end along the guide
- It is zero, since the virtual displacement of the end is zero
- It is negative, since the sleeve opposes the sliding

## Explanation

The sleeve is smooth, therefore the reaction it exerts is orthogonal to the guide, and it allows sliding, hence the virtual displacement of the constrained end is parallel to the guide. The reaction is applied only at that end, therefore its virtual work is the scalar product of two orthogonal vectors and is
$$
\delta L^{(v)} = \vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta A = 0 .
$$
The smooth sleeve is hence an ideal constraint. The virtual displacement of the end is not zero, since the constraint allows translation along the guide.
