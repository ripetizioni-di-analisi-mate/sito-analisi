A homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, has the end $A$ constrained to a smooth vertical guide and the end $B$ to a smooth horizontal guide, which meet at the origin $O$; an ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $B$ to $O$, as in the figure. The plane is vertical and $\vartheta \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$ is the angle between the rod and the horizontal guide. Besides the vertical configuration $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which equilibrium configuration does the system admit?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,2.4;B,3.2,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,180;B,carrello" linee="-0.3,0 4.2,0;0,-0.3 0,3.2;0.00,0.00 0.35,0.00 0.56,0.30 0.97,-0.30 1.39,0.30 1.81,-0.30 2.23,0.30 2.64,-0.30 2.85,0.00 3.20,0.00" punti="1.6,1.2,G,baricentro" forze="1.6 0.15,0,-0.6,mg" etichette="1.6,-0.62,k;2.72,0.22,ϑ" titolo="Rod with its ends on two orthogonal guides and spring between $B$ and the origin"}

- $\vartheta = \arccos\dfrac{m g}{4\,k\,\ell}$
- $\vartheta = \arcsin\dfrac{m g}{2\,k\,\ell}$
- $\vartheta = \arcsin\dfrac{m g}{4\,k\,\ell}$
- $\vartheta = \arctan\dfrac{m g}{4\,k\,\ell}$

## Explanation

From the length of the rod it follows that $B - O = 2\ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath}$ and $A - O = 2\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore the center of mass, the midpoint of the rod, has height $y_G = \ell\sin\vartheta$. The spring has length $2\ell\cos\vartheta$, hence the potential of the system is
$$
U(\vartheta) = -m g \ell\sin\vartheta - 2\,k\,\ell^2\cos^2\vartheta .
$$
The constraints are fixed, ideal and bilateral, therefore equilibrium is equivalent to $U'(\vartheta) = 0$, and differentiating one obtains
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -\cos\vartheta\,\big( m g \ell - 4\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta \big) = 0 .
$$
The first factor vanishes for $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, whereas the second gives $\sin\vartheta = \dfrac{m g}{4\,k\,\ell}$, that is, $\vartheta = \arcsin\dfrac{m g}{4\,k\,\ell}$, a configuration that is acceptable when $m g \leq 4\,k\,\ell$. The value $\arcsin\dfrac{m g}{2\,k\,\ell}$ is obtained by differentiating $\cos^2\vartheta$ without the factor $2$ coming from the chain rule.
