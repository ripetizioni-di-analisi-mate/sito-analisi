A particle in the plane is acted upon by the position-dependent force $\vec{F} = c\,(y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$, with $c > 0$ constant. Which function is a potential of the force?

- $U = \dfrac{c}{2}\,(x^2 + y^2)$
- $U = c\,x\,y$
- $U = c\,(x + y)$
- $U = \dfrac{c}{2}\,x^2\,y^2$

## Explanation

A function $U$ is a potential of the force when $\vec{F} = \operatorname{grad} U$, that is, when $\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial x} = c\,y$ and $\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial y} = c\,x$. The function $U = c\,x\,y$ has partial derivatives
$$
\frac{\partial U}{\partial x} = c\,y ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial U}{\partial y} = c\,x ,
$$
therefore it satisfies both conditions and is a potential of the force. The function $\dfrac{c}{2}\,(x^2 + y^2)$ has gradient $c\,(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath})$, hence it is the potential of a different force, with the components exchanged. The potential is determined up to an additive constant, since the plane is connected.
