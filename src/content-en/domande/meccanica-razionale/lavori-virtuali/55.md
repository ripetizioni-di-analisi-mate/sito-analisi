A force of constant magnitude $F$, directed at every instant from $P$ toward the fixed point $O$, acts on a material point $P$. The point moves from a position at distance $3\ell$ from $O$ to a position at distance $\ell$ from $O$, along an arbitrary path. What is the work of the force?

- $-2\,F\,\ell$
- $2\,F\,\ell$
- $4\,F\,\ell$
- $0$

## Explanation

Setting $r = |P - O|$ and $\hat{u} = \dfrac{P - O}{r}$, the force is written as $\vec{F} = -F\,\hat{u}$, hence it is a central force with $f(r) = -F$. A central force is conservative and has as potential an antiderivative of $f$, therefore $U(r) = -F\,r$. By the theorem on the work of a conservative force
$$
L = U(\ell) - U(3\ell) = -F\,\ell + 3\,F\,\ell = 2\,F\,\ell ,
$$
whatever the path followed. The work is positive, since the point approaches $O$, in the direction of the force. The value $-2\,F\,\ell$ is obtained by computing $U(3\ell) - U(\ell)$, that is, by exchanging the endpoints.
