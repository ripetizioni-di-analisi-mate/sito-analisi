A rigid rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ has the end $A$ on a smooth vertical guide and the end $B$ on a smooth horizontal guide, the two guides meeting at the point $O$; the rod forms the angle $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ with the horizontal guide, as in the figure. In a virtual displacement the end $A$ goes down by $\delta$. How does the end $B$ move?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,2.6;B,1.5,0;O,0,0,-" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,180;B,carrello" linee="-0.3,0 3,0;0,-0.3 0,3.2" punti="0,0,O" frecce="0,2.6,0,1.95,δ" etichette="1.2,0.22,ϑ" titolo="Rod with its ends on two orthogonal guides, inclined at $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to the horizontal guide"}

- By $\dfrac{\delta}{\sqrt{3}}$, moving away from $O$
- By $\delta$, moving away from $O$
- By $\sqrt{3}\,\delta$, moving toward $O$
- By $\sqrt{3}\,\delta$, moving away from $O$

## Explanation

With the origin at $O$ one has $y_A = \ell\sin\vartheta$ and $x_B = \ell\cos\vartheta$, hence the virtual displacements of the two ends are $\delta y_A = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta$ and $\delta x_B = -\ell\sin\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta$. Eliminating $\delta\vartheta$ one obtains the relation $\delta x_B = -\tan\vartheta\,\delta y_A$. The end $A$ goes down, so $\delta y_A = -\delta$, and for $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ one finds
$$
\delta x_B = \sqrt{3}\,\delta > 0 ,
$$
therefore $B$ moves away from $O$ by $\sqrt{3}\,\delta$. The value $\dfrac{\delta}{\sqrt{3}}$ is obtained by using $\cot\vartheta$ instead of $\tan\vartheta$, that is, by inverting the ratio between the two displacements.
