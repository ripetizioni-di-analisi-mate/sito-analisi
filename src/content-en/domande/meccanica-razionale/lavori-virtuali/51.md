A conservative force with potential energy $V(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,(x^2 + 4\,y^2)$, with $k > 0$, acts on a material point of the plane. What is the force at the point $P = (\ell, \ell)$?

- $k\,\ell\,(\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $-k\,\ell\,(\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell\,(\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $-k\,\ell\,(\hat{\imath} + 8\,\hat{\jmath})$

## Explanation

With the convention $\vec{F} = \operatorname{grad} U$ and $V = -U$, the force is the opposite of the gradient of the potential energy, $\vec{F} = -\operatorname{grad} V$. The partial derivatives of $V$ are $\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial x} = k\,x$ and $\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial y} = 4\,k\,y$, hence
$$
\vec{F} = -k\,x\,\hat{\imath} - 4\,k\,y\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
At the point $P = (\ell, \ell)$ one therefore obtains $\vec{F} = -k\,\ell\,(\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})$, a restoring force toward the origin that is stronger in the $y$ direction. The vector $k\,\ell\,(\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})$ is obtained by exchanging the potential energy with the potential, while $-k\,\ell\,(\hat{\imath} + 8\,\hat{\jmath})$ comes from forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the second term.
