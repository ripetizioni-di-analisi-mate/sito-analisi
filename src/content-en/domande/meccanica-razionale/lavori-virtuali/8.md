A homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, has the end $A$ constrained by a smooth sleeve to slide along a fixed horizontal guide and can rotate about $A$ in the vertical plane, below the guide. The constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, is applied at the end $B$, as in the figure. Taking as generalized coordinates the abscissa $s$ of $A$ and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the guide, measured below the guide, what is the Lagrangian component $Q_\vartheta$?

::struttura{nodi="A,1.8,0;B,4.4,-1.5" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,90" linee="-0.4,0 5,0" punti="0,0,O;3.1,-0.75,G,baricentro" forze="B,0.75,0,F;3.1 -2.3,0,-0.5,mg" quote="0 0,A,s,0.75" etichette="2.6,-0.35,ϑ" titolo="Rod with the end $A$ on a horizontal guide and a constant force applied at $B$"}

- $\ell\,(m g\cos\vartheta - 2\,F\sin\vartheta)$
- $\ell\,(m g\cos\vartheta + 2\,F\sin\vartheta)$
- $\ell\,(2\,m g\cos\vartheta - 2\,F\sin\vartheta)$
- $\ell\,(m g\sin\vartheta - 2\,F\cos\vartheta)$

## Explanation

With the frame of the figure one has $A - O = s\,\hat{\imath}$, therefore
$$
B - O = (s + 2\ell\cos\vartheta)\,\hat{\imath} - 2\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - O = (s + \ell\cos\vartheta)\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The derivatives with respect to $\vartheta$ are $\dfrac{\partial B}{\partial \vartheta} = -2\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - 2\ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial \vartheta} = -\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$. The active force system is made of the force $F\,\hat{\imath}$ applied at $B$ and of the weight $-m g\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $G$, hence
$$
Q_\vartheta = F\,\hat{\imath} \cdot \frac{\partial B}{\partial \vartheta} - m g\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot \frac{\partial G}{\partial \vartheta} = -2\,F\,\ell\sin\vartheta + m g \ell\cos\vartheta .
$$
The moment of the weight with respect to $A$ has moment arm $\ell\cos\vartheta$, whereas that of the force has moment arm $2\ell\sin\vartheta$, and the two contributions have opposite signs.
