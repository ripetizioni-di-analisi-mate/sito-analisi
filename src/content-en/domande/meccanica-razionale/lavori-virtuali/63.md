Two bodies, modeled as material points, are connected by an inextensible string, always taut, that passes over an ideal pulley with a fixed axis. The string exerts on each body a force of magnitude $\tau$, directed along its own segment toward the pulley. Which statement holds for the total virtual work of these two forces, in the virtual displacements compatible with the taut string?

- It is zero, since each of the two forces is orthogonal to the virtual displacement of the body to which it is applied
- It is positive, since the string pulls both bodies toward the pulley
- It is zero only if the two bodies have the same mass
- It is zero, since the sum of the lengths of the two segments of string stays constant

## Explanation

Let $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ be the lengths of the two segments of string, measured from each body to the pulley. The force on the first body is directed toward the pulley, hence its virtual work is $-\tau\,\delta\ell_1$, since a lengthening of the segment moves the body away from the pulley; in the same way the work of the force on the second body is $-\tau\,\delta\ell_2$. The string is inextensible and the pulley is ideal, therefore the sum $\ell_1 + \ell_2$ stays constant and $\delta\ell_1 + \delta\ell_2 = 0$. It follows that
$$
\delta L = -\tau\,(\delta\ell_1 + \delta\ell_2) = 0 ,
$$
and the taut string is therefore an ideal constraint. Each of the two forces, taken alone, does in general a virtual work different from zero, since the body can move along the direction of its own segment of string.
