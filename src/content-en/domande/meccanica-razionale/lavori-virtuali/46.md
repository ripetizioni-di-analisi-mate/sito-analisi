A body, modeled as a material point, moves in uniform rectilinear motion with speed $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for a time interval of $4\,\mathrm{s}$. Among the forces acting on it there is the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}$, with $h = 2\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}$. What is the work of the viscous resistance over the interval?

- $-24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $72\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-72\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-36\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The viscous resistance is applied to the point that moves with velocity $\vec{v}$, therefore its power is $\Pi = -h\,\vec{v} \cdot \vec{v} = -h\,v^2$. The speed is constant, hence the power is constant as well and equals $\Pi = -2 \cdot 3^2\,\mathrm{W} = -18\,\mathrm{W}$. The elementary work is $dL = \Pi\,dt$, so
$$
L = \Pi\,\Delta t = -18\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 4\,\mathrm{s} = -72\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is negative, since the resistance is opposite to the velocity at every instant. The value $-24\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by multiplying the magnitude of the force, $6\,\mathrm{N}$, by the duration of the motion instead of by the displacement, which is $12\,\mathrm{m}$.
