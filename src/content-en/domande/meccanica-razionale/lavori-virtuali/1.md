A particle is acted upon by the constant force $\vec{F} = F\,(2\,\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath})$, with $F > 0$, and the particle moves from the position $A = (0, 0)$ to the position $B = (\ell, 2\ell)$ along the segment $AB$. What is the work of the force?

- $4\,F\,\ell$
- $0$
- $2\,F\,\ell$
- $-2\,F\,\ell$

## Explanation

The force is constant, therefore the line integral depends only on the endpoints of the path and equals $L = \vec{F} \cdot (B - A)$. The displacement of the point of application is $B - A = \ell\,\hat{\imath} + 2\ell\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence
$$
L = F\,(2\,\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath}) \cdot (\ell\,\hat{\imath} + 2\ell\,\hat{\jmath}) = F\,(2\ell - 2\ell) = 0 .
$$
The work is zero since the force and the displacement are orthogonal. The value $4\,F\,\ell$ is obtained by adding the two contributions without taking into account the minus sign of the vertical component of the force.
