Two bodies of mass $m_1$ and $m_2$, which can be treated as particles, rest on two smooth planes inclined at the angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ to the horizontal, joined at the top, and are connected by an inextensible string that passes over an ideal pulley placed at the vertex, as in the figure. Which relation holds at equilibrium?

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 4,2.4 8,0" cerchi="4,2.68,0.24" nodi="P_1,1.6,0.96,-;P_2,6.4,0.96,-;Q,4,2.68,-" catene="P_1-Q;P_2-Q" punti="1.6,0.96,m_1;6.4,0.96,m_2" etichette="0.85,0.2,α;7.15,0.2,β" titolo="Two bodies on smooth inclined planes connected by a string that passes over the pulley at the vertex"}

- $m_1\cos\alpha = m_2\cos\beta$
- $m_1\sin\alpha = m_2\sin\beta$
- $m_1\sin\beta = m_2\sin\alpha$
- $m_1 = m_2$

## Explanation

The string is inextensible, therefore the system has one degree of freedom. Take as virtual displacement the one in which the first body moves up its plane by $\delta s > 0$, so that the second body descends along its own plane by the same amount. The heights of the two bodies change by $\delta s\sin\alpha$ and by $-\delta s\sin\beta$, hence the virtual work of the active force system is
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = -m_1 g\sin\alpha\,\delta s + m_2 g\sin\beta\,\delta s .
$$
The planes are smooth and the pulley is ideal, therefore equilibrium is equivalent to $\delta L^{(a)} = 0$ for every $\delta s$, whence $m_1\sin\alpha = m_2\sin\beta$. The normal reactions of the two planes and that of the pulley do not appear, since their virtual work is zero.
