A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a plane inclined at the angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal, in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects the center $C$ to a fixed point of the inclined plane and stays parallel to the plane, as in the figure. Denoting by $s$ the distance between the center $C$ and the fixed point, measured along the plane, what is the potential of the system?

- $-m g\,s\sin\alpha - \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2$
- $-m g\,s\cos\alpha - \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2$
- $m g\,s\sin\alpha - \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2$
- $-m g\,s\sin\alpha - k\,s^2$

## Explanation

Pure rolling relates the rotation of the disk to the displacement of the center, therefore the system has one degree of freedom and the coordinate $s$ describes it. When the center moves up the plane by $s$, its height increases by $s\sin\alpha$, hence the potential of the weight is $-m g\,s\sin\alpha$. The spring has zero natural length and length $s$, therefore its potential is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2$. The potentials add up and one obtains
$$
U(s) = -m g\,s\sin\alpha - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2 .
$$
The reaction of the pure rolling constraint does not appear, since it is applied at the point of contact, whose virtual displacement is zero.
