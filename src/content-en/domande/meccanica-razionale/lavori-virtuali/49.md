A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping down a plane inclined at the angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal; at a certain instant the center has speed $v$. What is, at that instant, the power of the weight of the disk?

- $m g v\sin\alpha$
- $m g v\cos\alpha$
- $-m g v\sin\alpha$
- $m g v$

## Explanation

The weight is applied at the center of mass, which for the homogeneous disk coincides with the center $C$, therefore its power is $\Pi = -m g\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot \vec{v}_C$. The rotation of the disk does not enter the computation, since the power of a force depends only on the velocity of its point of application. The center moves parallel to the plane and goes down, hence the vertical component of its velocity is $-v\sin\alpha$ and
$$
\Pi = -m g\,(-v\sin\alpha) = m g v\sin\alpha .
$$
The power is positive, since the weight does positive work on a body that goes down. The value $m g v\cos\alpha$ is obtained by projecting the velocity onto the horizontal direction instead of the vertical one.
