- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{4\,k\,\ell}{m g}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{2\,k\,\ell}{m g}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{m g}{2\,k\,\ell}$
- $\tan\vartheta = -\dfrac{2\,k\,\ell}{m g}$

## Explanation

The midpoint of the rod is $G - A = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath})$, therefore the potential of the weight is $m g \ell\cos\vartheta$. The square of the length of the spring is $|G - C|^2 = (\ell\sin\vartheta - 2\ell)^2 + \ell^2\cos^2\vartheta = \ell^2\,(5 - 4\sin\vartheta)$, hence the potential of the spring is $2\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta$ up to an additive constant and the potential of the system is
$$
U(\vartheta) = m g \ell\cos\vartheta + 2\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta .
$$
The constraints are fixed, ideal and bilateral, therefore equilibrium is equivalent to $U'(\vartheta) = 0$, that is,
$$
-m g \ell\sin\vartheta + 2\,k\,\ell^2\cos\vartheta = 0 .
$$
Since $\cos\vartheta = 0$ does not satisfy the equation, one can divide by $\cos\vartheta$ and obtain $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{2\,k\,\ell}{m g}$, which identifies two configurations in $[0, 2\pi)$. The value $\dfrac{4\,k\,\ell}{m g}$ is obtained by attaching the spring at the end $B$ instead of at the midpoint.
