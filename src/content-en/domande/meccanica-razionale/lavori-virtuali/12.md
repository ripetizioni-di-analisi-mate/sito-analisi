Two rings $P_1$ and $P_2$, which can be treated as particles of mass $m$ each, slide without friction along a fixed vertical guide. An ideal spring of constant $k$ connects $P_1$ to the fixed point $O$ of the guide and an ideal spring of constant $2k$ connects $P_1$ to $P_2$; both have zero natural length, as in the figure. At equilibrium, what is the height $y_2$ of $P_2$, measured from $O$ upwards?

- $-\dfrac{5\,m g}{2\,k}$
- $-\dfrac{2\,m g}{k}$
- $-\dfrac{3\,m g}{k}$
- $-\dfrac{5\,m g}{k}$

## Explanation

The two springs have zero natural length and lengths $|y_1|$ and $|y_2 - y_1|$, hence the potential of the system is
$$
U(y_1, y_2) = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,y_1^2 - k\,(y_2 - y_1)^2 - m g\,y_1 - m g\,y_2 .
$$
The constraints are fixed, ideal and bilateral, therefore equilibrium is equivalent to the vanishing of the two partial derivatives and one obtains the system
$$
\begin{cases}
-k\,y_1 + 2\,k\,(y_2 - y_1) - m g = 0 \\[6pt]
-2\,k\,(y_2 - y_1) - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation it follows that $y_2 - y_1 = -\dfrac{m g}{2\,k}$, and substituting into the first one obtains $y_1 = -\dfrac{2\,m g}{k}$. Hence
$$
y_2 = -\frac{2\,m g}{k} - \frac{m g}{2\,k} = -\frac{5\,m g}{2\,k} ,
$$
below the point $O$, as the weight requires. The value $-\dfrac{3\,m g}{k}$ is obtained by assigning to the second spring the constant $k$ instead of $2k$.
