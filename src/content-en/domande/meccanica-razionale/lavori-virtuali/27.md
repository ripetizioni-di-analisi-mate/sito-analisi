A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the origin with a smooth hinge and rotates in the vertical plane; the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical is measured counterclockwise. The constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, is applied at the end $A$, as in the figure. What is the Lagrangian component $Q_\vartheta$ of the force system made of the weight and the force $\vec{F}$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.5,-2.0" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="0.75,-1,G,baricentro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.4" forze="A,0.8,0,F;0.75 -1.8,0,-0.6,mg" etichette="0.3,-0.95,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with the constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$ applied at the end $A$"}

- $F\,\ell\cos\vartheta - \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$
- $F\,\ell\cos\vartheta + \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$
- $F\,\ell\cos\vartheta - m g \ell\sin\vartheta$
- $F\,\ell\sin\vartheta - \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$

## Explanation

The positions of the points of application are $A - O = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath})$ and $G - O = \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,(\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath})$, therefore their derivatives with respect to $\vartheta$ are
$$
\frac{\partial A}{\partial \vartheta} = \ell\,(\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}) ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial G}{\partial \vartheta} = \frac{\ell}{2}\,(\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}) .
$$
The Lagrangian component is the sum of the scalar products of each force with the derivative of its own point of application, hence
$$
Q_\vartheta = F\,\hat{\imath} \cdot \frac{\partial A}{\partial \vartheta} - m g\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot \frac{\partial G}{\partial \vartheta} = F\,\ell\cos\vartheta - \frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta .
$$
The same result is obtained as the resultant moment with respect to $O$, since the rod rotates about the fixed hinge and $\delta L = M_O\,\delta\vartheta$. The contribution $m g \ell\sin\vartheta$ is obtained by applying the weight at the end $A$ instead of at the midpoint.
