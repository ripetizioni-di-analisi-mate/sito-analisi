A material point rests on a smooth horizontal plane that can only push it upward, and the reaction of the plane is $\Phi\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $\Phi \geq 0$. Consider the virtual displacement $\delta P = \delta x\,\hat{\imath} + \delta h\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $\delta h \geq 0$. What is the virtual work of the reaction?

- It equals $-\Phi\,\delta h$, which is negative or zero
- It is zero, since the reaction of a smooth support is normal to the plane
- It equals $\Phi\,\delta h$, which is positive or zero
- It equals $\Phi\,(\delta x + \delta h)$

## Explanation

The virtual work of the reaction is the dot product
$$
\Phi\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot (\delta x\,\hat{\imath} + \delta h\,\hat{\jmath}) = \Phi\,\delta h .
$$
Since $\Phi \geq 0$ and $\delta h \geq 0$, the virtual work is positive or zero, as the definition of ideal constraint requires, $\delta L^{(v)} \geq 0$. For the displacements parallel to the plane, with $\delta h = 0$, the work is zero, while for those that lift the point it is positive when the reaction is different from zero. The unilateral support admits non-reversible virtual displacements, therefore for it the ideality condition remains an inequality and does not reduce to $\delta L^{(v)} = 0$.
