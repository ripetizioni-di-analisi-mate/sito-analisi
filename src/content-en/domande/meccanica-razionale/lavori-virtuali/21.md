A particle is acted upon by the weight, by the force of an ideal spring of constant $k$ and by the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}$, with $h > 0$. Which statement holds for the overall force system?

- It admits a potential, since the weight and the elastic force are conservative
- It does not admit a potential, since the viscous resistance depends on the velocity and not on the position alone
- It admits a potential only in the equilibrium configurations
- It admits a potential if $h < k$

## Explanation

A force is conservative when it depends on the position of its point of application alone and its field is the gradient of a function. The viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}$ depends on the velocity, therefore it is not a position-dependent force and does not admit a potential. The weight and the elastic force are conservative, with potentials $-m g\,y$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,(\ell - \ell_0)^2$, but the sum of a conservative force system and a non-conservative one is not conservative. The overall force system therefore does not admit a potential, whatever the value of $h > 0$.
