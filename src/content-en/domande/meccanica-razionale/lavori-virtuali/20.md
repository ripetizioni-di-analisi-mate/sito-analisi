A particle is acted upon by a conservative force of potential $U(x, y) = c\,(x^2 - y^2)$, with $c > 0$. What is the work of the force when the particle moves from the position $A = (\ell, 0)$ to the position $B = (0, \ell)$?

- $2\,c\,\ell^2$
- $-c\,\ell^2$
- $-2\,c\,\ell^2$
- $0$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the work of a conservative force the work is $L = U(B) - U(A)$ and does not depend on the path followed. At the two endpoints the potential is $U(A) = c\,\ell^2$ and $U(B) = -c\,\ell^2$, hence
$$
L = -c\,\ell^2 - c\,\ell^2 = -2\,c\,\ell^2 .
$$
The work is negative, therefore the force opposes the displacement overall. The value $2\,c\,\ell^2$ is obtained by exchanging the order of the endpoints, that is, by computing $U(A) - U(B)$.
