Two material points $P_1$ and $P_2$ are connected by an ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length. Both points move, and their distance goes from $\ell$ to $2\ell$. What is the total work of the two forces that the spring exerts on the two points?

- $0$
- $-3\,k\,\ell^2$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$
- $-\dfrac{3}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$

## Explanation

The two elastic forces are opposite, but they are applied to points that move differently, therefore their total work is in general different from zero. By the theorem on the potential of a spring, the pair of forces is conservative with potential $U = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,d^2$, where $d = |P_1 - P_2|$ is the distance between the ends. The work therefore depends only on the initial and on the final distance, and equals
$$
L = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,(2\ell)^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2 = -\frac{3}{2}\,k\,\ell^2 .
$$
The work is negative, since the spring is stretched. The value $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$ is obtained by using the square of the change in length, $(2\ell - \ell)^2$, instead of the difference of the squares of the two lengths.
