A particle rests on a rough horizontal plane and the reaction of the plane has a tangential friction component, opposite to the displacement of the particle. Which statement holds for that constraint?

- The constraint is ideal, since the normal reaction does zero virtual work
- The constraint is not ideal, since the friction component does negative virtual work
- The constraint is ideal only for the virtual displacements orthogonal to the plane
- The constraint is not ideal, since the normal reaction does positive virtual work

## Explanation

A constraint is ideal when the reactions it can exert have virtual work $\delta L^{(v)} \geq 0$ for every virtual displacement of the system. The virtual displacements of the resting particle are parallel to the plane, therefore the normal reaction does zero virtual work, whereas the friction component is opposite to the displacement and its scalar product with $\delta P$ is negative for every nonzero virtual displacement. One therefore has $\delta L^{(v)} < 0$ and the condition of ideality is not satisfied, hence the principle of virtual work does not apply. The equilibrium of a body resting with friction is studied with the cardinal equations of statics and with the Coulomb-Morin law.
