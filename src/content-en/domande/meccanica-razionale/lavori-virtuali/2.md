A particle $P$ is connected to the origin $O$ by an ideal spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero natural length, and moves from the position $P_1 = (2\ell, 0)$ to the position $P_2 = (\ell, \ell)$ along an arbitrary path. What is the work of the elastic force?

- $k\,\ell^2$
- $-k\,\ell^2$
- $2\,k\,\ell^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$

## Explanation

The spring has zero natural length and one end at the fixed point $O$, therefore the elastic force is conservative with potential $U = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2$, where $r$ is the distance of $P$ from the origin. In the two positions one has $r_1 = 2\ell$ and $r_2 = \sqrt{\ell^2 + \ell^2} = \ell\sqrt{2}$, hence by the theorem on the work of a conservative force
$$
L = U(P_2) - U(P_1) = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,(2\ell^2) + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,(4\ell^2) = k\,\ell^2 .
$$
The work is positive since the particle moves closer to the origin and the elastic force pulls back towards $O$. The value $-k\,\ell^2$ is obtained by exchanging the potential with the potential energy $V = -U$, whereas $2\,k\,\ell^2$ comes from having neglected the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
