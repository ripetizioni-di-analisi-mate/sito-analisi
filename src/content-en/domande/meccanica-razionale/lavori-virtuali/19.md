A particle in the plane is acted upon by the position-dependent force $\vec{F} = c\,\big( 2\,x\,y\,\hat{\imath} + \alpha\,x^2\,\hat{\jmath} \big)$, with $c > 0$ and $\alpha$ constant. For which value of $\alpha$ is the force conservative?

- $\alpha = 1$
- $\alpha = 2$
- $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha = -1$

## Explanation

The components of the force are of class $C^1$ in the plane, which is simply connected, therefore the force is conservative if and only if the closedness condition $\dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = \dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial x}$ holds. The two derivatives are
$$
\frac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = 2\,c\,x ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial F_y}{\partial x} = 2\,\alpha\,c\,x ,
$$
hence the equality is satisfied for every $x$ if and only if $\alpha = 1$. With this value the force admits the potential $U = c\,x^2\,y$, as one verifies by computing its gradient.
