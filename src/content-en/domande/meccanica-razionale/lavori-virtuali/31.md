A homogeneous disk of radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide. The center $C$ undergoes the virtual displacement $\delta C = \delta x\,\hat{\imath}$, with $\delta x > 0$. What is the virtual displacement of the point $E$ of the rim diametrically opposite the point of contact?

::struttura{cerchi="2.5,0.9,0.9" linee="-0.2,0 5,0" punti="2.5,0.9,C,baricentro;2.5,0,H,centro;2.5,1.8,E" frecce="2.5,0.9,3.3,0.9,δx" titolo="Disk rolling without slipping: the point of contact $H$ is the instantaneous center of rotation"}

- $\delta x\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\delta x\,\hat{\imath}$
- $2\,\delta x\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\vec{0}$

## Explanation

In pure rolling the point of contact $H$ has zero velocity, therefore it is the instantaneous center of rotation of the disk and its virtual displacement is zero. The virtual displacements of the points of a plane rigid body are then $\delta P = \delta\vec{\vartheta} \wedge (P - H)$, hence their magnitude is proportional to the distance from $H$. The center is at distance $R$ from $H$ and the point $E$ at distance $2R$, therefore
$$
|\delta E| = 2\,|\delta C| = 2\,\delta x ,
$$
and the two displacements have the same direction, because $C$ and $E$ lie on the same side with respect to $H$. Hence $\delta E = 2\,\delta x\,\hat{\imath}$.
