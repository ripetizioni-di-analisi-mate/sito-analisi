A block of mass $m$, modeled as a material point, rests on a smooth horizontal plane and is connected to a wall by a horizontal ideal spring of constant $k$. On the opposite side an inextensible string, horizontal on the plane, passes over an ideal pulley placed at the edge and holds a hanging body of mass $M$, as in the figure. By how much is the spring stretched with respect to its natural length, at equilibrium?

::struttura{linee="0,0 4,0;4,0 4,-2;0,0 0,1.2;0.00,0.35 0.18,0.35 0.28,0.50 0.49,0.20 0.70,0.50 0.90,0.20 1.11,0.50 1.32,0.20 1.42,0.35 1.60,0.35;2.4,0.35 4.15,0.35;4.4,0.1 4.4,-1.2" cerchi="4.15,0.1,0.25" poligoni="1.6,0 2.4,0 2.4,0.7 1.6,0.7;4.1,-1.2 4.7,-1.2 4.7,-1.8 4.1,-1.8" etichette="2,0.95,m;5,-1.5,M;0.8,0.8,k" titolo="Block on a smooth plane, with the spring toward the wall and the string holding the hanging body"}

- $\dfrac{(M + m)\,g}{k}$
- $\dfrac{M g}{2\,k}$
- $\dfrac{M g}{k}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{k}$

## Explanation

The string is inextensible, hence the system has one degree of freedom: if the block moves by $\delta x$ toward the pulley, the hanging body goes down by $\delta x$ and the spring is stretched by $\delta x$. The weight of the block is orthogonal to the plane and does no work, while the weight of the hanging body does the work $M g\,\delta x$. Denoting by $\Delta$ the elongation of the spring, the elastic force on the block has magnitude $k\,\Delta$ and is directed toward the wall, therefore
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = M g\,\delta x - k\,\Delta\,\delta x .
$$
The plane, the string and the pulley are ideal constraints, so equilibrium is equivalent to $\delta L^{(a)} = 0$, which gives $\Delta = \dfrac{M g}{k}$. The mass of the block does not appear, since the block moves horizontally.
