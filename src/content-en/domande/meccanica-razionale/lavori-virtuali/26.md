A homogeneous rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ rotates in a horizontal plane about a fixed hinge placed at one of its ends, and $\vartheta$ is the angle of rotation. What is the potential of the weight of the rod?

- It equals $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$
- It equals $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$
- It equals $-m g \ell\cos\vartheta$
- It is constant, since the height of the center of mass does not change

## Explanation

The potential of the weight of a body of mass $m$ is $U = -m g\,y_G$, where $y_G$ is the height of the center of mass. The rod moves in a horizontal plane, therefore its center of mass always stays at the same height and $y_G$ does not depend on $\vartheta$. Hence the potential is constant and can be discarded without consequences, since the work and the equilibrium configurations depend only on the derivatives of $U$. The weight of the rod, in this motion, does zero work and does not contribute to the Lagrangian component $Q_\vartheta$.
