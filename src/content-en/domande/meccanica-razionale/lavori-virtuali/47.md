A homogeneous chain of mass $m$, length $\ell$ and negligible thickness hangs vertically from the edge of a horizontal table, with its upper end at the height of the table top. The chain is pulled onto the table until it lies entirely on the top. What is the work done by the weight of the chain?

::struttura{nodi="E,3.1,0,-;F,3.1,-2,-" catene="E-F" poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,-0.2 0,-0.2" linee="0.3,-0.2 0.3,-2.3;2.4,-0.2 2.4,-2.3" quote="E,F,ℓ,-0.6" titolo="Chain of length $\ell$ hanging vertically from the edge of the table"}

- $-m g \ell$
- $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$
- $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The weight of a material system has potential $U = -m g\,y_G$, where $y_G$ is the height of the center of mass, also when the system deforms like the chain, since the theorem is obtained by adding the potentials of the weights of the single elements. Heights are measured from the table top. At the beginning the chain is a vertical segment between the heights $0$ and $-\ell$, hence its center of mass is the midpoint, at the height $-\dfrac{\ell}{2}$; at the end the chain lies on the top and the center of mass has height $0$. By the theorem on the work of a conservative force one therefore obtains
$$
L = U_2 - U_1 = 0 - m g\,\frac{\ell}{2} = -\frac{m g \ell}{2} .
$$
The work is negative, since the center of mass goes up. The value $-m g \ell$ is obtained by assuming that the whole mass rises by $\ell$, as if it were concentrated at the lower end, while the value $0$ considers only the horizontal sliding on the top and neglects the rise of the hanging segment.
