The positional force $\vec{F} = c\,x\,\hat{\imath}$, with constant $c > 0$, acts on a point particle of the plane. The particle moves along a segment from the position $A = (\ell, 0)$ to the position $B = (2\ell, 3\ell)$. What is the work of the force?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,c\,\ell^2$
- $2\,c\,\ell^2$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,c\,\ell^2$
- $6\,c\,\ell^2$

## Explanation

The force has only the component along $\hat{\imath}$ and this depends only on $x$, hence it is conservative with potential $U(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,c\,x^2$, since $\operatorname{grad} U = c\,x\,\hat{\imath}$. The work of a conservative force is the difference of the values of the potential between the final and the initial position, therefore
$$
L = U(B) - U(A) = \frac{1}{2}\,c\,\big( 4\ell^2 - \ell^2 \big) = \frac{3}{2}\,c\,\ell^2 .
$$
The vertical displacement of $3\ell$ gives no contribution, because the force is orthogonal to $\hat{\jmath}$. The value $2\,c\,\ell^2$ is obtained by multiplying the displacement by the force at the final position only, as if the force were constant, and $6\,c\,\ell^2$ by using the potential $\dfrac{1}{2}\,c\,(x^2 + y^2)$ of the elastic force.
