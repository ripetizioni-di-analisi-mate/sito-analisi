A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged to the ground at its lower end $O$ and rotates in the vertical plane; $\vartheta$ is the angle between the rod and the upward vertical. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $A$ to a ring that slides without friction along the vertical through $O$, so that the spring stays horizontal, as in the figure. For which values of $k$ does the system admit equilibrium configurations with the rod inclined, that is, with $\sin\vartheta \neq 0$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.15,2.11" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera" linee="0,-0.1 0,2.8;0.00,2.11 0.15,2.11 0.23,2.24 0.41,1.98 0.57,2.24 0.74,1.98 0.91,2.24 1.00,2.11 1.15,2.11" punti="0,2.11,,pieno;0.58,1.06,G,baricentro" forze="0.58 0.26,0,-0.6,mg" etichette="0.22,0.75,ϑ;0.58,2.55,k" titolo="Rod hinged at the bottom, with a spring that stays horizontal between $A$ and the vertical through $O$"}

- $k > \dfrac{m g}{2\,\ell}$
- $k > \dfrac{m g}{\ell}$
- $k < \dfrac{m g}{2\,\ell}$
- $k > \dfrac{2\,m g}{\ell}$

## Explanation

The center of mass is at height $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$ and the spring has length $\ell\,|\sin\vartheta|$, therefore the potential is
$$
U(\vartheta) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2\sin^2\vartheta .
$$
The constraints are fixed, ideal and bilateral, hence equilibrium is equivalent to
$$
U'(\vartheta) = \sin\vartheta \left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - k\,\ell^2\cos\vartheta \right) = 0 .
$$
With $\sin\vartheta \neq 0$ the second factor must vanish, that is, $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{m g}{2\,k\,\ell}$, and this equation has solutions with $\sin\vartheta \neq 0$ if and only if $\dfrac{m g}{2\,k\,\ell} < 1$, that is, $k > \dfrac{m g}{2\,\ell}$. In these configurations the moment of the weight about $O$, which tends to knock the rod down, is balanced by the moment of the elastic force.
