A disk rolls without slipping on a fixed guide. For what reason does the reaction of the pure rolling constraint do zero virtual work?

- Because it is orthogonal to the guide
- Because the disk is homogeneous
- Because its line of action passes through the center of the disk
- Because it is applied at the point of contact, whose virtual displacement is zero

## Explanation

In pure rolling the point of contact between disk and guide has zero velocity, therefore its virtual displacement is the zero vector. The reaction of the constraint is applied precisely at that point, hence its virtual work $\vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta P$ is zero whatever the direction of $\vec{\Phi}$. The pure rolling constraint is therefore ideal and its reactions do not appear in the equilibrium equations written with the principle of virtual work. The reaction in general also has a tangential component, which is the static friction force, therefore the reason is not orthogonality to the guide.
