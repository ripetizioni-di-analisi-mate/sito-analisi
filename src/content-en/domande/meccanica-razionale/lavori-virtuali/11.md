- $x = \dfrac{F}{k}$
- $x = \dfrac{F}{2\,k}$
- $x = \dfrac{2\,F}{k}$
- $x = \dfrac{4\,F}{k}$

## Explanation

The center $C$ moves along a horizontal line, therefore the weight of the disk has constant potential and can be discarded. The spring has one end at the fixed point $O$, placed at the same height as $C$, hence its length is $x$ and its potential is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$. The point of contact is the instantaneous center of rotation, therefore the point $E$ is at distance $2R$ from it and its virtual displacement is twice that of the center. The Lagrangian component of the constant force $F\,\hat{\imath}$ applied at $E$ is hence $2\,F$, to which the potential $2\,F\,x$ corresponds, and
$$
U(x) = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 + 2\,F\,x .
$$
Equilibrium is equivalent to $U'(x) = 0$, that is, $-k\,x + 2\,F = 0$, whence $x = \dfrac{2\,F}{k}$. The value $\dfrac{F}{k}$ is obtained by applying the force at the center instead of at the highest point of the rim.
