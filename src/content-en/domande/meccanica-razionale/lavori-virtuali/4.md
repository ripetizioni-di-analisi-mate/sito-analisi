A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, is hinged at the origin with a smooth hinge and moves in the vertical plane. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects the end $A$ to the fixed point $C = (2\ell, 0)$, as in the figure. Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise, what is the potential of the system, up to an additive constant?

- $m g \ell\cos\vartheta + 4\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta$
- $m g \ell\cos\vartheta - 4\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta$
- $-m g \ell\cos\vartheta + 4\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta$
- $2\,m g \ell\cos\vartheta + 4\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta$

## Explanation

The end of the rod is $A - O = 2\ell\,(\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath})$ and the center of mass is the midpoint, therefore $y_G = -\ell\cos\vartheta$ and the potential of the weight is $U_1 = -m g\,y_G = m g \ell\cos\vartheta$. The square of the length of the spring is
$$
|A - C|^2 = (2\ell\sin\vartheta - 2\ell)^2 + 4\ell^2\cos^2\vartheta = 8\ell^2\,(1 - \sin\vartheta) ,
$$
hence the potential of the spring is $U_2 = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,|A - C|^2 = -4\,k\,\ell^2 + 4\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta$. The potential of the system is the sum of the two potentials and, discarding the additive constant, one obtains $U(\vartheta) = m g \ell\cos\vartheta + 4\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta$. The term $2\,m g \ell\cos\vartheta$ is obtained by mistakenly placing the center of mass at the end $A$ instead of at the midpoint.
