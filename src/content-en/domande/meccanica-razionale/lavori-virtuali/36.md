A rigid rod $AB$, of negligible mass and length $3\ell$, is hinged at the point $O$ at distance $\ell$ from $A$ and moves in the vertical plane, as in the figure. A particle of mass $m$ is attached at the end $A$. Which mass $M$, attached at the end $B$, puts the system in equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;O,1.5,0;B,4.5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="O,cerniera" forze="0 -1.2,0,-0.6,mg;4.5 -1.2,0,-0.6,Mg" quote="A,O,ℓ,1;O,B,2 ℓ,1" titolo="Rod of negligible mass hinged at $O$, with the two particles at the ends"}

- $M = 2\,m$
- $M = m$
- $M = \dfrac{m}{2}$
- $M = \dfrac{m}{3}$

## Explanation

The rod has negligible mass, therefore the active force system is made of the weights of the two particles. In the virtual rotation $\delta\varphi$ about the hinge $O$ the two ends lie on opposite sides, hence, in the horizontal configuration, $\delta y_A = -\ell\,\delta\varphi$ and $\delta y_B = 2\ell\,\delta\varphi$. The virtual work of the active force system is then
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = m g\,\ell\,\delta\varphi - M g\,2\ell\,\delta\varphi .
$$
The hinge is ideal and bilateral, therefore equilibrium is equivalent to $\delta L^{(a)} = 0$ for every $\delta\varphi$, whence $m = 2\,M$, that is, $M = \dfrac{m}{2}$. The condition is that of the equilibrium of a lever, in which the moments of the two weights with respect to $O$ are opposite.
