In the differential pulley in the figure the upper block consists of two coaxial pulleys fixed to each other, of radii $R$ and $r < R$, which rotate about a fixed axis. A closed loop chain goes down from the left side of the large pulley to the movable pulley that holds the load of weight $W$, passes under it and goes back up to the right side of the small pulley; from the right side of the large pulley hangs instead the segment that the operator pulls downward with the force $F$, and the rest of the chain hangs slack. Chain and pulleys have negligible mass and the constraints are ideal. Which force $F$ keeps the load in equilibrium?

- $\dfrac{W\,(R - r)}{R}$
- $\dfrac{W\,r}{R}$
- $\dfrac{W\,(R - r)}{2\,R}$
- $\dfrac{W\,(R + r)}{2\,R}$

## Explanation

The system has one degree of freedom, and as virtual displacement one takes the rotation $\delta\varphi > 0$ of the upper block that winds the chain on the left side of the large pulley. The left segment then shortens by $R\,\delta\varphi$, while the segment hanging from the right side of the small pulley lengthens by $r\,\delta\varphi$, therefore the movable pulley, held by the two segments, goes up by $\dfrac{(R - r)\,\delta\varphi}{2}$. In the same rotation the segment pulled by the operator goes down by $R\,\delta\varphi$, hence
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = F\,R\,\delta\varphi - W\,\frac{R - r}{2}\,\delta\varphi .
$$
Equilibrium is equivalent to $\delta L^{(a)} = 0$, hence $F = \dfrac{W\,(R - r)}{2\,R}$. With $R$ and $r$ close to each other the required force is a small fraction of the load, and for this reason the differential pulley is used to lift heavy loads by hand.
