A holonomic system with fixed, ideal and bilateral constraints has one degree of freedom and generalized coordinate $\vartheta$; the active force system is conservative and the potential $U(\vartheta)$ is constant on the interval $(\vartheta_1, \vartheta_2)$. What holds for the configurations of that interval?

- None is an equilibrium configuration, since the potential has no maximum points
- Only the endpoints of the interval are equilibrium configurations
- They are equilibrium configurations only if the active force system is zero
- They are all equilibrium configurations, since the derivative of the potential is zero

## Explanation

By the theorem on equilibrium and the stationarity of the potential a configuration is an equilibrium configuration if and only if it is a stationary point of $U$. The potential is constant on $(\vartheta_1, \vartheta_2)$, therefore $U'(\vartheta) = 0$ for every $\vartheta$ of that interval and every configuration of the interval is an equilibrium configuration. A constant potential does not require the active force system to be zero, since the contributions of the single forces can balance each other, as happens in the hinged rod with a spring in which the moment of the elastic force with respect to the hinge balances the moment of the weight in every configuration.
