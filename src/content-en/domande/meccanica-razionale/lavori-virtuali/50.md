The positional force
$$
\vec{F} = \frac{c}{x^2 + y^2}\,\big( -y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath} \big) ,
$$
with $c > 0$ constant, acts on a material point of the plane with the origin removed, and satisfies the closedness condition $\dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = \dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial x}$. What is the work of the force along the circle of center $O$ and radius $\ell$, traveled once counterclockwise?

- $0$
- $2\pi\,c\,\ell$
- $2\pi\,c$
- $\pi\,c$

## Explanation

The circle is parametrized by $P(u) = \ell\,(\cos u\,\hat{\imath} + \sin u\,\hat{\jmath})$ for $u \in [0, 2\pi]$, hence $\dfrac{dP}{du} = \ell\,(-\sin u\,\hat{\imath} + \cos u\,\hat{\jmath})$. On the curve $x^2 + y^2 = \ell^2$, therefore the force equals $\dfrac{c}{\ell}\,(-\sin u\,\hat{\imath} + \cos u\,\hat{\jmath})$ and the integrand is $\vec{F} \cdot \dfrac{dP}{du} = c$. Integrating over $[0, 2\pi]$ one obtains
$$
L = 2\pi\,c ,
$$
independent of the radius. The work along a closed curve is different from zero, hence the force **is not conservative** in the plane with the origin removed. The closedness condition is satisfied, but the domain is not simply connected, therefore the condition does not guarantee the existence of a potential, and the value $0$ is obtained precisely by regarding it as sufficient.
