A particle $P$ is acted upon by the central force $\vec{F} = -\dfrac{c}{r^3}\,\hat{u}$, where $O$ is a fixed point, $r = |P - O|$, $\hat{u} = \dfrac{P - O}{r}$ and $c > 0$. Which function is a potential of the force?

- $U = -\dfrac{c}{2\,r^2}$
- $U = \dfrac{c}{2\,r^2}$
- $U = \dfrac{c}{r^2}$
- $U = -\dfrac{c}{3\,r^3}$

## Explanation

A central force of the form $\vec{F} = f(r)\,\hat{u}$ is conservative and admits as potential an antiderivative of $f$, since $\operatorname{grad} U(r) = U'(r)\,\hat{u}$. In the given case $f(r) = -\dfrac{c}{r^3}$, therefore the function $U$ with
$$
U'(r) = -\frac{c}{r^3}
$$
is required. The function $U = \dfrac{c}{2\,r^2}$ has derivative $-\dfrac{c}{r^3}$, hence it satisfies the condition and is a potential of the force. The function $\dfrac{c}{r^2}$ has derivative $-\dfrac{2\,c}{r^3}$, therefore it corresponds to a force of twice the magnitude.
