The rigid arch in the figure is a semicircle with center $O$ and radius $r$, formed by the points of coordinates $(r\cos\vartheta,\ r\sin\vartheta)$ with $0 \leq \vartheta \leq \pi$. The arch is acted on by a uniform pressure, that is a distributed load directed at every point along the outward normal $\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$, of constant intensity $p$ per unit length of the arch, with $p > 0$. What is the magnitude of the resultant of the load?

## Explanation

The element of arch of amplitude $d\vartheta$ has length $r\,d\vartheta$ and carries the force $p\,r\,(\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath})\,d\vartheta$. The resultant is the sum of these elementary forces, hence
$$
\vec{R} = p\,r \int_0^\pi (\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath})\,d\vartheta = p\,r\,\big[ \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big]_0^\pi = 2\,p\,r\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The horizontal components of the forces at points symmetric with respect to the $y$ axis are opposite, therefore they cancel, and the magnitude of the resultant equals $2\,p\,r$, that is the product of $p$ and the length $2r$ of the projection of the arch on the $x$ axis. All the lines of action pass through $O$, hence the resultant moment about $O$ vanishes and the line of action of the resultant passes through $O$.

The value $\pi\,p\,r$ is the sum of the magnitudes of the elementary forces, which coincides with the magnitude of the resultant only for parallel forces with the same sense.
