In the plane $Oxy$ the vector $\vec{F} = F\,(\hat{\imath} + 2\hat{\jmath})$ is applied at the point $P$ of coordinates $(3a, a)$, with $a > 0$ and $F > 0$. To compute the moment about $O$ with Varignon's theorem, $\vec{F}$ is decomposed into the components $F\,\hat{\imath}$ and $2F\,\hat{\jmath}$. Which choice of the points of application of the components gives the correct moment?

- Both at the point of coordinates $(3a, 0)$
- The component $F\,\hat{\imath}$ at $P$ and the component $2F\,\hat{\jmath}$ at $O$
- The component $F\,\hat{\imath}$ at the point of coordinates $(3a, 0)$ and the component $2F\,\hat{\jmath}$ at the point of coordinates $(0, a)$
- Both at the point of coordinates $\left( \dfrac{5a}{2},\ 0 \right)$

## Explanation

Varignon's theorem requires the components to be applied at the same point of the line of action of $\vec{F}$, which passes through $P$ and is parallel to $\hat{\imath} + 2\hat{\jmath}$. The points of that line have coordinates $(3a + t,\ a + 2t)$, and for $t = -\dfrac{a}{2}$ one obtains the point $\left( \dfrac{5a}{2},\ 0 \right)$, which therefore belongs to the line of action.

At that point the horizontal component has its line of action on the $x$ axis and zero moment, therefore
$$
M_O = \frac{5a}{2} \cdot 2F = 5\,a F ,
$$
equal to the direct value $3a \cdot 2F - a \cdot F = 5\,a F$.

The other choices move the components off their respective lines of action: with both at $(3a, 0)$ one obtains $6\,a F$, and with $F\,\hat{\imath}$ at $P$ and $2F\,\hat{\jmath}$ at $O$ one obtains $-\,a F$.
