The rigid rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $2\ell$ and placed along the $x$ axis with $A$ at the origin, is subject to the vector $-F\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $B$ and to a couple of moment $\mathcal{M}\,\hat{k}$ applied at $A$, with $F > 0$. Which statement is true if the couple is applied at $B$ instead of $A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" forze="B,0,-1.2,F" coppie="A,1,ℳ" quote="A,B,2ℓ,0.8" titolo="The rod with the vector applied at $B$ and the couple applied at $A$"}

- The new system is equivalent to the previous one
- The resultant moment about $A$ changes
- The line of action of the resultant shifts by $2\ell$
- The new system is equivalent to a single couple

## Explanation

A couple has zero resultant and the same moment about every pole, hence its contribution to $\vec{R}$ and to $\vec{M}_A$ does not depend on the point where it is applied.

The new system therefore has the same resultant $-F\,\hat{\jmath}$ and the same moment $M_A = -\,2F\ell + \mathcal{M}$ as the previous one, hence the two systems are **equivalent**. Consequently the line of action of the resultant also stays the same, the vertical line of abscissa
$$
x = \frac{M_A}{R_y} = 2\ell - \frac{\mathcal{M}}{F} .
$$
The resultant remains different from the zero vector, therefore the system does not reduce to a couple.
