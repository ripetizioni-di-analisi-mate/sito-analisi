A couple is formed by the applied vectors $(A, F\,\hat{\imath})$ and $(B, -F\,\hat{\imath})$ with $B - A = 2a\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $a > 0$ and $F > 0$. Which of the following couples, formed by the applied vectors $(C, \vec{w})$ and $(D, -\vec{w})$, is equivalent to it?

- The couple with $\vec{w} = 2F\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $D - C = a\,\hat{\imath}$
- The couple with $\vec{w} = 2F\,\hat{k}$ and $D - C = a\,\hat{\imath}$
- The couple with $\vec{w} = F\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $D - C = -\,a\,\hat{\imath}$
- The couple with $\vec{w} = 2F\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $D - C = -\,a\,\hat{\imath}$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the moment of a couple two couples are equivalent if and only if they have the same moment, whatever their arms and intensities. The given couple has moment
$$
\vec{M} = (A - B) \wedge F\,\hat{\imath} = -\,2a\,\hat{\jmath} \wedge F\,\hat{\imath} = 2\,a F\,\hat{k} .
$$
For $\vec{w} = 2F\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $D - C = -\,a\,\hat{\imath}$ one obtains
$$
(C - D) \wedge \vec{w} = a\,\hat{\imath} \wedge 2F\,\hat{\jmath} = 2\,a F\,\hat{k} ,
$$
equal to the given moment, because the arm is halved and the intensity is doubled.

With the same $\vec{w}$ and $D - C = a\,\hat{\imath}$ the moment reverses its sense and equals $-\,2\,a F\,\hat{k}$. The couple with $\vec{w} = 2F\,\hat{k}$ has moment $2\,a F\,\hat{\jmath}$, of the same magnitude but of a different direction, because its lines of action lie in a plane parallel to $Oxz$. The couple with $\vec{w} = F\,\hat{\jmath}$ has moment $a F\,\hat{k}$, of half the magnitude.
