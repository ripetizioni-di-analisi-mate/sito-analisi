A two-blade propeller rotates counterclockwise about the $z$ axis, with the hub at the origin $O$, and at the instant considered the blades are directed along the $x$ axis. At the points $P_1$ and $P_2$ of the blades, with $P_1 - O = r\,\hat{\imath}$ and $P_2 - O = -\,r\,\hat{\imath}$, act the air thrust $T\,\hat{k}$ and the air drag, opposite to the velocity of the blades, which equals $-D\,\hat{\jmath}$ at $P_1$ and $D\,\hat{\jmath}$ at $P_2$, with $r > 0$, $T > 0$ and $D > 0$. What is the resultant moment of the four forces about $O$?

- $\vec{0}$
- $-\,2\,r D\,\hat{k}$
- $2\,r D\,\hat{k}$
- $-\,2\,r D\,\hat{k} - 2\,r T\,\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

The moment about $O$ is the sum of the moments of the four forces. Since $\hat{\imath} \wedge \hat{k} = -\,\hat{\jmath}$, the two thrusts give
$$
r\,\hat{\imath} \wedge T\,\hat{k} = -\,r T\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
-\,r\,\hat{\imath} \wedge T\,\hat{k} = r T\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
which cancel, because the thrusts are equal vectors applied at points symmetric with respect to $O$. Since $\hat{\imath} \wedge \hat{\jmath} = \hat{k}$, the two drag forces give
$$
r\,\hat{\imath} \wedge (-D\,\hat{\jmath}) = -\,r D\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
-\,r\,\hat{\imath} \wedge D\,\hat{\jmath} = -\,r D\,\hat{k} ,
$$
which add up. Therefore $\vec{M}_O = -\,2\,r D\,\hat{k}$.

The two drag forces are opposite and have parallel and distinct lines of action, hence they form a couple, whose moment is opposite to the sense of rotation of the propeller. The zero vector is obtained by confusing the zero resultant of the two drag forces with a zero moment, and the term $-\,2\,r T\,\hat{\jmath}$ assigns the same moment to the two thrusts.
