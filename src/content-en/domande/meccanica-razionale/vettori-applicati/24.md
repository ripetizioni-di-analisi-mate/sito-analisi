A system of applied vectors has all its points of application in the plane $Oxy$ and all its vectors parallel to that plane, with resultant $\vec{R} \neq \vec{0}$. Which statement is true?

- The scalar invariant vanishes, therefore the system is equivalent to a single applied vector
- The scalar invariant vanishes, therefore the system is balanced
- The scalar invariant differs from zero whenever $\vec{M}_O \neq \vec{0}$
- The system is equivalent to a couple

## Explanation

In a planar system the resultant is parallel to the plane $Oxy$, whereas every moment $(P_i - O) \wedge \vec{v}_i$ is orthogonal to that plane, therefore $\vec{M}_O$ is parallel to $\hat{k}$.

The two vectors $\vec{R}$ and $\vec{M}_O$ are therefore orthogonal and the scalar invariant $I = \vec{R} \cdot \vec{M}_O$ vanishes. By the **reduction theorem**, since $\vec{R} \neq \vec{0}$ and $I = 0$, the system is equivalent to the single applied vector $(Q, \vec{R})$ with $Q$ on the central axis, which in the plane is the line of action of the resultant.

A vanishing invariant does not make the system balanced, since equilibrium requires $\vec{R} = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{M}_O = \vec{0}$.
