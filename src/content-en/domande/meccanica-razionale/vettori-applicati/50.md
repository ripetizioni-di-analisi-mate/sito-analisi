The applied vector $(O, F\,\hat{k})$ has its point of application at the origin, with $F > 0$, and $a > 0$ is a given length. Which set is formed by the poles $Q$ about which the moment of the vector has magnitude $2\,a F$?

- The spherical surface with center $O$ and radius $2a$
- The cylindrical surface with axis $z$ and radius $2a$
- The circle of the plane $Oxy$ with center $O$ and radius $2a$
- The two planes of equations $z = 2a$ and $z = -\,2a$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the moment and the arm the magnitude of the moment about the pole $Q$ is the product of $F$ and the distance of $Q$ from the line of action, which is the $z$ axis. The moment therefore has magnitude $2\,a F$ if and only if $Q$ is at distance $2a$ from the $z$ axis, that is if and only if $Q$ belongs to the cylindrical surface with axis $z$ and radius $2a$.

In components, with $Q - O = x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath} + z\,\hat{k}$, one obtains
$$
\vec{M}_Q = (O - Q) \wedge F\,\hat{k} = F\,(-\,y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath}) ,
\qquad
|\vec{M}_Q| = F\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} ,
$$
which does not depend on the height $z$ of the pole.

The spherical surface uses as arm the distance of $Q$ from $O$ instead of from the line of action, and the two planes use the height of the pole. The circle of the plane $Oxy$ contains only part of the required poles.
