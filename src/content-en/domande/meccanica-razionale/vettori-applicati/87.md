For which of the following systems does there exist a single applied vector that, added to the system, makes it balanced?

- Three applied vectors with lines of action concurrent at one point and nonzero resultant
- A couple of nonzero moment
- Two nonzero applied vectors with skew lines of action
- A nonzero applied vector and a couple of nonzero moment parallel to the vector

## Explanation

A single vector $(Q, -\vec{R})$ balances a system of resultant $\vec{R}$ and moment $\vec{M}_O$ if and only if $\vec{M}_O = (Q - O) \wedge \vec{R}$, and this equality requires $\vec{R} \neq \vec{0}$ and $I = \vec{R} \cdot \vec{M}_O = 0$.

For three vectors concurrent at $K$ **Varignon's theorem** gives $\vec{M}_O = (K - O) \wedge \vec{R}$, hence the vector $-\vec{R}$ applied at $K$ is enough. A couple has zero resultant, and an added nonzero vector would make the resultant different from the zero vector.

For two vectors with skew lines the invariant is the scalar triple product $\vec{v}_1 \cdot (P_2 - P_1) \wedge \vec{v}_2$, different from zero because the three vectors are not coplanar; for a vector with a parallel couple $I = \vec{R} \cdot \vec{M} \neq 0$. In these two cases equilibrium also requires a couple.
