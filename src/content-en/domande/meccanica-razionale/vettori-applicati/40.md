Four applied vectors, parallel to one and the same unit vector and with all their coefficients positive, are applied at the vertices of the square with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(2a, 0)$, $(2a, 2a)$ and $(0, 2a)$, with $a > 0$. Which statement about the center of the system is true?

- It belongs to the square, whatever the values of the coefficients
- It coincides with the center of the square, whatever the values of the coefficients
- It also depends on the common direction of the four vectors
- It falls outside the square when one coefficient is much greater than the other three

## Explanation

By the theorem on the center of a system of parallel vectors the center is the average of the four vertices weighted with the coefficients, and the formula does not contain the common unit vector, therefore the center does not depend on the direction of the vectors.

Since all the coefficients are positive, the weights $\dfrac{c_i}{c_1 + c_2 + c_3 + c_4}$ are positive and add up to $1$, therefore the center is a convex combination of the four vertices and belongs to the square.

The center coincides with the center of the square only when the coefficients are chosen symmetrically, for example all equal; with different coefficients the center moves toward the vertices of greater coefficient, while staying inside the square.
