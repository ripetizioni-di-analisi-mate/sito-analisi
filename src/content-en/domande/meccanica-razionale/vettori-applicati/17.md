The applied vector $(P, \vec{v})$ has $P - O = a\,(2\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ and $\vec{v} = v\,(\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath})$, with $a > 0$ and $v > 0$. About which of the following poles is the moment zero?

- The point of coordinates $(3a, 0)$
- The origin $O$
- The point of coordinates $(a, a)$
- The point of coordinates $(2a, 2a)$

## Explanation

By the **theorem on the moment and the arm** the moment about a pole vanishes if and only if the pole belongs to the line of action.

The line of action passes through the point of coordinates $(2a, a)$ and is parallel to $\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath}$, therefore it has equation $x + y = 3a$. Among the four proposed poles only the point of coordinates $(3a, 0)$ satisfies this equation.

The direct check confirms the result, since for that pole one has $P - O' = -\,a\,\hat{\imath} + a\,\hat{\jmath}$ and therefore $M = (-a)(-v) - a \cdot v = 0$.
