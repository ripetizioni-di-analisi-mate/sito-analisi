On a rigid body the two applied vectors $(A, F\,\hat{\jmath})$ and $(B, -F\,\hat{\jmath})$ with $B - A = 3a\,\hat{\imath}$ act, with $a > 0$ and $F > 0$. What is the moment of the system about an arbitrary pole?

- $\vec{0}$
- $3\,a F\,\hat{k}$
- $-\,3\,a F\,\hat{k}$
- $-\,6\,a F\,\hat{k}$

## Explanation

The two vectors are opposite and have parallel, distinct lines of action, therefore they form a **couple** of arm $d = 3a$.

By the theorem on the moment of a couple the moment is the same about every pole and equals
$$
\vec{M} = (A - B) \wedge F\,\hat{\jmath} = -\,3a\,\hat{\imath} \wedge F\,\hat{\jmath} = -\,3\,a F\,\hat{k} ,
$$
therefore the couple is clockwise and has magnitude $F d = 3\,a F$. The resultant vanishes whereas the resultant moment differs from the zero vector, hence the system is not balanced and is equivalent to the couple alone.
