The rigid rod $OA$, of length $\ell$, lies along the $x$ axis and is hinged at the end $O$, which coincides with the origin; $M$ is the midpoint of the rod and $F > 0$. Which of the following forces tends to rotate the rod clockwise about $O$?

- $F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ applied at $A$
- $F\,(\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath})$ applied at $M$
- $-F\,\hat{\imath}$ applied at $A$
- $F\,(-\,\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ applied at $M$

## Explanation

The sense of rotation is given by the sign of $M_O = x\,v_y - y\,v_x$, which is negative for a clockwise rotation. The points $A$ and $M$ lie on the $x$ axis, hence $y = 0$ and the moment reduces to $M_O = x\,v_y$; therefore only the vertical component of the force matters.

The force $F\,(\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath})$ applied at $M$ gives
$$
M_O = \frac{\ell}{2} \cdot (-F) = -\,\frac{F\ell}{2} ,
$$
hence it tends to produce a clockwise rotation. The two forces with positive vertical component give a positive, that is counterclockwise, moment, while $-F\,\hat{\imath}$ applied at $A$ has a line of action passing through $O$ and hence zero moment.
