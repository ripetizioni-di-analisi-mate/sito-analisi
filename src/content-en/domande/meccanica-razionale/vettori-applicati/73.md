A planar system, with points and vectors in the plane $Oxy$, has zero resultant moment about the point $A$ of coordinates $(0, 0)$ and about the point $B$ of coordinates $(2a, a)$, with $a > 0$. Which statement is true?

- The system is balanced
- The system is equivalent to a couple
- The resultant is different from the zero vector and orthogonal to $B - A$
- The resultant is zero, or it has a line of action passing through $A$ and $B$

## Explanation

By the change of pole formula $\vec{M}_B = \vec{M}_A + (A - B) \wedge \vec{R}$, and since both moments are zero one obtains $(A - B) \wedge \vec{R} = \vec{0}$. The cross product vanishes if and only if $\vec{R} = \vec{0}$ or $\vec{R}$ is parallel to $B - A$.

In the first case, since $\vec{M}_A$ is zero as well, the system is balanced. In the second the system is equivalent to the single vector $\vec{R}$ applied at $A$, since the moment about $A$ is zero, and the line of action, parallel to $B - A$, also passes through $B$.

A couple has a moment different from the zero vector about every pole, hence it is ruled out. Two poles are not enough to guarantee equilibrium, which follows instead from a zero moment about three non-collinear points.
