The slope of a roof is a rigid rod of length $\ell$ inclined at the angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal, with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The snow exerts a uniform vertical load of intensity $q_0$ per unit *horizontal* length, that is referred to the projection of the slope onto a horizontal plane. What is the intensity of the resultant of the load?

- $q_0\,\ell$
- $\dfrac{q_0\,\ell}{\cos\alpha}$
- $q_0\,\ell\sin\alpha$
- $q_0\,\ell\cos\alpha$

## Explanation

The load is referred to the horizontal length, hence the resultant is obtained by integrating $q_0$ along the horizontal projection of the slope, which is $\ell\cos\alpha$ long, and one obtains
$$
R = \int_0^{\ell\cos\alpha} q_0\,dx = q_0\,\ell\cos\alpha .
$$
A piece of the slope of length $ds$ has horizontal projection $\cos\alpha\,ds$, therefore along the slope the load is uniform with intensity $q_0\cos\alpha$, and the line of action of the resultant is the vertical line through the midpoint.

The value $q_0\,\ell$ treats $q_0$ as an intensity per unit length of the slope, and $\dfrac{q_0\,\ell}{\cos\alpha}$ is obtained by dividing by the cosine instead of multiplying.
