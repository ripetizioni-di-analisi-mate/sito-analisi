The rigid rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $3a$ and negligible weight, has the end $A$ at the origin and forms the angle $30^\circ$ with the horizontal $x$ axis. A load of weight $2P$ hangs at $A$ and a load of weight $P$ hangs at $B$, that is the rod is acted on by the vectors $-\,2P\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $A$ and $-P\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $B$, with $a > 0$ and $P > 0$. At which distance from $A$ does the line of action of the resultant meet the rod?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.598,1.5" aste="A-B" forze="0 -1.4,0,-1.4,;2.598 0.8,0,-0.7," tratteggi="0,0 2.4,0" etichette="0.3,-1.1,2P;2.9,0.95,P;0.95,0.2,30°" quote="A,B,3a,-0.6" titolo="The inclined rod with the two loads hanging at the ends"}

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,a$
- $2a$
- $a$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}\,a$

## Explanation

The two vectors are parallel, with coefficients $2P$ and $P$ whose sum is $3P \neq 0$, hence by the theorem on the center of a system of parallel vectors the system is equivalent to the resultant $-\,3P\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at the center $C$, with
$$
C - A = \frac{2P\,(A - A) + P\,(B - A)}{3P} = \frac{1}{3}\,(B - A) .
$$
The center is therefore the point of the rod at distance $\dfrac{3a}{3} = a$ from $A$, and the line of action of the resultant, which is the vertical through $C$, meets the rod at $C$.

The center does not depend on the common direction of the vectors, hence the angle $30^\circ$ plays no role and the point lies at one third of the rod for every inclination. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,a$ is the abscissa of $C$, that is its horizontal distance from $A$; the value $2a$ is obtained by swapping the two weights, and $\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}\,a$ by dividing $a$ by $\cos 30^\circ$.
