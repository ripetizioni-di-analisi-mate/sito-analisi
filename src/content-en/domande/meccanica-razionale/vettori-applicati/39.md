Two vertical forces, $+10\,\mathrm N$ at $x=0$ and $-9\,\mathrm N$ at $x=1\,\mathrm m$, have a nonzero resultant. Why does its line of action lie outside the segment between the points?

- The resultant is $+19\,\mathrm N$ and passes through $x=9\,\mathrm m$.
- The resultant is $-1\,\mathrm N$ and passes through $x=0$.
- Oppositely directed forces always form a pure couple.
- The resultant is $+1\,\mathrm N$ and must pass through $x=-9\,\mathrm m$ to preserve the moment.

## Explanation

The moment about the origin is $-9\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. An equivalent force of $+1\,\mathrm N$ needs a signed lever arm of $-9\,\mathrm m$. With opposite force signs the centre is not a positive-weight average.
