A rigid body can rotate about the $z$ axis, and $a > 0$ and $F > 0$ are given. Which of the following forces has zero axial moment about the $z$ axis?

- $F\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at the point of coordinates $(a, 0, a)$
- $F\,(\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath} + \hat{k})$ applied at the point of coordinates $(a, a, 0)$
- $F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ applied at the point of coordinates $(a, a, 0)$
- $F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ applied at the point of coordinates $(a, 0, 0)$

## Explanation

The axial moment about the $z$ axis is the third component of the moment about $O$, that is $M_z = x\,F_y - y\,F_x$, with the coordinates of the point of application.

For the force $F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ applied at $(a, a, 0)$ one obtains $M_z = a F - a F = 0$. The result has a geometric meaning: the line of action, of direction $\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}$, passes through the origin, hence it meets the axis and is coplanar with it.

The force $F\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $(a, 0, a)$ and the force $F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ applied at $(a, 0, 0)$ both give $M_z = a F$. The force $F\,(\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath} + \hat{k})$ applied at $(a, a, 0)$ gives $M_z = -\,a F - a F = -\,2\,a F$, since its component along $\hat{k}$ does not contribute to the axial moment.
