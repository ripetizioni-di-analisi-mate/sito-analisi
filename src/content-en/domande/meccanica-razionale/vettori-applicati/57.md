For a system of applied vectors the resultant moments about two poles are known, $\vec{M}_O = a F\,\hat{k}$ and $\vec{M}_{O'} = a F\,(2\hat{\jmath} + 3\hat{k})$, with $O' - O = a\,\hat{\imath}$, $a > 0$ and $F > 0$. What can be deduced about the resultant $\vec{R} = R_x\,\hat{\imath} + R_y\,\hat{\jmath} + R_z\,\hat{k}$?

- $R_y = -\,2F$ and $R_z = 2F$, while $R_x$ is not determined
- $\vec{R} = F\,(-\,2\hat{\jmath} + 2\hat{k})$
- $R_y = 2F$ and $R_z = -\,2F$, while $R_x$ is not determined
- $R_y = 2F$ and $R_z = 2F$, while $R_x$ is not determined

## Explanation

By the change of pole formula $\vec{M}_{O'} - \vec{M}_O = (O - O') \wedge \vec{R} = -\,a\,\hat{\imath} \wedge \vec{R}$, and the cross product in components gives
$$
-\,a\,\hat{\imath} \wedge \vec{R} = a R_z\,\hat{\jmath} - a R_y\,\hat{k} .
$$
Setting this vector equal to $\vec{M}_{O'} - \vec{M}_O = a F\,(2\hat{\jmath} + 2\hat{k})$ one obtains the system
$$
\begin{cases}
a R_z = 2\,a F \\[4pt]
-\,a R_y = 2\,a F
\end{cases}
$$
formed by the components along $\hat{\jmath}$ and along $\hat{k}$, hence $R_z = 2F$ and $R_y = -\,2F$.

The component $R_x$ does not appear, since the part of $\vec{R}$ parallel to $O - O'$ has zero cross product with $\hat{\imath}$; therefore the two moments do not determine it, and writing $\vec{R} = F\,(-\,2\hat{\jmath} + 2\hat{k})$ amounts to assuming $R_x = 0$ without any justification.
