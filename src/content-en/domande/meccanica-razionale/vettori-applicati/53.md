The applied vector $(P, \vec{v})$ has $P - O = a\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{v} = F\,(\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k})$, with $a > 0$ and $F > 0$. Among all oriented lines passing through $O$, what is the unit vector of the one about which the axial moment is maximum?

- $\dfrac{\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$
- $\hat{\imath}$
- $\dfrac{\hat{\jmath} - \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$
- $\dfrac{-\,\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$

## Explanation

For every line through $O$ with unit vector $\hat{u}$ the axial moment equals $M_r = \hat{u} \cdot \vec{M}_O$. The dot product of a unit vector with a fixed vector is maximum when the unit vector has the direction and orientation of the vector, and in that case it equals the magnitude of the vector.

The moment about $O$ equals
$$
\vec{M}_O = a\,\hat{\imath} \wedge F\,(\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}) = a F\,(-\,\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}) ,
$$
hence the required unit vector is $\dfrac{-\,\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$ and the maximum axial moment equals $\sqrt{2}\,a F$.

The opposite unit vector $\dfrac{\hat{\jmath} - \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$ gives the minimum value $-\sqrt{2}\,a F$. The lines with unit vectors $\hat{\imath}$ and $\dfrac{\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$ are orthogonal to $\vec{M}_O$, therefore they give zero axial moment.
