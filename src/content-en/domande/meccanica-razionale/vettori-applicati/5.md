Two couples act on a rigid body. The first is formed by the applied vectors $(A, F\,\hat{k})$ and $(B, -F\,\hat{k})$ with $B - A = 2a\,\hat{\imath}$; the second is formed by $(C, 2F\,\hat{\imath})$ and $(D, -\,2F\,\hat{\imath})$ with $D - C = a\,\hat{\jmath}$, where $a > 0$ and $F > 0$. What is the magnitude of the moment of the complete system?

- $2\sqrt{2}\,a F$
- $4\,a F$
- $2\,a F$
- $\sqrt{2}\,a F$

## Explanation

By the **theorem on the moment of a couple** each moment is computed with the formula $\vec{M} = (A - B) \wedge \vec{v}$ and does not depend on the pole. One therefore obtains
$$
\vec{M}_1 = -\,2a\,\hat{\imath} \wedge F\,\hat{k} = 2\,a F\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{M}_2 = -\,a\,\hat{\jmath} \wedge 2F\,\hat{\imath} = 2\,a F\,\hat{k} .
$$
The resultants of the two couples vanish, therefore the complete system has zero resultant and is equivalent to a single couple of moment $\vec{M}_1 + \vec{M}_2 = 2\,a F\,(\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k})$.

The two moments are orthogonal, therefore the magnitude of the sum equals $2\sqrt{2}\,a F$; the value $4\,a F$ would be obtained by adding the magnitudes, which is legitimate only for parallel vectors with the same orientation.
