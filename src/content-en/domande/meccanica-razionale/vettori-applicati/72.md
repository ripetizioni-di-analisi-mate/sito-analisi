The vector $F\,\hat{k}$ applied at the point $A$ with $A - O = a\,\hat{\imath}$ and a couple of moment $C\,\hat{\imath}$ act on a rigid body, with $a > 0$, $F > 0$ and $C > 0$. The system is equivalent to the single vector $F\,\hat{k}$ applied at a point $Q$ of the plane $Oxy$. What are the coordinates of $Q$?

- $\left( a,\ \dfrac{C}{F},\ 0 \right)$
- $\left( a,\ -\dfrac{C}{F},\ 0 \right)$
- $\left( a + \dfrac{C}{F},\ 0,\ 0 \right)$
- $\left( a - \dfrac{C}{F},\ 0,\ 0 \right)$

## Explanation

The resultant moment about $O$ equals
$$
\vec{M}_O = a\,\hat{\imath} \wedge F\,\hat{k} + C\,\hat{\imath} = C\,\hat{\imath} - a F\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
which is orthogonal to $\vec{R} = F\,\hat{k}$; hence $I = 0$ and the reduction to a single applied vector is possible.

The vector $F\,\hat{k}$ applied at the point $Q$ of coordinates $(x, y, 0)$ has moment $(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath}) \wedge F\,\hat{k} = F y\,\hat{\imath} - F x\,\hat{\jmath}$. Equivalence requires the components along $\hat{\imath}$ and along $\hat{\jmath}$ to coincide with those of $\vec{M}_O$, and this gives the system
$$
\begin{cases}
F y = C \\[4pt]
-\,F x = -\,a F
\end{cases}
$$
therefore $x = a$ and $y = \dfrac{C}{F}$. The couple thus has the effect of shifting the line of action of the vector by the distance $\dfrac{C}{F}$, in the direction of $\hat{\jmath}$, orthogonal both to the vector and to the moment of the couple.
