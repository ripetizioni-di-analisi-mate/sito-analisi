The rigid rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $2\ell$ and placed along the $x$ axis with $A$ at the origin, carries a uniform load of intensity $q_0$ on the part $0 \leq s \leq \ell$ and a triangular load growing linearly from $0$ at $s = \ell$ to $2q_0$ at $s = 2\ell$, both directed along $-\hat{\jmath}$. Through which abscissa does the line of action of the resultant of the complete load pass?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" carichi="A,3 0,0.6,0.6,q_0;3 0,B,0,1.2,2q_0" quote="A,3 0,ℓ,1.6;3 0,B,ℓ,1.6" riferimento=0 titolo="The uniform load on the first half and the triangular load on the second"}

- $s_C = \ell$
- $s_C = \dfrac{11\ell}{12}$
- $s_C = \dfrac{23\ell}{18}$
- $s_C = \dfrac{13\ell}{12}$

## Explanation

Each load reduces to its own resultant applied at its own center, and then the two parallel vectors are composed.

The uniform load has resultant $q_0\,\ell$ at the point of abscissa $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$; the triangular load has resultant $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2q_0 \cdot \ell = q_0\,\ell$ at the point of abscissa $\ell + \dfrac{2\ell}{3} = \dfrac{5\ell}{3}$, since the center of a triangular load falls at two thirds of the base from the side on which the intensity is greatest.

The two resultants are equal, therefore the center of the system is the midpoint between the two centers:
$$
s_C = \frac{q_0\,\ell \cdot \dfrac{\ell}{2} + q_0\,\ell \cdot \dfrac{5\ell}{3}}{2\,q_0\,\ell} = \frac{13\ell}{12} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{23\ell}{18}$ would be obtained by taking $2q_0\,\ell$ as the resultant of the triangular load, without halving it.
