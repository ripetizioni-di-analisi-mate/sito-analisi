On a rigid body only two applied vectors act, both different from the zero vector, and the system is balanced. Which statement is true?

- The two vectors are opposite and have the same line of action
- The two vectors are opposite and the lines of action can be parallel and distinct
- The two vectors have the same direction and the same orientation
- The resultant vanishes, whereas the resultant moment can differ from the zero vector

## Explanation

The equilibrium of the system requires $\vec{R} = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{M}_O = \vec{0}$, therefore the two conditions hold together.

From the first condition it follows that $\vec{v}_2 = -\,\vec{v}_1$, therefore the two vectors are opposite and have the same direction with opposite orientations. If the lines of action were parallel and distinct, the two vectors would form a couple of arm $d > 0$, whose moment has magnitude $|\vec{v}_1|\,d \neq 0$ about every pole, against the second condition.

The two lines of action therefore coincide, and this is the content of the **theorem on balanced systems of two vectors**.
