A pulley is a disk of radius $R$ that rotates about the fixed axis passing through its center $O$ and parallel to $\hat{k}$. The rope wound on the pulley hangs vertically on both sides, as in the figure: at the point of coordinates $(-R, 0)$ the tension is $-\,3F\,\hat{\jmath}$, at the point of coordinates $(R, 0)$ it is $-F\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $F > 0$. What is the axial moment of the two tensions about the axis of the pulley, oriented along $\hat{k}$?

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,1" punti="0,0,O,centro" linee="-1,0 -1,-1.9;1,0 1,-1.9" forze="-1 -1.4,0,-1.4,;1 -0.6,0,-0.6," etichette="-1.5,-1.1,3F;1.35,-0.45,F" quote="0 0,1 0,R,-0.35" titolo="The pulley and the tensions of the rope on the two sides"}

- $2 F R$
- $4 F R$
- $-\,2 F R$
- $0$

## Explanation

The points of application and the forces lie in the plane $Oxy$, hence the axial moment about the axis through $O$ parallel to $\hat{k}$ is the sum of the moments $x\,v_y - y\,v_x$ of the two tensions.

The tension on the left gives $(-R) \cdot (-\,3F) = 3 F R$, the one on the right gives $R \cdot (-F) = -F R$, therefore
$$
M_z = 3 F R - F R = 2 F R .
$$
The moment is positive, hence the pulley tends to rotate counterclockwise, toward the side of the larger tension. The two tensions have the same arm $R$ and tend to produce opposite rotations, therefore their moments subtract; the value $4 F R$ is obtained by adding them, and the value $0$ would occur only with equal tensions.
