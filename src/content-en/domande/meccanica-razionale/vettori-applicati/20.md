A rigid body rotates about the $z$ axis. On the body the force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{k}$ acts, applied at the point $P$ with $P - O = a\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$, where the origin $O$ belongs to the axis, with $a > 0$ and $F > 0$. Which statement is true?

- Both the axial moment about $z$ and the moment about $O$ vanish
- The axial moment about $z$ vanishes, whereas the moment about $O$ differs from the zero vector
- The axial moment about $z$ equals $\sqrt{2}\,a F$
- The axial moment about $z$ depends on the point of the axis chosen as the pole

## Explanation

The moment about $O$ equals
$$
\vec{M}_O = a\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}) \wedge F\,\hat{k} = a F\,(\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath}) ,
$$
which differs from the zero vector and has magnitude $\sqrt{2}\,a F$.

Its third component vanishes, therefore the axial moment about the $z$ axis equals $0$, since the force is parallel to the axis of rotation and therefore does not contribute to the equation of motion about that axis. By the **theorem on the independence of the axial moment of the point of the axis** the value $0$ is obtained by taking as pole any point of the $z$ axis.
