For which of the following systems, with $a > 0$ and $F > 0$, does the central axis not exist?

- The single applied vector $(O, F\,\hat{\imath})$
- The applied vectors $(O, F\,\hat{\imath})$ and $(A, F\,\hat{\jmath})$ with $A - O = a\,\hat{k}$
- The applied vectors $(O, F\,\hat{\imath})$ and $(A, -F\,\hat{\imath})$ with $A - O = a\,\hat{\jmath}$
- Three applied vectors with lines of action concurrent at $O$ and resultant $F\,\hat{k}$

## Explanation

The central axis is defined only for systems with a resultant different from the zero vector, and indeed its equation contains the division by $|\vec{R}|^2$.

The vectors $(O, F\,\hat{\imath})$ and $(A, -F\,\hat{\imath})$ have zero resultant and parallel, distinct lines of action, hence they form a **couple**, of moment
$$
(O - A) \wedge F\,\hat{\imath} = -\,a\,\hat{\jmath} \wedge F\,\hat{\imath} = a F\,\hat{k} ,
$$
the same about every pole; no pole is distinguished and the central axis does not exist.

The other three systems have a nonzero resultant. The single vector has its own line of action as the central axis, the concurrent vectors have as the central axis the line through $O$ parallel to $\hat{k}$, and the two vectors with skew lines have a central axis about which the moment is minimum but different from the zero vector.
