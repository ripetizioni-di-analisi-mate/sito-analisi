Two systems of applied vectors have resultants $\vec{R}_1 = F\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{R}_2 = 2F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, and have the same resultant moment about the point $A$. Letting $B$ be the point with $B - A = a\,\hat{\imath}$, with $a > 0$, which statement is true?

- The two systems have the same resultant moment about every pole
- The two systems have different resultant moments about $B$
- The two systems have the same resultant moment about $B$, but they are not equivalent
- The two systems have the same resultant moment about $A$ and about $B$, hence they are equivalent

## Explanation

By the change of pole formula, for each of the two systems $\vec{M}_B = \vec{M}_A + (A - B) \wedge \vec{R}$. The vector $A - B = -\,a\,\hat{\imath}$ is parallel to both resultants, hence the two cross products vanish and each system has $\vec{M}_B = \vec{M}_A$; therefore the moments about $B$ coincide as well.

The resultants, however, are different, hence the two systems **are not equivalent**. About a pole $O'$ off the line $AB$ the two moments differ by
$$
(A - O') \wedge (\vec{R}_1 - \vec{R}_2) = -\,F\,(A - O') \wedge \hat{\imath} ,
$$
which is different from the zero vector. Equivalence requires equal resultants, and when the resultants coincide a single pole is enough.
