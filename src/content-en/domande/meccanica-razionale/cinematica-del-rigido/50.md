A rigid straight rod lies on the $z$ axis and moves in space. At the instant considered each of its points $P = (0,\,0,\,z)$ has velocity $\vec{v}_P = -2z\;\hat{\imath}$, with $z$ in meters and $\vec{v}_P$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$. Which vectors $\vec{\omega}$ are compatible with this state of motion?

- Only $\vec{\omega} = (0,\,-2,\,0)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $\vec{\omega} = (0,\,2,\,c)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$, with $c$ any real number
- $\vec{\omega} = (0,\,-2,\,c)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$, with $c$ any real number
- $\vec{\omega} = (-2,\,0,\,c)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$, with $c$ any real number

## Explanation

The origin belongs to the rod and has zero velocity, therefore the fundamental formula requires $\vec{\omega} \wedge z\,\hat{k} = -2z\;\hat{\imath}$ for every $z$. Setting $\vec{\omega} = (\omega_x,\,\omega_y,\,\omega_z)$, one has $\vec{\omega} \wedge z\,\hat{k} = (\omega_y z,\; -\omega_x z,\; 0)$, hence $\omega_y = -2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\omega_x = 0$. The component $\omega_z$ does not appear in the equality, therefore it remains **arbitrary**. The theorem on the uniqueness of the angular velocity requires points that are not all aligned, and for a rod this hypothesis is not satisfied. Indeed, a rotation about the axis of the rod leaves the velocity of each of its points unchanged.
