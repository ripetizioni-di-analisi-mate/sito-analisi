The velocity field of a rigid body is written $\vec{v}_P = \vec{v}_O + \mathbb{A}\,(P - O)$, with the coordinates in meters, the velocities in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and the antisymmetric matrix
$$
\mathbb{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -3 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \mathrm{s^{-1}} .
$$
What is the angular velocity of the body?

- $(-1,\,-2,\,-3)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $(-1,\,2,\,-3)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $(3,\,-2,\,1)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $(1,\,2,\,3)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

By the fundamental formula $\vec{v}_P - \vec{v}_O = \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O)$ for every point $P$, therefore the angular velocity is the vector $\vec{\omega}$ such that $\mathbb{A}\,\vec{u} = \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u}$ for every vector $\vec{u}$. By the result on the vector associated with an antisymmetric matrix its components are $(-a_{23},\; a_{13},\; -a_{12})$, hence
$$
\vec{\omega} = \big( -(-1),\; 2,\; -(-3) \big) = (1,\,2,\,3)\;\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The check on the first column confirms the result, since $(1,\,2,\,3) \wedge \hat{\imath} = (0,\,3,\,-2)$, which is the first column of $\mathbb{A}$. The vector $(-1,\,-2,\,-3)$ is instead associated with the transposed matrix $\mathbb{A}^T = -\mathbb{A}$.
