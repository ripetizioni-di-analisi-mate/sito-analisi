The velocity field of a body is assigned in the frame $(O; x, y, z)$ as a function of the position $P = (x,\,y,\,z)$ of its points. Which of the following fields can be the state of motion of a rigid body?

- $\vec{v}(P) = (x,\; y,\; 0)$
- $\vec{v}(P) = (1 - 2y,\; 2x,\; 3)$
- $\vec{v}(P) = (y,\; x,\; 0)$
- $\vec{v}(P) = (-2y,\; 2x,\; z)$

## Explanation

The differential form of the rigidity constraint, written for the point $P$ and for the origin $O$, requires $(P - O) \cdot \big( \vec{v}(P) - \vec{v}(O) \big) = 0$ for every $P$. For the field $(1 - 2y,\; 2x,\; 3)$ one has $\vec{v}(O) = (1,\,0,\,3)$, therefore $\vec{v}(P) - \vec{v}(O) = (-2y,\; 2x,\; 0)$ and its dot product with $(x,\,y,\,z)$ equals $-2xy + 2xy = 0$. For the fields $(x,\; y,\; 0)$, $(y,\; x,\; 0)$ and $(-2y,\; 2x,\; z)$ the same dot product equals $x^2 + y^2$, $2xy$ and $z^2$ respectively, which are not zero for every $P$, hence the distance of some point from $O$ would change. The admissible field is written $\vec{v}(P) = \vec{v}(O) + \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O)$ with $\vec{\omega} = 2\,\hat{k}$, and since $\vec{\omega} \cdot \vec{v}(O) = 6 \neq 0$ it is a **helical** state of motion.
