A rigid plate moves in the plane $Oxy$, and $A$ and $B$ are two distinct points of it. Which set of data always determines the state of motion of the plate, that is, the velocity of each of its points?

- $\vec{v}_A$ and the component of $\vec{v}_B$ along the line $AB$
- The directions of $\vec{v}_A$ and of $\vec{v}_B$, not parallel to each other
- $\vec{v}_A$ and the direction of $\vec{v}_B$, not orthogonal to the line $AB$
- $\vec{v}_A$ and the magnitude of $\vec{v}_B$

## Explanation

By the fundamental formula $\vec{v}_P = \vec{v}_A + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge (P - A)$ for every point $P$, therefore the plane state of motion is determined by $\vec{v}_A$ and by $\dot{\vartheta}$, that is, by three numbers. If $\hat{u}$ is the unit vector of the direction of $\vec{v}_B$, the same formula written for $B$ gives $\lambda\,\hat{u} = \vec{v}_A + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge (B - A)$, a linear system of two equations in the unknowns $\lambda$ and $\dot{\vartheta}$. The vector $\hat{k} \wedge (B - A)$ is orthogonal to $AB$, while $\hat{u}$ is not, hence the two vectors are not parallel and the system has one and only one solution. The component of $\vec{v}_B$ along $AB$ equals that of $\vec{v}_A$ by equiprojectivity, therefore it adds no information. The directions of the two velocities determine the instantaneous center of rotation but not $\dot{\vartheta}$, and the magnitude of $\vec{v}_B$ gives an equation of second degree in $\dot{\vartheta}$, which for $|\vec{v}_B| > |\vec{v}_A|$ has two solutions of opposite sign.
