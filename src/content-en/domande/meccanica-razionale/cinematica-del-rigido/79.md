The angular velocity of a rigid body is written in the attached basis as $\vec{\omega} = \omega_1\,\hat{e}_1 + \omega_2\,\hat{e}_2 + \omega_3\,\hat{e}_3$. Which relation holds between the derivative $\dot{\vec{\omega}}$, computed in the fixed frame, and the vector $\dot{\omega}_1\,\hat{e}_1 + \dot{\omega}_2\,\hat{e}_2 + \dot{\omega}_3\,\hat{e}_3$?

- They differ by the term $\vec{\omega} \wedge \dot{\vec{\omega}}$
- They coincide for every motion of the body
- They coincide only when $|\vec{\omega}|$ is constant
- They differ by the term $\vec{\omega} \wedge \omega_1\,\hat{e}_1$

## Explanation

Differentiating the product of each component with its own unit vector one obtains
$$
\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \dot{\omega}_i\,\hat{e}_i + \sum_{i=1}^{3} \omega_i\,\dot{\hat{e}}_i .
$$
By Poisson's formulas $\dot{\hat{e}}_i = \vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_i$, therefore the second sum equals $\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$. The two derivatives hence **coincide** for every motion, and this allows one to write Euler's equations of dynamics with the attached components of $\dot{\vec{\omega}}$. For a vector $\vec{u}$ different from $\vec{\omega}$ the second sum equals instead $\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u}$, in general different from the zero vector.
