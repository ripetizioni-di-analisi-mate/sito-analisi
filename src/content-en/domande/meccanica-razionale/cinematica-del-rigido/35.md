Two photographs show a wheel rotating and moving right on a fixed floor in planar rigid motion. At one instant its material contact point slides to the right. Can that point be identified as the contact of the fixed and moving centrodes at that instant?

- Yes, because the wheel is circular.
- Yes, provided the centre moves at constant speed.
- No, because no planar motion with rotation has an instantaneous centre.
- No, because that point has nonzero velocity relative to the floor.

## Explanation

The fixed and moving centrodes meet at the instantaneous centre of rotation, where the corresponding body point has zero velocity. Sliding rules out the geometrical wheel–floor contact as that centre.
