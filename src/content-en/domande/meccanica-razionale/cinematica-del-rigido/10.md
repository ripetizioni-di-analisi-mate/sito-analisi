A homogeneous disk of radius $r$ and center $G$ rolls without slipping inside a fixed circular guide of radius $R > r$ and center $O$. What are the fixed centrode and the moving centrode of the motion of the disk?

- The fixed centrode is the circle of center $O$ and radius $R$, the moving centrode is the circle of center $G$ and radius $r$
- The fixed centrode is the circle of center $G$ and radius $r$, the moving centrode is the circle of center $O$ and radius $R$
- The fixed centrode is the circle of center $O$ and radius $R - r$, the moving centrode is the circle of center $G$ and radius $r$
- The fixed centrode and the moving centrode are both circles of radius $R - r$

## Explanation

The contact takes place without slipping on a fixed guide, hence the contact point $C$ has zero velocity and is the instantaneous center of rotation of the disk. In the fixed plane the point $C$ always belongs to the guide, therefore the fixed centrode is the circle of center $O$ and radius $R$. In the plane attached to the disk the point $C$ always lies on the rim, at the distance $r$ from $G$, therefore the moving centrode is the circle of center $G$ and radius $r$. The two curves are tangent at $C$, and the moving centrode rolls without slipping on the fixed centrode.
