A spinning top has its tip fixed at the point $O$ and moves in regular precession: its axis forms the constant angle $\vartheta = 30^\circ$ with the vertical and rotates about the vertical with $\dot{\psi} = \Omega$, while the top spins about its own axis with $\dot{\varphi} = 4\,\Omega$, where $\Omega > 0$. What is the magnitude of the angular acceleration $\dot{\vec{\omega}}$ of the top?

- $2\,\Omega^2$
- $0$
- $4\,\Omega^2$
- $2\sqrt{3}\;\Omega^2$

## Explanation

In a regular precession $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\psi}\,\hat{k} + \dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_3$, with $\dot{\psi}$ and $\dot{\varphi}$ constant and $\hat{k}$ fixed. Differentiating and applying Poisson's formula to the attached unit vector $\hat{e}_3$ one obtains
$$
\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \dot{\varphi}\,\dot{\hat{e}}_3 = \dot{\varphi}\;\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_3 = \dot{\varphi}\,\dot{\psi}\;\hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_3 ,
$$
because $\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_3 = \vec{0}$. The vector $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_3$ has magnitude $\sin\vartheta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore $|\dot{\vec{\omega}}| = 4\,\Omega \cdot \Omega \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 2\,\Omega^2$. The magnitude of $\vec{\omega}$ is constant, while its direction rotates about the $z$ axis, hence the angular acceleration is **different from the zero vector**.
