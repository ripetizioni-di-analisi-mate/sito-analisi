A disk of radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed straight guide and its center advances with constant velocity of magnitude $v > 0$. Which statement about the point $C$ of the disk in contact with the guide is true at the instant considered?

- $\vec{v}_C = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{a}_C \neq \vec{0}$
- $\vec{v}_C = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{a}_C = \vec{0}$
- $\vec{v}_C \neq \vec{0}$ and $\vec{a}_C = \vec{0}$
- $\vec{a}_C$ is parallel to the guide and different from the zero vector

## Explanation

Rolling without slipping on a fixed guide requires $\vec{v}_C = \vec{0}$, therefore $C$ is the instantaneous center of rotation of the disk. The acceleration is computed with the formula for the acceleration field, and since $\vec{a}_G = \vec{0}$ and the angular velocity is constant only the centripetal term remains. Then $\vec{a}_C = \dfrac{v^2}{R}\;\hat{\jmath}$, directed from $C$ toward the center $G$ and hence orthogonal to the guide. The velocity of the contact point is therefore zero while its acceleration is different from the zero vector.
