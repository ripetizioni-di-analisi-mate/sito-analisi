A rigid plate moves in the plane $Oxy$. The point $A = (0,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ has velocity $\vec{v}_A = (2,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the point $B = (4,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ has velocity $\vec{v}_B = (2,\,-3)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Where is the instantaneous center of rotation of the plate?

- $(1,\,-2)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(1,\,2)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(-1,\,2)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(2,\,-1)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The fundamental formula of rigid kinematics gives $\vec{v}_B = \vec{v}_A + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge (B - A)$, therefore from the component along $\hat{\jmath}$ one gets $-3 = 1 + 4\,\dot{\vartheta}$, hence $\dot{\vartheta} = -1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The instantaneous center of rotation is the point $C = A + \dfrac{\hat{k} \wedge \vec{v}_A}{\dot{\vartheta}}$. Since $\hat{k} \wedge \vec{v}_A = (-1,\,2,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, dividing by $\dot{\vartheta} = -1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ one obtains $C - A = (1,\,-2)\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore the instantaneous center of rotation is the point $(1,\,-2)\,\mathrm{m}$, and the plate turns about it in the clockwise sense.
