A rigid rod $AB$ moves in the plane. At the instant considered its angular velocity is zero, its angular acceleration equals $\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \alpha\,\hat{k}$ with $\alpha \neq 0$, and the endpoint $A$ has velocity $\vec{v}_A \neq \vec{0}$. Which statement is true at that instant?

- All points of the rod have the same velocity, and their accelerations are different
- All points of the rod have the same velocity and the same acceleration
- The velocities of the points of the rod are different, and their accelerations are equal
- All points of the rod have the same velocity, and their accelerations differ by a centripetal term

## Explanation

The fundamental formula with $\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$ gives $\vec{v}_P = \vec{v}_A$ for every point $P$, therefore the state of motion is **translational**. The centripetal term of the acceleration formula contains $\vec{\omega}$ twice and vanishes, hence
$$
\vec{a}_P = \vec{a}_A + \alpha\,\hat{k} \wedge (P - A) .
$$
The term $\alpha\,\hat{k} \wedge (P - A)$ is different from the zero vector for $P \neq A$, therefore the accelerations of the points are different. A translational state of motion at one instant does not imply that the motion is a translation; this is the case of the connecting rod of a slider-crank mechanism at the instant when the crank is orthogonal to the guide.
