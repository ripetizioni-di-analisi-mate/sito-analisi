A rigid body has angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = (0,\,0,\,3)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$ and its point $\Omega = (0,\,0,\,0)$ has velocity $\vec{v}_\Omega = (1,\,0,\,0)\;\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the velocity of the point $P = (2,\,1,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ of the body?

- $(4,\,6,\,0)\;\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(-3,\,6,\,0)\;\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(-2,\,-6,\,0)\;\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(-2,\,6,\,0)\;\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The fundamental formula of rigid kinematics gives $\vec{v}_P = \vec{v}_\Omega + \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega)$. The cross product equals $(0,\,0,\,3) \wedge (2,\,1,\,0) = (-3,\,6,\,0)\;\mathrm{m/s}$. Adding $\vec{v}_\Omega = (1,\,0,\,0)\;\mathrm{m/s}$ one obtains $\vec{v}_P = (-2,\,6,\,0)\;\mathrm{m/s}$. The term $\vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega)$ is orthogonal both to $\vec{\omega}$ and to $P - \Omega$, hence the velocity of $P$ differs from that of $\Omega$ by a vector of the plane $Oxy$.
