A rigid body moves in space and contains the points $A = (0,\,0,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$, $B = (0,\,0,\,3)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $P = (0,\,0,\,1)\,\mathrm{m}$, aligned on the $z$ axis. At the instant considered $\vec{v}_A = (1,\,2,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{v}_B = (4,\,-1,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the velocity of $P$?

- $(3,\,0,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\left( \dfrac{5}{2},\,\dfrac{1}{2},\,1 \right)\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(2,\,1,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(2,\,1,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The fundamental formula with pole $A$ gives $\vec{v}_P = \vec{v}_A + \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - A)$ and $\vec{v}_B = \vec{v}_A + \vec{\omega} \wedge (B - A)$. Since $P - A = \dfrac{1}{3}\,(B - A)$ and the cross product is linear, one has $\vec{\omega} \wedge (P - A) = \dfrac{1}{3}\,(\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A)$. One has $\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A = (3,\,-3,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, therefore
$$
\vec{v}_P = (1,\,2,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s} + (1,\,-1,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s} = (2,\,1,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Along a segment attached to the body the velocity therefore varies **linearly**. The computation does not require $\vec{\omega}$, which the data determine only up to its component along $\hat{k}$.
