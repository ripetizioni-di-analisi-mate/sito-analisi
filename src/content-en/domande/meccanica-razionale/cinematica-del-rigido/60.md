The Ferris wheel in the figure has radius $R$ and rotates about the fixed horizontal axis through $O$ with constant angular speed $\Omega$. A cabin is hinged at the point $A$ of the rim and always stays vertical, that is, it keeps its orientation. At the instant when the radius $OA$ is horizontal, what is the speed of the point $P$ of the cabin located at distance $h$ below $A$?

- $\Omega\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$
- $\Omega\,(R + h)$
- $\Omega h$
- $\Omega R$

## Explanation

The cabin keeps its orientation, therefore the angle between one of its segments and the $x$ axis is constant and its angular velocity is zero. With $\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$ the fundamental formula gives $\vec{v}_P = \vec{v}_A$, hence the state of motion of the cabin is **translational**. The point $A$ also belongs to the wheel, which rotates about $O$, therefore $|\vec{v}_A| = \Omega R$. Then $|\vec{v}_P| = \Omega R$, whereas the value $\Omega\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$ would be obtained by assigning to the cabin the angular velocity of the wheel.
