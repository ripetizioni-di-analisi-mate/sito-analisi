A wheel of radius $R$ skids on a fixed horizontal guide. The center $G$ moves with constant velocity $v\,\hat{\imath}$, with $v > 0$, and the wheel spins with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = -\dfrac{3v}{R}\;\hat{k}$. What are the fixed centrode and the moving centrode of the motion?

- The fixed centrode is the guide, the moving centrode is the rim of the wheel
- The fixed centrode is the line parallel to the guide at height $\dfrac{R}{3}$, the moving centrode is the circle with center $G$ and radius $\dfrac{2R}{3}$
- The fixed centrode is the line parallel to the guide at height $\dfrac{4R}{3}$, the moving centrode is the circle with center $G$ and radius $\dfrac{R}{3}$
- The fixed centrode is the line parallel to the guide at height $\dfrac{2R}{3}$, the moving centrode is the circle with center $G$ and radius $\dfrac{R}{3}$

## Explanation

The instantaneous center of rotation is $C = G + \dfrac{\hat{k} \wedge v\,\hat{\imath}}{\dot{\vartheta}}$ with $\dot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{3v}{R}$, therefore $C - G = -\dfrac{R}{3}\;\hat{\jmath}$. In the fixed plane the center is always at height $R - \dfrac{R}{3} = \dfrac{2R}{3}$ above the guide, hence the fixed centrode is the line parallel to the guide at that height. In the plane attached to the wheel the center is always at distance $\dfrac{R}{3}$ from $G$, therefore the moving centrode is the circle with center $G$ and radius $\dfrac{R}{3}$. The guide and the rim of the wheel are the fixed and moving centrodes only in rolling without slipping, that is, for $\vec{\omega} = -\dfrac{v}{R}\;\hat{k}$.
