A rigid body has angular velocity $\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$, and two of its distinct points $P$ and $Q$ have the same velocity. Which conclusion is correct?

- The segment $PQ$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$
- The segment $PQ$ is orthogonal to $\vec{\omega}$
- The state of motion is translational
- The kinematic invariant is zero

## Explanation

The fundamental formula, written for the two points, gives $\vec{v}_P - \vec{v}_Q = \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - Q)$. The two velocities are equal, therefore $\vec{\omega} \wedge (P - Q) = \vec{0}$. The cross product of two nonzero vectors vanishes only when they are parallel, hence $P - Q$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$. The state of motion is not translational, since $\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$, and the kinematic invariant may be different from zero, as happens in a helical state of motion.
