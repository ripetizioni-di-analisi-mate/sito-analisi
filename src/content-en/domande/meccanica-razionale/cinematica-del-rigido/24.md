A disk of radius $R$ and center $G$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide. At the instant considered the center has velocity $\vec{v}_G = v\,\hat{\imath}$ and acceleration $\vec{a}_G = a\,\hat{\imath}$, with $v > 0$ and $a > 0$. What is the acceleration of the point $T$ of the disk diametrically opposite to the contact?

- $a\,\hat{\imath} - \dfrac{v^2}{R}\;\hat{\jmath}$
- $2a\,\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{v^2}{R}\;\hat{\jmath}$
- $2a\,\hat{\imath}$
- $2a\,\hat{\imath} - \dfrac{v^2}{R}\;\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

The condition of rolling without slipping gives $\vec{\omega} = -\dfrac{v}{R}\;\hat{k}$ and, differentiating with respect to time, $\dot{\vec{\omega}} = -\dfrac{a}{R}\;\hat{k}$. The point $T$ satisfies $T - G = R\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore $\dot{\vec{\omega}} \wedge (T - G) = a\,\hat{\imath}$ and $-\,\dot{\vartheta}^{\,2}\,(T - G) = -\dfrac{v^2}{R}\;\hat{\jmath}$. Adding the three terms of the acceleration formula one obtains
$$
\vec{a}_T = 2a\,\hat{\imath} - \frac{v^2}{R}\;\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The horizontal component is twice that of the center, since $T$ is twice as far from the contact point as $G$ is.
