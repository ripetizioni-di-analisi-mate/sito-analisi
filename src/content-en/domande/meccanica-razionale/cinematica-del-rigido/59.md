A rigid body has angular velocity $\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$, and one of its points $\Omega$ has velocity $\vec{v}_\Omega$ different from zero and parallel to $\vec{\omega}$. Which statement is true?

- The point $\Omega$ belongs to the Mozzi axis and the state of motion is rotational
- The state of motion is translational
- The point $\Omega$ belongs to the Mozzi axis and the state of motion is helical
- The Mozzi axis is the line through $\Omega$ orthogonal to $\vec{\omega}$

## Explanation

Mozzi's theorem assigns to the axis the point $P^* = \Omega + \dfrac{\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_\Omega}{|\vec{\omega}|^2}$. The vectors $\vec{\omega}$ and $\vec{v}_\Omega$ are parallel, therefore their cross product is zero and $P^* = \Omega$, hence the Mozzi axis is the line through $\Omega$ parallel to $\vec{\omega}$. The kinematic invariant equals $I = \vec{\omega} \cdot \vec{v}_\Omega = \pm\,|\vec{\omega}|\,|\vec{v}_\Omega| \neq 0$, therefore the state of motion is **helical**. The velocity of $\Omega$ is then the minimum among the velocities of the points of the body.
