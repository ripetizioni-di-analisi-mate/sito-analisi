A rigid body rotates about the fixed $z$ axis with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \Omega\,\hat{k}$. What is the acceleration of its point $P$ which at the instant considered occupies the position $(R,\,0,\,h)$?

- $-\,\Omega^2\,\big( R\,\hat{\imath} + h\,\hat{k} \big)$
- $\Omega^2 R\,\hat{\imath}$
- $-\,\Omega^2 R\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\vec{0}$

## Explanation

The origin lies on the fixed axis, therefore it has zero velocity and acceleration, and the angular velocity is constant, hence $\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \vec{0}$. The acceleration formula then reduces to $\vec{a}_P = \vec{\omega} \wedge \big( \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O) \big)$. One has $\Omega\,\hat{k} \wedge \big( R\,\hat{\imath} + h\,\hat{k} \big) = \Omega R\,\hat{\jmath}$, and multiplying again on the left by $\Omega\,\hat{k}$ one obtains $\vec{a}_P = -\,\Omega^2 R\,\hat{\imath}$. The acceleration is directed from $P$ toward the point $(0,\,0,\,h)$ of the axis, because the component $h\,\hat{k}$ of $P - O$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$ and does not contribute to the double cross product.
