In the epicyclic gear train in the figure the sun gear, of radius $r$ and center $O$, rotates about the fixed axis through $O$ with angular velocity $\omega_s\,\hat{k}$, with $\omega_s > 0$. The planet gear, of radius $r$ and center $P$, meshes without slipping with the sun gear and with the fixed ring gear of radius $3r$ and center $O$, and its center is hinged to the carrier arm $OP$, free to rotate about $O$. What is the angular velocity of the arm?

- $\dfrac{\omega_s}{2}\;\hat{k}$
- $-\dfrac{\omega_s}{4}\;\hat{k}$
- $\dfrac{\omega_s}{3}\;\hat{k}$
- $\dfrac{\omega_s}{4}\;\hat{k}$

## Explanation

The point $K$ of contact between the planet gear and the fixed ring gear has zero velocity, therefore it is the instantaneous center of rotation of the planet gear. The point $S$ of contact with the sun gear has the velocity of the rim of the sun gear, of magnitude $\omega_s r$, and is at distance $2r$ from $K$, while the center $P$ is at distance $r$ from $K$. The speeds of the points of the planet gear are proportional to their distances from $K$, hence $|\vec{v}_P| = \dfrac{\omega_s r}{2}$, with the same sense as the velocity of $S$. The point $P$ also belongs to the arm, which rotates about $O$ with $|P - O| = 2r$, therefore the arm has angular speed $\dfrac{\omega_s r}{2 \cdot 2r} = \dfrac{\omega_s}{4}$. The arm hence turns in the same sense as the sun gear, with angular velocity $\dfrac{\omega_s}{4}\;\hat{k}$.
