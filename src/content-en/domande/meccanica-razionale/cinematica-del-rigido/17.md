A rigid body moves in space and contains the points $A = (0,\,0,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $B = (0,\,0,\,2)\,\mathrm{m}$. The velocity of $A$ is $\vec{v}_A = (1,\,1,\,2)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which of the following can be the velocity of $B$?

- $(3,\,0,\,2)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(1,\,1,\,3)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(3,\,0,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(2,\,2,\,4)\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By equiprojectivity the velocities of $A$ and of $B$ have the same component along the line joining them, which has direction $\hat{k}$. The component of $\vec{v}_A$ along $\hat{k}$ equals $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, therefore the third component of $\vec{v}_B$ must equal $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ as well. Among the velocities listed the only one with third component equal to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is $(3,\,0,\,2)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The other three would make the distance $|B - A|$ vary, hence they are incompatible with the rigidity constraint.
