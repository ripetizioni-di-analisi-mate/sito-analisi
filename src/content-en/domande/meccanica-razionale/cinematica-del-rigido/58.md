The propeller of an airplane rotates about its own axis with angular speed $100\;\mathrm{rad/s}$, while the airplane moves in a straight line along the same axis with speed $60\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the speed of the tip of a blade, which is at distance $0.8\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis?

- $140\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $100\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $80\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The points of the axis of the propeller have velocity parallel to $\vec{\omega}$, therefore the axis of the propeller is the **Mozzi axis** and the common velocity of its points is $\vec{v}_{\min}$, of magnitude $60\,\mathrm{m/s}$. By Mozzi's theorem the velocity of the tip $P$ is $\vec{v}_P = \vec{v}_{\min} + \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - P^*)$, where $P^*$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ to the axis, and the two terms are orthogonal. The second term has magnitude $100 \cdot 0.8\,\mathrm{m/s} = 80\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence, by the Pythagorean theorem,
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = \sqrt{60^2 + 80^2}\;\mathrm{m/s} = 100\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The state of motion of the propeller is helical, and the tip of the blade describes a cylindrical helix.
