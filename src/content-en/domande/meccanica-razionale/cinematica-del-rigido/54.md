A disk of radius $R$ and center $G$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, and the center moves to the right with constant speed $v$. What is the radius of curvature of the trajectory of a point of the rim at the instant when that point is in the highest position $T$?

- $2R$
- $4R$
- $R$
- $\dfrac{R}{2}$

## Explanation

The point $T$ is at distance $2R$ from the contact point, which is the instantaneous center of rotation, therefore $\vec{v}_T = 2v\,\hat{\imath}$. The angular velocity is constant and the center does not accelerate, hence the acceleration formula with $T - G = R\,\hat{\jmath}$ gives $\vec{a}_T = -\dfrac{v^2}{R}\,\hat{\jmath}$. This acceleration is orthogonal to the velocity, therefore it coincides with the normal component $\dfrac{|\vec{v}_T|^2}{\rho}$ of the acceleration of the point. Then
$$
\rho = \frac{|\vec{v}_T|^2}{|\vec{a}_T|} = \frac{4v^2 R}{v^2} = 4R .
$$
The radius of curvature of the **cycloid** at its highest point is therefore twice the distance of $T$ from the instantaneous center of rotation.
