For a rigid body in motion in space the velocities of three points are known, measured in $\mathrm{m/s}$:
$$
\vec{v}_A = (-2,\, 0,\, -2) ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_B = (-2,\, -2,\, -3) ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_C = (-1,\, -2,\, -2) ,
$$
with $A = (0,\,0,\,0)$, $B = (1,\,0,\,0)$ and $C = (0,\,0,\,1)$ in meters. What is the angular velocity of the body?

- $(-2,\,-1,\,2)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $(2,\,1,\,2)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $(2,\,1,\,-2)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $(0,\,1,\,-2)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The fundamental formula applied to the points $A$ and $B$ gives $\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A = \vec{\omega} \wedge (B - A)$, and since $B - A = (1,\,0,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ that cross product equals $(0,\,\omega_z,\,-\omega_y)$. Comparing with $\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A = (0,\,-2,\,-1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ one gets $\omega_z = -2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\omega_y = 1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Applying the same formula to the points $A$ and $C$, with $C - A = (0,\,0,\,1)\,\mathrm{m}$, one obtains $\vec{\omega} \wedge (C - A) = (\omega_y,\,-\omega_x,\,0)$, which compared with $(1,\,-2,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives $\omega_x = 2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The angular velocity is therefore $(2,\,1,\,-2)\;\mathrm{rad/s}$, and its scalar product with $\vec{v}_A$ vanishes, hence the state of motion is rotational.
