A rigid body has angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \Omega\,(\hat{e}_1 + \hat{e}_3)$, where $\hat{e}_1$, $\hat{e}_2$, $\hat{e}_3$ are the unit vectors of a right-handed triad attached to the body. The vector $\vec{u} = 2\,\hat{e}_1 + 3\,\hat{e}_3$ is attached to the body. What is its derivative $\dot{\vec{u}}$ computed in the fixed frame?

- $\Omega\,\hat{e}_2$
- $-5\,\Omega\,\hat{e}_2$
- $\vec{0}$
- $-\,\Omega\,\hat{e}_2$

## Explanation

The components of $\vec{u}$ in the attached basis are constant, therefore $\dot{\vec{u}} = 2\,\dot{\hat{e}}_1 + 3\,\dot{\hat{e}}_3$, and by **Poisson's formulas** $\dot{\vec{u}} = \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u}$. One has
$$
\Omega\,(\hat{e}_1 + \hat{e}_3) \wedge (2\,\hat{e}_1 + 3\,\hat{e}_3) = \Omega\,\big( 3\,\hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_3 + 2\,\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_1 \big) = \Omega\,\big( -3\,\hat{e}_2 + 2\,\hat{e}_2 \big) ,
$$
because $\hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_3 = -\,\hat{e}_2$ and $\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_1 = \hat{e}_2$. Then $\dot{\vec{u}} = -\,\Omega\,\hat{e}_2$. The derivative is different from the zero vector, although $\vec{u}$ has constant attached components, because its direction rotates together with the body.
