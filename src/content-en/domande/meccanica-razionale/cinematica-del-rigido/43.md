Let $\hat{e}_1$ be a unit vector of a frame attached to a rigid body with angular velocity $\vec{\omega}$. What is its derivative with respect to time $\dot{\hat{e}}_1$?

- $\hat{e}_1 \wedge \vec{\omega}$
- $\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_1$
- $\vec{0}$, since $\hat{e}_1$ has constant magnitude
- $(\vec{\omega} \cdot \hat{e}_1)\;\hat{e}_1$

## Explanation

Poisson's formulas establish that for every unit vector attached to the body one has $\dot{\hat{e}}_i = \vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_i$. The relation is obtained by applying the fundamental formula to the point $P_1 = \Omega + \hat{e}_1$, which is a point of the body, and comparing the two expressions of its velocity. The magnitude of $\hat{e}_1$ stays constant, whereas its direction varies in time, hence the derivative is not the zero vector. The cross product $\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_1$ is indeed orthogonal to $\hat{e}_1$, as the derivative of a vector of constant magnitude must be.
