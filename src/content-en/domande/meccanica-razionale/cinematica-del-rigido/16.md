A rigid plate moves in the plane $Oxy$ and contains the points $A = (0,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $B = (2,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$. Which pair of velocities is compatible with the rigidity constraint?

- $\vec{v}_A = (1,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{v}_B = (2,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\vec{v}_A = (0,\,2)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{v}_B = (3,\,2)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\vec{v}_A = (2,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{v}_B = (0,\,1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\vec{v}_A = (1,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{v}_B = (1,\,3)\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By the equiprojectivity of the velocity field, the velocities of two points of a rigid body have the same component along the line joining them. The line $AB$ has direction $\hat{\imath}$, therefore the condition reduces to $v_{Ax} = v_{Bx}$. The only pair listed that satisfies this equality is the one with $v_{Ax} = v_{Bx} = 1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, that is, $\vec{v}_A = (1,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{v}_B = (1,\,3)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In the other three pairs the components along $\hat{\imath}$ are different, hence the distance between $A$ and $B$ would vary in time.
