A rigid rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ moves in the plane. At the instant considered the instantaneous center of rotation $C$ lies on the line $AB$, on the side of $A$ opposite to $B$ and at the distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from $A$. If $|\vec{v}_A| = v$, what is $|\vec{v}_B|$?

- $3v$
- $2v$
- $\dfrac{v}{3}$
- $v$

## Explanation

For every point $P$ of the rod one has $|\vec{v}_P| = |\dot{\vartheta}|\,|P - C|$, therefore the magnitudes of the velocities are proportional to the distances from $C$. One has $|A - C| = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$ and, since $C$ lies beyond $A$, it follows that $|B - C| = \dfrac{\ell}{2} + \ell = \dfrac{3\ell}{2}$. The ratio of the two distances equals $3$, hence $|\vec{v}_B| = 3\,|\vec{v}_A| = 3v$. The two velocities are both orthogonal to $AB$ and have the same sense, since $A$ and $B$ lie on the same side of $C$.
