A disk of radius $r$ rolls without slipping on the outside of a fixed circle of radius $R$ and center $O$, staying in contact with it. The radius $OG$ that identifies the center of the disk forms the angle $\varphi$ with the $x$ axis, with $\dot{\varphi} > 0$. What is the magnitude of the angular velocity of the disk?

- $\dfrac{(R + r)\,\dot{\varphi}}{r}$
- $\dfrac{(R - r)\,\dot{\varphi}}{r}$
- $\dfrac{(R + r)\,\dot{\varphi}}{R}$
- $\dot{\varphi}$

## Explanation

The disk rests on the outside of the fixed circle, therefore its center $G$ stays at the distance $R + r$ from $O$ and describes the circle of radius $R + r$, hence $|\vec{v}_G| = (R + r)\,\dot{\varphi}$. The contact takes place without slipping on a fixed curve, therefore the contact point is the instantaneous center of rotation of the disk and lies at the distance $r$ from $G$. From the relation $|\vec{v}_G| = |\vec{\omega}|\,r$ one gets
$$
|\vec{\omega}| = \frac{(R + r)\,\dot{\varphi}}{r} .
$$
The disk therefore turns more rapidly than its center revolves about $O$.
