A rigid plate moves in the plane $Oxy$. At the instant considered its point $A = (0,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ has acceleration $\vec{a}_A = (2,\,0)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the plate has $\dot{\vartheta} = 1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\ddot{\vartheta} = 1\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. Which point of the plate has zero acceleration?

- $(2,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(1,\,-1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(-1,\,1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(1,\,1)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

One looks for the point $K$ with $\vec{a}_K = \vec{0}$ and sets $K - A = (x,\,y)$, with $x$ and $y$ in meters. The acceleration formula in the plane case gives
$$
\vec{a}_K = \vec{a}_A + \ddot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge (K - A) - \dot{\vartheta}^{\,2}\,(K - A) ,
$$
and since $\hat{k} \wedge (K - A) = (-y,\,x)$ setting both components to zero gives the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2 - y - x = 0 \\
x - y = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation gives $x = y$, and the first then gives $x = y = 1$. The point with zero acceleration is therefore $(1,\,1)\,\mathrm{m}$, whereas neglecting the term with $\ddot{\vartheta}$ one would find $(2,\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$.
