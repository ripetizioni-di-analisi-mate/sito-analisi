A rigid body with a fixed point moves so that, in a time interval, its attached axis $\zeta$ coincides with the fixed axis $z$, that is, with nutation angle $\vartheta = 0$. Which statement about the description with the Euler angles is true?

- The angular velocity equals $\dot{\psi}\,\hat{k}$, and the angle $\varphi$ plays no role
- The magnitude of the angular velocity satisfies $|\vec{\omega}|^2 = \dot{\psi}^{\,2} + \dot{\varphi}^{\,2}$
- Only the sum $\psi + \varphi$ is determined, and $\vec{\omega} = (\dot{\psi} + \dot{\varphi})\,\hat{k}$
- The angular velocity equals $(\dot{\psi} - \dot{\varphi})\,\hat{k}$

## Explanation

With $\hat{e}_3 = \hat{k}$ the product $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_3$ is zero, therefore the line of nodes is not defined and the angles $\psi$ and $\varphi$ are not determined separately. The precession and the proper rotation then take place about the same axis, and the orientation of the body depends only on the angle $\psi + \varphi$ between $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{e}_1$. The formula $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\psi}\,\hat{k} + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{N} + \dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_3$, with $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ and $\hat{e}_3 = \hat{k}$, gives $\vec{\omega} = (\dot{\psi} + \dot{\varphi})\,\hat{k}$. The same magnitude follows from $|\vec{\omega}|^2 = \dot{\psi}^{\,2} + \dot{\varphi}^{\,2} + 2\,\dot{\psi}\dot{\varphi}\cos\vartheta$ with $\cos\vartheta = 1$.
