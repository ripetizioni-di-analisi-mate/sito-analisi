A rigid body with a fixed point has, at the instant considered, $\dot{\psi} = 0$, $\dot{\varphi} = 0$ and $\dot{\vartheta} = \omega_0 \neq 0$. What is its angular velocity?

- $\omega_0\,\hat{k}$
- $\omega_0\,\hat{N}$, with $\hat{N}$ the unit vector of the line of nodes
- $\omega_0\,\hat{e}_3$
- $\omega_0\,\big( \hat{k} + \hat{e}_3 \big)$

## Explanation

The angular velocity in terms of the Euler angles is $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\psi}\,\hat{k} + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{N} + \dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_3$. With $\dot{\psi} = \dot{\varphi} = 0$ only the term $\omega_0\,\hat{N}$ remains, therefore the body rotates about the **line of nodes**. The attached axis $\zeta$ then tilts with respect to the fixed axis $z$ while remaining in the plane orthogonal to $\hat{N}$, and only the nutation changes. In the attached basis the components of $\vec{\omega}$ are $\omega_0\cos\varphi$, $-\,\omega_0\sin\varphi$ and $0$, that is, those of $\hat{N}$ multiplied by $\omega_0$.
