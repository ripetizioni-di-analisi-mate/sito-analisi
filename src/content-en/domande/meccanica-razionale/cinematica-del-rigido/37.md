In a plane rigid motion with $\dot{\vartheta} \neq 0$, which relation holds between the fixed centrode and the moving centrode at the point $C$?

- They are tangent at $C$, and the moving centrode rolls without slipping on the fixed centrode
- They are orthogonal at $C$
- They have the same curvature at $C$
- They are tangent at $C$, and the moving centrode slips on the fixed centrode with velocity of magnitude $|\dot{\vartheta}|\,|C - O|$

## Explanation

The geometric point $C$, considered as a point of the fixed centrode, has a velocity in the fixed frame, and considered as a point of the moving centrode it has a velocity in the attached frame. The theorem on the composition of velocities links the two through the velocity of the point of the body that occupies the position $C$, which is zero because $C$ is the instantaneous center of rotation. The two velocities coincide, therefore the two curves have the same tangent vector at $C$ and the arcs traveled in the same time interval have the same length. Then the moving centrode rolls without slipping on the fixed centrode.
