Two rigid rods are connected by an internal hinge at the point $A$, common to both. Which relation does the connection impose on the velocities?

- The velocity of $A$ as a point of the first rod is equal to the velocity of $A$ as a point of the second one
- The two rods have the same angular velocity
- The angular velocities of the two rods are opposite
- The velocity of $A$ is orthogonal to both rods

## Explanation

The point $A$ belongs to both rods, therefore it has a single velocity, and the internal hinge imposes precisely this coincidence. The fundamental formula is then written for each rod taking $A$ as the pole, with the same $\vec{v}_A$ and with the angular velocity proper to that body. The two angular velocities remain independent, since the hinge allows the relative rotation of the two rods. In the exercises this relation links the parameters that identify the configuration of the two bodies.
