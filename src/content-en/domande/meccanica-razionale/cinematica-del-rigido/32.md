For a rigid plate in motion in the plane it is known that the velocities of the points $A$ and $B$ are parallel to each other and nonzero, and that the line $AB$ is not orthogonal to them. What does one conclude about the state of motion?

- It is rotational, with instantaneous center of rotation on the segment $AB$
- It is translational
- It is rotational, with instantaneous center of rotation at the midpoint of $AB$
- The data are not compatible with a rigid motion

## Explanation

The instantaneous center of rotation belongs to the line through $A$ orthogonal to $\vec{v}_A$ and to the line through $B$ orthogonal to $\vec{v}_B$. The two velocities are parallel, therefore the two lines are parallel, and they are distinct since the line $AB$ is not orthogonal to the velocities. Two parallel and distinct lines do not meet, hence there exists no point with zero velocity and one has $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$. Then the state of motion is translational, and one says that the instantaneous center of rotation is at infinity, in the direction orthogonal to the velocities.
