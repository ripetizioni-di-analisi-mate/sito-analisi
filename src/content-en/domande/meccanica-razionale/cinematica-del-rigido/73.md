The disk in the figure, of radius $R$ and center $G$, rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide; $C$ is the contact point and $T$ the point of the rim diametrically opposite. How is the velocity of a point $P$ of the rim different from $C$ and from $T$ directed?

::struttura{cerchi="0,1,1" linee="-1.8,0 2,0" punti="0,1,G,baricentro;0,0,C,centro;0,2,T;0.94,1.34,P" riferimento=0 titolo="Disk rolling without slipping: contact point $C$, highest point $T$ and point $P$ of the rim"}

- Along the line $PG$
- Along the tangent to the rim at $P$
- Along the line $PT$
- Along the line $PC$

## Explanation

The point $C$ is the instantaneous center of rotation, therefore $\vec{v}_P = \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge (P - C)$ is orthogonal to the segment $CP$. The segment $CT$ is a diameter of the rim, hence the angle $\widehat{CPT}$, inscribed in a semicircle, is a right angle. The line $PT$ is then the line through $P$ orthogonal to $CP$, and therefore it has the direction of the velocity of $P$. The result concerns only the state of motion, hence it holds at every instant, even when the center accelerates.
