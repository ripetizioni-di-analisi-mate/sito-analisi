A homogeneous disk of radius $R$ and center $G$ rests on a horizontal belt that translates with velocity $\vec{u} = u\,\hat{\imath}$, and rolls on it without slipping. The center of the disk has velocity $\vec{v}_G = v\,\hat{\imath}$, with $u \neq v$. What is the angular velocity of the disk?

- $\dfrac{v - u}{R}\;\hat{k}$
- $-\dfrac{u + v}{R}\;\hat{k}$
- $-\dfrac{v}{R}\;\hat{k}$
- $\dfrac{u - v}{R}\;\hat{k}$

## Explanation

Rolling without slipping requires that the point of the disk and the point of the belt that occupy the contact position have the same velocity, therefore $\vec{v}_C = \vec{u}$. Setting $\vec{\omega} = \omega_z\,\hat{k}$ and $C - G = -R\,\hat{\jmath}$, the fundamental formula gives $\vec{v}_C = (v + \omega_z R)\,\hat{\imath}$. From the equality $v + \omega_z R = u$ one gets $\omega_z = \dfrac{u - v}{R}$, hence $\vec{\omega} = \dfrac{u - v}{R}\;\hat{k}$. When the belt is faster than the center the disk turns in the counterclockwise sense, and for $u = v$ the angular velocity vanishes.
