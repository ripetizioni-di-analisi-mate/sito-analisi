A disk of radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed straight guide and moves in the vertical plane. How many degrees of freedom does the disk have, and of what type is the constraint of pure rolling?

- Two degrees of freedom, and the constraint is holonomic
- Three degrees of freedom, and the constraint is nonholonomic
- One degree of freedom, and the constraint is nonholonomic
- One degree of freedom, and the constraint is holonomic

## Explanation

The condition of pure rolling links the abscissa $s$ of the center to the angle of rotation $\vartheta$ through $\dot{s} = -\,R\,\dot{\vartheta}$. This relation between the velocities integrates to $s = -\,R\,\vartheta + \text{constant}$, therefore it becomes a relation between the coordinates alone and the constraint is holonomic. The contact with the guide moreover requires that the height of the center equal $R$, hence of the three coordinates that identify a free disk in the plane only one remains independent. The disk therefore has one degree of freedom and the constraint of pure rolling is holonomic.
