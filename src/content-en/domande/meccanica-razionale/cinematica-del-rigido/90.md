In a bicycle the chainring, attached to the pedals, has radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the sprocket, attached to the rear wheel, has radius $4\,\mathrm{cm}$; chainring and sprocket are connected by an inextensible and taut chain. The rear wheel has radius $35\,\mathrm{cm}$ and rolls without slipping on the road. If the pedals turn with angular speed $2\;\mathrm{rad/s}$, with what speed does the bicycle move?

- $1.75\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.28\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The chain is inextensible and taut and does not slip on the teeth, therefore the points of the chainring and of the sprocket in contact with it have speeds of the same magnitude. Then $\omega_p \cdot 0.04\,\mathrm{m} = 2\;\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, hence the sprocket, and with it the rear wheel, turns with angular speed $\omega_p = 5\;\mathrm{rad/s}$. The wheel rolls without slipping, therefore the contact point with the road is the instantaneous center of rotation and the center of the wheel moves with speed $5\;\mathrm{rad/s} \cdot 0.35\,\mathrm{m} = 1.75\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The center of the wheel belongs to the frame, which translates, hence the bicycle moves with speed $1.75\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
