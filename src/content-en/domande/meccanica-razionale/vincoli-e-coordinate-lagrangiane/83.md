A disk rolls without slipping on a fixed rough guide, which exerts at the contact point $H$ a reaction with a normal component and a tangential friction component. For which reason is the pure rolling constraint ideal?

- Because the point of the disk in contact with the guide has zero virtual displacement
- Because the reaction of the guide is orthogonal to the guide itself
- Because in pure rolling the friction force is zero
- Because the rolling disk has a single degree of freedom

## Explanation

The point of the disk in contact with the guide has zero velocity in every act of motion compatible with the constraint, and the constraint is fixed, therefore its virtual displacement is zero, $\delta H = \vec{0}$. The virtual work of the reaction is then
$$
\delta L^{(v)} = \vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta H = 0 ,
$$
whatever the tangential component of $\vec{\Phi}$. The friction force is in general nonzero and the reaction is not orthogonal to the guide, hence ideality depends on the zero displacement of the point of application. For this reason systems with disks rolling without slipping are studied with the Lagrange equations, without introducing the friction force among the unknowns.
