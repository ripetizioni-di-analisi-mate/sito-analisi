A material point $P$ is constrained to the smooth and fixed spherical surface of center $O$ and radius $R$, and the colatitude $\vartheta$ and the longitude $\varphi$ are taken as Lagrangian coordinates, so that $P - O = R\,( \sin\vartheta\cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{k} )$. What is the magnitude of the velocity of $P$?

- $R\,\sqrt{\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \dot{\varphi}^2}$
- $R\,\sqrt{\dot{\vartheta}^2 \sin^2\vartheta + \dot{\varphi}^2}$
- $R\,\big( |\dot{\vartheta}| + |\dot{\varphi}|\,\sin\vartheta \big)$
- $R\,\sqrt{\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \dot{\varphi}^2 \sin^2\vartheta}$

## Explanation

The velocity is obtained by differentiating the position with respect to the two Lagrangian coordinates, hence
$$
\vec{v}_P = \frac{\partial P}{\partial \vartheta}\,\dot{\vartheta} + \frac{\partial P}{\partial \varphi}\,\dot{\varphi} .
$$
The two vectors $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial \vartheta}$ and $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial \varphi}$ are orthogonal, since the first is tangent to the meridian and the second to the parallel, and they have magnitudes $R$ and $R\sin\vartheta$. The Pythagorean theorem therefore gives
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = R\,\sqrt{\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \dot{\varphi}^2 \sin^2\vartheta} .
$$
The factor $\sin\vartheta$ accompanies $\dot{\varphi}$ and not $\dot{\vartheta}$, since the radius of the parallel of colatitude $\vartheta$ equals $R\sin\vartheta$; at the poles, where $\sin\vartheta = 0$, the longitude does not contribute to the velocity.
