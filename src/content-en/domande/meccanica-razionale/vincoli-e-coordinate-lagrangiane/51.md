Three material points $P_1$, $P_2$ and $P_3$, not collinear, are connected pairwise by three rigid rods of negligible mass and move freely in space. How many Cartesian coordinates does the system have, how many independent constraint equations and how many degrees of freedom?

- $9$ coordinates, $6$ equations, $3$ degrees of freedom
- $6$ coordinates, $3$ equations, $3$ degrees of freedom
- $9$ coordinates, $3$ equations, $6$ degrees of freedom
- $9$ coordinates, $2$ equations, $7$ degrees of freedom

## Explanation

Three points of space have $3 \cdot 3 = 9$ Cartesian coordinates. Each rod fixes the distance between two points, hence the constraints are the three equations
$$
|P_2 - P_1|^2 = \ell_{12}^2 , \qquad |P_3 - P_2|^2 = \ell_{23}^2 , \qquad |P_1 - P_3|^2 = \ell_{31}^2 .
$$
The three equations are independent, since the Jacobian matrix has rank $3$ when the points are not collinear, therefore the theorem on the degrees of freedom gives $n = 9 - 3 = 6$. The result coincides with the degrees of freedom of a free rigid body in space, because three non-collinear points identify the position of a rigid body. The $6$ coordinates and the $3$ degrees of freedom correspond instead to the planar case.
