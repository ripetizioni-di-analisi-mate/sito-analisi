A material point $P$ is constrained to the smooth, fixed and bilateral guide of equation $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ of the plane $Oxy$, with $a > b > 0$. Setting $P - O = a\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + b\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$, to which vector is the constraint reaction at $P$ parallel?

- $-a\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + b\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $a\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + b\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $\dfrac{\cos\vartheta}{a}\,\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{\sin\vartheta}{b}\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

The constraint is smooth and bilateral, therefore the theorem on the reaction of a smooth constraint applies and guarantees that the reaction is parallel to $\operatorname{grad} f$, where $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} - 1$. One computes the gradient and obtains
$$
\operatorname{grad} f = \frac{2x}{a^2}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{2y}{b^2}\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
Substituting $x = a\cos\vartheta$ and $y = b\sin\vartheta$ one gets
$$
\operatorname{grad} f = 2\,\Big( \frac{\cos\vartheta}{a}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\sin\vartheta}{b}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big) ,
$$
hence the reaction is parallel to $\dfrac{\cos\vartheta}{a}\,\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{\sin\vartheta}{b}\,\hat{\jmath}$. The vector $-a\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + b\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$ is instead tangent to the ellipse, therefore it is orthogonal to the reaction.
