A cart slides on a straight horizontal guide and a motor drags it, imposing at every instant the velocity $\dot{x} = v_0$, with $v_0$ an assigned constant. How is the constraint imposed by the motor classified?

- Holonomic and moving, because it is equivalent to $x - v_0\,t = \text{constant}$
- Nonholonomic, because it is a condition on the velocity
- Holonomic and fixed, because $v_0$ is constant
- Nonholonomic and fixed, because $v_0$ does not depend on the position

## Explanation

The condition $\dot{x} - v_0 = 0$ is the time derivative of the function $x - v_0\,t$, hence it holds at every instant if and only if
$$
x - v_0\,t = x_0 ,
$$
with $x_0$ the initial abscissa. The constraint can therefore be reduced to a relation between the position and time, so it is holonomic. Time appears explicitly in the relation, therefore the constraint is moving, even though the assigned velocity is constant. The guide and the motor together determine the position at every instant, hence the cart has zero degrees of freedom.
