A homogeneous chain of length $L$ lies on a rough horizontal table, with static friction coefficient $f_s = 0.25$, and a part of it hangs vertically over the edge, which is smooth, as in the figure. What maximum fraction of the length can hang without the chain slipping?

::struttura{nodi="A,-3.2,0;E,0,0,-;F,0,-1.2" aste="A-E;E-F" poligoni="-4,0 0,0 0,-0.25 -4,-0.25" quote="A,E,L - x,-0.5;E,F,x,-0.5" titolo="Chain on a rough table with a part of length $x$ hanging over the edge"}

- $0.25$
- $0.20$
- $0.33$
- $0.80$

## Explanation

Let $\lambda$ be the linear density and $x$ the length of the hanging part. The hanging part pulls the chain with the force $\lambda\,x\,g$, whereas the part on the table, of length $L - x$, receives from the table the normal reaction $\Phi_n = \lambda\,(L - x)\,g$ and a friction force that balances the pull. The Coulomb-Morin law then gives
$$
\lambda\,x\,g \leq f_s\,\lambda\,(L - x)\,g ,
$$
that is, $\dfrac{x}{L} \leq \dfrac{f_s}{1 + f_s} = \dfrac{0.25}{1.25} = 0.20$. The value $0.25$ would be obtained by using as normal component the weight of the whole chain, which instead bears on the table only for the part lying on it.
