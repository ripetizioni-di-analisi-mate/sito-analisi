A rigid rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ moves in space with the end $A$ forced to belong to the plane $z = 0$. Taking as coordinates the three components of $A$ and the three components of $B$, how many independent constraint equations are there?

- $1$
- $2$
- $3$
- $4$

## Explanation

The coordinates of the two ends are $6$. The rigidity of the rod imposes that the distance of its ends be assigned, hence it gives the equation $|B - A|^2 - \ell^2 = 0$, whereas the belonging of $A$ to the plane gives the equation $z_A = 0$. The constraint equations are therefore $2$, and they are independent, since the Jacobian matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
-2(x_B - x_A) & -2(y_B - y_A) & -2(z_B - z_A) & 2(x_B - x_A) & 2(y_B - y_A) & 2(z_B - z_A) \\[4pt]
0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
has rank $2$ in every admissible configuration, since the first row is not zero and the two rows are not proportional. The system then has $n = 6 - 2 = 4$ degrees of freedom.
