A rigid rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ is free in the plane $Oxy$ and in the configuration considered has $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (\ell, 0)$. Let $\varepsilon$ be a small positive number. Which pair of displacements $(\delta A, \delta B)$ is a virtual displacement of the rod?

- $\delta A = \varepsilon\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\delta B = -\varepsilon\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\delta A = \vec{0}$ and $\delta B = \varepsilon\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\delta A = \varepsilon\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\delta B = \varepsilon\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $\delta A = \varepsilon\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\delta B = -\varepsilon\,\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

The rigidity constraint is $f(A, B) = |B - A|^2 - \ell^2 = 0$, and its gradients with respect to $A$ and to $B$ are $-2\,(B - A)$ and $2\,(B - A)$. The theorem on virtual displacements then says that $(\delta A, \delta B)$ is virtual if and only if
$$
(B - A) \cdot (\delta B - \delta A) = 0 ,
$$
that is, if and only if the displacements of the two ends have the same component along the rod. With $B - A = \ell\,\hat{\imath}$ the condition requires that $\delta B - \delta A$ has no component along $\hat{\imath}$, and the pair $\delta A = \varepsilon\,\hat{\jmath}$, $\delta B = -\varepsilon\,\hat{\jmath}$ gives $\delta B - \delta A = -2\varepsilon\,\hat{\jmath}$, which satisfies it. This pair is a rotation about the midpoint, whereas the other three lengthen or shorten the rod.
