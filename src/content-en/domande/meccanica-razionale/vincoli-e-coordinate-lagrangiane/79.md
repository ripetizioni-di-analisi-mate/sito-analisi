Two rigid rods are connected by a smooth internal hinge at the point $B$; the hinge exerts on the first rod the force $\vec{\Phi}$ and on the second the force $-\vec{\Phi}$, both applied at $B$. What is the total virtual work of the two forces for an arbitrary virtual displacement of the system?

- $0$
- $\vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta B$
- $-\vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta B$
- $2\,\vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta B$

## Explanation

The two forces are opposite by the action-reaction principle and are applied at the same point $B$. The hinge imposes that the points of the two rods that occupy $B$ remain coincident, therefore in every virtual displacement they have the same displacement $\delta B$. The total virtual work is then
$$
\delta L^{(v)} = \vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta B + ( -\vec{\Phi} ) \cdot \delta B = 0 ,
$$
hence the smooth internal hinge is an ideal constraint. The result holds for any $\vec{\Phi}$, so the reactions of the internal hinge do not appear in the equation of virtual work written for the whole system.
