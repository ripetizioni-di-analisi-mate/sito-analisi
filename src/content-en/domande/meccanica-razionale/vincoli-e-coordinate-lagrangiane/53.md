The following relations restrict the velocities of a point $P = (x, y, z)$. Which of them defines a nonholonomic constraint?

- $x\,\dot{x} + y\,\dot{y} + z\,\dot{z} = 0$
- $\dot{z} - 2x\,\dot{x} = 0$
- $y\,\dot{x} + x\,\dot{y} = 0$
- $\dot{z} - y\,\dot{x} = 0$

## Explanation

The first three relations are time derivatives of functions of the coordinates, respectively of $\dfrac{1}{2}\,(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)$, of $z - x^2$ and of $xy$, therefore they are equivalent to the holonomic constraints that keep those functions constant. For the relation $\dot{z} - y\,\dot{x} = 0$ suppose that there exist $f(x, y, z)$ of class $C^2$ and $\lambda$ never zero with $\lambda\,(\dot{z} - y\,\dot{x}) = \dfrac{df}{dt}$. Equating the coefficients of the velocities one obtains the system
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x} = -\lambda\,y \\[8pt]
\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 0 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z} = \lambda
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation it follows that the mixed second derivatives of $f$ with respect to $y$ and $z$ and with respect to $y$ and $x$ vanish; since they do not depend on the order of differentiation, differentiating the third and the first equation with respect to $y$ one obtains $\dfrac{\partial \lambda}{\partial y} = 0$ and $-\lambda - y\,\dfrac{\partial \lambda}{\partial y} = 0$, that is, $\lambda = 0$. This contradicts the assumption on $\lambda$, therefore the relation cannot be reduced to a constraint on the positions and the constraint is **nonholonomic**.
