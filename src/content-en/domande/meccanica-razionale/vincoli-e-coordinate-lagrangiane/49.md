Two rigid disks of radii $R_1$ and $R_2$ move in the same plane and cannot interpenetrate; their centers are $C_1$ and $C_2$. How is the constraint classified and which relation expresses it?

- Bilateral, with $|C_2 - C_1| = R_1 + R_2$
- Unilateral, with $|C_2 - C_1| \leq R_1 + R_2$
- Nonholonomic, with $(\vec{v}_{C_2} - \vec{v}_{C_1}) \cdot (C_2 - C_1) \geq 0$
- Unilateral, with $|C_2 - C_1| \geq R_1 + R_2$

## Explanation

Two disks do not overlap if and only if the distance between the centers is not smaller than the sum of the radii, hence the constraint is written
$$
|C_2 - C_1| - (R_1 + R_2) \geq 0 .
$$
The relation is an inequality between the positions alone, therefore the constraint is holonomic and unilateral. The ordinary configurations have $|C_2 - C_1| > R_1 + R_2$, and the disks are separated and exchange no forces; the boundary configurations have $|C_2 - C_1| = R_1 + R_2$, and the disks are in contact. The equality written as a bilateral constraint would keep the disks always in contact, which the impenetrability constraint does not impose.
