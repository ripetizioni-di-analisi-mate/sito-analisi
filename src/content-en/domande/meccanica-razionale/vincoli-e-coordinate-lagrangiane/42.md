A point particle $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$, threaded on a smooth straight guide parallel to the $y$ axis that translates with assigned constant velocity $u\,\hat{\imath}$, where $u > 0$, so that $x_P = u\,t$. The reaction of the guide is $\Phi\,\hat{\imath}$, orthogonal to the guide. What can be said, at any instant, about the power of the reaction in the actual motion and about its work in a virtual displacement?

- The power equals $\Phi\,u$ and the virtual work is zero
- The power and the virtual work are both zero
- The power is zero and the virtual work equals $\Phi\,\delta y$
- The power equals $\Phi\,u$ and the virtual work equals $\Phi\,\delta y$

## Explanation

In the actual motion the velocity of the particle is $\vec{v}_P = u\,\hat{\imath} + \dot{y}\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence the power of the reaction equals $\Phi\,\hat{\imath} \cdot \vec{v}_P = \Phi\,u$, nonzero in general. A virtual displacement is performed at fixed time, with the guide at rest in the position it occupies at that instant, therefore $\delta P = \delta y\,\hat{\jmath}$. The virtual work of the reaction then equals $\Phi\,\hat{\imath} \cdot \delta y\,\hat{\jmath} = 0$. The constraint is moving, so the possible displacements $u\,dt\,\hat{\imath} + dy\,\hat{\jmath}$ differ from the virtual ones, and the reaction of the smooth constraint does work in the actual motion while having zero virtual work. The product $\Phi\,\delta y$ multiplies the horizontal component of the reaction by a vertical displacement, while the work is the scalar product of two orthogonal vectors, hence it is zero.
