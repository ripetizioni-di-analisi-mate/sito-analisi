The horizontal rod $CB$ in the figure, of length $2\ell$ and negligible mass, is hinged at its midpoint $A$ to a fixed point and at the end $B$ rests from above on a smooth simple support, which can only push it upward. A vertical load of magnitude $P$ directed downward acts at the end $C$. Which statement is true?

::struttura{nodi="C,0,0;A,2,0;B,4,0" aste="C-B" vincoli="A,cerniera" poligoni="3.85,-0.26 4.15,-0.26 4,0" linee="3.7,-0.26 4.3,-0.26" forze="C,0,-1,P" quote="C,A,ℓ,0.75;A,B,ℓ,0.75" titolo="Rod with a hinge at the midpoint $A$, a simple support at $B$ and a load at $C$"}

- The support exerts at $B$ the force $P$ directed downward and the rod remains in equilibrium
- Equilibrium is not possible, because the end $B$ detaches from the support and the rod rotates about $A$
- The support exerts at $B$ the force $P$ directed upward and the rod remains in equilibrium
- The support exerts at $B$ a zero force and the hinge at $A$ alone supports the load

## Explanation

Suppose the rod is in equilibrium with the support in contact and denote by $\Phi_B$ the vertical component of the reaction of the support, positive upward. The moment equation about $A$, with $C - A = -\ell\,\hat{\imath}$ and $B - A = \ell\,\hat{\imath}$, gives
$$
\ell\,P + \ell\,\Phi_B = 0 ,
$$
hence $\Phi_B = -P$. The simple support is a unilateral constraint and exerts only reactions with $\Phi_B \geq 0$, therefore no admissible reaction balances the load. The load at $C$ makes the rod rotate about $A$ counterclockwise, hence the end $B$ rises and detaches from the support. The force $P$ directed downward would be the reaction of a bilateral roller support, and with a zero reaction at $B$ the moment of the load about $A$ would remain unbalanced.
