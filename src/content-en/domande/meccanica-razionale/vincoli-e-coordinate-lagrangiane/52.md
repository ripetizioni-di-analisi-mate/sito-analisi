A material point $P$ is threaded on a thin smooth straight guide that coincides with the $x$ axis; the guide rotates about its own axis, that is, about the $x$ axis, with assigned constant angular velocity $\omega$. Which statement is true?

- The constraint is fixed, because the set of points of the guide does not change in time
- The constraint is moving, because the guide rotates with angular velocity $\omega$
- The constraint is nonholonomic, because the guide assigns the angular velocity $\omega$
- The point has two degrees of freedom, the abscissa and the rotation angle of the guide

## Explanation

The point belongs to the guide at every instant if and only if its coordinates satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
y = 0 \\[4pt]
z = 0
\end{cases}
$$
and these equations do not contain time, because the rotation of the guide about its own axis carries each of its points to a point of the guide itself. The constraint is therefore holonomic, bilateral and **fixed**. A material point has no orientation, so the rotation of the guide adds no configuration parameter to it and the system has a single degree of freedom, the abscissa $x$. A guide that rotates about an axis different from its own, as in the plane $Oxy$ about $O$, changes instead the set of its points and gives a moving constraint.
