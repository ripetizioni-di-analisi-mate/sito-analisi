A material point $P = (x, y)$ is constrained to the smooth, fixed and bilateral curve of equation $x^2 + 4y^2 = 4$ of the plane $Oxy$. Which direction does the virtual displacement of $P$ have in the configuration $P = (0, 1)$?

- Parallel to $\hat{\jmath}$
- Parallel to $\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}$
- Zero
- Parallel to $\hat{\imath}$

## Explanation

The theorem on virtual displacements guarantees that, with a bilateral holonomic constraint of equation $f = 0$, the virtual displacements are all and only the solutions of $\operatorname{grad} f \cdot \delta P = 0$. Setting $f(x, y) = x^2 + 4y^2 - 4$ one obtains $\operatorname{grad} f = 2x\,\hat{\imath} + 8y\,\hat{\jmath}$, which in the configuration $P = (0, 1)$ equals $8\,\hat{\jmath}$. The condition then becomes
$$
8\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot ( \delta x\,\hat{\imath} + \delta y\,\hat{\jmath} ) = 8\,\delta y = 0 ,
$$
therefore $\delta y = 0$ and $\delta x$ remains arbitrary, that is, the virtual displacement is parallel to $\hat{\imath}$. The result agrees with the geometry of the curve, since $(0, 1)$ is the vertex of the ellipse on the $y$ axis, where the tangent is horizontal.
