A homogeneous disk of radius $R$ rolls without slipping on the fixed horizontal guide $y = 0$ of the plane $Oxy$, and $\vartheta$ is its rotation angle, positive counterclockwise. The center of the disk advances through a length $d > 0$ toward the increasing abscissas. Through which angle does the disk rotate and in which sense?

- $\dfrac{R}{d}$ clockwise
- $\dfrac{d}{2R}$ clockwise
- $\dfrac{d}{R}$ counterclockwise
- $\dfrac{d}{R}$ clockwise

## Explanation

The condition of pure rolling on a fixed line is equivalent to the holonomic constraint $x_C + R\,\vartheta = \text{constant}$. Between the initial configuration and the final one the constant does not change, therefore $\Delta x_C + R\,\Delta\vartheta = 0$, that is,
$$
\Delta\vartheta = -\frac{\Delta x_C}{R} = -\frac{d}{R} .
$$
The amplitude of the rotation then equals $\dfrac{d}{R}$, and the negative sign says that the rotation takes place clockwise, since the angles are positive counterclockwise. The same result is read on the length of the arcs, since the length of guide travelled equals the arc of the rim that unrolls on it, hence $d = R\,|\Delta\vartheta|$.
