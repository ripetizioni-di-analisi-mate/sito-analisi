A rigid rod, regarded as a segment, moves freely in space. How many degrees of freedom does it have?

- $n = 3$
- $n = 4$
- $n = 5$
- $n = 6$

## Explanation

A free rigid body in space has $6$ degrees of freedom, that is, the three coordinates of one of its points and the three Euler angles. The rod regarded as a segment is a particular case, since the rotation about its own axis carries the set of its points into itself and therefore does not change the configuration of the system. Of the six parameters one therefore remains undetermined, hence the degrees of freedom are
$$
n = 6 - 1 = 5 ,
$$
and as Lagrangian coordinates one takes the three coordinates of one end and the two angles that identify the direction of the rod. A body to which the proper rotation is also attributed, such as a thin cylinder, has $6$ degrees of freedom again.
