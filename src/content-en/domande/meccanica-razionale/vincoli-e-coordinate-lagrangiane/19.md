Among the following constraints, which one is nonholonomic?

- A disk that rolls without slipping on a fixed line of its plane
- The blade of a skate whose support point has at every instant velocity parallel to the blade
- A material point constrained to the spherical surface of center $O$ and radius $R$
- A rigid rod with its ends constrained to two orthogonal straight guides

## Explanation

The constraint of the blade is written $\dot{x} \sin\vartheta - \dot{y} \cos\vartheta = 0$, where $(x, y)$ is the support point and $\vartheta$ the angle of the blade with the $x$ axis, and it is not reducible to a relation between $x$, $y$ and $\vartheta$, therefore the constraint is nonholonomic. The pure rolling of a disk on a line is instead a constraint on the velocities that integrates, since $\dot{x}_C + R\,\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ is equivalent to $x_C + R\,\vartheta = \text{constant}$, hence it is holonomic. The point on the spherical surface and the rod with its ends on the two guides are limited by relations between the positions alone, hence they are constrained in a holonomic way.
