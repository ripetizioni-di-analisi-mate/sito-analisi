A homogeneous disk of radius $r$ rolls without slipping inside a fixed circular guide of center $O$ and radius $R > r$, as in the figure. Let $\varphi$ be the angle that the vector $C - O$ forms with the downward vertical, where $C$ is the center of the disk, and let $\vartheta$ be the rotation angle of the disk; both angles are positive counterclockwise. Which relation links $\dot{\vartheta}$ to $\dot{\varphi}$?

- $\dot{\vartheta} = \dfrac{R - r}{r}\,\dot{\varphi}$
- $\dot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{R}{r}\,\dot{\varphi}$
- $\dot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{R - r}{r}\,\dot{\varphi}$
- $\dot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{R + r}{r}\,\dot{\varphi}$

## Explanation

The center of the disk keeps the distance $R - r$ from $O$, hence, setting $\hat{u} = \sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath}$, one has $C - O = (R - r)\,\hat{u}$, whereas the contact point satisfies $A - O = R\,\hat{u}$ and therefore $A - C = r\,\hat{u}$. Differentiating the position of the center one obtains $\vec{v}_C = (R - r)\,\dot{\varphi}\,\hat{w}$, where $\hat{w} = \cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath}$, and the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics gives
$$
\vec{v}_A = \vec{v}_C + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge (A - C) = \big[ (R - r)\,\dot{\varphi} + r\,\dot{\vartheta} \big]\,\hat{w} .
$$
Pure rolling imposes $\vec{v}_A = \vec{0}$, therefore
$$
\dot{\vartheta} = -\frac{R - r}{r}\,\dot{\varphi} .
$$
The negative sign says that the disk turns in the sense opposite to the one in which its center travels along the guide, contrary to what happens in external rolling, where one obtains $\dot{\vartheta} = \dfrac{R + r}{r}\,\dot{\varphi}$.
