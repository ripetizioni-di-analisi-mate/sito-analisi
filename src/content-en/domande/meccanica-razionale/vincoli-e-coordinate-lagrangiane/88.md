A body of mass $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pressed against a rough vertical wall by a horizontal force of magnitude $F$, orthogonal to the wall; the static friction coefficient is $f_s = 0.40$. Taking $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the minimum value of $F$ that prevents the body from sliding?

- $7.85\,\mathrm{N}$
- $19.62\,\mathrm{N}$
- $49.05\,\mathrm{N}$
- $24.53\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The force $F$ is orthogonal to the wall, therefore equilibrium in the horizontal direction gives the normal component of the reaction $\Phi_n = F$. The weight is parallel to the wall, hence equilibrium in the vertical direction requires a friction force directed upward, of magnitude $|\vec{\Phi}_t| = m g$. The Coulomb-Morin law then gives $m g \leq f_s\,F$, that is,
$$
F_{\min} = \frac{m g}{f_s} = \frac{2 \cdot 9.81}{0.40} = 49.05\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $7.85\,\mathrm{N}$ is the product $f_s\,m g$, which would be obtained by exchanging the roles of the weight and of the pressing force.
