In the block and tackle in the figure an inextensible string is fixed to the ceiling, goes down, passes under the movable pulley from which the load hangs, goes up, passes over the fixed pulley and ends with the free end; the segments of string are vertical. By how much does the load rise when the free end is lowered by $0.8\,\mathrm{m}$?

::struttura{linee="-0.3,4 3,4;0.5,4 0.5,1.6;1.3,1.6 1.3,3.4;2.1,3.4 2.1,1.1;0.9,1.6 0.9,0.75;1.7,3.4 1.7,4" cerchi="0.9,1.6,0.4;1.7,3.4,0.4" poligoni="0.5,0.75 1.3,0.75 1.3,0.1 0.5,0.1" frecce="2.5,1.6,2.5,0.8,0.8 m" titolo="Block and tackle with one movable pulley and one fixed pulley"}

- $0.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The heights are measured downward from the ceiling: $y_M$ is the height of the center of the movable pulley and $y_F$ that of the free end. The two segments that support the movable pulley each have length $y_M$ up to constants, hence the inextensibility of the string gives the holonomic constraint
$$
2\,y_M + y_F = \text{constant} .
$$
It follows that $2\,\Delta y_M + \Delta y_F = 0$, and with $\Delta y_F = 0.8\,\mathrm{m}$ one obtains $\Delta y_M = -0.4\,\mathrm{m}$. The load therefore rises by $0.4\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, by half the displacement of the free end, because the shortening of the string is shared between the two segments of the movable pulley.
