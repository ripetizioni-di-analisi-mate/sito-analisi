A rigid rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ has the end $A$ on a fixed vertical guide and the end $B$ on a fixed horizontal guide, and the two guides meet at $O$. Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle that the rod forms with the horizontal guide, what is the magnitude of the velocity of the midpoint $G$ of the rod?

- $\ell\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$
- $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,|\dot{\vartheta}|\,|\sin\vartheta|$
- $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$
- $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,|\dot{\vartheta}|\,\sqrt{1 + 3\sin^2\vartheta}$

## Explanation

With the end $A$ on the vertical guide and the end $B$ on the horizontal one, $A - O = \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $B - O = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath}$, therefore the midpoint satisfies
$$
G - O = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big) .
$$
Differentiating with respect to time one obtains $\vec{v}_G = \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}\,( -\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} )$, and the vector $-\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$ has magnitude $1$, hence
$$
|\vec{v}_G| = \frac{\ell}{2}\,|\dot{\vartheta}| .
$$
The result agrees with the equality $|G - O| = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$, which holds for every $\vartheta$: the midpoint travels along a circle of center $O$, therefore the magnitude of its velocity is the radius times the magnitude of the angular velocity.
