A body of mass $m = 10\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a plane inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal, with static friction coefficient $f_s = 0.30$, and would slide down if left free. A force parallel to the plane and directed up the plane holds it. Taking $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the minimum value of the force compatible with equilibrium?

- $23.56\,\mathrm{N}$
- $29.43\,\mathrm{N}$
- $49.05\,\mathrm{N}$
- $74.54\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

With the force $F$ at its minimum value the body is at the limit of sliding down, therefore the friction force is directed up the plane and has magnitude $f_s\,\Phi_n$. Equilibrium in the normal direction gives $\Phi_n = m g \cos\alpha$, and equilibrium along the plane gives
$$
F + f_s\,m g \cos\alpha = m g \sin\alpha ,
$$
hence
$$
F_{\min} = m g\,( \sin\alpha - f_s \cos\alpha ) = 98.1 \cdot ( 0.5 - 0.2598 ) = 23.56\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $49.05\,\mathrm{N}$ neglects friction, and the value $74.54\,\mathrm{N}$ is the force beyond which the body starts to move up, because in that case friction is directed downward.
