A disk of radius $R$ rolls without slipping on the fixed line $y = 0$ of its plane; its center $C$ has abscissa $x_C$ and $\vartheta$ is the rotation angle. For which reason is the constraint holonomic, although it is imposed on the velocities?

- Because the velocity of the contact point is zero in every admissible state of motion
- Because the contact between the disk and the guide takes place at a single point
- Because the condition $\dot{x}_C + R\,\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ is the derivative of the function $x_C + R\,\vartheta$, which therefore stays constant along the motion
- Because the disk has a single degree of freedom

## Explanation

A constraint on the velocities is holonomic when it is reducible to a relation between the coordinates alone and time. The left-hand side of the condition of pure rolling is the derivative with respect to time of the function $x_C + R\,\vartheta$, hence the condition holds at every instant if and only if
$$
x_C + R\,\vartheta = \text{constant} ,
$$
which is a relation between the coordinates alone. The constraint is therefore holonomic, and the disk has one degree of freedom with Lagrangian coordinate $x_C$ or $\vartheta$. The vanishing of the velocity of the contact point is the way in which the condition is obtained, whereas the integrability follows from the form of the left-hand side.
