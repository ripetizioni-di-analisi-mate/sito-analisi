A rigid rod $AB$ of length $\ell = a + b$ has the end $A$ on the vertical guide $x = 0$ and the end $B$ on the horizontal guide $y = 0$, as in the figure. The point $P$ of the rod is at distance $a$ from $A$ and $b$ from $B$. Which curve does $P$ describe?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,3;B,4,0;P,1.6,1.8,-" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,180;B,carrello,270" linee="0,-0.4 0,3.8;-0.4,0 5,0" punti="1.6,1.8,P,pieno" quote="A,P,a,-0.5;P,B,b,-0.5" titolo="Rod with its ends on two orthogonal guides and point $P$ dividing it into the segments $a$ and $b$"}

- $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{b^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{a^2} = 1$
- $x^2 + y^2 = \Big( \dfrac{a + b}{2} \Big)^2$
- $x^2 + y^2 = a\,b$

## Explanation

Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle that the rod forms with the horizontal guide, one has $A - O = \ell \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $B - O = \ell \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath}$. The point $P$ is obtained from $A$ by covering the fraction $\dfrac{a}{\ell}$ of the vector $B - A$, therefore
$$
P - O = (A - O) + \frac{a}{\ell}\,(B - A) = a \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + b \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
where $\ell - a = b$ has been used. The coordinates of $P$ hence satisfy $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = \cos^2\vartheta + \sin^2\vartheta = 1$, that is, $P$ describes an ellipse with the semi-axis $a$ along the horizontal guide. The mechanism is called the trammel of Archimedes, and for $a = b = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$ the ellipse becomes the circle described by the midpoint.
