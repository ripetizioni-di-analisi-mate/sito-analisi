A ladder, regarded as a homogeneous rod of mass $m$, rests with the foot $A$ on a rough horizontal floor, with static friction coefficient $f_s = 0.40$, and with the top $B$ on a smooth vertical wall, as in the figure. What is the minimum angle $\alpha$ between the ladder and the floor compatible with equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.29,3.28" aste="A-B" linee="-0.6,0 3.1,0;2.29,-0.1 2.29,3.9" punti="1.15,1.64,G,baricentro" etichette="0.75,0.2,α" titolo="Ladder between a rough floor and a smooth wall"}

- $21.80^\circ$
- $38.66^\circ$
- $68.20^\circ$
- $51.34^\circ$

## Explanation

The smooth wall exerts at $B$ only the horizontal reaction $\Phi_B$, whereas the floor exerts at $A$ the normal component $\Phi_{An}$ and the friction component $\Phi_{At}$. The cardinal equations of statics, with the moment about $A$, give
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{At} = \Phi_B \\[4pt]
\Phi_{An} = m g \\[4pt]
\Phi_B\,\ell \sin\alpha = m g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2} \cos\alpha
\end{cases}
$$
hence $\Phi_{At} = \dfrac{m g}{2 \tan\alpha}$. The Coulomb-Morin law requires $\Phi_{At} \leq f_s\,\Phi_{An}$, that is, $\tan\alpha \geq \dfrac{1}{2 f_s} = 1.25$, therefore the minimum angle is $\alpha = \arctan 1.25 = 51.34^\circ$. The value $68.20^\circ$ would be obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the lever arm of the weight, which is applied at the midpoint of the ladder.
