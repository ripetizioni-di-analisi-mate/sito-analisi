A body of mass $m = 5\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a plane inclined at $20^\circ$ to the horizontal, with static friction coefficient $f_s = 0.60$, and the only active forces are the weight and the reaction of the plane. Taking $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the magnitude of the friction force?

- $27.66\,\mathrm{N}$
- $29.43\,\mathrm{N}$
- $46.09\,\mathrm{N}$
- $16.78\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

One has $\tan 20^\circ = 0.36 < f_s$, therefore the Coulomb-Morin law is satisfied and the body remains at rest. At equilibrium the reaction is opposite to the weight, hence the friction component equals the component of the weight along the plane:
$$
|\vec{\Phi}_t| = m g \sin\alpha = 5 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \sin 20^\circ = 16.78\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $f_s\,m g \cos\alpha = 27.66\,\mathrm{N}$ is the maximum that friction can reach on this plane, and friction reaches it only at the limit of slipping. The Coulomb-Morin law is an inequality, so the actual friction force follows from the equilibrium equations.
