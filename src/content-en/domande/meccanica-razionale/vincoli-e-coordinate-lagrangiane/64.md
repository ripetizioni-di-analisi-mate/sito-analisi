A material point $P$ is constrained to the helix of parametric equations $x = R \cos\varphi$, $y = R \sin\varphi$, $z = \dfrac{p}{2\pi}\,\varphi$, with assigned radius $R$ and pitch $p$, and $\varphi$ is taken as Lagrangian coordinate. What is the magnitude of the velocity of $P$?

- $R\,|\dot{\varphi}|$
- $\sqrt{R^2 + p^2}\,|\dot{\varphi}|$
- $\sqrt{R^2 + \dfrac{p^2}{4\pi^2}}\,|\dot{\varphi}|$
- $\Big( R + \dfrac{p}{2\pi} \Big)\,|\dot{\varphi}|$

## Explanation

Differentiating the position with respect to time one obtains
$$
\vec{v}_P = \dot{\varphi}\,\Big( -R \sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + R \cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} + \frac{p}{2\pi}\,\hat{k} \Big) .
$$
The vector that multiplies $\dot{\varphi}$ has magnitude $\sqrt{R^2 + \dfrac{p^2}{4\pi^2}}$, constant along the curve, hence
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = \sqrt{R^2 + \frac{p^2}{4\pi^2}}\,|\dot{\varphi}| .
$$
The term $\dfrac{p}{2\pi}$ is the advance along the axis per radian, therefore the value with $p^2$ in place of $\dfrac{p^2}{4\pi^2}$ counts a whole pitch for each radian. The sum $R + \dfrac{p}{2\pi}$ instead adds the magnitudes of two orthogonal components.
