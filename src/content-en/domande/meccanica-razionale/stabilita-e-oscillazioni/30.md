Two point particles, of mass $m$ each, slide without friction on two fixed parallel vertical straight guides, placed at distance $d$. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects the two particles. How many equilibrium configurations does the system admit?

- One
- Two
- None
- Infinitely many

## Explanation

Calling $y_1$ and $y_2$ the heights of the two particles, the spring has length $\sqrt{d^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}$, therefore the potential energy of the system is
$$
V(y_1, y_2) = m g\,(y_1 + y_2) + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\big( d^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2 \big) .
$$
The two partial derivatives equal $\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial y_1} = m g + k\,(y_1 - y_2)$ and $\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial y_2} = m g - k\,(y_1 - y_2)$, and their sum equals $2 m g$, which is not zero. The gradient of the potential energy therefore does not vanish in any configuration, hence the system admits no equilibrium configuration. The two forces that the spring exerts on the particles are opposite, therefore their sum is zero and it cannot balance the total weight.
