A uniform disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed inclined plane, which forms the angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal. At the point $D$ of the rim diametrically opposite to the contact point $H$ a force $\vec{F}$ is applied, parallel to the plane and directed up the plane, as the diagram in the figure shows. What magnitude of $\vec{F}$ keeps the disk in equilibrium?

::struttura{cerchi="2.687,2.126,0.7,foro" linee="0,0 6,3;0,0 6,0;6,0 6,3" punti="2.687,2.126,C,baricentro;3,1.5,H,centro;2.374,2.752,D,pieno" forze="3.174 3.152,0.8,0.4," etichette="0.8,0.15,α;3.35,3.45,F" titolo="The disk on the inclined plane, with the force $\vec{F}$ applied at the point $D$"}

- $\dfrac{m g \sin\alpha}{3}$
- $\dfrac{m g \sin\alpha}{2}$
- $m g \sin\alpha$
- $2\,m g \sin\alpha$

## Explanation

We take as Lagrangian coordinate the abscissa $s$ of the center $C$ along the plane, positive downward. By the condition of pure rolling the point $H$ has zero velocity, hence the point $D$, which lies at distance $2R$ from $H$, has velocity $2\dot{s}$ parallel to the plane, and its virtual displacement equals $2\,\delta s$. The weight does the virtual work $m g \sin\alpha\,\delta s$, the force $\vec{F}$, directed up the plane, does the work $-2F\,\delta s$, and the reaction of the plane does no work, since the rolling constraint is ideal. It follows that
$$
Q_s = m g \sin\alpha - 2F ,
$$
and the configuration is one of equilibrium if and only if $Q_s = 0$, that is, for $F = \dfrac{m g \sin\alpha}{2}$.
