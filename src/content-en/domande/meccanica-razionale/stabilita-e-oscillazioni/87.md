The small damped oscillations of a system with one degree of freedom are described by the equation $\ddot{x} + 2\omega_0\,\dot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = 0$, with $\omega_0 > 0$, that is, with critical damping. The motion starts with $x(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = 0$. Which law describes the motion?

- $x(t) = x_0\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$
- $x(t) = x_0\,(1 + \omega_0 t)\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$
- $x(t) = x_0\cos(\omega_0 t)\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$
- $x(t) = x_0\,(1 - \omega_0 t)\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation $s^2 + 2\omega_0 s + \omega_0^2 = 0$ has the double root $s = -\omega_0$, hence the general solution is $x(t) = (C_1 + C_2 t)\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$, and the condition $x(0) = x_0$ gives $C_1 = x_0$. Since $\dot{x}(t) = \big( C_2 - \omega_0 C_1 - \omega_0 C_2 t \big)\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$, the condition $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ gives $C_2 = \omega_0 x_0$. Therefore
$$
x(t) = x_0\,(1 + \omega_0 t)\,e^{-\omega_0 t} ,
$$
which tends to zero without changing sign.
