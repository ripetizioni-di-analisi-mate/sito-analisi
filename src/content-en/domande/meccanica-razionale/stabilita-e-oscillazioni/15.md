A uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and moves in a vertical plane, subject to the weight alone. Calling $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, what is the potential $U$ of the weight, with the convention $\vec{F} = \operatorname{grad} U$?

- $U = -\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$
- $U = m g \ell\cos\vartheta$
- $U = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$
- $U = -\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$

## Explanation

With the origin at $A$ and the $y$-axis vertical upward, the center of mass of the rod lies at the midpoint, hence it has height $y_G = -\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$. With the convention $\vec{F} = \operatorname{grad} U$ the potential of the weight of a body of mass $m$ and center of mass $G$ is $U = -m g\,y_G$, hence
$$
U(\vartheta) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta .
$$
The potential energy is the opposite function, that is, $V(\vartheta) = -\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, and it has a minimum at $\vartheta = 0$, where the center of mass is lowest.
