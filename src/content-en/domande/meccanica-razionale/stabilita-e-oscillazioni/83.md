Two equal pendulums, connected by a weak spring, have in their small oscillations the in-phase mode $u_1 = (1, 1)$, with angular frequency $\omega_1 = 3.00\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and the antiphase mode $u_2 = (1, -1)$, with angular frequency $\omega_2 = 3.20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The first pendulum is displaced while the second one is in the equilibrium configuration, and both start with zero velocity. After how long does the amplitude of the oscillation of the first pendulum vanish for the first time?

- $31.42\,\mathrm{s}$
- $15.71\,\mathrm{s}$
- $7.85\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.01\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

With $x(0) = (c, 0)$ and kinetic matrix proportional to the identity, each of the two modes has amplitude $\dfrac{c}{2}$, hence
$$
x_1(t) = \frac{c}{2}\,\big( \cos\omega_1 t + \cos\omega_2 t \big) = c\cos\frac{(\omega_2 - \omega_1)\,t}{2}\,\cos\frac{(\omega_1 + \omega_2)\,t}{2} .
$$
The first pendulum oscillates with angular frequency $\dfrac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2}$ and amplitude $c\left| \cos\dfrac{(\omega_2 - \omega_1)\,t}{2} \right|$, which vanishes for the first time when $\dfrac{(\omega_2 - \omega_1)\,t}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Therefore
$$
t = \frac{\pi}{\omega_2 - \omega_1} = \frac{\pi}{0.20}\,\mathrm{s} \simeq 15.71\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
At that instant $x_2(t) = c\sin\dfrac{(\omega_2 - \omega_1)\,t}{2}\,\sin\dfrac{(\omega_1 + \omega_2)\,t}{2}$ has amplitude $c$, and the oscillation has passed entirely to the second pendulum.
