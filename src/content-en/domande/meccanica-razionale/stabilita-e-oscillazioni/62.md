A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ moves without friction in a horizontal plane. Two equal ideal springs, of stiffness $k$ and natural length $\dfrac{d}{2}$, connect $P$ to the fixed points $A = (-d, 0)$ and $B = (d, 0)$, so that in the equilibrium configuration $P = (0, 0)$ both springs are stretched. What is the square of the angular frequency of the small oscillations of $P$ in the direction perpendicular to $AB$?

- $\dfrac{2k}{m}$
- $\dfrac{k}{m}$
- $\dfrac{3k}{2m}$
- $\dfrac{k}{2m}$

## Explanation

The spring attached at $A$ has potential energy $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\left( \overline{AP} - \dfrac{d}{2} \right)^2$, with $\overline{AP} = \sqrt{(x + d)^2 + y^2}$. For $x = 0$ and small $y$ we have $\overline{AP} = d + \dfrac{y^2}{2d} + \cdots$, hence the elongation equals $\dfrac{d}{2} + \dfrac{y^2}{2d}$ and the expansion of the potential energy contains the term $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{k}{2}\,y^2$. The spring attached at $B$ gives the same contribution, therefore the transverse stiffness equals $V_{yy} = k$, while along $AB$ it equals $V_{xx} = 2k$. The kinetic matrix is $m$ times the identity and the Hessian is diagonal, hence the transverse motion is a normal mode with
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{k}{m} .
$$
