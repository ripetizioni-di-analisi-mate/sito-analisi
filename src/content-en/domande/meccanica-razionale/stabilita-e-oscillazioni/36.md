A compound pendulum of mass $m$ rotates in the vertical plane about the fixed horizontal axis through $O$, with respect to which it has moment of inertia $I_O$, and its center of mass $G$ is at distance $d$ from $O$. Calling $\vartheta$ the angle between $G - O$ and the upward vertical, a torsional spring of stiffness $k_t$ exerts on the pendulum the couple $-\,k_t\,\vartheta$, which vanishes when $G$ is on the vertical above $O$. For which values of $k_t$ does the pendulum perform small oscillations about $\vartheta = 0$?

- $0 < k_t < m g d$
- $k_t > m g d$
- $k_t = m g d$
- For every $k_t > 0$

## Explanation

The height of $G$ above $O$ equals $d\cos\vartheta$, hence the potential energy of the weight and of the spring is $V(\vartheta) = m g d\cos\vartheta + \dfrac{1}{2}\,k_t\,\vartheta^2$. Since $V'(0) = 0$, the configuration $\vartheta = 0$ is an equilibrium, and the second derivative equals $V''(0) = k_t - m g d$. If $k_t > m g d$, then $\vartheta = 0$ is a strict minimum point of $V$, and by the **Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem** the equilibrium is stable. The linearized equation is $I_O\,\ddot{\vartheta} + (k_t - m g d)\,\vartheta = 0$, therefore the pendulum oscillates with angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{k_t - m g d}{I_O}}$. For $k_t < m g d$ the coefficient is negative and the solution moves away from $\vartheta = 0$, while for $k_t = m g d$ the linearized equation reduces to $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$ and does not describe oscillations.
