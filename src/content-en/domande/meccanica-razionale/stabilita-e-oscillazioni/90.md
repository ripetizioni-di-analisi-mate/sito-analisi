A point particle of mass $m$ slides on a horizontal guide, connected to an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and to a viscous damper that exerts the force $-h\,\dot{x}$, with $h > 0$, where $x$ is the displacement from the equilibrium configuration. What is the time derivative of the mechanical energy $E = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$ along the motion?

- $0$
- $-h\,x\,\dot{x}$
- $-h\,\dot{x}^2$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,h\,\dot{x}^2$

## Explanation

The equation of motion is $m\ddot{x} + h\dot{x} + k x = 0$. Differentiating the energy one obtains
$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = m\dot{x}\,\ddot{x} + k x\,\dot{x} = \dot{x}\,\big( m\ddot{x} + k x \big) ,
$$
and from the equation of motion $m\ddot{x} + k x = -h\dot{x}$, therefore
$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = -h\,\dot{x}^2 \le 0 .
$$
The mechanical energy decreases at every instant in which the particle moves, and for this reason the motions about the equilibrium configuration are damped out.
