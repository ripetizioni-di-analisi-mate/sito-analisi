A conservative holonomic system with two degrees of freedom has kinetic matrix $\mathbb{A} = m\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and normal modes $u_1 = (1, 1)$ and $u_2 = (2, -1)$, with natural angular frequencies $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$. The motion starts from $x(0) = (c, 0)$ with $\dot{x}(0) = 0$, hence it is $x(t) = \xi_1(0)\,u_1\cos(\omega_1 t) + \xi_2(0)\,u_2\cos(\omega_2 t)$. What is $\xi_1(0)$?

- $\dfrac{c}{2}$
- $c$
- $\dfrac{c}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2c}{3}$

## Explanation

The two modes are orthogonal with respect to $\mathbb{A}$, since $u_1 \cdot \mathbb{A}\,u_2 = m\,\big( 1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot (-1) \big) = 0$. Taking the scalar product of $x(0) = \xi_1(0)\,u_1 + \xi_2(0)\,u_2$ with $\mathbb{A}\,u_1$, only the term of the first mode remains, hence
$$
\xi_1(0) = \frac{u_1 \cdot \mathbb{A}\,x(0)}{u_1 \cdot \mathbb{A}\,u_1} = \frac{m c}{3m} = \frac{c}{3} .
$$
In the same way $\xi_2(0) = \dfrac{2mc}{6m} = \dfrac{c}{3}$, and indeed $\dfrac{c}{3}\,(1, 1) + \dfrac{c}{3}\,(2, -1) = (c, 0)$.
