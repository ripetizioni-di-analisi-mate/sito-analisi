A point particle of mass $m$ moves on the inner surface of a smooth fixed spherical bowl of radius $R$, concave upward, subject to its weight only. What holds for the normal modes of the small oscillations about the lowest point?

- There are two modes, with angular frequencies $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{R}}$ and $\sqrt{\dfrac{2g}{R}}$
- Only the displacements along two fixed horizontal directions are normal modes
- There are no normal modes, since the two natural angular frequencies coincide
- Every horizontal direction identifies a normal mode, with angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{R}}$

## Explanation

We take as coordinates the horizontal components $x$ and $y$ of the displacement from the lowest point. Near the bottom the height equals $z = \dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{2R} + \cdots$ and the vertical velocity is of higher order, hence
$$
\mathbb{A} = m
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} ,
\qquad
\mathbb{V} = \frac{m g}{R}
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} .
$$
The characteristic equation $\left( \dfrac{m g}{R} - m\omega^2 \right)^2 = 0$ has the double root $\omega^2 = \dfrac{g}{R}$. For this value the matrix $\mathbb{V} - \omega^2\mathbb{A}$ is zero, therefore every vector $u \neq 0$ solves $(\mathbb{V} - \omega^2\mathbb{A})\,u = 0$, and every initial horizontal displacement with zero velocity produces a straight-line oscillation of angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{R}}$.
