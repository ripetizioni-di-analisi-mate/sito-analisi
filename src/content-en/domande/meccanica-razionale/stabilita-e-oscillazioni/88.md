A uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and moves in a vertical plane; with $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, the hinge exerts a resisting couple $-c\,\dot{\vartheta}$, with $c > 0$. How is the equilibrium configuration with $B$ above $A$ classified?

- Asymptotically stable, since the dissipation makes the motions tend to the equilibrium
- Stable, and not asymptotically stable
- Stable if $c$ exceeds the critical damping
- Unstable, for every value of $c$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta = \pi + x$ the linearized equation about the configuration is
$$
\frac{m\ell^2}{3}\,\ddot{x} + c\,\dot{x} - \frac{m g \ell}{2}\,x = 0 .
$$
The product of the roots of the characteristic equation $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}\,s^2 + c\,s - \dfrac{m g \ell}{2} = 0$ equals $-\dfrac{3g}{2\ell} < 0$, hence the two roots are real and of opposite sign. The solution therefore contains a growing exponential, and the configuration is **unstable** for every $c > 0$. Dissipation makes the minima of the potential energy asymptotically stable, while the maxima remain unstable.
