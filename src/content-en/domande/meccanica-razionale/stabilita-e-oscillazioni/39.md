A point particle of mass $m$ is constrained to the smooth fixed guide of equation $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, with $a > 0$, placed in a vertical plane with $y$ vertical upward; the weight alone acts on the particle. What is the angular frequency of the small oscillations about $x = 0$?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{a}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2g}{a}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2a}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{a}}$

## Explanation

Taking $x$ as the Lagrangian coordinate, the potential energy is $V(x) = \dfrac{m g}{2a}\,x^2$, hence $V'(0) = 0$ and $V''(0) = \dfrac{m g}{a} > 0$, therefore $x = 0$ is a stable equilibrium configuration. The kinetic energy is
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\left( 1 + \frac{x^2}{a^2} \right)\dot{x}^2 ,
$$
and in the equilibrium configuration the inertia coefficient equals $m$. Then $\omega^2 = \dfrac{g}{a}$, that is, the particle oscillates like a simple pendulum of length $a$, which is the radius of curvature of the guide at the vertex.
