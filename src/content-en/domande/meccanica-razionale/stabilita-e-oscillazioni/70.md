A simple pendulum, formed by a point particle of mass $m$ and by a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$, is hinged to the ceiling of a railway car that moves on a straight horizontal track with constant acceleration of magnitude $a$. In the frame of the car, what is the square of the angular frequency of the small oscillations about the stable equilibrium configuration?

- $\dfrac{g}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{g + a}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{g^2 + a^2}}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{g^2 - a^2}}{\ell}$

## Explanation

The frame of the car translates with constant acceleration $\vec{a}$, hence besides the weight the constant apparent force $-m\,\vec{a}$ acts on the particle, and it is conservative. The two constant forces add up to an effective weight $m\,\vec{g}_{\mathrm{eff}}$, with $|\vec{g}_{\mathrm{eff}}| = \sqrt{g^2 + a^2}$, and the suspension point is fixed in the car. With $\vartheta$ the angle measured from the direction of $\vec{g}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the potential energy equals $V = -m\,\ell\,\sqrt{g^2 + a^2}\,\cos\vartheta$, hence $V''(0) = m\ell\sqrt{g^2 + a^2}$, and with the inertia coefficient $m\ell^2$ we obtain
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{\sqrt{g^2 + a^2}}{\ell} .
$$
In the equilibrium configuration the rod forms the angle $\arctan\dfrac{a}{g}$ with the vertical.
