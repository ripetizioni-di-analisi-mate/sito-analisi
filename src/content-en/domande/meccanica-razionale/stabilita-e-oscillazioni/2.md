A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to a smooth fixed circular guide, of center $O$ and radius $R$, placed in a vertical plane. Besides the weight, the constant horizontal force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, acts on it. Calling $\vartheta$ the angle between $OP$ and the downward vertical, measured in the direction of $\vec{F}$, which configuration is one of stable equilibrium?

- $\vartheta = \arctan\dfrac{F}{m g}$
- $\vartheta = \arctan\dfrac{m g}{F}$
- $\vartheta = \pi + \arctan\dfrac{F}{m g}$
- $\vartheta = -\arctan\dfrac{F}{m g}$

## Explanation

With the origin at $O$ the particle has coordinates $x_P = R\sin\vartheta$ and $y_P = -R\cos\vartheta$, therefore the potential of the two constant forces is $U(\vartheta) = m g R\cos\vartheta + F R \sin\vartheta$. From the condition $U'(\vartheta) = R\,(F\cos\vartheta - m g \sin\vartheta) = 0$ it follows that $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{F}{m g}$, hence the equilibrium configurations are $\vartheta_1 = \arctan\dfrac{F}{m g}$ and $\vartheta_2 = \vartheta_1 + \pi$. Since $U''(\vartheta_1) = -R\sqrt{F^2 + m^2 g^2} < 0$, at $\vartheta_1$ the potential energy $V = -U$ has a strict minimum and the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem applies. The stable configuration is therefore $\vartheta = \arctan\dfrac{F}{m g}$, with the particle displaced toward the side in which $\vec{F}$ acts.
