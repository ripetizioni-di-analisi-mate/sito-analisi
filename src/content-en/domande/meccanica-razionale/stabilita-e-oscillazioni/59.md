A balloon, modeled as a point particle $P$ of mass $m$, is connected to the fixed point $O$ by a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$, hinged at $O$, and moves in a vertical plane. Besides the weight, the buoyancy of the air acts on the particle, a constant vertical upward force of magnitude $F > m g$. With $\vartheta$ the angle between $OP$ and the downward vertical, which configuration is one of stable equilibrium?

- $\vartheta = 0$, with $P$ below $O$
- Both vertical configurations
- No configuration
- $\vartheta = \pi$, with $P$ above $O$

## Explanation

The height of $P$ with respect to $O$ is $-\ell\cos\vartheta$ and the resultant of the two forces is vertical and upward, of magnitude $F - m g$, hence the potential energy equals
$$
V(\vartheta) = (F - m g)\,\ell\cos\vartheta .
$$
We have $V'(\vartheta) = -(F - m g)\,\ell\sin\vartheta$, which vanishes at $\vartheta = 0$ and at $\vartheta = \pi$, and $V''(\vartheta) = -(F - m g)\,\ell\cos\vartheta$. Since $F > m g$, we get $V''(0) < 0$ and $V''(\pi) > 0$; therefore the **stable** configuration is $\vartheta = \pi$, with the balloon above the attachment point.
