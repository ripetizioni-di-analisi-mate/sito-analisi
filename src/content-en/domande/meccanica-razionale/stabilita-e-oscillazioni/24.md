In the proof of the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem one considers a motion of mechanical energy $E$ smaller than the minimum $\mu$ of $V$ on the boundary $|q - q_0| = \varepsilon$, with $V(q_0) = 0$. Why does that motion not reach the boundary?

- Since $T \ge 0$, hence $V(q(t)) \le E < \mu$ at every instant
- Since the kinetic energy is conserved along the motion
- Since the boundary is a constraint of the system
- Since $V$ is increasing along the motion

## Explanation

The mechanical energy is conserved, therefore $T(t) + V(q(t)) = E$ at every instant. The kinetic energy is a positive definite quadratic form in the generalized velocities, hence $T \ge 0$ and it follows that $V(q(t)) \le E$. If the motion reached the boundary at an instant $t_1$, we would have $V(q(t_1)) \ge \mu > E$, against the inequality just obtained; therefore the boundary is not reached and the motion stays confined in the potential well.
