The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is supported in a vertical plane by two vertical ideal springs attached to its ends and to the ground: the one at $A$ has stiffness $k$, the one at $B$ has stiffness $2k$. The rod can translate vertically and rotate through small angles, as the diagram in the figure shows. Calling $\vartheta$ the counterclockwise rotation measured from the configuration with the springs unloaded, what is $\vartheta$ in the equilibrium configuration?

::struttura{nodi="A,0.6,2;B,4.4,2" aste="A-B" linee="0.60,2.00 0.82,1.65 0.38,1.52 0.82,1.39 0.38,1.26 0.82,1.14 0.38,1.01 0.82,0.88 0.38,0.75 0.60,0.40;4.40,2.00 4.62,1.65 4.18,1.52 4.62,1.39 4.18,1.26 4.62,1.14 4.18,1.01 4.62,0.88 4.18,0.75 4.40,0.40;0,0.4 5,0.4" punti="2.5,2,G,baricentro" frecce="2.5,2.5,2.5,3.1,y" etichette="1,1.2,k;4.85,1.2,2k;2.9,1.75,ϑ" titolo="The rod resting on the two springs of stiffness $k$ and $2k$"}

- $\dfrac{m g}{8 k \ell}$
- $\dfrac{3 m g}{8 k \ell}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{4 k \ell}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{2 k \ell}$

## Explanation

Calling $y$ the height of the center of mass measured from the configuration with the springs unloaded, for small angles the ends lower by $y - \dfrac{\ell \vartheta}{2}$ and $y + \dfrac{\ell \vartheta}{2}$, therefore
$$
V(y, \vartheta) = \frac{1}{2}\,k\left( y - \frac{\ell \vartheta}{2} \right)^2 + k\left( y + \frac{\ell \vartheta}{2} \right)^2 + m g y .
$$
The two equilibrium equations are
$$
\begin{cases}
3 k y + \dfrac{k \ell \vartheta}{2} + m g = 0 \\[6pt]
k y + \dfrac{3}{2}\,k \ell \vartheta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the second it follows that $y = -\dfrac{3}{2}\,\ell \vartheta$, and substituting into the first one obtains $-4 k \ell \vartheta + m g = 0$, hence $\vartheta = \dfrac{m g}{4 k \ell}$ and $y = -\dfrac{3 m g}{8 k}$. The rod tilts toward the end $A$, where the spring is more compliant.
