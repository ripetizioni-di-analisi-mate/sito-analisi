In an equilibrium configuration of a conservative holonomic system with fixed, bilateral and ideal constraints, the Hessian matrix of the potential energy admits a negative eigenvalue. What is concluded?

- Nothing, since the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem gives only a sufficient condition
- The equilibrium is unstable
- The equilibrium is stable if the other eigenvalues are positive
- The equilibrium is stable

## Explanation

If the Hessian matrix admits a negative eigenvalue, then there exists a direction along which the potential energy decreases to second order, therefore the configuration is not a minimum of $V$. The Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem does not apply, while the Lyapunov instability theorems ensure that the equilibrium is unstable. From the sign of the Hessian only the case in which the matrix is semidefinite and singular remains undecided.
