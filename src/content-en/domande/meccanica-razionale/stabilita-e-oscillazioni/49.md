The beam of a balance is a rigid body of mass $M$ that rotates without friction about the fixed fulcrum $O$, in a vertical plane; when the beam is horizontal, its center of mass $G$ lies at distance $h$ below $O$. Two pans, of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ with $m_1 > m_2$, are hung from the points $A$ and $B$ of the beam, aligned with $O$ on the perpendicular to $OG$ and placed at distance $a$ from $O$ on opposite sides, as the diagram in the figure shows. With $\vartheta$ the angle by which the beam rotates, positive when $A$ goes down, which condition identifies the equilibrium configuration?

::struttura{nodi="A,-2.5,2;B,2.5,2;O,0,2;G,0,1.4,-;I,0,0.9,-;P,-2.5,0.6,-;Q,2.5,0.6,-" aste="A-B;O-I" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" catene="A-P;B-Q" punti="0,1.4,G,baricentro;-2.5,0.6,m_1,pieno;2.5,0.6,m_2,pieno" quote="A,O,a,-0.5;O,B,a,-0.5;O,G,h,0.5" titolo="The beam of the balance with the fulcrum $O$, the center of mass $G$ below $O$ and the two pans hung at $A$ and $B$"}

- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{(m_1 - m_2)\,h}{M a}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{(m_1 + m_2)\,a}{M h}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{(m_1 - m_2)\,a}{M h}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{(m_1 - m_2)\,a}{(M + m_1 + m_2)\,h}$

## Explanation

Each pan hangs vertically, hence its height differs from that of its suspension point by a constant, and its potential is that of a point particle placed at the suspension point. After the rotation $\vartheta$, with respect to $O$, the point $A$ has height $-a\sin\vartheta$, the point $B$ has height $a\sin\vartheta$ and the center of mass of the beam has height $-h\cos\vartheta$. The potential is therefore
$$
U(\vartheta) = (m_1 - m_2)\,g a \sin\vartheta + M g h \cos\vartheta ,
$$
and the equilibrium condition $U'(\vartheta) = 0$ reads $(m_1 - m_2)\,g a\cos\vartheta = M g h \sin\vartheta$. Therefore
$$
\tan\vartheta = \frac{(m_1 - m_2)\,a}{M h} .
$$
For a given difference between the masses, the rotation is larger the closer $G$ is to the fulcrum, and the sensitivity of the balance depends on this.
