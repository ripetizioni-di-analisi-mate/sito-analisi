A conservative holonomic system with two degrees of freedom has kinetic matrix $\mathbb{A} = m\,\mathbb{I}$ and normal modes $u_1 = (1, 1)$ and $u_2 = (1, -1)$, with natural angular frequencies $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$. The motion starts with $x(0) = (c, c)$ and $\dot{x}(0) = 0$. Which motion follows?

- $x(t) = c\,(1, 1)\cos(\omega_1 t)$
- $x(t) = c\,(1, 1)\cos(\omega_2 t)$
- $x(t) = \dfrac{c}{2}\,(1, 1)\cos(\omega_1 t) + \dfrac{c}{2}\,(1, -1)\cos(\omega_2 t)$
- $x(t) = c\,(1, -1)\cos(\omega_1 t)$

## Explanation

The general motion is the superposition of the two modes, that is, $x(t) = c_1\,u_1\cos(\omega_1 t + \varphi_1) + c_2\,u_2\cos(\omega_2 t + \varphi_2)$, and from the datum $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ it follows that the two phases are zero. Since $u_1 \cdot \mathbb{A}\,u_2 = m\,(1 - 1) = 0$, the two eigenvectors are orthogonal with respect to $\mathbb{A}$, hence the amplitudes are obtained from
$$
c_r = \frac{u_r \cdot \mathbb{A}\,x(0)}{u_r \cdot \mathbb{A}\,u_r} .
$$
From the datum $x(0) = c\,(1, 1) = c\,u_1$ it follows that $c_1 = c$ and $c_2 = 0$, therefore the motion excites the first mode alone and equals $x(t) = c\,(1, 1)\cos(\omega_1 t)$.
