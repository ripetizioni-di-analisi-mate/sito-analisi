A uniform disk of mass $m$ and radius $r$ rolls without slipping inside a fixed circular guide of radius $R > r$, placed in a vertical plane, as the diagram in the figure shows. The small oscillations about the stable equilibrium configuration have the same period as a simple pendulum. What is the length of that pendulum?

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2.6,foro;0.9,-1.559,0.8,foro" punti="0,0,O;0.9,-1.559,C,baricentro;1.3,-2.252,H,centro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.6;0,0 1.3,-2.252" etichette="0.3,-1.5,ϑ" titolo="The disk of radius $r$ rolling inside the circular guide of radius $R$"}

- $\dfrac{3\,(R - r)}{2}$
- $R - r$
- $\dfrac{2\,(R - r)}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3R}{2}$

## Explanation

Calling $\vartheta$ the angle between $OC$ and the downward vertical, the center $C$ describes a circle of radius $R - r$, therefore $v_C = (R - r)\,\dot{\vartheta}$ and the potential energy is $V(\vartheta) = -m g\,(R - r)\cos\vartheta$, with $V''(0) = m g\,(R - r) > 0$. The condition of pure rolling gives $\dot{\varphi} = \dfrac{(R - r)\,\dot{\vartheta}}{r}$ for the angular velocity of the disk, hence
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,(R - r)^2\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,m r^2 \cdot \frac{(R - r)^2\dot{\vartheta}^2}{r^2} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,(R - r)^2\dot{\vartheta}^2 .
$$
Then $\omega^2 = \dfrac{m g\,(R-r)}{\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,(R-r)^2} = \dfrac{2g}{3\,(R-r)}$, and the comparison with the angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{L}}$ of the simple pendulum gives the reduced length $L = \dfrac{3\,(R-r)}{2}$.
