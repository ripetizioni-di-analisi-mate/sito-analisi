The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and moves in a vertical plane. A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ slides without friction on the smooth horizontal guide through $A$, and it is connected to the end $B$ by an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length, as the diagram in the figure shows. Which condition on $k$ is necessary and sufficient for the system to admit equilibrium configurations with the rod oblique?

::struttura{nodi="A,2.6,1.4;B,3.69,-1.61" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" linee="1,1.4 6,1.4;3.69,-1.61 3.70,-0.86 4.26,-0.79 3.88,-0.38 4.44,-0.31 4.05,0.10 4.61,0.17 4.23,0.58 4.79,0.65 4.80,1.40" tratteggi="2.6,1.4 2.6,-0.9" punti="4.8,1.4,P,pieno" quote="A,4.8 1.4,x,-0.55" etichette="3,0.3,ϑ;4.75,-0.6,k" titolo="The rod hinged at $A$ and the spring connecting $B$ to the particle $P$ on the horizontal guide"}

- $2 k \ell > m g$
- $2 k \ell < m g$
- $k \ell > m g$
- $k \ell > 2 m g$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical and $x$ the abscissa of $P$ measured from $A$, the potential is
$$
U(\vartheta, x) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\Big[ (x - \ell\sin\vartheta)^2 + \ell^2\cos^2\vartheta \Big] .
$$
From $\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial x} = -k\,(x - \ell\sin\vartheta) = 0$ it follows that $x = \ell\sin\vartheta$, and substituting into the other equation one obtains $\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = \ell\,\sin\vartheta\left( k \ell\cos\vartheta - \dfrac{m g}{2} \right) = 0$. Besides the two vertical configurations, given by $\sin\vartheta = 0$, there exist therefore the oblique configurations with $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{m g}{2 k \ell}$, which are acceptable only when $\dfrac{m g}{2 k \ell} < 1$, that is, when $2 k \ell > m g$.
