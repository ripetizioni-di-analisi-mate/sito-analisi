A rigid rod $AB$ of negligible mass and length $\ell$ is hinged to the ground at the end $A$ and stands vertical, with $B$ at the top. A constant vertical load $P$ directed downward acts at $B$, and an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ connects $B$ to a roller that slides on a vertical guide, so that the spring stays horizontal; the spring is unloaded when the rod is vertical, as the diagram in the figure shows. What is the critical value of the load, beyond which the vertical configuration is unstable?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,3;D,2.6,3" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello,0" linee="2.9,-0.3 2.9,4;0,3 0.3,3 0.45,3.2 0.75,2.8 1.05,3.2 1.35,2.8 1.65,3.2 1.95,2.8 2.25,3.2 2.4,3 2.6,3" forze="0 3,0,-0.9,P" quote="A,B,ℓ,-0.6" etichette="1.35,3.55,k" titolo="The vertical rod loaded at $B$ by the load $P$ and held by the horizontal spring"}

- $k\ell$
- $\dfrac{k\ell}{2}$
- $2k\ell$
- $4k\ell$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the upward vertical, the spring stays horizontal and is stretched by $\ell\sin\vartheta$, while the point $B$ has height $\ell\cos\vartheta$. The potential energy is therefore
$$
V(\vartheta) = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2\sin^2\vartheta + P\,\ell\cos\vartheta ,
$$
with $V'(0) = 0$ and
$$
V''(0) = k\,\ell^2 - P\,\ell .
$$
The vertical configuration is stable for $P < k\ell$ and unstable for $P > k\ell$, therefore the **critical load** equals $P_{\mathrm{cr}} = k\ell$.
