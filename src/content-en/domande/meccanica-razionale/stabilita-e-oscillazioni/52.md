A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to a smooth fixed circular guide, of center $O$ and radius $R$, placed in a vertical plane, and it is subject to its weight only. The particle is placed at the highest point of the guide and released with exactly zero velocity. What happens in the subsequent motion?

- The particle stays at rest at the highest point
- The particle goes down, since the configuration is one of unstable equilibrium
- The particle oscillates about the highest point
- The particle travels along the guide with constant speed

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ the angle between $OP$ and the downward vertical, the potential is $U(\vartheta) = m g R\cos\vartheta$, and its derivative $U'(\vartheta) = -m g R\sin\vartheta$ vanishes at $\vartheta = \pi$; hence the highest point is an equilibrium configuration. By definition the motion that starts from an equilibrium configuration with zero velocity stays in that configuration, since the constant function $\vartheta(t) = \pi$ solves the equation of motion and the solution of the Cauchy problem is unique. Instability concerns the motions with initial data *close* to those of equilibrium and different from them, and for these a small displacement is enough to make the particle go down along the guide.
