Two point particles of mass $m$ slide without friction on a fixed horizontal guide. The first is connected to the left wall by a spring of stiffness $k$, the second to the right wall by an equal spring, and the two particles are connected to each other by a spring of stiffness $3k$, as the diagram in the figure shows. What is the square of the greater natural angular frequency?

- $\dfrac{7k}{m}$
- $\dfrac{3k}{m}$
- $\dfrac{5k}{m}$
- $\dfrac{k}{m}$

## Explanation

Calling $x_1$ and $x_2$ the abscissas of the two particles measured from the equilibrium positions, the kinetic energy gives $\mathbb{A} = m\,\mathbb{I}$, while the potential energy
$$
V = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x_2^2 + \frac{3}{2}\,k\,(x_1 - x_2)^2
$$
has Hessian matrix
$$
\mathbb{V} = k\begin{bmatrix} 4 & -3 \\ -3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} .
$$
The characteristic equation $\det\big(\mathbb{V} - \omega^2\mathbb{A}\big) = (4k - m\omega^2)^2 - 9k^2 = 0$ has the roots $\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$ and $\omega^2 = \dfrac{7k}{m}$, therefore the greater natural angular frequency satisfies $\omega^2 = \dfrac{7k}{m}$. The corresponding mode is $u = (1, -1)$, in which the two particles move in opposite directions and the central spring deforms twice as much as each displacement. In the other mode, $u = (1, 1)$, the central spring stays unloaded and the angular frequency is that of a single mass restrained by a single spring.
