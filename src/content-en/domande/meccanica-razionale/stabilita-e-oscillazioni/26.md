A point particle of mass $m$ is constrained to the smooth fixed guide of equation $y = \dfrac{x^3}{3} - x$, placed in a vertical plane with $x$ and $y$ measured in meters and $y$ vertical upward; the weight alone acts on the particle. Which equilibrium configuration is stable?

- $x = -1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 0$
- $x = \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Taking $x$ as the Lagrangian coordinate, the potential energy of the weight is $V(x) = m g\left( \dfrac{x^3}{3} - x \right)$. From $V'(x) = m g\,(x^2 - 1) = 0$ it follows that the equilibrium configurations are $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x = -1\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, the points at which the tangent to the guide is horizontal. The second derivative equals $V''(x) = 2 m g x$, hence $V''(1) = 2 m g > 0$ and $V''(-1) = -2 m g < 0$. The equilibrium is therefore stable at $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$, where the guide has a minimum, and unstable at $x = -1\,\mathrm{m}$, where it has a maximum.
