A rigid square is formed by two uniform rods welded at a right angle at the common end $O$: the rod $OA$ has mass $m$ and length $\ell$, the rod $OB$ has mass $2m$ and length $2\ell$. The square is hung from a fixed hinge at $O$ and moves in a vertical plane, with $A$ and $B$ on opposite sides of the vertical through $O$, as the diagram in the figure shows. With $\alpha$ the angle between $OA$ and the downward vertical, which condition identifies the equilibrium configuration with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,3;A,-1.06,1.94;B,2.12,0.88" aste="O-A;O-B" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,3 0,0.8" punti="-0.53,2.47,G_1,baricentro;1.06,1.94,G_2,baricentro" quote="A,O,ℓ,-0.4;O,B,2ℓ,-0.4" etichette="-0.25,2.1,α;-1.2,2.5,m;1.75,1.75,2m" titolo="The square hung at the vertex $O$, with the arm $OA$ of mass $m$ and the arm $OB$ of mass $2m$"}

- $\tan\alpha = 2$
- $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\tan\alpha = 8$
- $\tan\alpha = 4$

## Explanation

Since the angle $AOB$ is a right angle, the rod $OB$ forms the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \alpha$ with the downward vertical. The centers of mass of the two rods lie at distances $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ and $\ell$ from $O$, hence their heights with respect to $O$ are $-\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\alpha$ and $-\ell\sin\alpha$, and the potential equals
$$
U(\alpha) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\alpha + 2 m g \ell \sin\alpha .
$$
The condition $U'(\alpha) = 0$ reads $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\alpha + 2 m g \ell\cos\alpha = 0$, therefore $\tan\alpha = 4$. The short arm, which is lighter, thus forms with the vertical an angle much larger than that of the long arm.
