Two point particles of mass $m$ slide without friction on a fixed horizontal guide; each is connected to the nearest wall by an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and the two particles are connected to each other by a third equal spring, as the diagram in the figure shows. In the normal mode of smaller angular frequency, how do the two particles move?

- In opposite directions and with the same amplitude, with the central spring deformed twice as much
- In the same direction, with amplitudes in the ratio $2 : 1$
- The first one oscillates and the second one stays at rest
- In the same direction and with the same amplitude, with the central spring unloaded

## Explanation

Calling $x_1$ and $x_2$ the abscissas measured from the equilibrium positions, we have $\mathbb{A} = m\,\mathbb{I}$ and
$$
\mathbb{V} = k
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & -1 \\
-1 & 2
\end{bmatrix} ,
$$
therefore the characteristic equation $(2k - m\omega^2)^2 - k^2 = 0$ gives $\omega_1^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$ and $\omega_2^2 = \dfrac{3k}{m}$. For $\omega_1^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$ the system $\big( \mathbb{V} - \omega_1^2\mathbb{A} \big)\,u = 0$ admits the solution $u_1 = (1, 1)$, hence the two particles move in the same direction and with the same amplitude. Their distance then stays constant, therefore the central spring does not deform and the angular frequency is that of a single mass restrained by a single spring.
