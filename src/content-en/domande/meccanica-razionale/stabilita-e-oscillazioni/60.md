A conservative holonomic system with one degree of freedom, with fixed, bilateral and ideal constraints, has potential energy $V(q) = 3q^4 - 4q^3$, shown in the graph. How are its equilibrium configurations classified?

::grafico{f="3*x^4-4*x^3" xmin=-0.8 xmax=1.6 ymin=-1.5 ymax=3.5 punti="0,0,pieno;1,-1,pieno" nomi="q,V" titolo="Potential energy $V(q) = 3q^4 - 4q^3$"}

- $q = 1$ is stable and $q = 0$ is unstable
- $q = 0$ and $q = 1$ are both stable
- $q = 0$ is stable and $q = 1$ is unstable
- Only $q = 1$ is an equilibrium configuration

## Explanation

The derivative $V'(q) = 12q^3 - 12q^2 = 12q^2\,(q - 1)$ vanishes at $q = 0$ and at $q = 1$, which are therefore the equilibrium configurations. Since $V''(q) = 36q^2 - 24q$, we have $V''(1) = 12 > 0$, therefore $q = 1$ is **stable**. At $q = 0$ we have $V''(0) = 0$ and the second derivative criterion does not apply. Since $V'(q) < 0$ both for $q < 0$ and for $0 < q < 1$, the function $V$ is decreasing through $q = 0$, which is an inflection point with horizontal tangent and is neither a minimum nor a maximum; as for $V = q^3$, the equilibrium at $q = 0$ is **unstable**.
