A uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and moves in a vertical plane. Near the vertical configuration with $B$ above $A$, the solution of the linearized equation for the angular displacement $x$ is a linear combination of $e^{\lambda t}$ and $e^{-\lambda t}$, with $\lambda > 0$. What is $\lambda$?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{3g}{\ell}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{3g}{2\ell}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2g}{3\ell}}$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical we have $V(\vartheta) = -\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, hence $V''(\pi) = -\dfrac{m g \ell}{2} < 0$ and the equilibrium at $\vartheta = \pi$ is unstable. Setting $\vartheta = \pi + x$, and since the inertia coefficient equals $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$, the linearized equation is
$$
\frac{m\ell^2}{3}\,\ddot{x} - \frac{m g \ell}{2}\,x = 0 ,
$$
that is, $\ddot{x} = \dfrac{3g}{2\ell}\,x$. The characteristic equation $s^2 = \dfrac{3g}{2\ell}$ has the roots $\pm\lambda$ with $\lambda = \sqrt{\dfrac{3g}{2\ell}}$, therefore the term $e^{\lambda t}$ makes the displacement grow as long as the linearization is acceptable. This is the case $\omega^2 = -\dfrac{3g}{2\ell} < 0$ of the characteristic equation of the small oscillations.
