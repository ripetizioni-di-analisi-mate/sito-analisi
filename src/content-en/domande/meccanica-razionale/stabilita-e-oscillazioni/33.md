A uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and moves in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects the end $B$ to the fixed point $C$, placed on the vertical through $A$ at distance $\ell$ above $A$, as the diagram in the figure shows. For which values of $k$ is the configuration with the rod vertical and downward one of stable equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,2.6;B,2.44,1.71" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" linee="2.44,1.71 2.24,2.0 1.91,1.98 2.16,2.59 1.5,2.57 1.76,3.18 1.09,3.15 1.35,3.76 0.68,3.73 0.94,4.34 0.28,4.32 0.53,4.93 0.2,4.91 0,5.2" tratteggi="0,2.6 0,0.6" punti="0,5.2,C;1.22,2.16,G,baricentro" forze="1.22 2.16,0,-0.8,mg" etichette="0.45,1.75,ϑ;1.1,4.6,k" titolo="The rod hinged at $A$ with the spring between $B$ and the fixed point $C$, placed above $A$"}

- $k > \dfrac{m g}{2\ell}$
- $k < \dfrac{m g}{\ell}$
- $k < \dfrac{m g}{2\ell}$
- $k < \dfrac{2 m g}{\ell}$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical we have $\overline{BC}^2 = 2\ell^2\,(1 + \cos\vartheta)$, therefore
$$
U(\vartheta) = \left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - k \ell^2 \right)\cos\vartheta - k \ell^2 .
$$
From $U'(\vartheta) = -\left( \dfrac{m g \ell}{2} - k \ell^2 \right)\sin\vartheta$ it follows that $\vartheta = 0$ is an equilibrium configuration for every $k$, and in it the second derivative of the potential energy equals $V''(0) = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2} - k \ell^2$. The equilibrium is stable when $V''(0) > 0$, that is, when $k \ell^2 < \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$, therefore when $k < \dfrac{m g}{2\ell}$. The spring pulls $B$ upward, and beyond that value its action prevails over that of the weight and the configuration becomes unstable.
