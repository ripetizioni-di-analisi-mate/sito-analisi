A point particle slides without friction on a fixed straight horizontal guide and is subject to a conservative force of potential $U(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$, with $k > 0$ and with the convention $\vec{F} = \operatorname{grad} U$, where $x$ is the abscissa along the guide. How is the equilibrium configuration $x = 0$ classified?

- Stable, since $U''(0) = k > 0$
- Unstable, since $V = -U$ has a maximum at $x = 0$
- The second derivative criterion does not allow a conclusion
- Stable, and not asymptotically stable

## Explanation

The force has component $F = U'(x) = k x$ along the guide, hence it pushes the particle away from the origin on both sides. The potential energy is $V(x) = -U(x) = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$, with $V'(0) = 0$ and $V''(0) = -k < 0$. By the second derivative criterion the configuration $x = 0$ is therefore one of **unstable** equilibrium. Indeed, the minimum of $U$ at $x = 0$ corresponds to a maximum of the potential energy $V$.
