The motion of a harmonic oscillator is described by the equation $\ddot{x} + x = 0$, in units in which the angular frequency equals $1$. For the equilibrium configuration $x = 0$ the tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$ is fixed. What is the largest value of $\delta$ such that the initial data $|x(0)| < \delta$ and $|\dot{x}(0)| < \delta$ guarantee $|x(t)| < \varepsilon$ and $|\dot{x}(t)| < \varepsilon$ for every $t \ge 0$?

- $\varepsilon$
- $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}$
- $\sqrt{2}\,\varepsilon$
- $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}$

## Explanation

Multiplying the equation by $\dot{x}$ one obtains $\dfrac{d}{dt}\big( \dot{x}^2 + x^2 \big) = 0$, hence along every motion $x^2(t) + \dot{x}^2(t) = x^2(0) + \dot{x}^2(0)$. The point $(x, \dot{x})$ then travels along a circle of radius $r = \sqrt{x^2(0) + \dot{x}^2(0)}$, and both $|x(t)|$ and $|\dot{x}(t)|$ reach the value $r$. From the conditions $|x(0)| < \delta$ and $|\dot{x}(0)| < \delta$ it follows that $r < \sqrt{2}\,\delta$, and by choosing $x(0) = \dot{x}(0)$ close to $\delta$ the radius gets as close as one wishes to $\sqrt{2}\,\delta$. Therefore the condition is guaranteed if and only if $\sqrt{2}\,\delta \le \varepsilon$, and the largest value is $\delta = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}$.
