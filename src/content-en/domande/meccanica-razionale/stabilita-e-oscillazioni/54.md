A simple pendulum is formed by a point particle of mass $m$ and by a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$, hinged at the fixed point $O$, in a vertical plane. The particle starts from the lowest configuration with speed $\sqrt{g \ell}$. What is the largest angle that the rod forms with the downward vertical during the motion?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy is conserved, since the constraint is fixed, bilateral and ideal and the weight is conservative. With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical the potential energy is $V(\vartheta) = m g \ell\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)$, zero in the lowest configuration, and in the configuration of largest displacement the velocity is zero. Hence
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m g \ell = m g \ell\,(1 - \cos\vartheta_{\max}) ,
$$
from which $\cos\vartheta_{\max} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\vartheta_{\max} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The motion stays in the region where $V \le E$, as in the proof of the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem, and this region shrinks around the minimum as the initial energy decreases.
