A simple pendulum, displaced by a small angle from the lowest position and released with zero velocity, oscillates indefinitely about that position without approaching it. How is the lowest position classified according to the Lyapunov definition of stability?

- Unstable, since the motion does not tend to the configuration
- Asymptotically stable
- Stable, and not asymptotically stable
- Stable only for oscillations of amplitude smaller than $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The definition of stability requires that the motions with initial data close enough to the configuration, in the coordinates and in the velocities, stay within an assigned tolerance for every subsequent time. The oscillations of the pendulum about the lowest position have amplitude as small as one wishes, provided one starts close enough and slowly enough, therefore the condition is satisfied and the configuration is stable. The definition does not require that the motion tend to the configuration, hence stability holds even if the amplitude of the oscillations stays constant. Convergence to the equilibrium appears when a dissipation is present.
