A metronome is modeled as a rigid rod of negligible mass hinged at the fixed point $O$, which oscillates in a vertical plane. Two point particles are fixed on the rod, one of mass $2m$ at distance $\ell$ below $O$ and one of mass $m$ at distance $\ell$ above $O$, as the diagram in the figure shows. What is the square of the angular frequency of the small oscillations about the stable equilibrium configuration?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,2;L,0.466,0.261,-;U,-0.466,3.739,-" aste="U-L" vincoli="O,cerniera,180" tratteggi="0,0 0,4" punti="0.466,0.261,2m,pieno;-0.466,3.739,m,pieno" quote="O,L,ℓ,0.4;U,O,ℓ,0.4" titolo="The metronome with the mass $2m$ below the pivot $O$ and the mass $m$ above"}

- $\dfrac{g}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{g}{2\ell}$
- $\dfrac{2g}{3\ell}$
- $\dfrac{g}{3\ell}$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the vertical, measured from the configuration with the mass $2m$ at the bottom, the two masses have heights $-\ell\cos\vartheta$ and $\ell\cos\vartheta$ with respect to $O$, hence
$$
V(\vartheta) = -2 m g \ell\cos\vartheta + m g \ell\cos\vartheta = -m g \ell\cos\vartheta .
$$
We have $V''(0) = m g \ell > 0$, therefore the configuration with the larger mass at the bottom is stable. Both masses lie at distance $\ell$ from $O$ and contribute to the inertia, which equals $2m\ell^2 + m\ell^2 = 3m\ell^2$; it follows that
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{m g \ell}{3 m \ell^2} = \frac{g}{3\ell} .
$$
The upper mass reduces the stiffness and increases the inertia, and for this reason the metronome beats more slowly than a simple pendulum of length $\ell$.
