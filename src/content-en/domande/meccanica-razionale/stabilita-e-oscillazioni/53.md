A point particle of mass $m$ slides without friction on a fixed straight horizontal guide and is subject to no forces other than its weight and the reaction of the guide. How are the configurations of the system classified according to the Lyapunov definition of stability?

- Every configuration is one of stable equilibrium, since the potential energy is constant
- No configuration is one of equilibrium
- Every configuration is one of equilibrium, and it is unstable
- Every configuration is one of stable equilibrium, and not asymptotically stable

## Explanation

With $x$ the abscissa of the particle along the guide, the weight is perpendicular to the guide, hence the potential energy does not depend on $x$ and every configuration $x_0$ is a stationary point, that is, an equilibrium configuration. The motion with initial data $x(0) = x_0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$ is $x(t) = x_0 + v_0 t$. However small $v_0 \neq 0$ is, the displacement $|x(t) - x_0| = |v_0|\,t$ exceeds any tolerance $\varepsilon$ for $t > \dfrac{\varepsilon}{|v_0|}$, therefore no $\delta$ satisfies the definition of stability and every configuration is **unstable**. The Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem does not apply, since $x_0$ is not a *strict* minimum of $V$.
