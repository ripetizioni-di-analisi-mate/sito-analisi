For a conservative holonomic system with two degrees of freedom the Hessian matrix of the potential energy, computed in an equilibrium configuration, has negative determinant. Why does this suffice to conclude that the equilibrium is unstable?

- Since the two eigenvalues have opposite signs, hence one is negative
- Since the Hessian matrix is not symmetric
- Since the trace of the matrix is then negative
- Since the potential energy admits no stationary points

## Explanation

The Hessian matrix of a function of class $C^2$ is symmetric, therefore its eigenvalues are real and the determinant is their product. A negative product requires that the two eigenvalues have opposite signs, hence one of the two is negative and the configuration is not a minimum of the potential energy. The Hessian criterion then ensures the instability, and the explicit computation of the eigenvalues is not necessary.
