A point particle of mass $m$ slides without friction on a fixed straight horizontal guide and is subject to a conservative force of potential energy $V(x) = V_0\left( \dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - 1 \right)^2$, with $V_0 > 0$ and $a > 0$, where $x$ is the abscissa along the guide. The particle starts from the stable equilibrium configuration $x = a$ with velocity of magnitude $v_0$ directed toward the origin. For which values of $v_0$ does the particle reach the region $x < 0$?

- $v_0 > \sqrt{\dfrac{2V_0}{m}}$
- $v_0 > \sqrt{\dfrac{V_0}{m}}$
- $v_0 > 2\sqrt{\dfrac{V_0}{m}}$
- $v_0 > \sqrt{\dfrac{V_0}{2m}}$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy $E = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + V(x)$ is conserved, since the guide is fixed and smooth and the force is conservative. Since $V(a) = 0$, we have $E = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m v_0^2$. Between the two wells the potential energy has the maximum $V(0) = V_0$, and along the motion $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 = E - V(x) \ge 0$ holds, hence the particle reaches $x = 0$ only if $E \ge V_0$. If $E = V_0$ and the particle arrived at $x = 0$, it would arrive there with zero velocity. Since $x = 0$ is an equilibrium configuration, by the uniqueness of the solution of the Cauchy problem the motion would then coincide with the constant function $x(t) = 0$, which does not start from $x = a$; therefore in this case the particle approaches the origin without reaching it. If $E > V_0$, the velocity stays different from zero on the whole interval $[0, a]$ and the particle crosses the origin. The particle therefore reaches the region $x < 0$ if and only if $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m v_0^2 > V_0$, that is, for $v_0 > \sqrt{\dfrac{2V_0}{m}}$.
