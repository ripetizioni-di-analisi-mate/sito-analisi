A uniform disk of mass $M$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed inclined plane, which forms the angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal, and moves in a vertical plane. A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is welded on the rim of the disk, as the diagram in the figure shows. Which condition on $\alpha$ is necessary and sufficient for the system to admit equilibrium configurations?

::struttura{cerchi="3.313,2.126,0.7,foro" linee="0,3 6,0;0,0 6,0;0,0 0,3" punti="3.313,2.126,C,baricentro;3,1.5,H,centro;3.074,2.784,P,pieno" etichette="5.15,0.17,α;2.35,2.75,M;3.3,3.05,m" titolo="The disk rolling on the inclined plane with the point particle $P$ welded on the rim"}

- $\sin\alpha \le \dfrac{m}{M}$
- $\tan\alpha \le \dfrac{m}{M + m}$
- $\sin\alpha \le \dfrac{2m}{M + m}$
- $\sin\alpha \le \dfrac{m}{M + m}$

## Explanation

We take as coordinate the angle $\varphi$ by which the disk rotates, positive when the disk goes down the plane. By the condition of pure rolling the center $C$ travels the distance $R\varphi$ along the plane, hence its height decreases by $R\varphi\sin\alpha$. The radius $CP$ rotates by the same angle, therefore, with $\psi_0$ the angle that $CP$ forms for $\varphi = 0$ with the horizontal half-line pointing downhill, measured counterclockwise, the height of $P$ with respect to $C$ equals $R\sin(\psi_0 - \varphi)$. The potential energy is therefore
$$
V(\varphi) = -(M + m)\,g R\,\varphi\sin\alpha + m g R\sin(\psi_0 - \varphi) + \text{constant} ,
$$
and the equilibrium condition $V'(\varphi) = 0$ reads
$$
\cos(\psi_0 - \varphi) = -\frac{(M + m)\sin\alpha}{m} .
$$
The equation has solutions if and only if the right-hand side lies between $-1$ and $1$, therefore the system admits equilibrium configurations if and only if $\sin\alpha \le \dfrac{m}{M + m}$. In these configurations the point $P$ lies on the uphill side of the disk, and its weight balances the tendency of the disk to roll down.
