A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ moves without constraints in a vertical plane $Oxy$, with $y$ vertical and pointing upward. Two ideal springs of zero natural length connect $P$ to the fixed points $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (2a, 0)$; the one attached at $A$ has stiffness $k$ and the one attached at $B$ has stiffness $3k$. What is the equilibrium configuration of $P$?

- $\left( \dfrac{3a}{2},\ -\dfrac{m g}{4k} \right)$
- $\left( a,\ -\dfrac{m g}{4k} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{a}{2},\ -\dfrac{m g}{4k} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{3a}{2},\ -\dfrac{m g}{k} \right)$

## Explanation

With $P = (x, y)$ the potential energy is
$$
V(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,(x^2 + y^2) + \frac{3}{2}\,k\,\big( (x - 2a)^2 + y^2 \big) + m g y .
$$
The equilibrium configuration is the stationary point of $V$, hence we solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
k x + 3k\,(x - 2a) = 0 \\[4pt]
k y + 3k y + m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $4kx = 6ka$, that is, $x = \dfrac{3a}{2}$, and the second gives $y = -\dfrac{m g}{4k}$. The abscissa of $P$ is the mean of the abscissas of $A$ and $B$ weighted with the stiffnesses of the springs, therefore $P$ lies closer to $B$, to which it is connected by the stiffer spring.
