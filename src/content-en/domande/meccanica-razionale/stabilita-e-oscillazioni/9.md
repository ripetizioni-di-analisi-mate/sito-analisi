The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, has the end $A$ hinged to a collar of negligible mass that slides without friction on a fixed vertical guide, and it rotates in the vertical plane about $A$. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$, placed along the guide, connects the collar to the fixed point $O$ of the guide and is unloaded when the collar is at $O$, as the diagram in the figure shows. What is the square of the natural angular frequency of the mode in which the rod rotates?

- $\dfrac{k}{m}$
- $\dfrac{3 g}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{6 g}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{3 g}{2\ell}$

## Explanation

Calling $y$ the abscissa of $A$ along the guide, measured from $O$ upward, and $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, the potential energy of the system is
$$
V(y, \vartheta) = m g\left( y - \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta \right) + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,y^2 ,
$$
hence the equilibrium configuration with the rod downward is $\left( -\dfrac{m g}{k},\ 0 \right)$ and the Hessian matrix there is diagonal, with entries $k$ and $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$. In the kinetic energy the mixed term contains the factor $\sin\vartheta$, which vanishes in that configuration, therefore the kinetic matrix there is diagonal as well and we obtain
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{m \ell^2}{3}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 .
$$
The two coordinates are then uncoupled and each natural angular frequency is obtained from the ratio between the entry of the Hessian and that of the kinetic matrix, therefore the rotation of the rod has
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}}{\dfrac{m \ell^2}{3}} = \frac{3 g}{2\ell} ,
$$
while the translation of the collar has $\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$.
