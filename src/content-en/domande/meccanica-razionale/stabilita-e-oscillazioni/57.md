Two point particles of mass $m$ slide without friction on a fixed straight horizontal guide and are connected to each other by an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and natural length $\ell_0$; there are no other springs and no walls. With $x_1$ and $x_2$ the abscissas of the two particles, how is a configuration with $x_2 - x_1 = \ell_0$ classified?

- Of stable equilibrium, by the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem
- It is not an equilibrium configuration
- Of unstable equilibrium
- Of asymptotically stable equilibrium

## Explanation

The potential energy is $V = \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,(x_2 - x_1 - \ell_0)^2$, which vanishes together with its gradient in all the configurations with $x_2 - x_1 = \ell_0$; these are therefore equilibrium configurations and form a line of minima. None of these minima is *strict*, therefore the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem does not apply. The motion that starts from one of them with $\dot{x}_1(0) = \dot{x}_2(0) = v_0$ is the translation $x_h(t) = x_h(0) + v_0 t$, with the spring always unloaded, and it moves away from the initial configuration however small $v_0 \neq 0$ is. The configuration is therefore one of **unstable** equilibrium.
