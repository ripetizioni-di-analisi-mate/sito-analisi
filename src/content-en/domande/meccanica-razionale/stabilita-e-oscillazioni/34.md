A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ slides without friction on a fixed circular guide, of center $O$ and radius $R$, placed in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects $P$ to the highest point $Q$ of the guide, as the diagram in the figure shows. For which value of $k$ is every configuration an equilibrium configuration?

- $k = \dfrac{m g}{2R}$
- $k = \dfrac{2 m g}{R}$
- $k = \dfrac{m g}{4R}$
- $k = \dfrac{m g}{R}$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ the angle between $OP$ and the downward vertical we have $y_P = -R\cos\vartheta$ and $\overline{PQ}^2 = 2R^2\,(1 + \cos\vartheta)$, therefore
$$
U(\vartheta) = \big( m g R - k R^2 \big)\cos\vartheta - k R^2 .
$$
From $U'(\vartheta) = -\big( m g R - k R^2 \big)\sin\vartheta$ it follows that the derivative of the potential is identically zero when $k R^2 = m g R$, that is, when $k = \dfrac{m g}{R}$. The potential is then constant, hence every configuration is an equilibrium configuration and the equilibrium is neutral; for $k$ smaller than that value only the two vertical configurations remain equilibrium configurations.
