The small damped oscillations of a system with one degree of freedom are described by the equation $\ddot{x} + 2\beta\,\dot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = 0$, with $\beta > \omega_0 > 0$. How does the solution behave?

- It oscillates with amplitude that decreases like $e^{-\beta t}$
- It oscillates with constant amplitude and angular frequency $\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \beta^2}$
- It tends to zero without oscillating
- It grows indefinitely

## Explanation

The characteristic equation $s^2 + 2\beta s + \omega_0^2 = 0$ has discriminant $4\,\big( \beta^2 - \omega_0^2 \big)$, which is positive since $\beta > \omega_0$. The two roots $s_{1,2} = -\beta \pm \sqrt{\beta^2 - \omega_0^2}$ are then real, and they are both negative because their product equals $\omega_0^2 > 0$ and their sum equals $-2\beta < 0$. The general solution $x = C_1 e^{s_1 t} + C_2 e^{s_2 t}$ therefore tends to zero in an aperiodic way, hence the equilibrium configuration is asymptotically stable.
