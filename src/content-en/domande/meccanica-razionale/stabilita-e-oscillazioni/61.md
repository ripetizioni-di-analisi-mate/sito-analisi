A point particle of mass $m$ is constrained to the smooth fixed surface of equation $z = \dfrac{x^2 + 3xy + y^2}{2a}$, with $a > 0$ and $z$ vertical and pointing upward, and it is subject to its weight only. The sections of the surface with the planes $y = 0$ and $x = 0$ are parabolas concave upward. How is the equilibrium configuration at the origin classified?

- Stable, since the two sections are concave upward
- Stable, since $V_{xx} > 0$
- Unstable, since the Hessian matrix of $V$ has a negative eigenvalue
- The Hessian criterion does not allow a conclusion

## Explanation

We take $x$ and $y$ as Lagrangian coordinates. The potential energy is $V = m g z = \dfrac{m g}{2a}\,\big( x^2 + 3xy + y^2 \big)$, whose gradient vanishes at the origin, and the Hessian matrix equals
$$
\mathbb{V} = \frac{m g}{a}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & \dfrac{3}{2} \\[8pt]
\dfrac{3}{2} & 1
\end{bmatrix} ,
$$
with $\det\mathbb{V} = \dfrac{m^2 g^2}{a^2}\left( 1 - \dfrac{9}{4} \right) < 0$. The two eigenvalues therefore have opposite signs, and the equilibrium is **unstable**. Along the line $y = -x$ the height equals $z = -\dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, therefore in that direction the surface goes down and the origin is a saddle point.
