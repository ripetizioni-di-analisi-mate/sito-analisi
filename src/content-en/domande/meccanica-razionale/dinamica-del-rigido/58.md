A compound pendulum oscillates about a fixed smooth horizontal axis; its center of mass lies at distance $d = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis and the measured period of small oscillations is $\mathcal{T} = 1.40\,\mathrm{s}$. An additional point mass is to be fixed to the body on the ray that starts from the axis, is perpendicular to it and passes through $G$. At what distance $x > 0$ from the axis must it be fixed so that the period stays $1.40\,\mathrm{s}$, whatever the added mass? Take $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.49\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.19\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.79\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $M$ be the mass of the body, $I_a$ its moment of inertia about the axis and $\mu$ the mass added at distance $x$. The moment of inertia and the first moment of mass about the axis are additive, hence the new reduced length is
$$
\ell_r' = \frac{I_a + \mu\,x^2}{M\,d + \mu\,x} .
$$
Imposing $\ell_r' = \ell_r = \dfrac{I_a}{M\,d}$ one obtains $I_a + \mu\,x^2 = \ell_r\,M\,d + \ell_r\,\mu\,x$, that is $\mu\,x^2 = \mu\,\ell_r\,x$, and for $x > 0$ there remains $x = \ell_r$ for every value of $\mu$. The mass must therefore be fixed at the center of oscillation, and from the measured period one finds
$$
\ell_r = \frac{g\,\mathcal{T}^2}{4\pi^2} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 1.40^2}{4\pi^2} \simeq 0.49\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ puts the mass at the center of mass, which is closer to the axis than $\ell_r$ and therefore changes the period; $0.19\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance $\ell_r - d$ of the center of oscillation from the center of mass, and $0.79\,\mathrm{m}$ measures $\ell_r$ from the center of mass instead of from the axis.
