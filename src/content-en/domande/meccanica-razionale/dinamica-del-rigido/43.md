A rigid body has the point $O$ fixed and at $O$ the principal moments of inertia are $A < B < C$. Among all the states of motion in which the angular momentum $\vec{K}_O$ has the same magnitude $K$, which one has the minimum kinetic energy?

- The rotation about the principal axis of moment $C$
- The rotation about the principal axis of moment $A$
- The rotation about the principal axis of moment $B$
- All the states of motion have the same kinetic energy

## Explanation

In the principal frame the angular momentum has components $K_\xi = A\,p$, $K_\eta = B\,q$ and $K_\zeta = C\,r$, hence the kinetic energy is written
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\big( A\,p^2 + B\,q^2 + C\,r^2 \big) = \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{K_\xi^2}{A} + \frac{K_\eta^2}{B} + \frac{K_\zeta^2}{C} \right) .
$$
Since $C$ is the largest moment, each term is greater than or equal to the one obtained with $C$ in the denominator, and the sum $K_\xi^2 + K_\eta^2 + K_\zeta^2$ equals $K^2$, therefore $T \geq \dfrac{K^2}{2C}$. Equality holds only for $K_\xi = K_\eta = 0$, that is, when $\vec{\omega}$ is directed along the principal axis of moment $C$. In the same way the rotation about the axis of moment $A$ gives the maximum kinetic energy, $\dfrac{K^2}{2A}$.
