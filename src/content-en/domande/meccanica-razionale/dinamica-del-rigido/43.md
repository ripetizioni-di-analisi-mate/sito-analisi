A rigid body moves torque-free with the point $O$ fixed and at $O$ the principal moments equal $A = 1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, $B = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and $C = 3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. At the initial instant the angular velocity is $\vec{\omega} = \hat{e}_\xi + \hat{e}_\eta + \hat{e}_\zeta$, in $\mathrm{rad/s}$. What is the kinetic energy during the motion?

- $3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $9\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In the Poinsot motion the constraint at $O$ is ideal and fixed and the moment of the active forces with respect to $O$ is zero, therefore from the kinetic energy theorem it follows that $T$ is constant in time. It is then enough to compute it at the initial instant with the quadratic form in the principal frame,
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\big( A\,p^2 + B\,q^2 + C\,r^2 \big) = \frac{1}{2}\,(1 + 2 + 3) = 3\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $6\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the value $9\,\mathrm{J}$ by multiplying the sum of the three moments by the squared magnitude of the angular velocity, that is $|\vec{\omega}|^2 = 3$. In the same motion $|\vec{K}_O|^2 = A^2 p^2 + B^2 q^2 + C^2 r^2$ is conserved as well.
