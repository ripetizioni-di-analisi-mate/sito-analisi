A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is released from rest on a plane inclined at $\alpha = 45^\circ$ to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction between disk and plane is $f = 0.2$, both static and kinetic. What is the acceleration of the center of the disk?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\,g \simeq 0.47\,g$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{5}\,g \simeq 0.57\,g$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,g \simeq 0.71\,g$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{5}\,g \simeq 0.85\,g$

## Explanation

For the disk the centroidal moment of inertia is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$, hence pure rolling on the inclined plane is possible only if $\tan\alpha \le 3\,f$. With $\alpha = 45^\circ$ this would require $f \ge \dfrac{1}{3}$, while $f = 0.2$, therefore the disk rolls while slipping. During slipping the contact point slides downward and the friction force is the kinetic one, directed up along the plane and of magnitude $f\,N = f\,m\,g\cos\alpha$. The first cardinal equation along the plane then gives
$$
a_G = g\,(\sin\alpha - f\cos\alpha) = g\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{5} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{5}\,g \simeq 0.57\,g .
$$
The second cardinal equation with pole at $G$ gives $R\,\ddot{\vartheta} = 2\,f\,g\cos\alpha \simeq 0.28\,g$, smaller than $a_G$, hence the downward velocity of the contact point increases and the slipping continues. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\,g$ imposes pure rolling, which is not possible here, $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,g$ neglects friction, and $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{5}\,g$ takes the friction force directed downward.
