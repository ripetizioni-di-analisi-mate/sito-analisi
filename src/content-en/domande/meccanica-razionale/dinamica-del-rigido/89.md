An axially symmetric rotor, with principal moments at $G$ equal to $A = B$ about the transverse axes and to $C \neq A$ about the axis of figure, is mounted on a smooth ball joint at its center of mass $G$ and moves by inertia. At the initial instant the angular velocity is not parallel to the axis of figure. Which statement is true?

- The angular velocity stays parallel to the axis of figure
- The component $r$ varies in time, while the magnitude of $\vec{\omega}$ stays constant
- The component $r$ and the magnitude of $\vec{\omega}$ stay constant, hence the angle between $\vec{\omega}$ and the axis of figure is constant
- The vector $\vec{\omega}$ is constant in the fixed frame

## Explanation

With $A = B$ and zero moment the third Euler equation becomes $C\,\dot{r} = (A - B)\,p\,q = 0$, hence the component $r$ is constant. The kinetic energy $\dfrac{1}{2}\big( A\,(p^2 + q^2) + C\,r^2 \big)$ is conserved in the motion by inertia, therefore $p^2 + q^2$ is also constant and the magnitude $|\vec{\omega}| = \sqrt{p^2 + q^2 + r^2}$ is constant. The cosine of the angle between $\vec{\omega}$ and the axis of figure equals $\dfrac{r}{|\vec{\omega}|}$, hence the angle is constant. The first two Euler equations give $\dot{p} = -\dfrac{(C - A)\,r}{A}\,q$ and $\dot{q} = \dfrac{(C - A)\,r}{A}\,p$, therefore the transverse component rotates in the body-fixed frame with nonzero angular velocity, $\vec{\omega}$ is not constant, and the axis of figure describes a cone about the fixed direction of $\vec{K}_G$.
