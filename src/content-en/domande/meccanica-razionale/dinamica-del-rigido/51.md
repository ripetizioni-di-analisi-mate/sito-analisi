A homogeneous solid cylinder of mass $m$, radius $R$ and height $h = \sqrt{3}\,R$ is constrained with its center of mass $G$ fixed, by a smooth constraint, and moves by inertia. At the initial instant the angular velocity has an arbitrary direction. How does the vector $\vec{\omega}$ evolve?

- It stays constant, and the cylinder rotates uniformly about a fixed axis
- The component along the axis of the cylinder stays constant and the transverse component rotates about it
- It stays constant only if at the initial instant it is directed along the axis of the cylinder
- Its magnitude grows, because the rotation about the intermediate axis is unstable

## Explanation

The principal moments at $G$ are the axial one $C = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$ and the transverse ones
$$
A = B = \frac{m\,(3R^2 + h^2)}{12} = \frac{m\,(3R^2 + 3R^2)}{12} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 ,
$$
hence $A = B = C$. The Euler equations with zero moment then reduce to $A\,\dot{p} = 0$, $A\,\dot{q} = 0$, $A\,\dot{r} = 0$, because all the differences between the principal moments vanish, therefore the components of $\vec{\omega}$ on the body-fixed frame are constant. Moreover $\vec{K}_G = A\,\vec{\omega}$ is constant in the fixed frame, hence $\vec{\omega}$ is also constant in the fixed frame and every axis through $G$ is an axis of permanent rotation. The rotation of the transverse component about the axis of figure occurs for $A = B \neq C$, a condition that is not satisfied here because $C$ is also equal to $A$.
