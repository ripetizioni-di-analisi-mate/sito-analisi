A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ about one of its diameters, which stays fixed; the center $G$ of the disk is at rest. What is the magnitude of the angular momentum $\vec{K}_G$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\omega$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega$
- $\dfrac{5}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega$
- $\dfrac{1}{8}\,m\,R^2\,\omega$

## Explanation

The diameter is an axis of material symmetry of the disk, hence it is a principal axis of inertia at $G$, and the angular velocity is directed along it. Therefore $\vec{K}_G = \mathbb{I}_G\,\vec{\omega}$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$ and its magnitude is the product of $\omega$ and the moment of inertia about the diameter. The sum of the moments about two orthogonal diameters equals the polar moment $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$, and by symmetry the two diameters have the same moment, hence
$$
I_{\text{diam}} = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2 ,
\qquad
|\vec{K}_G| = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega .
$$
The value $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\omega$ uses the moment about the axis perpendicular to the disk, the value $\dfrac{5}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega$ adds a transport term $m\,R^2$ that is not there, because the axis passes through $G$, and $\dfrac{1}{8}\,m\,R^2\,\omega$ introduces the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the kinetic energy.
