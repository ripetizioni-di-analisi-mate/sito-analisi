A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ lies on a smooth horizontal plane and has the end $A$ hinged to a fixed vertical pin. The rod rotates on the plane about the pin with constant angular velocity $\omega$. What is the horizontal component of the reaction of the pin on the rod?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell\,\omega^2$, directed from $A$ toward $G$
- $m\,\ell\,\omega^2$, directed from $G$ toward $A$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell\,\omega^2$, directed from $G$ toward $A$
- Zero, because $\omega$ is constant

## Explanation

The center of mass $G$ is the midpoint of the rod and describes a circle of radius $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ with constant angular velocity, hence its acceleration is centripetal, directed from $G$ toward $A$, with magnitude $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\omega^2$. The weight and the reaction of the plane are vertical and balance each other, therefore only the reaction of the pin appears in the horizontal component of the first cardinal equation, and one has
$$
\vec{\Phi}_{A,\text{hor}} = m\,\vec{a}_G ,
\qquad
|\vec{\Phi}_{A,\text{hor}}| = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell\,\omega^2 .
$$
The reaction therefore has the direction of the acceleration of $G$, from $G$ toward $A$. The value $m\,\ell\,\omega^2$ uses the length of the rod as radius instead of the distance of $G$ from the pin; a zero reaction would correspond to a center of mass with zero acceleration, while with constant $\omega$ the centripetal acceleration of $G$ is different from zero.
