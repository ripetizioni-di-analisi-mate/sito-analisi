A rotor of axial principal moment $C = 0.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ turns about its own axis of figure with $r = 80\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and is mounted on two bearings at distance $b = 0.4\,\mathrm{m}$. A constraint imposes on the axis of figure a precession of angular velocity $\Omega = 3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about a direction perpendicular to the axis. What are the forces that the bearings exert on the shaft?

- $120\,\mathrm{N}$
- $150\,\mathrm{N}$
- $600\,\mathrm{N}$
- $300\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The rotor spins fast about the axis of figure, hence $\vec{K}_O \simeq C\,r\,\hat{e}_\zeta$, and the imposed precession makes this vector turn with angular velocity $\Omega$ about a perpendicular direction. By the second cardinal equation the supports must then exert the gyroscopic couple of moment
$$
|\vec{M}_O| = C\,r\,\Omega = 0.5 \cdot 80 \cdot 3 = 120\,\mathrm{N\,m} ,
$$
directed perpendicularly to the axis of figure and to the axis of precession. Two opposite forces applied at the bearings, at distance $b$, form a couple of moment $F\,b$, therefore $F = \dfrac{120}{0.4} = 300\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $120\,\mathrm{N}$ is the moment of the couple and not a force, while $150\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by dividing by $2b$.
