A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a horizontal guide and the contact presents rolling friction with coefficient $f_v$. The disk advances freely, with no driving forces applied. What is the magnitude of the deceleration of the center?

- $\dfrac{f_v\,g}{2\,R}$
- $\dfrac{2\,f_v\,g}{3\,R}$
- $\dfrac{2\,f_v\,g}{R}$
- $\dfrac{f_v\,g}{R}$

## Explanation

Rolling friction is modeled by a resisting couple opposite to the rotation, of moment $f_v\,N$, where $N = m\,g$ is the normal reaction of the guide. The contact point $C$ is taken as pole. The normal reaction and the friction force are applied at $C$ and the weight has zero arm with respect to $C$, therefore only the moment of the resisting couple remains and the second cardinal equation gives
$$
I_C\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -f_v\,m\,g ,
$$
with $I_C = I_G + m\,R^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2$. The condition of pure rolling links the acceleration of the center to the angular acceleration through $a = R\,\ddot{\vartheta}$, hence $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R\,a = -f_v\,m\,g$ and the magnitude of the deceleration equals $\dfrac{2\,f_v\,g}{3\,R}$. The value $\dfrac{2\,f_v\,g}{R}$ is obtained by using the moment of inertia about the center of mass $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$ instead of $I_C$, while $\dfrac{f_v\,g}{2\,R}$ comes from taking $I_C = 2\,m\,R^2$, which is the moment of inertia of the thin ring with respect to the contact point.
