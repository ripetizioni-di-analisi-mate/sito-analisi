A rigid body has the point $O$ fixed and at $O$ the body-fixed frame is principal of inertia, with moments $A = 1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, $B = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and $C = 4\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. The body is at rest and at a certain instant a moment with respect to $O$ is applied, with components $M_\xi = 2\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, $M_\eta = 2\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, $M_\zeta = 0$. What is the angular acceleration at that instant, written on the principal frame in $\mathrm{rad/s^2}$?

- $(2,\ 2,\ 0)$
- $(1,\ 1,\ 0)$
- $(2,\ 1,\ 0)$
- $(2,\ 4,\ 0)$

## Explanation

At the instant considered the body is at rest, hence $p = q = r = 0$ and the quadratic terms of the Euler equations vanish. The equations reduce to $A\,\dot{p} = M_\xi$, $B\,\dot{q} = M_\eta$, $C\,\dot{r} = M_\zeta$, therefore
$$
\dot{p} = \frac{2}{1} = 2 ,
\qquad
\dot{q} = \frac{2}{2} = 1 ,
\qquad
\dot{r} = 0 ,
$$
and $\dot{\vec{\omega}} = (2,\ 1,\ 0)\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. The angular acceleration is not parallel to the applied moment, because the principal moments $A$ and $B$ are different. The vector $(2,\ 2,\ 0)$ treats the body as if it had unit moment of inertia in every direction, $(1,\ 1,\ 0)$ divides both components by $B$, and $(2,\ 4,\ 0)$ multiplies by the principal moments instead of dividing.
