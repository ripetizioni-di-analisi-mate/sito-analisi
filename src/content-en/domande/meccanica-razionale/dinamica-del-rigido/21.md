A rigid body turns about a fixed horizontal axis, held by ideal constraints, and the only active forces are the weight and an ideal spring with one end fastened to the ground. Which quantity is conserved during the motion?

- The angular momentum with respect to a point of the axis
- The linear momentum of the body
- The mechanical energy $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_a\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + V(\vartheta)$
- The angular velocity

## Explanation

The weight and the elastic force of an ideal spring are conservative, with potential $U(\vartheta)$ and potential energy $V = -U$, while the constraint is ideal and fixed, hence the work of the reactions of the axis is zero. From the kinetic energy theorem, with $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_a\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$, the first integral then follows
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,I_a\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + V(\vartheta) = E .
$$
The angular momentum is not conserved, because its axial component varies according to $I_a\,\ddot{\vartheta} = M_a^{(e)}$ and the axial moment of the active forces is not zero. Neither is the linear momentum conserved, since the center of mass describes a circle and its acceleration is different from zero; for the same reason the angular velocity varies in time.
