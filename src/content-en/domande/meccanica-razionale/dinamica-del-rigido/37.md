A horizontal force $\vec{F}$ applied at the center acts on a homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ that rolls without slipping on a horizontal guide, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the friction force at the contact point?

::struttura{linee="0,0 7,0" cerchi="2.5,1.2,1.2" punti="2.5,1.2,G,baricentro;2.5,0,C,centro" forze="4.5 1.2,2,0," etichette="1.05,2.1,m, R;4,1.6,F" titolo="Disk rolling with the force $\vec{F}$ applied at the center"}

- $\dfrac{F}{3}$, in the sense of $\vec{F}$
- $\dfrac{F}{2}$, in the sense opposite to $\vec{F}$
- $\dfrac{F}{3}$, in the sense opposite to $\vec{F}$
- $\dfrac{2\,F}{3}$, in the sense opposite to $\vec{F}$

## Explanation

The horizontal forces are $\vec{F}$, applied at the center, and the friction force $F_a$, applied at the contact point; the condition of pure rolling gives $a_G = R\,\ddot{\vartheta}$. The first cardinal equation projected on the guide and the second cardinal equation with pole at $G$ give
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a_G = F - F_a \\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{a_G}{R} = F_a\,R
\end{cases}
$$
where the force $\vec{F}$ does not appear in the second equation because it is applied at $G$. From the second equation it follows that $F_a = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,a_G$, hence substituting into the first one obtains $F = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,a_G$, whence $a_G = \dfrac{2\,F}{3\,m}$ and $F_a = \dfrac{F}{3}$. The positive sign found indicates that the friction is opposite to $\vec{F}$, since it is this force that generates the moment which makes the disk rotate.
