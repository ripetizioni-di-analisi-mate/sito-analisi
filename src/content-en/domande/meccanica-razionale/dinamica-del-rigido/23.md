A rigid body is held by ideal constraints to a fixed horizontal axis that passes through its center of mass $G$ and the only active force is the weight. Released from rest in an arbitrary position, does the body start to move?

- No, because the axial moment of the weight is zero in every position
- Yes, and it performs small oscillations about the position in which $G$ lies below the axis
- Yes, and it turns with constant angular velocity
- No, provided that $G$ coincides with the midpoint of the segment between the two constraints

## Explanation

The axial moment of the weight with respect to the axis equals $-M\,g\,d\,\sin\vartheta$, where $d$ is the distance of the center of mass from the axis. Here the axis passes through $G$, hence $d = 0$ and the axial moment of the weight is zero for every value of $\vartheta$. The equation of motion then becomes $I_a\,\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$, therefore $\dot{\vartheta}$ is constant and, since the body starts from rest, $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ remains at every instant. Every position is thus one of equilibrium and the body does not start to move. The period $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I_a}{M\,g\,d}}$ is not defined in this case, because the restoring moment that produces it is proportional to $d$.
