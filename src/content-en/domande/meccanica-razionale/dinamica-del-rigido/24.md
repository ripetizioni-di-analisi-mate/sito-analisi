A rotor is welded to a shaft in such a way that its center of mass lies on the axis of rotation, while the axis is not principal of inertia at any of its points. The shaft turns with constant angular velocity $\omega$. What can be said about the transverse reactions of the bearings?

- They coincide with the static ones, because the center of mass lies on the axis
- They are zero, because $\vec{a}_G = \vec{0}$
- They contain a part proportional to $\omega$, due to the products of inertia
- They contain a part proportional to $\omega^2$, due to the products of inertia

## Explanation

By the theorem on the reactions of a fixed axis, the transverse components contain the terms $-I_{\xi\zeta}\,\ddot{\vartheta} + I_{\eta\zeta}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and $-I_{\eta\zeta}\,\ddot{\vartheta} - I_{\xi\zeta}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$. The angular velocity is constant, therefore $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$ and only the part proportional to $\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \omega^2$ remains. The axis is not principal of inertia, hence at least one of the two products $I_{\xi\zeta}$ and $I_{\eta\zeta}$ is different from zero and that part does not vanish. The condition $\vec{a}_G = \vec{0}$ removes the contribution of the first cardinal equation, therefore static balancing alone does not reduce the reactions to those of statics.
