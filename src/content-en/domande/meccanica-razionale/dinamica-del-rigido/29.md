A homogeneous thin ring of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is suspended from a fixed smooth horizontal axis through one of its points $O$ and perpendicular to the plane of the ring, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the reduced length of the compound pendulum?

::struttura{cerchi="0,-1.5,1.5;0,-1.5,1.32,foro" nodi="O,0,0" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="0,-1.5,G,baricentro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-3.3" quote="O,0 -1.5,R,0.8" titolo="Thin ring hinged at one of its points $O$"}

- $R$
- $\dfrac{R}{2}$
- $2\,R$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,R$

## Explanation

The thin ring has all its mass at distance $R$ from the center, hence the moment of inertia with respect to the axis through the center of mass perpendicular to the plane equals $I_G = m\,R^2$. The center of mass coincides with the center and is at distance $d = R$ from the axis of suspension, therefore by the **Huygens-Steiner theorem** one has $I_O = m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = 2\,m\,R^2$. The reduced length is then
$$
\ell_r = \frac{I_O}{m\,d} = \frac{2\,m\,R^2}{m\,R} = 2\,R .
$$
The value $\dfrac{3}{2}\,R$ is the reduced length of the solid disk suspended from a point of the rim, which has $I_G = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$, while $R$ is merely the distance of the center of mass from the axis.
