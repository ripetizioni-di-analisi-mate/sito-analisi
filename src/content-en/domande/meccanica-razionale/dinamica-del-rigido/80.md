A door, modeled as a homogeneous rectangular lamina of mass $m$ and width $b$, is mounted on smooth hinges whose axis forms the angle $\beta = 30^\circ$ with the vertical. When released, the door settles with its center of mass in the lowest position and, slightly displaced, oscillates about it. What is the period of small oscillations?

- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,b}{3\,g}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,b}{3\,g}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,b}{3\sqrt{3}\,g}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{b}{g}}$

## Explanation

The door rotates about the fixed axis of the hinges, with respect to which the moment of inertia is that of a lamina about one of its sides, $I_a = \dfrac{m\,b^2}{3}$, and the center of mass lies at distance $\dfrac{b}{2}$ from the axis. The weight is decomposed into the component parallel to the axis, of magnitude $m\,g\cos\beta$, which has zero axial moment, and the component perpendicular to the axis, of magnitude $m\,g\sin\beta$. The problem is therefore that of a compound pendulum in which $g$ is replaced by $g\sin\beta$, and the angular frequency is
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{m\,g\sin\beta\cdot\frac{b}{2}}{\frac{m\,b^2}{3}}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\,g\sin\beta}{2\,b}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\,g}{4\,b}} ,
$$
whence the period $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,b}{3\,g}}$. The value $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,b}{3\,g}}$ uses the whole weight, as for a horizontal axis, $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,b}{3\sqrt{3}\,g}}$ uses $\cos\beta$ instead of $\sin\beta$, and $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{b}{g}}$ treats the door as a mass concentrated at the center of mass.
