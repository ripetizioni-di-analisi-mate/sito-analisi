A rigid body with the point $O$ fixed, held by a smooth constraint, has distinct principal moments at $O$ and moves by inertia, with initial angular velocity not directed along a principal axis. Which vector stays constant in the fixed frame?

- The angular velocity $\vec{\omega}$
- The vector of the components $(p,\ q,\ r)$ of $\vec{\omega}$ on the body-fixed frame
- Both $\vec{K}_O$ and $\vec{\omega}$, because the kinetic energy is also constant
- The angular momentum $\vec{K}_O$

## Explanation

In the motion by inertia the moment of the external forces with respect to $O$ is zero, because the active forces have zero moment and the reaction is applied at $O$. By the second cardinal equation $\dfrac{d\vec{K}_O}{dt} = \vec{0}$, hence **$\vec{K}_O$ is constant** in the fixed frame. The angular velocity varies, because the Euler equations with zero moment give $A\,\dot{p} = (B - C)\,q\,r$ and the two analogous ones, and at least one of the right-hand sides is different from zero when $\vec{\omega}$ is not directed along a principal axis and the moments are distinct. The kinetic energy $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\vec{\omega} \cdot \vec{K}_O$ is constant, and this fixes the projection of $\vec{\omega}$ on the direction of $\vec{K}_O$ without making $\vec{\omega}$ constant.
