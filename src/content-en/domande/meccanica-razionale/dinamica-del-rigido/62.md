The turbine rotor of an airplane has angular momentum $\vec{K}$ of constant magnitude, directed along the longitudinal axis from the tail toward the nose. The airplane turns left, that is it rotates with angular velocity $\Omega$ about a vertical axis, counterclockwise as seen from above. What effect does the gyroscopic couple transmitted by the rotor supports have on the airplane?

- It tends to lower the nose
- It tends to raise the nose
- It tends to turn the airplane to the right about the vertical axis
- None, because $\vec{\Omega}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{K}$

## Explanation

Take $\hat{\imath}$ toward the nose, $\hat{\jmath}$ toward the left wing and $\hat{k}$ upward, so that $\vec{K} = K\,\hat{\imath}$ and the turn has angular velocity $\Omega\,\hat{k}$. The vector $\vec{K}$ rotates with the airplane, hence its derivative is
$$
\frac{d\vec{K}}{dt} = \Omega\,\hat{k} \wedge K\,\hat{\imath} = K\,\Omega\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
and by the second cardinal equation the supports exert on the rotor a moment $K\,\Omega\,\hat{\jmath}$. By the action-reaction principle the rotor exerts on the airplane the moment $-K\,\Omega\,\hat{\jmath}$, directed toward the right wing, which tends to rotate the airplane about the transverse axis in the sense that carries $\hat{\imath}$ toward $(-\hat{\jmath}) \wedge \hat{\imath} = \hat{k}$. The couple therefore tends to **raise the nose**. The moment has the direction of the transverse axis, hence it does not act about the vertical axis, and the perpendicularity of $\vec{\Omega}$ and $\vec{K}$ makes its magnitude $K\,\Omega$ maximal.
