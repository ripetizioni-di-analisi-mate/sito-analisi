A wrench is thrown into the air spinning; air resistance is negligible. Which statement about its motion is true?

- The angular momentum $\vec{K}_G$ varies, because the weight acts during the whole flight
- The center of mass describes a parabola and the motion about $G$ is a motion by inertia, with $\vec{K}_G$ constant
- The center of mass describes a parabola and the wrench rotates about $G$ in a regular precession due to the weight
- The angular momentum $\vec{K}_G$ is conserved only if the wrench rotates about a principal axis of inertia

## Explanation

The only external force is the weight, hence by the first cardinal equation $M\,\vec{a}_G = M\,\vec{g}$ and the center of mass moves like a thrown point, along a parabola. The second cardinal equation holds with the pole at the center of mass even when $G$ moves, and the weight, applied at $G$, has zero moment with respect to $G$, therefore $\dfrac{d\vec{K}_G}{dt} = \vec{0}$. The motion about the center of mass is therefore a **Poinsot motion**, whatever the axis about which the wrench was set spinning. A regular precession due to the weight would require a fixed point distinct from $G$, with respect to which the weight has a nonzero moment.
