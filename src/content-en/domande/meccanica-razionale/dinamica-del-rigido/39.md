A rigid body with the point $O$ fixed moves torque-free and at $O$ the body-fixed frame is principal of inertia with moments $A = 1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, $B = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and $C = 4\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. At one instant the components of the angular velocity equal $p = 1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, $q = 2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $r = 3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. What is $\dot{p}$ at that instant?

- $+12\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $-6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $-18\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $-12\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

The motion is torque-free, hence the moment of the external forces with respect to $O$ is zero and the first Euler equation reduces to
$$
A\,\dot{p} + (C - B)\,q\,r = 0 .
$$
One then obtains $\dot{p} = -\dfrac{(C-B)\,q\,r}{A}$ and, substituting the data, $\dot{p} = -\dfrac{(4 - 2)\cdot 2 \cdot 3}{1} = -12\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. The value $+12\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ is obtained with the wrong sign in the quadratic term, while $-6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ comes from using the coefficient $(B - A)$, which belongs to the third equation, and $-18\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ from using the coefficient $(C - A)$.
