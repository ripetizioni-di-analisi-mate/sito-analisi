A flywheel of moment of inertia $I_a = 0.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ turns about its own fixed smooth axis with angular velocity $\omega_0 = 60\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. At a certain instant a brake applies a constant couple of moment $\mathcal{M} = 3\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, opposite to the sense of the rotation. After how long does the flywheel stop?

- $20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The axis is fixed and the constraint is ideal, therefore $I_a\,\ddot{\vartheta} = M_a^{(e)}$ holds, where the axial moment of the active forces is that of the braking couple and equals $-\mathcal{M}$. The angular acceleration is thus constant and equal to $\ddot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{\mathcal{M}}{I_a} = -6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, hence the angular velocity decreases linearly according to $\dot{\vartheta}(t) = \omega_0 - \dfrac{\mathcal{M}}{I_a}\,t$. Setting $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ one obtains
$$
t = \frac{I_a\,\omega_0}{\mathcal{M}} = \frac{0.5 \cdot 60}{3} = 10\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $20\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by omitting the moment of inertia in the numerator and the value $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$ by inverting the ratio.
