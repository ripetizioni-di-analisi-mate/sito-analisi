A top, modeled as a heavy gyroscope with the fixed point $O$ and the axis of figure horizontal, moves in regular precession. What happens to the precession velocity $\Omega$ if the spin velocity $\dot{\varphi}$ is doubled, leaving the mass, the axial moment and the distance of the center of mass from $O$ unchanged?

- It doubles
- It does not change
- It is halved
- It becomes four times smaller

## Explanation

With the axis of figure horizontal the nutation equals $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, hence the condition for the regular precession of the heavy gyroscope reduces to $C\,\Omega\,\dot{\varphi} = M\,g\,d$, whence
$$
\Omega = \frac{M\,g\,d}{C\,\dot{\varphi}} .
$$
The precession velocity is therefore inversely proportional to the spin velocity, and doubling $\dot{\varphi}$ with $M$, $d$ and $C$ unchanged one obtains half of the initial value. A fast top precesses slowly, and when the spin slows down because of friction the precession becomes faster.
