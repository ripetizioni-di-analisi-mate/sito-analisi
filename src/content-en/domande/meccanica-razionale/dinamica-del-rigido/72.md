A homogeneous rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is welded by one end $O$ to a vertical shaft, perpendicular to it, as in the diagram in the figure, and the shaft rotates in two bearings. Is the system statically balanced? Is it dynamically balanced?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;O,0,2.5;E,2.6,2.5,-" aste="A-B;O-E" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera,90" punti="1.3,2.5,G,baricentro" quote="O,E,ℓ,-0.6" titolo="Rod welded by one end, perpendicular to the shaft"}

- It is not statically balanced and it is dynamically balanced
- It is statically balanced and it is not dynamically balanced
- It is both statically and dynamically balanced
- It is neither statically nor dynamically balanced

## Explanation

The center of mass of the rod is its midpoint, which lies at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from the axis, hence the system **is not statically balanced**. For dynamic balancing take the body-fixed frame with origin at the welding point $O$, $\zeta$ along the shaft and $\xi$ along the rod. All points of the rod have $\zeta = 0$, therefore
$$
I_{\xi\zeta} = \int \xi\,\zeta\,dm = 0 ,
\qquad
I_{\eta\zeta} = \int \eta\,\zeta\,dm = 0 ,
$$
and the axis of rotation is a principal axis of inertia at its point $O$; the system is therefore **dynamically balanced**. With the shaft rotating, the dynamic reactions of the bearings have a resultant of magnitude $m\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\omega^2$, due only to the acceleration of $G$.
