A gyroscope of mass $M$ is mounted on a gimbal suspension, that is it is constrained with its center of mass $G$ fixed, and the constraint is smooth. The rotor spins fast and the axis of figure has an arbitrary orientation, varying in time. What is the constraint reaction at $G$?

- It is zero, because the weight has zero moment with respect to $G$
- It equals $M\,g$ upward plus a term proportional to the square of the angular velocity
- It equals $M\,g$ and is vertical and upward, at every instant
- It is perpendicular to the axis of figure and has magnitude $C\,r\,\Omega$, where $\Omega$ is the precession velocity

## Explanation

The center of mass is fixed, hence $\vec{a}_G = \vec{0}$ and the first cardinal equation reduces to $\vec{\Phi}_G + M\,\vec{g} = \vec{0}$. The reaction is therefore $\vec{\Phi}_G = -M\,\vec{g}$, that is $M\,g$ upward, and it does not depend on the motion of the rotor. The zero moment of the weight with respect to $G$ enters the second cardinal equation, where it makes the motion about $G$ a motion by inertia; the resultant of the weight is still $M\,\vec{g}$ and must be balanced by the constraint. The quantity $C\,r\,\Omega$ is the magnitude of a gyroscopic couple, that is of a moment, and does not have the dimensions of a force.
