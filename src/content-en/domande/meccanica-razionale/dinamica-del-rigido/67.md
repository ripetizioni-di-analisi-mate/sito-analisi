A rigid body with the point $O$ fixed moves by inertia, and at $O$ the principal moments are $A = 1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, $B = 3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and $C = 5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Consider the three permanent rotations about the principal axes whose angular momentum has the same magnitude, $|\vec{K}_O| = 10\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}$. Which one has the minimum kinetic energy?

- The one about the axis of moment $C$, with $T = 10\,\mathrm{J}$
- The one about the axis of moment $A$, with $T = 50\,\mathrm{J}$
- The one about the axis of moment $B$, with $T \simeq 16.7\,\mathrm{J}$
- The one about the axis of moment $C$, with $T = 20\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In a permanent rotation about the principal axis of moment $I$ the angular velocity is parallel to $\vec{K}_O$, hence $|\vec{K}_O| = I\,\omega$ and $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I\,\omega^2$. Eliminating $\omega$ one obtains
$$
T = \frac{|\vec{K}_O|^2}{2\,I} .
$$
For the same $|\vec{K}_O|$ the kinetic energy is minimum for the maximum moment of inertia, that is for $I = C = 5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, and equals $T = \dfrac{100}{10} = 10\,\mathrm{J}$. About the axes of moments $A$ and $B$ one obtains $50\,\mathrm{J}$ and about $16.7\,\mathrm{J}$, which are the maximum and the intermediate value, while $20\,\mathrm{J}$ forgets the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
