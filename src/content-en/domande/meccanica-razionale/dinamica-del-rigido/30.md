A rigid body oscillates about a fixed smooth horizontal axis at distance $d = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center of mass, and its radius of gyration about the center of mass with respect to that direction equals $\rho = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. At which other distance from the center of mass can the body be suspended, on an axis parallel to the first one, to obtain the same period?

- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.65\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By the Huygens-Steiner theorem the reduced length is written $\ell_r = d + \dfrac{\rho^2}{d}$, and the period depends only on $\ell_r$. Two distances $d$ and $d'$ give the same period when
$$
d + \frac{\rho^2}{d} = d' + \frac{\rho^2}{d'} ,
$$
an equality satisfied by $d' = \dfrac{\rho^2}{d}$, which is the distance of the center of oscillation from the center of mass. Substituting the data one obtains $d' = \dfrac{0.09}{0.20} = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$, and this is the property on which Kater's reversible pendulum is built. The value $0.65\,\mathrm{m}$ is the reduced length $\ell_r$, that is the distance of the center of oscillation from the axis, and not from the center of mass.
