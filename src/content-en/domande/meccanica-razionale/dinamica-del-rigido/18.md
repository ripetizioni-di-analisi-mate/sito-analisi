A rigid body has the point $O$ fixed and at $O$ the body-fixed frame is principal of inertia with moments $A = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, $B = 3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and $C = 5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. At one instant the components of the angular velocity equal $p = 3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, $q = 0$ and $r = 4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. What is the kinetic energy at that instant?

- $98\,\mathrm{J}$
- $49\,\mathrm{J}$
- $62.5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $53.5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The point $O$ is fixed, hence the kinetic energy equals $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\vec{\omega} \cdot \mathbb{I}_O\,\vec{\omega}$; in the principal frame this quadratic form is written
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\big( A\,p^2 + B\,q^2 + C\,r^2 \big) .
$$
Substituting the data one obtains $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\big( 2 \cdot 9 + 3 \cdot 0 + 5 \cdot 16 \big) = \dfrac{98}{2} = 49\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $98\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, while $62.5\,\mathrm{J}$ comes from using a single principal moment with the squared magnitude of the angular velocity, that is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,C\,|\vec{\omega}|^2$. This last expression holds only when $\vec{\omega}$ is directed along the axis $\zeta$.
