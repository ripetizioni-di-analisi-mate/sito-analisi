A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ hangs in a horizontal position from a vertical wire fastened to the ceiling and welded to the center of the disk, so that the disk can rotate about its own vertical axis. When the disk turns by an angle $\vartheta$, the twisted wire exerts a restoring couple of moment $-k_t\,\vartheta$. What is the period of the torsional oscillations?

- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m\,R^2}{k_t}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,k_t}{m\,R^2}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{3\,m\,R^2}{2\,k_t}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m\,R^2}{2\,k_t}}$

## Explanation

The disk rotates about the fixed vertical axis through its center, with respect to which the moment of inertia is $I_a = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$. The weight and the tension of the wire are parallel to the axis and have zero axial moment, hence the equation of motion about a fixed axis contains only the restoring couple and reads
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -k_t\,\vartheta .
$$
This is the equation of a harmonic oscillator of angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,k_t}{m\,R^2}}$, therefore the period is $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m\,R^2}{2\,k_t}}$; since the couple is linear in $\vartheta$, the period does not depend on the amplitude. The value $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m\,R^2}{k_t}}$ uses the moment of inertia of the ring, $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{3\,m\,R^2}{2\,k_t}}$ the one about an axis through the rim, and $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,k_t}{m\,R^2}}$ multiplies the angular frequency by $2\pi$ instead of dividing $2\pi$ by it.
