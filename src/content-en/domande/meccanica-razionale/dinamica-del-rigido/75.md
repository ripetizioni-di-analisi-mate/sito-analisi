A top of mass $M = 0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ has its tip fixed at the point $O$, by a smooth constraint, and its center of mass on the axis of figure; its principal moments at $O$ are $A = B = 8 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and $C = 2 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. The top moves in regular precession about the vertical through $O$ with nutation angle $\vartheta = 60^\circ$, precession velocity $\Omega = 5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and component of the angular velocity along the axis of figure $r = 60\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. At what distance $d$ from $O$ is the center of mass? Take $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $12.2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $14.3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $8.7\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10.2\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The top is a heavy gyroscope, hence the regular precession is possible if and only if the condition
$$
C\,\Omega\,r - A\,\Omega^2\cos\vartheta = M\,g\,d
$$
holds. The first term equals $2 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 5 \cdot 60 = 0.6\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ and the second $8 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 25 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 0.1\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, therefore $M\,g\,d = 0.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ and
$$
d = \frac{0.5}{0.5 \cdot 9.81}\,\mathrm{m} \simeq 0.102\,\mathrm{m} = 10.2\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The value $12.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ neglects the term $A\,\Omega^2\cos\vartheta$, which vanishes only with the axis of figure horizontal; $14.3\,\mathrm{cm}$ adds that term instead of subtracting it, and $8.7\,\mathrm{cm}$ uses $\sin\vartheta$ instead of $\cos\vartheta$.
