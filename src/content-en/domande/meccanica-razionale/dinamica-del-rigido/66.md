An extended rigid body oscillates as a compound pendulum about a fixed smooth horizontal axis at distance $d$ from its center of mass. Its period of small oscillations is compared with that of a simple pendulum of length $d$. Which statement is true?

- The two periods are equal, because in both cases the weight acts at distance $d$ from the axis
- The compound pendulum has the longer period, because its reduced length is $d + \dfrac{\rho^2}{d} > d$
- The compound pendulum has the shorter period, because part of its mass is closer to the axis than the center of mass
- The comparison depends on the mass of the body

## Explanation

The period of the compound pendulum is that of the simple pendulum whose length equals the reduced length $\ell_r = \dfrac{I_a}{M\,d}$. By the Huygens-Steiner theorem $I_a = I_G + M\,d^2 = M\,(\rho^2 + d^2)$, where $\rho$ is the centroidal radius of gyration, hence $\ell_r = d + \dfrac{\rho^2}{d}$. The centroidal moment of inertia $M\,\rho^2$ is positive for every extended body, however the mass is distributed, therefore $\ell_r > d$ and the compound pendulum **oscillates more slowly**. The mass $M$ cancels in the ratio $\dfrac{I_a}{M\,d}$, hence the comparison does not depend on it, and the periods would be equal only for a point body, with $\rho = 0$.
