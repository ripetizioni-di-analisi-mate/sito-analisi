A homogeneous rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is welded by its midpoint $G$ to a vertical shaft of axis $\zeta$, with which it forms the constant angle $\alpha$. Take the body-fixed frame with origin at $G$ and with $\xi$ in the plane that contains the rod and the shaft. What is the product of inertia $I_{\xi\zeta}$?

- $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{12}\,\sin 2\alpha$
- $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{48}\,\sin 2\alpha$
- $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{24}\,\sin 2\alpha$
- $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{24}\,\sin^2\alpha$

## Explanation

One takes as parameter the abscissa $s$ of a point of the rod measured from $G$, with $s$ varying between $-\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, so that the body-fixed coordinates of that point are $\xi = s\,\sin\alpha$ and $\zeta = s\,\cos\alpha$. The linear density is $\lambda = \dfrac{m}{\ell}$, therefore
$$
I_{\xi\zeta} = \int \xi\,\zeta\,dm = \frac{m}{\ell}\,\sin\alpha\,\cos\alpha \int_{-\ell/2}^{\ell/2} s^2\,ds = \frac{m}{\ell}\,\sin\alpha\,\cos\alpha\,\frac{\ell^3}{12} .
$$
Since $2\,\sin\alpha\,\cos\alpha = \sin 2\alpha$, one obtains $I_{\xi\zeta} = \dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{24}\,\sin 2\alpha$. The value $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{12}\,\sin 2\alpha$ is obtained by using $\sin 2\alpha$ instead of $\sin\alpha\,\cos\alpha$ without halving the coefficient, while the product of inertia vanishes only for $\alpha = 0$ and for $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, that is with the rod parallel or perpendicular to the shaft.
