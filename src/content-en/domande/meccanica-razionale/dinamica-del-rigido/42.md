A rigid body moves torque-free about its own center of mass, which is kept fixed, and its principal moments at $G$ satisfy $A < B < C$. Which permanent rotation is unstable?

- The one about the axis of moment $A$
- The one about the axis of moment $C$
- The one about the axis of moment $B$
- None, because in torque-free motion the kinetic energy is conserved

## Explanation

By the theorem on permanent rotations, the linearization of the Euler equations about the rotation $\vec{\omega} = p_0\,\hat{e}_\xi$ gives
$$
\ddot{q} = \frac{(C-A)(A-B)}{B\,C}\,p_0^2\,q ,
$$
and for the other two axes the relations obtained by cyclically permuting the principal moments hold. When the axis of the rotation is the one of largest moment of inertia or of smallest moment the two factors $(C-A)$ and $(A-B)$ have opposite signs, therefore the coefficient is negative and the perturbation oscillates staying small. When the axis is the one of intermediate moment the two factors have the same sign, the coefficient is positive and the general solution contains a growing exponential, therefore the rotation is unstable. With $A < B < C$ the intermediate axis is the one of moment $B$. The conservation of the kinetic energy and of $|\vec{K}_O|$ bounds the magnitude of $\vec{\omega}$, while it does not prevent its direction from changing completely.
