A thin homogeneous hoop of mass $m$ and radius $r$ starts from rest at height $h$ and rolls without slipping along a track ending in a circular loop of radius $R$, as in the diagram in the figure; the radius $r$ is negligible compared with $R$. What is the minimum height $h$ that allows the hoop to go around the loop while staying in contact with the track at the highest point?

::struttura{linee="-5,4.5 -1,0 1,0;1,0 1.39,0.05 1.75,0.2 2.06,0.44 2.3,0.75 2.45,1.11 2.5,1.5 2.45,1.89 2.3,2.25 2.06,2.56 1.75,2.8 1.39,2.95 1,3 0.61,2.95 0.25,2.8 -0.06,2.56 -0.3,2.25 -0.45,1.89 -0.5,1.5 -0.45,1.11 -0.3,0.75 -0.06,0.44 0.25,0.2 0.61,0.05 1,0" cerchi="-4.6,4.3,0.25" punti="1,1.5,O,centro" quote="-5.6 0,-5.6 4.5,h,0.4;1 1.5,2.5 1.5,R,-0.4" titolo="Hoop starting from rest at height $h$ and going around the loop of radius $R$"}

- $3\,R$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}\,R$
- $2\,R$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,R$

## Explanation

At the highest point the reaction of the track and the weight are both directed toward the center of the loop, hence contact is maintained if $m\,\dfrac{v^2}{R} \ge m\,g$, that is if $v^2 \ge g\,R$. In pure rolling friction acts at the contact point, which has zero velocity, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved. The hoop has $I_G = m\,r^2$ and angular velocity $\dfrac{v}{r}$, hence its kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,r^2\,\dfrac{v^2}{r^2} = m\,v^2$. Equating the initial energy to the energy at the highest point, which is at height $2R$, with $v^2 = g\,R$ one obtains
$$
m\,g\,h = m\,g\,(2R) + m\,g\,R ,
\qquad
h = 3\,R .
$$
The value $\dfrac{5}{2}\,R$ neglects the rotational energy, as for a point sliding without friction, $2R$ corresponds to zero velocity at the highest point, and $\dfrac{3}{2}\,R$ takes $R$ as the height of the highest point.
