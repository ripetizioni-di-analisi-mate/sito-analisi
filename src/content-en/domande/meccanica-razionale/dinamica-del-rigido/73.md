A gyroscope moves in regular precession with nutation angle $\vartheta = 60^\circ$, precession velocity $\dot{\psi} = 4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and spin velocity $\dot{\varphi} = 6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. What is the component $r$ of the angular velocity along the axis of figure?

- $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $9.46\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

In the regular precession the angular velocity is the sum of the precession velocity about the fixed axis and the spin velocity about the axis of figure, $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\psi}\,\hat{k} + \dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_\zeta$. The component on $\hat{e}_\zeta$ is obtained by projecting the two terms, and since $\hat{k} \cdot \hat{e}_\zeta = \cos\vartheta$,
$$
r = \dot{\varphi} + \dot{\psi}\cos\vartheta = 6 + 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 8\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The value $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ neglects the contribution of the precession, $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ adds the two velocities as if the two axes coincided, and $9.46\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ projects with $\sin\vartheta$ instead of $\cos\vartheta$.
