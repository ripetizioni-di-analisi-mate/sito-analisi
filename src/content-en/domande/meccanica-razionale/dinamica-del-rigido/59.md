A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rotates with angular velocity $\omega_0$ about a fixed smooth vertical axis through its center. A second homogeneous disk, at rest, of mass $2m$ and radius $R$, is lowered onto the same axis; the two disks come into contact and, because of the friction between their faces, end up rotating together, as in a clutch. What is the final angular velocity?

- $\dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2\,\omega_0}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{3}}$
- $\dfrac{\omega_0}{3}$

## Explanation

Consider the system of the two disks. The friction forces between the faces are internal and have zero resultant moment; the weights are parallel to the axis and the reactions of the smooth axis meet it or are parallel to it, hence the axial moment of the external forces is zero and the component of the angular momentum along the axis is conserved. The moments of inertia of the two disks are $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$ and $m\,R^2$, therefore
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0 = \left( \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 \right) \omega ,
$$
whence $\omega = \dfrac{\omega_0}{3}$. The value $\dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{3}}$ is obtained by imposing conservation of kinetic energy, which does not hold because friction dissipates energy during the sliding; $\dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$ treats the two disks as equal and $\dfrac{2\,\omega_0}{3}$ swaps the two moments of inertia.
