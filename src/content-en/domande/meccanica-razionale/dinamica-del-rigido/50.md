In a Kater reversible pendulum the two suspension knife-edges lie on opposite sides of the center of mass, at different distances from it, and are $L = 0.994\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. The movable masses of the pendulum are adjusted so that the period of small oscillations is the same when it is suspended from either knife-edge, and the measured period is $\mathcal{T} = 2.00\,\mathrm{s}$. Which value of $g$ is obtained?

- $19.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $39.24\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Let $d_1 \neq d_2$ be the distances of the knife-edges from the center of mass and $\rho$ the centroidal radius of gyration; the two reduced lengths are $d_1 + \dfrac{\rho^2}{d_1}$ and $d_2 + \dfrac{\rho^2}{d_2}$. The period is the same, hence the reduced lengths are equal, and subtracting them one obtains
$$
(d_1 - d_2)\left( 1 - \frac{\rho^2}{d_1\,d_2} \right) = 0 .
$$
Since $d_1 \neq d_2$, one has $d_1\,d_2 = \rho^2$, therefore the reduced length is $\ell_r = d_1 + \dfrac{\rho^2}{d_1} = d_1 + d_2 = L$. The pendulum then oscillates like a simple pendulum of length $L$, and from $\mathcal{T} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$ one gets
$$
g = \frac{4\pi^2\,L}{\mathcal{T}^2} = \frac{4\pi^2 \cdot 0.994}{2.00^2} \simeq 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The value $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ takes as reduced length half the distance between the knife-edges, $19.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ does not square the period, and $39.24\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ uses half of the measured period.
