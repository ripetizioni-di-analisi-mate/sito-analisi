A heavy gyroscope of mass $M$ has its fixed point $O$ in a smooth pivot and its center of mass $G$ on the axis of figure, at distance $d$ from $O$. The axis of figure is horizontal and the gyroscope moves in regular precession about the vertical through $O$ with angular velocity $\Omega$, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the horizontal component of the reaction of the pivot?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;B,3.4,0,-" aste="O-B" cerchi="3.4,0,1.1" vincoli="O,cerniera" frecce="0,-1.2,0,3,axis of precession;0,0,4.9,0,ζ" punti="3.4,0,G,baricentro" forze="3.4 0,0,-1.1,Mg" coppie="0 2.5,1,Ω" quote="O,3.4 0,d,1.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Gyroscope with horizontal axis of figure in regular precession about the vertical through $O$"}

- $0$, because the center of mass stays at the same height
- $M\,d\,\Omega^2$, directed from $G$ toward the axis of precession
- $M\,d\,\Omega^2$, directed from the axis of precession toward $G$
- $M\,g$, directed from $G$ toward the axis of precession

## Explanation

In the regular precession with $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the center of mass describes a horizontal circle centered on the vertical axis through $O$ with radius $d$, with constant angular velocity $\Omega$. Its acceleration is therefore centripetal and horizontal, of magnitude $d\,\Omega^2$, directed from $G$ toward the axis of precession. The external forces are the weight and the reaction of the pivot, therefore the first cardinal equation $\vec{\Phi}_O + M\,\vec{g} = M\,\vec{a}_G$ gives a vertical component of magnitude $M\,g$ and a horizontal component
$$
\Phi_{O,\text{hor}} = M\,d\,\Omega^2 ,
$$
with the direction of $\vec{a}_G$. A zero horizontal reaction would correspond to a center of mass at rest, while $M\,g$ is the magnitude of the vertical component.
