A homogeneous rectangular lamina of mass $m$ and sides $2\ell$ and $\ell$ is welded to a shaft along one of its diagonals, as in the diagram in the figure, and the shaft rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ in two bearings a distance $h$ apart. The center of mass of the lamina lies on the axis. What is the magnitude of the dynamic reactions of the bearings?

::struttura{poligoni="0,0.93 1.25,3.44 0,4.07 -1.25,1.56" nodi="A,0,-0.3;B,0,5.3" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera,90" punti="0,2.5,G,baricentro" quote="A,B,h,-1.9;0 0.93,1.25 3.44,2ℓ,0.4;1.25 3.44,0 4.07,ℓ,0.4" coppie="0 5.9,1,ω" titolo="Rectangular lamina welded to the shaft along a diagonal"}

- $0$
- $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{10\,h}$
- $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{6\,h}$
- $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{20\,h}$

## Explanation

Take the body-fixed frame with origin at $G$, $\zeta$ along the diagonal and $\xi$ in the plane of the lamina, perpendicular to the diagonal; let $x$ and $y$ be the axes through $G$ parallel to the sides $2\ell$ and $\ell$. One has $\displaystyle\int x^2\,dm = \dfrac{m\,(2\ell)^2}{12} = \dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{3}$, $\displaystyle\int y^2\,dm = \dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{12}$ and $\displaystyle\int x\,y\,dm = 0$, and the unit vectors are $\hat{e}_\zeta = \dfrac{(2,\ 1)}{\sqrt{5}}$ and $\hat{e}_\xi = \dfrac{(-1,\ 2)}{\sqrt{5}}$, therefore
$$
I_{\xi\zeta} = \int \frac{(-x + 2y)(2x + y)}{5}\,dm = \frac{1}{5}\left( -2\cdot\frac{m\,\ell^2}{3} + 2\cdot\frac{m\,\ell^2}{12} \right) = -\frac{m\,\ell^2}{10} ,
$$
while $I_{\eta\zeta} = 0$ because the lamina lies in the plane $\eta = 0$. The center of mass lies on the axis and $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$, hence by the theorem on the reactions of a fixed axis the two dynamic reactions are equal and opposite, of magnitude $\dfrac{|I_{\xi\zeta}|\,\omega^2}{h} = \dfrac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{10\,h}$. The diagonal of a rectangle with different sides is not an axis of symmetry of the lamina, and the answer $0$ holds only for the square; the value $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{6\,h}$ uses $\dfrac{m\,(2\ell)\,\ell}{12}$ as product of inertia, and $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{20\,h}$ splits the couple between the two bearings.
