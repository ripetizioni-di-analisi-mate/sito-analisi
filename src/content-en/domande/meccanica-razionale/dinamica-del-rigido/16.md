A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide and its center has velocity of magnitude $v$. What is the kinetic energy of the disk?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,v^2$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,v^2$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2$

## Explanation

The state of motion is roto-translational, hence by König's theorem the kinetic energy is the sum of the translational term of the center of mass and of the rotational term,
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\omega^2 ,
$$
with $I_G = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$. The condition of pure rolling links the angular velocity to the velocity of the center through $|\omega| = \dfrac{v}{R}$, therefore the second term equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\cdot\dfrac{v^2}{R^2} = \dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,v^2$. Adding the two contributions one obtains $T = \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2$ accounts for the translation alone and the value $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,v^2$ for the rotation alone.
