A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ starts from rest and rolls without slipping along an inclined plane, descending through a height difference $h$. What is the velocity of the center at that instant?

- $\sqrt{2\,g\,h}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{3\,g\,h}{4}}$
- $\sqrt{g\,h}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,g\,h}{3}}$

## Explanation

In pure rolling the friction force is applied at the contact point, which has zero velocity, therefore its work is zero and the mechanical energy is conserved. The kinetic energy of the rolling disk equals
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\cdot\frac{v^2}{R^2} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2 ,
$$
where the condition of pure rolling $|\omega| = \dfrac{v}{R}$ has been used. Equating this energy to the work of the weight one obtains $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2 = m\,g\,h$, hence $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{4\,g\,h}{3}} \simeq 1.15\,\sqrt{g\,h}$. The value $\sqrt{2\,g\,h}$ is the velocity of a body that slides without rotating, since it neglects the kinetic energy of rotation.
