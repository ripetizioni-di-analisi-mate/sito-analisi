A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a straight horizontal guide, and the geometric contact point $C$ is not a fixed point. Why can the second cardinal equation nevertheless be written in the form $I_C\,\ddot{\vartheta} = M_{Cz}^{(e)}$?

- Because $\vec{v}_C$ and $\vec{v}_G$ are parallel to the guide, hence the term $m\,\vec{v}_C \wedge \vec{v}_G$ is zero, and $I_C = I_G + m\,R^2$ stays constant
- Because $C$ is the instantaneous center of rotation, hence it has zero velocity and behaves like a fixed point
- Because the disk is homogeneous, hence the Huygens-Steiner theorem gives $I_C = I_G + m\,R^2$
- Because the reaction of the contact is applied at $C$, hence it has zero moment with respect to $C$

## Explanation

For a moving pole $P$ the second cardinal equation holds in the form $\dfrac{d\vec{K}_P}{dt} = \vec{M}_P^{(e)} - m\,\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{v}_G$. Taking as $P$ the geometric contact point, its velocity is parallel to the guide, and $\vec{v}_G$ is parallel to the guide as well, therefore the cross product vanishes at every instant. Moreover the distance of $G$ from the contact stays equal to the radius, hence $I_C = I_G + m\,R^2$ is constant and one has $\dfrac{d\vec{K}_C}{dt} = I_C\,\ddot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k}$. The *material* point of the disk that at that instant touches the guide has zero velocity, while the geometric contact point advances with the center, therefore $C$ is not a fixed point. That the reaction of the contact has zero moment with respect to $C$ makes the choice convenient, but it is not what justifies the form of the equation.
