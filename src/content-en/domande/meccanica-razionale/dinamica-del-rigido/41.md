A homogeneous square lamina of mass $m$ and side $\ell$ has the center of mass $G$ fixed and moves torque-free. At $G$ the body-fixed frame has $\xi$ and $\eta$ parallel to the sides and $\zeta$ perpendicular to the lamina. What does the third Euler equation give?

- $\dot{r} = -\dfrac{p\,q}{2}$
- $\dot{r} = 0$
- $\dot{r} = -p\,q$
- $\dot{r} = \dfrac{p\,q}{2}$

## Explanation

For the homogeneous square lamina the axes $\xi$ and $\eta$ are principal of inertia by symmetry and one has $A = B = \dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{12}$, while from the perpendicular axis theorem for a plane figure it follows that $C = A + B = \dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{6}$. The motion is torque-free, hence the third Euler equation reduces to
$$
C\,\dot{r} + (B - A)\,p\,q = 0 .
$$
Since $A = B$, the quadratic term vanishes and $C\,\dot{r} = 0$ remains, therefore $\dot{r} = 0$ at every instant and the component $r$ of the angular velocity along the axis perpendicular to the lamina is constant. The other two Euler equations stay coupled, because $C$ is different from $A$ and from $B$.
