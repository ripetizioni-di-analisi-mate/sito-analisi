A homogeneous solid sphere of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is thrown onto a rough horizontal lane with its center having horizontal velocity $v_0$ and with zero angular velocity, as in the diagram in the figure. The sphere initially slides; kinetic friction slows it down and sets it spinning until it begins to roll without slipping. What is the velocity of the center from that moment on?

::struttura{linee="-0.5,0 6,0" cerchi="1.5,1,1" punti="1.5,1,G,baricentro;1.5,0,C,centro" frecce="1.5,1,3.6,1,v_0" etichette="0.2,2.2,m, R" titolo="Sphere thrown with its center at velocity $v_0$ and without rotation"}

- $\dfrac{5}{7}\,v_0$
- $v_0$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{7}}\,v_0$
- $\dfrac{2}{7}\,v_0$

## Explanation

Take as pole a fixed point $O$ on the lane. The friction force acts along the lane, that is on a line through $O$, hence it has zero moment with respect to $O$; the weight and the normal reaction are equal and opposite and lie on the same vertical line through the center, therefore their resultant moment is zero as well. Consequently the angular momentum $\vec{K}_O = \vec{K}_G + (G - O) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_G$ is conserved. Initially its magnitude is $m\,R\,v_0$; at the end, with $I_G = \dfrac{2}{5}\,m\,R^2$ and $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R}$, the two contributions have the same sense and
$$
m\,R\,v + \frac{2}{5}\,m\,R^2\,\frac{v}{R} = m\,R\,v_0 ,
$$
whence $v = \dfrac{5}{7}\,v_0$. The value $\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{7}}\,v_0$ is obtained by imposing conservation of energy, which does not hold here because kinetic friction dissipates energy during the sliding; $v_0$ neglects friction and $\dfrac{2}{7}\,v_0$ swaps the translational share with the rotational one.
