A rigid body with the point $O$ fixed moves by inertia, and at $O$ the principal moments are $A = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, $B = 3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and $C = 6\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. The body rotates about the axis $\xi$ with $p_0 = 9\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and receives a small perturbation of the components $q$ and $r$. With what angular frequency do the perturbed components oscillate?

- $18\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $9\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $3\sqrt{6} \simeq 7.35\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $3\sqrt{2} \simeq 4.24\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The Euler equations are linearized about the rotation $\vec{\omega} = p_0\,\hat{e}_\xi$, neglecting products of two small quantities; one obtains $\dot{p} = 0$ and
$$
\begin{cases}
B\,\dot{q} = (C - A)\,p_0\,r \\[4pt]
C\,\dot{r} = (A - B)\,p_0\,q
\end{cases}
$$
Differentiating the first equation and substituting the second one gets
$$
\ddot{q} = \frac{(C - A)(A - B)}{B\,C}\,p_0^2\,q = \frac{4 \cdot (-1)}{18} \cdot 81\,q = -18\,q .
$$
The coefficient is negative, because $A$ is the minimum moment, hence the rotation is stable and $q$, like $r$, oscillates with angular frequency $\sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2} \simeq 4.24\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The value $18\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ forgets the square root, $9\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the angular velocity of the permanent rotation, and $3\sqrt{6}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is obtained by putting $A\,B$ instead of $B\,C$ in the denominator.
