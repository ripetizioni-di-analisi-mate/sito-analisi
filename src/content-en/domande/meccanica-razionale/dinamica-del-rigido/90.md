A heavy gyroscope of mass $M$ has the fixed point $O$ and a horizontal axis of figure, directed as $\hat{\imath}$, with center of mass $G = O + d\,\hat{\imath}$. The rotor spins fast and its angular momentum $\vec{K}_O$ is directed from $O$ toward $G$, as in the diagram in the figure. In which sense does the axis of figure precess, seen from above, that is from the side of $\hat{k}$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;B,3.4,0,-" aste="O-B" cerchi="3.4,0,1.1" vincoli="O,cerniera" frecce="0,-1.2,0,3,z;0,0,5.6,0,K_O" punti="3.4,0,G,baricentro" forze="3.4 0,0,-1.1,Mg" quote="O,3.4 0,d,1.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Gyroscope with horizontal axis of figure and angular momentum directed from $O$ toward $G$"}

- Clockwise
- Counterclockwise
- The axis does not precess and rotates in the vertical plane, moving downward
- The sense depends on the value of $d$

## Explanation

The moment of the weight with respect to $O$ is $\vec{M}_O = d\,\hat{\imath} \wedge (-M\,g\,\hat{k}) = M\,g\,d\,\hat{\jmath}$, because $\hat{\imath} \wedge \hat{k} = -\hat{\jmath}$. By the second cardinal equation $\dfrac{d\vec{K}_O}{dt} = M\,g\,d\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence the tip of $\vec{K}_O$, which is directed as $\hat{\imath}$, moves toward $\hat{\jmath}$. The angular momentum is almost parallel to the axis of figure, therefore by the gyroscopic effect the axis follows $\vec{K}_O$ and turns from $\hat{\imath}$ toward $\hat{\jmath}$, that is **counterclockwise** as seen from above. The displacement of the axis has the horizontal direction of the applied moment, hence the axis does not move downward; the distance $d$ enters the magnitude of the moment and not its sense, which is reversed only by reversing the spin of the rotor.
