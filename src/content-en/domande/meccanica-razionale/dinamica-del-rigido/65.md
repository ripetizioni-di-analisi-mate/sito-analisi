A rigid body has the point $O$ fixed, held by an ideal constraint. At an instant the angular velocity is $\vec{\omega} = \hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath} + 3\,\hat{k}$, in $\mathrm{rad/s}$, and the moment of the active forces with respect to $O$ is $\vec{M}_O = 2\,\hat{\imath} - \hat{k}$, in $\mathrm{N\,m}$. What is the time derivative of the kinetic energy at that instant?

- $5\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1\,\mathrm{W}$
- $-1\,\mathrm{W}$
- $0\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

By the kinetic energy theorem $\dfrac{dT}{dt}$ equals the power of the external forces. A system of forces acting on a rigid body has power $\Pi = \vec{R} \cdot \vec{v}_O + \vec{M}_O \cdot \vec{\omega}$; the point $O$ is fixed, hence the first term is zero, and the reaction is applied at $O$, therefore it has zero power. What remains is
$$
\frac{dT}{dt} = \vec{M}_O \cdot \vec{\omega} = 2 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 2 + (-1) \cdot 3 = -1\,\mathrm{W} ,
$$
and the kinetic energy is decreasing at that instant. The value $5\,\mathrm{W}$ adds the products of the components in absolute value, $1\,\mathrm{W}$ has the wrong sign, and $0\,\mathrm{W}$ assigns zero power to the moment of the active forces, which is zero only when $\vec{M}_O$ is perpendicular to $\vec{\omega}$.
