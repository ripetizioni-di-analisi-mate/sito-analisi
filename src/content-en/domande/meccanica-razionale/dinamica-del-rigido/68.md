A homogeneous cylinder descends along a rough inclined plane rolling without slipping, and a nonzero static friction force acts on the cylinder. Which statement about its mechanical energy is true?

- It decreases, because the friction force dissipates energy
- It is conserved, because the friction force is perpendicular to the velocity of the center
- It is conserved, because in pure rolling the friction force is zero
- It is conserved, because the friction force is applied at the contact point, which has zero velocity

## Explanation

The power of a force applied at a point is the scalar product of the force and the velocity of the point. In rolling without slipping the contact point is the instantaneous center of rotation, hence it has zero velocity, and the power of the friction force and of the normal reaction is zero. The only force doing work is the weight, which is conservative, therefore by the kinetic energy theorem the mechanical energy $T + V$ **is conserved**. The friction force is parallel to the plane, like the velocity of the center, and it is different from zero because its moment with respect to $G$ is the only one that increases the angular velocity. Dissipation would occur with slipping, when the contact point has nonzero velocity.
