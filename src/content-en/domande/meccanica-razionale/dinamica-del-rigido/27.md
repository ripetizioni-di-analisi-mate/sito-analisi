A wheel is statically balanced, that is its center of mass lies on the axis of rotation. Does it follow from this that the wheel is dynamically balanced as well?

- Yes, because $\vec{a}_G = \vec{0}$ makes the dynamic reactions vanish
- Yes, provided that the angular velocity is constant
- No, because dynamic balancing requires that the center of mass lie halfway between the two bearings
- No, because dynamic balancing requires that the axis of rotation be a principal axis of inertia

## Explanation

Static balancing is the condition $(G - O)_\perp = \vec{0}$, while dynamic balancing is the condition $I_{\xi\zeta} = I_{\eta\zeta} = 0$, that is the axis of rotation is a principal axis of inertia at one of its points. The two conditions are independent, as the homogeneous rod welded by its midpoint to a vertical shaft and inclined by $\alpha$ to it shows. In that system the center of mass lies on the axis, hence $\vec{a}_G = \vec{0}$, while $I_{\xi\zeta} = \dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{24}\,\sin 2\alpha$ is different from zero for $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. In that system the bearings exert two opposite forces proportional to $\omega^2$, therefore static balancing is not enough. The position of the center of mass along the axis enters neither of the two conditions.
