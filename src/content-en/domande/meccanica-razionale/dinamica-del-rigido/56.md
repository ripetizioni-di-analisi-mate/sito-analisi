The Earth is modeled as an axially symmetric rigid body, with principal moments at $G$ equal to $A = B$ about the equatorial axes and to $C$ about the polar axis, with $\dfrac{C - A}{A} = \dfrac{1}{305}$. Neglecting the moments of the forces exerted by the Sun and the Moon, the motion about $G$ is a motion by inertia, and the component $r$ of the angular velocity along the polar axis equals one turn per day. If $\vec{\omega}$ is not exactly parallel to the polar axis, its equatorial component rotates about the polar axis with respect to the frame fixed to the Earth. With what period?

- About $305$ days
- About $17.5$ days
- $1$ day
- About $26\,000$ years

## Explanation

In the motion by inertia with $A = B$ the third Euler equation gives $C\,\dot{r} = 0$, hence $r$ is constant. The first two equations become
$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{p} = -\nu\,q \\[4pt]
\dot{q} = \phantom{-}\nu\,p
\end{cases}
\qquad
\nu = \frac{(C - A)\,r}{A} ,
$$
therefore the vector with components $(p,\ q)$ rotates in the body-fixed frame with constant angular velocity $\nu$. The period equals
$$
\frac{2\pi}{\nu} = \frac{A}{C - A}\cdot\frac{2\pi}{r} = 305 \cdot 1\ \text{day} ,
$$
that is about $305$ days. The value of $1$ day is the period of the spin, $17.5$ days is obtained by setting $\nu = r\sqrt{\dfrac{C - A}{A}}$ as in the growth coefficient of an unstable rotation, and $26\,000$ years is the period of the precession of the equinoxes, which is due precisely to the moments of the Sun and the Moon excluded here.
