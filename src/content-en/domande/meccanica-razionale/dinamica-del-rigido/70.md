A flywheel of moment of inertia $I_a = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ rotates about its own fixed axis and is braked by a viscous couple of moment $-c\,\dot{\vartheta}$, with $c = 0.5\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s}$; no other active force has an axial moment. After how much time is the angular velocity halved?

- $4.00\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.77\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.39\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The equation of motion about a fixed axis is $I_a\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -c\,\dot{\vartheta}$, that is a first-order linear equation in the angular velocity $\omega = \dot{\vartheta}$, whose solution is
$$
\omega(t) = \omega_0\,e^{-\frac{c}{I_a}\,t} .
$$
The angular velocity is halved when $e^{-\frac{c}{I_a}\,t} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore
$$
t = \frac{I_a}{c}\,\ln 2 = 4\ln 2 \simeq 2.77\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $4.00\,\mathrm{s}$ is the time constant $\dfrac{I_a}{c}$, after which $\omega$ has decreased by a factor $e$; $2.00\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by assuming the deceleration constant and equal to the initial one, and $1.39\,\mathrm{s}$ multiplies half the time constant by $\ln 2$.
