A rigid body has the point $O$ fixed and the body-fixed frame $\xi$, $\eta$, $\zeta$ is principal of inertia at $O$, with principal moments $A$, $B$, $C$ distinct from one another. At one instant the angular velocity equals $\vec{\omega} = p\,\hat{e}_\xi + q\,\hat{e}_\eta$ with $p \neq 0$ and $q \neq 0$. Is the angular momentum $\vec{K}_O$ parallel to $\vec{\omega}$?

- No, because $A \neq B$
- Yes, always, because the frame is principal of inertia at $O$
- Yes, because $\vec{\omega}$ has zero component along $\hat{e}_\zeta$
- Yes, provided that $p = q$

## Explanation

In the principal frame the inertia tensor is diagonal, hence $\vec{K}_O = A\,p\,\hat{e}_\xi + B\,q\,\hat{e}_\eta$. Two vectors of the plane $\xi\eta$ are parallel if and only if the determinant of their components is zero, that is if $A\,p\,q - B\,q\,p = (A - B)\,p\,q = 0$. Since $p \neq 0$ and $q \neq 0$, the condition reduces to $A = B$, which is not satisfied because the three principal moments are distinct. Therefore $\vec{K}_O$ is not parallel to $\vec{\omega}$, and the same conclusion holds also with $p = q$. The angular momentum is parallel to the angular velocity only when $\vec{\omega}$ is directed along a principal axis.
