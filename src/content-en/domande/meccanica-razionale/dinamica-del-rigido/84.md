A homogeneous rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ has its end $O$ fixed, held by a smooth spherical hinge, and rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ about the vertical through $O$, forming a constant angle $\alpha$ with the downward vertical, as in the diagram in the figure. What is $\cos\alpha$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;B,1.8,-3.12,-" aste="O-B" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="0.9,-1.56,G,baricentro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-3.6" forze="0.9 -1.56,0,-0.9,mg" etichette="0.3,-1.25,α" coppie="0 -3.9,1,ω" quote="O,B,ℓ,-0.5" titolo="Rod with the end $O$ fixed rotating about the vertical at the constant angle $\alpha$"}

- $\dfrac{3\,g}{2\,\ell\,\omega^2}$
- $\dfrac{2\,g}{\ell\,\omega^2}$
- $\dfrac{g}{\ell\,\omega^2}$
- $\dfrac{6\,g}{\ell\,\omega^2}$

## Explanation

The second cardinal equation is written with the pole at the fixed point $O$. Let $\hat{e}$ be the unit vector of the rod; the point at distance $s$ from $O$ has velocity $\vec{\omega} \wedge s\,\hat{e}$, hence
$$
\vec{K}_O = \int_0^\ell \frac{m}{\ell}\,s^2\,\hat{e} \wedge (\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e})\,ds = \frac{m\,\ell^2}{3}\,\big( \vec{\omega} - (\vec{\omega} \cdot \hat{e})\,\hat{e} \big) .
$$
The vector $\vec{K}_O$ rotates rigidly with the rod, therefore $\dfrac{d\vec{K}_O}{dt} = \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{K}_O$; this vector is horizontal, perpendicular to the vertical plane containing the rod, and has magnitude $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{3}\,\omega^2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha$. The moment of the weight with respect to $O$ has the same direction and the same sense, and magnitude $m\,g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\sin\alpha$; equating the two magnitudes and dividing by $\sin\alpha \neq 0$ one obtains
$$
\cos\alpha = \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell\,\omega^2} ,
$$
which requires $\omega^2 > \dfrac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}$. The value $\dfrac{2\,g}{\ell\,\omega^2}$ concentrates the mass at the center of mass, as for a conical pendulum of length $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, $\dfrac{g}{\ell\,\omega^2}$ is the conical pendulum of length $\ell$, and $\dfrac{6\,g}{\ell\,\omega^2}$ uses the centroidal moment $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{12}$.
