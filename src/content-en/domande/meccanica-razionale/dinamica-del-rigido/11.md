A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ has the end $A$ resting on a smooth horizontal floor and the end $B$ resting on a smooth vertical wall, as in the diagram in the figure. The rod starts from rest forming the angle $\vartheta_0 = 60^\circ$ with the wall. At which angle $\vartheta$ with the wall does the end $B$ leave it?

::struttura{linee="0,4.2 0,0 5,0" nodi="A,2.6,0;B,0,3" aste="A-B" punti="1.3,1.5,G,baricentro" forze="1.3 1.5,0,-1,mg" etichette="0.33,2.25,ϑ" quote="B,A,ℓ,-0.75" titolo="Rod with one end on the floor and the other on the wall, both smooth"}

- $\vartheta \simeq 70.5^\circ$
- $\vartheta = 60^\circ$
- $\vartheta \simeq 48.2^\circ$
- $\vartheta = 90^\circ$

## Explanation

As long as the two supports are maintained the center of mass is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from the corner, hence $x_G = \dfrac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$ and $y_G = \dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, and the kinetic energy equals $T = \dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{6}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$. The constraints are smooth and fixed, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved and
$$
\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{3\,g}{\ell}\,\big( \cos\vartheta_0 - \cos\vartheta \big) ,
\qquad
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}\,\sin\vartheta .
$$
The reaction of the wall is the only horizontal force, hence it vanishes when $\ddot{x}_G = 0$; substituting one obtains
$$
\ddot{x}_G = \frac{3\,g\,\sin\vartheta}{4}\,\big( 3\cos\vartheta - 2\cos\vartheta_0 \big) ,
$$
which is zero for $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{2}{3}\cos\vartheta_0 = \dfrac{1}{3}$, that is for $\vartheta \simeq 70.5^\circ$. The angle $48.2^\circ$ is obtained by setting $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{2}{3}$, that is by forgetting the factor $\cos\vartheta_0$.
