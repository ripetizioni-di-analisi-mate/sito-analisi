A homogeneous rigid rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ lies on the $x$ axis with its midpoint at the origin $O$. The axes $x'$ and $y'$ are obtained by rotating the axes $x$ and $y$ by $45^\circ$ counterclockwise about $O$. What is the product of inertia $I_{x'y'} = \displaystyle\int x'\,y'\,dm$?

- $I_{x'y'} = 0$
- $I_{x'y'} = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{24}$
- $I_{x'y'} = -\dfrac{m\ell^2}{24}$
- $I_{x'y'} = -\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$

## Explanation

The coordinates in the frame rotated by $45^\circ$ are $x' = x\cos 45^\circ + y \sin 45^\circ$ and $y' = -x\sin 45^\circ + y\cos 45^\circ$. The point of the rod with abscissa $s$ has $y = 0$, hence
$$
x' = \frac{s}{\sqrt{2}} ,
\qquad
y' = -\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}} ,
\qquad
x'\,y' = -\frac{s^2}{2} .
$$
It follows that
$$
I_{x'y'} = -\frac{1}{2} \int_{-\frac{\ell}{2}}^{\frac{\ell}{2}} \frac{m}{\ell}\,s^2\,ds = -\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{m\ell^2}{12} = -\frac{m\ell^2}{24} .
$$
The product is negative because in the rotated frame the rod occupies the second and fourth quadrants, where $x'y' < 0$. The value $0$ holds in the original frame, in which the rod lies on an axis, $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{24}$ corresponds to the clockwise rotation and $-\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$ forgets the factor $\cos 45^\circ \sin 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
