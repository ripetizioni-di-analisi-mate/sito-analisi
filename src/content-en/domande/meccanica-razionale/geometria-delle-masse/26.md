A homogeneous rigid disk has mass $m$ and radius $R$. What is the radius of gyration about the axis orthogonal to the plane of the disk through the center?

- $\rho = \dfrac{R}{2}$
- $\rho = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,R \approx 0.707\,R$
- $\rho = R$
- $\rho = \dfrac{R}{4}$

## Explanation

About the axis orthogonal to the plane through the center the homogeneous disk has moment of inertia $I = \dfrac{mR^2}{2}$. The radius of gyration is defined by $I = m\rho^2$, therefore
$$
\rho = \sqrt{\frac{I}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{R^2}{2}} = \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,R \approx 0.707\,R .
$$
The radius of gyration is the distance from the axis at which the whole mass should be concentrated in order to obtain the same moment of inertia, hence for the disk it turns out to be smaller than $R$ and larger than $\dfrac{R}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{R}{2}$ is obtained by reading $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$ as $m\left( \dfrac{R}{2} \right)^2$, and $R$ is the radius of gyration of the ring about the same axis.
