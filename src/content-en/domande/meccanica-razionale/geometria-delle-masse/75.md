A homogeneous rigid solid cylinder has mass $m$, radius $R$ and height $h$. Which statement about its moment of inertia about its own axis is true?

- It equals $\dfrac{m\,(3R^2 + h^2)}{12}$
- It equals $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$ whatever $h$ is
- For the same mass and radius it increases with $h$
- It equals $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$ only if $h$ is small compared with $R$

## Explanation

The cylinder is divided into thin coaxial disks, each of mass $dm$ and radius $R$, and the moment of each disk about the axis equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\,dm\,R^2$. By the additivity of the moment of inertia one obtains
$$
I = \frac{R^2}{2} \int dm = \frac{mR^2}{2} .
$$
The height enters only through the mass, because for the same mass lengthening the cylinder moves the matter parallel to the axis and does not change its distance from the axis, therefore the moment does not depend on $h$. The expression $\dfrac{m\,(3R^2 + h^2)}{12}$ is the moment about the central axis orthogonal to that of the cylinder, which does depend on $h$.
