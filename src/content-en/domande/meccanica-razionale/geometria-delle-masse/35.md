An object of mass $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ has a measured moment of inertia $I_P=0.60\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. A drawing puts the centre of mass $0.50\,\mathrm m$ from axis $P$. How can the data be checked without knowing the object's shape?

- They are compatible if the object is a disc.
- They imply $I_G=1.35\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$.
- They are incompatible because $md^2=0.75\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}>I_P$.
- They imply that the entire mass lies on axis $P$.

## Explanation

The parallel-axis theorem requires $I_P=I_G+md^2$ with nonnegative $I_G$. The measurements would give $I_G=0.60-0.75<0$.
