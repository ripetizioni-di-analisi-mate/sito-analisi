A homogeneous rigid rod $AB$ has mass $m$ and length $\ell$, and its moment of inertia about the axis orthogonal to the rod through the end $A$ equals $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$. What is the moment of inertia about the axis orthogonal to the rod through the end $B$?

- $\dfrac{4\,m\ell^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{7\,m\ell^2}{12}$
- $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$

## Explanation

The center of mass of the homogeneous rod is the midpoint, therefore the two ends are at the same distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from it. Starting from the central value $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$ and applying the **Huygens-Steiner theorem** one obtains
$$
I_B = \frac{m\ell^2}{12} + m\,\frac{\ell^2}{4} = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} ,
$$
that is the same value that holds for the end $A$, as is also seen from the material symmetry of the rod with respect to the orthogonal plane at the midpoint.

The Huygens-Steiner theorem compares an axis with the parallel *central* axis, therefore it cannot be applied directly between the two axes through $A$ and through $B$. Adding $m\ell^2$ to the value $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$ would give $\dfrac{4\,m\ell^2}{3}$, which is wrong. The transport is carried out in two steps, first toward the central axis and then from the central axis to the one sought.
