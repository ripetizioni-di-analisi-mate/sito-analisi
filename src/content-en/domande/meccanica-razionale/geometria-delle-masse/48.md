Four material points of positive masses $m_1$, $m_2$, $m_3$, $m_4$, not known, occupy the vertices of the square $[0, \ell] \times [0, \ell]$ of the plane $Oxy$. Which statement about the center of mass $G$ of the system is true whatever the masses are?

- $G$ belongs to the square, boundary included
- $G$ coincides with the center of the square
- $G$ lies on one of the two diagonals of the square
- $G$ can lie outside the square if one mass is much larger than the others

## Explanation

Setting $M = m_1 + m_2 + m_3 + m_4$, the definition of center of mass gives
$$
x_G = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \frac{m_i}{M}\,x_i ,
\qquad
y_G = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \frac{m_i}{M}\,y_i ,
$$
with positive coefficients $\dfrac{m_i}{M}$ whose sum is $1$. Each $x_i$ equals $0$ or $\ell$, so $x_G$ is a weighted mean of numbers between $0$ and $\ell$ and therefore $0 \le x_G \le \ell$; the same holds for $y_G$. It follows that $G$ belongs to the square for every choice of the masses, and a very large mass brings $G$ closer to its own vertex without taking it outside. The masses $m$, $2m$, $3m$, $4m$ at the vertices $(0, 0)$, $(\ell, 0)$, $(\ell, \ell)$, $(0, \ell)$ give $G = \left( \dfrac{\ell}{2},\ \dfrac{7}{10}\,\ell \right)$, which is not the center and does not lie on any diagonal.
