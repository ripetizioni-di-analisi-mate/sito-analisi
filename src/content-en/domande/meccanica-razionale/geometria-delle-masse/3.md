A straight rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ has linear density
$$
\lambda(s) = \lambda_0 \left( 1 + \frac{s^2}{\ell^2} \right) ,
$$
where $s$ is the abscissa measured from the end $A$ and $\lambda_0$ is a positive constant. At what distance from $A$ is the center of mass of the rod located?

- $\dfrac{9}{16}\,\ell$
- $\dfrac{5}{8}\,\ell$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}\,\ell$
- $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$

## Explanation

The mass of the rod is the integral of the linear density, therefore
$$
M = \int_0^{\ell} \lambda_0 \left( 1 + \frac{s^2}{\ell^2} \right) ds = \lambda_0 \left( \ell + \frac{\ell}{3} \right) = \frac{4}{3}\,\lambda_0 \ell .
$$
The abscissa of the center of mass is obtained from the definition $s_G = \dfrac{1}{M} \displaystyle\int_0^{\ell} s\,\lambda(s)\,ds$, and the numerator equals
$$
\int_0^{\ell} \lambda_0 \left( s + \frac{s^3}{\ell^2} \right) ds = \lambda_0 \left( \frac{\ell^2}{2} + \frac{\ell^2}{4} \right) = \frac{3}{4}\,\lambda_0 \ell^2 .
$$
Hence $s_G = \dfrac{3}{4}\,\lambda_0 \ell^2 \cdot \dfrac{3}{4\,\lambda_0 \ell} = \dfrac{9}{16}\,\ell$, that is the center of mass is displaced toward $B$, where the density is larger. The value $\dfrac{3}{4}\,\ell$ is obtained by dividing by $\lambda_0 \ell$, that is by using the mass of the homogeneous rod of density $\lambda_0$, whereas $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ is the midpoint, which would be the center of mass if the rod were homogeneous.
