A homogeneous rigid tube has the shape of a hollow cylinder of height $h$, outer radius $R$ and inner radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$, and has mass $m$. What is its volume density $\rho$?

- $\rho = \dfrac{m}{\pi R^2 h}$
- $\rho = \dfrac{4m}{3\pi R^2 h}$
- $\rho = \dfrac{4m}{\pi R^2 h}$
- $\rho = \dfrac{4m}{5\pi R^2 h}$

## Explanation

The tube is homogeneous, therefore its density is the ratio between the mass and the volume it occupies. The cross section of the tube is the annulus between the radii $\dfrac{R}{2}$ and $R$, whose area is the difference of the areas of the two disks:
$$
A = \pi R^2 - \pi\,\frac{R^2}{4} = \frac{3\pi R^2}{4} .
$$
The volume therefore equals $V = A\,h = \dfrac{3\pi R^2 h}{4}$, hence
$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{4m}{3\pi R^2 h} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{m}{\pi R^2 h}$ is the density of the solid cylinder, that is the one obtained by neglecting the cavity; $\dfrac{4m}{\pi R^2 h}$ uses the volume of the cavity alone and $\dfrac{4m}{5\pi R^2 h}$ adds the areas of the two disks instead of subtracting them.
