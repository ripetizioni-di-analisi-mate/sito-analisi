A planet is modeled as a sphere with center $O$ and radius $R$ with volume density
$$
\rho(r) = \rho_0 \left( 1 - \frac{r}{R} \right) ,
$$
where $r$ is the distance from $O$ and $\rho_0$ is the density at the center. What is the mass of the sphere?

- $\dfrac{\pi\rho_0 R^3}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi\rho_0 R^3}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4\pi\rho_0 R^3}{3}$
- $\dfrac{7\pi\rho_0 R^3}{3}$

## Explanation

The density depends only on $r$, so the sphere is divided into spherical shells of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$, of volume $4\pi r^2\,dr$, in which the density is constant. The shell of radius $r$ has mass $dm = \rho_0 \left( 1 - \dfrac{r}{R} \right) 4\pi r^2\,dr$, therefore
$$
M = 4\pi\rho_0 \int_0^R \left( r^2 - \frac{r^3}{R} \right) dr = 4\pi\rho_0 \left( \frac{R^3}{3} - \frac{R^3}{4} \right) = \frac{\pi\rho_0 R^3}{3} .
$$
The ratio between $M$ and the volume $\dfrac{4\pi R^3}{3}$ gives the mean density $\dfrac{\rho_0}{4}$, smaller than the mean $\dfrac{\rho_0}{2}$ of the value at the center and the value on the surface, since most of the volume lies close to the surface, where the density is small. The value $\dfrac{2\pi\rho_0 R^3}{3}$ is obtained precisely with the density $\dfrac{\rho_0}{2}$, $\dfrac{4\pi\rho_0 R^3}{3}$ is the mass of the homogeneous sphere of density $\rho_0$ and $\dfrac{7\pi\rho_0 R^3}{3}$ changes the sign of the term in $r^3$.
