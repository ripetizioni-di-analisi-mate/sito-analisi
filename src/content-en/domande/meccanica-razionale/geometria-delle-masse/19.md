A homogeneous plate has the shape of the annulus with center $O$, inner radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ and outer radius $R$. Which statement about its center of mass is true?

- It lies on a point of the inner circumference, of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$
- It lies on a point of the circumference of radius $\dfrac{3R}{4}$, intermediate between the two
- It is not defined, since the plate has a hole
- It coincides with $O$, which does not belong to the plate

## Explanation

Two planes orthogonal to each other and orthogonal to the plate, both containing $O$, are planes of material symmetry of the annulus, since the reflection with respect to each of them maps the annulus onto itself preserving the masses. By the theorem on material symmetry the center of mass belongs to each of the two planes, therefore it lies on their line of intersection, that is on the axis through $O$ orthogonal to the plate; since the plate is plane, the center of mass coincides with $O$. The point $O$ lies in the hole, hence it does not belong to the annulus. The center of mass is defined by the integral $G - O = \dfrac{1}{M} \displaystyle\int (P - O)\,dm$ for every material system of positive mass, therefore it exists also when it is not a point of the body.
