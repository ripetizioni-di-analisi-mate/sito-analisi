A lampshade is modeled as the homogeneous lateral surface of a right circular cone with base radius $R$ and height $h$, without the base. At what distance from the plane of the circular rim is its center of mass?

- $\dfrac{h}{4}$
- $\dfrac{h}{2}$
- $\dfrac{h}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2h}{3}$

## Explanation

Every plane containing the axis of the cone is a plane of material symmetry, so the center of mass lies on the axis. The height $z$ is measured from the plane of the rim and the surface is divided into bands between the heights $z$ and $z + dz$. The band at height $z$ has radius $R\left(1 - \dfrac{z}{h}\right)$ and width $\dfrac{a}{h}\,dz$ along the surface, where $a = \sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$ is the slant height, therefore its area equals
$$
dA = 2\pi R\left(1 - \frac{z}{h}\right) \frac{a}{h}\,dz ,
$$
that is, it is proportional to $1 - \dfrac{z}{h}$. The constant factor cancels in the ratio, hence
$$
z_G = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^h z\left(1 - \frac{z}{h}\right) dz}{\displaystyle\int_0^h \left(1 - \frac{z}{h}\right) dz} = \frac{\dfrac{h^2}{6}}{\dfrac{h}{2}} = \frac{h}{3} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{h}{4}$ holds for the solid cone, in which the cross sections have area proportional to $\left(1 - \dfrac{z}{h}\right)^2$, whereas $\dfrac{2h}{3}$ is the same distance measured from the vertex.
