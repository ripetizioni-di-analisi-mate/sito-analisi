A homogeneous cylinder of radius $R$ and height $h$ has its lower base in the plane $z = 0$. Inside it there is a conical cavity whose base is the upper face of the cylinder and whose vertex is the center of the lower base, as the cross section in the figure shows. At what height is the center of mass of the solid?

::struttura{poligoni="-2,0 2,0 2,3 -2,3" fori="-2,3 2,3 0,0" tratteggi="0,-0.5 0,3.6" quote="2 0,2 3,h,0.6;-2 3,0 3,R,-0.6" frecce="2.6,0,3.3,0,x;-2.6,0,-2.6,1,z" riferimento=0 titolo="Cross section of the cylinder with the conical cavity whose base is the upper face and whose vertex is the center of the lower base"}

- $z_G = \dfrac{5h}{8}$
- $z_G = \dfrac{h}{2}$
- $z_G = \dfrac{3h}{8}$
- $z_G = \dfrac{5h}{12}$

## Explanation

The solid is symmetric about the axis of the cylinder, so the center of mass lies on the axis. The solid is treated as the difference between the solid cylinder, of volume $V = \pi R^2 h$ and center of mass at height $\dfrac{h}{2}$, and the cone, of volume $\dfrac{V}{3}$, whose center of mass is at a quarter of the height from its base, which lies at height $h$, and is therefore at height $\dfrac{3h}{4}$. With the same density for the two bodies one obtains
$$
z_G = \frac{V \cdot \dfrac{h}{2} - \dfrac{V}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3h}{4}}{V - \dfrac{V}{3}} = \frac{\dfrac{h}{4}}{\dfrac{2}{3}} = \frac{3h}{8} .
$$
The center of mass lies below half the height, because the removed mass is mostly in the upper part. The value $\dfrac{5h}{8}$ places the center of mass of the cone at a quarter of the height measured from the vertex, $\dfrac{5h}{12}$ places it at a third of the height from the base, as for the triangle, and $\dfrac{h}{2}$ neglects the cavity.
