A rigid disk of radius $R$ and mass $M$ has surface density
$$
\sigma(r) = \sigma_0\,\frac{r}{R} ,
$$
where $r$ is the distance from the center and $\sigma_0$ is a positive constant. What is the moment of inertia about the axis orthogonal to the disk through the center?

- $I = \dfrac{MR^2}{2}$
- $I = \dfrac{2\,MR^2}{5}$
- $I = \dfrac{3\,MR^2}{10}$
- $I = \dfrac{3\,MR^2}{5}$

## Explanation

The density depends only on $r$, so it is convenient to divide the disk into thin annuli of radius $r$ and width $dr$, which have area $2\pi r\,dr$ and mass $dm = \sigma_0\,\dfrac{r}{R} \cdot 2\pi r\,dr$. The mass of the disk is
$$
M = \int_0^R \frac{2\pi\sigma_0}{R}\,r^2\,dr = \frac{2\pi\sigma_0 R^2}{3} .
$$
All the points of an annulus are at distance $r$ from the axis, therefore
$$
I = \int_0^R r^2\,\frac{2\pi\sigma_0}{R}\,r^2\,dr = \frac{2\pi\sigma_0 R^4}{5} .
$$
Obtaining $2\pi\sigma_0 = \dfrac{3M}{R^2}$ from the mass gives $I = \dfrac{3\,MR^2}{5}$, which exceeds the value $\dfrac{MR^2}{2}$ of the homogeneous disk since the mass is more concentrated toward the rim. The value $\dfrac{2\,MR^2}{5}$ is obtained by expressing the result with the mass $\sigma_0 \pi R^2$ of the homogeneous disk of density $\sigma_0$, whereas $\dfrac{3\,MR^2}{10}$ assigns to each annulus the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the disk, which does not hold for a thin annulus.
