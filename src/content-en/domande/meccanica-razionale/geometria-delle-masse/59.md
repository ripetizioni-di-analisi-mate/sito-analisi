A homogeneous rigid solid sphere has mass $m$ and radius $R$. What is the moment of inertia about the center $O$, defined by $I_O = \displaystyle\int |P - O|^2\,dm$?

- $I_O = \dfrac{2\,mR^2}{5}$
- $I_O = \dfrac{6\,mR^2}{5}$
- $I_O = mR^2$
- $I_O = \dfrac{3\,mR^2}{5}$

## Explanation

Adding the three axial moments of inertia gives $I_{xx} + I_{yy} + I_{zz} = 2\,I_O$. With the origin at the center of the sphere each coordinate axis is a diameter, so each of the three moments equals $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{5}$ and therefore
$$
2\,I_O = 3 \cdot \frac{2\,mR^2}{5} ,
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
I_O = \frac{3\,mR^2}{5} .
$$
The direct computation by spherical shells, with $\rho = \dfrac{3m}{4\pi R^3}$ and $dm = \rho\,4\pi r^2\,dr$, confirms $I_O = \displaystyle\int_0^R r^2\,\rho\,4\pi r^2\,dr = \dfrac{4\pi\rho R^5}{5} = \dfrac{3\,mR^2}{5}$. The value $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{5}$ is the moment about a diameter, $\dfrac{6\,mR^2}{5}$ is the sum of the three axial moments without the division by $2$ and $mR^2$ corresponds to all the mass on the surface.
