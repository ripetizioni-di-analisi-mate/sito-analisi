- $G = \left( \dfrac{\ell}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,\ell \right)$
- $G = \left( \dfrac{\ell}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\,\ell \right)$
- $G = \left( \dfrac{\ell}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\ell \right)$
- $G = \left( \dfrac{\ell}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\,\ell \right)$

## Explanation

The vertex $C$ has coordinates $\left( \dfrac{\ell}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\ell \right)$, therefore the centers of mass of the three rods, which are the midpoints of the sides, are
$$
G_1 = \left( \frac{\ell}{2},\ 0 \right) ,
\qquad
G_2 = \left( \frac{3}{4}\,\ell,\ \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,\ell \right) ,
\qquad
G_3 = \left( \frac{\ell}{4},\ \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,\ell \right) .
$$
The three rods have equal masses, hence the center of mass of the frame is the arithmetic mean of the three midpoints, and it results
$$
x_G = \frac{1}{3}\left( \frac{\ell}{2} + \frac{3}{4}\,\ell + \frac{\ell}{4} \right) = \frac{\ell}{2} ,
\qquad
y_G = \frac{1}{3}\left( 0 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,\ell + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,\ell \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\,\ell .
$$
The height of the triangle measures $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\ell$, therefore $G$ is at one third of the height from the base, like the center of mass of the triangular plate. The ordinate $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\,\ell$ is obtained by placing $G$ at two thirds of the height starting from the base, whereas $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,\ell$ comes from counting only the two oblique rods.
