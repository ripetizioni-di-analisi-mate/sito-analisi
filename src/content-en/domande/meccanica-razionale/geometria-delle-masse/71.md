A homogeneous rigid disk has mass $m$ and radius $R$. Which description of the ellipsoid of inertia relative to the center of the disk is correct?

- It is a sphere, since the disk has rotational symmetry about its own axis
- It is an ellipsoid with three different semi-axes
- It is an ellipsoid of revolution about the axis of the disk, with the semi-axis along the axis longer than those in the plane of the disk
- It is an ellipsoid of revolution about the axis of the disk, with the semi-axis along the axis shorter than those in the plane of the disk

## Explanation

Relative to the center the principal axes are the axis of the disk and two orthogonal diameters, with principal moments
$$
A = B = \frac{mR^2}{4} ,
\qquad
C = \frac{mR^2}{2} .
$$
The semi-axes of the **ellipsoid of inertia** equal $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{A}}$, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{B}}$ and $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{C}}$, that is $\dfrac{2}{R\sqrt{m}}$ in the two directions of the plane and $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{R\sqrt{m}}$ along the axis. Since $A = B$, the ellipsoid is of revolution about the axis of the disk, and since $C > A$ the semi-axis along the axis is shorter than the other two, by a factor $\sqrt{2}$. The ellipsoid is therefore flattened in the direction of the axis, where the moment of inertia is larger; it would be a sphere only with three equal principal moments.
