A plane material system contained in the plane $Oxy$ has, relative to the pole $O$, the values $I_{xx} = I_{yy} = 3\,m\ell^2$ and $I_{xy} = m\ell^2$. What are the two principal moments of inertia relative to the principal axes contained in the plane?

- $3\,m\ell^2$ and $3\,m\ell^2$
- $2\,m\ell^2$ and $4\,m\ell^2$
- $m\ell^2$ and $3\,m\ell^2$
- $2\,m\ell^2$ and $6\,m\ell^2$

## Explanation

The system is plane, therefore $I_{xz} = I_{yz} = 0$ and the $z$ axis is principal; the two principal axes contained in the plane are found by diagonalizing the block
$$
m\ell^2 \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The trace equals $6\,m\ell^2$ and the determinant equals $(9 - 1)\,m^2\ell^4 = 8\,m^2\ell^4$, hence the characteristic equation is $\lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 8 = 0$ with $\lambda$ measured in $m\ell^2$. Its roots are $\lambda = 2$ and $\lambda = 4$, therefore the two principal moments in the plane equal $2\,m\ell^2$ and $4\,m\ell^2$, and their sum coincides with $I_{xx} + I_{yy}$, as it must be since the trace of the matrix does not change when the orthonormal basis is changed. The pair $3\,m\ell^2$ and $3\,m\ell^2$ would be obtained only with a vanishing product of inertia, that is if the axes $x$ and $y$ were already principal.
