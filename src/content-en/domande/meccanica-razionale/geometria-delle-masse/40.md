A rigid homogeneous rectangular plate has mass $m$ and sides of lengths $2\ell$ and $\ell$. What is the smallest of the principal moments of inertia relative to the center of mass?

- $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{5\,m\ell^2}{12}$
- $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{6}$
- $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$

## Explanation

The central axes parallel to the sides and the central axis orthogonal to the plate are principal, since the planes that contain them are planes of material symmetry of the rectangle. The three principal moments are then
$$
I_1 = \frac{m\ell^2}{12} ,
\qquad
I_2 = \frac{m\,(2\ell)^2}{12} = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} ,
\qquad
I_3 = \frac{m\left[ (2\ell)^2 + \ell^2 \right]}{12} = \frac{5\,m\ell^2}{12} ,
$$
where $I_1$ refers to the axis parallel to the long side, $I_2$ to the axis parallel to the short side and $I_3$ to the axis orthogonal to the plate. The smallest value is $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$, relative to the axis parallel to the side of length $2\ell$, since with respect to that axis the mass is at most at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{5\,m\ell^2}{12}$ is instead the largest of the three, and the relation $I_3 = I_1 + I_2$ proper to plane systems holds.
