A straight rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ has strictly increasing linear density $\lambda(s)$, where $s$ is the abscissa measured from the end $A$. Where is the center of mass of the rod located?

- At the midpoint of $AB$
- Between $A$ and the midpoint of $AB$
- At the end $B$
- Between the midpoint of $AB$ and $B$

## Explanation

Denoting by $M$ the mass of the rod, the distance of the center of mass from the midpoint is written
$$
s_G - \frac{\ell}{2} = \frac{1}{M} \int_0^{\ell} \left( s - \frac{\ell}{2} \right) \lambda(s)\,ds .
$$
With the substitution $s = \dfrac{\ell}{2} \pm u$ the integral becomes
$$
\int_0^{\frac{\ell}{2}} u \left[ \lambda\!\left( \frac{\ell}{2} + u \right) - \lambda\!\left( \frac{\ell}{2} - u \right) \right] du ,
$$
and the integrand is positive for every $u > 0$, since $\lambda$ is strictly increasing and therefore $\lambda\!\left( \dfrac{\ell}{2} + u \right) > \lambda\!\left( \dfrac{\ell}{2} - u \right)$. It follows that $s_G > \dfrac{\ell}{2}$, that is the center of mass lies beyond the midpoint, on the side of $B$. Since the density is positive along the whole rod, it also results $s_G < \ell$, hence the center of mass is an interior point of the segment between the midpoint and $B$.
