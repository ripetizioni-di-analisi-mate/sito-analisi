A dumbbell is made of a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$ carrying at its ends two material points of masses $m$ and $2m$. Consider the axes orthogonal to a fixed plane containing the rod. What is the minimum of the moments of inertia of the dumbbell about these axes?

- $\dfrac{3\,m\ell^2}{4}$
- $\dfrac{2\,m\ell^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4\,m\ell^2}{9}$
- $m\ell^2$

## Explanation

The axes considered are parallel to each other, so by the **Huygens-Steiner theorem** the moment about one of them equals $I_a = I_G + 3m\,d^2$, where $I_G$ is the moment about the axis of the family through the center of mass $G$ and $d$ is the distance between the two axes. Therefore the minimum of the moments is $I_G$. Measuring the abscissas along the rod from the point of mass $m$, the center of mass is at
$$
s_G = \frac{m \cdot 0 + 2m\,\ell}{3m} = \frac{2}{3}\,\ell ,
$$
so it is at distance $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\ell$ from the point of mass $m$ and $\dfrac{\ell}{3}$ from the point of mass $2m$. It follows that
$$
I_G = m\left( \frac{2}{3}\,\ell \right)^2 + 2m\left( \frac{\ell}{3} \right)^2 = \frac{2\,m\ell^2}{3} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{3\,m\ell^2}{4}$ is the moment about the axis through the midpoint of the rod, $m\ell^2$ that about the axis through the point of mass $2m$ and $\dfrac{4\,m\ell^2}{9}$ contains only the contribution of the point of mass $m$.
