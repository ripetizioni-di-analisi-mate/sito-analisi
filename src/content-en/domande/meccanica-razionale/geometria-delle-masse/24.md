A solid homogeneous right circular cone has height $h$, the base in the plane $z = 0$ and the vertex at $z = h$. At what height is its center of mass located?

- $z_G = \dfrac{h}{2}$
- $z_G = \dfrac{h}{3}$
- $z_G = \dfrac{h}{4}$
- $z_G = \dfrac{3h}{4}$

## Explanation

Every plane containing the axis of the cone is a plane of material symmetry, therefore the center of mass lies on the axis and its height remains to be computed. Denoting by $R$ the radius of the base, the slice at height $z$ is a disk of radius $R\left( 1 - \dfrac{z}{h} \right)$, hence, with total volume $V = \dfrac{\pi R^2 h}{3}$,
$$
z_G = \frac{3}{\pi R^2 h} \int_0^h \pi R^2 \left( 1 - \frac{z}{h} \right)^2 z\,dz = \frac{3}{h} \cdot \frac{h^2}{12} = \frac{h}{4} .
$$
The center of mass lies near the base since the wide slices, which weigh more, are at the bottom. The value $\dfrac{h}{3}$ is the height of the center of mass of the triangular plate obtained by cutting the cone with a plane through the axis, and $\dfrac{3h}{4}$ measures the distance of the center of mass from the vertex.
