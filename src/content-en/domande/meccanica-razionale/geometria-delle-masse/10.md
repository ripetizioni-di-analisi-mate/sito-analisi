- $I_O = \dfrac{4}{3}\,m\ell^2$
- $I_O = \dfrac{131}{96}\,m\ell^2 \approx 1.365\,m\ell^2$
- $I_O = \dfrac{107}{96}\,m\ell^2 \approx 1.115\,m\ell^2$
- $I_O = \dfrac{35}{96}\,m\ell^2 \approx 0.365\,m\ell^2$

## Explanation

The moment of inertia is additive, hence the contribution of the rod and that of the disk are added, both referred to the axis through $O$ orthogonal to the plane. For the rod the axis passes through one end, therefore its contribution is $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$. For the disk the central axis orthogonal to the plane gives $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\left( \dfrac{\ell}{4} \right)^2 = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{32}$, and since the center of the disk lies at $A$, which is at distance $\ell$ from $O$, the **Huygens-Steiner theorem** adds the term $m\ell^2$. Therefore
$$
I_O = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} + \frac{m\ell^2}{32} + m\ell^2 = \frac{32 + 3 + 96}{96}\,m\ell^2 = \frac{131}{96}\,m\ell^2 .
$$
The value $\dfrac{4}{3}\,m\ell^2$ is obtained by treating the disk as a material point at $A$, that is without its central moment of inertia, whereas $\dfrac{35}{96}\,m\ell^2$ comes from omitting the transport term of the disk.
