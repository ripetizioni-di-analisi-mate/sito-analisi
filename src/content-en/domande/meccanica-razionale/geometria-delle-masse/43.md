A rigid homogeneous spherical surface, that is a spherical shell of negligible thickness, has mass $m$ and radius $R$. What is the moment of inertia about a diameter?

- $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$
- $mR^2$

## Explanation

The surface density of the shell is $\sigma = \dfrac{m}{4\pi R^2}$, and in spherical coordinates the distance of a point from the $z$ axis equals $R\sin\vartheta$, whereas the area element is $dA = R^2 \sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta\,d\varphi$. Hence
$$
I_{zz} = \sigma \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} (R\sin\vartheta)^2\,R^2 \sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta\,d\varphi = \frac{m}{4\pi R^2} \cdot 2\pi R^4 \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{2\,mR^2}{3} .
$$
The value is larger than the one of the solid sphere of equal mass and radius, that is $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{5}$, since in the shell the whole mass lies at distance $R$ from the center, whereas in the solid sphere part of the mass lies closer to the axis. The value $mR^2$ holds for the ring about the axis orthogonal to its plane, where every point is at distance exactly $R$ from the axis.
