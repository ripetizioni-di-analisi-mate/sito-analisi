- $\mathbb{I}_O = m\ell^2 \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\mathbb{I}_O = m\ell^2 \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\mathbb{I}_O = m\ell^2 \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 & 0 \\ -2 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\mathbb{I}_O = m\ell^2 \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 & 0 \\ -2 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

All the points have $z = 0$, therefore $I_{xz} = I_{yz} = 0$ and $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$ holds. From the definitions one obtains
$$
I_{xx} = \sum_i m_i\,y_i^2 = 2m\ell^2 + 0 + m\ell^2 = 3\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{yy} = \sum_i m_i\,x_i^2 = 2m\ell^2 + m\ell^2 + 0 = 3\,m\ell^2 ,
$$
hence $I_{zz} = 6\,m\ell^2$. The product of inertia receives a contribution only from the point that has both coordinates nonzero, therefore
$$
I_{xy} = \sum_i m_i\,x_i\,y_i = 2m \cdot \ell \cdot \ell = 2\,m\ell^2 .
$$
In the matrix of the tensor the products of inertia appear with the sign changed, hence off the diagonal one reads $-2\,m\ell^2$, and the matrix with the positive products is wrong. The value $I_{zz} = 3\,m\ell^2$ would contradict the relation $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$, valid for plane systems.
