- $z_G = -\dfrac{R}{2}$
- $z_G = -\dfrac{7R}{48}$
- $z_G = \dfrac{R}{16}$
- $z_G = -\dfrac{R}{16}$

## Explanation

Every plane containing the $z$ axis is a plane of material symmetry of the solid, therefore the center of mass lies on that axis and its height remains to be computed. The cone has volume $\dfrac{\pi R^2 \cdot 2R}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi R^3}{3}$ and its center of mass is at one quarter of the height from the base, hence it lies at $z_1 = -\dfrac{R}{2}$; the half-sphere has volume $\dfrac{2\pi R^3}{3}$ and center of mass at $z_2 = \dfrac{3R}{8}$. The two volumes are equal and the solid is homogeneous, therefore the two masses are equal and the height of the center of mass is the average of the two heights:
$$
z_G = \frac{1}{2}\left( -\frac{R}{2} + \frac{3R}{8} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \left( -\frac{R}{8} \right) = -\frac{R}{16} .
$$
The center of mass therefore lies slightly below the common base, on the side of the cone. The value $-\dfrac{7R}{48}$ is obtained by placing the center of mass of the cone at one third of the height from the base, that is by using the value that holds for the triangular plate.
