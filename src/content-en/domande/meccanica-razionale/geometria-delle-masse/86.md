A homogeneous rigid rod $AB$ has mass $m$, length $\ell$ and midpoint $G$. Relative to which poles is the line containing the rod a principal axis of inertia?

- Only relative to $G$
- Only relative to $G$ and to the ends $A$ and $B$
- Relative to no pole, since the moment of inertia about that line is zero
- Relative to every point of the line

## Explanation

Any point $O$ of the line is chosen as origin and the line itself as $x$ axis. All the points of the rod have $y = z = 0$, so $I_{xy} = I_{xz} = 0$ and the first column of the tensor $\mathbb{I}_O$ is $(I_{xx},\ 0,\ 0)$. Therefore $\mathbb{I}_O\,\hat{\imath} = I_{xx}\,\hat{\imath}$, that is $\hat{\imath}$ is an eigenvector and the line is a principal axis relative to $O$, with principal moment $I_{xx} = 0$. The argument holds for every $O$ on the line, in agreement with the **theorem on the principal axes from material symmetry**, since the line of the rod is an axis of material symmetry. A zero principal moment is allowed, since principal moments are only required to be non-negative.
