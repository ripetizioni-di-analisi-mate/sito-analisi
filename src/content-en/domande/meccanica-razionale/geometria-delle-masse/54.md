A homogeneous rigid solid occupies the region
$$
\left\{ (x, y, z) : x^2 + y^2 \le \frac{R^2}{h}\,z,\ \ 0 \le z \le h \right\} ,
$$
that is a solid paraboloid of revolution with the vertex at the origin and the circular base of radius $R$ at height $h$. At what distance from the vertex is its center of mass?

- $\dfrac{3h}{4}$
- $\dfrac{h}{2}$
- $\dfrac{h}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2h}{3}$

## Explanation

Every plane containing the $z$ axis is a plane of material symmetry, so the center of mass lies on the $z$ axis. One integrates by horizontal slices: the slice at height $z$ is a disk of radius $r$ with $r^2 = \dfrac{R^2}{h}\,z$, therefore its area equals $\pi r^2 = \dfrac{\pi R^2}{h}\,z$ and is proportional to $z$. The solid is homogeneous, hence
$$
z_G = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^h z \cdot z\,dz}{\displaystyle\int_0^h z\,dz} = \frac{\dfrac{h^3}{3}}{\dfrac{h^2}{2}} = \frac{2h}{3} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{3h}{4}$ holds for the cone with the vertex at the origin, where the area of the slices grows as $z^2$; $\dfrac{h}{3}$ is the correct distance measured however from the base, and $\dfrac{h}{2}$ is the center of mass of the cylinder.
