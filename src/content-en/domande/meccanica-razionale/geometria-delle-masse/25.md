A solid homogeneous half-sphere of radius $R$ has its flat face in the plane $z = 0$ and is contained in the half-space $z \ge 0$. At what distance from the plane of the flat face is its center of mass located?

- $\dfrac{3R}{8}$
- $\dfrac{R}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3R}{4}$
- $\dfrac{R}{3}$

## Explanation

Every plane containing the $z$ axis is a plane of material symmetry, therefore the center of mass lies on that axis. In spherical coordinates the volume of the half-sphere is $\dfrac{2\pi R^3}{3}$ and the height of a point is $r\cos\vartheta$, hence
$$
z_G = \frac{3}{2\pi R^3} \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \int_0^R r\cos\vartheta \cdot r^2 \sin\vartheta\,dr\,d\vartheta\,d\varphi = \frac{3}{2\pi R^3} \cdot 2\pi \cdot \frac{R^4}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3R}{8} .
$$
The center of mass therefore lies below half the radius, since the slices near the flat face are the widest ones. The value $\dfrac{R}{2}$ is the height of the center of mass of the half spherical surface, where the whole mass lies at distance $R$ from the center, and $\dfrac{3R}{4}$ is obtained by doubling the correct result.
