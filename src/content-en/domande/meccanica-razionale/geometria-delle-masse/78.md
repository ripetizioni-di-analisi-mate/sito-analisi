A homogeneous rigid solid hemisphere has mass $m$ and radius $R$. What is the moment of inertia about its axis of symmetry, that is the line orthogonal to the flat face through its center?

- $\dfrac{mR^2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4\,mR^2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{5}$

## Explanation

The hemisphere and the hemisphere symmetric to it with respect to the plane of the face form a solid sphere of mass $2m$ with the same density. The reflection in that plane preserves the distance of every point from the axis of symmetry, which is orthogonal to the plane, so the two hemispheres have the same moment about the axis. By additivity the sum of the two moments is the moment of the sphere of mass $2m$ about a diameter, therefore
$$
2\,I = \frac{2}{5}\,(2m)\,R^2 ,
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
I = \frac{2\,mR^2}{5} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{mR^2}{5}$ halves the moment of the sphere of mass $m$ instead of that of mass $2m$, $\dfrac{4\,mR^2}{5}$ is the moment of the whole sphere and $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$ is the value of the cylinder.
