A homogeneous ring and a homogeneous disk have the same mass $m$ and the same radius $R$. The moments of inertia of the ring and of the disk about the axis orthogonal to the respective plane through the center are denoted by $I_a$ and by $I_d$. Which relation holds?

- $I_a = 2\,I_d$
- $I_d = 2\,I_a$
- $I_a = I_d$
- $I_a = 4\,I_d$

## Explanation

In the ring all the points are at distance $R$ from the axis, therefore $I_a = \displaystyle\int R^2\,dm = mR^2$. In the disk the mass is distributed over the whole circle and the integral in polar coordinates gives $I_d = \dfrac{mR^2}{2}$, because part of the mass lies closer to the axis and is weighted by the square of a distance smaller than $R$. Hence
$$
\frac{I_a}{I_d} = \frac{mR^2}{\dfrac{mR^2}{2}} = 2 ,
$$
that is $I_a = 2\,I_d$. Two bodies of equal mass but with mass distributed differently therefore have different moments of inertia, and the ring, which has all its mass at the maximum distance, is the one with the larger moment of inertia.
