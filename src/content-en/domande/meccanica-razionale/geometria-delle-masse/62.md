A homogeneous rigid rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ lies on the $z$ axis, with one end at the origin $O$ and the other at the point $(0, 0, \ell)$. Which matrix represents the inertia tensor relative to the pole $O$ in the basis $\hat{\imath}$, $\hat{\jmath}$, $\hat{k}$?

- $\mathbb{I}_O = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\mathbb{I}_O = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{12} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\mathbb{I}_O = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\mathbb{I}_O = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

All the points of the rod have $x = y = 0$, so the three products of inertia are zero and also $I_{zz} = \displaystyle\int (x^2 + y^2)\,dm = 0$. With the linear density $\dfrac{m}{\ell}$ one obtains
$$
I_{xx} = \int (y^2 + z^2)\,dm = \int_0^{\ell} \frac{m}{\ell}\,z^2\,dz = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} ,
$$
and for the same reason $I_{yy} = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$. The tensor is singular, because all the mass lies on a line through $O$ and the moment about that line is zero. The matrix with $I_{zz} = \dfrac{2\,m\ell^2}{3}$ applies the relation $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$, which holds for systems contained in the plane $Oxy$ and not for a rod orthogonal to that plane; the matrix with the factor $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$ uses the central value, which refers to axes through the midpoint and not through $O$.
