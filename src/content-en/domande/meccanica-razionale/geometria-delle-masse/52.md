A rigid container is made of a homogeneous hemispherical shell of mass $m$ and radius $R$ and of a flat lid, a homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ that closes the hemisphere along its rim. At what distance from the plane of the lid is the center of mass of the container?

- $\dfrac{R}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3R}{16}$
- $\dfrac{R}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3R}{8}$

## Explanation

Every plane containing the axis of the hemisphere is a plane of material symmetry for both parts, so the center of mass lies on that axis. The center of mass of the hemispherical shell is at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the plane of the rim, while the center of mass of the disk is its center, which lies in the plane of the lid. The two parts have the same mass, therefore by the **theorem on the center of mass of a composite system** $G$ is the midpoint between the two centers of mass and is at distance
$$
\frac{m \cdot \dfrac{R}{2} + m \cdot 0}{2m} = \frac{R}{4}
$$
from the plane of the lid. The value $\dfrac{3R}{16}$ is obtained by using the center of mass $\dfrac{3R}{8}$ of the solid hemisphere in place of that of the shell, while $\dfrac{R}{2}$ neglects the lid.
