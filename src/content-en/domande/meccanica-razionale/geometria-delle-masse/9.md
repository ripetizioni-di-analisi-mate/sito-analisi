A homogeneous rigid ring, that is a material circumference, has mass $m$ and radius $R$. What is the moment of inertia about the axis orthogonal to the plane of the ring through a point of the ring?

- $I = 2\,mR^2$
- $I = \dfrac{3\,mR^2}{2}$
- $I = mR^2$
- $I = \dfrac{mR^2}{2}$

## Explanation

All the points of the ring are at distance $R$ from the axis orthogonal to the plane through the center, therefore the moment of inertia about that axis equals $mR^2$. The center is the center of mass of the ring by material symmetry, hence that axis is central and the **Huygens-Steiner theorem** applies with distance $d = R$:
$$
I = mR^2 + mR^2 = 2\,mR^2 .
$$
The value $mR^2$ would be obtained by forgetting the transport term, whereas $\dfrac{3\,mR^2}{2}$ comes from using as central value $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$, which for the ring is the moment of inertia about a diameter and not about the axis orthogonal to the plane.
