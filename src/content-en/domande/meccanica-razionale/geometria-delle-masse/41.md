A rigid homogeneous solid sphere has mass $m$ and radius $R$. What is the moment of inertia about a line tangent to its surface?

- $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{5\,mR^2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{7\,mR^2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{22\,mR^2}{5}$

## Explanation

The center of mass of the homogeneous sphere is the center, and about every diameter the moment of inertia equals $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{5}$. A line tangent to the surface is at distance $R$ from the diameter parallel to it, therefore the **Huygens-Steiner theorem** applies:
$$
I = \frac{2\,mR^2}{5} + mR^2 = \frac{2\,mR^2 + 5\,mR^2}{5} = \frac{7\,mR^2}{5} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{5\,mR^2}{3}$ is obtained by starting from $\dfrac{2\,mR^2}{3}$, which is the moment of inertia of the spherical surface and not of the solid sphere, whereas $\dfrac{22\,mR^2}{5}$ follows from using $d = 2R$ in place of the distance $R$ between the tangent and the parallel diameter.
