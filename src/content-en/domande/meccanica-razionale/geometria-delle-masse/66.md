A homogeneous rigid disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ lies in the plane $Oxy$ with its center at the point $C = (2R, R)$. What is the product of inertia $I_{xy}$ relative to the pole $O$?

- $I_{xy} = 0$
- $I_{xy} = -2\,mR^2$
- $I_{xy} = \dfrac{9\,mR^2}{4}$
- $I_{xy} = 2\,mR^2$

## Explanation

The center $C$ is the center of mass of the disk, and with respect to the axes through $C$ parallel to $x$ and $y$ the disk has two axes of material symmetry, so its central product of inertia is zero. By the formula for the **transport of the products of inertia** one then has
$$
I_{xy}^{(O)} = I_{xy}^{(C)} + m\,x_C\,y_C = 0 + m \cdot 2R \cdot R = 2\,mR^2 .
$$
The value $0$ is the product relative to the central axes, without the transport term; $-2\,mR^2$ is the off-diagonal entry of the tensor, which contains the product with the sign changed; $\dfrac{9\,mR^2}{4}$ adds the moment $\dfrac{mR^2}{4}$ about a diameter, which does not appear in the transport of a product of inertia.
