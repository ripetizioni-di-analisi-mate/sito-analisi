A rigid body of mass $m$ has radius of gyration $\rho_G = \ell$ about an axis $a_G$ through the center of mass. What is the radius of gyration about the axis $a$ parallel to $a_G$ at distance $2\ell$ from it?

- $\rho_a = \sqrt{5}\,\ell$
- $\rho_a = 3\ell$
- $\rho_a = 2\ell$
- $\rho_a = \sqrt{3}\,\ell$

## Explanation

By the definition of radius of gyration the moment about $a_G$ equals $I_{a_G} = m\,\rho_G^2 = m\ell^2$. The axis $a$ is parallel to the central axis at distance $2\ell$ from it, hence by the **Huygens-Steiner theorem**
$$
I_a = m\ell^2 + m\,(2\ell)^2 = 5\,m\ell^2 .
$$
Therefore $\rho_a = \sqrt{\dfrac{I_a}{m}} = \sqrt{5}\,\ell$. The theorem adds the squares, $\rho_a^2 = \rho_G^2 + d^2$, and not the lengths. The value $3\ell$ is obtained by adding $\rho_G$ and the distance, $2\ell$ by neglecting the central moment and $\sqrt{3}\,\ell$ by subtracting the squares instead of adding them.
