The inertia tensor of a rigid body relative to the pole $O$ has, in the frame $(O; x, y, z)$, the diagonal entries $I_{xx} = 5\,m\ell^2$, $I_{yy} = 6\,m\ell^2$ and $I_{zz} = 7\,m\ell^2$. Two of the principal moments relative to $O$ equal $4\,m\ell^2$ and $6\,m\ell^2$. What is the third one?

- $8\,m\ell^2$
- $7\,m\ell^2$
- $5\,m\ell^2$
- $10\,m\ell^2$

## Explanation

For every orthonormal frame with origin $O$ one has $I_{xx} + I_{yy} + I_{zz} = 2\,I_O$, where $I_O = \displaystyle\int |P - O|^2\,dm$ depends only on the pole and not on the frame. Therefore the sum of the diagonal entries is the same in all the frames with origin $O$, in particular in the principal frame, where the diagonal entries are the principal moments. It follows that
$$
A + B + C = 5\,m\ell^2 + 6\,m\ell^2 + 7\,m\ell^2 = 18\,m\ell^2 ,
$$
hence the third principal moment equals $18\,m\ell^2 - 4\,m\ell^2 - 6\,m\ell^2 = 8\,m\ell^2$. The value $7\,m\ell^2$ is the largest diagonal entry, which coincides with a principal moment only if the $z$ axis is principal; $5\,m\ell^2$ is the diagonal entry left after associating $6\,m\ell^2$ with $I_{yy}$, and $10\,m\ell^2$ is the sum of the two known moments, which is the maximum value allowed for the third and not its value.
