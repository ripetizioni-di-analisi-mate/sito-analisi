A homogeneous plate of mass $m$ has the shape of the quarter disk
$$
\left\{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \le R^2,\ \ x \ge 0,\ \ y \ge 0 \right\}
$$
of the plane $Oxy$. What is the product of inertia $I_{xy}$ relative to the pole $O$?

- $I_{xy} = 0$
- $I_{xy} = \dfrac{mR^2}{2\pi}$
- $I_{xy} = \dfrac{mR^2}{8\pi}$
- $I_{xy} = \dfrac{16\,mR^2}{9\pi^2}$

## Explanation

The plate has area $\dfrac{\pi R^2}{4}$, so its density is $\sigma = \dfrac{4m}{\pi R^2}$. In polar coordinates $xy = r^2 \cos\vartheta\,\sin\vartheta$ and $dA = r\,dr\,d\vartheta$, therefore
$$
I_{xy} = \sigma \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos\vartheta\,\sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta \int_0^R r^3\,dr = \sigma \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{R^4}{4} = \frac{4m}{\pi R^2} \cdot \frac{R^4}{8} = \frac{mR^2}{2\pi} .
$$
The product is positive because $xy > 0$ in the whole first quadrant. The symmetry about the bisector exchanges $x$ and $y$ and gives $I_{xx} = I_{yy}$, but it does not make $I_{xy}$ vanish, therefore the value $0$ is wrong. The value $\dfrac{mR^2}{8\pi}$ is obtained with the density $\dfrac{m}{\pi R^2}$ of the whole disk, whereas $\dfrac{16\,mR^2}{9\pi^2} = m\,x_G\,y_G$ is the transport term alone and neglects the central product of inertia, which is not zero.
