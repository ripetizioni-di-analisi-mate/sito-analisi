A homogeneous rigid toy is made of a solid hemisphere of radius $R$ and a solid cylinder of radius $R$ and height $h$, resting with one base on the flat face of the hemisphere, as the cross section in the figure shows. What must $h$ be for the center of mass of the toy to coincide with the center $O$ of the common flat face?

- $h = \dfrac{R}{2}$
- $h = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,R$
- $h = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,R$
- $h = \dfrac{3R}{8}$

## Explanation

The height $z$ is measured from $O$ toward the cylinder. The toy is homogeneous, so the masses of the two parts are proportional to the volumes: the hemisphere has volume $\dfrac{2\pi R^3}{3}$ and center of mass at height $-\dfrac{3R}{8}$, the cylinder has volume $\pi R^2 h$ and center of mass at height $\dfrac{h}{2}$. By the **theorem on the center of mass of a composite system** the center of mass is at $O$ when
$$
\frac{2\pi R^3}{3} \cdot \left( -\frac{3R}{8} \right) + \pi R^2 h \cdot \frac{h}{2} = 0 ,
$$
that is when $\dfrac{h^2}{2} = \dfrac{R^2}{4}$, hence $h = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,R \approx 0.71\,R$. The value $\dfrac{R}{2}$ is obtained by placing the center of mass of the cylinder at height $h$ instead of $\dfrac{h}{2}$, while $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\,R$ uses for the hemisphere the distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$, which holds for the hemispherical shell and not for the solid hemisphere.
