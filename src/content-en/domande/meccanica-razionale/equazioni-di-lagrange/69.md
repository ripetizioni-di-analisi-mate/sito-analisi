The homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the endpoint $O$ and moves in the vertical plane; besides the reaction, the hinge exerts on the rod a viscous resisting torque of moment $-\beta\,\dot{\vartheta}$, with $\beta > 0$ and $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical. What is the time derivative of the mechanical energy $E = T + V$ along the motion?

- $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\beta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$
- $0$
- $-\beta\,\dot{\vartheta}$
- $-\beta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$

## Explanation

The virtual work of the torque is $-\beta\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\delta\vartheta$, therefore $Q_\vartheta^{*} = -\beta\,\dot{\vartheta}$, which coincides with $-\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{D}}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}}$ for $\mathcal{D} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\beta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$. With $T = \dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and $V = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta$ the equation of motion is
$$
\frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = -\beta\,\dot{\vartheta} .
$$
The left-hand side multiplied by $\dot{\vartheta}$ is the derivative of $E = \dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta$, hence multiplying both sides by $\dot{\vartheta}$ gives $\dfrac{dE}{dt} = -\beta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$. The result equals $-2\,\mathcal{D}$, and the mechanical energy **decreases** as long as the rod is moving.
