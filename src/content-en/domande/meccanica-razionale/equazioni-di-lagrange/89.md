The material point $A$ of mass $m$ slides on a smooth fixed horizontal guide; a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$ is hinged at $A$ and carries at its other endpoint the material point $P$ of mass $m$, and the system moves in the vertical plane. What is the angular frequency of the small oscillations of the rod about the downward vertical?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2\,\ell}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,g}{\ell}}$

## Explanation

With the abscissa $x$ of $A$ and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical one has
$$
T = m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + m\,\ell\,\dot{x}\,\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
U = m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta .
$$
The coordinate $x$ is cyclic, therefore $p_x = 2\,m\,\dot{x} + m\,\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta$ is constant, and its zero derivative, linearized about $\vartheta = 0$, gives $\ddot{x} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta}$. The equation for $\vartheta$, divided by $m\,\ell$ and linearized, is $\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta} + \ddot{x} + g\,\vartheta = 0$, and substituting $\ddot{x}$ gives
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta} + g\,\vartheta = 0 .
$$
The angular frequency is therefore $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,g}{\ell}}$, larger than that of the pendulum with fixed suspension, because $A$ moves opposite to $P$.
