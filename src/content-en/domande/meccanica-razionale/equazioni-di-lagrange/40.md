The homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed inclined plane of angle $\alpha$ and starts from rest. What is the velocity of the center after the center has descended along the plane by the stretch $d$?

- $\sqrt{2\,g\,d\sin\alpha}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,g\,d\sin\alpha}{7}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g\,d\sin\alpha}{3}}$
- $2\,\sqrt{\dfrac{g\,d\sin\alpha}{3}}$

## Explanation

The pure rolling links the angular velocity to the velocity of the center by $|\dot{\varphi}| = \dfrac{\dot{s}}{R}$, therefore with $I_G = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$ the kinetic energy is $T = \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{s}^2$. The rolling constraint is ideal and the active load is the weight alone, hence the mechanical energy is conserved. The center descends by the height $d\sin\alpha$, hence the work of the weight is $m\,g\,d\sin\alpha$ and the balance between the initial configuration and the final one gives
$$
\frac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2 = m\,g\,d\sin\alpha .
$$
From this equality it follows that $v^2 = \dfrac{4}{3}\,g\,d\sin\alpha$, that is $v = 2\,\sqrt{\dfrac{g\,d\sin\alpha}{3}}$, smaller than the value $\sqrt{2\,g\,d\sin\alpha}$ that one would have for a point that slides without friction.
