The material point $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to the inner surface of a smooth fixed sphere, of center $O$ and radius $R$. The angle $\vartheta$ between the radius $OP$ and the downward vertical and the angle $\varphi$ that identifies the meridian plane on which $P$ lies are taken as Lagrangian coordinates. For which value of $\dot{\varphi}$ does the point describe the horizontal circle of colatitude $\vartheta_0$, with $0 < \vartheta_0 < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{R}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g\cos\vartheta_0}{R}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{R\cos\vartheta_0}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{R\sin\vartheta_0}}$

## Explanation

The kinetic energy of the point constrained to the sphere is $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\big( \dot{\vartheta}^2 + \sin^2\vartheta\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big)$ and the potential of the weight is $U = m\,g\,R\cos\vartheta$, therefore Lagrange's equation relative to $\vartheta$ is
$$
m\,R^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m\,R^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta\,\dot{\varphi}^2 - m\,g\,R\sin\vartheta .
$$
On the horizontal circle of colatitude $\vartheta_0$ the coordinate $\vartheta$ stays equal to $\vartheta_0$, hence $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ and $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$, and the equation becomes $R\cos\vartheta_0\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = g$ after the division by $m\,R\sin\vartheta_0$, which is legitimate since $\sin\vartheta_0 \neq 0$. From here it follows that $\dot{\varphi} = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{R\cos\vartheta_0}}$, which is the motion of the conical pendulum. The condition $0 < \vartheta_0 < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ guarantees $\cos\vartheta_0 > 0$, therefore the value exists.
