The homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the endpoint $O$ with a smooth fixed hinge and moves in the vertical plane. The rod starts from rest in the horizontal configuration. What is the magnitude of the reaction of the hinge at the instant when the rod passes through the downward vertical?

- $m\,g$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,g$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,g$
- $4\,m\,g$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical one has $T = \dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and $U = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta$; the constraints are fixed and the load is conservative, therefore $T - U$ is conserved. In the initial configuration $T - U = 0$, hence when passing through $\vartheta = 0$ one has $\dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell$, that is $\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \dfrac{3\,g}{\ell}$. In that configuration Lagrange's equation $\ddot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}\sin\vartheta$ gives $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$, so $G$ has only the centripetal acceleration $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\,g$, directed toward $O$. The first cardinal equation projected on the vertical then gives
$$
\Phi_O - m\,g = m \cdot \frac{3}{2}\,g ,
$$
hence the reaction is vertical, directed upward, and equals $\dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,g$.
