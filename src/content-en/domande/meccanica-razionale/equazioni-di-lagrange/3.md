The material point $P$ of mass $m$ slides on a smooth fixed horizontal straight guide, is connected to the fixed point $O$ of the guide by a spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero rest length and is subject to the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}_P$, with $h > 0$. Taking the abscissa $x$ of $P$ measured from $O$ as Lagrangian coordinate, what is the equation of motion?

- $m\,\ddot{x} + h\,\dot{x} + k\,x = 0$
- $m\,\ddot{x} - h\,\dot{x} + k\,x = 0$
- $m\,\ddot{x} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,h\,\dot{x} + k\,x = 0$
- $m\,\ddot{x} + h\,\dot{x} - k\,x = 0$

## Explanation

The kinetic energy is $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2$ and the potential of the spring is $U = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$, therefore the conservative part of the load gives $Q_x = \dfrac{\partial U}{\partial x} = -k\,x$. The viscous resistance admits the Rayleigh dissipation function $\mathcal{D} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,h\,\dot{x}^2$ and its Lagrangian component is obtained by differentiating $\mathcal{D}$ with respect to the generalized velocity, hence $Q_x^{*} = -\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{D}}{\partial \dot{x}} = -h\,\dot{x}$. Lagrange's equation with the non-conservative part on the right-hand side then gives $m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x - h\,\dot{x}$. Taking everything to the left-hand side gives $m\,\ddot{x} + h\,\dot{x} + k\,x = 0$, which is the equation of the damped harmonic oscillator.
