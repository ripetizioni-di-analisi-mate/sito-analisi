The wedge of mass $M$, with its inclined face at angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal, rests on a smooth horizontal floor and can slide on it; on the inclined face, also smooth, slides the material point $P$ of mass $m$, as in the diagram in the figure. The system moves in the vertical plane under the weight force alone. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the wedge?

::struttura{poligoni="0.5,0 4.5,0 0.5,2.31" linee="-0.6,0 6,0" punti="2,1.444,P" frecce="2.15,1.75,3.05,1.23,s;0.5,-0.55,1.7,-0.55,X" etichette="3.75,0.2,α;1.15,0.65,M;1.8,1.15,m" titolo="Point sliding on the smooth face of a wedge free to slide on the smooth floor"}

- $\dfrac{m\,g\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{M + m}$
- $\dfrac{m\,g\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{M}$
- $\dfrac{m\,g\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{M + m\sin^2\alpha}$
- $\dfrac{m\,g\sin\alpha}{M + m\sin^2\alpha}$

## Explanation

The abscissa $X$ of the wedge and the abscissa $s$ of $P$ along the face, measured downward, are taken as Lagrangian coordinates; the velocity of $P$ is the sum of $\dot{X}\,\hat{\imath}$ and of $\dot{s}$ along the face, therefore
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,M\,\dot{X}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{X}^2 + \dot{s}^2 + 2\,\dot{X}\,\dot{s}\cos\alpha \big) ,
\qquad
U = m\,g\,s\sin\alpha .
$$
The two Lagrange equations are
$$
\begin{cases}
(M + m)\,\ddot{X} + m\,\ddot{s}\cos\alpha = 0 \\[4pt]
\ddot{s} + \ddot{X}\cos\alpha = g\sin\alpha
\end{cases}
$$
From the first one obtains $\ddot{s} = -\dfrac{(M + m)\,\ddot{X}}{m\cos\alpha}$, and substituting into the second gives $\ddot{X} = -\dfrac{m\,g\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{M + m\sin^2\alpha}$. The minus sign indicates that the wedge moves opposite to the horizontal displacement of $P$, as required by the conservation of the horizontal component of the momentum, which is the first equation.
