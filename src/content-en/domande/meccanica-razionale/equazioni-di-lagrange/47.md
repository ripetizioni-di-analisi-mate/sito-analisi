The material point $P$ of mass $m$ slides on a smooth horizontal plane, rigidly attached to a platform that rises with constant velocity $u$ imposed from outside. The constraint is bilateral and the reaction of the plane is $\vec{\Phi} = \Phi\,\hat{k}$, with the $z$ axis pointing vertically upward. Which statement about the reaction is true?

- Its virtual work is zero and its power during the motion is $\Phi\,u$
- Its virtual work and its power during the motion are both zero
- Its virtual work is $\Phi\,u\,\delta t$, hence the constraint is not ideal
- Its power is zero, because the reaction is orthogonal to the velocity of $P$ relative to the platform

## Explanation

With the Lagrangian coordinates $x$ and $y$ of $P$ on the plane, the position of the point is $P - O = x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath} + (z_0 + u\,t)\,\hat{k}$, where $z_0$ is the initial height of the plane. Virtual displacements are computed with time held fixed, therefore $\delta P = \delta x\,\hat{\imath} + \delta y\,\hat{\jmath}$ is horizontal and $\vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta P = 0$ for every virtual displacement, hence the constraint is **ideal**. The actual velocity, instead, also contains the term $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial t} = u\,\hat{k}$, so the power of the reaction is
$$
\Pi = \vec{\Phi} \cdot \vec{v}_P = \Phi\,u ,
$$
which is nonzero. With a moving constraint the actual displacement in an interval $dt$ is not a virtual displacement, and the reaction of an ideal constraint can do work during the motion.
