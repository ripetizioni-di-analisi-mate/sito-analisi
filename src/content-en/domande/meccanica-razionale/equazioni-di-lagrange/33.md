On a holonomic system with ideal constraints the weight, an ideal spring and a viscous resistance proportional to the velocity act. What form do the Lagrange equations take?

- $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_h} - \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_h} = 0$, with $\mathcal{L}$ built on all the active forces
- $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_h} - \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_h} = Q_h^{*}$, with $\mathcal{L}$ built on the weight and on the spring
- $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{q}_h} - \dfrac{\partial T}{\partial q_h} = 0$
- $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_h} + \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_h} = Q_h^{*}$

## Explanation

The weight and the spring admit a potential, while the viscous resistance does not admit one, therefore the load is conservative only in part and the conservative contribution is separated from the rest by setting $Q_h = \dfrac{\partial U}{\partial q_h} + Q_h^{*}$. The potential $U$ contains the term $-m\,g\,y_G$ of every body and the term $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,(\ell - \ell_0)^2$ of the spring, hence the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = T + U$ collects the kinetic energy and these two forces. The component $Q_h^{*}$ of the viscous resistance stays on the right-hand side and is computed from the virtual work, or else by differentiating the Rayleigh dissipation function with respect to the generalized velocity. The equations are therefore $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_h} - \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_h} = Q_h^{*}$.
