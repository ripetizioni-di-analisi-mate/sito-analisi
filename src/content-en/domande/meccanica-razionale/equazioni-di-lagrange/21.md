In the vertical plane the material point $P$ of mass $m$ slides on a smooth straight guide inclined at $60°$ to the horizontal, which translates horizontally with constant velocity $u$ imposed from outside, as in the diagram in the figure. The abscissa $s$ of $P$ along the guide, measured upwards starting from the point where the guide meets the horizontal, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate. What is the term $T_1$ of the kinetic energy?

::struttura{linee="-0.3,-0.52 2.2,3.81" tratteggi="0,0 2.4,0" punti="1.2,2.078,P" frecce="0.2,-0.95,1.6,-0.95,u" etichette="0.78,0.24,60°;1.72,2.3,m" titolo="Inclined straight guide translating horizontally with constant velocity $u$"}

- $m\,u\,\dot{s}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,u\,\dot{s}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,m\,u\,\dot{s}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,u\,\dot{s}$

## Explanation

The guide translates, therefore the constraint is moving and the position of $P$ depends also on time:
$$
P - O = \left( u\,t + s\cos 60° \right) \hat{\imath} + s\sin 60°\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
Differentiating with respect to time gives $\vec{v}_P = \left( u + \dfrac{\dot{s}}{2} \right) \hat{\imath} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\dot{s}\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence $v_P^2 = \dot{s}^2 + u\,\dot{s} + u^2$ and the kinetic energy is $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{s}^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,u\,\dot{s} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,u^2$. The term $T_1$ is the one of first degree in the generalized velocity, hence it is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,u\,\dot{s}$, while $T_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{s}^2$ and $T_0 = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,u^2$. The coefficient $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the cosine of the angle between the guide and the direction of the translation.
