For the homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolling without slipping on a fixed inclined plane of angle $\alpha$, Lagrange's equation in the coordinate $s$ of the center coincides with the second cardinal equation written with a suitable pole, with no need for other equations. Which pole?

- The contact point between the disk and the plane
- The center of the disk
- The highest point of the disk
- Any fixed point of the inclined plane

## Explanation

The reaction of the plane is unknown and is applied at the contact point $A$, therefore its moment is zero only about $A$. The geometric contact point moves parallel to the center $G$, hence in the cardinal equation with a moving pole the term $\vec{v}_A \wedge \vec{Q}$ is zero and what remains is $\dfrac{d\vec{K}_A}{dt} = \vec{M}_A^{(e)}$. With $I_A = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2$, by the Huygens-Steiner theorem, and with the moment of the weight $m\,g\,R\sin\alpha$, one obtains
$$
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\ddot{\varphi} = m\,g\,R\sin\alpha ,
$$
which with $\ddot{s} = R\,\ddot{\varphi}$ gives $\ddot{s} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha$, that is, Lagrange's equation. With the pole at the center, instead, the moment of the friction force appears, which is unknown, and the first cardinal equation is needed as well.
