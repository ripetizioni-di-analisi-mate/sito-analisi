The material point $P$ of mass $m$ starts with negligible velocity from the top of a smooth fixed hemisphere, of center $O$ and radius $R$, resting with its base on a horizontal plane, and slides on its outer surface in a vertical plane through $O$, as in the diagram in the figure. To compute the reaction, the constraint $|P - O| = R$ is removed, the distance $r$ of $P$ from $O$ and the angle $\vartheta$ between $OP$ and the upward vertical are taken as Lagrangian coordinates, and the reaction $\Phi\,\hat{e}_r$, where $\hat{e}_r$ is the unit vector of $P - O$, is added to the active forces. What is $\Phi$ as long as the point stays on the hemisphere?

- $m\,g\cos\vartheta$
- $m\,g\,\big( 2\cos\vartheta - 1 \big)$
- $m\,g\,\big( 2 - 3\cos\vartheta \big)$
- $m\,g\,\big( 3\cos\vartheta - 2 \big)$

## Explanation

In the plane of motion the velocity of $P$ has the components $\dot{r}$ and $r\,\dot{\vartheta}$, and the height of $P$ above $O$ is $r\cos\vartheta$, therefore
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 \big) ,
\qquad
U = -m\,g\,r\cos\vartheta .
$$
The virtual displacement of $P$ has the component $\delta r$ along $\hat{e}_r$, hence the virtual work of the reaction is $\Phi\,\delta r$ and the reaction appears only in the Lagrangian component for $r$. Lagrange's equation for $r$ is
$$
m\,\ddot{r} - m\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = -m\,g\cos\vartheta + \Phi ,
$$
and imposing $r = R$ gives $\Phi = m\,g\cos\vartheta - m\,R\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$. The constraint is fixed and smooth and the weight is conservative, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved; since the initial velocity is negligible, one has $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = m\,g\,R\,\big( 1 - \cos\vartheta \big)$. Substituting $m\,R\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = 2\,m\,g\,\big( 1 - \cos\vartheta \big)$ gives
$$
\Phi = m\,g\,\big( 3\cos\vartheta - 2 \big) .
$$
The reaction vanishes for $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{2}{3}$, hence in that configuration the point leaves the hemisphere.
