The homogeneous rod $OA$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at the smooth fixed point $O$ and oscillates in the vertical plane; at the endpoint $A$ the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}_A$ acts, with $h > 0$. With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, what is the equation of motion?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta} + m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0$

## Explanation

The act of motion is a rotation about the fixed axis through $O$ and $I_O = \dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$, therefore $T = \dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and the left-hand side of the equation contains the term $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta}$. The potential of the weight is $U = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta$, hence its derivative with respect to $\vartheta$ is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta$. The endpoint $A$ has velocity of magnitude $\ell\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$, hence $\mathcal{D} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and $Q_\vartheta^{*} = -h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}$. Lagrange's equation with the non-conservative part on the right-hand side therefore gives
$$
\frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta} + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0 .
$$
