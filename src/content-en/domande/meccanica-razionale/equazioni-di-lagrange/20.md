The homogeneous ring of mass $m$ and radius $R$, with all the mass distributed on the circumference, rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide. Taking the abscissa $x$ of the center as Lagrangian coordinate, what is the kinetic energy of the ring?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2$
- $m\,\dot{x}^2$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2$

## Explanation

All the mass is distributed at distance $R$ from the axis through the center orthogonal to the plane of the motion, therefore the moment of inertia of the ring with respect to that axis is $I_G = m\,R^2$. The pure rolling links the angular velocity to the velocity of the center by $|\dot{\varphi}| = \dfrac{\dot{x}}{R}$, hence by König's theorem
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\frac{\dot{x}^2}{R^2} = m\,\dot{x}^2 .
$$
The kinetic energy is therefore twice that of a material point of mass $m$ which translates with the same velocity, and it exceeds the value $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2$ of the homogeneous disk of equal mass, whose moment of inertia is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$.
