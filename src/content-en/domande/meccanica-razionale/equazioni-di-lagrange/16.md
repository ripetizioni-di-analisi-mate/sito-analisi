In the vertical plane the material point $A$ slides on a smooth fixed horizontal guide; the rigid rod $AB$ is hinged at $A$ and the second rigid rod $BC$ is hinged at its endpoint $B$, as in the diagram in the figure. The hinges are smooth and all the constraints are holonomic, bilateral and ideal. How many Lagrange equations are written in order to study the motion of the system?

::struttura{nodi="A,1.6,0;B,2.6,-1.73;C,4.33,-2.73" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,carrello,90" cerniere="B" linee="-0.4,0 5.2,0" etichette="0.3,0.4,fixed guide" titolo="Point $A$ on a horizontal guide with the rods hinged at $A$ and at $B$"}

- $1$
- $2$
- $6$
- $3$

## Explanation

The number of Lagrange equations equals the number of degrees of freedom of the system, therefore the Lagrangian coordinates needed to identify the configuration are counted. The position of $A$ on the guide is given by its abscissa, the rod $AB$ is identified by the angle that it forms with the downward vertical through $A$ and the rod $BC$ by the angle that it forms with the downward vertical through $B$, hence the system has three degrees of freedom. The Lagrange equations are therefore three, and the reactions of the constraints, which are ideal, do not appear in them. The number of initial data needed to determine the motion is instead twice as large, since the three coordinates and the three generalized velocities are prescribed at the initial instant.
