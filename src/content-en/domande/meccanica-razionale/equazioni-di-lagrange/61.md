The material point $P$ of mass $m$ is connected to the fixed point $O$ by a spring of elastic constant $k$ and rest length $\ell_0$ and moves in the vertical plane, as in the diagram in the figure. The length $r$ of the spring and the angle $\vartheta$ between $OP$ and the downward vertical are taken as Lagrangian coordinates. What is Lagrange's equation for $r$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,1.014,-2.175" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" linee="0.000,0.000 0.106,-0.227 0.354,-0.286 0.125,-0.742 0.621,-0.860 0.393,-1.316 0.889,-1.434 0.660,-1.890 0.909,-1.949 1.014,-2.175" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.6" etichette="0.16,-1.05,ϑ;0.85,-0.75,k;1.35,-2.3,m" quote="O,P,r,-0.6" titolo="Elastic pendulum: the point $P$ is suspended from $O$ by a spring of constant $k$"}

- $m\,\ddot{r} = m\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + m\,g\cos\vartheta - k\,(r - \ell_0)$
- $m\,\ddot{r} = m\,g\cos\vartheta - k\,(r - \ell_0)$
- $m\,\ddot{r} = -m\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + m\,g\cos\vartheta - k\,(r - \ell_0)$
- $m\,\ddot{r} = m\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 - m\,g\cos\vartheta - k\,(r - \ell_0)$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates centered at $O$ the velocity of $P$ has the components $\dot{r}$ and $r\,\dot{\vartheta}$, and the point is at height $-r\cos\vartheta$, therefore
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 \big) ,
\qquad
U = m\,g\,r\cos\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,(r - \ell_0)^2 .
$$
One has $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{r}} = m\,\ddot{r}$ and $\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial r} = m\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$, while $Q_r = \dfrac{\partial U}{\partial r} = m\,g\cos\vartheta - k\,(r - \ell_0)$. Lagrange's equation for $r$ is therefore $m\,\ddot{r} - m\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = m\,g\cos\vartheta - k\,(r - \ell_0)$. The term $m\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ arises from the dependence of $T$ on $r$ and corresponds to the centripetal part of the radial acceleration.
