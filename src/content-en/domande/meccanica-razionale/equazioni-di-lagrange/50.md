The material point $P$ of mass $m$ moves freely in space and its position is given by the cylindrical coordinates $r$, $\varphi$, $z$, taken as Lagrangian coordinates. In which configurations is the kinetic matrix not invertible?

- At the points of the $z$ axis, where $r = 0$
- At the points of the plane $z = 0$
- At the points of the half-plane $\varphi = 0$
- In no configuration

## Explanation

The velocity of the point has the components $\dot{r}$, $r\,\dot{\varphi}$ and $\dot{z}$ along the radial, transverse and vertical directions, which are mutually orthogonal, therefore
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 + \dot{z}^2 \big) .
$$
The position does not depend on time, hence $T = T_2$ and the kinetic matrix is diagonal, with entries $m$, $m\,r^2$, $m$; its determinant is $m^3\,r^2$. The determinant vanishes only for $r = 0$, that is, at the points of the $z$ axis. At those points a variation of $\varphi$ does not move $P$, therefore the matrix of the derivatives $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial q_h}$ does not have rank $3$ and the cylindrical coordinates are not admissible Lagrangian coordinates; the theorem on the kinetic matrix holds in the configurations with $r > 0$.
