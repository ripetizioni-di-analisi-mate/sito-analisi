The homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide; on the center $C$ the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}_C$ acts, with $h > 0$. Taking the abscissa $x$ of $C$ as Lagrangian coordinate, what is the Rayleigh dissipation function?

- $\dfrac{3}{4}\,h\,\dot{x}^2$
- $h\,\dot{x}^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,h\,\dot{x}^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,h\,\dot{x}^2$

## Explanation

The Rayleigh dissipation function is $\mathcal{D} = \dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_i h_i\,v_i^2$, where the sum extends only to the points at which a viscous resistance acts. Here the resistance acts at the center $C$ alone, whose velocity has magnitude $|\dot{x}|$, therefore
$$
\mathcal{D} = \frac{1}{2}\,h\,\dot{x}^2 .
$$
The moment of inertia of the disk does not enter the computation, since $\mathcal{D}$ is built with the coefficients $h_i$ of the resistances and not with the masses. The Lagrangian component of the resistance is then $Q_x^{*} = -\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{D}}{\partial \dot{x}} = -h\,\dot{x}$.
