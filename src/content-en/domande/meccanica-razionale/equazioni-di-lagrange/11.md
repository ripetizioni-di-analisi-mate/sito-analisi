The homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $2m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the endpoint $O$ by a smooth fixed hinge and rotates freely in a smooth horizontal plane; a ring of mass $m$, which can be treated as the material point $P$, slides on the rod without friction and is connected to $O$ by a spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero rest length, as in the diagram in the figure, seen from above. Taking the angle $\varphi$ of rotation of the rod and the distance $r$ of $P$ from $O$ as Lagrangian coordinates, what is the kinetic momentum conjugate to $\varphi$?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\varphi}$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\,r^2 \right) \dot{\varphi}$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\,r^2 \right) \dot{\varphi}$
- $\left( \dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\,r^2 \right) \dot{\varphi}$

## Explanation

The rod rotates about the fixed axis through $O$ and its moment of inertia is $I_O = \dfrac{1}{3}\,(2m)\,\ell^2 = \dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$. The ring moves in the plane with polar coordinates $r$ and $\varphi$, therefore its velocity has the components $\dot{r}$ and $r\,\dot{\varphi}$ and the overall kinetic energy is
$$
T = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) .
$$
The kinetic momentum conjugate to $\varphi$ is by definition $p_\varphi = \dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\varphi}}$, hence it is $\left( \dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\,r^2 \right) \dot{\varphi}$. The plane is horizontal and the spring has a potential that depends only on $r$, therefore $\varphi$ is a cyclic coordinate and $p_\varphi$ remains constant along every motion.
