The rod $OA$ is hinged at the smooth fixed point $O$ and at $A$ a smooth hinge connects it to the second rod $AB$; the system moves in the vertical plane under the weight alone and all the constraints are ideal. What appears in the two Lagrange equations of the system?

- The active forces and the reaction of the internal hinge at $A$
- The active forces alone, through the Lagrangian components $Q_1$ and $Q_2$
- The active forces and the reaction of the fixed hinge at $O$
- The active forces and the reactions of both hinges

## Explanation

The Lagrange equations are obtained from the symbolic relation of dynamics, in which the reactions of the ideal constraints have already disappeared because their overall virtual work is zero. In this system the fixed hinge at $O$ applies the reaction at a point that does not move, while the internal hinge at $A$ applies opposite reactions to the two rods at the same point, therefore the sum of their virtual works is zero. In the two equations there appear hence only the kinetic energy and the Lagrangian components $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ of the active forces, which here reduce to the weight of the two rods. The cardinal equations, written body by body, would instead contain the reactions of the hinges, which would then have to be eliminated.
