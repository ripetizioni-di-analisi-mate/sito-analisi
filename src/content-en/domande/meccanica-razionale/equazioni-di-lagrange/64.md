The rod $OA$ is hinged at the fixed point $O$ and moves in the vertical plane; the Lagrangian coordinate is the angle $\vartheta$, measured in radians, between the rod and the downward vertical. In which SI unit is the Lagrangian component $Q_\vartheta$ of the active load measured?

- $\mathrm{N}$
- $\mathrm{N/m}$
- $\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}$

## Explanation

The Lagrangian component is defined so that the virtual work of the active load is $\delta L = Q_\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta$. Work is measured in joules, that is in $\mathrm{N\,m}$, and the increment $\delta\vartheta$ is an angle in radians, hence dimensionless; therefore $Q_\vartheta$ is measured in $\mathrm{N\,m}$. For an angular coordinate $Q_\vartheta$ is the moment of the active forces about the axis of rotation, as one sees for the weight of a homogeneous rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, which gives $Q_\vartheta = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta$. When the coordinate is a length, instead, the Lagrangian component is measured in newtons, while $\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}$ is the unit of the kinetic momentum $p_\vartheta$.
