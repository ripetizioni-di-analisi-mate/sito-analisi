The homogeneous rod $OA$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at the smooth fixed point $O$ and rotates in the vertical plane $Oxy$; at the endpoint $A$ the constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, horizontal, acts, as in the diagram in the figure. The angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical, measured towards the positive $x$ semiaxis, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate. What is the Lagrangian component of the force $\vec{F}$ alone?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.2,-2.078" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.4" punti="0.6,-1.039,G,baricentro" forze="A,0.9,0,F" etichette="0.3,-1.1,ϑ" quote="O,A,ℓ,-0.65" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with the constant horizontal force $\vec{F}$ applied at $A$"}

- $F\,\ell\sin\vartheta$
- $-F\,\ell\sin\vartheta$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,F\,\ell\cos\vartheta$
- $F\,\ell\cos\vartheta$

## Explanation

With $\vartheta$ measured from the downward vertical towards the positive $x$ semiaxis one has $A - O = \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore
$$
\frac{\partial (A - O)}{\partial \vartheta} = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The Lagrangian component of a force applied at a point is the scalar product between the force and the derivative of the position of that point with respect to the Lagrangian coordinate, hence
$$
Q_\vartheta = F\,\hat{\imath} \cdot \big( \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big) = F\,\ell\cos\vartheta .
$$
The result vanishes for $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the configuration in which the rod is horizontal and the force has zero moment with respect to $O$.
