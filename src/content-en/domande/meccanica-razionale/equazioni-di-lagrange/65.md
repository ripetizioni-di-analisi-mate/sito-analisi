For the simple pendulum formed by the material point of mass $m$ hanging from a rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$, with $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, the Lagrangian is $\mathcal{L} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta$. Which of the following functions, used in place of $\mathcal{L}$, gives the same equation of motion? The constant $c$ is a nonzero real number.

- $3\,\mathcal{L} + c$
- $\mathcal{L} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 - m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta$
- $\mathcal{L} + c\,\vartheta$

## Explanation

From the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}$ one obtains the equation $m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0$. With $3\,\mathcal{L} + c$ all the derivatives are multiplied by $3$ and the additive constant does not appear, therefore one obtains $3\,\big( m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta \big) = 0$, which is the **same equation**. The other functions change the motion:
$$
2\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0 ,
\qquad
m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} - m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0 ,
\qquad
m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta = c .
$$
With $\mathcal{L} + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ the inertial term doubles, with $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 - m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta$ one obtains the equation of the inverted pendulum and with $\mathcal{L} + c\,\vartheta$ a constant torque $c$ appears.
