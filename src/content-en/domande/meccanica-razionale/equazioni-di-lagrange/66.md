The homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is free on a smooth horizontal plane and is at rest. At the initial instant the force $\vec{F}$ of magnitude $F$, parallel to the plane and orthogonal to the rod, is applied at the endpoint $A$, as in the diagram in the figure, seen from above. What is the acceleration of the endpoint $B$ at that instant?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3.2,0" aste="A-B" punti="1.6,0,G,baricentro" forze="A,0,0.9,F" etichette="1.6,-0.45,m" quote="A,B,ℓ,-0.9" titolo="Rod free on a smooth horizontal plane, with the force $\vec{F}$ applied at the endpoint $A$ (top view)"}

- $\dfrac{F}{m}$, in the direction of $\vec{F}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{4\,F}{m}$, in the direction of $\vec{F}$
- $\dfrac{2\,F}{m}$, opposite to $\vec{F}$

## Explanation

The rod is a rigid body free in the plane, therefore it has three degrees of freedom. The coordinates $x_G$ and $y_G$ of the center of mass and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the $x$ axis are taken as Lagrangian coordinates, with the $x$ axis directed from $A$ to $B$ and the $y$ axis in the direction of $\vec{F}$ in the initial configuration. By König's theorem, with $I_G = \dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2$,
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{x}_G^2 + \dot{y}_G^2 \big) + \frac{1}{24}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 .
$$
The weight and the reaction of the smooth plane do no virtual work. For $\vartheta = 0$ the virtual displacement of $A$ is $\delta A = \delta x_G\,\hat{\imath} + \left( \delta y_G - \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta \right) \hat{\jmath}$, hence $\delta L = F\,\delta y_G - \dfrac{1}{2}\,F\,\ell\,\delta\vartheta$. Lagrange's equations at the initial instant are therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{x}_G = 0 \\[4pt]
m\,\ddot{y}_G = F \\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,F\,\ell
\end{cases}
$$
and give $\ddot{y}_G = \dfrac{F}{m}$ and $\ddot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{6\,F}{m\,\ell}$. The rod starts from rest, so the endpoint $B$, located at $G + \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big)$, has the acceleration
$$
\ddot{y}_B = \ddot{y}_G + \frac{\ell}{2}\,\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{F}{m} - \frac{3\,F}{m} = -\frac{2\,F}{m} .
$$
The endpoint $B$ therefore accelerates with magnitude $\dfrac{2\,F}{m}$ opposite to $\vec{F}$, while $\dfrac{4\,F}{m}$, in the direction of $\vec{F}$, is the acceleration of the endpoint $A$.
