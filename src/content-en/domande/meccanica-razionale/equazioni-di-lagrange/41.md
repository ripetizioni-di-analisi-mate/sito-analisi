In the vertical plane the homogeneous rod $AB$ has the endpoint $A$ on a smooth horizontal guide and the endpoint $B$ on a smooth vertical guide, with the two guides fixed and orthogonal, and an ideal spring connects $B$ to the point of intersection of the guides. Which statement about the mechanical energy of the system is true?

- It is constant along the motion, since the constraints are fixed and the active load is conservative
- It is not constant, since the reactions of the two guides do work during the motion
- It is constant only if the spring has zero rest length
- It is constant only in the equilibrium configurations of the system

## Explanation

The two guides are fixed and smooth, therefore their reactions are orthogonal to the virtual displacements of the points of application and the constraints are ideal, and the kinetic energy reduces to the quadratic form $T = T_2$. The active load is formed by the weight of the rod and by the force of the spring, which admit the potential $U$, hence the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = T + U$ does not depend explicitly on time. With fixed constraints the Jacobi function coincides with the mechanical energy, since $T_0 = 0$, therefore $E = T + V$ is a first integral of the motion. The rest length of the spring changes the expression of the potential but not this conclusion.
