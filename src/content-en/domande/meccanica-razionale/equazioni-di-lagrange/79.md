The homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed inclined plane of angle $\alpha$, in the vertical plane. Lagrange's equation gives the acceleration of the center along the plane, $\ddot{s} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha$, directed downward. What is the component of the reaction of the plane parallel to the plane itself?

- $0$, because the pure rolling constraint is ideal
- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,g\sin\alpha$, directed up the plane
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,g\sin\alpha$, directed up the plane
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,g\cos\alpha$, directed up the plane

## Explanation

Lagrange's equation does not contain the reaction of the ideal constraint, therefore to compute it one writes a cardinal equation for the disk. The first cardinal equation projected along the plane, with the positive direction downward and with $F_a$ the tangential component of the reaction directed upward, is
$$
m\,\ddot{s} = m\,g\sin\alpha - F_a .
$$
Substituting $\ddot{s} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha$ gives $F_a = \dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,g\sin\alpha$, positive, hence directed up the plane. The constraint is ideal because the reaction is applied at the contact point, which has zero velocity; it follows that the virtual work of the reaction is zero, while its tangential component equals $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,g\sin\alpha$.
