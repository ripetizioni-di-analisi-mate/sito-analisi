The carts $P_1$ and $P_2$, which can be treated as material points of mass $m$, slide on a smooth fixed horizontal guide and are connected by a viscous damper, which exerts on $P_1$ the force $-h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)\,\hat{\imath}$ and on $P_2$ the opposite force, with $h > 0$. With the abscissas $x_1$ and $x_2$ of the two carts as Lagrangian coordinates, what is the Lagrangian component $Q_1^{*}$ of the forces of the damper?

- $-h\,\dot{x}_1$
- $h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)$
- $-h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)$

## Explanation

The virtual work of the two forces of the damper is
$$
\delta L = -h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)\,\delta x_1 + h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)\,\delta x_2 ,
$$
therefore the coefficient of $\delta x_1$ is $Q_1^{*} = -h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)$. The same result follows from the Rayleigh dissipation function $\mathcal{D} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)^2$, since $-\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{D}}{\partial \dot{x}_1} = -h\,(\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)$. The damper depends only on the relative velocity, hence when the two carts move with the same velocity its forces are zero; the value $-h\,\dot{x}_1$ would instead correspond to a resistance of the external medium on $P_1$ alone.
