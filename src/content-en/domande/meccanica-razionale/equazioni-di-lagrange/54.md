A smooth horizontal platform rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega\,\hat{k}$, imposed from outside, about the fixed vertical axis through its point $O$. The material point $P$ of mass $m$ moves freely on the platform and its position is given by the coordinates $x$ and $y$ in a frame attached to the platform, with origin $O$. What is Lagrange's equation for $x$?

- $\ddot{x} - \omega\,\dot{y} - \omega^2\,x = 0$
- $\ddot{x} - 2\,\omega\,\dot{y} + \omega^2\,x = 0$
- $\ddot{x} - 2\,\omega\,\dot{y} - \omega^2\,x = 0$
- $\ddot{x} + 2\,\omega\,\dot{y} - \omega^2\,x = 0$

## Explanation

The absolute velocity of $P$ is the sum of the relative velocity $\dot{x}\,\hat{\imath}' + \dot{y}\,\hat{\jmath}'$ and of the transport velocity $\omega\,\hat{k} \wedge (P - O) = -\omega\,y\,\hat{\imath}' + \omega\,x\,\hat{\jmath}'$, where $\hat{\imath}'$ and $\hat{\jmath}'$ are the unit vectors attached to the platform, therefore
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\Big[ \big( \dot{x} - \omega\,y \big)^2 + \big( \dot{y} + \omega\,x \big)^2 \Big] .
$$
The weight is balanced by the reaction of the platform and the virtual displacements are horizontal, hence $Q_x = 0$. One has $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{x}} = m\,\big( \ddot{x} - \omega\,\dot{y} \big)$ and $\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial x} = m\,\omega\,\big( \dot{y} + \omega\,x \big)$, so Lagrange's equation is $\ddot{x} - 2\,\omega\,\dot{y} - \omega^2\,x = 0$. The term in $\dot{y}$ arises from $T_1 = m\,\omega\,\big( x\,\dot{y} - y\,\dot{x} \big)$ and corresponds to the Coriolis force, while $\omega^2\,x$ arises from $T_0$ and corresponds to the centrifugal force.
