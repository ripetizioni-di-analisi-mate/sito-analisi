## Explanation

The center $C$ describes a circle of radius $R - r$, therefore $v_C = (R - r)\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$. The contact point is the instantaneous center of rotation and is at distance $r$ from $C$, hence the angular velocity of the disk has magnitude $\dfrac{(R - r)\,|\dot{\vartheta}|}{r}$, different from $|\dot{\vartheta}|$. By König's theorem
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,(R - r)^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,m\,r^2 \cdot \frac{(R - r)^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2}{r^2} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,(R - r)^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
$$
while $U = m\,g\,(R - r)\cos\vartheta$. Lagrange's equation gives $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,(R - r)^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -m\,g\,(R - r)\sin\vartheta$, and for small oscillations $\sin\vartheta$ is replaced by $\vartheta$, which gives the angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,g}{3\,(R - r)}}$. The value $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{R - r}}$ is that of a point sliding without friction in the guide, with no rotational energy.
