For which of the systems listed does the kinetic energy reduce to the quadratic form $T = T_2$ in the generalized velocities?

- A ring that slides on a straight rod rotating with imposed constant angular velocity
- A point constrained to a circle that rotates with imposed constant angular velocity about one of its diameters
- A disk that rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide
- A point that slides on a straight guide which translates with imposed constant velocity

## Explanation

The kinetic energy reduces to $T = T_2$ when the functions $P_i = P_i(q_1, \dots, q_n)$ do not depend explicitly on time, since then the derivatives $\dfrac{\partial P_i}{\partial t}$ are zero and with them the coefficients $b_h$ and $c$. The disk rolling on a fixed guide has fixed constraints, therefore its kinetic energy $T = \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2$ is a quadratic form in the single generalized velocity. In the three remaining cases the constraint is moving, since time enters the position of the point through the imposed angle or the imposed translation, hence the term $T_0$ appears and, for the translating guide, the term $T_1$ as well. In those situations the mechanical energy is not conserved, whereas the Jacobi function remains constant if the Lagrangian does not depend explicitly on time.
