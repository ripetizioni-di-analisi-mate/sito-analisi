The homogeneous rod $OA$ is hinged at the smooth fixed point $O$ and rotates in the vertical plane. The Lagrange equations do not contain the reaction of the hinge. In what way is that reaction computed during the motion?

- A cardinal equation of dynamics is written for the rod
- The Lagrangian is differentiated with respect to the Lagrangian coordinate $\vartheta$
- The reaction is added among the active forces, leaving the hinge in place, and the component $Q_\vartheta$ is computed again
- It is required that the virtual work of the reaction be different from zero

## Explanation

Lagrange's method eliminates the reactions of the ideal constraints already in the symbolic relation of dynamics, therefore the equation of motion of the rod does not contain them and no derivative of the Lagrangian returns them. The reaction is then obtained from the cardinal equations of dynamics, since the first cardinal equation written for the rod links the reaction $\vec{\Phi}_O$ to the weight and to the acceleration of the center of mass, which the already known motion provides. Adding the reaction among the active forces, leaving the hinge in place, changes nothing, since its virtual work remains zero and the component $Q_\vartheta$ is not modified.
