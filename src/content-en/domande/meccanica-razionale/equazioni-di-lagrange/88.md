A pendulum consists of a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$, hinged at the fixed point $O$, which carries at its endpoint $C$ a homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ mounted on a smooth pin through the center of the disk, so that the disk can rotate freely relative to the rod, as in the diagram in the figure. The system moves in the vertical plane. What is the angular frequency of the small oscillations of the rod about the downward vertical?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;C,0.821,-2.255,-" catene="O-C" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" cerchi="0.821,-2.255,0.5" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.9" punti="0.821,-2.255,C,centro" etichette="0.16,-0.85,ϑ;1.5,-2.5,m" quote="O,C,ℓ,-0.5" titolo="Pendulum with the disk mounted on a smooth pin at its center $C$"}

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,g\,\ell}{2\,\ell^2 + R^2}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,g}{3\,\ell}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell + R}}$

## Explanation

The system has two degrees of freedom: the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical and the rotation angle $\psi$ of the disk, measured from a fixed direction. The center of the disk has speed $\ell\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$, therefore by König's theorem
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\dot{\psi}^2 ,
\qquad
U = m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta .
$$
The coordinate $\psi$ is cyclic and does not appear in the term in $\dot{\vartheta}$, hence $\dot{\psi}$ stays constant and the equation for $\vartheta$ is $m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta$, that of the simple pendulum of length $\ell$. The angular frequency of the small oscillations is therefore $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$. If the disk were welded to the rod one would have $\psi = \vartheta$, the moment of inertia about $O$ would be $m\,\ell^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$ and the angular frequency would be $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,g\,\ell}{2\,\ell^2 + R^2}}$.
