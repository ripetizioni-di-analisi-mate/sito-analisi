The pendulum in the figure consists of a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$, carrying at its endpoint $P$ a material point of mass $m$. The suspension point $O$ is moved along a fixed vertical line with the assigned law $y_O = a\cos(\Omega\,t)$, the hinge at $O$ is smooth and the pendulum moves in the vertical plane. With $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, what is the equation of motion?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,0.930,-1.994" catene="O-P" tratteggi="0,1.3 0,-2.5" frecce="-0.45,-0.55,-0.45,0.55,y_O" punti="0,0,O" etichette="0.2,-0.85,ϑ;1.25,-2.1,m;-1.75,0.85,y_O = a cos(Ωt)" quote="O,P,ℓ,-0.45" titolo="Pendulum with the suspension point moved along the vertical with an assigned law"}

- $\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta} + \big( g - a\,\Omega^2\cos(\Omega\,t) \big)\sin\vartheta = 0$
- $\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta} + \big( g + a\,\Omega^2\cos(\Omega\,t) \big)\sin\vartheta = 0$
- $\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta} + g\sin\vartheta = 0$
- $\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta} + \big( g - a\,\Omega\sin(\Omega\,t) \big)\sin\vartheta = 0$

## Explanation

The constraint is moving and the position of the material point is $P = \big( \ell\sin\vartheta,\ y_O - \ell\cos\vartheta \big)$, therefore
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + 2\,\ell\,\dot{y}_O\,\dot{\vartheta}\sin\vartheta + \dot{y}_O^2 \big) ,
\qquad
U = -m\,g\,\big( y_O - \ell\cos\vartheta \big) .
$$
One has $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} = m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + m\,\ell\,\big( \ddot{y}_O\sin\vartheta + \dot{y}_O\,\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta \big)$ and $\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta} = m\,\ell\,\dot{y}_O\,\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta$, hence the terms in $\dot{y}_O\,\dot{\vartheta}$ cancel and what remains is $m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} + m\,\ell\,\ddot{y}_O\sin\vartheta = -m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta$. Since $\ddot{y}_O = -a\,\Omega^2\cos(\Omega\,t)$, dividing by $m\,\ell$ gives $\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta} + \big( g - a\,\Omega^2\cos(\Omega\,t) \big)\sin\vartheta = 0$. The motion of the support is therefore equivalent to replacing $g$ with the effective gravity $g + \ddot{y}_O$, which varies in time.
