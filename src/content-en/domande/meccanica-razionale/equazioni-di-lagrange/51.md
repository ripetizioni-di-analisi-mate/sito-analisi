A system with two degrees of freedom has Lagrangian coordinates $q_1 = x$ and $q_2 = \vartheta$, and the angle $\vartheta$ can take any value. Which of the following matrices cannot be its kinetic matrix?

- $\begin{pmatrix} m & 0 \\ 0 & m\,\ell^2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 2\,m & m\,\ell\cos\vartheta \\ m\,\ell\cos\vartheta & m\,\ell^2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} m & 2\,m\,\ell\cos\vartheta \\ 2\,m\,\ell\cos\vartheta & m\,\ell^2 \end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix} 3\,m & m\,\ell\cos\vartheta \\ m\,\ell\cos\vartheta & m\,\ell^2 \end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

The kinetic matrix is positive definite in every configuration, and a symmetric $2 \times 2$ matrix with positive first entry is positive definite if and only if its determinant is positive. The determinants of the four matrices are
$$
m^2\,\ell^2 , \qquad
m^2\,\ell^2\,\big( 2 - \cos^2\vartheta \big) , \qquad
m^2\,\ell^2\,\big( 1 - 4\cos^2\vartheta \big) , \qquad
m^2\,\ell^2\,\big( 3 - \cos^2\vartheta \big) .
$$
Since $\cos^2\vartheta \le 1$, the first, the second and the fourth are positive for every $\vartheta$. The third, instead, equals $-3\,m^2\,\ell^2$ for $\vartheta = 0$, therefore that matrix is not positive definite and **cannot be** a kinetic matrix.
