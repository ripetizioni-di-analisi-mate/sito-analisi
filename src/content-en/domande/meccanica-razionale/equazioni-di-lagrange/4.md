The pulley is a homogeneous disk of mass $3m$ and radius $R$, hinged at the center to a smooth fixed horizontal axis. An inextensible string of negligible mass is wound on the pulley, does not slip on it and holds at its other end the material point $P$ of mass $m$, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the acceleration of $P$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0,-;T,1.1,0,-;P,1.1,-3" catene="T-P" cerchi="0,0,1.1" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="0,0,O,centro" etichette="-1.9,0.4,3m;1.35,-3.05,m" titolo="Pulley hinged at the center with the material point $P$ hanging from the string"}

- $g$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,g$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}\,g$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,g$

## Explanation

The string is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, therefore the angular velocity of the pulley and the descent velocity of $P$ are linked by $|\dot{\varphi}| = \dfrac{\dot{y}}{R}$ and the system has a single degree of freedom. The moment of inertia of the pulley with respect to its axis is $I = \dfrac{1}{2}\,(3m)\,R^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2$, hence
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2 \cdot \frac{\dot{y}^2}{R^2} = \frac{5}{4}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 .
$$
The center of the pulley stays at rest, hence the only weight that does work is that of $P$ and the potential is $U = m\,g\,y$ with $y$ measured downwards. Lagrange's equation gives $\dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,\ddot{y} = m\,g$, therefore the acceleration of $P$ is $\dfrac{2}{5}\,g$, directed downwards and smaller than $g$ because part of the work of the weight goes into the rotational motion of the pulley.
