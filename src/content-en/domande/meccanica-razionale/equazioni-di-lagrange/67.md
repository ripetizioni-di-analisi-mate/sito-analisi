The material point $P$ of mass $m$ moves in the plane and is attracted by the fixed point $O$ with a force of magnitude $\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r^2}$, where $r$ is the distance of $P$ from $O$ and $\mu > 0$ is a constant. With the polar coordinates $r$ and $\varphi$ centered at $O$ as Lagrangian coordinates, and with the convention $\vec{F} = \operatorname{grad} U$, what is the Lagrangian?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) - \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) + \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) + \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2 + \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates the velocity has the radial component $\dot{r}$ and the transverse component $r\,\dot{\varphi}$, therefore $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big)$. The force is radial and directed toward $O$, that is $\vec{F} = -\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r^2}\,\hat{e}_r$, hence the potential depends only on $r$ and must satisfy $\dfrac{dU}{dr} = -\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r^2}$. It follows that $U = \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$, up to an additive constant, and the Lagrangian is
$$
\mathcal{L} = T + U = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) + \frac{\mu\,m}{r} .
$$
The expression with $-\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$ is $T - U$, that is, it has the sign of the potential reversed.
