The ring $P$ of mass $m$ slides without friction on a straight rod which rotates in a smooth horizontal plane with constant angular velocity $\omega$, imposed from outside, about its fixed point $O$. With $r$ the distance of $P$ from $O$, which function remains constant along the motion?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,r^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,r^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2$
- $m\,\dot{r}$

## Explanation

The plane is horizontal and smooth, therefore the weight is balanced by the reaction of the plane and does zero virtual work, hence $U = 0$. The velocity of the ring has the component $\dot{r}$ along the rod and the component $\omega\,r$ orthogonal to it, hence
$$
T_2 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2 ,
\qquad
T_0 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,r^2 .
$$
The constraint is moving, therefore the kinetic energy $T = T_2 + T_0$ is not conserved; the Lagrangian, however, does not depend explicitly on time, since $\omega$ is constant, hence the Jacobi function $\mathcal{H} = T_2 - T_0 + V$ is a first integral. With $V = 0$ the constant function is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,r^2$, which is obtained from the kinetic energy by changing the sign of the term $T_0$.
