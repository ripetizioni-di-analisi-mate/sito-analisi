In which of the listed systems is the Jacobi function not a first integral of the motion?

- A ring on a straight rod rotating in a horizontal plane with constant imposed angular velocity, connected by a spring to the fixed point of the rod
- A material point on a smooth circular guide rotating with constant imposed angular velocity about its own vertical diameter
- A cart on a smooth horizontal guide with a hinged pendulum, in the vertical plane
- A material point on a smooth horizontal guide, pulled by a spring toward a fixed point and subject to the horizontal force $F_0\cos(\Omega\,t)\,\hat{\imath}$

## Explanation

Along the motion $\dfrac{d\mathcal{H}}{dt} = -\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial t}$, therefore the Jacobi function is a first integral if and only if the Lagrangian does not depend explicitly on time. In the first two systems the constraints are moving, but the angular velocity is constant, hence $T_2$ and $T_0$ depend only on the configuration; in the third the constraints are fixed and $\mathcal{H}$ coincides with the mechanical energy. In the last one the force $F_0\cos(\Omega\,t)\,\hat{\imath}$ has at every instant the potential $F_0\,x\cos(\Omega\,t)$, and the Lagrangian
$$
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 + F_0\,x\cos(\Omega\,t)
$$
depends explicitly on time. It follows that $\dfrac{d\mathcal{H}}{dt} = F_0\,\Omega\,x\sin(\Omega\,t)$, which is not identically zero, hence in this system $\mathcal{H}$ is **not** conserved.
