A blade, modeled as a rod $OA$ of length $\ell$, rotates immersed in a fluid about the fixed axis passing through the endpoint $O$ and orthogonal to the blade. Each element $ds$ of the blade receives the viscous resistance $-c\,\vec{v}\,ds$, where $\vec{v}$ is the velocity of the element and $c > 0$ is a constant. With $\vartheta$ the rotation angle of the blade, what is the Rayleigh dissipation function?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,c\,\ell^3\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}\,c\,\ell^3\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{8}\,c\,\ell^3\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,c\,\ell^3\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$

## Explanation

The element of the blade at distance $s$ from $O$ has speed $s\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$ and receives a resistance with coefficient $c\,ds$, which takes the place of $h_i$ in the definition of $\mathcal{D}$. The sum thus becomes an integral along the blade:
$$
\mathcal{D} = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\ell} c\,s^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\,ds = \frac{1}{6}\,c\,\ell^3\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 .
$$
The Lagrangian component of the resistance is therefore $Q_\vartheta^{*} = -\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{D}}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} = -\dfrac{1}{3}\,c\,\ell^3\,\dot{\vartheta}$, which is the resisting moment about the axis. The values $\dfrac{1}{8}\,c\,\ell^3\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}\,c\,\ell^3\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ are obtained by concentrating the whole resistance $c\,\ell$ at the midpoint or at the endpoint $A$.
