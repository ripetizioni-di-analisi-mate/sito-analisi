The material point $P$ of mass $m$ slides down a fixed rough inclined plane of angle $\alpha$, with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu$. The abscissa $s$ of $P$ along the plane, measured downward, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate, and during the descent $\dot{s} > 0$. What is the Lagrangian component $Q_s^{*}$ of the friction?

- $-\mu\,m\,g\sin\alpha$
- $-\mu\,m\,g\cos\alpha$
- $\mu\,m\,g\cos\alpha$
- $-\mu\,m\,g$

## Explanation

The point slides on the plane, therefore the tangential component of the reaction does negative virtual work and the constraint is not ideal; the friction is thus treated as an active force. The trajectory is straight, so the acceleration has no component normal to the plane and Newton's law projected on the normal gives the normal reaction $N = m\,g\cos\alpha$. By the Coulomb-Morin law the kinetic friction has magnitude $\mu\,N$ and direction opposite to the velocity, that is, up the plane. The virtual displacement has the direction of the plane, therefore the virtual work of the friction is $-\mu\,m\,g\cos\alpha\,\delta s$ and
$$
Q_s^{*} = -\mu\,m\,g\cos\alpha .
$$
The equation of motion is therefore $m\,\ddot{s} = m\,g\,\big( \sin\alpha - \mu\cos\alpha \big)$.
