The homogeneous disk of center $C$, mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, in the vertical plane. The horizontal force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$ acts on the point of the disk that occupies the top, as in the diagram in the figure. With the abscissa $x$ of $C$ as Lagrangian coordinate, what is the Lagrangian component of $\vec{F}$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,1,-;C,3,1,-" cerchi="3,1,1" linee="-0.5,0 6,0" punti="3,1,C,baricentro;3,0,H,centro;3,2,S" forze="3 2,1.1,0,F" quote="0 0,3 0,x,0.6" titolo="Rolling disk, with the horizontal force applied at the highest point $S$"}

- $F$
- $2\,F$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,F$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,F$

## Explanation

The contact point $H$ has zero velocity and is the instantaneous center of rotation, therefore every point of the disk has velocity proportional to its distance from $H$. The highest point $S$ is at distance $2\,R$ from $H$, while $C$ is at distance $R$, hence the velocity of $S$ is $2\,\dot{x}\,\hat{\imath}$ and its virtual displacement is $\delta S = 2\,\delta x\,\hat{\imath}$. The virtual work of the force is then $\vec{F} \cdot \delta S = 2\,F\,\delta x$, and the Lagrangian component is
$$
Q_x = 2\,F .
$$
The weight and the reaction of the guide do not contribute, because the guide is horizontal and the pure rolling constraint is ideal.
