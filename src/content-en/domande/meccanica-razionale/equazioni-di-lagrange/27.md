A holonomic system with ideal constraints has three degrees of freedom and positive definite kinetic matrix. How many initial data are needed in order to determine the motion?

- $3$
- $4$
- $9$
- $6$

## Explanation

The Lagrange equations are three, one for each degree of freedom, and in each of them the derivative $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{q}_h}$ produces the terms $\displaystyle\sum_k a_{hk}\,\ddot{q}_k$ besides terms that contain only $q$, $\dot{q}$ and $t$. The kinetic matrix is positive definite, hence it is invertible and the system is solved for the accelerations, taking the normal form $\ddot{q} = \mathbb{A}^{-1}\,\vec{g}(q, \dot{q}, t)$. To a system of three second-order differential equations in normal form the existence and uniqueness theorem for the Cauchy problem applies, which requires the prescription of the configuration $q(t_0)$ and of the generalized velocities $\dot{q}(t_0)$. The initial data are therefore $3 + 3 = 6$, that is twice the number of degrees of freedom.
