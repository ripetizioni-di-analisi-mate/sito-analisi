- $\ddot{s} = g\sin\alpha$
- $\ddot{s} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha$
- $\ddot{s} = \dfrac{2}{7}\,g\sin\alpha$
- $\ddot{s} = \dfrac{5}{7}\,g\sin\alpha$

## Explanation

The pure rolling constraint links the angular velocity of the sphere to the velocity of the center by $|\dot{\varphi}| = \dfrac{\dot{s}}{R}$, therefore the system has a single degree of freedom. By König's theorem the kinetic energy is the sum of the term of the center of mass and that of the relative rotational motion, hence
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{s}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{5}\,m\,R^2 \cdot \frac{\dot{s}^2}{R^2} = \frac{7}{10}\,m\,\dot{s}^2 .
$$
The center descends by the height $s\sin\alpha$, therefore the potential of the weight is $U = m\,g\,s\sin\alpha$ up to an additive constant and $Q_s = m\,g\sin\alpha$. Lagrange's equation gives $\dfrac{7}{5}\,m\,\ddot{s} = m\,g\sin\alpha$, that is $\ddot{s} = \dfrac{5}{7}\,g\sin\alpha$. The acceleration is smaller than $g\sin\alpha$, which is the value for a point that slides without friction on the same plane, because part of the energy goes into the rotational motion.
