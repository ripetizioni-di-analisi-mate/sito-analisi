Lagrange's equations in the form $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{q}_h} - \dfrac{\partial T}{\partial q_h} = Q_h$ are proved for holonomic systems with ideal constraints. To which of the listed systems do they not apply in this form?

- A disk rolling without slipping on a fixed straight guide, in the vertical plane
- A double pendulum formed by two hinged rods, with smooth hinges
- A material point on a smooth straight guide rotating with constant imposed angular velocity
- A coin rolling without slipping on a horizontal plane while staying vertical and free to change direction

## Explanation

For the coin one uses the coordinates $x$ and $y$ of the contact point, the angle $\psi$ giving the direction of the plane of the coin and the rotation angle $\varphi$. Pure rolling imposes
$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{x} = R\,\dot{\varphi}\cos\psi \\[4pt]
\dot{y} = R\,\dot{\varphi}\sin\psi
\end{cases}
$$
and these relations among the velocities cannot be integrated into relations among the coordinates alone, because $\psi$ changes during the motion. The constraint is therefore **nonholonomic**, and the variations $\delta x$, $\delta y$, $\delta\varphi$, $\delta\psi$ are not independent, hence in the proof the coefficient of each of them cannot be set to zero separately. In the other three systems the constraints are holonomic; in particular, rolling on a straight guide integrates into the relation $x = R\,\varphi + c$ among the coordinates, and the rotating guide is a moving but holonomic constraint.
