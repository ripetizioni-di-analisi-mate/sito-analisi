The homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, rests with the endpoint $A$ on a smooth horizontal floor and moves in the vertical plane, as in the diagram in the figure. The rod starts from rest, tilted from the vertical, and falls keeping $A$ on the floor. Which statement about the motion of the center of mass $G$ is true?

::struttura{nodi="A,1,0;B,2.026,2.819" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,270" linee="-0.5,0 4,0" tratteggi="1,0 1,3" punti="1.513,1.41,G,baricentro" etichette="1.18,0.75,ϑ;2.1,1.6,m" quote="A,B,2ℓ,0.5" titolo="Rod resting with the endpoint $A$ on a smooth floor"}

- $G$ describes an arc of circle centered at $A$
- The endpoint $A$ stays at rest and $G$ rotates about it
- $G$ moves along a vertical straight line
- $G$ describes an arc of ellipse

## Explanation

The abscissa $x_G$ of the center of mass and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the vertical are taken as Lagrangian coordinates; the height of $G$ is $\ell\cos\vartheta$, therefore
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{x}_G^2 + \ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\sin^2\vartheta \big) + \frac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
U = -m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta .
$$
Neither $T$ nor $U$ depends on $x_G$, hence $x_G$ is a cyclic coordinate and the conjugate momentum $p_{x_G} = m\,\dot{x}_G$ is constant. The rod starts from rest, therefore $p_{x_G} = 0$ throughout the motion and $x_G$ stays constant, hence $G$ moves along the **vertical line** through its initial position. The result expresses the fact that the weight and the reaction of the smooth floor are vertical, while the endpoint $A$ slides horizontally.
