Two material points of masses $m$ and $2m$ are connected by an inextensible string of negligible mass and length $\ell$ and move on a smooth horizontal plane. At the initial instant the center of mass of the system is at rest; from that instant the string stays taut and rotates with constant angular velocity of magnitude $\omega$. What is the tension of the string?

- $m\,\omega^2\,\ell$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,\ell$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,\omega^2\,\ell$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\omega^2\,\ell$

## Explanation

The weights and the reactions of the plane are vertical and balance each other, whereas the tension of the string is an internal force. By the **theorem on the motion of the center of mass** the point $G$ therefore has zero acceleration and, since it is at rest at the beginning, it stays at rest. The center of mass divides the string into parts inversely proportional to the masses, therefore the point of mass $m$ is at distance $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\ell$ from $G$ and the point of mass $2m$ is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{3}$. Each point then travels along a circle with center $G$ with constant angular velocity $\omega$, and the Newton equation of the point of mass $m$, projected onto the centripetal direction, gives
$$
\tau = m\,\omega^2\,\frac{2}{3}\,\ell = \frac{2}{3}\,m\,\omega^2\,\ell .
$$
The same value is obtained from the point of mass $2m$, since $2m\,\omega^2\,\dfrac{\ell}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,\omega^2\,\ell$, as the principle of action and reaction requires for the two forces exerted by the string.
