Two coaxial homogeneous disks, of radius $R$ and masses $m$ and $2m$, can rotate without friction about a common fixed axis. The first rotates with angular velocity $\omega_0$ and the second is at rest; the second is brought into contact with the first and, because of the friction between the two faces in contact, in the end the two disks rotate together. How much energy has been dissipated by friction?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2$

## Explanation

The axis is fixed and smooth and the friction between the disks is an internal force, hence the axial moment of the external forces vanishes and the axial angular momentum of the system is conserved. The moments of inertia equal $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$ and $m\,R^2$, therefore
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0 = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_f , \qquad \omega_f = \frac{\omega_0}{3} .
$$
The initial kinetic energy equals $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2$ and the final one $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2 \cdot \dfrac{\omega_0^2}{9} = \dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2$. The dissipated energy is the difference, that is $\dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2$, because the friction between the two faces does negative work as long as the faces slide on each other.
