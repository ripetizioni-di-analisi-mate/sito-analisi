In a ballistic pendulum a bullet of mass $m$, with horizontal velocity of magnitude $v_0$, embeds itself in a block of mass $9m$ hanging at rest from a vertical inextensible string of length $\ell$. How high does the block with the bullet rise at most, assuming that the string stays taut?

::struttura{nodi="S,0,3.2,-" vincoli="S,incastro,90" linee="0,3.2 0,0.6" poligoni="-0.4,-0.2 0.4,-0.2 0.4,0.6 -0.4,0.6" punti="-2.2,0.2,,pieno" frecce="-2,0.2,-0.6,0.2" etichette="-1.3,0.5,v_0;-2.2,-0.2,m;0.85,0.2,9m;0.3,1.9,ℓ" titolo="Ballistic pendulum: the bullet embeds itself in the block"}

- $\dfrac{v_0^2}{200\,g}$
- $\dfrac{v_0^2}{20\,g}$
- $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,g}$
- $\dfrac{v_0^2}{100\,g}$

## Explanation

During the impact the string is vertical and its impulse is vertical, hence no external impulses act in the horizontal direction and the horizontal component of the linear momentum is conserved. Immediately after the impact the common velocity therefore equals $V = \dfrac{m\,v_0}{10\,m} = \dfrac{v_0}{10}$. In the subsequent rise the string is inextensible and its tension is orthogonal to the velocity, hence the mechanical energy is conserved and
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,(10m)\,V^2 = 10\,m\,g\,h , \qquad h = \frac{V^2}{2\,g} = \frac{v_0^2}{200\,g} .
$$
The kinetic energy is not conserved in the impact, which is perfectly inelastic, and the value $\dfrac{v_0^2}{20\,g}$ would be obtained by equating the kinetic energy of the bullet alone to the final potential energy.
