A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius negligible with respect to $R$ starts from rest at a height $h$ on a track ending in a loop-the-loop, that is, a vertical circular guide of radius $R$ traveled on the inside, and always rolls without slipping. What is the minimum height $h$, measured from the lowest point of the circular guide, that allows the disk to travel the whole loop without detaching?

- $\dfrac{11}{4}\,R$
- $2\,R$
- $\dfrac{13}{4}\,R$
- $\dfrac{7}{2}\,R$

## Explanation

At the highest point of the guide the reaction is directed downward, hence the first cardinal equation projected onto the centripetal direction gives $m\,\dfrac{v^2}{R} = m\,g + \Phi$. The condition for not detaching is $\Phi \ge 0$, therefore the velocity of the center at that point must satisfy $v^2 \ge g\,R$. The disk rolls without slipping, hence the reaction does no work and the mechanical energy is conserved, with $T = \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2$. In the limiting case
$$
m\,g\,h = m\,g\,(2R) + \frac{3}{4}\,m\,g\,R , \qquad h = \frac{11}{4}\,R .
$$
For a material point sliding without friction the minimum height would be $\dfrac{5}{2}\,R$. The disk requires a greater height, because part of the energy goes into the rotation.
