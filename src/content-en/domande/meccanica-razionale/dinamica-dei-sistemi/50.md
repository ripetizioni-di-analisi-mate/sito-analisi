A projectile of mass $2m$, at the highest point of its trajectory, has horizontal velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$ and explodes into two fragments of mass $m$. Immediately after the explosion one of the two fragments has zero velocity and from there falls vertically. What velocity does the other fragment have immediately after the explosion?

- $2\,v_0\,\hat{\imath}$
- $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\sqrt{2}\,v_0\,\hat{\imath}$
- $3\,v_0\,\hat{\imath}$

## Explanation

The forces of the explosion are internal to the system of the two fragments, hence they do not change the total linear momentum. The weight, the only external force, has a negligible impulse during the very short duration of the explosion, therefore the linear momentum immediately before and immediately after is the same. Before it equals $2m\,v_0\,\hat{\imath}$ and after it equals $m\,\vec{u}$, since the first fragment is at rest, hence
$$
m\,\vec{u} = 2\,m\,v_0\,\hat{\imath} , \qquad \vec{u} = 2\,v_0\,\hat{\imath} .
$$
The kinetic energy is not conserved, because the internal forces of the explosion do positive work. The center of mass of the two fragments continues along the parabola that the projectile would have described without exploding.
