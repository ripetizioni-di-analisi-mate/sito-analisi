- $\dfrac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$
- $m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$

## Explanation

The endpoints of the rod slide along two orthogonal guides, hence the instantaneous center of rotation $C$ is the point where the normals to the two guides drawn through $A$ and through $B$ meet, and the distance $|G - C|$ is equal to half the length of the rod, that is $\ell$. From the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics it then follows that $v_G = \ell\,\dot{\vartheta}$. The moment of inertia about the axis through $G$ orthogonal to the plane equals $\dfrac{1}{12}\,(2m)\,(2\ell)^2 = \dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$, therefore
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 .
$$
The distance $|G - C|$ stays constant during the motion, hence the kinetic energy does not depend on $\vartheta$.
