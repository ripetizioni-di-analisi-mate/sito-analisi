A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ has an inextensible string of negligible mass wound around its rim; the free end of the string is fixed to the ceiling and the free part is vertical, as in the figure. The disk is let go downward and the string unwinds without slipping. What is the tension of the string during the descent?

::struttura{nodi="S,1,3,-" vincoli="S,incastro,90" cerchi="0,0,1" linee="1,0 1,3" punti="0,0,G,baricentro" tratteggi="0,0 1,0" etichette="0.5,0.25,R;-0.75,-1.2,m" titolo="Disk descending while unwinding a string fixed to the ceiling"}

- $\dfrac{m\,g}{3}$
- $m\,g$
- $\dfrac{m\,g}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,g$

## Explanation

The disk has no fixed points, hence one writes the first cardinal equation and the second one with pole at $G$. Denoting by $a$ the downward acceleration of $G$ and by $\tau$ the tension, the string unwinds without slipping, therefore the angular acceleration equals $\dfrac{a}{R}$ and
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a = m\,g - \tau \\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{a}{R} = \tau\,R
\end{cases}
$$
The first is the first cardinal equation projected onto the vertical, the second is the second cardinal equation with pole $G$, where the weight has zero moment. From the second one obtains $\tau = \dfrac{m\,a}{2}$, and substituting into the first gives $a = \dfrac{2}{3}\,g$, hence $\tau = \dfrac{m\,g}{3}$.
