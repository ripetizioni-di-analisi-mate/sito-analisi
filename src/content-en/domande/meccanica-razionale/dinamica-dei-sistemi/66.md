A dumbbell, formed by two material points at the ends of a rigid rod of negligible mass, moves in an arbitrary way in the plane, with the center of mass $G$ in motion. The angular momentum about $G$ is computed once with the absolute velocities $\vec{v}_i$ and once with the velocities $\vec{v}_i'$ relative to the frame translating with $G$. Which statement is true?

- The two sums give the same vector, because $\sum_i m_i\,(P_i - G) = \vec{0}$
- Only the sum with the relative velocities gives $\vec{K}_G$, because $G$ is moving
- The two sums differ by the term $(G - O) \wedge M\,\vec{v}_G$
- The two sums coincide only if $\vec{v}_G$ is parallel to the rod

## Explanation

With $\vec{v}_i = \vec{v}_G + \vec{v}_i'$ the sum with the absolute velocities reads
$$
\sum_i (P_i - G) \wedge m_i\,\vec{v}_i = \sum_i (P_i - G) \wedge m_i\,\vec{v}_i' + \Big( \sum_i m_i\,(P_i - G) \Big) \wedge \vec{v}_G .
$$
By the definition of center of mass $\sum_i m_i\,(P_i - G) = \vec{0}$, hence the second term vanishes and the two sums coincide. This equality is the step of the proof of **König's theorem** that allows one to compute $\vec{K}_G$ with either velocity. The term $(G - O) \wedge M\,\vec{v}_G$ appears instead when one passes from the pole $G$ to a different pole $O$.
