Three material points form a system and attract one another in pairs with forces directed along the lines joining them, equal and opposite in pairs. What are the resultant $\vec{R}^{(i)}$ and the resultant moment $\vec{M}_O^{(i)}$ of these forces?

- $\vec{R}^{(i)} = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{M}_O^{(i)} = \vec{0}$ for every pole $O$
- $\vec{R}^{(i)} = \vec{0}$, whereas $\vec{M}_O^{(i)}$ vanishes only if $O$ coincides with the center of mass
- $\vec{R}^{(i)} = \vec{0}$, whereas $\vec{M}_O^{(i)}$ in general is different from zero
- Both depend on the distances between the three points

## Explanation

The hypotheses say that for every pair of points we have $\vec{F}_{ij} = -\,\vec{F}_{ji}$ and that the vector $\vec{F}_{ij}$ is parallel to $P_i - P_j$, that is the action and reaction principle in the strong form is satisfied. Adding the contributions in pairs, every sum $\vec{F}_{ij} + \vec{F}_{ji}$ vanishes, therefore $\vec{R}^{(i)} = \vec{0}$. For the moments, the sum of the two terms of a pair equals $(P_i - P_j) \wedge \vec{F}_{ij}$, which vanishes since the two factors are parallel. Every pair therefore gives a vanishing contribution and $\vec{M}_O^{(i)} = \vec{0}$ for every pole $O$, whatever the positions of the three points are.
