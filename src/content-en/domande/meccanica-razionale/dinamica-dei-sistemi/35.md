A rigid body moves in a plane with a roto-translational state of motion. What is the power of its internal forces?

- It vanishes only if the state of motion is translational
- It is equal and opposite to the power of the external forces
- It vanishes in every state of motion
- It vanishes only if the body is homogeneous

## Explanation

For a pair of points $P_i$ and $P_j$ of the body the power of the two internal forces equals $\vec{F}_{ij} \cdot (\vec{v}_i - \vec{v}_j)$, since $\vec{F}_{ji} = -\,\vec{F}_{ij}$. The fundamental formula of rigid kinematics gives $\vec{v}_i - \vec{v}_j = \vec{\omega} \wedge (P_i - P_j)$, a vector orthogonal to $P_i - P_j$, whereas $\vec{F}_{ij}$ is parallel to $P_i - P_j$, hence the scalar product vanishes. Every pair therefore gives a vanishing contribution and the power of the internal forces vanishes in any state of motion. The kinetic energy theorem then reduces to $\dot{T} = \Pi^{(e)}$, and the same holds for a system of rigid bodies joined by ideal constraints.
