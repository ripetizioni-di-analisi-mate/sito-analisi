A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide and its center is connected to a fixed point by an ideal spring of elastic constant $k$, parallel to the guide. The plane is horizontal, hence the weight does no work. The system starts from rest with the spring stretched by a length $d$ with respect to the rest length. What magnitude does the velocity of the center have at the instant at which the spring reaches the rest length?

- $d\,\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$
- $d\,\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{3\,m}}$
- $d\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,k}{m}}$
- $d\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,k}{3\,m}}$

## Explanation

The reaction of the guide is applied at the contact point, which has zero velocity, hence its power vanishes and the only force that does work is the one of the spring. The mechanical energy is therefore conserved, and the work of the spring between the initial configuration and the one at rest length equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,d^2$. The kinetic energy of the rolling disk equals $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2$, hence
$$
\frac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,d^2 .
$$
From this equality it follows that $v^2 = \dfrac{2\,k\,d^2}{3\,m}$, therefore $v = d\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,k}{3\,m}}$. The value $d\,\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ would be obtained by neglecting the kinetic energy of the rotation.
