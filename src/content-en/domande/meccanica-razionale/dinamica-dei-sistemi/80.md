Two material points are connected by an ideal spring of elastic constant $k$ and rest length $\ell_0$. At the given instant their distance equals $2\,\ell_0$ and increases at the rate $u$. What is the power of the internal forces exerted by the spring?

- $0$, because the internal forces are equal and opposite
- $k\,\ell_0\,u$
- $-\,k\,\ell_0\,u$
- $-2\,k\,\ell_0\,u$

## Explanation

The spring is stretched by $2\,\ell_0 - \ell_0 = \ell_0$, hence it exerts on the two points restoring forces of magnitude $k\,\ell_0$, each directed toward the other point. Adding the powers in pairs one obtains $\vec{F}_{12} \cdot (\vec{v}_1 - \vec{v}_2)$, which depends only on the relative velocity. The component of the relative velocity along the line joining the points equals $u$ and moves the two points apart, whereas the forces pull them together, therefore
$$
\Pi^{(i)} = -\,k\,\ell_0\,u .
$$
The power is nonzero because the distance between the two points changes, whereas in a rigid body, where the distances are constant, the power of the internal forces vanishes.
