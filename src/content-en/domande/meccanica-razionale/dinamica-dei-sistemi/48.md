Two material points of mass $m$ are connected by an ideal spring and move freely in a smooth horizontal plane, with no external forces in the plane. Are the two cardinal equations of dynamics enough to determine the motion of the system?

- Yes, because they provide three scalar equations for three degrees of freedom
- Yes, because the force of the spring is internal and does not appear in them
- No, because they provide three scalar equations, whereas the coordinates to be determined are four
- No, because they provide only two scalar equations, those of the first cardinal equation

## Explanation

Each point has two coordinates in the plane, hence the system has four degrees of freedom. In the plane the cardinal equations provide three scalar equations: two from the first one, on the components $x$ and $y$, and one from the second one, on the component along $\hat{k}$. The force of the spring is internal, therefore it appears in none of the three, and neither does the distance between the two points. The cardinal equations therefore determine the motion of the center of mass and the angular momentum, whereas the oscillation along the line joining the points remains undetermined and is obtained from the Newton equations of the single points. For a single plane rigid body, instead, the degrees of freedom are three and the cardinal equations are enough.
