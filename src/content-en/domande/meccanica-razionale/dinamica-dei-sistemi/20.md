A rigid body is thrown in vacuum and during the flight it rotates about itself. What trajectory does its center of mass describe?

- A parabola only if the body does not rotate
- A trajectory that depends on the initial angular velocity
- A parabola, with constant acceleration $-g\,\hat{\jmath}$
- A parabola only if the body is homogeneous

## Explanation

By the theorem on the motion of the center of mass we have $M\,\vec{a}_G = \vec{R}^{(e)}$, where $\vec{R}^{(e)}$ is the resultant of the external forces alone. In vacuum the only external force is the weight, whose resultant equals $-M\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence $\vec{a}_G = -g\,\hat{\jmath}$ throughout the flight. The acceleration of the center of mass is therefore constant and its trajectory is a parabola. The rotation of the body is due to the internal forces and to the moment of the weight about $G$, which do not enter the first cardinal equation, hence it does not change the motion of $G$.
