A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ rolls without slipping on a fixed guide with angular velocity of magnitude $\omega$, and $I_C$ denotes its moment of inertia about the axis through the contact point $C$ orthogonal to the plane of the motion. Which expression gives the kinetic energy of the disk?

- $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\omega^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2$
- $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\omega^2$
- $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\omega^2$
- $T = I_C\,\omega^2$

## Explanation

In rolling without slipping the velocity of the contact point vanishes, hence the state of motion is rotatory about the axis through $C$ and the kinetic energy equals $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\omega^2$. The term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2$ is already contained in this expression, since $I_C = I_G + m\,R^2$ and $v_G = \omega\,R$, therefore adding it again counts the same contribution twice. The expression $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\omega^2$ instead gives the energy of the motion relative to the center of mass alone and neglects the translation of $G$. The two equivalent ways of writing it are therefore $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\omega^2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\omega^2$.
