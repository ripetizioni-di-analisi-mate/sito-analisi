Two bodies, which can be treated as material points of masses $m$ and $4m$, rest on a smooth horizontal plane and are kept in contact with a compressed ideal spring, of negligible mass, which stores the elastic energy $E_0$. The system starts from rest and the bodies separate when the spring reaches its rest length. What magnitude does the velocity of the body of mass $m$ have after the separation?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,E_0}{5\,m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,E_0}{m}}$
- $2\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,E_0}{5\,m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,E_0}{5\,m}}$

## Explanation

In the horizontal direction no external forces act and the system starts from rest, hence the linear momentum stays zero and the magnitudes of the two velocities satisfy $m\,v_1 = 4m\,v_2$, from which $v_2 = \dfrac{v_1}{4}$. The plane is smooth and the spring is ideal, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved and all the elastic energy becomes kinetic energy, that is
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,(4m)\,\frac{v_1^2}{16} = E_0 .
$$
The left-hand side equals $\dfrac{5}{8}\,m\,v_1^2$, hence $v_1^2 = \dfrac{8\,E_0}{5\,m}$ and therefore $v_1 = 2\,\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,E_0}{5\,m}}$.
