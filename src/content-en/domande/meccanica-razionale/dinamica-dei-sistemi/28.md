Two material points of mass $m$ each move with velocities $\vec{v}_1 = v\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{v}_2 = -v\,\hat{\imath}$. What is the kinetic energy of the motion relative to the center of mass of the system?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2$
- $2\,m\,v^2$
- $m\,v^2$

## Explanation

The kinetic energy of the system equals $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 = m\,v^2$. The velocity of the center of mass is obtained from $\vec{Q} = M\,\vec{v}_G$, and since $\vec{Q} = m\,v\,\hat{\imath} - m\,v\,\hat{\imath} = \vec{0}$ it turns out that $\vec{v}_G = \vec{0}$. König's theorem gives $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,M\,v_G^2 + T'$, hence the first summand vanishes and $T' = m\,v^2$. The center of mass is at rest, therefore all the kinetic energy is energy of the motion relative to it.
