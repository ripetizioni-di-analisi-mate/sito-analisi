A material point of mass $m$ moves on a smooth horizontal plane, tied to an inextensible string of negligible mass which passes through a hole $O$ in the plane, as in the figure. At the beginning the point travels along a circle of radius $3R$ with velocity of magnitude $v_0$. The string is pulled slowly from below until the radius becomes $R$. What magnitude does the final velocity of the point have?

::struttura{punti="0,0,O,centro;4.5,0,P,pieno" linee="0,0 4.5,0" frecce="4.5,0,4.5,2.2,v_0" quote="0 0,4.5 0,3R,0.7" etichette="4.85,-0.35,m" titolo="View from above: the point turns about the hole, held by the string"}

- $9\,v_0$
- $3\,v_0$
- $\dfrac{v_0}{3}$
- $v_0$

## Explanation

The tension of the string is always directed toward the hole $O$, hence its moment about $O$ vanishes and the angular momentum $\vec{K}_O$ is conserved. In both configurations the velocity is orthogonal to the radius, therefore the magnitude of the angular momentum equals $m\,v_0\,(3R)$ at the beginning and $m\,v_f\,R$ at the end. From the equality of the two values it follows that $v_f = 3\,v_0$. The kinetic energy, on the contrary, increases, since the tension does work while the radius decreases.
