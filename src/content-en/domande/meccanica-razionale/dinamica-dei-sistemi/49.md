Two material points of equal mass $m$ slide on a smooth horizontal plane with velocities $v\,\hat{\imath}$ and $v\,\hat{\jmath}$, collide and stick together. What velocity does the resulting body have immediately after the impact?

- Magnitude $v$, directed along the bisector between $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$
- Magnitude $\dfrac{v}{2}$, directed along the bisector between $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$
- Magnitude $\sqrt{2}\,v$, directed along the bisector between $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$
- Magnitude $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,v$, directed along the bisector between $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

No external impulsive forces act in the plane of the impact, hence the linear momentum of the system is conserved as a vector. Before the impact $\vec{Q}^- = m\,v\,\hat{\imath} + m\,v\,\hat{\jmath}$, and after the impact the joined body has mass $2m$ and velocity $\vec{V}$, therefore
$$
\vec{V} = \frac{m\,v\,\hat{\imath} + m\,v\,\hat{\jmath}}{2\,m} = \frac{v}{2}\,\big(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}\big) .
$$
The magnitude of $\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}$ is $\sqrt{2}$, hence $|\vec{V}| = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,v$, and the direction is the bisector between the two axes. The components are conserved separately, whereas the magnitudes of the velocities do not add up.
