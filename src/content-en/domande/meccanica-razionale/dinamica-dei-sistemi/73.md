A homogeneous rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ rotates with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega}$ about a fixed axis that passes through its center of mass $G$ and forms an angle of $60^\circ$ with the rod. Which statement about the angular momentum $\vec{K}_G = \mathbb{I}_G\,\vec{\omega}$ is true?

- $\vec{K}_G$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$, because the axis passes through $G$
- $\vec{K}_G$ is zero, because the center of mass is at rest
- $\vec{K}_G$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$, because the rod is homogeneous
- $\vec{K}_G$ is not parallel to $\vec{\omega}$, because the axis of rotation is not a principal axis of inertia

## Explanation

In the principal frame with the axis $\xi$ along the rod the principal moments of inertia equal $0$, $\dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2$, $\dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2$, and the vector $\vec{\omega}$ has components $\omega\,\cos 60^\circ$ along the rod and $\omega\,\sin 60^\circ$ along an orthogonal direction $\eta$. The product $\mathbb{I}_G\,\vec{\omega}$ cancels the component along the rod, hence
$$
\vec{K}_G = \frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega\,\sin 60^\circ\,\hat{\eta} ,
$$
which is orthogonal to the rod and is not parallel to $\vec{\omega}$. The vector $\vec{\omega}$ is an eigenvector of $\mathbb{I}_G$, and therefore parallel to $\vec{K}_G$, only when the axis of rotation is a principal axis of inertia, that is, along the rod or orthogonal to it. During the motion $\vec{K}_G$ rotates together with the rod, therefore its derivative is nonzero and the supports of the axis must exert a moment.
