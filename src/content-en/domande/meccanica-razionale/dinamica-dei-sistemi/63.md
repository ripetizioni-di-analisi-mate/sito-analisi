A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ rotates in a smooth horizontal plane about a fixed hinge at the endpoint $A$, with constant angular velocity of magnitude $\omega$. What horizontal force does the hinge exert on the rod?

- None, because the angular velocity is constant
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,\ell$, directed along the rod toward $A$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,\ell$, directed along the rod toward $B$
- $m\,\omega^2\,\ell$, directed along the rod toward $A$

## Explanation

In the horizontal plane the only external force on the rod is the reaction of the hinge, because the weight and the reaction of the plane are vertical and balance each other. By the **theorem on the motion of the center of mass** that reaction equals $m\,\vec{a}_G$. The center of mass travels along a circle of radius $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ with constant angular velocity, hence its acceleration is only centripetal, directed from $G$ toward $A$, with magnitude $\omega^2\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}$. The hinge therefore exerts the force $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,\ell$ directed along the rod toward $A$, and this force is nonzero even though the angular velocity is constant.
