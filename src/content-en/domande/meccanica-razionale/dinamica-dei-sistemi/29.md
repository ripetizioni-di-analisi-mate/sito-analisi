A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ translates in a plane without rotating, with velocity $v\,\hat{\imath}$, and its center occupies the point $(0, R)$. What is the angular momentum of the disk about the origin $O$?

- $\vec{0}$
- $-\,m\,v\,R\,\hat{k}$
- $m\,v\,R\,\hat{k}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v\,R\,\hat{k}$

## Explanation

König's theorem gives $\vec{K}_O = \vec{K}_G + (G - O) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_G$. The state of motion is translational, hence the angular velocity vanishes and $\vec{K}_G = \vec{0}$, whereas the transport term equals
$$
(R\,\hat{\jmath}) \wedge (m\,v\,\hat{\imath}) = m\,v\,R\,(\hat{\jmath} \wedge \hat{\imath}) = -\,m\,v\,R\,\hat{k} .
$$
It follows that $\vec{K}_O = -\,m\,v\,R\,\hat{k}$. The angular momentum about a pole different from $G$ is therefore different from zero even when the body does not rotate, since the linear momentum $m\,v\,\hat{\imath}$ has arm $R$ with respect to $O$.
