A plank of mass $2m$ rests on a smooth horizontal floor. A block of mass $m$ is launched onto the plank with horizontal velocity $v_0$, as in the figure, and slides on it with friction until the two bodies move with the same velocity. What is the total work of the friction forces that the block and the plank exchange?

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 5,0 5,0.4 0,0.4;0.3,0.4 1.3,0.4 1.3,1.2 0.3,1.2" linee="-0.6,0 6,0" frecce="1.3,0.8,2.6,0.8" etichette="0.8,0.8,m;3.5,0.2,2m;2.1,1.1,v_0" titolo="Block launched onto a plank free to slide on a smooth floor"}

- $0$
- $-\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{6}$

## Explanation

The floor is smooth, hence no horizontal external forces act on the system formed by the block and the plank, and the horizontal linear momentum is conserved. The final common velocity is therefore $V = \dfrac{m\,v_0}{3\,m} = \dfrac{v_0}{3}$. The vertical external forces do no work, hence by the kinetic energy theorem the work of the internal forces equals the change of the kinetic energy:
$$
L^{(i)} = \frac{1}{2}\,(3m)\,\frac{v_0^2}{9} - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 = \frac{m\,v_0^2}{6} - \frac{m\,v_0^2}{2} = -\frac{m\,v_0^2}{3} .
$$
The two friction forces are equal and opposite, but their points of application have different velocities as long as the block slides, therefore their total work is negative and nonzero.
