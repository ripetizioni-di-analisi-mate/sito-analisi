A material system has total mass $M$ and center of mass $G$. For which poles $O$ is the second cardinal equation of dynamics written in the form $\dot{\vec{K}}_O = \vec{M}_O^{(e)}$?

- Only for $O$ fixed
- For $O$ fixed, for $O$ coinciding with $G$ and for $O$ whose velocity is parallel to $\vec{v}_G$
- For $O$ fixed and for $O$ coinciding with $G$, in no other case
- For every pole, moving ones included

## Explanation

With a moving pole the second cardinal equation holds in the form $\dot{\vec{K}}_O + \vec{v}_O \wedge M\,\vec{v}_G = \vec{M}_O^{(e)}$, hence the simplest form is obtained when the term $\vec{v}_O \wedge M\,\vec{v}_G$ vanishes. That cross product vanishes when $\vec{v}_O = \vec{0}$, that is with a fixed pole, and it vanishes also when $O$ coincides with $G$, since then $\vec{v}_O = \vec{v}_G$ and the two factors are parallel. For the same reason it vanishes when $\vec{v}_O$ is parallel to $\vec{v}_G$, hence the suitable poles are three. In the exercises the first two cases are used, since the third one requires the direction of $\vec{v}_G$ to be known already.
