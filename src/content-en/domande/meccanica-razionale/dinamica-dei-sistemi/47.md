A car starts from rest and accelerates on a horizontal road, with the wheels rolling without slipping. Consider the system formed by the whole car, engine included. Which external force makes the center of mass of the system accelerate forward?

- The force that the engine exerts on the driving wheels
- The static friction force that the road exerts on the driving wheels
- The normal reaction that the road exerts on the wheels
- The friction force that the driving wheels exert on the road

## Explanation

By the **theorem on the motion of the center of mass**, $M\,\vec{a}_G = \vec{R}^{(e)}$, hence the acceleration of $G$ is due only to the forces external to the system. The engine is part of the car, therefore the forces it exerts on the wheels are internal forces and their resultant vanishes. The external forces are the weight and the normal reactions of the road, both vertical, and the friction forces that the road exerts on the tires. The only horizontal external force directed forward is therefore the static friction that the road exerts on the driving wheels. The force that the wheels exert on the road is applied to the road, which does not belong to the system, and therefore it does not enter the first cardinal equation of the car.
