A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is placed on a rough horizontal plane with its center at rest and an angular velocity of magnitude $\omega_0$, clockwise. The disk slips, and friction sets it in motion until it starts rolling without slipping. What is the magnitude of the velocity of the center from that instant on?

- $\dfrac{R\,\omega_0}{2}$
- $R\,\omega_0$
- $\dfrac{R\,\omega_0}{\sqrt{3}}$
- $\dfrac{R\,\omega_0}{3}$

## Explanation

The pole is chosen at a fixed point $O$ of the plane. The friction force acts along the plane, hence its moment about $O$ vanishes, whereas the weight and the normal reaction have the same vertical line of action and zero resultant moment; therefore the angular momentum about $O$ is conserved. At the beginning $G$ is at rest and $K_O = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0$ in magnitude. When the disk rolls with center velocity $v$, by **König's theorem** $K_O = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{v}{R} + m\,R\,v = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R\,v$, with the same direction. From the equality
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0 = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,R\,v
$$
it follows that $v = \dfrac{R\,\omega_0}{3}$. The mechanical energy is not conserved, because during the slipping friction dissipates energy.
