A flywheel, which can be treated as a homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$, rotates about its fixed axis with angular velocity $\omega_0$. From a certain instant a brake applies a constant axial moment of magnitude $\Gamma$, opposite to the rotation, and the friction of the axis is negligible. After how much time does the flywheel stop?

- $\dfrac{m\,R^2\,\omega_0}{2\,\Gamma}$
- $\dfrac{m\,R^2\,\omega_0}{\Gamma}$
- $\dfrac{2\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0}{\Gamma}$
- $\dfrac{m\,R^2\,\omega_0}{4\,\Gamma}$

## Explanation

The axis is fixed, hence one projects the second cardinal equation onto the axis of rotation: the axial angular momentum equals $I\,\omega$ with $I = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$, and its derivative equals the axial moment of the external forces. The reaction of the axis and the weight have zero axial moment, therefore
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dot{\omega} = -\,\Gamma .
$$
The angular acceleration is constant, hence $\omega(t) = \omega_0 - \dfrac{2\,\Gamma}{m\,R^2}\,t$, which vanishes for $t = \dfrac{m\,R^2\,\omega_0}{2\,\Gamma}$.
