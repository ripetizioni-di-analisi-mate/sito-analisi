A system of rigid bodies with smooth and bilateral constraints is subject to the weight and to ideal springs, and one of the guides along which a body of the system slides moves with a law assigned in time. Is the mechanical energy of the system conserved?

- No, because the constraints are not fixed
- Yes, because all the forces that do work are conservative
- Yes, because the constraints are smooth and bilateral
- No, because the springs dissipate energy

## Explanation

The theorem on the conservation of the mechanical energy requires that the constraints be fixed and ideal and that all the forces that do work be conservative. Here the second hypothesis is satisfied, since the weight and the ideal springs admit a potential, whereas the first one is not satisfied, since one guide moves with an assigned law. The reaction of a smooth moving constraint is orthogonal to the constraint, but the velocity of the contact point is not tangent to the constraint in the instantaneous configuration, therefore that reaction does work in general and the mechanical energy changes. The ideal springs remain conservative, hence the loss is not due to them.
