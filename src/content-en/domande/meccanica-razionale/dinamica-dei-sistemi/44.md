A material point of mass $m$, moving with velocity of magnitude $v_0$, strikes elastically, along the line joining them, a material point of mass $3m$ initially at rest. What velocity does the point of mass $m$ have immediately after the impact?

- $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$, in the same direction
- $\dfrac{v_0}{4}$, in the opposite direction
- $\dfrac{3\,v_0}{4}$, in the same direction
- $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$, in the opposite direction

## Explanation

In the elastic impact between two points that move along the line joining them, with the second one initially at rest, the conservation of the linear momentum and that of the kinetic energy give
$$
v_1^+ = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1^- .
$$
With $m_1 = m$ and $m_2 = 3m$ one obtains $v_1^+ = \dfrac{m - 3m}{4m}\,v_0 = -\dfrac{v_0}{2}$. The negative sign says that the point of mass $m$ bounces back, hence after the impact it moves in the direction opposite to the initial one with velocity of magnitude $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$. The velocity $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$ in the same direction is instead the one acquired by the point of mass $3m$, which equals $\dfrac{2\,m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_0$.
