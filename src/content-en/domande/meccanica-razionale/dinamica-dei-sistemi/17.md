Two bodies hang from the endpoints of an inextensible string of negligible mass which passes over a pulley hinged at a fixed point, as in the figure, and the string does not slip on the pulley. The system formed by the two bodies, by the string and by the pulley is considered. How is the tension of the string classified and where does it appear?

- It is external, hence it appears in both cardinal equations of the system
- It is external, and its moment about the center of the pulley vanishes
- It is internal, and it appears nonetheless in the second cardinal equation of the system
- It is internal, hence it does not appear in the cardinal equations of the system

## Explanation

The string belongs to the system under consideration, hence the forces that it exerts on the two bodies and on the pulley are exerted by a body internal to the system and are therefore internal forces. The resultant and the resultant moment of the internal forces vanish, hence the tension appears neither in the first nor in the second cardinal equation of the whole system. The external forces remain the weight of the two bodies, the weight of the pulley and the reaction of the fixed hinge. To obtain the tension one must therefore write the equations of one of the bodies taken on its own, for which the string is an external body.
