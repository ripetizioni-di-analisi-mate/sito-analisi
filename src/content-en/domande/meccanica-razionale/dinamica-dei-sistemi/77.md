A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$, initially at rest, rolls without slipping on a horizontal guide and is pulled by a constant horizontal force of magnitude $F$ applied at the highest point of the disk, as in the figure (for instance by a tape unwound from the top). What is the magnitude of the velocity of the center when the center has traveled a distance $d$?

::struttura{cerchi="3,1.5,1.5" linee="0,0 7,0" punti="3,1.5,G,baricentro;3,0,C,centro" forze="4.3 3,1.3,0," etichette="4.5,3.3,F" titolo="Rolling disk pulled by a force applied at the highest point"}

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,F\,d}{3\,m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,F\,d}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,F\,d}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{8\,F\,d}{3\,m}}$

## Explanation

The reaction of the guide is applied at the contact point, which has zero velocity, hence it does no work, and the weight is orthogonal to the displacement. The highest point of the disk has velocity $2\,v_G$, therefore the power of the force equals $2\,F\,v_G$ and, while the center travels the distance $d$, the work of the force is $2\,F\,d$. The kinetic energy of the rolling disk equals $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v_G^2$, hence the kinetic energy theorem gives
$$
\frac{3}{4}\,m\,v_G^2 = 2\,F\,d , \qquad v_G = \sqrt{\frac{8\,F\,d}{3\,m}} .
$$
The value $\sqrt{\dfrac{4\,F\,d}{3\,m}}$ is the one obtained with the force applied at the center, whose point of application travels only the distance $d$.
