A disk rolls without slipping on a fixed inclined plane and the friction at the contact point $C$ is the one that prevents the slipping. What is the power of the reaction of the plane, including the normal component and the friction component?

- It is negative, because the friction dissipates energy
- It vanishes only if the friction is negligible
- It is negative and its magnitude equals the power of the weight
- It vanishes, because the reaction is applied at $C$, which has zero velocity

## Explanation

The power of a force applied at a point equals the scalar product of the force with the velocity of that point. In rolling without slipping the velocity of the contact point vanishes, hence the power of the reaction of the plane equals $\vec{\Phi} \cdot \vec{v}_C = 0$, whatever the normal component and the friction component are. The friction dissipates energy only when the two surfaces slide one on the other, that is when the contact point of the body has velocity different from zero. For this reason the mechanical energy of a disk rolling on a fixed inclined plane is conserved, even though friction is present.
