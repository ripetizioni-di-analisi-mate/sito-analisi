A disk of radius $R$ rolls without slipping to the right on a horizontal guide, with its center having velocity $v\,\hat{\imath}$, and a couple of moment $\Gamma\,\hat{k}$, with $\Gamma > 0$, acts on it. What is the power of the couple?

- $\dfrac{\Gamma\,v}{R}$
- $0$, because the resultant of the couple vanishes
- $-\dfrac{\Gamma\,v}{R}$
- $-\dfrac{2\,\Gamma\,v}{R}$

## Explanation

The power of a system of forces applied to a rigid body equals $\Pi = \vec{R} \cdot \vec{v}_G + \vec{M}_G \cdot \vec{\omega}$, hence for a couple, which has zero resultant and the same moment about every pole, it reduces to $\Pi = \Gamma\,\hat{k} \cdot \vec{\omega}$. The disk rolls to the right, therefore the pure rolling condition gives $\vec{\omega} = -\dfrac{v}{R}\,\hat{k}$, that is, a clockwise rotation. It follows that
$$
\Pi = \Gamma\,\hat{k} \cdot \Big( -\frac{v}{R}\,\hat{k} \Big) = -\frac{\Gamma\,v}{R} .
$$
The power is negative, because the counterclockwise couple opposes the clockwise rotation of the disk and tends to slow it down.
