A material point of mass $m$, moving with velocity of magnitude $v_0$, strikes a material point of mass $3m$ at rest and the two remain joined. What fraction of the initial kinetic energy is lost in the impact?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{16}$

## Explanation

No impulsive external forces act, hence the linear momentum is conserved in the impact and the common velocity after the impact equals $v^+ = \dfrac{m\,v_0}{4m} = \dfrac{v_0}{4}$. The kinetic energy before the impact equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2$, whereas after the impact it equals
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,(4m)\,\frac{v_0^2}{16} = \frac{1}{8}\,m\,v_0^2 .
$$
The energy lost is therefore $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 - \dfrac{1}{8}\,m\,v_0^2 = \dfrac{3}{8}\,m\,v_0^2$, and its ratio to the initial energy equals $\dfrac{3}{4}$. The impact is perfectly inelastic, hence the fraction lost depends only on the ratio of the two masses.
