A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ falls in a vertical plane staying horizontal, without rotating. At the instant when it has velocity of magnitude $v$ directed downward, the endpoint $A$ hits a fixed edge, as in the figure, and stays hooked to it; from that moment on the edge behaves as a smooth hinge. What is the magnitude of the angular velocity of the rod immediately after the impact?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,2;B,4,2" aste="A-B" poligoni="-1.6,0 0,0 0,1.95 -1.6,1.95" linee="-2,0 0.6,0" punti="2,2,G,baricentro" frecce="2,3.3,2,2.35,v" quote="A,B,ℓ,0.6" titolo="Rod falling horizontally that hits a fixed edge with the endpoint $A$"}

- $\dfrac{6\,v}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{2\,v}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}\,v}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{3\,v}{2\,\ell}$

## Explanation

During the impact the only external impulsive force is the reaction of the edge, applied at $A$, whereas the weight has a negligible impulse. The moment of that reaction about $A$ vanishes, hence in the impact the angular momentum about the fixed point $A$ is conserved. Before the impact the rod translates, therefore its angular momentum about $A$ is the moment of the linear momentum applied at $G$, which has lever arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, and it equals $m\,v\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ in magnitude. After the impact the rod rotates about $A$ with moment of inertia $I_A = \dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$, hence
$$
m\,v\,\frac{\ell}{2} = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega , \qquad \omega = \frac{3\,v}{2\,\ell} .
$$
The impact is inelastic and the kinetic energy is not conserved, therefore the value $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}\,v}{\ell}$, which would be obtained by equating the kinetic energies before and after the impact, is wrong.
