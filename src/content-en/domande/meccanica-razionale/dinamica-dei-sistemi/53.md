Two material points of masses $m$ and $2m$, connected by an ideal spring, rest on a smooth horizontal plane. A constant horizontal force $\vec{F}$ is applied to the point of mass $m$, while the spring stretches and shortens. What acceleration does the center of mass of the system have?

- $\dfrac{\vec{F}}{m}$
- $\dfrac{\vec{F}}{2\,m}$
- It depends on the elongation of the spring
- $\dfrac{\vec{F}}{3\,m}$

## Explanation

The weight and the reactions of the plane are vertical and balance each other, hence the resultant of the external forces is $\vec{F}$ alone. The force of the spring is internal to the system of the two points, therefore it does not appear in the first cardinal equation. The **theorem on the motion of the center of mass** then gives
$$
3\,m\,\vec{a}_G = \vec{F} , \qquad \vec{a}_G = \frac{\vec{F}}{3\,m} ,
$$
at every instant, whatever the elongation of the spring. The spring determines instead the accelerations of the single points, which oscillate about the value $\dfrac{\vec{F}}{3\,m}$.
