Two material points $P_1$ and $P_2$ of a system occupy the positions $P_1 = O = (0, 0)$ and $P_2 = (\ell, 0)$ of the fixed frame $(O; x, y)$. The forces they exchange are equal and opposite but not directed along the line joining them: the force $\vec{F}_{12} = F\,\hat{\jmath}$ acts on $P_1$ and the force $\vec{F}_{21} = -F\,\hat{\jmath}$ acts on $P_2$, as in the figure. What is the resultant moment about $O$ of these two internal forces?

::struttura{punti="0,0,P_1,pieno;3,0,P_2,pieno" forze="0 1.2,0,1.2,;3 -1.2,0,-1.2," etichette="0.55,0.75,F_12;3.55,-0.75,F_21;-0.35,-0.35,O" quote="0 0,3 0,ℓ,-0.5" titolo="Two equal and opposite internal forces with parallel and distinct lines of action"}

- $-\,F\,\ell\,\hat{k}$
- $\vec{0}$
- $F\,\ell\,\hat{k}$
- $-2\,F\,\ell\,\hat{k}$

## Explanation

The resultant moment is the sum of the moments of the two forces about $O$. The force $\vec{F}_{12}$ is applied at $O$, hence its moment vanishes. The moment of $\vec{F}_{21}$ equals
$$
(P_2 - O) \wedge \vec{F}_{21} = (\ell\,\hat{\imath}) \wedge (-F\,\hat{\jmath}) = -\,F\,\ell\,\hat{k} ,
$$
therefore $\vec{M}_O^{(i)} = -\,F\,\ell\,\hat{k}$. The resultant of the two forces vanishes, but the resultant moment does not, because the two forces do not have the same line of action: the theorem on the resultant moment of the internal forces requires the principle of action and reaction in the strong form, and here only the weak form $\vec{F}_{12} = -\,\vec{F}_{21}$ is satisfied.
