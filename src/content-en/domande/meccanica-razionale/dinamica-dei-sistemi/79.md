A body of mass $m$ rests on the floor of an elevator that goes up with constant speed $u$. What is the work done by the reaction of the floor on the body in a time interval $t$?

- $0$, because the reaction is orthogonal to the floor
- $m\,g\,u\,t$
- $-\,m\,g\,u\,t$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,u^2$

## Explanation

The body moves with uniform rectilinear motion, hence the resultant of the forces acting on it vanishes and the reaction of the floor equals $m\,g$, directed upward. The floor is a moving constraint, therefore the contact point has the velocity $u$ of the elevator, directed upward like the reaction, and the power of the reaction equals $m\,g\,u$. The work in the interval $t$ is therefore $m\,g\,u\,t$, equal and opposite to that of the weight, consistently with the constant kinetic energy. The reaction and the velocity of the contact point are both orthogonal to the floor, hence they are parallel, and this is why the moving constraint does work.
