A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a horizontal guide and its center has velocity of magnitude $v$. By König's theorem, what is the part of the kinetic energy due to the motion relative to the center of mass?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,v^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,v^2$

## Explanation

König's theorem gives $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2 + T'$, where $T'$ is the kinetic energy in the frame that translates with $G$. In that frame the disk rotates about $G$ with angular velocity $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R}$, since the rolling is without slipping, hence
$$
T' = \frac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\omega^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 \cdot \frac{v^2}{R^2} = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,v^2 .
$$
The total kinetic energy therefore equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,v^2 = \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v^2$, of which one third is due to the rotation about the center of mass.
