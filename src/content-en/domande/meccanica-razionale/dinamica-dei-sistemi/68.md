A small ball, which can be treated as a material point of mass $m$, hits a fixed smooth wall with velocity of magnitude $v$ that forms an angle of $60^\circ$ with the normal to the wall, as in the figure. The impact is elastic. What impulse does the wall exert on the ball?

::struttura{poligoni="-0.4,-2.2 0,-2.2 0,2.2 -0.4,2.2" tratteggi="0,0 2.6,0" punti="1.5,2.6,,pieno" frecce="1.5,2.6,0.1,0.17," etichette="0.75,0.35,60°;1.1,1.25,v" titolo="Small ball hitting a fixed smooth wall"}

- $2\,m\,v$, orthogonal to the wall and directed from the wall toward the ball
- $\sqrt{3}\,m\,v$, orthogonal to the wall and directed from the wall toward the ball
- $m\,v$, orthogonal to the wall and directed from the wall toward the ball
- $\dfrac{m\,v}{2}$, orthogonal to the wall and directed from the wall toward the ball

## Explanation

The wall is smooth, hence its impulsive reaction is orthogonal to the wall and the component of the velocity parallel to the wall does not change. The impact is elastic and the wall is fixed, therefore the kinetic energy of the ball is conserved and the magnitude of the velocity stays $v$. It follows that the normal component, which equals $v\,\cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{v}{2}$, only reverses its direction. Integrating the first cardinal equation over the duration of the impact, the impulse of the wall equals the change of the linear momentum, hence, with the normal axis oriented from the wall toward the ball,
$$
J = m\,\frac{v}{2} - \Big( -\,m\,\frac{v}{2} \Big) = m\,v .
$$
The value $2\,m\,v$ would be obtained if the ball hit the wall along the normal.
