A homogeneous rod is hinged at the endpoint $A$ to a fixed point of a vertical plane and moves under the action of the weight. For what reason does the second cardinal equation written with pole at $A$ give the equation of motion at once?

- Because $A$ coincides with the center of mass of the rod
- Because the moment of the weight about $A$ vanishes
- Because the angular momentum about $A$ vanishes
- Because the moment of the reaction of the hinge about $A$ vanishes

## Explanation

The reaction of the hinge is applied at $A$, hence its arm with respect to $A$ vanishes and its moment about that pole vanishes. The point $A$ is fixed, therefore the second cardinal equation holds in the form $\dot{\vec{K}}_A = \vec{M}_A^{(e)}$, and on the right-hand side only the moment of the weight appears. Since $K_A = I_A\,\dot{\vartheta}$ with $I_A$ constant, the equation becomes $I_A\,\ddot{\vartheta} = \vec{M}_A^{(e)} \cdot \hat{k}$, that is an equation in $\vartheta$ alone. The components of the reaction are then obtained from the first cardinal equation, once the motion is known.
