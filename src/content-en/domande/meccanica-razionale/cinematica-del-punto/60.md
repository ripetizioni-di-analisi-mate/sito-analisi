A particle moves in the plane $Oxy$ with $r(t) > 0$, in such a way that the quantity $r^2\,\dot{\vartheta}$ increases with time according to the law $r^2\,\dot{\vartheta} = k\,t$, where $k > 0$ is constant. What is the transverse acceleration at the instant when the particle is at distance $r$ from the pole?

- $\dfrac{k}{r^2}$
- $k\,r$
- $\dfrac{2\,k}{r}$
- $\dfrac{k}{r}$

## Explanation

The transverse acceleration can be written as a derivative of $r^2\,\dot{\vartheta}$, thanks to the identity
$$
r\,\ddot{\vartheta} + 2\,\dot{r}\,\dot{\vartheta} = \frac{1}{r}\,\frac{d}{dt}\big( r^2\,\dot{\vartheta} \big) ,
$$
which one checks by differentiating the product on the right. Since $\dfrac{d}{dt}(k\,t) = k$, the transverse acceleration equals $\dfrac{k}{r}$. In this motion the areal velocity $\dfrac{1}{2}\,r^2\dot{\vartheta}$ grows linearly with time, and for this reason the transverse acceleration is nonzero.
