An elevator moves up with constant acceleration $a_0\,\hat{\jmath}$, where $\hat{\jmath}$ is the upward vertical unit vector and $a_0 > 0$. Inside it an object detaches from the ceiling and falls freely, with acceleration $-\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$ with respect to the ground. What is the acceleration of the object with respect to the elevator?

- $-\,(g + a_0)\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $-\,(g - a_0)\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $-\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $-\,a_0\,\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

The elevator moves in translational motion, hence the angular velocity of the frame attached to it is zero and the Coriolis acceleration vanishes. The theorem on the composition of accelerations therefore reduces to $\vec{a}_a = \vec{a}_\Omega + \vec{a}_r$, from which
$$
\vec{a}_r = \vec{a}_a - \vec{a}_\Omega = -\,g\,\hat{\jmath} - a_0\,\hat{\jmath} = -\,(g + a_0)\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
With respect to the elevator the object thus falls faster than with respect to the ground, because the floor accelerates toward it.
