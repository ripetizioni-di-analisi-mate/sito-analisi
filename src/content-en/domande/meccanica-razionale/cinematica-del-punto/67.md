A moving frame, with orthonormal right-handed unit vectors $\hat{e}_1$, $\hat{e}_2$, $\hat{e}_3$, has at the instant considered angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \Omega\,(\hat{e}_1 + \hat{e}_2)$, with $\Omega > 0$. What is at that instant the derivative $\dot{\hat{e}}_3$?

- $\Omega\,(\hat{e}_2 - \hat{e}_1)$
- $\Omega\,(\hat{e}_1 + \hat{e}_2)$
- $-\,\Omega\,(\hat{e}_1 + \hat{e}_2)$
- $\Omega\,(\hat{e}_1 - \hat{e}_2)$

## Explanation

By Poisson's formulas $\dot{\hat{e}}_3 = \vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_3$. Since the triad is right-handed, $\hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_3 = -\,\hat{e}_2$ and $\hat{e}_2 \wedge \hat{e}_3 = \hat{e}_1$, hence
$$
\dot{\hat{e}}_3 = \Omega\,(\hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_3 + \hat{e}_2 \wedge \hat{e}_3) = \Omega\,(\hat{e}_1 - \hat{e}_2) .
$$
The result is orthogonal both to $\hat{e}_3$, as it must be for the derivative of a unit vector, and to $\vec{\omega}$.
