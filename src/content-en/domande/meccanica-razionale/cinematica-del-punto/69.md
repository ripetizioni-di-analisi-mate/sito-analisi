A moving frame, with orthonormal right-handed unit vectors $\hat{e}_1$, $\hat{e}_2$, $\hat{e}_3$, has angular velocity
$$
\vec{\omega} = \Omega\,\big( \cos(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_1 + \sin(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_2 \big) ,
$$
where $\Omega > 0$ and $\lambda > 0$ are constants. What is the derivative of $\vec{\omega}$ in the fixed frame?

- $\Omega\,\lambda\,\big( -\sin(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_1 + \cos(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_2 \big)$
- $\Omega\,\lambda\,\big( -\sin(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_1 + \cos(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_2 \big) + \Omega^2\lambda\,\hat{e}_3$
- $\vec{0}$
- $\Omega\,\lambda\,\big( \sin(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_1 - \cos(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_2 \big)$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the derivative of a vector in the two frames
$$
\frac{d\vec{\omega}}{dt} = \frac{d^{*}\vec{\omega}}{dt} + \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{\omega} ,
$$
and the cross product of a vector with itself is zero, hence the absolute derivative of $\vec{\omega}$ coincides with the relative one. The relative derivative is obtained by differentiating the moving components, therefore
$$
\frac{d\vec{\omega}}{dt} = \Omega\,\lambda\,\big( -\sin(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_1 + \cos(\lambda t)\,\hat{e}_2 \big) .
$$
The magnitude of $\vec{\omega}$ is constant and equals $\Omega$, but its direction changes; for this reason the derivative is nonzero and is orthogonal to $\vec{\omega}$.
