A particle moves in simple harmonic motion along the $x$ axis with amplitude $A$ and angular frequency $\omega$, about the center $x = 0$. What is the magnitude of the velocity at the instant when the particle is at $x = \dfrac{A}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,A\,\omega$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,A\,\omega$
- $A\,\omega$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,A\,\omega$

## Explanation

With $x = A\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$ one has $\dot{x} = -\,A\,\omega\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$, hence from the identity $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$ there follows
$$
\dot{x}^2 = \omega^2\big( A^2 - x^2 \big) .
$$
For $x = \dfrac{A}{2}$ one obtains $\dot{x}^2 = \omega^2\left( A^2 - \dfrac{A^2}{4} \right) = \dfrac{3}{4}\,A^2\omega^2$, therefore the magnitude of the velocity equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,A\,\omega$.
