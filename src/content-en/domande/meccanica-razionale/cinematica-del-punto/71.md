A moving frame has origin at $O$ and rotates about the fixed $z$ axis with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$, with $\omega > 0$. What is the relative derivative $\dfrac{d^{*}\hat{\imath}}{dt}$ of the fixed unit vector $\hat{\imath}$, that is, the derivative computed by the moving observer?

- $\vec{0}$
- $\omega\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $-\,\omega\,\hat{\imath}$
- $-\,\omega\,\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

The theorem on the derivative of a vector in the two frames, written for $\vec{u} = \hat{\imath}$, gives
$$
\frac{d^{*}\hat{\imath}}{dt} = \frac{d\hat{\imath}}{dt} - \vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{\imath} .
$$
The unit vector $\hat{\imath}$ is constant in the fixed frame, hence $\dfrac{d\hat{\imath}}{dt} = \vec{0}$, while $\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{\imath} = \omega\,\hat{k} \wedge \hat{\imath} = \omega\,\hat{\jmath}$. Therefore the relative derivative equals $-\,\omega\,\hat{\jmath}$, and the moving observer sees the unit vector $\hat{\imath}$ rotate clockwise with angular velocity $\omega$.
