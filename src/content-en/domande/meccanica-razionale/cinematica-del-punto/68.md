A moving frame has origin at $O$ and unit vectors
$$
\hat{e}_1 = \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \sin(\omega t)\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
\hat{e}_2 = \hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\hat{e}_3 = -\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{k} ,
$$
with $\omega > 0$ constant. What is its angular velocity?

- $\omega\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $\omega\,\hat{k}$
- $-\,\omega\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $-\,\omega\,\hat{k}$

## Explanation

The unit vector $\hat{e}_2 = \hat{\jmath}$ is constant, hence the triad rotates about the $y$ axis and $\vec{\omega}$ is parallel to $\hat{\jmath}$. Differentiating the first unit vector one obtains $\dot{\hat{e}}_1 = \omega\,\big( -\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{k} \big) = \omega\,\hat{e}_3$. By Poisson's formula $\dot{\hat{e}}_1 = \vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_1$ must hold, and with $\vec{\omega} = c\,\hat{e}_2$ one has $c\,\hat{e}_2 \wedge \hat{e}_1 = -\,c\,\hat{e}_3$; therefore $c = -\,\omega$ and
$$
\vec{\omega} = -\,\omega\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The negative sign says that the rotation carrying $\hat{\imath}$ toward $\hat{k}$ is clockwise with respect to the $y$ axis, since $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{\imath} = \hat{\jmath}$.
