A particle moves along a straight line with initial velocity of magnitude $v_0 > 0$ and constant acceleration opposite to the velocity, and it stops after traveling the distance $d$. How long does it take to stop?

- $\dfrac{2\,d}{v_0}$
- $\dfrac{d}{v_0}$
- $\dfrac{d}{2\,v_0}$
- $\dfrac{4\,d}{v_0}$

## Explanation

In uniformly accelerated motion the velocity varies linearly with time, hence the average velocity during the braking interval is the mean between the initial value $v_0$ and the final value $0$, that is $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$. The distance traveled is therefore $d = \dfrac{v_0}{2}\,t$. Solving for the time one obtains $t = \dfrac{2\,d}{v_0}$, without having to compute the acceleration first, which equals $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,d}$.
