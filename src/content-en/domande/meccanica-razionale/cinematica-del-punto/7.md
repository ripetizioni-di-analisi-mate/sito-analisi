A particle $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ moving away from the pole with the law $r(t) = r_0\,e^{\omega t}$, where $r_0 > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ are constants, and its areal velocity with respect to $O$ is constant and equals $c > 0$; at the initial instant $\vartheta = 0$. To what value does the angle $\vartheta$ tend as $t \to +\infty$?

- $\dfrac{c}{\omega\,r_0^2}$
- $\dfrac{2c}{\omega\,r_0^2}$
- $\dfrac{c}{2\,\omega\,r_0^2}$
- The angle grows without bound

## Explanation

From the definition of areal velocity there follows $\dfrac{1}{2}\,r^2\,\dot{\vartheta} = c$, hence
$$
\dot{\vartheta} = \frac{2c}{r_0^2}\,e^{-2\omega t} .
$$
Integrating between $0$ and $t$ with the initial datum $\vartheta(0) = 0$ one obtains $\vartheta(t) = \dfrac{c}{\omega\,r_0^2}\left( 1 - e^{-2\omega t} \right)$. Since $e^{-2\omega t} \to 0$ as $t \to +\infty$, the angle tends to $\dfrac{c}{\omega\,r_0^2}$, therefore the particle describes a finite total angle while it moves away indefinitely from the pole.
