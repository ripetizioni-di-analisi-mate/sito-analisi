Two particles start at the same instant from the same position on a circle and travel along it in uniform motion in opposite directions, the first with angular velocity of magnitude $\omega_1$ and the second with angular velocity of magnitude $\omega_2$. After how long do they meet for the first time?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{\omega_1 + \omega_2}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{|\omega_1 - \omega_2|}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega_1 + \omega_2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{\omega_1} + \dfrac{\pi}{\omega_2}$

## Explanation

Measuring the angles from the starting position, the first particle has $\vartheta_1 = \omega_1 t$ and the second $\vartheta_2 = -\,\omega_2 t$. The two particles meet when the difference between the angles is a multiple of $2\pi$, and for the first time when
$$
\omega_1 t + \omega_2 t = 2\pi .
$$
Therefore the first meeting occurs at the instant $t = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega_1 + \omega_2}$, when the arcs traveled by the two particles, added together, cover the whole circle.
