A vector $\vec{u}$ has constant components in the moving frame, which rotates with angular velocity $\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$. Which equality holds for the derivative of $\vec{u}$ in the fixed frame?

- $\dfrac{d\vec{u}}{dt} = \vec{0}$
- $\dfrac{d\vec{u}}{dt} = \vec{u} \wedge \vec{\omega}$
- $\dfrac{d\vec{u}}{dt} = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u}$
- $\dfrac{d\vec{u}}{dt} = \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u}$

## Explanation

The relative derivative of $\vec{u}$ is computed by differentiating its components in the moving basis, which by hypothesis are constant, hence $\dfrac{d^{*}\vec{u}}{dt} = \vec{0}$. The link between the two derivatives is $\dfrac{d\vec{u}}{dt} = \dfrac{d^{*}\vec{u}}{dt} + \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u}$, therefore there remains $\dfrac{d\vec{u}}{dt} = \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u}$. The vector $\vec{u}$ is attached to the moving triad, and its absolute derivative is due only to the rotation of the basis.
