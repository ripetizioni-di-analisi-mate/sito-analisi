A particle travels along a plane curve with scalar velocity $\dot{s} > 0$, and at the position occupied at the instant considered the trajectory has radius of curvature $\rho$. The intrinsic triad $(\hat{t}, \hat{n}, \hat{b})$, orthonormal and right-handed, is regarded as a moving frame. What is its angular velocity at that instant?

- $\dfrac{\dot{s}}{\rho}\,\hat{n}$
- $\dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}\,\hat{b}$
- $\dfrac{\dot{s}}{\rho}\,\hat{b}$
- $-\,\dfrac{\dot{s}}{\rho}\,\hat{b}$

## Explanation

The curve is plane, hence the binormal vector $\hat{b}$ is orthogonal to the plane of the curve and constant. By Poisson's formula $\dot{\hat{b}} = \vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{b} = \vec{0}$, therefore $\vec{\omega}$ is parallel to $\hat{b}$ and one writes $\vec{\omega} = c\,\hat{b}$. By the definition of the unit normal vector
$$
\dot{\hat{t}} = \frac{d\hat{t}}{ds}\,\dot{s} = \frac{\dot{s}}{\rho}\,\hat{n} ,
$$
while Poisson's formula gives $\dot{\hat{t}} = c\,\hat{b} \wedge \hat{t} = c\,\hat{n}$, since the triad is right-handed. Comparing the two expressions gives $c = \dfrac{\dot{s}}{\rho}$, hence $\vec{\omega} = \dfrac{\dot{s}}{\rho}\,\hat{b}$. For a uniform circular motion of radius $R$ and speed $v$ one recovers the angular velocity $\dfrac{v}{R}$ with which the position vector rotates.
