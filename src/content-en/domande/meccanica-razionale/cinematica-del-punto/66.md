A particle travels along a circular helix of radius $R$ and pitch $p$, that is, the distance between two successive turns measured along the axis, with constant magnitude of the velocity $v$. How long does it take to complete one full turn about the axis?

- $\dfrac{2\pi R}{v}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{4\pi^2R^2 + p^2}}{v}$
- $\dfrac{p}{v}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi R + p}{v}$

## Explanation

The cylinder on which the helix lies can be unrolled onto a plane, and one turn becomes the hypotenuse of a right triangle with one leg of length $2\pi R$, the circumference of the base, and the other of length $p$, the advance along the axis. The length of one turn is therefore
$$
L = \sqrt{4\pi^2R^2 + p^2} .
$$
The magnitude of the velocity is constant, therefore the arc length coordinate grows linearly and the time for one turn equals $\dfrac{L}{v} = \dfrac{\sqrt{4\pi^2R^2 + p^2}}{v}$.
