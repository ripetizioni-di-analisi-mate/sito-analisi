A particle $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ with
$$
x(t) = \ell\cos(\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = -\,\ell\sin(\omega t) ,
$$
where $\ell > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ are constants, and the polar angle $\vartheta$ is measured from the $x$ axis counterclockwise. What is the transverse component of the velocity?

- $\ell\,\omega$
- $0$
- $-\,\ell\,\omega$
- $-\,\ell\,\omega^2$

## Explanation

One has $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = \ell$, and the point with coordinates $\big( \ell\cos(\omega t),\ -\ell\sin(\omega t) \big)$ has polar angle $\vartheta = -\,\omega t$. Hence $\dot{\vartheta} = -\,\omega$ and the transverse velocity equals
$$
r\,\dot{\vartheta} = -\,\ell\,\omega .
$$
The negative sign says that the velocity has the direction opposite to $\hat{u}_\vartheta$, that is, the particle travels along the circle clockwise.
