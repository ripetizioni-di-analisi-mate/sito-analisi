A particle $P$ travels along the ellipse of equations
$$
x(t) = a\cos(\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = b\sin(\omega t) ,
$$
with $a > b > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ constants. What is the radius of curvature at the vertex $A = O + a\,\hat{\imath}$?

- $\dfrac{a^2}{b}$
- $\dfrac{b}{a}$
- $\dfrac{b^2}{a}$
- $\dfrac{a^2 + b^2}{a}$

## Explanation

The vertex $A$ is occupied at the instant $t = 0$, where $\vec{v}_P = b\,\omega\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{a}_P = -\,a\,\omega^2\,\hat{\imath}$. The two vectors are orthogonal, hence $|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P| = a\,b\,\omega^3$. From the formula $\rho = \dfrac{|\vec{v}_P|^3}{|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P|}$ there follows
$$
\rho = \frac{b^3\omega^3}{a\,b\,\omega^3} = \frac{b^2}{a} .
$$
The result does not contain $\omega$, since the radius of curvature depends only on the trajectory and not on the time law with which it is traveled.
