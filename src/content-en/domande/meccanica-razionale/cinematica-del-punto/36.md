A moving frame rotates with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{e}_3$, where $\hat{e}_1$, $\hat{e}_2$, $\hat{e}_3$ are its unit vectors, orthonormal and right-handed. A vector has moving components $\vec{u} = c\,t\,\hat{e}_1$, with $c > 0$ constant. What is the derivative of $\vec{u}$ in the fixed frame?

- $c\,\hat{e}_1 + \omega\,c\,t\,\hat{e}_2$
- $c\,\hat{e}_1$
- $\omega\,c\,t\,\hat{e}_2$
- $c\,\hat{e}_1 - \omega\,c\,t\,\hat{e}_2$

## Explanation

The absolute derivative and the relative one are linked by the formula $\dfrac{d\vec{u}}{dt} = \dfrac{d^{*}\vec{u}}{dt} + \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u}$. The relative derivative is obtained by differentiating the components in the moving basis, hence it equals $c\,\hat{e}_1$. The second term equals $\omega\,\hat{e}_3 \wedge c\,t\,\hat{e}_1 = \omega\,c\,t\,\hat{e}_2$, therefore the derivative in the fixed frame is $c\,\hat{e}_1 + \omega\,c\,t\,\hat{e}_2$.
