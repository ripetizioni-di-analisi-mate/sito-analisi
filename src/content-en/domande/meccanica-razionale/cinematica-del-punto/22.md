A particle travels along the circle of radius $R = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ with time law $s(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,t^2$, where $s$ is measured in meters and $t$ in seconds. At what instant is the normal acceleration equal to the tangential one?

- $1\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

From the time law there follow $\dot{s} = t$ and $\ddot{s} = 1$, hence the tangential acceleration equals $a_t = 1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, constant. The radius of curvature of the circle equals $2\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the normal acceleration equals $a_n = \dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho} = \dfrac{t^2}{2}$. Imposing $\dfrac{t^2}{2} = 1$ one obtains $t = \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{s}$.
