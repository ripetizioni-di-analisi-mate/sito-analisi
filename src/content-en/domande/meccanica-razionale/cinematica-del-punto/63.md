A particle moves in simple harmonic motion along the $x$ axis with law $x(t) = A\cos\left( \dfrac{2\pi}{T}\,t \right)$, where $A > 0$ and $T > 0$. How long does it take to go from the position $x = A$ to the position $x = \dfrac{A}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{T}{12}$
- $\dfrac{T}{8}$
- $\dfrac{T}{4}$
- $\dfrac{T}{6}$

## Explanation

At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at $x = A$ and from there it moves toward the center. Imposing $x = \dfrac{A}{2}$ one obtains $\cos\left( \dfrac{2\pi}{T}\,t \right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, whose first positive solution is $\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\,t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. Therefore the time interval equals $\dfrac{T}{6}$, which is more than half of the time $\dfrac{T}{4}$ needed to reach the center, since the particle is slow near the endpoint.
