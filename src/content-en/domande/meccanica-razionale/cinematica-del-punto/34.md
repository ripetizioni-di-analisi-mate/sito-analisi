A particle moves with simple harmonic motion along the $x$ axis with $x(t) = A\cos(\omega t)$, where $A > 0$ and $\omega > 0$. At which position is the magnitude of the velocity maximum?

- At the endpoints $x = \pm\,A$
- Halfway between the origin and an endpoint
- At the origin $x = 0$
- The magnitude of the velocity is constant

## Explanation

Differentiating one obtains $\dot{x}(t) = -\,A\,\omega\sin(\omega t)$, hence the magnitude of the velocity equals $A\,\omega\,|\sin(\omega t)|$. This expression is maximum when $|\sin(\omega t)| = 1$, and at those instants one has $\cos(\omega t) = 0$, therefore $x = 0$. The maximum value of the magnitude of the velocity is then $A\,\omega$ and it is attained at the passages through the origin, while at the endpoints of the oscillation the velocity is zero.
