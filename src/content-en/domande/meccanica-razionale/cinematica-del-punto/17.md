Two particles travel along the same circle with center $O$ and radius $R$, the first one with $\vartheta_1(t) = \omega t$ and the second one with $\vartheta_2(t) = \omega t + \dfrac{1}{2}\sin(\omega t)$, where $\omega > 0$. Which statement about the two motions is true?

- They have the same trajectory and the same time law
- They have the same trajectory and different time laws
- They have different trajectories, since the time laws are different
- They have the same velocity at every instant

## Explanation

The trajectory is the set of the positions occupied by the particle. One has $\dot{\vartheta}_2 = \omega\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\cos(\omega t) \right) > 0$, hence the second angle too grows without going back and takes every value, therefore both particles travel along the whole circle with center $O$ and radius $R$. The time law is $s = R\,\vartheta$, and the two functions $\vartheta_1$ and $\vartheta_2$ are different, hence the time laws are different. The scalar velocities equal $R\,\omega$ and $R\,\omega\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\cos(\omega t) \right)$, which coincide only at the instants with $\cos(\omega t) = 0$.
