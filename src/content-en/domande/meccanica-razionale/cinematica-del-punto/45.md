A particle $P$ is at rest in the fixed frame, at distance $d$ from the axis of rotation of a moving frame that rotates about that axis with constant angular velocity of magnitude $\omega$. What is the relative trajectory of $P$ and what is the magnitude of its relative velocity?

- A circle of radius $d$, traveled with velocity of magnitude $\omega\,d$
- A single point, hence the relative velocity is zero
- A circle of radius $d$, traveled with velocity of magnitude $2\,\omega\,d$
- A spiral, traveled with velocity of magnitude $\omega\,d$

## Explanation

The absolute velocity of $P$ is zero, hence from the theorem on the composition of velocities there follows $\vec{v}_r = -\,\vec{v}_\tau = -\,\vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega)$, with $\Omega$ chosen on the axis in the plane of the motion. The vector $P - \Omega$ is then orthogonal to $\vec{\omega}$ and has magnitude $d$, therefore the magnitude of the relative velocity equals $\omega\,d$. The distance of $P$ from the axis remains $d$ also for the moving observer, hence the relative trajectory is the circle of radius $d$, traveled with angular velocity $-\,\omega$.
