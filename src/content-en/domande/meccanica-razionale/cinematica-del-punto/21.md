A particle travels along a circle of radius $R$ with constant magnitude of the velocity equal to $v_0 > 0$. Which statement about its acceleration is true?

- The acceleration is zero, since the magnitude of the velocity is constant
- The acceleration is directed toward the center and has magnitude $\dfrac{v_0^2}{R}$
- The acceleration is tangent to the circle and has magnitude $\dfrac{v_0^2}{R}$
- The acceleration is directed toward the center and has magnitude $\dfrac{v_0}{R}$

## Explanation

The acceleration splits along the intrinsic triad as $\vec{a}_P = \ddot{s}\,\hat{t} + \dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}\,\hat{n}$. The magnitude of the velocity is constant, hence $\ddot{s} = 0$ and the tangential component is zero. The radius of curvature of a circle equals $R$ at every point and the unit normal vector is directed toward the center, therefore the acceleration is centripetal and has magnitude $\dfrac{v_0^2}{R}$.
