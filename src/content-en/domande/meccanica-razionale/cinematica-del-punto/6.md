A particle $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ with the polar coordinates
$$
r(t) = c\,t ,
\qquad
\vartheta(t) = \frac{1}{2}\,\beta t^2 ,
$$
with $c > 0$ and $\beta > 0$ constants. What is the transverse component of the acceleration?

- $c\,\beta\,t$
- $3\,c\,\beta\,t$
- $2\,c\,\beta\,t$
- $c\,\beta\,t^2$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates the transverse component of the acceleration equals $r\,\ddot{\vartheta} + 2\,\dot{r}\,\dot{\vartheta}$. From the two given laws there follow $\dot{r} = c$, $\dot{\vartheta} = \beta t$ and $\ddot{\vartheta} = \beta$. Substituting one obtains
$$
c\,t\,\beta + 2\,c\,\beta t = 3\,c\,\beta\,t ,
$$
hence the transverse component grows linearly in time. The first summand comes from the angular acceleration and the second one is the Coriolis term, due to the particle moving away from the pole while the radius rotates.
