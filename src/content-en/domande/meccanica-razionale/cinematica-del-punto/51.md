A particle moves with velocity never zero and with acceleration orthogonal to the velocity at every instant. Which statement is true?

- The trajectory is a straight line
- The magnitude of the velocity is constant
- The velocity is constant
- The trajectory is a circle

## Explanation

The derivative of the square of the magnitude of the velocity equals
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\,|\vec{v}_P|^2 = \frac{d}{dt}\,(\vec{v}_P \cdot \vec{v}_P) = 2\,\vec{v}_P \cdot \vec{a}_P .
$$
By hypothesis $\vec{v}_P \cdot \vec{a}_P = 0$, hence $|\vec{v}_P|$ is constant; in intrinsic terms the tangential acceleration $\ddot{s}$ is zero and the acceleration is entirely normal. The trajectory, instead, can be any curve; for example, a uniform motion on a circular helix has acceleration orthogonal to the velocity and a trajectory that is not a circle.
