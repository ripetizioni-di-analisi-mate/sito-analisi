A particle moves in the plane $Oxy$ with $r$ constant and $\vartheta$ not constant. Which statement is true?

- The acceleration is entirely transverse, since $r$ is constant
- The radial velocity equals $-\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}$
- The acceleration is zero if $\dot{\vartheta}$ is constant
- The radial acceleration equals $-\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$, therefore it is directed toward $O$

## Explanation

With $r$ constant one has $\dot{r} = 0$ and $\ddot{r} = 0$, hence the velocity equals $r\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{u}_\vartheta$ and its radial component is zero. The radial acceleration equals $\ddot{r} - r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = -\,r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$, which is negative since $\dot{\vartheta} \neq 0$, therefore that component is directed toward the pole $O$. The transverse component equals $r\,\ddot{\vartheta}$ and it vanishes when $\dot{\vartheta}$ is constant, a case in which the acceleration remains different from zero because the radial component does not vanish.
