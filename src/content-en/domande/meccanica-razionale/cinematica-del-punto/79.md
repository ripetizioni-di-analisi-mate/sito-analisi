The Earth rotates about its own axis with angular velocity $\vec{\omega}$, directed from the south pole to the north pole, of magnitude $\omega$. A train runs on a straight track along the equator, eastward, with velocity of magnitude $u$ with respect to the ground. What is the Coriolis acceleration of the train in the frame attached to the Earth?

- Vertical, pointing downward, of magnitude $2\,\omega\,u$
- Vertical, pointing upward, of magnitude $2\,\omega\,u$
- Horizontal, pointing north, of magnitude $2\,\omega\,u$
- Zero, since the relative velocity is horizontal

## Explanation

At the equator one takes the local triad formed by the unit vector $\hat{e}$ pointing east, the unit vector $\hat{n}$ pointing north and the unit vector $\hat{u}$ pointing up, which is right-handed, that is $\hat{e} \wedge \hat{n} = \hat{u}$. At that point the angular velocity of the Earth is parallel to the ground and points north, $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{n}$, while the relative velocity is $\vec{v}_r = u\,\hat{e}$. Therefore
$$
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r = 2\,\omega\,u\,(\hat{n} \wedge \hat{e}) = -\,2\,\omega\,u\,\hat{u} .
$$
The Coriolis acceleration is thus vertical, pointing downward, and it has the maximum magnitude $2\,\omega\,u$ because $\vec{v}_r$ is orthogonal to $\vec{\omega}$.
