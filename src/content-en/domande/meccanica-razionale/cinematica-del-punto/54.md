A car travels along a bend shaped as an arc of a circle and brakes, so that the magnitude of its velocity decreases. Which statement about its acceleration is true?

- It is opposite to the velocity, since the car is braking
- It has a component in the direction of the velocity and a component directed toward the center of the bend
- It has a component opposite to the velocity and a component directed toward the center of the bend
- It has a component opposite to the velocity and a component directed toward the outside of the bend

## Explanation

One orients the trajectory in the direction of motion, so that $\dot{s} > 0$ and $\vec{v}_P = \dot{s}\,\hat{t}$. By the theorem on the tangential and normal components
$$
\vec{a}_P = \ddot{s}\,\hat{t} + \frac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}\,\hat{n} .
$$
The magnitude of the velocity $\dot{s}$ decreases, hence $\ddot{s} < 0$ and the tangential component is opposite to the velocity. Since $\dot{s} \neq 0$ and the radius of curvature is the radius of the bend, the normal component $\dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}$ is positive and directed along $\hat{n}$, that is, toward the center of the bend. The acceleration is therefore tilted backward and toward the inside of the bend.
