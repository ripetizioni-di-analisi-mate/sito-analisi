A particle $P$ travels along a given trajectory with scalar velocity $\dot{s}(t) = v_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$, where $v_0 > 0$ and $\tau > 0$ are constants, and at the initial instant one has $s(0) = 0$. What total arc does it travel as $t \to +\infty$?

- $\dfrac{v_0}{\tau}$
- $2\,v_0\,\tau$
- $v_0\,\tau$
- The arc traveled grows without bound

## Explanation

The arc length coordinate is obtained by integrating the scalar velocity, hence
$$
s(t) = \int_0^t v_0\,e^{-\frac{\sigma}{\tau}}\,d\sigma = v_0\,\tau\left( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \right) .
$$
Since $e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \to 0$ as $t \to +\infty$, the arc length coordinate tends to $v_0\,\tau$. The particle therefore approaches indefinitely a limit position on the trajectory, after having traveled the arc $v_0\,\tau$.
