A particle $P$ is constrained to the helical guide of radius $R$ and pitch $2\pi h$, of equations
$$
P - O = R\cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + R\sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} + h\,\varphi\,\hat{k} ,
$$
and it travels along it starting from rest with $\varphi(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\alpha t^2$, where $\alpha > 0$ is constant. What is the tangential acceleration?

- $\alpha\,R$
- $\alpha\,t\,\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\alpha\,\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$
- $\alpha\,\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$

## Explanation

Differentiating the equations of the guide with respect to $\varphi$ one obtains a vector of magnitude $\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$, hence the arc length coordinate satisfies $\dfrac{ds}{d\varphi} = \sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$ and equals $s = \varphi\,\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$, choosing the origin of the arcs at the initial position. Substituting the given time law one gets $s(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\alpha t^2\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$. The tangential acceleration is $a_t = \ddot{s}$, therefore it equals $\alpha\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$ and it is constant in time.
