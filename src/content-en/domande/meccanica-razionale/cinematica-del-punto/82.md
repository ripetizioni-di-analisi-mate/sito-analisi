A moving frame rotates with constant angular velocity of magnitude $\omega$. A particle travels along a straight guide attached to the moving frame, which forms an angle of $30^\circ$ with the direction of $\vec{\omega}$, with relative velocity of magnitude $u$. What is the magnitude of the Coriolis acceleration?

- $2\,\omega\,u$
- $\sqrt{3}\,\omega\,u$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\omega\,u$
- $\omega\,u$

## Explanation

The Coriolis acceleration is $\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r$, and the magnitude of a cross product is the product of the magnitudes times the sine of the angle between the vectors. The relative velocity has the direction of the guide, hence it forms an angle of $30^\circ$ with $\vec{\omega}$ and
$$
|\vec{a}_c| = 2\,\omega\,u\sin 30^\circ = \omega\,u .
$$
The value $2\,\omega\,u$ is obtained only when the relative velocity is orthogonal to $\vec{\omega}$.
