Two ships move in uniform rectilinear motion: ship $A$ northward with velocity of magnitude $u$, ship $B$ eastward with velocity of the same magnitude $u$. What is the velocity of $B$ with respect to $A$, that is, the one measured by an observer attached to ship $A$?

- Of magnitude $u\sqrt{2}$, pointing south-east
- Of magnitude $u\sqrt{2}$, pointing north-east
- Of magnitude $2\,u$, pointing east
- Zero, since the two ships have velocities of equal magnitude

## Explanation

One takes $\hat{\imath}$ pointing east and $\hat{\jmath}$ pointing north. The frame attached to ship $A$ translates with velocity $u\,\hat{\jmath}$ and has zero angular velocity, hence by the theorem on the composition of velocities $\vec{v}_B = u\,\hat{\jmath} + \vec{v}_r$. Therefore
$$
\vec{v}_r = u\,\hat{\imath} - u\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
which has magnitude $u\sqrt{2}$ and points south-east, at $45^\circ$ between east and south.
