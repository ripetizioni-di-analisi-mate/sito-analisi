A particle $P$ travels along the circular helix of equations
$$
P - O = R\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} + h\,\omega t\,\hat{k} ,
$$
with $R > 0$, $h > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ constants. What is the radius of curvature of the trajectory?

- $\dfrac{R^2 + h^2}{h}$
- $R$
- $\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$
- $\dfrac{R^2 + h^2}{R}$

## Explanation

Differentiating one obtains $\vec{v}_P = \omega\big( -R\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} + h\,\hat{k} \big)$, hence $|\vec{v}_P| = \omega\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}$, constant in time. Differentiating a second time one gets $\vec{a}_P = -\,R\omega^2\big( \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} \big)$, of magnitude $R\,\omega^2$. Since the magnitude of the velocity is constant, one has $a_t = \ddot{s} = 0$, therefore the acceleration is entirely normal and $a_n = R\,\omega^2$. From the relation $a_n = \dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}$ there follows
$$
\rho = \frac{\omega^2\big( R^2 + h^2 \big)}{R\,\omega^2} = \frac{R^2 + h^2}{R} .
$$
