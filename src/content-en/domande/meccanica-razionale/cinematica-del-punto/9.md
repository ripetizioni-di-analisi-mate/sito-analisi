A particle $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ by composing two orthogonal simple harmonic motions of equal angular frequency and in phase with each other:
$$
x(t) = A\cos(\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = B\cos(\omega t) ,
$$
with $A > 0$, $B > 0$ and $\omega > 0$. What is the maximum value of the magnitude of the velocity?

- $\omega\,A$
- $\omega\,(A + B)$
- $\omega\,\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$
- $\omega^2\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$

## Explanation

Differentiating one obtains $\vec{v}_P = -\,\omega\sin(\omega t)\,\big( A\,\hat{\imath} + B\,\hat{\jmath} \big)$, hence the velocity has constant direction and magnitude $\omega\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}\,|\sin(\omega t)|$. The particle therefore moves with simple harmonic motion along the straight line $y = \dfrac{B}{A}\,x$, with amplitude $\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$. The factor $|\sin(\omega t)|$ attains the value $1$, therefore the maximum value of the magnitude of the velocity is $\omega\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$ and it is attained at the passages through the origin.
