A moving frame with origin $\Omega$ moves with translational motion. Which equality holds for the absolute velocity of a particle $P$?

- $\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_r$
- $\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\Omega + \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega)$
- $\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\Omega + \vec{v}_r + 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r$
- $\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\Omega + \vec{v}_r$, since $\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$

## Explanation

In a translational motion the unit vectors of the moving triad are constant, hence Poisson's formulas give $\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$. The theorem on the composition of velocities states that $\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\Omega + \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega) + \vec{v}_r$, and the term with the cross product vanishes. There remains therefore $\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\Omega + \vec{v}_r$, that is the two velocities add as in the Galilean composition.
