A train travels with velocity of constant magnitude $v$ along a clothoid transition curve, along which the curvature grows in proportion to the arc traveled:
$$
\frac{1}{\rho} = \frac{s}{A^2} ,
$$
where $s$ is the arc length measured from the start of the transition in the direction of motion and $A > 0$ is a constant. What is the time derivative of the normal acceleration of the train along the transition curve?

- $0$, since the magnitude of the velocity is constant
- $\dfrac{v^3}{A^2}$
- $\dfrac{v^2}{A^2}$
- $\dfrac{2\,v^3}{A^2}$

## Explanation

The magnitude of the velocity is constant and the arc length grows in the direction of motion, hence $\dot{s} = v$. The normal acceleration equals
$$
a_n = \frac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho} = \frac{v^2\,s}{A^2} .
$$
Since $v$ is constant, differentiating with respect to time gives $\dfrac{d a_n}{dt} = \dfrac{v^2\,\dot{s}}{A^2} = \dfrac{v^3}{A^2}$. The normal acceleration thus grows linearly in time starting from the zero value it has on the straight track, while the tangential acceleration stays zero; for this reason the clothoid is used to connect a straight track with a circular curve without a jump in the normal acceleration.
