A particle $P$ describes the cycloid with equations
$$
x(t) = R\,(\omega t - \sin\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = R\,(1 - \cos\omega t) ,
$$
with $R > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ constants. What is the acceleration of the particle at the instant $t = 0$, when the particle is on the $x$ axis?

- $R\,\omega^2\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $\vec{0}$
- $-\,R\,\omega^2\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $2\,R\,\omega^2\,\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

Differentiating the equations of motion twice one obtains
$$
\ddot{x} = R\,\omega^2\sin(\omega t) ,
\qquad
\ddot{y} = R\,\omega^2\cos(\omega t) ,
$$
hence at the instant $t = 0$ the acceleration equals $R\,\omega^2\,\hat{\jmath}$. At that instant the velocity $\big( R\omega(1 - \cos\omega t),\ R\omega\sin\omega t \big)$ is zero, hence the hypothesis $\vec{v}_P \neq \vec{0}$ of the theorem on the tangential and normal components is not satisfied and the intrinsic decomposition cannot be used. The acceleration is nonzero even though the particle is at rest, and it points upward, that is, toward the center of the circle that generates the cycloid.
