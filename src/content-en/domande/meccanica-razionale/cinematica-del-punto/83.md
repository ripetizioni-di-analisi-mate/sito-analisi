A particle $P$ is at rest in the fixed frame, at distance $d$ from the $z$ axis. A moving frame rotates about the $z$ axis with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$, with $\omega > 0$. What is the Coriolis acceleration of $P$ in the motion relative to the moving frame?

- Zero, since the particle is at rest
- Of magnitude $2\,\omega^2 d$, pointing toward the axis
- Of magnitude $2\,\omega^2 d$, pointing away from the axis
- Of magnitude $\omega^2 d$, pointing away from the axis

## Explanation

One takes the moving origin on the axis, in the plane orthogonal to it that contains $P$. The particle is at rest, hence $\vec{v}_a = \vec{0}$ and by the theorem on the composition of velocities $\vec{v}_r = -\,\vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega) = -\,\omega\,d\,\hat{u}_\vartheta$, where $\hat{u}_r$ and $\hat{u}_\vartheta$ are the radial and transverse unit vectors of $P$. Therefore
$$
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\omega\,\hat{k} \wedge \big( -\,\omega\,d\,\hat{u}_\vartheta \big) = 2\,\omega^2 d\,\hat{u}_r ,
$$
pointing away from the axis. It is nonzero because the relative velocity is not zero, since the moving observer sees the particle go around a circle of radius $d$.
