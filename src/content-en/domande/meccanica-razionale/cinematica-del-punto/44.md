A moving frame rotates about the fixed $z$ axis with angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega(t)\,\hat{k}$, where $\dot{\omega} = \alpha > 0$ is constant. A particle $P$ is at rest with respect to the moving frame, in the plane $Oxy$ and at distance $d$ from the axis. What is the transverse component of its absolute acceleration?

- $0$
- $\alpha\,d$
- $\omega^2 d$
- $2\,\omega\,d$

## Explanation

The particle is at relative rest, hence the relative acceleration and the Coriolis acceleration are zero and the absolute acceleration coincides with the transport one. Choosing the moving origin $\Omega$ on the axis in the plane of the motion one has
$$
\vec{a}_\tau = \dot{\vec{\omega}} \wedge (P - \Omega) + \vec{\omega} \wedge \big( \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega) \big) = \alpha\,d\,\hat{u}_\vartheta - \omega^2 d\,\hat{u}_r .
$$
The transverse component therefore equals $\alpha\,d$, and it vanishes only when the angular velocity is constant.
