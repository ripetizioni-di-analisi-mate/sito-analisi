A moving frame rotates with angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega(t)\,\hat{e}_3$, where $\hat{e}_1$, $\hat{e}_2$, $\hat{e}_3$ are its unit vectors, and a vector has moving components $\vec{u} = f(t)\,\hat{e}_3$, with $f$ differentiable and not constant. Which statement is true?

- The derivative of $\vec{u}$ in the fixed frame differs from the relative one by the term $f\,\omega\,\hat{e}_3$
- The derivative of $\vec{u}$ in the fixed frame equals the relative derivative, $\dot{f}\,\hat{e}_3$
- The derivative of $\vec{u}$ in the fixed frame is zero, since $\vec{u}$ has the direction of $\vec{\omega}$
- The relative derivative of $\vec{u}$ is zero, since $\vec{u}$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the derivative of a vector in the two frames one has
$$
\frac{d\vec{u}}{dt} = \frac{d^{*}\vec{u}}{dt} + \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u} .
$$
The relative derivative is computed by differentiating the moving components, hence it equals $\dot{f}\,\hat{e}_3$, which is nonzero because $f$ is not constant. The term $\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{u} = \omega\,f\,(\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_3)$ is zero, therefore the two derivatives coincide and equal $\dot{f}\,\hat{e}_3$.
