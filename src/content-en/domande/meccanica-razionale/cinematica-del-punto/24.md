A particle travels along the parabola $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2\,\ell}$, with $\ell > 0$, in such a way that the abscissa grows with constant velocity $\dot{x} = v_0 > 0$. What is the radius of curvature of the trajectory at the origin?

- $2\,\ell$
- $\ell$
- $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$
- $\dfrac{v_0^2}{\ell}$

## Explanation

From the condition $\dot{x} = v_0$ there follows $x = v_0t$, hence $y = \dfrac{v_0^2t^2}{2\,\ell}$, and differentiating one obtains $\vec{v}_P = v_0\,\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{v_0^2t}{\ell}\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{a}_P = \dfrac{v_0^2}{\ell}\,\hat{\jmath}$. At the origin one has $t = 0$, therefore the velocity equals $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$ and it is orthogonal to the acceleration, and one has $|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P| = \dfrac{v_0^3}{\ell}$. From the formula $\rho = \dfrac{|\vec{v}_P|^3}{|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P|}$ there follows then $\rho = \ell$.
