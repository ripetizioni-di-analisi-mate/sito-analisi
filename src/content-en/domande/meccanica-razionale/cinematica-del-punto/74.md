Rain falls vertically with velocity of magnitude $w$ with respect to the ground. A train travels on a straight horizontal track with constant velocity of magnitude $V$. What angle do the traces of the drops on the side windows of the train form with the vertical?

- $\arctan\dfrac{V}{w}$
- $\arctan\dfrac{w}{V}$
- $\arcsin\dfrac{V}{w}$
- $0$, since the rain falls vertically

## Explanation

The frame attached to the train moves in translational motion, hence its angular velocity is zero and $\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\Omega + \vec{v}_r$. With the $x$ axis in the direction of motion of the train and the $y$ axis upward, the absolute velocity of the drop is $-\,w\,\hat{\jmath}$ and that of the train is $V\,\hat{\imath}$, therefore
$$
\vec{v}_r = -\,V\,\hat{\imath} - w\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The traces have the direction of the relative velocity, which forms with the vertical the angle $\alpha$ with $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{V}{w}$; the traces are tilted backward with respect to the direction of motion.
