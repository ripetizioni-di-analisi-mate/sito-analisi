A particle starts from rest and moves along a straight line with constant acceleration. In the first second of motion it travels $2\,\mathrm{m}$. What distance does it travel in the third second, that is, between the instants $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t = 3\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The particle starts from rest, hence, taking the origin at the initial position, the law of motion is $x(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a_0 t^2$. From the condition $x(1\,\mathrm{s}) = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ one obtains $a_0 = 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The distance traveled in the third second is therefore
$$
x(3\,\mathrm{s}) - x(2\,\mathrm{s}) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot (9 - 4)\,\mathrm{m} = 10\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
More generally $x(n) - x(n - 1) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a_0\,(2n - 1)$, hence the distances traveled in successive seconds are in the ratio of the odd numbers $1$, $3$, $5$, …, as Galileo observed.
