A Ferris wheel with center $O$ rotates with constant angular velocity of magnitude $\omega$, and the cabins hang from the points $\Omega$ of the rim, at distance $R$ from $O$, in such a way that the floor always stays horizontal. A passenger is seated, at rest with respect to the cabin, at distance $h$ below the suspension point $\Omega$, as in the figure. What is the passenger's absolute acceleration?

- Zero, since the passenger is at rest with respect to the cabin
- Of magnitude $\omega^2 (R + h)$, pointing toward $O$
- Of magnitude $\omega^2 R$, directed as $O - \Omega$
- Of magnitude $\omega^2 R$, directed from the passenger toward $\Omega$

## Explanation

The floor of the cabin stays horizontal, hence the unit vectors of a frame attached to the cabin are constant and the cabin moves in translational motion, with $\vec{\omega}_{\text{cabin}} = \vec{0}$. The passenger is at relative rest, therefore $\vec{a}_r = \vec{0}$, and by the theorem on the composition of accelerations
$$
\vec{a}_a = \vec{a}_\Omega = -\,\omega^2\,(\Omega - O) .
$$
The suspension point $\Omega$ moves in uniform circular motion of radius $R$, hence the acceleration of the passenger has magnitude $\omega^2 R$ and is parallel to $O - \Omega$, whatever the passenger's position in the cabin.
