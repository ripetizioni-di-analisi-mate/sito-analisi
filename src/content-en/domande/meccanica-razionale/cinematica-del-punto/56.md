A particle travels along the circle of radius $R$ passing through the pole $O$, with polar equation $r = 2\,R\cos\vartheta$ and $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \vartheta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, in such a way that the polar angle varies with $\dot{\vartheta} = \omega$ constant. What is the magnitude of the velocity?

- $R\,\omega$
- $2\,R\,\omega\,|\cos\vartheta|$
- $4\,R\,\omega$
- $2\,R\,\omega$

## Explanation

Differentiating the equation of the trajectory one obtains the radial velocity $\dot{r} = -\,2\,R\,\omega\sin\vartheta$, while the transverse velocity equals $r\,\dot{\vartheta} = 2\,R\,\omega\cos\vartheta$. The two components are orthogonal, hence
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = \sqrt{4\,R^2\omega^2\sin^2\vartheta + 4\,R^2\omega^2\cos^2\vartheta} = 2\,R\,\omega .
$$
The motion is therefore uniform circular motion. The result agrees with the inscribed angle theorem, since the central angle of the circle is twice $\vartheta$; hence the particle rotates about the center with angular velocity $2\,\omega$ on a radius $R$.
