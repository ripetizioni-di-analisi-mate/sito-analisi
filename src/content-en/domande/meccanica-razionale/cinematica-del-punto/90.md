A particle moves in an arbitrary way and its acceleration is measured by the fixed observer and by an observer attached to a moving frame. In which of the following moving frames do the two accelerations coincide for every motion of the particle?

- A railroad car running on a straight track with constant velocity
- A merry-go-round rotating with constant angular velocity
- An elevator starting with constant acceleration
- A cabin of a Ferris wheel turning with constant angular velocity, with the floor always horizontal

## Explanation

By the theorem on the composition of accelerations $\vec{a}_a = \vec{a}_\tau + \vec{a}_r + \vec{a}_c$, hence $\vec{a}_a = \vec{a}_r$ for every motion of the particle if and only if $\vec{a}_\tau$ and $\vec{a}_c$ are zero for every position and every relative velocity. This requires $\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{a}_\Omega = \vec{0}$, that is, a frame in uniform rectilinear translational motion, like the railroad car. The merry-go-round has $\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$. The elevator and the cabin translate, but with $\vec{a}_\Omega \neq \vec{0}$, the elevator because it accelerates along the vertical and the cabin because its suspension point travels along a circle.
