A particle moves with rectilinear motion with scalar velocity that is not constant. Which statement about its acceleration is true?

- It is parallel to the velocity, since the curvature of the trajectory is zero
- It is orthogonal to the velocity
- It is zero
- It has normal component $\dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}$ with $\rho$ finite and positive

## Explanation

On a straight line the unit tangent vector is constant, hence $\dfrac{d\hat{t}}{ds} = \vec{0}$ and the curvature is zero. In the decomposition $\vec{a}_P = \ddot{s}\,\hat{t} + \dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}\,\hat{n}$ the normal component is therefore zero and $\vec{a}_P = \ddot{s}\,\hat{t}$ remains. Since the scalar velocity is not constant, at some instant one has $\ddot{s} \neq 0$, hence the acceleration is different from zero and parallel to the velocity.
