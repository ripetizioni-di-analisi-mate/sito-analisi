A moving frame has origin $\Omega$ coinciding with the fixed origin $O$ and rotates with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{e}_1$, with $\omega > 0$, where $\hat{e}_1$, $\hat{e}_2$, $\hat{e}_3$ are the moving unit vectors, orthonormal and right-handed. A particle $P$ moves along the $\eta$ axis with $P - \Omega = u\,t\,\hat{e}_2$ and $u > 0$; the diagram is in the figure. What is the Coriolis acceleration?

::struttura{nodi="Ω,0,0;P,1.3,0" punti="0,0;1.3,0" frecce="0,0,-1.5,-0.7,ξ;0,0,2.5,0,η;0,0,0,1.7,ζ;0,0,-0.64,-0.3,ω;1.3,0,1.8,0,v_r;1.3,0,1.3,0.8,v_τ" riferimento=0 titolo="Moving frame rotating about the $\xi$ axis, drawn in projection, with the particle $P$ running along the $\eta$ axis"}

- $\omega\,u\,\hat{e}_3$
- $2\,\omega\,u\,\hat{e}_2$
- $2\,\omega\,u\,\hat{e}_3$
- $-\,2\,\omega\,u\,\hat{e}_3$

## Explanation

The relative velocity is obtained by differentiating the coordinates that $P$ has in the moving frame, hence $\vec{v}_r = u\,\hat{e}_2$. The Coriolis acceleration equals
$$
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r = 2\,\omega\,u\;\hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_2 .
$$
Since the moving triad is orthonormal and right-handed, one has $\hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_2 = \hat{e}_3$, therefore $\vec{a}_c = 2\,\omega\,u\,\hat{e}_3$.
