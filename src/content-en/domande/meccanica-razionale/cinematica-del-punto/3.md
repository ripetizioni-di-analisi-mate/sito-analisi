A particle $P$ leaves the origin with velocity of magnitude $v_0$ inclined at the angle $\alpha \in \left( 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ to the $x$ axis and moves in the vertical plane $Oxy$ with constant acceleration $-\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$, so that
$$
x(t) = v_0\cos\alpha\;t ,
\qquad
y(t) = v_0\sin\alpha\;t - \frac{1}{2}\,g t^2 .
$$
What is the radius of curvature of the trajectory at the point of maximum height?

- $\dfrac{v_0^2\cos^2\alpha}{g}$
- $\dfrac{v_0^2}{g}$
- $\dfrac{v_0^2\cos\alpha}{g}$
- $\dfrac{v_0^2\sin^2\alpha}{g}$

## Explanation

The vertical component of the velocity equals $\dot{y} = v_0\sin\alpha - g t$ and it vanishes at the instant $t^{*} = \dfrac{v_0\sin\alpha}{g}$, which is the instant of maximum height. At that instant the velocity is horizontal and has magnitude $v_0\cos\alpha$, while the acceleration equals $-\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore the two vectors are orthogonal and one has $a_t = 0$ and $a_n = g$. From the relation $a_n = \dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}$ there follows
$$
\rho = \frac{v_0^2\cos^2\alpha}{g} .
$$
