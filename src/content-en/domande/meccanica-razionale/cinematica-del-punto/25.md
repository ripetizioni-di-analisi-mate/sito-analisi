A point particle $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ along the Archimedean spiral with polar equation $r = b\,\vartheta$, where $b > 0$, and the polar angle varies according to the law $\vartheta(t) = \omega t$, where $\omega > 0$ is constant. What is the radial component of the acceleration at the instant when $\vartheta = \pi$?

- $\pi\,b\,\omega^2$
- $2\,b\,\omega^2$
- $-\,\pi\,b\,\omega^2$
- $0$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates the radial component of the acceleration equals $\ddot{r} - r\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$. From the given laws it follows that $r(t) = b\,\omega t$, hence $\dot{r} = b\,\omega$ and $\ddot{r} = 0$, while $\dot{\vartheta} = \omega$. The radial component therefore reduces to $-\,r\,\omega^2$. At the instant when $\vartheta = \pi$ the distance from the pole is $r = \pi\,b$, so the radial component is $-\,\pi\,b\,\omega^2$, directed toward the pole. The value $2\,b\,\omega^2$ is the transverse component $2\,\dot{r}\,\dot{\vartheta}$, and the value $0$ is obtained by keeping only the term $\ddot{r}$.
