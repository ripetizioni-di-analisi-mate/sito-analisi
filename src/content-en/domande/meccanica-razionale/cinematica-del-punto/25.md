A particle moves in the plane $Oxy$ with the polar coordinates
$$
r(t) = r_0 + u\,t ,
\qquad
\vartheta(t) = \omega t ,
$$
where $r_0 > 0$, $u > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ are constants. What is the transverse component of the acceleration?

- $u\,\omega$
- $(r_0 + u\,t)\,\omega^2$
- $2\,u\,\omega$
- $0$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates the transverse component of the acceleration equals $r\,\ddot{\vartheta} + 2\,\dot{r}\,\dot{\vartheta}$. From the given laws there follow $\dot{r} = u$, $\dot{\vartheta} = \omega$ and $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$, hence the first summand is zero. There remains $2\,u\,\omega$, which is constant in time and is the Coriolis term of the polar motion, present because the particle moves away from the pole while the radius rotates.
