A train travels on a straight horizontal track with constant velocity $V\,\hat{\imath}$. A passenger drops an object from the window, and with respect to the train the object starts from rest and goes down with acceleration $-\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$. What trajectory does the object describe for an observer at rest on the ground?

- A vertical segment
- A straight segment tilted backward
- An arc of a circle
- An arc of a parabola

## Explanation

The frame of the train translates with constant velocity, hence $\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{a}_\Omega = \vec{0}$; by the composition theorems $\vec{v}_a = V\,\hat{\imath} + \vec{v}_r$ and $\vec{a}_a = \vec{a}_r = -\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$. With respect to the ground the object therefore starts with horizontal velocity $V\,\hat{\imath}$ and has constant vertical acceleration, and taking the origin at the point of release one has
$$
x = V\,t ,
\qquad
y = -\,\frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2 .
$$
Eliminating the time one obtains $y = -\,\dfrac{g\,x^2}{2\,V^2}$, which is an arc of a parabola. The passenger instead sees the object fall along a vertical segment, that is, the relative trajectory differs from the absolute one.
