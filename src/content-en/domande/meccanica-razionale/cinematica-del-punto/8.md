A particle $P$ moves on the $x$ axis with the equation $\ddot{x} = -\,\omega^2 x$ and at the initial instant it is at $x(0) = x_0 > 0$ with velocity $\dot{x}(0) = -\,v_0$, where $v_0 > 0$. What is the amplitude of the motion?

- $x_0 + \dfrac{v_0}{\omega}$
- $\sqrt{x_0^2 + v_0^2}$
- $\sqrt{x_0^2 + \dfrac{v_0^2}{\omega^2}}$
- $\sqrt{x_0^2 + \omega^2 v_0^2}$

## Explanation

The general solution of the equation of simple harmonic motion is $x(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, hence $\dot{x}(t) = -\,A\,\omega\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$. Imposing the initial data one obtains the two equalities $A\cos\varphi = x_0$ and $A\,\omega\sin\varphi = v_0$. Dividing the second one by $\omega$ and adding the squares one gets
$$
A^2 = x_0^2 + \frac{v_0^2}{\omega^2} ,
$$
therefore the amplitude equals $A = \sqrt{x_0^2 + \dfrac{v_0^2}{\omega^2}}$.
