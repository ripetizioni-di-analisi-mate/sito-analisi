A circular guide of radius $R$ and center $O$ rotates about its own vertical diameter with constant angular velocity of magnitude $\omega$. A bead $P$ moves along the guide and its position is located by the angle $\vartheta$ that $P - O$ forms with the downward vertical, with $\dot{\vartheta} \neq 0$; the diagram is in the figure. In which positions is the Coriolis acceleration of the bead, in the frame attached to the guide, zero?

- At the lowest point of the guide
- At the two points of the guide at the height of the center, $\vartheta = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- At the highest point and at the lowest point of the guide
- In no position, since the guide rotates

## Explanation

In the frame of the guide the bead moves on a circle, hence the relative velocity is tangent to the guide and has magnitude $R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$. The angular velocity is vertical, and the tangent to the guide at the point with angle $\vartheta$ forms with the vertical an angle whose sine equals $|\cos\vartheta|$, therefore
$$
|\vec{a}_c| = 2\,\omega\,R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|\,|\cos\vartheta| .
$$
This magnitude is zero only for $\vartheta = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, that is, at the points at the height of the center, where the relative velocity is vertical and hence parallel to $\vec{\omega}$. At the lowest point the magnitude is instead maximum and equals $2\,\omega\,R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$.
