A moving frame rotates with angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{e}_3$, with $\omega > 0$, and a particle moves with respect to it along the $\zeta$ axis, so that its relative velocity is parallel to $\hat{e}_3$ and different from the zero vector. Which statement about the Coriolis acceleration is true?

- It is zero, since the relative velocity is zero
- It has magnitude $2\,\omega\,|\vec{v}_r|$ and is directed along $\hat{e}_3$
- It is orthogonal to $\vec{\omega}$ and has magnitude $\omega\,|\vec{v}_r|$
- It is zero, since the relative velocity is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$

## Explanation

The Coriolis acceleration equals $\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r$. The relative velocity is parallel to $\hat{e}_3$ and the angular velocity has the same direction, hence the cross product of two parallel vectors is zero. The particle moves along the axis of rotation of the moving frame, therefore $\vec{a}_c = \vec{0}$ even though $\vec{v}_r \neq \vec{0}$.
