A particle $P$ moves on the $x$ axis with
$$
x(t) = \ell\left( \omega^2 t^2 - 2\,\omega t \right) ,
$$
where $\ell > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ are constants. What distance does it travel in the time interval $0 \le t \le \dfrac{2}{\omega}$?

- $2\,\ell$
- $0$
- $\ell$
- $4\,\ell$

## Explanation

The velocity equals $\dot{x} = 2\,\ell\,\omega\,(\omega t - 1)$, hence it is negative for $t < \dfrac{1}{\omega}$, vanishes at $t = \dfrac{1}{\omega}$ and is positive afterwards, so the particle reverses the direction of motion. At the instant of reversal it is at $x = -\ell$, while at $t = 0$ and at $t = \dfrac{2}{\omega}$ it is at $x = 0$. The distance traveled is therefore the sum of the two stretches, $\ell + \ell = 2\,\ell$. The displacement $x\!\left( \dfrac{2}{\omega} \right) - x(0)$ instead equals $0$, because the particle returns to its initial position.
