Two rigid rods $AB$ and $BC$ are connected by an internal hinge at $B$ and lie in a vertical plane; the end $A$ is constrained by a fixed hinge and the end $C$ by a roller support on a smooth horizontal guide. How many scalar equations and how many unknown reaction components does the equilibrium problem have, and what does one conclude?

- Six equations and six reaction components, therefore the system is statically determinate and has no degrees of freedom
- Six equations and seven reaction components, therefore the system is statically indeterminate
- Three equations and five reaction components, therefore the reactions are not determined
- Six equations and five reaction components, therefore one degree of freedom remains, whose coordinate is the sixth unknown

## Explanation

Taking the system apart, three cardinal equations are written for each rod, that is, six scalar equations. The unknown reaction components are two at the fixed hinge $A$, two at the internal hinge $B$ and one at the roller support $C$, that is, five. The six equations therefore contain the five reactions and the Lagrangian coordinate of the configuration, and the system has **one degree of freedom**; one of the six equations, chosen so that it contains no reactions, determines the equilibrium configurations. The count of six reactions is obtained by treating the roller support as a hinge, and that of three equations neglects taking the system apart.
