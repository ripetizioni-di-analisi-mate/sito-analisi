A plane rigid rod has a fixed hinge at $A$ and a roller support on a smooth guide at $B$, and known active forces act on it. Which pole makes the moment equation a single equation in the reaction of the roller support alone?

- The point $A$
- The point $B$
- The midpoint of the rod
- The point of application of the resultant of the active forces

## Explanation

In the moment equation a reaction does not appear when its line of action passes through the pole, since in that case its moment with respect to that pole is zero. The hinge at $A$ exerts a force of two components applied at $A$, therefore by choosing the pole at $A$ both components have zero moment and in the equation there remain the reaction of the roller support alone together with the moments of the active forces. With the pole at $B$ the reaction of the roller support would disappear instead and the two components of the hinge would remain, therefore the equation would contain two unknowns.
