In a system with one degree of freedom the equation that determines the equilibrium configuration does not contain unknown constraint reactions. Why, in this case, does rewriting the moment equation with respect to a second pole not constitute a check of the result?

- Because the constraint reactions are zero
- Because the moment equation does not apply to systems with one degree of freedom
- Because the constraints are smooth
- Because the change of pole reproduces the same equation

## Explanation

When the resultant of the external forces is zero, the formula for the change of pole gives $\vec{M}_{O'}^{(e)} = \vec{M}_O^{(e)}$, therefore the moment equation with respect to a second pole is not independent of the one already written. In the configuration equation of a system with one degree of freedom the unknown reactions do not appear, therefore on changing the pole no new terms enter and the same relation in the Lagrangian coordinate is obtained again. The check with a second pole is effective when unknown reactions appear in the computation, since the new equation contains them with different moment arms.

For a system with one degree of freedom one therefore resorts to a known limiting case, such as $k \to 0$ or $f \to 0$, or to the comparison between the equations of two subsystems.
