A particle of mass $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs in a vertical plane from two equal ideal strings, fixed to the ceiling at two points symmetric with respect to the vertical through the particle; each string makes the angle $\alpha = 60^\circ$ with the vertical, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the tension of each string?

::struttura{nodi="A,-3.46,2;B,3.46,2;P,0,0" catene="A-P;B-P" linee="-4.2,2 4.2,2" tratteggi="0,0 0,2" forze="P,0,-1.1,mg" etichette="-0.6,1.25,α" titolo="Particle hanging from two symmetric strings that make the angle $\alpha$ with the vertical"}

- $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$
- $11.33\,\mathrm{N}$
- $39.24\,\mathrm{N}$
- $19.62\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The particle is free and subject to the weight and to the two tensions, therefore equilibrium requires that the resultant vanish. The two tensions are equal by symmetry, their horizontal components cancel and each one has vertical component $T\cos\alpha$, therefore equilibrium in the vertical direction is written as
$$
2\,T\cos\alpha - m g = 0 .
$$
From it follows $T = \dfrac{m g}{2\cos\alpha}$, and with $\alpha = 60^\circ$ one obtains $T = m g = 19.62\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$ is half the weight, and it would be obtained only with vertical strings, that is, for $\alpha = 0$.
