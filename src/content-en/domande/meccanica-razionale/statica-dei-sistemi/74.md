A stepladder is formed by two homogeneous rods $AB$ and $BC$, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, hinged to each other at $B$, with the ends $A$ and $C$ resting on a smooth horizontal floor. A horizontal ideal tie connects the midpoints of the two rods, and each rod makes the angle $\vartheta$ with the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the tension of the tie?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,3.46;C,4,0;M,1,1.73,-;N,3,1.73,-" aste="A-B;B-C" cerniere="B" catene="M-N" linee="-0.8,0 4.8,0" forze="1 1.73,0,-1,mg;3 1.73,0,-1,mg" etichette="0.62,0.3,ϑ;3.38,0.3,ϑ" titolo="Stepladder on a smooth floor, with the horizontal tie between the midpoints of the rods"}

- $\dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{\tan\vartheta}$
- $m g\tan\vartheta$
- $\dfrac{2\,m g}{\tan\vartheta}$

## Explanation

The whole system is symmetric and the reactions of the floor are vertical, therefore the vertical component of the first cardinal equation gives $\Phi_A = \Phi_C = m g$. The system is then taken apart and the rod $AB$ is considered, subject to $\Phi_A$ at $A$, to the weight and the tension $T$ at the midpoint, and to the action of the other rod at $B$. The moment equation with respect to $B$ does not contain the action of the internal hinge; the lever arms with respect to $B$ are $\ell\cos\vartheta$ for $\Phi_A$, $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$ for the weight and $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$ for the tension, therefore
$$
-\,m g\,\ell\cos\vartheta + m g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + T\,\frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta = 0 ,
\qquad\text{whence}\qquad
T = \frac{m g}{\tan\vartheta} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$ is the tension of a tie connecting the ends $A$ and $C$, with lever arm $\ell\sin\vartheta$.
