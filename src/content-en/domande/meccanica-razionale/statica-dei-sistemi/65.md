A wheelbarrow is modeled as a rigid rod $OH$ of negligible mass, with the wheel hinged at the end $O$ and resting on the ground, and with the handles at the end $H$, at horizontal distance $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$. The load, of mass $50\,\mathrm{kg}$, has its center of mass $G$ on the vertical through the point at horizontal distance $0.3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$, as in the diagram in the figure. Which vertical force, applied at $H$, keeps the wheelbarrow in equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;H,4,0" aste="O-H" cerchi="0,0,0.5" linee="-1,-0.5 5,-0.5" punti="1,0,G" forze="1 0,0,-1.1,mg;H,0,1,F" quote="O,1 0,0.3 m,-0.8;O,H,1.2 m,1.3" titolo="Wheelbarrow: wheel at $O$, load at $G$ and vertical force $\vec{F}$ applied to the handles at $H$"}

- $367.88\,\mathrm{N}$
- $490.50\,\mathrm{N}$
- $122.63\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1962.00\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The forces acting on the wheelbarrow are the weight of the load, the force $\vec{F}$ applied at $H$ and the reaction of the ground, which is vertical and has its line of action through the axle $O$ of the wheel. The moment equation with respect to $O$ does not contain the reaction of the ground, therefore
$$
F \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m} = 50 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.3\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad\text{whence}\qquad
F = 122.63\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
directed upward. The vertical component of the first cardinal equation then gives the reaction of the ground, $(490.50 - 122.63)\,\mathrm{N} = 367.88\,\mathrm{N}$; this is the value obtained by exchanging the force at the handles with the reaction of the wheel.
