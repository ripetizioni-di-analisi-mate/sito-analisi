A nutcracker is formed by two rigid rods of negligible mass, hinged to each other at the end $O$. The nut is squeezed between the rods at distance $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ from $O$, and the hand applies at the other ends, at distance $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from $O$, two forces of magnitude $20\,\mathrm{N}$, as in the diagram in the figure. The forces of the hand and the reactions of the nut are taken orthogonal to the rods. What is the magnitude of the force that each rod exerts on the nut?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;U,5,1.25;D,5,-1.25" aste="O-U;O-D" cerniere="O" cerchi="1,0,0.22" forze="U,0.3,-1.2,F;D,0.3,1.2,F" quote="O,1 0,3 cm,-1.6;O,5 0,15 cm,-2.4" titolo="Nutcracker: two rods hinged at $O$, with the nut at $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the forces of the hand at $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from $O$"}

- $4.00\,\mathrm{N}$
- $200.00\,\mathrm{N}$
- $100.00\,\mathrm{N}$
- $80.00\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The system is taken apart and a single rod is considered, subject to the force of the hand, to the reaction $\Phi$ of the nut and to the action of the other rod at the hinge $O$. The moment equation with respect to $O$ does not contain the action of the hinge, and the two remaining forces have lever arms $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $3\,\mathrm{cm}$, therefore
$$
\Phi \cdot 3\,\mathrm{cm} = 20\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 15\,\mathrm{cm} ,
\qquad\text{whence}\qquad
\Phi = 100.00\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
By the action-reaction principle the rod presses on the nut with the same magnitude. The value $80.00\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by measuring the lever arm of the force of the hand from the nut instead of from the hinge.
