A particle of mass $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the lower end of a vertical ideal spring of constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and natural length $\ell_0 = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, fixed to the ceiling by its upper end. How long is the spring at equilibrium?

- $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The particle is subject to the weight and to the force of the spring, therefore equilibrium requires that the resultant vanish. Denoting by $\ell$ the length of the spring at equilibrium, the elastic force is directed upward and has magnitude $k\,(\ell - \ell_0)$, therefore equilibrium in the vertical direction is written as
$$
k\,(\ell - \ell_0) - m g = 0 .
$$
From it follows $\ell = \ell_0 + \dfrac{m g}{k}$, and with the assigned values the length equals $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by subtracting the elongation instead of adding it, that is, by compressing the spring with the weight.
