A rigid rod $AB$ of negligible mass and length $\ell$ hangs from the ceiling by two vertical ideal springs having the same natural length: the spring attached at $A$ has constant $3k$ and the one attached at $B$ has constant $k$. A vertical load $\vec{P}$ acts on the rod at the point at distance $x$ from $A$, as in the diagram in the figure. For which value of $x$ does the rod remain horizontal at equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,1;B,4,1" aste="A-B" linee="-0.6,3 4.6,3;0.00,3.00 0.00,2.70 0.20,2.61 -0.20,2.44 0.20,2.26 -0.20,2.09 0.20,1.91 -0.20,1.74 0.20,1.56 -0.20,1.39 0.00,1.30 0.00,1.00;4.00,3.00 4.00,2.70 4.20,2.61 3.80,2.44 4.20,2.26 3.80,2.09 4.20,1.91 3.80,1.74 4.20,1.56 3.80,1.39 4.00,1.30 4.00,1.00" forze="1 1,0,-0.9,P" quote="A,1 1,x,0.55;A,B,ℓ,1.3" etichette="-0.6,2,3k;4.5,2,k" titolo="Rod hanging from the springs of constants $3k$ and $k$, with the load $\vec{P}$ at distance $x$ from $A$"}

- $\dfrac{3\ell}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\ell}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\ell}{4}$

## Explanation

The rod remains horizontal if the two springs have the same elongation $\delta$, therefore the elastic forces are $3k\,\delta$ at $A$ and $k\,\delta$ at $B$. The moment equation with respect to the point of application of the load does not contain $\vec{P}$ and gives
$$
3k\,\delta\,x = k\,\delta\,(\ell - x) ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
x = \frac{\ell}{4} .
$$
The load therefore lies closer to the stiffer spring, which supports three quarters of it. The value $\dfrac{3\ell}{4}$ is obtained by exchanging the constants of the two springs.
