A flexible inextensible rope of mass $m$ hangs from two hooks $A$ and $B$ at the same height and, at equilibrium, takes the symmetric shape in the figure. At each end the rope makes the angle $\beta$ with the horizontal. What is the tension of the rope at each end?

- $\dfrac{m g}{2\cos\beta}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{2}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{\sin\beta}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{2\sin\beta}$

## Explanation

The rope is not a rigid body, but the cardinal equations of statics are necessary conditions for every material system in equilibrium, therefore they hold for the whole rope. The external forces are the weight $m g$ and the two forces of the hooks, directed along the tangent to the rope at the ends and, by symmetry, of equal magnitude $T$. Their horizontal components are opposite, and the vertical component of the first cardinal equation gives
$$
2\,T\sin\beta - m g = 0 ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
T = \frac{m g}{2\sin\beta} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{m g}{2\cos\beta}$ is obtained by projecting the tensions onto the horizontal instead of the vertical.
