A ring $P$ of mass $m$, which can be treated as a particle, is threaded on a smooth fixed circular guide of radius $R$ and center $O$, lying in a vertical plane. A constant horizontal force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, is applied to the ring, as in the diagram in the figure. Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle that $OP$ makes with the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise, which condition identifies the equilibrium configurations?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="0,0,1.29,-1.53;0,0,0,-2" vettori="1.29,-1.53,1.29,-2.63,mg;1.29,-1.53,2.49,-1.53,F" punti="1.29,-1.53,pieno,P;0,0,pieno,O" etichette="0.35,-1.15,ϑ" xmin=-2.6 xmax=3.3 ymin=-3 ymax=2.4 titolo="Ring on a smooth circular guide subject to the weight and to a constant horizontal force"}

- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{F}{m g}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{m g}{F}$
- $\sin\vartheta = \dfrac{F}{m g}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{F}{2\,m g}$

## Explanation

The guide is smooth, therefore the constraint reaction is orthogonal to the guide and equilibrium holds when the tangential component of the active forces vanishes. The unit tangent vector to the circle, oriented in the direction of increasing $\vartheta$, equals $\hat{t} = \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$, and the resultant of the active forces is $\vec{F} + (-\,m g\,\hat{\jmath})$, therefore the equilibrium condition is written as
$$
F\cos\vartheta - m g \sin\vartheta = 0 .
$$
Since $\cos\vartheta = 0$ does not satisfy the equation, one can divide by $m g \cos\vartheta$, and one obtains $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{F}{m g}$. The condition with the ratio inverted corresponds to interchanging the two components of the active force.
