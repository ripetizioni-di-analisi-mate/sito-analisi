A plane rigid rod is constrained by two fixed hinges, one at each of its ends, and known active forces act on it. What can be said of the constraint reactions?

- They are determined by the three cardinal equations
- They are all zero
- They are not determined by the cardinal equations alone, since the unknowns are four and the equations three
- The rod retains one residual degree of freedom

## Explanation

Each fixed hinge exerts a force of two components, therefore the unknown reaction components are four, whereas the cardinal equations of a plane rigid body are three scalar equations. The count $3 = n + r$ is not satisfied, given that $r = 4$ exceeds the number of the equations, therefore the reactions are not obtained from the cardinal equations alone and the rod is said to be statically indeterminate. The configurations remain determined, since the two ends are fixed and the rod has no residual degrees of freedom, whereas to distribute the reactions between the two hinges information on the deformability of the body is needed, which the rigid model does not provide.
