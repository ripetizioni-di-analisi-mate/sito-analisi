A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $A$ to a vertical wall and is kept horizontal by an ideal tie $BC$, fixed to the wall at the point $C$ above $A$ and inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure. What angle does the reaction of the hinge at $A$ make with the horizontal?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,0,2.31" aste="A-B" catene="B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera,180" linee="0,-0.8 0,3" forze="2 0,0,-0.6,mg" quote="A,B,ℓ,0.7" etichette="3.2,0.2,30°" titolo="Rod hinged to the wall at $A$ and supported by the tie $BC$ inclined at $30^\circ$"}

- $60^\circ$
- $30^\circ$
- $0^\circ$
- $90^\circ$

## Explanation

The forces acting on the rod are the weight, applied at the midpoint, the tension $T$ of the tie and the reaction at $A$. The moment equation with respect to $A$ gives $T\sin 30^\circ\,\ell = m g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, therefore $T = m g$. The two components of the first cardinal equation then give
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} - T\cos 30^\circ = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Ay} + T\sin 30^\circ - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
that is $\Phi_{Ax} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,m g$ and $\Phi_{Ay} = \dfrac{m g}{2}$. Hence $\dfrac{\Phi_{Ay}}{\Phi_{Ax}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ and the reaction makes the angle of $30^\circ$ with the horizontal, pointing upward and toward the rod. The same result follows from the fact that three non-parallel forces in equilibrium have concurrent lines of action, and the lines of the weight and of the tie meet on the vertical through the midpoint, at height $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\tan 30^\circ$.
