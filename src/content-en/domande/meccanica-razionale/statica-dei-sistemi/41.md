Two homogeneous rods $AB$ and $BC$, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, are hinged to each other at $B$ and hinged to the ground at $A$ and at $C$, in a vertical plane; the points $A$ and $C$ lie at the same height and each rod makes the angle $\vartheta$ with the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure. The weights are the only active forces. What is the vertical component of the reaction of the hinge at $A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,2;C,4,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,cerniera" cerniere="B" forze="1 1,0,-0.9,mg;3 1,0,-0.9,mg" etichette="0.6,0.3,ϑ" titolo="Arch formed by two rods hinged to each other at $B$ and to the ground at $A$ and at $C$"}

- $\dfrac{m g}{2}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$
- $m g$
- $2\,m g$

## Explanation

The equilibrium of the whole system under vertical translation gives $\Phi_{Ay} + \Phi_{Cy} = 2\,m g$, since the active forces are the two weights and the internal reactions at $B$ do not appear. The system is symmetric with respect to the vertical through $B$, both in its geometry and in its loads, therefore the two reactions at the ground have the same vertical component and one obtains
$$
\Phi_{Ay} = \Phi_{Cy} = m g .
$$
Each hinge therefore supports the weight of the rod attached to it, and the result does not depend on the angle $\vartheta$. The horizontal component, on the other hand, depends on $\vartheta$ and is obtained by taking the system apart and writing the moment equation of a single rod with respect to $B$.
