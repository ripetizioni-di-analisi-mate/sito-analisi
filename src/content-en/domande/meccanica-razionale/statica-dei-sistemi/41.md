Two planar rods are joined by an ideal internal hinge. Why do the equilibrium equations for the entire assembly alone fail to determine the two hinge-force components, even when the external reactions are known?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.6,1.0;C,5.3,0" aste="A-B;B-C" cerniere="B" riferimento="0" titolo="Rods AB and BC share pin B. External loads are omitted."}

- An internal hinge transmits only a moment.
- The vertical force component must vanish.
- The hinge forces cancel in the global balance; at least one rod must be isolated.
- Any two components are consistent with equilibrium of each individual rod.

## Explanation

The internal actions form an action–reaction pair at the same point. A free-body diagram of one rod contains only one of them, so suitable equilibrium equations can then determine it.
