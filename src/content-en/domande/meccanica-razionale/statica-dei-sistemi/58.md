A homogeneous square plate $ABCD$, of mass $m$ and side $a$, is held at rest in a horizontal plane by three vertical strings attached to the vertices $A$, $B$ and $D$; the vertex $C$, opposite to $A$, is free. What is the tension of the string attached at $A$?

- $\dfrac{m g}{3}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{2}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{m g}{4}$

## Explanation

The problem is in space. Take the origin at $A$, the $x$ axis along $AB$, the $y$ axis along $AD$ and the $z$ axis vertical upward, so that the center of mass is $G = \left(\dfrac{a}{2}, \dfrac{a}{2}, 0\right)$. The forces are all vertical, therefore the cardinal equations that are not identically satisfied are three, the vertical component of the resultant and the $x$ and $y$ components of the moment with respect to $A$:
$$
\begin{cases}
T_A + T_B + T_D - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
a\,T_D - \dfrac{a}{2}\,m g = 0 \\[4pt]
-\,a\,T_B + \dfrac{a}{2}\,m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The last two give $T_B = T_D = \dfrac{m g}{2}$, therefore the first gives $T_A = 0$. The result has a geometric reading, since $G$ lies on the diagonal $BD$, and therefore the strings at $B$ and at $D$ are enough to support the plate.
