An ideal spring of constant $k = 300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and natural length $\ell_0 = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ connects the points $A$ and $B$, which are at distance $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the force that the spring exerts on $B$ and how is it directed?

- $60\,\mathrm{N}$, directed from $B$ toward $A$
- $105\,\mathrm{N}$, directed from $B$ toward $A$
- $45\,\mathrm{N}$, directed from $B$ toward $A$
- $45\,\mathrm{N}$, directed from $A$ toward $B$

## Explanation

The spring exerts on $B$ a force directed along the line joining $A$ and $B$, of magnitude equal to $k$ times the change in length with respect to the natural length. The distance between the two points exceeds the natural length, therefore the spring is stretched by $0.35 - 0.20 = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ and the force is attractive, therefore
$$
|\vec{F}_B| = k\,\big(|B - A| - \ell_0\big) = 300 \cdot 0.15 = 45\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
directed from $B$ toward $A$. The value $105\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by using the whole distance instead of its change, that is, by treating the spring as if it had zero natural length.
