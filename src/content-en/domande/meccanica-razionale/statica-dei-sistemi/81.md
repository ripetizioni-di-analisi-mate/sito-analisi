A solid homogeneous hemisphere of radius $R$ rests with its curved surface on a rough inclined plane, in a vertical plane, and friction is sufficient to prevent sliding. The center of mass $G$ lies on the symmetry axis at distance $\dfrac{3}{8}\,R$ from the center $O$ of the base, as in the cross-section in the figure. What is the maximum inclination of the plane for which an equilibrium configuration exists?

- $30.00^\circ$
- $22.02^\circ$
- $25.11^\circ$
- $67.98^\circ$

## Explanation

The reaction of the plane is applied at the contact point $C$, which lies on the normal to the plane through $O$, at distance $R$ from $O$. The moment equation with respect to $C$ contains only the weight, therefore at equilibrium $G$ lies on the vertical through $C$. Denoting by $\alpha$ the inclination of the plane, the vertical through $C$ lies at distance $R\sin\alpha$ from the one through $O$; if the axis makes the angle $\psi$ with the vertical, $G$ lies at distance $\dfrac{3}{8}\,R\sin\psi$ from the vertical through $O$, hence equilibrium requires
$$
\frac{3}{8}\,R\sin\psi = R\sin\alpha ,
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
\sin\psi = \frac{8}{3}\sin\alpha .
$$
The solution exists if $\dfrac{8}{3}\sin\alpha \le 1$, therefore $\alpha \le \arcsin\dfrac{3}{8} = 22.02^\circ$. The value $25.11^\circ$ is obtained with the distance $\dfrac{4R}{3\pi}$, which is that of the center of mass of a half-disk.
