A body of mass $m = 5\,\mathrm{kg}$, which can be modeled as a particle, rests on a rough horizontal plane with coefficient of static friction $f = 0.4$ and is pushed by a horizontal force of magnitude $10\,\mathrm{N}$. What is the magnitude of the friction force that the plane exerts on the body?

- $19.62\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.00\,\mathrm{N}$
- $10.00\,\mathrm{N}$
- $49.05\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The normal component of the reaction is $\Phi_n = m g = 49.05\,\mathrm{N}$, therefore the limit value of friction is $f\,\Phi_n = 19.62\,\mathrm{N}$, greater than the push, and equilibrium is possible. The Coulomb-Morin law only fixes the upper bound $|\vec{\Phi}_t| \le f\,\Phi_n$; the actual value of the tangential component is obtained from the equilibrium equation in the horizontal direction,
$$
10\,\mathrm{N} - |\vec{\Phi}_t| = 0 .
$$
Therefore friction equals $10.00\,\mathrm{N}$ and is directed opposite to the push. The value $19.62\,\mathrm{N}$ is the maximum friction, which is reached only in the limit condition.
