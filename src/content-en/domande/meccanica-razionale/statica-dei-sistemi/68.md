A homogeneous rectangular plate $ABCD$, with the side $AB$ of length $2a$ and the side $BC$ of length $a$, hangs in a vertical plane from a smooth hinge at the vertex $A$, and the weight is the only active force. At equilibrium, with the center of mass below $A$, what angle does the side $AB$ make with the vertical?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.30,-1.93;C,1.33,-3.08;D,-0.96,-1.15" poligoni="0,0 2.30,-1.93 1.33,-3.08 -0.96,-1.15" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,0 0,-3.6" punti="0.67,-1.54,G,baricentro" quote="A,B,2a,-0.6;B,C,a,-0.6" titolo="Rectangular plate hanging from the vertex $A$, drawn in a generic configuration"}

- $45.00^\circ$
- $63.43^\circ$
- $26.57^\circ$
- $14.04^\circ$

## Explanation

The reaction of the hinge is applied at $A$, therefore the moment equation with respect to $A$ contains only the weight and requires that its lever arm be zero. At equilibrium the center of mass $G$, which is the center of the rectangle, therefore lies on the vertical through $A$. In the frame of the plate, with the origin at $A$, the $x$ axis along $AB$ and the $y$ axis along $AD$, one has $G - A = a\,\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{a}{2}\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore the angle $\varphi$ between $AB$ and $AG$ satisfies $\tan\varphi = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $AG$ is vertical, the side $AB$ makes with the vertical the angle $\varphi = \arctan\dfrac{1}{2} = 26.57^\circ$. The angle $45^\circ$ would be obtained for a square plate, and $63.43^\circ$ is the angle that $AB$ makes with the horizontal.
