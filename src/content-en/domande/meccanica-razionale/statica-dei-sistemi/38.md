Two particles are connected by an ideal string that passes over an ideal pulley placed at the upper vertex of a smooth inclined plane, which makes the angle $\alpha = 30^\circ$ with the horizontal. The first particle, of mass $m_1 = 3\,\mathrm{kg}$, lies on the inclined plane with the string parallel to the plane; the second, of mass $m_2$, hangs vertically, as in the diagram in the figure. What is $m_2$ at equilibrium?

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 6,3.46 6,0 0,0" nodi="K,6,3.46,-;P,3.4,1.96,-;Q,6.22,1.5,-" catene="P-K;K-Q" cerchi="6,3.46,0.22" punti="3.4,1.96,P_1;6.22,1.5,P_2" forze="3.4 1.96,0,-1,m_1g;6.22 1.5,0,-1,m_2g" etichette="0.95,0.2,α" titolo="Two particles connected by a string that passes over the pulley at the vertex of the smooth inclined plane"}

- $2.60\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $3.00\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6.00\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.50\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The second particle hangs vertically, therefore its equilibrium gives $T = m_2 g$, and the pulley is ideal, therefore it changes the direction of the string and leaves its tension unchanged. The inclined plane is smooth, therefore its reaction is orthogonal to the plane and the equilibrium of the first particle along the plane involves only the tension and the component of the weight parallel to the plane:
$$
T - m_1 g \sin\alpha = 0 .
$$
From it follows $m_2 = m_1 \sin\alpha = 1.50\,\mathrm{kg}$. The value $2.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ is obtained by using the component of the weight orthogonal to the plane, which is balanced by the constraint reaction and not by the string.
