A load of mass $m = 40\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the center of an ideal movable pulley, of negligible mass. An ideal string passes under the pulley; one branch goes up vertically to the ceiling, where it is fixed, and the other goes up vertically to the point where the vertical force $\vec{F}$ is applied, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the magnitude of $\vec{F}$ at equilibrium?

::struttura{linee="-0.3,4.5 0.9,4.5" cerchi="1,1.6,0.5" nodi="K,0.5,4.5,-;L,0.5,1.6,-;M,1.5,1.6,-;E,1.5,3.5,-;O,1,1.6,-;Q,1,0.4,-" catene="K-L;M-E;O-Q" punti="1,1.6;1,0.4" forze="1.5 4.4,0,0.9,;1 -0.5,0,-0.9," etichette="1.85,4.15,F;1.4,-0.2,mg" titolo="Movable pulley with the hanging load: one branch of the string is fixed to the ceiling, the other is pulled by the force $\vec{F}$"}

- $392.40\,\mathrm{N}$
- $196.20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $98.10\,\mathrm{N}$
- $130.80\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The pulley is ideal, therefore the tension of the string has the same value $T$ in the two branches, and in the pulled branch $T = F$. The system formed by the pulley and the load is subject to the weight $m g$ and to the forces of the two branches, both vertical and upward, hence the first cardinal equation gives
$$
2\,T - m g = 0 ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
F = \frac{m g}{2} = \frac{40 \cdot 9.81}{2}\,\mathrm{N} = 196.20\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $392.40\,\mathrm{N}$ is the weight of the load, that is, the force needed with a single fixed pulley.
