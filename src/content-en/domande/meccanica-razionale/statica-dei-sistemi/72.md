Two fixed ideal pulleys $L$ and $R$ are at the same height. An ideal string passes over $L$ and carries at its end a particle of mass $m_1 = 3\,\mathrm{kg}$; a second string passes over $R$ and carries at its end a particle of mass $m_2 = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$. The other two ends of the strings are tied at the point $N$, from which a particle of mass $M = 5\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs, as in the diagram in the figure. At equilibrium, what angle do the two strings going from $N$ to the pulleys make with each other?

::struttura{nodi="L,0,4;R,6,4;N,3,1.8;P1,-0.25,2.3,-;P2,6.25,2.7,-;L2,-0.25,4,-;R2,6.25,4,-;Q,3,0.6,-" cerchi="0,4,0.25;6,4,0.25" catene="N-L;N-R;L2-P1;R2-P2;N-Q" punti="-0.25,2.3;6.25,2.7;3,0.6" forze="P1,0,-0.9,m_{1}g;P2,0,-0.9,m_{2}g;Q,0,-0.9,Mg" titolo="Knot $N$ with the weight $M g$, connected by the strings passing over the pulleys $L$ and $R$ to the weights $m_1 g$ and $m_2 g$"}

- $120^\circ$
- $60^\circ$
- $90^\circ$
- $106.26^\circ$

## Explanation

The pulleys are ideal, therefore the strings going from $N$ to the pulleys have tensions $T_1 = m_1 g$ and $T_2 = m_2 g$, while the vertical string pulls $N$ downward with the force $M g$. The knot is in equilibrium, hence the resultant of the first two forces is opposite to the third, that is, vertical upward and of magnitude $5 g$. Denoting by $\gamma$ the angle between the two strings, the magnitude of the sum gives
$$
(5 g)^2 = (3 g)^2 + (4 g)^2 + 2 \cdot 3 g \cdot 4 g\cos\gamma ,
$$
therefore $\cos\gamma = 0$ and $\gamma = 90^\circ$. The value $106.26^\circ$ is twice the angle that the string directed toward $L$ makes with the vertical, which is $53.13^\circ$.
