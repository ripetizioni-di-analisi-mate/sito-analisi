A pulley formed by two rigidly connected disks of radii $R$ and $2R$ can rotate without friction about its own fixed horizontal axis, passing through the center $O$. A particle of mass $m_1 = 8\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the ideal string wound on the groove of radius $R$; a particle of mass $m_2$ hangs from the ideal string wound on the groove of radius $2R$, which comes down on the opposite side, as in the diagram in the figure. What is $m_2$ at equilibrium?

::struttura{cerchi="3,4,2;3,4,1" nodi="O,3,4;D,4,1,-;E,1,1,-" catene="4 4-D;1 4-E" vincoli="O,cerniera" punti="4,4;1,4;4,1;1,1" forze="4 1,0,-1,m_1g;1 1,0,-1,m_2g" etichette="3.55,4.3,R;1.6,4.3,2R" titolo="Two-groove pulley with the two weights hanging from the strings wound on the radii $R$ and $2R$"}

- $2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $16\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Each hanging particle is in equilibrium under its own weight and the tension of the string that supports it, therefore the two tensions equal $T_1 = m_1 g$ and $T_2 = m_2 g$. The pulley is a rigid body with one degree of freedom and the moment equation with respect to $O$ contains neither the reaction of the axis nor the weight of the pulley, which is applied at $O$; the two strings are tangent to the respective grooves and have moment arms $R$ and $2R$, with moments of opposite direction, therefore
$$
m_1 g R - 2\,m_2 g R = 0 .
$$
Therefore $m_2 = \dfrac{m_1}{2} = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$. The value $16\,\mathrm{kg}$ is obtained by inverting the ratio between the moment arms, whereas the double moment arm belongs to the string wound on the groove of radius $2R$.
