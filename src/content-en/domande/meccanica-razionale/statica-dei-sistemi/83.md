A horizontal rod $AB$ of negligible mass and length $\ell$ has a fixed hinge at $A$ and a roller support on a smooth horizontal guide at $B$; at the point $P$ with $AP = \dfrac{\ell}{3}$ a vertical force of magnitude $F$ directed downward acts. A student finds the vertical reactions $\Phi_A = \dfrac{F}{3}$ and $\Phi_B = \dfrac{2F}{3}$, both upward, and checks them with the moment equation with respect to $B$. What does the check give?

- The resultant moment with respect to $B$ is zero, therefore the reactions are correct
- The resultant moment with respect to $B$ equals $\dfrac{2F\ell}{3}$, therefore the reactions are wrong
- The resultant moment with respect to $B$ equals $\dfrac{F\ell}{3}$, therefore the reactions are wrong
- The check is not possible, since a reaction is applied at $B$

## Explanation

With respect to $B$ the reaction of the roller support has zero moment, $\Phi_A$ has lever arm $\ell$ and the force has lever arm $\dfrac{2\ell}{3}$. With the student's reactions the resultant moment, positive counterclockwise, is
$$
-\,\ell\,\frac{F}{3} + \frac{2\ell}{3}\,F = \frac{F\ell}{3} \neq 0 ,
$$
therefore the reactions **are wrong**. The correct reactions are $\Phi_A = \dfrac{2F}{3}$ and $\Phi_B = \dfrac{F}{3}$, since the force is closer to $A$, which supports the larger part of it, and the student exchanged them. The value $\dfrac{2F\ell}{3}$ is obtained by forgetting the moment of $\Phi_A$.
