For a rigid rod free in a plane, subject to known external forces, one verifies that in a certain configuration the resultant $\vec{R}^{(e)}$ is zero and the resultant moment $\vec{M}_A^{(e)}$ with respect to the end $A$ is zero. The rod is placed in that configuration with zero velocities. What happens?

- Nothing can be concluded, since the pole used is not the center of mass
- The rod remains at rest, since with zero resultant the resultant moment is zero with respect to every pole, in particular with respect to $G$
- The rod remains at rest only if $A$ coincides with the center of mass
- The rod rotates about $A$

## Explanation

By the change-of-pole formula one has $\vec{M}_G^{(e)} = \vec{M}_A^{(e)} + (A - G) \wedge \vec{R}^{(e)}$. Since $\vec{R}^{(e)} = \vec{0}$, it follows that $\vec{M}_G^{(e)} = \vec{M}_A^{(e)} = \vec{0}$. The cardinal equations with pole $G$ are therefore satisfied, and for a rigid body they are sufficient conditions for equilibrium, hence the rod **remains at rest**.
