A square plate $ABCD$ of side $a$ rests on a smooth horizontal plane. In the plane of the plate the force $F\,\hat{\imath}$ applied at the vertex $A$ and the force $-\,F\,\hat{\imath}$ applied at the opposite vertex $C$ act, where $\hat{\imath}$ is the unit vector of the side $AB$. Does the plate, placed at rest, remain in equilibrium?

- Yes, since the resultant of the applied forces is zero
- No, since the resultant of the applied forces has magnitude $2F$
- No, since the two forces form a couple of moment of magnitude $F a$
- No, since the two forces form a couple of moment of magnitude $F a\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The weight and the reaction of the plane are orthogonal to the plane and balance each other, therefore the two forces in the plane of the plate remain. Their resultant is zero, hence their resultant moment does not depend on the pole and can be computed with respect to $A$. With the origin at $A$, the $x$ axis along $AB$ and $C - A = a\,\hat{\imath} + a\,\hat{\jmath}$, one has
$$
(C - A) \wedge (-\,F\,\hat{\imath}) = a F\,\hat{k} \neq \vec{0} .
$$
The second cardinal equation is not satisfied, therefore the plate **is not in equilibrium** and starts to rotate. The lever arm of the couple is the distance $a$ between the two lines of action, parallel to the side $AB$, while the diagonal $AC$, of length $a\sqrt{2}$, is not orthogonal to the forces.
