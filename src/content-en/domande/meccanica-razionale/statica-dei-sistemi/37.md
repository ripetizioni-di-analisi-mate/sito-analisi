A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $A$ to a fixed point and kept horizontal in a vertical plane by a vertical ideal spring of constant $k$, attached to the end $B$ and to the ceiling, as in the diagram in the figure. By how much is the spring stretched at equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera" linee="5.00,0.00 5.00,0.40 5.25,0.80 4.75,1.20 5.25,1.60 4.75,2.00 5.00,2.40 5.00,2.80;4.3,2.8 5.7,2.8" forze="2.5 0,0,-1.1,mg" quote="A,B,ℓ,1.3" etichette="5.6,1.4,k" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$ and supported by the vertical spring attached at $B$"}

- $\dfrac{m g}{4 k}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{2 k}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{k}$
- $\dfrac{2\,m g}{k}$

## Explanation

The rod is a rigid body with the hinge at $A$, therefore the moment equation with respect to $A$ does not contain the reaction of the hinge and by itself determines the elongation of the spring. Denoting by $\Delta$ the elongation, the spring pulls $B$ upward with magnitude $k\,\Delta$ and moment arm $\ell$, whereas the weight acts at the midpoint with moment arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, therefore
$$
k\,\Delta\,\ell - \frac{m g \ell}{2} = 0 .
$$
Therefore $\Delta = \dfrac{m g}{2 k}$, that is, the spring supports half the weight of the rod. The value $\dfrac{m g}{k}$ would be obtained by applying the weight at the end $B$ instead of at the midpoint.
