A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ lies in a vertical plane with the end $A$ resting on a rough horizontal floor, of coefficient of static friction $f$, and the end $B$ hanging from the ceiling by a vertical ideal string. The rod makes the angle $\vartheta$ with the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the friction force that the floor exerts at $A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,2.5;K,4,4.4,-" aste="A-B" catene="B-K" linee="-0.8,0 5,0;3.3,4.4 4.7,4.4" forze="2 1.25,0,-1,mg" tratteggi="0,0 1.6,0" etichette="0.95,0.25,ϑ;0.7,-0.35,f" titolo="Rod resting on the rough floor at $A$ and hanging at $B$ from a vertical string"}

- $\dfrac{f\,m g}{2}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$
- $f\,m g$

## Explanation

The forces acting on the rod are the weight, the tension of the string, which is vertical, and the reaction of the floor at $A$, with the vertical normal component and the horizontal friction component. The horizontal component of the first cardinal equation contains only friction, since all the other forces are vertical, therefore friction is **zero** whatever $\vartheta$ is. The moment equation with respect to $A$ then gives $T\,\ell\cos\vartheta = m g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, that is $T = \dfrac{m g}{2}$, and the vertical component gives $\Phi_n = \dfrac{m g}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$ is the friction required when the end $B$ rests against a smooth vertical wall, which exerts a horizontal reaction.
