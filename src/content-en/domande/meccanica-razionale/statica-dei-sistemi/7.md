A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ can rotate without friction about its own center $O$, fixed to a horizontal axis, in a vertical plane. An ideal string wound on the rim of the disk hangs vertically on the right-hand side and supports a particle of mass $m_1$. A couple of moment $\mathcal{M}$ in the counterclockwise direction is applied to the disk, as in the diagram in the figure. What is $\mathcal{M}$ at equilibrium?

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2" nodi="O,0,0;Q,2,-3.2,-" catene="2 0-Q" vincoli="O,cerniera" coppie="-1 0.8,1,ℳ" punti="2,0;2,-3.2" forze="O,0,-1.4,mg;Q,0,-1.1,m_1g" titolo="Disk with fixed axis at $O$, string wound on the rim and couple $\mathcal{M}$ applied"}

- $\dfrac{m_1 g R}{2}$
- $(m + m_1)\,g R$
- $m_1 g R$
- $2\,m_1 g R$

## Explanation

The hanging particle is in equilibrium under its own weight and the tension of the string, therefore $T = m_1 g$. The disk is a rigid body with one degree of freedom and the moment equation with respect to $O$ does not contain the reaction of the axis, since its line of action passes through $O$; the weight of the disk is applied at $O$ and has zero moment, whereas the string, tangent to the rim, has moment arm $R$. Therefore the moment equation is
$$
\mathcal{M} - T R = 0 ,
$$
and the moment of the couple that maintains equilibrium equals $\mathcal{M} = m_1 g R$, in the counterclockwise direction. The value $(m + m_1)\,g R$ would be obtained by assigning a moment arm to the weight of the disk as well, which instead acts at the pole.
