A particle rests on a rough inclined plane, with coefficient of static friction $f = 0.5$, and the weight is the only active force. What is the largest inclination of the plane to the horizontal for which equilibrium is possible?

- $14.04^\circ$
- $26.57^\circ$
- $45.00^\circ$
- $63.43^\circ$

## Explanation

The particle is in equilibrium when the required reaction falls inside the friction cone, therefore the components of the weight along the plane and in the normal direction are to be written. Denoting by $\alpha$ the inclination of the plane, the normal component equals $m g \cos\alpha$ and the one parallel to the plane $m g \sin\alpha$, therefore the Coulomb-Morin law requires
$$
m g \sin\alpha \le f\,m g \cos\alpha .
$$
From it follows $\tan\alpha \le f$, that is, $\alpha \le \arctan 0.5 = 26.57^\circ$. The angle $63.43^\circ$ is $\arctan\dfrac{1}{f}$ and would be obtained by interchanging the two components of the weight.
