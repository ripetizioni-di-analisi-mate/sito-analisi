A particle rests on a rough surface with coefficient of static friction $f = 0.4$ and at equilibrium the normal component of the reaction equals $\Phi_n = 20\,\mathrm{N}$. What does the Coulomb-Morin law state about the tangential component $\vec{\Phi}_t$ of the reaction?

- $|\vec{\Phi}_t| \le 8\,\mathrm{N}$
- $|\vec{\Phi}_t| \le 20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $|\vec{\Phi}_t| \le 50\,\mathrm{N}$
- $|\vec{\Phi}_t| = 8\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The Coulomb-Morin law bounds the tangential component of the reaction by the inequality $|\vec{\Phi}_t| \le f\,\Phi_n$, where $f$ is the coefficient of static friction. With the assigned values one obtains
$$
|\vec{\Phi}_t| \le 0.4 \cdot 20 = 8\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $8\,\mathrm{N}$ is therefore the maximum that the tangential component can take and not the value that it does take, since the tangential component equals what is needed to balance the active forces. The value $50\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by dividing by $f$ instead of multiplying.
