A rigid table has a horizontal square top and four vertical legs at the vertices, resting on a smooth horizontal floor. An object is placed on the top, at an arbitrary point. Are the four reactions of the floor, all vertical, determined by the cardinal equations of statics?

- Yes, since the cardinal equations in space are six and the unknowns four
- No, since for forces that are all vertical the equations that are not identically satisfied are three and the unknowns four
- Yes, since the table is a rigid body and for it the cardinal equations are sufficient
- No, since the cardinal equations do not apply to systems in space

## Explanation

Take the $z$ axis vertical. All the forces are parallel to $z$, therefore the $x$ and $y$ components of the resultant and the $z$ component of the moment are identically zero, and three equations remain, $R_z = 0$, $M_x = 0$ and $M_y = 0$. The unknowns are the four reactions, hence the table is **statically indeterminate** and the reactions are not determined by the cardinal equations alone. The sufficiency of the cardinal equations for the rigid body guarantees equilibrium and leaves open the uniqueness of the reactions; with three legs the unknowns would be three and the reactions would be determined.
