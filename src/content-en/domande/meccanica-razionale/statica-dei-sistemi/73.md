A homogeneous rod $OA$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $O$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane. A torsional spring acts at the hinge, exerting on the rod a couple of moment $-\,c\,\vartheta$, where $c > 0$ is the spring constant and $\vartheta$ is the angle that $OA$ makes with the upward vertical, measured counterclockwise. Which value of $c$ keeps the rod in equilibrium in the horizontal configuration $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, as in the diagram in the figure?

- $\dfrac{2\,m g \ell}{\pi}$
- $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$
- $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2\pi}$
- $\dfrac{m g \ell}{\pi}$

## Explanation

The moment equation with respect to $O$ does not contain the reaction of the hinge. The center of mass lies at $G - O = -\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore the moment of the weight is $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$, positive because the weight tends to increase $\vartheta$. The equilibrium equation is therefore
$$
-\,c\,\vartheta + \frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta = 0 ,
$$
and for $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ it gives $c\,\dfrac{\pi}{2} = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$, that is $c = \dfrac{m g \ell}{\pi}$. The value $\dfrac{2\,m g \ell}{\pi}$ is obtained by applying the weight at the end $A$ instead of at the midpoint.
