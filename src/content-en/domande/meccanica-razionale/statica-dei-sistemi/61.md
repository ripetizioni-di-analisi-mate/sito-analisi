Two rigid rods $AB$ and $BC$ of negligible mass are hinged to each other at $B$ and have no other constraints; the system lies on a smooth horizontal plane. At the ends $A$ and $C$ two opposite forces of equal magnitude are applied, directed along the line $AC$ and such as to move $A$ away from $C$. In which configurations is the system in equilibrium?

- In every configuration, since the resultant and the resultant moment of the external forces are zero
- In no configuration
- Only in the configurations in which the two rods are perpendicular
- Only in the configurations in which the two rods are aligned

## Explanation

The two forces are opposite and have the same line of action $AC$, therefore the cardinal equations of the whole system are satisfied in every configuration. The articulated system is not a rigid body, hence these equations are necessary and not sufficient, and they must also be written for each rod. The rod $AB$ is subject only to the force applied at $A$ and to the action of the hinge at $B$, therefore the moment equation with respect to $B$ requires that the line of action of the force at $A$ pass through $B$. Since that force is directed along $AC$, the point $B$ lies on the line $AC$ and the two rods are **aligned**.
