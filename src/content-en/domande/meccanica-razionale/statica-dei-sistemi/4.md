A homogeneous rod $OA$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $O$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane, with a smooth hinge. A particle of mass $m_1$ is welded at the end $A$ and at $A$ the horizontal force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, acts as well, as in the diagram in the figure. Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle that $OA$ makes with the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise, which condition identifies the equilibrium configurations?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,2.12,-2.12" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,0 0,-3.2" punti="2.12,-2.12" forze="A,1.1,0,F;A,0,-1,m_1g;1.06 -1.06,0,-1,mg" etichette="0.5,-1.2,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with the particle of mass $m_1$ welded at $A$ and the force $\vec{F}$"}

- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{F}{(m + 2\,m_1)\,g}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{2 F}{(2 m + m_1)\,g}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{2 F}{(m + 2\,m_1)\,g}$
- $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{2 F}{(m + m_1)\,g}$

## Explanation

The rod with the welded particle is a rigid body with one degree of freedom, therefore the moment equation with respect to $O$ determines the equilibrium configurations; the reaction of the hinge does not appear, since its line of action passes through the pole. The weight of the rod acts at the midpoint, at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from $O$, whereas the weight of the welded particle and the force $\vec{F}$ act at $A$, and the moment equation is
$$
-\,\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta - m_1 g \ell \sin\vartheta + F \ell \cos\vartheta = 0 .
$$
Dividing by $\ell\cos\vartheta$ one obtains $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{2 F}{(m + 2\,m_1)\,g}$. The condition with $m + m_1$ in the denominator would be obtained by applying the moment arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ to the weight of the welded particle as well, which however belongs to the weight of the rod alone.
