A particle rests on a rough surface with coefficient of static friction $f = 0.3$. To balance the active forces in the configuration considered, a constraint reaction inclined at $20^\circ$ with respect to the normal to the surface would be needed. What does one conclude?

- The reaction falls inside the friction cone, therefore equilibrium is possible
- The reaction falls outside the friction cone, therefore equilibrium is not possible
- Equilibrium is possible, since the normal component of the reaction is positive
- Equilibrium is possible only if the surface is smooth

## Explanation

The reactions that the rough constraint can exert form the friction cone, of half-opening $\varphi = \arctan f$ around the normal. With $f = 0.3$ one has $\varphi = \arctan 0.3 = 16.70^\circ$, less than $20^\circ$. Equivalently, $\tan 20^\circ = 0.364 > f$, therefore the required tangential component exceeds $f$ times the normal component. The required reaction therefore falls **outside the friction cone** and the configuration is not an equilibrium configuration; the positivity of the normal component is a necessary condition, which alone is not enough.
