A particle $P$ of mass $m$ hangs, in a vertical plane, from two ideal springs of zero natural length. The first, of constant $k$, is fixed at the point $A$; the second, of constant $2k$, is fixed at the point $B$, at the same height as $A$ and at distance $3a$ from it, as in the diagram in the figure. At what distance below the line $AB$ does $P$ lie at equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;P,2,-1.6" linee="-0.6,0 3.6,0;0.00,0.00 0.30,-0.24 0.50,-0.22 0.56,-0.63 0.97,-0.60 1.03,-1.00 1.44,-0.97 1.50,-1.38 1.70,-1.36 2.00,-1.60;3.00,0.00 2.85,-0.24 2.91,-0.41 2.56,-0.45 2.68,-0.78 2.32,-0.82 2.44,-1.15 2.09,-1.19 2.15,-1.36 2.00,-1.60" punti="2,-1.6" forze="P,0,-1,mg" quote="A,B,3a,-0.6" etichette="0.6,-1.15,k;2.95,-0.95,2k" titolo="Particle hanging from the springs of constants $k$ and $2k$ fixed at $A$ and at $B$"}

- $\dfrac{m g}{2k}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{3k}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{k}$
- $\dfrac{2\,m g}{3k}$

## Explanation

The particle is free, therefore at equilibrium the resultant of the forces is zero. Since the natural lengths are zero, the spring fixed at $A$ exerts on $P$ the force $k\,(A - P)$ and the one fixed at $B$ the force $2k\,(B - P)$, hence
$$
k\,(A - P) + 2k\,(B - P) - m g\,\hat{\jmath} = \vec{0} .
$$
Taking the origin at $A$, with $B - A = 3a\,\hat{\imath}$ and $P - A = x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath}$, the vertical component of the equation gives $-\,3k\,y - m g = 0$, therefore $y = -\,\dfrac{m g}{3k}$, and the horizontal component gives $x = 2a$. The particle therefore lies at distance $\dfrac{m g}{3k}$ below the line $AB$, since in the vertical direction the two springs act as a single spring of constant $k + 2k = 3k$.
