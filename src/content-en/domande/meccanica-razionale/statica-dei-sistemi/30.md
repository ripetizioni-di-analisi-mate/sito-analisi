Two particles slide on a smooth horizontal line and are connected by an ideal spring of constant $k$ and natural length $\ell_0$; the force $-\,F\,\hat{\imath}$ is applied to the first and the force $F\,\hat{\imath}$ to the second, with $F > 0$. The cardinal equations of statics turn out to be satisfied for every distance $d$ between the two particles, whereas equilibrium holds only for $d = \ell_0 + \dfrac{F}{k}$. What is the conclusion?

- The cardinal equations are sufficient for this system as well
- The system is in equilibrium for every distance $d$
- The cardinal equations are not necessary for this system
- The cardinal equations are necessary and not sufficient, since the system is not a rigid body

## Explanation

The two applied forces have the same line of action and are opposite, therefore $\vec{R}^{(e)} = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{M}_O^{(e)} = \vec{0}$ whatever the distance between the two particles, and the cardinal equations are satisfied in every configuration. The equilibrium of each particle requires, however, that the elastic force balance the force applied to that particle, a condition satisfied only for $d = \ell_0 + \dfrac{F}{k}$, therefore the cardinal equations are necessary and not sufficient conditions. Sufficiency holds for the rigid body, where the three scalar equations in the plane are as many as the functions that determine the motion.

The system considered has two degrees of freedom and the distance between the particles can vary, therefore rigidity is not satisfied and the sufficiency theorem does not apply.
