A simple pendulum, formed by a particle of mass $m$ hanging from an inextensible string fixed at $O$, oscillates in a vertical plane and passes through the lowest position with nonzero velocity. Is the configuration in which the string is vertical and the particle lies below $O$ an equilibrium configuration?

- Yes, since the particle, placed in that configuration with zero velocity, remains there
- No, since in that configuration the particle has nonzero velocity
- No, since during the passage the tension of the string exceeds the weight
- Yes, but only if the passing velocity is small

## Explanation

A configuration is an equilibrium configuration if the system, placed in it with zero velocities, remains there at every subsequent instant; the definition concerns only the configuration, with zero initial velocities. With the string vertical and the particle at rest below $O$, the tension $T = m g$ balances the weight, therefore the particle remains at rest and the configuration is **an equilibrium configuration**. During the oscillation the tension at the lowest point exceeds $m g$, because the resultant must provide the centripetal acceleration. This fact concerns the motion passing through the configuration, therefore it does not change its nature.
