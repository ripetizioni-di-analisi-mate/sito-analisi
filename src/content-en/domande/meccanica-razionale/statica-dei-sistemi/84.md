A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ rests in a vertical plane with the end $A$ on a horizontal floor and the end $B$ against a vertical wall, both rough with the same coefficient of static friction $f < 1$. The computation gives, for the minimum angle of the rod with the horizontal, the relation $\tan\vartheta_{\min} = \dfrac{1 - f^2}{2f}$. Which check with a limit case is correct?

- For $f \to 0$ one has $\tan\vartheta_{\min} \to +\infty$, therefore with smooth supports equilibrium requires the rod to be vertical, as it must
- For $f \to 0$ one has $\vartheta_{\min} \to 0$, therefore with smooth supports the rod is in equilibrium in every configuration
- For $f = 1$ one has $\vartheta_{\min} = 45^\circ$, as for a particle on an inclined plane with $f = 1$
- For $f \to 0$ one has $\vartheta_{\min} \to 45^\circ$, since the weight is split equally between the two supports

## Explanation

With smooth supports the reactions are vertical at $A$ and horizontal at $B$, and the horizontal component of the first cardinal equation gives zero reaction at $B$. The moment equation with respect to $A$ then requires that the weight have zero lever arm, that is $\cos\vartheta = 0$, and the only equilibrium configuration is the vertical one. The formula must therefore give $\vartheta_{\min} \to 90^\circ$ for $f \to 0$, and indeed $\dfrac{1 - f^2}{2f} \to +\infty$. For $f = 1$ the formula gives $\tan\vartheta_{\min} = 0$, that is $\vartheta_{\min} = 0$. The check with $f \to 0$ is independent of the computation, since the case of smooth supports is solved separately with the cardinal equations.
