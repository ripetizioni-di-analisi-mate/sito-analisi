Two ideal springs of negligible mass, the first of constant $k$ and the second of constant $2k$, are connected in series, with the lower end of the first attached to the upper end of the second. The first spring is fixed to the ceiling and a particle of mass $m$ hangs from the second. At equilibrium, by how much has the total length of the two springs increased with respect to the sum of the natural lengths?

- $\dfrac{3\,m g}{2k}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{3k}$
- $\dfrac{2\,m g}{3k}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{k}$

## Explanation

The junction point of the two springs has zero mass, therefore the balance of forces on it requires that the two springs exert on it forces of equal magnitude. The equilibrium of the hanging particle fixes this magnitude at the value $m g$, hence the first spring elongates by $\dfrac{m g}{k}$ and the second by $\dfrac{m g}{2k}$. The total elongation is
$$
\frac{m g}{k} + \frac{m g}{2k} = \frac{3\,m g}{2k} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{m g}{3k}$ is the elongation of two springs in parallel, which share the load; in series each spring supports the whole weight.
