A spool is formed by two outer disks of radius $R$ and an inner cylinder of radius $r = \dfrac{R}{2}$, rigidly connected and coaxial, and rests with the outer disks on a rough horizontal plane, in a vertical plane; friction is sufficient to prevent sliding. An ideal string wound on the inner cylinder leaves it from the lower side and is pulled with a force $\vec{F}$ inclined at the angle $\beta$ to the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure. For which value of $\beta$ does the spool remain in equilibrium?

- $30^\circ$

## Explanation

The spool is subject to the weight, applied at the center $O$, to the force $\vec{F}$ and to the reaction of the plane at the contact point $C$. The moment equation with respect to $C$ does not contain the reaction of the plane, and the weight has its line of action through $C$, therefore equilibrium requires that the line of action of $\vec{F}$ pass through $C$ as well. The line of the string is tangent to the inner cylinder, hence it lies at distance $r$ from $O$, and the distance of $C$ from the same line is $|R\cos\beta - r|$; it vanishes for
$$
\cos\beta = \frac{r}{R} = \frac{1}{2} ,
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
\beta = 60^\circ .
$$
For smaller angles the spool rolls in the direction of the pull, for larger angles in the opposite direction. The value $30^\circ$ is obtained by writing $\sin\beta = \dfrac{r}{R}$ instead of $\cos\beta = \dfrac{r}{R}$.
