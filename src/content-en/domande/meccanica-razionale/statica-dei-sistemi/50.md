A crate of mass $m = 10\,\mathrm{kg}$, which can be modeled as a particle, rests on a rough horizontal floor with coefficient of static friction $f = 0.5$. A rope pulls the crate with a force $\vec{F}$ inclined at $\beta = 30^\circ$ to the horizontal and directed upward, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the maximum magnitude of $\vec{F}$ for which the crate remains in equilibrium?

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 2,0 2,1.2 0,1.2" linee="-0.8,0 4.4,0" tratteggi="2,0.6 3.6,0.6" forze="3.21 1.30,1.21,0.70,;1 0.6,0,-1,mg" etichette="3.46,1.40,F;3.0,0.82,β;1.2,-0.3,f" titolo="Crate pulled by a rope inclined at $\beta$ on a rough floor"}

- $43.95\,\mathrm{N}$
- $56.64\,\mathrm{N}$
- $79.63\,\mathrm{N}$
- $49.05\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The vertical component of $\vec{F}$ partly lifts the crate, therefore the normal component of the reaction of the floor is $\Phi_n = m g - F\sin\beta$; the horizontal component $F\cos\beta$ must be balanced by friction. The Coulomb-Morin law therefore requires
$$
F\cos\beta \le f\,(m g - F\sin\beta) ,
$$
whence $F \le \dfrac{f\,m g}{\cos\beta + f\sin\beta}$. With the data one obtains $F \le \dfrac{0.5 \cdot 98.1}{0.866 + 0.25}\,\mathrm{N} = 43.95\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $79.63\,\mathrm{N}$ corresponds to a vertical component of $\vec{F}$ that presses on the floor instead of lifting the crate, and the value $49.05\,\mathrm{N}$ neglects the inclination of the rope.
