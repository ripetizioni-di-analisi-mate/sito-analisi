A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ rests in a vertical plane with the end $A$ on a rough horizontal floor, of coefficient of static friction $f = 0.3$, and with the end $B$ on a smooth vertical wall; the rod makes the angle $\vartheta$ with the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure. What is the smallest angle for which equilibrium is possible?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.3,4.4" aste="A-B" linee="-0.9,0 3.4,0;2.3,0 2.3,5" forze="1.15 2.2,0,-1.2,mg" etichette="0.75,0.42,ϑ" titolo="Rod resting on the rough floor at $A$ and on the smooth wall at $B$"}

- $16.70^\circ$
- $30.96^\circ$
- $59.04^\circ$
- $73.30^\circ$

## Explanation

The wall is smooth, therefore at $B$ the horizontal reaction $N_B$ alone acts, whereas at $A$ the normal reaction $N_A$ and the friction component $T_A$ act. Translational equilibrium gives $N_A = m g$ and $T_A = N_B$, and the moment equation with respect to $A$, where $N_B$ has moment arm $\ell\sin\vartheta$ and the weight has moment arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, gives $N_B = \dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$. The Coulomb-Morin law requires $T_A \le f\,N_A$, therefore
$$
\frac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta} \le f\,m g ,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\tan\vartheta \ge \frac{1}{2 f} .
$$
With $f = 0.3$ one obtains $\tan\vartheta \ge \dfrac{5}{3}$, that is, $\vartheta_{\min} = 59.04^\circ$. The angle $73.30^\circ$ would be obtained from the condition $\tan\vartheta \ge \dfrac{1}{f}$, which forgets the factor $2$ carried by the moment arm of the weight.
