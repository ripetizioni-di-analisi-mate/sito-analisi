In the free-body diagram of a rod the horizontal component of the reaction of the hinge at $A$ has been drawn toward the right and denoted by $\Phi_{Ax}$; the cardinal equations give $\Phi_{Ax} = -\,12\,\mathrm{N}$. How is this result read?

- The component is directed toward the left and has magnitude $12\,\mathrm{N}$
- The computation contains an error, since a magnitude cannot be negative
- The hinge exerts no horizontal reaction
- The rod is not in equilibrium in the configuration considered

## Explanation

In the free-body diagram the direction of every reaction is chosen a priori and the symbol denotes the component of the reaction along that direction, not its magnitude. A negative value therefore means that the component is opposite to the direction drawn, so the hinge pulls the rod toward the left with magnitude $12\,\mathrm{N}$. The sign signals no error, since the cardinal equations are written with the component as the unknown and the result remains valid with its own sign.

A negative reaction is to be re-examined instead when the constraint is unilateral, such as a support or a string, because in that case the direction of the reaction is imposed by the constraint.
