A homogeneous rod $OA$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at its lower end $O$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects the end $A$ to the fixed point $D$, lying on the vertical through $O$ at distance $\ell$ above $O$, as in the diagram in the figure. Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle that $OA$ makes with the upward vertical, for which value of $k$ is every configuration of the rod an equilibrium configuration?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,-2.29,3.28;D,0,4" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera" tratteggi="0,0 0,4.6" linee="-2.29,3.28 -1.95,3.39 -1.89,3.56 -1.60,3.34 -1.49,3.69 -1.20,3.46 -1.09,3.81 -0.80,3.59 -0.69,3.94 -0.40,3.72 -0.34,3.89 0.00,4.00" punti="0,4" forze="-1.15 1.64,0,-1,mg" quote="O,D,ℓ,0.7" etichette="-0.42,1.45,ϑ;-0.95,4.05,k" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with the spring between the end $A$ and the fixed point $D$ above $O$"}

- $\dfrac{m g}{\ell}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{4\ell}$
- $\dfrac{m g}{2\ell}$
- $\dfrac{2\,m g}{\ell}$

## Explanation

The moment equation with respect to $O$ does not contain the reaction of the hinge. With $A - O = -\,\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $D - O = \ell\,\hat{\jmath}$, the force of the spring on $A$ is $-\,k\,(A - D)$ and its moment with respect to $O$ is $-\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta$; the weight, applied at the midpoint, has moment $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$. The equilibrium equation is therefore
$$
\left(\frac{m g \ell}{2} - k\,\ell^2\right)\sin\vartheta = 0 .
$$
For $k \neq \dfrac{m g}{2\ell}$ the only equilibrium configurations are $\vartheta = 0$ and $\vartheta = \pi$, while for $k = \dfrac{m g}{2\ell}$ the equation is satisfied for every $\vartheta$, therefore every configuration is an equilibrium configuration. The value $\dfrac{m g}{\ell}$ is obtained by applying the weight at the end $A$.
