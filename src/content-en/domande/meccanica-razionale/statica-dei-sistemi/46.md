A free particle is in equilibrium under the action of three forces. The first two are $\vec{F}_1 = 3\,\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{F}_2 = -\,5\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath}$, with the components expressed in newtons. What is the magnitude of the third force $\vec{F}_3$?

- $10.39\,\mathrm{N}$
- $8.25\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6.32\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.83\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The free particle is in equilibrium if and only if the resultant of the forces is zero, therefore $\vec{F}_3 = -\,(\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2)$. The sum of the first two forces is $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 = -\,2\,\hat{\imath} + 6\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence $\vec{F}_3 = 2\,\hat{\imath} - 6\,\hat{\jmath}$ and
$$
|\vec{F}_3| = \sqrt{2^2 + 6^2}\,\mathrm{N} = \sqrt{40}\,\mathrm{N} = 6.32\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $10.39\,\mathrm{N}$ is the sum of the magnitudes $|\vec{F}_1| + |\vec{F}_2| = (5 + \sqrt{29})\,\mathrm{N}$, which coincides with the magnitude of the resultant only for parallel forces with the same direction; the value $8.25\,\mathrm{N}$ is the magnitude of the difference $\vec{F}_1 - \vec{F}_2$.
