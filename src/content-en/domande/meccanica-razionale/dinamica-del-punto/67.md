A particle of mass $m$ slides along a smooth and fixed horizontal rectilinear guide and is subject to a force with potential energy $V(x) = V_0\left( 1 - \cos\dfrac{x}{a} \right)$, with $V_0 > 0$ and $a > 0$. At the initial instant the particle is at $x = 0$ with velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$, where $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 = 2V_0$. Which statement about the motion is true?

- It reaches $x = \pi a$ in a finite time, with zero velocity, and turns back
- It approaches $x = \pi a$ without reaching it at any finite instant
- It passes $x = \pi a$ and continues indefinitely in the positive direction
- It oscillates periodically between $-\pi a$ and $\pi a$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy is $E = 2V_0$, which is the maximum value of $V$, attained at $x = \pi a$; therefore the particle does not pass $\pi a$, and for $0 \le x < \pi a$ its velocity is positive. Moreover $V'(\pi a) = \dfrac{V_0}{a}\sin\pi = 0$, so $x = \pi a$ is an equilibrium configuration. If the particle arrived there at an instant $t_1$, it would arrive with zero velocity, and rest at $\pi a$ would be another solution with the same data at $t_1$; this contradicts the existence and uniqueness theorem, which applies because $V$ is regular. The particle therefore approaches $\pi a$ as $t$ grows without reaching it, and the motion is not periodic. Near $\pi a$ one has $E - V(x) \approx \dfrac{V_0}{2a^2}\,(\pi a - x)^2$, so the velocity is proportional to the distance from $\pi a$ and the approach is exponential.
