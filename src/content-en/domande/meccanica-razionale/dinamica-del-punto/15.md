A particle of mass $m$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ with law of motion
$$
P(t) - O = b\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + b\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
b > 0 ,
\qquad
\omega > 0 .
$$
Which force acts on the particle?

- $m\,\omega^2\,( P - O )$
- $-m\,\omega\,( P - O )$
- $-m\,\omega^2\,( P - O )$
- $-\dfrac{m\,\omega^2}{b}\,( P - O )$

## Explanation

The motion is assigned and the force is sought, therefore this is the inverse problem, in which one differentiates the law of motion twice and multiplies by the mass. Differentiating one obtains
$$
\vec{a}_P = -b\,\omega^2\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} - b\,\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} = -\omega^2\,( P - O ) .
$$
Therefore the fundamental equation gives $\vec{F} = -m\,\omega^2\,( P - O )$. The force is central and attractive with center $O$, with constant magnitude $m\,\omega^2 b$, as a uniform circular motion of radius $b$ requires.
