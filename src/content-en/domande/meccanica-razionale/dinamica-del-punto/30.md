A point particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed straight guide and is subject to a force with potential energy
$$
V(x) = V_0\,\frac{x^2}{x^2 + a^2} ,
\qquad
V_0 > 0 ,
\qquad
a > 0 .
$$
At the initial instant the particle is at the origin with velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$, where $v_0 > 0$. For which values of $v_0$ does the particle move away from the origin indefinitely?

- $v_0 \geq \sqrt{\dfrac{V_0}{m}}$
- $v_0 \geq \sqrt{\dfrac{2 V_0}{m}}$
- $v_0 \geq \sqrt{\dfrac{V_0}{2m}}$
- For every $v_0 > 0$

## Explanation

Since $V(0) = 0$, the mechanical energy equals $E = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2$. The derivative $V'(x) = \dfrac{2 V_0\,a^2\,x}{(x^2 + a^2)^2}$ is positive for $x > 0$, hence on the half-line $x > 0$ the potential energy increases, stays below $V_0$ and tends to $V_0$ as $x \to +\infty$. If $E \geq V_0$, then $E - V(x) > 0$ for every $x \geq 0$, therefore the velocity never vanishes and the particle proceeds toward $x \to +\infty$. If instead $E < V_0$, there is an abscissa $x_1 > 0$ with $V(x_1) = E$, which is a turning point, and the motion is periodic. The required condition is thus $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 \geq V_0$, that is $v_0 \geq \sqrt{\dfrac{2 V_0}{m}}$; the value $\sqrt{\dfrac{V_0}{m}}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the kinetic energy.
