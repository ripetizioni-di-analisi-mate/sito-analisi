A particle of mass $m$ slides along a smooth and fixed horizontal rectilinear guide, connected to a spring and to a damper, with natural angular frequency $\omega_0$ and damping coefficient $\gamma > 0$. The force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)\,\hat{\imath}$ also acts on the particle, with $\Omega$ much greater than $\omega_0$ and $\gamma$. The steady-state motion is $x_r(t) = A\cos(\Omega t - \delta)$, with $A > 0$ and $0 \le \delta \le \pi$. Which statement about the steady-state motion is true?

- It is nearly in phase with the forcing, with amplitude about $\dfrac{F_0}{m\,\omega_0^2}$
- It is nearly in phase opposition with the forcing, with amplitude about $\dfrac{F_0}{m\,\Omega^2}$
- It is in quadrature with the forcing, with amplitude about $\dfrac{F_0}{2m\,\gamma\,\Omega}$
- It is nearly in phase opposition with the forcing, with amplitude about $\dfrac{F_0}{m\,\omega_0^2}$

## Explanation

The amplitude and the phase of the steady-state motion satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
A\,(\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2) = \dfrac{F_0}{m}\cos\delta \\[6pt]
2\gamma\Omega\,A = \dfrac{F_0}{m}\sin\delta
\end{cases}
$$
For $\Omega > \omega_0$ the left-hand side of the first equation is negative and that of the second is positive, therefore $\cos\delta < 0$ and $\sin\delta > 0$. The ratio $\tan\delta = \dfrac{2\gamma\Omega}{\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2}$ tends to zero as $\Omega$ grows, so $\delta$ tends to $\pi$ and the motion is nearly in phase opposition. Under the square root of $A(\Omega)$ the dominant term is $\Omega^4$, therefore $A \approx \dfrac{F_0}{m\,\Omega^2}$, which is the amplitude of the motion of a free particle subject only to the forcing.
