A particle of mass $m$ travels along a circular orbit of radius $r_0$ around a center of Newtonian attraction, subject to the force $-\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r^2}\,\hat{u}_r$ with $\mu > 0$. With the potential energy equal to zero at infinity, what is the mechanical energy of the particle?

- $-\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r_0}$
- $\dfrac{\mu\,m}{2 r_0}$
- $-\dfrac{\mu\,m}{2 r_0}$
- $-\dfrac{3\mu\,m}{2 r_0}$

## Explanation

On the circular orbit the acceleration is centripetal with magnitude $\dfrac{v^2}{r_0}$, therefore the radial component of the fundamental equation gives $m\,\dfrac{v^2}{r_0} = \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r_0^2}$, that is $v^2 = \dfrac{\mu}{r_0}$. The potential vanishing at infinity is $U = \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$, so the potential energy is $V = -U = -\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r_0}$. Adding the kinetic energy $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 = \dfrac{\mu\,m}{2 r_0}$ one obtains
$$
E = \frac{\mu\,m}{2 r_0} - \frac{\mu\,m}{r_0} = -\frac{\mu\,m}{2 r_0} .
$$
The energy is negative, as in every bounded orbit, and the kinetic energy is half the absolute value of the potential energy.
