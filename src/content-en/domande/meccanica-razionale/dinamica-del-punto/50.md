A particle of mass $m$ rests on the horizontal floor of an elevator that moves downwards while slowing down, with acceleration of magnitude $a$ directed upwards. What is the magnitude of the reaction of the floor on the particle?

- $m g$
- $m\,(g - a)$
- $m a$
- $m\,(g + a)$

## Explanation

The fundamental equation is written in the frame of the Earth, which is assumed to be inertial, with $\hat{\jmath}$ vertical and upwards. The particle is at rest with respect to the floor, therefore it has the same acceleration as the elevator, $a\,\hat{\jmath}$. The weight $-m g\,\hat{\jmath}$ and the reaction $\Phi\,\hat{\jmath}$ act on the particle, so
$$
m\,a = \Phi - m g ,
$$
from which $\Phi = m\,(g + a)$. The reaction exceeds the weight, because the resultant of the forces has the direction of the acceleration, that is upwards.
