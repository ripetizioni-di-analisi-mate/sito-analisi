A particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, and is subject to a force of potential energy
$$
V(x) = c\left( \frac{x^3}{3} - \ell^2\,x \right) ,
\qquad
c > 0 ,
\qquad
\ell > 0 .
$$
What is the angular frequency of the small oscillations about the stable equilibrium configuration?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2 c\,\ell}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{c\,\ell}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{c\,\ell^2}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2 c\,\ell^2}{m}}$

## Explanation

The derivative of the potential energy is $V'(x) = c\,( x^2 - \ell^2 )$, therefore the equilibrium configurations are $x = \ell$ and $x = -\ell$. Since $V''(x) = 2 c\,x$, it turns out that $V''(\ell) = 2 c\,\ell > 0$ and $V''(-\ell) = -2 c\,\ell < 0$, therefore $x = \ell$ is a point of minimum of $V$ and is the stable equilibrium configuration. Setting $x = \ell + \xi$ and expanding $V$ to the second order, the linearized equation of motion is $m\,\ddot{\xi} + V''(\ell)\,\xi = 0$, hence the angular frequency equals
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{V''(\ell)}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 c\,\ell}{m}} .
$$
