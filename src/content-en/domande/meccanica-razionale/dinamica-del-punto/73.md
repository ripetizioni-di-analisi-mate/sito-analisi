A small ring of mass $m$ is threaded on a smooth and fixed helix with vertical axis, of radius $R$ and pitch $p$, that is with equations $x = R\cos\varphi$, $y = R\sin\varphi$, $z = \dfrac{p\,\varphi}{2\pi}$, and is subject to its weight. What is the magnitude of the tangential component of its acceleration?

- $\dfrac{g\,p}{\sqrt{p^2 + 4\pi^2R^2}}$
- $\dfrac{g\,p}{\sqrt{p^2 + R^2}}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi g\,R}{\sqrt{p^2 + 4\pi^2R^2}}$
- $g$

## Explanation

The guide is smooth, therefore the first intrinsic equation is $m\,\ddot{s} = \vec{F} \cdot \hat{t}$ and the reaction does not appear in it. The tangent unit vector is parallel to $\dfrac{dP}{d\varphi} = -R\sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + R\cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} + \dfrac{p}{2\pi}\,\hat{k}$, whose magnitude is $\sqrt{R^2 + \dfrac{p^2}{4\pi^2}}$, so the vertical component of $\hat{t}$ is
$$
\hat{t} \cdot \hat{k} = \frac{p}{\sqrt{p^2 + 4\pi^2R^2}} .
$$
The weight is $-m g\,\hat{k}$, therefore $|\ddot{s}| = \dfrac{g\,p}{\sqrt{p^2 + 4\pi^2R^2}}$, which is constant. The motion along the helix is thus uniformly accelerated, as along an inclined plane forming with the horizontal the angle $\beta$ with $\tan\beta = \dfrac{p}{2\pi R}$.
