A particle of mass $m$ travels with velocity of constant magnitude $v$ along the smooth and fixed circular guide of radius $R$ in the figure, placed in a horizontal plane and seen from above. What can be said about the reaction of the guide?

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" nodi="C,0,0;P,0,-2" punti="0,0;0,-2" frecce="0.25,-2,1.75,-2,v" quote="C,P,R,-0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Top view of the smooth circular guide of radius $R$, travelled with velocity of constant magnitude $v$"}

- It has horizontal component $\dfrac{m\,v^2}{R}$ directed outwards
- It has zero horizontal component, because the magnitude of the velocity is constant
- It has horizontal component $\dfrac{m\,v^2}{R}$ directed towards the center
- It has tangential component of magnitude $\dfrac{m\,v^2}{R}$

## Explanation

The motion is uniform circular, therefore the acceleration has the normal component alone, of magnitude $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$ and directed from the particle towards the center $C$. In the horizontal plane the weight has no components, therefore the second intrinsic equation gives
$$
m\,\frac{v^2}{R} = \vec{\Phi} \cdot \hat{n} ,
$$
where $\hat{n}$ is the principal normal unit vector, directed towards $C$. It follows that the reaction has horizontal component of magnitude $\dfrac{m\,v^2}{R}$ directed towards the center; the tangential component is zero, since the guide is smooth, and the vertical component balances the weight.
