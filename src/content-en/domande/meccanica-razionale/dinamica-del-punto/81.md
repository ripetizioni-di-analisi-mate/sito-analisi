A particle travels along an elliptic orbit with polar equation $r = \dfrac{p}{1 + e\cos\vartheta}$ around a center of Newtonian attraction placed at the focus $O$. The minimum and maximum distances from $O$ are $r_p$ and $r_a$. What is the eccentricity $e$?

- $\dfrac{r_a - r_p}{r_a}$
- $\dfrac{r_p}{r_a}$
- $\dfrac{r_a - r_p}{2\,(r_a + r_p)}$
- $\dfrac{r_a - r_p}{r_a + r_p}$

## Explanation

The distance is minimum for $\cos\vartheta = 1$ and maximum for $\cos\vartheta = -1$, therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
r_p = \dfrac{p}{1 + e} \\[6pt]
r_a = \dfrac{p}{1 - e}
\end{cases}
$$
Eliminating $p$, that is equating $r_p\,(1 + e)$ and $r_a\,(1 - e)$, one obtains $e\,(r_a + r_p) = r_a - r_p$, so $e = \dfrac{r_a - r_p}{r_a + r_p}$. For $r_a = r_p$ the eccentricity is zero and the orbit is a circle. From the same relations it also follows that $p = \dfrac{2\,r_a\,r_p}{r_a + r_p}$.
