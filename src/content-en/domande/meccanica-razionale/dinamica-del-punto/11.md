A particle of mass $m$ hangs at $O$ from an inextensible string of negligible mass and length $\ell$, and moves in the vertical plane. The particle starts from the lowest position with horizontal velocity of magnitude $v_0$, with $v_0^2 < 2 g \ell$, and the string remains taut. What is the tension of the string in the lowest position?

- $m g$
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{\ell}$
- $m\left( g + \dfrac{v_0^2}{\ell} \right)$
- $m\left( g + \dfrac{v_0^2}{2\ell} \right)$

## Explanation

In the lowest position the particle travels along the circle of radius $\ell$ with velocity of magnitude $v_0$, therefore the acceleration has the normal component alone, of magnitude $\dfrac{v_0^2}{\ell}$ and directed from $P$ towards $O$, that is, upwards. Along that direction there act the tension $\Phi$, directed towards $O$, and the weight, directed downwards, therefore the second intrinsic equation gives
$$
m\,\frac{v_0^2}{\ell} = \Phi - m g .
$$
It follows that $\Phi = m\left( g + \dfrac{v_0^2}{\ell} \right)$, larger than the weight because the string must also bend the trajectory.
