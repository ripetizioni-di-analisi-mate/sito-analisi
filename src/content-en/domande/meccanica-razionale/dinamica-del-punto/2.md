A particle of mass $m$, at rest at $O$ at the instant $t = 0$, slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, and is subject to the force $F_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F_0 > 0$ and $\tau > 0$, besides the weight. To which value does the velocity tend as $t \to +\infty$?

- $\dfrac{F_0}{m\,\tau}$
- $\dfrac{F_0\,\tau}{m}$
- $\dfrac{F_0\,\tau}{2m}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The guide is horizontal and smooth, therefore along the $x$ axis the assigned force alone acts and the fundamental equation is $m\,\ddot{x} = F_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$. Integrating between $0$ and $t$ with $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ one obtains
$$
\dot{x}(t) = \frac{F_0\,\tau}{m}\left( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \right) .
$$
Since $e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \to 0$ as $t \to +\infty$, the velocity tends to $\dfrac{F_0\,\tau}{m}$. The force vanishes only in the limit, therefore the acceleration tends to zero and the velocity settles at a finite value, equal to the total impulse of the force divided by the mass.
