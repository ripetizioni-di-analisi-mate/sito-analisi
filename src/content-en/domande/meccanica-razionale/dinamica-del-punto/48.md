A particle is constrained to the surface of a smooth and fixed sphere and is subject to its weight. How many real numbers are needed as initial data to determine its motion?

- $4$
- $6$
- $3$
- $2$

## Explanation

The position of a particle on the sphere is identified by two coordinates, for instance latitude and longitude, and the velocity, which is tangent to the sphere, by two components. The reaction of the smooth constraint is orthogonal to the sphere, therefore the components of the fundamental equation tangent to the sphere do not contain it and form two second order differential equations in the two coordinates. Hence the motion is determined by the values of the two coordinates and of their derivatives at the initial instant, that is by $4$ numbers. Compared with the $6$ data of the free particle two are missing, because the constraint fixes the distance from the center and makes the radial component of the velocity vanish.
