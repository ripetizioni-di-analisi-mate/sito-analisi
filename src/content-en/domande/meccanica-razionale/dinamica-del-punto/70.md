A small ring of mass $m$ is threaded on the smooth and fixed circular guide of radius $R$ in the figure, placed in a vertical plane. At a certain instant the ring passes through the endpoint $P$ of the horizontal diameter with velocity of magnitude $v$. What is the reaction of the guide at that instant?

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" nodi="C,0,0;P,2,0" punti="0,0;2,0" tratteggi="-2,0 2,0" frecce="2.45,-0.7,2.45,0.7,v" quote="C,P,R,-0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Ring on a vertical circular guide at the endpoint $P$ of the horizontal diameter"}

- $m g$, vertical and directed upwards
- $\dfrac{m\,v^2}{R} + m g$, directed towards the center
- $\dfrac{m\,v^2}{R}$, horizontal and directed towards the center
- $\dfrac{m\,v^2}{R}$, horizontal and directed outwards

## Explanation

At $P$ the tangent to the guide is vertical, therefore the weight is entirely tangential and has zero component along the principal normal, which is horizontal and directed from $P$ towards the center $C$. The guide is smooth, so the reaction is orthogonal to the guide; its binormal component is zero, because the weight lies in the plane of the guide. The second intrinsic equation then gives
$$
m\,\frac{v^2}{R} = \vec{\Phi} \cdot \hat{n} ,
$$
therefore the reaction has magnitude $\dfrac{m\,v^2}{R}$, is horizontal and is directed towards the center. At that instant the weight determines only the tangential component of the acceleration, whose magnitude is $g$.
