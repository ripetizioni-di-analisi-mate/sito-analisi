A particle of mass $m$ moves with a central motion with center $O$, with angular momentum $\vec{K}_O \neq \vec{0}$. Which statement is true?

- The motion takes place in the plane through $O$ orthogonal to $\vec{K}_O$
- The motion takes place in the plane through $O$ that contains $\vec{K}_O$
- The magnitude of the velocity is constant
- The trajectory is necessarily a conic

## Explanation

In a central motion the force is parallel to $P - O$, therefore the moment of the force with respect to $O$ is zero and $\vec{K}_O$ is constant. From the definition $\vec{K}_O = ( P - O ) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_P$ it follows that $( P - O ) \cdot \vec{K}_O = 0$ at every instant, therefore $P$ always belongs to the plane through $O$ orthogonal to the constant vector $\vec{K}_O$. The magnitude of the velocity varies in general, since the force has a non-zero tangential component outside the circular orbits, and the trajectory is a conic only for the Newtonian force.
