A particle of mass $m$ slides along a smooth and fixed horizontal rectilinear guide and is subject to a force with potential energy $V(x) = c\,|x|^3$, with $c > 0$. The particle oscillates between the turning points $-A$ and $A$. How does the period $T$ depend on the amplitude $A$?

- It does not depend on $A$
- It is proportional to $\sqrt{A}$
- It is proportional to $A$
- It is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{A}$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy is $E = V(A) = c\,A^3$, therefore the formula for the period of a one-dimensional motion gives
$$
T = \sqrt{2m}\int_{-A}^{A} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{c\,(A^3 - |x|^3)}} .
$$
With the substitution $x = A\,u$ one has $dx = A\,du$ and $A^3 - |x|^3 = A^3\,(1 - |u|^3)$, so
$$
T = \sqrt{\frac{2m}{c}}\,\frac{A}{A^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{du}{\sqrt{1 - |u|^3}} = \frac{K}{\sqrt{A}} ,
$$
where the integral is finite and $K$ does not depend on $A$. The period is therefore inversely proportional to $\sqrt{A}$, and wide oscillations are faster than small ones. For $V = c\,|x|^n$ the same substitution gives the factor $A^{1 - \frac{n}{2}}$, which is constant only for $n = 2$, that is for the harmonic oscillator.
