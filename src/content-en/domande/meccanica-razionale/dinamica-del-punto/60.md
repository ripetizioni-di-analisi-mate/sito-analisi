A particle of mass $m$ is launched vertically upwards with velocity of magnitude $v_0$ and is subject only to its weight. What is the power of the weight at the instant $t$, before the particle returns to its starting height?

- $-m g\,(v_0 - g t)$
- $m g\,(v_0 - g t)$
- $-m g\,v_0$
- $-m g^2\,t$

## Explanation

With the $y$ axis upwards the acceleration is constant and equals $-g\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore the velocity of the particle is $\vec{v}_P = (v_0 - g t)\,\hat{\jmath}$. The power of the weight is the scalar product of the force and the velocity, so
$$
\Pi = -m g\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot (v_0 - g t)\,\hat{\jmath} = -m g\,(v_0 - g t) .
$$
The power is negative during the ascent, vanishes at the highest point, at the instant $\dfrac{v_0}{g}$, and becomes positive during the descent. By the kinetic energy theorem $\dfrac{dT}{dt} = \Pi$, therefore the kinetic energy decreases during the ascent and increases during the descent.
