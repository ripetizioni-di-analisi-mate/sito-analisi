A particle of mass $m$ moves with a central motion with center $O$. At the initial instant it is at the distance $r_0$ from $O$ with velocity of magnitude $v_0$ orthogonal to the position vector. What is the areal constant $c = r^2\,\dot{\vartheta}$?

- $\dfrac{r_0\,v_0}{2}$
- $r_0\,v_0$
- $m\,r_0\,v_0$
- $r_0^2\,v_0$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates the velocity is $\vec{v}_P = \dot{r}\,\hat{u}_r + r\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{u}_\vartheta$, therefore a velocity orthogonal to the position vector has $\dot{r} = 0$ and magnitude $r\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$. At the initial instant it then turns out that $r_0\,\dot{\vartheta}_0 = v_0$, therefore
$$
c = r_0^2\,\dot{\vartheta}_0 = r_0\,v_0 .
$$
The quantity $c$ remains constant during the motion, since the angular momentum $\vec{K}_O$ is constant in a central motion and its magnitude equals $m\,|c|$; the areal velocity is half of $c$.
