A simple pendulum of length $\ell$ hangs from the ceiling of a railway car that moves on a straight horizontal track with constant acceleration of magnitude $a = \dfrac{3}{4}\,g$. What is the period of the small oscillations of the pendulum, in the vertical plane containing the track, about its position of relative equilibrium?

- $4\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{5g}}$
- $4\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{7g}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$
- $4\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{3g}}$

## Explanation

In the frame of the car the weight, the tension of the string and the transport force $-m\,\vec{a}$, constant and horizontal, act on the particle; the Coriolis force is zero, because the car does not rotate. The weight and the transport force add up to the constant force $m\,\vec{g}_{\text{eff}}$, with $\vec{g}_{\text{eff}} = \vec{g} - \vec{a}$, whose magnitude is
$$
g_{\text{eff}} = \sqrt{g^2 + a^2} = \sqrt{g^2 + \frac{9}{16}\,g^2} = \frac{5}{4}\,g .
$$
The equations of the relative motion therefore coincide with those of a simple pendulum in a uniform weight field $\vec{g}_{\text{eff}}$, with the equilibrium position along the direction of $\vec{g}_{\text{eff}}$. The period of the small oscillations is thus
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g_{\text{eff}}}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{4\ell}{5g}} = 4\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{5g}} .
$$
