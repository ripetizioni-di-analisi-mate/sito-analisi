A particle is subject to the repulsive central force $\vec{F} = \dfrac{\alpha}{r^3}\,\hat{u}_r$, with $\alpha > 0$, where $r$ is the distance from the center $O$. With the convention $\vec{F} = \operatorname{grad} U$, which function is the potential of the force?

- $\dfrac{\alpha}{2r^2}$
- $-\dfrac{\alpha}{r^2}$
- $-\dfrac{3\alpha}{r^4}$
- $-\dfrac{\alpha}{2r^2}$

## Explanation

For a function $U(r)$ that depends only on the distance from $O$ the gradient is $U'(r)\,\hat{u}_r$, therefore the condition $\operatorname{grad} U = \vec{F}$ is equivalent to $U'(r) = \dfrac{\alpha}{r^3}$. An antiderivative of $\dfrac{\alpha}{r^3}$ is $-\dfrac{\alpha}{2r^2}$, so $U = -\dfrac{\alpha}{2r^2}$, up to an additive constant. The potential energy is $V = -U = \dfrac{\alpha}{2r^2}$, which decreases moving away from $O$, in agreement with the fact that the force is repulsive.
