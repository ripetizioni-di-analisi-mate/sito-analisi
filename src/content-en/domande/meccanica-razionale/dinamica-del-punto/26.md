A particle of mass $m$ is at rest at the origin and slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, subject to the force
$$
F_0\left( 1 - \frac{x}{d} \right)\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
F_0 > 0 ,
\qquad
d > 0 ,
$$
besides the weight. With which velocity does it arrive at the abscissa $x = d$?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2 F_0\,d}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{F_0\,d}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{F_0\,d}{2m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{F_0\,d}{4m}}$

## Explanation

The guide is horizontal and smooth, therefore the weight and the reaction of the guide do no work and the kinetic energy theorem applies with the work of the assigned force alone. That work equals
$$
L = \int_0^d F_0\left( 1 - \frac{x}{d} \right) dx = F_0\,d - \frac{F_0\,d}{2} = \frac{F_0\,d}{2} .
$$
Since the particle starts from rest, it turns out that $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 = \dfrac{F_0\,d}{2}$, therefore $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{F_0\,d}{m}}$. The force decreases linearly and vanishes exactly at $x = d$, where the velocity therefore reaches its maximum value.
