At the North Pole a particle of mass $m$ moves on a smooth horizontal plane with velocity of magnitude $v$ with respect to the Earth, which rotates with angular velocity $\omega_T$. Which statement about the Coriolis force acting on the particle in the terrestrial frame is true?

- It is zero, because the velocity is horizontal
- It has magnitude $2m\,\omega_T\,v$, is horizontal and points to the right with respect to the direction of motion
- It has magnitude $2m\,\omega_T\,v$, is horizontal and points to the left with respect to the direction of motion
- It has magnitude $m\,\omega_T\,v$ and is vertical

## Explanation

At the North Pole the angular velocity of the Earth $\vec{\omega}_T$ is vertical and directed upwards, therefore it is orthogonal to the relative velocity $\vec{v}_r$, which is horizontal. The Coriolis force $-2m\,\vec{\omega}_T \wedge \vec{v}_r$ thus has magnitude $2m\,\omega_T\,v$ and is horizontal. One chooses $\hat{\imath}$ in the direction of motion, $\hat{k}$ upwards and $\hat{\jmath} = \hat{k} \wedge \hat{\imath}$, which points to the left with respect to the motion; then $\vec{\omega}_T \wedge \vec{v}_r = \omega_T\,v\,\hat{k} \wedge \hat{\imath} = \omega_T\,v\,\hat{\jmath}$. The force is therefore $-2m\,\omega_T\,v\,\hat{\jmath}$ and points to the right, so at the North Pole the relative trajectory of the particle bends to the right.
