A particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, and is subject to a force of potential energy $V(x) = c\,x^3$, with $c > 0$. The origin is an equilibrium configuration; of which nature is it?

- Unstable, because $x = 0$ is not a point of minimum of $V$
- Stable, because $x = 0$ is a stationary point of $V$
- Stable, because $V''(0) = 0$
- It cannot be established, because $V''(0) = 0$

## Explanation

The derivative is $V'(x) = 3 c\,x^2$, therefore $V'(0) = 0$ and the origin is an equilibrium configuration. The second derivative is $V''(x) = 6 c\,x$ and vanishes at the origin, therefore the nature of the equilibrium is established by studying directly the sign of $V$. Since $V(x) < 0$ for $x < 0$ and $V(x) > 0$ for $x > 0$, the origin is not a point of minimum. The force equals $-V'(x) = -3 c\,x^2$ and is directed towards the negative abscissae for every $x \neq 0$, therefore a particle displaced slightly from the origin moves away indefinitely and the equilibrium is unstable.
