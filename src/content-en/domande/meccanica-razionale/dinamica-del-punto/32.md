A point particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed straight guide and is subject to a force with potential energy
$$
V(x) = V_0\,\sin\frac{x}{a} ,
\qquad
V_0 > 0 ,
\qquad
a > 0 .
$$
The particle is released from rest at the origin. What is its acceleration at the initial instant?

- $\dfrac{V_0}{m\,a}\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\vec{0}$
- $-\,\dfrac{V_0}{a}\,\hat{\imath}$
- $-\,\dfrac{V_0}{m\,a}\,\hat{\imath}$

## Explanation

The force is related to the potential energy by $\vec{F} = -\,V'(x)\,\hat{\imath}$, and the derivative equals $V'(x) = \dfrac{V_0}{a}\cos\dfrac{x}{a}$. At the origin one hence has $\vec{F} = -\,\dfrac{V_0}{a}\,\hat{\imath}$, which is nonzero, therefore the origin is not an equilibrium configuration even though $V(0) = 0$. By Newton's second law the initial acceleration equals $\dfrac{\vec{F}}{m} = -\,\dfrac{V_0}{m\,a}\,\hat{\imath}$, directed toward negative abscissae, where the potential energy decreases. The value $\vec{0}$ comes from confusing $V(0) = 0$ with $V'(0) = 0$, while $-\,\dfrac{V_0}{a}\,\hat{\imath}$ is the force not divided by the mass.
