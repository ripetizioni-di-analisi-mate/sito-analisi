A particle of mass $m$ slides along the half-line $x > 0$ of a smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, and is subject to a force of potential energy
$$
V(x) = \frac{a}{x^2} + b\,x ,
\qquad
a > 0 ,
\qquad
b > 0 .
$$
At which abscissa is the equilibrium configuration?

- $\left( \dfrac{a}{b} \right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$
- $\left( \dfrac{a}{2b} \right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$
- $\left( \dfrac{2a}{b} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}}$
- $\left( \dfrac{2a}{b} \right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$

## Explanation

The equilibrium configuration makes the derivative of the potential energy vanish, therefore one solves
$$
V'(x) = -\frac{2a}{x^3} + b = 0 .
$$
Multiplying by $x^3$, which is positive on the half-line, one obtains $b\,x^3 = 2a$, therefore $x_e = \left( \dfrac{2a}{b} \right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$. Since $V''(x) = \dfrac{6a}{x^4} > 0$ for every $x > 0$, the potential energy is convex and the equilibrium found is stable.
