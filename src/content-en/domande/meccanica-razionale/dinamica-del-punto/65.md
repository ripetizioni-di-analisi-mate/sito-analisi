Which of the following positional forces, defined in the whole plane $Oxy$ with $c > 0$ constant, is conservative?

- $c\,(y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $c\,(-y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $c\,(y\,\hat{\imath} - x\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $c\,(x\,y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$

## Explanation

The force $c\,(y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$ is conservative, because it is the gradient of $U = c\,x\,y$, whose partial derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial x} = c\,y$ and $\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial y} = c\,x$. If $F_x\,\hat{\imath} + F_y\,\hat{\jmath}$ is the gradient of a function of class $C^2$, the mixed second derivatives of that function coincide, therefore $\dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = \dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial x}$ must hold. For $c\,(-y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$ the two derivatives are $-c$ and $c$, for $c\,(y\,\hat{\imath} - x\,\hat{\jmath})$ they are $c$ and $-c$, for $c\,(x\,y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$ they are $c\,x$ and $c$; hence the other three forces are not conservative. In agreement with this conclusion, the work of $c\,(-y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$ along the circle of radius $R$ and center $O$, traveled counterclockwise, equals $2\pi c\,R^2 \neq 0$.
