A particle of mass $m$ is threaded on a smooth and fixed horizontal straight guide, and is pulled by a constant force $\vec{F}$ of magnitude $F$, which forms the angle $\beta$ with the guide and points upward, as in the figure. What is the reaction of the guide?

::struttura{linee="-1,0 5.5,0" punti="2,0,P" forze="3.3 0.75,1.3,0.75," etichette="3.5,0.95,F;2.75,0.18,β" titolo="Particle threaded on a smooth horizontal guide and pulled by the force $\vec{F}$ inclined at the angle $\beta$"}

- $m g\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $(m g - F\sin\beta)\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $(m g + F\sin\beta)\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $(m g - F\cos\beta)\,\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

The particle stays on the horizontal guide, therefore its acceleration is parallel to $\hat{\imath}$. The guide is smooth, so the reaction is orthogonal to the guide, and it has no component along $\hat{k}$ because the given forces lie in the vertical plane $Oxy$; hence $\vec{\Phi} = \Phi\,\hat{\jmath}$. The vertical component of the fundamental equation gives
$$
0 = F\sin\beta - m g + \Phi ,
$$
hence $\vec{\Phi} = (m g - F\sin\beta)\,\hat{\jmath}$. The horizontal component gives the acceleration $\dfrac{F\cos\beta}{m}$. If $F\sin\beta > m g$ the reaction points downward, and the guide can exert it because the particle is threaded on it.
