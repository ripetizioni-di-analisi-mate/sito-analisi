A particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, subject to the elastic force $-k\,x\,\hat{\imath}$, with $k > 0$, and oscillates with amplitude $A$. If the amplitude is doubled, how does the period of the oscillations change?

- It stays the same
- It doubles
- It halves
- It becomes $\sqrt{2}$ times larger

## Explanation

The force is proportional to the displacement, therefore the equation of motion is $\ddot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = 0$ with $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$, and the motion is harmonic with period $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega_0}$. In the angular frequency there appear only the mass and the elastic constant, while the initial data determine the amplitude and the phase. Therefore the period equals $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$ whatever the amplitude is. Doubling the amplitude doubles instead the maximum velocity, which equals $\omega_0\,A$.
