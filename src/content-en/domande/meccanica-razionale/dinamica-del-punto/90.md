A smooth straight tube, of negligible cross-section, has one end at the point $O$ of a vertical axis and forms with the axis the constant angle $\alpha$, with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, rising from $O$; the tube rotates about the axis with constant angular velocity $\omega$, as in the figure. A particle of mass $m$, subject to its weight, slides in the tube, and $s$ is its distance from $O$. Which configuration of relative equilibrium does the particle have, and of what nature is it?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,1.49,2.13" linee="0,0 2.41,3.44" tratteggi="0,-0.4 0,4.2" punti="1.49,2.13" coppie="0 3.8,1,ω" quote="O,P,s,0.5" etichette="0.3,1.15,α" riferimento=0 titolo="Smooth tube inclined by the angle $\alpha$ to the vertical axis, rotating with angular velocity $\omega$"}

- $s = \dfrac{g\cos\alpha}{\omega^2\sin^2\alpha}$, stable
- $s = \dfrac{g\cos\alpha}{\omega^2\sin\alpha}$, unstable
- $s = \dfrac{g\cos\alpha}{\omega^2\sin^2\alpha}$, unstable
- $s = \dfrac{g}{\omega^2\cos\alpha}$, stable

## Explanation

In the frame attached to the tube the weight, the centrifugal force, the Coriolis force and the reaction of the tube act; the Coriolis force is orthogonal to the relative velocity, hence to the tube, and together with the reaction it does not appear in the equation of motion along the tube. The height of the particle is $s\cos\alpha$ and its distance from the axis is $s\sin\alpha$, therefore the potential of the weight and of the centrifugal force is
$$
U(s) = -m g\,s\cos\alpha + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2 s^2\sin^2\alpha .
$$
Relative equilibrium makes $U'(s) = -m g\cos\alpha + m\,\omega^2 s\sin^2\alpha$ vanish, so $s_e = \dfrac{g\cos\alpha}{\omega^2\sin^2\alpha}$. Since $U''(s) = m\,\omega^2\sin^2\alpha > 0$, the configuration is a minimum point of $U$, that is a maximum point of the potential energy $V = -U$, therefore the equilibrium is unstable. If the particle is displaced outwards, the component of the centrifugal force along the tube exceeds that of the weight, which is constant, and the particle moves further away.
