A bead of mass $m$ is threaded on a smooth and fixed circular guide, placed in a vertical plane, and moves on it subject to the weight. Is the mechanical energy conserved?

- No, because the reaction of the guide does work
- Yes, but only on the stretch along which the bead descends
- No, because the trajectory is curved
- Yes, because the reaction of the guide is orthogonal to the velocity

## Explanation

The weight is conservative with potential $U = -m g\,y$, therefore the mechanical energy can change only through the work of the constraint reaction. The guide is smooth and fixed, therefore the reaction is orthogonal to the guide, while the velocity of the bead is tangent to it; it follows that $\vec{\Phi} \cdot \vec{v}_P = 0$. The hypothesis of the theorem of conservation of mechanical energy is hence satisfied and the sum $T + V$ remains constant throughout the motion.
