At the equator a particle is dropped from rest, relative to the Earth, from the top of a tower of height $h_0 = 125\,\mathrm{m}$. Assume $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the angular velocity of the Earth $\omega_T = 7.29 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and neglect air resistance. Where does the particle hit the ground, relative to the foot of the vertical through the starting point?

- $3.07\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the west
- $1.53\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the east
- $9.20\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the east
- $3.07\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the east

## Explanation

In the terrestrial frame the centrifugal force is already included in the value of $g$, and the falling particle is also acted on by the Coriolis force $-2m\,\vec{\omega}_T \wedge \vec{v}_r$. At the equator $\vec{\omega}_T$ is horizontal and points north, while $\vec{v}_r$ is vertical and points downward, so $\vec{\omega}_T \wedge \vec{v}_r$ points west and the Coriolis force points east. To first order in $\omega_T$ one can set $v_r = g\,t$, therefore the eastward acceleration is $2\,\omega_T\,g\,t$ and the eastward displacement at time $t$ is $\dfrac{\omega_T\,g\,t^3}{3}$. The fall time is $t_c = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 h_0}{g}} = 5.05\,\mathrm{s}$, hence
$$
d = \frac{\omega_T\,g\,t_c^3}{3} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\,\omega_T\sqrt{\frac{h_0^3}{g}} = 3.07 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
that is, the particle hits the ground $3.07\,\mathrm{cm}$ east of the foot of the vertical.
