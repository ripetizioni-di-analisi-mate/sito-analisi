A particle of mass $m$ slides from rest along a smooth and fixed inclined plane, which makes the angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal, and descends through the drop in height $h$. What is the magnitude of the velocity with which it arrives at the bottom?

- $\sqrt{2 g h}$, whatever $\alpha$ is
- $\sqrt{2 g h\sin\alpha}$
- $\sqrt{2 g h}\,\sin\alpha$
- $\sqrt{2 g h\cos\alpha}$

## Explanation

The plane is smooth and fixed, therefore the reaction is normal to the plane and has zero power, hence the mechanical energy is conserved. The weight is conservative with potential $U = -m g\,y$, therefore the work done during the descent equals $m g h$ and depends only on the drop in height. Since the particle starts from rest, it turns out that $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 = m g h$, therefore $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$. The inclination determines the acceleration, which equals $g\sin\alpha$, and with it the time of descent, while the final velocity stays the same.
