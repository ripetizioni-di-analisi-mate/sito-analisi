A particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, and is subject to a force of potential energy
$$
V(x) = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 - F_0\,x ,
\qquad
k > 0 ,
\qquad
F_0 > 0 .
$$
At the initial instant the particle is at rest at $x = 0$. Which are the turning points of the motion?

- $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{F_0}{k}$
- $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{2F_0}{k}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{F_0}{k}$
- $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{F_0}{2k}$

## Explanation

The particle starts from rest at $x = 0$, therefore the mechanical energy equals $E = V(0) = 0$ and the energy relation is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 = -V(x)$. The turning points are the solutions of $V(x) = 0$ at which $V'$ is different from zero; the equation
$$
x\left( \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x - F_0 \right) = 0
$$
gives $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{2F_0}{k}$, and at both of them $V'(x) = k\,x - F_0$ does not vanish. The motion is therefore periodic between these two abscissae, with the equilibrium configuration $x = \dfrac{F_0}{k}$ at the midpoint, where the velocity is maximum.
