An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero rest length has one end fixed at $O$ and the other attached to a particle. How much work does the elastic force do when the particle passes from the distance $\ell$ from $O$ to the distance $2\ell$?

- $-\dfrac{3}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$
- $-3\,k\,\ell^2$

## Explanation

The elastic force is conservative and, with zero rest length, has potential $U = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2$, where $r$ is the distance of the particle from $O$. The work then depends only on the endpoints and equals $L = U_{\text{final}} - U_{\text{initial}}$, therefore
$$
L = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,( 2\ell )^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2 = -2 k\,\ell^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2 .
$$
It follows that $L = -\dfrac{3}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$. The work is negative because the spring stretches, while the elastic force is directed towards $O$, that is, in the direction opposite to the displacement.
