A cart, modeled as a particle of mass $m$, starts from rest on a smooth and fixed horizontal straight track. A motor applies to it a force directed along the track, with constant power $\Pi_0$; resistances are negligible. What is the magnitude of the velocity of the cart at time $t$?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{\Pi_0\,t}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{\Pi_0\,t}{2m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\Pi_0\,t}{m}}$
- $2\sqrt{\dfrac{\Pi_0\,t}{m}}$

## Explanation

The weight and the reaction of the track are vertical, while the velocity is horizontal, therefore their power is zero. By the kinetic energy theorem one then has $\dfrac{dT}{dt} = \Pi_0$, and since the cart starts from rest, integrating between $0$ and $t$ gives $T(t) = \Pi_0\,t$. Equating $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2$ to $\Pi_0\,t$ one obtains
$$
v(t) = \sqrt{\frac{2\Pi_0\,t}{m}} .
$$
The force of the motor equals $\dfrac{\Pi_0}{v}$, therefore it decreases as the velocity grows, and the velocity grows as $\sqrt{t}$ rather than linearly.
