A particle of mass $m$ is launched with velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$ along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, and is immersed in a fluid that exerts the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}_P$, with $h > 0$; besides it, the weight alone acts. What distance does it travel in all?

- $\dfrac{m\,v_0}{h}$
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0}{2h}$
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{2h}$
- $\dfrac{h\,v_0}{m}$

## Explanation

Along the guide the viscous resistance alone acts, therefore the equation of motion is $m\,\dot{v} = -h\,v$ and the velocity equals $v(t) = v_0\,e^{-\frac{h}{m}t}$. The velocity does not vanish at any finite instant, therefore the distance travelled is obtained by integrating over the whole half-line of positive times:
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} v_0\,e^{-\frac{h}{m}t}\,dt = \frac{m\,v_0}{h} .
$$
The particle therefore travels a finite distance, although it remains in motion at every instant.
