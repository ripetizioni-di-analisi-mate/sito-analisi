A particle of mass $m$, subject to the elastic force $-k\,x\,\hat{\imath}$ along a smooth and fixed horizontal straight guide, oscillates with amplitude $A$ about the origin. At which positions is its kinetic energy three times the elastic potential energy?

- $x = \pm\dfrac{A}{2}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,A$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{A}{4}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{A}{\sqrt{3}}$

## Explanation

The elastic force is conservative with potential energy $V = \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$ and the reaction of the smooth and fixed guide has zero power, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved. At the turning points $x = \pm A$ the velocity is zero, so $E = \dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,A^2$. If $T = 3V$, then $E = T + V = 4V$, that is
$$
4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,A^2 ,
$$
hence $x^2 = \dfrac{A^2}{4}$ and $x = \pm\dfrac{A}{2}$. At half the amplitude the potential energy is therefore one quarter of the total energy, and the velocity is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ of the maximum velocity $\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}\,A$.
