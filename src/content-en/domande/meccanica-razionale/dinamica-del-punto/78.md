In a vertical plane a circle is given, with vertical diameter of length $D$ and highest point $A$, as in the figure. Some smooth and fixed straight guides, which are chords of the circle, start from $A$, and along each of them a particle slides from rest to the other endpoint of the chord. Which statement about the descent time is true?

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" nodi="A,0,2" linee="0,2 1.732,-1;0,2 1.879,0.684;0,2 -1.970,-0.347;0,2 0,-2" punti="0,2;1.732,-1;1.879,0.684;-1.970,-0.347;0,-2" quote="0 2,0 -2,D,2.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Smooth chords starting from the highest point $A$ of a vertical circle of diameter $D$"}

- It is minimum along the vertical chord
- It is proportional to the length of the chord
- It is the same for all chords and equals $\sqrt{\dfrac{D}{g}}$
- It is the same for all chords and equals $\sqrt{\dfrac{2D}{g}}$

## Explanation

Let $\beta$ be the angle that a chord forms with the vertical diameter. The triangle whose sides are the chord and the diameter through $A$ is inscribed in a semicircle, therefore it is a right triangle, and the chord has length $D\cos\beta$. Along the chord, which is a smooth inclined plane, the motion is uniformly accelerated with acceleration $g\cos\beta$. Starting from rest the particle travels the chord in the time $t$ that satisfies $\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\cos\beta\,t^2 = D\cos\beta$, so
$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2D}{g}}
$$
for every chord, that is the time of free fall along the diameter. Passing to a more inclined chord, the length and the acceleration decrease in the same ratio $\cos\beta$.
