A free particle of mass $m$ is subject only to the force $\vec{F} = c\,\vec{v}_P \wedge \hat{k}$, with $c > 0$. At the initial instant it has velocity of magnitude $v_0$ orthogonal to $\hat{k}$. What is the radius of curvature of the trajectory?

- $\dfrac{c\,v_0}{m}$
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0}{c}$
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{c}$
- $\dfrac{2m\,v_0}{c}$

## Explanation

The power of the force is $c\,(\vec{v}_P \wedge \hat{k}) \cdot \vec{v}_P = 0$, because the triple product contains two equal vectors. By the kinetic energy theorem $T$ is constant, therefore the magnitude of the velocity remains $v_0$. The force is orthogonal to $\hat{k}$, as is the initial velocity, so the motion takes place in a plane orthogonal to $\hat{k}$; in that plane the force is orthogonal to the velocity, has magnitude $c\,v_0$ and is directed along the principal normal. The second intrinsic equation then gives
$$
m\,\frac{v_0^2}{\varrho} = c\,v_0 ,
$$
so $\varrho = \dfrac{m\,v_0}{c}$. The radius of curvature is constant, therefore the trajectory is a circle of that radius, traveled with velocity of constant magnitude.
