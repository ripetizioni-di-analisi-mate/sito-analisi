A particle of mass $m$ slides along a smooth and fixed horizontal rectilinear guide, connected to the wall by an ideal spring of constant $k$ and by a damper that exerts the force $-h\,\dot{x}\,\hat{\imath}$, with $h = \dfrac{5}{2}\sqrt{m k}$; one sets $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$. The particle starts from rest at $x = d \neq 0$. As $t \to +\infty$, $x(t)$ tends to zero with the same behavior as which function?

- $e^{-\frac{5}{4}\omega_0 t}$
- $e^{-2\omega_0 t}$
- $e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega_0 t}$
- $e^{-\omega_0 t}$

## Explanation

The damping coefficient is $\gamma = \dfrac{h}{2m} = \dfrac{5}{4}\,\omega_0$, greater than $\omega_0$, therefore the system is overdamped. The roots of the characteristic equation are
$$
\lambda_{1,2} = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2} = -\frac{5}{4}\,\omega_0 \pm \frac{3}{4}\,\omega_0 ,
$$
that is $\lambda_1 = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,\omega_0$ and $\lambda_2 = -2\,\omega_0$. Imposing $x(0) = d$ and $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ one obtains $x(t) = \dfrac{4d}{3}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega_0 t} - \dfrac{d}{3}\,e^{-2\omega_0 t}$. The second term tends to zero faster than the first, so for large $t$ the function $x(t)$ behaves like $e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega_0 t}$. The return to the rest position is therefore slower than with critical damping, in which the exponent is $-\omega_0 t$.
