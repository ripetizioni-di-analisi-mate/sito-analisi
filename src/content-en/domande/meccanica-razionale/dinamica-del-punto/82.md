A free particle of mass $m$ is subject to the central elastic force $-k\,(P - O)$, with $k > 0$. At the initial instant it is at the distance $r_0$ from $O$ with velocity of magnitude $v_0$ orthogonal to $P - O$, and $v_0 \neq r_0\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$. Which trajectory does it describe?

- An ellipse with its center at $O$
- An ellipse with a focus at $O$
- A circle with center $O$ and radius $r_0$
- A parabola with its focus at $O$

## Explanation

The $x$ axis is chosen along the line joining $O$ with the initial position and the $y$ axis parallel to the initial velocity. The fundamental equation $m\,\vec{a}_P = -k\,(P - O)$ splits into two harmonic oscillator equations with the same angular frequency $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$, and with the assigned initial data
$$
x(t) = r_0\cos(\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = \frac{v_0}{\omega}\sin(\omega t) .
$$
It follows that $\dfrac{x^2}{r_0^2} + \dfrac{\omega^2 y^2}{v_0^2} = 1$, therefore the trajectory is an ellipse with its center at $O$ and semi-axes $r_0$ and $\dfrac{v_0}{\omega}$, which are different by the hypothesis $v_0 \neq r_0\,\omega$. With the elastic force the center of force is the center of the ellipse, while in Keplerian orbits it is a focus.
