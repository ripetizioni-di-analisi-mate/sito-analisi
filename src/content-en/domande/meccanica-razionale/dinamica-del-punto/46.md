A particle of mass $m$ slides along a smooth and fixed horizontal rectilinear guide, and its abscissa follows the time law
$$
x(t) = a\ln\left( 1 + \frac{t}{\tau} \right) ,
\qquad
a > 0 ,
\qquad
\tau > 0 .
$$
Which horizontal force acts on the particle, expressed as a function of its velocity $v = \dot{x}$?

- $-\dfrac{m}{\tau}\,v\,\hat{\imath}$
- $-\dfrac{m}{a}\,v^2\,\hat{\imath}$
- $-\dfrac{2m}{a}\,v^2\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\dfrac{m}{a}\,v^2\,\hat{\imath}$

## Explanation

The motion is assigned and the force is sought, therefore this is an inverse problem. Differentiating the time law twice one obtains
$$
v = \frac{a}{\tau + t} ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = -\frac{a}{(\tau + t)^2} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{\tau + t} = \dfrac{v}{a}$, the acceleration can be rewritten as $\ddot{x} = -\dfrac{v^2}{a}$. The guide is horizontal and smooth, so the horizontal component of the fundamental equation gives the force $-\dfrac{m}{a}\,v^2\,\hat{\imath}$. This is a resistance proportional to the square of the velocity, under which the particle slows down without stopping and covers a distance that grows without bound.
