A particle of mass $m$ slides along a smooth and fixed horizontal rectilinear guide, connected to the wall by an ideal spring of constant $k$, without damper; one sets $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$. The force $F_0\cos(\omega_0 t)\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F_0 > 0$, also acts on the particle, and at the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at rest in the rest position. How does the amplitude of the oscillations vary in time?

- It grows linearly, as $\dfrac{F_0\,t}{2m\,\omega_0}$
- It remains constant and equals $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$
- It grows exponentially in time
- It grows linearly, as $\dfrac{F_0\,t}{m\,\omega_0}$

## Explanation

The equation of motion is $\ddot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = \dfrac{F_0}{m}\cos(\omega_0 t)$. The forcing has the same angular frequency as the solutions of the homogeneous equation, so a particular solution is sought in the form $t\,\big( A\cos(\omega_0 t) + B\sin(\omega_0 t) \big)$. Substituting one finds $A = 0$ and $B = \dfrac{F_0}{2m\,\omega_0}$, and this solution already satisfies the zero initial data, therefore
$$
x(t) = \frac{F_0}{2m\,\omega_0}\,t\sin(\omega_0 t) .
$$
The amplitude $\dfrac{F_0\,t}{2m\,\omega_0}$ hence grows linearly and without bound. The result is consistent with the maximum steady-state amplitude of the damped oscillator, $\dfrac{F_0}{2m\,\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}}$, which diverges for $\gamma \to 0$.
