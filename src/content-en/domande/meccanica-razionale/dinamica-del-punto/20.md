A particle of mass $m$ slides down from rest along the smooth and fixed inclined plane in the figure, which makes the angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal, and is immersed in a fluid that exerts the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}_P$, with $h > 0$. What is the terminal velocity of the particle?

::struttura{linee="0,0 5,2.887 5,0 0,0" nodi="O,0,0;P,3.248,1.875" punti="3.248,1.875" forze="3.248 1.875,0,-1,mg" etichette="0.95,0.22,α" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle descending along the smooth inclined plane, immersed in a viscous fluid"}

- $\dfrac{m g}{h}$
- $\dfrac{m g\cos\alpha}{h}$
- $\dfrac{m g\sin\alpha}{h}$
- $\dfrac{m g\sin\alpha}{2h}$

## Explanation

Along the line of steepest slope there act the component of the weight, of magnitude $m g\sin\alpha$, and the viscous resistance, while the reaction of the smooth plane is normal to the direction of the motion. The equation of motion is therefore
$$
m\,\dot{v} = m g\sin\alpha - h\,v ,
$$
which is the equation of the motion with a constant force and viscous resistance. The velocity tends to the value that makes the acceleration vanish, therefore the terminal velocity equals $\dfrac{m g\sin\alpha}{h}$, and it is reached the more quickly the smaller the characteristic time $\dfrac{m}{h}$ is.
