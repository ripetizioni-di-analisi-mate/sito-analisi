A particle of mass $m$ slides from rest along a smooth and fixed guide, placed in a vertical plane, and descends through the drop in height $h$. At the base the guide becomes horizontal and the particle compresses an ideal spring of constant $k$, placed along the guide. What is the maximum compression of the spring?

- $\dfrac{2 m g h}{k}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{m g h}{k}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2 m g h}{k}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{m g h}{2k}}$

## Explanation

The guide is smooth and fixed and the spring is ideal, therefore the mechanical energy is conserved throughout the motion. Along the descending stretch the weight does the work $m g h$, therefore the particle arrives at the base with kinetic energy $m g h$. Along the horizontal stretch the height does not change, hence at the maximum compression $\delta$ the kinetic energy has passed entirely to the spring and equals
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,k\,\delta^2 = m g h .
$$
It follows that $\delta = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 m g h}{k}}$.
