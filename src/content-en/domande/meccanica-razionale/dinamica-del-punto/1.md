A particle of mass $m$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure. At the instant $t = 0$ it is at $O$ with velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$, with $v_0 > 0$, and from that instant it is subject to the constant force $-F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, besides the weight. What distance does it travel before stopping?

::struttura{linee="-0.5,0 7,0" nodi="O,0,0" punti="3,0,P" forze="3 0,-1.3,0,F" frecce="3,0.55,4.3,0.55,v_0" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on a smooth horizontal guide with the constant force $F$ opposite to the initial velocity"}

- $\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{2F}$
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{F}$
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0}{F}$
- $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2F}$

## Explanation

The guide is horizontal and smooth, therefore the weight and the reaction of the guide cancel each other and along the $x$ axis the force $-F$ alone acts. The kinetic energy theorem between the initial instant and the instant at which the particle stops gives
$$
0 - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 = -F\,d ,
$$
since the force is constant and the displacement equals $d$. Therefore $d = \dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{2F}$. The same value follows from the fundamental equation. The acceleration equals $-\dfrac{F}{m}$, therefore the motion is uniformly decelerated and the particle stops at the instant $\dfrac{m\,v_0}{F}$, having travelled exactly the distance $\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{2F}$.
