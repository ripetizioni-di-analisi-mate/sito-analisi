To the system formed by a particle of mass $m$, by a spring of constant $k$ and by a damper of constant $h = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{m k}$, on a smooth horizontal guide, the force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)\,\hat{\imath}$ is applied. Setting $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$, what is the resonant angular frequency?

- $\omega_0$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{4}\,\omega_0$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{4}\,\omega_0$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{4}\,\omega_0$

## Explanation

The damping coefficient equals $\gamma = \dfrac{h}{2m} = \dfrac{1}{4}\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = \dfrac{\omega_0}{4}$. The resonant angular frequency is the point of maximum of the steady-state amplitude and equals $\Omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2}$, provided that $\gamma < \dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$, a condition satisfied here. Substituting one obtains
$$
\Omega_r = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{8}} = \frac{\sqrt{14}}{4}\,\omega_0 \approx 0.935\,\omega_0 ,
$$
therefore the resonance falls a little below the natural angular frequency.
