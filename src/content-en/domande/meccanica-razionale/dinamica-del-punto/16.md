A particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, subject to the force $-k\,x^3\,\hat{\imath}$, with $k > 0$, besides the weight. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at $x = 0$ with zero velocity. What is the motion of the particle?

- Rest at $x = 0$
- A harmonic motion of angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$
- A periodic motion that is not harmonic
- A uniformly accelerated motion

## Explanation

The assigned force vanishes at $x = 0$, therefore the constant function $x(t) = 0$ satisfies both the equation $m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x^3$ and the initial data $x(0) = 0$ and $\dot{x}(0) = 0$. The force is regular, therefore the theorem of existence and uniqueness applies and the solution with those initial data is a single one. It follows that the motion is rest at $x = 0$. With a non-zero initial velocity the motion would instead be periodic but not harmonic, since the force is not proportional to the displacement.
