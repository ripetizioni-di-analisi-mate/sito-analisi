A particle travels a circular orbit of radius $r_0$ about a center of Newtonian attraction, subject to the force $-\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r^2}\,\hat{u}_r$ with $\mu > 0$. If the radius of the circular orbit is doubled, how does the period of revolution change?

- It doubles
- It becomes $\sqrt{2}$ times larger
- It becomes $2\sqrt{2}$ times larger
- It becomes $4$ times larger

## Explanation

On a circular orbit of radius $r_0$ the velocity equals $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mu}{r_0}}$ and the period is
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r_0^3}{\mu}} ,
$$
therefore the ratio $\dfrac{T^2}{r_0^3}$ has the same value for all the circular orbits about the same center, as Kepler's third law states. It follows that the period is proportional to $r_0^{\frac{3}{2}}$. Doubling the radius, the period is therefore multiplied by $2^{\frac{3}{2}} = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.83$.
