A particle of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is released from rest in a fluid that exerts the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}_P$, with $h = 0.25\,\mathrm{kg/s}$; one assumes $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At which instant does the velocity reach half of the terminal value?

- $0.35\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.00\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.39\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

On the particle there act the weight and the viscous resistance, therefore the velocity equals $v(t) = \dfrac{m g}{h}\left( 1 - e^{-\frac{h}{m}t} \right)$ and the terminal value is $v_\ell = \dfrac{m g}{h}$. The condition $v(t_m) = \dfrac{v_\ell}{2}$ gives $e^{-\frac{h}{m}t_m} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore
$$
t_m = \frac{m\ln 2}{h} .
$$
With the assigned data $\dfrac{m}{h} = 2.00\,\mathrm{s}$, hence $t_m = 2.00 \cdot 0.693 = 1.39\,\mathrm{s}$. The characteristic time $\dfrac{m}{h}$ measures how quickly the terminal velocity is reached, and the value sought is a fraction of it.
