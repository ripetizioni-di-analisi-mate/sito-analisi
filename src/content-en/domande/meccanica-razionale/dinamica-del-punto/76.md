A particle of mass $m$ hangs from the fixed point $O$ by an inextensible string of length $\ell$ and travels with uniform motion along a horizontal circle, with the string forming the constant angle $\alpha$ with the vertical, as in the figure. What is the period of the motion?

- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell\cos\alpha}{g}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell\sin\alpha}{g}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g\cos\alpha}}$

## Explanation

The circle has radius $\ell\sin\alpha$ and is traveled with constant angular velocity $\omega$, therefore the acceleration is horizontal, directed towards the axis and of magnitude $\omega^2\,\ell\sin\alpha$. The weight and the tension $\Phi$, directed along the string towards $O$, act on the particle, so the vertical and horizontal components of the fundamental equation are
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi\cos\alpha - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi\sin\alpha = m\,\omega^2\,\ell\sin\alpha
\end{cases}
$$
The second gives $\Phi = m\,\omega^2\,\ell$, and substituting into the first one obtains $\omega^2 = \dfrac{g}{\ell\cos\alpha}$. The period $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$ is therefore $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell\cos\alpha}{g}}$, and for $\alpha \to 0$ it tends to the period of the small oscillations of the simple pendulum.
