A particle travels along an elliptic orbit around the center of force $O$, subject to a central force. At the minimum distance $r_p$ from $O$ the velocity has magnitude $v_p$. What is the magnitude of the velocity at the maximum distance $r_a$?

- $v_p\,\dfrac{r_a}{r_p}$
- $v_p\,\dfrac{r_p}{r_a}$
- $v_p\sqrt{\dfrac{r_p}{r_a}}$
- $v_p\,\dfrac{r_p^2}{r_a^2}$

## Explanation

In a central motion the quantity $c = r^2\dot{\vartheta}$ is constant. At the points of minimum and maximum distance $r$ is stationary, therefore $\dot{r} = 0$ and the velocity is orthogonal to the position vector, with magnitude $r\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$; hence at those points $|c| = r\,v$. Equating the two values one obtains $r_p\,v_p = r_a\,v_a$, therefore
$$
v_a = v_p\,\frac{r_p}{r_a} .
$$
The particle is slower at the maximum distance, in agreement with the constancy of the areal velocity.
