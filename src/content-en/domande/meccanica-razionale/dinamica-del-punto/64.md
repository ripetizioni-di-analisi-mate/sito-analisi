A particle of mass $m$ rests on a horizontal shelf, which is raised with constant velocity of magnitude $v$ through a height difference $h$; the particle remains at rest with respect to the shelf. How much work does the reaction of the shelf do on the particle?

- $0$
- $m g h$
- $-m g h$
- $m g h + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2$

## Explanation

The particle moves with uniform rectilinear motion, therefore its kinetic energy remains constant and the kinetic energy theorem gives $L_{\Phi} + L_{\text{weight}} = 0$. The work of the weight in a rise of height $h$ is $-m g h$, so the reaction does the work $m g h$. The reaction equals $m g\,\hat{\jmath}$ and is parallel to the velocity, therefore its power $m g\,v$ is nonzero. The constraint is moving, and the reaction of a moving constraint in general has nonzero power, even when it is orthogonal to the supporting surface.
