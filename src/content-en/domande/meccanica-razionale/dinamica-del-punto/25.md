A particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, connected to the wall by an ideal spring of constant $k$ and by a damper. The equation of motion is $\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\,\dot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = \dfrac{F_0}{m}\cos(\Omega t)$, with $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ and $\gamma = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$. What is the amplitude of the steady-state motion for $\Omega = \omega_0$?

- $\dfrac{F_0}{2k}$
- $\dfrac{2F_0}{k}$
- $\dfrac{4F_0}{k}$
- $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$

## Explanation

The steady-state amplitude equals
$$
A(\Omega) = \frac{\dfrac{F_0}{m}}{\sqrt{( \omega_0^2 - \Omega^2 )^2 + 4\gamma^2\Omega^2}} ,
$$
therefore for $\Omega = \omega_0$ the first summand under the square root vanishes and there remains $A(\omega_0) = \dfrac{F_0}{2 m\,\gamma\,\omega_0}$. Since $\gamma = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$, it turns out that $2\gamma\,\omega_0 = \omega_0^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$, therefore $A(\omega_0) = \dfrac{F_0}{k}$. The value coincides with the elongation that a force of magnitude $F_0$ would produce on the spring under static conditions.
