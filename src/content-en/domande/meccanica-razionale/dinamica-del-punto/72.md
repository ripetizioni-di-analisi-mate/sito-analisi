A particle of mass $m$ moves on the inner surface of a smooth and fixed circular cylinder of radius $R$, with vertical axis, and is subject to its weight. At the initial instant the particle has horizontal velocity of magnitude $v_0$, tangent to the surface. What is the magnitude of the reaction of the cylinder during the motion?

- $\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{R} + m g$
- $\sqrt{\left( \dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{R} \right)^2 + (m g)^2}$
- $\dfrac{m\,(v_0^2 + 2 g z)}{R}$, where $z$ is the height through which the particle has descended
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{R}$, constant

## Explanation

Cylindrical coordinates are used with the $z$ axis vertical and upwards; the particle has constant distance $R$ from the axis and azimuth $\varphi$. The surface is smooth, therefore the reaction is orthogonal to it, that is horizontal and directed along the radius, while the weight is vertical. The transverse, vertical and radial components of the fundamental equation are
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,R\,\ddot{\varphi} = 0 \\[4pt]
m\,\ddot{z} = -m g \\[4pt]
-m\,R\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = -\Phi
\end{cases}
$$
The first gives $R\,\dot{\varphi} = v_0$ constant, the second a free fall along the vertical and the third $\Phi = \dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{R}$, directed towards the axis. The reaction does not depend on the falling velocity, because this velocity is parallel to the generatrices of the cylinder, which are straight lines.
