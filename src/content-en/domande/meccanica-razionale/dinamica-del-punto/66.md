A particle of mass $m$ slides along a smooth and fixed horizontal rectilinear guide, subject to a force of constant magnitude $F_0$ directed, at every position $x \neq 0$, towards the origin, that is with potential energy $V(x) = F_0\,|x|$. At the initial instant the particle is at the origin with velocity of magnitude $v_0$. What is the period of the motion?

- $\dfrac{2m\,v_0}{F_0}$
- $\dfrac{m\,v_0}{F_0}$
- $\dfrac{4m\,v_0}{F_0}$
- $\dfrac{8m\,v_0}{F_0}$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy is $E = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2$, therefore the turning points are the solutions of $F_0\,|x| = E$, that is $x = \pm\dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{2F_0}$, and the motion is periodic between them. For $x > 0$ the force equals $-F_0$, so the motion is uniformly decelerated and the particle goes from velocity $v_0$ to zero velocity in the time $\dfrac{m\,v_0}{F_0}$. By the symmetry of $V$ the period consists of four stretches traveled in the same time, therefore
$$
T = \frac{4m\,v_0}{F_0} .
$$
The same value is obtained from the formula $T = \sqrt{2m}\displaystyle\int_{x_1}^{x_2} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{E - V(x)}}$. The period grows with the initial velocity, therefore the oscillations are not isochronous.
