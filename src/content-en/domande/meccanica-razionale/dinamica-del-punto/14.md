- $-\dfrac{3\,m\,a\,c^2}{r^4}\,\hat{u}_r$
- $-\dfrac{m\,a\,c^2}{r^4}\,\hat{u}_r$
- $-\dfrac{3\,m\,a\,c^2}{r^3}\,\hat{u}_r$
- $-\dfrac{3\,m\,a^2\,c^2}{r^5}\,\hat{u}_r$

## Explanation

Setting $u = \dfrac{1}{r}$, the equation of the orbit gives $u = \dfrac{1}{a\,( 1 + \cos\vartheta )}$. Differentiating twice with respect to $\vartheta$ and adding $u$ one obtains
$$
\frac{d^2u}{d\vartheta^2} + u = \frac{3}{a\,( 1 + \cos\vartheta )^2} = 3\,a\,u^2 .
$$
Binet's formula expresses the radial component of the force as $f = -m\,c^2\,u^2\left( \dfrac{d^2u}{d\vartheta^2} + u \right)$, therefore $f = -3\,m\,a\,c^2\,u^4$. Going back to the variable $r$ one has $\vec{F} = -\dfrac{3\,m\,a\,c^2}{r^4}\,\hat{u}_r$, therefore the force is attractive and inversely proportional to the fourth power of the distance.
