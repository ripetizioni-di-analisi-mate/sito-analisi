A smooth rectilinear guide is placed in a horizontal plane and rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ about the vertical axis through its point $O$; the figure shows it from above. A particle of mass $m$ is threaded on the guide, with no other connections. At a certain instant the particle is at the distance $s$ from $O$ and moves away from $O$ with relative velocity of magnitude $v_r$. What is, at that instant, the magnitude of the horizontal component of the reaction of the guide?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,3,0" linee="-1,0 5.5,0" punti="0,0;3,0" frecce="3,0.45,4.4,0.45,v_r" coppie="O,1,ω" quote="O,P,s,0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Top view of the smooth guide rotating about the vertical axis through $O$"}

- $m\,\omega\,v_r$
- $m\,\omega^2 s$
- $0$
- $2m\,\omega\,v_r$

## Explanation

In the frame attached to the guide the weight, the reaction $\vec{\Phi}$, the centrifugal force $m\,\omega^2 s$, directed along the guide, and the Coriolis force $-2m\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r$ act on the particle. The relative velocity is directed along the guide and $\vec{\omega}$ is vertical, so the Coriolis force is horizontal, orthogonal to the guide and of magnitude $2m\,\omega\,v_r$. The relative motion takes place along the guide, therefore the relative acceleration has no components orthogonal to the guide, and the reaction, orthogonal to the guide because the guide is smooth, balances the Coriolis force in the horizontal plane and the weight along the vertical. The horizontal component of the reaction hence has magnitude $2m\,\omega\,v_r$ and is directed in the sense of the rotation. The centrifugal force, parallel to the guide, accelerates the particle along it and does not enter the reaction.
