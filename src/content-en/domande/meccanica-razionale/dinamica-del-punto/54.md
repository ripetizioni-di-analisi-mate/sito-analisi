A particle of mass $m$ falls along the vertical in a fluid that exerts a resistance of magnitude $c\,v^2$, with $c > 0$, opposite to the velocity; the weight acts in addition to it. What is the terminal velocity?

- $\dfrac{m g}{c}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{m g}{c}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2 m g}{c}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{c}}$

## Explanation

With the axis directed downwards, during the fall the equation of motion is $m\,\dot{v} = m g - c\,v^2$. The terminal velocity is the constant value of $v$ that makes the acceleration vanish, therefore it satisfies $c\,v^2 = m g$, so $v_\ell = \sqrt{\dfrac{m g}{c}}$. If the particle starts from rest, the right-hand side remains positive as long as $v < v_\ell$, so the velocity increases and tends to $v_\ell$ without exceeding it. With the quadratic resistance the terminal velocity is proportional to the square root of the mass, while with the viscous resistance it is proportional to the mass.
