A particle moves along a smooth rectilinear guide that rotates with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$ about a fixed axis parallel to the guide. Denoting by $v_r$ the velocity of the particle relative to the guide and by $d$ the distance of the particle from the axis, what is its Coriolis acceleration?

- Of magnitude $2\,\omega\,v_r$, orthogonal to the guide
- Of magnitude $\omega\,v_r$, orthogonal to the guide
- $\vec{0}$, because $\vec{v}_r$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$
- Of magnitude $\omega^2\,d$, directed towards the axis

## Explanation

The Coriolis acceleration equals $\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r$, therefore it vanishes when the relative velocity is parallel to the angular velocity. The guide is parallel to the axis of rotation and the particle moves along the guide, therefore $\vec{v}_r$ is parallel to $\vec{\omega}$ and the cross product is zero. It follows that $\vec{a}_c = \vec{0}$. The term of magnitude $\omega^2\,d$ directed towards the axis appears instead in the transport acceleration, which does not vanish as long as the particle lies outside the axis.
