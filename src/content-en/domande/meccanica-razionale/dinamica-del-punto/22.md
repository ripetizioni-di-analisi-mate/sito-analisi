A point particle of mass $m$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed straight guide, connected by an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ to an attachment point $Q$ that can be moved along the guide. The particle oscillates with amplitude $A$ about the equilibrium configuration $x = 0$. At the instant when the particle is at $x = A$ with zero velocity, the attachment point $Q$ is shifted instantaneously by $\dfrac{A}{2}$ in the direction of positive $x$. What is the amplitude of the subsequent oscillations?

- $A$
- $\dfrac{A}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3A}{2}$
- $\dfrac{A}{\sqrt{2}}$

## Explanation

The equilibrium configuration is the one in which the spring has its natural length, therefore the shift of $Q$ moves it from $x = 0$ to $x = \dfrac{A}{2}$. The angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ does not change, since the mass and the spring stiffness stay the same. The shift is instantaneous, hence immediately afterward the particle is still at $x = A$ with zero velocity. A position with zero velocity is a turning point, and its distance from the new equilibrium is the amplitude, so the amplitude equals $A - \dfrac{A}{2} = \dfrac{A}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{3A}{2}$ is obtained by shifting the equilibrium in the opposite direction, and $\dfrac{A}{\sqrt{2}}$ by assuming that the energy relative to the new equilibrium is half of the initial one, while it is one quarter.
