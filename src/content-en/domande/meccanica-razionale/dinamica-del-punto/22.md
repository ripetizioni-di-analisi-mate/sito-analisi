A particle of mass $m = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides along a horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, connected to the wall by an ideal spring of constant $k = 80\,\mathrm{N/m}$. The abscissa $x$ is measured from the position in which the spring has its rest length. At the instant $t = 0$ one has $x = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and velocity of magnitude $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the amplitude of the oscillations?

- $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The natural angular frequency equals $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{400} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The amplitude is obtained from the initial data by the formula $A = \sqrt{x_0^2 + \dfrac{v_0^2}{\omega_0^2}}$, therefore
$$
A = \sqrt{0.010 + \frac{4.0}{400}} = \sqrt{0.020}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.14\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The two quantities under the square root combine like the squares of the legs of a right triangle, therefore the amplitude is smaller than the sum $x_0 + \dfrac{v_0}{\omega_0} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$.
