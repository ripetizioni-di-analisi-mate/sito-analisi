A particle of mass $m$ moves resting on top of the smooth and fixed bump in the figure, whose profile in the vertical plane $Oxy$ is the parabola $y = -\dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, with $a > 0$. The constraint is unilateral, because the bump can only push the particle outwards. What is the maximum velocity with which the particle can pass through the vertex $O$ without detaching?

- $\sqrt{2 g a}$
- $\sqrt{g a}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{g a}{2}}$
- $2\sqrt{g a}$

## Explanation

The radius of curvature of the graph of $y = f(x)$ is $\varrho = \dfrac{\big( 1 + f'(x)^2 \big)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{|f''(x)|}$, and at the vertex $f'(0) = 0$ and $|f''(0)| = \dfrac{1}{a}$, therefore $\varrho = a$. At the vertex the principal normal is directed downwards, so the second intrinsic equation gives
$$
m\,\frac{v^2}{a} = m g - \Phi ,
$$
where $\Phi \ge 0$ is the thrust of the bump, directed upwards. The condition $\Phi \ge 0$ is equivalent to $v^2 \le g a$, therefore the maximum velocity is $\sqrt{g a}$. For larger velocities the required reaction would be negative, so the particle detaches already at the vertex.
