A particle of mass $m$ is subject to the Newtonian force $-\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r^2}\,\hat{u}_r$, with $\mu > 0$, and is launched from the distance $r_0$ from the center $O$ with velocity directed radially outwards. What is the minimum magnitude of the velocity for which the particle moves indefinitely far from $O$?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mu}{r_0}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mu}{2 r_0}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\mu}{r_0}}$
- $\dfrac{2\mu}{r_0}$

## Explanation

The force is conservative with potential $U = \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$, because $U'(r) = -\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r^2}$, therefore the mechanical energy $E = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 - \dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$ is constant. The potential energy $-\dfrac{\mu\,m}{r}$ is negative and tends to $0$ for $r \to +\infty$; hence if $E < 0$ the motion remains in the region $r \le \dfrac{\mu\,m}{|E|}$, while if $E \ge 0$ the radial velocity never vanishes and the particle moves indefinitely far away. The condition $E \ge 0$ at $r = r_0$ gives $v_0^2 \ge \dfrac{2\mu}{r_0}$, therefore the minimum velocity is $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\mu}{r_0}}$. This value is $\sqrt{2}$ times the velocity of the circular orbit of radius $r_0$.
