A particle of mass $m$ is threaded on a smooth and fixed circular guide of radius $R$, placed in a horizontal plane. The particle starts from rest and is pushed by the force $F_0\,\hat{t}$, of constant magnitude $F_0$ and always tangent to the guide in the direction of motion; the weight acts as well. With what speed does the particle pass through its starting position for the first time?

- $0$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\pi R\,F_0}{m}}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi R\,F_0}{m}}$
- $2\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi R\,F_0}{m}}$

## Explanation

The guide is smooth and fixed, therefore the reaction is orthogonal to the velocity and has zero power; the weight also has zero power, because it is vertical and the velocity is horizontal. The force $F_0\,\hat{t}$ is parallel to the velocity and has the same direction, so its power is $F_0\,\dot{s}$ and its work along an arc of length $s$ is $F_0\,s$. After a full turn the particle has covered the arc $2\pi R$, and the kinetic energy theorem gives
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 = 2\pi R\,F_0 ,
$$
hence $v = 2\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi R\,F_0}{m}}$. The force has done nonzero work along a closed path, therefore it is not conservative.
