A conducting sphere of radius $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the charge $30\,\mathrm{nC}$. A thin conducting wire connects it to a second, uncharged conducting sphere of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, placed at a distance much larger than the radii. At equilibrium, what charge does the sphere of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carry?

- $10\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $20\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $15\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The two spheres and the wire form a single conductor, so at equilibrium they have the same potential. The spheres are far apart, therefore the potential of each is that of an isolated sphere with its own charge:
$$
\frac{kQ_1}{R_1} = \frac{kQ_2}{R_2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \frac{R_1}{R_2} = 2 .
$$
The charge on the wire is negligible and the total charge is conserved, so $Q_1 + Q_2 = 30\,\mathrm{nC}$. It follows that
$$
Q_2 = 30\,\mathrm{nC} \cdot \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} = 30\,\mathrm{nC} \cdot \frac{2.0}{6.0} = 10\,\mathrm{nC} .
$$
