A parallel-plate capacitor is connected to an ideal source that fixes the potential difference. Edge effects are neglected. Initially, the given quantities are the capacitance $C = 8\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ and the voltage across it $U_{e} = 12\,\mathrm{V}$. In the second configuration, we change the voltage across it to $18\,\mathrm{V}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $q$?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $3$

## Explanation

The definition of capacitance is $C=q/U_e$. The product of microfarads and volts gives microcoulombs. Applying $q = C \cdot U_{e}$ gives $96\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ before the change and $144\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
