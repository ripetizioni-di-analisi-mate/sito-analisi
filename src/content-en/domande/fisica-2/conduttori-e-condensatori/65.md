A defibrillator contains a $100\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor charged to $2.0\,\mathrm{kV}$. During use the capacitor discharges completely in $5.0\,\mathrm{ms}$. What is the average power delivered?

- $80\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $40\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $40\,\mathrm{W}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The energy stored in the charged capacitor is
$$
U = \frac{1}{2} C\,\Delta V^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot (2.0 \cdot 10^{3})^2\,\mathrm{J} = 200\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The discharge is complete, so all this energy is delivered in the time $\Delta t = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}$. Therefore the average power is
$$
P = \frac{U}{\Delta t} = \frac{200}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{W} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{W} = 40\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
