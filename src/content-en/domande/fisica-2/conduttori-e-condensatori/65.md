An isolated capacitor has constant charge and is initially in vacuum. A linear dielectric then fills the entire space between its plates. The geometry remains fixed. Initially, the given quantities are the initial voltage $U_{0} = 48\,\mathrm{V}$ and the relative permittivity $\epsilon = 4$. In the second configuration, we change the initial voltage to $60\,\mathrm{V}$ and the relative permittivity to $2$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $U_{e}$?

- $\dfrac{52}{5}\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-18\,\mathrm{V}$
- $18\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-72\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

At fixed charge, the voltage is inversely proportional to the capacitance. The dielectric multiplies the capacitance by the relative permittivity. Applying $U_{e} = \dfrac{U_{0}}{\epsilon}$ gives $12\,\mathrm{V}$ before the change and $30\,\mathrm{V}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $18\,\mathrm{V}$.
