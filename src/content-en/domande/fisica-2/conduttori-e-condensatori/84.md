A capacitor of capacitance $40\,\mathrm{pF}$ in vacuum stays connected to a $100\,\mathrm{V}$ source while the space between the plates is filled with a dielectric of constant $\varepsilon_r = 3.0$. How much work does the source do during the insertion?

- $0.40\,\mu\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2\,\mu\mathrm{J}$
- $0.60\,\mu\mathrm{J}$
- $0.80\,\mu\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The potential difference stays $\Delta V = 100\,\mathrm{V}$, so the charge goes from $Q_0 = C_0\,\Delta V = 4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ to $Q = \varepsilon_r C_0\,\Delta V = 12\,\mathrm{nC}$.

The source moves the charge $Q - Q_0 = 8.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ between two points with constant potential difference $\Delta V$, therefore it does the work
$$
W_{\text{gen}} = \Delta V\,(Q - Q_0) = 100 \cdot 8.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J} = 0.80\,\mu\mathrm{J} .
$$
The energy of the capacitor increases by $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\Delta V\,(Q - Q_0) = 0.40\,\mu\mathrm{J}$, that is by half the work of the source; the other half is the work done by the field on the entering dielectric.
