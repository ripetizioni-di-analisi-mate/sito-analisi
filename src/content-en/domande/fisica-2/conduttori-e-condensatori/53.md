On a patch of the surface of a conductor in equilibrium the surface charge density is $\sigma = 3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$. What is the force per unit area that the other charges of the conductor exert on the charge of that patch?

- $0.51\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

By **Coulomb's theorem** the field just outside the conductor is $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, while in the material it is zero. The charge of the small patch produces the field $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ on each side, like a charged plane; therefore the other charges of the conductor produce at the patch the field $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$, directed outward.

A charge exerts no force on itself, so only the field $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ acts on the charge $\sigma\,\Delta A$ of the patch. The force per unit area is then
$$
p = \frac{\sigma^2}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{(3.0 \cdot 10^{-6})^2}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{Pa} \approx 0.51\,\mathrm{Pa} ,
$$
directed outward from the conductor.
