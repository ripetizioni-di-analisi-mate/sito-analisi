An isolated conducting sphere of radius $R$ carries the charge $Q$. What fraction of the energy stored in the electric field lies between the spherical surfaces of radii $R$ and $2R$ concentric with the sphere?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{7}{8}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

Inside the sphere the field is zero, while for $r > R$ it is $E = \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}$. A spherical shell of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$ has volume $4\pi r^2\,dr$, so it contains the energy
$$
dU = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2 \cdot 4\pi r^2\,dr = \frac{Q^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{dr}{r^2} .
$$
Integrating, the energy between $R$ and $2R$ is $\dfrac{Q^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \dfrac{1}{R} - \dfrac{1}{2R} \right) = \dfrac{Q^2}{16\pi\varepsilon_0 R}$, while the total energy, between $R$ and $+\infty$, is $\dfrac{Q^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 R}$.

Therefore the required fraction is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
