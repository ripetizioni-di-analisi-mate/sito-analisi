A charged parallel-plate capacitor is completely filled with a homogeneous dielectric with $\varepsilon_r = 4$. Which statement about the polarization charges is true?

- On the face of the dielectric facing the positive plate the polarization charge is positive
- The polarization charge is distributed uniformly throughout the volume of the dielectric
- On the face of the dielectric facing the positive plate the polarization charge is negative
- On each face of the dielectric the polarization charge has the same magnitude as the free charge of the nearby plate

## Explanation

In the field of the capacitor each molecule of the dielectric becomes a small dipole oriented along the field, that is with its negative end toward the positive plate. Inside a homogeneous dielectric the charges of neighboring dipoles compensate each other, so the volume stays neutral.

On the face near the positive plate the negative ends of the dipoles are left uncompensated, therefore there the **polarization charge is negative**. Its magnitude is $\sigma_p = \sigma \left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_r} \right) = \dfrac{3}{4}\,\sigma$, smaller than the free charge density.
