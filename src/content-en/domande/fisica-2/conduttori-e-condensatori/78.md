A parallel-plate capacitor is completely filled with a dielectric with $\varepsilon_r = 3.0$, and the free charge density on the plates is $\sigma = 6.0\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$. What is the magnitude of the polarization charge density on the faces of the dielectric?

- $2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $6.0\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $4.0\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $18\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$

## Explanation

In the dielectric the field is $\varepsilon_r$ times smaller than it would be in vacuum with the same charge, so $E = \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r}$.

The field in the dielectric is produced by the free charge of the plate and by the polarization charge, of opposite sign, on the nearby face; by **Gauss's law** applied to a small cylinder with one base in the plate and the other in the dielectric, $E = \dfrac{\sigma - \sigma_p}{\varepsilon_0}$. Equating the two expressions,
$$
\sigma_p = \sigma \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\varepsilon_r} \right) = 6.0 \cdot \frac{2}{3}\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2} = 4.0\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2} .
$$
