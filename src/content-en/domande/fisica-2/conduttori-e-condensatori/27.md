A capacitor of capacitance $C$, with charges $\pm Q$ on its plates, is disconnected from the source and connected in parallel to a second, uncharged capacitor of capacitance $3C$. What fraction of the initial electrostatic energy remains stored in the two capacitors once the charges have reached equilibrium?

- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{16}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $1$

## Explanation

Initially the energy is $U_i = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C}$.

The system is isolated, so the total charge on the plates connected together remains $Q$. At equilibrium the connected plates are at the same potential, therefore the two capacitors form a parallel combination of capacitance $C + 3C = 4C$.

The final energy is therefore $U_f = \dfrac{Q^2}{2 \cdot 4C} = \dfrac{1}{4}\,U_i$, that is, **one quarter** of the initial energy remains stored. The other three quarters are dissipated in the connections during the transient.
