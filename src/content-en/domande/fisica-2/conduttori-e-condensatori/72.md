Which of the following conductors or capacitors, all in vacuum, has the largest capacitance?

- A spherical capacitor with radii $R_1 = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $R_2 = 11\,\mathrm{cm}$
- A parallel-plate capacitor with plates of area $0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at a distance of $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$
- An isolated conducting sphere of radius $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$
- A cylindrical capacitor $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long with $b = 3a$

## Explanation

The four capacitances are computed with $4\pi\varepsilon_0 = \dfrac{1}{k} \approx 1.11 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F/m}$.

The spherical capacitor has $C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0\,\dfrac{R_1 R_2}{R_2 - R_1} = 1.11 \cdot 10^{-10} \cdot 1.1\,\mathrm{F} \approx 122\,\mathrm{pF}$. The parallel-plate capacitor has $C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} \approx 88.5\,\mathrm{pF}$. The isolated sphere has $C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 R \approx 100\,\mathrm{pF}$. The cylindrical capacitor has $C = \dfrac{2\pi\varepsilon_0\,\ell}{\ln 3} \approx 50\,\mathrm{pF}$.

Therefore the largest capacitance is that of the **spherical capacitor**, whose plates are close together and have a large area.
