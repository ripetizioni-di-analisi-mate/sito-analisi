In a parallel-plate capacitor, half the area is filled with a linear dielectric occupying the full thickness. The other half is in vacuum. Edge effects are neglected. Given the relative permittivity of the dielectric $\epsilon = 5$ and the overall capacitance $C = 30\,\mathrm{pF}$, what is the capacitance of the entire capacitor in vacuum $C_{0}$?

- $6\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $18\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $20\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $10\,\mathrm{pF}$

## Explanation

The two halves have the same voltage, so they are equivalent to capacitances in parallel. Their values are $C_0/2$ and $\varepsilon C_0/2$. In the indicated units, $C = \dfrac{C_{0} \cdot \left(\epsilon + 1\right)}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $C_{0} = \dfrac{2 \cdot C}{\epsilon + 1}$. Using the given values, we obtain $10\,\mathrm{pF}$.
