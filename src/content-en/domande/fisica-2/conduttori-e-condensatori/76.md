Two initially uncharged capacitors of $2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ and $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ are connected in series to a $10\,\mathrm{V}$ source. Once charging is complete, the two terminals of the $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor are joined by a wire, while the source stays connected. What is the final charge on the $2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor?

- $12\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $30\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $20\,\mu\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The wire brings the plates of the $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor to the same potential, so this capacitor discharges and its potential difference becomes zero.

The source stays connected, therefore its whole voltage of $10\,\mathrm{V}$ lies across the $2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor. Consequently its charge is
$$
Q = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{V} = 20\,\mu\mathrm{C} ,
$$
greater than the $12\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ it carried before the wire was connected, when the series combination had capacitance $1.2\,\mu\mathrm{F}$.
