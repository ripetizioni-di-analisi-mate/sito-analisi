A small conducting sphere with charge $+4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, held by an insulating thread, is lowered into the cavity of an isolated conducting shell carrying the charge $+6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. The sphere touches the inner wall of the cavity and is then removed without touching anything else. What charge does the sphere carry after it is removed?

- $+2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $0\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $+4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $+5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

During the contact the sphere and the shell form a single conductor in equilibrium, and the surface of the sphere is part of the wall of the cavity of this conductor.

The cavity contains no charges, so the charge on its wall is zero. Therefore no charge remains on the surface of the sphere, and the whole charge $+4.0\,\mathrm{nC} + 6.0\,\mathrm{nC} = +10\,\mathrm{nC}$ lies on the outer surface of the shell.

Consequently the removed sphere has **zero charge**, whatever the initial charge of the shell.
