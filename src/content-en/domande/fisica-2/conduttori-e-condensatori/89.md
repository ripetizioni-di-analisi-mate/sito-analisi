Two points on the surface of a charged conductor in electrostatic equilibrium in vacuum are compared. The field just outside the second point has magnitude $E_2$ and is stronger than at the first point. Given the field magnitude at the second point $E_{2} = 500\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the ratio of the surface density magnitudes, second to first $b = \dfrac{5}{2}$, what is the field magnitude at the first point $E_{1}$?

- $100 \cdot \sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $400\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $200\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $1250\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

The field just outside has magnitude $\lvert\sigma\rvert/\varepsilon_0$, because the field inside the conductor is zero. The ratio of the densities equals the ratio of the fields. In the indicated units, $b = \dfrac{E_{2}}{E_{1}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $E_{1} = \dfrac{E_{2}}{b}$. Using the given values, we obtain $200\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
