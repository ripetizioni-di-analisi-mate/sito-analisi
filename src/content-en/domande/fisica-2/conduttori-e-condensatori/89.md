A dielectric slab that fills the whole thickness between the plates is partly inserted into a charged, isolated parallel-plate capacitor. Neglecting friction, what force does the electric field exert on the slab?

- A force that tends to pull the slab into the capacitor
- A force that tends to push the slab out of the capacitor
- No force, because the slab is electrically neutral
- A nonzero force only if the capacitor is connected to a source

## Explanation

The partly filled capacitor is equivalent to two capacitors in parallel, so its capacitance grows as the slab enters, because the part of the area occupied by the dielectric increases.

The capacitor is isolated, therefore the charge $Q$ stays constant and the energy $U = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C}$ decreases as the slab enters. No source does work, so the work of the field on the slab equals the decrease of $U$ and is positive for a displacement inward.

Therefore the force of the field **tends to pull the slab into** the capacitor.
