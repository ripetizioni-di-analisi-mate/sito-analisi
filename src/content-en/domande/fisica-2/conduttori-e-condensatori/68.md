The plates of a parallel-plate capacitor are $6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart with a potential difference of $120\,\mathrm{V}$ between them, and the negative plate is at potential $0$. Neglecting edge effects, what is the potential at a point between the plates $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the negative plate?

- $40\,\mathrm{V}$
- $80\,\mathrm{V}$
- $60\,\mathrm{V}$
- $120\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Between the plates the field is uniform, directed from the positive plate to the negative one, and equals
$$
E = \frac{\Delta V}{d} = \frac{120}{6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
Let $x$ be the distance of the point $P$ from the negative plate. Integrating the field from $P$ to the negative plate along a segment perpendicular to the plates gives $V(P) - 0 = E\,x$, so the potential grows linearly with $x$.

Therefore $V(P) = 2.0 \cdot 10^{4} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} = 40\,\mathrm{V}$.
