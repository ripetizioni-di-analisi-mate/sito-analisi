A neutral conducting spherical shell contains a point charge $q$ in its cavity. The charge is moved from the center of the cavity to a point near the wall, without touching it. At equilibrium, what is the electric field outside the shell like?

- It is stronger on the side toward which the charge was moved
- It is zero, because the shell is an electrostatic shield
- It is radial with respect to the position of the charge $q$
- It is radial with respect to the center of the shell, equal to the field of a charge $q$ placed at the center

## Explanation

The charge $-q$ is induced on the wall of the cavity and, since the shell is neutral, the charge $+q$ lies on the outer surface.

The charge $q$ and the charge induced on the wall of the cavity together produce zero field at every point of the material of the shell and outside it, whatever the position of $q$. The field outside is therefore produced only by the charge on the outer surface.

The outer surface is an equipotential sphere and there are no other charges outside the shell, therefore the charge $+q$ distributes uniformly on it. Consequently the field outside is **radial with respect to the center of the shell** and coincides with that of a charge $q$ placed at the center, as if the charge had not been moved.
