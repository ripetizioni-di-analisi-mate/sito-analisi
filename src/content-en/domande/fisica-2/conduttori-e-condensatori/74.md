In a parallel-plate capacitor, half the area is filled with a linear dielectric occupying the full thickness. The other half is in vacuum. Edge effects are neglected. Initially, the given quantities are the capacitance of the entire capacitor in vacuum $C_{0} = 10\,\mathrm{pF}$ and the relative permittivity of the dielectric $\epsilon = 5$. In the second configuration, we change the relative permittivity of the dielectric to $3$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $C$?

- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{9}{10}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The two halves have the same voltage, so they are equivalent to capacitances in parallel. Their values are $C_0/2$ and $\varepsilon C_0/2$. Applying $C = \dfrac{C_{0} \cdot \left(\epsilon + 1\right)}{2}$ gives $30\,\mathrm{pF}$ before the change and $20\,\mathrm{pF}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
