An ideal capacitor is charged and then isolated. Its capacitance and the magnitude of the charge on one plate are known. Given the capacitance $C = 6\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ and the stored energy $U = 12\,\mathrm{\mu J}$, what is the charge magnitude $q$?

- $12 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $24\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $12\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $6 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{\mu C}$

## Explanation

The total work required to charge it is obtained by integrating $q'/C$ from zero to $q$. The result is $q^2/(2C)$. In the indicated units, $U = \dfrac{q^{2}}{2 \cdot C}$. Solving for the unknown gives $q = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{C} \cdot \sqrt{U}$. Using the given values, we obtain $12\,\mathrm{\mu C}$.
