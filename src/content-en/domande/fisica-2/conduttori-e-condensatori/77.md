Two initially uncharged capacitors of $10\,\mathrm{nF}$ and $40\,\mathrm{nF}$ are connected in series to a $100\,\mathrm{V}$ source. What is the potential difference across the $40\,\mathrm{nF}$ capacitor?

- $80\,\mathrm{V}$
- $20\,\mathrm{V}$
- $50\,\mathrm{V}$
- $25\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Capacitors in series, initially uncharged, carry the same charge $Q$, so $\Delta V_1 = \dfrac{Q}{C_1}$ and $\Delta V_2 = \dfrac{Q}{C_2}$. The sum of the two voltages is the source voltage, therefore
$$
Q \left( \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} \right) = \Delta V
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta V_2 = \frac{Q}{C_2} = \Delta V\,\frac{C_1}{C_1 + C_2} .
$$
With the data, $\Delta V_2 = 100\,\mathrm{V} \cdot \dfrac{10}{50} = 20\,\mathrm{V}$. The capacitor with the larger capacitance therefore has the smaller voltage.
