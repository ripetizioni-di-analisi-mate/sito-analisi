The charge of an isolated conducting sphere is increased from $Q$ to $2Q$. Which statement is true?

- The capacitance stays the same and the potential doubles
- The capacitance doubles and the potential stays the same
- The capacitance stays the same and the potential halves
- The capacitance and the potential both double

## Explanation

The capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere of radius $R$ is $C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 R$, so it depends only on the radius and not on the charge. Multiplying the charge by $2$ multiplies the density $\sigma$, the field and the potential by $2$, because the field depends linearly on the charges.

Therefore the **capacitance stays the same** and the potential $V = \dfrac{Q}{C}$ doubles.
