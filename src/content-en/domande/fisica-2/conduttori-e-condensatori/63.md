An isolated conductor of arbitrary shape, in vacuum, has capacitance $C$. A second conductor is built with the same shape and with all its dimensions multiplied by $2$. What is the capacitance of the second conductor?

- $4C$
- $C$
- $\dfrac{C}{2}$
- $2C$

## Explanation

Let the same charge $Q$ be distributed on the enlarged conductor in the same way, that is, with the same charge $dq$ on each corresponding portion of the surface.

The potential at a point of the surface is $V = k \displaystyle\int \frac{dq}{r}$, and every distance $r$ is multiplied by $2$, so the potential becomes $\dfrac{V}{2}$ at every point of the surface. The surface is still equipotential, therefore this is the equilibrium distribution.

It follows that the capacitance of the enlarged conductor is $\dfrac{2Q}{V} = 2C$. Indeed, for the sphere one finds $C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 R$, proportional to the radius.
