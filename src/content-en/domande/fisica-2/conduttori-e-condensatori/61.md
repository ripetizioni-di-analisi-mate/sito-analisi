An isolated conducting sphere of radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, in vacuum, carries the charge $10\,\mathrm{nC}$. How much energy is stored in its electric field?

- $9.0\,\mu\mathrm{J}$
- $4.5\,\mu\mathrm{J}$
- $45\,\mu\mathrm{J}$
- $2.2\,\mu\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The isolated sphere is a conductor of capacitance $C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 R = \dfrac{R}{k}$. The energy of a charged conductor is the work needed to bring the charge $Q$ onto it, that is $U = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C}$, by the same reasoning used for the capacitor.

Therefore
$$
U = \frac{kQ^2}{2R} = \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot (1.0 \cdot 10^{-8})^2}{2 \cdot 0.10}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 4.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{J} = 4.5\,\mu\mathrm{J} .
$$
