An isolated conducting sphere is in vacuum and the potential is measured relative to infinity. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the sphere radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the sphere charge $q = 2\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the sphere potential $U_{e}$?

- $360\,\mathrm{V}$
- $18\,\mathrm{V}$
- $180\,\mathrm{V}$
- $90\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Outside, the sphere behaves like a point charge at its center. At the surface, the potential is $kq/R$. In the indicated units, $q = \dfrac{R \cdot U_{e}}{900}$. Solving for the unknown gives $U_{e} = \dfrac{900 \cdot q}{R}$. Using the given values, we obtain $180\,\mathrm{V}$.
