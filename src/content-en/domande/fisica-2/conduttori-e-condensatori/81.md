A dielectric with $\varepsilon_r = 3.0$ and dielectric strength $1.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{V/m}$ fills a capacitor. What is the maximum energy density the electric field can have in the dielectric?

- $4.4 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J/m^3}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J/m^3}$
- $2.7 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J/m^3}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J/m^3}$

## Explanation

In a dielectric of constant $\varepsilon_r$ the energy density of the field is $u = \dfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r E^2$. The field cannot exceed the dielectric strength, therefore the maximum density occurs for $E = E_{\max}$:
$$
u_{\max} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 3.0 \cdot (1.0 \cdot 10^{7})^2\,\mathrm{J/m^3} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J/m^3} .
$$
