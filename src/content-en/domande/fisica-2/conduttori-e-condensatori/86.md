A parallel-plate capacitor has its plates $d = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart and contains, parallel to the plates, a dielectric slab with $\varepsilon_r = 4.0$ and thickness $s = 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ that covers the whole area. The potential difference between the plates is $150\,\mathrm{V}$. Neglecting edge effects, what is the electric field in the slab?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,3;3,0 3,3" poligoni="0.5,0 2.5,0 2.5,3 0.5,3" segmenti="0,-0.4,3,-0.4;0.5,3.4,2.5,3.4" etichette="1.5,1.5,εr = 4;-0.3,3.2,+;3.3,3.2,−;1.5,-0.8,d;1.5,3.75,s" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-1.2 ymax=4.1 titolo="Dielectric slab of thickness $s$ between the plates of a parallel-plate capacitor with separation $d$"}

- $5.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

Let $E_0$ be the field in the empty regions; in the slab the field is $\dfrac{E_0}{\varepsilon_r}$, because the free charge of the plates is the same but the dielectric becomes polarized. The empty regions have total thickness $d - s = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, so
$$
\Delta V = E_0\,(d - s) + \frac{E_0}{\varepsilon_r}\,s = E_0\,(1.0 + 0.50) \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
It follows that $E_0 = \dfrac{150}{1.5 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$, therefore in the slab the field is $\dfrac{E_0}{4.0} = 2.5 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$.
