A coaxial cable in vacuum has an inner conductor of radius $a$ and an outer conductor of inner radius $b = 5a$. The inner conductor carries the charge $\lambda = 10\,\mathrm{nC/m}$ per unit length and the outer one the opposite charge. Neglecting edge effects, how much energy is stored per unit length of cable?

- $2.9\,\mu\mathrm{J/m}$
- $0.90\,\mu\mathrm{J/m}$
- $0.72\,\mu\mathrm{J/m}$
- $1.4\,\mu\mathrm{J/m}$

## Explanation

A piece of cable of length $\ell$ is a cylindrical capacitor of capacitance $C = \dfrac{2\pi\varepsilon_0\,\ell}{\ln \dfrac{b}{a}}$, with charge $Q = \lambda\ell$. Its energy is therefore
$$
U = \frac{Q^2}{2C} = \frac{\lambda^2 \ell\,\ln \dfrac{b}{a}}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = k\,\lambda^2 \ell\,\ln\frac{b}{a} .
$$
Hence the energy per unit length is
$$
\frac{U}{\ell} = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot (1.0 \cdot 10^{-8})^2 \cdot \ln 5\,\mathrm{J/m} \approx 1.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{J/m} .
$$
