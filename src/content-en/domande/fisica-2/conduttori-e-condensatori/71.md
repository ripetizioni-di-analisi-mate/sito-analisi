A coaxial cable in vacuum has an inner conductor of radius $a = 0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and an outer conductor of inner radius $b = 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$; the potential difference between the two conductors is $277\,\mathrm{V}$. What is the maximum magnitude of the electric field between the conductors?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $5.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

Let $\lambda$ be the charge per unit length of the inner conductor. By Gauss's law, between the conductors $E(r) = \dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$, and integrating from $a$ to $b$ gives $\Delta V = \dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0} \ln\dfrac{b}{a}$. Eliminating $\lambda$,
$$
E(r) = \frac{\Delta V}{r \ln \dfrac{b}{a}} .
$$
The field decreases with $r$, so it is maximum on the surface of the inner conductor, for $r = a$:
$$
E_{\max} = \frac{277}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot \ln 4}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 4.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
