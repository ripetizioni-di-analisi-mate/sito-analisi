The space between the plates of a parallel-plate capacitor is filled with two dielectric layers parallel to the plates, of equal thickness, with $\varepsilon_r = 2.0$ for the first and $\varepsilon_r = 6.0$ for the second. The same capacitor in vacuum has capacitance $C_0 = 10\,\mathrm{pF}$. Neglecting edge effects, what is the capacitance?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,3;4,0 4,3" poligoni="0,0 2,0 2,3 0,3;2,0 4,0 4,3 2,3" segmenti="2,0,2,3" etichette="1,1.5,εr = 2;3,1.5,εr = 6;-0.3,3.2,+;4.3,3.2,−" xmin=-0.8 xmax=4.8 ymin=-0.4 ymax=3.6 titolo="Two dielectric layers of equal thickness between the plates of a parallel-plate capacitor"}

- $40\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $80\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $20\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $30\,\mathrm{pF}$

## Explanation

The boundary between the two layers is parallel to the plates and the field is perpendicular to it, so the capacitor is equivalent to two capacitors in series, each of thickness $\dfrac{d}{2}$.

A layer of thickness $\dfrac{d}{2}$ and constant $\varepsilon_r$ has capacitance $\dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d/2} = 2\varepsilon_r C_0$, therefore the two layers have $40\,\mathrm{pF}$ and $120\,\mathrm{pF}$. The capacitance of the series combination is
$$
C = \frac{40 \cdot 120}{40 + 120}\,\mathrm{pF} = 30\,\mathrm{pF} .
$$
