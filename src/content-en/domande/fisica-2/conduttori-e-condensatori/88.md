Two points on the surface of a charged conductor in electrostatic equilibrium in vacuum are compared. The field just outside the second point has magnitude $E_2$ and is stronger than at the first point. Given the field magnitude at the first point $E_{1} = 200\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the field magnitude at the second point $E_{2} = 500\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the ratio of the surface density magnitudes, second to first $b$?

- $\dfrac{25}{4}$
- $5$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$

## Explanation

The field just outside has magnitude $\lvert\sigma\rvert/\varepsilon_0$, because the field inside the conductor is zero. The ratio of the densities equals the ratio of the fields. In the indicated units, $b = \dfrac{E_{2}}{E_{1}}$. Using the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{5}{2}$.
