An isolated conducting sphere of radius $R = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ has surface charge density $\sigma = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$. What is the magnitude of the electric field at $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center?

- $2.3 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $7.5 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

The charge of the sphere is $Q = 4\pi R^2 \sigma$, distributed uniformly on the surface. By **Gauss's law**, at a distance $r > R$ the field is that of a point charge $Q$ at the center:
$$
E(r) = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{R^2}{r^2} .
$$
For $r = R$ this gives back the value $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ of Coulomb's theorem; at $r = 3R$ the field is nine times smaller:
$$
E = \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}} \cdot \frac{1}{9}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
