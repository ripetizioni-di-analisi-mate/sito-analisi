Between the plates of a parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum, of area $A = 0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $d = 4.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart, an uncharged copper slab of area $A$ and thickness $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ is inserted parallel to the plates. Neglecting edge effects, what is the capacitance?

- $59\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $44\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $35\,\mathrm{pF}$

## Explanation

In the copper slab the field is zero and the charges $-Q$ and $+Q$ are induced on its faces, facing the charges of the plates. The system is therefore equivalent to two vacuum capacitors in series, whose thicknesses add up to $d - s = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$.

The capacitance of the series combination is therefore that of a parallel-plate capacitor of thickness $d - s$:
$$
C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - s} = \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.020}{3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 5.9 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{F} = 59\,\mathrm{pF} ,
$$
whatever the position of the slab.
