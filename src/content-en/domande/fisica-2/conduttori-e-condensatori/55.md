A thin isolated conducting sheet has two faces of area $0.50\,\mathrm{m^2}$ each and carries the charge $Q = 8.85\,\mathrm{nC}$. Neglecting edge effects, what is the magnitude of the electric field near the center of one face, outside the sheet?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

The charge of a conductor in equilibrium lies on its surface and, by symmetry, divides equally between the two faces. Each face therefore has density
$$
\sigma = \frac{Q}{2A} = \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}}{2 \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} .
$$
By **Coulomb's theorem** the field just outside a face is perpendicular to the sheet and equals
$$
E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
The same value is obtained by adding the fields $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ of the two faces, which have the same direction outside the sheet.
