A charged spherical capacitor, in vacuum, has plates of radii $R_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $R_2 = 12\,\mathrm{cm}$. At what distance from the center is the magnitude of the electric field between the plates one quarter of its maximum value?

- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $4.2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $7.5\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Between the plates a spherical surface of radius $r$ encloses only the charge $Q$ of the inner sphere, so by **Gauss's law** the field is radial with magnitude $E(r) = \dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$.

The field decreases with $r$, therefore its maximum value occurs on the inner sphere and equals $E_{\max} = \dfrac{kQ}{R_1^2}$.

The condition $E(r) = \dfrac{E_{\max}}{4}$ is equivalent to $r^2 = 4R_1^2$, so $r = 2R_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, which lies between $R_1$ and $R_2$.
