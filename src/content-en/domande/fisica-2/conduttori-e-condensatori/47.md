A conducting spherical shell is connected to ground by a wire. A point charge $q > 0$ sits at the center of the cavity. At equilibrium, which statement is true?

- The outer surface carries the charge $+q$ and outside the field is that of a charge $q$ placed at the center
- The inner surface is uncharged and the charge $-q$ lies on the outer surface
- The outer surface is uncharged and the field outside is zero
- The field in the cavity is zero, because the shell is grounded

## Explanation

A closed surface lying inside the material of the shell has zero flux, so it encloses zero total charge; therefore the wall of the cavity carries the charge $-q$.

The ground connection brings the shell to the potential of the earth, which is taken equal to the potential at infinity, that is $V = 0$. Let $Q_e$ be the charge on the outer surface, of radius $R$. By spherical symmetry, outside the shell the field is that of the total charge $q - q + Q_e = Q_e$ placed at the center, so the potential of the shell is $\dfrac{kQ_e}{R}$. Since this potential is zero, $Q_e = 0$.

Therefore the **outer surface is uncharged and the field outside is zero**, while in the cavity the field of the charge $q$ remains nonzero.
