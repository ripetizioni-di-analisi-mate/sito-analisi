Eight identical spherical drops of mercury, far from each other, each have potential $10\,\mathrm{V}$ with respect to infinity. The drops merge into a single spherical drop without losing charge. What is the potential of the final drop?

- $80\,\mathrm{V}$
- $40\,\mathrm{V}$
- $20\,\mathrm{V}$
- $10\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Let $r$ be the radius and $q$ the charge of one drop. Each drop is a conducting sphere, so $V_0 = \dfrac{kq}{r} = 10\,\mathrm{V}$.

The volume is conserved, therefore the radius $R$ of the final drop satisfies $R^3 = 8r^3$, that is $R = 2r$. The charge is conserved too, so the final drop carries $Q = 8q$.

The potential of the final drop is then
$$
V = \frac{kQ}{R} = \frac{8kq}{2r} = 4V_0 = 40\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
