An isolated conducting spherical shell of outer radius $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ has net charge $Q_g = +3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. A point charge $q = -5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ sits in the cavity. What is the electric field at $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center of the shell?

- $3.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V/m}$, directed outward
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V/m}$, directed toward the center
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V/m}$, directed toward the center
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V/m}$, directed outward

## Explanation

A closed surface lying inside the material of the shell has zero flux, so it encloses zero total charge; therefore the charge $-q = +5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is induced on the wall of the cavity. The net charge of the shell is conserved, so the outer surface carries $Q_g - 5.0\,\mathrm{nC} = -2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$.

The spherical surface of radius $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ encloses the total charge $q + Q_g = -2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, distributed with spherical symmetry on the outer surface of the shell. By **Gauss's law**
$$
E = k\,\frac{|q + Q_g|}{r^2} = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{(0.30)^2}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 2.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
The enclosed charge is negative, therefore the field is **directed toward the center**.
