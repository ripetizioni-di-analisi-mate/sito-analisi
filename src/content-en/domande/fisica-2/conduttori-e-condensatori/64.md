A $2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor withstands at most $100\,\mathrm{V}$ and a $6.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor withstands at most $200\,\mathrm{V}$. The two capacitors, initially uncharged, are connected in series. What is the maximum potential difference that can be applied to the series combination?

- $1.3 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Capacitors in series, initially uncharged, carry the same charge $Q$. The $2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor reaches $100\,\mathrm{V}$ with the charge $200\,\mu\mathrm{C}$, while the $6.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor reaches $200\,\mathrm{V}$ with the charge $1200\,\mu\mathrm{C}$.

Therefore the common charge cannot exceed $200\,\mu\mathrm{C}$, and with this charge the $6.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor has the potential difference $\dfrac{200\,\mu\mathrm{C}}{6.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}} \approx 33\,\mathrm{V}$.

The maximum potential difference that can be applied to the series combination is thus
$$
\Delta V_{\max} = 100\,\mathrm{V} + 33\,\mathrm{V} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V} ,
$$
less than the sum of the two maximum voltages.
