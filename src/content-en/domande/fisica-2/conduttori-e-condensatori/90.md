In a parallel-plate capacitor there is a dielectric slab parallel to the plates, with the same area as the plates and a thickness smaller than their distance, placed halfway between the plates. The slab is moved, always parallel to the plates, until it touches one of them. Neglecting edge effects, what happens to the capacitance?

- It increases
- It stays the same
- It decreases
- It becomes equal to that of the capacitor completely filled with dielectric

## Explanation

With a slab of thickness $s$ and constant $\varepsilon_r$ parallel to the plates the capacitance is
$$
C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - s + \dfrac{s}{\varepsilon_r}} ,
$$
which depends on the thickness of the slab and not on its position. Moving the slab only changes how the empty region, of total length $d - s$, is divided between the two sides of the slab.

Therefore the capacitance **stays the same**.
