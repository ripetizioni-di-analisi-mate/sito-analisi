A charged, isolated parallel-plate capacitor has a field of $3.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$ between its plates. Neglecting edge effects, what is the field at a point outside the capacitor, $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the outer face of the positive plate?

- $1.5 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

The charges $+Q$ and $-Q$ lie on the facing sides of the plates, with densities $\pm\sigma$. Each face, treated as an infinite plane, produces on both sides a field of magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$, pointing away from the positive face and toward the negative face.

Outside the capacitor the two fields have the same magnitude and opposite directions, so they cancel; between the plates they have the same direction and give $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$. Therefore at the outside point the field is **zero**, at any distance from the plate.
