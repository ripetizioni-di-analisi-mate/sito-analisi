A capacitor consists of three parallel flat plates, each of area $A = 0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$, at a distance $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from one another, in vacuum. The two outer plates are connected to each other and to terminal $B$, the middle one to terminal $A$. Neglecting edge effects, what is the capacitance between $A$ and $B$?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,3;2,0 2,3;4,0 4,3;0,3 0,4 4,4 4,3;2,4 2,4.7;2,0 2,-0.8" punti="2,4.7,pieno,B;2,-0.8,pieno,A" etichette="1,1.5,d;3,1.5,d" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1.3 ymax=5.2 titolo="Three parallel plates: the two outer ones connected to $B$, the middle one connected to $A$"}

- $89\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $44\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $3.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{pF}$

## Explanation

The middle plate forms with each outer plate a parallel-plate capacitor of capacitance $\dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$; the charge of the middle plate divides between its two faces.

The two capacitors share the middle plate, connected to $A$, and have their outer plates both connected to $B$, so they have the same potential difference. Therefore they are **in parallel** and
$$
C = \frac{2\varepsilon_0 A}{d} = \frac{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.010}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F} = 1.8 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{pF} .
$$
