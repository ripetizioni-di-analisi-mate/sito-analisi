An uncharged, very large conducting slab is placed in a uniform electric field of magnitude $E_0 = 5.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$, perpendicular to its faces. What is the induced charge density on the face through which the field lines leave the slab?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 unicolore=1 poligoni="2,0 3,0 3,3 2,3" vettori="0,0.5,1.8,0.5;0,1.5,1.8,1.5;0,2.5,1.8,2.5,E₀;3.2,0.5,5,0.5;3.2,1.5,5,1.5;3.2,2.5,5,2.5,E₀" etichette="2.5,3.35,slab" xmin=-0.3 xmax=5.4 ymin=-0.3 ymax=3.7 titolo="Uncharged conducting slab in a uniform field $\vec{E}_0$ perpendicular to its faces"}

- $-0.44\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $+0.22\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $+0.89\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $+0.44\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2}$

## Explanation

By electrostatic induction opposite charges, of densities $\pm\sigma$, form on the two faces and cancel the field inside the slab. Outside the slab the field of these two opposite distributions is zero, so outside the slab the field remains $E_0$.

On the exit face the outside field points away from the slab, that is along the outward normal $\hat{n}$. By **Coulomb's theorem** $\vec{E} = \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}\,\hat{n}$, therefore on that face the density is positive and equals
$$
\sigma = \varepsilon_0 E_0 = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} \approx +0.44\,\mu\mathrm{C/m^2} .
$$
