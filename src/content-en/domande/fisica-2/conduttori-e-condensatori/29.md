A coaxial capacitor has length $L$ and radii $a,b$. Only $L$ is doubled, neglecting edge effects. If its initial capacitance is $25\,\mathrm{pF}$, what does it become?

- $50\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $25\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $100\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $200\,\mathrm{pF}$

## Explanation

The capacitance of a coaxial capacitor is $C=\dfrac{2\pi\varepsilon L}{\ln(\dfrac{b}{a})}$. With the radii and medium fixed, it is proportional to the length. The result is $50\,\mathrm{pF}$.
