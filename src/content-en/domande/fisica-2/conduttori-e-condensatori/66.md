A charged parallel-plate capacitor contains, parallel to the plates, a dielectric slab with $\varepsilon_r = 4.0$ that covers the whole area and fills half of the distance between the plates; the rest is vacuum. Neglecting edge effects, what fraction of the energy of the electric field lies in the vacuum region?

- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{16}{17}$

## Explanation

Let $E_0$ be the field in the vacuum region. In the slab the field is $\dfrac{E_0}{\varepsilon_r}$, because the free charge on the plates is the same and the dielectric becomes polarized.

The energy density is $\dfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E_0^2$ in the vacuum region and $\dfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r \left( \dfrac{E_0}{\varepsilon_r} \right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_r} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E_0^2$ in the slab. The two regions have the same volume, so their energies are in the ratio of the densities, that is, as $1$ to $\dfrac{1}{4}$.

Therefore the fraction of the energy in the vacuum region is
$$
\frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{1}{4}} = \frac{4}{5} .
$$
