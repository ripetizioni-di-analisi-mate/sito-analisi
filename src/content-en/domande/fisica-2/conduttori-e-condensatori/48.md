An uncharged, isolated small conducting sphere is brought near a fixed positive point charge. What happens to the small sphere?

- It is repelled by the charge
- It experiences no force, because its total charge is zero
- It is attracted by the charge only if it is grounded
- It is attracted by the charge

## Explanation

The field of the positive charge moves the free charges of the small sphere, which stays neutral. By electrostatic induction a negative charge forms on the side facing the charge and a positive charge of equal magnitude on the opposite side.

The induced negative charge is closer to the fixed charge than the positive one. Since the Coulomb force decreases with the square of the distance, the attraction on the near charge is greater than the repulsion on the far charge.

Therefore the small sphere is **attracted**, even though its total charge is zero.
