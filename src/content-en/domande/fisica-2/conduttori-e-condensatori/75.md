In a parallel-plate capacitor, half the area is filled with a linear dielectric occupying the full thickness. The other half is in vacuum. Edge effects are neglected. Initially, the given quantities are the capacitance of the entire capacitor in vacuum $C_{0} = 10\,\mathrm{pF}$ and the relative permittivity of the dielectric $\epsilon = 5$. In the second configuration, we change the capacitance of the entire capacitor in vacuum to $8\,\mathrm{pF}$ and the relative permittivity of the dielectric to $7$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $C$?

- $2\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $6\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $- \dfrac{8}{3}\,\mathrm{pF}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{pF}$

## Explanation

The two halves have the same voltage, so they are equivalent to capacitances in parallel. Their values are $C_0/2$ and $\varepsilon C_0/2$. Applying $C = \dfrac{C_{0} \cdot \left(\epsilon + 1\right)}{2}$ gives $30\,\mathrm{pF}$ before the change and $32\,\mathrm{pF}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $2\,\mathrm{pF}$.
