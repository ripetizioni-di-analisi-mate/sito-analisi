Three identical capacitors of $6.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ each are available. Which equivalent capacitance can *not* be obtained by connecting all three?

- $12\,\mu\mathrm{F}$
- $9.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$
- $4.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$
- $2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$

## Explanation

With three identical capacitors of capacitance $C = 6.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ there are four possible connections.

- All in parallel: $3C = 18\,\mu\mathrm{F}$.
- All in series: $\dfrac{C}{3} = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$.
- Two in series, that is $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$, in parallel with the third: $9.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$.
- Two in parallel, that is $12\,\mu\mathrm{F}$, in series with the third: $\dfrac{12 \cdot 6.0}{18}\,\mu\mathrm{F} = 4.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$.

The capacitance of $12\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is obtained only with two capacitors in parallel, leaving the third disconnected, therefore it **cannot be obtained using all three**.
