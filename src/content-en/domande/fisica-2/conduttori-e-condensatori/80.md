An isolated conducting sphere is in vacuum and the potential is measured relative to infinity. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the sphere potential $U_{e} = 180\,\mathrm{V}$ and the sphere charge $q = 2\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the sphere radius $R$?

- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Outside, the sphere behaves like a point charge at its center. At the surface, the potential is $kq/R$. In the indicated units, $q = \dfrac{R \cdot U_{e}}{900}$. Solving for the unknown gives $R = \dfrac{900 \cdot q}{U_{e}}$. Using the given values, we obtain $10\,\mathrm{cm}$.
