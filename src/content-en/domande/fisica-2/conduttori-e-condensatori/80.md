The dielectric of a parallel-plate capacitor is a polyethylene film $0.20\,\mathrm{mm}$ thick, with $\varepsilon_r = 2.3$ and dielectric strength $2.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{V/m}$. What is the maximum potential difference that can be applied to the plates?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{kV}$
- $1.7\,\mathrm{kV}$
- $9.2\,\mathrm{kV}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{kV}$

## Explanation

The film fills the space between the plates, so in the dielectric the field is uniform and equals $E = \dfrac{\Delta V}{d}$, whatever $\varepsilon_r$ is. The dielectric does not become a conductor as long as $E \leq E_{\max}$, therefore the maximum potential difference is
$$
\Delta V_{\max} = E_{\max}\,d = 2.0 \cdot 10^{7} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The constant $\varepsilon_r$ determines the capacitance and the charge, but it does not enter this calculation.
