A battery, modeled as a real source, delivers $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$ with $10\,\mathrm{V}$ across its terminals and $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ with $8.0\,\mathrm{V}$ across its terminals, as in the graph. What current would it deliver with the terminals connected by a wire of negligible resistance?

::grafico{punti="1,10,pieno;3,8,pieno" segmenti="1,0,1,10;0,10,1,10;3,0,3,8;0,8,3,8" xmin=-0.6 xmax=12.6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=12.6 nomi="I,ΔV" titolo="Terminal voltage of a battery, measured with two different loads: $I$ in amperes, $\Delta V$ in volts"}

- $11\,\mathrm{A}$
- $5.5\,\mathrm{A}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

For a real source $\Delta V = \mathcal{E} - r I$, so the measured points lie on a straight line with slope $-r$. From the two points we get $r = \dfrac{10 - 8.0}{3.0 - 1.0}\,\Omega = 1.0\,\Omega$, and then $\mathcal{E} = 10\,\mathrm{V} + 1.0\,\Omega \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{A} = 11\,\mathrm{V}$. With the terminals short-circuited $\Delta V = 0$, therefore the current is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{r} = 11\,\mathrm{A}$, that is, the intersection of the line with the current axis. The value $5.5\,\mathrm{A}$ is the current $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{2r}$, which occurs with a load equal to the internal resistance.
