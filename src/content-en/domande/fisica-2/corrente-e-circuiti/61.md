A power line with total resistance $2.0\,\Omega$ carries a power of $100\,\mathrm{kW}$ to a user, who receives it at a voltage of $10\,\mathrm{kV}$. How much power is dissipated in the line?

- $5.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{W}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{W}$
- $20\,\mathrm{W}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The line is in series with the user, so it carries the same current, $I = \dfrac{P}{\Delta V} = \dfrac{1.0 \cdot 10^{5}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{4}}\,\mathrm{A} = 10\,\mathrm{A}$. By the Joule effect the power dissipated in the line is
$$
P_{\ell} = R_{\ell}\, I^2 = 2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The value $5.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{W}$ is obtained with $\dfrac{\Delta V^2}{R_\ell}$, but $10\,\mathrm{kV}$ is the voltage at the user, not the voltage across the line. This is why energy is transmitted at high voltage: for the same power the current is small and the losses $R_\ell I^2$ are reduced.
