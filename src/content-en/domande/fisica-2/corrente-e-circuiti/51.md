A nichrome wire, with resistivity $1.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\Omega \cdot \mathrm{m}$ and diameter $0.80\,\mathrm{mm}$, carries a steady current of $2.2\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the magnitude of the electric field inside the wire?

- $4.8\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $19\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

The cross section has radius $0.40\,\mathrm{mm}$, so its area is $A = \pi\,(0.40 \cdot 10^{-3})^2\,\mathrm{m^2} \approx 5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. By **Ohm's law in local form** $E = \rho J = \dfrac{\rho I}{A}$, therefore
$$
E = \frac{1.1 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 2.2}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 4.8\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
The field is nonzero because a wire carrying a current is not in electrostatic equilibrium. The value $1.2\,\mathrm{V/m}$ is obtained by using the diameter instead of the radius in the area.
