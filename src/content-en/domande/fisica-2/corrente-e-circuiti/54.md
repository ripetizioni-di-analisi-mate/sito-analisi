A graphite block shaped as a rectangular box has edges of $1\,\mathrm{cm}$, $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The resistance is measured between two opposite faces, covered by metal plates of negligible resistance. Between which faces is the resistance smallest?

- Between the two $2\,\mathrm{cm} \times 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ faces
- Between the two $1\,\mathrm{cm} \times 2\,\mathrm{cm}$ faces
- Between the two $1\,\mathrm{cm} \times 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ faces
- The resistance is the same for every pair of faces

## Explanation

Between two opposite faces the current crosses the block perpendicularly to them, so the resistance is $R = \rho\,\dfrac{\ell}{A}$, with $A$ the area of the faces and $\ell$ the perpendicular edge. For the $2 \times 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ faces we have $\ell = 1\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $A = 8\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, and the ratio $\dfrac{\ell}{A}$ is $\dfrac{1}{8}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. For the $1 \times 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ faces it is $\dfrac{2}{4}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and for the $1 \times 2\,\mathrm{cm}$ faces it is $\dfrac{4}{2}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. Therefore the resistance is **smallest between the $2\,\mathrm{cm} \times 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ faces**, which have the largest area and the shortest distance.
