In the Drude model, what is the mean time between two collisions for the conduction electrons of copper, which has resistivity $1.7 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\Omega \cdot \mathrm{m}$ and $n = 8.5 \cdot 10^{28}$ conduction electrons per cubic meter? Use $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$ and $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

- $4.9 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4.1 \cdot 10^{13}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.9 \cdot 10^{-33}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

In the Drude model the resistivity is $\rho = \dfrac{m_e}{n e^2 t_c}$, so the mean time between two collisions is $t_c = \dfrac{m_e}{n e^2 \rho}$. Substituting the values,
$$
t_c = \frac{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}{8.5 \cdot 10^{28} \cdot (1.60 \cdot 10^{-19})^2 \cdot 1.7 \cdot 10^{-8}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $4.1 \cdot 10^{13}\,\mathrm{s}$ is the reciprocal of the result, and $3.9 \cdot 10^{-33}\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained with the charge $e$ not squared.
