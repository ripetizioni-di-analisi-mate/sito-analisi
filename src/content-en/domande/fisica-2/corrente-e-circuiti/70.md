An ohmic resistor remains at constant temperature. Calculate the dissipated power from the known voltage and resistance. Initially, the given quantities are the voltage across it $U_{e} = 24\,\mathrm{V}$ and the resistance $R_{e} = 12\,\Omega$. In the second configuration, we change the voltage across it to $18\,\mathrm{V}$ and the resistance to $18\,\Omega$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $P$?

- $30\,\mathrm{W}$
- $-30\,\mathrm{W}$
- $-15\,\mathrm{W}$
- $36\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

For Joule heating, $P=U_e I_e$. Ohm's law allows the substitution $I_e=U_e/R_e$. Applying $P = \dfrac{U_{e}^{2}}{R_{e}}$ gives $48\,\mathrm{W}$ before the change and $18\,\mathrm{W}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $-30\,\mathrm{W}$.
