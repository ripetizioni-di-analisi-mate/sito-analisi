An ideal $3.0\,\mathrm{V}$ source supplies a $2.5\,\Omega$ resistor. To measure the current, an ammeter with internal resistance $0.50\,\Omega$ is inserted in series. What current does the ammeter read?

- $1.2\,\mathrm{A}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{A}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

The ammeter is in series with the resistor, so its internal resistance adds to that of the circuit. The current flowing through it, which is therefore its reading, is
$$
I = \frac{3.0}{2.5 + 0.50}\,\mathrm{A} = 1.0\,\mathrm{A} .
$$
Without the ammeter the current would be $\dfrac{3.0}{2.5}\,\mathrm{A} = 1.2\,\mathrm{A}$: the measurement is altered because the resistance of the ammeter is not small compared with that of the branch.
