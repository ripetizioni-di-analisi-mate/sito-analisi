Twelve identical resistors, $12\,\Omega$ each, are placed along the edges of a cube, and the vertices of the cube are the nodes. What is the equivalent resistance between two opposite vertices, that is, at the ends of a diagonal of the cube?

- $7.0\,\Omega$
- $10\,\Omega$
- $6.0\,\Omega$
- $4.0\,\Omega$

## Explanation

Apply the voltage between the opposite vertices $A$ and $B$ and go through the cube from $A$ to $B$. By symmetry the three vertices adjacent to $A$ are at the same potential, and so are the three vertices adjacent to $B$; the current in resistors between points at the same potential is zero, so the equipotential vertices can be joined. The cube becomes the series of three groups: $3$ resistors in parallel, then $6$ in parallel, then $3$ in parallel. Therefore
$$
R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{R}{3} + \frac{R}{6} + \frac{R}{3} = \frac{5}{6}\, R = 10\,\Omega .
$$
The value $7.0\,\Omega$ is the resistance between the ends of an edge, and $4.0\,\Omega$ is the parallel combination of only the three resistors leaving $A$.
