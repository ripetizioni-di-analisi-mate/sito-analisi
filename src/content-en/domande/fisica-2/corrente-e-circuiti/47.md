A uniform wire of constant cross section conducts a steady current. The current density is uniform across each cross section. Given the cross-sectional area $A = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ and the current $I_{e} = 3\,\mathrm{A}$, what is the magnitude of the current density $j$?

- $9\,\mathrm{A/mm^2}$
- $12\,\mathrm{A/mm^2}$
- $6\,\mathrm{A/mm^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{A/mm^2}$

## Explanation

The current is the flux of current density. Since the density is uniform and parallel to the normal, it is multiplied by the area. In the indicated units, $I_{e} = A \cdot j$. Solving for the unknown gives $j = \dfrac{I_{e}}{A}$. Using the given values, we obtain $6\,\mathrm{A/mm^2}$.
