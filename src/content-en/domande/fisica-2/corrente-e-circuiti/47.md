The graph shows the current in a conductor between $t = 0$ and $t = 6\,\mathrm{s}$. How much charge crosses a cross section of the conductor in this interval?

::grafico{f="(x>=0&&x<=6?1:NaN)*(x<=4?1.5*x:18-3*x)" punti="4,6,pieno" segmenti="4,0,4,6" xmin=-0.6 xmax=7.6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=7.4 nomi="t,i" titolo="Current in a conductor, with $t$ in seconds and $i$ in amperes: it increases linearly up to $6\,\mathrm{A}$ at $t = 4\,\mathrm{s}$ and then returns to zero at $t = 6\,\mathrm{s}$"}

- $36\,\mathrm{C}$
- $12\,\mathrm{C}$
- $18\,\mathrm{C}$
- $3\,\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The charge crossing the section is $q = \displaystyle\int_0^{6} i(t)\,dt$, that is, the area under the graph of $i(t)$. The graph and the time axis enclose a triangle with base $6\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $6\,\mathrm{A}$, so
$$
q = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 6\,\mathrm{A} = 18\,\mathrm{C} .
$$
The value $36\,\mathrm{C}$ corresponds to the rectangle, that is, to a constant current of $6\,\mathrm{A}$ over the whole interval, and $12\,\mathrm{C}$ is the area of the increasing part alone.
