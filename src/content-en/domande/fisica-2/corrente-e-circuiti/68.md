A real source, with fixed electromotive force $\mathcal{E}$ and internal resistance $r$, supplies a resistor with adjustable resistance $R$. Which statement holds when $R = r$?

- The efficiency of the source is maximum
- The current delivered by the source is maximum
- The power on the resistor is maximum and half of the delivered power is dissipated in the internal resistance
- The terminal voltage of the source equals $\mathcal{E}$

## Explanation

The power on the resistor is $P(R) = \dfrac{\mathcal{E}^2 R}{(R + r)^2}$, which by the **maximum power transfer theorem** is maximum for $R = r$. Under that condition the efficiency is $\dfrac{R}{R + r} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so half of the power $\mathcal{E} I$ goes to the resistor and half is dissipated in $r$. The efficiency approaches $1$ as $R$ grows, the current $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R + r}$ is maximum for $R = 0$, and the terminal voltage is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{2}$.
