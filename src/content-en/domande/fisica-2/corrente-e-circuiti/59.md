In a cylindrical wire of radius $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ the current density is parallel to the axis and has magnitude $J(r) = J_0\,\dfrac{r}{a}$, where $r$ is the distance from the axis and $J_0 = 3.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$. What is the current in the wire?

- $9.4\,\mathrm{A}$
- $4.7\,\mathrm{A}$
- $6.3\,\mathrm{A}$
- $3.1\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

The current is the flux of $\vec{J}$ through the cross section. The density depends only on $r$, so the cross section is divided into circular rings of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$, of area $2\pi r\,dr$, on each of which $J$ is constant. Therefore
$$
I = \int_0^{a} J_0\,\frac{r}{a}\, 2\pi r\,dr = \frac{2\pi J_0}{a} \cdot \frac{a^3}{3} = \frac{2}{3}\,\pi J_0 a^2 = \frac{2}{3}\,\pi \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{6} \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 6.3\,\mathrm{A} .
$$
The value $9.4\,\mathrm{A}$ is obtained with the uniform density $J_0$ over the whole cross section, $4.7\,\mathrm{A}$ with the arithmetic mean $\dfrac{J_0}{2}$, and $3.1\,\mathrm{A}$ by using $\pi r\,dr$ as the area of the ring.
