An uncharged capacitor is charged by an ideal source through a resistor, with time constant $\tau = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. At what instant is the power at which the capacitor receives energy maximum?

- $0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The power received by the capacitor is the product of the voltage across it and the current:
$$
P(t) = \mathcal{E} \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \right) \cdot \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R}\, e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = \frac{\mathcal{E}^2}{R}\, x\,(1 - x) , \qquad x = e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} .
$$
The product $x\,(1 - x)$ is maximum for $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the power is maximum when $e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, at $t = \tau \ln 2 \approx 1.4\,\mathrm{s}$. At that instant the voltage across the capacitor equals the voltage across the resistor. At the initial instant the voltage across the capacitor is zero, therefore the received power is zero even though the current is maximum.
