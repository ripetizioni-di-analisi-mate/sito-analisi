A smartphone battery has electromotive force $3.7\,\mathrm{V}$ and capacity $3000\,\mathrm{mAh}$, that is, it can deliver a current of $3000\,\mathrm{mA}$ for one hour. Assuming the electromotive force stays constant, how much energy does the electromotive force supply during a complete discharge?

- $1.1 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.7 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $11\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The capacity gives the charge that the battery drives around the circuit in a complete discharge, so
$$
q = I\,\Delta t = 3.0\,\mathrm{A} \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 1.08 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{C} .
$$
The electromotive force is work per unit charge, therefore the energy supplied is $W = \mathcal{E}\, q = 3.7 \cdot 1.08 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 4.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $1.1 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the charge in coulombs, $6.7 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{J}$ counts one hour as $60\,\mathrm{s}$, and $11\,\mathrm{J}$ is the product $3.7 \cdot 3.0$, which is the energy in watt-hours.
