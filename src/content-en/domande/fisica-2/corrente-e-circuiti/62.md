Two wires with the same length and the same cross section, one of copper and one of nichrome, are connected in series to a source. Which of the two dissipates more power?

- The copper wire, because it conducts better and carries a larger current
- They dissipate the same power, because they carry the same current
- The nichrome wire, because it has the same voltage across it as the copper wire and a larger resistance
- The nichrome wire, because it carries the same current and has a larger resistance

## Explanation

The two wires are in series, so they carry the same current $I$. The resistance is $R = \rho\,\dfrac{\ell}{A}$ with equal $\ell$ and $A$, therefore the nichrome wire, whose resistivity is about sixty times larger, has a larger resistance. By the Joule effect the power is $P = R I^2$, and for the same current it is larger in the **nichrome** wire. The voltages across the two wires are different: in series the voltage divides in proportion to the resistances.
