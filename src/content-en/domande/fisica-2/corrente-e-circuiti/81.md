A galvanometer with internal resistance $50\,\Omega$ reaches full scale with $1.0\,\mathrm{mA}$. To turn it into an ammeter with full scale $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$, which resistor is connected to it, and how?

- About $0.050\,\Omega$, in series
- About $50\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$, in series
- About $0.050\,\Omega$, in parallel
- About $50\,\Omega$, in parallel

## Explanation

At full scale the galvanometer must carry $1.0\,\mathrm{mA}$ while the total current is $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$, so the excess current, $0.999\,\mathrm{A}$, must flow in a parallel branch, called a *shunt*. The galvanometer and the shunt are in parallel and have the same voltage, $50\,\Omega \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.050\,\mathrm{V}$. Therefore the shunt has resistance
$$
R_s = \frac{0.050\,\mathrm{V}}{0.999\,\mathrm{A}} \approx 0.050\,\Omega .
$$
A $50\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistor in series would instead turn the galvanometer into a voltmeter with a full scale of about $50\,\mathrm{V}$.
