A uniform wire of constant cross section conducts a steady current. The current density is uniform across each cross section. Given the magnitude of the current density $j = 6\,\mathrm{A/mm^2}$ and the cross-sectional area $A = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, what is the current $I_{e}$?

- $\dfrac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{A}$
- $3\,\mathrm{A}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{A}$
- $6\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

The current is the flux of current density. Since the density is uniform and parallel to the normal, it is multiplied by the area. In the indicated units, $I_{e} = A \cdot j$. Using the given values, we obtain $3\,\mathrm{A}$.
