In a conductor the current, starting at $t = 0$, is $i(t) = 4\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}}$, with $i$ in amperes and $t$ in seconds. How much charge crosses a cross section of the conductor from $t = 0$ onward?

- $8\,\mathrm{C}$
- $2\,\mathrm{C}$
- $4\,\mathrm{C}$
- $16\,\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The charge crossing the section is the integral of the current over time, so
$$
q = \int_0^{+\infty} 4\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\,dt = 4 \cdot \left[ -2\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}} \right]_0^{+\infty} = 4 \cdot 2\,\mathrm{C} = 8\,\mathrm{C} .
$$
The value $4\,\mathrm{C}$ is the charge that would pass in one second at the constant initial current, while $2\,\mathrm{C}$ is obtained by multiplying by $\dfrac{1}{2}$ instead of $2$, that is, by differentiating the exponential instead of integrating it.
