A cylindrical wire of ohmic material is kept at the same temperature. The material is homogeneous. Given the length $L = 5\,\mathrm{m}$, the resistivity $\rho = \dfrac{1}{50}\,\Omega\,\mathrm{mm^2/m}$ and the resistance $R_{e} = \dfrac{1}{20}\,\Omega$, what is the cross-sectional area $A$?

- $1\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $4\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$

## Explanation

The resistance is the resistivity multiplied by the length and divided by the cross-sectional area. The resistivity units are consistent with meters and square millimeters. In the indicated units, $R_{e} = \dfrac{L \cdot \rho}{A}$. Solving for the unknown gives $A = \dfrac{L \cdot \rho}{R_{e}}$. Using the given values, we obtain $2\,\mathrm{mm^2}$.
