A network consists of $R_1 = 2.0\,\Omega$ between the nodes $A$ and $C$, $R_2 = 4.0\,\Omega$ between $C$ and $B$, $R_3 = 3.0\,\Omega$ between $A$ and $D$, $R_4 = 6.0\,\Omega$ between $D$ and $B$, and $R_5 = 10\,\Omega$ between $C$ and $D$. What is the equivalent resistance between $A$ and $B$?

- $15\,\Omega$
- $13.6\,\Omega$
- $2.3\,\Omega$
- $3.6\,\Omega$

## Explanation

The network is a bridge, and $\dfrac{R_1}{R_2} = \dfrac{2.0}{4.0} = \dfrac{3.0}{6.0} = \dfrac{R_3}{R_4}$, so the bridge is balanced. With a voltage between $A$ and $B$, the potentials of $C$ and $D$ are the same fraction of the applied voltage, because the two branches are dividers with the same ratio. Therefore $V_C = V_D$, no current flows in $R_5$, and $R_5$ can be removed. What remains is the parallel combination of the branches $R_1 + R_2 = 6.0\,\Omega$ and $R_3 + R_4 = 9.0\,\Omega$, and
$$
R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{6.0 \cdot 9.0}{6.0 + 9.0}\,\Omega = 3.6\,\Omega .
$$
