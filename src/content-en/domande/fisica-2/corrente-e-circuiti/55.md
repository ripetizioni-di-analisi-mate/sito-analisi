The ends $A$ and $B$ of a resistor carrying a steady current have potentials $V_A = 3\,\mathrm{V}$ and $V_B = 15\,\mathrm{V}$. In which direction does the current flow in the resistor?

- From $A$ to $B$
- It depends on the sign of the charge carriers
- No current flows, because both potentials are positive
- From $B$ to $A$

## Explanation

In a resistor the current flows from the point at higher potential to the point at lower potential, because the resistance is positive. Since $V_B > V_A$, the current flows **from $B$ to $A$**, with $\Delta V = V_B - V_A = 12\,\mathrm{V}$ across the resistor. The direction of the current is the conventional one of positive charges: in a metal the electrons move from $A$ to $B$, but the current still flows from $B$ to $A$.
