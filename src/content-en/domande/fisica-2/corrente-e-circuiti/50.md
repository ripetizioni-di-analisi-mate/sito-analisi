An insulating ring carries a uniformly distributed charge $Q = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ and rotates about its own axis making $50$ revolutions per second. What is the current associated with the rotating charge?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{A}$
- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{A}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{A}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

Fix an ideal cross section, that is, a point on the circumference of the ring. In each revolution the whole charge $Q$ passes that point, and there are $f = 50$ revolutions per second. Hence the charge passing in one second is $Q f$, and the current is
$$
I = Q f = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 50\,\mathrm{A} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{A} .
$$
The value $6.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{A}$ uses the angular velocity $\omega = 2\pi f$ instead of the frequency, which counts the revolutions.
