A battery with electromotive force $12\,\mathrm{V}$ and internal resistance $0.40\,\Omega$ is being recharged: a charger keeps $14\,\mathrm{V}$ across its terminals and drives through it $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$, which enter at the positive terminal. How much power is stored in the battery as chemical energy?

- $60\,\mathrm{W}$
- $70\,\mathrm{W}$
- $10\,\mathrm{W}$
- $80\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The battery absorbs from the charger the power $\Delta V\, I = 14 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{W} = 70\,\mathrm{W}$. Of this, the internal resistance dissipates $r I^2 = 0.40 \cdot 25\,\mathrm{W} = 10\,\mathrm{W}$ by the Joule effect. The rest, $\mathcal{E} I = 12 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{W} = 60\,\mathrm{W}$, is the power that the current gives to the electromotive force while crossing it from the positive to the negative terminal, and it is stored as **chemical energy**. Indeed $\Delta V = \mathcal{E} + r I = 14\,\mathrm{V}$, in agreement with the data.
