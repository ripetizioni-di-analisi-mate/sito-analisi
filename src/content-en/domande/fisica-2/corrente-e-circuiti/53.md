A copper tube, with resistivity $1.7 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\Omega \cdot \mathrm{m}$, is $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long and has inner radius $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and outer radius $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The current flows along the tube with uniform density over the cross section. What is the resistance of the tube between its two ends?

- $2.7 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\Omega$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$
- $3.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\Omega$
- $9.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\Omega$

## Explanation

The cross section of the tube is an annulus, whose area is the difference between the areas of the two circles:
$$
A = \pi\,(r_2^2 - r_1^2) = \pi\,(4.0 - 1.0) \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2} \approx 9.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2} .
$$
The current density is uniform and parallel to the axis, so the formula for the resistance of a wire applies, $R = \rho\,\dfrac{\ell}{A} = \dfrac{1.7 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot 2.0}{9.4 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\Omega \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\Omega$. The value $2.7 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\Omega$ comes from using the area of the full disk of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $1.1 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$ from using $\pi\,(r_2 - r_1)^2$.
