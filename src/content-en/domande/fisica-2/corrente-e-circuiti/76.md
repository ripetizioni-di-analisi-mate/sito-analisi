A light bulb is rated $6.0\,\mathrm{V}$, $3.0\,\mathrm{W}$, that is, it dissipates $3.0\,\mathrm{W}$ when it has $6.0\,\mathrm{V}$ across it. It is to be powered by a $9.0\,\mathrm{V}$ ideal source, with a resistor connected in series. What resistance must the resistor have for the bulb to operate at its rated values?

- $6.0\,\Omega$
- $12\,\Omega$
- $18\,\Omega$
- $3.0\,\Omega$

## Explanation

At its rated values the bulb carries the current $I = \dfrac{P}{\Delta V} = \dfrac{3.0}{6.0}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.50\,\mathrm{A}$. The resistor is in series with the bulb, so it carries the same current. By the **loop rule** the voltage across the resistor is $9.0\,\mathrm{V} - 6.0\,\mathrm{V} = 3.0\,\mathrm{V}$, therefore
$$
R = \frac{3.0}{0.50}\,\Omega = 6.0\,\Omega .
$$
The value $12\,\Omega$ is the resistance of the bulb, $18\,\Omega$ is the total resistance of the circuit, and $3.0\,\Omega$ is obtained with $\dfrac{\Delta V^2}{P}$, using the voltage across the resistor and the power of the bulb.
