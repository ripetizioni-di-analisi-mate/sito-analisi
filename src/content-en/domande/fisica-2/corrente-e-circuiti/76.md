A cylindrical wire of ohmic material is kept at the same temperature. The material is homogeneous. Given the length $L = 5\,\mathrm{m}$, the cross-sectional area $A = 2\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ and the resistivity $\rho = \dfrac{1}{50}\,\Omega\,\mathrm{mm^2/m}$, what is the resistance $R_{e}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{40}\,\Omega$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}\,\Omega$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\Omega$
- $\dfrac{1}{20}\,\Omega$

## Explanation

The resistance is the resistivity multiplied by the length and divided by the cross-sectional area. The resistivity units are consistent with meters and square millimeters. In the indicated units, $R_{e} = \dfrac{L \cdot \rho}{A}$. Using the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{1}{20}\,\Omega$.
