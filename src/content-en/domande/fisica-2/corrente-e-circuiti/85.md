An uncharged $200\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor is fully charged by an ideal $10\,\mathrm{V}$ source through a resistor. How much energy is dissipated in the resistor during the whole charging process?

- $20\,\mathrm{mJ}$
- $10\,\mathrm{mJ}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{mJ}$
- It depends on the value of the resistance

## Explanation

In the complete charging the source moves the charge $C\mathcal{E}$ through the electromotive force, so it delivers the energy $C\mathcal{E}^2 = 200 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 100\,\mathrm{J} = 20\,\mathrm{mJ}$. The charged capacitor stores $\dfrac{1}{2} C\mathcal{E}^2 = 10\,\mathrm{mJ}$. By the **energy balance in charging** the remaining part, $\dfrac{1}{2} C\mathcal{E}^2 = 10\,\mathrm{mJ}$, is dissipated in the resistor, whatever the value of $R$: a larger resistance makes the current smaller but the charging longer.
