A cylindrical wire of ohmic material is kept at the same temperature. The material is homogeneous. Given the cross-sectional area $A = 2\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, the resistivity $\rho = \dfrac{1}{50}\,\Omega\,\mathrm{mm^2/m}$ and the resistance $R_{e} = \dfrac{1}{20}\,\Omega$, what is the length $L$?

- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{5}{4}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The resistance is the resistivity multiplied by the length and divided by the cross-sectional area. The resistivity units are consistent with meters and square millimeters. In the indicated units, $R_{e} = \dfrac{L \cdot \rho}{A}$. Solving for the unknown gives $L = \dfrac{A \cdot R_{e}}{\rho}$. Using the given values, we obtain $5\,\mathrm{m}$.
