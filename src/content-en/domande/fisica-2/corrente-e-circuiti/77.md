A branch of a circuit goes from point $A$ to point $B$ and contains, in this order, a $2.0\,\Omega$ resistor carrying $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the direction from $A$ to $B$, and an ideal $5.0\,\mathrm{V}$ source that, going from $A$ to $B$, is crossed from the negative to the positive terminal. What is $V_A - V_B$?

- $11\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-1.0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-11\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Go along the branch from $A$ to $B$ adding up the potential changes. The resistor is crossed in the direction of the current, so the potential decreases by $R I = 6.0\,\mathrm{V}$. The source is crossed from the negative to the positive terminal, so the potential increases by $5.0\,\mathrm{V}$. Therefore $V_B = V_A - 6.0\,\mathrm{V} + 5.0\,\mathrm{V}$, and
$$
V_A - V_B = 6.0\,\mathrm{V} - 5.0\,\mathrm{V} = 1.0\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
