A metal wire is heated. According to the Drude model, what happens to the mean time $t_c$ between two collisions of the conduction electrons and to the resistivity of the metal?

- $t_c$ decreases and the resistivity increases
- $t_c$ increases and the resistivity increases
- $t_c$ decreases and the resistivity decreases
- $t_c$ is unchanged and the resistivity increases because of thermal expansion

## Explanation

When the metal is heated the lattice vibrations become larger, so the collisions of the electrons with the lattice are more frequent and the mean time $t_c$ between two collisions decreases. In the Drude model the resistivity is $\rho = \dfrac{m_e}{n e^2 t_c}$, inversely proportional to $t_c$, therefore the resistivity **increases**. This explains the positive temperature coefficient of metals; thermal expansion changes the dimensions of the wire by a much smaller amount.
