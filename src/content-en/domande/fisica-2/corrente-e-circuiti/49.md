A copper wire, with $n = 8.5 \cdot 10^{28}$ conduction electrons per cubic meter and a cross section of $1.0\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, carries a current of $1.7\,\mathrm{A}$. On average, how long does a conduction electron take to travel $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ of wire?

- $8.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.25 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The electrons move along the wire with the drift velocity
$$
v_d = \frac{I}{n e A} = \frac{1.7}{8.5 \cdot 10^{28} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.25 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The time to travel $\ell = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is therefore $t = \dfrac{\ell}{v_d} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}$, more than two hours. The value $3.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}$ is the time taken by light: it is of the order of the time in which the electric field sets up in the wire, and it does not concern the motion of the electrons.
