A uniform wire of constant cross section conducts a steady current. The current density is uniform across each cross section. Initially, the given quantities are the magnitude of the current density $j = 6\,\mathrm{A/mm^2}$ and the cross-sectional area $A = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$. In the second configuration, we change the cross-sectional area to $1\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $I_{e}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

The current is the flux of current density. Since the density is uniform and parallel to the normal, it is multiplied by the area. Applying $I_{e} = A \cdot j$ gives $3\,\mathrm{A}$ before the change and $6\,\mathrm{A}$ afterward. The ratio is $2$.
