An uncharged $100\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor is charged by an ideal $10\,\mathrm{V}$ source through a resistor. How much energy is stored in the capacitor after a time equal to one time constant?

- $3.2\,\mathrm{mJ}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{mJ}$
- $0.68\,\mathrm{mJ}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{mJ}$

## Explanation

During charging the voltage across the capacitor is $\Delta V(t) = \mathcal{E} \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \right)$, so at $t = \tau$ it equals $\mathcal{E}\,(1 - e^{-1}) \approx 0.632 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{V} = 6.32\,\mathrm{V}$. The stored energy is $\dfrac{1}{2} C\, \Delta V^2$, therefore
$$
U = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 6.32^2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.0\,\mathrm{mJ} .
$$
The energy grows with the square of the voltage: the value $3.2\,\mathrm{mJ}$ applies to the final energy, $5.0\,\mathrm{mJ}$, the fraction $0.632$ instead of its square.
