An ideal source supplies a resistor $R_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ in series with a resistor $R_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$; an uncharged $10\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor is connected in parallel with $R_2$. When the switch is closed the capacitor charges with an exponential law. What is the time constant?

- $90\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $30\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $60\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $20\,\mathrm{ms}$

## Explanation

Let $\Delta V$ be the voltage across the capacitor, whose charge is therefore $C\,\Delta V$. The current in $R_1$ is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E} - \Delta V}{R_1}$ and the one in $R_2$ is $\dfrac{\Delta V}{R_2}$; by the **junction rule** the difference charges the capacitor, so
$$
C\,\frac{d\Delta V}{dt} = \frac{\mathcal{E} - \Delta V}{R_1} - \frac{\Delta V}{R_2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{d\Delta V}{dt} + \frac{R_1 + R_2}{R_1 R_2 C}\, \Delta V = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R_1 C} .
$$
The equation is linear of the first order, therefore the time constant is $\tau = \dfrac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}\, C = 2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega} \cdot 10\,\mu\mathrm{F} = 20\,\mathrm{ms}$. The resistance that appears is that of $R_1$ and $R_2$ in parallel, seen by the capacitor with the source replaced by a wire; the value $90\,\mathrm{ms}$ uses the series.
