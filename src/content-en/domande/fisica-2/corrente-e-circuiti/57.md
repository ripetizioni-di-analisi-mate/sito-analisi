A $20\,\Omega$ resistor at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ whose resistance does not depend on temperature is to be built by connecting in series a copper resistor, with temperature coefficient $3.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$, and a carbon resistor, with coefficient $-6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. What resistance must the copper resistor have at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

- $17\,\Omega$
- $10\,\Omega$
- $2.7\,\Omega$
- $3.1\,\Omega$

## Explanation

In the linear model the resistance of the series is $R_{\mathrm{Cu}}\,(1 + \alpha_{\mathrm{Cu}} \Delta T) + R_{\mathrm{C}}\,(1 + \alpha_{\mathrm{C}} \Delta T)$, so it does not depend on $\Delta T$ if the two variations cancel out. The conditions to be satisfied together are
$$
\begin{cases}
R_{\mathrm{Cu}} + R_{\mathrm{C}} = 20\,\Omega \\[4pt]
3.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\, R_{\mathrm{Cu}} - 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\, R_{\mathrm{C}} = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation $R_{\mathrm{C}} = 6.5\, R_{\mathrm{Cu}}$, therefore the first one gives $7.5\, R_{\mathrm{Cu}} = 20\,\Omega$ and $R_{\mathrm{Cu}} \approx 2.7\,\Omega$. The value $17\,\Omega$ is the resistance of the carbon resistor.
