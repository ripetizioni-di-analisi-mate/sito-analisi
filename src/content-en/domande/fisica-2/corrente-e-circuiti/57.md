Two resistors are in parallel. Calculate the resistance seen at the two terminals. Given the second resistance $R_{2} = 6\,\Omega$ and the resistance $R_{e} = 4\,\Omega$, what is the first resistance $R_{1}$?

- $18\,\Omega$
- $24\,\Omega$
- $12\,\Omega$
- $6\,\Omega$

## Explanation

The voltage is the same across both branches. The total current is the sum of the currents, so the conductances add. In the indicated units, $R_{e} = \dfrac{R_{1} \cdot R_{2}}{R_{1} + R_{2}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $R_{1} = \dfrac{R_{2} \cdot R_{e}}{R_{2} - R_{e}}$. Using the given values, we obtain $12\,\Omega$.
