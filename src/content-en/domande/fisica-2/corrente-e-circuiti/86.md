A resistor has resistance $100\,\Omega$ at the reference temperature. Its temperature coefficient is constant and positive over the interval considered. Given the temperature increase $T = 50\,\mathrm{K}$ and the final resistance $R_{e} = 120\,\Omega$, what is the temperature coefficient $a$?

- $\dfrac{1}{250}\,10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $4\,10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $8\,10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $2\,10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$

## Explanation

The linear approximation uses $R=R_0(1+\alpha\Delta T)$. The numerical value of $a$ is expressed in thousandths per kelvin. In the indicated units, $R_{e} = \dfrac{T \cdot a}{10} + 100$. Solving for the unknown gives $a = \dfrac{10 \cdot \left(R_{e} - 100\right)}{T}$. Using the given values, we obtain $4\,10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$.
