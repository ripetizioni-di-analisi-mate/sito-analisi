A graphite conductor, with resistivity $1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\Omega \cdot \mathrm{m}$, has the shape of a truncated cone $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, with bases of radius $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ covered by metal plates of negligible resistance. Assuming the current density is uniform over every cross section perpendicular to the axis, what is the resistance between the two bases?

- $2.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$
- $9.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$
- $3.2 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$

## Explanation

Let $x$ be the distance from the smaller base. The radius grows linearly, $r(x) = a + \dfrac{b - a}{\ell}\, x$, with $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, $b = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\ell = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The slice between $x$ and $x + dx$ is a wire of length $dx$ and cross section $\pi r^2$, so it has resistance $dR = \dfrac{\rho\,dx}{\pi r^2}$. The slices carry the same current, therefore they are in series and, with $dx = \dfrac{\ell}{b - a}\,dr$,
$$
R = \frac{\rho\,\ell}{\pi\,(b - a)} \int_a^b \frac{dr}{r^2} = \frac{\rho\,\ell}{\pi\,(b - a)} \left( \frac{1}{a} - \frac{1}{b} \right) = \frac{\rho\,\ell}{\pi a b} .
$$
Substituting, $R = \dfrac{1.0 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}{\pi \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\Omega \approx 3.2 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$. The value $2.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$ uses the mean radius $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, while $9.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$ and $1.1 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega$ use the radius of the smaller base and that of the larger base.
