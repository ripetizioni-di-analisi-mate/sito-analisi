A $2.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ electric kettle contains $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Assuming that all the energy dissipated by the Joule effect heats the water, how long does it take to bring it to $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$? The specific heat of water is $4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg \cdot K)}$.

- $2.1 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.17\,\mathrm{s}$
- $40\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Heating the water by $\Delta T = 80\,\mathrm{K}$ requires the energy $Q = m c\,\Delta T = 1.0 \cdot 4186 \cdot 80\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3.35 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$. The kettle dissipates the energy $P\,t$ in the time $t$, so
$$
t = \frac{m c\,\Delta T}{P} = \frac{3.35 \cdot 10^{5}}{2.0 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{s} ,
$$
a little less than three minutes. The value $2.1 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{s}$ uses a temperature increase of $100\,\mathrm{K}$, that is, the final temperature instead of the difference.
