Two resistors are in parallel. Calculate the resistance seen at the two terminals. Initially, the given quantities are the first resistance $R_{1} = 12\,\Omega$ and the second resistance $R_{2} = 6\,\Omega$. In the second configuration, we change the first resistance to $20\,\Omega$ and the second resistance to $5\,\Omega$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $R_{e}$?

- $7\,\Omega$
- $- \dfrac{16}{3}\,\Omega$
- $4\,\Omega$
- $0\,\Omega$

## Explanation

The voltage is the same across both branches. The total current is the sum of the currents, so the conductances add. Applying $R_{e} = \dfrac{R_{1} \cdot R_{2}}{R_{1} + R_{2}}$ gives $4\,\Omega$ before the change and $4\,\Omega$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $0\,\Omega$.
