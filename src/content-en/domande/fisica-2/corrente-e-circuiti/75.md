A lamp is connected to an ideal source. A second, identical lamp is connected in parallel with the first. What happens to the power dissipated by the first lamp?

- It stays the same
- It is halved
- It is reduced to one quarter
- It doubles

## Explanation

The two lamps are in parallel across the terminals of the ideal source, so each of them has the voltage $\mathcal{E}$ across it, whatever the delivered current. The first lamp carries the same current as before, $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R}$, and dissipates the same power $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}^2}{R}$. The current delivered by the source, instead, doubles. With a real source the terminal voltage would decrease, and the first lamp would dissipate a little less.
