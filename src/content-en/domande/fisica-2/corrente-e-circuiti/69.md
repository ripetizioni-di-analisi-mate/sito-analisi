An ohmic resistor remains at constant temperature. Calculate the dissipated power from the known voltage and resistance. Initially, the given quantities are the voltage across it $U_{e} = 24\,\mathrm{V}$ and the resistance $R_{e} = 12\,\Omega$. In the second configuration, we change the voltage across it to $36\,\mathrm{V}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $P$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{9}{4}$
- $\dfrac{4}{9}$

## Explanation

For Joule heating, $P=U_e I_e$. Ohm's law allows the substitution $I_e=U_e/R_e$. Applying $P = \dfrac{U_{e}^{2}}{R_{e}}$ gives $48\,\mathrm{W}$ before the change and $108\,\mathrm{W}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{9}{4}$.
