A car battery with electromotive force $12\,\mathrm{V}$ and internal resistance $0.050\,\Omega$ is accidentally short-circuited by a metal wrench of negligible resistance. How much power is dissipated inside the battery?

- $0.72\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $0\,\mathrm{W}$
- $2.9\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The wrench has negligible resistance, so the current is the short-circuit current, $I = \dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{r} = \dfrac{12}{0.050}\,\mathrm{A} = 240\,\mathrm{A}$. All this current flows through the internal resistance, therefore the power dissipated by the Joule effect is
$$
P = r I^2 = \frac{\mathcal{E}^2}{r} = \frac{144}{0.050}\,\mathrm{W} \approx 2.9\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The terminal voltage is zero, but the power dissipated inside the battery is not. The value $0.72\,\mathrm{kW}$ is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}^2}{4r}$, the maximum power the battery can transfer to an external load.
