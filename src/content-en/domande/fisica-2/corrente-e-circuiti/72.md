In the circuit of the figure the ideal source has $\mathcal{E} = 12\,\mathrm{V}$, and $R_1 = 4.0\,\Omega$, $R_2 = 8.0\,\Omega$. A wire of negligible resistance connects the ends of $R_2$. What current does the source deliver?

- $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

The wire and $R_2$ are connected between the same two nodes, so they are in parallel. The wire has zero resistance, therefore the voltage between the two nodes is zero and the current in $R_2$ is zero as well: all the current flows in the wire. The circuit is thus equivalent to $R_1$ alone connected to the source, and
$$
I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R_1} = \frac{12}{4.0}\,\mathrm{A} = 3.0\,\mathrm{A} .
$$
The value $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$ neglects the wire and treats $R_1$ and $R_2$ as being in series.
