An initially charged capacitor discharges through a resistor. The time required for the voltage to fall to a quarter of its initial value is measured. Given the capacitance $C = 20\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ and the required time $t = 400 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{ms}$, what is the resistance $R_{e}$?

- $20\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$
- $5 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$
- $5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$
- $10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$

## Explanation

The voltage is proportional to $e^{-t/(R_e C)}$. Imposing the factor $1/4$ gives $\ln 4$. The product of kilohms and microfarads is expressed in milliseconds. In the indicated units, $t = \log{\left(4^{C \cdot R_{e}} \right)}$. Solving for the unknown gives $R_{e} = \dfrac{t}{C \cdot \log{\left(4 \right)}}$. Using the given values, we obtain $10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$.
