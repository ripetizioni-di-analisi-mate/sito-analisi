Solving a circuit with Kirchhoff's laws, after choosing an arbitrary direction for each current, the value $I_2 = -0.50\,\mathrm{A}$ is obtained for the current in one branch. What does this result mean?

- The calculation contains an error, because a current cannot be negative
- A current of $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$ flows in the branch in the direction opposite to the chosen one
- The branch carries $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$ in the chosen direction, transported by electrons
- The branch certainly absorbs a power of $0.50\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The directions of the currents are chosen before writing the equations, and Kirchhoff's laws hold whatever the choice. A negative value indicates that the current, of magnitude $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$, flows in the direction opposite to the chosen one. The result contains no error and there is no need to redo the calculation: it is enough to reverse the arrow of the branch. To know whether the branch absorbs or delivers power, the components of the branch and the voltages across them are also needed.
