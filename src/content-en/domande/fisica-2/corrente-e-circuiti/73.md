A voltage divider consists of two $10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistors in series, connected to an ideal $12\,\mathrm{V}$ source. A $10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ load is connected across the second resistor. What is the voltage across the load?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The load is in parallel with the second resistor, and two $10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistors in parallel are equivalent to $5.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$. The divider thus becomes the series of $10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $5.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$, and the voltage across the parallel combination is
$$
\Delta V = \frac{5.0}{10 + 5.0} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{V} = 4.0\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The value $6.0\,\mathrm{V}$ is the voltage of the unloaded divider: the load lowers it because it reduces the resistance of the part across which the voltage is taken.
