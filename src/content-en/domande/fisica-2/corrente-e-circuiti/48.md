A uniform wire of constant cross section conducts a steady current. The current density is uniform across each cross section. Given the magnitude of the current density $j = 6\,\mathrm{A/mm^2}$ and the current $I_{e} = 3\,\mathrm{A}$, what is the cross-sectional area $A$?

- $1\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$

## Explanation

The current is the flux of current density. Since the density is uniform and parallel to the normal, it is multiplied by the area. In the indicated units, $I_{e} = A \cdot j$. Solving for the unknown gives $A = \dfrac{I_{e}}{j}$. Using the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$.
