A homogeneous metal wire carrying a steady current has a part where the cross-sectional area is reduced to one half. What can be said about the narrow part compared with the wide part?

- The current is doubled and the drift velocity is the same
- The current and the current density are the same
- The current is halved and the current density is the same
- The current is the same and the drift velocity is doubled

## Explanation

In the steady state, by conservation of charge, the current $I$ is the same at every cross section of a wire without branches. The current density is $J = \dfrac{I}{A}$, so in the part with half the area $J$ is doubled. Since $v_d = \dfrac{J}{n e}$ and the electron density $n$ is the same, the drift velocity is **doubled** as well.
