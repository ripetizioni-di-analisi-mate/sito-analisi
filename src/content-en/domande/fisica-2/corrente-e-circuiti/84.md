An ideal $12\,\mathrm{V}$ source supplies, through a switch, a resistor $R_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ in series with a resistor $R_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$; an uncharged capacitor is connected in parallel with $R_2$. What current does the source deliver immediately after the switch is closed?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{mA}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{mA}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{mA}$
- $0\,\mathrm{mA}$

## Explanation

Immediately after closing the switch the capacitor is still uncharged, so the voltage across it is zero and it behaves like a wire. The capacitor is in parallel with $R_2$, therefore the voltage across $R_2$ is zero too and no current flows in $R_2$. The whole voltage of the source is across $R_1$, and the delivered current is
$$
I = \frac{12}{2.0 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{A} = 6.0\,\mathrm{mA} .
$$
The value $1.5\,\mathrm{mA}$ is the steady-state current, when the capacitor is charged and behaves like an open circuit.
