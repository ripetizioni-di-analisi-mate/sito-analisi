An initially charged capacitor discharges through a resistor. The time required for the voltage to fall to a quarter of its initial value is measured. Initially, the given quantities are the resistance $R_{e} = 10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and the capacitance $C = 20\,\mathrm{\mu F}$. In the second configuration, we change the resistance to $20\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $t$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $1$
- $4$

## Explanation

The voltage is proportional to $e^{-t/(R_e C)}$. Imposing the factor $1/4$ gives $\ln 4$. The product of kilohms and microfarads is expressed in milliseconds. Applying $t = \log{\left(4^{C \cdot R_{e}} \right)}$ gives $400 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{ms}$ before the change and $800 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{ms}$ afterward. The ratio is $2$.
