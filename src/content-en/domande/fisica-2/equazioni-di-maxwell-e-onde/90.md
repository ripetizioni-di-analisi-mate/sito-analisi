For water the relative permittivity measured with static fields is about $80$, and $\mu_r \approx 1$; with these values the formula $n = \sqrt{\varepsilon_r \mu_r}$ would give a refractive index of about $9$. For visible light, however, one measures $n \approx 1.33$. Which fact explains the difference?

- The formula holds only for conducting media
- The relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r$ depends on the frequency of the field
- In water light travels faster than in vacuum
- The frequency of light changes when it enters water

## Explanation

The speed of a wave in an insulating medium is $v = \dfrac{c}{\sqrt{\mu_r \varepsilon_r}}$, where $\varepsilon_r$ is the value the medium has at the frequency of the wave.
The value $80$ is measured with static fields, to which the water molecules have time to align; at the frequency of visible light, about $5 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$, this alignment does not occur and $\varepsilon_r$ is much smaller.
Indeed, from $n \approx 1.33$ one gets $\varepsilon_r = n^2 \approx 1.8$ at the frequency of light.
The other statements are false: water is an insulator, $v = \dfrac{c}{n} < c$, and the frequency stays the same when passing from one medium to another.
