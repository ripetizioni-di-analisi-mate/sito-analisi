A beam of intensity $I = 900\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ strikes perpendicularly a surface that reflects half of the incident energy and absorbs the other half. What is the radiation pressure?

- $3.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Split the beam into two parts of intensity $\dfrac{I}{2}$ each.
The absorbed part exerts the pressure $\dfrac{I}{2c}$, and the reflected part the pressure $\dfrac{2}{c} \cdot \dfrac{I}{2} = \dfrac{I}{c}$.
Therefore the total pressure is
$$
p = \frac{I}{2c} + \frac{I}{c} = \frac{3I}{2c} = \frac{3 \cdot 900}{2 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 4.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa} ,
$$
between the value for an absorbing surface and that for a mirror.
