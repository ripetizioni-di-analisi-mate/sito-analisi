A uniform beam is normally incident on an ideal mirror. The light is completely reflected. Use $c=300\,\mathrm{Mm/s}$. Given the beam intensity $J = 90\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and the magnitude of the radiation force $F = 120\,\mathrm{nN}$, what is the illuminated area $A$?

- $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{m^2}$

## Explanation

Reflection reverses the normal momentum. The force is twice the incident power $JA$ divided by the speed of light. In the stated units, we have $F = \dfrac{20 \cdot A \cdot J}{3}$. Solving for the unknown gives $A = \dfrac{3 \cdot F}{20 \cdot J}$. With the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
