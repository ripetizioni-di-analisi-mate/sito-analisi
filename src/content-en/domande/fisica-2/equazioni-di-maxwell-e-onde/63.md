In a region the magnetic field is uniform and equals $\vec{B} = b\,t\,\hat{k}$, with $b > 0$ constant. Which of the following electric fields satisfies Faraday's law in local form?

- $\dfrac{b}{2}\left( -y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath} \right)$
- $b\, x\,\hat{\jmath}$
- $\dfrac{b}{2}\left( y\,\hat{\imath} - x\,\hat{\jmath} \right)$
- $b\left( x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath} \right)$

## Explanation

The law in local form is $\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\dfrac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t} = -b\,\hat{k}$.
For a field $\vec{E} = E_x\,\hat{\imath} + E_y\,\hat{\jmath}$ that depends only on $x$ and $y$, the curl is $\left( \dfrac{\partial E_y}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial E_x}{\partial y} \right)\hat{k}$.
For $\dfrac{b}{2}(y\,\hat{\imath} - x\,\hat{\jmath})$ we get $\left( -\dfrac{b}{2} - \dfrac{b}{2} \right)\hat{k} = -b\,\hat{k}$, which is the required value.
The other fields have curl $b\,\hat{k}$, $b\,\hat{k}$ and $\vec{0}$, so **only $\dfrac{b}{2}(y\,\hat{\imath} - x\,\hat{\jmath})$** satisfies the law.
