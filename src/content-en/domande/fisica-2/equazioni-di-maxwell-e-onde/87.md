A light signal travels along an optical fiber of length $L = 2.0\,\mathrm{km}$, whose core has refractive index $n = 1.5$. Taking the path of the light as straight along the axis of the fiber, how long does the signal take to travel through it?

- $6.7\,\mu\mathrm{s}$
- $4.4\,\mu\mathrm{s}$
- $10\,\mu\mathrm{s}$
- $15\,\mu\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

In the medium the wave propagates with speed $v = \dfrac{c}{n}$, smaller than in vacuum.
Hence the travel time is
$$
t = \frac{L}{v} = \frac{n L}{c} = \frac{1.5 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{3}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s} = 10\,\mu\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $6.7\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ is the time light would take in vacuum, and $4.4\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by dividing by $n$ instead of multiplying.
