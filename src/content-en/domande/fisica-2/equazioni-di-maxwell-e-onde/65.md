A uniform beam is normally incident on an ideal mirror. The light is completely reflected. Use $c=300\,\mathrm{Mm/s}$. Initially, the beam intensity $J = 90\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and the illuminated area $A = \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ are given. In the second configuration, the beam intensity becomes $60\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and the illuminated area becomes $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $F$?

- $1\,\mathrm{nN}$
- $2\,\mathrm{nN}$
- $0\,\mathrm{nN}$
- $120\,\mathrm{nN}$

## Explanation

Reflection reverses the normal momentum. The force is twice the incident power $JA$ divided by the speed of light. Applying $F = \dfrac{20 \cdot A \cdot J}{3}$ gives $120\,\mathrm{nN}$ before the change and $120\,\mathrm{nN}$ after it. The final value minus the initial value is $0\,\mathrm{nN}$.
