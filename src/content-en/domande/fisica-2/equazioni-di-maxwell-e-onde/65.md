Let $E_0$, $k$ and $a$ be positive constants and $c$ the speed of light. Which of the following functions is a solution of the wave equation $\dfrac{\partial^2 E}{\partial x^2} = \dfrac{1}{c^2}\,\dfrac{\partial^2 E}{\partial t^2}$?

- $E_0 \cos(kx) \cos(kct)$
- $E_0 \cos(kx) \sin(2kct)$
- $E_0\,\dfrac{x^2 - c^2 t^2}{a^2}$
- $E_0\, e^{-\frac{x^2 + c^2 t^2}{a^2}}$

## Explanation

For $E = E_0 \cos(kx)\cos(kct)$ we have $\dfrac{\partial^2 E}{\partial x^2} = -k^2 E$ and $\dfrac{\partial^2 E}{\partial t^2} = -k^2 c^2 E$, so $\dfrac{1}{c^2}\dfrac{\partial^2 E}{\partial t^2} = -k^2 E$ and the equation is satisfied.
It is the standing wave obtained by adding two equal harmonic waves traveling in opposite directions.
With $\sin(2kct)$ the second time derivative gives $-4k^2 c^2 E$, so the two sides differ.
For $E_0\dfrac{x^2 - c^2t^2}{a^2}$ the two sides equal $\dfrac{2E_0}{a^2}$ and $-\dfrac{2E_0}{a^2}$, and for the Gaussian in $x$ and $t$ they differ wherever $x^2 \neq c^2 t^2$.
