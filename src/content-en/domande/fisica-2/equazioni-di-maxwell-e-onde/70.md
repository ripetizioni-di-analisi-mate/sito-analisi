A plane surface absorbs all the incident light. The angle between the beam and the normal to the surface is $60^\circ$. The intensity is measured on a plane perpendicular to the beam. Initially, the beam intensity $J = 240\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and the area $A = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ are given. In the second configuration, the beam intensity becomes $360\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and the area becomes $\dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $P$?

- $15\,\mathrm W$
- $-15\,\mathrm W$
- $-30\,\mathrm W$
- $- 15 \cdot \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm W$

## Explanation

The area exposed to the beam is the projection $A\cos60^\circ$. The absorbed power is the intensity times the projected area. Applying $P = \dfrac{A \cdot J}{2}$ gives $60\,\mathrm W$ before the change and $45\,\mathrm W$ after it. The final value minus the initial value is $-15\,\mathrm W$.
