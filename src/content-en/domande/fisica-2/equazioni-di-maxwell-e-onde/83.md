A probe of mass $m = 10\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pushed by a perfectly reflecting sail of area $A = 100\,\mathrm{m^2}$, perpendicular to sunlight of intensity $I = 1.36\,\mathrm{kW/m^2}$. Neglecting gravity, what is the acceleration of the probe?

- $4.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $9.1 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The sail reflects all the radiation, so the radiation pressure is $p_{\text{rad}} = \dfrac{2I}{c}$ and the force is $F = \dfrac{2IA}{c}$.
By Newton's second law
$$
a = \frac{2 I A}{m c} = \frac{2 \cdot 1360 \cdot 100}{10 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 9.1 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
With an absorbing sail the acceleration would be half as large.
