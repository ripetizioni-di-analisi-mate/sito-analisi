In a cylindrical region of radius $R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ there is a uniform magnetic field, parallel to the axis, whose magnitude increases at the rate $\dfrac{dB}{dt} = 0.50\,\mathrm{T/s}$. What is the magnitude of the induced electric field at the point $P$ at the distance $r = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 unicolore=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29;cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="0,0,1,0" vettori="0,0,-1.414,1.414,R" punti="1,0,pieno" etichette="0.45,-0.3,r;1.25,0.15,P;-0.7,-1.4,⊙;0.7,-1.4,⊙;-1.45,-0.5,⊙;1.45,-0.9,⊙;0.5,1.45,⊙;-0.3,0.45,⊙" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.6 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.4 titolo="Cross section of the region: the field $\vec{B}$ points out of the page and increases; the point $P$ is at the distance $r$ from the axis, inside the region of radius $R$"}

- $0.025\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $0.050\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $0.016\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

By rotational symmetry the lines of the induced electric field are coaxial circles, and on each of them the magnitude of $\vec{E}$ is constant.
Apply **Faraday's law** to the circle of radius $r$: the circulation is $2\pi r E$ and the enclosed magnetic flux is $B\,\pi r^2$, because $r < R$.
Therefore $2\pi r E = \pi r^2 \dfrac{dB}{dt}$, and
$$
E = \frac{r}{2}\,\frac{dB}{dt} = \frac{0.10}{2} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{V/m} = 0.025\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
The numerical value $0.016$ is that of the circulation $\pi r^2 \dfrac{dB}{dt}$, which is measured in volts and is not a field.
