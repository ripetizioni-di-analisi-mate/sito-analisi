A light signal travels in a straight line between an emitter and a detector in a transparent medium. Use $c=300\,\mathrm{Mm/s}$. Initially, the refractive index $n = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and the distance between emitter and detector $d = 600\,\mathrm m$ are given. In the second configuration, the refractive index becomes $3$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $t$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

Light propagates at speed $c/n$. Dividing the distance by this speed and converting the time to microseconds gives the delay. Applying $t = \dfrac{d \cdot n}{300}$ gives $3\,\mathrm{\mu s}$ before the change and $6\,\mathrm{\mu s}$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
