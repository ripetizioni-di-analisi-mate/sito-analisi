In a traveling plane wave in vacuum, at a point $P$ and at a certain instant, the electric field $\vec{E}$ points north and the magnetic field $\vec{B}$ points up. At the same instant, at another point $Q$, both fields point the opposite way. In which direction does the wave propagate at the two points? East, north and up form a right-handed triad.

- East at $P$ and west at $Q$
- West at both points
- North at both points
- East at both points

## Explanation

In a plane wave in vacuum the direction of propagation is that of the cross product $\vec{E} \times \vec{B}$.

With the right-handed triad east, north, up, the cross product of the north unit vector with the up unit vector is the east unit vector, so at $P$ the wave propagates east. At $Q$ both vectors are reversed, therefore $(-\vec{E}) \times (-\vec{B}) = \vec{E} \times \vec{B}$ and at $Q$ the propagation is also east.

The wave therefore propagates **east at both points**. The answer "west at both points" comes from computing $\vec{B} \times \vec{E}$ instead of $\vec{E} \times \vec{B}$.
