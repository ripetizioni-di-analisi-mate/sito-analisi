Two short light pulses strike the two faces of a perfectly reflecting mirror, initially at rest, at the same instant and at normal incidence. The pulse arriving from the left carries the energy $6.0\,\mathrm{mJ}$, the one arriving from the right $4.0\,\mathrm{mJ}$. What total momentum does the mirror receive? Here $c$ denotes the speed of light, and the displacement of the mirror during the reflection is negligible.

- $\dfrac{2.0\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c}$, to the right
- $\dfrac{20\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c}$, to the right
- $\dfrac{4.0\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c}$, to the right
- $\dfrac{4.0\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c}$, to the left

## Explanation

A pulse of energy $E$ has momentum $\dfrac{E}{c}$ in its direction of propagation. On reflection at normal incidence the momentum reverses, so the pulse transfers to the mirror the momentum $\dfrac{2E}{c}$ in the direction of incidence.

The pulse from the left therefore pushes the mirror to the right with $\dfrac{12\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c}$, while the one from the right pushes it to the left with $\dfrac{8.0\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c}$.

The two contributions have opposite directions, so the momentum received is $\dfrac{12\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c} - \dfrac{8.0\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c} = \dfrac{4.0\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c}$, to the right. The value $\dfrac{2.0\,\mathrm{mJ}}{c}$ is obtained by treating the mirror as an absorbing surface.
