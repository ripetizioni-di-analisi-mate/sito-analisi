A closed surface in vacuum contains a point charge $q_1 = +3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and a point charge $q_2 = -1.0\,\mathrm{nC}$; outside the surface, close to it, there is a third charge $q_3 = +5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. What is the outward flux of the electric field through the surface?

- $4.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V\cdot m}$
- $3.4 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V\cdot m}$
- $2.3 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V\cdot m}$
- $7.9 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V\cdot m}$

## Explanation

By **Gauss's law**, the first Maxwell equation, the outward flux is $\dfrac{Q_{\text{int}}}{\varepsilon_0}$, where $Q_{\text{int}}$ is the algebraic sum of the enclosed charges.
The charge $q_3$ is outside, so it changes the field on the surface but not the total flux.
Therefore $Q_{\text{int}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{nC} - 1.0\,\mathrm{nC} = 2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and
$$
\Phi_E = \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{V\cdot m} \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{V\cdot m} .
$$
