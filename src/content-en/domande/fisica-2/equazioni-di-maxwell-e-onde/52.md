A parallel-plate capacitor with circular plates of radius $R$, disconnected from any circuit, is filled with a homogeneous material with relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r$, relative permeability $\mu_r = 1$ and resistivity $\rho$. The capacitor discharges through the material: between the plates the electric field is uniform and equals $E(t) = E(0)\, e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$, with $\tau = \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r \rho$, and the conduction current density is $J = \dfrac{E}{\rho}$, in the direction of the field. In the material the Ampère-Maxwell law holds with $\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r$ in place of $\varepsilon_0$. Neglecting edge effects, what is the magnitude of the magnetic field between the plates at the distance $r < R$ from the axis?

- $\dfrac{\mu_0 E r}{2\rho}$
- $\dfrac{\mu_0 E r}{\rho}$
- $\dfrac{\mu_0 E R^2}{2\rho\, r}$
- $0$

## Explanation

In the material the displacement current density is
$$
\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r \frac{\partial E}{\partial t} = -\frac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r}{\tau}\, E = -\frac{E}{\rho} ,
$$
so it is equal and opposite to the conduction current density $J = \dfrac{E}{\rho}$.
Therefore, through the disk of radius $r$ coaxial with the plates, the conduction current and the displacement current add up to zero.
By rotational symmetry the magnetic field is tangent to the circle of radius $r$ and has constant magnitude on it, so the **Ampère-Maxwell law** gives $2\pi r B = 0$.
The magnetic field between the plates is therefore **zero**; the value $\dfrac{\mu_0 E r}{2\rho}$ would be obtained by considering the conduction current alone.
