Let $B_0 > 0$ be a constant and $a > 0$ a length. Which of the following fields, defined in all of space, can be a magnetic field?

- $\dfrac{B_0}{a}\left( x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath} \right)$
- $\dfrac{B_0}{a}\left( y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath} \right)$
- $\dfrac{B_0}{a}\, x\,\hat{\imath}$
- $\dfrac{B_0}{a}\left( x\,\hat{\imath} - y\,\hat{\jmath} + z\,\hat{k} \right)$

## Explanation

Every magnetic field satisfies the second Maxwell equation in local form, $\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0$, so we compute the divergence of the four fields.
For $\dfrac{B_0}{a}(y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$ we have $\dfrac{B_0}{a}\left( \dfrac{\partial y}{\partial x} + \dfrac{\partial x}{\partial y} \right) = 0$.
For the other three fields the divergence equals $\dfrac{2B_0}{a}$, $\dfrac{B_0}{a}$ and $\dfrac{B_0}{a}(1 - 1 + 1) = \dfrac{B_0}{a}$, all different from zero.
Therefore only **$\dfrac{B_0}{a}(y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$** is a possible magnetic field.
