In a region where the fields do not change with time, the circulation of the electric field along every closed line is zero. Which Maxwell equation guarantees this, and under which condition?

- Gauss's law, because the enclosed charge is zero
- Faraday's law, because the magnetic flux is constant
- The Ampère-Maxwell law, because there are no currents
- Gauss's law for magnetism, because the magnetic flux is zero

## Explanation

The circulation of the electric field appears only in **Faraday's law**, $\displaystyle\oint_\gamma \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{l} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$.
If the fields do not change with time, the magnetic flux through every fixed surface is constant, so $\dfrac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = 0$.
Therefore the circulation of $\vec{E}$ is zero and the electrostatic field is conservative, even in the presence of charges, which appear only in Gauss's law.
