A light beam of intensity $I = 600\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, measured on a plane perpendicular to the beam, strikes a flat surface that absorbs it completely, at an angle of $60^\circ$ to the normal to the surface. What is the pressure exerted perpendicular to the surface?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Let $A$ be the area of the surface. The beam strikes it obliquely, so its perpendicular cross section is $A\cos 60^\circ$ and in the time $\Delta t$ the energy $U = I A \cos 60^\circ\, \Delta t$ arrives.
This energy carries the momentum $\dfrac{U}{c}$ along the beam, and its component perpendicular to the surface is $\dfrac{U}{c}\cos 60^\circ$.
The surface absorbs all of it, therefore by the impulse-momentum theorem the perpendicular force is $\dfrac{I A \cos^2 60^\circ}{c}$ and the pressure is
$$
p = \frac{I \cos^2 60^\circ}{c} = \frac{600 \cdot 0.25}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
