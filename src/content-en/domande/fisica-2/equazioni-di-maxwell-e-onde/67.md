A plane surface absorbs all the incident light. The angle between the beam and the normal to the surface is $60^\circ$. The intensity is measured on a plane perpendicular to the beam. Given the area $A = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and the absorbed power $P = 60\,\mathrm W$, what is the beam intensity $J$?

- $240\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- $80 \cdot \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- $480\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- $120\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$

## Explanation

The area exposed to the beam is the projection $A\cos60^\circ$. The absorbed power is the intensity times the projected area. In the stated units, we have $P = \dfrac{A \cdot J}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $J = \dfrac{2 \cdot P}{A}$. With the given values, we obtain $240\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.
