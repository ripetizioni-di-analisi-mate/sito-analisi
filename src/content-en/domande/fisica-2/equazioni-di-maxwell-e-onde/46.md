In a copper wire with resistivity $\rho = 1.7 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\Omega\cdot\mathrm{m}$, the electric field is $E(t) = E_0 \sin(\omega t)$, with frequency $f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The conduction current density is $J = \dfrac{E}{\rho}$, and the displacement current density is taken as $\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{\partial E}{\partial t}$. What is the ratio of the amplitude of the displacement current density to the amplitude of the conduction current density?

- $7.5 \cdot 10^{-18}$
- $4.7 \cdot 10^{-17}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{-19}$
- $2.1 \cdot 10^{16}$

## Explanation

The conduction current density is $J = \dfrac{E_0}{\rho}\sin(\omega t)$, so its amplitude is $\dfrac{E_0}{\rho}$.
The displacement current density is $\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{\partial E}{\partial t} = \varepsilon_0\, \omega E_0 \cos(\omega t)$, so its amplitude is $\varepsilon_0\, \omega E_0$.
Therefore the ratio is
$$
\varepsilon_0\, \omega\, \rho = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 2\pi \cdot 50 \cdot 1.7 \cdot 10^{-8} \approx 4.7 \cdot 10^{-17} .
$$
In a good conductor at power-line frequency the displacement current is therefore negligible compared with the conduction current.
