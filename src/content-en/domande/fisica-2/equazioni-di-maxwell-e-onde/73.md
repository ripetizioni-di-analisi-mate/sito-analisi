Two flat, parallel metal mirrors in vacuum are $L = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. A harmonic standing wave forms between them, and the electric field is zero at every instant on the surface of each mirror. What is the lowest possible frequency of the wave?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{GHz}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{GHz}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{GHz}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{GHz}$

## Explanation

In the standing wave $2E_0 \sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)$, with the mirror at $x = 0$, the field is zero at points that are $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ apart.
The second mirror, at $x = L$, must also be at one of these points, so $L$ is an integer multiple of $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$.
The lowest frequency corresponds to the largest wavelength, $\lambda = 2L = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.
Therefore $f = \dfrac{c}{2L} = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{0.30}\,\mathrm{Hz} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
