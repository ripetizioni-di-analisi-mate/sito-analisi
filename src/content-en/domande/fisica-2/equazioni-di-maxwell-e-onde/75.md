In vacuum two harmonic plane waves propagate in the positive direction of the $x$-axis, with electric fields directed along $\hat{\jmath}$:
$$
E_1 = E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t) , \qquad E_2 = E_0 \sin\left( kx - \omega t + \frac{2\pi}{3} \right) .
$$
What is the amplitude of the electric field of the resulting wave?

- $2E_0$
- $\sqrt{3}\,E_0$
- $E_0$
- $\dfrac{E_0}{2}$

## Explanation

The wave equation is linear, so the resulting field is the sum $E_1 + E_2$.
By the sum-to-product formula $\sin\alpha + \sin\beta = 2\sin\dfrac{\alpha + \beta}{2}\cos\dfrac{\alpha - \beta}{2}$, we get
$$
E_1 + E_2 = 2E_0 \cos\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right) \sin\left( kx - \omega t + \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = E_0 \sin\left( kx - \omega t + \frac{\pi}{3} \right) .
$$
Therefore the resulting wave is still harmonic and has amplitude **$E_0$**; the value $2E_0$ would occur only with the two waves in phase.
