In a cylindrical region in vacuum, with no currents, there is a uniform electric field pointing along the positive $z$ axis, parallel to the axis of the cylinder, whose magnitude decreases with time. How are the lines of the induced magnetic field oriented, as seen by an observer on the positive $z$ axis looking toward the origin?

- Coaxial circles traversed counterclockwise
- Coaxial circles traversed clockwise
- Straight lines parallel to the positive $z$ axis
- Straight lines parallel to the negative $z$ axis

## Explanation

By the symmetry of the region the magnetic field lines are coaxial circles.
Orient the disk bounded by one of them with normal $\hat{k}$; by the right-hand rule the positive direction of the circle is then counterclockwise as seen from the positive $z$ axis.
The magnitude of the field decreases, so $\dfrac{d\Phi_E}{dt} < 0$, and by the Ampère-Maxwell law the circulation $\displaystyle\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \dfrac{d\Phi_E}{dt}$ is negative.
Therefore the magnetic field lines are traversed **clockwise**, opposite to the case of an increasing electric field.
