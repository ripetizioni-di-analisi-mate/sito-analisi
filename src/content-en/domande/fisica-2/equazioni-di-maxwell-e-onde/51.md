A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum with circular plates of radius $R$ is charged by a constant current. Neglecting edge effects, at which point of the plane halfway between the plates is the magnitude of the magnetic field largest?

- On the axis of the plates
- At the distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the axis
- At the distance $R$ from the axis, at the edge of the plates
- At no point, because the field has the same magnitude everywhere

## Explanation

From the Ampère-Maxwell law, at the distance $r$ from the axis the field is $B = \dfrac{\mu_0\, i\, r}{2\pi R^2}$ for $r \le R$ and $B = \dfrac{\mu_0\, i}{2\pi r}$ for $r \ge R$.
The first part increases with $r$, because the circle encloses a growing part of the electric flux.
The second part decreases with $r$, because the enclosed flux is the whole flux and the circle gets longer.
Therefore the field is largest **at the edge of the plates**, where it equals $\dfrac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi R}$, and it is zero on the axis.
