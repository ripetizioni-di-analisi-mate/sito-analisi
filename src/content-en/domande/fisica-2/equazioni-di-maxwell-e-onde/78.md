A laser emits a continuous beam of power $P = 3.0\,\mathrm{W}$ in vacuum. How much electromagnetic energy is contained in a $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long section of the beam?

- $3.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Let $A$ be the cross-sectional area of the beam. By the energy flux of a plane wave the intensity is $I = u c$, where $u$ is the average energy density, so $u = \dfrac{I}{c} = \dfrac{P}{A c}$.
The section of length $L$ has volume $A L$, therefore it contains the energy
$$
U = u A L = \frac{P L}{c} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 1.0}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{J} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
It is the energy emitted in the time $\dfrac{L}{c}$ that light takes to cover the section.
