A plane wave in vacuum travels in the positive direction of the $x$ axis, and its electric field oscillates along the direction of the unit vector $\dfrac{\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$: at the instant considered $\vec{E} = E\,\dfrac{\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$ with $E > 0$. What is the direction of the magnetic field at the same point and at the same instant?

- Along $\dfrac{\hat{\jmath} - \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$
- Along $\dfrac{\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}}{\sqrt{2}}$
- Along $\hat{\imath}$
- Along $\dfrac{\hat{k} - \hat{\jmath}}{\sqrt{2}}$

## Explanation

In a plane wave traveling toward increasing $x$ the magnetic field is $\vec{B} = \dfrac{1}{c}\,\hat{\imath} \times \vec{E}$, because it is perpendicular to $\vec{E}$ and to the direction of propagation, it has magnitude $\dfrac{E}{c}$ and $\vec{E} \times \vec{B}$ points along $\hat{\imath}$.
Since $\hat{\imath} \times \hat{\jmath} = \hat{k}$ and $\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k} = -\hat{\jmath}$, we get $\hat{\imath} \times (\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}) = \hat{k} - \hat{\jmath}$.
Therefore the magnetic field points along **$\dfrac{\hat{k} - \hat{\jmath}}{\sqrt{2}}$**; one checks that $\dfrac{(\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}) \times (\hat{k} - \hat{\jmath})}{2} = \hat{\imath}$.
