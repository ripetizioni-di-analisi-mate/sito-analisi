Suppose that the fourth Maxwell equation in local form were $\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J}$, without Maxwell's term, also for time-varying fields. Which consequence contradicts the experimental facts?

- $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = 0$ everywhere, false near a charge
- $\nabla \cdot \vec{B} \neq 0$, that is, isolated magnetic charges would exist
- Electromagnetic waves would travel at infinite speed
- $\nabla \cdot \vec{J} = 0$ everywhere, false on the plates of a charging capacitor

## Explanation

The divergence of a curl is zero, so taking the divergence of both sides would give $\mu_0\, \nabla \cdot \vec{J} = 0$.
By the continuity equation $\nabla \cdot \vec{J} = -\dfrac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}$, therefore the charge density could not change at any point.
On the plates of a charging capacitor charge accumulates, so there $\nabla \cdot \vec{J} \neq 0$ and the equation without Maxwell's term **is not compatible with conservation of charge**.
