A cylindrical wire of radius $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ carries the steady current $i = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$, and the potential difference between its ends is $V = 1.0\,\mathrm{V}$. Neglecting end effects, what is the Poynting vector on the lateral surface of the wire?

- $4.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, pointing away from the wire
- $8.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, pointing toward the axis of the wire
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, pointing toward the axis of the wire
- $2.5\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, pointing toward the axis of the wire

## Explanation

On the surface the electric field is parallel to the wire, along the current, and equals $E = \dfrac{V}{L}$; the magnetic field is tangent to the circles around the wire and, by Ampère's law, equals $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi a}$.
The two fields are perpendicular, so
$$
S = \frac{E B}{\mu_0} = \frac{V i}{2\pi a L} = \frac{1.0 \cdot 5.0}{2\pi \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 2.0}\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \approx 4.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{W/m^2} .
$$
With the current along $\hat{k}$, at a point of the surface on the $x$ axis the magnetic field points along $\hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{k} \times \hat{\jmath} = -\hat{\imath}$, therefore $\vec{S}$ **points toward the axis of the wire**.
Multiplying $S$ by the lateral area $2\pi a L$ gives $V i$, the power dissipated by the Joule effect, which enters the wire from the outside field.
