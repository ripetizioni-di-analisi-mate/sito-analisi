Outside Earth's atmosphere solar radiation has intensity $I = 1.36\,\mathrm{kW/m^2}$, and the Sun's rays reach Earth practically parallel. Earth is a sphere of radius $R_T = 6.37 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$. How much power of the solar radiation does Earth intercept?

- $3.5 \cdot 10^{17}\,\mathrm{W}$
- $6.9 \cdot 10^{17}\,\mathrm{W}$
- $5.5 \cdot 10^{16}\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^{17}\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The intensity is the power per unit area of a surface perpendicular to the rays.
Earth stops the rays that cross a disk of radius $R_T$ perpendicular to their direction, that is, the cross section of the sphere, so the area to use is $\pi R_T^2$.
Therefore the intercepted power is
$$
P = I \pi R_T^2 = 1.36 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot \pi \cdot \left( 6.37 \cdot 10^{6} \right)^2\,\mathrm{W} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^{17}\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The area of the illuminated hemisphere, $2\pi R_T^2$, would give twice as much, because most of its surface is tilted with respect to the rays.
