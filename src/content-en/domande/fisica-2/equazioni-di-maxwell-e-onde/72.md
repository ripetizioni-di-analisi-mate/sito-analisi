Inside a conductor carrying a steady current, the magnetic field is $\vec{B} = b\left( -y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath} \right)$, with $b = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T/m}$ and $x$, $y$ in meters, and the electric field does not change in time. What is the current density in the conductor?

- $1.6 \cdot 10^{3}\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $-3.2 \cdot 10^{3}\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $3.2 \cdot 10^{3}\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{A/m^2}$

## Explanation

The electric field does not change in time, so the fourth Maxwell equation in differential form becomes $\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J}$.
The magnetic field has components only along $x$ and $y$ and does not depend on $z$, therefore its curl is
$$
\nabla \times \vec{B} = \left( \frac{\partial B_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial B_x}{\partial y} \right)\hat{k} = (b + b)\,\hat{k} = 2b\,\hat{k} .
$$
Hence
$$
\vec{J} = \frac{2b}{\mu_0}\,\hat{k} = \frac{2 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{A/m^2} \approx 3.2 \cdot 10^{3}\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{A/m^2} .
$$
This is the field inside a straight wire along the $z$-axis with uniform current density.
