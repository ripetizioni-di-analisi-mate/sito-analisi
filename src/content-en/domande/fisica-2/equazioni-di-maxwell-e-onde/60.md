At a point of a conductor the volume charge density decreases at the rate $\dfrac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = -3.0\,\mathrm{C/(m^3\cdot s)}$. What is the divergence of the current density $\nabla \cdot \vec{J}$ at that point?

- $-3.0\,\mathrm{A/m^3}$
- $0$
- $3.4 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{A/m^3}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{A/m^3}$

## Explanation

Maxwell's equations in local form imply the **continuity equation** $\nabla \cdot \vec{J} + \dfrac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = 0$.
Therefore $\nabla \cdot \vec{J} = -\dfrac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = 3.0\,\mathrm{A/m^3}$.
The divergence is positive, that is, more current leaves the point than enters it, and this is why the charge decreases.
