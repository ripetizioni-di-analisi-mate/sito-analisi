A flat metal mirror occupies the plane $x = 0$, and in front of it, in vacuum, there is the standing wave with electric field $\vec{E} = 2E_0 \sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}$ and magnetic field directed along $\hat{k}$, with $\omega = ck$. What is the amplitude of the oscillation of the magnetic field on the surface of the mirror?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{E_0}{c}$
- $\dfrac{2E_0}{c}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}\,E_0}{c}$

## Explanation

For a plane wave with $\vec{E} = E(x,t)\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{B} = B(x,t)\,\hat{k}$ the relation $\dfrac{\partial E}{\partial x} = -\dfrac{\partial B}{\partial t}$ holds, which follows from Faraday's law.
Since $\dfrac{\partial E}{\partial x} = 2E_0 k \cos(kx)\cos(\omega t)$, integrating in time and dropping the static field, which is not part of the wave, gives
$$
B(x,t) = -\frac{2E_0 k}{\omega} \cos(kx)\sin(\omega t) = -\frac{2E_0}{c} \cos(kx)\sin(\omega t) .
$$
At $x = 0$ we have $\cos(kx) = 1$, therefore on the surface of the mirror the magnetic field oscillates with amplitude **$\dfrac{2E_0}{c}$**, while the electric field is zero at every instant.
