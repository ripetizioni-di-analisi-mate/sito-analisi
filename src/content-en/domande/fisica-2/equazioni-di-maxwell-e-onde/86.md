A plane wave in vacuum, with average energy density $\langle u \rangle = 3.0\,\mu\mathrm{J/m^3}$, strikes perpendicularly a mirror that reflects it completely. What is the radiation pressure on the mirror?

- $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{Pa}$
- $6.0\,\mu\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.5\,\mu\mathrm{Pa}$
- $12\,\mu\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The intensity of the incident wave is $I = c \langle u \rangle$.
For a mirror the radiation pressure is $\dfrac{2I}{c}$, so
$$
p_{\text{rad}} = \frac{2 c \langle u \rangle}{c} = 2\langle u \rangle = 6.0\,\mu\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The speed $c$ cancels, so on a mirror the pressure is twice the average energy density, while on an absorbing surface it equals it.
