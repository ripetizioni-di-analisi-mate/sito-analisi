A very large plane carries a uniformly distributed charge with surface density $\sigma = +8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$ and produces, on each side, a uniform field perpendicular to the plane of magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$. A charge $q = +3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ moves $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ away from the plane along the perpendicular. How much work does the electric force do?

- $-6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The magnitude of the field is
$$
E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 500\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
The plane is positively charged, so the field points away from the plane, that is in the direction of the displacement.

In a uniform field the work is $W = qE\,d$, with $d$ the projection of the displacement on the direction of the field. Therefore $W = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 500 \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{J} = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}$, positive because the force on the positive charge has the direction of the displacement.
