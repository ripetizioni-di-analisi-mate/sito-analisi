A charge $+2\,\mathrm{nC}$ moves from $r=0{,}20\,\mathrm m$ to $r=0{,}40\,\mathrm m$ relative to $Q=4\,\mathrm{nC}$. How much work does the field do in microjoules? Assume $k=9{,}0\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

- $0{,}09\,\mu\mathrm J$
- $0{,}18\,\mu\mathrm J$
- $0{,}36\,\mu\mathrm J$
- $0{,}72\,\mu\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The work is $kQq(\dfrac{1}{r_A}-\dfrac{1}{r_B})$. The positive charge moves away from a positive source, so the work is positive. The result is $0{,}18\,\mu\mathrm J$.
