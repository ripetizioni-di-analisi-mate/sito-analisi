A thin ring of radius $R$ carries the charge $Q > 0$ uniformly distributed. At what distance from the center, along the axis of the ring, is the potential half of its value at the center? The potential is zero at infinity.

- $R$
- $2R$
- $\sqrt{3}\,R$
- $\sqrt{2}\,R$

## Explanation

All elements of the ring are at distance $\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}$ from the point of the axis at distance $x$ from the center, so the potential at that point is $V(x) = \dfrac{kQ}{\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}}$. At the center we therefore have $V(0) = \dfrac{kQ}{R}$.

The condition $V(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,V(0)$ is equivalent to $\sqrt{R^2 + x^2} = 2R$, that is, to $R^2 + x^2 = 4R^2$.

The required point is therefore at $x = \sqrt{3}\,R$ from the center. The value $2R$ is obtained by treating the ring as a point charge placed at the center.
