A thin spherical shell carries a uniformly distributed charge. What happens to the potential and to the magnitude of the field when crossing the surface of the shell, from inside to outside?

- The potential has a jump and the field varies continuously
- The potential and the field both vary continuously
- The potential and the field both have a jump
- The potential varies continuously and the field has a jump

## Explanation

Inside, the field of the shell is zero and outside it equals $\dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$, so at the surface it goes from $0$ to $\dfrac{kQ}{R^2}$: the field has a **jump**.

The potential equals $\dfrac{kQ}{R}$ throughout the interior and $\dfrac{kQ}{r}$ outside, so at $r = R$ the two values coincide.

The potential is the integral of the field along a radius, and the integral of a bounded function has no jumps, even if the function has some. Therefore the potential varies **continuously**.
