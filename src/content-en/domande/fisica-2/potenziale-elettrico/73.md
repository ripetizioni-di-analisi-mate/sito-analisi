A thin ring of radius $R$ carries a charge distributed with linear density $\lambda(\varphi) = \lambda_0 \cos\varphi$, where $\varphi$ is the angle that locates the point on the ring and $\lambda_0 > 0$. What is the potential at the center of the ring, zero at infinite distance?

- $2k\lambda_0$
- $4k\lambda_0$
- $0$
- $2\pi k\lambda_0$

## Explanation

The ring element of angular width $d\varphi$ has length $R\,d\varphi$ and charge $dq = \lambda_0 \cos\varphi\,R\,d\varphi$, and it is at distance $R$ from the center.

By the potential of a continuous distribution
$$
V = k \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\lambda_0 \cos\varphi\,R}{R}\,d\varphi = k\lambda_0 \int_0^{2\pi} \cos\varphi\,d\varphi = 0 .
$$
All the elements are at distance $R$ from the center, so the potential at the center is $\dfrac{k}{R}$ times the total charge, which is zero. The field at the center, instead, is nonzero, because the positive half and the negative half of the ring produce fields pointing the same way.
