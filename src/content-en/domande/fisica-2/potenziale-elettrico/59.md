Two equal positive point charges $+q$ are fixed at the ends of a segment of length $d$. A small positive test charge can move only along the segment, without friction. At which points of the segment is it in equilibrium, and what kind of equilibrium is it?

- At the midpoint, and the equilibrium is unstable
- At the midpoint, and the equilibrium is stable
- At no point of the segment
- At two points symmetric about the midpoint, both of stable equilibrium

## Explanation

With $s$ the distance from the first charge, the potential along the segment is
$$
V(s) = kq \left( \frac{1}{s} + \frac{1}{d - s} \right), \qquad 0 < s < d .
$$
The derivative $V'(s) = kq \left( -\dfrac{1}{s^2} + \dfrac{1}{(d - s)^2} \right)$ vanishes only for $s = d - s$, that is at the midpoint $s = \dfrac{d}{2}$, where therefore the force on the test charge is zero.

The second derivative $V''(s) = kq \left( \dfrac{2}{s^3} + \dfrac{2}{(d - s)^3} \right)$ is positive, so $V$ has a minimum at the midpoint. The test charge is positive, therefore the potential energy $U = q_0 V$ also has a minimum and the equilibrium is **stable** for displacements along the segment.
