In a region the potential is $V(x, y, z) = a\,(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)$, with $a = 100\,\mathrm{V/m^2}$. Using $\vec{E} = -\nabla V$ and the local form of Gauss's law, $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}$, what is the volume charge density in the region?

- $+5.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$
- $-1.8 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$
- $-5.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$
- $-3.5 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$

## Explanation

The field is $\vec{E} = -\nabla V = -2a\,(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath} + z\,\hat{k})$.

Its divergence is $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = -2a - 2a - 2a = -6a$, a constant.

By the local form of Gauss's law $\rho = \varepsilon_0\,\nabla \cdot \vec{E}$, so
$$
\rho = -6a\,\varepsilon_0 = -6 \cdot 100 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \approx -5.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} .
$$
The sign is consistent with the field, which points toward the origin like that of a negative charge distribution.
