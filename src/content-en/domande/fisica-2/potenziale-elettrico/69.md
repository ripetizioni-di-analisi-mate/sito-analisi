A positive point charge is isolated in a vacuum. Its potential is measured at two distances, with the zero reference at infinity. The larger radius is three times the smaller radius. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Initially, the given quantities are the central charge $q = 8\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the smaller radius $r = 4\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the smaller radius to $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $U_{e}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The potentials are $kq/r$ and $kq/(3r)$, so the difference is $2kq/(3r)$. The coefficient $600$ includes the unit conversions. Applying $U_{e} = \dfrac{600 \cdot q}{r}$ gives $1200\,\mathrm{V}$ before the change and $600\,\mathrm{V}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
