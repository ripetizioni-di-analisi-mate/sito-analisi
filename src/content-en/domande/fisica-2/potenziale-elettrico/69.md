Two thin concentric coplanar rings have radii $R_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $R_2 = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and carry the charges $+Q$ and $-Q$, with $Q = 6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, uniformly distributed. What is the potential at the common center, zero at infinite distance?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29;2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno,O" segmenti="0,0,0.71,0.71;0,0,-1.41,1.41" etichette="0.95,0.4,+Q;2.05,1.05,−Q;0.55,0.2,R₁;-0.55,0.95,R₂" xmin=-2.5 xmax=2.5 ymin=-2.3 ymax=2.3 titolo="Concentric coplanar rings of radii $R_1$ and $R_2$ with uniformly distributed charges $+Q$ and $-Q$"}

- $1.6 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-5.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $5.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The potential of a ring at its center is $\dfrac{kQ}{R}$, because all its elements are at distance $R$ from the center. By the superposition principle the potential at the common center is the sum of the two contributions:
$$
V = \frac{kQ}{R_1} - \frac{kQ}{R_2} = 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 6.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \left( \frac{1}{0.050} - \frac{1}{0.10} \right)\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
Hence $V \approx 54 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{V} \approx 5.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$. The total charge is zero, but the potential at the center is positive, because the positive ring is closer.
