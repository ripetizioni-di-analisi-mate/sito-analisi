A thin spherical shell of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the charge $Q = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ uniformly distributed. What is the electrostatic energy of the distribution, that is, the work needed to bring the charge onto the shell little by little from infinity?

- $0.36\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.22\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.090\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.18\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

When the shell already carries the charge $q$, its potential is $\dfrac{kq}{R}$, so bringing the charge $dq$ from infinity requires the work $\dfrac{kq}{R}\,dq$.

Adding the contributions from $q = 0$ to $q = Q$ gives
$$
U = \int_0^Q \frac{kq}{R}\,dq = \frac{kQ^2}{2R} = \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot (2.0 \cdot 10^{-6})^2}{2 \cdot 0.10}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 0.18\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ appears because the potential of the shell grows from $0$ to $\dfrac{kQ}{R}$ during charging.
