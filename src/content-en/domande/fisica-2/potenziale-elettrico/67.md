A thin disk of radius $R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a uniformly distributed charge with density $\sigma = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$. What is the potential at the point on the axis of the disk at distance $x = R$ from the center, zero at infinite distance?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $35\,\mathrm{V}$
- $41\,\mathrm{V}$
- $83\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The potential of a disk on its axis is $V(x) = \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} \left( \sqrt{R^2 + x^2} - x \right)$.

With the data $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \dfrac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 500\,\mathrm{V/m}$. For $x = R$ one has $\sqrt{R^2 + x^2} - x = (\sqrt{2} - 1)\,R \approx 0.083\,\mathrm{m}$.

Therefore $V \approx 500 \cdot 0.083\,\mathrm{V} \approx 41\,\mathrm{V}$. The disk cannot be replaced by a ring of radius $R$ with the same charge, because its elements are closer to the point than $\sqrt{2}\,R$.
