A positive point charge is isolated in a vacuum. Its potential is measured at two distances, with the zero reference at infinity. The larger radius is three times the smaller radius. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the smaller radius $r = 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the difference between the inner and outer potentials $U_{e} = 1200\,\mathrm{V}$, what is the central charge $q$?

- $8\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $16\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $4\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $\dfrac{16}{3}\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The potentials are $kq/r$ and $kq/(3r)$, so the difference is $2kq/(3r)$. The coefficient $600$ includes the unit conversions. In the indicated units, $U_{e} = \dfrac{600 \cdot q}{r}$. Solving for the unknown gives $q = \dfrac{U_{e} \cdot r}{600}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $8\,\mathrm{nC}$.
