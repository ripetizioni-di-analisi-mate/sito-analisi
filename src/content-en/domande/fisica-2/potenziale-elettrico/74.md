An infinite straight wire carries a uniformly distributed charge. What are its equipotential surfaces?

- Planes perpendicular to the wire
- Spheres centered at a point of the wire
- Cylinders coaxial with the wire
- Planes containing the wire

## Explanation

The potential of the wire depends only on the distance $r$ from the wire, because $V(r) = \dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0} \ln \dfrac{r_0}{r}$.

An equipotential surface is therefore the set of points at the same distance from the wire, that is, a cylinder coaxial with the wire.

The field of the wire is radial and perpendicular to the wire, so it is perpendicular at every point to these cylinders, as must happen for every equipotential surface.
