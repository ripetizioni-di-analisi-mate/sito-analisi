Around a positive point charge $Q$, with the potential zero at infinite distance, the equipotential surface at $300\,\mathrm{V}$ and the one at $100\,\mathrm{V}$ are spheres whose radii differ by $20\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is $Q$?

- $6.7\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $3.3\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $2.2\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $10\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

For a point charge $V = \dfrac{kQ}{r}$, so the radius of the equipotential sphere is inversely proportional to the potential. The sphere at $100\,\mathrm{V}$ therefore has three times the radius of the one at $300\,\mathrm{V}$: $r_2 = 3r_1$.

From the condition $r_2 - r_1 = 2r_1 = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ one gets $r_1 = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$.

Then
$$
Q = \frac{V_1\,r_1}{k} = \frac{300 \cdot 0.10}{8.99 \cdot 10^9}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 3.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C} .
$$
