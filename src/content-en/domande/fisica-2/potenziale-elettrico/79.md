In a region around the origin the potential depends only on the distance $r$ from the origin and equals $V(r) = b\,r^2$, with $b > 0$. What is the electric field in the region like?

- Radial, pointing away from the origin, of magnitude $2br$
- Radial, pointing toward the origin, of magnitude $br$
- Radial, pointing toward the origin, of magnitude $2br$
- Zero throughout the region, because the potential has a minimum at the origin

## Explanation

The potential depends only on $r$, so the field is radial with component $E_r = -\dfrac{dV}{dr}$.

Differentiating gives $E_r = -2br$. Since $b > 0$, the radial component is negative for $r > 0$, therefore the field points **toward the origin** and has magnitude $2br$.

The field is zero only at the origin, where $V$ has its minimum; at the other points the potential increases moving away from the origin and the field points toward lower potentials.
