Two positive charges are at the same distance $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the measurement point in a vacuum. The potential reference is infinity. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Initially, the given quantities are the first charge $q_{1} = 4\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the second charge $q_{2} = 6\,\mathrm{nC}$. In the second configuration, we change the second charge to $10\,\mathrm{nC}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $U_{e}$?

- $3$
- $\dfrac{5}{7}$
- $\dfrac{7}{5}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The potentials add as scalars. Each nanocoulomb at the indicated distance contributes $300\,\mathrm V$. Applying $U_{e} = 300 \cdot q_{1} + 300 \cdot q_{2}$ gives $3000\,\mathrm{V}$ before the change and $4200\,\mathrm{V}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{7}{5}$.
