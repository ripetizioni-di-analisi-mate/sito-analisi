With $a$ a positive constant, which of the following fields in the $xy$-plane **cannot** be an electrostatic field?

- $\vec{E} = a\,(y\,\hat{\imath} - x\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $\vec{E} = a\,(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $\vec{E} = a\,(2xy\,\hat{\imath} + x^2\,\hat{\jmath})$
- $\vec{E} = a\,(x\,\hat{\imath} - y\,\hat{\jmath})$

## Explanation

An electrostatic field has zero circulation along every closed curve, so in local form it satisfies $\nabla \times \vec{E} = \vec{0}$. For a field in the plane it suffices to check the $z$-component of the curl, $\dfrac{\partial E_y}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial E_x}{\partial y}$.

For $a\,(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath})$ and for $a\,(x\,\hat{\imath} - y\,\hat{\jmath})$ one gets $0 - 0 = 0$. For $a\,(2xy\,\hat{\imath} + x^2\,\hat{\jmath})$ one gets $2ax - 2ax = 0$.

For $a\,(y\,\hat{\imath} - x\,\hat{\jmath})$, instead, one gets $-a - a = -2a \neq 0$. Its field lines are circles around the origin, and the circulation along one of them is nonzero; therefore this field **is not electrostatic**.
