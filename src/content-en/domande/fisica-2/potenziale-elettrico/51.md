Two charges $+Q$ and $-Q$, with $Q > 0$, are at rest at distance $2a$. A test charge $q_0 > 0$ goes from the midpoint $M$ to the point $P$ on the line of the charges that is at distance $2a$ from $-Q$ and $4a$ from $+Q$, as in the figure. What is the work done by the electric force, with the potential zero at infinite distance?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1.6,0 3.6,0" punti="-1,0,pieno,+Q;1,0,pieno,−Q;0,0,pieno,M;3,0,pieno,P" segmenti="-1,-0.4,0,-0.4;0,-0.4,1,-0.4;1,-0.4,3,-0.4" etichette="-0.5,-0.65,a;0.5,-0.65,a;2,-0.65,2a" xmin=-1.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-1 ymax=0.7 titolo="Charges $+Q$ and $-Q$ at distance $2a$, midpoint $M$ and point $P$ on the line of the charges"}

- $-\dfrac{kQq_0}{4a}$
- $\dfrac{3kQq_0}{4a}$
- $\dfrac{kQq_0}{2a}$
- $\dfrac{kQq_0}{4a}$

## Explanation

The work of the field is $W = q_0\,(V_M - V_P)$, and the potentials are computed by adding the contributions of the two charges.

The point $M$ is at distance $a$ from both charges, so $V_M = \dfrac{kQ}{a} - \dfrac{kQ}{a} = 0$.

The point $P$ is at distance $4a$ from $+Q$ and $2a$ from $-Q$, therefore
$$
V_P = \frac{kQ}{4a} - \frac{kQ}{2a} = -\frac{kQ}{4a} .
$$
Then $W = q_0 \left( 0 + \dfrac{kQ}{4a} \right) = \dfrac{kQq_0}{4a}$, positive because the positive charge moves toward lower potentials.
