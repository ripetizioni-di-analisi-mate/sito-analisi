A proton and an alpha particle, of charge $2e$ and mass about four times that of the proton, start from rest and are accelerated by the same potential difference. Which statement is correct, neglecting weight?

- The alpha particle has twice the kinetic energy and a higher speed
- The two particles have the same kinetic energy and the alpha particle is slower
- The alpha particle has four times the kinetic energy and the same speed
- The alpha particle has twice the kinetic energy and a lower speed

## Explanation

The kinetic energy acquired is $K = |q|\,|\Delta V|$, so it depends on the charge and not on the mass. The alpha particle has twice the charge, therefore it has **twice** the kinetic energy.

The speed is $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m}}$. For the alpha particle $K$ is doubled and $m$ is quadrupled, so $\dfrac{K}{m}$ is half that of the proton.

Then the speed of the alpha particle is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ times that of the proton, that is, **lower**.
