Two positive charges are at the same distance $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the measurement point in a vacuum. The potential reference is infinity. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the first charge $q_{1} = 4\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the second charge $q_{2} = 6\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the potential at the point $U_{e}$?

- $6000\,\mathrm{V}$
- $3000\,\mathrm{V}$
- $600\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1500\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The potentials add as scalars. Each nanocoulomb at the indicated distance contributes $300\,\mathrm V$. In the indicated units, $U_{e} = 300 \cdot q_{1} + 300 \cdot q_{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $3000\,\mathrm{V}$.
