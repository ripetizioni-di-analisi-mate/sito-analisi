In a uniform field $\vec{E} = E\,\hat{\imath}$, with $E = 5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$, a charge $q = +2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ travels along the semicircle of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from point $A$ to the diametrically opposite point $B$, as in the figure. What is the work done by the electric force?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,3.1416" vettori="-1.5,-0.45,-0.5,-0.45,E;0.5,-0.45,1.5,-0.45;0.15,1,-0.15,1" punti="1,0,pieno,A;-1,0,pieno,B;0,0,pieno,O" segmenti="0,0,1,0" etichette="0.5,0.13,R" unicolore=1 xmin=-1.8 xmax=1.8 ymin=-0.8 ymax=1.4 titolo="Semicircle with center $O$ and radius $R$ traveled from $A$ to $B$ in the uniform field $\vec{E}$"}

- $-2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $+2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3.1 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The electrostatic field is conservative, so the work depends only on $A$ and $B$ and can be computed along the diameter.

In a uniform field $W = q\,\vec{E} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB}$. The displacement $\overrightarrow{AB}$ has length $2R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and points opposite to the field, therefore $\vec{E} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = -E \cdot 2R$.

Then
$$
W = -2.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{J} = -2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The result contains only the projection of the displacement onto the direction of the field, and the length $\pi R$ of the arc does not appear.
