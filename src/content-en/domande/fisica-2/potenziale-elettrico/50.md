In a region the electric field is $\vec{E} = E_0\,\dfrac{y}{\ell}\,\hat{\imath}$ for $y > 0$, with $E_0$ and $\ell$ positive constants: the field lines are straight lines parallel to the $x$-axis and the magnitude grows with the distance from the axis, as in the figure. Can this field be produced by charges at rest?

::grafico{vettori="0.3,0.5,0.8,0.5;0.3,1,1.3,1;0.3,1.5,1.8,1.5;0.3,2,2.3,2;0.3,2.5,2.8,2.5;0.3,3,3.3,3,E" unicolore=1 nomi="x,y" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4 ymin=-0.3 ymax=3.5 titolo="Field parallel to the $x$-axis with magnitude proportional to $y$"}

- Yes, because the field lines are straight and therefore do not close
- No, because the circulation along a rectangle with two sides parallel to the $x$-axis is nonzero
- Yes, because the field has the same direction at every point
- No, because an electrostatic field cannot have parallel field lines

## Explanation

Compute the circulation along a rectangle with horizontal sides at heights $y_1 < y_2$ and length $s$, traversed counterclockwise.

On the vertical sides the field is perpendicular to the displacement, so the contribution is zero. On the lower side, traversed in the direction of the field, the contribution is $E_0\,\dfrac{y_1}{\ell}\,s$; on the upper side, traversed in the opposite direction, it is $-E_0\,\dfrac{y_2}{\ell}\,s$.

Therefore the circulation is $-\dfrac{E_0\,s}{\ell}\,(y_2 - y_1) \neq 0$. A field produced by charges at rest has zero circulation along every closed curve, so this field **is not electrostatic**. Parallel lines in themselves are compatible with an electrostatic field, as the uniform field shows.
