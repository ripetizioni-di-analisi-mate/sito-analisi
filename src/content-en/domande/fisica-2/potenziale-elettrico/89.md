A beam of electrons with kinetic energy $3.0\,\mathrm{eV}$ crosses two parallel grids perpendicularly; the second grid is at potential $-2.0\,\mathrm{V}$ relative to the first. With what kinetic energy do the electrons pass beyond the second grid?

- $5.0\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{eV}$
- No electron reaches the second grid
- $1.0\,\mathrm{eV}$

## Explanation

The electrons go from the first grid to the second, so they cross the potential difference $\Delta V = -2.0\,\mathrm{V}$.

Their potential energy changes by $\Delta U = q\,\Delta V = (-e) \cdot (-2.0\,\mathrm{V}) = +2.0\,\mathrm{eV}$. By conservation of energy the kinetic energy decreases by the same amount.

Since $3.0\,\mathrm{eV}$ is greater than $2.0\,\mathrm{eV}$, the electrons pass beyond the second grid with kinetic energy $3.0\,\mathrm{eV} - 2.0\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.0\,\mathrm{eV}$. If the retarding potential difference exceeded $3.0\,\mathrm{V}$, the electrons would stop before.
