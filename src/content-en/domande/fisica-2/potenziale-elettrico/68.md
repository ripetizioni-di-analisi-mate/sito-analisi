Three point charges are brought slowly, one at a time, from infinite distance to their final positions. Does the total external work depend on the order in which the charges are brought?

- Yes, because the first charge brought requires no work
- Yes, because the charge brought last moves in the potential of the other two
- No, because the total external work is always zero
- No, because it equals the sum of the terms $\dfrac{k\,q_i q_j}{r_{ij}}$ over all pairs, which depends only on the final positions

## Explanation

With the charges $q_1$, $q_2$, $q_3$ brought in this order, the first requires no work, the second requires $\dfrac{k\,q_1 q_2}{r_{12}}$ and the third $q_3 \left( \dfrac{k q_1}{r_{13}} + \dfrac{k q_2}{r_{23}} \right)$.

The sum is
$$
W = \frac{k\,q_1 q_2}{r_{12}} + \frac{k\,q_1 q_3}{r_{13}} + \frac{k\,q_2 q_3}{r_{23}} ,
$$
that is one term for each pair. With a different order the same three terms are obtained, added in another order.

Therefore the total external work does not depend on the order and equals the electrostatic energy of the system, which depends only on the charges and on their final distances.
