An electron starts from rest at distance $1.0\,\mathrm{nm}$ from a proton held fixed. What is its speed when its distance from the proton has become $0.50\,\mathrm{nm}$? Neglect its weight.

- $1.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The only force is the electric one, so the sum of the kinetic energy and the potential energy $U(r) = -\dfrac{ke^2}{r}$ is conserved.

The final kinetic energy is the decrease in potential energy:
$$
K = ke^2 \left( \frac{1}{r_2} - \frac{1}{r_1} \right) = 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot (1.60 \cdot 10^{-19})^2 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Therefore $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m_e}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \cdot 2.3 \cdot 10^{-19}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 7.1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
