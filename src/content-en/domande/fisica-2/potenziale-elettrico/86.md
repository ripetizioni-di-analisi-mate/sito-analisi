In a vacuum, two positive charges fixed at different points produce potentials $V_1$ and $V_2$ at the point to which a third charge of $2\,\mathrm{nC}$ is brought. Given the potential of the second source $V_{2} = 35\,\mathrm{V}$ and the increase in interaction energy due to the third charge $U = 100\,\mathrm{nJ}$, what is the potential of the first source $V_{1}$?

- $65\,\mathrm{V}$
- $15\,\mathrm{V}$
- $30\,\mathrm{V}$
- $\dfrac{15}{2}\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The two pairs involving the new charge are added to the existing interactions. The added energy is the charge times the total potential produced by the sources. In the indicated units, $U = 2 \cdot V_{1} + 2 \cdot V_{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $V_{1} = \dfrac{U}{2} - V_{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $15\,\mathrm{V}$.
