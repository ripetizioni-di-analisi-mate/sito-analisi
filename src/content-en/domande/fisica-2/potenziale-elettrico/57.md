The electric field is directed along the $x$-axis: it equals $E_x = E_0$, with $E_0 > 0$, in the strip $0 < x < d$ and is zero for $x < 0$ and for $x > d$. Which description of the potential $V(x)$ is correct?

- $V$ is constant for $x < 0$, increases linearly for $0 < x < d$ and is constant for $x > d$
- $V$ is zero for $x < 0$ and for $x > d$ and decreases linearly for $0 < x < d$
- $V$ is constant for $x < 0$, decreases linearly for $0 < x < d$ and is constant for $x > d$
- $V$ decreases linearly for $x < 0$ and for $x > d$ and is constant for $0 < x < d$

## Explanation

The field is the derivative of the potential with the sign changed, $E_x = -\dfrac{dV}{dx}$.

Where the field is zero, $\dfrac{dV}{dx} = 0$, so $V$ is constant for $x < 0$ and for $x > d$. In the strip $\dfrac{dV}{dx} = -E_0 < 0$, therefore $V$ decreases linearly, with slope $-E_0$.

The potential is continuous, so the constant value for $x > d$ is lower than the one for $x < 0$ by $E_0\,d$. For this reason $V$ cannot be zero in both outer regions.
