A system consists of three point charges $q_1 = q_2 = +2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q_3 = -3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. The potential produced by the other two charges is $-30\,\mathrm{V}$ at the position of $q_1$, $-30\,\mathrm{V}$ at the position of $q_2$ and $+120\,\mathrm{V}$ at the position of $q_3$, with zero potential at infinite distance. What is the electrostatic energy of the system?

- $-2.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-4.8 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $+2.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-6.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The energy of the system can be written as $U = \dfrac{1}{2} \displaystyle\sum_i q_i V_i$, where $V_i$ is the potential produced by the other charges at the position of $q_i$. The factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ appears because in the sum each pair is counted twice.

With the data
$$
\sum_i q_i V_i = \big( 2.0 \cdot (-30) + 2.0 \cdot (-30) + (-3.0) \cdot 120 \big) \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J} = -4.8 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Therefore $U = -2.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}$.
