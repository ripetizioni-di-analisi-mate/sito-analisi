Two positive point charges $q$ and $4q$ are fixed at a distance $L$ from each other. Along the segment joining them the electric potential has a minimum. At what distance from the charge $q$ is this minimum?

- $\dfrac{L}{3}$
- $\dfrac{L}{5}$
- $\dfrac{L}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2L}{3}$

## Explanation

At the point of the segment at distance $x$ from the charge $q$, with $0 < x < L$, the potential is $V(x) = \dfrac{kq}{x} + \dfrac{4kq}{L - x}$.

At the minimum the derivative of $V$ is zero, that is, the field component along the segment, $E_x = -\dfrac{dV}{dx}$, is zero. Therefore $\dfrac{kq}{x^2} = \dfrac{4kq}{(L - x)^2}$, and since the distances are positive, $L - x = 2x$.

The minimum is therefore at $x = \dfrac{L}{3}$ from the charge $q$. The value $\dfrac{L}{5}$ is obtained by equating the two contributions to the potential instead of the two fields.
