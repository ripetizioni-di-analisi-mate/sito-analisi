In a region the electric field is $\vec{E} = a\,(y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$, with $a = 100\,\mathrm{V/m^2}$. A charge $q = 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ goes from the origin $O$ to the point $B = (2.0\,\mathrm{m};\ 3.0\,\mathrm{m})$ along an arbitrary curve. What is the work done by the electric force?

- $-6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The field has zero curl, because $\dfrac{\partial E_y}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial E_x}{\partial y} = a - a = 0$; hence it is conservative and the work does not depend on the curve.

A potential of the field is $V(x, y) = -a\,x y$, since $-\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial x} = a y = E_x$ and $-\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial y} = a x = E_y$.

Therefore $V(O) = 0$ and $V(B) = -100 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{V} = -600\,\mathrm{V}$, and the work is
$$
W = q\,\big( V(O) - V(B) \big) = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 600\,\mathrm{J} = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
