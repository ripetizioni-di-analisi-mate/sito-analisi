In a region the electric field is directed along the $x$-axis and has component $E_x(x) = b\,x$, with $b = 500\,\mathrm{V/m^2}$. How much work does the electric force do on a charge $q = +2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ that moves along the axis from $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ to $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $8.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The field varies along the path, so the work is computed with the line integral $W = q \displaystyle\int \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{\ell}$.

The displacement is along the $x$-axis, therefore $\vec{E} \cdot d\vec{\ell} = b\,x\,dx$ and
$$
W = q\,b \int_{1}^{3} x\,dx = q\,b\,\frac{3^2 - 1^2}{2}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 4\,q\,b\,\mathrm{m^2} .
$$
Substituting gives $W = 4 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 500\,\mathrm{J} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$, positive because the positive charge moves in the direction of the field.
