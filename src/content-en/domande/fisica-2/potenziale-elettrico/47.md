Two positive charges are at the same distance $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the measurement point in a vacuum. The potential reference is infinity. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the second charge $q_{2} = 6\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the potential at the point $U_{e} = 3000\,\mathrm{V}$, what is the first charge $q_{1}$?

- $2\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $14\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $4\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $8\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The potentials add as scalars. Each nanocoulomb at the indicated distance contributes $300\,\mathrm V$. In the indicated units, $U_{e} = 300 \cdot q_{1} + 300 \cdot q_{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $q_{1} = \dfrac{U_{e}}{300} - q_{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $4\,\mathrm{nC}$.
