An electric dipole has moment $p = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C \cdot m}$. At large distance its potential is $V = \dfrac{k\,p \cos\theta}{r^2}$. What is the magnitude of the field at a point on the axis of the dipole at $r = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center, much larger than the distance between the charges?

- $6.7 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $40\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

On the axis of the dipole $\theta = 0$, and by symmetry the field is directed along the axis, that is, along the radial direction. By the field as the gradient of the potential, the radial component is $E_r = -\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial r}$.

Differentiating with respect to $r$ at fixed $\theta$ gives
$$
E_r = \frac{2k\,p \cos\theta}{r^3} , \qquad \text{so on the axis} \qquad E = \frac{2kp}{r^3} .
$$
Substituting, $E = \dfrac{2 \cdot 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-12}}{(0.030)^3}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$.
