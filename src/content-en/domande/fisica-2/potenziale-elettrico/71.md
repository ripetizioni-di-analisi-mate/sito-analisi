A thin spherical shell of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the charge $Q = -3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ uniformly distributed, and at its center there is a point charge $q = +2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. What is the potential at the point $P$ at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center, zero at infinite distance?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno,q;1,0,pieno,P" segmenti="0,0,1.41,1.41" etichette="0.55,0.85,R;1.6,1.6,Q" xmin=-2.4 xmax=2.4 ymin=-2.3 ymax=2.3 titolo="Thin spherical shell of radius $R$ with charge $Q$, point charge $q$ at the center and point $P$ at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center"}

- $90\,\mathrm{V}$
- $3.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-1.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-90\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

By the superposition principle the potential at $P$ is the sum of the potential of the point charge and that of the shell.

The charge $q$ is $\dfrac{R}{2} = 0.050\,\mathrm{m}$ from $P$, so it contributes $\dfrac{kq}{0.050\,\mathrm{m}} \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$. Inside the shell the potential of the shell is constant and equals $\dfrac{kQ}{R} \approx -2.7 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$.

Therefore
$$
V_P = 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \left( \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.050} - \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.10} \right)\,\mathrm{V} \approx 90\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
