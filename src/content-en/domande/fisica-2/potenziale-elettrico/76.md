In a region of the plane the equipotential lines of a uniform field are the straight lines in the figure, with the potentials shown and the coordinates in centimeters. What are the magnitude, direction and sense of the field at point $P$?

::grafico{linee="0,1 1,0;0,2 2,0;0,3 3,0;0,4 4,0" punti="1.5,1,pieno,P" etichette="0.35,0.9,40 V;0.35,1.9,30 V;0.35,2.9,20 V;0.35,3.9,10 V" nomi="x,y" uguale=1 xmin=-0.3 xmax=4.4 ymin=-0.3 ymax=4.4 titolo="Equipotential lines of a uniform field in the plane, with coordinates in centimeters"}

- $1.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$, perpendicular to the lines and pointing down and to the left
- $1.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$, perpendicular to the lines and pointing up and to the right
- $1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$, parallel to the $x$-axis and pointing to the right
- $7.1 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V/m}$, perpendicular to the lines and pointing up and to the right

## Explanation

The field is perpendicular to the equipotential lines and points toward decreasing potentials. The lines have equation $x + y = $ constant and the potential decreases moving away from the origin, so the field is directed along the bisector, **up and to the right**.

Two consecutive lines, for example $x + y = 2\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $x + y = 3\,\mathrm{cm}$, are $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{cm} \approx 0.71\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart measured along the perpendicular, and differ by $10\,\mathrm{V}$.

Therefore
$$
E = \frac{|\Delta V|}{\Delta s} = \frac{10}{0.0071}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 1.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
