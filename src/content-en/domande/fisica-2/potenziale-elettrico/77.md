Two equal point charges $+q$ are at rest at distance $d$. What shape do the equipotential surfaces have at distances from the midpoint much larger than $d$?

- They are nearly planes perpendicular to the line joining the charges
- They are nearly cylinders whose axis is the line joining the charges
- They are nearly planes containing the line joining the charges
- They are nearly spheres centered at the midpoint, as for a charge $2q$

## Explanation

At a point at distance $r$ from the midpoint, with $r$ much larger than $d$, the distances from the two charges differ from $r$ by less than $\dfrac{d}{2}$, so they are both approximately equal to $r$.

Therefore the potential is approximately $\dfrac{kq}{r} + \dfrac{kq}{r} = \dfrac{k\,(2q)}{r}$, the potential of a charge $2q$ placed at the midpoint.

It follows that at large distance the equipotential surfaces are **nearly spheres** centered at the midpoint. Near the charges, instead, the shape is different: around each charge the surfaces are small separate spheres.
