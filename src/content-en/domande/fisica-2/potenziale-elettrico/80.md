Two opposite charges of equal magnitude $q$ are separated by a distance of $r$. The potential energy of the pair is zero at infinity. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the separation $r = 8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the potential energy of the pair $U = -1800\,\mathrm{nJ}$, what is the magnitude of each charge $q$?

- $2\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $4 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $8\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $4\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The energy of the pair is $kq_1q_2/r$. The product of the charges is negative and only one pair is counted. In the indicated units, $U = - \dfrac{900 \cdot q^{2}}{r}$. Solving for the unknown gives $q = \dfrac{\sqrt{r} \cdot \sqrt{- U}}{30}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $4\,\mathrm{nC}$.
