Along the $x$-axis the potential is $V(x) = a\,(x^3 - 12\,x)$, with $x$ in meters and $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{V/m^3}$. A small positive charge can move only along the $x$-axis. At which point is it in stable equilibrium?

- $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = -2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 0$
- At both points $x = \pm 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The force on the charge is $F_x = q E_x = -q\,\dfrac{dV}{dx} = -q a\,(3x^2 - 12)$, so it vanishes for $x = \pm 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

The equilibrium is stable where the potential energy $U = qV$ has a minimum. The charge is positive, so the minima of $U$ coincide with the minima of $V$.

Since $V''(x) = 6ax$, one has $V''(2) > 0$ and $V''(-2) < 0$. Therefore $V$ has a minimum at $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, where the equilibrium is **stable**, and a maximum at $x = -2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, where it is unstable.
