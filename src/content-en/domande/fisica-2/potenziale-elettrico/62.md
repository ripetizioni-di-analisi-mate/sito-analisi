For a point charge $Q = +2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ the potential is chosen to be zero on the sphere of radius $r_0 = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ centered on the charge, instead of at infinite distance. With this choice, what is the potential at distance $r = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the charge?

- $90\,\mathrm{V}$
- $72\,\mathrm{V}$
- $18\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The potential is defined up to an additive constant. With zero at infinite distance it equals $\dfrac{kQ}{r}$; with zero at $r_0$ the value $\dfrac{kQ}{r_0}$ is subtracted at every point, hence
$$
V(r) = kQ \left( \frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{r_0} \right) .
$$
With the data $V = 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot (5.0 - 1.0)\,\mathrm{V} \approx 72\,\mathrm{V}$.

Potential differences and the field do not change with the new choice; only the value of the potential at individual points changes.
