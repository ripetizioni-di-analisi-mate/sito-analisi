A positive point charge is isolated in a vacuum. Its potential is measured at two distances, with the zero reference at infinity. The larger radius is three times the smaller radius. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the central charge $q = 8\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the smaller radius $r = 4\,\mathrm{cm}$, what is the difference between the inner and outer potentials $U_{e}$?

- $600\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1200\,\mathrm{V}$
- $2400\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1800\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The potentials are $kq/r$ and $kq/(3r)$, so the difference is $2kq/(3r)$. The coefficient $600$ includes the unit conversions. In the indicated units, $U_{e} = \dfrac{600 \cdot q}{r}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $1200\,\mathrm{V}$.
