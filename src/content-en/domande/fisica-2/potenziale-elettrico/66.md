Two point charges $+2q$ and $-q$, with $q > 0$, are at rest at distance $d$. Considering only the points of the line joining them, at finite distance, which statement about the points where the potential (zero at infinite distance) vanishes and the points where the field vanishes is correct?

- The potential vanishes at two points and the field at only one point
- The potential and the field vanish at the same two points
- The potential vanishes at only one point and the field at two points
- The potential vanishes at only one point, between the charges, and the field at no point

## Explanation

Place $+2q$ at $x = 0$ and $-q$ at $x = d$. The potential vanishes where $\dfrac{2}{|x|} = \dfrac{1}{|x - d|}$, that is, where the distance from $+2q$ is twice the distance from $-q$. This happens at $x = \dfrac{2}{3}d$, between the charges, and at $x = 2d$, beyond the charge $-q$; to the left of $+2q$ the distance from $+2q$ is always smaller, so there are no solutions there.

Between the charges the two fields point the same way, so the field does not vanish. To the left of $+2q$ the field of $+2q$ has a larger magnitude than that of $-q$, because the charge is larger and closer, so the field does not vanish there either. Beyond $-q$ the fields point opposite ways and the magnitudes are equal where $\dfrac{2}{x^2} = \dfrac{1}{(x - d)^2}$, that is, at $x = (2 + \sqrt{2})\,d$.

Therefore the potential vanishes at **two points** and the field at **only one point**, different from both.
