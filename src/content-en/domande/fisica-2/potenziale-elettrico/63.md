In a region with an electrostatic field, can the $20\,\mathrm{V}$ equipotential surface and the $30\,\mathrm{V}$ equipotential surface have a point in common?

- Yes, at the points where the field is zero
- No, because at the common point the potential would have two different values
- Yes, at the points where the field is very strong
- No, because equipotential surfaces are always parallel planes

## Explanation

The potential is a function of the point, so at each point it has only one value.

A point common to the two surfaces would have potential $20\,\mathrm{V}$, because it belongs to the first, and $30\,\mathrm{V}$, because it belongs to the second. Therefore two equipotential surfaces with different potentials have no points in common.

Equipotential surfaces have different shapes depending on the charges: for a point charge they are concentric spheres, which indeed do not intersect.
