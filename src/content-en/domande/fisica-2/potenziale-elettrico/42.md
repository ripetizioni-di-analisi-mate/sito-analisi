A thin ring of radius $R$ carries the charge $Q$ uniformly distributed, and at its center the electric field is zero. What is the potential at the center, taken as zero at infinity?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{kQ}{R}$
- $\dfrac{kQ}{R^2}$
- $\dfrac{2kQ}{R^2}$

## Explanation

The field at the center is zero because the contributions of two diametrically opposite elements of the ring are equal and opposite vectors.

The potential, on the other hand, is a scalar quantity, and all elements of the ring are at distance $R$ from the center. The contributions $\dfrac{k\,dq}{R}$ therefore all have the same sign and add up, so $V = \dfrac{kQ}{R}$.

The answer $0$ comes from deducing a zero potential from a zero field, while $\dfrac{kQ}{R^2}$ has the dimensions of an electric field.
