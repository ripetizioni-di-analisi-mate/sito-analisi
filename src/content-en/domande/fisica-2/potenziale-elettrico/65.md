A dipole consists of the charges $+q$ and $-q$ at distance $d$. A test charge $q_0$ is brought from point $P$, on the line perpendicular to the dipole through its center, to the center $O$, along that line, as in the figure. What is the work done by the electric force?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="-0.5,0,pieno,−q;0.5,0,pieno,+q;0,0,pieno,O;0,3.2,pieno,P" segmenti="0,0,0,3.6" vettori="0.3,2.8,0.3,0.6" etichette="0.25,-0.35,d" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-0.7 ymax=3.8 titolo="Test charge brought from $P$, on the line perpendicular to the dipole through its center, to the center $O$"}

- It is positive, because the field at the center of the dipole is nonzero
- It is negative, because the test charge gets closer to the charge $+q$
- It is zero
- It depends on the distance of $P$ from the dipole

## Explanation

Every point of the line perpendicular to the dipole through its center is equidistant from the two charges, so the two contributions to the potential are opposite and the potential is zero.

In particular $V_P = 0$ and $V_O = 0$, therefore the work of the field is $W = q_0\,(V_P - V_O) = 0$.

Along this line the field of the dipole is parallel to the dipole, hence perpendicular to the displacement; therefore the electric force does no work on any segment, even though the field is nonzero.
