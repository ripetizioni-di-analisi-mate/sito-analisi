The graph shows the potential energy $U(r)$ of two point charges as a function of their distance $r$, with $U = 0$ at infinite distance. What can be said about the charges and the force between them?

::grafico{f="-1/x" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=0.6 nomi="r,U" legenda=0 titolo="Potential energy $U(r)$ of two point charges as a function of distance, zero at infinite distance"}

- The charges have the same sign and the force is repulsive
- The charges have opposite signs and the force is attractive
- The charges have opposite signs and the force is repulsive
- The charges have the same sign and the force is attractive

## Explanation

The potential energy of two charges is $U(r) = \dfrac{k\,q_1 q_2}{r}$, and the graph shows $U < 0$ for every $r$. Hence $q_1 q_2 < 0$ and the charges have **opposite signs**.

The radial component of the force is $F_r = -\dfrac{dU}{dr}$. The graph is increasing, therefore $\dfrac{dU}{dr} > 0$ and $F_r < 0$, so the force is directed toward the other charge.

Then the force is **attractive**, as it must be for two charges of opposite sign.
