Why is the potential of an infinite wire not usually set to zero at infinity?

- The wire produces no field
- The potential is a vector
- The potential difference diverges logarithmically at infinity
- The wire's charge is always zero

## Explanation

The field of the wire varies as $\dfrac{1}{r}$. The radial integral contains $\ln r$. A reference at a finite distance is therefore chosen.
