The graph shows the potential $V$ as a function of the coordinate $x$ in a region where the field is directed along the $x$-axis. What is the component $E_x$ of the field for $4\,\mathrm{cm} < x < 6\,\mathrm{cm}$?

::grafico{f="x<2 ? 1.5*x : (x<4 ? 3 : 3-2*(x-4))" punti="2,3,pieno;4,3,pieno;6,-1,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,3;4,0,4,3;6,0,6,-1" etichette="2,3.4,30 V;4,3.4,30 V;6.5,-1.2,−10 V" nomi="x,V" xmin=0 xmax=7.5 ymin=-1.8 ymax=4 titolo="Potential $V$ as a function of $x$: centimeters on the $x$-axis, tens of volts on the $V$-axis"}

- $-2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $+2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $+1.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

The field is obtained from the potential with $E_x = -\dfrac{dV}{dx}$, and on the straight segment the derivative is the slope of the graph.

Between $x = 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $x = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ the potential goes from $30\,\mathrm{V}$ to $-10\,\mathrm{V}$, so the slope is
$$
\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta x} = \frac{-10 - 30}{0.020}\,\mathrm{V/m} = -2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
Therefore $E_x = +2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$: the field points in the direction of increasing $x$, that is, toward lower potentials.
