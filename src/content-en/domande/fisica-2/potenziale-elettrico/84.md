Two point charges $+q$ are at rest at distance $2a$. A third charge $-q$ is brought slowly from infinity to the midpoint of the segment joining them. By how much does the electrostatic energy of the system change?

- $+2\,\dfrac{kq^2}{a}$
- $-2\,\dfrac{kq^2}{a}$
- $-\dfrac{kq^2}{a}$
- $-4\,\dfrac{kq^2}{a}$

## Explanation

The pair formed by the two charges $+q$ does not change, so the change in energy is given only by the two new terms containing $-q$.

Each charge $+q$ is at distance $a$ from the midpoint, therefore the potential of the two charges at the midpoint is $V = \dfrac{2kq}{a}$.

The change in energy is the product of the added charge and this potential, $\Delta U = -q \cdot \dfrac{2kq}{a} = -2\,\dfrac{kq^2}{a}$. The external work equals $\Delta U$ and is negative, because the charge $-q$ is attracted toward its final position.
