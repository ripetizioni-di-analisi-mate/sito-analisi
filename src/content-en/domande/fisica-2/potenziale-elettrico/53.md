A thin rod of length $L = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the charge $Q = 3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ uniformly distributed. Point $P$ lies on the extension of the rod, at distance $d = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the nearer end. What is the potential at $P$, zero at infinite distance?

- $2.7 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $54\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The linear charge density is $\lambda = \dfrac{Q}{L} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m}$. Let $s$ be the distance from $P$ of an element $ds$ of the rod, which carries the charge $dq = \lambda\,ds$; then $s$ ranges from $d$ to $d + L$.

By the potential of a continuous distribution
$$
V = k \int_{d}^{d + L} \frac{\lambda\,ds}{s} = k\lambda \ln \frac{d + L}{d} .
$$
With the data $\dfrac{d + L}{d} = 4$, hence $V = 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot \ln 4\,\mathrm{V} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$.

The value differs from that of a point charge $Q$ placed at the center of the rod, $\dfrac{kQ}{0.25\,\mathrm{m}} \approx 1.1 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$, because $P$ is not far from the rod compared with its length.
