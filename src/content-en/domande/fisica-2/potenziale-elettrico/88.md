A small ball of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{g}$ and charge $+4.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ is in a uniform vertical electric field, pointing downward, of magnitude $1.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V/m}$. The ball starts from rest and descends $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. With $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglecting air resistance, what speed does it reach?

- $5.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The weight and the electric force are both conservative, so the sum of the kinetic energy, the gravitational potential energy and the electric potential energy is conserved.

Descending by $h = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ the gravitational potential energy decreases by $mgh = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{J} \approx 9.8 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$. The positive charge moves in the direction of the field, so the electric potential energy also decreases, by $qEh = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^4 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{J} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{J}$.

Therefore the final kinetic energy is about $3.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{J}$ and
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 5.5\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
