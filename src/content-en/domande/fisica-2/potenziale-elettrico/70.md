A thin ring of radius $R = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the charge $Q = 2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ uniformly distributed and is held fixed. A proton is launched from very far away along the axis of the ring, toward its center. What minimum initial kinetic energy does it need to pass through the ring? Neglect its weight.

- $3.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{eV}$

## Explanation

On the axis the potential of the ring is $V(x) = \dfrac{kQ}{\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}}$, which has its maximum value $\dfrac{kQ}{R}$ at the center. The proton passes through the ring if it reaches the center with nonnegative kinetic energy.

By conservation of energy, with $V = 0$ at infinity, the condition is $K_0 \geq e\,\dfrac{kQ}{R}$.

Since $\dfrac{kQ}{R} = \dfrac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.030}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 6.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$, the minimum energy is $K_0 \approx 6.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{eV}$.
