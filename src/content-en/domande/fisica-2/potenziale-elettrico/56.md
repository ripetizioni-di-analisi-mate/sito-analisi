An isolated conducting sphere of radius $R = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the charge $Q = +1.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. What minimum speed must an electron have when it leaves the surface of the sphere in order to move away indefinitely? Gravity is neglected.

- $1.3 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.2 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Outside, the sphere produces the same potential as a point charge $Q$ at its center, so at the surface the potential is $\dfrac{kQ}{R} = \dfrac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.010}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 899\,\mathrm{V}$, while at infinite distance it is zero.

The electron has charge $-e$, therefore as it moves from the surface to infinite distance its potential energy increases by $e \cdot 899\,\mathrm{V} = 899\,\mathrm{eV}$. By conservation of energy the electron moves away indefinitely if its initial kinetic energy is at least equal to this increase.

Hence
$$
v_{\min} = \sqrt{\frac{2e\,kQ}{m_e R}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 899}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
