A uniformly charged ring of radius $R$ is in a vacuum. The potential at the center is measured relative to infinity. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the total positive charge $q = 3\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the potential at the center $U_{e} = 450\,\mathrm{V}$, what is the radius of the ring $R$?

- $3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Each charge element is at the same distance $R$ from the center. In the potential integral, the factor $1/R$ is factored out. In the indicated units, $U_{e} = \dfrac{900 \cdot q}{R}$. Solving for the unknown gives $R = \dfrac{900 \cdot q}{U_{e}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.
