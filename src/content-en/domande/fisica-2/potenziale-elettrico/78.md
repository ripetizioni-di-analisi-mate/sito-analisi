A point moves $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ along a straight field line, in the direction of the field. Along the whole segment the magnitude of the field, which is not uniform, lies between $300\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and $500\,\mathrm{V/m}$. What can be stated with certainty about the potential change $\Delta V$ between the final point and the initial point?

- $6.0\,\mathrm{V} \leq \Delta V \leq 10\,\mathrm{V}$
- $\Delta V = -8.0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V} \leq \Delta V \leq -6.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{V} \leq \Delta V \leq -6.0\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Along a straight field line, traveled in the direction of the field, $\Delta V = -\displaystyle\int_0^{s} E\,ds'$, with $s = 0.020\,\mathrm{m}$.

Since $300\,\mathrm{V/m} \leq E \leq 500\,\mathrm{V/m}$ at every point of the segment, the integral lies between $300 \cdot 0.020\,\mathrm{V} = 6.0\,\mathrm{V}$ and $500 \cdot 0.020\,\mathrm{V} = 10\,\mathrm{V}$.

Hence $-10\,\mathrm{V} \leq \Delta V \leq -6.0\,\mathrm{V}$. The exact value depends on how the field varies along the segment and cannot be determined from the data; in particular $-8.0\,\mathrm{V}$ is only one possible value.
