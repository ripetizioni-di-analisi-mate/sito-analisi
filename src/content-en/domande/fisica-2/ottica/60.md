A light ray travels in a glass with refractive index $1.50$ and strikes, at an angle of $55^\circ$, the plane surface that separates the glass from water ($n = 1.33$). What happens to the ray?

- It is refracted into the water at an angle of about $67^\circ$
- It is totally reflected
- It is refracted into the water at an angle of about $47^\circ$
- It is refracted into the water at an angle of about $38^\circ$

## Explanation

The ray goes toward a less optically dense medium, and the critical angle between glass and water is $\theta_c = \arcsin\dfrac{1.33}{1.50} \approx 62.5^\circ$. The angle of incidence $55^\circ$ is smaller than $\theta_c$, so there is no total internal reflection and the ray is refracted.

By **Snell's law** $\sin\theta_2 = \dfrac{1.50 \sin 55^\circ}{1.33} \approx 0.924$, therefore the angle of refraction is $\theta_2 \approx 67^\circ$. With air instead of water the critical angle would be $41.8^\circ$ and the same ray would be totally reflected.
