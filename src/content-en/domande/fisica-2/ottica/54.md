A ray of white light strikes from air, at an angle of $60^\circ$, the plane surface of a glass that has refractive index $1.50$ for red light and $1.54$ for violet light. In the glass the ray splits into colors. What angle do the red ray and the violet ray form, and which of the two is closer to the normal?

- $1.0^\circ$, with violet closer to the normal
- $1.0^\circ$, with red closer to the normal
- $2.1^\circ$, with violet closer to the normal
- $2.1^\circ$, with red closer to the normal

## Explanation

By **Snell's law** the red ray has $\sin\theta_{\mathrm{r}} = \dfrac{\sin 60^\circ}{1.50} \approx 0.577$, so $\theta_{\mathrm{r}} \approx 35.3^\circ$.

The violet ray has $\sin\theta_{\mathrm{v}} = \dfrac{\sin 60^\circ}{1.54} \approx 0.562$, so $\theta_{\mathrm{v}} \approx 34.2^\circ$.

The two rays therefore form the angle $35.3^\circ - 34.2^\circ \approx 1.0^\circ$. Violet has the larger index, so it moves closer to the normal.
