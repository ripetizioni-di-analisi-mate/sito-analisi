A concave spherical mirror forms a real image of an object on the axis. The paraxial approximation is used. The object distance is greater than the focal length. Initially, the radius of curvature $R = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the object distance $p = 25\,\mathrm{cm}$ are given. In the second configuration, the radius of curvature becomes $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the object distance becomes $36\,\mathrm{cm}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $d$?

- $- \dfrac{4}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{13}{7}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $-28\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The mirror has focal length $R/2$. Substituting it into $1/f=1/p+1/d$ gives the positive image distance. Applying $d = \dfrac{R \cdot p}{- R + 2 \cdot p}$ gives $\dfrac{50}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$ before the change and $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ after it. The final value minus the initial value is $\dfrac{4}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$.
