A laser beam parallel to the optical axis, $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide, passes through two thin converging lenses placed on the same axis $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. The first lens has focal length $f_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the second $f_2 = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$. How does the beam leave the second lens?

- It converges to a point $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ after the second lens
- Parallel to the axis and $0.40\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide
- Parallel to the axis and $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide
- Parallel to the axis and $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide

## Explanation

The incident beam is parallel to the axis, so by the **thin lens equation** the first lens makes it converge at its focus, at $q_1 = f_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens.

This point acts as the object for the second lens, at distance $p_2 = 12\,\mathrm{cm} - 2.0\,\mathrm{cm} = 10\,\mathrm{cm} = f_2$. The object is at the focus of the second lens, therefore $\dfrac{1}{q_2} = \dfrac{1}{f_2} - \dfrac{1}{p_2} = 0$ and the rays leave parallel to the axis.

After the focus of the first lens the rays diverge, and by similar triangles their distance from the axis grows in proportion to the distance from the focus. On the first lens the edge of the beam is $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis, at $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the focus; on the second lens, at $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the focus, it is therefore $1.0\,\mathrm{mm} \cdot \dfrac{10}{2.0} = 5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis. Hence the beam leaves $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide, that is, widened by the factor $\dfrac{f_2}{f_1} = 5$.
