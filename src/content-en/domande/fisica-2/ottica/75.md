In a Young double-slit experiment the light has wavelength $600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The screen is far away and the small-angle approximation holds. Initially, the screen distance $L = 2\,\mathrm m$ and the slit separation $b = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$ are given. In the second configuration, the screen distance becomes $3\,\mathrm m$ and the slit separation becomes $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{mm}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $d$?

- $3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The path difference is approximately $by/L$. To reach the next maximum it must increase by one wavelength. Conversion to millimeters gives the coefficient $0{,}6$. Applying $d = \dfrac{3 \cdot L}{5 \cdot b}$ gives $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ before the change and $6\,\mathrm{mm}$ after it. The final value minus the initial value is $3\,\mathrm{mm}$.
