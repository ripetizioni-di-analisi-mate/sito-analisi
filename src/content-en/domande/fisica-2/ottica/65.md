A thin converging lens produces a real image of a real object. The object is beyond the focus. Initially, the focal length $f = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the object distance from the lens $p = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ are given. In the second configuration, the focal length becomes $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the object distance from the lens becomes $36\,\mathrm{cm}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $d$?

- $4\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Taking real distances as positive, the lens equation is $1/f=1/p+1/d$. We subtract $1/p$ and then take the reciprocal. Applying $d = \dfrac{f \cdot p}{- f + p}$ gives $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ before the change and $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ after it. The final value minus the initial value is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$.
