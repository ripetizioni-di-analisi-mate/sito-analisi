The lens of a camera behaves as a thin converging lens with focal length $50\,\mathrm{mm}$. The lens is focused on a very distant landscape; then a person at $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is brought into focus. By how much, and in which direction, must the lens be moved with respect to the sensor?

- By $1.3\,\mathrm{mm}$, moving it toward the sensor
- By $2.6\,\mathrm{mm}$, moving it away from the sensor
- By $51\,\mathrm{mm}$, moving it away from the sensor
- By $1.3\,\mathrm{mm}$, moving it away from the sensor

## Explanation

For a very distant object the image forms in the focal plane, so the sensor is $50\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the lens.

For the person the **thin lens equation** gives
$$
\frac{1}{q} = \frac{1}{50\,\mathrm{mm}} - \frac{1}{2000\,\mathrm{mm}} = \frac{39}{2000\,\mathrm{mm}} ,
$$
so $q \approx 51.3\,\mathrm{mm}$. The image forms farther from the lens, therefore the lens must be moved away from the sensor by about $1.3\,\mathrm{mm}$.
