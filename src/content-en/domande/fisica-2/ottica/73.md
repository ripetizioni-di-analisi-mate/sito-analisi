In a Young double-slit experiment the light has wavelength $600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The screen is far away and the small-angle approximation holds. Given the screen distance $L = 2\,\mathrm m$ and the distance between two consecutive maxima $d = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$, what is the slit separation $b$?

- $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The path difference is approximately $by/L$. To reach the next maximum it must increase by one wavelength. Conversion to millimeters gives the coefficient $0{,}6$. In the stated units, we have $d = \dfrac{3 \cdot L}{5 \cdot b}$. Solving for the unknown gives $b = \dfrac{3 \cdot L}{5 \cdot d}$. With the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$.
