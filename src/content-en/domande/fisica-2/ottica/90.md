A concave spherical mirror has radius of curvature $R = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$. A ray parallel to the optical axis, at distance $h = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis, strikes the mirror; the ray is far from the axis, so the paraxial approximation does not hold. At what distance from the vertex does the reflected ray meet the axis?

- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $8.5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $11.5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Let $A$ be the point of incidence and $C$ the center of curvature. The line $CA$ is the normal at $A$, and it forms with the incident ray the angle $\theta$ such that $\sin\theta = \dfrac{h}{R} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\theta = 30^\circ$. The incident ray is parallel to the axis, therefore the angle between $CA$ and the axis is also $\theta$.

By the **law of reflection** the reflected ray forms the same angle $\theta$ with $CA$. Let $I$ be the point where the reflected ray meets the axis; the triangle $CAI$ has two angles equal to $\theta$, at $C$ and at $A$, so it is isosceles with $CI = IA$. Projecting the two equal sides onto the base $CA = R$ gives $2\,CI\cos\theta = R$, therefore
$$
CI = \frac{R}{2\cos\theta} = \frac{20}{2 \cos 30^\circ}\,\mathrm{cm} \approx 11.5\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The point $I$ is therefore $R - CI \approx 8.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex, less than the focal length $f = \dfrac{R}{2} = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Rays far from the axis meet the axis between the focus and the vertex, and this defect of the spherical mirror is called spherical aberration.
