A light ray passes through the center of curvature $C$ of a concave spherical mirror and then strikes the mirror at a point different from the vertex. How is the ray reflected?

- It is reflected through the focus $F$
- It goes back along itself, passing through $C$ again
- It is reflected parallel to the optical axis
- It is reflected symmetrically with respect to the optical axis

## Explanation

Every line that passes through $C$ and meets the mirror is a radius of the sphere, so it is perpendicular to the mirror at the point of incidence.

The ray therefore strikes at an angle of $0^\circ$ with respect to the normal, and by the **law of reflection** the angle of reflection is also $0^\circ$. The reflected ray thus goes back along the same line and passes through $C$ again. The argument uses only the geometry of the sphere, therefore it also holds for rays far from the axis.
