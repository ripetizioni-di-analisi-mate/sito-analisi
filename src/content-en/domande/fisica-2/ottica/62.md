A spherical mirror forms, of a real object placed $60\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex, an upright image one quarter as tall as the object. What is the focal length of the mirror, with the sign convention for mirrors and lenses?

- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $-12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $-20\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The image is upright and one quarter as tall as the object, so the magnification is $m = \dfrac{1}{4}$, positive.

From $m = -\dfrac{q}{p}$ it follows that $q = -m\,p = -15\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the image is virtual, behind the mirror. By the **mirror equation**
$$
\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{60\,\mathrm{cm}} - \frac{1}{15\,\mathrm{cm}} = -\frac{3}{60\,\mathrm{cm}} ,
$$
so $f = -20\,\mathrm{cm}$. The focal length is negative, therefore the mirror is **convex**.
