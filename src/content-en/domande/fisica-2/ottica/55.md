Two plane mirrors form a right angle. A light ray, lying in a plane perpendicular to the common edge, is reflected first by the horizontal mirror and then by the vertical one. What angle does the emerging ray form with the incident ray?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,5 0,0 5,0" vettori="4,3,2.5,0" etichette="2.6,-0.4,mirror;-1.5,2.6,mirror;3.3,3.4,incident ray" xmin=-1.8 xmax=5.5 ymin=-0.8 ymax=5.3 titolo="Two perpendicular plane mirrors and the incident ray"}

- $90^\circ$, that is, it leaves perpendicular to the incident ray
- $0^\circ$, that is, it leaves with the same direction and sense as the incident ray
- $180^\circ$, that is, it leaves parallel to the incident ray and in the opposite sense
- It depends on the angle of incidence on the first mirror

## Explanation

Take the mirrors along the $x$ and $y$ axes and denote by $(a, b)$ the direction of the incident ray.

By the **law of reflection** the reflection on the mirror along the $x$ axis changes the sign of the component perpendicular to it, so the direction becomes $(a, -b)$. The reflection on the mirror along the $y$ axis changes the sign of the component $a$, therefore the final direction is $(-a, -b)$.

The final direction is opposite to the initial one for any angle of incidence, so the ray leaves parallel to the incident ray and in the opposite sense. With three mutually perpendicular mirrors the same property holds for every direction in space, and reflectors are based on this.
