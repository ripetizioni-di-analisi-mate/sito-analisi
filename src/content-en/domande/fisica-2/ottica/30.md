A real object is at $p = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from a converging lens with $f = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the image distance $q$, with the convention that assigns negative distances to virtual images?

- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $-5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

From the thin lens equation, $\dfrac{1}{p} + \dfrac{1}{q} = \dfrac{1}{f}$, it follows that $\dfrac{1}{q} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{p}$.

With the data we get $\dfrac{1}{q} = \dfrac{1}{10\,\mathrm{cm}} - \dfrac{1}{5\,\mathrm{cm}} = -\dfrac{1}{10\,\mathrm{cm}}$, so $q = -10\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The distance is negative, therefore the image is virtual, as always happens when the object lies between a converging lens and its focal point. The value $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ is obtained by neglecting the negative sign of $\dfrac{1}{q}$.
