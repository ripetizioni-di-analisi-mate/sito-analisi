A real object slowly approaches a thin converging lens, starting from very far away and stopping just before the focus. How does its image behave during the motion?

- It stays real, moves toward the lens and becomes smaller and smaller
- It becomes virtual when the object passes the point at distance $2f$ from the lens
- It stays real and as tall as the object throughout the motion
- It stays real, moves away from the lens and becomes larger and larger

## Explanation

For $p > f$ the **thin lens equation** gives
$$
q = \frac{p f}{p - f} > 0 ,
$$
so the image stays real throughout the motion.

The derivative $\dfrac{dq}{dp} = -\dfrac{f^2}{(p - f)^2}$ is negative, therefore when $p$ decreases $q$ increases, from about $f$ for the distant object up to very large values near the focus. The magnification has modulus $|m| = \dfrac{q}{p} = \dfrac{f}{p - f}$, which grows as $p$ decreases, so the image becomes larger and larger.

For $p = 2f$ we have $m = -1$, that is, the image is as tall as the object only in that position; the image becomes virtual only for $p < f$.
