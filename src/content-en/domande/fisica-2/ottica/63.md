A diffraction grating with spacing $d = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ is illuminated perpendicularly with light containing many wavelengths. In the direction where the second-order maximum of $600\,\mathrm{nm}$ light is observed, a third-order maximum also falls. What is the wavelength of the light of this third-order maximum?

- $900\,\mathrm{nm}$
- $300\,\mathrm{nm}$
- $400\,\mathrm{nm}$
- $200\,\mathrm{nm}$

## Explanation

By the condition for the **principal maxima of the grating**, the second-order maximum of $600\,\mathrm{nm}$ light lies in the direction with
$$
d \sin\theta = 2 \cdot 600\,\mathrm{nm} = 1200\,\mathrm{nm} ,
$$
that is, with $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1.2}{2.0} = 0.60$, which exists because it is less than $1$.

In the same direction falls the third-order maximum of the wavelength $\lambda'$ such that $3\lambda' = 1200\,\mathrm{nm}$, so $\lambda' = 400\,\mathrm{nm}$. Therefore, with white light, the second-order and third-order spectra partly overlap.
