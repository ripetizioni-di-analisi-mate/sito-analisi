A thin converging lens forms on a screen the sharp real image of a candle. The upper half of the lens is covered with an opaque card, without moving either the lens or the screen. What is observed on the screen?

- Only the lower half of the image of the candle is seen
- Only the upper half of the image of the candle is seen
- The image moves and is no longer sharp on the screen
- The image stays whole and in the same position, but it is less bright

## Explanation

Every point of the candle sends rays onto the whole surface of the lens, and all the rays that pass through the lens converge at the same image point. Therefore the uncovered half of the lens also receives rays from every point of the candle and makes them converge at the corresponding image points.

The position of the image depends only on $p$ and $f$, by the **thin lens equation**, so it does not change, and the image stays whole and sharp. However, each image point now receives about half of the rays it received before, so the image is less bright.

The principal rays are used to construct the image, and the image forms even when some of them are blocked.
