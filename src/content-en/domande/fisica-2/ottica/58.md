An optical fiber has a core with refractive index $n_1 = 1.50$ and a cladding with index $n_2 = 1.40$. A ray traveling in air enters the core through the entrance face, which is plane and perpendicular to the fiber axis, forming the angle $\theta_a$ with the axis. What is the largest value of $\theta_a$ for which the ray stays guided in the core by total internal reflection?

- $21.0^\circ$
- $69.0^\circ$
- $32.6^\circ$
- $41.8^\circ$

## Explanation

The critical angle between core and cladding is $\theta_c = \arcsin\dfrac{1.40}{1.50} \approx 69.0^\circ$.

A ray that in the core forms the angle $\alpha$ with the axis strikes the lateral wall at the angle $90^\circ - \alpha$ with respect to the normal. Total internal reflection requires $90^\circ - \alpha \geq \theta_c$, so $\alpha \leq 90^\circ - \theta_c \approx 21.0^\circ$.

By **Snell's law** at the entrance face $\sin\theta_a = 1.50 \sin\alpha$, therefore the largest value is reached for $\alpha = 90^\circ - \theta_c$:
$$
\sin\theta_a = n_1 \cos\theta_c = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2} = \sqrt{1.50^2 - 1.40^2} \approx 0.539 ,
$$
so $\theta_a \approx 32.6^\circ$.
