Already polarized light passes through an ideal polarizer. The polarization direction and the transmission axis form an acute angle. Given the incident intensity $J_{0} = 120\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and the cosine of the angle with the transmission axis $b = \dfrac{1}{2}$, what is the transmitted intensity $J$?

- $15\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- $30\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- $60\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- $90\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$

## Explanation

The polarizer projects the electric field along its axis. The intensity is proportional to the square of the field, as in Malus's law. In the stated units, we have $J = J_{0} \cdot b^{2}$. With the given values, we obtain $30\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.
