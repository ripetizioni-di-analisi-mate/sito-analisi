A slit of width $a$ is illuminated perpendicularly with monochromatic light of wavelength $\lambda$. Consider the direction $\theta$ of the first diffraction minimum. In that direction, what is the path difference between the secondary waves leaving the two edges of the slit?

- $\lambda$
- $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$
- $2\lambda$
- $0$

## Explanation

The secondary waves leaving the two edges, a distance $a$ apart, have in the direction $\theta$ the path difference $a\sin\theta$, by the same reasoning as in Young's experiment.

The first diffraction minimum lies in the direction with $a\sin\theta = \lambda$, so the path difference between the two edges is $\lambda$.

The difference $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ occurs instead between each point of the upper half of the slit and the point $\dfrac{a}{2}$ lower. The waves of each of these pairs arrive in phase opposition and cancel, therefore the intensity in that direction is zero.
