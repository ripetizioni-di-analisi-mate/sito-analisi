A beam of light crosses a glass slab $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ thick, with refractive index $n = 1.50$, perpendicularly. How long does the light take to cross the slab? Use $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $6.7 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

In glass the light propagates with speed $v = \dfrac{c}{n} = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{1.50}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The beam is perpendicular to the slab, so the path traveled in the glass is the thickness $s = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore the crossing time is
$$
t = \frac{s}{v} = \frac{n\,s}{c} = \frac{1.50 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.5 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
In vacuum the same path is traveled in $1.0 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{s}$, so in glass the time is larger by a factor $n$.
