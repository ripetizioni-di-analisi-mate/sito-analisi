In a Young double-slit experiment the light has wavelength $600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The screen is far away and the small-angle approximation holds. Given the slit separation $b = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the distance between two consecutive maxima $d = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$, what is the screen distance $L$?

- $4\,\mathrm m$
- $1\,\mathrm m$
- $3\,\mathrm m$
- $2\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The path difference is approximately $by/L$. To reach the next maximum it must increase by one wavelength. Conversion to millimeters gives the coefficient $0{,}6$. In the stated units, we have $d = \dfrac{3 \cdot L}{5 \cdot b}$. Solving for the unknown gives $L = \dfrac{5 \cdot b \cdot d}{3}$. With the given values, we obtain $2\,\mathrm m$.
