A diffraction grating is illuminated perpendicularly with white light, and on the screen the zero-order maximum is white. In the first-order spectrum, which color is deviated most with respect to the direction of incidence?

- Violet, which has the shortest wavelength
- Red, which has the longest wavelength
- Green, which is in the middle of the visible spectrum
- All colors are deviated by the same angle

## Explanation

The first-order maxima lie in the directions with $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{d}$, and the sine is increasing between $0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the deviation angle grows with the wavelength.

Red, with $\lambda \approx 700\,\mathrm{nm}$, is therefore deviated more than violet, with $\lambda \approx 400\,\mathrm{nm}$. In the zero order $\sin\theta = 0$ for every wavelength, so all colors overlap and the maximum is white. The order of the colors is the reverse of that of the prism, where violet light is deviated most.
