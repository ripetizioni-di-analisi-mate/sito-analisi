Already polarized light passes through an ideal polarizer. The polarization direction and the transmission axis form an acute angle. Given the incident intensity $J_{0} = 120\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and the transmitted intensity $J = 30\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, what is the cosine of the angle with the transmission axis $b$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The polarizer projects the electric field along its axis. The intensity is proportional to the square of the field, as in Malus's law. In the stated units, we have $J = J_{0} \cdot b^{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $b = \dfrac{\sqrt{J}}{\sqrt{J_{0}}}$. With the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
