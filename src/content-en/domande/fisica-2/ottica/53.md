A light ray of fixed direction strikes a plane mirror. The mirror is rotated by $10^\circ$ about an axis lying in its plane and perpendicular to the plane of incidence, and the ray keeps striking the mirror. By what angle does the reflected ray rotate?

- $10^\circ$
- $20^\circ$
- $5^\circ$
- $40^\circ$

## Explanation

If the angle of incidence is $\theta$, by the **law of reflection** the reflected ray forms the angle $180^\circ - 2\theta$ with the incident ray.

Rotating the mirror by $10^\circ$ rotates the normal by the same amount, so the angle of incidence changes by $10^\circ$. Therefore the angle $180^\circ - 2\theta$ changes by $20^\circ$. The incident ray is fixed, so the reflected ray rotates by $20^\circ$, twice the rotation of the mirror.
