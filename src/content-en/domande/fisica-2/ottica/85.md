A light beam consists of unpolarized light, of intensity $20\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, and of light linearly polarized in the vertical direction, also of intensity $20\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. The beam passes through an ideal polarizer, which is rotated about the direction of the beam. What are the maximum and the minimum transmitted intensity?

- Maximum $40\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, minimum $20\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- Maximum $30\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, minimum $0\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- Maximum $20\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, minimum $10\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$
- Maximum $30\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, minimum $10\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$

## Explanation

The polarizer transmits half of the unpolarized light, that is, $10\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, whatever the orientation of its axis.

By **Malus's law** the polarized part is transmitted with intensity $20 \cos^2\theta\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the axis and the vertical. The two parts have no stable phase relation, so the transmitted intensities add:
$$
I(\theta) = \left( 10 + 20\cos^2\theta \right) \mathrm{W/m^2} .
$$
Therefore the maximum intensity occurs with the axis vertical and equals $30\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, and the minimum occurs with the axis horizontal and equals $10\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.
