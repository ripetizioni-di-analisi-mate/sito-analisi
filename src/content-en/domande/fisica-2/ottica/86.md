Two plane mirrors $A$ and $B$ are parallel and face each other, $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. A point object lies between the two mirrors, $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from mirror $A$. The rays reflected by $B$ that reach $A$ form a second image, formed by $A$ from the image in $B$. How far from the object is this second image?

- $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Take an $x$ axis perpendicular to the mirrors, with the origin at the object, mirror $A$ at $x = -1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and mirror $B$ at $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

The image in a plane mirror is the symmetric point of the object with respect to the mirror, so the image in $B$ is at $x = 6.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The rays reflected by $B$ seem to come from this image, therefore it acts as the object for mirror $A$, which forms its symmetric image with respect to $x = -1.0\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, at
$$
x = -2.0\,\mathrm{m} - 6.0\,\mathrm{m} = -8.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The second image is therefore $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the object, twice the distance between the mirrors, whatever the position of the object between them.
