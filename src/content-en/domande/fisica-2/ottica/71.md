The figure shows a thin diverging lens, with the foci $F$ and $F'$ and the center $O$, and a ray that arrives from the left parallel to the optical axis. After the lens the ray moves away from the axis. Through which point does the backward extension of the emerging ray pass?

- Through the focus $F'$, which is on the side opposite to the one from which the light arrives
- Through the center $O$ of the lens
- Through the focus $F$, which is on the side from which the light arrives
- Through the point of the axis at distance $2|f|$ from the lens, on the side from which the light arrives

## Explanation

A diverging lens makes rays parallel to the axis diverge as if they came from the focus $F$, which is on the side from which the light arrives, so the backward extension of the emerging ray passes through $F$.

The same result follows from the **thin lens equation**. A beam parallel to the axis corresponds to an object at infinity, for which $\dfrac{1}{q} = \dfrac{1}{f}$ and $q = f < 0$, therefore the image is virtual and lies at the focus on the side of the object.
