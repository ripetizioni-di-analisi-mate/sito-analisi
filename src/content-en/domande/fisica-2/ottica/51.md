A ray strikes at an angle of $60^\circ$ a parallel-faced glass slab, $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ thick, with refractive index $n = 1.50$ and immersed in air. By how much is the emerging ray shifted sideways with respect to the extension of the incident ray?

- $0.84\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.7\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

By **Snell's law** $\sin\theta_2 = \dfrac{\sin 60^\circ}{1.50} \approx 0.577$, so in the glass the ray forms the angle $\theta_2 \approx 35.3^\circ$ with the normal.

The path in the glass crosses the thickness $s = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ with inclination $\theta_2$ to the normal, so it is $\dfrac{s}{\cos\theta_2} \approx 2.45\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. This path forms the angle $\theta_1 - \theta_2 \approx 24.7^\circ$ with the extension of the incident ray, therefore the lateral shift is
$$
d = \frac{s \sin(\theta_1 - \theta_2)}{\cos\theta_2} \approx 2.45 \cdot \sin 24.7^\circ\,\mathrm{cm} \approx 1.0\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
