A beam of unpolarized light passes through an ideal polarizer, which is slowly rotated through a full turn about the direction of the beam. How does the transmitted intensity vary during the rotation?

- It varies as $\cos^2\theta$, between zero and the incident intensity
- It stays constant and equals half the incident intensity
- It stays constant and equal to the incident intensity
- It varies between half the incident intensity and zero

## Explanation

In unpolarized light the direction of the electric field changes randomly among all those perpendicular to the propagation, so no direction is preferred.

The polarizer transmits on average the fraction $\dfrac{1}{2}$, the mean value of $\cos^2\theta$ over all directions, and this value does not depend on the orientation of the axis. Therefore during the rotation the transmitted intensity stays constant and equals half the incident one. The variation according to Malus's law is observed only with light that is already polarized.
