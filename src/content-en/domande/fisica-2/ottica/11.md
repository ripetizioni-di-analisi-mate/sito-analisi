A beam of rays parallel to the optical axis strikes a thin diverging lens. One ray of the beam crosses the lens $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis and, $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ past the lens, is $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis, on the same side. What is the focal length of the lens, with the sign convention for lenses?

- $-15\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $+30\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $-30\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $-45\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

In a diverging lens the rays parallel to the axis emerge as if they came from the focal point, which lies on the side of the incoming light; therefore the focal point is where the backward extension of the emerging ray meets the axis.

The emerging ray moves away from the axis by $3.0 - 2.0 = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ every $15\,\mathrm{cm}$. Extending it backward from the lens, the distance from the axis decreases by $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ every $15\,\mathrm{cm}$, so it becomes zero after $2.0 \cdot 15\,\mathrm{cm} = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The focal point is therefore $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens and is virtual, so $f = -30\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $-45\,\mathrm{cm}$ is obtained by measuring the distance from the point where the ray is $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis.
