A grating with spacing $d = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ is illuminated perpendicularly with light that contains a red line at $700\,\mathrm{nm}$ and a blue line at $450\,\mathrm{nm}$. On a flat screen parallel to the grating, $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ away, how far apart are the two first-order lines that lie on the same side of the center?

- $12.5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $29\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $14\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $37\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The first-order maxima lie in the directions with $d\sin\theta = \lambda$. For the red line $\sin\theta_{\mathrm{r}} = \dfrac{0.70}{2.0} = 0.35$, so $\theta_{\mathrm{r}} \approx 20.5^\circ$; for the blue line $\sin\theta_{\mathrm{b}} = 0.225$, so $\theta_{\mathrm{b}} \approx 13.0^\circ$.

The angles are tens of degrees, therefore the position on the screen is computed with the tangent, $y = L\tan\theta$. One obtains $y_{\mathrm{r}} \approx 37.4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $y_{\mathrm{b}} \approx 23.1\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the two lines are about $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart.
