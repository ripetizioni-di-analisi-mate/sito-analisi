In a Young double-slit experiment the light has wavelength $600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The screen is far away and the small-angle approximation holds. Initially, the screen distance $L = 2\,\mathrm m$ and the slit separation $b = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$ are given. In the second configuration, the slit separation becomes $\dfrac{4}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $d$?

- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

The path difference is approximately $by/L$. To reach the next maximum it must increase by one wavelength. Conversion to millimeters gives the coefficient $0{,}6$. Applying $d = \dfrac{3 \cdot L}{5 \cdot b}$ gives $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ before the change and $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{mm}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
