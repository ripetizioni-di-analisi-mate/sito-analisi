A thin diverging lens has focal length $f = -20\,\mathrm{cm}$. A real object is placed at any distance in front of the lens. Where can its image be?

- Less than $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens, on the same side as the object
- More than $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens, on the same side as the object
- Less than $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens, on the side opposite to the object
- At any distance from the lens, on the same side as the object

## Explanation

By the **thin lens equation**
$$
\frac{1}{q} = -\frac{1}{20\,\mathrm{cm}} - \frac{1}{p} .
$$
The object is real, so $p > 0$ and $\dfrac{1}{q} < -\dfrac{1}{20\,\mathrm{cm}}$. Therefore $q$ is negative, that is, the image is virtual and lies on the same side as the object, and $\dfrac{1}{|q|} > \dfrac{1}{20\,\mathrm{cm}}$, that is, $|q| < 20\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The image is thus always between the lens and the focus $F$. As $p$ grows the image approaches the focus, and as $p$ tends to zero it approaches the lens.
