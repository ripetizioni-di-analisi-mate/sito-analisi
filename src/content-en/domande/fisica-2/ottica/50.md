A light ray strikes from air, with angle of incidence $60^\circ$, a layer of water ($n = 1.33$) that covers a glass plate ($n = 1.60$). The separating surfaces are plane and parallel. What angle does the ray traveling in the glass form with the normal?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-3,0 3,0 3,-1.4 -3,-1.4;-3,-1.4 3,-1.4 3,-2.8 -3,-2.8" linee="-3,0 3,0;-3,-1.4 3,-1.4" segmenti="0,-2.8,0,2" vettori="-1.73,1,0,0" etichette="-0.62,1.15,60°;-2.8,0.3,air;-2.8,-0.7,water;-2.8,-2.1,glass" xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-3 ymax=2.2 titolo="Ray striking from air a water layer with $n = 1.33$ lying on a glass plate with $n = 1.60$"}

- $40.6^\circ$
- $32.8^\circ$
- $46.0^\circ$
- $24.0^\circ$

## Explanation

The surfaces are parallel, so the normal has the same direction in all the layers. At each surface **Snell's law** preserves the quantity $n\sin\theta$, therefore
$$
1.00 \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 1.33 \sin\theta_{\mathrm{w}} = 1.60 \sin\theta_{\mathrm{g}} .
$$
It follows that $\sin\theta_{\mathrm{g}} = \dfrac{\sin 60^\circ}{1.60} \approx 0.541$, so $\theta_{\mathrm{g}} \approx 32.8^\circ$.

The angle in the glass depends only on the indices of the first and last media; the water layer shifts the ray sideways, but its direction in the glass is the same as it would be without the water.
