Which of the following images can be collected on a screen?

- The image of a face in a plane mirror
- The image of a stamp formed by a magnifying glass, with the stamp between the focus and the lens
- The image of a slide formed by the converging objective of a projector, with the slide just beyond the focus
- The image of a car in a convex rear-view mirror

## Explanation

A screen collects only real images, that is, those where the rays actually converge; with the sign convention they are those with $q > 0$.

The image in a plane mirror is virtual, because it is the symmetric point of the object behind the mirror. For a converging lens with $p < f$ we have $\dfrac{1}{q} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{p} < 0$, so the image of the magnifying glass is virtual. For a convex mirror $f < 0$, therefore $\dfrac{1}{q} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{p} < 0$ and the image of the car is virtual too.

In the projector $f < p < 2f$, so $0 < \dfrac{1}{q} < \dfrac{1}{2f}$ and the image is real, beyond $2f$ and enlarged. Therefore only the image of the slide can be collected on a screen.
