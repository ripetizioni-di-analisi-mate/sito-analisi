A concave spherical mirror forms a real image of an object on the axis. The paraxial approximation is used. The object distance is greater than the focal length. Given the object distance $p = 25\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the image distance from the mirror $d = \dfrac{50}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$, what is the radius of curvature $R$?

- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $40\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $100\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The mirror has focal length $R/2$. Substituting it into $1/f=1/p+1/d$ gives the positive image distance. In the stated units, we have $d = \dfrac{R \cdot p}{- R + 2 \cdot p}$. Solving for the unknown gives $R = \dfrac{2 \cdot d \cdot p}{d + p}$. With the given values, we obtain $20\,\mathrm{cm}$.
