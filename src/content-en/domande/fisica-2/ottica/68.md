Two thin converging lenses, each with focal length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, are placed on the same axis $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. An object lies on the axis, $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ in front of the first lens. Where does the final image form?

- $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ after the second lens, real
- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ after the second lens, real
- $12.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ after the second lens, real
- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ before the second lens, virtual

## Explanation

By the **thin lens equation** the first lens gives $\dfrac{1}{q_1} = \dfrac{1}{10\,\mathrm{cm}} - \dfrac{1}{15\,\mathrm{cm}} = \dfrac{1}{30\,\mathrm{cm}}$, so $q_1 = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$.

This image acts as the object for the second lens, at distance $p_2 = 50\,\mathrm{cm} - 30\,\mathrm{cm} = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$. Therefore
$$
\frac{1}{q_2} = \frac{1}{10\,\mathrm{cm}} - \frac{1}{20\,\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{1}{20\,\mathrm{cm}} ,
$$
and the final image is real, $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ after the second lens. The overall magnification is $m = m_1 m_2 = (-2) \cdot (-1) = 2$, so the image is upright.
