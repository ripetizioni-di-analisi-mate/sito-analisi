A solenoid $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, with cross-sectional area $10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, has a uniform magnetic field of $0.10\,\mathrm{T}$ inside; outside, the field is negligible. How much magnetic energy is stored in the solenoid? Use $\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$.

- $0.80\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The magnetic energy is distributed in the field with the **energy density** $u_B = \dfrac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$, which here is
$$
u_B = \frac{0.10^2}{2 \cdot 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{J/m^3} \approx 4.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J/m^3} .
$$
The field is uniform inside and negligible outside, so the energy is the product of $u_B$ and the inner volume $A\ell = 10 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{m^3} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

The stored energy is therefore $U = 4.0 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 0.80\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $1.6\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $4.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the energy density without the volume.
