A field perpendicular to a fixed loop increases uniformly. The area of the loop is $0{,}04\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and self-induction is negligible. Given the resistance $R_{e} = 2\,\Omega$ and the magnitude of the induced current $I_{e} = \dfrac{1}{10}\,\mathrm A$, what is the rate of increase of the field $b$?

- $10\,\mathrm{T/s}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{T/s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{T/s}$
- $\dfrac{5}{4}\,\mathrm{T/s}$

## Explanation

The emf has magnitude $A\,dB/dt$. To find the current, divide by the resistance of the loop. In the stated units, we have $I_{e} = \dfrac{b}{25 \cdot R_{e}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $b = 25 \cdot I_{e} \cdot R_{e}$. With the given values, we obtain $5\,\mathrm{T/s}$.
