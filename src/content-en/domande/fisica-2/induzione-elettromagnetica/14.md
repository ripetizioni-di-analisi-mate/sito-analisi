A coil of $200$ identical turns has inductance $40\,\mathrm{mH}$. When it carries the current $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$, what is the flux of its magnetic field through a single turn?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Wb}$

## Explanation

By the definition of **inductance** the self-linked flux of the coil is
$$
\Phi_B = L i = 40 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{Wb} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb} .
$$
The flux linked with a coil of $N$ identical turns is $N$ times the flux through one turn, so the flux through one turn is $\dfrac{\Phi_B}{N}$.

Therefore the flux through a single turn is $\dfrac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}{200}\,\mathrm{Wb} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Wb}$. The value $2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$ is the flux linked with the whole coil.
