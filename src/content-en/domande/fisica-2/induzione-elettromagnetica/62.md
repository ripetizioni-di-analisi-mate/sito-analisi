Two parallel horizontal rails, $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart, are connected to an ideal source of $0.60\,\mathrm{V}$ in series with a resistor of $2.0\,\Omega$. A conducting rod, initially at rest, closes the circuit and can slide without friction on the rails, in a uniform magnetic field of $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to their plane. Neglecting the other resistances and self-induction, what velocity does the rod approach?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The current of the source flows through the rod, which is therefore subject to the magnetic force $B \ell i$ and starts to accelerate. When it moves with velocity $v$, the rod has the motional emf $B \ell v$, which by **Lenz's law** opposes the emf of the source, so the current is
$$
i = \frac{\mathcal{E} - B \ell v}{R} .
$$
The rod accelerates as long as the current is nonzero, therefore it approaches the velocity for which $B \ell v = \mathcal{E}$, that is $v = \dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{B \ell} = \dfrac{0.60}{0.50 \cdot 0.40}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The limiting value is independent of $R$, which determines only how quickly it is reached; the value $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by also dividing by $R$.
