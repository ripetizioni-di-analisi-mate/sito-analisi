A flat loop of area $A$ rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$. The axis of rotation lies in the plane of the loop and is *parallel* to $\vec{B}$. What is the emf induced in the loop?

- $B A \omega \sin(\omega t)$
- It is zero at every instant
- $B A \omega$, constant
- $B A \omega \cos(\omega t)$

## Explanation

The normal to the loop is perpendicular to the plane of the loop, so it is also perpendicular to the axis of rotation, which lies in that plane. Since the axis is parallel to $\vec{B}$, the normal stays perpendicular to the field during the whole rotation. The flux linkage $B A\cos\theta$ therefore has $\theta = 90^\circ$ at every instant and is always zero. By the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** the emf is hence **zero at every instant**. The formula $B A \omega \sin(\omega t)$ holds when the axis of rotation is perpendicular to the field.
