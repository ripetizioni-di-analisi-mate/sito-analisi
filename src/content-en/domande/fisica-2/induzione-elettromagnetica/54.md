An airplane with a wingspan of $30\,\mathrm{m}$ flies horizontally northward at $250\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In that area the Earth's magnetic field has a vertical component of magnitude $4.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ and a horizontal component, pointing north, of magnitude $3.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the emf induced between the wingtips?

- $0.38\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.23\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.53\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The charges in the wings experience the Lorentz force per unit charge $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$. The velocity is parallel to the horizontal component of the field, so the cross product of $\vec{v}$ with this component is zero. The vertical component is perpendicular both to the velocity and to the wings, therefore it contributes the **motional emf** $B_v \ell v$:
$$
\mathcal{E} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 30 \cdot 250\,\mathrm{V} = 0.30\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The value $0.38\,\mathrm{V}$ is obtained with the magnitude of the total field, $5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$, instead of the vertical component alone.
