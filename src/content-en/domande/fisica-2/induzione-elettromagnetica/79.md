In a very large circular region, a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the plane varies with time. A concentric circle entirely within the region is considered. Given the magnitude of the derivative of the field $b = 8\,\mathrm{T/s}$ and the radius of the circle $r = \dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm m$, what is the magnitude of the induced electric field $E$?

- $\dfrac{6}{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $\dfrac{9}{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $\dfrac{12}{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

The magnitude of the circulation is $2\pi rE$. Faraday's law equates it to the magnitude of the change in flux, $\pi r^2 b$. In the stated units, we have $E = \dfrac{b \cdot r}{2}$. With the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{6}{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$.
