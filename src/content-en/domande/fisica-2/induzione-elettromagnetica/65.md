The inductance of an ideal solenoid is constant. The field energy is calculated with a steady current. Initially, the inductance $L_{e} = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the current $I_{e} = 5\,\mathrm{A}$ are given. In the second configuration, the inductance becomes $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the current becomes $4\,\mathrm{A}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $U$?

- $- \dfrac{1}{10}\,\mathrm J$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm J$
- $- \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm J$
- $- \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The source must supply work while the current increases. Integrating $L_e I\,dI$ gives $L_e I_e^2/2$. Applying $U = \dfrac{I_{e}^{2} \cdot L_{e}}{2}$ gives $5\,\mathrm J$ before the change and $\dfrac{24}{5}\,\mathrm J$ after it. The final value minus the initial value is $- \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm J$.
