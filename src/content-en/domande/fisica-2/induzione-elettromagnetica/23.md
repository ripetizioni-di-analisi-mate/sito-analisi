During the free decay of the current in an RL circuit the current is $0.80\,\mathrm{A}$ at a certain instant and $0.40\,\mathrm{A}$ after $20\,\mathrm{ms}$. The instant at which the source was disconnected is not known. What is the time constant $\tau$ of the circuit?

- $14\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $20\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $29\,\mathrm{ms}$
- It cannot be found without knowing the initial current

## Explanation

During the decay the current is $i(t) = I_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$, so the ratio of the currents at two instants separated by the interval $\Delta t$ is
$$
\frac{i(t + \Delta t)}{i(t)} = e^{-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau}} .
$$
This ratio depends neither on $I_0$ nor on the initial instant, therefore the two readings are enough to determine $\tau$.

Since $\dfrac{0.40}{0.80} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, we have $e^{-\frac{20\,\mathrm{ms}}{\tau}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, $\dfrac{20\,\mathrm{ms}}{\tau} = \ln 2$. The time constant is therefore $\tau = \dfrac{20\,\mathrm{ms}}{\ln 2} \approx 29\,\mathrm{ms}$. The value $20\,\mathrm{ms}$ is the time in which the current halves, and $14\,\mathrm{ms}$ is obtained by multiplying by $\ln 2$ instead of dividing.
