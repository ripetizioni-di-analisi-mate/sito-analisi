A rod $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ long slides at constant velocity $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on two rails closed by a resistor, moving away from the resistor. The magnetic field is uniform, perpendicular to the plane of the rails, and increases in time according to $B(t) = k t$ with $k = 0.20\,\mathrm{T/s}$. At the instant $t = 0$ the rod is at the resistor. What is the magnitude of the induced emf at the instant $t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $1.2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.60\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.8\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

At the instant $t$ the rod is at distance $v t$ from the resistor, so the area of the circuit is $\ell v t$ and the flux is $\Phi_B = k t \cdot \ell v t = k \ell v t^2$. By the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** the magnitude of the emf is
$$
|\mathcal{E}| = \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = 2 k \ell v t = 2 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{V} = 1.2\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The result is the sum of two equal contributions of $0.60\,\mathrm{V}$: the term $B \ell v$, due to the motion of the rod, and the term $A\,\dfrac{dB}{dt}$, due to the change of the field. The value $0.60\,\mathrm{V}$ accounts for only one of the two.
