A loop has its oriented normal pointing in the same direction as a uniform field. The field decreases without changing direction. The area is constant and the flux remains positive. Given the area $A = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the initial magnitude of the field $B_{0} = \dfrac{4}{5}\,\mathrm T$ and the final magnitude of the field $B_{1} = \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm T$, what is the decrease in flux $\Phi$?

- $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{Wb}$

## Explanation

Since the field is parallel to the normal, the flux is $AB$. The decrease is the initial value minus the final value. In the stated units, we have $\Phi = A \cdot \left(B_{0} - B_{1}\right)$. With the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{Wb}$.
