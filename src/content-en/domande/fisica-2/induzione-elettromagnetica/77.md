An ideal inductor of $0.40\,\mathrm{H}$ carries a current of $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. At time $t = 0$ the source is disconnected and the inductor closes on two resistors in series, of $10\,\Omega$ and $30\,\Omega$. How much heat is produced in the $30\,\Omega$ resistor from $t = 0$ until the current vanishes?

- $0.80\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.60\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The energy stored in the inductor at $t = 0$ is $\dfrac{1}{2} L I_0^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.40 \cdot 2.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.80\,\mathrm{J}$. During the discharge there are no sources, so all this energy is converted into heat in the two resistors. The resistors are in series and carry the same current at every instant, therefore the dissipated powers are in the ratio of the resistances, and so are the amounts of heat. The $30\,\Omega$ resistor therefore receives the fraction $\dfrac{30}{40}$ of the energy:
$$
Q = \frac{30}{40} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{J} = 0.60\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $0.80\,\mathrm{J}$ is the heat produced in the two resistors together.
