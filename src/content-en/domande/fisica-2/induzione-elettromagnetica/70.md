A series RL circuit discharges through the resistor alone after the source is disconnected. The current falls to one quarter of its initial value. Initially, the inductance $L_{e} = \dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the resistance $R_{e} = 3\,\Omega$ are given. In the second configuration, the inductance becomes $\dfrac{9}{10}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the resistance becomes $2\,\Omega$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $t$?

- $- \dfrac{\log{\left(2 \right)}}{2}\,\mathrm s$
- $\dfrac{\log{\left(2 \right)}}{2}\,\mathrm s$
- $\dfrac{\log{\left(2 \right)}}{4}\,\mathrm s$
- $1\,\mathrm s$

## Explanation

The time constant is $L_e/R_e$ and the remaining fraction of the current is $e^{-tR_e/L_e}$. Setting this fraction equal to $1/4$ gives the logarithm of four. Applying $t = \log{\left(4^{\dfrac{L_{e}}{R_{e}}} \right)}$ gives $\dfrac{2 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}}{5}\,\mathrm s$ before the change and $\dfrac{9 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}}{10}\,\mathrm s$ after it. The final value minus the initial value is $\dfrac{\log{\left(2 \right)}}{2}\,\mathrm s$.
