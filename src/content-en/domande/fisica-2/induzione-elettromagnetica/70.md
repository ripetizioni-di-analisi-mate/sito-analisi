In a circular region a uniform magnetic field, perpendicular to the plane of the region, increases at a constant rate of $10\,\mathrm{T/s}$. A proton travels on a circle of radius $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, concentric with the region and entirely contained in it. What energy does the proton gain in one revolution because of the induced electric field?

- $3.9\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $16\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $7.9\,\mathrm{eV}$

## Explanation

The work of the induced electric field in one revolution is the charge times the circulation of the field along the circle. By **Faraday's law in integral form** the circulation has magnitude $\pi r^2 \left| \dfrac{dB}{dt} \right| = \pi \cdot 0.50^2 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{V} \approx 7.9\,\mathrm{V}$. The magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity and does no work, therefore the energy gained in one revolution is
$$
W = e \cdot 7.9\,\mathrm{V} = 7.9\,\mathrm{eV} .
$$
The energy gained in one revolution is nonzero because the induced field is not conservative; this is the principle of the betatron.
