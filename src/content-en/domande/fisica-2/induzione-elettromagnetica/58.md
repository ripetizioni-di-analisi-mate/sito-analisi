An isolated conducting rod, held vertical in the plane of the page, moves to the right with constant velocity in a uniform magnetic field pointing into the page. Which end of the rod is at the higher potential?

- The lower end
- Neither, because the rod is not part of a closed circuit
- The lower end if the velocity is small, the upper end if it is large
- The upper end

## Explanation

Take the $x$ axis to the right, the $y$ axis upward and the $z$ axis out of the page; then $\vec{v} = v\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{B} = -B\,\hat{k}$. The positive charges experience the **Lorentz force** $q\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B} = q v B\,\hat{\imath} \times (-\hat{k}) = q v B\,\hat{\jmath}$, directed upward. The positive charges therefore accumulate at the upper end and the negative ones at the lower end, until the resulting electric field balances the magnetic force. Hence the upper end is at the higher potential, with a potential difference $B \ell v$ even without a circuit.
