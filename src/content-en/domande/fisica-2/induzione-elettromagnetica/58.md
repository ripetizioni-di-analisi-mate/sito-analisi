A field perpendicular to a fixed loop increases uniformly. The area of the loop is $0{,}04\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and self-induction is negligible. Given the rate of increase of the field $b = 5\,\mathrm{T/s}$ and the magnitude of the induced current $I_{e} = \dfrac{1}{10}\,\mathrm A$, what is the resistance $R_{e}$?

- $4\,\Omega$
- $2\,\Omega$
- $50\,\Omega$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\Omega$

## Explanation

The emf has magnitude $A\,dB/dt$. To find the current, divide by the resistance of the loop. In the stated units, we have $I_{e} = \dfrac{b}{25 \cdot R_{e}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $R_{e} = \dfrac{b}{25 \cdot I_{e}}$. With the given values, we obtain $2\,\Omega$.
