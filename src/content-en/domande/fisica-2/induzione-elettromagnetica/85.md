A very long straight wire and a rectangular loop lie in the same plane. The sides of the loop of length $h = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ are parallel to the wire, the nearer one at distance $d = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$; the other two sides are $w = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. What is the mutual inductance between the wire and the loop?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,-0.5 0,3.5;1,0.5 3,0.5 3,2.5 1,2.5 1,0.5" vettori="0,1.2,0,2.2,i" segmenti="0,0.2,1,0.2;1,0.2,3,0.2" etichette="0.5,-0.1,d;2,-0.1,w;3.4,1.5,h" xmin=-0.8 xmax=4 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.6 titolo="Straight wire carrying the current $i$ and rectangular loop in the same plane"}

- $8.8 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{H}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{H}$
- $2.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{H}$
- $4.4 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{H}$

## Explanation

The wire carrying the current $i$ produces at distance $r$ the field $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi r}$, perpendicular to the plane of the loop. The field varies with $r$, so the flux is computed by adding the contributions of strips of height $h$ and width $dr$:
$$
\Phi = \int_d^{d + w} \frac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi r}\,h\,dr = \frac{\mu_0 i h}{2\pi} \ln\frac{d + w}{d} .
$$
By the definition of **mutual inductance** $M = \dfrac{\Phi}{i} = 2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 0.20 \cdot \ln 3\,\mathrm{H} \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{H}$. The value $8.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{H}$ is obtained by taking the field at distance $d$ as uniform over the loop.
