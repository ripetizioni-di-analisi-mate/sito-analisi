Two pendulums have bobs shaped as copper plates of the same size: the first plate is solid, the second has a series of parallel slits that make it look like a comb. The pendulums swing between the poles of an electromagnet that produces a strong uniform field perpendicular to the plates. Which statement is true?

- The two plates swing in the same way, because copper is not a ferromagnetic material.
- The solid plate stops more quickly, because the eddy currents in it are stronger.
- The comb-shaped plate stops more quickly, because the slits increase the number of edges on which current is induced.
- The solid plate is accelerated by the eddy currents when it enters the field.

## Explanation

When a plate enters or leaves the field, the flux through portions of the plate changes, and the induced electric field drives **eddy currents** in the copper. By Lenz's law these currents produce forces that oppose the motion, so they brake the pendulum. In the solid plate the currents can flow along wide closed paths. The slits of the comb-shaped plate interrupt these paths and reduce the strength of the currents, therefore the comb-shaped plate is braked less. The braking is due to the currents induced in the conductor, not to the magnetic properties of the material.
