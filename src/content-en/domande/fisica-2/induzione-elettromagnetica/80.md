In a very large circular region, a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the plane varies with time. A concentric circle entirely within the region is considered. Given the radius of the circle $r = \dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm m$ and the magnitude of the induced electric field $E = \dfrac{6}{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$, what is the magnitude of the derivative of the field $b$?

- $12\,\mathrm{T/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{T/s}$
- $16\,\mathrm{T/s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{T/s}$

## Explanation

The magnitude of the circulation is $2\pi rE$. Faraday's law equates it to the magnitude of the change in flux, $\pi r^2 b$. In the stated units, we have $E = \dfrac{b \cdot r}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $b = \dfrac{2 \cdot E}{r}$. With the given values, we obtain $8\,\mathrm{T/s}$.
