A series RL circuit discharges through the resistor alone after the source is disconnected. The current falls to one quarter of its initial value. Initially, the inductance $L_{e} = \dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the resistance $R_{e} = 3\,\Omega$ are given. In the second configuration, the resistance becomes $6\,\Omega$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $t$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The time constant is $L_e/R_e$ and the remaining fraction of the current is $e^{-tR_e/L_e}$. Setting this fraction equal to $1/4$ gives the logarithm of four. Applying $t = \log{\left(4^{\dfrac{L_{e}}{R_{e}}} \right)}$ gives $\dfrac{2 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}}{5}\,\mathrm s$ before the change and $\dfrac{\log{\left(2 \right)}}{5}\,\mathrm s$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
