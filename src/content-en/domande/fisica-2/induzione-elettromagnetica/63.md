The inductance of an ideal solenoid is constant. The field energy is calculated with a steady current. Given the inductance $L_{e} = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the stored energy $U = 5\,\mathrm J$, what is the current $I_{e}$?

- $5\,\mathrm{A}$
- $\dfrac{5 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{A}$
- $5 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{A}$
- $10\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

The source must supply work while the current increases. Integrating $L_e I\,dI$ gives $L_e I_e^2/2$. In the stated units, we have $U = \dfrac{I_{e}^{2} \cdot L_{e}}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $I_{e} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{U}}{\sqrt{L_{e}}}$. With the given values, we obtain $5\,\mathrm{A}$.
