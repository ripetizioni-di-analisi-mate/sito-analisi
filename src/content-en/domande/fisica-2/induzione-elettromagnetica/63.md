Two parallel horizontal conducting rails are *not* connected to each other, so they do not form a closed circuit. They are in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to their plane, and a conducting rod is launched along them without friction. How does the rod move?

- It slows down exponentially until it stops, because the induced emf opposes the motion.
- It keeps moving at constant velocity, because no current flows in the rod and the magnetic force on the rod is zero.
- It stops immediately, because the charges accumulated at the ends block it.
- It accelerates, because the induced emf supplies energy to the rod.

## Explanation

Between the ends of the rod the emf $B \ell v$ arises, and the charges separate until the electric field they produce balances the Lorentz force. Since the circuit is open, after this short separation no current flows in the rod. The magnetic force on a conductor is $I\,\vec{\ell} \times \vec{B}$, so with $I = 0$ it is zero. The only forces on the rod are its weight and the reaction of the rails, both perpendicular to the motion, therefore the rod **keeps moving at constant velocity**. Braking occurs only if the circuit is closed and the induced current can flow.
