A rod of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ slides without friction on two horizontal rails closed by a resistor of $0.10\,\Omega$, in a uniform magnetic field of $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the plane of the rails. At the initial instant the rod has velocity $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and then moves with no external forces. Neglecting the other resistances and self-induction, what total distance does the rod travel?

- $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.75\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The magnetic braking force has magnitude $\dfrac{B^2 \ell^2 v}{R}$, so the velocity decreases as $v_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$ with $\tau = \dfrac{m R}{B^2 \ell^2}$. With the data one has
$$
\tau = \frac{0.20 \cdot 0.10}{0.50^2 \cdot 0.20^2}\,\mathrm{s} = 2.0\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The distance traveled is the integral of the velocity from $0$ to $+\infty$, that is, $v_0 \tau = 1.5 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The rod stops only asymptotically, but the total distance is finite.
