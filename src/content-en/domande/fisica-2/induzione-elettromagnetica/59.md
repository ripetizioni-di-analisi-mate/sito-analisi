A field perpendicular to a fixed loop increases uniformly. The area of the loop is $0{,}04\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and self-induction is negligible. Initially, the rate of increase of the field $b = 5\,\mathrm{T/s}$ and the resistance $R_{e} = 2\,\Omega$ are given. In the second configuration, the resistance becomes $4\,\Omega$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $I_{e}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$
- $1$

## Explanation

The emf has magnitude $A\,dB/dt$. To find the current, divide by the resistance of the loop. Applying $I_{e} = \dfrac{b}{25 \cdot R_{e}}$ gives $\dfrac{1}{10}\,\mathrm A$ before the change and $\dfrac{1}{20}\,\mathrm A$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
