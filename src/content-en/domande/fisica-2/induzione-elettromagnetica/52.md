A coil of $200$ turns, each of area $2.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, has its turns perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field and is connected to a circuit of total resistance $40\,\Omega$. The coil is flipped over, rotating it by $180^\circ$, and the charge $6.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{C}$ flows through the circuit. Neglecting self-induction, what is the magnitude of the field?

- $0.060\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.015\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.12\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.030\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

Before the rotation the flux linkage is $N B A$; after the rotation the normal has the opposite direction, so the flux is $-N B A$. The change in flux therefore has magnitude $2 N B A$. By **Felici's law** the charge that flows through the circuit has magnitude $q = \dfrac{2 N B A}{R}$, and hence
$$
B = \frac{q R}{2 N A} = \frac{6.0 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 40}{2 \cdot 200 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{T} = 0.030\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
The value $0.060\,\mathrm{T}$ is obtained by taking a change in flux equal to $N B A$, as if the flux became zero.
