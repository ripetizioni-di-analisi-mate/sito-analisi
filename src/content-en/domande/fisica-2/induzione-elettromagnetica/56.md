A copper disk of radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ rotates at $3000$ revolutions per minute about its own axis, which is parallel to a uniform magnetic field of $0.80\,\mathrm{T}$. Two sliding contacts touch the axis and the rim of the disk. What emf is measured between the two contacts?

- $2.5\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.3\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.63\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Each radius of the disk behaves like a bar rotating about one end, so between the center and the rim there is the emf $\mathcal{E} = \dfrac{1}{2} B \omega r^2$. The angular frequency is
$$
\omega = \frac{3000 \cdot 2\pi}{60}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 314\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
Therefore $\mathcal{E} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.80 \cdot 314 \cdot 0.10^2\,\mathrm{V} \approx 1.3\,\mathrm{V}$. The value $2.5\,\mathrm{V}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $0.20\,\mathrm{V}$ by using the revolutions per second instead of the angular frequency.
