Two equal circular loops are coaxial and face each other at a short distance. In the first loop flows a current that increases in time, while the second is not connected to any source. Which statement is true?

- In the second loop the induced current flows in the same direction as the current in the first, and the loops attract each other.
- In the second loop the induced current flows in the opposite direction to the current in the first, and the loops attract each other.
- In the second loop the induced current flows in the opposite direction to the current in the first, and the loops repel each other.
- In the second loop the induced current flows in the same direction as the current in the first, and the loops repel each other.

## Explanation

The field of the first loop passes through the second and grows with the current, so the flux linked with the second loop increases. By **Lenz's law** the induced current generates inside the second loop a field opposite to that of the first. Therefore the induced current flows in the opposite direction to the current in the first loop. Two coaxial loops carrying currents in opposite directions repel each other, like two parallel wires carrying opposite currents. The repulsion tends to move the second loop away and hence to reduce the increase in flux, in agreement with Lenz's law.
