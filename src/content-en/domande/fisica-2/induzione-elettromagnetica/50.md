A bar magnet lies along the axis of a conducting loop, with its north pole facing the loop, and moves away from it. What is the direction of the current induced in the loop, as seen by an observer on the side of the magnet?

- Counterclockwise
- Clockwise
- The current is zero, because the field of the magnet does not change direction
- Clockwise if the magnet moves away slowly, counterclockwise if it moves away quickly

## Explanation

Field lines leave the north pole, so in the loop the field of the magnet points away from the observer, that is, into the page for him. The magnet moves away, therefore the magnitude of the field in the loop decreases and the inward flux decreases. By **Lenz's law** the induced current generates a field that opposes the decrease, that is, a field also pointing inward. By the right-hand rule an inward field inside the loop is produced by a current that, as seen by the observer, flows **clockwise**. The direction depends on the sign of the change in flux, not on its rate.
