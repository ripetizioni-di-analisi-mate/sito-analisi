A real coil has inductance $0.30\,\mathrm{H}$ and resistance $1.0\,\Omega$. It is connected in series with a resistor of $5.0\,\Omega$ and an ideal source of $12\,\mathrm{V}$, and the circuit has reached steady state. What potential difference is measured across the coil?

- $0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $12\,\mathrm{V}$
- $10\,\mathrm{V}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

A real coil is represented as an ideal inductor in series with its resistance. In steady state the current is constant, so the voltage $L\,\dfrac{di}{dt}$ across the ideal inductor is zero. The current is therefore $I = \dfrac{12}{5.0 + 1.0}\,\mathrm{A} = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$, and across the coil there remains only the drop on its resistance, $1.0 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{V} = 2.0\,\mathrm{V}$. The value $0\,\mathrm{V}$ holds for an ideal inductor, and $10\,\mathrm{V}$ is the voltage across the external resistor.
