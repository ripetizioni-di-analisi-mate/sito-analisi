A piece of wire $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ long, placed at the origin and oriented along the $z$ axis, carries the current $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the direction of $\hat{k}$. Which vector is the contribution $d\vec{B}$ of the piece to the magnetic field at the point $P = (3.0,\ 4.0,\ 0)\,\mathrm{cm}$?

- $(-6.4\,\hat{\imath} + 4.8\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $(6.4\,\hat{\imath} - 4.8\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $(-4.8\,\hat{\imath} + 6.4\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

The vector from the piece to $P$ is $\vec{r} = (3.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$, of magnitude $r = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$.

Since $\hat{k} \times \hat{\imath} = \hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{k} \times \hat{\jmath} = -\hat{\imath}$, we have $d\vec{l} \times \vec{r} = dl\,(-4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$. By the Biot-Savart law, written in the form $d\vec{B} = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\,\dfrac{I\,d\vec{l} \times \vec{r}}{r^3}$,
$$
d\vec{B} = 10^{-7} \cdot \frac{2.0 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{(5.0 \cdot 10^{-2})^3}\,(-4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{T} = (-6.4\,\hat{\imath} + 4.8\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
The contribution is perpendicular to $\vec{r}$, as confirmed by the dot product $(-6.4)(3.0) + (4.8)(4.0) = 0$.
