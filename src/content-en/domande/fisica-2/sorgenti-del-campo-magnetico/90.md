An ideal solenoid has a homogeneous linear core filling its interior. The geometry and current are the same as in the measurement without a core. Given the relative permeability of the core $\mu = 5$ and the field magnitude with the core $B = \dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{mT}$, what is the field magnitude without the core $B_{0}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{50}\,\mathrm{mT}$
- $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{mT}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{mT}$
- $\dfrac{15}{2}\,\mathrm{mT}$

## Explanation

The magnetizing field remains fixed by the free current. The permeability is multiplied by $\mu$, so the magnitude of $\vec B$ increases by the same factor. In the indicated units, $B = B_{0} \cdot \mu$. Solving for the unknown gives $B_{0} = \dfrac{B}{\mu}$. Using the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{mT}$.
