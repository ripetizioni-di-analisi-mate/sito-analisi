The field produced by a very long straight wire is measured. The distance is in centimeters. Use $\mu_0/(2\pi)=2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Given the distance from the wire $r = 3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the field magnitude $B = 40\,\mathrm{\mu T}$, what is the current $I_{e}$?

- $12\,\mathrm{A}$
- $60\,\mathrm{A}$
- $6\,\mathrm{A}$
- $18\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

For a long wire, $B=\mu_0 I/(2\pi r)$. Converting the distance to meters and the result to microteslas gives the coefficient $20$. In the indicated units, $B = \dfrac{20 \cdot I_{e}}{r}$. Solving for the unknown gives $I_{e} = \dfrac{B \cdot r}{20}$. Using the given values, we obtain $6\,\mathrm{A}$.
