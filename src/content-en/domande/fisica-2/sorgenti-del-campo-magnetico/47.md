A proton moves with speed $1.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At the instant considered, what is the magnitude of the magnetic field produced by the proton at a point $1.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ from it, along a direction perpendicular to the velocity? Use $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $3.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

In a piece of wire the moving charge $dq$ travels the segment $d\vec{l}$ in the time $dt$, so $I\,d\vec{l} = \dfrac{dq}{dt}\,d\vec{l} = dq\,\vec{v}$. Therefore a charge $q$ with velocity $\vec{v}$ is treated in the Biot-Savart law as a current element with $I\,d\vec{l} = q\,\vec{v}$, and its field has magnitude
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\,\frac{q v \sin\varphi}{r^2} .
$$
In the direction perpendicular to the velocity $\sin\varphi = 1$, hence
$$
B = 10^{-7} \cdot \frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{6}}{(1.0 \cdot 10^{-6})^2}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
