A straight segment $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, part of a circuit whose remainder is far away, carries the current $10\,\mathrm{A}$. The point $P$ lies on the perpendicular to the segment through its midpoint, $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the segment. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field produced by the segment at $P$?

- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

For the field of a straight segment
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}\,(\sin\theta_2 - \sin\theta_1) ,
$$
with $d = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$ the angles under which the endpoints are seen from $P$.

The foot of the perpendicular is the midpoint, and the endpoints are $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from it, as much as the distance $d$; hence $\theta_2 = 45^\circ$ and $\theta_1 = -45^\circ$. Therefore
$$
B = \frac{10^{-7} \cdot 10}{0.050}\,\bigl(\sin 45^\circ + \sin 45^\circ\bigr)\,\mathrm{T} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 2.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
The value $4.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ is the field of an infinite wire at the same distance, which corresponds to $\theta_2 = 90^\circ$ and $\theta_1 = -90^\circ$.
