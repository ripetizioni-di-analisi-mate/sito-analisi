Two straight parallel wires carry currents of equal magnitude in opposite directions. At the midpoint between the wires, how do the magnetic fields combine?

- They point in opposite directions and cancel
- They point in the same direction and the resultant magnitude is twice the contribution of one wire
- They are perpendicular and the magnitude increases by a factor of $\sqrt2$
- Only the first wire contributes to the field

## Explanation

Between the wires, reversing the current direction and moving to the opposite side of the wire produces the same field direction. The distances are equal, so the two magnitudes add.
