The International System fixes the elementary charge at the exact value $e = 1.602176634 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, and the ampere is defined from this value. How many elementary charges cross the cross section of a wire each second when it carries the steady current $1.0\,\mathrm{mA}$?

- $6.2 \cdot 10^{18}$
- $6.2 \cdot 10^{12}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-22}$
- $6.2 \cdot 10^{15}$

## Explanation

A steady current $I$ carries in one second the charge $I \cdot 1\,\mathrm{s}$, so in one second the charge $1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{C}$ crosses the section.

The number of elementary charges is therefore
$$
\frac{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{C}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}} \approx 6.2 \cdot 10^{15} .
$$
With the same definition, the current of $1\,\mathrm{A}$ corresponds to about $6.2 \cdot 10^{18}$ elementary charges per second.
