A very long thin-walled cylindrical metal tube has radius $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and carries the current $10\,\mathrm{A}$, parallel to the axis and uniformly distributed over the wall. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field $5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis?

- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

By cylindrical symmetry the field is tangent to the coaxial circles and its magnitude depends only on the distance $r$ from the axis.

The coaxial circle of radius $5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ lies inside the tube and is not crossed by any current, because all the current flows in the wall.

By Ampère's law we therefore have $B\,2\pi r = 0$, so the field inside the tube is **zero**. The value $4.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$ is the one a thin wire along the axis would produce at the same distance.
