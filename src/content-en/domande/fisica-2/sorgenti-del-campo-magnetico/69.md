The graph shows the magnitude of the magnetic field as a function of the distance $r$ from the axis of a distribution of straight currents with cylindrical symmetry. Which distribution produces this field?

::grafico{nomi="r,B" f="max(0,min(x,1/x,(16-x*x)/(7*x)))" segmenti="1,0,1,1;3,0,3,0.333" etichette="1.1,-0.12,a;3.1,-0.12,b;4.1,-0.12,c" legenda="B(r)" xmin=0 xmax=5.4 ymin=-0.2 ymax=1.3 titolo="Magnitude of the magnetic field as a function of the distance $r$ from the axis"}

- A solid wire with current uniformly distributed over its cross section
- A thin-walled cylindrical tube carrying a current
- A coaxial cable with currents in the same direction in the two conductors
- A coaxial cable with equal and opposite currents in the two conductors

## Explanation

By Ampère's law applied to the coaxial circle of radius $r$ we have $B\,2\pi r = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}(r)$.

The linear part up to $a$ and the part proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r}$ up to $b$ correspond to a solid conductor of radius $a$ with uniform current, surrounded by a region without current.

Between $b$ and $c$ the field drops to zero faster than $\dfrac{1}{r}$, so the enclosed current decreases, and therefore a current flows in the opposite direction in that region. For $r \geq c$ the enclosed current is zero, therefore the field is that of a **coaxial cable** with equal and opposite currents.
