A wire segment follows a semicircle of radius $R$ centred on O. At the two ends the wire continues along radial segments extending away from O. When calculating the field at O due to these three parts, why can only the semicircular integral be kept?

- The radial segments always generate equal and opposite fields even if their lengths differ.
- On each radial segment $d\boldsymbol{\ell}$ is parallel or antiparallel to the vector towards O.
- O lies outside the segments, so any wire that does not pass through O can be ignored.
- A straight segment creates zero field anywhere in its plane.

## Explanation

In the Biot–Savart law the cross product $d\boldsymbol{\ell}\times\hat{\mathbf r}$ vanishes element by element on each radial section. On the arc the vectors are perpendicular, giving a nonzero contribution.
