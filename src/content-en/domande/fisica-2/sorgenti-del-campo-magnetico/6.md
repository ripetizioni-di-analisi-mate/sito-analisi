A very long wire is bent into a hairpin: two parallel, semi-infinite straight segments are joined by a semicircle with center $O$ and radius $R = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, and they start from the ends of the diameter, perpendicular to it. The wire carries the current $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field at $O$? Use $\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$.

- $7.9 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.9 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

The field at $O$ is the sum of the contributions of the semicircle and of the two straight segments, which by the right-hand rule are all perpendicular to the plane of the wire and have the same direction.

The semicircle is an arc of angle $\pi$, so it contributes $\dfrac{\mu_0 I \pi}{4\pi R} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4R}$. Each straight segment is semi-infinite and ends at the foot of the perpendicular drawn from $O$, at distance $R$, therefore it contributes $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R}$.

The magnitude of the field is therefore
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4R} + 2 \cdot \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{R} \left( \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2\pi} \right) \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
The value $7.9 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ is the field of the semicircle alone, and $1.8 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$ is obtained by using the field of an infinite wire for the straight segments.
