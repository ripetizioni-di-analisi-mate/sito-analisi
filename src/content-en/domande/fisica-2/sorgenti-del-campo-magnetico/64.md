An infinite straight wire carries the current $20\,\mathrm{A}$. A straight segment of a second circuit, carrying the current $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$, lies in a plane containing the wire, is perpendicular to the wire, and extends from a distance of $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ to a distance of $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on the segment?

- $6.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

At the points of the segment the field of the wire is perpendicular to the plane and has magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi r}$, which varies along the segment; therefore the segment is divided into elements of length $dr$, on each of which the field is uniform.

Each element is perpendicular to the field, so it experiences a force of magnitude $dF = I_2\,dr\,\dfrac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi r}$, and all the elementary forces have the same direction, parallel to the infinite wire. Adding them up gives
$$
F = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi} \int_a^b \frac{dr}{r} = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi}\,\ln\frac{b}{a} = 2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 20 \cdot 5.0 \cdot \ln 4\,\mathrm{N} \approx 2.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
