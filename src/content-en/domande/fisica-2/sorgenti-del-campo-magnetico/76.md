A long solenoid is made of two coaxial layers of turns, with the same number $n$ of turns per unit length: the second layer is wound in the opposite sense to the first, so that the currents in the two layers circulate around the axis in opposite directions. What is the magnetic field on the axis, far from the ends, when the wire carries the current $I$?

- $\mu_0 n I$
- $0$
- $2\mu_0 n I$
- $\dfrac{\mu_0 n I}{2}$

## Explanation

Each layer is an ideal solenoid with $n$ turns per unit length carrying the current $I$, which produces inside it the uniform field $\mu_0 n I$, parallel to the axis, and outside it a zero field.

The axis is inside both layers, so it receives from each a field of magnitude $\mu_0 n I$.

The currents circulate in opposite directions, therefore by the right-hand rule the two fields have opposite directions and the field on the axis is **zero**.
