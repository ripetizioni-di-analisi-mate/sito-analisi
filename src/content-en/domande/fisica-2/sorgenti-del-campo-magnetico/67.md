A thin, very large flat conducting sheet carries a uniformly distributed current, all in the same direction: $200\,\mathrm{A}$ flow through each meter of width of the sheet. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field near the sheet, far from its edges?

- $2.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

By symmetry the field is parallel to the sheet and perpendicular to the current, has the same magnitude $B$ on the two sides of the sheet and opposite directions.

Apply Ampère's law to a rectangle with two sides of length $h$ parallel to the sheet, one on each side, and two sides perpendicular to the sheet, which do not contribute because there $\vec{B}$ is perpendicular to $d\vec{l}$.

The circulation is $2Bh$ and the enclosed current is $K h$, with $K = 200\,\mathrm{A/m}$, so $2Bh = \mu_0 K h$. Therefore
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 K}{2} = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 200}{2}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} ,
$$
independent of the distance from the sheet.
