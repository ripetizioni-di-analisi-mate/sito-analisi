Two long straight parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. The field is measured exactly halfway between the wires. The first current is stronger and each wire is at a distance of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the point. Use $\mu_0/(2\pi)=2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Initially, the given quantities are the first current $I_{1} = 12\,\mathrm A$ and the second current $I_{2} = 4\,\mathrm A$. In the second configuration, we change the first current to $18\,\mathrm A$ and the second current to $6\,\mathrm A$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $B$?

- $20\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $30\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $-20\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $40\,\mathrm{\mu T}$

## Explanation

At the center, the two fields point in opposite directions according to the right-hand rule. The distance is the same for both, and each ampere produces $5\,\mathrm{\mu T}$. Applying $B = 5 \cdot I_{1} - 5 \cdot I_{2}$ gives $40\,\mathrm{\mu T}$ before the change and $60\,\mathrm{\mu T}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $20\,\mathrm{\mu T}$.
