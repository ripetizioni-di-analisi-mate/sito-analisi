A very long cylindrical wire of radius $R = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries the total current $8.0\,\mathrm{A}$, parallel to the axis. The current density is not uniform: it is proportional to the distance $r$ from the axis, $J = k r$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis?

- $1.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.7 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $5.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $6.7 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

The current through the coaxial disk of radius $r$ is obtained by adding the contributions of the annuli of radius $s$ and thickness $ds$, of area $2\pi s\,ds$:
$$
I(r) = \int_0^r k s \cdot 2\pi s\,ds = \frac{2\pi k r^3}{3} .
$$
For $r = R$ this gives the total current $I$, hence $I(r) = I\,\dfrac{r^3}{R^3}$. At $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$, that is, at half the radius, the enclosed current is therefore $\dfrac{I}{8} = 1.0\,\mathrm{A}$.

By cylindrical symmetry the field is tangent to the coaxial circle of radius $r$ and has constant magnitude along it, so by Ampère's law $B\,2\pi r = \mu_0 I(r)$ and
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I(r)}{2\pi r} = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 1.0}{1.5 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
The value $2.7 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$ corresponds to a current uniformly distributed over the cross section, for which the enclosed current would be $\dfrac{I}{4}$.
