Two circular loops of radius $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ have the same center and lie in two perpendicular planes; they are insulated at the points where they cross, and each carries the current $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field at the common center?

- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $3.1 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $4.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

Each loop produces at the center the field $B_0 = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R} = \dfrac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 3.0}{2 \cdot 0.060}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.14 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$, directed along its own axis.

The planes of the loops are perpendicular, so the two axes and the two fields are perpendicular too. Therefore the total field has magnitude
$$
B = \sqrt{B_0^2 + B_0^2} = \sqrt{2}\,B_0 \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
The value $6.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ would be obtained by adding the magnitudes as if the fields were parallel.
