Two long straight parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. The field is measured exactly halfway between the wires. The first current is stronger and each wire is at a distance of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the point. Use $\mu_0/(2\pi)=2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Given the first current $I_{1} = 12\,\mathrm A$ and the second current $I_{2} = 4\,\mathrm A$, what is the magnitude of the resultant field $B$?

- $40\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $80\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $60\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $20\,\mathrm{\mu T}$

## Explanation

At the center, the two fields point in opposite directions according to the right-hand rule. The distance is the same for both, and each ampere produces $5\,\mathrm{\mu T}$. In the indicated units, $B = 5 \cdot I_{1} - 5 \cdot I_{2}$. Using the given values, we obtain $40\,\mathrm{\mu T}$.
