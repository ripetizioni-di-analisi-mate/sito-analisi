Consider the vector field $\vec{F} = k\,(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath})$, with $k$ a nonzero constant. Can it be a magnetic field?

- Yes, because it is defined and differentiable everywhere
- No, because it is not uniform
- No, because its divergence equals $2k$ and is not zero
- Yes, because its curl is zero

## Explanation

By Gauss's law for the magnetic field, in local form, every magnetic field has zero divergence, $\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0$.

For the given field
$$
\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial (kx)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (ky)}{\partial y} = 2k \neq 0 ,
$$
so the outward flux through a closed surface enclosing a volume $V$ equals $2kV$ and is not zero.

Therefore $\vec{F}$ **cannot** be a magnetic field. A zero curl would indicate the absence of currents in the region, but the condition on the divergence is not satisfied.
