The field produced by a very long straight wire is measured. The distance is in centimeters. Use $\mu_0/(2\pi)=2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Initially, the given quantities are the current $I_{e} = 6\,\mathrm{A}$ and the distance from the wire $r = 3\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the distance from the wire to $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $B$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

For a long wire, $B=\mu_0 I/(2\pi r)$. Converting the distance to meters and the result to microteslas gives the coefficient $20$. Applying $B = \dfrac{20 \cdot I_{e}}{r}$ gives $40\,\mathrm{\mu T}$ before the change and $20\,\mathrm{\mu T}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
