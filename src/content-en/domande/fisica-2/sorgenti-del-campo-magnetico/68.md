In a region inside a conductor the steady magnetic field is $\vec{B} = c\,(-y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$, with $c = 0.50\,\mathrm{T/m}$. What is the magnitude of the current density in that region?

- $4.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$

## Explanation

For steady currents the local form of Ampère's law holds, $\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J}$.

The components of the field are $B_x = -cy$, $B_y = cx$ and $B_z = 0$, and they do not depend on $z$; hence the curl has only the component along $\hat{k}$, which equals
$$
\frac{\partial B_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial B_x}{\partial y} = c + c = 2c .
$$
Therefore the current density is directed along $\hat{k}$ and has magnitude
$$
J = \frac{2c}{\mu_0} = \frac{1.0}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{A/m^2} \approx 8.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{A/m^2} .
$$
