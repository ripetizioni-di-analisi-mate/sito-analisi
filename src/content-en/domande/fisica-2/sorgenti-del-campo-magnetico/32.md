Three long parallel straight wires, $A$, $B$ and $C$, cross in this order a line perpendicular to the wires, with $AB = BC$. They carry currents in the same direction, of magnitude $I$ in wire $A$ and $3I$ in wire $C$. How is the resultant magnetic force on wire $B$ directed?

- Toward $A$
- Perpendicular to the plane of the three wires
- The resultant force is zero
- Toward $C$

## Explanation

Wires carrying currents in the same direction attract each other, so $A$ pulls $B$ toward $A$ and $C$ pulls $B$ toward $C$.

The force per unit length between two parallel wires is $\dfrac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}$ and the distances $AB$ and $BC$ are equal, therefore the two forces are in the ratio of the currents in wires $A$ and $C$. The attraction toward $C$ is therefore three times the attraction toward $A$.

The resultant force on wire $B$ is therefore directed **toward $C$**, and its magnitude is twice the attraction exerted by $A$.
