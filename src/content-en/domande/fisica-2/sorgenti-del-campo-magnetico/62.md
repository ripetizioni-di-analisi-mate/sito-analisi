Two long parallel wires are separated by $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The current magnitudes are positive. Use $\mu_0/(2\pi)=2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Given the current in the second wire $I_{2} = 6\,\mathrm A$ and the magnitude of the force per unit length $b = 192\,\mathrm{\mu N/m}$, what is the current in the first wire $I_{1}$?

- $16\,\mathrm A$
- $8\,\mathrm A$
- $186\,\mathrm A$
- $4\,\mathrm A$

## Explanation

The field of the first wire at the second is $\mu_0 I_1/(2\pi d)$. To find the force per unit length, multiply by $I_2$. In the indicated units, $b = 4 \cdot I_{1} \cdot I_{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $I_{1} = \dfrac{b}{4 \cdot I_{2}}$. Using the given values, we obtain $8\,\mathrm A$.
