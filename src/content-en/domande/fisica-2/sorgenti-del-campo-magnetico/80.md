A long solenoid has radius $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $1000$ turns per meter carrying the current $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. A circular loop of radius $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ surrounds the solenoid, coaxial with it and far from the ends. What is the flux of the magnetic field through the loop?

- $7.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $7.9 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $2.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb}$

## Explanation

Choose as surface the flat disk bounded by the loop.

Inside the solenoid the field is uniform, parallel to the axis, and equals $B = \mu_0 n I \approx 2.51 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$, while outside the solenoid it is zero. Therefore only the part of the disk inside the solenoid, of radius $r_1 = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, contributes to the flux, and
$$
\Phi_B = B\pi r_1^2 = 2.51 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \pi \cdot (0.010)^2\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 7.9 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{Wb} .
$$
The value $7.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb}$ would be obtained by using the area of the loop, as if the field were present outside the solenoid too.
