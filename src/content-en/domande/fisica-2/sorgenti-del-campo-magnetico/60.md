A circular loop in vacuum carries current. Its radius is expressed in centimeters. Use $\mu_0=4\pi\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Initially, the given quantities are the current $I_{e} = 3\,\mathrm{A}$ and the loop radius $R = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the current to $5\,\mathrm{A}$ and the loop radius to $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $B$?

- $10 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $1\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $2\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $0\,\mathrm{\mu T}$

## Explanation

The Biot–Savart law integrated around the circle gives $\mu_0 I/(2R)$. The contributions are all along the normal. Applying $B = \dfrac{20 \cdot \pi \cdot I_{e}}{R}$ gives $10 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{\mu T}$ before the change and $10 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{\mu T}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $0\,\mathrm{\mu T}$.
