In the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom the electron moves on a circular orbit of radius $5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$ with speed $2.2 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Treating the motion of the electron as a loop carrying a current, what is the magnitude of the magnetic field at the nucleus? Use $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

- $25\,\mathrm{T}$
- $13\,\mathrm{T}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{T}$
- $6.3\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

The electron completes one revolution in the time $\dfrac{2\pi r}{v}$, so the equivalent current is
$$
I = \frac{e v}{2\pi r} = \frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.2 \cdot 10^{6}}{2\pi \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 1.06 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{A} .
$$
The nucleus is at the center of the orbit, therefore by the field at the center of a loop
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2r} = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 1.06 \cdot 10^{-3}}{2 \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 13\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
