Two infinite parallel straight wires are $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart and carry the currents $4.0\,\mathrm{A}$ and $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the same direction. A third wire, parallel to the first two and carrying a current, is placed in their plane. In which position is the total magnetic force on the third wire zero?

- Between the two wires, $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the $4.0\,\mathrm{A}$ wire
- Between the two wires, $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the $4.0\,\mathrm{A}$ wire
- At the midpoint, $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from each wire
- Outside the two wires, $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$ wire

## Explanation

The force on the third wire is zero where the field produced by the first two is zero.

The currents are in the same direction, so between the two wires their fields have opposite directions, while outside the strip between the wires they have the same direction and do not cancel.

Calling $x$ the distance from the $4.0\,\mathrm{A}$ wire, the magnitudes are equal if
$$
\frac{\mu_0 \cdot 4.0}{2\pi x} = \frac{\mu_0 \cdot 1.0}{2\pi\,(0.30 - x)} ,
$$
that is, $4.0\,(0.30 - x) = x$, which gives $x = 0.24\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore the third wire must be placed between the two, $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the $4.0\,\mathrm{A}$ wire, whatever its current.
