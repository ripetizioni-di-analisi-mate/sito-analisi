A very long horizontal straight wire is fixed and carries the current $50\,\mathrm{A}$. Above it, parallel to it, a second wire with mass per unit length $1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg/m}$ carries the current $50\,\mathrm{A}$ in the opposite direction and remains suspended in equilibrium. At what distance from the fixed wire is it? Use $\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5.1\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The currents are in opposite directions, so the two wires repel each other and the magnetic force on the upper wire points upward.

On a stretch of length $\ell$ and mass $m$ of the upper wire the magnetic force is $\dfrac{\mu_0 I^2 \ell}{2\pi d}$ and the weight is $m g$. At equilibrium the two forces have the same magnitude, therefore
$$
d = \frac{\mu_0 I^2}{2\pi g} \cdot \frac{\ell}{m} = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 50^2}{2\pi \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 5.1 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The suspended wire is therefore $5.1\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the fixed wire. The value $0.10\,\mathrm{cm}$ is obtained by writing the current without squaring it, and $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ by using $4\pi$ instead of $2\pi$ in the force between the wires.
