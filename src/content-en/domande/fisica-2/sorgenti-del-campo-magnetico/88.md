A vertical infinite straight wire carries a current directed upward. An electron is near the wire and moves radially, away from the wire. How is the magnetic force on the electron directed?

- Parallel to the wire, upward
- Radial, toward the wire
- Parallel to the wire, downward
- Tangent to the field lines of the wire

## Explanation

Take the $z$ axis along the wire, upward, and use the unit vectors $\hat{r}$, radial pointing away from the wire, and $\hat{\phi}$, tangent to the circles in the counterclockwise direction seen from above. The velocity of the electron is $\vec{v} = v\,\hat{r}$, and by the right-hand rule the field of the wire is $\vec{B} = B\,\hat{\phi}$ with $B > 0$.

Since $\hat{r} \times \hat{\phi} = \hat{k}$, the Lorentz force on a charge $q$ is $q\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B} = q v B\,\hat{k}$.

The charge of the electron is $-e$, so the force is $-evB\,\hat{k}$. Therefore the force is **parallel to the wire and directed downward**, opposite to the current.
