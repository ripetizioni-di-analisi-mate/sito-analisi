A permanent magnet shaped as a long thin cylinder is uniformly magnetized along its axis, with magnetization of magnitude $8.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{A/m}$. What is the magnetic field inside the magnet, far from its bases?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.16\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

A cylinder uniformly magnetized along its axis produces the same field as a surface current of $M$ amperes per meter, that is, as a solenoid with $nI = M$.

The cylinder is long and thin, so far from the bases it behaves as an ideal solenoid and the field inside is
$$
B = \mu_0 M = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 8.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.0\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
There are no currents in turns, therefore the whole field is due to the magnetization.
