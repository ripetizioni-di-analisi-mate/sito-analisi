A long conducting cylinder has radius $4\,\mathrm{mm}$. The current is uniformly distributed across its cross section. Internal coaxial circles are considered. Given the distance from the axis $r = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the internal field magnitude $B = 30\,\mathrm{\mu T}$, what is the field magnitude at the surface $B_{s}$?

- $120\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $60\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $15\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $30\,\mathrm{\mu T}$

## Explanation

The enclosed current increases with the area, and thus with $r^2$. The Amperian circumference increases with $r$, so the internal field is linear in $r$. In the indicated units, $B = \dfrac{B_{s} \cdot r}{4}$. Solving for the unknown gives $B_{s} = \dfrac{4 \cdot B}{r}$. Using the given values, we obtain $60\,\mathrm{\mu T}$.
