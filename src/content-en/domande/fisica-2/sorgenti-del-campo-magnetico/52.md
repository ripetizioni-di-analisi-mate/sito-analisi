A semi-infinite straight wire, part of a circuit whose remainder is far away, carries the current $10\,\mathrm{A}$. The point $P$ is $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the line of the wire, and the foot $H$ of the perpendicular from $P$ to the line lies outside the wire, $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from its end. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field produced by the wire at $P$?

- $5.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $3.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

Use the coordinate $s$ along the line of the wire, with origin at $H$ and oriented in the direction of the current. The wire occupies the positions from $s_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $+\infty$, or from $-\infty$ to $s_2 = -5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, and in both cases the difference of the sines has the same value.

In the first case $\sin\theta_2 \to 1$ and $\sin\theta_1 = \dfrac{5.0}{\sqrt{5.0^2 + 5.0^2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. Therefore, by the field of a straight segment,
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}\left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{10^{-7} \cdot 10}{0.050} \cdot 0.293\,\mathrm{T} \approx 5.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
The value $2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ is the field of a semi-infinite wire ending exactly at $H$.
