Two long straight parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. The field is measured exactly halfway between the wires. The first current is stronger and each wire is at a distance of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the point. Use $\mu_0/(2\pi)=2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Given the second current $I_{2} = 4\,\mathrm A$ and the magnitude of the resultant field $B = 40\,\mathrm{\mu T}$, what is the first current $I_{1}$?

- $8\,\mathrm A$
- $24\,\mathrm A$
- $4\,\mathrm A$
- $12\,\mathrm A$

## Explanation

At the center, the two fields point in opposite directions according to the right-hand rule. The distance is the same for both, and each ampere produces $5\,\mathrm{\mu T}$. In the indicated units, $B = 5 \cdot I_{1} - 5 \cdot I_{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $I_{1} = \dfrac{B}{5} + I_{2}$. Using the given values, we obtain $12\,\mathrm A$.
