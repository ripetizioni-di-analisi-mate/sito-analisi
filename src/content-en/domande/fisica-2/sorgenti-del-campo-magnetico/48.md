The field produced by a very long straight wire is measured. The distance is in centimeters. Use $\mu_0/(2\pi)=2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Given the current $I_{e} = 6\,\mathrm{A}$ and the field magnitude $B = 40\,\mathrm{\mu T}$, what is the distance from the wire $r$?

- $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $3\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

For a long wire, $B=\mu_0 I/(2\pi r)$. Converting the distance to meters and the result to microteslas gives the coefficient $20$. In the indicated units, $B = \dfrac{20 \cdot I_{e}}{r}$. Solving for the unknown gives $r = \dfrac{20 \cdot I_{e}}{B}$. Using the given values, we obtain $3\,\mathrm{cm}$.
