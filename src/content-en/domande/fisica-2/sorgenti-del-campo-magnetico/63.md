Two long parallel straight wires carry the currents $I_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{A}$ and $I_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the same direction. Which statement about the magnetic forces acting on segments of equal length of the two wires is true?

- The two forces have the same magnitude and opposite directions
- The force on wire $2$ is three times the force on wire $1$
- The force on wire $1$ is three times the force on wire $2$
- The two forces have the same magnitude and the same direction

## Explanation

Wire $2$ lies in the field of wire $1$, of magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi d}$, so a segment of length $\ell$ experiences a force of magnitude $I_2 \ell\,\dfrac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi d}$.

In the same way wire $1$ experiences a force of magnitude $I_1 \ell\,\dfrac{\mu_0 I_2}{2\pi d}$, which equals the previous one, because both contain the product $I_1 I_2$.

The currents are in the same direction, therefore each wire is attracted toward the other and the two forces have opposite directions, in agreement with Newton's third law.
