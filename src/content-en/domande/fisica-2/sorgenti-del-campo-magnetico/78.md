In a region of space the magnetic field is $\vec{B} = k\,(x\,\hat{\imath} - y\,\hat{\jmath})$, with $k = 0.20\,\mathrm{T/m}$. A cube of side $L = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ has a vertex at the origin and its edges along the positive half-axes. What is the outward flux of $\vec{B}$ through the face of the cube lying in the plane $y = L$?

- $2.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $-2.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $-5.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $-1.3 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$

## Explanation

On the face $y = L$ the outward normal is $\hat{\jmath}$, and the component of the field along $\hat{\jmath}$ is $B_y = -kL$ at every point of the face.

The flux is therefore
$$
\Phi_B = -kL \cdot L^2 = -0.20 \cdot 0.50^3\,\mathrm{Wb} = -2.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb} ,
$$
and the negative sign means that the field enters the cube through that face.

The outward flux through the face $x = L$ is $+kL \cdot L^2$, while on the other four faces the normal component of the field is zero; the total flux is therefore zero, in agreement with Gauss's law for the magnetic field.
