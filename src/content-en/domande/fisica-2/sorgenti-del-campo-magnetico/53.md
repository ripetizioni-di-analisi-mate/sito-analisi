A very long vertical straight wire carries the current $20\,\mathrm{A}$ directed upward. Near the wire the horizontal component of the Earth's magnetic field is $2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ and points north. At which point of the horizontal plane is the total horizontal magnetic field zero?

- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire, to the east
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire, to the west
- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire, to the west
- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire, to the south

## Explanation

The field of the wire is horizontal with magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi d}$; the total field is zero where the field of the wire has the same magnitude as the Earth's field and the opposite direction, that is, pointing south.

The magnitudes are equal for
$$
d = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi B_T} = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 20}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Seen from above, with north up and east to the right, the current comes out of the page, so by the right-hand rule the field lines circulate counterclockwise. At the point west of the wire the counterclockwise direction points south, therefore the total field is zero $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire, to the west.
