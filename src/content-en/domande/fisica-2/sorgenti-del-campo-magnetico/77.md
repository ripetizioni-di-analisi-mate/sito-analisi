An ideal toroid has $N$ turns, carrying the current $I$, wound on a ring of inner radius $a$ and outer radius $b$. Which statement about the magnetic field at the points of the ring at distance $r$ from the axis is true?

- It equals the field of an ideal solenoid with $n = \dfrac{N}{2\pi r}$ turns per unit length
- It is uniform throughout the ring
- It is largest at the outer edge, for $r = b$
- It has magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r}$ also outside the ring

## Explanation

By Ampère's law applied to the coaxial circle of radius $r$, with $a < r < b$, the field is $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r}$.

The circle has length $2\pi r$, so $\dfrac{N}{2\pi r}$ is the number of turns per unit length measured along it, and $B = \mu_0 n I$ with $n = \dfrac{N}{2\pi r}$.

The field depends on $r$, therefore it is not uniform, and it is largest at the inner edge; outside the ring the enclosed current is zero and the field is zero.
