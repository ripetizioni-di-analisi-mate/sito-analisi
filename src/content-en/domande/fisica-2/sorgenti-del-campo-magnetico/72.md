A long cylindrical wire of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries a current uniformly distributed over its cross section. At the surface of the wire the magnetic field is $5.0\,\mathrm{mT}$. What is the magnitude of the current density in the wire?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $9.9 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$

## Explanation

At the surface the field is $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi R}$, and the uniform current is $I = J\pi R^2$.

Substituting gives $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 J \pi R^2}{2\pi R} = \dfrac{\mu_0 J R}{2}$, hence
$$
J = \frac{2B}{\mu_0 R} = \frac{2 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{A/m^2} \approx 4.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{A/m^2} .
$$
