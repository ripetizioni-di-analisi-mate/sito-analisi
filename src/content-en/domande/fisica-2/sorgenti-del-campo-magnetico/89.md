An infinite coaxial cable has an inner conductor of radius $a$ and an outer conductor with radii $b$ and $c$, with $a < b < c$; the conductors carry equal and opposite currents, uniformly distributed over the cross sections. At which distance $r$ from the axis is the magnitude of the magnetic field largest?

- $r = 0$
- $r = a$
- $r = b$
- $r = c$

## Explanation

By Ampère's law, for $0 \leq r \leq a$ the field is $\dfrac{\mu_0 I r}{2\pi a^2}$ and grows with $r$.

For $a \leq r \leq b$ the field is $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$ and decreases; between $b$ and $c$ it keeps decreasing, because $r$ increases while the enclosed current decreases, and it vanishes at $r = c$.

Hence the field is largest at the surface of the inner conductor, at $r = a$, where it equals $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi a}$; on the axis it is zero.
