A circular loop in vacuum carries current. Its radius is expressed in centimeters. Use $\mu_0=4\pi\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Given the current $I_{e} = 3\,\mathrm{A}$ and the field magnitude at the center $B = 10 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{\mu T}$, what is the loop radius $R$?

- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{6}{\pi}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $3\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The Biot–Savart law integrated around the circle gives $\mu_0 I/(2R)$. The contributions are all along the normal. In the indicated units, $B = \dfrac{20 \cdot \pi \cdot I_{e}}{R}$. Solving for the unknown gives $R = \dfrac{20 \cdot \pi \cdot I_{e}}{B}$. Using the given values, we obtain $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.
