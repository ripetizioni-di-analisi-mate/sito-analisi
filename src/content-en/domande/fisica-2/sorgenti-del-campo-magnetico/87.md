A long conducting cylinder has radius $4\,\mathrm{mm}$. The current is uniformly distributed across its cross section. Internal coaxial circles are considered. Given the field magnitude at the surface $B_{s} = 60\,\mathrm{\mu T}$ and the internal field magnitude $B = 30\,\mathrm{\mu T}$, what is the distance from the axis $r$?

- $2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $2 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $4\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The enclosed current increases with the area, and thus with $r^2$. The Amperian circumference increases with $r$, so the internal field is linear in $r$. In the indicated units, $B = \dfrac{B_{s} \cdot r}{4}$. Solving for the unknown gives $r = \dfrac{4 \cdot B}{B_{s}}$. Using the given values, we obtain $2\,\mathrm{mm}$.
