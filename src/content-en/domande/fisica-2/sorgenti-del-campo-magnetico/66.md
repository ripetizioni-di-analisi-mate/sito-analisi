An infinite straight wire carries the current $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$. A closed curve lies in a plane perpendicular to the wire and goes around the wire twice, always in the same direction, before returning to its starting point; its direction of traversal is related to the current by the right-hand rule. What is the circulation of the magnetic field along the curve?

- $1.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$
- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$
- $3.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$

## Explanation

In polar coordinates with the pole on the wire the dot product is $\vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}\,d\phi$, whatever the shape of the curve.

Along the curve the angle $\phi$ increases by $2\pi$ at each turn, so by $4\pi$ in total. Therefore
$$
\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi} \cdot 4\pi = 2\mu_0 I = 2 \cdot 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} .
$$
The result agrees with Ampère's law, because a surface bounded by the curve is crossed twice by the wire in the same direction, and the enclosed current is $2I$.
