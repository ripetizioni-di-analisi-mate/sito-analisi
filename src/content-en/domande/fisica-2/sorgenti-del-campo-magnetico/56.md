A very long straight wire, carrying the current $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$, forms a circular ring of radius $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ tangent to the straight part, as in the figure; the wire is insulated at the contact point. The straight part carries the current to the right and the ring counterclockwise. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field at the center $C$ of the ring?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3,0 3,0" curve="cos(t),1+sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="1.6,0,2.6,0,I;-1,1.3,-1,0.7" punti="0,1,pieno,C" segmenti="0,1,0,0" etichette="0.25,0.5,R" xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-0.6 ymax=2.4 titolo="Straight wire with a tangent ring of radius $R$; the current $I$ flows to the right in the straight part and counterclockwise in the ring"}

- $4.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $7.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $8.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

The field at $C$ is the sum of the field of the ring and that of the straight part, which is treated as an infinite wire at distance $R$ from $C$.

The ring produces at $C$ the field $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R} = \dfrac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 5.0}{2 \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 6.28 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$, out of the page, because the current circulates counterclockwise.

The straight part produces $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi R} = \dfrac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 5.0}{0.050}\,\mathrm{T} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$, also out of the page, because $C$ lies above a wire carrying current to the right.

The two fields point the same way, so $B \approx 6.28 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} + 2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 8.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$.
