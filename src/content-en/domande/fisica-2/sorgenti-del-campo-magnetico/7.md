Two concentric semicircles with center $O$ and radii $R$ and $2R$ are joined at their ends by two radial straight segments and form a single loop, carrying the current $I$. As seen by the observer, the current runs counterclockwise along the inner semicircle. What are the magnitude and direction of the magnetic field at $O$?

- $\dfrac{3\mu_0 I}{8R}$, toward the observer
- $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{8R}$, away from the observer
- $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{8R}$, toward the observer
- $0$

## Explanation

The radial segments are aligned with $O$, so at each of their points $d\vec{l}$ is parallel to the vector from the segment to $O$ and, by the Biot-Savart law, their contribution is zero.

The inner semicircle, traversed counterclockwise, produces at $O$ a field of magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4R}$ pointing toward the observer. In the loop the outer semicircle is traversed clockwise, therefore its field, of magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4 \cdot 2R} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{8R}$, points the opposite way.

The total field therefore has magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4R} - \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{8R} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{8R}$ and points toward the observer. The value $\dfrac{3\mu_0 I}{8R}$ is obtained by adding the two contributions as if they had the same direction.
