A long solenoid is made of a single layer of copper wire of diameter $0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$, with adjacent turns touching each other; the thickness of the insulation is negligible. What is the magnetic field inside, far from the ends, when the wire carries the current $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$?

- $7.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $3.8 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $7.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

The turns touch each other, so each turn occupies along the axis a length equal to the diameter of the wire, and the number of turns per unit length is
$$
n = \frac{1}{0.50 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}} .
$$
By the field of an ideal solenoid
$$
B = \mu_0 n I = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{T} \approx 7.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
