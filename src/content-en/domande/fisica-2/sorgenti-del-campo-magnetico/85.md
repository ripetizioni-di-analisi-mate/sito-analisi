A long conducting cylinder has radius $4\,\mathrm{mm}$. The current is uniformly distributed across its cross section. Internal coaxial circles are considered. Given the field magnitude at the surface $B_{s} = 60\,\mathrm{\mu T}$ and the distance from the axis $r = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$, what is the internal field magnitude $B$?

- $15\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $120\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $30\,\mathrm{\mu T}$
- $60\,\mathrm{\mu T}$

## Explanation

The enclosed current increases with the area, and thus with $r^2$. The Amperian circumference increases with $r$, so the internal field is linear in $r$. In the indicated units, $B = \dfrac{B_{s} \cdot r}{4}$. Using the given values, we obtain $30\,\mathrm{\mu T}$.
