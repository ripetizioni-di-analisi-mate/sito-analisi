In a region between two concentric spheres the electric field is radial and equals $\vec{E} = \dfrac{c}{r^3}\,\hat{r}$, with $c > 0$, where $r$ is the distance from the center and $\hat{r}$ the outward radial unit vector. Which statement about the charge density in the region is true?

- The density is negative and equals $-\dfrac{\varepsilon_0 c}{r^4}$
- The density is zero, because the field is radial
- The density is positive, because the field points outward
- The density is negative and equals $-\dfrac{3\varepsilon_0 c}{r^4}$

## Explanation

By **Gauss's law** the charge enclosed by the sphere of radius $r$ is $q_{\text{int}}(r) = \varepsilon_0 \cdot \dfrac{c}{r^3} \cdot 4\pi r^2 = \dfrac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 c}{r}$. This charge decreases as $r$ increases, so the layers of the region carry negative charge.

The shell of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$ has volume $4\pi r^2\,dr$ and contains the charge $dq = \dfrac{dq_{\text{int}}}{dr}\,dr = -\dfrac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 c}{r^2}\,dr$. Therefore
$$
\rho = \frac{1}{4\pi r^2}\,\frac{dq_{\text{int}}}{dr} = -\frac{\varepsilon_0 c}{r^4} .
$$
An outward radial field that decreases faster than $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$ therefore corresponds to a negative density.
