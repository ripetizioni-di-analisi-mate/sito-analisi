A very long straight wire has uniform charge. Far from the ends, the field at a distance of $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ has magnitude $E_0$. Given the distance of the second point from the wire $r = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the field magnitude at the second point $E = 40\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the field magnitude at $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ $E_{0}$?

- $\dfrac{40}{3}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $240\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $120\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $360\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

For a coaxial Gaussian cylinder, the enclosed charge per unit length is fixed. The lateral area increases linearly with $r$, so $E$ decreases as $1/r$. In the indicated units, $E = \dfrac{2 \cdot E_{0}}{r}$. Solving for the unknown gives $E_{0} = \dfrac{E \cdot r}{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $120\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
