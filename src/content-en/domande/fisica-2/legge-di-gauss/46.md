In a region of space the electric field is $\vec{E} = c\,y\,\hat{\imath}$, with $c = 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/(C \cdot m)}$. A square of side $a = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ lies in the plane $x = 0$, with $0 \leq y \leq a$ and $0 \leq z \leq a$, and is oriented with normal $\hat{n} = \hat{\imath}$. What is the flux of the field through the square?

- $54\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $18\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $27\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $90\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$

## Explanation

On the square $\vec{E} \cdot \hat{n} = c\,y$, which depends on $y$, so the flux is computed by integrating over the surface:
$$
\Phi_E = \int_0^a \!\! \int_0^a c\,y\,dy\,dz = c\,a \cdot \frac{a^2}{2} = \frac{c\,a^3}{2} .
$$
With the data, $\Phi_E = \dfrac{2.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 0.027}{2}\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} = 27\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$.

The value $c\,a^3 = 54\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$ would be obtained by using over the whole square the field on the side $y = a$, where the field is largest.
