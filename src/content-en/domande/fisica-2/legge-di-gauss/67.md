Two infinite parallel planes are uniformly charged with the same surface density $+\sigma$. Which statement about the electric field is true?

- Between the planes the field has magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ and outside it is zero
- Between the planes the field is zero and outside it has magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, pointing away from the planes
- The field has magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ in all three regions
- Between the planes the field has magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ and outside it has magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$

## Explanation

Each plane produces in each half-space a uniform field of magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$, pointing away from the plane.

Between the planes the two fields have the same direction and opposite senses, because each points away from its own plane; by the **superposition principle** their sum is **zero**.

In each outer region the two fields both point away from the planes, so the magnitudes add and the field equals $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$. The situation is the opposite of that of the plates of a capacitor, which have charges of opposite sign.
