Inside a uniformly charged insulating sphere, the field is initially measured at $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center. All points considered are inside the material. Given the new distance from the center $r = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the field magnitude at the new point $E = 180\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the initial field magnitude $E_{0}$?

- $60\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $540\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $120\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

The enclosed charge increases with $r^3$ and the Gaussian surface area with $r^2$. Their ratio, and therefore $E$, increases linearly with $r$. In the indicated units, $E = \dfrac{E_{0} \cdot r}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $E_{0} = \dfrac{2 \cdot E}{r}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $60\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
