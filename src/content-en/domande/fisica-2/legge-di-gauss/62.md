An insulating sphere of radius $R$ has the charge $Q > 0$ uniformly distributed in its volume and is crossed by a thin diametral tunnel. A particle of mass $m$ and charge $-q$, with $q > 0$, is released from rest at a point of the tunnel inside the sphere, other than the center. Neglecting gravity, what is the period of its motion?

- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{qQ}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m R^3}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m R^2}{qQ}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m R^3}{qQ}}$
- $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2\pi\varepsilon_0 m R^3}{qQ}}$

## Explanation

Inside the sphere the field is radial, and along the tunnel it has component $\dfrac{Q\,x}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3}$, where $x$ is the position measured from the center.

The force on the particle is therefore $F_x = -\dfrac{qQ}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3}\,x$, proportional to the displacement and directed toward the center. The equation $m\ddot{x} = F_x$ is that of a **harmonic oscillator** with
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{qQ}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m R^3} ,
$$
therefore the period is $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m R^3}{qQ}}$. The period does not depend on the starting point, because the particle always stays inside the sphere.
