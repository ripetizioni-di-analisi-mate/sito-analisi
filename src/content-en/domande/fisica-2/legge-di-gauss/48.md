In a region the electric field is $\vec{E} = (E_0 + b\,z)\,\hat{k}$, with $E_0 = 1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and $b = 5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/(C \cdot m)}$. A box shaped as a rectangular prism has a square base of side $a = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ in the plane $z = 0$ and height $h = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ along the $z$-axis. What is the charge enclosed by the box?

- $0$
- $0.18\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $0.53\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $0.35\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The field is parallel to the $z$-axis, so the flux through the four lateral faces is zero.

On the base $z = 0$ the outward normal is $-\hat{k}$ and the flux is $-E_0 a^2$. On the top face $z = h$ the outward normal is $\hat{k}$ and the flux is $(E_0 + b h)\,a^2$. The total outward flux is therefore
$$
\Phi_E = b\,h\,a^2 = 5.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} = 20\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} .
$$
By **Gauss's law** $q_{\text{int}} = \varepsilon_0 \Phi_E \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{C} = 0.18\,\mathrm{nC}$. The uniform part $E_0$ of the field gives opposite fluxes through the two bases and does not contribute to the charge.
