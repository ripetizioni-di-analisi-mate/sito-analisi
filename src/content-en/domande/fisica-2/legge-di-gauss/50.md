A point charge $q = 4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at distance $d = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from an uncharged infinite plane. What is the flux of the field of the charge through the plane, oriented with the normal pointing away from the side of the charge?

- $4.5 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $2.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Every ray leaving the charge toward the plane meets it once, so the plane is seen from the charge under the solid angle $2\pi$, half of the total solid angle $4\pi$.

The contribution to the flux of each element of the plane is $\dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,d\Omega$, therefore
$$
\Phi_E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \cdot 2\pi = \frac{q}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} .
$$
The result does not depend on the distance $d$. The value $\dfrac{q}{\varepsilon_0}$ is the outward flux through a closed surface enclosing the charge.
