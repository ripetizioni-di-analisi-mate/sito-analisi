Two infinite parallel straight wires, a distance $d = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart, are uniformly charged with the same density $\lambda = 3.0\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. What is the magnitude of the force acting on a segment of length $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ of one of the two wires?

- $3.2 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By **Gauss's law** applied to a cylinder coaxial with one of the wires, that wire produces at the points of the other wire a field perpendicular to the wires with magnitude
$$
E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 d} = \frac{2k\lambda}{d} .
$$
The field is the same at all points of the other wire, so a segment of length $L$, which carries the charge $\lambda L$, is acted on by the force $F = \lambda L\,E$. Therefore
$$
F = \frac{2k\lambda^2 L}{d} = \frac{2 \cdot 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot (3.0 \cdot 10^{-9})^2 \cdot 1.0}{0.050}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 3.2 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The force is repulsive, because the charges have the same sign. The value $6.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$ would be obtained by dividing by $d^2$, as for two point charges.
