A hemispherical surface of radius $R$, without its base, is in a uniform electric field of magnitude $E$, perpendicular to the symmetry axis of the hemisphere. What is the flux of the field through the hemispherical surface, oriented with the normal pointing toward the convex side?

- $\pi R^2 E$
- $0$
- $2\pi R^2 E$
- $\dfrac{\pi R^2 E}{2}$

## Explanation

The hemisphere is closed with the base disk. The closed surface obtained contains no charge, so by **Gauss's law** the total outward flux is zero.

The field is perpendicular to the axis of the hemisphere, so it is parallel to the base disk, and the flux through the disk is zero. It follows that the flux through the hemispherical surface is also **zero**.

The field enters through one half of the hemisphere and leaves through the other, and the two contributions add up to zero. The value $\pi R^2 E$ is the flux when the field is parallel to the axis.
