A thin charged layer lies in the plane $x = 0$, in vacuum. Just to the right of the layer the component $E_x$ of the electric field is $+5\,\mathrm{N/C}$, just to the left it is $-3\,\mathrm{N/C}$. What is $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, where $\sigma$ is the surface charge density of the layer?

- $2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $4\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $-8\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $8\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

**Gauss's law** is applied to a small cylinder with its bases of area $A$ parallel to the layer, one just to the right and one just to the left, and with negligible height.

The flux through the lateral surface is negligible. On the right base the outward normal is $+\hat{\imath}$ and on the left base it is $-\hat{\imath}$, therefore the outward flux is $\Phi_E = 5A - (-3)A = 8A$, in $\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}$.

The charge enclosed in the cylinder is $\sigma A$, so $8A = \dfrac{\sigma A}{\varepsilon_0}$ and $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = 8\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The value $2\,\mathrm{N/C}$ is obtained by adding the two components instead of subtracting them.
