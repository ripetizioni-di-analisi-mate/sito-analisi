Inside a uniformly charged insulating sphere, the field is initially measured at $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center. All points considered are inside the material. Initially, the given quantities are the initial field magnitude $E_{0} = 60\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the new distance from the center $r = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the new distance from the center to $3\,\mathrm{cm}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $E$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$

## Explanation

The enclosed charge increases with $r^3$ and the Gaussian surface area with $r^2$. Their ratio, and therefore $E$, increases linearly with $r$. Applying $E = \dfrac{E_{0} \cdot r}{2}$ gives $180\,\mathrm{N/C}$ before the change and $90\,\mathrm{N/C}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
