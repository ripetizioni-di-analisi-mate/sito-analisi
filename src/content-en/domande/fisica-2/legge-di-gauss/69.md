A uniform electric field $\vec{E}_0 = E_0\,\hat{\imath}$, with $E_0 = 600\,\mathrm{N/C}$, is produced by distant charges. The infinite plane $x = 0$ is added, charged with uniform surface density $\sigma$. Which value of $\sigma$ makes the total field zero in the region $x < 0$?

::grafico{unicolore=1 assi=0 linee="0,-1.6 0,1.6" vettori="-2.6,0.8,-1.2,0.8,E₀;-2.6,-0.8,-1.2,-0.8;1.2,0.8,2.6,0.8;1.2,-0.8,2.6,-0.8" etichette="0.15,1.45,σ;-2.2,-1.35,x < 0;1.6,-1.35,x > 0" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1.7 ymax=1.7 titolo="The plane $x = 0$, seen edge-on, in the uniform field $\vec{E}_0$ produced by distant charges"}

- $-1.1 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $+5.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $+1.1 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $+2.1 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$

## Explanation

The plane produces in each half-space a uniform field of magnitude $\dfrac{|\sigma|}{2\varepsilon_0}$. For $\sigma > 0$ the field of the plane points away from the plane, so in the region $x < 0$ it is directed along $-\hat{\imath}$, opposite to $\vec{E}_0$.

The total field in the region $x < 0$ is zero if $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = E_0$, that is
$$
\sigma = 2\varepsilon_0 E_0 = 2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 600\,\mathrm{C/m^2} \approx +1.1 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} .
$$
In the region $x > 0$ the two fields point the same way, and the total field is $2E_0$. With $\sigma < 0$ the field of the plane would reinforce $\vec{E}_0$ in the region $x < 0$.
