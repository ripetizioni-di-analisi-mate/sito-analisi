An oil drop of mass $m = 3.2 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and charge $q = +8.0 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$ stays at rest above a large, thin, horizontal sheet that is uniformly charged, far from its edges. What is the surface charge density of the sheet? Use $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $3.5 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $1.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $7.1 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$
- $6.9 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$

## Explanation

Above the sheet the field is perpendicular to the sheet and has magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$; with $\sigma > 0$ it points upward.

The drop is in equilibrium, so the electric force $q\,\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$, pointing upward, balances the weight $mg$. Therefore
$$
\sigma = \frac{2\varepsilon_0 m g}{q} = \frac{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 3.2 \cdot 10^{-15} \cdot 9.81}{8.0 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} \approx 6.9 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} .
$$
The value $3.5 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$ corresponds to the field $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, which holds between two plates with opposite charges and not near an isolated sheet.
