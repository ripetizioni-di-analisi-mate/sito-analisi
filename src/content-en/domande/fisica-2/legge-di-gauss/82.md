A Gaussian cylinder coaxial with a uniformly charged wire has length $L$. The wire crosses both bases. Consider the total outward flux. Given the linear charge density of the wire $\lambda = 12\,\mathrm{nC/m}$ and the length of the cylinder $L = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the flux multiplied by $\varepsilon_0$ $q$?

- $9\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $6\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $12\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $3\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The enclosed charge is the linear charge density multiplied by the length of wire inside the cylinder. Gauss's law identifies this charge with $\varepsilon_0\Phi$. In the indicated units, $q = L \cdot \lambda$. Using the values given in the problem gives $6\,\mathrm{nC}$.
