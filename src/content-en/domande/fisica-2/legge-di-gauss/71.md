In a region the electric field is $\vec{E} = E_0 \sin(kx)\,\hat{\imath}$, with $E_0 = 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and $k = 5.0\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. What is the charge density on the plane $x = 0$?

- $0$
- $8.9 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$
- $4.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$

## Explanation

The field has only the component $E_x = E_0 \sin(kx)$, so $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \dfrac{\partial E_x}{\partial x} = E_0 k \cos(kx)$.

By the **local form of Gauss's law** $\rho = \varepsilon_0 E_0 k \cos(kx)$, which at $x = 0$ equals
$$
\rho = \varepsilon_0 E_0 k = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} .
$$
At $x = 0$ the field is zero, while its derivative has its maximum value; the density depends on the derivative, therefore it is nonzero.
