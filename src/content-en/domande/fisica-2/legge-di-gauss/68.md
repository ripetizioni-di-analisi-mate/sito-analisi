Two very large parallel plates, at distance $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, are uniformly charged with densities $+\sigma$ and $-\sigma$, with $\sigma = 8.85\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$. An electron starts from rest near the negative plate. Neglecting its weight, with what speed does it reach the positive plate?

- $1.9 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Between the plates the field is uniform and has magnitude $E = \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \dfrac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C} = 1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$.

The electron has negative charge, so the force of magnitude $eE$ pushes it toward the positive plate with constant acceleration $a = \dfrac{eE}{m_e}$. In uniformly accelerated motion starting from rest $v^2 = 2ad$, therefore
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2eEd}{m_e}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 0.010}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.9 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Using the field $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ of a single plate would give $1.3 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
