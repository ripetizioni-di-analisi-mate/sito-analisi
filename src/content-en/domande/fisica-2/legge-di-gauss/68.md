Inside a uniformly charged insulating sphere, the field is initially measured at $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center. All points considered are inside the material. Given the initial field magnitude $E_{0} = 60\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the field magnitude at the new point $E = 180\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the new distance from the center $r$?

- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2 \cdot \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The enclosed charge increases with $r^3$ and the Gaussian surface area with $r^2$. Their ratio, and therefore $E$, increases linearly with $r$. In the indicated units, $E = \dfrac{E_{0} \cdot r}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $r = \dfrac{2 \cdot E}{E_{0}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.
