A solid cube of edge $a$ has the charge $Q$ uniformly distributed in its volume. Which statement is true?

- The field on the surface of the cube is perpendicular to the faces and has magnitude $\dfrac{Q}{6\varepsilon_0 a^2}$ at every point
- At every point outside the cube the field coincides with that of a point charge $Q$ placed at the center
- The outward flux through the sphere inscribed in the cube is $\dfrac{Q}{\varepsilon_0}$
- The outward flux through each face of the cube is $\dfrac{Q}{6\varepsilon_0}$

## Explanation

By **Gauss's law** the outward flux through the whole surface of the cube is $\dfrac{Q}{\varepsilon_0}$. The six faces are equivalent by the symmetry of the cube, so each one is crossed by the flux $\dfrac{Q}{6\varepsilon_0}$.

Cubic symmetry, on the other hand, does not allow the field to be taken out of the integral, because on a face the field is not perpendicular to the surface and its magnitude changes from point to point. For the same reason the outside field coincides with that of a point charge only far from the cube.

The inscribed sphere encloses the volume $\dfrac{\pi a^3}{6}$ and hence the charge $\dfrac{\pi}{6}\,Q$, therefore its flux is $\dfrac{\pi Q}{6\varepsilon_0}$.
