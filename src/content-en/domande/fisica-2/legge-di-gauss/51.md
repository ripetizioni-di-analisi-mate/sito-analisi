A closed surface contains two charges. The first is positive and the second negative, with different magnitudes. There are other charges outside. Given the magnitude of the enclosed positive charge $q_{1} = 9\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the magnitude of the enclosed negative charge $q_{2} = 4\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the flux multiplied by $\varepsilon_0$ $\Phi$?

- $5\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $13\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $9\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $10\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

By Gauss's law, the outward flux multiplied by $\varepsilon_0$ is the net enclosed charge. The external charges contribute zero to the net flux. In the indicated units, $\Phi = q_{1} - q_{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $5\,\mathrm{nC}$.
