A very long straight wire has uniform charge. Far from the ends, the field at a distance of $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ has magnitude $E_0$. Given the field magnitude at $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ $E_{0} = 120\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the field magnitude at the second point $E = 40\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the distance of the second point from the wire $r$?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2 \cdot \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

For a coaxial Gaussian cylinder, the enclosed charge per unit length is fixed. The lateral area increases linearly with $r$, so $E$ decreases as $1/r$. In the indicated units, $E = \dfrac{2 \cdot E_{0}}{r}$. Solving for the unknown gives $r = \dfrac{2 \cdot E_{0}}{E}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.
