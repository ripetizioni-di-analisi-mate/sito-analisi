A thin spherical shell is uniformly charged with surface density $\sigma > 0$. A very small circular hole is cut in the shell. What is the magnitude of the electric field at the center of the hole?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{\sigma}{4\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$

## Explanation

The complete shell is split into the small disk that fills the hole and the rest of the shell. Near the disk, the disk produces a field like that of a plane, of magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ and pointing away from it on both sides. The rest of the shell produces in the region of the hole a field $\vec{E}_r$ directed along the normal, which does not change across the disk.

Just inside the complete shell the field is zero, so $E_r - \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 0$, that is $E_r = \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$. Just outside, the field equals $E_r + \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, in agreement with the **discontinuity** $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ of the normal component.

Removing the disk leaves only $\vec{E}_r$, therefore the field in the hole has magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ and points outward.
