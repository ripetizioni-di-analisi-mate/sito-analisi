An infinite straight wire is uniformly charged. Which transformation, leaving the charge distribution unchanged, allows one to conclude that at a point $P$ the field has no component parallel to the wire?

- The reflection across the plane perpendicular to the wire through $P$
- The rotation about the wire
- The translation along the wire
- The reflection across the plane containing the wire and the point $P$

## Explanation

The reflection across the plane perpendicular to the wire through $P$ leaves the point $P$ and the distribution fixed, and reverses the component of the field parallel to the wire. The field at $P$ must stay the same, so that component equals its opposite, that is, it is **zero**.

The rotation about the wire leaves the parallel component unchanged, and the translation along the wire shows only that the field does not depend on the position along the wire; therefore neither of the two rules out that component.

The reflection across the plane containing the wire and $P$ rules out instead the component tangent to the circles around the wire.
