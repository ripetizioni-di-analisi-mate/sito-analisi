A solid insulating sphere has radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and uniformly distributed positive charge. A concentric Gaussian sphere is drawn inside the material. Given the radius of the Gaussian sphere $r = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the enclosed charge $q = 10\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the total charge of the sphere $Q$?

- $160\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $40\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $80\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $20\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The volume charge density is uniform. The enclosed fraction equals the ratio of the volumes, which scales with the cube of the radii. In the indicated units, $q = \dfrac{Q \cdot r^{3}}{1000}$. Solving for the unknown gives $Q = \dfrac{1000 \cdot q}{r^{3}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $80\,\mathrm{nC}$.
