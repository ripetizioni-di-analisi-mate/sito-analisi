A solid insulating sphere has radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and uniformly distributed positive charge. A concentric Gaussian sphere is drawn inside the material. Initially, the given quantities are the total charge of the sphere $Q = 80\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the radius of the Gaussian sphere $r = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the total charge of the sphere to $160\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the radius of the Gaussian sphere to $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $q$?

- $- \dfrac{68}{5}\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-8\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $\dfrac{218}{25}\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $- \dfrac{218}{25}\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The volume charge density is uniform. The enclosed fraction equals the ratio of the volumes, which scales with the cube of the radii. Applying $q = \dfrac{Q \cdot r^{3}}{1000}$ gives $10\,\mathrm{nC}$ before the change and $\dfrac{32}{25}\,\mathrm{nC}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $- \dfrac{218}{25}\,\mathrm{nC}$.
