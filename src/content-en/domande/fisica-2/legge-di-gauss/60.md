Two thin concentric spherical shells have radii $a < b$. The inner shell carries the charge $+Q$ and the outer shell the charge $-Q$, both uniformly distributed. Which description of the field magnitude $E(r)$, with $r$ the distance from the center and $k = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$, is correct?

- It is zero for $r < a$, equals $\dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$ for $a < r < b$ and equals $\dfrac{2kQ}{r^2}$ for $r > b$
- It is zero for $r < a$, equals $\dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$ for $a < r < b$ and is zero for $r > b$
- It equals $\dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$ for $r < a$ and is zero for $r > a$
- It is zero for $r < a$ and for $r > b$, and is constant for $a < r < b$

## Explanation

The distribution has spherical symmetry, so in each region $E(r) = \dfrac{k\,q_{\text{int}}}{r^2}$, where $q_{\text{int}}$ is the charge enclosed by the Gaussian sphere of radius $r$.

For $r < a$ the Gaussian sphere encloses no charge, so the field is zero. For $a < r < b$ it encloses only the inner shell, therefore $E = \dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$. For $r > b$ it encloses both shells, with total charge $Q - Q = 0$, and the field is **zero** again.

The field is therefore nonzero only between the two shells, where it decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$.
