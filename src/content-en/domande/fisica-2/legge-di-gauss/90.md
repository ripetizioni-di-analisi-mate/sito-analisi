A point charge $q = 1.77\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at the center of a sphere of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$, placed in a uniform electric field $\vec{E}_0$ of magnitude $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$ produced by external charges. The sphere is divided into two hemispheres by the plane through its center perpendicular to $\vec{E}_0$. What is the outward flux of the total field through the hemisphere from which the field $\vec{E}_0$ exits?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $37\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$

## Explanation

By the **superposition principle** the flux of the total field is the sum of the flux of the field of the charge and the flux of the uniform field.

The charge is at the center, so its field crosses each hemisphere with half of the total flux, that is with $\dfrac{q}{2\varepsilon_0} = \dfrac{1.77 \cdot 10^{-9}}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} = 100\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$.

The uniform field is produced by charges outside the sphere, so its outward flux through the closed surface formed by the hemisphere and the diametral disk is zero. The uniform field enters the disk perpendicularly, with outward flux $-\pi R^2 E_0$, therefore the outward flux of $\vec{E}_0$ through the hemisphere is $\pi R^2 E_0 = \pi \cdot 0.010 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} \approx 63\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$.

The total flux is thus $100 + 63 \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$. The value $37\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$ corresponds to the opposite hemisphere, into which the uniform field enters.
