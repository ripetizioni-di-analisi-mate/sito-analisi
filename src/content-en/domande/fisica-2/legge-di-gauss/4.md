Two closed surfaces $S_1$ and $S_2$ bound two adjacent regions that share a face. The outward fluxes of the electric field are $+7\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}$ through $S_1$ and $-2\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}$ through $S_2$. What is the outward flux through the closed surface that bounds the union of the two regions?

- $9\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}$
- $-5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}$
- $7\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}$
- $5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}$

## Explanation

The flux through $S_1$ is the sum of the flux through the shared face and the flux through the rest of $S_1$, and the same holds for $S_2$.

On the shared face the outward normal of $S_1$ is opposite to the outward normal of $S_2$, so the two fluxes through that face are opposite. Adding the fluxes of $S_1$ and $S_2$, the contributions of the shared face therefore cancel, and what remains is the flux through the outer boundary of the union.

The required flux is therefore $7 + (-2) = 5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}$. The same result follows from Gauss's law, because the charge enclosed by the union is the sum of the charges enclosed by $S_1$ and by $S_2$.
