A thin, very long cylindrical surface of radius $R = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is uniformly charged with surface density $\sigma = 5.0\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$. What is the magnitude of the electric field $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis, far from the ends?

- $2.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $5.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $2.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $90\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

A piece of surface of length $h$ has area $2\pi R h$, so the charge per unit length is $\lambda = 2\pi R\,\sigma$.

For $r > R$ the coaxial Gaussian cylinder of radius $r$ and height $h$ encloses the charge $\lambda h$, and **Gauss's law** gives the field of the infinite wire
$$
E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r} = \frac{\sigma R}{\varepsilon_0 r} = \frac{5.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 0.020}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} \approx 5.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$ is the field just outside the surface, at $r = R$.
