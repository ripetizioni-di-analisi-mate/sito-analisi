Two infinite planes perpendicular to each other intersect along a line, and each is uniformly charged with density $\sigma = 3.0\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$. What is the magnitude of the electric field at a point $P$ not on the planes?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,0 2,0;0,-1.6 0,1.6" punti="1.2,0.8,pieno,P" etichette="1.75,0.18,σ;0.15,1.45,σ" xmin=-2.2 xmax=2.2 ymin=-1.7 ymax=1.7 titolo="The two charged planes seen edge-on, perpendicular to the page"}

- $2.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $3.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Each plane produces a field perpendicular to itself, of magnitude
$$
\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C} ,
$$
the same at all points of the two half-spaces.

The two planes are perpendicular, so at $P$ the two fields are perpendicular to each other. By the **superposition principle** the magnitude of the total field is $\sqrt{2}\,\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$, the same in all four regions. The value $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} \approx 3.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$ would be obtained by adding the magnitudes as if the fields were parallel.
