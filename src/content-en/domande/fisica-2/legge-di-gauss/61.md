An infinite insulating slab occupies the region $-\dfrac{d}{2} \leq x \leq \dfrac{d}{2}$ and is charged with uniform density $\rho > 0$. On its midplane $x = 0$ lies a charged plane with uniform surface density $\sigma = -\rho d$. What is the component $E_x$ of the field at the point with $x = \dfrac{d}{4}$?

- $\dfrac{\rho d}{4\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{3\rho d}{4\varepsilon_0}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{\rho d}{4\varepsilon_0}$

## Explanation

By the **superposition principle** the field is the sum of the field of the slab and the field of the plane.

Inside the slab the field of the slab has component $\dfrac{\rho x}{\varepsilon_0}$, which at $x = \dfrac{d}{4}$ equals $\dfrac{\rho d}{4\varepsilon_0}$.

The plane has negative charge, so for $x > 0$ its field points toward the plane, with component $-\dfrac{|\sigma|}{2\varepsilon_0} = -\dfrac{\rho d}{2\varepsilon_0}$.

Therefore $E_x = \dfrac{\rho d}{4\varepsilon_0} - \dfrac{\rho d}{2\varepsilon_0} = -\dfrac{\rho d}{4\varepsilon_0}$. The system has zero total charge per unit area, and the field is zero only outside the slab.
