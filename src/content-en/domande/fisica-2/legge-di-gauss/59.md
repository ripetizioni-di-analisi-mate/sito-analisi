A solid insulating sphere has radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and uniformly distributed positive charge. A concentric Gaussian sphere is drawn inside the material. Initially, the given quantities are the total charge of the sphere $Q = 80\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the radius of the Gaussian sphere $r = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the radius of the Gaussian sphere to $\dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $q$?

- $\dfrac{1}{8}$
- $8$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The volume charge density is uniform. The enclosed fraction equals the ratio of the volumes, which scales with the cube of the radii. Applying $q = \dfrac{Q \cdot r^{3}}{1000}$ gives $10\,\mathrm{nC}$ before the change and $\dfrac{5}{4}\,\mathrm{nC}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{8}$.
