In a simplified model of an atom, the nucleus is a point charge $+q$ at the center of a spherical cloud of radius $R$ carrying the charge $-q$ uniformly distributed in its volume. What is the magnitude of the electric field at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center? Let $k = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$.

- $\dfrac{4kq}{R^2}$
- $\dfrac{kq}{2R^2}$
- $\dfrac{7kq}{2R^2}$
- $\dfrac{9kq}{2R^2}$

## Explanation

The distribution has spherical symmetry, so the field is radial and **Gauss's law** gives $E(r) = \dfrac{k\,q_{\text{int}}(r)}{r^2}$.

The Gaussian sphere of radius $r < R$ encloses the nucleus and the fraction $\dfrac{r^3}{R^3}$ of the cloud, so $q_{\text{int}} = q\left(1 - \dfrac{r^3}{R^3}\right)$, which for $r = \dfrac{R}{2}$ equals $\dfrac{7}{8}\,q$. Therefore
$$
E = \frac{k \cdot \frac{7}{8}\,q}{\frac{R^2}{4}} = \frac{7kq}{2R^2} ,
$$
pointing away from the center. The value $\dfrac{4kq}{R^2}$ is the field of the nucleus alone, without the contribution of the cloud.
