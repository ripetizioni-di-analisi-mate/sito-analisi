A Gaussian cylinder coaxial with a uniformly charged wire has length $L$. The wire crosses both bases. Consider the total outward flux. Given the linear charge density of the wire $\lambda = 12\,\mathrm{nC/m}$ and the flux multiplied by $\varepsilon_0$ $q = 6\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the length of the cylinder $L$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The enclosed charge is the linear charge density multiplied by the length of wire inside the cylinder. Gauss's law identifies this charge with $\varepsilon_0\Phi$. In the indicated units, $q = L \cdot \lambda$. Solving for the unknown gives $L = \dfrac{q}{\lambda}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$.
