A closed surface contains two charges. The first is positive and the second negative, with different magnitudes. There are other charges outside. Initially, the given quantities are the magnitude of the enclosed positive charge $q_{1} = 9\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the magnitude of the enclosed negative charge $q_{2} = 4\,\mathrm{nC}$. In the second configuration, we change the magnitude of the enclosed positive charge to $11\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the magnitude of the enclosed negative charge to $7\,\mathrm{nC}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $\Phi$?

- $-1\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $2\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $1\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $5\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

By Gauss's law, the outward flux multiplied by $\varepsilon_0$ is the net enclosed charge. The external charges contribute zero to the net flux. Applying $\Phi = q_{1} - q_{2}$ gives $5\,\mathrm{nC}$ before the change and $4\,\mathrm{nC}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $-1\,\mathrm{nC}$.
