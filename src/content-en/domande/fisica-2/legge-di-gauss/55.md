A cube contains the charge $q = 3.54\,\mathrm{nC}$. The outward fluxes of the electric field through five of its faces are $120$, $120$, $80$, $80$ and $-40\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$. What is the outward flux through the sixth face?

- $67\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $-40\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $40\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $3.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$

## Explanation

By **Gauss's law** the outward flux through the whole cube is $\dfrac{q}{\varepsilon_0} = \dfrac{3.54 \cdot 10^{-9}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} = 400\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$.

The total flux is the sum of the fluxes through the six faces, and the five known faces give $120 + 120 + 80 + 80 - 40 = 360\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$. Therefore the outward flux through the sixth face is $400 - 360 = 40\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$.

The fluxes through the faces differ from each other, so the charge is not at the center of the cube, and the division into six equal parts $\dfrac{q}{6\varepsilon_0}$ does not hold.
