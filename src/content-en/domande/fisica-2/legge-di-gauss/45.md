A charge lies exactly on the ideal Gaussian surface. Which precaution allows Gauss's law to be used in the usual way without ambiguity?

- Choose a surface that does not pass through the charge
- Always count it twice
- Always consider it external without taking limits
- Replace the charge with zero

## Explanation

The point-charge field is singular at the charge. The usual formulation requires a well-defined integral over the surface. A small deformation avoids the singularity.
