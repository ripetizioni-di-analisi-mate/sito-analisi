A uniform electric field passes through a plane surface. The angle between the oriented normal and the field is $60^\circ$. Initially, the given quantities are the electric field magnitude $E = 180\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the area $A = \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. In the second configuration, we change the area to $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $\Phi$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

The dot product with the area vector contains the cosine of the angle to the normal. Here, this cosine equals $1/2$. Applying $\Phi = \dfrac{A \cdot E}{2}$ gives $18\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$ before the change and $36\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$ afterward. The ratio is $2$.
