A cylinder of radius $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and length $L = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ is in a uniform electric field of magnitude $E = 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$, perpendicular to its axis. What is the flux of the field through the half of the lateral surface from which the field leaves, oriented with the normal pointing out of the cylinder?

::grafico{unicolore=1 assi=0 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" legenda=0 linee="0,-1 0,1" vettori="-2.7,0.5,-1.5,0.5,E;-2.7,-0.5,-1.5,-0.5" segmenti="0,0,0.866,-0.5" etichette="0.55,-0.05,r" xmin=-2.9 xmax=2.2 ymin=-1.4 ymax=1.4 titolo="Cross section of the cylinder perpendicular to the axis; the uniform field $\vec{E}$ is perpendicular to the axis and the vertical diameter divides the lateral surface into two halves"}

- $63\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $0$
- $40\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$

## Explanation

The half of the lateral surface is closed with the diametral rectangle of sides $2r$ and $L$ and with the two half bases. The closed surface obtained contains no charge, so by **Gauss's law** the total outward flux is zero.

The half bases are parallel to the field, therefore their flux is zero. The field is perpendicular to the diametral rectangle and enters through it, so the outward flux through the rectangle is $-E \cdot 2rL$.

It follows that the flux through the half of the lateral surface is
$$
\Phi_E = 2\,r L E = 2 \cdot 0.050 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} = 40\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} .
$$
The value $63\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$ would be obtained by multiplying the field by the area $\pi r L$ of the half surface, without taking into account the tilt of the normal.
