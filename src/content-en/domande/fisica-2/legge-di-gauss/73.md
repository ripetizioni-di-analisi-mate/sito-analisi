Two parallel plane plates of area $A = 0.050\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart, carry the charges $+Q$ and $-Q$ uniformly distributed, with $Q = 20\,\mathrm{nC}$. Neglecting edge effects, what is the magnitude of the force with which the plates attract each other?

- $4.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The charge of one plate is acted on by the field produced by the other plate, because the field that a charged plane produces on itself exerts no net force on it.

The negative plate has density $\sigma = \dfrac{Q}{A}$ and produces, like an infinite plane, a uniform field of magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$. Therefore the force on the positive plate is
$$
F = Q\,\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q^2}{2\varepsilon_0 A} = \frac{(20 \cdot 10^{-9})^2}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 4.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
and it does not depend on the distance between the plates. The value $9.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N}$ would be obtained with the field $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ between the plates, which also includes the contribution of the plate on which the force acts. The value $0.90\,\mathrm{N}$ is the force between two point charges $\pm Q$ at a distance of $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$.
