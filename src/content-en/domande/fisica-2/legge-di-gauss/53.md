An infinite straight wire is uniformly charged with density $\lambda = 3.0\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. A sphere of radius $r = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ has its center at a point of the wire. What is the outward flux of the electric field through the sphere?

- $51\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$

## Explanation

The center of the sphere is on the wire, so the wire crosses the sphere along a diameter and the enclosed piece of wire has length $2r$. The enclosed charge is therefore $q_{\text{int}} = 2\lambda r = 2 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 0.15\,\mathrm{C} = 0.90\,\mathrm{nC}$.

By **Gauss's law**
$$
\Phi_E = \frac{2\lambda r}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{0.90 \cdot 10^{-9}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} \approx 1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} .
$$
The computation does not require the field on the sphere, which is not perpendicular to the surface and does not have constant magnitude.
