An infinite plane is uniformly charged with density $\sigma = 2.0\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$. A square of side $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is parallel to the plane, $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from it, and is oriented with the normal pointing away from the plane. What is the flux of the field through the square?

- $9.0\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $2.3\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$
- $23\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$

## Explanation

The field of the plane is perpendicular to the plane, uniform in each half-space and of magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$. For $\sigma > 0$ it points away from the plane, so it is parallel to the normal of the square and has the same sense.

Therefore
$$
\Phi_E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\,A = \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 0.040}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} \approx 4.5\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} .
$$
The distance from the plane does not appear, because the field of the plane does not depend on the distance. The value $9.0\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$ would be obtained with the field $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, which holds between two planes with opposite charges.
