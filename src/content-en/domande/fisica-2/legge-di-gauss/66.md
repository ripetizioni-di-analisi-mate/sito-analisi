Inside a uniformly charged insulating sphere, the field is initially measured at $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center. All points considered are inside the material. Given the initial field magnitude $E_{0} = 60\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the new distance from the center $r = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$, what is the field magnitude at the new point $E$?

- $20\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $180\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $360\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $540\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

The enclosed charge increases with $r^3$ and the Gaussian surface area with $r^2$. Their ratio, and therefore $E$, increases linearly with $r$. In the indicated units, $E = \dfrac{E_{0} \cdot r}{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $180\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
