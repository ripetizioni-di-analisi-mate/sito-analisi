A straight wire $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long is uniformly charged with density $\lambda$. At which point is the field well approximated by the expression $\dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$ of the infinite wire, where $r$ is the distance from the wire?

- At $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the midpoint of the wire, on the perpendicular to the wire
- At $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from one end, on the extension of the wire
- At $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the midpoint of the wire, on the perpendicular to the wire
- At $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the midpoint of the wire, on the perpendicular to the wire

## Explanation

The infinite-wire model describes the field at points whose distance from the wire is small compared with the distance from the ends, because there the distribution has approximately cylindrical symmetry.

At $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the midpoint the ends are about $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ away, so the condition is satisfied. At $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ the distance from the wire equals the distance from the ends, and the actual field is about $30\%$ smaller than $\dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$.

At $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ the wire looks almost like a point charge, and the field decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$. On the extension of the wire the field is parallel to the wire, and cylindrical symmetry does not hold.
