Two identical insulating spheres of radius $R$, uniformly charged in their volume with charges $+Q$ and $-Q$, touch at the point $P$. What is the magnitude of the electric field at $P$? Let $k = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$.

- $0$
- $\dfrac{2kQ}{R^2}$
- $\dfrac{kQ}{R^2}$
- $\dfrac{kQ}{2R^2}$

## Explanation

The point $P$ lies on the surface of both spheres, so it is at distance $R$ from each center, and each sphere produces at $P$ a field of magnitude $\dfrac{kQ}{R^2}$ directed along the line through the centers.

The field of the positive sphere points away from it, so at $P$ it points toward the negative sphere. The field of the negative sphere points into it, so at $P$ it also points toward the negative sphere.

By the **superposition principle** the two magnitudes add, therefore the field at $P$ equals $\dfrac{2kQ}{R^2}$. The value $\dfrac{kQ}{2R^2}$ would be obtained by using the distance $2R$ between the two centers instead of the distance $R$ from $P$ to each center.
