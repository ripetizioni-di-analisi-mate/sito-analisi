In a region of space the electric field is $\vec{E} = b\,(y\,\hat{\imath} + x\,\hat{\jmath})$, with $b$ a nonzero constant. Which statement about the charge density $\rho$ in the region is true?

- $\rho = 2\varepsilon_0 b$ at every point
- $\rho = \varepsilon_0 b\,(x + y)$
- $\rho$ is zero only on the coordinate axes
- $\rho$ is zero at every point, even though the field is nonzero

## Explanation

By the **local form of Gauss's law** $\rho = \varepsilon_0\,\nabla \cdot \vec{E}$.

The component $E_x = b\,y$ does not depend on $x$ and the component $E_y = b\,x$ does not depend on $y$, so $\dfrac{\partial E_x}{\partial x} = 0$ and $\dfrac{\partial E_y}{\partial y} = 0$. Therefore $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = 0$, and the charge density is **zero** at every point of the region.

The density depends on the derivatives of the field, and a nonzero field can have zero divergence. The value $2\varepsilon_0 b$ would be obtained for the field $b\,(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath})$, in which each component depends on its own coordinate.
