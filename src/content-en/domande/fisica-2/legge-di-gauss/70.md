Inside a uniformly charged insulating sphere, the field is initially measured at $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center. All points considered are inside the material. Initially, the given quantities are the initial field magnitude $E_{0} = 60\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the new distance from the center $r = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the initial field magnitude to $80\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the new distance from the center to $4\,\mathrm{cm}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $E$?

- $160\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $-20\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $-220\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

The enclosed charge increases with $r^3$ and the Gaussian surface area with $r^2$. Their ratio, and therefore $E$, increases linearly with $r$. Applying $E = \dfrac{E_{0} \cdot r}{2}$ gives $180\,\mathrm{N/C}$ before the change and $160\,\mathrm{N/C}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $-20\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
