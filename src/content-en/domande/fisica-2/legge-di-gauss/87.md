An insulating sphere with charge $Q$ uniformly distributed in its volume and a thin spherical shell with the same charge $Q$ uniformly distributed on its surface have the same radius $R$ and the same center, and are considered separately. In which region do the two fields have the same magnitude at every point?

- In all of space
- Only in the region $r < R$
- In the region $r > R$, and at no point with $0 < r < R$
- At no point

## Explanation

For $r > R$ the Gaussian sphere of radius $r$ encloses the charge $Q$ in both cases, and the symmetry is spherical, so **Gauss's law** gives for both $E = \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}$.

For $r < R$ the Gaussian sphere does not enclose the charge of the shell, therefore the field of the shell is zero. The solid sphere instead encloses the charge $Q\,\dfrac{r^3}{R^3}$ and has field $\dfrac{Q\,r}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3}$, nonzero for $0 < r < R$.

The two fields therefore coincide at all outside points and differ at all inside points other than the center.
