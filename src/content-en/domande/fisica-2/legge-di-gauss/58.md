A solid insulating sphere has radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and uniformly distributed positive charge. A concentric Gaussian sphere is drawn inside the material. Given the total charge of the sphere $Q = 80\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the enclosed charge $q = 10\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the radius of the Gaussian sphere $r$?

- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{5 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{5}{4}\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The volume charge density is uniform. The enclosed fraction equals the ratio of the volumes, which scales with the cube of the radii. In the indicated units, $q = \dfrac{Q \cdot r^{3}}{1000}$. Solving for the unknown gives $r = \dfrac{10 \cdot \sqrt[3]{q}}{\sqrt[3]{Q}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $5\,\mathrm{cm}$.
