A hollow insulating sphere has inner radius $a$ and outer radius $b$ and is charged with uniform density $\rho$ in the region $a \leq r \leq b$, where $r$ is the distance from the center. What is the magnitude of the electric field for $a \leq r \leq b$?

- $\dfrac{\rho\, r}{3\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{\rho\,(r^3 - a^3)}{3\varepsilon_0 r^2}$
- $\dfrac{\rho\,(b^3 - a^3)}{3\varepsilon_0 r^2}$
- $\dfrac{\rho\,(r^2 - a^2)}{2\varepsilon_0 r}$

## Explanation

The distribution has spherical symmetry, so the field is radial and the outward flux through the concentric Gaussian sphere of radius $r$ is $E(r) \cdot 4\pi r^2$.

The Gaussian sphere encloses the material between the radii $a$ and $r$, with charge $q_{\text{int}} = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi\rho\,(r^3 - a^3)$. By **Gauss's law**
$$
E(r) \cdot 4\pi r^2 = \frac{4\pi\rho\,(r^3 - a^3)}{3\varepsilon_0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E(r) = \frac{\rho\,(r^3 - a^3)}{3\varepsilon_0 r^2} .
$$
The expression $\dfrac{\rho\, r}{3\varepsilon_0}$ holds for the solid sphere, which has charge also in the region $r < a$.
