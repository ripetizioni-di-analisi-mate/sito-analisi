An insulating sphere with center $O$ is charged with uniform density $\rho > 0$, and inside it there is an empty spherical cavity with center $O'$. Let $\vec{d}$ be the vector from $O$ to $O'$. Which statement about the electric field inside the cavity is true?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29;0.9+0.7*cos(t),0.4+0.7*sin(t),0,6.29" legenda=0 punti="0,0,pieno;0.9,0.4,pieno,O′" vettori="0,0,0.9,0.4" etichette="-0.22,-0.28,O;0.3,0.45,d;-1.3,1.1,ρ" xmin=-2.4 xmax=2.4 ymin=-2.3 ymax=2.3 titolo="Sphere charged with uniform density $\rho$ and empty spherical cavity with center $O'$"}

- The field is zero at every point of the cavity
- The field is uniform and equals $\dfrac{\rho}{3\varepsilon_0}\,\vec{d}$
- The field is radial with respect to $O'$ and grows with the distance from $O'$
- The field is uniform and equals $\dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}\,\vec{d}$

## Explanation

By the **superposition principle** the sphere with the cavity is equivalent to a solid sphere of density $\rho$ plus a sphere with center $O'$ and density $-\rho$ filling the cavity.

At a point $P$ of the cavity the solid sphere produces the field $\dfrac{\rho}{3\varepsilon_0}\,\vec{r}$, where $\vec{r}$ is the vector from $O$ to $P$, and the negative sphere produces the field $-\dfrac{\rho}{3\varepsilon_0}\,\vec{r}\,'$, where $\vec{r}\,'$ is the vector from $O'$ to $P$.

The sum is $\dfrac{\rho}{3\varepsilon_0}\,(\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,')$, and $\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,' = \vec{d}$ for every point $P$. Therefore the field in the cavity is **uniform**, equals $\dfrac{\rho}{3\varepsilon_0}\,\vec{d}$ and is parallel to the line through the two centers.
