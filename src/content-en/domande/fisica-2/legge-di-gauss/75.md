A very long insulating cylinder has radius $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and uniform volume charge density. The field is measured inside, far from the ends. The field at the surface has magnitude $E_s$. Initially, the given quantities are the field magnitude at the surface $E_{s} = 100\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the distance from the axis $r = 2\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the field magnitude at the surface to $150\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the distance from the axis to $1\,\mathrm{cm}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $E$?

- $-10\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $500\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $10\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $30\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

For an internal Gaussian cylinder, the enclosed charge grows as $r^2$ and the lateral area as $r$. Therefore, $E(r)/E_s=r/5$ with the distances in centimeters. Applying $E = \dfrac{E_{s} \cdot r}{5}$ gives $40\,\mathrm{N/C}$ before the change and $30\,\mathrm{N/C}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $-10\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
