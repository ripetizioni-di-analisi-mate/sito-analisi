A Gaussian cylinder coaxial with a uniformly charged wire has length $L$. The wire crosses both bases. Consider the total outward flux. Given the length of the cylinder $L = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$ and the flux multiplied by $\varepsilon_0$ $q = 6\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the linear charge density of the wire $\lambda$?

- $12\,\mathrm{nC/m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{nC/m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{nC/m}$
- $24\,\mathrm{nC/m}$

## Explanation

The enclosed charge is the linear charge density multiplied by the length of wire inside the cylinder. Gauss's law identifies this charge with $\varepsilon_0\Phi$. In the indicated units, $q = L \cdot \lambda$. Solving for the unknown gives $\lambda = \dfrac{q}{L}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $12\,\mathrm{nC/m}$.
