An infinite slab of thickness $d$, charged with uniform density $\rho$, has its midplane at $x = 0$. To find $E_x(x)$ inside, one uses a cylinder with axis parallel to the $x$-axis and bases of area $A$ at the positions $-x$ and $x$, with $0 < x < \dfrac{d}{2}$. Why is the outward flux through the cylinder written as $2E_x(x)\,A$?

- The field is uniform throughout the slab
- The reflection across the midplane gives $E_x(-x) = -E_x(x)$, so the outward fluxes through the two bases are equal
- The field is zero on the lateral surface of the cylinder
- The charge enclosed by the cylinder does not depend on $x$

## Explanation

On the base at $x$ the outward normal is $\hat{\imath}$ and the flux is $E_x(x)\,A$. On the base at $-x$ the outward normal is $-\hat{\imath}$ and the flux is $-E_x(-x)\,A$.

The slab is unchanged by the reflection across the midplane, so the field satisfies $E_x(-x) = -E_x(x)$, and the second flux also equals $E_x(x)\,A$.

On the lateral surface the field is parallel to the surface, therefore the lateral flux is zero even though the field is not. Inside the slab the field is not uniform, and the enclosed charge $2\rho x A$ depends on $x$.
