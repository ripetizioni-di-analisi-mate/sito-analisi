On a clear day the atmospheric electric field is vertical and points downward; its magnitude is $150\,\mathrm{N/C}$ at the ground and $100\,\mathrm{N/C}$ at an altitude of $1500\,\mathrm{m}$. Treating the ground as flat and the field as depending only on altitude, what is the average charge density of the air between the ground and that altitude?

- $-3.0 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$
- $+8.9 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$
- $+1.5 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$
- $+3.0 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$

## Explanation

As Gaussian surface take a vertical cylinder with bases of area $A$ at the ground and at altitude $h = 1500\,\mathrm{m}$. The field is vertical, so the flux through the lateral surface is zero.

On the top base the outward normal points up, opposite to the field, and the flux is $-100\,A$; on the bottom base the outward normal points down, in the direction of the field, and the flux is $+150\,A$, with $A$ in square meters and the fluxes in $\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C}$. The total flux is therefore $50\,A$.

By **Gauss's law** the enclosed charge $\rho\,A h$ equals $\varepsilon_0 \cdot 50\,A$, therefore
$$
\rho = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \cdot 50}{1500}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \approx +3.0 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} .
$$
The density is positive because the outward flux through the cylinder is positive.
