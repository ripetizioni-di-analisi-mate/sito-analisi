A very long insulating cylinder has radius $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and uniform volume charge density. The field is measured inside, far from the ends. The field at the surface has magnitude $E_s$. Initially, the given quantities are the field magnitude at the surface $E_{s} = 100\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the distance from the axis $r = 2\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the second configuration, we change the distance from the axis to $4\,\mathrm{cm}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $E$?

- $4$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

For an internal Gaussian cylinder, the enclosed charge grows as $r^2$ and the lateral area as $r$. Therefore, $E(r)/E_s=r/5$ with the distances in centimeters. Applying $E = \dfrac{E_{s} \cdot r}{5}$ gives $40\,\mathrm{N/C}$ before the change and $80\,\mathrm{N/C}$ afterward. The ratio is $2$.
