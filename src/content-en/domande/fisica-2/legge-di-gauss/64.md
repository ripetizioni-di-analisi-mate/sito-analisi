An infinite insulating cylinder of radius $R$ is charged with density $\rho(r) = \rho_0\,\dfrac{r}{R}$, where $r$ is the distance from the axis. What is the magnitude of the electric field inside the cylinder, at distance $r$ from the axis?

- $\dfrac{\rho_0 r^2}{2\varepsilon_0 R}$
- $\dfrac{\rho_0 r^2}{3\varepsilon_0 R}$
- $\dfrac{\rho_0 r^2}{4\varepsilon_0 R}$
- $\dfrac{\rho_0 r}{2\varepsilon_0}$

## Explanation

The charge contained in a coaxial cylinder of radius $r$ and height $h$ is obtained by adding the cylindrical shells of radius $s$ and thickness $ds$, of volume $2\pi s h\,ds$:
$$
q_{\text{int}} = \int_0^r \rho_0\,\frac{s}{R}\, 2\pi s h\, ds = \frac{2\pi\rho_0 h\, r^3}{3R} .
$$
The field is radial, so the outward flux through the Gaussian cylinder is $E(r) \cdot 2\pi r h$. By **Gauss's law** $E(r) \cdot 2\pi r h = \dfrac{2\pi\rho_0 h\, r^3}{3\varepsilon_0 R}$, therefore
$$
E(r) = \frac{\rho_0 r^2}{3\varepsilon_0 R} .
$$
The expression $\dfrac{\rho_0 r^2}{4\varepsilon_0 R}$ is the field of the same density in a sphere.
