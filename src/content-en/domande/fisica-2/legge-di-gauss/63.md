An infinite insulating cylinder of radius $a$ is charged with uniform density $\rho > 0$. It is surrounded by a thin coaxial cylindrical surface of radius $b = 3a$, with charge per unit length $-\rho\,\pi a^2$. What is the magnitude of the electric field at distance $r = 2a$ from the axis?

- $\dfrac{\rho a}{4\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{\rho a}{2\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{\rho a}{\varepsilon_0}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The distribution has cylindrical symmetry, so the field is radial and the outward flux through the coaxial Gaussian cylinder of radius $r$ and height $h$ is $E(r) \cdot 2\pi r h$.

For $r = 2a$ the Gaussian cylinder encloses the whole charged cylinder and no part of the outer surface, so $q_{\text{int}} = \rho\,\pi a^2 h$. **Gauss's law** gives $E(r) = \dfrac{\rho a^2}{2\varepsilon_0 r}$, which at $r = 2a$ equals $\dfrac{\rho a}{4\varepsilon_0}$.

The outer surface does not contribute to the field for $r < b$; the total field vanishes only for $r > b$. The value $\dfrac{\rho a}{\varepsilon_0}$ would be obtained by using the inner expression $\dfrac{\rho r}{2\varepsilon_0}$ outside the charged cylinder.
