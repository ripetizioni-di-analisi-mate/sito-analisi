Two infinite parallel straight wires, at distance $2d$ from each other, are uniformly charged with densities $+\lambda$ and $-\lambda$, with $\lambda = 2.0\,\mathrm{nC/m}$ and $d = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the magnitude of the electric field at the midpoint $M$ of the segment perpendicular to the wires that joins them?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="-2,0,pieno,+λ;2,0,pieno,−λ;0,0,pieno,M" segmenti="-2,-0.5,0,-0.5;0,-0.5,2,-0.5" etichette="-1.05,-0.85,d;0.95,-0.85,d" xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-1.2 ymax=0.8 titolo="Cross section perpendicular to the two wires; $M$ is the midpoint of the segment joining them"}

- $0$
- $7.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $2.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $1.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

Each wire produces at $M$, at distance $d$, a field of magnitude
$$
\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 d} = \frac{2k\lambda}{d} = \frac{2 \cdot 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.050}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 7.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C} .
$$
The field of the positive wire points away from that wire and the field of the negative wire points toward it, so at $M$ both point toward the negative wire.

By the **superposition principle** the two magnitudes add, therefore $E \approx 1.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The field at $M$ would be zero if the two wires had charges of the same sign.
