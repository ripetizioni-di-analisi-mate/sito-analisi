The electric field outside a uniformly charged spherical shell is measured. Initially, the measurement point is outside. The distance from the center is increased without changing the shell. Initially, the given quantities are the initial field magnitude $E_{0} = 180\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the ratio of final to initial distance $b = 3$. In the second configuration, we change the initial field magnitude to $160\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the ratio of final to initial distance to $2$, with all other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $E$?

- $-20\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $40\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $-980\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

Gauss's law makes $E$ constant for every concentric surface. The same enclosed charge gives $E\,4\pi r^2$ constant outside. Applying $E = \dfrac{E_{0}}{b^{2}}$ gives $20\,\mathrm{N/C}$ before the change and $40\,\mathrm{N/C}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $20\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
