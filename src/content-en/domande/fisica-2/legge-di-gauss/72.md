An infinite straight wire is uniformly charged with density $\lambda = 1.0\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. An electron moves on a circular orbit of radius $r = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ in a plane perpendicular to the wire, with its center on the wire. Neglecting gravity, what is the speed of the electron?

- $1.3 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By **Gauss's law** applied to a cylinder coaxial with the wire, the field at distance $r$ is radial, directed away from the wire, with magnitude $E = \dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$. The electron has negative charge, so the electric force of magnitude $eE$ points toward the wire and provides the centripetal force.

From the condition $\dfrac{m_e v^2}{r} = \dfrac{e\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$ the radius cancels, therefore
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{e\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 m_e}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The speed is the same for every orbit radius, because the field of the wire decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r}$. The value $1.8 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ would be obtained by setting $m_e v^2$ equal to the force, without dividing by $r$.
