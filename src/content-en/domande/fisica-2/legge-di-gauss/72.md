A very long insulating cylinder has radius $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and uniform volume charge density. The field is measured inside, far from the ends. The field at the surface has magnitude $E_s$. Given the distance from the axis $r = 2\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the internal field magnitude $E = 40\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the field magnitude at the surface $E_{s}$?

- $250\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $16\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $200\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $100\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

For an internal Gaussian cylinder, the enclosed charge grows as $r^2$ and the lateral area as $r$. Therefore, $E(r)/E_s=r/5$ with the distances in centimeters. In the indicated units, $E = \dfrac{E_{s} \cdot r}{5}$. Solving for the unknown gives $E_{s} = \dfrac{5 \cdot E}{r}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $100\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
