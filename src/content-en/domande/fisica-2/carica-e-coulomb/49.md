Two point charges are at rest in a vacuum at a separation of $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Initially, the given quantities are the magnitude of the first charge $q_{1} = 3\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the magnitude of the second charge $q_{2} = 8\,\mathrm{nC}$. In the second configuration, we change the magnitude of the first charge to $9\,\mathrm{nC}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $F$?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $1$
- $3$
- $6$

## Explanation

The force is $kq_1q_2/r^2$. With $r=0{,}03\,\mathrm m$, the product of the magnitudes in nanocoulombs must be multiplied by $10$ to obtain micronewtons. Applying $F = 10 \cdot q_{1} \cdot q_{2}$ gives $240\,\mathrm{\mu N}$ before the change and $720\,\mathrm{\mu N}$ afterward. The ratio is $3$.
