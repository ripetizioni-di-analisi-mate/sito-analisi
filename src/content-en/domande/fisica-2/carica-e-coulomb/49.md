An isolated conducting sphere has charge $+6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. Far from other charged bodies, it is connected to the ground with a metal wire and then the wire is removed. What charge does the sphere have at the end?

- $0$
- $+3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $+6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The metal wire connects the sphere to the Earth, which is a very large conductor, so the conduction charges pass between the sphere and the Earth. No other charged body is nearby, therefore equilibrium is reached when the sphere is discharged. Electrons for a charge of $-6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ come up from the Earth and neutralize the sphere. Splitting the charge in half holds for two identical conductors, and the Earth is much larger than the sphere. Hence the sphere has **zero** charge.
