Two identical conducting spheres $A$ and $B$, isolated and far from other charged bodies, are brought into contact and then separated; in the end each has charge $+2\,\mathrm{nC}$. During the contact electrons carrying a total charge of magnitude $3\,\mathrm{nC}$ moved from $A$ to $B$. What were the initial charges of the two spheres?

- $q_A = +5\,\mathrm{nC}$, $q_B = -1\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $q_A = +4\,\mathrm{nC}$, $q_B = 0$
- $q_A = +2\,\mathrm{nC}$, $q_B = +2\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $q_A = -1\,\mathrm{nC}$, $q_B = +5\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

By conservation of charge the sum of the initial charges equals the final one, $q_A + q_B = 2 + 2 = 4\,\mathrm{nC}$.

Each electron carries negative charge, so $A$, which gave away electrons for $3\,\mathrm{nC}$, increased its charge by $3\,\mathrm{nC}$. Therefore $q_A + 3\,\mathrm{nC} = 2\,\mathrm{nC}$, that is, $q_A = -1\,\mathrm{nC}$.

Sphere $B$ received those electrons and its charge decreased by $3\,\mathrm{nC}$, so $q_B = 2 + 3 = +5\,\mathrm{nC}$, in agreement with the sum $-1 + 5 = 4\,\mathrm{nC}$. The answer $q_A = +5\,\mathrm{nC}$, $q_B = -1\,\mathrm{nC}$ comes from treating the charge of the transferred electrons as positive.
