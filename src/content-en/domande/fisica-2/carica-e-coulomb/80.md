Two point charges $q_1 = +4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q_2 = +9.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ are in vacuum at $x = 0$ and at $x = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$, and are free to move. What charge $q_3$, placed on the segment joining them, puts all three charges in equilibrium?

- $+1.44\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-1.44\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-2.77\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-3.24\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The charge $q_3$ must be in equilibrium between $q_1$ and $q_2$, so it lies at the point
$$
x_0 = \frac{\sqrt{q_1}}{\sqrt{q_1} + \sqrt{q_2}}\,d = \frac{2}{2 + 3} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{cm} = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The charge $q_1$ is repelled by $q_2$, therefore $q_3$ must attract it and is negative. Equating the magnitudes of the forces on $q_1$ gives $k\,\dfrac{q_1 q_2}{d^2} = k\,\dfrac{q_1\,|q_3|}{x_0^2}$, hence
$$
|q_3| = q_2\,\frac{x_0^2}{d^2} = 9.0 \cdot \frac{4.0^2}{10^2}\,\mathrm{nC} = 1.44\,\mathrm{nC} .
$$
The forces on $q_1$ and on $q_3$ are zero and the sum of the internal forces is zero, therefore $q_2$ is in equilibrium too. Hence $q_3 = -1.44\,\mathrm{nC}$.
