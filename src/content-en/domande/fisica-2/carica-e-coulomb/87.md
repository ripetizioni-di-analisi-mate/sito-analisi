Two positive charges are fixed in vacuum on the $x$ axis, at $x = 0$ and at $x = 3$ (in centimeters), and the second is four times the first. The graph shows the component $F_x$ of the total force on a charge $q_0$ placed on the axis between the two charges. What is the sign of $q_0$, and what kind of equilibrium does it have at $x = 1$ for displacements along the axis?

::grafico{curve="t,1/t^2-4/(3-t)^2,0.35,2.55" punti="1,0,pieno" xmin=-0.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-6 ymax=6 nomi="x,Fₓ" legenda=0 titolo="Component $F_x$ of the force on $q_0$ for $0 < x < 3$, with $x$ in centimeters and $F_x$ in arbitrary units"}

- $q_0$ is positive and the equilibrium along the axis is unstable
- $q_0$ is negative and the equilibrium along the axis is stable
- $q_0$ is negative and the equilibrium along the axis is unstable
- $q_0$ is positive and the equilibrium along the axis is stable

## Explanation

Near $x = 0$ the graph is positive, so the force pushes $q_0$ to the right, away from the fixed charge at $x = 0$, which is the nearest one there. A positive charge repels $q_0$ only if $q_0$ is positive, therefore $q_0$ is **positive**. At the point $x = 1$ the force is zero, and $F_x$ is positive to the left and negative to the right of that point. If $q_0$ moves to the left the force pushes it to the right, and if it moves to the right it pushes it to the left; hence the force brings it back to $x = 1$ and the equilibrium along the axis is **stable**.
