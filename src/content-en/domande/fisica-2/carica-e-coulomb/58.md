Two identical spheres of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ carry the same charge $q$ and are in vacuum, far from other bodies, at a distance much larger than their radius. What value of $|q|$ makes the electric repulsion equal in magnitude to the gravitational attraction? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$ and $G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$.

- $7.4 \cdot 10^{-21}\,\mathrm{C}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{C}$
- $8.6 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{C}$
- $4.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The spheres are far apart compared with their radius, so they are treated as point charges and point masses at distance $r$. The two forces have magnitudes $k\,\dfrac{q^2}{r^2}$ and $G\,\dfrac{m^2}{r^2}$, which both depend on $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$, therefore the equality does not depend on the distance. From the equality $kq^2 = Gm^2$ one obtains
$$
|q| = m\,\sqrt{\frac{G}{k}} = 1.0 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}}{8.99 \cdot 10^{9}}}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 8.6 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{C} .
$$
This charge corresponds to about $5 \cdot 10^{8}$ elementary charges, a very small amount for a body of $1\,\mathrm{kg}$.
