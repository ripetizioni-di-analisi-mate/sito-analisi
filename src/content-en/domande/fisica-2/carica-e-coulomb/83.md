Two identical small spheres of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{g}$, with the same charge $q = 20\,\mathrm{nC}$, hang from the same point on two insulating threads of length $\ell = 50\,\mathrm{cm}$ and negligible mass. Using the small-angle approximation, how far apart are the spheres at equilibrium? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1.3,3 1.3,3;0,3 -0.7,0.2;0,3 0.7,0.2" punti="-0.7,0.2,pieno;0.7,0.2,pieno" segmenti="0,3,0,0.2" etichette="-1.05,0.05,q;1.05,0.05,q;0.6,1.75,ℓ;-0.15,2.3,θ" xmin=-2.5 xmax=2.5 ymin=-0.4 ymax=3.3 titolo="Two small spheres with the same charge $q$ hanging from the same point on threads of length $\ell$"}

- $5.7\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.9\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $14\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $7.2\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Each sphere is in equilibrium under its weight, the tension of the thread and the electric repulsion, so the angle of the thread with the vertical satisfies $\tan\theta = \dfrac{F}{mg}$. For small angles $\tan\theta \approx \sin\theta = \dfrac{r}{2\ell}$, and by **Coulomb's law** $F = k\,\dfrac{q^2}{r^2}$, therefore
$$
r^3 = \frac{2kq^2\ell}{mg} = \frac{2 \cdot 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \left(20 \cdot 10^{-9}\right)^2 \cdot 0.50}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 9.81}\,\mathrm{m^3} \approx 3.7 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3} .
$$
Hence $r \approx 7.2 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} = 7.2\,\mathrm{cm}$. The approximation is justified, because $\sin\theta = \dfrac{r}{2\ell} \approx 0.072$ corresponds to an angle of about $4^\circ$.
