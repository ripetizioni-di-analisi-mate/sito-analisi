Two identical isolated conducting spheres have charges $q_A = -6.4\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q_B = +2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. They are brought into contact, then separated and moved apart. How many electrons passed from one sphere to the other, and in which direction? Use $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

- $1.4 \cdot 10^{10}$ electrons, from $A$ to $B$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^{10}$ electrons, from $B$ to $A$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^{10}$ electrons, from $A$ to $B$
- $5.3 \cdot 10^{10}$ electrons, from $A$ to $B$

## Explanation

The spheres are identical, so after the contact each one has the charge
$$
\frac{q_A + q_B}{2} = \frac{-6.4 + 2.0}{2}\,\mathrm{nC} = -2.2\,\mathrm{nC} .
$$
The charge of $A$ goes from $-6.4\,\mathrm{nC}$ to $-2.2\,\mathrm{nC}$, that is, it increases by $4.2\,\mathrm{nC}$, therefore $A$ gave up electrons carrying a charge of $-4.2\,\mathrm{nC}$. By **conservation of charge** these electrons passed to $B$, whose charge indeed goes from $+2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ to $-2.2\,\mathrm{nC}$. Their number is
$$
n = \frac{4.2 \cdot 10^{-9}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}} \approx 2.6 \cdot 10^{10} ,
$$
so about $2.6 \cdot 10^{10}$ electrons passed from $A$ to $B$.
