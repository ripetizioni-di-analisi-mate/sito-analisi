A pair of opposite charges $\pm 2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart, is fixed in vacuum. On the line perpendicular to the segment joining them through its midpoint, at $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ from that point, there is the charge $q_0 = 1.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. What is, to two significant figures, the magnitude of the force on $q_0$? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$.

- $7.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0$
- $2.9 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.4 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Choose the origin at the midpoint and the $x$ axis along the segment, so the charges $+q$ and $-q$, with $q = 2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, are at $(-a, 0)$ and $(a, 0)$, and $q_0$ is at $(0, y)$. For two opposite charges the force on $q_0$ is parallel to the segment joining them and has magnitude $F = \dfrac{2kq\,q_0\,a}{\left(a^2 + y^2\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$. Here $a = 0.010\,\mathrm{m}$ and $y = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, so $y \gg a$ and $\left(a^2 + y^2\right)^{\frac{3}{2}} \approx y^3$. Therefore
$$
F \approx \frac{2kq\,q_0\,a}{y^3} = \frac{2 \cdot 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 0.010}{0.50^3}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 2.9 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The force of only one of the two charges would be $7.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N}$. The two contributions almost cancel, and the result decreases like $\dfrac{1}{y^3}$.
