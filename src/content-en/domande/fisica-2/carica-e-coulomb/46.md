In Millikan's experiment three oil drops have the measured charges $3.2 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $4.8 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$ and $8.0 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$. What is the largest value of the elementary charge compatible with the three measurements?

- $3.2 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$
- $0.80 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$
- $5.3 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

By the **quantization of charge** every measured charge is an integer multiple of the elementary charge, so the elementary charge divides all three measurements. In units of $10^{-20}\,\mathrm{C}$ the measurements are $32$, $48$ and $80$, and their greatest common divisor is $16$. Therefore the largest compatible value is $1.6 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, and the drops carry $2$, $3$ and $5$ elementary charges respectively. The value $3.2 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$ does not divide $4.8 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, while $0.80 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$ divides the three measurements and is smaller.
