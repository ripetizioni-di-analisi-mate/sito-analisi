Two equal point charges $q > 0$ are fixed in vacuum at $x = 0$ and at $x = d$, and the charge $q_0 > 0$ is in equilibrium at the midpoint. If $q_0$ is displaced along the axis by a small distance $\delta$, the total force on it is approximately $F_x = -K\,\delta$. What is $K$?

- $\dfrac{16\,kqq_0}{d^3}$
- $\dfrac{8\,kqq_0}{d^3}$
- $\dfrac{32\,kqq_0}{d^3}$
- $\dfrac{64\,kqq_0}{d^3}$

## Explanation

At the point $x$ between the two charges the force on $q_0$ is
$$
F_x(x) = kqq_0\left(\frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{1}{(d - x)^2}\right) ,
$$
which vanishes at $x = \dfrac{d}{2}$. For a small displacement $F_x$ is approximated by its tangent line at $\dfrac{d}{2}$, so $F_x \approx F_x'\!\left(\dfrac{d}{2}\right)\delta$. The derivative is
$$
F_x'(x) = kqq_0\left(-\frac{2}{x^3} - \frac{2}{(d - x)^3}\right) ,
$$
and at $x = \dfrac{d}{2}$ it equals $kqq_0\left(-\dfrac{16}{d^3} - \dfrac{16}{d^3}\right) = -\dfrac{32\,kqq_0}{d^3}$. Therefore $K = \dfrac{32\,kqq_0}{d^3}$, and the minus sign of $F_x$ confirms that along the axis the equilibrium is stable.
