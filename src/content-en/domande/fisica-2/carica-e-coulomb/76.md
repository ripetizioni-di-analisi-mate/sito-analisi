Five equal point charges $q > 0$ occupy the vertices of a regular pentagon inscribed in a circle of radius $R$, in vacuum; then the charge at one of the vertices is removed. What force acts on the charge $q_0 > 0$ placed at the center of the pentagon?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,2 1.902,0.618 1.176,-1.618 -1.176,-1.618 -1.902,0.618 0,2" punti="0,2,vuoto;1.902,0.618,pieno;1.176,-1.618,pieno;-1.176,-1.618,pieno;-1.902,0.618,pieno;0,0,pieno" etichette="2.2,0.75,q;1.4,-1.85,q;-1.4,-1.85,q;-2.2,0.75,q;0.3,0.15,q₀;0.95,0.95,R" segmenti="0,0,1.902,0.618" xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-2.2 ymax=2.5 titolo="Regular pentagon inscribed in the circle of radius $R$, with the top vertex empty and the charge $q_0$ at the center"}

- Magnitude $\dfrac{kqq_0}{R^2}$, directed away from the empty vertex
- Magnitude $\dfrac{kqq_0}{R^2}$, directed towards the empty vertex
- Magnitude $\dfrac{4kqq_0}{R^2}$, directed towards the empty vertex
- Zero force, by the symmetry of the pentagon

## Explanation

With all five charges the configuration does not change when the pentagon is rotated by $72^\circ$ about the center, so the force on $q_0$ does not change under this rotation either; the only vector with this property is the zero vector. By the **superposition principle** the force with four charges is the force with five charges minus the contribution of the removed charge. That contribution has magnitude $\dfrac{kqq_0}{R^2}$ and points from the vertex towards the center, because the charges repel each other. Therefore the force with four charges has magnitude $\dfrac{kqq_0}{R^2}$ and is directed **towards the empty vertex**.
