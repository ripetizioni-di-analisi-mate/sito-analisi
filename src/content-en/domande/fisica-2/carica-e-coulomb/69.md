From Coulomb's law $F = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\dfrac{|q_1|\,|q_2|}{r^2}$ one obtains the unit of the vacuum permittivity $\varepsilon_0$. How is this unit expressed in terms of the base units of the International System (kilogram, meter, second, ampere)?

- $\mathrm{A^2 \cdot s^4/(kg \cdot m^3)}$
- $\mathrm{kg \cdot m^3/(A^2 \cdot s^4)}$
- $\mathrm{A \cdot s^2/(kg \cdot m^3)}$
- $\mathrm{A^2 \cdot s^2/(kg \cdot m)}$

## Explanation

From **Coulomb's law** one obtains $\varepsilon_0 = \dfrac{|q_1|\,|q_2|}{4\pi F r^2}$, and $4\pi$ is a pure number, so the unit of $\varepsilon_0$ is $\mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)}$. Since $1\,\mathrm{C} = 1\,\mathrm{A\cdot s}$ and $1\,\mathrm{N} = 1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$, we have
$$
\frac{\mathrm{C^2}}{\mathrm{N\cdot m^2}} = \frac{\mathrm{A^2\cdot s^2}}{\mathrm{kg\cdot m\cdot s^{-2}\cdot m^2}} = \frac{\mathrm{A^2\cdot s^4}}{\mathrm{kg\cdot m^3}} .
$$
The inverse unit, $\mathrm{kg \cdot m^3/(A^2 \cdot s^4)}$, is that of the constant $k = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$.
