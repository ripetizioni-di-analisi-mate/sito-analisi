On the $x$ axis, in vacuum, an unbounded row of equal charges $+q$ occupies the points $x = a, 2a, 3a, \dots$, with $a > 0$. What is the $x$ component of the total force on the charge $q_0 > 0$ placed at the origin? Use $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$.

- $+\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}\,\dfrac{kqq_0}{a^2}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}\,\dfrac{kqq_0}{a^2}$
- $-\dfrac{kqq_0}{a^2}$
- The force is not finite, because there are infinitely many charges

## Explanation

Each charge lies to the right of the origin and has the same sign as $q_0$, so it repels it to the left. The charge at $x = na$ gives the contribution $-\dfrac{kqq_0}{n^2 a^2}$. By the **superposition principle** the total force is the sum of all the contributions, hence
$$
F_x = -\frac{kqq_0}{a^2} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = -\frac{\pi^2}{6}\,\frac{kqq_0}{a^2} .
$$
The series converges because the contributions decrease like $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$, therefore the force is finite even with infinitely many charges. The nearest charge alone gives about $61\%$ of the total.
