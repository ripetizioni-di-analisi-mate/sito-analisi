A small sphere of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{g}$ and charge $+50\,\mathrm{nC}$ hangs from a vertical insulating thread. On the same vertical line, $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the sphere, the point charge $-50\,\mathrm{nC}$ is fixed. What is the tension of the thread at equilibrium? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $1.1 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.9 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The forces on the sphere are the tension $T$ upwards, the weight $mg$ downwards and the electric force of the fixed charge. The charges have opposite signs, so the electric force is attractive and directed downwards, with magnitude
$$
F = k\,\frac{|Q|\,q}{d^2} = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{\left(50 \cdot 10^{-9}\right)^2}{0.050^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 9.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The sphere is in **equilibrium**, therefore the tension equals the sum of the magnitudes of the weight and of the electric force, hence
$$
T = mg + F = \left(2.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 9.81 + 9.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\right)\mathrm{N} \approx 2.9 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
