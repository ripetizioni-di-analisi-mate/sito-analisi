In vacuum the charge $q_0 > 0$ is at the origin, and two equal charges $q > 0$ are at the points $(a, 0)$ and $(0, a)$. What charge $Q$ must be placed at the point $(-a, -a)$ so that the total force on $q_0$ is zero?

- $-2\sqrt{2}\,q$
- $+2\sqrt{2}\,q$
- $+\sqrt{2}\,q$
- $+2q$

## Explanation

The charges $q$ repel $q_0$ in the negative directions of the $x$ and $y$ axes, with perpendicular forces of magnitude $\dfrac{kqq_0}{a^2}$. Their sum has magnitude $\sqrt{2}\,\dfrac{kqq_0}{a^2}$ and is directed towards the point $(-a, -a)$. The charge $Q$ must therefore push $q_0$ away from $(-a, -a)$, that is repel it, so $Q$ is positive. The distance of $(-a, -a)$ from the origin is $\sqrt{2}\,a$, and equating the magnitudes gives
$$
k\,\frac{Q\,q_0}{2a^2} = \sqrt{2}\,\frac{kqq_0}{a^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Q = 2\sqrt{2}\,q .
$$
