Four point charges are at the vertices of a square of side $a$, in vacuum. The two left vertices carry the charge $+q$, the two right vertices the charge $-q$, with $q > 0$. What is the magnitude of the force on the charge $q_0 > 0$ placed at the center of the square?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 2,0 2,2 0,2 0,0" punti="0,0,pieno;0,2,pieno;2,0,pieno;2,2,pieno;1,1,pieno" etichette="-0.35,2.2,+q;-0.35,-0.25,+q;2.3,2.2,−q;2.3,-0.25,−q;1.2,1.2,q₀;1,-0.3,a" xmin=-0.9 xmax=2.9 ymin=-0.6 ymax=2.5 titolo="Square of side $a$ with the charges $+q$ on the left, $-q$ on the right and $q_0$ at the center"}

- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}\,kqq_0}{a^2}$
- $\dfrac{8\,kqq_0}{a^2}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}\,kqq_0}{a^2}$

## Explanation

The center is at half the diagonal from each vertex, that is at $\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$, so each charge exerts on $q_0$ a force of magnitude
$$
F_1 = k\,\frac{q\,q_0}{\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2} = \frac{2kqq_0}{a^2} .
$$
The charges $+q$ repel $q_0$ and the charges $-q$ attract it, so all four forces have a horizontal component directed to the right. By symmetry the vertical components cancel in pairs, and each force has horizontal component $F_1 \cos 45^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}\,kqq_0}{a^2}$. Therefore the total force is directed to the right and has magnitude $4 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}\,kqq_0}{a^2} = \dfrac{4\sqrt{2}\,kqq_0}{a^2}$.
