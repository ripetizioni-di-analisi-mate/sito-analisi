On a very humid day, charging experiments by rubbing work poorly, because the rubbed rods quickly lose their charge. Which fact explains the effect?

- Humid air has a relative permittivity close to $80$, and the electric forces become much weaker
- The water molecules in the air give protons to negatively charged bodies
- A thin layer of water forms on the surface of the bodies, which conducts and lets the charge leak away
- Water vapor is a better insulator than dry air and prevents the transfer of electrons

## Explanation

Dry air is an insulator, so a charged body surrounded by dry air keeps its charge for a long time. With humid air a thin layer of water containing dissolved ions settles on the surface of the bodies, and a salt solution is a conductor. Therefore the charges move along the water layer to the support, to the hand and to the Earth, and the rod discharges. The vapor dispersed in the air has a relative permittivity close to $1$, so the forces between the charges do not change appreciably.
