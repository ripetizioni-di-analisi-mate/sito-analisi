A test charge experiences two mutually perpendicular Coulomb forces, one horizontal and one vertical. Initially, the given quantities are the magnitude of the horizontal force $F_{x} = 9\,\mathrm{\mu N}$ and the magnitude of the vertical force $F_{y} = 12\,\mathrm{\mu N}$. In the second configuration, we change the magnitude of the horizontal force to $5\,\mathrm{\mu N}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $F$?

- $2\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $-4\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $4\,\mathrm{\mu N}$

## Explanation

Superposition adds the forces as vectors. The two components are perpendicular, so the Pythagorean theorem applies. Applying $F = \sqrt{F_{x}^{2} + F_{y}^{2}}$ gives $15\,\mathrm{\mu N}$ before the change and $13\,\mathrm{\mu N}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $-2\,\mathrm{\mu N}$.
