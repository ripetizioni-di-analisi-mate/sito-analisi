A droplet acquires electrons in two stages. The magnitude of the elementary charge is $0{,}16\,\mathrm{aC}$. Given the number of electrons in the first stage $N_{1} = 20$ and the magnitude of the acquired charge $q = 8\,\mathrm{aC}$, what is the number of electrons in the second stage $N_{2}$?

- $15$
- $30$
- $45$
- $60$

## Explanation

Each electron adds $-0{,}16\,\mathrm{aC}$. The numbers of electrons acquired in the two stages add. In the indicated units, $q = \dfrac{4 \cdot N_{1}}{25} + \dfrac{4 \cdot N_{2}}{25}$. Solving for the unknown gives $N_{2} = - N_{1} + \dfrac{25 \cdot q}{4}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $30$.
