Measuring the force between two fixed charges at different distances gives the values $F = 3.6\,\mathrm{mN}$ at $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, $F = 0.90\,\mathrm{mN}$ at $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $F = 0.40\,\mathrm{mN}$ at $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Which law describes the data?

- $F$ is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r}$
- $F$ is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r^3}$
- $F$ is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$
- $F$ decreases linearly with $r$

## Explanation

If $F$ is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r^n}$, the product $F\,r^n$ is the same for all the measurements. With $n = 2$, in $\mathrm{mN \cdot cm^2}$, one gets $3.6 \cdot 1.0^2 = 0.90 \cdot 2.0^2 = 0.40 \cdot 3.0^2 = 3.6$, so the data are described by a force proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$, as **Coulomb's law** predicts. With $n = 1$ the products are $3.6$, $1.8$ and $1.2$, and with $n = 3$ they are $3.6$, $7.2$ and $10.8$, therefore in those cases they are not constant. A linear decrease would give equal differences between consecutive measurements, while here the differences are $2.7\,\mathrm{mN}$ and $0.50\,\mathrm{mN}$.
