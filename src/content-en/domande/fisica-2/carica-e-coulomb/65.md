Two positive charges lie on the $x$ axis. The first is at the origin, the second at $d>0$. A third positive charge is in equilibrium at $x=2\,\mathrm{cm}$ between them. Initially, the given quantities are the first charge $q_{1} = 4\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the second charge $q_{2} = 9\,\mathrm{nC}$. In the second configuration, we change the first charge to $9\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the second charge to $36\,\mathrm{nC}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $d$?

- $-1\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $\dfrac{7}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The magnitudes of the forces on the third charge must be equal. Equating $q_1/2^2$ and $q_2/(d-2)^2$ gives the segment $d-2$. Applying $d = 2 + \dfrac{2 \cdot \sqrt{q_{2}}}{\sqrt{q_{1}}}$ gives $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ before the change and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $1\,\mathrm{cm}$.
