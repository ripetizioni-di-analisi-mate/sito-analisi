A light ball coated with metallic paint, neutral, hangs from an insulating thread. A positively charged glass rod is brought near it: the ball is attracted, touches the rod and right afterwards is repelled by it. Which fact explains the repulsion?

- On contact the ball acquired a positive charge, of the same sign as that of the rod
- On contact the ball acquired a negative charge, which the rod repels
- On contact the ball became neutral again, and a neutral body is repelled by a charged body
- On contact the rod lost all its charge, and the ball returns to its initial position

## Explanation

The ball is conducting and neutral, so by **electrostatic induction** the rod attracts the conduction electrons to the near side, and the ball is attracted. On contact some of these electrons pass to the rod, therefore the ball is left with a positive charge. The ball and the rod now have charges of the same sign, so they repel each other. Glass is an insulator, therefore the rod gives up or acquires charge only in the contact region and remains positively charged.
