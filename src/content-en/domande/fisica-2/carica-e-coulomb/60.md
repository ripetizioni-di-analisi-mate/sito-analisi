Two identical conducting spheres with initially positive charges are brought into contact and then separated. They exchange no charge with their surroundings. Initially, the given quantities are the initial charge of the first sphere $q_{1} = 10\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the initial charge of the second sphere $q_{2} = 6\,\mathrm{nC}$. In the second configuration, we change the initial charge of the first sphere to $12\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the initial charge of the second sphere to $10\,\mathrm{nC}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $q$?

- $6\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-1\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $3\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The total charge is conserved. The identical spheres, separated symmetrically after contact, share the charge equally. Applying $q = \dfrac{q_{1}}{2} + \dfrac{q_{2}}{2}$ gives $8\,\mathrm{nC}$ before the change and $11\,\mathrm{nC}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $3\,\mathrm{nC}$.
