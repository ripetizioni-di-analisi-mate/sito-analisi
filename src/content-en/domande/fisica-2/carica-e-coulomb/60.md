In water at room temperature, with relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r = 80$, an $\mathrm{Na^+}$ ion and a $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ ion are $0.50\,\mathrm{nm}$ apart. Treating the ions as point charges $+e$ and $-e$ in a homogeneous medium, what is the magnitude of the force between them? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$ and $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

- $9.2 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $7.4 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

In a homogeneous insulator that fills space the force between two charges is the force in vacuum divided by $\varepsilon_r$, hence
$$
F = \frac{k}{\varepsilon_r}\,\frac{e^2}{r^2} = \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^{9}}{80} \cdot \frac{\left(1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\right)^2}{\left(0.50 \cdot 10^{-9}\right)^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
In vacuum the same force would be $9.2 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{N}$. The reduction by a factor of $80$ is due to the polarization of the water, and it makes the attraction between the ions weak enough for the salt to dissolve.
