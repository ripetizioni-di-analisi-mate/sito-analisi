In Rutherford's experiment an $\alpha$ particle, with charge $2e$, comes within $3.0 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}$ of the center of a gold nucleus, which has atomic number $79$ and mass number $197$. Treating the two bodies as point charges, what is the magnitude of the electric force between them? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$ and $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

- $20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $40\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The atomic number counts the protons of the nucleus, while the mass number also counts the neutrons, which are neutral; so the charge of the gold nucleus is $79e$.

By **Coulomb's law** the magnitude of the force is
$$
F = k\,\frac{(2e)(79e)}{r^2} = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{158 \cdot \left(1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\right)^2}{\left(3.0 \cdot 10^{-14}\right)^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 40\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $20\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by giving the $\alpha$ particle the charge $e$, and $1.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$ by using the mass number instead of the atomic number. The value $1.2 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained instead by dividing by the distance without squaring it.
