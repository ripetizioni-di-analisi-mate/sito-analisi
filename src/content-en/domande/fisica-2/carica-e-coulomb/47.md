Two point charges are at rest in a vacuum at a separation of $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the magnitude of the second charge $q_{2} = 8\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the magnitude of the Coulomb force $F = 240\,\mathrm{\mu N}$, what is the magnitude of the first charge $q_{1}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $3\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $6\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The force is $kq_1q_2/r^2$. With $r=0{,}03\,\mathrm m$, the product of the magnitudes in nanocoulombs must be multiplied by $10$ to obtain micronewtons. In the indicated units, $F = 10 \cdot q_{1} \cdot q_{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $q_{1} = \dfrac{F}{10 \cdot q_{2}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $3\,\mathrm{nC}$.
