Two equal positive charges are fixed in vacuum at the points $(-a, 0)$ and $(a, 0)$. A negative charge $q_0$, subject only to electric forces, is in equilibrium at the origin. For which small displacements in the plane is the equilibrium of $q_0$ stable?

- Stable for displacements along the $y$ axis, unstable for displacements along the $x$ axis
- Stable for displacements along the $x$ axis, unstable for displacements along the $y$ axis
- Stable for displacements in every direction
- Unstable for displacements in every direction

## Explanation

If $q_0$ moves along the $x$ axis, it gets closer to one of the two positive charges, which attracts it more than the other, so the force moves it further away from the origin and along the $x$ axis the equilibrium is **unstable**. If $q_0$ moves to the point $(0, y)$, both charges attract it towards themselves; the components along $x$ cancel and those along $y$ point towards the origin. Therefore along the $y$ axis the force brings $q_0$ back to the equilibrium position, which in that direction is **stable**. The existence of a direction of instability agrees with Earnshaw's theorem.
