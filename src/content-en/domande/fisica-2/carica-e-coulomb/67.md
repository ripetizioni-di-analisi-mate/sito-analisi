A test charge experiences two mutually perpendicular Coulomb forces, one horizontal and one vertical. Given the magnitude of the vertical force $F_{y} = 12\,\mathrm{\mu N}$ and the magnitude of the resultant force $F = 15\,\mathrm{\mu N}$, what is the magnitude of the horizontal force $F_{x}$?

- $9\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $18\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $\dfrac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $3\,\mathrm{\mu N}$

## Explanation

Superposition adds the forces as vectors. The two components are perpendicular, so the Pythagorean theorem applies. In the indicated units, $F = \sqrt{F_{x}^{2} + F_{y}^{2}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $F_{x} = \sqrt{F^{2} - F_{y}^{2}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $9\,\mathrm{\mu N}$.
