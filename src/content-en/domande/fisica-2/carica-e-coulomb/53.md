A leaf electroscope is uncharged. A positively charged rod is brought close to its metal sphere, without touching it. What happens to the leaves while the rod is close?

- They spread apart, because they acquire a positive charge
- They spread apart, because they acquire a negative charge
- They stay closed, because the total charge of the electroscope is zero
- They stay closed, because the induced charge is all on the sphere

## Explanation

The sphere, the rod and the leaves form a single isolated conductor. By **electrostatic induction** the positive rod attracts the conduction electrons towards the sphere, so a positive charge remains on the leaves, which are the farthest part. The two leaves have charges of the same sign and repel each other, therefore they **spread apart**. The total charge of the electroscope stays zero, and when the rod is moved away the leaves close again.
