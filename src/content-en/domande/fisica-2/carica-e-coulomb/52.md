Two neutral, isolated conducting spheres, $A$ and $B$, are in contact. A negatively charged rod is brought close to $A$, on the side opposite to $B$, without touching it. Keeping the rod still, the spheres are separated, then the rod is moved away. What charges do $A$ and $B$ have at the end?

- $A$ negative and $B$ positive, with the same absolute value
- $A$ positive and $B$ negative, with the same absolute value
- Both neutral
- Both negative

## Explanation

The two spheres in contact form a single conductor. The negative rod repels the conduction electrons, which move to the farthest part, that is onto $B$, and a positive charge remains on $A$. The spheres are separated while the rod is close, so each keeps the charge it had at that moment. By the **conservation of charge** the sum stays zero, therefore $A$ is **positive** and $B$ is **negative**, with the same absolute value.
