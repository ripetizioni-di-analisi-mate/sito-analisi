In vacuum the charge $q_1 = +3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at the origin and the charge $q_2 = -2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ at the point $(3.0\,\mathrm{cm};\,4.0\,\mathrm{cm})$. What is the $x$ component of the force exerted by $q_1$ on $q_2$? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$.

::grafico{uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,q₁;3,4,pieno,q₂" segmenti="3,0,3,4;0,4,3,4" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=5.2 nomi="x,y" legenda=0 titolo="Positions of the two charges, with coordinates in centimeters"}

- $+1.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-1.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-1.7 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-2.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The distance between the charges is $r = \sqrt{0.030^2 + 0.040^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.050\,\mathrm{m}$. By the vector form of **Coulomb's law** $\vec{F}_{12} = k\,\dfrac{q_1 q_2}{r^3}\,(\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1)$, hence
$$
F_{12,x} = k\,\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^3}\,x_2 = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot \left(-2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\right)}{0.050^3} \cdot 0.030\,\mathrm{N} \approx -1.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The negative sign indicates that $q_2$ is attracted towards the origin, where $q_1$ is, as happens for charges of opposite sign. The magnitude of the force is $2.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$, and the $x$ component is the fraction $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of it, because $\dfrac{x_2}{r} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.
