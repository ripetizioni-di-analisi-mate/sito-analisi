An insulating sphere of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ has the charge $Q = +4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ uniformly distributed throughout its volume. A point charge $q = +1.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is in vacuum at $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the surface of the sphere. What is the magnitude of the force on $q$? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$.

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

For a sphere with uniformly distributed charge **Coulomb's law** also holds outside the sphere, with the distance measured from the center. The charge $q$ is at distance $r = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm} + 4.0\,\mathrm{cm} = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center, hence
$$
F = k\,\frac{Qq}{r^2} = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.060^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
Both charges are positive, therefore the force is repulsive.
