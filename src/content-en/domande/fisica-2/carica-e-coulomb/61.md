Two positive charges lie on the $x$ axis. The first is at the origin, the second at $d>0$. A third positive charge is in equilibrium at $x=2\,\mathrm{cm}$ between them. Given the first charge $q_{1} = 4\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the second charge $q_{2} = 9\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the separation between the two fixed charges $d$?

- $\dfrac{13}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The magnitudes of the forces on the third charge must be equal. Equating $q_1/2^2$ and $q_2/(d-2)^2$ gives the segment $d-2$. In the indicated units, $d = 2 + \dfrac{2 \cdot \sqrt{q_{2}}}{\sqrt{q_{1}}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $5\,\mathrm{cm}$.
