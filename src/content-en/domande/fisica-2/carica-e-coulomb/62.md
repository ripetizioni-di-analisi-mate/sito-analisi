Two positive charges lie on the $x$ axis. The first is at the origin, the second at $d>0$. A third positive charge is in equilibrium at $x=2\,\mathrm{cm}$ between them. Given the second charge $q_{2} = 9\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the separation between the two fixed charges $d = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$, what is the first charge $q_{1}$?

- $6\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $4\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $8\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $2\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The magnitudes of the forces on the third charge must be equal. Equating $q_1/2^2$ and $q_2/(d-2)^2$ gives the segment $d-2$. In the indicated units, $d = 2 + \dfrac{2 \cdot \sqrt{q_{2}}}{\sqrt{q_{1}}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $q_{1} = \dfrac{4 \cdot q_{2}}{\left(d - 2\right)^{2}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $4\,\mathrm{nC}$.
