In vacuum the charge $q_0 = +1.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ is at the origin, the charge $q_1 = -2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at the point $(0.30\,\mathrm{m};\,0)$ and the charge $q_2 = +1.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at the point $(0;\,0.15\,\mathrm{m})$. What is the direction of the total force on $q_0$? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$.

::grafico{uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,q₀;3,0,pieno,q₁;0,1.5,pieno,q₂" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=2.5 nomi="x,y" legenda=0 titolo="Positions of the charges, with coordinates in decimeters"}

- It makes an angle of about $27^\circ$ below the positive $x$ direction
- It makes an angle of about $63^\circ$ above the positive $x$ direction
- It makes an angle of about $63^\circ$ below the positive $x$ direction
- It makes an angle of about $63^\circ$ below the negative $x$ direction

## Explanation

The charge $q_1$ is negative and attracts $q_0$ towards itself, that is in the positive $x$ direction, with magnitude
$$
F_1 = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.30^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.20\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The charge $q_2$ is positive and repels $q_0$ in the negative $y$ direction, with magnitude
$$
F_2 = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{1.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.15^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.40\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
By the **superposition principle** the total force has components $F_x \approx 0.20\,\mathrm{N}$ and $F_y \approx -0.40\,\mathrm{N}$, so it lies in the fourth quadrant. The angle $\varphi$ below the $x$ axis satisfies $\tan\varphi = \dfrac{0.40}{0.20} = 2$, therefore $\varphi \approx 63^\circ$.
