The charge $+Q$ is fixed in vacuum at the point $(-a, 0)$ and the charge $-Q$ at the point $(a, 0)$, with $Q > 0$. What direction does the force on the charge $q_0 > 0$ placed at the point $(0, y)$, with $y > 0$, have?

::grafico{uguale=1 punti="-2,0,pieno,+Q;2,0,pieno,−Q;0,2,pieno,q₀" segmenti="-2,0,0,2;2,0,0,2" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=3 nomi="x,y" legenda=0 titolo="The charges $+Q$ at $(-a, 0)$ and $-Q$ at $(a, 0)$, and the charge $q_0$ on the $y$ axis"}

- Parallel to the $x$ axis, in the negative direction
- Parallel to the $x$ axis, in the positive direction
- Parallel to the $y$ axis, in the positive direction
- Parallel to the $y$ axis, in the negative direction

## Explanation

The charge $q_0$ has the same distance $\sqrt{a^2 + y^2}$ from the two charges, so the two forces have the same magnitude. The charge $+Q$ repels $q_0$ in the direction of the vector $a\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath}$, while the charge $-Q$ attracts it in the direction of the vector $a\,\hat{\imath} - y\,\hat{\jmath}$, which goes from $q_0$ towards $-Q$. The components along $\hat{\jmath}$ are opposite and cancel, while those along $\hat{\imath}$ are equal and positive. Therefore the force is **parallel to the** $x$ **axis**, in the direction going from $+Q$ towards $-Q$.
