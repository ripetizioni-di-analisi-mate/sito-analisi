On the $x$ axis, in vacuum, there are the charges $+q$ at $x = -a$, $-q$ at $x = 0$ and $+q$ at $x = a$, with $q > 0$ and $a > 0$. What is the $x$ component of the total force on the charge placed at $x = a$?

- $+\dfrac{3kq^2}{4a^2}$
- $-\dfrac{5kq^2}{4a^2}$
- $-\dfrac{3kq^2}{4a^2}$
- $+\dfrac{5kq^2}{4a^2}$

## Explanation

The charge $+q$ at $x = -a$ is at distance $2a$ and repels the charge at $x = a$ to the right, so it gives the contribution $+\dfrac{kq^2}{4a^2}$. The charge $-q$ at the origin is at distance $a$ and attracts it to the left, so it gives the contribution $-\dfrac{kq^2}{a^2}$. By the **superposition principle**
$$
F_x = \frac{kq^2}{4a^2} - \frac{kq^2}{a^2} = -\frac{3kq^2}{4a^2} ,
$$
therefore the total force is directed towards the origin.
