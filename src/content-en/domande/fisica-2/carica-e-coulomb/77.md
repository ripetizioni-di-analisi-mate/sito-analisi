The point charges $+q$ and $-q$, with $q > 0$, are fixed in vacuum at the points $x = 0$ and $x = d$ of the $x$-axis. Where is an equilibrium position for a third charge $q_0$, subject only to the forces of these two?

- At no point at a finite distance
- At the midpoint $x = \dfrac{d}{2}$
- At the point $x = 2d$, beyond the charge $-q$
- At the point $x = -d$, beyond the charge $+q$

## Explanation

Off the $x$-axis the forces of the two charges on $q_0$ have the directions of two different lines, so their sum is not zero. On the axis, between the two charges, one charge repels $q_0$ and the other attracts it toward itself, therefore the two forces have the same direction and their sum is not zero. Outside the segment the forces have opposite directions, but $q_0$ is closer to one of the two charges, which have the same absolute value, so the force of the closer charge has the larger magnitude. Therefore there is no **equilibrium position** at a finite distance.
