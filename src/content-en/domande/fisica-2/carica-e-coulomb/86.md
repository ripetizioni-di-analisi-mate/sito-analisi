A small sphere with charge $q = +50\,\mathrm{nC}$ is attached to the free end of a horizontal insulating spring, on a smooth plane. The point charge $Q = +50\,\mathrm{nC}$ is fixed on the axis of the spring, beyond the sphere: the spring shortens by $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and at equilibrium the two charges are $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. What is the spring constant $K$ of the spring? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$.

- $0.62\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $1.8\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $0.045\,\mathrm{N/m}$

## Explanation

The charges have the same sign, so $Q$ pushes the sphere toward the spring and the spring shortens. At **equilibrium** only the electric repulsion and the elastic force act on the sphere along the axis, and they have equal magnitudes. The repulsion is computed with the equilibrium distance $d = 0.050\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
F = k\,\frac{Qq}{d^2} = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{\left(50 \cdot 10^{-9}\right)^2}{0.050^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 9.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
Therefore, with the shortening $x = 0.010\,\mathrm{m}$,
$$
K = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{9.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.010}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 0.90\,\mathrm{N/m} .
$$
