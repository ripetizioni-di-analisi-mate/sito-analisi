A meson consists of an up quark, with charge $+\dfrac{2}{3}\,e$, and a down antiquark, with charge $+\dfrac{1}{3}\,e$. What is the charge of the meson?

- $+\dfrac{1}{3}\,e$
- $+e$
- $+\dfrac{2}{9}\,e$
- $+\dfrac{1}{2}\,e$

## Explanation

The charge of a composite particle is the algebraic sum of the charges of its constituents. Hence the charge of the meson is
$$
\frac{2}{3}\,e + \frac{1}{3}\,e = +e .
$$
The result is an integer multiple of $e$, in agreement with the **quantization of charge**, which concerns particles observed in isolation; quarks are not observed in isolation.
