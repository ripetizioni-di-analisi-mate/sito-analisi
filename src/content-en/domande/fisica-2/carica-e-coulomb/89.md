A plastic rod has a charge of $-10\,\mathrm{nC}$ distributed over its whole surface. The tip of the rod touches for an instant an isolated, neutral metal sphere, then the rod is moved away. What charge does the sphere have?

- About $-5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, half the charge of the rod
- $-10\,\mathrm{nC}$, all the charge of the rod
- $+10\,\mathrm{nC}$, by induction
- A negative charge much smaller than $10\,\mathrm{nC}$ in absolute value

## Explanation

Plastic is an insulator, so the charge of the rod stays in the regions where it is and does not move towards the point of contact. During the contact only the excess electrons of the small region touching the sphere pass to the sphere. Therefore the sphere becomes **negatively** charged, with a charge much smaller in absolute value than $10\,\mathrm{nC}$. Splitting the charge in half holds for two identical conductors, and the rod is an insulator.
