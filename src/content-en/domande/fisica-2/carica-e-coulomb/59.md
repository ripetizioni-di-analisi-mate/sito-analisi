In the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom the electron moves on a circular orbit of radius $5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$ around the proton, which is at rest, and the Coulomb force acts as the centripetal force. What is the magnitude of the velocity of the electron? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$, $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$ and $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

- $4.8 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.1 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.2 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The motion is uniform circular motion, so the magnitude of the Coulomb force of the proton on the electron equals the mass times the centripetal acceleration:
$$
k\,\frac{e^2}{r^2} = m_e\,\frac{v^2}{r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = e\,\sqrt{\frac{k}{m_e\,r}} .
$$
Substituting the data gives
$$
v = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{8.99 \cdot 10^{9}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.2 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The speed is less than $1\%$ of the speed of light, so a non-relativistic treatment is adequate.
