A point charge $Q = +0.20\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ is fixed at the base of a smooth insulating inclined plane, which makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the horizontal. A small sphere of mass $5.0\,\mathrm{g}$ and charge $q = +0.20\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ rests on the plane, higher than $Q$ along the line of steepest slope. At what distance from $Q$ is the sphere in equilibrium? Use $k = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 4,0 4,2.309" punti="0.45,0.43,pieno;2.45,1.58,pieno" etichette="0.35,0.85,Q;2.3,2.0,q;0.85,0.17,30°" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-0.4 ymax=2.8 titolo="Smooth inclined plane at $30^\circ$ with the charge $Q$ fixed at the base and the small sphere of charge $q$ resting higher up"}

- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $9.2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $8.6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The plane is smooth, so along the line of steepest slope only the component of the weight $mg\sin 30^\circ$, downwards, and the repulsion of $Q$, upwards, act; the reaction of the plane is perpendicular to the plane and has no component along it. At **equilibrium** the two forces have equal magnitudes, therefore
$$
k\,\frac{Qq}{d^2} = mg\sin 30^\circ
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = \sqrt{\frac{kQq}{mg\sin 30^\circ}} = \sqrt{\frac{8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \left(0.20 \cdot 10^{-6}\right)^2}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.12\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Hence the sphere is in equilibrium at about $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ from $Q$.
