An ideal inductor is supplied with a sinusoidal voltage. Rms values are used and the frequency is $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Given the inductance $L_{e} = \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the rms voltage $U_{e} = 60 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$, what is the rms current $I_{e}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm A$
- $3\,\mathrm A$
- $6\,\mathrm A$
- $3 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm A$

## Explanation

The inductive reactance is $2\pi fL$. The rms voltage is its product with the rms current. In the stated units, we have $U_{e} = 100 \cdot \pi \cdot I_{e} \cdot L_{e}$. Solving for the unknown gives $I_{e} = \dfrac{U_{e}}{100 \cdot \pi \cdot L_{e}}$. With the given values, we obtain $3\,\mathrm A$.
