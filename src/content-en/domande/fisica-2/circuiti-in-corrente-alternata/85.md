An inductive load, supplied with a sinusoidal AC voltage, has power factor $0.50$. For what fraction of each period is the instantaneous power it absorbs negative, that is, the load returns energy to the source?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

With $v(t) = V_0\sin(\omega t)$ the current is $i(t) = I_0\sin(\omega t - \varphi)$, and from $\cos\varphi = 0.50$ with $\varphi > 0$ it follows that $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

The instantaneous power $p = vi$ is negative when $v$ and $i$ have opposite signs. Within one period this happens for $0 < \omega t < \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and for $\pi < \omega t < \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, that is, in two intervals measuring $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ in total out of the full angle. Therefore the instantaneous power is negative for $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the period, even though the average power is positive.
