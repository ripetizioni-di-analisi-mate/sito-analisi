In a series $RLC$ circuit without a source the resistance is less than the critical resistance, and the charge undergoes damped oscillations. The resistance is increased, remaining less than the critical resistance. How does the period of the damped oscillations change?

- It decreases, because the angular frequency $\omega_s$ increases
- It stays equal to $2\pi\sqrt{LC}$, because it does not depend on $R$
- It increases, because the angular frequency $\omega_s$ decreases
- It decreases, because the energy is dissipated faster

## Explanation

The angular frequency of the damped oscillations is $\omega_s = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$, with $\gamma = \dfrac{R}{2L}$ and $\omega_0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$.

Increasing $R$ increases $\gamma$, while $\omega_0$ does not change, so $\omega_s$ decreases. The period $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega_s}$ therefore **increases**, and it tends to infinity as $R$ approaches the critical resistance, for which $\omega_s = 0$.
