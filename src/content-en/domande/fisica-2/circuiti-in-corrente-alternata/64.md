An ideal inductor of inductance $L = 0.20\,\mathrm{H}$ carries a sinusoidal current with rms value $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the maximum energy stored in the inductor?

- $0.40\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.28\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The energy of the inductor is $\dfrac{1}{2} L i^2$, maximum when the current is maximum in magnitude, that is, equal to the amplitude $I_0$.

For a sinusoidal current $I_0 = \sqrt{2}\,I_{\text{eff}} = 2\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{A}$, so $I_0^2 = 8.0\,\mathrm{A^2}$. Therefore the maximum energy is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.20 \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{J} = 0.80\,\mathrm{J}$.
