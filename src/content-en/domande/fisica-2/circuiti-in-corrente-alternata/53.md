In a series $RLC$ circuit without a source the resistance is $R = 8.0\,\Omega$. At a certain instant the circuit carries the current $0.25\,\mathrm{A}$. At what rate does the energy stored in the capacitor and in the inductor decrease at that instant?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{W}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{W}$
- $0\,\mathrm{W}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The energy of the circuit is $U = \dfrac{q^2}{2C} + \dfrac{1}{2} L i^2$. Differentiating and using the equation of the circuit gives $\dfrac{dU}{dt} = -R i^2$, which is the opposite of the power dissipated by Joule heating in the resistor.

Therefore the energy decreases at the rate
$$
R i^2 = 8.0 \cdot 0.25^2\,\mathrm{W} = 0.50\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The result depends only on the instantaneous current and not on the charge of the capacitor.
