An ideal inductor is supplied with a sinusoidal voltage. Rms values are used and the frequency is $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Initially, the inductance $L_{e} = \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the rms current $I_{e} = 3\,\mathrm A$ are given. In the second configuration, the inductance becomes $\dfrac{1}{10}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the rms current becomes $2\,\mathrm A$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $U_{e}$?

- $- 20 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$
- $-40\,\mathrm V$
- $40 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$
- $- 40 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$

## Explanation

The inductive reactance is $2\pi fL$. The rms voltage is its product with the rms current. Applying $U_{e} = 100 \cdot \pi \cdot I_{e} \cdot L_{e}$ gives $60 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$ before the change and $20 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$ after it. The final value minus the initial value is $- 40 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$.
