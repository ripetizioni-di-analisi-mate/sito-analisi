In an ideal $LC$ circuit the charge $q$ on the capacitor oscillates with amplitude $Q_0$ and the current $i$ oscillates with amplitude $I_0$. What curve does the point with coordinates $(q, i)$ trace during the oscillations?

- An ellipse centered at the origin, with semi-axes $Q_0$ and $I_0$
- A segment of the line $i = \omega_0\, q$
- An arc of a parabola with vertex at the origin
- A branch of a hyperbola

## Explanation

In the $LC$ circuit the total energy is conserved, so at every instant
$$
\frac{q^2}{2C} + \frac{1}{2} L i^2 = \frac{Q_0^2}{2C} .
$$
Dividing by $\dfrac{Q_0^2}{2C}$ gives $\dfrac{q^2}{Q_0^2} + \dfrac{LC\, i^2}{Q_0^2} = 1$. Since $I_0 = \dfrac{Q_0}{\sqrt{LC}}$, we have $\dfrac{LC}{Q_0^2} = \dfrac{1}{I_0^2}$, therefore
$$
\frac{q^2}{Q_0^2} + \frac{i^2}{I_0^2} = 1 .
$$
Hence the point $(q, i)$ traces the ellipse centered at the origin with semi-axes $Q_0$ and $I_0$, like the position-velocity pair of a mass-spring oscillator.
