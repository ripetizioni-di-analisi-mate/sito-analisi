A resistor and an inductor are connected in series. The phasor of the applied voltage is $\tilde{V} = 100\,\mathrm{V}$ and that of the current is $\tilde{I} = 2.0\,e^{-j\frac{\pi}{3}}\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the resistance?

- $50\,\Omega$
- $25\,\Omega$
- $43\,\Omega$
- $29\,\Omega$

## Explanation

The complex impedance is the ratio of the phasors, $Z = \dfrac{\tilde{V}}{\tilde{I}} = \dfrac{100}{2.0}\,e^{j\frac{\pi}{3}}\,\Omega = 50\,e^{j\frac{\pi}{3}}\,\Omega$.

For a resistor and an inductor in series $Z = R + j\omega L$, so the resistance is the real part of $Z$. Therefore $R = 50\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}\,\Omega = 25\,\Omega$, while the inductive reactance is $50\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}\,\Omega \approx 43\,\Omega$.
