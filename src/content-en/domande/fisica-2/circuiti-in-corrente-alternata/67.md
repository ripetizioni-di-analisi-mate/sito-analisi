Two alternating voltage sources connected in series provide the voltages $v_1(t) = 10\sin(\omega t)\,\mathrm{V}$ and $v_2(t) = 10\sin\left(\omega t + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)\,\mathrm{V}$. What is the amplitude of the total voltage $v_1 + v_2$?

- $20\,\mathrm{V}$
- $17\,\mathrm{V}$
- $10\,\mathrm{V}$
- $14\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The phasor of the sum is the vector sum of the phasors, that is, of two vectors of length $10$ forming the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

The two vectors have the same length, so their sum is the diagonal of the rhombus they form and bisects the angle between them. The length of the diagonal is therefore $2 \cdot 10\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} = 10\sqrt{3}$, and the amplitude of the total voltage is $10\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 17\,\mathrm{V}$.
