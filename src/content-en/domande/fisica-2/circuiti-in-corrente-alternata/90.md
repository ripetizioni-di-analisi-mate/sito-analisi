An ideal resistor carries a sinusoidal current. The current amplitude is known and the average power is sought. Given the resistance $R_{e} = 12\,\Omega$ and the average dissipated power $P = 150\,\mathrm W$, what is the peak current $I_{0}$?

- $5 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm A$
- $5\,\mathrm A$
- $10\,\mathrm A$
- $\dfrac{5 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm A$

## Explanation

The rms current is the peak current divided by $\sqrt2$. The average power is $R_e I_{\mathrm{eff}}^2$. In the stated units, we have $P = \dfrac{I_{0}^{2} \cdot R_{e}}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $I_{0} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{P}}{\sqrt{R_{e}}}$. With the given values, we obtain $5\,\mathrm A$.
