A charged capacitor of capacitance $C = 10\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is connected at time $t = 0$ to an ideal inductor of inductance $L = 40\,\mathrm{mH}$, in a circuit with negligible resistance. After how much time is the capacitor discharged for the first time?

- $0.99\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $0.63\,\mathrm{ms}$

## Explanation

The charge of the capacitor is $q(t) = Q_0\cos(\omega_0 t)$, with $\omega_0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$. Since $\sqrt{LC} = \sqrt{4.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 6.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$, the angular frequency is $\omega_0 \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

The charge vanishes for the first time when $\omega_0 t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, that is, after a quarter of a period. Therefore
$$
t = \frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{LC} \approx 0.99\,\mathrm{ms} .
$$
