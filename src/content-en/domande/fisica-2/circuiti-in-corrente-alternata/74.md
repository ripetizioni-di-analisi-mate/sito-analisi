A resistor of resistance $30\,\Omega$ and a capacitor of reactance $40\,\Omega$ are connected in series to a source with rms voltage $100\,\mathrm{V}$. What is the rms voltage across the capacitor?

- $57\,\mathrm{V}$
- $100\,\mathrm{V}$
- $80\,\mathrm{V}$
- $133\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The impedance of the circuit is $Z = \sqrt{30^2 + 40^2}\,\Omega = 50\,\Omega$, so the rms current is $I_{\text{eff}} = \dfrac{100}{50}\,\mathrm{A} = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$.

The rms voltage across the capacitor is the product of the reactance and the rms current, therefore $V_C = 40 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{V} = 80\,\mathrm{V}$. The rms voltage across the resistor is $60\,\mathrm{V}$, and $\sqrt{60^2 + 80^2} = 100$ gives back the source voltage.
