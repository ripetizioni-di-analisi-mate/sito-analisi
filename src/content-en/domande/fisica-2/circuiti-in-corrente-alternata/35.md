A series RLC circuit with positive resistance operates at resonance. The source frequency is reduced while the rms voltage is kept constant. How do the phase lead of the current and its rms magnitude change?

- The current lags the voltage and its magnitude decreases.
- The current leads the voltage and its magnitude increases.
- The current leads the voltage and its magnitude decreases.
- The current remains in phase and its magnitude is unchanged.

## Explanation

Below resonance, $X_L=\omega L$ decreases and $X_C=1/(\omega C)$ increases. The net reactance is negative, so the current leads. Since $|Z|=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}$ exceeds the resonant value $R$, the rms current decreases at fixed voltage.
