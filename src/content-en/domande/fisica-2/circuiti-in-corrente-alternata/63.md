A resistor and a capacitor are connected in series to an alternating voltage source of fixed amplitude, and the voltage across the capacitor is measured. How does the amplitude of this voltage change as the frequency of the source increases?

- It increases and tends to the amplitude of the source voltage
- It stays equal to the amplitude of the source voltage
- It increases without bound
- It decreases and tends to zero

## Explanation

With $X_C = \dfrac{1}{\omega C}$ the current has amplitude $I_0 = \dfrac{V_0}{\sqrt{R^2 + X_C^2}}$, so the voltage across the capacitor has amplitude
$$
V_{C} = X_C I_0 = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{\left( \frac{R}{X_C} \right)^2 + 1}} .
$$
As the frequency increases $X_C$ decreases, so $\dfrac{R}{X_C}$ grows and $V_C$ **decreases**, tending to zero. The circuit passes the low-frequency components of a signal to the capacitor and attenuates the high-frequency ones; it is a low-pass filter.
