A real coil, connected to a DC voltage of $6.0\,\mathrm{V}$, carries a steady current of $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$. Connected to an alternating voltage with rms value $10\,\mathrm{V}$ and frequency $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$, it carries the rms current $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the inductance of the coil?

- $0.16\,\mathrm{H}$
- $0.095\,\mathrm{H}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{H}$
- $0.13\,\mathrm{H}$

## Explanation

In DC at steady state the voltage across the inductor is zero, so the current is limited only by the resistance of the winding, $R = \dfrac{6.0}{0.20}\,\Omega = 30\,\Omega$.

In AC the coil is equivalent to a resistor and an inductor in series, with impedance $Z = \dfrac{10}{0.20}\,\Omega = 50\,\Omega$. Since $Z = \sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2}$, the reactance is $X_L = \sqrt{50^2 - 30^2}\,\Omega = 40\,\Omega$. Therefore
$$
L = \frac{X_L}{2\pi f} = \frac{40}{100\pi}\,\mathrm{H} \approx 0.13\,\mathrm{H} .
$$
