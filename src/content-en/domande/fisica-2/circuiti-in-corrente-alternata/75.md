A series $RLC$ circuit with $L = 0.10\,\mathrm{H}$ and $C = 100\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is supplied by the grid at $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Which statement about the circuit is true?

- It is inductive and the current lags the voltage
- It is at resonance and the current is in phase with the voltage
- It is capacitive and the current leads the voltage
- It is capacitive and the current lags the voltage

## Explanation

At the angular frequency $\omega = 100\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 314\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ the reactances are $X_L = \omega L \approx 31.4\,\Omega$ and $X_C = \dfrac{1}{\omega C} \approx 31.8\,\Omega$.

Since $X_C > X_L$, the phase angle $\varphi$ is negative and the circuit is **capacitive**, with the current leading the voltage. The natural angular frequency of the circuit is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \approx 316\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, so the grid operates slightly below resonance.
