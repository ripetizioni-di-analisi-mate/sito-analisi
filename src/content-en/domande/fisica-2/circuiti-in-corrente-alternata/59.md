An ideal inductor is supplied with a sinusoidal voltage. Rms values are used and the frequency is $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Initially, the inductance $L_{e} = \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{H}$ and the rms current $I_{e} = 3\,\mathrm A$ are given. In the second configuration, the rms current becomes $6\,\mathrm A$, with the other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $U_{e}$?

- $2$
- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The inductive reactance is $2\pi fL$. The rms voltage is its product with the rms current. Applying $U_{e} = 100 \cdot \pi \cdot I_{e} \cdot L_{e}$ gives $60 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$ before the change and $120 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm V$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
