Two alternating currents with frequency $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$ are $i_1(t) = I_0\sin(\omega t)$ and $i_2(t) = I_0\sin\left(\omega t - \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$. How long after $i_1$ does the current $i_2$ reach its maximum value?

- $6.7\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $3.3\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $1.7\,\mathrm{ms}$
- $10\,\mathrm{ms}$

## Explanation

The current $i_2$ takes the same values as $i_1$ with a delay $\Delta t$ such that $\omega\,\Delta t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The angular frequency is $\omega = 2\pi f = 100\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, so
$$
\Delta t = \frac{\pi}{3 \cdot 100\pi}\,\mathrm{s} = \frac{1}{300}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 3.3\,\mathrm{ms} .
$$
The delay is one sixth of the period $T = 20\,\mathrm{ms}$, just as the phase difference $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is one sixth of a full turn.
