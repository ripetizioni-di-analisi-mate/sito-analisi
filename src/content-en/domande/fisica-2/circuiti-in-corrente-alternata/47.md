A mass-spring oscillator has mass $m = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and spring constant $k = 80\,\mathrm{N/m}$. An ideal $LC$ circuit with inductance $L = 0.50\,\mathrm{H}$ must oscillate with the same angular frequency as the oscillator. What capacitance is needed?

- $2.5\,\mathrm{mF}$
- $100\,\mathrm{mF}$
- $12.5\,\mathrm{mF}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{mF}$

## Explanation

The angular frequency of the oscillator is $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{400}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

In the analogy between the two oscillators the mass corresponds to the inductance and the spring constant to the inverse of the capacitance, so the angular frequency of the circuit is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$. Equating the two angular frequencies gives $\dfrac{1}{LC} = \omega^2$, therefore
$$
C = \frac{1}{\omega^2 L} = \frac{1}{400 \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{F} = 5.0\,\mathrm{mF} .
$$
