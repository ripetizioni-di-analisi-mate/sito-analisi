An ideal inductor of inductance $L$ and an ideal capacitor of capacitance $C$ are connected in parallel across an alternating voltage source with rms value $V_{\text{eff}}$ and angular frequency $\omega_0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$. What is the rms current delivered by the source?

- It is infinite
- $0$
- $\dfrac{V_{\text{eff}}}{\omega_0 L}$
- $\dfrac{2V_{\text{eff}}}{\omega_0 L}$

## Explanation

The two components have the same voltage across them. The current in the inductor has rms value $\dfrac{V_{\text{eff}}}{\omega_0 L}$ and lags the voltage by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$; the current in the capacitor has rms value $\omega_0 C V_{\text{eff}}$ and leads by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Since $\omega_0 L = \dfrac{1}{\omega_0 C}$, the two rms values are equal, so the phasors of the two currents are opposite. By the junction rule the phasor of the source current is their sum, therefore the delivered current is **zero**, while each of the two branches carries the rms current $\dfrac{V_{\text{eff}}}{\omega_0 L}$.
