The voltage $v(t) = \left( 5.0 + 2.0\sin(1000\,t) \right)\mathrm{V}$, with $t$ in seconds, is applied across a capacitor of capacitance $10\,\mu\mathrm{F}$. What is the rms value of the current in the capacitor?

- $14\,\mathrm{mA}$
- $20\,\mathrm{mA}$
- $49\,\mathrm{mA}$
- $0\,\mathrm{mA}$

## Explanation

At every instant the charge on the capacitor is $q = Cv$, so the current is $i = C\,\dfrac{dv}{dt}$.

The constant component of the voltage has zero derivative and does not contribute to the current, therefore
$$
i(t) = 10 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 1000\cos(1000\,t)\,\mathrm{A} = 0.020\cos(1000\,t)\,\mathrm{A} .
$$
The current is sinusoidal with amplitude $20\,\mathrm{mA}$, so its rms value is $\dfrac{20}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{mA} \approx 14\,\mathrm{mA}$.
