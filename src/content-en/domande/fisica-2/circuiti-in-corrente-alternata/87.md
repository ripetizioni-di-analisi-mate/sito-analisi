The secondary of an ideal transformer with $\dfrac{N_1}{N_2} = 10$ supplies a resistor of resistance $8.0\,\Omega$. What resistance should a resistor connected directly to the primary source have in order to draw the same current?

- $80\,\Omega$
- $0.080\,\Omega$
- $8.0\,\Omega$
- $800\,\Omega$

## Explanation

Let $V_1$ and $I_1$ be the rms values of voltage and current at the primary. The secondary voltage is $V_2 = \dfrac{V_1}{10}$, and the current in the load is $I_2 = \dfrac{V_2}{8.0\,\Omega}$.

In the ideal transformer $I_1 = \dfrac{N_2}{N_1}\,I_2 = \dfrac{I_2}{10}$, so $\dfrac{V_1}{I_1} = 100\,\dfrac{V_2}{I_2} = 800\,\Omega$. Therefore the source carries the same current that a resistor of $800\,\Omega$ would draw, that is, the load resistance multiplied by $\left( \dfrac{N_1}{N_2} \right)^2$.
