At a point on the Earth's surface the magnetic field has a horizontal component of $3.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ pointing north and a vertical component of $4.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ pointing down. A cosmic-ray proton moves horizontally eastward with speed $v = 5.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on the proton?

- $2.4 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.2 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.6 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The two components of the field are perpendicular to each other, so the field has magnitude
$$
B = \sqrt{(3.0 \cdot 10^{-5})^2 + (4.0 \cdot 10^{-5})^2}\,\mathrm{T} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
East is perpendicular both to north and to the vertical, therefore the velocity is perpendicular to the total field.

By the **Lorentz force** law the force then has magnitude $F = e\,v B = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $5.6 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by adding the magnitudes of the components instead of combining them as vectors.
