In a region of uniform magnetic field a beam of charged particles follows a straight path. If the direction of the beam is rotated by $90^\circ$, leaving the speed unchanged, the path becomes curved. No other forces act on the particles. How was the initial velocity directed with respect to the field?

- Perpendicular to the field
- Parallel or antiparallel to the field
- At $45^\circ$ to the field
- It cannot be determined without knowing the sign of the charge

## Explanation

The magnitude of the Lorentz force is $F = |q|\,v B \sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the velocity and the field. After the rotation the path is curved, so the force is not zero and therefore $q \neq 0$ and $B \neq 0$.

Before the rotation the path is straight and no other forces act, so the magnetic force is zero. Since $q$, $v$ and $B$ are nonzero, it must be $\sin\theta = 0$, that is, $\theta = 0^\circ$ or $\theta = 180^\circ$. The velocity was therefore **parallel or antiparallel** to the field, whatever the sign of the charge.
