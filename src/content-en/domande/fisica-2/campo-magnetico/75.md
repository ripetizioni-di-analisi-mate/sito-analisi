A conducting rod of mass $m = 50\,\mathrm{g}$ rests on two smooth horizontal rails $\ell = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ apart, in a uniform vertical magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$. A generator connected to the rails drives the current $I = 10\,\mathrm{A}$ through the rod. What is the acceleration of the rod at the instant it starts from rest?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The rod is horizontal and the field is vertical, so the part of the rod between the rails is perpendicular to the field and by the **force on a straight wire** it experiences the horizontal force
$$
F = I \ell B = 10 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N} = 1.0\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The rails are smooth, and the weight and the normal force from the rails are vertical and balance each other. Therefore by Newton's second law $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{1.0}{0.050}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The value $2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is obtained by using the mass in grams.
