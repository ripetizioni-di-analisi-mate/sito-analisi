A rigid rectangular loop has sides of length $a = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $b = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and carries the current $I = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. It is in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the sides of length $a$, and the normal to the loop makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the field. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on each of the sides of length $a$?

- $0.050\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.087\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The field is perpendicular to the sides of length $a$ in every position of the loop, because the loop rotates about an axis parallel to those sides. So by the **force on a straight wire** each of them experiences a force of magnitude
$$
F = I a B = 2.0 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N} = 0.10\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The angle between the normal and the field does not appear in the magnitude of the force. It determines the lever arm $b\sin\theta$ of the couple formed by the two forces, and hence the torque $\tau = I a B \cdot b \sin\theta$. The value $0.087\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by multiplying the force by $\sin 60^\circ$.
