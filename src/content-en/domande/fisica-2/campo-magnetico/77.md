Three wires connect the same points $P$ and $Q$: a straight wire, an arc-shaped wire and a zigzag wire, all in the plane of the page. They carry the same current from $P$ to $Q$ in a uniform magnetic field into the page. How do the total magnetic forces on the three wires compare?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 4,0;0,0 0.5,-0.8 1,0 1.5,-0.8 2,0 2.5,-0.8 3,0 3.5,-0.8 4,0" curve="2-2*cos(t),1.4*sin(t),0,3.1416" punti="0,0,pieno,P;4,0,pieno,Q" etichette="-0.6,1.6,⊗;-0.6,-1.4,⊗;2,0.6,⊗;4.6,1.6,⊗;4.6,-1.4,⊗;2,-1.6,⊗" xmin=-1.1 xmax=5.1 ymin=-2 ymax=2 titolo="Three wires with the same endpoints $P$ and $Q$ in a uniform field into the page"}

- The largest force is on the longest wire
- The three forces are equal
- The force on the straight wire is zero
- The smallest force is on the arc-shaped wire

## Explanation

By the **force on a wire of arbitrary shape**, in a uniform field the force on a wire going from $P$ to $Q$ is
$$
\vec{F} = I\,\overrightarrow{PQ} \times \vec{B} ,
$$
because the integral of the infinitesimal displacements along the wire is the total displacement $\overrightarrow{PQ}$.

The three wires have the same endpoints and the same current, so the total forces are **equal**, in magnitude, direction and sense. The longest wire experiences larger forces on its individual segments, but they have different directions and their sum is the same.
