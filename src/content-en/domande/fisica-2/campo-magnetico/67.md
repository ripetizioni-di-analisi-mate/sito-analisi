In a velocity selector the electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular, and particles with speed $v_0$ pass undeflected. The directions of both fields are reversed, with their magnitudes unchanged. Which particles, entering with the same direction and sense as before, now cross the selector undeflected?

- Particles with speed $v_0$, as before
- No particle
- Particles with speed $2 v_0$
- Only particles with charge opposite to that of before

## Explanation

The electric force $q\,\vec{E}$ reverses when $\vec{E}$ is reversed, and the magnetic force $q\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ reverses when $\vec{B}$ is reversed.

Both forces reverse and keep their magnitudes, so they remain opposite to each other. The equilibrium condition $|q|\,E = |q|\,v B$ is the same as before, therefore particles with $v = \dfrac{E}{B} = v_0$, of either sign, pass undeflected. Reversing only one of the two fields would make the forces point the same way, and no particle would cross the selector in a straight line.
