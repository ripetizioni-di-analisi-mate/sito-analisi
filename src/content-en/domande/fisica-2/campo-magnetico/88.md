A compass needle has magnetic moment $\mu = 0.10\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$ and moment of inertia $J = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about its axis of rotation, and is in a uniform horizontal magnetic field $B = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$. Displaced by a small angle from its equilibrium position and released, it oscillates without friction. What is the period of the small oscillations?

- $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{s}$
- $31\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.3\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

When the needle makes the angle $\theta$ with the field, the torque of the magnetic forces has component $-\mu B \sin\theta$ along the axis of rotation, because it brings the needle back toward the field. For small angles $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, so the equation of motion of the rigid body becomes
$$
J\,\ddot{\theta} = -\mu B\,\theta .
$$
This is the equation of simple harmonic motion with angular frequency $\Omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{\mu B}{J}}$, therefore the period is
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{J}{\mu B}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-7}}{0.10 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}}}\,\mathrm{s} = 2\pi \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.3\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $2\pi$.
