A charge $q = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ moves with velocity $\vec{v} = \left( 3.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = 0.50\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the magnetic force on the charge?

- $\vec{F} = \left( -4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $\vec{F} = \left( 4.0\,\hat{\imath} - 3.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $\vec{F} = \left( 3.0\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $\vec{F} = \left( 4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the **Lorentz force** law, $\vec{F} = q\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$.

Since $\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k} = -\hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{k} = \hat{\imath}$, the cross product is
$$
\vec{v} \times \vec{B} = 0.50 \cdot 10^3 \left( 3.0\,\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{k} \right) \mathrm{T \cdot m/s} = \left( 2.0\,\hat{\imath} - 1.5\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{T \cdot m/s} .
$$
Multiplying by $q = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C}$ gives $\vec{F} = \left( 4.0\,\hat{\imath} - 3.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$. Its dot product with $\vec{v}$ is $12 - 12 = 0$, as it must be for a force perpendicular to the velocity.
