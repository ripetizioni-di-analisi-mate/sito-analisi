In the half-space $x > 0$ there is a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$ into the page, while for $x < 0$ the field is zero. A proton, of mass $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, crosses the boundary $x = 0$ with velocity perpendicular to the boundary. How long does the proton stay in the field region?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,-1.6 0,3.4" vettori="-2.2,0,-0.6,0,v" punti="-2.2,0,pieno" etichette="-2.6,-0.45,+e;0.15,3.1,x = 0;0.8,-1.2,⊗;0.8,0.3,⊗;0.8,1.8,⊗;2.2,-1.2,⊗;2.2,0.3,⊗;2.2,1.8,⊗;3.6,-1.2,⊗;3.6,0.3,⊗;3.6,1.8,⊗" xmin=-3 xmax=4.2 ymin=-1.8 ymax=3.6 titolo="The proton arrives from $x < 0$ with velocity perpendicular to the boundary of the region of uniform field into the page"}

- $3.3 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $8.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $5.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

In the field region the velocity is perpendicular to $\vec{B}$, so by **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field** the proton moves along a circle whose center lies on the line $x = 0$, because the force at the entry point is parallel to the boundary.

Therefore the proton travels half a circle and crosses the boundary again at the diametrically opposite point, with velocity opposite to the entry velocity. The time is half the period:
$$
t = \frac{T}{2} = \frac{\pi m_p}{e\,B} = \frac{\pi \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.20}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The time does not depend on the speed of the proton, because the period does not depend on it.
