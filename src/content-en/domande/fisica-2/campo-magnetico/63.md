An electron moves perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field $B = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{T}$. By what angle does its velocity rotate in a time interval of $1.0\,\mathrm{ns}$?

- About $100^\circ$
- About $16^\circ$
- About $50^\circ$
- About $200^\circ$

## Explanation

By **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field** the velocity rotates with the cyclotron angular frequency
$$
\omega = \frac{e\,B}{m_e} = \frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The motion is uniform circular motion, so the angle of rotation is proportional to the time and equals $\omega\,t \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{rad} = 1.76\,\mathrm{rad}$. In degrees this is $1.76 \cdot \dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx 100^\circ$. The value $16^\circ$ is obtained by using the frequency $f = \dfrac{\omega}{2\pi}$ instead of the angular frequency.
