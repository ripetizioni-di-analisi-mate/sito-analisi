A particle enters the field of a mass spectrometer perpendicularly. Its charge magnitude is $2\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ and its velocity is $100\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Given the radius of the path $r = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ and the particle mass $m = \dfrac{1}{50}\,\mathrm{mg}$, what is the magnetic field magnitude $B$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

The centripetal force gives $m=\lvert q\rvert Br/v$. The mass is converted from kilograms to milligrams. In the indicated units, $m = \dfrac{B \cdot r}{50}$. Solving for the unknown gives $B = \dfrac{50 \cdot m}{r}$. Using the given values, we obtain $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{T}$.
