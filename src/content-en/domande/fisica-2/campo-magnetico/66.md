In a mass spectrometer, ions starting from rest are accelerated by the same potential difference and enter the same uniform magnetic field, perpendicular to their velocity. The masses are approximated by the mass number in atomic mass units. Which pair of ions hits the plate at the same point?

- $^{1}\mathrm{H}^+$ and $^{2}\mathrm{H}^+$
- $^{2}\mathrm{H}^+$ and $^{4}\mathrm{He}^{2+}$
- $^{4}\mathrm{He}^+$ and $^{4}\mathrm{He}^{2+}$
- $^{3}\mathrm{He}^+$ and $^{4}\mathrm{He}^+$

## Explanation

For the **mass spectrometer** the radius of the semicircle is
$$
r = \frac{1}{B}\sqrt{\frac{2 m\,\Delta V}{|q|}} ,
$$
so, for the same $\Delta V$ and $B$, it depends only on the ratio $\dfrac{m}{|q|}$.

The $^{2}\mathrm{H}^+$ ion has $\dfrac{m}{|q|} = \dfrac{2\,\mathrm{u}}{e}$ and the $^{4}\mathrm{He}^{2+}$ ion has $\dfrac{m}{|q|} = \dfrac{4\,\mathrm{u}}{2e} = \dfrac{2\,\mathrm{u}}{e}$, therefore they describe the same semicircle. In the other pairs the ratios are $1$ and $2$, $4$ and $2$, $3$ and $4$ in units of $\dfrac{\mathrm{u}}{e}$, and so the impact points are different.
