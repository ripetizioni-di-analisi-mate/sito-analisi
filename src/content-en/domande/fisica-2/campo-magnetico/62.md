In a bubble chamber there is a uniform magnetic field out of the page. A particle leaves the track shown in the figure, a circular arc traveled in the direction indicated by the arrow. What is the sign of the particle's charge?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t)-1,0.52,2.62" vettori="1.73,0,2.03,-0.52" etichette="-2.75,-2.3,⊙;-1.45,-2.3,⊙;-0.15,-2.3,⊙;1.15,-2.3,⊙;2.45,-2.3,⊙;-2.75,-1.1,⊙;-1.45,-1.1,⊙;-0.15,-1.1,⊙;1.15,-1.1,⊙;2.45,-1.1,⊙;-2.75,1.1,⊙;2.45,1.1,⊙;-0.15,0.1,⊙" xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-2.8 ymax=1.6 titolo="Track of a charged particle in a uniform field out of the page: the arc is traveled from left to right, clockwise"}

- Negative
- Positive
- The particle is neutral
- It cannot be determined without knowing the mass

## Explanation

The track is curved, so the particle experiences a force and is not neutral. The force points toward the center of the arc, that is, downward at the highest point of the track, where the velocity points to the right.

With the $x$ axis to the right, the $y$ axis upward and the field $\vec{B} = B\,\hat{k}$, at that point $\vec{v} \times \vec{B} = v B\,(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k}) = -v B\,\hat{\jmath}$, pointing downward. The force $q\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ has the same direction as $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$, so $q > 0$ and the charge is **positive**. The sign depends only on the sense of rotation and on the direction of the field, not on the mass.
