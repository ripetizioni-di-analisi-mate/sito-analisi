At a point on the equator the Earth's magnetic field is horizontal and points north. A horizontal power line, stretched in the east-west direction, carries a direct current flowing east. What is the direction of the magnetic force on the line?

- Vertical, downward
- Vertical, upward
- Horizontal, toward the north
- Horizontal, toward the west

## Explanation

By the **force on a straight wire** one has $\vec{F} = I\,\vec{\ell} \times \vec{B}$, with $\vec{\ell}$ pointing in the direction of the current.

Choose $\hat{\imath}$ toward the east, $\hat{\jmath}$ toward the north and $\hat{k}$ upward. Then $\vec{\ell} = \ell\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{B} = B\,\hat{\jmath}$, so
$$
\vec{F} = I \ell B\,(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{\jmath}) = I \ell B\,\hat{k} .
$$
Therefore the force is **vertical, upward**. With the current flowing west the force would point downward.
