A straight wire carries the current $I = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$, and the vector describing it in the direction of the current is $\vec{\ell} = \left( 0.30\,\hat{\imath} + 0.40\,\hat{\jmath} \right)\mathrm{m}$. The wire is placed in the uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = 0.50\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the magnetic force on the wire?

- $-0.40\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.40\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-0.30\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-0.50\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the **force on a straight wire**, $\vec{F} = I\,\vec{\ell} \times \vec{B}$.

The component of $\vec{\ell}$ along $\hat{\imath}$ is parallel to the field, so it gives a zero cross product, since $\hat{\imath} \times \hat{\imath} = \vec{0}$. The component along $\hat{\jmath}$ remains, and since $\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{\imath} = -\hat{k}$,
$$
\vec{F} = 2.0 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 0.50\,(\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{\imath})\,\mathrm{N} = -0.40\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $-0.50\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by using the whole length of the wire, $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, as if the wire were perpendicular to the field.
