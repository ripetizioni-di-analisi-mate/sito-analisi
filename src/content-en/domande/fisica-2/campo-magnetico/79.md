A copper wire with cross section $A = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ and $n = 8.5 \cdot 10^{28}$ conduction electrons per cubic meter carries the current $I = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ and is perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the magnitude of the average magnetic force on a single conduction electron?

- $1.2 \cdot 10^{-29}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.4 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the relation between current and drift velocity,
$$
v_d = \frac{I}{n\,e\,A} = \frac{2.0}{8.5 \cdot 10^{28} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The drift velocity is parallel to the wire, hence perpendicular to the field, and by the **Lorentz force** law each electron experiences on average the force $e\,v_d B \approx 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{N}$. The sum of these forces on all the electrons of a segment of wire gives the force $I \ell B$ on the segment. The value $1.2 \cdot 10^{-29}\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by using the cross section in square meters as if it were $1.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
