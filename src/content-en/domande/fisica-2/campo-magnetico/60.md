In a velocity selector, the electric and magnetic fields are perpendicular. The beam enters perpendicular to both, so the two forces are opposite. Initially, the given quantities are the electric field magnitude $E = 120\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the magnetic field magnitude $B = \dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{T}$. In the second configuration, we change the electric field magnitude to $90\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the magnetic field magnitude to $\dfrac{3}{20}\,\mathrm{T}$, with the other data unchanged. What is the final value minus the initial value of $v$?

- $- \dfrac{45}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $400\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-200\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $200\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The path remains straight when the magnitudes $\lvert q\rvert E$ and $\lvert q\rvert vB$ are equal. The charge cancels. Applying $v = \dfrac{E}{B}$ gives $400\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before the change and $600\,\mathrm{m/s}$ afterward. The final value minus the initial value is $200\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
