In a cyclotron, protons, of mass $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, gain energy every half turn and travel ever larger semicircles in a uniform magnetic field $B = 1.5\,\mathrm{T}$. They exit when the radius of their path reaches the radius $R = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ of the machine. What is their kinetic energy at the exit?

- $54\,\mathrm{MeV}$
- $13\,\mathrm{MeV}$
- $108\,\mathrm{MeV}$
- $27\,\mathrm{MeV}$

## Explanation

At the exit the radius is $R$, so by **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field** the speed is $v = \dfrac{e B R}{m_p}$.

The kinetic energy is therefore
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} m_p v^2 = \frac{(e B R)^2}{2 m_p} = \frac{(1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.5 \cdot 0.50)^2}{2 \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 4.3 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Dividing by $1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J/eV}$ gives $K \approx 2.7 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{eV} = 27\,\mathrm{MeV}$. The final energy depends on the field and on the radius of the machine, and not on the voltage that accelerates the protons at each passage.
