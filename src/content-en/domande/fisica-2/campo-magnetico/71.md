A semiconductor strip, of width $w$ and thickness $s$, with $n$ carriers per unit volume, carries a fixed current $I$ and is placed in a uniform magnetic field $B$ perpendicular to its wide faces. Which of these quantities does not affect the Hall voltage between the edges?

- The thickness $s$
- The carrier density $n$
- The width $w$
- The magnitude $B$ of the field

## Explanation

In the steady state the Hall field is $E_H = v_d B$ and the voltage is $\Delta V_H = E_H\, w = v_d B w$.

At fixed current the drift velocity is $v_d = \dfrac{I}{n\,|q|\,w s}$, so it decreases when the width increases. Substituting gives
$$
\Delta V_H = \frac{I}{n\,|q|\,w s}\, B\, w = \frac{I B}{n\,|q|\,s} ,
$$
where the width $w$ cancels. Therefore the Hall voltage depends on $s$, $n$ and $B$, while the **width** does not affect it.
