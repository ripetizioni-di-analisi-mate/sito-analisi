The needle of a compass, free to rotate in a horizontal plane, settles with its north pole pointing toward geographic north. The Earth's magnetic field outside the Earth is modeled as the field of a bar magnet. What is located near the geographic North Pole?

- A magnetic north pole
- A point where the magnetic field is zero
- A magnetic south pole
- No magnetic pole, because the lines of the Earth's field are closed

## Explanation

A magnetic needle free to rotate settles along the field line, and its north pole indicates the direction of the field. So in the northern hemisphere the horizontal component of the Earth's field points toward geographic north.

Outside a magnet the field lines leave the north pole and enter the south pole. The lines of the Earth's field point toward geographic north, therefore they enter the Earth near the geographic North Pole, where there is a **magnetic south pole**.

Closed lines rule out the existence of an isolated magnetic pole, but a magnet, and hence also the Earth modeled as a magnet, has a north pole and a south pole.
