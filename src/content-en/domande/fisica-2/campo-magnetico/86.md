A moving-coil galvanometer has a coil of $N = 200$ turns, each of area $1.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, in a radial magnetic field of magnitude $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$. The spiral spring has torsion constant $\kappa = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m/rad}$. By what angle does the pointer rotate when the current $I = 50\,\mu\mathrm{A}$ flows in the coil?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{rad}$

## Explanation

The field is radial, so the plane of the coil always contains the direction of the field and the torque of the magnetic forces equals $N I A B$ in every position.

At equilibrium this torque equals the restoring torque of the spring $\kappa\,\phi$, therefore
$$
\phi = \frac{N A B\, I}{\kappa} = \frac{200 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.20 \cdot 50 \cdot 10^{-6}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{rad} = 0.20\,\mathrm{rad} .
$$
The value $2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$ is obtained by converting $1.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ into $10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ instead of $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
