A proton launched with velocity $\vec{v} = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field experiences no force. Launched with velocity $\vec{v} = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the same point, it experiences the force $\vec{F} = -4.8 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N}$. What is the magnetic field?

- $\vec{B} = -1.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $\vec{B} = 1.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $\vec{B} = 1.0\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $\vec{B} = -1.0\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

Along the $x$ axis the proton experiences no force, so the field is parallel to the $x$ axis and can be written $\vec{B} = B_x\,\hat{\imath}$.

The second velocity is perpendicular to the field, therefore the force has its maximum magnitude and
$$
|B_x| = \frac{F_{\max}}{e\,v} = \frac{4.8 \cdot 10^{-14}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^5}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.0\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
By the **Lorentz force** law, $\vec{F} = e\,v B_x\,(\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{\imath}) = -e\,v B_x\,\hat{k}$. The measured force points along $-\hat{k}$, so $B_x > 0$ and the field is $\vec{B} = 1.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{T}$.
