A proton, of mass $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, moves horizontally in a region where the Earth's magnetic field is horizontal, equals $5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ and is perpendicular to the velocity. What speed must the proton have for the magnetic force to have the same magnitude as its weight? Use $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $2.1 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The velocity is perpendicular to the field, so the magnetic force has magnitude $e\,v B$.

Setting this magnitude equal to the weight $m_p g$ gives
$$
v = \frac{m_p\, g}{e\,B} = \frac{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 9.81}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
A cosmic-ray proton has speeds many orders of magnitude larger, therefore for it the weight is negligible compared with the magnetic force. The value $2.1 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by dividing the mass, instead of the weight, by $e\,B$.
