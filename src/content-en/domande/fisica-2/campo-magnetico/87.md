In a DC electric motor a coil rotates in a uniform magnetic field, and a commutator reverses the current in the coil every half turn. Why is this reversal needed?

- To make the torque zero when the plane of the coil is parallel to the field
- To double the magnitude of the coil's magnetic moment
- To keep the same sense of rotation when the magnetic moment passes through the direction of the field
- To keep the magnitude of the torque constant in every position

## Explanation

The torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu} \times \vec{B}$ rotates $\vec{\mu}$ toward the direction of $\vec{B}$, which is the position of stable equilibrium.

With the current always in the same direction, once that position is passed the torque reverses and brings the coil back, which would therefore oscillate about the equilibrium. The commutator reverses the current exactly when $\vec{\mu}$ passes through the direction of $\vec{B}$; hence $\vec{\mu}$ reverses and the torque keeps acting in the same sense of rotation. The magnitude of the torque still varies, because it depends on $\sin\theta$.
