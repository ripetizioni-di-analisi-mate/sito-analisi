The coil of a loudspeaker has $N = 50$ circular turns of radius $r = 1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and carries the current $I = 0.40\,\mathrm{A}$. In the region of the coil the magnet produces a radial field, perpendicular to the wire at each of its points, of magnitude $B = 0.80\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the magnitude of the total magnetic force on the coil?

- $0.24\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.48\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the **force on a wire of arbitrary shape** each element $d\vec{\ell}$ experiences the force $I\,d\vec{\ell} \times \vec{B}$. The element is tangent to the turn and the field is radial, so the two vectors are perpendicular and the force has magnitude $I\,d\ell\,B$ and is parallel to the axis of the coil.

All the contributions have the same direction, therefore their magnitudes add up. The total length of the wire is $N \cdot 2\pi r$, and hence
$$
F = I \cdot 2\pi r N \cdot B = 0.40 \cdot 2\pi \cdot 0.015 \cdot 50 \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.5\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The total force on a closed loop is zero in a uniform field, and the radial field is not uniform, because its direction changes from point to point. The value $0.24\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by using the radius instead of the length $2\pi r$ of one turn.
