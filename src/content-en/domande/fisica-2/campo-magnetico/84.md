A current-carrying loop can rotate in a uniform magnetic field, and $\theta \in [0^\circ, 180^\circ]$ is the angle between the normal to the loop and the field. For which values of $\theta$ is the magnitude of the torque of the magnetic forces half of its maximum value?

- $30^\circ$ and $150^\circ$
- $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$
- $45^\circ$ and $135^\circ$
- $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$

## Explanation

The torque has magnitude $\tau = \mu B \sin\theta$, so its maximum value is $\mu B$ and occurs at $\theta = 90^\circ$.

The magnitude is half the maximum if and only if $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$. In the interval $[0^\circ, 180^\circ]$ this equation has the solutions $\theta = 30^\circ$ and $\theta = 150^\circ$. The values $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$ instead solve $\cos\theta = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, they confuse the torque with the energy $U = -\mu B\cos\theta$.
