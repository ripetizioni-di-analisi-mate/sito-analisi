A particle enters the field of a mass spectrometer perpendicularly. Its charge magnitude is $2\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ and its velocity is $100\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Given the magnetic field magnitude $B = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{T}$ and the particle mass $m = \dfrac{1}{50}\,\mathrm{mg}$, what is the radius of the path $r$?

- $4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The centripetal force gives $m=\lvert q\rvert Br/v$. The mass is converted from kilograms to milligrams. In the indicated units, $m = \dfrac{B \cdot r}{50}$. Solving for the unknown gives $r = \dfrac{50 \cdot m}{B}$. Using the given values, we obtain $2\,\mathrm{m}$.
