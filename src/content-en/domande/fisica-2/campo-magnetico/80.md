An electron and a positron, which have the same mass and opposite charges, start from the same point with the same velocity, perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field. Their weight and their mutual interaction are neglected. What happens?

- They travel along the same circle in opposite directions
- They travel along two circles of equal radius, tangent at the starting point, and pass through it together after one period
- They travel along two circles of different radii, because their charges have opposite signs
- They meet after half a period, at the point diametrically opposite to the starting point

## Explanation

The two particles have the same mass, the same velocity and charges of equal magnitude, so by **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field** they have the same radius $r = \dfrac{m v}{e\,B}$ and the same period $T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{e\,B}$.

At the starting point the magnetic forces are opposite, because the charges have opposite signs. Therefore the centers of the two circles lie on opposite sides of the initial velocity, and the two circles are tangent at the starting point.

Two tangent circles have only the point of tangency in common, so the particles are at the same point again only when both have completed a full turn, that is, after one period. After half a period each one is at the point of its own circle opposite to the starting point, and the two particles are $4r$ apart.
