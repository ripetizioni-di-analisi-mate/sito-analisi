A Hall probe is calibrated so that it reads the magnitude of the magnetic field when the field is perpendicular to the broad faces of the strip. The probe is placed in a uniform field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$, with the normal to the broad faces making an angle of $60^\circ$ with the field. What value does the probe read?

- $0.40\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.35\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

Choose the $x$ axis along the current, the $y$ axis along the width and the $z$ axis normal to the broad faces. The carriers move along $x$, and since $\hat{\imath} \times \vec{B} = -B_z\,\hat{\jmath} + B_y\,\hat{k}$, the magnetic force along the width depends only on the component $B_z$.

The Hall voltage is measured between the edges, that is, along the width, so by the **Hall effect** it is proportional to $B_z$. The normal component is $B_z = B\cos 60^\circ = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$, therefore the probe reads $0.20\,\mathrm{T}$.

The value $0.35\,\mathrm{T}$ is obtained by using $\sin 60^\circ$, that is, the component of the field parallel to the faces.
