In the Bohr model an electron travels a circular orbit with uniform motion, and its orbital angular momentum has magnitude $L = 2.1 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic moment associated with the orbit?

- $1.8 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$
- $3.7 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$
- $9.2 \cdot 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

Let $r$ be the radius of the orbit and $v$ the speed. The electron passes through each point of the orbit once per period, so the motion is equivalent to a loop carrying the current $I = \dfrac{e v}{2\pi r}$, and the **magnetic moment** is
$$
\mu = I\,\pi r^2 = \frac{e v r}{2} .
$$
The angular momentum is $L = m_e v r$, therefore $v r = \dfrac{L}{m_e}$ and
$$
\mu = \frac{e\,L}{2 m_e} = \frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.1 \cdot 10^{-34}}{2 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} .
$$
The value $3.7 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $1.0 \cdot 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$ by using the mass of the proton.
