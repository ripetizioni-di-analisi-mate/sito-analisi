A rigid square loop lies in the plane of the page, with its sides parallel to the edges of the page, and carries a current counterclockwise as seen by the reader. It is in a uniform magnetic field parallel to the page and pointing to the right. The loop is free to rotate about its center and starts from rest. How does it begin to rotate?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 unicolore=1 linee="0,0 2,0 2,2 0,2 0,0" vettori="2.3,0.6,2.3,1.4,I;-1.2,2.6,3.2,2.6,B;-1.2,-0.6,3.2,-0.6" xmin=-1.6 xmax=3.6 ymin=-1 ymax=3 titolo="Square loop in the plane of the page, carrying a counterclockwise current, in a uniform field parallel to the page and pointing to the right"}

- The right side moves out of the page and the left side moves into the page
- The right side moves into the page and the left side moves out of the page
- The upper side moves out of the page and the lower side moves into the page
- The loop rotates clockwise in the plane of the page

## Explanation

Choose $\hat{\imath}$ to the right, $\hat{\jmath}$ upward and $\hat{k}$ out of the page, so $\vec{B} = B\,\hat{\imath}$. The upper and lower sides are parallel to the field, therefore by the **force on a straight wire** they experience no force.

With a counterclockwise current, in the right side the current flows upward and experiences the force $I a B\,(\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{\imath}) = -I a B\,\hat{k}$, into the page. In the left side the current flows downward, so the force is $I a B\,\hat{k}$, out of the page.

The two forces form a couple, therefore **the right side moves into the page and the left side moves out of the page**. The result agrees with $\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu} \times \vec{B}$: the magnetic moment points out of the page and rotates toward the direction of the field.
