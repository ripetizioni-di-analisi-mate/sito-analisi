Two charge distributions are superimposed on a ring of radius $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. The measurement point is on the axis at a distance of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center. The charges are uniform and positive. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the charge of the first distribution $q_{1} = 3\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the charge of the second distribution $q_{2} = 2\,\mathrm{nC}$, what is the electric field magnitude $E$?

- $2880\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $14400\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $28800\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $18000\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

The transverse components cancel by symmetry. Each element is at a distance of $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the point and the axial projection factor is $4/5$. The axial contributions point in the same direction. In the indicated units, $E = 2880 \cdot q_{1} + 2880 \cdot q_{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $14400\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
