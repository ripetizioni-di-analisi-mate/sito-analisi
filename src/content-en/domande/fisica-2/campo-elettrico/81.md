An infinite straight wire has uniform linear charge density $\lambda = 1.0\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. An electron moves, in the plane perpendicular to the wire, along a circular orbit of radius $r = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ centered on the wire, subject only to the electric force. What is its speed?

- $1.3 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.2 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

At distance $r$ the wire produces a radial field of magnitude $\dfrac{2k\lambda}{r}$, pointing outward because $\lambda > 0$. So the electron experiences a force of magnitude $\dfrac{2ke\lambda}{r}$ directed toward the wire, which acts as the centripetal force.

From the condition $\dfrac{m_e v^2}{r} = \dfrac{2ke\lambda}{r}$ one obtains $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2ke\lambda}{m_e}}$, which does not depend on the radius of the orbit.

Therefore
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
