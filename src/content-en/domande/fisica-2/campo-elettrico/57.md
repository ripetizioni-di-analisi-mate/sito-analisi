To compute the field on the axis of a disk of radius $R$ with uniform surface density $\sigma$, the disk is divided into concentric rings of radius $s$ and width $ds$. What is the charge $dq$ of one of these rings?

- $dq = \pi\sigma\,s^2\,ds$
- $dq = 2\pi\sigma\,ds$
- $dq = \sigma\,s\,ds$
- $dq = 2\pi\sigma\,s\,ds$

## Explanation

The charge of an element is written with the density as $dq = \sigma\,dA$, so the area of the ring is needed.

The ring has length $2\pi s$ and width $ds$, therefore its area is $dA = 2\pi s\,ds$, which is also the differential of the area $\pi s^2$ of the disk of radius $s$.

Hence $dq = 2\pi\sigma\,s\,ds$; integrating from $0$ to $R$ gives the total charge $\pi R^2 \sigma$, as it must.
