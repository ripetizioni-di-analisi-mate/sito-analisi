A thin rod lying on the $x$-axis between $x = 0$ and $x = L = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ has linear charge density $\lambda(x) = \alpha x$, with $\alpha = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$. What is the total charge?

- $1.8\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $0.45\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The density is not uniform, so the total charge is obtained by integrating $dq = \lambda(x)\,dx$ along the rod.

We have
$$
Q = \int_0^L \alpha x\,dx = \frac{\alpha L^2}{2} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot 0.30^2}{2}\,\mathrm{C} = 9.0 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{C} .
$$
Therefore the total charge is $0.90\,\mathrm{nC}$, half of what the rod would have with the uniform density $\alpha L$.
