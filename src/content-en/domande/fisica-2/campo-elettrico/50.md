Three point charges occupy the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side $a$: two are $+q$ and the third is $-q$, with $q > 0$. What are the magnitude and direction of the electric field at the centroid of the triangle?

- $6\,\dfrac{kq}{a^2}$, away from the vertex with the charge $-q$
- $3\,\dfrac{kq}{a^2}$, toward the vertex with the charge $-q$
- $6\,\dfrac{kq}{a^2}$, toward the vertex with the charge $-q$
- $0$

## Explanation

With three equal charges $+q$ the field at the centroid is zero, because the three fields have the same magnitude and form angles of $120^\circ$. The given configuration is obtained from that one by adding a charge $-2q$ at the vertex of the negative charge, so by the **superposition principle** the field is that of the charge $-2q$ alone.

The centroid is at distance $r = \dfrac{a}{\sqrt{3}}$ from the vertices, therefore the magnitude is
$$
E = k\,\frac{2q}{r^2} = k\,\frac{2q}{a^2 / 3} = 6\,\frac{kq}{a^2} .
$$
The field of a negative charge points inward, so it is directed toward the vertex with the charge $-q$.
