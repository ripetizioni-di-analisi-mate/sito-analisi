Two point charges $+3q$ and $-q$, with $q > 0$, are held at distance $d$ from each other. Which field approximates their field at points at distance $r \gg d$?

- The field of a dipole of moment $qd$, which decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^3}$
- The field of a point charge $+4q$, which decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$
- The field of a point charge $+3q$, which decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$
- The field of a point charge $+2q$, which decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$

## Explanation

For $r \gg d$ the distances of a point from the two charges are almost equal to $r$, so the two fields have almost the same direction and magnitudes of about $k\,\dfrac{3q}{r^2}$ and $k\,\dfrac{q}{r^2}$.

The field of $+3q$ points outward and that of $-q$ points inward, therefore the two contributions subtract and the resulting field is about $k\,\dfrac{2q}{r^2}$, pointing outward.

Hence at large distance the system produces the field of a point charge equal to the **total charge** $+2q$. The dipole term, which decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^3}$, dominates only when the total charge is zero.
