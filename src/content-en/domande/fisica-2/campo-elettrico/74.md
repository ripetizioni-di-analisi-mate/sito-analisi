An infinite plane with uniform surface density $\sigma > 0$ has a circular hole of radius $R$. What is the magnitude of the field on the axis of the hole, at distance $z = R$ from its center?

- $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right)$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\sqrt{2}\,\varepsilon_0}$

## Explanation

The plane with the hole is equivalent to the complete plane with density $\sigma$ plus a disk of radius $R$ with density $-\sigma$ in place of the hole, so by the **superposition principle** the field is the difference between the field of the plane and that of the disk.

The plane gives $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ and the disk gives $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left( 1 - \dfrac{z}{\sqrt{z^2 + R^2}} \right)$ in the same sense, therefore
$$
E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2 + R^2}} .
$$
For $z = R$ the fraction equals $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, so $E = \dfrac{\sigma}{2\sqrt{2}\,\varepsilon_0}$.
