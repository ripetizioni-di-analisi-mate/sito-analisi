Two field contributions at the measurement point are directed along the positive Cartesian axes, one along $x$ and one along $y$. Given the component along $x$ $E_{x} = 120\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the component along $y$ $E_{y} = 50\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the electric field magnitude $E$?

- $170\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $130\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $70\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $260\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

The total field is the vector sum of the two contributions. Its magnitude is the norm of the vector of components. In the indicated units, $E = \sqrt{E_{x}^{2} + E_{y}^{2}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $130\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
