A charge $q_1 = 9.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is fixed at $x = 0$ and a charge $q_2 = -4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is fixed at $x = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$. At which point of the $x$-axis, at a finite distance, is the electric field zero?

- $x = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $x = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $x = -20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $x = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Between the charges both fields point toward $q_2$, so they do not cancel. To the left of $q_1$ the field of $q_1$, larger in magnitude, prevails over that of $q_2$, which is farther away. Therefore the point sought is to the right of $q_2$, that is, with $x > 10\,\mathrm{cm}$.

There the fields have opposite senses and the magnitudes are equal if
$$
\frac{9}{x^2} = \frac{4}{(x - 10)^2} ,
$$
with $x$ in centimeters. Taking the square root gives $\dfrac{3}{x} = \dfrac{2}{x - 10}$, so $3x - 30 = 2x$ and $x = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$, $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ beyond the negative charge.
