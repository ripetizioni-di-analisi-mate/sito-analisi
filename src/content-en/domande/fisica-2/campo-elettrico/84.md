A positive point charge is at a distance of $r$ from a measurement point. The charge is replaced by another of twice the magnitude and the distance is increased by a factor of $b$. Given the initial field magnitude $E_{0} = 72\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the new field magnitude $E = 16\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the ratio of final to initial distance $b$?

- $9$
- $\dfrac{3 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $6$
- $3$

## Explanation

The field is proportional to the source charge and inversely proportional to the square of the distance. The two factors are applied together. In the indicated units, $E = \dfrac{2 \cdot E_{0}}{b^{2}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $b = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{E_{0}}}{\sqrt{E}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $3$.
