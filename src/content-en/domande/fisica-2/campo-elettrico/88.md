Denoting by $\sigma$ a surface charge density, by $\lambda$ a linear density, by $p$ a dipole moment and by $r$ a distance, which of the following expressions has the dimensions of an electric field?

- $\dfrac{\lambda}{\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{p}{\varepsilon_0\,r^2}$
- $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{\sigma\,r}{\varepsilon_0}$

## Explanation

From the formula for a point charge $E = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\dfrac{q}{r^2}$, a field has the dimensions of a charge divided by $\varepsilon_0$ and by a squared length.

The surface density $\sigma$ is a charge divided by an area, so $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ has the dimensions of a field, as the field of the plane $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ confirms.

The other expressions have an extra length in the numerator: $\lambda$ is a charge per unit length, $p$ is a charge times a length and $\sigma r$ is a charge per unit length.
