An oil drop of mass $m = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{kg}$ stays at rest between two horizontal plates, where there is a uniform vertical field of magnitude $E = 3.27 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N/C}$ pointing downward. The buoyancy of the air is neglected and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the charge of the drop?

- $+3e$
- $-6e$
- $-2e$
- $-3e$

## Explanation

The drop is in equilibrium, so the electric force balances the weight and points upward. The field points downward, therefore the charge is **negative**.

From the equality of magnitudes $|q|\,E = m g$ we get
$$
|q| = \frac{m g}{E} = \frac{1.6 \cdot 10^{-15} \cdot 9.81}{3.27 \cdot 10^{4}}\,\mathrm{C} = 4.8 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{4.8 \cdot 10^{-19}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}} = 3$, the charge of the drop is $-3e$.
