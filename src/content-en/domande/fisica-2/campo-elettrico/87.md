A straight wire of length $2\ell$ has charge $Q$ uniformly distributed. On the perpendicular to the wire through its midpoint, at distance $r$, the field is $E = \dfrac{2k\lambda\,\ell}{r\sqrt{r^2 + \ell^2}}$, with $\lambda = \dfrac{Q}{2\ell}$. How does $E$ depend on $r$ for $r \ll \ell$ and for $r \gg \ell$?

- For $r \ll \ell$ as $\dfrac{1}{r}$, for $r \gg \ell$ as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$
- For $r \ll \ell$ as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$, for $r \gg \ell$ as $\dfrac{1}{r}$
- As $\dfrac{1}{r}$ both for $r \ll \ell$ and for $r \gg \ell$
- As $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$ both for $r \ll \ell$ and for $r \gg \ell$

## Explanation

For $r \ll \ell$ the term $r^2$ is negligible compared with $\ell^2$, so $\sqrt{r^2 + \ell^2} \approx \ell$ and $E \approx \dfrac{2k\lambda}{r}$. In this region the wire has the field of an infinite wire, which decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r}$.

For $r \gg \ell$ instead $\sqrt{r^2 + \ell^2} \approx r$, so $E \approx \dfrac{2k\lambda\,\ell}{r^2} = \dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$.

Therefore far from the wire the field is that of a point charge $Q$ and decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$.
