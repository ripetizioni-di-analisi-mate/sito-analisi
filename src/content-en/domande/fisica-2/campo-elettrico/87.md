A negatively charged particle crosses two regions in which the uniform field points in the same direction. The second region has a weaker field. The ratio of charge magnitude to mass is $2\,\mathrm{C/kg}$. Given the field magnitude in the first region $E_{1} = 10\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the decrease in acceleration magnitude $a = 12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the field magnitude in the second region $E_{2}$?

- $4\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $8\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $2\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $6\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

By Newton's second law, the acceleration magnitude is the product of the field magnitude and the charge-to-mass ratio. In the indicated units, $a = 2 \cdot E_{1} - 2 \cdot E_{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $E_{2} = E_{1} - \dfrac{a}{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $4\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
