A point charge $q = 10\,\mathrm{nC}$ produces at the origin the field $\vec{E} = \left( -600\,\hat{\imath} + 800\,\hat{\jmath} \right)\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Where is the charge located?

- $(-0.18\,\mathrm{m},\, 0.24\,\mathrm{m})$
- $(0.18\,\mathrm{m},\, -0.24\,\mathrm{m})$
- $(0.24\,\mathrm{m},\, -0.18\,\mathrm{m})$
- $(0.54\,\mathrm{m},\, -0.72\,\mathrm{m})$

## Explanation

The field has magnitude $E = \sqrt{600^2 + 800^2}\,\mathrm{N/C} = 1000\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and unit vector $\left( -\dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$.

From the formula for the field of a point charge we get the distance $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{kq}{E}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-8}}{1000}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.

The charge is positive, so the field points away from the charge and the charge lies on the side opposite to the sense of the field. Therefore its position is $0.30\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \left( \dfrac{3}{5}, -\dfrac{4}{5} \right) = (0.18\,\mathrm{m},\, -0.24\,\mathrm{m})$.
