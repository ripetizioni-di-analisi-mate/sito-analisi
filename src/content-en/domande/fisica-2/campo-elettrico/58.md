A dipole has moment of magnitude $p = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C\cdot m}$. What is the field at a point of the perpendicular bisector, that is, of the line perpendicular to the dipole axis through its center, at distance $r = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center, much larger than the separation of the charges?

- $1.7 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$, opposite to $\vec{p}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$, in the same direction as $\vec{p}$
- $3.3 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$, in the same direction as $\vec{p}$
- $3.3 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$, opposite to $\vec{p}$

## Explanation

On the perpendicular bisector, for $r \gg d$, the dipole field is $\vec{E} \approx -\dfrac{k\,\vec{p}}{r^3}$.

The magnitude is
$$
E = \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-12}}{0.060^3}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C} .
$$
The minus sign indicates that the field is **opposite to** $\vec{p}$, because the components perpendicular to the dipole axis cancel and the parallel components point from the positive charge toward the negative one. The value $3.3 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$ is the field on the dipole axis at the same distance.
