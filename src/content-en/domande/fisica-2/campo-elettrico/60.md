In the water molecule each of the two O-H bonds has a dipole moment of magnitude $p_0 = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\cdot m}$, directed along the bond, and the angle between the two bonds is $104.5^\circ$. What is the magnitude of the dipole moment of the molecule?

- $6.1 \cdot 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\cdot m}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-29}\,\mathrm{C\cdot m}$
- $7.9 \cdot 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\cdot m}$
- $3.1 \cdot 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\cdot m}$

## Explanation

The dipole moment of a system of charges is the vector sum of the moments of its parts, so the moment of the molecule is $\vec{p} = \vec{p}_1 + \vec{p}_2$.

The two vectors have the same magnitude and form an angle of $104.5^\circ$, therefore the components perpendicular to the bisector of the angle cancel. Each vector forms an angle of $52.25^\circ$ with the bisector, so the components along the bisector are $p_0 \cos 52.25^\circ$ and they add.

Therefore
$$
p = 2\,p_0 \cos 52.25^\circ = 2 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-30} \cdot 0.612\,\mathrm{C\cdot m} \approx 6.1 \cdot 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\cdot m} ,
$$
directed along the bisector of the angle between the bonds.
