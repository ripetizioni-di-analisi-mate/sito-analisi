A semi-infinite straight wire, with uniform linear density $\lambda > 0$, starts at the point $O$. Consider the point $P$ on the perpendicular to the wire through $O$, at distance $r$ from $O$. What are the magnitude and direction of the field at $P$?

- $\dfrac{k\lambda}{r}$, perpendicular to the wire
- $\sqrt{2}\,\dfrac{k\lambda}{r}$, inclined at $45^\circ$ to the perpendicular to the wire
- $2\,\dfrac{k\lambda}{r}$, perpendicular to the wire
- $\dfrac{k\lambda}{r}$, parallel to the wire

## Explanation

We place the wire on the $x$-axis for $x \geq 0$ and $P = (0, r)$. The element $\lambda\,dx$ is at distance $\sqrt{x^2 + r^2}$ from $P$. The whole wire lies on one side of $P$, so the component parallel to the wire does not cancel and both components are computed.

The perpendicular component is
$$
E_y = k\lambda\,r \int_0^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{\left( x^2 + r^2 \right)^{\frac{3}{2}}} = \frac{k\lambda}{r} ,
$$
and the parallel component, directed away from the wire, is
$$
|E_x| = k\lambda \int_0^{+\infty} \frac{x\,dx}{\left( x^2 + r^2 \right)^{\frac{3}{2}}} = \frac{k\lambda}{r} .
$$
The two components are equal, therefore the field has magnitude $\sqrt{2}\,\dfrac{k\lambda}{r}$ and is inclined at $45^\circ$ to the perpendicular to the wire.
