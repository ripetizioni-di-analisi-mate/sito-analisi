A thin ring of radius $R$ has the upper half with charge $+q$ and the lower half with charge $-q$, each uniformly distributed. What are the magnitude and direction of the electric field at the center $O$?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,3.1416;cos(t),sin(t),3.1416,6.2832" punti="0,0,pieno,O" etichette="0,1.3,+q;0,-1.35,−q;0.35,0.2,R" segmenti="0,0,0.71,0.71" xmin=-1.8 xmax=1.8 ymin=-1.7 ymax=1.7 titolo="Ring of radius $R$: the upper half has charge $+q$, the lower half $-q$, both uniformly distributed"}

- $\dfrac{4kq}{\pi R^2}$, toward the negative half
- $\dfrac{4kq}{\pi R^2}$, toward the positive half
- $\dfrac{2kq}{\pi R^2}$, toward the negative half
- $0$

## Explanation

Each half-ring has density $\lambda = \dfrac{q}{\pi R}$. For a semicircle the components parallel to the diameter cancel by symmetry, and the perpendicular component is
$$
\int_0^{\pi} \frac{k\lambda}{R}\sin\varphi\,d\varphi = \frac{2k\lambda}{R} = \frac{2kq}{\pi R^2} .
$$
The positive half produces at $O$ an outward field, pointing downward; the negative half produces an inward field, also pointing downward. The two fields have the same sense and add.

Therefore the field at the center has magnitude $\dfrac{4kq}{\pi R^2}$ and points toward the **negative half**.
