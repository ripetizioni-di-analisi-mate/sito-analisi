A beam of identical particles enters horizontally two consecutive regions of uniform vertical electric field. In the first region the vertical acceleration of the particles is $+a$ for a time $\tau$, in the second it is $-a$ for the same time $\tau$. At the entrance the vertical velocity is zero and gravity is negligible. How does the beam leave the second region?

- Horizontal, at the entrance height
- Horizontal, displaced upward by $a\tau^2$
- Tilted upward, with vertical velocity $2a\tau$
- Horizontal, displaced upward by $\dfrac{a\tau^2}{2}$

## Explanation

In the first region the vertical motion is uniformly accelerated with zero initial velocity, so at the exit the vertical velocity is $a\tau$ and the upward displacement is $\dfrac{a\tau^2}{2}$.

In the second region the acceleration is $-a$ for the time $\tau$, therefore the vertical velocity returns to $a\tau - a\tau = 0$ and the beam leaves horizontally. During this stretch the vertical velocity remains positive, so the beam keeps rising, by $a\tau \cdot \tau - \dfrac{a\tau^2}{2} = \dfrac{a\tau^2}{2}$.

The total displacement is therefore $\dfrac{a\tau^2}{2} + \dfrac{a\tau^2}{2} = a\tau^2$ upward. The answer "at the entrance height" comes from confusing the vanishing of the vertical velocity with the vanishing of the displacement.
