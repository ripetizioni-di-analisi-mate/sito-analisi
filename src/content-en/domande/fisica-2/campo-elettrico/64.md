An initially stationary positive charge is free in a uniform field. Other forces are neglected. Its mass is $2\,\mathrm{mg}$. Initially, the given quantities are the charge $q = 4\,\mathrm{\mu C}$, the field magnitude $E = 30\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the elapsed time $t = \dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{s}$. In the second configuration, we change the elapsed time to $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{s}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $v$?

- $1$
- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$

## Explanation

The constant acceleration is $qE/m$ and the velocity increases from zero. Here, the ratio of a charge in microcoulombs to a mass in milligrams is directly in coulombs per kilogram. Applying $v = \dfrac{E \cdot q \cdot t}{2}$ gives $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before the change and $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ afterward. The ratio is $2$.
