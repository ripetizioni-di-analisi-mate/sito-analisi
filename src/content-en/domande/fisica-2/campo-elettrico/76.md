An infinite slab of thickness $2a$ has uniform volume charge density $\rho > 0$. Thinking of the slab as a superposition of thin planes, what is the magnitude of the field outside the slab?

- $\dfrac{\rho\,a}{\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{\rho\,a}{2\varepsilon_0}$
- $\dfrac{2\rho\,a}{\varepsilon_0}$
- $0$

## Explanation

A thin layer of thickness $dx$ has surface density $\rho\,dx$, so it produces a field of magnitude $\dfrac{\rho\,dx}{2\varepsilon_0}$, which does not depend on the distance.

At an outside point all the layers lie on the same side, therefore their fields have the same sense and add. The total thickness is $2a$, so
$$
E = \frac{\rho \cdot 2a}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\rho\,a}{\varepsilon_0} .
$$
Outside, the slab behaves like a plane with surface density $2\rho a$, and the field is uniform.
