An infinite plane has uniform surface density $\sigma = 1.77\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$. An electron is released from rest $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the plane. After how long does it reach the plane, considering only the electric force?

- $2.4 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4.8 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.4 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The plane produces a uniform field $E = \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \dfrac{1.77 \cdot 10^{-9}}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C} = 100\,\mathrm{N/C}$, so the electron, attracted by the positive plane, has constant acceleration.

The acceleration is $a = \dfrac{eE}{m_e} = \dfrac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 100}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^{13}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The motion starts from rest, therefore $d = \dfrac{1}{2}at^2$ and
$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2d}{a}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 0.010}{1.76 \cdot 10^{13}}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 3.4 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
