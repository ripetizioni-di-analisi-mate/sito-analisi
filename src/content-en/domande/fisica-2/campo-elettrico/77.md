Two field contributions at the measurement point are directed along the positive Cartesian axes, one along $x$ and one along $y$. Initially, the given quantities are the component along $x$ $E_{x} = 120\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the component along $y$ $E_{y} = 50\,\mathrm{N/C}$. In the second configuration, we change the component along $y$ to $90\,\mathrm{N/C}$, with all other data unchanged. What is the ratio of the final value to the initial value of $E$?

- $\dfrac{3}{7}$
- $\dfrac{13}{15}$
- $\dfrac{15}{13}$
- $\dfrac{21}{17}$

## Explanation

The total field is the vector sum of the two contributions. Its magnitude is the norm of the vector of components. Applying $E = \sqrt{E_{x}^{2} + E_{y}^{2}}$ gives $130\,\mathrm{N/C}$ before the change and $150\,\mathrm{N/C}$ afterward. The ratio is $\dfrac{15}{13}$.
