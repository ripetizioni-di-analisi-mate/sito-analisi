Two infinite parallel planes have the same uniform surface density $\sigma = 4.43\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$. What is the field between the planes and outside?

- Between the planes $5.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$, outside $0$
- Between the planes $0$, outside $2.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- Between the planes $0$, outside $5.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$ both between the planes and outside

## Explanation

Each plane produces a uniform field of magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$, pointing outward because $\sigma > 0$.

Between the planes the two fields have opposite senses and cancel; outside they have the same sense, pointing away from the pair of planes, and they add. Therefore outside
$$
E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{4.43 \cdot 10^{-9}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 5.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C} ,
$$
while between the planes the field is **zero**. The situation is the opposite of that of two planes with opposite charges.
