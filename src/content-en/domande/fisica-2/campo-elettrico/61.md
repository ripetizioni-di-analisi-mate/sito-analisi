A small dipole is near a fixed point charge $+Q$, with its axis on the line through the charges and $\vec{p}$ pointing away from the charge, as in the figure. What is the direction of the net force on the dipole?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,+Q;2.6,0,pieno,−q;3.4,0,pieno,+q" segmenti="0,0,2.6,0" vettori="2.6,0.45,3.4,0.45,p" xmin=-0.6 xmax=4.2 ymin=-0.9 ymax=1.1 titolo="Dipole aligned with the line through the charges, with $\vec{p}$ pointing away from the charge $Q$"}

- Away from the charge $Q$
- The net force is zero
- Toward the charge $Q$
- Perpendicular to the line through the charges

## Explanation

The vector $\vec{p}$ goes from the negative charge to the positive one and points away from $Q$, so the charge $-q$ of the dipole is the closer one to $Q$.

The charge $-q$ is attracted by $Q$ and the charge $+q$ is repelled, and the magnitudes of the two forces are $\dfrac{kQq}{r_1^2}$ and $\dfrac{kQq}{r_2^2}$ with $r_1 < r_2$. The attractive force is therefore stronger than the repulsive one.

Therefore the net force is directed **toward the charge** $Q$. In general, in a nonuniform field a dipole aligned with the field experiences a force directed toward the region where the field is stronger.
