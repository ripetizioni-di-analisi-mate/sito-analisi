Two charge distributions are superimposed on a ring of radius $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. The measurement point is on the axis at a distance of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center. The charges are uniform and positive. Use $k=9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$. Given the charge of the first distribution $q_{1} = 3\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the electric field magnitude $E = 14400\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the charge of the second distribution $q_{2}$?

- $3\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $1\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $4\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $2\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

The transverse components cancel by symmetry. Each element is at a distance of $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the point and the axial projection factor is $4/5$. The axial contributions point in the same direction. In the indicated units, $E = 2880 \cdot q_{1} + 2880 \cdot q_{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $q_{2} = \dfrac{E}{2880} - q_{1}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $2\,\mathrm{nC}$.
