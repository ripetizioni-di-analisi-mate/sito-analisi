A straight wire $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ long has uniform linear density $\lambda = 2.0\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. What is the field at a point at distance $r = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire, on the perpendicular through its midpoint?

- $2.4 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $3.2 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

The wire has half-length $\ell = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the point lies on the bisector of the wire, so the formula for the **field of a straight wire** of length $2\ell$ applies:
$$
E = \frac{2k\lambda\,\ell}{r\sqrt{r^2 + \ell^2}} .
$$
Since $\sqrt{0.15^2 + 0.20^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$, we get
$$
E = \frac{2 \cdot 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 0.20}{0.15 \cdot 0.25}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 1.9 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C} .
$$
The infinite wire would give $\dfrac{2k\lambda}{r} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$, but the infinite-wire approximation is not adequate, because here $r$ is not small compared with the length of the wire.
