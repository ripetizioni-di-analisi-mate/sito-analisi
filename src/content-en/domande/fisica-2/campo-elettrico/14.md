On a thin rod of length $L$ the linear charge density grows with the distance $x$ from one end according to $\lambda(x) = \alpha x$, with $\alpha > 0$ constant. What fraction of the total charge lies on the half of the rod farther from that end?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

The charge on the segment between $0$ and $x$ is obtained by integrating the linear density:
$$
q(x) = \int_0^x \alpha s\,ds = \frac{\alpha x^2}{2} .
$$
Therefore the total charge is $q(L) = \dfrac{\alpha L^2}{2}$, while the half near the end contains $q\!\left(\dfrac{L}{2}\right) = \dfrac{\alpha L^2}{8}$, that is, one quarter of the total. The far half therefore contains **three quarters** of the charge, $\dfrac{3}{4}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ corresponds to a uniform density, and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ is the fraction on the half near the end.
