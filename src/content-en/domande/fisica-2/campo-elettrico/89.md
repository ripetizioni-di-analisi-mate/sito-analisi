A positive charge passes between two regions in which the uniform fields are parallel and point in the same direction. The second field is stronger than the first. Given the field magnitude in the second region $E_{2} = 500\,\mathrm{N/C}$, the field magnitude in the first region $E_{1} = 200\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the increase in force magnitude $d = 900\,\mathrm{\mu N}$, what is the charge $q$?

- $\dfrac{9}{5}\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $6\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$
- $\dfrac{9}{7}\,\mathrm{\mu C}$

## Explanation

In each region, the force is $qE$. The charge is the same, so the increase is proportional to the difference in field magnitudes. In the indicated units, $d = q \cdot \left(- E_{1} + E_{2}\right)$. Solving for the unknown gives $q = - \dfrac{d}{E_{1} - E_{2}}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$.
