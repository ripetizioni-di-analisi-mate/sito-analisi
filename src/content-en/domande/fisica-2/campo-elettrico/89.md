An infinite plane with uniform surface density $\sigma$ is perpendicular to a uniform external field. The total field is $300\,\mathrm{N/C}$ on one side of the plane and $500\,\mathrm{N/C}$ on the other, in both cases directed along $\hat{\imath}$, from the first side toward the second. What is $\sigma$?

- $1.77\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$
- $3.54\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$
- $0.885\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$
- $-1.77\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$

## Explanation

The field of the plane has magnitude $\dfrac{|\sigma|}{2\varepsilon_0}$ and opposite senses on the two sides, while the external field $E_0$ is the same everywhere. For $\sigma > 0$ the field of the plane points outward, so
$$
\begin{cases}
E_0 - \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 300\,\mathrm{N/C} \\[8pt]
E_0 + \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 500\,\mathrm{N/C}
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting the first equation from the second gives $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = 200\,\mathrm{N/C}$, which is positive as assumed.

Therefore $\sigma = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 200\,\mathrm{C/m^2} = 1.77 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$, and the external field is $E_0 = 400\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
