A thin ring has charge $Q < 0$ uniformly distributed, and the $z$-axis coincides with its axis, with the origin at the center. How is the electric field directed at the points $z = +3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $z = -3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ of the axis?

- At both points away from the center of the ring
- At both points toward the center of the ring
- At both points along $+\hat{k}$
- At both points perpendicular to the axis

## Explanation

On the axis the field is $E_z = \dfrac{kQz}{\left( z^2 + R^2 \right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ and, by symmetry, it is directed along the axis.

Since $Q < 0$, the component $E_z$ has the sign opposite to that of $z$. Hence at $z = +3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ we have $E_z < 0$ and at $z = -3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ we have $E_z > 0$.

Therefore at both points the field points **toward the center** of the ring, like the field of a negative charge, which points inward.
