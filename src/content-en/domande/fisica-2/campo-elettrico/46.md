At the point $P$ the electric field is $\vec{E} = \left( 3.0\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N/C}$. What force acts on a point charge $q = -2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ placed at $P$?

- $\vec{F} = \left( -6.0\,\hat{\imath} + 8.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $\vec{F} = \left( 6.0\,\hat{\imath} - 8.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $\vec{F} = \left( -6.0\,\hat{\imath} - 8.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $\vec{F} = \left( -1.5\,\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The force on a charge placed in the field is $\vec{F} = q\,\vec{E}$, so each component of the field must be multiplied by $q$ with its sign.

Since $q = -2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}$, we have $F_x = -2.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N} = -6.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$ and $F_y = -2.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot \left( -4.0 \cdot 10^{3} \right)\,\mathrm{N} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$.

Therefore $\vec{F} = \left( -6.0\,\hat{\imath} + 8.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$, and the force has the direction of the field and the **opposite sense**, because the charge is negative.
