An infinite straight wire has linear charge density $\lambda=2\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. What is the field at $r=0{,}10\,\mathrm m$? Assume $k=9{,}0\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

- $360\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $180\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $720\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $1440\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

The field of an infinite wire is $E=\dfrac{2k|\lambda|}{r}$. It is therefore inversely proportional to the distance. The result is $360\,\mathrm{N/C}$.
