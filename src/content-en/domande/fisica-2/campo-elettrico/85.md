A simple pendulum $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ long has a small sphere of mass $10\,\mathrm{g}$ and charge $+20\,\mu\mathrm{C}$, hanging from an insulating string, in a uniform vertical field $E = 4.9 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N/C}$ pointing downward. With $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the period of small oscillations?

- $1.4\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.71\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The electric force $qE$ is constant, vertical and has the same direction as the weight, so the sphere behaves as in a gravitational field of acceleration
$$
g' = g + \frac{qE}{m} = \left( 9.81 + \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 4.9 \cdot 10^{3}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}} \right)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 19.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The period of small oscillations is obtained by replacing $g$ with $g'$ in the formula for the simple pendulum, therefore
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.50}{19.6}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.0\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
Without the field the period would be about $1.4\,\mathrm{s}$.
