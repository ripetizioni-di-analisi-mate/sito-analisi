A negatively charged particle crosses two regions in which the uniform field points in the same direction. The second region has a weaker field. The ratio of charge magnitude to mass is $2\,\mathrm{C/kg}$. Given the field magnitude in the first region $E_{1} = 10\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the field magnitude in the second region $E_{2} = 4\,\mathrm{N/C}$, what is the decrease in acceleration magnitude $a$?

- $28\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $24\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

By Newton's second law, the acceleration magnitude is the product of the field magnitude and the charge-to-mass ratio. In the indicated units, $a = 2 \cdot E_{1} - 2 \cdot E_{2}$. Using the values given in the problem gives $12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
