A thin wire bent into a quarter-circle arc of radius $R$ has uniform linear density $\lambda > 0$. What is the magnitude of the electric field at the center of the circle?

- $2\,\dfrac{k\lambda}{R}$
- $\sqrt{2}\,\dfrac{k\lambda}{R}$
- $\dfrac{k\lambda}{R}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\dfrac{k\lambda}{R}$

## Explanation

We take the symmetry axis of the arc as reference and denote by $\theta$ the angle of an element with respect to this axis, with $-\dfrac{\pi}{4} \leq \theta \leq \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The element $dq = \lambda R\,d\theta$ is at distance $R$ from the center and produces a field of magnitude $\dfrac{k\lambda\,d\theta}{R}$.

By symmetry the components perpendicular to the axis cancel, so the component along the axis remains:
$$
E = \int_{-\frac{\pi}{4}}^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \frac{k\lambda}{R}\cos\theta\,d\theta = \frac{k\lambda}{R} \cdot 2\sin\frac{\pi}{4} = \sqrt{2}\,\frac{k\lambda}{R} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\dfrac{k\lambda}{R}$ is the sum of the magnitudes of the contributions, which ignores their directions.
