Two mutually perpendicular infinite planes, the plane $x = 0$ and the plane $y = 0$, have the same uniform surface charge density $\sigma = 8.85\,\mathrm{nC/m^2}$. What is the magnitude of the electric field at the point $(2.0\,\mathrm{cm},\, 3.0\,\mathrm{cm},\, 0)$?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $7.1 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$
- $3.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$

## Explanation

Each plane produces a uniform field of magnitude $\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \dfrac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-9}}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C} = 500\,\mathrm{N/C}$, perpendicular to the plane and pointing away from it, because $\sigma > 0$.

The point has $x > 0$ and $y > 0$, so the plane $x = 0$ gives $500\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the plane $y = 0$ gives $500\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{N/C}$. By the **superposition principle** the field is the sum of two perpendicular vectors of equal magnitude.

Therefore $E = \sqrt{2} \cdot 500\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 7.1 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}$, directed at $45^\circ$ to the two planes; the result is the same at every point of the quadrant with $x > 0$ and $y > 0$.
