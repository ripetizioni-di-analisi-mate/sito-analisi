What is the limit of the sequence $a_n = \left( \sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n} \right) \sqrt{n}$ as $n \to +\infty$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $0$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

The difference $\sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n}$ tends to $0$ and $\sqrt{n}$ tends to $+\infty$, so the general term is in the indeterminate form $0 \cdot \infty$.

Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{n + 1} + \sqrt{n}$ we obtain
$$
a_n = \frac{(n + 1 - n) \sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n + 1} + \sqrt{n}} = \frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n + 1} + \sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{n}} + 1} ,
$$
where in the last step we divided by $\sqrt{n}$. Since $\dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, the denominator tends to $2$ and the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
