For which of the following functions are the hypotheses of Bolzano's theorem satisfied on the interval $[-1, 1]$?

- $f(x) = x^3 + x$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$
- $f(x) = x^2 - 1$
- $f(x) = \cos x$

## Explanation

Bolzano's theorem requires $f$ to be continuous on $[-1, 1]$ and $f(-1)\, f(1) < 0$. The function $x^3 + x$ is a polynomial, so it is continuous, and $f(-1) = -2$, $f(1) = 2$; therefore the hypotheses are satisfied. The function $\dfrac{1}{x}$ equals $-1$ and $1$ at the endpoints, but it is not defined at $0$, so it is not continuous on $[-1, 1]$. For $x^2 - 1$ we have $f(-1) = f(1) = 0$, and for $\cos x$ we have $f(-1) = f(1) = \cos 1 > 0$, therefore in both cases the product of the values at the endpoints is not negative.
