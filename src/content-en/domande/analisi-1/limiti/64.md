What value does the quotient $\dfrac{\sin\left(x^2\right)}{x}$ tend to as $x \to 0$?

- $1$
- $+\infty$
- $0$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

The argument of the sine is $x^2$, therefore to obtain the standard limit we multiply and divide by $x^2$:
$$
\frac{\sin\left(x^2\right)}{x} = \frac{\sin\left(x^2\right)}{x^2} \cdot x .
$$
By the **standard limit of the sine** with $y = x^2 \to 0$ the first factor tends to $1$, while the second tends to $0$. So the limit equals $0$.
