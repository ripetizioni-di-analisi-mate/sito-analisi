What is the oblique asymptote of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{2x^2 + 1}{x + 1}$?

- $y = 2x + 2$
- $y = 2x - 2$
- $y = 2x$
- $y = 2x + 1$

## Explanation

The slope is
$$
m = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2x^2 + 1}{x^2 + x} = 2 ,
$$
because in a ratio of polynomials as $x \to \pm\infty$ only the highest-degree terms matter. The intercept is
$$
q = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \left( \frac{2x^2 + 1}{x + 1} - 2x \right) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{1 - 2x}{x + 1} = -2 .
$$
Since $m$ and $q$ exist and are finite and $m \neq 0$, the line $y = 2x - 2$ is an **oblique asymptote** both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$.
