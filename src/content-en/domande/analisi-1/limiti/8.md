- $1$
- $+\infty$
- The limit does not exist
- $0$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. We write
$$
\frac{x^{3} + \ln x}{e^{x}} = \frac{x^{3}}{e^{x}} + \frac{\ln x}{x} \cdot \frac{x}{e^{x}} .
$$
By the **hierarchy of infinities**, with base $e > 1$, the quotients $\dfrac{x^{3}}{e^{x}}$, $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$ and $\dfrac{x}{e^{x}}$ tend to $0$. Therefore the limit equals $0 + 0 \cdot 0 = 0$.
