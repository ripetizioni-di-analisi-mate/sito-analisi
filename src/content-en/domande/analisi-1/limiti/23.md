A polynomial $P$ satisfies $P(-1)=0$ and $\lim_{x\to-1}\dfrac{P(x)}{x+1}=0$. What is the greatest integer exponent $m$ for which $(x+1)^m$ must divide $P$?

- $1$, because the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$ would always have limit zero.
- $2$
- $3$
- $0$

## Explanation

The first condition gives $P=(x+1)Q$. The limit then equals $Q(-1)$ and forces a second factor $x+1$. The example $P(x)=(x+1)^2$ meets both conditions without a third factor.
