What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^3 - 1}{x^2 + x - 2}$?

- $3$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $0$
- $1$

## Explanation

At $x = 1$ the numerator and the denominator equal $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$ and $x^2 + x - 2 = (x - 1)(x + 2)$, for $x \neq 1$ we have
$$
\frac{x^3 - 1}{x^2 + x - 2} = \frac{x^2 + x + 1}{x + 2} .
$$
The resulting function is continuous at $x = 1$, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1 + 1 + 1}{1 + 2} = 1$.
