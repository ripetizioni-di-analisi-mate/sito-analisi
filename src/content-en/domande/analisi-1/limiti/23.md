Let $f(x) = \dfrac{x^3 + 1}{x + 1}$, defined for $x \neq -1$. What value does $f(x)$ tend to as $x \to -1$?

- $0$
- $3$
- $1$
- $-3$

## Explanation

At $x = -1$ both the numerator and the denominator equal $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

The numerator is a sum of cubes, therefore $x^3 + 1 = (x + 1)(x^2 - x + 1)$. For $x \neq -1$ the factor $x + 1$ can be canceled, and we obtain
$$
\frac{x^3 + 1}{x + 1} = x^2 - x + 1 .
$$
The polynomial $x^2 - x + 1$ is continuous, so the limit equals $(-1)^2 - (-1) + 1 = 3$.
