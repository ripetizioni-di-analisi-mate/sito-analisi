- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $3$
- $0$

## Explanation

Both square roots tend to $+\infty$, so the limit takes the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$. Multiplying and dividing by the sum of the two square roots we obtain
$$
\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} - \sqrt{x^2 - x} = \frac{(x^2 + 2x) - (x^2 - x)}{\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} + \sqrt{x^2 - x}} = \frac{3x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} + \sqrt{x^2 - x}} .
$$
For $x > 1$ we have $\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} = x \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{2}{x}}$ and $\sqrt{x^2 - x} = x \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{x}}$, so, canceling $x$, the quotient becomes
$$
\frac{3}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{2}{x}} + \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{x}}} .
$$
The denominator tends to $1 + 1 = 2$, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
