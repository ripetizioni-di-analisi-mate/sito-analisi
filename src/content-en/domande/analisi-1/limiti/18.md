What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x + \sin x}{2x - \cos x}$?

- $2$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ we divide the numerator and the denominator by $x$ and obtain
$$
\frac{x + \sin x}{2x - \cos x} = \frac{1 + \dfrac{\sin x}{x}}{2 - \dfrac{\cos x}{x}} .
$$
The functions $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ are bounded and $\dfrac{1}{x} \to 0$, so by the **bounded times infinitesimal** theorem we have $\dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 0$ and $\dfrac{\cos x}{x} \to 0$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1 + 0}{2 - 0} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, even though $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ have no limit as $x \to +\infty$.
