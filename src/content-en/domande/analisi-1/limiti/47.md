The difference $\dfrac{1}{x - 2} - \dfrac{4}{x^2 - 4}$ is not defined at $x = 2$. What value does it tend to as $x \to 2$?

- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

For $x \to 2^{+}$ both fractions tend to $+\infty$, so the difference is in the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$.

Since $x^2 - 4 = (x - 2)(x + 2)$, the two fractions have the common denominator $x^2 - 4$ and
$$
\frac{1}{x - 2} - \frac{4}{x^2 - 4} = \frac{x + 2 - 4}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} = \frac{x - 2}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} .
$$
For $x \neq 2$ we cancel $x - 2$ and the expression becomes $\dfrac{1}{x + 2}$, which is continuous at $2$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$.
