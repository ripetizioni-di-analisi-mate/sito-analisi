- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $x^2 \to 0$, from the fundamental limit of the exponential with the change of variable $y = x^2$ we have $e^{x^2} - 1 \sim x^2$. From the **fundamental sine limit** we have $1 - \cos x \sim \dfrac{x^2}{2}$. Since in a quotient a function can be replaced by an asymptotically equivalent one, the limit equals
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^2}{\dfrac{x^2}{2}} = 2 .
$$
