What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{1 + 3x} - 1}{x}$?

- $3$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. We have $\sqrt{1 + 3x} = (1 + 3x)^{\frac{1}{2}}$, so we use the fundamental limit $\displaystyle\lim_{y \to 0} \frac{(1 + y)^{\alpha} - 1}{y} = \alpha$ with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the change of variable $y = 3x \to 0$. We write
$$
\frac{\sqrt{1 + 3x} - 1}{x} = 3 \cdot \frac{(1 + 3x)^{\frac{1}{2}} - 1}{3x} .
$$
The second factor tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore the limit equals $3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
