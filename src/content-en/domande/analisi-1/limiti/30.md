The limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{(1 + 2x)^{5} - 1}{x}$ is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. What is its value?

- $5$
- $10$
- $2$
- $32$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

We use the standard limit $\dfrac{(1 + y)^{\alpha} - 1}{y} \to \alpha$ with $\alpha = 5$ and $y = 2x \to 0$:
$$
\frac{(1 + 2x)^{5} - 1}{x} = \frac{(1 + 2x)^{5} - 1}{2x} \cdot 2 .
$$
The first factor tends to $5$, therefore the limit equals $5 \cdot 2 = 10$.
