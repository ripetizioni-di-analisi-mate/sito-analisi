To verify by the definition that $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} (5x + 2) = 7$, we fix $\varepsilon > 0$. Which choice of $\delta$ satisfies the definition for every $\varepsilon > 0$?

- $\delta = 5\varepsilon$
- $\delta = \varepsilon$
- $\delta = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}$
- $\delta = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{5}$

## Explanation

We have $|(5x + 2) - 7| = |5x - 5| = 5|x - 1|$. If $0 < |x - 1| < \delta$, then $|f(x) - 7| < 5\delta$, so the condition $|f(x) - 7| < \varepsilon$ is guaranteed when $5\delta \leq \varepsilon$. With $\delta = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{5}$ we get $5\delta = \varepsilon$, therefore the definition is satisfied. With $\delta = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}$, $\delta = \varepsilon$ or $\delta = 5\varepsilon$ we have $5\delta > \varepsilon$, and for $x$ close enough to $1 + \delta$ the distance $|f(x) - 7|$ exceeds $\varepsilon$.
