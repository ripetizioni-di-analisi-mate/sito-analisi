For which of the following functions does the sign-preservation theorem guarantee the existence of a neighborhood of $0$ in which $f(x) > 0$ for $x \neq 0$?

- $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$
- $f(x) = x^2$
- $f(x) = \cos x - 1$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{|x|}$

## Explanation

The sign-preservation theorem applies when the limit at $x_0$ exists and is positive. For $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ the limit at $0$ equals $1 > 0$, so the theorem guarantees a neighborhood of $0$ in which the function is positive. The function $x^2$ is positive for $x \neq 0$, but its limit at $0$ equals $0$, therefore the theorem does not apply. The function $\cos x - 1$ also has limit $0$ at $0$. For $\dfrac{x}{|x|}$ the one-sided limits at $0$ equal $1$ and $-1$, so the limit does not exist.
