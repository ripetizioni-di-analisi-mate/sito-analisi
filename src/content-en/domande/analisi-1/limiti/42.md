The equation $e^{x} = 3 - x$ has exactly one real solution. Which interval contains it?

- $(0, 1)$
- $(1, 2)$
- $(-1, 0)$
- $(2, 3)$

## Explanation

Let $g(x) = e^{x} + x - 3$, which is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. We have $g(0) = -2 < 0$ and $g(1) = e - 2 > 0$, so by **Bolzano's theorem** there is a solution in $(0, 1)$. The function $g$ is strictly increasing, being the sum of the increasing functions $e^{x}$ and $x - 3$, therefore it vanishes at only one point. It follows that the other three intervals contain no solutions.
