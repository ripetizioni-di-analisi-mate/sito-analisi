What is the oblique asymptote as $x \to +\infty$ of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 2x}$?

- $y = x$
- $y = x + 2$
- $y = x + 1$
- $y = x - 1$

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ we have $\dfrac{f(x)}{x} = \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{2}{x}} \to 1$, so $m = 1$. The intercept is computed by rationalizing the form $+\infty - \infty$:
$$
q = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 2x} - x \right) = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} + x} = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{2}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{2}{x}} + 1} = 1 .
$$
Since $m$ and $q$ exist and are finite and $m \neq 0$, the line $y = x + 1$ is an **oblique asymptote** as $x \to +\infty$.
