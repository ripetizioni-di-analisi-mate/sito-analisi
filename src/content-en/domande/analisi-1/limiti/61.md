What is the limit as $x \to +\infty$ of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 3x} - 2x$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

The square root and $2x$ tend to $+\infty$, so the difference is in the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$.

For $x > 0$ we have $\sqrt{x^2} = x$, therefore factoring out $x$ we obtain
$$
\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} - 2x = x \left( \sqrt{1 + \frac{3}{x}} - 2 \right) .
$$
The second factor tends to $1 - 2 = -1$, so the product tends to $-\infty$. The coefficients of $x$ in the two terms are different, therefore rationalization is not needed.
