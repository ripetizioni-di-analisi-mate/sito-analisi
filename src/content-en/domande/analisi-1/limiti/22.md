What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{2x^3 + x}{1 - x^2}$?

- $-\infty$
- $-2$
- $+\infty$
- $2$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Factoring out the highest-degree terms we obtain
$$
\frac{2x^3 + x}{1 - x^2} = \frac{x^3 \left( 2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2} \right)}{x^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{x^2} - 1 \right)} = x \cdot \frac{2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}}{\dfrac{1}{x^2} - 1} .
$$
The second factor tends to $\dfrac{2}{-1} = -2$ and the first tends to $-\infty$. The product of a quantity tending to $-\infty$ and a negative number tends to $+\infty$, therefore the limit equals $+\infty$.
