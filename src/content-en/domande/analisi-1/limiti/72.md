What is the limit as $x \to 0$ of $\dfrac{\sin 2x + x^{3}}{\tan 3x - x^{2}}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

By the standard limit of the sine $\sin 2x = 2x + o(x)$, and since $\dfrac{x^3}{x} \to 0$ we have $x^{3} = o(x)$; therefore the numerator is $2x + o(x)$. In the same way $\tan 3x = 3x + o(x)$ and $x^{2} = o(x)$, so the denominator is $3x + o(x)$. Dividing the numerator and the denominator by $x$ we obtain
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2 + \dfrac{o(x)}{x}}{3 + \dfrac{o(x)}{x}} = \frac{2}{3} .
$$
