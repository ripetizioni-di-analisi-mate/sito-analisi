For which value of $a \in \mathbb{R}$ is the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 + a x - 6}{x - 2}$ finite, and what is its value in that case?

- $a = 1$ and the limit equals $5$
- $a = 1$ and the limit equals $0$
- $a = -1$ and the limit equals $5$
- $a = 3$ and the limit equals $7$

## Explanation

The denominator tends to $0$ and the numerator tends to $4 + 2a - 6 = 2a - 2$. If $2a - 2 \neq 0$, the limit is in the form $\dfrac{L}{0}$ with $L \neq 0$, so it is infinite or does not exist.

The limit can therefore be finite only if $2a - 2 = 0$, that is $a = 1$. In this case the numerator is $x^2 + x - 6 = (x - 2)(x + 3)$ and, for $x \neq 2$,
$$
\frac{x^2 + x - 6}{x - 2} = x + 3 .
$$
Therefore for $a = 1$ the limit equals $2 + 3 = 5$.
