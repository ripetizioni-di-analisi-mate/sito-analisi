Which of the following functions has a removable discontinuity at $x = 0$?

- $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{|x|}$
- $f(x) = \sin \dfrac{1}{x}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x}$
- $f(x) = \dfrac{|x|}{x^2}$

## Explanation

We have $\dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x} = x \cdot \dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \to 0 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 0$ by the fundamental sine limit. Since the limit exists and is finite and the function is not defined at $0$, the discontinuity is **removable**. For $\dfrac{\sin x}{|x|}$ the one-sided limits equal $1$ and $-1$, so the discontinuity is a jump. The limit of $\sin \dfrac{1}{x}$ does not exist and $\dfrac{|x|}{x^2} = \dfrac{1}{|x|}$ tends to $+\infty$, therefore in both cases the discontinuity is of the second kind.
