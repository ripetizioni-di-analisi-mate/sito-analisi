The figure shows the graph of a function $f$ defined on $[-2, 3]$, with $f(1) = 1$.

::grafico{f="x+1-1.5*(1+(x-1)/abs(x-1))+0*sqrt((x+2)*(3-x))" xmin=-2.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=2.6 punti="1,2,vuoto;1,-1,vuoto;1,1,pieno" legenda=0 titolo="Graph of $y = f(x)$"}

What are the one-sided limits of $f$ at $x = 1$, and what kind of discontinuity is it?

- Left $2$, right $-1$; jump
- Left $2$, right $-1$; second kind
- Left $1$, right $1$; removable
- Left $-1$, right $2$; jump

## Explanation

For $x < 1$ the graph is a segment that approaches the empty point $(1, 2)$, so the left limit equals $2$. For $x > 1$ the graph is a segment that starts from the empty point $(1, -1)$, so the right limit equals $-1$.

The value $f(1) = 1$ plays no role in the one-sided limits. Since the two one-sided limits exist, are finite and are different, the point $x = 1$ is a **jump discontinuity**.
