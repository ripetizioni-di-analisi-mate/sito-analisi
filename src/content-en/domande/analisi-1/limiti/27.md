What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} x \sin \frac{2}{x}$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

The factor $x$ tends to $+\infty$ and $\sin \dfrac{2}{x}$ tends to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $0 \cdot \infty$. With the change of variable $y = \dfrac{2}{x}$ we have $y \to 0^{+}$ and $x = \dfrac{2}{y}$, therefore
$$
x \sin \frac{2}{x} = 2 \cdot \frac{\sin y}{y} .
$$
By the **fundamental sine limit** the quotient $\dfrac{\sin y}{y}$ tends to $1$, so the limit equals $2$.
