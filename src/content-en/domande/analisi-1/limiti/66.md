The infinitesimal functions $e^{x} - e^{\sin x}$ and $x - \sin x$ are compared as $x \to 0$. What is the limit of their ratio?

- $0$
- $e$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

Factoring out $e^{\sin x}$ in the numerator we obtain
$$
\frac{e^{x} - e^{\sin x}}{x - \sin x} = e^{\sin x} \cdot \frac{e^{x - \sin x} - 1}{x - \sin x} .
$$
The first factor tends to $e^{0} = 1$. In the second factor we set $t = x - \sin x$, which tends to $0$ and is different from $0$ for $x \neq 0$, so by the **standard limit** $\dfrac{e^{t} - 1}{t} \to 1$ the second factor also tends to $1$. Therefore the limit equals $1$.
