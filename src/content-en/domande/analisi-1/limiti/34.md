Which of the following statements is true as $x \to 0$?

- $\sin x = o(x)$
- $x = o(x^2)$
- $\ln(1 + x) = o(x)$
- $1 - \cos x = o(x)$

## Explanation

We have $f(x) = o(g(x))$ if the ratio $\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ tends to $0$. By the fundamental sine limit, $\dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x} = x \cdot \dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \to 0 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 0$, so $1 - \cos x = o(x)$. The ratios $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ and $\dfrac{\ln(1 + x)}{x}$ tend to $1$, therefore $\sin x$ and $\ln(1 + x)$ are asymptotically equivalent to $x$ and are not $o(x)$. The ratio $\dfrac{x}{x^2} = \dfrac{1}{x}$ is infinite as $x \to 0$, so $x$ is not $o(x^2)$.
