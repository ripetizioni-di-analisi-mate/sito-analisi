As $x \to 0$, what value does the limit of $\dfrac{\ln\left(1 + x^{2}\right) - \sin^{2} x}{x^{4}}$ have?

- $-\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$

## Explanation

The two functions in the numerator are both asymptotic to $x^2$, so their principal parts cancel and expansions up to the fourth order are needed.

By **Taylor's formula** with $t = x^2$ we have $\ln\left(1 + x^{2}\right) = x^{2} - \dfrac{x^{4}}{2} + o(x^{4})$. From $\sin x = x - \dfrac{x^{3}}{6} + o(x^{3})$ it follows that
$$
\sin^{2} x = x^{2} - 2 \cdot x \cdot \frac{x^{3}}{6} + o(x^{4}) = x^{2} - \frac{x^{4}}{3} + o(x^{4}) .
$$
Therefore the numerator is $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3} \right) x^{4} + o(x^{4}) = -\dfrac{x^{4}}{6} + o(x^{4})$ and the limit equals $-\dfrac{1}{6}$.
