As $x \to \pm\infty$ the function $f(x) = \dfrac{2x^{2} - 3}{x^{2} + 1}$ has a finite limit. Which line is its horizontal asymptote?

- $y = -3$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $y = 0$
- $y = 2$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator are polynomials of the same degree $2$. Factoring out $x^{2}$ we obtain
$$
\frac{2x^{2} - 3}{x^{2} + 1} = \frac{2 - \dfrac{3}{x^{2}}}{1 + \dfrac{1}{x^{2}}} ,
$$
which tends to $2$ both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$. Therefore the line $y = 2$ is a **horizontal asymptote** on both sides.
