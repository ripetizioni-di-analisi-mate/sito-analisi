As $x \to 0$, to which of the following functions is $\sqrt{1 + x^2} - 1$ asymptotically equivalent?

- $\dfrac{x}{2}$
- $x^2$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2}$
- $2x^2$

## Explanation

We have $\sqrt{1 + x^2} - 1 = (1 + x^2)^{\frac{1}{2}} - 1$. Since $x^2 \to 0$, the fundamental limit $(1 + y)^{\alpha} - 1 \sim \alpha y$ with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $y = x^2$ gives $\sqrt{1 + x^2} - 1 \sim \dfrac{x^2}{2}$. Hence the ratio of $\sqrt{1 + x^2} - 1$ to $\dfrac{x^2}{2}$ tends to $1$, while the ratio to $x^2$ tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$, the ratio to $2x^2$ tends to $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and the ratio to $\dfrac{x}{2}$ tends to $0$.
