What is the limit of $\dfrac{\ln x}{x^2 - 1}$ as $x \to 1$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$

## Explanation

As $x \to 1$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

We set $t = x - 1$, which tends to $0$. Since $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1) = t(t + 2)$, we have
$$
\frac{\ln x}{x^2 - 1} = \frac{\ln(1 + t)}{t} \cdot \frac{1}{t + 2} .
$$
By the **fundamental logarithmic limit** the first factor tends to $1$, while the second tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Hence the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
