What are the horizontal asymptotes of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}}{x + 1}$?

- $y = 2$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = -2$ as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = 2$ both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = -2$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = 2$ as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = 4$ both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$

## Explanation

We have $\sqrt{4x^2 + 1} = |x| \sqrt{4 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}}$ and $x + 1 = x \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{x} \right)$. For $x > 0$ we have $|x| = x$, so $f(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{4 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}}}{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} \to 2$ as $x \to +\infty$. For $x < 0$ we have $|x| = -x$, so $f(x) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{4 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}}}{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} \to -2$ as $x \to -\infty$. Therefore the line $y = 2$ is a **horizontal asymptote** as $x \to +\infty$ and the line $y = -2$ is one as $x \to -\infty$.
