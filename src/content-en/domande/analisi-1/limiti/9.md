Let $f(x) = \dfrac{x + 2}{x^2 - 1}$. What are the right-hand limit and the left-hand limit of $f$ as $x \to 1$?

- Right $-\infty$, left $+\infty$
- Right $+\infty$, left $+\infty$
- Right $+\infty$, left $-\infty$
- Right $-\infty$, left $-\infty$

## Explanation

As $x \to 1$ the numerator tends to $3$ and the denominator to $0$, so the limit is infinite and its sign depends on the sign of the denominator. We have $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$, and near $1$ the factor $x + 1$ is positive. As $x \to 1^{+}$ we have $x - 1 > 0$, so the denominator tends to $0^{+}$ and $f(x) \to +\infty$. As $x \to 1^{-}$ we have $x - 1 < 0$, so the denominator tends to $0$ while remaining negative and $f(x) \to -\infty$. Since the one-sided limits are different, the limit as $x \to 1$ does not exist.
