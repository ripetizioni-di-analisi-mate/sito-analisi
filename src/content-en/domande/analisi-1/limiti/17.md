Let $f(x) = \dfrac{|x - 2|}{x^2 - 4}$. What are the right-hand limit and the left-hand limit of $f$ as $x \to 2$?

- Right $\dfrac{1}{4}$, left $-\dfrac{1}{4}$
- Right $-\dfrac{1}{4}$, left $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- Right $\dfrac{1}{4}$, left $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- Right $+\infty$, left $-\infty$

## Explanation

We have $x^2 - 4 = (x - 2)(x + 2)$. For $x > 2$ we have $|x - 2| = x - 2$, so $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x + 2}$ and the right-hand limit is $\dfrac{1}{4}$. For $x < 2$ we have $|x - 2| = -(x - 2)$, so $f(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x + 2}$ and the left-hand limit is $-\dfrac{1}{4}$. Since the one-sided limits are different, the limit of $f$ as $x \to 2$ does not exist.
