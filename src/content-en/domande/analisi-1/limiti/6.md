- $e^{3}$
- $e^{5}$
- $e^{6}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$. Setting $t = \dfrac{x}{3}$, we have $t \to +\infty$, $\dfrac{3}{x} = \dfrac{1}{t}$ and $2x = 6t$, so
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{3}{x} \right)^{2x} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{6} .
$$
By the fundamental limit $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} = e$ and by the continuity of the sixth power, the limit equals $e^{6}$.
