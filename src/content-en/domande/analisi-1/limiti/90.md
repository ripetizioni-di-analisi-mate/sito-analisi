A function $f$ is continuous on $[0, 4]$, with $f(0) = f(4) = 0$ and $f(2) = 3$. Which statement is certainly true?

- The maximum of $f$ on $[0, 4]$ equals $3$
- The minimum of $f$ on $[0, 4]$ equals $0$
- $f$ takes the value $1$ at least at two points of $(0, 4)$
- $f$ vanishes at least at one point of $(0, 4)$

## Explanation

On $[0, 2]$ the function is continuous and takes the values $0$ and $3$, so by the **intermediate value theorem** there exists $x_1 \in (0, 2)$ with $f(x_1) = 1$, because $1$ lies between $0$ and $3$. In the same way on $[2, 4]$ there exists $x_2 \in (2, 4)$ with $f(x_2) = 1$. Since $x_1 < 2 < x_2$, the two points are distinct.

The other statements are not certain. The function $f(x) = \dfrac{3}{4} x (4 - x)$ satisfies the data and is positive on $(0, 4)$, so it does not vanish on $(0, 4)$. The broken line joining the points $(0, 0)$, $(1, 5)$, $(2, 3)$, $(4, 0)$ satisfies the data and has maximum $5$, while the broken line joining $(0, 0)$, $(1, -1)$, $(2, 3)$, $(4, 0)$ satisfies the data and has minimum $-1$.
