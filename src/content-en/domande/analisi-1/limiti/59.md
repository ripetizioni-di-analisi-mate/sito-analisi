What value does the difference $\sqrt{x + 5} - \sqrt{x}$ tend to as $x \to +\infty$?

- $\sqrt{5}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$
- $0$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

Both square roots tend to $+\infty$, so the difference is in the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$.

Multiplying and dividing by the sum $\sqrt{x + 5} + \sqrt{x}$ we obtain
$$
\sqrt{x + 5} - \sqrt{x} = \frac{(x + 5) - x}{\sqrt{x + 5} + \sqrt{x}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{x + 5} + \sqrt{x}} .
$$
The denominator tends to $+\infty$ and the numerator is constant, therefore the limit equals $0$.
