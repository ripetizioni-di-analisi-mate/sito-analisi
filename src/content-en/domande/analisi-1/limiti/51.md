As $x \to +\infty$ the numerator and the denominator of $\dfrac{3 \cdot 2^{x} + x^{5}}{2^{x} + 1}$ tend to $+\infty$. What is the limit of the ratio?

- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $3$
- $0$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$.

By the **hierarchy of infinities** we have $\dfrac{x^5}{2^x} \to 0$, therefore the fastest-growing term in the numerator is $3 \cdot 2^x$. Factoring out $2^x$ we obtain
$$
\frac{3 \cdot 2^{x} + x^{5}}{2^{x} + 1} = \frac{2^{x}\left(3 + \dfrac{x^5}{2^x}\right)}{2^{x}\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{2^x}\right)} = \frac{3 + \dfrac{x^5}{2^x}}{1 + \dfrac{1}{2^x}} .
$$
The fractions $\dfrac{x^5}{2^x}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2^x}$ tend to $0$, so the limit equals $3$.
