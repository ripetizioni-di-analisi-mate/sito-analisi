- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $0$
- $3$

## Explanation

The function $f$ is continuous at $x = 0$ if $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = f(0) = a$. For $x \neq 0$ we write $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x} = 3 \cdot \dfrac{\sin 3x}{3x}$, and with the change of variable $y = 3x \to 0$ the second factor tends to $1$ by the **fundamental sine limit**. Hence the limit of $f$ as $x \to 0$ equals $3$, and $f$ is continuous at $0$ if and only if $a = 3$.
