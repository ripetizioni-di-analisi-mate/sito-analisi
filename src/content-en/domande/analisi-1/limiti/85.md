We study the behavior of $f(x) = x^{2} \sin \dfrac{1}{x}$ as $x \to +\infty$. Which statement about the oblique asymptote is correct?

- The oblique asymptote is $y = x + 1$
- The oblique asymptote is $y = x - \dfrac{1}{6}$
- There is no oblique asymptote
- The oblique asymptote is $y = x$

## Explanation

With $t = \dfrac{1}{x} \to 0^{+}$ we have $\dfrac{f(x)}{x} = x \sin \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{\sin t}{t}$, which tends to $1$ by the standard limit of the sine. So $m = 1$.

The intercept is the limit of $f(x) - x$. With the same substitution
$$
x^{2} \sin \frac{1}{x} - x = \frac{\sin t - t}{t^{2}} .
$$
By **Taylor's formula** $\sin t - t = -\dfrac{t^{3}}{6} + o(t^{3})$, therefore the quotient is $-\dfrac{t}{6} + o(t)$ and tends to $0$. So $q = 0$ and the oblique asymptote is $y = x$.
