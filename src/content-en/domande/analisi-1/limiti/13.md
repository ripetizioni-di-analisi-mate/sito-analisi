The equation $x^3 - 4x + 1 = 0$ has three real solutions. Which intervals contain one each?

- $[-2, -1]$, $[0, 1]$, $[1, 2]$
- $[-3, -2]$, $[0, 1]$, $[1, 2]$
- $[-3, -2]$, $[-1, 0]$, $[1, 2]$
- $[-3, -2]$, $[0, 1]$, $[2, 3]$

## Explanation

Setting $f(x) = x^3 - 4x + 1$, we have $f(-3) = -14$, $f(-2) = 1$, $f(0) = 1$, $f(1) = -2$ and $f(2) = 1$. The function $f$ is continuous and takes values of opposite sign at the endpoints of $[-3, -2]$, of $[0, 1]$ and of $[1, 2]$, so by the **intermediate zero theorem** it has a zero in each of these intervals. A polynomial of degree three has at most three real roots, therefore the solutions are exactly these three. It follows that the intervals $[-2, -1]$, $[-1, 0]$ and $[2, 3]$ contain no solutions.
