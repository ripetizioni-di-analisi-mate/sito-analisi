The figure shows the graph of $f(x) = x^{2}$ on the open interval $(-1, 2)$.

::grafico{f="x^2+0*sqrt((x+1)*(2-x))" xmin=-2 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=5 punti="-1,1,vuoto;2,4,vuoto" legenda=0 titolo="$y = x^2$ for $-1 < x < 2$"}

Which statement about the maximum and minimum of $f$ on $(-1, 2)$ is true?

- The minimum is $0$ and the maximum is $4$
- The minimum is $1$ and the maximum is $4$
- Neither the maximum nor the minimum exists
- The minimum is $0$ and the maximum does not exist

## Explanation

The interval $(-1, 2)$ is not closed, so Weierstrass's theorem does not apply and the existence of the maximum and the minimum has to be checked directly.

Since $x^{2} \geq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$ with $0 \in (-1, 2)$, the **minimum** equals $0$. For $-1 < x < 2$ we have $x^{2} < 4$, and the values approach $4$ as $x \to 2^{-}$; therefore $4$ is the supremum of the values, but it is not attained, and the **maximum does not exist**.
