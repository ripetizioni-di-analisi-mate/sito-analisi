What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{x + 1}{x - 2} \right)^{x}$?

- $e^{-3}$
- $e^{3}$
- $e^{-1}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$. For $x > 2$ we write
$$
\left( \frac{x + 1}{x - 2} \right)^{x} = \frac{\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{x} \right)^{x}}{\left( 1 - \dfrac{2}{x} \right)^{x}} .
$$
By the fundamental limit of the number $e$ the numerator tends to $e$. With the change of variable $t = -\dfrac{x}{2}$ we have $\left( 1 - \dfrac{2}{x} \right)^{x} = \left[ \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{-2}$ with $t \to -\infty$, so the denominator tends to $e^{-2}$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{e}{e^{-2}} = e^{3}$.
