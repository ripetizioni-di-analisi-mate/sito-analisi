Let $f : [0, 1] \to [0, 1]$ be a continuous function. Which statement is certainly true?

- There exists $c \in (0, 1)$ with $f(c) = c$
- There exists exactly one $c \in [0, 1]$ with $f(c) = c$
- There exists $c \in [0, 1]$ with $f(c) = c$
- $f$ takes every value of $[0, 1]$

## Explanation

Consider $g(x) = f(x) - x$, continuous on $[0, 1]$. Since the values of $f$ lie in $[0, 1]$, we have $g(0) = f(0) \geq 0$ and $g(1) = f(1) - 1 \leq 0$.

If $g(0) = 0$ or $g(1) = 0$, the required point is $c = 0$ or $c = 1$. Otherwise $g(0) > 0 > g(1)$ and, by the **zero theorem**, there exists $c \in (0, 1)$ with $g(c) = 0$. In every case there exists $c \in [0, 1]$ with $f(c) = c$.

The other statements are not certain. The constant function $f(x) = 1$ has $c = 1$ as the only point with $f(c) = c$ and does not take the value $0$, while $f(x) = x$ satisfies $f(c) = c$ at every point.
