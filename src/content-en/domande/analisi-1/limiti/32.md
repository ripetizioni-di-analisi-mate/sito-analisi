What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \pi} \frac{\sin x}{x - \pi}$?

- $-1$
- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{\pi}$

## Explanation

As $x \to \pi$ we have $\sin x \to 0$ and $x - \pi \to 0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. With the change of variable $y = x - \pi$ we have $y \to 0$ and $\sin x = \sin(y + \pi) = -\sin y$. Therefore
$$
\lim_{x \to \pi} \frac{\sin x}{x - \pi} = \lim_{y \to 0} \frac{-\sin y}{y} = -1
$$
by the **fundamental sine limit**.
