Two models predict the errors $E_1(t)=t^2e^{-t}$ and $E_2(t)=e^{-\dfrac{t}{2}}$ as $t\to+\infty$. Which ratio shows that the first error becomes negligible relative to the second?

- $\dfrac{E_1}{E_2}=t^2e^{\dfrac{t}{2}}\longrightarrow+\infty$
- $\dfrac{E_1}{E_2}=t^2e^{-\dfrac{t}{2}}\longrightarrow0$
- $\dfrac{E_1}{E_2}=t^2e^{-\dfrac{t}{2}}\to+\infty$, because the polynomial factor would dominate.
- $\dfrac{E_1}{E_2}=e^{-\dfrac{1}{2}}$

## Explanation

Dividing the exponentials subtracts their exponents. The denominator factor $e^{\dfrac{t}{2}}$ grows faster than $t^2$, so the ratio of the errors tends to zero.
