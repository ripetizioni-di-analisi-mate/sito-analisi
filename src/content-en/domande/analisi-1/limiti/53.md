The product $x^{2} e^{x}$ is in the form $0 \cdot \infty$ as $x \to -\infty$. What is its limit?

- $+\infty$
- $0$
- $-\infty$
- $1$

## Explanation

As $x \to -\infty$ we have $x^2 \to +\infty$ and $e^x \to 0$, so the product is in the indeterminate form $0 \cdot \infty$.

Set $t = -x$; then $t \to +\infty$ and
$$
x^{2} e^{x} = \frac{t^{2}}{e^{t}} .
$$
By the **hierarchy of infinities** the exponential $e^t$ grows faster than the power $t^2$, therefore $\dfrac{t^2}{e^t} \to 0$ and the limit equals $0$.
