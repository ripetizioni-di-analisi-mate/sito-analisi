What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x - \sin x}{x^3}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $1$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The substitutions $\tan x \sim x$ and $\sin x \sim x$ are not allowed, because they appear in a difference. Since $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$, we factor out $\tan x$ and obtain $\tan x - \sin x = \tan x \, (1 - \cos x)$, so
$$
\frac{\tan x - \sin x}{x^3} = \frac{\tan x}{x} \cdot \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} .
$$
By the **fundamental sine limit** the first factor tends to $1$ and the second to $\dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
