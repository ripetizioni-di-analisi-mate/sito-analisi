Which of the following functions has an oblique asymptote as $x \to +\infty$?

- $f(x) = x + \ln x$
- $f(x) = x + \sqrt{x}$
- $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$
- $f(x) = x + \dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

For $f(x) = x + \dfrac{1}{x}$ we have $m = \displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{x^2} \right) = 1$ and $q = \displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{1}{x} = 0$, so $y = x$ is an oblique asymptote. For $x + \ln x$ and $x + \sqrt{x}$ we still have $m = 1$, but $f(x) - x$ equals $\ln x$ or $\sqrt{x}$ and tends to $+\infty$. Since $q$ is not finite, these two functions have no oblique asymptote. For $\sqrt{x}$ we have $\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{x} \to 0$, so $m = 0$ and the condition $m \neq 0$ is not satisfied.
