- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $1$
- $4$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. From the **fundamental limits** of the logarithm and of the exponential, with the changes of variable $y = 4x$ and $y = 3x$, we have $\ln(1 + 4x) \sim 4x$ and $e^{3x} - 1 \sim 3x$. Since in a quotient a function can be replaced by an asymptotically equivalent one, the limit equals
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{4x}{3x} = \frac{4}{3} .
$$
