Let $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 + 1}{x - 3}$. Which statement about the sign of $f$ near $x_0 = 5$ is correct?

- $f > 0$ in a neighborhood of $5$, by the sign-preservation theorem
- $f < 0$ in a neighborhood of $5$, by the sign-preservation theorem
- $f > 0$ in a neighborhood of $5$, by the intermediate zero theorem
- $f > 0$ in a neighborhood of $5$, by the Weierstrass theorem

## Explanation

The function $f$ is a quotient of polynomials and the denominator at $x = 5$ equals $2 \neq 0$, so $f$ is continuous at $5$ and the limit is computed by substitution.
$$
\lim_{x \to 5} \frac{x^2 + 1}{x - 3} = \frac{26}{2} = 13
$$
Since the limit is positive, the **sign-preservation theorem** applies, and it guarantees the existence of a neighborhood of $5$ in which $f(x) > 0$. The intermediate zero theorem ensures the existence of a zero and the Weierstrass theorem the existence of a maximum and a minimum on a closed and bounded interval; neither of them concerns the sign of $f$ near a point.
