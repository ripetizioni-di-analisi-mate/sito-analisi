To verify with the definition that $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x^{4}} = +\infty$, we fix $M > 0$ and look for $\delta > 0$ such that $0 < |x| < \delta$ implies $\dfrac{1}{x^{4}} > M$. Which choice of $\delta$ satisfies the definition for every $M > 0$?

- $\delta = \sqrt[4]{M}$
- $\delta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{M}}$
- $\delta = \dfrac{1}{M}$
- $\delta = M^{4}$

## Explanation

For $x \neq 0$ the inequality $\dfrac{1}{x^{4}} > M$ is equivalent to $x^{4} < \dfrac{1}{M}$, that is to $|x| < \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{M}}$. So the choice $\delta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{M}}$ satisfies the definition for every $M > 0$.

A value of $\delta$ works for every $M$ only if it never exceeds $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{M}}$. For $M = 16$ we have $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{M}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, while $\sqrt[4]{M} = 2$ and $M^{4} = 65536$ are larger; for $M = \dfrac{1}{16}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{M}} = 2$, while $\dfrac{1}{M} = 16$ is larger. Therefore the other choices do not satisfy the definition for every $M$.
