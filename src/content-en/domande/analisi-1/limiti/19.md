Which of the following limits equals $0$?

- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} x \sin \frac{1}{x}$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \sin \frac{1}{x}$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x}$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} x \sin \frac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

For $x \neq 0$ we have $\left|\sin \dfrac{1}{x}\right| \leq 1$ and $x \to 0$, so by the **bounded times infinitesimal** theorem $x \sin \dfrac{1}{x} \to 0$. The limit of $\sin \dfrac{1}{x}$ as $x \to 0$ does not exist, because in every neighborhood of $0$ the function takes both the value $1$ and the value $-1$ infinitely many times. The limit of $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ as $x \to 0$ equals $1$ by the fundamental sine limit. With the change of variable $y = \dfrac{1}{x} \to 0^{+}$ we have $x \sin \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{\sin y}{y} \to 1$ as $x \to +\infty$.
