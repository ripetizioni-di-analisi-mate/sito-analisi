What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{(2x + 1)^3}{(x^2 + 1)(3x - 1)}$?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- $2$
- $0$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator are polynomials of degree $3$ that tend to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. The highest-degree term of the numerator is $(2x)^3 = 8x^3$, and that of the denominator is $x^2 \cdot 3x = 3x^3$. In a ratio of polynomials as $x \to +\infty$ only the highest-degree terms matter, therefore the limit equals
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{8x^3}{3x^3} = \frac{8}{3} .
$$
