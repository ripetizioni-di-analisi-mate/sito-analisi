What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $4$
- $0$

## Explanation

At $x = 4$ the numerator and the denominator equal $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $x - 4 = (\sqrt{x} - 2)(\sqrt{x} + 2)$ for $x \geq 0$, for $x \neq 4$ we have
$$
\frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2} .
$$
The resulting function is continuous at $x = 4$, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2 + 2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
