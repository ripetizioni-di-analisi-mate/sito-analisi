- $\dfrac{5}{3}$
- $1$
- $\sin \dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ we have $\sin 3x \to 0$ and $\sin 5x \to 0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. For $x \neq 0$ near $0$ we write
$$
\frac{\sin 3x}{\sin 5x} = \frac{\sin 3x}{3x} \cdot \frac{5x}{\sin 5x} \cdot \frac{3}{5} .
$$
With the changes of variable $y = 3x \to 0$ and $y = 5x \to 0$, by the **fundamental sine limit** the first two factors tend to $1$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{3}{5}$.
