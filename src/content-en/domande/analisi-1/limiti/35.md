What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} x \ln x$?

- $-\infty$
- $0$
- $1$
- $-1$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $x \to 0$ and $\ln x \to -\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $0 \cdot \infty$. With the change of variable $t = \dfrac{1}{x}$ we have $t \to +\infty$ and $\ln x = -\ln t$, therefore
$$
x \ln x = -\frac{\ln t}{t} .
$$
By the **hierarchy of growth rates** the logarithm grows more slowly than every power, so $\dfrac{\ln t}{t} \to 0$ and the limit equals $0$.
