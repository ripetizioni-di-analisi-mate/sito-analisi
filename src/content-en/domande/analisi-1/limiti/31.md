What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2^{x} - 1}{x}$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{\ln 2}$
- $\ln 2$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. We write $2^{x} = e^{x \ln 2}$, so with the change of variable $y = x \ln 2 \to 0$ we obtain
$$
\frac{2^{x} - 1}{x} = \frac{e^{x \ln 2} - 1}{x \ln 2} \cdot \ln 2 .
$$
By the fundamental limit $\displaystyle\lim_{y \to 0} \frac{e^{y} - 1}{y} = 1$ the first factor tends to $1$, therefore the limit equals $\ln 2$.
