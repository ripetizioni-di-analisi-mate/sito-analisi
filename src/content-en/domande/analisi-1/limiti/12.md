- Jump for $f$, removable for $g$
- Removable for $f$, jump for $g$
- Second kind for $f$, removable for $g$
- Jump for $f$, second kind for $g$

## Explanation

We have $\dfrac{|x|}{x} = 1$ for $x > 0$ and $\dfrac{|x|}{x} = -1$ for $x < 0$, so the one-sided limits of $f$ at $0$ equal $1$ and $-1$. Since they are finite and different, $x = 0$ is a **jump discontinuity** for $f$. For $x \neq 1$ we have $\dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = x + 1$, so the limit of $g$ as $x \to 1$ equals $2$. Since the limit exists and is finite and $g(1)$ is not defined, $x = 1$ is a **removable discontinuity** for $g$.
