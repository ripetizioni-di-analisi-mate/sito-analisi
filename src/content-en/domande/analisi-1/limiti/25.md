What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x \right)$?

- $2$
- $0$
- $-2$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

The square root tends to $+\infty$ and $x$ tends to $-\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $+\infty - \infty$. Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x$ we obtain
$$
\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x = \frac{(x^2 + 4x) - x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x} = \frac{4x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x} .
$$
For $x < -4$ we have $\sqrt{x^2} = |x| = -x$, so $\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} = -x \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{x}}$ and the denominator is $-x \left( \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{x}} + 1 \right)$. Canceling $x$, the quotient becomes $\dfrac{-4}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{x}} + 1}$, which tends to $-\dfrac{4}{2} = -2$.
