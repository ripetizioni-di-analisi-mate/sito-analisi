For the function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{\frac{1}{x}}}$, what are the one-sided limits at $x = 0$, and what kind of discontinuity is it?

- Right $1$, left $0$; jump
- Right $0$, left $1$; jump
- Right $0$, left $1$; second kind
- Right $\dfrac{1}{2}$, left $\dfrac{1}{2}$; removable

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to +\infty$, so $e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to +\infty$ and the denominator tends to $+\infty$. Therefore the right limit equals $0$.

As $x \to 0^{-}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to -\infty$, so $e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to 0$ and the denominator tends to $1$. Therefore the left limit equals $1$.

The two one-sided limits exist, are finite and are different, so $x = 0$ is a **jump discontinuity**.
