Which statement about $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \cos x$ is true?

- The limit equals $0$
- The limit does not exist
- The limit equals $1$
- The limit equals $-1$

## Explanation

Suppose, by contradiction, that the limit equals $L \in \mathbb{R}$. By the definition of limit with $\varepsilon = \dfrac{1}{2}$ there exists $M$ such that $|\cos x - L| < \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $x > M$.

For a large enough integer $n$ the points $2n\pi$ and $(2n + 1)\pi$ are greater than $M$, and at these points the cosine equals $1$ and $-1$. Hence $|1 - L| < \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $|-1 - L| < \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore
$$
2 = |1 - (-1)| \leq |1 - L| + |L + 1| < 1 ,
$$
which is absurd.

The limit cannot be infinite either, because $|\cos x| \leq 1$ for every $x$. Therefore the limit **does not exist**.
