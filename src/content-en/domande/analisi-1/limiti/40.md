For the function $f(x) = e^{\frac{1}{x}}$, what are the one-sided limits at $x = 0$ and what kind of discontinuity is it?

- Right $0$, left $+\infty$; second kind
- Right $+\infty$, left $0$; second kind
- Right $+\infty$, left $0$; jump
- Right $1$, left $1$; removable

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to +\infty$, so $e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to +\infty$. As $x \to 0^{-}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to -\infty$, so $e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to 0$. Since the right-hand limit is infinite, the point $x = 0$ is a **discontinuity of the second kind**. A jump discontinuity requires both one-sided limits to be finite.
