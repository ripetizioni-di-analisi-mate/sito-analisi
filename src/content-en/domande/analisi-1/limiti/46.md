The numerator and the denominator of $\dfrac{x^3 - 3x + 2}{x^2 - 2x + 1}$ vanish at $x = 1$. What is the limit of the quotient as $x \to 1$?

- $0$
- $1$
- $3$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

At $x = 1$ the numerator equals $1 - 3 + 2 = 0$ and the denominator equals $0$, so the limit is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

Ruffini's rule gives $x^3 - 3x + 2 = (x - 1)(x^2 + x - 2) = (x - 1)^2 (x + 2)$, while the denominator is the square $(x - 1)^2$. Since $1$ is a double root of both polynomials, for $x \neq 1$ we have
$$
\frac{x^3 - 3x + 2}{x^2 - 2x + 1} = \frac{(x - 1)^2 (x + 2)}{(x - 1)^2} = x + 2 .
$$
Therefore the limit equals $1 + 2 = 3$.
