The function $f(x) = \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 2}$, defined on $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{2\}$, is injective. What is its inverse?

- $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{y - 2}{y + 3}$
- $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{2y - 3}{y - 1}$
- $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{2y + 3}{y - 1}$
- $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{2y + 3}{1 - y}$

## Explanation

We solve the equation $y = \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 2}$ for $x$. Multiplying by $x - 2$ gives $y(x - 2) = x + 3$, that is, $xy - x = 2y + 3$. Factoring out $x$ we have $x(y - 1) = 2y + 3$, so for $y \neq 1$ we obtain $x = \dfrac{2y + 3}{y - 1}$. For $y = 1$ the equation becomes $0 = 5$, which has no solutions; therefore the range is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}$ and $f^{-1}(y) = \dfrac{2y + 3}{y - 1}$. The expression $\dfrac{y - 2}{y + 3}$ is the reciprocal $\dfrac{1}{f(y)}$.
