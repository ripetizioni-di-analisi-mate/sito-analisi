In the inductive step of the proof that $n^3 + 5n$ is divisible by $6$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, one computes the difference $\bigl( (n+1)^3 + 5(n+1) \bigr) - (n^3 + 5n)$. What does it equal?

- $3n(n+1) + 5$
- $3n(n+1) + 6$
- $3n(n+1) + 1$
- $n(n+1) + 6$

## Explanation

Expanding the cube gives $(n+1)^3 = n^3 + 3n^2 + 3n + 1$, so $(n+1)^3 + 5(n+1) = n^3 + 3n^2 + 3n + 1 + 5n + 5$. Subtracting $n^3 + 5n$ leaves $3n^2 + 3n + 6 = 3n(n+1) + 6$. The product $n(n+1)$ of two consecutive natural numbers is even, therefore $3n(n+1)$ is divisible by $6$. Then $(n+1)^3 + 5(n+1)$ is the sum of $n^3 + 5n$, a multiple of $6$ by the inductive hypothesis, and of $3n(n+1) + 6$, which is also a multiple of $6$; hence it is divisible by $6$.
