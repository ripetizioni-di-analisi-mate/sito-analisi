We want to prove by induction that $4^n + 1$ is divisible by $3$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$. For every $n$ the equality $4^{n+1} + 1 = 4\,(4^n + 1) - 3$ holds. Which statement is true?

- The inductive step holds and the property holds for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$
- The inductive step holds, but the property holds for no $n \in \mathbb{N}$
- The inductive step does not hold, but the property holds for every $n \geq 1$
- The property holds only for even $n$

## Explanation

Suppose that $4^n + 1 = 3m$ with $m$ an integer. Then $4^{n+1} + 1 = 4 \cdot 3m - 3 = 3\,(4m - 1)$, so $4^{n+1} + 1$ is also a multiple of $3$ and the **inductive step** holds.

The base case, however, is false, because for $n = 0$ we get $4^0 + 1 = 2$, which is not divisible by $3$. Without the base case the principle of induction does not apply.

In fact $4^n - 1 = (4 - 1)(4^{n-1} + \cdots + 4 + 1)$ is a multiple of $3$ for every $n \geq 1$, and it equals $0$ for $n = 0$. Since $4^n + 1 = (4^n - 1) + 2$, dividing $4^n + 1$ by $3$ leaves remainder $2$ for every $n$. Therefore the property **holds for no** $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
