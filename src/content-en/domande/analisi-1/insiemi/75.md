Let $f(x) = 2x + 1$ and $g(x) = x^2$. For which $x \in \mathbb{R}$ does $(g \circ f)(x) = (f \circ g)(x)$ hold?

- $x = -2$ or $x = 0$
- Only for $x = 0$
- $x = 0$ or $x = 2$
- For no $x$

## Explanation

By definition of composite function, $(g \circ f)(x) = g(2x + 1) = (2x + 1)^2$ and $(f \circ g)(x) = f(x^2) = 2x^2 + 1$.

The equality $(2x + 1)^2 = 2x^2 + 1$ becomes $4x^2 + 4x + 1 = 2x^2 + 1$, that is, $2x^2 + 4x = 0$. Factoring out $2x$ gives $2x(x + 2) = 0$.

Hence the two composites coincide only for $x = 0$ and for $x = -2$. At the other points they are different, in agreement with the fact that composition depends on the order.
