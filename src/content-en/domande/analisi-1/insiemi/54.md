The set $A$ consists of the numbers $\dfrac{1}{m} + \dfrac{1}{n}$, with $m$ and $n$ natural numbers greater than or equal to $1$ chosen independently. What can be said about its bounds?

- $\max A = 2$ and $\min A = 0$
- $\max A = 2$ and $\inf A = 1$
- $\sup A = 2$, $A$ has no maximum and $\inf A = 0$
- $\max A = 2$, $\inf A = 0$ and $A$ has no minimum

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{1}{m} \leq 1$ and $\dfrac{1}{n} \leq 1$, every element is less than or equal to $2$; the value $2$ is obtained with $m = n = 1$, so $\max A = 2$.

Every element is positive, so $0$ is a lower bound. Given $\varepsilon > 0$, by the Archimedean property there is a natural number $n > \dfrac{2}{\varepsilon}$; with $m = n$ we get the element $\dfrac{2}{n} < \varepsilon$. Both conditions of the characterization are satisfied, therefore $\inf A = 0$.

Since every element is strictly positive, $0 \notin A$ and so $A$ **has no minimum**. The value $1$ is the infimum of the elements with $m = 1$ only.
