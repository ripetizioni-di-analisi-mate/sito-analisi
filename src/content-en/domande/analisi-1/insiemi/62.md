What is the term that does not contain $x$ in the expansion of $\left( x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x} \right)^6$?

- $20$
- $6$
- The expansion has no term without $x$
- $15$

## Explanation

By the binomial theorem the general term of the expansion is
$$
\binom{6}{k} \left( x^2 \right)^{6 - k} \left( \frac{1}{x} \right)^{k} = \binom{6}{k}\, x^{12 - 2k - k} = \binom{6}{k}\, x^{12 - 3k} ,
$$
with $k = 0, 1, \dots, 6$.

The term does not contain $x$ when the exponent is zero, that is, for $12 - 3k = 0$, hence for $k = 4$. The term therefore equals $\dbinom{6}{4} = \dbinom{6}{2} = 15$.

The value $20 = \dbinom{6}{3}$ is the central coefficient, which corresponds to $x^{3}$.
