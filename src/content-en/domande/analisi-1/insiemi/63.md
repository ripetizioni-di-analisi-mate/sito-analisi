Which number is obtained by expanding $\left( 1 + \sqrt{2} \right)^4$?

- $17 + 12\sqrt{2}$
- $7 + 3\sqrt{2}$
- $1 + 15\sqrt{2}$
- $13 + 12\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

By the binomial theorem with the coefficients $1, 4, 6, 4, 1$ of row $n = 4$,
$$
\left( 1 + \sqrt{2} \right)^4 = 1 + 4\sqrt{2} + 6 \left( \sqrt{2} \right)^2 + 4 \left( \sqrt{2} \right)^3 + \left( \sqrt{2} \right)^4 .
$$
Since $\left( \sqrt{2} \right)^2 = 2$, $\left( \sqrt{2} \right)^3 = 2\sqrt{2}$ and $\left( \sqrt{2} \right)^4 = 4$, the sum becomes $1 + 4\sqrt{2} + 12 + 8\sqrt{2} + 4$.

Collecting the rational terms and those with $\sqrt{2}$ gives $17 + 12\sqrt{2}$.
