What is the solution set of the inequality $\sin x > \cos x$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$?

- $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4},\ \dfrac{3\pi}{4} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4},\ \dfrac{5\pi}{4} \right)$
- $\left[ 0,\ \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) \cup \left( \dfrac{5\pi}{4},\ 2\pi \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4},\ \dfrac{7\pi}{4} \right)$

## Explanation

By the addition formula, $\sqrt{2} \sin\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = \sqrt{2} \left( \sin x \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \cos x \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) = \sin x - \cos x$. Hence the inequality is equivalent to $\sin\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) > 0$.

For $x \in [0, 2\pi)$ the angle $t = x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ ranges over $\left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{4},\ \dfrac{7\pi}{4} \right)$, and in this interval $\sin t > 0$ only for $0 < t < \pi$.

Therefore $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$. The endpoints are the two points of $[0, 2\pi)$ where $\sin x = \cos x$.
