In the proof by induction of the formula $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n} 2^k = 2^{n+1} - 1$, one assumes that it holds for $n$. Which expression is equal to $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1} 2^k$?

- $2^{n+1} + 2^n - 1$
- $2^{n+2} - 1$
- $2^{2n+2} - 1$
- $2^{n+2} - 2$

## Explanation

The sum up to $n + 1$ is the sum up to $n$ plus the term $2^{n+1}$. By the inductive hypothesis the sum up to $n$ equals $2^{n+1} - 1$, so the required sum is $\bigl( 2^{n+1} - 1 \bigr) + 2^{n+1} = 2 \cdot 2^{n+1} - 1$. Since $2 \cdot 2^{n+1} = 2^{n+2}$, we obtain $2^{n+2} - 1$, which is the right-hand side of the formula with $n + 1$ in place of $n$. The result $2^{2n+2} - 1$ is obtained by replacing the sum $2^{n+1} + 2^{n+1}$ with the product $2^{n+1} \cdot 2^{n+1} = 2^{2n+2}$.
