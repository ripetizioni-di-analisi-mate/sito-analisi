In the sum $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} (k + 1)$ we set $j = k + 1$. How is the same sum written with the index $j$?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n} j$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n+1} j$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n} j$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} j$

## Explanation

With $j = k + 1$ the general term $k + 1$ becomes $j$. For $k = 1$ we have $j = 2$ and for $k = n$ we have $j = n + 1$, so $j$ runs over the integers from $2$ to $n + 1$.

Therefore $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} (k + 1) = \sum_{j=2}^{n+1} j$. For example, with $n = 3$ both sums equal $2 + 3 + 4 = 9$.

The sum $\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n} j$ changes the general term but leaves the upper limit unchanged, and thus loses the term $n + 1$.
