What is the fundamental period of the function $f(x) = \sin^2 x$?

- $2\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$
- $4\pi$

## Explanation

From the **double-angle formula** $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$ we obtain $\sin^2 x = \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$. The function $\cos x$ has fundamental period $2\pi$, so $\cos 2x$, which is obtained by a horizontal compression by a factor of $2$, has fundamental period $\pi$. Subtracting from $1$ and dividing by $2$ are vertical transformations, which leave the periods unchanged, therefore the fundamental period of $\sin^2 x$ is $\pi$. Indeed $\sin^2 (x + \pi) = (-\sin x)^2 = \sin^2 x$. The value $2\pi$ is a period of $\sin^2 x$, but not the smallest one, and the value $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is not a period, because $\sin^2 0 = 0$ and $\sin^2 \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$.
