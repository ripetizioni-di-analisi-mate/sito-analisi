The function $f(x) = x^2 - 4x$, restricted to the interval $[2, +\infty)$, is strictly increasing. What is its inverse function, together with its domain?

- $f^{-1}(y) = 2 - \sqrt{y + 4}$, for $y \geq -4$
- $f^{-1}(y) = 2 + \sqrt{y - 4}$, for $y \geq 4$
- $f^{-1}(y) = 2 + \sqrt{y + 4}$, for $y \geq -4$
- $f^{-1}(y) = \sqrt{y + 4} - 2$, for $y \geq -4$

## Explanation

Completing the square gives $f(x) = (x - 2)^2 - 4$. For $x \geq 2$ the quantity $(x - 2)^2$ takes all the values of $[0, +\infty)$, so the image of the restriction is $[-4, +\infty)$ and this is the domain of the inverse.

We solve $y = (x - 2)^2 - 4$ for $x$: we get $(x - 2)^2 = y + 4$, that is, $x - 2 = \pm\sqrt{y + 4}$. Since $x \geq 2$, the difference $x - 2$ is greater than or equal to $0$, therefore we choose the sign $+$.

Hence $f^{-1}(y) = 2 + \sqrt{y + 4}$ for $y \geq -4$. The sign $-$ gives the inverse of the restriction to $(-\infty, 2]$.
