The following four subsets of $\mathbb{Q}$ are nonempty and bounded above. Which of them does *not* have a supremum in $\mathbb{Q}$?

- $\{x \in \mathbb{Q} : x \geq 0,\ x^2 < 3\}$
- $\{x \in \mathbb{Q} : x^2 < 4\}$
- $\{x \in \mathbb{Q} : x < 1\}$
- $\left\{ x \in \mathbb{Q} : x^2 \leq \dfrac{9}{4} \right\}$

## Explanation

In $\mathbb{R}$ the supremum of $\{x \in \mathbb{Q} : x \geq 0,\ x^2 < 3\}$ is $\sqrt{3}$, which is irrational. Every rational upper bound $L$ is also an upper bound in $\mathbb{R}$, so $L \geq \sqrt{3}$, and $L \neq \sqrt{3}$ because $\sqrt{3} \notin \mathbb{Q}$. By the **density** of $\mathbb{Q}$ there then exists a rational $q$ with $\sqrt{3} < q < L$, which is an upper bound smaller than $L$. Therefore no rational upper bound is the least of the rational upper bounds, and the set **has no supremum** in $\mathbb{Q}$. The other three sets have a rational supremum: $2$, $1$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$, and in the last case $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is also the maximum.
