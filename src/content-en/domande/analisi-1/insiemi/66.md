How many integers belong to the natural domain of $f(x) = \sqrt{x - 1} + \sqrt{3 - x}$?

- $1$
- $2$
- Infinitely many
- $3$

## Explanation

Each square root requires an argument greater than or equal to $0$, and the two conditions must hold together:
$$
\begin{cases}
x - 1 \geq 0 \\[4pt]
3 - x \geq 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\[4pt]
x \leq 3
\end{cases}
$$
Hence the domain is $[1, 3]$. The endpoints belong to the domain, because at $x = 1$ and at $x = 3$ one of the two radicands equals $0$ and the root of $0$ is defined.

Therefore the integers in the domain are $1$, $2$, $3$, that is, three of them. Excluding the endpoints, only $2$ would remain.
