The inequality $2^n \geq n + 1$, for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, is a special case of Bernoulli's inequality $(1 + x)^n \geq 1 + nx$, valid for $x \geq -1$. For which value of $x$ is it obtained?

- $x = 2$
- $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x = 1$
- $x = -1$

## Explanation

The left-hand side $(1 + x)^n$ coincides with $2^n$ when $1 + x = 2$, that is, for $x = 1$. The value $x = 1$ satisfies the hypothesis $x \geq -1$, so **Bernoulli's inequality** applies. With $x = 1$ the right-hand side becomes $1 + n$, therefore we obtain $2^n \geq n + 1$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$. With $x = 2$ one obtains instead $3^n \geq 1 + 2n$.
