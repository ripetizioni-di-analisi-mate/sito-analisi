For which $x \in \mathbb{R}$ does the equality $\lfloor 2x \rfloor = 2\lfloor x \rfloor$ hold? The symbol $\lfloor t \rfloor$ denotes the integer part (floor) of $t$, that is, the largest integer less than or equal to $t$.

- $\displaystyle\bigcup_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} \left[ m + \frac{1}{2},\ m + 1 \right)$
- $\mathbb{Z}$
- $\displaystyle\bigcup_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} \left[ m,\ m + \frac{1}{2} \right)$
- $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

We write $x = m + s$, with $m = \lfloor x \rfloor$ an integer and $0 \leq s < 1$. Then $2x = 2m + 2s$, and since $2m$ is an integer we have $\lfloor 2x \rfloor = 2m + \lfloor 2s \rfloor$.

The equality $\lfloor 2x \rfloor = 2m$ therefore holds if and only if $\lfloor 2s \rfloor = 0$, that is, $0 \leq 2s < 1$, that is, $0 \leq s < \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Therefore the required set is the union of the intervals $\left[ m,\ m + \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ as $m$ ranges over $\mathbb{Z}$. For example, for $x = 0.7$ we have $\lfloor 1.4 \rfloor = 1$ and $2\lfloor 0.7 \rfloor = 0$.
