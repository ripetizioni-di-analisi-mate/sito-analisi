In the inductive step of the inequality $n! \geq 2^{\,n-1}$, valid for $n \geq 1$, one writes $(n+1)! = (n+1) \cdot n! \geq (n+1) \cdot 2^{\,n-1}$. Which inequality, true for every $n \geq 1$, makes it possible to conclude that $(n+1)! \geq 2^n$?

- $n + 1 \geq 2$
- $n + 1 \geq 2^n$
- $n \geq 2$
- $n + 1 \geq 2^{\,n-1}$

## Explanation

Since $2^n = 2 \cdot 2^{\,n-1}$, comparing $(n+1) \cdot 2^{\,n-1}$ with $2^n$ reduces to comparing the factors $n + 1$ and $2$. For every $n \geq 1$ we have $n + 1 \geq 2$, and multiplying by $2^{\,n-1} > 0$ gives $(n+1) \cdot 2^{\,n-1} \geq 2^n$. Therefore $(n+1)! \geq 2^n$, which is the inequality with $n + 1$ in place of $n$. The inequality $n \geq 2$ is false for $n = 1$, the inequality $n + 1 \geq 2^n$ is false for $n = 2$, and the inequality $n + 1 \geq 2^{\,n-1}$ is false for $n = 4$.
