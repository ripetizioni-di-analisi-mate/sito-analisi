In the inductive step of the proof that $5^n - 1$ is divisible by $4$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, one writes $5^{n+1} - 1$ in terms of $5^n - 1$. Which equality is correct?

- $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\,(5^n - 1) + 1$
- $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\,(5^n - 1)$
- $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\,(5^n - 1) - 4$
- $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\,(5^n - 1) + 4$

## Explanation

We have $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5 \cdot 5^n - 1$. Since $5 \cdot 5^n = 5\,(5^n - 1) + 5$, we obtain $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\,(5^n - 1) + 5 - 1 = 5\,(5^n - 1) + 4$. By the inductive hypothesis $5^n - 1 = 4m$ with $m$ an integer, so $5^{n+1} - 1 = 20m + 4 = 4\,(5m + 1)$. Therefore $5^{n+1} - 1$ is also divisible by $4$, and the **inductive step** is verified.
