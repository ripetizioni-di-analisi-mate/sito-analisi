In the proof by induction of the formula $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^3 = \frac{n^2 (n+1)^2}{4}$, one assumes that it holds for $n$. Which expression is equal to $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1} k^3$?

- $\dfrac{n^2 (n+1)^2 + (n+1)^3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{(n+1)^2 (n^2 + n + 1)}{4}$
- $\dfrac{(n+1)^2 (n^2 + 4)}{4}$
- $\dfrac{(n+1)^2 (n+2)^2}{4}$

## Explanation

The sum up to $n + 1$ is the sum up to $n$ plus the term $(n+1)^3$, and by the inductive hypothesis the sum up to $n$ equals $\dfrac{n^2 (n+1)^2}{4}$. To bring the two terms to the same denominator, we write $(n+1)^3 = \dfrac{4(n+1)^3}{4}$. In the numerator we factor out $(n+1)^2$ and obtain $(n+1)^2 \bigl( n^2 + 4(n+1) \bigr) = (n+1)^2 (n^2 + 4n + 4)$. Since $n^2 + 4n + 4 = (n+2)^2$, the sum equals $\dfrac{(n+1)^2 (n+2)^2}{4}$. This expression is the right-hand side of the formula with $n + 1$ in place of $n$, therefore the **inductive step** is verified.
