The sequence $(a_n)$ is defined by $a_1 = 1$ and $a_{n+1} = 2a_n + 1$ for every $n \geq 1$. Which formula holds for every $n \geq 1$?

- $a_n = 2n - 1$
- $a_n = 2^{n-1}$
- $a_n = 2^{n+1} - 1$
- $a_n = 2^n - 1$

## Explanation

From the definition we obtain the first terms $a_1 = 1$, $a_2 = 3$, $a_3 = 7$, $a_4 = 15$, which coincide with the values of $2^n - 1$.

The formula $a_n = 2^n - 1$ is proved by induction. For $n = 1$ we have $2^1 - 1 = 1 = a_1$, so the base case holds. If $a_n = 2^n - 1$, then $a_{n+1} = 2\,(2^n - 1) + 1 = 2^{n+1} - 1$, which is the formula with $n + 1$ in place of $n$; therefore the **inductive step** holds. By the **principle of induction** the formula holds for every $n \geq 1$.

The formula $2n - 1$ gives the correct values for $n = 1$ and $n = 2$, but for $n = 3$ it equals $5$ instead of $7$.
