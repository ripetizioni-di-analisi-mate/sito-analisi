What is the natural domain of $f(x) = \sqrt{\log_{\frac{1}{2}} (x - 1)}$?

- $(1, 2]$
- $[2, +\infty)$
- $(1, 2)$
- $(-\infty, 2]$

## Explanation

The logarithm requires a positive argument and the square root a radicand greater than or equal to $0$:
$$
\begin{cases}
x - 1 > 0 \\[4pt]
\log_{\frac{1}{2}} (x - 1) \geq 0 = \log_{\frac{1}{2}} 1
\end{cases}
$$
The base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is less than $1$, so the logarithm is strictly decreasing and comparing the arguments reverses the inequality; therefore the second condition is equivalent to $x - 1 \leq 1$, that is, $x \leq 2$.

Combining $x > 1$ and $x \leq 2$ gives the domain $(1, 2]$. The point $x = 2$ belongs to the domain, because there the logarithm equals $0$.
