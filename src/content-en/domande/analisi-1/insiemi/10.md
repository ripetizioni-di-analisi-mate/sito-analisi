To prove that $\sqrt{5}$ is irrational, one assumes $\sqrt{5} = \dfrac{m}{n}$, with $m$ and $n$ natural numbers and the fraction in lowest terms. From $m^2 = 5n^2$ it follows that $5$ divides $m$, that is, $m = 5k$ with $k$ a natural number. Which equality is obtained by substituting $m = 5k$ into $m^2 = 5n^2$?

- $n^2 = 25k^2$
- $n^2 = 5k^2$
- $5n^2 = k^2$
- $n = 5k$

## Explanation

Substituting $m = 5k$ gives $(5k)^2 = 5n^2$, that is, $25k^2 = 5n^2$. Dividing both sides by $5$ yields $n^2 = 5k^2$. Hence $5$ divides $n^2$ and, since $5$ is prime, $5$ divides $n$. Then $5$ divides both $m$ and $n$, contradicting the assumption that the fraction is in lowest terms; therefore $\sqrt{5}$ is **irrational**.
