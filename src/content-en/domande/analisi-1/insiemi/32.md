- $1 < x < 2$
- $-1 < x < 2$
- $x > 2$
- $0 < x < 2$

## Explanation

The domain conditions of the two logarithms form the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\[4pt]
x - 1 > 0
\end{cases}
$$
which is satisfied for $x > 1$. For $x > 1$ the sum of the logarithms is $\log_2 \bigl( x(x - 1) \bigr)$ and $1 = \log_2 2$; since the base $2$ is greater than $1$, the inequality is equivalent to $x(x - 1) < 2$. The trinomial $x^2 - x - 2$ has roots $-1$ and $2$, so $x^2 - x - 2 < 0$ for $-1 < x < 2$. The solutions are the points of $(-1, 2)$ with $x > 1$, that is, $1 < x < 2$.
