Let $A = \left\{ \dfrac{n}{n+2} : n \in \mathbb{N},\ n \geq 1 \right\}$ and fix $\varepsilon > 0$. Which natural numbers $n$ satisfy $\dfrac{n}{n+2} > 1 - \varepsilon$?

- $n > \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon} - 2$
- $n > \dfrac{2}{\varepsilon}$
- $n < \dfrac{2}{\varepsilon} - 2$
- $n > \dfrac{2}{\varepsilon} - 2$

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{n}{n+2} = 1 - \dfrac{2}{n+2}$, the inequality is equivalent to $1 - \dfrac{2}{n+2} > 1 - \varepsilon$, that is, to $\dfrac{2}{n+2} < \varepsilon$. The numbers $n + 2$ and $\varepsilon$ are positive, so multiplying both sides by $\dfrac{n+2}{\varepsilon}$ gives $\dfrac{2}{\varepsilon} < n + 2$. Therefore the inequality is satisfied for $n > \dfrac{2}{\varepsilon} - 2$. For every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exist natural numbers with this property, so the second condition of the **characterization of the supremum** is satisfied. Since $\dfrac{2}{n+2} > 0$, every element of $A$ is less than $1$; therefore the first condition is also satisfied and $\sup A = 1$.
