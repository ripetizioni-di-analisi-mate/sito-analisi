What is the infimum of $A = \left\{ (-1)^n \, \dfrac{n}{n+1} : n \in \mathbb{N},\ n \geq 1 \right\}$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $-1$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

For odd $n$ the elements equal $-\dfrac{n}{n+1}$ and are negative, while for even $n$ they are positive; hence the infimum depends only on the terms with odd $n$. Since $\dfrac{n}{n+1} < 1$, every element is greater than $-1$, and therefore $-1$ is a lower bound. We have $-\dfrac{n}{n+1} = -1 + \dfrac{1}{n+1}$, so, given $\varepsilon > 0$, every odd $n$ with $n + 1 > \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}$ gives an element less than $-1 + \varepsilon$. By the **characterization of the infimum** it follows that $\inf A = -1$. The value $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, obtained for $n = 1$, is the largest of the negative terms.
