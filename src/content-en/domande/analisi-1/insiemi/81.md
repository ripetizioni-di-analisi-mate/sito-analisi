How many integers satisfy the inequality $|x - 3| \leq 2$?

- $3$
- $4$
- $5$
- $6$

## Explanation

The inequality $|x - 3| \leq 2$ says that the distance of $x$ from $3$ is at most $2$, so it is equivalent to $-2 \leq x - 3 \leq 2$.

Adding $3$ to the three members gives $1 \leq x \leq 5$, that is, the interval $[1, 5]$. The endpoints belong to the set, because at them the equality $|x - 3| = 2$ holds.

Therefore the integers satisfying the inequality are $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$, that is, five of them. With the strict inequality one would get only $2$, $3$, $4$.
