Denoting by $\lfloor t \rfloor$ the integer part (floor) of $t$, that is, the largest integer less than or equal to $t$, which real numbers solve the equation $\lfloor x^2 \rfloor = 1$?

- $\left[ 1,\ \sqrt{2} \right)$
- $\{-1, 1\}$
- $\left( -\sqrt{2},\ -1 \right] \cup \left[ 1,\ \sqrt{2} \right)$
- $\left[ -\sqrt{2},\ -1 \right) \cup \left( 1,\ \sqrt{2} \right]$

## Explanation

By definition $\lfloor t \rfloor = 1$ if and only if $1 \leq t < 2$. With $t = x^2$ the equation is therefore equivalent to $1 \leq x^2 < 2$.

The condition $x^2 \geq 1$ is satisfied for $x \leq -1$ or $x \geq 1$, and the condition $x^2 < 2$ for $-\sqrt{2} < x < \sqrt{2}$. The two conditions must hold together, therefore the solution set is $\left( -\sqrt{2},\ -1 \right] \cup \left[ 1,\ \sqrt{2} \right)$.

The endpoints $\pm 1$ are included, because $\lfloor 1 \rfloor = 1$; the endpoints $\pm\sqrt{2}$ are excluded, because $\lfloor 2 \rfloor = 2$.
