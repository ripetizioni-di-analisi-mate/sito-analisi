Given $f(x) = x^2 - 1$ and $g(x) = \sqrt{x + 3}$, what is the composite function $f \circ g$, together with its domain?

- $(f \circ g)(x) = x + 2$, with domain $\mathbb{R}$
- $(f \circ g)(x) = x + 2$, with domain $[-3, +\infty)$
- $(f \circ g)(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 2}$, with domain $\mathbb{R}$
- $(f \circ g)(x) = x + 4$, with domain $[-3, +\infty)$

## Explanation

By definition $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$, and the domain of the composite is the set of $x$ in the domain of $g$ for which $g(x)$ belongs to the domain of $f$. The domain of $g$ is $[-3, +\infty)$ and $f$ is defined on $\mathbb{R}$, so the domain of the composite is $[-3, +\infty)$. For $x \geq -3$ we have $f(g(x)) = \bigl( \sqrt{x + 3} \bigr)^2 - 1 = x + 3 - 1 = x + 2$. The expression $x + 2$ makes sense for every real $x$, while the composite is defined only where $g$ is defined; therefore the domain remains $[-3, +\infty)$. The expression $\sqrt{x^2 + 2}$ is the composite $g \circ f$, in the opposite order.
