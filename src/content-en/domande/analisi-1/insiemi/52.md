Which of the following numbers is rational?

- $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{8}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{18}}{\sqrt{2}}$
- $\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{6}$
- $\sqrt{12} - \sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

Each number is simplified with the properties of roots. We have $\dfrac{\sqrt{18}}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{9} = 3$, which is rational.

The other three reduce to roots of prime numbers: $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{8} = \sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{2} = 3\sqrt{2}$, then $\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{6} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{12} - \sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3} = \sqrt{3}$.

The numbers $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$ are irrational, because they are square roots of prime numbers. The number $3\sqrt{2}$ is irrational too, because if $3\sqrt{2} = q$ with $q$ rational, then $\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{q}{3}$ would be rational.
