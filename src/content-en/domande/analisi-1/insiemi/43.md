What is the value of $\arcsin\left( \sin \dfrac{5\pi}{6} \right)$?

- $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

By the addition formula, since $\sin \pi = 0$ and $\cos \pi = -1$, we have $\sin\left( \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right) = \sin \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, so $\sin \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. By definition $\arcsin \dfrac{1}{2}$ is the unique angle in $\left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$ whose sine equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Therefore $\arcsin\left( \sin \dfrac{5\pi}{6} \right) = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The angle $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ also has sine $\dfrac{1}{2}$, but it does not belong to the interval $\left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, so it is not the value of the arcsine. The equality $\arcsin(\sin x) = x$ holds only for $x \in \left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$.
