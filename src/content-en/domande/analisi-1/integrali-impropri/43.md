For the integral
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
$$
which statement is true?

- Converges absolutely, because $\left| \dfrac{\sin x}{x} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{x}$
- Converges, but does not converge absolutely
- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x}$
- It is oscillatory, because $\sin x$ has no limit as $x \to +\infty$

## Explanation

Integrating by parts, with $\dfrac{1}{x}$ as the factor to differentiate and $\sin x$ as the factor to integrate, one gets
$$
\int_1^{t} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx = \cos 1 - \frac{\cos t}{t} - \int_1^{t} \frac{\cos x}{x^{2}}\,dx .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ the term $\dfrac{\cos t}{t}$ tends to $0$, and the last integral converges absolutely because $\left| \dfrac{\cos x}{x^{2}} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$; hence the given integral converges. The integral of $\dfrac{|\sin x|}{x}$ over $[1, +\infty)$, instead, diverges, therefore the given integral **converges but does not converge absolutely**. The inequality $\left| \dfrac{\sin x}{x} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{x}$ is true, but the comparison integral with $\alpha = 1$ diverges and the **comparison test** gives no information.
