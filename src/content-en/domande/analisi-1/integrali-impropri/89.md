Find the value of
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{e^{-x}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx .
$$

- $\sqrt{\pi}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$
- $2\sqrt{\pi}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The singular points are $0$, where the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$, and $+\infty$, where the integrand is less than $e^{-x}$; both pieces converge. With the substitution $x = u^{2}$, $dx = 2u\,du$, the integral over $[a, b]$ with $0 < a < b$ becomes
$$
\int_a^{b} \frac{e^{-x}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx = \int_{\sqrt{a}}^{\sqrt{b}} 2\,e^{-u^{2}}\,du ,
$$
and as $a \to 0^{+}$, $b \to +\infty$ one gets $2 \displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-u^{2}}\,du$. The Gaussian integral over $\mathbb{R}$ equals $\sqrt{\pi}$ and the integrand is even, so $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-u^{2}}\,du = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$ and the given integral **converges** and equals $\sqrt{\pi}$.
