Compute
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{e^{x}}{e^{2x} + 1}\,dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\pi$

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous and positive on $[0, +\infty)$, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. With the substitution $u = e^{x}$, $du = e^{x}\,dx$, the endpoint $x = 0$ becomes $u = 1$ and the endpoint $x = t$ becomes $u = e^{t}$, therefore
$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{e^{x}}{e^{2x} + 1}\,dx = \int_1^{e^{t}} \frac{du}{u^{2} + 1} = \arctan e^{t} - \frac{\pi}{4} .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ one has $e^{t} \to +\infty$ and so $\arctan e^{t} \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Therefore the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
