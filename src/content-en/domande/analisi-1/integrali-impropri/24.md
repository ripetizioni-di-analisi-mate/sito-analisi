Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the integral
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{\alpha}\,(1 + x)}
$$
converges.

- $\alpha < 1$
- $\alpha > 0$
- $0 \leq \alpha \leq 1$
- $0 < \alpha < 1$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, +\infty)$ and the point $+\infty$ is critical, as is the point $0$ when $\alpha > 0$; so the integral is split at the point $1$. As $x \to 0^{+}$ one has $1 + x \to 1$, therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha}}$ and the integral over $(0, 1]$ converges if and only if $\alpha < 1$. As $x \to +\infty$ one has $1 + x \sim x$, therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha + 1}}$ and the integral over $[1, +\infty)$ converges if and only if $\alpha + 1 > 1$, that is $\alpha > 0$. The integral converges if both pieces converge, hence it **converges if and only if** $0 < \alpha < 1$.
