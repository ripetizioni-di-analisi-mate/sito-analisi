As $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the integral
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{\arctan x}{x^{\alpha}}\,dx
$$
converges for

- $\alpha > 1$
- $\alpha < 2$
- $1 < \alpha < 2$
- For no value of $\alpha$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, +\infty)$, and the integral is split at $1$. As $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $\arctan x \sim x$, so the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha - 1}}$ and the piece on $(0, 1]$ converges if and only if $\alpha - 1 < 1$, that is $\alpha < 2$. As $x \to +\infty$ we have $\arctan x \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{\pi}{2\,x^{\alpha}}$ and the piece on $[1, +\infty)$ converges if and only if $\alpha > 1$. Therefore the integral **converges** if and only if $1 < \alpha < 2$.
