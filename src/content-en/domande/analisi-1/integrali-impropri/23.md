For the integral
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \frac{x}{1 + x^{2}}\,dx
$$
which statement is true?

- Converges and equals $0$, because the integrand is odd
- Does not converge, because the integral over $[0, +\infty)$ diverges
- Converges and equals $\pi$
- Converges, because the integrand tends to $0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$

## Explanation

The critical points are $-\infty$ and $+\infty$, so the integral is split at $0$ and converges only if both pieces converge. As $x \to +\infty$ the integrand is positive and asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x}$, therefore the integral over $[0, +\infty)$ **diverges** by the **limit comparison test**. Hence the given integral **does not converge**. The symmetric limit $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to +\infty} \int_{-t}^{t} \frac{x}{1 + x^{2}}\,dx$ equals $0$ because the integrand is odd; this limit, however, does not coincide with the improper integral, which by definition requires the convergence of each piece.
