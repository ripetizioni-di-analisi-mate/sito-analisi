The inequalities
$$
\frac{1}{x^{2}} \leq f(x) \leq \frac{1}{x} \qquad \text{for every } x \geq 1
$$
hold for a function $f$ continuous on $[1, +\infty)$. Which statement about $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$ is true?

::grafico{f="1/x;1/x^2" area="1/x;1/x^2;1;9" xmin=-0.5 xmax=9 ymin=-0.15 ymax=1.25 titolo="The graph of $f$ lies in the shaded region, between $y = \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$"}

- Converges, by the comparison test with $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$
- Diverges, by the comparison test with $\dfrac{1}{x}$
- These inequalities are not enough to determine the behavior of the integral
- It is oscillatory

## Explanation

By the **comparison test**, convergence follows from an upper bound with convergent integral, but $\dfrac{1}{x}$ has a divergent integral on $[1, +\infty)$. Divergence follows from a lower bound with divergent integral, but $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$ has a convergent integral. Hence the two inequalities **are not enough**, because the function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ satisfies them and has a convergent integral, while the function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ satisfies them and has a divergent integral. Since $f \geq 0$, the integral cannot be oscillatory.
