Compute
$$
\int_e^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x \ln^{3} x} .
$$

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

For $x \geq e$ the integrand is continuous and positive, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. The integral reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)^{3}}\,dx = -\frac{1}{2 f(x)^{2}} + c$ with $f(x) = \ln x$, therefore
$$
\int_e^{t} \frac{dx}{x \ln^{3} x} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2 \ln^{2} t} .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ one has $\ln t \to +\infty$, hence the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
