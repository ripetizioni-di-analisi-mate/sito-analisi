The value of the integral
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-x} \sin x\,dx
$$
is

- $1$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Integrating by parts twice gives
$$
\int e^{-x} \sin x\,dx = -e^{-x} \sin x - e^{-x} \cos x - \int e^{-x} \sin x\,dx ,
$$
so an antiderivative is $-\dfrac{e^{-x}\,(\sin x + \cos x)}{2}$. Therefore
$$
\int_0^{t} e^{-x} \sin x\,dx = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{e^{-t}\,(\sin t + \cos t)}{2} .
$$
Since $|\sin t + \cos t| \leq 2$ and $e^{-t} \to 0$, the second term tends to $0$ as $t \to +\infty$, so the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
