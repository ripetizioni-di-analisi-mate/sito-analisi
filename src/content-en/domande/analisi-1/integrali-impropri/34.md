Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{\sqrt{x}}{\sin x}\,dx .
$$

- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x}$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is unbounded near $0$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\sqrt{x}$

## Explanation

On $(0, 1]$ one has $\sin x > 0$, so the integrand is positive; as $x \to 0^{+}$ the denominator tends to $0$ and the critical point is $0$. By the standard limit of the sine, $\sin x \sim x$, therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{x} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$. The order of infinity at $0$ is $\dfrac{1}{2} < 1$, hence the integral **converges** by the **limit comparison test**.
