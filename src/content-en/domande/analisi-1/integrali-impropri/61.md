The integrand of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^{3}}}
$$
tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to 1^{-}$. Which statement about the integral is true?

- Diverges, because the integrand is unbounded near $1$
- Diverges, because near $1$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{(1 - x)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$
- Converges, because the interval of integration is bounded
- Converges, because near $1$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3\,(1 - x)}}$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $[0, 1)$ and tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to 1^{-}$, so the singular point is $1$. From the factorization $1 - x^{3} = (1 - x)\,(1 + x + x^{2})$ and $1 + x + x^{2} \to 3$ it follows that $1 - x^{3} \sim 3\,(1 - x)$, therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}\,(1 - x)^{\frac{1}{2}}}$. The blow-up order is $\dfrac{1}{2} < 1$, so the integral **converges** by the **limit comparison test**.
