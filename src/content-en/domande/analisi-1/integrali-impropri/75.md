The integrand $\dfrac{1}{(1 + x)^{\alpha}}$ is continuous on $[0, +\infty)$ for every $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$. For which $\alpha$ does $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{(1 + x)^{\alpha}}$ converge?

- $\alpha \geq 1$
- $\alpha > 0$
- $\alpha > 1$
- $\alpha < 1$

## Explanation

The only critical point is $+\infty$. As $x \to +\infty$ we have $1 + x \sim x$, so the integrand is positive and asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha}}$. The comparison integral on $[1, +\infty)$ converges if and only if $\alpha > 1$, and the integral on $[0, 1]$ is ordinary, therefore by the **limit comparison test** the integral **converges** if and only if $\alpha > 1$.
