Compute
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} x\,e^{-x}\,dx .
$$

- $1$
- $-1$
- $0$
- $2$

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous on $[0, +\infty)$, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. Integrating by parts, with $x$ as the factor to differentiate and $e^{-x}$ as the factor to integrate, one gets
$$
\int_0^{t} x\,e^{-x}\,dx = \Big[ -x\,e^{-x} \Big]_0^{t} + \int_0^{t} e^{-x}\,dx = -t\,e^{-t} + 1 - e^{-t} .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ one has $t\,e^{-t} \to 0$, because the exponential grows faster than every power, and $e^{-t} \to 0$. Hence the integral **converges** and equals $1$.
