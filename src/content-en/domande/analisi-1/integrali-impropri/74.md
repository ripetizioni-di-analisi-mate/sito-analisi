Let $f$ be continuous and positive on $[1, +\infty)$. Which of the following pieces of information guarantees that $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$ converges?

- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} x\,f(x) = 0$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x) = 0$
- $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} x^{2} f(x) = 0$
- $f$ is decreasing and $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x) = 0$

## Explanation

If $x^{2} f(x) \to 0$, the ratio of $f$ to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$ tends to $0$, so eventually $f(x) < \dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = 2$ converges, therefore the integral of $f$ **converges** by the **comparison test**. The other three pieces of information are not enough, because the function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x\,(1 + \ln x)}$ is positive, decreasing, satisfies $x\,f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + \ln x} \to 0$, and its integral diverges, because $\displaystyle\int_1^{t} f(x)\,dx = \ln(1 + \ln t) \to +\infty$.
