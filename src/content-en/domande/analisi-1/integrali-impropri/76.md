As the real parameter $\alpha$ varies, the integral
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} e^{\alpha x}\,dx
$$
converges if and only if

- $\alpha < 0$
- $\alpha \leq 0$
- $\alpha > 0$
- $\alpha \neq 0$

## Explanation

For $\alpha = 0$ we have $\displaystyle\int_0^{t} dx = t \to +\infty$, so the integral diverges. For $\alpha \neq 0$,
$$
\int_0^{t} e^{\alpha x}\,dx = \frac{e^{\alpha t} - 1}{\alpha} .
$$
If $\alpha < 0$ the term $e^{\alpha t}$ tends to $0$ and the integral converges to $-\dfrac{1}{\alpha}$; if $\alpha > 0$ it tends to $+\infty$ and the integral diverges. Therefore the integral **converges** if and only if $\alpha < 0$.
