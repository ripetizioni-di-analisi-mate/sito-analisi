The exponent of $x^{\ln x}$ grows with $x$. What does the comparison test give about the behavior of $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{\ln x}}$?

- Converges, because for $x \geq e^{2}$ the integrand lies between $0$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$
- Diverges, because for $x \geq 1$ the integrand is less than or equal to $1$
- Diverges, because for $x \geq 1$ the integrand is greater than $e^{-x}$
- Its behavior cannot be determined, because the exponent is not constant

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous and positive on $[1, +\infty)$, so the only critical point is $+\infty$. For $x \geq e^{2}$ we have $\ln x \geq 2$, so $x^{\ln x} \geq x^{2}$ and $0 < \dfrac{1}{x^{\ln x}} \leq \dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = 2$ converges, therefore the integral on $[e^{2}, +\infty)$ converges by the **comparison test**, while the integral on $[1, e^{2}]$ is ordinary. Consequently the given integral **converges**.
