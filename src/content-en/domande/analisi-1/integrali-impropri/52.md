Find
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{\arcsin x}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\,dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- The integral diverges
- $\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{8}$

## Explanation

The denominator tends to $0$ as $x \to 1^{-}$, while the numerator tends to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the singular point is $1$. Since the derivative of $\arcsin x$ is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}$, the integral reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int f'(x)\,f(x)\,dx = \frac{f(x)^{2}}{2}$, and
$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{\arcsin x}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\,dx = \frac{(\arcsin t)^{2}}{2} .
$$
As $t \to 1^{-}$ we have $\arcsin t \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\pi^{2}}{4} = \dfrac{\pi^{2}}{8}$.
