For the integral
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\ln x}{x^{2}}\,dx
$$
which statement is true?

- Converges, because for $x \geq 1$ one has $\ln x \leq \sqrt{x}$ and so the integrand is at most $\dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$
- Diverges, because $\ln x \to +\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$
- Diverges, because for $x \geq e$ the integrand is greater than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$

## Explanation

The function $h(x) = \sqrt{x} - \ln x$ has derivative $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} - \dfrac{1}{x}$, negative for $x < 4$ and positive for $x > 4$, so $h$ has its minimum at $4$ and $h(4) = 2 - \ln 4 > 0$. Therefore for $x \geq 1$ one has $0 \leq \ln x \leq \sqrt{x}$, and consequently $0 \leq \dfrac{\ln x}{x^{2}} \leq \dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = \dfrac{3}{2} > 1$ converges, hence the given integral **converges** by the **comparison test**. The integrand is not asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$, because the ratio of the two functions is $\ln x$, which tends to $+\infty$.
