Compute
$$
\int_0^{1} \ln x\,dx .
$$

- $1$
- $0$
- $-1$
- $-\infty$

## Explanation

The function $\ln x$ is unbounded near $0$, so the critical point is $0$. Integration by parts gives the antiderivative $x \ln x - x$, therefore
$$
\int_t^{1} \ln x\,dx = -1 - t \ln t + t .
$$
As $t \to 0^{+}$ one has $t \ln t \to 0$, by the comparison between power and logarithm, and $t \to 0$. Hence the integral **converges** and equals $-1$. The value is negative because $\ln x < 0$ on $(0, 1)$.
