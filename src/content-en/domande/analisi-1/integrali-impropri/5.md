## Explanation

The integrand is unbounded near $1$, so the critical point is the upper endpoint. An antiderivative of $(1 - x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ is $-2\sqrt{1 - x}$, therefore
$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - x}} = 2 - 2\sqrt{1 - t} .
$$
Since $\sqrt{1 - t} \to 0$ as $t \to 1^{-}$, the integral **converges** and equals $2$.
