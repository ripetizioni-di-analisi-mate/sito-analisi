Evaluate
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{1 + x}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- The integral diverges

## Explanation

The integrand is $x^{-\frac{1}{2}} + x^{\frac{1}{2}}$, unbounded near $0$, so the singular point is $0$. An antiderivative is $2\sqrt{x} + \dfrac{2}{3}\,x^{\frac{3}{2}}$, therefore
$$
\int_t^{1} \frac{1 + x}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx = \frac{8}{3} - 2\sqrt{t} - \frac{2}{3}\,t^{\frac{3}{2}} .
$$
As $t \to 0^{+}$ the last two terms tend to $0$, so the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{8}{3}$.
