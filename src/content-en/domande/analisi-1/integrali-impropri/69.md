The function $\dfrac{\sin^{2} x}{x}$ is nonnegative on $[1, +\infty)$. Which statement about its integral
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\sin^{2} x}{x}\,dx
$$
is true?

- Converges, because for $x \geq 1$ the integrand is less than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{x}$
- It is oscillatory, because $\sin^{2} x$ has no limit as $x \to +\infty$
- Converges, because $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx$ converges
- Diverges, because $\sin^{2} x = \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$, the integral of $\dfrac{1}{2x}$ diverges and that of $\dfrac{\cos 2x}{2x}$ converges

## Explanation

The integrand is nonnegative, so the integral either converges or diverges to $+\infty$. From the formula $\sin^{2} x = \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$ one gets
$$
\int_1^{t} \frac{\sin^{2} x}{x}\,dx = \int_1^{t} \frac{dx}{2x} - \int_1^{t} \frac{\cos 2x}{2x}\,dx .
$$
By parts, $\displaystyle\int_1^{t} \frac{\cos 2x}{2x}\,dx = \left[ \frac{\sin 2x}{4x} \right]_1^{t} + \int_1^{t} \frac{\sin 2x}{4x^{2}}\,dx$, and both terms have a finite limit, since $\left| \dfrac{\sin 2x}{4x^{2}} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{4x^{2}}$. The first integral instead tends to $+\infty$, so the given integral **diverges**.
