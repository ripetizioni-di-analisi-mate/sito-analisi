Regarding the integral
$$
\int_2^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{2} \ln x}
$$
which statement is correct?

- Diverges, because for $x \geq 2$ the integrand is greater than $\dfrac{1}{x^{3}}$
- Its behavior cannot be determined, because the integrand is not asymptotic to any power of $x$
- Diverges, because $\dfrac{1}{\ln x}$ has a divergent integral on $[2, +\infty)$
- Converges, because for $x \geq e$ the integrand lies between $0$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$

## Explanation

On $[2, +\infty)$ the integrand is continuous and positive, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. For $x \geq e$ we have $\ln x \geq 1$, therefore $0 < \dfrac{1}{x^{2} \ln x} \leq \dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = 2$ converges, so the integral over $[e, +\infty)$ converges by the **comparison test**; the integral over $[2, e]$ is ordinary. Consequently the given integral **converges**.
