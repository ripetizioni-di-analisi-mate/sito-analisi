Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{x} \right) dx .
$$

- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x}$
- Converges, because the integrand tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{2x^{2}}$
- Diverges, because $\cos \dfrac{1}{x} \to 1$ as $x \to +\infty$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 1$ one has $0 < \dfrac{1}{x} \leq 1$, so $\cos \dfrac{1}{x} < 1$ and the integrand is positive. By the standard cosine limit, $1 - \cos y \sim \dfrac{y^{2}}{2}$ as $y \to 0$, therefore with $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{2x^{2}}$ as $x \to +\infty$. The decay order is $2 > 1$, hence the integral **converges** by the **limit comparison test**.
