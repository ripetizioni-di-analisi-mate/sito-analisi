What is the value of the integral
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-\sqrt{x}}\,dx
$$
over the whole positive half-line?

- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $4$

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous and positive on $[0, +\infty)$, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. With the substitution $x = u^{2}$, $dx = 2u\,du$, one gets
$$
\int_0^{t} e^{-\sqrt{x}}\,dx = \int_0^{\sqrt{t}} 2u\,e^{-u}\,du .
$$
By parts, $\displaystyle\int_0^{s} u\,e^{-u}\,du = \bigl[-u\,e^{-u}\bigr]_0^{s} + \int_0^{s} e^{-u}\,du = 1 - s\,e^{-s} - e^{-s}$, which tends to $1$ as $s \to +\infty$. Since $\sqrt{t} \to +\infty$ as $t \to +\infty$, the integral **converges** and equals $2 \cdot 1 = 2$.
