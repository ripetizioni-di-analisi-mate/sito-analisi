- Diverges, because at infinity the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is unbounded near $0$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, +\infty)$ and the singular points are $0$ and $+\infty$, so the integral is split at the point $1$. As $x \to 0^{+}$ one has $1 + x \to 1$, therefore the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{1}{3}}}$ and its blow-up order at $0$ is $\dfrac{1}{3}$. Since $\dfrac{1}{3} < 1$, the integral over $(0, 1]$ converges. As $x \to +\infty$ one has $1 + x \sim x$, therefore the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{4}{3}}}$ and its decay order is $\dfrac{4}{3}$. Since $\dfrac{4}{3} > 1$, the integral over $[1, +\infty)$ converges. Both pieces converge, hence the given integral **converges**.
