Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{\sin \frac{1}{x}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx .
$$

- Diverges, because $\sin \dfrac{1}{x}$ has no limit as $x \to 0^{+}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$
- Converges absolutely, because the absolute value of the integrand is at most $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
- It is indeterminate, because the integrand changes sign infinitely many times near $0$

## Explanation

The critical point is $0$, near which the integrand changes sign infinitely many times, so the comparison tests are applied to the absolute value. Since $\left| \sin \dfrac{1}{x} \right| \leq 1$, on $(0, 1]$ one has $\left| \dfrac{\sin \frac{1}{x}}{\sqrt{x}} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$. The benchmark at $0$ with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2} < 1$ converges, therefore the integral of the absolute value converges by the **comparison test**. Hence the given integral **converges absolutely** and therefore converges. The equivalence $\sin y \sim y$ holds as $y \to 0$, while here $\dfrac{1}{x} \to +\infty$.
