What is the value of $\displaystyle\int_1^{2} \frac{dx}{x \sqrt{x^{2} - 1}}$? The substitution $x = \dfrac{1}{u}$ can be used.

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- The integral diverges
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 1^{+}$ the factor $\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}$ tends to $0$, so the integrand is unbounded and the critical point is $1$. With $x = \dfrac{1}{u}$ we have $dx = -\dfrac{du}{u^{2}}$ and, for $0 < u < 1$, $x\sqrt{x^{2} - 1} = \dfrac{\sqrt{1 - u^{2}}}{u^{2}}$, hence
$$
\int_t^{2} \frac{dx}{x \sqrt{x^{2} - 1}} = \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{t}} \frac{du}{\sqrt{1 - u^{2}}} = \arcsin \frac{1}{t} - \frac{\pi}{6} .
$$
As $t \to 1^{+}$ we have $\arcsin \dfrac{1}{t} \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, therefore the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
