Compute
$$
\int_0^{2} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{4 - x^{2}}} .
$$

- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The integrand is unbounded near $2$, where $4 - x^{2}$ vanishes, so the critical point is the upper endpoint. The integral reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^{2} - x^{2}}} = \arcsin \frac{x}{a} + c$ with $a = 2$, therefore
$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{4 - x^{2}}} = \arcsin \frac{t}{2} .
$$
As $t \to 2^{-}$ one has $\arcsin \dfrac{t}{2} \to \arcsin 1 = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, hence the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
