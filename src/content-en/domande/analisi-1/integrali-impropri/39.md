With $\alpha$ a real parameter, consider
$$
\int_0^{1} (1 - \cos x)^{\alpha}\,dx .
$$
What is the set of all $\alpha$ for which the integral converges?

- $\alpha > -1$
- $\alpha > -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha \geq -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha < -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

On $(0, 1]$ we have $1 - \cos x > 0$. For $\alpha \geq 0$ the integrand is bounded and the integral is ordinary; for $\alpha < 0$ the integrand tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$ and the singular point is $0$. From the standard limit $1 - \cos x \sim \dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$ it follows that the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{2^{-\alpha}}{x^{-2\alpha}}$, with blow-up order $-2\alpha$. The comparison integral converges if and only if $-2\alpha < 1$, so the integral **converges** if and only if $\alpha > -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
