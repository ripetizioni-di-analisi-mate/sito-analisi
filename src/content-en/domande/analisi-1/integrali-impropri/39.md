Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{x - \ln(1 + x)}{x^{\alpha}}\,dx .
$$

- $\alpha < 2$
- $\alpha \leq 3$
- $\alpha < 3$
- $\alpha > 3$

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ one has $\ln(1 + x) < x$, so the integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$ for every $\alpha$. From the Taylor expansion $\ln(1 + x) = x - \dfrac{x^{2}}{2} + o(x^{2})$ it follows that $x - \ln(1 + x) \sim \dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$ as $x \to 0$, therefore the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{x^{2}}{2\,x^{\alpha}} = \dfrac{1}{2\,x^{\alpha - 2}}$. The comparison integral at $0$ converges if and only if $\alpha - 2 < 1$, that is $\alpha < 3$. Hence, by the **limit comparison test**, the integral **converges if and only if** $\alpha < 3$.
