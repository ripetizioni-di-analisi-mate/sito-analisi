The integrand of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}\,\ln x}
$$
is unbounded near both endpoints. Which statement about the integral is true?

- Converges, because near $0$ the absolute value of the integrand is less than $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is unbounded near $0$
- Diverges, because near $1$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x - 1}$
- Converges, because near $1$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x - 1}$ and the blow-up order is $1$

## Explanation

On $(0, 1)$ the integrand is negative and unbounded near $0$ and near $1$, so the integral is split at $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and one studies the positive function $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}\,\ln x}$. Near $0$ we have $|\ln x| \geq 1$ for $x \leq \dfrac{1}{e}$, so $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}\,|\ln x|} \leq \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ and the piece near $0$ converges by comparison. Near $1$ we have $\ln x \sim x - 1$ and $\sqrt{x} \to 1$, so $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}\,\ln x} \sim \dfrac{1}{1 - x}$. The blow-up order is $1$, therefore the piece near $1$ diverges. Consequently the integral **diverges**, to $-\infty$.
