At infinity $f(x)=\dfrac3x+O(x^{-2})$. Which constant $c$ makes $\int_1^\infty(f(x)-\dfrac{c}{x})\,dx$ converge, assuming $f$ is continuous on $[1,+\infty)$?

- $0$, taking the term $\dfrac{3}{x}$ to be integrable at infinity.
- $1$
- Every $c>0$, because the difference tends to zero.
- $3$

## Explanation

The difference is $\dfrac{3-c}{x}+O(x^{-2})$. Its remainder is absolutely integrable at infinity. The coefficient of $\dfrac{1}{x}$ must therefore vanish, since it would otherwise yield a divergent logarithmic term.
