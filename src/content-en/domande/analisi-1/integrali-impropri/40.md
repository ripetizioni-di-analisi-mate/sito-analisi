Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} x^{\alpha} \left( e^{\frac{1}{x}} - 1 \right) dx .
$$

- $\alpha < 1$
- $\alpha > 0$
- $\alpha \leq 0$
- $\alpha < 0$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 1$ one has $e^{\frac{1}{x}} > 1$, so the integrand is positive for every $\alpha$ and the only singular point is $+\infty$. By the standard exponential limit, $e^{y} - 1 \sim y$ as $y \to 0$, therefore with $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{x^{\alpha}}{x} = \dfrac{1}{x^{1 - \alpha}}$. The comparison integral converges if and only if $1 - \alpha > 1$, that is $\alpha < 0$. Hence, by the **limit comparison test**, the integral **converges if and only if** $\alpha < 0$.
