Which statement about the integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \ln(\sin x)\,dx$ is true?

- Diverges, because $\ln(\sin x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$
- Converges, because the interval of integration is bounded
- Converges, because near $0$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\ln x$, which has a convergent integral on $(0, 1]$
- Diverges, because near $0$ the integrand is asymptotic to $-\dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

On $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$ we have $0 < \sin x \leq 1$, so the integrand is nonpositive and tends to $-\infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$; the singular point is $0$, and one studies the positive function $-\ln(\sin x)$. Since $\ln(\sin x) = \ln x + \ln \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ and $\ln \dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 0$, we have $-\ln(\sin x) \sim -\ln x$. Moreover $\displaystyle\int_t^{1} (-\ln x)\,dx = 1 - t + t \ln t \to 1$ as $t \to 0^{+}$, so by the **limit comparison test** the given integral **converges**.
