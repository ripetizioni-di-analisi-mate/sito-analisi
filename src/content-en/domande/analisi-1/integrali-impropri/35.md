Find all values of $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the integral
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \bigl( x^{2} + 1 \bigr)^{\alpha}\,dx
$$
is convergent.

- $\alpha < 0$
- $\alpha < -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha \leq -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha > -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous and positive on $[1, +\infty)$, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. We have $\bigl(x^{2} + 1\bigr)^{\alpha} = x^{2\alpha} \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{x^{2}} \right)^{\alpha}$ and the second factor tends to $1$, therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $x^{2\alpha} = \dfrac{1}{x^{-2\alpha}}$. The comparison integral converges if and only if $-2\alpha > 1$, so by the **limit comparison test** the integral **converges** if and only if $\alpha < -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
