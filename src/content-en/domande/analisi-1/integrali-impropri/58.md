Choose the correct argument about the behavior of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \bigl( \sqrt{x + 1} - \sqrt{x} \bigr)\,dx .
$$

- Converges, because the integrand tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$
- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2} < 1$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$

## Explanation

Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x}$ gives
$$
\sqrt{x + 1} - \sqrt{x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x}} ,
$$
so the integrand is positive. As $x \to +\infty$ the denominator is asymptotic to $2\sqrt{x}$, therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$, with decay order $\dfrac{1}{2} \leq 1$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ on $[1, +\infty)$ diverges, so the integral **diverges** by the **limit comparison test**.
