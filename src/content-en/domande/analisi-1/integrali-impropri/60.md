The numerator of $\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{x^{2} + 1}$ tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$. Which conclusion about the behavior of $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x}}{x^{2} + 1}\,dx$ is correct?

- Diverges, because the numerator tends to $+\infty$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous and positive on $[1, +\infty)$, so the only critical point is $+\infty$. As $x \to +\infty$ we have $x^{2} + 1 \sim x^{2}$, therefore
$$
\frac{\sqrt{x}}{x^{2} + 1} \sim \frac{\sqrt{x}}{x^{2}} = \frac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}} .
$$
The decay order is $\dfrac{3}{2} > 1$, so the integral **converges** by the **limit comparison test** with the comparison integral with $\alpha = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
