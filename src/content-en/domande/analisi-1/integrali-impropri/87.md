The integral
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x\,(1 - x)}}
$$
has two singular points. What is its value?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $2$
- The integral diverges
- $\pi$

## Explanation

The integrand is unbounded near $0$ and near $1$, so the integral is split at $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The derivative of $\arcsin(2x - 1)$ is $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{1 - (2x - 1)^{2}}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x\,(1 - x)}}$, therefore
$$
\int_t^{\frac{1}{2}} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x\,(1 - x)}} = -\arcsin(2t - 1) \to \frac{\pi}{2} \quad (t \to 0^{+}), \qquad
\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{s} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x\,(1 - x)}} = \arcsin(2s - 1) \to \frac{\pi}{2} \quad (s \to 1^{-}) .
$$
Both pieces converge, so the integral **converges** and equals $\pi$.
