A body is at distance $R$ from the center of a planet and is attracted by a force of magnitude $\dfrac{k}{r^{2}}$, with $k > 0$, where $r$ is the distance from the center. The work needed to move it to infinity is
$$
W = \int_R^{+\infty} \frac{k}{r^{2}}\,dr .
$$
What is $W$?

- $\dfrac{k}{R}$
- $\dfrac{k}{R^{2}}$
- $-\dfrac{k}{R}$
- The integral diverges, so the work is infinite

## Explanation

For $r \geq R > 0$ the integrand is continuous and positive, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. An antiderivative of $\dfrac{k}{r^{2}}$ is $-\dfrac{k}{r}$, therefore
$$
\int_R^{t} \frac{k}{r^{2}}\,dr = \frac{k}{R} - \frac{k}{t} .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ the term $\dfrac{k}{t}$ tends to $0$, so the integral **converges** and the work is $W = \dfrac{k}{R}$.
