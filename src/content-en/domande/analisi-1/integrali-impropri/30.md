For the integral
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{2 + \cos x}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx
$$
which statement is true?

- Converges, because the integrand is less than or equal to $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{x}}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is greater than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
- It is indeterminate, because $\cos x$ has no limit as $x \to +\infty$
- Converges, because the integrand tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$

## Explanation

For every $x$ one has $1 \leq 2 + \cos x \leq 3$, so for $x \geq 1$ the integrand is positive and $\dfrac{2 + \cos x}{\sqrt{x}} \geq \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$. The benchmark integral $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}}$ diverges, since $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2} \leq 1$. Therefore the given integral **diverges** by the **comparison test**. The inequality with $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{x}}$ is true, but an upper bound whose integral diverges gives no information on the behavior.
