For which value of the constant $c > 0$ does
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{c}{(1 + x)^{3}}\,dx = 1 ?
$$

::grafico{f="1/(1+x)^3" area="1/(1+x)^3;0;0;8" xmin=-0.5 xmax=8 ymin=-0.15 ymax=1.25 titolo="The shaded region lies between the graph of $y = \dfrac{1}{(1 + x)^{3}}$ and the $x$-axis, for $x \geq 0$"}

- $c = 1$
- $c = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $c = 3$
- $c = 2$

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous and positive on $[0, +\infty)$, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. An antiderivative of $(1 + x)^{-3}$ is $-\dfrac{1}{2\,(1 + x)^{2}}$, therefore
$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{dx}{(1 + x)^{3}} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2\,(1 + t)^{2}} \to \frac{1}{2} \qquad (t \to +\infty) .
$$
Consequently the given integral equals $\dfrac{c}{2}$, and the condition $\dfrac{c}{2} = 1$ gives $c = 2$.
