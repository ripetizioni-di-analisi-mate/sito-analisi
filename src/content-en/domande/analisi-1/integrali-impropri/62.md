Study the convergence of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \Bigl( x^{\frac{1}{x}} - 1 \Bigr)\,dx .
$$

- Converges, because the integrand tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$ and the logarithm grows more slowly than any power
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$

## Explanation

Write $x^{\frac{1}{x}} = e^{\frac{\ln x}{x}}$, and $\dfrac{\ln x}{x} \to 0$ as $x \to +\infty$. From the standard limit $e^{y} - 1 \sim y$ as $y \to 0$ it follows that the integrand is positive for $x > 1$ and asymptotic to $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$. The ratio of $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$ to $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is $\ln x$, which tends to $+\infty$, so eventually $\dfrac{\ln x}{x} > \dfrac{1}{x}$ and its integral diverges by **comparison** with the comparison integral $\alpha = 1$. By the **limit comparison test** the given integral also **diverges**.
