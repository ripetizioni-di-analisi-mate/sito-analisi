Compute
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{\sin(2x)}{x}\,dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $2\pi$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ one has $\sin(2x) \sim 2x$, so the integrand tends to $2$ and extends continuously at $0$. Therefore the only critical point is $+\infty$. With the substitution $u = 2x$ one has $x = \dfrac{u}{2}$ and $dx = \dfrac{du}{2}$, so $\dfrac{dx}{x} = \dfrac{du}{u}$ and
$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{\sin(2x)}{x}\,dx = \int_0^{2t} \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ also $2t \to +\infty$, and $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du = \frac{\pi}{2}$ is one of the three notable integrals. Hence the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
