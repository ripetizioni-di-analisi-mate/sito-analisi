What can be said about the integral
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{\ln x}{x}\,dx
$$
among the following statements?

- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Converges and equals $0$
- Diverges to $-\infty$
- Converges and equals $-1$

## Explanation

On $(0, 1]$ the integrand is continuous and nonpositive, and as $x \to 0^{+}$ it tends to $-\infty$, so the singular point is $0$. The integral reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int f'(x)\,f(x)\,dx = \frac{f(x)^{2}}{2}$ with $f(x) = \ln x$, therefore
$$
\int_t^{1} \frac{\ln x}{x}\,dx = 0 - \frac{(\ln t)^{2}}{2} .
$$
As $t \to 0^{+}$ we have $(\ln t)^{2} \to +\infty$, so the integral **diverges to** $-\infty$.
