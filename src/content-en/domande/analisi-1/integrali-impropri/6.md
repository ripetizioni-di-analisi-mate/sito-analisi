- $-\dfrac{1}{\ln 2}$
- $\ln 2$
- $\dfrac{1}{\ln^{2} 2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\ln 2}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ the product $x \ln^{2} x$ tends to $0$ and is positive, so the integrand is unbounded near $0$ and the critical point is $0$. The integral is reducible to the immediate integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)^{2}}\,dx = -\frac{1}{f(x)} + c$ with $f(x) = \ln x$, therefore an antiderivative is $-\dfrac{1}{\ln x}$ and
$$
\int_t^{\frac{1}{2}} \frac{dx}{x \ln^{2} x} = -\frac{1}{\ln \frac{1}{2}} + \frac{1}{\ln t} = \frac{1}{\ln 2} + \frac{1}{\ln t} .
$$
Since $\ln t \to -\infty$ as $t \to 0^{+}$, the term $\dfrac{1}{\ln t}$ tends to $0$, hence the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{1}{\ln 2}$.
