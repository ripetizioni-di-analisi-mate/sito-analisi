Determine the set of all $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the integral
$$
\int_0^{1} |\ln x|^{\alpha}\,dx
$$
converges.

- For every $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$
- $\alpha > -1$
- $\alpha < 1$
- $\alpha \geq -1$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, 1)$. Near $1$ we have $\ln x \sim x - 1$, so $|\ln x|^{\alpha} \sim \dfrac{1}{(1 - x)^{-\alpha}}$. For $\alpha < 0$ the point $1$ is singular, and the integral near $1$ converges if and only if $-\alpha < 1$. The point $0$ is singular for $\alpha > 0$; since $\sqrt{x}\,|\ln x|^{\alpha} \to 0$, in a right neighborhood of $0$ we have $|\ln x|^{\alpha} \leq \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ and the integral near $0$ converges by comparison. Hence the integral **converges** if and only if $\alpha > -1$.
