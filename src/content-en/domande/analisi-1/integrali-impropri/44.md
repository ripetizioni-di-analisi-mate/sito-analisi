Compute
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-x^{2}}\,dx .
$$

- $\sqrt{\pi}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$

## Explanation

The Gaussian integral over the whole line converges and equals $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} e^{-x^{2}}\,dx = \sqrt{\pi}$. The integrand $e^{-x^{2}}$ is even, so the integrals over $(-\infty, 0]$ and over $[0, +\infty)$ converge and are equal. Therefore the given integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$.
