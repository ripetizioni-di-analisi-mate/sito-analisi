Near $0$ the integrand of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{dx}{x^{2} + \sqrt{x}}
$$
is unbounded. Which conclusion about the behavior of the integral is correct?

- Diverges, because near $0$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$
- Converges, because near $0$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
- Converges, because the interval of integration is bounded
- Diverges, because the integrand is unbounded near $0$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$ and unbounded near $0$, so the singular point is $0$. As $x \to 0^{+}$ we have $\dfrac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{x}} = x^{\frac{3}{2}} \to 0$, so $x^{2} + \sqrt{x} \sim \sqrt{x}$ and the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$. The blow-up order at the singular point is $\dfrac{1}{2} < 1$, therefore the integral **converges** by the **limit comparison test**. The term $x^{2}$ dominates the term $\sqrt{x}$ only as $x \to +\infty$, which is not relevant here.
