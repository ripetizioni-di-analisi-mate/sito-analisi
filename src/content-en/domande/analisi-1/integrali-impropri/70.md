About a function $f$ continuous on $[1, +\infty)$ we only know that $|f(x)| \leq \dfrac{2}{x^{3}}$ for every $x \geq 1$. Which conclusion about $I = \displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$ is guaranteed?

- $I$ converges and $0 \leq I \leq 1$
- $I$ converges and $|I| \leq 1$
- The behavior of $I$ cannot be determined, because $f$ may change sign
- $I$ converges and $|I| \leq \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The comparison integral with $\alpha = 3$ converges, and
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{2}{x^{3}}\,dx = \lim_{t \to +\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{t^{2}} \right) = 1 .
$$
Since $|f(x)| \leq \dfrac{2}{x^{3}}$, the integral of $|f|$ converges by the **comparison test**, so $I$ **converges absolutely** and therefore converges. By the theorem on absolute convergence,
$$
|I| \leq \int_1^{+\infty} |f(x)|\,dx \leq \int_1^{+\infty} \frac{2}{x^{3}}\,dx = 1 .
$$
The function $f(x) = -\dfrac{2}{x^{3}}$ satisfies the hypotheses and gives $I = -1$, so neither $I \geq 0$ nor $|I| \leq \dfrac{1}{2}$ is guaranteed.
