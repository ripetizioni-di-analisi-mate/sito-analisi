To decide the behavior of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{e^{x}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx
$$
one uses the comparison test. Which conclusion is correct?

- Diverges, because the integrand is unbounded near $0$
- Diverges, because for $x \in (0, 1]$ the integrand is greater than $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $e^{x}$
- Converges, because for $x \in (0, 1]$ the integrand lies between $0$ and $\dfrac{e}{\sqrt{x}}$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$ and tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$, so the singular point is $0$. For $x \in (0, 1]$ we have $e^{x} \leq e$, therefore $0 < \dfrac{e^{x}}{\sqrt{x}} \leq \dfrac{e}{\sqrt{x}}$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ on $(0, 1]$ converges, so the integral **converges** by the **comparison test**. The lower bound by $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ is true, but a lower bound with convergent integral gives no information.
