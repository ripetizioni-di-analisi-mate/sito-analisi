Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \sin \frac{1}{x}\,dx .
$$

- Converges, because the integrand tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$
- It is oscillatory, because the sine changes sign
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 1$ one has $0 < \dfrac{1}{x} \leq 1 < \pi$, so the integrand is positive and the only singular point is $+\infty$. As $x \to +\infty$ one has $\dfrac{1}{x} \to 0$; by the standard sine limit, $\sin y \sim y$ as $y \to 0$, therefore $\sin \dfrac{1}{x} \sim \dfrac{1}{x}$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = 1$ diverges, hence the given integral **diverges** by the **limit comparison test**.
