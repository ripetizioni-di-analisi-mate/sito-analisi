For the integral
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \left( \frac{1}{x} - \sin \frac{1}{x} \right) dx
$$
which statement is true?

- The integrand has order of infinitesimal $1$ and the integral diverges
- The integrand has order of infinitesimal $3$ and the integral converges
- The integrand has order of infinitesimal $2$ and the integral converges
- The integrand has order of infinitesimal $3$ and the integral diverges

## Explanation

For $y > 0$ one has $\sin y < y$, so with $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ the integrand is positive on $[1, +\infty)$. From the Taylor expansion $\sin y = y - \dfrac{y^{3}}{6} + o(y^{3})$ it follows that $y - \sin y \sim \dfrac{y^{3}}{6}$ as $y \to 0$, therefore as $x \to +\infty$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{6x^{3}}$. The **order of infinitesimal** is $3 > 1$, hence the integral **converges** by the **limit comparison test**.
