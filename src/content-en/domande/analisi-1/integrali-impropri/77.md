The integrand of
$$
\int_0^{1} x^{\alpha} e^{\frac{1}{x}}\,dx
$$
depends on the real parameter $\alpha$. For which $\alpha$ does the integral converge?

- For no value of $\alpha$
- $\alpha > -1$
- $\alpha > 1$
- For every $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$ and the singular point is $0$. With $y = \dfrac{1}{x} \to +\infty$ we have
$$
x \cdot x^{\alpha} e^{\frac{1}{x}} = \frac{e^{y}}{y^{\alpha + 1}} \to +\infty ,
$$
because the exponential dominates every power. Hence the ratio of the integrand to $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $+\infty$, and in a right neighborhood of $0$ we have $x^{\alpha} e^{\frac{1}{x}} > \dfrac{1}{x}$. The comparison integral $\dfrac{1}{x}$ diverges on $(0, 1]$, therefore by the **comparison test** the integral **diverges for every** $\alpha$.
