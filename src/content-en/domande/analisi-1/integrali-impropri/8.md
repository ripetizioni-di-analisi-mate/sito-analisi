- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$
- Converges, because the integrand tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$
- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 1$ the integrand is continuous and positive, so the only critical point is $+\infty$. As $x \to +\infty$ the term $\sqrt{x}$ is negligible with respect to $x$, so $x + \sqrt{x} \sim x$ and the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x}$. The benchmark integral $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x}$ diverges, therefore the given integral **diverges** by the **limit comparison test**.
