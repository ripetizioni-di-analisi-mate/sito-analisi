At the critical point $0$ the numerator of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{\sin x - x \cos x}{x^{4}}\,dx
$$
is an infinitesimal that is studied with Taylor expansions. Which statement about the blow-up order of the integrand at $0$ and the behavior of the integral is true?

- The blow-up order is $1$ and the integral converges
- The blow-up order is $1$ and the integral diverges
- The blow-up order is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the integral converges
- The blow-up order is $3$ and the integral diverges

## Explanation

From the expansions $\sin x = x - \dfrac{x^{3}}{6} + o(x^{3})$ and $x \cos x = x - \dfrac{x^{3}}{2} + o(x^{3})$ it follows that
$$
\sin x - x \cos x = \frac{x^{3}}{3} + o(x^{3}) ,
$$
so the numerator is asymptotic to $\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}$, and on $(0, 1]$ the integrand is positive. Therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{3x}$, with blow-up order $1$ at the critical point $0$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = 1$ on $(0, 1]$ diverges, so the integral **diverges** by the **limit comparison test**.
