Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \sin x\,dx .
$$

- Converges and equals $1$
- Converges and equals $0$
- Diverges to $+\infty$
- It is oscillatory

## Explanation

An antiderivative of $\sin x$ is $-\cos x$, so
$$
\int_0^{t} \sin x\,dx = 1 - \cos t .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ the function $\cos t$ oscillates between $-1$ and $1$, therefore $1 - \cos t$ takes the values $0$ and $2$ infinitely many times and has no limit. Hence the integral is **oscillatory**. The oscillatory behavior is possible because the integrand changes sign.
