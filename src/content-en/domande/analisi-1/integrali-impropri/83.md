In the integral
$$
\int_0^{\pi} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{|\cos x|}}
$$
the cosine vanishes at a point inside the interval. Which statement is true?

- Converges, because near $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\left|x - \frac{\pi}{2}\right|}}$
- Diverges, because the integrand is unbounded near $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- Diverges, because near $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\left|x - \frac{\pi}{2}\right|}$
- Converges, because the interval of integration is bounded

## Explanation

The cosine vanishes at $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which is an interior singular point, so the integral is split into the pieces on $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ and on $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right]$. Since $\cos x = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x\right)$ and $\sin y \sim y$ as $y \to 0$, we have $|\cos x| \sim \left|x - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right|$. The integrand is positive and asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\left|x - \frac{\pi}{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}$, with blow-up order $\dfrac{1}{2} < 1$, therefore both pieces converge by the **limit comparison test** and the integral **converges**.
