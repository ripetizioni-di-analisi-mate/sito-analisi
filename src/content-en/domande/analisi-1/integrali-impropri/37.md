For the integral
$$
\int_2^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{\ln x}
$$
which statement is true?

- Converges, because $\ln x \to +\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$
- Converges, by the comparison integral with the logarithm with $\beta = 1$
- Its behavior cannot be determined, because the integrand is not asymptotically equivalent to any power of $x$
- Diverges, because the ratio of the integrand to $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $+\infty$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 2$ the integrand is continuous and positive, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. The ratio of the integrand to $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is $\dfrac{x}{\ln x}$, which tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$, so eventually $\dfrac{1}{\ln x} > \dfrac{1}{x}$. The comparison integral with $\alpha = 1$ diverges, therefore the given integral **diverges** by the **comparison test**. The comparison integral with the logarithm has integrand $\dfrac{1}{x\,(\ln x)^{\beta}}$, which contains the factor $\dfrac{1}{x}$ missing from $\dfrac{1}{\ln x}$.
