Determine for which $\beta \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_e^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x\,(\ln x)^{2\beta}} .
$$

- $\beta > \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\beta > 1$
- $\beta \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\beta < \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

For $x \geq e$ the integrand is continuous and positive, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. With the substitution $u = \ln x$, $du = \dfrac{dx}{x}$, the integral over $[e, t]$ becomes the integral of $\dfrac{1}{u^{2\beta}}$ over $[1, \ln t]$, and $\ln t \to +\infty$; therefore the given integral has the same behavior as $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{du}{u^{2\beta}}$. By the theorem on comparison integrals this integral converges if and only if $2\beta > 1$. Hence the given integral **converges if and only if** $\beta > \dfrac{1}{2}$.
