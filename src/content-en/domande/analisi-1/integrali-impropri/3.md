## Explanation

The integral is reducible to the immediate integral $\displaystyle\int f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}\,dx = e^{f(x)} + c$ with $f(x) = -x^{2}$, so an antiderivative of the integrand is $-\dfrac{e^{-x^{2}}}{2}$. It follows that
$$
\int_0^{t} x\,e^{-x^{2}}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{e^{-t^{2}}}{2} .
$$
Since $e^{-t^{2}} \to 0$ as $t \to +\infty$, the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
