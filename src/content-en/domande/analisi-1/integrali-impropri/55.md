Using a suitable substitution, find the value of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{e^{\sqrt{x}}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx .
$$

- $2\,(e - 1)$
- $e - 1$
- $2e$
- The integral diverges

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^{+}$ the numerator tends to $1$ and the denominator to $0$, so the singular point is $0$. The derivative of $e^{\sqrt{x}}$ is $\dfrac{e^{\sqrt{x}}}{2\sqrt{x}}$, therefore an antiderivative of the integrand is $2e^{\sqrt{x}}$ and
$$
\int_t^{1} \frac{e^{\sqrt{x}}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx = 2e - 2e^{\sqrt{t}} .
$$
As $t \to 0^{+}$ we have $e^{\sqrt{t}} \to 1$, so the integral **converges** and equals $2e - 2 = 2\,(e - 1)$.
