- Converges, because the integrand extends continuously to $0$
- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{2}{x}$
- Converges, because the numerator tends to $0$ as $x \to 0^{+}$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$. By the standard exponential limit, $e^{2x} - 1 \sim 2x$ as $x \to 0$, so the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{2x}{x^{2}} = \dfrac{2}{x}$ and the singular point is $0$. The comparison integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{1} \frac{dx}{x}$ diverges, since it is the comparison integral with $\alpha = 1$. Therefore the given integral **diverges** by the **limit comparison test**.
