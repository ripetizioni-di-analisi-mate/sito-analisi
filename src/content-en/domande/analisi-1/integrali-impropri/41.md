Consider the integral
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x - \sin x}} .
$$
What is the blow-up order of the integrand at the singular point $0$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $3$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ one has $\sin x < x$, so the integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$ and unbounded near $0$. From the Taylor expansion $\sin x = x - \dfrac{x^{3}}{6} + o(x^{3})$ it follows that $x - \sin x \sim \dfrac{x^{3}}{6}$ as $x \to 0$, so $\sqrt{x - \sin x} \sim \dfrac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{6}}$ and the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$. Therefore the **blow-up order** is $\dfrac{3}{2}$. Since $\dfrac{3}{2} \geq 1$, the integral diverges.
