Let
$$
I = \int_1^{+\infty} e^{-x^{2}}\,dx .
$$
Which statement is true?

- $0 < I \leq \dfrac{1}{e}$
- $I = \dfrac{1}{2e}$
- $I = \dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$
- $I \geq \dfrac{1}{e}$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 1$ we have $x^{2} \geq x$, so, since the exponential is increasing, $0 < e^{-x^{2}} \leq e^{-x}$. Since $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} e^{-x}\,dx = \lim_{t \to +\infty} \bigl(e^{-1} - e^{-t}\bigr) = \frac{1}{e}$, the integral $I$ converges by the **comparison test** and $0 < I \leq \dfrac{1}{e}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2e}$ is that of $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty} x\,e^{-x^{2}}\,dx$, and $\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$ is the Gaussian integral over $[0, +\infty)$, which is larger than $I$.
