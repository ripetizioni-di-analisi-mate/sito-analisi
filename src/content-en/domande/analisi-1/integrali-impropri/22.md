For the integral
$$
\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{dx}{\sqrt[3]{x}}
$$
which statement is true?

- Diverges, because the integrand is unbounded near $0$
- Converges and equals $3$
- Converges and equals $0$
- Converges and equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The integrand is unbounded near $0$, which is interior to the interval, so the integral is split at $0$. On $(0, 1]$ the integrand is $\dfrac{1}{x^{\frac{1}{3}}}$, that is, the comparison integrand with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3} < 1$, therefore the integral over $(0, 1]$ converges and equals $\dfrac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{3}{2}$. The integrand is odd, so the substitution $x = -u$ shows that the integral over $[-1, 0)$ converges and equals $-\dfrac{3}{2}$. Both pieces converge, therefore the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{3}{2} - \dfrac{3}{2} = 0$.
