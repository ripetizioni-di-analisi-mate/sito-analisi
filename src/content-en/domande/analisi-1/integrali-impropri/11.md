- Diverges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{2\,x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$
- Converges, because the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$. By the standard cosine limit, $1 - \cos x \sim \dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$ as $x \to 0$, so the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to
$$
\frac{x^{2}}{2\,x^{\frac{7}{2}}} = \frac{1}{2\,x^{\frac{3}{2}}} .
$$
The blow-up order at the singular point $0$ is $\dfrac{3}{2} \geq 1$, therefore the integral **diverges** by the **limit comparison test**.
