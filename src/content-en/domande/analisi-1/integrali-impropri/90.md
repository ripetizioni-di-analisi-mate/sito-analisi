Compute the improper integral
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x \sqrt{x - 1}} .
$$

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$
- $2$
- The integral diverges

## Explanation

The singular points are $1$, where the integrand is unbounded, and $+\infty$. With the substitution $u = \sqrt{x - 1}$, that is $x = u^{2} + 1$ and $dx = 2u\,du$, one gets $\dfrac{dx}{x \sqrt{x - 1}} = \dfrac{2\,du}{1 + u^{2}}$, so an antiderivative of the integrand is $2 \arctan \sqrt{x - 1}$. As $x \to 1^{+}$ this antiderivative tends to $0$ and as $x \to +\infty$ it tends to $2 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \pi$, therefore both pieces converge. The integral **converges** and equals $\pi$.
