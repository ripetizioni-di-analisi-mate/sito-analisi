Compute
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{2} + 4} .
$$

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $2\pi$

## Explanation

The singular points are $-\infty$ and $+\infty$, so the integral is split at the point $0$. The integral reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{x^{2} + a^{2}} = \frac{1}{a} \arctan \frac{x}{a} + c$ with $a = 2$, therefore
$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{dx}{x^{2} + 4} = \frac{1}{2} \arctan \frac{t}{2} \to \frac{\pi}{4} \qquad (t \to +\infty) .
$$
The integrand is even, so the integral over $(-\infty, 0]$ also converges and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Both pieces converge, therefore the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
