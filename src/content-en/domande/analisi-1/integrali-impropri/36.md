Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_1^{2} \frac{dx}{x^{2} - 1} .
$$

- Diverges, because near $1$ the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{2\,(x - 1)}$
- Converges, because near $1$ the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{2\,(x - 1)}$
- Converges, because the interval of integration is bounded
- It is oscillatory, because the integrand is not defined at $x = 1$

## Explanation

For $x \in (1, 2]$ the integrand is positive and as $x \to 1^{+}$ the denominator tends to $0$, so the singular point is $1$. Since $x^{2} - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$ and $x + 1 \to 2$, the integrand is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{2\,(x - 1)}$. The blow-up order at the singular point $1$ is $1 \geq 1$, hence the integral **diverges** by the **limit comparison test**.
