The function $\dfrac{e^{x}}{(1 + e^{x})^{2}}$ is the density of the logistic distribution. What is the value of $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \frac{e^{x}}{(1 + e^{x})^{2}}\,dx$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$
- The integral diverges

## Explanation

The critical points are $-\infty$ and $+\infty$, so the integral is split at $0$. The integral reduces to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int f'(x)\,f(x)^{-2}\,dx = -\frac{1}{f(x)}$ with $f(x) = 1 + e^{x}$, therefore an antiderivative is $-\dfrac{1}{1 + e^{x}}$, which equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ at $0$, tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$ and tends to $-1$ as $x \to -\infty$. Hence the piece on $[0, +\infty)$ converges and equals $0 - \left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and the piece on $(-\infty, 0]$ converges and equals $-\dfrac{1}{2} - (-1) = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Both pieces converge, therefore the integral **converges** and equals $1$.
