Compute
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x\,(x + 1)} .
$$

- $1$
- $-\ln 2$
- $\ln 2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The integrand is continuous and positive on $[1, +\infty)$, so the only singular point is $+\infty$. The partial fraction decomposition gives $\dfrac{1}{x\,(x + 1)} = \dfrac{1}{x} - \dfrac{1}{x + 1}$, therefore an antiderivative is $\ln x - \ln(x + 1) = \ln \dfrac{x}{x + 1}$ and
$$
\int_1^{t} \frac{dx}{x\,(x + 1)} = \ln \frac{t}{t + 1} - \ln \frac{1}{2} = \ln \frac{t}{t + 1} + \ln 2 .
$$
As $t \to +\infty$ the ratio $\dfrac{t}{t + 1}$ tends to $1$ and its logarithm tends to $0$, hence the integral **converges** and equals $\ln 2$.
