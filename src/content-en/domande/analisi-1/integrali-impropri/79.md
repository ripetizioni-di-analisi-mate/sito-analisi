The integral
$$
\int_2^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{\alpha} \ln x}
$$
converges exactly for

- $\alpha \geq 1$
- $\alpha > 0$
- $\alpha > 1$
- $\alpha < 1$

## Explanation

The integrand is positive on $[2, +\infty)$ and the only singular point is $+\infty$. For $\alpha > 1$ and $x \geq e$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha} \ln x} \leq \dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha}}$, so the integral converges by comparison. For $\alpha = 1$ the integral is the comparison integral with the logarithm with $\beta = 1$, which diverges. For $\alpha < 1$ the ratio of the integrand to $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is $\dfrac{x^{1 - \alpha}}{\ln x} \to +\infty$, so the integral diverges by comparison. Therefore the integral **converges** if and only if $\alpha > 1$.
