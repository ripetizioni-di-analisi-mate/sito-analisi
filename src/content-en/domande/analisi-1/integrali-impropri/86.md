Consider the integral over the whole real line
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} e^{-x}\,dx .
$$
What is its behavior?

- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Converges and equals $1$
- Converges and equals $2$
- Converges and equals $0$

## Explanation

The singular points are $-\infty$ and $+\infty$, so the integral is split at $0$. The piece on $[0, +\infty)$ converges, because $\displaystyle\int_0^{t} e^{-x}\,dx = 1 - e^{-t} \to 1$. The piece on $(-\infty, 0]$ diverges, because
$$
\int_t^{0} e^{-x}\,dx = e^{-t} - 1 \to +\infty \qquad (t \to -\infty) .
$$
One piece diverges to $+\infty$ and the other converges, so the integral **diverges to** $+\infty$.
