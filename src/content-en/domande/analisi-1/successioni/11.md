Which of these sequences cannot converge simply because it is not bounded?

- $a_n = (-1)^n$
- $a_n = \dfrac{\sin n}{n}$
- $a_n = (-1)^n \sqrt{n}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{n + (-1)^n}{n}$

## Explanation

Every convergent sequence is bounded, so a sequence that is not bounded does not converge. We have $\left| (-1)^n \sqrt{n} \right| = \sqrt{n}$, which exceeds any constant, so $(-1)^n \sqrt{n}$ **is not bounded** and cannot converge.

The sequence $(-1)^n$ is bounded and does not converge for another reason, since the subsequences of even and odd index have limits $1$ and $-1$. The other two are convergent, since $\dfrac{\sin n}{n} \to 0$ and $\dfrac{n + (-1)^n}{n} = 1 + \dfrac{(-1)^n}{n} \to 1$.
