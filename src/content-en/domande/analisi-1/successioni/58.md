The ratio $\dfrac{2^n + 3^{n+1}}{3^n - 2^n}$ contains exponentials with different bases. What does it tend to?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $+\infty$
- $3$

## Explanation

We divide the numerator and the denominator by $3^n$, the power with the largest base. Since $3^{n+1} = 3 \cdot 3^n$, we obtain
$$
\frac{2^n + 3^{n+1}}{3^n - 2^n} = \frac{\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{n} + 3}{1 - \left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{n}} .
$$
The geometric sequence $\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{n}$ has ratio between $-1$ and $1$, so it tends to $0$. Therefore the ratio tends to $\dfrac{0 + 3}{1 - 0} = 3$.
