Every subsequence of $a_n$ has a further subsequence tending to $L$. Which construction would yield a contradiction if $a_n$ failed to tend to $L$?

- Two unequal consecutive terms.
- A subsequence staying entirely outside one fixed neighborhood of $L$.
- A tail never exactly equal to $L$.
- A subsequence omitting the first term.

## Explanation

If convergence failed, some $\varepsilon>0$ would allow infinitely many increasing indices with $|a_{n_k}-L|\geq\varepsilon$. Every further subsequence would retain that minimum distance. None could converge to $L$, contradicting the hypothesis.
