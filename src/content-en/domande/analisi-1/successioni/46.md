With the recurrence $a_{n+1} = \dfrac{a_n^2 + 6}{5}$, for which values of the first term $a_1$ is the sequence constant?

- $a_1 = -2$ or $a_1 = -3$
- Only $a_1 = 2$
- $a_1 = \dfrac{6}{5}$
- $a_1 = 2$ or $a_1 = 3$

## Explanation

The sequence is constant if and only if $a_2 = a_1$, because then the recurrence always produces the same value. The condition $a_1 = \dfrac{a_1^2 + 6}{5}$ is equivalent to
$$
a_1^2 - 5a_1 + 6 = 0 ,
$$
that is, $(a_1 - 2)(a_1 - 3) = 0$. Therefore the sequence is constant for $a_1 = 2$ and for $a_1 = 3$.
