To check with the definition that $\ln n \to +\infty$, the threshold $M = 3$ is fixed. What is the smallest $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $\ln n > 3$ for every $n > N$?

- $N = 27$
- $N = 9$
- $N = 3$
- $N = 20$

## Explanation

The natural logarithm is increasing, so $\ln n > 3$ is equivalent to $n > e^3$.

Since $2.718 < e < 2.719$, cubing we obtain $20.07 < e^3 < 20.11$, so $20 < e^3 < 21$. Hence the inequality holds for every $n \geq 21$, that is, for every $n > 20$, while for $n = 20$ we have $\ln 20 < 3$. The smallest index is therefore $N = 20$. The value $27 = 3^3$ is obtained by mistakenly replacing $e$ with $3$.
