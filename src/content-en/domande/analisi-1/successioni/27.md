Writing the expression $\dfrac{n^2 + 1}{n + 1} - n$ over a common denominator, which limit is obtained as $n \to +\infty$?

- $0$
- $-1$
- $1$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

Both terms diverge to $+\infty$, so the difference is in the form $+\infty - \infty$. Over a common denominator we obtain
$$
\frac{n^2 + 1}{n + 1} - n = \frac{n^2 + 1 - n^2 - n}{n + 1} = \frac{1 - n}{n + 1} .
$$
The numerator and the denominator have degree $1$, with coefficients of $n$ equal to $-1$ and $1$. Therefore the limit equals $-1$.
