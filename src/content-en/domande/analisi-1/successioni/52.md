What is the smallest index $n_0$ such that the sequence $a_n = \sqrt[n]{n}$ satisfies $a_{n+1} < a_n$ for every $n \geq n_0$?

- $n_0 = 3$
- $n_0 = 1$
- $n_0 = 2$
- $n_0 = 4$

## Explanation

The terms are positive, so raising to the power $n(n + 1)$ the inequality $\sqrt[n+1]{n + 1} < \sqrt[n]{n}$ is equivalent to $(n + 1)^n < n^{n+1}$. Dividing by $n^n$ we obtain the equivalent condition
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^{n} < n .
$$
The sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}$ is increasing with limit $e$, so $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n} < e < 3$ and the condition holds for every $n \geq 3$. For $n = 2$, instead, $\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^{2} = \dfrac{9}{4} > 2$, so $a_3 > a_2$, and for $n = 1$ we have $a_2 = \sqrt{2} > 1 = a_1$. The smallest index is therefore $n_0 = 3$.
