The ratio $\dfrac{\sin \frac{1}{n}}{\ln\left( 1 + \frac{2}{n} \right)}$ is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. What does it tend to?

- $2$
- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Multiplying and dividing by $\dfrac{1}{n}$ and by $\dfrac{2}{n}$, two standard limits appear
$$
\frac{\sin \frac{1}{n}}{\ln\left( 1 + \frac{2}{n} \right)} = \frac{\sin \frac{1}{n}}{\frac{1}{n}} \cdot \frac{\frac{2}{n}}{\ln\left( 1 + \frac{2}{n} \right)} \cdot \frac{\frac{1}{n}}{\frac{2}{n}} .
$$
By the standard limits with $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$ and with $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{2}{n}$, the first two factors tend to $1$. The third equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the limit is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
