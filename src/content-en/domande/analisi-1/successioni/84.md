An interval contains $a_n$ for infinitely many indices. In the bisection proof of Bolzano–Weierstrass it is split into two closed halves. Why must at least one half still contain terms for infinitely many indices?

- All the terms are distinct.
- The left half always contains the first term.
- The union of two finite sets of indices would be finite.
- Both halves must contain the same number of indices.

## Explanation

Associate each half with the set of indices whose terms it contains. If both sets were finite, their union would be finite as well. That union includes all the infinitely many indices in the original interval, which rules out this possibility.
