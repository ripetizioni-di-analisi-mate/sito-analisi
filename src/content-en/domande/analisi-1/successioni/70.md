The ratio test is applied to $a_n = \dfrac{n!\, e^n}{n^n}$. What is obtained?

- The ratio tends to $e$, so $a_n \to +\infty$
- The ratio tends to $\dfrac{1}{e}$, so $a_n \to 0$
- The ratio tends to $1$ and the test gives no information
- The ratio tends to $0$, so $a_n \to 0$

## Explanation

We have
$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(n + 1)!\, e^{n+1}}{(n + 1)^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{n^n}{n!\, e^n} = e \left( \frac{n}{n + 1} \right)^{n} = \frac{e}{\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}} .
$$
The denominator tends to $e$, so the ratio tends to $\ell = 1$. For $\ell = 1$ the **ratio test** gives no information about the limit of $a_n$. The value $\dfrac{1}{e}$ is the limit of the ratio for $\dfrac{n!}{n^n}$, without the factor $e^n$.
