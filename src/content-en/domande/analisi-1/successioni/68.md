Inside the logarithm of $\dfrac{\ln\left( 2^n + n^5 \right)}{n}$ there are an exponential and a power. What does the sequence tend to?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $2$
- $\ln 2$

## Explanation

Factoring out $2^n$, the infinity of higher order, we obtain $2^n + n^5 = 2^n \left( 1 + \dfrac{n^5}{2^n} \right)$. By the properties of logarithms
$$
\frac{\ln\left( 2^n + n^5 \right)}{n} = \ln 2 + \frac{1}{n} \ln \left( 1 + \frac{n^5}{2^n} \right) .
$$
By the **hierarchy of infinities** $\dfrac{n^5}{2^n} \to 0$, so $\ln \left( 1 + \dfrac{n^5}{2^n} \right) \to \ln 1 = 0$ and the second term tends to $0$. Therefore the limit equals $\ln 2$.
