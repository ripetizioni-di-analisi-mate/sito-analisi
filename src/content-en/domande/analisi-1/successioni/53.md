The proof of the convergence of $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}$ also involves $b_n = \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n+1}$, with $n \geq 1$. Which statement about $(b_n)$ is true?

- It is increasing and tends to $e$
- It is decreasing and tends to $e^2$
- It is increasing and diverges to $+\infty$
- It is decreasing and tends to $e$

## Explanation

In the proof, Bernoulli's inequality is used to check that $\dfrac{b_{n-1}}{b_n} > 1$ for $n \geq 2$, so $(b_n)$ is **decreasing**. For example $b_1 = 4$ and $b_2 = \dfrac{27}{8}$.

Moreover $b_n = \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n} \cdot \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)$, the product of a sequence tending to $e$ and one tending to $1$. Therefore $b_n \to e$. It follows that $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n} < e < b_n$ for every $n$.
