In the sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{3}{n} \right)^{n^2}$ the base tends to $1$ and the exponent is $n^2$. How does the sequence behave?

- It converges to $e^3$
- It converges to $1$
- It diverges to $+\infty$
- It converges to $e^9$

## Explanation

We write
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{3}{n} \right)^{n^2} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{3}{n} \right)^{n} \right]^{n} .
$$
The sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{3}{n} \right)^{n} - 2$ tends to $e^3 - 2 > 0$, so by sign preservation $\left( 1 + \dfrac{3}{n} \right)^{n} > 2$ eventually. Therefore the term of the sequence is eventually greater than $2^n$. Since $2^n \to +\infty$, by the **comparison for divergent sequences** the sequence diverges to $+\infty$.
