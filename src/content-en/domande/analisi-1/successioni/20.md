Let $a_n \to 3$, $b_n \to +\infty$ and $c_n \to 0$. For which expression is the limit not determined by the limits of the three sequences alone?

- $b_n\, c_n$
- $a_n + b_n$
- $\dfrac{a_n}{b_n}$
- $a_n\, b_n$

## Explanation

The product $b_n\, c_n$ is in the indeterminate form $\infty \cdot 0$. With $b_n = n$ we get a different limit depending on $c_n$, because $n \cdot \dfrac{1}{n} \to 1$, $n \cdot \dfrac{1}{n^2} \to 0$ and $n \cdot \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \to +\infty$.

The other expressions have a limit determined by the rules for infinite limits. We have $a_n + b_n \to 3 + (+\infty) = +\infty$ and $\dfrac{a_n}{b_n} \to 0$. Moreover $a_n\, b_n \to +\infty$, because the limit $3$ of $a_n$ is positive.
