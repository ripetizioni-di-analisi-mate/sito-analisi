The sequence is defined by $a_1 = 1$ and $a_{n+1} = a_n + \dfrac{1}{a_n}$. How does it behave as $n \to +\infty$?

- It converges to $1$
- It converges to $2$
- It has no limit
- It diverges to $+\infty$

## Explanation

By induction $a_n > 0$ for every $n$, because $a_1 = 1 > 0$ and, if $a_n > 0$, then $a_{n+1} = a_n + \dfrac{1}{a_n}$ is positive too. Hence $a_{n+1} - a_n = \dfrac{1}{a_n} > 0$ and the sequence is **strictly increasing**.

By the monotone sequence theorem the sequence has a limit $L$, finite or $+\infty$. If $L$ were finite, we would have $L \geq a_1 = 1$, and passing to the limit in the recurrence we would get $L = L + \dfrac{1}{L}$, that is, $\dfrac{1}{L} = 0$, which is impossible. Therefore $L = +\infty$.
