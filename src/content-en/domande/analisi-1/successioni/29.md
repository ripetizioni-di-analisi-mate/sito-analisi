The sequence $a_n = \dfrac{5 - n}{2n + 1}$, with $n \geq 1$, tends to $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Which statement about the sign of its terms is true?

- $a_n < 0$ eventually, but not for every $n \geq 1$
- $a_n < 0$ for every $n \geq 1$
- $a_n < -\dfrac{1}{2}$ eventually
- $a_n > 0$ eventually

## Explanation

The limit $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ is negative, so by the **sign-preservation theorem** we have $a_n < 0$ eventually. In this case it can also be seen directly, because the numerator $5 - n$ is negative for every $n > 5$.

The first term is $a_1 = \dfrac{4}{3} > 0$, so the inequality does not hold for every $n \geq 1$. Moreover
$$
a_n + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2(5 - n) + 2n + 1}{2(2n + 1)} = \frac{11}{2(2n + 1)} > 0 ,
$$
so $a_n > -\dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $n$, and the terms approach the limit while staying above it.
