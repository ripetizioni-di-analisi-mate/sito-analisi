Knowing that $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n} \to e$, what is $\displaystyle\lim_{k \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{k^2} \right)^{k^2}$?

- $e$
- $1$
- $e^2$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

Setting $a_n = \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}$ and $n_k = k^2$, the indices $n_k$ are strictly increasing and
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{1}{k^2} \right)^{k^2} = a_{n_k} .
$$
Hence the sequence is a **subsequence** of $(a_n)$, and by the theorem on the limit of subsequences it tends to the same limit $e$. The result differs from that of $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{k^2} \right)^{k}$, which tends to $1$, because there the exponent does not coincide with the denominator.
