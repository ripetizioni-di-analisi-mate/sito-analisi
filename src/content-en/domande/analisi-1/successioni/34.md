A convergent sequence $(a_n)$ satisfies $\dfrac{1}{n} < a_n < 2 + \dfrac{1}{n}$ for every $n \geq 1$. What can be said about its limit $L$?

- $0 < L < 2$
- $0 \leq L \leq 2$
- $L = 1$
- $1 \leq L \leq 3$

## Explanation

In the limit, non-strict inequalities are preserved. From $a_n - \dfrac{1}{n} > 0$ we get $L - 0 \geq 0$, that is, $L \geq 0$, and from $2 + \dfrac{1}{n} - a_n > 0$ we get $2 - L \geq 0$, that is, $L \leq 2$.

Strict inequalities, on the other hand, are not preserved. The sequence $a_n = \dfrac{2}{n}$ satisfies both estimates and tends to $0$, and the constant sequence $a_n = 2$ satisfies them and tends to $2$. Hence $L$ can also equal $0$ or $2$, and the only correct conclusion is $0 \leq L \leq 2$.
