The sequence $a_n = 1 + \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{9} + \dots + \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ is increasing and satisfies $a_n < 2$ for every $n \geq 1$. What follows?

- It converges to $2$
- It diverges to $+\infty$, because it is increasing
- It converges to a limit $L \leq 2$
- It may have no limit

## Explanation

The sequence is increasing, because $a_{n+1} - a_n = \dfrac{1}{(n + 1)^2} > 0$, and it is bounded above by $2$. By the **monotone sequence theorem** it converges to the supremum $L$ of its terms. Since $2$ is an upper bound, we have $L \leq 2$.

The theorem does not guarantee that the limit is $2$, because an upper bound is not necessarily the supremum, and in this case the limit is less than $2$. For $k \geq 3$ we have $\dfrac{1}{k^2} < \dfrac{1}{k(k - 1)} = \dfrac{1}{k - 1} - \dfrac{1}{k}$, so for $n \geq 3$
$$
a_n < 1 + \frac{1}{4} + \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{n} \right) < \frac{7}{4} .
$$
Also $a_1$ and $a_2$ are less than $\dfrac{7}{4}$, so $\dfrac{7}{4}$ is an upper bound of the terms and $L \leq \dfrac{7}{4} < 2$.
