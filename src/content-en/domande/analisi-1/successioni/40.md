Let $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{2}$ for $x>0$ and $f(0)=1$. Starting at $a_1=1$, set $a_{n+1}=f(a_n)$. Why does the sequence's limit $0$ fail to satisfy $L=f(L)$?

- The terms change sign at every step.
- The sequence reaches $0$ after finitely many steps.
- The function is discontinuous at $0$.
- The sequence is unbounded.

## Explanation

The terms are $1,\dfrac12,\dfrac14,\ldots$ and stay positive. Thus $f(a_n)=\dfrac{a_n}{2}\to0$, whereas $f(0)=1$. Discontinuity at the limiting point prevents substituting $L$ into $f$.
