We want to study the right-hand limit at $0$ of $f(x)=\ln x$, defined for $x>0$. Which sequence can be used directly to test a consequence of that limit?

- $x_n=1+\dfrac{1}{n}$
- $x_n=-\dfrac{1}{n}$
- $x_n=0$
- $x_n=e^{-n}$

## Explanation

The points must belong to the positive domain and approach zero. The sequence $e^{-n}$ meets both conditions and $\ln(e^{-n})=-n\to-\infty$. This example is consistent with the right-hand limit, although it alone does not prove the limit along every approach.
