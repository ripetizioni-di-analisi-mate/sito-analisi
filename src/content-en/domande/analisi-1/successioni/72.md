A power of the logarithm appears in $\dfrac{(\ln n)^3}{n^{\frac{1}{10}}}$. What happens as $n \to +\infty$?

- It diverges to $+\infty$
- It tends to $1$
- It tends to $0$
- It tends to $30$

## Explanation

The sequence can be written as the cube
$$
\frac{(\ln n)^3}{n^{\frac{1}{10}}} = \left( \frac{\ln n}{n^{\frac{1}{30}}} \right)^{3} .
$$
By the **hierarchy of infinities** $\dfrac{\ln n}{n^{\alpha}} \to 0$ for every $\alpha > 0$, in particular for $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{30}$. Hence the cube tends to $0$. For moderate values of $n$ the numerator is larger; for example, with $n = 10^{10}$ the numerator exceeds $12\,000$ and the denominator equals $10$, but the limit is $0$.
