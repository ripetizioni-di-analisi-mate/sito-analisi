The terms of a convergent sequence are reordered by a permutation of the indices, using each term exactly once. Why is the limit unchanged?

- Every permutation changes only finitely many positions.
- The first $n$ terms must have the same sum for every $n$.
- For each tolerance, there are still only finitely many terms outside the band after reordering.
- The reordered sequence must be monotone.

## Explanation

Fix a neighborhood of the limit. Only finitely many original terms lie outside it. A permutation moves those terms to another finite set of positions. If there are any such positions, every term beyond their largest index is in the neighborhood. If there are none, every term is already in it.
