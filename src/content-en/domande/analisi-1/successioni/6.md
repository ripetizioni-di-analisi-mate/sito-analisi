For $a_n = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ and a fixed number $\varepsilon > 0$, the inequality $|a_n - 0| < \varepsilon$ holds exactly for the indices $n$ greater than which quantity?

- $\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}$
- $\varepsilon^2$
- $\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^2}$

## Explanation

Since $a_n > 0$, the inequality is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} < \varepsilon$, that is, $\sqrt{n} > \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}$.

Squaring two positive numbers preserves the direction of the inequality, so the condition is equivalent to $n > \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^2}$. Therefore in the definition of limit we can choose as $N$ a natural number greater than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^2}$. For example, with $\varepsilon = \dfrac{1}{10}$ we need $n > 100$.
