We want the limit of $n^2 \left( \ln\left( n^2 + 1 \right) - 2 \ln n \right)$. Which value is obtained?

- $1$
- $0$
- $2$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

By the properties of logarithms $2 \ln n = \ln\left( n^2 \right)$, so
$$
\ln\left( n^2 + 1 \right) - 2 \ln n = \ln \frac{n^2 + 1}{n^2} = \ln \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n^2} \right) .
$$
With $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ the sequence becomes $\dfrac{\ln(1 + \varepsilon_n)}{\varepsilon_n}$, which tends to $1$ by the standard limit of the logarithm.
