We write $\sqrt[n]{3} = 1 + h_n$, with $h_n > 0$. Which inequality, valid for every $n \geq 1$, follows from Bernoulli's inequality?

- $h_n \leq \dfrac{2}{n}$
- $h_n \leq \dfrac{1}{n}$
- $h_n \leq \dfrac{2}{n^2}$
- $h_n \leq \dfrac{1}{3n}$

## Explanation

Raising to the power $n$ we get $3 = (1 + h_n)^n$, and by **Bernoulli's inequality** $(1 + h_n)^n \geq 1 + n h_n$. Hence $3 \geq 1 + n h_n$, that is,
$$
0 < h_n \leq \frac{2}{n} ,
$$
and by the squeeze theorem $h_n \to 0$, that is, $\sqrt[n]{3} \to 1$.

The other estimates are false for some $n$. For $n = 1$ we have $h_1 = 2$, which is greater than $1$ and than $\dfrac{1}{3}$. For $n = 2$ we have $h_2 = \sqrt{3} - 1 > \dfrac{1}{2}$, because $\sqrt{3} > \dfrac{3}{2}$, while $\dfrac{2}{n^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
