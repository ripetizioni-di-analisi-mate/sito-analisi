The sequence defined by $a_1 = 2$ and $a_{n+1} = \dfrac{a_n^2 + 4}{5}$ satisfies $1 < a_n \leq 2$ for every $n \geq 1$. What is its limit?

- $4$
- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{8}{5}$

## Explanation

We have
$$
a_{n+1} - a_n = \frac{a_n^2 - 5a_n + 4}{5} = \frac{(a_n - 1)(a_n - 4)}{5} ,
$$
which is negative for $1 < a_n \leq 2$. Hence the sequence is decreasing and bounded below by $1$, and by the **monotone sequence theorem** it has a finite limit $L$.

Passing to the limit in the recurrence relation we obtain $5L = L^2 + 4$, that is, $L = 1$ or $L = 4$. Since $a_n \leq 2$ for every $n$, we have $L \leq 2$, so the limit is $L = 1$. The value $\dfrac{8}{5}$ is the second term $a_2$.
