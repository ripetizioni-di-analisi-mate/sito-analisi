To approximate $\sqrt2$, start with $x>0$ and calculate $y=\dfrac12\left(x+\dfrac2x\right)$. Which identity expresses the new error exactly in terms of the old one?

- $y-\sqrt2=\dfrac{x-\sqrt2}{2}$
- $y-\sqrt2=\dfrac{(x-\sqrt2)^2}{2x}$
- $y-\sqrt2=-\dfrac{x-\sqrt2}{x}$
- $y-\sqrt2=\dfrac{(x-\sqrt2)^2}{2}$

## Explanation

Write $y-\sqrt2$ over the common denominator $2x$. Its numerator is $x^2-2\sqrt2\,x+2$, the square of $x-\sqrt2$. In particular, the new error is always nonnegative and contains the square of the previous one.
