Let $a_n=\dfrac{1+(-1)^n}{n}$. Why cannot the usual test based on $\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}$ be applied directly to the entire tail?

- The sequence is unbounded.
- The denominator of the ratio is zero at every odd index.
- The ratio always equals one.
- The term $a_n$ is negative at even indices.

## Explanation

When $n$ is odd, $1+(-1)^n=0$, so the next-to-current ratio is undefined. Infinitely many odd indices remain after discarding any initial segment. Convergence to zero still follows from $0\leq a_n\leq\dfrac{2}{n}$.
