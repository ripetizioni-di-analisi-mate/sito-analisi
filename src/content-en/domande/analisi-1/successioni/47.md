The sequence $a_n = \dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$ is not monotone. Which statement about it is true?

- It converges to $0$
- It has no limit, because it is not monotone
- It is eventually decreasing
- The subsequences of even and odd index have different limits

## Explanation

We have $|a_n - 0| = \dfrac{1}{n}$, which tends to $0$, so the sequence **converges** to $0$. The subsequences of even and odd index both tend to $0$.

The monotone sequence theorem gives a sufficient condition for the existence of the limit, not a necessary one. The terms change sign at every step, so the difference $a_{n+1} - a_n$ changes sign for every $n$ and the sequence is not monotone even from some index on.
