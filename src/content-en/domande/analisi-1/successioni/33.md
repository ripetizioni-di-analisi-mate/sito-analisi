A counter increases by $\dfrac1k$ at step $k$. Why can its reading grow without bound even though the increments tend to zero?

- From step $m+1$ through step $2m$, at least $\dfrac12$ is added for every $m$.
- The increments become negative for large $k$.
- An individual increment eventually exceeds $1$.
- The counter increases by the same amount at every step.

## Explanation

The block from $m+1$ through $2m$ contains $m$ terms, each at least $\dfrac1{2m}$. It therefore adds at least $\dfrac12$. Taking $m=1,2,4,8,\ldots$ gives successive disjoint blocks and unbounded total growth.
