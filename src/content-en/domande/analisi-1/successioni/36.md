The factor $(-1)^n$ appears in the sequence $a_n = n + (-1)^n \sqrt{n}$. How does the sequence behave?

- It has no limit, because $(-1)^n$ has no limit
- It diverges to $+\infty$
- It diverges to $-\infty$
- It converges to $1$

## Explanation

Since $(-1)^n \sqrt{n} \geq -\sqrt{n}$, for every $n$ we have
$$
a_n \geq n - \sqrt{n} = \sqrt{n} \left( \sqrt{n} - 1 \right) .
$$
The right-hand side is the product of two sequences that diverge to $+\infty$, so it diverges to $+\infty$. Therefore, by the **comparison for divergent sequences**, also $a_n \to +\infty$. The factor $(-1)^n$ multiplies only $\sqrt{n}$, which is negligible with respect to $n$, so the sequence has a limit even though $(-1)^n$ does not.
