What is the limit of the sum $s_n = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{4} - \dots + \left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{n}$?

- $2$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- The limit does not exist, because the terms change sign

## Explanation

For $q \neq 1$ we have $1 + q + q^2 + \dots + q^n = \dfrac{1 - q^{n+1}}{1 - q}$. With $q = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ we have $1 - q = \dfrac{3}{2}$, so
$$
s_n = \frac{2}{3} \left( 1 - \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right)^{n+1} \right) .
$$
The geometric sequence with ratio $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ tends to $0$, because $-1 < -\dfrac{1}{2} < 1$. Therefore $s_n \to \dfrac{2}{3}$. The value $2$ is obtained with the ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$ instead of $-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
