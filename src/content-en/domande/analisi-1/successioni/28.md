What is the limit of the ratio between the sum $1 + 2 + \dots + n$ and the square $n^2$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $0$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

By the formula for the sum of the first $n$ natural numbers, $1 + 2 + \dots + n = \dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2}$. Hence
$$
\frac{1 + 2 + \dots + n}{n^2} = \frac{n^2 + n}{2n^2} \to \frac{1}{2} .
$$
Each term $\dfrac{k}{n^2}$ tends to $0$, but the number of terms grows with $n$, so the limit is not obtained by adding the limits of the terms.
