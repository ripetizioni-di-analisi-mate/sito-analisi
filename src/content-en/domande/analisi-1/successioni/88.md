Each of the two exponentials in $n \left( e^{\frac{1}{n}} - e^{-\frac{1}{n}} \right)$ tends to $1$. What is the limit of the sequence?

- $0$
- $1$
- $2$
- $e$

## Explanation

Adding and subtracting $1$, the sequence splits into two parts
$$
n \left( e^{\frac{1}{n}} - e^{-\frac{1}{n}} \right) = n \left( e^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \right) - n \left( e^{-\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \right) .
$$
With $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$ the first term is $\dfrac{e^{\varepsilon_n} - 1}{\varepsilon_n} \to 1$. With $\varepsilon_n = -\dfrac{1}{n}$, infinitesimal and nonzero, we have $n = -\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_n}$, so the second term is $-n \left( e^{\varepsilon_n} - 1 \right) = \dfrac{e^{\varepsilon_n} - 1}{\varepsilon_n} \to 1$. Hence the limit equals $1 + 1 = 2$.
