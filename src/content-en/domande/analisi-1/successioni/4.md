Among the following sequences, only one diverges to $-\infty$. Which one?

- $a_n = (-1)^n\, n^2$
- $a_n = n - n^2$
- $a_n = \dfrac{1 - n^2}{n^2 + 1}$
- $a_n = -\dfrac{1}{n}$

## Explanation

Factoring out $n^2$ we obtain $n - n^2 = n^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{n} - 1 \right)$. The first factor tends to $+\infty$ and the second to $-1$, so the product **diverges** to $-\infty$.

The sequence $(-1)^n\, n^2$ is indeterminate, because the terms of even index tend to $+\infty$ and those of odd index to $-\infty$. The other two are convergent. The ratio $\dfrac{1 - n^2}{n^2 + 1}$ tends to the ratio of the leading coefficients, that is, to $-1$, and $-\dfrac{1}{n}$ tends to $0$.
