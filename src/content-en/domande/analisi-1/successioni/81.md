Which choice of the indices $n_k$, with $k \geq 1$, does not define a subsequence $\left( a_{n_k} \right)$?

- $n_k = 3k$
- $n_k = k^2$
- $n_k = 2^k$
- $n_k = k + (-1)^k$

## Explanation

A subsequence requires **strictly increasing** indices. With $n_k = k + (-1)^k$ we obtain the indices
$$
n_1 = 0, \quad n_2 = 3, \quad n_3 = 2, \quad n_4 = 5, \quad \dots
$$
and since $n_3 < n_2$ the sequence of indices is not increasing. Hence $\left( a_{n_k} \right)$ is not a subsequence, because it does not preserve the order of the terms.

The other choices are strictly increasing, since $3(k + 1) > 3k$, $(k + 1)^2 > k^2$ and $2^{k+1} > 2^k$.
