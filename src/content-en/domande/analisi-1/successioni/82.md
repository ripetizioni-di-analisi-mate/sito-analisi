The product $n^2 \left( 1 - \cos \dfrac{3}{n} \right)$ is in the form $\infty \cdot 0$. What is its limit?

- $9$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{9}{2}$

## Explanation

With the infinitesimal sequence $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{3}{n}$, different from $0$, we have $n^2 = \dfrac{9}{\varepsilon_n^2}$, so
$$
n^2 \left( 1 - \cos \frac{3}{n} \right) = 9 \cdot \frac{1 - \cos \varepsilon_n}{\varepsilon_n^2} .
$$
By the standard limit $\dfrac{1 - \cos \varepsilon_n}{\varepsilon_n^2} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$, transferred to sequences with the bridge theorem, the fraction tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore the limit equals $9 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{9}{2}$.
