Four sequences have a base tending to $1$. For which of them is the limit $e^{-2}$?

- $\left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{2n} \right)^{n}$
- $\left( 1 + \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{-2n}$
- $\left( 1 - \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{n^2}$
- $\left( 1 - \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{n}$

## Explanation

By the limit $\left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{n} \right)^n \to e^x$ with $x = -2$, the sequence $\left( 1 - \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{n}$ tends to $e^{-2}$.

With $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ we get instead $\left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{2n} \right)^{n} \to e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$. Moreover $\left( 1 + \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{-2n} = \left[ \left( 1 + \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{n} \right]^{-2} \to e^{-4}$. Finally $\left( 1 - \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{n}$ tends to $e^{-2} < \dfrac{1}{2}$, so it eventually lies between $0$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and its $n$-th power $\left( 1 - \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{n^2}$ tends to $0$.
