How does the sequence $a_n = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n}}$ behave?

- It converges to $0$
- It converges to $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- It diverges to $+\infty$
- It converges to $1$

## Explanation

Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by $\sqrt{n + 1} + \sqrt{n}$, the denominator becomes $(n + 1) - n = 1$. Hence
$$
a_n = \sqrt{n + 1} + \sqrt{n} \geq \sqrt{n} ,
$$
and since $\sqrt{n} \to +\infty$, the sequence **diverges** to $+\infty$. The same result follows by observing that the denominator is positive and tends to $0$, and the reciprocal of a positive infinitesimal sequence diverges to $+\infty$.
