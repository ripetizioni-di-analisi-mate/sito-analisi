Let $a_k\to L$. Given any $\varepsilon>0$, choose $K$ so that $|a_k-L|<\varepsilon$ for all $k>K$. Set $C=\sum_{k=1}^{K}|a_k-L|$. For $n>K$, which bound explains why the mean $M_n=\dfrac{a_1+\cdots+a_n}{n}$ also tends to $L$?

- $|M_n-L|=C$
- $|M_n-L|\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon}{n}$
- $|M_n-L|\leq \dfrac{C}{n^2}$
- $|M_n-L|\leq \dfrac{C}{n}+\varepsilon$

## Explanation

The first $K$ absolute errors sum to $C$, and the remaining $n-K$ sum to at most $(n-K)\varepsilon$. Dividing by $n$ and applying the triangle inequality gives the stated bound. For any target tolerance, we can choose a smaller $\varepsilon$ and then make $\dfrac{C}{n}$ small too.
