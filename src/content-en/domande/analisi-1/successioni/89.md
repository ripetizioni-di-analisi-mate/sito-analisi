Let $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$, so that $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = 1$. The sequence $(x_n)$ equals $0$ for even $n$ and $\dfrac{1}{n}$ for odd $n$. What can be said about $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} f(x_n)$?

- It equals $1$
- It equals $0$
- It does not exist
- It equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

We have $x_n \to 0$, but $x_n = 0$ for infinitely many indices. For even $n$ we have $f(x_n) = f(0) = 0$, while for $n = 2k + 1$
$$
f(x_{2k+1}) = \frac{\sin \frac{1}{2k + 1}}{\frac{1}{2k + 1}} \to 1 .
$$
The subsequences of even and odd index have different limits, so the limit of $f(x_n)$ **does not exist**. This does not contradict the bridge theorem, because the theorem concerns only sequences with $x_n \neq x_0$, and here the hypothesis $x_n \neq 0$ is not satisfied.
