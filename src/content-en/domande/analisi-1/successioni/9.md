Set $m=n-1$ with $n\geq2$. Which expression is exactly equal to $\left(\dfrac{n+2}{n-1}\right)^n$ for every $n$, before taking the limit?

- $\left(1+\dfrac2m\right)^{m+1}$
- $\left(1+\dfrac3m\right)^m\left(1+\dfrac3m\right)$
- $\left(1+\dfrac3m\right)^m$
- $\left(1+\dfrac3m\right)^m+1$

## Explanation

The fraction becomes $\dfrac{m+3}{m}=1+\dfrac3m$. The exponent is $m+1$, so the power with exponent $m$ must be multiplied by one more copy of the base. This extra factor tends to one but is part of the identity at each finite index.
