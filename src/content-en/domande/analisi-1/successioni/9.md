Which sequence is bounded and, at the same time, has no limit?

- $a_n = n \sin \dfrac{1}{n}$
- $a_n = (-1)^n\, n$
- $a_n = (-1)^n + \dfrac{1}{n}$
- $a_n = \ln n$

## Explanation

We have $\left| (-1)^n + \dfrac{1}{n} \right| \leq 1 + 1 = 2$, so the sequence is bounded. The subsequence of even index tends to $1$ and the one of odd index to $-1$, so the limit **does not exist**.

The other sequences do not have both properties. By the standard limit $\dfrac{\sin \varepsilon_n}{\varepsilon_n} \to 1$ with $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$, the sequence $n \sin \dfrac{1}{n}$ converges to $1$. The sequences $(-1)^n\, n$ and $\ln n$ are not bounded.
