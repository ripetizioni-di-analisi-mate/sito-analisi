A sequence is defined by $a_1 = 1$ and $a_{n+1} = \dfrac{a_n}{1 + a_n}$. Which expression gives the general term $a_n$?

- $a_n = \dfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{1}{n + 1}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{1}{n!}$

## Explanation

The first terms are $a_1 = 1$, $a_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $a_3 = \dfrac{a_2}{1 + a_2} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, and they suggest $a_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$. The formula holds for $n = 1$ and, if it holds for $n$, then
$$
a_{n+1} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{n}}{1 + \dfrac{1}{n}} = \frac{1}{n + 1} .
$$
By induction $a_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$ for every $n \geq 1$. The expression $\dfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}$ gives $a_3 = \dfrac{1}{4}$, and $\dfrac{1}{n + 1}$ gives $a_1 = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so neither satisfies the recurrence with $a_1 = 1$.
