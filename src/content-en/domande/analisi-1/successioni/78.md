The exponent of $a_n = n^{1 + (-1)^n}$, with $n \geq 1$, equals $2$ or $0$ depending on the parity of $n$. Which description of the sequence is correct?

- It is not bounded and has a convergent subsequence
- It diverges to $+\infty$
- Every subsequence of it diverges to $+\infty$
- It is bounded

## Explanation

For $n = 2k$ the exponent equals $2$, so $a_{2k} = (2k)^2 \to +\infty$ and the sequence **is not bounded**. For $n = 2k + 1$ the exponent equals $0$, so $a_{2k+1} = 1$ for every $k$, and this subsequence converges to $1$.

The sequence does not diverge to $+\infty$, because in that case every subsequence would have to diverge to $+\infty$. The example shows that a sequence that is not bounded can have convergent subsequences, even though the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem does not guarantee it.
