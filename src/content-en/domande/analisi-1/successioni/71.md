The factor $n$ multiplies $4^n$ in the denominator of $\dfrac{5^n - 4^n}{5^n + n\, 4^n}$. What is the limit?

- $0$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

Dividing the numerator and the denominator by $5^n$ we obtain
$$
\frac{5^n - 4^n}{5^n + n\, 4^n} = \frac{1 - \left( \dfrac{4}{5} \right)^{n}}{1 + n \left( \dfrac{4}{5} \right)^{n}} .
$$
The geometric sequence $\left( \dfrac{4}{5} \right)^{n}$ tends to $0$. Moreover $n \left( \dfrac{4}{5} \right)^{n} = \dfrac{n}{\left( \frac{5}{4} \right)^{n}}$ tends to $0$ by the **hierarchy of infinities**, with $\alpha = 1$ and $a = \dfrac{5}{4}$. Therefore the limit equals $1$.
