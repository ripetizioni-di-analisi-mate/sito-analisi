The following sequences are all infinitesimal. For which of them does the reciprocal $\dfrac{1}{a_n}$ diverge to $+\infty$?

- $a_n = \dfrac{2 + (-1)^n}{n}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$
- $a_n = -\dfrac{1}{n^2}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{\sin n}{n}$

## Explanation

If $a_n \to 0$ and $a_n > 0$ eventually, then $\dfrac{1}{a_n} \to +\infty$. For $a_n = \dfrac{2 + (-1)^n}{n}$ we have $\dfrac{1}{n} \leq a_n \leq \dfrac{3}{n}$, so $a_n > 0$ for every $n$ and, by the squeeze theorem, $a_n \to 0$. Therefore the reciprocal **diverges** to $+\infty$.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$ is $(-1)^n\, n$, which has no limit, and the reciprocal of $-\dfrac{1}{n^2}$ is $-n^2 \to -\infty$. Finally $\sin x$ is positive on the intervals $\left( 2k\pi, (2k + 1)\pi \right)$ and negative on the intervals $\left( (2k + 1)\pi, (2k + 2)\pi \right)$, and each of them, of length $\pi > 1$, contains an integer. Hence $\sin n$ is positive for infinitely many $n$ and negative for infinitely many $n$. So $\dfrac{n}{\sin n}$ takes negative values infinitely many times, and by sign preservation it cannot diverge to $+\infty$.
