The graph shows the first terms of $a_n = (-1)^n \dfrac{n}{n + 1}$, with $n \geq 1$. What are the supremum and the infimum of the set of terms?

- $\sup = \dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\inf = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\sup = 1$ and $\inf = -1$
- $\sup = 1$ and $\inf = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\sup = \dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\inf = -1$

## Explanation

For even $n$ we have $a_n = \dfrac{n}{n + 1} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{n + 1}$, which increases starting from $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and stays less than $1$. For odd $n$ we have $a_n = -\dfrac{n}{n + 1}$, which decreases starting from $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and stays greater than $-1$. Hence $-1 < a_n < 1$ for every $n$.

The terms of even index tend to $1$, so no number less than $1$ is an upper bound and $\sup = 1$. Similarly the terms of odd index tend to $-1$, so $\inf = -1$. The values $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ are the smallest term of even index and the largest term of odd index.
