The sequence $a_n = 2 + (-1)^n$ equals $3$ for even $n$ and $1$ for odd $n$. For which $\varepsilon > 0$ does the inequality $|a_n - 3| < \varepsilon$ hold eventually?

- For every $\varepsilon > 2$
- For every $\varepsilon \geq 2$
- For every $\varepsilon > 1$
- For no $\varepsilon > 0$

## Explanation

For even $n$ we have $|a_n - 3| = 0$, which is less than every $\varepsilon > 0$. For odd $n$, on the other hand, $|a_n - 3| = |1 - 3| = 2$.

However $N$ is chosen, there are odd indices greater than $N$, so the inequality holds eventually if and only if $2 < \varepsilon$. For $\varepsilon = 2$ it does not hold, because $2$ is not less than $2$. Since the definition of limit requires the inequality for **every** $\varepsilon > 0$ and it does not hold, for example, with $\varepsilon = 1$, the sequence does not tend to $3$.
