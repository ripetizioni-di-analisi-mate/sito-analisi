What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} n \left( \sqrt[3]{1 + \frac{3}{n}} - 1 \right)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $1$
- $3$
- $0$

## Explanation

With $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{3}{n}$ we have $n = \dfrac{3}{\varepsilon_n}$, so
$$
n \left( \sqrt[3]{1 + \frac{3}{n}} - 1 \right) = 3 \cdot \frac{(1 + \varepsilon_n)^{\frac{1}{3}} - 1}{\varepsilon_n} .
$$
By the standard limit $\dfrac{(1 + \varepsilon_n)^{\alpha} - 1}{\varepsilon_n} \to \alpha$ with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$, the fraction tends to $\dfrac{1}{3}$. Therefore the limit equals $3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = 1$.
