What is the limit of the sequence $\left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{-n}$, with $n \geq 2$?

- $e$
- $e^{-1}$
- $1$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

With $a_n = -n$ we have $1 + \dfrac{1}{a_n} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{n}$, so
$$
\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n} \right)^{-n} = \left( 1 + \frac{1}{a_n} \right)^{a_n} .
$$
Since $a_n \to -\infty$, the theorem on **limits that reduce to the number $e$** applies in this case too, and the limit equals $e$. The same result follows from $\left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n} \to e^{-1}$, by taking reciprocals.
