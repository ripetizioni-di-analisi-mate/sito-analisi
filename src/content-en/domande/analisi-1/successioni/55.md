Suppose $a_{2k}\to0$ and $a_{3k}\to0$. To construct an example in which $a_n$ fails to tend to zero, on which family of indices can we set the value to $1$ while leaving all other terms zero?

- $6k+2$
- $6k+1$
- $6k$
- $6k+3$

## Explanation

The indices $6k+1$ are odd and are not divisible by three. Changing them leaves both observed subsequences untouched. Since there are infinitely many such indices, the value $1$ keeps occurring at arbitrarily large indices.
