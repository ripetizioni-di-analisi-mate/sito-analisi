Which of these sequences, defined for $n \geq 1$, is increasing?

- $a_n = n + (-1)^n$
- $a_n = \dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$
- $a_n = n^2 - 5n$
- $a_n = n + \dfrac{(-1)^n}{2}$

## Explanation

For $a_n = n + \dfrac{(-1)^n}{2}$ we have
$$
a_{n+1} - a_n = 1 + \frac{(-1)^{n+1} - (-1)^n}{2} = 1 - (-1)^n ,
$$
which equals $0$ for even $n$ and $2$ for odd $n$. Hence $a_{n+1} \geq a_n$ for every $n$ and the sequence is **increasing**, though not strictly, since $a_2 = a_3 = \dfrac{5}{2}$.

For $n + (-1)^n$ the difference is $1 - 2(-1)^n$, which equals $-1$ for even $n$, for example $a_2 = 3$ and $a_3 = 2$. The terms of $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$ change sign at every step. Finally $n^2 - 5n$ equals $-4$ for $n = 1$ and $-6$ for $n = 2$, and it increases only from $n = 3$ on.
