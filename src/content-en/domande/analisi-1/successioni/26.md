For which $\alpha > 0$ is the limit of $n^{\alpha} \left( \sqrt{n^2 + 1} - n \right)$ finite and different from $0$, and what is its value in that case?

- $\alpha = 2$, with limit $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha = 1$, with limit $1$
- $\alpha = 1$, with limit $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, with limit $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{n^2 + 1} + n$ we obtain $\sqrt{n^2 + 1} - n = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n^2 + 1} + n}$, so
$$
n^{\alpha} \left( \sqrt{n^2 + 1} - n \right) = \frac{n^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{n^2 + 1} + n} = n^{\alpha - 1} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{n^2}} + 1} .
$$
The second factor tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The factor $n^{\alpha - 1}$ tends to $0$ for $\alpha < 1$ and to $+\infty$ for $\alpha > 1$, so the limit is finite and nonzero only for $\alpha = 1$, and in that case it equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
