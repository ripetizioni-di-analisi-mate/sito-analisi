The sequence $b_n = \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n^2 + k}}$ is a sum of $n$ terms. What is its limit?

- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

For $1 \leq k \leq n$ we have $n^2 + 1 \leq n^2 + k \leq n^2 + n$, so each term lies between $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n^2 + n}}$ and $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n^2 + 1}}$. Adding the $n$ terms we obtain
$$
\frac{n}{\sqrt{n^2 + n}} \leq b_n \leq \frac{n}{\sqrt{n^2 + 1}} .
$$
Dividing the numerator and the denominator by $n$, the two outer sequences become $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{n}}}$ and $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{n^2}}}$, and both tend to $1$. Therefore, by the **squeeze theorem**, $b_n \to 1$.
