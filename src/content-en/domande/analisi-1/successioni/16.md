For which value of the real parameter $k$ is the sequence $a_n = \dfrac{k n^2 + 3n}{2n + 5}$ convergent, and what is its limit?

- For every $k$, with limit $\dfrac{k}{2}$
- Only for $k = 0$, with limit $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- Only for $k = 0$, with limit $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- For no value of $k$

## Explanation

For $k \neq 0$ the numerator has degree $2$ and the denominator degree $1$. Factoring we obtain
$$
a_n = n \cdot \frac{k + \dfrac{3}{n}}{2 + \dfrac{5}{n}} ,
$$
where the first factor tends to $+\infty$ and the second to $\dfrac{k}{2} \neq 0$. Hence the sequence diverges, to $+\infty$ for $k > 0$ and to $-\infty$ for $k < 0$.

For $k = 0$ we have $a_n = \dfrac{3n}{2n + 5}$, a ratio of two polynomials of degree $1$, so $a_n \to \dfrac{3}{2}$.
