Which set consists exactly of the limits of the convergent subsequences of $a_n = \cos \dfrac{2n\pi}{3}$?

- $\{ -1,\ 1 \}$
- $\left\{ -\dfrac{1}{2},\ 1 \right\}$
- $[ -1,\ 1 ]$
- $\{ 1 \}$

## Explanation

The values repeat with period $3$. For $n = 3k$ we have $\cos(2k\pi) = 1$; for $n = 3k + 1$ and $n = 3k + 2$ the angle differs from $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and from $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ by a multiple of $2\pi$, and the cosine equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Hence the terms take only the values $1$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, each infinitely many times.

The subsequences $\left( a_{3k} \right)$ and $\left( a_{3k+1} \right)$ are constant and tend to $1$ and to $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. If a subsequence converges to $L$, from some index on its terms are less than $\dfrac{3}{4}$ away from $L$, so they are less than $\dfrac{3}{2}$ apart from each other. Since $1$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ are $\dfrac{3}{2}$ apart, from that index on the subsequence is constant, so its limit is $1$ or $-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
