For $a_n = \left( 1 + (-1)^n \right) \dfrac{n}{n + 1}$, what do the subsequence of even index and the subsequence of odd index tend to?

- To $2$ and to $0$
- To $1$ and to $-1$
- To $2$ and to $-2$
- To $1$ and to $0$

## Explanation

For $n = 2k$ we have $1 + (-1)^{2k} = 2$, so
$$
a_{2k} = \frac{2 \cdot 2k}{2k + 1} \to 2 .
$$
For $n = 2k + 1$ we have $1 + (-1)^{2k+1} = 0$, so $a_{2k+1} = 0$ for every $k$ and the subsequence tends to $0$. Since the two limits are different, by the theorem on the **limit of subsequences** the sequence $(a_n)$ has no limit.
