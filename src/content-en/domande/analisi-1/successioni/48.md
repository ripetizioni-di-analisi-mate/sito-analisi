What does $\left( 1 + \dfrac{2}{n} \right)^{n + 5}$ tend to as $n \to +\infty$?

- $e^7$
- $e^2$
- $1$
- $e$

## Explanation

Splitting the exponent into two parts we obtain
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{2}{n} \right)^{n + 5} = \left( 1 + \frac{2}{n} \right)^{n} \cdot \left( 1 + \frac{2}{n} \right)^{5} .
$$
By the limit $\left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{n} \right)^n \to e^x$ with $x = 2$, the first factor tends to $e^2$. The second is the fifth power of a sequence tending to $1$, so it tends to $1$. Therefore the limit equals $e^2$, and the term $5$ in the exponent does not change it.
