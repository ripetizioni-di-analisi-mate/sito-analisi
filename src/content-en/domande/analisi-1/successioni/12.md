For a sequence $(a_n)$ it is known that, for every $n \geq 1$, both $|a_n - 1| < \dfrac{1}{n}$ and $|a_n - b| < \dfrac{2}{n}$ hold, with $b \in \mathbb{R}$. Which values can $b$ take?

- Every $b$ with $|b - 1| < 3$
- $b = 1$ or $b = 3$
- Every $b \in [-1, 3]$
- Only $b = 1$

## Explanation

The first estimate gives $a_n \to 1$ and the second gives $a_n \to b$. By the **uniqueness of the limit** we therefore have $b = 1$.

The same result follows from the triangle inequality, because
$$
|1 - b| \leq |1 - a_n| + |a_n - b| < \frac{3}{n} \qquad \text{for every } n \geq 1 ,
$$
and the only nonnegative number less than $\dfrac{3}{n}$ for every $n$ is $0$. The value $b = 1$ is actually possible, for example with the constant sequence $a_n = 1$.
