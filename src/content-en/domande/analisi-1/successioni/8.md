The terms of $a_n = 10^6 + \dfrac{1}{n}$ exceed the number $10^6$ for every $n \geq 1$. What can be said about the limit of this sequence?

- It diverges to $+\infty$, because the terms exceed the threshold $10^6$
- It converges to $10^6 + 1$
- It converges to $10^6$
- It has no limit

## Explanation

By the limit of a sum, $10^6 + \dfrac{1}{n} \to 10^6 + 0$, so the sequence **converges** to $10^6$.

The definition of the limit $+\infty$ requires the terms to eventually exceed *every* threshold $M > 0$, not one fixed threshold. With $M = 10^6 + 1$ we have $a_n \leq M$ for every $n \geq 1$, so the sequence does not diverge. The number $10^6 + 1$ is the first term $a_1$, which does not coincide with the limit.
