Which subsequence of $a_n = \sin \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$ converges to $-1$?

- $\left( a_{4k+1} \right)$
- $\left( a_{2k} \right)$
- $\left( a_{4k+3} \right)$
- $\left( a_{4k} \right)$

## Explanation

The values of $\sin \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$ repeat with period $4$. For $n = 4k$ and $n = 4k + 2$ the angle is a multiple of $\pi$ and the sine equals $0$; for $n = 4k + 1$ it equals $\sin \left( 2k\pi + \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = 1$; for $n = 4k + 3$ it equals
$$
\sin \left( 2k\pi + \frac{3\pi}{2} \right) = -1 .
$$
Hence the subsequence $\left( a_{4k+3} \right)$ is constant and equal to $-1$, and it converges to $-1$. The subsequences $\left( a_{4k+1} \right)$, $\left( a_{2k} \right)$ and $\left( a_{4k} \right)$ are constant and equal to $1$, $0$ and $0$.
