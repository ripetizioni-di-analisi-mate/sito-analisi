The base and the exponent of $a_n = n^{\frac{1}{\ln n}}$, with $n \geq 2$, tend to $+\infty$ and to $0$ respectively. What value does the sequence tend to?

- $e$
- $1$
- $+\infty$
- $0$

## Explanation

For $n \geq 2$ we have $n = e^{\ln n}$, so
$$
n^{\frac{1}{\ln n}} = e^{\ln n \cdot \frac{1}{\ln n}} = e^{1} = e .
$$
The sequence is constant and its limit equals $e$. The form $\infty^{0}$ is indeterminate: for example, $\sqrt[n]{n} = n^{\frac{1}{n}}$ is also in the form $\infty^{0}$ and tends to $1$.
