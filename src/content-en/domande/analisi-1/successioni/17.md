As $n \to +\infty$, how does the sequence $a_n = \dfrac{n^3 - 2n}{1 - 5n^2}$ behave?

- It diverges to $+\infty$
- It converges to $-\dfrac{1}{5}$
- It diverges to $-\infty$
- It converges to $0$

## Explanation

Factoring out $n^3$ in the numerator and $n^2$ in the denominator we obtain
$$
a_n = n \cdot \frac{1 - \dfrac{2}{n^2}}{\dfrac{1}{n^2} - 5} .
$$
The first factor tends to $+\infty$ and the second to $-\dfrac{1}{5}$, which is negative. Therefore, by the product rule with an infinite limit, the sequence **diverges** to $-\infty$.
