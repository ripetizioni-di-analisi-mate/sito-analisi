The sine appears in the base of $\left( 1 + \sin \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}$. What is the limit?

- $1$
- $e$
- $\sqrt{e}$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

For $n \geq 1$ we have $0 < \sin \dfrac{1}{n}$, and $\sin \dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$. Setting $a_n = \dfrac{1}{\sin \frac{1}{n}}$, we have $a_n \to +\infty$ and therefore $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{a_n} \right)^{a_n} \to e$.

The exponent is written as $n = a_n \cdot n \sin \dfrac{1}{n}$, and by the standard limit of the sine $n \sin \dfrac{1}{n} \to 1$. Therefore
$$
\left( 1 + \sin \frac{1}{n} \right)^{n} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{a_n} \right)^{a_n} \right]^{n \sin \frac{1}{n}} \to e^{1} = e .
$$
