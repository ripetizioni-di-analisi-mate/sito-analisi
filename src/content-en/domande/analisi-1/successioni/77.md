For which of these sequences does the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem guarantee the existence of a convergent subsequence?

- $a_n = \sin\left( n^2 \right)$
- $a_n = n^{(-1)^n}$
- $a_n = n \cos(n\pi)$
- $a_n = \ln n$

## Explanation

The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem requires the sequence to be **bounded**. Since $\left| \sin\left( n^2 \right) \right| \leq 1$, the hypothesis is satisfied and the theorem guarantees a convergent subsequence, although it does not say which one.

The other sequences are not bounded, so the theorem does not apply. The sequence $n^{(-1)^n}$ does have a convergent subsequence, because the terms of odd index equal $\dfrac{1}{n}$ and tend to $0$, but this does not follow from the theorem. Finally $n \cos(n\pi) = (-1)^n n$ and $\ln n$ have no convergent subsequences, because every subsequence of theirs has terms whose absolute value diverges.
