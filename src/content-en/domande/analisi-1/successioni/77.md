For which $p\in\mathbb R$ does $n^p(\sqrt[n]{2}-1)$ have a finite, strictly positive limit?

- $p=-1$
- $p=2$
- $p=0$
- $p=1$

## Explanation

Write $\sqrt[n]{2}=\exp(\dfrac{\ln 2}{n})$. Since $\dfrac{e^t-1}{t}\to1$, the difference from one is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{\ln 2}{n}$. The product is therefore equivalent to $(\ln2)n^{p-1}$ and has a finite positive limit only for $p=1$.
