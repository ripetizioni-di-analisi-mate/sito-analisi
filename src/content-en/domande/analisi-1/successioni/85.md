The limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \cos x$ does not exist. Which pair of sequences $(x_n)$, $(y_n)$ allows us to prove it with the bridge theorem?

- $x_n = 2n\pi$, $y_n = 4n\pi$
- $x_n = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$, $y_n = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$
- $x_n = 2n\pi$, $y_n = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$
- $x_n = 2n\pi$, $y_n = \pi - 2n\pi$

## Explanation

The sequences $x_n = 2n\pi$ and $y_n = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$ both tend to $+\infty$, and $\cos x_n = 1$, $\cos y_n = 0$ for every $n$. If the limit existed and equaled $L$, by the **bridge theorem** both sequences of values would tend to $L$. Since they tend to $1$ and to $0$, the limit does not exist.

With $x_n = 2n\pi$ and $y_n = 4n\pi$ the values of the cosine both tend to $1$, and with $x_n = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$ and $y_n = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$ they both tend to $0$, so these pairs do not lead to a contradiction. The sequence $y_n = \pi - 2n\pi$ tends to $-\infty$, so it does not concern the limit as $x \to +\infty$.
