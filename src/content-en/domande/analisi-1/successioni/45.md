For a positive sequence, $\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\to1$. Which pair of examples shows that this result alone cannot decide between a zero limit and an infinite limit?

- $a_n=1$ and $b_n=2$
- $a_n=n$ and $b_n=n^2$
- $a_n=2^{-n}$ and $b_n=2^n$
- $a_n=\dfrac1n$ and $b_n=n$

## Explanation

For $\dfrac1n$ the ratio is $\dfrac{n}{n+1}$, and for $n$ it is $\dfrac{n+1}{n}$. Both ratios tend to one, yet the sequences tend to zero and $+\infty$, respectively. The limiting ratio is therefore compatible with either behavior.
