For $f(x)=\sin(\dfrac{\pi}{x})$, the sample points $x_n=\dfrac{1}{n}$ always give zero. Which second sequence tends to $0$ and always gives $f(x_n)=1$, disproving the existence of the limit?

- $x_n=\dfrac{2}{4n+3}$
- $x_n=\dfrac{1}{2n}$
- $x_n=\dfrac{1}{2n+1}$
- $x_n=\dfrac{2}{4n+1}$

## Explanation

To make the sine one, the angle must be $\dfrac{\pi}{2}+2n\pi$. Solving $\dfrac{\pi}{x}=\dfrac{\pi}{2}+2n\pi$ gives $x=\dfrac{2}{4n+1}$. These points tend to zero and their function values differ from those of the original samples.
