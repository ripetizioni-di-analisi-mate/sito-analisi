Let $a_n=(1-\dfrac{1}{n})^n$ for $n\geq2$. Why is it legitimate to take the logarithm of $a_n$ at every index under consideration?

- The logarithm is defined at every real number.
- The base is positive and so is its power.
- The exponent is always even.
- A finite limit alone suffices.

## Explanation

For $n\geq2$ we have $1-\dfrac{1}{n}\geq\dfrac{1}{2}>0$. A power of a positive base is positive, so its real logarithm is defined. At $n=1$ the base would be zero, explaining the index restriction.
