What is obtained as $n \to +\infty$ from the ratio $\dfrac{n^{100}}{1.01^{n}}$?

- $+\infty$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{1.01}$
- $0$

## Explanation

By the **hierarchy of infinities**, $\dfrac{n^{\alpha}}{a^{n}} \to 0$ for every $\alpha > 0$ and every $a > 1$. With $\alpha = 100$ and $a = 1.01$ the limit therefore equals $0$.

For the first values of $n$ the numerator is much larger than the denominator; for example, for $n = 10$ we have $10^{100}$ against about $1.1$. The limit, however, depends only on what happens eventually, and an exponential with base greater than $1$ is an infinity of higher order than any power of $n$.
