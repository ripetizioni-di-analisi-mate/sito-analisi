The sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n^2} \right)^{n}$ is in the form $1^{\infty}$. What is its limit?

- $e$
- $+\infty$
- $1$
- $\sqrt{e}$

## Explanation

The sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{m} \right)^{m}$ is increasing with limit $e$, so it is less than $e$ for every $m$. With $m = n^2$ we get $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n^2} \right)^{n^2} < e$, and taking the $n$-th root
$$
1 < \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n^2} \right)^{n} < \sqrt[n]{e} .
$$
Since $\sqrt[n]{e} \to 1$, by the **squeeze theorem** the limit equals $1$.
