How does the $n$-th root of the factorial, $\sqrt[n]{n!}$, behave?

- It converges to $1$
- It converges to $e$
- It has no limit
- It diverges to $+\infty$

## Explanation

Let $n \geq 2$. In the product $n! = 1 \cdot 2 \cdots n$ the factors $k$ with $k \geq \dfrac{n}{2}$ are at least $\dfrac{n}{2}$ in number, and each of them is at least $\dfrac{n}{2}$. The other factors are at least $1$, so
$$
n! \geq \left( \frac{n}{2} \right)^{\frac{n}{2}} , \qquad \text{hence} \qquad \sqrt[n]{n!} \geq \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}} .
$$
Since $\sqrt{\dfrac{n}{2}} \to +\infty$, by the **comparison for divergent sequences** also $\sqrt[n]{n!} \to +\infty$. The limit $\sqrt[n]{n} \to 1$ concerns the $n$-th root of $n$, not that of $n!$.
