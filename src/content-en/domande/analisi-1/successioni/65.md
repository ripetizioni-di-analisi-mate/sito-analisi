In which case does the ratio test allow no conclusion about the limit of the sequence?

- $a_n = \dfrac{n^3}{2^n}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{n!}{10^n}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{n^2 + 1}{n^5}$
- $a_n = \dfrac{3^n}{n!}$

## Explanation

For $a_n = \dfrac{n^2 + 1}{n^5}$ we have
$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(n + 1)^2 + 1}{n^2 + 1} \cdot \left( \frac{n}{n + 1} \right)^{5} \to 1 ,
$$
so $\ell = 1$ and the test **gives no information**. The limit of this sequence is $0$, but it follows from comparing the degrees.

For the others the test decides. The ratio for $\dfrac{n^3}{2^n}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2} \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{3} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$, the one for $\dfrac{n!}{10^n}$ is $\dfrac{n + 1}{10} \to +\infty$ and the one for $\dfrac{3^n}{n!}$ is $\dfrac{3}{n + 1} \to 0$.
