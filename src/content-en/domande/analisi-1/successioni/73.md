What is the limit of the difference $2^n - n^{10}$?

- $+\infty$
- $-\infty$
- $0$
- $1$

## Explanation

The difference is in the form $+\infty - \infty$. Factoring out the infinity of higher order we obtain
$$
2^n - n^{10} = 2^n \left( 1 - \frac{n^{10}}{2^n} \right) .
$$
By the hierarchy of infinities $\dfrac{n^{10}}{2^n} \to 0$, so the second factor tends to $1$ and the product diverges to $+\infty$. For $n = 10$ the difference is negative, because $2^{10} < 10^{10}$, but it is eventually positive.
