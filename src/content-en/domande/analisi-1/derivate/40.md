What is the $n$-th derivative of $f(x) = \ln(1 + x)$ for $x > -1$ and $n \geq 1$?

- $\dfrac{(n-1)!}{(1 + x)^{n}}$
- $\dfrac{(-1)^{n}\,n!}{(1 + x)^{n+1}}$
- $\dfrac{(-1)^{n-1}\,(n-1)!}{(1 + x)^{n}}$
- $\dfrac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(1 + x)^{n}}$

## Explanation

The first derivative is $f'(x) = (1 + x)^{-1}$. Since the derivative of $(1 + x)^{-k}$ is $-k\,(1 + x)^{-k-1}$, going from one derivative to the next the exponent decreases by $1$ and the factor $-k$ appears. The first derivatives are therefore $f''(x) = -(1 + x)^{-2}$, $f'''(x) = 2\,(1 + x)^{-3}$, $f^{(4)}(x) = -6\,(1 + x)^{-4}$. From the first derivative to the $n$-th derivative the product $(-1)(-2)\cdots\bigl(-(n-1)\bigr) = (-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!$ accumulates, therefore
$$
f^{(n)}(x) = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}\,(n-1)!}{(1 + x)^{n}} .
$$
