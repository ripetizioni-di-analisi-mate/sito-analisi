For which value of $a$ is the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x - x + a x^3}{x^5}$ finite?

- $a = -\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{120}$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$
- $a = 1$

## Explanation

With the expansion $\sin x = x - \dfrac{x^3}{6} + \dfrac{x^5}{120} + o(x^6)$ the numerator becomes
$$
\left( a - \frac{1}{6} \right) x^3 + \frac{x^5}{120} + o(x^5) .
$$
Dividing by $x^5$ we obtain $\dfrac{a - \frac{1}{6}}{x^2} + \dfrac{1}{120} + \dfrac{o(x^5)}{x^5}$. If $a \neq \dfrac{1}{6}$, the first term tends to $+\infty$ or to $-\infty$ according to the sign of $a - \dfrac{1}{6}$, so the limit is not finite. For $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$ the first term is zero and the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{120}$; therefore the limit is finite **only** for $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$.
