For the function $f(x) = |x|\,\sin x$, which statement about the point $x = 0$ is true?

- $f$ is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 0$
- $f$ is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 1$
- $x = 0$ is a corner point
- $x = 0$ is a cusp

## Explanation

Since $f(0) = 0$, the difference quotient at $0$ is $\dfrac{|h|\,\sin h}{h}$. For $h \neq 0$ it can be written as $|h| \cdot \dfrac{\sin h}{h}$, the product of a factor that tends to $0$ and a factor that tends to $1$ by the standard limit of the sine. Therefore the quotient tends to $0$ both as $h \to 0^{+}$ and as $h \to 0^{-}$, and $f$ is **differentiable** at $0$ with $f'(0) = 0$.
