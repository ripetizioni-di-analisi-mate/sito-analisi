For which values of $\alpha > 0$ is the function $f(x) = |x|^{\alpha}$ differentiable at $x = 0$?

- $\alpha > 1$
- $\alpha \geq 1$
- $\alpha > 0$
- $\alpha \geq 2$

## Explanation

Since $f(0) = 0$, the difference quotient at $0$ is $\dfrac{|h|^{\alpha}}{h}$, whose absolute value is $|h|^{\alpha - 1}$. If $\alpha > 1$, then $|h|^{\alpha - 1} \to 0$, so the quotient tends to $0$ and $f'(0) = 0$. If $\alpha = 1$, the quotient equals $1$ for $h > 0$ and $-1$ for $h < 0$, therefore $0$ is a corner point. If $0 < \alpha < 1$, then $|h|^{\alpha - 1} \to +\infty$, so the quotient tends to $+\infty$ as $h \to 0^{+}$ and to $-\infty$ as $h \to 0^{-}$, and $0$ is a cusp. The function is continuous at $0$ for every $\alpha > 0$, but it is differentiable at $0$ **if and only if** $\alpha > 1$.
