For which values of $a$ and $b$ is the function
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
a x^2 + 1 & \text{if } x \leq 1 \\[4pt]
\ln x + b & \text{if } x > 1
\end{cases}
$$
differentiable at $x = 1$?

- $a = 2$, $b = 3$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $b = \dfrac{3}{2}$
- $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $b = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $a = 1$, $b = 2$

## Explanation

A function that is differentiable at $1$ is continuous at $1$. We have $f(1) = a + 1$ and, as $x \to 1^{+}$, $\ln x + b \to b$, so continuity requires $a + 1 = b$. The derivatives of the two expressions are $2ax$ and $\dfrac{1}{x}$, which tend to $2a$ and to $1$ as $x \to 1$; if $f$ is continuous at $1$, by the **limit-of-the-derivative theorem** these are the values of the one-sided derivatives. The conditions to be satisfied simultaneously are
$$
\begin{cases}
a + 1 = b \\[4pt]
2a = 1
\end{cases}
$$
therefore $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $b = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
