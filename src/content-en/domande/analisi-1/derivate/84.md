The function $f(x) = e^{\sin x}$ is the composition of the exponential with the sine. Up to degree three, what is its Maclaurin expansion?

- $1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{x^3}{6}$
- $1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2} - \dfrac{x^3}{6}$
- $1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2}$
- $1 + x - \dfrac{x^3}{6}$

## Explanation

Let $t = \sin x = x - \dfrac{x^3}{6} + o(x^3)$. Since $t \to 0$ and $t$ has the same order as $x$, the expansion of the exponential gives $e^{t} = 1 + t + \dfrac{t^2}{2} + \dfrac{t^3}{6} + o(x^3)$. Up to order $3$ we have $t^2 = x^2 + o(x^3)$ and $t^3 = x^3 + o(x^3)$, so
$$
e^{\sin x} = 1 + x - \frac{x^3}{6} + \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^3}{6} + o(x^3) = 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2} + o(x^3) .
$$
The two third-degree terms cancel, therefore the polynomial of order $3$ is $1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2}$.
