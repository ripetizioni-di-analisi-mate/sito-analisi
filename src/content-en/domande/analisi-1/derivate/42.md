The functions $e^{x^2}$ and $\cos x$ both equal $1$ at $x = 0$. What value does the ratio $\dfrac{e^{x^2} - \cos x}{x^2}$ tend to as $x \to 0$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The numerator and the denominator tend to $0$. Substituting $x^2$ for $x$ in the expansion of the exponential gives $e^{x^2} = 1 + x^2 + o(x^2)$; moreover $\cos x = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2} + o(x^2)$. The numerator is therefore
$$
e^{x^2} - \cos x = x^2 + \frac{x^2}{2} + o(x^2) = \frac{3}{2}\,x^2 + o(x^2) .
$$
Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
