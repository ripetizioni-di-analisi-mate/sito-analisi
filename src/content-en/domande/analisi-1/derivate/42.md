What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x - 1 + \dfrac{x^2}{2}}{x^4}$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{24}$
- $\dfrac{1}{24}$
- $\dfrac{1}{12}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The denominator is $x^4$, so the cosine is expanded up to order $4$ with the **Maclaurin expansion** $\cos x = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{x^4}{24} + o(x^5)$. Substituting, the numerator becomes
$$
1 - \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^4}{24} + o(x^5) - 1 + \frac{x^2}{2} = \frac{x^4}{24} + o(x^4) ,
$$
because the terms of degree $0$ and $2$ cancel and $o(x^5) = o(x^4)$. Since $\dfrac{o(x^4)}{x^4} \to 0$, the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{24}$.
