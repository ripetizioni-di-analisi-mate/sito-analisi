The function $f(x) = x \ln x$ is defined for $x > 0$. What is the largest interval on which $f$ is strictly increasing?

- $\left( 0, \dfrac{1}{e} \right]$
- $[e, +\infty)$
- $[1, +\infty)$
- $\left[ \dfrac{1}{e}, +\infty \right)$

## Explanation

By the product rule $f'(x) = \ln x + x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} = \ln x + 1$. We have $f'(x) > 0$ if and only if $\ln x > -1$, that is, $x > \dfrac{1}{e}$. Hence, by the theorem on **monotonicity and sign of the derivative**, $f$ is strictly increasing on $\left( \dfrac{1}{e}, +\infty \right)$; since $f$ is continuous at $\dfrac{1}{e}$, strict monotonicity also holds on $\left[ \dfrac{1}{e}, +\infty \right)$. On $\left( 0, \dfrac{1}{e} \right]$ the derivative is negative, except at the right endpoint, and $f$ is strictly decreasing. Therefore the largest interval on which $f$ is strictly increasing is $\left[ \dfrac{1}{e}, +\infty \right)$, which contains $[1, +\infty)$ and $[e, +\infty)$.
