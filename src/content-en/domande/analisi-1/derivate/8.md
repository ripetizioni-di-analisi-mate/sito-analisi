For the function $f(x) = \bigl| x^2 - 4 \bigr|$, what are the points of non-differentiability and of what type are they?

- $x = -2$ and $x = 2$, cusps
- $x = 0$, $x = -2$ and $x = 2$, corner points
- Only $x = 2$, corner point
- $x = -2$ and $x = 2$, corner points

## Explanation

Away from the points where $x^2 - 4 = 0$, that is, $x = \pm 2$, the function coincides with $x^2 - 4$ or with $4 - x^2$, so it is differentiable; in particular near $0$ we have $f(x) = 4 - x^2$. For $x > 2$ we have $f'(x) = 2x$, which tends to $4$ as $x \to 2^{+}$, while for $-2 < x < 2$ we have $f'(x) = -2x$, which tends to $-4$ as $x \to 2^{-}$. Since $f$ is continuous, by the limit-of-the-derivative theorem the one-sided derivatives at $2$ are $f'_{-}(2) = -4$ and $f'_{+}(2) = 4$. They are finite and different, so $x = 2$ is a **corner point**. In the same way at $x = -2$ we obtain $f'_{-}(-2) = -4$ and $f'_{+}(-2) = 4$, therefore $x = -2$ is also a corner point.
