The function $f$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ and even, that is, $f(-x) = f(x)$ for every $x$. Which statement about the derivative $f'$ is true?

- $f'$ is odd
- $f'$ is even
- $f'(x) > 0$ for every $x > 0$
- $f'$ is odd only if $f$ is a polynomial

## Explanation

We differentiate both sides of the identity $f(-x) = f(x)$. By the **derivative of a composite function** the derivative of $f(-x)$ is $f'(-x) \cdot (-1)$, so $-f'(-x) = f'(x)$ for every $x$, that is, $f'(-x) = -f'(x)$. Therefore $f'$ is **odd** for every differentiable even function. The even function $\cos x$ has derivative $-\sin x$, which is negative on $(0, \pi)$; hence the inequality $f'(x) > 0$ for every $x > 0$ does not hold in general.
