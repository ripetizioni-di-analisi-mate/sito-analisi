At which point of the graph of $f(x) = x^2 - 3x$ is the tangent line parallel to the line $y = x + 5$?

- $(1, -2)$
- $(2, -2)$
- $\left( \dfrac{3}{2}, -\dfrac{9}{4} \right)$
- $(2, 7)$

## Explanation

Two lines are parallel if they have the same slope, so we look for the point where $f'(x) = 1$. Since $f'(x) = 2x - 3$, the equation $2x - 3 = 1$ gives $x = 2$. The ordinate of the point of the graph is $f(2) = 4 - 6 = -2$, therefore the required point is $(2, -2)$. The tangent line at this point is $y = -2 + (x - 2) = x - 4$.
