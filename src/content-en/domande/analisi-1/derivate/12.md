- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $4$
- $2$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since the derivative of $e^{2x}$ is $2e^{2x}$, the ratio of the derivatives is $\dfrac{2e^{2x} - 2}{2x}$, again in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$; the derivatives of the denominators, $2x$ and $2$, are different from $0$ for $x \neq 0$. Differentiating the numerator and the denominator again we obtain $\dfrac{4e^{2x}}{2} = 2e^{2x}$, which tends to $2$. Since this limit exists, by **L'Hôpital's rule** the limit of $\dfrac{2e^{2x} - 2}{2x}$ equals $2$, and by the same rule the given limit also equals $2$.
