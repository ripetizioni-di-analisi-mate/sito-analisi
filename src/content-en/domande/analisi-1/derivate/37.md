What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x - x}{x^3}$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ numerator and denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The derivative of the numerator is $1 + \tan^2 x - 1 = \tan^2 x$, and that of the denominator is $3x^2$, which is nonzero for $x \neq 0$. The ratio of the derivatives is
$$
\frac{\tan^2 x}{3x^2} = \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{\tan x}{x} \right)^2 ,
$$
which tends to $\dfrac{1}{3}$ by the standard limit $\dfrac{\tan x}{x} \to 1$. Since this limit exists, by **L'Hôpital's rule** the given limit also equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
