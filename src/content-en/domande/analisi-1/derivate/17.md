For the function $f(x) = |x - 1|\,(x + 2)$, what are the one-sided derivatives at the point $x = 1$?

- $f'_{-}(1) = -1$ and $f'_{+}(1) = 1$
- $f'_{-}(1) = 0$ and $f'_{+}(1) = 0$
- $f'_{-}(1) = 3$ and $f'_{+}(1) = -3$
- $f'_{-}(1) = -3$ and $f'_{+}(1) = 3$

## Explanation

Since $f(1) = 0$, the difference quotient at $1$ is
$$
\frac{f(1 + h) - f(1)}{h} = \frac{|h|\,(3 + h)}{h} .
$$
For $h > 0$ we have $|h| = h$, so the quotient equals $3 + h$ and tends to $3$. For $h < 0$ we have $|h| = -h$, so the quotient equals $-(3 + h)$ and tends to $-3$. Therefore $f'_{-}(1) = -3$ and $f'_{+}(1) = 3$; the one-sided derivatives are finite and different, and $x = 1$ is a **corner point**.
