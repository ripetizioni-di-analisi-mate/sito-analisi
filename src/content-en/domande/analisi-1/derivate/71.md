Which statement about the function $f(x) = x + \sin x$ is true?

- $f$ is not monotonic on $\mathbb{R}$, because $f'$ vanishes infinitely many times
- $f$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$
- $f$ is increasing on $\mathbb{R}$, but not strictly
- $f$ is decreasing on $[\pi, 2\pi]$

## Explanation

We have $f'(x) = 1 + \cos x \geq 0$ for every $x$, and the derivative vanishes only at the isolated points $x = \pi + 2k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Let $x_1 < x_2$ be two points of the same interval $[\pi + 2k\pi, 3\pi + 2k\pi]$. By the **mean value theorem** there exists $c \in (x_1, x_2)$ with $f(x_2) - f(x_1) = f'(c)\,(x_2 - x_1)$, and $f'(c) > 0$ because $c$ is interior to the interval; hence $f(x_1) < f(x_2)$. Therefore $f$ is strictly increasing on each of these intervals, which cover $\mathbb{R}$ and pairwise share an endpoint, and consequently $f$ is **strictly increasing** on $\mathbb{R}$.
