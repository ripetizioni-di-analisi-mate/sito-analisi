The graph is that of the derivative $f'$ of a function $f$ differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$; the derivative vanishes at $x = -1$ and at $x = 2$. Which point is a local minimum point of $f$?

::grafico{f="x^2-x-2" xmin=-3 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=5 punti="-1,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" legenda="y = f'(x)" titolo="Graph of the derivative $f'$"}

- $x = -1$
- $x = 2$
- $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x = -2$

## Explanation

From the graph, $f'$ is positive for $x < -1$ and for $x > 2$ and is negative for $-1 < x < 2$. By the relation between **monotonicity and sign of the derivative**, $f$ is decreasing on $[-1, 2]$ and increasing on $[2, +\infty)$, so $x = 2$ is a local minimum point. The point $x = -1$ is instead a local maximum point, because $f$ is increasing before $-1$ and decreasing after it. The point $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is the minimum point of $f'$ and is not an extremum of $f$.
