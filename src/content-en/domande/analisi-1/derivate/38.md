What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} (\cos x)^{\frac{1}{x^2}}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$
- $1$
- $\sqrt{e}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $1^{\infty}$, so we write $(\cos x)^{\frac{1}{x^2}} = e^{\frac{\ln \cos x}{x^2}}$. The exponent is in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$ and the derivative of the denominator, $2x$, is nonzero for $x \neq 0$. The ratio of the derivatives is
$$
\frac{-\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}}{2x} = -\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\tan x}{x} ,
$$
which tends to $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, so by **L'Hôpital's rule** the exponent tends to $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since the exponential function is continuous, the limit equals $e^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$.
