What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{4 + h} - 2}{h}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{8}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $0$

## Explanation

With $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ and $x_0 = 4$ we have $f(4) = 2$, so the expression is the difference quotient of $f$ at $4$ with increment $h$. The limit is therefore the derivative $f'(4)$. Since $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$ for $x > 0$, we obtain $f'(4) = \dfrac{1}{2 \cdot 2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. The same value is obtained by rationalizing, since multiplying numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{4 + h} + 2$ turns the quotient into $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4 + h} + 2}$, which tends to $\dfrac{1}{4}$.
