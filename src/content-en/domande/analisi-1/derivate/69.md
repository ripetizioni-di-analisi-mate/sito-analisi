How many real solutions does the equation $x^5 + 2x + 1 = 0$ have?

- $0$
- $3$
- $5$
- $1$

## Explanation

Let $f(x) = x^5 + 2x + 1$. We have $f'(x) = 5x^4 + 2 > 0$ for every $x$, so $f$ is **strictly increasing** on $\mathbb{R}$ and vanishes at most at one point. Moreover $f(-1) = -2 < 0$ and $f(0) = 1 > 0$, therefore by the intermediate zero theorem $f$ vanishes at least at one point of $(-1, 0)$. The equation therefore has exactly one real solution.
