Which of the following functions is continuous at $x = 0$ but not differentiable at $x = 0$?

- $x\,|x|$
- $\cos |x|$
- $|x|^2 + x$
- $e^{|x|}$

## Explanation

For $h > 0$ the difference quotient of $e^{|x|}$ at $0$ is $\dfrac{e^{h} - 1}{h}$, which tends to $1$; for $h < 0$ it is $\dfrac{e^{-h} - 1}{h}$, which tends to $-1$. The one-sided derivatives are different, so $e^{|x|}$ has a **corner point** at $0$. The other three functions are differentiable at $0$. Since the cosine is even and $|x|^2 = x^2$, we have $\cos |x| = \cos x$ and $|x|^2 + x = x^2 + x$ for every $x$. The difference quotient of $x\,|x|$ at $0$ is $\dfrac{h\,|h|}{h} = |h|$, which tends to $0$, so $x\,|x|$ is differentiable at $0$ with zero derivative.
