The functions $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = x^3$ satisfy the hypotheses of Cauchy's mean value theorem on the interval $[0, 1]$. Which point $c \in (0, 1)$ satisfies $\dfrac{f'(c)}{g'(c)} = \dfrac{f(1) - f(0)}{g(1) - g(0)}$?

- $c = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $c = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$
- $c = \dfrac{3}{4}$
- $c = \dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

Since $f(1) - f(0) = 1$ and $g(1) - g(0) = 1$, the right-hand side equals $1$. For $c \neq 0$ the left-hand side is $\dfrac{2c}{3c^2} = \dfrac{2}{3c}$. The equation $\dfrac{2}{3c} = 1$ has the unique solution $c = \dfrac{2}{3}$, which belongs to $(0, 1)$. The value $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ is instead the point given by the **mean value theorem** for $g$ alone, where $3c^2 = 1$.
