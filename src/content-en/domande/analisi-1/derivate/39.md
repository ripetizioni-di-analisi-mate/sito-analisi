What is the value of the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{x} + x}{\cos x + x}$?

- $2$
- $1$
- $0$
- The limit does not exist

## Explanation

Numerator and denominator are continuous functions at $0$ and their values are $e^{0} + 0 = 1$ and $\cos 0 + 0 = 1$. The denominator is nonzero, so the limit is computed by substitution and equals $\dfrac{1}{1} = 1$. The quotient is *not* an indeterminate form, therefore L'Hôpital's rule does not apply. Differentiating numerator and denominator anyway would give $\dfrac{e^{x} + 1}{1 - \sin x} \to 2$, a value different from the limit.
