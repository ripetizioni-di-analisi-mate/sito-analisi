For the function $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3$, what are the local extrema?

- A minimum at $x = 3$ and a maximum at $x = 0$
- Only a minimum, at $x = 3$
- A minimum at $x = 0$ and a minimum at $x = 3$
- A maximum at $x = 3$ and a minimum at $x = 0$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x^2 = 4x^2 (x - 3)$, so the stationary points are $x = 0$ and $x = 3$. The factor $4x^2$ is positive for $x \neq 0$, therefore for $x \neq 0$ the sign of $f'$ coincides with the sign of $x - 3$. It follows that $f' < 0$ on $(-\infty, 0)$ and on $(0, 3)$, while $f' > 0$ on $(3, +\infty)$. The function is therefore strictly decreasing on $(-\infty, 3]$ and strictly increasing on $[3, +\infty)$, and $x = 3$ is a **minimum** point, with $f(3) = 81 - 108 = -27$. The point $x = 0$ is stationary but is *not* an extremum, because $f$ is decreasing both to the left and to the right of $0$.
