As $x \to 1$ the function $\dfrac{\ln x}{x^2 - 1}$ takes the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. What is its limit?

- $1$
- $2$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

As $x \to 1$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The two functions are differentiable and the derivative of the denominator, $2x$, is different from $0$ near $1$. The ratio of the derivatives is $\dfrac{1}{x} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2x} = \dfrac{1}{2x^2}$, which tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore, by **L'Hôpital's rule**, the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
