The Maclaurin polynomial of order $2$ of $f(x) = \sqrt{1 + 2x}$ is $T_2(x) = 1 + x - \dfrac{x^2}{2}$. Which bound on the error $\left| f\!\left( \dfrac{1}{10} \right) - T_2\!\left( \dfrac{1}{10} \right) \right|$ is obtained with the Lagrange remainder?

- $\dfrac{3}{2000}$
- $\dfrac{1}{16000}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2000}$
- $\dfrac{1}{200}$

## Explanation

By **Taylor's formula with Lagrange remainder** there exists $c$ strictly between $0$ and $\dfrac{1}{10}$ such that the error equals $\dfrac{|f'''(c)|}{3!} \cdot \left( \dfrac{1}{10} \right)^{3}$. Differentiating $(1 + 2x)^{\frac{1}{2}}$ three times, with the factor $2$ from the derivative of the argument at each step, we obtain $f'''(x) = 3\,(1 + 2x)^{-\frac{5}{2}}$. For $x \geq 0$ we have $1 + 2x \geq 1$, so $|f'''(c)| \leq 3$. Therefore
$$
\left| f\!\left( \frac{1}{10} \right) - T_2\!\left( \frac{1}{10} \right) \right| \leq \frac{3}{3!} \cdot \frac{1}{1000} = \frac{1}{2000} .
$$
