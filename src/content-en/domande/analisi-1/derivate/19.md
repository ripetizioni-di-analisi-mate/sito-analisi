For the function $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{(x - 1)^2}$, what type of point is $x = 1$?

- A corner point
- An inflection point with vertical tangent
- A point where $f$ is differentiable, with $f'(1) = 0$
- A cusp

## Explanation

The function is continuous at $1$ and $f(1) = 0$, so the difference quotient at $1$ is
$$
\frac{\sqrt[3]{h^2}}{h} = \frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{h}} .
$$
As $h \to 0^{+}$ the quotient tends to $+\infty$, while as $h \to 0^{-}$ it tends to $-\infty$. The one-sided limits of the difference quotient are infinite and of opposite sign, so $x = 1$ is a **cusp**.
