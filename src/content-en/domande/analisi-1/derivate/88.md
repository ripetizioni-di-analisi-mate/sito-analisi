The quantity $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is subtracted from $\dfrac{1}{e^{x} - 1}$. What value does the difference tend to as $x \to 0$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\infty - \infty$. Bringing to a common denominator we have
$$
\frac{1}{e^{x} - 1} - \frac{1}{x} = \frac{x - e^{x} + 1}{x\,(e^{x} - 1)} .
$$
By the expansion $e^{x} = 1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2} + o(x^2)$ the numerator is $-\dfrac{x^2}{2} + o(x^2)$ and the denominator is $x\,\bigl( x + o(x) \bigr) = x^2 + o(x^2)$. Therefore the limit equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
