What value does the expression $x - x^2 \ln\!\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{x} \right)$ tend to as $x \to +\infty$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

With the substitution $t = \dfrac{1}{x}$ we have $t \to 0^{+}$ and the expression becomes
$$
\frac{1}{t} - \frac{\ln(1 + t)}{t^2} = \frac{t - \ln(1 + t)}{t^2} .
$$
By the expansion $\ln(1 + t) = t - \dfrac{t^2}{2} + o(t^2)$ the numerator is $\dfrac{t^2}{2} + o(t^2)$. Therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
