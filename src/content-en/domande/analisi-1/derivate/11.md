- $0$
- $e$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$

## Explanation

The limit takes the indeterminate form $0^{0}$, so we write $x^{x} = e^{x \ln x}$. The product $x \ln x$ is in the form $0 \cdot \infty$ and is rewritten as a quotient
$$
\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} x \ln x = \lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \frac{\ln x}{\frac{1}{x}} ,
$$
which is in the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. The denominator $\dfrac{1}{x}$ has derivative $-\dfrac{1}{x^2} \neq 0$. The ratio of the derivatives is $\dfrac{1}{x} \cdot \left( -x^2 \right) = -x$, which tends to $0$, so by **L'Hôpital's rule** $x \ln x \to 0$. Since the exponential function is continuous, $x^{x} = e^{x \ln x} \to e^{0} = 1$.
