What are the absolute maximum and minimum of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2$ on the interval $[-2, 4]$?

- Maximum $0$, minimum $-4$
- Maximum $16$, minimum $-20$
- Maximum $16$, minimum $-4$
- Maximum $0$, minimum $-20$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on the closed and bounded interval $[-2, 4]$, so by the Weierstrass theorem it has an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum. Since $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x = 3x(x - 2)$, the stationary points are $x = 0$ and $x = 2$; the candidates are these two points and the endpoints $-2$ and $4$. We have
$$
f(-2) = -20 , \qquad f(0) = 0 , \qquad f(2) = -4 , \qquad f(4) = 16 .
$$
Therefore the absolute **maximum** is $16$, at $x = 4$, and the absolute **minimum** is $-20$, at $x = -2$.
