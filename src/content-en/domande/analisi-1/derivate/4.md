- $f'(x) = \dfrac{-x^2 + 2x + 1}{(x - 1)^2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{3x^2 - 2x + 1}{(x - 1)^2}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 1}{x - 1}$
- $f'(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 1}{(x - 1)^2}$

## Explanation

With $N(x) = x^2 + 1$ and $D(x) = x - 1$ we have $N'(x) = 2x$ and $D'(x) = 1$. By the **quotient rule**
$$
f'(x) = \frac{N'(x)\,D(x) - N(x)\,D'(x)}{D(x)^2} = \frac{2x(x - 1) - (x^2 + 1)}{(x - 1)^2} .
$$
The numerator equals $2x^2 - 2x - x^2 - 1 = x^2 - 2x - 1$, so $f'(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 1}{(x - 1)^2}$ for $x \neq 1$. Swapping the two products $N'D$ and $ND'$ would give the opposite of the derivative.
