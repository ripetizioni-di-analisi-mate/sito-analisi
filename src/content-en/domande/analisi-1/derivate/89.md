The function $x^{\frac{1}{x}}$ is defined for $x > 0$. What is its limit as $x \to +\infty$?

- $0$
- $e$
- $+\infty$
- $1$

## Explanation

The limit is in the indeterminate form $\infty^{0}$. We write $x^{\frac{1}{x}} = e^{\frac{\ln x}{x}}$. The exponent $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}$ is in the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$ and the ratio of the derivatives $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $0$, so by **L'Hôpital's rule** the exponent tends to $0$. By the continuity of the exponential the limit equals $e^{0} = 1$.
