For $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ we have $\cos x > 0$, so $f(x) = \ln(\cos x)$ is defined. Which function is $f'(x)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{\cos x}$
- $\tan x$
- $-\tan x$
- $-\sin x\,\ln(\cos x)$

## Explanation

By the **derivative of a composite function**, with outer function $\ln t$ and argument $t = \cos x$, we have $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{\cos x} \cdot (\cos x)'$. Since $(\cos x)' = -\sin x$, we obtain $f'(x) = -\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = -\tan x$.
