Let $f$ be differentiable on $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$, with $f'(x) = 0$ for every $x \neq 0$ and $f(1) = 2$. Which statement is true?

- $f(-1) = 2$, because $f$ is constant
- $f(-1) = -2$, because $f$ is odd
- The value $f(-1)$ is not determined by the data
- $f(-1) = 0$

## Explanation

The theorem on the **zero derivative on an interval** requires the domain to be an interval, and $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ is the union of the two intervals $(-\infty, 0)$ and $(0, +\infty)$. The theorem applies separately to each of them, so $f$ is constant on $(0, +\infty)$, where it equals $2$, and it is constant on $(-\infty, 0)$, with a value that the data do not fix. For example, the function equal to $2$ for $x > 0$ and to $5$ for $x < 0$ satisfies all the hypotheses and has $f(-1) = 5$. Therefore the value $f(-1)$ is **not determined**.
