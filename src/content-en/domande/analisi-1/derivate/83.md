The number $e$ is approximated by the value at $x = 1$ of the Maclaurin polynomial of order $n$ of $e^{x}$, that is, by $T_n(1) = \displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{1}{k!}$. Using the Lagrange remainder and the estimate $e^{c} < 3$ for $0 < c < 1$, what is the smallest $n$ that guarantees an error less than $\dfrac{1}{1000}$?

- $n = 5$
- $n = 6$
- $n = 7$
- $n = 4$

## Explanation

By Taylor's formula with **Lagrange remainder** of order $n$ centered at $0$ and evaluated at $x = 1$, there exists $c \in (0, 1)$ with
$$
e - T_n(1) = \frac{e^{c}}{(n + 1)!} .
$$
Since $e^{c} < 3$, the error is less than $\dfrac{3}{(n + 1)!}$, and this quantity is less than $\dfrac{1}{1000}$ when $(n + 1)! > 3000$. We have $6! = 720$ and $7! = 5040$, so the condition holds for $n + 1 = 7$ and does not hold for $n + 1 = 6$. Therefore the smallest $n$ is $6$.
