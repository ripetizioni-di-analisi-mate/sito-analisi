For which $x > 0$ does the inequality $\ln x \leq x - 1$ hold?

- Only for $x \geq 1$
- Only for $0 < x \leq 1$
- Only for $x = 1$
- For every $x > 0$

## Explanation

Let $g(x) = x - 1 - \ln x$ for $x > 0$. We have $g'(x) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{x - 1}{x}$, negative for $0 < x < 1$ and positive for $x > 1$, so $g$ has its absolute minimum at $x = 1$. Since $g(1) = 0$, we have $g(x) \geq 0$ for every $x > 0$, that is, $\ln x \leq x - 1$. Geometrically, the line $y = x - 1$ is the tangent to the graph of $\ln x$ at the point $(1, 0)$ and the graph lies below it.
