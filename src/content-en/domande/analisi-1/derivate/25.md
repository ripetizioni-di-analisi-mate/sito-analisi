The function $f(x) = x^3 + x$ is invertible on $\mathbb{R}$. What is the derivative of the inverse function at the point $y_0 = 2$?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{13}$
- $13$

## Explanation

We have $f(1) = 1 + 1 = 2$, so $y_0 = 2$ corresponds to $x_0 = 1$. Since $f'(x) = 3x^2 + 1 > 0$ for every $x$, $f$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$, so it has a continuous inverse; moreover $f'(1) = 4 \neq 0$. The hypotheses of the theorem on the **derivative of the inverse function** are therefore satisfied, and
$$
(f^{-1})'(2) = \frac{1}{f'(1)} = \frac{1}{4} .
$$
The derivative of $f$ is evaluated at $x_0 = 1$, the point where $f$ equals $2$.
