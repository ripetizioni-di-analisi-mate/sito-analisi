Let $f(x) = \ln(1 + x^2)$. What is the value of the fourth derivative $f^{(4)}(0)$?

- $-12$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $12$
- $-6$

## Explanation

Substituting $t = x^2$ in the expansion $\ln(1 + t) = t - \dfrac{t^2}{2} + o(t^2)$ we obtain, as $x \to 0$,
$$
\ln(1 + x^2) = x^2 - \frac{x^4}{2} + o(x^4) .
$$
By Taylor's formula the coefficient of $x^4$ in the Maclaurin polynomial is $\dfrac{f^{(4)}(0)}{4!}$, so $\dfrac{f^{(4)}(0)}{24} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore $f^{(4)}(0) = -12$.
