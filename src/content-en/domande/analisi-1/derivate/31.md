The function $f(x) = x^2$, considered on the interval $[1, 3]$, has its absolute maximum at $x = 3$ and $f'(3) = 6$. Which statement is true?

- Fermat's theorem does not apply, because $f$ is not differentiable at $3$
- $x = 3$ is not a maximum point, because $f'(3) \neq 0$
- Fermat's theorem does not apply at $x = 3$, because $3$ is not an interior point of the interval
- The absolute maximum is at $x = 0$, where $f'(0) = 0$

## Explanation

Fermat's theorem requires the extremum point to be *interior* to the domain and $f$ to be differentiable at that point. The point $x = 3$ is an endpoint of the interval $[1, 3]$, so this hypothesis is not satisfied and the theorem **does not apply**. Since $f'(x) = 2x > 0$ on $(1, 3)$, $f$ is increasing on $[1, 3]$ and the absolute maximum is $f(3) = 9$, even though $f'(3) \neq 0$. For this reason the endpoints of the interval are among the candidates in the search for the maximum and the minimum.
