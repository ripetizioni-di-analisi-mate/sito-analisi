What is the derivative of $f(x) = \ln \dfrac{1 + x}{1 - x}$ for $-1 < x < 1$?

- $\dfrac{1 - x}{1 + x}$
- $\dfrac{2}{(1 - x)^2}$
- $-\dfrac{2x}{1 - x^2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{1 - x^2}$

## Explanation

For $-1 < x < 1$ the expressions $1 + x$ and $1 - x$ are positive, so by the properties of the logarithm $f(x) = \ln(1 + x) - \ln(1 - x)$. By the chain rule the derivative of $\ln(1 - x)$ is $\dfrac{1}{1 - x} \cdot (-1)$. Therefore
$$
f'(x) = \frac{1}{1 + x} + \frac{1}{1 - x} = \frac{(1 - x) + (1 + x)}{1 - x^2} = \frac{2}{1 - x^2} .
$$
Without the factor $-1$ from the inner derivative one would obtain $\dfrac{1}{1 + x} - \dfrac{1}{1 - x} = -\dfrac{2x}{1 - x^2}$.
