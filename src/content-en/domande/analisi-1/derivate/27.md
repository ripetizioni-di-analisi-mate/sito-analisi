For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ we have $x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1} > 0$, so $f(x) = \ln\bigl( x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1} \bigr)$ is defined on $\mathbb{R}$. What is the simplified expression of $f'(x)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$
- $\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$

## Explanation

By the **derivative of a composite function**, the derivative of $\ln g(x)$ is $\dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}$, with $g(x) = x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}$. The derivative of $\sqrt{x^2 + 1}$ is $\dfrac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$, so
$$
g'(x) = 1 + \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} = \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 1} + x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} .
$$
The numerator of $g'(x)$ coincides with $g(x)$, therefore $f'(x) = \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$.
