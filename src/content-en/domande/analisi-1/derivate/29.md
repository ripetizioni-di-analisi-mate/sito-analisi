The function $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ satisfies the hypotheses of Rolle's theorem on the interval $\left[ -\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3} \right]$. Which points $c$ satisfy the conclusion of the theorem?

- Only $c = 1$
- Only $c = 0$
- $c = -\sqrt{3}$ and $c = \sqrt{3}$
- $c = -1$ and $c = 1$

## Explanation

We have $f\bigl( \sqrt{3} \bigr) = 3\sqrt{3} - 3\sqrt{3} = 0$ and $f\bigl( -\sqrt{3} \bigr) = -3\sqrt{3} + 3\sqrt{3} = 0$, so the values at the endpoints coincide. The conclusion concerns the points $c \in \left( -\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3} \right)$ with $f'(c) = 0$. Since $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3(x - 1)(x + 1)$, the derivative vanishes at $c = -1$ and at $c = 1$. Both points belong to the open interval, because $1 < \sqrt{3}$.
