The side of a square is $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ long and increases by $0.1\,\mathrm{cm}$. By how much does the area increase, approximating the function $A(\ell) = \ell^2$ by its tangent line at $\ell_0 = 10$?

- $0.01\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $1\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{cm^2}$

## Explanation

The tangent line to the graph of $A$ at $\ell_0 = 10$ is $y = A(10) + A'(10)\,(\ell - 10)$. Since $A'(\ell) = 2\ell$, we have $A'(10) = 20$. With this approximation the increase of the area is $A'(10) \cdot 0.1 = 20 \cdot 0.1 = 2\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The exact increase is $10.1^2 - 100 = 2.01\,\mathrm{cm^2}$; the difference of $0.01\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ is the term $(0.1)^2$, which the tangent line neglects.
