The functions $\ln x$ and $\sqrt{x}$ both tend to $+\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$. What value does their ratio $\dfrac{\ln x}{\sqrt{x}}$ tend to?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

As $x \to +\infty$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $+\infty$, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. The ratio of the derivatives is $\dfrac{1}{x} \cdot 2\sqrt{x} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{x}}$, which tends to $0$. Therefore, by **L'Hôpital's rule**, the limit equals $0$. The result agrees with the hierarchy of growth rates: the logarithm grows more slowly than every power of $x$ with positive exponent.
