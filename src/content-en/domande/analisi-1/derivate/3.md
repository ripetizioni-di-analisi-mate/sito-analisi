- $f'(x) = 3x^2 \sin\bigl( x^3 \bigr)$
- $f'(x) = -\sin\bigl( x^3 \bigr)$
- $f'(x) = -3x^2 \sin\bigl( x^3 \bigr)$
- $f'(x) = -\sin\bigl( 3x^2 \bigr)$

## Explanation

The function is a composite function, with the cosine as outer function and argument $g(x) = x^3$. By the **chain rule** we differentiate the cosine leaving the argument unchanged, and we obtain $-\sin\bigl( x^3 \bigr)$; then we multiply by the derivative of the argument, $g'(x) = 3x^2$. Hence
$$
f'(x) = -\sin\bigl( x^3 \bigr) \cdot 3x^2 = -3x^2 \sin\bigl( x^3 \bigr) .
$$
The minus sign comes from the derivative of the cosine, which is $-\sin x$.
