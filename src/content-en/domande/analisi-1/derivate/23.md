What is the derivative of $f(x) = x^{x}$ for $x > 0$?

- $x \cdot x^{x - 1}$
- $x^{x} \ln x$
- $\ln x + 1$
- $x^{x}\,(\ln x + 1)$

## Explanation

The rule $\left( x^{\alpha} \right)' = \alpha\,x^{\alpha - 1}$ holds for constant $\alpha$; here the exponent also depends on $x$, so we write $x^{x} = e^{x \ln x}$. By the chain rule, the derivative is $e^{x \ln x}$ multiplied by the derivative of the exponent $x \ln x$, which by the product rule equals $\ln x + x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} = \ln x + 1$. Therefore
$$
f'(x) = e^{x \ln x}\,(\ln x + 1) = x^{x}\,(\ln x + 1) .
$$
