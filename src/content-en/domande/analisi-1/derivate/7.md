- $a = 0$
- $a = -1$
- $a = 1$
- Every $a \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

Both expressions equal $0$ at $x = 0$, so $f$ is continuous at $0$ for every value of $a$. For $x < 0$ we have $f'(x) = \cos x$, which tends to $1$ as $x \to 0^{-}$. For $x > 0$ we have $f'(x) = 2x + a$, which tends to $a$ as $x \to 0^{+}$. Since $f$ is continuous at $0$, by the **limit-of-the-derivative theorem** the one-sided derivatives are $f'_{-}(0) = 1$ and $f'_{+}(0) = a$. The function is differentiable at $0$ if and only if the one-sided derivatives coincide, therefore $a = 1$.
