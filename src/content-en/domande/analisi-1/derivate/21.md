For the function
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^2 + 1 & \text{if } x \leq 0 \\[4pt]
x^2 & \text{if } x > 0
\end{cases}
$$
which statement about the point $x = 0$ is true?

- $f$ is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 0$
- $f$ is not differentiable at $0$, because it is not continuous at $0$
- $x = 0$ is a corner point
- $f$ is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 1$

## Explanation

We have $f(0) = 1$, while as $x \to 0^{+}$ the function tends to $0$, so $f$ is *not* continuous at $0$. Since a function that is differentiable at a point is continuous at that point, $f$ is **not differentiable** at $0$. The derivatives of the two expressions equal $2x$ and both tend to $0$; however, the limit-of-the-derivative theorem requires continuity at $0$, so it does not apply here. Indeed, for $h > 0$ the difference quotient equals $\dfrac{h^2 - 1}{h}$ and tends to $-\infty$.
