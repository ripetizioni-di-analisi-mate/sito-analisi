Compute
$$
\int x^2 e^{x}\, dx .
$$

- $e^{x} \left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right) + c$
- $e^{x} \left( x^2 - 2x \right) + c$
- $e^{x} \left( x^2 - 2x - 2 \right) + c$
- $e^{x} \left( x^2 - 2x + 2 \right) + c$

## Explanation

We apply **integration by parts**, differentiating the polynomial, with $f(x) = x^2$ and $g'(x) = e^{x}$, so
$$
\int x^2 e^{x}\, dx = x^2 e^{x} - 2 \int x e^{x}\, dx .
$$
The new integral is computed again by parts with $f(x) = x$ and $g'(x) = e^{x}$, and it equals $\displaystyle\int x e^{x}\, dx = x e^{x} - e^{x} + c$. Therefore
$$
\int x^2 e^{x}\, dx = x^2 e^{x} - 2x e^{x} + 2 e^{x} + c = e^{x} \left( x^2 - 2x + 2 \right) + c .
$$
