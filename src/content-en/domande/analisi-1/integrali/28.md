Compute
$$
\int_0^{3} \frac{x}{\sqrt{x + 1}}\, dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- $12$
- $\dfrac{3}{2} - \ln 2$

## Explanation

We apply the **substitution** $t = \sqrt{x + 1}$, that is $x = t^2 - 1$, so $dx = 2t\, dt$. The limits are transformed as well. For $x = 0$ one has $t = 1$, and for $x = 3$ one has $t = 2$. Therefore
$$
\int_0^{3} \frac{x}{\sqrt{x + 1}}\, dx = \int_1^{2} \frac{t^2 - 1}{t} \cdot 2t\, dt = 2 \int_1^{2} \left( t^2 - 1 \right) dt = 2 \left[ \frac{t^3}{3} - t \right]_1^{2} = 2 \left( \frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3} \right) = \frac{8}{3} .
$$
