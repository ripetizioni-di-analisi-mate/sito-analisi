Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x (t - 1)(t - 3)\, dt$. What is the maximum value of $F$ on the interval $[0, 4]$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $4$

## Explanation

By the **fundamental theorem of calculus** $F'(x) = (x - 1)(x - 3)$, which is positive on $[0, 1)$ and on $(3, 4]$ and negative on $(1, 3)$. So $F$ increases on $[0, 1]$, decreases on $[1, 3]$ and increases on $[3, 4]$, and the maximum is the larger of $F(1)$ and $F(4)$. An antiderivative of the integrand is $\dfrac{t^3}{3} - 2t^2 + 3t$, which equals $0$ at $t = 0$, therefore
$$
F(1) = \frac{1}{3} - 2 + 3 = \frac{4}{3}, \qquad F(4) = \frac{64}{3} - 32 + 12 = \frac{4}{3} .
$$
The maximum value is $\dfrac{4}{3}$ and it is attained at the two points $x = 1$ and $x = 4$.
