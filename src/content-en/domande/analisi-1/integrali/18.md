Compute
$$
\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}\, dx .
$$

- $2\sqrt{x^2 + 1} + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \sqrt{x^2 + 1} + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right) + c$
- $\sqrt{x^2 + 1} + c$

## Explanation

The integral is reducible to the immediate integral $\displaystyle\int f'(x)\, [f(x)]^{\alpha}\, dx = \frac{[f(x)]^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1} + c$ with $f(x) = x^2 + 1$ and $\alpha = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. One obtains
$$
\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}\, dx = \frac{1}{2} \int 2x \left( x^2 + 1 \right)^{-\frac{1}{2}} dx = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\left( x^2 + 1 \right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{1}{2}} + c = \sqrt{x^2 + 1} + c .
$$
