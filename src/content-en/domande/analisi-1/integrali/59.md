About a function $f$ continuous on $[0, 4]$ it is known that $\displaystyle\int_0^4 f(t)\, dt = 6$. What is then the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^2 f(2x)\, dx$?

- $6$
- $3$
- $12$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

With the **substitution** $t = 2x$ one has $dt = 2\, dx$, so $dx = \dfrac{dt}{2}$. The limits of integration change as well. For $x = 0$ one has $t = 0$ and for $x = 2$ one has $t = 4$. Therefore
$$
\int_0^2 f(2x)\, dx = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^4 f(t)\, dt = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6 = 3 .
$$
