Exactly one of the following functions is an antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{x^3 + x}$ on the interval $(0, +\infty)$. Which one?

- $\ln x - \ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right)$
- $\ln x - \arctan x$
- $\ln x - \dfrac{1}{2} \ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right)$
- $\ln x + \dfrac{1}{2} \ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right)$

## Explanation

The denominator factors as $x \left( x^2 + 1 \right)$, and the factor $x^2 + 1$ has negative discriminant. The **partial fraction** decomposition is
$$
\frac{1}{x \left( x^2 + 1 \right)} = \frac{A}{x} + \frac{Bx + C}{x^2 + 1}, \qquad 1 = A \left( x^2 + 1 \right) + (Bx + C)\, x .
$$
For $x = 0$ one gets $A = 1$; the coefficient of $x^2$ gives $A + B = 0$, so $B = -1$, and the coefficient of $x$ gives $C = 0$. Therefore the function is $\dfrac{1}{x} - \dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1}$, and since $2x$ is the derivative of $x^2 + 1$, an antiderivative is $\ln x - \dfrac{1}{2} \ln\left( x^2 + 1 \right)$.
