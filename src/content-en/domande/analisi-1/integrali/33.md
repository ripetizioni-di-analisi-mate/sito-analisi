Compute
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{1}{x^2 + 3x + 2}\, dx .
$$

- $\ln \dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\ln \dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\ln 3$
- $\ln \dfrac{4}{3}$

## Explanation

The denominator factors as $(x + 1)(x + 2)$, so it is decomposed into **partial fractions** with $1 = A(x + 2) + B(x + 1)$. For $x = -1$ one obtains $A = 1$ and for $x = -2$ one obtains $B = -1$. On $[0, 1]$ the expressions $x + 1$ and $x + 2$ are positive, therefore
$$
\int_0^{1} \left( \frac{1}{x + 1} - \frac{1}{x + 2} \right) dx = \left[ \ln \frac{x + 1}{x + 2} \right]_0^{1} = \ln \frac{2}{3} - \ln \frac{1}{2} = \ln \frac{4}{3} .
$$
