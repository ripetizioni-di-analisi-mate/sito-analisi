Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^{x} \sin\left( t^2 \right) dt}{x^3} .
$$

- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $3$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator and the denominator tend to $0$, so the limit takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $\sin\left( t^2 \right)$ is continuous, by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** the derivative of the numerator is $\sin\left( x^2 \right)$, while the derivative of the denominator is $3x^2$, which is different from $0$ for $x \neq 0$.

The limit of the ratio of the derivatives exists by the standard limit $\displaystyle\lim_{y \to 0} \frac{\sin y}{y} = 1$ with $y = x^2$, so **L'Hôpital's rule** applies and
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^{x} \sin\left( t^2 \right) dt}{x^3} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin\left( x^2 \right)}{3x^2} = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
