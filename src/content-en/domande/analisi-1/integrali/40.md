Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\, dt$. What are the inflection points of $F$?

- $x = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
- $x = 0$
- $F$ has no inflection points
- $x = -1$ and $x = 1$

## Explanation

The function $e^{-t^2}$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, so by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** $F'(x) = e^{-x^2}$, and differentiating again $F''(x) = -2x\, e^{-x^2}$. Since $e^{-x^2} > 0$, one has $F''(x) > 0$ for $x < 0$ and $F''(x) < 0$ for $x > 0$. Therefore $F$ is convex on $(-\infty, 0]$ and concave on $[0, +\infty)$, and the only **inflection point** is $x = 0$. The points $x = \pm \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ are instead the inflection points of the integrand $e^{-x^2}$.
