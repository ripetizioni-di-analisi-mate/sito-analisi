Let $F(x) = \sin^2 x$ and $G(x) = -\cos^2 x$. Which statement is true?

- $F$ and $G$ are antiderivatives of $\sin 2x$ and $F(x) - G(x) = 1$
- $F$ and $G$ are antiderivatives of $\sin 2x$ and $F(x) = G(x)$
- $F$ is an antiderivative of $\sin 2x$ and $G$ is an antiderivative of $-\sin 2x$
- $F$ is an antiderivative of $2 \sin x$ and $G$ is an antiderivative of $2 \cos x$

## Explanation

By the chain rule, $F'(x) = 2 \sin x \cos x = \sin 2x$ and $G'(x) = -2 \cos x \cdot (-\sin x) = \sin 2x$. So $F$ and $G$ are **antiderivatives of the same function** on the interval $\mathbb{R}$, and therefore they differ by a constant.

The value of the constant follows from the identity $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$, since $F(x) - G(x) = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$ for every $x$.
