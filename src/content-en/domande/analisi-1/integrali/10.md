- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3} \left( e^{\frac{3}{2}} - 1 \right)$

## Explanation

We apply the **substitution** $t = \ln x$, so $dt = \dfrac{1}{x}\, dx$. The limits of integration are transformed as well. For $x = 1$ one has $t = \ln 1 = 0$, and for $x = e$ one has $t = \ln e = 1$. Therefore
$$
\int_1^{e} \frac{\sqrt{\ln x}}{x}\, dx = \int_0^{1} t^{\frac{1}{2}}\, dt = \left[ \frac{2}{3}\, t^{\frac{3}{2}} \right]_0^1 = \frac{2}{3} .
$$
