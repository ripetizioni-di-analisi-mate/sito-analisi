Compute
$$
\int_1^{e} x \ln x\, dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{e^2 - 1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{e^2 + 1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{e^2 + 1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{e^2}{4}$

## Explanation

We apply **integration by parts** with $f(x) = \ln x$ and $g'(x) = x$, so $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$. Since $\ln e = 1$ and $\ln 1 = 0$, the formula for the definite integral gives
$$
\int_1^{e} x \ln x\, dx = \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x \right]_1^{e} - \int_1^{e} \frac{x}{2}\, dx = \frac{e^2}{2} - \left[ \frac{x^2}{4} \right]_1^{e} .
$$
The second term equals $\dfrac{e^2 - 1}{4}$, therefore the integral equals $\dfrac{e^2}{2} - \dfrac{e^2 - 1}{4} = \dfrac{e^2 + 1}{4}$.
