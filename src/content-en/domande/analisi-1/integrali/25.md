Compute
$$
\int x \sin 2x\, dx .
$$

- $-\dfrac{x}{2} \cos 2x + \dfrac{1}{4} \sin 2x + c$
- $-\dfrac{x}{2} \cos 2x - \dfrac{1}{4} \sin 2x + c$
- $-\dfrac{x}{2} \cos 2x + \dfrac{1}{2} \sin 2x + c$
- $\dfrac{x}{2} \cos 2x + \dfrac{1}{4} \sin 2x + c$

## Explanation

We apply **integration by parts**, differentiating the polynomial, with $f(x) = x$ and $g'(x) = \sin 2x$. An antiderivative of $\sin 2x$ is $g(x) = -\dfrac{1}{2} \cos 2x$, so
$$
\int x \sin 2x\, dx = -\frac{x}{2} \cos 2x + \frac{1}{2} \int \cos 2x\, dx .
$$
Since $\displaystyle\int \cos 2x\, dx = \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x + c$, the result is $-\dfrac{x}{2} \cos 2x + \dfrac{1}{4} \sin 2x + c$.
