On the interval $\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ the function $\dfrac{x}{\cos^2 x}$ is continuous. Which expression describes its indefinite integral?

- $x \tan x + \ln|\cos x| + c$
- $x \tan x - \ln|\cos x| + c$
- $x \tan x + c$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} \tan x + c$

## Explanation

Apply **integration by parts** differentiating the polynomial, so $f(x) = x$ and $g'(x) = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$. Then $f'(x) = 1$ and $g(x) = \tan x$, and the formula gives
$$
\int \frac{x}{\cos^2 x}\, dx = x \tan x - \int \tan x\, dx .
$$
The integral of $\tan x = -\dfrac{-\sin x}{\cos x}$ is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\, dx = \ln|f(x)| + c$ with $f(x) = \cos x$, so it equals $-\ln|\cos x| + c$. Therefore
$$
\int \frac{x}{\cos^2 x}\, dx = x \tan x + \ln|\cos x| + c .
$$
