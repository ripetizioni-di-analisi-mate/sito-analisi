What is the value of the integral of the function $\dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$ over the symmetric interval $[-1, 1]$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$

## Explanation

An antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$ is $\arctan x$. By the **Newton–Leibniz formula**
$$
\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1}{1 + x^2}\, dx = \arctan 1 - \arctan(-1) = \frac{\pi}{4} - \left( -\frac{\pi}{4} \right) = \frac{\pi}{2} .
$$
The value at the lower limit is negative, so subtracting it adds $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
