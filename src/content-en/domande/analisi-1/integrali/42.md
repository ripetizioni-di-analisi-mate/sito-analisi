Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x |t - 1|\, dt$. Which statement is true?

- $F$ is not differentiable at the point $x = 1$
- $F$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ and increasing
- $F$ has a relative minimum point at $x = 1$
- $F$ is decreasing on $(-\infty, 1]$

## Explanation

The function $|t - 1|$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, so by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** $F$ is differentiable on all of $\mathbb{R}$ and $F'(x) = |x - 1|$. Since $F'(x) \geq 0$ and it vanishes only at $x = 1$, the function $F$ is **increasing** on $\mathbb{R}$.

At $x = 1$ the derivative vanishes without changing sign, therefore $x = 1$ is not a relative extremum point. The graph of $F'(x) = |x - 1|$ has a corner point at $x = 1$, while $F$ is differentiable at that point as well.
