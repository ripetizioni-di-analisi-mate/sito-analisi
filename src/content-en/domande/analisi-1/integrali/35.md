Which of the following functions is Riemann integrable on the interval $[0, 1]$?

- $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ for $x \in (0, 1]$ and $f(0) = 0$
- $f(x) = 1$ for rational $x$ and $f(x) = 0$ for irrational $x$
- $f(x) = x$ for rational $x$ and $f(x) = 0$ for irrational $x$
- $f(x) = \lfloor 3x \rfloor$, the integer part of $3x$

## Explanation

The function $\lfloor 3x \rfloor$ is increasing on $[0, 1]$, so it is **integrable** because every monotone function on a closed and bounded interval is integrable.

The function $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is not bounded on $(0, 1]$, therefore the definition of the Riemann integral, which requires a bounded function, does not apply. For the other two functions every interval of a partition contains both rational and irrational numbers, so every lower sum equals $0$. Every upper sum, on the other hand, equals $1$ for the Dirichlet function and is at least $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x\, dx = \frac{1}{2}$ for the function equal to $x$ on the rationals and $0$ on the irrationals, therefore neither of the two is integrable.
