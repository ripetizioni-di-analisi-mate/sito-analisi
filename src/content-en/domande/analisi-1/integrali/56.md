For $x \geq 0$ consider the function $e^{\sqrt{x}}$. What is its indefinite integral?

- $2 \sqrt{x}\, e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $2 \left( \sqrt{x} + 1 \right) e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $2 \left( \sqrt{x} - 1 \right) e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$
- $\left( \sqrt{x} - 1 \right) e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$

## Explanation

With the **substitution** $t = \sqrt{x}$, that is $x = t^2$, one has $dx = 2t\, dt$, so the integral becomes $\displaystyle\int 2t\, e^{t}\, dt$. This is computed **by parts** differentiating the polynomial, with $f(t) = 2t$ and $g'(t) = e^t$:
$$
\int 2t\, e^{t}\, dt = 2t\, e^{t} - \int 2 e^{t}\, dt = 2 (t - 1)\, e^{t} + c .
$$
Going back to the variable $x$ with $t = \sqrt{x}$ gives $2 \left( \sqrt{x} - 1 \right) e^{\sqrt{x}} + c$.
