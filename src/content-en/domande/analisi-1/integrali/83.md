The integrand of $G(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x (x - t) \cos t\, dt$ also contains the variable $x$. What is obtained by differentiating $G$?

- $0$
- $1 - \cos x$
- $x \sin x$
- $\sin x$

## Explanation

The integrand also contains $x$, which does not depend on the integration variable $t$, so by **linearity** $x$ is taken out of the integral:
$$
G(x) = x \int_0^x \cos t\, dt - \int_0^x t \cos t\, dt .
$$
By the product rule and by the **fundamental theorem of calculus**
$$
G'(x) = \int_0^x \cos t\, dt + x \cos x - x \cos x = \sin x .
$$
