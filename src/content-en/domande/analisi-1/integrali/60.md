What is the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin x \cos^2 x\, dx$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi^3}{24}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

With the **substitution** $t = \cos x$ one has $dt = -\sin x\, dx$. The limits change. For $x = 0$ one has $t = \cos 0 = 1$, and for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ one has $t = 0$. Therefore
$$
\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin x \cos^2 x\, dx = \int_1^0 \left( -t^2 \right) dt = \int_0^1 t^2\, dt = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
Swapping the limits compensates the minus sign of the differential, and the result is positive, like the integral of a nonnegative function on $\left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$.
