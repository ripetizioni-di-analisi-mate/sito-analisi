For the function $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_{x}^{x^2} e^{-t^2}\, dt$, what is the value of $F'(1)$?

- $\dfrac{3}{e}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{2}{e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$

## Explanation

The function $e^{-t^2}$ is continuous and the limits $x$ and $x^2$ are differentiable, so the formula for the **derivative of an integral function with variable limits** applies:
$$
F'(x) = e^{-(x^2)^2} \cdot 2x - e^{-x^2} \cdot 1 = 2x\, e^{-x^4} - e^{-x^2} .
$$
For $x = 1$ one obtains $F'(1) = 2e^{-1} - e^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{e}$.
