The function $\dfrac{x^3}{x^4 + 1}$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. Which expression describes all its antiderivatives?

- $\ln\left( x^4 + 1 \right) + c$
- $4 \ln\left( x^4 + 1 \right) + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \arctan\left( x^2 \right) + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{4} \ln\left( x^4 + 1 \right) + c$

## Explanation

The derivative of the denominator $x^4 + 1$ is $4x^3$, so one multiplies and divides by $4$. The integral is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\, dx = \ln|f(x)| + c$:
$$
\int \frac{x^3}{x^4 + 1}\, dx = \frac{1}{4} \int \frac{4x^3}{x^4 + 1}\, dx = \frac{1}{4} \ln\left( x^4 + 1 \right) + c .
$$
The absolute value is omitted because $x^4 + 1 > 0$.
