Compute
$$
\int_0^{1} \sqrt{1 - x^2}\, dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $1$

## Explanation

We apply the **inverse substitution** $x = \sin t$ with $t \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, so $dx = \cos t\, dt$. On this interval $\cos t \geq 0$, therefore $\sqrt{1 - \sin^2 t} = \cos t$. The limits $x = 0$ and $x = 1$ correspond to $t = 0$ and $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Using the formula $\cos^2 t = \dfrac{1 + \cos 2t}{2}$ one obtains
$$
\int_0^{1} \sqrt{1 - x^2}\, dx = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \cos^2 t\, dt = \left[ \frac{t}{2} + \frac{\sin 2t}{4} \right]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \frac{\pi}{4} .
$$
