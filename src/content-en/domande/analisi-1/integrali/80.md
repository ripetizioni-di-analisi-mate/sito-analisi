For $x > 0$ the function $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_x^{2x} \frac{1}{t}\, dt$ has both limits of integration variable. Which property does $F$ have?

- $F$ is constant on $(0, +\infty)$ and equals $\ln 2$
- $F$ is increasing on $(0, +\infty)$
- $F(x) = \ln x$ for every $x > 0$
- $F'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{2x}$ for every $x > 0$

## Explanation

The function $\dfrac{1}{t}$ is continuous on $(0, +\infty)$ and the limits $x$ and $2x$ are differentiable and positive for $x > 0$, so the formula for the **derivative of an integral function with variable limits** applies:
$$
F'(x) = \frac{1}{2x} \cdot 2 - \frac{1}{x} \cdot 1 = 0 .
$$
Since the derivative is zero on the interval $(0, +\infty)$, the function $F$ is constant. Its value is computed at $x = 1$, and it equals $F(1) = \displaystyle\int_1^{2} \frac{1}{t}\, dt = \ln 2$.
