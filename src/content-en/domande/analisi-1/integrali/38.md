Let $I = \displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\, dx$. Which inequality is true?

- $0 \leq I \leq \dfrac{1}{e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e} \leq I \leq 1$
- $1 \leq I \leq e$
- $\dfrac{1}{e^2} \leq I \leq \dfrac{1}{e}$

## Explanation

For $x \in [0, 1]$ one has $0 \leq x^2 \leq 1$, so $-1 \leq -x^2 \leq 0$. Since the exponential function is increasing, it follows that $e^{-1} \leq e^{-x^2} \leq 1$ for every $x \in [0, 1]$.

By the **monotonicity** of the integral, we integrate the inequality over $[0, 1]$, which has length $1$, and we obtain $\dfrac{1}{e} \leq I \leq 1$. The antiderivatives of $e^{-x^2}$ cannot be written in terms of elementary functions, therefore the estimate is obtained without computing $I$.
