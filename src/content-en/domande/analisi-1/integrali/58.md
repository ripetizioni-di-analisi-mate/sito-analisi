Which function has derivative $\dfrac{x}{1 + x^4}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2} \arctan\left( x^2 \right)$
- $\arctan\left( x^2 \right)$
- $2 \arctan\left( x^2 \right)$
- $\dfrac{1}{4} \ln\left( 1 + x^4 \right)$

## Explanation

With the **substitution** $t = x^2$ one has $dt = 2x\, dx$, so $x\, dx = \dfrac{dt}{2}$, and moreover $x^4 = t^2$. The integral becomes
$$
\int \frac{x}{1 + x^4}\, dx = \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{1}{1 + t^2}\, dt = \frac{1}{2} \arctan t + c .
$$
Going back to the variable $x$, an antiderivative is $\dfrac{1}{2} \arctan\left( x^2 \right)$.
