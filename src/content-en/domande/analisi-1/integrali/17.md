Let $F$ be an antiderivative of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ on the domain $(-\infty, 0) \cup (0, +\infty)$, with $F(1) = 0$ and $F(-1) = 2$. What is the value of $F(-e)$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $3$
- There is no antiderivative with these two values

## Explanation

The domain of $f$ is the union of two disjoint intervals, so the antiderivative has its own constant on each of them. Hence $F(x) = \ln|x| + c_1$ for $x > 0$ and $F(x) = \ln|x| + c_2$ for $x < 0$, with $c_1$ and $c_2$ **independent**.

The condition $F(1) = 0$ gives $c_1 = 0$ and the condition $F(-1) = 2$ gives $c_2 = 2$, so the two conditions are compatible. Since $-e < 0$, one has $F(-e) = \ln e + 2 = 3$.
