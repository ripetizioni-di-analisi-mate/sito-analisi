What is the value of the following limit?
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} \left( \frac{k}{n} \right)^2
$$

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

Let $P_n$ be the partition of $[0, 1]$ into $n$ intervals of length $\dfrac{1}{n}$ and let $f(x) = x^2$. Since $f$ is increasing on $[0, 1]$, its supremum on $\left[ \dfrac{k-1}{n}, \dfrac{k}{n} \right]$ is $\left( \dfrac{k}{n} \right)^2$, so the given sum is the upper sum $S(P_n)$. The lower sum $s(P_n)$ uses the values at the left endpoints, and the difference is $S(P_n) - s(P_n) = \dfrac{1}{n} \bigl( f(1) - f(0) \bigr) = \dfrac{1}{n}$. Since $s(P_n) \leq \displaystyle\int_0^1 x^2\, dx \leq S(P_n)$ and the difference tends to $0$, one obtains
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} S(P_n) = \int_0^1 x^2\, dx = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
