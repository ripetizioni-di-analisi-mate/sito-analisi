One of the antiderivatives of $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{4+x^2}$ is odd on all of $\mathbb R$. What is its value at $x=2$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, using $\arctan(\dfrac{x}{2})$ as the antiderivative without the outer factor.
- $1$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$

## Explanation

All antiderivatives have the form $F(x)=\dfrac12\arctan(\dfrac{x}{2})+C$. An odd function vanishes at the origin, forcing $C=0$. Thus $F(2)=\dfrac12\arctan 1=\dfrac{\pi}{8}$.
