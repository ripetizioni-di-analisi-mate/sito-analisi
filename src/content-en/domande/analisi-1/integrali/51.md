The denominator of the rational function $\dfrac{1}{2x^2 + 8}$ never vanishes. What is its indefinite integral?

- $\dfrac{1}{2} \arctan \dfrac{x}{2} + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{4} \arctan x + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{8} \arctan \dfrac{x}{2} + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{4} \arctan \dfrac{x}{2} + c$

## Explanation

Factor $8$ out of the denominator:
$$
\frac{1}{2x^2 + 8} = \frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{1}{1 + \left( \frac{x}{2} \right)^2} .
$$
The derivative of $\dfrac{x}{2}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so one multiplies and divides by $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the integral is reducible to the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(x)}{1 + [f(x)]^2}\, dx = \arctan f(x) + c$:
$$
\int \frac{1}{2x^2 + 8}\, dx = \frac{1}{8} \cdot 2 \int \frac{\frac{1}{2}}{1 + \left( \frac{x}{2} \right)^2}\, dx = \frac{1}{4} \arctan \frac{x}{2} + c .
$$
