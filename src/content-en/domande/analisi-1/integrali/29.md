With the substitution $t = x^2$, the integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{2} x \cos\left( x^2 \right) dx$ becomes

- $\dfrac{1}{2} \displaystyle\int_0^{2} \cos t\, dt$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{4} \cos t\, dt$
- $2 \displaystyle\int_0^{4} \cos t\, dt$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \displaystyle\int_0^{4} \cos t\, dt$

## Explanation

From $t = x^2$ it follows that $dt = 2x\, dx$, so $x\, dx = \dfrac{1}{2}\, dt$. In a definite integral the substitution transforms **the limits as well**, therefore $x = 0$ becomes $t = 0$ and $x = 2$ becomes $t = 4$. Hence
$$
\int_0^{2} x \cos\left( x^2 \right) dx = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{4} \cos t\, dt ,
$$
and the computation is completed in the variable $t$.
