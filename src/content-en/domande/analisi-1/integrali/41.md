Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_x^{3} e^{t^2}\, dt$. Which expression gives $F'(x)$?

- $e^{x^2}$
- $e^{9} - e^{x^2}$
- $-e^{x^2}$
- $-2x\, e^{x^2}$

## Explanation

With the convention on swapping the limits one writes $F(x) = -\displaystyle\int_3^x e^{t^2}\, dt$. The function $e^{t^2}$ is continuous, so by the **fundamental theorem of calculus** the derivative of $\displaystyle\int_3^x e^{t^2}\, dt$ is $e^{x^2}$. Therefore $F'(x) = -e^{x^2}$.
