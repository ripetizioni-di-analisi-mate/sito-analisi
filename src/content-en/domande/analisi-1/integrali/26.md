Which function is an antiderivative of $\dfrac{\sin 2x}{1 + \cos^2 x}$?

- $\ln\left( 1 + \cos^2 x \right)$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2} \ln\left( 1 + \cos^2 x \right)$
- $-\arctan(\cos x)$
- $-\ln\left( 1 + \cos^2 x \right)$

## Explanation

By the double-angle formula $\sin 2x = 2 \sin x \cos x$. With the **substitution** $t = \cos x$ one has $dt = -\sin x\, dx$, so $\sin 2x\, dx = -2t\, dt$ and the integral becomes
$$
\int \frac{-2t}{1 + t^2}\, dt = -\ln\left( 1 + t^2 \right) + c ,
$$
because the numerator $2t$ is the derivative of the denominator $1 + t^2$. Going back to the variable $x$, an antiderivative is $-\ln\left( 1 + \cos^2 x \right)$.
