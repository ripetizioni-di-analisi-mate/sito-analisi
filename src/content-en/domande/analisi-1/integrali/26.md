Compute
$$
\int \frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{x}}\, dx .
$$

- $2\sqrt{x} + 2 \ln\left( 1 + \sqrt{x} \right) + c$
- $\ln\left( 1 + \sqrt{x} \right) + c$
- $2 \ln\left( 1 + \sqrt{x} \right) + c$
- $2\sqrt{x} - 2 \ln\left( 1 + \sqrt{x} \right) + c$

## Explanation

We apply **integration by substitution** with $t = \sqrt{x}$, that is $x = t^2$, so $dx = 2t\, dt$. Since $\dfrac{2t}{1 + t} = 2 - \dfrac{2}{1 + t}$, the integral becomes
$$
\int \frac{2t}{1 + t}\, dt = \int \left( 2 - \frac{2}{1 + t} \right) dt = 2t - 2 \ln|1 + t| + c .
$$
Returning to the variable $x$, and since $1 + \sqrt{x} > 0$, the result is $2\sqrt{x} - 2 \ln\left( 1 + \sqrt{x} \right) + c$.
