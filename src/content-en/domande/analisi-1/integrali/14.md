## Explanation

Since $x + 1 < 0$ for $x < -1$ and $x + 1 \geq 0$ for $x \geq -1$, one has $|x + 1| = -(x + 1)$ on $[-2, -1]$ and $|x + 1| = x + 1$ on $[-1, 2]$.

By the **additivity** of the integral with respect to the interval, we split $[-2, 2]$ at the point $-1$:
$$
\int_{-2}^{2} |x + 1|\, dx = -\int_{-2}^{-1} (x + 1)\, dx + \int_{-1}^{2} (x + 1)\, dx = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{9}{2} = 5 .
$$
The first integral equals $-\left[ \dfrac{x^2}{2} + x \right]_{-2}^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the second equals $\left[ \dfrac{x^2}{2} + x \right]_{-1}^{2} = \dfrac{9}{2}$.
