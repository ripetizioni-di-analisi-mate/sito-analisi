For which values of the real parameter $k$ are the antiderivatives of $\dfrac{1}{x^2 + 2x + k}$ written with an arctangent?

- $k < 1$
- $k > 1$
- $k = 1$
- $k > 0$

## Explanation

The discriminant of the trinomial is $\Delta = 4 - 4k$. For $k > 1$ one has $\Delta < 0$, so the trinomial has no real roots; **completing the square** gives $x^2 + 2x + k = (x + 1)^2 + (k - 1)$, and the antiderivatives are $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k - 1}} \arctan \dfrac{x + 1}{\sqrt{k - 1}} + c$. For $k = 1$ the denominator is $(x + 1)^2$ and the antiderivatives are $-\dfrac{1}{x + 1} + c$. For $k < 1$ the trinomial has two distinct real roots, and the partial fractions give two logarithms; this holds also for $0 < k < 1$.
