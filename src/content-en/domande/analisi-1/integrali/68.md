Compute the definite integral of $\sqrt{x}$ over the interval $[1, 4]$.

- $\dfrac{14}{3}$
- $\dfrac{16}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{14}{3}$
- $7$

## Explanation

By the table of elementary integrals with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, an antiderivative of $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$ is $\dfrac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}} = \dfrac{2}{3}\, x \sqrt{x}$. By the **Newton–Leibniz formula**
$$
\int_1^{4} \sqrt{x}\, dx = \frac{2}{3} \left( 4 \sqrt{4} - 1 \right) = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 7 = \frac{14}{3} .
$$
