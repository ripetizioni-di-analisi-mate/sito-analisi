Which expression gives the partial fraction decomposition of $\dfrac{x + 1}{x^2 (x - 2)}$, with $A$, $B$, $C$ real constants?

- $\dfrac{A}{x} + \dfrac{B}{x - 2}$
- $\dfrac{A}{x^2} + \dfrac{B}{x - 2}$
- $\dfrac{A}{x} + \dfrac{B}{x^2} + \dfrac{C}{(x - 2)^2}$
- $\dfrac{A}{x} + \dfrac{B}{x^2} + \dfrac{C}{x - 2}$

## Explanation

The denominator has the root $x = 0$ with multiplicity $2$ and the simple root $x = 2$. The double root corresponds to two terms, $\dfrac{A}{x}$ and $\dfrac{B}{x^2}$, and the simple root corresponds to the term $\dfrac{C}{x - 2}$. For this function one finds $A = -\dfrac{3}{4}$, $B = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $C = \dfrac{3}{4}$. Since the three constants are nonzero, an expression with fewer terms, or with $(x - 2)^2$ in place of $x - 2$, gives a different function.
