Compute
$$
\int e^{x} \sin x\, dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{e^{x} \left( \sin x + \cos x \right)}{2} + c$
- $\dfrac{e^{x} \left( \sin x - \cos x \right)}{2} + c$
- $e^{x} \left( \sin x - \cos x \right) + c$
- $\dfrac{e^{x} \left( \cos x - \sin x \right)}{2} + c$

## Explanation

Let $I = \displaystyle\int e^{x} \sin x\, dx$. We apply **integration by parts** with $f(x) = \sin x$ and $g'(x) = e^{x}$, and we obtain $I = e^{x} \sin x - \displaystyle\int e^{x} \cos x\, dx$.

A second integration by parts, with $f(x) = \cos x$ and $g'(x) = e^{x}$, gives $\displaystyle\int e^{x} \cos x\, dx = e^{x} \cos x + I$. So **the original integral appears again**:
$$
I = e^{x} \sin x - e^{x} \cos x - I .
$$
Therefore $2I = e^{x} \left( \sin x - \cos x \right)$, and $I = \dfrac{e^{x} \left( \sin x - \cos x \right)}{2} + c$.
