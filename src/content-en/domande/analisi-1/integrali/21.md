Compute
$$
\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}\, dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{1}{2} \arcsin \dfrac{x}{2} + c$
- $2 \arcsin \dfrac{x}{2} + c$
- $\arcsin \dfrac{x}{2} + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \arcsin x + c$

## Explanation

Factoring $4$ out of the square root gives $\sqrt{4 - x^2} = 2 \sqrt{1 - \left( \dfrac{x}{2} \right)^2}$, so
$$
\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}\, dx = \int \frac{\frac{1}{2}}{\sqrt{1 - \left( \frac{x}{2} \right)^2}}\, dx .
$$
The integral is therefore reducible to the immediate integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}\, dx = \arcsin x + c$ with $\dfrac{x}{2}$ in place of $x$, since $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the derivative of $\dfrac{x}{2}$. The result is $\arcsin \dfrac{x}{2} + c$.
