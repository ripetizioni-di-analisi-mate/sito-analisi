Compute
$$
\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}\, dx .
$$

- $\dfrac{1}{2} \arcsin \dfrac{x}{2} + c$
- $2 \arcsin \dfrac{x}{2} + c$
- $\arcsin \dfrac{x}{2} + c$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} \arcsin x + c$

## Explanation

The integral is reducible to the standard integral
$$
\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}}\, dx = \arcsin \frac{x}{a} + c
$$
with $a = 2$, therefore the result is $\arcsin \dfrac{x}{2} + c$.
