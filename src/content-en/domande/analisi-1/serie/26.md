Which statement about the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2 + \sin n}{n}$ is true?

- It converges by comparison with $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3}{n}$
- It is oscillatory, because $\sin n$ has no limit
- It diverges to $+\infty$ by comparison with $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}$
- It converges, because the general term tends to $0$

## Explanation

Since $-1 \leq \sin n \leq 1$, we have $1 \leq 2 + \sin n \leq 3$, and hence
$$
\frac{1}{n} \leq \frac{2 + \sin n}{n} \leq \frac{3}{n} .
$$
The harmonic series diverges, therefore by the **comparison test** the given series **diverges** to $+\infty$.

The upper bound with $\dfrac{3}{n}$ is true but does not allow a conclusion, because $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3}{n}$ diverges. The terms are positive, so the oscillatory case does not occur.
