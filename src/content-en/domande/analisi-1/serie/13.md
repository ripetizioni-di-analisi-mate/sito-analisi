For which $x \in \mathbb{R}$ does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{x+1}{3} \right)^{\! n}$ converge?

- $[-4, 2]$
- $(-2, 4)$
- $(-2, 0)$
- $(-4, 2)$

## Explanation

For every fixed $x$ the series is a **geometric series** with ratio $q = \dfrac{x+1}{3}$, so it converges if and only if $|q| < 1$.

The inequality $\left| \dfrac{x+1}{3} \right| < 1$ is equivalent to $|x+1| < 3$, that is to $-3 < x + 1 < 3$, and therefore to $-4 < x < 2$.

At the endpoints the series does not converge, because for $x = 2$ the ratio is $1$ and the series diverges to $+\infty$, while for $x = -4$ the ratio is $-1$ and the series is indeterminate. Consequently the series converges exactly for $x \in (-4, 2)$.
