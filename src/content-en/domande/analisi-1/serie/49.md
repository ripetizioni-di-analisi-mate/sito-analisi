Start from a square of side $1$. Joining the midpoints of its sides gives a second square, and the same procedure is repeated indefinitely on each new square. What is the sum of the areas of all the squares, including the first one?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $2$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

If a square has side $\ell$, the side of the next square joins the midpoints of two consecutive sides, so it measures $\sqrt{\left( \dfrac{\ell}{2} \right)^2 + \left( \dfrac{\ell}{2} \right)^2} = \dfrac{\ell}{\sqrt{2}}$. Hence the area of each square is half the area of the previous one.

The areas are $1, \dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{4}, \dots$, that is $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\! n}$ with $n \geq 0$, and their sum is the **geometric series** with ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$:
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{\! n} = \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{1}{2}} = 2 .
$$
The value $1$ is the sum of the areas without the first square.
