What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{n}{n+1}$?

- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Converges absolutely
- Is oscillatory

## Explanation

The absolute value of the general term is $\dfrac{n}{n+1} \to 1$, so the general term does not tend to $0$. By the **necessary condition for convergence** the series does not converge, and the alternating series test does not apply.

Adding the terms in pairs we get $a_{2k-1} + a_{2k} = \dfrac{2k}{2k+1} - \dfrac{2k-1}{2k} = \dfrac{1}{2k(2k+1)}$. Therefore the partial sums with even index $s_{2K}$ are increasing and smaller than $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{4k^2}$, so they converge to a number $L$.

The partial sums with odd index are $s_{2K+1} = s_{2K} - \dfrac{2K+1}{2K+2}$, so they tend to $L - 1$. Since two subsequences of $s_N$ have different limits, $s_N$ has no limit and the series is **oscillatory**.
