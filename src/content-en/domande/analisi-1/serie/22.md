For which of the following series does the necessary condition for convergence allow us to conclude that the series does not converge?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sin \frac{1}{n}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \sin \frac{1}{n}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}$

## Explanation

The **necessary condition for convergence** allows a conclusion only when the general term does not tend to $0$.

Since $\dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, by the standard limit of the sine
$$
n \sin \frac{1}{n} = \frac{\sin \dfrac{1}{n}}{\dfrac{1}{n}} \to 1 \neq 0 ,
$$
so the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \sin \frac{1}{n}$ **does not converge**.

In the other three series the general term tends to $0$, therefore the necessary condition is satisfied and allows no conclusion. These three series diverge, and this is established by comparison, direct or limit comparison, with the harmonic series.
