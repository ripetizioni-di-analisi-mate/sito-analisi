The integral test is applied to the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2 + 1}$ with $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x^2 + 1}$ on $[1, +\infty)$. Which statement is true?

- The integral diverges, so the series diverges
- The integral equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the series converges with sum $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- The integral equals $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, so the series converges with sum $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- The integral equals $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, so the series converges

## Explanation

The function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x^2 + 1}$ is positive and decreasing on $[1, +\infty)$, so the hypotheses of the **integral test** are satisfied. The integral is
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^2 + 1} = \lim_{t \to +\infty} \bigl( \arctan t - \arctan 1 \bigr) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{4} ,
$$
therefore the series **converges**.

The test determines only the behavior of the series. Indeed the partial sum $s_3 = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{4}{5}$ is already greater than $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, and the terms are positive, so the sum is greater than $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
