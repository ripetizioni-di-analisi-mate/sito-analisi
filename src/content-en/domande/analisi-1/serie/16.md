The partial sums of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ are $s_N = \dfrac{2N}{N+1}$ for every $N \geq 1$. What is the sum of the series?

- $0$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $2$

## Explanation

By definition the **sum** of the series is the limit of the partial sums, when this limit is finite.

The numerator and the denominator of $\dfrac{2N}{N+1}$ have degree $1$, so
$$
\lim_{N \to +\infty} \frac{2N}{N+1} = 2 ,
$$
and therefore the series converges with sum $2$.

The value $1$ is the first partial sum $s_1 = a_1$, and $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is $s_3$. The value $0$ is the limit of the general term $a_n = s_n - s_{n-1} = \dfrac{2}{n(n+1)}$, which tends to $0$ as the necessary condition requires.
