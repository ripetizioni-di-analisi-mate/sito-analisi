For the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ it is known that the series of absolute values $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |a_n|$ converges with sum $3$. Which statement is true?

- $\sum a_n$ converges with sum $3$
- $\sum a_n$ may be oscillatory, if the terms change sign
- $\sum a_n$ converges only if the terms have alternating signs
- $\sum a_n$ converges, and its sum $s$ satisfies $|s| \leq 3$

## Explanation

The series converges absolutely, so by the theorem on **absolute convergence** it converges, whatever the signs of the terms are.

The same theorem gives the inequality $\left| \sum a_n \right| \leq \sum |a_n| = 3$. The sum equals $3$ only in special cases, for instance when all terms are non-negative: with $a_n = \dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}\, 3}{2^n}$ the series of absolute values has sum $3$, while the given series has sum $1$.
