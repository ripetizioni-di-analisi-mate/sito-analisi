What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n\, 3^n}$?

- $\ln \dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\ln \dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\ln \dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

For $|x| < 1$ we have $\ln(1 + x) = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} x^n}{n}$. Substituting $-x$ for $x$, since $(-1)^{n+1} (-1)^n = -1$, we obtain
$$
\ln(1 - x) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n} .
$$
With $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ we get $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n\, 3^n} = -\ln \frac{2}{3} = \ln \frac{3}{2}$.

The value $\ln \dfrac{4}{3}$ is obtained by substituting $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ into the series of $\ln(1 + x)$, which has alternating signs, and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the sum of the geometric series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{3^n}$.
