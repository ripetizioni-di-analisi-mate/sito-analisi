What is $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to +\infty} \sqrt[n]{n^3 + 2^n}$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $3$
- $+\infty$

## Explanation

By the **hierarchy of infinities** we have $\dfrac{n^3}{2^n} \to 0$. Factoring out $2^n$ under the root gives
$$
\sqrt[n]{n^3 + 2^n} = \sqrt[n]{2^n \left( 1 + \frac{n^3}{2^n} \right)} = 2 \left( 1 + \frac{n^3}{2^n} \right)^{\! \frac{1}{n}} .
$$
The base $1 + \dfrac{n^3}{2^n}$ tends to $1$ and the exponent $\dfrac{1}{n}$ tends to $0$, therefore the power tends to $1^0 = 1$.

Consequently the limit equals $2$. The root of a sum is different from the sum of the roots, so the value $1 + 2 = 3$, obtained from $\sqrt[n]{n^3} + \sqrt[n]{2^n}$, is wrong.
