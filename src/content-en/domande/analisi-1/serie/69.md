What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sqrt{n}\, \tan \frac{1}{n^2}$, and why?

- It diverges to $+\infty$, because the general term is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$
- It is oscillatory, because the tangent is a periodic function
- It converges, because the general term is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{n \sqrt{n}}$
- It diverges to $+\infty$, because the factor $\sqrt{n}$ tends to $+\infty$

## Explanation

For $n \geq 1$ we have $0 < \dfrac{1}{n^2} \leq 1 < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so $\tan \dfrac{1}{n^2} > 0$ and the series has positive terms.

Since $\dfrac{1}{n^2} \to 0$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\tan t}{t} = 1$, we have $\tan \dfrac{1}{n^2} \sim \dfrac{1}{n^2}$, hence
$$
\sqrt{n}\, \tan \frac{1}{n^2} \sim \frac{\sqrt{n}}{n^2} = \frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}} .
$$
The generalized harmonic series with $\alpha = \dfrac{3}{2} > 1$ converges, so by the **limit comparison test** the given series **converges**.

The asymptotic term $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ is obtained by replacing $\tan \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ with $\dfrac{1}{n}$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$.
