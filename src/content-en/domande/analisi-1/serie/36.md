Let $s$ be the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^3}$. In which interval does $s$ certainly lie?

- $\left[ 0, \dfrac{7}{8} \right]$
- $\left[ \dfrac{7}{8}, 1 \right]$
- $\left[ 1, \dfrac{9}{8} \right]$
- $\left[ -1, -\dfrac{7}{8} \right]$

## Explanation

The sequence $b_n = \dfrac{1}{n^3}$ is positive, decreasing and tends to $0$, so the **alternating series test** applies and the series converges.

The first two partial sums are $s_1 = 1$ and $s_2 = 1 - \dfrac{1}{8} = \dfrac{7}{8}$. The partial sums with odd index decrease to $s$ and those with even index increase to $s$, hence $s_2 \leq s \leq s_1$.

Therefore $s$ lies in $\left[ \dfrac{7}{8}, 1 \right]$; its numerical value is $s \approx 0.902$.
