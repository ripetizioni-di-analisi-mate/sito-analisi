What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{\ln n}{n}$?

- Converges absolutely
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Is indeterminate

## Explanation

For $n \geq 3$ we have $\ln n > 1$, so $\dfrac{\ln n}{n} > \dfrac{1}{n}$. The harmonic series diverges, therefore by the comparison test the series of absolute values diverges and the given series does not converge absolutely.

The function $f(x) = \dfrac{\ln x}{x}$ has derivative $f'(x) = \dfrac{1 - \ln x}{x^2}$, negative for $x > e$, so $b_n = \dfrac{\ln n}{n}$ is decreasing for $n \geq 3$; moreover $b_n \geq 0$ and, by the hierarchy of infinities, $b_n \to 0$.

The hypotheses of the **Leibniz test** are therefore satisfied from $n = 3$ on, and the behavior does not depend on the first terms. Therefore the series **converges conditionally but not absolutely**.
