What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^n + 3^n}{6^n}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{7}{2}$
- $6$
- $2$

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{2^n + 3^n}{6^n} = \left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{\! n} + \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\! n}$, the general term is the sum of the terms of two **geometric series** with ratios $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$, both convergent.

By the **linearity of the sum** we have
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^n + 3^n}{6^n} = \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{1}{3}} + \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{1}{2}} = \frac{3}{2} + 2 = \frac{7}{2} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is obtained by starting both series at $n = 1$, and the value $6$ by adding the ratios into a single geometric series with ratio $\dfrac{5}{6}$.
