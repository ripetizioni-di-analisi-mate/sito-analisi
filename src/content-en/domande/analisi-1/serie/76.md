The base of the power $\left( \sqrt[n]{n} - 1 \right)^{n}$ contains the $n$-th root of $n$. What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \sqrt[n]{n} - 1 \right)^{n}$?

- The root test gives no information, because $\sqrt[n]{n} \to 1$
- Converges
- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Is oscillatory

## Explanation

For $n \geq 1$ we have $\sqrt[n]{n} \geq 1$, so the general term is non-negative, and the **root test** applies:
$$
\sqrt[n]{a_n} = \sqrt[n]{n} - 1 .
$$
Since $\sqrt[n]{n} = e^{\frac{\ln n}{n}} \to 1$, the $n$-th root of the general term tends to $1 - 1 = 0$. Since $0 < 1$, the series **converges**.

The deciding limit is that of $\sqrt[n]{n} - 1$, which equals $0$, not that of $\sqrt[n]{n}$.
