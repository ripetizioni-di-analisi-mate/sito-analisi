What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{(-1)^n}{n} + \frac{1}{n} \right)$?

- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Converges with sum $0$
- Is indeterminate

## Explanation

The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges by the Leibniz test, while the harmonic series diverges. By the **linearity of the sum**, the sum of a convergent series and a non-convergent series does not converge.

The general term equals $0$ for odd $n$ and $\dfrac{2}{n}$ for even $n$, so it is nonnegative, and the series either converges or diverges to $+\infty$.

Since the series does not converge, the series **diverges** to $+\infty$.
