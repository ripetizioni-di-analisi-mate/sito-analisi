For which real $x$ does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (\ln x)^n$ converge?

- $(0, e)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{e}, e \right)$
- $\left[ \dfrac{1}{e}, e \right]$
- $\left[ \dfrac{1}{e}, e \right)$

## Explanation

The general term is defined for $x > 0$. For every fixed $x > 0$ the series is a **geometric series** with ratio $q = \ln x$, so it converges if and only if $|\ln x| < 1$.

The inequality $|\ln x| < 1$ is equivalent to $-1 < \ln x < 1$, and since the logarithm is increasing it is equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{e} < x < e$.

At the endpoints the series does not converge, because for $x = e$ the ratio is $1$ and the series diverges to $+\infty$, while for $x = \dfrac{1}{e}$ the ratio is $-1$ and the series is indeterminate. Therefore the series converges exactly for $x \in \left( \dfrac{1}{e}, e \right)$.
