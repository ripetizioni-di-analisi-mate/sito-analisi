Consider the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^2}{(1 + x^2)^n}$ and denote by $S(x)$ its sum, where it exists. Which statement is true?

- It converges for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, with $S(x) = 1 + x^2$ for every $x$
- It converges only for $x \neq 0$, with $S(x) = 1 + x^2$
- It converges for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, with $S(0) = 0$ and $S(x) = 1 + x^2$ for $x \neq 0$
- It converges for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, with $S(0) = 0$ and $S(x) = \dfrac{x^2 (1 + x^2)}{2 + x^2}$ for $x \neq 0$

## Explanation

For $x = 0$ every term equals $0$, so the series converges with sum $S(0) = 0$.

For $x \neq 0$ the series is the **geometric series** with ratio $q = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$, multiplied by $x^2$. Since $1 + x^2 > 1$, we have $0 < q < 1$, so the series converges, and its sum is
$$
S(x) = \frac{x^2}{1 - \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}} = \frac{x^2}{\dfrac{x^2}{1 + x^2}} = 1 + x^2 .
$$
Therefore $S(x) \to 1$ as $x \to 0$, while $S(0) = 0$, and the sum is not continuous at $x = 0$ even though every term of the series is a continuous function.
