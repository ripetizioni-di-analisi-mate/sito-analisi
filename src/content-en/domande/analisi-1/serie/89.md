Studying the endpoints as well, what is the set of convergence of the power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{n}{2n+1} \right)^{\! n} x^n$?

- $[-2, 2]$
- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$
- $(-2, 2)$
- $[-2, 2)$

## Explanation

We have $\sqrt[n]{|c_n|} = \dfrac{n}{2n+1} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the radius of convergence is $R = 2$.

At $x = 2$ the general term is
$$
\left( \frac{2n}{2n+1} \right)^{\! n} = \left( 1 - \frac{1}{2n+1} \right)^{\! n} \to e^{-\frac{1}{2}} ,
$$
which is different from $0$, so by the necessary condition the series does not converge. At $x = -2$ the absolute value of the general term has the same limit $e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, so the series does not converge there either.

The set of convergence is therefore $(-2, 2)$.
