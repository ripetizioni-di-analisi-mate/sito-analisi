For which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{n^{\alpha}}{n^2 + 1}$ converge conditionally but not absolutely?

- $1 \leq \alpha < 2$
- $1 < \alpha < 2$
- $\alpha < 2$
- $0 < \alpha < 1$

## Explanation

Setting $b_n = \dfrac{n^{\alpha}}{n^2 + 1}$, we have $b_n \sim n^{\alpha - 2}$. By limit comparison the series of absolute values converges if and only if $2 - \alpha > 1$, that is for $\alpha < 1$.

For $\alpha < 2$ we have $b_n \to 0$, and the derivative of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^{\alpha}}{x^2 + 1}$ has the sign of $(\alpha - 2) x^2 + \alpha$, which is negative for large $x$; hence $b_n$ is eventually decreasing and by the **alternating series test** the series converges. For $\alpha \geq 2$ the general term does not tend to $0$, so the series does not converge.

Therefore the convergence is conditional but not absolute for $1 \leq \alpha < 2$. The value $\alpha = 1$ is included, because in that case $b_n \sim \dfrac{1}{n}$ and the series of absolute values diverges.
