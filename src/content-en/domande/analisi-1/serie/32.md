Which statement about the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{n} \right)^{\! n^2}$ is true?

- It diverges to $+\infty$, because the $n$-th root of the general term tends to $e$
- It converges, because the $n$-th root of the general term tends to $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- The root test gives no information, because the $n$-th root of the general term tends to $1$
- It converges, because the $n$-th root of the general term tends to $0$

## Explanation

The general term is nonnegative and is a power with exponent $n^2 = n \cdot n$, so the **root test** applies. The $n$-th root is
$$
\sqrt[n]{\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n} \right)^{\! n^2}} = \left( 1 - \frac{1}{n} \right)^{\! n} .
$$
By the standard limit $\left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{n} \right)^{n} \to e^{x}$ with $x = -1$, the root tends to $\dfrac{1}{e}$.

Since $\dfrac{1}{e} < 1$, the series **converges**. The answer with limit $1$ comes from considering only the base $1 - \dfrac{1}{n}$, which tends to $1$, without the exponent $n$ that remains after taking the root.
