Which description is correct for $\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty} \dfrac{2 \left(-1\right)^{n} n}{5 n + 3}$?

- Converges to a finite sum
- Diverges to positive infinity
- The partial sums oscillate without a limit
- Diverges to negative infinity

## Explanation

The even partial sums are the sum of the differences between the absolute values of two consecutive terms. These differences are of order $1/n^2$, so the even subsequence converges. The odd subsequence has a different limit because the absolute values tend to $\dfrac{2}{5}$.
