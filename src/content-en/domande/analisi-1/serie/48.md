A ball is dropped from a height of $2\,\mathrm{m}$. After each bounce it rises to a height equal to $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the height from which it fell. Assuming the bounces go on indefinitely, how many meters does the ball travel in total, adding descents and ascents?

- $8$
- $14$
- $16$
- $12$

## Explanation

The first descent measures $2$. After the $n$-th bounce, with $n \geq 1$, the ball rises up to $2 \left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{\! n}$ and then falls back by the same amount, so it travels $4 \left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{\! n}$.

The total distance is therefore
$$
2 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 4 \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{\! n} = 2 + 4 \cdot \frac{\dfrac{3}{4}}{1 - \dfrac{3}{4}} = 2 + 12 = 14 ,
$$
where the series is geometric with ratio $\dfrac{3}{4}$. The value $8$ counts only the descents, and $16$ counts the first descent twice as well.
