Which description is correct for $\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty} - n + \sqrt{n^{2} + 3}$?

- Diverges to negative infinity
- Diverges to positive infinity
- The partial sums oscillate without a limit
- Converges to a finite sum

## Explanation

Rationalizing, the general term is $\dfrac{3}{n + \sqrt{n^{2} + 3}}$. It is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{3}{2 n}$, a multiple of the general term of the harmonic series.
