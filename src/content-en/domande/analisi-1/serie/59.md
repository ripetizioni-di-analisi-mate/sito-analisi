The sequence defined by $a_1 = 1$ and $a_{n+1} = \sqrt{2 + a_n}$ is increasing and satisfies $a_n < 2$ for every $n$. Which statement is true?

- It converges to $\sqrt{2}$
- It diverges to $+\infty$, because it is increasing
- It may be oscillatory
- It converges to $2$

## Explanation

The sequence is increasing and bounded above, so by the theorem on **bounded monotone sequences** it converges to a limit $L$, with $1 \leq L \leq 2$.

Passing to the limit in the relation $a_{n+1} = \sqrt{2 + a_n}$, since $a_{n+1} \to L$ as well, one obtains $L = \sqrt{2 + L}$. Squaring gives $L^2 - L - 2 = 0$, that is $(L - 2)(L + 1) = 0$.

Since $L \geq 1$, the solution $L = -1$ is discarded, hence the sequence **converges to** $2$.
