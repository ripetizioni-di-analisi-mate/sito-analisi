The factor $\cos(n\pi)$ in the numerator equals $1$ or $-1$ according to the parity of $n$. What is the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(n\pi)}{2^n}$?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $2$
- $-\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $0$

## Explanation

For every integer $n$ we have $\cos(n\pi) = (-1)^n$, so the general term is $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\! n}$ and the series is geometric with ratio $q = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Since $|q| < 1$, the series converges, and starting from $n = 0$ its sum is
$$
\frac{1}{1 - \left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right)} = \frac{1}{\dfrac{3}{2}} = \frac{2}{3} .
$$
The value $2$ is obtained by using $\dfrac{1}{2}$ as the ratio, that is without the sign given by $\cos(n\pi)$.
