- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Converges
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Is indeterminate

## Explanation

The general term $a_n = \dfrac{n^3}{n!}$ is positive and contains a factorial, so the **ratio test** applies. Since $(n+1)! = (n+1)\, n!$, one has
$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(n+1)^3}{(n+1)!} \cdot \frac{n!}{n^3} = \frac{(n+1)^2}{n^3} .
$$
The numerator has degree $2$ and the denominator has degree $3$, therefore the ratio tends to $0$. Since $0 < 1$, the series **converges**.
