- Converges absolutely
- Is indeterminate
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Diverges to $+\infty$

## Explanation

The series of absolute values is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2n+1}$. Since $\dfrac{1}{2n+1} \sim \dfrac{1}{2n}$ and the harmonic series diverges, by the limit comparison test the series of absolute values diverges, so the given series does not converge absolutely.

The sequence $b_n = \dfrac{1}{2n+1}$ is positive, decreasing, because the denominator increases, and tends to $0$. Hence the hypotheses of the **Leibniz test** are satisfied and the series converges.

Therefore the series **converges conditionally but not absolutely**.
