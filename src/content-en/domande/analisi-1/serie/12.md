- Converges absolutely
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Is indeterminate
- Diverges to $+\infty$

## Explanation

The series of absolute values is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n \ln n}$. The function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x \ln x}$ is positive and decreasing on $[2, +\infty)$, and
$$
\int_{2}^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x \ln x} = \lim_{t \to +\infty} \bigl( \ln \ln t - \ln \ln 2 \bigr) = +\infty ,
$$
so by the **integral test** the series of absolute values diverges and the given series does not converge absolutely.

The sequence $b_n = \dfrac{1}{n \ln n}$ is positive, decreasing, because $n \ln n$ increases, and tends to $0$. Hence, by the **Leibniz test**, the series converges, and therefore it converges conditionally but not absolutely.
