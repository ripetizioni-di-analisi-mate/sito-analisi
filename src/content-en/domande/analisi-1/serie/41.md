What is the set of convergence of the power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2n}}{4^n}$, which contains only even powers of $x$?

- $(-4, 4)$
- $[-2, 2]$
- $(-2, 2)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$

## Explanation

The general term can be written as $\left( \dfrac{x^2}{4} \right)^{\! n}$, so for each fixed $x$ the series is geometric with ratio $q = \dfrac{x^2}{4} \geq 0$.

The geometric series converges if and only if $|q| < 1$, that is $x^2 < 4$, hence it converges for $-2 < x < 2$.

For $x = \pm 2$ the ratio equals $1$ and every term equals $1$, so the general term does not tend to $0$ and the series diverges. The set of convergence is $(-2, 2)$. The answer $(-4, 4)$ comes from confusing the condition on $x^2$ with a condition on $x$.
