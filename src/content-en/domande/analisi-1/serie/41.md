What is the set of convergence of the power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x-2)^n}{n^2}$?

- $(1, 3)$
- $[1, 3)$
- $[1, 3]$
- $[-1, 1]$

## Explanation

The series has center $x_0 = 2$ and coefficients $c_n = \dfrac{1}{n^2}$, so
$$
\frac{|c_{n+1}|}{|c_n|} = \frac{n^2}{(n+1)^2} \to 1 .
$$
Therefore the **radius of convergence** is $R = 1$, and the series converges absolutely for $|x - 2| < 1$, that is for $1 < x < 3$.

For $x = 3$ the series becomes $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$, which converges. For $x = 1$ the series becomes $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$, whose series of absolute values is the same $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$, so it converges absolutely. The set of convergence is $[1, 3]$.
