The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ converges, and nothing else is known about the general term. Which of the following series certainly converges?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |a_n|$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n^2$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( a_n + \frac{1}{n^2} \right)$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sqrt{|a_n|}$

## Explanation

The series $\sum a_n$ and $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ both converge, so by **linearity of the sum** the series $\sum \left( a_n + \dfrac{1}{n^2} \right)$ converges too.

The other three series may fail to converge. With $a_n = \dfrac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ the series $\sum a_n$ converges by the alternating series test, while $\sum |a_n| = \sum \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$, $\sum a_n^2 = \sum \dfrac{1}{n}$ and $\sum \sqrt{|a_n|} = \sum \dfrac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{4}}}$ diverge, because they are generalized harmonic series with exponent less than or equal to $1$.
