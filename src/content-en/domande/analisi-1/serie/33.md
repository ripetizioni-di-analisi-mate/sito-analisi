For which $a > 0$ does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{a n + 1}{2n + 3} \right)^{\! n}$ converge?

- $0 < a \leq 2$
- $a > 2$
- $0 < a < 2$
- $0 < a < \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The general term is positive and is an $n$-th power, so the **root test** applies. The $n$-th root is $\dfrac{an + 1}{2n + 3}$, which tends to $\dfrac{a}{2}$.

Therefore the series converges for $\dfrac{a}{2} < 1$, that is for $0 < a < 2$, and diverges for $a > 2$.

For $a = 2$ the limit of the root is $1$ and the test gives no information. In this case
$$
\left( \frac{2n + 1}{2n + 3} \right)^{\! n} = \left( 1 - \frac{2}{2n + 3} \right)^{\! n} \to e^{-1} \neq 0 ,
$$
because $n \cdot \dfrac{2}{2n + 3} \to 1$. Hence by the necessary condition the series does not converge. Consequently the series converges if and only if $0 < a < 2$.
