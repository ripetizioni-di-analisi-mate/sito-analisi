What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \ln \frac{n+1}{n}$?

- Converges with sum $\ln 2$
- Converges with sum $1$
- Is oscillatory
- Diverges to $+\infty$

## Explanation

By the properties of the logarithm $\ln \dfrac{n+1}{n} = \ln(n+1) - \ln n$, so the general term can be written as $a_n = b_n - b_{n+1}$ with $b_n = -\ln n$, and the series is **telescoping**.

The partial sums are
$$
s_N = b_1 - b_{N+1} = 0 + \ln(N+1) = \ln(N+1) ,
$$
and since $\ln(N+1) \to +\infty$, the series **diverges** to $+\infty$.

The general term tends to $\ln 1 = 0$, so the necessary condition is satisfied; the series diverges nonetheless, like the harmonic series.
