- Converges
- Is indeterminate
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Diverges to $+\infty$

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{1}{n} > 0$, one has $e^{\frac{1}{n}} > 1$, so the general term is positive and the series either converges or diverges to $+\infty$.

Since $\dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, by the **standard limit of the exponential** one has
$$
e^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \sim \frac{1}{n} .
$$
The harmonic series diverges to $+\infty$, therefore, by the **limit comparison test**, the given series also **diverges** to $+\infty$, even though the general term tends to $0$.
