As $p \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, when does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n^p}$ converge?

- $p > 1$
- $p \geq 1$
- $p > 2$
- $p > 0$

## Explanation

If $p > 1$, choose $r$ with $1 < r < p$. Since $\dfrac{\ln n}{n^{p - r}} \to 0$ by the hierarchy of growth rates, eventually $\dfrac{\ln n}{n^p} \leq \dfrac{1}{n^r}$, and the series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^r}$ converges. Hence by the **comparison test** the series converges.

If $p \leq 1$, for $n \geq 3$ we have $\ln n \geq 1$ and $n^p \leq n$, so $\dfrac{\ln n}{n^p} \geq \dfrac{1}{n}$. Since the harmonic series diverges, by the comparison test the series diverges.

Therefore the series converges if and only if $p > 1$. For $0 < p \leq 1$ the general term tends to $0$, but the series diverges.
