What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{n} \right)^{\! n}$?

- Converges
- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Is indeterminate
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely

## Explanation

By the standard limit $\left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{n} \right)^{n} \to e^{x}$ with $x = -1$ we have
$$
\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n} \right)^{\! n} \to \frac{1}{e} \neq 0 ,
$$
so by the **necessary condition for convergence** the series does not converge.

The terms are nonnegative, therefore the series either converges or diverges to $+\infty$. Since it does not converge, the series **diverges** to $+\infty$.
