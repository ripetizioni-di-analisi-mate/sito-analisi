The power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n x^n$ converges at $x = 3$ and does not converge at $x = -5$. What can be said about its radius of convergence $R$?

- $3 \leq R \leq 5$
- $R = 4$
- $3 < R < 5$
- $R \geq 5$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the **radius of convergence**, the series does not converge at the points with $|x| > R$. Since it converges at $x = 3$, we have $3 \leq R$.

The series converges absolutely at the points with $|x| < R$. Since it does not converge at $x = -5$, we have $5 \geq R$. Hence $3 \leq R \leq 5$.

Both endpoints are possible. The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{3^n n^2}$ has $R = 3$, converges at $x = 3$ and does not converge at $x = -5$; the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{5^n}$ has $R = 5$, converges at $x = 3$ and is indeterminate at $x = -5$.
