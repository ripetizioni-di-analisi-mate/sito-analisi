The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ converges with sum $2$ and the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n$ diverges to $+\infty$. What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (a_n + b_n)$?

- Converges with sum $2$
- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Is oscillatory
- It cannot be determined without knowing $a_n$ and $b_n$

## Explanation

Denoting by $s_N$ and $t_N$ the partial sums of the two series, the $N$-th partial sum of $\sum (a_n + b_n)$ is $s_N + t_N$.

Since $s_N \to 2$ and $t_N \to +\infty$, by the algebra of limits $s_N + t_N \to +\infty$. Hence the series **diverges** to $+\infty$, whatever $a_n$ and $b_n$ are.
