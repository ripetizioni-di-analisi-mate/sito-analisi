What kind of convergence does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \right)$ have?

- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Is oscillatory
- Converges absolutely
- Diverges

## Explanation

Since $\cos \dfrac{1}{n} \leq 1$, the series of absolute values is $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \right)$.

Since $\dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, by the standard limit of the cosine $1 - \cos \dfrac{1}{n} \sim \dfrac{1}{2n^2}$, and the series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges. Hence by the **limit comparison test** the series of absolute values converges, and the given series **converges absolutely**.
