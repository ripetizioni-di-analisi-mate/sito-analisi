In the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n} \sin \frac{n\pi}{2}$ the terms with even index are zero. How does the series behave?

- Converges absolutely
- Is oscillatory, because $\sin \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$ has no limit
- Does not converge, because the signs do not alternate at every term
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely

## Explanation

For even $n$ we have $\sin \dfrac{n\pi}{2} = 0$, while for $n = 2k + 1$ we have $\sin \dfrac{(2k+1)\pi}{2} = (-1)^k$. Hence the nonzero terms form the series
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} - \cdots .
$$
The zero terms do not change the partial sums, because $s_{2k+2} = s_{2k+1}$, so the given series converges if and only if this one does. The sequence $\dfrac{1}{2k+1}$ is positive, decreasing and tends to $0$, hence by the **alternating series test** the series converges.

The series of absolute values is $\sum \dfrac{1}{2k+1}$, which diverges by limit comparison with the harmonic series, because $\dfrac{1}{2k+1} \sim \dfrac{1}{2k}$. Therefore the series **converges conditionally but not absolutely**.
