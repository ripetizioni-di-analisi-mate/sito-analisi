What is the behavior of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\arctan n}{n^2}$?

- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Is oscillatory
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Converges

## Explanation

As $n \to +\infty$ the argument $n$ tends to $+\infty$, so $\arctan n \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The general term is positive and
$$
\frac{\arctan n}{n^2} \sim \frac{\pi}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{n^2} .
$$
The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges, because $\alpha = 2 > 1$. Therefore by the **limit comparison test** the given series **converges**.

The equivalence $\arctan t \sim t$ holds for $t \to 0$, so it does not apply here, and the general term is not asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{n}$.
