In every term of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{1 + \frac{1}{n}}}$ the exponent of $n$ is greater than $1$. Which statement about the series is true?

- It converges, because the exponent $1 + \dfrac{1}{n}$ is greater than $1$ for every $n$
- It converges, because the general term is less than $\dfrac{1}{n}$
- It is oscillatory, because the exponent depends on $n$
- It diverges to $+\infty$, because the general term is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{n}$

## Explanation

The general term can be written as $\dfrac{1}{n \cdot n^{\frac{1}{n}}}$. Since $n^{\frac{1}{n}} = e^{\frac{\ln n}{n}}$ and $\dfrac{\ln n}{n} \to 0$, we have $n^{\frac{1}{n}} \to 1$, so the general term is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{n}$.

The harmonic series diverges, hence by the **limit comparison test** the series **diverges** to $+\infty$.

The theorem on the generalized harmonic series concerns a constant exponent $\alpha > 1$; here the exponent depends on $n$ and tends to $1$, so that theorem does not apply. The inequality with $\dfrac{1}{n}$ is true, but since the harmonic series diverges the comparison test gives no information.
