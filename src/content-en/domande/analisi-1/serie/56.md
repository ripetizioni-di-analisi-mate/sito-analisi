What is the value of the series of logarithms $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \ln\!\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n^2} \right)$?

- $\ln 2$
- $-\ln 2$
- $-\infty$
- $\ln \dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

Since $1 - \dfrac{1}{n^2} = \dfrac{(n-1)(n+1)}{n^2}$, by the properties of logarithms
$$
\ln\!\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n^2} \right) = \ln \frac{n-1}{n} - \ln \frac{n}{n+1} .
$$
Hence the series is **telescoping** with $b_n = \ln \dfrac{n-1}{n}$, and since it starts at $n = 2$ its partial sums are
$$
s_N = b_2 - b_{N+1} = \ln \frac{1}{2} - \ln \frac{N}{N+1} .
$$
Since $\dfrac{N}{N+1} \to 1$, we have $\ln \dfrac{N}{N+1} \to 0$, hence the series converges with sum $\ln \dfrac{1}{2} = -\ln 2$. The value $\ln \dfrac{3}{4}$ is only the first term.
