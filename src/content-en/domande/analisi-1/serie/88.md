In the power series expansion centered at $0$ of the function $f(x) = e^{-x^2}$, what is the coefficient of $x^6$?

- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{720}$
- $\dfrac{1}{720}$

## Explanation

Substituting $-x^2$ for $x$ in the Taylor series of the exponential one obtains, for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$
e^{-x^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-x^2)^n}{n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n\, x^{2n}}{n!} .
$$
The power $x^6$ corresponds to $n = 3$, so the coefficient is $\dfrac{(-1)^3}{3!} = -\dfrac{1}{6}$. The value $-\dfrac{1}{720}$ is obtained by dividing by $6!$ instead of $3!$.
