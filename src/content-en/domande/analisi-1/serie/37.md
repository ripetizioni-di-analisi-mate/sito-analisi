Let $s$ be the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$. What is the smallest $N$ for which the error estimate of the Leibniz test guarantees $|s - s_N| < \dfrac{1}{100}$?

- $9$
- $99$
- $10$
- $100$

## Explanation

The sequence $b_n = \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ is positive, decreasing and tends to $0$, so the **Leibniz test** applies with the error estimate
$$
|s - s_N| \leq b_{N+1} = \frac{1}{(N+1)^2} .
$$
The estimate guarantees $|s - s_N| < \dfrac{1}{100}$ when $\dfrac{1}{(N+1)^2} < \dfrac{1}{100}$, that is when $(N+1)^2 > 100$, and therefore when $N + 1 > 10$.

Hence the smallest $N$ is $10$. For $N = 9$ the estimate gives only $|s - s_9| \leq \dfrac{1}{100}$, without the strict inequality.
