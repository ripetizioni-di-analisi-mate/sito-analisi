In the denominator of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n!}{2^{n^2}}$ the exponent of $2$ is $n^2$. How does the series behave?

- It diverges to $+\infty$, because $n!$ grows faster than every exponential $a^n$
- The ratio test gives no information
- It is oscillatory
- It converges

## Explanation

Since $(n+1)^2 - n^2 = 2n + 1$, the ratio is
$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(n+1)!}{n!} \cdot \frac{2^{n^2}}{2^{(n+1)^2}} = \frac{n+1}{2^{2n+1}} \to 0 ,
$$
where the limit follows from the hierarchy of growth rates. Since $0 < 1$, by the **ratio test** the series **converges**.

The denominator $2^{n^2}$ is not an exponential of the form $a^n$, because the exponent is $n^2$, and it grows faster than $n!$.
