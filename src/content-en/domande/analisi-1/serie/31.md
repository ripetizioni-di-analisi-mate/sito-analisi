Which statement about the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n+1}{n^3}$ is true?

- The ratio test gives no information, and the series converges by limit comparison with $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$
- The ratio tends to $1$, so by the ratio test the series diverges
- The ratio tends to $1$, so by the ratio test the series converges
- The ratio test gives no information, and the series diverges by limit comparison with $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}$

## Explanation

The ratio is $\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \dfrac{n+2}{(n+1)^3} \cdot \dfrac{n^3}{n+1}$, which is a quotient of polynomials of degree $4$ with equal leading coefficients, so it tends to $1$. With limit $1$ the **ratio test** gives no information.

The numerator $n + 1$ is asymptotically equivalent to $n$, so $\dfrac{n+1}{n^3} \sim \dfrac{1}{n^2}$. The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges, therefore by the **limit comparison test** the given series **converges**.
