For which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ does the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{n^5 + 1}}$ converge?

- $\alpha < \dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\alpha < \dfrac{5}{2}$
- $\alpha > \dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\alpha \leq \dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

Since $\sqrt{n^5 + 1} \sim n^{\frac{5}{2}}$, the general term, which is positive, satisfies
$$
\frac{n^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{n^5 + 1}} \sim \frac{n^{\alpha}}{n^{\frac{5}{2}}} = \frac{1}{n^{\frac{5}{2} - \alpha}} .
$$
By the **limit comparison test** the series has the same behavior as the generalized harmonic series with exponent $\dfrac{5}{2} - \alpha$, which converges if and only if $\dfrac{5}{2} - \alpha > 1$.

Hence the series converges if and only if $\alpha < \dfrac{3}{2}$. For $\alpha = \dfrac{3}{2}$ the general term is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{n}$ and the series diverges.
