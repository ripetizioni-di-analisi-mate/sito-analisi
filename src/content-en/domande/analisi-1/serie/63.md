Consider the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n = 1 - 1 + 1 - 1 + \cdots$. Which statement is true?

- It converges with sum $0$, because $(1 - 1) + (1 - 1) + \cdots = 0$
- It converges with sum $\dfrac{1}{2}$, given by $\dfrac{1}{1 - q}$ with $q = -1$
- It diverges, because the partial sums are not bounded
- It is oscillatory, even though grouping the terms in pairs gives a series with sum $0$

## Explanation

The partial sums are $s_N = 1$ for even $N$ and $s_N = 0$ for odd $N$, so they have no limit and the series is **oscillatory**, as stated by the theorem on the geometric series with $q = -1$.

Grouping the terms in pairs amounts to considering only the partial sums with odd index, which all equal $0$. The behavior of the series, however, depends on the whole sequence of partial sums, which has no limit. The formula $\dfrac{1}{1 - q}$ holds only for $|q| < 1$.
