- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Is indeterminate
- Converges conditionally but not absolutely

## Explanation

The general term is positive, so the series either converges or diverges to $+\infty$, and if it converges the convergence is also absolute.

Since $\dfrac{1}{n^2} \to 0$, by the **standard limit of the logarithm** one has
$$
\ln\!\left( 1 + \frac{1}{n^2} \right) \sim \frac{1}{n^2} .
$$
The generalized harmonic series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges, because $\alpha = 2 > 1$. Hence, by the **limit comparison test**, the given series also **converges**.
