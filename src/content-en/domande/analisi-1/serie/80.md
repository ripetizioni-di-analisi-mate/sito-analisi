Which of the following series converges conditionally but not absolutely?

- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \sqrt{n}$
- $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$

## Explanation

The sequence $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ is positive, decreasing and tends to $0$, so by the **alternating series test** the series $\displaystyle\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ converges; the series of absolute values $\sum \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ is a generalized harmonic series with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and diverges. Hence the convergence is conditional.

The series $\sum \dfrac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ converges absolutely, because $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges. The series $\sum (-1)^n \sqrt{n}$ does not converge, because its general term does not tend to $0$. The series $\sum \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ diverges.
