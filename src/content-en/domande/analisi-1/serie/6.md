- Converges conditionally but not absolutely
- Converges
- Is indeterminate

## Explanation

Multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}$ one obtains
$$
\sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}} \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}} .
$$
So the general term, which is positive, satisfies
$$
\frac{\sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n}}{n} \sim \frac{1}{2\, n^{\frac{3}{2}}} .
$$
The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ converges, because $\alpha = \dfrac{3}{2} > 1$. Therefore, by the **limit comparison test**, the given series **converges**.
