Applying the formula $\dfrac{1}{1 - q}$ to the geometric series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( \frac{5}{4} \right)^{\! n}$ one would get $-4$. What is the behavior of the series?

- Diverges to $+\infty$
- Converges with sum $-4$
- Converges with sum $\dfrac{4}{9}$
- Is oscillatory

## Explanation

The series is geometric with ratio $q = \dfrac{5}{4}$. Since $q \geq 1$, the series **diverges** to $+\infty$.

The formula $\dfrac{1}{1 - q}$ holds only for $|q| < 1$. With $q = \dfrac{5}{4}$ it would give $-4$, a negative number, while all terms of the series are positive and its partial sums increase.
