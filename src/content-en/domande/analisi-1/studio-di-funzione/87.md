What kind of discontinuity does the function $f(x) = \arctan \dfrac{1}{x}$ have at $x = 0$?

- A jump discontinuity, with jump $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- A removable discontinuity
- A discontinuity of the second kind
- A jump discontinuity, with jump $\pi$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0^+$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to +\infty$, so $f(x) \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$; as $x \to 0^-$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x} \to -\infty$, so $f(x) \to -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The one-sided limits are finite and different, therefore $x = 0$ is a **jump discontinuity**. The size of the jump is the difference of the one-sided limits, $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = \pi$.
