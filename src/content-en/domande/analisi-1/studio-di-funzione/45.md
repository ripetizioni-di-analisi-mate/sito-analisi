How many real solutions does the equation $x^3 - 3x + 1 = 0$ have?

- One
- Two
- Three
- None

## Explanation

Let $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 1$. The derivative $f'(x) = 3(x^2 - 1)$ is positive for $|x| > 1$ and negative for $|x| < 1$, so $f$ has relative maximum $f(-1) = 3$ and relative minimum $f(1) = -1$. Moreover, $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$ and $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$. On each of the three intervals $(-\infty, -1]$, $[-1, 1]$ and $[1, +\infty)$ the function is strictly monotonic and takes values of opposite sign, so by the **intermediate zero theorem** it vanishes exactly once on each of them. Therefore the equation has **three** real solutions.
