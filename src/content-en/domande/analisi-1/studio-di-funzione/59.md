The figure shows the graph of the derivative $f'$ of a function $f$ differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$; $f'$ vanishes only at $x = -1$ and at $x = 2$. On which maximal interval is $f$ decreasing?

::grafico{f="(x+1)*(x-2)" xmin=-3 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=5 punti="-1,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" legenda="y = f'(x)" titolo="Graph of the derivative $f'$, zero at $x = -1$ and at $x = 2$"}

- $(-\infty, -1] \cup [2, +\infty)$
- $[-1, 2]$
- $\left(-\infty, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$
- $[-2, 1]$

## Explanation

The figure represents $f'$, so the monotonicity of $f$ is read from the **sign** of $f'$, that is, from the position of the graph relative to the $x$-axis. The graph lies below the axis between $-1$ and $2$, therefore $f'(x) < 0$ for $-1 < x < 2$. By the monotonicity test $f$ is decreasing on $[-1, 2]$ and increasing on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[2, +\infty)$. The part where the graph of $f'$ goes down, that is, $\left(-\infty, \dfrac{1}{2}\right]$, shows where $f'$ decreases, and concerns the concavity of $f$.
