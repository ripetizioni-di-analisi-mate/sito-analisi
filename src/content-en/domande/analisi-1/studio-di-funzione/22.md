What kind of point is $x = 1$ for the function $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{(x - 1)^2}$?

- A corner point
- A cusp
- An inflection point with vertical tangent
- A stationary point

## Explanation

The function is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and, for $x \neq 1$, its derivative is
$$
f'(x) = \frac{2}{3\sqrt[3]{x - 1}} .
$$
As $x \to 1^-$ we have $\sqrt[3]{x - 1} \to 0^-$, so $f'(x) \to -\infty$; as $x \to 1^+$ we have $f'(x) \to +\infty$. Since the one-sided limits of the derivative are infinite with opposite signs, the point $x = 1$ is a **cusp**. At an inflection point with vertical tangent the two limits are infinite with the same sign.
