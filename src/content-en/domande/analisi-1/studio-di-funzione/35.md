What is the absolute minimum of the function $f(x) = x - 2\sin x$ on the interval $[0, \pi]$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3} - 1$
- $-\sqrt{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on the closed and bounded interval $[0, \pi]$, so by the **Weierstrass theorem** the absolute minimum exists. The derivative $f'(x) = 1 - 2\cos x$ vanishes for $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, in $(0, \pi)$, only for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The values at the candidates are
$$
f(0) = 0, \qquad f\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3}, \qquad f(\pi) = \pi .
$$
Since $\dfrac{\pi}{3} \approx 1.05$ and $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.73$, the value at $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is negative, so the **absolute minimum** is $\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3}$.
