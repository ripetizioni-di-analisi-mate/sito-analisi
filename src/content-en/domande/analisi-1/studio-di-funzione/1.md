For the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 3}{x - 2}$, what is the oblique asymptote?

- $y = x - 2$
- $y = x + 2$
- $y = x$
- $y = 2$

## Explanation

The slope of the asymptote is
$$
m = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x^2 - 3}{x^2 - 2x} = 1 .
$$
The intercept is
$$
q = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \bigl(f(x) - x\bigr) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x^2 - 3 - x^2 + 2x}{x - 2} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2x - 3}{x - 2} = 2 .
$$
Since both limits are finite, the line $y = x + 2$ is an **oblique asymptote** both as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$.
