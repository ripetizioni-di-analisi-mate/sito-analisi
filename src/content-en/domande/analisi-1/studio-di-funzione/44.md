What are the abscissas of the inflection points of the function $f(x) = e^{-x^2}$?

- $x = \pm 1$
- $x = 0$
- $x = \pm\sqrt{2}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

## Explanation

By the chain rule $f'(x) = -2x\,e^{-x^2}$ and, by the product rule,
$$
f''(x) = -2e^{-x^2} + 4x^2 e^{-x^2} = 2(2x^2 - 1)\,e^{-x^2} .
$$
Since $e^{-x^2} > 0$, the second derivative vanishes for $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and changes sign at both points. Therefore the **inflection points** have abscissas $x = \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The point $x = 0$ is stationary with $f''(0) = -2 < 0$, so it is a relative maximum point.
