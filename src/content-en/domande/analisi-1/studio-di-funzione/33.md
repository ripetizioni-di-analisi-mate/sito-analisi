A function $f$ differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ has derivative $f'(x) = x^2(x - 1)$. Which statement about its stationary points is true?

- $x = 0$ is a relative maximum point and $x = 1$ is a relative minimum point
- $x = 1$ is a relative minimum point and $x = 0$ is neither a maximum nor a minimum point
- $x = 0$ and $x = 1$ are both relative minimum points
- $x = 0$ is a relative minimum point and $x = 1$ is neither a maximum nor a minimum point

## Explanation

The stationary points are the zeros of $f'$, that is, $x = 0$ and $x = 1$. The factor $x^2$ is positive for $x \neq 0$, so away from $0$ the sign of $f'$ is that of $x - 1$, negative for $x < 1$ and positive for $x > 1$. Since $f'$ is negative both to the left and to the right of $0$, by the **first derivative test** the point $x = 0$ is neither a maximum nor a minimum point. At $x = 1$ the derivative changes from negative to positive, so $x = 1$ is a **relative minimum point**.
