How does the function $f(x) = \dfrac{e^{x}}{x}$ behave near the point $x = 0$, which is excluded from the domain?

- $f(x) \to +\infty$ both as $x \to 0^-$ and as $x \to 0^+$
- $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to 0^-$ and $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to 0^+$
- $f(x) \to 1$, so $x = 0$ is not a vertical asymptote
- $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to 0^-$ and $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to 0^+$

## Explanation

As $x \to 0$ the numerator $e^{x}$ tends to $1$, while the denominator tends to $0$. As $x \to 0^-$ the denominator is negative, so the quotient tends to $-\infty$; as $x \to 0^+$ it is positive, so the quotient tends to $+\infty$. Therefore the line $x = 0$ is a **vertical asymptote** on both sides, with the graph going down on the left and up on the right.
