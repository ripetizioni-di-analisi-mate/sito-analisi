On which set is the function $f(x) = \dfrac{(x - 1)\,e^{x}}{x + 2}$ positive?

- $(-2, 1)$
- $(1, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, -2) \cup (1, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, -2] \cup [1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The domain is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{-2\}$. Since $e^{x} > 0$ for every $x$, the sign of $f$ is that of $\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 2}$. The numerator is positive for $x > 1$ and the denominator for $x > -2$, so the quotient is positive where the two have the same sign, that is, for $x < -2$ and for $x > 1$. Therefore $f(x) > 0$ on $(-\infty, -2) \cup (1, +\infty)$; the point $x = 1$ is excluded because $f(1) = 0$, and the point $x = -2$ because it does not belong to the domain.
