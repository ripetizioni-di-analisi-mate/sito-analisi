Which statement about the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^3}{3} - x^2 + x$ is true?

- It has a relative minimum point at $x = 1$
- It has a relative maximum point at $x = 1$
- It is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$
- It is decreasing on $[0, 1]$ and increasing on $[1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The derivative is $f'(x) = x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2$, which vanishes only at $x = 1$ and is positive at every other point. Hence $f'(x) > 0$ at the interior points of $(-\infty, 1]$ and of $[1, +\infty)$, and by the monotonicity test $f$ is strictly increasing on both intervals. Since the two intervals share the point $1$, the function is **strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$**. The point $x = 1$ is stationary but is neither a maximum nor a minimum point, because $f'$ has the same sign on both sides.
