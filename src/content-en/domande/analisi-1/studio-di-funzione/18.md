What are the horizontal asymptotes of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{3e^{x} + 1}{e^{x} + 2}$?

- Only $y = 3$
- $y = 3$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = 1$ as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = 3$ as $x \to -\infty$
- $y = 3$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to -\infty$

## Explanation

Dividing numerator and denominator by $e^{x}$ we get
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{3 + e^{-x}}{1 + 2e^{-x}} = 3 ,
$$
because $e^{-x} \to 0$ as $x \to +\infty$. As $x \to -\infty$ we have $e^{x} \to 0$, so $f(x) \to \dfrac{0 + 1}{0 + 2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore the line $y = 3$ is a **horizontal asymptote** as $x \to +\infty$ and the line $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is a horizontal asymptote as $x \to -\infty$.
