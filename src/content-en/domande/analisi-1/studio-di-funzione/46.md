Which set of real numbers is the domain of $\ln\!\left(x^2 - 5x + 6\right)$?

- $(-\infty, 2] \cup [3, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, 2) \cup (3, +\infty)$
- $(2, 3)$
- $(-\infty, -3) \cup (-2, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The logarithm exists when its argument is strictly positive, so we require $x^2 - 5x + 6 > 0$. Since $x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)$, the trinomial vanishes at $2$ and at $3$ and is positive outside the interval between the roots. Therefore the domain is $(-\infty, 2) \cup (3, +\infty)$, with the endpoints $2$ and $3$ excluded because the argument equals $0$ there.
