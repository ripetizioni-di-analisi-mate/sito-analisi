Where does the function $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x^2}\,(x - 5)$ have a relative maximum?

- $x = 2$
- The function has no relative maximum points
- $x = 5$
- $x = 0$

## Explanation

We write $f(x) = x^{\frac{5}{3}} - 5x^{\frac{2}{3}}$, so for $x \neq 0$
$$
f'(x) = \frac{5}{3}\,x^{\frac{2}{3}} - \frac{10}{3}\,x^{-\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{5(x - 2)}{3\sqrt[3]{x}} .
$$
The derivative does not exist at $x = 0$, which must therefore be added to the candidates. For $x < 0$ numerator and denominator are negative and $f' > 0$; for $0 < x < 2$ we have $f' < 0$; for $x > 2$ we have $f' > 0$. The **first derivative test** does not require differentiability at the point, so $x = 0$ is a **relative maximum point**, with $f(0) = 0$, and $x = 2$ is a relative minimum point.
