For the function $f(x) = x^2 + \dfrac{2}{x}$, what is the exact set of domain points where $f^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0$?

- $(0, +\infty)$
- $\left[-\sqrt[3]{2}, 0\right)$
- $\left(-\infty, -\sqrt[3]{2}\right]$ and $(0, +\infty)$
- $\left(-\infty, -\sqrt[3]{2}\right]$ and $\left[\sqrt[3]{2}, +\infty\right)$

## Explanation

The derivatives are $f'(x) = 2x - \dfrac{2}{x^2}$ and $f''(x) = 2 + \dfrac{4}{x^3} = \dfrac{2(x^3 + 2)}{x^3}$. The numerator is positive for $x > -\sqrt[3]{2}$ and the denominator is positive for $x > 0$, so $f''(x) \geq 0$ for $x \leq -\sqrt[3]{2}$, where the numerator is negative or zero and the denominator is negative, and for $x > 0$. By the characterization of convexity through the second derivative, $f$ is **convex** on $\left(-\infty, -\sqrt[3]{2}\right]$ and on $(0, +\infty)$, and concave on $\left[-\sqrt[3]{2}, 0\right)$.
