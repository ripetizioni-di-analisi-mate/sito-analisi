The graph of $y = \sin x$ is compared with the line $y = \dfrac{x}{10}$. At how many points do they meet?

- Three
- Six
- Seven
- Nine

## Explanation

Since $|\sin x| \leq 1$, the solutions satisfy $\left|\dfrac{x}{10}\right| \leq 1$, that is, $-10 \leq x \leq 10$. Both functions are odd, so the solutions are $x = 0$ and pairs $\pm x_0$, and it is enough to count the positive ones. For $0 < x \leq 10$ the line $y = \dfrac{x}{10}$ has positive ordinate, so it meets the sine only on the arches where $\sin x > 0$; on each of them the sine is concave, therefore the difference $\sin x - \dfrac{x}{10}$ is concave and vanishes at most twice. On the arch $(0, \pi)$ the difference vanishes once, because $\sin x > \dfrac{x}{10}$ near $0$ and $\sin \pi = 0 < \dfrac{\pi}{10}$; on the arch $(2\pi, 3\pi)$ it vanishes twice, because at $\dfrac{5\pi}{2} \approx 7.85$ the sine equals $1$, greater than $\dfrac{x}{10} \approx 0.785$, while at the endpoints of the arch the difference is negative; the next positive arch begins at $4\pi > 10$. There are therefore three positive solutions, and $2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 7$ solutions in all.
