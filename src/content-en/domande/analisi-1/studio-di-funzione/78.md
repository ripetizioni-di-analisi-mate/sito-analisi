Consider the equation $x^3 - 3x^2 = k$, with $k$ a real parameter. In which case does it have three distinct real roots?

- $-4 \leq k \leq 0$
- $-4 < k < 0$
- $0 < k < 2$
- $0 < k < 4$

## Explanation

Let $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2$. The derivative $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x = 3x(x - 2)$ is positive for $x < 0$ and for $x > 2$, negative for $0 < x < 2$. Hence $f$ increases from $-\infty$ up to the relative maximum $f(0) = 0$, decreases down to the relative minimum $f(2) = 8 - 12 = -4$ and then increases towards $+\infty$. The line $y = k$ meets the graph at three distinct points when it cuts all three monotonic pieces, that is, for $-4 < k < 0$. For $k = 0$ and for $k = -4$ the line is tangent at a relative extremum and there are two distinct solutions.
