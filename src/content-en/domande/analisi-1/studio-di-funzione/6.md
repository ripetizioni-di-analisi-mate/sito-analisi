For the function $f(x) = x^2 \ln x$, defined for $x > 0$, which point is a relative minimum point?

- $x = \dfrac{1}{e}$
- $x = 1$
- $x = e^{-\frac{3}{2}}$
- $x = e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$

## Explanation

By the product rule, $f'(x) = 2x \ln x + x^2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} = x\,(2 \ln x + 1)$. Since $x > 0$ in the domain, the sign of $f'$ is that of $2 \ln x + 1$, which is positive for $\ln x > -\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, for $x > e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$. Therefore $f$ decreases on $\left(0, e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right]$ and increases on $\left[e^{-\frac{1}{2}}, +\infty\right)$. By the **first derivative test**, $x = e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ is a relative minimum point, with $f\!\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{2e}$.
