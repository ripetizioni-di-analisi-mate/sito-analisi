For which real $x$ is the expression $\sqrt{\dfrac{x}{x - 2}}$ meaningful?

- $(-\infty, 0) \cup (2, +\infty)$
- $[0, 2)$
- $(-\infty, 0] \cup (2, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, 0] \cup [2, +\infty)$

## Explanation

There are two conditions for existence, to be satisfied together:
$$
\begin{cases} x - 2 \neq 0 \\[4pt] \dfrac{x}{x - 2} \geq 0 . \end{cases}
$$
The quotient vanishes at $x = 0$ and is positive where numerator and denominator have the same sign, that is, for $x < 0$ and for $x > 2$. Therefore the domain is $(-\infty, 0] \cup (2, +\infty)$, with $0$ included because the radicand may be zero and $2$ excluded because it makes the denominator vanish.
