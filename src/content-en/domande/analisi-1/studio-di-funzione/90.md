What happens at the point $x = 0$ to the function $f(x) = \sqrt{1 - \cos x}$?

- It is differentiable and $f'(0) = 0$
- It has a corner point, with left derivative $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and right derivative $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- It has a cusp
- It has a corner point, with left derivative $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and right derivative $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

By the half-angle formula $1 - \cos x = 2\sin^2 \dfrac{x}{2}$, so $f(x) = \sqrt{2}\,\left|\sin \dfrac{x}{2}\right|$. The difference quotient at $0$ is
$$
\frac{f(h)}{h} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{\left|\sin \frac{h}{2}\right|}{\frac{h}{2}} ,
$$
and since $\dfrac{\sin t}{t} \to 1$, it tends to $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ as $h \to 0^+$ and to $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ as $h \to 0^-$. The one-sided derivatives are finite and different, therefore $x = 0$ is a **corner point**.
