At the point $x = 0$, what can be said about the differentiability of the product $f(x) = x\,|x|$?

- It has a corner point
- It is differentiable and $f'(0) = 0$
- It is not differentiable, because $|x|$ is not differentiable at $0$
- It is differentiable and $f'(0) = 1$

## Explanation

The difference quotient at $0$ is
$$
\frac{f(h) - f(0)}{h} = \frac{h\,|h|}{h} = |h| ,
$$
which tends to $0$ both as $h \to 0^-$ and as $h \to 0^+$. Hence $f$ is **differentiable** at $0$ with $f'(0) = 0$. The fact that $|x|$ is not differentiable at $0$ is not enough to conclude, because the product with the factor $x$, which vanishes at $0$, makes the difference quotient converge.
