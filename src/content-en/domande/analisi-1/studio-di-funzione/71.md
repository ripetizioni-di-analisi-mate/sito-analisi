What is the minimum distance from the point $P = (0, 2)$ to the points of the parabola $y = x^2$?

- $\dfrac{7}{4}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$

## Explanation

The square of the distance from $P$ to the point $\left(x, x^2\right)$ of the parabola is
$$
d^2(x) = x^2 + \left(x^2 - 2\right)^2 = x^4 - 3x^2 + 4 ,
$$
and the distance is smallest where $d^2$ is smallest, because the square root is increasing. The derivative $\left(d^2\right)'(x) = 4x^3 - 6x = 2x\left(2x^2 - 3\right)$ vanishes at $0$ and at $\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}$, and it is negative for $0 < x < \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}$ and positive for $x > \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}$; the function is even, so the points $\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}$ are absolute minimum points. There $d^2 = \dfrac{9}{4} - \dfrac{9}{2} + 4 = \dfrac{7}{4}$, therefore the minimum distance is $\sqrt{\dfrac{7}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$, smaller than the distance $2$ from the vertex.
