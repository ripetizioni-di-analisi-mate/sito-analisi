The range of $\dfrac{x^2}{x - 1}$ is not the whole of $\mathbb{R}$. Which values of $k$ make the equation $\dfrac{x^2}{x - 1} = k$ impossible?

- $0 \leq k \leq 4$
- $0 < k < 2$
- $k > 4$
- $0 < k < 4$

## Explanation

Let $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{x - 1}$, defined for $x \neq 1$. By the quotient rule $f'(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 2x}{(x - 1)^2} = \dfrac{x(x - 2)}{(x - 1)^2}$, so on $(-\infty, 1)$ the function has relative maximum $f(0) = 0$ and on $(1, +\infty)$ relative minimum $f(2) = 4$. Since $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$ and as $x \to 1^-$, on $(-\infty, 1)$ the range is $(-\infty, 0]$; since $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to 1^+$ and as $x \to +\infty$, on $(1, +\infty)$ the range is $[4, +\infty)$. Therefore the equation has no solutions exactly for $0 < k < 4$.
