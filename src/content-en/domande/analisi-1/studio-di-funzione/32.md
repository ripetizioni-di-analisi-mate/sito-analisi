Which point of the open interval $(0, 2\pi)$ is a relative maximum point of the function $f(x) = \sin x + \cos x$?

- $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The derivative $f'(x) = \cos x - \sin x$ vanishes for $\sin x = \cos x$, that is, in $(0, 2\pi)$, for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$. The second derivative is $f''(x) = -\sin x - \cos x$, with $f''\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = -\sqrt{2} < 0$ and $f''\!\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{4}\right) = \sqrt{2} > 0$. By the **second derivative test**, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is a relative maximum point, with $f\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \sqrt{2}$, and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ is a relative minimum point.
