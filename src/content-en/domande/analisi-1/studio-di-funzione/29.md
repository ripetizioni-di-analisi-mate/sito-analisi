For the function $f(x) = \cos x + \dfrac{x^2}{2}$, what can be said about the point $x = 0$?

- It is a relative minimum point
- It is a relative maximum point
- It is a horizontal inflection point
- It is not a stationary point

## Explanation

The derivatives are $f'(x) = x - \sin x$, $f''(x) = 1 - \cos x$, $f'''(x) = \sin x$ and $f^{(4)}(x) = \cos x$. At $x = 0$ we have $f'(0) = f''(0) = f'''(0) = 0$ and $f^{(4)}(0) = 1$, so the second derivative test does not decide. By the **higher-order derivative test**, with $n = 4$ even and $f^{(4)}(0) > 0$, the point $x = 0$ is a **relative minimum point**. The same result follows from the sign of $f'(x) = x - \sin x$, which is negative for $x < 0$ and positive for $x > 0$.
