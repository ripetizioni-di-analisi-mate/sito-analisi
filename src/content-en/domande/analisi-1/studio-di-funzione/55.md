Which statement about the behavior of $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ as $x \to +\infty$ is true?

- The line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote and the graph never crosses it
- The line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote and the graph crosses it at infinitely many points
- There is no horizontal asymptote, because $\sin x$ has no limit as $x \to +\infty$
- The line $y = 1$ is a horizontal asymptote

## Explanation

For $x > 0$ we have $-\dfrac{1}{x} \leq \dfrac{\sin x}{x} \leq \dfrac{1}{x}$, and both bounds tend to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$. By the **squeeze theorem** we therefore have $f(x) \to 0$, and the line $y = 0$ is a horizontal asymptote. Moreover $f(k\pi) = 0$ for every integer $k \geq 1$, so the graph crosses the asymptote at infinitely many points. The limit $1$ is the one as $x \to 0$ and does not concern the behavior at infinity.
