At the point $x = 0$ the function $f(x) = \sqrt{|x|}$ is continuous but not differentiable. What kind of point is it?

- A cusp
- A corner point
- A point of inflection with vertical tangent
- A point where $f$ is differentiable with $f'(0) = 0$

## Explanation

The difference quotient at $0$ is $\dfrac{\sqrt{|h|}}{h}$. As $h \to 0^+$ it equals $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{h}} \to +\infty$, as $h \to 0^-$ it equals $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{|h|}} \to -\infty$. The one-sided derivatives are therefore infinite and of opposite sign, and the function is continuous at $0$. Therefore $x = 0$ is a **cusp**, with vertical tangent $x = 0$ and the graph coming down from above on both sides.
