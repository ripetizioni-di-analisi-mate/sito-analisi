On which intervals is the function $f(x) = x\,e^{\frac{1}{x}}$ strictly increasing?

- $[1, +\infty)$
- $(0, 1]$
- $(-\infty, 0)$ and $[1, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, 0)$ and $(0, 1]$

## Explanation

The domain is $(-\infty, 0) \cup (0, +\infty)$. By the product rule and the chain rule,
$$
f'(x) = e^{\frac{1}{x}} + x\,e^{\frac{1}{x}} \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{x^2}\right) = e^{\frac{1}{x}}\,\frac{x - 1}{x} .
$$
Since $e^{\frac{1}{x}} > 0$, the sign of $f'$ is that of $\dfrac{x - 1}{x}$, which is positive for $x < 0$ and for $x > 1$, and negative for $0 < x < 1$. By the **monotonicity test**, applied separately to each interval, $f$ is strictly increasing on $(-\infty, 0)$ and on $[1, +\infty)$.
