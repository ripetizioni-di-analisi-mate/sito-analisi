Which vertical lines are asymptotes of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin x}{x^2 - x}$?

- $x = 0$ and $x = 1$
- Only $x = 0$
- No vertical line
- Only $x = 1$

## Explanation

The denominator $x(x - 1)$ vanishes at $0$ and at $1$, which are excluded from the domain. By the fundamental limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$ we have
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} \cdot \frac{1}{x - 1} = -1 ,
$$
so at $0$ the limit is finite and the line $x = 0$ is not an asymptote. At $x = 1$ the numerator tends to $\sin 1 > 0$ and the denominator tends to $0$, so $f(x) \to \pm\infty$ as $x \to 1^{\pm}$. Therefore the only **vertical asymptote** is the line $x = 1$.
