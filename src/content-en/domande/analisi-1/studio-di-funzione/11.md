At which points does the function $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 1$ attain its absolute minimum on the interval $[0, 5]$?

- $x = 0$ and $x = 3$
- Only $x = 3$
- Only $x = 0$
- $x = 1$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on the closed and bounded interval $[0, 5]$, so by the **Weierstrass theorem** the absolute minimum exists. The derivative $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9 = 3(x - 1)(x - 3)$ vanishes at $1$ and at $3$, which are the interior stationary points. The values at the candidates are
$$
f(0) = 1, \qquad f(1) = 5, \qquad f(3) = 1, \qquad f(5) = 21 .
$$
The smallest value is $1$, so the **absolute minimum** is attained both at $x = 0$ and at $x = 3$.
