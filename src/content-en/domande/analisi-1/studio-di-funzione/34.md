What is the absolute minimum of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{x}\,(x - 3)$ on its domain $[0, +\infty)$?

- $-2$
- $0$
- $-3$
- $1$

## Explanation

For $x > 0$, by the product rule,
$$
f'(x) = \frac{x - 3}{2\sqrt{x}} + \sqrt{x} = \frac{x - 3 + 2x}{2\sqrt{x}} = \frac{3(x - 1)}{2\sqrt{x}} .
$$
The derivative is negative for $0 < x < 1$ and positive for $x > 1$, so $f$ decreases on $[0, 1]$ and increases on $[1, +\infty)$. Therefore $f(x) \geq f(1)$ for every $x \geq 0$, and the **absolute minimum** is $f(1) = 1 \cdot (1 - 3) = -2$. The function has no absolute maximum, because $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$.
