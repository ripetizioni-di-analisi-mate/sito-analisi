On the closed interval $[0, 9]$ consider $f(x) = x - 2\sqrt{x}$. What is its absolute maximum?

- $-1$
- $9$
- $0$
- $3$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on $[0, 9]$, so by the **extreme value theorem** it has an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum. For $x > 0$ we have $f'(x) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$, which vanishes only at $x = 1$; at $x = 0$ the function is not differentiable, but $0$ is an endpoint of the interval. The candidates are therefore $0$, $1$ and $9$, with
$$
f(0) = 0, \qquad f(1) = -1, \qquad f(9) = 9 - 6 = 3 .
$$
Therefore the absolute maximum is $3$, attained at the endpoint $x = 9$, while the stationary point $x = 1$ gives the absolute minimum $-1$.
