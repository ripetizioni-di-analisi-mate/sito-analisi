At which points is the function $f(x) = \left|x^3 - x\right|$ not differentiable?

- Only at $x = -1$ and $x = 1$
- Only at $x = 0$
- At $x = -1$, $x = 0$ and $x = 1$
- The function is differentiable at every point

## Explanation

Let $g(x) = x^3 - x = x(x - 1)(x + 1)$. Where $g(x) \neq 0$ the function $|g|$ coincides with $g$ or with $-g$ in a neighborhood, so it is differentiable. At a zero $x_0$ of $g$ with $g'(x_0) \neq 0$ the one-sided derivatives of $|g|$ equal $-|g'(x_0)|$ and $|g'(x_0)|$, which are different, so $x_0$ is a corner point. Since $g'(x) = 3x^2 - 1$ equals $2$ at $\pm 1$ and $-1$ at $0$, all three zeros are **corner points** and $f$ is not differentiable at $x = -1$, $x = 0$ and $x = 1$.
