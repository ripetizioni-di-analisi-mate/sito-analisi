For the function $f(x) = x - \dfrac{1}{x}$, defined for $x \neq 0$, which statement is true?

- It is strictly increasing on its whole domain
- It is strictly increasing on $(-\infty, 0)$ and on $(0, +\infty)$, and it is not monotonic on its domain
- It is strictly decreasing on $(0, +\infty)$
- It has a stationary point at $x = 1$

## Explanation

The derivative $f'(x) = 1 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ is positive at every point of the domain. The monotonicity test applies on an interval, so $f$ is strictly increasing on $(-\infty, 0)$ and on $(0, +\infty)$ separately. On the domain the function is not increasing, because $-1 < \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $f(-1) = 0 > -\dfrac{3}{2} = f\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$. Nor is it decreasing, because $\dfrac{1}{2} < 1$ and $f\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) < 0 = f(1)$. Therefore $f$ is **not monotonic** on its domain.
