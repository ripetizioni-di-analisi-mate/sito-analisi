On which interval is the function $f(x) = x\sqrt{x + 3}$ decreasing?

- $[-3, -2]$
- $(-\infty, -2]$
- $[-2, +\infty)$
- $\left[-3, -\dfrac{3}{2}\right]$

## Explanation

The radicand must be nonnegative, so the domain is $[-3, +\infty)$. For $x > -3$, by the product rule and the derivative of the square root,
$$
f'(x) = \sqrt{x + 3} + \frac{x}{2\sqrt{x + 3}} = \frac{2(x + 3) + x}{2\sqrt{x + 3}} = \frac{3(x + 2)}{2\sqrt{x + 3}} .
$$
The denominator is positive, so $f'(x) < 0$ for $-3 < x < -2$ and $f'(x) > 0$ for $x > -2$. Since $f$ is continuous at $-3$, by the **monotonicity test** the function is decreasing on $[-3, -2]$.
