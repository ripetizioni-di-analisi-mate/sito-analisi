What is the oblique asymptote of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 4x}$ as $x \to +\infty$?

- $y = x + 2$
- $y = x + 4$
- $y = -x - 2$
- $y = x$

## Explanation

The slope is $m = \displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x}}{x} = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \sqrt{1 + \frac{4}{x}} = 1$. The intercept is obtained by rationalizing, and equals
$$
q = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \bigl(\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x\bigr) = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{4x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x} = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{4}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{4}{x}} + 1} = 2 .
$$
Since both limits are finite, the line $y = x + 2$ is an **oblique asymptote** as $x \to +\infty$. As $x \to -\infty$, instead, $m = -1$, and the oblique asymptote is the line $y = -x - 2$.
