The graph of the cube root $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$ passes through the origin. How is the point $x = 0$ classified?

- A cusp
- A point of inflection with vertical tangent
- A corner point
- A point of inflection with horizontal tangent

## Explanation

The difference quotient at $0$ is $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{h}}{h} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{h^2}}$, which tends to $+\infty$ both as $h \to 0^-$ and as $h \to 0^+$. Hence the function is not differentiable at $0$ and the graph has at $(0, 0)$ the **vertical tangent** $x = 0$, with both one-sided derivatives infinite and of the same sign. For $x \neq 0$ we have $f''(x) = -\dfrac{2}{9\,x\sqrt[3]{x^2}}$, positive for $x < 0$ and negative for $x > 0$. The function is continuous at $0$ and changes concavity, therefore $0$ is a **point of inflection with vertical tangent**.
