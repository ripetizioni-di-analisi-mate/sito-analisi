Let $f(x) = x^2 \sin \dfrac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, with $f(0) = 0$. What can be said about $f$ at the point $0$?

- $f$ is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 0$
- $f$ is not differentiable at $0$, because $\lim\limits_{x \to 0} f'(x)$ does not exist
- $f$ is differentiable at $0$ and $f'(0) = 1$
- $f$ is not continuous at $0$

## Explanation

The difference quotient at $0$ is $\dfrac{h^2 \sin\frac{1}{h}}{h} = h \sin \dfrac{1}{h}$, and since $\left|h \sin \dfrac{1}{h}\right| \leq |h|$, by the squeeze theorem it tends to $0$. Hence $f$ is **differentiable** at $0$ with $f'(0) = 0$. For $x \neq 0$ we have $f'(x) = 2x \sin \dfrac{1}{x} - \cos \dfrac{1}{x}$, which has no limit as $x \to 0$. The derivative at $0$ is defined by the difference quotient, so the absence of the limit of $f'$ does not prevent differentiability; therefore $f'$ exists at every point but is not continuous at $0$.
