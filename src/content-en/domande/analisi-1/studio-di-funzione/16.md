Which statement describes the function $f(x) = (x - 2)\,e^{x}$ on the interval $[0, 1]$?

- It is strictly decreasing and convex
- It is strictly decreasing and concave
- It is strictly increasing and convex
- It is strictly increasing and concave

## Explanation

By the product rule $f'(x) = e^{x} + (x - 2)\,e^{x} = (x - 1)\,e^{x}$ and, differentiating again, $f''(x) = e^{x} + (x - 1)\,e^{x} = x\,e^{x}$. Since $e^{x} > 0$, the sign of $f'$ is that of $x - 1$ and the sign of $f''$ is that of $x$. Therefore at the interior points of $[0, 1]$ we have $f'(x) < 0$ and $f''(x) > 0$. By the **monotonicity test** $f$ is strictly decreasing on $[0, 1]$ and, by the characterization of convexity through the second derivative, it is **convex** on $[0, 1]$. The two endpoints of the interval are key points of the sign chart, because at $x = 0$ we have a zero of $f''$ and at $x = 1$ we have a zero of $f'$.
