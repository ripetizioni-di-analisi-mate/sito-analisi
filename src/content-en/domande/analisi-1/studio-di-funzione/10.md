What asymptotes does the function $f(x) = \ln x - x$ have?

- No asymptotes
- The vertical asymptote $x = 0$ and the oblique asymptote $y = -x$
- Only the oblique asymptote $y = -x$
- Only the vertical asymptote $x = 0$

## Explanation

The domain is $(0, +\infty)$. Since $\ln x \to -\infty$ as $x \to 0^+$, also $f(x) \to -\infty$, so the line $x = 0$ is a **vertical asymptote** from the right. As $x \to +\infty$ we have
$$
\frac{f(x)}{x} = \frac{\ln x}{x} - 1 \to -1
$$
by the hierarchy of infinities. However, $f(x) - (-x) = \ln x \to +\infty$, so the limit that gives the intercept is not finite and the oblique asymptote does not exist.
