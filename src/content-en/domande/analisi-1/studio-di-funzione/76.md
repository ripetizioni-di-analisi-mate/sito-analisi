The polynomial equation $x^5 + x - 3 = 0$ has degree five. How many real roots does it have?

- One
- None
- Three
- Five

## Explanation

Let $g(x) = x^5 + x - 3$. The derivative $g'(x) = 5x^4 + 1$ is positive for every $x$, so $g$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$ and vanishes at most once. Moreover $g(1) = -1 < 0$ and $g(2) = 31 > 0$, therefore by the **intermediate value theorem for zeros** $g$ vanishes at a point of $(1, 2)$. The equation thus has **exactly one** real solution.
