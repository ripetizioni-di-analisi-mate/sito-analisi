What is the ordinate of the relative maximum point of the graph of $f(x) = x\,e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}}$?

- $1$
- $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$

## Explanation

By the product rule and the chain rule
$$
f'(x) = e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}} + x \cdot (-x)\,e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}} = \left(1 - x^2\right) e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}} .
$$
The exponential is positive, so $f'$ has the sign of $1 - x^2$, and it is negative for $x < -1$, positive for $-1 < x < 1$, negative for $x > 1$. By the **first derivative test** $x = 1$ is a relative maximum point and $x = -1$ a relative minimum point. The relative maximum value is $f(1) = e^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$.
