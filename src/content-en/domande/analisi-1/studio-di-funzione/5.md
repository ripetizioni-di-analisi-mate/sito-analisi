On which set is the function $f(x) = \ln\!\left(x^2 + 1\right)$ convex?

- $(-\infty, -1]$ and $[1, +\infty)$
- $[-1, 1]$
- $[0, +\infty)$
- $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

The first derivative is $f'(x) = \dfrac{2x}{x^2 + 1}$, and by the quotient rule
$$
f''(x) = \frac{2(x^2 + 1) - 2x \cdot 2x}{(x^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{2(1 - x^2)}{(x^2 + 1)^2} .
$$
The denominator is positive, so $f''(x) \geq 0$ if and only if $1 - x^2 \geq 0$, that is, for $-1 \leq x \leq 1$. By the characterization of convexity through the second derivative, $f$ is **convex** on $[-1, 1]$ and concave on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[1, +\infty)$.
