The equation $x\,e^{x} = 1$ has exactly one real solution. Which interval does it belong to?

- $\left(0, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- $(-1, 0)$
- $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right)$
- $(1, 2)$

## Explanation

Let $g(x) = x\,e^{x} - 1$. For $x \leq 0$ we have $x\,e^{x} \leq 0$, so $g(x) < 0$ and no solution is less than or equal to $0$. For $x > 0$ the derivative $g'(x) = (x + 1)\,e^{x}$ is positive, therefore $g$ is strictly increasing on $[0, +\infty)$ and vanishes at most once. We have $g\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{e}}{2} - 1 < 0$, because $\sqrt{e} < 2$, while $g(1) = e - 1 > 0$. By the **intermediate value theorem for zeros** the only solution therefore belongs to the interval $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right)$.
