What is the absolute maximum of the function $f(x) = x^2 e^{-x}$ on the interval $[-1, 4]$?

- $\dfrac{4}{e^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{e}$
- $e$
- $\dfrac{16}{e^4}$

## Explanation

The function is continuous on the closed and bounded interval $[-1, 4]$, so by the **Weierstrass theorem** the absolute maximum exists. The derivative $f'(x) = 2x\,e^{-x} - x^2 e^{-x} = x(2 - x)\,e^{-x}$ vanishes at $0$ and at $2$. The values at the candidates are
$$
f(-1) = e, \qquad f(0) = 0, \qquad f(2) = \frac{4}{e^2}, \qquad f(4) = \frac{16}{e^4} .
$$
Since $e > 2$, we have $e^2 > 4$ and $e^4 > 16$, so $\dfrac{4}{e^2} < 1 < e$ and $\dfrac{16}{e^4} < 1 < e$. Therefore the **absolute maximum** is $e$, attained at the endpoint $x = -1$.
