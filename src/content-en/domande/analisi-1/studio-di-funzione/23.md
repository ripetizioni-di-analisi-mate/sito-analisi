What are the stationary points of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 + 3}{x + 1}$?

- $x = -3$ and $x = 1$
- $x = 3$ and $x = -1$
- Only $x = 1$
- $x = -3$, $x = -1$ and $x = 1$

## Explanation

The domain is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1\}$. By the quotient rule,
$$
f'(x) = \frac{2x(x + 1) - (x^2 + 3)}{(x + 1)^2} = \frac{x^2 + 2x - 3}{(x + 1)^2} = \frac{(x + 3)(x - 1)}{(x + 1)^2} .
$$
The derivative vanishes where the numerator vanishes, that is, for $x = -3$ and for $x = 1$, both in the domain. Therefore the **stationary points** are $x = -3$ and $x = 1$; the point $x = -1$ makes the denominator zero and does not belong to the domain.
