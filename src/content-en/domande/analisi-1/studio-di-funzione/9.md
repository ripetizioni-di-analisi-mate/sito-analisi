For the function $f(x) = |x - 1|\,e^{x}$, what are the inflection points?

- $x = 1$
- $x = -1$
- $x = -1$ and $x = 1$
- $x = 0$

## Explanation

For $x < 1$ we have $f(x) = (1 - x)\,e^{x}$, so $f'(x) = -x\,e^{x}$ and $f''(x) = -(x + 1)\,e^{x}$. The second derivative changes sign at $x = -1$, from positive to negative, so $x = -1$ is an **inflection point**. For $x > 1$ we have $f(x) = (x - 1)\,e^{x}$, $f'(x) = x\,e^{x}$ and $f''(x) = (x + 1)\,e^{x} > 0$, so at $x = 1$ the function changes from concave to convex. The one-sided derivatives at $x = 1$ are $-e$ and $e$, so $x = 1$ is a corner point. Since at a corner point the graph has no tangent line, $x = 1$ is not an inflection point.
