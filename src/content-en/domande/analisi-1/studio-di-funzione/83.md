Which value must be assigned at $x = 0$ to the function $f(x) = \dfrac{\sin(3x)}{x}$ so that the extension is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$?

- $3$
- $0$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

The extension is continuous if it is given at $0$ the value of the limit. We write
$$
\frac{\sin(3x)}{x} = 3 \cdot \frac{\sin(3x)}{3x} ,
$$
and since $3x \to 0$ as $x \to 0$, by the **standard limit** $\dfrac{\sin t}{t} \to 1$ we get $\lim\limits_{x \to 0} f(x) = 3$. Therefore the value to assign is $3$, and the point $x = 0$ is a removable discontinuity.
