The equation $y' - y = y^2$ is a Bernoulli equation. On which maximal interval is its solution satisfying $y(0) = 1$ defined?

- $\mathbb{R}$
- $(\ln 2, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, \ln 2)$
- $(-\infty, \ln 2]$

## Explanation

The equation is a Bernoulli equation with $\alpha = 2$. Since $y(0) = 1 \neq 0$, near $x = 0$ we use the substitution $z = \dfrac{1}{y}$, which gives the linear equation $z' + z = -1$ with general solution $z = -1 + C e^{-x}$. The condition $z(0) = 1$ gives $C = 2$, so
$$
y = \frac{1}{2e^{-x} - 1} .
$$
The denominator vanishes for $e^{-x} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, for $x = \ln 2$, and as $x \to (\ln 2)^-$ the solution tends to $+\infty$. The maximal interval is the largest interval containing $0$ on which the function is differentiable, hence it is $(-\infty, \ln 2)$.
