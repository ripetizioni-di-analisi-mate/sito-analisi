What is the general solution of the equation $y'' + 3y' = 0$?

- $y = C_1 + C_2 e^{3x}$
- $y = C_1 + C_2 e^{-3x}$
- $y = C_1 x + C_2 e^{-3x}$
- $y = \left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{-3x}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation is $r^2 + 3r = r (r + 3) = 0$, with the distinct real roots $r_1 = 0$ and $r_2 = -3$. The root $r_1 = 0$ corresponds to the solution $e^{0 \cdot x} = 1$, that is, the constant function. Therefore the general solution is $y = C_1 + C_2 e^{-3x}$ with $C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}$. The function $C_1 x$ is not a solution for $C_1 \neq 0$, because $\left( C_1 x \right)'' + 3 \left( C_1 x \right)' = 3 C_1$.
