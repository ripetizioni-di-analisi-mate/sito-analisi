For the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' = \dfrac{1}{2y} \\[8pt]
y(0) = 1
\end{cases}
$$
what is the interval of existence of the solution?

- $[-1, +\infty)$
- $(-1, +\infty)$
- $(0, +\infty)$
- $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

The equation is separable and, since $y(0) = 1 > 0$, we look for a solution with $y > 0$. Separating the variables we obtain
$$
\int 2y \, dy = \int dx \quad\implies\quad y^2 = x + c ,
$$
and the condition $y(0) = 1$ gives $c = 1$. Since $y > 0$, the solution is $y = \sqrt{x + 1}$. At $x = -1$ the function equals $0$, so at that point the right-hand side $\dfrac{1}{2y}$ is not defined. Therefore the interval of existence is the half-line $(-1, +\infty)$.
