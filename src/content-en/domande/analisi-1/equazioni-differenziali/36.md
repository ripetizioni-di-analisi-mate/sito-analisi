Which function is a particular solution of the equation $y'' - 3y' + 2y = 4 e^{x}$?

- $y = 4x e^{x}$
- $y = -2x e^{x}$
- $y = -4x e^{x}$
- $y = -4x^2 e^{x}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation $r^2 - 3r + 2 = (r - 1)(r - 2) = 0$ has the roots $1$ and $2$. The number associated with the forcing term $4 e^{x}$ is $\gamma = 1$, which is a simple root, so by the **resonance rule** we look for $y_p = a_0\, x e^{x}$. Since $y_p' = a_0 (1 + x)\, e^{x}$ and $y_p'' = a_0 (2 + x)\, e^{x}$, substituting we obtain
$$
a_0 e^{x} \bigl( (2 + x) - 3(1 + x) + 2x \bigr) = -a_0 e^{x} = 4 e^{x} ,
$$
so $a_0 = -4$. Therefore a particular solution is $y_p = -4x e^{x}$.
