What is the general solution of the equation $y'' + 6y' + 9y = 0$?

- $y = \left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{-3x}$
- $y = C_1 e^{-3x} + C_2 e^{-3x}$
- $y = \left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{3x}$
- $y = C_1 e^{3x} + C_2 e^{-3x}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation is $r^2 + 6r + 9 = 0$, with discriminant $\Delta = 36 - 36 = 0$. Since $r^2 + 6r + 9 = (r + 3)^2$, the root $r = -3$ is a real double root. Therefore the general solution is $y = \left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{-3x}$ with $C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}$. The combination $C_1 e^{-3x} + C_2 e^{-3x} = \left( C_1 + C_2 \right) e^{-3x}$ depends on a single constant, so it does not describe all the solutions.
