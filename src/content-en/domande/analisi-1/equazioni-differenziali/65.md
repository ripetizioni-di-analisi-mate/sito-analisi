A tank initially contains $100$ liters of pure water. From time $t = 0$, $2$ liters per minute of salt water, with $0.1$ kg of salt per liter, flow in, and $1$ liter per minute of the mixture, kept uniform, flows out. The volume at time $t$ is therefore $100 + t$ liters, and the amount of salt $Q(t)$, in kg with $t$ in minutes, satisfies
$$
Q' = 0.2 - \frac{Q}{100 + t}, \qquad Q(0) = 0 .
$$
How many kg of salt does the tank contain after $100$ minutes?

- $20$
- $15$
- $10$
- $20\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{e}\right)$

## Explanation

The equation reads $Q' + \dfrac{Q}{100 + t} = 0.2$ and is linear with $a(t) = \dfrac{1}{100 + t}$, continuous for $t \geq 0$. Since $100 + t > 0$, an antiderivative of $a$ is $A(t) = \ln(100 + t)$, so the integrating factor is $e^{A(t)} = 100 + t$. Multiplying gives
$$
\bigl((100 + t)\, Q\bigr)' = 0.2\,(100 + t) \quad\implies\quad (100 + t)\, Q = 0.1\,(100 + t)^2 + C .
$$
The condition $Q(0) = 0$ gives $0 = 1000 + C$, that is, $C = -1000$, therefore
$$
Q(t) = 0.1\,(100 + t) - \frac{1000}{100 + t} .
$$
For $t = 100$ we get $Q = 20 - 5 = 15$ kg. The value $20\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{e}\right)$ would be obtained with a constant volume of $100$ liters, whereas here the volume increases.
