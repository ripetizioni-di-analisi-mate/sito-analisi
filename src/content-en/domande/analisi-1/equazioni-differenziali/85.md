For the equation $y'' - y = 2 + e^{2x}$ a particular solution is found by adding the particular solutions for the two terms of the right-hand side. Which function is obtained?

- $y_p = 2 + e^{2x}$
- $y_p = -2 + \dfrac{e^{2x}}{5}$
- $y_p = 2 + \dfrac{e^{2x}}{3}$
- $y_p = -2 + \dfrac{e^{2x}}{3}$

## Explanation

The roots of $r^2 - 1 = 0$ are $\pm 1$, so neither $0$ nor $2$ is a root of the characteristic equation.

For the term $2$ we look for a constant $c$, and $-c = 2$ gives $c = -2$. For the term $e^{2x}$ we look for $a\, e^{2x}$, and $4a - a = 1$ gives $a = \dfrac{1}{3}$. By the **linearity** of the equation, the sum $y_p = -2 + \dfrac{e^{2x}}{3}$ is a particular solution.
