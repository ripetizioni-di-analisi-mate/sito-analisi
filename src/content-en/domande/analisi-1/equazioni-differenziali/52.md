The temperature $T(t)$ of a body, in degrees Celsius and with $t$ in minutes, satisfies the equation $T' = -k\,(T - 20)$ with $k > 0$ constant. If $T(0) = 80$ and $T(10) = 50$, what is $k$?

- $\dfrac{\ln 2}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}\ln\dfrac{8}{5}$
- $10 \ln 2$
- $\dfrac{\ln 3}{10}$

## Explanation

The equation is separable in the unknown $T$ and has the constant solution $T \equiv 20$. For $T \neq 20$, separating the variables gives $\ln\lvert T - 20 \rvert = -kt + c$, so
$$
T(t) = 20 + C e^{-kt} .
$$
The condition $T(0) = 80$ gives $C = 60$. Imposing $T(10) = 50$ gives $60\, e^{-10k} = 30$, that is, $e^{-10k} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore $k = \dfrac{\ln 2}{10}$.
