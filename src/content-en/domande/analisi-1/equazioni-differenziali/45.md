The right-hand side of the equation $y'' + y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ is not defined at $x = 0$. With the conditions $y(1) = 0$ and $y'(1) = 1$, on which maximal interval is the solution defined?

- $(0, +\infty)$
- $\mathbb{R}$
- $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$
- $(1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The forcing term $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ is continuous on the intervals $(-\infty, 0)$ and $(0, +\infty)$, and the initial point $x_0 = 1$ belongs to the second one. By the theorem on the **second-order initial value problem**, the problem has one and only one solution, defined on the whole interval that contains $x_0$ and on which $f$ is continuous. So the solution is defined on $(0, +\infty)$. The set $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ is not an interval, and at $x = 0$ the forcing term is not defined.
