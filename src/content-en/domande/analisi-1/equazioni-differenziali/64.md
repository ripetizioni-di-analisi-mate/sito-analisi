The function $e^{3x}$ is the right-hand side of the equation $y' - 3y = e^{3x}$ and is also a solution of the associated homogeneous equation. What are all the solutions of the equation?

- $y = (x + C)\, e^{-3x}$
- $y = e^{3x} + C e^{3x}$
- $y = x\, e^{3x} + C$
- $y = (x + C)\, e^{3x}$

## Explanation

The equation is linear with $a(x) = -3$, so $A(x) = -3x$ and the integrating factor is $e^{-3x}$. Multiplying gives
$$
\left(e^{-3x} y\right)' = e^{-3x}\, e^{3x} = 1 ,
$$
so $e^{-3x} y = x + C$. Dividing by $e^{-3x}$ gives $y = (x + C)\, e^{3x}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$.

The particular solution $x\, e^{3x}$ contains the factor $x$ because $e^{3x}$ solves the associated homogeneous equation.
