The variables are separated in the equation $y' = \dfrac{2x}{\cos y}$. Which function is obtained by imposing the condition $y(0) = 0$?

- $y = x^2$
- $y = \arcsin(x^2)$
- $y = -\arcsin(x^2)$
- $y = \sin(x^2)$

## Explanation

The equation is separable with $g(x) = 2x$ and $h(y) = \dfrac{1}{\cos y}$. The function $h$ never vanishes, so the equation has no constant solutions.

Separating the variables gives
$$
\int \cos y\, dy = \int 2x\, dx \quad\implies\quad \sin y = x^2 + C .
$$
The condition $y(0) = 0$ gives $C = 0$, so $\sin y = x^2$. Near $x = 0$ the solution takes values in $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, where $\cos y \neq 0$ and the inverse of the sine is the arcsine, therefore $y = \arcsin(x^2)$.
