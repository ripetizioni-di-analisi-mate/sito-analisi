The method of undetermined coefficients applied to the equation $y'' - y = x\, e^{x}$ gives a particular solution. Which one?

- $y_p = \dfrac{x^2 + x}{4}\, e^{x}$
- $y_p = \dfrac{x^2}{4}\, e^{x}$
- $y_p = \dfrac{x^2 - x}{4}\, e^{x}$
- $y_p = \dfrac{x^2 - x}{2}\, e^{x}$

## Explanation

The roots of $r^2 - 1 = 0$ are $\pm 1$, and the number $1$ associated with the right-hand side $x\, e^{x}$ is a simple root. By the **resonance rule** the trial function $(ax + b)\, e^{x}$ must be multiplied by $x$, so we look for $y_p = (ax^2 + bx)\, e^{x}$.

Computing the derivatives and substituting gives
$$
y_p'' - y_p = \bigl(4ax + 2a + 2b\bigr)\, e^{x} ,
$$
and equating to $x\, e^{x}$ gives $4a = 1$ and $2a + 2b = 0$. Therefore $a = \dfrac{1}{4}$, $b = -\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $y_p = \dfrac{x^2 - x}{4}\, e^{x}$.
