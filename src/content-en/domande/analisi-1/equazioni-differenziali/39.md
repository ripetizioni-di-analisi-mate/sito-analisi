Which function is a particular solution of the equation $y'' - 2y' + y = e^{x}$?

- $y = x^2 e^{x}$
- $y = \dfrac{x e^{x}}{2}$
- $y = \dfrac{e^{x}}{2}$
- $y = \dfrac{x^2 e^{x}}{2}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation $r^2 - 2r + 1 = (r - 1)^2 = 0$ has the double root $r = 1$. The number associated with the forcing term $e^{x}$ is $\gamma = 1$, which is a double root, so by the **resonance rule** the trial function $a_0 e^{x}$ must be multiplied by $x^2$. For $y_p = a_0 x^2 e^{x}$ we have $y_p' = a_0 \left( x^2 + 2x \right) e^{x}$ and $y_p'' = a_0 \left( x^2 + 4x + 2 \right) e^{x}$, and substituting we obtain
$$
a_0 e^{x} \bigl( x^2 + 4x + 2 - 2x^2 - 4x + x^2 \bigr) = 2 a_0 e^{x} = e^{x} ,
$$
so $a_0 = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore a particular solution is $y_p = \dfrac{x^2 e^{x}}{2}$. The functions $\dfrac{e^{x}}{2}$ and $\dfrac{x e^{x}}{2}$ are solutions of the homogeneous equation, so substituted into the equation they give $0$.
