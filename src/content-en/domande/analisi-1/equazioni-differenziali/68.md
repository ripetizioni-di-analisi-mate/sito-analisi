In the regions where $y > 0$, which substitution turns the Bernoulli equation $y' + x\, y = x\, y^{\frac{2}{3}}$ into a linear equation?

- $z = y^{-\frac{1}{3}}$
- $z = y^{\frac{1}{3}}$
- $z = y^{\frac{2}{3}}$
- $z = y^{-\frac{2}{3}}$

## Explanation

The equation is a Bernoulli equation with $a(x) = x$, $b(x) = x$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{2}{3}$. The substitution that makes it linear is $z = y^{1 - \alpha}$, and since $1 - \alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$ we set $z = y^{\frac{1}{3}}$.

This substitution gives the linear equation $z' + \dfrac{x}{3}\, z = \dfrac{x}{3}$, whose coefficients are those of the original equation multiplied by $1 - \alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$.
