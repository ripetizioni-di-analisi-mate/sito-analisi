The constant functions $y \equiv 1$ and $y \equiv -1$ are solutions of the equation $y' = (1 - y^2)\, e^{x}$. Let $y$ be the solution of the initial value problem formed by this equation and the condition $y(0) = 0$. Which statement is true?

::grafico{f="1;-1" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=2 legenda="y = 1;y = -1" titolo="The constant solutions and the initial point $(0, 0)$"}

- $y(x) > 1$ for $x$ large enough, because $y'(0) > 0$.
- $y$ is decreasing in a neighborhood of $x = 0$.
- The problem has more than one solution, because $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ vanishes at the point $(0, 0)$.
- $-1 < y(x) < 1$ for every $x$ where the solution is defined.

## Explanation

The functions $f(x, y) = (1 - y^2)\, e^{x}$ and $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} = -2y\, e^{x}$ are continuous in the whole plane, so the local existence and uniqueness theorem applies at every point.

If the graph of $y$ touched the line $y = 1$ at a point $x_1$, the solution $y$ and the constant solution $y \equiv 1$ would solve the same initial value problem with datum $y(x_1) = 1$, and by uniqueness they would coincide; this contradicts $y(0) = 0$. The same argument holds for the line $y = -1$.

Since $y(0) = 0$ lies between $-1$ and $1$ and the graph does not touch the two lines, by continuity $-1 < y(x) < 1$ for every $x$. In this strip $y' = (1 - y^2)\, e^{x} > 0$, so the solution is increasing.
