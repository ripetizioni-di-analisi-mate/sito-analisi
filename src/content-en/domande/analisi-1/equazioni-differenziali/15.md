Which of the following functions is a solution of the equation $y' = \dfrac{2y}{x}$ on the interval $(0, +\infty)$?

- $y = 2x$
- $y = x^2$
- $y = x^2 + 1$
- $y = e^{2x}$

## Explanation

To check that a function is a solution, one computes its derivative and substitutes it into the equation. For $y = x^2$ we have $y' = 2x$ and $\dfrac{2y}{x} = \dfrac{2x^2}{x} = 2x$, so the equation becomes an identity on $(0, +\infty)$ and $y = x^2$ is a **solution**. For $y = 2x$ we have $y' = 2$, while $\dfrac{2y}{x} = 4$, therefore the equation is not satisfied. For $y = x^2 + 1$ the right-hand side equals $2x + \dfrac{2}{x}$, which is different from $y' = 2x$. For $y = e^{2x}$ the right-hand side equals $\dfrac{2e^{2x}}{x}$, which is different from $y' = 2e^{2x}$ for $x \neq 1$.
