Which statement about the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' = x^2 + y^2 \\[4pt]
y(0) = 0
\end{cases}
$$
is true?

- It has at least two solutions, because $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ is not linear in $y$.
- It has no solutions, because the equation is not separable.
- It has one and only one solution, defined in a neighborhood of $0$.
- The local existence and uniqueness theorem does not apply, because $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ vanishes at $(0, 0)$.

## Explanation

The function $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ is continuous on the whole plane, and so is its partial derivative $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 2y$. So the hypotheses of the **local existence and uniqueness theorem** are satisfied on every open rectangle containing $(0, 0)$. Therefore the problem has **one and only one solution**, defined in a neighborhood of $0$. The theorem requires the continuity of the partial derivative, which may also vanish, and it does not require the equation to be linear or separable.
