Which function, equal to $0$ at $x = 0$, solves the equation $y' = \cos^2 y$?

- $y = \arctan x$
- $y = \tan x$
- $y = -\arctan x$
- $y = \arctan(2x)$

## Explanation

The equation is separable with $g(x) = 1$ and $h(y) = \cos^2 y$. The constant solutions are $y \equiv \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, and none of them equals $0$ at $x = 0$.

Since $\cos^2 0 = 1 \neq 0$, near the initial value the variables can be separated:
$$
\int \frac{dy}{\cos^2 y} = \int dx \quad\implies\quad \tan y = x + C .
$$
The condition $y(0) = 0$ gives $C = \tan 0 = 0$, so $\tan y = x$. The graph of the solution cannot cross the constant solutions $y \equiv \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, therefore its values stay in $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, where the inverse of the tangent is the arctangent. Hence the solution is $y = \arctan x$.
