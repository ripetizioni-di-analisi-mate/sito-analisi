What is the general solution of the equation $y''' - 4y' = 0$?

- $y = C_1 e^{2x} + C_2 e^{-2x}$
- $y = C_1 + C_2 \cos(2x) + C_3 \sin(2x)$
- $y = C_1 + C_2 e^{2x} + C_3 e^{-2x}$
- $y = C_1 x + C_2 e^{2x} + C_3 e^{-2x}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation is $r^3 - 4r = r (r - 2)(r + 2) = 0$, with the three simple real roots $0$, $2$ and $-2$. Each root $r$ corresponds to the solution $e^{r x}$, and the root $0$ corresponds to the constant function $1$. The solutions form a vector space of dimension $3$, therefore the general solution is $y = C_1 + C_2 e^{2x} + C_3 e^{-2x}$ with $C_1, C_2, C_3 \in \mathbb{R}$. The combination $C_1 e^{2x} + C_2 e^{-2x}$ depends on only two constants, so it does not describe all the solutions.
