The functions $y_1 = \cos x$ and $y_2 = \sin x$ solve the homogeneous equation associated with $y'' + y = \dfrac{1}{\cos x}$ and have Wronskian $W = 1$. Which particular solution does variation of parameters give on the interval $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$?

- $y_p = x\sin x - \cos x\, \ln(\cos x)$
- $y_p = x\sin x + \cos x\, \ln(\cos x)$
- $y_p = x\cos x + \sin x\, \ln(\cos x)$
- $y_p = \dfrac{x\sin x}{2}$

## Explanation

With $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\cos x}$ and $W = 1$, the **variation of parameters** formula gives
$$
y_p = -\cos x \int \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\, dx + \sin x \int \frac{\cos x}{\cos x}\, dx .
$$
The integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\, dx$ reduces to the basic integral $\displaystyle\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\, dx$ and equals $-\ln(\cos x)$, since $\cos x > 0$ on the interval. The second integral equals $x$. Therefore $y_p = \cos x\, \ln(\cos x) + x\sin x$.
