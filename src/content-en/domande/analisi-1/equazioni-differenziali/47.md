After separating the variables, the equation $y' = \dfrac{2x}{1 + 2y}$ leads to a quadratic equation in $y$. Which solution satisfies $y(0) = 0$?

- $y = x$
- $y = \dfrac{-1 - \sqrt{1 + 4x^2}}{2}$
- $y = \dfrac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 4x^2}}{2}$
- $y = \dfrac{-1 + \sqrt{1 - 4x^2}}{2}$

## Explanation

The equation is separable with $g(x) = 2x$ and $h(y) = \dfrac{1}{1 + 2y}$, defined for $y \neq -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The function $h$ never vanishes, so the equation has no constant solutions.

Separating the variables gives
$$
\int (1 + 2y)\, dy = \int 2x\, dx \quad\implies\quad y + y^2 = x^2 + C .
$$
The condition $y(0) = 0$ gives $C = 0$, so $y^2 + y - x^2 = 0$. The roots of this quadratic equation in $y$ are $y = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 4x^2}}{2}$. At $x = 0$ the root with the $+$ sign equals $0$ and the one with the $-$ sign equals $-1$, therefore the solution is $y = \dfrac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 4x^2}}{2}$, defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$.
