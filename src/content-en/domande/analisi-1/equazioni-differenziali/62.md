The linear equation $y' + e^{x}\, y = e^{x}$ has the constant solution $y \equiv 1$. Which family describes all of its solutions?

- $y = 1 + C e^{-e^{x}}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}$
- $y = 1 + C e^{e^{x}}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}$
- $y = 1 + C e^{-x}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}$
- $y = C\left(1 + e^{-e^{x}}\right)$, $C \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

By the structure of the general solution, the solutions are $y = C e^{-A(x)} + y_p$, where $A$ is an antiderivative of the coefficient $a(x) = e^{x}$ and $y_p$ is a particular solution.

The constant function $y_p \equiv 1$ is a solution, because $0 + e^{x} \cdot 1 = e^{x}$. An antiderivative of $e^{x}$ is $A(x) = e^{x}$, so the general solution of the associated homogeneous equation is $C e^{-e^{x}}$. Therefore the general solution is $y = 1 + C e^{-e^{x}}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$.
