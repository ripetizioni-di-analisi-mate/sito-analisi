- $y = 2 + e^{-2x}$
- $y = 2 + 3 e^{-2x}$
- $y = 4 - e^{-2x}$
- $y = 2 + e^{2x}$

## Explanation

The equation is linear with $a(x) = 2$, so the integrating factor is $e^{2x}$ and we obtain
$$
\left( e^{2x} y \right)' = 4 e^{2x} \quad\implies\quad e^{2x} y = 2 e^{2x} + C .
$$
Therefore the general solution is $y = 2 + C e^{-2x}$. Imposing the initial condition we have $y(0) = 2 + C = 3$, so $C = 1$. The solution of the problem is $y = 2 + e^{-2x}$, defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$.
