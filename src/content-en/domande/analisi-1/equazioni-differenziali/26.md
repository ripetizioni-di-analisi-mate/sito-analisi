What is the solution, for $x > 0$, of the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' + \dfrac{2}{x}\, y = 4x \\[8pt]
y(1) = 2
\end{cases}
$$

- $y = x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}$
- $y = x^2 + 1$
- $y = 2x^2$
- $y = x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

The equation is linear with $a(x) = \dfrac{2}{x}$. Since $x > 0$, an antiderivative of $a$ is $A(x) = 2 \ln x$, so the integrating factor is $e^{2 \ln x} = x^2$. Multiplying the equation by $x^2$ we obtain
$$
\left( x^2 y \right)' = 4x^3 \quad\implies\quad x^2 y = x^4 + C \quad\implies\quad y = x^2 + \frac{C}{x^2} .
$$
The condition $y(1) = 2$ gives $1 + C = 2$, so $C = 1$. Therefore the solution is $y = x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}$, defined on $(0, +\infty)$.
