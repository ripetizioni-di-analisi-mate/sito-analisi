What are the solutions of the equation $y' + y\tan x = \cos x$ on the interval $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$?

- $y = (x + C)\cos x$, $C \in \mathbb{R}$
- $y = \dfrac{x + C}{\cos x}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}$
- $y = x\cos x + C$, $C \in \mathbb{R}$
- $y = C\cos x$, $C \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

The equation is linear with $a(x) = \tan x$, continuous on the interval. Since $\cos x > 0$ on the interval, an antiderivative of $\tan x$ is $A(x) = -\ln(\cos x)$, so the integrating factor is $e^{A(x)} = \dfrac{1}{\cos x}$. Multiplying gives
$$
\left(\frac{y}{\cos x}\right)' = \frac{\cos x}{\cos x} = 1 ,
$$
so $\dfrac{y}{\cos x} = x + C$. Therefore the solutions are $y = (x + C)\cos x$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$.
