What is the general solution of the equation $y' + 2x\, y = x$?

- $y = \dfrac{1}{2} + C e^{x^2}$
- $y = x + C e^{-x^2}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{2} + C e^{-x^2}$
- $y = 2 + C e^{-x^2}$

## Explanation

The equation is a first-order linear equation with $a(x) = 2x$, so $A(x) = x^2$ and the integrating factor is $e^{x^2}$. Multiplying the equation by $e^{x^2}$ we obtain
$$
\left( e^{x^2} y \right)' = x e^{x^2} \quad\implies\quad e^{x^2} y = \frac{1}{2} e^{x^2} + C ,
$$
because $\dfrac{1}{2} e^{x^2}$ is an antiderivative of $x e^{x^2}$. Dividing by $e^{x^2}$ we obtain $y = \dfrac{1}{2} + C e^{-x^2}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$. The term $C e^{-x^2}$ is the general solution of the associated homogeneous equation and the constant $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is a particular solution of the nonhomogeneous equation.
