- $y = (1 + 2x)\, e^{x}$
- $y = (1 + 3x)\, e^{x}$
- $y = (3 - 2x)\, e^{x}$
- $y = (1 + 4x)\, e^{-x}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation is $r^2 - 2r + 1 = (r - 1)^2 = 0$, so $r = 1$ is a double root and the general solution is $y = (C_1 + C_2 x)\, e^{x}$. The derivative is $y' = (C_1 + C_2 + C_2 x)\, e^{x}$. The initial conditions give
$$
\begin{cases}
y(0) = C_1 = 1 \\[4pt]
y'(0) = C_1 + C_2 = 3
\end{cases}
$$
therefore $C_1 = 1$ and $C_2 = 2$. The solution of the problem is $y = (1 + 2x)\, e^{x}$, defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$.
