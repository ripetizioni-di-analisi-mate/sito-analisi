Which family describes the nonzero solutions of $y' + \dfrac{y}{x} = y^2$ on intervals of $(0,+\infty)$ where the formula is defined?

- $y = \dfrac{1}{x \left( C + \ln x \right)}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{x \left( C - \ln x \right)}$
- $y = \dfrac{x}{C - \ln x}$
- $y = x \left( C - \ln x \right)$

## Explanation

The equation is a Bernoulli equation with $a(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$, $b(x) = 1$ and $\alpha = 2$. For $y \neq 0$ the substitution $z = \dfrac{1}{y}$ transforms it into the linear equation $z' - \dfrac{z}{x} = -1$. Since $x > 0$, an antiderivative of $-\dfrac{1}{x}$ is $-\ln x$, so the integrating factor is $e^{-\ln x} = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and we obtain
$$
\left( \frac{z}{x} \right)' = -\frac{1}{x} \quad\implies\quad \frac{z}{x} = C - \ln x \quad\implies\quad z = x \left( C - \ln x \right) .
$$
Since $y = \dfrac{1}{z}$, the nonzero solutions are $y = \dfrac{1}{x \left( C - \ln x \right)}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$. For each $C$ the denominator vanishes at $x=e^C$, so the formula applies separately on $(0,e^C)$ and on $(e^C,+\infty)$.
