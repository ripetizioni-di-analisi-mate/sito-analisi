- $y = x \left( \ln x + C \right)$
- $y = \dfrac{\ln x}{x} + C$
- $y = \dfrac{C}{x} - \dfrac{1}{x^2}$
- $y = \dfrac{\ln x + C}{x}$

## Explanation

The equation is linear of the first order with $a(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$. Since $x > 0$, an antiderivative of $a$ is $A(x) = \ln x$, so the integrating factor is $e^{\ln x} = x$. Multiplying the equation by $x$ we obtain
$$
\left( x\, y \right)' = \frac{1}{x} \quad\implies\quad x\, y = \ln x + C .
$$
Dividing by $x$ we get the general solution $y = \dfrac{\ln x + C}{x}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$.
