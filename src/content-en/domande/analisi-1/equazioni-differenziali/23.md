What are the constant solutions of the equation $y' = \left( x^2 + 1 \right) \left( y^2 - 4y + 3 \right)$?

- $y \equiv -1$ and $y \equiv -3$
- $y \equiv 1$ and $y \equiv 3$
- $y \equiv 0$, $y \equiv 1$ and $y \equiv 3$
- $y \equiv 0$

## Explanation

The equation is separable with $g(x) = x^2 + 1$ and $h(y) = y^2 - 4y + 3$. A constant function $y \equiv \bar{y}$ has zero derivative, so it is a solution if and only if $\left( x^2 + 1 \right) h(\bar{y}) = 0$ for every $x$. Since $x^2 + 1 > 0$, this happens if and only if $h(\bar{y}) = 0$. Since $y^2 - 4y + 3 = (y - 1)(y - 3)$, the constant solutions are $y \equiv 1$ and $y \equiv 3$. The function $y \equiv 0$ is not a solution, because $h(0) = 3$ and the right-hand side equals $3\left( x^2 + 1 \right) \neq 0$.
