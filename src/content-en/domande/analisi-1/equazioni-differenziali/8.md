- $y \equiv 0$ and $y = \dfrac{e^{-x}}{C + x}$
- $y \equiv 0$ and $y = \dfrac{e^{x}}{C - x}$
- $y \equiv 0$ and $y = \dfrac{e^{-x}}{C - x}$
- $y \equiv 0$ and $y = (C - x)\, e^{x}$

## Explanation

The equation is a Bernoulli equation with $a(x) = 1$, $b(x) = e^{x}$ and $\alpha = 2$. Since $\alpha > 0$, the zero function $y \equiv 0$ is a solution. For $y \neq 0$ the substitution $z = y^{1 - \alpha} = \dfrac{1}{y}$ transforms the equation into the linear equation $z' - z = -e^{x}$. With the integrating factor $e^{-x}$ we obtain
$$
\left( e^{-x} z \right)' = -1 \quad\implies\quad e^{-x} z = C - x \quad\implies\quad z = (C - x)\, e^{x} .
$$
Since $y = \dfrac{1}{z}$, the nonzero solutions are $y = \dfrac{e^{-x}}{C - x}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$.
