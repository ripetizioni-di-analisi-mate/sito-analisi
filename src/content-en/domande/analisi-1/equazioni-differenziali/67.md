The equation $y' + y = x\, y^{\alpha}$ is a Bernoulli equation for every $\alpha \neq 0$, $\alpha \neq 1$. For which of the following values of $\alpha$ is the zero function $y \equiv 0$ *not* a solution?

- $\alpha = 2$
- $\alpha = -1$
- $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\alpha = 3$

## Explanation

For $\alpha > 0$ we have $0^{\alpha} = 0$, so substituting $y \equiv 0$ both sides equal $0$ and the zero function is a solution. This holds for $\alpha = 2$, for $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and for $\alpha = 3$.

For $\alpha = -1$ the right-hand side is $\dfrac{x}{y}$, which is not defined for $y = 0$, therefore the zero function is not a solution.
