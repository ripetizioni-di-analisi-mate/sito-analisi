What is the solution of the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' - y = 0 \\[4pt]
y(0) = 2 \\[4pt]
y'(0) = 0
\end{cases}
$$

- $y = 2 e^{x}$
- $y = 2 \cos x$
- $y = e^{x} - e^{-x}$
- $y = e^{x} + e^{-x}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation $r^2 - 1 = 0$ has the roots $\pm 1$, so the general solution is $y = C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{-x}$, with derivative $y' = C_1 e^{x} - C_2 e^{-x}$. The initial conditions give
$$
\begin{cases}
y(0) = C_1 + C_2 = 2 \\[4pt]
y'(0) = C_1 - C_2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
therefore $C_1 = C_2 = 1$. The solution of the problem is $y = e^{x} + e^{-x}$, defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$.
