What is the general solution of the equation $y' - 2y = e^{3x}$?

- $y = \dfrac{1}{5} e^{3x} + C e^{-2x}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{3} e^{3x} + C e^{2x}$
- $y = e^{3x} + C e^{-2x}$
- $y = e^{3x} + C e^{2x}$

## Explanation

The equation is a first-order linear equation with $a(x) = -2$, so $A(x) = -2x$ and the integrating factor is $e^{-2x}$. Multiplying the equation by $e^{-2x}$ we obtain
$$
\left( e^{-2x} y \right)' = e^{x} \quad\implies\quad e^{-2x} y = e^{x} + C .
$$
Dividing by $e^{-2x}$ we obtain $y = e^{3x} + C e^{2x}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$. The function $\dfrac{1}{5} e^{3x} + C e^{-2x}$ is the general solution of $y' + 2y = e^{3x}$, that is, of the equation with the sign of $a(x)$ reversed.
