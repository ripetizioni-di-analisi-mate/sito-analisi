Consider the initial value problem formed by the Bernoulli equation $y' + y = \sqrt{y}$ and the condition $y(0) = 0$, with solutions defined on $[0, +\infty)$. Which statement is true?

- The zero function $y \equiv 0$ is the only solution, by the local existence and uniqueness theorem.
- The problem has no solutions, because the substitution $z = \sqrt{y}$ requires $y > 0$.
- The functions $y \equiv 0$ and $y = \left(1 - e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right)^2$ both solve the problem.
- The only solution is $y = \left(1 - e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right)^2$, because the zero function does not solve the equation.

## Explanation

The equation is a Bernoulli equation with $a(x) = b(x) = 1$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $\alpha > 0$, the zero function $y \equiv 0$ is a solution and satisfies $y(0) = 0$.

For $y > 0$ the substitution $z = y^{1 - \alpha} = \sqrt{y}$ gives the linear equation $z' + \dfrac{z}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, with general solution $z = 1 + C e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$. With $C = -1$ we get $z = 1 - e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$, which equals $0$ at $x = 0$ and is positive for $x > 0$. For $y = \left(1 - e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right)^2$ we have $y' = \left(1 - e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right) e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$, so
$$
y' + y = 1 - e^{-\frac{x}{2}} = \sqrt{y} \qquad \text{for } x \geq 0 ,
$$
and this function also solves the problem.

The derivative $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{y}} - 1$ is unbounded near $y = 0$, therefore the local existence and uniqueness theorem does not apply at the point $(0, 0)$ and uniqueness is not guaranteed.
