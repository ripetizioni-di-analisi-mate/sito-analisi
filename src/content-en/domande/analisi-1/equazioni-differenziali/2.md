A solution of $y'=y\cos x$ passes through $(0,2)$. Without finding the integration constant, determine the ratio $\dfrac{y(\pi)}{y(0)}$.

- $1$
- $e^{-2}$, using $\cos\pi-\cos0$ as the exponent.
- $-1$
- $0$

## Explanation

The solution with positive initial value remains positive. Integrating $\dfrac{y'}{y}=\cos x$ from $0$ to $\pi$ gives $\ln(\dfrac{y(\pi)}{y(0)})=\sin\pi-\sin0=0$. The ratio is therefore one.
