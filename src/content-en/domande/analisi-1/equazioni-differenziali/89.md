A particular solution of the equation $y'' - 2y' + 2y = e^{x}\sin x$ is sought with the method of undetermined coefficients. Which one is found?

- $y_p = -\dfrac{x\, e^{x}\cos x}{2}$
- $y_p = -x\, e^{x}\cos x$
- $y_p = \dfrac{x\, e^{x}\cos x}{2}$
- $y_p = -\dfrac{e^{x}\cos x}{2}$

## Explanation

The roots of $r^2 - 2r + 2 = 0$ are $1 \pm i$, and the number associated with the right-hand side $e^{x}\sin x$ is exactly $1 \pm i$, a simple root. By the **resonance rule** we look for $y_p = x\, e^{x}\bigl(a\cos x + b\sin x\bigr)$.

Substituting into the equation gives
$$
y_p'' - 2y_p' + 2y_p = e^{x}\bigl(2b\cos x - 2a\sin x\bigr) ,
$$
so $2b = 0$ and $-2a = 1$ give $a = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $b = 0$. Therefore $y_p = -\dfrac{x\, e^{x}\cos x}{2}$.
