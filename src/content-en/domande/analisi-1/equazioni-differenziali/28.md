Let $y$ be the solution of the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' + \dfrac{y}{2} = 3 \\[8pt]
y(0) = 1
\end{cases}
$$
What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} y(x)$?

- $3$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $6$

## Explanation

The equation is linear with $a(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the integrating factor is $e^{\frac{x}{2}}$ and we obtain
$$
\left( e^{\frac{x}{2}} y \right)' = 3 e^{\frac{x}{2}} \quad\implies\quad e^{\frac{x}{2}} y = 6 e^{\frac{x}{2}} + C \quad\implies\quad y = 6 + C e^{-\frac{x}{2}} .
$$
The condition $y(0) = 1$ gives $6 + C = 1$, so $C = -5$ and the solution is $y = 6 - 5 e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$. Since $e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \to 0$ as $x \to +\infty$, the limit equals $6$. The value $6$ is the constant solution of the equation, obtained by setting $y' = 0$.
