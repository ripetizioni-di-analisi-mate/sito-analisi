- $y \equiv 0$ and $y = \dfrac{2}{1 + C e^{2x}}$
- $y \equiv 0$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2 + C e^{-2x}}$
- $y \equiv 0$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2} + C e^{-2x}$
- $y \equiv 0$ and $y = \dfrac{2}{1 + C e^{-2x}}$

## Explanation

The equation is a Bernoulli equation with $a(x) = -2$, $b(x) = -1$ and $\alpha = 2 > 0$, so the zero function $y \equiv 0$ is a solution. For $y \neq 0$ the substitution $z = \dfrac{1}{y}$ gives the linear equation $z' + 2z = 1$, whose general solution is $z = \dfrac{1}{2} + C e^{-2x}$. Since $y = \dfrac{1}{z}$, we obtain
$$
y = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{2} + C e^{-2x}} = \frac{2}{1 + 2C e^{-2x}} .
$$
The product $2C$ is still an arbitrary constant, therefore the nonzero solutions can be written as $y = \dfrac{2}{1 + C e^{-2x}}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$. For $C = 0$ we obtain the constant solution $y \equiv 2$.
