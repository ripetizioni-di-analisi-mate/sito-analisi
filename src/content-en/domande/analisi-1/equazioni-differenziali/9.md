For the Bernoulli equation $y'=2y-y^2$, a solution starts at $y(0)=1$. What is its behaviour as $x\to+\infty$?

- It tends to $1$, because its limit must equal the initial value.
- It becomes unbounded in finite time.
- It reaches $2$ at a finite time and then stays constant.
- It increases to $2$ without reaching it in finite time.

## Explanation

The equilibria are $0$ and $2$. Between them the field $y(2-y)$ is positive. Uniqueness prevents a solution from crossing an equilibrium. It increases, stays bounded and extends to every positive time. Its limit must be a zero of the field, so it equals $2$.
