- $y = 6 + C e^{-3x}$
- $y = 2 + C e^{-3x}$
- $y = 2 + C e^{3x}$
- $y = -2 + C e^{-3x}$

## Explanation

The equation is linear of the first order with $a(x) = 3$, so $A(x) = 3x$ and the integrating factor is $e^{3x}$. Multiplying the equation by $e^{3x}$ we obtain
$$
\left( e^{3x} y \right)' = 6 e^{3x} \quad\implies\quad e^{3x} y = 2 e^{3x} + C .
$$
Dividing by $e^{3x}$ we get $y = 2 + C e^{-3x}$ with $C \in \mathbb{R}$. The term $C e^{-3x}$ is the general solution of the associated homogeneous equation, and the constant $y = 2$ is a particular solution of the complete equation.
