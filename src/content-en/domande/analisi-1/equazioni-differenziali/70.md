For the Bernoulli equation $y' + y = y^3$ the initial condition $y(0) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is imposed. Which solution is obtained?

- $y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 3e^{-x}}}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5e^{2x} - 1}}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 3e^{2x}}}$
- $y = 1 + 3e^{2x}$

## Explanation

The equation is a Bernoulli equation with $a(x) = 1$, $b(x) = 1$ and $\alpha = 3$. Since $y(0) \neq 0$, near $x = 0$ we set $z = y^{1 - \alpha} = y^{-2}$, which turns the equation into the linear equation
$$
z' - 2z = -2 .
$$
Its general solution is $z = 1 + C e^{2x}$, and $z(0) = \dfrac{1}{y(0)^2} = 4$ gives $C = 3$. Since $z = \dfrac{1}{y^2}$ and $y(0) > 0$, we obtain $y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 3e^{2x}}}$.
