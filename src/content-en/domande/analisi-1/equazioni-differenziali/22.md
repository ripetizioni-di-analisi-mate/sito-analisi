What is the solution of the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' = \left( 1 + y^2 \right) \cos x \\[4pt]
y(0) = 0
\end{cases}
$$

- $y = \sin(\tan x)$
- $y = \tan x$
- $y = \tan(\sin x)$
- $y = \arctan(\sin x)$

## Explanation

The equation is separable with $g(x) = \cos x$ and $h(y) = 1 + y^2$, which never vanishes. Separating the variables we obtain
$$
\int \frac{dy}{1 + y^2} = \int \cos x \, dx \quad\implies\quad \arctan y = \sin x + c .
$$
The condition $y(0) = 0$ gives $c = 0$, so $\arctan y = \sin x$. Since $|\sin x| \leq 1 < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for every $x$, we can apply the tangent to both sides. Therefore the solution is $y = \tan(\sin x)$, defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$.
