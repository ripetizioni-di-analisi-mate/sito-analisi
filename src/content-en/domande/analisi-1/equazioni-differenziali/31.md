What is the solution, in a neighborhood of $x_0 = 0$, of the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' = y + \sqrt{y} \\[4pt]
y(0) = 1
\end{cases}
$$

- $y = 2 e^{\frac{x}{2}} - 1$
- $y = \left( 2 e^{x} - 1 \right)^2$
- $y = \left( 2 e^{-\frac{x}{2}} - 1 \right)^2$
- $y = \left( 2 e^{\frac{x}{2}} - 1 \right)^2$

## Explanation

The equation can be written $y' - y = y^{\frac{1}{2}}$, so it is a Bernoulli equation with $a(x) = -1$, $b(x) = 1$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $y(0) = 1 > 0$, we look for a positive solution and set $z = y^{1 - \alpha} = \sqrt{y}$, which satisfies the linear equation $z' - \dfrac{z}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ with $z(0) = 1$. With the integrating factor $e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$ we obtain
$$
\left( e^{-\frac{x}{2}} z \right)' = \frac{1}{2} e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \quad\implies\quad e^{-\frac{x}{2}} z = C - e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \quad\implies\quad z = C e^{\frac{x}{2}} - 1 .
$$
The condition $z(0) = 1$ gives $C = 2$. Since $y = z^2$, the solution is $y = \left( 2 e^{\frac{x}{2}} - 1 \right)^2$, valid where $2 e^{\frac{x}{2}} - 1 > 0$, that is, for $x > -2 \ln 2$.
