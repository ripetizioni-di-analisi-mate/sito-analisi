Consider the equation $y' = \sqrt[3]{y}$ with the initial condition $y(x_0) = y_0$. For which of the following initial conditions are the hypotheses of the local existence and uniqueness theorem *not* satisfied?

- $y(0) = 1$
- $y(0) = -1$
- $y(2) = 8$
- $y(1) = 0$

## Explanation

Here $f(x, y) = \sqrt[3]{y}$ is continuous on the whole plane. Its partial derivative with respect to $y$ is
$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \frac{1}{3\sqrt[3]{y^2}} ,
$$
which is continuous at the points with $y \neq 0$ and is not defined for $y = 0$. So every point $(x_0, y_0)$ with $y_0 \neq 0$ is contained in an open rectangle on which the hypotheses are satisfied, and for the conditions $y(0) = 1$, $y(0) = -1$, $y(2) = 8$ the theorem applies. The point $(1, 0)$ has zero ordinate, therefore the partial derivative is not continuous on any rectangle containing it, and for the condition $y(1) = 0$ the hypothesis of the theorem **is not satisfied**.
