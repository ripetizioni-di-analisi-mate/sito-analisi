For the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' = 2x\, y^2 \\[4pt]
y(0) = 1
\end{cases}
$$
what is the interval of existence of the solution?

- $\mathbb{R}$
- $[-1, 1]$
- $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 1\}$
- $(-1, 1)$

## Explanation

The constant solution $y \equiv 0$ does not satisfy the condition $y(0) = 1$, so we look for the solution with $y \neq 0$. Separating the variables we obtain
$$
\int \frac{dy}{y^2} = \int 2x \, dx \quad\implies\quad -\frac{1}{y} = x^2 + c .
$$
The condition $y(0) = 1$ gives $c = -1$, therefore the solution is $y = \dfrac{1}{1 - x^2}$. This function is differentiable on $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 1\}$, which is a union of three intervals. The interval of existence is the largest interval containing $x_0 = 0$ on which the solution is differentiable, so it is $(-1, 1)$.
