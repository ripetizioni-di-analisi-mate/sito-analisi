For the equation $y'' - y = 1$ the conditions $y(0) = 0$ and $y'(0) = 0$ are imposed. Which function solves this initial value problem?

- $y = 1 - \dfrac{e^{x} + e^{-x}}{2}$
- $y = \cos x - 1$
- $y \equiv 0$
- $y = \dfrac{e^{x} + e^{-x}}{2} - 1$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation $r^2 - 1 = 0$ has the roots $\pm 1$, and the constant $y_p = -1$ is a particular solution, because $0 - (-1) = 1$. So the general solution is $y = C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{-x} - 1$, with derivative $y' = C_1 e^{x} - C_2 e^{-x}$.

The initial conditions give the system
$$
\begin{cases}
C_1 + C_2 - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
C_1 - C_2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
whence $C_1 = C_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore the solution is $y = \dfrac{e^{x} + e^{-x}}{2} - 1$.
