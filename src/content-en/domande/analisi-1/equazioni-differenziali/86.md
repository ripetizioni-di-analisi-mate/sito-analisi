For which value of $\omega > 0$ does the equation $y'' + 9y = \sin(\omega x)$ have unbounded solutions on $\mathbb{R}$?

- $9$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $6$
- $3$

## Explanation

The roots of the characteristic equation are $\pm 3i$ and the solutions of the homogeneous equation, $C_1\cos(3x) + C_2\sin(3x)$, are bounded.

If $\omega \neq 3$, the number $\pm i\omega$ is not a root and the method of undetermined coefficients gives $y_p = \dfrac{\sin(\omega x)}{9 - \omega^2}$, so all solutions are bounded. If $\omega = 3$, by the **resonance rule** we look for $y_p = x\bigl(a\cos(3x) + b\sin(3x)\bigr)$ and find $y_p = -\dfrac{x\cos(3x)}{6}$, which is unbounded. Therefore unbounded solutions occur only for $\omega = 3$.
