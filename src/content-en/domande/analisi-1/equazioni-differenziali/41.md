To which of the following equations does the method of undetermined coefficients *not* apply, while variation of constants does?

- $y'' + y = x e^{x}$
- $y'' + y = \tan x$
- $y'' + y = \sin x$
- $y'' + y = e^{x} \cos x$

## Explanation

The method of undetermined coefficients applies when the forcing term is a polynomial, an exponential, a combination of sine and cosine with the same argument, or a product of a polynomial or an exponential with these terms. The term $x e^{x}$ is the product of a polynomial and an exponential, $e^{x} \cos x$ is the product of an exponential and a cosine, and $\sin x$ belongs to the sine and cosine row, with resonance because $\pm i$ is a root of $r^2 + 1 = 0$. The function $\tan x$ has none of these forms, so for $y'' + y = \tan x$ the method of undetermined coefficients does not apply. **Variation of constants** applies to every continuous forcing term, hence also to $\tan x$ on an interval such as $\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$.
