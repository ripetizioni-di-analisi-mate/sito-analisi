A bacterial population $N(t)$, with $t$ in hours, satisfies the equation $N' = kN$ with $k > 0$ constant and doubles in $3$ hours. After how many hours from the initial time is the population $8$ times the initial one?

- $12$
- $9$
- $24$
- $6$

## Explanation

The equation $N' = kN$ is separable and its general solution is $N(t) = N_0\, e^{kt}$, where $N_0 = N(0)$.

Since the population doubles in $3$ hours, $e^{3k} = 2$. We look for $t$ with
$$
e^{kt} = 8 = 2^3 = \left(e^{3k}\right)^3 = e^{9k} .
$$
The exponential function is injective, so $kt = 9k$ and, since $k > 0$, we get $t = 9$ hours.
