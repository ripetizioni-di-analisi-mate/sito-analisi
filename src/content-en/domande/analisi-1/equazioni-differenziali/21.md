For the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' = y^2 \\[4pt]
y(0) = 2
\end{cases}
$$
what is the interval of existence of the solution?

- $\left( -\infty, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, +\infty \right)$
- $\mathbb{R}$
- $(-\infty, 2)$

## Explanation

The constant solution $y \equiv 0$ does not satisfy the condition $y(0) = 2$, so we look for the solution with $y \neq 0$. Separating the variables we obtain
$$
\int \frac{dy}{y^2} = \int dx \quad\implies\quad -\frac{1}{y} = x + c ,
$$
and the condition $y(0) = 2$ gives $c = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore the solution is $y = \dfrac{2}{1 - 2x}$, differentiable on $\mathbb{R} \setminus \left\{ \dfrac{1}{2} \right\}$. The interval of existence is the largest interval containing $x_0 = 0$ on which the solution is differentiable, so it is $\left( -\infty, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$.
