The graph of the solution of the initial value problem $y' = -\dfrac{x}{y}$, $y(0) = 2$ is an arc of a circle. On which maximal interval is the solution defined?

- $(-2, 2)$
- $[-2, 2]$
- $\mathbb{R}$
- $(0, 2)$

## Explanation

The equation is separable and reads $y\, y' = -x$. Integrating gives $\dfrac{y^2}{2} = -\dfrac{x^2}{2} + c$, and the condition $y(0) = 2$ gives $y^2 = 4 - x^2$.

Since $y(0) = 2 > 0$, the solution is $y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$, whose graph is the upper semicircle centered at the origin with radius $2$. This function is differentiable only for $-2 < x < 2$, and at the endpoints $x = \pm 2$ it equals $0$, where the right-hand side $-\dfrac{x}{y}$ is not defined. Therefore the maximal interval is $(-2, 2)$.
