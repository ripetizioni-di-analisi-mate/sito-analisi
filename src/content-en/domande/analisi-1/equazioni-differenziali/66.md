The coefficient of $y$ in the equation $y' + \dfrac{2x}{1 + x^2}\, y = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$ is continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}$. What is the value at $x = 1$ of the solution that satisfies $y(0) = 2$?

- $6$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The equation is linear with $a(x) = \dfrac{2x}{1 + x^2}$ and $b(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$, continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. An antiderivative of $a$ is $A(x) = \ln(1 + x^2)$, so the integrating factor is $e^{A(x)} = 1 + x^2$. Multiplying gives
$$
\bigl((1 + x^2)\, y\bigr)' = 1 \quad\implies\quad (1 + x^2)\, y = x + C .
$$
The condition $y(0) = 2$ gives $C = 2$. Dividing by $1 + x^2$ gives $y = \dfrac{x + 2}{1 + x^2}$, therefore $y(1) = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
