What is the general solution of the equation $y'' - 4y' + 13y = 0$?

- $y = e^{-2x} \left( C_1 \cos(3x) + C_2 \sin(3x) \right)$
- $y = e^{3x} \left( C_1 \cos(2x) + C_2 \sin(2x) \right)$
- $y = e^{2x} \left( C_1 \cos(3x) + C_2 \sin(3x) \right)$
- $y = e^{2x} \left( C_1 \cos(6x) + C_2 \sin(6x) \right)$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation is $r^2 - 4r + 13 = 0$, with discriminant $\Delta = 16 - 52 = -36 < 0$. So the roots are complex conjugates, equal to
$$
r = \frac{4 \pm 6i}{2} = 2 \pm 3i ,
$$
that is, $\lambda = 2$ and $\mu = 3$. Therefore the general solution is $y = e^{2x} \left( C_1 \cos(3x) + C_2 \sin(3x) \right)$ with $C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}$.
