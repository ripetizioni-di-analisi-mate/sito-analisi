The functions $y_1 = e^{x}$ and $y_2 = e^{2x}$ solve the equation $y'' - 3y' + 2y = 0$ and are used for variation of parameters. What is their Wronskian $W(x) = y_1\, y_2' - y_1'\, y_2$?

- $e^{3x}$
- $2e^{3x}$
- $-e^{3x}$
- $3e^{3x}$

## Explanation

The derivatives are $y_1' = e^{x}$ and $y_2' = 2e^{2x}$. Substituting into the definition gives
$$
W(x) = e^{x} \cdot 2e^{2x} - e^{x} \cdot e^{2x} = 2e^{3x} - e^{3x} = e^{3x} .
$$
The Wronskian never vanishes, as happens for two non-proportional solutions of the homogeneous equation.
