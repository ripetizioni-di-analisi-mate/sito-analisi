Which linear equation is obtained from the Bernoulli equation $y' + 2y = x\, y^{4}$ with the substitution $z = y^{-3}$, in the regions where $y \neq 0$?

- $z' + 2z = x$
- $z' - 6z = 3x$
- $z' - 6z = -3x$
- $z' + 6z = -3x$

## Explanation

The equation is a Bernoulli equation with $a(x) = 2$, $b(x) = x$ and $\alpha = 4$, so the substitution is $z = y^{1 - \alpha} = y^{-3}$. Dividing the equation by $y^{4}$ we obtain $y^{-4} y' + 2 y^{-3} = x$. Since $z' = -3\, y^{-4} y'$, we have $y^{-4} y' = -\dfrac{z'}{3}$, and the equation becomes $-\dfrac{z'}{3} + 2z = x$. Multiplying by $-3$ we obtain the linear equation $z' - 6z = -3x$, in agreement with the formula $z' + (1 - \alpha)\, a(x)\, z = (1 - \alpha)\, b(x)$.
