For the nonzero solutions of the Bernoulli equation $y' = y - x\, y^2$, the substitution $z = \dfrac{1}{y}$ leads to the linear equation $z' + z = x$, whose general solution is $z = x - 1 + C e^{-x}$. Which family describes the nonzero solutions of the original equation?

- $y = x - 1 + C e^{-x}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{x - 1} + C e^{x}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{x - 1 + C e^{-x}}$
- $y = \dfrac{1}{x + 1 + C e^{x}}$

## Explanation

The substitution $z = \dfrac{1}{y}$ can be inverted in the regions where $z \neq 0$ and gives $y = \dfrac{1}{z}$. Since $z = x - 1 + C e^{-x}$, the nonzero solutions of the original equation are
$$
y = \frac{1}{x - 1 + C e^{-x}}, \qquad C \in \mathbb{R},
$$
on the intervals where the denominator does not vanish. The function $x - 1 + C e^{-x}$ is the auxiliary function $z$, which solves the linear equation in $z$.
