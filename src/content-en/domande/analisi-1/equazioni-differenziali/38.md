What is the general solution of the equation $y'' + 2y' + y = 9 e^{2x}$?

- $y = \left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{x} + e^{2x}$
- $y = \left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{-x} + e^{2x}$
- $y = \left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{-x} + 9 e^{2x}$
- $y = C_1 e^{-x} + C_2 e^{x} + e^{2x}$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation $r^2 + 2r + 1 = (r + 1)^2 = 0$ has the double root $r = -1$, so the general solution of the homogeneous equation is $\left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{-x}$. The number associated with the forcing term is $\gamma = 2$, which is not a root, therefore by the method of undetermined coefficients we look for $y_p = a_0 e^{2x}$. Substituting we obtain $(4 + 4 + 1)\, a_0 e^{2x} = 9 e^{2x}$, so $a_0 = 1$ and $y_p = e^{2x}$. By the structure of the solution space, the general solution is $y = \left( C_1 + C_2 x \right) e^{-x} + e^{2x}$ with $C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}$.
