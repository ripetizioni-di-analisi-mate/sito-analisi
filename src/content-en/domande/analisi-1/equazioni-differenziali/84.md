The right-hand side of the equation $y'' + 2y' + y = 2\cos x$ contains only the cosine. Which of the following functions solves the equation?

- $y = \sin x$
- $y = 2\cos x$
- $y = -\sin x$
- $y = x\sin x$

## Explanation

The characteristic equation $r^2 + 2r + 1 = 0$ has the double root $-1$, so $\pm i$ is not a root and we look for $y_p = a\cos x + b\sin x$. Since $y_p'' = -y_p$, substituting gives
$$
-y_p + 2y_p' + y_p = 2y_p' = 2\left(-a\sin x + b\cos x\right) = 2\cos x ,
$$
hence $b = 1$ and $a = 0$. Therefore the required function is $y = \sin x$. The trial function also contains the sine because the term $2y'$ involves the derivative of the sine, which is the cosine.
