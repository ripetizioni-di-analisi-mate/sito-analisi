What is the solution of the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y' = \dfrac{x}{y} \\[8pt]
y(0) = 2
\end{cases}
$$

- $y = \sqrt{x^2 + 2}$
- $y = \sqrt{x^2 + 4}$
- $y = x + 2$
- $y = -\sqrt{x^2 + 4}$

## Explanation

The equation is separable with $g(x) = x$ and $h(y) = \dfrac{1}{y}$. Separating the variables we obtain
$$
\int y \, dy = \int x \, dx \quad\implies\quad \frac{y^2}{2} = \frac{x^2}{2} + c \quad\implies\quad y^2 = x^2 + k .
$$
The condition $y(0) = 2$ gives $k = 4$. Since $y(0) = 2 > 0$, we choose the positive root, so the solution is $y = \sqrt{x^2 + 4}$, defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$. The function $y = -\sqrt{x^2 + 4}$ solves the equation, but it equals $-2$ at $x = 0$.
