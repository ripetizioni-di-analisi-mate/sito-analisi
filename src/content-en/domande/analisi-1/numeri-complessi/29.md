In the expression $z = -2\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} + i \sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$ the factor $-2$ is negative. What is the exponential form of $z$, with positive modulus and principal argument?

- $2\, e^{i \frac{5\pi}{6}}$
- $2\, e^{-i \frac{5\pi}{6}}$
- $2\, e^{i \frac{\pi}{6}}$
- $2\, e^{-i \frac{\pi}{6}}$

## Explanation

Since $-1 = e^{i\pi}$, one has $z = 2\, e^{i\pi}\, e^{i\frac{\pi}{6}} = 2\, e^{i\frac{7\pi}{6}}$. The argument $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ does not lie in $(-\pi, \pi]$, so one subtracts $2\pi$ and obtains the principal argument $\dfrac{7\pi}{6} - 2\pi = -\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. Therefore $z = 2\, e^{-i\frac{5\pi}{6}}$; in algebraic form $z = -\sqrt{3} - i$, a point in the third quadrant.
