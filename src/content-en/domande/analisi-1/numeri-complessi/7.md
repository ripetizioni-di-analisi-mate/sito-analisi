What do the solutions in $\mathbb{C}$ of the equation $z^4 = 16$ represent in the complex plane?

- The two points $-2$ and $2$ of the real axis
- The vertices of the square inscribed in the circle with center at the origin and radius $2$, with the vertices on the axes
- The vertices of the square inscribed in the circle with center at the origin and radius $4$, with the vertices on the axes
- The vertices of the square inscribed in the circle with center at the origin and radius $2$, with the vertices on the bisectors of the quadrants

## Explanation

The number $16$ has modulus $16$ and argument $0$, so by the formula for the $n$-th roots the solutions are
$$
z_k = \sqrt[4]{16}\; e^{\,i \frac{2k\pi}{4}} = 2\, e^{\,i \frac{k\pi}{2}}, \qquad k = 0, 1, 2, 3 .
$$
For $k = 0, 1, 2, 3$ one obtains $2$, $2i$, $-2$ and $-2i$. The four solutions have modulus $2$ and arguments that differ by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so they are the vertices of a square inscribed in the circle with center at the origin and radius $2$. Since the arguments are multiples of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the vertices lie on the axes.
