What is the algebraic form of $z = 4\, e^{-i \frac{2\pi}{3}}$?

- $-2 + 2\sqrt{3}\, i$
- $2 - 2\sqrt{3}\, i$
- $-2\sqrt{3} - 2i$
- $-2 - 2\sqrt{3}\, i$

## Explanation

By Euler's formula
$$
z = 4 \left( \cos\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) + i \sin\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) \right) .
$$
Cosine is an even function and sine is an odd function, so $\cos\left(-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin\left(-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Therefore $z = -2 - 2\sqrt{3}\, i$, a point in the third quadrant, consistent with the argument $-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.
