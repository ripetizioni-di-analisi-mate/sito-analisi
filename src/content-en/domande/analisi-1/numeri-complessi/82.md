What is the factorization of the polynomial $z^2 + 9$ into first-degree factors in $\mathbb{C}$?

- $(z - 3i)(z + 3i)$
- $(z - 3)(z + 3)$
- $(z - 9i)(z + 9i)$
- $(z + 3i)^2$

## Explanation

The roots of $z^2 + 9$ are the solutions of $z^2 = -9$, that is $z = 3i$ and $z = -3i$. By the fundamental theorem of algebra a polynomial of degree $2$ with leading coefficient $1$ and roots $z_1$, $z_2$ equals $(z - z_1)(z - z_2)$, so $z^2 + 9 = (z - 3i)(z + 3i)$. The product $(z - 3)(z + 3)$ is instead $z^2 - 9$.
