What is the solution in $\mathbb{C}$ of the equation $z\bar{z} + 2iz = 3 + 4i$?

- $2 - i$
- $1 + 2i$
- $2 + i$
- $2 + 3i$

## Explanation

Setting $z = x + iy$ with $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, one has $z\bar{z} = x^2 + y^2$ and $2iz = 2ix - 2y$. The equation becomes $x^2 + y^2 - 2y + 2ix = 3 + 4i$, so it is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 - 2y = 3 \\[4pt]
2x = 4
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation one obtains $x = 2$. Substituting into the first one obtains $y^2 - 2y + 1 = 0$, that is $(y - 1)^2 = 0$, therefore $y = 1$ and the only solution is $z = 2 + i$.
