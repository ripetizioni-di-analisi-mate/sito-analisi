What is the value of $\left(\dfrac{1 + i\sqrt{3}}{1 - i}\right)^6$?

- $8i$
- $-8i$
- $-64i$
- $8$

## Explanation

The numerator has modulus $2$ and argument $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, the denominator has modulus $\sqrt{2}$ and argument $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. In a quotient the moduli are divided and the arguments are subtracted, so
$$
\frac{1 + i\sqrt{3}}{1 - i} = \sqrt{2}\, e^{i \frac{7\pi}{12}} ,
$$
because $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{7\pi}{12}$. By **De Moivre's formula** the sixth power has modulus $\left(\sqrt{2}\right)^6 = 8$ and argument $\dfrac{7\pi}{2}$. Since $\dfrac{7\pi}{2} - 4\pi = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, one has $e^{i \frac{7\pi}{2}} = -i$, therefore the result is $-8i$.
