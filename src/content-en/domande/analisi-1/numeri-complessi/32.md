For $z = 2 + i$, how is the point $iz$ obtained from $z$ in the complex plane?

- By rotating $z$ by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ clockwise, which gives $1 - 2i$
- By reflection across the imaginary axis, which gives $-2 + i$
- By rotating $z$ by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ counterclockwise, which gives $-1 + 2i$
- By rotating $z$ by $\pi$, which gives $-2 - i$

## Explanation

The number $i$ has modulus $1$ and argument $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. In a product the moduli are multiplied and the arguments are added, so $iz$ has the same modulus as $z$ and argument increased by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Therefore $iz$ is obtained by rotating $z$ by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ counterclockwise about the origin. In algebraic form one indeed has $iz = 2i + i^2 = -1 + 2i$.
