For which complex numbers $z$ does $z^2 + |z|^2 = 0$ hold?

- The single number $z = 0$
- The two numbers $z = i$ and $z = -i$
- All numbers $z = iy$ with $y \in \mathbb{R}$
- All real numbers

## Explanation

Setting $z = x + iy$, one has $z^2 = x^2 - y^2 + 2ixy$ and $|z|^2 = x^2 + y^2$, so the equation becomes $2x^2 + 2ixy = 0$. Separating real and imaginary parts one obtains the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x^2 = 0 \\[4pt]
2xy = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $x = 0$, and with $x = 0$ the second is satisfied for every $y$. Therefore the solutions are all numbers $iy$ with $y$ real, that is, the points of the imaginary axis, origin included.
