In the complex plane, what does the equation $\operatorname{Re}\left(\dfrac{1}{z}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, with $z \neq 0$, represent?

- The line $x = 2$
- The circle with center $(0, 1)$ and radius $1$, without the origin
- The circle with center $(1, 0)$ and radius $1$, without the origin
- The circle with center $(2, 0)$ and radius $2$, without the origin

## Explanation

Setting $z = x + iy \neq 0$, by the reciprocal formula
$$
\frac{1}{z} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2} = \frac{x - iy}{x^2 + y^2} ,
$$
so $\operatorname{Re}\left(\dfrac{1}{z}\right) = \dfrac{x}{x^2 + y^2}$. The equation becomes $2x = x^2 + y^2$, that is $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1$. This is the circle with center $(1, 0)$ and radius $1$; the origin belongs to it but must be excluded, because the reciprocal is not defined for $z = 0$.
