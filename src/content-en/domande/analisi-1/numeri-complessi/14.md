Given that $z = i$ is a root of the polynomial $p(z) = z^3 - 2z^2 + z - 2$, what is the factorization of $p$ into real factors?

- $(z + 2)(z^2 + 1)$
- $(z - 2)(z^2 + 1)$
- $(z - 2)(z^2 - 1)$
- $(z - 1)(z^2 + 2)$

## Explanation

The polynomial $p$ has real coefficients and $i$ is one of its roots, so by the theorem on **complex conjugate roots** $-i$ is also a root of $p$. Therefore $p$ is divisible by $(z - i)(z + i) = z^2 + 1$. Grouping one has $p(z) = z^2(z - 2) + (z - 2) = (z - 2)(z^2 + 1)$, so the third root is $2$. The factor $z^2 + 1$ has negative discriminant, therefore it cannot be factored further over $\mathbb{R}$.
