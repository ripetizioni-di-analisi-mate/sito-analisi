Which locus do the numbers $z$ with $|z - 1| = |z + i|$ describe in the complex plane?

- The line $y = x$
- The line $x + y = 1$
- The line $y = x - 1$
- The line $y = -x$

## Explanation

In the complex plane $|z - 1|$ is the distance of $z$ from the point $(1, 0)$, and $|z + i| = |z - (-i)|$ is the distance of $z$ from the point $(0, -1)$. The locus is therefore the perpendicular bisector of the segment with endpoints $(1, 0)$ and $(0, -1)$. Setting $z = x + iy$ and squaring both sides, which are nonnegative, one obtains
$$
(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = x^2 + (y + 1)^2 ,
$$
that is $-2x + 1 = 2y + 1$. Therefore the locus is the line $y = -x$. The line $y = x - 1$ is the one through the two points, perpendicular to the bisector.
