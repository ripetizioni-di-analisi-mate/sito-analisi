What are the solutions in $\mathbb{C}$ of the equation $z^4 = -4$?

- $\pm 1$ and $\pm i$
- $\pm \sqrt{2}$ and $\pm i\sqrt{2}$
- $\pm 1 \pm i$
- $\pm \sqrt{2} \pm i\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The number $-4$ has modulus $4$ and argument $\pi$, so by the formula for the **$n$-th roots**
$$
z_k = \sqrt[4]{4}\; e^{\,i \frac{\pi + 2k\pi}{4}} = \sqrt{2}\, e^{\,i \left( \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{k\pi}{2} \right)}, \qquad k = 0, 1, 2, 3 .
$$
The arguments $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$ correspond to the bisectors of the quadrants, and a point of modulus $\sqrt{2}$ on a bisector has coordinates $(\pm 1, \pm 1)$. Therefore the solutions are $1 + i$, $-1 + i$, $-1 - i$ and $1 - i$, that is $\pm 1 \pm i$.
