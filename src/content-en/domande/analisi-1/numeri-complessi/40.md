The number $z_0 = 1 + 2i$, shown in the figure, is one of the fourth roots of a complex number $w$. What are the other three fourth roots of $w$?

::grafico{curve="sqrt(5)*cos(t),sqrt(5)*sin(t),0,6.29" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" punti="1,2,pieno,z₀" legenda=0 titolo="The point $z_0 = 1 + 2i$ on the circle with center at the origin and radius $\sqrt{5}$"}

- $-2 + i$, $-1 - 2i$, $2 - i$
- $-1 + 2i$, $-1 - 2i$, $1 - 2i$
- $2 + i$, $-1 - 2i$, $-2 - i$
- $i$, $-1$, $-i$

## Explanation

The four fourth roots of $w$ have the same modulus and arguments that differ by $\dfrac{2\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Multiplication by $i = e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}$ rotates a point by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ counterclockwise and preserves its modulus, so the other roots are $i z_0$, $i^2 z_0$ and $i^3 z_0$. Since $i(1 + 2i) = -2 + i$, $-(1 + 2i) = -1 - 2i$ and $-i(1 + 2i) = 2 - i$, the other three roots are $-2 + i$, $-1 - 2i$ and $2 - i$. The number $w$ is $(1 + 2i)^4 = -7 - 24i$.
