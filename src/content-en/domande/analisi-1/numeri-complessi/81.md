The points $z$ whose distance from $1$ is twice the distance from $-1$, that is, those with $|z - 1| = 2\,|z + 1|$, form a circle. What are its center and radius?

- Center $\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}, 0\right)$ and radius $1$
- Center $\left(\dfrac{5}{3}, 0\right)$ and radius $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- Center $\left(-\dfrac{5}{3}, 0\right)$ and radius $\dfrac{16}{9}$
- Center $\left(-\dfrac{5}{3}, 0\right)$ and radius $\dfrac{4}{3}$

## Explanation

Setting $z = x + iy$ and squaring both sides, which are nonnegative, one obtains
$$
(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 4\left((x + 1)^2 + y^2\right),
$$
that is $3x^2 + 10x + 3y^2 + 3 = 0$. Dividing by $3$ and completing the square gives $\left(x + \dfrac{5}{3}\right)^2 + y^2 = \dfrac{25}{9} - 1 = \dfrac{16}{9}$. Therefore the locus is the circle with center $\left(-\dfrac{5}{3}, 0\right)$ and radius $\dfrac{4}{3}$.
