What are the square roots in $\mathbb{C}$ of $w = -5 + 12i$?

- $\pm(2 + 3i)$
- $\pm(3 + 2i)$
- $\pm(2 - 3i)$
- $\pm(3 - 2i)$

## Explanation

One looks for $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $(x + iy)^2 = -5 + 12i$. Since $(x + iy)^2 = x^2 - y^2 + 2ixy$, equating real parts and imaginary parts one obtains the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 - y^2 = -5 \\[4pt]
xy = 6
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation one gets $y = \dfrac{6}{x}$, and substituting into the first one obtains $x^4 + 5x^2 - 36 = 0$, that is $\left(x^2 + 9\right)\left(x^2 - 4\right) = 0$. Since $x$ is real, $x^2 = 4$, so $x = \pm 2$ and $y = \dfrac{6}{x} = \pm 3$ with the same sign. Therefore the square roots of $w$ are $\pm(2 + 3i)$.
