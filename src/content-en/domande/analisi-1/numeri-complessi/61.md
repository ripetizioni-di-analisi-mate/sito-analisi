What is $e^{i\theta} + e^{-i\theta}$ for $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$?

- $\sqrt{3}$
- $1$
- $0$
- $2$

## Explanation

By Euler's formula $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ and $e^{-i\theta} = \cos\theta - i\sin\theta$, since cosine is even and sine is odd. Adding, the imaginary parts cancel and one obtains $e^{i\theta} + e^{-i\theta} = 2\cos\theta$. For $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ the sum is therefore $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$.
