The equation $z^2 - 2iz - 2 = 0$ has coefficients that are not all real. What are its two solutions in $\mathbb{C}$?

- $z = 1 + i$ and $z = -1 + i$
- $z = 1 - i$ and $z = -1 - i$
- $z = \sqrt{3} + i$ and $z = -\sqrt{3} + i$
- $z = 1 + i$ and $z = 1 - i$

## Explanation

Since $(z - i)^2 = z^2 - 2iz + i^2 = z^2 - 2iz - 1$, the equation can be written $(z - i)^2 - 1 = 0$, that is $(z - i)^2 = 1$. Hence $z - i = 1$ or $z - i = -1$, and the solutions are $z = 1 + i$ and $z = -1 + i$. The coefficients of the equation are not all real, therefore the two solutions are not conjugate.
