How many solutions in $\mathbb{C}$ does the equation $z^2 = \bar{z}$ have?

- $4$
- $2$
- $3$
- $1$

## Explanation

The equation contains $\bar{z}$, so one sets $z = x + iy$ with $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$. Since $z^2 = x^2 - y^2 + 2ixy$ and $\bar{z} = x - iy$, the equation is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 - y^2 = x \\[4pt]
2xy = -y
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation one has $y = 0$ or $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. For $y = 0$ the first becomes $x^2 = x$, so $x = 0$ or $x = 1$. For $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ the first becomes $\dfrac{1}{4} - y^2 = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is $y^2 = \dfrac{3}{4}$, so $y = \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Therefore the equation has **four solutions**, $0$, $1$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2} \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\, i$.
