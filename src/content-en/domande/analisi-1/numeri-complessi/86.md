Which of the following polynomials is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$?

- $z^2 + z + 1$
- $z^2 - 4$
- $z^2 + 4z + 4$
- $z^2 - z - 1$

## Explanation

A real polynomial of degree two factors over $\mathbb{R}$ into two first-degree factors if and only if its discriminant is greater than or equal to zero. The discriminant of $z^2 + z + 1$ is $1 - 4 = -3 < 0$, so its roots are complex conjugate and the polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$. The other three polynomials have discriminant $16$, $0$ and $5$, and indeed $z^2 - 4 = (z - 2)(z + 2)$, $z^2 + 4z + 4 = (z + 2)^2$, while $z^2 - z - 1$ has the real roots $\dfrac{1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$.
