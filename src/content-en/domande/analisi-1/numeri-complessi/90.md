The polynomial $z^4 + 4$ has no real roots. How is it written as a product of two real polynomials of degree two?

- $\left(z^2 + 2\right)^2$
- $\left(z^2 - 2\right)\left(z^2 + 2\right)$
- $\left(z^2 - 2z - 2\right)\left(z^2 + 2z - 2\right)$
- $\left(z^2 - 2z + 2\right)\left(z^2 + 2z + 2\right)$

## Explanation

The roots of $z^4 + 4$ are the fourth roots of $-4 = 4\, e^{i\pi}$, that is $\sqrt{2}\, e^{\,i\frac{\pi + 2k\pi}{4}}$ with $k = 0, 1, 2, 3$, hence $1 + i$, $-1 + i$, $-1 - i$ and $1 - i$. Pairing each root with its conjugate gives the factors
$$
\begin{gathered}
(z - 1 - i)(z - 1 + i) = z^2 - 2z + 2, \\[4pt]
(z + 1 - i)(z + 1 + i) = z^2 + 2z + 2 .
\end{gathered}
$$
Both have discriminant $-4 < 0$, therefore the real factorization is $z^4 + 4 = \left(z^2 - 2z + 2\right)\left(z^2 + 2z + 2\right)$.
