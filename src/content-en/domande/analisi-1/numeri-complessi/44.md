A polynomial $p$ with real coefficients of degree $5$ has $1 + i$ and $2i$ among its roots. Which statement is true?

- $p$ has exactly two roots in $\mathbb{C}$
- $1 - i$ and $-2i$ are also roots of $p$, and the fifth root is real
- $-1 - i$ and $-2i$ are also roots of $p$, and the fifth root is real
- $1 - i$ and $-2i$ are also roots of $p$, and the fifth root may be a non-real number

## Explanation

By the **fundamental theorem of algebra** the polynomial $p$ has exactly $5$ roots counted with multiplicity. Since the coefficients are real, by the complex conjugate root theorem $\overline{1 + i} = 1 - i$ and $\overline{2i} = -2i$ are also roots of $p$. The non-real roots come in conjugate pairs with the same multiplicity, so their number is even. Therefore the fifth root is **real**.
