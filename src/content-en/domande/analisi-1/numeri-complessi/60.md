The number $z = 3\, e^{i\frac{2\pi}{5}}$ is written in exponential form. How is its conjugate $\bar{z}$ written in the same form?

- $3\, e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{5}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\, e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{5}}$
- $3\, e^{i\frac{3\pi}{5}}$
- $3\, e^{-i\frac{3\pi}{5}}$

## Explanation

Since cosine is even and sine is odd,
$$
\bar{z} = 3\left(\cos\frac{2\pi}{5} - i\sin\frac{2\pi}{5}\right) = 3\left(\cos\left(-\frac{2\pi}{5}\right) + i\sin\left(-\frac{2\pi}{5}\right)\right).
$$
Hence $\bar{z} = 3\, e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{5}}$, that is, the conjugate has the same modulus and opposite argument, as the reflection of $z$ across the real axis.
