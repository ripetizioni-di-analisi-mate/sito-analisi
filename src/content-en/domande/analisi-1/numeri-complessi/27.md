The numbers $z$ whose real part equals their modulus, that is, $\operatorname{Re}(z) = |z|$, form a set in the complex plane. Which one?

- The whole real axis
- The real half-axis of the numbers $x \geq 0$
- The imaginary axis
- Only the point $z = 0$

## Explanation

Setting $z = x + iy$ with $x, y$ real, the equation becomes $x = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. The right-hand side is nonnegative, so every solution has $x \geq 0$. For $x \geq 0$ both sides are nonnegative, hence the equation is equivalent to the equation between their squares, $x^2 = x^2 + y^2$, that is, $y = 0$. The set is therefore formed by the real numbers $x \geq 0$, that is, by the nonnegative real half-axis, origin included. Negative real numbers are excluded: for $z = -2$ one has $\operatorname{Re}(z) = -2$ and $|z| = 2$.
