What is the principal argument, in $(-\pi, \pi]$, of the product $(-1 + i)(-1 + i\sqrt{3})$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$
- $\dfrac{7\pi}{12}$
- $-\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$
- $-\dfrac{7\pi}{12}$

## Explanation

The number $-1 + i$ lies in the second quadrant and has principal argument $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$; $-1 + i\sqrt{3}$ also lies in the second quadrant and has principal argument $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. In a product the arguments add, so an argument of the product is $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \dfrac{17\pi}{12}$. This value exceeds $\pi$, therefore the principal argument is $\dfrac{17\pi}{12} - 2\pi = -\dfrac{7\pi}{12}$. In algebraic form the product equals $(1 - \sqrt{3}) - i(1 + \sqrt{3})$, which has negative real and imaginary parts, in agreement with an argument between $-\pi$ and $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
