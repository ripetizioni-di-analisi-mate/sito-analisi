How many solutions in $\mathbb{C}$ does the equation $z^6 = -64$ have, and how many of them are real?

- Two solutions, $2i$ and $-2i$
- Six solutions, two of them real
- Three solutions, none of them real
- Six solutions, none of them real

## Explanation

The number $-64$ is nonzero, so by the theorem on **$n$-th roots** the equation has exactly $6$ distinct solutions. Since $-64 = 64\, e^{i\pi}$, the solutions are $z_k = 2\, e^{\,i \frac{\pi + 2k\pi}{6}}$ with $k = 0, \dots, 5$, and their arguments are $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$. None of these arguments is a multiple of $\pi$, therefore no solution is real. The same result follows from the fact that the sixth power of a real number is greater than or equal to $0$.
