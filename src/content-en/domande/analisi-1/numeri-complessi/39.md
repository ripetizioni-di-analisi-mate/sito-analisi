Which of the following numbers is a sixth root of unity, that is a solution of $z^6 = 1$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}\, i$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\, i$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\, i$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}\, i$

## Explanation

The sixth roots of unity are $z_k = e^{\,i \frac{2k\pi}{6}} = e^{\,i \frac{k\pi}{3}}$ with $k = 0, \dots, 5$, so they have modulus $1$ and argument a multiple of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The number $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}\, i$ has modulus $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, therefore it is excluded. The numbers $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}\, i$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\, i$ have modulus $1$ but arguments $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, which are not multiples of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The number $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\, i$ has modulus $1$ and argument $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so it is the sixth root of unity $z_1$.
