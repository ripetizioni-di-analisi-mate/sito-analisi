The polynomial $p(z) = z^2 - (1 + i)\,z + i$ has the root $z = i$. Which statement is true?

- The roots of $p$ are $i$ and $-i$, by the conjugate root theorem
- The roots of $p$ are $i$ and $1$, and the conjugate root theorem does not apply
- The roots of $p$ are $i$ and $-1$
- The only root of $p$ is $i$, with multiplicity $2$

## Explanation

By the fundamental theorem of algebra $p(z) = (z - i)(z - z_2)$, and expanding the product the coefficient of $z$ is $-(i + z_2)$. Comparing it with the coefficient $-(1 + i)$ of $p$ gives $i + z_2 = 1 + i$, so $z_2 = 1$. The conjugate root theorem requires real coefficients, and here the hypothesis is not satisfied because the coefficients $-(1 + i)$ and $i$ are not real. Indeed $p(-i) = -1 + (1 + i)\,i + i = -2 + 2i \neq 0$.
