The points $z$ whose sum of distances from $2$ and from $-2$ equals $6$, that is, those with $|z - 2| + |z + 2| = 6$, form a curve. Which one?

- The ellipse with foci $(\pm 2, 0)$ and semi-axes $3$ and $\sqrt{5}$
- The ellipse with foci $(\pm 2, 0)$ and semi-axes $6$ and $4\sqrt{2}$
- The circle with center at the origin and radius $3$
- The ellipse with foci $(0, \pm 2)$ and semi-axes $3$ and $\sqrt{5}$

## Explanation

The number $|z - 2|$ is the distance of $z$ from the point $(2, 0)$ and $|z + 2|$ is the distance of $z$ from the point $(-2, 0)$. The points whose sum of distances from two fixed points is constant, and greater than the distance between the two points, form the ellipse with those points as foci; here $6 > 4$. The sum of the distances is $2a = 6$, so the semi-major axis is $a = 3$; half the focal distance is $c = 2$, therefore the semi-minor axis is $b = \sqrt{a^2 - c^2} = \sqrt{5}$. The Cartesian equation is $\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{5} = 1$.
