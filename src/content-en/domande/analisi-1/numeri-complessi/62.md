The complex number $z$ has principal argument $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. What is the principal argument, in $(-\pi, \pi]$, of its opposite $-z$?

- $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$

## Explanation

Since $-1 = e^{i\pi}$, one has $-z = e^{i\pi} z$, so by the product formula in exponential form the argument of $-z$ is obtained by adding $\pi$ to the argument of $z$. This gives $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + \pi = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, which does not belong to $(-\pi, \pi]$. Subtracting $2\pi$ gives the principal argument $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} - 2\pi = -\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. The angles $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ are the principal arguments of $\bar{z}$ and of $-\bar{z}$.
