Two reservoirs are connected by pipes in series, one of diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and one of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$. The pipe that was downstream is moved upstream, reversing their order. Which statement is true in the long-pipeline model?

- The discharge stays the same
- The discharge increases if the larger pipe is upstream
- The discharge increases if the smaller pipe is upstream
- The piezometric head at the intermediate junction stays the same

## Explanation

The equivalent resistance of the pipes in series is the sum $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = r_1 + r_2$, independent of their order. The reservoir water levels are unchanged, so the discharge $Q = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta z}{r_{\mathrm{eq}}}}$ **stays the same**.

The intermediate junction head is $h_N = z_{\mathrm{upstream}} - r_{\mathrm{upstream}}\,Q^2$, where $r_{\mathrm{upstream}}$ is the resistance of the upstream pipe. The $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe has the greater resistance, so placing it upstream lowers the junction head. The hydraulic grade line therefore changes shape between the same two reservoir levels.
