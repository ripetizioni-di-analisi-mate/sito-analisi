A pipe of resistance $r_1 = 900\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ leads from a reservoir to node $N$, where $40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is withdrawn. A second pipe of resistance $r_2 = 1500\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ continues from the node and carries $60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to a customer. What is the total head loss from the reservoir to the customer?

- $14.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $24.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8.64\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Water is withdrawn at junction $N$. The two pipes therefore carry different discharges and their resistances cannot simply be added. Continuity at the junction gives the discharge in the first pipe,
$$
Q_1 = 60 + 40 = 100\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
Each pipe's head loss must be calculated with its own discharge, giving
$$
\Delta H_1 = 900 \cdot 0.100^2 = 9.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\Delta H_2 = 1500 \cdot 0.060^2 = 5.40\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Both losses occur along the same flow path. They therefore add to give a total head loss of $9.00 + 5.40 = 14.40\,\mathrm{m}$.

Adding the resistances and using the upstream discharge throughout would give $24.00\,\mathrm{m}$. This calculation would be valid only if no water were withdrawn at the intermediate junction.
