The loop shown receives $150\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at node $1$ and delivers $30\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at node $2$, $70\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at node $3$ and $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at node $4$. To start the Hardy Cross method, select an initial distribution $(Q_{12};\ Q_{23};\ Q_{14};\ Q_{43})$ in $\mathrm{L/s}$, taking the flows from $1$ to $2$, $2$ to $3$, $1$ to $4$ and $4$ to $3$. Which distribution can be used?

::schema{nodi="1,4,16;2,20,16;3,20,4;4,4,4" condotte="1-2,0.4,;2-3,0.4,;4-3,0.4,;1-4,0.4," frecce="0.5,16,3.2,16,;20.9,16,23.5,16,;20.9,4,23.5,4,;4,3.1,4,0.6," etichette="1.8,17.4,150 L/s;25.8,16,30 L/s;25.8,4,70 L/s;4,-0.7,50 L/s" titolo="Loop with one inflow and three withdrawals"}

- $(80;\ 50;\ 70;\ 30)$
- $(75;\ 40;\ 75;\ 25)$
- $(80;\ 50;\ 70;\ 20)$
- $(90;\ 50;\ 60;\ 20)$

## Explanation

The initial flow distribution for the Hardy Cross method must satisfy all junction equations. Successive corrections then bring the flow distribution into agreement with the loop equation.

For distribution $(80;\ 50;\ 70;\ 20)$, the balance at junction $1$ is $80 + 70 = 150$, at junction $2$ it is $80 = 50 + 30$, at junction $4$ it is $70 = 20 + 50$, and at junction $3$ it is $50 + 20 = 70$. Thus **all junction equations are satisfied**.

Each other distribution violates at least one balance. With $(80;\ 50;\ 70;\ 30)$, junction $4$ receives $70\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and discharges $80$. With $(75;\ 40;\ 75;\ 25)$, junction $2$ receives $75\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and discharges $70$. With $(90;\ 50;\ 60;\ 20)$, junction $2$ receives $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and discharges $80$.
