Two reservoirs whose surface elevations differ by $\Delta z = 30.00\,\mathrm{m}$ must exchange $120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. An existing pipe of length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$ will be joined in series to a new section of length $600\,\mathrm{m}$ with $\lambda = 0.022$. What minimum diameter does the long-pipeline model require for the new section?

- $221\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $194\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $250\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $345\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The existing pipe has resistance
$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1000}{9.81\,\pi^2\,0.250^5} = 1861\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
The available head equals the total head loss, so the total resistance cannot exceed $\dfrac{\Delta z}{Q^2} = \dfrac{30.00}{0.120^2} = 2083\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. Resistances in series add, leaving an allowable resistance of $r_2 \leq 2083 - 1861 = 222\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ for the new pipe.

Solving the defining equation for hydraulic resistance gives
$$
D_2 \geq \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 600}{9.81\,\pi^2 \cdot 222}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = 0.345\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $345\,\mathrm{mm}$. A new pipe of diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ would raise the total resistance to $2978\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and reduce the discharge to $100\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

The value $221\,\mathrm{mm}$ allocates the entire available resistance to the new pipe, ignoring the existing pipe. The value $194\,\mathrm{mm}$ results from adding $r_1$ instead of subtracting it.
