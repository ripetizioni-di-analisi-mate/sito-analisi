To calibrate a horizontal pipe of diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, the discharge $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the pressures at sections $600\,\mathrm{m}$ apart are measured, as shown. The pressures are $p_1 = 420.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ upstream and $p_2 = 384.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$ downstream. What is the Darcy friction factor $\lambda$?

::schema{nodi="1,2,4;2,22,4" condotte="1-2,0.6,D = 250 mm" frecce="-4.5,4,-0.6,4," etichette="-2.6,5.3,Q = 60 L/s;2,6.4,p_1 = 420.0 kPa;22,6.4,p_2 = 384.1 kPa" quote="1,2,600 m,1.6" titolo="Horizontal pipe section between two pressure gauges"}

- $0.0100$
- $0.00500$
- $0.0200$
- $0.196$

## Explanation

The two sections have the same elevation and diameter, so their elevation heads and velocity heads are equal. The head loss between them therefore equals the difference in pressure head,
$$
\Delta H = \frac{p_1 - p_2}{\gamma} = \frac{35.9}{9.81} = 3.66\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The loss is $\Delta H = r\,Q^2$ with $r = \dfrac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5}$, so
$$
\lambda = \frac{\Delta H\,g\,\pi^2 D^5}{8\,L\,Q^2} = \frac{3.66 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5}{8 \cdot 600 \cdot 0.060^2} = 0.0200 .
$$
The value $0.0100$ results from writing the velocity head as $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$. The value $0.00500$ uses the hydraulic radius $\dfrac{D}{4}$ in place of the diameter in the Darcy–Weisbach equation, while $0.196$ results from dividing the pressure difference by $\rho$ instead of $\gamma$.
