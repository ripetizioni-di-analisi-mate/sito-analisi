A pump with characteristic curve $H_p = 60 - 1500\,Q^2$ ($H_p$ in meters, $Q$ in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$) conveys water from a reservoir with free surface at $20.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to one with free surface at $44.00\,\mathrm{m}$ through a branch of resistance $r = 2500\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. What discharge does the long-pipeline model predict?

- $94.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $120\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $190\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $145\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

At the operating point, both the branch energy balance and the pump characteristic must hold,
$$
\begin{cases}
20.00 + H_p = 44.00 + 2500\,Q^2 \\[4pt]
H_p = 60 - 1500\,Q^2
\end{cases}
$$
Substituting the second equation into the first gives $20 + 60 - 1500\,Q^2 = 44 + 2500\,Q^2$, or $4000\,Q^2 = 36$. Thus
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{36}{4000}} = 0.0949\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 94.9\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
At this operating point the pump head is $H_p = 60 - 1500 \cdot 0.0090 = 46.5\,\mathrm{m}$, equal to the static lift of $24.00\,\mathrm{m}$ plus a head loss of $22.5\,\mathrm{m}$.

The value $120\,\mathrm{L/s}$ treats the pump head as a constant $60\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $190\,\mathrm{L/s}$ subtracts the pump coefficient from the resistance instead of adding it, and $145\,\mathrm{L/s}$ adds the static lift to the pump head instead of subtracting it.
