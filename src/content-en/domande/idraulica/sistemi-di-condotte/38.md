A single reservoir supplies a branched network, and the withdrawals at every node are known. How is the discharge in any branch found?

- By adding the nodal withdrawals downstream of the branch
- From the network loop equations
- By dividing the flow in inverse proportion to the square roots of the resistances
- By the Hardy Cross method

## Explanation

A branched network contains no closed loops. Each junction therefore has a single path to the reservoir, and the discharge through a branch supplies precisely the junctions downstream of it.

Applying continuity first at the terminal junctions and working back toward the reservoir gives the flow in each branch as the **sum of the downstream junction demands**. This step requires no branch resistances.

The resistances enter in the next step. Starting at the reservoir and following each flow path, calculate the head loss in each branch and subtract it to obtain the piezometric heads at successive junctions.
