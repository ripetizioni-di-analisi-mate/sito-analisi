The initial distribution chosen for a Hardy Cross loop calculation is in error: at node $2$, the inflow exceeds the sum of outflow and withdrawal by $5\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What happens to this imbalance in successive iterations?

- It vanishes after the first correction
- It decreases at each iteration and tends to zero
- It remains $5\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at every iteration
- It halves at each iteration

## Explanation

Junction $2$ joins two consecutive branches of the loop. In the chosen loop direction, one enters the junction and the other leaves it. The correction $\Delta Q$ therefore appears once with a positive sign and once with a negative sign in the junction balance.

Whatever its value, the correction leaves every junction balance unchanged. The initial imbalance **remains $5\,\mathrm{L/s}$** at every iteration. The method drives the signed head-loss sum to zero, but the final distribution still violates continuity at junction $2$. The starting distribution must therefore satisfy all the junction equations.
