A network branch of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ contains a valve with minor-loss coefficient $\xi = 8$. What hydraulic resistance does the valve add to the branch?

- $2066\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $413\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $826\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $4078\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$

## Explanation

The local head loss is $\Delta H_{\mathrm{loc}} = \xi\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ and the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{4Q}{\pi D^2}$, so
$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{loc}} = \xi\,\frac{16\,Q^2}{2g\,\pi^2 D^4} = \frac{8\,\xi}{g\,\pi^2 D^4}\,Q^2 .
$$
The loss is proportional to $Q^2$. The valve therefore acts as a branch with resistance
$$
r_v = \frac{8\,\xi}{g\,\pi^2 D^4} = \frac{8 \cdot 8}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.200^4} = 413\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
The valve and pipe carry the same discharge and are in series, so their resistances add. The valve contributes $r_v$ to the pipe resistance.

The value $2066\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ uses $D^5$ in the denominator, as in the formula for frictional head loss. The value $826\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ uses $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ for the velocity head, and $4078\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ omits the factor $\pi^2$.
