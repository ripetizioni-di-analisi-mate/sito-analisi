Two pipes in series carry $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The first has length $600\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_1 = 0.022$. The second has length $400\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.024$. What is the total head loss?

- $1.62\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8.92\,\mathrm{m}$
- $21.1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The resistances of the two pipes are
$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 600}{9.81\,\pi^2\,0.300^5} = 449\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.024 \cdot 400}{9.81\,\pi^2\,0.200^5} = 2479\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
The pipes are in series, so $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 449 + 2479 = 2928\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and the total head loss is
$$
\Delta H = r_{\mathrm{eq}}\,Q^2 = 2928 \cdot 0.060^2 = 10.5\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe accounts for $8.92\,\mathrm{m}$, or $85\%$ of the total head loss. The value $21.1\,\mathrm{m}$ results from writing the velocity head as $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ instead of $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$.
