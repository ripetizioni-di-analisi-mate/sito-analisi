A pipe connects two reservoirs whose surface elevations differ by a fixed amount. It is replaced by another pipe of the same length and friction factor but twice the diameter. By what factor does the discharge increase?

- $5.66$ times
- $4$ times
- $32$ times
- $2.83$ times

## Explanation

The resistance is $r = \dfrac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5}$. At a fixed length and friction factor, it is therefore inversely proportional to $D^5$. Doubling the diameter reduces the resistance to $\dfrac{r}{32}$.

Both pipes have the same available head, so $\Delta z = r\,Q^2$ gives $Q = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta z}{r}}$. Discharge is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{r}$ and hence directly proportional to $D^{\frac{5}{2}}$. The discharge ratio is therefore
$$
\frac{Q'}{Q} = 2^{\frac{5}{2}} = \sqrt{32} = 5.66 .
$$
The value $32$ is the resistance ratio rather than the discharge ratio.
