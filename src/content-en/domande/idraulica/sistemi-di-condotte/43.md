In a clockwise loop traversal, branch $1\!-\!4$ carries $55\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $1$ to $4$, opposite to the traversal direction. The first Hardy Cross correction is $\Delta Q = -5.00\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the branch discharge after the correction?

- $50.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $1$ toward $4$
- $60.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $1$ toward $4$
- $55.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $1$ toward $4$
- $60.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $4$ toward $1$

## Explanation

Add the correction to each signed branch discharge, taking the chosen loop direction as positive. Flow in branch $1\!-\!4$ opposes this direction, so its initial signed discharge is $-55\,\mathrm{L/s}$. After correction it becomes
$$
-55.0 + (-5.00) = -60.0\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The result is still negative. The flow direction is unchanged and the branch carries $60.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $1$ to $4$.

A negative correction reduces flows along the loop direction and increases the magnitudes of opposing flows. The corrected discharges therefore continue to satisfy the junction equations.
