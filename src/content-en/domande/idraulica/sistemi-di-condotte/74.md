In a branched network, a reservoir feeds node $A$. Branch $A\!-\!B$ leads from that node to terminal node $B$, and branch $A\!-\!C$ leads to terminal node $C$. All node withdrawals are fixed. Pipe $A\!-\!C$ is replaced with a larger pipe. Which pressures change?

- Only the pressure at node $C$ increases
- The pressures increase at every node
- The pressure at node $C$ increases and the pressure at node $B$ decreases
- None, because the withdrawals determine the discharges

## Explanation

In a branched network, continuity alone sets branch discharges equal to the sum of downstream demands. Changing a pipe diameter therefore leaves those discharges unchanged.

The main supplying $A$ retains both its discharge and its resistance, so the head at junction $A$ stays the same. The same holds for branch $A\!-\!B$ and the head at junction $B$. Branch $A\!-\!C$ now has a smaller resistance at the same discharge. Its head loss falls and the head at junction $C$ rises. Since the junction elevations are fixed, **only the pressure at $C$ increases**.
