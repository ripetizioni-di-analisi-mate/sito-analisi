In analyzing a pipe system, the hydraulic resistance of each branch is taken to be independent of discharge. What assumption justifies this?

- The flow is laminar in every branch
- Minor losses dominate friction losses
- The discharge is the same in every branch
- The flow is fully rough turbulent in every branch

## Explanation

The branch resistance is $r = \dfrac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5}$. Only the friction factor $\lambda$ can introduce a dependence on discharge.

In the **fully rough turbulent** regime, the friction factor depends only on the relative roughness $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D}$ and is independent of the Reynolds number. As discharge varies, both $\lambda$ and the branch resistance therefore remain constant.

In laminar flow, $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re}$. Increasing the discharge decreases $\lambda$, so the branch resistance must be updated at each step of the calculation.
