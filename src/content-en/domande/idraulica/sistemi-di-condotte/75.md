A reservoir with free surface at $118.00\,\mathrm{m}$ feeds a branched network. At peak demand the pressure at node $B$, at elevation $72.00\,\mathrm{m}$, is $31.40\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. What is the pressure at $B$ at night when all withdrawals are zero?

- $31.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $16.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $46.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $118.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With zero demand there is no flow anywhere in the network. All losses $r\,Q^2$ vanish and the hydraulic grade line is horizontal at the reservoir water level. The head at junction $B$ is then $118.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the pressure head is
$$
\frac{p_B}{\gamma} = 118.00 - 72.00 = 46.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $451\,\mathrm{kPa}$. This is the maximum pressure at the junction. At peak demand, losses along the supply path reduce it by $14.60\,\mathrm{m}$.

The value $31.40\,\mathrm{m}$ assumes unchanged losses. The value $16.80\,\mathrm{m}$ subtracts the losses a second time instead of restoring the lost head, and $118.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is the junction head rather than its pressure head.
