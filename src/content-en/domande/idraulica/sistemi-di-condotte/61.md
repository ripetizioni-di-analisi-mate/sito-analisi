Two parallel pipes of equal length and friction factor connect the same nodes. Their diameters are $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $200\,\mathrm{mm}$. The mean velocity in the pipe of diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ is $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the mean velocity in the pipe of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$?

- $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.38\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.22\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The two pipes connect the same junctions and therefore have the same head loss. Using the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives
$$
\lambda\,\frac{L}{D_1}\,\frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \lambda\,\frac{L}{D_2}\,\frac{v_2^2}{2g} ,
$$
Since both $\lambda$ and $L$ are the same, $\dfrac{v_1^2}{D_1} = \dfrac{v_2^2}{D_2}$. Hence
$$
v_2 = v_1\,\sqrt{\frac{D_2}{D_1}} = 1.50 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{200}{300}} = 1.22\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Velocity increases as $\sqrt{D}$ and is therefore lower in the smaller-diameter pipe.

The value $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ assumes equal velocities in the branches. The value $3.38\,\mathrm{m/s}$ applies continuity as though the two pipes were in series, and $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ assumes velocity is proportional to diameter.
