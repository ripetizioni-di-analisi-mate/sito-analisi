Two parallel pipes connect the same nodes and have the same length and diameter. The first is old and encrusted with roughness coefficient $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, and the second is new with $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. The resistances follow the Gauckler–Strickler equation. What coefficient $k_s$ would make a single pipe of the same length and diameter equivalent to the pair?

- $86.0\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$
- $120\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$
- $60.0\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$
- $29.2\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$

## Explanation

The Gauckler–Strickler equation gives $r = \dfrac{10.29\,L}{k_s^2\,D^{\frac{16}{3}}}$ for the resistance of a full circular pipe. Thus
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}} = \frac{k_s\,D^{\frac{8}{3}}}{\sqrt{10.29\,L}} .
$$
For two parallel branches, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_{\mathrm{eq}}}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_1}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_2}}$. Both pipes and the equivalent pipe have the same length and diameter, so the factor $\dfrac{D^{\frac{8}{3}}}{\sqrt{10.29\,L}}$ cancels, leaving
$$
k_{s,e} = k_{s,1} + k_{s,2} = 50 + 70 = 120\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s} .
$$
The equivalent coefficient exceeds either individual coefficient because the equivalent pipe must carry the flow of two pipes through one cross section.

The value $86.0\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ adds the squared coefficients, effectively adding inverse resistances instead of inverse square roots. The value $60.0\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ averages the coefficients, and $29.2\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ applies $\dfrac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$, the rule for electrical resistors in parallel, directly to the roughness coefficients.
