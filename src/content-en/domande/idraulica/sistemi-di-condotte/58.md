Two parallel branches connect nodes $M$ and $V$, and the combined discharge is held fixed. A third branch is added between the same nodes. What happens to the discharges in the two existing branches?

- Both stay the same because the head loss between the nodes is unchanged
- Only the discharge in the higher-resistance branch decreases
- Both decrease, and the lower-resistance branch loses a larger fraction of its discharge
- Both decrease in the same proportion

## Explanation

A third branch adds a positive term to $\sum_i \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_i}}$, reducing the equivalent resistance of the parallel branches. With total discharge fixed, the head loss between the junctions $\Delta H = r_{\mathrm{eq}}\,Q^2$ therefore decreases.

Each original branch carries $Q_i = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta H}{r_i}}$, so its discharge is multiplied by the same factor $\sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta H'}{\Delta H}}$, where $\Delta H'$ is the new head loss. Their ratio $\dfrac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{r_2}{r_1}}$ depends only on the resistances. It stays constant and the two discharges **decrease in the same proportion**.
