A reservoir with free surface at $120.00\,\mathrm{m}$ feeds node $N$ at elevation $95.00\,\mathrm{m}$ through a pipe of resistance $r = 780\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ carrying $120\,\mathrm{L/s}$, as shown. What is the pressure head at the node in meters of water?

::schema{nodi="S,3,16;N,24,5" condotte="S-N,0.6,r = 780" acqua="0,13 3,13 3,19 0,19" solidi="0,12 3,12 3,13 0,13" pelo="0,3,19,120.00 m" etichette="24,2.6,z_N = 95.00 m;13,13,Q = 120 L/s" titolo="Pipe supplying node $N$"}

- $25.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13.77\,\mathrm{m}$
- $11.23\,\mathrm{m}$
- $36.23\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The head loss in the pipe is
$$
\Delta H = r\,Q^2 = 780 \cdot 0.120^2 = 11.23\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
In the long-pipe approximation, subtracting this loss from the upstream head gives the piezometric head at the junction, $h_N = 120.00 - 11.23 = 108.77\,\mathrm{m}$.

Subtracting the junction elevation from its piezometric head gives the pressure head,
$$
\frac{p_N}{\gamma} = h_N - z_N = 108.77 - 95.00 = 13.77\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $135\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $25.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is the elevation difference between the free surface and the junction. It would give the pressure head only at zero discharge.
