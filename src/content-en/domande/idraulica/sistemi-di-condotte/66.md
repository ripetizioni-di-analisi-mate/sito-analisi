A pipe of length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ and total resistance $r = 1500\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ receives $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ upstream. All the inflow is withdrawn uniformly along the pipe, leaving zero downstream throughflow. What is the head loss over the upstream half?

- $2.03\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.08\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.52\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.54\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Resistance is proportional to length, so the upstream half of the pipe has resistance $750\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. Discharge decreases linearly from $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to zero and is therefore $45\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at the midpoint.

The upstream half thus has uniform withdrawal, inflow $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and outflow $45\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Its head loss is
$$
\Delta H_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{750}{3}\left(0.090^2 + 0.090 \cdot 0.045 + 0.045^2\right) = 250 \cdot 0.014175 = 3.54\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The head loss in the entire pipe is $\dfrac{1500 \cdot 0.090^2}{3} = 4.05\,\mathrm{m}$, leaving only $0.51\,\mathrm{m}$ for the downstream half. Most of the loss occurs upstream, where the discharge is greater.

The value $2.03\,\mathrm{m}$ is half the total loss. The value $6.08\,\mathrm{m}$ assumes a constant discharge of $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ throughout the upstream half, and $1.52\,\mathrm{m}$ assumes the midpoint discharge throughout that half.
