The discharge distribution in a square loop is known. The discharges are $Q_{12} = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$, $Q_{23} = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$, $Q_{14} = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $Q_{43} = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$, each directed from its first named node to its second, and the corresponding resistances are $r_{12} = 900$, $r_{23} = 2500$, $r_{14} = 1600$ and $r_{43} = 1050\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. Node $1$ has piezometric head $85.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and node $3$ lies at elevation $76.00\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the pressure head at node $3$ in meters of water?

- $4.76\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.76\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13.24\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

To find the head at junction $3$, start at junction $1$ and follow any connecting path, subtracting losses along branches traversed in the flow direction. Along path $1\!-\!2\!-\!3$, the losses are $900 \cdot 0.060^2 = 3.24\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2500 \cdot 0.020^2 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, giving
$$
h_3 = 85.00 - 3.24 - 1.00 = 80.76\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Along path $1\!-\!4\!-\!3$ the result is $1600 \cdot 0.040^2 + 1050 \cdot 0.040^2 = 2.56 + 1.68 = 4.24\,\mathrm{m}$, the same total loss as required by the loop equation.

The pressure head at the junction is therefore
$$
\frac{p_3}{\gamma} = 80.76 - 76.00 = 4.76\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $46.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $5.76\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the head loss in branch $2\!-\!3$, $13.24\,\mathrm{m}$ adds the losses to the head instead of subtracting them, and $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$ neglects the losses.
