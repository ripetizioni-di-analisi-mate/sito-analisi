Two pipes in series connect reservoirs whose water surfaces differ in elevation by $\Delta z = 25.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The first pipe has length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ and diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$, with $\lambda_1 = 0.022$. The second has length $900\,\mathrm{m}$ and diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, with $\lambda_2 = 0.023$. Using the long-pipeline approximation, find the discharge.

- $167\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $97.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $119\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $137\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The hydraulic resistance of a pipe section is $r = \dfrac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5}$. For the two sections this gives
$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1200}{9.81\,\pi^2\,0.300^5} = 898\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 900}{9.81\,\pi^2\,0.250^5} = 1751\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
The two pipes carry the same discharge, and their head losses add. The equivalent resistance is therefore $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = r_1 + r_2 = 2649\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

In the long-pipeline approximation, the water-surface elevation difference equals the total head loss. Solving $\Delta z = r_{\mathrm{eq}} Q^2$ gives
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{25.00}{2649}} = 0.0971\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 97.1\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The value $167\,\mathrm{L/s}$ accounts only for the first pipe, and $119\,\mathrm{L/s}$ only for the second. The value $137\,\mathrm{L/s}$ results from averaging the two resistances instead of adding them.
