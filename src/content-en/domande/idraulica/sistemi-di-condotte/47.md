A pipe of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.020$ carries $100\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What percentage of the friction head loss does the velocity head represent?

- $1.00\%$
- $2.00\%$
- $0.250\%$
- $0.500\%$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{4Q}{\pi D^2} = \dfrac{4 \cdot 0.100}{\pi \cdot 0.300^2} = 1.41\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving a velocity head of $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.102\,\mathrm{m}$.

The **Darcy–Weisbach** equation gives the frictional head loss
$$
\Delta H = \lambda\,\frac{L}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.020 \cdot \frac{1500}{0.300} \cdot 0.102 = 10.2\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The ratio of the two quantities is $\dfrac{D}{\lambda L} = \dfrac{0.300}{0.020 \cdot 1500} = 0.0100$, or $1.00\%$, and is independent of discharge. The velocity head is therefore negligible compared with the frictional head loss, justifying the **long-pipe approximation** here.

The value $2.00\%$ results from writing the velocity head as $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$. The value $0.500\%$ uses $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ only in the friction loss calculation. The value $0.250\%$ uses the hydraulic radius $\dfrac{D}{4}$ in place of the diameter in the Darcy–Weisbach equation.
