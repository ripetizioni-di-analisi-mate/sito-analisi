All branches of a loop carry laminar flow. Each branch head loss is proportional to discharge, $\Delta H_i = k_i\,Q_i$, where $Q_i$ has its sign for the chosen traversal direction. Applying the reasoning behind the Hardy Cross method, what correction follows?

- $\Delta Q = -\dfrac{\sum_i k_i Q_i}{\sum_i k_i}$, which gives the exact solution in a single iteration
- $\Delta Q = -\dfrac{\sum_i k_i Q_i}{2\sum_i k_i}$, which requires several iterations
- $\Delta Q = -\dfrac{\sum_i k_i Q_i |Q_i|}{2\sum_i k_i |Q_i|}$, the same correction as for turbulent flow
- None, because the method applies only to losses proportional to the square of discharge

## Explanation

Adding the same correction $\Delta Q$ to all signed discharges in the loop preserves the junction balances. The signed head-loss sum around the loop then becomes
$$
F(\Delta Q) = \sum_i k_i\left(Q_i + \Delta Q\right) = \sum_i k_i Q_i + \Delta Q \sum_i k_i .
$$
The function $F$ is linear, so the loop equation $F(\Delta Q) = 0$ can be solved exactly,
$$
\Delta Q = -\,\frac{\sum_i k_i Q_i}{\sum_i k_i} .
$$
Its tangent is the function itself, so a single Newton step gives the exact solution. Thus **one iteration** suffices for an isolated loop. In turbulent flow the factor $2$ comes from the derivative $2|t|$ of $t\,|t|$. Here the derivative of $t$ is simply $1$.
