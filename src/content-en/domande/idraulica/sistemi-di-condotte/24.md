Two pipes of resistances $r_1 = 1600\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_2 = 3600\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ connect the same two nodes in parallel. What is their equivalent resistance?

- $5200\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $576\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $1108\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $2600\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$

## Explanation

The same head loss acts across both parallel branches, so $Q_i = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta H}{r_i}}$. Continuity gives $Q = \sqrt{\Delta H}\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_1}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_2}}\right)$, and comparison with $\Delta H = r_{\mathrm{eq}} Q^2$ yields
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_1}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_2}}\right)^2} .
$$
Since $\sqrt{1600} = 40$ and $\sqrt{3600} = 60$, we have $\dfrac{1}{40} + \dfrac{1}{60} = \dfrac{1}{24}$ and hence
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 24^2 = 576\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
The equivalent resistance is less than either branch resistance because two branches carry more flow than one at the same head loss. The formula $\dfrac{r_1 r_2}{r_1 + r_2}$ would give $1108\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. It applies to electrical resistors, for which voltage drop is proportional to current. Here, head loss varies with discharge squared.
