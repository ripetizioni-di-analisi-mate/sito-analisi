A pipe receives $Q_m$ at its upstream end and supplies a uniform withdrawal along its length, leaving no downstream throughflow. How does its actual head loss compare with the head loss if the entire discharge $Q_m$ traveled the full length?

- Two thirds
- One third
- One half
- One sixth

## Explanation

With uniformly distributed withdrawal, the head loss is
$$
\Delta H = \frac{r}{3}\left(Q_m^2 + Q_m Q_v + Q_v^2\right) ,
$$
With $Q_v = 0$, the two terms containing $Q_v$ vanish, leaving $\Delta H = \dfrac{r\,Q_m^2}{3}$.

If the discharge $Q_m$ remained constant along the whole pipe, the head loss would be $r\,Q_m^2$. The loss with distributed withdrawal is therefore **one third** of this value.

The equivalent discharge gives the same result. With $Q_v = 0$, it is $Q_e = \dfrac{Q_m}{\sqrt{3}}$, so $\Delta H = r\,Q_e^2 = \dfrac{r\,Q_m^2}{3}$.
