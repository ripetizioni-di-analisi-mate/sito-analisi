Two parallel pipes connect nodes $M$ and $V$. The first has length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_1 = 0.022$. The second has length $700\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.023$. At node $M$ the inflow is $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the discharge in the first pipe?

- $71.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $48.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $82.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $60.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The branch resistances are $r_1 = 1861\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_2 = 4157\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

The branches connect the same nodes and therefore have the same head loss $\Delta H$. Using $\Delta H = r_i Q_i^2$ gives
$$
\frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \sqrt{\frac{r_2}{r_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{4157}{1861}} = 1.495 .
$$
Since $Q_1 + Q_2 = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$, this gives
$$
Q_1 = 120 \cdot \frac{1.495}{1 + 1.495} = 71.9\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The branch discharges are inversely proportional to the *square roots* of the resistances. The value $82.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$ incorrectly divides the flow in inverse proportion to the resistances themselves.
