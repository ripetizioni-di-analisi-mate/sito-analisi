Two parallel branches of resistances $r_1 = 1210\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_2 = 4840\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ connect two network nodes and carry a combined discharge of $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the head loss between the nodes?

- $9.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $39.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.36\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The two values are $\sqrt{1210} = 34.79$ and $\sqrt{4840} = 69.57$. The second is twice the first, so
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{34.79} + \dfrac{1}{69.57}\right)^2} = 537.8\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
The head loss between the two junctions is therefore
$$
\Delta H = r_{\mathrm{eq}}\,Q^2 = 537.8 \cdot 0.090^2 = 4.36\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The flow split provides a check. Since $\sqrt{r_2} = 2\sqrt{r_1}$, the first branch carries $60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the second $30\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Both give the same head loss, $1210 \cdot 0.060^2 = 4840 \cdot 0.030^2 = 4.36\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $9.80\,\mathrm{m}$ would result if the first branch alone carried the entire discharge.
