A reservoir with free surface at $78.00\,\mathrm{m}$ feeds a turbine through a penstock of resistance $r = 400\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. The turbine discharges into a reservoir with free surface at $30.00\,\mathrm{m}$. In the long-pipeline model, the head used by the turbine is $H_t = \Delta z - r\,Q^2$ and the power delivered by the water is $\gamma\,Q\,H_t$. At what discharge is this power greatest?

- $346\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $245\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $200\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $40.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The difference between the reservoir water levels is $\Delta z = 78.00 - 30.00 = 48.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The power delivered to the turbine as a function of discharge is therefore
$$
P(Q) = \gamma\,Q\left(\Delta z - r\,Q^2\right) = \gamma\left(\Delta z\,Q - r\,Q^3\right) .
$$
Its derivative $P'(Q) = \gamma\left(\Delta z - 3\,r\,Q^2\right)$ is positive at small $Q$ and negative at large $Q$. The power therefore reaches its maximum where the derivative is zero,
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta z}{3\,r}} = \sqrt{\frac{48.00}{3 \cdot 400}} = 0.200\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 200\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
At this discharge the pipe loses $400 \cdot 0.200^2 = 16.0\,\mathrm{m}$, one third of the available head. The net turbine head is $32.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and the power is $9.81 \cdot 0.200 \cdot 32.0 = 62.8\,\mathrm{kW}$.

The value $346\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is the discharge at zero net head, where the power is zero. The value $245\,\mathrm{L/s}$ corresponds to a head loss equal to half the available head, and $40.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is the ratio $\dfrac{\Delta z}{3\,r}$ before taking the square root.
