The equation around a closed loop is $\sum_i r_i\,Q_i\,|Q_i| = 0$. Why does the product $Q_i |Q_i|$ appear in place of the square $Q_i^2$?

- Because it makes the loop equation linear
- Because it also accounts for minor losses
- To make the sum of the head losses around the loop positive
- Because the signed head loss changes sign when the flow direction reverses

## Explanation

Choose a direction around the loop. A branch discharge is positive if the flow follows that direction and negative if it opposes it. Piezometric head falls in the direction of flow, so the branch loss enters the loop sum with a positive sign in the first case and a negative sign in the second.

The square $Q_i^2$ is positive in either direction. The product $Q_i |Q_i|$ has the sign of $Q_i$ and the magnitude of $Q_i^2$. It therefore **changes sign when the flow reverses** and equals $Q_i^2$ when $Q_i > 0$.

The equation is still quadratic in the discharges, so solving the network still requires iteration.
