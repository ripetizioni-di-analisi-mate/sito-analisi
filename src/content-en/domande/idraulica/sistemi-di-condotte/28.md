A pipe connects two reservoirs with a fixed difference in water-surface elevation. An identical pipe is added in parallel over the entire route. How does the total discharge change?

- It increases by $41\%$
- It doubles
- It quadruples
- It increases by $32\%$

## Explanation

The two identical pipes connect the same junctions in parallel, so the resistance of the duplicated section is
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{r}}\right)^2} = \frac{r}{4} .
$$
The difference between the reservoir water levels stays the same. It follows from $\Delta z = r_{\mathrm{eq}} Q^2$ that
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta z}{\dfrac{r}{4}}} = 2\sqrt{\frac{\Delta z}{r}} = 2\,Q_0 ,
$$
so the discharge **doubles**. An increase of $41\%$ would result from halving the resistance instead of reducing it to one quarter.
