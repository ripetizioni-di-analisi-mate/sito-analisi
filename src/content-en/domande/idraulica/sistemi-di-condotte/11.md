In the network shown, a reservoir with free surface at $110.00\,\mathrm{m}$ feeds a pipe of length $700\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$, which carries $140\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to node $A$. From node $A$, branch $A\!-\!B$ delivers $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to node $B$ and branch $A\!-\!C$ ($900\,\mathrm{m}$, $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.022$) delivers $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to node $C$ at elevation $75.00\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the pressure at node $C$ in meters of water?

- $12.78\,\mathrm{m}$
- $11.63\,\mathrm{m}$
- $25.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $35.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The supply main has resistance $r_0 = 500\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$, so the piezometric head at node $A$ is
$$
h_A = 110.00 - 500 \cdot 0.140^2 = 100.20\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Branch $A\!-\!C$ has resistance $r_C = 1675\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and carries $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$, giving
$$
h_C = 100.20 - 1675 \cdot 0.090^2 = 86.63\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Subtract the node elevation from its piezometric head to find the pressure head:
$$
\frac{p_C}{\gamma} = 86.63 - 75.00 = 11.63\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $114\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $25.20\,\mathrm{m}$ neglects the loss in branch $A\!-\!C$, and $35.00\,\mathrm{m}$ neglects all the losses.
