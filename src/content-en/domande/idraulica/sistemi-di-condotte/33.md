The pipe shown has length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$, upstream discharge $Q_m = 105\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and downstream throughflow $Q_v = 30\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The rest is withdrawn uniformly along the pipe. What is the withdrawal per unit length?

::schema{nodi="M,2,6;V,20,6" condotte="M-V,0.45," frecce="4,5.4,4,3.8,;6,5.4,6,3.8,;8,5.4,8,3.8,;10,5.4,10,3.8,;12,5.4,12,3.8,;14,5.4,14,3.8,;16,5.4,16,3.8,;18,5.4,18,3.8," etichette="2,7.6,Q_m = 105 L/s;20,7.6,Q_v = 30 L/s;11,8.8,L = 1500 m;11,2.8,uniform withdrawal" titolo="Pipe with water withdrawn uniformly along its length"}

- $0.0500\,\mathrm{L/(s\,m)}$
- $0.0700\,\mathrm{L/(s\,m)}$
- $0.0200\,\mathrm{L/(s\,m)}$
- $0.0900\,\mathrm{L/(s\,m)}$

## Explanation

The discharge withdrawn along the pipe equals the upstream inflow minus the throughflow at the outlet, giving $105 - 30 = 75\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

Since withdrawal is uniform, the withdrawal per unit length is the total discharge withdrawn divided by the pipe length,
$$
p = \frac{Q_m - Q_v}{L} = \frac{75}{1500} = 0.0500\,\mathrm{L/(s\,m)} .
$$
The discharge at distance $x$ from the upstream end is then $Q(x) = 105 - 0.0500\,x$. At $x = 1500\,\mathrm{m}$, this gives $Q_v = 30\,\mathrm{L/s}$ as required.
