Three identical parallel pipes of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.023$ connect the same two nodes. What diameter must an equivalent single pipe of the same length and friction factor have?

- $600\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $249\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $346\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $310\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

For $n$ identical parallel branches, each of resistance $r$, the equivalent resistance is
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{n}{\sqrt{r}}\right)^2} = \frac{r}{n^2} ,
$$
so for three branches $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \dfrac{r}{9}$.

For the same length and friction factor, resistance is inversely proportional to $D^5$. Dividing the resistance by $9$ is therefore equivalent to multiplying $D^5$ by $9$:
$$
D_e = 9^{\frac{1}{5}} \cdot 200 = 1.552 \cdot 200 = 310\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
Because resistance depends on the fifth power of diameter, the equivalent diameter is not the sum of the three pipe diameters, which would give $600\,\mathrm{mm}$.
