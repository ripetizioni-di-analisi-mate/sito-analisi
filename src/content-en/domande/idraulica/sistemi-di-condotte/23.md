Two parallel branches connect nodes $M$ and $V$, as shown. The first has resistance $r_1 = 900\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and carries $60\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The second carries $40\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the resistance of the second branch?

::schema{nodi="M,4,8,-;V,24,8,-;B,14,12,-;C,14,4,-" condotte="M-B-V,0.5,r_1 = 900;M-C-V,0.42,r_2" etichette="4,6.8,M;24,6.8,V;14,14.4,Q_1 = 60 L/s;14,1.6,Q_2 = 40 L/s;2.0,9.3,100 L/s" frecce="0.4,8,3.2,8,;24.8,8,27.6,8," titolo="Two parallel branches between nodes $M$ and $V$"}

- $400\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $1350\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $2025\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $600\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$

## Explanation

The piezometric head at each junction has a single value. Both branches connecting $M$ and $V$ therefore have the same head loss, giving
$$
r_1\,Q_1^2 = r_2\,Q_2^2 .
$$
Solving this equation for the resistance of the second branch gives
$$
r_2 = r_1\left(\frac{Q_1}{Q_2}\right)^2 = 900 \cdot \left(\frac{60}{40}\right)^2 = 900 \cdot 2.25 = 2025\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
Calculating the head loss independently for each branch confirms the result, since $900 \cdot 0.060^2 = 2025 \cdot 0.040^2 = 3.24\,\mathrm{m}$.

The discharge ratio is the square root of the inverse resistance ratio. The value $1350\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$, obtained by multiplying $r_1$ by the unsquared ratio $\dfrac{Q_1}{Q_2}$, therefore underestimates the resistance.
