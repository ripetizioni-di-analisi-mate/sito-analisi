The closed loop shown passes through nodes $1$, $2$, $3$ and $4$ in clockwise order. Flow in branches $1\!-\!2$ and $2\!-\!3$ follows that direction, while flow in $4\!-\!3$ and $1\!-\!4$ opposes it. The head losses in the first three branches are $\Delta H_{12} = 4.20\,\mathrm{m}$, $\Delta H_{23} = 2.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\Delta H_{43} = 3.10\,\mathrm{m}$. If the discharges satisfy the loop equation, what is the head loss in branch $1\!-\!4$?

::schema{nodi="1,4,16;2,20,16;3,20,4;4,4,4" condotte="1-2,0.4,;2-3,0.4,;4-3,0.4,;1-4,0.4," frecce="11,16.9,13.5,16.9,;20.9,10.6,20.9,8.1,;11,4.9,13.5,4.9,;4.9,10.6,4.9,8.1," etichette="12,22.4,ΔH_12 = 4.20 m;12,21,ΔH_23 = 2.60 m;12,19.6,ΔH_43 = 3.10 m;12,10,clockwise loop direction" titolo="Closed loop showing flow directions in its four branches"}

- $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.90\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.70\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.80\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Each junction has a single piezometric head, so the signed head losses around a closed loop sum to zero. A branch loss enters the sum with a positive sign where flow follows the loop direction and a negative sign where flow opposes it. Thus
$$
\Delta H_{12} + \Delta H_{23} - \Delta H_{43} - \Delta H_{14} = 0 .
$$
Substituting the given values gives
$$
\Delta H_{14} = 4.20 + 2.60 - 3.10 = 3.70\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The junction heads give the same result. From $1$ to $3$, the head drops by $6.80\,\mathrm{m}$ along path $1\!-\!2\!-\!3$ and by $3.70 + 3.10 = 6.80\,\mathrm{m}$ along path $1\!-\!4\!-\!3$. The two drops are equal.

Adding all three losses with the same sign would give $9.90\,\mathrm{m}$, which fails to satisfy the loop equation.
