A network branch has hydraulic resistance $r = 1250\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and the measured head loss between its ends is $\Delta H = 12.50\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the branch discharge?

- $10.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $70.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $141\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $100\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The head loss and discharge in the branch are related by $\Delta H = r\,Q^2$. Taking the square root gives the discharge,
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta H}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{12.50}{1250}} = \sqrt{0.0100} = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $100\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

The value $10.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is the ratio $\dfrac{\Delta H}{r}$ before taking the square root, so it does not have the dimensions of discharge. The values $70.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $141\,\mathrm{L/s}$ correspond to halving and doubling the head loss, respectively.
