A pipe of length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ receives $Q_m = 72\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at its upstream end. All the water is withdrawn uniformly along its length, leaving zero throughflow at its downstream end. The head available between the ends is $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Assuming $\lambda = 0.022$, what is the minimum diameter?

- $269\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $147\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $250\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $216\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

For $Q_v = 0$ the equivalent discharge is
$$
Q_e = \sqrt{\frac{Q_m^2}{3}} = \frac{Q_m}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{0.072}{1.732} = 0.0416\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $41.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$. This constant discharge produces the same head loss as the actual flow with distributed withdrawal.

Set $r\,Q_e^2 = \Delta H$ and use the Darcy–Weisbach equation for $r$ to obtain
$$
D = \left(\frac{8\,\lambda\,L\,Q_e^2}{g\,\pi^2\,\Delta H}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1200 \cdot 0.0416^2}{9.81\,\pi^2 \cdot 8.00}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = 0.216\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
The minimum diameter is therefore $216\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the standard diameter to use is $250\,\mathrm{mm}$. Using $Q_m$ in place of $Q_e$ would give $269\,\mathrm{mm}$, an unnecessarily large diameter.
