Two parallel branches connect the same nodes and have the same friction factor. Branch $A$ has length $400\,\mathrm{m}$ and diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$. Branch $B$ has length $1600\,\mathrm{m}$ and diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$. Which branch carries the greater discharge?

- Branch $A$
- Branch $B$
- The two branches carry the same discharge
- It depends on the inflow to the node

## Explanation

The head loss $\Delta H$ is the same in both branches, so $Q_i = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta H}{r_i}}$. For a fixed friction factor, resistance is proportional to $\dfrac{L}{D^5}$, and each branch discharge is therefore proportional to $\dfrac{D^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\sqrt{L}}$.

The two branches give
$$
A:\ \frac{0.200^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\sqrt{400}} = \frac{0.01789}{20} = 8.94 \cdot 10^{-4} ,
\qquad
B:\ \frac{0.250^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\sqrt{1600}} = \frac{0.03125}{40} = 7.81 \cdot 10^{-4} ,
$$
Thus **branch $A$** carries about $1.14$ times the discharge of branch $B$. The larger diameter of branch $B$ cannot offset its fourfold length.

The ratio $\dfrac{Q_A}{Q_B} = \sqrt{\dfrac{r_B}{r_A}}$ depends only on the branch resistances, so this comparison holds for any total inflow.
