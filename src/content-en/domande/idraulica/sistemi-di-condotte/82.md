Two reservoirs $S_1$ and $S_2$ supply a network. Along path $S_1\!-\!A\!-\!B\!-\!S_2$, the network solution gives losses of $4.10\,\mathrm{m}$ in branch $S_1\!-\!A$ with flow from $S_1$ to $A$, $2.30\,\mathrm{m}$ in branch $A\!-\!B$ with flow from $A$ to $B$, and $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$ in branch $B\!-\!S_2$ with flow from $S_2$ to $B$. By how much is the free surface of reservoir $S_2$ lower than that of reservoir $S_1$?

- $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.40\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Head falls in the direction of flow. Along the path from $S_1$ to $S_2$, subtract losses where the path follows the flow and add them where it opposes the flow. Starting at the water surface of $S_1$ gives $h_A = z_1 - 4.10$ and $h_B = h_A - 2.30 = z_1 - 6.40$.

Branch $B\!-\!S_2$ carries water from $S_2$ to $B$. The reservoir level at $S_2$ therefore exceeds the head at junction $B$ by the head loss in that branch,
$$
z_2 = h_B + 1.60 = z_1 - 4.10 - 2.30 + 1.60 = z_1 - 4.80 .
$$
The water level at $S_2$ is thus $4.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below that at $S_1$. Along a path between two reservoirs the signed losses sum to the difference between their water levels. Around a closed loop the sum is zero.

The value $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$ adds every loss with the same sign. The value $6.40\,\mathrm{m}$ stops at junction $B$, and $3.40\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the wrong sign for the head loss in branch $A\!-\!B$.
