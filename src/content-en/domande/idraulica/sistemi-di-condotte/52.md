Two reservoirs with free surfaces at $100.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $80.00\,\mathrm{m}$ are linked by pipes in series of resistances $r_1 = 800\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_2 = 1200\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. The junction $N$ is a high point on the route, with pipe centerline at elevation $96.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What pressure head does the long-pipeline model give at the junction in meters of water?

::schema{nodi="A,3.5,14,-;N,15,16;B,28,-3,-" condotte="A-N,0.6,r_1 = 800;N-B,0.6,r_2 = 1200" acqua="0,12 3.5,12 3.5,20 0,20;28,-5 32,-5 32,0 28,0" solidi="0,11 3.5,11 3.5,12 0,12;28,-6 32,-6 32,-5 28,-5" pelo="0,3.5,20,100.00 m;28,32,0,80.00 m" etichette="11,18.6,z_N = 96.00 m" titolo="Pipes in series with a high point at node $N$"}

- $+4.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-4.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-8.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-6.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pipes are in series, so $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 800 + 1200 = 2000\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. The difference between the reservoir water levels equals the total head loss, giving
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{20.00}{2000}} = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
Subtract the head loss in the first pipe from the upstream water-surface elevation to obtain the piezometric head at the junction,
$$
h_N = 100.00 - 800 \cdot 0.100^2 = 92.00\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The junction lies above the hydraulic grade line, so its gauge pressure is negative,
$$
\frac{p_N}{\gamma} = h_N - z_N = 92.00 - 96.00 = -4.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $-39.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The pipe around the high point operates as a siphon.

The value $+4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is the elevation difference between the upstream water surface and the junction, which would give the pressure head only in the absence of losses. The value $-8.00\,\mathrm{m}$ subtracts the head loss in the second pipe instead of the first, and $-6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ splits the total head loss equally between the two pipes.
