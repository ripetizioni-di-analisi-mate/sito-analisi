Reservoirs $A$ and $B$ have the same free-surface elevation $70.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and connect to node $N$ through branches of resistance $r_A = 900\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_B = 3600\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. A branch of resistance $r_C = 400\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ carries water from this node, where there is no withdrawal, to reservoir $C$ with free surface at $50.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What discharge reaches reservoir $C$?

- $124\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $158\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $134\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $63.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Reservoirs $A$ and $B$ have the same water-surface elevation. Branches $A\!-\!N$ and $B\!-\!N$ therefore start at the same head and meet at the same junction. They experience the same head loss and act as parallel branches with equivalent resistance
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_p}} = \frac{1}{30} + \frac{1}{60} = \frac{1}{20} ,
\qquad
r_p = 400\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
These parallel branches are in series with branch $C$, so the total resistance is $400 + 400 = 800\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. The available head of $20.00\,\mathrm{m}$ gives
$$
Q_C = \sqrt{\frac{20.00}{800}} = 0.158\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 158\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The junction head is $h_N = 50.00 + 400 \cdot 0.025 = 60.00\,\mathrm{m}$. At this head, branches $A$ and $B$ carry $105\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $52.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Their sum equals the discharge in branch $C$ as required.

The value $124\,\mathrm{L/s}$ ignores reservoir $B$. The value $134\,\mathrm{L/s}$ combines $r_A$ and $r_B$ using $\dfrac{r_A r_B}{r_A + r_B}$ rather than the square-root relation, while $63.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$ puts all three branches in series.
