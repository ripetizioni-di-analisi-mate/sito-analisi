A pipeline of constant diameter is $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ long. Its first $1600\,\mathrm{m}$ are old and encrusted, with $\lambda = 0.032$, and its last $400\,\mathrm{m}$ have been renewed, with $\lambda = 0.016$. What friction factor would give a uniform pipe of the same diameter and length the same hydraulic resistance?

- $0.0240$
- $0.0288$
- $0.0480$
- $0.0192$

## Explanation

The two pipes are in series, so their resistances add. Their diameters are equal and the factor $\dfrac{8}{g\,\pi^2 D^5}$ is common to both. Equating the original and equivalent resistances gives
$$
\lambda_e\,L = \lambda_1 L_1 + \lambda_2 L_2 .
$$
It follows that
$$
\lambda_e = \frac{0.032 \cdot 1600 + 0.016 \cdot 400}{2000} = \frac{51.2 + 6.4}{2000} = 0.0288 ,
$$
This is the average of the friction factors weighted by the pipe lengths.

The value $0.0240$ is an unweighted average, giving equal weight to pipes of different lengths. The value $0.0192$ interchanges the two weights, and $0.0480$ adds the friction factors.
