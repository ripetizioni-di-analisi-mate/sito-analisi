Two reservoirs whose surface elevations differ by $\Delta z = 22.00\,\mathrm{m}$ are linked by a common pipe of length $600\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$, followed by two parallel branches. The first has length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_1 = 0.022$. The second has length $800\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.023$. What is the total discharge?

- $177\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $55.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $139\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $112\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The common pipe has resistance $r_c = 428\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and the two branches have resistances $r_1 = 1861\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_2 = 4751\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

The equivalent resistance of the parallel branches is
$$
r_p = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1861}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4751}}\right)^2} = 704\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
$$
and the parallel section is in series with the common pipe, so $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 428 + 704 = 1132\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

The difference in reservoir water levels equals the total loss, giving
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{22.00}{1132}} = 0.139\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 139\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
Putting all three pipes in series would give $55.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The value $112\,\mathrm{L/s}$ uses $\dfrac{r_1 r_2}{r_1 + r_2}$, the relation for electrical resistances, which does not apply to pipes obeying a quadratic head-loss law.
