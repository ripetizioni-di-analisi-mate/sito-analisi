Two parallel pipes connect the same two nodes. The pipes have equal lengths and friction factors, but one has diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the other $200\,\mathrm{mm}$. Which statement is true?

- The two pipes have the same head loss
- The two pipes carry the same discharge
- The two pipes have the same mean velocity
- The smaller-diameter pipe has the greater head loss

## Explanation

Each junction has a single piezometric head. Consequently, two branches connecting the same junctions $M$ and $V$ experience the same head loss $\Delta H = h_M - h_V$. This defining property of a parallel connection **holds for every pair of branches** between those junctions, regardless of diameter.

Their discharges differ, however, because $\Delta H = r_i Q_i^2$ gives $\dfrac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{r_2}{r_1}} = \left(\dfrac{300}{200}\right)^{\frac{5}{2}} = 2.76$.

Their mean velocities also differ. Since $v = \dfrac{4Q}{\pi D^2}$, the velocity ratio is $2.76 \cdot \left(\dfrac{200}{300}\right)^2 = 1.22$.
