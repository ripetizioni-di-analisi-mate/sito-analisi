Two parallel branches of resistances $r_1 = 900\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_2 = 1600\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ carry a combined discharge of $100\,\mathrm{L/s}$. A control valve is added to branch $1$ so that the two branches carry equal discharges. What head loss must the valve produce?

- $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.75\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.25\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With the valve adjusted, each branch carries $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Branch $2$ is unchanged, so its loss gives the head loss between the junctions,
$$
\Delta H = r_2\,Q_2^2 = 1600 \cdot 0.050^2 = 4.00\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Branch $1$ connects the same junctions and must also have a total head loss of $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Its pipe accounts for $900 \cdot 0.050^2 = 2.25\,\mathrm{m}$. The valve, in series with the pipe, must account for the rest,
$$
\Delta H_v = 4.00 - 2.25 = 1.75\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is the loss between the junctions and $2.25\,\mathrm{m}$ is the pipe loss alone in branch $1$. The value $7.00\,\mathrm{m}$ results from multiplying the difference between the resistances by the square of the total discharge.
