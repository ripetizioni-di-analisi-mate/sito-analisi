Traverse the triangular loop shown in direction $1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 1$. Branch $1\!-\!2$ ($r = 1000\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$) contains a pump that supplies a constant head $H_p = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ from $1$ toward $2$. Its discharge is $60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $1$ to $2$. Branch $2\!-\!3$ ($r = 2000\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$) carries $40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $2$ to $3$ and branch $1\!-\!3$ ($r = 400\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$) carries $20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $1$ to $3$. The discharges satisfy the nodal equations. What is the first Hardy Cross correction?

::schema{nodi="1,3,4;2,25,4;3,14,16" condotte="1-2,0.45,;2-3,0.45,;1-3,0.45," pompa="14,4,P" etichette="14,1.6,r = 1000;22.5,11.2,r = 2000;5.5,11.2,r = 400;14,8.6,direction 1 → 2 → 3 → 1" titolo="Triangular loop with a pump in branch $1\!-\!2$"}

- $-22.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $-35.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $-17.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $-8.92\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The flow in branches $1\!-\!2$ and $2\!-\!3$ follows the loop direction. In branch $1\!-\!3$ it runs from $1$ to $3$, opposite to that direction. The signed head-loss sum is therefore
$$
\sum_i r_i Q_i |Q_i| = 1000 \cdot 0.060^2 + 2000 \cdot 0.040^2 - 400 \cdot 0.020^2 = 3.60 + 3.20 - 0.16 = 6.64\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pump drives flow in the chosen loop direction, contributing $-H_p$ to the loop equation. The function whose zero is sought is $6.64 - 4.00 = 2.64\,\mathrm{m}$. The constant pump head contributes nothing to the derivative, so the denominator remains
$$
2\sum_i r_i |Q_i| = 2\left(1000 \cdot 0.060 + 2000 \cdot 0.040 + 400 \cdot 0.020\right) = 296\,\mathrm{s/m^2} .
$$
The correction is therefore
$$
\Delta Q = -\,\frac{2.64}{296} = -0.00892\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = -8.92\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The value $-22.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$ ignores the pump. The value $-35.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$ adds the pump head to the losses instead of subtracting it, and $-17.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$ omits the factor $2$ in the denominator.
