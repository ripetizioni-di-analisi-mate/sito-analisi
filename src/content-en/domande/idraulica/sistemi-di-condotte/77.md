Four branches meet at node $N$, as shown. Branch $1$ carries $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ toward the node, branch $2$ carries $30\,\mathrm{L/s}$ toward it, branch $3$ carries $45\,\mathrm{L/s}$ away from it, and $15\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is withdrawn at the node. What is the discharge in branch $4$, and in which direction does it flow?

::schema{nodi="N,12,8,-;P1,2,8,-;P2,12,16,-;P3,22,8,-;P4,12,0,-" condotte="P1-N,0.4,;P2-N,0.4,;N-P3,0.4,;N-P4,0.4," frecce="3,9.4,7.5,9.4,50 L/s;13.2,14,13.2,10.5,30 L/s;15.5,9.2,19.5,9.2,45 L/s;12.8,7.2,15.6,4.4,15 L/s" etichette="1.2,8,1;12,17.2,2;23,8,3;12,-1.2,4;10.6,6.6,N" titolo="Junction with four branches and a point withdrawal"}

- $20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ toward the node
- $35\,\mathrm{L/s}$ away from the node
- $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ away from the node
- $20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ away from the node

## Explanation

Continuity at the junction requires the total inflow to equal the total outflow, including the local withdrawal. Let $Q_4$ be the discharge in branch $4$, positive away from the junction. Then
$$
50 + 30 = 45 + 15 + Q_4 ,
$$
giving $Q_4 = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The positive value means branch $4$ carries **$20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ away from the junction**.

The value $35\,\mathrm{L/s}$ omits the junction withdrawal, while $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ counts the withdrawal as an inflow.
