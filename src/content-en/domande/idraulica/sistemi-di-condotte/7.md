A pipe of length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$ receives $Q_m = 140\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at its upstream end and supplies a uniform withdrawal along its length, leaving $Q_v = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at the downstream end. What is the head loss in the pipe?

- $14.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $29.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.74\,\mathrm{m}$
- $11.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pipe resistance is
$$
r = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 2000}{9.81\,\pi^2\,0.300^5} = 1496\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
With uniform withdrawal, discharge decreases linearly from $Q_m$ to $Q_v$. Integrating the head loss along the pipe gives
$$
\Delta H = \frac{r}{3}\left(Q_m^2 + Q_m Q_v + Q_v^2\right) .
$$
Substitution into $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ gives
$$
\Delta H = \frac{1496}{3}\left(0.140^2 + 0.140 \cdot 0.050 + 0.050^2\right) = 14.5\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $29.3\,\mathrm{m}$ uses only $Q_m$ over the entire length. The value $11.0\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the term $Q_m Q_v$.
