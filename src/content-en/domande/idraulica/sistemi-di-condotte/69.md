Three reservoirs with water-surface elevations $z_A > z_B > z_C$ connect to a junction with zero withdrawal. A gate valve is partly closed in the branch leading to reservoir $C$, the lowest of the three, increasing the branch resistance. How does the junction's piezometric head change?

- It decreases
- It increases
- It stays the same because it depends only on reservoir elevations
- It increases or decreases according to the direction of flow in branch $B$

## Explanation

The junction head is the zero of the function
$$
\Phi(h) = \sum_{i} \operatorname{sgn}(z_i - h)\,\sqrt{\frac{|z_i - h|}{r_i}} ,
$$
which sums the discharges directed toward the junction and is strictly decreasing. The junction head lies between $z_C$ and $z_A$, so branch $C$ always carries water away from the junction and contributes $-\sqrt{\dfrac{h - z_C}{r_C}}$ to the sum.

Increasing $r_C$ makes this contribution less negative at every $h > z_C$, so $\Phi$ increases at every point. The function $\Phi$ is still decreasing, but its graph has shifted upward. Its zero therefore moves to the right and the junction head **increases**, regardless of the direction of flow in branch $B$.
