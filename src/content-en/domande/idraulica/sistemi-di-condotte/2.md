A pipeline consists of two pipes in series. The first has length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_1 = 0.021$. The second has length $800\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.024$. What diameter must an equivalent pipe of length $1800\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\lambda_e = 0.022$ have?

- $155\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $250\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $225\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $300\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The two pipe resistances are
$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 1000}{9.81\,\pi^2\,0.300^5} = 713\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.024 \cdot 800}{9.81\,\pi^2\,0.200^5} = 4959\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
$$
and since the pipes are in series, $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = r_1 + r_2 = 5672\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

The equivalent pipe must have the same resistance. Solving the resistance equation gives
$$
D_e = \left(\frac{8\,\lambda_e\,L_e}{g\,\pi^2\,r_{\mathrm{eq}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1800}{9.81\,\pi^2 \cdot 5672}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = 0.225\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $225\,\mathrm{mm}$. The equivalent diameter lies between the two original diameters. Their arithmetic mean would give $250\,\mathrm{mm}$.
