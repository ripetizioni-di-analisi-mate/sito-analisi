Two parallel branches of resistances $r_1 = 400\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_2 = 1600\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ connect nodes $M$ and $V$ and together carry $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Traverse the resulting loop from $M$ to $V$ along branch $1$ and back from $V$ to $M$ along branch $2$. Starting with $Q_1 = Q_2 = 45\,\mathrm{L/s}$, what is the discharge in branch $1$ after the first Hardy Cross correction?

- $72.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $58.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $31.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $45.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Relative to the chosen loop direction, the discharge is positive in branch $1$ and negative in branch $2$. Thus
$$
\sum_i r_i Q_i |Q_i| = 400 \cdot 0.045^2 - 1600 \cdot 0.045^2 = 0.81 - 3.24 = -2.43\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
$$
2\sum_i r_i |Q_i| = 2\left(400 + 1600\right) \cdot 0.045 = 180\,\mathrm{s/m^2} .
$$
The correction is $\Delta Q = \dfrac{2.43}{180} = +0.0135\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The discharge in branch $1$, which follows the loop direction, becomes $45 + 13.5 = 58.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The discharge magnitude in the opposing branch $2$ falls to $31.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Their sum remains $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$, as continuity requires.

Further iterations converge to the exact solution $Q_1 = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$, since $\dfrac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1600}{400}} = 2$.

The value $72.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ omits the factor $2$ in the denominator. The value $31.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$ applies the correction with the wrong sign, while $45.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is the initial estimate.
