A pipe in a distribution network receives $Q_m = 180\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at its upstream end, supplies a uniform withdrawal along its length and delivers a throughflow of $Q_v = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at the downstream end. What is the equivalent discharge for the pipe?

- $120\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $180\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $104\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $125\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The equivalent discharge is the constant discharge that would produce the same head loss as the uniformly distributed withdrawal. Comparing $\Delta H = \dfrac{r}{3}\left(Q_m^2 + Q_m Q_v + Q_v^2\right)$ with $\Delta H = r\,Q_e^2$ gives
$$
Q_e = \sqrt{\frac{Q_m^2 + Q_m Q_v + Q_v^2}{3}} .
$$
Substituting the values in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ gives
$$
Q_e = \sqrt{\frac{0.180^2 + 0.180 \cdot 0.060 + 0.060^2}{3}} = \sqrt{0.0156} = 0.125\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $125\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The equivalent discharge exceeds the arithmetic mean $120\,\mathrm{L/s}$ because head loss varies with discharge squared, giving greater weight to the upstream part of the pipe.
