In the network shown, $Q$ enters node $1$ and all of it leaves at node $3$. There is no withdrawal at nodes $2$ or $4$. Branches $1\!-\!2$ and $1\!-\!4$ have equal resistance, as do branches $2\!-\!3$ and $4\!-\!3$. Nodes $2$ and $4$ are linked by branch $2\!-\!4$. What is the discharge in branch $2\!-\!4$?

- $\dfrac{Q}{2}$, from $2$ toward $4$
- Zero
- $\dfrac{Q}{4}$, from $2$ toward $4$
- It depends on the resistance of branch $2\!-\!4$

## Explanation

Consider the distribution $Q_{12} = Q_{14} = \dfrac{Q}{2}$, $Q_{23} = Q_{43} = \dfrac{Q}{2}$, and $Q_{24} = 0$. At junction $1$ the inflow is $Q$ and the outflow is $\dfrac{Q}{2} + \dfrac{Q}{2}$. At junctions $2$ and $4$, the inflow and outflow are each $\dfrac{Q}{2}$. Junction $3$ receives $\dfrac{Q}{2} + \dfrac{Q}{2}$, so the junction equations are satisfied.

Branches $1\!-\!2$ and $1\!-\!4$ have equal resistances and carry equal discharges. Their losses are equal, so junctions $2$ and $4$ have the same head. The signed losses around each loop thus sum to zero, satisfying both loop equations.

The distribution satisfies every network equation and is therefore the solution. Branch $2\!-\!4$ connects two junctions at the same head, so its **discharge is zero** for any value of its resistance.
