For a full circular pipe of diameter $400\,\mathrm{mm}$, the hydraulic resistance is calculated with the Gauckler–Strickler equation and coefficient $k_s = 80\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. Which Darcy friction factor $\lambda$ gives the same resistance in the Darcy–Weisbach equation?

- $0.0105$
- $0.0195$
- $0.0132$
- $0.0264$

## Explanation

The two formulas give the following expressions for resistance,
$$
r = \frac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5} ,
\qquad
r = \frac{10.29\,L}{k_s^2\,D^{\frac{16}{3}}} ,
$$
where the second uses the hydraulic radius $R = \dfrac{D}{4}$ of a full pipe.

Equating the expressions cancels the length, giving
$$
\lambda = \frac{10.29\,g\,\pi^2}{8\,k_s^2\,D^{\frac{1}{3}}} = \frac{10.29 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \pi^2}{8 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.400^{\frac{1}{3}}} = \frac{996.3}{37\,720} = 0.0264 .
$$
The equivalent friction factor depends on diameter through $D^{\frac{1}{3}}$. The correspondence between $k_s$ and $\lambda$ therefore holds only for the specified diameter.

The value $0.0105$ results from using $\dfrac{D}{2}$ as the hydraulic radius. The value $0.0195$ uses the exponent $5$ instead of $\dfrac{16}{3}$, while $0.0132$ results from writing the velocity head as $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ in the Darcy–Weisbach equation.
