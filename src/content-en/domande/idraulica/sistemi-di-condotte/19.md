Two pipes in series connect two reservoirs whose surface elevations differ by $\Delta z = 21.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Their respective resistances are $r_1 = 640\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_2 = 1460\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. What is the head loss in the second pipe?

::schema{nodi="A,3.5,18;N,15,12,-;V,28,1,-;P,30.5,21,-;Q,30.5,0,-" etichette="15.95,12.95,N;32.8,2.6,downstream" condotte="A-N,0.7,r_1;N-V,0.6,r_2" acqua="0,14 3.5,14 3.5,21 0,21;26,-3 30.5,-3 30.5,0 26,0" solidi="0,13 3.5,13 3.5,14 0,14;26,-4 30.5,-4 30.5,-3 26,-3" pelo="0,3.5,21,upstream;26,30.5,0," quote="Q,P,21.00 m,1.2" titolo="Two pipes in series between two reservoirs"}

- $6.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $14.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $21.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

There is no withdrawal at the intermediate junction. Both pipes therefore carry the same discharge, and their head losses add, giving $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 640 + 1460 = 2100\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

In the long-pipe approximation, the difference between the reservoir water levels equals the total head loss. Thus
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{21.00}{2100}} = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The head loss in the second pipe is therefore
$$
\Delta H_2 = r_2\,Q^2 = 1460 \cdot 0.100^2 = 14.60\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and the first pipe accounts for the remaining $6.40\,\mathrm{m}$. Each pipe's share of the head loss is proportional to its resistance.
