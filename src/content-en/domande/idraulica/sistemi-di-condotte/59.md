A pipe section of length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$ and diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ is to be represented by a pipe of diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ with the same friction factor and hydraulic resistance. How long must the pipe of diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ be?

- $2488\,\mathrm{m}$
- $402\,\mathrm{m}$
- $833\,\mathrm{m}$
- $482\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The resistance is $r = \dfrac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5}$. With the same friction factor, two pipes have equal resistance when they have the same ratio $\dfrac{L}{D^5}$,
$$
\frac{L_e}{0.250^5} = \frac{1000}{0.300^5} .
$$
It follows that
$$
L_e = 1000 \cdot \left(\frac{250}{300}\right)^5 = 1000 \cdot 0.402 = 402\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The smaller pipe has the greater resistance per unit length, so a shorter length gives the same total resistance.

The value $2488\,\mathrm{m}$ inverts the diameter ratio, $833\,\mathrm{m}$ uses its first power, and $482\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the exponent $4$ in place of $5$.
