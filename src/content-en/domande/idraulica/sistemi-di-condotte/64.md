A network calculation expresses discharges in liters per second and gives $\Delta H = 0.00125\,Q^2$ for a branch, with $\Delta H$ in meters and $Q$ in $\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the branch resistance in $\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$?

- $1.25\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $1.25 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $1250\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $0.00125\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$

## Explanation

One liter equals $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, so the numerical discharge in liters per second is $1000$ times that in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$, giving $Q_{\mathrm{L/s}} = 1000\,Q_{\mathrm{m^3/s}}$. Substitution into the head-loss equation gives
$$
\Delta H = 0.00125\,\left(1000\,Q_{\mathrm{m^3/s}}\right)^2 = 0.00125 \cdot 10^6\,Q_{\mathrm{m^3/s}}^2 = 1250\,Q_{\mathrm{m^3/s}}^2 ,
$$
Hence $r = 1250\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. At $40\,\mathrm{L/s}$, both forms yield the same head loss, $0.00125 \cdot 40^2 = 1250 \cdot 0.040^2 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

The value $1.25\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ multiplies by $1000$ instead of its square. The value $1.25 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ divides by $10^6$ instead of multiplying, while $0.00125\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ omits the conversion.
