Take initial discharges $Q_{12} = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$, $Q_{23} = 30\,\mathrm{L/s}$, $Q_{14} = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $Q_{43} = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and traverse the loop clockwise. What is the first Hardy Cross correction?

## Explanation

The four branch resistances are $r_{12} = 931\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$, $r_{23} = 2376\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$, $r_{43} = 1782\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and $r_{14} = 745\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

In a clockwise loop traversal, the discharges $Q_{12}$ and $Q_{23}$ are positive. The discharges $Q_{14}$ and $Q_{43}$ oppose the traversal direction and enter with negative signs. Thus
$$
\sum_i r_i Q_i |Q_i| = 931 \cdot 0.070^2 + 2376 \cdot 0.030^2 - 1782 \cdot 0.020^2 - 745 \cdot 0.050^2 = 4.12\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
$$
2\sum_i r_i |Q_i| = 2\left(931 \cdot 0.070 + 2376 \cdot 0.030 + 1782 \cdot 0.020 + 745 \cdot 0.050\right) = 418.6\,\mathrm{s/m^2} .
$$
The correction formula gives
$$
\Delta Q = -\,\frac{4.12}{418.6} = -0.00985\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = -9.85\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
$$
The correction therefore reduces the clockwise discharges. Omitting the factor $2$ from the denominator would give $-19.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
