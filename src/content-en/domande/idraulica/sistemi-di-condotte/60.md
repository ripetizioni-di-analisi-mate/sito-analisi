Two reservoirs with a fixed head difference can be connected in four ways using parallel pipes. Every pipe has the same length and friction factor. Which arrangement carries the greatest discharge?

- Two pipes of diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$
- Three pipes of diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$
- One pipe of diameter $400\,\mathrm{mm}$
- Four pipes of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Each pipe connects the two reservoirs, so the full head difference acts across it and it carries $Q_i = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta z}{r_i}}$. At fixed length and friction factor, $r_i \propto D_i^{-5}$. The discharge through one pipe is therefore proportional to $D_i^{\frac{5}{2}}$, and the total through $n$ identical pipes is proportional to $n\,D^{\frac{5}{2}}$.

For the four arrangements, the results are
$$
0.400^{\frac{5}{2}} = 0.1012 ,
\quad
2 \cdot 0.300^{\frac{5}{2}} = 0.0986 ,
\quad
3 \cdot 0.250^{\frac{5}{2}} = 0.0938 ,
\quad
4 \cdot 0.200^{\frac{5}{2}} = 0.0716 ,
$$
The **single $400\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe** therefore carries the greatest discharge.

The arrangements with multiple pipes have a total flow area equal to or greater than that of the $400\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe, yet they carry less water. Pipe resistance varies as $D^{-5}$, whereas cross-sectional area varies only as $D^2$.
