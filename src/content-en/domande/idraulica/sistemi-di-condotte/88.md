A branch in a distribution tree has length $200\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$. It must carry $45\,\mathrm{L/s}$ with head loss no greater than $12.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and mean velocity no greater than $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which is the smallest of the standard diameters $150$, $200$ and $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ that meets both conditions?

- $150\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $250\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $144\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $200\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The head-loss constraint gives the minimum diameter
$$
D \geq \left(\frac{8\,\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{g\,\pi^2\,\Delta H}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 200 \cdot 0.045^2}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 12.00}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = 0.144\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which the commercially available diameter $150\,\mathrm{mm}$ already meets.

With $150\,\mathrm{mm}$, however, the mean velocity is
$$
v = \frac{4\,Q}{\pi D^2} = \frac{4 \cdot 0.045}{\pi \cdot 0.150^2} = 2.55\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
This exceeds the limit and rules out that diameter. With $200\,\mathrm{mm}$, the velocity falls to $1.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the head loss to $2.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Select diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$. In this short branch with ample head available, the velocity limit governs.

The value $150\,\mathrm{mm}$ meets only the head-loss requirement. The value $144\,\mathrm{mm}$ is the calculated minimum diameter, which is unavailable as a standard size. The value $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ meets both requirements, but a smaller standard diameter also does so.
