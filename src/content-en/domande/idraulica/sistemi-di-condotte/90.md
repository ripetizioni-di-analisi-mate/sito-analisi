A pump centerline lies $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the free surface of the suction reservoir, as shown. The suction pipe has resistance $r = 500\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and carries $60\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What gauge pressure does the long-pipeline model give at the pump inlet in meters of water?

::schema{nodi="I,4.5,-1.5,-;G,4.5,4.5,-;P,11,4.5,-;Z,13,0,-;W,13,4.5,-" condotte="I-G-P,0.4,r = 500" pompa="11,4.5,P" acqua="-1,-3 7,-3 7,0 -1,0" solidi="-1,-4 7,-4 7,-3 -1,-3" pelo="-1,7,0,suction" quote="Z,W,4.50 m,0.9" titolo="Pump installed above the suction reservoir surface"}

- $-6.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-4.50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-2.70\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-1.80\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Take the reservoir water surface as the elevation datum, so its head is zero. The suction pipe loses $r\,Q^2 = 500 \cdot 0.060^2 = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a piezometric head of $h = 0 - 1.80 = -1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ at the pump inlet.

Subtracting the elevation of the pump centerline from the piezometric head gives the pressure head,
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = h - z = -1.80 - 4.50 = -6.30\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $-61.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Both the elevation of the pump and the suction-line loss contribute to the negative gauge pressure. To check for cavitation, compare the corresponding absolute pressure with the vapor pressure.

The value $-4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ ignores the head loss, $-2.70\,\mathrm{m}$ adds it to the head instead of subtracting it, and $-1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ gives the piezometric head rather than the pressure head.
