Two pipes of equal length and friction factor connect two reservoirs in series. The first has diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the second $200\,\mathrm{mm}$. In the long-pipeline model, which segment of the hydraulic grade line is steeper?

- The segment along the smaller-diameter pipe
- The segment along the larger-diameter pipe
- The two segments have the same slope
- The slope depends on the direction of flow

## Explanation

The slope of the hydraulic grade line along a pipe is its hydraulic gradient $J_i = \dfrac{r_i Q^2}{L_i}$. Pipes in series carry the same discharge.

The two pipes have the same length, so their hydraulic gradients are proportional to their resistances. Since $r = \dfrac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5}$ and the fifth power of the diameter appears in the denominator, the $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe has the greater resistance, $\left(\dfrac{300}{200}\right)^5 = 7.59$ times that of the other pipe.

Its hydraulic gradient is therefore greater. The **steeper segment corresponds to the smaller-diameter pipe**.
