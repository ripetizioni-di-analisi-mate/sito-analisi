A pipe of length $2500\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$ connects two reservoirs whose surface elevations differ by $\Delta z = 30.00\,\mathrm{m}$. An identical parallel pipe is added along the first $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ to increase capacity. What is the new discharge?

- $130\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $175\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $259\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $183\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The original pipe resistance is $r = 1785\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. Resistance is proportional to length, so the first $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ have resistance $r' = 1071\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and the final $1000\,\mathrm{m}$ have resistance $r'' = 714\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

The section where a pipe is added contains two identical parallel branches, giving resistance $\dfrac{r'}{4} = 268\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. This section is in series with the remaining single pipe, so
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{r'}{4} + r'' = 268 + 714 = 982\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
The elevation difference equals the total loss, so
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{30.00}{982}} = 0.175\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 175\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The value $259\,\mathrm{L/s}$ would require adding an identical pipe along the entire route, doubling the original discharge.
