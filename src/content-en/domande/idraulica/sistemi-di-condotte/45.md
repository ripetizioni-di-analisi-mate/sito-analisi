The pump in the branch shown conveys $100\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from a suction reservoir with free surface at $40.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to a delivery reservoir with free surface at $95.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The branch resistance is $r = 900\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. What head must the pump supply?

::schema{nodi="A,3.5,3;V,28,16" condotte="A-V,0.6,r = 900" pompa="10.85,6.9,P" acqua="0,0 3.5,0 3.5,5 0,5;28,13 32,13 32,18 28,18" solidi="0,-1 3.5,-1 3.5,0 0,0;28,12 32,12 32,13 28,13" pelo="0,3.5,5,40.00 m;28,32,18,95.00 m" etichette="20,9,Q = 100 L/s" titolo="Pump in the branch between two reservoirs"}

- $55.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $46.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $64.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $73.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pump raises the total head by $H_p$ from inlet to outlet. The branch energy balance between the two reservoir surfaces is therefore
$$
z_A + H_p = z_V + r\,Q^2 .
$$
The head loss in the branch is $\Delta H = 900 \cdot 0.100^2 = 9.00\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
H_p = (95.00 - 40.00) + 9.00 = 64.00\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pump head must cover both the static lift and the head loss. The value $55.00\,\mathrm{m}$ allows for no head loss, while $46.00\,\mathrm{m}$ results from subtracting the loss rather than adding it.
