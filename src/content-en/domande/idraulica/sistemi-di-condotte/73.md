A reservoir feeds node $A$ of a branched network through a supply main. A discharge of $10\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is withdrawn at the node. Two branches leave $A$. Branch $A\!-\!B$ has length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, supplies $0.025\,\mathrm{L/(s\,m)}$ uniformly along its length, and ends at node $B$, where $15\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is withdrawn. Branch $A\!-\!C$ supplies no withdrawal along its route and ends at node $C$, where $25\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is withdrawn. What is the supply-main discharge?

- $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $70\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $65\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

In a branched network, the discharge in any branch equals the sum of downstream demands, including water withdrawn along the branches themselves.

Branch $A\!-\!B$ supplies a distributed demand of $0.025 \cdot 1200 = 30\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Its inflow at junction $A$ must therefore be $30 + 15 = 45\,\mathrm{L/s}$, while branch $A\!-\!C$ receives $25\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The upstream main also supplies the demand at junction $A$, so it carries
$$
Q_0 = 10 + 45 + 25 = 80\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The value $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ omits the distributed withdrawal, $70\,\mathrm{L/s}$ omits the demand at junction $A$, and $65\,\mathrm{L/s}$ counts only half the distributed withdrawal. The mean-discharge coefficient $0.55$ is used to estimate head loss in branch $A\!-\!B$ and does not enter the continuity equation.
