In a pressure pipe, water acts from below against the top half of the pipe wall. What can be said about the vertical component of the water force on the upper half?

- It is zero because the surface is closed.
- It acts upward and equals the weight of the imaginary volume of water above the surface.
- It acts downward and equals the weight of water in the pipe.
- It acts upward and equals the weight of the water above the wall.

## Explanation

The vertical force on a curved surface is $\gamma V_p$, where $V_p$ is the volume bounded by the surface, the hydrostatic head plane, and the vertical projection of its perimeter. If the liquid is below the surface, this volume above it is only hypothetical and the force acts **upward**. In a pressurized pipe, the head plane may be far above the pipe, so the hypothetical volume can greatly exceed the volume inside the pipe.
