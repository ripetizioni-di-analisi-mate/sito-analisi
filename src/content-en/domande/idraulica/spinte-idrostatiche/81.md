A wooden block of volume $0.0200\,\mathrm{m}^3$ and density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is ballasted with steel of density $7850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ attached beneath it. What mass of steel allows the assembly to remain in equilibrium fully submerged without touching the bottom?

- $9.17\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $8.00\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $7.10\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $12.0\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The fully submerged assembly is in equilibrium when its weight equals the buoyancy of both the wood and the steel. Let $m$ be the steel mass. Its volume is $\dfrac{m}{7850}$, and dividing the balance by $g$ gives
$$
600 \cdot 0.0200 + m = 1000 \left( 0.0200 + \frac{m}{7850} \right) .
$$
This gives $m \left( 1 - \dfrac{1000}{7850} \right) = 20.0 - 12.0 = 8.00\,\mathrm{kg}$. Since $1 - \dfrac{1000}{7850} = 0.8726$, the result is $m = \dfrac{8.00}{0.8726} = 9.17\,\mathrm{kg}$. Neglecting the buoyancy on the steel would give $8.00\,\mathrm{kg}$.
