A vertical wall retains water up to its upper edge. The hydrostatic force per unit width is $49.05\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. What is the water depth?

- $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.24\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.16\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The triangular pressure diagram gives a force per unit width of $\gamma\,\dfrac{h^2}{2}$. Equating this to the specified force gives
$$
h = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 49.05}{9.81}} = \sqrt{10.0} = 3.16\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The force is proportional to depth squared. Simply dividing the force by specific weight would give $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ rather than the required depth $h$.
