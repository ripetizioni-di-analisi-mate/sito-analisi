An opening in a vertical wall extends between depths $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. What is the hydrostatic force on the opening per unit width?

- $103\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $44.1\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $123\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $206\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

The pressures at the edges of the opening are $p_1 = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 = 19.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_2 = 9.81 \cdot 5.00 = 49.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The resulting trapezoidal pressure diagram has height $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and its area gives the force per unit width,
$$
\frac{19.6 + 49.1}{2} \cdot 3.00 = 103\,\mathrm{kN/m} .
$$
The general formula gives the same result. Since $h_G = 3.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and the area per unit width is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}^2$, the force is $9.81 \cdot 3.50 \cdot 3.00 = 103\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Omitting the factor of one-half in averaging the two pressures would give $206\,\mathrm{kN/m}$.
