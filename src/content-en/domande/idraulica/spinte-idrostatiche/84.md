On a pontoon in stable equilibrium, a load is moved vertically from the deck to the hold without changing the total weight. What happens to the pontoon’s stability?

- Metacentric height decreases because the center of buoyancy moves down.
- Metacentric height increases because the center of gravity moves down and the metacenter stays in place.
- Metacentric height stays the same because total weight is unchanged.
- Metacentric radius increases because the displaced volume increases.

## Explanation

The total weight is unchanged, so the displaced volume and draft stay constant. The center of buoyancy and metacentric radius $r = \dfrac{I}{V_c}$ are therefore unchanged, fixing the position of the metacenter. Lowering the load lowers the pontoon’s center of gravity $G$ and increases its metacentric height $\overline{GM}$, making it more stable.
