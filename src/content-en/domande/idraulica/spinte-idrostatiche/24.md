A vertical wall retains water of depth $3.60\,\mathrm{m}$, with the free surface at its upper edge. How far above the bottom does the resultant pressure force act?

- $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.900\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The water reaches the upper edge. The triangular pressure diagram has its vertex at the free surface and base $\gamma h$ at the bottom. Its resultant acts through the triangle’s centroid, one-third of the height from the base. The distance above the bottom is therefore
$$
\frac{h}{3} = \frac{3.60}{3} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The distance $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ locates the wall centroid, above the center of pressure. Measuring $\dfrac{2h}{3}$ from the bottom instead of from the free surface gives $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$.
