A cube of side $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and density $920\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ floats with horizontal faces at the interface between water and oil of density $820\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The oil is deep enough to cover it, as shown. What height of the cube is in the water?

::schema{nodi="C1,-0.5,1.15,-;C2,0.5,1.15,-;C3,0.5,2.15,-;C4,-0.5,2.15,-" aste="C1-C2;C2-C3;C3-C4;C4-C1" acqua="-2,0 2,0 2,1.6 -2,1.6" poligoni="-2,1.6 2,1.6 2,3 -2,3" solidi="-2.3,-0.3 2.3,-0.3 2.3,0 -2.3,0;-2.3,0 -2,0 -2,3.4 -2.3,3.4;2,0 2.3,0 2.3,3.4 2,3.4" pelo="1,2,3," etichette="-1.3,2.6,oil;-1.3,0.6,water" quote="C4,C3,a = 0.20 m,-0.4" riferimento=0 titolo="Cube floating at the interface between the water and the oil layer"}

- $11.1\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $18.4\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $8.89\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Let $x$ be the height of cube in water and $a - x$ the height in oil. Between its top and bottom faces there are $a - x$ of oil and $x$ of water, giving a bottom pressure $\rho_o g\,(a - x) + \rho g\,x$ greater than the top pressure. The horizontal side forces cancel. Dividing the vertical force balance by $g\,a^2$ gives
$$
920 \cdot 0.20 = 1000\,x + 820\,(0.20 - x) ,
$$
and thus $180\,x = 184 - 164 = 20$ and $x = 0.111\,\mathrm{m} = 11.1\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $18.4\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the draft the cube would have in water alone, neglecting the buoyancy supplied by the oil. The value $8.89\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the height in oil.
