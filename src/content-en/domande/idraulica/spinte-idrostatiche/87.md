A fully submerged body is in equilibrium, with its weight equal to the buoyant force, as for a submerged submarine. What condition makes it stable against rotation?

- The center of gravity must coincide with the center of buoyancy.
- It is sufficient for weight to equal buoyancy.
- The center of gravity must lie below the center of buoyancy.
- The center of gravity must lie above the center of buoyancy.

## Explanation

The body is wholly submerged and has no waterplane. Its metacentric radius $r = \dfrac{I}{V_c}$ is zero, and the metacenter coincides with the center of buoyancy $C$. Rotation does not change the displaced volume’s shape, so buoyancy continues to act vertically through $C$. If $G$ lies below $C$, weight and buoyancy form a restoring couple that brings $G$ back vertically below $C$, giving stable equilibrium. If $G$ lies above $C$, the couple increases the rotation and equilibrium is unstable. When $G$ and $C$ coincide, the couple vanishes in every orientation and equilibrium is neutral.
