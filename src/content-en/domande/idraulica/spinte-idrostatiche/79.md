A uniform wooden rod of constant cross section, length $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and density $640\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is hinged at one end above the free surface. It floats at an angle with its other end submerged. What length of rod is underwater?

- $1.28\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.800\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The rod is subject to its weight at midlength, buoyancy at the centroid of the submerged length, and the hinge reaction. Let $x$ be the submerged length. Buoyancy is proportional to $\rho\,x$ and acts $L - \dfrac{x}{2}$ along the rod from the hinge. Both vertical forces’ moment arms contain the same factor $\cos\theta$. Taking moments about the hinge and dividing by $g$, the cross-sectional area, and $\cos\theta$ gives
$$
640 \cdot 2.00 \cdot \frac{2.00}{2} = 1000\,x \left( 2.00 - \frac{x}{2} \right) ,
$$
or $x^2 - 4.00\,x + 2.56 = 0$. The roots are $0.800\,\mathrm{m}$ and $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The second exceeds the length of the rod, leaving $x = 0.800\,\mathrm{m}$. Equating buoyancy to weight would give $1.28\,\mathrm{m}$. That would apply to a freely floating body, whereas the hinge carries part of this rod’s weight.
