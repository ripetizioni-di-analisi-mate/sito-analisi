An empty buried concrete tank has external rectangular plan dimensions $6.00 \times 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, vertical walls, and total weight $450\,\mathrm{kN}$. The surrounding groundwater level is $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above its base. Neglecting soil friction on its walls, what is the factor of safety against flotation, defined as tank weight divided by upward water force?

- $2.55$
- $0.510$
- $1.27$
- $0.785$

## Explanation

Groundwater exerts horizontal forces on the vertical walls. Opposite wall forces balance and have no vertical component. The base is at a uniform depth $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the water table, so the uniform pressure $9.81 \cdot 1.50 = 14.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$ gives an uplift force of
$$
U = 14.7 \cdot 6.00 \cdot 4.00 = 353\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
equal to the buoyancy of the portion of the tank below the water table. The factor is therefore $\dfrac{450}{353} = 1.27$. The triangular uplift diagram used under a dam would give $2.55$, but groundwater surrounds this tank on all sides. Using the sidewall force in place of the base force would give $0.510$.
