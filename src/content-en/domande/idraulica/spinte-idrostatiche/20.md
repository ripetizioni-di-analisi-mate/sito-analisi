A rectangular gate of downslope length $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is mounted in a wall inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal. Its centroid is at depth $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. How far from the centroid is the center of pressure, measured along the gate?

- $0.222\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.333\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0556\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.111\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Measure distance along the gate plane from its intersection with the hydrostatic head plane. The coordinate of the centroid is therefore
$$
y_G = \frac{h_G}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{1.50}{\sin 30^\circ} = 3.00\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Let $b$ be the width and $\ell = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the length along the slope. Then $I_G = \dfrac{b\,\ell^3}{12}$ and $A = b\,\ell$. The width cancels, so the **center-of-pressure formula** gives
$$
y_C - y_G = \frac{I_G}{y_G\,A} = \frac{\ell^2}{12\,y_G} = \frac{2.00^2}{12 \cdot 3.00} = 0.111\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Using the vertical depth $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ in place of the distance $y_G$ measured along the plane would give $0.222\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance $0.333\,\mathrm{m}$ would apply to a triangular pressure diagram, as for a gate extending up to the free surface.
