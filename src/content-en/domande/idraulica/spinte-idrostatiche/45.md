A pontoon has its metacenter $2.10\,\mathrm{m}$ above the keel and its center of gravity $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the keel. What type of equilibrium does it have?

- The equilibrium is unstable.
- The equilibrium is stable, with metacentric height $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.
- The equilibrium is neutral.
- The equilibrium is stable because the metacenter is above the center of buoyancy.

## Explanation

A floating body is stable when its metacenter is above its center of gravity, giving a positive metacentric height $\overline{GM}$. Here the metacenter lies $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ below the center of gravity. A small initial roll produces a couple from weight and buoyancy that increases the roll, so equilibrium is **unstable**. The metacenter always lies above the center of buoyancy because $\overline{CM} = \dfrac{I}{V_c}$ is positive. That fact alone cannot establish stability.
