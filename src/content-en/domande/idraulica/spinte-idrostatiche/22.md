A vertical rectangular gate of fixed dimensions is placed at progressively greater depths. How does the distance between its centroid and its center of pressure change?

- It stays the same.
- It increases in proportion to depth.
- It decreases and approaches zero.
- It increases until it reaches half the gate height.

## Explanation

The distance from the centroid to the center of pressure is $\dfrac{I_G}{y_G A}$. The gate shape and area are fixed, so $I_G$ and $A$ stay constant as $y_G$ increases. The distance therefore **decreases** in proportion to $\dfrac{1}{y_G}$ and approaches zero. A vertical gate of height $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has its center of pressure $0.167\,\mathrm{m}$ below its centroid at depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, but only $0.017\,\mathrm{m}$ below it at depth $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$. At greater depths the pressure varies less over the height of the gate relative to its mean value.
