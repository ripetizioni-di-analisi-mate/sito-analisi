Two tanks have horizontal bases of equal area and contain water to depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The first is cylindrical. The second widens upward and contains twice as much water. How do the forces on their bases compare?

- The force is the same in both tanks.
- The force is twice as large in the tank that widens upward.
- The force is twice as large in the cylindrical tank.
- The force is proportional to the weight of water in the tank.

## Explanation

Pressure $p = \gamma h$ is uniform over the horizontal bottom, so the force $S = \gamma h A$ depends only on depth and bottom area. Both tanks have the same water level and the same bottom area. Each bottom carries **the same force**, $9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot A$. This is the **hydrostatic paradox**. In the second tank, the vertical component of the water force on the sloping walls accounts for the difference in water weight.
