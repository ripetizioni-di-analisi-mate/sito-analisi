A hemispherical dome projects outward from the vertical wall of a tank. It communicates with the tank and is full of water and entirely below the free surface. What can be said about the vertical component of the water force on it?

- It is zero because the forces on the upper and lower halves cancel.
- It equals the weight of water inside the dome and acts downward.
- It equals the weight of water inside the dome and acts upward.
- It equals the weight of water between the dome and free surface and acts downward.

## Explanation

Cut the dome with the horizontal plane through its center. On the upper half the liquid lies below the surface. Its upward force $\gamma V_1$ uses the hypothetical volume $V_1$ between that surface and the free surface. On the lower half the liquid lies above the surface. Its downward force $\gamma V_2$ uses the volume $V_2$ between the lower half and the free surface. Volume $V_2$ comprises $V_1$ plus the hemisphere, so the net vertical force is $\gamma\,(V_2 - V_1)$ downward, the **weight of the water inside the dome**.
