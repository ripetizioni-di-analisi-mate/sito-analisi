- $15.4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $35.0\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $4.21\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

The vent gives zero gauge pressure along the top of the tank. The hydrostatic head plane is therefore the horizontal plane tangent to the tank at its crown. The bolts restrain the upper half, where the water acts upward. The vertical force $\gamma V_p$ uses the hypothetical volume $V_p$ between the curved wall and the head plane. Per meter of tank length, the corresponding area is the rectangle of width $2R$ and height $R$ less the semicircle,
$$
V_p = 2R^2 - \frac{\pi R^2}{2} = 2.00 - 1.571 = 0.429\,\mathrm{m^3/m} .
$$
The two quarters of the upper shell have equal and opposite horizontal loads, so the bolts carry $S_z = 9.81 \cdot 0.429 = 4.21\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The value $15.4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ is the weight of the water inside the upper half, which would apply if the head plane coincided with the bolt plane.
