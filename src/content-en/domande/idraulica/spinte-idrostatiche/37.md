The radial gate shown has radius $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Its pin $O$ is at the upstream free surface. The water depth is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream, and the gate touches the bottom directly below $O$. The downstream side is dry. What is the vertical component of the water force?

- $46.2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $29.4\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $58.9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $12.6\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The liquid is below the wetted surface, so its vertical force acts upward. Its magnitude is $\gamma$ times the volume of liquid that would occupy the space above the gate. The gate projects horizontally between the pin and the support. The region between its surface and the hydrostatic head plane is a quarter cylinder of radius $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$,
$$
V_p = \frac{\pi \cdot 2.00^2}{4} \cdot 1.50 = 4.71\,\mathrm{m}^3 .
$$
Thus $S_z = 9.81 \cdot 4.71 = 46.2\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts upward. Using the full square of side $R$ instead of a quarter circle would give $58.9\,\mathrm{kN}$. The value $29.4\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the horizontal component.
