The foot of a vertical wall is joined to the bottom by a quarter-circle cylindrical surface of radius $R = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$; the water depth above the bottom is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown in the figure. What angle does the hydrostatic force on the transition make with the horizontal?

- $50.0^\circ$
- $40.0^\circ$
- $27.6^\circ$
- $74.4^\circ$

## Explanation

The horizontal component is the force on the vertical projection of the transition, a rectangle $R = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ high and $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ wide lying between the depths $1.50$ and $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
S_x = \gamma\,h_G\,A_x = 9.81 \cdot 2.25 \cdot 3.00 = 66.2\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The vertical component is $\gamma$ times the pressure volume, made of the prism of height $H - R = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the transition and of the quarter cylinder, therefore
$$
S_z = \gamma\left[R\,(H - R) + \frac{\pi R^2}{4}\right] b = 9.81 \cdot (2.25 + 1.77) \cdot 2.00 = 78.8\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The angle of the force with the horizontal is thus $\arctan\dfrac{S_z}{S_x} = \arctan 1.19 = 50.0^\circ$, and the line of action passes through the center $O$ of the arc, since every elementary pressure force is normal to the cylindrical surface. The value $40.0^\circ$ is the angle with the vertical, $27.6^\circ$ comes from counting only the quarter cylinder in the pressure volume, and $74.4^\circ$ comes from placing the centroid of the projection at half the radius.
