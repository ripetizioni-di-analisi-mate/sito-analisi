A rectangular gate of width $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and downslope length $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is mounted on a wall inclined at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal, with water above it. Its upper edge is hinged at the free surface. A force $F$ normal to the wall acts at its lower edge to hold it closed, as shown. What is $F$?

::schema{nodi="A,1,1.7321,-;B,0,0,-" aste="A-B" cerniere="A" acqua="-3,0 0,0 1,1.7321 -3,1.7321" solidi="-3.4,-0.6 3,-0.6 3,2.1 1.2125,2.1 1,1.7321 0,0 -3.4,0" pelo="-3,-1,1.7321,p_g = 0" reazioni="B,-0.78,0.45,F" quote="B,A,L = 2.00 m,-0.5" etichette="0.55,0.18,60°" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate inclined at $60^\circ$, hinged at the free surface and held by force $F$ at its lower edge"}

- $12.7\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $8.50\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $17.0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Measured down the slope, the gate centroid is $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge. Its vertical depth is $h_G = 1.00 \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 0.866\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 0.866 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 2.00 = 25.5\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The upper edge is at the free surface, so pressure varies linearly from zero along the gate. The resultant acts two-thirds of the gate length from the top, at distance $1.33\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge. Taking moments about the hinge gives $F \cdot 2.00 = 25.5 \cdot 1.33$ and hence $F = 17.0\,\mathrm{kN}$. Placing the force at the centroid gives $12.7\,\mathrm{kN}$. Treating the distance from the hinge to the centroid along the gate as the vertical depth gives $19.6\,\mathrm{kN}$.
