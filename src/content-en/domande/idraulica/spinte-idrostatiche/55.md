A square gate with horizontal and vertical edges and a circular gate are mounted in a vertical wall. They have equal areas and their centroids are at the same depth. Which has the lower center of pressure?

- The circle, because its vertical extent is greater.
- The square, because for equal areas it has the greater centroidal second moment of area.
- Their centers of pressure have the same depth because their areas and centroid depths are equal.
- It depends on the centroid depth.

## Explanation

The center of pressure lies $\dfrac{I_G}{y_G A}$ below the centroid. The values $y_G$ and $A$ are common to both gates, leaving just the second moment $I_G$ to compare. The square of side $a$ has $A = a^2$, giving $I_G = \dfrac{a^4}{12} = \dfrac{A^2}{12} = 0.0833\,A^2$. The circle of diameter $D$ has $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$, giving $I_G = \dfrac{\pi D^4}{64} = \dfrac{A^2}{4\pi} = 0.0796\,A^2$. The square has the larger second moment, so its center of pressure is slightly lower at every depth.
