A closed tank holds water to depth $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ under air at gauge pressure $-9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the shape of the pressure diagram over the wetted part of the vertical wall?

- Triangular, from zero at the surface to $29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the bottom.
- Rectangular, with pressure $-9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$ over the full depth.
- Linear, from $9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the free surface to $39.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the bottom.
- Linear, negative over the first meter below the surface and positive farther down, reaching $19.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the bottom.

## Explanation

At the free surface, water pressure equals air pressure $-9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The hydrostatic pressure law gives an increase $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$ for each meter of depth. The linear pressure diagram crosses zero at depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, the hydrostatic head plane, and reaches $-9.81 + 9.81 \cdot 3.00 = 19.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the bottom. The negative gauge pressure in the top meter gives a net load inward on that part of the wall. Reversing the sign of the air pressure would give a surface pressure of $9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
