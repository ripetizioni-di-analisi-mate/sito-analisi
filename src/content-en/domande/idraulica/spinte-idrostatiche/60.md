A vertical gate slides in side guides and closes a channel of width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. It extends from the bed to above the upstream water level, where depth is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The downstream channel is dry. The gate weighs $5.00\,\mathrm{kN}$ and the gate–guide friction coefficient is $0.30$. What upward force initiates lifting?

- $11.8\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $28.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5.00\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $16.8\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The horizontal water force presses the gate against its downstream guides with force
$$
S = \gamma\,\frac{b\,h^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{2.00 \cdot 2.00^2}{2} = 39.2\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The contact produces friction $f\,S = 0.30 \cdot 39.2 = 11.8\,\mathrm{kN}$ opposing vertical sliding. To raise the gate, the lifting force must overcome both its weight and this friction, giving $F = W + f\,S = 5.00 + 11.8 = 16.8\,\mathrm{kN}$. Using bottom pressure in place of centroid pressure would double the water force and give $F = 28.5\,\mathrm{kN}$.
