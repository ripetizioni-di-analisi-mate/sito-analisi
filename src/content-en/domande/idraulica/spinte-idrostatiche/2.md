A vertical rectangular gate of width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ closes an opening between depths $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. At what depth is its center of pressure?

- $2.94\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.75\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.67\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The gate height is $4.00 - 1.50 = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a centroid depth $y_G = 2.75\,\mathrm{m}$ and area $A = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}^2$. The second moment of area about the horizontal centroidal axis is
$$
I_G = \frac{b\,\ell^3}{12} = \frac{2.00 \cdot 2.50^3}{12} = 2.60\,\mathrm{m}^4 .
$$
Because the wall is vertical, distances measured down the gate equal vertical depths. The **center-of-pressure formula** therefore gives
$$
y_C = y_G + \frac{I_G}{y_G\,A} = 2.75 + \frac{2.60}{2.75 \cdot 5.00} = 2.94\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The depth $2.75\,\mathrm{m}$ locates the centroid. The center of pressure lies farther down.
