A retaining wall has base width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The resultant of all forces intersects the foundation plane at a distance $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ from the downstream toe. What can be said about the bearing pressure beneath the base?

- The resultant is outside the middle third, so tension would develop under the upstream heel.
- The resultant lies inside the base, so the entire base is in compression.
- The resultant is outside the middle third, so tension would develop under the downstream toe.
- The resultant lies within the middle third, so the entire base is in compression.

## Explanation

The midpoint of the base is $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from the downstream toe, giving a resultant eccentricity $e = 1.50 - 0.80 = 0.70\,\mathrm{m}$. The middle-third limit is $\dfrac{B}{6} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Since $0.70\,\mathrm{m} > 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, the resultant lies outside the middle third toward the toe. A linear bearing-pressure distribution would peak at the toe and become negative at the upstream heel. The latter would require tension at the foundation–soil contact, which the contact cannot transmit.
