Water reaches the upper edge of a vertical wall. If the depth changes from $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, how does the hydrostatic force per unit width change?

- It doubles.
- It triples.
- It increases by a factor of eight.
- It increases by a factor of four.

## Explanation

The triangular pressure diagram gives a force per unit width of $\gamma\,\dfrac{h^2}{2}$, proportional to the square of the water depth. Doubling $h$ therefore **quadruples the force**, from $9.81 \cdot \dfrac{2.00^2}{2} = 19.6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ to $9.81 \cdot \dfrac{4.00^2}{2} = 78.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The moment about the foot of the wall is proportional to depth cubed and increases eightfold.
