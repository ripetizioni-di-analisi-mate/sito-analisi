A semicircular trough of radius $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ consists of two quarter-cylindrical shells joined by a longitudinal hinge at their lowest point $B$ and connected by horizontal tie rods at the top. It is full of water to the rim, as shown. Neglecting the trough weight, what tie force is required per meter of length?

- $1.64\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $3.27\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $7.70\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $4.91\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

Isolate the right half and take moments about hinge $B$. This half is a cylindrical sector. Every elemental pressure force acts through axis $O$, so the resultant passes through $O$, vertically above $B$ at elevation $R$. Its vertical component has zero moment arm about $B$. Only its horizontal component contributes to the moment. This component equals the force on the vertical projection of the half,
$$
S_x = \gamma\,\frac{R^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{1.00^2}{2} = 4.91\,\mathrm{kN/m} ,
$$
acting with moment arm $R$. The tie acts at the top with moment arm $R$, giving $T\,R = S_x\,R$ and $T = 4.91\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Alternatively, apply each force component along its own line of action. The component $S_x$ acts $\dfrac{R}{3}$ above the bottom, giving moment $\gamma\,\dfrac{R^3}{6}$. The component $S_z = \gamma\,\dfrac{\pi R^2}{4}$ acts through the centroid of the overlying water volume, $\dfrac{4R}{3\pi}$ from the vertical through $B$, giving moment $\gamma\,\dfrac{R^3}{3}$. Their sum $\gamma\,\dfrac{R^3}{2}$, divided by $R$, again gives $4.91\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Including only the moment of $S_x$ would give $1.64\,\mathrm{kN/m}$.
