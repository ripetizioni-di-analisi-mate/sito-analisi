The upstream face of a wall is a quarter cylinder of radius $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ convex toward the water. It is vertical at the foot and horizontal at the top. Water reaches the top, $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom, as shown. What is the vertical component of the force per meter of wall length?

::schema{acqua="-6,0 -3,0 -2.8978,0.7765 -2.5981,1.5 -2.1213,2.1213 -1.5,2.5981 -0.7765,2.8978 0,3 -6,3" solidi="-3,0 -2.8978,0.7765 -2.5981,1.5 -2.1213,2.1213 -1.5,2.5981 -0.7765,2.8978 0,3 1.2,3 1.2,0;-6.4,-0.6 1.6,-0.6 1.6,0 -6.4,0" pelo="-6,-4.2,3,p_g = 0" punti="0,0,O,centro" tratteggi="0,0 -2.1213,2.1213" etichette="-0.6,1.35,R = 3.00 m" quote="-6 0,-6 3,3.00 m,-0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Quarter-circular upstream face bulging toward the water, centered at $O$ at the foot of the wall"}

- $18.9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $69.3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $88.3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $44.1\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

The water lies above the curved wall face, so its vertical force acts downward. Its magnitude equals the weight of the water bounded by the face, the free surface, and the vertical line through the foot of the wall. Per meter of wall length, the corresponding area is the square of side $R$ minus the quarter circle occupied by the wall,
$$
V_p = R^2 - \frac{\pi R^2}{4} = 9.00 - 7.07 = 1.93\,\mathrm{m^3/m} ,
$$
so $S_z = 9.81 \cdot 1.93 = 18.9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The value $69.3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ is the weight of a quarter cylinder of water per meter, which would apply to a wall face concave toward the water. The value $44.1\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ is the horizontal force component.
