A catamaran has two identical vertical-sided hulls, each with a rectangular waterplane of length $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Their longitudinal centerlines are $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ apart and the draft is $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What is the metacentric radius about the longitudinal axis of symmetry?

::schema{acqua="-4,0 4,0 4,1.2 -4,1.2" poligoni="-1.75,0.8 -1.25,0.8 -1.25,1.8 -1.75,1.8;1.25,0.8 1.75,0.8 1.75,1.8 1.25,1.8;-1.75,1.8 1.75,1.8 1.75,2 -1.75,2" tratteggi="-1.5,0.4 -1.5,2.5;1.5,0.4 1.5,2.5;0,0.2 0,2.7" pelo="3.1,4,1.2," quote="-1.5 2.5,1.5 2.5,3.00 m,-0.4;-1.75 0.8,-1.25 0.8,0.50 m,0.4;1.75 0.8,1.75 1.2,0.40 m,0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Transverse section through both hulls of the catamaran"}

- $5.68\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0521\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8.93\,\mathrm{m}$
- $22.6\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The waterplane comprises two rectangles $6.00 \times 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ whose centroids are each $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from the symmetry axis. The parallel-axis theorem gives the second moment about this axis as
$$
I = 2\left( \frac{6.00 \cdot 0.50^3}{12} + 6.00 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 1.50^2 \right) = 2\,(0.0625 + 6.75) = 13.63\,\mathrm{m}^4 .
$$
The displaced volume is $V_c = 2 \cdot 6.00 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 0.40 = 2.40\,\mathrm{m}^3$, giving $r = \dfrac{13.63}{2.40} = 5.68\,\mathrm{m}$. Omitting the parallel-axis contribution would give only $r = 0.0521\,\mathrm{m}$. The catamaran’s transverse stability therefore comes primarily from the separation of its hulls. Using the full spacing $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ instead of half the spacing would give $22.6\,\mathrm{m}$.
