Uplift under a dam base of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has a triangular distribution, from $\gamma h$ at the upstream heel, where water depth is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, to zero at the downstream toe. What is the resultant uplift per unit length?

- $39.2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $58.9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $118\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $78.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

Uplift pressure at the upstream heel is $\gamma h = 9.81 \cdot 4.00 = 39.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the triangular distribution extends over base width $B = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The area of the triangle gives the resultant per unit length,
$$
U = \frac{\gamma\,h\,B}{2} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 3.00}{2} = 58.9\,\mathrm{kN/m} ,
$$
acting upward at distance $\dfrac{B}{3} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ from the heel. Uplift reduces the vertical load transmitted to the foundation and reduces stability against both sliding and overturning. The value $39.2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ is the pressure at the heel, in $\mathrm{kPa}$. Omitting the factor of one-half in the area of the triangle gives $118\,\mathrm{kN/m}$.
