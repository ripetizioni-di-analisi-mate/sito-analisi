To measure the metacentric height of a pontoon of total weight $250\,\mathrm{kN}$, an onboard weight $5.00\,\mathrm{kN}$ is shifted sideways by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The pontoon heels through angle $4.0^\circ$. What is its metacentric height?

- $0.286\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0400\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.572\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Moving the load $w$ a distance $d$ shifts the pontoon’s center of gravity transversely by
$$
\overline{GG'} = \frac{w\,d}{P} = \frac{5.00 \cdot 2.00}{250} = 0.0400\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pontoon heels until its new center of gravity $G'$ lies on the vertical through the metacenter. Weight and buoyancy then act along the same line, giving $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\overline{GG'}}{\overline{GM}}$. It follows that $\overline{GM} = \dfrac{0.0400}{\tan 4.0^\circ} = \dfrac{0.0400}{0.0699} = 0.572\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0.0400\,\mathrm{m}$ is the shift of the center of gravity before division by $\tan\theta$.
