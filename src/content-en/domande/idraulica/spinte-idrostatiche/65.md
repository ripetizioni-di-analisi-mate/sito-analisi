A circular opening in a horizontal tank floor is closed by a downward-projecting hemispherical cap of radius $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The cap is full of water and the water surface is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor, as shown. What force acts on the cap?

::schema{nodi="H0,-0.5,0,-;H1,-0.433,-0.25,-;H2,-0.25,-0.433,-;H3,0,-0.5,-;H4,0.25,-0.433,-;H5,0.433,-0.25,-;H6,0.5,0,-" aste="H0-H1;H1-H2;H2-H3;H3-H4;H4-H5;H5-H6" acqua="-2,0 -0.5,0 -0.433,-0.25 -0.25,-0.433 0,-0.5 0.25,-0.433 0.433,-0.25 0.5,0 2,0 2,2 -2,2" solidi="-2.3,-0.2 -0.5,-0.2 -0.5,0 -2.3,0;0.5,-0.2 2.3,-0.2 2.3,0 0.5,0;-2.3,0 -2,0 -2,2.4 -2.3,2.4;2,0 2.3,0 2.3,2.4 2,2.4" pelo="-1.7,-0.5,2,p_g = 0" tratteggi="0,0 0.433,-0.25" etichette="0.95,-0.45,R = 0.50 m" quote="2 0,2 2,2.00 m,0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Hemispherical cap covering an opening in the tank bottom"}

- $12.8\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $15.4\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $18.0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $20.5\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The cap is symmetric about its vertical axis, so the horizontal force components cancel. The resultant is vertical, with magnitude $\gamma V_p$. The liquid lies above the cap. The actual water volume above it comprises the cylinder above the opening and the hemisphere itself,
$$
V_p = \pi R^2 H + \frac{2}{3}\pi R^3 = \pi \cdot 0.50^2 \cdot 2.00 + \frac{2}{3}\pi \cdot 0.50^3 = 1.571 + 0.262 = 1.833\,\mathrm{m}^3 .
$$
giving a downward force $S = 9.81 \cdot 1.833 = 18.0\,\mathrm{kN}$. Subtracting the hemisphere instead of adding it would give $12.8\,\mathrm{kN}$, as if the cap projected inward into the tank. Counting only the cylinder would give $15.4\,\mathrm{kN}$.
