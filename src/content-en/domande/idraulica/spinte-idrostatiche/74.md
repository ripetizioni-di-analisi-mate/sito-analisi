The upstream face of a gravity dam is inclined, with its foot farther upstream than its top. Some water therefore lies above the face. How does the vertical water force affect the checks against overturning and sliding?

- It acts upward and reduces the normal force on the foundation.
- It acts downward and increases both normal force and stabilizing moment.
- It acts downward, increasing the normal force but reducing the stabilizing moment.
- It is zero because the force on a plane surface is normal to that surface.

## Explanation

The water lies above the inclined face, so the vertical component of the water force acts downward and equals the weight of the water between the face and the free surface. It adds to the dam’s weight in the resultant vertical load $N$, increasing the available friction $f\,N$ and the factor of safety against sliding. Its line of action crosses the upstream part of the base, well away from the downstream toe about which the dam would overturn. Its moment is therefore stabilizing and also increases the factor of safety against overturning. The pressure force acts normal to the inclined face, which accounts for its vertical component.
