A stone weighs $540\,\mathrm{N}$ in air and has apparent weight $330\,\mathrm{N}$ when fully submerged in water. What is its volume?

- $21.4\,\mathrm{L}$
- $55.0\,\mathrm{L}$
- $210\,\mathrm{L}$
- $33.6\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

The scale reads the stone’s weight less the buoyant force, giving a buoyant force $540 - 330 = 210\,\mathrm{N}$. By **Archimedes’ principle**, it equals the weight of displaced water. Thus $S = \gamma V$ with $\gamma = 9810\,\mathrm{N/m^3}$, and
$$
V = \frac{210}{9810} = 0.0214\,\mathrm{m}^3 = 21.4\,\mathrm{L} .
$$
Dividing the force by the density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ instead of the specific weight would give $210\,\mathrm{L}$, roughly ten times the actual volume.
