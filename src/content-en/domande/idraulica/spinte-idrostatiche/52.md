Four flat gates are mounted in a vertical wall, all fully submerged. Which gate must have its center of pressure directly below its centroid?

- A right triangle with one vertical leg.
- A rectangle with unequal sides inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal.
- An ellipse with unequal semiaxes and axes inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal.
- A rhombus with one vertical and one horizontal diagonal.

## Explanation

The horizontal coordinate of the center of pressure is $x_C = x_G + \dfrac{I_{xy}}{y_G A}$, where $I_{xy}$ is the product of area inertia about the horizontal and vertical centroidal axes. The rhombus is symmetric about its vertical diagonal, so $I_{xy} = 0$ and the center of pressure lies on that diagonal, which passes through the centroid. The other three shapes have no vertical axis of symmetry and their products of inertia about those axes are nonzero. For example, when the axes of the ellipse are inclined at $45^\circ$, the magnitude of its product of inertia is $\dfrac{|I_1 - I_2|}{2}$. Here $I_1$ and $I_2$ are the second moments of area about its principal axes. They differ because the semiaxes differ. The center of pressure is therefore displaced sideways from the centroid for each of those three gates.
