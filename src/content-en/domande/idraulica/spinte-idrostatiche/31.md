A vertical rectangular gate of width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on the bottom and extends above the upstream surface. The water depths are $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream and $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream. At what height above the bottom does the net force act?

- $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.08\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.667\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Both pressure diagrams are triangular. The force $S_1 = 9.81 \cdot \dfrac{2.00 \cdot 3.00^2}{2} = 88.3\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom, and $S_2 = 9.81 \cdot \dfrac{2.00 \cdot 1.00^2}{2} = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts $0.333\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. Their difference is $88.3 - 9.81 = 78.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. Taking moments about the bottom locates its line of action:
$$
z = \frac{88.3 \cdot 1.00 - 9.81 \cdot 0.333}{78.5} = 1.08\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The downstream hydrostatic force acts below the upstream force. Subtracting it raises the line of action of the resultant. Its elevation, $1.08\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore exceeds $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, the elevation at which the upstream force acts alone.
