A quarter-cylindrical fillet of radius $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ joins a vertical wall to the bottom. The water depth is $3.80\,\mathrm{m}$ as shown. What is the vertical component of the hydrostatic force on the fillet?

- $121\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $102\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $49.9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $158\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The vertical force is $\gamma\,V_p$, where $V_p$ is the volume enclosed by the curved face, the hydrostatic head plane, and the vertical surfaces through its boundary. Divide this volume into a rectangular prism of height $H - R = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and depth $R$ and a quarter cylinder. This gives
$$
V_p = \left[ R\,(H - R) + \frac{\pi R^2}{4} \right] b = \left( 1.80 \cdot 2.00 + 2.54 \right) \cdot 2.00 = 12.3\,\mathrm{m}^3 .
$$
The liquid lies above the curved surface, so the vertical force acts downward and equals
$$
S_z = 9.81 \cdot 12.3 = 121\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The value $102\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the horizontal component. Counting the quarter cylinder alone and omitting the prism above it would give $49.9\,\mathrm{kN}$.
