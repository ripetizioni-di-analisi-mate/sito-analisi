A fully submerged vertical plane hatch of area $0.600\,\mathrm{m^2}$ separates two open tanks: one contains water, the other an oil of specific gravity $0.80$. The two free surfaces are at the same elevation, and the centroid of the hatch is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below them. What is the net hydrostatic force on the hatch?

- $2.35\,\mathrm{kN}$, directed toward the denser liquid
- $11.8\,\mathrm{kN}$, directed toward the less dense liquid
- $2.35\,\mathrm{kN}$, directed toward the less dense liquid
- Zero

## Explanation

On each face the force is $\gamma\,h_G\,A$, with the same centroid depth $h_G = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, because the free surfaces are at the same elevation. The face wetted by water receives $S_1 = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 0.600 = 11.8\,\mathrm{kN}$, while the face wetted by oil, of specific weight $0.80 \cdot 9.81 = 7.85\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$, receives $S_2 = 7.85 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 0.600 = 9.42\,\mathrm{kN}$. The two forces act in opposite directions, so the net force is their difference,
$$
S = S_1 - S_2 = (\gamma_1 - \gamma_2)\,h_G\,A = 1.96 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 0.600 = 2.35\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
directed from the water toward the oil, that is, toward the less dense liquid. The net force is nonzero even with the free surfaces at the same elevation, since at equal depth the pressure of water is greater than that of oil.
