A vessel of water rests on a scale that reads $50.0\,\mathrm{N}$. An aluminum block of volume $1.50\,\mathrm{L}$ and density $2700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is suspended from a thread and fully submerged without touching the bottom or sides. What does the scale now read?

- $50.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $35.3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $89.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $64.7\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Water exerts an upward buoyant force $S = 9810 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 10^{-3} = 14.7\,\mathrm{N}$ on the block. The block exerts an equal downward force on the water. As the level rises, the increased bottom pressure transmits that force to the tank, and the scale reads $50.0 + 14.7 = 64.7\,\mathrm{N}$. The string supports the rest of the block’s weight. The reading $89.7\,\mathrm{N}$ would apply if the block rested on the bottom with the string slack, because the block weighs $9.81 \cdot 2700 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 10^{-3} = 39.7\,\mathrm{N}$.
