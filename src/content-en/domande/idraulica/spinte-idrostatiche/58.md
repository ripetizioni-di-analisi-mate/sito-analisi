A vertical wall retains water of depth $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, with the surface at the upper edge. A horizontal joint is to divide the wall into two sections carrying equal water forces. At what depth should the joint be placed?

- $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.17\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.83\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Between the free surface and depth $y$ the pressure diagram is triangular, giving a force per unit width $\gamma\,\dfrac{y^2}{2}$. Place the joint where this is half the force on the entire wall, so
$$
\gamma\,\frac{y^2}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\,\gamma\,\frac{h^2}{2} ,
$$
giving $y = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{4.00}{1.414} = 2.83\,\mathrm{m}$. A joint at midheight would leave only one-quarter of the total force on the upper portion because pressure increases with depth. The distance from the joint to the bottom is $1.17\,\mathrm{m}$.
