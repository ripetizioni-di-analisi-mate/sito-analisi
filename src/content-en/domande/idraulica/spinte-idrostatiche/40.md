Seepage beneath the foundation of a gravity dam produces uplift. How does uplift affect the factors of safety against overturning and sliding?

- Neither changes, because uplift is an internal force.
- It lowers the sliding factor and leaves the overturning factor unchanged.
- It raises the overturning factor by adding a stabilizing moment.
- It lowers both.

## Explanation

Uplift acts upward. It reduces the resultant vertical load $N$ on the foundation and hence the available friction $f N$, lowering the factor of safety against sliding. Its line of action lies a distance $\dfrac{B}{3}$ from the upstream heel, on the opposite side of the base from the downstream toe about which the dam would overturn. Its moment opposes the stabilizing moment of the dam’s weight, so the factor of safety against overturning also falls. Drains near the heel reduce the uplift pressure and improve **both** stability checks.
