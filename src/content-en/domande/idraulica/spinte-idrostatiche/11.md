A concrete retaining wall of specific weight $23\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ has a vertical upstream face, height $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and base width $2.80\,\mathrm{m}$. The water depth is $4.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and there is no uplift, as shown. What is the factor of safety against overturning?

::schema{solidi="-3.6,-0.9 5.6,-0.9 5.6,0 -3.6,0;0,0 2.8,0 2.8,5 0,5" acqua="-3.4,0 0,0 0,4.2 -3.4,4.2" pelo="-3.4,-0.6,4.2,p_g = 0" quote="0 0,2.8 0,B = 2.80 m,1.5;2.8 0,2.8 5,5.00 m,1;-3.4 0,-3.4 4.2,4.20 m,-0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Retaining wall of height $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with base width $2.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and water depth $4.20\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $2.48$
- $3.72$
- $1.86$
- $2.05$

## Explanation

Consider a unit length of wall and take moments about its downstream toe. The wall’s weight $W = 23 \cdot 5.00 \cdot 2.80 = 322\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts at midwidth. Its moment arm is $1.40\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a stabilizing moment of $322 \cdot 1.40 = 451\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m}$. The water pressure distribution is triangular, so
$$
S_x = \gamma\,\frac{h^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{4.20^2}{2} = 86.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}
$$
and acts $\dfrac{h}{3} = 1.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. The overturning moment is therefore $86.5 \cdot 1.40 = 121\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m}$, and the ratio of the two moments gives $F_r = \dfrac{451}{121} = 3.72$. Using $\dfrac{2h}{3}$ as the moment arm would give $1.86$. That distance is measured from the free surface, whereas the pivot is at the base.
