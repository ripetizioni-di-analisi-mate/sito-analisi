A square gate of side $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is mounted in a vertical wall with one diagonal vertical. Its highest corner is at depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$. How far below the centroid is the center of pressure?

- $55.6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $83.3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $48.8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $195\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The square has diagonal $\sqrt{2} = 1.414\,\mathrm{m}$, placing its centroid at depth $y_G = 1.00 + 0.707 = 1.707\,\mathrm{m}$. Its two perpendicular centroidal symmetry axes have equal second moments and zero product of inertia. The second moment about any centroidal axis, including the horizontal diagonal, is therefore $I_G = \dfrac{a^4}{12} = 0.0833\,\mathrm{m}^4$. Thus
$$
y_C - y_G = \frac{I_G}{y_G A} = \frac{0.0833}{1.707 \cdot 1.00} = 0.0488\,\mathrm{m} = 48.8\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
Putting the centroid half a side below the corner, as for a square with horizontal sides, would give $55.6\,\mathrm{mm}$. Using the diagonal instead of the side in the second moment would give $195\,\mathrm{mm}$.
