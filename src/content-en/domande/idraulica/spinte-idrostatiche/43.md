A pontoon initially floats in fresh water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and is then moved to seawater of density $1025\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. How does its draft change?

- It increases because seawater is denser.
- It decreases because a smaller displaced volume is sufficient.
- It stays the same because the pontoon’s weight has not changed.
- It increases by $2.5\%$.

## Explanation

Flotation requires $\gamma V_c = P$, relating the displaced volume to the pontoon’s weight. The weight is unchanged while the specific weight of the liquid rises from $9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ to $10.06\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$. The displaced volume therefore **decreases**, and so does the draft. The ratio of the two drafts is $\dfrac{1000}{1025} = 0.976$, a draft reduction of about $2.4\%$.
