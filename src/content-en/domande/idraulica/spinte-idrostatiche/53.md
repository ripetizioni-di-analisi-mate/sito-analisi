A closed tank contains water beneath air at gauge pressure $9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$. A rectangular gate of height $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ in the vertical wall has its upper edge at the free surface, as shown. At what depth is the center of pressure?

::schema{nodi="A,4,1,-;B,4,3,-" aste="A-B" acqua="0,0 4,0 4,3 0,3" solidi="-0.4,-0.4 4.4,-0.4 4.4,0 -0.4,0;-0.4,0 0,0 0,4.4 -0.4,4.4;0,4 4.4,4 4.4,4.4 0,4.4;4,3 4.4,3 4.4,4 4,4;4,0 4.4,0 4.4,1 4,1" pelo="0.3,1.6,3," etichette="2,3.5,air at p_0 = 9.81 kPa" quote="4 1,4 3,2.00 m,0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Closed tank with pressurized air and a gate in its vertical wall"}

- $1.17\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.33\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.17\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The air pressure is equivalent to a water column of height $\dfrac{9.81}{9.81} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, placing the hydrostatic head plane $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the free surface. Distances are measured from that plane. The gate centroid is therefore $y_G = 1.00 + 1.00 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below it, and with $\ell = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$,
$$
y_C = y_G + \frac{\ell^2}{12\,y_G} = 2.00 + \frac{2.00^2}{12 \cdot 2.00} = 2.17\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
This distance is measured from the head plane, so the center of pressure lies $2.17 - 1.00 = 1.17\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. Without the pressurized air, the diagram would be triangular and the center of pressure would lie at $1.33\,\mathrm{m}$. The air adds uniform pressure, shifting the center of pressure toward the centroid.
