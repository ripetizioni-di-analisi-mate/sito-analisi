A concrete gravity dam has a triangular cross section with upstream heel at $x = 0$, crest at horizontal coordinate $x = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and elevation $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and downstream toe at $x = 4.80\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. The concrete has specific weight $24\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$, the water depth is $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and the base friction coefficient is $0.60$. Neglecting uplift, what is the factor of safety against sliding?

::schema{nodi="T,0,0;K,1.2,6,-;P,4.8,0" aste="T-K;K-P;T-P" acqua="-3.5,0 0,0 1,5 -3.5,5" solidi="-4,-1 7,-1 7,0 -4,0" pelo="-3.5,-0.3,5,p_g = 0" quote="T,P,B = 4.80 m,1.7;-3.5 0,-3.5 5,5.00 m,-0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Gravity dam of height $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with a sloping upstream face and base width $4.80\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $5.27$
- $1.69$
- $1.57$
- $1.81$

## Explanation

The triangular cross section has area $\dfrac{4.80 \cdot 6.00}{2} = 14.4\,\mathrm{m}^2$, giving the dam a weight of $W = 24 \cdot 14.4 = 346\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ per unit length. Since the upstream face slopes, the water also exerts a downward force equal to the weight of the water above the face. Its cross section is a right triangle with legs $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and area $2.50\,\mathrm{m}^2$, so the downward force is $S_z = 24.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The resultant vertical load on the foundation is therefore $N = 346 + 24.5 = 370\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, and the horizontal water force is $S_x = \gamma\,\dfrac{5.00^2}{2} = 123\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Thus
$$
F_s = \frac{f\,N}{S_x} = \frac{0.60 \cdot 370}{123} = 1.81 .
$$
Neglecting the vertical component of the hydrostatic force would give $1.69$.
