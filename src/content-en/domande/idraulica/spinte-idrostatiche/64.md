A circular gate of diameter $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ closes a bottom outlet in a vertical wall. Its center is at depth $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$. The gate is hinged at its highest point, and a horizontal force $F$ at its lowest point holds it closed. What is $F$?

- $3.08\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $6.16\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $4.11\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $3.56\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The resultant force is $S = 9.81 \cdot 0.80 \cdot \dfrac{\pi \cdot 1.00^2}{4} = 6.16\,\mathrm{kN}$. For the circle, $\dfrac{I_G}{y_G A} = \dfrac{D^2}{16\,y_G}$, so the center of pressure lies below the gate center by
$$
\frac{1.00^2}{16 \cdot 0.80} = 0.0781\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $0.50 + 0.078 = 0.578\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge. Moment equilibrium about the hinge gives $F \cdot 1.00 = 6.16 \cdot 0.578$ and hence $F = 3.56\,\mathrm{kN}$. Placing the resultant at the gate center would give $3.08\,\mathrm{kN}$, too little force to hold it closed.
