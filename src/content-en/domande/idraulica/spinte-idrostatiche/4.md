A rectangular gate of width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and downslope length $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is mounted on a wall inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal. Its upper edge is at depth $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ as shown. What force does the water exert on it?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0.5272;B,1.2728,1.8" aste="A-B" acqua="-2.6,0 -0.5272,0 2.4728,3 -2.6,3" solidi="-3,-0.8 4,-0.8 4,3.4 2.8728,3.4 -0.5272,0 -3,0" pelo="-2.6,2.4728,3,p_g = 0" quote="A,B,1.80 m,0.9;1.2728 1.8,1.2728 3,1.20 m,-1.6" etichette="-0.42,0.08,α" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate of length $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ in a wall inclined at $45^\circ$"}

- $42.4\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $64.9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $74.2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $45.9\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The centroid is halfway along the gate, a vertical distance $0.90 \cdot \sin 45^\circ = 0.636\,\mathrm{m}$ below its upper edge. Its depth is therefore $h_G = 1.20 + 0.636 = 1.836\,\mathrm{m}$. With area $A = 2.00 \cdot 1.80 = 3.60\,\mathrm{m}^2$, the resultant is
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 1.836 \cdot 3.60 = 64.9\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
normal to the gate. The plane-surface force formula holds at any inclination. The sine of the angle is needed only to calculate centroid depth. Multiplying the force by $\sin 45^\circ$ would give $45.9\,\mathrm{kN}$, while treating $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ as a vertical distance would give $74.2\,\mathrm{kN}$.
