On a vertical wall, a plane gate has the shape of an isosceles triangle, with its horizontal base, $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ wide, at the free surface and its vertex $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ lower. What is the hydrostatic force on the gate?

- $6.62\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $4.41\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $8.83\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $13.2\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The centroid of a triangle lies at one third of the height measured from the base, and the base is at the free surface, so the depth of the centroid is $h_G = \dfrac{1.50}{3} = 0.500\,\mathrm{m}$. The area of the gate is $A = \dfrac{1.20 \cdot 1.50}{2} = 0.900\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The **theorem on the force on a plane surface** applies, therefore
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 0.500 \cdot 0.900 = 4.41\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
Placing the centroid at mid-height would give $6.62\,\mathrm{kN}$, measuring one third of the height from the vertex instead of the base would give $8.83\,\mathrm{kN}$, and taking the depth of the vertex would give $13.2\,\mathrm{kN}$.
