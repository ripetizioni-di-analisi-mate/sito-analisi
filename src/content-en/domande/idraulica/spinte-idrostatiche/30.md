A vertical rectangular gate of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on the bottom and extends above the upstream surface. The water depths are $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream and $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream, as shown. What is the net force on the gate?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4.4" aste="A-B" acqua="-2.6,0 0,0 0,4 -2.6,4;0,0 2.6,0 2.6,1.5 0,1.5" solidi="-3,-0.7 3,-0.7 3,0 -3,0" pelo="-2.6,-1.2,4,upstream;1.2,2.6,1.5,downstream" quote="-2.6 0,-2.6 4,4.00 m,-0.7;2.6 0,2.6 1.5,1.50 m,0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate with water depths $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream and $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream"}

- $235\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $269\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $202\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $92.0\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Calculate the two forces separately using each side’s hydrostatic head plane. The gate extends above both water levels, so both pressure diagrams are triangular. On the upstream side,
$$
S_1 = \gamma\,\frac{b\,h_1^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{3.00 \cdot 4.00^2}{2} = 235\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
while the downstream face gives $S_2 = 9.81 \cdot \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 1.50^2}{2} = 33.1\,\mathrm{kN}$. The two forces oppose one another, so the net force $235 - 33.1 = 202\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downstream. Adding the two instead of taking their difference would give $269\,\mathrm{kN}$.
