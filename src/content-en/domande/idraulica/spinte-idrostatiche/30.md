A fully submerged vertical plane gate separates two freshwater basins open to the atmosphere, whose free surfaces differ by $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown in the figure. The gate can rotate about a horizontal axis through the centroid $G$ of its surface. What is the moment of the net hydrostatic force about the axis?

::schema{solidi="2.91,0 3.09,0 3.09,0.6 2.91,0.6;2.91,2 3.09,2 3.09,3.7 2.91,3.7" acqua="0,0 2.91,0 2.91,3.24 0,3.24;3.09,0 6.0,0 6.0,2.84 3.09,2.84" pelo="0,2.91,3.24,;3.09,6,2.84," linee="0,0 6,0;3,0.6 3,2" punti="3,1.3,G,centro" riferimento="0" titolo="Vertical section of the gate between the two basins: the rotation axis passes through $G$ perpendicular to the drawing"}

- Nonzero, tending to open the upper part downstream
- Zero only if the gate is rectangular
- Zero, whatever the shape of the gate
- Nonzero, tending to open the lower part downstream

## Explanation

At a point of the gate at depth $y$ below the lower free surface, the pressure is $\gamma\,(y + 0.40)$ on the side of the higher basin and $\gamma\,y$ on the other side. The net pressure is therefore $\gamma \cdot 0.40 = 3.92\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at every point, that is, it is uniform over the whole surface. The resultant of a uniform pressure passes through the centroid of the surface, so the net force, equal to $3.92\,\mathrm{kPa}$ times the area, has zero lever arm about the axis. The moment is therefore zero, whatever the shape of the gate, provided that the axis passes through the centroid and the gate lies entirely below both free surfaces.
