A rectangular hatch of length $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ in a horizontal tank floor is hinged along one short side and opens downward. Atmospheric air is below the hatch and the water depth is $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting hatch weight, what vertical force at the opposite edge holds it closed?

- $15.7\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $11.8\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $23.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $7.85\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

All points on the hatch are at depth $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and pressure $p = 9.81 \cdot 2.50 = 24.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The downward force is therefore
$$
S = p\,A = 24.5 \cdot 1.20 \cdot 0.80 = 23.5\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
With uniform pressure, the center of pressure is the hatch centroid, a distance $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge. Taking moments about the hinge gives $F \cdot 1.20 = 23.5 \cdot 0.60$, hence an upward force $F = 11.8\,\mathrm{kN}$. Placing the center of pressure two-thirds of the way along the hatch would give $15.7\,\mathrm{kN}$. That location applies to triangular pressure loading, whereas pressure on this horizontal hatch is uniform.
