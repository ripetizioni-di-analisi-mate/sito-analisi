A vertical wall holds a layer of oil of specific weight $8.00\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ and thickness $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ floating over $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, as shown. What is the total force per meter of wall width?

::schema{acqua="0,0 4,0 4,2 0,2" poligoni="0,2 4,2 4,3.2 0,3.2" solidi="-0.5,-0.5 0,-0.5 0,3.7 -0.5,3.7;0,-0.5 4.4,-0.5 4.4,0 0,0" pelo="1.6,4,3.2," etichette="2.3,2.6,oil γ_o = 8.00 kN/m³;2.3,1,water" quote="4 2,4 3.2,1.20 m,0.5;4 0,4 2,2.00 m,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Layer of oil over water against a vertical wall"}

- $50.2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $44.6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $38.8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $25.4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

In the oil layer, pressure rises from zero at the free surface to $8.00 \cdot 1.20 = 9.60\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the interface. In the water it then rises from that value to $9.60 + 9.81 \cdot 2.00 = 29.22\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the bottom, with a slope set by the specific weight of water. The pressure diagram is triangular in the oil and trapezoidal in the water. The force per meter of width is the sum of their areas,
$$
S = \frac{9.60 \cdot 1.20}{2} + \frac{9.60 + 29.22}{2}\cdot 2.00 = 5.76 + 38.82 = 44.6\,\mathrm{kN/m} .
$$
Neglecting the pressure that the oil exerts on the water would give $25.4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The value $50.2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ corresponds to $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$ of water alone.
