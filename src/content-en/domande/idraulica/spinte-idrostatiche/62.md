An L-shaped gate is hinged at corner $O$ on a channel bed. Its vertical leg extends above the upstream water level. The horizontal leg of length $a = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ extends upstream and rests on a sill, so water acts only on its top surface. The downstream channel is dry, as shown. Neglecting the gate weight, at what water depth $h$ does the gate begin to rotate about $O$?

::schema{nodi="O,0,0;T,0,2.6,-;E,-1,0,-" aste="O-T;O-E" cerniere="O" acqua="-3.5,0 0,0 0,1.9 -3.5,1.9" solidi="-3.9,-0.6 3,-0.6 3,0 -3.9,0" pelo="-3.5,-1.6,1.9,p_g = 0" quote="E,O,a = 1.00 m,-0.4;-3.5 0,-3.5 1.9,h,-0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="L-shaped gate hinged at corner $O$"}

- $1.73\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.45\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Per unit width, the force $\gamma\,\dfrac{h^2}{2}$ on the vertical gate leaf acts $\dfrac{h}{3}$ above the bottom. Its moment $\gamma\,\dfrac{h^3}{6}$ about $O$ tends to open the gate. Pressure $\gamma h$ is uniform across the horizontal leaf. Its resultant $\gamma\,h\,a$ acts at the midpoint and produces the closing moment $\gamma\,h\,\dfrac{a^2}{2}$. The gate opens when the opening moment exceeds the closing moment, that is, when
$$
\frac{h^3}{6} > \frac{h\,a^2}{2} ,
$$
or $h > \sqrt{3}\,a = 1.73\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$ would result from applying the force on the vertical leaf halfway up instead of one-third of the way up.
