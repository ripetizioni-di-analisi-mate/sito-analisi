Flow in a full circular pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ is laminar. At what distance from the centerline does the local velocity equal the mean velocity?

- $50.0\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $100\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $35.4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $70.7\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

In laminar flow, the velocity profile is parabolic, $v(r) = \dfrac{\gamma J}{4\mu}\left( r_0^2 - r^2 \right)$. The maximum velocity, $v_{\max} = \dfrac{\gamma J r_0^2}{4\mu}$, occurs at the centerline, and the mean velocity is half the maximum. The condition $v(r) = \dfrac{v_{\max}}{2}$ therefore gives
$$
r_0^2 - r^2 = \frac{r_0^2}{2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
r = \frac{r_0}{\sqrt{2}} .
$$
Since $r_0 = \dfrac{D}{2} = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$, the required distance is $r = 70.7\,\mathrm{mm}$. The local velocity equals the mean closer to the wall than to the centerline because the outer annuli have larger areas and contribute more to the cross-sectional average.
