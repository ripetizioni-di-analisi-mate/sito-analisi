A pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water and discharges below the free surface of a large reservoir. Which statement about the piezometric head at the last pipe section, immediately before the outlet, is correct?

- It equals the reservoir water-surface elevation
- It lies $0.129\,\mathrm{m}$ above the reservoir water-surface elevation
- It lies $0.129\,\mathrm{m}$ below the reservoir water-surface elevation
- It equals the pipe centerline elevation at the outlet

## Explanation

Discharge into a large reservoir is a sudden expansion with $A_2 \to \infty$ and hence $v_2 \to 0$. In the derivation of the Borda–Carnot loss, the pressure difference is $p_1 - p_2 = \rho\,v_2\,(v_2 - v_1)$. It vanishes as $v_2 \to 0$, so the pressure at the last pipe section equals the hydrostatic pressure in the reservoir at that elevation. The piezometric head therefore **equals the reservoir free-surface elevation**. The energy grade line lies above it by the velocity head, $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \dfrac{1.59^2}{19.62} = 0.129\,\mathrm{m}$. This entire head is dissipated at the outlet, consistent with $\xi = 1$.
