On the Moody diagram, what do the horizontal lines approached by the curves at high Reynolds number represent?

- The straight line for laminar flow, $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re}$
- The curve for hydraulically smooth pipes
- The transition region between laminar and turbulent flow
- The values of $\lambda$ in the fully rough regime, one for each relative roughness

## Explanation

The Moody diagram plots $\lambda$ against $Re$ on logarithmic axes, with a separate curve for each relative roughness. The turbulent-flow curves therefore represent the Colebrook–White equation. As the Reynolds number increases, $\dfrac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\lambda}}$ becomes negligible and the equation reduces to the Prandtl–Nikuradse relation, in which $\lambda$ depends only on relative roughness. At sufficiently high $Re$, each curve therefore levels off at the friction factor for that relative roughness. The laminar-flow line slopes downward on the left of the diagram and extends to $Re = 2000$.
