A horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L = 200\,\mathrm{m}$, and friction factor $\lambda = 0.020$ connects two reservoirs and carries $Q = 30\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. The outlet is submerged in the downstream reservoir, with its free surface $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the pipe centerline. What is the gauge pressure at the centerline halfway along the pipe?

- $29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $67.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $10.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $48.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The submerged outlet into the reservoir acts as a sudden expansion. The entire velocity head is lost, and the pressure at the last pipe section equals the hydrostatic pressure in the reservoir at that elevation. The hydraulic grade line at that section is therefore at the downstream free-surface elevation, $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the pipe centerline. Moving upstream, the hydraulic grade line rises at a rate given by the energy gradient
$$
J = \lambda\,\frac{1}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{0.020}{0.15} \cdot 0.147 = 0.0196 ,
$$
where $v = \dfrac{0.030}{0.01767} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.147\,\mathrm{m}$. The midpoint is $100\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream of the outlet, so the pressure head there is $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = 3.00 + 0.0196 \cdot 100 = 4.96\,\mathrm{m}$ and the gauge pressure is $p = 48.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Subtracting the head loss would give $10.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Using the full pipe length would give $67.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
