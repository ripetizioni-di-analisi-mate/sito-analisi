A horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carrying $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water contains an orifice plate with a loss coefficient $\xi = 18$ based on pipe velocity. Neglecting wall shear in the length surrounding the plate, what force does the water exert on it?

- $0.917\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $0.0467\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $0.458\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $1.83\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Take a control volume extending from section $1$ upstream of the orifice plate to section $2$ downstream, where the flow has reattached to the wall. Apply the integral momentum equation along the pipe axis, positive downstream. The sections have equal areas and velocities, so the net axial momentum flux is zero. The weight acts vertically, and wall shear is neglected. The force of the plate on the fluid therefore balances the net pressure force at the two sections. In turn, the fluid pushes the plate downstream with force $F = (p_1 - p_2)\,A$.

The pipe is horizontal and its diameter is constant, so $p_1 - p_2 = \gamma\,\Delta H_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.040}{0.03142} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the velocity head is $0.0826\,\mathrm{m}$, and the head loss is $\Delta H_{\mathrm{loc}} = 18 \cdot 0.0826 = 1.487\,\mathrm{m}$. The force is therefore
$$
F = \gamma\,\Delta H_{\mathrm{loc}}\,A = 9810 \cdot 1.487 \cdot 0.03142 = 458\,\mathrm{N} = 0.458\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
Substituting density for specific weight would give $0.0467\,\mathrm{kN}$. Using $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ in place of $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ would double the result.
