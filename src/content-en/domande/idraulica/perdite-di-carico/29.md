Oil flows through a steel pipe in the laminar regime. What determines the friction factor?

- Only the relative roughness of the wall
- Only the Reynolds number
- The Reynolds number and relative roughness
- Only the kinematic viscosity of the oil

## Explanation

In laminar flow, Newton’s law of viscosity relates shear stress to the velocity gradient. The velocity profile and the energy gradient can therefore be derived analytically. Equating the energy gradients from the Hagen–Poiseuille and Darcy–Weisbach equations gives
$$
\lambda = \frac{64}{Re} ,
$$
which contains no roughness term because the roughness elements remain submerged in the layer of fluid adhering to the wall. The Reynolds number combines the effects of viscosity, velocity, and diameter, so viscosity alone does not determine $\lambda$.
