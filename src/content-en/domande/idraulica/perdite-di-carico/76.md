A concrete box culvert with an internal rectangular section $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ wide and $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ high runs full under pressure and carries $Q = 3.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ of water, as shown. Its roughness coefficient is $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. What is the energy gradient?

::schema{acqua="0,0 2,0 2,1 0,1" solidi="-0.3,-0.3 2.3,-0.3 2.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,1 2.3,1 2.3,1.3 -0.3,1.3;-0.3,0 0,0 0,1 -0.3,1;2,0 2.3,0 2.3,1 2,1" quote="0 -0.3,2 -0.3,2.00 m,0.45;2.3 0,2.3 1,1.00 m,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Cross section of the box culvert flowing full"}

- $0.00116$
- $0.000459$
- $0.00199$
- $0.000955$

## Explanation

The Gauckler–Strickler equation uses the hydraulic radius, so it also applies to a rectangular conduit flowing full. The box culvert is under pressure and all four sides are wetted. The cross-sectional area is $A = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the wetted perimeter is $C = 6.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and the hydraulic radius is $R = \dfrac{2.00}{6.00} = 0.333\,\mathrm{m}$. The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{3.0}{2.00} = 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving
$$
J = \frac{v^2}{k_s^2\,R^{\frac{4}{3}}} = \frac{1.50^2}{70^2 \cdot 0.333^{\frac{4}{3}}} = \frac{2.25}{4900 \cdot 0.231} = 0.00199 .
$$
Excluding the roof from the wetted perimeter, as for an open channel, would give $R = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and an energy gradient of $0.00116$. Using the exponent $\dfrac{2}{3}$ in place of $\dfrac{4}{3}$ would give $0.000955$.
