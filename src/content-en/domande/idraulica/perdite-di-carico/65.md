Water flows uniformly at mean velocity $v = 1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a constant-diameter pipe reach, exerting total tangential force $F = 2.40\,\mathrm{kN}$ on its wall. What power does the water dissipate in this reach?

- $2.00\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $1.73\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $2.88\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $0.294\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The total tangential force is the wall shear stress times the wetted wall area, $F = \tau_0\,C\,L$. Using $\tau_0 = \gamma R J$ with $R = \dfrac{A}{C}$ gives $F = \gamma\,A\,J\,L = \gamma\,A\,\Delta H$. The dissipated power equals the weight flow rate times the head loss, $P = \gamma\,Q\,\Delta H$. Since $Q = A\,v$, this gives
$$
P = \gamma\,A\,\Delta H\,v = F\,v = 2400 \cdot 1.20 = 2880\,\mathrm{W} = 2.88\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The result is independent of pipe diameter and inclination. Dividing the force by the velocity would give the erroneous result $2.00\,\mathrm{kW}$. Force divided by velocity does not have the dimensions of power.
