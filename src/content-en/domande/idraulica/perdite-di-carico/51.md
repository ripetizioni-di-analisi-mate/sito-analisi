A horizontal pipe of constant diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. At section $1$, a Pitot tube facing the flow reads $6.20\,\mathrm{m}$ of water above the centerline. At section $2$ downstream, a piezometer reads $5.10\,\mathrm{m}$ above the centerline, as shown. What is the head loss between the two sections?

::schema{nodi="A,0,1,-;B,12,1,-" condotte="A-B,0.5" linee="1.3,1 2,1 2,6.2;9,1.25 9,5.1" tratteggi="1.7,6.2 2.3,6.2;8.7,5.1 9.3,5.1" frecce="4.6,1,6.0,1,v" etichette="2,6.6,Pitot;9,5.5,piezometer;2.3,0.2,1;9,0.2,2" quote="2.9 1,2.9 6.2,6.20 m,-0.1;9.9 1,9.9 5.1,5.10 m,-0.1" riferimento=0 titolo="Pitot tube at section $1$ and piezometer at section $2$"}

- $1.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.23\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.971\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.842\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The Pitot tube brings the fluid to rest at its inlet. Its water column therefore measures the pressure head plus the velocity head, giving the total head relative to the pipe centerline, $H_1 = 6.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The piezometer measures only the pressure head, so the velocity head must be added to its reading to obtain the total head at section $2$. The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.050}{0.03142} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.129\,\mathrm{m}$, giving
$$
\Delta H = H_1 - H_2 = 6.20 - (5.10 + 0.129) = 0.971\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Simply subtracting the two readings gives $1.10\,\mathrm{m}$. That calculation treats the total head at section $1$ as a piezometric head.
