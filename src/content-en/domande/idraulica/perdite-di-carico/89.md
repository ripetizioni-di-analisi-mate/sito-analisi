A pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $45\,\mathrm{m}$, and friction factor $\lambda = 0.025$ carries $Q = 10\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. It has six bends and two valves. The catalog gives equivalent lengths in pipe diameters: $30\,D$ for each bend and $10\,D$ for each valve. What is the total head loss?

- $0.929\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.06\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.69\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.34\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The equivalent length of the fittings is $(6 \cdot 30 + 2 \cdot 10)\,D = 200\,D = 20.0\,\mathrm{m}$, so the head loss is the same as in a straight pipe of length $45 + 20 = 65\,\mathrm{m}$. The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.010}{0.007854} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.0826\,\mathrm{m}$. The Darcy–Weisbach equation therefore gives
$$
\Delta H = \lambda\,\frac{L + L_{\mathrm{eq}}}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.025 \cdot \frac{65}{0.10} \cdot 0.0826 = 1.34\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
An equivalent length of $30\,D$ corresponds to a loss coefficient $\xi = 30\,\lambda = 0.75$ for each bend. Omitting the fittings would give $0.929\,\mathrm{m}$. Reading $30$ and $10$ as lengths in meters rather than as numbers of pipe diameters would give $5.06\,\mathrm{m}$.
