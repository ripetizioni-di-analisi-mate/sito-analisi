A pump draws water from a tank through a pipe of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L = 10\,\mathrm{m}$, and friction factor $\lambda = 0.022$, with a foot valve and strainer ($\xi = 3.0$) and a bend ($\xi = 0.3$), as shown. The discharge is $Q = 30\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The gauge pressure at the pump inlet must remain above $-7.0\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. What is the maximum elevation $z_a$ of the pump centerline above the tank surface?

::schema{nodi="A,1.5,0.5,-;B,1.5,5,-;C,4,5,-" condotte="A-B-C,0.35" acqua="0,0 3,0 3,2 0,2" pelo="0,3,2," linee="0,2.6 0,0 3,0 3,2.6" pompa="4.4,5,P" tratteggi="3,2 5.6,2;4.8,5 5.6,5" etichette="1.5,-0.45,foot valve;0.8,4.6,bend" quote="5.6 2,5.6 5,z_a,-0.1" riferimento=0 titolo="Pump suction pipe"}

- $7.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.85\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.48\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Apply Bernoulli’s equation with head loss from the tank free surface, taken as the elevation datum, to the pump inlet. Both velocity and gauge pressure are zero at the free surface, so
$$
0 = z_a + \frac{p}{\gamma} + \left( 1 + \lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \sum_i \xi_i \right)\frac{v^2}{2g} .
$$
The pipe velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.030}{0.01767} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.147\,\mathrm{m}$. The velocity head and the head losses therefore total $(1 + 1.47 + 3.3) \cdot 0.147 = 0.847\,\mathrm{m}$. Setting $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = -7.0\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $z_a = 7.0 - 0.847 = 6.15\,\mathrm{m}$. The velocity head and the head losses use up part of the allowable pressure drop, reducing the maximum suction lift. Adding them would give $7.85\,\mathrm{m}$.
