Oil of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ flows through a pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$. Above what discharge does the flow leave the laminar range?

- $1.57\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $6.28\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $12.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $25.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Pipe flow remains laminar while the Reynolds number is below $2000$. The characteristic length in the Reynolds number is the pipe diameter. Setting $Re = \dfrac{v D}{\nu} = 2000$ gives the limiting mean velocity
$$
v = \frac{2000\,\nu}{D} = \frac{2000 \cdot 4 \cdot 10^{-5}}{0.100} = 0.800\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The cross-sectional area is $A = 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the corresponding discharge is
$$
Q = v\,A = 0.800 \cdot 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3} = 6.28 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $6.28\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Above this discharge, the flow enters the transition regime. Using $Re = 4000$, the threshold for turbulent flow, would give $12.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
