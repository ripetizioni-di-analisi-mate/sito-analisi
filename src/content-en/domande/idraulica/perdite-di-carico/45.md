A transmission main has diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L = 4000\,\mathrm{m}$, friction factor $\lambda = 0.020$, and fittings with $\sum \xi_i = 5.0$. Which statement about minor losses is correct?

- They account for about half the total loss
- They account for about $20\,\%$ of the total loss
- They account for about $2\,\%$ of the total loss and can therefore be neglected
- They exceed the friction loss because there are so many fittings

## Explanation

Both friction loss and minor loss are proportional to the same velocity head, so their relative magnitudes depend on the dimensionless coefficients $\lambda\dfrac{L}{D}$ and $\sum \xi_i$. The first is
$$
\lambda\,\frac{L}{D} = 0.020 \cdot \frac{4000}{0.30} = 267 ,
$$
and the second is $5.0$. The fraction of the total due to minor losses is therefore $\dfrac{5.0}{5.0 + 267} = 0.018$, or $1.8\,\%$. In a pipe more than ten thousand diameters long, the contribution of minor losses is smaller than the uncertainty associated with wall roughness. The **long-pipeline approximation** therefore neglects minor losses and velocity heads.
