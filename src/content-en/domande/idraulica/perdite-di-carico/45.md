A pipeline of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ includes a bend with loss coefficient $\xi_1 = 0.40$ and, in a reach reduced to diameter $100\,\mathrm{mm}$, a valve with coefficient $\xi_2 = 2.0$ referred to the velocity in the reduced reach. What single coefficient, referred to the velocity in the $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ reach, gives the sum of the two minor losses?

- $2.40$
- $8.40$
- $32.4$
- $0.525$

## Explanation

By the continuity equation the velocities are inversely proportional to the areas, so in the reduced reach the velocity is $v_2 = v_1\left(\dfrac{200}{100}\right)^2 = 4\,v_1$. The loss in the valve is then
$$
\xi_2\,\frac{v_2^2}{2g} = 2.0 \cdot 16\,\frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 32\,\frac{v_1^2}{2g} .
$$
Adding the loss in the bend, $0.40\,\dfrac{v_1^2}{2g}$, gives the overall coefficient referred to $v_1$, that is, $\xi = 0.40 + 32 = 32.4$. Two coefficients referred to different velocities cannot be added directly, so $2.40$ does not represent the total loss. The value $8.40$ uses the velocity ratio instead of its square, and $0.525$ divides by $16$ instead of multiplying.
