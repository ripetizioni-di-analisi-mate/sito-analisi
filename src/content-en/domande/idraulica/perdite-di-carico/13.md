The pipe shown, of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.023$, includes a sharp-edged entrance ($\xi = 0.5$), two bends ($\xi = 0.3$ each), a check valve ($\xi = 2.0$), and an exit ($\xi = 1$). What is the total equivalent length of the fittings?

- $26.7\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.629\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

All coefficients correspond to the same velocity, so they can be added:
$$
\sum_i \xi_i = 0.5 + 0.3 + 0.3 + 2.0 + 1 = 4.10 .
$$
Equate the minor loss to the friction loss along an equivalent length of pipe: $\xi\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \lambda\dfrac{L_{\mathrm{eq}}}{D}\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, giving $L_{\mathrm{eq}} = \xi\dfrac{D}{\lambda}$. Adding the contributions from the fittings gives
$$
L_{\mathrm{eq}} = 4.10 \cdot \frac{0.15}{0.023} = 26.7\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The check valve contributes the most, at $13.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Omitting the exit would give $20.2\,\mathrm{m}$.
