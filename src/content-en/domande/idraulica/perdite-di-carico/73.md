A cast-iron pipe of relative roughness $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 0.0010$ operates in the transitionally rough regime at $Re = 1.0 \cdot 10^5$. If discharge and Reynolds number increase, how does the friction factor change?

- It increases because the flow velocity increases
- It decreases toward the constant value for fully rough turbulent flow
- It stays constant because the relative roughness stays the same
- It decreases as $\dfrac{64}{Re}$

## Explanation

In the transitionally rough turbulent regime, the Darcy friction factor depends on both the Reynolds number and the relative roughness. On the Moody diagram, the curve for a fixed relative roughness slopes downward as $Re$ increases and eventually levels off. The Colebrook–White equation gives $\lambda = 0.0222$ at $Re = 10^5$, then $0.0199$ at $Re = 10^6$ and $0.0197$ at $Re = 10^7$. These values approach $0.0196$, the value given by the Prandtl–Nikuradse relation for $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 0.0010$. Thus, the friction factor **decreases** toward its fully rough value. The relation $\dfrac{64}{Re}$ applies only to laminar flow.
