Where is energy dissipated when a pressurized pipe contracts abruptly?

- Between the upstream section and the vena contracta, where the flow accelerates
- At the vena contracta, where the velocity is greatest
- Along the downstream pipe, in proportion to its length
- In the expansion between the vena contracta and the downstream section

## Explanation

As the flow enters the smaller pipe, it separates at the sharp edge and contracts to section $c$, the vena contracta, of area $A_c = C_c A_2$. Upstream of the vena contracta, the flow accelerates along converging streamlines and dissipates little energy. From the vena contracta to the section where the flow reattaches to the wall, the jet expands abruptly and recirculating eddies dissipate energy. The Borda–Carnot relation therefore applies between these two sections, giving
$$
\Delta H = \frac{(v_c - v_2)^2}{2g} = \left( \frac{1}{C_c} - 1 \right)^2 \frac{v_2^2}{2g} .
$$
Friction loss along the downstream pipe is calculated separately with the Darcy–Weisbach equation.
