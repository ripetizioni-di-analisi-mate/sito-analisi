A pipe of diameter $D = 120\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 400\,\mathrm{m}$ carries oil in laminar flow with $\rho = 880\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $\nu = 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. The measured head loss between the ends is $\Delta H = 7.00\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the discharge?

- $9.93\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $17.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $140\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $8.74\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The energy gradient is $J = \dfrac{\Delta H}{L} = \dfrac{7.00}{400} = 0.0175$. The oil has dynamic viscosity $\mu = \rho\,\nu = 880 \cdot 10^{-4} = 0.0880\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ and specific weight $\gamma = \rho\,g = 8630\,\mathrm{N/m^3}$. The **Hagen–Poiseuille law** gives
$$
Q = \frac{\pi\,\gamma\,J\,D^4}{128\,\mu} = \frac{\pi \cdot 8630 \cdot 0.0175 \cdot 0.120^4}{128 \cdot 0.0880} = 8.74 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $8.74\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The resulting mean velocity is $0.773\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the Reynolds number is $927$, below $2000$. The laminar-flow assumption is therefore valid. Using the specific weight of water in place of that of the oil would give $9.93\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
