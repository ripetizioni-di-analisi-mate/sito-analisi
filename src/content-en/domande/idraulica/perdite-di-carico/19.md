In uniform flow through a full circular pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, the wall shear stress is $\tau_0 = 7.20\,\mathrm{Pa}$. What is the shear stress on the surface of a coaxial cylinder of radius $r = 25\,\mathrm{mm}$?

- $1.80\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $0.900\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.60\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $7.20\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The integral momentum equation applied to a cylinder of radius $r$ concentric with the pipe gives $\tau(r) = \gamma\dfrac{r}{2}J$. The shear stress therefore increases in proportion to the distance from the centerline. Expressing it in terms of its value at the wall, where $r = \dfrac{D}{2}$, gives
$$
\tau(r) = \tau_0\,\frac{2r}{D} = 7.20 \cdot \frac{2 \cdot 0.025}{0.200} = 1.80\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The specified radius is one quarter of the pipe radius, so the shear stress is one quarter of the wall shear stress. This distribution holds for both laminar and turbulent flow because its derivation requires no relation between shear stress and velocity.
