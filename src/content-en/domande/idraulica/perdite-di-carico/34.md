In the Colebrook–White equation, $Re = 3.0 \cdot 10^5$ and $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 0.0010$. Starting with $\lambda = 0.0250$, what friction factor does the first iteration give?

- $0.0250$
- $0.00387$
- $0.0205$
- $0.0137$

## Explanation

Substitute the initial estimate into the right-hand side of the equation. The first term is
$$
\frac{2.51}{Re\,\sqrt{\lambda}} = \frac{2.51}{3.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 0.158} = 5.29 \cdot 10^{-5} ,
$$
and the second is $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3.71 D} = 2.70 \cdot 10^{-4}$. Their sum is therefore $3.22 \cdot 10^{-4}$, giving
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = -2\,\log_{10}\!\left( 3.22 \cdot 10^{-4} \right) = 6.98 ,
$$
and hence $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{6.98^2} = 0.0205$. Further iterations give $0.0206$, so the first iteration is already close to the final value. Dropping the roughness term would treat the pipe as hydraulically smooth and give $0.0137$.
