A straight steel pipe of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries water at a discharge of $Q = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and a temperature of $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The kinematic viscosity is $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. What is the Reynolds number?

- $8.91 \cdot 10^4$
- $1.78 \cdot 10^5$
- $3.57 \cdot 10^5$
- $3.57 \cdot 10^8$

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.070}{0.0491} = 1.43\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
For flow in a circular pipe, the characteristic length in the Reynolds number is the diameter. Hence
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu} = \frac{1.43 \cdot 0.25}{10^{-6}} = 3.57 \cdot 10^5 .
$$
The value is well above $4000$, so the flow is **turbulent**. Using the pipe radius in place of the diameter would give $1.78 \cdot 10^5$. Using the hydraulic radius $R = \dfrac{D}{4}$ would give $8.91 \cdot 10^4$.
