A siphon of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ connects two reservoirs and carries $Q = 30\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water, as shown. Its crest $V$ is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the upstream free surface. From the sharp-edged entrance to the crest, the length is $20\,\mathrm{m}$ and the friction factor is $\lambda = 0.025$. The entrance loss coefficient is $\xi = 0.5$. What is the gauge pressure at the crest?

- $-29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-30.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-25.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-36.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Apply Bernoulli’s equation with head loss from the upstream free surface, taken as the elevation datum, to the summit of the siphon. Both the velocity and the gauge pressure are zero at the free surface, so
$$
0 = 3.00 + \frac{p_V}{\gamma} + \frac{v^2}{2g} + \left( \xi + \lambda\,\frac{L}{D} \right)\frac{v^2}{2g} .
$$
The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.030}{0.01767} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the velocity head is $0.147\,\mathrm{m}$, and the head losses total $\left( 0.5 + 0.025 \cdot \dfrac{20}{0.15} \right) \cdot 0.147 = 3.83 \cdot 0.147 = 0.563\,\mathrm{m}$. It follows that $\dfrac{p_V}{\gamma} = -3.00 - 0.147 - 0.563 = -3.71\,\mathrm{m}$ and $p_V = -36.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The magnitude of the negative pressure head at the summit exceeds the elevation rise because the water has a finite velocity head there and has already lost head along the way.
