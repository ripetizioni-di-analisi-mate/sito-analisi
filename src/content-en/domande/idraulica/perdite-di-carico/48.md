A pipe of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 35\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. What is the vertical distance between the energy grade line and hydraulic grade line?

- $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0125\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Total head exceeds piezometric head by the velocity head, since $H = h + \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. The vertical distance between the energy grade line and the hydraulic grade line is therefore $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.15^2}{4} = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and the mean velocity is
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.035}{0.01767} = 1.98\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The vertical distance is therefore $\dfrac{1.98^2}{19.62} = 0.200\,\mathrm{m}$. Using $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ would double the result to $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculating the area as $\pi D^2$ would give $0.0125\,\mathrm{m}$.
