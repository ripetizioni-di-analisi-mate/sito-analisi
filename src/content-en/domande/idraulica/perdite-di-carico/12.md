A straight pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 800\,\mathrm{m}$ carries discharge $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The measured head loss is $\Delta H = 9.60\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What is its Gauckler–Strickler coefficient?

::schema{nodi="A,0,1,-;B,12,1,-" condotte="A-B,0.45" linee="1.5,1.22 1.5,5.2;10.5,1.22 10.5,3.4" piezometrica="1.5,5.2 10.5,3.4" tratteggi="1.5,5.2 11.3,5.2" etichette="0.2,5.4,piezometers;5.4,0.3,D = 200 mm · Q = 40 L/s" quote="1.5 0,10.5 0,L = 800 m,1.4;11.3 3.4,11.3 5.2,ΔH = 9.60 m,0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Head loss measured over $800\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $3.03\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$
- $53.9\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$
- $85.6\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$
- $31.5\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.040}{0.0314} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the hydraulic radius is $R = \dfrac{D}{4} = 0.0500\,\mathrm{m}$ and the measured energy gradient is
$$
J = \frac{\Delta H}{L} = \frac{9.60}{800} = 0.0120 .
$$
Solving the **Gauckler–Strickler equation** $v = k_s R^{\frac{2}{3}} J^{\frac{1}{2}}$ gives
$$
k_s = \frac{v}{R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,J^{\frac{1}{2}}} = \frac{1.27}{0.0500^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.0120^{\frac{1}{2}}} = 85.6\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s} .
$$
The value is typical of a pipe in good condition. Using the geometric radius instead of the hydraulic radius would give $53.9\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. Using head loss instead of energy gradient would give $3.03\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$.
