A closed cooling loop consists of a pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and total length $L = 60\,\mathrm{m}$ with friction factor $\lambda = 0.022$. From the pump, it rises $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$ to a rooftop unit and then returns to the pump. The fittings, including the heat exchanger, have $\sum \xi_i = 6.0$. What increase in total head must the pump supply to circulate $Q = 12\,\mathrm{L/s}$?

- $2.28\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.57\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.57\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Follow the circuit from the pump discharge around the loop to the pump suction. The two sections are at the same location and have the same elevation and velocity, so their total heads differ only by the head lost around the circuit. The $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rise contributes no net elevation change because the water descends the same distance on its return. The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.012}{0.007854} = 1.53\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.119\,\mathrm{m}$, so the required pump head is
$$
H_p = \left( \lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \sum_i \xi_i \right)\frac{v^2}{2g} = (13.2 + 6.0) \cdot 0.119 = 2.28\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Adding the elevation rise would give $10.3\,\mathrm{m}$. Omitting minor losses would give $1.57\,\mathrm{m}$.
