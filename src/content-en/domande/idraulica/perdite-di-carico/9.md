Two piezometers on a steel pipe of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.0195$ are $L = 300\,\mathrm{m}$ apart and show an elevation difference $\Delta h = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as illustrated. What is the discharge?

::schema{nodi="A,0,1,-;B,12,1,-" condotte="A-B,0.5" linee="2,1.25 2,5.0;10,1.25 10,4.2" piezometrica="2,5.0 10,4.2" tratteggi="2,5.0 10.8,5.0" etichette="0.6,5.2,piezometers;5.4,0.3,D = 250 mm · λ = 0.0195" quote="2 0,10 0,L = 300 m,1.4;10.8 4.2,10.8 5.0,Δh = 3.00 m,0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Piezometric head difference over $300\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $77.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $55.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $38.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $123\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The pipe has constant diameter, so the velocity heads at the two sections are equal. The difference between the piezometer levels therefore equals the head loss, giving an energy gradient of
$$
J = \frac{\Delta h}{L} = \frac{3.00}{300} = 0.0100 .
$$
Solving the **Darcy–Weisbach equation** for velocity gives
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\,g\,D\,J}{\lambda}} = \sqrt{\frac{19.62 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 0.0100}{0.0195}} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
and multiplying by the area $A = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$ gives $Q = 0.0779\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $77.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Writing the velocity head as $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ would give $55.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
