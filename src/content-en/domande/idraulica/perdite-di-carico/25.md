A cast-iron pipe operates in fully rough turbulent flow. What determines its friction factor?

- Only the relative roughness $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D}$
- Only the Reynolds number
- The Reynolds number and relative roughness
- Only the mean flow velocity

## Explanation

In the fully rough turbulent regime, the term $\dfrac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\lambda}}$ in the Colebrook–White equation is negligible compared with $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3.71 D}$. The equation therefore reduces to the **Prandtl–Nikuradse** relation
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = 2\,\log_{10}\!\left( \frac{3.71\,D}{\varepsilon} \right) ,
$$
in which the Reynolds number no longer appears. Consequently, $\lambda$ remains constant as the discharge varies, and the energy gradient is proportional to the square of the velocity. The Chézy and Gauckler–Strickler power-law formulas apply in this regime, with roughness coefficients determined by the pipe wall.
