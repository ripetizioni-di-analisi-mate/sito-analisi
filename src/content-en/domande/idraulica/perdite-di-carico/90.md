An elevated tank supplies a user through a pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L = 200\,\mathrm{m}$, and friction factor $\lambda = 0.025$, with fittings having $\sum \xi_i = 3.0$. At the terminal section the user requires $Q = 15\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at gauge pressure $150\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the minimum elevation difference between the tank surface and that section?

- $15.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $25.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $35.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Applying Bernoulli’s equation with head loss from the reservoir free surface to the terminal section gives
$$
\Delta z = \frac{p}{\gamma} + \frac{v^2}{2g} + \left( \lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \sum_i \xi_i \right)\frac{v^2}{2g} ,
$$
because the velocity and gauge pressure are zero at the free surface. The mean velocity in the pipe is $v = \dfrac{0.015}{0.007854} = 1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.186\,\mathrm{m}$, so the head losses total $(50 + 3.0) \cdot 0.186 = 9.85\,\mathrm{m}$. The specified outlet pressure requires a pressure head of $\dfrac{150\,000}{9810} = 15.3\,\mathrm{m}$. The minimum elevation difference is therefore $\Delta z = 15.3 + 0.186 + 9.85 = 25.3\,\mathrm{m}$. Without losses, $15.5\,\mathrm{m}$ would suffice. The value $10.0\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the required pressure head.
