In a vertical pipe of diameter $D = 8.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, oil of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 1.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ flows downward under gravity alone. The pressure is the same at every section. Assuming laminar flow, what is the mean velocity?

- $0.262\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.131\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.0327\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.0654\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Since the diameter and pressure are constant, the head loss between two sections equals their elevation difference. The pipe is vertical, so head decreases by one meter for every meter of pipe and $J = 1$. Solving the Hagen–Poiseuille relation $J = \dfrac{32\,\nu\,v}{g\,D^2}$ for the mean velocity gives
$$
v = \frac{g\,D^2}{32\,\nu} = \frac{9.81 \cdot (0.0080)^2}{32 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^{-4}} = 0.131\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The Reynolds number is $\dfrac{0.131 \cdot 0.0080}{1.5 \cdot 10^{-4}} = 7$, confirming the assumption of **laminar flow**. The value $0.262\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the centerline velocity.
