A full circular pipe of diameter $D = 800\,\mathrm{mm}$ and a rectangular channel of width $b = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and water depth $h = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ have concrete walls with the same roughness coefficient $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. Both carry uniform flow with the same energy gradient $J = 0.0010$. Which statement about their mean velocities according to Gauckler–Strickler is correct?

- The velocity is higher in the pipe because the full section has a larger area
- The velocity is higher in the channel because the free surface is excluded from the wetted perimeter
- The velocity in the pipe is $1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ because its hydraulic radius is $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- Both velocities equal $0.757\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The Gauckler–Strickler equation, $v = k_s R^{\frac{2}{3}} J^{\frac{1}{2}}$, relates mean velocity to roughness, hydraulic radius, and energy gradient. For the same $k_s$ and $J$, two flows with equal hydraulic radii therefore have the same mean velocity. For the full circular pipe, $R = \dfrac{D}{4} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The flow area in the channel is $A = 1.20 \cdot 0.30 = 0.36\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The wetted perimeter includes the bed and both sidewalls, giving $C = 1.20 + 2 \cdot 0.30 = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and hence $R = \dfrac{0.36}{1.80} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The mean velocities are therefore **equal**, with
$$
v = 70 \cdot 0.20^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.0010^{\frac{1}{2}} = 70 \cdot 0.342 \cdot 0.0316 = 0.757\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Using the pipe radius in place of the hydraulic radius would give $1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
