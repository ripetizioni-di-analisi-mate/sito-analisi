The horizontal pipe shown expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the minor-loss coefficient based on the upstream velocity?

::schema{nodi="A,0,2,-;B,5,2,-;C,12,2,-" condotte="A-B,0.9;B-C,1.2" frecce="1.6,2,3.0,2,v_1;8.0,2,9.4,2,v_2" etichette="5.0,3.0,1;9.0,3.0,2;2.2,1.0,D_1 = 150 mm;9.6,0.85,D_2 = 200 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Sudden expansion from $150$ to $200\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

- $0.191$
- $0.0625$
- $0.438$
- $0.605$

## Explanation

The Borda–Carnot head loss is $\Delta H = \dfrac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g}$, and continuity gives $v_2 = v_1\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}$. Factoring out the upstream velocity head gives
$$
\Delta H = \left( 1 - \frac{A_1}{A_2} \right)^2 \frac{v_1^2}{2g} ,
\qquad
\xi = \left( 1 - \frac{A_1}{A_2} \right)^2 .
$$
The area ratio is the square of the diameter ratio. Hence $\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = \left( \dfrac{150}{200} \right)^2 = 0.5625$ and
$$
\xi = (1 - 0.5625)^2 = 0.191 .
$$
Substituting the diameter ratio for the area ratio would give $0.0625$. Using $1 - \dfrac{A_1}{A_2}$ without squaring it would give $0.438$.
