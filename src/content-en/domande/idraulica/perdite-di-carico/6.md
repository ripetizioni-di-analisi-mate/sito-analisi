The pipe shown has diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L = 80\,\mathrm{m}$, water discharge $Q = 28\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and friction factor $\lambda = 0.0194$. What is the friction head loss?

- $0.282\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.61\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.32\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.65\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.028}{0.01767} = 1.58\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving a velocity head of $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.128\,\mathrm{m}$. The **Darcy–Weisbach equation** gives the friction loss:
$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{cont}} = \lambda\,\frac{L}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.0194 \cdot \frac{80}{0.15} \cdot 0.128 = 1.32\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The ratio $\dfrac{L}{D}$ is $533$, so the factor $\lambda\dfrac{L}{D}$ is $10.3$. Calculate entrance, bend, gate-valve and exit losses separately, each with its own coefficient $\xi$, and add them to the friction loss to obtain the total head loss.
