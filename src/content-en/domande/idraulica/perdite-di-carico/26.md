Oil of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ flows through a pipe of diameter $D = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$ at mean velocity $v = 0.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the energy gradient?

- $0.00261$
- $0.174$
- $0.0435$
- $0.0870$

## Explanation

The Reynolds number is
$$
Re = \frac{0.60 \cdot 0.060}{8 \cdot 10^{-5}} = 450 ,
$$
which is below $2000$. The flow is therefore **laminar**, and the Hagen–Poiseuille law gives the energy gradient as
$$
J = \frac{32\,\nu\,v}{g\,D^2} = \frac{32 \cdot 8 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 0.60}{9.81 \cdot 0.060^2} = 0.0435 .
$$
The Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the same value with $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re} = 0.142$. Using the radius in place of the diameter would give $0.174$, four times the correct result.
