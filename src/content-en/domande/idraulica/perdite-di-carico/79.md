A horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ leaves a large reservoir through a sharp-edged entrance ($\xi = 0.5$). Its centerline is $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. The discharge is $Q = 45\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Neglecting friction loss up to the section where the flow reattaches to the wall, what is the gauge pressure on the centerline at that section?

- $4.95\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $6.57\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $8.19\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Apply Bernoulli’s equation with head loss from the reservoir free surface, where both velocity and gauge pressure are zero, to the section where the flow has reattached. Take the pipe centerline as the elevation datum. The mean velocity in the pipe is $v = \dfrac{0.045}{0.01767} = 2.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.331\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
1.00 = \frac{p}{\gamma} + \frac{v^2}{2g} + 0.5\,\frac{v^2}{2g}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{p}{\gamma} = 1.00 - 1.5 \cdot 0.331 = 0.504\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The gauge pressure is therefore $p = 9810 \cdot 0.504 = 4.95\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the hydrostatic pressure that would apply if the water were at rest. Omitting the entrance loss would give $6.57\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
