Sections $1$ and $2$ of a constant-diameter pipeline are $80\,\mathrm{m}$ apart along the centerline, with section $2$ higher by $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The sections are connected to a differential mercury manometer with $\rho_{\mathrm{Hg}} = 13\,600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, as shown. Water flows from $1$ toward $2$ and the mercury menisci differ in elevation by $\Delta m = 0.180\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the head loss between the two sections?

::schema{nodi="A,0,3,-;B,10,4.5,-;P,2,3.3,-;S,8,4.2,-" condotte="A-B,0.5" linee="2,3.05 2,0.4 8,0.4 8,3.95" tratteggi="1.6,0.9 2.4,0.9;7.6,1.6 8.4,1.6" frecce="3.6,4.1,5.2,4.34,v" etichette="2,4.0,1;8,4.9,2;4.9,0.05,mercury" quote="8.9 0.9,8.9 1.6,Δm = 0.180 m,-0.1" riferimento=0 titolo="Differential mercury manometer connected to two sections of an inclined pipe"}

- $0.180\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.268\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pressures in the two manometer legs are equal at the elevation of the lower mercury meniscus. Moving down from section $1$ to that elevation passes through water alone. From section $2$ the path passes through water followed by a mercury column of height $\Delta m$. Hence
$$
p_1 + \gamma z_1 = p_2 + \gamma z_2 + (\gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}} - \gamma)\,\Delta m .
$$
Dividing by $\gamma$ gives the difference in piezometric head, $h_1 - h_2 = \left( \dfrac{\gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}}}{\gamma} - 1 \right)\Delta m = 12.6 \cdot 0.180 = 2.27\,\mathrm{m}$. The section elevations do not appear explicitly in this result. Since the pipe diameter is constant, the velocity heads are equal and the drop in piezometric head is the head loss. Thus $\Delta H = 2.27\,\mathrm{m}$. The manometer reading already accounts for the $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ elevation difference between the sections, so no elevation correction is needed.
