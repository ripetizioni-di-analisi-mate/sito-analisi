A pipeline connects two constant-level reservoirs and has a gate valve halfway along it. If the valve is partially closed, what happens to the pressure immediately upstream of it?

- It increases because the discharge drops, reducing both the upstream head loss and the velocity head
- It decreases because the gate valve introduces an additional head loss
- It stays the same because the upstream reservoir level is unchanged
- It decreases because the velocity increases through the restricted gate-valve opening

## Explanation

Partially closing the gate valve increases its loss coefficient $\xi$. The available head between the reservoirs is fixed, so the discharge and pipe velocity $v$ decrease. Apply Bernoulli’s equation with head loss between the upstream free surface, at elevation $z_m$, and the section immediately upstream of the valve, at elevation $z$. This gives
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = z_m - z - \left( 1 + \xi_{\mathrm{imb}} + \lambda\,\frac{L_1}{D} \right)\frac{v^2}{2g} ,
$$
where $L_1$ is the upstream pipe length. The valve loss occurs downstream of this section and therefore lies outside the energy balance. The term subtracted from the elevation difference decreases with $v^2$, so the pressure **increases**. With the valve fully closed, the velocity is zero and the pressure reaches its hydrostatic value $\gamma\,(z_m - z)$.
