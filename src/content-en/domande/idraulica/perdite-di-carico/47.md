In a constant-diameter pipe, section $A$ is at elevation $z_A = 0$ and has gauge pressure $p_A = 250\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Section $B$ is at elevation $z_B = 20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and has gauge pressure $p_B = 40\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In which direction does the water flow, and what is the head loss?

- From $A$ toward $B$, with $\Delta H = 1.41\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $B$ toward $A$, with $\Delta H = 1.41\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $A$ toward $B$, with $\Delta H = 21.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $B$ toward $A$, with $\Delta H = 20.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pipe has a constant diameter, so the velocity heads are equal and the difference in total head equals the difference in piezometric head. The piezometric heads are
$$
h_A = 0 + \frac{250\,000}{9810} = 25.48\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_B = 20.0 + \frac{40\,000}{9810} = 24.08\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Head decreases in the direction of flow, so water flows from the section with higher head to the one with lower head, **from $A$ to $B$**. Using the unrounded heads gives a head loss of $\Delta H = 25.48 - 24.08 = 1.41\,\mathrm{m}$. Comparing pressures alone would neglect the $20\,\mathrm{m}$ difference in elevation and give $21.4\,\mathrm{m}$. Comparing elevations alone would suggest that water flows downhill from $B$ to $A$.
