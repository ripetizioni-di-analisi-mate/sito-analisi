A pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water and discharges into a large reservoir. What is the exit head loss?

- $0.0930\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.186\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0465\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.372\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Discharge into a reservoir is a sudden expansion in which the downstream area is so large that the velocity in the reservoir is negligible. Setting $v_2 = 0$ in the Borda–Carnot equation therefore gives $\xi = 1$. The entire velocity head of the pipe flow is dissipated. The mean velocity in the pipe is $v = \dfrac{0.060}{0.0314} = 1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the exit loss is
$$
\Delta H = 1 \cdot \frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.91^2}{19.62} = 0.186\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The coefficient $0.5$, which would give $0.0930\,\mathrm{m}$, applies to a sharp-edged entrance, where water flows from the reservoir into the pipe.
