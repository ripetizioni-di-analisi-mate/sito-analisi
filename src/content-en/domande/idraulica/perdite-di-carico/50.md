A transmission main carries $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water; the head loss between its ends is $\Delta H = 15.0\,\mathrm{m}$. How much power is dissipated by flow resistance?

- $17.7\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $1.80\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $0.0177\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $1.77 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

Head loss is the energy dissipated per unit weight of fluid passing through the pipe. The weight that passes per unit time is $\gamma Q$, so the power dissipated is
$$
P = \gamma\,Q\,\Delta H = 9810 \cdot 0.120 \cdot 15.0 = 17\,660\,\mathrm{W} = 17.7\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
Substituting density for specific weight would give $1.80\,\mathrm{kW}$. Leaving the discharge in liters per second would give a value a thousand times too large.
