Oil flows in the laminar regime through a pipe. With the same energy gradient and fluid, how does the discharge change if the diameter is halved?

- It falls to one sixteenth of its original value
- It falls to one quarter of its original value
- It is halved
- It falls to one thirty-second of its original value

## Explanation

In laminar flow, the **Hagen–Poiseuille** law gives
$$
Q = \frac{\pi\,\gamma\,J\,D^4}{128\,\mu} ,
$$
so for a specified fluid and energy gradient the discharge is proportional to the fourth power of the diameter. Halving the diameter reduces the discharge by a factor of $2^4 = 16$. The dependence on diameter is weaker in turbulent flow, where discharge increases approximately as the square root of the energy gradient and as the diameter raised to the power $\dfrac{5}{2}$.
