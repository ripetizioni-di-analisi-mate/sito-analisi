The pipe shown carries $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. The first reach has diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, a sharp-edged entrance ($\xi = 0.5$), and a bend ($\xi = 0.3$). A reducer follows, taking the diameter to $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\xi = 0.15$ based on downstream velocity. The second reach has an open gate valve ($\xi = 0.2$) and an exit to the downstream reservoir ($\xi = 1$). What is the total minor loss?

- $0.561\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.178\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.419\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.158\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Each loss coefficient $\xi$ multiplies the velocity head at its reference section. Add the losses for fittings in the first pipe section using $v_1$ and those referenced to the second section using $v_2$. The mean velocities are $v_1 = \dfrac{0.040}{0.03142} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = \dfrac{0.040}{0.01767} = 2.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with corresponding velocity heads of $0.0826\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.261\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus
$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{loc}} = (0.5 + 0.3) \cdot 0.0826 + (0.15 + 0.2 + 1) \cdot 0.261 = 0.066 + 0.353 = 0.419\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Adding all the coefficients and using a single velocity head would give $0.561\,\mathrm{m}$ with $v_2$ or $0.178\,\mathrm{m}$ with $v_1$.
