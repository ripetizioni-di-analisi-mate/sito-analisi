Oil flows uniformly in the laminar regime in one pipe and water flows uniformly in the turbulent regime in another. For which flows can the wall shear stress be calculated using $\tau_0 = \gamma R J$?

- Only for oil, because the relation requires Newton's law of viscosity
- Only for water, because in laminar flow shear stress depends on viscosity
- For both, because the relation follows from the force balance on a segment of uniform flow
- For neither, because the relation applies only to horizontal pipes

## Explanation

The relation $\tau_0 = \gamma R J$ follows from the axial component of the integral momentum equation for a length of pipe in uniform flow. The incoming and outgoing momentum fluxes are equal, so the pressure forces at the two sections, the axial component of the fluid’s weight, and the wall force sum to zero. The derivation requires no relation between shear stress and velocity, so it applies to **both** flows, whether laminar or turbulent. It also applies to an inclined pipe because it includes the axial component of the fluid’s weight. The flow regime determines how $\tau_0$ is related to the mean velocity through the value of $\lambda$.
