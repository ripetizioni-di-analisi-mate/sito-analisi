A full circular pipe of diameter $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ has Chézy coefficient $\chi = 55\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}/s}$ and carries water with energy gradient $J = 0.0050$. What is the discharge?

- $155\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $219\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $309\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $3.46\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The **Chézy** equation gives the mean velocity as $v = \chi\sqrt{R\,J}$, where $R$ is the hydraulic radius. For a circular pipe flowing full, $R = \dfrac{D}{4} = 0.100\,\mathrm{m}$. Substituting the given values gives
$$
v = 55\,\sqrt{0.100 \cdot 0.0050} = 55 \cdot 0.0224 = 1.23\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The cross-sectional area is $A = 0.1257\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the discharge is
$$
Q = v\,A = 1.23 \cdot 0.1257 = 0.155\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $155\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The specified Chézy coefficient corresponds to $\lambda = \dfrac{8g}{\chi^2} = 0.0259$, within the usual range for pipes in service. Using the pipe radius $\dfrac{D}{2}$ in place of the hydraulic radius would give $219\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
