A circular pipe of diameter $D$, length $L$, and friction factor $\lambda$ carries discharge $Q$. Which expression gives friction head loss as a function of discharge?

- $\Delta H = \dfrac{16\,\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{\pi^2 g\,D^5}$
- $\Delta H = \dfrac{8\,\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{\pi^2 g\,D^5}$
- $\Delta H = \dfrac{8\,\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{\pi^2 g\,D^4}$
- $\Delta H = \dfrac{\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{2\,\pi^2 g\,D^5}$

## Explanation

Start with the Darcy–Weisbach equation, $\Delta H = \lambda\dfrac{L}{D}\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. Substitute the mean velocity, $v = \dfrac{4Q}{\pi D^2}$, whose square is $\dfrac{16\,Q^2}{\pi^2 D^4}$. This gives
$$
\Delta H = \lambda\,\frac{L}{D}\cdot\frac{16\,Q^2}{2g\,\pi^2 D^4} = \frac{8\,\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{\pi^2 g\,D^5} .
$$
For a fixed $\lambda$, the head loss is therefore proportional to the square of the discharge and inversely proportional to the fifth power of the diameter. A coefficient of $16$ would result from writing $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ in place of $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. An exponent of $4$ would result from omitting the diameter in the factor $\dfrac{L}{D}$.
