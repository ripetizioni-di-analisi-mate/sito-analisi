A constant-diameter pipe of length $L = 1.20\,\mathrm{km}$ carries steady flow with energy gradient $J = 0.0045$. What is the head loss between its ends?

- $0.00540\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.00450\,\mathrm{m}$
- $267\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The energy gradient is the head loss per unit length. As a ratio of two lengths, it is dimensionless. The pipe length must therefore be in the same units as the head loss. In meters, $L = 1200\,\mathrm{m}$. Since $\Delta H = J L$, the head loss is
$$
\Delta H = 0.0045 \cdot 1200 = 5.40\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Using the numerical length in kilometers would give $0.0054\,\mathrm{m}$, one thousandth of the correct value.
