In uniform flow through a full circular pipe, how does shear stress vary from the centerline to the wall?

- It is constant across the whole section and equals $\tau_0$
- It is $\tau_0$ at the centerline and zero at the wall
- It increases with the square of the distance from the centerline
- It increases in proportion to the distance from the centerline, from zero to $\tau_0$ at the wall

## Explanation

Apply the integral momentum equation to a cylinder of fluid of radius $r$ concentric with the pipe. Its cross-sectional area is $\pi r^2$ and its perimeter is $2\pi r$, so its hydraulic radius is $\dfrac{r}{2}$. Hence
$$
\tau(r) = \gamma\,\frac{r}{2}\,J = \tau_0\,\frac{2r}{D} .
$$
This relation is linear in $r$. The shear stress is therefore zero at the centerline, where the cylindrical surface area vanishes, and reaches its maximum value $\tau_0$ at the wall. The distribution holds for both laminar and turbulent flow because the derivation does not use Newton’s law of viscosity.
