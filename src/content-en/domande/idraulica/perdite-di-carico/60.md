A laboratory model of a pipe of diameter $D = 500\,\mathrm{mm}$ has diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$. It has the same relative roughness and operates at the same Reynolds number as the full-size pipe. Which statement about their friction factors is correct?

- They are equal because $\lambda$ depends only on $Re$ and $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D}$
- The value for the full-scale pipe is ten times as large because its diameter is ten times as large
- The model value is smaller because its absolute roughness is only one tenth as large
- They are equal only if the flow is laminar in both pipes

## Explanation

Nikuradse’s experiments showed that the Darcy friction factor depends on just two dimensionless groups, $\lambda = \lambda\!\left( Re, \dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} \right)$. The model and the full-scale pipe have the same Reynolds number and relative roughness, so they have **the same Darcy friction factor** in any flow regime. The absolute roughness and the diameter of the model are each one tenth of their full-scale values, leaving the ratio $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D}$ unchanged. This is the basis for using scale models to determine head losses in full-scale pipes.
