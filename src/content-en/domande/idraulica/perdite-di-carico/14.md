A cast-iron pipe of diameter $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 250\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water in uniform flow and has friction factor $\lambda = 0.0211$. What shear stress does the wall exert on the fluid?

- $1.06\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $10.4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $20.9\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $41.8\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.250}{0.1257} = 1.99\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.202\,\mathrm{m}$. The Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the energy gradient:
$$
J = \frac{0.0211}{0.40} \cdot 0.202 = 0.0106 .
$$
The hydraulic radius of a full circular pipe is $R = \dfrac{D}{4} = 0.100\,\mathrm{m}$. The relation between **wall shear stress and energy gradient** then gives
$$
\tau_0 = \gamma\,R\,J = 9810 \cdot 0.100 \cdot 0.0106 = 10.4\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
Using the geometric radius in place of the hydraulic radius would double the result to $20.9\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Using density instead of specific weight would give $1.06\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
