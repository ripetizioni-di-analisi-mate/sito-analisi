The pipe shown connects two reservoirs with an elevation difference $\Delta z = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Its diameter is $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L = 50\,\mathrm{m}$, and friction factor $\lambda = 0.022$; the fittings have $\sum \xi_i = 2.5$. What is the discharge?

- $157\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $57.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $49.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $35.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Bernoulli’s equation between the reservoir free surfaces gives $\Delta z = \Delta H$, since velocity in the reservoirs and gauge pressure at both surfaces are zero. Factoring out the velocity head in the pipe gives
$$
\Delta z = \left( \lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \sum_i \xi_i \right)\frac{v^2}{2g} .
$$
The coefficient is $0.022 \cdot \dfrac{50}{0.15} + 2.5 = 7.33 + 2.5 = 9.83$, so
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 g \,\Delta z}{9.83}} = \sqrt{\frac{19.62 \cdot 4.00}{9.83}} = 2.83\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
and multiplying by the cross-sectional area, $0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2}$, gives $Q = 0.0499\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $49.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Neglecting minor losses would give $57.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Treating the full elevation difference as a velocity head and neglecting all losses would give $157\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
