A pipe operates in transitionally rough turbulent flow. What happens if the Prandtl–Nikuradse relation is used in place of the Colebrook–White equation to calculate the friction factor?

- A larger Darcy friction factor and hence an overestimate of the head loss
- A smaller Darcy friction factor and hence an underestimate of the head loss
- The same Darcy friction factor, because both equations agree throughout the turbulent regime
- A Darcy friction factor that depends only on the Reynolds number

## Explanation

The Colebrook–White equation is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = -2\,\log_{10}\!\left( \dfrac{2.51}{Re\,\sqrt{\lambda}} + \dfrac{\varepsilon}{3.71\,D} \right)$. Dropping the first term inside the logarithm gives the Prandtl–Nikuradse relation. Removing this positive term decreases the argument. Applying $-2\,\log_{10}$ to the smaller argument gives a larger value, so $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}$ increases and $\lambda$ is **smaller**. In the transitionally rough regime, the first term is significant. For $Re = 2 \cdot 10^5$ and $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 0.0005$, the Colebrook–White equation gives $\lambda = 0.0188$, while the Prandtl–Nikuradse relation gives $\lambda = 0.0167$. Since head loss is proportional to $\lambda$, the latter underestimates the loss by about $11\,\%$.
