A PVC pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and absolute roughness $\varepsilon = 0.0015\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries water with $Re = 1.5 \cdot 10^5$. The friction factor is approximately $\lambda = 0.0166$. In which regime is the pipe operating?

- It behaves as a hydraulically smooth pipe because the roughness term is about one thirtieth of the other term
- Fully rough turbulent, because the Reynolds number exceeds $4000$
- Transitional, because the two terms in the Colebrook–White equation are of the same order of magnitude
- Fully rough turbulent, because the roughness term dominates the other term

## Explanation

Evaluate the two terms in the Colebrook–White equation. The Reynolds number term and the relative roughness term, respectively, are
$$
\frac{2.51}{Re\,\sqrt{\lambda}} = \frac{2.51}{1.5 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 0.129} = 1.30 \cdot 10^{-4} ,
\qquad
\frac{\varepsilon}{3.71\,D} = \frac{1.5 \cdot 10^{-5}}{3.71} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-6} .
$$
The second term is about $0.03$ times the first, well below one tenth, so the pipe behaves as **hydraulically smooth**. The full equation gives $\lambda = 0.01666$ and the smooth-pipe equation gives $0.01656$, a difference of $0.6\,\%$. A Reynolds number above $4000$ establishes only that the flow is turbulent.
