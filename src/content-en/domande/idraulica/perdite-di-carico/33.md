A pipe operates with $Re = 2.0 \cdot 10^5$, $\lambda = 0.021$, and relative roughness $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 0.0010$. Is the flow fully rough and turbulent?

- Yes, because the Reynolds number exceeds $4000$
- No, because the parameter $Re\sqrt{\lambda}\,\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D}$ is about $29$
- Yes, because the parameter $Re\sqrt{\lambda}\,\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D}$ is about $290$
- No, because the flow is laminar

## Explanation

In the fully rough turbulent regime, the dimensionless group $Re\sqrt{\lambda}\,\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D}$ must exceed approximately $200$. For the given data,
$$
Re\,\sqrt{\lambda}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{D} = 2.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot \sqrt{0.021} \cdot 0.0010 = 29 ,
$$
so the criterion is not met. The pipe operates in the **transitionally rough regime**, so the Darcy friction factor still depends on the Reynolds number and must be found from the full Colebrook–White equation. The Prandtl–Nikuradse relation would give $\lambda = 0.0196$, underestimating the head loss by about $7\,\%$.
