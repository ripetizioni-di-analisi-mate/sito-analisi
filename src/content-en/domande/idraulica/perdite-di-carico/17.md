A pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 15\,\mathrm{m}$ has friction factor $\lambda = 0.025$ and fittings with $\sum \xi_i = 3.0$. What fraction of its total head loss is due to the fittings?

- $55.6\,\%$
- $88.9\,\%$
- $44.4\,\%$
- $80.0\,\%$

## Explanation

Both friction loss and minor loss are proportional to the same velocity head. The total head loss is therefore
$$
\Delta H = \left( \lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \sum_i \xi_i \right)\frac{v^2}{2g} ,
$$
so it is enough to compare the two dimensionless coefficients. The coefficient for friction loss is $\lambda\dfrac{L}{D} = 0.025 \cdot 150 = 3.75$, and the coefficient for minor losses is $3.0$. The fraction due to minor losses is therefore
$$
\frac{3.0}{3.0 + 3.75} = 0.444 ,
$$
or $44.4\,\%$. In a pipe only $150$ diameters long, the two losses are of the same order of magnitude, so both must be included.
