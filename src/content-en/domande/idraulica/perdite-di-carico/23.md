At fixed discharge, length, and friction factor, how does the friction head loss in a pressurized pipe change if the diameter doubles?

- It is halved
- It falls to one quarter of its original value
- It falls to one eighth of its original value
- It falls to one thirty-second of its original value

## Explanation

The Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the friction head loss as $\Delta H = \lambda\dfrac{L}{D}\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. At a specified discharge, the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{4Q}{\pi D^2}$, so it is proportional to $D^{-2}$. Thus $v^2$ is proportional to $D^{-4}$. Since the head loss also contains a factor of $\dfrac{1}{D}$, the resulting dependence is $D^{-5}$. Specifically,
$$
\Delta H \propto \frac{1}{D}\cdot\frac{1}{D^4} = \frac{1}{D^5} .
$$
Doubling the diameter therefore reduces the head loss by a factor of $2^5 = 32$. This strong dependence explains why moving up just one standard pipe size can substantially change the energy gradient.
