A straight pipe of constant diameter with no fittings carries steady discharge $Q$. What does the difference between the piezometric heads at two sections measure?

- The head loss between the sections, since their velocity heads are equal
- Only the difference in elevation between the two sections
- The head loss, but only if the pipe is horizontal
- The sum of the head loss and velocity head

## Explanation

The total head at a cross section is $H = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma} + \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, and the head loss between the two sections is $H_1 - H_2$. Since the pipe diameter and discharge are the same at both sections, continuity gives $v_1 = v_2$. The velocity heads are therefore equal. Subtracting the total heads cancels these terms and leaves
$$
\Delta H = \left( z_1 + \frac{p_1}{\gamma} \right) - \left( z_2 + \frac{p_2}{\gamma} \right) ,
$$
which is the difference in piezometric head. This result holds for any pipe inclination because the piezometric heads already include the elevations of the two sections.
