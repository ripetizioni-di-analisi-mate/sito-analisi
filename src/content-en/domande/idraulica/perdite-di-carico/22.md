To study in the laboratory the flow of an oil with kinematic viscosity $\nu_o = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, moving with mean velocity $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a pipe of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$, a tube of diameter $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ carrying water with $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ is used. At what mean velocity must the water flow for the Reynolds number to be the same?

- $3.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.150\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.300\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.0750\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The Reynolds number of the oil pipe is
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu_o} = \frac{1.50 \cdot 0.200}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{4} ,
$$
so the flow is turbulent. The water tube has the same Reynolds number if $\dfrac{v_a\,D_a}{\nu} = Re$, therefore
$$
v_a = \frac{Re\,\nu}{D_a} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{4} \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.100} = 0.300\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The viscosity of water is ten times smaller and the diameter of the tube is half as large, so the oil velocity is multiplied by $\dfrac{2}{10}$. Accounting only for the diameter ratio would give $3.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and only for the viscosity ratio $0.150\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
