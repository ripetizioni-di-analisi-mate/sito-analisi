A pipe of diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 1.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. What is the Reynolds number?

- $9.55 \cdot 10^3$
- $1.91 \cdot 10^4$
- $3.82 \cdot 10^4$
- $3.82 \cdot 10^7$

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.050^2}{4} = 1.963 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is
$$
v = \frac{0.0015}{1.963 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 0.764\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
With the diameter expressed in meters, the Reynolds number is
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu} = \frac{0.764 \cdot 0.050}{10^{-6}} = 3.82 \cdot 10^4 .
$$
This exceeds $4000$, so the flow is **turbulent**. The Darcy friction factor must therefore be found from the Colebrook–White equation or the Moody diagram. Using the numerical diameter in millimeters would give $3.82 \cdot 10^7$.
