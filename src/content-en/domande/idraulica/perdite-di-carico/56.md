An inclined pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ is full of water at rest behind a closed gate valve at its downstream end. Two gauges, $100\,\mathrm{m}$ apart along the centerline with elevation difference $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ between the taps, show a pressure difference of $49.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the wall shear stress?

- $24.5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $49.1\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.50\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $0$

## Explanation

In a fluid at rest, the hydrostatic pressure law gives the same piezometric head $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ at every point. The measured pressure difference corresponds to $\dfrac{49\,100}{9810} = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, exactly the elevation difference between the two taps. The difference in piezometric head is therefore zero, giving $J = 0$. The relation $\tau_0 = \gamma R J$ then gives $\tau_0 = 0$, as expected for a fluid at rest, which cannot sustain shear stress. The value $24.5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ comes from dividing the drop in pressure head by the distance between the taps. This calculation confuses a change in pressure head with a change in piezometric head.
