A horizontal pipe expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. How does pressure change between the sections upstream and downstream of the expansion?

- It decreases by $1.64\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- It increases by $3.48\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- It increases by $1.84\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- It decreases by $1.84\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The continuity equation gives mean velocities of $v_1 = 2.83\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 1.02\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The **Borda–Carnot head loss** is therefore
$$
\Delta H = \frac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(2.83 - 1.02)^2}{19.62} = 0.167\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Since the pipe is horizontal, Bernoulli’s equation with head loss between the two sections gives
$$
\frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{2g} - \Delta H = 0.355 - 0.167 = 0.188\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so $p_2 - p_1 = 9810 \cdot 0.188 = 1.84\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The pressure rises because the decrease in velocity head, $0.355\,\mathrm{m}$, exceeds the head loss. The integral momentum equation gives the same pressure rise. For a sudden expansion, it yields $p_2 - p_1 = \rho\,v_2\,(v_1 - v_2)$.
