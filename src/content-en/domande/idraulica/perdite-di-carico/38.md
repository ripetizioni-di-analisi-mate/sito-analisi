In which flow regime do the Chézy and Gauckler–Strickler power laws apply?

- In laminar flow, where $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re}$
- In the transition region, for $2000 < Re < 4000$
- In the fully rough turbulent regime, where $\lambda$ is independent of the Reynolds number
- Throughout the turbulent regime, regardless of relative roughness

## Explanation

The coefficients are related by $\chi = \sqrt{\dfrac{8g}{\lambda}}$. The Gauckler–Strickler relation also gives $\chi = k_s R^{\frac{1}{6}}$. This second expression depends only on wall roughness and cross-sectional geometry, so the Chézy coefficient is independent of discharge. For both expressions to hold, $\lambda$ must be independent of the Reynolds number. This occurs in the **fully rough turbulent regime**. Cast-iron and concrete water mains generally operate in this regime at normal service velocities, which is why these power-law formulas are used in design.
