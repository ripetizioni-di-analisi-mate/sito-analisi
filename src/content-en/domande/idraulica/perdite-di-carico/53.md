Two pipe sections in series, the first of diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the second of diameter $D_2 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, have the same friction factor and carry discharge $Q$. Neglecting the minor loss at the change in diameter, which statement about the energy grade line and hydraulic grade line is correct?

- In the $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ section, the two lines are steeper and farther apart
- In the $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ section, the two lines are steeper and closer together
- The two lines have the same slope in both sections because the discharge is the same
- In the $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ section, the lines are less steep because the velocity is higher

## Explanation

At a given discharge, the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{4Q}{\pi D^2}$, so the velocity head is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{D^4}$. The Darcy–Weisbach energy gradient, $J = \lambda\dfrac{1}{D}\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, is therefore proportional to $\dfrac{1}{D^5}$. In the $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe, the gradient is larger by a factor of $\left( \dfrac{300}{200} \right)^5 = 7.6$. The vertical separation between the energy grade line and the hydraulic grade line equals the velocity head, which is larger by a factor of $\left( \dfrac{300}{200} \right)^4 = 5.1$ in the same pipe. Consequently, both lines are **steeper** and **farther apart** in the smaller pipe.
