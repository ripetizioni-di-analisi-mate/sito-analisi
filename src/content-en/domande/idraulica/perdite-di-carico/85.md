A horizontal pipe expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$. The gauge pressure just downstream of the step is $p_1 = 80\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Under the assumption used to derive the Borda–Carnot loss, what force does the water exert on the annular face of the step?

- $5.65\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $1.41\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $17.0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $4.24\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The derivation of the Borda–Carnot loss assumes that the pressure at the entrance to the enlarged pipe is $p_1$ over the entire area $A_2$. This includes the annular face of the step in contact with the recirculating water. Its area is
$$
A_2 - A_1 = \frac{\pi}{4}\left( 0.30^2 - 0.15^2 \right) = 0.0530\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
$$
so the water exerts an upstream force of magnitude $F = p_1\,(A_2 - A_1) = 80\,000 \cdot 0.0530 = 4240\,\mathrm{N} = 4.24\,\mathrm{kN}$. Multiplying the pressure by the entire area $A_2$ would give $5.65\,\mathrm{kN}$. Using only the area $A_1$ would give $1.41\,\mathrm{kN}$.
