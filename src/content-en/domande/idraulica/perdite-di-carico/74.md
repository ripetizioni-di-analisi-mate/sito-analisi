The graph plots friction factor $\lambda$ against $\log_{10} Re$ for four pipes in turbulent flow, labeled I, II, III, and IV. Which curve represents the hydraulically smooth pipe?

::grafico{f="1/(1.8*log(6.9/10^x+0.00010947)/log(10))^2;1/(1.8*log(6.9/10^x)/log(10))^2;1/(1.8*log(6.9/10^x+0.00065336)/log(10))^2;1/(1.8*log(6.9/10^x+0.000018342)/log(10))^2" xmin=3.6 xmax=8.6 ymin=0 ymax=0.05 nomi=",λ" etichette="8.25,0.0016,log₁₀ Re;7.75,0.0225,I;7.75,0.0095,II;7.75,0.0335,III;7.75,0.0162,IV" titolo="Darcy friction factor $\lambda$ against Reynolds number for four pipes"}

- Curve I
- Curve II
- Curve III
- Curve IV

## Explanation

On the Moody diagram, the rough-pipe curves initially follow the smooth-pipe curve. As the Reynolds number increases, they depart from it and level off in the fully rough regime. The smooth-pipe curve is therefore the lowest curve and continues to slope downward even at the highest Reynolds numbers. This is **curve II**. Curves I, III, and IV level off, so they represent rough pipes. Their limiting friction factors increase with relative roughness, making III the roughest of the three and IV the least rough.
