A new cast-iron pipe of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 1200\,\mathrm{m}$, with roughness coefficient $k_s = 90\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, carries $Q = 90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. What is the head loss along the pipe?

- $3.01\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.35\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.00633\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.59\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.090}{0.0707} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In a full circular pipe the hydraulic radius is one quarter of the diameter, so $R = \dfrac{0.30}{4} = 0.0750\,\mathrm{m}$. Solving the **Gauckler–Strickler equation** for the energy gradient gives
$$
J = \frac{v^2}{k_s^2\,R^{\frac{4}{3}}} = \frac{1.27^2}{90^2 \cdot 0.0750^{\frac{4}{3}}} = 0.00633 ,
$$
and since $\Delta H = J L$, the head loss is $0.00633 \cdot 1200 = 7.59\,\mathrm{m}$. Using the geometric radius $\dfrac{D}{2}$ instead of the hydraulic radius would give $3.01\,\mathrm{m}$.
