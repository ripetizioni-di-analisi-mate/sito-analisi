Two cast-iron pipes with equal absolute roughness $\varepsilon = 0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$, one of diameter $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the other of diameter $500\,\mathrm{mm}$, operate in fully rough turbulent flow. Which has the greater friction factor?

- The $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe, because it has greater relative roughness
- The $500\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe, because it has greater wall surface area
- They have the same Darcy friction factor because the absolute roughness is the same
- The $500\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe, because it has the higher Reynolds number

## Explanation

In the fully rough turbulent regime, the friction factor depends only on relative roughness, as given by the Prandtl–Nikuradse relation $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = 2\,\log_{10}\!\left( \dfrac{3.71\,D}{\varepsilon} \right)$. With the same absolute roughness, the smaller pipe has the greater relative roughness, $\dfrac{0.5}{100} = 0.005$ compared with $\dfrac{0.5}{500} = 0.001$. Substitution gives $\lambda = 0.0303$ for the $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe and $\lambda = 0.0196$ for the $500\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe. The **$100\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe** therefore has the higher friction factor. The Reynolds number does not enter the calculation because $\lambda$ is independent of it in this regime.
