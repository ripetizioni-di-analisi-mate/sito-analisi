A fountain is supplied from a reservoir with its free surface $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the nozzle, as shown. The supply pipe has diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L = 20\,\mathrm{m}$, friction factor $\lambda = 0.025$, and fittings with $\sum \xi_i = 5.1$ (entrance, bends, and gate valve). It ends in an upward-facing nozzle of the same diameter discharging to the atmosphere. Neglecting air resistance, how high does the jet rise above the nozzle?

- $0.545\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.984\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.373\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Apply Bernoulli’s equation with head loss between the reservoir free surface and the nozzle outlet, where the gauge pressure is zero. The water leaves the nozzle at the pipe velocity $v$, so the available head supplies both the exit velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ and the head losses. Thus
$$
6.00 = \left( 1 + \lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \sum_i \xi_i \right)\frac{v^2}{2g} = (1 + 10 + 5.1)\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 16.1\,\frac{v^2}{2g} .
$$
The jet rises until its velocity head has been converted into elevation head. Its height above the nozzle is therefore $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \dfrac{6.00}{16.1} = 0.373\,\mathrm{m}$, with $v = 2.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Omitting minor losses would give $0.545\,\mathrm{m}$. With no losses, the jet would rise to the reservoir free-surface elevation, $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the nozzle.
