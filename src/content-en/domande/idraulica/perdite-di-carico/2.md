A horizontal cast-iron pipe of constant diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 900\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 45\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water and has friction factor $\lambda = 0.0254$. The upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 500\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the downstream gauge pressure?

- $383\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $488\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $265\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $617\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.045}{0.0314} = 1.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.105\,\mathrm{m}$. The **Darcy–Weisbach equation** gives
$$
\Delta H = \lambda\,\frac{L}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.0254 \cdot \frac{900}{0.20} \cdot 0.105 = 11.95\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pipe is horizontal and of constant diameter, so both sections have the same elevation and velocity head. The entire head loss therefore appears as a drop in pressure head:
$$
p_2 = p_1 - \gamma\,\Delta H = 500 - 9.81 \cdot 11.95 = 383\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Subtracting the head loss as if it were expressed in kilopascals would give $488\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
