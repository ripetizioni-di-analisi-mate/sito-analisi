A constant-diameter pipe descends uniformly. Over a length of $500\,\mathrm{m}$ its centerline drops by $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Pressure gauges throughout the reach all give the same reading. What is the energy gradient?

- $0$
- $125$
- $4.00$
- $0.00800$

## Explanation

Since the pipe diameter is constant, the velocity heads are equal and the head loss between the ends of the pipe equals the drop in piezometric head. The pressures are also equal, so the drop in piezometric head reduces to the elevation difference and the head loss is $\Delta H = z_1 - z_2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The energy gradient is the head loss per unit length,
$$
J = \frac{\Delta H}{L} = \frac{4.0}{500} = 0.00800 ,
$$
which equals the downward slope of the pipe centerline. The hydraulic grade line is therefore parallel to the pipe. The constant pressure means that friction dissipates all the energy released as the water descends.
