Two pipes of equal diameter, length, and friction factor carry the same water discharge. The first is horizontal. The second rises so that its downstream section is $30\,\mathrm{m}$ above its upstream section. Which statement is correct?

- The second pipe has a head loss greater by $30\,\mathrm{m}$
- The pipes have the same pressure drop because they have the same head loss
- The second pipe has a smaller head loss because some energy becomes elevation head
- The head losses are equal, and the pressure drop in the second pipe exceeds that in the first by $294\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The Darcy–Weisbach equation, $\Delta H = \lambda\dfrac{L}{D}\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, depends on pipe length, diameter, friction factor, and velocity. Elevation does not enter the equation, so the two pipes have **the same head loss**. Since the velocity heads at the two ends are equal, each pipe satisfies $\dfrac{p_1 - p_2}{\gamma} = \Delta H + (z_2 - z_1)$. In the rising pipe, the elevation difference $z_2 - z_1 = 30\,\mathrm{m}$ adds $\gamma \cdot 30 = 9810 \cdot 30\,\mathrm{Pa} = 294\,\mathrm{kPa}$ to the pressure drop. The inclination therefore affects the pressure drop even though the friction head loss is the same.
