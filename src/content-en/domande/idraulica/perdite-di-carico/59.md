A hydropower station is supplied by a reservoir whose free surface lies $100\,\mathrm{m}$ above that of the tailrace. The penstock has diameter $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L = 2000\,\mathrm{m}$, and friction factor $\lambda = 0.018$, and carries $Q = 0.30\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ of water. Neglecting minor losses and outlet velocity head, what power does the water deliver to the turbine?

- $294\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $76.9\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $140\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $217\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The total head drops by $100\,\mathrm{m}$ between the reservoir free surface and the tailrace free surface. Part of this head, $\Delta H$, is dissipated in the penstock. The remainder, $H_t = 100 - \Delta H$, is the head available to the turbine. The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.30}{0.1257} = 2.39\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.290\,\mathrm{m}$, so the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives
$$
\Delta H = 0.018 \cdot \frac{2000}{0.40} \cdot 0.290 = 26.1\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Thus $H_t = 100 - 26.1 = 73.9\,\mathrm{m}$ and the hydraulic power delivered to the turbine is $P = \gamma\,Q\,H_t = 9810 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 73.9 = 217\,\mathrm{kW}$. The value $294\,\mathrm{kW}$ neglects head loss in the penstock, while $76.9\,\mathrm{kW}$ is the power dissipated by friction.
