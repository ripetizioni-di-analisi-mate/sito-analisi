The horizontal pipe shown contracts abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries $Q = 45\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. The contraction coefficient is $C_c = 0.63$. What is the head loss at the contraction?

::schema{nodi="A,0,2,-;B,5,2,-;C,12,2,-" condotte="A-B,1.3;B-C,0.8" tratteggi="5,2.65 5.9,2.32 7.0,2.32 8.3,2.4;5,1.35 5.9,1.68 7.0,1.68 8.3,1.6" frecce="1.6,2,3.0,2,v_1;9.6,2,11.0,2,v_2" etichette="2.2,0.70,D_1 = 250 mm;9.8,0.95,D_2 = 150 mm;6.4,2.9,vena contracta c" riferimento=0 titolo="Sudden contraction from $250$ to $150\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

- $0.502\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.135\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0452\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.114\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The downstream velocity is $v_2 = \dfrac{0.045}{0.01767} = 2.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The area at the vena contracta is $C_c A_2$, so continuity gives
$$
v_c = \frac{v_2}{C_c} = \frac{2.55}{0.63} = 4.04\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Dissipation occurs in the sudden expansion from the vena contracta to the downstream section. Apply the **Borda–Carnot loss** between those sections:
$$
\Delta H = \frac{(v_c - v_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(4.04 - 2.55)^2}{19.62} = 0.114\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The corresponding loss coefficient based on downstream velocity is $\xi = \left( \dfrac{1}{C_c} - 1 \right)^2 = 0.345$. The upstream velocity does not enter the calculation because dissipation in the accelerating flow before the vena contracta is negligible.
