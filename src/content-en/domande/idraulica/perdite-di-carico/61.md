Oil of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ flows through a pipe of diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ at mean velocity $v = 1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the flow regime?

- Laminar, because $Re = 3000$ is less than $4000$
- Turbulent, because $Re = 6.00 \cdot 10^4$ exceeds $4000$
- Transitional, because $Re = 3000$ lies between $2000$ and $4000$
- Laminar, because $Re = 300$ is less than $2000$

## Explanation

The Reynolds number is
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu} = \frac{1.20 \cdot 0.050}{2 \cdot 10^{-5}} = 3000 .
$$
Pipe flow is laminar for $Re < 2000$ and turbulent for $Re > 4000$. At $Re = 3000$, the flow is therefore in the **transition regime**. Neither the laminar relation $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re}$ nor the Colebrook–White equation reliably predicts head loss in this range. Using the viscosity of water would give $6.00 \cdot 10^4$. The value $300$ would result from entering the diameter as $0.005\,\mathrm{m}$.
