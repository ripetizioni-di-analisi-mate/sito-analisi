The horizontal pipe shown expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries $Q = 75\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. What is the head loss at the expansion?

::schema{nodi="A,0,2,-;B,5,2,-;C,12,2,-" condotte="A-B,0.8;B-C,1.2" frecce="1.6,2,3.0,2,v_1;8.0,2,9.4,2,v_2" etichette="5.0,3.1,1;9.0,3.1,2;2.2,1.05,D_1 = 200 mm;9.6,0.85,D_2 = 300 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Sudden expansion from $200$ to $300\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

- $0.233\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0897\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.179\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.290\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Continuity gives the following mean velocities:
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.075}{0.0314} = 2.39\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.075}{0.0707} = 1.06\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The **Borda–Carnot loss** depends on the difference between the velocities, so
$$
\Delta H = \frac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(2.39 - 1.06)^2}{19.62} = 0.0897\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The difference between the velocity heads, $0.233\,\mathrm{m}$, is the total change in kinetic energy per unit weight. Only part of that change is dissipated. The rest is recovered as an increase in pressure.
