On a test stand, a valve is installed in a horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ carrying $Q = 8.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. The measured pressure difference between taps upstream and downstream of the valve on the same pipe is $p_1 - p_2 = 9.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Friction loss between the taps is negligible. What is the valve loss coefficient?

- $3.55$
- $7.11$
- $69.7$
- $114$

## Explanation

The pressure taps are on the same horizontal, constant-diameter pipe. Their elevations and velocity heads are therefore equal, so the valve head loss equals the drop in pressure head, $\Delta H_{\mathrm{loc}} = \dfrac{9000}{9810} = 0.917\,\mathrm{m}$. The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.0080}{0.005027} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.129\,\mathrm{m}$. The definition $\Delta H_{\mathrm{loc}} = \xi\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ then gives
$$
\xi = \frac{0.917}{0.129} = 7.11 .
$$
Dividing the pressure drop by $\rho v^2$ without the factor of $2$ would give half the correct coefficient, $3.55$.
