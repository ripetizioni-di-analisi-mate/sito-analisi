A cast-iron pipe of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ has absolute roughness $\varepsilon = 0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$. Above what Reynolds number is the flow fully rough, using the threshold $Re\sqrt{\lambda}\,\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 200$?

- $4.27 \cdot 10^6$
- $2.29 \cdot 10^5$
- $6.54 \cdot 10^5$
- $1.00 \cdot 10^5$

## Explanation

In the fully rough turbulent regime, the Prandtl–Nikuradse relation gives the Darcy friction factor from the relative roughness alone. Here $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 0.0020$, so
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = 2\,\log_{10}\!\left( \frac{3.71}{0.0020} \right) = 6.54 ,
\qquad
\lambda = 0.0234 .
$$
The threshold condition $Re\sqrt{\lambda}\,\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 200$ therefore gives
$$
Re = \frac{200}{\sqrt{0.0234} \cdot 0.0020} = 6.54 \cdot 10^5 ,
$$
which for water corresponds to a mean velocity of $2.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Omitting the square root of $\lambda$ would give $4.27 \cdot 10^6$. Using $70$, the threshold for the different group $\dfrac{u_*\varepsilon}{\nu}$, would give $2.29 \cdot 10^5$.
