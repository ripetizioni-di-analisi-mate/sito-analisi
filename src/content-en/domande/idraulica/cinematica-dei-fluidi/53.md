In the following plane velocity fields, $k$, $a$, and $u_0$ are positive constants, $t$ is time, and coordinates are in meters. Which field gives steady, nonuniform flow?

- $\vec{v} = (u_0,\; 0)$
- $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\; -k\,y)$
- $\vec{v} = (a\,t,\; 0)$
- $\vec{v} = (a\,t\,y,\; a\,t\,x)$

## Explanation

Steady flow requires $\dfrac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial t} = \vec{0}$ at every point. The two fields containing $t$ are therefore unsteady. $(a\,t,\;0)$ describes unsteady, uniform flow, and $(a\,t\,y,\;a\,t\,x)$ describes unsteady, nonuniform flow. The velocity in field $(u_0,\;0)$ is the same everywhere, so this flow is steady and uniform. The streamlines of field $(k\,x,\;-k\,y)$ are the hyperbolas $x\,y = \mathrm{constant}$. The speed $k\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ varies along each hyperbola as the distance from the origin changes. The flow described by $(k\,x,\;-k\,y)$ is therefore **steady and nonuniform**.
