In laminar flow through a circular pipe, the speed at radial distance $r$ from the centerline is
$$
u = u_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right) ,
$$
where $r_0$ is the pipe radius. If the centerline speed is $0.900\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the mean speed?

- $0.300\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.600\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.900\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.450\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Integrate velocity over the cross section using the area element $dA = 2\pi r\,dr$ of a thin annulus,
$$
Q = \int_0^{r_0} u_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right) 2\pi r\,dr = 2\pi u_{\max}\left[\frac{r^2}{2} - \frac{r^4}{4 r_0^2}\right]_0^{r_0} = \frac{\pi r_0^2 u_{\max}}{2} .
$$
Dividing by the area $A = \pi r_0^2$ gives
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{u_{\max}}{2} = 0.450\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The radius cancels, so in laminar flow through a circular pipe the mean velocity is always half the centerline velocity. The ratio $\dfrac{2}{3}$, which would give $0.600\,\mathrm{m/s}$, applies to a parabolic velocity profile over the depth of a channel, where the integration uses a strip of constant width.
