At the junction shown, pipe $1$ supplies discharge $Q_1 = 140\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Pipes $2$ and $3$ are outlets, with discharge $Q_2 = 55\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in the former and diameter $D_3 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ in the latter. What is the mean speed in pipe $3$?

::schema{nomi=0 nodi="N,0,0;A,-3.4,0;B,3,1.8;C,3,-1.8" condotte="A-N,0.8;N-B,0.6;N-C,0.65,D_3 = 250 mm" frecce="-3.0,0,-1.8,0,Q_1 = 140 L/s;2.1,1.26,3.1,1.86,Q_2 = 55 L/s;2.1,-1.26,3.1,-1.86,Q_3 = ?" riferimento=0 titolo="Junction in a distribution network"}

- $0.433\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.85\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.73\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Fluid does not accumulate at the junction, so total inflow equals total outflow and
$$
Q_3 = Q_1 - Q_2 = 140 - 55\,\mathrm{L/s} = 85\,\mathrm{L/s} = 0.0850\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
Pipe $3$ has area $A_3 = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving a mean velocity of
$$
v_3 = \frac{Q_3}{A_3} = \frac{0.0850}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.73\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The discharge check is immediate. Adding the branch flows as $55 + 85 = 140\,\mathrm{L/s}$ recovers the inlet discharge.
