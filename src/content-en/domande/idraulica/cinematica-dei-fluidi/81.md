Incompressible plane flow has the velocity field
$$
\vec{v} = \frac{k}{x^2 + y^2}\,(x,\;y) ,
$$
with $k = 0.500\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ and coordinates in meters, defined everywhere except at the origin. What is the net outward discharge per unit out-of-plane thickness across a circle of radius $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ centered at the origin?

- $0$
- $3.14\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $6.28\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $1.57\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$

## Explanation

At points away from the origin,
$$
\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} = k\,\frac{y^2 - x^2}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} , \qquad \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = k\,\frac{x^2 - y^2}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} ,
$$
so $\operatorname{div}\vec{v} = 0$. On the circle of radius $R$ the velocity is radial and has magnitude $\dfrac{k\,R}{R^2} = \dfrac{k}{R}$. Its outward normal component is $\dfrac{k}{R}$ everywhere, so
$$
q = 2\pi R \cdot \frac{k}{R} = 2\pi k = 2\pi \cdot 0.500\,\mathrm{m^2/s} = 3.14\,\mathrm{m^2/s} .
$$
The flux is nonzero even though the divergence vanishes away from the origin. The disk encloses the origin, where the field is undefined. The field is therefore not of class $C^1$ throughout the disk and the divergence theorem does not apply. The origin acts as a source supplying a discharge per unit thickness of $3.14\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. The value $0$ comes from applying the theorem without checking its hypotheses. Taking the speed as $k$ gives $6.28\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, while taking it as $\dfrac{k}{R^2}$ gives $1.57\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$.
