A pipe of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries water at discharge $Q = 25\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and temperature $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Given kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, what is the Reynolds number?

- $5.31 \cdot 10^4$
- $1.06 \cdot 10^5$
- $2.12 \cdot 10^5$
- $8.49 \cdot 10^5$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.0250}{0.0177}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.41\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
because the cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.150^2}{4} = 0.0177\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The characteristic length in the Reynolds number for a full circular pipe is its diameter, so
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu} = \frac{1.41 \cdot 0.150}{10^{-6}} = 2.12 \cdot 10^5 .
$$
Equivalently, use $Re = \dfrac{4\,Q}{\pi\,D\,\nu}$ directly with the discharge, since $v = \dfrac{4Q}{\pi D^2}$. The value is well above $4000$, indicating the turbulent flow typical of water mains.
