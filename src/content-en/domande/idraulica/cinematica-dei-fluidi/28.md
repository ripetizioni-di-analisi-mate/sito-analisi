At a flow section, mean speed is defined by the ratio $v = \dfrac{Q}{A}$. What does this quantity represent?

- The local speed at the point in the section where it is greatest
- The pipe centerline speed
- The speed measured at a fixed point and averaged over time
- The uniform speed that would carry the same discharge across the entire section

## Explanation

Discharge is the flux of velocity through the section, $Q = \displaystyle\int_A u\,dA$, where $u$ is the local velocity. Dividing by the area gives
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{1}{A}\int_A u\,dA ,
$$
so $v$ is the area average of the local velocity. The product $A\,v$ therefore gives the actual discharge, and $v$ is the uniform velocity that would deliver the same flow rate. Because of viscosity, velocity is zero at the wall and highest at the centerline. The mean velocity is therefore less than the centerline velocity. For laminar flow in a circular pipe, it is exactly half the maximum velocity.
