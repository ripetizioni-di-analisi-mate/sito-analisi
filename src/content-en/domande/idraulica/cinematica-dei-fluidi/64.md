A rectangular channel has width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and depth $h = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Velocity is vertically uniform and increases linearly across each half-width from zero at the bank to $1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the center, as shown. What is the discharge?

::schema{acqua="0,0 8,0 8,4 0,4" solidi="0,4 8,4 8,4.4 0,4.4;0,-0.4 8,-0.4 8,0 0,0" frecce="2,0.5,2.75,0.5;2,1,3.5,1;2,1.5,4.25,1.5;2,2,5,2,1.20 m/s;2,2.5,4.25,2.5;2,3,3.5,3;2,3.5,2.75,3.5" linee="2,0 5,2 2,4;2,0 2,4" quote="7 0,7 4,b = 4.00 m,0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of the channel. Velocity is zero at the banks and greatest at the center"}

- $4.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.20\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.40\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $3.20\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

Discharge is the integral of velocity over the section, $Q = \displaystyle\int_A u\,dA$. Velocity is independent of elevation, so use $dA = h\,dy$, where $y$ is distance from one bank. This gives
$$
Q = h\int_0^{b} u(y)\,dy .
$$
The integral across the width is the area under the triangular velocity diagram, $\dfrac{4.00 \cdot 1.20}{2} = 2.40\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, so $Q = 1.00 \cdot 2.40 = 2.40\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The mean velocity is half the maximum and equals $0.600\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Multiplying the flow area by the maximum velocity would give $4.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The value $3.20\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ corresponds to a mean-to-maximum velocity ratio of $\dfrac{2}{3}$, which applies to a parabolic profile.
