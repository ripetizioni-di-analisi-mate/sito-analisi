At a pipe section the mean speed is $v = 2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the kinetic energy correction factor is $\alpha = 1.08$. What is the corrected velocity head?

- $0.220\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.189\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.441\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity head of a real flow includes the kinetic energy correction factor and equals $\alpha\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. Substituting the data with $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ gives
$$
\alpha\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 1.08 \cdot \frac{2.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.08 \cdot 0.204\,\mathrm{m} = 0.220\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The factor $\alpha$ accounts for the velocity distribution, so the actual velocity head exceeds the mean-velocity estimate $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.204\,\mathrm{m}$ by $8\%$. In pressurized pipe calculations, $\alpha = 1$ is commonly assumed because the factor generally differs from unity by only a few hundredths. The denominator contains twice the acceleration due to gravity. Using $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ would therefore double the term and give $0.441\,\mathrm{m}$.
