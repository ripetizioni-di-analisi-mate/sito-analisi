A water layer of thickness $a$ lies between a fixed plate and a parallel plate moving at speed $U$, as shown. In laminar flow, velocity varies linearly with distance $y$ from the fixed plate according to $u = U\,\dfrac{y}{a}$. What is the correction factor $\alpha$ at a section normal to the plates?

::schema{acqua="0,0 6,0 6,2 0,2" solidi="0,-0.3 6,-0.3 6,0 0,0;0,2 6,2 6,2.3 0,2.3" frecce="1,0.5,1.5,0.5;1,1,2,1;1,1.5,2.5,1.5;3.6,2.6,5.1,2.6,U" linee="1,0 3,2" tratteggi="1,0 1,2" quote="5.4 0,5.4 2,a,0.4" etichette="3,-0.65,fixed plate;1.6,2.65,moving plate" riferimento=0 titolo="Water layer between a fixed plate and a moving plate"}

- $1.33$
- $1.00$
- $0.500$
- $2.00$

## Explanation

Take a strip of height $a$ and arbitrary width $b$. Since $dA = b\,dy$, averaging over the area is equivalent to averaging over $y$ from $0$ to $a$. The mean velocity is
$$
v = \frac{1}{a}\int_0^a U\,\frac{y}{a}\,dy = \frac{U}{2} ,
$$
and the mean of velocity cubed is $\dfrac{1}{a}\displaystyle\int_0^a U^3\,\frac{y^3}{a^3}\,dy = \dfrac{U^3}{4}$. The definition of $\alpha$ therefore gives
$$
\alpha = \frac{\dfrac{U^3}{4}}{\left(\dfrac{U}{2}\right)^3} = \frac{8}{4} = 2.00 .
$$
The value $1.33$ is the momentum correction factor $\beta = \dfrac{4}{3}$, which uses velocity squared. A value below $1$ is impossible here because the mean of velocity cubed is at least the cube of the mean velocity.
