A pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries oil of density $\rho = 880\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ at mean speed $v = 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the mass flow rate?

- $41.5\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $10.4\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $47.1\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $166\,\mathrm{kg/s}$

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area and mean velocity give the volumetric flow rate,
$$
Q = A\,v = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.200^2}{4} \cdot 1.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0314 \cdot 1.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0471\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
Since density is uniform across the section, the mass flow rate is $Q_m = \rho\,Q$, giving
$$
Q_m = 880 \cdot 0.0471\,\mathrm{kg/s} = 41.5\,\mathrm{kg/s} .
$$
The value $47.1\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ uses the density of water, which is greater than that of oil. At the same volumetric flow rate, the oil therefore carries less mass per unit time.
