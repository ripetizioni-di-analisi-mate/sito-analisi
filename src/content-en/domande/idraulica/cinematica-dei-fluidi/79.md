A rectangular channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and depth $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ carries uniform flow of speed $0.600\,\mathrm{m/s}$ parallel to the banks. In the center, a semicylindrical screen of radius $R = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and vertical axis spans the full depth with its convex side upstream, as shown in plan. Assuming uniform velocity through the screen, what discharge crosses it?

::schema{acqua="0,0 9,0 9,3 0,3" solidi="0,-0.35 9,-0.35 9,0 0,0;0,3 9,3 9,3.35 0,3.35" linee="4.5,0.5 4.24,0.53 4.0,0.63 3.79,0.79 3.63,1.0 3.53,1.24 3.5,1.5 3.53,1.76 3.63,2.0 3.79,2.21 4.0,2.37 4.24,2.47 4.5,2.5" frecce="0.5,1.5,2.0,1.5,v" tratteggi="4.5,1.5 3.79,0.79" etichette="5.5,1.5,R = 1.00 m" quote="8.2 0,8.2 3,3.00 m,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of the channel containing the semicylindrical screen"}

- $1.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.83\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $0.900\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $5.65\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The discharge through a surface is the flux of velocity, $Q = \displaystyle\int_A \vec{v} \cdot \hat{n}\,dA$. Let $\varphi$ be the angle between the screen normal and the flow direction, ranging from $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The normal velocity component is $v\cos\varphi$ and the surface area element is $dA = R\,h\,d\varphi$, so
$$
Q = \int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} v\cos\varphi\,R\,h\,d\varphi = 2\,v\,R\,h = 2 \cdot 0.600 \cdot 1.00 \cdot 1.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 1.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
This equals the discharge across the diametral rectangle of width $2R$ and height $h$ perpendicular to the flow, because all fluid passing through the screen then crosses the rectangle. Multiplying the speed by the curved area $\pi R\,h$ gives $2.83\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and overestimates the discharge because the flow is oblique to almost the entire screen. Using the radius in place of the diameter gives $0.900\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, and using the entire cylindrical surface gives $5.65\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
