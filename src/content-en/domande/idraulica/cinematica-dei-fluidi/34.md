Two outlet pipes of diameters $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $D_2 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ leave a junction at the same mean speed, as shown. The total inflow is $Q = 130\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the discharge through the pipe of diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$?

::schema{nomi=0 nodi="N,0,0;A,-3.4,0;B,3.2,1.8;C,3.2,-1.8" condotte="A-N,0.9;N-B,0.6,D_1 = 200 mm;N-C,0.9,D_2 = 300 mm" frecce="-3.0,0,-1.8,0,Q = 130 L/s" riferimento=0 titolo="Junction with two pipes carrying flow at the same mean velocity"}

- $90.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $40.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $65.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $78.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The two pipes carry flow at the same mean velocity $v$, so the junction balance is $Q = v\,(A_1 + A_2)$. The areas are $A_1 = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A_1 + A_2} = \frac{0.130}{0.102}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The discharge in the larger pipe is therefore
$$
Q_2 = A_2\,v = 0.0707 \cdot 1.27\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0900\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 90.0\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The other branch carries $40.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and the two discharges add up to the incoming $130\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The discharge divides in proportion to the areas, hence to the squares of the diameters. Dividing it in proportion to the diameters would give $78.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
