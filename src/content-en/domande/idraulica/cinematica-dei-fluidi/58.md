A vertical pipe of diameter $D = 800\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries one-dimensional upward flow. At a given section the centerline gauge pressure is $15.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the gauge pressure at the wall in that section?

- $18.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $11.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $22.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $15.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

In one-dimensional flow, pressure varies hydrostatically across the section and the piezometric head $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ is constant throughout it. The pipe is vertical, so a transverse section is horizontal and all of its points have the same elevation $z$. Pressure is therefore uniform across the section, giving $15.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the wall. The values $18.9$ and $11.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$ add or subtract $\gamma\,\dfrac{D}{2} = 3.92\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Such a pressure difference would occur between the centerline and the bottom or top of a horizontal pipe.
