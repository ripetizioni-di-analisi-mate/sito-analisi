The pipe shown has diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries one-dimensional flow at discharge $Q = 45\,\mathrm{L/s}$. A plane inclined at $30^\circ$ to the normal cross section cuts the pipe over an oblique area $A'$. What discharge crosses this oblique section?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,7,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.0" tratteggi="2.6,-0.5 2.6,0.5;5.18,-0.5 4.6,0.5" frecce="1.0,0,2.0,0,v" etichette="2.6,0.82,A;5.0,0.82,A′" riferimento=0 titolo="Normal section of area $A$ and oblique section of area $A'$"}

- $39.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $45.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $52.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $90.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The pipe between the normal and oblique sections forms a streamtube. All the fluid entering the first section leaves through the second, so both carry the same discharge, $45.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Direct integration gives the same result. The oblique area is $A' = \dfrac{A}{\cos 30^\circ}$, and the velocity makes an angle $30^\circ$ with the normal to $A'$, so
$$
\int_{A'} \vec{v} \cdot \hat{n}\,dA = A'\,v\,\cos 30^\circ = \frac{A}{\cos 30^\circ}\,v\,\cos 30^\circ = A\,v = Q .
$$
Multiplying the oblique area by the mean speed without the cosine factor would give $52.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. This treats the full oblique area as perpendicular to the flow. The relation $Q = A\,v$ in this form thus applies only to sections normal to the streamlines.
