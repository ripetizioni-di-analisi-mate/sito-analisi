At a water-pipe section the discharge is $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$, mean speed is $v = 1.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and momentum correction factor is $\beta = 1.05$. What is the momentum flux through the section?

- $67.2\,\mathrm{N}$
- $128\,\mathrm{N}$
- $134\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1320\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The momentum flux through a cross section includes the momentum correction factor $\beta$ and is given by $\beta\,\rho\,Q\,v$. With discharge $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s} = 0.0800\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and water density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, this gives
$$
\beta\,\rho\,Q\,v = 1.05 \cdot 1000 \cdot 0.0800 \cdot 1.60\,\mathrm{N} = 134\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The factor $\beta$ corrects the estimate based on mean velocity. The actual flux is therefore $5\%$ greater than the product $\rho\,Q\,v = 128\,\mathrm{N}$. Momentum flux has the dimensions of force and is measured in newtons, so the expression must use density. Substituting specific weight $\gamma = 9810\,\mathrm{N/m^3}$ for density would give $1320\,\mathrm{N}$.
