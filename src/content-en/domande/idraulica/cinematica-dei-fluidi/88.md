The plane field $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\; k\,y)$ with $k = 0.500\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and coordinates in meters describes an expanding gas. What is the net outward velocity flux per unit out-of-plane thickness across the square with vertices $(\pm 1;\,\pm 1)$?

- $0$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $4.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $8.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$

## Explanation

The divergence is $\operatorname{div}\vec{v} = k + k = 2k = 1.00\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ everywhere, so the field cannot describe incompressible flow. Apply the divergence theorem to a prism with the given square as its base and unit thickness. The outward flux equals the volume integral of divergence,
$$
\Phi = \int_{\text{square}} \operatorname{div}\vec{v}\;dA = 1.00 \cdot 4.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s} = 4.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s} .
$$
Direct calculation confirms the result. Along side $x = 1$, the outward normal velocity $k \cdot 1 = 0.500\,\mathrm{m/s}$ acts over a length $2\,\mathrm{m}$, giving $1.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ per unit thickness. Each of the four sides contributes equally. The positive flux is the net volume driven out of the square each second per unit thickness as the gas expands.
