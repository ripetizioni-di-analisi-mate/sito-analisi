Water flows at a discharge of $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ through a water-supply pipe of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the mean velocity in the pipe?

- $0.306\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.22\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.960\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.89\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The discharge is $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s} = 0.0600\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, since $1\,\mathrm{L/s} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The area of the circular cross section is
$$
A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2} .
$$
From the definition of mean velocity,
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.0600}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.22\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
This falls within the usual range for water-supply pipes, from $0.5$ to $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and is consistent with the given data.
