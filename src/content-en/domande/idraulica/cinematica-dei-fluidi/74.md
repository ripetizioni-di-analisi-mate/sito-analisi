A boat has long been traveling at constant speed across an otherwise still lake, with no wind or other source of waves. How is the water motion around it classified?

- Steady for observers both on the bank and aboard the boat
- Unsteady for observers both on the bank and aboard the boat
- Steady for an observer on the bank and unsteady aboard the boat
- Unsteady for an observer on the bank and steady aboard the boat

## Explanation

Flow is steady when velocity at every fixed point in the chosen reference frame is independent of time. To an observer on shore, the water at a fixed point is initially at rest, moves as the boat passes, and then returns nearly to rest. The velocity changes with time, so the flow is **unsteady** in the shore frame. Points fixed in the boat’s frame move with the boat. After the boat has traveled steadily for a long time, water approaches and passes around it in a pattern that no longer changes. The velocity at each point of this frame is constant in time, so the flow is **steady** on board. This is why flow past a body moving at constant velocity is analyzed in a body-fixed frame, much like flow past a bridge pier in a river.
