Near the stagnation point of a jet striking a flat horizontal wall, the velocity field of an incompressible fluid is
$$
\vec{v} = (k\,x,\; k\,y,\; -2k\,z) ,
$$
with $k$ a positive constant and $z$ the distance from the wall. A fluid cube of side $1.00\,\mathrm{cm}$ aligned with the axes deforms into a rectangular block. When its horizontal edges reach length $1.50\,\mathrm{cm}$, what is its vertical dimension?

- $0.667\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.444\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.00\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.333\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The field has divergence $k + k - 2k = 0$, so it satisfies local continuity for incompressible flow and every material volume remains constant. The cube initially occupies $1.00\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Each horizontal edge has stretched to $1.50\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the vertical edge $c$ satisfies $1.50 \cdot 1.50 \cdot c = 1.00$, giving
$$
c = \frac{1.00}{1.50^2}\,\mathrm{cm} = 0.444\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The particle paths give the same result. $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = k\,x$ integrates to $x = x_0\,e^{k t}$, and $\dfrac{dz}{dt} = -2k\,z$ to $z = z_0\,e^{-2k t}$. Each horizontal edge therefore stretches by the factor $e^{k t} = 1.50$, while the vertical edge scales by the factor $e^{-2k t} = \dfrac{1}{1.50^2}$. The value $0.667\,\mathrm{cm}$ accounts for only one horizontal extension, as in plane flow. The value $0.333\,\mathrm{cm}$ results from dividing by $2 \cdot 1.50$ instead of $1.50^2$.
