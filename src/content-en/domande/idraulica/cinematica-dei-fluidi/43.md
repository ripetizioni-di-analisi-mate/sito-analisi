Water has kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ and a particular oil has $\nu = 4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. In which of the following cases is the flow laminar?

- Water in a tube of diameter $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ at mean speed $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- Oil in a pipe of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ at mean speed $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- Oil in a tube of diameter $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ at mean speed $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- Water in a pipe of diameter $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ at mean speed $0.500\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The flow regime depends on the Reynolds number $Re = \dfrac{v\,D}{\nu}$. Flow is stably laminar for $Re < 2000$. For oil in the tube of diameter $20\,\mathrm{mm}$,
$$
Re = \frac{1.00 \cdot 0.020}{4 \cdot 10^{-5}} = 5.0 \cdot 10^2 ,
$$
which is below $2000$, so this flow is laminar. Water in the same tube gives $Re = 2.0 \cdot 10^4$. Oil in the pipe of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ gives $Re = 5.0 \cdot 10^3$, and water in the pipe of diameter $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ at mean velocity $0.500\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives $Re = 5.0 \cdot 10^4$. All three flows are turbulent. High viscosity and small characteristic dimensions favor laminar flow, as encountered in oils, filters, and porous media. Water in supply mains typically has $Re$ between $10^5$ and $10^6$.
