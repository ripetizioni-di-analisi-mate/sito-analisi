The inverted conical tank shown has a vertical axis. At height $h$ above the vertex the free surface has radius $r = \dfrac{h}{2}$. It receives constant inflow $Q = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and has no outlets. How fast does the surface rise when the water depth is $h = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$?

::schema{acqua="0,0 1,2 -1,2" linee="-1.5,3 0,0 1.5,3" tratteggi="0,0 0,3.2" pelo="-1,1,2" frecce="0.45,3.7,0.45,2.4,Q = 20 L/s" quote="1.9 0,1.9 2,h = 2.00 m,0.3" riferimento=0 titolo="Conical tank with its apex at the bottom"}

- $6.37\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $19.1\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $1.59\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $25.5\,\mathrm{mm/s}$

## Explanation

The water occupies a cone of radius $r = \dfrac{h}{2}$ and depth $h$, so its volume is $V = \dfrac{\pi r^2 h}{3} = \dfrac{\pi h^3}{12}$. The tank has no outlet, so the rate of increase in volume equals the inflow, $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = Q$. Differentiating $V$ with respect to time gives
$$
\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{\pi h^2}{4}\,\frac{dh}{dt} = \pi r^2\,\frac{dh}{dt} ,
$$
which is the free-surface area times the rate at which the level rises. At $h = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the radius is $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the surface area is $\pi\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so
$$
\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{Q}{\pi r^2} = \frac{0.0200}{3.14}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.37 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.37\,\mathrm{mm/s} .
$$
The free-surface area increases with $h$. The level therefore rises more slowly as the tank fills. The value $19.1\,\mathrm{mm/s}$ uses the average area $\dfrac{V}{h}$, only one-third of the free-surface area.
