A straight horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries one-dimensional flow. The centerline gauge pressure is $120\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the gauge pressure at the bottom of the section?

- $118\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $120\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $122\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $124\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The flow is one-dimensional, so pressure varies hydrostatically with elevation across the section and the piezometric head $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ is the same at every point. The bottom of the pipe is one radius, $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$, below the centerline. Thus
$$
p_{\mathrm{bottom}} = p_{\mathrm{axis}} + \gamma \cdot 0.200 = 120 + 9.81 \cdot 0.200\,\mathrm{kPa} = 122\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Pressure increases with depth, so it is higher at the bottom than at the centerline. Using the diameter instead of the radius would give $124\,\mathrm{kPa}$ because it would count an elevation difference of $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$ instead of $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$.
