Oil of density $\rho = 900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu = 0.0900\,\mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$ flows at mean speed $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$. Which statement about its Reynolds number is correct?

- $Re = 1.11$, so the flow is laminar
- $Re = 1.00 \cdot 10^5$, so the flow is turbulent
- $Re = 1.00 \cdot 10^6$, so the flow is turbulent
- $Re = 1000$, so the flow is laminar

## Explanation

The Reynolds number uses kinematic viscosity. Divide the dynamic viscosity by density to obtain
$$
\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho} = \frac{0.0900}{900}\,\mathrm{m^2/s} = 1.00 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s} .
$$
The Reynolds number is therefore
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu} = \frac{1.00 \cdot 0.100}{1.00 \cdot 10^{-4}} = 1000 ,
$$
which is less than $2000$, so the flow is **laminar**. Using $\mu$ instead of $\nu$ gives $Re = 1.11$. Using the kinematic viscosity of water $10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ gives $1.00 \cdot 10^5$, and entering the diameter in millimeters instead of meters gives $1.00 \cdot 10^6$.
