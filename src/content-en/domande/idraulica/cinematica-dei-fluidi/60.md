In a plane flow with a velocity field of class $C^1$, two streamlines at the same instant pass through a point $P$ where velocity is nonzero. Which statement is correct?

- The lines can cross at $P$ if the flow is unsteady
- The lines can cross at $P$ if speed reaches its maximum at $P$
- They coincide near $P$ because only one streamline passes through $P$
- They coincide near $P$ only in steady flow

## Explanation

Assume $v_x(P) \neq 0$. If $v_x(P) = 0$, then $v_y(P) \neq 0$ and the same argument applies with $x$ and $y$ interchanged. Near $P$, the streamlines at the chosen instant solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{v_y}{v_x}$. The right-hand side is of class $C^1$ in a neighborhood of $P$. By the existence and uniqueness theorem for initial-value problems, only one solution passes through $P$, so the two curves coincide in a neighborhood of $P$. This argument holds at a single instant and therefore applies to unsteady flow too. The streamlines may change with time, but distinct streamlines cannot cross at a given instant. They can meet only where velocity vanishes.
