To measure discharge in a pipe of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$, a tracer is introduced and is detected $450\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream after time $300\,\mathrm{s}$. Assuming the tracer travels at the mean speed, what is the discharge?

- $47.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $106\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $424\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $26.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The tracer travels $450\,\mathrm{m}$ in $300\,\mathrm{s}$ at the mean flow velocity, giving
$$
v = \frac{450}{300}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.300^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the discharge is
$$
Q = A\,v = 0.0707 \cdot 1.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.106\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $106\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Reversing the distance-to-time ratio gives $0.667\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $47.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Using $\pi D^2$ for the area gives $424\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Substituting the radius for the diameter in $\dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$ gives $26.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
