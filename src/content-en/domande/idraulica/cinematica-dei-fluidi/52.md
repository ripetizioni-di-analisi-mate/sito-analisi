A plane flow has the velocity field $\vec{v} = (-k\,y,\; k\,x)$ with $k = 0.500\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and coordinates in meters. A particle is at $(2;\,0)$ at time $t = 0$. Where is it at time $t = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $(-2;\,0)$
- $(1.41;\,1.41)$
- $(0;\,2)$
- $(0;\,-2)$

## Explanation

The field is independent of time, so the flow is steady and the particle follows the streamline through $(2;\,0)$. The streamlines satisfy $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{x}{y}$, or $x\,dx + y\,dy = 0$, giving the circles $x^2 + y^2 = \mathrm{constant}$. The particle therefore stays on the circle of radius $2\,\mathrm{m}$. Its speed on this circle is $k \cdot 2 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and initially $v_y = k\,x > 0$, so the particle moves counterclockwise. Its angular speed is $k$. By time $t$ it has swept through an angle
$$
\theta = k\,t = 0.500 \cdot \pi = \frac{\pi}{2} ,
$$
and reached $\left(2\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2};\,2\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = (0;\,2)$. Point $(-2;\,0)$ corresponds to an angle of $\pi$, obtained by taking the angular speed as $1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Point $(0;\,-2)$ corresponds to clockwise motion.
