A plane flow has velocity field $\vec{v} = (k\,x^2,\;-2k\,x\,y)$ with $k = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^{-1}s^{-1}}$, coordinates in meters, and velocities in $\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the speed at point $(3.00;\,2.00)$?

- $0.900\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $15.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Substituting the coordinates of the point into the two velocity components gives
$$
v_x = k\,x^2 = 0.100 \cdot 3.00^2\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.900\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_y = -2k\,x\,y = -2 \cdot 0.100 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 2.00\,\mathrm{m/s} = -1.20\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The magnitude of the velocity is therefore
$$
|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{0.900^2 + 1.20^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{2.25}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The vertical component is negative, so the fluid moves downward and to the right at this point. The component magnitudes sum to $2.10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, whereas the magnitude of the vector follows from the Pythagorean theorem.
