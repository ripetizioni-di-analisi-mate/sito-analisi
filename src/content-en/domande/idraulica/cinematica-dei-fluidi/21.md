Why is the flow rate through the lateral surface of a streamtube zero?

- The lateral surface is impermeable, like a pipe wall
- Velocity is tangent to the lateral surface at every point, so its normal component is zero
- Viscosity makes velocity zero on every boundary surface
- The fluid is incompressible, so the volume inside the tube stays constant

## Explanation

Streamlines form the lateral surface of a streamtube. Since velocity is tangent to a streamline at every point, $\vec{v} \cdot \hat{n} = 0$ everywhere on that surface, and the integral defining the flow rate,
$$
Q = \int_A \vec{v} \cdot \hat{n}\,dA ,
$$
is zero. The streamtube thus behaves like a conduit with impermeable walls because the velocity is tangent to its boundary. The velocity on the lateral surface is generally nonzero because that surface lies within the fluid and is not a solid wall.
