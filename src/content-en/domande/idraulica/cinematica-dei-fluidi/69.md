In plane flow the particle positions are given by
$$
x = x_0 + u_0\,t , \qquad y = y_0\,e^{k t} ,
$$
where $(x_0;\,y_0)$ is the particle position at time $t = 0$ and $u_0$ and $k$ are positive constants. Which statement about the Eulerian description is correct?

- The velocity field is $\vec{v} = (u_0,\;k\,y\,e^{k t})$ and the flow is unsteady
- The velocity field is $\vec{v} = (u_0,\;k\,y)$ and the flow is steady
- The flow is unsteady because each particle’s velocity changes with time
- The velocity field is $\vec{v} = (u_0,\;k\,y)$ and the flow is unsteady

## Explanation

The given equations follow individual particles and therefore describe the flow in Lagrangian coordinates. Differentiating with respect to time gives the particle velocity components $u_0$ and $k\,y_0\,e^{k t}$. At time $t$ the particle’s vertical coordinate is $y = y_0\,e^{k t}$, so its second velocity component is $k\,y$. The Eulerian field, which gives velocity at a specified point and time, is thus
$$
\vec{v}(x, y, t) = (u_0,\;k\,y) .
$$
The field is independent of $t$, so the flow is **steady**. Each particle accelerates because $k\,y_0\,e^{k t}$ increases with time as the particle moves into regions of higher velocity. At a fixed point the velocity remains constant. The expression $k\,y\,e^{k t}$ results from replacing $y_0$ by $y$ while retaining $e^{k t}$, a factor already included in $y$.
