A continuous water jet leaves a faucet vertically downward with diameter $16.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and uniform speed $0.500\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The flow is steady and each particle falls freely under gravity. What is the jet diameter $20.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ lower down?

- $3.92\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $7.92\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $32.3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $5.38\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The free surface of the jet consists of streamlines because the surface particles move tangentially to it. The jet is therefore a streamtube and has the same discharge at every section even in the absence of solid walls. Each particle falls freely, so at a point $20.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ lower its speed is
$$
v_2 = \sqrt{v_1^2 + 2 g\,\Delta z} = \sqrt{0.500^2 + 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.200}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The continuity equation $\dfrac{\pi D_1^2}{4}\,v_1 = \dfrac{\pi D_2^2}{4}\,v_2$ gives
$$
D_2 = D_1\sqrt{\frac{v_1}{v_2}} = 16.0 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{0.500}{2.04}}\,\mathrm{mm} = 7.92\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
As the jet falls it accelerates and narrows. Using the velocity ratio without taking its square root gives $3.92\,\mathrm{mm}$, inverting the ratio gives $32.3\,\mathrm{mm}$, and using $v_1 + 2g\,\Delta z$ for the velocity without taking the square root gives $5.38\,\mathrm{mm}$.
