Oil of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ flows in a pipe of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ at mean speed $v = 0.500\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the flow regime?

- Laminar, with $Re = 750$
- Transitional, with $Re = 3000$
- Laminar, with $Re = 1500$
- Turbulent, with $Re = 15\,000$

## Explanation

In a full circular pipe, the characteristic length for the Reynolds number is the diameter. Thus
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu} = \frac{0.500 \cdot 0.150}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}} = 1500 .
$$
This is below $2000$, so the flow is stably laminar and adjacent layers move without mixing. At the same velocity in the same pipe, water, whose kinematic viscosity is fifty times smaller, would give $Re = 7.5 \cdot 10^4$ and the flow would be turbulent. Using the radius instead of the diameter would halve the result and give $Re = 750$.
