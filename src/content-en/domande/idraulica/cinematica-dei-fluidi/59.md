Two piezometers are connected to the same section of a straight horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$, far from bends, one at the top and one at the bottom. How do their water levels compare?

- Both rise to the same level
- The piezometer connected to the lower point reads $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$ higher
- The piezometer connected to the upper point reads $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$ higher
- The two levels differ by the velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$

## Explanation

Far from a bend the flow is one-dimensional, so the transverse pressure distribution is hydrostatic and the piezometric head $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ is constant across the section. Water in each piezometer rises to elevation $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$, the piezometric head at its connection. The two water columns therefore reach **the same elevation**. Pressure at the lower point exceeds that at the upper point by $\gamma D = 3.92\,\mathrm{kPa}$, equivalent to a water column of height $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$. The lower point is also $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$ below the upper one, so the differences cancel. A flush wall tap senses only static pressure, so the velocity head does not enter the reading.
