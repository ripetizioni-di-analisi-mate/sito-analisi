A conical pipe section carrying constant discharge has steady, nonuniform flow. Which derivative of the velocity field vanishes?

- The derivative $\dfrac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial s}$ along the flow
- Both derivatives, with respect to time and along the flow, vanish
- Both derivatives, with respect to time and along the flow, are nonzero
- The time derivative $\dfrac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial t}$

## Explanation

Steady flow satisfies $\dfrac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial t} = \vec{0}$, so the time derivative is zero. Uniform flow satisfies $\dfrac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial s} = \vec{0}$ along the flow direction. Here the flow is nonuniform, so that spatial derivative is nonzero. The diameter of the tapered pipe varies along its length, and continuity requires $A\,v = Q$ with $Q$ constant. The mean velocity must therefore change along the pipe. A particle accelerates as it travels, although a measurement at a fixed section gives the same velocity at every instant.
