A laboratory tube carries water of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ at constant discharge $Q = 0.0100\,\mathrm{L/s}$. For what diameters is the laminar flow stable, that is, $Re < 2000$?

- $D < 6.37\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $D > 3.18\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $D > 6.37\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $D < 3.18\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

At fixed discharge, the Reynolds number is $Re = \dfrac{4\,Q}{\pi\,D\,\nu}$ and therefore varies inversely with diameter. In a wider pipe, mean velocity decreases as $\dfrac{1}{D^2}$ while the diameter in the numerator of $\dfrac{v\,D}{\nu}$ increases only as $D$. The condition $Re < 2000$ is therefore equivalent to
$$
D > \frac{4\,Q}{2000\,\pi\,\nu} = \frac{4 \cdot 1.00 \cdot 10^{-5}}{2000\,\pi \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{m} = 6.37 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The flow is stably laminar for $D > 6.37\,\mathrm{mm}$. Assuming that $Re$ increases with diameter reverses the inequality. Using the radius instead of the diameter in the Reynolds number gives $3.18\,\mathrm{mm}$.
