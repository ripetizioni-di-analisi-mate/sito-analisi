A showerhead is fed by a pipe of diameter $D = 15\,\mathrm{mm}$ carrying water at mean speed $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. It distributes the water among $30$ identical openings of diameter $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the mean exit speed?

- $0.750\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $11.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $338\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By continuity, the showerhead divides the pipe discharge equally among its thirty identical openings. Each opening therefore carries one-thirtieth of the pipe discharge,
$$
\frac{\pi D^2}{4}\,v = 30 \cdot \frac{\pi d^2}{4}\,v_f .
$$
Canceling $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ gives
$$
v_f = \frac{D^2\,v}{30\,d^2} = \frac{15^2 \cdot 1.50}{30 \cdot 1.0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 11.3\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The combined area of the openings, $23.6\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, is smaller than the pipe area $177\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, so the exit velocity exceeds the pipe velocity. The value $338\,\mathrm{m/s}$ assumes that all the discharge leaves through one opening. The value $0.750\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the diameter ratio without squaring it.
