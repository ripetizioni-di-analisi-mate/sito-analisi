Two circular pipe sections carry the same discharge. The second has twice the diameter of the first. How does the mean speed change from the first to the second?

- It is halved
- It falls to one quarter
- It doubles
- It quadruples

## Explanation

The fluid is incompressible and the flow is steady, so continuity gives $A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$. Mean velocity is therefore inversely proportional to area. A circular section has area proportional to diameter squared, so
$$
\frac{v_2}{v_1} = \frac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^{\!2} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\!2} = \frac{1}{4} .
$$
The mean velocity in the second reach is one-quarter of that in the first. Reversing the calculation shows that halving the diameter quadruples the mean velocity.
