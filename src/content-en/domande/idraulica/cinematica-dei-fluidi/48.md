Steady plane flow of an incompressible fluid in the corner between the positive $x$ and $y$ axes has velocity field $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\;-k\,y)$ with $k = 0.500\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, coordinates in meters, and velocities in $\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the discharge per unit thickness normal to the flow plane between the walls and the streamline through $P = (2;\,1)$?

::schema{solidi="-0.3,-0.3 5.6,-0.3 5.6,0 0,0 0,4.6 -0.3,4.6" linee="0.5,4 0.667,3 0.8,2.5 1,2 1.333,1.5 1.6,1.25 2,1 2.5,0.8 3,0.667 4,0.5 5,0.4" frecce="0.3,4.4,0.3,3.4,v;4.4,0.2,5.4,0.2,v" etichette="2.25,1.25,P;1.55,2.3,x·y = 2" riferimento=0 titolo="Flow in the corner between two walls and the streamline through $P$"}

- $1.12\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $0.250\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $2.50\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $1.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$

## Explanation

The streamlines satisfy $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{y}{x}$ and are therefore the hyperbolas $x\,y = \mathrm{constant}$. The walls lie on $x\,y = 0$, and the streamline through $P$ is the hyperbola $x\,y = 2$. No fluid crosses a streamline, so the discharge between the two streamlines is the same through any segment joining them. Choose the vertical segment $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, $0 \le y \le 1\,\mathrm{m}$, from wall $y = 0$ to point $P$. Its normal points along the $x$ axis, so the normal velocity component is $v_x = k \cdot 2 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ everywhere on the segment. Thus
$$
q = \int_0^{1} v_x\,dy = 1.00 \cdot 1.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s} .
$$
At $P$ the speed is $1.12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Multiplying that speed by the segment length gives $1.12\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, and multiplying it by the distance $OP = 2.24\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $2.50\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. Only the normal component contributes to discharge. Integrating $v_y$ rather than $v_x$ over the vertical segment would give $0.250\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$.
