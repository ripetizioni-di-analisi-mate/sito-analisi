The pipe shown changes from diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$. Its downstream mean speed is $v_2 = 2.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the upstream mean speed?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3.8,0,-;D,7.2,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.2,D_1 = 300 mm;C-D,0.8,D_2 = 200 mm" linee="3,0.6 3.8,0.4;3,-0.6 3.8,-0.4" frecce="1.3,0,2.5,0,v_1;5.0,0,6.2,0,v_2 = 2.20 m/s" riferimento=0 titolo="Reducer between the upstream and downstream pipe sections"}

- $0.978\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.47\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.30\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.95\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

For steady incompressible flow, continuity gives $A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$. Both cross sections are circular, so their area ratio is the square of their diameter ratio and
$$
v_1 = v_2\,\frac{A_2}{A_1} = v_2\left(\frac{D_2}{D_1}\right)^{\!2} = 2.20 \cdot \left(\frac{0.200}{0.300}\right)^{\!2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.978\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The upstream section is wider, so it has the lower velocity. The diameter ratio is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and its square is $\dfrac{4}{9}$. Using the unsquared diameter ratio would give $1.47\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
