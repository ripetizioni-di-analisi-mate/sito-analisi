A channel carries discharge $Q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at mean speed $v = 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The section kinetic energy correction factor is $\alpha = 1.20$. At what rate does kinetic energy cross the section?

- $3.38\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $8.10\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $39.7\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $4.05\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The volume of fluid crossing area element $dA$ in one second is $u\,dA$. It carries kinetic energy $\dfrac{\rho\,u^2}{2}\,u\,dA$. Integrating over the section and applying the definition of $\alpha$ gives
$$
P = \int_A \frac{\rho\,u^3}{2}\,dA = \alpha\,\frac{\rho\,A\,v^3}{2} = \alpha\,\rho\,Q\,\frac{v^2}{2} .
$$
Substituting the data gives
$$
P = 1.20 \cdot 1000 \cdot 3.00 \cdot \frac{1.50^2}{2}\,\mathrm{W} = 4050\,\mathrm{W} = 4.05\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The same power can be written as $\gamma\,Q\,\alpha\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, the weight flow rate times the velocity head. Assuming $\alpha = 1$ would give $3.38\,\mathrm{kW}$. The value $39.7\,\mathrm{kW}$ comes from the product $\gamma\,Q\,\alpha\,\dfrac{v^2}{2}$, which omits division by $g$.
