At which of the following sections does pressure vary with elevation according to the hydrostatic pressure law?

- A section of a straight constant-diameter pipe far from bends
- A section halfway through a $90^\circ$ elbow
- A section just downstream of a bottom outlet where the streamlines are still strongly curved
- A section in a sudden expansion

## Explanation

The pressure distribution in a cross section is hydrostatic when the flow is approximately one-dimensional, with straight, parallel streamlines or gently diverging streamlines of small curvature. Acceleration normal to the streamlines is then negligible, so the transverse pressure balance is the same as in a fluid at rest. A straight, constant-diameter pipe far from any bend satisfies this condition. Its piezometric head $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ is therefore constant across the section. In an elbow, immediately downstream of an opening, or in a sudden expansion, the streamlines bend sharply or diverge substantially, so this one-dimensional approximation fails.
