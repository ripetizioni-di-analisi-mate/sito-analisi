A garden hose of inside diameter $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ fills a bucket of volume $12\,\mathrm{L}$ in time $20\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the mean speed in the hose?

- $1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.477\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.64\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.191\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Discharge equals the collected volume divided by the collection time. With $12\,\mathrm{L} = 12 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, this gives
$$
Q = \frac{12 \cdot 10^{-3}}{20}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 6.00 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The internal cross-sectional area of the pipe is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.020^2}{4} = 3.14 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{6.00 \cdot 10^{-4}}{3.14 \cdot 10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.91\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $0.477\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from calculating the area as $\pi D^2$, and $7.64\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from substituting the radius for the diameter in $\dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$. The value $0.191\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from the conversion $1\,\mathrm{L} = 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, which is off by a factor of $10$.
