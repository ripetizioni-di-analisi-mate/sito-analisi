The cylindrical tank shown has diameter $D = 6.00\,\mathrm{m}$, inflow $Q_e = 25\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and outflow $Q_u = 10\,\mathrm{L/s}$. How long does its level take to rise by $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$?

::schema{acqua="0,0 5,0 5,1.8 0,1.8" solidi="-0.3,-0.3 5.3,-0.3 5.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,4.2 -0.3,4.2;5,0 5.3,0 5.3,0.65 5,0.65;5,0.95 5.3,0.95 5.3,4.2 5,4.2" pelo="0,5,1.8" tratteggi="0,3.6 5,3.6" nodi="P,-1.8,3.7,-;R,1.2,3.7,-;S,5,0.8,-;T,8.8,0.8,-" condotte="P-R,0.35;S-T,0.3" frecce="-1.0,3.7,0.2,3.7,Q_e = 25 L/s;6.0,0.8,7.2,0.8,Q_u = 10 L/s" quote="0 1.8,0 3.6,1.80 m,-0.75" riferimento=0 titolo="Cylindrical tank of diameter $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $1450\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2040\,\mathrm{s}$
- $13\,600\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3390\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The inflow exceeds the outflow, so water accumulates in the tank. The volume balance gives
$$
A\,\frac{dh}{dt} = Q_e - Q_u = 0.0250 - 0.0100\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0150\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The water surface has area $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 6.00^2}{4} = 28.3\,\mathrm{m^2}$. A rise $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ therefore requires an additional volume $\Delta V = 28.3 \cdot 1.80 = 50.9\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Both flow rates are constant, giving a filling time of
$$
t = \frac{\Delta V}{Q_e - Q_u} = \frac{50.9}{0.0150}\,\mathrm{s} = 3390\,\mathrm{s} ,
$$
or about $56$ and a half minutes. The corresponding rate of rise is $5.31 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, equivalent to $1.91\,\mathrm{m/h}$. Over $3390\,\mathrm{s}$, this again gives a rise of $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$.
