A plane flow has a velocity field that is uniform in space but varies with time,
$$
\vec{v} = (u_0,\; a\,t) ,
$$
with $u_0 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $a = 0.500\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, time in seconds, and coordinates in meters. What is the pathline of the particle at the origin at time $t = 0$?

- $y = \dfrac{x^2}{4}$
- $y = x$
- $y = 0$
- $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$

## Explanation

The particle pathline solves the initial-value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{dx}{dt} = u_0 , & x(0) = 0 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{dy}{dt} = a\,t , & y(0) = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Integration gives $x = u_0\,t$ and $y = \dfrac{a\,t^2}{2}$. Eliminating time using $t = \dfrac{x}{u_0}$ gives
$$
y = \frac{a}{2\,u_0^2}\,x^2 = \frac{0.500}{2 \cdot 1.00^2}\,x^2 = \frac{x^2}{4} ,
$$
which is a parabola. The line $y = x$ is the streamline at time $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$, when the two velocity components are equal. The line $y = 0$ is the streamline at time $t = 0$. The flow is **unsteady**, so its streamlines change with time and the pathline coincides with none of them. The parabola $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$ results from omitting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ when integrating $a\,t$.
