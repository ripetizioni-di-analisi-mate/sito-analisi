Two pipes supply the junction shown with discharges $Q_1 = 48\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $Q_2 = 35\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and two pipes carry water away. One outlet carries $62\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the discharge in the other outlet?

::schema{nomi=0 nodi="N,0,0;A,-3.2,1.6;B,-3.2,-1.6;C,3.2,1.6;D,3.2,-1.6" condotte="A-N,0.6;B-N,0.55;N-C,0.6;N-D,0.5" frecce="-2.6,1.3,-1.5,0.75,Q_1 = 48 L/s;-2.6,-1.3,-1.5,-0.75,Q_2 = 35 L/s;2.0,1.0,3.1,1.55,Q_3 = 62 L/s;2.0,-1.0,3.1,-1.55,Q_4 = ?" riferimento=0 titolo="Junction with two incoming and two outgoing pipes"}

- $13\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $27\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $21\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $145\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

No fluid accumulates at the junction, so the sum of the inflows equals the sum of the outflows,
$$
Q_1 + Q_2 = Q_3 + Q_4 .
$$
Substituting the given values gives $48 + 35 = 62 + Q_4$ and hence
$$
Q_4 = 83 - 62\,\mathrm{L/s} = 21\,\mathrm{L/s} = 0.0210\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
As a check, $62 + 21 = 83\,\mathrm{L/s}$ equals the sum of the two inflows.
