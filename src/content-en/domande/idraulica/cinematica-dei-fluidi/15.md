A plane flow has velocity field $\vec{v} = (k\,y,\;-k\,x)$ with $k = 0.400\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and coordinates in meters. Which family of curves describes the streamlines?

- Circles centered at the origin
- The rectangular hyperbolas $x\,y = \mathrm{constant}$
- Straight lines through the origin
- The parabolas $y = c\,x^2$

## Explanation

A streamline is tangent to the velocity vector at every point, so it satisfies $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{v_y}{v_x}$. Here this gives
$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-k\,x}{k\,y} = -\frac{x}{y} .
$$
Separating variables gives $y\,dy = -x\,dx$, and integration yields
$$
\frac{y^2}{2} = -\frac{x^2}{2} + c \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2 + y^2 = \mathrm{constant} .
$$
The streamlines are therefore circles centered at the origin. Since $\operatorname{div}\vec{v} = 0$, the field is consistent with incompressible flow. It describes clockwise rigid-body rotation about the origin.
