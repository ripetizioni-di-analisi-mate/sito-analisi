A plane flow has velocity field $\vec{v} = (a\,t\,x,\; -a\,t\,y)$, where $a$ is a positive constant, $t$ is time, and coordinates are in meters. Which statement is correct?

- The flow is steady because the streamlines are the same at every instant
- The field does not satisfy the differential continuity equation because it depends on time
- Pathlines differ from streamlines because the flow is unsteady
- The flow is unsteady and satisfies the differential continuity equation at every instant

## Explanation

At a fixed point, velocity varies with $t$ because $\dfrac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial t} = (a\,x,\; -a\,y)$ differs from $\vec{0}$ away from the origin, so the flow is **unsteady**. At every instant the divergence is $a\,t - a\,t = 0$, and the field therefore satisfies $\operatorname{div}\vec{v} = 0$, as incompressible flow must even when it is unsteady. The streamline equation is $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{y}{x}$, in which $t$ cancels. At every time $t > 0$, the streamlines are therefore the same hyperbolas $x\,y = \mathrm{constant}$. Velocity changes in magnitude with time but maintains its direction. Along a particle path,
$$
\frac{d(x\,y)}{dt} = \frac{dx}{dt}\,y + x\,\frac{dy}{dt} = a\,t\,x\,y - a\,t\,x\,y = 0 ,
$$
so each particle stays on its own hyperbola. In this unsteady flow the pathlines therefore coincide with the streamlines.
