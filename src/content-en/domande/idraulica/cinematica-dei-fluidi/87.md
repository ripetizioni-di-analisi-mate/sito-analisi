An incompressible fluid flows steadily through a plane contraction. Its velocity component along the $x$-axis is
$$
v_x = v_0\left(1 + \frac{x}{L}\right) ,
$$
where $v_0$ and $L$ are positive constants. Which component $v_y$ satisfies continuity and vanishes on the $y = 0$-axis?

- $v_y = -\dfrac{v_0\,y}{L}$
- $v_y = \dfrac{v_0\,y}{L}$
- $v_y = -\dfrac{v_0\,x}{L}$
- $v_y = -v_0\left(1 + \dfrac{x}{L}\right)\dfrac{y}{L}$

## Explanation

Since the fluid is incompressible, $\dfrac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \dfrac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = 0$ everywhere. Because $\dfrac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} = \dfrac{v_0}{L}$, the unknown component satisfies $\dfrac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = -\dfrac{v_0}{L}$. Integration with respect to $y$ gives
$$
v_y = -\frac{v_0\,y}{L} + f(x) .
$$
The condition $v_y = 0$ at $y = 0$ requires $f(x) = 0$, so $v_y = -\dfrac{v_0\,y}{L}$. The negative sign means that the flow converges toward the axis as it accelerates in the $x$ direction. Above the axis $v_y < 0$, and below it $v_y > 0$. The derivative of $-\dfrac{v_0\,x}{L}$ with respect to $y$ is zero. The last expression has derivative $-\dfrac{v_0}{L}\left(1 + \dfrac{x}{L}\right)$, so neither expression makes the divergence vanish.
