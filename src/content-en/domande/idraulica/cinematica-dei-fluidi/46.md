An incompressible fluid in plane flow has the velocity field
$$
\vec{v} = \big(c\,(y - 1),\; c\,(x - 3)\big) ,
$$
with $c = 0.500\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, coordinates in meters, and velocities in $\mathrm{m/s}$. Where is the velocity zero?

- $(1;\,3)$
- $(3;\,1)$
- $(-3;\,-1)$
- $(0;\,0)$

## Explanation

Velocity is zero where both components vanish, so solve
$$
\begin{cases}
c\,(y - 1) = 0 \\
c\,(x - 3) = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation sets the $v_x$ component to zero. Since $c \neq 0$, it gives $y = 1$. The second sets the $v_y$ component to zero and gives $x = 3$. The required point is therefore $(3;\,1)$. Interchanging the coordinates gives $(1;\,3)$, where the velocity is $(1.00;\,-1.00)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At the origin the velocity is $(-0.500;\,-1.50)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A point at which the velocity vanishes and the streamline direction is undefined is called a **stagnation point**.
