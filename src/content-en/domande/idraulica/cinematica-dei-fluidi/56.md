A reservoir supplies a pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$. Opening a valve increases discharge linearly from zero to $60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in $30\,\mathrm{s}$. Assuming incompressible flow, what is the derivative $\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial t}$ of the section mean speed during this interval?

- $0.00200\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.0159\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.0637\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.255\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

At a fixed section the velocity changes with time. The flow is therefore unsteady and $\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial t}$ is nonzero. The cross-sectional area $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.200^2}{4} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is constant, so $v = \dfrac{Q}{A}$ implies $\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial t} = \dfrac{1}{A}\,\dfrac{dQ}{dt}$. The discharge increases linearly, giving $\dfrac{dQ}{dt} = \dfrac{0.0600}{30} = 0.00200\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ and
$$
\frac{\partial v}{\partial t} = \frac{0.00200}{0.0314}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 0.0637\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The same result follows from the final velocity $1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}$ reached in $30\,\mathrm{s}$. The number $0.00200$ is the rate of change of discharge and has different units from acceleration. The values $0.0159$ and $0.255\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ result from calculating the area as $\pi D^2$ or substituting the radius for the diameter in $\dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$.
