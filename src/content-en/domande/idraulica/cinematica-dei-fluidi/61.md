In steady, incompressible plane flow, two adjacent streamlines are $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart where the speed is $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart farther downstream, as shown. Assuming uniform velocity between them at each section, what is the downstream speed?

::schema{linee="0,0 7,0;0,2 2.5,2 4.5,1.5 7,1.5" tratteggi="1,0 1,2;6,0 6,1.5" quote="1 0,1 2,4.0 cm,-0.5;6 0,6 1.5,3.0 cm,0.5" frecce="1.4,1,2.6,1,v_1 = 1.50 m/s;4.2,0.75,5.3,0.75,v_2" riferimento=0 titolo="Two streamlines converging downstream"}

- $1.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Take unit thickness perpendicular to the plane of flow. The two streamlines then bound a streamtube whose sides admit no flow. By continuity, the discharge per unit thickness is the same at both sections. Thus $v_1\,d_1 = v_2\,d_2$ and
$$
v_2 = v_1\,\frac{d_1}{d_2} = 1.50 \cdot \frac{4.0}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
In plane flow the cross-sectional area of the streamtube is proportional to the spacing between the streamlines. Using the square of the spacing, as one would for a circular pipe diameter, would give $2.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The velocity **increases** where the streamlines draw closer together.
