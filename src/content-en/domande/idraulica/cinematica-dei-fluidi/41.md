A circular pipe of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ flows full. What is its hydraulic radius?

- $0.0750\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0375\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.150\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.300\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The hydraulic radius is the flow area divided by the wetted perimeter. When the pipe is full, the area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$ and the wetted perimeter equals the full circumference $C = \pi D$. Thus
$$
R = \frac{A}{C} = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{\pi D} = \frac{D}{4} = \frac{0.300}{4}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0750\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The hydraulic radius of a full circular pipe is therefore half its geometric radius, which is $0.150\,\mathrm{m}$. The relation $R = \dfrac{D}{4}$ also gives the equivalent diameter for a section of any shape because $D_e = 4R$.
