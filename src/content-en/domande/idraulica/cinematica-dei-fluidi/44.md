Which of the following plane velocity fields can describe an incompressible fluid, with $k$ a nonzero constant?

- $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\;k\,y)$
- $\vec{v} = (k\,x^2,\;k\,y^2)$
- $\vec{v} = (k\,y,\;k\,y)$
- $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\;-k\,y)$

## Explanation

A velocity field is consistent with incompressible flow if it satisfies the local continuity equation at every point,
$$
\operatorname{div}\vec{v} = \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = 0 .
$$
For the field $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\;-k\,y)$, the derivatives are $\dfrac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} = k$ and $\dfrac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = -k$. Their sum is zero everywhere, as required. The other divergences are $2k$ for $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\;k\,y)$, $2k\,(x + y)$ for $\vec{v} = (k\,x^2,\;k\,y^2)$, and $k$ for $\vec{v} = (k\,y,\;k\,y)$. Since $k$ is nonzero, none of these fields has zero divergence throughout. For the field $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\;-k\,y)$, the streamline equation $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{y}{x}$ gives the hyperbolas $x\,y = \mathrm{constant}$.
