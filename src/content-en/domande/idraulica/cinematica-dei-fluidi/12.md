The tank shown has length $L = 15.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and a trapezoidal cross section with bottom width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and side slope $1:1$. What volume is withdrawn when the level falls from $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor?

::schema{acqua="0,0 3,0 5,2 -2,2" solidi="0,0 -2.5,2.5 -3.6,2.5 -3.6,-1.1 6.6,-1.1 6.6,2.5 5.5,2.5 3,0" pelo="-2,5,2" tratteggi="-0.5,0.5 3.5,0.5" quote="0 0,3 0,b = 3.00 m,0.4" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal tank with side slope $1:1$, initial level $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and final level $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $124\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $67.5\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $95.6\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $180\,\mathrm{m^3}$

## Explanation

With a bank side slope of $1:1$, the water surface has width $b + 2h$ at depth $h$. The wetted cross-sectional area is therefore
$$
A(h) = \frac{b + (b + 2h)}{2}\,h = b\,h + h^2 .
$$
At the initial depth the cross-sectional area is $A(2.00) = 3.00 \cdot 2.00 + 2.00^2 = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and at the final depth it is $A(0.50) = 1.50 + 0.25 = 1.75\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The tank is prismatic, so multiply the difference in areas by the length to find the volume removed:
$$
V = \left[A(2.00) - A(0.50)\right] L = (10.0 - 1.75) \cdot 15.0\,\mathrm{m^3} = 124\,\mathrm{m^3} .
$$
Using only the bottom width would neglect the sloping sides and give $67.5\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Using the surface width instead of the mean width would give $180\,\mathrm{m^3}$.
