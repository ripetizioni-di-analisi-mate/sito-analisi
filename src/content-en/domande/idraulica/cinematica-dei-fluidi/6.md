A pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries oil of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 2.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. What discharge corresponds to $Re = 2000$, above which laminar flow becomes unstable?

- $0.982\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $3.93\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $5.00\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $15.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Solving the definition $Re = \dfrac{v\,D}{\nu}$ for the mean velocity at the specified Reynolds number gives
$$
v = \frac{Re\,\nu}{D} = \frac{2000 \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{-5}}{0.100}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.500\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.100^2}{4} = 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the discharge is
$$
Q = A\,v = 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 0.500\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 3.93 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 3.93\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The oil’s kinematic viscosity is twenty-five times that of water. Its flow remains laminar at discharges of a few liters per second, whereas water reaches the same $Re$ at much lower discharges.
