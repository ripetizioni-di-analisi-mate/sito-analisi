A hydraulic jump in a stilling basin has upstream depth $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and downstream depth $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$. What range of jump lengths do measurements by the United States Bureau of Reclamation suggest?

- Between $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- Between $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $12.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- Between $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- Between $10.8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $14.4\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The sequent-depth ratio $\dfrac{h_2}{h_1} = 6$ gives $Fr_1^2 = \dfrac{6 \cdot 7}{2} = 21$, so $Fr_1 = 4.58$ and the jump is in the steady range. Measurements by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation place the jump length in this range between $4.5\,h_2$ and $6\,h_2$, giving
$$
4.5 \cdot 2.40 = 10.8\,\mathrm{m} \leq L \leq 6 \cdot 2.40 = 14.4\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The interval from $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ to $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the upstream depth in place of the downstream depth. The interval from $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $12.0\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the depth difference $h_2 - h_1$, and the interval from $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ to $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the multiplication by depth.
