A very wide river has hydraulic radius equal to the water depth, and its resistance follows the Gauckler–Strickler equation. The bed slope is $i = 0.0004$, the normal depth is $h_0 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the critical depth is $h_c = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. What is $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ at a section where the water depth is $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $-4.42 \cdot 10^{-4}$
- $-6.59 \cdot 10^{-4}$
- $-7.64 \cdot 10^{-4}$
- $-1.01 \cdot 10^{-3}$

## Explanation

For a unit-width strip with $R = h$, the friction slope is $J = \dfrac{q^2}{k_s^2\,h^{10/3}}$. Uniform flow gives $i = \dfrac{q^2}{k_s^2\,h_0^{10/3}}$, so $J = i\left(\dfrac{h_0}{h}\right)^{\frac{10}{3}}$. The Froude number satisfies $Fr^2 = \dfrac{q^2}{g\,h^3}$, and $q^2 = g\,h_c^3$, so $Fr^2 = \left(\dfrac{h_c}{h}\right)^3$. The profile equation becomes
$$
\frac{dh}{dx} = i\,\frac{1 - \left(\dfrac{h_0}{h}\right)^{\frac{10}{3}}}{1 - \left(\dfrac{h_c}{h}\right)^3} = 0.0004 \cdot \frac{1 - 1.25^{\frac{10}{3}}}{1 - 0.75^3} = 0.0004 \cdot \frac{-1.104}{0.578} = -7.64 \cdot 10^{-4} .
$$
The negative sign confirms that the depth, between $h_c$ and $h_0$, decreases downstream on an M2 profile. The value $-4.42 \cdot 10^{-4}$ omits the denominator. The value $-6.59 \cdot 10^{-4}$ uses the exponent $3$ in place of $\dfrac{10}{3}$ in the friction slope, and $-1.01 \cdot 10^{-3}$ uses $Fr^2 = \left(\dfrac{h_c}{h}\right)^2$.
