Water flows down a long, steep reach with normal depth $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and enters a second long reach that is still steep but has a gentler slope and normal depth $0.55\,\mathrm{m}$. The critical depth is $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ in both. What happens immediately downstream of the slope change?

- An S2 profile with depth decreasing from $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ toward $0.55\,\mathrm{m}$
- A jump followed by an S1 profile
- An M3 profile with depth increasing toward critical depth
- An S3 profile with depth increasing from $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ toward $0.55\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In both reaches normal depth is below critical depth, so the flow is supercritical and profiles are traced downstream. The first reach is long and downstream conditions cannot affect its supercritical flow. The depth at the slope change is therefore $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, the starting depth for the second reach. It is below both $h_{02} = 0.55\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_c$, placing the profile in zone 3. The **S3** profile increases in depth downstream and approaches $0.55\,\mathrm{m}$. The flow remains supercritical throughout, so there is no jump. An S2 profile would require a starting depth between $h_{02}$ and $h_c$.
