A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0010$ carries $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in gradually varied flow. Two sections $250\,\mathrm{m}$ apart have measured depths of $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream and $1.40\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream. What is the mean friction slope over this reach?

- $0.346 \cdot 10^{-3}$
- $0.654 \cdot 10^{-3}$
- $1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}$
- $1.35 \cdot 10^{-3}$

## Explanation

The mean friction slope is the loss of total head divided by the reach length. The velocities are $v_1 = \dfrac{8.00}{4.00 \cdot 1.50} = 1.333\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = \dfrac{8.00}{4.00 \cdot 1.40} = 1.429\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the specific energies are $E_1 = 1.50 + 0.0906 = 1.5906\,\mathrm{m}$ and $E_2 = 1.40 + 0.1040 = 1.5040\,\mathrm{m}$. Total head is $H = z + E$, and the bed elevation falls by $i\,L = 0.250\,\mathrm{m}$, giving
$$
\Delta H = i\,L + E_1 - E_2 = 0.250 + 0.0866 = 0.3366\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\bar{J} = \frac{\Delta H}{L} = \frac{0.3366}{250} = 1.35 \cdot 10^{-3} .
$$
The value $0.346 \cdot 10^{-3}$ omits the bed elevation change, and $0.654 \cdot 10^{-3}$ subtracts the drop in specific energy instead of adding it. The value $1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}$ equals the bed slope and would apply in uniform flow. Here the depth changes between the two sections.
