A rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, roughness coefficient $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0006$ carries $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a normal depth of $h_0 = 1.64\,\mathrm{m}$. The sluice gate releases water at depth $0.220\,\mathrm{m}$, whose conjugate depth exceeds $h_0$. Using one direct step, find the distance from the gate to the start of the jump.

- $5.38\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.24\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.187\,\mathrm{m}$
- The jump starts immediately downstream of the gate

## Explanation

The depth conjugate to the gate exit depth exceeds $h_0$, so the jump is swept downstream. It begins where the M3 depth is the upstream depth conjugate to $h_0$. Using $v_0 = 1.22\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $Fr_0 = 0.304$ gives
$$
h_a = \frac{1.64}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 0.304^2} - 1\right) = 0.261\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The specific energies are $E(0.220) = 4.432\,\mathrm{m}$ and $E(0.261) = 3.254\,\mathrm{m}$. The friction slopes are $0.2785$ and $0.1608$, with mean $\bar{J} = 0.2196$, so
$$
\Delta x = \frac{E(0.261) - E(0.220)}{i - \bar{J}} = \frac{3.254 - 4.432}{0.0006 - 0.2196} = 5.38\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $4.24\,\mathrm{m}$ uses only the initial friction slope, and $0.187\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the difference in flow depths in place of the difference in specific energies. The jump would begin immediately downstream of the gate only if the depth conjugate to the gate exit depth equaled $h_0$.
