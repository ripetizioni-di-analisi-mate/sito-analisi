Upstream of a hydraulic jump in a horizontal rectangular channel, the flow is supercritical with Froude number $Fr_1 = 4.00$. The momentum function in force units at each section is the sum of the hydrostatic force $\dfrac{\gamma\,b\,h^2}{2}$ and the momentum flux $\rho\,Q\,v$. Which term predominates upstream, and which predominates downstream?

- Hydrostatic force dominates upstream and momentum flux downstream
- Momentum flux dominates upstream and hydrostatic force downstream
- Momentum flux dominates at both sections
- The two terms are equal at both sections because the momentum function is conserved

## Explanation

Since $Q = b\,h\,v$ and $\rho = \dfrac{\gamma}{g}$, the ratio of momentum flux to hydrostatic force is
$$
\frac{\rho\,Q\,v}{\dfrac{\gamma\,b\,h^2}{2}} = \frac{2\,v^2}{g\,h} = 2\,Fr^2 .
$$
Upstream, $2\,Fr_1^2 = 32$, so **momentum flux** dominates. The sequent depth is $h_2 = \dfrac{h_1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 16} - 1\right) = 5.18\,h_1$. The discharge per unit width is unchanged across the jump and $Fr = \dfrac{q}{\sqrt{g}\,h^{3/2}}$, giving
$$
Fr_2 = Fr_1\left(\frac{h_1}{h_2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{4.00}{5.18^{\frac{3}{2}}} = 0.339 .
$$
Downstream the ratio is $2\,Fr_2^2 = 0.230$, so **hydrostatic force** dominates. The momentum function has the same value at both sections, but the relative contributions of its two terms change.
