In a rectangular channel on a mild slope, the normal depth is $h_0 = 1.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and the critical depth is $h_c = 0.70\,\mathrm{m}$. At one section the water depth is $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Does the water surface rise or fall downstream, and why?

- It falls because the numerator of the profile equation is negative and the denominator positive
- It rises because the numerator and denominator are both negative
- It falls because the numerator and denominator are both positive
- It stays parallel to the bed because the depth lies between $h_c$ and $h_0$

## Explanation

The measured depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ lies between $h_c$ and $h_0$, placing the flow in zone 2 on an M2 profile. The friction slope decreases as depth increases and equals the bed slope at $h = h_0$. Thus $h < h_0$ implies $J > i$ and the numerator $i - J$ is negative. The depth exceeds critical depth, so the flow is subcritical, the Froude number is less than $1$, and the denominator $1 - Fr^2$ is positive. Their ratio is therefore negative, $\dfrac{dh}{dx} < 0$, so the depth **decreases** downstream.
