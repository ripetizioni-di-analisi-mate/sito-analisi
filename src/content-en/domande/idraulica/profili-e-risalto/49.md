An M1 profile forms upstream of a weir on a mild slope. How does the specific energy $E = h + \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ change downstream?

- It decreases because total head always falls in the direction of flow
- It increases because in zone 1 the friction slope is smaller than the bed slope
- It remains constant because the discharge remains constant
- It decreases because velocity decreases downstream

## Explanation

Total head is $H = z + E$, with $\dfrac{dz}{dx} = -i$ and $\dfrac{dH}{dx} = -J$. Differentiating with respect to $x$ therefore gives
$$
\frac{dE}{dx} = i - J .
$$
On an M1 profile, the depth exceeds $h_0$, so the friction slope is less than the bed slope and $\dfrac{dE}{dx} > 0$. Specific energy therefore **increases** downstream. Total head decreases by $J$ per unit length, but the bed drops by the larger amount $i > J$. Specific energy, measured from the bed, consequently increases. Velocity head decreases downstream, but depth increases by a greater amount, consistent with the sign of $\dfrac{dE}{dx}$.
