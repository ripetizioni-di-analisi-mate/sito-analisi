Which of the following reaches can be analyzed using the equation for steady, gradually varied flow?

- The jet from the edge of a sluice gate to the vena contracta
- The hydraulic jump at the foot of an outlet
- The backwater extending several kilometers upstream of a weir, where the free surface rises only a few centimeters per hundred meters
- The nappe passing over the crest of a sharp-crested weir

## Explanation

The profile equation requires depth to change slowly enough for streamlines to remain nearly parallel to the bed. The pressure distribution is then hydrostatic and the friction slope can be evaluated from the uniform-flow formula at local depth. In the backwater upstream of a weir, the depth changes by only a few centimeters in a hundred meters, a variation small compared with $h$ over a distance many times the flow depth. Both assumptions hold and the reach has a **gradually varied flow profile**. Under a gate edge or above a sharp weir crest, streamlines curve sharply and pressure is nonhydrostatic. In a jump the depth increases greatly over a few meters and dissipation is concentrated, so use the integral momentum equation.
