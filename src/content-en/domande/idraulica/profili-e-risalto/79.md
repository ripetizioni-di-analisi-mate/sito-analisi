A rectangular channel has two long reaches, each on a mild slope. The upstream normal depth is $1.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and the normal depth on the gentler downstream slope is $1.45\,\mathrm{m}$. The critical depth is $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$. Which profile forms in the upstream reach near the change of slope?

- An M2 profile with depth decreasing from $1.10\,\mathrm{m}$ toward critical depth
- An M3 profile with depth increasing toward critical depth
- An S1 profile preceded by a jump
- An M1 profile with depth increasing from $1.10\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1.45\,\mathrm{m}$ at the slope change

## Explanation

In both reaches, normal depth is above critical depth, so flow is subcritical and profiles are traced upstream. The downstream reach is long and uniform, imposing depth $1.45\,\mathrm{m}$ at the slope change. Use this as the starting condition for the upstream reach. Upstream, $1.45\,\mathrm{m}$ exceeds both $h_{01} = 1.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_c$, placing the profile in zone 1. Its **M1** depth increases downstream and approaches $1.10\,\mathrm{m}$ asymptotically upstream. An M2 profile would require a steeper downstream reach with normal depth below $1.10\,\mathrm{m}$.
