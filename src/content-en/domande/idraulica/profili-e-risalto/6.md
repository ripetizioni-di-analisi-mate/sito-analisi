In a horizontal rectangular channel a very thin longitudinal splitter divides a stationary hydraulic jump into two identical lanes. Each receives half the discharge and half the width, with unchanged upstream depth. Neglecting additional friction, what happens to the theoretical conjugate depth in each lane?

- It is halved.
- It doubles.
- It decreases by a factor of $\sqrt2$.
- It stays the same as in the undivided channel.

## Explanation

The momentum balance per unit width depends on $q=Q/b$ and the upstream depth. Halving both $Q$ and $b$ preserves $q$, so the upstream Froude number and the conjugate depth stay the same.
