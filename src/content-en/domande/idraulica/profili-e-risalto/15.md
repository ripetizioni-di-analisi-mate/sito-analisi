In steady, gradually varied open-channel flow, where does the energy grade line lie relative to the free surface?

- It coincides with the free surface because gauge pressure there is zero
- It lies above the free surface by the velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$
- It lies below the free surface by the velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$
- It lies above the free surface by $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$

## Explanation

Gauge pressure is zero at the free surface, so the piezometric head there is $z + h$, where $z$ is the bed elevation and $h$ is the water depth. Pressure is distributed hydrostatically across the section, so the piezometric head is the same at every point in that section, and the free surface is the hydraulic grade line. Total head also includes the velocity head, giving
$$
H = z + h + \frac{v^2}{2g}
$$
so the energy grade line lies $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ above the free surface. The quantity $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ is twice the velocity head and does not appear in the expression for total head.
