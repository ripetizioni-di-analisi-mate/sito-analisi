The conjugate depths of a hydraulic jump in a horizontal rectangular channel are $h_1 = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_2 = 1.65\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the head loss across the jump?

- $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.33\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.485\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.582\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The head loss across a hydraulic jump is the difference between the specific energies at its two ends. Using the relation between sequent depths gives
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(h_2 - h_1)^3}{4\,h_1 h_2} .
$$
Substituting the specified depths gives
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(1.65 - 0.45)^3}{4 \cdot 0.45 \cdot 1.65} = \frac{1.728}{2.970} = 0.582\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The loss is positive, so total head decreases in the direction of flow, as the energy balance requires. The value $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is the difference between the two depths. The value $2.33\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the factor $4$ in the denominator, and $0.485\,\mathrm{m}$ squares the depth difference in place of cubing it.
