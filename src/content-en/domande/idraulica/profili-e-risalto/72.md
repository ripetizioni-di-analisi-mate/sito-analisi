A rectangular channel of width $b = 6.00\,\mathrm{m}$, roughness coefficient $k_s = 40\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0005$ carries $Q = 12.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. A weir across the channel imposes a depth of $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ immediately upstream, as shown. Using one direct step, locate the section where the depth is $2.80\,\mathrm{m}$.

::schema{solidi="0,0.60 12,0 12,-0.50 0,0.10;12,0 12.6,0 12.6,3.40 12,3.40" acqua="0,0.60 12,0 12,3.00 9,2.97 6,2.92 3,2.85 0,2.78" quote="11.5 0,11.5 3.00,3.00 m,-0.6" frecce="0.8,1.70,2.6,1.66,Q" etichette="12.3,3.70,weir;5.0,-0.10,i = 0.0005" riferimento=0 titolo="Backwater upstream of a weir"}

- $611\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream of the weir
- $611\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream of the weir
- $393\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream of the weir
- $290\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream of the weir

## Explanation

The critical depth is $0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ and the normal depth is $1.99\,\mathrm{m}$, so both specified depths lie in zone 1. The M1 profile is traced upstream from the weir. The specific energies are $E(3.00) = 3.0227\,\mathrm{m}$ and $E(2.80) = 2.8260\,\mathrm{m}$. The friction slopes are $0.000162$ and $0.000195$, with mean $\bar{J} = 0.000178$, yielding
$$
\Delta x = \frac{E(2.80) - E(3.00)}{i - \bar{J}} = \frac{2.8260 - 3.0227}{0.0005 - 0.000178} = -611\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The negative sign puts the section **upstream** of the weir, at a distance of $611\,\mathrm{m}$. This agrees with the increasing depth downstream on an M1 profile. The value $393\,\mathrm{m}$ neglects the friction slope, and $290\,\mathrm{m}$ adds the mean friction slope to the bed slope instead of subtracting it.
