A hydraulic jump forms some distance downstream of a sluice gate on a mild slope, preceded by an M3 profile. A weir later built far downstream raises the water level in the subcritical flow following the jump. In which direction does the jump move?

- Downstream, because the supercritical flow has more room to slow down
- It stays at the same section because downstream conditions do not influence the supercritical flow
- Downstream, because the momentum function of the subcritical flow decreases
- Toward the gate, because the subcritical depth increases and its conjugate depth decreases

## Explanation

The jump occurs where the depth on the M3 profile is conjugate to the downstream subcritical depth. In subcritical flow a higher water level gives a larger momentum function, requiring a smaller conjugate supercritical depth. The M3 profile increases in depth downstream from the gate, so that smaller depth occurs closer to the gate. The jump therefore **moves upstream**. If the tailwater continues rising, the conjugate depth eventually equals the depth leaving the gate. The jump then reaches the gate and submerges it. Downstream conditions cannot alter the supercritical M3 profile, but they do determine the jump location.
