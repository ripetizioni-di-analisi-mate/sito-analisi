A horizontal rectangular channel carries discharge per unit width $q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ at a supercritical depth of $h_1 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Consider the alternate depth of $h_1$, at which the subcritical flow has the same specific energy, and the conjugate depth of $h_1$, at which the subcritical flow has the same momentum function. Which statement is true?

- The conjugate depth is smaller than the alternate depth because the jump dissipates energy and specific energy increases with depth in subcritical flow
- The two depths coincide because they are both greater than critical depth
- The conjugate depth is greater than the alternate depth because the momentum function increases with depth
- The conjugate depth is greater than the alternate depth because the flow gains energy in the jump

## Explanation

Across a hydraulic jump, the momentum function is conserved and specific energy decreases by $\Delta E > 0$. Thus the sequent depth $h_2$ has specific energy $E(h_2) = E(h_1) - \Delta E$, below the energy of the alternate depth, $E(h_1)$. On the subcritical branch, specific energy increases with depth. The sequent depth is therefore **smaller than the alternate depth**. Here $Fr_1 = \dfrac{3.00}{0.50\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.50}} = 2.71$, giving
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.50}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 2.71^2} - 1\right) = 1.68\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The alternate depth satisfies $h + \dfrac{q^2}{2 g h^2} = E(h_1) = 2.33\,\mathrm{m}$ and equals $2.24\,\mathrm{m}$. Both exceed $h_c = 0.972\,\mathrm{m}$. The two depths differ because the sequent depth preserves the momentum function and the alternate depth preserves specific energy.
