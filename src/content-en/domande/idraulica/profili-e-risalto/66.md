In a horizontal rectangular channel, the upstream Froude number is exactly $1$. What does the conjugate-depth equation predict?

- A jump with downstream depth twice the upstream depth
- An undular jump dissipating half the upstream specific energy
- An infinite conjugate depth
- A conjugate depth equal to the upstream depth, so there is no jump and no head loss

## Explanation

For $Fr_1 = 1$, the sequent-depth equation gives
$$
\frac{h_2}{h_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8} - 1\right) = 1 ,
$$
so $h_2 = h_1$. The flow is critical, hence $h_1 = h_c$. At this depth the momentum function reaches its minimum, and only one depth corresponds to this value of $S$. The loss $\Delta E = \dfrac{(h_2 - h_1)^3}{4\,h_1 h_2}$ is zero, so **no hydraulic jump forms**. A jump requires $Fr_1 > 1$. When the upstream Froude number is only slightly above $1$, the jump is undular and dissipates very little energy.
