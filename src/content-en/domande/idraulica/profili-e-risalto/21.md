A gradually varied flow profile approaches critical depth. What follows from the flow profile equation?

- The derivative $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ tends to zero and the free surface becomes parallel to the bed
- The derivative $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ tends to a finite, nonzero value
- The magnitude of $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ tends to infinity, so the assumption of gradual variation breaks down
- The derivative $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ changes sign while remaining finite

## Explanation

The Froude number equals $1$ when the depth equals $h_c$, so as $h \to h_c$ the denominator $1 - Fr^2$ of the profile equation tends to zero. The numerator $i - J$ tends to a nonzero value because $h_c$ and $h_0$ differ on a bed whose slope is noncritical. Hence $\left|\dfrac{dh}{dx}\right| \to \infty$, and the computed water surface tends toward a direction perpendicular to the bed. Such rapid variation contradicts the derivation, so near critical depth the calculated profile indicates only the flow tendency. The actual transition occupies a short reach. For a change from supercritical to subcritical flow it takes the form of a jump.
