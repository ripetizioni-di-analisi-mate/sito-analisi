A mildly sloping rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 9.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The sluice gate releases water at a depth of $h_1 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and the downstream reach attains uniform flow at $h_0 = 1.21\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Where does the hydraulic jump occur?

::schema{solidi="0,0.90 12,0.30 12,-0.20 0,0.40;3.85,0.70 4.15,0.70 4.15,2.90 3.85,2.90" acqua="0,0.90 3.85,0.71 3.85,2.45 0,2.60;4.15,0.69 12,0.30 12,0.60 4.15,0.99" frecce="0.8,1.70,2.4,1.68,Q" etichette="4.0,3.15,gate;6.6,1.33,h_1 = 0.40 m;10.4,1.00,h_0 = 1.21 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Sluice gate in a mildly sloping rectangular channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- Immediately downstream of the gate, because the conjugate depth equals $h_0$
- Against the gate, which remains submerged because the tailwater is too deep
- Farther downstream, at the end of an M3 profile, because the tailwater is too shallow
- Nowhere, because the flow remains supercritical throughout the channel

## Explanation

The velocity immediately downstream of the gate is $v_1 = \dfrac{9.00}{4.00 \cdot 0.40} = 5.63\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the Froude number is $Fr_1 = 2.84$, so the flow is supercritical. The depth conjugate to $h_1$ is
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.40}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 2.84^2} - 1\right) = 1.42\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which exceeds the normal depth $h_0 = 1.21\,\mathrm{m}$. The tailwater is therefore shallower than the jump would require at the gate. Supercritical flow follows a rising M3 profile downstream until its depth is conjugate to $h_0$. The jump is **swept out**, and its distance from the gate follows from the direct step method.
