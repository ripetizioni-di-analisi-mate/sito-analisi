A large reservoir feeds a steep rectangular channel of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The reservoir surface lies $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bed at the entrance, as shown. Entrance losses are negligible. What discharge enters the channel?

::schema{solidi="-4,-0.6 0,-0.6 0,0 -4,0;0,0 9,-2.4 9,-3.0 0,-0.6;-4.6,-0.6 -4,-0.6 -4,2.5 -4.6,2.5" acqua="-4,0 0,0 0,1.80 -4,1.80" pelo="-4,-1,1.80," quote="0.4 0,0.4 1.80,1.80 m,-0.6" etichette="-2.0,0.80,reservoir;5.6,-0.65,i > i_c" frecce="1.2,0.15,2.6,-0.22,Q" riferimento=0 titolo="Reservoir feeding a steep channel of width $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $6.69\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $10.3\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $18.9\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $26.7\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

Water in the reservoir is almost at rest. The steep channel carries supercritical flow, so the flow passes through critical depth at the entrance, which acts as the control section. With negligible entrance losses the specific energy there equals the reservoir level above the entrance bed, $E = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$. For critical flow in a rectangular section $v^2 = g\,h_c$, so $E = h_c + \dfrac{h_c}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}\,h_c$ and
$$
h_c = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 1.80 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Q = b\sqrt{g\,h_c^3} = 2.50\,\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.20^3} = 10.3\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The value $18.9\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ treats the entire reservoir head as the critical depth, whereas $6.69\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ uses half that head. The value $26.7\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ applies Torricelli’s law to the full head, treating the entrance flow as having depth $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and velocity $\sqrt{2 g \cdot 1.80}$.
