An M1 backwater profile forms upstream of a weir on a mild slope. The depth immediately upstream of the weir is known. How can the direct step method be used to find the water depth $200\,\mathrm{m}$ farther upstream?

- Substitute $\Delta x = 200\,\mathrm{m}$ into the step equation and solve directly for the unknown depth
- Use normal depth because the profile has already reached it at a distance $200\,\mathrm{m}$ from the weir
- Start at normal depth and proceed downstream to the desired section
- Choose trial depths, calculate the distance from the weir for each, and select the depth giving a distance of $200\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The direct-step method calculates the distance between two sections of specified depth from
$$
\Delta x = \frac{E_{j+1} - E_j}{i - \bar{J}} .
$$
The unknown depth enters both specific energy and mean friction slope nonlinearly, so the step equation does not give depth explicitly. Choose trial depths and calculate the distance for each. This distance is negative because the section is upstream. Select the depth for which the magnitude of that distance is $200\,\mathrm{m}$, interpolating between trials if necessary. The weir is the control section and the flow is subcritical, so start there and work upstream. Normal depth is an asymptote reached only at infinite distance. It provides neither the unknown depth nor a starting section.
