A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, roughness coefficient $k_s = 60\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0010$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Using one direct step, find the distance between the section where the depth is $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and the section where it is $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$.

- $8.23\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.65\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.95\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.89\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocities at the two sections are $5.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $4.44\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The specific energies are therefore $E(0.40) = 1.6742\,\mathrm{m}$ and $E(0.45) = 1.4568\,\mathrm{m}$, and the friction slopes are $J(0.40) = 0.03229$ and $J(0.45) = 0.02258$. Their mean is $\bar{J} = 0.02743$, so the direct-step method gives
$$
\Delta x = \frac{E_{j+1} - E_j}{i - \bar{J}} = \frac{1.4568 - 1.6742}{0.0010 - 0.02743} = 8.23\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Both depths are below the critical depth $h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ and the normal depth $h_0 = 1.331\,\mathrm{m}$. The M3 profile therefore increases in depth downstream, and the positive step length confirms that the deeper section is downstream. The value $7.65\,\mathrm{m}$ adds the average friction slope to the bed slope. The value $6.95\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the friction slope at the initial section alone, and $1.89\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the change in depth in place of the change in specific energy.
