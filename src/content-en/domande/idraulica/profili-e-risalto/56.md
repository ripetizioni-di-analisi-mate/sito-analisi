A very wide river has hydraulic radius approximately equal to its water depth. It carries discharge per unit width $q = 4.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, with roughness coefficient $k_s = 30\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0003$. What is the normal depth?

- $3.90\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.77\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.40\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

For a unit-width strip in this wide channel, the flow area per unit width is $h$ and the hydraulic radius is $R = h$. The Gauckler–Strickler equation therefore gives
$$
q = k_s\,h\,h^{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{i} = k_s\,h^{\frac{5}{3}}\sqrt{i} .
$$
Solving explicitly for normal depth gives
$$
h_0 = \left(\frac{q}{k_s\sqrt{i}}\right)^{\frac{3}{5}} = \left(\frac{4.00}{30 \cdot 0.01732}\right)^{\frac{3}{5}} = 7.698^{\frac{3}{5}} = 3.40\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $3.90\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the exponent $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and omits the area factor $h$ in the discharge. The value $2.77\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the exponent $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $1.18\,\mathrm{m}$ is the critical depth $\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{q^2}{g}}$, which is independent of both roughness and bed slope.
