A sluice gate stands midway along a long, mildly sloping channel that ends in a bed drop, as shown. The flow leaving the gate is supercritical and its conjugate depth exceeds the normal depth $h_0$. The channel is long enough between structures for uniform flow to develop. In what sequence do the profiles occur from upstream to downstream?

::schema{solidi="0,1.00 16,0.40 16,-0.60 0,-0.60;16,-1.40 19,-1.40 19,-1.80 16,-1.80;7.85,1.00 8.15,1.00 8.15,3.20 7.85,3.20" frecce="1.0,2.10,2.8,2.03,Q" etichette="8.0,3.45,gate;17.6,-0.90,bed drop;4.0,1.20,i < i_c;12.0,0.95,i < i_c" riferimento=0 titolo="Mildly sloping channel with an intermediate sluice gate and a bed drop at the outlet"}

- M1, gate, M3, jump, uniform flow, M2, bed drop
- M1, gate, M3, jump, M1, bed drop
- M2, gate, M3, uniform flow, M2, bed drop
- M1, gate, jump, M3, uniform flow, M2, bed drop

## Explanation

The gate raises the upstream level above $h_0$, creating an **M1** profile traced upstream from the gate. The depth leaving the gate is below $h_c$, so the flow follows a rising **M3** profile. The conjugate depth of the issuing flow exceeds $h_0$. The jump is therefore swept downstream to the section on the M3 profile with depth conjugate to $h_0$. After the jump the flow is subcritical at depth $h_0$ and continues uniformly. Critical depth is established at the outlet drop, so the final reach has an **M2** profile that falls from $h_0$ to $h_c$ and is traced upstream from the outlet. An M1 profile immediately before the drop would require an outlet depth above $h_0$, and a jump immediately after the gate would require conjugate depth equal to $h_0$.
