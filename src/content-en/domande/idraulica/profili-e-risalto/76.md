A rectangular channel contracts gradually in width from $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ over a distance of $50\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant bed slope. Can the profile in this reach be calculated with the equation $\dfrac{dh}{dx} = \dfrac{i - J}{1 - Fr^2}$?

- Yes, because the width changes gradually
- No, because the equation applies to a prismatic channel, where the flow area depends only on water depth
- Yes, provided that the flow in the reach is subcritical
- No, because discharge is not conserved through a contraction

## Explanation

The derivation of the profile equation uses $\dfrac{dA}{dx} = B\,\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ for the derivative of flow area. This is valid only when $A$ depends on water depth alone, as in a **prismatic** channel. In the contraction the area is $A = b(x)\,h$, so $\dfrac{dA}{dx} = B\,\dfrac{dh}{dx} + h\,\dfrac{db}{dx}$ and an additional term arises from the width variation. A gradual contraction is needed to preserve hydrostatic pressure, but that alone is insufficient. Discharge is conserved through a steady contraction. Its profile can be calculated section by section from the specific-energy balance using the local width.
