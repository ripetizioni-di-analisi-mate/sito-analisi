A horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 12.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The measured depth downstream of a hydraulic jump is $h_2 = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the supercritical depth upstream of the jump?

- $0.453\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.35\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.939\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.25\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The downstream velocity is $v_2 = \dfrac{12.0}{4.00 \cdot 1.80} = 1.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
Fr_2 = \frac{1.67}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.80}} = 0.397
$$
The downstream flow is therefore subcritical. The relation between conjugate depths is symmetric. Starting from downstream, it gives
$$
h_1 = \frac{h_2}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8\,Fr_2^2} - 1\right) = \frac{1.80}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 0.397^2} - 1\right) = 0.453\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
This is below the critical depth $h_c = 0.972\,\mathrm{m}$, as required for supercritical flow. The upstream Froude number is $3.15$. The values $1.35\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.25\,\mathrm{m}$ result from omitting the term $-1$ or reversing its sign and both exceed $h_c$.
