How do the water surface and energy grade line change from upstream to downstream across a hydraulic jump on a horizontal bed?

- Both rise because the water depth increases
- The free surface rises by $h_2 - h_1$ and the energy grade line falls by the head loss $\Delta E$
- The free surface rises and the energy grade line stays horizontal because the jump conserves energy
- The free surface falls and the energy grade line rises

## Explanation

The depth increases from $h_1$ to $h_2 > h_1$, so the free surface rises by $h_2 - h_1$. On the horizontal bed the total head drop equals the decrease in specific energy across the jump,
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(h_2 - h_1)^3}{4\,h_1 h_2} > 0 .
$$
The energy grade line therefore falls by $\Delta E$. The velocity head falls by more than the water depth rises. The momentum function is conserved across the jump, while energy is dissipated.
