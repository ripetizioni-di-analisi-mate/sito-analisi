An M3 profile downstream of a sluice gate is computed by the direct step method. Starting at the gate outlet, the calculation proceeds downstream in equal depth increments toward critical depth. What happens to the horizontal step lengths as the depth approaches critical depth?

- They lengthen because the mean friction slope approaches the bed slope
- They stay the same because the depth increments are equal
- They shorten because specific energy changes very little with depth near critical depth
- They shorten because the friction slope tends to zero near critical depth

## Explanation

The length of each step is $\Delta x = \dfrac{E_{j+1} - E_j}{i - \bar{J}}$. For a rectangular channel with unit discharge $q = \dfrac{Q}{b}$, the derivative of specific energy with respect to depth is
$$
\frac{dE}{dh} = 1 - \frac{q^2}{g\,h^3} = 1 - Fr^2 ,
$$
which tends to zero as $h$ approaches $h_c$, so for the same depth increment the specific-energy difference $E_{j+1} - E_j$ becomes progressively smaller. On the M3 profile the water depth stays below $h_0$, the friction slope remains greater than $i$ and the denominator $i - \bar{J}$ stays away from zero. The horizontal steps therefore **shorten**, consistent with the behavior near $h_c$, where the magnitude of $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ tends to infinity. The friction slope approaches the bed slope only at $h \to h_0$, which the M3 profile never reaches. The friction slope remains positive because the water is flowing.
