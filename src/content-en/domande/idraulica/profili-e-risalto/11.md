A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, roughness coefficient $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0009$ carries $Q = 7.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Water leaves a sluice gate at a depth of $h_1 = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$. The downstream reach is long enough to establish uniform flow at $h_0 = 1.88\,\mathrm{m}$. Where does the hydraulic jump occur?

- Against the gate, which remains submerged
- Immediately downstream of the gate, with upstream depth $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$
- Farther downstream, at the end of an M3 profile
- At the section where the water depth reaches critical depth

## Explanation

The velocity leaving the gate is $v_1 = 7.14\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the Froude number is $Fr_1 = 3.85$. The depth conjugate to $h_1$ is therefore
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.35}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 3.85^2} - 1\right) = 1.74\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
This conjugate depth is below the normal depth $h_0 = 1.88\,\mathrm{m}$. The tailwater is therefore deeper than the jump requires at the gate, so it pushes the jump upstream against the gate, leaving the gate **submerged**. The free-jump conjugate-depth formula used above no longer applies in that form. A swept-out jump preceded by an M3 profile would require the depth conjugate to $h_1$ to exceed $h_0$.
