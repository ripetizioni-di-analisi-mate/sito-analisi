Water passes down a spillway into a horizontal stilling basin. The upstream reservoir surface lies $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the basin floor and the discharge per unit width is $q = 4.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, as shown. Neglecting losses, what is the supercritical depth at the foot of the spillway?

::schema{solidi="0,-0.6 2.0,-0.6 2.0,1.80 2.6,1.80 3.6,1.30 4.8,0.50 6.0,0 13,0 13,-0.6 6.0,-0.6 6.0,-0.6;-4,-0.6 0,-0.6 0,-0.2 -4,-0.2" acqua="-4,-0.2 0,-0.2 0,-0.6 2.0,-0.6 2.0,1.80 2.6,1.80 2.6,2.40 2.0,2.90 -4,3.00" tratteggi="-4,3.00 13,3.00" quote="12.4 0,12.4 3.00,3.00 m,0.6" frecce="7.0,0.30,8.6,0.30,q" etichette="-2.0,1.50,reservoir;9.5,-0.35,tank" riferimento=0 titolo="Spillway discharging into a horizontal stilling basin"}

- $0.521\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.737\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.581\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.18\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The reservoir velocity is negligible and head losses are neglected. At the toe of the spillway, the specific energy relative to the basin floor is therefore $E = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the depth $h_1$ satisfies
$$
h_1 + \frac{q^2}{2 g\,h_1^2} = 3.00\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The flow at the toe is supercritical, so the required root is less than the critical depth $\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{q^2}{g}} = 1.18\,\mathrm{m}$. Writing $h_1 = \dfrac{q}{\sqrt{2 g\,(3.00 - h_1)}}$ and starting with $h_1 = 0$ gives the successive values $0.521$, $0.574$, $0.580$, and $0.581\,\mathrm{m}$, so $h_1 = 0.581\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0.521\,\mathrm{m}$ is the first iterate, which neglects the flow depth in comparison with the specific energy. The value $0.737\,\mathrm{m}$ uses $g$ in place of $2g$ in this first step. The value $1.18\,\mathrm{m}$ is the critical depth at $Fr = 1$, whereas the flow here is supercritical.
