Why is the integral momentum equation used to find the conjugate depths of a hydraulic jump instead of Bernoulli’s equation?

- Because discharge is not conserved between the upstream and downstream ends of the jump
- Because the integral momentum equation also gives the length of the jump
- Because the pressure distribution is not hydrostatic at the sections bounding the jump
- Because the energy balance contains the unknown head loss, while the momentum equation does not

## Explanation

A jump dissipates energy over only a few meters, and the loss is unknown beforehand. The energy balance between the end sections therefore has two unknowns, the downstream depth and the head loss. The streamwise momentum balance involves the end-section forces, the weight of water in the control volume and the boundary reaction. On a horizontal bed, neglecting bed resistance, the last two have zero streamwise component. This gives $S(h_1) = S(h_2)$, one equation for one unknown, from which the conjugate-depth formula follows. Once both depths are known, the energy balance gives the head loss.
