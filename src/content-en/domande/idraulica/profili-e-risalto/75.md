An M2 profile upstream of a bed drop is computed by the direct step method, starting at critical depth at the outlet and proceeding upstream. Why can the calculation never reach a section at which the depth is exactly $h_0$?

- Because as the depth approaches $h_0$ the mean friction slope tends to $i$ and the step length increases without bound
- Because the flow becomes supercritical upstream of the outlet
- Because the specific energy is undefined for $h = h_0$
- Because the direct step method applies only to supercritical flow

## Explanation

Each step has length $\Delta x = \dfrac{E_{j+1} - E_j}{i - \bar{J}}$. As the endpoint depth approaches $h_0$, the friction slope approaches the bed slope. The denominator $i - \bar{J}$ tends to zero while the specific-energy difference remains nonzero, so the step length increases without bound. This is the numerical expression of $h_0$ as an **asymptote**, reached only at infinite distance because $\dfrac{dh}{dx} \to 0$. Stop the calculation at a depth close to $h_0$. Flow on the M2 profile remains subcritical because the depth exceeds $h_c$, and the direct step method applies to both subcritical and supercritical profiles.
