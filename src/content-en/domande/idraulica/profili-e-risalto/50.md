Upstream of a weir, a rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, roughness coefficient $k_s = 40\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0010$ carries $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. At a section in the backwater reach, the water depth is $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, greater than the normal depth $h_0 = 1.61\,\mathrm{m}$. How does the free-surface elevation relative to a horizontal datum change over a distance of $100\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream?

- It rises by $7.22\,\mathrm{cm}$
- It falls by $10.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- It falls by $2.78\,\mathrm{cm}$
- It remains at the same elevation

## Explanation

At the given section, $A = 12.5\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $R = \dfrac{12.5}{10.0} = 1.25\,\mathrm{m}$, and $v = 0.800\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Consequently,
$$
J = \frac{0.800^2}{40^2 \cdot 1.25^{\frac{4}{3}}} = 0.000297 ,
\qquad
Fr^2 = \frac{0.800^2}{9.81 \cdot 2.50} = 0.0261 .
$$
The profile equation then gives
$$
\frac{dh}{dx} = \frac{0.0010 - 0.000297}{1 - 0.0261} = 0.000722 .
$$
The free-surface elevation is $z + h$ and the bed slope is given by $\dfrac{dz}{dx} = -i$. The free-surface slope is therefore $0.000722 - 0.0010 = -0.000278$, so in $100\,\mathrm{m}$ the free surface **falls by $2.78\,\mathrm{cm}$**. The rise of $7.22\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the increase in water depth relative to the bed. A drop of $10.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ would require the water surface to be parallel to the bed, as in uniform flow. A horizontal surface is approached only in the limit of very large depth.
