A rectangular channel has a critical bed slope, so its normal depth equals its critical depth. A downstream barrier raises the water level above critical depth. Which profile forms upstream of the barrier?

- An M1 profile approaching $h_0$ asymptotically upstream
- An S1 profile preceded by a jump
- A C3 profile with depth increasing downstream
- A C1 profile with a nearly horizontal water surface

## Explanation

When $i = i_c$, the normal-depth line $h_0$ and the critical-depth line $h_c$ coincide. Only two zones remain, and the profiles are designated $C$. The depth imposed by the barrier is above $h_0 = h_c$, so the flow occupies zone 1 and the profile is **C1**. In this zone the numerator and denominator of the profile equation are positive and both vanish at the same depth. Their ratio remains close to $i$, giving $\dfrac{dh}{dx} \simeq i$. The bed falls by $i$ per unit length, so the free surface is nearly horizontal. M1 and S1 profiles require slopes below and above critical, respectively. A C3 profile lies below critical depth.
