A reservoir feeds a mildly sloping rectangular channel long enough for its downstream end to have no influence near the entrance. Entrance losses are negligible. What depth is established at the entrance?

- Critical depth, because the water accelerates from rest at the entrance
- The height of the reservoir surface above the entrance bed
- The channel normal depth, with discharge determined by the available energy
- Two thirds of the height of the reservoir surface above the entrance bed

## Explanation

Normal flow on a mild slope is subcritical, so the flow depth depends on downstream conditions. There is no structure in the reach after the entrance and the downstream end is distant. Uniform flow therefore extends to the entrance, where the depth is $h_0$. The discharge must give this uniform flow a specific energy equal to the reservoir head $H$. It must therefore satisfy both equations
$$
\begin{cases}
H = h_0 + \dfrac{Q^2}{2 g\,b^2 h_0^2} \\[8pt]
Q = k_s\,b\,h_0\,R_0^{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{i}
\end{cases}
$$
The first relation is the energy balance from reservoir to entrance, and the second is the Gauckler–Strickler equation. Critical depth, equal to two thirds of the reservoir level above the entrance bed, is established there only on a steep slope, where flow changes from subcritical to supercritical. The entrance depth could equal the full reservoir level only at zero velocity.
