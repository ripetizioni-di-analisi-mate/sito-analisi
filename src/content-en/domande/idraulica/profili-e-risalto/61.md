A horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 4.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. A vertical sluice gate opens to a height of $a = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and the emerging jet has contraction coefficient $C_c = 0.61$, as shown. Neglecting losses at the gate, what is the depth immediately upstream?

::schema{solidi="0,0 12,0 12,-0.5 0,-0.5;4.85,0.40 5.15,0.40 5.15,3.20 4.85,3.20" acqua="0,0 4.85,0 4.85,2.70 0,2.70;5.15,0 9.0,0 9.0,0.244 6.0,0.244 5.15,0.40" frecce="1.0,1.20,2.6,1.20,Q" etichette="5.0,3.45,gate;2.4,2.95,h_m;7.2,0.75,a = 0.40 m;9.0,1.35,C_c = 0.61" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical sluice gate in a horizontal channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.09\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.25\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.15\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At the vena contracta the depth is $h_1 = C_c\,a = 0.244\,\mathrm{m}$. With a unit discharge of $q = \dfrac{Q}{b} = 1.50\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, the velocity is $v_1 = 6.15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the specific energy is $E_1 = 0.244 + 1.927 = 2.170\,\mathrm{m}$. The bed is horizontal and head loss is neglected, so the specific energy is the same upstream of the gate. The upstream depth $h_m$ therefore satisfies
$$
h_m + \frac{q^2}{2 g\,h_m^2} = 2.170\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The upstream flow is subcritical, so the required root exceeds the critical depth $0.612\,\mathrm{m}$. Starting with $h_m = 2.170\,\mathrm{m}$ and iterating $h_m = 2.170 - \dfrac{0.1147}{h_m^2}$ gives $h_m = 2.15\,\mathrm{m}$ in two steps. The value $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ neglects contraction by taking $h_1 = a$. The value $4.09\,\mathrm{m}$ uses $\dfrac{v_1^2}{g}$ in place of $\dfrac{v_1^2}{2g}$. The value $1.25\,\mathrm{m}$ is the depth conjugate to $h_1$ across a hydraulic jump. It does not determine the level upstream of the gate.
