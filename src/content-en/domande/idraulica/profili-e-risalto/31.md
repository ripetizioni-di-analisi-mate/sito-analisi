A horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 15.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a supercritical depth of $h_1 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. What fraction of the upstream specific energy is dissipated in a hydraulic jump?

- $36.6\%$
- $63.4\%$
- $47.5\%$
- $57.7\%$

## Explanation

The upstream velocity is $v_1 = \dfrac{15.0}{5.00 \cdot 0.40} = 7.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the Froude number is $Fr_1 = \dfrac{7.50}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.40}} = 3.79$ and the sequent depth is
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.40}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 3.79^2} - 1\right) = 1.95\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The specific energy of the supercritical flow is $E_1 = h_1 + \dfrac{v_1^2}{2g} = 0.40 + 2.87 = 3.27\,\mathrm{m}$, and the energy loss across the jump is
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(h_2 - h_1)^3}{4\,h_1 h_2} = \frac{(1.95 - 0.40)^3}{4 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 1.95} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Their ratio is therefore $\dfrac{1.20}{3.27} = 0.366$, or $36.6\%$ of the upstream specific energy. The value $63.4\%$ is the fraction of energy remaining downstream. The value $57.7\%$ divides the loss by the downstream specific energy, and $47.5\%$ uses the depth increase $h_2 - h_1$ as the head loss.
