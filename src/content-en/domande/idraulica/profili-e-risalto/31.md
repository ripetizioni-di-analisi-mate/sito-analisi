In a rectangular channel with a horizontal bed, a supercritical flow of depth $h_1 = 0.200\,\mathrm{m}$ and Froude number $Fr_1 = 5.00$ forms a hydraulic jump. What is the loss of specific energy across the jump?

- $1.32\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0912\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The downstream sequent depth follows from the sequent-depth formula,
$$
h_2 = \frac{h_1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8\,Fr_1^2} - 1\right) = \frac{0.200}{2}\left(\sqrt{201} - 1\right) = 1.318\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The loss of specific energy in the jump is then
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(h_2 - h_1)^3}{4\,h_1 h_2} = \frac{(1.318 - 0.200)^3}{4 \cdot 0.200 \cdot 1.318} = \frac{1.397}{1.054} = 1.32\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The jump therefore dissipates about half of the upstream specific energy, which is $E_1 = h_1 + \dfrac{Fr_1^2\,h_1}{2} = 2.70\,\mathrm{m}$. Taking the difference $h_2 - h_1$ as the loss would give $1.12\,\mathrm{m}$, dropping the factor $4$ would give $5.30\,\mathrm{m}$, and using $Fr_1$ instead of its square in the sequent-depth formula would give $0.0912\,\mathrm{m}$.
