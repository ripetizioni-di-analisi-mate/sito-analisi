A mildly sloping rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 5.40\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a normal depth of $h_0 = 1.40\,\mathrm{m}$. It discharges into a lake whose surface lies $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the outlet bed, as shown. Which profile develops in the final reach?

::schema{solidi="0,0.50 9,0 9,-1.60 0,-1.60;9,-1.60 14,-1.60 14,-2.00 9,-2.00" acqua="9,-1.60 14,-1.60 14,1.00 9,1.00" pelo="10.5,14,1.00,lake" quote="8.6 0,8.6 1.00,1.00 m,-0.6" frecce="1.0,1.75,2.8,1.65,Q" etichette="4.5,-0.40,i < i_c" riferimento=0 titolo="Mildly sloping channel discharging into a lake"}

- An M2 profile ending at depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at the outlet
- An M2 profile ending at critical depth $0.691\,\mathrm{m}$ at the outlet
- An M1 profile ending at depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at the outlet
- Uniform flow up to the outlet, followed by a jump in the lake

## Explanation

The critical depth is
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{5.40^2}{9.81 \cdot 3.00^2}} = 0.691\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The lake level $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ exceeds $h_c$, so the outlet flow is subcritical and the lake imposes depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ there. The outlet is the control section. The imposed depth lies between $h_c$ and $h_0$, placing the final reach in zone 2. Its **M2** profile falls from $h_0$ to $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and is traced upstream from the outlet. Critical depth could be established at the outlet only if the lake level were below $h_c$. An M1 profile would require a lake level above $h_0$.
