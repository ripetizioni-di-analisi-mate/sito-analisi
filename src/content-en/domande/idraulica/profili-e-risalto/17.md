The gradually varied flow equation cannot be used to analyze a hydraulic jump. Which assumption of the model breaks down in a jump?

- The discharge must be constant in time, but varies in a jump
- The channel must be prismatic, but its width changes in a jump
- The depth must vary slowly enough for pressure to remain hydrostatic, but in a jump the depth changes over just a few meters
- The correction factor must be $1$, but its value in a jump is $2$

## Explanation

Gradually varied flow assumes that streamlines are nearly parallel to the bed. This allows the pressure distribution to be treated as hydrostatic and the friction slope to be calculated from the uniform-flow formula at the local depth. In a hydraulic jump, the depth changes from $h_1$ to $h_2$ over just a few meters. The streamlines curve sharply, so this assumption fails. Discharge is still constant because the flow is steady, and the channel remains prismatic. The integral momentum equation relates the forces at the end sections to the change in momentum flux across the jump without requiring the head loss.
