Can a gradually varied profile cross critical depth in passing from supercritical to subcritical flow?

- Yes, provided that the bed slope is small enough
- Yes, on any mild bed
- Yes, provided that the discharge is constant in time
- No, the transition occurs through a hydraulic jump, a form of rapidly varied flow

## Explanation

The denominator $1 - Fr^2$ in the profile equation vanishes at $h = h_c$. As depth approaches critical, the derivative grows without bound in magnitude and the assumption of gradual variation breaks down. A gradually varied profile therefore stays in its original zone. For example, a flow leaving a gate on a mild slope at a depth below $h_c$ follows an M3 profile with increasing depth downstream. It cannot continue as an M2 profile, whose depth decreases downstream. The transition from supercritical to subcritical flow occurs through a **hydraulic jump**, in which depth increases from $h_1$ to $h_2$ over a few meters and energy dissipation is concentrated.
