A mildly sloping rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 4.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a normal depth of $h_0 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. A full-width, sharp-crested rectangular weir at the channel outlet has crest height $p = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bed and discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.41$, as shown. Neglecting the approach velocity head, what depth is established immediately upstream of the weir, and which profile develops in the channel?

::schema{solidi="0,0.40 10,0 10,-0.50 0,-0.10;10,0 10.3,0 10.3,1.00 10,1.00" acqua="0,0.40 10,0 10,1.67 7,1.66 4,1.64 0,1.62" frecce="0.8,1.10,2.4,1.04,Q" quote="10.3 0,10.3 1.00,p,0.5" etichette="10.1,2.15,weir;12.4,0.50,p = 1.00 m;4.5,-0.75,i < i_c" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel ending at a sharp-crested weir"}

- $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$, with an M1 profile
- $0.672\,\mathrm{m}$, with an M2 profile
- $1.67\,\mathrm{m}$, with an M1 profile
- $1.85\,\mathrm{m}$, with an M1 profile

## Explanation

For a rectangular weir, discharge is related to the head $h$ above the crest by
$$
Q = \mu\,b\,h\sqrt{2 g h} ,
$$
so the section immediately upstream of the weir is a control section. Solving for the head gives
$$
h = \left(\frac{Q}{\mu\,b\sqrt{2g}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = \left(\frac{4.00}{0.41 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 4.43}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 0.672\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The water depth above the channel bed is therefore $p + h = 1.00 + 0.672 = 1.67\,\mathrm{m}$. This exceeds $h_0 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, which in turn exceeds the critical depth $\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{Q^2}{g\,b^2}} = 0.467\,\mathrm{m}$. The flow is therefore in zone 1 on an **M1** profile traced upstream from the weir. The value $0.672\,\mathrm{m}$ gives only the head above the crest and omits its height above the bed. The values $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.85\,\mathrm{m}$ result, respectively, from using $\dfrac{3}{2}$ in place of $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\sqrt{g h}$ in place of $\sqrt{2 g h}$.
