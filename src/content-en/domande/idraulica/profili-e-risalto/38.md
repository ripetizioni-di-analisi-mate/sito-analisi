The conjugate depths of a hydraulic jump in a rectangular channel are $h_1 = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_2 = 1.55\,\mathrm{m}$. What jump length does Smetana’s formula predict?

- $2.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $11.4\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The jump length depends on the turbulent flow structure and cannot be determined from the balance equations alone. It is estimated from laboratory measurements. For a rectangular channel, Smetana’s formula gives
$$
L \simeq 6\,(h_2 - h_1) = 6\,(1.55 - 0.35) = 7.20\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
an estimate valid for $2 < Fr_1 < 10$. The ratio of the two depths gives $Fr_1 = 3.47$, within this range. The values $2.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $9.30\,\mathrm{m}$ each multiply only one depth by $6$, while $11.4\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the sum of the depths in place of their difference.
