A hydraulic jump forms on a chute with bed slope $10\%$. Can the conjugate depths be calculated using $\dfrac{h_2}{h_1} = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8\,Fr_1^2} - 1\right)$?

- Yes, because the formula is independent of bed slope
- Yes, provided that the upstream flow is supercritical
- No, because the weight of water in the control volume has a streamwise component that remains in the momentum equation
- No, because on a sloping bed discharge is not conserved across the jump

## Explanation

The sequent-depth formula follows from the streamwise integral momentum equation for a horizontal bed with negligible bed resistance. The weight acts perpendicular to the channel axis, leaving only the end-section pressure forces and momentum fluxes. Thus $S(h_1) = S(h_2)$. On a sloping bed, the water within the jump has a component of weight along the flow, so the momentum function **is not conserved** and the formula must be modified. This weight component depends on the water volume in the jump, hence on the jump shape and length, and must be estimated separately. Discharge is conserved in both cases because the flow is steady.
