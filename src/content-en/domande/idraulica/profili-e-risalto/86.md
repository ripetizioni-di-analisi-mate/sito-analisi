A sluice gate stands midway along a long, steep channel, as shown. It raises the upstream water level above critical depth and releases water at a depth below normal. What features form upstream and downstream of the gate?

::schema{solidi="0,2.40 12,0.20 12,-0.30 0,1.90;5.85,1.75 6.15,1.75 6.15,4.00 5.85,4.00" frecce="0.8,3.10,2.4,2.82,Q" etichette="6.0,4.25,gate;3.6,2.45,i > i_c;9.0,1.55,i > i_c" riferimento=0 titolo="Sluice gate midway along a steep channel"}

- An M1 profile upstream and an M3 profile followed by a jump downstream
- Uniform flow upstream as far as the gate and an S2 profile downstream
- A jump followed by an S1 profile up to the gate, then an S3 profile tending to $h_0$ downstream
- An S1 profile without a jump upstream, and a jump followed by uniform flow downstream

## Explanation

Normal flow on the steep bed is supercritical. Upstream, the gate imposes a depth greater than $h_c$, placing the flow in zone 1. The resulting S1 profile is subcritical and traced upstream from the gate. It cannot join the arriving supercritical flow continuously without crossing $h_c$. A **jump** therefore precedes the S1 profile, which continues to the gate. Downstream the depth is below $h_0$ and $h_c$, producing an **S3** profile traced downstream, with depth increasing asymptotically toward $h_0$. M profiles require a mild bed. Uniform flow cannot extend up to the gate because the gate imposes a depth different from $h_0$.
