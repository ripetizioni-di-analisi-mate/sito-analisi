In a horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, the depths measured upstream and downstream of a hydraulic jump are $h_1 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_2 = 1.52\,\mathrm{m}$, respectively. What is the channel discharge?

- $2.39\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $10.2\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $7.18\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $5.18\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

Conservation of the momentum function across the jump, written in terms of discharge per unit width $q = \dfrac{Q}{b}$, gives
$$
h_1 h_2 (h_1 + h_2) = \frac{2\,q^2}{g} ,
$$
and hence
$$
q = \sqrt{\frac{g\,h_1 h_2 (h_1 + h_2)}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{9.81 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 1.52 \cdot 1.92}{2}} = 2.39\,\mathrm{m^2/s} .
$$
The channel discharge is therefore $Q = q\,b = 2.39 \cdot 3.00 = 7.18\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $7180\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The value $2.39\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ is the discharge per unit width and still has to be multiplied by the channel width. The value $10.2\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ omits the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ under the square root.
