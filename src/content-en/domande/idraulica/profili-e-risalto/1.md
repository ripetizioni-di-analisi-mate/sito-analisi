A rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, with $k_s = 55\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0007$, carries a discharge of $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Find the normal depth.

- $0.742\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.45\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.21\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.33\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

For uniform flow the energy slope equals the bed slope. The normal depth $h_0$ therefore satisfies the Gauckler–Strickler equation
$$
Q = k_s\,b\,h_0\left(\frac{b\,h_0}{b + 2 h_0}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{i} .
$$
Substituting the data and solving by trial gives $h_0 = 1.45\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ is the critical depth $h_c = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{Q^2}{g\,b^2}}$, which depends only on discharge and width and is independent of roughness and slope. The values $1.21\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.33\,\mathrm{m}$ result, respectively, from setting the hydraulic radius equal to the flow depth and from counting only one sidewall when calculating the wetted perimeter as $b + h_0$.
