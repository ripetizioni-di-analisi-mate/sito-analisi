An M2 profile forms in a mildly sloping channel ending at a bed drop. How does the distance between the energy grade line and the free surface change downstream?

- It increases because the depth decreases and the velocity increases
- It decreases because the flow loses energy
- It remains constant because the discharge remains constant
- It falls to zero at the outlet, where the depth is critical

## Explanation

The free surface is the hydraulic grade line. The energy grade line lies above it by the velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, which is the required separation. On the M2 profile the depth decreases downstream from $h_0$ toward $h_c$, so the flow area also decreases. At constant discharge, the mean velocity $v = \dfrac{Q}{A}$ therefore increases, and the separation between the lines **increases** downstream. The outlet depth is critical, where the velocity head is $\dfrac{h_c}{2}$. This is the largest separation along the profile and is greater than zero.
