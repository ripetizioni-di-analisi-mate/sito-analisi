Measurements at sections $A$ and $B$ of a constant-diameter pipe give total heads $H_A = 18.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and $H_B = 19.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and piezometric heads $h_A = 18.25\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_B = 18.95\,\mathrm{m}$. In which direction does the water flow, and what is the head loss?

- From $A$ to $B$, with head loss $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $A$ to $B$, with head loss $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $B$ to $A$, with head loss $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $B$ to $A$, with head loss $0.55\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The total head of a real fluid decreases in the direction of flow because viscous stresses dissipate mechanical energy. Since $H_B > H_A$, section $B$ is upstream and water flows from $B$ toward $A$. The head loss is the difference in total head,
$$
\Delta H_{B,A} = H_B - H_A = 19.10 - 18.40 = 0.70\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
At both sections, the piezometric head is lower than the total head by $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$. This velocity head is the same at both sections because the diameter is constant, so the piezometric heads also differ by $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$.
