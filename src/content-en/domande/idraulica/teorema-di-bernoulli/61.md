The constant-diameter pipe shown has length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ and connects reservoir $A$, with surface elevation $40.00\,\mathrm{m}$, to reservoir $B$, with surface elevation $20.00\,\mathrm{m}$. For the first $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ the centerline is horizontal at elevation $32.00\,\mathrm{m}$, then slopes down toward $B$. Head loss is uniform along the pipe. Neglect velocity head and local losses. At what distance from $A$ does gauge pressure become zero?

- $1200\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1500\,\mathrm{m}$
- $700\,\mathrm{m}$
- $800\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With velocity heads and local losses neglected, the hydraulic and energy grade lines coincide. The head loss is uniform, so the line is straight between the free surfaces of reservoirs $A$ and $B$. Its downward slope is
$$
J = \frac{40.00 - 20.00}{2000} = 0.0100 ,
$$
so the piezometric head a distance $x$ from $A$ is $h = 40.00 - 0.0100\,x$. Gauge pressure is zero where this line intersects the pipe centerline, that is, where $h = 32.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus
$$
x = \frac{40.00 - 32.00}{0.0100} = 800\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
From that point to the end of the horizontal reach, a distance of $700\,\mathrm{m}$, the hydraulic grade line lies below the centerline and gauge pressure is negative. The value $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance from the same intersection to $B$.
