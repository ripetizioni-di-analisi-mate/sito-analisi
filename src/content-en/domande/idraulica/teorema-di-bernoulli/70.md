A penstock carries a discharge $Q = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ from a hydroelectric plant’s headwater reservoir to its turbine. The reservoir surface lies $180\,\mathrm{m}$ above the water level in the turbine tailrace, and head loss in the penstock is $30.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Taking the tailrace water level as the datum, what hydraulic power reaches the turbine inlet?

- $3.53\,\mathrm{MW}$
- $2.94\,\mathrm{MW}$
- $0.589\,\mathrm{MW}$
- $0.300\,\mathrm{MW}$

## Explanation

Total head at the reservoir free surface is $180\,\mathrm{m}$, so **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** gives $H = 180 - 30.0 = 150\,\mathrm{m}$ at the turbine inlet. Total head is energy per unit weight, and the weight of fluid passing through the section per unit time is $\gamma Q$. The hydraulic power is therefore
$$
P = \gamma\,Q\,H = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 150 = 2943\,\mathrm{kW} = 2.94\,\mathrm{MW} .
$$
The power dissipated in the pipe is $\gamma\,Q\,\Delta H = 0.589\,\mathrm{MW}$. These two powers add up to $3.53\,\mathrm{MW}$, the power available at the reservoir free surface. The turbine converts a fraction of the incoming hydraulic power into mechanical power according to its efficiency.
