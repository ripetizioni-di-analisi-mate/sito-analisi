Two outlets of equal area are installed at the same depth in the vertical wall of a constant-level tank. One is a short, smoothly converging nozzle and the other a circular thin-wall orifice. Which delivers the larger discharge?

- The thin-wall orifice, because a thin wall causes no head loss
- The converging nozzle, because the jet from the thin-wall orifice contracts to an area smaller than the hole
- Both deliver the same discharge because exit speed depends only on depth
- The thin-wall orifice, because velocity is greater at the vena contracta

## Explanation

Apart from small losses, **Bernoulli’s equation** between the free surface and either jet gives the same exit speed $\sqrt{2 g h}$ because both openings are at the same depth $h$. Streamlines leave the smoothly contoured nozzle parallel to one another, so the jet occupies the entire exit area and $Q = A\sqrt{2 g h}$. At a thin, sharp-edged orifice, fluid approaches along the wall from all directions and cannot turn abruptly. The jet therefore contracts downstream to a fraction of the orifice area. With the same exit speed, discharge is proportional to the area of the jet, so the **converging nozzle** delivers more water. The contraction coefficient quantifies this effect and is examined in the chapter on flow through openings.
