A closed tank holds water with its surface $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom and air at gauge pressure $-20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ above the water. Taking the bottom as the datum, what is the piezometric head at the bottom?

- $3.96\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8.04\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.96\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The water is at rest, so the **hydrostatic pressure law** gives the same piezometric head throughout the tank. Evaluate it at the free surface, where the elevation is $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the gauge pressure equals that of the air above the water. The pressure head is $\dfrac{-20}{9.81} = -2.04\,\mathrm{m}$, giving
$$
h = 6.00 - 2.04 = 3.96\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
At the bottom the elevation is zero, so the pressure head is $3.96\,\mathrm{m}$ and the gauge pressure is $38.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of water adds $58.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$ to the air’s gauge pressure of $-20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Treating the partial vacuum as a positive gauge pressure would give $8.04\,\mathrm{m}$.
