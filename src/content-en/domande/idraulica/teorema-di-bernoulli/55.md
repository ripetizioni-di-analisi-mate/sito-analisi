A downward water jet leaves a faucet with diameter $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting air resistance and surface tension, what is the jet diameter $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ below the faucet outlet?

- $14.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $16.9\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $20.1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $23.7\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The jet is surrounded by air, so gauge pressure is zero at both sections. **Bernoulli’s equation** between the faucet outlet and the lower section therefore contains only elevation and velocity terms,
$$
0.20 + \frac{v_0^2}{2g} = \frac{v^2}{2g}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v = \sqrt{2.00^2 + 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.20} = 2.81\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
By the **continuity equation**, velocity times diameter squared is constant. Thus
$$
d = d_0\sqrt{\frac{v_0}{v}} = 20\sqrt{\frac{2.00}{2.81}} = 16.9\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
As the jet falls it accelerates and narrows. The diameter ratio depends on the square root of the velocity ratio. Using the velocity ratio directly would give $14.2\,\mathrm{mm}$.
