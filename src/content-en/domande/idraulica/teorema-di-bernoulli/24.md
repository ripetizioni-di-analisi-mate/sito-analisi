Above the profile of a constant-diameter pipe carrying a given discharge, the two lines shown are parallel and fall downstream. Which is the hydraulic grade line?

::schema{nodi="M,2.6,2,-;N,14,2,-" condotte="M-N,0.7" linee="2.6,5.4 14,4.2;2.6,4.6 14,3.4;1.6,0 15,0" etichette="1.5,5.5,line a;1.5,4.7,line b;3.4,0.4,datum" riferimento=0 titolo="Two lines plotted above the profile of a constant-diameter pipe"}

- Line $a$, because piezometric head includes velocity head
- Line $a$, because the hydraulic grade line is always above the energy grade line
- Line $b$, because its separation from line $a$ equals the velocity head
- It cannot be determined because the lines are parallel

## Explanation

Total head $H = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma} + \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ exceeds piezometric head $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ by the velocity head, which is positive. The **energy grade line** therefore lies a vertical distance $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ above the **hydraulic grade line**, making the latter the lower line. The pipe diameter is constant, so the **continuity equation** gives the same velocity and velocity head at every section. The lines are therefore parallel. Their vertical separation gives the velocity head, and their common downward slope gives the head loss per unit length.
