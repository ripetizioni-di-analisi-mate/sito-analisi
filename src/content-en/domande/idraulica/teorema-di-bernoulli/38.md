A horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ contains an orifice plate with hole diameter $D_2 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharge coefficient $C = 0.60$. Its manometer measures a piezometric head difference $\Delta h = 2.40\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the discharge?

- $55.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $33.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $23.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $134\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The areas are $A_1 = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.00785\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving an area ratio of $0.250$ and a value of $0.968$ for the factor $\sqrt{1 - 0.0625}$. For an **orifice plate**, multiply the Venturi discharge expression by the discharge coefficient to obtain
$$
Q = C\,\frac{A_2}{\sqrt{1 - \left(\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}\right)^2}}\;\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta h}
= 0.60 \cdot \frac{0.00785}{0.968} \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.40} = 0.0334\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
which corresponds to $33.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The coefficient accounts for contraction of the jet downstream of the orifice and the energy subsequently dissipated. Omitting it would give $55.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$, two-thirds greater than the actual discharge.
