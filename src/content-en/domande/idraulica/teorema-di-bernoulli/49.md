A pipeline carries oil of specific gravity $0.85$. At a section with centerline elevation $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the datum, gauge pressure is $250\,\mathrm{kPa}$. How far above the pipe axis would oil rise in a piezometer?

- $25.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $21.7\,\mathrm{m}$
- $30.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $35.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The piezometer contains the fluid flowing through the pipe, so the height above the pipe centerline must be calculated using the specific weight of oil. That specific weight is $\gamma_o = 0.85 \cdot 9.81 = 8.34\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$, giving
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma_o} = \frac{250}{8.34} = 30.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Using the specific weight of water would give $25.5\,\mathrm{m}$, the height of water needed to produce the same pressure. The lighter oil requires a taller column. The value $35.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is the elevation of the liquid level in the piezometer, which equals the piezometric head at the section.
