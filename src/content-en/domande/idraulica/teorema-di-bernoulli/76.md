In which of the following cases can the ideal-fluid Bernoulli equation be applied between points $1$ and $2$ on the same pathline with negligible error?

- Point $1$ is just upstream and $2$ just downstream of an operating pump
- Both points are in a pipe while a gate valve closes rapidly
- Point $1$ is just upstream and $2$ just downstream of an orifice plate where the jet contracts and then expands abruptly
- Both points are close together in the free jet leaving a nozzle

## Explanation

**Bernoulli’s equation** assumes steady flow of an inviscid, incompressible fluid with gravity as the only body force. The pump blades do work on the fluid and add energy, so the downstream total head exceeds the upstream value. During rapid closure of a gate valve, the velocity changes with time and the flow is unsteady. Downstream of an orifice plate, abrupt expansion of the jet produces eddies that dissipate appreciable energy, so viscous effects cannot be neglected. In the **free jet**, the flow is steady and pressure is atmospheric. Dissipation and air resistance are negligible over a short distance, so total head is conserved.
