A Venturi meter in a horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ has throat diameter $D_2 = 125\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Neglecting head loss, what is the difference in piezometric head between the upstream section and the throat?

- $1.14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.22\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.28\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.571\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The areas are $A_1 = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.0123\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The **continuity equation** therefore gives velocities $v_1 = 1.22\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 4.89\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since the pipe is horizontal, **Bernoulli’s equation for one-dimensional flow** gives
$$
\Delta h = \frac{v_2^2}{2g} - \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{4.89^2 - 1.22^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 1.14\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Using only the throat velocity head and omitting the upstream velocity head from the subtraction would give $1.22\,\mathrm{m}$. The actual head difference is the difference in liquid levels between two piezometers connected to the two sections.
