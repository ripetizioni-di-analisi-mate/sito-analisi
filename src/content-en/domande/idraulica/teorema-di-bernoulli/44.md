A pump inlet is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the free surface of its constant-level supply reservoir. The suction-line velocity head is $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and the suction-line loss $1.10\,\mathrm{m}$. Atmospheric pressure is equivalent to $10.33\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. What is the absolute inlet pressure expressed as a head of water?

::schema{acqua="0.2,0.5 4.6,0.5 4.6,3 0.2,3" solidi="0,0 4.8,0 4.8,0.5 0,0.5;0,0 0.2,0 0.2,3.4 0,3.4;4.6,0 4.8,0 4.8,3.4 4.6,3.4" pelo="0.2,4.6,3" condotte="2.4 1.2-2.4 7-7 7,0.55" pompa="7.7,7,P" quote="1.2 3,1.2 7,4.00 m,0" etichette="4.4,7.8,suction pipe" riferimento=0 titolo="Suction pipe of a pump located above the free surface"}

- $5.23\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.03\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-5.30\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Use absolute pressures in the energy balance between the free surface, where pressure is atmospheric and velocity is negligible, and the pump inlet. **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** gives
$$
\frac{p_{\mathrm{abs}}}{\gamma} = \frac{p_{\mathrm{atm}}}{\gamma} - z_a - \frac{v^2}{2g} - \Delta H
= 10.33 - 4.00 - 0.20 - 1.10 = 5.03\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pump is above the free surface, so the elevation rise, velocity head, and head loss all reduce the atmospheric head available at the inlet. The corresponding gauge pressure head is $5.03 - 10.33 = -5.30\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, so the inlet pressure is below atmospheric. It is nevertheless well above the vapor pressure of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, equivalent to a head of $0.24\,\mathrm{m}$.
