In the proof of Bernoulli’s theorem, the work–energy theorem is applied to an ideal fluid inside an elementary streamtube between two sections normal to the pathline. Why do the forces on the lateral surface do zero work?

- The pressure forces on the lateral surface cancel in pairs
- The weight of fluid is normal to the lateral surface
- Velocity is tangent to the lateral surface and the only surface force, due to pressure, is normal to it
- The flow is steady, so the forces on the lateral surface are constant in time

## Explanation

The lateral surface of a streamtube is formed by streamlines, so the fluid velocity is tangent to the surface at every point. An inviscid fluid exerts no shear stress. The surrounding fluid therefore exerts only a pressure force normal to the surface. Power is the dot product of force and local velocity. Here the vectors are perpendicular, so the power and the corresponding work are zero. Gravity contributes a separate work term $-\gamma\,dV\,(z_2 - z_1)$. Under steady conditions, the change in kinetic energy reduces to that associated with transfer of the mass $dm$. In a real fluid, viscous shear does negative work, giving rise to **head loss**.
