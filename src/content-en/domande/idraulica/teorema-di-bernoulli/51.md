An open tank maintains a water depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The vertical constant-diameter pipe shown extends $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ downward from its floor and discharges to the atmosphere. Neglecting head loss, what is the gauge pressure at inlet section $1$ at the tank floor?

::schema{acqua="0.2,3 4.2,3 4.2,5 0.2,5" solidi="0,2.8 1.8,2.8 1.8,3 0,3;2.2,2.8 4.4,2.8 4.4,3 2.2,3;0,2.8 0.2,2.8 0.2,5.6 0,5.6;4.2,2.8 4.4,2.8 4.4,5.6 4.2,5.6" pelo="0.2,4.2,5" condotte="2 3-2 0,0.4" frecce="2,-0.2,2,-1.2,v" etichette="2.55,2.6,1" quote="5.2 3,5.2 5,2.00 m,0;1 0,1 3,3.00 m,0" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical outlet pipe beneath the bottom of a tank"}

- $19.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-49.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Take the outlet as the elevation datum. The free surface is then at elevation $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and section $1$ is at elevation $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The outlet jet is exposed to the atmosphere, so **Bernoulli’s equation** between the free surface and the outlet gives $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Since the diameter is constant, the **continuity equation** gives the same velocity and velocity head at section $1$. Applying Bernoulli’s equation between the free surface and section $1$ then gives
$$
5.00 = 3.00 + \frac{p_1}{\gamma} + 5.00
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} = -3.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which gives $p_1 = -29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The hydrostatic bottom pressure $19.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ applies to water at rest. In the flowing water, the velocity head $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ must be subtracted, leaving the pipe inlet below atmospheric pressure.
