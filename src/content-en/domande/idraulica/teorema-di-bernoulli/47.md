Compare three points relative to the same datum. Point $P$ is in a reservoir at rest, $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below its free surface at elevation $7.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Point $Q$ is on a pipe centerline at elevation $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, with gauge pressure $30\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and speed $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Point $R$ is in a free water jet at elevation $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with speed $7.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which point has the greatest total head?

- Point $P$
- Point $Q$
- All three points have the same total head
- Point $R$

## Explanation

The water in the reservoir is at rest, so the velocity head is zero. By the **hydrostatic pressure law**, $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ equals the free-surface elevation, giving $H_P = 7.00\,\mathrm{m}$. In the pipe, add the three head components to obtain
$$
H_Q = 3.00 + \frac{30}{9.81} + \frac{2.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 3.00 + 3.06 + 0.20 = 6.26\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The jet is exposed to the atmosphere, so gauge pressure at point $R$ is zero and
$$
H_R = 4.50 + \frac{7.80^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 4.50 + 3.10 = 7.60\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The greatest total head therefore occurs at point **$R$**. Point $P$ has the highest pressure, but elevation and velocity also contribute to total head.
