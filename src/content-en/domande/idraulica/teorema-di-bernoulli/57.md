A pipe from a constant-level reservoir discharges to the atmosphere at mean speed $v$. Where does the hydraulic grade line pass at the outlet?

- Through the pipe centerline
- Through the reservoir free surface
- $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ above the pipe centerline
- $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ below the pipe centerline

## Explanation

The outlet jet is exposed to the atmosphere. The gauge pressure is therefore zero, and the piezometric head $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ equals the centerline elevation $z$. The **hydraulic grade line** consequently passes through the pipe centerline at the outlet. The energy grade line lies $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ higher because the outlet velocity head is the only contribution above the centerline elevation. At the upstream end the reservoir free surface gives the initial total head. Head losses reduce it before the outlet, so the elevation difference between the free surface and the outlet centerline is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} + \Delta H$.
