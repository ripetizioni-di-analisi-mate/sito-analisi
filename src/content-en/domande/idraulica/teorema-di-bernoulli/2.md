A pipe carries discharge $Q = 90\,\mathrm{L/s}$. At section $1$ the diameter is $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$, centerline elevation $z_1 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$, and gauge pressure $p_1 = 350\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At section $2$ the corresponding values are $D_2 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, $z_2 = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and $p_2 = 300\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the head loss between the sections?

::schema{nodi="A,1,1.6,-;B,5,1.6,-;C,8,4.3,-;D,12,4.3,-" condotte="A-B,0.8;B-C,0.6;C-D,0.6" linee="0,0 13,0" etichette="2.6,2.6,D_1;9.4,5.2,D_2" quote="2 0,2 1.6,1.50 m,-0.9;11.4 0,11.4 4.3,4.00 m,0.9" punti="3.4,1.6,1,pieno;10.4,4.3,2,pieno" riferimento=0 titolo="Two pipe sections showing the elevations of their centerlines"}

- $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-2.26\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.26\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The mean velocities are $v_1 = \dfrac{0.090}{0.0707} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = \dfrac{0.090}{0.0314} = 2.86\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving velocity heads of $0.083\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.418\,\mathrm{m}$. The total heads at the two sections are therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_1 &= 1.50 + \frac{350}{9.81} + 0.083 = 37.26\,\mathrm{m} , \\[6pt]
H_2 &= 4.00 + \frac{300}{9.81} + 0.418 = 35.00\,\mathrm{m} .
\end{aligned}
$$
Total head decreases in the direction of flow, so the water flows from section $1$ to section $2$. The head loss is the difference $\Delta H = H_1 - H_2 = 2.26\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting the velocity heads would give $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$ because the smaller section has a greater velocity head than the upstream section.
