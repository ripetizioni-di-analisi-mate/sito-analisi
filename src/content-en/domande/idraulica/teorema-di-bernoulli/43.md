Two constant-level reservoirs with surface elevation difference $\Delta z = 7.00\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by a pipe submerged at both ends. At the actual discharge the distributed friction loss is $6.20\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the sum of the local losses?

- $6.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At the free surface of each reservoir, gauge pressure is zero and velocity is negligible, so total head equals the free-surface elevation. Applying **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** between the two surfaces gives
$$
\Delta z = \Delta H ,
$$
so the entire elevation difference is dissipated as head loss. The total loss $7.00\,\mathrm{m}$ consists of pipe friction and local losses, so the local losses sum to $7.00 - 6.20 = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$. In long pipelines this remainder is a small fraction of the total and is neglected in the long-pipeline approximation.
