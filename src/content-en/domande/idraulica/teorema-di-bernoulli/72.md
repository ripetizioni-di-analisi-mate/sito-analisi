A horizontal constant-diameter pipe carries water at speed $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ through a valve. Gauges immediately upstream and downstream read $320\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $275\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting friction loss over the short distance between gauges, what is the valve loss coefficient $\xi$?

- $11.3$
- $4.59$
- $45.0$
- $22.5$

## Explanation

The two sections are at the same elevation and, by the **continuity equation**, have the same velocity. The elevation and velocity terms therefore cancel in **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss**, leaving the loss equal to the difference in pressure heads,
$$
\Delta H = \frac{320 - 275}{9.81} = 4.59\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The local head loss is $\Delta H = \xi\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, with $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \dfrac{2.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.204\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
\xi = \frac{4.59}{0.204} = 22.5 .
$$
The dimensionless loss coefficient expresses the loss as a multiple of the velocity head. The value $4.59$ is the head loss in meters, while $11.3$ results from dividing by $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$.
