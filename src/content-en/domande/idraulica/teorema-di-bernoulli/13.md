Two constant-level reservoirs are connected by a pipe of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$. The surface elevations differ by $\Delta z = 9.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and total head loss is $\Delta H = 45\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. What discharge does the pipe carry?

- $939\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $140\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $99.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $560\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

At both reservoir surfaces, gauge pressure is zero and velocity is negligible. The total heads therefore equal the surface elevations. Applying the **energy equation with head loss** between the two surfaces gives $\Delta z = \Delta H$. The entire elevation difference is accounted for by losses.
$$
9.00 = 45\,\frac{v^2}{2g}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.200\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The velocity head gives $v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.200} = 1.98\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The discharge is therefore
$$
Q = v A = 1.98 \cdot 0.0707 = 0.140\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 140\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
Neglecting losses would give $939\,\mathrm{L/s}$, corresponding to a speed of $13.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is unrealistic for the pipe.
