A nozzle projects a water jet into the air at speed $12.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and angle $60^\circ$ above the horizontal. Neglecting air resistance, what maximum height does the jet reach above the nozzle?

- $7.34\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.83\,\mathrm{m}$
- $11.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Gauge pressure is zero along the jet, so **Bernoulli’s equation** between the nozzle exit and the highest point contains only elevation and velocity terms,
$$
\frac{v_0^2}{2g} = z + \frac{u^2}{2g} .
$$
Gravity is the only force on the jet and acts vertically, so the horizontal velocity component remains $v_0 \cos 60^\circ = 6.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At the highest point the velocity is horizontal, giving $u = 6.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Thus
$$
z = \frac{12.0^2 - 6.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = \frac{108}{19.62} = 5.50\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The jet retains the velocity head associated with its horizontal motion. Setting its speed to zero at the highest point would give $7.34\,\mathrm{m}$, the height reached by a vertical jet.
