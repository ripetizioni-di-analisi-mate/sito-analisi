A Venturi meter and an orifice plate have equal constriction diameters and are tested in the same pipe at the same discharge. How do the meters differ?

- The orifice plate dissipates less energy and has a higher discharge coefficient
- The meters have the same discharge coefficient because their constriction diameters are equal
- The orifice plate dissipates more energy and has a lower discharge coefficient
- The orifice plate needs no discharge coefficient because the hole area is known

## Explanation

In a Venturi meter the flow follows the walls of the converging section and then the diffuser, so energy dissipation is small. At an orifice plate the jet contracts after passing through the opening and then expands abruptly, dissipating much more energy. The **discharge coefficient** accounts for jet contraction and dissipation. Typical values are about $0.98$ for a Venturi meter and only $0.60$ for an orifice plate. For the same measured piezometric head difference, the orifice plate therefore passes less flow. The coefficients are determined experimentally and depend on the diameter ratio and the Reynolds number.
