A closed tank contains water beneath air at gauge pressure $p_0 = 80\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The water level stays constant, and a nozzle of diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ discharges water from depth $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. Neglecting head loss, what is the exit speed?

- $5.42\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $13.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9.73\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Take the nozzle centerline as the datum. At the free surface the velocity is negligible and the gauge pressure equals that of the air above the water. The total head at the surface is therefore
$$
H = 1.50 + \frac{80}{9.81} = 1.50 + 8.15 = 9.65\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
At the outlet the elevation is zero and the jet is open to the atmosphere, so gauge pressure is zero. The total head is entirely velocity head. **Bernoulli’s equation** then gives
$$
v = \sqrt{2 g H} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 9.65} = 13.8\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The compressed air contributes more head than the elevation difference. Omitting its contribution gives $5.42\,\mathrm{m/s}$, less than half the actual speed.
