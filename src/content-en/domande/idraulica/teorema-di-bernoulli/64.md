A horizontal channel carries water of depth $h_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and mean speed $v_1 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Farther downstream it narrows gradually, and water depth at the constriction is $h_2 = 1.90\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting head loss, what is the mean speed at the constriction?

- $1.40\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.22\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.72\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.05\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The flow is one-dimensional at both sections. In **Bernoulli’s equation for one-dimensional flow**, the piezometric head at each section can therefore be evaluated at the free surface, where gauge pressure is zero. It equals the free-surface elevation. With the channel bed as datum, the total heads are
$$
H_1 = 2.00 + \frac{1.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 2.051\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
H_2 = 1.90 + \frac{v_2^2}{2g} .
$$
Losses are neglected, so $H_1 = H_2$ and hence $\dfrac{v_2^2}{2g} = 0.151\,\mathrm{m}$, which gives $v_2 = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.151} = 1.72\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The drop in free-surface elevation is the piezometric head converted to velocity head. The value $1.05\,\mathrm{m/s}$ follows from applying continuity with constant channel width, whereas the width changes here.
