A straight constant-diameter pipe of length $800\,\mathrm{m}$ carries steady water flow at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The measured total head downstream is $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ lower than upstream. Which assumption of Bernoulli’s theorem fails?

- The fluid is inviscid
- The fluid is incompressible
- The flow is steady
- The discharge is constant along the pipe

## Explanation

**Bernoulli’s equation** gives constant total head for steady flow of an inviscid, incompressible fluid under gravity alone. The flow is stated to be steady, and water is treated as incompressible. The discharge is the same at every section by the **continuity equation**, which also applies to real fluids. The decrease in total head is therefore attributable to viscosity. Viscous shear along the walls opposes the flow and dissipates some mechanical energy as heat. This decrease is the **head loss**. For a real fluid, $H_1 = H_2 + \Delta H_{1,2}$ with $\Delta H_{1,2} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$.
