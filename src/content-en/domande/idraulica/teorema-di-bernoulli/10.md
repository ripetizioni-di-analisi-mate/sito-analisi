A Venturi meter in a horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ has throat diameter $D_2 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$. A differential mercury manometer reads a level difference $\Delta = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$. Mercury has specific gravity $13.6$. Neglecting head loss, what is the discharge?

- $13.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $49.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $51.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $34.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The **differential manometer** relates the difference in the mercury levels to the difference in piezometric head, giving
$$
\Delta h = \Delta\left(\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma} - 1\right) = 0.150 \cdot 12.6 = 1.89\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The areas are $A_1 = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.00785\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the area ratio is $0.250$. The **Venturi meter** equation gives
$$
Q = \frac{0.00785}{\sqrt{1 - 0.0625}} \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.89} = 0.0494\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $49.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$. In the manometer, the mercury displaces a water column of the same height. The conversion factor is therefore $\dfrac{\gamma_m}{\gamma} - 1 = 12.6$. Using $13.6$ would give $51.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
