A water pipe rises in the direction of flow. What can be said about the total head between its upstream and downstream sections?

- It increases downstream because elevation increases
- It decreases downstream, regardless of the elevation profile
- It stays the same because the elevation rise offsets head loss
- It decreases only along downward-sloping pipe sections

## Explanation

**Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** gives $H_1 = H_2 + \Delta H_{1,2}$, with $\Delta H_{1,2} \geq 0$. Viscous shear opposes the motion and removes mechanical energy from the flow. Head loss is therefore subtracted when proceeding downstream, whatever the shape of the pipe. Total head decreases even along a rising pipe. As elevation increases, the combined pressure and velocity heads must decrease by the elevation rise plus the head loss. Piezometric head itself can either rise or fall, depending on how the velocity head changes along the pipe.
