After leaving a turbine, water flows through a draft tube that discharges below the constant tailwater level. The turbine outlet is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above tailwater and the speed there is $3.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Head loss from that section to tailwater, including the exit loss, is $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the gauge pressure at the turbine outlet?

- $-28.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-33.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-23.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-39.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Take the tailwater surface as the elevation datum. Gauge pressure there is zero and velocity is negligible, so the total head is zero. Turbine outlet section $1$ lies upstream of the tailwater. Applying **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** from section $1$ to the tailwater therefore places the loss on the downstream side of the balance,
$$
3.00 + \frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{3.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0 + 0.60
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} = 0.60 - 3.00 - 0.459 = -2.86\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which gives $p_1 = -28.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Like the inlet of a pump above the free surface, this outlet operates below atmospheric pressure and must be checked for **cavitation**. Neglecting the loss would give $-33.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Adding it on the side of section $1$ would give $-39.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
