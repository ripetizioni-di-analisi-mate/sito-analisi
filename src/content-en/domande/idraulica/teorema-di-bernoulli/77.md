A piezometer protrudes into the flow instead of ending flush with the inner pipe wall. Its tip is bent to face upstream. How does the water level in the tube compare with the local piezometric head?

- Equal, because the tube connects to the same pipe section
- Higher, because the flow stagnates at the opening and the tube acts as a Pitot tube
- Lower, because the flow passing the opening lowers the pressure in the tube
- Lower, because the bent tube dissipates some head

## Explanation

A **piezometer** tap is flush with the wall and perpendicular to the flow. It therefore leaves the flow undisturbed and its liquid level gives the piezometric head $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$. When the opening faces upstream, fluid arriving there comes to rest and the opening becomes a stagnation point. Applying **Bernoulli’s equation** along the streamline ending there shows that stagnation pressure exceeds undisturbed static pressure by $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho u^2$, where $u$ is the local velocity. The water in the tube is at rest, so by the **hydrostatic pressure law** the liquid level exceeds the piezometric head by the velocity head $\dfrac{u^2}{2g}$, as in a **Pitot tube**. Since there is no flow in the tube, there is no head loss in it.
