In the device shown, a horizontal pipe contracts to a throat of diameter $D_2 = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$, then expands through a diffuser to diameter $D_3 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharges to the atmosphere. A vertical tube connects the throat to an open tank whose surface is $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ below the pipe axis. Neglecting head loss, what minimum discharge raises water from the tank to the throat?

- $15.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $26.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $1.74\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $6.96\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The throat $2$ and outlet $3$ are at the same elevation, and $p_3 = 0$. Applying the **continuity equation** and **Bernoulli’s equation** together gives
$$
\begin{cases}
v_2 A_2 = v_3 A_3 \\[4pt]
\dfrac{p_2}{\gamma} + \dfrac{v_2^2}{2g} = \dfrac{v_3^2}{2g}
\end{cases}
$$
The area ratio is the square of the diameter ratio, so $v_2 = 4\,v_3$ and $\dfrac{p_2}{\gamma} = -15\,\dfrac{v_3^2}{2g}$. The water in the small tube is at rest. By the **hydrostatic pressure law**, it can reach the throat only if $\dfrac{p_2}{\gamma} \leq -0.60\,\mathrm{m}$. This requires $\dfrac{v_3^2}{2g} \geq 0.040\,\mathrm{m}$, or $v_3 \geq 0.886\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the minimum discharge is
$$
Q = v_3 A_3 = 0.886 \cdot 0.00785 = 0.00696\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 6.96\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
Using the diameter ratio in place of the area ratio would give $\dfrac{p_2}{\gamma} = -\dfrac{v_3^2}{2g}$ and a discharge of $26.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
