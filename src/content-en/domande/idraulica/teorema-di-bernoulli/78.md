A constant-diameter pipe rises uniformly. Between two taps $50.0\,\mathrm{m}$ apart along its centerline, the elevation increases by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Water flows from the lower tap to the higher. A differential manometer containing mercury of specific gravity $13.6$ shows a level difference $\Delta = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$, with the mercury lower on the upstream side. What is the energy gradient?

- $0.0101$
- $0.0501$
- $0.000800$
- $0.0400$

## Explanation

The **differential manometer** measures the difference in piezometric head between the taps regardless of their elevation difference. Thus
$$
h_1 - h_2 = \Delta\left(\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma} - 1\right) = 0.040 \cdot 12.6 = 0.504\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pipe diameter is constant. By the **continuity equation**, both sections have the same velocity head, so **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** gives a head loss of $h_1 - h_2$. The hydraulic gradient is therefore
$$
J = \frac{0.504}{50.0} = 0.0101 .
$$
The elevation rise $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is already included in the piezometric head difference. Adding it to the reading would give $0.0501$, while $0.0400$ is the slope of the pipe centerline.
