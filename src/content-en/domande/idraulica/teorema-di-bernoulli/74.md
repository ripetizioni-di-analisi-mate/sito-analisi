A centerline Pitot tube in a circular pipe reads $\Delta$ above a piezometer at the same section. Discharge is estimated as $Q = A\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta}$, where $A$ is the cross-sectional area. How does this estimate compare with the actual discharge?

- Equal to the actual discharge, because the Pitot tube measures the section mean speed
- Greater than the actual discharge, because the centerline speed exceeds the mean speed
- Less than the actual discharge, because the centerline speed is below the mean speed
- Equal to the actual discharge only for laminar flow

## Explanation

A **Pitot tube** measures the local velocity at its opening because the head difference $\Delta$ is the velocity head of the fluid brought to rest there. In a pipe the velocity is zero at the wall and reaches its maximum at the centerline, where it exceeds the mean velocity $\dfrac{Q}{A}$. The product $A\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta}$ is therefore **greater** than the actual discharge. In laminar flow the centerline velocity is twice the mean, so the estimate doubles the discharge. In turbulent flow the velocity profile is more uniform, giving a smaller overestimate. To determine discharge with a Pitot tube, take velocity readings at several points across the section.
