A horizontal pipe contracts from diameter $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 200\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting head loss, what is the gauge pressure in the narrower section?

- $193\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $207\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $186\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

From the **continuity equation**, the speeds are $v_1 = \dfrac{0.070}{0.0491} = 1.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = \dfrac{0.070}{0.0177} = 3.96\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving velocity heads of $0.104\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.800\,\mathrm{m}$. Since the pipe is horizontal, the elevation terms cancel in **Bernoulli’s equation**, leaving
$$
\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = \frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} - \frac{v_2^2}{2g} = 20.39 + 0.104 - 0.800 = 19.69\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Multiplying by specific weight gives $p_2 = 19.69 \cdot 9.81 = 193\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The flow accelerates through the throat, so its pressure drops. Reversing the sign of the velocity-head difference gives $207\,\mathrm{kPa}$, which would be higher than the upstream pressure.
