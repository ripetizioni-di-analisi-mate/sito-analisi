A full horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries one-dimensional flow. The centerline gauge pressure is $30\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the gauge pressure at the top of the cross section?

- $30.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $28.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $32.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $26.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Under the conditions for **Bernoulli’s equation for one-dimensional flow**, pressure varies hydrostatically across the section, so $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ has the same value at every point. The top of the pipe is $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$ above the centerline, a distance equal to the radius. Thus
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = \frac{30}{9.81} - 0.200 = 3.058 - 0.200 = 2.858\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Multiplying by the specific weight gives $p = 2.858 \cdot 9.81 = 28.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The elevation difference is one radius, so using the diameter would give $26.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
