A horizontal pipe contracts from diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Neglecting head loss, how far does the hydraulic grade line drop from the upstream section to the narrower section?

::schema{nodi="A,1,2,-;B,7,2,-;C,13,2,-" condotte="A-B,0.9;B-C,0.45" frecce="1.3,2,2.5,2,Q" punti="4.5,2,1,pieno;10.5,2,2,pieno" etichette="4.5,3.2,D_1 = 300 mm;10.5,2.9,D_2 = 150 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Diameter reduction in a horizontal pipe"}

- $0.408\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.765\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.383\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.434\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The **continuity equation** gives velocities $v_1 = \dfrac{0.050}{0.0707} = 0.707\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = \dfrac{0.050}{0.0177} = 2.83\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with velocity heads $0.026\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.408\,\mathrm{m}$. Since losses are neglected, the energy grade line is horizontal. The drop in the hydraulic grade line therefore equals the increase in velocity head,
$$
\Delta h = \frac{v_2^2}{2g} - \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 0.408 - 0.026 = 0.383\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Using only the velocity head at the throat would omit the upstream velocity head from the subtraction and overestimate the drop as $0.408\,\mathrm{m}$.
