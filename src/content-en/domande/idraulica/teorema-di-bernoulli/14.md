A pump draws discharge $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from a constant-level reservoir through a pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$. Its inlet is $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the reservoir surface and the suction-line head loss is $\Delta H = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the inlet gauge pressure?

- $-59.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $41.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-57.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-50.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The mean speed is $v = \dfrac{0.060}{0.0314} = 1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $0.186\,\mathrm{m}$. Take the reservoir free surface as the datum. Its gauge pressure is zero and its velocity is negligible. The **energy equation with head loss** between the surface and the pump inlet gives
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = -5.00 - 0.186 - 0.90 = -6.09\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Multiplying by specific weight gives $p = -6.09 \cdot 9.81 = -59.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The inlet operates below atmospheric pressure because the elevation rise, velocity head, and head loss are all subtracted from the head at the free surface.
