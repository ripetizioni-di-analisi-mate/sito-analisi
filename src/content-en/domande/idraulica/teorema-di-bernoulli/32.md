A pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ leaves a constant-level reservoir and discharges to the atmosphere with its outlet axis $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. The total head loss is $\Delta H = 5.70\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the discharge?

- $341\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $53.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $305\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $76.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Take the outlet centerline as the elevation datum. At the reservoir free surface, gauge pressure is zero and velocity is negligible, so the total head is $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$. At the outlet the jet is exposed to the atmosphere, leaving only the velocity head. **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** therefore gives
$$
6.00 = \frac{v^2}{2g} + 5.70
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.30\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
giving outlet velocity $v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.30} = 2.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and discharge $Q = v A = 2.43 \cdot 0.0314 = 0.0762\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $76.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Neglecting the head loss would give a velocity of $10.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$, inconsistent with the losses in this pipe.
