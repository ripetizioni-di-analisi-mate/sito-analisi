Water leaves an open constant-level tank through a short converging nozzle at speed $6.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting head loss, how far below the free surface is the nozzle axis?

- $3.67\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.83\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.917\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.306\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

For **discharge from a constant-level tank**, the exit velocity is $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$, where $h$ is the depth of the center of the opening below the free surface. Squaring and solving for depth gives
$$
h = \frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{6.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = \frac{36.0}{19.62} = 1.83\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The required depth therefore equals the jet’s velocity head. It is the distance a body must fall from rest in a vacuum to reach velocity $6.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Omitting the factor $2$ in the denominator and dividing by gravitational acceleration alone would give $3.67\,\mathrm{m}$.
