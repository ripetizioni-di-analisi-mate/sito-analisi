A Venturi meter in a horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ has throat diameter $D_2 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$. The upstream gauge pressure is $50\,\mathrm{kPa}$, atmospheric pressure is $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the vapor pressure of water is $2.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting losses, what maximum discharge can pass without vapor forming at the throat?

- $96.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $79.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $136\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $1230\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Vapor pressure is an absolute pressure, so use absolute pressures throughout the calculation. The upstream pressure is $p_1 = 50 + 101.3 = 151.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the lowest allowable throat pressure is $2.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The maximum piezometric head difference is therefore
$$
\Delta h = \frac{151.3 - 2.34}{9.81} = 15.18\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The area ratio is $\dfrac{1}{9}$, so the **Venturi meter** equation gives
$$
Q = \frac{0.00785}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{81}}} \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 15.18} = 0.136\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 136\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
$$
with velocities $17.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the throat and $1.93\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the pipe. Limiting the throat pressure to atmospheric pressure would give $79.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Negative gauge pressure is permissible down to the point where absolute pressure reaches the vapor pressure.
