A vertical piezometer open at the top is connected at the crown of a horizontal pipe. The centerline gauge pressure is $-20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is observed in the tube?

- Water rises to $2.04\,\mathrm{m}$ above the pipe centerline
- Water comes to rest $2.04\,\mathrm{m}$ below the pipe centerline
- The tube cannot sustain a water column and admits air into the pipe
- Water stays at the tap level, where pressure is atmospheric

## Explanation

For water at rest in a tube, the **hydrostatic pressure law** puts its free surface at the local piezometric head, which is $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = \dfrac{-20}{9.81} = -2.04\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the pipe centerline. The required level is below the centerline, but the tube starts at the top of the pipe and extends upward. The water column therefore has no equilibrium level within the tube. Pressure at the tap is below atmospheric, so water drains from the tube into the pipe and air enters through the open end. An open **piezometer** is therefore unsuitable for negative gauge pressure. A differential manometer can be used instead.
