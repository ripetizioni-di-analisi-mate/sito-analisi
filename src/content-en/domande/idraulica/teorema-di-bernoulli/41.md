Water leaves an open constant-level tank through a short converging nozzle at a specified depth. If the nozzle diameter doubles at the same depth, how does discharge change?

- It increases to four times its initial value
- It doubles
- It increases to eight times its initial value
- It stays the same because depth has not changed

## Explanation

For **discharge from a constant-level tank**, the exit velocity $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$ depends only on the depth of the opening. It therefore stays the same when the diameter doubles. Discharge is velocity times area, and a circular area is proportional to the square of its diameter, so
$$
\frac{Q_2}{Q_1} = \frac{A_2}{A_1} = \left(\frac{D_2}{D_1}\right)^2 = 4 .
$$
The discharge therefore quadruples. A converging nozzle of diameter $60\,\mathrm{mm}$ at a depth of $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ discharges $26.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$, whereas a nozzle of diameter $120\,\mathrm{mm}$ at the same depth discharges $106\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
