A vertical sluice gate in a horizontal rectangular channel of width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ allows water to pass beneath it. The upstream depth is $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the depth at the downstream vena contracta is $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Flow is one-dimensional at both sections. Neglecting head loss, what is the discharge?

- $3.13\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $3.62\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.56\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $4.43\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

In one-dimensional flow the piezometric head is constant across the section. At the free surface, gauge pressure is zero, so with the channel bed as datum the piezometric head equals the water depth. The width is the same at both sections. Applying the **continuity equation** and **Bernoulli’s equation for one-dimensional flow** gives
$$
\begin{cases}
v_1 \cdot 1.00 = v_2 \cdot 0.50 \\[4pt]
1.00 + \dfrac{v_1^2}{2g} = 0.50 + \dfrac{v_2^2}{2g}
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $v_2 = 2\,v_1$. The second then gives $3\,\dfrac{v_1^2}{2g} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, so $\dfrac{v_1^2}{2g} = 0.167\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v_1 = 1.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The discharge is therefore
$$
Q = v_1\,b\,h_1 = 1.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 1.00 = 3.62\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The upstream velocity head is one-quarter of the downstream value and cannot be neglected. Taking the upstream velocity as zero would give $3.13\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
