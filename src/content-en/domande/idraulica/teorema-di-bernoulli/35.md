A piezometer is fitted perpendicular to the flow in a pressure pipe, with its pressure tap flush with the inner wall. What does the elevation of the water in the tube indicate?

- Velocity head at the section
- Total head at the section
- Piezometric head at the section
- Absolute pressure at the section, expressed in meters of water

## Explanation

The water in the piezometer is at rest. By the **hydrostatic pressure law**, $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ is constant throughout the tube, including at its free surface, where gauge pressure is zero. The elevation of that surface therefore equals the piezometric head $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ at the tap and marks a point on the **hydraulic grade line**. The tap is flush with the wall and perpendicular to the flow, so it has no upstream-facing opening at which the flow stagnates. Its reading therefore excludes the velocity head. A Pitot tube faces upstream and includes this head, so its liquid level stands $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ above that in the piezometer.
