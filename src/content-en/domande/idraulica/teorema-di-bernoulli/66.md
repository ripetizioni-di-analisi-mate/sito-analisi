The siphon shown has diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and draws from a constant-level reservoir, discharging to the atmosphere $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. Crest $S$ is $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the surface. Total head loss is $7\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ and loss from inlet to crest is $2\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. What is the gauge pressure at the crest?

- $-32.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-36.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-28.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-24.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Take the free surface as the elevation datum. Gauge pressure there is zero and velocity is negligible. The outlet jet is exposed to the atmosphere, so **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** between the free surface and the outlet gives
$$
0 = -3.20 + \frac{v^2}{2g} + 7\,\frac{v^2}{2g}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.400\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
giving a velocity of $v = 2.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Applying the same equation between the free surface and the siphon crest, with a head loss of $2 \cdot 0.400 = 0.800\,\mathrm{m}$ over that part of the pipe, gives
$$
0 = 2.50 + \frac{p_S}{\gamma} + 0.400 + 0.800
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{p_S}{\gamma} = -3.70\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
hence $p_S = -3.70 \cdot 9.81 = -36.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At the crest, its elevation, the velocity head, and the accumulated head loss must all be subtracted. Omitting the velocity head would give $-32.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
