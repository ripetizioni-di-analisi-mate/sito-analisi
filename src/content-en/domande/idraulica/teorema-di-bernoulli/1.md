A pipe of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries a discharge of $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$. At the section in question the pipe centerline is $6.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the datum, and the pressure gauge reads $p = 120\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the total head at this section?

- $18.73\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18.87\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12.37\,\mathrm{m}$
- $19.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean speed is $v = \dfrac{Q}{A} = \dfrac{0.080}{0.0491} = 1.63\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.63^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.135\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Divide gauge pressure by specific weight to obtain the pressure head, $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = \dfrac{120}{9.81} = 12.23\,\mathrm{m}$. Total head is the sum of the three head terms, giving
$$
H = 6.50 + 12.23 + 0.135 = 18.87\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Elevation and pressure head together give the piezometric head $h = 18.73\,\mathrm{m}$. It is less than total head by exactly the velocity head.
