A Pitot-Prandtl tube is placed on the axis of a water pipe, with the stagnation tap and the static tap at the same elevation, connected to a differential manometer. With a gauge pressure of $150\,\mathrm{kPa}$ on the axis, the manometer reads $2.00\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In a second test, at the same discharge, the pressure on the axis is $180\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What does the differential manometer read?

- $32.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $30.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $2.40\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The stagnation tap measures the stagnation pressure $p + \dfrac{\rho v^2}{2}$, while the static tap measures the pressure $p$ of the flow. The differential manometer reads their difference, that is, the dynamic pressure $\dfrac{\rho v^2}{2}$, in which $p$ does not appear. The discharge is the same, so the velocity on the axis does not change and the reading remains $2.00\,\mathrm{kPa}$, corresponding to $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \cdot 2000}{1000}} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The $30\,\mathrm{kPa}$ increase acts on both taps and cancels in the difference, while the value $2.40\,\mathrm{kPa}$ would assume a reading proportional to the pressure.
