A Venturi meter is moved from a horizontal to an inclined reach of the same pipe, with unchanged diameters and discharge. Its differential manometer remains connected to the same two taps. How does its reading change?

- It increases by the elevation difference between the sections
- It decreases by the elevation difference between the sections
- It falls to zero because the taps are at different elevations
- It stays the same because the formula uses the difference in piezometric head

## Explanation

The **Venturi meter** equation relates discharge to the piezometric head difference $\Delta h = h_1 - h_2$. Each head already includes the elevation of its section. The **differential manometer** measures this difference directly, regardless of the pipe inclination or the elevations of the pressure taps. With the same diameters and discharge, the velocities remain the same. The difference in velocity head and the manometer reading are therefore unchanged. A Venturi meter thus works on the same principle in a horizontal, vertical, or inclined pipe.
