Four cross sections of a pressure pipe in steady flow are under consideration. In which section does $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ vary across the section?

- In a straight inclined reach
- In a sharp bend
- In a straight, horizontal, large-diameter reach
- In a straight vertical reach

## Explanation

The sum $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ is constant across a section in one-dimensional flow with straight, parallel streamlines. Acceleration normal to the streamlines is then zero, so the pressure distribution across the section obeys the **hydrostatic pressure law**, as in a fluid at rest. This applies in a straight reach regardless of the pipe’s inclination or diameter. In a tight bend the particles follow curved paths and accelerate toward the center of curvature. A transverse pressure difference provides this acceleration. Pressure therefore rises toward the outside of the bend more steeply than the hydrostatic law predicts, and $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ varies across the section.
