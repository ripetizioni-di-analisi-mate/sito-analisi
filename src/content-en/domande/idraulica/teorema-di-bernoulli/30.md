In a constant-diameter pipe the centerline rises or falls from elevation $z_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at section $1$ to elevation $z_2 = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at section $2$. The respective gauge pressures are $p_1 = 300\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_2 = 260\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Flow goes from $1$ to $2$. What is the head loss?

- $1.08\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.08\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.08\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-1.08\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The diameter is constant, so the **continuity equation** gives the same velocity at both sections. The velocity heads therefore cancel when the total heads are subtracted. The piezometric heads are
$$
h_1 = 2.00 + \frac{300}{9.81} = 32.58\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_2 = 5.00 + \frac{260}{9.81} = 31.50\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so their difference gives the head loss $\Delta H_{1,2} = h_1 - h_2 = 1.08\,\mathrm{m}$. The pipe rises in the direction of flow, so the balance must include the elevation increase $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Using only the pressure difference would give $4.08\,\mathrm{m}$.
