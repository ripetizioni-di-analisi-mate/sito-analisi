A horizontal water jet leaves a nozzle into the atmosphere. How is gauge pressure distributed across the jet a short distance downstream?

- It increases linearly downward, as in one-dimensional flow
- It is greatest on the axis and zero at the jet boundary
- It equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$ everywhere in the section
- It is zero everywhere in the section

## Explanation

The jet boundary is exposed to the atmosphere, so gauge pressure there is zero. After the fluid leaves the opening, each particle falls with downward acceleration $g$. Gravity alone supplies this acceleration, so the net pressure force on a particle is zero and pressure is uniform across the jet. Gauge pressure is therefore **zero** throughout. A hydrostatic pressure distribution requires zero acceleration normal to the streamlines, as in one-dimensional flow. The jet streamlines curve downward, so that condition does not hold. This is why discharge calculations use $p = 0$ throughout the jet section.
