A horizontal pipe carries a constant discharge in steady flow and decreases in diameter. Neglecting head loss, how do conditions in the narrower section compare with those upstream?

- Speed decreases and pressure increases
- Speed increases and pressure stays the same
- Both speed and pressure increase
- Speed increases and pressure decreases

## Explanation

By the **continuity equation**, the discharge is the same at every section, so the mean velocity $v = \dfrac{Q}{A}$ increases as the flow area decreases. The pipe is horizontal, so the elevation terms cancel in **Bernoulli’s equation**, leaving the sum of pressure head and velocity head constant,
$$
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{p_2}{\gamma} + \frac{v_2^2}{2g} .
$$
As the velocity head increases, the pressure head decreases by the same amount. Pressure is therefore **lower** at the throat. Venturi meters and orifice meters use this principle to determine discharge from the pressure difference between the upstream pipe and the constriction.
