A pipe leads from an open constant-level reservoir. At a section $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below its free surface the mean speed is $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting head loss, what is the gauge pressure there?

- $58.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $56.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $54.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $60.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Choose the pipe centerline at the section of interest as the elevation datum. At the reservoir free surface, gauge pressure is zero, velocity is negligible, and elevation is $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The total head there is therefore $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The velocity head at the pipe section is
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{2.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.204\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so **Bernoulli’s equation** gives $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = 6.00 - 0.204 = 5.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and hence $p = 5.80 \cdot 9.81 = 56.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting the velocity head would give $58.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$, the pressure at the same depth in the reservoir, where the water is at rest.
