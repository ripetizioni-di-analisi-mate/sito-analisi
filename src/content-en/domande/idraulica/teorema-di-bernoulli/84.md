A vertical cylindrical tank of diameter $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ drains through a short converging bottom nozzle of diameter $50\,\mathrm{mm}$. Neglect losses and treat the flow as steady at each instant. How fast is the free surface falling when it is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the nozzle axis?

- $313\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $62.6\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $3.92\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $15.7\,\mathrm{mm/s}$

## Explanation

For **discharge from a tank**, the exit velocity is $v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.00} = 6.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The discharge through the nozzle equals the rate at which water leaves the tank, so the **continuity equation** gives the downward speed of the free surface as
$$
v_p = v\,\frac{d^2}{D^2} = 6.26 \cdot \left(\frac{0.050}{1.00}\right)^2 = 0.0157\,\mathrm{m/s} = 15.7\,\mathrm{mm/s} .
$$
The free-surface velocity head is $\dfrac{v_p^2}{2g} = 1.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}$, negligible compared with the available head $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. This justifies neglecting free-surface velocity when calculating the exit speed. The area ratio is the square of the diameter ratio. Using the diameter ratio alone would give $313\,\mathrm{mm/s}$.
