The hydraulic grade line lies below the pipe centerline over part of a pipe. What follows for that section of pipe?

- Absolute pressure is negative
- Gauge pressure is negative and absolute pressure remains positive
- Total head is negative
- Total head is less than piezometric head

## Explanation

The elevation of the hydraulic grade line relative to the pipe centerline is the pressure head $\dfrac{p}{\gamma}$. If the line lies below the centerline, this ratio is negative and so is the gauge pressure. Gauge pressure is measured relative to atmospheric pressure, so the corresponding absolute pressure is still positive, between zero and $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Total head exceeds piezometric head by the positive velocity head regardless of the sign of the gauge pressure. The pipe can operate below atmospheric pressure provided the absolute pressure remains above the vapor pressure.
