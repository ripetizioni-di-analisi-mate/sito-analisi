Over a constant-diameter pipe section of length $600\,\mathrm{m}$, the energy grade line falls from elevation $48.60\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream to $45.00\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream while the pipe centerline falls from $30.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $18.00\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the energy gradient?

- $0.0200$
- $0.0260$
- $0.0140$
- $0.0060$

## Explanation

The hydraulic gradient is the head loss per unit length, given by the downward slope of the energy grade line in the direction of flow. Thus
$$
J = \frac{H_1 - H_2}{L} = \frac{48.60 - 45.00}{600} = \frac{3.60}{600} = 0.0060 .
$$
The slope of the pipe centerline, $\dfrac{12.00}{600} = 0.0200$, depends on the pipe alignment and does not measure a loss. The constant diameter makes the hydraulic grade line parallel to the energy grade line, so the two have the same slope. The pipe centerline falls faster than the energy grade line. Their separation therefore grows from $18.60\,\mathrm{m}$ to $27.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and pressure increases downstream.
