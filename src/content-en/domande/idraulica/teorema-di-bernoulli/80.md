In a Venturi meter in a horizontal pipe, the diffuser returns the flow from the throat to the original pipe diameter. How does the pressure just after the diffuser compare with the pressure just before the converging section?

- Equal to the throat pressure because the discharge is the same
- Higher than upstream because the flow has slowed
- Much lower than upstream because the jet separates from the diffuser wall as it does at an orifice plate
- Slightly lower than upstream because of the small head loss in the meter

## Explanation

The two sections have the same diameter and elevation. By the **continuity equation**, their velocities are equal, so **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** makes the pressure head difference equal to the head loss between them. In the gradual diffuser the flow slows while remaining attached to the walls. It recovers nearly all the pressure head lost in the converging section, so the loss is small and downstream pressure is **only slightly lower** than upstream pressure. At an orifice plate, the jet contracts beyond the opening and then expands abruptly, producing a much greater loss. Downstream pressure cannot exceed upstream pressure here because total head decreases downstream and the velocity heads are equal.
