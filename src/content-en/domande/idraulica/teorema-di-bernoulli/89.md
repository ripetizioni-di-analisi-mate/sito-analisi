A Bernoulli calculation using gauge pressures gives pressure head $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = -11.50\,\mathrm{m}$ at a pipe crest, with atmospheric pressure equivalent to $10.33\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. What can be concluded?

- The crest operates at subatmospheric pressure and the pipe functions normally
- The calculated absolute pressure would be negative, so vapor forms at the crest and the predicted flow cannot occur
- Absolute pressure at the crest is equivalent to $11.50\,\mathrm{m}$ of water
- Water flows in the direction opposite to that assumed in the calculation

## Explanation

Adding atmospheric pressure to gauge pressure gives absolute pressure. At the crest, this would give
$$
\frac{p_{\mathrm{abs}}}{\gamma} = 10.33 - 11.50 = -1.17\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Negative absolute pressure is impossible here. In fact, vaporization begins as soon as the absolute pressure reaches the **vapor pressure**, equivalent to a head of $0.24\,\mathrm{m}$ for water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. A vapor pocket therefore forms at the crest, breaks the liquid column, and prevents the calculated discharge from passing. Negative gauge pressure is acceptable only while the corresponding absolute pressure remains sufficiently above the vapor pressure.
