A river flowing at speed $2.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a bridge pier. Upstream the flow is undisturbed and the pressure distribution is hydrostatic. What is the gauge pressure at the stagnation point on the pier nose, $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface?

- $14.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $21.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $17.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $3.13\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Apply **Bernoulli’s equation** along the streamline that ends at the stagnation point, starting in the undisturbed flow upstream at the same depth. Upstream the pressure distribution is hydrostatic, so the pressure is $\gamma \cdot 1.50 = 14.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the velocity is $2.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At the stagnation point the velocity is zero and the elevation is unchanged. Thus
$$
p_A = 14.7 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = 14.7 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1000 \cdot 2.50^2 \cdot 10^{-3} = 14.7 + 3.13 = 17.8\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Expressed as a water-column height, the stagnation pressure is $1.50 + 0.319 = 1.82\,\mathrm{m}$, of which $0.319\,\mathrm{m}$ is the velocity head of the flow. The nose of the pier acts like the opening of a Pitot tube.
