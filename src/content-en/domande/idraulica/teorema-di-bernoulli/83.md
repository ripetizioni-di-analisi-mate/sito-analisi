The tank shown is closed at the top. A vertical tube open to the atmosphere passes through the lid and ends in the water $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ above a side nozzle’s axis. The free surface is $2.30\,\mathrm{m}$ above the axis, and air bubbles enter from the tube tip during discharge. Neglecting head loss, what is the exit speed?

- $3.96\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.72\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.42\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Air enters through the lower end of the tube, so gauge pressure there is zero. The water near that point is effectively at rest. Taking the nozzle centerline as datum gives a total head of $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ at the tube end. The outlet jet is exposed to the atmosphere, so applying **Bernoulli’s equation** between the lower end of the tube and the outlet gives
$$
0.80 = \frac{v^2}{2g}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.80} = 3.96\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
By the **hydrostatic pressure law**, the air above the water has gauge pressure $-9.81 \cdot 1.50 = -14.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$, which offsets the pressure due to the $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ of water above the tube end. Using the full depth $2.30\,\mathrm{m}$ as though the tank were open would give $6.72\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
