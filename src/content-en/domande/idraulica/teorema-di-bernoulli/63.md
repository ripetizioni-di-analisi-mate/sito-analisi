In a very wide channel, speed increases linearly from zero at the bed to $u_s$ at the surface according to $u = u_s\,\dfrac{y}{h}$, where $y$ is distance above the bed and $h$ is water depth. What is the kinetic energy correction factor $\alpha$?

- $2$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

Since the channel is very wide, consider a strip of unit width with area $h$. The mean velocity and the integral of the cube of the velocity are
$$
v = \frac{1}{h}\int_0^h u_s\,\frac{y}{h}\,dy = \frac{u_s}{2} ,
\qquad
\int_0^h u^3\,dy = \frac{u_s^3}{h^3}\cdot\frac{h^4}{4} = \frac{u_s^3\,h}{4} .
$$
The definition of the **kinetic energy correction factor** then gives
$$
\alpha = \frac{1}{h\,v^3}\int_0^h u^3\,dy = \frac{8}{h\,u_s^3}\cdot\frac{u_s^3\,h}{4} = 2 .
$$
Using velocity squared instead of velocity cubed yields $\dfrac{4}{3}$, the correction factor for momentum flux. The value $\dfrac{1}{4}$ results from dividing by $u_s^3$ instead of by the cube of the mean velocity.
