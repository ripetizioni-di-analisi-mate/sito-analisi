A rectangular channel carries one-dimensional flow of depth $h = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ at mean speed $v = 1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The bed elevation is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the datum. What is the piezometric head?

- $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.57\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.50\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By **Bernoulli’s equation for one-dimensional flow**, the sum $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ is constant across the section. The most convenient point at which to evaluate it is the free surface, where the gauge pressure is zero and the elevation is $4.00 + 1.50 = 5.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus
$$
z + \frac{p}{\gamma} = 5.50 + 0 = 5.50\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The piezometric head of an open-channel flow therefore equals the free-surface elevation. The same value follows at the bed, where the elevation is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the pressure head is $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.073\,\mathrm{m}$ enters the total head, which is $5.57\,\mathrm{m}$.
