Viscous oil flows through a circular pipe in laminar flow. A Pitot tube on the centerline reads a level $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ above a piezometer at the same section. Centerline speed is twice mean speed and the kinetic energy correction factor is $\alpha = 2$. How far above the hydraulic grade line is the energy grade line of the flow?

- $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.020\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.040\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.160\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

A **Pitot tube** measures the local velocity at its opening, so the observed level difference $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ equals the centerline velocity head $\dfrac{u_{\max}^2}{2g}$. The mean velocity is half of $u_{\max}$, so its velocity head is one-quarter of the observed difference,
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{0.080}{4} = 0.020\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
By **Bernoulli’s equation for one-dimensional flow**, the total head of the flow is $h + \alpha\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. The energy grade line therefore lies above the hydraulic grade line by
$$
\alpha\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 2 \cdot 0.020 = 0.040\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The Pitot level is higher because the centerline velocity exceeds the mean. Using $\alpha = 1$ would give $0.020\,\mathrm{m}$ for the spacing between the grade lines.
