Air initially at rest at atmospheric pressure flows over a warehouse’s flat roof at speed $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The air inside remains at rest at atmospheric pressure. Given air density $\rho = 1.20\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and negligible elevation differences, what is the inside-to-outside pressure difference across the roof?

- $375\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $750\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $30.0\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $38.2\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

At these velocities, air can be treated as incompressible, so the pressure form of **Bernoulli’s equation** applies along a streamline. Neglecting elevation differences gives the following relation between the still air and the air flowing over the roof,
$$
p_{\mathrm{atm}} = p + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
p_{\mathrm{atm}} - p = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.20 \cdot 25^2 = 375\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The indoor pressure is atmospheric, so the roof experiences a net upward pressure of $375\,\mathrm{Pa}$, equivalent to an upward force of $0.375\,\mathrm{kN}$ per square meter. Dynamic pressure includes the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so using $\rho v^2$ would give $750\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
