A horizontal pipe from a constant-level reservoir ends in a gate valve. A centerline gauge just upstream reads $300\,\mathrm{kPa}$ with the valve shut and $270\,\mathrm{kPa}$ with it open, when speed at the gauge is $3.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the head loss between the reservoir and gauge with the valve open?

- $3.06\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.52\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.14\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Take the pipe centerline as the elevation datum. When the gate valve is closed, the water is at rest. By the **hydrostatic pressure law**, the reservoir free-surface elevation is
$$
\frac{300}{9.81} = 30.58\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which is also the reservoir total head. With the valve open, the pressure head is $\dfrac{270}{9.81} = 27.52\,\mathrm{m}$ and the velocity head is $\dfrac{3.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.459\,\mathrm{m}$. Applying **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss** from the free surface to the pressure gauge therefore gives
$$
\Delta H = 30.58 - 27.52 - 0.459 = 2.60\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Part of the pressure drop $30\,\mathrm{kPa}$ supplies the velocity head acquired by the flow. Attributing the entire drop to head loss would give $3.06\,\mathrm{m}$.
