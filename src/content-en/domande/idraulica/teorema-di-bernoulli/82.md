Two constant-level tanks are separated by a vertical wall containing a short converging nozzle of outlet diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$. Its axis is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the upstream water level and $2.20\,\mathrm{m}$ below the downstream water level, where the jet discharges. Neglecting nozzle losses, what is the discharge?

- $69.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $33.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $46.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $51.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Take the nozzle centerline as the elevation datum and apply **Bernoulli’s equation** from the upstream free surface to the nozzle exit. The jet discharges into still water in the downstream tank, so the exit pressure equals the local hydrostatic pressure there. The **hydrostatic pressure law** gives $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = 2.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus
$$
4.00 = 2.20 + \frac{v^2}{2g}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.80} = 5.94\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
giving discharge $Q = 5.94 \cdot 0.00785 = 0.0467\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $46.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The available head is the elevation difference between the two free surfaces. Using the full upstream depth $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ would give $69.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$, the discharge when the nozzle opens to the atmosphere.
