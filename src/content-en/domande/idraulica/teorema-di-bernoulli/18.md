The piezometric heads at two pipe sections are calculated first relative to ground level and then relative to a datum $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ lower. What changes?

- Both piezometric heads increase by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and their difference stays the same
- Both piezometric heads stay the same because the pressures do not change
- Both piezometric heads increase by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and their difference also increases by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- Only the piezometric head at the lower section increases by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The piezometric head is $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$. Lowering the datum by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ increases the elevation of each point by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, while the pressure at each point is independent of the datum. Both piezometric heads therefore increase by the same amount. Their difference is
$$
h_1 - h_2 = (z_1 + 2.00) + \frac{p_1}{\gamma} - (z_2 + 2.00) - \frac{p_2}{\gamma} ,
$$
where the two increments cancel and leave the difference unchanged. Any datum can therefore be used, provided it is the same for all sections.
