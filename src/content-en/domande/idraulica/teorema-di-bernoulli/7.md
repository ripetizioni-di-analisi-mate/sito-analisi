A short horizontal nozzle in the vertical wall of a constant-level tank is $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface and $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Neglecting head loss and air resistance, how far from the wall does the jet reach the ground?

- $6.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.39\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.80\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Apply **Bernoulli’s equation** between the free surface and the outlet. The available head equals the depth of the opening, giving the exit speed $v = \sqrt{2 g \cdot 3.20} = 7.92\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Once it leaves the nozzle, the jet follows the path of a projectile launched horizontally. Its height above the ground determines the flight time
$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.80}{9.81}} = 0.606\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
Horizontal speed stays constant, so the distance traveled is $x = v\,t = 7.92 \cdot 0.606 = 4.80\,\mathrm{m}$. The depth below the free surface sets the launch speed, while the height above the ground sets the flight time.
