In closed reservoir $A$ the free surface is at elevation $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the air above it is at gauge pressure $50\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Open reservoir $B$ has its surface at elevation $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$. A pipe connects the two constant-level reservoirs. Which way does water flow, and what is the total head difference between the two surfaces?

- From $B$ to $A$, with head difference $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $A$ to $B$, with head difference $7.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $A$ to $B$, with head difference $2.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $B$ to $A$, with head difference $2.10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Velocity is negligible at both free surfaces. In $A$ the surface gauge pressure equals that of the air above the water, so
$$
H_A = 2.00 + \frac{50}{9.81} = 2.00 + 5.10 = 7.10\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
whereas in $B$ the surface gauge pressure is zero and $H_B = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$. By **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss**, total head decreases in the direction of flow. Water therefore flows **from $A$ to $B$**, although the free surface in $A$ is lower. The entire head difference $H_A - H_B = 2.10\,\mathrm{m}$ is dissipated along the pipe. Comparing the free-surface elevations alone would incorrectly predict flow from $B$ to $A$.
