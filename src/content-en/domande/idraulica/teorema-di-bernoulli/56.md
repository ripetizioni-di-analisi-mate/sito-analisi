A constant-level reservoir with surface elevation $50.00\,\mathrm{m}$ supplies a pipe without a pump. Converging and diverging fittings connect sections of different diameters. Can the hydraulic grade line rise above elevation $50.00\,\mathrm{m}$ anywhere?

- Yes, in the larger-diameter reaches where flow slows and the hydraulic grade line rises
- Yes, where the pipe centerline descends well below the reservoir
- No, because piezometric head cannot exceed total head, which cannot exceed the reservoir surface elevation
- No, because the hydraulic grade line always falls downstream

## Explanation

Gauge pressure at the reservoir free surface is zero and velocity there is negligible, so the total head is $50.00\,\mathrm{m}$. There are no pumps along the pipe. By **Bernoulli’s equation with head loss**, total head decreases or remains constant downstream, giving $H \leq 50.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at every section. The piezometric head is $h = H - \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, so $h \leq H$ and the hydraulic grade line stays below elevation $50.00\,\mathrm{m}$ throughout the pipe. At an increase in diameter, the velocity head decreases and the hydraulic grade line can rise, but it remains below the energy grade line. Lowering the pipe centerline increases the pressure head by increasing its distance below the hydraulic grade line. It does not raise the line itself.
