Water leaves an open constant-level tank through a short horizontal converging nozzle as a free jet. A vertical Pitot tube open at the top has its inlet on the jet axis just downstream of the nozzle, facing upstream. Neglecting head loss, to what elevation does water rise in the tube?

- To the reservoir surface elevation
- To the jet axis elevation, because gauge pressure in the jet is zero
- Halfway between the jet axis and the reservoir surface
- Above the reservoir surface by the velocity head of the jet

## Explanation

Gauge pressure in the jet is zero, so its piezometric head equals the centerline elevation $z$ and its total head is $z + \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. The liquid level in a **Pitot tube** exceeds the piezometric head by the velocity head and thus reads the jet’s total head. In the absence of losses, **Bernoulli’s equation** between the reservoir free surface and the jet section gives the same total head as at the free surface. The water in the tube therefore rises **to the elevation of the reservoir free surface**. A piezometer would reach only the jet centerline elevation because it measures piezometric head alone.
