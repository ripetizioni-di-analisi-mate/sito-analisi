A pump draws from a constant-level reservoir. Noise and vibration at the inlet indicate cavitation. At the same discharge, which change reduces the cavitation risk?

- Reduce the suction-line diameter
- Lengthen the suction line to move the pump farther from the reservoir
- Install the pump higher above the free surface
- Install the pump at a lower elevation relative to the free surface

## Explanation

The energy balance between the free surface and the pump gives the absolute inlet pressure,
$$
\frac{p_{\mathrm{abs}}}{\gamma} = \frac{p_{\mathrm{atm}}}{\gamma} - z_a - \frac{v^2}{2g} - \Delta H .
$$
Cavitation risk decreases as this pressure rises, so at least one of the subtracted terms must decrease. Lowering the pump reduces its elevation $z_a$ and therefore **increases** the inlet pressure. At the same discharge, a smaller pipe diameter raises the velocity and increases both the velocity head and the head loss. A longer pipe also increases head loss. Both changes lower the inlet pressure, as does raising the pump.
