Absolute pressure at the throat of a Venturi meter falls to the vapor pressure and bubbles form. Where in the meter does cavitation damage the walls?

- At the throat, where the bubbles form
- In the converging section upstream of the throat
- In the diffuser downstream of the throat, where pressure recovers and bubbles collapse
- Nowhere, because the bubbles contain only water vapor

## Explanation

At the throat, absolute pressure reaches the **vapor pressure**, so the liquid vaporizes and bubbles form. The flow carries the bubbles into the diffuser, where velocity decreases and, by **Bernoulli’s equation**, pressure recovers above the vapor pressure. The bubbles then collapse rapidly. Bubbles collapsing against the wall subject it to extremely high local pressures, so erosion occurs **in the diffuser downstream of the throat**. Pressure in the converging section is higher than at the throat, and the bubbles have not yet formed there.
