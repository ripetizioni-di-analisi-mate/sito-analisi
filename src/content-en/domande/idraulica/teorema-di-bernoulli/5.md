A converging nozzle on a horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ discharges to the atmosphere through an outlet of diameter $D_2 = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$. The upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 150\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting head loss, what is the discharge?

- $62.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $88.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $353\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $551\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The jet leaves the nozzle at atmospheric pressure, so the outlet gauge pressure is zero. Since the pipe is horizontal, **Bernoulli’s equation** between the upstream section and the exit gives
$$
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{v_2^2}{2g} .
$$
The **continuity equation** gives $v_1 = v_2\,\dfrac{A_2}{A_1} = 0.16\,v_2$. Substitution yields $\dfrac{v_2^2}{2g}\left(1 - 0.16^2\right) = 15.29\,\mathrm{m}$, hence $v_2 = 17.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Multiplying the speed by the cross-sectional area gives the discharge
$$
Q = v_2 A_2 = 17.5 \cdot 0.00503 = 0.0882\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 88.2\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
