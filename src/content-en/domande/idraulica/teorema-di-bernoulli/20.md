A constant-diameter pipe descends from elevation $z_1 = 12.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $z_2 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Its upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 50\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting head loss, what is the downstream gauge pressure?

- $88.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $50.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $138\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-38.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The pipe diameter is constant, so the **continuity equation** gives the same velocity at both sections. The velocity heads therefore cancel in **Bernoulli’s equation**, leaving the piezometric head constant. Thus
$$
\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = z_1 - z_2 + \frac{p_1}{\gamma} = 9.00 + \frac{50}{9.81} = 9.00 + 5.10 = 14.10\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Multiplying by the specific weight gives $p_2 = 14.10 \cdot 9.81 = 138\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The pipe drops by $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$, so the pressure head increases by that amount and pressure rises in the direction of flow.
