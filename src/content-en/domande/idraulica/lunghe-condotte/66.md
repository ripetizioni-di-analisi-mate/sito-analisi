A long pipe discharges into a constant-level reservoir. The outlet centerline is $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ below its free surface. In the long-pipeline model, what is the gauge pressure at the outlet section?

- $0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $145\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $4.50\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $44.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The long-pipeline model neglects the outlet velocity head, so the hydraulic grade line ends at the downstream reservoir free surface. The pressure head at the outlet equals the vertical distance from the pipe centerline to that surface, $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = 4.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The gauge pressure is therefore
$$
p = 9.81 \cdot 4.5 = 44.1\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
A free jet discharging to the atmosphere would have zero gauge pressure. The value $145\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the absolute pressure at the submerged outlet.
