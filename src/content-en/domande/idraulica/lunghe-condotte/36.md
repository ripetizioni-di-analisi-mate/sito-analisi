A long pipeline starts at reservoir elevation $96\,\mathrm{m}$ and operates with energy gradient $J = 0.009$. The profile shown passes through a section at distance $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance and centerline elevation $86\,\mathrm{m}$, then one at distance $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ and elevation $81\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the gauge pressure at the more critical of the two sections?

- $-0.800\,\mathrm{m}$
- $+3.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $+15.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-3.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The gauge pressure head at a section is $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = z_1 - \big(z + J x\big)$, so it is lowest where $z + J x$ is largest. At the two sections the values are
$$
86 + 0.009 \cdot 1200 = 96.8\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
81 + 0.009 \cdot 2000 = 99.0\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so the critical section is the one at $2000\,\mathrm{m}$, where the pressure head is $96 - 99.0 = -3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. At the higher point on the route the pressure head is $96 - 96.8 = -0.800\,\mathrm{m}$, a higher value because less friction loss has accumulated there. The lowest pressure therefore occurs at the lower of the two sections.
