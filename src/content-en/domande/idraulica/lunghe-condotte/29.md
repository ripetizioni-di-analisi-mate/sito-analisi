A pipe of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ must carry $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$ with available head $25\,\mathrm{m}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.022$. What is the theoretical diameter?

- $0.182\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.169\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.300\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.256\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The head loss in a long pipeline is $\Delta H = \dfrac{8 \lambda L Q^2}{\pi^2 g D^5}$. Solving for the diameter gives
$$
D = \left(\frac{8\,\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{\pi^2 g\,\Delta H}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1500 \cdot 0.100^2}{\pi^2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 25}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} .
$$
The numerator is $2.64$ and the denominator is $2421$, so the quantity in parentheses is $1.091 \cdot 10^{-3}$ and its fifth root is $0.256\,\mathrm{m}$. The theoretical diameter requested is therefore $256\,\mathrm{mm}$. For the actual design, one would select the next larger standard pipe size, $300\,\mathrm{mm}$.
