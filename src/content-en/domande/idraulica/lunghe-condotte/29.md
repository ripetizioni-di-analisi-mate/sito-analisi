A reservoir with free surface at elevation $120\,\mathrm{m}$ supplies a user located at elevation $80\,\mathrm{m}$ through a long pipe of $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ with friction factor $\lambda = 0.022$. The user requires the discharge $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ with a pressure head of at least $20\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the theoretical diameter of the pipe?

- $0.187\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.270\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.391\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.215\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The head available for losses is the elevation difference between the free surface and the user, reduced by the required pressure head, so $\Delta H = 120 - 80 - 20 = 20\,\mathrm{m}$. In the long-pipe scheme the loss is $\Delta H = \dfrac{8 \lambda L Q^2}{\pi^2 g D^5}$, therefore the theoretical diameter is
$$
D = \left(\frac{8\,\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{\pi^2 g\,\Delta H}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 2000 \cdot 0.050^2}{\pi^2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 20}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(4.54 \cdot 10^{-4}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = 0.215\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
In design the next larger commercial diameter, $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, is adopted. Using the whole $40\,\mathrm{m}$ elevation difference, without reserving the user's pressure head, would give $0.187\,\mathrm{m}$, a diameter that does not guarantee the required pressure; the value $0.270\,\mathrm{m}$ uses $\pi$ in place of $\pi^2$, and $0.391\,\mathrm{m}$ forgets to square the discharge.
