A pipeline of length $900\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.024$ carries water from an open reservoir with free surface at elevation $40\,\mathrm{m}$ into a closed reservoir with free surface at $22\,\mathrm{m}$. The gas above the second reservoir has gauge pressure $p = 58.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$, as shown. What is the discharge?

::schema{acqua="0,5 1.8,5 1.8,3.4 0,3.4;10,2.2 12,2.2 12,0.3 10,0.3" solidi="0,3.1 1.8,3.1 1.8,3.4 0,3.4;9.7,0 12.3,0 12.3,0.3 9.7,0.3;9.7,0.3 10,0.3 10,3.4 9.7,3.4;12,0.3 12.3,0.3 12.3,3.4 12,3.4;9.7,3.4 12.3,3.4 12.3,3.7 9.7,3.7" nodi="A,1.8,3.8,-;U,9.7,1,-" condotte="A-U,0.16,D=200 mm" pelo="0,1.8,5,40.0 m;10,12,2.2,22.0 m" etichette="11,4.25,gas p=58.9 kPa;5.8,1.7,L=900 m" titolo="Closed downstream reservoir with pressurized gas above the free surface"}

- $56.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $46.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $32.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $65.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

At the free surface in the closed reservoir, the gauge pressure equals that of the gas above the water. The total head there is therefore
$$
H_2 = z_2 + \frac{p}{\gamma} = 22 + \frac{58.9}{9.81} = 22 + 6.00 = 28.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The energy balance between the two free surfaces gives $J = \dfrac{40 - 28.0}{900} = 0.0133$, and the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.200 \cdot 0.0133}{0.024}} = 1.48\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The discharge is $Q = 1.48 \cdot 0.0314 = 0.0464\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $46.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Ignoring the gas pressure would give $56.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Adding the gas pressure head to the elevation difference would give $65.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
