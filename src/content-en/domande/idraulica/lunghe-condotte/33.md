A constant-diameter pipeline of length $2400\,\mathrm{m}$ connects reservoirs with free-surface elevations $85\,\mathrm{m}$ and $61\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the piezometric head at distance $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance?

- $100\,\mathrm{m}$
- $76.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $61.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $70.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The energy gradient is $J = \dfrac{85 - 61}{2400} = 0.0100$ and is constant along the entire pipe because the diameter is constant. In the long-pipeline model, the piezometric head decreases linearly with distance from the inlet, giving
$$
h(1500) = z_1 - J x = 85 - 0.0100 \cdot 1500 = 70.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The calculated head lies between the two reservoir surface elevations, as expected for this constant-diameter pipe. Subtract the local pipe centerline elevation to obtain the pressure head. The value $100\,\mathrm{m}$ adds the head loss instead of subtracting it.
