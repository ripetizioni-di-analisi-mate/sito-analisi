Two constant-level reservoirs with elevation difference $20\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by a long pipeline of length $2500\,\mathrm{m}$. The pipe is replaced by another of equal length and roughness but twice the diameter. What is the new energy gradient?

- $0.00800$
- $0.00400$
- $0.0160$
- $2.50 \cdot 10^{-4}$

## Explanation

In the long-pipeline model the full reservoir elevation difference is consumed by pipe friction. The energy gradient is therefore
$$
J = \frac{z_1 - z_2}{L} = \frac{20}{2500} = 0.00800 ,
$$
which depends only on the elevation difference and pipe length. The diameter does not appear, so the gradient remains $0.00800$ after the pipe is replaced. Discharge does depend on diameter. For a fixed gradient and friction factor, discharge is proportional to $D^{\frac{5}{2}}$, so doubling the diameter increases the discharge to $5.66$ times its original value.
