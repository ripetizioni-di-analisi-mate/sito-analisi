A constant-level reservoir feeds the long pipeline shown, of length $600\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.024$. A nozzle of diameter $d = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$ at the outlet discharges a jet to the atmosphere. Its centerline is $30\,\mathrm{m}$ below the reservoir surface. Minor losses, including those in the nozzle, are negligible. What is the discharge?

::schema{acqua="0,6 1.8,6 1.8,4.4 0,4.4" solidi="0,4.1 1.8,4.1 1.8,4.4 0,4.4" nodi="A,1.8,4.8,-;B,9.4,1.2,-;C,10.2,1.2,-" condotte="A-B,0.18,D=150 mm;B-C,0.08,d=40 mm" pelo="0,1.8,6,reservoir" frecce="10.3,1.2,11.8,1.2,jet" tratteggi="1.8,6 12.6,6;10.2,1.2 12.6,1.2" quote="12.4 6,12.4 1.2,30.0 m" etichette="5.4,2.4,L=600 m" titolo="Long pipe ending in a nozzle"}

- $43.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $30.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $17.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $25.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The jet discharges to the atmosphere, so the gauge pressure at the nozzle exit is zero. Taking the nozzle centerline as the elevation datum, the total head there equals the jet velocity head, $\dfrac{v_u^2}{2g}$. Continuity gives $v_u = v\left(\dfrac{D}{d}\right)^2$, so
$$
\frac{v_u^2}{2g} = \left(\frac{D}{d}\right)^4 \frac{v^2}{2g} = 197.8\,\frac{v^2}{2g} ,
$$
while the friction head loss in the pipe is $\lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D}\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 96\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. The jet velocity head is greater than the friction loss, so both terms enter the energy balance from the reservoir free surface to the jet. Thus
$$
30 = (96 + 197.8)\,\frac{v^2}{2g} .
$$
It follows that $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.102\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v = 1.42\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The discharge is therefore $Q = 1.42 \cdot \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.150^2}{4} = 0.0250\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $25.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The value $43.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$ follows from applying the long-pipeline approximation and neglecting the jet velocity head. The value $30.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$ follows from applying Torricelli’s law to the nozzle and neglecting pipe friction.
