A square concrete tunnel of side $a = 0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ flows full under pressure as a long conduit with energy gradient $J = 0.01$. Its roughness coefficient is $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and the flow is fully rough and turbulent. What discharge does the Gauckler–Strickler formula give?

- $694\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $530\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $43.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $438\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The tunnel flows full, so its wetted perimeter is the entire perimeter, $C = 4a = 2\,\mathrm{m}$. The cross-sectional area is $A = a^2 = 0.25\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving a hydraulic radius of
$$
R = \frac{A}{C} = \frac{0.25}{2} = 0.125\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $\dfrac{a}{4}$, analogous to $\dfrac{D}{4}$ for a full circular pipe. Since $0.125^{\frac{2}{3}} = 0.25$, the Gauckler–Strickler equation gives
$$
v = k_s\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,J^{\frac{1}{2}} = 70 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 0.1 = 1.75\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
so $Q = 1.75 \cdot 0.25 = 0.438\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $438\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The value $694\,\mathrm{L/s}$ assumes $R = \dfrac{a}{2}$. The value $530\,\mathrm{L/s}$ excludes the top side from the wetted perimeter as in an open channel, and $43.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$ results from using $J$ in place of $J^{\frac{1}{2}}$.
