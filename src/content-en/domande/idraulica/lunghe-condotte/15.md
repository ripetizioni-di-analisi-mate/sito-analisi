A pressurized pipe of length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.022$, has an entrance, bends, and a gate valve with combined loss coefficient $\sum \xi = 2.5$. The velocity head is lost at the outlet. What is the ratio of the friction-loss coefficient to the combined coefficient for the minor losses and exit?

- $52.8$
- $37.7$
- $1710$
- $0.0265$

## Explanation

The friction head loss is $\lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D}\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. The sum of the minor losses and the velocity head lost at the outlet is $\left(\sum \xi + 1\right)\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. Both expressions contain the same velocity head, so the two coefficients can be compared directly. The coefficient for friction along the full pipe is
$$
\lambda\,\frac{L}{D} = 0.022 \cdot \frac{1200}{0.200} = 132 ,
$$
while the coefficient for the other losses is $2.5 + 1 = 3.5$. Their ratio is therefore $\dfrac{132}{3.5} = 37.7$. Friction accounts for nearly forty times as much head loss as all the other losses combined, so the long-pipeline approximation is reasonable. The value $52.8$ omits the term $1$ for the velocity head the water carries out of the pipe.
