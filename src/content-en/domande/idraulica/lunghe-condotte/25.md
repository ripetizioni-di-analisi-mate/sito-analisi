Two reservoirs with elevation difference $20\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by a long pipeline of length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and roughness coefficient $k_s = 75\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. What is the discharge?

- $92.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $146\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $5.80\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $58.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The energy gradient is $J = \dfrac{20}{2000} = 0.0100$ and the hydraulic radius of the full circular pipe is $R = \dfrac{D}{4} = 0.0625\,\mathrm{m}$. The Gauckler–Strickler equation therefore gives
$$
v = k_s\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,J^{\frac{1}{2}} = 75 \cdot 0.0625^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.0100^{\frac{1}{2}} = 75 \cdot 0.1575 \cdot 0.100 = 1.18\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Since the cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the discharge is $Q = 1.18 \cdot 0.0491 = 0.0580\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $58.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The mean velocity lies within the usual range for water mains, between $0.5$ and $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
