Two reservoirs maintain constant water levels with an elevation difference of $15\,\mathrm{m}$. They are connected by the pipe shown, of length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.026$. Using the long-pipeline approximation, find the discharge.

- $58.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $41.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $82.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $330\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

In the long-pipeline approximation the entire difference in water-surface elevation is lost to wall friction. The hydraulic gradient is therefore
$$
J = \frac{15}{1000} = 0.0150 .
$$
Solving the Darcy–Weisbach equation for velocity gives
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 g D J}{\lambda}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250 \cdot 0.0150}{0.026}} = 1.68\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the discharge is $Q = 1.68 \cdot 0.0491 = 0.0826\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $82.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$, toward the lower reservoir. The Reynolds number is $4.2 \cdot 10^{5}$, indicating turbulent flow.
