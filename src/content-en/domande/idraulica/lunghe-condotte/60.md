A pipeline of total length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ must carry $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$ between reservoirs with elevation difference $24\,\mathrm{m}$, with no residual head. The theoretical diameter lies between standard sizes $250$ and $300\,\mathrm{mm}$. The upstream reach therefore has diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the downstream reach diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, both with $\lambda = 0.022$, as shown. How long must the reach of diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ be?

- $812\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1190\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3210\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the energy gradient $J = \dfrac{8 \lambda Q^2}{\pi^2 g D^5}$ in each pipe section, hence $J_1 = 0.00748$ for $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $J_2 = 0.0186$ for $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$. The two lengths must add to the total route length, and their combined losses must equal the elevation difference, giving
$$
\begin{cases}
L_1 + L_2 = 2000 \\
0.00748\,L_1 + 0.0186\,L_2 = 24
\end{cases}
$$
Substituting $L_2 = 2000 - L_1$ into the second equation gives $37.2 - 0.0111\,L_1 = 24$. Thus $L_1 = 1188\,\mathrm{m}$, or about $1190\,\mathrm{m}$, and $L_2 = 812\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $812\,\mathrm{m}$ is the length of the $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ section. The value $3210\,\mathrm{m}$ is the length needed to dissipate the full elevation difference using only $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe, which would exceed the length of the route.
