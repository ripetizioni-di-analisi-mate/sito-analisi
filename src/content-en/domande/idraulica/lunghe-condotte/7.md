A pipeline of length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.0235$, connects reservoir free surfaces at elevations $72\,\mathrm{m}$ and $44\,\mathrm{m}$. At a distance $800\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance the centerline is at elevation $66\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What is the gauge pressure at that section in meters of water?

- $-5.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $+5.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-22.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The energy gradient is $J = \dfrac{72 - 44}{2000} = 0.0140$ and is constant throughout this constant-diameter pipe. In the long-pipeline model the piezometric head at distance $x$ is $h(x) = z_1 - J x$, giving
$$
h(800) = 72 - 0.0140 \cdot 800 = 60.8\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Since $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ and the pipe centerline at this section is at $66\,\mathrm{m}$,
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = 60.8 - 66 = -5.20\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $p = -51.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The hydraulic grade line lies below the pipe centerline, indicating **negative gauge pressure** at this section. The pressure remains above the vapor-pressure limit of $-10.09\,\mathrm{m}$, so the flow is possible.
