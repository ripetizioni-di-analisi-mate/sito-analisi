In a long pipeline of energy gradient $J = 0.008$, the centerline between distances $800\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1400\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance descends linearly from elevation $52.0\,\mathrm{m}$ to elevation $47.2\,\mathrm{m}$. Which statement about the pressure in this reach is correct?

- It is the same at every section of the reach
- It increases downstream
- It decreases downstream
- It is zero at every section of the reach

## Explanation

The centerline slope is $\dfrac{52.0 - 47.2}{600} = 0.008$, equal to the energy gradient. Over $600\,\mathrm{m}$ the hydraulic grade line therefore falls by $0.008 \cdot 600 = 4.8\,\mathrm{m}$, exactly as much as the centerline. The pressure head $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = h(x) - z(x)$ is the vertical separation of these parallel lines, so pressure **is the same** throughout this section of pipe. Its value depends on the unspecified upstream reservoir level and need not be zero.
