A variable-speed pump lifts water between two constant-level reservoirs. At two different rotational speeds the operating points $Q = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ with head $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and $Q = 0.200\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ with head $26.0\,\mathrm{m}$ are measured. In the long-pipe scheme, what is the static lift between the two reservoirs?

- $14.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Both operating points lie on the system curve of the pipeline, $H = \Delta z + r\,Q^2$, which does not depend on the pump speed. The two measurements therefore give the system
$$
\begin{cases}
20.0 = \Delta z + r \cdot 0.100^2 \\[4pt]
26.0 = \Delta z + r \cdot 0.200^2
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting the first equation from the second gives $6.0 = 0.030\,r$, so $r = 200\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. Substituting into the first equation yields $\Delta z = 20.0 - 200 \cdot 0.0100 = 18.0\,\mathrm{m}$, and the second gives the same value. The value $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is the difference between the two heads, that is, the increase in head loss, while $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is the head at the first point, which also includes the loss.
