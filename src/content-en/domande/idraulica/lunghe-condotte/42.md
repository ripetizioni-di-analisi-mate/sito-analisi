A pump with characteristic curve $H_p = 48 - 800\,Q^2$, with $Q$ in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and $H_p$ in meters, lifts water through a static head of $26\,\mathrm{m}$ along a pipe of hydraulic resistance $r = 1700\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. What is the operating head?

- $48.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $41.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $58.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $72.8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The system curve is $H_c(Q) = 26 + 1700\,Q^2$. At the operating discharge, the head supplied by the pump equals the head required by the system, so $48 - 800\,Q^2 = 26 + 1700\,Q^2$. Collecting the terms in $Q^2$ gives
$$
Q^2 = \frac{48 - 26}{800 + 1700} = \frac{22}{2500} = 8.80 \cdot 10^{-3} ,
$$
so $Q = 0.0938\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The corresponding operating head is
$$
H = 26 + 1700 \cdot 8.80 \cdot 10^{-3} = 41.0\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and the pump curve gives the same value, $48 - 800 \cdot 8.80 \cdot 10^{-3} = 41.0\,\mathrm{m}$, as it must at the operating point. The value $48.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is the shutoff head, which the pump develops at zero discharge.
