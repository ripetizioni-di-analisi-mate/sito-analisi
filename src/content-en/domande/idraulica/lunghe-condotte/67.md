Two constant-level reservoirs are connected by a pipe, with the upstream surface higher than the downstream surface. The pipe crosses a ridge, with its centerline at the crest $3\,\mathrm{m}$ above the upstream free surface, as shown. Which statement is correct?

::schema{acqua="0,3 1.8,3 1.8,1.6 0,1.6;10,1.2 11.8,1.2 11.8,0 10,0" solidi="0,1.3 1.8,1.3 1.8,1.6 0,1.6;10,-0.3 11.8,-0.3 11.8,0 10,0" nodi="A,1.8,2.2,-;C,5,4.2,-;U,10,0.6,-" condotte="A-C-U,0.16" pelo="0,1.8,3,upstream;10,11.8,1.2,downstream" tratteggi="1.8,3 7.8,3;5,4.2 7.8,4.2" quote="7.6 3,7.6 4.2,3.0 m" etichette="5,4.7,high point" titolo="Pipe summit above the upstream reservoir surface"}

- Flow starts spontaneously when the downstream gate valve opens because the upstream reservoir surface is higher
- Flow starts only after the pipe is filled, and the summit gauge pressure remains negative while water flows
- During flow, the summit gauge pressure is positive because the pipe is full
- The water can never pass the summit because it lies above the upstream reservoir surface

## Explanation

Before flow begins, the hydraulic grade line is horizontal at the upstream reservoir level. Water fills the pipe only up to that elevation, leaving the summit $3\,\mathrm{m}$ above it empty. Flow can begin only after the pipe has been filled, or **primed**, for example by removing air at the summit. During flow the summit piezometric head is $z_1 - J x_c$, below $z_1$ and therefore below the centerline there. Summit gauge pressure is consequently **negative**. The pipe operates as a siphon as long as the pressure remains above the allowable minimum.
