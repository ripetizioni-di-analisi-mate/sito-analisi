A supply reservoir at elevation $60\,\mathrm{m}$ and a delivery reservoir at elevation $52\,\mathrm{m}$ both have constant levels. They are connected by a long pipeline of length $3000\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.022$. To deliver $Q = 90\,\mathrm{L/s}$, more than gravity alone would supply, a booster pump is installed as shown. What head must it supply?

::schema{acqua="0,6 1.6,6 1.6,4.6 0,4.6;10.6,5.2 12.4,5.2 12.4,3.8 10.6,3.8" solidi="0,4.3 1.6,4.3 1.6,4.6 0,4.6;10.6,3.5 12.4,3.5 12.4,3.8 10.6,3.8" nodi="A,1.6,5,-;P,3.4,4.4,-;U,10.6,4.2,-" condotte="A-P-U,0.14,D=250 mm" pompa="3.4,4.4,P" pelo="0,1.6,6,60.0 m;10.6,12.4,5.2,52.0 m" etichette="7,3.5,L=3000 m" titolo="Booster pump between two reservoirs with the outlet lower than the inlet"}

- $53.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $45.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $37.2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The hydraulic resistance of the pipe is
$$
r = \frac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5} = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 3000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 5580\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
$$
so the head loss at the required discharge is $r Q^2 = 5580 \cdot 0.090^2 = 45.2\,\mathrm{m}$. The reservoir energy balance gives $H_p = (z_2 - z_1) + r Q^2 = (52 - 60) + 45.2 = 37.2\,\mathrm{m}$, so the favorable elevation difference of $8\,\mathrm{m}$ supplies part of that loss. The value $53.2\,\mathrm{m}$ adds the elevation difference as if the receiving reservoir were higher. Gravity alone would deliver $\sqrt{\dfrac{8}{5580}} = 0.0379\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, so the pump is needed.
