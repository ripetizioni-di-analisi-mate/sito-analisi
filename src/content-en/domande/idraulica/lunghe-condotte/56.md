A long pipeline of length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.022$, starts at a reservoir with free surface at $48\,\mathrm{m}$. It discharges freely to the atmosphere at centerline elevation $30\,\mathrm{m}$ above a receiving tank whose surface is at $24\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What is the discharge?

::schema{acqua="0,5 1.8,5 1.8,3.4 0,3.4;9.8,0.8 12.4,0.8 12.4,-0.4 9.8,-0.4" solidi="0,3.1 1.8,3.1 1.8,3.4 0,3.4;9.8,-0.7 12.4,-0.7 12.4,-0.4 9.8,-0.4" nodi="A,1.8,3.8,-;U,9.4,2.6,-" condotte="A-U,0.16,D=200 mm" pelo="0,1.8,5,48.0 m;10.6,12.4,0.8,24.0 m" frecce="9.5,2.6,10.2,1.6,jet" etichette="8.4,3.25,centerline 30.0 m;5.6,2.5,L=1200 m" titolo="Pipe discharging freely into air above a tank"}

- $59.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $36.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $51.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $206\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

At the outlet the jet is exposed to atmospheric pressure. Gauge pressure is zero, so the hydraulic grade line reaches the outlet centerline elevation, $30\,\mathrm{m}$. The water surface of the tank below does not enter the balance because the jet falls through air before it reaches the tank. The energy gradient is therefore $J = \dfrac{48 - 30}{1200} = 0.015$ and the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 g D J}{\lambda}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.200 \cdot 0.015}{0.022}} = 1.64\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The discharge is therefore $Q = 1.64 \cdot 0.0314 = 0.0514\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $51.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Using the free-surface elevation of the tank below the jet in place of the outlet elevation would give $59.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
