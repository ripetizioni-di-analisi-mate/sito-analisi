A water main must carry $Q = 130\,\mathrm{L/s}$ over a route of length $2200\,\mathrm{m}$ with an available elevation difference $28\,\mathrm{m}$. The steel pipe has roughness coefficient $k_s = 80\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. What theoretical diameter does the Gauckler–Strickler equation give?

- $0.316\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.265\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.718\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The available energy gradient is $J = \dfrac{28}{2200} = 0.012727$. In a full circular pipe the hydraulic radius is $R = \dfrac{D}{4}$, so the Gauckler–Strickler equation for the gradient becomes
$$
J = \frac{10.29\;Q^2}{k_s^2\,D^{\frac{16}{3}}} ,
$$
Solving for diameter gives
$$
D = \left(\frac{10.29\;Q^2}{k_s^2\,J}\right)^{\frac{3}{16}} = \left(\frac{10.29 \cdot 0.130^2}{80^2 \cdot 0.012727}\right)^{\frac{3}{16}} = 0.316\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The theoretical diameter is not a standard size. Select the next larger standard diameter, $400\,\mathrm{mm}$. The value $0.265\,\mathrm{m}$ results from setting $R = \dfrac{D}{2}$, which confuses the hydraulic radius with the geometric radius.
