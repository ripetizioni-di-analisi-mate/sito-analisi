A hydropower plant has gross head $120\,\mathrm{m}$ between its upstream reservoir and tailwater. Its penstock of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 500\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.016$, delivers $Q = 0.55\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ to a turbine of efficiency $\eta = 0.85$, as shown. What is the turbine output power?

- $462\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $550\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $640\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $374\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The friction loss coefficient for the full pipe is $\lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D} = 48$, supporting the long-pipeline approximation. The resistance is $r = \dfrac{8 \cdot 0.016 \cdot 1500}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.5^5} = 63.5\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and the pipe loss is $r Q^2 = 63.5 \cdot 0.55^2 = 19.2\,\mathrm{m}$. The turbine extracts head $H_t$ from the flow. The energy balance between free surfaces gives $120 = 19.2 + H_t$, so the net turbine head is $H_t = 100.8\,\mathrm{m}$. The turbine converts only part of the water power to output power, giving
$$
P = \eta\,\gamma\,Q\,H_t = 0.85 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.55 \cdot 100.8 = 462\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The value $550\,\mathrm{kW}$ uses the gross head. The value $640\,\mathrm{kW}$ divides by the efficiency, as in a pump input-power calculation.
