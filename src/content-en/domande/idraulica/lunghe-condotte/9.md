A pipeline of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.024$, connects reservoirs with free surfaces at $58\,\mathrm{m}$ and $30\,\mathrm{m}$. At a distance $600\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance, its centerline reaches a crest of elevation $56\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. If the gauge pressure at the crest must be at least $-6\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, what is the maximum discharge?

- $128\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $151\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $80.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $63.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The pressure at the summit is $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = z_1 - J x_c - z_c$, so the condition $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} \geq -6\,\mathrm{m}$ gives the following upper bound on the energy gradient:
$$
J \leq \frac{z_1 - z_c + 6}{x_c} = \frac{58 - 56 + 6}{600} = 0.013333 .
$$
At this gradient, the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.300 \cdot 0.013333}{0.024}} = 1.81\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Q = 1.81 \cdot 0.0707 = 0.128\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $128\,\mathrm{L/s}$. With unrestricted flow the energy gradient would rise to $0.018667$ and the discharge to $151\,\mathrm{L/s}$, but the summit pressure would fall to $-9.20\,\mathrm{m}$, ruling out that discharge. At the permissible discharge the friction loss is $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and the gate valve downstream of the summit dissipates the remaining head, $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$.
