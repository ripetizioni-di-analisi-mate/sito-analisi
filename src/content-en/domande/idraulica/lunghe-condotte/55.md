Two constant-level reservoirs with free-surface elevation difference $18\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by a long pipeline with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ carrying $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What power is dissipated by flow resistance in the pipe?

- $0.173\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $2.16\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $21\,200\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $21.2\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

In the long-pipeline model, the full difference between the reservoir levels is lost to friction, so $\Delta H = 18\,\mathrm{m}$. Head loss is the energy dissipated per unit weight. The weight flow rate is $\gamma Q$, so the power dissipated is
$$
P_d = \gamma\,Q\,\Delta H = 9.81 \cdot 0.120 \cdot 18 = 21.2\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The value $0.173\,\mathrm{kW}$ is the kinetic energy flux, $\gamma Q\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, at $v = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$. This is the contribution neglected in the long-pipeline model. Substituting density for specific weight would give $2.16\,\mathrm{kW}$. Leaving the discharge in liters per second would give $21\,200\,\mathrm{kW}$.
