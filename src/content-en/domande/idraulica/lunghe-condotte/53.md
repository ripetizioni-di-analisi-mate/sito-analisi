A cast-iron pipeline of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and roughness coefficient $k_s = 90\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, is analyzed using Gauckler–Strickler. What is its hydraulic resistance $r$, defined by $\Delta H = r Q^2$?

- $1170\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $784\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $1.05 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$
- $0.781\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$

## Explanation

The Gauckler–Strickler equation gives the hydraulic resistance of a full circular pipe as
$$
r = \frac{10.29\,L}{k_s^2\,D^{\frac{16}{3}}} .
$$
Here $D^{\frac{16}{3}} = 0.300^{\frac{16}{3}} = 1.63 \cdot 10^{-3}$, so
$$
r = \frac{10.29 \cdot 1500}{90^2 \cdot 1.63 \cdot 10^{-3}} = \frac{15\,435}{13.2} = 1170\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
The value $784\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ results from using the Darcy–Weisbach exponent $5$ in place of $\dfrac{16}{3}$. Omitting the pipe length would give $0.781\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.
