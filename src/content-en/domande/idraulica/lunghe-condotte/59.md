A long pipeline between two constant-level reservoirs is designed for $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Its theoretical diameter is $232\,\mathrm{mm}$ and standard size $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ is selected. No control valve is installed at the downstream end. Which statement is correct?

- The pipe carries more than $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$, namely the discharge for which the friction loss equals the elevation difference
- The pipe carries $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the residual head leaves the pipe as velocity head at the outlet
- The pipe carries $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the residual head remains as pressure at the downstream reservoir surface
- The pipe carries less than $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$ because the selected diameter differs from the theoretical diameter

## Explanation

For constant reservoir levels and negligible minor losses, the energy balance between the free surfaces requires friction loss to equal the elevation difference, $J L = z_1 - z_2$. With the selected diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, which exceeds the theoretical diameter, a discharge of $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$ would give too little friction loss. The discharge therefore increases until friction consumes all the available head. The pipe carries the discharge calculated for its actual diameter, which exceeds $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The receiving reservoir surface has zero gauge pressure and the long-pipeline model neglects outlet velocity head. A gate valve must dissipate the residual head if the pipe is to deliver exactly the design discharge.
