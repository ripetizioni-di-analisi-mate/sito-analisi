A pipeline of length $1700\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.025$, connects reservoir free surfaces at elevations $64\,\mathrm{m}$ and $30\,\mathrm{m}$. The route must cross a ridge $500\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance. The minimum permissible gauge pressure is $-7\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. What is the maximum pipe centerline elevation at this section?

- $47.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $54.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $71.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $61.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The energy gradient is $J = \dfrac{64 - 30}{1700} = 0.0200$, so the piezometric head at the ridge is
$$
h(500) = 64 - 0.0200 \cdot 500 = 54.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The gauge pressure there is $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = h(500) - z$. The requirement $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} \geq -7\,\mathrm{m}$ can therefore be written as $54.0 - z \geq -7$, giving
$$
z \leq 54.0 + 7 = 61.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Above this elevation, the gauge pressure would fall below the permitted minimum. The route would have to be lowered or the discharge reduced. The elevation $54.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is the hydraulic grade line elevation, the limiting value for zero gauge pressure.
