A pump centerline is $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ above its suction tank surface. The suction pipe has $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, carries $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and its combined friction and minor losses are $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the gauge pressure at the pump inlet?

- $-20.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-18.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-14.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $80.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Take the tank free surface as the elevation datum and write the energy balance between that surface and the pump inlet. The total head at the free surface is $0$, so
$$
0 = z_a + \frac{p}{\gamma} + \frac{v^2}{2g} + \Delta H_a .
$$
For this short suction line, the velocity head must be included. The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.040}{0.0177} = 2.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.261\,\mathrm{m}$, giving
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = -1.5 - 0.261 - 0.35 = -2.11\,\mathrm{m} , \qquad p = 9.81 \cdot (-2.11) = -20.7\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The value $-18.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$ neglects the velocity head. The value $-14.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$ accounts only for the elevation of the pump, and $80.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the absolute pressure.
