A long pipeline carries a prescribed discharge. It is replaced by a pipe of the same length and friction factor but twice the diameter. How does the head loss change at the same discharge?

- It falls to $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- It is halved
- It falls to $\dfrac{1}{32}$
- It falls to $\dfrac{1}{16}$

## Explanation

The head loss in a long pipeline is $\Delta H = r Q^2$, where
$$
r = \frac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5} ,
$$
so for fixed length, friction factor, and discharge, head loss is proportional to $D^{-5}$. Doubling the diameter multiplies the loss by $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^5$, so it **falls to one thirty-second** of its initial value. This fifth-power dependence also explains the effect of deposits, which reduce the effective diameter while increasing roughness.
