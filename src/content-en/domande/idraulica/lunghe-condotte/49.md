A constant-diameter pipeline connects two constant-level reservoirs. Its elevation profile rises and falls along the route. Why is the hydraulic grade line nevertheless straight?

- Because the pipe centerline is straight in plan view
- Because the gauge pressure is the same at every section
- Because discharge, diameter and Darcy friction factor are the same at every section, so the energy gradient is constant
- Because the minor losses are uniformly distributed along the route

## Explanation

In the long-pipeline model the piezometric head at coordinate $x$ is $h(x) = z_1 - J x$, where $z_1$ is the upstream free-surface elevation. The energy gradient $J = \lambda\,\dfrac{1}{D}\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ depends on mean speed, diameter, and the Darcy friction factor. Continuity gives the same discharge at each section. With constant diameter, the mean speed and hence $J$ are constant, so $h(x)$ varies linearly with $x$. The pipe elevation profile $z(x)$ does not enter $h(x)$. Instead it determines the local pressure head $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = h(x) - z(x)$, which can vary from section to section.
