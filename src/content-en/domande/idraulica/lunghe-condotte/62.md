A pipeline starts at a reservoir with free surface at $120\,\mathrm{m}$. Its lowest point is $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance and has centerline elevation $64\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. While the pipe is carrying flow its energy gradient is $0.006$. What is the gauge pressure at the lowest point when the downstream gate valve is closed?

::schema{acqua="0,6 1.8,6 1.8,4.6 0,4.6;11,3.4 12.8,3.4 12.8,2.2 11,2.2" solidi="0,4.3 1.8,4.3 1.8,4.6 0,4.6;11,1.9 12.8,1.9 12.8,2.2 11,2.2" nodi="A,1.8,5,-;B,6.5,0.8,-;U,11,2.6,-" condotte="A-B-U,0.16" pelo="0,1.8,6,120.0 m;11,12.8,3.4,downstream" etichette="6.5,0.15,centerline 64.0 m at 2000 m;8.6,3.0,closed gate valve" titolo="Profile with its lowest point $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance"}

- $432\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $651\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $56.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $549\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

When the gate valve is closed and the water is at rest, the discharge and energy gradient are zero. The hydraulic grade line is horizontal at the upstream free-surface elevation, $120\,\mathrm{m}$. The pressure head at the lowest point is then $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = 120 - 64 = 56.0\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a gauge pressure of
$$
p = 9.81 \cdot 56.0 = 549\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
During flow, the hydraulic grade line at the same section is at elevation $120 - 0.006 \cdot 2000 = 108\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a lower pressure of $432\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $651\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the absolute pressure. The static pressure is the maximum steady-state pressure at this section and is the pressure used here to select the wall thickness.
