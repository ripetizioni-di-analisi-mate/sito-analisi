A transmission main of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$ between reservoirs with an elevation difference $20\,\mathrm{m}$. It is analyzed as a long pipeline. What is the velocity head neglected in this model?

- $0.102\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.00638\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.63\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.300^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.100}{0.0707} = 1.41\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The velocity head is therefore
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.41^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.102\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
about $0.5\,\%$ of the available elevation difference of $20\,\mathrm{m}$. Its small contribution justifies the long-pipeline approximation. The value $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds to $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$. The result $1.63\,\mathrm{m}$ follows from substituting the radius for the diameter when calculating the area.
