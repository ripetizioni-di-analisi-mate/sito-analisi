A pumping system delivers $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at head $H_p = 49.5\,\mathrm{m}$ and overall efficiency $\eta = 0.76$. It operates for $5000$ hours per year and electricity costs $0.22\,\text{€/kWh}$. What is the annual energy cost?

- $64\,100\,\text{€}$
- $48\,700\,\text{€}$
- $84\,300\,\text{€}$
- $383\,000\,\text{€}$

## Explanation

The hydraulic output power is $P_u = \gamma Q H_p = 9.81 \cdot 0.120 \cdot 49.5 = 58.3\,\mathrm{kW}$. Because the pump draws more power than it transfers to the water, its input power is
$$
P = \frac{P_u}{\eta} = \frac{58.3}{0.76} = 76.6\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The station therefore consumes $76.6 \cdot 5000 = 3.83 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{kWh}$ per year, giving an annual electricity cost of
$$
3.83 \cdot 10^{5} \cdot 0.22 = 84\,300\,\text{€} .
$$
Energy is the main operating expense. Its cost increases with the head loss in the discharge main, which is proportional to $D^{-5}$. This is the trade-off used to choose the most economical diameter. The value $64\,100\,\text{€}$ uses hydraulic output power rather than input power and therefore neglects efficiency.
