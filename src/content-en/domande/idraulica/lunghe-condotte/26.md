A long pipeline of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ has energy gradient $J = 0.012$ and carries water of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. What is the product $Re\sqrt{\lambda}$, which can be found directly from the given data in a capacity calculation?

- $4.29 \cdot 10^{4}$
- $3.03 \cdot 10^{4}$
- $6.07 \cdot 10^{4}$
- $6.07 \cdot 10^{7}$

## Explanation

The Darcy–Weisbach equation written in terms of velocity gives $v\sqrt{\lambda} = \sqrt{2 g D J}$. Multiplying both sides by $\dfrac{D}{\nu}$ yields
$$
Re\,\sqrt{\lambda} = \frac{D}{\nu}\,\sqrt{2 g D J} = \frac{0.250}{10^{-6}}\,\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250 \cdot 0.012} ,
$$
where the quantity under the square root is $0.05886\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ and its square root is $0.2426\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Hence $Re\sqrt{\lambda} = 2.50 \cdot 10^{5} \cdot 0.2426 = 6.07 \cdot 10^{4}$. On the right-hand side of the Colebrook–White equation the unknown friction factor appears only in this product, so the factor can be determined in one step. The value $4.29 \cdot 10^{4}$ would result from writing $\sqrt{g D J}$ in place of $\sqrt{2 g D J}$.
