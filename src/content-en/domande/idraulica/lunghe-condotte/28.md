When analyzing a long pipeline with Colebrook–White, in which case can the friction factor be calculated in one step without iteration?

- When the diameter and energy gradient are known
- When the diameter and discharge are known
- When the discharge and energy gradient are known
- When the length and elevation difference are known

## Explanation

In the Colebrook–White equation the unknown $\lambda$ appears on the left-hand side and, on the right, only in the product $Re\sqrt{\lambda}$. When the diameter and energy gradient are known, this product can be calculated directly from the given data, since
$$
Re\,\sqrt{\lambda} = \frac{D}{\nu}\,\sqrt{2 g D J} ,
$$
so the right-hand side has a known value and $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}$ follows directly. If the diameter and discharge are known, the Reynolds number is known, but $\lambda$ remains on both sides. Starting with $\lambda_0 = 0.02$ then gives the friction factor in three or four iterations.
