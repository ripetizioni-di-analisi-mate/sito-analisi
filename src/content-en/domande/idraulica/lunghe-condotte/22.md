A long pipeline of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.024$, carries $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What total head loss does the hydraulic-resistance equation give?

- $2.97\,\mathrm{m}$
- $14.9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $372\,\mathrm{m}$
- $29.8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The hydraulic resistance combines pipe length, diameter, and Darcy friction factor into a single quantity,
$$
r = \frac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5} = \frac{8 \cdot 0.024 \cdot 1500}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.200^5} = 9296\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} .
$$
Since the head loss is proportional to the square of the discharge,
$$
\Delta H = r\,Q^2 = 9296 \cdot 0.040^2 = 14.9\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the same value. The mean velocity is $1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the energy gradient is $9.91 \cdot 10^{-3}$. Multiplying the resistance by the discharge to the first power would give $372\,\mathrm{m}$.
