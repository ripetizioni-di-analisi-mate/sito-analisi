A straight horizontal pipe reach of length $L = 500\,\mathrm{m}$ has diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.025$, and carries the discharge $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Two pressure gauges are mounted at the same elevation at the beginning and at the end of the reach. What is the difference between their readings?

- $8.07\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $79.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $158\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $4.95\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{Q}{A} = \dfrac{0.050}{0.0314} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the Darcy-Weisbach formula gives the friction slope
$$
J = \lambda\,\frac{1}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.025 \cdot \frac{1}{0.200} \cdot \frac{1.59^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.0161 .
$$
The head loss of the reach is $\Delta H = J L = 0.0161 \cdot 500 = 8.07\,\mathrm{m}$. The pipe is horizontal and has constant diameter, so the elevation and the velocity head are the same in the two sections and the whole loss appears as a difference in pressure head. The difference between the readings is therefore $\Delta p = \gamma\,\Delta H = 9.81 \cdot 8.07 = 79.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $8.07\,\mathrm{kPa}$ confuses meters of water column with kilopascals, $158\,\mathrm{kPa}$ uses $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ in place of the velocity head, and $4.95\,\mathrm{kPa}$ computes the area with the diameter in place of the radius.
