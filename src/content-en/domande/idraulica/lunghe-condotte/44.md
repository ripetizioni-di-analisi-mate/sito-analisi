A pump suction pipe has length $8\,\mathrm{m}$, $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, and friction factor $\lambda = 0.024$. Its fittings have combined loss coefficient $\xi = 2.2$, and it carries $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the head loss in the suction line?

- $0.284\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.124\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.816\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.408\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is $0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.050}{0.0314} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity head is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.129\,\mathrm{m}$. In the suction line the minor losses are of the same order as the friction loss, so both must be included. The head loss is
$$
\Delta H_a = \left(\lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \xi\right)\frac{v^2}{2g} = \left(0.024 \cdot \frac{8}{0.200} + 2.2\right) \cdot 0.129 = 0.408\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The friction loss coefficient is $0.96$ and the combined minor loss coefficient is $2.2$. The long-pipeline approximation is therefore unsuitable for this section. Including only the minor losses would give $0.284\,\mathrm{m}$.
