A pump must deliver water from a tank to a higher reservoir through a long pipeline of length $2\,\mathrm{km}$. Why is it installed near the supply tank instead of near the delivery reservoir?

- Because less pump head is required close to the tank
- Because input power is lower near the start of the pipeline
- Because the pump delivers a greater discharge close to the tank
- Because the pipe upstream of the pump is the suction line, and excessive length would reduce $\mathrm{NPSH}_d$ too much through friction losses

## Explanation

The required pump head is $H_p = (z_2 - z_1) + r Q^2$, where $r$ is the resistance of the entire pipeline. The required head, discharge, and input power are therefore independent of pump location. The location sets the length of the suction line, along which the hydraulic grade line falls from the tank surface at the energy gradient. The available net positive suction head is $\mathrm{NPSH}_d = \dfrac{p_{\mathrm{atm}} - p_v}{\gamma} - z_a - \Delta H_a$. With the pump $2\,\mathrm{km}$ from the tank, the suction loss $\Delta H_a$ would include nearly the whole route and amount to several meters. The available suction head would fall below the manufacturer's requirement and the pump would cavitate. Placing the pump near the tank keeps the suction pipe short and $\Delta H_a$ small.
