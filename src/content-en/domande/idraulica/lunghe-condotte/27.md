A long pipeline of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ has energy gradient $J = 0.009$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.021$. What is the discharge?

- $56.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $112\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $79.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $449\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Solving the Darcy–Weisbach equation, $J = \lambda\,\dfrac{1}{D}\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, for the mean velocity gives
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 g D J}{\lambda}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.300 \cdot 0.009}{0.021}} = \sqrt{2.523} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.300^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the discharge is
$$
Q = 1.59 \cdot 0.0707 = 0.112\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 112\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The value $449\,\mathrm{L/s}$ would result from calculating the area as $\pi D^2$, using the diameter in place of the radius. In the specified pipe that discharge would require a velocity outside the normal range for a water main.
