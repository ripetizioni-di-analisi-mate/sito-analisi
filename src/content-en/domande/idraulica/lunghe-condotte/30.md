A water main carries $Q = 90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ along a route of length $1600\,\mathrm{m}$ with an available elevation difference $19.2\,\mathrm{m}$. A standard diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and roughness coefficient $k_s = 90\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ are selected. What residual head must be dissipated by the downstream gate valve?

- $10.1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.08\,\mathrm{m}$
- $19.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With the selected diameter the actual energy gradient is
$$
J = \frac{10.29\;Q^2}{k_s^2\,D^{\frac{16}{3}}} = \frac{10.29 \cdot 0.090^2}{90^2 \cdot 0.300^{\frac{16}{3}}} = 6.33 \cdot 10^{-3} ,
$$
so the friction head loss is $\Delta H_{\mathrm{eff}} = 6.33 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1600 = 10.1\,\mathrm{m}$. Since the standard diameter exceeds the theoretical diameter, the actual loss is less than the available head. The residual head is therefore
$$
19.2 - 10.1 = 9.08\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which can be dissipated by a control valve at the downstream end or retained as additional delivery pressure. The mean velocity is $1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
