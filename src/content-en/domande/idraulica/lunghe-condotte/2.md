A transmission main of length $1800\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.0225$, carries $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What is the total head loss in the long-pipeline model?

- $19.8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $39.7\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.95\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.24\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pipe area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.300^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is
$$
v = \frac{0.120}{0.0707} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the energy gradient
$$
J = \lambda\,\frac{1}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.0225 \cdot \frac{1}{0.300} \cdot \frac{1.70^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.01102 .
$$
In the long-pipeline model the total loss equals the friction loss, so $\Delta H = J L = 0.01102 \cdot 1800 = 19.8\,\mathrm{m}$. The hydraulic resistance $r = \dfrac{8 \lambda L}{g \pi^2 D^5} = 1377\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ gives the same value through $\Delta H = r Q^2 = 1377 \cdot 0.120^2 = 19.8\,\mathrm{m}$.
