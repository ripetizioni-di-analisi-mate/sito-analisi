A pump supplies head $H_p = 50\,\mathrm{m}$ and lifts water through a static head of $36\,\mathrm{m}$ along a discharge pipe of hydraulic resistance $r = 1500\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. What discharge does it deliver?

- $183\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $239\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $96.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $9.33\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The pump must supply the static lift plus the head lost in the pipe. Thus
$$
H_p = (z_2 - z_1) + r\,Q^2 .
$$
Substituting the data gives $50 = 36 + 1500\,Q^2$. The head available to overcome friction is therefore $50 - 36 = 14\,\mathrm{m}$, giving
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{14}{1500}} = 0.0966\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 96.6\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
Thus pipe friction accounts for $14\,\mathrm{m}$ of the $50\,\mathrm{m}$ pump head. The value $183\,\mathrm{L/s}$ would result from assigning the full pump head to friction and neglecting the static lift.
