A pipeline between two reservoirs uses the next larger standard diameter than the theoretical design value. The residual head is dissipated in a downstream gate valve. Which description of its hydraulic grade line is correct?

- It is the straight line joining the two reservoir surfaces
- It falls from the upstream water surface less steeply than the line joining the two surfaces, then drops vertically across the gate valve to the downstream level
- It falls from the upstream water surface more steeply than the line joining the two surfaces, then rises to the downstream level
- It drops vertically by the residual head at the entrance, then descends at the energy gradient to the downstream water surface

## Explanation

When the pipe diameter exceeds the theoretical value, its friction loss is less than the reservoir elevation difference. Its actual energy gradient is therefore smaller than $\dfrac{z_1 - z_2}{L}$, the slope of the line joining the reservoir surfaces. The hydraulic grade line descends from the upstream surface at this gentler slope to the gate valve. Across the valve the residual head is dissipated over a very short distance. The grade line drops vertically by that amount to the downstream level. A local loss lowers the grade line only downstream of the fitting, so placing the valve at the far end gives higher pressures throughout the pipe than the straight line joining the free surfaces would indicate.
