A drip irrigation tube with $D = 10\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $50\,\mathrm{m}$ connects a tank to a free outlet whose centerline is $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. Assuming $\lambda = 0.03$, Darcy–Weisbach gives velocity $v = 0.162\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The water has kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. Which statement is correct?

- The calculation is valid because the Reynolds number is about $1600$ and the flow is turbulent
- The calculation is valid because the Reynolds number plays no part in the check with a specified $\lambda$
- The flow is laminar because the Reynolds number is about $1600$, so $\lambda$ and the velocity must be recalculated using $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re}$
- The flow is laminar, but the velocity remains valid because $\lambda$ is independent of the flow regime

## Explanation

In a capacity calculation with a specified $\lambda$, the Reynolds number must be checked afterward to confirm the assumed flow regime. Here
$$
Re = \frac{v D}{\nu} = \frac{0.162 \cdot 0.010}{10^{-6}} = 1620 ,
$$
which is below $2000$. The flow is therefore laminar, so the assumed value $\lambda = 0.03$, typical of turbulent flow, is inconsistent with the result. In laminar flow, $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re}$. Using this relation in the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives $v = \dfrac{g D^2 J}{32\,\nu}$ with $J = \dfrac{0.20}{50} = 0.004$, so
$$
v = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.010^2 \cdot 0.004}{32 \cdot 10^{-6}} = 0.123\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The resulting Reynolds number is $1230$, consistent with laminar flow.
