A steel pipe with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and absolute roughness $\varepsilon = 0.15\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. What friction factor $\lambda$ does the Colebrook–White equation give?

- $0.0733$
- $0.0183$
- $1.40 \cdot 10^{-4}$
- $0.399$

## Explanation

The pipe area is $0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{0.090}{0.0491} = 1.83\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the Reynolds number is
$$
Re = \frac{v D}{\nu} = \frac{1.83 \cdot 0.250}{10^{-6}} = 4.58 \cdot 10^{5} .
$$
The relative roughness is $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = \dfrac{0.00015}{0.250} = 6.00 \cdot 10^{-4}$, so the Colebrook–White equation becomes
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = -2\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\lambda}} + \frac{6.00 \cdot 10^{-4}}{3.71}\right) .
$$
Starting from $\lambda_0 = 0.0200$ gives $\lambda_1 = 0.0183$. The next iteration agrees within $10^{-4}$, so take $\lambda = 0.0183$. This lies in the usual range from $0.01$ to $0.08$. The value $1.40 \cdot 10^{-4}$ comes from $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re}$, which applies only to laminar flow.
