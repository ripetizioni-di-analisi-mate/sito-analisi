A pump delivers $Q = 75\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at head $H_p = 38\,\mathrm{m}$ with overall efficiency $\eta = 0.80$. What is its input power?

- $28.0\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $22.4\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $3.50 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $35.0\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The hydraulic power delivered to the water equals the weight flow rate times the pump head, giving
$$
P_u = \gamma\,Q\,H_p = 9.81 \cdot 0.075 \cdot 38 = 28.0\,\mathrm{kW} ,
$$
where specific weight is in $\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ and power is in $\mathrm{kW}$. Because mechanical and hydraulic friction dissipate energy, the pump requires more input power than it delivers to the fluid. Its input power is therefore
$$
P = \frac{P_u}{\eta} = \frac{28.0}{0.80} = 35.0\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The value $28.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ is the hydraulic power. Multiplying by the efficiency in place of dividing would give $22.4\,\mathrm{kW}$, an input power lower than the power delivered to the water.
