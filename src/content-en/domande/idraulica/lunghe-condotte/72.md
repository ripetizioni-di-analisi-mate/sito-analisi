A pumping system operates at head $H_p = 45\,\mathrm{m}$ and overall efficiency $\eta = 0.75$. How much energy does it consume to lift one cubic meter of water?

- $0.123\,\mathrm{kWh}$
- $0.0920\,\mathrm{kWh}$
- $0.164\,\mathrm{kWh}$
- $589\,\mathrm{kWh}$

## Explanation

The energy transferred to the water equals its weight times the pump head. One cubic meter of water weighs $9.81\,\mathrm{kN}$, so it receives $9.81 \cdot 45 = 441\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The input energy is the energy delivered divided by the efficiency, giving
$$
E = \frac{9.81 \cdot 45}{0.75} = 589\,\mathrm{kJ} .
$$
Since $1\,\mathrm{kWh} = 3600\,\mathrm{kJ}$, the input energy per cubic meter is $E = \dfrac{589}{3600} = 0.164\,\mathrm{kWh}$. The value $0.123\,\mathrm{kWh}$ is the energy delivered to the water before accounting for efficiency. The value $589\,\mathrm{kWh}$ omits the conversion from kilojoules to kilowatt-hours.
