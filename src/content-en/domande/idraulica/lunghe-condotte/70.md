At the crest of a transmission main the gauge pressure is $-6.5\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, and the atmospheric pressure is $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the absolute pressure at the crest?

- $37.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-63.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $165\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $94.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Expressed in kilopascals, the gauge pressure is $p = 9.81 \cdot (-6.5) = -63.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Adding atmospheric pressure gives the absolute pressure,
$$
p_{\mathrm{abs}} = 101.3 - 63.8 = 37.5\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
$$
which is positive and exceeds the vapor pressure of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, namely $2.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $94.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$ follows from subtracting the head in meters directly from the pressure in kilopascals without converting it using the specific weight.
