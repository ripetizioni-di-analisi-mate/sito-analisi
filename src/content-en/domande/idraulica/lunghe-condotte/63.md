Two pressure gauges on a pipe with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.02$ are $400\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. At section $A$, centerline elevation $35\,\mathrm{m}$, the gauge reads $294.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At section $B$, centerline elevation $23\,\mathrm{m}$, the reading is $392.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the discharge and in which direction does the water flow?

- $54.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$, from $B$ to $A$
- $122\,\mathrm{L/s}$, from $B$ to $A$
- $54.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$, from $A$ to $B$
- $133\,\mathrm{L/s}$, from $A$ to $B$

## Explanation

The water flows in the direction of decreasing piezometric head. The heads at the two sections are
$$
h_A = 35 + \frac{294.3}{9.81} = 35 + 30 = 65.0\,\mathrm{m} , \qquad h_B = 23 + \frac{392.4}{9.81} = 23 + 40 = 63.0\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so water flows **from $A$ to $B$** and the energy gradient is $J = \dfrac{65.0 - 63.0}{400} = 0.005$. The Darcy–Weisbach equation gives $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250 \cdot 0.005}{0.02}} = 1.11\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and hence $Q = 1.11 \cdot 0.0491 = 0.0544\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The result $122\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from $B$ to $A$ comes from comparing pressures alone. Comparing elevations alone would give $133\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
