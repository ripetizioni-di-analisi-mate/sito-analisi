Two constant-level reservoirs with free surfaces at $48\,\mathrm{m}$ and $21\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by a constant-diameter pipeline of length $1800\,\mathrm{m}$. What is its energy gradient?

- $0.0267$
- $0.0117$
- $66.7$
- $0.0150$

## Explanation

Both free surfaces have zero gauge pressure and negligible velocity. The total heads are therefore $H_1 = 48\,\mathrm{m}$ and $H_2 = 21\,\mathrm{m}$. Applying the energy equation with head loss gives $H_1 = H_2 + \Delta H$. The long-pipeline model retains only friction loss, $\Delta H = J L$. Hence
$$
J = \frac{z_1 - z_2}{L} = \frac{48 - 21}{1800} = 0.0150 .
$$
The energy gradient is therefore known before the discharge is calculated. This is the basis of the capacity analysis for a specified pipe. Using the upstream elevation in place of the elevation difference would give $0.0267$.
