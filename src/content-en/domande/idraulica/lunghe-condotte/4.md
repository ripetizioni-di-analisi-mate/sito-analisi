Two reservoirs with elevation difference $22\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by a cast-iron pipe of length $1600\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and absolute roughness $\varepsilon = 0.3\,\mathrm{mm}$. The kinematic viscosity is $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. What discharge does the long-pipeline model predict?

- $100\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $70.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $22.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $142\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The specified data give an energy gradient of $J = \dfrac{22}{1600} = 0.01375$. Since the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives $v\sqrt{\lambda} = \sqrt{2 g D J}$, the product $Re\sqrt{\lambda}$ can be calculated before the discharge is known:
$$
Re\sqrt{\lambda} = \frac{D}{\nu}\,\sqrt{2 g D J} = \frac{0.300}{10^{-6}}\,\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.300 \cdot 0.01375} = 8.53 \cdot 10^{4} .
$$
With $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}$, the Colebrook–White equation now gives $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = 7.05$ directly, so $\lambda = 0.0201$. It follows that
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.300 \cdot 0.01375}{0.0201}} = 2.01\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Q = 2.01 \cdot 0.0707 = 0.142\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
or $142\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The Reynolds number is $6.0 \cdot 10^{5}$, confirming turbulent flow.
