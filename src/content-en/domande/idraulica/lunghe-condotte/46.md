Two constant-level tanks with elevation difference $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by the pipe shown, of length $20\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.025$. The entrance and open gate valve have combined coefficient $\sum \xi = 0.7$, and the velocity head is lost at the exit. What is the discharge?

::schema{acqua="0,3 2,3 2,0.2 0,0.2;6,1.8 8,1.8 8,0.2 6,0.2" solidi="0,-0.1 2,-0.1 2,0.2 0,0.2;6,-0.1 8,-0.1 8,0.2 6,0.2" nodi="A,2,0.8,-;B,6,0.8,-" condotte="A-B,0.2,D=200 mm" pelo="0,2,3,upstream;6,8,1.8,downstream" tratteggi="2,3 9,3;8,1.8 9,1.8" quote="9 3,9 1.8,1.5 m" etichette="4,0.3,L=20 m" titolo="Short pipe between tanks with a head difference of $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $108\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $83.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $95.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $170\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The friction loss coefficient for the full pipe is $\lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D} = 0.025 \cdot \dfrac{20}{0.200} = 2.5$, while the minor losses including the exit give $\sum \xi + 1 = 1.7$. The two are comparable, so the long-pipeline approximation is unsuitable. Include all losses in the energy balance between the reservoir surfaces to obtain
$$
\Delta z = \left(\lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \sum \xi + 1\right)\frac{v^2}{2g} = 4.2\,\frac{v^2}{2g} .
$$
It follows that $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.5}{4.2}} = 2.65\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving $Q = 2.65 \cdot \dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.200^2}{4} = 0.0832\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $83.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Including only pipe friction would give $108\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Omitting the velocity head lost at the outlet would give $95.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
