A pump installed at a fixed elevation above the suction tank surface is operated at a higher discharge. Which statement about the available net positive suction head $\mathrm{NPSH}_d$ is correct?

- It decreases because suction-line losses increase with discharge
- It increases because the velocity at the pump inlet increases
- It stays the same because it depends only on the installation elevation
- It decreases because the atmospheric pressure on the tank surface falls

## Explanation

The available net positive suction head is $\mathrm{NPSH}_d = \dfrac{p_{\mathrm{atm}} - p_v}{\gamma} - z_a - \Delta H_a$. Atmospheric pressure, vapor pressure, and the suction lift $z_a$ are independent of discharge. Suction-line losses are proportional to the velocity head and thus to the square of the discharge. As discharge rises, these losses increase and $\mathrm{NPSH}_d$ **decreases**. For the same installation, the margin above the required value $\mathrm{NPSH}_r$ therefore shrinks, increasing the risk of cavitation.
