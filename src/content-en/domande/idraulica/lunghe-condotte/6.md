A steel pipe of length $1100\,\mathrm{m}$ must carry $Q = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$ with an available elevation difference $18\,\mathrm{m}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.0176$. Which standard diameter should be selected?

- $150\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $200\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $250\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $300\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Solving the head-loss relation $\Delta H = \dfrac{8 \lambda L Q^2}{\pi^2 g D^5}$ for diameter gives
$$
D = \left(\frac{8\,\lambda\,L\,Q^2}{\pi^2 g\,\Delta H}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.0176 \cdot 1100 \cdot 0.070^2}{\pi^2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 18}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = 0.213\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The theoretical diameter is $213\,\mathrm{mm}$. Select the **next larger standard diameter**, $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, which reduces the actual head loss to $8.03\,\mathrm{m}$. This leaves a residual head of $9.97\,\mathrm{m}$ for a control device to dissipate. With $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ the velocity would rise to $2.23\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the loss to $24.5\,\mathrm{m}$, more than the available head. That diameter could not carry the required discharge.
