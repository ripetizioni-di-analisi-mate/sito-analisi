A cast-iron pipe carries water at Reynolds number $Re = 2 \cdot 10^{6}$ and relative roughness $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 2 \cdot 10^{-3}$. In the Colebrook–White equation, the term $\dfrac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\lambda}}$ is about $1.5\,\%$ of the roughness term. What quantities determine the friction factor in this case?

- Only the relative roughness
- Only the Reynolds number
- Only the diameter
- Only the mean velocity

## Explanation

In the Colebrook–White equation
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = -2\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{2.51}{Re\sqrt{\lambda}} + \frac{\varepsilon}{3.71\,D}\right)
$$
the argument of the logarithm is the sum of a term that depends on the Reynolds number and a term that depends on relative roughness. For the given data the first term is negligible compared with the second, so the Darcy friction factor depends **only on relative roughness**. This is the fully rough turbulent regime. Substitution gives $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = -2\log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{2 \cdot 10^{-3}}{3.71}\right) = 6.54$, so $\lambda = 0.0234$. Power-law formulas independent of the Reynolds number, such as the Gauckler–Strickler equation, apply in this regime.
