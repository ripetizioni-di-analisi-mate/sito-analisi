A long pipeline starts at a reservoir with free surface at elevation $70\,\mathrm{m}$ and operates with energy gradient $J = 0.0125$. At distance $900\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance the centerline elevation is $52\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What is the gauge pressure there?

::schema{nodi="A,1.8,4.63,-;B,6.6,2.63,-;U,11.4,1.9,-" acqua="0,5.2 1.8,5.2 1.8,4.1 0,4.1" solidi="0,3.8 1.8,3.8 1.8,4.1 0,4.1" condotte="A-B-U,0.16" pelo="0,1.8,5.2,70.0 m" etichette="7.2,1.35,centerline 52.0 m at 900 m;3.1,3.25,J=0.0125" titolo="Long-pipeline profile with the section $900\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance"}

- $66.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $168\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $177\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-66.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

At a distance $x$ from the inlet, the piezometric head is $h(x) = z_1 - J x$, giving
$$
h(900) = 70 - 0.0125 \cdot 900 = 58.75\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pressure head is the vertical distance from the pipe centerline to the hydraulic grade line, so
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = 58.75 - 52 = 6.75\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and multiplying by the specific weight gives $p = 9.81 \cdot 6.75 = 66.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Gauge pressure is positive, so the hydraulic grade line lies above the pipe centerline at this section. The value $168\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the absolute pressure. The question asks for gauge pressure.
