A pump’s suction and discharge flanges have equal elevation and diameter. In operation, the suction vacuum gauge reads $-35\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the discharge pressure gauge reads $420\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the pump head?

- $42.8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $46.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $39.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.64\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Pump head is the rise in total head between the inlet and outlet sections. The sections are at the same elevation and have the same diameter, so continuity gives equal velocities. The total head rise therefore equals the difference in pressure head, giving
$$
H_p = \frac{p_m - p_a}{\gamma} = \frac{420 - (-35)}{9.81} = \frac{455}{9.81} = 46.4\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Using the discharge gauge reading alone would give $42.8\,\mathrm{m}$. Treating the suction gauge reading as positive before subtracting it would give $39.2\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $4.64\,\mathrm{m}$ follows from an error by a factor of $10$ in specific weight, using $98.1$ in place of $9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$.
