A pressurized pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ has mean velocity $v = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.028$. What is the energy gradient from the Darcy–Weisbach equation?

- $0.0321$
- $0.00321$
- $0.0161$
- $0.0642$

## Explanation

The Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the energy gradient as the product of the Darcy friction factor, the reciprocal of the diameter, and the velocity head. Thus
$$
J = \lambda\,\frac{1}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.028 \cdot \frac{1}{0.200} \cdot \frac{1.5^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} .
$$
The velocity head is $\dfrac{1.5^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.1147\,\mathrm{m}$, giving $J = 0.028 \cdot 5 \cdot 0.1147 = 0.0161$. Over a pipe length of $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, the corresponding head loss is $16.1\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0.0642$ would result from substituting the hydraulic radius $R = \dfrac{D}{4}$ for the pipe diameter in the Darcy–Weisbach equation.
