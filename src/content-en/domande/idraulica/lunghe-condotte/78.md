Two identical pumps operate in parallel, each with characteristic curve $H_p = 50 - 2400\,Q^2$, where $Q$ is measured in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and $H_p$ in meters. The static lift is $30\,\mathrm{m}$ and the discharge-pipe resistance is $r = 1400\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. What total discharge do the pumps deliver?

- $72.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $100\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $145\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $87.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Two identical pumps in parallel develop the same head and share the total discharge equally. Substituting $\dfrac{Q}{2}$ into the single-pump curve therefore gives the combined pump curve
$$
H_p = 50 - 2400\left(\frac{Q}{2}\right)^2 = 50 - 600\,Q^2 .
$$
At the operating point, the pump head equals the head required by the system. Thus $50 - 600\,Q^2 = 30 + 1400\,Q^2$, giving $Q^2 = \dfrac{20}{2000} = 0.01$ and $Q = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The value $72.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is the discharge with one pump operating alone. Simply doubling it gives $145\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and overlooks the increase in pipe friction at the higher discharge.
