A pump with curve $H_p = 60 - 1000\,Q^2$ lifts water through a static head of $40\,\mathrm{m}$ along a pipe of resistance $r = 2000\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$, with $Q$ in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and heads in meters. The discharge is to be reduced to $60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ by partially closing a gate valve on the discharge side. What head loss must the valve produce?

- $16.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At the target discharge $Q = 0.060\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, the pump develops the head
$$
H_p = 60 - 1000 \cdot 0.060^2 = 56.4\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
while the system without the gate valve requires $40 + 2000 \cdot 0.060^2 = 47.2\,\mathrm{m}$. At the new operating point the system must require the full head supplied by the pump. The valve must therefore dissipate the difference, $56.4 - 47.2 = 9.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $16.4\,\mathrm{m}$ neglects friction along the pipe, while $7.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is the pipe friction loss alone.
