A pipeline of length $30\,\mathrm{m}$, with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and friction factor $\lambda = 0.024$, has minor losses with combined coefficient $\sum \xi = 1.4$ and loses its velocity head at the outlet. What fraction of the total loss is due to friction along the pipe?

- $45.5\,\%$
- $67.3\,\%$
- $98.0\,\%$
- $54.5\,\%$

## Explanation

All losses are proportional to the same velocity head, so their relative contributions depend on their coefficients. For friction along the pipe, the coefficient is
$$
\lambda\,\frac{L}{D} = 0.024 \cdot \frac{30}{0.250} = 2.88 ,
$$
while the minor losses and the outlet give $\sum \xi + 1 = 2.4$. The fraction of the total loss due to pipe friction is therefore
$$
\frac{2.88}{2.88 + 2.4} = 0.545 ,
$$
or $54.5\,\%$. The two contributions are comparable in size, so this pipe requires the full energy balance.
