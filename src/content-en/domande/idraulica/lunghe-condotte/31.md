A design gives theoretical diameter $213\,\mathrm{mm}$. The available standard sizes are $100$, $150$, $200$, $250$, and $300\,\mathrm{mm}$. Why is $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ selected instead of $200\,\mathrm{mm}$?

- Because the smaller diameter would give a velocity below the allowable limit
- Because the larger diameter eliminates minor losses
- Because the smaller diameter would produce a head loss greater than the available head
- Because the larger diameter reduces the cost of the pipe

## Explanation

At fixed discharge, head loss is proportional to $D^{-5}$ and decreases as diameter increases. The theoretical diameter is the one for which the loss exactly equals the available head. With a smaller diameter the loss **would exceed the available head**, so the pipe could not deliver the specified discharge. With the next larger standard diameter the actual loss is smaller, leaving a residual head that a control device at the downstream end can dissipate. Pipe cost grows with diameter, so it does not explain the choice.
