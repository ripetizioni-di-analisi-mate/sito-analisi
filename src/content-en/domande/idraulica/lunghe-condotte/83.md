Two pumps $A$ and $B$, installed in turn in the same system, share an operating point when the delivery reservoir is at its mean level. The curve for $A$ is very steep, while that of $B$ is almost flat. The reservoir level fluctuates by a few meters about its mean during the day. Which statement is correct?

- The discharge varies less with pump $B$
- The discharge varies by the same amount for both pumps because the system curve is the same
- The discharge varies less with pump $A$
- The discharge stays constant with both pumps because the pipe resistance stays the same

## Explanation

A change in the receiving reservoir level changes the static lift, $\Delta z_g$, and shifts the system curve $H_c = \Delta z_g + r Q^2$ vertically without changing its shape. The new operating point still satisfies $H_p(Q) = \Delta z_g + r Q^2$. Differentiating with respect to $\Delta z_g$ gives, for a small change in level,
$$
\delta Q \simeq -\frac{\delta(\Delta z_g)}{s + 2 r Q_F} ,
$$
where $s = |H_p'(Q_F)|$ is the magnitude of the pump-curve slope at the operating point. For the same level change, $|\delta Q|$ is smaller when $s$ is larger. The discharge therefore varies less with pump $A$, whose curve is steeper. For pump $B$, the flatter curve gives a smaller $s$, so the same change in level causes a larger change in discharge.
