An oil pipeline with $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries oil of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ at mean velocity $v = 0.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the energy gradient?

- $0.0130$
- $0.00652$
- $6.52 \cdot 10^{-5}$
- $0.00326$

## Explanation

The Reynolds number is $Re = \dfrac{v D}{\nu} = \dfrac{0.8 \cdot 0.200}{10^{-4}} = 1600$, below $2000$, so the flow is laminar. The Hagen–Poiseuille law gives the Darcy friction factor for laminar flow as $\lambda = \dfrac{64}{Re} = 0.04$. The Darcy–Weisbach equation then gives
$$
J = \lambda\,\frac{1}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.04 \cdot \frac{1}{0.200} \cdot \frac{0.8^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.00652 .
$$
The value $6.52 \cdot 10^{-5}$ follows from using the viscosity of water in the same calculation. The value $0.00326$ follows from using $\lambda = \dfrac{32}{Re}$.
