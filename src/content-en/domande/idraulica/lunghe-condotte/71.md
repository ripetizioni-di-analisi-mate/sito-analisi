A pump takes water from a tank with free-surface elevation $10\,\mathrm{m}$ and adds head $H_p = 55\,\mathrm{m}$. Its discharge pipe has length $2400\,\mathrm{m}$ and energy gradient $J = 0.008$. At a distance $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pump it crosses a crest at centerline elevation $52\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Neglecting suction-pipe losses, what is the gauge pressure at the crest?

::schema{acqua="0,1 1.6,1 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;11,4.6 12.8,4.6 12.8,3.2 11,3.2" solidi="0,-0.7 1.6,-0.7 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;11,2.9 12.8,2.9 12.8,3.2 11,3.2" nodi="A,1.6,0.4,-;P,3,0.4,-;C,7,5.2,-;U,11,3.6,-" condotte="A-P-C-U,0.14" pompa="3,0.4,P" pelo="0,1.6,1,10.0 m;11,12.8,4.6,outlet" etichette="7,5.7,high point 52.0 m at 1500 m" titolo="Discharge main crossing a summit $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pump"}

- $-9.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $+1.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-6.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $+13.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Suction-line losses are neglected, so the head upstream of the pump equals the reservoir surface elevation, $10\,\mathrm{m}$. The pump raises the head, placing the hydraulic grade line immediately downstream at $10 + 55 = 65\,\mathrm{m}$. The grade line falls along the discharge main at the energy gradient, so at the summit
$$
h = 65 - 0.008 \cdot 1500 = 53.0\,\mathrm{m} , \qquad \frac{p}{\gamma} = 53.0 - 52 = +1.00\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Omitting the upstream free-surface elevation would give $-9.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Subtracting the loss along the entire discharge line rather than only the loss up to the summit would give $-6.20\,\mathrm{m}$.
