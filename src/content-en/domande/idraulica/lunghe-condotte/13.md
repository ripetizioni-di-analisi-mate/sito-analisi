A pump draws $Q = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from a tank through the suction pipe shown, of length $10\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.0235$. The minor losses have combined coefficient $\xi = 1.8$. The manufacturer specifies $\mathrm{NPSH}_r = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at the operating discharge. Atmospheric pressure is $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the vapor pressure is $2.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the maximum pump elevation above the tank surface?

::schema{nodi="S,1.6,0.4,-;G,1.6,2.4,-;P,5.2,3.2,-" acqua="0,1.6 3,1.6 3,-0.8 0,-0.8" solidi="0,-1.1 3,-1.1 3,-0.8 0,-0.8" condotte="S-G-P,0.14" pompa="5.2,3.2,P" pelo="0,3,1.6,tank" tratteggi="1.6,1.6 6.6,1.6;5.2,3.2 6.6,3.2" quote="6.4 1.6,6.4 3.2,z_a" etichette="2.6,3.5,L=10 m;2.6,2.9,ξ=1.8;3.4,2,D=250 mm" titolo="Suction pipe for a pump to be installed above the free surface"}

- $6.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12.8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.1\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity in the suction line is $v = \dfrac{0.070}{0.0491} = 1.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the velocity head is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.104\,\mathrm{m}$ and the total suction-line loss is
$$
\Delta H_a = \left(\lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \xi\right)\frac{v^2}{2g} = \left(0.0235 \cdot \frac{10}{0.250} + 1.8\right) \cdot 0.104 = 0.284\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The barometric term is $\dfrac{p_{\mathrm{atm}} - p_v}{\gamma} = \dfrac{101.3 - 2.34}{9.81} = 10.09\,\mathrm{m}$. The condition $\mathrm{NPSH}_d \geq \mathrm{NPSH}_r$ therefore gives
$$
z_a \leq 10.09 - 0.284 - 3.0 = 6.80\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Above this elevation the available net positive suction head would fall below the pump requirement and vapor bubbles would form in the impeller. The value $10.09\,\mathrm{m}$ includes only the barometric term and would be the theoretical limit with no losses and zero $\mathrm{NPSH}_r$.
