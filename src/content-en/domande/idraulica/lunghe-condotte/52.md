An old cast-iron pipe with $D = 500\,\mathrm{mm}$ and absolute roughness $\varepsilon = 1\,\mathrm{mm}$ operates in fully rough turbulent flow, so the term containing $Re\sqrt{\lambda}$ in Colebrook–White is negligible. What is the friction factor?

- $0.0343$
- $0.00441$
- $0.0234$
- $0.153$

## Explanation

The relative roughness is $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = \dfrac{1}{500} = 0.002$. The term containing the Reynolds number is negligible, so the Colebrook–White equation reduces to
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} = -2\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{0.002}{3.71}\right) = -2\log_{10}\!\left(5.39 \cdot 10^{-4}\right) = 6.54 ,
$$
giving $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{6.54^2} = 0.0234$. The value $0.0343$ follows from omitting the factor $3.71$. Using the natural logarithm would give $0.00441$. The result $0.153$ follows from omitting the square root of $\lambda$.
