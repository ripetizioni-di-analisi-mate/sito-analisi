Electricity becomes more expensive while the discharge, static lift, pipe cost, and operating hours of a pumping system remain the same. Which statement about the economical discharge-pipe diameter is correct?

- It decreases because pipe cost makes up a greater part of the total cost
- It stays the same because it depends only on discharge and static lift
- It increases because it pays to reduce the head loss and therefore the required pump head
- It decreases because the input power increases with pipe diameter

## Explanation

The annual cost is $C(D) = a + \dfrac{b}{D^5} + c\,D$, where $\dfrac{b}{D^5}$ is the energy cost of overcoming the friction head loss $r Q^2$ and $b$ is proportional to the price of electricity. Setting the derivative to zero gives the economic diameter
$$
D_{\mathrm{ec}} = \left(\frac{5\,b}{c}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}} ,
$$
which increases with $b$. Higher electricity prices therefore favor a **larger** diameter because the reduction in head loss and required pump head offsets the higher pipe cost.
