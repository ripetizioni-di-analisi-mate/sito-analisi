A pump draws input power $P = 22\,\mathrm{kW}$ at overall efficiency $\eta = 0.73$ and lifts water through a static head of $30\,\mathrm{m}$ along a pipe of length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.0235$, and hydraulic resistance $r = 799\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. What discharge does it deliver?

- $54.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $51.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $66.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $85.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The discharge must satisfy both the input-power relation and the pump-head relation:
$$
\begin{cases}
P = \dfrac{\gamma\,Q\,H_p}{\eta} \\[8pt]
H_p = 30 + r\,Q^2
\end{cases}
$$
Substitution of the second relation into the first gives $9.81\,Q\,(30 + 799\,Q^2) = 22 \cdot 0.73 = 16.06$. At the initial estimate $Q_0 = 0.050\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, the left-hand side is $15.69\,\mathrm{kW}$, so the discharge must be increased. At $Q = 0.0510\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ the left-hand side is $16.05\,\mathrm{kW}$, giving a pumped discharge of $51.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The corresponding head is $H_p = 30 + 799 \cdot 0.0510^2 = 32.1\,\mathrm{m}$, including $2.08\,\mathrm{m}$ of head loss. Neglecting the loss would give $54.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$, an overestimate of the discharge.
