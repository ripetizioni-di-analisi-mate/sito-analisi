A transmission main of length $3000\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ is analyzed using the long-pipeline model. What does this model assume about the hydraulic grade line and energy grade line?

- They coincide because velocity head is neglected
- The hydraulic grade line lies above the energy grade line
- The two lines have different slopes
- The energy grade line is horizontal

## Explanation

The total head at a section is $H = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma} + \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ and the piezometric head is $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$. Their difference is the velocity head. The long-pipeline approximation neglects velocity head compared with friction head loss, so $H \simeq h$ and the energy grade line and hydraulic grade line **coincide**. Both lines fall at the energy gradient $J$, which is constant because the pipe diameter and hence the mean velocity are constant. The energy grade line would be horizontal only in the absence of losses, as for an ideal fluid.
