A small hole opens at the crest of an operating pipeline where the gauge pressure is $-4\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. What happens at the hole?

- Water emerges as a jet rising $4\,\mathrm{m}$ above the hole
- Air enters the pipe
- Nothing enters or leaves because the absolute pressure at the summit is positive
- Water flows out until the upstream reservoir is empty

## Explanation

The summit gauge pressure is negative, so the absolute pressure inside the pipe is below the atmospheric pressure outside. Fluid crosses the hole from higher to lower pressure, so air enters the pipe. The internal absolute pressure is positive, about $10.3 - 4 = 6.3\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, but the flow direction depends on its comparison with the outside atmospheric pressure. Air accumulates at the summit and forms a pocket that reduces the effective flow area.
