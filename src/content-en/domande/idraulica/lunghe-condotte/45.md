In a pumping system the elevation difference does not fix the discharge-pipe diameter, because the pump supplies the required head. Why is there an economical diameter?

- Because pipe cost increases with diameter and energy cost decreases, so their sum has a minimum
- Because above a certain diameter the velocity drops below the allowable value
- Because the required pump head increases with the diameter of the discharge main
- Because standard pipe diameters have an upper limit

## Explanation

Pipe cost increases approximately in proportion to diameter, so the annualized capital cost increases with $D$. At a specified discharge, annual energy cost is proportional to the pump head, which contains the friction term $r Q^2$ proportional to $D^{-5}$. The annual energy cost therefore decreases as $D$ increases. The sum of these two costs has a **minimum**. The corresponding diameter is the economic pipe diameter, which is then rounded to the nearest standard size.
