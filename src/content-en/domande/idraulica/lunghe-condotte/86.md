The total annual cost of a pumping system, in thousands of euros, is given below as a function of the discharge-pipe diameter $D$ in meters.
$$
C(D) = 18 + \frac{0.05}{D^5} + 80\,D .
$$
Which diameter minimizes the cost?

- $0.292\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.382\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.315\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.224\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The second term is the energy cost, which decreases as $D$ increases. The third is the annualized pipe cost, which increases with $D$. The derivative is
$$
C'(D) = -\frac{5 \cdot 0.05}{D^6} + 80 ,
$$
which is negative for small $D$ and positive for large $D$, so the minimum occurs where it is zero. From $D^6 = \dfrac{0.25}{80} = 3.125 \cdot 10^{-3}$ we obtain $D = 0.382\,\mathrm{m}$, corresponding to standard diameter $400\,\mathrm{mm}$. The value $0.292\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the factor $5$ in the derivative of $D^{-5}$, while $0.315\,\mathrm{m}$ uses exponent $\dfrac{1}{5}$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{6}$.
