A mountain water main is at an elevation where the atmospheric pressure is $88.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Water temperature is $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and vapor pressure is $2.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At what gauge pressure, in meters of water, does the water reach its vapor pressure?

- $-10.1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $+8.73\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-7.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-8.73\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Water reaches its vapor pressure when its absolute pressure equals $p_v$. The corresponding gauge pressure is
$$
p - p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 2.34 - 88.0 = -85.7\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
$$
or $\dfrac{-85.7}{9.81} = -8.73\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. At sea level, the corresponding pressure head is $-10.1\,\mathrm{m}$ for $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The limiting gauge pressure head is therefore closer to zero at altitude. The value $-7\,\mathrm{m}$ is the practical sea-level limit used to provide a margin against the release of dissolved air.
