Two pipelines of equal length, diameter, and friction factor connect the same two constant-level reservoirs. One is laid on flat ground. The other rises and falls across hilly terrain, but always remains below the hydraulic grade line. Which statement about the discharges is correct under the long-pipeline model?

- The second pipe carries less water because it must lift the water over the high points
- The discharges are equal because the energy gradient is $\dfrac{z_1 - z_2}{L}$ in both pipes
- The second pipe carries more water because the flow accelerates on descending sections
- The discharge in the second pipe depends on the elevation of its highest point

## Explanation

The long-pipeline equation assigns the whole reservoir head difference to friction, $z_1 - z_2 = J L$, so the energy gradient $J = \dfrac{z_1 - z_2}{L}$ is the same in both pipes. Their diameters and friction factors are also equal, so the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the same velocity $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 g D J}{\lambda}}$ and hence the same discharge. The pipe centerline elevation does not appear in the balance between reservoir surfaces. At a given section the piezometric head $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ is the same in both pipes. Where the centerline is higher, the pressure head is lower by the same amount. The elevation profile therefore determines the pressure distribution alone. Gauge pressure in the second pipe remains positive because its centerline lies below the hydraulic grade line.
