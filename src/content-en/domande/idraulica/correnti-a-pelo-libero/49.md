In which of the following channel sections does the gauge pressure at the bed differ from $\gamma\,h$, where $h$ is the local vertical water depth?

- At the brink of a bed drop, where the water falls and the streamlines curve sharply downward
- At a section with uniform supercritical flow
- At a subcritical section where the flow slows gradually many kilometers from a sluice gate
- On the bed of a trapezoidal channel, where hydraulic depth is smaller than water depth

## Explanation

The bed pressure is $\gamma\,h$ when the pressure distribution is hydrostatic. This holds for straight, parallel streamlines. At the brink of a bed drop the streamlines curve sharply downward. Acceleration toward the center of curvature changes the pressure distribution and lowers the bed pressure below $\gamma\,h$. In uniform flow the streamlines are parallel to the bed at any Froude number, so even supercritical flow has a hydrostatic pressure distribution. Gradually varied flow has nearly parallel streamlines and can be treated the same way. A trapezoidal section changes the hydraulic depth used in the Froude number, while at the bed the local depth remains $h$ and the pressure $\gamma\,h$.
