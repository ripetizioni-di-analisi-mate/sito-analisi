The specific energy of a subcritical flow increases at constant discharge. What happens to the water depth?

- It decreases
- It stays the same
- It becomes equal to critical depth
- It increases

## Explanation

At constant discharge, the derivative of specific energy with respect to water depth is
$$
\frac{dE}{dh} = 1 - Fr^2 .
$$
For subcritical flow $Fr < 1$, so $\dfrac{dE}{dh} > 0$ and the function $E(h)$ increases over the interval $h > h_c$. An increase in specific energy therefore corresponds to an **increase in water depth**. A downward bed step increases specific energy and consequently raises the water surface relative to the bed in subcritical flow.
