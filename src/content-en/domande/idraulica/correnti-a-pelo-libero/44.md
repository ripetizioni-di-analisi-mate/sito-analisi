Water in a rectangular channel is initially at rest at a depth of $h = 0.900\,\mathrm{m}$. At what speed does a small free-surface disturbance travel?

- $1.49\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $8.83\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

A free-surface disturbance whose amplitude is small compared with the water depth travels at a speed $c = \sqrt{g\,h}$ relative to the water. This follows from applying continuity and the integral momentum equation to a control volume containing the wavefront, in a frame moving with it. Substitution gives
$$
c = \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.900} = \sqrt{8.829} = 2.97\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $8.83\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the product $g\,h$, with dimensions of velocity squared. A square root is therefore necessary.
