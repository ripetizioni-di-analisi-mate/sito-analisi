The headrace of a hydroelectric plant is $4.00\,\mathrm{km}$ long and carries $15.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in uniform flow on a bed slope of $i = 0.0003$. How much power is dissipated by flow resistance along the headrace?

- $18.0\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $177\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $44.1\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

In uniform flow the energy gradient equals the bed slope, giving a head loss of $\Delta H = i\,L = 0.0003 \cdot 4000 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ over the channel length. Each unit weight of water loses energy $\Delta H$ and the weight of water passing per second is $\gamma\,Q$, so the power dissipated is
$$
P = \gamma\,Q\,\Delta H = 9.81 \cdot 15.0 \cdot 1.20 = 177\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The plant loses this power because the water surface at the head tank is $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ below the intake. The value $18.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ uses density rather than specific weight, and $44.1\,\mathrm{kW}$ is the power dissipated in one kilometer. A zero result confuses constant velocity with zero head loss.
