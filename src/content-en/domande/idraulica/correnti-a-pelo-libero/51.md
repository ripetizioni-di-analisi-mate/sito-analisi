At a section of a trapezoidal channel, the bottom width is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, the side slope is $m = 1$, the water depth is $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the discharge is $6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. How far above the water surface should the energy grade line be drawn?

- $0.408\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.459\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With a hydrostatic distribution of pressure, the hydraulic grade line coincides with the free surface and the energy grade line lies one velocity head above it. The flow area is $A = (2.00 + 1 \cdot 1.00) \cdot 1.00 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so $v = \dfrac{6.00}{3.00} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{2.00^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.204\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Using only the rectangular area $b\,h$ would give $0.459\,\mathrm{m}$. Using $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ in place of $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ would give $0.408\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is the specific energy, the vertical distance from the bed to the energy grade line.
