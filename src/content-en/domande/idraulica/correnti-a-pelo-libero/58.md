A rectangular channel several kilometers long has a horizontal bed with slope $i = 0$ and a constant cross section. Can it carry a discharge of $Q > 0$ in uniform flow?

- No, because uniform flow requires $J = i = 0$, whereas moving water dissipates energy
- Yes, if the water depth equals critical depth
- Yes, at any depth, because the weight has no component in the flow direction
- Yes, provided that the flow is supercritical

## Explanation

In uniform flow the friction slope equals the bed slope, so $i = 0$ would require $J = 0$ and thus zero head loss. Water moving at $v > 0$ exerts shear stress on the boundary and loses head. Hence $J > 0$ and the condition $J = i$ cannot hold. The same conclusion follows from $\tau_0 = \gamma\,R\,i$, which for $i = 0$ would give zero boundary shear stress. On a horizontal bed the water depth must therefore vary along the channel. Such profiles are treated in the chapter on gradually varied flow and hydraulic jumps.
