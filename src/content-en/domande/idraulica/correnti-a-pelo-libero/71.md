For a rectangular channel at a given discharge, the specific-energy curve $E(h) = h + \dfrac{q^2}{2 g h^2}$ is plotted with $E$ on the horizontal axis and $h$ on the vertical axis. Which straight line does the curve approach at large water depths?

- The horizontal line $h = h_c$
- The vertical line $E = E_{\min}$
- The depth axis, $E = 0$
- The angle bisector $E = h$

## Explanation

As $h$ increases, the kinetic term $\dfrac{q^2}{2 g h^2}$ tends to zero. The difference $E - h$ therefore tends to zero and the curve approaches the line $E = h$. In physical terms, a deep, slow flow has negligible velocity head, so specific energy almost equals water depth. As $h$ approaches zero, the kinetic term diverges and the curve approaches the energy axis. The line $h = h_c$ passes through the curve's minimum and the line $E = E_{\min}$ is tangent to the curve there.
