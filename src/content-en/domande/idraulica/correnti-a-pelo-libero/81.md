A river is wide enough for its hydraulic radius to be approximated by the water depth. The discharge per unit width is $q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ and the bed roughness coefficient is $k_s = 35\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. What is the critical slope?

- $0.0899$
- $0.00809$
- $0.0274$
- $0.283$

## Explanation

The critical depth is $h_c = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{9.00}{9.81}} = 0.972\,\mathrm{m}$ and the critical velocity is $v_c = \dfrac{q}{h_c} = 3.09\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With $R_c = h_c$ the definition of critical slope gives
$$
i_c = \frac{v_c^2}{k_s^2\,h_c^{\frac{4}{3}}} = \frac{3.09^2}{35^2 \cdot 0.962} = 0.00809 .
$$
Since $v_c^2 = g\,h_c$ at critical flow, the same equation can be written as $i_c = \dfrac{g}{k_s^2\,h_c^{\frac{1}{3}}}$ and gives the same value. The value $0.0899$ treats $i_c^{\frac{1}{2}}$ as the slope. The value $0.283$ omits the square on $k_s$.
