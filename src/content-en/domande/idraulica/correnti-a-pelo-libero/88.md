A rectangular channel carries critical flow at a depth of $h_c$. A small disturbance is generated at the free surface. How does the part of the disturbance traveling against the flow move relative to the banks?

- It travels upstream with velocity $\sqrt{g\,h_c}$
- It remains stationary at the point where it formed
- It is swept downstream with velocity $2\sqrt{g\,h_c}$
- It travels upstream with velocity $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{g\,h_c}$

## Explanation

At critical flow $Fr = 1$, so the flow velocity equals the small-disturbance wave speed, $v = c = \sqrt{g\,h_c}$. Relative to the banks, the upstream-traveling disturbance moves at $c - v = 0$. It therefore **stays at the point where it formed**. The other disturbance travels downstream at $c + v = 2\sqrt{g\,h_c}$. The speed $\sqrt{g\,h_c}$ is relative to the water. For the upstream-traveling disturbance, its velocity relative to the water is equal and opposite to the velocity of the flow carrying it downstream.
