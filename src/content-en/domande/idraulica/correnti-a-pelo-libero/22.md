A precast semicircular channel of diameter $D = 0.600\,\mathrm{m}$ has bed slope $i = 0.005$ and coefficient $k_s = 80\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. What is the uniform-flow discharge when the water reaches the rim of the channel?

- $0.358\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $0.226\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $0.452\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $0.310\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

With the water at the rim, the flow area is the half circle, of area $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{8} = 0.141\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and the wetted perimeter is the half circumference $C = \dfrac{\pi D}{2}$. The hydraulic radius is therefore $R = \dfrac{A}{C} = \dfrac{D}{4} = 0.150\,\mathrm{m}$, the same as for a full circular pipe. In uniform flow the friction slope equals the bed slope, so the Gauckler-Strickler formula gives
$$
v = k_s\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}} = 80 \cdot 0.150^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.005^{\frac{1}{2}} = 80 \cdot 0.282 \cdot 0.0707 = 1.60\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The discharge follows from the continuity equation, $Q = A\,v = 0.141 \cdot 1.60 = 0.226\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Using the geometric radius $\dfrac{D}{2}$ instead of the hydraulic radius would give $0.358\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, using the area of the full circle $0.452\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, and using the exponent $\dfrac{1}{2}$ on the hydraulic radius $0.310\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
