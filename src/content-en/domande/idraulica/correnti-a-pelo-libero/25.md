The stage–discharge relation $Q(h)$ of a rectangular channel is strictly increasing. What does this imply about the normal depth?

- Each discharge has two normal depths, one subcritical and one supercritical
- Normal depth is independent of discharge
- Each discharge has a unique normal depth
- Normal depth equals critical depth

## Explanation

A strictly increasing function takes each value at most once. Normal depth is defined by $Q(h_0) = Q$, so each specified discharge corresponds to **a single** normal depth. The discharge increases monotonically because both flow area and hydraulic radius increase with depth, and the discharge $Q = k_s\,A\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}$ increases with both. The depths that occur in pairs are alternate depths, which solve the specific-energy equation at a specified discharge.
