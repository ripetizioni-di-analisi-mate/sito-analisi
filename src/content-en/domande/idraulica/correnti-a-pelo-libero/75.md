On a channel's specific-energy curve at constant discharge, the derivative at the point representing the flow is $\dfrac{dE}{dh} = 0.750$. What is the Froude number, and what is the flow regime?

- $Fr = 0.750$, subcritical flow
- $Fr = 0.866$, subcritical flow
- $Fr = 1.32$, supercritical flow
- $Fr = 0.500$, subcritical flow

## Explanation

At constant discharge the specific-energy derivative is $\dfrac{dE}{dh} = 1 - Fr^2$ for any cross section. Thus $Fr^2 = 1 - 0.750 = 0.250$ and $Fr = 0.500$. The Froude number is below $1$, so the flow is **subcritical**. The positive derivative also places the point on the rising branch of the curve. The value $0.750$ equates the Froude number with the derivative, and $0.866$ takes its square root. The value $1.32$ results from the sign error $\dfrac{dE}{dh} = Fr^2 - 1$.
