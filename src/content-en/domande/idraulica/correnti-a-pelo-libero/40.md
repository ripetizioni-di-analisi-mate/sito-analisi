A supercritical flow encounters an upward step in the channel bed and remains supercritical downstream. Neglecting head loss through the transition, how does the water depth change across the step?

- It increases
- It decreases
- It stays the same
- It becomes equal to critical depth

## Explanation

Total head is conserved and the bed rises by the step height $\Delta z$, so the downstream specific energy decreases to $E_2 = E_1 - \Delta z$. The derivative of specific energy with respect to depth is $\dfrac{dE}{dh} = 1 - Fr^2$. In supercritical flow $Fr > 1$, so $\dfrac{dE}{dh} < 0$ and the function $E(h)$ decreases over the interval $h < h_c$. A decrease in specific energy therefore causes an **increase in water depth**. The step raises the water surface in supercritical flow and lowers it in subcritical flow, where the derivative is positive.
