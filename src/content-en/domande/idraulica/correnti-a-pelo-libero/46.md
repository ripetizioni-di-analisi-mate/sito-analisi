The cross section of a natural channel was surveyed by measuring water depths at verticals spaced $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ apart from bank to bank, as shown. The successive depths are $0$, $0.80$, $1.40$, $1.00$ and $0\,\mathrm{m}$. Approximating the bed by straight segments between the verticals, what is the flow area?

::schema{nomi=0 acqua="0,1.4 2,0.6 4,0 6,0.4 8,1.4" solidi="-1,1.9 0,1.4 2,0.6 4,0 6,0.4 8,1.4 9,1.9 9,-0.6 -1,-0.6" pelo="0,8,1.4" tratteggi="2,1.4 2,0.6;4,1.4 4,0;6,1.4 6,0.4" quote="0 1.4,2 1.4,2.00 m,-0.5;2 1.4,4 1.4,2.00 m,-0.5;4 1.4,6 1.4,2.00 m,-0.5;6 1.4,8 1.4,2.00 m,-0.5" etichette="2.55,1.05,0.80 m;4.55,0.6,1.40 m;6.55,1.1,1.00 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Natural channel section surveyed at verticals $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ apart, showing measured depths"}

- $11.2\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $6.40\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $5.12\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $12.8\,\mathrm{m^2}$

## Explanation

Approximating the bed profile with straight segments divides the flow area into four trapezoids of width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The two outer trapezoids are triangles. The parallel sides of each trapezoid are adjacent sounding depths, so its area is the width times the mean of the two depths:
$$
A = 2.00\left(\frac{0 + 0.80}{2} + \frac{0.80 + 1.40}{2} + \frac{1.40 + 1.00}{2} + \frac{1.00 + 0}{2}\right) = 2.00 \cdot 3.20 = 6.40\,\mathrm{m^2} .
$$
The value $5.12\,\mathrm{m^2}$ multiplies the free-surface width, $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$, by the arithmetic mean of the five soundings. This gives the bank soundings the same weight as the interior ones. The value $11.2\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is the area of a rectangle as deep as the maximum sounding.
