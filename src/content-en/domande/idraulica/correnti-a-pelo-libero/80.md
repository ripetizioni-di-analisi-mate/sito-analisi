Subcritical flow passes through a gradual expansion in a rectangular channel with a horizontal bed and negligible head loss. How does the water depth change in the wider reach?

- It decreases because the same discharge spreads over a greater width
- It stays the same because specific energy is conserved
- It increases
- It becomes equal to critical depth

## Explanation

The bed is horizontal and losses are negligible, so the specific energy $E$ remains constant. The discharge per unit width $q = \dfrac{Q}{b}$ decreases because $b$ increases. The definition gives $h = E - \dfrac{q^2}{2 g h^2}$. At fixed $E$, decreasing $q$ reduces the kinetic term. On the subcritical branch the water depth therefore **increases**, approaching $E$. For instance, with $E = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$, a change from $q = 2.00$ to $q = 1.50\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ raises the subcritical depth from $1.40$ to $1.45\,\mathrm{m}$.
