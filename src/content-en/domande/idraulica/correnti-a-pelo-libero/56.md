A concrete channel has hydraulic radius $R = 0.600\,\mathrm{m}$ and mean velocity $v = 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The Reynolds number based on the equivalent diameter $4R$ is about $3.6 \cdot 10^{6}$. Why can the Gauckler–Strickler equation be used to find the velocity even though its coefficient $k_s$ is independent of the Reynolds number?

- The flow is laminar, so resistance is proportional to velocity
- The flow is subcritical, so the Froude number is below $1$
- The bed slope is small, so the friction slope is negligible
- The flow is fully rough turbulent, so the Darcy friction factor is independent of the Reynolds number

## Explanation

The Chézy and Gauckler–Strickler equations use empirical coefficients based on the channel boundary. They apply in the **fully rough turbulent regime**, where the Darcy friction factor depends on relative roughness and is independent of Reynolds number. At a Reynolds number of order $10^6$, a concrete channel operates in this regime. Laminar flow occurs below a Reynolds number of about $2000$. The Froude number measures proximity to critical flow and does not characterize flow resistance. In uniform flow the friction slope equals the bed slope.
