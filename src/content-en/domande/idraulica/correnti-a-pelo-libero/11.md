A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries the discharge $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in subcritical flow, with water depth $h_1 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. An upward step is built on the bed. Neglecting head losses, what is the maximum step height that the flow passes without raising the upstream level?

- $0.388\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.849\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.758\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.478\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The upstream velocity is $v_1 = \dfrac{8.00}{4.00 \cdot 1.50} = 1.33\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the upstream specific energy is $E_1 = 1.50 + \dfrac{1.33^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 1.591\,\mathrm{m}$. The discharge per unit width is $q = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, therefore the critical depth and the minimum specific energy are
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{4.00}{9.81}} = 0.742\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
E_{\min} = \frac{3}{2}\,h_c = 1.112\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The total head is conserved and the bed rises by $\Delta z$, so above the step the specific energy is $E_1 - \Delta z$. The discharge passes without raising the upstream level as long as $E_1 - \Delta z \geq E_{\min}$, hence the maximum step is
$$
\Delta z_{\max} = E_1 - E_{\min} = 1.591 - 1.112 = 0.478\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and above that step the flow is critical. Subtracting $E_{\min}$ from the water depth alone, without the velocity head, would give $0.388\,\mathrm{m}$, while subtracting $h_c$ instead of $E_{\min}$ would give $0.849\,\mathrm{m}$.
