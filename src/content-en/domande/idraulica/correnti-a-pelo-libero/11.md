A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a discharge of $Q = 12.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a depth of $h = 0.600\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the alternate depth?

- $0.972\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.72\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{12.0}{4.00 \cdot 0.600} = 5.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \dfrac{25.0}{19.62} = 1.274\,\mathrm{m}$ and the specific energy $E = 1.874\,\mathrm{m}$. With $q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, the equation for depths at this specific energy is
$$
h^3 - 1.874\,h^2 + \frac{q^2}{2g} = h^3 - 1.874\,h^2 + 0.459 = 0 ,
$$
Its positive roots are $0.600\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.72\,\mathrm{m}$. Critical depth is $h_c = 0.972\,\mathrm{m}$, so the alternate depth exceeds $h_c$ and corresponds to subcritical flow, as expected since the given flow is supercritical.
