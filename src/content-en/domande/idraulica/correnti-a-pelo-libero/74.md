A rectangular channel of width $b_1 = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 9.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a depth of $h_1 = 1.40\,\mathrm{m}$. It contracts gradually to width $b_2 = 3.50\,\mathrm{m}$ without a change in bed elevation. Neglecting head loss, what is the depth in the narrower reach?

- $0.627\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.28\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.877\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Upstream $v_1 = \dfrac{9.00}{5.00 \cdot 1.40} = 1.29\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving $E_1 = 1.40 + 0.0843 = 1.484\,\mathrm{m}$ and $Fr_1 = \dfrac{1.29}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.40}} = 0.347$, so the flow is subcritical. With no change in bed elevation or head loss, the specific energy in the contraction remains $1.484\,\mathrm{m}$. The discharge per unit width increases to $q_2 = \dfrac{9.00}{3.50} = 2.57\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. At $q_2$ the minimum specific energy is $\dfrac{3}{2}\,h_{c2} = \dfrac{3}{2} \cdot 0.877 = 1.32\,\mathrm{m}$, below the energy available, so the discharge can pass. The equation
$$
h^3 - 1.484\,h^2 + 0.337 = 0
$$
has two positive roots, $1.28\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.627\,\mathrm{m}$. Here the lossless transition preserves the flow regime. Choose the subcritical root, giving $h_2 = 1.28\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0.877\,\mathrm{m}$ is the critical depth in the contraction.
