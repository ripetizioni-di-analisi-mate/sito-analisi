A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 7.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in uniform flow at a depth of $h_0 = 0.700\,\mathrm{m}$. How is the bed slope classified for this discharge?

- Mild, since $h_0 > h_c = 0.683\,\mathrm{m}$
- Steep, since $h_0 > h_c = 0.683\,\mathrm{m}$
- Mild, since $h_0 < h_c = 0.860\,\mathrm{m}$
- Steep, since $h_0 < h_c = 0.860\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Classify the bed slope by comparing normal depth with critical depth at the specified discharge. The discharge per unit width is $q = \dfrac{Q}{b} = \dfrac{7.50}{3.00} = 2.50\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, so critical depth is
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.25}{9.81}} = 0.860\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Normal depth is below critical depth, so the bed slope exceeds the critical slope and the channel has a **steep slope**. Uniform flow is therefore supercritical. Direct calculation confirms this because $v = 3.57\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $Fr = 1.36$. The value $0.683\,\mathrm{m}$ would result from using $2g$ in place of $g$ under the cube root.
