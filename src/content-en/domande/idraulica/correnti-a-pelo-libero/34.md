A rectangular channel with a horizontal bed narrows into a contraction to pass under a bridge. The discharge is $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, the specific energy of the upstream flow is $E = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$, and head losses are neglected. What is the minimum width of the contraction that lets the discharge through without raising the upstream level?

- $0.793\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.46\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.24\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.75\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The bed is horizontal and losses are neglected, so in the contraction the specific energy remains $E$. As the section narrows, the discharge per unit width increases, and the minimum width corresponds to critical flow, in which $E = E_{\min} = \dfrac{3}{2}\,h_c$; therefore $h_c = \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot 1.80 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. At critical flow the discharge per unit width is
$$
q_c = \sqrt{g\,h_c^3} = \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.20^3} = 4.12\,\mathrm{m^2/s} ,
$$
so the minimum width is
$$
b_{\min} = \frac{Q}{q_c} = \frac{6.00}{4.12} = 1.46\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
With a narrower contraction the flow requires a specific energy greater than $E$, so the upstream level rises. Taking $h_c = E$ would give $0.793\,\mathrm{m}$, taking $h_c = \dfrac{E}{2}$ would give $2.24\,\mathrm{m}$, and writing $q_c = \sqrt{g\,h_c}$ would give $1.75\,\mathrm{m}$.
