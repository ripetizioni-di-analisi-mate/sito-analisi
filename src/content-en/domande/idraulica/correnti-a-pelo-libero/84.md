An earthen channel with coefficient $k_s = 35\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ is lined with concrete, raising the coefficient to $k_s = 80\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. The cross section, bed slope and discharge stay the same. What happens to the critical slope?

- It increases, making a mild slope more likely
- It decreases, so the bed can change classification from mild to steep
- It stays the same because critical depth is independent of roughness
- It decreases, so the bed must become mild

## Explanation

Critical depth depends only on discharge and cross-sectional shape, so the lining leaves it unchanged, along with $A_c$, $R_c$ and $v_c$. The critical slope $i_c = \dfrac{v_c^2}{k_s^2\,R_c^{\frac{4}{3}}}$ is inversely proportional to $k_s^2$ and falls to $\left(\dfrac{35}{80}\right)^2 = 0.191$ times its original value. The unchanged bed slope can then exceed the new critical slope, giving a steep slope and supercritical uniform flow. The claim that critical slope stays constant confuses critical depth with critical slope.
