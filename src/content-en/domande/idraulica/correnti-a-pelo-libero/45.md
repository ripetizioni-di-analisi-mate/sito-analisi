A rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 9.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a depth of $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. If a sluice gate downstream is adjusted, will the effect travel upstream?

- Yes, the disturbance travels upstream with velocity $1.93\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- Yes, the disturbance travels upstream with velocity $4.93\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- No, the flow is supercritical and carries the disturbance downstream
- No, because disturbances in a channel can travel only downstream

## Explanation

The mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{9.00}{5.00 \cdot 1.20} = 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and small disturbances travel at $c = \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.20} = 3.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water, so
$$
Fr = \frac{v}{c} = \frac{1.50}{3.43} = 0.437
$$
and the flow is subcritical. In subcritical flow $c > v$, so the disturbance generated by the gate operation travels upstream at a speed of
$$
c - v = 3.43 - 1.50 = 1.93\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
and the operation affects the upstream reach. The second disturbance travels downstream at $c + v = 4.93\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
