A rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a depth of $h_1 = 1.60\,\mathrm{m}$. Its bed rises by $\Delta z = 0.250\,\mathrm{m}$ at the step shown. Neglecting head loss through the transition, what is the water depth downstream of the step?

::schema{nomi=0 acqua="0,0 4,0 4,0.5 9,0.5 9,3.12 0,3.2" solidi="0,-1 9,-1 9,0.5 4,0.5 4,0 0,0" linee="0,3.2 9,3.12" tratteggi="4,0 9.8,0" quote="2.7 0,2.7 3.2,1.60 m,0;9.6 0,9.6 0.5,Δz = 0.250 m,0.5;6.5 0.5,6.5 3.12,h_2,0" frecce="0.5,1.6,1.5,1.6,v_1" riferimento=0 titolo="Upward bed step of $\Delta z = 0.250\,\mathrm{m}$ in a channel of width $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carrying $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

- $1.31\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.35\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.56\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.458\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Upstream of the step, the mean velocity is $v_1 = 1.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the specific energy is $E_1 = 1.60 + 0.0796 = 1.680\,\mathrm{m}$. Since total head is conserved and the bed rises by $\Delta z$, the downstream specific energy is
$$
E_2 = E_1 - \Delta z = 1.680 - 0.250 = 1.430\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
This exceeds $E_{\min} = \dfrac{3}{2}\,h_c = 1.11\,\mathrm{m}$, so the discharge can pass the step. With $q = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, the positive roots of $h^3 - 1.430\,h^2 + 0.204 = 0$ are $1.31\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.458\,\mathrm{m}$. The upstream flow is subcritical because $h_1 > h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}$. A lossless transition preserves the regime, so choose the root greater than $h_c$, giving $h_2 = 1.31\,\mathrm{m}$.
