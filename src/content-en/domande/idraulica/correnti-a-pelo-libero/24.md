A channel carries uniform flow. Its flow cross section and roughness stay the same while the bed slope increases to four times its original value. How does the discharge change?

- It doubles
- It increases by a factor of $2.52$
- It quadruples
- It increases by a factor of $16$

## Explanation

Uniform-flow discharge is $Q = k_s\,A\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}$. The flow area, hydraulic radius and roughness coefficient stay constant, so discharge varies as $i^{\frac{1}{2}}$. The ratio of the new discharge to the original discharge is
$$
\frac{Q'}{Q} = \sqrt{\frac{4i}{i}} = \sqrt{4} = 2 .
$$
The discharge **doubles**. The slope exponent $\dfrac{1}{2}$ reflects the fact that resistance increases as the square of velocity. Quadrupling the slope therefore doubles the velocity.
