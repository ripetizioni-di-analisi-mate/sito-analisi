Two channels have the same cross section, water depth and bed slope. The first has a smooth concrete lining with $k_s = 85\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. The second is an earthen channel with $k_s = 35\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. Which carries the greater discharge in uniform flow?

- The earthen channel, because a smaller coefficient gives a higher velocity
- The channels carry the same discharge because their cross sections and bed slopes are equal
- The earthen channel, because it has a larger hydraulic radius
- The concrete channel, because discharge is proportional to $k_s$

## Explanation

For uniform flow the Gauckler–Strickler equation gives $Q = k_s\,A\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}$. The two channels have identical cross sections, water depths and bed slopes. Their flow areas and hydraulic radii are therefore equal. Only the coefficient $k_s$ differs, and discharge is proportional to it. The ratio of discharges is
$$
\frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \frac{85}{35} = 2.43 .
$$
The **smooth concrete** channel carries $2.43$ times the discharge of the earthen channel because its smoother wall offers less resistance.
