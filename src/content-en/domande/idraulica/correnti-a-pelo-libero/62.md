Two rectangular channels have the same bed slope and roughness coefficient $k_s$ and carry the same discharge in uniform flow. The first has width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the second $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$. How do their normal depths compare?

- They are equal because the discharge, slope and roughness are the same
- The channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has the greater depth because its wetted perimeter is larger
- The channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has the smaller depth
- The channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has the smaller depth only if the flow is supercritical

## Explanation

At the same water depth, the channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has twice the flow area and a greater hydraulic radius because $R = \dfrac{b\,h}{b + 2h}$ increases with $b$. The Gauckler–Strickler equation therefore gives a greater discharge at this depth. Since discharge increases with depth, a shallower flow in the wider channel suffices to carry the same discharge. For example, $Q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, $i = 0.001$ and $k_s = 60\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ give $h_0 = 1.19\,\mathrm{m}$ in the narrow channel and $h_0 = 0.640\,\mathrm{m}$ in the wide one. This comparison holds for every flow regime because normal depth depends only on the balance of weight and resistance.
