A trapezoidal channel has bottom width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and side slope $m = 1.5$, so each bank extends $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ horizontally for every meter of vertical rise. The water depth is $h = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What is the wetted perimeter?

::schema{nodi="S,-1.5,1;T,3.5,1" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 2,0 3.5,1 -1.5,1" solidi="-4,1.5 -2.25,1.5 0,0 2,0 4.25,1.5 6,1.5 6,-0.5 -4,-0.5" pelo="-1.5,3.5,1,p_a = 0" quote="1.6 0,1.6 1,1.00 m,0;0 -0.5,2 -0.5,2.00 m,0.4;S,T,B,-0.4" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal channel with $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, side slope $m = 1.5$ and $h = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.61\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.6\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The wetted perimeter is the length of the boundary in contact with water. It includes the bed and both sloping banks, and excludes the free surface. Each bank rises by $h$ over a horizontal distance of $m\,h$. Its length is therefore the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $h$ and $m\,h$:
$$
\ell = h\sqrt{1 + m^2} = 1.00 \cdot \sqrt{1 + 2.25} = 1.803\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
This gives
$$
C = b + 2h\sqrt{1 + m^2} = 2.00 + 2 \cdot 1.803 = 5.61\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The result $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ would use the horizontal projection $m\,h$ of each bank, which is shorter than the bank itself.
