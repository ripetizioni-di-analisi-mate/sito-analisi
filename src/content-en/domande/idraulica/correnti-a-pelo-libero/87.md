To measure discharge in a rectangular channel of width $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and water depth $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$, divide its section into three vertical strips. Each outer strip is $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ wide and the center strip is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ wide. A current meter gives mean velocities of $0.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the outer strips and $1.40\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the center. What is the discharge?

- $7.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $12.6\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $9.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The total discharge is the sum of the strip discharges, each of which equals strip area times mean velocity:
$$
Q = 1.50 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 0.60 + 3.00 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 1.40 + 1.50 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 0.60 = 1.35 + 6.30 + 1.35 = 9.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The section area is $6.00 \cdot 1.50 = 9.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is $v = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $7.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ multiplies the total area by an unweighted average of the three velocities. That gives the center strip as much weight as either outer strip although it is twice as wide. The value $12.6\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ applies the maximum velocity over the entire section, and $6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ omits the water depth.
