A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has specific energy $E = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the greatest discharge it can carry at this specific energy?

- $4.75\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $6.18\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $6.72\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $17.5\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

At constant specific energy the discharge is $Q(h) = A(h)\sqrt{2g\,(E - h)}$. In a rectangular section its maximum occurs at $h = \dfrac{2}{3}\,E$, the critical depth for that discharge. Thus
$$
h = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 1.20 = 0.800\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and substitution gives
$$
Q_{\max} = 3.00 \cdot 0.800 \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot (1.20 - 0.800)} = 2.40 \cdot 2.801 = 6.72\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The same result follows from $q = \sqrt{g\,h_c^3} = \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.512} = 2.24\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, giving $Q = 3.00 \cdot 2.24 = 6.72\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
