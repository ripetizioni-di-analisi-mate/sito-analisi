The compound channel shown has a main channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and a floodplain of width $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ whose bed lies $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the main channel bed. All walls are vertical. If the water depth in the main channel is $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the wetted perimeter of the entire section?

::schema{nomi=0 acqua="0,0 4,0 4,1.5 10,1.5 10,2.5 0,2.5" solidi="-1,3 0,3 0,0 4,0 4,1.5 10,1.5 10,3 11,3 11,-1.0 -1,-1.0" pelo="0,10,2.5" quote="0 0,0 2.5,2.50 m,-0.6;0 0,4 0,4.00 m,0.4;4 0,4 1.5,1.50 m,0.5;4 1.5,10 1.5,6.00 m,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Compound cross section with main channel width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and floodplain width $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $13.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $25.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The wetted perimeter is the length of the boundary in contact with water and excludes the free surface. Moving from left to right, include $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ of wetted wall in the main channel, the main channel bed of length $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, the step of height $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ up to the floodplain, the floodplain width $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and its wetted outer wall of height $2.50 - 1.50 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus
$$
C = 2.50 + 4.00 + 1.50 + 6.00 + 1.00 = 15.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $13.5\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the step between the main channel and the floodplain. The value $25.0\,\mathrm{m}$ includes the top width, which is outside the wetted perimeter. The value $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is the wetted perimeter of the main channel alone when treated as rectangular.
