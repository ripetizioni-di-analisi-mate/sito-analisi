At critical flow in a rectangular section, how are the velocity head and water depth related?

- The velocity head equals the water depth
- The velocity head is one third of the water depth
- The velocity head is twice the water depth
- The velocity head is half the water depth

## Explanation

At critical flow $q^2 = g\,h_c^3$, since critical depth in a rectangular channel satisfies $h_c^3 = \dfrac{q^2}{g}$. The velocity head at this section is therefore
$$
\frac{v_c^2}{2g} = \frac{q^2}{2\,g\,h_c^2} = \frac{g\,h_c^3}{2\,g\,h_c^2} = \frac{h_c}{2} ,
$$
or **half the water depth**. This gives the minimum specific energy $E_{\min} = h_c + \dfrac{h_c}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}\,h_c$, which determines whether the specified discharge can pass through a section.
