In a rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, a sluice gate holds back water to a depth of $h_1 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The depth at the vena contracta downstream is $h_2 = 0.450\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Neglecting head loss between the sections, what is the discharge?

- $4.97\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $5.98\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $3.51\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.66\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The two sections have the same bed elevation and negligible losses between them, so they have the same specific energy. Thus $h_1$ and $h_2$ are alternate depths for the same discharge per unit width $q$:
$$
h_1 + \frac{q^2}{2 g h_1^2} = h_2 + \frac{q^2}{2 g h_2^2} .
$$
Solving for $q^2$ gives
$$
q^2 = \frac{2 g\,h_1^2\,h_2^2}{h_1 + h_2} = \frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.00^2 \cdot 0.450^2}{1.45} = 2.74\,\mathrm{m^4/s^2} ,
$$
so $q = 1.66\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ and $Q = b\,q = 4.97\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The value $5.98\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ assigns a velocity of $\sqrt{2 g h_1}$ at the contracted section, omitting the upstream velocity head and the downstream water depth. The value $1.66$ is discharge per unit width.
