Roadside ditches of symmetric triangular cross section are to have the same flow area $A$, bed slope and roughness coefficient $k_s$. Which side slope $m$ gives the greatest discharge in uniform flow?

- $m = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$
- $m = 2$
- $m = \sqrt{3}$
- $m = 1$

## Explanation

At fixed area, slope and $k_s$, the discharge $Q = k_s\,A^{\frac{5}{3}}\,C^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}$ is greatest when the wetted perimeter is smallest. For a triangular section $A = m\,h^2$, so $h = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{m}}$ and
$$
C = 2h\sqrt{1 + m^2} = 2\sqrt{A}\,\sqrt{m + \frac{1}{m}} .
$$
The wetted perimeter is smallest when $f(m) = m + \dfrac{1}{m}$ is a minimum. Its derivative $f'(m) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{m^2}$ vanishes at $m = 1$, is negative for $m < 1$ and positive for $m > 1$. The most efficient side slope is therefore $m = 1$. The sides are inclined at $45^\circ$ and form a right angle at the vertex. The value $m = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ instead corresponds to the $60^\circ$ sides of the most efficient trapezoidal section, which has a nonzero bottom width.
