In a closed circular conduit, the uniform-flow discharge increases with depth up to a certain degree of filling and then decreases. What accounts for this behavior?

- Pressure develops at the top of the section
- Velocity decreases when the free surface rises above half the pipe diameter
- The flow area decreases as the pipe approaches full flow
- As the pipe approaches full flow, the wetted perimeter increases faster than the flow area

## Explanation

Substituting $R = \dfrac{A}{C}$ in the Gauckler–Strickler equation gives the discharge as
$$
Q = k_s\,A^{\frac{5}{3}}\,C^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}} ,
$$
Discharge therefore increases with flow area and decreases with wetted perimeter. Near the crown the pipe wall is almost horizontal and the water-surface width tends to zero. A depth increment $dh$ adds only the small area $B\,dh$ but considerably increases the wetted perimeter. The factor $C^{-\frac{2}{3}}$ falls faster than $A^{\frac{5}{3}}$ rises, so the discharge decreases. Maximum discharge occurs at about $94\%$ of the diameter. Until the pipe fills completely the free surface remains exposed to atmospheric pressure and no positive gauge pressure develops at the crown.
