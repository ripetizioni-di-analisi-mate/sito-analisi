The specific energy available at a channel section is less than the minimum specific energy for the given discharge. What follows?

- The section cannot pass that discharge at the available specific energy
- The discharge passes as supercritical flow
- The discharge passes as subcritical flow
- The discharge passes at critical depth

## Explanation

At constant discharge the function $E(h)$ reaches its minimum at critical depth and tends to $+\infty$ both as $h \to 0^+$ and as $h \to +\infty$. It takes all values above $E_{\min}$ and none below. The equation $E(h) = E$ with $E < E_{\min}$ therefore has no solution. No depth can carry the specified discharge at this specific energy. For instance, if a bed step is too high or a contraction too narrow, the water surface rises upstream of the obstruction until the available specific energy reaches $E_{\min}$.
