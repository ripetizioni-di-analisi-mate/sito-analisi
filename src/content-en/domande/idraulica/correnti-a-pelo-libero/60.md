The stage–discharge relation for a rectangular channel gives the following four pairs: $Q = 1.74\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at $h = 0.600\,\mathrm{m}$, $Q = 2.64\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at $h = 0.800\,\mathrm{m}$, $Q = 3.62\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at $h = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $Q = 4.66\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Which interval contains the normal depth for a discharge of $3000\,\mathrm{L/s}$?

- Between $0.600$ and $0.800\,\mathrm{m}$
- Between $0.800$ and $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- Between $1.00$ and $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- Above $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The discharge is $3000\,\mathrm{L/s} = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The depth–discharge curve is strictly increasing because both flow area and hydraulic radius increase with depth. Each discharge therefore has a unique depth, and a larger discharge corresponds to a larger depth. Since $2.64 < 3.00 < 3.62$, normal depth lies between $0.800$ and $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Trials within this interval give $h_0 = 0.875\,\mathrm{m}$.
