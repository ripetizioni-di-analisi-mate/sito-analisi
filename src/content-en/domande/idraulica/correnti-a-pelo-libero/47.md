A pressure transducer is mounted on the bed of a rectangular channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and mild slope. Above the transducer, the streamlines are straight, the water is $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ deep and the discharge is $7.20\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. What gauge pressure does the transducer measure?

- $13.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $113\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $11.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $9.77\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

In one-dimensional flow with essentially straight, parallel streamlines, the pressure distribution is hydrostatic. Every point across the section therefore has the same piezometric head $z_f + h$ as the free surface, where gauge pressure is zero. The bed elevation is $z_f$, so $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = h$ and
$$
p = \gamma\,h = 9.81 \cdot 1.20 = 11.8\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The mean velocity $v = \dfrac{7.20}{3.00 \cdot 1.20} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$ does not enter this calculation. Velocity head is the separation of the energy grade line from the water surface and does not change the pressure. Adding it would give $13.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and subtracting it would give $9.77\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $113\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is absolute pressure.
