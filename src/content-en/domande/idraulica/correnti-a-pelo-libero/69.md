A flume with a symmetric triangular section carries a discharge that produces critical flow at a water depth of $h_c = 0.400\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the specific energy at that section?

- $0.600\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.800\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.500\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At critical flow $Fr = 1$, so $v^2 = g\,h_m$ and the velocity head is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \dfrac{h_m}{2}$. In a triangular section $A = m\,h^2$ and $B = 2\,m\,h$, so $h_m = \dfrac{A}{B} = \dfrac{h}{2}$ for every side slope and the velocity head is $\dfrac{h_c}{4}$. Consequently,
$$
E = h_c + \frac{h_c}{4} = \frac{5}{4}\,h_c = \frac{5}{4} \cdot 0.400 = 0.500\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0.600\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the relation $E_{\min} = \dfrac{3}{2}\,h_c$, which applies to a rectangular section where $h_m = h$. The value $0.800\,\mathrm{m}$ sets the velocity head equal to the water depth. The value $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$ omits the velocity head.
