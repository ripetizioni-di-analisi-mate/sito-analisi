A lowland stream of width $80\,\mathrm{m}$ carries uniform flow at a depth of $h_0 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Because the stream is wide, take $R = h_0$. The bed slope is $i = 0.0003$ and the roughness coefficient is $k_s = 30\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. What is the Froude number for uniform flow, and how is the bed slope classified?

- $Fr = 0.186$, steep slope
- $Fr = 0.132$, mild slope
- $Fr = 0.186$, mild slope
- $Fr = 5.37$, steep slope

## Explanation

With $R = h_0$ the Gauckler–Strickler equation gives
$$
v = k_s\,h_0^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}} = 30 \cdot 2.00^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.0173 = 0.825\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
giving
$$
Fr = \frac{v}{\sqrt{g\,h_0}} = \frac{0.825}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 2.00}} = 0.186 .
$$
Uniform flow is subcritical, so $h_0 > h_c$. Normal depth above critical depth implies a **mild slope**, with $i < i_c$. Direct calculation confirms that $q = v\,h_0 = 1.65\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ and hence $h_c = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{q^2}{g}} = 0.652\,\mathrm{m} < h_0$. Pairing the same value of $Fr$ with a steep slope is wrong because uniform flow on a steep slope is supercritical. The value $0.132$ uses $\sqrt{2 g h_0}$ in the denominator. The value $5.37$ is the reciprocal of the Froude number.
