To derive the wave speed of a small disturbance in a rectangular channel, observe the wave front from a frame moving with it. In this frame, water approaches at velocity $c$ and depth $h$ and leaves at velocity $c - \delta v$ and depth $h + \delta h$, with $\delta h$ small compared with $h$. Neglecting second-order terms, what relation between $\delta v$ and $\delta h$ follows from continuity?

- $\delta v = \dfrac{c\,\delta h}{h}$
- $\delta v = \dfrac{g\,\delta h}{c}$
- $\delta v = \dfrac{h\,\delta h}{c}$
- $\delta v = \dfrac{c\,h}{\delta h}$

## Explanation

The discharge per unit width is the same on both sides of the front, so
$$
c\,h = (c - \delta v)(h + \delta h) = c\,h + c\,\delta h - h\,\delta v - \delta v\,\delta h .
$$
The product $\delta v\,\delta h$ is second order and can be neglected, giving $c\,\delta h = h\,\delta v$ and $\delta v = \dfrac{c\,\delta h}{h}$. The relation $\delta v = \dfrac{g\,\delta h}{c}$ comes from the momentum equation applied to a control volume enclosing the wave front, with hydrostatic forces at the two sections. Equating the two expressions for $\delta v$ gives $c^2 = g\,h$ and hence wave speed $c = \sqrt{g\,h}$.
