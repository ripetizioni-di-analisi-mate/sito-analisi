The normal depth of a rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is calculated iteratively. The initial estimate sets the hydraulic radius equal to the water depth, as in a very wide channel. How does that estimate compare with the exact normal depth?

- It is smaller because $R < h$, and the actual channel carries less discharge at the same depth
- It is larger because using $R = h$ gives a wetted perimeter larger than its actual value
- It equals the exact depth because the flow area $b\,h$ is calculated exactly
- It is either larger or smaller, depending on whether the flow is subcritical or supercritical

## Explanation

For the actual channel $R = \dfrac{b\,h}{b + 2h}$, which is smaller than $h$ at every depth because the denominator $b + 2h$ exceeds $b$. At any given depth, equation $Q = k_s\,b\,h\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}$ therefore gives a smaller discharge in the actual channel than the calculation with $R = h$. The actual channel needs a greater depth to carry the specified discharge, so the first estimate is low. Successive iterations of
$$
h = \left(\frac{Q}{k_s\,b\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{3}{5}}\left(\frac{b + 2h}{b}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}}
$$
increase the depth because the factor $\left(\dfrac{b + 2h}{b}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}}$ exceeds $1$.
