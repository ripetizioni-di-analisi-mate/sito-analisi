A tank of constant plan area $\Omega = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ drains without inflow through a vertical pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ extending down from its floor. The pipe ends in a contoured nozzle of exit diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $C_v = 0.97$ and $C_c = 1$, as shown. Initially the free surface is $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor. If the pipe stays full and its losses are negligible, how long does the surface take to reach the floor?

- $948\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Let $h$ be the free-surface height above the tank bottom. The nozzle outlet lies $L$ below the bottom, so Bernoulli’s equation from the free surface to the outlet gives the head $h + L$. The discharge is therefore $Q = C_v\,A\sqrt{2g\left(h + L\right)} = k\sqrt{h + L}$ with $k = C_v\,A\sqrt{2g} = 0.97 \cdot 1.96 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 4.43 = 8.44 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}$. The nozzle area is much smaller than the tank’s plan area, so the level falls slowly enough to treat the flow as quasi-steady. Conservation of volume gives $\Omega\,(-dh) = k\sqrt{h + L}\;dt$. Separating variables and integrating from $h_0 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0$ gives
$$
t = \frac{2\,\Omega}{k}\left(\sqrt{h_0 + L} - \sqrt{L}\right) = \frac{2 \cdot 2.00}{8.44 \cdot 10^{-3}}\left(2.000 - 1.732\right)\mathrm{s} = 127\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The vertical tube adds its length to the head at the nozzle throughout drainage. Mounting the nozzle directly at the tank bottom would give $t = \dfrac{2\,\Omega\sqrt{h_0}}{k} = 474\,\mathrm{s}$.
