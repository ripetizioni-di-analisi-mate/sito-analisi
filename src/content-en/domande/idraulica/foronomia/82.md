A broad-crested weir of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $\mu = 0.385$ spans a rectangular channel. The upstream surface is $H = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above the crest. The approach speed is $v_0 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and its velocity head must be included. What is the discharge?

- $2.83\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.38\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $3.30\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.96\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The broad-crested weir equation follows from conservation of specific energy relative to the crest. When the upstream approach velocity is appreciable, replace $H$ with $E = H + \dfrac{v_0^2}{2g} = 0.60 + 0.073 = 0.673\,\mathrm{m}$. This gives
$$
Q = \mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\;E^{\frac{3}{2}} = 0.385 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 4.43 \cdot 0.552\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 2.83\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The critical depth on the crest is $\dfrac{2}{3}\,E = 0.449\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting the upstream velocity head would give $2.38\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, which is $16\%$ lower.
