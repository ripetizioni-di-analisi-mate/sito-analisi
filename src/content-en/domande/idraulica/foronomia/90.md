A contoured nozzle at the end of a pipe discharges a horizontal free jet at speed $v$. Immediately beyond the exit, where are the hydraulic and energy grade lines relative to the jet axis?

- Both pass through the jet axis because gauge pressure is zero
- The hydraulic grade line is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ above the axis and the energy grade line is at reservoir surface elevation
- The energy grade line is on the axis and the hydraulic grade line below it, because speed is greatest
- The hydraulic grade line passes through the jet axis and the energy grade line is $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ above it

## Explanation

The free jet is surrounded by air, so gauge pressure is zero throughout each section. The piezometric head $z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ therefore equals the elevation $z$ of the jet centerline, and the hydraulic grade line lies **on the jet centerline**. The energy grade line also includes velocity head and thus lies $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ above the centerline. Upstream in the pipe, positive gauge pressure places the hydraulic grade line above the pipe axis. The energy grade line drops through the nozzle only by the small head loss $\left(1 - C_v^2\right)H$.
