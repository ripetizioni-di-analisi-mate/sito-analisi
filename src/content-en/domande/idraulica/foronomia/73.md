A detention basin of constant plan area $\Omega = 2000\,\mathrm{m^2}$ drains without inflow over a sharp-crested rectangular weir of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $\mu = 0.42$. How long does the head above the crest take to fall from $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $884\,\mathrm{s}$
- $442\,\mathrm{s}$
- $279\,\mathrm{s}$
- $728\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

With head $h$, the weir discharges $Q = k\,h^{\frac{3}{2}}$, where $k = \mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g} = 3.72\,\mathrm{m^{3/2}/s}$. Conservation of volume gives $\Omega\,(-dh) = k\,h^{\frac{3}{2}}\,dt$. Separating variables and integrating between the two heads gives
$$
t = \frac{\Omega}{k}\int_{h_1}^{h_0} h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,dh = \frac{2\,\Omega}{k}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_1}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{h_0}}\right) .
$$
Substitution gives $t = \dfrac{2 \cdot 2000}{3.72} \cdot \left(2.236 - 1.414\right)\,\mathrm{s} = 884\,\mathrm{s}$. The time dependence differs from that for a tank draining through an orifice because the weir discharge varies as the $\dfrac{3}{2}$ power of the head, whereas orifice discharge varies as its square root. For the same reason, the average discharge is not the arithmetic mean of the initial and final rates. Dividing the drained volume by that mean would give $728\,\mathrm{s}$.
