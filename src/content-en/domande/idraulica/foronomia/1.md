## Explanation

The orifice area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0113\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The reservoir level is constant and the vertical dimension of the opening is small compared with the head, so the small-orifice discharge equation applies, $Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g h}$. With $h = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, the ideal exit speed is $\sqrt{2 g h} = 7.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The discharge is therefore $Q = 0.61 \cdot 0.0113 \cdot 7.00 = 0.0483\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $48.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Omitting the discharge coefficient would give $79.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$, the ideal discharge in the absence of jet contraction and energy loss at the entrance.
