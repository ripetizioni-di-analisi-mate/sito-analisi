A rectangular opening of height $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ is cut in a vertical tank wall. The free surface lies $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ below its top edge. How is this opening classified, and which head describes it?

- A weir opening with head $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ over the crest
- An orifice with head $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ at its centroid
- A weir opening with head $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ over the crest
- A submerged orifice with head equal to the level difference $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

An opening acts as an orifice when its entire perimeter is below the upstream free surface. It acts as a weir when the surface intersects it. Here the free surface is $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ below the top edge, so it intersects the opening and the water flows out with a free surface exposed to air. The opening therefore acts as a **weir**. The lower edge is the crest, and the head over it is the height of the free surface above that edge, $0.40 - 0.10 = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. The opening height $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ is a geometric dimension and does not give the water level.
