A vertical-sided navigation lock of plan dimensions $12.0 \times 80.0\,\mathrm{m}$ fills from a constant-level upstream reservoir through openings submerged on both sides, with total area $2.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.60$. How fast is the surface in the lock rising when it is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the reservoir surface?

- $1.85\,\mathrm{cm/s}$
- $0.783\,\mathrm{cm/s}$
- $0.554\,\mathrm{cm/s}$
- $1.11\,\mathrm{cm/s}$

## Explanation

The orifices are submerged on both sides, so the instantaneous inflow is $Q = \mu A\sqrt{2g\,\Delta} = 0.60 \cdot 2.00 \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 4.00} = 10.6\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. During the interval $dt$, the incoming volume $Q\,dt$ raises the lock-chamber surface by $dz$. Thus $\Omega\,dz = Q\,dt$ with $\Omega = 12.0 \cdot 80.0 = 960\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The instantaneous rate of rise is $\dfrac{dz}{dt} = \dfrac{Q}{\Omega} = \dfrac{10.6}{960} = 0.0111\,\mathrm{m/s}$, or $1.11\,\mathrm{cm/s}$, approximately $0.66\,\mathrm{m}$ per minute. The reservoir level is constant, so the head difference decreases at the same rate and the rise slows as the two surfaces approach each other. Omitting the discharge coefficient would give $1.85\,\mathrm{cm/s}$.
