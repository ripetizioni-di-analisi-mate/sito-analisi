A sharp-crested Thomson V-notch weir with included angle $\theta = 90^\circ$ and discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.60$ is installed in a measuring tank. The vertex of the notch is $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ above the tank bottom, and the staff gauge, with its zero on the bottom, reads an upstream level of $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the discharge?

- $69.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $127\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $25.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $251\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The head on a V-notch weir is measured from the vertex, so it is $h = 0.50 - 0.30 = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. With $\theta = 90^\circ$ we have $\tan\dfrac{\theta}{2} = 1$, therefore Thomson's formula gives
$$
Q = \frac{8}{15}\,\mu\,\sqrt{2g}\;h^{\frac{5}{2}} = 1.417 \cdot 0.20^{\frac{5}{2}} = 1.417 \cdot 0.0179 = 0.0254\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
that is, $25.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Taking the gauge reading, $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, as the head would give $251\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and taking the height of the vertex above the bottom would give $69.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$. With the exponent $\dfrac{3}{2}$ of the rectangular weir in place of $\dfrac{5}{2}$ one would obtain $127\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
