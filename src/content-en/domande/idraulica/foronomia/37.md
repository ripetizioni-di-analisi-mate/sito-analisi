A sharp-crested Thomson V-notch weir has included angle $\theta = 90^\circ$ and discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.60$. The head above the vertex is $h = 0.18\,\mathrm{m}$. What discharge does it measure?

- $36.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $45.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $19.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $108\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

For a V-notch weir, $Q = \dfrac{8}{15}\,\mu\,\tan\dfrac{\theta}{2}\,\sqrt{2g}\;h^{\frac{5}{2}}$. With $\theta = 90^\circ$, this gives $\tan\dfrac{\theta}{2} = 1$. The numerical coefficient is $\dfrac{8}{15} \cdot 0.60 \cdot \sqrt{2g} = 1.417$, so $Q = 1.417\,h^{\frac{5}{2}}$ when $Q$ is expressed in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and $h$ in meters. Since $0.18^{\frac{5}{2}} = 0.01375$, the discharge is $Q = 1.417 \cdot 0.01375 = 0.0195\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $19.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The head exponent is $\dfrac{5}{2}$ because the notch width increases with elevation. Using the exponent $\dfrac{3}{2}$ would give $108\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
