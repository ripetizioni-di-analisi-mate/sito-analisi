A small orifice in a thin wall discharges a free jet into air. In the discharge calculation gauge pressure is taken as zero everywhere across the vena contracta. Which property of that section justifies this?

- Speed is greatest there, so Bernoulli’s equation gives minimum pressure
- The pathlines are straight and parallel, and the section perimeter is exposed to the atmosphere
- The wall is thin, so the liquid touches the opening only at its inner edge
- Head loss between the free surface and the vena contracta is zero

## Explanation

At the vena contracta the jet has finished contracting. The streamlines are locally straight and parallel, so the pressure distribution across the section is hydrostatic. The jet is surrounded by air, making the gauge pressure zero along its boundary. Because the orifice is small, the hydrostatic variation across it is negligible and the gauge pressure is effectively zero throughout the section. Applying Bernoulli’s equation between the reservoir free surface and the vena contracta therefore gives $v_c = C_v\sqrt{2 g h}$.
