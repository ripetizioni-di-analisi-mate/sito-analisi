A constant-head tank has a circular orifice of diameter $D = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$ in a thin vertical wall. The head at the orifice center is $h = 2.20\,\mathrm{m}$. A test gives discharge $Q = 4.90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and vena contracta diameter $D_c = 31.5\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the discharge coefficient?

- $0.593$
- $0.620$
- $0.957$
- $0.839$

## Explanation

The discharge coefficient is actual discharge divided by the product of opening area and ideal velocity $\sqrt{2 g h}$, so $\mu = \dfrac{Q}{A\,\sqrt{2 g h}}$. The data give $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 1.257 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\sqrt{2 g h} = 6.57\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence $A\,\sqrt{2 g h} = 8.26\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $\mu = \dfrac{4.90}{8.26} = 0.593$. The contracted jet diameter gives the contraction coefficient $C_c = \left(\dfrac{31.5}{40}\right)^2 = 0.620$. Since $\mu = C_c\,C_v$, it follows that $C_v = 0.957$.
