What head drives flow through the orifice?

## Explanation

Bubbles of atmospheric air enter at the lower end of the tube, so the liquid there is at zero gauge pressure. The liquid surrounding the orifice is nearly at rest. The hydrostatic relation from the tube end to the orifice centroid gives $p = \gamma \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, so the head at the orifice is $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. The air above the free surface is at subatmospheric gauge pressure $-\gamma \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$. As the surface falls, incoming air keeps the gauge pressure at the tube end at zero. The head and discharge thus remain constant until the surface reaches the tube end. This is the principle of the **Mariotte bottle**.
