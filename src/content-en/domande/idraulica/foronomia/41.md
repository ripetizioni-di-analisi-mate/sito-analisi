Discharge $Q = 85\,\mathrm{L/s}$ can be measured with a rectangular sharp-crested weir with $b = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ at head $h = 0.148\,\mathrm{m}$, or with a Thomson V-notch weir with $\theta = 90^\circ$ at head $h = 0.324\,\mathrm{m}$. With a head reading error $1\,\mathrm{mm}$, which gives the smaller relative discharge error?

- The rectangular weir because its discharge equation has a smaller head exponent
- The rectangular weir because it has a lower discharge coefficient
- The V-notch weir because it operates at a greater head
- Both give the same relative error because they measure the same discharge

## Explanation

For both weirs, $\dfrac{dQ}{Q} = n\,\dfrac{dh}{h}$, with $n = \dfrac{3}{2}$ for the rectangular weir and $n = \dfrac{5}{2}$ for the V-notch weir. The rectangular weir gives $\dfrac{3}{2} \cdot \dfrac{0.001}{0.148} = 1.0\%$ and the V-notch weir gives $\dfrac{5}{2} \cdot \dfrac{0.001}{0.324} = 0.77\%$. At the same low discharge, the V-notch weir produces a greater head. This offsets its higher exponent, so **the V-notch weir is preferred for small discharges**.
