A constant-level tank supplies a second tank through an orifice of area $A_1 = 0.0200\,\mathrm{m^2}$, submerged on both sides of the partition. The second tank drains to the atmosphere through a thin-wall orifice of area $A_2 = 0.0150\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The orifices have equal center elevations and discharge coefficients. The first surface is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above their centers. At steady state, how high above them is the second surface?

- $1.71\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.08\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.92\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $h$ be the required water depth. The first orifice is submerged on both sides and operates under the free-surface elevation difference $3.00\,\mathrm{m} - h$. The second discharges to the atmosphere under head $h$. At steady state the water level in the second tank is constant, so inflow equals outflow,
$$
\mu\,A_1\sqrt{2g\left(3.00 - h\right)} = \mu\,A_2\sqrt{2g\,h} .
$$
Canceling $\mu\sqrt{2g}$ and squaring gives $A_1^2\left(3.00 - h\right) = A_2^2\,h$, hence
$$
h = \frac{3.00\,A_1^2}{A_1^2 + A_2^2} = \frac{3.00 \cdot 4.00}{4.00 + 2.25}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.92\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
where the squared areas are expressed in $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^4}$. Since the inlet orifice is larger, the free surface settles closer to the level in the first tank than to the orifice centroids. Using the areas without squaring them would give $1.71\,\mathrm{m}$.
