A rectangular thin-wall orifice has height $a = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and head $h_G = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ at its centroid. How does the small-orifice approximation compare with the exact discharge?

- A discharge about $0.26\%$ greater
- A discharge about $0.26\%$ lower
- A discharge about $2.6\%$ higher
- The same discharge because the head is measured at the centroid

## Explanation

Let $\varepsilon = \dfrac{a}{2\,h_G}$. Neglecting higher-order terms, the ratio of the exact discharge to the small-orifice estimate is $1 - \dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{24}$. For $a = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_G = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ we obtain $\varepsilon = 0.25$, so the discrepancy is $\dfrac{0.0625}{24} = 0.0026$. The small-orifice formula thus overestimates the discharge by approximately $0.26\%$. As long as the orifice height remains below the head at its centroid, the error is on the order of one percent.
