A constant-level tank discharges downward through a circular thin-wall orifice of diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ in its horizontal floor with the free surface $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the opening. Take $C_c = 0.62$ and $C_v = 0.98$. Neglecting air resistance, what is the jet diameter $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the vena contracta?

- $30.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $34.5\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $43.8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $52.9\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

At the vena contracta, $d_c = D\sqrt{C_c} = 39.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $v_c = C_v\sqrt{2gh} = 0.98 \cdot 5.42 = 5.32\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Gauge pressure in the jet is zero, so Bernoulli’s equation between the vena contracta and the section $z = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below it gives $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \dfrac{v_c^2}{2g} + z$, or $v = \sqrt{28.3 + 19.6} = 6.92\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Continuity requires the jet area to vary inversely with speed, so the diameter is
$$
d = d_c\sqrt{\frac{v_c}{v}} = 39.4 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{5.32}{6.92}}\,\mathrm{mm} = 34.5\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
Taking the diameter itself to be inversely proportional to speed and omitting the square root would give $30.2\,\mathrm{mm}$.
