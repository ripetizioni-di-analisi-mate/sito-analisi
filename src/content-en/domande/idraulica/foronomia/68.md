A vertical-sided prismatic barge floats in still water with empty draft $T_0 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Water enters through a circular bottom hole of diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\mu = 0.61$. At a certain instant the water inside is $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ deep and the draft is $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. What is the inflow at that instant?

- $4.74\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $3.75\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $2.90\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $5.56\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Water covers the hole on both sides, so it acts as a submerged orifice under the difference $\Delta h = 0.80 - 0.30 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ between the outside and inside water levels. We obtain $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 1.96 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\sqrt{2g\,\Delta h} = 3.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving $Q = 0.61 \cdot 1.96 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 3.13 = 3.75\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Because the barge sides are vertical, buoyancy equilibrium requires the increase in draft to equal the depth of water admitted. The level difference therefore remains equal to the empty draft $T_0$, and the inflow remains constant while the barge floats. Using the draft $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ as the head for discharge into air would give $4.74\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
