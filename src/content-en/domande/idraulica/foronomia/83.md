A broad-crested weir spans the full width of a rectangular channel, and the downstream level is below the crest. What flow regimes occur upstream, at the downstream end of the crest, and immediately downstream?

- Supercritical upstream, critical on the crest, subcritical downstream
- Subcritical everywhere because the weir slows the flow
- Critical upstream, subcritical on the crest, supercritical downstream
- Subcritical upstream, critical at the downstream end of the crest, supercritical downstream

## Explanation

Upstream, the weir raises the water level and the velocity is low. Specific energy is nearly all depth, so $Fr < 1$ and the flow is subcritical. Over the crest specific energy is conserved and the depth decreases. At the downstream edge the flow reaches critical depth, where specific energy is a minimum for the given discharge and $Fr = 1$. Beyond the crest the water drops toward the downstream bed, gains speed, and becomes supercritical. The crest thus forms a **control section** that relates the discharge to the upstream water level alone.
