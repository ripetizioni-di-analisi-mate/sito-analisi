A long pipe supplied by a constant-level reservoir ends in a contoured nozzle that discharges to the atmosphere. If only the nozzle outlet diameter decreases, what happens to discharge and jet speed?

- Discharge stays the same and jet speed increases
- Discharge increases and jet speed decreases
- Both discharge and jet speed decrease
- Discharge decreases and jet speed increases

## Explanation

Let $A$ be the nozzle exit area, $D$, $L$, and $A_t$ the pipe diameter, length, and cross-sectional area, and $H_0$ the elevation difference between the reservoir free surface and the nozzle axis. The two unknowns $H$ and $Q$ satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
H = H_0 - \lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D}\,\dfrac{1}{2g}\left(\dfrac{Q}{A_t}\right)^2 \\[8pt]
Q = C_v\,A\,\sqrt{2 g H}
\end{cases}
$$
Substituting the first equation into the second and solving for discharge gives
$$
Q = \frac{C_v\,A\,\sqrt{2 g H_0}}{\sqrt{1 + \lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D}\left(\dfrac{C_v\,A}{A_t}\right)^2}} ,
$$
so reducing the exit area reduces the discharge. The shaped nozzle suppresses contraction, giving a jet velocity $v = \dfrac{Q}{A}$ and hence
$$
v = \frac{C_v\,\sqrt{2 g H_0}}{\sqrt{1 + \lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D}\left(\dfrac{C_v\,A}{A_t}\right)^2}} ,
$$
so the jet speed increases as the exit area decreases. The lower discharge reduces head loss in the pipe. The head $H$ then approaches the available elevation head $H_0$ and the jet speed approaches $C_v\sqrt{2 g H_0}$.
