In a rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, a vertical sluice gate is raised $a = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bed. The upstream depth is $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and downstream discharge is free, as shown. Taking gate discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.58$, find the discharge.

::schema{solidi="0,-0.35 6,-0.35 6,0 0,0;2.9,0.45 3.1,0.45 3.1,3.2 2.9,3.2" acqua="0,0 2.9,0 2.9,2.6 0,2.6;2.9,0 3.1,0.45 3.5,0.3 6,0.275 6,0 3.1,0" pelo="0,2.9,2.6,;4,5.6,0.275," tratteggi="3.5,0 3.5,0.8" frecce="3.9,0.14,4.7,0.14,v_c" quote="0 0,0 2.6,2.60 m,-0.5;3 0,3 0.45,0.45 m,-0.45" titolo="Flat gate raised by $a = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ in a channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, with upstream water depth $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $5.09\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $5.59\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $3.95\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $9.64\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

Raising the gate by $a$ creates an opening of area $A = b\,a = 1.35\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The downstream level is below the lip, giving free discharge $Q = \mu\,b\,a\,\sqrt{2 g h}$. With $h = 2.60\,\mathrm{m}$, this gives $\sqrt{2 g h} = 7.14\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and then $Q = 0.58 \cdot 1.35 \cdot 7.14 = 5.59\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The head under the square root is the upstream depth measured from the bed. The empirical coefficient $\mu$ accounts for the finite depth of the contracted jet. Using the level difference $h - a$ instead would give $5.09\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
