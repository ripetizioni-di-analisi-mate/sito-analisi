A constant-level distribution tank receives discharge $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and discharges to the atmosphere through circular thin-wall orifices of diameters $D_1 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$. Their centers are at the same elevation and both have discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.61$. What discharge leaves through the orifice of diameter $150\,\mathrm{mm}$?

- $30.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $34.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $25.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $41.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The centroids of the two orifices are at the same elevation below the same free surface. They therefore experience the same head $h$, and each discharges $\mu A_i\sqrt{2gh}$. Since $\mu\sqrt{2gh}$ is common to both, the flow divides in proportion to the areas, or equivalently to the squares of the diameters,
$$
Q_2 = Q\,\frac{D_2^2}{D_1^2 + D_2^2} = 50 \cdot \frac{0.0225}{0.0100 + 0.0225}\,\mathrm{L/s} = 34.6\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The water level in the small tank settles at the head $h = 0.526\,\mathrm{m}$, for which $\mu\left(A_1 + A_2\right)\sqrt{2gh} = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Dividing the flow in proportion to the diameters would give $30.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
