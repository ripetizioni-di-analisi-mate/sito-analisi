A large rectangular orifice in a vertical wall of a constant-level reservoir extends from depth $h_1$ to depth $h_2$ below the free surface. How does the theoretical exit speed vary over its height?

- It increases in proportion to depth
- It increases in proportion to the square root of depth
- It is uniform and equals the speed at the orifice centroid
- It decreases with depth because pressure increases

## Explanation

A streamline issuing at depth $y$ starts at the free surface with total head $y$ relative to the outlet elevation and emerges at zero gauge pressure. Bernoulli’s equation gives the theoretical speed $\sqrt{2gy}$, which varies as the **square root of depth**. The speed increases from $\sqrt{2gh_1}$ at the top edge to $\sqrt{2gh_2}$ at the bottom edge. The discharge through a tall opening is therefore the sum of the contributions $\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2gy}\;dy$ from horizontal strips. Integrating $\sqrt{y}$ gives the powers $h^{\frac{3}{2}}$.
