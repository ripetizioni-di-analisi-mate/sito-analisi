Two constant-level tanks, one containing water and the other oil of density $850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, discharge through identical thin-wall orifices to the atmosphere. Both have the same head in meters at the orifice center and the same discharge coefficient. Which statement about their volumetric flow rates is true?

- They are equal because the theoretical speed $\sqrt{2gh}$ is independent of density
- The oil discharge is smaller because oil has a lower specific weight
- The oil discharge is larger because a lighter liquid accelerates more
- The oil discharge is smaller because pressure at the orifice center is lower

## Explanation

The pressure at the orifice centroid is $\rho g h$, which is lower for oil. The kinetic energy per unit volume $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$ is also proportional to density. Density cancels in Bernoulli’s equation between the free surface and the vena contracta, so $v_c = C_v\sqrt{2gh}$ applies to both liquids. With the same orifice and coefficient, $Q = \mu A\sqrt{2gh}$ holds in both cases. The volumetric flow rates are therefore **equal**. The oil mass flow rate is lower, in the ratio $\dfrac{850}{1000}$.
