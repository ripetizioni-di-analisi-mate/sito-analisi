A thin vertical wall of a constant-level tank has two small orifices whose centers are $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, with identical velocity coefficient $C_v = 0.97$. Both jets leave horizontally and fall freely. Neglecting air resistance, at what horizontal distance from the wall do they intersect?

- $0.970\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.43\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.94\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.91\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

A jet issuing under head $h$ has speed $v_c = C_v\sqrt{2gh}$ and moves under gravity alone. At a horizontal distance $x$ from the wall it has fallen by $y = \dfrac{g\,x^2}{2\,v_c^2} = \dfrac{x^2}{4\,C_v^2\,h}$. The lower opening is $h_2 - h_1 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the upper one, so at the intersection the upper jet has fallen $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ farther than the lower jet. Thus
$$
\frac{x^2}{4\,C_v^2}\left(\frac{1}{h_1} - \frac{1}{h_2}\right) = h_2 - h_1 .
$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{h_1} - \dfrac{1}{h_2} = \dfrac{h_2 - h_1}{h_1\,h_2}$, this gives $x^2 = 4\,C_v^2\,h_1\,h_2$, or $x = 2\,C_v\sqrt{h_1\,h_2} = 2 \cdot 0.97 \cdot \sqrt{0.50 \cdot 2.00} = 1.94\,\mathrm{m}$. The upper jet has fallen $h_2 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at that point, placing the intersection $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. Omitting the factor $2$ would give $0.970\,\mathrm{m}$.
