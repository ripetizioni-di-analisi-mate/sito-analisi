A triangular opening in a thin vertical wall of a constant-level tank has its apex at the free surface and its horizontal base of width $B = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ at depth $H = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Taking discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.61$ for each horizontal strip, what is the discharge?

- $1.59\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.72\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.11\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.38\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The opening widens linearly from vertex to base. At depth $y$ its width is $B\,\dfrac{y}{H}$, and the strip between $y$ and $y + dy$ carries discharge $\mu\,B\,\dfrac{y}{H}\sqrt{2gy}\;dy$. Integrating between $0$ and $H$ gives
$$
Q = \frac{\mu\,B\,\sqrt{2g}}{H}\int_0^H y^{\frac{3}{2}}\,dy = \frac{2}{5}\,\mu\,B\,\sqrt{2g}\;H^{\frac{3}{2}} = 0.4 \cdot 0.61 \cdot 1.20 \cdot 4.43 \cdot 1.84\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 2.38\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The centroid of the triangle lies $\dfrac{2}{3}\,H = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the vertex. The small-orifice formula evaluated at this head gives $2.43\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, which is $2\%$ higher. Thomson’s formula with $\tan\dfrac{\theta}{2} = \dfrac{B}{2H}$ applies to a V-notch with its vertex at the bottom and would give $1.59\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
