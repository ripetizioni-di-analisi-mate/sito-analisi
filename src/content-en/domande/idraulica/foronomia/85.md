A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries discharge $Q = 4.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at depth $h_0 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. A full-width sill with a level crest is built on the bed. Neglecting losses, what is the minimum sill height for flow on the crest to become critical?

- $0.939\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.633\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.749\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The upstream velocity is $v_0 = \dfrac{4.50}{3.00 \cdot 1.50} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the specific energy is $E_0 = 1.50 + \dfrac{1.00^2}{2g} = 1.551\,\mathrm{m}$ and the flow is subcritical with $Fr_0 = 0.26$. The critical depth is $h_c = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{Q^2}{g\,b^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{20.25}{9.81 \cdot 9.00}} = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}$, at which the specific energy reaches its minimum $E_{\min} = \dfrac{3}{2}\,h_c = 0.918\,\mathrm{m}$. With negligible losses, the specific energy relative to a crest of height $\Delta z$ is $E_0 - \Delta z$. Flow becomes critical when this energy decreases to its minimum, giving $\Delta z = E_0 - \dfrac{3}{2}\,h_c = 1.551 - 0.918 = 0.633\,\mathrm{m}$. Using $h_c$ instead of $\dfrac{3}{2}\,h_c$ for the minimum energy would give $0.939\,\mathrm{m}$.
