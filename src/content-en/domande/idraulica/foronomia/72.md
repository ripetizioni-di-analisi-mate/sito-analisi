For a tank of constant plan area draining through an orifice, the quasi-steady draining-time formula is $t = \dfrac{2\,\Omega\sqrt{h_0}}{\mu A\sqrt{2g}}$. Each tank below has $\mu = 0.60$. For which tank is the estimate *unreliable*?

- A tank of plan area $20\,\mathrm{m^2}$ with a bottom orifice of diameter $100\,\mathrm{mm}$
- A tank of plan area $1.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ with a bottom orifice of area $0.40\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- A cylindrical tank of diameter $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with a side orifice of diameter $80\,\mathrm{mm}$
- A tank of plan area $50\,\mathrm{m^2}$ with a bottom orifice of area $0.05\,\mathrm{m^2}$

## Explanation

The formula treats the flow at each instant as steady under the instantaneous head, as though the free surface were stationary. This is a good approximation when the orifice area is much smaller than the tank’s plan area, since the free-surface speed $\dfrac{\mu A}{\Omega}\sqrt{2gh}$ is then negligible compared with the orifice velocity. For the tank of area $1.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and orifice of area $0.40\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the ratio is $\dfrac{\mu A}{\Omega} = 0.24$, so the surface falls at about one-quarter of the orifice velocity. Its velocity head cannot be neglected. In the other three cases the ratio $\dfrac{A}{\Omega}$ is at most $10^{-3}$, and the assumption holds.
