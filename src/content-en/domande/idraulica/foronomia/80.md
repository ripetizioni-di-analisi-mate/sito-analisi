For a Thomson weir with $\theta = 90^\circ$ and $\mu = 0.60$, the relation is $Q = 1.42\,h^{\frac{5}{2}}$ when $Q$ is in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and $h$ in meters. What coefficient $k$ gives the equivalent formula $Q = k\,h^{\frac{5}{2}}$ with $Q$ in liters per second and $h$ in centimeters?

- $14.2$
- $0.0142$
- $1.42 \cdot 10^{-5}$
- $1.42$

## Explanation

Let $Q'$ denote the numerical discharge in liters per second and $h'$ the head in centimeters. The conversions are $Q = \dfrac{Q'}{1000}$ and $h = \dfrac{h'}{100}$. Substituting into the equation gives $\dfrac{Q'}{1000} = 1.42\,\dfrac{h'^{\frac{5}{2}}}{100^{\frac{5}{2}}}$. Since $100^{\frac{5}{2}} = 10^5$, it follows that $Q' = 1.42 \cdot \dfrac{1000}{10^5}\,h'^{\frac{5}{2}} = 0.0142\,h'^{\frac{5}{2}}$. Checking with $h' = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ gives $0.0142 \cdot 30^{\frac{5}{2}} = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$, the same as $1.42 \cdot 0.30^{\frac{5}{2}} = 0.070\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Dividing by $100$ without raising it to the power $\dfrac{5}{2}$ would give $14.2$.
