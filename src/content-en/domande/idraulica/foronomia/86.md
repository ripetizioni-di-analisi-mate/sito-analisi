A fountain projects water vertically upward through a contoured nozzle with $C_v = 0.95$ and $C_c = 1$. Total head just upstream is $H = 12.0\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the exit. Neglecting air resistance, how high above the nozzle does the jet rise?

- $12.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $21.7\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The jet leaves the nozzle at speed $v = C_v\sqrt{2gH}$ with velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = C_v^2\,H$. It moves under gravity alone at zero gauge pressure. Total head along the jet remains constant, so velocity head converts to elevation head until the speed reaches zero. The jet therefore rises to $C_v^2\,H = 0.9025 \cdot 12.0 = 10.8\,\mathrm{m}$. The difference $\left(1 - C_v^2\right)H = 1.17\,\mathrm{m}$ is the head loss in the nozzle. Dividing by $C_v^2$ instead of multiplying would give $13.3\,\mathrm{m}$, exceeding the available head.
