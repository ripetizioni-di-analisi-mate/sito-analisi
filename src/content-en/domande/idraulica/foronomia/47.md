A laboratory tests a circular sharp-edged thin-wall orifice with a head of several orifice diameters. Which reported set of contraction, velocity, and discharge coefficients is physically possible?

- $C_c = 0.62$, $C_v = 0.97$, $\mu = 0.64$
- $C_c = 0.97$, $C_v = 0.62$, $\mu = 0.60$
- $C_c = 0.62$, $C_v = 1.03$, $\mu = 0.64$
- $C_c = 0.62$, $C_v = 0.97$, $\mu = 0.60$

## Explanation

The discharge coefficient is the product $\mu = C_c\,C_v$ of the contraction and velocity coefficients. With $C_c = 0.62$ and $C_v = 0.97$, the product is $\mu = 0.601$, so the combination with $C_v = 0.97$ and $\mu = 0.64$ is inconsistent. The head loss between the free surface and the vena contracta is $\Delta H = \left(1 - C_v^2\right)h$ and cannot be negative. Thus $C_v \leq 1$, ruling out the combination with $C_v = 1.03$. The values $C_c = 0.97$ and $C_v = 0.62$ satisfy the product relation but imply almost no contraction and a loss of $62\%$ of the available head. Experiments with sharp-edged orifices show appreciable contraction, with $C_c$ between $0.61$ and $0.64$, and a small head loss, with $C_v$ between $0.96$ and $0.99$. The consistent values are therefore $C_c = 0.62$, $C_v = 0.97$, and $\mu = 0.60$.
