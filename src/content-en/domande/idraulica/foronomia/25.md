An opening of width $b = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ in a vertical tank wall extends from the free surface to depth $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$. The discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.60$. What is the discharge?

- $1.60\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.27\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.51\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.52\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The top edge of the opening lies at the free surface, so set $h_1 = 0$ and $h_2 = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ in the large-orifice formula. Since $h_2^{\frac{3}{2}} = 0.854$, the result is $Q = \dfrac{2}{3}\,\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\;h_2^{\frac{3}{2}} = \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot 0.60 \cdot 1.00 \cdot 4.43 \cdot 0.854 = 1.51\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The height of the opening is twice the head at its centroid, so the small-orifice formula would give $1.60\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, an error of $6\%$. The result has the same form as the equation for a sharp-crested rectangular weir, where the head is measured upward from the crest.
