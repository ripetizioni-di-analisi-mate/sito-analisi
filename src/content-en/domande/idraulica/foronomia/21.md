A jet issues into the atmosphere from an orifice in a thin vertical wall. A point on its axis lies $x = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ horizontally and $y = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the center of the vena contracta. What is the speed at the vena contracta?

- $3.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.64\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.825\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Beyond the vena contracta, gravity is the only force on the free jet. Its particles follow parabolic paths with initial horizontal speed $v_c$. Eliminating time between $x = v_c\,t$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}g\,t^2$ gives $v_c = x\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2 y}}$. Substitution yields $v_c = 1.80 \cdot \sqrt{\dfrac{9.81}{1.00}} = 1.80 \cdot 3.13 = 5.64\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $3.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the vertical velocity component at the point in question, namely $\sqrt{2 g y}$.
