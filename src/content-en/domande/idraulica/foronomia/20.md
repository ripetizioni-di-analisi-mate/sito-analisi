A constant-level tank must discharge $Q = 30\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to the atmosphere through a circular thin-wall orifice of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.60$. What head is required at the orifice center?

- $0.324\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.744\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.07\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Rearranging $Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g h}$ gives the theoretical speed $\sqrt{2 g h} = \dfrac{Q}{\mu\,A}$. With $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, this gives $\sqrt{2 g h} = \dfrac{0.030}{0.60 \cdot 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 6.37\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Squaring and dividing by $2g$ yields $h = \dfrac{6.37^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 2.07\,\mathrm{m}$. Omitting the discharge coefficient would give $0.744\,\mathrm{m}$, a head too low to deliver the required discharge.
