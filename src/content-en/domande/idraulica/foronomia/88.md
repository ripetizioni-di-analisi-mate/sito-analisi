A constant-level reservoir supplies a long pipe ending in a contoured nozzle. Its free surface is $H_0 = 60.0\,\mathrm{m}$ above the nozzle axis. Pipe head loss is $k\,Q^2$ with constant $k$, and the nozzle velocity coefficient is independent of outlet diameter. If the outlet diameter is chosen for maximum jet power, what is the total head immediately upstream of the nozzle?

- $30.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $40.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $60.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The head immediately upstream of the nozzle equals the available elevation head minus pipe friction losses, $H = H_0 - k\,Q^2$. The jet power is $P = \gamma\,Q\,C_v^2\,H = \gamma\,C_v^2\left(H_0\,Q - k\,Q^3\right)$. The outlet diameter determines the discharge, so power is maximized when its derivative with respect to $Q$ is zero, giving $H_0 - 3\,k\,Q^2 = 0$. At that operating point the pipe head loss is $k\,Q^2 = \dfrac{H_0}{3} = 20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and the head upstream of the nozzle is $H = \dfrac{2}{3}\,H_0 = 40.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The full head $60.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is available only at zero discharge, when the jet delivers no power.
