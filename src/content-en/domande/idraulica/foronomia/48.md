A constant-level tank discharges to the atmosphere through a small orifice in a thin wall, with head $h$ at its center. What is the mean gauge pressure across the opening in the plane of the wall?

- Positive and less than $\gamma h$
- Zero, as at the vena contracta
- Negative, because the speed in the opening exceeds $\sqrt{2gh}$
- Equal to $\gamma h$ because the liquid has not yet left the reservoir

## Explanation

The discharge through the orifice plane equals that at the vena contracta. The mean speed in the orifice plane is therefore $v = \dfrac{Q}{A} = C_c\,v_c$, less than $v_c$. Applying Bernoulli’s equation between the reservoir free surface and the orifice plane, where losses are still negligible, gives $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} \approx h - \dfrac{v^2}{2g}$. Since $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} < \dfrac{v_c^2}{2g} \leq h$, the gauge pressure is **positive**. Because the liquid is moving, $v > 0$ and the pressure is below $\gamma h$. The streamlines are still curved at the orifice plane. The gauge pressure falls to zero farther downstream at the vena contracta.
