A constant-level tank discharges to the atmosphere through a square orifice of side $\ell = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ in a thin vertical wall. Its center is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface and the discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.61$. What is the discharge?

- $54.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $88.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $42.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $38.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The orifice area is $A = \ell^2 = 0.0100\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and its height is small relative to the available head, so the small-orifice formula $Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g h}$ applies. With $h = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, we obtain $\sqrt{2 g h} = 8.86\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and hence $Q = 0.61 \cdot 0.0100 \cdot 8.86 = 0.0540\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $54.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The theoretical exit speed is $\sqrt{2 g h}$ because the entire head converts to velocity head in the absence of losses. Using $\sqrt{g h}$ would give $38.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
