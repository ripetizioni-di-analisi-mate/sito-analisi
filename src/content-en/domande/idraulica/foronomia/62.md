Two tanks are separated by a vertical partition with a rectangular opening of width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ submerged on both sides, as shown. Its upper edge is $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below the upstream surface. The downstream surface is lower by $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, and both levels are constant. Given discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.60$, what is the discharge?

- $7.55\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $5.93\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $4.51\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $3.19\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

At each horizontal strip the jet pressure equals the hydrostatic pressure of the downstream liquid. For a strip at depth $y$, Bernoulli’s equation gives speed $C_v\sqrt{2g\,\Delta h}$, where $\Delta h$ is the difference between the free-surface elevations and is independent of $y$. Every strip therefore operates under the same head. Even for a tall opening, integrating over its height gives area times velocity, with the discharge coefficient applied, $Q = \mu A\sqrt{2g\,\Delta h}$. With $A = 2.40\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\sqrt{2g \cdot 0.50} = 3.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the result is $Q = 0.60 \cdot 2.40 \cdot 3.13 = 4.51\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Integrating with depths measured below the downstream free surface as if the jet discharged into air would give $5.93\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
