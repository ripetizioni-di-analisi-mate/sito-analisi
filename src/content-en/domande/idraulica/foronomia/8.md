A tank with constant rectangular plan dimensions $4.00 \times 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ drains without inflow through a circular bottom orifice of diameter $D = 120\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.60$. The initial water level is $2.80\,\mathrm{m}$ above the orifice center. How long does complete drainage take?

- $557\,\mathrm{s}$
- $445\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1113\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1863\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The tank plan area is $\Omega = 4.00 \cdot 2.50 = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the orifice area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0113\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and $\mu\,A\,\sqrt{2g} = 0.0301\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}$. The opening is much smaller than the tank, so the surface falls slowly enough to evaluate the instantaneous discharge at the current head. Integrating the tank volume balance gives $t = \dfrac{2\,\Omega\sqrt{h_0}}{\mu\,A\,\sqrt{2g}} = \dfrac{2 \cdot 10.0 \cdot \sqrt{2.80}}{0.0301} = 1113\,\mathrm{s}$, about $18.6\,\mathrm{min}$. Holding the initial discharge $Q_0 = 50.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$ constant would give $557\,\mathrm{s}$. Over the complete draining interval, the mean discharge is half the initial value.
