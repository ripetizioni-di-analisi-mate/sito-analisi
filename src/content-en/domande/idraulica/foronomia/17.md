A constant-level tank discharges to the atmosphere through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$ in a thin vertical wall. The head at the orifice center is $h = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the measured discharge is $Q = 13.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Given contraction coefficient $C_c = 0.63$, what is the velocity coefficient?

- $0.383$
- $0.609$
- $0.966$
- $1.04$

## Explanation

For a small orifice the discharge is $Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g h}$, so the measured flow gives the discharge coefficient. The data give $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 2.83 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\sqrt{2 g h} = 7.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence $A\,\sqrt{2 g h} = 21.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $\mu = \dfrac{13.2}{21.7} = 0.609$. Since $\mu = C_c\,C_v$, it follows that $C_v = \dfrac{0.609}{0.63} = 0.966$. This lies in the experimental range $0.96 \div 0.99$ for the velocity coefficient of a sharp-edged orifice.
