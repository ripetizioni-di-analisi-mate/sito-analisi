A constant-level tank sits on a floor, with its water surface $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor. A small thin-wall orifice is to be cut in a vertical side so that its jet lands as far from the wall as possible. Neglect air resistance and take $C_v$ independent of head. At what elevation above the floor should the orifice be cut?

- $0\,\mathrm{m}$, at the foot of the wall
- $1.33\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.67\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $y$ be the height of the orifice above the floor. The head at the orifice is $h = 4.00 - y$ and the speed at the vena contracta is $v_c = C_v\sqrt{2gh}$. The jet leaves horizontally and falls through $y$ in time $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2y}{g}}$, reaching the floor at a horizontal distance
$$
x = v_c\,t = 2\,C_v\sqrt{h\,y} = 2\,C_v\sqrt{\left(4.00 - y\right)y} .
$$
The product $\left(4.00 - y\right)y$ is largest at $y = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, so placing the opening halfway up gives the maximum range $4.00\,C_v\,\mathrm{m}$. At the foot of the wall the head is greatest, but the jet has no height through which to fall and its horizontal range is zero.
