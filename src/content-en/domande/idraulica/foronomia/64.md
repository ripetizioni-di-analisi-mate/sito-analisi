A submerged orifice of area $A = 0.0200\,\mathrm{m^2}$ with $\mu = 0.60$ connects an open tank to a closed tank, as shown. The open surface is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the orifice center. The closed tank has water level $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the center and air at gauge pressure $p = 14.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The levels and air pressure remain constant. What is the discharge?

- $75.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $37.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $99.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $92.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The driving head for a submerged orifice is the difference between the piezometric heads in the two tanks, referred to the orifice centroid. This follows from Bernoulli’s equation between the upstream free surface and the jet in the downstream liquid. The closed tank has piezometric head $1.00 + \dfrac{14.7}{9.81} = 1.00 + 1.50 = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, so $\Delta h = 3.00 - 2.50 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and water flows from the open tank into the closed one. With $\sqrt{2g\,\Delta h} = 3.137\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the discharge is $Q = 0.60 \cdot 0.0200 \cdot 3.137 = 0.0376\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $37.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Ignoring the air pressure would leave only the elevation difference $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and give $75.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
