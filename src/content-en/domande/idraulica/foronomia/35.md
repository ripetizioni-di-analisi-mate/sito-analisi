A rectangular sharp-crested weir has a horizontal crest of width $b = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and a ventilated nappe, as shown. The measured upstream head is $h = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$. Use Bazin’s formula $\mu = 0.405 + \dfrac{0.003}{h}$ for the discharge coefficient, with $h$ in meters. What is the discharge?

::schema{solidi="0,-0.35 6,-0.35 6,0 0,0;2.9,0 3.1,0 3.1,1.5 2.9,1.5" acqua="0,0 2.9,0 2.9,2.1 0,2.1;2.9,2.1 3.3,2.0 3.7,1.6 3.95,1.05 4.1,0.35 4.15,0 3.65,0 3.6,0.55 3.4,1.1 3.2,1.42 3.1,1.5" pelo="0,2.9,2.1," tratteggi="2.0,2.1 4.6,2.1;2.0,1.5 4.6,1.5" quote="3 1.5,3 2.1,0.12 m,-0.55" etichette="5.1,1.2,nappe" titolo="Sharp-crested rectangular weir with crest width $b = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and head $h = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$ measured upstream"}

- $74.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $79.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $52.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $229\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

With the head in meters, Bazin’s formula gives $\mu = 0.405 + \dfrac{0.003}{0.12} = 0.430$. For an aerated nappe over a sharp-crested rectangular weir, $Q = \mu\,b\,h\,\sqrt{2 g h}$. With $h = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$, we obtain $\sqrt{2 g h} = 1.53\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and hence $Q = 0.430 \cdot 1.00 \cdot 0.12 \cdot 1.53 = 0.0792\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $79.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The term $\dfrac{0.003}{h}$ becomes more important as the head decreases. Dropping it would give $74.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
