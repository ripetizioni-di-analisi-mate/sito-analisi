A constant-level tank discharges to the atmosphere through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\mu = 0.61$ in a wall inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal. The orifice center is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ from the free surface, measured along the wall. What is the discharge?

- $16.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $23.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $11.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $66.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The head at the orifice is the vertical elevation difference between the free surface and the orifice centroid, as required by Bernoulli’s equation. The wall is inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal, giving a vertical difference of $h = 3.00 \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. With $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\sqrt{2gh} = 5.42\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the discharge is $Q = 0.61 \cdot 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 5.42 = 0.0166\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $16.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Using the distance measured along the wall as the head would give $23.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
