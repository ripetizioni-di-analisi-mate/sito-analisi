A rectangular orifice of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ in a vertical reservoir wall extends from depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at its top to depth $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$ at its bottom, as shown. Its discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.61$. What is the discharge?

::schema{solidi="-3.3,-0.3 0.3,-0.3 0.3,0 -3.3,0;-3.3,0 -3,0 -3,3.4 -3.3,3.4;0,0 0.3,0 0.3,0.4 0,0.4;0,2 0.3,2 0.3,3.4 0,3.4" acqua="-3,0 0,0 0,3 -3,3;0,0.4 0.9,0.5 1.8,0.54 1.8,1.92 0.9,1.95 0,2" pelo="-3,0,3,free surface" quote="0 2,0 3,1.00 m,-0.5;0 0.4,0 3,2.60 m,-1.4" titolo="Rectangular orifice of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with its edges at depths $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface"}

- $11.5\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $7.29\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $17.3\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $18.7\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The opening height is comparable with the head at its centroid. Use the large-orifice formula $Q = \dfrac{2}{3}\,\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\left(h_2^{\frac{3}{2}} - h_1^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)$, obtained by integrating the flow through horizontal strips. With $h_1 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_2 = 2.60\,\mathrm{m}$, the two terms are $h_2^{\frac{3}{2}} = 4.19$ and $h_1^{\frac{3}{2}} = 1.00$ and differ by $3.19$. Since $\dfrac{2}{3} \cdot 0.61 \cdot 2.00 \cdot \sqrt{2g} = 3.60$, the result is $Q = 3.60 \cdot 3.19 = 11.5\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Omitting the integration factor $\dfrac{2}{3}$ would give $17.3\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
