A rectangular channel of width $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ contains a full-width sharp-crested rectangular weir. Its crest stands $p = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bed and the nappe is ventilated, as shown. The measured upstream head is $h = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and the discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.42$. What is the approach velocity head at the measurement section?

- $52.9\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $5.88\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $13.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $11.8\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Bazin’s equation gives $Q = 0.42 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 0.30 \cdot \sqrt{2g \cdot 0.30} = 0.459\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. At the measuring section the water extends from the channel bed to the free surface, a depth $p + h = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$, so the mean velocity is $v_0 = \dfrac{0.459}{1.50 \cdot 0.90} = 0.340\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The velocity head is therefore $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2g} = 5.88 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$, approximately $2\%$ of the measured head, and can be neglected in the weir equation. Using only the layer of depth $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ above the crest as the flow section would give $52.9\,\mathrm{mm}$.
