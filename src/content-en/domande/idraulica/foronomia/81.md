A channel weir has a horizontal crest of streamwise length $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$. The upstream surface is $H = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the crest. Does the broad-crested weir formula $Q = 0.385\,b\,H\sqrt{2gH}$ apply?

- Yes, because the crest length exceeds the head
- Yes, because the crest is longer than half the head and is therefore broad
- No, because the crest length is less than about three times the head
- Yes, because the equation applies to any crest length

## Explanation

The formula assumes that the flow reattaches to the crest, with straight, parallel streamlines over part of its length, and reaches critical depth at the downstream edge. This requires a crest length greater than approximately three times the head, here $3 \cdot 0.40 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The actual length $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is shorter, leaving too little room for parallel flow to develop. The broad-crested formula **does not apply**. The half-minimum-dimension criterion distinguishes thin- from thick-walled orifices.
