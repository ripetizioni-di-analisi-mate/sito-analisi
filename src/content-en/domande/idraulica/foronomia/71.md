An axisymmetric vessel with no inflow drains through a small opening at its lowest point. Assume a constant discharge coefficient and quasi-steady flow. How must the free-surface radius $r$ vary with its height $h$ above the opening for the level to fall at constant speed, as in a water clock?

- A constant $r$, as in a cylindrical tank
- $r$ proportional to $h$, as in a conical tank
- $r$ proportional to $\sqrt{h}$
- $r$ proportional to $\sqrt[4]{h}$

## Explanation

During time $dt$, the decrease in stored volume $\pi r^2\,(-dh)$ equals the volume discharged through the orifice $\mu A\sqrt{2gh}\;dt$, so the free surface falls at the rate
$$
-\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{\mu A\sqrt{2g}\,\sqrt{h}}{\pi r^2} .
$$
The rate is constant if $r^2$ is proportional to $\sqrt{h}$, or equivalently if $r$ is proportional to $\sqrt[4]{h}$. In a cylindrical tank the rate of fall is proportional to $\sqrt{h}$ and decreases during drainage. In a conical tank it is proportional to $h^{-\frac{3}{2}}$. When $r$ is proportional to $\sqrt{h}$, it is proportional to $h^{-\frac{1}{2}}$. In both of these latter cases the surface falls faster as it approaches the orifice.
