Two constant-level tanks communicate through an orifice submerged on both sides, with velocity coefficient $C_v = 0.97$. The discharge is $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$ for a surface level difference $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. How much power is dissipated in transferring water between tanks?

- $0.104\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $3.53\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $0.883\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $1.77\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

Gauge pressure is zero and velocity is negligible at both free surfaces, so each tank’s total head equals its free-surface elevation. Bernoulli’s equation between the surfaces therefore gives a total head loss $\Delta H = \Delta h = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Of this, $\left(1 - C_v^2\right)\Delta h = 0.089\,\mathrm{m}$ is lost at the orifice entrance. The jet’s remaining velocity head dissipates in the downstream tank as an exit loss. Dissipated power is the weight flow rate times the head loss, $P = \gamma\,Q\,\Delta h = 9.81 \cdot 0.120 \cdot 1.50 = 1.77\,\mathrm{kW}$. Counting only the entrance loss would give $0.104\,\mathrm{kW}$.
