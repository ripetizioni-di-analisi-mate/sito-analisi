Two tanks of constant plan areas $\Omega_1 = 15.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\Omega_2 = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ communicate through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 120\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.60$. It remains submerged on both sides throughout the transfer. Initially the surface levels differ by $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. How long do they take to equalize?

- $282\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1412\,\mathrm{s}$
- $565\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2353\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The orifice area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0113\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving $k = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2g} = 0.0301\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}$. As liquid flows from one tank to the other, the upstream surface falls and the downstream surface rises. Both changes reduce the driving head, so the transfer time is $t = \dfrac{2\left(\sqrt{\Delta_0} - \sqrt{\Delta_1}\right)}{k\left(\dfrac{1}{\Omega_1} + \dfrac{1}{\Omega_2}\right)}$. We obtain $k\left(\dfrac{1}{15.0} + \dfrac{1}{10.0}\right) = 5.01 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}/s}$. The final levels are equal, giving $\Delta_1 = 0$ and $t = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2.00}}{5.01 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 565\,\mathrm{s}$, or approximately $9.4\,\mathrm{min}$. Accounting only for the fall in the first tank would give $1412\,\mathrm{s}$.
