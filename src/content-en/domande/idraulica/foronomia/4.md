A constant-level tank discharges to the atmosphere through an orifice in a thin vertical wall. The head at its center is $h = 2.40\,\mathrm{m}$. A point on the jet axis lies $x = 2.80\,\mathrm{m}$ horizontally and $y = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ below the center of the vena contracta, as shown. What head loss occurs at the orifice?

- $0.114\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.222\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Beyond the vena contracta, only gravity acts on the jet, so a fluid particle follows a parabola. Eliminating time from the two equations of motion gives $v_c = x\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2 y}}$. Substitution gives $v_c = 2.80 \cdot \sqrt{\dfrac{9.81}{1.80}} = 6.54\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and hence $C_v = \dfrac{v_c}{\sqrt{2 g h}} = \dfrac{6.54}{6.86} = 0.953$. Head loss is the part of the available head that does not appear as velocity head at the vena contracta, $\Delta H = \left(1 - C_v^2\right) h$. The loss is therefore $\Delta H = \left(1 - 0.908\right) \cdot 2.40 = 0.222\,\mathrm{m}$ and the jet velocity head is $2.18\,\mathrm{m}$.
