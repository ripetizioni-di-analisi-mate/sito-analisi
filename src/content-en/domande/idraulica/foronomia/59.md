In the vertical wall of a constant-level reservoir, a rectangular orifice extends from depth $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ to depth $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the surface. Assume a common discharge coefficient for all horizontal strips. At what depth does a horizontal line divide the opening into two portions with equal discharge?

- $2.13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.96\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.56\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.55\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

A horizontal strip at depth $y$ carries discharge $\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2gy}\;dy$. The part of the opening from its top edge to depth $y_m$ carries $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\left(y_m^{\frac{3}{2}} - h_1^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)$, and the part below that depth carries $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\left(h_2^{\frac{3}{2}} - y_m^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)$. The two discharges are equal when
$$
y_m^{\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{h_1^{\frac{3}{2}} + h_2^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2} = \frac{0.125 + 8.00}{2} = 4.06 ,
$$
giving $y_m = 4.06^{\frac{2}{3}} = 2.55\,\mathrm{m}$. The dividing line lies below the midheight of the opening, at depth $2.13\,\mathrm{m}$, because deeper strips carry more water per unit height at their higher velocities. The depth $1.96\,\mathrm{m}$ instead locates the point where the theoretical speed equals the average speed over the opening.
