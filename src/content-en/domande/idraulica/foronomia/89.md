A constant-level reservoir supplies a pipe of length $L = 200\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, and Darcy friction factor $\lambda = 0.020$. A nozzle at the end has exit diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $C_v = 0.97$ and $C_c = 1$, as shown. The reservoir surface is $H_0 = 40.0\,\mathrm{m}$ above the nozzle axis. Neglecting local losses, what is the discharge?

- $53.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $10.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $46.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $40.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

Let $A$ and $A_t$ be the cross-sectional areas of the nozzle exit and pipe, respectively. The head $H$ immediately upstream of the nozzle and the discharge satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
H = H_0 - \lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D}\,\dfrac{1}{2g}\left(\dfrac{Q}{A_t}\right)^2 \\[8pt]
Q = C_v\,A\,\sqrt{2gH}
\end{cases}
$$
where the first equation applies Bernoulli’s equation between the reservoir and nozzle with the Darcy–Weisbach head loss, and the second gives the nozzle discharge. Solving the second for $2gH$ and substituting into the first gives
$$
Q = \frac{C_v\,A\,\sqrt{2gH_0}}{\sqrt{1 + \lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D}\left(\dfrac{C_v\,A}{A_t}\right)^2}} = \frac{0.0534}{\sqrt{1 + 26.7 \cdot 0.0116}}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0466\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The pipe velocity is $2.64\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the head upstream of the nozzle is $H = 30.5\,\mathrm{m}$, so friction consumes $9.5\,\mathrm{m}$ of the available elevation head. Neglecting head loss would give $53.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
