A sharp-crested weir has a trapezoidal notch, shown from downstream. Its horizontal crest is $b = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ wide and each side extends outward by $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ horizontally per meter of rise. The head over the crest is $h = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Taking coefficient $\mu' = 0.62$ for every horizontal strip of the nappe, what is the discharge?

::schema{solidi="0,0 6,0 6,1.5 3.75,1.5 3.3,0.6 2.7,0.6 2.25,1.5 0,1.5" acqua="2.7,0.6 3.3,0.6 3.45,0.9 2.55,0.9" pelo="2.55,3.45,0.9," tratteggi="1.6,0.6 2.7,0.6;1.6,0.9 2.55,0.9" quote="1.8 0.6,1.8 0.9,0.30 m,-0.5;2.7 0.6,3.3 0.6,0.60 m,0.35" titolo="Downstream view of a trapezoidal weir with crest width $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$. Its sides flare by $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ per meter of height, and the head is $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$"}

- $180\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $253\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $217\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $307\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

A strip at depth $y$ below the free surface lies $h - y$ above the crest. It has width $b + 2 \cdot 0.50\,(h - y)$ and carries discharge $\mu'\left(b + 2 \cdot 0.50\,(h - y)\right)\sqrt{2gy}\;dy$. Integrating from $0$ to $h$, the term containing $b$ gives the rectangular-weir contribution, while the term containing $h - y$ gives the V-notch contribution with $\tan\dfrac{\theta}{2} = 0.50$,
$$
Q = \frac{2}{3}\,\mu'\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\;h^{\frac{3}{2}} + \frac{8}{15}\,\mu' \cdot 0.50 \cdot \sqrt{2g}\;h^{\frac{5}{2}} .
$$
The first term is $180.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the second is $36.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$, giving a total discharge $Q = 217\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Using the combined widening of both sides $1.00$ in place of $\tan\dfrac{\theta}{2}$ in the triangular term would give $253\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
