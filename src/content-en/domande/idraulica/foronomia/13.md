A rectangular channel has a broad-crested weir of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and crest length $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$, with sharp edges. The upstream water surface is $H = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ above the crest, approach velocity is negligible, and the discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.385$, as shown. What is the discharge?

- $1.03\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.12\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.89\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.29\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The crest is more than three head lengths thick, so the nappe reattaches and the flow reaches critical depth over the crest. Thus $Q = \mu\,b\,H\,\sqrt{2 g H}$. With $H = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$, this gives $\sqrt{2 g H} = 2.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and hence $Q = 0.385 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 0.45 \cdot 2.97 = 1.03\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The critical depth $h_c = \dfrac{2}{3}H = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ gives the same result, because the critical velocity is $\sqrt{g\,h_c}$ and therefore $Q = b\,h_c\sqrt{g\,h_c} = 1.03\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
