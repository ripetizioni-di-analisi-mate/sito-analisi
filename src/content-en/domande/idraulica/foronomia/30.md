A tank of constant plan area $\Omega = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ must empty completely in $10$ minutes with no inflow, through a bottom orifice of discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.60$, as shown. The initial level is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the orifice center. What orifice area is required?

::schema{solidi="-0.3,0 0,0 0,3.2 -0.3,3.2;3,0 3.3,0 3.3,3.2 3,3.2;-0.3,-0.3 1.35,-0.3 1.35,0 -0.3,0;1.65,-0.3 3.3,-0.3 3.3,0 1.65,0" acqua="0,0 3,0 3,2 0,2;1.35,0 1.65,0 1.6,-1.2 1.4,-1.2" pelo="0,3,2," quote="3 0,3 2,2.00 m,0.6" titolo="Prismatic tank of plan area $\Omega = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ with a bottom orifice and an initial water level $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the orifice centroid"}

- $0.0106\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $0.00532\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $0.00639\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $0.0150\,\mathrm{m^2}$

## Explanation

The time for a tank of constant plan area to drain completely is $t = \dfrac{2\,\Omega\sqrt{h_0}}{\mu\,A\,\sqrt{2g}}$. Solving for the orifice area gives $A = \dfrac{2\,\Omega\sqrt{h_0}}{\mu\,t\,\sqrt{2g}}$. The specified time is $t = 600\,\mathrm{s}$, yielding $2\,\Omega\sqrt{h_0} = 2 \cdot 6.00 \cdot 1.414 = 17.0\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}}$ and $\mu\,t\,\sqrt{2g} = 0.60 \cdot 600 \cdot 4.43 = 1594\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}}$. Thus $A = 0.0106\,\mathrm{m^2}$, corresponding to a circular orifice of diameter $116\,\mathrm{mm}$. The orifice area is much smaller than the tank’s plan area, consistent with the assumption that the free surface falls slowly.
