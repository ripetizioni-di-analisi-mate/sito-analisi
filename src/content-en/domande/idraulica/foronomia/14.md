A contoured nozzle at a pipe outlet has exit diameter $d = 45\,\mathrm{mm}$, velocity coefficient $C_v = 0.97$, and $C_c = 1$. The total head just upstream is $H = 55.0\,\mathrm{m}$ above the outlet axis. What is the jet power?

- $15.8\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $25.7\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $27.3\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $51.5\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The nozzle suppresses jet contraction, so the jet velocity is $v = C_v\sqrt{2 g H} = 0.97 \cdot 32.8 = 31.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and, with $A = \dfrac{\pi d^2}{4} = 1.59 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the discharge is $Q = A\,v = 0.0507\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The outlet has zero gauge pressure and lies at the datum. The entire total head of the jet is therefore its velocity head $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 51.7\,\mathrm{m}$. This gives $P = \gamma\,Q\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 9.81 \cdot 0.0507 \cdot 51.7 = 25.7\,\mathrm{kW}$. The available power $\gamma\,Q\,H$ is $27.3\,\mathrm{kW}$, so the nozzle delivers $94\%$ of the incoming hydraulic power to the jet, consistent with the ratio $C_v^2$.
