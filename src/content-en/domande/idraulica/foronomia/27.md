Two closed vessels $A$ and $B$ contain water and are connected by an orifice submerged on both sides, as shown in the figure. The free surface in $A$ is $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above the one in $B$, while the gauge pressure of the air above $B$ exceeds the one above $A$ by $8.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In which direction does the water flow through the orifice?

- From $B$ to $A$
- From $A$ to $B$
- The water stays at rest
- The direction depends on the discharge coefficient of the orifice

## Explanation

Through a submerged orifice the water moves from the side where the piezometric head is greater, and in each vessel the head is the sum of the free-surface elevation and the pressure head of the air above it. The excess air pressure in $B$ corresponds to the head
$$
\frac{\Delta p}{\gamma} = \frac{8.0}{9.81} = 0.815\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which is greater than the $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ level difference in favor of $A$. The piezometric head in $B$ therefore exceeds the one in $A$ by $0.815 - 0.60 = 0.215\,\mathrm{m}$, so the water flows through the orifice from $B$ to $A$, even though the free surface in $B$ is lower. The discharge coefficient affects the value of the discharge and not its direction.
