Two tanks communicate through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 180\,\mathrm{mm}$ in their partition, submerged on both sides, as shown. The surfaces remain at $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the orifice center. The discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.60$. What is the discharge?

- $90.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $121\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $145\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $151\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The orifice is submerged on both sides, so the driving head is the difference $\Delta h = 3.20 - 1.40 = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ between the free-surface elevations. The area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0254\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta h} = 5.94\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving $Q = 0.60 \cdot 0.0254 \cdot 5.94 = 0.0907\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $90.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Using the upstream head $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$ alone would yield $121\,\mathrm{L/s}$, the discharge through the same orifice if it were open to the atmosphere downstream.
