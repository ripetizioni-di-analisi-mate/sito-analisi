An inverted U-tube differential manometer is connected above two taps in a water-filled pipe at rest. Its manometer fluid has specific gravity $\delta = 0.80$. The interface on the $B$ side is $\Delta = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ higher than the one on the $A$ side. What is the piezometric head difference $h_A - h_B$?

- $0.480\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.600\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-0.120\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.120\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The **differential manometer** relation $h_A - h_B = \Delta\,(\delta - 1)$ also applies when the manometer liquid is lighter than water. Since $\delta = 0.80$, the factor $\delta - 1$ equals $-0.20$, giving
$$
h_A - h_B = 0.60 \cdot (-0.20) = -0.120\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The piezometric head at $A$ is therefore lower than at $B$, reversing the ordering obtained with mercury. For a manometer liquid lighter than water, the head difference has the opposite sign to the specified meniscus elevation difference.
