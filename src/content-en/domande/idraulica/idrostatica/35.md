A pump draws water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ from a well. At this temperature the absolute vapor pressure is $p_v = 2.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and atmospheric pressure is $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the maximum theoretical suction lift?

- $10.1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.33\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.239\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At the onset of vapor formation the absolute liquid pressure reaches the vapor pressure, giving the minimum allowable gauge pressure $2.34 - 101.3 = -98.96\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The maximum elevation difference is the water-column height equivalent to this pressure deficit,
$$
h = \frac{98.96}{9.81} = 10.1\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Neglecting vapor pressure would give $10.33\,\mathrm{m}$, corresponding to a perfect vacuum. In practice the allowable lift is kept well below this limit because vapor bubbles form and cavitation begins as the pressure approaches the vapor pressure.
