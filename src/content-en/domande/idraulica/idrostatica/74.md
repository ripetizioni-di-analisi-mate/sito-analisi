A water-filled bottle is inverted with its neck submerged in a basin, as shown. At equilibrium, air is trapped at the top and the water surface inside is $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ above the basin surface. What is the gauge pressure of the trapped air?

::schema{acqua="0,0 5,0 5,1 0,1;1.6,1 2.4,1 2.4,1.3 1.6,1.3" solidi="-0.2,-0.2 5.2,-0.2 5.2,0 -0.2,0;-0.2,0 0,0 0,1.5 -0.2,1.5;5,0 5.2,0 5.2,1.5 5,1.5;1.5,0.4 1.6,0.4 1.6,2.7 1.5,2.7;2.4,0.4 2.5,0.4 2.5,2.7 2.4,2.7;1.5,2.7 2.5,2.7 2.5,2.8 1.5,2.8" pelo="0.2,1.3,1;4.2,4.9,1;1.7,2.3,1.3" tratteggi="2.5,1.3 3.45,1.3" etichette="2,2,air;3.85,1.08,0.15 m" quote="3 1,3 1.3,,0.3" riferimento=0 titolo="Inverted bottle with its neck submerged in a basin"}

- $1.47\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-1.47\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $99.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The water in the basin and in the bottle forms one connected body at rest. The hydrostatic head plane coincides with the basin free surface, which is exposed to the atmosphere. The surface inside the bottle lies $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ above that plane, so its gauge pressure is negative and equals
$$
p = -9.81 \cdot 0.15 = -1.47\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The air touching the water surface has the same pressure. This pressure deficit supports the water column in the bottle above the basin level. The value $99.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the corresponding absolute pressure.
