A U-tube open at both ends contains water. Oil of specific gravity $0.80$ is poured into one limb, forming a column of height $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the interface, as shown. How far below the oil surface is the free water surface in the other limb?

::schema{acqua="0,0 0,-0.7 2,-0.7 2,0.4 1.5,0.4 1.5,-0.4 0.5,-0.4 0.5,0" poligoni="0,0 0.5,0 0.5,0.5 0,0.5" solidi="-0.15,-0.85 2.15,-0.85 2.15,-0.7 -0.15,-0.7;-0.15,-0.7 0,-0.7 0,1 -0.15,1;2,-0.7 2.15,-0.7 2.15,1 2,1;0.5,-0.4 1.5,-0.4 1.5,1 0.5,1" linee="0,0 0.5,0" pelo="0,0.5,0.5;1.5,2,0.4" quote="0 0,0 0.5,0.50 m,-0.45" etichette="0.25,0.82,oil;1.75,0.82,water" riferimento=0 titolo="Open U-tube with a column of oil above the water in one limb"}

- $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.100\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.500\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.625\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pressure at the interface is continuous across the two liquids and equals $\gamma_o \cdot 0.50 = 0.80 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50 = 3.92\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In the water limb this pressure supports a water column of height
$$
\frac{3.92}{9.81} = 0.400\,\mathrm{m}
$$
above the interface, so that is the height of the water surface. The oil column has height $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, placing the water surface $0.50 - 0.40 = 0.100\,\mathrm{m}$ below the oil surface.
