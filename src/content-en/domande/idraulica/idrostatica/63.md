An elevated tank supplies a neighborhood. Its free surface stands $45.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above street level. With no withdrawals, the water in the network is at rest. What is the greatest height above the street at which a tap can still deliver a gauge pressure of at least $1.50\,\mathrm{bar}$?

- $29.7\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $60.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $43.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The distribution system and the reservoir contain a single connected body of water at rest. Its hydrostatic head plane coincides with the reservoir surface at elevation $45.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The required pressure $1.50\,\mathrm{bar} = 150\,\mathrm{kPa}$ corresponds to a pressure head
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = \frac{150}{9.81} = 15.3\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so the tap must lie at least $15.3\,\mathrm{m}$ below the head plane. Its maximum elevation is therefore $45.00 - 15.3 = 29.7\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $43.5\,\mathrm{m}$ would result from converting $1.50\,\mathrm{bar}$ to $15.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ instead of $150\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
