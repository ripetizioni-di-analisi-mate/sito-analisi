Glycerin has specific gravity $\delta = 1.26$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu = 0.95\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$. What is its kinematic viscosity?

- $9.50 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $7.54 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $7.69 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $1.33 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$

## Explanation

Specific gravity is the ratio of the fluid density to that of water, so $\rho = 1.26 \cdot 1000 = 1260\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Dividing dynamic viscosity by density gives kinematic viscosity:
$$
\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho} = \frac{0.95}{1260} = 7.54 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s} .
$$
The denominator is the mass density in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Using the specific weight $\gamma = 12\,360\,\mathrm{N/m^3}$ would give $7.69 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$.
