A gauge on a pipe carrying water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ indicates gauge pressure $-120\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Given $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and absolute vapor pressure $p_v = 2.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$, which statement about this reading is correct?

- The reading is possible because gauge pressure can have any negative value
- The reading is possible and corresponds to an absolute pressure of $221\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- The reading is impossible because it corresponds to an absolute pressure of $-18.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- The reading is impossible because gauge pressure cannot be negative

## Explanation

The absolute pressure corresponding to the reading is
$$
p_{\mathrm{abs}} = p_{\mathrm{rel}} + p_{\mathrm{atm}} = -120 + 101.3 = -18.7\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Liquid water reaches its vapor pressure at the onset of vapor formation, so the minimum gauge pressure in this model is $2.34 - 101.3 = -98.96\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The reading is below this limit and cannot describe the stated liquid-water condition. It indicates a faulty or miscalibrated instrument. Negative gauge pressures down to this limit are possible, for example at the crest of a siphon.
