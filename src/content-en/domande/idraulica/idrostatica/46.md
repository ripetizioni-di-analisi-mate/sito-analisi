The Mariotte bottle shown holds water beneath a stopper. A tube through the stopper is open to the atmosphere and extends to $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. The free surface in the bottle is $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom, with air above it. If the water is at rest and the tube contains air down to its tip, what is the gauge pressure at the bottom?

- $6.87\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $2.45\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $4.41\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $9.32\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

At the lower end of the tube the water is in contact with air at atmospheric pressure, so its gauge pressure is zero. The hydrostatic head plane therefore passes through the tube end, $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. The water surface inside the bottle is at a different elevation. The hydrostatic pressure law gives the bottom pressure as
$$
p = 9.81 \cdot 0.25 = 2.45\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The water surface in the bottle lies $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ above the head plane, so the air above it has negative gauge pressure $-9.81 \cdot 0.45 = -4.41\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The pressure $6.87\,\mathrm{kPa}$ would apply to an open bottle. As long as the water surface remains above the tube end, the bottom pressure stays at $2.45\,\mathrm{kPa}$ regardless of the surface elevation.
