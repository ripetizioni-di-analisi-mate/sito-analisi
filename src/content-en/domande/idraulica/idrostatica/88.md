The water-filled U-tube shown rotates at $n = 90\,\mathrm{rpm}$ about the vertical axis of one limb. The other limb is $L = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis, and the water rotates as a rigid body. How much higher is the level in the outer limb?

::schema{acqua="-0.15,0 3.15,0 3.15,1.5 2.85,1.5 2.85,0.3 0.15,0.3 0.15,1.5 -0.15,1.5" linee="-0.15,2.6 -0.15,0 3.15,0 3.15,2.6;0.15,2.6 0.15,0.3 2.85,0.3 2.85,2.6" pelo="-0.15,0.15,1.5;2.85,3.15,1.5" tratteggi="0,-0.7 0,3.3" frecce="-0.7,3,0.7,3,ω" quote="0 0,3 0,0.30 m,0.5" etichette="1.5,1.85,level with the tube at rest;0.55,3.45,axis of rotation" riferimento=0 titolo="U-tube that rotates about the axis of one limb, shown at rest"}

- $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.815\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.407\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0432\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The angular velocity is
$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi \cdot 90}{60} = 9.425\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
In rigid-body rotation the constant-pressure surfaces are the paraboloids $z = z_v + \dfrac{\omega^2 r^2}{2g}$. The two free surfaces have zero gauge pressure and lie on the same paraboloid, whose vertex is in the limb on the rotation axis. The outer limb is at radius $r = L$, giving an elevation difference
$$
\Delta z = \frac{\omega^2 L^2}{2g} = \frac{9.425^2 \cdot 0.30^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.407\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds to $\dfrac{\omega^2 L^2}{4g}$, the rise of the rim above the initial level in an open cylindrical vessel. For the U-tube, the difference between the two limbs is the full $\dfrac{\omega^2 L^2}{2g}$.
