A closed tank contains water at rest. The gauge pressure of the air above it rises from $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$ to $25\,\mathrm{kPa}$. By how much does the pressure increase at depth $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface?

- $5.00\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $15.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $44.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The hydrostatic pressure law gives pressure $p = p_0 + \gamma\,h$ at depth $h$ below the free surface. Because the depth remains constant, the increase in pressure at the point equals the increase in air pressure,
$$
\Delta p = 25 - 10 = 15.0\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Thus a pressure increase applied at the surface produces the same increase throughout the liquid. This is **Pascal’s principle**. The term $\gamma\,h = 29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$ remains constant and contributes nothing to the change.
