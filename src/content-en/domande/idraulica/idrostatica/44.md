An open container holds water to a depth of $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and is lifted with constant upward acceleration $a = 2.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The water is at rest relative to the container. What is the gauge pressure at the bottom?

::schema{acqua="0,0 2,0 2,1.6 0,1.6" solidi="-0.2,-0.2 2.2,-0.2 2.2,0 -0.2,0;-0.2,0 0,0 0,2 -0.2,2;2,0 2.2,0 2.2,2 2,2" pelo="0,2,1.6" frecce="2.9,0.4,2.9,1.5,a" quote="0 0,0 1.6,1.60 m,-0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Open container being raised with upward acceleration $a$"}

- $4.00\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $15.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $19.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $11.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The liquid is at rest in a frame attached to the container. Because this frame accelerates upward, the inertial force acts downward and adds to gravity. The resulting body force per unit mass has magnitude $g + a$. Integrating the **differential equation of hydrostatic equilibrium** vertically gives
$$
p = \rho\,(g + a)\,h = 1000 \cdot (9.81 + 2.50) \cdot 1.60 = 19\,700\,\mathrm{Pa} = 19.7\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
With the container stationary, the bottom pressure would be $15.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Upward acceleration therefore increases it. For downward acceleration, $g - a$ would replace $g + a$.
