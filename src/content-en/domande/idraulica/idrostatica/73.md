A closed tank contains water to depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ beneath air at gauge pressure $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Two piezometers connect to the wall, one at the bottom and one $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above it. How high above the bottom does water rise in each?

- To $4.04\,\mathrm{m}$ in the first piezometer and $5.54\,\mathrm{m}$ in the second
- To $4.04\,\mathrm{m}$ in the first piezometer and $2.54\,\mathrm{m}$ in the second
- To $4.04\,\mathrm{m}$ in both piezometers
- In both piezometers the water reaches the tank’s free surface, at $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Both piezometers are connected to the same body of water at rest and reach the same hydrostatic head plane. Pressurized air raises that plane by
$$
\frac{p_0}{\gamma} = \frac{20}{9.81} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m}
$$
above the free surface, placing it $4.04\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. In the second piezometer the pressure head $2.54\,\mathrm{m}$ is measured upward from its tap, which is $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. The water therefore reaches $4.04\,\mathrm{m}$ there as well.
