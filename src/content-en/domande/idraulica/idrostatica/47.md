Estimate the percentage increase in the density of water at depth $4000\,\mathrm{m}$ compared with its surface value. Use $\varepsilon = 2.1 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ for water and take $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ constant when calculating pressure.

- $0.190\%$
- $0.934\%$
- $1.87\%$
- $0.00187\%$

## Explanation

The hydrostatic pressure law gives the gauge pressure at that depth as
$$
\Delta p = 9810 \cdot 4000 = 3.92 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
A water element retains its mass as its volume decreases by the fraction $\dfrac{\Delta p}{\varepsilon}$. To first order, its density therefore increases by the same fraction,
$$
\frac{\Delta\rho}{\rho} = \frac{3.92 \cdot 10^{7}}{2.1 \cdot 10^{9}} = 0.0187 = 1.87\% .
$$
Computing pressure as $\rho\,h$ and omitting $g$ would give $0.190\%$. Dividing a pressure in kilopascals by $\varepsilon$ in pascals would give $0.00187\%$. Even at this depth, the change is only about $2\%$, which explains the usual incompressible-water approximation in hydrostatics.
