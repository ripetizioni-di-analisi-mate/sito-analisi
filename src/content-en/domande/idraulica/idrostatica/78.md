A mechanical pressure gauge is connected to a pipe of still water through a water-filled tube. The gauge is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the pipe centerline and reads $1.20\,\mathrm{bar}$. What is the gauge pressure at the centerline?

- $80.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $159\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $120\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $51.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The **mechanical pressure gauge** measures the gauge pressure $1.20\,\mathrm{bar} = 120\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at its own elevation. The water in the connecting tube and pipe is a single body at rest. The pipe centerline is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ lower, so the hydrostatic pressure law gives the centerline pressure
$$
p = 120 + 9.81 \cdot 4.00 = 159\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Subtracting $\gamma\,\Delta z$ would give $80.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$, the pressure at a point $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the gauge. The value $51.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$ results from the conversion $1\,\mathrm{bar} = 10\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
