Take the bulk modulus and density of water to be $\varepsilon = 2.1 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. At what speed does a small pressure disturbance propagate through the water?

- $1449\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $463\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.10 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The speed of a small pressure disturbance in a liquid is $c = \sqrt{\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\rho}}$, giving
$$
c = \sqrt{\frac{2.1 \cdot 10^{9}}{1000}} = \sqrt{2.1 \cdot 10^{6}} = 1449\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Under the square root is the ratio of bulk modulus to density in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Substituting specific weight would give $463\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and omitting the square root would give $2.10 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. This wave speed enters water-hammer calculations for pipes.
