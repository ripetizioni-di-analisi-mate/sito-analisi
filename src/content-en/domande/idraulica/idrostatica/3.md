Two pressure gauges are mounted on the vertical wall of a closed tank full of still water under an air cushion, with their taps at elevations that differ by $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$. The gauge pressure of the air is raised from $10$ to $30\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the water level does not change. By how much does the difference between the readings of the two gauges change?

- $0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $7.85\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $20.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $40.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

By **Stevin's law** the gauge pressure at depth $h$ below the free surface is $p = p_0 + \gamma h$, where $p_0$ is the air pressure. The increase of $p_0$ is therefore transmitted unchanged to every point of the liquid, and each gauge reads $20.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ more. The difference between the two readings is $\gamma\,\Delta z = 9.81 \cdot 0.80 = 7.85\,\mathrm{kPa}$ before and after the increase, since it does not contain $p_0$, so its change is $0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $7.85\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the difference itself, and $20.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the increase in the reading of each gauge.
