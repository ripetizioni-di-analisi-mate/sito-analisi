The water in a closed tank is $3.50\,\mathrm{m}$ deep. The air above it is maintained at gauge pressure $p_0 = -28\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the gauge pressure at the bottom?

- $6.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-6.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $34.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $62.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Retain the negative sign of the air gauge pressure in the **hydrostatic pressure law**, giving $p = p_0 + \gamma\,h$. With $p_0 = -28\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $h = 3.50\,\mathrm{m}$,
$$
p = -28 + 9.81 \cdot 3.50 = -28 + 34.3 = 6.34\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The water-column contribution exceeds the magnitude of the negative air gauge pressure, giving positive gauge pressure at the bottom. Gauge pressure is zero within the liquid at a depth $\dfrac{28}{9.81} = 2.85\,\mathrm{m}$ below the surface.
