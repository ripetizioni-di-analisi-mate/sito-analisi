The capillary rise of water in a glass piezometer must not exceed $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. Given $\sigma = 0.0728\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and a zero contact angle, what is the minimum inside diameter?

- $9.89\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $4.95\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $19.8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $2.47\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

By **Jurin’s law**, the capillary rise is $h = \dfrac{4\,\sigma\cos\theta}{\gamma\,D}$ and varies inversely with tube diameter. Setting $h = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and solving for $D$ gives
$$
D = \frac{4\,\sigma}{\gamma\,h} = \frac{4 \cdot 0.0728}{9810 \cdot 0.0030} = 9.89 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
A larger diameter produces a smaller rise, so this is the minimum acceptable diameter. Capillary effects are the reason piezometer tubes are commonly made at least a centimeter in diameter.
