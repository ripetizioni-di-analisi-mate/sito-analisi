A vertical cylinder full of water is closed at the top by a frictionless piston of mass $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and diameter $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, exposed to the atmosphere above. A piezometer connects to the cylinder wall. How high above the underside of the piston does the water rise?

- $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.637\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.55\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.260\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The piston is in equilibrium. The gauge pressure of the water on its lower face supports its weight, so that pressure is
$$
p = \frac{m\,g}{\dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}} = \frac{20 \cdot 9.81}{\dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.10^2}{4}} = 24.98\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Because the piezometer is open to the atmosphere, water rises to the hydrostatic head plane, a distance above the piston face of
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = \frac{24.98}{9.81} = 2.55\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Omitting the factor $g$ from the piston weight would give $0.260\,\mathrm{m}$, and using the area $D^2$ would give $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$.
