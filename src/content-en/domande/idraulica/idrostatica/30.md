A closed tank contains water with air above it at gauge pressure $p_0 = -18\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Where is the hydrostatic head plane?

- $1.84\,\mathrm{m}$ above the free surface
- $1.84\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface
- $18.0\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface
- At the free-surface elevation

## Explanation

The **hydrostatic head plane** lies a signed vertical distance $\dfrac{p_0}{\gamma}$ above the free surface, with the same sign as $p_0$. Here the air gauge pressure is negative, so
$$
\frac{p_0}{\gamma} = \frac{-18}{9.81} = -1.84\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and the plane lies $1.84\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. Water between this plane and the free surface is at negative gauge pressure.
