An excavation of depth $12.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is filled with a homogeneous slurry of water and sand. The sand has specific gravity $2.65$ and accounts for $20\%$ of the volume. What is the gauge pressure at the bottom?

- $118\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $157\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $215\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $62.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

One cubic meter of the slurry contains $0.80\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of water and $0.20\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of sand. Its mass is the sum of their masses, so the mixture’s specific gravity is
$$
\delta = 0.80 \cdot 1 + 0.20 \cdot 2.65 = 1.33 .
$$
The mixture is homogeneous and at rest. Applying the hydrostatic pressure law with the mixture’s specific weight gives the bottom pressure
$$
p = 1.33 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 12.0 = 157\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The simple average of the two specific gravities, $1.825$, neglects the volume fractions and gives $215\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $118\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the pressure for water alone.
