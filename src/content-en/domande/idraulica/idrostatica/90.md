A pressure transmitter at the bottom of an open diesel tank converts pressure to level by dividing by $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$, as if the tank contained water. The diesel has specific gravity $0.84$ and a depth of $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. What level does the instrument indicate?

- $3.57\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.52\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.480\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By the hydrostatic pressure law, the bottom pressure is
$$
p = 0.84 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.00 = 24.7\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The instrument divides this pressure by the specific weight of water, so its reading is
$$
h = \frac{24.7}{9.81} = 2.52\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
the height of a water column producing the same pressure. Diesel fuel is less dense than water, so the indicated level is below the actual level. The value $3.57\,\mathrm{m}$ results from reversing the correction and dividing the actual depth by $0.84$.
