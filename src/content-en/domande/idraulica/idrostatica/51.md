In a concentric-cylinder viscometer the inner cylinder, of radius $R = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and height $H = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$, turns at angular velocity $\omega = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. An oil film of thickness $s = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ separates it from the fixed outer cylinder. Maintaining rotation requires torque $0.40\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Neglecting end effects, what is the dynamic viscosity of the oil?

- $0.0637\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- $1.02\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- $0.509\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- $0.0255\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$

## Explanation

The inner cylinder has surface speed $\omega R$. The gap is thin, so the velocity gradient is effectively constant. By **Newton’s law of viscosity**, the shear stress is $\tau = \mu\,\dfrac{\omega R}{s}$. It acts over the lateral area $2\pi R H$ at moment arm $R$, giving a resisting torque
$$
M = \mu\,\frac{\omega R}{s}\,2\pi R H\,R = \frac{2\pi\,\mu\,\omega R^3 H}{s} .
$$
Solving for $\mu$ gives
$$
\mu = \frac{M\,s}{2\pi\,\omega R^3 H} = \frac{0.40 \cdot 0.0010}{2\pi \cdot 10 \cdot 0.050^3 \cdot 0.100} = 0.509\,\mathrm{Pa\,s} .
$$
Omitting the moment arm $R$ would give $0.0255\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$. Using the diameter instead of the radius would give $0.0637\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$.
