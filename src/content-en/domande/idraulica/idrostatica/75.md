A pump is stopped with its discharge valve closed. Its suction pipe extends into a well and contains water at rest. A vacuum gauge at the pump inlet reads gauge pressure $-0.42\,\mathrm{bar}$. How far above the well surface is the inlet?

- $0.428\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.04\,\mathrm{m}$
- $14.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.28\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The water in the pipe and well forms one connected body at rest, so the hydrostatic head plane coincides with the well’s free surface. The gauge pressure $-0.42\,\mathrm{bar} = -42\,\mathrm{kPa}$ shows that the pump inlet lies above this plane by
$$
\frac{|p|}{\gamma} = \frac{42}{9.81} = 4.28\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $6.04\,\mathrm{m}$ follows from dividing the absolute pressure $101.3 - 42 = 59.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$ by $\gamma$, whereas the elevation difference is determined by gauge pressure. The conversion $1\,\mathrm{bar} = 10\,\mathrm{kPa}$ would give $0.428\,\mathrm{m}$.
