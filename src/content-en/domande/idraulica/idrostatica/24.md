A gauge connected to a pipe indicates a gauge pressure of $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Given a mercury specific gravity of $13.6$, what is the equivalent pressure in millimeters of mercury?

- $150\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $1500\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $2040\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $11.0\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The liquid-column height equivalent to a pressure equals that pressure divided by the liquid’s specific weight. For mercury, $\gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}} = 13.6 \cdot 9.81 = 133.4\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$, so
$$
h = \frac{20}{133.4} = 0.150\,\mathrm{m} = 150\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
The same pressure corresponds to a water column of height $\dfrac{20}{9.81} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m}$. At equal pressure the mercury column is shorter than the water column by a factor of $13.6$. This ratio is the basis for using mercury in liquid-column manometers.
