A flat vertical wall of an open tank is wetted from the free surface to the bottom. What is the shape of the gauge-pressure diagram on the wall?

- A triangle with its vertex at the free surface and its base at the bottom
- A triangle with its vertex at the bottom and its base at the free surface
- A rectangle with height equal to the water depth
- A trapezoid with its shorter base at the free surface

## Explanation

By the **hydrostatic pressure law**, the gauge pressure is $p = \gamma\,h$, where $h$ is the depth below the free surface. Pressure increases linearly with depth, from zero at the surface to its maximum at the bottom. The pressure diagram is therefore triangular, with its vertex at the top and its base at the bottom. A wall that does not extend to the hydrostatic head plane has a trapezoidal pressure diagram, as in a closed tank with pressurized air above the water.
