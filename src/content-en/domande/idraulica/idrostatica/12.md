The open tank shown contains a layer of oil of specific gravity $\delta = 0.86$ and thickness $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above a layer of water of thickness $2.20\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the gauge pressure at the bottom?

::schema{acqua="0,0 3.6,0 3.6,2.2 0,2.2" poligoni="0,2.2 3.6,2.2 3.6,3.7 0,3.7" solidi="-0.3,-0.3 3.9,-0.3 3.9,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,4.1 -0.3,4.1;3.6,0 3.9,0 3.9,4.1 3.6,4.1" pelo="0,3.6,3.7" punti="1.8,0,F" etichette="1.8,2.9,oil δ = 0.86;1.8,1.05,water" quote="0 3.7,0 2.2,1.50 m,-0.8;0 2.2,0 0,2.20 m,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Open tank with a layer of oil above a layer of water"}

- $21.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $31.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $34.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $36.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Apply the **hydrostatic pressure law** separately in each liquid, using its own specific weight. The two calculations must give the same pressure at the interface. In the oil layer,
$$
p_i = 0.86 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.50 = 12.7\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
$$
and adding the contribution of the water layer gives
$$
p_f = 12.7 + 9.81 \cdot 2.20 = 34.2\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Oil is less dense than water. Treating the full depth $3.70\,\mathrm{m}$ as water would therefore give the larger pressure $36.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
