An oil film of dynamic viscosity $\mu = 0.40\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ and thickness $6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ flows over a fixed flat plate. Velocity varies with distance $y$ from the plate according to $v(y) = 6\,y - 500\,y^2$, where $y$ is in meters and $v$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$. What shear stress does the oil exert on the plate?

- $1.20\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $0\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $7.20 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.40\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

By **Newton’s law of viscosity**, the shear stress is $\tau = \mu\,\dfrac{dv}{dy}$. The velocity gradient must be evaluated at the plate, where $y = 0$. Differentiating the velocity profile gives
$$
\frac{dv}{dy} = 6 - 1000\,y ,
$$
which equals $6\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ at $y = 0$, giving
$$
\tau = 0.40 \cdot 6 = 2.40\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
Because the velocity profile is parabolic, the average gradient $\dfrac{v(s)}{s} = 3\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is smaller than the gradient at the plate and would give $1.20\,\mathrm{Pa}$. At $y = 6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ the gradient vanishes. Zero shear stress therefore occurs at the film’s free surface.
