A closed cylindrical vessel of radius $R = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ is completely full of water and rotates about its vertical axis at $\omega = 12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The liquid rotates as a rigid body. A small hole at the center of the horizontal lid exposes that point to atmospheric pressure. What is the gauge pressure on the underside of the lid at its rim?

- $36.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $9.00\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $1.50\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $18.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

For rigid-body rotation, integrating the hydrostatic equilibrium equation with the centrifugal body force gives
$$
p = p_0 + \rho\,\frac{\omega^2 r^2}{2} - \gamma\,(z - z_0) ,
$$
where $p_0$ is the pressure on the rotation axis at elevation $z_0$. Elevation is constant along the lid, and gauge pressure on the axis is zero. Thus $p = \rho\,\dfrac{\omega^2 r^2}{2}$, giving at the rim
$$
p = 1000 \cdot \frac{12^2 \cdot 0.50^2}{2} = 18\,000\,\mathrm{Pa} = 18.0\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Omitting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ would give $36.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The value $9.00\,\mathrm{kPa}$ corresponds to the level shift $\dfrac{\omega^2 R^2}{4g}$ in an open vessel. That shift follows from conservation of volume beneath a free surface, which a closed, completely full vessel does not have.
