An open test tube containing water rotates in a centrifuge at constant angular speed $\omega = 200\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ with its axis horizontal and radial. The water rotates as a rigid body. The free surface is $r_1 = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the rotation axis and the bottom of the tube is $r_2 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ from it. Neglecting gravity compared with the centrifugal effect, what is the gauge pressure at the bottom?

- $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $128\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $32.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $256\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

With gravity neglected, the effective body force per unit mass in the rotating frame is the outward centrifugal acceleration $\omega^2 r$. The radial **differential equation of hydrostatic equilibrium** gives $\dfrac{dp}{dr} = \rho\,\omega^2 r$. Constant-pressure surfaces are cylinders concentric with the rotation axis. The free surface, at zero gauge pressure, has radius $r_1$. Integrating from $r_1$ to $r_2$ gives
$$
p = \rho\,\frac{\omega^2\,(r_2^2 - r_1^2)}{2} = 1000 \cdot \frac{200^2 \cdot (0.100^2 - 0.060^2)}{2} = 128\,000\,\mathrm{Pa} = 128\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Integrating from the axis, as though the free surface were at $r = 0$, would give $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Using $(r_2 - r_1)^2$ in place of $r_2^2 - r_1^2$ would give $32.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At the bottom, the centrifugal acceleration is $200^2 \cdot 0.100 = 4000\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, approximately $400$ times $g$, which justifies neglecting gravity.
