An open tank contains a layer of oil of specific gravity $0.90$ and thickness $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above water. How far from the oil surface is the hydrostatic head plane of the water?

- $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$ below the oil surface
- $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$ above the oil surface
- $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below the oil surface
- $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the oil surface

## Explanation

The pressure at the interface is $0.90 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.00 = 17.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In water this corresponds to a column of height
$$
\frac{17.7}{9.81} = 1.80\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The water’s **hydrostatic head plane** therefore lies $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ above the interface, whereas the oil surface is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above it. The plane is thus $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$ below the oil surface. Water in a piezometer connected to the water layer rises to this plane.
