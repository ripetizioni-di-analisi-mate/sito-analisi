In a closed tank the hydrostatic head plane is $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below the water surface. What can be said about the pressure in the water between the head plane and the free surface?

- Gauge pressure is positive and increases upward
- Absolute pressure is negative throughout that layer
- Gauge pressure is zero throughout that layer
- Gauge pressure is negative throughout that layer

## Explanation

The gauge pressure is $p = \gamma\,(h - z)$, where $h$ is the elevation of the hydrostatic head plane. Gauge pressure is positive below the plane, zero on it, and negative above it. The points in question lie above it and therefore have negative gauge pressure. At the free surface the pressure deficit is $-9.81 \cdot 0.80 = -7.85\,\mathrm{kPa}$, the same as in the air immediately above it. The absolute pressure remains positive. Under ordinary atmospheric conditions the limiting suction head is approximately ten meters of water.
