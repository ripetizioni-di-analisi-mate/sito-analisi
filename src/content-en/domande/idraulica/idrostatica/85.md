The accelerometer shown is a water-filled U-tube mounted on a vehicle that accelerates horizontally. The vertical limbs are $L = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. With the liquid at rest relative to the tube, the level in the rear limb exceeds that in the front by $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the vehicle acceleration?

::schema{acqua="-0.2,0 4.2,0 4.2,2 3.8,2 3.8,0.4 0.2,0.4 0.2,2.6 -0.2,2.6" linee="-0.2,3.4 -0.2,0 4.2,0 4.2,3.4;0.2,3.4 0.2,0.4 3.8,0.4 3.8,3.4" pelo="-0.2,0.2,2.6;3.8,4.2,2" tratteggi="0.2,2.6 5.3,2.6;4.2,2 5.3,2" quote="5 2,5 2.6,,0.6;0 0,4 0,0.40 m,0.6" frecce="1.2,4,2.8,4,a" etichette="0,3.75,rear;4,3.75,front;6.25,2.22,6.0 cm" riferimento=0 titolo="U-tube accelerometer mounted on an accelerating vehicle"}

- $1.47\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.736\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.94\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $65.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

In the vehicle frame the liquid is at rest. Its constant-pressure surfaces are planes inclined at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal, with $\tan\theta = \dfrac{a}{g}$. Both free surfaces have zero gauge pressure and lie in the same plane. Its slope is
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{0.060}{0.40} = 0.15 .
$$
giving
$$
a = g\,\tan\theta = 9.81 \cdot 0.15 = 1.47\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The higher level is in the rear limb, so the acceleration is forward. Dividing the distance between the limbs by the elevation difference would give $65.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, the reciprocal of the slope.
