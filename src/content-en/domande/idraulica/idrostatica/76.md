A suction cup of diameter $D = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ adheres to glass. The *absolute* pressure of the trapped air is $30\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Given $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and treating the cup as a flat disk of diameter $D$, what force normal to the glass is needed to detach it?

- $358\,\mathrm{N}$
- $509\,\mathrm{N}$
- $151\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1430\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Atmospheric pressure acts on the outside of the suction cup and the trapped air pressure acts on the inside. Both act normal to the disk, whose area is
$$
A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.080^2}{4} = 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2} .
$$
The force pressing the cup against the plate is the pressure difference times this area. Detaching the cup requires a force at least this large,
$$
F = (101.3 - 30) \cdot 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3} = 0.358\,\mathrm{kN} = 358\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The difference $30 - 101.3 = -71.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the gauge pressure of the trapped air, so gauge pressures give the same holding force. The value $509\,\mathrm{N}$ neglects the trapped air pressure. The value $151\,\mathrm{N}$ counts only that pressure, while $1430\,\mathrm{N}$ comes from using the diameter as the radius.
