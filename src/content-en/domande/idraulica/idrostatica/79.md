The micromanometer shown measures air pressure in a ventilation duct. It contains alcohol of specific gravity $0.80$. The reading tube is open to the atmosphere and inclined at $20^\circ$ to the horizontal. The reservoir is large enough for its level to remain constant. The duct pressure moves the meniscus $120\,\mathrm{mm}$ along the tube. What is the air gauge pressure?

- $942\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $885\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $403\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $322\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The meniscus moves $120\,\mathrm{mm}$ along the inclined tube, a vertical rise of
$$
\Delta z = 0.120 \cdot \sin 20^\circ = 0.0410\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The reading tube is open to the atmosphere. The air pressure above the reservoir balances the alcohol column between the reservoir surface and the tube meniscus, so
$$
p = 0.80 \cdot 9810 \cdot 0.0410 = 322\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The inclined scale gives a reading $\dfrac{1}{\sin 20^\circ} = 2.92$ times as long as in a vertical tube, which makes small pressure differences easier to resolve. Using the reading along the tube as the elevation difference would give $942\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
