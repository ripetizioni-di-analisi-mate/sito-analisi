A U-tube differential manometer measures a piezometric head difference $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ between pipe taps. The manometer fluid can be mercury of specific gravity $13.6$ or a liquid of specific gravity $1.60$. Which gives the larger interface level difference, and what is that difference?

- The liquid of specific gravity $1.60$, with a level difference of $83.3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- Mercury, with a level difference of $630\,\mathrm{mm}$
- The liquid of specific gravity $1.60$, with a level difference of $31.3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- Both liquids give the same level difference, $50.0\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The **differential manometer** satisfies $h_A - h_B = \Delta\,(\delta - 1)$, so its meniscus elevation difference is
$$
\Delta = \frac{h_A - h_B}{\delta - 1} .
$$
Mercury gives $\Delta = \dfrac{50}{12.6} = 3.97\,\mathrm{mm}$, while the liquid of specific gravity $1.60$ gives $\Delta = \dfrac{50}{0.60} = 83.3\,\mathrm{mm}$. The closer $\delta$ is to $1$, the greater the elevation difference for a given pressure difference. Manometer liquids only slightly denser than water are therefore suitable for measuring small head differences. Multiplying by $\delta - 1$ instead of dividing would give $630\,\mathrm{mm}$.
