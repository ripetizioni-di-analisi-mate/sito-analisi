The U-tube shown is open at both ends. It holds a liquid of specific gravity $1.6$ with a water column above it in the left limb. Point $P$ is at the interface and $Q$ is at the same elevation in the right limb. Point $R$ is in the water and $S$ is at the same elevation in the heavier liquid in the right limb. Which two points have equal pressure?

::schema{poligoni="0,0 0,-0.7 2,-0.7 2,0.5 1.5,0.5 1.5,-0.4 0.5,-0.4 0.5,0" acqua="0,0 0.5,0 0.5,0.8 0,0.8" solidi="-0.15,-0.85 2.15,-0.85 2.15,-0.7 -0.15,-0.7;-0.15,-0.7 0,-0.7 0,1.1 -0.15,1.1;2,-0.7 2.15,-0.7 2.15,1.1 2,1.1;0.5,-0.4 1.5,-0.4 1.5,1.1 0.5,1.1" linee="0,0 0.5,0" pelo="0,0.5,0.8;1.5,2,0.5" punti="0.25,0,P;1.75,0,Q;0.25,0.3,R;1.75,0.3,S" tratteggi="-0.45,0 0,0;2,0 2.45,0;-0.45,0.3 0,0.3;2,0.3 2.45,0.3" etichette="0.25,1.3,water;1,-1.15,liquid δ = 1.6" riferimento=0 titolo="U-tube with a column of water above a heavier liquid in the left limb"}

- $R$ and $S$, because they are at the same elevation
- $P$ and $R$, because they are in the same limb
- $P$ and $Q$, because they are at the same elevation in one connected body of the same liquid
- No two points, because the limbs contain different liquids

## Explanation

Two points at the same elevation have equal pressure if they lie in one connected body of the same liquid at rest. The heavier liquid fills the entire tube segment joining $P$ and $Q$, so their pressures are equal. Points $R$ and $S$ are both $y$ above $P$ and $Q$, respectively. Rising by $y$ reduces pressure by $\gamma\,y$ in water and by $1.6\,\gamma\,y$ in the heavier liquid. The pressure at $R$ is therefore higher than at $S$. Point $R$ is higher than $P$ in the same column and therefore has lower pressure.
