A stationary liquid has specific weight $\gamma = 8.50\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$. Its gauge pressure at point $M$ is $62\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and point $N$ is $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above $M$. What is the gauge pressure at $N$?

- $41.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $20.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $38.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $82.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

By the **hydrostatic pressure law**, piezometric head is constant throughout a connected body of liquid at rest. An increase in elevation of $\Delta z$ reduces pressure by $\gamma\,\Delta z$. Point $N$ is $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above $M$, giving
$$
p_N = p_M - \gamma\,\Delta z = 62 - 8.50 \cdot 2.40 = 62 - 20.4 = 41.6\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Use the specific weight of the specified liquid. Substituting the specific weight of water would give $38.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
