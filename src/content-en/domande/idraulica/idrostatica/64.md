A straight tube inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal is full of water at rest up to its upper end, which is open to the atmosphere. What is the gauge pressure at a point $12.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the upper end, measured along the tube axis?

- $118\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $102\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $58.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

By the hydrostatic pressure law, pressure depends on the vertical depth below the free surface. Along an axis inclined at $30^\circ$, a distance $12.0\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds to the elevation change
$$
h = 12.0 \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 6.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
giving
$$
p = 9.81 \cdot 6.00 = 58.9\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Taking the full distance along the axis as the depth would give $118\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Projecting it with the cosine would give $102\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
