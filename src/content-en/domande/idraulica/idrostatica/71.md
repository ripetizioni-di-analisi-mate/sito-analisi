The two tanks shown communicate through an opening at the bottom and contain still water. The left tank is open, with its water surface $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. The right tank is closed, with air above its water surface at height $1.90\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. What is the gauge pressure of this air?

- $18.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $31.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $-12.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $12.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The two tanks contain one connected body of water at rest with a single hydrostatic head plane at the free surface in the open tank. The free surface in the closed tank lies $3.20 - 1.90 = 1.30\,\mathrm{m}$ below that plane. Its gauge pressure, equal to that of the air immediately above it, is therefore
$$
p = 9.81 \cdot 1.30 = 12.8\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The gauge pressure is positive because the closed tank’s free surface is below the hydrostatic head plane. If the air pressure were below atmospheric, the level in the closed tank would be higher than in the open one.
