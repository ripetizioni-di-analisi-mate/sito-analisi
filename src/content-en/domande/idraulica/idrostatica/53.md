A rheometer measures the shear stress $\tau$ in fluids $A$ and $B$ at three velocity gradients $\dfrac{dv}{dy}$, namely $50$, $100$, and $200\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Fluid $A$ gives $\tau = 0.60$, $1.20$, and $2.40\,\mathrm{Pa}$, while fluid $B$ gives $\tau = 0.60$, $0.90$, and $1.30\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Which statement is correct?

- $A$ is Newtonian, with $\mu = 0.012\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- $B$ is Newtonian, with $\mu = 0.012\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- $A$ is Newtonian, with $\mu = 83.3\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- Both are Newtonian because shear stress increases with velocity gradient

## Explanation

A Newtonian fluid obeys **Newton’s law of viscosity** $\tau = \mu\,\dfrac{dv}{dy}$ with constant $\mu$. The ratio of shear stress to velocity gradient must therefore be the same for every measurement. For fluid $A$,
$$
\frac{0.60}{50} = \frac{1.20}{100} = \frac{2.40}{200} = 0.012\,\mathrm{Pa\,s} ,
$$
so $A$ is Newtonian, with dynamic viscosity $0.012\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$. For fluid $B$ the ratios $0.012$, $0.009$, and $0.0065\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ decrease as the gradient increases. Thus $B$ is non-Newtonian even though its shear stress increases with the gradient. The value $83.3$ is the reciprocal ratio, velocity gradient divided by shear stress.
