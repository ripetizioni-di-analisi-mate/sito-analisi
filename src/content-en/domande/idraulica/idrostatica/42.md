An open tank contains gasoline of specific gravity $0.74$ and depth $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above water of depth $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the gauge pressure at the interface between the two liquids?

- $8.71\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $11.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $15.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $26.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Gasoline is less dense than water, so it floats above the water with a horizontal interface. Applying the hydrostatic pressure law to the gasoline layer with its specific weight $\gamma_b = 0.74 \cdot 9.81 = 7.26\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ gives
$$
p_i = 7.26 \cdot 1.20 = 8.71\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The water below the interface does not enter the calculation because the interface pressure is set by the liquid above it. Treating the gasoline as water would give $11.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$, an overestimate.
