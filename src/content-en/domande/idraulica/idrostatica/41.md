A mercury barometer at a mountain location has a column height $742\,\mathrm{mm}$. Given $\gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}} = 133.4\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$, what is the absolute atmospheric pressure there?

- $7.28\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $9.90\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $99.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Only mercury vapor at negligible pressure remains above the meniscus in the tube. The **hydrostatic pressure law** applied to the mercury gives $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = \gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}}\,h$. With $h = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}$, this yields
$$
p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 133.4 \cdot 0.742 = 99.0\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Use the specific weight of mercury. The specific weight of water would give $7.28\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The calculated pressure is below the sea-level value $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$, as expected at elevation.
