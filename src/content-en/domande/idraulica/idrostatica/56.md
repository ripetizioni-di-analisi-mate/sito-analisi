Two parallel glass plates with a gap of $b = 0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$ are immersed vertically in water. Surface tension is $\sigma = 0.0728\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the contact angle is zero. Neglecting edge effects, how far does water rise between the plates?

- $59.4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $14.8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $119\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $29.7\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Consider a length $\ell$ of the parallel plates. Surface tension acts along two contact lines, one on each plate, giving a total upward force $2\,\sigma\,\ell$. The raised liquid has volume $b\,\ell\,h$. Equating its weight to the upward force gives
$$
\gamma\,b\,\ell\,h = 2\,\sigma\,\ell ,
\qquad\text{which gives}\qquad
h = \frac{2\,\sigma}{\gamma\,b} = \frac{2 \cdot 0.0728}{9810 \cdot 0.00050} = 0.0297\,\mathrm{m} = 29.7\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
Jurin’s circular-tube formula with $D = b$ would give twice this height, $59.4\,\mathrm{mm}$. For the same rise, the contact-line length per unit volume of lifted liquid between the plates is half that in a tube of diameter $b$.
