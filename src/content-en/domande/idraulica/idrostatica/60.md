A tank full of water accelerates horizontally forward at $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The liquid is at rest relative to the tank. In which direction does pressure increase most rapidly?

- Vertically downward, as in a stationary tank
- Downward and toward the front wall, at $11.5^\circ$ to the vertical
- Downward and toward the rear wall, at $78.5^\circ$ to the vertical
- Downward and toward the rear wall, at $11.5^\circ$ to the vertical

## Explanation

The **differential equation of hydrostatic equilibrium** gives $\operatorname{grad} p = \rho\,\vec{f}$. The pressure gradient points in the direction of steepest pressure increase. In the frame attached to the tank, the effective body force per unit mass combines gravity and the inertial force. With $x$ forward and $z$ upward, it is $\vec{f} = (-a,\,-g)$. The vector $\vec{f}$ points downward and toward the rear wall, making an angle with the vertical of
$$
\arctan\frac{a}{g} = \arctan\frac{2.0}{9.81} = 11.5^\circ .
$$
The value $78.5^\circ$ is the angle from the horizontal. Constant-pressure surfaces are perpendicular to the pressure gradient, so the free surface slopes at $11.5^\circ$ and falls toward the front wall.
