A diver descends in seawater of specific gravity $1.03$. The atmospheric pressure at the surface is $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At what depth is the absolute pressure three times its value at the surface?

- $30.1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20.1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.52\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By the hydrostatic pressure law, the absolute pressure at depth $h$ is $p_{\mathrm{atm}} + \gamma\,h$. Three times the surface pressure is $3\,p_{\mathrm{atm}}$. Equality therefore requires $\gamma\,h = 2\,p_{\mathrm{atm}}$, a gauge pressure of two atmospheres. Seawater has specific weight $1.03 \cdot 9.81 = 10.10\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$, giving
$$
h = \frac{2 \cdot 101.3}{10.10} = 20.1\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Setting the gauge pressure equal to three atmospheres would give $30.1\,\mathrm{m}$. The depth $10.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is where the absolute pressure doubles.
