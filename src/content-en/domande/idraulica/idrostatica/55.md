A solid cylinder of weight $W = 40\,\mathrm{N}$, diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$, and length $L = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ descends vertically inside a coaxial tube open at both ends. The radial clearance $s = 0.10\,\mathrm{mm}$ is filled with oil of dynamic viscosity $\mu = 0.50\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$. Neglecting end pressure forces, what is the terminal speed of the cylinder?

- $1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.637\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.318\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

At terminal speed $V$, the weight is balanced by the viscous force on the cylinder’s lateral surface. The clearance is small. By **Newton’s law of viscosity**, the shear stress is $\mu\,\dfrac{V}{s}$ and the force is $\mu\,\dfrac{V}{s}\,\pi D L$. Force equilibrium gives
$$
V = \frac{W\,s}{\mu\,\pi D L} = \frac{40 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}}{0.50 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.050 \cdot 0.080} = 0.637\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The lateral area is $\pi D L$. Using $\pi R L$ would double the result to $1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$, while using half the radial clearance as the gap thickness would give $0.318\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
