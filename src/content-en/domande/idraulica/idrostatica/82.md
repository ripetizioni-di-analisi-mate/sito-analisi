The bubbler shown measures the water level in a well. A vertical tube ends $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. Air is fed slowly into it, and bubbles emerge from the tip when its gauge reads $41.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting the weight of air in the tube, what is the water depth?

::schema{acqua="0,0 3.4,0 3.4,4.7 0,4.7" solidi="-0.4,-0.4 3.8,-0.4 3.8,0 -0.4,0;-0.4,0 0,0 0,6 -0.4,6;3.4,0 3.8,0 3.8,6 3.4,6" linee="1.15,1 1.15,6.6 2.2,6.6;1.25,1 1.25,6.5 2.2,6.5" cerchi="1.2,0.8,0.07,foro;1.32,0.55,0.06,foro;1.22,0.3,0.05,foro" pelo="0,3.4,4.7" quote="1.25 0,1.25 1,,0.5" frecce="4.4,6.55,2.3,6.55,air" etichette="1.8,7.05,p = 41.2 kPa;0.55,2.6,water;2.5,0.42,0.50 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Measuring the water level in a well with a bubbler"}

- $4.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.70\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.70\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.420\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Air bubbles leave the tube when the air pressure equals the water pressure at the tube tip. The weight of the air column is negligible, so the gauge reads the water pressure at that point. By the hydrostatic pressure law, the tip lies below the free surface at a depth
$$
h = \frac{41.2}{9.81} = 4.20\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The tip is $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom, so the total water depth in the well is $4.20 + 0.50 = 4.70\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $4.20\,\mathrm{m}$ gives only the depth of the tip. Its height above the bottom must be added.
