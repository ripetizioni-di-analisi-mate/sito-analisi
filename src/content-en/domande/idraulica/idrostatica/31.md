In a closed tank the bottom is at elevation $0$, the water surface is at $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$, and the air above the water is at gauge pressure $12\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At what elevation on the vertical wall is the gauge pressure $25\,\mathrm{kPa}$?

- $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.55\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.82\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The air pressure corresponds to a water head $\dfrac{12}{9.81} = 1.22\,\mathrm{m}$, placing the hydrostatic head plane at elevation $2.60 + 1.22 = 3.82\,\mathrm{m}$. The gauge pressure at a point is $p = \gamma\,(h - z)$, so the required point lies below the plane by
$$
h - z = \frac{25}{9.81} = 2.55\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The point’s elevation is thus $3.82 - 2.55 = 1.27\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting the air pressure would give elevation $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$, nearly at the bottom.
