In a salt lake of depth $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, the specific weight of the water increases linearly from $10.0\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ at the surface to $12.4\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ at the bottom. What is the bottom gauge pressure?

- $62.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $56.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $50.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $112\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The specific weight varies with depth, so the hydrostatic relation $p = \gamma\,h$, which requires constant $\gamma$, cannot be used directly. Integrate the **differential equation of hydrostatic equilibrium** in the vertical direction. Taking $y$ downward from the free surface gives $\dfrac{dp}{dy} = \gamma(y)$ with $\gamma(y) = 10.0 + 0.48\,y$, hence
$$
p = \int_0^{5} (10.0 + 0.48\,y)\,dy = 50.0 + 0.24 \cdot 25 = 56.0\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
This is the depth multiplied by the mean specific weight $11.2\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$. Using the bottom specific weight would give $62.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and using the surface specific weight would give $50.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
