Water drops slowly form at the tip of a vertical dropper. Surface tension is $\sigma = 0.0728\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the tip diameter is $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. A drop remains attached until its weight exceeds the vertical surface-tension force around the contact line. What is its mass at detachment?

- $23.3\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $46.6\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $93.3\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $457\,\mathrm{mg}$

## Explanation

Surface tension is a force per unit length of contact line. The contact line runs around the outlet rim, a length $\pi d$. With the surface-tension force acting vertically, the maximum force supporting the drop is
$$
F = \sigma\,\pi d = 0.0728 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.0020 = 4.57 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
At detachment the drop’s weight equals this force, so its mass is
$$
m = \frac{F}{g} = \frac{4.57 \cdot 10^{-4}}{9.81} = 4.66 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{kg} = 46.6\,\mathrm{mg} .
$$
Using the radius in place of the diameter would give $23.3\,\mathrm{mg}$, and counting the contact line twice would give $93.3\,\mathrm{mg}$. The value $457\,\mathrm{mg}$ comes from omitting division by $g$, effectively reading a force in newtons as a mass in kilograms.
