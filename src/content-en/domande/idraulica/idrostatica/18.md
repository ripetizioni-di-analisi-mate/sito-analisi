Water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ has kinematic viscosity $\nu = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ and density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. What is its dynamic viscosity?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- $9.81 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$

## Explanation

Kinematic viscosity is dynamic viscosity divided by density, $\nu = \dfrac{\mu}{\rho}$. Rearranging gives
$$
\mu = \rho\,\nu = 1000 \cdot 10^{-6} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s} .
$$
Dynamic viscosity is measured in $\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ and kinematic viscosity in $\mathrm{m^2/s}$. Their numerical values therefore cannot be equated. Multiplying by the specific weight $\gamma = 9810\,\mathrm{N/m^3}$ would give $9.81 \cdot 10^{-3}$, which has the wrong dimensions for dynamic viscosity.
