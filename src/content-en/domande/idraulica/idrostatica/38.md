A flat tank wall is inclined at $40^\circ$ to the horizontal and holds back water at rest. In what direction does the water exert a force on a small element of that wall?

- Vertically downward, because the force comes from the weight of the water above
- Perpendicular to the element and directed into it
- Parallel to the wall and directed toward the bottom of the tank
- With a tangential component that increases as the wall tilt increases

## Explanation

The water is at rest and the velocity gradient is zero. By **Newton’s law of viscosity**, the shear stress on every surface element is therefore zero. The water force on the element is purely normal, directed into the surface. Its magnitude is $p\,\Delta A$. The pressure $p$ follows from the hydrostatic pressure law and, by the **isotropy of pressure**, has the same value for every orientation. Tilting the wall changes the force direction, while the pressure at the point depends only on depth.
