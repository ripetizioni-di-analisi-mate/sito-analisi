Two rigid containers hold equal initial volumes of liquid, one water with $\varepsilon = 2.1 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and the other oil with $\varepsilon = 1.4 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Which liquid undergoes the greater decrease in volume for the same pressure rise?

- Water, because it has the larger bulk modulus
- Both volumes decrease equally because the pressure rise is the same
- Water, because it has the higher density
- Oil, because it has the smaller bulk modulus

## Explanation

The fractional volume decrease is $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V} = -\dfrac{\Delta p}{\varepsilon}$. For a given $\Delta p$ it is inversely proportional to the bulk modulus. Oil has the smaller bulk modulus, so its volume decreases more. For $\Delta p = 10\,\mathrm{MPa}$, the fractional decrease is $4.76 \cdot 10^{-3}$ for water and $7.14 \cdot 10^{-3}$ for oil. Density does not enter this relation and therefore does not determine which liquid compresses more.
