A differential manometer containing mercury of specific gravity $\delta = 13.6$ connects two separate pipes containing water at rest, as shown. The centerline of pipe $A$ is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above that of $B$. The mercury interface on the $B$ side is $\Delta = 0.28\,\mathrm{m}$ higher than on the $A$ side. What is the piezometric head difference $h_A - h_B$?

- $0.280\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.81\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.53\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.53\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The **differential manometer** gives the difference in piezometric head through $h_A - h_B = \Delta\,(\delta - 1)$, where $\Delta$ is the difference in meniscus elevations. Substituting the data gives
$$
h_A - h_B = 0.28 \cdot (13.6 - 1) = 0.28 \cdot 12.6 = 3.53\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The manometer fluid replaces an equal volume of water, so the relevant factor is $\delta - 1$ instead of $\delta$. Using $13.6$ in place of $12.6$ would give $3.81\,\mathrm{m}$. The centerlines differ in elevation by $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, but their elevations cancel in this calculation.
