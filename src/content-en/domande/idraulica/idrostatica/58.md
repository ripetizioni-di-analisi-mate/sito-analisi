A spherical water drop has diameter $D = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The water–air surface tension is $\sigma = 0.0728\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Use equilibrium of half the drop to find the pressure difference between its interior and the surrounding air.

- $291\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $146\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $582\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $72.8\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Cut the drop through its center and consider one hemisphere. The pressure difference $\Delta p$ acts across the circular cut of area $\dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$, tending to separate the hemispheres. Surface tension acts along the circumference $\pi D$ and holds them together. Force equilibrium gives
$$
\Delta p\,\frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \sigma\,\pi D ,
\qquad\text{which gives}\qquad
\Delta p = \frac{4\,\sigma}{D} = \frac{4 \cdot 0.0728}{0.0010} = 291\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
Replacing the diameter by the radius in the final expression would double the result to $582\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The excess pressure varies inversely with diameter, so its effect is appreciable for small drops and bubbles, typically on the millimeter scale.
