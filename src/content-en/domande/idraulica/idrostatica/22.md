Mercury does not wet glass and has contact angle $\theta \simeq 130^\circ$. What happens when a narrow glass tube is dipped into a basin of mercury?

- Mercury rises higher in the tube than water would because its surface tension is greater
- The mercury level in the tube falls below the outside free surface because $\cos\theta$ is negative
- Mercury stays level with the outside free surface because it does not wet the glass
- Mercury rises less in the tube than water would because its specific weight is greater

## Explanation

By **Jurin’s law**, the capillary rise is $h = \dfrac{4\,\sigma\cos\theta}{\gamma\,D}$ and its sign is set by $\cos\theta$. At $\theta \simeq 130^\circ$ the cosine is negative, so $h$ is negative and the liquid level in the tube lies below the free surface in the basin. The meniscus is convex. Water on clean glass forms a concave meniscus, with $\theta = 0$ and a rise in level.
