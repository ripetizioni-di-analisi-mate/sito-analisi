The pipe in the figure has centerline elevation $z_A = 0$ and is connected to an open-tube manometer containing mercury of specific gravity $13.6$. The water–mercury interface is at elevation $-0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and the mercury level in the open limb is at $+0.65\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the gauge pressure at point $A$?

- $86.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $111\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $115\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $8.34\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Follow the tube from $A$ to the mercury surface in the open arm, where gauge pressure is zero. Descending through water from $A$ to the interface increases pressure by $\gamma \cdot 0.20$. Ascending through mercury from that interface to the free surface decreases it by $\gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}} \cdot 0.85$. Therefore
$$
p_A + 9.81 \cdot 0.20 = 13.6 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.85 = 113.4\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
This gives
$$
p_A = 113.4 - 1.96 = 111\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The mercury level difference is the vertical separation $0.85\,\mathrm{m}$ between the two menisci. Using the single elevation $0.65\,\mathrm{m}$ would give $86.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
