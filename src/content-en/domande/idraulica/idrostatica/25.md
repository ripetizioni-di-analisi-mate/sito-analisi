Two open tanks contain water to the same depth $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$. One is cylindrical with diameter $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, the other has a square base of side $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the gauge pressure at the bottom of each?

- $44.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$ in the cylindrical tank and a higher value in the square tank
- A value proportional to the volume of water in each tank
- $44.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$ in the square tank and a lower value in the cylindrical tank
- $44.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$ in both tanks

## Explanation

By the **hydrostatic pressure law**, gauge pressure at a point equals the specific weight of the liquid times its depth below the hydrostatic head plane, giving
$$
p = \gamma\,h = 9.81 \cdot 4.50 = 44.1\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
in both tanks. The relation is independent of the vessel shape and liquid volume, so the bottom pressure is the same. The total bottom forces differ because force equals pressure times area. The force is $139\,\mathrm{kN}$ in the cylindrical tank and $1590\,\mathrm{kN}$ in the prismatic tank.
