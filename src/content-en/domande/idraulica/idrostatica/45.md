An open cylindrical container of radius $R$ rotates at constant angular velocity $\omega$ about its vertical axis. The water rotates as a rigid body. Where is the free surface relative to its original level?

- The entire free surface rises by $\dfrac{\omega^2 R^2}{4 g}$
- The rim rises by $\dfrac{\omega^2 R^2}{2 g}$ and the vertex remains at its original level
- The vertex drops by $\dfrac{\omega^2 R^2}{2 g}$ and the rim rises by the same amount
- The vertex drops by $\dfrac{\omega^2 R^2}{4 g}$ and the rim rises by the same amount

## Explanation

The free surface is the paraboloid of revolution $z = z_v + \dfrac{\omega^2 r^2}{2 g}$, so the level at the rim exceeds that at the vertex by $\dfrac{\omega^2 R^2}{2 g}$. Liquid volume is conserved, and the mean value of $r^2$ over a disk of radius $R$ is $\dfrac{R^2}{2}$. The mean surface elevation therefore stays at the initial level. The vertex falls by $\dfrac{\omega^2 R^2}{4 g}$ and the rim rises by the same amount. With $R = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and $n = 100\,\mathrm{rpm}$, each displacement has magnitude $0.252\,\mathrm{m}$.
