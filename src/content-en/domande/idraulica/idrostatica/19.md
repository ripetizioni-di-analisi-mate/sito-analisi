Between a fixed flat plate and a parallel plate moving at constant velocity $U$ there are two superposed layers of different, immiscible oils, both of thickness $s$. The oil in contact with the fixed plate has dynamic viscosity $2\mu$, the one in contact with the moving plate has dynamic viscosity $\mu$, and in each layer the velocity varies linearly with the distance from the fixed plate. What is the velocity at the interface between the two oils?

- $\dfrac{U}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2U}{3}$
- $\dfrac{U}{3}$
- $\dfrac{U}{4}$

## Explanation

Let $u$ be the velocity at the interface, so in the lower layer the velocity goes from $0$ to $u$ and in the upper layer from $u$ to $U$. The interface is in equilibrium, therefore the shear stress exerted on it by the upper layer equals the one exerted by the lower layer. By **Newton's law of viscosity** we then have
$$
2\mu\,\frac{u}{s} = \mu\,\frac{U - u}{s} ,
$$
that is, $2u = U - u$, hence $u = \dfrac{U}{3}$. The more viscous layer has the smaller velocity gradient, so across it the velocity increases by only one third of $U$. The value $\dfrac{U}{2}$ would hold for two oils of equal viscosity, and $\dfrac{2U}{3}$ comes from swapping the viscosities of the two layers.
