A flat plate of area $A = 0.40\,\mathrm{m^2}$ moves at constant speed $V = 1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over an oil film of thickness $s = 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu = 0.25\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$. How much power is dissipated?

- $0.072\,\mathrm{W}$
- $36.0\,\mathrm{W}$
- $60.0\,\mathrm{W}$
- $72.0\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

Across the film, velocity varies linearly from $0$ to $V$, giving a constant gradient $\dfrac{V}{s}$. **Newton’s law of viscosity** then gives the shear stress and the force:
$$
\tau = \mu\,\frac{V}{s} = 0.25 \cdot \frac{1.20}{0.0020} = 150\,\mathrm{Pa} ,
\qquad
F = \tau\,A = 150 \cdot 0.40 = 60.0\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The plate moves at speed $V$ under a force $F$ acting in the direction of motion. The dissipated power is therefore
$$
P = F\,V = 60.0 \cdot 1.20 = 72.0\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
Express the film thickness in meters. Using $s = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ would give a power smaller by a factor of one thousand.
