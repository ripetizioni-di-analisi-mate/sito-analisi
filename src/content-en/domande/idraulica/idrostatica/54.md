A flat disk of radius $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ rotates at angular velocity $\omega = 50\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ above a fixed plate, separated from it by an oil film of uniform thickness $s = 0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu = 0.20\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$. What resisting torque does the oil exert?

- $6.28\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $3.14\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $4.19\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $1.57\,\mathrm{N\,m}$

## Explanation

A point on the disk at radius $r$ has speed $\omega r$. By **Newton’s law of viscosity**, the shear stress is $\tau = \mu\,\dfrac{\omega r}{s}$ and increases linearly with $r$. The annular element at radius $r$ with thickness $dr$ has area $2\pi r\,dr$ and moment arm $r$. Integrating over the disk gives the torque,
$$
M = \int_0^R \mu\,\frac{\omega r}{s}\,2\pi r^2\,dr = \frac{\pi\,\mu\,\omega R^4}{2 s} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.20 \cdot 50 \cdot 0.10^4}{2 \cdot 0.00050} = 3.14\,\mathrm{N\,m} .
$$
The value $6.28\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ comes from applying the rim stress over the entire disk with moment arm $R$. Both stress and moment arm increase with $r$, so their contributions must be integrated over the disk.
