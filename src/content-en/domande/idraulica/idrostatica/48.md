A valve closes suddenly at the downstream end of a reservoir-fed water pipe of length $2900\,\mathrm{m}$. Given $\varepsilon = 2.1 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ for water, and neglecting deformation of the pipe, how long does the pressure disturbance take to travel to the reservoir and back?

- $4.00\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{s}$
- $12.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $8.00\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

A small pressure disturbance travels through water at the wave speed
$$
c = \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{2.1 \cdot 10^{9}}{1000}} = 1449\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The disturbance travels the length of the pipe twice, from the valve to the reservoir and back. The elapsed time is therefore
$$
t = \frac{2L}{c} = \frac{2 \cdot 2900}{1449} = 4.00\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $2.00\,\mathrm{s}$ is the one-way travel time. The value $12.5\,\mathrm{s}$ results from using specific weight in place of density under the square root. In water-hammer analysis, the *round-trip wave travel time* $\dfrac{2L}{c}$ is compared with the valve closure time.
