A container of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is taken to the Moon, where the gravitational acceleration is $1.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What are the density and specific weight of the water there?

- $\rho = 165\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $\gamma = 1.62\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$
- $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$
- $\rho = 165\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$
- $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $\gamma = 1.62\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$

## Explanation

Density is mass divided by volume, neither of which depends on gravitational acceleration here. Its value on the Moon remains $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Specific weight is density multiplied by $g$, so
$$
\gamma = \rho\,g = 1000 \cdot 1.62 = 1620\,\mathrm{N/m^3} = 1.62\,\mathrm{kN/m^3} .
$$
At the same depth, hydrostatic pressure on the Moon is therefore about one-sixth of that on Earth. The value $165\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ would follow from also scaling density by the gravity ratio, which scales weight alone.
