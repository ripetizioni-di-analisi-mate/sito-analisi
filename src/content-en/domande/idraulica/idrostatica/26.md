At point $P$ of elevation $15.20\,\mathrm{m}$ in water at rest, the gauge pressure is $78\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the elevation of the hydrostatic head plane?

- $7.95\,\mathrm{m}$
- $23.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.25\,\mathrm{m}$
- $93.2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The **hydrostatic head plane** is the horizontal plane whose elevation equals the liquid’s piezometric head, $h = z + \dfrac{p}{\gamma}$. Substituting the data at point $P$ gives
$$
h = 15.20 + \frac{78}{9.81} = 15.20 + 7.95 = 23.2\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pressure head $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = 7.95\,\mathrm{m}$ is the vertical distance from the point to that plane and must be added to the point’s elevation. Water in a piezometer connected at $P$ rises to elevation $23.2\,\mathrm{m}$.
