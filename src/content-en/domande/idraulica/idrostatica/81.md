A cylindrical diving bell of height $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is open at the bottom and lowered vertically into a lake. At the surface it contains only air at atmospheric pressure $p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The air compresses isothermally, so its absolute pressure times its volume is constant. When the water surface inside is $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ below the lake surface, what height does the air occupy?

- $0.681\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.32\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.67\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The trapped air in the diving bell has the same pressure as the water at the internal free surface. The hydrostatic pressure law gives the absolute pressure there as
$$
p_2 = p_{\mathrm{atm}} + \gamma\,h = 101.3 + 9.81 \cdot 20.0 = 297.5\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
For the isothermal gas, the pressure–volume product is constant when absolute pressure is used, with zero pressure defined by a vacuum. The bell is cylindrical, so air volume is proportional to air-column height. Thus
$$
h_{\mathrm{air}} = 2.00 \cdot \frac{101.3}{297.5} = 0.681\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $1.32\,\mathrm{m}$ is the height of the water that has entered. The value $1.67\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from calculating $\gamma\,h$ without the factor $g$, while $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ would treat the air as incompressible.
