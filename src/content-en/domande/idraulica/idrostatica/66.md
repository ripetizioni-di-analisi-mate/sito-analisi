The hydraulic press shown contains oil of specific gravity $0.90$. A force $F_1 = 200\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on its small piston of diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$. The large piston has diameter $D = 500\,\mathrm{mm}$, and its underside is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the underside of the small piston. Neglecting piston weights and friction, what force on the large piston maintains equilibrium?

::schema{acqua="0,-1 4.5,-1 4.5,2 2.5,2 2.5,-0.5 0.5,-0.5 0.5,0 0,0" solidi="0,0 0.5,0 0.5,0.2 0,0.2;2.5,2 4.5,2 4.5,2.25 2.5,2.25" linee="0,0.7 0,-1 4.5,-1 4.5,2.7;0.5,0.7 0.5,-0.5 2.5,-0.5 2.5,2.7" tratteggi="0.5,0 5.4,0" forze="0.25 0.2,0,-0.9,F_1" quote="4.9 0,4.9 2,2.00 m,0.3" etichette="3.5,0.6,oil δ = 0.90;-0.75,0.1,d = 50 mm;3.5,2.6,D = 500 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Hydraulic press with the face of the larger piston $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the face of the smaller piston"}

- $20.0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $23.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $16.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The pressure beneath the small piston is
$$
p_1 = \frac{F_1}{\dfrac{\pi d^2}{4}} = \frac{200}{\dfrac{\pi \cdot 0.050^2}{4}} = 101.9\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The larger piston is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ higher in the same body of oil. By the hydrostatic pressure law, the pressure beneath it is lower and equals
$$
p_2 = 101.9 - 0.90 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.00 = 84.2\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The force on the large piston is $p_2$ multiplied by its area, giving
$$
F_2 = 84.2 \cdot \frac{\pi \cdot 0.500^2}{4} = 16.5\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
Neglecting the elevation difference would give $20.0\,\mathrm{kN}$, the force for two pistons at the same elevation.
