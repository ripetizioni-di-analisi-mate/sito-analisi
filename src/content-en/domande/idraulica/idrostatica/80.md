A horizontal pipe is full of water at rest. At its highest point the gauge pressure is $-20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. A tap is opened there to install a piezometer, a vertical tube open to the atmosphere. What happens?

- Water rises in the piezometer to $2.04\,\mathrm{m}$ above the tap
- Water stops at the tap elevation, indicating zero gauge pressure
- Water stays below the tap, and air enters the pipe through the tap
- Water rises in the piezometer to $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$ above the tap

## Explanation

Water in a piezometer rises to the hydrostatic head plane. Its signed vertical displacement from the tap is
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = \frac{-20}{9.81} = -2.04\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
or $2.04\,\mathrm{m}$ *below* the tap. The required level would lie below the tap at the top of the full pipe. Atmospheric pressure at the open connection exceeds the pressure of the water, so air enters the pipe. Negative gauge pressures are therefore measured with a liquid-column manometer or a mechanical vacuum gauge. An open piezometer of this kind is suitable for positive gauge pressures.
