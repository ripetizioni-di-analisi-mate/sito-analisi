A closed water-filled barrel of height $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has a horizontal lid of area $0.50\,\mathrm{m^2}$. A vertical tube of diameter $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ passes through the lid and holds water to height $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above it. What force does the water exert on the lid?

- $39.2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $44.1\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $6.16\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The tube and barrel contain one connected body of water at rest, so the hydrostatic head plane is at the free surface in the tube, $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the lid. The hydrostatic pressure law gives the same gauge pressure $9.81 \cdot 8.00 = 78.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$ everywhere on the underside of the lid. The resulting upward force is
$$
F = 78.5 \cdot 0.50 = 39.2\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The water in the tube weighs only $6.16\,\mathrm{N}$. The lid force is approximately six thousand times as large because the pressure depends only on the vertical distance below the head plane. This is the *hydrostatic paradox*. The value $44.1\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the force on an equal area at the barrel bottom.
