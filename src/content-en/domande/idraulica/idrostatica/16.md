A rigid pipe section holds $0.80\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of water. Its pressure is raised by $\Delta p = 12\,\mathrm{MPa}$ for a pressure test. Taking $\varepsilon = 2.1 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ for water, what volume of additional water must be injected?

- $4.57\,\mathrm{L}$
- $5.71\,\mathrm{L}$
- $7.14\,\mathrm{L}$
- $0.457\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

The bulk modulus of water gives the fractional volume decrease,
$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V} = -\frac{\Delta p}{\varepsilon} = -\frac{12 \cdot 10^{6}}{2.1 \cdot 10^{9}} = -5.71 \cdot 10^{-3} .
$$
The pipe is rigid and already full, so the volume of water to be injected equals the decrease in volume of the water already present,
$$
|\Delta V| = 5.71 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 0.80 = 4.57 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 4.57\,\mathrm{L} .
$$
The fractional decrease must be multiplied by the water volume. The value $5.71\,\mathrm{L}$ would apply to a pipe containing $1\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of water.
