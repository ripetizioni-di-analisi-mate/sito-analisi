A mercury barometer is read at ground level and at the top of a building of height $300\,\mathrm{m}$. Take constant densities $\rho = 1.20\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ for air and $\gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}} = 133.4\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ for mercury. How does the reading change at the top?

- It increases by $26.5\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It decreases by $360\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It decreases by $2.70\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It decreases by $26.5\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Air at rest has weight and obeys the hydrostatic pressure law. Assuming constant density, rising by $300\,\mathrm{m}$ decreases atmospheric pressure by
$$
\Delta p = \rho\,g\,\Delta z = 1.20 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 300 = 3532\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
In a mercury barometer this pressure decrease shortens the column by
$$
\Delta h = \frac{3532}{133\,400} = 0.0265\,\mathrm{m} = 26.5\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
Dividing by the specific weight of water would give $360\,\mathrm{mm}$, the change in a water barometer. Omitting $g$ in the calculation of $\Delta p$ would give $2.70\,\mathrm{mm}$.
