A radial gate with a cylindrical upstream face controls a horizontal rectangular channel. Apply momentum between an upstream section and the downstream vena contracta, neglecting bed resistance. What does the momentum equation in the flow direction determine?

- The total force on the gate, including its vertical components
- The horizontal fluid force on the gate, given by the same expression as for a flat gate
- Nothing, because pressure is not hydrostatic on the curved gate face
- The vertical force component, equal to the weight of the overlying volume of water

## Explanation

The integral momentum equation involves the flow-section quantities and the resultant boundary force $\vec{R}$. It determines this resultant without requiring the pressure distribution over the gate itself. At both flow sections the streamlines are straight and parallel, so the section pressure forces and momentum fluxes are independent of the gate shape. With bed resistance neglected, the gate exerts the only horizontal boundary force. The streamwise equation therefore gives its horizontal load as $S = \dfrac{\gamma b h_1^2}{2} - \dfrac{\gamma b h_2^2}{2} - \rho Q (v_2 - v_1)$, the same expression as for a flat gate. The vertical component remains undetermined because the vertical equation includes the unknown bed reaction.
