A water jet of diameter $d = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ meets the edge of a fixed, symmetrical wedge. It divides equally, with each half deflected through $60^\circ$ to one side of the jet axis. Neglecting friction and weight, what force does the jet exert on the wedge?

- $283\,\mathrm{N}$
- $245\,\mathrm{N}$
- $70.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $141\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The area $A = 1.257 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ gives $Q = A v = 18.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and the jet momentum flux is $\rho Q v = 283\,\mathrm{N}$. The jet is free, and friction is neglected. Each half therefore leaves at speed $v$ at angle $60^\circ$ to the jet axis. By symmetry, their transverse components cancel. The momentum balance along the jet axis gives
$$
R_x = 2 \cdot \rho\,\frac{Q}{2}\,v \cos 60^\circ - \rho\,Q\,v = -\rho\,Q\,v\,(1 - \cos 60^\circ) .
$$
The jet therefore exerts a force of $\rho Q v\,(1 - \cos 60^\circ) = 141\,\mathrm{N}$ on the wedge in the jet direction. The value $283\,\mathrm{N}$ is the force on a normal plate, which would turn the jet through $90^\circ$. The value $245\,\mathrm{N}$ uses $\sin 60^\circ$ in place of $1 - \cos 60^\circ$.
