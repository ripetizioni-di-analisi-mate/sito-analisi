The force on a horizontal reducer is calculated twice, once with axis $x$ pointing downstream and once with axis $x$ pointing upstream. What changes in the result?

- The sign of $R_x$ changes while the physical force on the reducer stays the same
- The magnitude of the force on the reducer changes
- The force on the reducer reverses and acts upstream
- Nothing changes, including the sign of $R_x$

## Explanation

Reversing the axis changes the sign of every $x$ component, including velocity, section pressure forces, and wall reaction. This multiplies the entire component equation by $-1$ and reverses the sign of $R_x$ without changing its magnitude. The physical force on the reducer has the same magnitude and still acts downstream in both calculations. State that final direction in words to make the sign convention clear.
