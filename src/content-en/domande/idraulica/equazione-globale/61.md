A horizontal fitting turns the flow through $90^\circ$ while reducing the diameter from $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, as shown. The discharge is $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the inlet gauge pressure is $p_1 = 200\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting losses, what angle does the fluid force on the fitting make with the inlet axis?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3,3,-" condotte="A-B,0.6;B-C,0.36" tratteggi="1.5,-0.55 1.5,0.55;2.62,2.2 3.38,2.2" frecce="0.1,0,0.8,0,Q" etichette="1.5,0.85,1;3.6,2.2,2;0.4,-0.9,D_1 = 250 mm;4.1,1.4,D_2 = 150 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of a reducing elbow"}

- $45.0^\circ$
- $69.4^\circ$
- $17.3^\circ$
- $20.6^\circ$

## Explanation

The areas are $A_1 = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.0177\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving $v_1 = 1.63\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 4.53\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Bernoulli’s equation applied between the two sections at the same elevation gives $p_2 = p_1 - \dfrac{\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2)}{2} = 191.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The force component along the inlet axis has magnitude $F_x = p_1 A_1 + \rho Q v_1 = 9.82 + 0.13 = 9.95\,\mathrm{kN}$ and acts in the direction of the incoming flow. The component along the outlet axis has magnitude $F_y = p_2 A_2 + \rho Q v_2 = 3.38 + 0.36 = 3.74\,\mathrm{kN}$ and acts opposite to the outgoing flow. The angle with the inlet axis is therefore
$$
\varphi = \arctan\frac{F_y}{F_x} = \arctan\frac{3.74}{9.95} = 20.6^\circ .
$$
The components differ because the outlet area is smaller, so the resultant does not lie on the angle bisector. The angle $45.0^\circ$ applies to a constant-diameter elbow under the stated conditions. The value $69.4^\circ$ is measured from the outlet axis, while $17.3^\circ$ subtracts each momentum contribution from its pressure contribution.
