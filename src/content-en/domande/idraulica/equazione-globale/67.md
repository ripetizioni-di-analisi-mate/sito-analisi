A horizontal elbow of constant diameter carries discharge $Q$ at pressure $p$ in both sections. If the flow reverses at the same discharge and pressure, how does the force on the elbow change?

- It reverses along the same line
- Its magnitude and direction stay the same
- It rotates through $90^\circ$
- Only the pressure contribution remains

## Explanation

Reversing the flow interchanges inlet and outlet and reverses both velocities. The new outlet velocity minus the new inlet velocity is $(-\vec{v}_1) - (-\vec{v}_2) = \vec{v}_2 - \vec{v}_1$, the same difference as before. The section pressure forces still point into the same control volume, so their sum is unchanged. The momentum equation therefore gives the same $\vec{R}$. The force on the elbow remains $2 K \sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}$, with $K = \rho Q v + pA$, directed outward along the angle bisector. For either flow direction the elbow must exert an inward force to turn the water.
