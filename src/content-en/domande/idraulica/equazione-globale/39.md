A free jet strikes a fixed flat plate perpendicular to its axis. If the jet speed doubles while its diameter stays the same, the force on the plate

- doubles
- stays the same
- quadruples
- increases by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The force on a plate perpendicular to a jet is $S = \rho Q v$. Since $Q = A v$, the force is $S = \rho A v^2$. At fixed area it is proportional to the square of the jet speed, so doubling the speed quadruples the force. This quadratic dependence explains why jet forces become appreciable at high speeds. For $d = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$, the force is $126\,\mathrm{N}$ at $v = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $503\,\mathrm{N}$ at $v = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
