A constant-diameter bend lying in a horizontal plane deflects the flow by $45^\circ$, and the gauge pressure is the same in the two sections. At the same discharge, the pressure in both sections rises from $200$ to $300\,\mathrm{kPa}$. How does the force exerted by the flow on the bend change?

- It stays the same
- Its magnitude decreases and its direction is unchanged
- Its magnitude increases and its direction changes
- Its magnitude increases and its direction is unchanged

## Explanation

For a constant-diameter bend with the same pressure in the two sections, the force of the flow has magnitude $F = 2K\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}$, with $K = \rho Q v + p A$, and it acts along the bisector of the angle between the two sections, toward the outside of the bend. The discharge and the diameter do not change, so the momentum flux $\rho Q v$ stays the same, while the pressure force $pA$ on each section increases with the pressure. Therefore $K$ increases, and with it the magnitude of the force. The direction of the bisector depends only on the angle $\theta$ of the bend, so the force **increases in magnitude and keeps its direction**. The difference between the pressures of the two sections remains zero, but the two pressure forces have different directions and their vector sum increases with $p$.
