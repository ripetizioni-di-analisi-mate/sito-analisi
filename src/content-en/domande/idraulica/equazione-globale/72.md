A nozzle is attached by a bolted flange to the end of a horizontal pressure pipe and discharges to the atmosphere. How are the bolts loaded?

- In compression, because the jet pushes the nozzle upstream
- In shear, because the fluid force is normal to the pipe axis
- They are unloaded because outlet gauge pressure is zero
- In tension, because the flow pushes the nozzle downstream

## Explanation

The force exerted by the water on the nozzle is $F = p_1 A_1 - \rho Q (v_2 - v_1)$. The outlet gauge pressure is zero, so there is no outlet pressure-force term. Bernoulli’s equation gives $p_1 = \dfrac{\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2)}{2}$ and continuity gives $Q = A_1 v_1$, so $p_1 A_1 = \rho Q (v_2 - v_1)\,\dfrac{v_1 + v_2}{2 v_1}$. The factor $\dfrac{v_1 + v_2}{2 v_1}$ exceeds $1$ because $v_2 > v_1$. Thus $F$ is positive, directed downstream, and tends to pull the nozzle away from the pipe. The bolts resist this force in tension.
