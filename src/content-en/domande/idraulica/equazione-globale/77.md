In a horizontal rectangular channel, water flows beneath a flat gate inclined at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal, with its upper edge farther downstream, as shown. Neglect gate shear and bed resistance. Applying momentum from an upstream section to the downstream vena contracta gives the horizontal force component $S_x = 30.0\,\mathrm{kN}$ on the gate. What is the force magnitude?

::schema{acqua="0,0 2.9,0 2.9,0.5 4.05,2.4 0,2.4;2.9,0 8,0 8,0.4 3.3,0.4 2.9,0.5" solidi="0,-0.4 8,-0.4 8,0 0,0;2.9,0.5 4.5,3.27 4.63,3.2 3.03,0.43" pelo="0,4.05,2.4,p_a;3.3,8,0.4" frecce="0.4,1.2,1.5,1.2,Q" etichette="3.75,0.75,60°" riferimento=0 titolo="Inclined flat gate in a rectangular channel"}

- $30.0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $60.0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $34.6\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $26.0\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Shear stress on the gate is neglected, so the force is due to pressure alone and acts normal to the gate face. The gate makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the horizontal, so its normal makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the horizontal and the horizontal force component is $S_x = F \cos 30^\circ$. Hence
$$
F = \frac{S_x}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{30.0}{0.866} = 34.6\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
with a downward vertical component of $F \sin 30^\circ = 17.3\,\mathrm{kN}$. The vertical momentum equation alone cannot determine this component because it also contains the unknown bed reaction. The value $30.0\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the horizontal component alone. The value $26.0\,\mathrm{kN}$ multiplies by $\cos 30^\circ$ instead of dividing, and $60.0\,\mathrm{kN}$ divides by $\cos 60^\circ$, placing the normal at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal.
