A constant-level tank discharges through a Borda mouthpiece, a short tube projecting inward. The jet separates at its edge and stays clear of the tube wall. The mouthpiece axis is $h$ below the surface, as shown. Velocities near the tank walls are negligible, so wall pressure is hydrostatic even opposite the mouthpiece. Neglecting losses, what is the jet contraction coefficient $C_c$?

## Explanation

The mouthpiece has area $A$ and the vena contracta has area $C_c A$. Bernoulli’s equation between the free surface and the vena contracta gives $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$, so the discharge is $Q = C_c A v$. The hydrostatic forces on the opposite vertical walls balance except over the area $A$ opposite the opening. The fluid therefore experiences a resultant force $\gamma h A$ toward the mouthpiece. The outgoing momentum flux is $\rho Q v = \rho\,C_c A \cdot 2 g h$, so the horizontal momentum equation gives
$$
\rho\,C_c\,A \cdot 2 g h = \gamma\,h\,A ,
$$
and, since $\gamma = \rho g$, the contraction coefficient is $C_c = \dfrac{1}{2} = 0.500$. The value $0.611$ applies to a thin sharp-edged orifice. There the velocities near the orifice are appreciable, so this hydrostatic wall-pressure argument does not apply.
