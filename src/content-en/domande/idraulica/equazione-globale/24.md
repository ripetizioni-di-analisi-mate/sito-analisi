A nozzle discharges to the atmosphere through an outlet of area $A_2 = 0.00500\,\mathrm{m^2}$, where gauge pressure is zero and absolute pressure is $101\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Absolute pressure is mistakenly used at this section without accounting for atmospheric pressure on the exterior. What is the resulting error in the force?

- $505\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $253\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1010\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The outlet discharges into the atmosphere, so the gauge pressure and hence the gauge pressure force at that section are zero. Using absolute pressure at the outlet would introduce the force
$$
\Pi_2 = 101\,\mathrm{kPa} \cdot 0.00500\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.505\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
or $505\,\mathrm{N}$. It would be unbalanced because the atmospheric force on the outside of the fitting was omitted. The error in the calculated force is therefore $505\,\mathrm{N}$. A balance using absolute pressures is valid when the atmospheric force on the entire exterior is also included.
