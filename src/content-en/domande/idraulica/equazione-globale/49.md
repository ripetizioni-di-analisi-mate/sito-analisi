A tank rests on a scale. A vertical jet enters from above with discharge $Q = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and speed $v_1 = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ where it meets the free surface. The same discharge leaves through the bottom as a vertical jet of speed $v_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The level is constant and the water in the tank weighs $400\,\mathrm{N}$. By how much does the scale reading exceed the empty tank weight?

- $560\,\mathrm{N}$
- $300\,\mathrm{N}$
- $620\,\mathrm{N}$
- $500\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Choose the water in the tank as the control volume. The inlet is where the jet enters the free surface, and the outlet is at the bottom opening. Gauge pressure vanishes at both sections, so their pressure forces vanish. With $z$ positive upward, the velocity components are $-v_1$ and $-v_2$. The vertical momentum equation gives
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_1 - v_2) = -G + R_z .
$$
It follows that $R_z = G + \rho Q (v_1 - v_2) = 400 + 1000 \cdot 0.020 \cdot 5.0 = 500\,\mathrm{N}$. This is the upward force the tank exerts on the water. By Newton’s third law the water exerts a downward force of $500\,\mathrm{N}$ on the tank. The value $560\,\mathrm{N}$ accounts only for the incoming jet momentum and omits the momentum leaving through the bottom. The value $620\,\mathrm{N}$ adds the two momentum flux magnitudes instead of taking their difference.
