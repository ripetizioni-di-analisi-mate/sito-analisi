A vertical-plane elbow of diameter $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ turns a horizontal flow vertically upward. The discharge is $Q = 200\,\mathrm{L/s}$, the outlet gauge pressure is $p_2 = 60\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the volume of water in the elbow is $W = 0.250\,\mathrm{m^3}$. What is the vertical component of the fluid force on the fitting?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3,3,-" condotte="A-B-C,0.7" tratteggi="0.9,-0.75 0.9,0.75;2.1,2.1 3.9,2.1" frecce="0.1,0,0.8,0,Q" etichette="0.7,1.05,1;4.05,2.1,2;0.2,-1.15,D = 400 mm;4.1,1,p_2 = 60 kPa" riferimento=0 titolo="Pipe elbow in a vertical plane"}

- $5.41\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $7.54\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $7.86\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $10.3\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The area is $A = 0.1257\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{Q}{A} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving $\rho Q v = 318\,\mathrm{N}$, $p_2 A = 7.54\,\mathrm{kN}$, and fluid weight $G = \gamma W = 9.81 \cdot 0.250 = 2.45\,\mathrm{kN}$. The inlet section is vertical, so its pressure force is horizontal and has no vertical component. The outlet pressure force and the fluid weight both act downward. Taking upward as positive in the vertical momentum equation gives
$$
\rho\,Q\,v = -p_2 A - G + R_z ,
$$
so $R_z = 0.318 + 7.54 + 2.45 = 10.3\,\mathrm{kN}$. The fluid therefore exerts a downward force of $10.3\,\mathrm{kN}$ on the fitting. Neglecting the weight of the contained fluid would give $7.86\,\mathrm{kN}$, an error of about one quarter. In a fitting of this volume the weight is comparable to the other forces.
