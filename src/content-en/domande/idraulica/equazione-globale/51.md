At which of the following sections is the momentum correction factor $\beta$ closest to $1$?

- In a pipe with laminar flow and a parabolic velocity profile
- Just downstream of a sudden expansion where recirculating fluid occupies part of the section
- At the outlet of a well-rounded nozzle where velocity is nearly uniform
- Between two parallel flat plates in laminar flow

## Explanation

The momentum correction factor $\beta = \dfrac{1}{v^2 A}\displaystyle\int_A u^2\,dA$ equals $1$ when the local velocity $u$ equals the mean velocity everywhere. It increases as the profile becomes less uniform. A well-contoured nozzle accelerates the flow and flattens its velocity profile. At the exit the velocity is nearly uniform, so $\beta$ is close to $1$. For laminar pipe flow the parabolic profile gives $\beta = \dfrac{4}{3}$, while laminar flow between parallel plates has a different parabolic profile and gives $\beta = \dfrac{6}{5}$. Just downstream of a sudden expansion the discharge is concentrated in only part of the section. The resulting nonuniform velocities make $\beta$ appreciably greater than $1$.
