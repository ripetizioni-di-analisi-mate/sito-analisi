A partially closed butterfly valve in a horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ carrying discharge $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$ has loss coefficient $\xi = 5.0$ based on the pipe velocity head. The diameters upstream and downstream are equal. Neglecting wall resistance between the sections, what downstream force does the fluid exert on the valve?

- $1.02\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $0.102\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2.04\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $0.509\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

With $A = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{Q}{A} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the valve head loss is $\Delta H = \xi\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 5.0 \cdot 0.212 = 1.06\,\mathrm{m}$. The diameter and elevation are constant, so Bernoulli’s equation gives $p_1 - p_2 = \gamma\,\Delta H$ and the net momentum flux is zero. The axial momentum equation reduces to $0 = (p_1 - p_2)\,A + R_x$, giving the force on the valve as
$$
F = -R_x = \gamma\,\Delta H\,A = 0.509\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
directed downstream. The valve force is therefore the pressure drop corresponding to its head loss multiplied by the pipe area. The value $1.02\,\mathrm{kN}$ uses $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ for velocity head, while $0.102\,\mathrm{kN}$ omits the loss coefficient $\xi$.
