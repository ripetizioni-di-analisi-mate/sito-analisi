A horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ ends in a horizontal $90^\circ$ elbow of the same diameter that discharges to the atmosphere, as shown. The discharge is $Q = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the elbow loss is $\xi\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ with $\xi = 0.90$. What is the magnitude of the fluid force on the elbow?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3,2.4,-" condotte="A-B-C,0.45" acqua="2.84,2.4 3.16,2.4 3.16,3.5 2.84,3.5" tratteggi="1.5,-0.45 1.5,0.45;2.65,2.4 3.35,2.4" frecce="0.1,0,0.8,0,Q;3.5,2.7,3.5,3.5,v" etichette="1.5,0.7,1;2.4,2.4,2;0.4,-0.75,D = 100 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of an outlet elbow discharging to the atmosphere"}

- $72.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $104\,\mathrm{N}$
- $50.9\,\mathrm{N}$
- $89.7\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The area is $A = 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and the speed $v = 2.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the same at both sections, so $\rho Q v = 50.9\,\mathrm{N}$. Gauge pressure vanishes at the outlet. Since the sections have the same elevation and speed, Bernoulli’s equation with the bend loss gives $\dfrac{p_1}{\gamma} = \xi\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = 0.297\,\mathrm{m}$, hence $p_1 = 2.92\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_1 A = 22.9\,\mathrm{N}$. The outlet pressure force is zero, so the components of the force on the elbow have different magnitudes. They are $F_x = p_1 A + \rho Q v = 73.9\,\mathrm{N}$ in the incoming flow direction and $F_y = \rho Q v = 50.9\,\mathrm{N}$ opposite the outgoing flow. The resultant has magnitude
$$
F = \sqrt{73.9^2 + 50.9^2} = 89.7\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $72.0\,\mathrm{N}$ neglects the upstream pressure. The value $104\,\mathrm{N}$ applies the equal-pressure elbow formula even though the inlet and outlet pressures differ.
