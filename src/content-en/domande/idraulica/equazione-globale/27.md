A nozzle discharges to the atmosphere. The pipe diameter, outlet diameter, and upstream pressure are known. Which procedure gives the force that the flange bolts must resist?

- First use Bernoulli’s equation for the discharge, then the momentum equation for the force
- First use the momentum equation for the discharge, then Bernoulli’s equation for the force
- Use only Bernoulli’s equation because it contains the pressures and speeds
- Use only the momentum equation because it contains the wall reaction

## Explanation

Bernoulli’s equation is an energy balance relating elevation, pressure, and velocity. Applied from the pipe section to the outlet, where the available head is the velocity head relative to the outlet elevation, it gives the discharge. The integral momentum equation also includes the force of the walls on the water, so it gives that force once the section velocities and pressures are known. The equations are used in sequence because each contains an unknown absent from the other. The momentum balance alone cannot determine the discharge, and the energy balance alone cannot determine the wall force.
