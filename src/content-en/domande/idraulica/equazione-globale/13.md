A horizontal pipe of discharge $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$ expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the local head loss at the expansion?

- $0.102\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.163\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.265\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The areas $A_1 = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$ give mean velocities $v_1 = 2.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 1.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Combining the momentum and energy balances for the sudden expansion gives the Borda–Carnot head loss,
$$
\Delta H = \frac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(2.55 - 1.13)^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.102\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The difference $0.265\,\mathrm{m}$ between the two velocity heads $\dfrac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{2g}$ exceeds the loss because some of that energy is recovered as a pressure-head increase of $0.163\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0.204\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from writing $\dfrac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{g}$ and omitting $2$ from the denominator.
