A fixed vane deflects a free jet. Why are the pressure forces at the flow sections zero when the integral momentum equation is applied to the control volume containing the jet and vane?

- Gauge pressure is zero at every flow section
- The inlet and outlet areas are equal
- The inlet and outlet speeds are equal
- The weight of the fluid in the control volume is neglected

## Explanation

The boundary of the free jet is in contact with the atmosphere, so gauge pressure is zero across its flow sections. The pressure force at a section is $\Pi = p_G A$, which vanishes at zero gauge pressure regardless of area. Thus only the momentum-flux terms enter the force on the vane, giving $2\rho Q v \sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}$. In a pressurized pipe the pressure term $p A$ is typically one or two orders of magnitude greater than $\rho Q v$.
