A constant-level tank discharges into the atmosphere through a well-rounded nozzle of diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$, with its horizontal axis $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface; losses are neglected. Just after leaving the nozzle, the jet strikes a fixed flat plate normal to its axis. What is the force exerted by the jet on the plate?

- $57.8\,\mathrm{N}$
- $231\,\mathrm{N}$
- $462\,\mathrm{N}$
- $116\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Bernoulli's theorem between the free surface and the outlet section, without losses, gives the jet velocity $v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.00} = 7.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The jet area is $A = 1.963 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the discharge is $Q = A v = 15.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$. On the normal plate the jet loses all of its momentum along its axis, therefore
$$
F = \rho\,Q\,v = 1000 \cdot 0.0151 \cdot 7.67 = 116\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
Substituting $v^2 = 2gh$ gives $F = 2\,\gamma h A$, that is, twice the hydrostatic force $\gamma h A = 57.8\,\mathrm{N}$ that the water would exert on a plug closing the nozzle. The value $231\,\mathrm{N}$ corresponds to a $180^\circ$ deflection of the jet, and $462\,\mathrm{N}$ comes from computing the jet area with the diameter in place of the radius.
