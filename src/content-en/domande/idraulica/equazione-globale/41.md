A water jet of discharge $Q = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ strikes a fixed, smooth, flat plate at angle $\alpha = 60^\circ$ to its plane and divides into two sheets. Neglecting friction, what is the smaller sheet discharge?

- $1.34\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $10.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $15.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $5.00\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The plate is smooth, so it exerts no force on the fluid parallel to its surface. In that direction the momentum equation gives $Q_1 - Q_2 = Q\cos\alpha$. Combining this with continuity, $Q_1 + Q_2 = Q$, gives
$$
Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2}\,(1 - \cos\alpha) = \frac{20}{2}\,(1 - 0.500) = 5.00\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
$$
while the larger sheet carries $15.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Adding the two gives the full jet discharge. The value $10.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is half that discharge and applies only when the jet strikes the plate normally. The value $1.34\,\mathrm{L/s}$ comes from using sine in place of cosine.
