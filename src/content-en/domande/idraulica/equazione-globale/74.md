A pump is installed in a straight horizontal pipe with equal diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ at the suction and discharge sections. The suction gauge pressure is $50\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the discharge gauge pressure is $350\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What are the magnitude and direction of the axial fluid force on the pump?

- $9.42\,\mathrm{kN}$ toward the discharge side
- Zero, because the velocity does not change
- $9.42\,\mathrm{kN}$ toward the suction side
- $12.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ toward the suction side

## Explanation

The inlet and outlet diameters are equal, so the velocities are equal and the net momentum flux is zero. With $x$ positive in the flow direction and $A = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the momentum balance gives $0 = p_1 A - p_2 A + R_x$. Thus $R_x = (p_2 - p_1)\,A = 300 \cdot 0.0314 = 9.42\,\mathrm{kN}$ is positive. The pump pushes the fluid toward the discharge side. By Newton’s third law the fluid exerts a force of $9.42\,\mathrm{kN}$ on the pump toward the suction side. The value $12.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ adds the two pressure-force magnitudes instead of taking their difference.
