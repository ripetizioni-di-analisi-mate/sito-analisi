A horizontal conical reducer changes the pipe diameter from $D_1 = 350\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 200\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The inlet and outlet gauge pressures are $p_1 = 280\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_2 = 273.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What force does the fluid exert on the reducer?

- $13.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $26.9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $13.1\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $40.4\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The two areas are $A_1 = 0.0962\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving velocities $v_1 = 2.08\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 4.07\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The sections are aligned and the fitting is horizontal, so the momentum balance along the flow direction gives
$$
F = p_1 A_1 - p_2 A_2 - \rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 26.94 - 13.44 - 0.40 = 13.1\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
directed downstream. Neglecting the change in momentum flux gives $13.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. Adding the two pressure forces gives $40.4\,\mathrm{kN}$ and overlooks that the outlet normal points into the control volume.
