A free water jet strikes a stationary smooth plate and splits into sheets that flow along it. With friction and elevation changes neglected, why do the sheets have the same speed as the incoming jet?

- From momentum, because the plate force is normal to the plate
- From Bernoulli’s equation, because the jet and both sheets have zero gauge pressure and there are no losses
- From continuity, because discharge is conserved
- Because momentum flux is conserved along the plate

## Explanation

Apply Bernoulli’s equation between a section of the incoming jet and a section of an outgoing sheet. The elevation change is negligible. Both sections have zero gauge pressure because the jet and sheets are exposed to the air. With negligible friction there is no head loss, so the total heads and hence the velocity heads are equal. The jet and sheets therefore have the same speed. Continuity relates their flow rates. Once their speeds are known, the momentum equation determines the force and how the flow divides.
