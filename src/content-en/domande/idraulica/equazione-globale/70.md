A waterjet boat travels through still water at constant speed $u = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its pump draws discharge $Q = 0.20\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ through an opening in the flat bottom of the hull and ejects it astern as a horizontal free jet of speed $w = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the boat. What propulsive force does the water exert on the boat?

- $4.00\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5.60\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2.40\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $0.800\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Use the reference frame fixed to the boat, which travels at constant velocity. Flow is steady in that frame, so use relative velocities in the momentum balance. Take the water between the hull opening and the jet outlet as the control volume, with horizontal axis $x$ directed aft. The surrounding water is stationary relative to shore, so it enters the boat frame with horizontal velocity $u$ directed aft and leaves with velocity $w$ in the same direction. The intake lies in the horizontal hull bottom and the jet has zero gauge pressure, so neither section contributes a horizontal pressure force. Projecting onto $x$ gives
$$
R_x = \rho\,Q\,(w - u) = 1000 \cdot 0.20 \cdot (20 - 8.0) = 2400\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The hull therefore pushes the water aft with a force of $2.40\,\mathrm{kN}$. By Newton’s third law the water pushes the boat forward with the same force. The value $4.00\,\mathrm{kN}$ omits the momentum of the water entering the intake. The value $5.60\,\mathrm{kN}$ adds the inlet contribution instead of subtracting it, and $0.800\,\mathrm{kN}$ subtracts $u$ from the absolute jet speed $w - u$, mixing two frames of reference.
