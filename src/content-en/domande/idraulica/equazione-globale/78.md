Two horizontal water jets merge. The first has discharge $Q_1 = 10\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and velocity $v_1 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along axis $x$. The second has discharge $Q_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and velocity $v_2 = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along axis $y$. Neglecting weight and air friction, what angle does the merged jet make with axis $x$?

- $31.0^\circ$
- $36.9^\circ$
- $51.3^\circ$
- $45.0^\circ$

## Explanation

The control volume around the region where the jets meet has no solid walls, and the gauge pressure is zero at every flow section. There is therefore no external horizontal force. The momentum equation reduces to $\rho Q_3 \vec{v}_3 = \rho Q_1 \vec{v}_1 + \rho Q_2 \vec{v}_2$. The outgoing momentum flux has component $\rho Q_1 v_1 = 120\,\mathrm{N}$ along $x$ and component $\rho Q_2 v_2 = 90\,\mathrm{N}$ along $y$. The merged jet follows the direction of this vector, giving
$$
\varphi = \arctan\frac{Q_2 v_2}{Q_1 v_1} = \arctan\frac{90}{120} = 36.9^\circ .
$$
The value $31.0^\circ$ uses only the flow rates, and $51.3^\circ$ uses only the speeds. The direction depends on the product of flow rate and speed for each incoming jet.
