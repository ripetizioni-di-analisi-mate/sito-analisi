A tank on a frictionless cart discharges horizontally through a smoothly rounded nozzle of diameter $d = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ whose center is $h = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. A vertical supply keeps the level constant, as shown. Neglecting losses and jet contraction, what horizontal force holds the cart stationary?

::schema{acqua="0.2,0 3,0 3,3.3 0.2,3.3;3.2,0.62 4.7,0.62 4.7,0.98 3.2,0.98;1.15,3.3 1.35,3.3 1.35,4.3 1.15,4.3" solidi="0,-0.25 3.2,-0.25 3.2,0 0,0;0,0 0.2,0 0.2,3.6 0,3.6;3,0 3.2,0 3.2,0.6 3,0.6;3,1 3.2,1 3.2,3.6 3,3.6;-0.4,-1.05 5,-1.05 5,-0.85 -0.4,-0.85" cerchi="0.6,-0.55,0.3;2.6,-0.55,0.3" pelo="0.2,3,3.3" tratteggi="1.9,0.8 3,0.8" quote="2.3 0.8,2.3 3.3,h = 2.50 m,0.4" frecce="1.6,4.3,1.6,3.6,Q;3.7,1.35,4.6,1.35,v" etichette="4.1,0.3,d = 80 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Tank on a cart discharging through a nozzle"}

- $247\,\mathrm{N}$
- $123\,\mathrm{N}$
- $493\,\mathrm{N}$
- $986\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The reservoir level is constant and losses are neglected, so Bernoulli’s equation between the free surface and the jet gives $v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.50} = 7.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Choose the water in the reservoir up to the jet outlet as the control volume. Water enters vertically and leaves horizontally at zero gauge pressure. Only the outgoing momentum flux contributes to the horizontal force, so the reservoir exerts a force of $\rho Q v = \rho A v^2$ on the water in the jet direction. The water therefore exerts an equal and opposite force on the reservoir, with magnitude
$$
F = \rho\,A\,v^2 = 2\,\gamma\,h\,A = 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.50 \cdot 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3} = 0.247\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
or $247\,\mathrm{N}$. An external force of that magnitude in the jet direction holds the cart stationary. The value $123\,\mathrm{N}$ is the hydrostatic force $\gamma h A$ on the closed outlet, half the jet thrust.
