A nozzle discharges a horizontal water jet of diameter $d = 30\,\mathrm{mm}$ at speed $v = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At a horizontal distance $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$, the falling jet strikes a smooth vertical plate and divides into sheets along it. Neglecting air resistance, what force does the jet exert on the plate?

- $61.5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $90.5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $45.2\,\mathrm{N}$
- $83.5\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Gravity is the only force acting on the jet during its flight. The horizontal velocity component therefore remains $v_x = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ while the downward component increases. The plate is vertical and smooth, so its force on the water is horizontal. Since the sheets leave parallel to the plate, only the incoming horizontal component $v_x$ contributes to the horizontal momentum balance. The discharge is $Q = \dfrac{\pi d^2}{4}\,v = 5.65\,\mathrm{L/s}$, giving a force of
$$
F = \rho\,Q\,v_x = 1000 \cdot 5.65 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 8.0 = 45.2\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
the same as if the plate were close to the nozzle. The value $61.5\,\mathrm{N}$ uses the full impact speed, $10.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which includes a vertical component. The value $90.5\,\mathrm{N}$ would apply if the plate reversed the jet.
