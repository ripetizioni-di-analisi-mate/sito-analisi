A water jet of discharge $Q = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and speed $v = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a fixed vane and leaves at angle $\theta = 120^\circ$ to the inlet direction. Friction reduces the outlet speed to $0.80\,v$. What is the component of the force on the vane along the incoming jet direction?

- $300\,\mathrm{N}$
- $700\,\mathrm{N}$
- $750\,\mathrm{N}$
- $500\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The jet is free, so the gauge pressure vanishes at the inlet and outlet sections. Only momentum flux contributes to the force in the jet direction, and its inlet value is $\rho Q v = 1000 \cdot 0.020 \cdot 25 = 500\,\mathrm{N}$. The outlet speed is $0.80\,v$ and the outlet velocity makes an angle $\theta$ with the incoming jet. Its component along the inlet direction is therefore $0.80\,v\cos 120^\circ = -0.40\,v$. The momentum balance in the incoming jet direction gives the force on the vane as
$$
F_x = \rho\,Q\,v\,(1 - 0.80\cos\theta) = 500 \cdot (1 + 0.40) = 700\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
Neglecting friction would give $\rho Q v\,(1 - \cos\theta) = 750\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $300\,\mathrm{N}$ treats the cosine of $120^\circ$ as positive.
