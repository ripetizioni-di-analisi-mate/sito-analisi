Two pipes of diameters $D$ and $2D$ carry the same discharge. Compared with the momentum flux in the pipe of diameter $D$, the momentum flux in the pipe of diameter $2D$

- is four times as large
- is twice as large
- is the same
- is one quarter as large

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is proportional to the square of the diameter, so doubling the diameter quadruples the area. The discharge is fixed, so the mean velocity $v = \dfrac{Q}{A}$ falls to one quarter of its original value. The momentum flux $\rho Q v$ therefore also falls to one quarter. At a given discharge this explains why the momentum flux can be small in large pipes but appreciable in jets and nozzles, where velocities are high.
