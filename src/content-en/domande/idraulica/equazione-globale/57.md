A pressure gauge at the top of a horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ reads gauge pressure $30.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the pressure force on the cross section through the tap?

- $33.9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $47.2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $162\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $40.6\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The streamlines at the section are straight and parallel, so pressure is distributed hydrostatically. The resultant is $\Pi = p_G A$, where $p_G$ is the pressure at the centroid on the pipe centerline. The centerline lies $\dfrac{D}{2} = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ below the crown, so the hydrostatic law gives
$$
p_G = 30.0 + 9.81 \cdot 0.60 = 35.9\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
With $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 1.131\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the resulting pressure force is $\Pi = 35.9 \cdot 1.131 = 40.6\,\mathrm{kN}$. The value $33.9\,\mathrm{kN}$ uses the pressure measured at the crown and $47.2\,\mathrm{kN}$ uses the pressure at the bottom of the pipe. The value $162\,\mathrm{kN}$ uses $\pi D^2$ for the area.
