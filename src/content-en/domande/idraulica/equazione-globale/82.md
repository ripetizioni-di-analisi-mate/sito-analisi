A water jet of diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a fixed flat plate normal to the jet. The plate has a coaxial central hole of diameter $d_f = 20\,\mathrm{mm}$. The jet core passes through unchanged while the rest flows along the plate in sheets. What force does the jet exert on the plate?

- $785\,\mathrm{N}$
- $660\,\mathrm{N}$
- $126\,\mathrm{N}$
- $911\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The discharge passing through the hole is $Q_f = \dfrac{\pi d_f^2}{4}\,v = 6.28\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the total jet discharge is $Q = \dfrac{\pi d^2}{4}\,v = 39.27\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Along the jet axis, the incoming momentum flux is $\rho Q v$. Only the central jet contributes an outgoing axial flux, $\rho Q_f v$, because the remaining water leaves in sheets parallel to the plate. The momentum balance gives $R_x = \rho Q_f v - \rho Q v$, so the force on the plate is
$$
F = \rho\,(Q - Q_f)\,v = 1000 \cdot (0.03927 - 0.00628) \cdot 20 = 660\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $785\,\mathrm{N}$ applies to a plate without a hole, and $126\,\mathrm{N}$ is the momentum flux in the central jet alone.
