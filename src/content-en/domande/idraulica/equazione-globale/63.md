A horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ ends in a diffuser of outlet diameter $D_2 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ discharging to the atmosphere, as shown. The discharge is $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the diffuser loss is $\Delta H = \xi\,\dfrac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g}$ with $\xi = 0.25$. What are the magnitude and direction of the fluid force on the diffuser?

::schema{acqua="0,0.95 2.6,0.95 3.8,0.5 6.4,0.5 6.4,2.1 3.8,2.1 2.6,1.65 0,1.65" linee="0,0.95 2.6,0.95 3.8,0.5 6.4,0.5;0,1.65 2.6,1.65 3.8,2.1 6.4,2.1" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q;6.7,1.3,7.6,1.3,v_2" tratteggi="1.6,0.7 1.6,1.9;6.4,0.25 6.4,2.35" etichette="1.6,2.2,1;6.4,2.65,2;0.3,1.9,D_1 = 200 mm;5,0,D_2 = 300 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Outlet diffuser discharging to the atmosphere"}

- $115\,\mathrm{N}$ upstream
- $292\,\mathrm{N}$ downstream
- $61.4\,\mathrm{N}$ downstream
- $49.1\,\mathrm{N}$ downstream

## Explanation

The velocities are $v_1 = 3.18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 1.41\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the outlet gauge pressure is zero. Applying Bernoulli’s equation with head loss between the two sections at the same elevation gives
$$
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{v_2^2}{2g} + \xi\,\frac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g} - \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 0.1020 + 0.0398 - 0.5164 = -0.3746\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so $p_1 = -3.67\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_1 A_1 = -115.4\,\mathrm{N}$. The outlet pressure force is zero, so the force on the diffuser is
$$
F = p_1 A_1 - \rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = -115.4 + 176.8 = 61.4\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
which is positive and therefore acts downstream. In a diffuser inside a pressurized pipeline, the force acts upstream when the excess of $p_2 A_2$ over $p_1 A_1$ dominates. Here the outlet is at zero gauge pressure, the inlet pressure is negative, and the momentum-flux term dominates. The value $292\,\mathrm{N}$ treats $p_1$ as positive. Neglecting head loss would give $49.1\,\mathrm{N}$, and neglecting momentum flux would give $115\,\mathrm{N}$ upstream.
