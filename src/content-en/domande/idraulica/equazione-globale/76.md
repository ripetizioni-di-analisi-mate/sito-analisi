Water leaves a vertical nozzle of diameter $d = 30\,\mathrm{mm}$ at upward speed $v_0 = 10.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and supports a horizontal disk at height $z = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above it, as shown. Beneath the disk, the jet spreads into a horizontal sheet. Neglect the water weight inside the control volume and air resistance. What is the disk weight?

::schema{acqua="-0.15,0 0.15,0 0.19,2 -0.19,2;-1.3,2 1.3,2 1.3,2.1 -1.3,2.1" solidi="-0.45,-0.5 -0.15,-0.5 -0.15,0 -0.45,0;0.15,-0.5 0.45,-0.5 0.45,0 0.15,0;-1.2,2.1 1.2,2.1 1.2,2.3 -1.2,2.3" frecce="0.6,0.3,0.6,1.1,v_0" forze="0 2.3,0,-0.7,W" quote="-1.5 0,-1.5 2,z = 2.00 m,-0.4" etichette="1.2,-0.3,d = 30 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical jet supporting a disk"}

- $70.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $43.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $110\,\mathrm{N}$
- $55.1\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The discharge $Q = A_0 v_0 = 7.07 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 10.0 = 7.07\,\mathrm{L/s}$ is constant along the jet. Because the jet is free, its gauge pressure is zero. Bernoulli’s equation between the nozzle and the elevation of the disk gives
$$
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 - 2 g z} = \sqrt{100 - 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.00} = 7.79\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The water leaves the underside of the disk as a horizontal sheet. The vertical momentum balance therefore gives an upward force of $\rho Q v = 1000 \cdot 7.07 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 7.79 = 55.1\,\mathrm{N}$ on the disk, equal to its weight in equilibrium. The value $70.7\,\mathrm{N}$ uses the nozzle velocity. The value $43.0\,\mathrm{N}$ uses the nozzle area as though the jet retained that area at impact. In fact the jet widens as it slows and its discharge remains constant.
