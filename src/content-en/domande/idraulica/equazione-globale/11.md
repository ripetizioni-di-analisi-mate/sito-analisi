A horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ carries discharge $Q = 4.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. A flat sluice gate maintains upstream depth $h_1 = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting losses, what is the downstream depth?

- $0.336\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.691\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.329\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The upstream specific energy is
$$
E = h_1 + \frac{Q^2}{2 g b^2 h_1^2} = 1.80 + 0.051 = 1.851\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and is conserved because losses beneath the gate are neglected. The downstream depth is therefore the alternate depth, the smaller root of $h + \dfrac{Q^2}{2 g b^2 h^2} = E$. It is $h_2 = 0.329\,\mathrm{m}$ with $v_2 = 5.46\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The larger root $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is the upstream depth. The value $0.691\,\mathrm{m}$ is the critical depth $h_c = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{Q^2}{g b^2}}$ separating subcritical and supercritical flow.
