A straight horizontal pipe of constant diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries discharge $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The upstream and downstream gauge pressures are $p_1 = 200\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_2 = 195\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What downstream force does the fluid exert on the pipe wall?

- $0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $314\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6283\,\mathrm{N}$
- $157\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

With constant diameter, continuity gives equal inlet and outlet velocities, so the net momentum flux is zero. The pipe section is horizontal and weight has no component along the flow axis. The integral momentum equation therefore reduces to
$$
0 = p_1 A - p_2 A + R_x ,
$$
which gives $R_x = -(p_1 - p_2)\,A = -5\,\mathrm{kPa} \cdot 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2} = -0.157\,\mathrm{kN}$. The fluid therefore exerts a force of $157\,\mathrm{N}$ on the wall in the flow direction. The wall exerts an equal and opposite resistance on the fluid, the resultant of the wall shear stresses. The pressure drop along this straight length of pipe therefore measures that resistance.
