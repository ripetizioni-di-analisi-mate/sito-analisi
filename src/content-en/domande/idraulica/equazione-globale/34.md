A nozzle on a horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ discharges to the atmosphere through an opening of diameter $d_2 = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$. The gauge pressure in the pipe is $p_1 = 250\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting losses, what is the discharge?

- $31.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $44.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $138\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $196\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The areas are $A_1 = 0.0177\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 1.963 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The outlet gauge pressure is zero, and both sections are at the same elevation, so Bernoulli’s equation between them gives
$$
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{Q^2}{2 g A_1^2} = \frac{Q^2}{2 g A_2^2} .
$$
Solving gives $Q = 44.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$, corresponding to $v_1 = 2.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 22.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $31.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$ uses $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ for the velocity head. The value $138\,\mathrm{L/s}$ treats the pressure $250\,\mathrm{kPa}$ as a head in meters of water without dividing by the specific weight.
