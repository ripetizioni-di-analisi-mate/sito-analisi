The discharge through a horizontal pipe elbow is measured, together with gauge pressures at its inlet and outlet. Must the head loss through the elbow also be known to calculate the force that the fluid exerts on it?

- No, because the momentum equation uses the measured pressures and contains no head loss term
- Yes, because head loss appears in the momentum equation
- Yes, because the velocities cannot be found without the head loss
- No, because an elbow always has zero head loss

## Explanation

The integral momentum equation contains the momentum fluxes, the section pressure forces, and the weight. Head loss does not enter directly. Continuity gives the velocities from discharge and area, and the measured pressures give the section forces. These known terms determine the wall reaction $\vec{R}$. The head loss is needed only if a section pressure has to be inferred from Bernoulli’s equation. The bend loss is small but positive because the turning flow dissipates energy.
