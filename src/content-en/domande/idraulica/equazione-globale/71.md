A horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ ends in a nozzle of diameter $d_2 = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$ discharging to the atmosphere. Upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 150\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The jet strikes a stationary flat plate normal to its axis. Neglecting losses, what force does the jet exert on the plate?

- $424\,\mathrm{N}$
- $855\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1710\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.93\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Gauge pressure is zero at the outlet. The pipe section and outlet are at the same elevation, and continuity gives $v_1 = v_2\,\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}$. Bernoulli’s equation therefore yields
$$
v_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2\,p_1}{\rho\left[1 - \left(\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}\right)^2\right]}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 150\,000}{1000 \cdot (1 - 0.0081)}} = 17.39\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
With $A_2 = 2.83 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the discharge is $Q = 49.17\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The free jet strikes the plate normally, so the plate force equals the incoming jet momentum flux, $S = \rho Q v_2 = 1000 \cdot 0.04917 \cdot 17.39 = 855\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $424\,\mathrm{N}$ is the product $p_1 A_2$, which does not account for jet velocity. The value $3.93\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the force on the nozzle, which the flange bolts transmit to the pipe.
