In deriving the integral momentum equation, the term $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\displaystyle\int_W \rho\,\vec{v}\,dW$ vanishes. Which assumption guarantees this?

- The fluid is incompressible
- The flow is steady
- The flow is one-dimensional at the inlet and outlet
- The correction factor is $\beta = 1$

## Explanation

This term is the rate of change of the momentum contained in the control volume. In steady flow, the velocity at each fixed point is independent of time, so the momentum stored in the volume is constant and its time derivative is zero. Incompressibility allows the same volumetric flow rate to be used at both sections. Straight, parallel streamlines allow calculation of the pressure forces $\vec{\Pi}$, and the assumption $\beta = 1$ gives the momentum flux as $\rho Q v$. None of these three conditions alone makes the time derivative vanish.
