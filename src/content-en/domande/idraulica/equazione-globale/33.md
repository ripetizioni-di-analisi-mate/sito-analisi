A horizontal reducer changes the pipe diameter from $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The inlet gauge pressure is $p_1 = 250\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting losses, what is the outlet gauge pressure?

- $250.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $255.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $238.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $244.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The areas are $A_1 = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving velocities of $v_1 = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 3.82\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The sections are at the same elevation and head losses are neglected, so Bernoulli’s equation reduces to $\dfrac{p_1}{\gamma} + \dfrac{v_1^2}{2g} = \dfrac{p_2}{\gamma} + \dfrac{v_2^2}{2g}$ and gives
$$
p_2 = p_1 - \gamma\,\frac{v_2^2 - v_1^2}{2g} = 250 - 5.85 = 244.1\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
As the pipe narrows, the velocity increases and the pressure drops. The value $255.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$ reverses the sign of the change in velocity head, and $250.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ neglects the change in velocity head altogether. The value $238.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$ uses $\dfrac{v^2}{g}$ for the velocity head.
