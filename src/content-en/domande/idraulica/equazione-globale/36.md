A horizontal pipe expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$. The gauge pressure immediately upstream is $p_1 = 120\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Assume uniform pressure $p_1$ across the annular step. What is the axial force on the annulus?

::schema{acqua="0,1.1 2.2,1.1 2.2,0.9 5,0.9 5,1.9 2.2,1.9 2.2,1.7 0,1.7" linee="0,1.1 2.2,1.1 2.2,0.9 5,0.9;0,1.7 2.2,1.7 2.2,1.9 5,1.9" frecce="0.3,1.4,1.1,1.4,Q" tratteggi="1.5,0.95 1.5,1.85;3.8,0.7 3.8,2.1" etichette="1.5,2.35,1;3.8,2.35,2;0.6,2,D_1 = 150 mm;3.6,0.5,D_2 = 250 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Sudden cross-sectional expansion"}

- $3.77\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2.12\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5.89\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $7.54\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The areas are $A_1 = 0.0177\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A_2 = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the annular step where the flow separates has area $A_2 - A_1 = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Measurements show that the pressure on this face is close to $p_1$. The axial force of the water on the face therefore has magnitude
$$
\Pi = p_1\,(A_2 - A_1) = 120 \cdot 0.0314 = 3.77\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
and acts upstream. The inward normal to the fluid control volume at the annular face points downstream, so the force exerted by the water on the face points the other way. The value $5.89\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the pressure force on the entire area $A_2$, and $2.12\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the force on the upstream section alone.
