A hydraulic jump forms downstream of a gate on a horizontal bed. Why is the integral momentum equation used to relate the upstream and downstream depths, instead of a lossless Bernoulli equation?

- Because flow in a jump is unsteady
- Because flow is not one-dimensional at the upstream and downstream sections
- Because Bernoulli’s theorem cannot be applied to open-channel flow
- Because a jump dissipates an initially unknown amount of mechanical energy, which does not enter the momentum equation

## Explanation

Applying Bernoulli’s equation between two sections requires the head loss $\Delta H$. The eddies in a hydraulic jump dissipate a substantial amount of energy, and the loss is unknown before the depths are determined. The integral momentum equation involves section pressure forces, momentum fluxes, and boundary forces. Dissipation does not enter the force balance because the forces exchanged within the turbulent flow are internal. Neglecting bed resistance over the short length of the jump gives a streamwise equation relating the two depths with no additional unknowns. Once the depths are known, Bernoulli’s equation gives the loss as the difference in specific energy. See the chapter on gradually varied flow profiles and hydraulic jumps.
