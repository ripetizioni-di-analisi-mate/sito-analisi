A pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries a turbulent discharge $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ with momentum correction factor $\beta = 1.04$. What is the momentum flux at a section?

- $110\,\mathrm{N}$
- $119\,\mathrm{N}$
- $115\,\mathrm{N}$
- $238\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is $A = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{Q}{A} = 1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the product $\rho Q v$ equals $115\,\mathrm{N}$. The momentum correction factor accounts for the velocity distribution across the section, giving
$$
M = \beta\,\rho\,Q\,v = 1.04 \cdot 115 = 119\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $115\,\mathrm{N}$ omits the correction factor, while $110\,\mathrm{N}$ divides by $\beta$ in place of multiplying. Since $\beta$ exceeds $1$ here, the actual momentum flux is greater than the estimate based on mean velocity alone.
