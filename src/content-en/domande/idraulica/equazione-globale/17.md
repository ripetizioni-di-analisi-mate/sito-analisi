A pipe of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries discharge $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Taking $\beta = 1$, what is the momentum flux across a section?

- $50.9\,\mathrm{N}$
- $102\,\mathrm{N}$
- $204\,\mathrm{N}$
- $407\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{Q}{A} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With $\beta = 1$, the momentum flux is
$$
\rho\,Q\,v = 1000 \cdot 0.120 \cdot 1.70 = 204\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
which is measured in newtons, the same units as force. The value $102\,\mathrm{N}$ is half the correct value and comes from using $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2 A$ for the momentum flux. The value $50.9\,\mathrm{N}$ uses $\pi D^2$ for the area, substituting the diameter for the radius.
