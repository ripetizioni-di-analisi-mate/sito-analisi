A vertical pipe section of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 10.0\,\mathrm{m}$ carries discharge $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ upward. The gauge pressures are $p_1 = 250.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the lower section and $p_2 = 150.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the upper section, as shown. What axial force does the fluid exert on the pipe wall?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,0,5,-" condotte="A-B,0.7" tratteggi="-0.55,0.6 0.55,0.6;-0.55,4.4 0.55,4.4" frecce="0,1.9,0,3.1,Q" quote="0 0.6,0 4.4,L = 10.0 m,-1.3" etichette="0.75,0.6,1;0.75,4.4,2;2.2,0.6,p_1 = 250.0 kPa;2.2,4.4,p_2 = 150.5 kPa;-1.6,5.2,D = 200 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical pipe segment with upward flow"}

- $3.13\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $3.08\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $6.21\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $44.0\,\mathrm{N}$ upward

## Explanation

The diameter is constant, so continuity gives the same velocity at both sections and the net momentum flux is zero. With $z$ positive upward and $A = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the vertical forces are the two pressure forces, the fluid weight $G = \gamma A L = 3.082\,\mathrm{kN}$, and the wall force $R_z$. Their balance gives
$$
0 = p_1 A - p_2 A - \gamma A L + R_z .
$$
Thus $R_z = \gamma A L - (p_1 - p_2)\,A = 3.082 - 3.126 = -0.0440\,\mathrm{kN}$. The wall pushes downward on the fluid and the fluid exerts $44.0\,\mathrm{N}$ upward on the wall, in the direction of flow. Almost the entire pressure-force difference supports the water’s weight. The remainder, $\gamma A\,\Delta H$ with head loss $\Delta H = 0.143\,\mathrm{m}$, balances the wall resistance. The value $3.13\,\mathrm{kN}$ neglects weight, while $6.21\,\mathrm{kN}$ adds it with the wrong sign.
