In a horizontal rectangular channel, a vertical flat gate changes the depth from $h_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream to $h_2 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream. Compared with the difference between the hydrostatic forces at these depths, the force the flow exerts on the gate is

- larger, because momentum flux is added to the hydrostatic term
- equal, because incoming and outgoing momentum fluxes cancel
- smaller, because pressure is not hydrostatic at the two sections
- smaller, because part of the upstream pressure force accelerates the flow

## Explanation

The streamwise momentum equation includes the two hydrostatic forces and the change in momentum flux, giving $S = \dfrac{\gamma b h_1^2}{2} - \dfrac{\gamma b h_2^2}{2} - \rho Q (v_2 - v_1)$. The flow becomes shallower and accelerates, so $\rho Q (v_2 - v_1)$ is positive. This term is subtracted from the difference in hydrostatic forces, reducing the force on the gate. If the water were at rest on both sides, the momentum-flux term would be zero and the gate force would equal the difference in hydrostatic forces. At the two flow sections the streamlines are straight and parallel, so pressure there is hydrostatic and the section forces can be calculated as in a static fluid.
