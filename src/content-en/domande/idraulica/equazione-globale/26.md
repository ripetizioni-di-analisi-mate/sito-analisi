A $90^\circ$ bend of a pipe of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$, lying in a horizontal plane, is anchored to a concrete block. During the acceptance test the pipe is full of still water at the gauge pressure $p = 1.50\,\mathrm{MPa}$. What is the force exerted by the water on the bend?

- $106\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $75.0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $150\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $212\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The water is still, so the discharge is zero and the momentum fluxes in the momentum equation vanish. What remains are the pressure forces on the two sections, each of magnitude $pA$, with $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$, therefore $pA = 1500 \cdot 0.0707 = 106\,\mathrm{kN}$. The formula for the force on a bend then applies with $K = pA$, and gives
$$
F = 2\,p A \sin\frac{\theta}{2} = 2 \cdot 106 \cdot \sin 45^\circ = 150\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
directed along the bisector of the angle between the two sections, toward the outside of the bend. The value $106\,\mathrm{kN}$ is only one of the two components, $212\,\mathrm{kN}$ comes from adding the two components as if they were parallel, and $75.0\,\mathrm{kN}$ comes from dropping the factor $2$ in the formula.
