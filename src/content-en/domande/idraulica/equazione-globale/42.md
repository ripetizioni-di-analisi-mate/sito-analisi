A water jet of diameter $d = 35\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a stationary double-cup vane that turns it through $\theta = 180^\circ$. Neglecting friction, what force does the jet exert on the vane?

- $385\,\mathrm{N}$
- $544\,\mathrm{N}$
- $770\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1539\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The jet area is $A = 9.621 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving a discharge of $Q = A v = 19.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and an incoming momentum flux of $\rho Q v = 385\,\mathrm{N}$. With negligible friction, the outgoing velocity has the same magnitude as the incoming velocity and the opposite direction. The change in momentum flux therefore has twice the magnitude of the incoming flux, giving
$$
F = 2\,\rho\,Q\,v\,\sin\frac{\theta}{2} = 2 \cdot 385 = 770\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
A deflection of $180^\circ$ gives twice the force that the same jet would exert on a flat plate normal to it, $385\,\mathrm{N}$. This is why Pelton turbine buckets have two spoon-shaped halves.
