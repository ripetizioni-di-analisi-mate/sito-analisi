A horizontal water jet enters a fixed vane lying in a vertical plane and leaves it deflected by $90^\circ$, directed vertically upward. Neglecting friction on the vane and the weight of the water, what is the direction of the force exerted by the jet on the vane?

- Horizontal, in the direction of the incoming jet
- Inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal, in the direction of the incoming jet and upward
- Inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal, in the direction of the incoming jet and downward
- Vertical, downward

## Explanation

The jet is free, so the pressure forces on the flow sections are zero and, neglecting the weight, the momentum equation reduces to $\rho Q\,(\vec{v}_2 - \vec{v}_1) = \vec{R}$, where $\vec{R}$ is the force exerted by the vane on the water. Taking the $x$ axis in the direction of the incoming jet and the $z$ axis upward, $\vec{v}_1 = v\,(1,\,0)$ and, since friction is neglected, $\vec{v}_2 = v\,(0,\,1)$. The force on the vane is $-\vec{R}$, therefore
$$
\vec{F} = \rho\,Q\,(\vec{v}_1 - \vec{v}_2) = \rho\,Q\,v\,(1,\,-1) .
$$
The two components have the same magnitude $\rho Q v$, so the force is **inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal, in the direction of the incoming jet and downward**, and its magnitude is $\sqrt{2}\,\rho Q v$. The upward component belongs to the force $\vec{R}$ exerted by the vane on the water, while a purely horizontal force would occur with a flat plate normal to the jet.
