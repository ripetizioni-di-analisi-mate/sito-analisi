A water jet of diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a plate normal to its axis. The plate is mounted on a cart moving away from the nozzle at constant speed $u = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the jet direction. What force does the jet exert on the plate?

- $471\,\mathrm{N}$
- $283\,\mathrm{N}$
- $785\,\mathrm{N}$
- $660\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

In the frame fixed to the plate, which moves at constant velocity, the flow is steady. Apply the integral momentum equation using relative velocities. The jet reaches the plate at relative speed $v - u = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the intercepted flow rate is $A\,(v - u)$. The rest of the nozzle discharge lengthens the jet between nozzle and plate. With $A = 1.963 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and the outgoing sheets parallel to the plate, the momentum balance gives
$$
F = \rho\,A\,(v - u)^2 = 1000 \cdot 1.963 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 12^2 = 283\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $471\,\mathrm{N}$ uses the nozzle discharge $A v$ in place of the flow rate actually reaching the moving plate. The value $785\,\mathrm{N}$ is the force on a stationary plate.
