Fluid particles in a control volume exert internal pressure and viscous forces on one another. Why do these forces drop out of the integral momentum equation?

- They are negligible compared with the pressure forces on the flow sections
- They are internal forces occurring in equal and opposite pairs with zero resultant
- They cancel only in steady flow
- They are included in the wall reaction $\vec{R}$

## Explanation

The integral momentum equation applies Newton’s second law to the fluid occupying the control volume, so its force term is the resultant of the external forces. Two adjacent fluid particles exert equal and opposite forces on each other by Newton’s third law. The internal forces therefore cancel regardless of their individual magnitudes. The remaining forces are those exerted by the surrounding fluid at the flow sections, the weight, and the wall forces. The vector $\vec{R}$ includes the pressure and shear forces exerted by the solid boundary on the fluid. Forces between fluid particles are internal to the volume.
