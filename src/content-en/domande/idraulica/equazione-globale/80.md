A jet of speed $v = 40.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a Pelton wheel. The buckets turn the jet through about $180^\circ$. The continuous succession of buckets intercepts the entire discharge $Q$. Neglecting bucket friction, at what peripheral bucket speed $u$ is the power transmitted to the wheel greatest?

- $40.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $13.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $20.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $28.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

In the vane frame, the jet arrives at relative speed $v - u$. With negligible friction it leaves at the same relative speed in the opposite direction, giving a relative velocity change $2\,(v - u)$. The wheel intercepts the entire discharge $Q$. The force on the vanes is therefore $F = 2 \rho Q (v - u)$, and the transmitted power is
$$
P = F\,u = 2\,\rho\,Q\,(v - u)\,u .
$$
For fixed $Q$ and $v$, the power is a quadratic function of $u$. It vanishes at $u = 0$ and $u = v$, so it reaches its maximum at $u = \dfrac{v}{2} = 20.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $13.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, equal to $\dfrac{v}{3}$, applies to a single vane moving away from the nozzle, which intercepts only the flow rate $A\,(v - u)$.
