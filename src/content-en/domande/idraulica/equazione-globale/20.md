A pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries discharge $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at gauge pressure $p = 400\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Which statement correctly compares the pressure force $p A$ on a section with the momentum flux $\rho Q v$?

- The pressure force exceeds the momentum flux by about two orders of magnitude
- The momentum flux exceeds the pressure force by about two orders of magnitude
- The two terms have the same order of magnitude
- The terms are equal because they have the same dimensions

## Explanation

The cross-sectional area is $A = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and the mean velocity is $v = \dfrac{Q}{A} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The momentum flux is therefore $\rho Q v = 79.6\,\mathrm{N}$, and the pressure force at the section is $p A = 12.6\,\mathrm{kN}$. The pressure force is about $160$ times the momentum flux, roughly two orders of magnitude larger. The force on a fitting in this pressurized pipe therefore depends primarily on operating pressure. A thrust block sized for momentum flux alone would be inadequate. The two terms can be added because both have the dimensions of force, although their magnitudes differ.
