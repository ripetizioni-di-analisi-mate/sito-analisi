A water jet of diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v = 16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a fixed, smooth, flat plate at angle $\alpha = 45^\circ$ to its plane and splits into two sheets along the plate. Neglecting friction, what is the larger sheet discharge?

::schema{acqua="0,1 3.80,1 4.20,1.4 0,1.4;4.20,1.40 5.12,2.32 4.95,2.49 4.03,1.57;3.80,1.00 3.31,0.51 3.23,0.58 3.73,1.07" solidi="3.29,0.07 5.56,2.33 5.34,2.54 3.08,0.28" frecce="0.6,1.2,1.8,1.2,v = 16 m/s" etichette="1.2,1.65,d = 50 mm;4.9,0.85,α = 45°" riferimento=0 titolo="Jet striking a plate inclined at $45^\circ$"}

- $4.60\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $15.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $22.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $26.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The jet area is $A = 1.963 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and its total discharge is $Q = A v = 31.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The smooth plate exerts no tangential force, so the component of the momentum balance along the plate gives $Q_1 - Q_2 = Q\cos\alpha$. Together with continuity $Q_1 + Q_2 = Q$, this gives
$$
Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2}\,(1 + \cos\alpha) = \frac{31.4}{2}\,(1 + 0.707) = 26.8\,\mathrm{L/s} .
$$
The smaller sheet carries the remaining discharge $4.60\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and the two add to the jet flow rate. The value $15.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$, half the total discharge, would apply if the jet struck the plate normally.
