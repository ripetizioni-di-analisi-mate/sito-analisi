A $90^\circ$ pipe elbow of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ turns a horizontal flow vertically upward. The discharge is $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$, the upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 200\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the outlet is $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ higher than the inlet. Neglecting losses, what is the outlet gauge pressure?

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3,3,-" condotte="A-B-C,0.55" tratteggi="0.9,-0.6 0.9,0.6;2.4,2.1 3.6,2.1" quote="0.9 0,0.9 2.1,0.80 m,-1.1" frecce="0.1,0,0.8,0,Q" etichette="0.7,0.85,1;3.75,2.1,2;0.2,-0.95,D = 250 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="An $90^\circ$ elbow in a vertical plane"}

- $192.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $199.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $200.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $207.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Constant diameter and continuity give equal speeds at the two sections, so the velocity heads cancel from Bernoulli’s equation. With negligible losses, total head is conserved, leaving $z_1 + \dfrac{p_1}{\gamma} = z_2 + \dfrac{p_2}{\gamma}$. Setting $z_2 - z_1 = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ gives
$$
p_2 = p_1 - \gamma\,(z_2 - z_1) = 200 - 9.81 \cdot 0.80 = 192.2\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
As the water rises, its pressure falls. The value $207.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$ reverses the sign of the elevation difference. Using density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ in place of specific weight $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ would give $199.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
