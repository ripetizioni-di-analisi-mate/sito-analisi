A hydraulic jump forms in a horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The measured depths are $h_1 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ just upstream and $h_2 = 1.60\,\mathrm{m}$ just downstream, as shown. Neglecting bed resistance across the short jump, what discharge does the integral momentum equation give?

::schema{acqua="0,0 8,0 8,1.6 5,1.6 3.2,0.4 0,0.4" solidi="0,-0.4 8,-0.4 8,0 0,0" pelo="0,3,0.4;5.2,8,1.6,p_a" tratteggi="2.4,0 2.4,0.4;6,0 6,1.6" quote="0.4 0,0.4 0.4,h_1,-0.35;7.2 0,7.2 1.6,h_2,0.4" frecce="1.1,0.2,1.9,0.2,Q" etichette="2.4,0.8,1;6,2,2;3.6,2.4,b = 2.00 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Hydraulic jump in a rectangular channel"}

- $5.01\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $4.01\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $7.09\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $2.51\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The streamlines at sections $1$ and $2$ are straight and parallel, so the pressure distribution is hydrostatic. The upstream pressure force is $\dfrac{\gamma b h_1^2}{2}$ downstream and the downstream pressure force is $\dfrac{\gamma b h_2^2}{2}$ upstream. The bed is horizontal and its resistance is neglected, so the solid boundary exerts no streamwise force. Using $v = \dfrac{Q}{b h}$, the momentum balance gives
$$
\rho\,Q \left(\frac{Q}{b h_2} - \frac{Q}{b h_1}\right) = \frac{\gamma b h_1^2}{2} - \frac{\gamma b h_2^2}{2} .
$$
Dividing by $h_1 - h_2$ and using $\gamma = \rho g$ gives
$$
Q^2 = \frac{g\,b^2\,h_1 h_2\,(h_1 + h_2)}{2} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 2.00}{2} = 25.1\,\mathrm{m^6/s^2} ,
$$
or $Q = 5.01\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The value $4.01\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ follows from a lossless Bernoulli equation, which omits the energy dissipated by the eddies in a hydraulic jump. The value $7.09\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ omits the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the pressure forces, and $2.51\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ is obtained by dividing the discharge by the channel width.
