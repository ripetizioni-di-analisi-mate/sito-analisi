A sudden expansion is to be installed in a horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$. Assume the pressure on the annular step equals upstream pressure. At fixed discharge, which downstream diameter $D_2$ maximizes the pressure rise?

- $D_2 = 283\,\mathrm{mm}$, that is, $A_2 = 2 A_1$
- $D_2 = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$, that is, $A_2 = 4 A_1$
- $D_2 = 238\,\mathrm{mm}$, that is, $A_2 = \sqrt{2}\,A_1$
- The largest possible diameter, because the downstream speed approaches zero

## Explanation

With the assumed pressure on the annular step, the axial momentum equation gives $\rho Q (v_2 - v_1) = (p_1 - p_2)\,A_2$. Since $Q = A_2 v_2$, the pressure rise is
$$
p_2 - p_1 = \rho\,v_2\,(v_1 - v_2) .
$$
At a fixed discharge the upstream velocity $v_1$ is fixed. The pressure rise is therefore a quadratic function of $v_2$ that vanishes at $v_2 = 0$ and $v_2 = v_1$. Its maximum occurs halfway between these values, at $v_2 = \dfrac{v_1}{2}$. Continuity then gives $A_2 = 2 A_1$, or $D_2 = \sqrt{2}\,D_1 = 283\,\mathrm{mm}$. A lossless Bernoulli calculation would predict the greatest pressure rise for the largest available outlet diameter. That conclusion is inapplicable to a sudden expansion, where the Borda–Carnot loss is significant.
