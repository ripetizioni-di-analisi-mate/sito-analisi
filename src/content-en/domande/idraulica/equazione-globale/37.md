A horizontal pipe expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Assuming pressure on the annular step equals the upstream pressure, what is the pressure rise from inlet to outlet?

- $7.60\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $4.75\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $2.85\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $1.90\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The velocities are $v_1 = 3.18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = 0.796\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With the assumed uniform pressure on the annular step, the axial momentum equation gives $\rho Q (v_2 - v_1) = (p_1 - p_2)\,A_2$. Since $Q = A_2 v_2$, this yields
$$
p_2 - p_1 = \rho\,v_2\,(v_1 - v_2) = 1000 \cdot 0.796 \cdot 2.387 = 1900\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.90\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
The value $4.75\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is the pressure rise predicted by a lossless Bernoulli equation. The difference, $2.85\,\mathrm{kPa}$, is the Borda–Carnot loss expressed as a pressure. The value $7.60\,\mathrm{kPa}$ uses the upstream area as the divisor in place of the downstream area.
