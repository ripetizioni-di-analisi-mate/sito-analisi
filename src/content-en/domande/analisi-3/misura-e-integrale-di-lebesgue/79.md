For $g\in L^1(0,1)$ set $G(x)=\int_x^1g(y)\,dy$. Which function $w$ satisfies $\int_0^1G(x)\,dx=\int_0^1w(y)\,g(y)\,dy$ for every $g\in L^1(0,1)$?

- $w(y)=1-y$
- $w(y)=1$
- $w(y)=y$
- $w(y)=y^2$

## Explanation

We write $\int_0^1G(x)\,dx=\int_0^1\int_0^1\mathbf 1_{\{x<y\}}\,g(y)\,dy\,dx$.
Since $\int_0^1\int_0^1\mathbf 1_{\{x<y\}}\,|g(y)|\,dy\,dx\le\|g\|_1<\infty$, Fubini's theorem applies and the order of integration can be exchanged.
For fixed $y$, the variable $x$ ranges over the interval $(0,y)$, whose length is $y$.
Hence $\int_0^1G(x)\,dx=\int_0^1y\,g(y)\,dy$, and the weight is $w(y)=y$.
The weight $w(y)=1-y$ comes from taking the interval $(y,1)$ instead of $(0,y)$.
