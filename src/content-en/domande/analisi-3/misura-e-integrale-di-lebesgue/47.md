Let $f\ge0$ be measurable on a measure space. If $\int f\,d\mu=0$, what follows?

- $f$ must be continuous.
- $f=0$ at every point.
- $f=0$ almost everywhere.
- Every measurable set has measure zero.

## Explanation

For every positive integer $k$, the integral over $\{f\ge\dfrac1k\}$ is at least one $k$th of its measure, so the set has measure zero. Their union contains every point where $f$ is positive.
