Let $H(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\dfrac{\sin(tx)}{x}\,dx$. What is $H'(1)$?

- $-\dfrac12$
- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac12$

## Explanation

The absolute derivative of the integrand is at most $e^{-x}$, which is integrable. At zero the integrand vanishes. Differentiating under the integral sign and integrating $e^{-x}\cos x$ therefore gives the real part of $\dfrac1{1-i}$.
