Let $f_n=-n\mathbf1_{(0,\frac1n)}$ on $(0,1)$. What is the pair $\left(\int\liminf_n f_n\,dm,\ \liminf_n\int f_n\,dm\right)$?

- $(0,-1)$
- $(0,0)$
- $(-1,-1)$
- $(-1,0)$

## Explanation

For fixed $x\in(0,1)$ we have $x\ge\dfrac1n$ for every sufficiently large $n$, hence $f_n(x)=0$ eventually and $\liminf_nf_n(x)=0$.
Therefore $\int\liminf_nf_n\,dm=0$.
Each $f_n$ equals $-n$ on an interval of length $\dfrac1n$, so $\int f_n\,dm=-1$ for every $n$ and the limit inferior of the integrals is $-1$.
The pair is thus $(0,-1)$, and since $0>-1$ the inequality $\int\liminf f_n\,dm\le\liminf\int f_n\,dm$ does not hold.
Fatou's lemma does not apply, because the $f_n$ are not nonnegative.
