Let $F(t,x)=\log(1+tx)$ on $0<x<1$. For $0<\lvert h\rvert\le\dfrac12$, which bound on the difference quotient in $t=0$ follows from the mean value theorem?

- $\left\lvert\dfrac{F(h,x)-F(0,x)}h\right\rvert\le2x$
- $\left\lvert\dfrac{F(h,x)-F(0,x)}h\right\rvert=0$
- $\left\lvert\dfrac{F(h,x)-F(0,x)}h\right\rvert\le2x^2$
- $\left\lvert\dfrac{F(h,x)-F(0,x)}h\right\rvert\le x$

## Explanation

The difference quotient equals the derivative with respect to the parameter at an intermediate point. There the denominator $1+tx$ is at least one half, so the absolute value is at most $2x$. This bound holds for increments of either sign.
