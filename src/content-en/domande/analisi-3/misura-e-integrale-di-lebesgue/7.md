Compute $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty\dfrac{e^{-5x}}{1+\dfrac{x}{n}}\,dx$.

- $0$
- $5$
- $\frac1{5}$
- $-\frac1{5}$

## Explanation

At every point of the half-line the denominator tends to one and is at least one for all indices. The decaying exponential is consequently both the limit and a common integrable majorant. Dominated convergence reduces the calculation to the integral of this exponential.

$$
0\le\frac{e^{-5x}}{1+\dfrac{x}{n}}\le e^{-5x},\qquad \int_0^\infty e^{-5x}dx=\frac1{5}.
$$
