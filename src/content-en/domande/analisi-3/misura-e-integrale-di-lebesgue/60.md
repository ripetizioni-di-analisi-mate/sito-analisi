Compute $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1\dfrac{\cos(nx)}{1+nx}\,dx$.

- $1$
- The limit does not exist.
- $\dfrac12$
- $0$

## Explanation

For every $x>0$ the denominator tends to infinity and the cosine remains bounded, so the quotient tends to zero. Its absolute value is at most one, and that constant is integrable on the domain. Dominated convergence therefore applies.
