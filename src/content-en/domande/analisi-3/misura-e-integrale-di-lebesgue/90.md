Let $J(t)=\int_0^\infty\dfrac{e^{-x}-e^{-tx}}x\,dx$ for $t>0$. Which observation verifies the integrability condition for at least one parameter value required for differentiation under the integral sign?

- The numerator is independent of $t$.
- At $t=1$ the integrand vanishes identically.
- For each $t>0$ the derivative with respect to $t$ is constant in $x$.
- Each exponential divided by $x$ is integrable near zero.

## Explanation

The theorem requires the integrand to be integrable at one parameter value to begin with. At $t=1$ the two terms cancel pointwise, so this condition holds. Individually the terms would each have a nonintegrable singularity near zero.
