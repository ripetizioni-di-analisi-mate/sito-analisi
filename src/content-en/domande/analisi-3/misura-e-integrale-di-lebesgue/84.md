For $t>0$, let $F(t)=\int_0^\infty x e^{-tx}\,dx$. What is $F'(2)$?

- $-\dfrac18$
- $\dfrac14$
- $-\dfrac12$
- $-\dfrac14$

## Explanation

On a compact neighborhood of two, the absolute derivative is bounded by $x^2e^{-x}$. The given integral equals $t^{-2}$ by integration by parts on the half-line. Its derivative is $-2t^{-3}$, evaluated at two.
