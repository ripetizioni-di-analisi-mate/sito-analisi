On $(0,\infty)$, what is $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty\dfrac{n e^{-x}}{1+n^2x^2}\,dx$?

- $0$
- $+\infty$
- $\dfrac\pi2$
- $\dfrac\pi4$

## Explanation

The substitution $u=nx$ gives the integrand $\dfrac{e^{-\frac un}}{1+u^2}$. It converges to $\dfrac1{1+u^2}$ and is dominated by this same function, which is integrable on the half-line. Dominated convergence gives the difference of the values of the arctangent antiderivative at infinity and zero.
