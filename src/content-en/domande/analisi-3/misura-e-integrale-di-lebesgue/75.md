Let $E=\left\{(x,y)\in(0,1)^2:\ xy<\dfrac{1}{4}\right\}$. Which integral in the single variable $x$ equals the Lebesgue measure of $E$?

- $\int_0^1\dfrac{1}{4x}\,dx$
- $\int_0^1 4x\,dx$
- $\int_0^{\frac{1}{4}}1\,dx+\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^1\dfrac{1}{4x}\,dx$
- $\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^1\left(1-\dfrac{1}{4x}\right)dx$

## Explanation

The indicator of $E$ is measurable and nonnegative, so by Tonelli's theorem the measure of $E$ is the integral in $x$ of the length of the section $E_x=\left\{y\in(0,1):xy<\frac14\right\}$.
If $0<x\le\dfrac14$, then $xy<\dfrac14$ for every $y\in(0,1)$, hence $E_x=(0,1)$ has length $1$.
If $\dfrac14<x<1$, the condition is equivalent to $y<\dfrac{1}{4x}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4x}<1$, hence $E_x$ has length $\dfrac{1}{4x}$.
Therefore the measure of $E$ is $\int_0^{\frac14}1\,dx+\int_{\frac14}^1\dfrac{1}{4x}\,dx$.
The integral $\int_0^1\dfrac{1}{4x}\,dx$ ignores the fact that the section is contained in $(0,1)$, and indeed it diverges.
