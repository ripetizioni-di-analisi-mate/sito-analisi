On the real line with Lebesgue measure set $f_n=\dfrac{1}{n}\mathbf 1_{(0,n)}$. What can be said about the convergence of $f_n$ to zero in $L^\infty(\mathbb R)$ and in $L^1(\mathbb R)$?

- It converges to zero in $L^1$ and not in $L^\infty$.
- It converges to zero in $L^\infty$ and not in $L^1$.
- It converges to zero in both spaces.
- It converges to zero in neither space.

## Explanation

The function $f_n$ equals $\dfrac1n$ on $(0,n)$ and zero elsewhere, so $\|f_n\|_\infty=\dfrac1n$ tends to zero.
Therefore the sequence converges to zero in $L^\infty$.
On the other hand $\|f_n\|_1=\dfrac1n\cdot n=1$ for every $n$, so the sequence does not converge to zero in $L^1$.
On a space of finite measure convergence in $L^\infty$ would imply convergence in $L^1$, by the bound $\|f\|_1\le\mu(X)\,\|f\|_\infty$.
Here the line has infinite measure, so this bound is not available.
