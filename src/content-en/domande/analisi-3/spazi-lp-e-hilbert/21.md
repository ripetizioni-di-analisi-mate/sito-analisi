Which function belongs to $L^1(0,\infty)$ but not to $L^2(0,\infty)$?

- $x^{-\frac34}\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(x)$
- $\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(x)$
- $(1+x)^{-\frac34}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The function $x^{-\frac34}\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(x)$ vanishes beyond one, so only the singularity at zero matters. The power with exponent $-\dfrac34$ is integrable at the origin. Its square has exponent $-\dfrac32$, so the integral of the square diverges. The indicator function, by comparison, belongs to both spaces.
