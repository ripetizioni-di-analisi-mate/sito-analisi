In $L^2(0,2)$ we approximate $f(x)=x$ by the functions $s$ equal to a constant $a$ on $(0,1)$ and to a constant $b$ on $(1,2)$. Which pair $(a,b)$ minimizes the distance $\|f-s\|_2$?

- $(1,1)$
- $\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$
- $(0,2)$
- $\left(\dfrac{1}{4},\dfrac{3}{4}\right)$

## Explanation

The functions $s$ form the subspace spanned by $\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}$ and $\mathbf 1_{(1,2)}$, which are orthogonal in $L^2(0,2)$.
By the projection theorem the distance is minimal when the residual $f-s$ is orthogonal to both indicators, that is, when
$$
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\int_0^1(x-a)\,dx=0\\[8pt]
\displaystyle\int_1^2(x-b)\,dx=0 .
\end{cases}
$$
Since each interval has length $1$, the first condition gives $a=\int_0^1x\,dx=\dfrac12$ and the second gives $b=\int_1^2x\,dx=\dfrac32$.
Hence the required pair is $\left(\dfrac12,\dfrac32\right)$, made of the averages of $f$ on the two intervals.
The pair $\left(\dfrac14,\dfrac34\right)$ comes from dividing the integrals by the length $2$ of the whole interval.
