In a Hilbert space $e_1,e_2$ are orthonormal. For which complex values of $\lambda$ do $\dfrac{e_1+e_2}{\sqrt2}$ and $\lambda(e_1-e_2)$ also form an orthonormal system?

- $|\lambda|=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$
- $\lambda=1$ only.
- $\lambda=0$ only.
- For every $\lambda$ with zero real part.

## Explanation

The sum and difference are orthogonal because the diagonal terms cancel and the cross terms vanish. The difference has norm $\sqrt2$. Thus $|\lambda|\sqrt2=1$ is required, with no restriction on the phase.
