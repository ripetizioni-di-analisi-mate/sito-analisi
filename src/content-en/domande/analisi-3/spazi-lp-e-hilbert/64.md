In a complex Hilbert space the vectors $e_1,e_2$ are orthonormal. For which $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ do the vectors $\dfrac{e_1+e_2}{\sqrt2}$ and $\lambda(e_1-e_2)$ also form an orthonormal system?

- $|\lambda|=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$
- Only for $\lambda=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$
- $|\lambda|=\sqrt2$
- $|\lambda|=1$

## Explanation

Since $\langle e_1,e_2\rangle=0$, we have $\|e_1+e_2\|^2=\|e_1\|^2+\|e_2\|^2=2$, so the first vector has norm one.
Moreover $\langle e_1+e_2,\lambda(e_1-e_2)\rangle=\overline\lambda\left(\|e_1\|^2-\|e_2\|^2\right)=0$ for every $\lambda$, because the mixed terms vanish, therefore the two vectors are orthogonal.
Finally $\|\lambda(e_1-e_2)\|=|\lambda|\,\|e_1-e_2\|=|\lambda|\sqrt2$, and this norm equals one if and only if $|\lambda|=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$.
The condition involves only the modulus, so every $\lambda=\dfrac{e^{i\theta}}{\sqrt2}$ with $\theta\in\mathbb R$ is admissible.
The condition $|\lambda|=1$ comes from forgetting that $e_1-e_2$ has norm $\sqrt2$.
