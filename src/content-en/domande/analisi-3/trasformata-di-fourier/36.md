Let $f$ be a Schwartz function and let $\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-i\xi x}\,dx$. It is known that the transform $\widehat{xf}$ of $xf(x)$ vanishes on an open interval $J$. Which conclusion follows?

- $\widehat f$ vanishes on $J$.
- $\widehat{f'}$ vanishes on $J$.
- $\widehat f$ vanishes outside $J$.
- $\widehat f$ is constant on $J$.

## Explanation

Since $f$ is a Schwartz function, we can differentiate under the integral sign, and we obtain
$$
\frac{d}{d\xi}\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}(-ix)f(x)e^{-i\xi x}\,dx=-i\,\widehat{xf}(\xi).
$$
By hypothesis $\widehat{xf}=0$ on $J$, hence the derivative of $\widehat f$ vanishes on $J$.
Since $J$ is an interval, it follows that $\widehat f$ is constant on $J$.
The value of the constant is not fixed by the hypothesis, so $\widehat f$ may be nonzero on $J$.
The conclusion $\widehat{f'}=0$ comes from confusing multiplication by $x$ with differentiation, whereas $\widehat{f'}(\xi)=i\xi\,\widehat f(\xi)$ is in general not zero on $J$.
