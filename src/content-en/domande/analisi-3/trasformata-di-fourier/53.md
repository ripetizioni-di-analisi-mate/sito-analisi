Why is the classical derivative of $\mathbf1_{[0,1]}$, which vanishes almost everywhere, unsuitable for the classical formula $\widehat{f'}=i\xi\widehat f$?

- The function has jumps and is not locally absolutely continuous
- The function does not belong to $L^1$
- The Fourier kernel is discontinuous
- The frequency must be an integer

## Explanation

The function jumps at both endpoints of its support. Integrating by parts across the line requires local absolute continuity. The contributions from the jumps appear in the distributional derivative.
