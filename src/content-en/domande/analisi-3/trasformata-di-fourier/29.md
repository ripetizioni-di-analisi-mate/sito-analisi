With $\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-i\xi x}\,dx$, what is the transform of $f(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\,e^{-|x|}$?

- $-\dfrac{2i\xi}{1+\xi^2}$
- $\dfrac{2i\xi}{1+\xi^2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{1+\xi^2}$
- $-\dfrac{2\xi}{1+\xi^2}$

## Explanation

We write $f(x)=e^{-x}\mathbf 1_{[0,\infty)}(x)-e^{x}\mathbf 1_{(-\infty,0]}(x)$ and transform the two terms separately.
The first term gives $\int_0^\infty e^{-(1+i\xi)x}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{1+i\xi}$.
The second gives $\int_{-\infty}^0e^{(1-i\xi)x}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{1-i\xi}$, because the real part of the coefficient is positive and the antiderivative tends to zero as $x\to-\infty$.
Hence
$$
\widehat f(\xi)=\frac{1}{1+i\xi}-\frac{1}{1-i\xi}=\frac{(1-i\xi)-(1+i\xi)}{1+\xi^2}=-\frac{2i\xi}{1+\xi^2}.
$$
The result is purely imaginary and odd, as the transform of a real odd function must be, while $\dfrac{2}{1+\xi^2}$ is the transform of the even function $e^{-|x|}$.
