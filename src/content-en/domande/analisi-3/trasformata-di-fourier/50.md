Let $f\in L^1$ and $x^2f\in L^1$. What is the second derivative of $F=\widehat f$ at zero frequency?

- $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R}x^2f(x)\,dx$
- $-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R}x^2f(x)\,dx$
- $-i\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R}x^2f(x)\,dx$
- $0$ for every $f$

## Explanation

Differentiating the kernel twice gives the square of $-ix$, namely $-x^2$. The second-moment hypothesis supplies an integrable bound. At zero frequency the exponential factor is one.
