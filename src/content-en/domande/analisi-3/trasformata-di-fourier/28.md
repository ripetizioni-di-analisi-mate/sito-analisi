For $f(x)=e^{-2x}\mathbf1_{(-\infty,0]}(x)$, can we use the integral definition at zero frequency?

- Yes, and the integral equals $\dfrac12$
- Yes, and the integral equals $0$
- Yes, and the integral equals $-\dfrac12$
- No, because the function grows on the negative half-line

## Explanation

As $x$ tends to minus infinity, the exponent $-2x$ tends to plus infinity. At zero frequency the kernel is one, so the resulting integral of a positive function growing toward that end diverges.
