How does $e^{\frac13 L(z)}$ change when the branch $L$ is replaced by $L+2\pi i$?

- It stays the same.
- It is multiplied by $-1$.
- It is multiplied by $e^{\frac{2\pi i}{3}}$.
- It increases by $2\pi i$.

## Explanation

The new exponent is $\dfrac13 L(z)+\dfrac{2\pi i}{3}$. By the exponential law the sum of exponents gives the product of the two exponentials. The additional factor is therefore a cube root of unity distinct from one.
