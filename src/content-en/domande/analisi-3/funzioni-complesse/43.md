For $f(z)=z^2+\overline z$, where do the Cauchy–Riemann equations hold?

- Throughout the plane.
- On the real axis.
- Only at zero.
- Nowhere.

## Explanation

The differential of $z^2$ is complex linear everywhere, while that of the conjugate term always contains $\overline h$ with coefficient one. The conjugate component is therefore nonzero everywhere, preventing the Cauchy–Riemann equations from holding.
