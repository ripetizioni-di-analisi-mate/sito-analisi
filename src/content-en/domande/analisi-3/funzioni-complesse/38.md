Consider the image of the line $z(t)=t+2it$, with $t\in\mathbb R$, under $w=e^{z}$. When $t$ increases so that the point $w(t)$ makes exactly one turn around the origin, by which factor is $|w(t)|$ multiplied?

- $e^{2\pi}$
- $e$
- $e^{\pi}$
- $1$

## Explanation

Since $w(t)=e^{t}e^{2it}$, the modulus of $w(t)$ is $e^{t}$ and one of its arguments is $2t$.
A full turn around the origin corresponds to an increase of the argument by $2\pi$, hence to an increase of $t$ by $\pi$.
Therefore the modulus goes from $e^{t}$ to $e^{t+\pi}$, that is, it is multiplied by $e^{\pi}$, whatever the starting point.
The factor $e^{2\pi}$ comes from confusing the increase of the argument with the increase of $t$.
