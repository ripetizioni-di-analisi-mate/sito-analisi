For $u(x,y)=e^x\cos y$, which $v$ makes $u+iv$ holomorphic and satisfies $v(0,0)=0$?

- $e^x\sin y$
- $-e^x\sin y$
- $e^{-x}\sin y$
- $e^x\sin y+1$

## Explanation

Euler's formula identifies $u$ as the real part of the holomorphic function $e^{x+iy}$. Its imaginary part is $e^x\sin y$ and vanishes at the origin. On the connected plane all conjugates differ by a constant, so this normalization selects exactly that one.
