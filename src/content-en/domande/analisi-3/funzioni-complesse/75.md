For $u(x,y)=e^x\cos y$, which $v$ makes $u+iv$ holomorphic on $\mathbb C$ and satisfies $v(0,0)=0$?

- $e^x\sin y$
- $-e^x\sin y$
- $e^{-x}\sin y$
- $e^x\sin y+1$

## Explanation

By Euler's formula $e^{x+iy}=e^x\cos y+ie^x\sin y$, hence $u$ is the real part of the entire function $e^{z}$.
Therefore $e^x\sin y$ is a harmonic conjugate of $u$, and it vanishes at the origin.
Since the plane is connected, two harmonic conjugates of $u$ differ by a constant, and the condition $v(0,0)=0$ makes this constant equal to zero.
Hence $v(x,y)=e^x\sin y$.
The function $-e^x\sin y$ comes from a sign error in the Cauchy-Riemann equations $u_x=v_y$ and $u_y=-v_x$.
