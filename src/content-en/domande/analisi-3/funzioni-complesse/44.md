Set $f(0)=0$ and let $f(x+iy)=\dfrac{x^2y}{x^2+y^2}$ away from the origin. Which property holds at zero?

- The Cauchy–Riemann equations hold and the complex derivative does not exist.
- The partial derivative with respect to $x$ does not exist.
- The real part is discontinuous.
- The complex derivative is zero.

## Explanation

The function vanishes on both axes, so the partial derivatives at the origin vanish and both equations hold. Along $z=t(1+i)$ the difference quotient equals $\dfrac{1}{2(1+i)}$, whereas on the real axis it is zero. The two values rule out a complex derivative.
