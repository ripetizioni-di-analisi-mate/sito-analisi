Solve $e^z=-e^{5}$ in $\mathbb C$.

- $z=5+2m\pi i,\quad m\in\mathbb Z$
- $z=-5+2m\pi i,\quad m\in\mathbb Z$
- $z=5+i(2m+1)\pi,\quad m\in\mathbb Z$
- $z=5+i\pi\quad\text{only}$

## Explanation

With $z=x+iy$, equating moduli gives $e^x=e^5$, hence $x=5$. A negative real number has argument congruent to $\pi$ modulo $2\pi$. Thus all possible imaginary parts are $(2m+1)\pi$ for integer $m$.
