Find the harmonic conjugate $v$ of $u(x,y)=5(x^2-y^2)$ on $\mathbb R^2$, subject to $v(0,0)=0$.

- $v(x,y)=5xy$
- $v(x,y)=5(x^2+y^2)$
- $v(x,y)=10xy$
- $v(x,y)=-10xy$

## Explanation

The two pure second derivatives of $u$ are negatives of each other, so $u$ is harmonic. Integrating $v_y=u_x=10x$ with $x$ fixed gives $v=10xy+C(x)$. The equation $v_x=-u_y=10y$ gives $C'(x)=0$. The plane is connected, so $C$ is constant. The condition $v(0,0)=0$ determines the constant and gives $v=10xy$.
