For $u(t,x)=\cos t\sin x$ on $(0,\pi)$ with $c=1$, what is the total energy $\dfrac{1}{2}\int_0^\pi(u_t^2+u_x^2)\,dx$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$
- $0$

## Explanation

The solution is smooth and satisfies the equation with fixed ends, so its energy can be evaluated at time zero. At that instant the velocity is zero and the spatial derivative is $\cos x$. The integral of $\cos^2 x$ is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
