An infinite string vibrates according to $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$, with zero initial displacement and continuous initial velocity $u_t(0,x)=g(x)$. It is known that $u(t,0)=0$ for every $t>0$. Which relation follows for $g$?

- $g(t)=0$ for every $t>0$.
- $g(t)=g(-t)$ for every $t>0$.
- $\int_0^t g(s)\,ds=0$ for every $t>0$.
- $g(t)+g(-t)=0$ for every $t>0$.

## Explanation

Since the initial displacement is zero, d'Alembert's formula gives $u(t,x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{x-t}^{x+t}g(s)\,ds$.
At $x=0$ the hypothesis therefore becomes $\int_{-t}^{t}g(s)\,ds=0$ for every $t>0$.
Since $g$ is continuous, we can differentiate with respect to $t$ by the fundamental theorem of calculus, and we obtain $g(t)+g(-t)=0$ for every $t>0$.
Hence $g$ is odd.
The other relations do not follow, because $g(x)=x$ satisfies the hypothesis, is not zero for $t>0$ and has $\int_0^tg(s)\,ds=\dfrac{t^2}{2}\neq0$.
