An infinite string satisfies $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$, with zero initial displacement and continuous initial velocity $g$. Measurements show $u(t,0)=0$ for every $t>0$. Which identity follows for $g$?

- $g(t)=0$ for every $t>0$.
- $g(t)=g(-t)$ for every $t>0$.
- $\int_0^t g(s)\,\mathrm ds=0$ for every $t>0$.
- $g(t)+g(-t)=0$ for every $t>0$.

## Explanation

D’Alembert’s formula gives $2u(t,0)=\int_{-t}^{t}g(s)\,\mathrm ds$. Differentiating with respect to $t$ adds the values at both endpoints. The identically zero measurement forces this sum to vanish.
