Seek a weak solution of $-u''=\delta_{\dfrac{1}{2}}$ on $(0,1)$ with zero endpoint values. If $u$ is continuous and affine on either side of $\dfrac{1}{2}$, what condition must the one-sided slopes satisfy?

- $u'(\dfrac{1}{2}^+)-u'(\dfrac{1}{2}^-)=1$
- $u'(\dfrac{1}{2}^-)=u'(\dfrac{1}{2}^+)$
- $u'(\dfrac{1}{2}^-)-u'(\dfrac{1}{2}^+)=1$
- $u'(\dfrac{1}{2}^-)+u'(\dfrac{1}{2}^+)=1$

## Explanation

The weak formulation is $\int_0^1u'v'\,\mathrm dx=v(\dfrac{1}{2})$. Integrating on the two intervals separately leaves the left slope minus the right slope multiplied by $v(\dfrac{1}{2})$. Its coefficient must be one.
