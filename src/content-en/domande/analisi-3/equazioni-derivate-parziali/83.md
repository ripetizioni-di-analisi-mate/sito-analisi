We look for the weak solution $u\in H_0^1(0,1)$ of $-u''=\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}$, that is, the function satisfying $\int_0^1u'v'\,dx=v\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)$ for every $v\in H_0^1(0,1)$. If $u$ is continuous and affine on each of the intervals $\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)$ and $\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)$, with slopes $m_-$ and $m_+$, which condition do the slopes satisfy?

- $m_+-m_-=1$
- $m_-=m_+$
- $m_--m_+=1$
- $m_-+m_+=1$

## Explanation

On the first interval $u'=m_-$, hence $\int_0^{\frac12}u'v'\,dx=m_-\left(v\left(\frac12\right)-v(0)\right)=m_-\,v\left(\frac12\right)$, because $v(0)=0$.
On the second interval, for the same reason, $\int_{\frac12}^1u'v'\,dx=m_+\left(v(1)-v\left(\frac12\right)\right)=-m_+\,v\left(\frac12\right)$.
Adding up we get $(m_--m_+)\,v\left(\frac12\right)=v\left(\frac12\right)$ for every $v\in H_0^1(0,1)$, hence $m_--m_+=1$.
The solution is therefore $u(x)=\dfrac{x}{2}$ for $x\le\dfrac12$ and $u(x)=\dfrac{1-x}{2}$ for $x\ge\dfrac12$, with $m_-=\dfrac12$ and $m_+=-\dfrac12$.
The condition $m_+-m_-=1$ comes from a sign error in the second integral.
