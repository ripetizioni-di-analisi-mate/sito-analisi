For a classical solution of the heat equation with zero boundary values, let $E(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int u(t,x)^2\,dx$. If $E(0)=0$ and $E'(t)\le0$, what follows?

- $E(t)<0$ for every $t>0$
- $u(t,x)=0$ for every $t,x$
- $u(t,x)$ can be a nonzero constant in space
- $\int u(t,x)\,dx=0$ but $E(t)>0$

## Explanation

The energy is nonnegative because it is the integral of a square. It starts at zero and is nonincreasing, so it stays zero. At each time the spatial profile is continuous, and the integral of its square can vanish only if the profile vanishes identically.
