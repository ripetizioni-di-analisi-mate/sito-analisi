For the mode $\cos(2\theta)$ on the entire disk, which property distinguishes the radial factor $r^{-2}$ from $r^2$?

- $r^{-2}$ is unbounded as the center is approached along $\theta=0$
- $r^{-2}$ vanishes on the unit circle
- $r^{-2}$ is constant along $\theta=0$
- $r^{-2}$ equals $-1$ on the unit circle

## Explanation

Both factors equal one at $r=1$, so their boundary values coincide. Along $\theta=0$ the cosine equals one and $r^{-2}$ diverges as $r$ tends to zero. Continuity at the center therefore rules out this factor.
