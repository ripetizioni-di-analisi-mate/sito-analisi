For $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $(0,\pi)$ with insulated ends and initial data $2+\cos x$, what is $\int_0^\pi u(t,x)\,dx$?

- $2\pi e^{-t}$
- $\pi$
- $0$
- $2\pi$

## Explanation

Integrating the equation over the interval gives $u_x(t,\pi)-u_x(t,0)$ for the rate of change of the total mass. Both terms vanish because the ends are insulated. The mass retains its initial value $2\pi$ since the cosine integrates to zero.
