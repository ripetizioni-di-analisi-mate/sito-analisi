On $(0,\pi)$, suppose $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$ with fixed ends and initial data $u(0,x)=\sin x$, $u_t(0,x)=\sin(2x)$. What is $u(t,\dfrac{\pi}{2})$?

- $\cos t+\dfrac{1}{2}\sin(2t)$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\sin(2t)$
- $\cos(2t)$
- $\cos t$

## Explanation

The initial displacement contributes $\cos t\sin x$, and the initial velocity contributes $\dfrac{1}{2}\sin(2t)\sin(2x)$. At the midpoint the second spatial sine vanishes, so the second mode contributes zero at every time.
