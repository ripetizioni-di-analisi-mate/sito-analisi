For $u_{tt}=4u_{xx}$ on the real line, with $u(0,x)=0$ and $u_t(0,x)=3$, what is $u(t,x)$?

- $6t$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}t$
- $3t$
- $3x$

## Explanation

The initial displacement is zero, so only the initial velocity contributes to d'Alembert's formula. The interval between the two characteristic footpoints $x-2t$ and $x+2t$ has length $4t$. The integral of the constant velocity is therefore $12t$, and multiplying by $\dfrac{1}{4}$ gives $3t$.
