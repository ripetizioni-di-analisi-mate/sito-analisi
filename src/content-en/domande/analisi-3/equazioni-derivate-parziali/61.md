If $f\in C^2(\mathbb R)$, which initial velocity produces the wave $u(t,x)=f(x-ct)$ for $c>0$?

- $-cf'(x)$
- $cf'(x)$
- $c^2f''(x)$
- $0$ for every $f$

## Explanation

Differentiating with respect to time gives the derivative of $f$ multiplied by the derivative of its argument, which is $-c$. At time zero the argument is $x$. The initial velocity is therefore $-cf'(x)$.
