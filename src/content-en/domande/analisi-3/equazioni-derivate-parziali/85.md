A function equals $x$ on $(0,\dfrac{1}{2})$ and $x+a$ on $(\dfrac{1}{2},1)$. For which $a$ can its distributional derivative on $(0,1)$ be represented by an $L^2$ function?

- $a=0$
- $a=1$
- $a=-1$
- For every real $a$.

## Explanation

The classical derivative is one on both sides. The jump adds the Dirac mass $a\delta_{\dfrac{1}{2}}$, which cannot be represented by a square-integrable function unless its coefficient vanishes.
