On $(0,1)$ let $u(x)=x$ for $0<x<\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $u(x)=x+a$ for $\dfrac{1}{2}<x<1$, with $a\in\mathbb R$. For which values of $a$ is the distributional derivative of $u$ a function in $L^2(0,1)$?

- $a=0$
- $a=-1$
- For every $a\in\mathbb R$
- For no $a\in\mathbb R$

## Explanation

For a test function $\varphi$ we split the integral at the point $\dfrac12$ and integrate by parts on each interval.
Since $u$ has classical derivative $1$ on both intervals and a jump $a$ at $\dfrac12$, we obtain $-\int_0^1u\varphi'\,dx=\int_0^1\varphi\,dx+a\,\varphi\left(\frac12\right)$.
Hence the distributional derivative is $u'=1+a\,\delta_{\frac12}$.
The Dirac delta is not represented by any locally integrable function, therefore $u'\in L^2(0,1)$ if and only if $a=0$.
The value $a=-1$ comes from imposing $u(1)=0$, a condition that concerns the space $H_0^1$ and not the existence of the weak derivative.
