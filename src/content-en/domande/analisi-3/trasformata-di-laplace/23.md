For $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, compute $\mathcal L[\sin^2t](s)$.

- $\dfrac1{(s^2+1)^2}$
- $\dfrac1{2s}+\dfrac{s}{2(s^2+4)}$
- $\dfrac2{s(s^2+4)}$
- $\dfrac{s}{2(s^2+4)}$

## Explanation

The double-angle identity expresses the squared sine as half the difference between one and $\cos(2t)$. Evaluate both transforms separately. With a common denominator the remaining numerator is two.
