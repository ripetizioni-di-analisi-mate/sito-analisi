For $F(s)=\dfrac{2}{s^2+9}$, why does the limit of $sF(s)$ at zero fail to give a final value?

- The product $sF(s)$ has a pole at zero.
- The inverse transform grows exponentially.
- $sF(s)$ has poles on the imaginary axis.
- The limit of $sF(s)$ diverges.

## Explanation

The inverse transform is a nonzero multiple of the sine of angular frequency three and keeps oscillating. The product $sF(s)$ has two purely imaginary poles, violating the hypothesis of the final value theorem.
