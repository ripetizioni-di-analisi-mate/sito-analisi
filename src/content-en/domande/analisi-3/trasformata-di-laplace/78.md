A causal signal $f$ has a continuous derivative on $[0,+\infty)$, and $f$ and $f'$ are of exponential order. As $s\to+\infty$ the transform of $f$ satisfies $F(s)=\dfrac{3}{s}-\dfrac{4}{s^2}+O\left(\dfrac{1}{s^3}\right)$. What is $f'(0^+)$?

- $3$
- $-4$
- $-1$
- $0$

## Explanation

By the initial value theorem $f(0^+)=\lim_{s\to+\infty}sF(s)=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\left(3-\dfrac4s+O\left(\dfrac{1}{s^2}\right)\right)=3$.
The transform of the derivative is therefore $\mathcal L[f'](s)=sF(s)-f(0^+)=-\dfrac{4}{s}+O\left(\dfrac{1}{s^2}\right)$.
Applying the initial value theorem again, this time to $f'$, we obtain $f'(0^+)=\lim_{s\to+\infty}s\,\mathcal L[f'](s)=-4$.
The value $3$ is $f(0^+)$, and the value $-1$ comes from adding the two coefficients of the expansion.
