For which value of $a\in\mathbb R$ does the causal inverse transform of $F(s)=\dfrac{s+a}{(s+1)(s+3)}$ contain no terms proportional to $e^{-3t}$?

- $a=3$
- $a=1$
- $a=-3$
- $a=0$

## Explanation

By partial fractions we write $F(s)=\dfrac{A}{s+1}+\dfrac{B}{s+3}$, so the causal inverse transform is $Ae^{-t}+Be^{-3t}$.
The coefficient $B$ is the residue at the simple pole $s=-3$, that is, $B=\dfrac{-3+a}{-3+1}=\dfrac{3-a}{2}$.
Therefore $B=0$ if and only if $a=3$.
In this case the factor $s+3$ cancels, $F(s)=\dfrac{1}{s+1}$ and the inverse transform is $e^{-t}$.
The value $a=1$ instead makes the coefficient $A$ vanish, that is, the term $e^{-t}$.
