Set $F(s)=\dfrac{1}{s + 3}$. What is the unilateral transform of $t^2f(t)$ if $F=\mathcal L[f]$?

- $\dfrac{1}{\left(s + 3\right)^{3}}$
- $\dfrac{2}{\left(s + 3\right)^{3}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\left(s + 3\right)^{2}}$
- $- \dfrac{2}{\left(s + 3\right)^{3}}$

## Explanation

Multiplication by $t$ corresponds to differentiating with respect to $s$ and reversing the sign. Applying this twice gives positive overall sign. The second derivative of the reciprocal of $s+3$ has numerator two and denominator $(s+3)^3$.
