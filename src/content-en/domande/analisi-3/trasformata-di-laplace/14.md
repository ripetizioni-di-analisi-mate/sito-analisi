Let $F=\mathcal L[f]$ and assume $\int_0^\infty e^{-2t}|f(t)|\,dt<\infty$. What justifies differentiating $F$ at $s=3$?

- The derivative of $f$ must vanish
- The extra exponential damping controls the factor $t$ in the differentiated kernel
- The factor $t$ is bounded on the half-line
- The condition $F(3)=0$ is required

## Explanation

On the disk of radius one half centered at three, the real part of the parameter is at least five halves. The product of $t$ and $e^{-\frac{t}{2}}$ is bounded, so the differentiated integrand is dominated by a constant times the given integrable function.
