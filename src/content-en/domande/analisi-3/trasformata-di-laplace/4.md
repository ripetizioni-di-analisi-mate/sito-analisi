Why does $F(s)=\dfrac1{s-2}$, the transform of $e^{2t}$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>2$, fail to give the Laplace integral at $s=1$?

- The formula has a pole at $s=1$
- At $s=1$ the integrand is $e^{-t}$
- The transform of an exponential exists only for real $s$
- At $s=1$ the integrand is $e^t$ and its integral diverges

## Explanation

Substituting one in the kernel gives $e^{-t}e^{2t}=e^t$, whose integral over the half-line diverges. The rational formula has a finite value there because it continues the transform beyond the domain of the integral.
