What is the causal inverse transform of $\dfrac{s+2}{(s+2)^2+1}+\dfrac2{(s+2)^2+1}$?

- $e^{-2t}(\cos t+2\sin t)$
- $e^{-2t}(2\cos t+\sin t)$
- $e^{-2t}(\cos t-2\sin t)$
- $e^{2t}(\cos t+2\sin t)$

## Explanation

The term with numerator $s+2$ is the frequency-shifted transform of a cosine of unit angular frequency. The constant-numerator term is twice the transform of a sine at the same frequency. The shift multiplies both time-domain terms by $e^{-2t}$.
