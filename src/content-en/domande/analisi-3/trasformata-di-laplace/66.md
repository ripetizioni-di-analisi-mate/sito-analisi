Which equation does $Y(s)=\mathcal L[y](s)$ satisfy if $y''+y=1$, $y(0)=0$, and $y'(0)=2$?

- $(s^2+1)Y(s)=\dfrac1s-2$
- $(s^2+1)Y(s)=\dfrac1s+2$
- $(s^2+1)Y(s)=\dfrac1s+2s$
- $(s^2-1)Y(s)=\dfrac1s+2$

## Explanation

The second derivative transform contains $-y'(0)$, equal here to minus two, and $-sy(0)$, equal to zero. The constant forcing has transform $\dfrac1s$. Moving the initial term to the other side gives the stated right-hand side.
