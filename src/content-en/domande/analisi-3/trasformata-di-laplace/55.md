What is the causal inverse transform of $F(s)=\dfrac1{s^2(s+1)}$?

- $t+1-e^{-t}$
- $1-e^{-t}$
- $t-1+e^{-t}$
- $t e^{-t}$

## Explanation

There is a double pole at zero and a simple pole at minus one. Partial fractions give $\dfrac1{s^2}-\dfrac1s+\dfrac1{s+1}$. Inverting term by term gives a ramp, a negative constant, and a decaying exponential.
