A bounded linear operator $A$ on a Banach space satisfies $A^3=0$ and $\|A\|=4$. Which operator is the inverse of $I-A$?

- $I-A+A^2$
- $I+A$
- $I+A+\dfrac{A^2}{2}$
- $I+A+A^2$

## Explanation

Since $\|A\|=4>1$, the Neumann series theorem does not apply, so the product is checked directly.
We have $(I-A)(I+A+A^2)=I+A+A^2-A-A^2-A^3=I-A^3$.
Since $A^3=0$, the product equals $I$.
In the same way $(I+A+A^2)(I-A)=I-A^3=I$, hence $I+A+A^2$ is the inverse of $I-A$.
All the terms of the Neumann series $\sum_{n\ge0}A^n$ from $A^3$ on vanish, so the series coincides with this finite sum even though $\|A\|>1$.
