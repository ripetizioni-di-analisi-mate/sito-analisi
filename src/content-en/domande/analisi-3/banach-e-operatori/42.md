For $(Tx)_n=\dfrac{x_n}{n}$ on $\ell^2$, consider $T^{-1}$ defined only on the range of $T$, with the induced norm. Which sequence proves that this inverse is unbounded?

- $y_n=e_1$, with $T^{-1}y_n=e_1$
- $y_n=\dfrac{e_n}{n}$, with $T^{-1}y_n=e_n$
- $y_n=\dfrac{e_1}{n}$, with $T^{-1}y_n=\dfrac{e_1}{n}$
- $y_n=ne_1$, with $T^{-1}y_n=ne_1$

## Explanation

The vector $\dfrac{e_n}{n}$ is in the range because it is the image under $T$ of $e_n$. Its norm tends to zero, while its preimage has norm one. The ratio of the norms of preimage to image therefore grows as $n$.
