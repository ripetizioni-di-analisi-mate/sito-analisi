Let $P$ be a nonzero bounded linear projection and let $T=I+\dfrac{1}{2}P$. Which expression is the inverse of $T$?

- $I-\dfrac{1}{2}P$
- $I-\dfrac{1}{3}P$
- $I+\dfrac{1}{3}P$
- $I+\dfrac{3}{2}P$

## Explanation

Since $P^2=P$, the product $(I+\dfrac{1}{2}P)(I+cP)$ equals $I+(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{3}{2}c)P$. Taking $c=-\dfrac{1}{3}$ makes the coefficient vanish. The factors commute because they are polynomials in $P$, so multiplying in the opposite order also gives the identity. Since the projection is nonzero, the inverse acts on its range by multiplication by two thirds.
