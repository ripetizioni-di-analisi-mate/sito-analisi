In Euclidean $\mathbb R^2$, let $M$ be the line spanned by $(1,1)$ and let $\ell(t,t)=t$. Which extension preserves the norm of $\ell$?

- $\widetilde\ell(x,y)=\dfrac{x+y}{2}$
- $\widetilde\ell(x,y)=x$
- $\widetilde\ell(x,y)=x+y$
- $\widetilde\ell(x,y)=\dfrac{x-y}{2}$

## Explanation

On the line the ratio of absolute value to norm is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$. The functional with coefficients $\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{1}{2}$ agrees with $\ell$ for $x=y$ and has norm $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{4}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. It therefore preserves the norm. Other coefficients may give the correct values on the line without preserving the norm.
