Let $(f_n)$ be Cauchy in $C^1([0,1])$ for the norm $\|f\|_\infty+\|f'\|_\infty$. If $f_n\to f$ and $f_n'\to g$ uniformly, which identity proves that the limit belongs to the space?

- $f(x)=g(x)$
- $g(x)=f(x)-f(0)$
- $f(x)=f(0)+\int_0^x g(t)\,dt$
- $f(x)=f(0)+xg(x)$

## Explanation

For each $n$, the difference $f_n(x)-f_n(0)$ equals the integral of $f_n'$ between zero and $x$. Uniform convergence allows passage to the limit under the integral, giving the identity with $g$. Since $g$ is continuous, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives $f'=g$.
