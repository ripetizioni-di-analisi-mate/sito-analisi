On a nonempty complete metric space a map $T:X\to X$ has the property that $T^2$ is a contraction. If $p$ is the unique fixed point of $T^2$, which step proves that $T$ has the same fixed point?

- $T^2p=p$ implies $T$ is the identity on the whole space.
- $T^2(Tp)=T(T^2p)=Tp$, so uniqueness gives $Tp=p$.
- The Lipschitz constant of $T$ must be the square root of that of $T^2$.
- Every orbit of $T$ must become constant after two steps.

## Explanation

Composing $T$ with $T^2$ in either order gives the same map. Thus $Tp$ is another fixed point of $T^2$ and equals $p$. Any fixed point of $T$ is also fixed by $T^2$, proving uniqueness.
