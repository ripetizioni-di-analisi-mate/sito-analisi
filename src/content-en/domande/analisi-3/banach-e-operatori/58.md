Which sequence of points shows that the family $f_n(x)=x^n$ fails to be equicontinuous at $x=1$?

- $x_n=1$, for which $|f_n(1)-f_n(x_n)|=0$
- $x_n=2^{-\dfrac{1}{n}}$, for which $|f_n(1)-f_n(x_n)|=\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x_n=\dfrac{1}{2}$, for which $|f_n(1)-f_n(x_n)|\to1$
- $x_n=2^{\dfrac{1}{n}}$, for which $|f_n(1)-f_n(x_n)|=1$

## Explanation

The sequence $2^{-\dfrac{1}{n}}$ lies in the domain and tends to one. Yet its $n$th power is always one half, while $f_n(1)=1$. Thus points arbitrarily close to one give a fixed positive difference in the function values.
