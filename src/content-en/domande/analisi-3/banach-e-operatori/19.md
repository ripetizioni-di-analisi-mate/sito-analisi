On $C([0,1])$ with the uniform norm consider the operator $Tf(x)=f(x)-f(0)$. What is $\|T\|$?

- $0$
- $1$
- $2$
- The operator is not bounded.

## Explanation

For every $x\in[0,1]$ we have $|Tf(x)|\le|f(x)|+|f(0)|\le2\|f\|_\infty$, therefore $\|T\|\le2$.
The function $f(t)=1-2t$ has uniform norm one and takes the value $1$ at $0$ and $-1$ at $1$.
For this function $Tf(x)=-2x$, so $\|Tf\|_\infty=2$ while $\|f\|_\infty=1$.
Hence the bound is attained and $\|T\|=2$.
Testing with $f=1$ and with $f(t)=t$ gives only $0$ and $1$, because in those cases $f(x)$ and $f(0)$ never have opposite signs.
