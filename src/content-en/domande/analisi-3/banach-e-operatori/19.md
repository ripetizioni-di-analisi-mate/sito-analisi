On $C([0,1])$ with the supremum norm let $Tf(x)=\int_0^1(2t-1)f(t)\,\mathrm dt$, constant in $x$. The norm of $T$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Why does no continuous unit function attain it?

- The kernel vanishes everywhere on the interval.
- Every continuous function on $[0,1]$ has constant sign.
- Equality would require opposite values on either side of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and modulus one almost everywhere.
- The operator is unbounded.

## Explanation

The integral of $|2t-1|$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. To attain it, the phase of $(2t-1)f(t)$ must be constant almost everywhere. This forces a jump at $t=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Continuous transitions on shrinking intervals approximate the jump and approach the norm.
