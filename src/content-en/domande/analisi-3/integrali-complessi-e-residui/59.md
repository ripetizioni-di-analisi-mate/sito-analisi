What type of singularity does $f(z)=\dfrac1{\sin\!\left(\dfrac1z\right)}$ have at zero?

- An isolated essential singularity.
- A pole of order one.
- A nonisolated singularity.
- A removable singularity.

## Explanation

The denominator vanishes at $z=\dfrac1{k\pi}$ for nonzero integers $k$. The reciprocal has a pole at each such point. These points approach zero, so every punctured disk about zero contains other singularities.
