In the proof of Goursat's lemma, why does the integral of the affine part of $f$ around the triangle vanish?

- The affine part has a polynomial antiderivative.
- The affine part must vanish identically.
- The boundary has zero length.
- The derivatives along the three sides are equal.

## Explanation

The affine function $f(a)+f'(a)(z-a)$ is a polynomial in $z$. It has an antiderivative on the entire plane, so the integral around the closed boundary is the difference of two equal values.
