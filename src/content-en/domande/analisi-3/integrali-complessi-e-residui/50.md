A function $f$ is holomorphic in the annuli $0<|z|<1$ and $1<|z|<3$, and on the circle $|z|=1$ it has a single singular point, a pole with residue $-2$. We compare the Laurent expansions of $f$ centered at $0$ in the two annuli. How does the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ change when passing from the first annulus to the second?

- It increases by $2$.
- It decreases by $2$.
- It stays the same.
- It decreases by $4\pi i$.

## Explanation

In each annulus the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ is $c_{-1}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=r}f(z)\,dz$, where the circle of radius $r$ lies in the annulus and is traversed counterclockwise.
Take two radii $r_1<1<r_2$, the first in the inner annulus and the second in the outer one.
The difference $\oint_{|z|=r_2}f\,dz-\oint_{|z|=r_1}f\,dz$ is the integral over the boundary of the annulus $r_1<|z|<r_2$, which contains only the pole, so by the residue theorem it equals $2\pi i\cdot(-2)$.
Dividing by $2\pi i$ we find that the coefficient in the second annulus is that of the first decreased by $2$.
The value $4\pi i$ comes from not dividing by $2\pi i$.
