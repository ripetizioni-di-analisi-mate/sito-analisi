A function has two Laurent series about zero, one for $0<|z|<1$ and one for $1<|z|<3$. When a circle is expanded from the first annulus into the second, the only singularity crossed is a pole of residue $-2$. How does the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ change?

- It increases by $2$.
- It decreases by $2$.
- It stays the same because the centre is unchanged.
- It is multiplied by $-2$.

## Explanation

On each annulus the coefficient is the integral around a positively oriented circle divided by $2\pi i$. Subtracting the inner integral from the outer one gives $2\pi i$ times the residue of the crossed pole. The change is $-2$.
