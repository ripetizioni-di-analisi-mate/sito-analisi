Evaluate $\int_{\mathbb R}\dfrac{x\sin x}{x^2+1}\,dx$ as an improper integral.

- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{e}$
- $\dfrac{2 \pi}{e}$
- $- \dfrac{\pi}{e}$

## Explanation

Consider $\dfrac{ze^{iz}}{z^2+1}$ in the upper half-plane. The rational factor tends uniformly to zero on the semicircle, so Jordan's lemma gives a vanishing arc integral in the limit. The pole $i$ has residue $\dfrac{e^{-1}}2$. The required integral is the imaginary part. Improper convergence follows from Dirichlet's test because $\dfrac{x}{x^2+1}$ decreases to zero for $x>1$ and the integrand is even.
