What type of singularity does $\dfrac{e^{\frac1{z-5}}}{z-5}$ have at $z=5$?

- Essential.
- Removable.
- A pole of order one.
- A pole of order two.

## Explanation

The exponential expansion around five contains nonzero terms with arbitrarily negative exponents. Division by $z-5$ lowers each exponent by one, so the Laurent series still has infinitely many terms in its principal part.
