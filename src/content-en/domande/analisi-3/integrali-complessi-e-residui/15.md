Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(x^{2} + 4\right)^{2}}\,dx$.

- $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{32}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{16}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

In the upper half-plane $2i$ is a double pole. Its residue is the derivative of $(z+2i)^{-2}$ evaluated at $2i$, equal to $-\dfrac{i}{32}$. The arc contribution vanishes in the limit, so the residue theorem gives $\dfrac{\pi}{16}$.
