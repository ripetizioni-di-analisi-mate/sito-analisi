In the proof of Rouché's theorem using $F_t=f+tg$, what property makes the zero count independent of $t$?

- It is a continuous integer-valued function on an interval.
- It is a differentiable complex-valued function.
- It is an increasing function of $t$.
- It is bounded on a finite parameter set.

## Explanation

The strict inequality prevents $F_t$ from vanishing on the boundary. Thus the integral of the logarithmic derivative depends continuously on $t$. Divided by $2\pi i$, it equals the integer zero count and hence is constant.
