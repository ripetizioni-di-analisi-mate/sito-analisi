Consider the system
$$
\begin{cases}
xy=2x,\\
x+y=3.
\end{cases}
$$
What is its set of real solutions?

- $\{(1,2)\}$
- $\{(0,3)\}$
- $\{(1,2),(3,0)\}$
- $\{(0,3),(1,2)\}$

## Explanation

The first equation is equivalent to $xy-2x=0$, that is, $x(y-2)=0$, so $x=0$ or $y=2$. If $x=0$, the second equation gives $y=3$. If $y=2$, the second equation gives $x=1$. Both pairs satisfy the two equations, therefore the solution set is $\{(0,3),(1,2)\}$. Dividing the first equation by $x$ would lose the pair $(0,3)$, because the division requires $x\ne0$.
