Which equality holds for all real numbers $a$ and $b$?

- $(a+b)^2=a^2+b^2$
- $(a-b)^3=(b-a)^3$
- $(a-b)^2=(b-a)^2$
- $(a-b)(a+b)=a^2+b^2$

## Explanation

We have $b-a=-(a-b)$, and a number and its opposite have the same square. Hence $(b-a)^2=(a-b)^2$ for all $a$ and $b$. The other equalities do not hold in general. For example, with $a=2$ and $b=1$ we get $(a+b)^2=9$ versus $a^2+b^2=5$, $(a-b)^3=1$ versus $(b-a)^3=-1$, and $(a-b)(a+b)=3$ versus $5$.
