Solve $\dfrac1{x+2}<-1$ on its domain.

- $[-3,-2)$
- $(-2,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-3)$
- $(-3,-2)$

## Explanation

Moving all terms to the left gives $\dfrac{x+3}{x+2}<0$. The quotient is negative when the factors have opposite signs, so $-3<x<-2$. The first endpoint gives equality and the second makes the denominator zero.
