Marta says: ‘If a restaurant uses only fresh ingredients, its customers come back. Customers of the Gallo trattoria come back, so the Gallo trattoria uses only fresh ingredients.’ What error does Marta's reasoning contain?

- It extends to every restaurant a fact about a single trattoria.
- It treats a sufficient condition as if it were necessary.
- It compares absolute numbers of customers instead of proportions.
- It applies the rule to a place that falls outside its scope.

## Explanation

The rule has the form $P\implies Q$, with $P$ ‘the restaurant uses only fresh ingredients’ and $Q$ ‘customers come back’. Fresh ingredients are therefore a **sufficient** condition for customers to return. Marta starts from $Q$ and concludes $P$, that is, she reasons as if customers could come back only because of fresh ingredients. The rule instead leaves open the possibility that customers return for other reasons, such as prices or location. The reasoning does not generalize, uses no numbers and concerns a restaurant to which the rule applies.
