During a town council meeting, a councilor states: ‘Either the town hires new drivers, or the evening bus lines will be cut. Nobody wants to cut the evening lines, so the town must hire new drivers.’ What is the weakness of the reasoning?

- It treats a necessary condition as if it were sufficient.
- It extends to every line a fact about a single line.
- It confuses an absolute number with a proportion.
- It presents two alternatives as the only possible ones without justifying this.

## Explanation

The reasoning has the form ‘$P$ or $Q$; $Q$ is to be avoided; therefore $P$’. This deduction is valid only if $P$ and $Q$ really exhaust the possibilities. The councilor does not justify this, and other solutions remain possible, such as different shifts for the drivers already employed or reduced evening service. The weakness therefore lies in **presenting two alternatives as the only ones**. The reasoning contains no necessary conditions, no generalization from a single line and no numerical data.
