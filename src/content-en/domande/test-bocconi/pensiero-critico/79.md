Cities in a region with more bookstores per inhabitant record fewer road accidents per inhabitant. A regional councilor proposes funding new bookstores to reduce accidents. What is the weakness of the councilor's reasoning?

- It compares absolute values instead of values per inhabitant.
- It uses data from cities that belong to different regions.
- It infers a causal relation from a mere association between two sets of data.
- It treats a sufficient condition as if it were necessary.

## Explanation

The data show that the two phenomena occur together in the same cities. The councilor infers that bookstores reduce accidents, thus interpreting the association as a causal relation. A third factor, such as pedestrian historic centers or more public transport, could explain both sets of data. The proposal therefore rests on **a causal link that the data do not show**. The data are already per inhabitant and concern cities in a single region.
