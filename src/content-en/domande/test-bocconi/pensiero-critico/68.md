A bicycle shop asks its customers how they get to work, and nine out of ten answer that they ride a bicycle. A local newspaper concludes that nine out of ten workers in the city cycle to work. What error does the newspaper's conclusion contain?

- It extends to all workers in the city a result obtained from a group that does not represent them.
- It treats a necessary condition as if it were sufficient.
- It adds two overlapping groups of people.
- It mistakes the cause of a phenomenon for its effect.

## Explanation

The survey concerns the customers of a bicycle shop, a group in which cyclists are more common than in the rest of the city. The observed proportion therefore holds for that group. The newspaper attributes it to every worker in the city, that is, it applies the figure to a group different from the one observed. The error therefore consists in **extending the result to a population that the sample does not represent**. The reasoning contains no implications, sums of groups or cause-and-effect relations.
