A high school started an optional afternoon math course. At the end of the year, the math average of students enrolled in the course rose more than that of the other students. The principal concludes that the course raised the grades. Which piece of information most weakens the conclusion?

- Almost all students enrolled in the course had started private math lessons in the same period.
- The course met two afternoons a week.
- Enrolled students rated the course's lessons as useful.
- In the other subjects, the average of the enrolled students did not change.

## Explanation

The conclusion attributes the rise in the enrolled students' average to the course. If almost all of them were taking private math lessons in the same period, the rise has a second possible cause. In that case the data do not allow the effect of the course to be separated from that of the private lessons, so the conclusion **is weakened**. The weekly schedule and the students' ratings do not concern grades. The unchanged average in other subjects shows that the rise is limited to math and makes the conclusion more credible.
