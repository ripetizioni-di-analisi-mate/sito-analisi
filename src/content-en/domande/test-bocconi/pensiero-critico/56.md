At a music conservatory, someone wants to establish that at least one violinist also plays the piano. Which of the following pairs of facts is enough to conclude this?

- The conservatory has at least one violinist and at least one pianist.
- Every violinist at the conservatory plays the piano, and the conservatory has at least one pianist.
- Some violinists at the conservatory play the organ, and some organists at the conservatory play the piano.
- Every violinist at the conservatory plays the piano, and the conservatory has at least one violinist.

## Explanation

Let $V$ be the set of violinists at the conservatory and $P$ the set of pianists. If $V\subseteq P$ and $V$ contains at least one element, that violinist also belongs to $P$, so **the inclusion together with the existence of a violinist is enough**. The inclusion $V\subseteq P$ alone is consistent with $V$ being empty, so the existence of a pianist does not guarantee any violinist. A violinist and a pianist may be different people, so the existence of both is not enough. Finally, the violinists who play the organ and the organists who play the piano may be different people, so two statements with ‘some’ do not allow the conclusion.
