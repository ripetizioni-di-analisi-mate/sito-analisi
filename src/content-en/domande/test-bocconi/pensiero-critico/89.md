A guide claims its index is reliable “because it lists every important entry.” Asked how it identifies important entries, the author answers, “They are the ones in the index.” Why does the second statement fail to verify the first?

- It proves that there are too many index entries.
- It refutes the idea that every entry has a heading.
- It uses inclusion in the index as the sole standard for judging that same index complete.
- It compares two independently compiled lists.

## Explanation

Under that definition an omission could never be detected: an absent entry would be deemed unimportant precisely because it is absent. Importance must be assessed independently of the index.
