A movie theater lowers the ticket price from 10 euros to 8 euros. The manager predicts that ticket revenue will increase because the lower price will attract more moviegoers. Which assumption does the prediction rely on?

- The number of tickets sold will increase by more than one quarter.
- The number of tickets sold will increase by one fifth.
- The theater's staff costs will stay the same.
- Moviegoers will prefer evening screenings.

## Explanation

Let $n$ be the number of tickets sold at 10 euros and $m$ the number sold at 8 euros. Revenue increases if $8m>10n$, that is, if $m>\dfrac{10}{8}n=\dfrac{5}{4}n$. The prediction therefore requires that **ticket sales increase by more than one quarter**. With an increase of one fifth, revenue becomes $8\cdot\dfrac{6}{5}n=\dfrac{48}{5}n$, which is less than $10n$, so the prediction would be false. Staff costs and preferred screening times play no part in the revenue calculation.
