A climate chart shows for each month the mean temperature, read on the left vertical axis, and the total rainfall, read on the right vertical axis. The tick marks of the two axes are aligned and both scales start at zero: on the left each tick is worth $4\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, on the right $15\,\mathrm{mm}$. The rainfall bar reaches the sixth tick in July and the third tick in August. By how many millimetres does the rainfall decrease from July to August?

- $12\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $90\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $45\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Rainfall is read on the right axis, where each tick is worth $15\,\mathrm{mm}$. The bar is therefore worth $6\cdot 15=90\,\mathrm{mm}$ in July and $3\cdot 15=45\,\mathrm{mm}$ in August. The decrease is thus $90-45=45\,\mathrm{mm}$. The scale of the left axis refers only to temperature and plays no part in the calculation.
