A bus company wants to calculate the cost per passenger-kilometre of a route in March; one passenger-kilometre is one passenger carried for one kilometre. The company knows the operating cost of the route in March and the length of the route. Which piece of information is still needed?

- The number of trips run in March.
- The total kilometres travelled by all passengers in March.
- The number of stops on the route.
- The operating cost of the route in February.

## Explanation

The cost per passenger-kilometre is the month's cost divided by the passenger-kilometres of the same month. Passenger-kilometres are obtained by adding up the kilometres travelled by each passenger, so they equal the total kilometres travelled by the passengers in March. The number of trips, multiplied by the length of the route, gives instead the kilometres travelled by the buses, which do not depend on how many people are on board. The stops and the February cost do not enter the ratio.
