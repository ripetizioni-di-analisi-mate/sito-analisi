A neighbourhood covers $150$ hectares and has $4{,}500$ residents. One hectare equals $10{,}000\,\mathrm{m^2}$. What is the population density of the neighbourhood in inhabitants per square kilometre?

- $30$
- $3{,}000$
- $300$
- $30{,}000$

## Explanation

A square kilometre is a square with side $1{,}000\,\mathrm{m}$, so it measures $1{,}000\cdot 1{,}000=1{,}000{,}000\,\mathrm{m^2}$, that is $100$ hectares. The area of the neighbourhood is therefore $\dfrac{150}{100}=1.5\,\mathrm{km^2}$. The density is the number of residents divided by the area, which gives $\dfrac{4{,}500}{1.5}=3{,}000$ inhabitants per square kilometre.
