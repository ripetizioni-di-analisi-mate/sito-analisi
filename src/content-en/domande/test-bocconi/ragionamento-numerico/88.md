A shop records $1{,}200$ receipts in March, some of which were paid in cash. From February to March the average revenue per receipt fell. A colleague reports four results on the shop's data. Which result is certainly wrong, even without knowing any other data?

- The index of total revenue in March, with February equal to $100$, is $112$.
- The relative change in the average revenue per receipt is $-8\%$.
- The March receipts paid in cash number $480$.
- The share of March receipts paid in cash is $112\%$.

## Explanation

The receipts paid in cash are part of the $1{,}200$ March receipts, so their share lies between $0\%$ and $100\%$. Hence a share of $112\%$ is certainly wrong. Total revenue is the product of the average revenue and the number of receipts, and the number of receipts in February is not known. So total revenue can grow even if the average revenue falls, and an index of $112$ is possible. The change of $-8\%$ is negative, as a falling average revenue requires, and $480$ receipts out of $1{,}200$ are consistent with the data.
