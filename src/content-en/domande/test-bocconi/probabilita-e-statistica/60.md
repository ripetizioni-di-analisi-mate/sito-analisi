Two distinct numbers are chosen from the integers $1$ to $10$, without regard to order. Which statement about the resulting pairs is true?

- There are as many pairs with an even sum as pairs with an odd sum
- There are $25$ pairs with an even sum
- There are more pairs with an even sum than with an odd sum
- There are $25$ pairs with an odd sum, five more than those with an even sum

## Explanation

From $1$ to $10$ there are five even and five odd numbers. The sum is even when the two numbers have the same parity, so there are $\dbinom52+\dbinom52=10+10=20$ pairs with an even sum. The sum is odd when one number is even and the other odd, so there are $5\cdot5=25$ such pairs. The total $20+25=45$ agrees with $\dbinom{10}{2}$.
