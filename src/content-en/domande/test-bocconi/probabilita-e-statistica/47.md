A restaurant offers menus consisting of a first course and a main course, or of a single one-dish meal. There are three first courses, four main courses, and five one-dish meals. A student counts the possible menus with the product $3\cdot4\cdot5=60$. Which statement is correct?

- The two kinds of menu are disjoint cases, so there are $3\cdot4+5=17$ menus
- The count is correct, because each menu is a choice made in three stages
- There are $(3+4)\cdot5=35$ menus, because the one-dish meal is paired with a first or a main course
- There are $3+4+5=12$ menus, because each dish is chosen only once

## Explanation

A menu contains either a first course and a main course or a one-dish meal, and no menu is of both kinds. Menus of the first kind are counted with the multiplication rule, giving $3\cdot4=12$. There are $5$ menus of the second kind. Since the two cases are disjoint, the addition rule applies and there are $12+5=17$ menus. The product $3\cdot4\cdot5$ counts triples of a first course, a main course, and a one-dish meal, which the restaurant does not offer.
