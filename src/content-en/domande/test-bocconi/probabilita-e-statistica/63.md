A token is chosen with equal probability from one with two red faces and one with a red face and a blue face. A face chosen at random is revealed to be red. Without turning the token, two more clear photographs are taken of the face already visible. How does the probability that the token has two red faces change from the initial observation to the photographs?

- It does not change.
- It increases by $\dfrac16$.
- It increases by $\dfrac29$.
- It decreases by $\dfrac16$.

## Explanation

After observing red, two of the three compatible faces belong to the all-red token, giving probability $\dfrac23$. The photographs show the face already known to be red and are certain under either remaining hypothesis. The probability stays at $\dfrac23$.
