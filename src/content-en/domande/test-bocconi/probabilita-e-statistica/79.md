In an online store, $80\%$ of the returned products had been bought at a discount. Which data are needed, in addition to this, to compute the percentage of returns among products bought at a discount?

- The percentage of returns and the percentage of discounted purchases, both out of all purchases
- No data, because the required percentage is $80\%$
- Only the percentage of discounted purchases out of all purchases
- Only the percentage of returns out of all purchases

## Explanation

Let $R$ be the event “the product is returned” and $S$ the event “the product is bought at a discount.” The given figure is $P(S\mid R)=0.8$, whereas the required quantity is $P(R\mid S)$, which in general is different. By Bayes' rule, $P(R\mid S)=\dfrac{P(S\mid R)P(R)}{P(S)}$. Therefore both $P(R)$ and $P(S)$ are needed, and neither one alone determines the result.
