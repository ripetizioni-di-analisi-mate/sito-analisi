> In many European languages the word for mother begins with a sound made with closed lips, like the *m* in *madre*, *mother* and *Mutter*. Some scholars link this fact to the first sounds that babies are able to make.
>
> In Georgian, however, mother is *deda* and father is *mama*.

What role does the second paragraph play in relation to the first?

- It presents a case that does not follow the pattern described.
- It confirms the pattern described in the first paragraph with a new example.
- It explains why babies say *m* first.
- It lists other European languages in which the word for mother begins with *m*.

## Explanation

The first paragraph describes a pattern, the initial *m* in the word for mother, and reports an explanation for it. The second paragraph, introduced by *however*, cites a language in which mother is *deda* and the *m* appears in the word for father. The paragraph therefore presents **a case that runs against the pattern**.
