> The painter was highly regarded by the patrons of his day and worked for several of the city's families. After his death his canvases ended up in storage, and for almost two centuries no guidebook mentioned them. The artist's fortunes revived only when a scholar published the complete catalogue of his work, which led museums to put the paintings back on display.

What does the word “fortunes” mean in this passage?

- The wealth the artist accumulated from his commissions.
- The chance event that allowed the scholar to find the canvases.
- The price the canvases reached at auction.
- The attention and esteem the artist's work receives over time.

## Explanation

The artist's fortunes *revive* after two centuries in which guidebooks did not mention his canvases, and they revive thanks to a catalogue that brings the paintings back into museums. Since the artist had died long before, the word cannot refer to his earnings. In context, “fortunes” therefore means **the regard in which the work is held** by scholars and the public.
