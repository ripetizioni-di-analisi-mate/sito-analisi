> In the neighbourhoods where new metro stations have opened, apartment prices rose over the following two years. The city's report notes that the stations had been placed where many commuters already lived. Part of the increase could therefore be due to a demand for housing that already existed before the stations.

What does the report claim about the rise in prices?

- The rise could be partly due to demand that was already there.
- The new stations are the only cause of the rise.
- Prices had risen before the stations opened.
- The stations reduced the number of commuters in the neighbourhoods.

## Explanation

The report links the choice of station locations to the presence of many commuters. Since those neighbourhoods already had a demand for housing, part of the rise can be explained even without the stations. The report uses *could* and *part*, so it presents **a possible and partial cause**, without ruling out the effect of the stations.
