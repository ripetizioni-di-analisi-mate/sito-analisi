> Until the mid-fifteenth century, books in Europe were copied by hand, and copying a volume could take months. With movable-type printing, once a page had been set, many identical copies could be printed from it. The cost of typesetting was thus spread over the whole print run. For this reason printed books reached readers who could not previously have afforded a manuscript.

Which sentence best expresses the main idea of the passage?

- Printing made the lettering of books more elegant than that of manuscripts.
- After printing was invented, copyists stopped working at once.
- Printing lowered the cost of each copy, because the same typesetting served for many copies.
- Before printing, books were read only in monasteries.

## Explanation

The passage compares copying by hand, which took months per volume, with printing, which produced many copies from a single setting of type. Since the cost of typesetting was spread over the whole print run, each copy cost less, and the last sentence links this fact to the new readers. The main idea is therefore **the lower cost per copy**. The elegance of lettering, the end of copyists and reading in monasteries are topics the passage does not discuss.
