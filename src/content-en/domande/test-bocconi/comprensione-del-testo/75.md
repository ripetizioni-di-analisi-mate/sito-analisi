> A group of teachers compared two ways of correcting in-class math tests. In one class the students received only a grade, in the other they received comments on their mistakes and no grade. On the next test the second class repeated the same mistakes less often. The teachers point out that the two classes had similar results at the start of the year.

Which summary is most faithful to the passage?

- In two similar classes, comments on mistakes were followed by fewer repeated mistakes.
- Grades always harm the learning of mathematics.
- The two classes had similar results at the start of the year.
- The teachers decided to abolish grades in all classes.

## Explanation

The passage reports a comparison between two classes with similar initial results and an outcome, namely fewer repeated mistakes in the class that received comments. A faithful summary keeps **the comparison, the outcome and the restriction to the two classes** observed. The statement that grades *always* harm learning is too broad, the one about initial results is a detail, and the decision to abolish grades does not appear in the text.
