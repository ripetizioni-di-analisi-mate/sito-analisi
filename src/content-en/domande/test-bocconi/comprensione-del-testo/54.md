> A study of two hundred volunteers found that people who take a walk after dinner fall asleep, on average, a few minutes earlier than others. The result is interesting, but the sample is small and sleep times were collected through questionnaires filled in by the participants. Before drawing conclusions that apply to everyone, direct measurements and larger groups would be needed.

What attitude does the author express towards the study?

- Cautious interest, with reservations about the method.
- Unreserved enthusiasm.
- Outright rejection of the results.
- Indifference to the subject of the study.

## Explanation

The author calls the result *interesting*, thereby granting it some value. Right after, the author points out two limits of the method, the small sample and data collected through questionnaires. The last sentence calls for further checks before generalising, so the attitude is **interest combined with caution**.
