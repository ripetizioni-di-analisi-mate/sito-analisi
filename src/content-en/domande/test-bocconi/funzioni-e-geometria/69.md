The parabola $y=x^2$ and the line $y=2x+q$ are tangent. What is the value of $q$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $-4$
- $-1$

## Explanation

The common points have the same $y$-coordinate at the same $x$-coordinate, so their $x$-coordinates solve $x^2=2x+q$, that is, $x^2-2x-q=0$. A non-vertical line is tangent to the parabola when this equation has exactly one root, which is a double root, that is, when the discriminant is zero. The discriminant is $(-2)^2-4\cdot 1\cdot(-q)=4+4q$, therefore tangency requires $4+4q=0$, hence $q=-1$. With $q=-1$ the equation becomes $(x-1)^2=0$, so the line $y=2x-1$ is tangent to the parabola at the point $(1,1)$.
