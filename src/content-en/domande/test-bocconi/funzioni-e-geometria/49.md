On which set does the graph of $f(x)=\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}$ lie strictly below the $x$-axis?

- $-1<x<2$
- $x<-1$ or $x>2$
- $-1<x\leq 2$
- $x<2$

## Explanation

The graph lies below the $x$-axis where $f(x)<0$, that is, where the numerator and the denominator have opposite signs. For $x<-1$ both are negative and for $x>2$ both are positive. For $-1<x<2$ the numerator is negative and the denominator is positive, so $f(x)<0$ exactly on this interval. At $x=2$ the function equals $0$, therefore that point lies on the axis and is excluded.
