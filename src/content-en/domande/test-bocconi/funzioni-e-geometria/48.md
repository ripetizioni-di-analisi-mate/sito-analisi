The function $f(x)=x^3-3x$ is increasing for $x\leq -1$ and for $x\geq 1$ and decreasing between $-1$ and $1$, as the graph shows. How many real solutions does the equation $f(x)=1$ have?

::grafico{f="x^3-3*x" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.6 ymin=-3.5 ymax=3.5 punti="-2,-2,pieno;-1,2,pieno;1,-2,pieno;2,2,pieno" legenda="y=x^3-3x" titolo="The graph passes through $(-2,-2)$, $(-1,2)$, $(1,-2)$ and $(2,2)$."}

- $1$
- $2$
- $3$
- $0$

## Explanation

The solutions of $f(x)=1$ are the $x$-coordinates of the points where the graph meets the line $y=1$. On $x\leq -1$ the function is increasing and goes from $f(-2)=-2$ to $f(-1)=2$, so it takes the value $1$ exactly once. Between $-1$ and $1$ it is decreasing and goes down from $2$ to $-2$, therefore it takes that value a second time. For $x\geq 1$ it is increasing and rises from $f(1)=-2$ to $f(2)=2$, hence it takes that value a third time. So there are $3$ solutions.
