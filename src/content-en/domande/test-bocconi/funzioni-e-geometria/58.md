The graph of $y=\sqrt x$ is shifted $3$ units to the left and $2$ units down. What is the equation of the resulting curve?

- $y=\sqrt{x-3}-2$
- $y=\sqrt{x+3}+2$
- $y=\sqrt{x+3}-2$
- $y=\sqrt{x-2}+3$

## Explanation

The graph of $f(x-h)+k$ is obtained from that of $f$ by a shift of $h$ horizontally and $k$ vertically. The shift of $3$ to the left corresponds to $h=-3$, so the argument of the square root becomes $x+3$. The shift of $2$ down corresponds to $k=-2$. Therefore the curve is $y=\sqrt{x+3}-2$, which starts at the point $(-3,-2)$.
