Among the following functions defined on $\mathbb R$, which one has a graph symmetric about the vertical axis?

- $f(x)=x|x|$
- $f(x)=x^2+|x|$
- $f(x)=x^3+x^2$
- $f(x)=(x-1)^2$

## Explanation

The graph is symmetric about the vertical axis when $f(-x)=f(x)$ for every $x$. For $f(x)=x^2+|x|$ we have $(-x)^2+|-x|=x^2+|x|$, so the condition is satisfied. For $f(x)=x|x|$ we get $(-x)|-x|=-x|x|$, therefore the graph is symmetric about the origin. The function $x^3+x^2$ equals $2$ at $x=1$ and $0$ at $x=-1$, while $(x-1)^2$ equals $0$ at $x=1$ and $4$ at $x=-1$. Therefore neither of them has this symmetry.
