For which real numbers $x$ is the expression $\dfrac{\sqrt{x+2}}{x^2-x}$ defined?

- $(-2,0)\cup(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)$
- $[-2,0)\cup(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)$
- $[-2,1)\cup(1,+\infty)$
- $[-2,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The radicand must be nonnegative and the denominator must be nonzero, so
$$
\begin{cases}
x+2\geq 0\\
x^2-x\neq 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first condition is equivalent to $x\geq -2$. Since $x^2-x=x(x-1)$, the second one excludes $x=0$ and $x=1$. The point $x=-2$ is still allowed, because there the square root equals $0$ and the denominator equals $6$. Hence the expression is defined for $x\in[-2,0)\cup(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)$.
