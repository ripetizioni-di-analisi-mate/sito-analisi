Seven places must be allocated to two offices in proportion to their enrolments, which are $60$ and $40$. Each office first receives the integer part of its share, and any places left over go to the offices with the larger decimal part. How many places does the office with $40$ enrolments receive?

- $2$
- $4$
- $5$
- $3$

## Explanation

In a **division into directly proportional parts** the shares are $7 \cdot \dfrac{60}{100} = 4.2$ and $7 \cdot \dfrac{40}{100} = 2.8$. The integer parts give $4$ places to the first office and $2$ to the second, so $7 - 6 = 1$ place is left over. Since the decimal part $0.8$ of the second office is greater than $0.2$, the remaining place goes to the office with $40$ enrolments, which therefore receives $3$ places. The value $2$ gives the remaining place to the larger office, and the value $4$ is the integer part of the other office's share.
