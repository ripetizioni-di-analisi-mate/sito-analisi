The side length of a square increases by $10\%$. By what percentage does its area increase?

- $21\%$
- $20\%$
- $121\%$
- $10\%$

## Explanation

The area is the side length times itself, so the side-length multiplier $1.1$ appears twice, as in two **successive percentage changes**. The area is therefore multiplied by $1.1^2 = 1.21$, an increase of $21\%$. For example, with an initial side length of $10$ cm, the area changes from $100\ \text{cm}^2$ to $121\ \text{cm}^2$. Adding $10\%$ twice gives $20\%$. The final area is $121\%$ of the initial area.
