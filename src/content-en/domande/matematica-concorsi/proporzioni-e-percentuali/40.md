The price of a product increases by $50\%$. For the same expenditure, by what percentage does the quantity you can buy decrease?

- $33.\overline{3}\%$
- $25\%$
- $50\%$
- $66.\overline{6}\%$

## Explanation

For a fixed expenditure, the unit price and quantity purchased are **inversely proportional** because their product is the expenditure. The price is multiplied by $1.5 = \dfrac{3}{2}$, so the quantity is multiplied by $\dfrac{2}{3}$. The quantity therefore decreases by a fraction $\dfrac{1}{3}$, or $33.\overline{3}\%$. The value $50\%$ assumes the quantity decreases by the same percentage as the price increases. The value $66.\overline{6}\%$ gives the quantity still affordable as a percentage of the original quantity.
