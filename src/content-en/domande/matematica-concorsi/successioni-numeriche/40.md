In every row of the table, the third number is obtained from the first two using the same rule.
$$
\begin{array}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
5 & 3 & 22 \\ \hline
4 & 7 & 9 \\ \hline
2 & 1 & 3 \\ \hline
6 & 10 & ? \\ \hline
\end{array}
$$
Which number replaces the question mark?

- $26$
- $46$
- $16$
- $60$

## Explanation

In the first row, $5^2 - 3 = 25 - 3 = 22$, so the third number could be the square of the first minus the second. The rule also fits the other complete rows because $4^2 - 7 = 9$ and $2^2 - 1 = 3$. The missing number in the last row is therefore $6^2 - 10 = 26$. The value $46$ adds the second number instead of subtracting it, and $16$ is the sum $6 + 10$, a rule that already fails in the first row.
