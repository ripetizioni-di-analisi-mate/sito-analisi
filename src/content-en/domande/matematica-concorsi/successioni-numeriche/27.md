How many terms of the arithmetic sequence $4, 7, 10, 13, \dots$ must be added, starting with the first, to obtain $175$?

- $10$
- $9$
- $11$
- $58$

## Explanation

With $a_1 = 4$ and $d = 3$, the term in position $n$ is $a_n = 4 + 3(n - 1) = 3n + 1$, so the sum of the first $n$ terms is $S_n = \dfrac{(4 + 3n + 1) \cdot n}{2} = \dfrac{(3n + 5) \cdot n}{2}$. For $n = 10$ we obtain $S_{10} = \dfrac{35 \cdot 10}{2} = 175$, so $10$ terms are needed. Since the terms are positive, the sum increases with $n$, and indeed $S_9 = 144$ and $S_{11} = 209$. The value $58$ is the position of the term equal to $175$, since $4 + 57 \cdot 3 = 175$, and confuses the term with the sum.
