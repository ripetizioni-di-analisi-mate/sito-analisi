The rows $(6,2,10)$ and $(5,3,7)$ of a table follow the rule $z=2x-y$. If $3$ is added to the first entry $x$, how must $y$ change to keep the row's result unchanged?

- Increase it by $6$
- Decrease it by $3$
- Decrease it by $6$
- Increase it by $3$

## Explanation

Increasing $x$ by $3$ increases $2x$ by $6$. Since the second entry is subtracted, it must also increase by $6$ to compensate: $2(x+3)-(y+6)=2x-y$.
