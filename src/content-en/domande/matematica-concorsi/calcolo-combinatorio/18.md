How many six-digit numbers can be written using only the digits $0$, $0$, $1$, $1$, $1$, and $2$, each exactly as many times as it appears in the list?

- $60$
- $20$
- $40$
- $30$

## Explanation

The distinct arrangements of the six digits are **permutations with repeated objects**. The digit $0$ appears $2$ times and $1$ appears $3$ times, giving $\dfrac{6!}{2! \cdot 3!} = 60$ arrangements. A six-digit number cannot begin with $0$, so remove the arrangements starting with $0$. In those arrangements the other digits are $0$, $1$, $1$, $1$, and $2$, which can be ordered in $\dfrac{5!}{3!} = 20$ ways. By **counting with complements**, there are $60 - 20 = 40$ numbers. The result $60$ includes arrangements starting with $0$, and $20$ is exactly the number of those arrangements.
