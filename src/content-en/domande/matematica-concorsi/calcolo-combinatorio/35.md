In the plane, $10$ distinct points are marked. Four lie on the same line, and no other set of three points is collinear. How many distinct lines pass through at least two of the marked points?

- $45$
- $39$
- $42$
- $40$

## Explanation

If no three points were collinear, each pair would determine a different line, giving $\dbinom{10}{2} = 45$ lines. However, the $\dbinom{4}{2} = 6$ pairs formed by the four collinear points all determine the same line. The count $45$ includes that line $6$ times instead of once, so the number of lines is
$$
45 - 6 + 1 = 40 .
$$
The result $39$ removes all $6$ pairs without adding back the line containing the four points. The result $45$ ignores the collinearity.
