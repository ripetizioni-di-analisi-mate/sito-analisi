An office has $6$ employees. Let $x$ be the number of ways to select $3$ representatives with the same role. Let $y$ be the number of ways to assign $3$ different duties to $3$ different people. Let $z$ be the number of ways to award $3$ different prizes, with each prize open to anyone, including previous winners. Which relationship is true?

- $y < x < z$
- $x < z < y$
- $z < x < y$
- $x < y < z$

## Explanation

For representatives, order does not matter and nobody is chosen twice, so $x = C_{6,3} = \dfrac{6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 20$. For duties, order matters and repetition is not allowed, so $y = D_{6,3} = 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 = 120$. For prizes, order matters and someone can win more than once, so $z = D'_{6,3} = 6^3 = 216$. Thus $x < y < z$, in agreement with the inequality $C_{n,k} \leq D_{n,k} \leq n^k$ for the same $n$ and $k$. The relationship $y < x < z$ would result from switching combinations and ordered selections without repetition.
