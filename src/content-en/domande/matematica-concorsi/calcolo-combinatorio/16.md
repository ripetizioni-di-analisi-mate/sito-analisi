A store divides the $7$ evening closing shifts in a week, one per day, among three employees. Aldo takes $3$ shifts, Bruna $2$, and Carlo $2$. In how many ways can the shifts be assigned?

- $210$
- $420$
- $5040$
- $2187$

## Explanation

A shift assignment is an ordered list of $7$ names, one per day, with Aldo appearing $3$ times, Bruna $2$ times, and Carlo $2$ times. The assignments are therefore **permutations with repeated objects**, giving
$$
\frac{7!}{3! \cdot 2! \cdot 2!} = \frac{5040}{24} = 210 .
$$
The same result comes from choosing Aldo’s $3$ days in $\dbinom{7}{3} = 35$ ways and then Bruna’s $2$ days from the $4$ remaining in $\dbinom{4}{2} = 6$ ways, since $35 \cdot 6 = 210$. The result $420 = \dfrac{7!}{3! \cdot 2!}$ allows for the repetition of only one of the two names that appear twice. The result $2187 = 3^7$ ignores the required number of shifts for each employee.
