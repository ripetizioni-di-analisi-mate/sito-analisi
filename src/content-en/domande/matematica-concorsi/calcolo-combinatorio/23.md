An office of $5$ employees, including Rossi, must assign $3$ different duties to $3$ different employees. One of the three duties must go to Rossi. In how many ways can they be assigned?

- $60$
- $12$
- $24$
- $36$

## Explanation

Rossi can receive any of the $3$ duties, giving $3$ choices. The other two duties go to two of Rossi’s $4$ colleagues. They are different duties, so order matters and they can be assigned in $D_{4,2} = 4 \cdot 3 = 12$ ways. By the **multiplication principle**, there are $3 \cdot 12 = 36$ assignments. The result $60 = D_{5,3}$ ignores the requirement about Rossi. The result $12$ overlooks the choice of which duty goes to Rossi, and $24 = D_{4,3}$ counts assignments in which Rossi receives no duty.
