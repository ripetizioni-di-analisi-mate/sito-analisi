Which of the following is counted by $C_{7,3}$, the number of combinations without repetition when selecting $3$ of $7$ objects to form groups of size $3$?

- The number of ways to choose $3$ delegates with the same role from $7$ colleagues
- The number of possible podium finishes in a race with $7$ athletes and no ties
- The number of $3$-digit codes that can be formed from $7$ digits, allowing repetition
- The number of lineups that can be formed with $7$ people

## Explanation

Combinations without repetition count groups of distinct objects where order does *not* matter and no object is repeated. When $3$ delegates share the same role, exchanging any two leaves the group unchanged, and nobody is chosen twice, so the answer is $C_{7,3} = 35$. The three places on a podium differ, so order matters and there are $D_{7,3} = 210$ podium finishes. In a code with repeatable digits, order matters and repetition is allowed, giving $7^3 = 343$ codes. The lineups of $7$ people are the permutations $P_7 = 7! = 5040$.
