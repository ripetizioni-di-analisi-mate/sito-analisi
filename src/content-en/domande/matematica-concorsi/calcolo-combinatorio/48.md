An office uses codes made of one letter chosen from A, B and C, followed by one digit chosen from $1$, $2$, $3$ and $4$. If the letter is A, the digit must be even, while with B and with C all four digits are allowed. How many different codes can be formed?

- $6$
- $8$
- $10$
- $12$

## Explanation

The codes split into three alternative cases according to the first letter. With A the digit can only be $2$ or $4$, so there are $2$ codes starting with A. With B and with C the digit is chosen from four values, so there are $4$ codes for each of the two letters. By the **addition principle** the number of codes is $2+4+4=10$. The value $12$ is the product $3\cdot4$, which ignores the condition on the letter A, and the value $6$ applies the condition to all three letters.
