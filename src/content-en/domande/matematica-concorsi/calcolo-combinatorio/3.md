A library has $12$ novels and $8$ nonfiction books, all different. A patron may borrow one book, or two books provided one is a novel and the other is nonfiction. How many choices does the patron have?

- $96$
- $20$
- $210$
- $116$

## Explanation

The choices fall into two disjoint cases. To borrow one book, the patron chooses one of $12 + 8 = 20$ books. To borrow two, the patron can choose the novel in $12$ ways and the nonfiction book in $8$ ways. By the multiplication principle, there are $12 \cdot 8 = 96$ pairs. By the **addition principle**, there are $20 + 96 = 116$ choices in total. The result $210 = 20 + \dbinom{20}{2}$ also allows two novels or two nonfiction books, which the rules exclude.
