A parking lot has $9$ open spaces. When $3$ cars arrive, each takes a different space. Both the occupied spaces and which car takes each space matter. In how many ways can the $3$ cars park?

- $84$
- $729$
- $27$
- $504$

## Explanation

The first car can take any of $9$ spaces, the second one of the $8$ left, and the third one of the $7$ left. The cars are distinguishable and two cannot occupy the same space, so count the **ordered selections without repetition**, $D_{9,3} = 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 504$. The result $84 = C_{9,3}$ counts only which spaces are occupied, ignoring which car is where. The result $729 = 9^3$ allows two cars in the same space, and $27 = 9 \cdot 3$ multiplies the number of spaces by the number of cars.
