Marta travels from home to work through the town square. There are $3$ different roads from her home to the square and $2$ from the square to her office. She also returns through the square and wants to use a different route home, meaning that at least one of the two roads must differ. How many round trips are possible?

- $36$
- $30$
- $12$
- $15$

## Explanation

By the **multiplication principle**, the outbound route can be chosen in $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ ways, and the same is true for the return. Without the restriction there would be $6 \cdot 6 = 36$ round trips. Of these, $6$ retrace the outbound route, one for each outbound route, so $36 - 6 = 30$ trips meet the requirement. The result $12 = 6 \cdot 2$ requires both roads on the return trip to be different, a stronger condition than the question imposes.
