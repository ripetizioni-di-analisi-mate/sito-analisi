A block of ice has a volume of $45\ \text{dm}^3$. The density of ice is $0.9\ \text{kg/dm}^3$ and the density of water is $1\ \text{kg/dm}^3$. How many liters of water do you get when the block melts completely?

- $40.5$ liters
- $4.05$ liters
- $45$ liters
- $50$ liters

## Explanation

By the definition of **density**, the block has a mass of $m = d \cdot V = 0.9 \cdot 45 = 40.5$ kg. Mass stays the same when ice melts, so you get $40.5$ kg of water. Water has a density of $1\ \text{kg/dm}^3$, so $40.5$ kg of water occupies $40.5\ \text{dm}^3$, or $40.5$ liters. The result $50$ liters divides $45$ by $0.9$ instead of multiplying, and $45$ liters assumes the volume stays the same.
