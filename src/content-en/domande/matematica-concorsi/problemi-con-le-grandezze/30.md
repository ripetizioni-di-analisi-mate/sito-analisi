Six workers, all working at the same constant rate, can pave a road in $10$ days. After $4$ days of work, two workers are moved to another site, and the four remaining workers complete the job. How many days does the entire job take?

- $9$ days
- $13$ days
- $8$ days
- $15$ days

## Explanation

In $4$ days the six workers complete $\dfrac{4}{10} = \dfrac{2}{5}$ of the road, leaving $\dfrac{3}{5}$ to pave. Six workers working for $10$ days supply $60$ worker-days, so one worker paves $\dfrac{1}{60}$ of the road per day. The four remaining workers therefore pave $\dfrac{4}{60} = \dfrac{1}{15}$ of the road per day and finish the rest in $\dfrac{3}{5} \cdot 15 = 9$ days. The entire job takes $4 + 9 = 13$ days. The result $9$ days is the duration of the second phase alone, and $15$ days is the time four workers would need to pave the whole road.
