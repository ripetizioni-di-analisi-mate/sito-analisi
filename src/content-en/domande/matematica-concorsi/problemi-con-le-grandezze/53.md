Walking at a constant speed of $4$ km/h, an office worker reaches the office in $45$ minutes. How many minutes does it take to cover the same road by bicycle, at a constant speed of $12$ km/h?

- $15$
- $135$
- $30$
- $25$

## Explanation

The $45$ minutes are $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of an hour, so on foot the worker covers $4 \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} = 3$ km. By bicycle the same road takes $\dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{1}{4}$ of an hour, that is $15$ minutes. The same result follows from the fact that, for a fixed distance, the time is **inversely proportional** to the speed: since the speed triples, the time falls to one third of $45$ minutes. The value $135$ comes from direct proportionality, the value $25$ from reading $0.25$ hours as $25$ minutes and the value $30$ is the time saved.
