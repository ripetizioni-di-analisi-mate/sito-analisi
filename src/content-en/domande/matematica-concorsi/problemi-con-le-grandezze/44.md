A traveler exchanges $400$ euros for dollars at a rate of $1$ euro $= 1.25$ dollars. The exchange office charges a fee of $2\%$ of the amount in dollars. The traveler spends nothing and, on returning, exchanges all the dollars for euros at the same rate. The office charges another fee of $2\%$ of the amount in euros. How many euros does the traveler receive?

- $384.16$ euros
- $392$ euros
- $400$ euros
- $384$ euros

## Explanation

The traveler receives $400 \cdot 1.25 = 500$ dollars minus $2\%$, leaving $500 \cdot 0.98 = 490$ dollars. On the return trip, $490$ dollars is worth $\dfrac{490}{1.25} = 392$ euros, and the second fee leaves $392 \cdot 0.98 = 384.16$ euros. Since the amount is first multiplied and then divided by $1.25$, the exchange rate cancels, giving $400 \cdot 0.98 \cdot 0.98$. The result $384$ euros deducts $4\%$ all at once, although the second fee applies to an amount already reduced by the first. The result $392$ euros ignores the second fee.
