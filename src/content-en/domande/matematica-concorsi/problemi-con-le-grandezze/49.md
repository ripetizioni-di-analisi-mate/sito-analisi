On a scale drawing, a rectangular plot of land is represented by a rectangle measuring $2$ cm by $5$ cm. The actual area of the plot is $1000\ \text{m}^2$. What is the scale of the drawing?

- $1:1000$
- $1:10\,000$
- $1:100$
- $1:1\,000\,000$

## Explanation

The rectangle on the drawing has area $2 \cdot 5 = 10\ \text{cm}^2$, so each square centimetre of the drawing represents $\dfrac{1000}{10} = 100\ \text{m}^2$ of land. On a **scale** drawing the ratio of areas is the square of the ratio of lengths, so each centimetre of the drawing represents $\sqrt{100} = 10$ m, that is $1000$ cm. The scale of the drawing is therefore $1:1000$. The value $1:10\,000$ comes from reading $100$ as metres per centimetre without taking the square root, and the value $1:1\,000\,000$ uses the ratio of areas as the scale, that is $1\ \text{cm}^2$ against $100\ \text{m}^2 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
