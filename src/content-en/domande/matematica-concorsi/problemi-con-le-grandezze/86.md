To prepare $540\ \text{g}$ of a solution that is $18\%$ sugar by mass, you mix a $10\%$ sugar solution with a $30\%$ sugar solution. How many grams of the $30\%$ solution do you need?

- $162$
- $270$
- $324$
- $216$

## Explanation

The final **mass of sugar** is $540\cdot0.18=97.2\ \text{g}$. If all $540\ \text{g}$ were the $10\%$ solution, it would contain $54\ \text{g}$ of sugar. Each gram replaced by the $30\%$ solution adds $0.20\ \text{g}$ of sugar. You therefore need $(97.2-54)\div0.20=216\ \text{g}$.
