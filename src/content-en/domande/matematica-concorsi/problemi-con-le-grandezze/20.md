A cyclist rides uphill from a village to a mountain lodge at an average speed of $12$ km/h and returns along the same road at an average speed of $36$ km/h. The uphill and downhill trips take a total of $2$ hours, with no stops. How long is the road from the village to the lodge?

- $18$ km
- $24$ km
- $48$ km
- $36$ km

## Explanation

Let $d$ be the length of the road in km. The uphill trip takes $\dfrac{d}{12}$ and the downhill trip takes $\dfrac{d}{36}$, so
$$
\frac{d}{12} + \frac{d}{36} = 2 .
$$
Since $\dfrac{d}{12} = \dfrac{3d}{36}$, the left-hand side equals $\dfrac{4d}{36} = \dfrac{d}{9}$, giving $d = 18$ km. The result $36$ km is the round-trip distance. The results $48$ km and $24$ km use the arithmetic mean of $24$ km/h instead of the **average speed for the round trip**, which is $\dfrac{2 \cdot 12 \cdot 36}{48} = 18$ km/h.
