You mix $6$ kg of rice priced at $2$ euros per kilogram with rice priced at $3.50$ euros per kilogram. The resulting mixture costs $2.60$ euros per kilogram. How many kilograms of the rice priced at $3.50$ euros per kilogram did you use?

- $10$ kg
- $6$ kg
- $4$ kg
- $9$ kg

## Explanation

To find the **quantities to mix for a given price**, use the fact that they are inversely proportional to how far each price is from $2.60$ euros. The differences are $0.60$ euros for the cheaper rice and $0.90$ euros for the more expensive rice. The ratio of cheaper rice to more expensive rice is therefore $0.90 : 0.60 = 3 : 2$. There are $6$ kg of the cheaper rice, so there are $4$ kg of the more expensive rice. To check, $\dfrac{6 \cdot 2 + 4 \cdot 3.50}{10} = \dfrac{26}{10} = 2.60$. The result $9$ kg reverses the ratio, and $10$ kg is the total mass of the mixture.
