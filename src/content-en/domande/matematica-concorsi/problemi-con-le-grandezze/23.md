Two cyclists leave at the same time from towns $60$ km apart and ride toward each other on the same road at constant speeds. The first rides at $18$ km/h and the second at $12$ km/h. How far from the first cyclist's town do they meet?

- $36$ km
- $30$ km
- $24$ km
- $40$ km

## Explanation

Each hour the distance between the cyclists decreases by $18 + 12 = 30$ km, so the **time until they meet** is $\dfrac{60}{30} = 2$ hours. In $2$ hours, the first cyclist travels $18 \cdot 2 = 36$ km, the distance requested. The result $24$ km is the distance traveled by the second cyclist, and $30$ km is half the distance between the towns. The midpoint would be the meeting point only if their speeds were equal.
