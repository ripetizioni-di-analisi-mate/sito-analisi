A driver works out that, travelling at $60$ km/h, he would reach his destination half an hour later than travelling at $80$ km/h. How long is the road?

- $240$ km
- $120$ km
- $60$ km
- $10$ km

## Explanation

Let $d$ be the length of the road in kilometres; at $60$ km/h the journey takes $\dfrac{d}{60}$ hours and at $80$ km/h it takes $\dfrac{d}{80}$ hours. The difference between the two times is half an hour, so $\dfrac{d}{60} - \dfrac{d}{80} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $\dfrac{d}{60} - \dfrac{d}{80} = \dfrac{4d - 3d}{240} = \dfrac{d}{240}$, we get $d = 120$ km. Indeed at $60$ km/h it takes $2$ hours and at $80$ km/h it takes $1.5$ hours. The value $240$ km corresponds to a difference of one hour instead of half an hour, and the value $10$ km is the difference of the speeds multiplied by half an hour.
