From a well-shuffled Italian deck of $40$ cards, $2$ cards are drawn at the same time. What is the probability that the two cards are from the same suit?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{16}$
- $\dfrac{9}{40}$
- $\dfrac{3}{13}$

## Explanation

There are $C_{40,2} = \dfrac{40 \cdot 39}{2} = 780$ possible pairs of cards. To get a pair of the same suit, choose the suit in $4$ ways and two cards from it in $C_{10,2} = 45$ ways. This gives $4 \cdot 45 = 180$ pairs, so $P = \dfrac{180}{780} = \dfrac{3}{13}$. Alternatively, whatever the first card is, the $39$ remaining cards include $9$ of its suit, and $\dfrac{9}{39} = \dfrac{3}{13}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{4}$ gives the result with replacement. The value $\dfrac{9}{40}$ removes the first card only when counting favorable outcomes.
