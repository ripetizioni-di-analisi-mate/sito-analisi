Urn $A$ contains $3$ red balls and $1$ white ball. Urn $B$ contains $2$ red balls and $3$ white balls. All the balls feel identical. A fair coin is flipped. If it lands heads, one ball is drawn from urn $A$. If it lands tails, one ball is drawn from urn $B$. What is the probability of drawing a red ball?

- $\dfrac{23}{40}$
- $\dfrac{5}{9}$
- $\dfrac{3}{10}$
- $\dfrac{3}{8}$

## Explanation

Two paths in the tree diagram lead to a red ball. The path “heads, then red” has probability $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{3}{8}$, and “tails, then red” has probability $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{1}{5}$. The paths are mutually exclusive, so the **addition rule** gives
$$
P = \frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{15}{40} + \frac{8}{40} = \frac{23}{40} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{5}{9}$ pools the balls from the two urns as if the $9$ balls were equally likely. Each ball in urn $A$ is actually more likely to be drawn because urn $A$ contains fewer balls. The value $\dfrac{3}{8}$ counts only the first path.
