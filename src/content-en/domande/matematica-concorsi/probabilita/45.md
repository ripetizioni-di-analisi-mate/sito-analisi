An urn contains $6$ white balls and $4$ black balls that feel identical. Two balls are drawn successively without replacement, and the color of the first is not observed. What is the probability that the second ball is black?

- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4}{9}$
- $\dfrac{4}{15}$

## Explanation

Without replacement, each ball is equally likely to occupy the first or the second position in the drawing order. By **symmetry**, the probability that the second ball is black is the same as for the first, namely $\dfrac{4}{10} = \dfrac{2}{5}$. The tree diagram confirms this result, since
$$
P = \frac{6}{10} \cdot \frac{4}{9} + \frac{4}{10} \cdot \frac{3}{9} = \frac{24}{90} + \frac{12}{90} = \frac{36}{90} = \frac{2}{5} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{4}{15} = \dfrac{24}{90}$ counts only the path “white, then black.” The value $\dfrac{4}{9}$ assumes the first ball is white, and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ assumes it is black.
