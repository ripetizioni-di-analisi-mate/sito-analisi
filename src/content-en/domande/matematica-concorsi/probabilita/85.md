From $5$ internal candidates and $4$ external candidates, $3$ people are chosen at random. Order does not matter and all groups are equally likely. What is the probability that the group includes exactly two internal candidates?

- $\dfrac{5}{9}$
- $\dfrac{10}{21}$
- $\dfrac{5}{21}$
- $\dfrac{4}{9}$

## Explanation

The two internal candidates can be chosen in $\dbinom{5}{2}=10$ ways and the external candidate in $4$ ways. There are $40$ favorable groups out of $\dbinom{9}{3}=84$, giving a probability of $\dfrac{10}{21}$.
