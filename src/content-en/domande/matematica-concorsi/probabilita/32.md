Two cards are drawn successively without replacement from a well-shuffled Italian deck of $40$ cards. The first card is a king. What is the probability that the second is also a king?

- $\dfrac{1}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{130}$
- $\dfrac{1}{13}$
- $\dfrac{4}{39}$

## Explanation

After the first king is drawn, $39$ cards remain, including $3$ kings. The **conditional probability** that the second card is a king is $\dfrac{3}{39} = \dfrac{1}{13}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{4}{40}$ gives the result with replacement. The value $\dfrac{4}{39}$ reduces the number of cards in the deck without reducing the number of kings. The value $\dfrac{1}{130} = \dfrac{4}{40} \cdot \dfrac{3}{39}$ gives the probability that *both* cards are kings, calculated before the first card is known.
