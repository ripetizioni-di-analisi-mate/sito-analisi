Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. What is the probability that the larger of the two numbers is $3$? If they are equal, take their common value as the larger number.

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{12}$
- $\dfrac{5}{36}$

## Explanation

The larger number is $3$ if both dice show at most $3$ and at least one shows $3$. Requiring both numbers to be at most $3$ gives $3 \cdot 3 = 9$ pairs. Of these, the $2 \cdot 2 = 4$ pairs made from $1$ and $2$ do not contain $3$. There are therefore $9 - 4 = 5$ favorable outcomes, namely $(1, 3)$, $(2, 3)$, $(3, 3)$, $(3, 1)$, and $(3, 2)$, giving a probability of $\dfrac{5}{36}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{9}{36}$ gives the probability that the larger number is *at most* $3$. The value $\dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{6}{36}$ counts $(3, 3)$ twice.
