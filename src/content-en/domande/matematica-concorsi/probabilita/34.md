A fair six-sided die is rolled twice and at least one $6$ is known to have appeared. What is the probability that both rolls were $6$?

- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{11}$
- $\dfrac{1}{36}$
- $\dfrac{1}{12}$

## Explanation

Excluding $6$ gives $5 \cdot 5 = 25$ ordered pairs with neither roll showing it. Requiring at least one $6$ therefore leaves $36 - 25 = 11$ pairs. Given that at least one $6$ appeared, the sample space is restricted to those $11$ pairs, and only $(6, 6)$ is favorable. The **conditional probability** is $\dfrac{1}{11}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{6}$ assumes we know *which* roll gave $6$. The value $\dfrac{1}{12}$ counts $12$ pairs with at least one $6$ because it counts $(6, 6)$ twice.
