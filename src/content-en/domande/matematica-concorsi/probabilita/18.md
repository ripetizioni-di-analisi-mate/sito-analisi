An integer from $1$ to $50$ is chosen at random, with all numbers equally likely. What is the probability that it is a multiple of $4$ or $6$, including numbers that are multiples of both?

- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{8}{25}$
- $\dfrac{2}{25}$
- $\dfrac{6}{25}$

## Explanation

Counting the multiples of $4$ up to $50$ gives $12$, from $4$ to $48$. The multiples of $6$ number $8$, from $6$ to $48$. The events overlap because the common multiples of $4$ and $6$ are multiples of their least common multiple $12$, namely $12$, $24$, $36$, and $48$. By the **general addition rule**, there are $12 + 8 - 4 = 16$ favorable outcomes, giving $P = \dfrac{16}{50} = \dfrac{8}{25}$. The value $\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{20}{50}$ counts the four multiples of $12$ twice. The value $\dfrac{6}{25} = \dfrac{12}{50}$ counts only the multiples of $4$. Excluding the multiples of $12$ would give the same value, but that would read “or” as exclusive when the question explicitly includes multiples of both.
