A two-digit integer from $10$ to $99$ is chosen at random, with all numbers equally likely. What is the probability that its two digits are identical?

- $\dfrac{1}{11}$
- $\dfrac{9}{100}$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{9}$

## Explanation

The integers from $10$ through $99$ give $99 - 10 + 1 = 90$ possible outcomes. The numbers with matching digits are $11$, $22$, …, $99$, giving $9$ favorable outcomes. By the **classical definition of probability**, $P = \dfrac{9}{90} = \dfrac{1}{10}$. The value $\dfrac{9}{100}$ uses $100$ possible outcomes. The value $\dfrac{1}{11} = \dfrac{9}{99}$ counts the numbers from $1$ to $99$, whereas there are $90$ two-digit numbers.
