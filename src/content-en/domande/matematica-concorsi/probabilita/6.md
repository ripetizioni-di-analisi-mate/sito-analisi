Two fair coins are flipped. What is the probability of getting one head and one tail?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

The coins are distinguishable, so the equally likely outcomes are the $4$ ordered pairs $TT$, $TC$, $CT$, and $CC$, where T denotes heads and C denotes tails. The event “one head and one tail” has $2$ favorable outcomes, $TC$ and $CT$. By the **classical definition of probability**, $P = \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{3}$ treats “two heads,” “two tails,” and “one head and one tail” as equally likely, although they correspond to $1$, $1$, and $2$ pairs, respectively. The value $\dfrac{1}{4}$ counts only $TC$.
