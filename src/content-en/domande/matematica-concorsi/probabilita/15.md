A charity raffle sells $200$ tickets and draws a single winning ticket. Laura bought $6$ tickets. What is the probability that she does not win?

- $\dfrac{3}{100}$
- $\dfrac{199}{200}$
- $\dfrac{97}{100}$
- $\dfrac{1}{200}$

## Explanation

Laura wins if the ticket drawn is one of her $6$ tickets, so her probability of winning is $\dfrac{6}{200} = \dfrac{3}{100}$. “Laura does not win” is the complement. By the **complement rule**, $P = 1 - \dfrac{3}{100} = \dfrac{97}{100}$. Counting the $194$ tickets that do not belong to Laura out of the $200$ sold gives the same result. The value $\dfrac{199}{200}$ accounts for only one of Laura’s tickets, and $\dfrac{3}{100}$ gives her probability of winning.
