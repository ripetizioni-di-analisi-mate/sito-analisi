An instructor has prepared $10$ questions for an oral exam, and a student has studied $6$ of them. The instructor asks $3$ different questions chosen at random from the $10$. What is the probability that the student has studied all three questions asked?

- $\dfrac{27}{125}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}$

## Explanation

The questions are different, so selection is without replacement. One fewer question remains after each selection. The probability that the student studied the first question is $\dfrac{6}{10}$. If so, the probability that the student studied the second is $\dfrac{5}{9}$. If the student studied both, the probability of having studied the third is $\dfrac{4}{8}$. The **general multiplication rule** gives
$$
P = \frac{6}{10} \cdot \frac{5}{9} \cdot \frac{4}{8} = \frac{120}{720} = \frac{1}{6} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{27}{125} = \left( \dfrac{3}{5} \right)^{3}$ gives the result with replacement, which would allow the same question to appear more than once. The value $\dfrac{1}{3}$ multiplies only the first two factors.
