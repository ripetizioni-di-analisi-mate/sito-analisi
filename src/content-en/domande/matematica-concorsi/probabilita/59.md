In a first sample of $20$ answers, $12$ are correct. A second sample of $10$ answers is then examined, and the relative frequency of correct answers in the two samples together stays at $60\%$. How many correct answers does the second sample contain?

- $18$
- $8$
- $12$
- $6$

## Explanation

In the first sample the **relative frequency** of correct answers is $\dfrac{12}{20}=60\%$. The two samples together contain $30$ answers, and $60\%$ of $30$ is $18$, so there are $18$ correct answers in total. Therefore the second sample contains $18-12=6$ correct answers. Indeed, the overall frequency stays at $60\%$ only if the second sample also has frequency $60\%$, and $60\%$ of $10$ is $6$. The value $18$ is the total number of correct answers, and the value $8$ comes from subtracting from $18$ the number $10$ of answers in the second sample.
