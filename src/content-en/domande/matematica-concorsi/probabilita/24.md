A fair six-sided die is rolled. Let $A$ be the event “roll an even number” and $B$ the event “roll a number less than $3$.” Which statement is true?

- $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive and dependent
- $A$ and $B$ can occur together and are dependent
- $A$ and $B$ can occur together and are independent
- $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive and independent

## Explanation

The outcome $2$ is even and less than $3$, so $A$ and $B$ have a favorable outcome in common and **can occur together**. We have $P(A) = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Given that $B$ occurred, the possible outcomes are $1$ and $2$, of which only $2$ is even. Thus $P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The occurrence of $B$ does not change the probability of $A$, so the events are **independent**. The product confirms this, since $P(A \cap B) = \dfrac{1}{6}$ and $P(A) \cdot P(B) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{6}$.
