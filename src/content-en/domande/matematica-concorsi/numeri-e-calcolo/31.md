Which of the following fractions has a terminating decimal representation?

- $\dfrac{7}{30}$
- $\dfrac{5}{12}$
- $\dfrac{21}{60}$
- $\dfrac{4}{15}$

## Explanation

A fraction in lowest terms gives a terminating decimal if the only possible prime factors of its denominator are $2$ and $5$. Reducing by $3$ gives $\dfrac{21}{60} = \dfrac{7}{20}$, and since $20 = 2^2 \cdot 5$, the fraction gives the **terminating** decimal $\dfrac{7}{20} = 0.35$. The other three fractions are already in lowest terms and have the factor $3$ in the denominator, so they give repeating decimals, as in $\dfrac{7}{30} = 0.2\overline{3}$. The denominator $60 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$ contains the factor $3$, but the rule applies to the fraction in lowest terms, in which the factor $3$ no longer appears.
