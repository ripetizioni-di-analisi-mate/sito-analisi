What is the value of the expression $\dfrac{18}{\sqrt{3}} + \sqrt{12}$?

- $6 + 2\sqrt{3}$
- $20\sqrt{3}$
- $4\sqrt{3}$
- $8\sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

To remove the radical from the denominator, multiply both the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3}$, giving $\dfrac{18}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{18\sqrt{3}}{3} = 6\sqrt{3}$. Also, $\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} = 2\sqrt{3}$. The two radicals have the same radicand, so add their coefficients to obtain $6\sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} = 8\sqrt{3}$. The result $6 + 2\sqrt{3}$ divides $18$ by $3$ instead of $\sqrt{3}$, and $4\sqrt{3}$ divides $18\sqrt{3}$ by $9$ as though $\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} = 9$ were true.
