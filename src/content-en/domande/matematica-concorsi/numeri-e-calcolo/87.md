If $a=\sqrt{11}+\sqrt{7}$ and $b=\sqrt{11}-\sqrt{7}$, what is $\dfrac{1}{a}+\dfrac{1}{b}$?

- $\dfrac1{2\sqrt{11}}$
- $2\sqrt{11}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}2$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt7}2$

## Explanation

Using the **common denominator** gives $\dfrac{a+b}{ab}$. The numerator is $2\sqrt{11}$, and the product in the denominator is the difference of squares $11-7=4$, so the result is $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}2$.
