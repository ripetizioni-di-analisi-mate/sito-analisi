What is the smallest natural number that, when divided by $4$, leaves a remainder of $3$, when divided by $5$, leaves a remainder of $4$, and when divided by $6$, leaves a remainder of $5$?

- $59$
- $61$
- $60$
- $119$

## Explanation

In each division the remainder is $1$ less than the divisor, so increasing the required number by $1$ gives a number divisible by $4$, $5$, and $6$. The smallest number with this property is $\operatorname{mcm}(4, 5, 6) = 60$, so the required number is $60 - 1 = 59$. Indeed, $59 = 4 \cdot 14 + 3 = 5 \cdot 11 + 4 = 6 \cdot 9 + 5$. The result $61$ adds $1$ to the least common multiple instead of subtracting it, and $61$ divided by $4$ leaves a remainder of $1$. The number $119 = 2 \cdot 60 - 1$ gives the same remainders and is greater than $59$.
