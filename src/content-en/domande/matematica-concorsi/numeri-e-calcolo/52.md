What is the value of the product $\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{3}{4}\cdot\dfrac{4}{5}\cdots\dfrac{9}{10}$, in which the numerator and the denominator of each factor exceed those of the previous factor by $1$?

- $\dfrac{1}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $\dfrac{2}{9}$
- $\dfrac{9}{10}$

## Explanation

Each number from $3$ to $9$ appears in the denominator of one factor and in the numerator of the next factor. So these numbers cancel, and only the numerator $2$ of the first factor and the denominator $10$ of the last one remain. Therefore the product equals $\dfrac{2}{10}=\dfrac{1}{5}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{10}$ also cancels the numerator $2$, which has no equal denominator, and the value $\dfrac{2}{9}$ takes $9$ as the last denominator.
