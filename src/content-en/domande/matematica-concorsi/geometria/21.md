The diagonal of a square measures $6$ cm. What is its area?

- $36\ \text{cm}^2$
- $18\ \text{cm}^2$
- $9\ \text{cm}^2$
- $72\ \text{cm}^2$

## Explanation

A square is a rhombus with equal diagonals, so its area is half the product of the diagonals, or $\dfrac{6 \cdot 6}{2} = 18\ \text{cm}^2$. The side length gives the same result, since $d = l\sqrt{2}$ gives $l = 3\sqrt{2}$ cm and hence $l^2 = 18\ \text{cm}^2$. The value $36\ \text{cm}^2$ treats the diagonal as a side length, and $9\ \text{cm}^2$ squares half the diagonal.
