At the same time, a vertical pole $2$ m tall casts a shadow $3$ m long on level ground, and a nearby vertical tree casts a shadow $12$ m long. How tall is the tree?

- $18$ m
- $11$ m
- $8$ m
- $24$ m

## Explanation

At the same moment the sun's rays have the same inclination, so the pole and its shadow form a right triangle similar to the one formed by the tree and its shadow. The tree's shadow is $\dfrac{12}{3} = 4$ times as long as the pole's shadow. The tree is therefore $4$ times as tall as the pole, or $8$ m tall. The value $18$ m reverses the ratio of height to shadow length. The value $11$ m adds the difference in shadow lengths to the pole's height, treating the relationship as additive rather than multiplicative.
