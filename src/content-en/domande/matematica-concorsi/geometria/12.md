Which of the following triples could be the side lengths of a right triangle?

- $(9, 40, 41)$
- $(10, 24, 25)$
- $(12, 16, 21)$
- $(6, 9, 11)$

## Explanation

By the **Pythagorean theorem** and its converse, three positive lengths are the sides of a right triangle if and only if the square of the longest equals the sum of the squares of the other two. For $(9, 40, 41)$ we have $81 + 1600 = 1681 = 41^2$, so this is a Pythagorean triple. For $(10, 24, 25)$ the sum $100 + 576 = 676$ equals $26^2$ instead of $25^2$, and for $(12, 16, 21)$ the sum $144 + 256 = 400$ equals $20^2$ instead of $21^2$. For $(6, 9, 11)$ we get $36 + 81 = 117$, which differs from $121$.
