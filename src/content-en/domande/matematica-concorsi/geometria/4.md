A map uses Cartesian coordinates, with each unit on the axes representing $1$ km. A shelter is at point $A(-3, 1)$ and a station at point $B(9, -4)$. What is the straight-line distance in kilometers between the shelter and the station?

- $17$ km
- $3\sqrt{5}$ km
- $169$ km
- $13$ km

## Explanation

The **distance between two points** is found by applying the Pythagorean theorem to the coordinate differences. The horizontal difference is $9 - (-3) = 12$ and the vertical difference is $-4 - 1 = -5$, so the distance is $\sqrt{12^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13$ km. The value $17$ km adds the differences, giving the distance traveled along the two legs. The value $169$ km omits the square root, while $3\sqrt{5}$ km adds the coordinates instead of subtracting them, giving $\sqrt{6^2 + (-3)^2}$.
