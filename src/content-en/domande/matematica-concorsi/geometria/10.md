In triangle $ABC$, the interior angles at vertices $A$, $B$, and $C$ measure $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$, respectively. The exterior angle at vertex $C$, the supplement of $\gamma$, measures $130^\circ$, and $\alpha$ exceeds $\beta$ by $20^\circ$. What is $\alpha$?

- $55^\circ$
- $75^\circ$
- $65^\circ$
- $50^\circ$

## Explanation

The interior angle at $C$ is supplementary to the exterior angle, so $\gamma = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ$. From the **sum of the interior angles** of a triangle, we then have $\alpha + \beta = 180^\circ - 50^\circ = 130^\circ$. Since $\alpha = \beta + 20^\circ$, we obtain $2\beta + 20^\circ = 130^\circ$, so $\beta = 55^\circ$ and $\alpha = 75^\circ$. The value $65^\circ$ halves $130^\circ$, ignoring the difference of $20^\circ$ between the two angles. The value $50^\circ$ is angle $\gamma$.
