A square and a rectangle each have a perimeter of $40$ cm. The base of the rectangle measures $14$ cm. By how much does the area of the square exceed the area of the rectangle?

- $0\ \text{cm}^2$
- $4\ \text{cm}^2$
- $84\ \text{cm}^2$
- $16\ \text{cm}^2$

## Explanation

The square has side length $\dfrac{40}{4} = 10$ cm and area $100\ \text{cm}^2$. The base and height of the rectangle add up to its semiperimeter, $20$ cm, so its height is $20 - 14 = 6$ cm and its area is $14 \cdot 6 = 84\ \text{cm}^2$. The area of the square therefore exceeds the area of the rectangle by $100 - 84 = 16\ \text{cm}^2$. The value $0\ \text{cm}^2$ assumes that two figures with the same perimeter must have the same area. The value $84\ \text{cm}^2$ is the area of the rectangle.
