The side length of a square is tripled. How many times as large as the original area is the area of the new square?

- $3$
- $9$
- $27$
- $6$

## Explanation

The squares are similar with scale factor $k = 3$. By the rule for the **ratio of the areas** of similar figures, their areas have ratio $k^2 = 9$. Indeed, if the original side length is $l$, the area changes from $l^2$ to $(3l)^2 = 9l^2$. The value $3$ uses the side-length ratio for the area, and $27$ uses $k^3$, the volume factor.
