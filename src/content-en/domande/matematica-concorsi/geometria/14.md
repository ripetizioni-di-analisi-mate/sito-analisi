Starting at point $P$ on level ground, a cyclist rides straight for $16$ km east, then $5$ km north, then another $7$ km north. What is the cyclist's final straight-line distance from $P$?

- $28$ km
- $\sqrt{330}$ km
- $\sqrt{281}$ km
- $20$ km

## Explanation

The two northbound legs have the same direction, giving a total displacement of $12$ km north. East and north are perpendicular, so the distance from $P$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs of $16$ km and $12$ km. By the **Pythagorean theorem**, the distance is $\sqrt{256 + 144} = \sqrt{400} = 20$ km. The value $28$ km is the sum of the distances traveled. The value $\sqrt{330}$ km adds the squares of the three distances as if all three displacements were mutually perpendicular. The value $\sqrt{281}$ km omits the last leg of the trip.
