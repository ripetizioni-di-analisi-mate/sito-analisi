In triangle $ABC$, line $r$ passes through vertex $C$ and is parallel to side $AB$. On one side of $C$, line $r$ meets side $CA$ at an angle of $70^\circ$. On the other side, it meets side $CB$ at an angle of $45^\circ$. Neither of these angles contains the triangle. What is the measure of the interior angle at vertex $C$?

- $115^\circ$
- $25^\circ$
- $65^\circ$
- $135^\circ$

## Explanation

The two given angles and the interior angle at $C$ share vertex $C$ and lie on the same side of line $r$. Side by side, they extend from one ray of $r$ to the opposite ray, so they form a straight angle. The angle at $C$ therefore measures $180^\circ - 70^\circ - 45^\circ = 65^\circ$. The angles of $70^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ are alternate interior to the triangle's angles at $A$ and $B$ with respect to the **parallel lines** $r$ and $AB$. Thus the triangle's angles measure $70^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $65^\circ$, with sum $180^\circ$. The value $115^\circ$ adds the two given angles, and $135^\circ$ is the supplement of the $45^\circ$ angle alone.
