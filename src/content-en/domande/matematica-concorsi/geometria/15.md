The hypotenuse of a right triangle measures $10$ cm, and one leg is twice the other. What is the area of the triangle?

- $40\ \text{cm}^2$
- $20\ \text{cm}^2$
- $25\ \text{cm}^2$
- $50\ \text{cm}^2$

## Explanation

If $a$ is the shorter leg, the other leg is $2a$. By the **Pythagorean theorem**, $a^2 + 4a^2 = 100$, or $a^2 = 20$. The area is half the product of the legs, $\dfrac{a \cdot 2a}{2} = a^2$, so it equals $20\ \text{cm}^2$ without having to calculate $a = 2\sqrt{5}$ cm. The value $40\ \text{cm}^2$ gives the product of the legs without dividing by $2$. The value $25\ \text{cm}^2$ is the area of an isosceles right triangle with the same hypotenuse, so it ignores the given ratio of the legs.
