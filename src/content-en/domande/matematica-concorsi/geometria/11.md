A right triangle has a hypotenuse of $29$ cm and one leg of $20$ cm. How long is the other leg?

- $21$ cm
- $9$ cm
- $\sqrt{1241}$ cm
- $441$ cm

## Explanation

By the **Pythagorean theorem**, the square of the unknown leg is the square of the hypotenuse minus the square of the known leg, or $841 - 400 = 441$. The leg therefore measures $\sqrt{441} = 21$ cm, and $(20, 21, 29)$ is a Pythagorean triple. The value $9$ cm is the difference $29 - 20$ of the lengths. The value $441$ cm gives the square of the leg without taking the square root. The value $\sqrt{1241}$ cm adds the squares instead of subtracting them, giving a leg longer than the hypotenuse.
