The $12$ participants in a training course completed a mean of $20$ hours of practical training. One participant who completed $42$ hours withdraws. What is the mean number of practical training hours for the remaining $11$ participants?

- $16.5$ hours
- $18$ hours
- $19.8$ hours
- $22$ hours

## Explanation

By the relationship between the **sum of the values and the mean**, the hours for the $12$ participants add up to $12 \cdot 20 = 240$. Removing the $42$ hours completed by the participant who withdrew leaves a total of $240 - 42 = 198$ hours and $11$ participants, so the new mean is $\dfrac{198}{11} = 18$ hours. The value removed is above the mean, so the mean decreases. The value $16.5$ hours divides the new total by $12$ as if there were still twelve participants, and $19.8$ hours divides it by $10$ as if two had withdrawn. The value $22$ hours raises the mean by $2$ hours instead of lowering it.
