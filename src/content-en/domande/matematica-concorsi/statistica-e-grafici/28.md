In which case is the standard deviation of a data set equal to zero?

- When all the values are equal
- When the mean of the data is zero
- When the deviations from the mean sum to zero
- When the mean and median are equal

## Explanation

The **standard deviation** is the square root of the mean of the squared deviations. Squared deviations are nonnegative, so their mean is zero only when each deviation is zero, which means every observation equals the mean. Thus the standard deviation is zero exactly when all values are equal. Deviations from the mean sum to zero for any data set. The values $1$, $2$, and $3$ have deviations that sum to zero and a mean and median both equal to $2$, yet their standard deviation is nonzero. The values $-1$ and $1$ have mean zero and deviations $-1$ and $1$, so their standard deviation is also nonzero.
