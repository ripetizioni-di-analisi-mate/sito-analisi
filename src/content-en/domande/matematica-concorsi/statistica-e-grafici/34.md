The table shows a company's $200$ employees by office location and level of education.

| | Rome | Turin | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| College degree | $36$ | $30$ | $66$ |
| No college degree | $84$ | $50$ | $134$ |
| Total | $120$ | $80$ | $200$ |

Which of the following statements is true?

- There are more college graduates in Turin than in Rome
- $36\%$ of the employees in Rome have a college degree
- The percentage of employees with college degrees is higher in Turin than in Rome
- More than half of the college graduates work in Turin

## Explanation

College graduates account for $\dfrac{36}{120} = 30\%$ of the employees in Rome and $\dfrac{30}{80} = 37.5\%$ in Turin, so the percentage is higher in Turin. Rome has more graduates in absolute terms, $36$ versus $30$, so the statement about the number of graduates is false. The number $36$ is the count of graduates in Rome, and their percentage is $30\%$. The graduates working in Turin account for $30$ of the $66$ graduates, less than half. The two correct percentages use **different totals**, one for each office.
