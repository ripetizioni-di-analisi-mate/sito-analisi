An examiner finds the mean score of $20$ candidates to be $61$, then notices that one score was recorded as $55$ when the correct score was $85$. What is the correct mean score?

- $59.5$
- $62.5$
- $91$
- $65.25$

## Explanation

By the relationship between the **sum of the values and the mean**, the examiner's original total was $20 \cdot 61 = 1220$. To correct it, subtract the incorrect score and add the correct one. The correct sum is therefore $1220 - 55 + 85 = 1250$, and the correct mean is $\dfrac{1250}{20} = 62.5$. The value $59.5$ subtracts the difference $\dfrac{30}{20} = 1.5$ instead of adding it, $91$ adds the entire difference $30$ without dividing it by $20$, and $65.25$ adds the correct score without subtracting the incorrect one.
