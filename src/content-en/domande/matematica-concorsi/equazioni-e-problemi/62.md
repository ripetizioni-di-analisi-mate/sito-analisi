Before solving the equation $\dfrac{1}{x-1}=\dfrac{2}{x+1}$ by cross-multiplying, which values of $x$ must be excluded?

- $0$ and $1$
- $1$ and $2$
- $1$ and $3$
- $-1$ and $1$

## Explanation

A rational equation makes sense only for the values of the unknown that do not make any denominator zero. The denominator $x-1$ is zero for $x=1$ and the denominator $x+1$ is zero for $x=-1$, so $x=1$ and $x=-1$ are excluded. For all other values the product of the denominators is nonzero, so cross-multiplying gives the equivalent equation $x+1=2\,(x-1)$. This equation has the solution $x=3$, which is acceptable because it differs from $1$ and from $-1$.
