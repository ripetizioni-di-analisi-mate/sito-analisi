A class has $4$ more girls than boys. If $2$ girls transferred to another class, the number of girls remaining would be $\dfrac{5}{4}$ of the number of boys. How many students are in the class now?

- $18$
- $12$
- $8$
- $20$

## Explanation

Let $r$ be the number of boys and $g$ the number of girls. This gives the system
$$
\begin{cases}
g = r + 4 \\
g - 2 = \dfrac{5}{4}\,r
\end{cases}
$$
Using **substitution**, write $r + 4$ in place of $g$ to get $r + 2 = \dfrac{5}{4}\,r$. Multiplying by $4$ gives $4r + 8 = 5r$, so $r = 8$. There are therefore $12$ girls and $20$ students in all. After the transfer, there would be $10 = \dfrac{5}{4} \cdot 8$ girls. The value $18$ counts the students after the transfer, and $12$ and $8$ count the girls and boys, respectively.
