A nonzero number $x$ satisfies $x - \dfrac{1}{x} = 3$. What is $x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}$?

- $9$
- $7$
- $10$
- $11$

## Explanation

Square both sides of the given equation. In the **square of a binomial** with terms $a = x$ and $b = \dfrac{1}{x}$, twice the product is $2 \cdot x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} = 2$, so
$$
\left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = x^2 - 2 + \frac{1}{x^2} = 9 .
$$
This gives $x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2} = 9 + 2 = 11$. The value $9$ omits twice the product, $7$ subtracts $2$ instead of adding it, and $10$ uses $x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} = 1$ for twice the product, omitting the factor $2$.
