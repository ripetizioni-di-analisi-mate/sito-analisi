What is the greatest integer that satisfies the inequality $2\,(x - 3) < 7 - x$?

- $5$
- $3$
- $12$
- $4$

## Explanation

Expanding the product gives $2x - 6 < 7 - x$, and rearranging gives $3x < 13$. Dividing by the positive number $3$ preserves the inequality sign, so $x < \dfrac{13}{3}$, and $\dfrac{13}{3}$ lies between $4$ and $5$. The greatest integer that satisfies the inequality is therefore $4$. Indeed, $x = 4$ gives $2 < 3$, while $x = 5$ gives $4 < 2$, which is false. The value $3$ multiplies by $2$ only the variable $x$, giving $2x - 3$. The value $12$ omits division by $3$, giving $x < 13$.
