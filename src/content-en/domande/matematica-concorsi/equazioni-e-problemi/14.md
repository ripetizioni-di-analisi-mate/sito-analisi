A company uses small and large vans, all loaded to capacity. Three small vans and two large vans carry a total of $46$ metric quintals, and a large van carries $8$ metric quintals more than a small one. How many metric quintals does a large van carry?

- $6$ metric quintals
- $15.6$ metric quintals
- $9.2$ metric quintals
- $14$ metric quintals

## Explanation

Let $p$ and $g$ be the loads carried by a small van and a large van, respectively. This gives the system
$$
\begin{cases}
3p + 2g = 46 \\
g = p + 8
\end{cases}
$$
Using **substitution**, write $p + 8$ in place of $g$ in the first equation to get $3p + 2p + 16 = 46$, or $5p = 30$. Then $p = 6$ and $g = 14$, and indeed $18 + 28 = 46$. A small van carries $6$ metric quintals. The result of $15.6$ metric quintals multiplies only the term $p$ by $2$ and leaves $8$ undoubled, giving $3p + 2p + 8 = 46$. The result of $9.2$ metric quintals is the average $\dfrac{46}{5}$, which assumes that all five vans carry the same load.
