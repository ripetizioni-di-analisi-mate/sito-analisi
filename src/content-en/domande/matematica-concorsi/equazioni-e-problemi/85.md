Two full-price tickets and one reduced ticket cost $23$ euros in total. To find the price of each type of ticket, the total cost of a second purchase is also known. Which of the following second purchases makes it possible to find both prices?

- Four full-price and two reduced
- One full-price and two reduced
- Six full-price and three reduced
- Eight full-price and four reduced

## Explanation

Calling $x$ the price of a full-price ticket and $y$ that of a reduced ticket, the first purchase gives the equation $2x+y=23$. A purchase of four full-price and two reduced tickets costs $4x+2y=2\,(2x+y)$, and the same holds for six full-price and three reduced and for eight full-price and four reduced, which cost three and four times $2x+y$. So the cost of these purchases is already determined by the first equation and adds no information about the prices. The purchase of one full-price and two reduced tickets instead gives an equation $x+2y=c$, whose coefficients are not proportional to $2$ and $1$. Therefore the system formed by the two equations has exactly one solution, and both prices can be found.
