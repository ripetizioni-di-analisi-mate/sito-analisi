What is the solution of $\dfrac{x + 1}{2} - \dfrac{2x - 1}{3} = \dfrac{x}{6} - 1$?

- $\dfrac{7}{2}$
- $\dfrac{11}{2}$
- $3$
- $11$

## Explanation

The least common multiple of the denominators is $6$, so multiply every term by $6$, including $-1$, which becomes $-6$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
3\,(x + 1) - 2\,(2x - 1) &= x - 6 \\[6pt]
3x + 3 - 4x + 2 &= x - 6 \\[6pt]
11 &= 2x .
\end{aligned}
$$
The solution is therefore $x = \dfrac{11}{2}$. The value $11$ stops at the intermediate equation $2x = 11$. The product $-2\,(2x - 1)$ equals $-4x + 2$ because $(-2) \cdot (-1) = 2$. Writing $-4x - 2$ instead gives $\dfrac{7}{2}$. The value $3$ leaves the term $-1$ unchanged instead of multiplying it by $6$.
