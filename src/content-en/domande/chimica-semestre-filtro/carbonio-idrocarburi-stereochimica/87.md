After electrophilic attack on benzene, which step restores aromaticity in ordinary electrophilic substitution?

- Loss of a proton from the electrophile-bearing carbon, restoring the pi system.
- Proton loss from another carbon while leaving the electrophile-bearing carbon saturated.
- Nucleophilic attack on the intermediate, producing an addition product saturated at that site.
- Removal of hydride H⁻ from the electrophile-bearing carbon.

## Explanation

Initial attack produces an intermediate lacking aromatic continuity. A base removes H⁺, and the C–H electron pair remains in the system to restore pi bonding rather than departing as H⁻. The electrophile-bearing carbon becomes sp² again. Nucleophilic addition or deprotonation leaving that carbon saturated does not achieve this restoration.
