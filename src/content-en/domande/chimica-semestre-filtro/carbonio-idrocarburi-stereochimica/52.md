A strong bulky base removes a beta proton more readily than it attacks a shielded carbon. Which distinction explains this behaviour?

- Steric hindrance changes only the base’s formal charge.
- Nucleophilicity is defined only by protonation equilibrium.
- Every strong base is necessarily the fastest nucleophile on every substrate.
- Basicity and nucleophilicity measure different properties affected differently by steric hindrance.

## Explanation

Basicity concerns protonation equilibrium; nucleophilicity concerns attack rate on an electrophile. Access to a proton can be less hindered than approach to carbon for SN2. A bulky base can therefore favour elimination without contradicting its high basicity.
