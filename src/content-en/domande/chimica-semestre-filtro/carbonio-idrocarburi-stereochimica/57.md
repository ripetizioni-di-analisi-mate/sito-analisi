SN2 occurs at a stereogenic centre. Is it correct to assign S automatically because the reactant was R?

- Product CIP priorities must be recalculated after geometric inversion.
- Final configuration follows only from optical-rotation sign.
- Yes, every incoming substituent always preserves the CIP order.
- No, SN2 always retains the reactant’s geometric arrangement.

## Explanation

Arrangement inversion is characteristic of backside attack, but the incoming group may occupy a different CIP rank. The absolute descriptor combines geometry and priorities, so it cannot be changed using a letter-only rule. If relative priority ordering is preserved, R and S exchange.
