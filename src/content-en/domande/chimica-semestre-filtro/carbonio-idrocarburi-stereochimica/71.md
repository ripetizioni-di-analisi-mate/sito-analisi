Branching often lowers the boiling point of isomeric alkanes. Why cannot the same rule automatically be transferred to melting point?

- C–C bonds are always broken during melting.
- Melting also depends on symmetry and crystal packing.
- Melting depends only on contact surface in the liquid, not on solid structure.
- More symmetrical molecules must always form less stable crystals than asymmetrical ones.

## Explanation

The solid requires collective molecular organization in a lattice. A compact symmetrical shape may pack favourably, altering crystal energy. This adds a factor beyond the interactions considered for boiling. Ordinary melting of a molecular solid is not covalent cleavage of its molecules.
