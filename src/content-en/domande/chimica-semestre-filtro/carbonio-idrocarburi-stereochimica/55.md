Why does a tertiary halide hinder ordinary SN2 compared with a methyl halide?

- SN2 first requires beta-hydrogen abstraction by the nucleophile.
- Tertiary substitution always makes the leaving group poorer, regardless of the halogen.
- Substitution necessarily requires a hydrogen on the attacked carbon.
- Nearby groups shield backside approach to carbon.

## Explanation

SN2 attack must reach carbon opposite the leaving group. Three carbon groups increase steric obstruction to that geometry. The explanation does not involve carbocation stability because the model is concerted. Any SN1 or E2 pathway requires a separate analysis of conditions.
