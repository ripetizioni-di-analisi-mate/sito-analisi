Two groups are exchanged once at a tetrahedral centre with four different substituents, without changing group identities. What happens to configuration?

- The centre’s configuration is inverted.
- The centre necessarily loses stereogenicity.
- Only conformation changes through bond rotation.
- Configuration is always preserved.

## Explanation

One exchange changes the parity of the four-group arrangement and inverts the centre. Since substituent identities remain unchanged, CIP priorities also remain unchanged and R becomes S or vice versa. The centre still has four different groups; the exchange alone does not remove stereogenicity.
