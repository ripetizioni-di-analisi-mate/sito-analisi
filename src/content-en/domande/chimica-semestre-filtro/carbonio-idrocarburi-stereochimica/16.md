Why does rotation around C=C face a much higher barrier than ordinary rotation around C–C?

- A second sigma bond between the same carbons must be broken.
- A sigma bond by itself forbids all rotation even in alkanes.
- Rotation disrupts the sideways overlap forming the pi bond.
- The barrier comes only from C–H rotation without changing pi overlap.

## Explanation

The pi bond requires suitably aligned p orbitals at the two ends. Twisting reduces overlap at substantial energetic cost. C=C contains one sigma contribution, not two. Single-bond rotation has conformational barriers but lacks this pi constraint, which allows distinguishable E and Z configurations.
