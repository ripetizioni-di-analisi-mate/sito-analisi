Two pure enantiomers have equivalent properties in an achiral environment but bind with different affinities to the same chiral protein site. No covalent transformation of either ligand occurs. Which interpretation is consistent?

- The chiral site forms diastereomeric complexes with the two ligands, with nonequivalent interactions.
- The site necessarily converts one ligand into its enantiomer before binding.
- Different affinities demonstrate different ligand connectivity.
- Affinity depends only on molar mass, so the two values must coincide.

## Explanation

Producing the mirror image of the entire complex would require reflecting both ligand and protein site. Here only the ligand changes, while the site retains its configuration: the two complexes are not an enantiomeric pair and can have different stabilities. Chiral discrimination requires no R/S conversion, connectivity change or ligand mass difference.
