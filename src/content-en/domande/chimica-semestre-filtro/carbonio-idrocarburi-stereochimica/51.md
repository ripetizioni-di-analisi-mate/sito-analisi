Water, an amine and a thiolate can act as nucleophiles. What property do they share during the attack step?

- Availability of an electron pair to donate to the electrophile.
- The ability to donate a proton independently of available electron pairs.
- An obligatory negative charge on every reagent.
- An unpaired electron transferred in a radical attack.

## Explanation

The operational definition concerns pair donation. A nucleophile may be neutral, such as water or an amine, or anionic, such as a thiolate. Charge often affects reactivity but is not the sole criterion. Proton donation instead describes acidic behaviour; an unpaired electron belongs to a radical model, distinct from electron-pair attack.
