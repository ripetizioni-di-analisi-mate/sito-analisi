With substrate and anionic nucleophile fixed, what effect of a polar aprotic solvent can favour SN2 over a protic solvent strongly solvating the anion?

- The rate becomes independent of nucleophile concentration.
- Obligatory formation of a free carbocation before attack.
- The solvent donates stronger hydrogen bonds to the anion, increasing its accessibility for attack.
- Less nucleophile shielding by solvent-donated hydrogen bonds.

## Explanation

A protic solvent can strongly stabilize the anion through hydrogen bonding, reducing its availability for attack. A polar aprotic solvent does not donate those hydrogen bonds and can make many anions more reactive. This is a trend under comparable conditions, not a guarantee of SN2 for every substrate-solvent combination.
