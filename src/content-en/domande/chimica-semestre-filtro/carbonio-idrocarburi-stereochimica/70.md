For similarly sized isomeric alkanes, which boiling-point trend often accompanies greater branching?

- No possible change because molecular formula is identical.
- An obligatory increase because molecular mass increases.
- An increase because a compact shape always offers more intermolecular contact surface.
- A decrease through less effective dispersion interactions between molecular surfaces.

## Explanation

Isomers have equal molecular mass but different shape and contact surfaces. A more compact structure can reduce effective dispersion interactions and lower boiling point. Identical formula does not imply identical physical properties, and compactness must not be confused with an obligatory increase in surface available for intermolecular contacts.
