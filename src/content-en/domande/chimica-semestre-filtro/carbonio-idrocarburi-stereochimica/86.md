Which description distinguishes a purine skeleton from a pyrimidine skeleton?

- Purine and pyrimidine each have one six-membered ring; only nitrogen count differs.
- Purine has two fused six-membered rings.
- Purine contains two separate rings connected only by one single bond.
- Purine has fused six- and five-membered rings.

## Explanation

Purine is a fused heterocyclic system, whereas pyrimidine has one six-membered ring. “Fused” means the rings share atoms and an edge, rather than simply being joined by an external bond. The aromatic purine system contains ten pi electrons and occurs in adenine and guanine bases.
