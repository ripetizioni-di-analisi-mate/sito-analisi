SN1 of a chiral substrate produces both enantiomers but not exactly in a 1:1 ratio. Which interpretation is compatible?

- The carbocation must be tetrahedral with four intact bonds.
- Carbocation rotation around a sigma bond alternately converts it between R and S.
- Any deviation from 1:1 necessarily proves pure SN2.
- The carbocation can be planar while ion pairing or solvent makes attack at its faces unequal.

## Explanation

Planarity permits two attack directions but does not make them equally accessible in a real medium. A nearby leaving group and solvent organization can influence distribution. Complete racemization is therefore a useful limiting case, not a universal quantitative consequence of every SN1.
