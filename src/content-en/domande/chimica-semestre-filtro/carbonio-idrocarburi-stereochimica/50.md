An electron-withdrawing substituent is moved further from a centre through a sigma-bond chain without conjugation. How does its inductive effect on that centre generally change?

- It is identical at any distance.
- It increases as the group moves further along the chain.
- It weakens with distance.
- It necessarily becomes resonance donation.

## Explanation

The inductive effect is polarization transmitted through sigma bonds and attenuates along the chain. Resonance instead requires an orbital arrangement allowing delocalization; simply increasing distance does not create it. Distance and conjugation must therefore be considered separately in an electronic comparison.
