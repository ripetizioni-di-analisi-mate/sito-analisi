A substitution produces a carbon skeleton rearranged relative to the substrate. Which mechanistic feature is supported, subject to further controls?

- A resonance contributor with nuclei transferred between positions.
- Simple conformational rotation changing carbon connectivity.
- An intermediate capable of rearrangement, such as a carbocation.
- An elementary intermediate-free SN2 as the only explanation.

## Explanation

An alkyl shift at a carbocation can change carbon connectivity before nucleophilic attack. A simple hydride shift instead moves the positive centre without itself changing the carbon skeleton. The result is consistent with a multistep pathway. The datum alone does not identify every mechanistic detail, but elementary SN2 preserves the skeleton during concerted attack and does not contain that intermediate step.
