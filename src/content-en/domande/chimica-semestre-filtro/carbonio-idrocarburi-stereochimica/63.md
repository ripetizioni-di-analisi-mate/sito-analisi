In alkyl bromide elimination described by the elementary E2 law, initial rate is proportional to base concentration at fixed substrate and temperature. What does this dependence indicate?

- The substrate does not appear in the rate law.
- Proportionality proves that two HBr molecules are lost per substrate molecule.
- The base participates in the rate-determining concerted step.
- The base acts only after a rate-limiting ionization independent of it.

## Explanation

The model follows $v=k[\mathrm{RX}][\mathrm B]$. With fixed substrate concentration, base dependence remains linear, consistent with its participation in the concerted step. An E1 model with rate-limiting ionization would lack this elementary dependence. Kinetic order does not determine how many HBr molecules are eliminated: the formal balance of one event corresponds to one HBr, with the proton transferred to the base and bromide departing as the leaving group.
