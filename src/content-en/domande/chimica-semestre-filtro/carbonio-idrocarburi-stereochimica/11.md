In an SN1 solvolysis with rate-limiting ionization, increasing nucleophile does not appreciably change the initial rate at unchanged solvent and substrate. How is this interpreted?

- Substitution must be concerted and bimolecular.
- The rate-limiting step precedes nucleophilic attack.
- The nucleophile participates in no reaction step.
- Substrate concentration is also irrelevant to the rate.

## Explanation

The model follows $v=k[\mathrm{RX}]$: ionization produces the intermediate before nucleophilic attack. The nucleophile remains necessary for product formation despite its absence from the rate-limiting law. Lack of rate dependence on a reactant therefore does not mean that it is absent from the full mechanism.
