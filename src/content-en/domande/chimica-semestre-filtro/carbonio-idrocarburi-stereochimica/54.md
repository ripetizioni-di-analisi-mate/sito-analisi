For comparable alkyl halides under common substitution conditions, why is iodide generally a better leaving group than fluoride?

- Iodine departs as a cation, leaving the pair on carbon.
- Because greater nucleophilicity always implies better leaving-group ability.
- Fluoride is better because C–F is stronger.
- Departure is favoured by iodide’s lower basicity and the weaker C–I bond.

## Explanation

A leaving group must accept the bond pair and form a relatively stable species. In the usual alkyl-halide comparison, iodide is a weaker base and C–I is easier to break than C–F. The stated context avoids transferring this trend to different aromatic mechanisms.
