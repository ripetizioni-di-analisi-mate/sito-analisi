Why cannot the ordinary tertiary alkyl-halide SN1 scheme automatically be applied to chlorobenzene?

- Chloride departure generates a benzylic carbocation without changing the skeleton.
- A protic solvent makes aryl and alkyl C–Cl bonds equivalent in ionization.
- The chlorine-bearing carbon is sp³, as in benzyl chloride.
- Chlorine is attached to an aromatic carbon and ionization would require a different type of cation.

## Explanation

An aryl halide has C–Cl attached to an sp² ring carbon. Forming an aryl cation is not equivalent to forming a conventionally stabilized tertiary alkyl carbocation. Other aromatic substitutions can occur under suitable conditions, but require their own models rather than automatic transfer of alkyl SN1.
