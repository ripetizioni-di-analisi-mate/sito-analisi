Why can SN1 and E1 compete during solvolysis of the same suitable substrate?

- A carbocation forces both pathways to give the same product.
- E1 always uses a carbanion identical to the SN1 intermediate.
- Both require concerted backside attack before leaving-group departure.
- They can share a carbocation followed by nucleophilic attack or beta-H loss.

## Explanation

After ionization, the intermediate can be captured by a nucleophile or lose a proton from an adjacent carbon to form an alkene. A shared step does not make the later transformations equivalent. Solvent, temperature and the species present influence the substitution-to-elimination product ratio.
