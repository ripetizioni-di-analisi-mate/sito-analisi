Replacing an unhindered base with a strong bulky base can cause which E2 regioselectivity change with a suitable substrate?

- C–H/leaving-group geometry ceases to matter.
- The less substituted alkene fraction can increase through easier access to certain beta hydrogens.
- The bulky base stabilizes the E2 carbocation intermediate and favours its rearrangement.
- The most substituted alkene becomes obligatory for every substrate.

## Explanation

Steric bulk can favour abstraction of less shielded hydrogens and alter competition between regioisomeric pathways. This explains why Zaitsev orientation is not absolute. A beta hydrogen and suitable conformation remain necessary. The E2 model is concerted, so it does not contain the carbocation intermediate postulated in one alternative.
