Why does a bulky cyclohexane substituent often favour an equatorial position?

- It reduces 1,3-diaxial steric interactions.
- The equatorial position increases 1,3-diaxial contacts and makes them stabilizing.
- The axial position changes above/below configuration and produces a different diastereoisomer.
- The conformers necessarily have equal energy because they have the same formula.

## Explanation

In an axial position the group approaches other axial bonds or substituents on the same side of the ring. Greater bulk makes these contacts less favourable, often stabilizing the equatorial conformer. The comparison concerns conformational energies of the same molecule, without composition changes or new covalent bonds.
