Why does an alkane generally not form a concentrated aqueous solution?

- Its weakly polar surface does not effectively compensate water interactions through hydrogen bonding.
- C–H bonds make the alkane an effective hydrogen-bond donor to water.
- The hydrocarbon surface forms stronger hydrogen bonds than water, preventing mixing.
- A nonpolar molecule cannot have any dispersion interaction with water.

## Explanation

Alkanes are weakly polar and lack groups suited to ordinary hydrogen-bond donation or acceptance with water. Transfer into aqueous solution is therefore less favoured than for compounds bearing suitable polar groups. This is low solubility, not an absolute prohibition on dissolution.
