A copper–nickel alloy is stated to be uniform and single-phase. What prevents automatically classifying it as heterogeneous?

- Two different elements must always occupy two phases.
- A solution necessarily requires water as solvent.
- An alloy is heterogeneous whenever its pure components have different densities.
- A solution can also be solid.

## Explanation

Solution classification concerns homogeneity and component distribution, not necessarily the liquid state. The stated single-phase structure supports a solid-solution model; different pure-component densities do not require separate phases. This does not mean every alloy is single-phase: another alloy would require information about its structure and composition.
