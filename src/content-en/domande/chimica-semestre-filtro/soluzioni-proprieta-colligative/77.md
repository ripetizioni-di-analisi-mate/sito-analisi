Two ideal solutions, $0.10\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ $\mathrm{CaCl_2}$ with $i=3$ and $0.30\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ glucose with $i=1$, are at the same temperature. What follows without membrane data?

- Glucose produces no osmotic pressure because it is not an electrolyte.
- They are isoosmotic, but isotonicity relative to a cell requires more information.
- They are necessarily isotonic to every cell.
- Calcium chloride gives triple osmotic pressure because it is a salt.

## Explanation

Both have $c_{\mathrm{osm}}=0.30\,\mathrm{Osm\,L^{-1}}$ in the model. This establishes equal ideal osmotic pressures at equal temperature, not equal sustained effects on cell volume. Tonicity depends on which solutes cross or remain effectively separated by the membrane over the observation period.
