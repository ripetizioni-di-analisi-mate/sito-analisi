Two solutions are mixed and the solute being considered partly precipitates. Why does total initial solute divided by final volume not necessarily give dissolved concentration?

- Part of the solute is in the solid phase and must be excluded from the liquid-phase balance.
- Precipitation changes only the denominator, leaving all initial moles dissolved.
- Every precipitation completely removes the solute from solution, making its dissolved concentration zero.
- The dissolved amount always equals that in the initially more concentrated solution.

## Explanation

Total solute is conserved in a closed system but is distributed between phases. Dissolved concentration concerns only the liquid portion. Reaction, stoichiometry and solubility equilibrium must therefore be considered. The no-reaction mixing formula cannot automatically describe only the solute remaining in solution.
