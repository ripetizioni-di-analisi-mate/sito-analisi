Two solutions use $5.0\,\mathrm g$ reagent and final volume $100\,\mathrm{mL}$: anhydrous glucose ($M=180$) or monohydrate ($M=198$), in $\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$. Which contains fewer glucose molecules per litre?

- The solution prepared from anhydrous glucose.
- The solution prepared from monohydrate.
- The denser solution: comparison is impossible without knowing both densities.
- They must match because masses match.

## Explanation

At equal mass the reagent with greater molar mass supplies fewer formula-unit moles. Each monohydrate unit contains one glucose molecule, while crystal water joins the solvent. Specifying reagent form is therefore necessary in calculations based on mass–volume percentage: equal grams do not ensure equal molecule counts.
