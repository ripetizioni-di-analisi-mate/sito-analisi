At constant temperature a solution is in equilibrium with undissolved solid. More of the same solid is added without changing the solvent. What happens to equilibrium concentration?

- It becomes the ratio of all added solute, dissolved and solid, to solution volume.
- It remains at the solubility while solid persists.
- It remains constant only while the mixture is stirred.
- It is no longer definable because two phases prevent assigning concentration to the liquid phase.

## Explanation

The solution is saturated under the stated conditions. Added solid increases the undissolved phase but does not change equilibrium concentration while temperature, solvent and solid identity remain unchanged. Confusing total solute with dissolved solute would predict an increase excluded by the saturation constraint.
