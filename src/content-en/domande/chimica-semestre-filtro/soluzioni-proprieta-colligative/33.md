On an ionic-charge basis, what is equivalent to $2.5\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ of $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$?

- $1.25\,\mathrm{mEq\,L^{-1}}$.
- $2.5\,\mathrm{mEq\,L^{-1}}$.
- $7.5\,\mathrm{mEq\,L^{-1}}$.
- $5.0\,\mathrm{mEq\,L^{-1}}$.

## Explanation

Each calcium ion carries two positive elementary charges: $2\cdot2.5=5.0\,\mathrm{mEq\,L^{-1}}$. This counts charge equivalents, not osmotic particles. Divalence does not turn one ion into two particles; total solution osmolarity also requires information about anions and other solutes.
