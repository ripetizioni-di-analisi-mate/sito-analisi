A cell initially contains non-permeating solutes totalling $0.300\,\mathrm{Osm\,L^{-1}}$. It is placed in a solution containing only urea at $0.300\,\mathrm{Osm\,L^{-1}}$, at the same temperature. The membrane passes water and urea. Does equal initial osmolarity establish sustained isotonicity? Explain.

- Yes: urea entry leaves internal osmolarity unchanged by definition.
- No: urea is a non-electrolyte and therefore does not contribute to initial osmolarity.
- No: initial isoosmolarity does not ensure isotonicity when solute crosses the membrane.
- Yes: urea maintains the initial osmotic gradient indefinitely even after equilibration.

## Explanation

Equal total particle concentrations establish initial isoosmolarity. Urea can cross the membrane and accumulate inside, where the non-permeating solutes remain. Tonicity depends on effectively retained solutes and observation time, so initial isoosmolarity does not guarantee constant volume. Water entry can consequently cause swelling; an equilibrium volume would require further conditions on the external compartment and the cell’s mechanical properties.
