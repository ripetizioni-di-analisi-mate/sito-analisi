A real solution has vapour pressure different from the ideal Raoult prediction. Which quantity describes the solvent's thermodynamic contribution?

- Analytical solute concentration alone, without correction factors.
- Mass fraction automatically substituted for mole fraction.
- Solvent activity relative to an appropriate standard state.
- Solute dissociation fraction alone, sufficient to correct all solvent interactions.

## Explanation

In a non-ideal description, mole fraction alone does not represent all interactions. Solvent activity incorporates departure from ideal solution behaviour and connects to its chemical potential. In the ideal limit, using pure solvent as reference, activity equals mole fraction and the simple Raoult form is recovered.
