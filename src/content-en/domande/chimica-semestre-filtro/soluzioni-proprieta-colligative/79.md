A cell initially has $300\,\mathrm{mOsm\,L^{-1}}$ non-permeating solutes. A large external medium contains $300\,\mathrm{mOsm\,L^{-1}}$ non-permeating solutes plus $100\,\mathrm{mOsm\,L^{-1}}$ freely permeating urea. In an ideal model without mechanical effects, can volume return to its initial value after urea equilibrates?

- No: urea necessarily remains outside the cell.
- Yes: the medium is initially hyperosmotic, but its non-permeating component is isotonic in this model.
- No: every hyperosmotic medium is permanently hypertonic.
- Yes: because urea never contributes to measured osmolarity.

## Explanation

Once urea distributes across both sides, its contribution balances. Non-permeating concentrations match at the initial volume, which can therefore be recovered in the model. This does not exclude transient volume changes. Urea counts towards total osmolarity, but its permeability changes the meaning of the long-term comparison.
