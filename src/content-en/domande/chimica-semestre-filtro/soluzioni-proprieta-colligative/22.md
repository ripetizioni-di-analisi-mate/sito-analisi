A very dilute strong-electrolyte solution is compared with a concentrated weak-electrolyte solution. What do ‘strong’ and ‘weak’ correctly describe?

- Higher or lower solution density.
- A universal conductivity ranking of the two samples.
- Only the solute amount per litre.
- The extent of dissociation or ionisation under the stated conditions.

## Explanation

Electrolyte strength and concentration answer different questions. A strong electrolyte can be very dilute, while a weak one can be concentrated yet partly ionised. Comparing conductivity also requires ion amounts, charges and mobilities; the strength label alone does not determine the experimental result.
