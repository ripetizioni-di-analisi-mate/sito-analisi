In an ideal completely dissociated $\mathrm{CaCl_2}$ model, why is the osmotic factor three while calcium charge equivalents per mole of salt are two?

- Double charge turns each calcium ion into two independent osmotic particles.
- Osmolarity counts three ions; calcium equivalents count the cation's two charges.
- The values must match, so the model is necessarily inconsistent.
- The osmotic factor sums all charge magnitudes, while equivalents count ions without considering their charges.

## Explanation

One formula unit yields one calcium and two chlorides: three particles, irrespective of their charge magnitudes. Calcium alone carries two positive charges. The two chlorides also supply two negative equivalents altogether, satisfying neutrality. Particles, salt moles and equivalents are distinct counts selected according to the question.
