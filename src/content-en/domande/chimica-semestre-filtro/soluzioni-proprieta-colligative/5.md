Dissolve $6.00\,\mathrm g$ of urea, with molar mass $60.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$, in $400\,\mathrm g$ of water. Assume ideal behaviour and undissociated urea. Calculate molality and osmolality.

- $0.250\,\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$ and $0.250\,\mathrm{Osm\,kg^{-1}}$.
- $0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$ and $0.100\,\mathrm{Osm\,kg^{-1}}$.
- $0.250\,\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$ and $0.500\,\mathrm{Osm\,kg^{-1}}$.
- $250\,\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$ and $250\,\mathrm{Osm\,kg^{-1}}$.

## Explanation

The added urea amounts to $\dfrac{6.00}{60.0}=0.100\,\mathrm{mol}$. Solvent mass is $0.400\,\mathrm{kg}$, giving a molality of $0.250\,\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$. One mole of urea ideally supplies one mole of particles, so osmolality has the same numerical value. Neither quantity uses the final solution volume, which is not provided. The numerical equality depends on absent dissociation, not on a general identity between molality and osmolality.
