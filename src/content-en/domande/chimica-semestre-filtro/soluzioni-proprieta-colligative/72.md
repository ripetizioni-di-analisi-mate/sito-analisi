In an ideal dilute model with equal temperature on both sides, a neutral solute freely crosses a membrane and reaches equal activity on both sides under matching pressure conditions. What contribution can it still sustain by itself?

- A permanent concentration gradient without other constraints.
- No sustained osmotic difference from its own unequal distribution.
- A permanent osmotic difference equal to the initial one.
- Twice the osmotic pressure because it is now on both sides.

## Explanation

Permeation lets the solute equilibrate. Its balanced contribution no longer sustains the initial difference between compartments, although it still contributes to each solution's osmotic properties. Other retained solutes can maintain a volume effect. Absence of a transmembrane difference must not be confused with absence of particles.
