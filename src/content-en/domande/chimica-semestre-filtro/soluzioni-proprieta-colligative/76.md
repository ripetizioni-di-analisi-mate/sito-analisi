Only the concentration gradient is known for an ion across a membrane. Why may this be insufficient to predict passive-transport direction?

- The electrical-potential difference also contributes to the driving force.
- Ions always require active transport, even when a passive pathway is available.
- Charge makes every concentration gradient irrelevant.
- Direction is always determined by ion charge alone, irrespective of the potential difference.

## Explanation

A charged species responds to both chemical and electrical contributions. They can favour the same direction or oppose each other, requiring the electrochemical gradient together with membrane permeability. Simple concentration-only Fick diffusion describes a restricted case, not every biological ion-transport process.
