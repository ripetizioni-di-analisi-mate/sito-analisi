Glucose dissolves readily in water but remains mainly molecular. What follows?

- Every soluble solute is necessarily a strong electrolyte.
- A neutral molecule cannot interact with water.
- High solubility does not imply strong ionic dissociation.
- Hydrogen bonding always produces free ions.

## Explanation

Glucose's polar groups hydrogen-bond with water without requiring ion formation. Solubility describes how much compound dissolves; electrolyte strength describes production of ionic species. The distinction also matters when counting particles for colligative properties.
