Two ideal gases have equal mole amounts but different molar masses in the same container. How do their partial pressures compare?

- They depend only on each gas's total mass.
- They are equal at the same temperature and volume.
- The gas with greater molar mass has greater pressure.
- They are inversely proportional to molar masses.

## Explanation

For each ideal component, $p_j=n_jRT/V$. Equal moles, temperature and volume give equal partial pressures. Molar mass does not appear directly; it would only convert a specified mass into moles. Confusing mass fractions with mole fractions leads to an incorrect allocation of pressure.
