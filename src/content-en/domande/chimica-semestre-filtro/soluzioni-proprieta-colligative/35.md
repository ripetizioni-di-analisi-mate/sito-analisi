$0.010\,\mathrm{mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$ is neutralised to $\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}$, transferring one proton per molecule. How many acid–base equivalents participate?

- $0.030\,\mathrm{Eq}$.
- $0.020\,\mathrm{Eq}$.
- $0.0033\,\mathrm{Eq}$.
- $0.010\,\mathrm{Eq}$.

## Explanation

The specified reaction transfers one proton per molecule, so $n_{\mathrm{eq}}=1\cdot0.010=0.010\,\mathrm{Eq}$. Counting all three acidic hydrogens would assume complete neutralisation, different from the stated process. Equivalents are not fixed by molecular formula alone; they must be related to the process being considered.
