Molar mass is inferred by freezing-point depression assuming undissociated solute, but the solute actually produces more particles per mole. In the ideal model, what happens to apparent molar mass?

- It is higher than the true value.
- It remains exact because cryoscopy does not depend on particle count.
- It equals a single ion's molar mass for every electrolyte.
- It is lower than the true value.

## Explanation

Dissociation produces greater depression than the same undissociated solute amount. Assigning the whole effect to independent molecules overestimates compound moles. Dividing mass by too many moles gives a molar mass that is too small. Correction requires the effective factor under measurement conditions.
