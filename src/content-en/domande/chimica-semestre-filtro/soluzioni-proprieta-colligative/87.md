Which statement correctly distinguishes interstitial oedema from haemolysis?

- Oedema depends only on total osmolarity without pressure or protein contributions.
- Oedema concerns fluid in interstitial spaces; haemolysis is red-cell rupture.
- Oedema means red-cell volume increase; haemolysis means fluid accumulation outside vessels.
- Haemolysis is defined as a reversible decrease in red-cell volume.

## Explanation

The terms describe different phenomena and organisational scales. Oedema also involves hydrostatic pressures, plasma proteins, capillary permeability and lymphatic drainage. Haemolysis compromises red-cell integrity; strong osmotic water entry is one possible cause. Total-osmolarity comparison alone does not explain every capillary exchange.
