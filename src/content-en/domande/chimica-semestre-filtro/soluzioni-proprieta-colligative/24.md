A $\mathrm{CaCl_2}$ solution is $0.040\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and completely dissociated. What is the $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ concentration?

- $0.080\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $0.120\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $0.040\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $0.020\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.

## Explanation

Each formula unit produces two chloride anions, so $c_{\mathrm{Cl^-}}=2\cdot0.040=0.080\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. A factor of three would count calcium and chloride together, giving total particle concentration in the ideal limit. Calcium’s divalent charge does not double calcium ion number; it determines the stoichiometric ratio required for neutrality.
