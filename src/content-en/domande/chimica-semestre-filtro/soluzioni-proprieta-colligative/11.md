An ideal solution contains $0.200\,\mathrm{mol}$ of non-volatile, undissociated and unassociated solute and $9.80\,\mathrm{mol}$ of volatile solvent. Pure-solvent vapour pressure at the same temperature is $30.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Calculate solution pressure and its lowering.

- $p=30.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $\Delta p=-0.600\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $p=0.600\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $\Delta p=29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $p=29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $\Delta p=0.600\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $p=30.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $\Delta p=0$.

## Explanation

Solvent mole fraction is $\dfrac{9.80}{10.00}=0.980$. Raoult gives $p=0.980\cdot30.0=29.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The positive lowering is $30.0-29.4=0.600\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Pressure and pressure decrease are different quantities. Solute non-volatility is essential to identifying the solvent contribution with the total vapour pressure.
