A solution contains only $10\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ and $20\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of $\mathrm{Cl^-}$. Which balance verifies electroneutrality?

- 30 positive mEq and 30 negative mEq.
- 10 positive mEq and 10 negative mEq.
- 10 positive mEq and 20 negative mEq.
- 20 positive mEq and 20 negative mEq.

## Explanation

Neutrality requires equal positive and negative charge amounts, not equal cation and anion counts. Divalent calcium supplies two equivalents per mole, whereas chloride supplies one. The solution can therefore be neutral while containing twice as many anions as cations; all these species contribute to particle counting.
