In cytoplasm, macromolecules and many solutes form a crowded environment. Why can an ideal dilute model be inadequate?

- Interactions and excluded volume can alter activities relative to concentrations alone.
- Molarity already accounts exactly for every particle interaction.
- Analytical concentration can no longer be defined when the medium contains macromolecules.
- Crowding changes reaction rates only, with no effect on equilibrium properties.

## Explanation

The ideal model neglects interaction contributions that can matter in a concentrated, macromolecule-rich environment. Crowding limits accessible space and can change solute thermodynamic behaviour. Analytical concentration remains definable but does not describe every chemical-potential contribution; system-appropriate models or coefficients are needed.
