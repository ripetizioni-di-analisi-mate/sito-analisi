Two solutions supply a total of $0.060\,\mathrm{mol}$ of the same solute. Initial volumes sum to $200\,\mathrm{mL}$ but measured final volume is $190\,\mathrm{mL}$. Without reaction, which molarity is correct?

- $3.16\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $0.060\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- Approximately $0.316\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $0.300\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.

## Explanation

The balance conserves moles but does not guarantee additive volumes. Use the measured value: $c=\dfrac{0.060}{0.190}\simeq0.316\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Contraction makes concentration higher than division by the initial volume sum would suggest. No additional numerator correction is needed because no substance was lost.
