At the stated temperature, a sparingly soluble non-reactive gas has $k_H=1.60\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,atm^{-1}}$ in the convention $c=k_Hp$. Its partial pressure is $0.750\,\mathrm{atm}$. Calculate equilibrium concentration in the dilute limit.

- $1.20\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $2.13\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $1.20\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $1.60\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.

## Explanation

The constant is defined by $c=k_Hp$, so multiply $1.60\cdot10^{-3}$ by $0.750$. The product is $1.20\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$; temperature and gas properties remain those specified for the constant. Dividing the constant by pressure is not an alternative convention: the ratio would not have concentration units. The pressure unit cancels, leaving amount per solution volume.
