A membrane retains the solute. Pressure exceeding the osmotic difference relative to solvent is applied on the solution side. Which process is possible?

- Flow always towards the solution regardless of pressure.
- Flow stops at every pressure above the threshold, with no possible reversal.
- Dilution by water entry despite pressure exceeding the threshold.
- Reverse osmosis, driving solvent from solution towards the less concentrated side.

## Explanation

Mechanical pressure can oppose and exceed the osmotic tendency. If solute remains retained while solvent can pass, net flow can reverse. Neither solute chemical identity nor membrane selectivity must change. The relevant comparison is the pressure difference between sides, not absolute pressure alone.
