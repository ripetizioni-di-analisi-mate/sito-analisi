For a salt ideally producing three ions, a colligative experiment gives an effective factor below three. Which interpretation is possible?

- The effective factor always equals cation charge.
- The salt's chemical formula must necessarily be wrong.
- The result proves that every particle is undissociated.
- Ionic interactions or association cause departure from the ideal limit.

## Explanation

Stoichiometric counting gives the ideal complete-dissociation limit. Real interactions and association can reduce the colligative effect relative to it. A lower value alone does not determine a universal dissociation fraction; deriving that fraction requires a model separating dissociation from non-ideality.
