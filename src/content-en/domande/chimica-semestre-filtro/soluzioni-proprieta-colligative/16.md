A sample looks uniform to the naked eye but strongly scatters light and contains dispersed particles of colloidal size. Which classification fits best?

- Colloidal dispersion.
- Molecular solution, because every visually uniform sample is single-phase.
- Coarse suspension that must already have settled.
- Pure solvent, because no sediment is visible.

## Explanation

Observation scale matters: a colloid may look macroscopically uniform while containing a distinct dispersed phase. Particle size and light scattering support the colloidal model. Absence of visible sediment proves neither purity nor molecular dispersion; Brownian motion can contribute to dispersion stability.
