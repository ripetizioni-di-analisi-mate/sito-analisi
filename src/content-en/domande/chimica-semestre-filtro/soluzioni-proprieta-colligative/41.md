A problem supplies the activity coefficient of a single ion. Which qualification is correct?

- A single-ion value requires a convention; mean ionic activities are experimentally accessible.
- Every individual ionic activity is measurable without additional conventions.
- An individual coefficient numerically equals the ion's charge number.
- Ions always have coefficient exactly one, even in concentrated solutions.

## Explanation

Separating individual-ion contributions introduces a conventional reference; mean electrolyte quantities are directly connected to thermodynamic measurements. A problem can supply individual coefficients if that convention is stated. The coefficient represents interactions and depends on conditions, whereas charge number describes a different ionic property.
