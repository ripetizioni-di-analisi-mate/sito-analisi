A solution containing only urea is initially isoosmotic to red blood cells permeable to urea. Why can it cause swelling?

- Urea enters but must be excluded from intracellular particle counting because it is not ionised.
- Urea enters while non-permeating intracellular solutes remain retained, favouring water entry.
- The initial urea gradient directly implies net water exit, independently of other solutes.
- Equal initial osmolarity prevents any later change.

## Explanation

Initial isoosmolarity compares all particles but does not account for different permeabilities. Urea can equilibrate and does not provide a lasting counterbalance to retained intracellular solutes. Water can therefore enter and volume increase, potentially causing haemolysis. No unmodelled urea dissociation is required.
