An inert gas is added to an ideal gas mixture at constant temperature and gas-phase volume. The system is in equilibrium with a dilute aqueous solution; gas-phase oxygen moles and its Henry coefficient are assumed unchanged. How does equilibrium dissolved oxygen concentration change?

- It increases because inert gas necessarily drives dissolution towards the liquid even at constant partial pressure.
- It necessarily increases with total pressure.
- It stays unchanged because oxygen partial pressure stays unchanged.
- It decreases because oxygen mole fraction decreases.

## Explanation

For ideal gas, $p_{\mathrm{O_2}}=n_{\mathrm{O_2}}RT/V$. Every quantity on the right stays constant, even though total pressure rises and mole fraction falls. Henry uses partial pressure, so those changes do not alter the predicted concentration. Compression with changing volume would be a different experiment.
