In classical Claisen self-condensation driven by beta-keto ester deprotonation, why does the donor require at least two alpha hydrogens?

- Both are removed together to form the product's C=C double bond.
- They protonate two alkoxides expelled from each ester molecule.
- One allows enolate formation and another remains in the product for deprotonation.
- The second H regenerates base without deprotonating the beta-keto ester.

## Explanation

Initial alpha-H removal forms the nucleophile. After acylation an H must remain between the two carbonyls: base removes it, and anion stabilization drives equilibrium. No alkene forms by simultaneous loss of both H atoms, nor does each ester expel two alkoxides. Product deprotonation consumes base; the requirement concerns this classical pathway.
