A nucleophile opens an epoxide by backside attack at the selected stereogenic carbon. What happens to that center's local geometry in the substitution step?

- A racemic mixture necessarily forms through a free carbocation.
- The original geometry is necessarily retained.
- The bond arrangement at the attacked center is inverted.
- Every other stereogenic center in the molecule is always inverted as well.

## Explanation

Attack from the side opposite the C–O bond gives local inversion, as in an SN2 substitution. This does not require inversion at unattacked centers. Geometric inversion and a change of the R/S letter are also not automatically synonymous: substitution can change the priority order assigned to groups.
