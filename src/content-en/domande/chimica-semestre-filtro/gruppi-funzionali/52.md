A strongly electron-withdrawing group near an aldehyde can favor equilibrium toward the geminal diol. Which explanation is consistent?

- It makes carbonyl carbon less electrophilic, thereby facilitating water attack.
- It acts only as a catalyst and therefore shifts the equilibrium constant.
- It favors water loss from the diol, increasing its equilibrium fraction.
- It makes C more electrophilic and can relatively stabilize the hydrated form.

## Explanation

Hydration adds water to the carbonyl and produces two OH groups on the same carbon. An electron-withdrawing substituent changes the forms' relative energies and can favor the hydrate; it is not merely a catalyst. Greater electrophilicity favors attack, while rate alone does not determine K. Favoring dehydration would instead favor the carbonyl.
