When HCl is added to ethylamine in water, which transformation describes immediate salt formation?

- Nitrogen accepts a proton and ethylammonium forms.
- Nitrogen loses a proton and a nitrogen anion forms.
- Ethylamine remains neutral because HCl can protonate only the solvent.
- Chloride forms a covalent N–Cl bond instead of acting as a counterion.

## Explanation

The amine acts as a Brønsted base: its lone pair binds H+ and nitrogen becomes positively charged. Chloride is the counterion, not a new N–Cl group. The transformation changes protonation and interactions with water without introducing a carbonyl or breaking the existing C–N bond.
