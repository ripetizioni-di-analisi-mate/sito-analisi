Why is rotation around an amide C–N bond hindered relative to an ordinary single C–N bond in an amine?

- Carbonyl carbon is sp3 and therefore blocks rotation.
- The barrier arises only from hydrogen bonds between different molecules.
- Rotation requires complete cleavage of the C–N sigma bond.
- Delocalization gives the bond partial double-bond character.

## Explanation

The N pair participates in conjugation with C=O. Substantial rotation reduces stabilizing overlap and requires energy without completely breaking the sigma bond. Carbonyl carbon is approximately planar; an intrinsic barrier exists without attributing it solely to intermolecular hydrogen bonds. This explains the peptide group's relative rigidity.
