Which feature contributes to the acyl reactivity of a thioester such as acetyl-CoA relative to a comparable oxygen ester?

- A thioester necessarily avoids every tetrahedral intermediate in substitution.
- Sulfur donates to the carbonyl more effectively than oxygen, increasing electrophilicity.
- Sulfur resonance donation toward the carbonyl is less effective.
- Acyl transfer first requires reduction of an S–S bridge in acetyl-CoA.

## Explanation

The thioester retains a carbonyl, but its different electronic interaction with S contributes to greater acyl-transfer availability than many oxygen esters. Leaving-group identity also matters. Acetyl-CoA is not a disulfide: CoA is attached to the acyl group through one sulfur.
