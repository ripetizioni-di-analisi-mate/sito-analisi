For comparison in water under the same conditions, assume $pK_a=10$ for phenol and $pK_a=16$ for cyclohexanol. What is $\dfrac{K_a(\text{phenol})}{K_a(\text{cyclohexanol})}$?

- $6$.
- $10^{-6}$.
- $\dfrac{16}{10}$.
- $10^6$.

## Explanation

Since $K_a=10^{-pK_a}$, the ratio is $\dfrac{10^{-10}}{10^{-16}}=10^6$. The pKa difference becomes an exponent: directly subtracting or dividing the pKa values does not give the ratio of constants. Greater phenoxide stabilization is consistent with phenol's larger dissociation constant.
