Why can an extremely low pH slow imine formation even though moderate acid catalysis is useful?

- Protonated amine is always more nucleophilic than its neutral form.
- Oxygen protonation always makes the carbonyl unable to receive a nucleophile.
- Acid makes every step of imine formation irreversible.
- Most of the amine becomes ammonium and loses nucleophilic availability.

## Explanation

Part of the mechanism benefits from protonation, which facilitates water loss. However, initial attack requires an amine with an available pair. If acid-base equilibrium converts nearly all of it to ammonium, that step is impeded: increasing acidity need not increase the rate monotonically.
