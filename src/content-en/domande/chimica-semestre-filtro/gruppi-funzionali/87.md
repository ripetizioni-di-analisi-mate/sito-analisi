Ethyl benzoate has no enolizable alpha H relative to its carbonyl. Which role can it play in a suitably designed crossed Claisen reaction?

- Acyl acceptor for another compound's enolate.
- Donor of its own enolate by obligatorily removing an aromatic H.
- No role, because every acceptor ester must have alpha H.
- Donor of its own enolate by removing an ethoxy H.

## Explanation

Absence of alpha H prevents ordinary enolate formation on the acyl side but does not remove carbonyl electrophilicity. An external enolate can attack it and displace ethoxide. Ethoxy hydrogens are not alpha hydrogens of the acyl group and should not be used to assign the same mechanism.
