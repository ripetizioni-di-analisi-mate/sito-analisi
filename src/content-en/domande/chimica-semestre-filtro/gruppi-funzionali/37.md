At the same solvent and temperature, why is aniline normally less basic than a simple alkylamine?

- Protonation stabilizes anilinium by delocalizing the N pair into the ring.
- The nitrogen lone pair is delocalized toward the aromatic ring.
- Phenyl inductively donates more electron density to N than an ordinary alkyl group.
- Aniline's pair is more localized on N than the alkylamine's pair.

## Explanation

In aniline the pair contributes to ring conjugation and stabilizes the neutral base. Protonating N uses that pair to form N–H, so it no longer delocalizes in the same way: anilinium must not be assigned the same stabilization. Phenyl does not provide an ordinary alkyl group's inductive donation. The comparison requires the same solvent and temperature.
