A rare cytosine tautomer changes a proton's position and double-bond distribution. Which molecular consequence can follow?

- A different arrangement of hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors.
- Cytosine necessarily gains a proton overall, as in protonation.
- The donor arrangement must remain unchanged by definition.
- Only electrons change, with no proton movement.

## Explanation

Moving a proton can change a hydrogen-bond acceptor into a donor, or vice versa, according to the new bond distribution. Pairing can therefore change. The proton is redistributed rather than added externally: total formula and charge remain unchanged, also distinguishing tautomerism from protonation.
