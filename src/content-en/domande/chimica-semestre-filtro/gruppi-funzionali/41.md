A secondary amine is sought selectively by alkylating a primary amine with a reactive alkyl halide. Which problem can reduce selectivity?

- The secondary amine formed always lacks a lone pair.
- Initial alkylation proceeds only through E2, without substitution products.
- The product amine can undergo further alkylation.
- A primary amine can never be a precursor of quaternary ammonium.

## Explanation

The secondary product retains a lone pair and can compete with the starting amine for additional halide. Tertiary amine and quaternary ammonium may follow. Selectivity therefore does not follow merely from specifying one nominal equivalent: relative concentrations and synthetic strategy must control subsequent reactions.
