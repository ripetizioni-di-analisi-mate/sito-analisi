In an unprotonated carbonyl, why does a nucleophile normally attack carbon rather than oxygen?

- Carbon already bears a localized negative charge.
- Attack at C requires retaining all original pi and sigma bonds simultaneously.
- Oxygen is less electronegative than carbon.
- The bond is polarized toward O, leaving C partially positive.

## Explanation

Oxygen draws electron density toward itself, making carbon the electrophilic center. During attack, the pi electrons move to O: carbon does not acquire five bonds while keeping C=O intact. Reactant polarization and mechanistic electron movement are parts of the same explanation.
