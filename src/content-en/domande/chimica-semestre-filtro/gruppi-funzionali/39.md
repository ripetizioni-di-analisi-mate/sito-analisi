Which property distinguishes $\mathrm{R_4N^+}$ from $\mathrm{R_3NH^+}$, with alkyl R groups?

- R4N+ lacks an N–H proton whose removal would simply regenerate a neutral amine.
- R4N+ always becomes neutral in basic water solely by nitrogen deprotonation.
- R3NH+ has four carbon groups and no hydrogen on N.
- R4N+ has a nucleophilic lone pair whereas R3NH+ does not.

## Explanation

Both species have nitrogen with four bonds and a positive charge. In trialkylammonium, one bond is N–H, so a base can regenerate R3N. In tetraalkylammonium, all four substituents are carbon groups: removing a proton from N is impossible because that proton is absent.
