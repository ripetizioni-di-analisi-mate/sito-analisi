An amine is almost completely protonated by greatly lowering pH. How does the availability of its nitrogen lone pair for nucleophilic attack normally change?

- It decreases because the pair is used in the new N–H bond.
- The neutral-base fraction increases because low pH favors B over BH+.
- It remains identical because protonating N leaves the same lone pair available.
- It increases because positive charge makes N more electron-rich.

## Explanation

Ammonium has four bonds rather than the neutral base's lone pair. If nearly all molecules are protonated, the concentration available to attack the electrophile decreases. Basicity and nucleophilicity remain distinct concepts, but protonation equilibrium can control how much nucleophilic species is present.
