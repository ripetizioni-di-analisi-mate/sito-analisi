In ethyl ethanoate Claisen self-condensation, why is ethoxide commonly used instead of a different alkoxide?

- Because ethoxide is the only base able to remove an ester alpha H.
- To isolate the neutral product directly before acid work-up.
- To avoid introducing a different OR group through transesterification.
- To supply the ethyl group forming the condensation's new C–C bond.

## Explanation

An alkoxide can attack the ester and exchange OR. If it matches the existing group, that exchange does not produce a different ester. Base still forms enolate and deprotonates the beta-keto ester; this choice removes neither those steps nor the need for final acid work-up.
