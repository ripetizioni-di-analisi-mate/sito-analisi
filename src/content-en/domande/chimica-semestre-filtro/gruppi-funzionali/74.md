In the resonance model of a simple carboxylate, how are the two equivalent C–O bonds described?

- They have intermediate single/double-bond character through delocalization.
- They become equivalent only by moving a proton between oxygens.
- One is a covalent bond and the other a purely ionic interaction.
- One remains permanently single and the other permanently double, distinguishable without perturbation.

## Explanation

The two resonance contributors distribute electrons differently but represent one delocalized species. In the symmetric model, both oxygens contribute equivalently and no proton must move. Reading one Lewis contributor as permanently different bonds confuses the drawing with the actual structure.
