In metabolism, the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex converts pyruvate to acetyl-CoA. Which description distinguishes this transformation from simple thermal decarboxylation of a beta-keto acid?

- It is an enzymatic oxidative decarboxylation involving CoA and NAD+ reduction.
- It is nonoxidative decarboxylation to acetaldehyde without transfer to CoA.
- It is predicted from heating pyruvate alone without cofactors.
- It is only tautomerization without carbon loss.

## Explanation

Pyruvate is an alpha-keto acid: it loses one carbon as CO2 and the two-carbon fragment is transferred to CoA, with a redox balance reducing NAD+. The process requires a specific complex and cofactors. The thermal beta-keto acid rule does not replace this mechanism or identify its product by itself.
