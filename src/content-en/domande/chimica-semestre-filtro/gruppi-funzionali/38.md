Why is amide nitrogen much less basic than nitrogen in an ordinary aliphatic amine?

- Amide nitrogen is necessarily quaternary ammonium.
- The amide C–N bond has no pi contribution.
- Its lone pair participates in delocalization with the carbonyl.
- The carbonyl completely localizes the pair and makes it more available.

## Explanation

Contribution of the N pair to the carbonyl system stabilizes the amide and gives C–N partial double-bond character. The pair is therefore less available for protonation than in an amine. Under sufficiently acidic conditions, ordinary amides generally favor protonation at carbonyl oxygen. An amide can be neutral: low basicity and permanent positive charge are not the same property.
