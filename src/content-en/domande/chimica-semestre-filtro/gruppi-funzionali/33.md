An ordinary acyclic ether and an ester are compared in aqueous basic hydrolysis. Why is the ether normally much less reactive under customary saponification conditions?

- The ether's OR group leaves more readily than the ester's.
- The ether reacts through the same tetrahedral acyl intermediate as the ester.
- Saponification requires free OH, present in the ester but absent from the ether.
- It offers no electrophilic carbonyl for the acyl addition-elimination pathway.

## Explanation

An ester has a carbonyl that receives the nucleophile and a group that can leave while C=O is restored. An ordinary ether lacks this pathway; cleavage requires different conditions to enable departure of the oxygen-containing group. Having C–O in both molecules is insufficient to make them equivalent.
