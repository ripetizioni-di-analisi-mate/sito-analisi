In an enolate-based aldol reaction, which role can formaldehyde play when it has no alpha carbon bearing hydrogens?

- No role, because every partner must be enolizable.
- Carbonyl acceptor, but not an enolate donor in the stated pathway.
- Only an acid catalyst, without joining the skeleton.
- Enolate donor by removal of its aldehydic H.

## Explanation

Generating the carbon nucleophile of an ordinary aldol requires an alpha H, not simply H directly attached to the carbonyl. Formaldehyde cannot supply it but has an electrophilic carbon accessible to another compound's enolate. The two partners' roles therefore do not require identical properties.
