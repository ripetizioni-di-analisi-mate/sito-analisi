Which difference distinguishes the neutral product of classical Claisen condensation between two esters from the addition-only aldol product of two aldehydes?

- Claisen gives a beta-keto ester; aldol addition gives a beta-hydroxyaldehyde.
- Claisen gives a beta-hydroxy ester; aldol addition gives a beta-keto aldehyde.
- Claisen gives a beta-keto aldehyde; aldol addition gives a beta-hydroxy ester.
- Claisen gives an alpha-keto ester; aldol addition gives an alpha-hydroxyaldehyde.

## Explanation

For the ester, the tetrahedral intermediate can expel alkoxide and restore a carbonyl, giving the beta-keto ester. The aldehyde lacks that OR leaving group: protonation gives the beta-hydroxyaldehyde. C–C bond formation is shared, but the fate of oxygen and the leaving group differs.
