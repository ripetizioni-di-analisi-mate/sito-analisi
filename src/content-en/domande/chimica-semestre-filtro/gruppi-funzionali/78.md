With comparable steric effects and conditions, which factor contributes to an anhydride's greater reactivity than an amide in acyl substitution?

- A carboxylate is a more favorable leaving group than an unprotonated amide anion.
- The N pair always makes the amide carbonyl more electrophilic.
- An amide anion is always a better leaving group than carboxylate.
- The leaving group does not contribute to acyl-derivative reactivity.

## Explanation

Carboxylate is resonance-stabilized and corresponds to a relatively weak base, making it a favorable leaving group. An amide instead benefits from strong N-pair donation to the carbonyl and, without activation, has an unfavorable leaving group. This comparison does not replace specification of reaction conditions.
