Why does a tertiary alcohol not normally give a ketone through the same mild oxidation that converts a secondary alcohol while preserving the skeleton?

- There is never a hydrogen on oxygen.
- C=O formation first requires complete cleavage of the C–O bond.
- Tertiary alcohol oxidation must always give an intact aldehyde.
- There is no hydrogen on the OH-bearing carbon.

## Explanation

The tertiary carbon is bonded to OH and three carbons: it has no C–H bond to transform in the ordinary conversion to C=O. Producing a carbonyl would also require changing skeletal bonds. Ordinary conversion retains the C–O sigma bond without requiring its complete cleavage. This restriction concerns the specified mild transformation, rather than an absolute inability to undergo harsh oxidation.
