Under the same conditions, a ketone has alpha-H $pK_a=20$ and a beta-dicarbonyl has $pK_a=11$. Which explanation matches the latter's greater acidity?

- The conjugate base loses all resonance possibilities.
- The alpha proton becomes a hydroxyl proton already present in the reactant.
- The conjugate base can delocalize charge over a system containing two carbonyls.
- A lower pKa here means less favorable dissociation.

## Explanation

The proton between two carbonyls can be removed to form a particularly stabilized anion. Additional delocalization makes acid dissociation more favorable and lowers pKa. The values are compared under identical conditions; the reasoning concerns carbon-bound H, not an initially present OH.
