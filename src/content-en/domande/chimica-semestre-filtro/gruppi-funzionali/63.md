At unchanged temperature, a catalyst is added to an ideal keto-enol system without changing final activities through other reactions. What changes?

- The equilibrium ratio is inverted because the enol forms more rapidly.
- The interconversion rate, not the equilibrium constant.
- The standard free-energy difference between the two tautomers.
- The forward rate increases while the reverse rate necessarily stays unchanged.

## Explanation

The catalyst changes the pathway and kinetic barriers, accelerating interconversion in both directions. Under the stated assumptions it does not change the tautomers' free-energy difference, so K and the equilibrium ratio do not change. Considering only forward acceleration would ignore pathway reversibility.
