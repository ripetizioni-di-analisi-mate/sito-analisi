Why is chloroacetic acid more acidic than acetic acid in water under the same conditions?

- Chlorine makes carboxylate charge more concentrated and necessarily less stable.
- Chlorine near the carboxyl group stabilizes the conjugate base through an inductive effect.
- Chlorine replaces the acidic proton in the O–H bond.
- Increasing anion stability must always decrease the acid's acidity.

## Explanation

The electron-withdrawing substituent acts through sigma bonds and relatively stabilizes the carboxylate. Deprotonation becomes more favorable. Chlorine is bonded to the alpha carbon rather than directly replacing hydroxyl H; the inductive effect would weaken if the substituent were moved farther from the carboxyl group.
