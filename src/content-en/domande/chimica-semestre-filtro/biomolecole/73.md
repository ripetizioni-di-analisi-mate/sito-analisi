A flavin cofactor passes through a one-electron-reduced intermediate before reaching FADH₂. Which interpretation is correct?

- A one-electron state implies absence of any fully reduced form.
- Flavin can support one-electron steps despite a two-electron total balance.
- Every FAD reduction necessarily requires an indivisible hydride.
- The total reduction balance to FADH₂ is therefore only one electron.

## Explanation

The flavin system can stabilise a semiquinone, a one-electron intermediate. The overall reduction $\mathrm{FAD+2H^++2e^-\to FADH_2}$ still involves two electrons but can proceed through successive steps. This allows connection of donors and acceptors with different electron-transfer modes.
