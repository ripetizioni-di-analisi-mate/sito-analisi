Tocopherol donates H from its phenolic group to a peroxyl radical. Why can the resulting tocopheroxyl radical effectively interrupt propagation?

- Delocalisation pairs all electrons, so tocopheroxyl ceases to be a radical.
- Donation of an H atom produces only a phenoxide ion with no unpaired electrons.
- Stabilisation makes it more effective than peroxyl at abstracting H from lipids.
- Delocalisation stabilises the radical and limits its ability to propagate the chain.

## Explanation

Tocopherol transfers hydrogen to the peroxyl and forms a radical stabilised by the aromatic system. This product is less able than the intercepted peroxyl to abstract H from lipids and continue the chain. Delocalisation does not remove the unpaired electron: the product remains a radical. Donating an H atom, which also carries an electron, differs from losing only H⁺ to form a phenoxide ion.
