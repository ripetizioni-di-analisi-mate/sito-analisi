Glycine has formula $\mathrm{H_2N{-}CH_2{-}COOH}$. Why is no R/S configuration assigned to its α-carbon?

- Two α-carbon substituents are identical hydrogens.
- The α-carbon is a planar carbonyl carbon.
- Proton exchange at the amino group continuously racemises the α-carbon.
- Rapid rotation about the Cα–C bond always makes the α-carbon achiral.

## Explanation

A stereogenic tetrahedral centre requires four different substituents. Glycine has two H substituents, so its mirror-image arrangements are superimposable. The α-carbon remains tetrahedral and distinct from the carboxyl carbon. Nitrogen protonation does not racemise the α-carbon or change the identities of its substituents. Bond rotation changes conformation without making four different groups identical.
