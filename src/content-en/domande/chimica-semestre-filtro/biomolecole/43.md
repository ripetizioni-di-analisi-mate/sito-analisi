Of $0.50\,\mathrm{mol}$ of lactose, $40\%$ is hydrolysed. How many total moles of carbohydrate molecules remain, counting residual lactose and monosaccharides? Neglect side reactions.

- $0.70\,\mathrm{mol}$.
- $1.00\,\mathrm{mol}$.
- $0.40\,\mathrm{mol}$.
- $0.20\,\mathrm{mol}$.

## Explanation

$0.40(0.50)=0.20\,\mathrm{mol}$ is hydrolysed, producing $0.20$ mol glucose and $0.20$ mol galactose. Residual lactose is $0.30$ mol, so the total is $0.30+0.20+0.20=0.70\,\mathrm{mol}$. Each mole hydrolysed increases the amount of carbohydrate molecules by one mole.
