Amylose and cellulose both release glucose upon complete hydrolysis, but an enzyme specific for α$(1\to4)$ bonds acts only on the former. Which property explains selectivity?

- All cellulose links involve glucose C6.
- Cellulose is more branched than amylose through α(1→6) links.
- The bond’s anomeric configuration differs.
- Cellulose uses only L-glucose, whereas amylose uses D-glucose.

## Explanation

The monomers match, but amylose and cellulose use α and β bonds respectively. This changes local orientation and fit to the enzyme site. The stated specificity recognises α$(1\to4)$ and does not imply β$(1\to4)$ activity. Identical final products do not make the substrates identical before hydrolysis.
