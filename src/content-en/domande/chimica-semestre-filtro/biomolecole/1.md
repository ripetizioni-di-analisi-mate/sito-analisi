An Asp → Asn substitution retains side-chain length but converts $\mathrm{CH_2COO^-}$ into $\mathrm{CH_2CONH_2}$. At pH 7, what changes in the side chain?

- From charge $0$ to charge $+1$, forming an amine.
- From charge $-1$ to charge $-2$, adding an acidic group.
- From charge $-1$ to charge $0$, retaining a polar group.
- Charge $-1$ remains, because the carbonyl is retained.

## Explanation

Aspartate has a side-chain carboxylate, whereas asparagine has a mainly neutral amide. The substitution therefore removes a negative charge without making the chain nonpolar. The amide carbonyl can still accept hydrogen bonds; the presence of a carbonyl alone does not determine a negative charge.
