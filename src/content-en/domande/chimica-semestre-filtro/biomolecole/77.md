RNA and DNA with unmodified backbones are exposed to the same alkaline conditions. Which group favours intramolecular attack on the RNA backbone?

- The C5′ OH of every internal residue, assumed to remain free after polymerisation.
- The terminal C3′ OH, present only in RNA.
- Ribose C2′ OH.
- C5′ phosphate, absent from the DNA backbone.

## Explanation

Deprotonation of C2′ OH generates a nucleophile that can attack the nearby phosphate and promote backbone cleavage. DNA has H at that position and lacks the same intramolecular route. The C3′ and C5′ oxygens of internal residues instead participate in phosphodiester linkages. DNA also contains phosphates and can have a terminal C3′ OH: those groups do not explain the requested difference.
