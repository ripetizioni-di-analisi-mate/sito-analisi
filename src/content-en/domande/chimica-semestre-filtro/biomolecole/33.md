A monosaccharide belongs to the D series and has a measured negative optical rotation. Which interpretation is correct?

- D necessarily indicates positive rotation in every solvent.
- The observations are compatible: configuration and optical sign are distinct.
- Negative rotation proves the assigned series must be L.
- The sample must be an equimolar enantiomer mixture.

## Explanation

D/L is assigned from the relative configuration of the reference centre in a Fischer projection. The rotation sign is measured experimentally under specified conditions and does not follow from D. D-fructose, for example, can be levorotatory. The rotation sign cannot be inferred from the series name; an equimolar mixture of both enantiomers would instead have zero net rotation under the same conditions.
