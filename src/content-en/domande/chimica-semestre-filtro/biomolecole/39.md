Fructose gives a positive reducing-sugar test in alkaline conditions despite being a ketose. Which transformation explains this result?

- Obligatory hydrolysis of a bond between two monosaccharides.
- Interconversion through an enediol with formation of aldoses.
- Prior oxidation of the C6 alcohol to an aldehyde while leaving the C2 ketone unchanged.
- Direct conversion of the α anomer to an aldehyde without opening.

## Explanation

In alkaline conditions, tautomerisation through an enediol allows ketose–aldose interconversion. The accessible aldehyde forms can reduce the test reagent. Tautomerism changes the C1–C2 system without requiring prior oxidation of C6. Fructose is already a monosaccharide, so disaccharide hydrolysis is unnecessary; anomerisation changes a stereocentre but does not by itself move the carbonyl from C2 to C1.
