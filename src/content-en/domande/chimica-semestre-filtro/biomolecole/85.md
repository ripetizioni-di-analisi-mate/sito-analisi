A carotenoid quenches singlet oxygen by transferring its energy into the conjugated system and dissipating it. Which interpretation is appropriate?

- Every conjugated double bond must be hydrogenated during quenching.
- Physical quenching is possible without stoichiometric carotenoid consumption at every event.
- Quenching necessarily requires net transfer of one H atom.
- Every event requires irreversible oxidation of one carotenoid molecule.

## Explanation

In physical energy transfer, the carotenoid is excited and can dissipate energy to return to its initial state. Its conjugated chain enables this electronic interaction. Chemical reactions consuming carotenoids also exist: the described mode does not imply unlimited protection or identical behaviour in every environment.
