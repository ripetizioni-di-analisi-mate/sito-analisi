Cholesterol’s OH is esterified with a fatty acid. Which structural change distinguishes cholesteryl ester from free cholesterol?

- The free OH is replaced by an ester and an acyl chain is added.
- A free carboxylate permanently exposed to solvent forms.
- A second OH forms and the molecule becomes more hydrophilic.
- An acyl group is added through a C–C bond while retaining the free OH.

## Explanation

Cholesterol OH reacts to form O–CO–R: the free O–H donor disappears and an acyl chain is added. The molecule becomes more hydrophobic overall while retaining ester oxygens and the sterol skeleton. The product is a neutral ester, so it is neither a free carboxylate nor a second hydroxyl.
