A sugar residue is transferred from an activated donor to a protein by a specific enzyme. A second sample reacts slowly with glucose without enzymes. How are the processes distinguished?

- Both are glycation because they add sugars.
- First: glycation; second: enzymatic glycosylation.
- Both are enzymatic glycosylation because covalent bonds form.
- First: enzymatic glycosylation; second: glycation.

## Explanation

Enzyme-catalysed transfer from an activated donor describes enzymatic glycosylation. Spontaneous reaction of a sugar carbonyl with nucleophilic protein groups describes glycation. Both can covalently modify a protein: the presence of a covalent bond does not distinguish them, whereas substrates and mechanism do.
