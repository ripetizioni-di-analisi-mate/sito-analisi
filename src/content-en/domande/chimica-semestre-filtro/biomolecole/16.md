A model treats every peptide-backbone bond as freely rotatable. Which chemical correction is necessary?

- Make C–N freely rotatable only in the trans form.
- Treat Cα–N as always planar and prevent any variation of that angle.
- Restrict only Cα–C because it formally contains a double bond.
- Restrict amide C–N rotation because of electron delocalisation.

## Explanation

The nitrogen lone pair is delocalised towards the carbonyl, giving amide C–N partial double-bond character. This produces planarity and a rotation barrier even in the trans configuration. The main variable backbone angles instead involve bonds on either side of Cα; peptide-unit planarity does not block all these rotations.
