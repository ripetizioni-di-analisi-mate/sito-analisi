Which distinction between $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$ and $\mathrm{{}^{\bullet}OH}$ is correct?

- Peroxide always carries a permanent negative charge.
- Both are radicals because they contain oxygen.
- The hydroxyl radical is identical to hydroxide ion $\mathrm{OH^-}$.
- Peroxide is not a radical; the hydroxyl radical has an unpaired electron.

## Explanation

A radical species contains an unpaired electron, indicated by the dot in $\mathrm{{}^{\bullet}OH}$. Hydrogen peroxide has paired electrons and can be reactive without being a radical. Hydroxide has negative charge and a different electronic structure: charge and the presence of unpaired electrons are not equivalent.
