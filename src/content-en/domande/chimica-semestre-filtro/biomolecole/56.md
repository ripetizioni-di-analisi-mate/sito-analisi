A triacylglycerol has four C=C bonds in total across its tails. Complete hydrogenation of only the C=C bonds consumes how many moles of H₂ per mole of lipid?

- $4\,\mathrm{mol}$.
- $8\,\mathrm{mol}$.
- $2\,\mathrm{mol}$.
- $3\,\mathrm{mol}$.

## Explanation

Each C=C adds one H₂ molecule to become C–C. Four double bonds therefore consume four moles of H₂ per mole of triacylglycerol. Three tails do not imply three double bonds; the count comes from the data. Ester carbonyls are excluded from the stated selective reduction.
