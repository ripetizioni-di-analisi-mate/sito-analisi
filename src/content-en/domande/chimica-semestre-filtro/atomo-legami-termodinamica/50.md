F₂ and I₂ are nonpolar molecules. Which difference supports stronger dispersion forces in I₂?

- The I₂ cloud is less deformable, making attractions stronger.
- I₂ necessarily has a larger permanent dipole.
- The energy to break I-I equals the energy to separate two I₂ molecules.
- I₂ has a more readily polarisable electron cloud.

## Explanation

Dispersion forces arise from correlated electron-density fluctuations. I₂’s more extended cloud is more readily deformed and therefore more polarisable. Both homonuclear molecules remain without permanent dipoles. Separating two interacting molecules differs from breaking the covalent bond within each molecule, so the two energies are not identical.
