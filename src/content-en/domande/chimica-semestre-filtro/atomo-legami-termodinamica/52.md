Two nonpolar surfaces tend to associate in water. Which description identifies the hydrophobic effect?

- Water’s Gibbs-energy contribution stays identical for any exposed nonpolar area.
- Association always requires new covalent bonds between the surfaces.
- Association changes solvent organisation and can lower total Gibbs energy.
- Association results only from opposite permanent surface charges.

## Explanation

Association must be evaluated together with water, whose organisation changes as exposed nonpolar area changes. The balance can favour surface contact. New covalent bonds or opposite surface charges are not required. The effect depends on conditions and contributes to membranes and protein folding.
