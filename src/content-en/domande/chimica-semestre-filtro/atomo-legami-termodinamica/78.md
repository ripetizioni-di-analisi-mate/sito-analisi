A surfactant lowers an interface’s surface tension. For the same area increase under the same conditions, what happens to required reversible work?

- It increases.
- It decreases.
- It must become negative for every area increase.
- It remains unchanged because it depends only on area.

## Explanation

Surface work depends on both created area and surface tension. Reducing the latter lowers the energy cost of the same area increase. Work need not become negative: a smaller positive tension still requires positive work. This model explains surfactant effects without changing water’s covalent bonds.
