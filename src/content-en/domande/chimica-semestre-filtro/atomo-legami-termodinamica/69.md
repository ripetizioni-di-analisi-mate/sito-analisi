Why does heating an ideal gas in a rigid closed vessel increase pressure?

- Only rotational energy increases, without any translational change.
- The number of molecules in the vessel necessarily increases.
- Collisions become inelastic and every particle loses all its energy at each collision.
- Mean translational energy and momentum transfer to the walls increase.

## Explanation

Pressure comes from momentum transfer in wall collisions. Heating increases mean translational energy, increasing momentum flux per unit area and time at fixed volume and amount. The kinetic model uses elastic collisions, not total energy loss at every collision. Rotational energy alone does not replace the translational contribution to pressure.
