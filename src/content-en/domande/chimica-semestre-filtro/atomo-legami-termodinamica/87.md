A reaction has $\Delta G<0$ under the stated conditions but negligible rate. Which interpretation is consistent?

- The direction is thermodynamically favoured, but a kinetic barrier can slow it.
- The reaction must already be at equilibrium.
- ΔG must be positive because the process is slow.
- A catalyst must make ΔG more negative for it to proceed.

## Explanation

Gibbs energy compares states and supplies the thermodynamic driving force under the stated conditions. Rate instead depends on pathways and activation barriers. A favourable transformation can be extremely slow. A catalyst provides a faster pathway without changing the Gibbs balance between the same initial and final states.
