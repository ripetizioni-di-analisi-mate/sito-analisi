Nitrogen and phosphorus belong to the same group. What supports phosphorus having a generally larger atomic radius?

- Phosphorus outer electrons are less shielded and therefore farther away.
- More protons push the outer electrons farther away.
- The outer level stays the same, but electron repulsion alone increases.
- Its outer electrons occupy a higher principal level.

## Explanation

Phosphorus outer electrons occupy the third level, nitrogen’s the second. More occupied levels and shielding outweigh increased nuclear charge, which alone would attract electrons towards the nucleus. The outer level therefore does not stay the same. The trend down a group must be distinguished from the trend across a period.
