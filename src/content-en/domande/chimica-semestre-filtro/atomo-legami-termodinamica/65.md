Solid NaCl conducts poorly, whereas molten NaCl conducts through ions. What change explains the difference?

- Only delocalised electrons conduct in the melt, as in a metal.
- Ions remain fixed and only lattice vibrations carry charge in the melt.
- Melting requires discrete NaCl molecules to dissociate into previously absent ions.
- In the melt, ions can move over macroscopic distances.

## Explanation

In the solid lattice, ions remain around defined positions and cannot freely carry charge across the sample. Melting allows migration while preserving their charges. Conduction is ionic; merely having electrons within ions does not imply the electron mobility of a metal.
