For an ordinary real gas, which conditions generally favour the ideal-gas approximation?

- Molecular volume comparable with available volume.
- High density and temperature near condensation.
- Low density and temperature far from condensation.
- Strong intermolecular attractions dominating thermal motion.

## Explanation

The ideal model neglects particle volume and interactions between collisions. Dilution reduces the importance of occupied volume, while conditions far from condensation reduce the relative role of attractions. High densities and proximity to phase changes instead make departures from the model more apparent.
