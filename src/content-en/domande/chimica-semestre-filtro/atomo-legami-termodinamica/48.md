CO₂ is linear and symmetrical, with two polar C-O bonds. Why is its total dipole moment zero?

- Carbon’s zero formal charge alone eliminates any molecular dipole.
- The equal bond contributions point in opposite directions.
- Every double bond must be nonpolar.
- O and C have equal electronegativity.

## Explanation

Molecular polarity depends on the vector sum of dipoles. In CO₂, equivalent oxygens lie on opposite sides of carbon, so their contributions cancel. Each C-O bond remains polar. Multiple bonding does not impose nonpolarity; geometry and charge distribution remain necessary.
