Comparing NH₃ with NH₄⁺, which description of nitrogen lone pairs is correct for hydrogen bonding?

- Neither can donate a hydrogen bond because N-H is covalent.
- NH₃ can accept through its lone pair; NH₄⁺ lacks that available lone pair.
- Both have one free nitrogen lone pair.
- NH₄⁺ has two available lone pairs and NH₃ none.

## Explanation

Ammonia retains an unshared pair that can act as an acceptor site. In ammonium, all four nitrogen pairs form N-H bonds, removing that acceptor site. N-bound hydrogens can nevertheless act as donors. Donor behaviour requires the covalent bond between H and an electronegative atom.
