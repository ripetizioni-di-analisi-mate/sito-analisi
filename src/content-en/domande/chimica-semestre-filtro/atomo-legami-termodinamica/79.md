Which distinction between triple and critical points is correct?

- Solid, liquid and gas always coexist at the critical point; only two at the triple point.
- The triple point is the highest temperature the substance can reach.
- Three phases coexist at the triple point; liquid–vapour distinction ends at the critical point.
- The critical point always lies on the solid–liquid coexistence curve.

## Explanation

The triple point joins the three coexistence curves and permits solid, liquid and gas equilibrium. The critical point ends the liquid–vapour curve, where those phases become indistinguishable. Neither is an absolute temperature limit for the substance. Their coordinates are material-specific properties.
