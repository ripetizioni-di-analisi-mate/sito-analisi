Calculate moles and formula units in $5.85\,\mathrm g$ of NaCl, using $M=58.5\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ and $N_A=6.02214\cdot10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$.

- $0.0100\,\mathrm{mol}$ and $6.02\cdot10^{21}$ formula units.
- $0.100\,\mathrm{mol}$ and $6.02\cdot10^{22}$ formula units.
- $0.100\,\mathrm{mol}$ and $6.02\cdot10^{23}$ formula units.
- $1.00\,\mathrm{mol}$ and $6.02\cdot10^{23}$ formula units.

## Explanation

The amount is $n=\dfrac{5.85}{58.5}=0.100\,\mathrm{mol}$. Multiplication by Avogadro’s constant gives $N\simeq6.02\cdot10^{22}$. One tenth of a mole contains one tenth of the Avogadro number, checking the exponent. NaCl forms an ionic lattice: the counted entities are formula units, each with a one-to-one Na:Cl ratio.
