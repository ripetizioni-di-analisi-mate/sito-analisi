$\mathrm{Na^+}$ and $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ both have ten electrons. Under comparable coordination conditions, why is Mg²⁺ smaller?

- It has fewer protons and thus less nuclear repulsion.
- It has one fewer occupied principal level than Na⁺.
- It has two more valence electrons than Na⁺.
- It has greater nuclear charge at the same electron count.

## Explanation

The ions are isoelectronic and have the same occupied levels. Magnesium has twelve protons against sodium’s eleven, attracting an equal-electron cloud more strongly. Greater contraction explains the smaller radius. This comparison concerns ions under similar conditions, not the radii of the two neutral atoms.
