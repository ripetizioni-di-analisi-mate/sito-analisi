Why does rotation around a C=C double bond require more energy than rotation around a C-C single bond?

- Rotating one fragment primarily removes σ overlap along the axis.
- Rotation reduces the sideways overlap forming the π bond.
- The π orbital has complete cylindrical symmetry around the axis.
- The double bond contains two identically oriented σ bonds.

## Explanation

A double bond combines one sigma and one pi component. The pi component requires aligned sideways orbitals; rotating one fragment reduces their overlap and raises energy. Sigma overlap along the axis is instead approximately retained. The different symmetries of these contributions explain the rotational barrier.
