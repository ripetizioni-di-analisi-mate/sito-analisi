Near ordinary conditions, ice occupies more volume than the same mass of liquid water. Which pressure effect on melting is consistent?

- Pressure favours solid and raises melting temperature.
- Temperature stays fixed because solid and liquid are only slightly compressible.
- Pressure favours liquid but nevertheless raises melting temperature.
- Pressure favours liquid and lowers melting temperature.

## Explanation

Increasing pressure favours the smaller-volume phase, here liquid. Ordinary water’s melting curve therefore has negative slope in a pressure–temperature diagram. This concerns ordinary ice near the stated conditions; other high-pressure solid-water phases require a broader description.
