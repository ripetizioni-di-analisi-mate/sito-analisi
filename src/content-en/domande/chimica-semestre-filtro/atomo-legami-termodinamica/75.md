Starting at atmospheric pressure and lowering it moderately while remaining in the stable-liquid range, how does pure water’s boiling temperature change?

- It decreases because a lower vapour pressure is sufficient.
- It always decreases in the same ratio as pressure expressed in bar.
- It increases because the liquid opposes a lower pressure.
- It always remains 100 °C regardless of pressure.

## Explanation

Boiling occurs when vapour pressure equals external pressure. Because vapour pressure rises with T, lower external pressure is reached at a lower temperature. Water’s approximately 100 °C boiling point refers to ordinary atmospheric pressure, not to a universal condition-independent temperature. Vapour pressure does not depend on T through a simple direct proportion.
