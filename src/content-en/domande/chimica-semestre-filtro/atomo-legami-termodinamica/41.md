In an ideal carbonate ion, all three C-O bonds are equivalent. How does this relate to Lewis resonance forms?

- All three bonds are localised single bonds, without a π contribution.
- The ion physically oscillates among three molecules with atoms at different positions.
- One double bond remains permanently localised on the same oxygen.
- Electrons are delocalised; resonance forms represent the same species.

## Explanation

Carbonate resonance forms differ in electron placement while retaining the same atomic framework. The actual distribution is delocalised, making all three bonds equivalent in the symmetrical model. Each bond order is intermediate between single and double. Resonance describes neither atomic movement nor the absence of a π contribution.
