NH₃ forms NH₄⁺ by accepting H⁺. What does this example show?

- A base can accept a proton without OH in its formula.
- NH₃ acts as an acid because H⁺ participates in the transformation.
- NH₄⁺ is the conjugate base of NH₃ because it contains one more hydrogen.
- An oxygen-free molecule can act as an acid, but never as a base.

## Explanation

Ammonia’s lone pair binds a proton, forming ammonium. NH₃ acts as a base because it receives H⁺, without requiring oxygen or OH in its formula. A proton’s presence in the reaction does not automatically identify NH₃’s role: its transfer must be followed. NH₄⁺ is the conjugate acid because it can return the acquired proton.
