A pure liquid and its vapour are at equilibrium in a closed vessel at constant T. Vapour space doubles and enough liquid remains. What final state is expected?

- Some vapour condenses, restoring the initial pressure.
- More liquid evaporates and the same vapour pressure is restored.
- Final pressure remains halved by applying Boyle’s law to the vapour phase alone.
- Final pressure doubles along with the available volume.

## Explanation

An equilibrium pure liquid’s vapour pressure depends on temperature. Expansion initially lowers vapour pressure, favouring net evaporation until equilibrium returns. Remaining liquid is necessary: if it all evaporated, two-phase coexistence would no longer constrain the system.
