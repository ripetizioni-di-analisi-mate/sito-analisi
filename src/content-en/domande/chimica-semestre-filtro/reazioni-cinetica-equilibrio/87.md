A sample contains total calcium at $1.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, eighty percent of which is complexed. Assuming unit activity coefficient, which calcium concentration enters the calcium-oxalate ion product?

- $1.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, because total calcium matters.
- $2.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, normalised to the standard.
- $5.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, by dividing by the free fraction.
- $8.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, because only complexes enter.

## Explanation

The free fraction is twenty percent: $0.20\cdot10^{-3}=2.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. The solubility product contains free calcium-ion activity, obtained here by normalising this concentration because gamma is one. Using the total would overestimate calcium's contribution fivefold. Complexes participate in their own equilibria and the total balance.
