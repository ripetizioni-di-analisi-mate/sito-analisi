For $-\dfrac{d[A]}{dt}=k$ with constant k, which comparison between samples of different initial concentrations is correct?

- Half-lives are equal because k is equal.
- The sample with twice the initial concentration has twice the half-life.
- The sample with twice the initial concentration has half the half-life.
- Half-life is always $\dfrac{\ln2}{k}$.

## Explanation

A zero-order model removes the same concentration per unit time. Starting at twice the concentration requires removing twice as much to reach half, so twice the time is needed. The formula is $t_{1/2}=\dfrac{[A]_0}{2k}$. Independence from initial value and the logarithm-of-two formula instead belong to first order.
