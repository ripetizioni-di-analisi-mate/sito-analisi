Two reactants collide frequently, but only a small fraction of encounters produces reaction. Which factor is consistent with effective-collision theory?

- A collision with adequate energy is always effective, regardless of encounter geometry.
- Only a fraction of encounters has suitable energy and orientation.
- Every collision must react above zero kelvin.
- A favourable orientation makes a collision effective regardless of available energy.

## Explanation

Encounter frequency is one kinetic factor. Transformation also requires access to the reactive pathway and an arrangement compatible with changing bonds. Frequency and effective fraction must therefore be distinguished. Heating may increase the fraction with sufficient energy, while orientational selectivity contributes to the prefactor in the simplified model.
