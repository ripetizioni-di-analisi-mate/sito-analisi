For $\mathrm{MX_2}$, $K_{\mathrm{ps}}=3.2\cdot10^{-11}$ at fixed temperature. In ideal pure water with excess solid and no side reactions, what is molar solubility?

- $1.6\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- Approximately $5.7\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- Approximately $3.2\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.
- $2.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.

## Explanation

With normalised concentrations, $K_{\mathrm{ps}}=s(2s)^2=4s^3$. Hence $s^3=8.0\cdot10^{-12}$ and $s=2.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Taking a square root uses MX stoichiometry; taking the cube root of K alone omits factor four. Solubility counts formula units, whereas X concentration is twice as large.
