For ideal $\mathrm A(g)\rightleftharpoons2\mathrm B(g)$, inert gas is added at constant temperature and total pressure. The vessel expands. Which direction is favoured from equilibrium?

- Towards A, because total gas amount increases.
- Towards B, because dilution lowers Q below K.
- Towards B, because inert gas increases K at the same temperature.
- No change, because every inert-gas addition leaves partial pressures unchanged.

## Explanation

Expansion lowers reacting partial pressures by a common factor below one. Q contains squared B pressure and first-power A pressure, so it falls by that same factor. Forward reaction produces more gaseous moles. This differs from constant-volume addition, where partial pressures would remain unchanged.
