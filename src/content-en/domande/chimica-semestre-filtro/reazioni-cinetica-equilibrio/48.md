At constant active-enzyme amount, increasing an already abundant substrate produces progressively smaller rate increases. Which interpretation is consistent with saturable sites?

- A large fraction of sites is occupied and substrate dependence approaches a plateau.
- The plateau proves the enzyme was consumed stoichiometrically.
- The plateau proves that substrate no longer binds the enzyme.
- Every substrate increase must produce an unlimited proportional rate increase.

## Explanation

When almost all active sites are engaged, an additional substrate molecule is less likely to find a free site. Available enzyme turnover capacity therefore limits rate in the model. The plateau establishes neither catalyst consumption nor absence of binding: sites are mostly occupied, not inactive. In the saturation regime, apparent substrate order may approach zero.
