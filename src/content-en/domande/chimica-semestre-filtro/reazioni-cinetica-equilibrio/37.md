Disappearance follows $-\dfrac{d[A]}{dt}=k[A]^2$ with constant k. If going from $[A]_0$ to $\dfrac{[A]_0}{2}$ takes t, how long does the next decrease to $\dfrac{[A]_0}{4}$ take?

- $4t$.
- $\dfrac{t}{2}$.
- $t$.
- $2t$.

## Explanation

For this second-order law, half-life from starting concentration C is $\dfrac1{kC}$. The second interval starts at half the concentration and therefore takes twice as long. Total time to reach one quarter is three times the first interval. Equal successive times would instead describe first order, not the specified law.
