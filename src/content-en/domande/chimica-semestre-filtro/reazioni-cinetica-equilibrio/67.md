An ideal equilibrium solution for $\mathrm A+\mathrm B\rightleftharpoons\mathrm C$ is diluted to twice its volume at constant temperature. Before reaction, how does Q change?

- It quadruples because only reactant dilution matters.
- It doubles, initially favouring C dissociation.
- It stays unchanged because every concentration halves.
- It halves, initially favouring C formation.

## Explanation

The numerator of $Q=\dfrac{a_C}{a_Aa_B}$ halves, while the denominator becomes one quarter. Their ratio therefore doubles and exceeds K. Dissociation increases dissolved-species number in the model and returns the quotient towards equilibrium. Saying every concentration changes equally is insufficient; their exponents also matter.
