Helium is added to an equilibrium ideal-gas mixture at constant volume and temperature, with no helium reactions. What immediately happens to reacting partial pressures and Q?

- Reacting partial pressures and Q remain unchanged.
- Partial pressures increase in proportion to total pressure.
- Q changes because K increases with total pressure.
- Partial pressures decrease because mole fractions decrease.

## Explanation

Each reacting gas obeys $p_i=\dfrac{n_iRT}{V}$. Addition changes neither its mole number nor T or V, so $p_i$ stays unchanged. Mole fraction decreases, but total pressure increases so their product is preserved. At constant temperature, K is unchanged in the specified ideal model.
