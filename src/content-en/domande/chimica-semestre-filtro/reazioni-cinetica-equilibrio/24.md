A solution of A is rapidly diluted with inert solvent; A mole number stays constant during the intervention. Which interpretation of decreasing $[A]$ is correct?

- A moles decrease in the same proportion as $[A]$, even when volume changes.
- Chemical rate must be proportional to added volume.
- Every concentration decrease directly measures reacted moles.
- The change can arise from volume and does not alone measure a consumption reaction.

## Explanation

Since $[A]=\dfrac{n_A}{V}$, increasing V lowers concentration even at unchanged moles. The observation therefore does not alone represent chemical disappearance. Recovering consumption at variable volume requires a mole balance or subtraction of the dilution contribution. A kinetic formula based only on the concentration derivative requires attention to the volume constraint.
