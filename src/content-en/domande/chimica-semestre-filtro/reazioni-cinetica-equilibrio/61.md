A small salt amount dissolves completely and the final ion product is below $K_{\mathrm{ps}}$. No solid remains. Which conclusion is correct?

- The ion product must equal K even without the solid phase.
- The solution is undersaturated and the available amount is insufficient to impose saturation.
- The absence of solid makes that phase's solubility product undefined at the assigned temperature.
- The solution must precipitate because all salt is present as ions.

## Explanation

Equality to the solubility product describes saturation in equilibrium with the relevant solid phase. If too little salt was introduced, the material balance is exhausted first. K remains an equilibrium property at the assigned temperature. More salt could dissolve if added, while an ion product below K does not favour precipitation.

[Theory: Solid exhausted before saturation](/en/chimica-semestre-filtro/reazioni-cinetica-equilibrio/teoria#prodotto-solubilita).
