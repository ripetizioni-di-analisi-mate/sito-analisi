Using $s\simeq\dfrac{K_{\mathrm{ps}}}{c_0}$ for MX gives $s=2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ with $c_0=1\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Which judgement is correct?

- The approximation is unjustified; solve $s(c_0+s)=K_{\mathrm{ps}}$.
- It is justified because every sparingly soluble salt automatically satisfies $s\ll c_0$.
- It is justified because both absolute concentrations are small.
- Replace $c_0+s$ by $s$ while retaining the same final formula.

## Explanation

The simplification requires newly supplied s to be much smaller than existing common ion. Here their ratio is two, so the neglected term is even larger than the retained one. Small absolute concentrations may support ideality, but not this particular comparison. The complete equation gives a different, physically consistent value.
