For a reaction with $\Delta_rH^\circ>0$ over the studied range, how is the constant predicted to change as T increases?

- K remains unchanged because it is a constant.
- K increases in direct proportion to T over every interval.
- K decreases.
- K increases.

## Explanation

The derivative $\dfrac{d\ln K}{dT}=\dfrac{\Delta_rH^\circ}{RT^2}$ is positive, so both ln K and K increase. Constant means the ratio is fixed for a given temperature and reaction, not independent of every condition. The relation does not imply direct proportionality between K and T: the increase also depends on enthalpy and absolute temperatures.
