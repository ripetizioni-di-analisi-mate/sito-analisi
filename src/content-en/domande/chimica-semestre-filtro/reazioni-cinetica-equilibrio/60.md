An ideal saturated solution is in equilibrium with a macroscopic pure salt. More solid of the same phase is added at constant temperature. What equilibrium outcome is expected, neglecting particle-size effects?

- The salt's K increases in proportion to added amount.
- Saturation ion activities remain consistent with the same solubility product.
- Ionic concentrations double whenever solid mass doubles.
- Solid activity becomes equal to the total mole number present.

## Explanation

The pure phase has unit activity and the system was already saturated. Adding more increases the solid reservoir without requiring a different saturated composition. Surface area and exchange rates may change while temperature and thermodynamic phase remain as specified. The macroscopic limit excludes effects specific to very small particles from the usual model.
