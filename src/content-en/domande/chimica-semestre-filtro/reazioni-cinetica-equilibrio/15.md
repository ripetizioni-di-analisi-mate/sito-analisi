Which ionic equation conserves both atoms and charge in forming iron(III) hydroxide?

- $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}+\mathrm{OH^-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3(s)}$.
- $3\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}+3\mathrm{OH^-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3(s)}$.
- $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}+3\mathrm{OH^-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3(s)}$.
- $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}+3\mathrm{OH^-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3(s)}$.

## Explanation

The solid contains one iron and three OH groups. Three hydroxide ions also balance charge plus three, giving zero total charge as in the solid. The iron coefficient is not multiplied by its ionic charge. Replacing iron(III) with iron(II) changes the species and leaves a net charge inconsistent with the written product.
