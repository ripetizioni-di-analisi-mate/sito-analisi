Lowering pH increases neutral uric acid relative to urate anion. Why can this favour uric-acid crystals even while free urate decreases?

- The same product $a_{Na^+}a_{U^-}$ describes both phases interchangeably.
- Lower anion concentration must always increase every possible solid's solubility.
- Protonation changes only kinetics, not equilibrium species distribution.
- Solid uric acid and monosodium urate are different phases with different relevant equilibria and species.

## Explanation

pH changes distribution between protonated species and anion. Saturation relative to a solid requires its corresponding species and equilibrium relation, so decreased anion alone does not describe the neutral phase's tendency. Distinguishing uric acid from monosodium urate avoids using one constant for chemically different substances and phases.
