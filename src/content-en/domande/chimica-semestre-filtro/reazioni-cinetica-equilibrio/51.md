An unfavourable process becomes favourable when a mechanism couples it to ATP hydrolysis. Why does this not contradict invariance of K under catalysis alone?

- The overall transformation now also includes hydrolysis reactants and products.
- The catalyst changed the original reaction's K without changing its balance.
- The two reaction constants add to give the overall constant.
- Favourable hydrolysis guarantees coupling even without shared intermediates or linked steps.

## Explanation

Coupling combines transformations and their free energies. The overall reaction therefore has a different equation and its own constant, while the original reaction retains its thermodynamics. The catalyst facilitates the connecting mechanism. Formal addition must be accompanied by physical coupling; ATP's presence in the same solution alone does not establish it. Standard energies add and constants multiply.
