A zero-order law extrapolated far beyond the data would predict $[A]<0$. Which conclusion is correct?

- The negative sign proves the rate constant has become negative.
- The law must cease to describe the system by the time the species is exhausted.
- The negative value should be replaced by its magnitude while keeping the same disappearance model.
- A negative concentration indicates physical consumption beyond the initial A amount.

## Explanation

Concentration cannot become negative. The form $[A]=[A]_0-kt$ describes only the range where reactant availability and mechanism sustain constant rate. Conditions change near exhaustion. Taking the magnitude would artificially introduce A regrowth, while assigning negative k confuses a model limit with a kinetic measurement.
