Two reactions are to be added to obtain an overall constant at $310\,\mathrm{K}$. Available values are $K_1$ at $298\,\mathrm{K}$ and $K_2$ at $310\,\mathrm{K}$. What is needed before multiplying constants?

- Multiply directly because K never depends on T.
- Obtain $K_1$ at $310\,\mathrm{K}$ using suitable thermodynamic data.
- Use the arithmetic mean of constants instead of their product.
- Multiply $K_1$ by $\dfrac{310}{298}$ without further information.

## Explanation

The product rule follows from adding standard free energies at the same temperature. Constants determined at different temperatures do not directly represent that sum. K1 must be known or estimated at the requested temperature, for example using enthalpy over a valid approximation range. Averaging constants or assuming direct proportionality to T does not replace this thermodynamic step.
