The law is $v=k[A][B]$, but B is in large excess and changes negligibly during measurement. Which observed law is appropriate?

- $v=k[A]$ with the same original k and units.
- $v=k_{\mathrm{obs}}[A]$ with $k_{\mathrm{obs}}=k[B]$.
- $v=k_{\mathrm{obs}}$ independent of A because one concentration is constant.
- $v=k[A]^2$ because B is in excess.

## Explanation

The nearly constant [B] factor is incorporated into the observed coefficient, leaving linear dependence on A. Apparent order in the monitored species is one, while the complete law remains second order. The new coefficient has different units from the second-order k and changes if another large B excess is used.
