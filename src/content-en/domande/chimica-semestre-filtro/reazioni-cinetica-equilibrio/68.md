An ideal mixture of $\mathrm{H_2(g)}+\mathrm{I_2(g)}\rightleftharpoons2\mathrm{HI(g)}$ is compressed isothermally. Every species remains gaseous. What follows for Q immediately after compression?

- It decreases because HI is the only written product species.
- It becomes equal to total pressure.
- It remains unchanged because the net stoichiometric gas-mole change is zero.
- It increases because every partial pressure increases.

## Explanation

The numerator contains squared HI pressure and the denominator two first-power pressures. Multiplying every pressure by the same factor introduces its square both above and below, so it cancels. Gas coefficients, not the number of species names, matter. Ideality and retention of gaseous phases are assumptions of the prediction.
