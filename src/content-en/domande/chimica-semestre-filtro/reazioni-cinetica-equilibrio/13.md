Equal volumes containing $[M^+]=2.0\cdot10^{-4}$ and $[X^-]=4.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ are mixed. For MX, $K_{\mathrm{ps}}=1.0\cdot10^{-8}$ at the assigned temperature. With additive volumes and ideality, which comparison is correct before precipitation?

- $Q_{\mathrm{ps}}=8.0\cdot10^{-8}>K_{\mathrm{ps}}$.
- $Q_{\mathrm{ps}}=1.0\cdot10^{-8}=K_{\mathrm{ps}}$.
- $Q_{\mathrm{ps}}=5.0\cdot10^{-9}<K_{\mathrm{ps}}$.
- $Q_{\mathrm{ps}}=2.0\cdot10^{-8}>K_{\mathrm{ps}}$.

## Explanation

Final volume is twice each initial volume, so both concentrations halve. Their product is $(1.0\cdot10^{-4})(2.0\cdot10^{-4})=2.0\cdot10^{-8}$. Eight times ten to the minus eight would ignore both dilutions. Supersaturation indicates favoured precipitation; immediate appearance of visible solid also depends on nucleation.
