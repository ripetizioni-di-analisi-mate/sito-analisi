Observed enzyme rate rises between two moderate temperatures but falls at a higher temperature accompanied by structural loss. Which interpretation distinguishes the contributions?

- The decrease proves the reaction's K fell in the same proportion as rate.
- Higher temperature must always increase every enzyme's observed rate.
- The decrease proves that the intrinsic activation energy of the remaining steps has become negative.
- Heating can accelerate chemical steps while simultaneously reducing functional enzyme amount.

## Explanation

Arrhenius applied to a step with unchanged parameters describes one kinetic contribution. The experiment also changes the active-catalyst population, which can decrease through denaturation. Overall rate may therefore fall despite acceleration among still-functional molecules. A change in K requires thermodynamic information; activity loss does not establish a negative intrinsic activation energy either and must be distinguished from decreased active population.
