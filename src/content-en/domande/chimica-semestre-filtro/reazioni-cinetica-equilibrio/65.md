For an ideal solution of $\mathrm A\rightleftharpoons\mathrm B$ initially at equilibrium, B is added without appreciable volume or temperature changes. What immediately happens to Q and which direction is favoured?

- Q rises above K; the direction towards A is favoured.
- Q falls below K; the direction towards B is favoured.
- K rises to the new Q without requiring reaction.
- Q remains unchanged because temperature is constant.

## Explanation

The quotient is $\dfrac{[B]}{[A]}$; addition raises the numerator while initially leaving the denominator unchanged. Q therefore exceeds original K. Reverse progress consumes some B and forms A, reducing the ratio. Constant temperature preserves K, while altered composition changes Q before chemical evolution.
