A pure liquid is present as its own phase, using the usual pure-phase standard state. How is its activity treated in the working equilibrium expression?

- It is taken as one in the pure-phase model.
- It is replaced by total liquid mass in grams.
- The pure liquid's numerical molar concentration is used without a standard-state ratio.
- It is taken as zero because it is not a gas.

## Explanation

The pure phase serves as its standard reference, so its contribution is unity in the usual model. A factor of one may be omitted without making the constant zero. Available mass matters for whether the phase remains present. A solvent in a very concentrated solution instead requires attention to its activity and is not automatically the stated pure-phase case.
