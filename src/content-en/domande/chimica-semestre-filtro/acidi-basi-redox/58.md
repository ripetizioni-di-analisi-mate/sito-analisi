A ligand forms soluble complexes with the cation of a sparingly soluble salt. At equal pH, why can the total amount of dissolved salt increase?

- It necessarily increases thermodynamic $K_{sp}$ at fixed temperature.
- It forces total metal concentration to equal free concentration.
- It raises free-cation activity, thereby favouring further dissolution.
- It binds some free cation, allowing further dissolution without the same increase in the free-activity product.

## Explanation

The solubility product involves the free species in the dissolution reaction. Complex formation removes free cation and can shift that equilibrium towards more dissolved solid. The total includes free and complexed species; it can rise without changing the constant for the same solid phase at the same temperature.
