Starting with $40\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of a weak monoprotic acid HA and neglecting its small initial dissociation in the preparation balance, which treatment prepares appreciable amounts of both HA and A⁻ without excess strong reagent?

- Add $40\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of NaOH.
- Add $10\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of NaCl.
- Add $50\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of NaOH.
- Add $10\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of NaOH.

## Explanation

Partial neutralisation converts ten millimoles of HA into A⁻ and leaves thirty of HA. At equivalence, almost all the acid has been converted; beyond equivalence, excess strong base remains. Sodium chloride neither supplies the conjugate base A⁻ nor produces a stoichiometric amount of it.
