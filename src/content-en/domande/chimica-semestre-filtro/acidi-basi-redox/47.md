A nonreacting electrolyte is added to a real buffer. The A⁻/HA concentration ratio remains fixed, but ionic strength changes. Must pH remain exactly the same?

- No: increasing ionic strength necessarily makes every buffer more acidic.
- Yes: pH always depends only on the concentration ratio.
- Yes: a nonreacting electrolyte cannot change any solution property.
- No: activity coefficients and the activity ratio can change.

## Explanation

The thermodynamic expression uses activities. At fixed concentration ratio, changing coefficients can change the activity ratio and therefore pH. No reaction producing strong acid is required. Unchanged pH is a prediction of the ideal model within its assumptions, not a universal rule for real solutions.
