A buffer contains $0.0100\,\mathrm{mol}$ HA and $0.0100\,\mathrm{mol}$ $\mathrm{A^-}$. Add $0.00200\,\mathrm{mol}$ NaOH. Calculate final pH with $pK_a=4.76$, assuming complete hydroxide reaction and valid concentration-based Henderson–Hasselbalch.

- $\mathrm{pH}\simeq4.94$.
- $\mathrm{pH}=4.76$.
- $\mathrm{pH}\simeq4.58$.
- $\mathrm{pH}=12.00$.

## Explanation

Hydroxide consumes HA through $\mathrm{HA+OH^-\rightarrow A^-+H_2O}$. Final mole amounts are $0.00800$ HA and $0.0120$ $\mathrm{A^-}$. Both remain present; the ratio is $1.5$ and pH is $4.76+\log_{10}1.5=4.93609\simeq4.94$. Using the initial ratio would ignore neutralisation. Added base increases the base-to-acid ratio and therefore raises pH.
