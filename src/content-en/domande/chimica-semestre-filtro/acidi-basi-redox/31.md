An ideal 1:1 salt contains an acidic monovalent cation with $K_a=5.6\cdot10^{-10}$ and a basic anion with $K_b=2.0\cdot10^{-5}$. In the usual dilute model at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$ with negligible water contribution, which character predominates?

- Neutral, because both constants are below one.
- Acidic, because a cation is necessarily a strong acid.
- Neutral, because the salt has equal positive and negative charge.
- Basic, because the anion's basic tendency is greater.

## Explanation

Compare the constants describing the ions' actual roles. The given basic constant is much greater than the acid constant, so basic behaviour predominates in this model. Overall solution electroneutrality does not imply acid–base neutrality: it concerns the sum of all charges.
