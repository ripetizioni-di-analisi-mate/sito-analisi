When sulfite $\mathrm{SO_3^{2-}}$ is oxidised to sulfate $\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$, how many electrons are lost per sulfur atom?

- $4$.
- $2$.
- $6$.
- $1$.

## Explanation

For sulfite, $x+3(-2)=-2$ gives sulfur at $+4$; for sulfate, $x+4(-2)=-2$ gives $+6$. The rise of two units corresponds to losing two electrons. In acidic medium the half-reaction can be written $\mathrm{SO_3^{2-}+H_2O\rightarrow SO_4^{2-}+2H^++2e^-}$, also conserving atoms and charge.
