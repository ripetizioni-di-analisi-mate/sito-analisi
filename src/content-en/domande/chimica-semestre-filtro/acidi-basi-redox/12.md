For $\mathrm{CaC_2O_4(s)\rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+}+C_2O_4^{2-}}$, describe how oxalate protonation affects dissolution. Start with a saturated solution in equilibrium with the solid present. Assume fixed temperature and neglect other calcium equilibria and activity-coefficient changes.

- Protonation lowers free oxalate and favours further dissolution.
- Protonation necessarily changes $K_{ps}$ at the same temperature.
- Protonation increases free oxalate and favours dissolution.
- Protonation consumes calcium and leaves solubility unchanged.

## Explanation

The solubility product involves free calcium and oxalate activities. Converting some $\mathrm{C_2O_4^{2-}}$ into $\mathrm{HC_2O_4^-}$ lowers free-anion activity. The ion product falls below $K_{ps}$, allowing more solid to dissolve until a new equilibrium. $K_{ps}$ remains unchanged at fixed temperature in the adopted model. Total dissolved oxalate must not be confused with its free, unprotonated fraction.
