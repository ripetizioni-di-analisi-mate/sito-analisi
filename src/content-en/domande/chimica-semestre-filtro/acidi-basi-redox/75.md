Every coefficient in a cell reaction is doubled. How do $n$, $\Delta_rG^\circ$ and $E^\circ$, as referred to the written equation, change?

- All three double.
- Only $E^\circ$ doubles.
- $n$ doubles, while $\Delta_rG^\circ$ and $E^\circ$ halve.
- $n$ and $\Delta_rG^\circ$ double; $E^\circ$ is unchanged.

## Explanation

The doubled equation transfers twice as many electrons and represents twice the transformation, so the associated Gibbs energy doubles too. In $E^\circ=-\dfrac{\Delta_rG^\circ}{nF}$, the factors cancel. Potential is intensive and is not multiplied like stoichiometric coefficients.
