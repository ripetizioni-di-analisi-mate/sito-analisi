In biochemical tables, what does $E^{\circ\prime}$ indicate compared with chemical standard potential $E^\circ$?

- The same reaction potential multiplied by its proton count.
- A transformed convention with fixed pH, often seven, to be specified with the other conditions.
- A value necessarily defined at body temperature, with protons in their chemical standard state.
- A potential that never depends on pH for any reaction.

## Explanation

The biochemical convention fixes proton activity rather than imposing the chemical standard state for protons. The value depends on the stated convention, often pH seven, and table conditions. Treating $E^{\circ\prime}$ as $E^\circ$ without checking definitions can double-count or omit the proton contribution.
