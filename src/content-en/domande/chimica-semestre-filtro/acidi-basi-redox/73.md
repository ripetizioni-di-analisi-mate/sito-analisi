In a Daniell cell with Zn/Zn²⁺ and Cu²⁺/Cu, why does an inert salt bridge mainly send anions towards the zinc half-cell and cations towards the copper half-cell?

- It consumes Zn²⁺ at the anode and produces Cu²⁺ at the cathode.
- It carries electrons through the electrolyte to copper.
- It necessarily precipitates both metal cations.
- It compensates for Zn²⁺ production at the anode and Cu²⁺ consumption at the cathode.

## Explanation

During discharge, zinc oxidation adds positive charge to its solution; copper(II) reduction removes positive charge from the other. Ionic migration limits these imbalances and allows the circuit to operate. Electrons instead follow the external metal connection: the bridge is not an electronic wire.
