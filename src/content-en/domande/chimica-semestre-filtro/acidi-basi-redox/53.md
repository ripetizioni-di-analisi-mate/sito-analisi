A bicarbonate-buffer formula uses total dissolved CO₂ in the denominator and an apparent constant. Can the $pK_a$ of the H₂CO₃ molecule alone be substituted without other changes?

- No: acid constants can never be used for buffers.
- Yes: H₂CO₃ concentration always equals all dissolved CO₂.
- No: that would change the species definition and the equilibrium included in the constant.
- Yes: in dilute solution, apparent constants and single-species constants are always interchangeable.

## Explanation

The apparent constant incorporates the convention for the species counted and their connection to CO₂ hydration. Dissociation of H₂CO₃ alone has a different definition. The constant must match the denominator, temperature and conditions of the formula; values associated with different equilibria must not be mixed.
