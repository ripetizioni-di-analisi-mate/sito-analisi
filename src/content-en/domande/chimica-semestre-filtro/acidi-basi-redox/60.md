At the same temperature and with consistent standard states, given $\mathrm{CaA(s)\rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+}+A^{2-}}$ with constant $K_{sp}$ and $\mathrm{HA^-\rightleftharpoons H^++A^{2-}}$ with constant $K_a$, what is the constant for $\mathrm{CaA(s)+H^+\rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+}+HA^-}$?

- $K_{sp}+K_a$.
- $\dfrac{K_{sp}}{K_a}$.
- $\dfrac{K_a}{K_{sp}}$.
- $K_{sp}K_a$.

## Explanation

Add dissolution to the reversed second reaction, which protonates A²⁻. Reversing a reaction inverts its constant; adding reactions multiplies their constants. The result is therefore $\dfrac{K_{sp}}{K_a}$. This explicitly connects anion protonation to solid dissolution.
