In the ideal model, a solution contains total oxalate $1.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{M}$, but only fraction $0.010$ is free C₂O₄²⁻. Free Ca²⁺ concentration is $1.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{M}$ and $K_{sp}=2.0\cdot10^{-9}$. What does the ion product indicate?

- $Q=1.0\cdot10^{-6}>K_{sp}$: total oxalate must always be used.
- $Q=1.0\cdot10^{-11}<K_{sp}$: the solution is undersaturated.
- $Q=1.0\cdot10^{-8}>K_{sp}$: precipitation is thermodynamically favoured.
- $Q=K_{sp}$ because calcium is present.

## Explanation

The concentration required in the ion product is $10^{-3}\cdot0.010=10^{-5}\,\mathrm{M}$. Multiplying by free calcium gives $Q=10^{-8}$, greater than the constant. This is a thermodynamic prediction: nucleation times and rates can delay observable precipitation. Using the total would ignore oxalate protonation.
