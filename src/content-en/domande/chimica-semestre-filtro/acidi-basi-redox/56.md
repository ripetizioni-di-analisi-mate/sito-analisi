In a model where neutral uric acid is less soluble than its deprotonated form, what can lowering pH favour?

- Greater negative charge on every uric acid molecule.
- Complete acid ionisation and always greater solubility.
- A larger neutral fraction and uric acid precipitation if its solubility is exceeded.
- Dissolution of any solid, regardless of its nature.

## Explanation

Lower pH favours the protonated form. If that is the less soluble form and the amount present exceeds the equilibrium limit, a solid may appear. The effect of pH therefore has no universal direction: it depends on the species, equilibria and solid phase considered.
