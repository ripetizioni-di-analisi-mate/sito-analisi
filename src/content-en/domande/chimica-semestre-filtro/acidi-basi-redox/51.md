Why should the physiological bicarbonate buffer not simply be treated as a closed mixture whose total amounts of both components are always conserved?

- The system maintains the same pH for any amount of added acid.
- Bicarbonate does not participate in acid–base equilibria.
- CO₂ can exchange with the surroundings and the components can be physiologically regulated.
- The Henderson–Hasselbalch formula requires bicarbonate to remain constant regardless of CO₂ exchange.

## Explanation

Ventilation allows CO₂ exchange, and other processes, including renal processes, affect the bicarbonate balance. Chemical equilibria still apply, but the system constraints differ from those of a sealed bottle. This neither makes buffer capacity infinite nor forces pH to equal the apparent constant's value.
