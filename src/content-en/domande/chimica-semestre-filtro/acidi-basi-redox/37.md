How does equivalence differ from an endpoint observed with an indicator?

- Equivalence always means neutral pH; the endpoint always means basic pH.
- The endpoint is stoichiometric; equivalence is defined by the indicator colour.
- They are identical by definition, whatever indicator is chosen.
- Equivalence is stoichiometric; the endpoint depends on the observed colour signal.

## Explanation

Equivalence concerns reagent amounts consumed according to the reaction. The endpoint is an experimental criterion, such as a persistent colour. A suitable indicator makes their difference small without making the concepts synonymous. The titration curve helps choose a signal close to equivalence.
