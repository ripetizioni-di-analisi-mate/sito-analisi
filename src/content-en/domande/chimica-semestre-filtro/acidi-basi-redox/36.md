A weak monoprotic acid is titrated exactly to equivalence with a strong base in an ideal solution at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$. Why can pH exceed seven?

- A weak acid can never be stoichiometrically neutralised.
- Strong base must necessarily remain in excess.
- All salt ions are spectators, but greater volume makes water basic.
- Remaining conjugate base reacts with water, forming hydroxide.

## Explanation

At equivalence, strong base has been consumed in its stoichiometric amount, leaving the conjugate-base salt. That base can accept protons from water and produce hydroxide. Basic character comes from salt hydrolysis, not an incorrect equivalence point or a necessary titrant excess.
