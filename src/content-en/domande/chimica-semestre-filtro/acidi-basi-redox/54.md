An ideal NH₃/NH₄⁺ buffer has $pK_b=4.75$ and ratio $[\mathrm{NH_3}]/[\mathrm{NH_4^+}]=1/2$. At $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, take $pK_w=14.00$. What is the approximate pH?

- $8.95$.
- $4.45$.
- $9.55$.
- $5.05$.

## Explanation

For the weak base, $\mathrm{pOH}=pK_b+\log_{10}([\mathrm{NH_4^+}]/[\mathrm{NH_3}])=4.75+\log_{10}2=5.05$. Then $\mathrm{pH}=14.00-5.05=8.95$. Inverting the ratio reverses the correction; stopping at the first result confuses pOH with pH. The protonated form predominates, so pH is below the pair's $pK_a$.
