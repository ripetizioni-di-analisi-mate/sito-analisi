A weak monofunctional base B, initially $0.0100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, gives pH $10.00$ at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$. Calculate $K_b$ assuming $pK_w=14.00$, ideal dilute behaviour and negligible autoprotolysis.

- $K_b=1.00\cdot10^{-10}$.
- $K_b=1.00\cdot10^{-4}$.
- $K_b\simeq1.01\cdot10^{-6}$.
- $K_b=1.00\cdot10^{-2}$.

## Explanation

pOH is $14.00-10.00=4.00$, so $[\mathrm{OH^-}]=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. The reaction produces the same $\mathrm{BH^+}$ concentration and leaves $0.0100-0.0001=0.0099\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ of B. Thus $K_b=\dfrac{(10^{-4})^2}{0.0099}\simeq1.01\cdot10^{-6}$ in the numerical reduced-concentration convention. Both product concentrations enter the numerator; using only one would not represent the equilibrium.
