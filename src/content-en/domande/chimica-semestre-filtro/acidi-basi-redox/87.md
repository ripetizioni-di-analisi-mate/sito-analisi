The net Haber–Weiss equation is often presented as a reaction between superoxide and peroxide. Which caution is correct?

- Iron’s absence from the net equation proves that it cannot affect the rate.
- The net equation does not establish a fast uncatalysed direct reaction; metal-mediated routes can matter.
- A balanced equation always proves that the direct process is instantaneous.
- Thermodynamic direction alone determines the rate constant.

## Explanation

The balance states overall reactants and products but does not necessarily identify one fast step. The uncatalysed direct reaction is slow under commonly considered aqueous conditions; transition-metal cycles can provide another route. Thermodynamics, mechanism and kinetics answer different questions.
