For a redox reaction at fixed temperature, the quotient satisfies $Q<K$. What follows from $\Delta_rG=RT\ln(\dfrac{Q}{K})$ and $\Delta_rG=-nFE$, with $n>0$?

- $\Delta_rG=0$ and $E=0$.
- $\Delta_rG<0$ and $E>0$ in the written direction.
- $\Delta_rG>0$ and $E>0$ in the written direction.
- $\Delta_rG<0$ and $E<0$ in the written direction.

## Explanation

The ratio $\dfrac{Q}{K}$ is below one, so its logarithm is negative. Reaction Gibbs energy is therefore negative and potential positive. Both become zero at equilibrium, where $Q=K$. This criterion identifies the thermodynamic direction without determining the reaction rate.
