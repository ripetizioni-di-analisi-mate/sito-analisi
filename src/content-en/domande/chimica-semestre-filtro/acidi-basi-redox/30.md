HF is a weak acid. Which reaction explains the basic character of an ideal NaF solution, neglecting sodium hydrolysis?

- $\mathrm{Na^++H_2O\rightleftharpoons NaOH+H^+}$.
- $\mathrm{F^-+H_2O\rightleftharpoons HF+H_3O^+}$.
- Merely separating into $\mathrm{Na^+}$ and $\mathrm{F^-}$ directly produces hydroxide.
- $\mathrm{F^-+H_2O\rightleftharpoons HF+OH^-}$.

## Explanation

Fluoride is HF's conjugate base and can accept a proton from water, leaving hydroxide. The correct reaction also conserves charge. Dissolution and hydrolysis are conceptually distinct: the first separates ions, while the second describes the proton transfer that changes pH.
