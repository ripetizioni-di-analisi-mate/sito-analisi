A buffer is diluted until its concentrations are comparable to those of ions produced by water. Is it reliable to keep the pH obtained from the ratio of initially weighed amounts unchanged?

- Yes: water does not participate in acid–base equilibria.
- No: dilution necessarily changes the thermodynamic value of $K_a$.
- Yes: the ratio of weighed amounts always determines pH exactly.
- No: water autoionisation and equilibrium concentrations must be reconsidered.

## Explanation

At extreme dilution, equilibrium concentrations can no longer safely be identified with prepared concentrations. Ions from water and redistribution of the conjugate forms become relevant. The acid constant need not change at fixed temperature; the balances and equilibria must instead be solved together.
