A cell's temperature changes. Is its $E^\circ$ at $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ generally sufficient to calculate the new potential by changing only $T$ in $RT/(nF)$?

- No: $E^\circ$, activities and mixture equilibria can also change.
- No: the Nernst relation applies only at $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
- Yes: temperature appears only in the denominator of $Q$.
- Yes: standard potential is by definition independent of temperature.

## Explanation

The general Nernst equation applies at different temperatures, but requires consistent data at the chosen temperature. Standard potential depends on reaction thermodynamics and can vary; composition and activities can change too. The familiar numerical factor at twenty-five degrees does not make the other data universal.
