Which half-line satisfies the double inequality $3x-1<2x+1\le x+5$?

- $(-\infty,2]$
- $(-\infty,4]$
- $(-\infty,2)$
- $[2,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The left comparison becomes $x<2$ after subtracting $2x-1$. The right comparison becomes $x\le4$ after subtracting $x+1$. Every number below $2$ already meets the upper bound $4$. The intersection is therefore $(-\infty,2)$. At $x=2$ the first comparison becomes $5<5$, which is false even though the second still holds. Both comparisons are required, so a value that fails either one is outside the solution set.
