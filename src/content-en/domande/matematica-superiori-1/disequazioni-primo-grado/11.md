Solve $2(x-3)<6$. What is the solution set?

- $\left(6, \infty\right)$
- $\left[6, \infty\right)$
- $\left(-\infty, 6\right)$
- $\left(-\infty, 6\right]$

## Explanation

Divide by two, a positive number. Then add $3$ to both sides without reversing the sign. The boundary value does not satisfy the strict inequality.

$$
x-3<3,\qquad x<6
$$

The result is $\left(-\infty, 6\right)$.
