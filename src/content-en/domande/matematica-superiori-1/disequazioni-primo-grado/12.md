To solve $-2(3-x)>4$, divide by $-2$, subtract $3$, then divide by $-1$. How many times does the inequality sign reverse in these steps?

- $0$
- $1$
- $3$
- $2$

## Explanation

Dividing by $-2$ gives $3-x<-2$, the first reversal. Subtracting $3$ preserves the sign and gives $-x<-5$. Multiplying by $-1$ reverses the sign again to give $x>5$. Expanding the product first would directly give $-6+2x>4$ and lead to a method with a different number of reversals. The number of reversals is specific to the steps listed in the question.
