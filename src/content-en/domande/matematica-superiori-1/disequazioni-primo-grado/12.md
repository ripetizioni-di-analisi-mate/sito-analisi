Solve $2(x-4)<6$. What is the solution set?

- $\left(7, \infty\right)$
- $\left[7, \infty\right)$
- $\left(-\infty, 7\right]$
- $\left(-\infty, 7\right)$

## Explanation

Divide by two, a positive number. Then add $4$ to both sides without reversing the sign. The boundary value does not satisfy the strict inequality.

$$
x-4<3,\qquad x<7
$$

The result is $\left(-\infty, 7\right)$.
