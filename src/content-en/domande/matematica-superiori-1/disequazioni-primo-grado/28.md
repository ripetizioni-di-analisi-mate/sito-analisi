Let $A=\dfrac{x+1}{2}$ and $B=\dfrac{x+2}{3}$. By how much does $A-B$ increase when $x$ increases by $6$?

- $2$
- $3$
- $5$
- $1$

## Explanation

Increasing $x$ by $6$ raises the numerator of $A$ by $6$, so $A$ increases by $3$. At the same time $B$ increases by $2$. Their difference therefore increases by $3-2=1$. Another method reduces the difference to $\dfrac{x-1}{6}$. This rises by one whenever its numerator rises by six. The same increase applies for every starting point because the difference is linear.
