Which inequality results from multiplying $\dfrac{x-2}{-6}+\dfrac{x+1}{4}\ge1$ by $12$, before expanding the products?

- $2(x-2)+3(x+1)\ge12$
- $-2(x+2)+3(x+1)\ge12$
- $-2(x-2)+3(x+1)\ge12$
- $-2(x-2)+3(x+1)\le12$

## Explanation

The overall multiplier is $12$, so the sign is preserved. In the first term the quotient $12:(-6)$ equals $-2$ and multiplies the whole numerator $x-2$. In the second term $12:4=3$. The right side becomes $12$. Expanding afterwards gives $x+7\ge12$, so the boundary is $x=5$. Here the coefficient of $x$ after expansion is positive, so the last step keeps the order.
