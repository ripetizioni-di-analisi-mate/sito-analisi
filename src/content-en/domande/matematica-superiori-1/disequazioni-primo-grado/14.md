Solve $2(x-6)<6$. What is the solution set?

- $\left(9, \infty\right)$
- $\left(-\infty, 9\right)$
- $\left[9, \infty\right)$
- $\left(-\infty, 9\right]$

## Explanation

Divide by two, a positive number. Then add $6$ to both sides without reversing the sign. The boundary value does not satisfy the strict inequality.

$$
x-6<3,\qquad x<9
$$

The result is $\left(-\infty, 9\right)$.
