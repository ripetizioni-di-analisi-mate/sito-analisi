Which rule should you apply first to $\dfrac34:\left(\dfrac56-\dfrac13\right)$?

- Calculate $\dfrac34:\dfrac56$
- Find the reciprocal of $\dfrac13$
- Find the reciprocal of $\dfrac34$
- Give the two fractions in parentheses a common denominator

## Explanation

The divisor is the value of the entire group in parentheses. To find it, write $\dfrac13=\dfrac26$ and subtract: $\dfrac56-\dfrac26=\dfrac36=\dfrac12$. Only then can division be replaced by multiplication by the reciprocal $2$, giving $\dfrac32$. Inverting a single fraction before subtracting would change one term instead of acting on the whole divisor.
