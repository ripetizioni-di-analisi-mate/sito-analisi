$100\,\mathrm g$ of a salt solution at $12\%$ by mass are mixed with $300\,\mathrm g$ of a salt solution at $28\%$ by mass. What percentage of salt by mass does the mixture have?

- $40\%$
- $16\%$
- $24\%$
- $20\%$

## Explanation

The salt in the first solution is $12\%$ of $100\,\mathrm g$, that is $12\,\mathrm g$, and the salt in the second is $28\%$ of $300\,\mathrm g$, that is $84\,\mathrm g$. The mixture therefore contains $12+84=96\,\mathrm g$ of salt in $100+300=400\,\mathrm g$ of solution. So the percentage of salt is

$$
100\cdot\frac{96}{400}=24.
$$

The mixture contains $24\%$ salt by mass. The value $20\%$ is the arithmetic mean of $12\%$ and $28\%$, which gives the same weight to two different masses, while $16\%$ is obtained by swapping the two masses.
