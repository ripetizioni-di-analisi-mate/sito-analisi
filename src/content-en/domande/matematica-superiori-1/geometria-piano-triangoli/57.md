A triangle has perimeter $36$ cm. One side measures $10$ cm and the other two sides differ by $4$ cm. How long is the longest side of the triangle?

- $15\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $13\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $11\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $19\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Writing $x$ for the shorter of the two unknown sides, the other measures $x+4$, so the perimeter gives the equation $10+x+(x+4)=36$.

We get $2x+14=36$, therefore $2x=22$ and $x=11$. The two sides thus measure $11$ cm and $15$ cm, and the longest side of the triangle is the $15$ cm one, because it also exceeds the $10$ cm side. The triangle inequality holds, since $15<10+11$. The answer $13$ cm is half of $26$ cm and ignores the difference between the two sides, while the answer $11$ cm is the shorter of the two.
