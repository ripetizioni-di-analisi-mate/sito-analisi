In triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ we have $AB=DE=9$ cm, $BC=EF=5$ cm and $\widehat A=\widehat D=30^\circ$. What can be concluded?

- The triangles are congruent by SAS
- The triangles are congruent by ASA
- The triangles cannot be congruent
- The data are not enough to decide whether the triangles are congruent

## Explanation

Sides $AB$ and $BC$ share the vertex $B$, so the angle included between them is $\widehat B$, therefore SAS does not apply with the angle $\widehat A$. ASA requires two angles and the included side, while here only one angle is given.

The vertex $C$ lies on the ray forming the $30^\circ$ angle at $A$ and is $5$ cm from $B$. The right triangle with hypotenuse $AB$ and a $30^\circ$ angle at $A$ is half of an equilateral triangle with side $9$ cm, so the distance from $B$ to the ray is $\dfrac{9}{2}=4.5$ cm. Since $4.5<5<9$, the circle with centre $B$ and radius $5$ cm meets the ray in two points, which give two triangles with different sides $AC$. Two copies of the same triangle also satisfy the data, therefore the data are not enough to decide whether the triangles are congruent.
