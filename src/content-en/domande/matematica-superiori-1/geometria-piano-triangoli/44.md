A triangle is isosceles and has a right angle. Where is the right-angle vertex?

- At an endpoint of the base
- At any vertex without restriction
- At both endpoints of the base
- At the meeting point of the equal sides

## Explanation

The two base angles are congruent. If one were right, the other would also measure $90^\circ$, leaving a zero vertex angle. The right angle must therefore occur where the equal sides meet. Two angles of $45^\circ$ remain at the base, positive and complementary as required in a right triangle.
