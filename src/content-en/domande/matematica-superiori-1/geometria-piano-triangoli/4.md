The figure shows that $AM=BM$ can hold even off the segment. What additional fact is enough to conclude that $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-3.1 xmax=3.1 ymin=-0.85 ymax=2.75 linee="-2.35,0 2.35,0;-2.35,0 0,2 2.35,0;0,0 0,2" punti="-2.35,0;2.35,0;0,0;0,2" etichette="-2.55,-0.38,A;2.53,-0.38,B;0,-0.38,N;0,2.43,M;-1.46,1.2,l;1.46,1.2,l" titolo="Both sloping segments have length $l$"}

- $AM$ is perpendicular to $AB$
- $M$ is distinct from $A$ and $B$
- $N$ lies on segment $AB$
- $M$ lies on segment $AB$

## Explanation

The definition requires two facts together. The point lies on the segment and divides it into two parts of equal length. The figure shows that a point off the segment can be equally distant from the endpoints. The location of a different point such as $N$ leaves the position of $M$ unchanged.
