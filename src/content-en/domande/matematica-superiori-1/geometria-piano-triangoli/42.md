In $ABC$, $AB=AC$. $AH$ is the altitude to the base and $H$ lies inside $BC$. What follows?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-2.95 xmax=2.95 ymin=-0.85 ymax=3.75 linee="-2.3,0 2.3,0 0,3 -2.3,0;0,0 0,3;0,0.24 0.24,0.24 0.24,0" punti="-2.3,0;2.3,0;0,3;0,0" etichette="-2.45,-0.39,B;2.43,-0.39,C;0,3.39,A;0,-0.44,H" titolo="The mark indicates a right angle at $H$"}

- $AH=BH$
- $BH=HC$
- $AB=BC$
- $\widehat A=90^\circ$

## Explanation

In an isosceles triangle the bisector of the vertex angle is both an altitude and a median. There is only one perpendicular to the base through $A$. The given altitude therefore coincides with that median, and its foot divides the base into two equal lengths. The right-angle mark is at the base.
