A construction gives $AB=8$, $AP=3$, and $BP=5$ as shown. What is the outcome?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.65 xmax=8.75 ymin=-1.55 ymax=1.55 curve="3*cos(t),3*sin(t),-0.48,0.48;8+5*cos(t),5*sin(t),2.85,3.43" linee="0,0 8,0" punti="0,0;3,0;8,0" etichette="0,-0.38,A;3,-0.42,P;8,-0.38,B;1.5,0.29,3;5.5,0.29,5" titolo="The arcs centred at $A$ and $B$ meet at $P$"}

- The three points are collinear
- Two congruent triangles result
- An isosceles triangle results
- The arcs should be disjoint

## Explanation

The radii add to exactly the distance between the centres. The one common point touches both arcs and lies on the segment joining the centres. In fact, $AP+PB=3+5=8=AB$. Producing a triangle with three noncollinear vertices would require a strict inequality between that sum and the distance.
