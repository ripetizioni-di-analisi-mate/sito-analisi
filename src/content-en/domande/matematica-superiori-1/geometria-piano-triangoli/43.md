A triangle's three interior angles all have the same measure. Which conclusion about its sides is justified?

- Two sides are equal and the third must differ
- All three sides have different lengths
- All three sides are congruent
- The sides can be scalene if the perimeter changes

## Explanation

The converse of the isosceles triangle theorem applies to each pair of equal angles. Equal angles at two vertices give equal opposite sides. Repeating the argument with another pair gives two equalities involving all three sides. The common angle measure is $60^\circ$, while the common side length can vary.
