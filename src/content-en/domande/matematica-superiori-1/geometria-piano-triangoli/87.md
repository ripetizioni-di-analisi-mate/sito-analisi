Segments $PQ$ and $RS$ meet at point $O$, which is the midpoint of $PQ$. The aim is to prove $PR=QS$ by applying SAS to triangles $POR$ and $QOS$. Which equality is still needed and does not follow from the data?

- $PO=OQ$
- $\widehat{POR}=\widehat{QOS}$
- $OR=OS$
- $PR=QS$

## Explanation

Point $O$ is the midpoint of $PQ$, so $PO=OQ$ by the definition of midpoint. Segments $PQ$ and $RS$ lie on lines that intersect at $O$, therefore the angles $\widehat{POR}$ and $\widehat{QOS}$ are vertically opposite and hence congruent.

SAS also requires the equality of sides $OR$ and $OS$, and the data do not guarantee it, because $O$ is not necessarily the midpoint of $RS$. The equality $PR=QS$ is the conclusion to be proved, so it cannot be used as a step of the proof. The missing equality is therefore $OR=OS$.
