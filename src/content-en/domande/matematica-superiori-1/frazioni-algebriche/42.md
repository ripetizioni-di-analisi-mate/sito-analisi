For $x\ne 4$ and $x\ne -4$, calculate $\dfrac1{x-4}+\dfrac1{x+4}$.

- $\dfrac{2}{x^{2} - 16}$
- $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} - 16}$
- $\dfrac{8}{x^{2} - 16}$
- $\dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

The product of the two linear factors is a common denominator. The new numerators are x plus $4$ and x minus $4$, respectively. Their constant terms cancel when added.

$$
\frac{x+4+x-4}{(x-4)(x+4)}=\frac{2x}{x^2-16}
$$

The result is $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} - 16}$.
