For $x\ne3$, calculate $\dfrac{x+2}{x-3}-\dfrac{x-1}{x-3}$.

- $\dfrac{1}{x - 3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{x - 3}$
- $\dfrac{2 x + 1}{x - 3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{x + 3}$

## Explanation

The common denominator stays the same. The minus sign in front of the second numerator reverses the sign of each of its terms. The terms in x cancel and three remains.

$$
\frac{x+2-(x-1)}{x-3}=\frac3{x-3}
$$

The result is $\dfrac{3}{x - 3}$.

At $x=0$ the original difference is $-\dfrac{2}{3}-\dfrac{1}{3}=-1$. This check catches the common error of distributing the minus sign only to the term in $x$.
