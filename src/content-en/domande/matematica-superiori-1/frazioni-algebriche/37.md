To test the conjecture $\dfrac1{x+1}+\dfrac1{x+2}=\dfrac2{2x+3}$, set $x=0$. What do you get on the two sides?

- $\tfrac32$ on the left and $\tfrac23$ on the right
- $\tfrac23$ on both sides
- $\tfrac32$ on both sides
- The right side is undefined at $0$.

## Explanation

At $x=0$ all the denominators are nonzero and the comparison is valid. The left side is $1+\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2}$ and the right side is $\dfrac{2}{3}$. This one input is sufficient to disprove the conjecture. The correct sum has denominator $(x+1)(x+2)$ and numerator $(x+2)+(x+1)$.
