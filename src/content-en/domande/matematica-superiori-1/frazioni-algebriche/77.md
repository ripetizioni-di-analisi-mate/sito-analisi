For which real value of $k$ does the domain of $\dfrac1{x-1}\div\dfrac{x-k}{x-1}$ exclude just one value?

- $k=1$
- $k=-1$
- $k=0$
- $k=2$

## Explanation

The shared denominator already excludes $1$. The divisor is zero at $x=k$ when $k\ne1$, so the domain loses a second value. With $k=1$ the divisor is $1$ everywhere on the initial domain, and the nonzero requirement adds no further exclusion. The ratio is therefore defined for every $x\ne1$.
