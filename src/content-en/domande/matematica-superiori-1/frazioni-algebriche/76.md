Which conditions make the expression $\dfrac1{x+3}:\dfrac{x-1}{x+3}$ defined?

- $x\ne -3$
- $x\ne1$
- $x\ne 3\;\text{and}\;x\ne-1$
- $x\ne -3\;\text{and}\;x\ne1$

## Explanation

The denominators of both fractions must be nonzero. The divisor must also be nonzero, so its numerator must not vanish either. Both conditions must hold at the same time.

The result is $x\ne -3\;\text{and}\;x\ne1$.

At $x=1$ the divisor equals $\dfrac{0}{4}=0$. At $x=-3$ the divisor is undefined in the original expression. Both cases prevent you from evaluating the quotient.
