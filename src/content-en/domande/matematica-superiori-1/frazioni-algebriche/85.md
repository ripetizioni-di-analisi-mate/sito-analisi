For $x\ne2$, what single fraction equals $\dfrac1{(x-2)^2}-\dfrac1{x-2}$?

- $\dfrac{3-x}{(x-2)^2}$
- $\dfrac{1-x}{(x-2)^2}$
- $\dfrac{x-3}{(x-2)^2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The first term already has the least denominator $(x-2)^2$. To rewrite the second term, multiply its numerator and denominator by $x-2$. The numerator of the difference is then $1-(x-2)=3-x$. At $x=3$ both original terms are $1$, consistent with the zero in the result.
