For $x\ne0$ and $x\ne2$, which fraction must you multiply by $\dfrac{x}{x-2}$ to get $1$?

- $\dfrac{x}{2-x}$
- $\dfrac{x-2}{2x}$
- $\dfrac{x}{x-2}$
- $\dfrac{x-2}{x}$

## Explanation

On this domain the numerator and denominator are both nonzero. Swapping them gives $\dfrac{x-2}{x}$. In the product, every factor in the numerator has a matching copy in the denominator, so cancellation leaves $1$. At $x=4$, for instance, the original ratio is $2$ and its reciprocal is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
