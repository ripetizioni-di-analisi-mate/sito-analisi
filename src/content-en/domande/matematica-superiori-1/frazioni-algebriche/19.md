For $f(x)=\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{(x-2)(x+4)}$, which description of $x=2$ is correct?

- It is a zero of the fraction.
- It is allowed and gives a value of one.
- It is outside the domain, although it makes the reduced numerator zero.
- It is allowed because the numerator contains two copies of the same factor.

## Explanation

The original denominator contains one copy of $x-2$, so the fraction is undefined at $2$. After cancelling that copy, $\dfrac{x-2}{x+4}$ remains, and its extension gives zero at $2$. The extension’s zero corresponds to a missing point in the original graph.
