Which new fraction results from multiplying above and below $\dfrac{1}{x-2}$ by a nonconstant polynomial, leaving its natural domain and values unchanged?

- $\dfrac{x+1}{(x-2)(x+1)}$
- $\dfrac{x^2-1}{(x-2)(x^2-1)}$
- $\dfrac{x^2+1}{(x-2)(x^2+1)}$
- $\dfrac{x^2+1}{x-2}$

## Explanation

The polynomial $x^2+1$ is strictly positive at every real input, so it introduces no new denominator roots. The factor cancels everywhere on the original domain and gives the required values. The factors $x+1$ and $x^2-1$ would each remove at least the point $-1$, where the original fraction has the well-defined value $-\dfrac{1}{3}$.
