For every $x\ne1$, when does $\left(\dfrac{1-x}{x-1}\right)^k$ equal $1$ for a positive integer $k$?

- When $k$ is odd.
- For every $k$.
- Only for $k=1$.
- When $k$ is even.

## Explanation

On the domain the base equals $-1$ because the numerator is the negative of the denominator. A positive integer power multiplies the number of copies of $-1$ specified by the exponent. Each pair of copies gives $1$. With an even exponent all copies pair up, while an odd exponent leaves one copy of $-1$.
