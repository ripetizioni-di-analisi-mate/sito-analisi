You want to take the reciprocal of $\dfrac{x}{x-3}-2$. Which formula preserves the correct domain of that reciprocal?

- $\dfrac{x-3}{6-x}$ with $x\ne6$
- $\dfrac{x-3}{6-x}$ with $x\ne3,6$
- $\dfrac{6-x}{x-3}$ with $x\ne3,6$
- $\dfrac{x-3}{x-6}$ with $x\ne3,6$

## Explanation

Combine the original terms. You get $\dfrac{x-2(x-3)}{x-3}=\dfrac{6-x}{x-3}$ for every $x\ne3$. The reciprocal exists where this value is nonzero, so $6$ must also be excluded. Inverting puts $x-3$ in the numerator. At $3$ that new formula alone would give zero, but the original reciprocal still requires the exclusion.
