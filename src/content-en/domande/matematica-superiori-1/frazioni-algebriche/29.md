What is the monic least common denominator of $\dfrac1{x^2-4}$ and $\dfrac1{(x-2)^2}$? A monic polynomial has leading coefficient $1$.

- $(x-2)^2(x+2)$
- $(x-2)(x+2)$
- $(x-2)^3(x+2)$
- $(x-2)^2(x+2)^2$

## Explanation

Factor $x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)$. The first fraction requires one copy of each factor, and the second requires two copies of $x-2$. Take each factor to the highest power required, giving $(x-2)^2(x+2)$. Multiplying the denominators would accumulate an unnecessary copy of $x-2$.
