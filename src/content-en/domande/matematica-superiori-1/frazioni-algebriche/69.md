Why does the graph of $\dfrac{x^2-1}{x+1}$ meet the horizontal axis only at $(1,0)$?

- The numerator is zero at $1$ and $-1$, and $-1$ is excluded.
- The numerator is zero only at $1$.
- At $-1$ the numerator is $-2$.
- At $-1$ the denominator is $-2$.

## Explanation

Factoring $x^2-1=(x+1)(x-1)$ reveals two zeros of the numerator. Check the denominator at each candidate. At $1$ it is $2$, so the quotient is indeed zero. At $-1$ it is zero and the quotient is undefined. On the allowed domain the graph is the line $y=x-1$ with the point $(-1,-2)$ missing.
