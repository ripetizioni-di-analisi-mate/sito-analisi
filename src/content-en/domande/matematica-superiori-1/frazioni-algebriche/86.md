For $x\ne -3$, calculate $\dfrac{x^2-9}{(x+3)^2}$.

- $\dfrac{x + 3}{x - 3}$
- $\dfrac{x - 3}{x + 3}$
- $1$
- $x - 3$

## Explanation

Factor the numerator into a sum and a difference. The denominator contains two copies of the sum factor. Cancel only one common copy and keep the remaining factor.

$$
\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x+3)^2}=\frac{x-3}{x+3}
$$

The result is $\dfrac{x - 3}{x + 3}$.

The value $3$ is still allowed and still makes the numerator zero. This gives a point on the horizontal axis at $3$, while $-3$ remains outside the domain.
