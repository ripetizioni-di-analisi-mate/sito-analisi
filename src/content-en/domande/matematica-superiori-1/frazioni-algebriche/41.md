For $x\ne 3$ and $x\ne -3$, calculate $\dfrac1{x-3}+\dfrac1{x+3}$.

- $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} - 9}$
- $\dfrac{2}{x^{2} - 9}$
- $\dfrac{6}{x^{2} - 9}$
- $\dfrac{1}{x}$

## Explanation

The product of the two linear factors is a common denominator. The new numerators are x plus $3$ and x minus $3$, respectively. Their constant terms cancel when added.

$$
\frac{x+3+x-3}{(x-3)(x+3)}=\frac{2x}{x^2-9}
$$

The result is $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} - 9}$.
