What is $\dfrac{\dfrac1{x+1}-\dfrac1{x-1}}{\dfrac1{x^2-1}}$ for $x\ne\pm1$?

- $2$
- $-2$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{-2}{(x^2-1)^2}$

## Explanation

For the difference in the top part, use $(x+1)(x-1)=x^2-1$. Its numerator becomes $(x-1)-(x+1)=-2$. Then divide $-\dfrac{2}{x^2-1}$ by the reciprocal $\dfrac{1}{x^2-1}$. This gives $-2$ because both parts contain the shared nonzero factor $\dfrac{1}{x^2-1}$.
