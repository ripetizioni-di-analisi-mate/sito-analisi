In the expression $\dfrac{A}{B}\div\dfrac{C}{D}$ the letters $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ denote real numbers. Which letters must be nonzero for the expression to be meaningful?

- $A,B,C$
- $A,C,D$
- $A,B,D$
- $B,C,D$

## Explanation

The fractions $\dfrac{A}{B}$ and $\dfrac{C}{D}$ are defined only if the denominators are nonzero, so $B\ne0$ and $D\ne0$. The divisor $\dfrac{C}{D}$ must be nonzero, because division by zero is not defined. A fraction equals zero when its numerator vanishes, hence $C\ne0$ is also required. The numerator $A$, on the other hand, may be zero, and in that case the expression equals zero.
