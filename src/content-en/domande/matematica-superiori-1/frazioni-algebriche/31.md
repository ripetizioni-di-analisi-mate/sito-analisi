The algebraic fraction $\dfrac{1}{x^2+bx+c}$ is defined for every real number $x$ except $2$ and $-3$. What is its denominator?

- $x^2+x+6$
- $x^2-5x+6$
- $x^2+x-6$
- $x^2-x-6$

## Explanation

The values excluded from the domain are the zeros of the denominator, so the denominator vanishes at $x=2$ and at $x=-3$. The coefficient of $x^2$ is $1$, hence the denominator is $(x-2)(x+3)$. Expanding the product gives $x^2+x-6$.
