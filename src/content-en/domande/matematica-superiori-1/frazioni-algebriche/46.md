For $x\ne 3$ and $x\ne -3$, calculate $\dfrac1{x-3}-\dfrac1{x+3}$.

- $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} - 9}$
- $\dfrac{6}{x^{2} - 9}$
- $- \dfrac{6}{x^{2} - 9}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Use the product of the two denominators as a common denominator. Subtract the entire second numerator. The terms in x cancel and the two numerical terms add.

$$
\frac{(x+3)-(x-3)}{x^2-9}=\frac{6}{x^2-9}
$$

The result is $\dfrac{6}{x^{2} - 9}$.

Using $x=0$ gives $-\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{3}=-\dfrac{2}{3}$, which is also $\dfrac{6}{-9}$. The addends have opposite denominators at that point, so their difference is nonzero.
