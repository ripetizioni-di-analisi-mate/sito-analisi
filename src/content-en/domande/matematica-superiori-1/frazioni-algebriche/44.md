For $x\ne 6$ and $x\ne -6$, calculate $\dfrac1{x-6}+\dfrac1{x+6}$.

- $\dfrac{2}{x^{2} - 36}$
- $\dfrac{12}{x^{2} - 36}$
- $\dfrac{1}{x}$
- $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} - 36}$

## Explanation

The product of the two linear factors is a common denominator. The new numerators are x plus $6$ and x minus $6$, respectively. Their constant terms cancel when added.

$$
\frac{x+6+x-6}{(x-6)(x+6)}=\frac{2x}{x^2-36}
$$

The result is $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} - 36}$.
