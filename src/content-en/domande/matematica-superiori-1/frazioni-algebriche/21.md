Which values does the denominator of $\dfrac{(x-4)^2}{(x-4)^3(x+1)}$ exclude?

- $-1$ and $4$
- $4$
- $-1$
- No values

## Explanation

The denominator is a product, which is zero if at least one factor is zero. The power $(x-4)^3$ vanishes only at $4$, while $x+1$ vanishes at $-1$. Multiplicity tells you how many copies of a factor occur without providing any extra values to exclude.
