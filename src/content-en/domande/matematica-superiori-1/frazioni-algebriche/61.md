Simplify $\dfrac{x+2}{x-1}\cdot\dfrac{x-1}{x-3}$ while retaining the conditions on the original expression.

- $\dfrac{x+2}{x-3}$ with $x\ne1,3$
- $\dfrac{x+2}{x-3}$ with $x\ne3$
- $\dfrac{x+2}{x-3}$ with $x\ne-2,1,3$
- $\dfrac{x+1}{x-3}$ with $x\ne1,3$

## Explanation

Before multiplying, the first denominator excludes $1$ and the second excludes $3$. On that domain you may cancel the factor $x-1$ between numerator and denominator. The value $x=-2$ is still allowed and makes the product zero, as the final numerator shows.
