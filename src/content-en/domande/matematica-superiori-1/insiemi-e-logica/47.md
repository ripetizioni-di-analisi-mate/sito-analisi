The set $A=\{x\in\mathbb Z\mid x^2<5\}$ consists of the integers whose square is less than $5$. Which of the following membership statements is correct?

- $2\notin A$
- $5\in A$
- $-2\in A$
- $-3\in A$

## Explanation

The elements of $A$ are the integers whose square is less than $5$. Since $0^2=0$, $(-1)^2=1^2=1$ and $(-2)^2=2^2=4$, the integers $-2$, $-1$, $0$, $1$ and $2$ satisfy the condition. The integers greater than $2$ or less than $-2$ have a square of at least $9$, so $A=\{-2;-1;0;1;2\}$. The number $-3$ does not belong to $A$, because $(-3)^2=9$ and not $-9$, and the number $5$ does not belong to it, because $5^2=25$.

Therefore the true statement is $-2\in A$.
