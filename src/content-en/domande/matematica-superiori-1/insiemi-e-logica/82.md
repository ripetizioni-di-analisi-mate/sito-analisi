A family of sets partitions $\{1,2,3,4\}$ into nonempty disjoint blocks. If one block is $\{1,3\}$, how many different ways are there to complete the partition?

- $1$
- $2$
- $3$
- $4$

## Explanation

Each of $2$ and $4$ must be covered exactly once. They can share one block or form two singleton blocks.
