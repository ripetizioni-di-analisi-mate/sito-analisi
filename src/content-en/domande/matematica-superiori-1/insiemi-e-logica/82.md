In the universe $U=\{1;2;3;4;5;6\}$ consider $A=\{1;2;3;4\}$ and $B=\{3;4;5\}$. Which elements lie outside at least one of the two sets, that is, in $A^c\cup B^c$?

- $\{6\}$
- $\{1;2;5;6\}$
- $\{3;4\}$
- $\{1;2;5\}$

## Explanation

The complements with respect to $U$ are $A^c=\{5;6\}$ and $B^c=\{1;2;6\}$. The union collects the elements that lie in at least one of the two, so $A^c\cup B^c=\{1;2;5;6\}$. By De Morgan's law the same set is $(A\cap B)^c$, and indeed $A\cap B=\{3;4\}$ has complement $\{1;2;5;6\}$. The set $\{6\}$ is instead the intersection $A^c\cap B^c$, that is, the complement of $A\cup B$.

Therefore $A^c\cup B^c=\{1;2;5;6\}$.
