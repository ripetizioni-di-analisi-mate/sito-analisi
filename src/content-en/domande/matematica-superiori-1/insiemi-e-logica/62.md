In the universe $U=\{1;2;3;\ldots;12\}$ let $A$ be the set of even numbers and $B$ the set of multiples of $3$. How many elements of $U$ belong to exactly one of the two sets?

- $8$
- $6$
- $10$
- $2$

## Explanation

In $U$ there are $6$ even numbers and $4$ multiples of $3$, namely $3$, $6$, $9$ and $12$. The common elements are the multiples of $6$, so $A\cap B=\{6;12\}$ and $|A\cap B|=2$. Each common element is counted once in $|A|$ and once in $|B|$, so, to count the elements that lie in only one set, it must be removed twice, which gives

$$
6+4-2\cdot2=6 .
$$

The value $8$ is instead the cardinality of the union, which also includes the two common elements.

Therefore $6$ elements belong to exactly one of the two sets.
