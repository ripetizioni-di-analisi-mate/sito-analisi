An element $x$ belonging to $B$ but outside $A$ is added to $A$. How do $A\cup B$ and $A\cap B$ change?

- Both gain $x$.
- The union gains $x$ and the intersection stays the same.
- The union stays the same and the intersection gains $x$.
- Both stay the same.

## Explanation

The element $x$ already belonged to the union because it was in $B$. Before the change it was only in $B$. Now it belongs to both sets and moves into the overlap. Membership is unchanged for every other element, so the only change is that the intersection gains $x$.
