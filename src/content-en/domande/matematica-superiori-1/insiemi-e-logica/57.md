Of the two propositions $p$ and $q$, exactly one is true, and the implication $p\Rightarrow q$ is true. What are the truth values of $p$ and $q$?

- $p$ false, $q$ true
- $p$ true, $q$ false
- $p$ true, $q$ true
- $p$ false, $q$ false

## Explanation

Since exactly one of the two propositions is true, two cases remain: $p$ true with $q$ false, or $p$ false with $q$ true. An implication is false exactly when the hypothesis is true and the conclusion is false, so in the first case $p\Rightarrow q$ would be false. The first case therefore contradicts the data. In the second case the hypothesis is false and the implication is true, as required.

Hence $p$ is false and $q$ is true.
