In a nonempty universal set $U$, suppose $(A\cup B)^c=\varnothing$ and $(A\cap B)^c=U$. What can be said of every element of $U$?

- It belongs to exactly one of $A$ and $B$.
- It belongs to both sets.
- It lies outside both sets.
- It must belong to $A$.

## Explanation

The first complement is empty, so $A\cup B$ fills $U$ and every element belongs to at least one of the sets. The second complement is all of $U$, so $A\cap B$ is empty and no element occurs in both. Together these two facts give exactly one membership for each element.
