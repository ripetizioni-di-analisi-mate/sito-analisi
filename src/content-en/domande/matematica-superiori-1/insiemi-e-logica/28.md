Let $A=\{0;1;2\}$ and $B=\{1;2;3;4\}$. How many pairs in $A\times B$ have different coordinates?

- $8$
- $9$
- $12$
- $10$

## Explanation

The Cartesian product contains $3\cdot4=12$ pairs. A pair with equal coordinates can only be made from an element shared by $A$ and $B$. The intersection is $\{1;2\}$, so remove exactly $(1;1)$ and $(2;2)$. This leaves $12-2=10$ pairs with different coordinates.
