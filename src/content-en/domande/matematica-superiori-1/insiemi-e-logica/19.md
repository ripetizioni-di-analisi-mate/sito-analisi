Let $A=\{1;3\}$. Which pair could give a universal set $U$ and the complement $A^c$ in it?

- $U=\{1;3;5\},\quad A^c=\{1;5\}$
- $U=\{0;1;3;4\},\quad A^c=\{0\}$
- $U=\{0;1;3;4\},\quad A^c=\{0;4\}$
- $U=\{0;1;4\},\quad A^c=\{0;4\}$

## Explanation

There are two checks. First, $A$ must be contained in $U$, so both $1$ and $3$ must appear in the universal set. Then $A^c$ must contain all and only the other elements of $U$. For $U=\{0;1;3;4\}$, removing $1$ and $3$ leaves exactly $\{0;4\}$.
