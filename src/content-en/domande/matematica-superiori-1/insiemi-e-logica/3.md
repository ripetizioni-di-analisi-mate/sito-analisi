For two sets $A$ and $B$, suppose $A\cup B=B$. What can always be inferred?

- $B\subsetneq A$
- $A\cap B=\varnothing$
- $A\subseteq B$
- $A\cup B=A$

## Explanation

Every element of $A$ belongs to the union. By hypothesis the union equals $B$, so that element belongs to $B$. This proves $A\subseteq B$. The inclusion can be proper, as with $A=\{1\}$ and $B=\{1;2\}$, or the two sets can be equal. The premise permits both possibilities.
