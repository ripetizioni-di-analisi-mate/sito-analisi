Solve $\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}=\dfrac{2x-4}{x+1}$ over the real numbers.

- $\{-1\}$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{-1\}$
- $\{2\}$
- $\varnothing$

## Explanation

Exclude $x=-1$ because it makes the common denominator zero. On the domain, multiplying by $x+1$ leaves an equality between the numerators. The candidate $x=2$ is allowed and makes both fractions equal to zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-2&=2x-4 \\[6pt]
x&=2
\end{aligned}
$$
