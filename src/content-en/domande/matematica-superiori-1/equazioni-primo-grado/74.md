Does the equation $\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}=1-\dfrac4{x+1}$ have any real solutions?

- Only $x=-1$
- No solutions
- Only $x=2$
- Every $x\ne-1$

## Explanation

For $x\ne-1$ we can multiply by $x+1$, including the term $1$ on the right. This gives $x-2=x+1-4$, or $x-2=x-3$. Subtracting $x$ produces the contradiction $-2=-3$. None of the allowed values make the sides equal: throughout the domain their difference is $\dfrac1{x+1}$, which is never zero. The gap may switch sign as the denominator changes, but its numerator is always one.
