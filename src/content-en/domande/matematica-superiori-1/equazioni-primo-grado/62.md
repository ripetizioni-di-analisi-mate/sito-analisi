What is wrong with the conclusion “$\dfrac{x}{x+3}=1$ for every $x\ne-3$”?

- The value $x=0$ must also be excluded
- The equality would require $x=x+3$
- The only solution is $x=-3$
- The equality holds exactly when $x=3$

## Explanation

In the allowed domain, a fraction is one when its numerator equals its denominator. Multiplying by $x+3$ gives $x=x+3$, or $0=3$. The value $x=0$ is defined but gives $0:3=0$. We cannot even substitute $x=-3$. There are no solutions, and the allowed domain does not change that outcome.
