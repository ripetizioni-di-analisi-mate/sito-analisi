For $\dfrac3{x-2}=\dfrac1{x+2}$, which option gives the exact domain and the correct cross-products?

- $D=\mathbb R\setminus\{2\},\quad 3(x+2)=x-2$
- $D=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,2\},\quad 3(x+2)=x-2$
- $D=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,2\},\quad 3(x-2)=x+2$
- $D=\mathbb R,\quad 3(x+2)=x-2$

## Explanation

Exclude $2$ and $-2$ because they make $x-2$ and $x+2$ zero, respectively. Multiplying by $(x-2)(x+2)$ leaves the factor $x+2$ with the numerator on the left and $x-2$ on the right. This gives $3x+6=x-2$, hence $x=-4$. This value belongs to the domain, and both fractions equal $-\dfrac12$ when checked. The equation after cross-multiplication no longer shows which values make the denominators zero.
