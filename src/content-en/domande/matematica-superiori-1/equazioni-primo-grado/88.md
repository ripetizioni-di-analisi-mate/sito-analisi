For which value of $m$ does the number $3$ solve $(m-2)x=m+4$?

- $2$
- $3$
- $-5$
- $5$

## Explanation

Here $x=3$ is known and the parameter is unknown. After substitution, $3(m-2)=m+4$ becomes $3m-6=m+4$. Subtracting $m$ and adding $6$ gives $2m=10$, so $m=5$. At that parameter, the equation in $x$ becomes $3x=9$, which does have solution $3$. At $m=2$ the coefficient would be zero and the equation would require $0=6$. The equation in $m$ and the equation in $x$ answer different questions about the same model.
