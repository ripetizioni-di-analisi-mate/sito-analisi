At constant speed you travel $15\,\mathrm{km}$ in $10$ minutes. How many kilometres do you travel in $6$ minutes?

- $11$
- $25$
- $9$
- $\dfrac23$

## Explanation

At constant speed, distance divided by time is the same in both intervals. If $d$ is the distance sought in kilometres, the equation is $\dfrac d6=\dfrac{15}{10}=\dfrac32$. Multiplying by $6$ gives $d=9$. A scaling check gives the same result: six minutes are three fifths of ten, so the distance must be three fifths of fifteen.
