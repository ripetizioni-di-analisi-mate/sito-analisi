What equality results from multiplying **both** sides of $2x+3=11$ by $-2$?

- $-4x-6=-22$
- $-4x+3=-22$
- $-4x-6=11$
- $-4x+6=-22$

## Explanation

On the left, the factor $-2$ multiplies all of $2x+3$, giving $-4x-6$. The right side becomes $-22$. Dividing the new equation by $-2$ gives back the original exactly, so the solution set stays the same. For instance, $x=4$ gives $-22$ on both sides of the new equation. Applying the factor only to the first term would give the wrong constant in the transformed equation.
