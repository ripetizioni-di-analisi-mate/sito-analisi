What equality follows when you multiply $\dfrac{x+4}{5}-\dfrac{3x-2}{10}=1$ by the least common multiple of the denominators?

- $2(x+4)-(3x-2)=10$
- $2(x+4)-(3x-2)=1$
- $(x+4)-2(3x-2)=10$
- $2(x+4)-(3x+2)=10$

## Explanation

The least common multiple is $10$. Multiply the first numerator by $10:5=2$ and the second by $10:10=1$. Keep the minus sign in front of the whole second numerator. Multiply the term $1$ on the right by $10$ as well. Expanding then gives $-x+10=10$, so $x=0$. To check, at $x=0$ the first fraction is four fifths, and subtracting the second adds one fifth.
