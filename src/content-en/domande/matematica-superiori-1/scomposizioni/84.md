Use $u=x^2$ to factor $x^4-5x^2+4$ into real linear factors. Which product do you get?

- $(x-1)(x+1)(x-4)(x+4)$
- $(x-1)^2(x-2)^2$
- $(x-1)(x+1)(x-2)^2$
- $(x-1)(x+1)(x-2)(x+2)$

## Explanation

The substitution gives $u^2-5u+4=(u-1)(u-4)$, since $-1-4=-5$ and $(-1)(-4)=4$. Returning to $x$ gives the two differences of squares $x^2-1$ and $x^2-4$. Their numerical bases are $1$ and $2$, so each difference produces two factors with opposite constants. The final product has four distinct zeros.
