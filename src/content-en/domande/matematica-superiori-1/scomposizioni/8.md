Starting from $11x^3-11x$ you obtain $11x(x^2-1)$. Why can the factorisation continue?

- $11$ is a perfect square
- $x$ also divides $-1$ as a polynomial
- $x^2-1$ is the square of $x-1$
- $x^2-1$ is a difference of squares

## Explanation

The initial step removes only the common factor. The remaining factor splits into $(x-1)(x+1)$, giving three linear factors apart from the numerical coefficient.
