Factoring $x^4-16$ gives $(x-2)(x+2)(x^2+4)$. Why can the final factor not be written as a product of two real linear binomials?

- $4$ is not a perfect square.
- $x^2+4$ vanishes only when $x=0$.
- $x^2+4$ is strictly positive for every real $x$.
- A quadratic polynomial never has linear factors.

## Explanation

For all real $x$, $x^2\ge 0$, so $x^2+4\ge4$. If the quadratic had a real linear factor $ax+b$ with $a\ne0$, it would vanish at $x=-b/a$. The inequality rules out any real zero. The two binomials $x-2$ and $x+2$ come from $x^2-4$ and vanish at $2$ and $-2$, respectively.
