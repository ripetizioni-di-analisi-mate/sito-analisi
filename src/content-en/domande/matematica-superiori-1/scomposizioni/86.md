What value of $x$ makes the factor $x+3$ zero in the factorisation $(x+3)(x+2)$?

- $3$
- $-3$
- $-6$
- $5$

## Explanation

To make the given factor zero, the sum of x and the number must be zero. The value sought is the opposite of that number. Substitute it in the factor to check that it vanishes.

$$
x+3=0,\qquad x=-3
$$

The result is $-3$.

The constant term has the opposite sign to the factor’s zero. At $-3$ the second factor equals $-1$, but the first already makes the product zero.
