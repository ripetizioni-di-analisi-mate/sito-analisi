Factor $3x^3-3x$.

- $3 x \left(x - 1\right)^{2}$
- $\left(x + 1\right) \left(3 x - 3\right)$
- $3 x \left(x^{2} + 1\right)$
- $3 x \left(x - 1\right) \left(x + 1\right)$

## Explanation

First factor out the common monomial. The remaining factor is a difference of squares. Factor it too to complete the calculation.

$$
3x^3-3x=3x(x^2-1)=3x(x-1)(x+1)
$$

The result is $3 x \left(x - 1\right) \left(x + 1\right)$.

It is essential to retain the extracted monomial. It gives the zero $0$ and multiplies the coefficients by three when the final product is expanded.
