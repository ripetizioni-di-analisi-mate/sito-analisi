For which value of $c$ is $x-4$ a factor of $x^2+x+c$?

- $-16$
- $12$
- $20$
- $-20$

## Explanation

If $x-4$ is a factor, the value at $4$ must be zero. This gives $c=-20$, and $(x-4)(x+5)=x^2+x-20$ verifies the factorisation.

$$
4^2+4+c=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c=-20
$$

The given factor vanishes at $4$, so the trinomial must satisfy $4^2+4+c=0$. This gives $c=-20$. To check that the value works, expand $(x-4)(x+5)$: the cross terms give $x$ and the constant is $-20$.
