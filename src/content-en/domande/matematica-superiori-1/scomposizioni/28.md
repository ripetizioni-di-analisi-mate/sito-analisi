The trinomial $x^2+kx-14$ has a factor $x+2$, with integer $k$. What must $k$ be?

- $5$
- $-9$
- $9$
- $-5$

## Explanation

The constant in the second monic binomial must be $-7$, since $2\cdot(-7)=-14$. The cross terms are therefore $2x$ and $-7x$, which add to $-5x$. Hence $k=-5$. A second check uses the zero of the given factor: substituting $x=-2$ gives $4-2k-14=0$.
