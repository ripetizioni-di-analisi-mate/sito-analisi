Which product is a factorisation of $x^2-9$?

- $\left(x - 3\right)^{2}$
- $\left(x - 3\right) \left(x + 3\right)$
- $\left(x + 3\right)^{2}$
- $\left(x - 3\right) \left(x + 1\right)$

## Explanation

The polynomial is a difference of squares. Its factorisation uses the sum and the difference of the two bases. When the factors are multiplied, the mixed products cancel.

$$
x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)
$$

The result is $\left(x - 3\right) \left(x + 3\right)$.

As a check, $-3$ and $3$ have product $-9$ and sum zero. Their product therefore recovers the constant term without introducing a linear term.
