Factor $x^2-6x+9$.

- $\left(x + 3\right)^{2}$
- $\left(x - 3\right) \left(x + 3\right)$
- $x \left(x - 6\right)$
- $\left(x - 3\right)^{2}$

## Explanation

The outside terms are squares and the middle term is the negative of twice the product. The trinomial is therefore the square of a difference. The constant term stays positive in the expansion.

$$
x^2-6x+9=(x-3)^2
$$

The result is $\left(x - 3\right)^{2}$.

The constant term is positive because it comes from $(-3)(-3)$. The linear term is negative because each cross product contains exactly one minus sign.
