How many distinct real zeros does the polynomial $3x(x-4)^2(x+1)$ have?

- $1$
- $2$
- $3$
- $4$

## Explanation

Set each factor $x$, $x-4$, and $x+1$ to zero separately. Squaring the second factor gives the same zero $4$, counted once.

$$
\{x:P(x)=0\}=\{-1,0,4\}
$$

At $4$, the factor $(x-4)^2$ is repeated. It is counted only once because both copies of $x-4$ vanish at the same point. The other linear factors vanish at $0$ and $-1$, which are distinct values.
