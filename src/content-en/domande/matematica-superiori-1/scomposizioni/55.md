Which factorisation of $n^3-n$ represents it as a product of three consecutive integers, proving divisibility by $6$ for every integer $n$?

- $(n-1)n(n+2)$
- $n(n-2)(n+2)$
- $(n-1)n(n+1)$
- $n(n+1)(n+2)$

## Explanation

Factoring out $n$ and splitting $n^2-1$ gives $(n-1)n(n+1)$. Among three consecutive integers one is a multiple of $3$ and at least one is even, so their product contains both factors $2$ and $3$.
