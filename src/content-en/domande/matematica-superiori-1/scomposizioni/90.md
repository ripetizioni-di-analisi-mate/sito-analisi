The polynomials $P(x)=(x-3)(x+1)$ and $Q(x)=2(x-3)(x+1)$ have the same zeros. Which single-input check shows that they are different?

- At $3$, we get $P(3)=Q(3)=0$.
- At $0$, we get $P(0)=-3$ and $Q(0)=-6$.
- At $-1$, we get $P(-1)=Q(-1)=0$.
- At $0$, we get $P(0)=Q(0)=-3$.

## Explanation

At each shared zero, both polynomials give the same value $0$. At $0$, neither linear factor vanishes. We have $P(0)=(-3)\cdot1=-3$ and $Q(0)=2\cdot(-3)=-6$. One difference in value is enough to rule out an identity, whereas agreement at both zeros leaves room for a different constant multiplier.
