What is the simplified form of $(x^2+1)(x^3-2x)$?

- $x^5-2x^3-2x$
- $x^6-x^3-2x$
- $x^5-3x^3-2x$
- $x^5-x^3-2x$

## Explanation

By the distributive property, each term of $x^2+1$ is multiplied by each term of $x^3-2x$, adding the exponents of the same letter. This gives

$$
(x^2+1)(x^3-2x)=x^5-2x^3+x^3-2x .
$$

The like terms $-2x^3$ and $x^3$ add up to $-x^3$. The polynomial $x^6-x^3-2x$ comes from multiplying the exponents of $x^2$ and $x^3$ instead of adding them, while $x^5-2x^3-2x$ leaves out the product $1\cdot x^3$.

Therefore the simplified form is $x^5-x^3-2x$.
