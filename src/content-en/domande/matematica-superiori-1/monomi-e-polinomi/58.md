Consider $P(x)=(a-1)x(x^2+3)$ with real $a$. Which values of $a$ make $P$ a polynomial of degree $3$?

- $a=1$
- $a\ne1$
- $a\ne0$
- $a>1$

## Explanation

Expanding gives $(a-1)x^3+3(a-1)x$. If $a\ne1$, the cubic coefficient is nonzero and the degree is $3$, including when $a<1$. If $a=1$, both coefficients vanish at once and the result is the zero polynomial. In particular, $a=0$ is included because it gives $-x^3-3x$. This example shows that a negative coefficient preserves the degree just as a positive one does.
