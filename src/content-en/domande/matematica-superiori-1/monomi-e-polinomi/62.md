Two rectangles have the same height $y$ and bases $x$ and $2x$. They are placed side by side along a side of length $y$ and form a single rectangle with base $3x$. By how much does the sum of the perimeters of the two rectangles exceed the perimeter of the resulting rectangle?

- $y$
- $2y$
- $3x$
- $2x+y$

## Explanation

The perimeters of the two rectangles are $2x+2y$ and $4x+2y$, so their sum is $6x+4y$. The resulting rectangle has base $3x$ and height $y$, so its perimeter is $6x+2y$. The difference is

$$
(6x+4y)-(6x+2y)=2y .
$$

The result is explained by the common side, which appears once in each of the two original perimeters and is not part of the boundary of the single rectangle.

Therefore the sum of the perimeters exceeds the perimeter of the resulting rectangle by $2y$.
