Which quotient, on the domain allowed by its original denominator, has the variable $y$ remaining in the denominator after simplification?

- $\dfrac{5x^4y^3}{10x^2y^2}$
- $\dfrac{5x^4y}{10x^2y^2}$
- $\dfrac{5x^4y^2}{10x^2y^2}$
- $\dfrac{5xy^2}{10x^2y^2}$

## Explanation

In each case the denominator requires $x\ne0$ and $y\ne0$. For $y$ to remain in the denominator, its exponent in the numerator must be smaller than its exponent in the denominator. The sole negative difference is $1-2=-1$, which gives $\dfrac{x^2}{2y}$. The quotient $\dfrac{5xy^2}{10x^2y^2}$ leaves only $x$ in the denominator. Cancellation always starts from the original denominator.
