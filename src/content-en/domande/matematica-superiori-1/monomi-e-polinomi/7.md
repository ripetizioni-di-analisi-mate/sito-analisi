The monomial $2x^2y$ is multiplied by a monomial with coefficient $-3$. The product has the same exponent in $x$ and in $y$ and total degree $6$. What is the second monomial?

- $-3x^2y$
- $-3xy$
- $-3xy^2$
- $-3y^3$

## Explanation

The product has total degree $6$ and two equal exponents, so each exponent is $3$ and the literal part of the product is $x^3y^3$. In a product of monomials the exponents of the same letter are added, so the second monomial has exponent $3-2=1$ in $x$ and exponent $3-1=2$ in $y$. The product is then $2x^2y\cdot(-3xy^2)=-6x^3y^3$. The monomial $-3x^2y$ leads instead to $-6x^4y^2$, which has degree $6$ but different exponents.

Therefore the second monomial is $-3xy^2$.
