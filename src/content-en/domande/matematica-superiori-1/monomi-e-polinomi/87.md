For which values of $a$ and $b$ are the polynomials $P(x)=x^3+ax^2-2$ and $Q(x)=x^3+3x^2+bx-2$ equal for every $x$?

- $a=3$ and $b=1$
- $a=0$ and $b=3$
- $a=3$ and $b=0$
- $a=3$ and any $b$

## Explanation

The difference of the two polynomials is $Q(x)-P(x)=(3-a)x^2+bx$. The two polynomials are equal for every $x$ exactly when this difference reduces to the zero polynomial, that is, when all its coefficients are zero. This gives the system

$$
\begin{cases}
3-a=0\\
b=0
\end{cases}
$$

whose solution is $a=3$ and $b=0$. The value $b=1$ comes from giving coefficient $1$ to the first-degree term, which is missing in $P$ and therefore has coefficient $0$. The answer with any $b$ instead overlooks the term $bx$ of $Q$.

Therefore $a=3$ and $b=0$.
