A grid of square cells has $n$ rows and $n+1$ columns, with $n\ge3$. All the border cells are coloured, that is, those of the first and last rows and those of the first and last columns. How many cells remain white?

- $n(n-1)$
- $(n-2)^2$
- $(n-2)(n-1)$
- $(n-1)(n+1)$

## Explanation

The white cells form a rectangle inside the grid. Removing the first and last rows leaves $n-2$ rows, and removing the first and last columns leaves $(n+1)-2=n-1$ columns. The number of white cells is therefore the product of the number of remaining rows and the number of remaining columns. The expression $n(n-1)$ is obtained by removing only the two outer columns, while $(n-2)^2$ comes from subtracting two from $n$ for the columns as well.

Therefore $(n-2)(n-1)$ cells remain white.
