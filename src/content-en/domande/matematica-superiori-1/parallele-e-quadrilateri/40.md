In the convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ it is known that $AB\parallel CD$ and $AB=CD$. Which argument proves that $BC$ and $AD$ are parallel as well?

- Drawing the diagonal $AC$ and comparing triangles $ABC$ and $CDA$ by SSS
- Using the fact that the diagonals of $ABCD$ bisect each other
- Using the fact that the opposite angles of $ABCD$ are congruent
- Drawing the diagonal $AC$ and comparing triangles $BAC$ and $DCA$ by SAS

## Explanation

The side $AC$ is common to triangles $BAC$ and $DCA$, and $AB=CD$ by hypothesis. The angles $\angle BAC$ and $\angle DCA$ are alternate interior angles for the parallel lines $AB$ and $CD$ cut by $AC$, so they are congruent, and each is included between the two sides listed. Therefore the triangles are congruent by SAS, and it follows that $\angle BCA=\angle DAC$. These two angles are alternate interior angles for lines $BC$ and $AD$ cut by $AC$, so by the converse $BC\parallel AD$. SSS would require $BC=AD$, which is not given, while the bisecting diagonals and the congruent opposite angles are properties of parallelograms, that is, of the conclusion to be proved.

The correct argument is therefore the comparison of triangles $BAC$ and $DCA$ by SAS.
