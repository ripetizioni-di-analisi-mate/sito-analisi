Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. The bisectors of two same-side interior angles are drawn, and they meet at a point between the two lines. What is the measure of the angle formed by the two bisectors at that point?

- $90^\circ$
- $45^\circ$
- $180^\circ$
- It depends on the measures of the two same-side interior angles

## Explanation

Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be the measures of the two same-side interior angles. Since the lines are parallel, same-side interior angles are supplementary, that is, $\alpha+\beta=180^\circ$. The two bisectors and the transversal form a triangle whose angles on the transversal measure $\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\beta}{2}$, with sum $\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{2}=90^\circ$. The angles of a triangle add up to $180^\circ$, so the angle at the meeting point of the bisectors measures $180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ$, whatever the measures of the two angles.

Therefore the two bisectors form an angle of $90^\circ$.
