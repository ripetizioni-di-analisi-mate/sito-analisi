Which of the following facts is incompatible with an isosceles trapezoid having exactly one pair of parallel opposite sides?

- Both angles on one base measure $110^\circ$.
- The diagonals are congruent.
- The shorter base is half as long as the longer base.
- One interior angle is right.

## Explanation

In an isosceles trapezoid, if one angle is right, the other angle on the same base is right too. Both legs would then be perpendicular to the same line, making them parallel to each other. This gives two parallel pairs, contrary to the stated convention. Two angles of $110^\circ$ on the shorter base and two of $70^\circ$ on the longer base are possible.
