In a convex trapezoid with unequal base lengths, which condition guarantees that it is isosceles?

- The angles at the two endpoints of the longer base are congruent.
- The angles at the endpoints of the same leg are supplementary.
- The bases are parallel.
- The angles sum to $360^\circ$.

## Explanation

The two equal angles on the longer base must be acute: if they were right, the bases would be equal, and if they were obtuse, the opposite base would be longer. Drop perpendicular heights from both ends of the shorter base. The side right triangles share the same height and, by the given base-angle condition, have the same acute angle. They are congruent, making the legs, which are their hypotenuses, equal in length. The supplementary-angle relationships hold for any trapezoid, including one whose legs differ.
