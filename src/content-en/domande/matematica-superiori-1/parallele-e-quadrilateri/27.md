Why can a convex quadrilateral not have three angles measuring $34^\circ$, $44^\circ$, and $52^\circ$?

- The fourth angle would have to measure $50^\circ$.
- The three angles add up to more than $360^\circ$.
- The fourth angle would have to measure $230^\circ$.
- Every angle of a convex quadrilateral must be right.

## Explanation

A diagonal divides the quadrilateral into two triangles, so its interior angles total $360^\circ$. The three given measures add to $130^\circ$, making the fourth $360^\circ-130^\circ=230^\circ$. That exceeds $180^\circ$, which is incompatible with convexity. Checking the total is necessary, and so is checking the allowed range for each individual interior angle.
