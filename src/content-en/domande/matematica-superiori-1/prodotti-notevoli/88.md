Which pair $(x,y)$ disproves the proposed identity $(x+y)^2=x^2+y^2$?

- $(2,0)$
- $(0,-2)$
- $(0,0)$
- $(2,-2)$

## Explanation

For the pair $(2,-2)$, the sum is zero, so the left side is $0$. The separate squares each equal $4$ and add to $8$. For all the other pairs at least one variable is zero, which makes the missing mixed term vanish. Agreement at those pairs holds only in special cases. Choosing two opposites makes the first value immediate to calculate.
