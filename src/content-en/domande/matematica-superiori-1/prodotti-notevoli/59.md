For two squares of real numbers $A$ and $B$, which pair of values $S=A+B$, $D=A-B$ is impossible?

- $S=16$, $D=0$
- $S=16$, $D=-16$
- $S=16$, $D=20$
- $S=16$, $D=12$

## Explanation

Every square is nonnegative. The formula $B=\dfrac{S-D}{2}$ shows that $D>S$ would make $B$ negative. The pair $S=16$, $D=20$ yields $B=-2$. Since $A=\dfrac{S+D}{2}$ must also be nonnegative, the full criterion is $-S\le D\le S$. The case $D=-S$ gives $A=0$ and is allowed. When $D=0$, both squares equal half the sum.
