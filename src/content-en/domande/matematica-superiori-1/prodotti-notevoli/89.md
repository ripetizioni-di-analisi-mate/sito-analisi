Which observation establishes that $(x+1)^2-(x-1)^2=4x$ is an identity?

- The left side expands to $4x$ for every $x$
- Both sides equal zero at $x=0$
- Both sides equal $4$ at $x=1$
- The sides agree at $x=0$ and $x=1$, and the degree need not be known

## Explanation

Subtracting the expansions cancels $x^2$ and $1$ and leaves $2x-(-2x)=4x$. The simplification holds for every real number. Checks at one or two points alone might miss a term such as $x(x-1)$, which is zero at those two inputs but changes other results. The same formula also follows by multiplying the sum and difference of the binomials.
