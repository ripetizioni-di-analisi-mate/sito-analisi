Which of the following fractions has a repeating decimal expansion?

- $\dfrac{21}{35}$
- $\dfrac{9}{40}$
- $\dfrac{33}{150}$
- $\dfrac{7}{24}$

## Explanation

A fraction in lowest terms has a **terminating decimal** expansion if and only if its denominator has no prime factors other than $2$ and $5$. Reduce the fractions before applying the rule. This gives $\dfrac{21}{35} = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{33}{150} = \dfrac{11}{50}$, with denominators $5$ and $50 = 2 \cdot 5^2$, so both decimals terminate. The decimal for $\dfrac{9}{40}$ also terminates because $40 = 2^3 \cdot 5$. The fraction $\dfrac{7}{24}$ is already in lowest terms, and $24 = 2^3 \cdot 3$ contains the factor $3$, so its expansion is **repeating** and equals $\dfrac{7}{24} = 0.291\overline{6}$.
