What is the smallest positive denominator of a fraction strictly between $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4}$?

- $4$
- $7$
- $5$
- $6$

## Explanation

A fraction $\dfrac{n}{d}$ with $d > 0$ lies strictly between $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4}$ if and only if the integer numerator $n$ satisfies $\dfrac{2d}{3} < n < \dfrac{3d}{4}$. For $d$ from $1$ to $6$ the bounds are $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4}$, $\dfrac{4}{3}$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$, $2$ and $\dfrac{9}{4}$, $\dfrac{8}{3}$ and $3$, $\dfrac{10}{3}$ and $\dfrac{15}{4}$, $4$ and $\dfrac{9}{2}$, and in no case is an integer strictly between them. For $d = 7$ the bounds are $\dfrac{14}{3} \approx 4.67$ and $\dfrac{21}{4} = 5.25$, so the numerator $n = 5$ satisfies the condition. Therefore the smallest denominator is $7$, with the fraction $\dfrac{5}{7}$. The denominator $6$ would work only by accepting $n = 4$, that is, the fraction $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$, which is an endpoint and does not lie strictly between the two fractions.
