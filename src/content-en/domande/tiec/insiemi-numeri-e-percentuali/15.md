The sum of two square roots is compared with the square root of an integer. Which proposed relation is confirmed by comparing squares?

- $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} < \sqrt{10}$
- $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} = \sqrt{5}$
- $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} > \sqrt{10}$
- $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} < \sqrt{5}$

## Explanation

The numbers are positive, so squaring preserves their order and we can compare their squares. The square of $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ is $2 + 2\sqrt{6} + 3 = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}$, and since $\sqrt{6} < 2.5$, we have $5 + 2\sqrt{6} < 10$. Therefore $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} < \sqrt{10}$. Numerically, $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} \approx 3.146$ and $\sqrt{10} \approx 3.162$. The equality involving $\sqrt{5}$ comes from adding the radicands. However, the square root of a sum differs here from the sum of the square roots, and the square $5 + 2\sqrt{6}$ is greater than $5$.
