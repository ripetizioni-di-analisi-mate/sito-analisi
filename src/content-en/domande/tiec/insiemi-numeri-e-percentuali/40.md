Let $q$ be rational and $r$ irrational. Which of the following numbers is always irrational?

- $q \cdot r$
- $q + r$
- $r^2$
- $r \cdot \sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

If $q + r$ equaled a rational number $s$, then $r = s - q$ would be a difference of two rational numbers and therefore rational, contrary to the assumption. Thus $q + r$ is always **irrational**. The other expressions can be rational. Taking $q = 0$ gives $q \cdot r = 0$, and taking $r = \sqrt{2}$ gives $r^2 = 2$ and $r \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2$.
