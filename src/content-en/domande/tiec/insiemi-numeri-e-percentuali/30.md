Of $100$ students, $50$ chose a math course, $40$ a physics course, and $30$ a chemistry course. There are $15$ students who chose math and physics, $10$ who chose math and chemistry, and $8$ who chose physics and chemistry. Each count includes the $5$ students who chose all three courses. How many students chose none of the three?

- $13$
- $3$
- $8$
- $5$

## Explanation

Fill in the regions of the Venn diagram starting with the $5$ students in the center who chose all three courses. The numbers taking exactly two courses are therefore $15 - 5 = 10$ for math and physics, $10 - 5 = 5$ for math and chemistry, and $8 - 5 = 3$ for physics and chemistry. The numbers taking just one course are $50 - 10 - 5 - 5 = 30$ for math, $40 - 10 - 3 - 5 = 22$ for physics, and $30 - 5 - 3 - 5 = 17$ for chemistry. The seven regions add up to $92$, so $100 - 92 = 8$ students chose no course. The value $13$ adds the three course totals and subtracts the three pairwise counts without adding back the $5$ students who were subtracted as many times as they were added.
