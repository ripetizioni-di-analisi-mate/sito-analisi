What is the smallest natural number by which $180$ must be multiplied to obtain the square of a natural number?

- $3$
- $5$
- $2$
- $10$

## Explanation

The prime factorization is $180 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$. A natural number is a square when every prime factor in its factorization has an even exponent, so just one factor of $5$ is missing. Multiplying by $5$ gives $900 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^2 = 30^2$. Multiplying by $3$, $2$, or $10$ gives $540$, $360$, or $1800$, respectively. Each still has a prime factor with an odd exponent, so none is a square.
