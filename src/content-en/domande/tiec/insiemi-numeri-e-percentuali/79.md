The price $p$ of a product, in euros, satisfies $12 \leq p \leq 15$, and the quantity sold $q$ satisfies $100 \leq q \leq 120$. Which inequality describes the revenue $R = p \cdot q$, in euros?

- $1440 \leq R \leq 1500$
- $1200 \leq R \leq 1800$
- $1200 \leq R \leq 1500$
- $1500 \leq R \leq 1800$

## Explanation

All the quantities are positive, and multiplying an inequality by a positive number preserves its direction. Revenue is therefore smallest when both price and quantity are smallest, giving $12 \cdot 100 = 1200$, and largest when both are largest, giving $15 \cdot 120 = 1800$. Thus $1200 \leq R \leq 1800$. The chain from $1440$ to $1500$ multiplies opposite endpoints, $12 \cdot 120$ and $15 \cdot 100$.
