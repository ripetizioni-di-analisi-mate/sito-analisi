What is the equation of the line through $P(-1;2)$ parallel to $3x - y + 4 = 0$?

- $x + 3y - 5 = 0$
- $3x + y + 1 = 0$
- $3x - y - 5 = 0$
- $3x - y + 5 = 0$

## Explanation

The given line can be written as $y = 3x + 4$, so it has slope $3$. **Parallel** lines have equal slopes, so the required line is $y - 2 = 3(x + 1)$, or $y = 3x + 5$, which in general form is $3x - y + 5 = 0$. The equation $3x - y - 5 = 0$ has the correct slope, but its line does not pass through $P$ because $-3 - 2 - 5 \neq 0$. The lines $x + 3y - 5 = 0$ and $3x + y + 1 = 0$ pass through $P$ but have slopes $-\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $-3$. Thus the first is perpendicular to the given line and the second uses the wrong sign for $m$.
