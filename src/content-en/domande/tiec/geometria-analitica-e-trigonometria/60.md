Where does the parabola $y = 4x^2 - 12x + 9$ meet the $x$-axis?

- at the points $\left(\dfrac{3}{2};0\right)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2};0\right)$
- at no point
- only at the point $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2};0\right)$
- only at the point $\left(\dfrac{3}{2};0\right)$

## Explanation

The horizontal coordinates of the points on the $x$-axis satisfy $4x^2 - 12x + 9 = 0$. The discriminant is $\Delta = 144 - 144 = 0$, so the parabola is **tangent** to the $x$-axis at its vertex. The trinomial is the square $(2x - 3)^2$, so its only solution is $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$. Answers containing $-\dfrac{3}{2}$ result from using the wrong sign when factoring or from solving the equation as if it were $4x^2 = 9$.
