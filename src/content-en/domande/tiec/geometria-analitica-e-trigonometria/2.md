What is the slope of the line $5x + 2y - 1 = 0$?

- $-\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{5}{2}$
- $5$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}$

## Explanation

Solving for $y$ gives $2y = -5x + 1$, or $y = -\dfrac{5}{2}x + \dfrac{1}{2}$ in slope-intercept form. The **slope** is the coefficient of $x$ in this form, so it equals $-\dfrac{5}{2}$. The formula $m = -\dfrac{a}{b}$ with $a = 5$ and $b = 2$ gives the same value. The value $\dfrac{5}{2}$ omits the minus sign, $-\dfrac{2}{5}$ interchanges $a$ and $b$, and $5$ is the coefficient of $x$ in the general form.
