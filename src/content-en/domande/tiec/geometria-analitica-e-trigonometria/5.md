What is the vertex of the parabola $y = -x^2 + 6x - 5$?

- $(3;-4)$
- $(-3;-32)$
- $(6;-5)$
- $(3;4)$

## Explanation

The horizontal coordinate of the **vertex** is $-\dfrac{b}{2a}$, with $a = -1$ and $b = 6$, so it equals $-\dfrac{6}{-2} = 3$. Substituting $x = 3$ gives $y = -9 + 18 - 5 = 4$, so the vertex is $(3;4)$. Since $a < 0$, the vertex is the highest point of the parabola. The point $(-3;-32)$ uses the wrong sign for the horizontal coordinate, $(6;-5)$ uses the horizontal coordinate $-\dfrac{b}{a}$ without the factor $2$, and $(3;-4)$ uses the wrong sign in the formula $-\dfrac{\Delta}{4a}$.
