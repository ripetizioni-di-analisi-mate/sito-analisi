For what value is the line $y = 2x + q$ tangent to the parabola $y = x^2$?

- $q = -1$
- $q = -4$
- $q = 1$
- $q = 0$

## Explanation

The horizontal coordinates of the common points satisfy $x^2 = 2x + q$, or $x^2 - 2x - q = 0$. The line is **tangent** if this equation has only one solution, so its discriminant must be zero, $\Delta = 4 + 4q = 0$. This gives $q = -1$, and the point of tangency is $(1;1)$. The value $q = 1$ uses the wrong sign for $-q$ in the discriminant, and $q = -4$ results from calculating $\Delta = 4 + q$. The value $q = 0$ gives the line $y = 2x$, which meets the parabola at $(0;0)$ and $(2;4)$.
