An angle of $2$ radians is drawn counterclockwise on the unit circle from the positive $x$-axis. Where is its terminal side?

- the second quadrant
- the fourth quadrant
- the first quadrant
- the third quadrant

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.57$ and $\pi \approx 3.14$, the number $2$ is between $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\pi$. Thus the angle of $2$ radians, approximately $115^\circ$, lies between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, and its terminal side is in the **second quadrant**. Reading $2$ radians as $2^\circ$ would put the angle in the first quadrant. It follows that $\sin 2 > 0$ and $\cos 2 < 0$.
