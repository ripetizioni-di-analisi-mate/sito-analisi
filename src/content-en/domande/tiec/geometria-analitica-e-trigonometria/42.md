The price $p$ in euros and quantity $q$ of a good are related by the demand line $p = 50 - 2q$ and the supply line $p = 10 + 3q$. At equilibrium, supply and demand give the same price for the same quantity. What is the equilibrium point?

- $q = 12$ and $p = 26$ euros
- $q = 8$ and $p = 26$ euros
- $q = 8$ and $p = 34$ euros
- $q = 6$ and $p = 38$ euros

## Explanation

The equilibrium point is the **intersection** of the two lines, so set the two expressions for $p$ equal. This gives $50 - 2q = 10 + 3q$, hence $5q = 40$ and $q = 8$. The corresponding price is $p = 50 - 16 = 34$ euros, and the supply line also gives $10 + 24 = 34$ euros. The pair $q = 8$, $p = 26$ uses the coefficient $2$ on the supply line to calculate the price, and the pair $q = 12$, $p = 26$ results from the sign error $5q = 60$. The pair $q = 6$, $p = 38$ lies on the demand line but not on the supply line.
