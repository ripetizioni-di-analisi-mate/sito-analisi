At which points does the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 4y + 5 = 0$ meet the $x$-axis?

- none
- $(-1;0)$ and $(-5;0)$
- $(1;0)$ and $(5;0)$
- $(0;1)$ and $(0;5)$

## Explanation

Points on the $x$-axis have vertical coordinate $0$, so set $y = 0$ in the equation to obtain $x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0$. The solutions are $x = 1$ and $x = 5$ because their sum is $6$ and their product is $5$. The common points are therefore $(1;0)$ and $(5;0)$. The points $(0;1)$ and $(0;5)$ place the solutions on the $y$-axis. Setting $x = 0$ actually gives $y^2 - 4y + 5 = 0$, with a negative discriminant, so the circle does not meet the $y$-axis.
