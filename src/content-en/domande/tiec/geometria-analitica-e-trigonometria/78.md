For every angle $x$, what does the expression $(\sin x + \cos x)^2 + (\sin x - \cos x)^2$ equal?

- $0$
- $4\sin x\cos x$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

Expanding the two squares gives $\sin^2 x + 2\sin x\cos x + \cos^2 x$ and $\sin^2 x - 2\sin x\cos x + \cos^2 x$. Adding cancels the cross terms and leaves $2\sin^2 x + 2\cos^2 x$. By the **Pythagorean identity**, $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$, so the expression equals $2$. The expression $4\sin x\cos x$ is the *difference* of the two squares, and the value $1$ omits the factor $2$.
