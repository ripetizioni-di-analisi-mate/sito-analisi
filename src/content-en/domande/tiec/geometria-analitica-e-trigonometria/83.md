The legs of a right triangle have lengths $1$ and $\sqrt{3}$. What is the angle opposite the leg of length $\sqrt{3}$?

- $30^\circ$
- $60^\circ$
- $120^\circ$
- $45^\circ$

## Explanation

When **solving a right triangle**, the opposite leg equals the adjacent leg times the tangent of the angle, so $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{1} = \sqrt{3}$. Among acute angles, the only one with tangent $\sqrt{3}$ is $60^\circ$. The value $30^\circ$ is the angle opposite the leg of length $1$, and $45^\circ$ would require two equal legs. The value $120^\circ$ is ruled out because the two remaining angles of a right triangle are acute.
