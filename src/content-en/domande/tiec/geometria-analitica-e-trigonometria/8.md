One leg of a right triangle has length $9$ and the acute angle adjacent to that leg measures $60^\circ$. How long is the hypotenuse?

- $9\sqrt{3}$
- $18$
- $6\sqrt{3}$
- $\dfrac{9}{2}$

## Explanation

When **solving a right triangle**, the leg adjacent to an angle equals the hypotenuse times the cosine of that angle, so $9 = i \cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{i}{2}$. The hypotenuse therefore measures $18$. The value $\dfrac{9}{2}$ multiplies the leg by the cosine instead of dividing, giving a value shorter than the leg. The value $6\sqrt{3}$ uses sine in place of cosine, and $9\sqrt{3}$ is the other leg, $9 \tan 60^\circ$.
