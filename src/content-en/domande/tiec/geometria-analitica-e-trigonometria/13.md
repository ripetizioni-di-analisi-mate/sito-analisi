What is the reflection of $A(4;-1)$ across the point $C(1;2)$, that is, the point $A'$ such that $C$ is the midpoint of the segment $AA'$?

- $(-2;5)$
- $\left(\dfrac{5}{2};\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- $(7;-4)$
- $(-3;3)$

## Explanation

The point $C$ is the **midpoint** of $AA'$, so $1 = \dfrac{4 + x'}{2}$ and $2 = \dfrac{-1 + y'}{2}$. The first equality gives $x' = 2 - 4 = -2$ and the second gives $y' = 4 + 1 = 5$, so $A' = (-2;5)$. The point $\left(\dfrac{5}{2};\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ is the midpoint of $A$ and $C$. The coordinates of $(-3;3)$ are the differences between the coordinates of $C$ and $A$. The point $(7;-4)$ is the reflection of $C$ across $A$, obtained by interchanging the roles of the two points.
