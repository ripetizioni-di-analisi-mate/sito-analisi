What is the equation of the line that meets the $x$-axis at $(5;0)$ and the $y$-axis at $(0;-2)$?

- $2x - 5y + 10 = 0$
- $2x + 5y - 10 = 0$
- $2x - 5y - 10 = 0$
- $5x - 2y - 10 = 0$

## Explanation

The line has vertical intercept $q = -2$ and slope $m = \dfrac{-2 - 0}{0 - 5} = \dfrac{2}{5}$. Its slope-intercept form is $y = \dfrac{2}{5}x - 2$. Multiplying by $5$ gives $5y = 2x - 10$, or $2x - 5y - 10 = 0$. As a check, both $(5;0)$ and $(0;-2)$ give $10 - 10 = 0$. The equation $5x - 2y - 10 = 0$ interchanges the roles of the axes and gives intercepts $(2;0)$ and $(0;-5)$, while the other two lines cross the $y$-axis at $(0;2)$.
