A vertical building casts a shadow $15$ m long on level ground when the sun’s rays make an angle of $30^\circ$ with the ground. How tall is the building?

- $15\sqrt{3}$ m
- $\dfrac{15}{2}$ m
- $10\sqrt{3}$ m
- $5\sqrt{3}$ m

## Explanation

The building and its shadow are the legs of a right triangle, and the angle of $30^\circ$ is adjacent to the shadow. When **solving a right triangle**, the opposite leg equals the adjacent leg times the tangent, so the height is $15 \tan 30^\circ = 15 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} = 5\sqrt{3}$ m, approximately $8.7$ m. The value $15\sqrt{3}$ m uses the tangent of $60^\circ$, and $\dfrac{15}{2}$ m multiplies by the sine as if the shadow were the hypotenuse. The value $10\sqrt{3}$ m is the length of the hypotenuse, the path of a ray of sunlight from the top of the building to the tip of the shadow.
