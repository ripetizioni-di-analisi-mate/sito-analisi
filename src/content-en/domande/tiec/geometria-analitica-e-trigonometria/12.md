What is the midpoint of the segment with endpoints $A\left(\dfrac{1}{2};-3\right)$ and $B\left(\dfrac{5}{2};4\right)$?

- $\left(\dfrac{3}{2};\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- $\left(1;\dfrac{7}{2}\right)$
- $\left(\dfrac{3}{2};-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- $(3;1)$

## Explanation

The coordinates of the **midpoint** are the averages of the corresponding endpoint coordinates. The horizontal coordinates add up to $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{5}{2} = 3$, so their average is $\dfrac{3}{2}$. The average of the vertical coordinates is $\dfrac{-3 + 4}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The point $(3;1)$ gives the sums without dividing by $2$, while $\left(1;\dfrac{7}{2}\right)$ gives half the coordinate differences. The vertical coordinate of $\left(\dfrac{3}{2};-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ has the wrong sign.
