Given $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $270^\circ < x < 360^\circ$, what is $\sin x$?

- $-\dfrac{8}{9}$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

By the **Pythagorean identity**, $\sin^2 x = 1 - \cos^2 x = 1 - \dfrac{1}{9} = \dfrac{8}{9}$, so $\sin x = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{8}}{3} = \pm\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$. The angle is in the fourth quadrant, where the sine is negative. Therefore $\sin x = -\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$. The positive value has the wrong sign, $-\dfrac{8}{9}$ omits the square root, and $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ comes from $1 - \cos x$.
