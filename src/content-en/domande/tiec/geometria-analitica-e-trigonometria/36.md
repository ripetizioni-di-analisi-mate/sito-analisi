For what value are the lines $y = (k - 1)x + 3$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}x - 1$ perpendicular?

- $k = \dfrac{3}{2}$
- $k = 3$
- $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $k = -1$

## Explanation

The lines are **perpendicular** if the product of their slopes is $-1$, so $(k - 1) \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = -1$. It follows that $k - 1 = -2$, or $k = -1$. The value $k = \dfrac{3}{2}$ gives $k - 1 = \dfrac{1}{2}$, making the lines parallel. The value $k = 3$ takes the reciprocal $2$ without changing the sign, and $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$ changes the sign without taking the reciprocal.
