Which point on the line $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + 4$ has vertical coordinate $7$?

- $(-6;7)$
- $(6;7)$
- $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2};7\right)$
- $\left(7;\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

## Explanation

Substituting $y = 7$ into the equation gives $7 = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + 4$, or $-\dfrac{1}{2}x = 3$. Multiplying by $-2$ gives $x = -6$, so the point is $(-6;7)$. The point $(6;7)$ drops the sign, and $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2};7\right)$ multiplies $3$ by $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ instead of dividing. The point $\left(7;\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ is the point on the line with *horizontal coordinate* $7$.
