Read the text and answer the question:

> For many years, night trains seemed to belong to the past. Flights were cheap and fast, and railway companies closed most of their overnight services. Recently, however, several new night routes have opened across Europe. Some travellers choose them because they want to reduce the pollution caused by their journeys. Others simply like the idea of falling asleep in one city and waking up in another, without losing a day. Night trains are not always cheaper than planes, and tickets for sleeping cabins often sell out quickly in summer. Nevertheless, the companies that run them say that demand is still growing.

In the last sentence, "Nevertheless" introduces

- a consequence of the high ticket prices.
- a fact that is true despite the problems just mentioned.
- an example of a problem with night trains.
- a reason why travellers prefer planes.

## Explanation

The previous sentence lists two disadvantages of night trains. Tickets are not always cheaper than flights, and sleeping berths sell out in summer. *Nevertheless* means *however, even so* and introduces a fact that holds despite those disadvantages, namely that demand is growing. A consequence of high prices would take a linking word such as *therefore*, and rising demand is not a consequence of high prices. The sentence neither describes a problem nor mentions airplanes.
