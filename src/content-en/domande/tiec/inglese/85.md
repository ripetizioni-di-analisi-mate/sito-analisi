Read the text and answer the question:

> In many small shops, customers now pay by card even for a coffee or a newspaper. Shop owners have mixed feelings about this change. On the one hand, they handle less cash, so they spend less time counting money at the end of the day and feel safer when they close the shop at night. On the other hand, the bank charges them a small fee for every card payment. For a shop that sells many cheap items, these fees can add up to a large amount over a year. Some owners have asked customers to pay in cash for purchases under five euros, but not all customers are happy to do so.

What can be inferred about shops that sell mainly cheap items?

- Card fees can noticeably reduce what they earn.
- They have stopped accepting card payments.
- Their customers prefer to pay in cash.
- They are robbed more often at night.

## Explanation

The passage says that the bank takes a small fee for each card payment and that, for a store selling many inexpensive items, these fees *can add up to a large amount over a year*. It follows that the fees can cut substantially into these stores’ profits. The passage says only that some shopkeepers ask customers to pay cash for purchases under five euros. It does not say that the stores have stopped accepting cards. According to the passage, customers are not always willing to pay cash. It does not mention nighttime thefts.
