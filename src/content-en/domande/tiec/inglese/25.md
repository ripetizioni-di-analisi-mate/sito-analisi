Read the sentence: "If the company had invested in new software, it would have saved a lot of money." What does it mean?

- The company did not invest in new software and did not save money.
- The company invested in new software and saved money.
- The company will save money if it invests in new software.
- The company invested in new software but did not save money.

## Explanation

The condition contains the past perfect *had invested* and the main clause contains *would have saved*, so the sentence is a **third conditional**. The third conditional describes a past possibility that did not materialize, so the implied meaning is that the company did not invest in the software and missed the savings that investment would have brought. The interpretation in which the company invested and saved treats the hypothetical situation as a fact. The option with *will save* changes the sentence into a future-oriented first conditional, and the option describing investment without savings contradicts the stated consequence.
