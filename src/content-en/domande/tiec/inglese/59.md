Complete the sentence: "Our plane arrives …… Rome at 10 a.m., and then we'll go straight …… the hotel."

- at / to
- in / at
- to / in
- in / to

## Explanation

With *arrive*, cities and countries take *in*, while buildings and precise locations take *at*, so *Rome* requires *in*. Movement toward a place takes *to*, giving *go straight to the hotel*. *At / to* uses the preposition for specific locations with the name of a city. *In / at* uses *at* to express movement, and *to / in* reverses the two prepositions.
