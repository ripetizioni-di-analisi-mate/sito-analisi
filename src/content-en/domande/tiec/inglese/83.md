Read the text and answer the question:

> Buying second-hand clothes used to be something many young people avoided. Today, however, apps that let users sell the clothes they no longer wear are becoming more and more popular. Sellers take a few photos, write a short description and decide the price themselves. Buyers, on the other hand, can find good-quality items for much less than in a shop. Supporters say that these apps help the environment, because fewer new clothes need to be produced. Critics point out that many users buy more than they need simply because prices are low, and that every parcel has to be transported. The real effect on the environment, they argue, depends on how people use the apps.

According to the critics, why might these apps not help the environment?

- The clothes sold on the apps are often of poor quality.
- Sellers usually set prices that are too high.
- Low prices can lead people to buy too much, and every parcel must be transported.
- The apps reduce the number of new clothes produced.

## Explanation

The sentence that gives the answer is *Critics point out that many users buy more than they need simply because prices are low, and that every parcel has to be transported*. According to the critics, low prices therefore encourage people to buy too much, and each package has to be shipped. The claim that the clothes are of poor quality contradicts the passage, which mentions *good-quality items*. High prices contradict the low prices cited by the critics, and reducing the need for new clothes is the supporters’ argument.
