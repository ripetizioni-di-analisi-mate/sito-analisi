Read the following passage.

> A cooperative that maintains public green spaces takes on about ten interns each year. They are recent school graduates who spend six months working alongside experienced gardeners. Until three years ago, the cooperative only reimbursed their bus fares. Today it pays each of them $600$ euros a month.
>
> The president explains that the decision was not motivated by generosity. In fact, when interns were only reimbursed for their fares, many left after a few weeks for better-paying seasonal jobs, and the cooperative lost the time it had spent training them. Since the internships became paid positions, almost all the interns have stayed for the full six months, and about half are subsequently hired. The cooperative now spends more on internships, but the president considers it an investment because the people it hires already know the job and their coworkers.

According to the passage, why did many interns leave the cooperative after a few weeks?

- Gardening was too physically demanding
- They found seasonal jobs that paid more than the travel reimbursement
- The cooperative was unable to train them
- Other cooperatives hired them

## Explanation

The answer appears in the second paragraph, which says that when interns were only reimbursed for their fares, many “left after a few weeks for better-paying seasonal jobs.” The statement about seasonal jobs therefore restates a sentence from the passage. The passage says nothing about the physical demands of the job or about interns being hired by other cooperatives, so neither claim is supported by a line of text. The statement about training reverses what the passage says. The cooperative trained the interns and lost the time it had invested when they left.
