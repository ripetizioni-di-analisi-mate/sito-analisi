> An editorial note introduces three interviews with people who changed occupations. “We sought different voices in age and experience,” it says, “without claiming that three stories represent the whole city.” The interviews took place on different days using the same five questions. A reader proposed counting how many people mentioned pay in their first answer and presenting that number as a percentage of the city's workers. The editors chose to set the three answers side by side instead. The shared questions made it easier to compare the selected stories, but the deliberate selection did not justify estimating how common each motive was among all workers.

Why did the editors use the same five questions?

- To make the three interview dates coincide
- To prevent pay from being mentioned
- To make it easier to compare the three accounts
- To turn the three interviewees into a sample representing the whole city

## Explanation

Shared questions give the answers common points of comparison. The passage distinguishes this purpose from representativeness. Deliberate selection of three careers leaves population frequencies open.
