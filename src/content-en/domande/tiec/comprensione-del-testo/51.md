Read the following passage.

> Some companies that receive many applications have introduced recorded video interviews. Applicants receive a link by email and answer five questions in front of their computer cameras. The questions are the same for everyone, and applicants have two minutes for each answer. Recruiters watch the recordings at their convenience and invite only some applicants to an in-person interview.
>
> This saves the company considerable time. Applicants’ opinions are more divided. Those who live far away avoid a trip, but many find it unnatural to speak to a screen without seeing a listener’s reaction. Some companies let applicants record an answer a second time, while others do not. One recruiter notes that, in the first few weeks, applicants who had practiced with their phones at home appeared more at ease than the others.

Which of the following conclusions is NOT supported by the passage?

- Not all applicants who record a video are invited to an in-person interview
- At some companies, applicants cannot record an answer a second time
- All applicants receive the same video interview questions
- Practicing with a phone at home increases an applicant’s chance of being hired

## Explanation

The recruiter observes only that applicants who had practiced appeared more at ease. The passage says nothing about hiring, so the conclusion about an increased chance of being hired goes beyond the text, attributing to practice an effect that no sentence identifies. The other three statements are supported. Recruiters invite “only some applicants” to an in-person interview, some companies let applicants record an answer a second time “while others do not,” and the five questions are “the same for everyone.”
