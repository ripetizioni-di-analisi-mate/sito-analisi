> The orchestra newsletter devotes a page to open rehearsals. A note in the margin reads, “Seats reserved for students still unoccupied will be offered to the public ten minutes before the start.” A reader points out that “still unoccupied” is uncomfortably close to “students”. The editors acknowledge the awkward wording and move a few words without changing the rule. Students retain priority until the stated deadline. Then it is the seat, if nobody has taken it, that becomes available to the public. The start time and the number of reserved seats stay as printed in the original version of the newsletter.

Which revision removes the ambiguity while preserving the rule?

- Ten minutes after the start the public will receive all student seats
- Before the start students will give the public the seats they occupy
- Ten minutes before the start, any seats still empty among those reserved for students will be offered to the public
- Students may take empty public seats only in the final ten minutes

## Explanation

The revision must preserve timing, recipients and condition. Only still-empty student seats become available to the public ten minutes before the start. The alternatives change the deadline, reverse the recipients or include occupied seats.
