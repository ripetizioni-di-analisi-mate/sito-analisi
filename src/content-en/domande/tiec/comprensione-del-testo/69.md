Read the following passage.

> For three years, a group of orthoptists visited elementary schools in one region to screen first graders’ vision. The screening took a few minutes. From a set distance, each child identified pictures of decreasing size, first with one eye and then with the other. Of the roughly $8000$ children screened, one in ten was referred to an ophthalmologist for a full examination. In half of those cases, the examination confirmed a vision problem that needed correction with glasses.
>
> Many parents had been unaware of it because a young child who has always had poor vision does not know it is possible to see better and rarely complains. The project leaders emphasize that school screening does not replace visits to the pediatrician, but it also reaches families that rarely visit one.

In the sentence “Many parents had been unaware of it,” what does the pronoun “it” refer to?

- the vision screening carried out at school
- the full examination by the ophthalmologist
- the pictures of decreasing size
- the child’s vision problem confirmed by the examination

## Explanation

The pronoun *it* refers back to something just mentioned. The last sentence of the first paragraph describes a vision problem requiring correction with glasses, and the sentence with *it* goes on to explain that a child who has always had poor vision rarely complains. What the parents had been unaware of was therefore the vision problem. The school screening and the ophthalmologist’s examination were the means of discovering it, and the pictures were part of the screening.
