Read the following passage.

> Ten walking groups have formed in a health district in a lowland area. Participants, most of them over sixty, meet twice a week for an hour’s walk along set routes with a trained volunteer. There is no registration or fee, and the slowest walkers set the pace. Family doctors in the district suggest the groups to patients who get little exercise because regular, moderate activity helps control blood pressure and blood glucose.
>
> After a year, organizers asked participants why they kept coming. Few mentioned health. Most said the walks had become a regular chance to meet people they knew and that without the group they would have spent those afternoons at home.

What is the main purpose of the passage?

- To persuade people over sixty to sign up for a group
- To criticize doctors who do not recommend exercise to their patients
- To compare walking groups with gyms
- To describe how walking groups work and explain why participants keep coming back

## Explanation

The passage reports facts without passing judgment or urging action. It describes who participates, when and how the groups walk, who recommends them, and what participants said. Its **purpose** is therefore to inform. The passage does not encourage anyone to participate and in fact says there is no need to register. The district’s doctors are described as recommending the groups, so they are not criticized. Gyms are not mentioned.
