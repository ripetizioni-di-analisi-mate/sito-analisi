> At a band meeting the treasurer proposed dividing the cost of new music stands among all members. “Everyone would pay the same amount,” he said. The percussionist did not challenge the arithmetic. She pointed out that her section had already bought stands with the proceeds from a concert. The conductor replied that those stands still belonged to the section and that after the collective purchase the percussionists could also use the band's stands. The members debated for some time without changing a single figure in the estimate. The unresolved issue was how to take past contributions and future access into account.

What is the main point of disagreement?

- Which division of the expense would be fair
- The number of concerts still to be arranged
- Whether the percussionists bought stands
- The estimated price of each stand

## Explanation

The percussionist accepts the treasurer's calculation. She points to an earlier purchase, while the conductor stresses access to the new stands. Both comments concern the criterion for sharing the cost.
