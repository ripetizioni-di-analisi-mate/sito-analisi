Read the following passage.

> On summer evenings, some squares in the historic center fill with young people drinking and talking outside bars until late at night. For bar owners, these are the most profitable hours of the week. For people living above the bars, they are hours of lost sleep.
>
> Last year, the city ordered the bars to close at one in the morning. Noise in the square decreased, but some young people moved into side streets, where they stayed with drinks bought at all-night stores. This year, the city government changed its approach. Bars may stay open until two, but they must pay for two attendants who ask customers not to linger in the street. After midnight, alcohol sales for takeout are prohibited.

In the phrase “For people living above the bars, they are hours of lost sleep,” which hours are being discussed?

- The evening hours when young people linger outside the bars
- The hours when all-night stores are open
- The hours after the bars close
- The working hours of the attendants paid by the bars

## Explanation

In the Italian original, the subject of this clause is understood. The English “they” refers to the same hours described in the preceding clause as “the most profitable” for bar owners. These are the evening and nighttime hours described in the first sentence, when young people drink and talk outside the bars. They are profitable because the bars are open, so they cannot be the hours after closing. The all-night stores and the attendants appear only in the second paragraph.
