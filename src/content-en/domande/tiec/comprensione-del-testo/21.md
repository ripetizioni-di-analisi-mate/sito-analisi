> A weekly newspaper received two letters about the new Monday market. A florist wrote that since the stalls had moved near the station she saw more customers before eight. A neighbour of the old square complained that he could no longer fit his shopping into his quarter-hour break. The proposed headline was “Two opposing views on customer numbers”. Before sending it to print, the editor changed it. The letters were both about the move, but only the florist had reported anything about the number of people. The neighbour was talking about the distance between his workplace and the market stalls.

Which pair of observations could both be true?

- The move shortens the journey for both writers
- Both writers observe the market from the old square
- The neighbour counted fewer customers and the florist more in the same hour
- The florist sees more people before eight and the neighbour cannot reach the stalls on his break

## Explanation

The observations concern two different effects. Travel time matters to the neighbour, while traffic past the stall matters to the florist. The letters do not offer conflicting counts.
