Read the following passage.

> Two years ago, the owner of an appliance repair shop bought a 3D printer. Many breakdowns are due to small plastic parts, such as a hinge or a knob, that manufacturers no longer sell separately or supply only with a whole assembly that costs ten times as much. With the printer, the owner can measure the broken part, draw a copy on the computer, and print it in a few hours.
>
> Not everything can be reproduced this way. Parts that must withstand heat, such as those near an oven’s heating element, require materials that the shop’s printer does not use. For simple parts, however, the repair costs less than buying a new appliance, and customers who once gave up on repairs now return to the shop.

It can be inferred from the passage that:

- if an oven breaks down because of a part near its heating element, the shop cannot replace it with a part printed on site
- the printer has reduced the shop’s customer numbers
- manufacturers have stopped selling spare parts
- with the printer, every repair costs less than a new appliance

## Explanation

The passage says that parts near an oven’s heating element must withstand heat and require materials that the shop’s printer does not use. It follows that such a part cannot be printed in the shop, so the inference is **supported**. The other three statements go beyond the text. Manufacturers no longer sell *some* small parts separately, and the repair costs less than a new appliance only *for simple parts*. Finally, the passage says that customers are returning to the shop, which is the opposite of a decline.
