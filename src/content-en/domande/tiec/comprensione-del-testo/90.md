Read the following passage.

> In many offices, the workday continues after employees leave. Messages and emails arrive in the evening and on weekends, and people who do not reply worry about seeming uncooperative. Some companies have established a rule that employees are not expected to reply outside working hours.
>
> This rule is useful, but it risks existing only on paper if managers keep writing at ten at night. An employee who receives a message from the boss is unlikely to leave it unanswered, whatever the rules say. It would be more effective to focus on the senders. Some email programs already let people write a message in the evening and schedule it to be sent the next morning. If managers were the first to adopt this habit, the rule would stand a better chance of being followed.

Which of the following conclusions is NOT supported by the passage?

- Some employees reply to evening messages to avoid seeming uncooperative
- Scheduling messages for later delivery reduces managers’ working hours
- Some email programs can delay the delivery of a message
- A company rule may be insufficient to change employee behavior

## Explanation

The passage presents scheduled delivery as a way to keep messages from reaching employees in the evening. It says nothing about the managers’ working hours, and the managers still write the messages in the evening. The conclusion that their work is reduced is therefore unsupported. The other three statements each have a line to support them. The first paragraph says that people who do not reply worry about seeming uncooperative, the next-to-last sentence describes email programs that schedule delivery, and the second paragraph says the rule may not be followed.
