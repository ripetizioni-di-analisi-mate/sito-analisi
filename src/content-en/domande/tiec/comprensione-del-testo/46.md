> A cookery magazine received this letter: “A dinner listed as ready in twenty minutes requires an hour of marinating. The promised time is wrong.” The editor reread the column heading, “Minutes at the stove”. The recipe required twelve minutes of cooking and eight of browning. The stove was not used during marinating. She nevertheless decided to add the total time from starting to serving beside the heading. The reader had taken the figure as answering the question someone arriving home late would ask: if I start now, when can I sit down to eat? The column was answering a different question.

What is the function of “nevertheless” in the sentence about the editor's decision?

- It signals that the editor discovered an arithmetic error
- It introduces a useful addition even though the old figure matched the heading
- It states that marinating and cooking always happen together
- It shows the reader already knew the intended meaning of the heading

## Explanation

The two stove stages do add up to twenty minutes. That would justify keeping the existing figure. “Nevertheless” introduces the choice to supply the additional measure the reader needed.
