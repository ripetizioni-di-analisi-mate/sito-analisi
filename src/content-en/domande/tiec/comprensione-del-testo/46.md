Read the following passage.

> Fourth-year students at a technical high school attend a weekly financial literacy workshop. There are no lectures. The class receives a practical problem, such as choosing between two phone plans or finding the true cost of buying something in installments, and solves it in groups using a spreadsheet. At the end of the year, each group prepares a budget for an imaginary family, including salaries, rent, unexpected expenses, and savings.
>
> Teachers have noticed that students who struggle with math participate more readily than in regular classes because they immediately see what the calculations are for. The workshop does have a drawback. It takes up two hours previously devoted to Italian and history, and some teachers are asking to move it to the afternoon.

It can be inferred from the passage that:

- the students who struggled more with math improved their grades
- fourth-year students now have fewer hours of Italian and history per week than before
- all the teachers are asking to move the workshop to the afternoon
- the workshop includes some lectures

## Explanation

The passage says the workshop takes up two hours previously devoted to Italian and history. It follows that those subjects now have two fewer hours each week, so the inference is **supported**. The passage says that students who struggle more participate more readily, but it says nothing about their grades. It is *some* teachers who are asking for the change, and the second sentence explicitly rules out lectures.
