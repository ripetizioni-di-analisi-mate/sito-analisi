> Describing a valley, a writer said that its houses “followed the water”. A reader pictured buildings carried downstream by floods. The next sentence, however, described how workshop doors along the canal all opened towards the landing stages. Later the writer recalled that the flour cargoes arrived by boat and the narrowest lanes ended at the piers. None of the buildings described had moved during the period under discussion. The writer had chosen a verb of motion to make the fixed layout of the village visible on the page, much as someone might trace the course of a stream with a finger across a map.

What does the verb “followed” convey here?

- A flood had moved the workshops towards the piers
- Houses were rebuilt as the canal shifted
- The route taken by boats depended on the shape of the houses
- The arrangement of buildings reflected the course of the canal

## Explanation

Doors facing landing stages and lanes ending at piers describe the village layout. The passage rules out the buildings physically moving. The verb guides the eye along a fixed shape.
