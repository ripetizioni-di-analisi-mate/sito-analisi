> A report on a board-game lending service says that loans rose from forty to sixty in one month. A councillor exclaimed that twenty new families had discovered the service. The clerk gave an example from the register. A single card appeared on four separate lines because one family had swapped its game every week. The published figures counted transactions rather than distinct cards. For the same reason, the clerk added, one could not conclude that there were fewer than twenty new users either. Some old cards might not have been used at any point in the second month under discussion.

Which comparison would identify cards used in the second month but not in the first?

- Compare only the two published totals
- Compare the distinct card identifiers appearing in the two months
- Subtract loans of the most requested game
- Divide each month's total by four

## Explanation

Comparing the two months requires recognising the same cards when they recur. Identifiers in the second list but missing from the first give the group sought. Transaction totals lose this information.
