Read the following passage.

> A covered market built in the 1930s in a working-class neighborhood had about forty fruit, meat, and fish stalls until a few years ago. As supermarkets spread, the number of stalls fell to twelve, and the city considered selling the building. After a renovation, traditional stalls still occupy half the space. The other half houses small eateries serving food at shared tables. Attendance has risen, especially in the evenings and on weekends.
>
> Not all residents are happy. Some longtime vendors complain that stall rents have gone up, and several older residents say the market has become a place for tourists and young people where it costs more to buy groceries.

What purpose do the views of the longtime vendors and older residents serve in the passage?

- they show that the renovation has failed
- they explain why attendance has risen
- they propose selling the building
- they show that the transformation of the market has negative effects for some residents

## Explanation

The first paragraph describes the renovation and the rise in attendance. The second begins “Not all residents are happy” and reports higher stall rents and more expensive groceries. It therefore presents negative effects for some groups. The passage does not say the renovation failed. In fact, it reports that attendance has risen. The two views neither explain the rise in attendance nor make proposals. Selling the building was an idea the city considered before the renovation.
