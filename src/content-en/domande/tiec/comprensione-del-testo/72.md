Read the following passage.

> Many prices in stores end in $99$ cents. A pair of shoes costs $49.99$ euros instead of $50$, and a subscription costs $9.99$ euros a month. The difference is only one cent, but people reading a price from left to right often stop at the first digit and register $49$ instead of $50$. Retailers know this. This is why prices ending in $99$ have become almost the rule at sales and in clothing stores.
>
> Not all retailers use them, though. Some high-end stores prefer round prices, such as $120$ euros, because a price ending in $99$ suggests a discount and a budget product, an image these stores want to avoid. In other words, a price communicates more than its amount.

In the last sentence of the passage, what does “in other words” introduce?

- an objection to what the first paragraph says
- a general restatement of what the preceding examples show
- a new example of a price
- the reason high-end stores use round prices

## Explanation

The expression *in other words* introduces a restatement, much as *that is* does. The examples in the two paragraphs show that a price ending in $99$ looks lower and that a round price suggests a product of value. The last sentence says the same thing in general terms, so it restates what the examples show. The sentence neither contradicts the first paragraph nor mentions any prices. The reason for the high-end stores’ choice already appears in the preceding sentence, introduced by *because*.
