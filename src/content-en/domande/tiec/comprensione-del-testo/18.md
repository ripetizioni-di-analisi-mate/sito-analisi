Read the following passage.

> A covered market built in the 1930s in a working-class neighborhood had about forty fruit, meat, and fish stalls until a few years ago. As supermarkets spread, the number of stalls fell to twelve, and the city considered selling the building. After a renovation, traditional stalls still occupy half the space. The other half houses small eateries serving food at shared tables. Attendance has risen, especially in the evenings and on weekends.
>
> Not all residents are happy. Some longtime vendors complain that stall rents have gone up, and several older residents say the market has become a place for tourists and young people where it costs more to buy groceries.

What can be inferred from the passage about the market before the renovation?

- the city had sold the building
- almost all the remaining stalls sold fruit
- more than two-thirds of the original stalls had closed
- attendance was especially high in the evenings

## Explanation

There had been about forty stalls, but only twelve remained, so about $28$ had closed. Since
$$
\frac{28}{40} = 0.7 > \frac{2}{3} ,
$$
more than two-thirds of the stalls had closed. The conclusion holds even if the initial number was a few more or fewer than forty. The city had only *considered* selling the building. The passage does not say what the remaining stalls sold, and evening attendance rose after the renovation.
