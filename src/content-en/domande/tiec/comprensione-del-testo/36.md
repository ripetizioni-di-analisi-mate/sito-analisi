> A fabric shop displayed three samples in the window with a sign asking customers to choose their favourite. The following Saturday it put only the winning fabric on sale. A customer looked in vain for the striped cloth and asked whether it had sold out. The assistant explained that the shop had never ordered it. The customer replied that she had seen it right there two days earlier. Both remembered correctly. The cloth on display had been a small swatch supplied so that the shop could consider a future purchase. The decision about which rolls to buy came after the vote.

Which misunderstanding explains the exchange?

- The customer takes a display sample as evidence that stock was available
- The assistant confuses Saturday with the voting day
- The customer remembers stripes on a plain fabric
- The assistant has forgotten the outcome of the vote

## Explanation

A swatch could be on display before any rolls were ordered. Seeing it was therefore compatible with no stock being on sale. The passage explicitly confirms that both memories were accurate.
