Read the following passage.

> A cooperative that maintains public green spaces takes on about ten interns each year. They are recent school graduates who spend six months working alongside experienced gardeners. Until three years ago, the cooperative only reimbursed their bus fares. Today it pays each of them $600$ euros a month.
>
> The president explains that the decision was not motivated by generosity. In fact, when interns were only reimbursed for their fares, many left after a few weeks for better-paying seasonal jobs, and the cooperative lost the time it had spent training them. Since the internships became paid positions, almost all the interns have stayed for the full six months, and about half are subsequently hired. The cooperative now spends more on internships, but the president considers it an investment because the people it hires already know the job and their coworkers.

Consider the sentence “In fact, when interns were only reimbursed for their fares, many left after a few weeks for better-paying seasonal jobs.” What purpose does this sentence serve in the passage?

- it reports an objection to the president’s decision
- it identifies a consequence of paying the interns
- it explains why the decision to pay the interns was not motivated by generosity
- it adds a second benefit of paid internships

## Explanation

The connective *in fact* introduces an **explanation**, clarifying the preceding sentence. That sentence says the decision to pay the interns was not motivated by generosity. The sentence with *in fact* gives the reason. When interns received only a travel reimbursement, the cooperative lost both interns and the time it had spent training them. The consequences of payment appear only in the following sentence, which begins “Since the internships became paid positions.” No one in the passage objects to the president’s decision.
