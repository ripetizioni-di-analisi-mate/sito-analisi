Read the following passage.

> Many households pour leftover frying oil or oil from cans of tuna down the sink. Once in the pipes, the oil cools and combines with other waste, forming deposits that narrow the drains. It also makes water more expensive to treat at wastewater plants. Yet used oil has value. If collected separately, it can be turned into biofuel or lubricants.
>
> In a city of $20\,000$ residents, the waste management company gave every household a three-liter container and set up collection bins near supermarkets. It collected $14\,000$ liters in the first year. According to the company’s technicians, the city generates at least three times that much used oil each year, so most of it still goes uncollected.

According to the technicians quoted in the passage, how much used oil does the city generate each year?

- At least $42\,000$ liters
- At least $28\,000$ liters
- About $4700$ liters
- At least $60\,000$ liters

## Explanation

The passage says that $14\,000$ liters were collected in the first year and that, according to the technicians, at least three times that amount is generated. The amount of used oil generated is therefore at least
$$
3 \cdot 14\,000 = 42\,000 \text{ liters} .
$$
The value $28\,000$ is twice the amount collected, and about $4700$ is what you get if you divide by three instead of multiplying. The value $60\,000$ is three times the number of residents, which is not a quantity of oil.
