> When my sister took over the newspaper kiosk she asked our father which paper mattered most for the front display. He pointed to an empty shelf. I thought a delivery was late. My sister, who had worked there all summer, took a card and wrote the first bus time on it. Every morning people approached just to read it and some came back the next day to buy something. Ever since, I have wondered how many habits we call commercial begin with small services for which nobody goes to the till. I cannot say how many papers the shelf sold, but I know why my sister did not immediately fill it.

What difference between the characters emerges at the empty shelf?

- Father and narrator refuse to inform travellers
- The sister knows the exact revenue and the narrator disputes it
- The father wants to sell tickets, while the sister prefers newspapers
- The narrator sees something missing, while the sister recognises a use

## Explanation

The narrator imagines a late delivery. The sister immediately uses the space for information sought by passers-by. The final sentence acknowledges that the exact effect on sales is unknown.
