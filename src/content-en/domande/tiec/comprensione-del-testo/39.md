Read the following passage.

> For three years, a group of orthoptists visited elementary schools in one region to screen first graders’ vision. The screening took a few minutes. From a set distance, each child identified pictures of decreasing size, first with one eye and then with the other. Of the roughly $8000$ children screened, one in ten was referred to an ophthalmologist for a full examination. In half of those cases, the examination confirmed a vision problem that needed correction with glasses.
>
> Many parents had been unaware of it because a young child who has always had poor vision does not know it is possible to see better and rarely complains. The project leaders emphasize that school screening does not replace visits to the pediatrician, but it also reaches families that rarely visit one.

According to the passage, why had many parents failed to notice that their child had poor vision?

- Parents never take their children to the pediatrician
- The vision problem develops only when a child starts school
- Teachers failed to tell families
- A child who has always had poor vision does not know it is possible to see better and rarely complains

## Explanation

The word *because* introduces the reason in the sentence “Many parents had been unaware of it because a young child who has always had poor vision does not know it is possible to see better and rarely complains.” The correct answer restates that sentence. The passage says some families rarely visit a pediatrician, so the claim that parents never go is an exaggeration. The passage does not say when the problem develops or what role teachers play.
