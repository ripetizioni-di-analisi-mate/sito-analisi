Read the following passage.

> A small coffee roaster in a provincial town needed $40\,000$ euros to buy a larger machine. The bank offered a loan, but the owners felt the interest rate was too high. Instead, they launched an online fundraiser among their customers. Anyone who contributed $50$ euros would receive a kilogram of coffee a month for a year. Anyone who contributed $200$ would also get a guided tour of the roasting facility. In six weeks, the fundraiser surpassed its target, raising $46\,000$ euros from about six hundred supporters.
>
> The owners acknowledge that raising money this way comes at a cost because the coffee they promised has to be produced and shipped. However, the campaign also brought something a loan could not. Many supporters told friends and coworkers about the roaster, and orders through its website increased.

In relation to the sentence that precedes it, what does the sentence introduced by “However” express?

- a contrast, because it presents a benefit of the fundraiser after describing a cost
- a consequence, because it follows from the cost of the promised coffee
- a cause, because it explains why raising the money comes at a cost
- a concession, because it acknowledges a drawback of the fundraiser

## Explanation

The preceding sentence presents a cost of the fundraiser, namely producing and shipping the coffee. The sentence with *however* goes in the other direction, presenting a benefit that a loan does not provide. Customers tell others about the roaster and orders increase. The relationship is therefore one of **contrast**. The benefit neither follows from the cost of the coffee nor explains it, so the relationship is neither a consequence nor a cause. The drawback of the fundraiser is acknowledged in the preceding sentence, not in the one introduced by *however*.
