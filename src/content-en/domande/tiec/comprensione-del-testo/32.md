> A hiking group published a map with three trails. The times printed beside them had been measured by stopping the watch at every halt. A month later a family wrote to complain that a walk marked as two and a half hours had taken them three hours. They also described eating lunch in front of a mill and waiting for a flock to pass. The mapmakers added a note to clarify their timing method without immediately changing the printed duration. To tell whether the walking time was wrong, they needed information besides the times the family had recorded for departure and arrival.

What information is missing if the two times are to be compared on the same basis?

- The number of people who ate lunch
- The total duration of the family's stops
- The age of the mill beside the trail
- The time when the flock left the village

## Explanation

The map reports only time spent walking. The family's figure covers the whole interval from departure to arrival. Subtracting all stops makes the comparison consistent.
