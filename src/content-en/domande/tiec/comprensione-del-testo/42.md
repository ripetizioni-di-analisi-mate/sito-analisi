> To choose a book cover the editors prepared two pictures, one of a closed door and another of a crowded street. They did not ask their test readers which picture they preferred. They gave them just the title and the first two pages and asked which cover would better prepare them for the story. The door won. Someone wrote in the minutes that the group disliked crowded scenes. The editor deleted that sentence. The question had asked participants to judge the relationship between an image and a story. A reader could prefer the other image yet recognise that the door better suggested the waiting central to the opening pages.

What further question would help assess the deleted claim?

- Which cover best introduces the same opening pages
- Which picture participants prefer independently of the story
- Who physically wrote up the minutes
- Exactly how many people appear in the street

## Explanation

The deleted claim concerns the readers' taste. The test instead measured how well each picture suited the story. A question about independent preference would address that difference.
