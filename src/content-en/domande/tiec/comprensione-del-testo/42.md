Read the following passage.

> Many prices in stores end in $99$ cents. A pair of shoes costs $49.99$ euros instead of $50$, and a subscription costs $9.99$ euros a month. The difference is only one cent, but people reading a price from left to right often stop at the first digit and register $49$ instead of $50$. Retailers know this. This is why prices ending in $99$ have become almost the rule at sales and in clothing stores.
>
> Not all retailers use them, though. Some high-end stores prefer round prices, such as $120$ euros, because a price ending in $99$ suggests a discount and a budget product, an image these stores want to avoid. In other words, a price communicates more than its amount.

In the sentence “Retailers know this,” the pronoun “this” refers to the fact that

- the difference between $49.99$ and $50$ euros is one cent
- people reading a price often stop at the first digit
- the shoes cost $50$ euros
- high-end stores prefer round prices

## Explanation

The pronoun *this* refers to a fact mentioned immediately before it. The preceding sentence says that people reading a price from left to right stop at the first digit and register $49$ instead of $50$. The sentence containing *this* adds that *this is why* prices ending in $99$ have become almost the rule. What retailers know, then, is how customers read prices. The one-cent difference does not explain the retailers’ choice, and high-end stores’ preference appears only in the second paragraph.
