> A translator's diary gives two versions of a line. In the first a guest tells his host, “You have a long memory.” In the second he says, “You haven't forgotten a thing.” The line comes after the host brings exactly the dessert the guest mentioned years before. The translator notes that both versions convey the fact, but the first, heard without context, might sound like an accusation of bearing a grudge. She chooses the second for an edition intended for a public reading. Listeners will hear the line only once and will not be able to glance back at the preceding lines.

What does the guest mean to convey in the scene?

- He accuses his host of nursing a grudge
- He reproaches the host for serving dessert late
- He appreciates the host remembering something he likes
- He asks for a poorly remembered recipe to be changed

## Explanation

The dessert recalls a preference remembered years later. The line comments on that consideration. A grudge appears only as a possible misunderstanding of the first wording out of context.
