Read the following passage.

> Every summer, many small towns hold literary festivals, inviting writers to speak, organizing events in the town square, and setting up bookstalls. A festival’s success is often judged by attendance, and towns proudly announce that they drew more than ten thousand visitors in a weekend. This is a convenient measure, but what it mostly measures is the ability to attract passing visitors.
>
> A festival leaves its mark when its effects continue beyond the event itself. The town library may see more books checked out in the following months, schools may prepare for author visits by reading the authors’ books, and book clubs may form. For this reason, towns would do well to count these slower effects alongside attendance figures. They reveal whether the festival changed the habits of the people who live there.

In the passage, the expression “A festival leaves its mark” means that the festival

- leaves trash in the squares
- is commemorated with a plaque
- produces effects that last over time
- attracts many visitors

## Explanation

The sentence continues with “when its effects continue beyond the event itself” and lists effects in the following months, such as library loans and book clubs. *Leaving its mark* therefore has the **figurative** meaning of producing lasting effects. The trash and the plaque take the expression literally. The number of visitors is precisely the criterion that the author distinguishes from lasting effects.
