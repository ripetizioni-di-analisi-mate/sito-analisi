Read the following passage.

> Each year, some of the fruit and vegetables harvested never reaches supermarket shelves because the produce is irregularly shaped, has blemished skin, or falls outside the required size range. The taste and nutritional properties are the same, but the produce is either sold to juice manufacturers at a very low price or left in the fields.
>
> One farm began selling this fruit directly to consumers in mixed boxes delivered every week, at a $30\%$ discount from the price of its top-grade fruit. At first, the farm worried that customers would see it as lower in quality. The opposite happened. Many subscribers particularly enjoy the surprise of receiving different produce each week. They say they waste less because the box encourages them to cook what they have at home.

According to the passage, why do many subscribers say they waste less food?

- Because irregularly shaped fruit keeps longer
- Because the box encourages them to cook what they have at home
- Because the box contains less fruit than they used to buy
- Because the $30\%$ discount leads them to buy only what they eat

## Explanation

The last sentence says that many subscribers report wasting less “because the box encourages them to cook what they have at home.” The correct answer restates the cause introduced by *because*. A longer shelf life may sound plausible, but the passage does not discuss it, so it is a **true but unstated** distractor. The passage does not compare the amount of fruit in the box with previous purchases. The $30\%$ discount is mentioned only as a price difference from top-grade fruit and is not linked to waste.
