> At a school tournament each win earned three points, each draw one and each loss none. The school paper reported, “With six points from three games the team won twice.” A reader suggested changing “won” to “might have won”, arguing that points never establish results with certainty. Before accepting her correction the editor listed the possibilities with at most one win. Even a win and two draws would give only five points. Three wins, on the other hand, would give nine. The editor left the sentence as it was. The scoring rules and the stated number of games were available to every reader.

What must the remaining game have been, besides the two wins?

- A loss
- A game whose result cannot be determined
- A win
- A draw

## Explanation

Two wins already supply all six reported points. To keep that total, the remaining game must be worth zero. The rules give zero only for a loss.
