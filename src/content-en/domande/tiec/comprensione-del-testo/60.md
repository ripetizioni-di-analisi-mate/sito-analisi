> In the letter donating his notes to a library, a traveller wrote that they would help “those looking for lost paths”. Beside each route he wrote the names of shepherds who had shown him a crossing and often the place name as they pronounced it in their dialect. Many years later all these crossings appeared on an updated map. The librarian nevertheless refused to file the notes among the duplicates. The map now sufficed for finding one's way. For learning how the paths had been known and named before the map, the notebooks still offered something the new symbols did not preserve.

For which purpose do the notebooks remain particularly suitable?

- Finding the current condition of every path
- Studying local names associated with the crossings
- Finding a route missing from the new map
- Calculating the cost of printing the map

## Explanation

The traveller recorded names and pronunciations supplied by shepherds. The new map shows the routes without necessarily preserving those voices. The current condition of the paths is outside the scope of the old notes.
