Read the following passage.

> In one midsize city, single-person households rose from $24\%$ to $36\%$ of all households over twenty years. These include young people working far from home, people who have separated from their partners, and, most of all, older adults who now live alone.
>
> The housing market has been slow to adapt. Most apartments for sale still have three or four rooms because they were built when families with children were the norm. Two-room apartments, meanwhile, are scarce and command the city’s highest price per square meter. Some developers have begun dividing large older apartments into two smaller units. Doing so requires city approval and expensive work, such as installing a second electrical system and adding a second entrance. It pays off only in buildings where demand for two-room apartments is high.

According to the passage, why do most apartments for sale have three or four rooms?

- Because developers make more money selling large homes
- Because the city does not approve the construction of two-room apartments
- Because people who live alone prefer large homes
- Because they were built when families with children were the norm

## Explanation

The second paragraph says that most apartments for sale have three or four rooms “because they were built when families with children were the norm.” The correct answer restates this reason, introduced by *because*. The claim about developers’ profits may sound plausible, but the passage does not discuss their profits, so this is a **true but unstated** distractor. The passage mentions city approval only in connection with dividing large apartments. It does not say that the city prohibits two-room apartments. The preferences of single-person households are not given.
