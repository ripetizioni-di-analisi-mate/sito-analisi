> In a carpenter's notebook the measurements of a cupboard are followed by the note “a finger taken off for the wall”. His grandson, making a replacement shelf, initially imagines a rough hand measurement. On entering the room he sees that the wall protrudes exactly at shelf height. On the old shelf a narrow strip is missing near the corner but does not extend along the whole edge. He decides to measure the shape of the projection on site. Subtracting the same width along the entire shelf would solve the local problem but leave unnecessary space along the rest of the edge.

Which detail shows that a local adjustment was involved?

- The shelf needs to be made again
- The notebook mentions a finger
- The missing strip ends near the corner of the shelf
- The measurements were written down by the grandfather

## Explanation

The extent of the cut distinguishes a notch from a uniform reduction. The wall protrudes at precisely the same height. The expression “a finger” alone suggests at most an approximate width.
