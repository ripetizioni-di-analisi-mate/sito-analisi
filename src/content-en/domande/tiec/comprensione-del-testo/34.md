Read the following passage.

> Many households pour leftover frying oil or oil from cans of tuna down the sink. Once in the pipes, the oil cools and combines with other waste, forming deposits that narrow the drains. It also makes water more expensive to treat at wastewater plants. Yet used oil has value. If collected separately, it can be turned into biofuel or lubricants.
>
> In a city of $20\,000$ residents, the waste management company gave every household a three-liter container and set up collection bins near supermarkets. It collected $14\,000$ liters in the first year. According to the company’s technicians, the city generates at least three times that much used oil each year, so most of it still goes uncollected.

Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the passage?

- Oil poured down the sink makes water more expensive to treat
- Every household in the city received a container for used oil
- More than half of the used oil generated in the city goes uncollected
- The oil collected in the city is turned into biofuel

## Explanation

The passage says that used oil collected separately *can* be turned into biofuel or lubricants, but it does not say what happens to the oil collected in this particular city. The statement about biofuel therefore turns a possibility into a fact and has no line to support it. The other three statements have support. The first paragraph discusses the costs at wastewater plants, and the second mentions the container given to every household. Since at least three times as much oil is generated as is collected, at most one third is collected and most goes uncollected, as the last sentence says.
