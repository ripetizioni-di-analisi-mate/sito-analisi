> The label beside a portrait identifies a woman as “maker of the copy”. A visitor protests, having always understood that the original painting was made by a much earlier painter. The attendant shows him the museum register. It lists an old painting kept elsewhere and a replica commissioned years later, the very object hanging in the room. As the register note explains, the label attributes the making of what the visitor sees, while its next line identifies the source model. Reading the whole notice again, the visitor realises that he had regarded the two lines as rival proposals for the name of a single artist.

What does “the very object hanging in the room” refer to?

- The label read by the visitor
- The line naming the maker of the model
- The old work kept elsewhere
- The replica commissioned years later

## Explanation

The register distinguishes a distant original from a replica. The phrase picks up the replica just named and identifies it with the painting in the room. The original is explicitly located elsewhere.
