Read the following passage.

> Many households pour leftover frying oil or oil from cans of tuna down the sink. Once in the pipes, the oil cools and combines with other waste, forming deposits that narrow the drains. It also makes water more expensive to treat at wastewater plants. Yet used oil has value. If collected separately, it can be turned into biofuel or lubricants.
>
> In a city of $20\,000$ residents, the waste management company gave every household a three-liter container and set up collection bins near supermarkets. It collected $14\,000$ liters in the first year. According to the company’s technicians, the city generates at least three times that much used oil each year, so most of it still goes uncollected.

What is the main purpose of the passage?

- To criticize households that pour oil down the sink
- To compare biofuels with conventional fuels
- To describe how a wastewater treatment plant works
- To explain why it is worthwhile to collect used oil separately and report the results of a city collection program

## Explanation

The first paragraph explains the damage done by pouring oil down drains and the value of collected oil. The second reports how a city organized a collection program and how much oil it collected. The **purpose** is therefore to inform readers about the problem and report the results of the collection. The passage describes household behavior without passing judgment, so its purpose is not to criticize the households. The passage does not discuss conventional fuels or how treatment plants work. It mentions the plants only in connection with treatment costs.
