Read the following passage.

> In one midsize city, single-person households rose from $24\%$ to $36\%$ of all households over twenty years. These include young people working far from home, people who have separated from their partners, and, most of all, older adults who now live alone.
>
> The housing market has been slow to adapt. Most apartments for sale still have three or four rooms because they were built when families with children were the norm. Two-room apartments, meanwhile, are scarce and command the city’s highest price per square meter. Some developers have begun dividing large older apartments into two smaller units. Doing so requires city approval and expensive work, such as installing a second electrical system and adding a second entrance. It pays off only in buildings where demand for two-room apartments is high.

Which of the following conclusions is NOT supported by the passage?

- Fewer than two-thirds of the city’s households now have two or more members
- Many apartments for sale were built for families with children
- Dividing an apartment requires city approval
- In this city, a two-room apartment costs more than a four-room apartment

## Explanation

The passage says two-room apartments have the city’s highest price **per square meter**. A four-room apartment, however, has many more square meters, so it can cost more in total even at a lower price per square meter. The conclusion that the two-room apartment costs more is therefore unsupported. The other statements follow from the text. Single-person households account for $36\%$, leaving $64\%$, less than two-thirds, for other households. Large apartments were built when families with children were the norm, and the final sentence mentions city approval.
