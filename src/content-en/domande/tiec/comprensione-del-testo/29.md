> During the restoration of a stage curtain technicians found two rows of small holes along its edge. The theatre seamstress said that one row must have held a lining. Looking at an old production photograph, the director noticed a light strip at the base of the curtain and proposed replacing it. The photograph, however, showed only the side facing the audience, whereas the lining marks were on the back. The seamstress suggested recording the two observations separately until evidence linking them turned up. Two traces consistent with the same reconstruction could still come from alterations made at quite different times.

Which new evidence would directly address the seamstress's reservation?

- The exact width of the stage today
- A light-coloured fabric sample used in a different theatre
- A work note describing the strip being attached with the lining stitches
- A second photograph showing only the audience during the show

## Explanation

The seamstress wants a documented link between the visible strip and the stitching on the back. The work note would describe a single alteration joining them. The other information still allows for two different dates.
