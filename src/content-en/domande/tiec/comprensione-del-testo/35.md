> A young cook was transcribing the family recipe book. Beside one cake she found the words “as for the bread, wait for it to come back”. None of her relatives remembered what was supposed to come back. On a page about bread, however, her grandmother advised pressing the dough gently with a finger and watching for the surface to recover its shape. The cook put this explanation in a note, marking it as her interpretation. She kept the original sentence in the recipe so that a future reader could distinguish the grandmother's words from the reading proposed during her own work of transcription.

Why is the reference to the bread page relevant?

- It demonstrates that a relative recognised the phrase
- The cake instruction explicitly refers to something done for bread
- It establishes that the two doughs contain identical ingredients
- It proves that the grandmother made bread and cake on the same day

## Explanation

The words “as for the bread” justify comparing the pages. The gesture described elsewhere suggests a plausible meaning of “come back”. The cook keeps the clue distinct from certainty.
