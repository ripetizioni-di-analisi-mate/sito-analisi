Read the following passage.

> A city extended its $30$-kilometer-per-hour speed limit to almost all residential streets, retaining the $50$ limit only on major avenues. Many drivers feared it would take much longer to cross the city. Measurements from the first year tell a different story. Average travel times rose by less than a minute per trip because, with intersections, traffic lights, and parked cars, actual speeds on residential streets were already close to $30$ kilometers per hour.
>
> That same year, crashes resulting in injuries fell by a fifth. The city government does not attribute the entire decrease to the new limit because it also redesigned many crosswalks during that period. It nevertheless decided to keep the measure and review the figures after three years.

Which of the following conclusions is NOT supported by the passage?

- Before the new limit, speeds on residential streets were already close to $30$ kilometers per hour
- The new speed limit reduced crashes resulting in injuries by a fifth
- The speed limit on major avenues remained $50$ kilometers per hour
- The new limit added little to average travel times

## Explanation

The passage says that crashes resulting in injuries fell by a fifth in the same year the new limit took effect. It adds that the city government does not attribute the entire decrease to the limit because many crosswalks were redesigned during the same period. Attributing the entire decrease to the limit would assert a causal relationship the text does not establish. Each of the other three conclusions has a line to support it. Actual speeds “were already close to $30$ kilometers per hour,” the $50$ limit was retained “on major avenues,” and travel times rose by “less than a minute per trip.”
