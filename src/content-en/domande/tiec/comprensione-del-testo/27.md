Read the following passage.

> On summer evenings, some squares in the historic center fill with young people drinking and talking outside bars until late at night. For bar owners, these are the most profitable hours of the week. For people living above the bars, they are hours of lost sleep.
>
> Last year, the city ordered the bars to close at one in the morning. Noise in the square decreased, but some young people moved into side streets, where they stayed with drinks bought at all-night stores. This year, the city government changed its approach. Bars may stay open until two, but they must pay for two attendants who ask customers not to linger in the street. After midnight, alcohol sales for takeout are prohibited.

What can be inferred from the passage about the effect of last year’s rule?

- residents of the side streets asked for the bars to reopen
- bars made more money than the year before
- the noise problem shifted partly from the square to the side streets
- noise disappeared from the historic center

## Explanation

The passage says that when bars had to close at one, noise in the square decreased, but some young people moved into the side streets and stayed there. It follows that the problem partly shifted elsewhere, so the inference is **supported**. The claim that noise disappeared is an exaggeration. Noise in the square only decreased, and the young people stayed in the city center. The passage does not mention requests by residents of the side streets or the bars’ earnings.
