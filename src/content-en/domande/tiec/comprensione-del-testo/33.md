Read the following passage.

> An accounting firm with twelve employees experienced a data theft last year. Someone guessed an employee’s email password and downloaded documents belonging to several clients. Since then, the firm has used two-factor authentication. A password alone no longer gives employees access to their email. The system sends a code to the employee’s phone. It is valid for a few minutes and must be entered right after the password. Someone who steals a password therefore cannot use it without also having the phone.
>
> Employees complained during the first few weeks because it took a few extra steps to sign in. The owner then allowed the system to remember office computers for thirty days, so employees are asked for a code only the first time they sign in that month or when they sign in from a new device.

In the passage, the expression “Since then” refers to a period beginning with

- the data theft at the firm
- the opening of the firm
- the employees’ complaints
- the decision to have the system remember office computers

## Explanation

An expression such as *since then* refers back to a time mentioned just before it. The preceding sentence describes last year’s data theft, so the firm has used two-factor authentication since that theft. The employees’ complaints and the owner’s decision appear later, in the second paragraph, after the new system was introduced. The passage does not mention the opening of the firm.
