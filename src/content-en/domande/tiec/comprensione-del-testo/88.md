Read the following passage.

> Ten walking groups have formed in a health district in a lowland area. Participants, most of them over sixty, meet twice a week for an hour’s walk along set routes with a trained volunteer. There is no registration or fee, and the slowest walkers set the pace. Family doctors in the district suggest the groups to patients who get little exercise because regular, moderate activity helps control blood pressure and blood glucose.
>
> After a year, organizers asked participants why they kept coming. Few mentioned health. Most said the walks had become a regular chance to meet people they knew and that without the group they would have spent those afternoons at home.

In the expression “regular, moderate activity,” what does the adjective “moderate” mean?

- led by a moderator
- lasting only a short time
- of medium intensity, requiring no excessive effort
- done infrequently

## Explanation

The activity described is an hour-long walk at the pace set by the slowest participants. In this **context**, *moderate* refers to an activity of limited intensity that does not require excessive effort. The moderator suggests another use of the word associated with debates. An hour’s walk is not a brief activity, and infrequent activity contradicts both the adjective *regular* and the twice-weekly schedule.
