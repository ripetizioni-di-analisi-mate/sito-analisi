> Two sentences appear on the last page of a neighbourhood magazine: “Everyone who sent us a story received a reply. Some replies invited the writer to rewrite the ending.” One reader concludes that all the other stories have already been accepted for publication. The editor reminds him that a reply might also thank the writer and explain a lack of space or a decision to return to the subject later. The sentences were written to reassure authors that every submission had been read. The number of stories to be printed would be announced only after a meeting the following week.

Why can the reader not infer acceptance for stories without a revision request?

- Every story received the same reply
- The editors had already announced how many stories they would print
- Replies other than either revision requests or acceptances were possible
- Authors always had to rewrite the ending before receiving a reply

## Explanation

The reader treats revision and acceptance as the only possibilities. The editor gives examples of other replies. The general assurance concerns reading and responding, not publication.
