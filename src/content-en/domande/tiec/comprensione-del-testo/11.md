Read the following passage.

> Bicycle couriers who deliver food for online platforms are often paid per delivery instead of by the hour. Orders increase on rainy evenings, and couriers can earn as much in a few hours as they would in an entire afternoon of good weather. On quiet afternoons, however, they may wait a long time without an order, and they are not paid for that time. Their hourly earnings therefore vary considerably from day to day, and they have trouble predicting how much they will earn by the end of the month.
>
> Some cities have opened rest areas where couriers can wait for orders under shelter, use a restroom, and charge their phones. The more delicate issues remain unresolved, including accident insurance and minimum pay for each hour a courier is available.

The passage supports which inference about a courier who is paid per delivery?

- working on rainy evenings is prohibited
- monthly earnings are always low
- two shifts of the same length can yield very different earnings
- time spent waiting is paid at the same rate as time spent making deliveries

## Explanation

The passage says couriers are paid per delivery, orders increase on rainy evenings, and couriers spend long stretches of quiet afternoons waiting without pay. Two shifts of equal length can therefore involve very different numbers of deliveries and yield very different earnings. This inference is justified, and the passage confirms it by saying that hourly earnings vary considerably from day to day. The text says that waiting time is unpaid and presents rainy evenings as the most profitable time to work. All it says about monthly earnings is that they are hard to predict.
