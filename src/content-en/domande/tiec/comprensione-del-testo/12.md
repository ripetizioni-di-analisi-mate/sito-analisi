> A canteen committee wanted to find out which side dish diners liked best. For one week it offered carrots and fennel and weighed the leftovers after lunch. The leftover carrots weighed less. The cook pointed out, however, that almost twice as many portions of fennel had been served because it was also available at the sandwich counter that day. A teacher proposed recording both the starting weight served for each dish and the weight left on plates the following week. She also asked staff to weigh separately anything that had never left the kitchen. Food discarded before service cannot express a diner's judgment.

Which comparison implements the teacher's proposal?

- Add kitchen waste to plate leftovers for each dish
- For each dish compare the weight left on plates with the weight served
- Count only completely empty plates after lunch
- Divide all food cooked by the number of staff

## Explanation

The weight served supplies the reference missing from the first comparison. Plate leftovers come from food actually offered to diners. Waste that stayed in the kitchen must be kept separate.
