Read the following passage.

> At some middle schools, teachers spend the last half hour on Fridays reading aloud. The teacher reads a novel, one chapter a week, and students listen without having to take notes or answer questions. The people who proposed the program say that listening to someone read a story well gets students used to long sentences and a richer vocabulary than they use every day. They also say that after the last chapter, many students ask for other books by the same author.
>
> There is no shortage of doubts. Some parents think the half hour should be used for grammar exercises, which would be more useful for tests. Teachers reply that reading does not take time away from grammar because it takes place at a time of the week when students pay little attention to exercises anyway.

According to the passage, who says that listening to someone read a story well gets students used to long sentences and a richer vocabulary?

- The people who proposed the program
- The author of the passage, who presents the idea as a personal opinion
- Some parents, who would like more grammar exercises
- The students, after hearing the last chapter

## Explanation

The first paragraph says, “The people who proposed the program say that listening to someone read a story well gets students used to long sentences,” so the idea belongs to the program’s proponents. The author reports their view with the verb *say* and attributes it to others instead of presenting it as a personal opinion. The parents express a reservation about the program because they would prefer the half hour to be used for grammar. Of the students, the passage says only that many ask for other books by the same author.
