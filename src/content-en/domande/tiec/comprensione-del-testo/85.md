> While editing a neighbourhood history, a contributor proposes calling the haberdashery in the oldest surviving town hall register “the first shop”. The author prefers “the first shop documented in this collection”. He explains that the register begins on page twelve and that some years are missing from the numbering. The contributor asks whether he therefore believes an earlier business definitely existed. He answers that he would choose the same wording even if he knew nothing of other shops. His adjective concerns the limits of the material he can show his reader, not an opinion about when the very first business actually opened.

If proof of an earlier shop emerged in a different collection tomorrow, which statement about the original collection could remain true?

- The haberdashery is the first shop attested in its documents
- The missing interval covers no years of commercial activity
- The town hall has kept every register that ever existed
- No shop preceded the haberdashery in the neighbourhood

## Explanation

The discovery would change what is known about the neighbourhood's actual history. It would not automatically add a document to the original collection. The author's qualification limits his claim precisely to that collection.
