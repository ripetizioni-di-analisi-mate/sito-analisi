> Residents call a lane connecting the theatre and the square “the shortcut”. A guide for newcomers recommends it as the fastest way to the square from anywhere in the district. The theatre newsagent objects. For someone leaving the theatre the lane saves a long detour. Someone coming down from the hill, however, reaches the square before the entrance to the lane and would have to walk past it to follow the advice. A later guide keeps the traditional name on the map but revises the sentence. A name based on the experience of people starting from a particular place can circulate even where that experience differs.

What part does the example of someone coming down from the hill play?

- It provides a counterexample to the claim that the advice works from every starting point
- It shows the lane is useless even for people leaving the theatre
- It explains why the theatre was built near the square
- It shows the traditional name must be erased from the map

## Explanation

The original advice contains the universal expression “from anywhere”. A single starting point from which the route is longer refutes it. Its benefit for those leaving the theatre remains.
