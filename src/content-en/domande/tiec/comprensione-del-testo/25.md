Read the following passage.

> At some middle schools, teachers spend the last half hour on Fridays reading aloud. The teacher reads a novel, one chapter a week, and students listen without having to take notes or answer questions. The people who proposed the program say that listening to someone read a story well gets students used to long sentences and a richer vocabulary than they use every day. They also say that after the last chapter, many students ask for other books by the same author.
>
> There is no shortage of doubts. Some parents think the half hour should be used for grammar exercises, which would be more useful for tests. Teachers reply that reading does not take time away from grammar because it takes place at a time of the week when students pay little attention to exercises anyway.

How do teachers respond to parents who would like the half hour to be used for grammar exercises?

- Reading takes place at a time of the week when students pay little attention to exercises anyway
- Grammar is less useful than reading for tests
- Many students ask for other books by the same author
- Students can do grammar exercises at home

## Explanation

The last sentence says that in the teachers’ view, reading does not take time away from grammar “because it takes place at a time of the week when students pay little attention to exercises anyway.” The correct answer restates this reason. The point about students asking for other books is an argument made by the people who proposed the program, quoted in the first paragraph. It is not the teachers’ reply to the parents. The passage does not compare the usefulness of grammar and reading for tests or discuss doing exercises at home.
