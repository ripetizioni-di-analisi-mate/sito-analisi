> “It's not a kind word,” says the elderly character in a story, looking at a “demolition” sign on the house next door. Her grandson thinks she wants the sign changed to make it less harsh. Instead she gets a pencil and sketches the front of the house in her notebook, marking the kitchen window and the spot where people used to buy a paper. She sends the builders no complaint. On the last page she explains that her problem with the word is how little it tells of what came before demolition. It is enough to announce the work on a sign. Remembering a house in a notebook takes many more words.

Which title best reflects the central contrast in the story?

- How to stop demolition with a protest
- A word for the building site, a notebook for the house
- Why signs must include floor plans
- The newsagent who prevented the work

## Explanation

The sign has the practical job of announcing work. The notebook preserves details and memories that a single word cannot contain. No action is taken to stop the work.
