Read the following passage.

> In many offices, the workday continues after employees leave. Messages and emails arrive in the evening and on weekends, and people who do not reply worry about seeming uncooperative. Some companies have established a rule that employees are not expected to reply outside working hours.
>
> This rule is useful, but it risks existing only on paper if managers keep writing at ten at night. An employee who receives a message from the boss is unlikely to leave it unanswered, whatever the rules say. It would be more effective to focus on the senders. Some email programs already let people write a message in the evening and schedule it to be sent the next morning. If managers were the first to adopt this habit, the rule would stand a better chance of being followed.

Which position does the author of the passage support?

- Companies should ban the use of email outside working hours
- The rule allowing employees to leave messages unanswered outside working hours is useless
- Employees should reply to their bosses even in the evening
- To give employees the freedom to leave messages unanswered outside working hours, companies need to address the behavior of the senders, starting with managers

## Explanation

The **thesis** appears in the conditional statements “It would be more effective to focus on the senders” and “If managers were the first to adopt this habit, the rule would stand a better chance of being followed.” The author proposes addressing the behavior of the people writing the messages, starting with managers. The rule is described as *useful*, so the claim that it is useless contradicts the text. The author proposes scheduling messages for later delivery, not banning email. The claim that employees should reply in the evening reverses the author’s position.
