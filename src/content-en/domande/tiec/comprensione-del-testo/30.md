> When a bell was installed in a potter's workshop, the apprentice assumed it would announce customers. The door, however, acquired neither wires nor springs. The master attached a ribbon instead to the table where freshly painted cups waited to be put away. Whoever took the last cup pulled the ribbon, ringing the bell in the room immediately next door. A colleague could then clear another shelf without coming in to check each time. In a later account the apprentice called the sound “a question saved”. Customers, he recalled, never had to knock any louder to be heard by the two craftspeople.

Which question did the bell make unnecessary?

- Has another customer arrived at the door?
- Which cup still needs decorating?
- Who attached the ribbon to the table?
- Have all the cups been taken off the table?

## Explanation

The bell rings when the final cup is taken. The colleague receives information previously obtained by entering the room to check. The apprentice's initial idea about customers is contradicted by the mechanism.
