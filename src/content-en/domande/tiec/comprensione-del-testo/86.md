> Each time he took an order, a waiter wrote the table number on his pad and drew a small square beside the name of the dish. Only when the kitchen handed it over did he fill the square. During a busy shift a colleague found two empty squares beside the same soup. She concluded that the kitchen had forgotten two dishes. The waiter showed her that one order had been taken a minute ago and the other twenty minutes ago. An empty square identified what was still to reach the table, but by itself it did not distinguish normal waiting time from a delay.

What do the two empty squares show for certain under the waiter's convention?

- That the kitchen has not yet handed over either of the two soups ordered
- That both soups have been forgotten
- That the two customers ordered at the same time
- That the kitchen has not started preparing either soup

## Explanation

The square is filled when the kitchen hands over the dish. An empty square records the absence of that step and does not establish its cause. Assessing delay also requires the order time and an expected wait.
