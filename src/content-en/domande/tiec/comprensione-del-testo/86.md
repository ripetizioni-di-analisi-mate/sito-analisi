Read the following passage.

> In many villages of a few hundred residents, the last grocery store has closed, and people must drive to buy bread. In one mountain town, the owner of the last remaining general store decided to hold out by adding services. In addition to buying groceries, customers can now pick up packages ordered online, pay bills, add credit to their phones, and drop off prescriptions for medicines that a pharmacy in the valley delivers the next day.
>
> None of these services brings in much money on its own. Together, however, they bring in people who stop by for a package and leave with bread and a newspaper. The owner says revenue is back to where it was ten years ago. The town has also lowered the rent on her store, which occupies a publicly owned building.

In the sentence “Together, however, they bring in people who stop by for a package and leave with bread and a newspaper,” what does the connective “however” contrast?

- the small earnings from each service and the effect of the combined services on purchases
- groceries and newspapers
- the closing of the other stores and the opening of the general store
- packages ordered online and medicines

## Explanation

The preceding sentence says no service brings in much money on its own. The sentence with *however* goes in the other direction, explaining that the services together attract customers who also buy other things. The connective therefore contrasts the small earnings from a single service with the combined effect. The words *on its own* and *together* mark the two sides. Groceries and newspapers are both purchases. The closing of the other stores appears in the first paragraph. Packages and medicines are two services in the same list.
