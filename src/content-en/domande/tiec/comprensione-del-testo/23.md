> The head of a sewing workshop put a sheet beside the hemming machine. When changing a spool, workers had to record the colour and draw a line below the previous entry. After a month lines also appeared where two consecutive spools were both black. An apprentice wanted to erase those marks because the colour had not changed. The supervisor showed him the purchasing register. A new entry corresponded to a spool started and helped estimate how many spools to keep in stock. Colour helped staff reorder the right thread. For the first question it was enough to count the entries. For the second one had to read them.

What is the “first question” referred to at the end of the passage?

- How much thread was left on each spool?
- How many spools have been started?
- Who changed the last spool?
- Which colour was used most frequently?

## Explanation

The preceding account links every entry to a spool started. Counting the entries is sufficient to count the spools. Distinguishing colours requires reading what the entries actually say.
