Read the following passage.

> In a hilly area, the local health authority offers some follow-up appointments by video to patients with chronic conditions who live far from hospitals. Patients join from their home computers or tablets. They measure their own blood pressure, weight, and blood glucose using devices supplied by the health authority, and the readings reach the doctor before the call. A video visit replaces the periodic checkup, while the first visit and tests requiring hospital equipment still take place in person.
>
> For patients, the main benefit is avoiding trips that can take half a day by public transportation. The service ran into an unexpected obstacle because many older patients did not know how to use a tablet. The health authority therefore enlisted pharmacies in the local towns, where a pharmacist helps patients connect from a private room.

In the passage, a “periodic checkup” is a checkup that

- takes place only once, at the onset of the illness
- recurs at regular intervals
- is published in a newspaper
- concerns a brief period of acute illness

## Explanation

The passage distinguishes between the periodic checkup, which a video visit replaces, and the first visit, which still takes place in person. A periodic checkup is therefore one that a patient with a chronic condition has at regular intervals. A checkup done only once at the beginning corresponds to the first visit. The meaning associated with publications is another use of the Italian word *periodico*, and the conditions discussed in the passage are chronic.
