Read the following passage.

> A university city of $150\,000$ residents has about $30\,000$ students, nearly half of whom come from other provinces. University residences offer $1500$ beds, allocated on the basis of income and academic merit. All other students from out of town look for rooms on the private market. Over the past five years, the average monthly rent for a single room has risen from $300$ to $420$ euros. Many students have chosen to live in nearby towns, spending an hour a day on the train.
>
> The university has proposed that the city convert a former barracks into a residence with $600$ beds. The project will take at least four years. In the meantime, the university has started a service that matches students with older adults who live alone and have a spare room. In return for the room, students pay a low rent and provide some company.

Based on the figures in the passage, the beds in university residences are enough for approximately what fraction of the students from out of town?

- One twentieth
- One tenth
- One hundredth
- One half

## Explanation

There are about $30\,000$ students, and nearly half come from other provinces, so about $15\,000$ are from out of town. There are $1500$ beds, enough for
$$
\frac{1500}{15\,000} = \frac{1}{10}
$$
of the students from out of town. You get one twentieth if you divide by total enrollment and one hundredth if you divide by the city’s population. One half is the fraction of all enrolled students who are from out of town, which is a different figure.
