> The chess club advertised its courtyard tournament as “also open to beginners”. It received entries from people who knew how the pieces moved and enquiries from others asking whether someone would teach the rules before play. The president noticed the difference when reading the requests. He did not want to reserve the tournament for experts, but games would occupy the tables from the very first minute. He added a line inviting anyone who did not know the moves to a lesson on the Saturday before the tournament. The lesson remained optional and could also be attended by people who did not enter the tournament.

Which expectation did the president want to correct?

- That teaching the moves was part of the start of the tournament
- That experienced players could enter the event
- That one could attend the lesson without playing in the tournament
- That games would start on the tables immediately

## Explanation

Openness to beginners concerned eligibility for entry. Some enquiries took it as a promise of instruction before play. The new notice gave a separate date for learning the moves.
