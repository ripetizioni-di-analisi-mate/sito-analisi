> A stock card describes every parcel using three details in order: shelving unit, shelf level and position from the left. For example “B, second, three” means unit B, the second shelf and the third parcel from the left. While moving stock, two workers stand facing one another on opposite sides of an open shelving unit. They read the same card but point to different parcels. The supervisor adds an arrow on the floor and the instruction “read from the entrance aisle”. The cards, she explains, still work in their usual order. Only a shared reference for one of the three details was missing.

Which detail gains an unambiguous meaning from the arrow?

- The order of the three fields on the card
- The shelf level
- The position from the left
- The unit's letter

## Explanation

People facing one another have opposite left-right orientations. Specifying the aisle establishes the side from which to look. The arrow changes neither letters nor levels nor the order of the fields.
