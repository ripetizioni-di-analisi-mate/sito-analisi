> In the introduction to a collection of letters the editor says she retained spelling errors but expanded some abbreviations. Where the manuscript reads “Mr”, the edition prints “Mister”. Where a word has one consonant although current usage expects two, the edition leaves it alone. A reader asks why fidelity to the document applies in only one case. The editor answers that the abbreviations, all listed at the beginning, were conventions familiar to the correspondents but often obscure to readers today. Errors, on the other hand, might reveal something about the identity of the particular person who had written an unsigned letter.

What information could be lost if all spellings were standardised?

- The meaning of every listed abbreviation
- A possible clue to the authorship of an unsigned letter
- The printing date of the modern edition
- The number of letters in the collection

## Explanation

The editor treats individual spellings as clues to who wrote a letter. Correcting all of them would erase those very differences. The opening list separately preserves a record of the expanded abbreviations.
