> A notebook manufacturer offered a stationery shop a discount for each box returned intact. The owner recorded returns on a sheet pinned near the door. After a month she congratulated the Saturday assistant, whose name appeared more often than anyone else's. He pointed out that the manufacturer's van always called on Saturdays. The name on the sheet belonged to whoever handed the box to the driver, not to whoever had emptied and saved it. The following month the owner continued counting returns to check the discount but stopped using the list to compare the assistants' contributions to the shop's box-saving effort.

For which use is the sheet still suitable?

- Comparing Saturday sales with sales on other days
- Checking how many returned boxes qualify for the discount
- Measuring how fast people empty boxes
- Determining who takes the best care of packaging

## Explanation

Each entry represents a box handed over. The count therefore records the condition on which the discount depends. The identity of the person making the handover does not measure the earlier work of colleagues.
