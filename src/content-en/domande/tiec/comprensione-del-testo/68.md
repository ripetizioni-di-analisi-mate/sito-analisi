Read the following passage.

> A city extended its $30$-kilometer-per-hour speed limit to almost all residential streets, retaining the $50$ limit only on major avenues. Many drivers feared it would take much longer to cross the city. Measurements from the first year tell a different story. Average travel times rose by less than a minute per trip because, with intersections, traffic lights, and parked cars, actual speeds on residential streets were already close to $30$ kilometers per hour.
>
> That same year, crashes resulting in injuries fell by a fifth. The city government does not attribute the entire decrease to the new limit because it also redesigned many crosswalks during that period. It nevertheless decided to keep the measure and review the figures after three years.

In the sentence “The city government does not attribute the entire decrease to the new limit because it also redesigned many crosswalks during that period,” what does “because” introduce?

- the reason the city government does not attribute the entire decrease to the new limit
- a consequence of the decrease in crashes
- a fact that contrasts with the first part of the sentence
- a second effect of the new speed limit

## Explanation

The word *because*, like *since*, introduces a **cause**. In this sentence, the work on the crosswalks is the reason the city government does not attribute the entire decrease to the speed limit. That work may also have reduced crashes. The work did not result from the decrease in crashes or run counter to the first part of the sentence. It gives the reason for it. Finally, redesigning the crosswalks was a separate measure and did not result from the speed limit.
