For which values of the parameters $k$ and $h$ does the following system have infinitely many solutions?
$$
\begin{cases}
x + ky = 2 \\
2x + 4y = h
\end{cases}
$$

- $k = 2$ and $h = 4$
- $k = 2$ and $h \neq 4$
- $k = 4$ and $h = 2$
- $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $h = 4$

## Explanation

The system has infinitely many solutions when the two equations represent the same line, that is, when the second is a multiple of the first. The coefficient of $x$ in the second equation is twice its coefficient in the first, so the other coefficients must also double, giving $4 = 2k$ and $h = 2 \cdot 2$. Thus $k = 2$ and $h = 4$, and the system has infinitely many solutions. With $k = 2$ and $h \neq 4$ the lines are parallel and distinct, so the system has no solution. The answer with $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$ inverts the ratio of the coefficients of $y$.
