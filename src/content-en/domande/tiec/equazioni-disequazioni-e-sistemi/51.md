For which values of $x$ does $2x^2 + 3x - 2 \geq 0$ hold?

- $-2 \leq x \leq \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x \leq -\dfrac{1}{2} \;\vee\; x \geq 2$
- $x \leq -2 \;\vee\; x \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x \leq -4 \;\vee\; x \geq 1$

## Explanation

The corresponding equation has $\Delta = 9 + 16 = 25$, and the **quadratic formula** gives $x = \dfrac{-3 \pm 5}{4}$, or $-2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, so the quadratic is positive outside its roots and zero at the roots. The solutions of the inequality are therefore $x \leq -2 \;\vee\; x \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$. The answer $-2 \leq x \leq \dfrac{1}{2}$ takes values between the roots. The answer with $-4$ and $1$ divides by $2$ instead of $2a = 4$.
