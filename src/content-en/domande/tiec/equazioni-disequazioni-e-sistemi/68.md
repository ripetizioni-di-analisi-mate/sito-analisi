For which real values of $x$ is $\sqrt{\dfrac{x + 3}{x - 1}}$ defined?

- $x \leq -3 \;\vee\; x \geq 1$
- $-3 \leq x < 1$
- $x \leq -3 \;\vee\; x > 1$
- $x > 1$

## Explanation

The fraction must be defined, so $x \neq 1$. The radicand must also be greater than or equal to zero, so solve $\dfrac{x + 3}{x - 1} \geq 0$. The numerator is positive for $x > -3$ and the denominator is positive for $x > 1$. In the **sign chart**, the quotient is positive for $x < -3$ and $x > 1$. Include the zero of the numerator, $-3$, because the radicand there equals $0$. Exclude the zero of the denominator. The expression is therefore defined for $x \leq -3 \;\vee\; x > 1$. The answer with $x \geq 1$ includes a value that makes the denominator zero, and $-3 \leq x < 1$ takes the interval where the radicand is negative.
