In the equation $x^2 - 6x + k = 0$, one root is twice the other. What is $k$?

- $8$
- $9$
- $18$
- $2$

## Explanation

Let the roots be $r$ and $2r$. The formula for the **sum of the roots** gives $r + 2r = 6$, so $r = 2$ and the roots are $2$ and $4$. The formula for their **product** gives $k = r \cdot 2r = 2 \cdot 4 = 8$. As a check, the roots of $x^2 - 6x + 8 = 0$ are $2$ and $4$, and $4$ is twice $2$. The value $9$ makes the roots coincide because $x^2 - 6x + 9 = (x - 3)^2$. The value $18$ takes $r = 3$, half the sum, as the smaller root.
