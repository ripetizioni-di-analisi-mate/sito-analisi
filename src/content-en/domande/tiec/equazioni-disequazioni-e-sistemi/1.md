When solving $\dfrac{x-1}{2}-\dfrac{x+1}{3}=1$, both sides are multiplied by six. Which line is equivalent to the starting equation after that operation?

- $3(x-1)-2(x+1)=1$
- $2(x-1)-3(x+1)=6$
- $3(x-1)-2(x+1)=6$
- $3(x-1)-2x+2=6$

## Explanation

Multiplying the first quotient by six leaves a factor of $3$, and the second a factor of $2$. The minus sign affects both terms of $x+1$, so the brackets must be retained. The right side is multiplied by six as well.
