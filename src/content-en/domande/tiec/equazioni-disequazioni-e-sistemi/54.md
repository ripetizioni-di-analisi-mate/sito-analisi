For which values of $x$ does $x(x - 2) \leq 3$ hold?

- $x \leq 3$
- $-3 \leq x \leq 1$
- $x \leq -1 \;\vee\; x \geq 3$
- $-1 \leq x \leq 3$

## Explanation

The right side is nonzero, so first move all terms to the left to obtain $x^2 - 2x - 3 \leq 0$. The corresponding equation has roots $3$ and $-1$, with sum $2$ and product $-3$. The coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, so the quadratic is negative or zero between the roots, including the endpoints, giving $-1 \leq x \leq 3$. The answer $x \leq 3$ imposes $x \leq 3$ and $x - 2 \leq 3$ on the two factors, a procedure that does not apply when the right side is nonzero. The answer $x \leq -1 \;\vee\; x \geq 3$ takes values outside the roots.
