The graph shows the lines $y = 2x - 3$ and $y = 3 - x$. For which values of $x$ does $y = 2x - 3$ lie above $y = 3 - x$?

::grafico{f="2*x-3;3-x" punti="2,1,pieno" legenda="y = 2x - 3;y = 3 - x" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=6 titolo="The lines intersect at $(2;1)$"}

- $x < 2$
- $x > 1$
- $x > 2$
- $x > \dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

One line lies above the other where its vertical coordinate is larger at the same $x$, so solve $2x - 3 > 3 - x$. Moving terms gives $3x > 6$, or $x > 2$. The number $2$ is the horizontal coordinate of the intersection $(2;1)$. To the right of that point, the graph shows $y = 2x - 3$ above the other line. The answer $x > 1$ uses the vertical coordinate of the intersection instead of the horizontal one. The answer $x > \dfrac{3}{2}$ solves $2x - 3 > 0$, comparing the line with the $x$-axis, and $x < 2$ gives the values where $y = 2x - 3$ lies below the other line.
