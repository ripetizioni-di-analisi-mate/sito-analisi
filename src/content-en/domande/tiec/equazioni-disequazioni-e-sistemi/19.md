Which equation has roots $x = -3$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$?

- $2x^2 - 5x - 3 = 0$
- $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$
- $2x^2 + 5x + 3 = 0$
- $x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$

## Explanation

To form an equation with roots $-3$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$, set the product $(x + 3)(2x - 1)$ equal to zero. Expanding gives $2x^2 - x + 6x - 3 = 2x^2 + 5x - 3$. As a check, the **sum** of the roots is $-\dfrac{5}{2} = -3 + \dfrac{1}{2}$ and their **product** is $-\dfrac{3}{2} = -3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}$. The equation $2x^2 - 5x - 3 = 0$ has roots with the opposite signs, $3$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. For $x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$ the product of the roots is $-3$, so these are not the required roots.
