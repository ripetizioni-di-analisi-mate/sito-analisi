A student divides $(x^2+1)(x-3)\leq0$ by $x^2+1$ without reversing the inequality. Which property makes this valid on the entire real line?

- $x^2+1$ is negative for $x<0$
- $x^2+1$ has a strictly positive minimum
- The factor $x-3$ has one zero
- Every even-degree expression is positive everywhere

## Explanation

The minimum of $x^2+1$ is $1$. The factor never vanishes and stays positive, so division preserves the direction everywhere. The remaining condition is simply $x-3\leq0$.
