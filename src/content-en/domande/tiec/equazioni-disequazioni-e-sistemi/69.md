A student solves $\dfrac{2}{x} > 1$ by multiplying both sides by $x$ and obtains $x < 2$. Which of the following numbers shows that this result is incorrect?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $-1$
- $3$

## Explanation

A number less than $2$ that fails to satisfy the inequality would disprove the student’s result. At $x = -1$ we have $\dfrac{2}{-1} = -2$, which is not greater than $1$, yet $-1 < 2$. Therefore $-1$ belongs to the student’s set without being a solution. The error is multiplying by $x$, which reverses the inequality when $x$ is negative. The correct solutions are $0 < x < 2$. The numbers $1$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$ are solutions and belong to $x < 2$. The number $3$ is neither a solution nor a member of that set. Therefore none of these three numbers reveals the error.
