Let $a$ be a real number. What are the solutions of $x^2 - (a + 1)x + a = 0$ in the unknown $x$?

- $x = -1$ and $x = -a$
- $x = 1$ and $x = -a$
- $x = a$ and $x = a + 1$
- $x = 1$ and $x = a$

## Explanation

The coefficient of $x^2$ is $1$. By the relations between the coefficients and the **sum and product** of the roots, we need two numbers with sum $a + 1$ and product $a$. The numbers $1$ and $a$ satisfy both conditions, and indeed $x^2 - (a + 1)x + a = (x - 1)(x - a)$. The discriminant is $(a + 1)^2 - 4a = (a - 1)^2 \geq 0$, so the roots are real for every $a$ and coincide for $a = 1$. The numbers $-1$ and $-a$ have sum $-(a + 1)$, with the wrong sign. The pair $a$ and $a + 1$ incorrectly uses the coefficient $a + 1$ as a root.
