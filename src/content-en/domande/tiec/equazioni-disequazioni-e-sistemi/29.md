For which value of $k$ are the two roots of $x^2 + (k - 3)x - 5 = 0$ opposites?

- $k = -3$
- $k = 5$
- $k = 3$
- $k = 0$

## Explanation

Two numbers are opposites when their sum is zero. The product of the roots is $-5 < 0$, and the roots are real for every $k$ because the discriminant $(k - 3)^2 + 20$ is positive. By the formula for the **sum of the roots**, $x_1 + x_2 = -(k - 3)$, which is zero for $k = 3$. With $k = 3$ the equation becomes $x^2 = 5$, with opposite roots $\pm\sqrt{5}$. The value $-3$ comes from using the wrong sign when solving $k - 3 = 0$.
