Which inequality has the interval $(-2, 3)$ as its solution set?

- $x^2 + x - 6 < 0$
- $x^2 - x - 6 > 0$
- $x^2 - x + 6 < 0$
- $x^2 - x - 6 < 0$

## Explanation

A quadratic with a positive coefficient of $x^2$ is negative between the roots of the corresponding equation. Therefore, look for a quadratic with zeros $-2$ and $3$ and the sign $<$. The quadratic $(x + 2)(x - 3) = x^2 - x - 6$ has these zeros, so the required inequality is $x^2 - x - 6 < 0$. The inequality $x^2 - x - 6 > 0$ has solutions outside the roots. The quadratic $x^2 + x - 6$ has zeros $2$ and $-3$, while $x^2 - x + 6$ has a negative discriminant, so $x^2 - x + 6 < 0$ has no solutions.
