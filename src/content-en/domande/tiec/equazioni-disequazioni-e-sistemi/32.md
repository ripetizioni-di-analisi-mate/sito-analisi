In $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, the coefficients $a$ and $c$ are nonzero and have opposite signs. Which statement is always true?

- It has no real roots
- It has two distinct real roots
- It has two real roots only if $b \neq 0$
- It has two positive real roots

## Explanation

If $a$ and $c$ have opposite signs, the product $ac$ is negative, so $-4ac$ is positive. The **discriminant** $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ is therefore the sum of $b^2 \geq 0$ and a positive number. Hence $\Delta > 0$ for every value of $b$, even for $b = 0$. The equation thus has two distinct real roots. Their product $\dfrac{c}{a}$ is negative, so one is positive and the other is negative. The statement that both roots are positive is therefore false.
