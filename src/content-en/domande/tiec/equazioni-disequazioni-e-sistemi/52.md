The inequality $9x^2 - 6x + 1 > 0$ holds for which values of $x$?

- For every real $x$
- Only for $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$
- For $x > \dfrac{1}{3}$
- For every $x \neq \dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

The quadratic is the square of the binomial $3x - 1$, namely $9x^2 - 6x + 1 = (3x - 1)^2$, and its discriminant is zero. A square is positive except where its base equals zero, which happens at $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$. The inequality is strict, so it holds **for every** $x \neq \dfrac{1}{3}$. The answer “for every real $x$” includes the value where the quadratic is zero. The answer “only for $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$” solves $(3x - 1)^2 \leq 0$.
