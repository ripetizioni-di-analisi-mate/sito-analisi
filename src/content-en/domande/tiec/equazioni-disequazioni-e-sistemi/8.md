With simple interest, the final balance is $M = C(1 + it)$, where the principal satisfies $C > 0$, the annual interest rate satisfies $i > 0$, and $t$ is the number of years. Which expression gives $t$ in terms of $M$, $C$, and $i$?

- $t = \dfrac{M}{Ci} - 1$
- $t = \dfrac{M - C}{i}$
- $t = \dfrac{M - C}{Ci}$
- $t = \dfrac{M - 1}{Ci}$

## Explanation

The formula is a linear equation in $t$, with both $C$ and $i$ nonzero. Dividing both sides by $C$ gives $\dfrac{M}{C} = 1 + it$, so $it = \dfrac{M}{C} - 1 = \dfrac{M - C}{C}$. Dividing by $i$ gives $t = \dfrac{M - C}{Ci}$. The expression $\dfrac{M}{Ci} - 1$ divides only the first term by $i$, while $\dfrac{M - C}{i}$ omits division by $C$.
