What are the solutions of $\dfrac{x + 1}{x} = \dfrac{2x}{x + 1}$?

- $x = 1 \pm \sqrt{2}$
- $x = -1 \pm \sqrt{2}$
- Only $x = 1$
- $x = 1 \pm \sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

The domain restrictions are $x \neq 0$ and $x \neq -1$. Multiplying both sides by $x(x + 1)$ gives $(x + 1)^2 = 2x^2$, or $x^2 + 2x + 1 = 2x^2$, so $x^2 - 2x - 1 = 0$. The **quadratic formula for an even linear coefficient** gives $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = 1 + 1 = 2$ and therefore $x = 1 \pm \sqrt{2}$. Neither value makes a denominator zero. The answer “only $x = 1$” comes from $(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 1$, omitting the middle term of the square. The answer $-1 \pm \sqrt{2}$ uses the wrong sign for $\dfrac{b}{2}$.
