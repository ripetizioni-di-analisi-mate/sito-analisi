What are the solutions of $\dfrac{x^2}{2} - \dfrac{x}{3} = \dfrac{1}{6}$?

- $x = -1$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$
- $x = 1$ and $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $x = 2$ and $x = -\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $x = 3$ and $x = -1$

## Explanation

Multiplying both sides by $6$ clears the denominators and gives $3x^2 - 2x = 1$, or $3x^2 - 2x - 1 = 0$. The **quadratic formula for an even linear coefficient** gives $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = 1 + 3 = 4$, so $x = \dfrac{1 \pm 2}{3}$, or $x = 1$ and $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$. The pair $-1$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ has the signs reversed. The pairs $2$ and $-\dfrac{2}{3}$, and $3$ and $-1$, come from $\dfrac{2 \pm 4}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2 \pm 4}{2}$, using the wrong denominator in the quadratic formula.
