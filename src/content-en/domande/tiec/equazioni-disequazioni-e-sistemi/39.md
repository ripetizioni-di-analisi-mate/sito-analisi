What are the solutions of $\dfrac{1}{x - 1} + \dfrac{1}{x + 1} = \dfrac{4}{3}$?

- $x = -2$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- Only $x = 2$
- $x = \dfrac{3}{4}$
- $x = 2$ and $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The domain restrictions are $x \neq 1$ and $x \neq -1$, and the common denominator is $3(x - 1)(x + 1)$. Multiplying by this denominator gives $3(x + 1) + 3(x - 1) = 4(x^2 - 1)$, or $6x = 4x^2 - 4$, so $2x^2 - 3x - 2 = 0$. We have $\Delta = 9 + 16 = 25$ and the **quadratic formula** gives $x = \dfrac{3 \pm 5}{4}$, or $2$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Both satisfy the domain restrictions. The answer $\dfrac{3}{4}$ adds the numerators and the denominators separately, giving $\dfrac{2}{2x}$. The answer “only $x = 2$” discards a valid solution without justification.
