What is the product of the real solutions of the equation $\dfrac{1}{x-1}+\dfrac{1}{x+1}=\dfrac{4}{3}$?

- $-\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$
- $-1$

## Explanation

For $x\ne\pm1$ the sum of the two fractions is $\dfrac{2x}{x^2-1}$, so the equation becomes $6x=4(x^2-1)$, that is $2x^2-3x-2=0$. The discriminant is $9+16=25>0$, so the solutions are real and distinct; they are $2$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, both different from $\pm1$. The product of the solutions of $ax^2+bx+c=0$ is $\dfrac{c}{a}$, so it equals $\dfrac{-2}{2}=-1$.
