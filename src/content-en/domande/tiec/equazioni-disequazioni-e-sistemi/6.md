Which operation, when performed on both sides of an equation in the unknown $x$, always gives an equivalent equation?

- Multiply by $x$
- Divide by $x - 1$
- Square both sides
- Divide by $x^2 + 1$

## Explanation

By the **properties of equality**, both sides can be divided by the same nonzero number. Since $x^2 + 1 \geq 1$ for every $x$, dividing by $x^2 + 1$ always divides by a nonzero number and preserves the solutions. Multiplying by $x$ can introduce the solution $x = 0$ because $x = 2$ becomes $x^2 = 2x$, which also holds for $0$. Dividing by $x - 1$ loses the solution $x = 1$ when it is present. Squaring can introduce solutions because $x = 3$ becomes $x^2 = 9$, which also holds for $-3$.
