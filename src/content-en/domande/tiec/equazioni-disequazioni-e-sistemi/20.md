For which values of $k$ does $x^2 - 4x + k = 0$ have two distinct real roots?

- $k > 4$
- $k \leq 4$
- $k < 16$
- $k < 4$

## Explanation

The roots are real and distinct when the **discriminant** is positive. Since $b = -4$ is even, use $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = 4 - k$. The condition $4 - k > 0$ gives $k < 4$. At $k = 4$ the equation becomes $(x - 2)^2 = 0$, with a double root, so $k \leq 4$ includes a value that must be excluded. The answer $k < 16$ comes from $\Delta = 16 - k$, omitting the factor $4$ in front of $ac$ in the discriminant.
