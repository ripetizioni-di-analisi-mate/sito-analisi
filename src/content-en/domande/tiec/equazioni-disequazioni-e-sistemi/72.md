For which values of $x$ does $\dfrac{x + 2}{x - 1} \geq \dfrac{x}{x - 1}$ hold?

- For every $x \neq 1$
- $x > 1$
- For every real $x$
- $x \geq 1$

## Explanation

Moving all terms to the left gives two fractions with the same denominator, so the inequality becomes $\dfrac{x + 2 - x}{x - 1} \geq 0$, or $\dfrac{2}{x - 1} \geq 0$. The numerator $2$ is positive, so the fraction is positive when $x - 1 > 0$ and is never zero. The solutions are therefore $x > 1$. The answer “for every $x \neq 1$” comes from clearing the denominator to get $2 \geq 0$ without accounting for the sign of $x - 1$. Indeed, at $x = 0$ the left side equals $-2$ and the right side equals $0$, and $-2 \geq 0$ is false.
