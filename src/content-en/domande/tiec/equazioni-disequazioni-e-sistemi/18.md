Let $x_1$ and $x_2$ be the roots of $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$. What is $\dfrac{1}{x_1} + \dfrac{1}{x_2}$?

- $\dfrac{3}{7}$
- $\dfrac{7}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{7}{3}$
- $\dfrac{7}{2}$

## Explanation

Calculating the discriminant gives $\Delta = 49 - 24 = 25 > 0$, so the equation has two real roots. Adding the fractions gives $\dfrac{1}{x_1} + \dfrac{1}{x_2} = \dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{x_1 x_2}$. By the relations between the coefficients and the **sum and product** of the roots, $x_1 + x_2 = \dfrac{7}{2}$ and $x_1 x_2 = \dfrac{3}{2}$, so the result is $\dfrac{7}{3}$. The value $\dfrac{3}{7}$ inverts the ratio, and $\dfrac{7}{2}$ is just the sum of the roots.
