What is the solution of $\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{2x} = \dfrac{1}{4}$?

- $x = \dfrac{4}{3}$
- $x = 6$
- $x = \dfrac{1}{6}$
- $x = 8$

## Explanation

The domain restriction is $x \neq 0$, and the common denominator of all terms is $4x$. Multiplying both sides by $4x$ gives $4 + 2 = x$, so $x = 6$. To check, note that $\dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. The value $\dfrac{4}{3}$ comes from writing the sum $\dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{1}{2x}$ as $\dfrac{1}{3x}$ by adding the denominators. The value $8$ overlooks the factor $2$ in the second denominator.
