For which real values of $x$ is the quadratic $-x^2 + 2x - 3$ negative?

- For no real $x$
- For every real $x$
- For $-1 < x < 3$
- For $x < -3 \;\vee\; x > 1$

## Explanation

Multiplying by $-1$ reverses the inequality and gives $x^2 - 2x + 3 > 0$. For this quadratic, $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = 1 - 3 = -2 < 0$ and the coefficient of $x^2$ is positive. By the rule for the **sign of a quadratic**, it is positive for every $x$. The inequality thus holds for every real $x$. The answer “for no $x$” keeps the direction of the inequality. The interval answers use the roots of different quadratics, $x^2 - 2x - 3$ and $x^2 + 2x - 3$, resulting from a sign error.
