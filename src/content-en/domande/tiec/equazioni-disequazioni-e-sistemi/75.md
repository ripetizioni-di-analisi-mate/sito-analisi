Consider pairs of linear equations, allowing degenerate rows such as $0x+0y=c$. Adding two equations gives $0=0$. A student concludes that the system has infinitely many solutions. Which extra piece of information makes this conclusion valid for two linear equations in $x,y$?

- The first equation represents a line
- At least one constant term is nonzero
- The coefficients are listed in alphabetical order
- Both constant terms are integers

## Explanation

The identity obtained by adding means the second equation is the negative of the first. If the first represents a line, all of its infinitely many points also satisfy the second. Two impossible equations such as $0=1$ and $0=-1$ show why the sum alone is insufficient.
