Increasing the side length of a square by $3$ cm increases its area by $39$ cm². What was the original side length?

- $6.5$ cm
- $5$ cm
- $10$ cm
- $\sqrt{39} - 3$ cm

## Explanation

Let $x$ be the side length in centimeters. The area changes from $x^2$ to $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$, so the increase is $6x + 9$. The $x^2$ terms cancel, so the equation $6x + 9 = 39$ is linear and gives $x = 5$. To check, note that $64 - 25 = 39$. The value $10$ comes from writing the middle term as $3x$, and $6.5$ omits the term $9$. The value $\sqrt{39} - 3$ sets the new area equal to $39$ instead of setting the increase equal to it.
