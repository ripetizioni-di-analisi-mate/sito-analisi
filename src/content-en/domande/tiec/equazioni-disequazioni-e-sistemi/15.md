What are the solutions of $x^2 + 4x - 1 = 0$?

- $x = 2 \pm \sqrt{5}$
- $x = -2 \pm \sqrt{3}$
- $x = -2 \pm \sqrt{5}$
- $x = -4 \pm \sqrt{5}$

## Explanation

The coefficient $b = 4$ is even, so use the **quadratic formula for an even linear coefficient**. We obtain $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = 2^2 - 1 \cdot (-1) = 5$ and therefore $x = \dfrac{-2 \pm \sqrt{5}}{1} = -2 \pm \sqrt{5}$. The answer $2 \pm \sqrt{5}$ omits the minus sign before $\dfrac{b}{2}$. The answer $-2 \pm \sqrt{3}$ comes from $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = 4 - 1$, with the wrong sign for $c$, and $-4 \pm \sqrt{5}$ uses $b$ instead of $\dfrac{b}{2}$ outside the radical.
