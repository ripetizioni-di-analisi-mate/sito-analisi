In the system
$$
\begin{cases}
3x + 2y = 5 \\
4x - 3y = 1
\end{cases}
$$
how can you eliminate $y$ by adding or subtracting the equations?

- Multiply the first equation by $3$ and the second by $2$, then add their corresponding sides
- Multiply the first equation by $3$ and the second by $2$, then subtract their corresponding sides
- Multiply the first equation by $2$ and the second by $3$, then add their corresponding sides
- Multiply the first equation by $4$ and the second by $3$, then subtract their corresponding sides

## Explanation

In **elimination**, the variable to be eliminated must have opposite coefficients if the equations are added or equal coefficients if they are subtracted. Multiplying the first equation by $3$ and the second by $2$ gives $9x + 6y = 15$ and $8x - 6y = 2$. Adding gives $17x = 17$, or $x = 1$. After the same multiplications, subtraction gives $x + 12y = 13$, which still contains $y$. Multiplying the first equation by $2$ and the second by $3$ makes the coefficients of $y$ equal to $4$ and $-9$, which are not opposites. Multiplying the first equation by $4$ and the second by $3$ makes the coefficients of $x$ equal, so subtraction eliminates $x$.
