For which value of $q$ does the line $y = 2x + q$ meet the parabola $y = x^2$ at just one point?

- $q = -1$
- $q = 1$
- $q = -4$
- $q = 0$

## Explanation

The common points are the solutions of the system formed by the two equations. Substitution gives $x^2 = 2x + q$, or $x^2 - 2x - q = 0$. The line and the parabola have just one point in common when this equation has a repeated root, that is, when the **discriminant** is zero. We have $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = 1 + q$, which is zero for $q = -1$. With $q = -1$ the equation becomes $(x - 1)^2 = 0$, and the common point is $(1;1)$. The value $q = -4$ comes from $\Delta = 4 + q$, omitting the factor $4$ in front of $q$.
