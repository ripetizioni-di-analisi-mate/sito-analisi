Let $(x;y)$ be the solution of the system
$$
\begin{cases}
3x - y = 7 \\
x + 2y = 0
\end{cases}
$$
What is $x + y$?

- $3$
- $-1$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{21}{5}$

## Explanation

The second equation gives $x = -2y$. Using **substitution**, the first equation becomes $-6y - y = 7$, or $-7y = 7$. Thus $y = -1$ and $x = -2 \cdot (-1) = 2$, so $x + y = 2 - 1 = 1$. The value $3$ is the difference $x - y$. The value $-1$ comes from the pair $(-2;1)$, obtained by solving the first equation for $y = 3x + 7$ with the wrong sign. The value $\dfrac{21}{5}$ comes from $x = 2y$, losing the sign when solving for $x$.
