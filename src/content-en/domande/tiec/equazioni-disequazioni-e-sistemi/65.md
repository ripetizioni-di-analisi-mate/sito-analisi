Consider the inequalities $\dfrac{x - 2}{x + 1} \geq 0$ and $(x - 2)(x + 1) \geq 0$. Which statement is true?

- They have the same solutions
- Their solution sets differ only at $x = 2$
- Their solution sets differ only at $x = -1$, which satisfies the second inequality but not the first
- They have no solutions in common

## Explanation

A quotient has the same sign as the product of the same factors everywhere it is defined, so the inequalities have the same solutions for $x \neq -1$. The **sign chart** gives $x \leq -1 \;\vee\; x \geq 2$ as the solutions of the second inequality. At $x = -1$ the product equals $0$ while the quotient is undefined, so the solutions of the first inequality are $x < -1 \;\vee\; x \geq 2$. At $x = 2$ both left sides equal $0$, so $2$ satisfies both inequalities.
