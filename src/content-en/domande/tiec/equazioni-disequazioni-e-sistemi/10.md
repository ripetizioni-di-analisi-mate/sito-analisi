When subtracting the squares of $x+3$ and $x-1$, how can the degree of the result be predicted before expanding?

- The bases have equal degree, so their squared difference is zero
- A fourth-degree term survives
- The sum of the bases is always four
- The difference of the bases is constant and their sum is linear

## Explanation

A difference of squares is the product of the difference and the sum of the bases. Here these are $4$ and $2x+2$. Their product is linear, so equating it to a constant produces a linear equation.
