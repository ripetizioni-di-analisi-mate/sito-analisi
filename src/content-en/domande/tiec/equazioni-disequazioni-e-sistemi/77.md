What is the solution of the following system?
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{y}{3} = 3 \\
x - y = 1
\end{cases}
$$

- $(4;3)$
- $(3;4)$
- $(3;2)$
- $(1;0)$

## Explanation

Multiplying the first equation by $6$ clears the denominators and gives $3x + 2y = 18$. The second equation gives $x = y + 1$, and **substitution** gives $3(y + 1) + 2y = 18$, or $5y = 15$. Therefore $y = 3$ and $x = 4$. The pair $(3;4)$ interchanges the values of the variables, and $(3;2)$ satisfies only the second equation. The pair $(1;0)$ comes from multiplying only the left side by $6$, giving $3x + 2y = 3$.
