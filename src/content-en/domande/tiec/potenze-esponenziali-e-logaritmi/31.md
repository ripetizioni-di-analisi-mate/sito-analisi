Which line is an asymptote of the graph of the function $y = \log_{2} (x - 3)$?

- $x = 0$
- $y = 0$
- $x = -3$
- $x = 3$

## Explanation

The function is defined when its argument is positive, meaning $x - 3 > 0$, or $x > 3$. As $x$ approaches $3$ from the right, the argument $x - 3$ approaches $0$, and the logarithm with base $2$ decreases without bound. Therefore the line $x = 3$ is a **vertical asymptote**. The line $x = 0$ is the asymptote of $y = \log_{2} x$, whose graph here is shifted $3$ units to the right. The line $x = -3$ shifts in the wrong direction. The line $y = 0$ is the horizontal asymptote of exponential functions.
