The amount of a drug in the bloodstream decreases by one-third every hour. After $3$ hours, what fraction of the initial amount remains?

- $\dfrac{1}{27}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{8}{27}$
- $\dfrac{4}{9}$

## Explanation

If one-third is lost each hour, the fraction $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the amount present at the beginning of the hour remains. After $3$ hours the amount has been multiplied by $\dfrac{2}{3}$ three times, so the fraction remaining is $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{3} = \dfrac{8}{27}$. The result $0$ subtracts one-third of the *initial* amount each hour, and $\dfrac{1}{27}$ multiplies by $\dfrac{1}{3}$, the fraction lost instead of the fraction remaining. The result $\dfrac{4}{9}$ stops after the second hour.
