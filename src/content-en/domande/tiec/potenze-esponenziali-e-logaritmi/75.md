The solutions of the equation $4^{x} - 5 \cdot 2^{x} + 4 = 0$ are:

- $x = 1 \;\vee\; x = 4$
- $x = 2$
- $x = \log_{2} \dfrac{4}{3}$
- $x = 0 \;\vee\; x = 2$

## Explanation

Since $4^{x} = \left(2^{x}\right)^{2}$, the **change of variable** $t = 2^{x}$ turns the equation into $t^{2} - 5t + 4 = 0$, with solutions $t = 1$ and $t = 4$. Both values are positive and therefore admissible. From $2^{x} = 1$ it follows that $x = 0$, and from $2^{x} = 4$ it follows that $x = 2$. The result $x = 1 \;\vee\; x = 4$ stops at the values of $t$, and $x = 2$ loses the solution arising from $t = 1$. The result $x = \log_{2} \dfrac{4}{3}$ writes $4^{x}$ as $2 \cdot 2^{x}$ instead of $\left(2^{x}\right)^{2}$.
