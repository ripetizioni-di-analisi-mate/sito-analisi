To solve $4^x+2^x-6=0$, David sets $u=2^x$ and finds $u=2$ or $u=-3$. Which final line gives exactly the real solutions of the original equation?

- No $x$, because one of the roots in $u$ is negative
- Only $x=2$, because $u$ must be positive
- $x=1$ and $x=-3$, because both zeros must be kept
- Only $x=1$, because $2^x$ is never negative

## Explanation

The substitution requires $u>0$ even before the trinomial is solved. Hence only $u=2$ is admissible. Returning to the original variable, $2^x=2$ gives $x=1$.
