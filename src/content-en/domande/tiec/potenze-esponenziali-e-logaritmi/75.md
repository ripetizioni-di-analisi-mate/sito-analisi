What are the real solutions of the equation $4^{x} + 2^{x} - 6 = 0$?

- The equation has no real solutions
- $x = 2$
- $x = 1 \;\vee\; x = -3$
- $x = 1$

## Explanation

Since $4^{x} = \left( 2^{x} \right)^{2}$, we set $t = 2^{x}$ and the equation becomes $t^{2} + t - 6 = 0$, with the solutions $t = 2$ and $t = -3$. The value $t = -3$ is discarded, because $2^{x}$ is positive for every $x$. From $2^{x} = 2$ it follows that $x = 1$, which is therefore the only solution.
