The number $\sqrt{9 \cdot 10^{-6}}$ is equal to:

- $3 \cdot 10^{-6}$
- $3 \cdot 10^{-3}$
- $4.5 \cdot 10^{-3}$
- $3 \cdot 10^{3}$

## Explanation

By the rule for the **square root of a product**, $\sqrt{9 \cdot 10^{-6}} = \sqrt{9} \cdot \sqrt{10^{-6}}$. We have $\sqrt{9} = 3$ and $\sqrt{10^{-6}} = 10^{-\frac{6}{2}} = 10^{-3}$, since taking a square root halves the exponent. The number is therefore $3 \cdot 10^{-3} = 0.003$, and indeed $0.003^{2} = 0.000009$. The result $3 \cdot 10^{-6}$ takes the square root of only the coefficient, $4.5 \cdot 10^{-3}$ halves the coefficient instead of taking its square root, and $3 \cdot 10^{3}$ loses the sign of the exponent.
