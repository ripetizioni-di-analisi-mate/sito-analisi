For $a > 0$, the number $\sqrt{a\sqrt{a}}$ is equal to:

- $a^{\frac{1}{4}}$
- $a$
- $a^{\frac{3}{2}}$
- $a^{\frac{3}{4}}$

## Explanation

Write the inner root as a power, $\sqrt{a} = a^{\frac{1}{2}}$, so $a\sqrt{a} = a^{1 + \frac{1}{2}} = a^{\frac{3}{2}}$. Taking the square root of $a^{\frac{3}{2}}$ halves the exponent and gives $a^{\frac{3}{4}}$. The result $a^{\frac{3}{2}}$ overlooks the outer root, while $a^{\frac{1}{4}}$ drops the factor $a$ in front of the inner root. The result $a$ adds the exponents $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the two roots.
