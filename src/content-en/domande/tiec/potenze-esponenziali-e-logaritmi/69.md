For $x > 0$, the expression $\log_{2} x + \log_{2} (x + 1) - \log_{2} x^{2}$ is equal to:

- $\log_{2} (x + 1)$
- $\log_{2} \dfrac{x + 1}{x}$
- $\log_{2} \dfrac{2x + 1}{x^{2}}$
- $\log_{2} \left( x^{3} + x^{2} \right)$

## Explanation

By the product rule for logarithms, $\log_{2} x + \log_{2} (x + 1) = \log_{2} \left(x(x + 1)\right)$. By the quotient rule for logarithms, subtracting $\log_{2} x^{2}$ amounts to dividing the argument by $x^{2}$, giving $\log_{2} \dfrac{x(x + 1)}{x^{2}} = \log_{2} \dfrac{x + 1}{x}$. The result $\log_{2} \dfrac{2x + 1}{x^{2}}$ adds the arguments $x$ and $x + 1$, while $\log_{2} \left(x^{3} + x^{2}\right)$ multiplies by $x^{2}$ instead of dividing. The result $\log_{2} (x + 1)$ cancels $x$ with $x^{2}$ as if they were equal.
