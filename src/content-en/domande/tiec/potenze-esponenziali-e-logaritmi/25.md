For which pairs of numbers $u \geq 0$ and $v \geq 0$ does the equality $\sqrt{u + v} = \sqrt{u} + \sqrt{v}$ hold?

- For exactly the pairs with $uv = 1$
- For exactly the pairs with $u + v = 1$
- For exactly the pairs with $u = v$
- For exactly the pairs with $uv = 0$

## Explanation

The two sides are non-negative numbers, so they are equal if and only if their squares are equal. The square of the left side is $u + v$, while the square of the right side is $u + 2\sqrt{uv} + v$. The equality therefore holds if and only if $2\sqrt{uv} = 0$, that is if and only if $uv = 0$. So the equality holds only when at least one of the two numbers is zero, as in $\sqrt{9 + 0} = 3 + 0$.
