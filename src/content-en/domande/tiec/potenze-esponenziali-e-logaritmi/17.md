For every $a \neq 0$ one sets $a^{0} = 1$. Which argument justifies this definition?

- Every base raised to an even exponent gives $1$
- The exponent is multiplied by the base and gives $0$
- The quotient $\dfrac{a^{3}}{a^{3}}$ equals both $1$ and $a^{3-3}$
- The product of zero factors equal to $a$ equals $a$

## Explanation

Since $a \neq 0$, also $a^{3} \neq 0$, so the quotient $\dfrac{a^{3}}{a^{3}}$ exists and equals $1$. The quotient rule for powers with the same base gives instead $\dfrac{a^{3}}{a^{3}} = a^{3-3} = a^{0}$. For the rule to remain true with the exponent $0$, one must therefore set $a^{0} = 1$. The statement about even exponents is false, since for example $2^{2} = 4$.
