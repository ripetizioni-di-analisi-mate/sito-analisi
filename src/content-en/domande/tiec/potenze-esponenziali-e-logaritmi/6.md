Which of the following points lies on the graph of the function $y = 3^{x}$?

- $(0;0)$
- $(-1;-3)$
- $\left( -1; \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$
- $(2;6)$

## Explanation

A point lies on the graph when its y-coordinate equals $3$ raised to its x-coordinate. For $x = -1$, we have $3^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, so the point $\left( -1; \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$ lies on the graph. The point $(0;0)$ is excluded because $3^{0} = 1$, and the point $(-1;-3)$ is excluded because a power with a positive base is never negative. The point $(2;6)$ comes from multiplying the base by the exponent, whereas $3^{2} = 9$.
