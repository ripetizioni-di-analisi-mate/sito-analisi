For every real number $x$, the number $\sqrt{x^{2}}$ is equal to:

- $x$
- $|x|$
- $-x$
- $\left( \sqrt{x} \right)^{2}$

## Explanation

The square root of a number is never negative, while $x$ can be negative. For $x \geq 0$, we have $\sqrt{x^{2}} = x$, and for $x < 0$, we have $\sqrt{x^{2}} = -x$. In both cases the result is the **absolute value** $|x|$. For example, when $x = -7$, we have $\sqrt{(-7)^{2}} = \sqrt{49} = 7$, which differs from $x$. The expression $\left(\sqrt{x}\right)^{2}$ equals $x$ for $x \geq 0$ and is undefined for $x < 0$.
