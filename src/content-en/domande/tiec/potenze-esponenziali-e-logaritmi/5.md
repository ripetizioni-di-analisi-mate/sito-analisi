The inequality $\log_{3} (2x - 1) > 2$ holds for:

- $x > 5$
- $\dfrac{1}{2} < x < 5$
- $x > \dfrac{7}{2}$
- $x > 4$

## Explanation

The domain restriction is $2x - 1 > 0$, or $x > \dfrac{1}{2}$. Write $2 = \log_{3} 3^{2} = \log_{3} 9$. Since the base $3$ is greater than $1$, the inequality sign stays the same, giving $2x - 1 > 9$, or $x > 5$. The solutions must satisfy both $x > \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $x > 5$, so they are $x > 5$. The result $x > \dfrac{7}{2}$ comes from $3^{2} = 6$, and $x > 4$ from a sign error when moving $-1$ to the right side. The interval $\dfrac{1}{2} < x < 5$ reverses the inequality sign.
