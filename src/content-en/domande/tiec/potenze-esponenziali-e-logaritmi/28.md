What is the side length of a square whose area is twice that of a square with side $32$ cm?

- $64$ cm
- $128$ cm
- $32\sqrt{2}$ cm
- $16\sqrt{2}$ cm

## Explanation

The square with side $32$ cm has area $32^{2}$ square centimetres, so the required square has area $2 \cdot 32^{2}$ square centimetres. Its side is the square root of the area, that is $\sqrt{2 \cdot 32^{2}}$. By the rule for the root of a product, $\sqrt{2 \cdot 32^{2}} = \sqrt{2} \cdot 32$, so the side is $32\sqrt{2}$ cm. With the side doubled to $64$ cm, the area would instead be four times larger.
