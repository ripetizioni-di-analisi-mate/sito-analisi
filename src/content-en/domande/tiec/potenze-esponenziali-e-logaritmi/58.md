Which of the following points lies on the graph of the function $y = \log_{4} x$?

- $(16;4)$
- $(0;1)$
- $\left( -2; \dfrac{1}{16} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{1}{16}; -2 \right)$

## Explanation

The point $(x_{0}; y_{0})$ lies on the graph if $y_{0} = \log_{4} x_{0}$, or equivalently if $4^{y_{0}} = x_{0}$. Since $4^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{16}$, we have $\log_{4} \dfrac{1}{16} = -2$, and the point $\left(\dfrac{1}{16}; -2\right)$ lies on the graph. The point $(16;4)$ is excluded because $\log_{4} 16 = 2$, and $(0;1)$ is excluded because the logarithm is undefined at $0$. The point $\left(-2; \dfrac{1}{16}\right)$ has its coordinates switched and lies on the graph of $y = 4^{x}$.
