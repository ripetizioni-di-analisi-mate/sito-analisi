The equation $\left( \dfrac{1}{5} \right)^{x+1} = 25$ has the following solution:

- $x = 1$
- $x = -3$
- $x = -1$
- $x = 3$

## Explanation

Both bases are powers of $5$, since $\dfrac{1}{5} = 5^{-1}$ and $25 = 5^{2}$. The left side becomes $5^{-(x+1)} = 5^{-x-1}$, and **rewriting both sides with the same base** gives $5^{-x-1} = 5^{2}$. Equating exponents gives $-x - 1 = 2$, so $x = -3$. The value $x = 1$ overlooks the minus sign from $\dfrac{1}{5} = 5^{-1}$, while $x = -1$ changes the sign of only the term $x$.
