The number $\sqrt[3]{-64} + \sqrt{(-4)^{2}}$ is equal to:

- $-8$
- $0$
- $8$
- $12$

## Explanation

The cube root is defined even for negative numbers, and $\sqrt[3]{-64} = -4$ since $(-4)^{3} = -64$. The square root, however, is never negative, so $\sqrt{(-4)^{2}} = \sqrt{16} = 4$, the absolute value of $-4$. The sum therefore equals $-4 + 4 = 0$. The result $-8$ cancels the square root and the square to obtain $-4$, and $8$ assigns the value $4$ to the cube root of $-64$. The result $12$ overlooks the square root and adds $-4$ and $16$.
