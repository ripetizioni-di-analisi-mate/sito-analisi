The equation $\log_{5} (2x - 3) = \log_{5} (x + 1)$ has the following solution:

- $x = 4$
- $x = -2$
- $x = \dfrac{2}{3}$
- $x = 2$

## Explanation

The domain restrictions are $2x - 3 > 0$ and $x + 1 > 0$, which together give $x > \dfrac{3}{2}$. A logarithm takes different values at different points, so equality of the logarithms is equivalent to equality of their arguments, $2x - 3 = x + 1$. This gives $x = 4$, which satisfies $x > \dfrac{3}{2}$. The values $x = -2$ and $x = \dfrac{2}{3}$ arise from sign errors when moving terms and fail to satisfy the domain restrictions. The value $x = 2$ gives the arguments $1$ and $3$, which are different.
