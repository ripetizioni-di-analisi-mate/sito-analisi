The expression $\log_{x} 5$ is defined for:

- $x > 0$
- $x > 1$
- $x \neq 0 \;\wedge\; x \neq 1$
- $x > 0 \;\wedge\; x \neq 1$

## Explanation

In $\log_{x} 5$, the unknown is the base, and the base of a logarithm must be positive and different from $1$. The argument $5$ is positive, so it adds no restrictions. The expression is therefore defined for $x > 0$ with $x \neq 1$. For example, $\log_{\frac{1}{5}} 5 = -1$ is defined, whereas the condition $x > 1$ would exclude it. The condition $x \neq 0 \;\wedge\; x \neq 1$ admits negative bases, and $x > 0$ admits the base $1$, for which the equation $1^{c} = 5$ has no solution.
