By how much does the sum $\log_{2} 8 + \log_{2} 8$ exceed the number $\log_{2} (8 + 8)$?

- $2$
- $4$
- $-2$
- $0$

## Explanation

Since $8 = 2^{3}$, we have $\log_{2} 8 = 3$, so the sum $\log_{2} 8 + \log_{2} 8$ equals $6$. Since $8 + 8 = 16 = 2^{4}$, we have $\log_{2} (8 + 8) = 4$. The sum therefore exceeds the number $\log_{2} (8 + 8)$ by $6 - 4 = 2$. The sum of the two logarithms is in fact the logarithm of the product, $\log_{2} (8 \cdot 8) = \log_{2} 64 = 6$.
