What are the solutions of the inequality $\log_{3} (x - 1) + \log_{3} (x + 1) < 1$?

- $-2 < x < 2$
- $1 < x < 2$
- $x < 2$
- $1 < |x| < 2$

## Explanation

The domain conditions are $x - 1 > 0$ and $x + 1 > 0$, which together give $x > 1$. By the product rule for logarithms, the inequality becomes $\log_{3} \left( x^{2} - 1 \right) < \log_{3} 3$. Since the base $3$ is greater than $1$, this is equivalent to $x^{2} - 1 < 3$, that is $-2 < x < 2$. The solutions must satisfy both conditions together, so
$$
\begin{cases}
x > 1 \\
-2 < x < 2
\end{cases}
\iff
1 < x < 2 .
$$
