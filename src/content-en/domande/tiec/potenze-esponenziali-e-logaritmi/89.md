The inequality $\log_{3} (x - 1) + \log_{3} (x + 1) < 1$ holds for:

- $-2 < x < 2$
- $1 < x < 2$
- $x < 2$
- $1 < x < \sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The domain restrictions are $x - 1 > 0$ and $x + 1 > 0$, which together give $x > 1$. By the product rule for logarithms, the inequality becomes $\log_{3} \left(x^{2} - 1\right) < \log_{3} 3$. Since the base is greater than $1$, this gives $x^{2} - 1 < 3$, or $-2 < x < 2$. Intersecting this with $x > 1$ gives the solutions $1 < x < 2$. The result $-2 < x < 2$ overlooks the domain restrictions, and $1 < x < \sqrt{2}$ compares $x^{2} - 1$ with $1$ instead of $3^{1} = 3$.
