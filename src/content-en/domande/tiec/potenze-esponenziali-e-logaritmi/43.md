What is the range of the function $y = 2^{x} - 3$ as $x$ varies over $\mathbb{R}$?

- $(0, +\infty)$
- $(-3, +\infty)$
- $[-3, +\infty)$
- $(-\infty, -3)$

## Explanation

The function $2^{x}$ takes every positive value and no others, since for every $b > 0$ the equation $2^{x} = b$ has the solution $x = \log_{2} b$. After subtracting $3$, the values of $y$ are all the numbers greater than $-3$, giving the interval $(-3, +\infty)$. The value $-3$ is excluded because it would require $2^{x} = 0$, which is impossible. The interval $(0, +\infty)$ is the range of $2^{x}$ before subtracting $3$.
