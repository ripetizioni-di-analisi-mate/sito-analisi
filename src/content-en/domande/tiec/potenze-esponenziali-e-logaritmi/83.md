The equation $\log_{2} (x - 1) = -1$ has the following solution:

- $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x = -1$
- $x = 3$
- $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The domain restriction is $x - 1 > 0$, or $x > 1$. By the definition of a logarithm, the equation is equivalent to $x - 1 = 2^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$, which satisfies $x > 1$. The result $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ overlooks the need to add $1$, and $x = -1$ sets $x - 1 = -2$, treating a negative exponent as a minus sign. The result $x = 3$ sets $x - 1 = 2$, ignoring the minus sign.
