The product $\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt[4]{8}$ is equal to:

- $2$
- $\sqrt[6]{16}$
- $2\sqrt[4]{2}$
- $\sqrt[8]{16}$

## Explanation

Write the roots as powers of $2$, namely $\sqrt{2} = 2^{\frac{1}{2}}$ and $\sqrt[4]{8} = 2^{\frac{3}{4}}$. The product is $2^{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{4}} = 2^{\frac{5}{4}}$, and since $\dfrac{5}{4} = 1 + \dfrac{1}{4}$, the result is $2 \cdot 2^{\frac{1}{4}} = 2\sqrt[4]{2}$. The result $2$ multiplies the radicands without converting the roots to a common index, since $\sqrt[4]{2 \cdot 8} = 2$. The results $\sqrt[6]{16}$ and $\sqrt[8]{16}$ multiply the radicands and either add or multiply the indices. To multiply roots with different indices this way, first convert them to a common index.
