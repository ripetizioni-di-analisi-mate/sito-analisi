The number $\left( 2^{-1} + 3^{-1} \right)^{-1}$ is equal to:

- $5$
- $\dfrac{5}{6}$
- $-\dfrac{5}{6}$
- $\dfrac{6}{5}$

## Explanation

By the definition of a **negative exponent**, $2^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $3^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, so the sum is $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{5}{6}$. The outer exponent $-1$ calls for the reciprocal of this sum, or $\dfrac{6}{5}$. The result $5$ applies the exponent $-1$ to each term as if the reciprocal of a sum were the sum of the reciprocals. The result $\dfrac{5}{6}$ overlooks the outer exponent, while $-\dfrac{5}{6}$ interprets the exponent $-1$ as a change of sign.
