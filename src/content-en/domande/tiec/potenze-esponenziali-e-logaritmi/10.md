The inequality $\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{x} < \dfrac{9}{4}$ holds for:

- $x > -2$
- $x < -2$
- $x > 2$
- $x < 2$

## Explanation

Write the right side as a power of $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Since $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2} = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^{2} = \dfrac{9}{4}$, the inequality becomes $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{x} < \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2}$. The base lies between $0$ and $1$, so the exponential function is decreasing and **the inequality sign reverses** when comparing the exponents. This gives $x > -2$. The result $x < -2$ keeps the sign, while $x > 2$ and $x < 2$ come from writing $\dfrac{9}{4} = \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{2}$, which actually equals $\dfrac{4}{9}$. As a check, $x = 0$ gives $1 < \dfrac{9}{4}$, and $0$ belongs to the interval $x > -2$.
