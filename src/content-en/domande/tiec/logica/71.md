Which statement is equivalent to “Tonight Luca cooks or orders a pizza”?

- If Luca cooks tonight, he does not order a pizza.
- If Luca orders a pizza tonight, he does not cook.
- If Luca does not order a pizza tonight, he does not cook.
- If Luca does not cook tonight, he orders a pizza.

## Explanation

Using the **equivalence** $A \Rightarrow B \equiv \lnot A \lor B$ with $\lnot C$ in place of $A$ shows that $C \lor P$ is equivalent to $\lnot C \Rightarrow P$. With $C$ = “Luca cooks” and $P$ = “Luca orders a pizza,” this gives “If Luca does not cook tonight, he orders a pizza.” Indeed, the disjunction is false only when Luca neither cooks nor orders a pizza, the only case that also disproves the implication. “If Luca cooks, he does not order a pizza” and “If he orders a pizza, he does not cook” rule out doing both, which an inclusive “or” allows.
