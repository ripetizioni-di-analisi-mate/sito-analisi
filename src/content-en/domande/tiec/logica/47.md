Which of the following arguments is valid?

- If Ugo is in Paris, he is in France. Ugo is in France. Therefore Ugo is in Paris.
- If Ugo is in Paris, he is in France. Ugo is not in France. Therefore Ugo is not in Paris.
- If Ugo is in Paris, he is in France. Ugo is not in Paris. Therefore Ugo is not in France.
- If Ugo is in Paris, he is in France. If Ugo is in France, he speaks French. Ugo speaks French. Therefore Ugo is in Paris.

## Explanation

By the rules for **valid inferences**, a true implication $A \Rightarrow B$ and a false $B$ imply that $A$ is false. The argument that infers “Ugo is not in Paris” from “Ugo is not in France” follows this pattern and is therefore valid. Inferring “Ugo is in Paris” from “Ugo is in France” affirms the consequent, since Ugo could be in Lyon. Inferring “Ugo is not in France” from “Ugo is not in Paris” denies the antecedent. The argument involving “Ugo speaks French” repeats the error of affirming the consequent along a chain.
