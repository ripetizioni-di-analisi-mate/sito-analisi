“All the company's managers are college graduates. Some college graduates at the company speak Chinese.” Given these premises, which statement is correct?

- Some managers at the company speak Chinese.
- No manager at the company speaks Chinese.
- All college graduates at the company are managers.
- The premises do not determine whether any manager speaks Chinese.

## Explanation

In the **three-circle diagram**, the managers' circle lies inside the graduates' circle, and the second premise places an element in the overlap between graduates and speakers of Chinese. That element may be inside the managers' circle or outside it. The premises therefore allow both a manager who speaks Chinese and a situation where no manager speaks Chinese. Thus neither “Some managers speak Chinese” nor “No manager speaks Chinese” follows. “All graduates are managers” reverses the first premise.
