Consider a group of $40$ tourists and their visits to Rome, Florence, and Venice. Of the tourists, $20$ visited Rome, $18$ Florence, and $15$ Venice. The counts are $7$ for Rome and Florence, $6$ for Rome and Venice, and $5$ for Florence and Venice. Another $2$ tourists visited none of the three cities. How many visited all three?

- $3$
- $5$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

The number of tourists who visited at least one city is $40 - 2 = 38$. By the **formula for the union of three sets**, letting $x$ be the number who visited all three cities gives
$$
20 + 18 + 15 - 7 - 6 - 5 + x = 38 ,
$$
or $35 + x = 38$, so $x = 3$. The value $5$ sets the size of the union equal to $40$, overlooking the $2$ tourists who visited none of the cities.
