What is the negation of “An instructor in the course knows every enrolled student”?

- No instructor in the course knows any enrolled student.
- One of the instructors in the course does not know at least one enrolled student.
- For every instructor in the course, there is at least one enrolled student the instructor does not know.
- Every instructor in the course knows at least one enrolled student.

## Explanation

The statement contains two quantifiers, and the negation changes both. By the rule for **negating quantifiers**, “there is an instructor” becomes “every instructor,” and “knows every student” becomes “does not know at least one student.” Thus the negation is “For every instructor in the course, there is at least one enrolled student the instructor does not know.” “There is an instructor who does not know at least one student” changes only the second quantifier. It and the original statement are both true in a course with two instructors, one who knows every student and one who does not.
