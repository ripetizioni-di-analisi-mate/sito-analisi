Which statement is equivalent to “If the parking lot is full, Marco takes the subway”?

- The parking lot is full or Marco takes the subway.
- The parking lot is not full or Marco takes the subway.
- The parking lot is not full and Marco takes the subway.
- The parking lot is full and Marco takes the subway.

## Explanation

The **equivalence** $A \Rightarrow B \equiv \lnot A \lor B$ holds because both statements are false in the same single case, when $A$ is true and $B$ is false. With $A$ = “the parking lot is full” and $B$ = “Marco takes the subway,” this gives “The parking lot is not full or Marco takes the subway.” “The parking lot is full or Marco takes the subway” is false if spaces are available and Marco drives, a case where the implication is true. Each statement containing “and” is true in only one case, whereas the implication is true in three cases.
