“All the books on the top shelf are in English. No book in English is a novel.” Which statement does *not* follow from the premises?

- No book on the top shelf is a novel.
- No novel is on the top shelf.
- No novel is in English.
- All books that are not novels are in English.

## Explanation

The premises follow one of the **valid patterns**, “All $A$ are $B$, and no $B$ is $C$,” which gives “No $A$ is $C$,” or “No book on the top shelf is a novel.” Statements of the form “no … is …” work in either direction, so “No novel is on the top shelf” also follows, as does “No novel is in English” from the second premise. “All books that are not novels are in English” does not follow because a nonfiction book in Italian is neither a novel nor in English, and the premises allow such a book.
