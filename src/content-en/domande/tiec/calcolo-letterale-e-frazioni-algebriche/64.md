What is the complete factorization of $x^4 - 10x^2 + 9$?

- $(x^2 + 1)(x^2 + 9)$
- $(x - 1)(x + 1)(x^2 + 9)$
- $(x - 3)(x + 3)(x^2 + 1)$
- $(x - 1)(x + 1)(x - 3)(x + 3)$

## Explanation

Treat the polynomial as a quadratic in $x^2$ and find two numbers with **sum** $-10$ and **product** $9$, giving $x^4 - 10x^2 + 9 = (x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 9)$. Both factors are differences of squares, so they factor as $(x - 1)(x + 1)$ and $(x - 3)(x + 3)$. The product $(x^2 + 1)(x^2 + 9)$ expands to $x^4 + 10x^2 + 9$, with the wrong sign on the middle term. Each of the other two products contains a factor, $x^2 + 1$ or $x^2 + 9$, that does not divide the polynomial.
