How does the quadratic $-x^2 + x + 6$ factor?

- $(x - 3)(x + 2)$
- $(x + 3)(2 - x)$
- $(3 - x)(x + 2)$
- $-(x - 3)(x - 2)$

## Explanation

Factor out a minus sign, giving $-x^2 + x + 6 = -(x^2 - x - 6)$, so the quadratic has leading coefficient $1$. To factor $x^2 - x - 6$, look for two numbers with **sum** $-1$ and **product** $-6$, giving $(x - 3)(x + 2)$. This gives $-(x - 3)(x + 2)$, and moving the minus sign into the first factor yields $(3 - x)(x + 2)$. The result $(x - 3)(x + 2)$ omits the minus sign that was factored out, and at $x = 0$ it equals $-6$ instead of $6$.
