A polynomial of degree $5$ is divided by a polynomial of degree $2$. Which statement must be true?

- The quotient has degree $3$ and the remainder has degree $1$
- The quotient has degree $10$ and the remainder is zero or has degree less than $2$
- The quotient has degree $3$ and the remainder is zero or has degree less than $2$
- The quotient has degree $2$ and the remainder is zero or has degree less than $3$

## Explanation

In **polynomial division**, $A(x) = B(x)\,Q(x) + R(x)$, where $R$ is zero or has degree less than the degree of $B$, here less than $2$. Since the remainder is zero or has degree below $5$, the product $B(x)\,Q(x)$ has degree $5$. The degree of a product is the sum of the degrees of its factors, so the quotient has degree $5 - 2 = 3$. The remainder can have degree $1$ or $0$ or can be zero, as when $x^5$ is divided by $x^2$. Thus the remainder does not necessarily have degree $1$. The degree $10$ comes from multiplying the degrees instead of taking their difference.
