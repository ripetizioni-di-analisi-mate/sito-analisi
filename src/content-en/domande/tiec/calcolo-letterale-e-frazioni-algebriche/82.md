For which values is the quotient $\dfrac{1}{x - 1} : \dfrac{x + 2}{x}$ defined?

- $x \neq 1 \;\wedge\; x \neq 0$
- $x \neq 1 \;\wedge\; x \neq -2$
- $x \neq 1 \;\wedge\; x \neq 0 \;\wedge\; x \neq -2$
- $x \neq 1 \;\wedge\; x \neq 0 \;\wedge\; x \neq 2$

## Explanation

The fractions are defined when their denominators are nonzero, so $x \neq 1$ and $x \neq 0$. Division inverts the second fraction. Its numerator $x + 2$ becomes a denominator, so $x \neq -2$ is also required. The domain restrictions are therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
x \neq 1 \\
x \neq 0 \\
x \neq -2
\end{cases}
$$
The answer $x \neq 1 \;\wedge\; x \neq 0$ overlooks the restriction on the numerator of the divisor.
