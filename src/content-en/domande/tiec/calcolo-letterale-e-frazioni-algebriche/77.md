What is the least common multiple of $x^2 - 9$ and $x - 3$?

- $x^2 - 9$
- $x - 3$
- $(x - 3)(x^2 - 9)$
- $x + 3$

## Explanation

The polynomial $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$ already contains the factor $x - 3$. The **least common multiple** uses each factor once at its greatest exponent, so it is $(x - 3)(x + 3) = x^2 - 9$. The product $(x - 3)(x^2 - 9)$ is a common multiple, but it contains the factor $x - 3$ twice. The binomial $x - 3$ is the GCD of the two polynomials.
