What is the remainder when $x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 3$ is divided by $x^2 - 1$?

- $1$
- $5$
- $-2x + 1$
- $3$

## Explanation

The divisor has degree $2$, so use **polynomial long division**. Any nonzero remainder must have degree less than $2$. The first term of the quotient is $x$, giving $x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 3 - x(x^2 - 1) = 2x^2 + 3$. The second term is $2$, giving $2x^2 + 3 - 2(x^2 - 1) = 5$. Therefore the quotient is $x + 2$ and the remainder is $5$. The remainder $-2x + 1$ comes from dividing by $x^2 + 1$ instead of $x^2 - 1$.
