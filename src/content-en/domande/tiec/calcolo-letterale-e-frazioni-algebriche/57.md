How does the quadratic $6x^2 - x - 2$ factor?

- $(2x + 1)(3x - 2)$
- $(2x - 1)(3x + 2)$
- $(3x + 1)(2x - 2)$
- $\left(x + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(x - \dfrac{2}{3}\right)$

## Explanation

The discriminant is $\Delta = 1 + 48 = 49$, so the roots of $6x^2 - x - 2 = 0$ are $x = \dfrac{1 \pm 7}{12}$, or $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. The factorization formula for a **quadratic trinomial** gives $6x^2 - x - 2 = 6\left(x - \dfrac{2}{3}\right)\left(x + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$. Writing $6 = 3 \cdot 2$ gives $(3x - 2)(2x + 1)$. The product $\left(x + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(x - \dfrac{2}{3}\right)$ omits the coefficient $6$, and $(2x - 1)(3x + 2)$ expands to $6x^2 + x - 2$.
