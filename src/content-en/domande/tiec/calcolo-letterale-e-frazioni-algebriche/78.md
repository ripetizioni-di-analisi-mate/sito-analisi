Which of the following fractions is defined for every real number $x$?

- $\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{x}$
- $\dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1}$
- $\dfrac{1}{x^2 - 1}$
- $\dfrac{x + 1}{x^2 + 2x + 1}$

## Explanation

A fraction is defined for every $x$ when its denominator never vanishes. The denominator $x^2 + 1$ is greater than or equal to $1$ for every $x$, so $\dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1}$ is always defined. The denominators in the other fractions vanish at $0$, $\pm 1$, and $-1$, respectively, because $x^2 + 2x + 1 = (x + 1)^2$. The numerator $x^2 + 1$ of the fraction $\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{x}$ is always positive, but the domain restrictions come only from the denominator.
