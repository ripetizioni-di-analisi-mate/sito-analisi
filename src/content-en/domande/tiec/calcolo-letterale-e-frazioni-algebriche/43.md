The polynomial $2x^3 + 3x^2 - 3x - 2$ is zero at $x = 1$. What are its other zeros?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $2$
- $-1$ and $-2$
- $-2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-2$

## Explanation

Since $1$ is a zero, the polynomial is divisible by $x - 1$, and **synthetic division** gives the quotient $2x^2 + 5x + 2$. The quadratic has discriminant $\Delta = 25 - 16 = 9$, so its zeros are $x = \dfrac{-5 \pm 3}{4}$, or $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-2$. The other zeros of the polynomial are therefore $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-2$. Indeed, $2x^3 + 3x^2 - 3x - 2 = (x - 1)(2x + 1)(x + 2)$. The pair $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $2$ has the signs reversed. In the pair $-1$ and $-2$, the zero of the factor $2x + 1$ is taken to be $-1$ without dividing by $2$.
