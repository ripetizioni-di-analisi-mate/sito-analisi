What is the expansion of $(x^2 - x + 1)^2$?

- $x^4 + x^2 + 1$
- $x^4 - 2x^3 + x^2 - 2x + 1$
- $x^4 - 2x^3 + 3x^2 - 2x + 1$
- $x^4 - 2x^3 + 3x^2 + 2x + 1$

## Explanation

In the **square of a trinomial**, include the squares of the three terms, $x^4$, $x^2$, and $1$, and the three cross terms $2 \cdot x^2 \cdot (-x) = -2x^3$, $2 \cdot x^2 \cdot 1 = 2x^2$, and $2 \cdot (-x) \cdot 1 = -2x$. The square $x^2$ and the cross term $2x^2$ are like terms and combine to give $3x^2$. The expansion is therefore $x^4 - 2x^3 + 3x^2 - 2x + 1$. The result $x^4 - 2x^3 + x^2 - 2x + 1$ omits the cross term $2x^2$, and $x^4 - 2x^3 + 3x^2 + 2x + 1$ uses the wrong sign for twice the product of $-x$ and $1$.
