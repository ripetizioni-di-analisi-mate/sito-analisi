Which numerical check gives a counterexample to the claim “$(x+1)^2=x^2+1$ for every real $x$”?

- $x=0$, because both sides equal $1$
- $x=1$, because the two sides are $4$ and $2$
- $x=1$, because both sides equal $2$
- $x=0$, because the sides equal $0$ and $1$

## Explanation

To disprove an identity, one needs a value at which its sides differ. At $x=1$ the first side is $2^2=4$ and the second is $1+1=2$. At $x=0$ the equality does hold.
