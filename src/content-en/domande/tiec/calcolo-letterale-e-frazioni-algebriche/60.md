How does the polynomial $x^2 - y^2 + 4x + 4$ factor?

- $(x - 2 - y)(x - 2 + y)$
- $(x + 2 - y)(x + 2 + y)$
- $(x + y + 2)(x + y - 2)$
- $(x + y)(x - y + 4)$

## Explanation

The terms $x^2 + 4x + 4$ form the **square of a binomial**, $(x + 2)^2$, so the polynomial is $(x + 2)^2 - y^2$. This is a **difference of squares** and therefore factors as $(x + 2 - y)(x + 2 + y)$. The product $(x + y + 2)(x + y - 2)$ expands to $(x + y)^2 - 4$, which contains the term $2xy$. The product $(x + y)(x - y + 4)$ contains $4y$, which does not appear in the original polynomial.
