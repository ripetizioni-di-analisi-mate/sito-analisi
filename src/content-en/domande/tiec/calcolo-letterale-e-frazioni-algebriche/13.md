What is the sum $\dfrac{1}{2}x^2y - \dfrac{2}{3}x^2y + x^2y$?

- $\dfrac{5}{6}x^2y$
- $-\dfrac{1}{6}x^2y$
- $\dfrac{13}{6}x^2y$
- $\dfrac{5}{6}x^6y^3$

## Explanation

The three monomials are **like terms** because they have the same variable part $x^2y$. Their sum has that same variable part and a coefficient equal to the sum of the coefficients. The coefficient of the third monomial is $1$, so calculate $\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{2}{3} + 1 = \dfrac{3 - 4 + 6}{6} = \dfrac{5}{6}$. The result $-\dfrac{1}{6}x^2y$ omits the coefficient $1$ of $x^2y$, and $\dfrac{13}{6}x^2y$ uses the wrong sign for $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Addition leaves the exponents of the common variable part unchanged, so $x^6y^3$ is incorrect.
