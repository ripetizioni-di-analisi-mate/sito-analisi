For every $x\ne-2$, which form preserves the value of $\dfrac{x}{x+2}$ and writes it as an integer plus a proper rational term in $x$?

- $1+\dfrac{2}{x+2}$
- $1-\dfrac{2}{x}$
- $1-\dfrac{2}{x+2}$
- $1+\dfrac{x}{2}$

## Explanation

In the numerator write $x=(x+2)-2$. Dividing each term by $x+2$ gives $1$ for the first and $-2/(x+2)$ for the second. The denominator keeps the restriction $x\ne-2$.
