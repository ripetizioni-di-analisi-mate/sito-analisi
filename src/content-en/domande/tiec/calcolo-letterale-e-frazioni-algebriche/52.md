Divide $P(x)$ by $x-a$ to obtain $P(x)=(x-a)Q(x)+r$. A classmate calls $Q(a)$ the remainder. Which check shows that this rule can fail?

- For $P(x)=1$ the quotient and remainder are both $1$
- For $P(x)=x-a$ the quotient is $1$ and the remainder is $0$
- For $P(x)=0$ the quotient equals $1$
- For $P(x)=x-a$ the remainder equals $a$

## Explanation

A polynomial divided by itself gives quotient $1$ and zero remainder. In the stated example $Q(a)=1$ while $r=0$. In general it is $P(a)$ that gives the remainder.
