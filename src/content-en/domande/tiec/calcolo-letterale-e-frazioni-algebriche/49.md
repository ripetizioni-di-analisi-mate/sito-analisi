What is the remainder when $(x + 1)^{10}$ is divided by $x + 2$?

- $-1$
- $1$
- $2^{10}$
- $3^{10}$

## Explanation

By the **remainder theorem**, the remainder upon division by $x + 2$ is the value of the polynomial at $-2$, so there is no need to expand the power. This gives $(-2 + 1)^{10} = (-1)^{10} = 1$ because an even power of $-1$ equals $1$. The value $3^{10}$ is the polynomial’s value at $2$, which corresponds to the divisor $x - 2$, and $-1$ overlooks the fact that the exponent is even.
