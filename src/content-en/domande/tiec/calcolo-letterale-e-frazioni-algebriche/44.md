What is the remainder when $4x^3 - 2x^2 + 3$ is divided by $2x - 1$?

- $27$
- $3$
- $2$
- $5$

## Explanation

The divisor $2x - 1$ has degree $1$, so the remainder is a constant $R$ and for every $x$ we have $4x^3 - 2x^2 + 3 = (2x - 1)\,Q(x) + R$. Substituting the zero of the divisor, $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, makes the first term vanish, so $R$ is the polynomial’s value at $\dfrac{1}{2}$, as in the **remainder theorem**. This gives $4 \cdot \dfrac{1}{8} - 2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} + 3 = \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{2} + 3 = 3$. The value $27$ is the value of the polynomial at $2$, which comes from reading the divisor as $x - 2$.
