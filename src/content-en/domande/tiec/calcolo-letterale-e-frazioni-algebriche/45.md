A polynomial $P(x)$ divided by $x - 1$ gives remainder $3$, and divided by $x + 1$ gives remainder $5$. What is the remainder when $P(x)$ is divided by $x^2 - 1$?

- $x + 4$
- $4$
- $-x + 4$
- $8$

## Explanation

The divisor $x^2 - 1$ has degree $2$, so the remainder has degree at most $1$ if nonzero and can be written as $mx + q$. In $P(x) = (x^2 - 1)\,Q(x) + mx + q$, substitute $x = 1$ and $x = -1$, where $x^2 - 1$ vanishes, to obtain $P(1) = m + q$ and $P(-1) = -m + q$. By the **remainder theorem**, $P(1) = 3$ and $P(-1) = 5$, so
$$
\begin{cases}
m + q = 3 \\
-m + q = 5
\end{cases}
$$
Adding the equations gives $2q = 8$, so $q = 4$ and $m = -1$, and the remainder is $-x + 4$. The remainder $x + 4$ comes from interchanging the two given values.
