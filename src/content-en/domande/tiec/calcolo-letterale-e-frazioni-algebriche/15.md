The monomial $(2x^ny)^3$, where $n$ is a natural number, has degree $15$. What is $n$?

- $5$
- $4$
- $14$
- $6$

## Explanation

The power equals $(2x^ny)^3 = 8x^{3n}y^3$ because each exponent is multiplied by $3$. The degree is the sum of the exponents, or $3n + 3$, and the coefficient $8$ does not affect it. Therefore set $3n + 3 = 15$, which gives $3n = 12$ and $n = 4$. The value $5$ omits the exponent of $y$, and $14$ omits multiplication of the exponents by $3$.
