In a regression of $y$ on $x$, $\operatorname{cov}(x,y)=-6$ and $\sigma_x^2=4$. What is the slope?

- $m=\dfrac32$
- $m=-\dfrac32$
- $m=-3$
- $m=-\dfrac23$

## Explanation

The slope is covariance divided by the variance of the x-coordinates. This gives $\dfrac{-6}{4}=-\dfrac32$. The denominator is positive, so the slope has the same sign as the covariance.

The denominator is already the variance of $x$, so it is $4$. Using its square root $2$ instead would give $-3$ and change the ratio. The computed slope means that a unit increase in the input reduces the predicted output by $\dfrac32$.
