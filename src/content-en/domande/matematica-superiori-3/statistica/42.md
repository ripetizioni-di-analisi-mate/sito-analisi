A nonempty set of measurements has variance zero. Which property follows?

- All observations equal the mean.
- The mean is zero.
- The data contain at least two distinct values.
- The sum of the data is zero.

## Explanation

Variance is the mean of nonnegative squares. If it is zero, every deviation is zero, so each observation equals the mean.

A single positive square would make the entire sum positive because the other squares are nonnegative and the divisor is positive. Every deviation must therefore be zero. This constrains all the observations to a common value. It could, for example, be $4$, because variance depends on deviations and leaves the location of the mean unrestricted.
