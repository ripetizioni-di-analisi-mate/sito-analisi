Why can $\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\,\sqrt b$ fail as an identity between real expressions even when the product $ab$ is positive?

- The product of two positive numbers can be negative.
- Both factors could be negative.
- The square root of a positive number is always an integer.
- One of the factors must be zero.

## Explanation

With $a=-1$ and $b=-4$, the product is $4$ and the radical on the left equals $2$. The separate square roots of $-1$ and $-4$ are not real, so the product on the right is undefined over the reals. The formula applies when both factors are nonnegative.
