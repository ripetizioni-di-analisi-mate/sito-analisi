Find the real domain of $\sqrt{x+3}+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2-x}}$.

- $[-3,2]$
- $[-3,2)$
- $(-\infty,2)$
- $(-3,2)$

## Explanation

The root in the first term requires $x+3\geq0$. The one in the denominator requires $2-x>0$. The intersection satisfies both constraints and includes only the endpoint $-3$. The first condition gives $x\geq-3$, and the second gives $x<2$. At $x=-3$ the first root is zero and the denominator is positive. At $x=2$ the denominator is zero, so that point is excluded.
