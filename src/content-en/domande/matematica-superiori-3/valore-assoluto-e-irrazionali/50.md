If $a$ and $b$ are nonnegative measurements, why does comparing $\sqrt a$ and $\sqrt b$ give the same order as comparing $a$ and $b$?

- Squaring is strictly increasing on the whole real line.
- The sum of the square roots is always $1$.
- Squaring is strictly increasing on the nonnegative numbers.
- Every square root is smaller than its radicand.

## Explanation

Principal square roots are nonnegative. If $\sqrt a<\sqrt b$, squaring on this ray gives $a<b$. Conversely, if $a<b$, the square root of $a$ cannot be greater than or equal to that of $b$, since squaring would give the reverse order or equality.
