What is the real domain of $1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2-4}}$?

- $(-\infty,-2)\cup(2,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-2]\cup[2,+\infty)$
- $[-2,2]$
- $(-2,2)$

## Explanation

The denominator is a square root, so we need $x^2-4>0$. The square must exceed $4$, which means $x<-2$ or $x>2$. At $-2$ and $2$ the denominator equals zero.
