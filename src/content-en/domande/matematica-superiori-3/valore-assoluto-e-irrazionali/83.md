Can $\sqrt{x+4}\geq-2$ be squared while preserving exactly its solution set?

- No. The original comparison holds throughout the domain, while squaring would require $x\geq0$.
- Yes. The square of $-2$ is $4$.
- No. The square root is undefined for every $x$.

## Explanation

On the domain $x\geq-4$, the square root is at least zero and therefore exceeds $-2$. Squaring would give $x+4\geq4$, or $x\geq0$. For example, $x=-4$ satisfies the original comparison and is lost when the squares are compared.
