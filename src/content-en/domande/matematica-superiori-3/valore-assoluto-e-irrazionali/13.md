Solve $\sqrt{x+4}=\sqrt{2x-1}$.

- $\{1\}$
- $\{3\}$
- $\varnothing$
- $\{5\}$

## Explanation

The common domain is $x\geq\dfrac12$. Both sides are nonnegative, so set the radicands equal. This gives $x+4=2x-1$ and hence $x=5$, which belongs to the domain. The first radicand requires $x\geq-4$, and the second requires $x\geq\dfrac12$. At $x=5$, both equal $9$ and the two roots equal $3$. This checks the equality before squaring.
