Find the real domain of $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6-3x}}$.

- $(2,+\infty)$
- $[2,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,2]$
- $(-\infty,2)$

## Explanation

We need $6-3x>0$ because the square root in the denominator must exist and be nonzero. Dividing $-3x>-6$ by $-3$ reverses the inequality and gives $x<2$. At $x=2$ the radicand is zero, so the denominator is zero as well. At $x=0$ the denominator is instead $\sqrt6>0$. This distinguishes the excluded boundary from the admissible values.
