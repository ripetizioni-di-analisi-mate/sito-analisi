What is the real domain of $\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{1}{x+2}}$?

- $(-2,+\infty)$
- $\mathbb R$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{-2\}$
- $[-2,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The cube root accepts radicands of either sign. The fraction still requires $x+2\neq0$. Only $-2$ is excluded. At a point such as $x=-3$ the radicand is $-1$ and its cube root is $-1$, so the left branch is allowed. At $x=-2$, the fraction is undefined before taking the root.
