In $\sqrt{x+5}-\sqrt x=1$ the two radicals differ by $1$. What must their sum be?

- $1$
- $\sqrt5$
- $5$
- $25$

## Explanation

The product of the sum and the difference is $(x+5)-x=5$. Since the difference is $1$, the sum must be $5$. The radicals are therefore $3$ and $2$, and substituting $x=4$ confirms the result.
