Solve $\sqrt{x+1}=|x-1|$.

- $\{3\}$
- $\{0\}$
- $[-1,3]$
- $\{0,3\}$

## Explanation

On the domain $x\geq-1$, both sides are nonnegative. Squaring gives $x+1=(x-1)^2$, or $x(x-3)=0$. Both $0$ and $3$ belong to the domain and satisfy the original equation. At $x=0$ both sides equal $1$. At $x=3$ they equal $2$. The absolute value on the right makes both candidates admissible without an additional sign restriction on $x-1$.
