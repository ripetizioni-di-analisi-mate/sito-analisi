For which $t$ does $y^2+4y+t=0$ describe exactly one horizontal line?

- $t=-4$
- $t=4$
- $t=0$
- $t=2$

## Explanation

Completing the square gives $(y+2)^2=4-t$. There must be exactly one value of $y$, so $4-t=0$. The line is $y=-2$.

For $t<4$ there would be two distinct ordinates and hence two horizontal lines. For $t>4$ the square would have to be negative and the locus would be empty. At equality the abscissa stays free, and the only allowed ordinate is $-2$.
