What is the abscissa of the intersection of $y=x^2$ and $y=x^2-3x+6$?

- $-2$
- $2$
- $3$
- $6$

## Explanation

Equating and canceling $x^2$ gives $-3x+6=0$. The solution is $x=2$, where both ordinates equal $4$.

At $(2,4)$ the second equation gives $4-6+6=4$, which checks the intersection. The squared terms cancel because they have the same coefficient. The linear term keeps a nonzero coefficient, giving exactly one abscissa.
