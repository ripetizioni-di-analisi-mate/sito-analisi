Find $q$ so that $y=4x+q$ is tangent to $y=x^2$.

- $q=4$
- $q=-2$
- $q=-4$
- $q=-16$

## Explanation

Substituting the line gives $x^2-4x-q=0$. Its discriminant is $16+4q$, which vanishes at $q=-4$. The repeated root is $x=2$.

At $q=-4$ the ordinate difference is $x^2-(4x-4)=(x-2)^2$. Thus the line stays below the parabola and reaches it only at $x=2$. Raising the intercept from $-4$ to $-2$ would set the square equal to $2$ and produce two intersections.
