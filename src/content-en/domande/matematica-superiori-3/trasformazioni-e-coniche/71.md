When $q>-1$, how many common points do $y=x^2$ and $y=2x+q$ have?

- $0$
- $1$
- $4$
- $2$

## Explanation

The equation $x^2-2x-q=0$ has discriminant $4+4q>0$ when $q>-1$. It has two distinct real roots and hence two points.

The result also follows from $(x-1)^2=q+1$. When $q>-1$ the square equals a positive number, so the abscissas are equally far from $1$ on either side. At $q=-1$ they meet at $1$.
