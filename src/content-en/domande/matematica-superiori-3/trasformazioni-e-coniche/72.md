For which positive value of $t$ is the line $x=t$ tangent to $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$?

- $3$
- $4$
- $9$
- $16$

## Explanation

Substituting $x=t$ leaves $y^2=9(1-\dfrac{t^2}{16})$. There is one point when the right-hand side is zero. The positive value is $t=4$.

At the positive tangent the point is $(4,0)$, which satisfies $\dfrac{16}{16}+0=1$. The line $x=3$ lies within the ellipse’s horizontal span and crosses it twice. The denominator $16$ is the square of the horizontal semiaxis, whose length is $4$.
