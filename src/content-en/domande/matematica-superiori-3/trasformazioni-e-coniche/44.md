Which lines constitute the locus $x^2-4x+3=0$ in the plane?

- $y=1$ and $y=3$
- $x=-1$ and $x=-3$
- $x=1$ and $x=3$
- $x=-1$ and $x=3$

## Explanation

The product $(x-1)(x-3)$ vanishes at $x=1$ or $x=3$. The ordinate is unrestricted, so each value of $x$ gives a vertical line.

The points $(1,0)$ and $(1,5)$ both satisfy the equation because changing the ordinate does not affect the left-hand side. The same holds for every point with abscissa $3$. The horizontal lines $y=1$ and $y=3$ would instead allow abscissas that are not roots of the polynomial.
