How far is the vertex of $(x+1)^2=-6(y-4)$ from the directrix?

- $6$
- $3$
- $\dfrac23$
- $\dfrac32$

## Explanation

Comparing with $(x-h)^2=4p(y-k)$ gives $p=-\dfrac32$. The distance is the absolute value of $p$, namely $\dfrac32$.

The vertex has ordinate $4$ and the directrix is above it because the focus is below. The line therefore has ordinate $4-p=\dfrac{11}{2}$. Measuring vertically from the vertex gives $\dfrac{11}{2}-4=\dfrac32$. The negative sign of $p$ gives the opening direction, and the distance stays positive.
