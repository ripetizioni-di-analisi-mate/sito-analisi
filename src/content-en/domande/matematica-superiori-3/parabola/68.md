The graph shows $V(3,1)$, $P(2,2)$ and $Q(2,0)$. Why is the entire curve not a function $y=f(x)$?

::grafico{curve="-(t-1)^2+3,t,-1.7,3.7" xmin=-3.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-2.2 ymax=4.2 punti="3,1,pieno,V;2,2,pieno,P;2,0,pieno,Q" legenda=0 titolo="P and Q lie on the parabola"}

- The ordinate $1$ occurs at the vertex.
- The parabola has an axis of symmetry.
- Two x-coordinates must always give the same ordinate.
- The same x-coordinate $2$ has two ordinates.

## Explanation

The vertical line $x=2$ passes through both $P$ and $Q$. A function $y=f(x)$ assigns one value to that x-coordinate, but here the ordinates are $2$ and $0$.
