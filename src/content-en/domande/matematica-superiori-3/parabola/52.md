Which polynomial must result from subtracting the tangent at x-coordinate $3$ from $y=-2x^2+x+4$?

- $(x-3)^2$
- $-2(x-3)^2$
- $-2(x+3)^2$
- $-2(x-3)$

## Explanation

Subtracting a line preserves the quadratic coefficient, $-2$. To have a double root at $3$, the polynomial must therefore be $-2(x-3)^2$.

Expanding gives $-2x^2+12x-18$. The original quadratic minus this polynomial is the line $y=-11x+22$. At $x=3$, the line and the parabola both have ordinate $-11$, and the squared factor ensures that the equality has multiplicity two.
