Where does $y=-3(x+2)^2$ have a positive y-coordinate?

- Only at $x=-2$.
- For no value of $x$.
- For every $x\ne-2$.
- For $x<-2$.

## Explanation

The square is nonnegative, so multiplying by $-3$ always gives a value at most zero. At $x=-2$ the value is exactly zero.

Zero is the maximum and occurs only at the vertex $(-2,0)$. One unit from the axis, for example at $x=-1$, the ordinate is $-3$. The contact with the axis at $x=-2$ gives $y=0$, while the question requires $y>0$.
