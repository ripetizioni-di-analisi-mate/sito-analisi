Find $q$ such that $y=4x+q$ is tangent to $y=x^2+1$.

- $q=-3$
- $q=-4$
- $q=3$
- $q=5$

## Explanation

Substitution gives $x^2-4x+1-q=0$, or $(x-2)^2=q+3$. The root is double when the right-hand side is zero, so $q=-3$.

With this value the line gives ordinate $4\cdot2-3=5$ at $x=2$, equal to $2^2+1$. As $q$ varies, $q+3$ measures how far the line is above or below the tangent position. If it were positive, there would be two distinct x-coordinates of intersection.
