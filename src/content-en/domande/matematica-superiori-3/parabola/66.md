Which formula describes the upper branch of $x=2(y+1)^2-3$?

- $y=-1+\sqrt{\dfrac{x+3}{2}}$
- $y=-1-\sqrt{\dfrac{x+3}{2}}$
- $y=1+\sqrt{\dfrac{x+3}{2}}$
- $y=-1+\sqrt{2(x+3)}$

## Explanation

We get $(y+1)^2=\dfrac{x+3}{2}$. The upper branch has $y\ge-1$, so take $y+1$ equal to the nonnegative square root.

The square root requires $x\ge-3$. At $x=-3$ both branches give the vertex $(-3,-1)$. For example, at $x=-1$ the radicand equals $1$. Then the upper branch gives $y=0$ and the lower gives $y=-2$. Choosing a sign specifies which ordinate is represented.
