For which values of $x$ does $y=(x-4)^2-4$ lie above the x-axis?

- $(2,6)$
- $(-\infty,2)\cup(6,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,2]\cup[6,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,0)\cup(8,+\infty)$

## Explanation

We need $(x-4)^2>4$, so the distance from $x$ to $4$ must exceed $2$. The two possibilities are $x<2$ and $x>6$. At $2$ and $6$ the graph is on the axis.

The product $(x-2)(x-6)$ is the same polynomial. Between its two roots the factors have opposite signs, making the product negative. Outside the interval the factors have the same sign. The strict condition excludes both endpoints.
