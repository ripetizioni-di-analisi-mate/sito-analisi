Where does $y=6x-9$ touch $y=x^2$?

- $(6,36)$
- $(3,-9)$
- $(3,9)$
- $(-3,9)$

## Explanation

The difference $x^2-6x+9$ is $(x-3)^2$. The double root is $3$, so substitution in the parabola gives the point $(3,9)$.

The intercept $-9$ is the height of the line at $x=0$, while the ordinate of contact is found at the double root. Substitution into the line gives $6\cdot3-9=9$. The difference $(x-3)^2$ is positive for $x\ne3$, so the point of contact is unique.
