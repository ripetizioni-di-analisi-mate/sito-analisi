Solve $2^{x^2-4x}=1$.

- $\{2\}$
- $\{4\}$
- $\{0,-4\}$
- $\{0,4\}$

## Explanation

Write $1$ as $2^0$. The base-$2$ exponential is one-to-one, so $x^2-4x=0$. Factoring out $x$ gives $x(x-4)=0$, so $x=0$ or $x=4$. Both make the exponent zero and give the power $1$. Dividing the equation for the exponents by $x$ at the outset would lose the root $x=0$. Factoring keeps both inputs, one from each zero of the product.
