For which real $x$ does $4^x+2\cdot2^x+1=0$ hold?

- For no $x$.
- For every $x$.
- Only for $x=0$.
- Only for $x=-1$.

## Explanation

The three terms form the square $(2^x+1)^2$. A square vanishes only when the expression being squared vanishes. This would require $2^x=-1$, impossible because the power is strictly positive. Even as $x$ decreases without bound, $2^x+1$ approaches $1$ from above and remains positive. In particular, $x=0$ gives $(1+1)^2=4$, so exponent zero does not solve the equation either.
