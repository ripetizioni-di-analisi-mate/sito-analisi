Solve $4^{-x}-3\cdot2^{-x}+2=0$.

- $\{1,2\}$
- $\{-2,-1\}$
- $\{0,1\}$
- $\{-1,0\}$

## Explanation

Since $4^{-x}=(2^{-x})^2$, set $u=2^{-x}>0$. The equation becomes $u^2-3u+2=(u-1)(u-2)=0$. For $u=1=2^0$ we get $-x=0$, and for $u=2=2^1$ we get $-x=1$. Thus $x=0$ and $x=-1$. The minus sign belongs to the exponent and must be carried through when converting the root $u=2$. The substitution decreases, so heights above $1$ correspond to negative inputs, as with this root.
