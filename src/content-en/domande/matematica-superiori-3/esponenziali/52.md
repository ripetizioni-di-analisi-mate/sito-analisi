How many solutions does $4^x-4\cdot2^x+4=0$ have?

- One, at $x=1$.
- One, at $x=2$.
- None.
- Two distinct solutions.

## Explanation

Setting $u=2^x$ gives $u^2-4u+4=(u-2)^2$. The quadratic has a double root at $u=2$, which is only one distinct value. Because the base-$2$ exponential is one-to-one, $2^x=2$ gives just $x=1$. Substituting $1$ in the original expression returns $4-8+4=0$. Algebraic multiplicity two does not give a second solution. The only height required of $2^x$ is $2$.
