Solve $4^x=2^{x+3}$.

- $\{3\}$
- $\{1\}$
- $\{-3\}$
- $\{6\}$

## Explanation

Write $4=2^2$, so $4^x=(2^2)^x=2^{2x}$. The base-$2$ exponential is one-to-one, so equality with $2^{x+3}$ requires $2x=x+3$. Subtracting $x$ from both sides yields $x=3$. To check the result, $4^3=64$ and $2^{3+3}=2^6=64$. Rewriting the bases comes before comparing the exponents. At $x=1$ the sides would be $4$ and $16$, so rewriting the bases does not make the equation an identity.
