A student searches for real numbers $x$ with $2^x+2^{-x}<2$. Which expression proves that there are none?

- $2^x+2^{-x}-2=\dfrac{(2^x-1)^2}{2^x}$
- $2^x+2^{-x}-2=\dfrac{(2^x+1)^2}{2^x}$
- $2^x+2^{-x}-2=2^{x-x}-2$
- $2^x+2^{-x}-2=(2^x-1)^2$

## Explanation

Combining the terms over the denominator $2^x$ gives the numerator $(2^x)^2-2\cdot2^x+1=(2^x-1)^2$. The numerator is nonnegative and the denominator is positive. The difference from $2$ is therefore nonnegative for every $x$.
