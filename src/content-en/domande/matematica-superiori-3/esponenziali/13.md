Solve $4^x-8\cdot2^x=0$.

- $\{0,3\}$
- $\varnothing$
- $\{8\}$
- $\{3\}$

## Explanation

Since $4^x=(2^x)^2$, putting $u=2^x$ gives $u^2-8u=u(u-8)=0$. The root $u=0$ lies outside the range of $2^x$. This leaves $u=8$, so $2^x=2^3$ and $x=3$. Alternatively, divide the original equation directly by $2^x$, which is positive, to cancel the factor that can never vanish. The excluded zero is a root in $u$, while $x=0$ gives $u=1$.
