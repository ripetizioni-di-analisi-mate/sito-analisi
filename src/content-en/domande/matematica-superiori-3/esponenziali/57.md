How many solutions does $4^x-c\cdot2^x+1=0$ have if $c>2$?

- Infinitely many.
- Two distinct solutions.
- One.
- None.

## Explanation

Putting $u=2^x>0$ gives $u^2-cu+1=0$. For $c>2$ the discriminant $c^2-4$ is positive, so there are two distinct real roots. Their product $1$ means they have the same sign. Their sum is $c>0$, so both are positive. Each positive root gives a different real exponent, and the original equation therefore also has two distinct solutions.
