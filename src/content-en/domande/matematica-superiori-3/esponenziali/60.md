What is the $y$-intercept of $f(x)=2^{x+1}-3$?

- $(0,1)$
- $(1,0)$
- $(0,-3)$
- $(0,-1)$

## Explanation

The vertical axis has horizontal coordinate zero. Compute $f(0)=2^{0+1}-3=2-3=-1$, giving $(0,-1)$. The term $1$ in the exponent makes the power equal to $2$ at $x=0$, and the term $-3$ lowers that value. The number $-3$ is the height of the asymptote, which here differs from the intercept. To find the horizontal intercept, set $f(x)=0$ instead of setting the input to zero.
