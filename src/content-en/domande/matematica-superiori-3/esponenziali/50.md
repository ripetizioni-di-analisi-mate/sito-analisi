Which substitution makes $16^x-6\cdot4^x+8=0$ quadratic?

- $u=16^x>0$
- $u=4x$
- $u=4^x>0$
- $u=x^2$

## Explanation

The terms $16^x$ and $4^x$ are related by $16^x=(4^2)^x=(4^x)^2$. Setting $u=4^x>0$ gives $u^2-6u+8=0$. Positivity restricts the admissible values of $u$. The factorisation $(u-2)(u-4)$ shows that both roots can be converted back to $x$, giving $x=\dfrac12$ and $x=1$, respectively. Their positivity permits taking the base-$4$ logarithm of both roots to recover the original unknown.
