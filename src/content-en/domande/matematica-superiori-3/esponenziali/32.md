Solve $2^{\sqrt{x-1}}=4$.

- $\{5\}$
- $\{3\}$
- $\{4\}$
- $\{1\}$

## Explanation

The square root requires $x-1\geq0$, or $x\geq1$. Because $4=2^2$, injectivity gives $\sqrt{x-1}=2$. Both sides are nonnegative, so squaring gives $x-1=4$ and then $x=5$. The value satisfies the domain condition, and checking gives $2^{\sqrt{5-1}}=2^2=4$. The root must be exactly $2$. The condition $x-1=2$ would give exponent $\sqrt2$ and a value below $4$.
