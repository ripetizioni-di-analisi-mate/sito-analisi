What is the horizontal asymptote of $f(x)=2^{x+1}-4$?

- $x=-4$
- $y=-4$
- $y=4$
- $y=-3$

## Explanation

As $x$ becomes more negative, the exponent $x+1$ decreases without bound and $2^{x+1}$ becomes arbitrarily small. The term $-4$ shifts the limiting height from zero to $-4$, so the line is $y=-4$. The curve always lies above it because $f(x)+4=2^{x+1}>0$. At $x=0$, by contrast, its height is $-2$. For instance, $f(-3)=-\dfrac{15}{4}$, just $\dfrac14$ above the line.
