For a fixed $a>1$, which formula solves $a^{2x}=k$ for every $k>0$?

- $x=2+\log_a k$
- $x=\dfrac12\log_a k$
- $x=\log_a(k^2)$
- $x=\log_a(k+2)$

## Explanation

For $k>0$ both sides have a base-$a$ logarithm. By definition, $\log_a(a^{2x})=2x$, so $2x=\log_a k$ and $x=\dfrac12\log_a k$. Checking gives $a^{2\cdot(\frac12\log_a k)}=k$. In particular, for $k=a^2$ the formula returns $x=1$, because $\log_a(a^2)=2$. The factor $\dfrac12$ results from dividing the logarithm by the coefficient of $x$. Multiplying by $2$ would instead give an exponent four times as large for $k\ne1$.
