How does $g(x)=4-\left(\dfrac12\right)^x$ vary?

- It increases up to $x=0$ and then decreases.
- It is constant.
- It is strictly increasing.
- It is strictly decreasing.

## Explanation

For $x_1<x_2$, the power with base $\dfrac12$ satisfies $\left(\dfrac12\right)^{x_1}>\left(\dfrac12\right)^{x_2}$. Subtracting both quantities from $4$ reverses the comparison, so $g(x_1)<g(x_2)$. The function is strictly increasing. Indeed, $g(0)=3$ and $g(1)=\dfrac72$. As $x$ increases, the heights approach $4$ from below because the subtracted power stays positive. Thus the range lies entirely below $4$, while including values arbitrarily close to that level.
