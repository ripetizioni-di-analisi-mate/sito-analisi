Solve $\log_2(x-4)=1$.

- $\{4\}$
- $\{2\}$
- $\{5\}$
- $\{6\}$

## Explanation

The argument $x-4$ must be strictly positive, so $x>4$. On that domain, $\log_2(x-4)=1$ is equivalent to $x-4=2^1=2$. Adding $4$ gives $x=6$. To check the result, the original argument at $6$ is $2$. The logarithm is defined there and equals exactly $1$. The value $x=5$ would be in the domain but would give argument $1$ and therefore logarithm zero.
