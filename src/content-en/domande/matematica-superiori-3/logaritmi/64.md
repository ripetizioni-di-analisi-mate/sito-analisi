Solve $\log_2\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}<0$.

- $(1,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-2)$
- $(-2,1)$
- $(-\infty,-2)\cup(1,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The quotient is positive for $x<-2$ or $x>1$. On the domain, the base-$2$ logarithm is negative when the quotient is less than $1$. Subtracting $1$ gives $\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}-1=\dfrac{-3}{x+2}$. This fraction is negative if $x>-2$, so the intersection leaves only $x>1$. At $x=1$ the argument is zero. For instance, at $x=-3$ the quotient is $4$ and the logarithm is positive.
