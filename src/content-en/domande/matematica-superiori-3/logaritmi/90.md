For which $x$ does $\log_9(x^2)=\log_3 x$ hold?

- Only for $x=3$.
- For every $x\ne0$.
- For every $x>0$.
- Only for $x=1$.

## Explanation

The logarithm on the right requires $x>0$, which also gives $x^2>0$. On that common domain, changing base gives $\log_9(x^2)=\dfrac{\log_3(x^2)}{\log_3 9}=\dfrac{2\log_3 x}{2}=\log_3 x$. The equality therefore holds for every $x>0$. For $x<0$ the left-hand side is defined, but the right-hand side has no real value. At $x=1$, both values are zero and the equality is within the domain.
