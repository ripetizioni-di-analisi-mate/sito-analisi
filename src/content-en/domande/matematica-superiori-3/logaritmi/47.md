At which $x$-coordinate does the graph of $y=\log_2(x-2)-1$ cross the $x$-axis?

- $5$
- $4$
- $3$
- $2$

## Explanation

Points on the $x$-axis have $y$-coordinate zero, so we solve $\log_2(x-2)-1=0$, that is, $\log_2(x-2)=1$. By the definition of logarithm the argument is $x-2=2^1=2$, therefore $x=4$.

The value $x=4$ satisfies the existence condition $x-2>0$, so the graph meets the $x$-axis at the point $(4,0)$. At $x=3$ only $\log_2(x-2)$ vanishes, while the function equals $-1$.
