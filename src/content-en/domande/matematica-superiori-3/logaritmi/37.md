Find the domain of $\ln(x^2-4x+4)$.

- $\mathbb R\setminus\{2\}$
- $\mathbb R$
- $(2,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,2]$

## Explanation

Completing the square gives $x^2-4x+4=(x-2)^2$. The argument is strictly positive on both sides of $2$, because the square of a nonzero number is positive. It is zero only at $x=2$. The domain is therefore $\mathbb R\setminus\{2\}$. For example, $x=1$ is allowed and gives $\ln1=0$. The value $x=3$ also gives $\ln1=0$. A zero logarithm is different from a zero argument.
