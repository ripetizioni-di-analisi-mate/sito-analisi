What is the domain of $f(x)=\log_3\dfrac{x-4}{x+1}$?

- $(-\infty,-1]\cup[4,+\infty)$
- $(4,+\infty)$
- $(-1,4)$
- $(-\infty,-1)\cup(4,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The quotient must be positive for the logarithm to exist. For $x<-1$ both factors are negative, while for $x>4$ both are positive. Between $-1$ and $4$ they have opposite signs. At $-1$ the denominator is zero and at $4$ the argument is zero, so both endpoints are excluded. The domain is $(-\infty,-1)\cup(4,+\infty)$. For instance, $x=0$ gives quotient $-4$ and therefore lies outside the domain.
