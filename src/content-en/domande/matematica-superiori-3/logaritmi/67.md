Solve $\log_2(x^2+1)<0$.

- $(-1,1)$
- $\varnothing$
- $\{0\}$
- $\mathbb R$

## Explanation

For every real $x$, $x^2+1\geq1$ and the base-$2$ logarithm is increasing. Its value is therefore at least $\log_2 1=0$. At $x=0$ the minimum is $0$, while for $x\ne0$ the logarithm is positive. No input gives a value strictly below zero. The domain is all of $\mathbb R$, because the argument never falls below $1$.
