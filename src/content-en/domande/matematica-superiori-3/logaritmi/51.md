What is the inverse of $f(x)=2^x+3$, defined for $x\in\mathbb R$?

- $f^{-1}(x)=\log_2(x-3)$ for $x>3$.
- $f^{-1}(x)=\log_2 x-3$ for $x>0$.
- $f^{-1}(x)=\log_2(x-3)$ for $x\ne3$.
- $f^{-1}(x)=\log_2(x+3)$ for $x>-3$.

## Explanation

To invert $y=2^x+3$, subtract $3$ and solve $2^x=y-3$. The exponential takes exactly the positive values, so $y>3$. This gives $x=\log_2(y-3)$. Using $x$ for the inverse's input, $f^{-1}(x)=\log_2(x-3)$ for $x>3$. The value $3$ is the boundary of the domain and is excluded. As a check, applying $f$ to $\log_2(x-3)$ recovers $(x-3)+3=x$ for $x>3$.
