Why is base $1$ excluded from the definition of the logarithm?

- $1^x$ can take negative values.
- $1^x$ is defined only for $x>0$.
- $1^x$ is always zero.
- $1^x$ always takes the same value.

## Explanation

For every real exponent, $1^x=1$. Thus infinitely many exponents correspond to the argument $1$, and none correspond to other arguments. The relation does not define a logarithmic function.
