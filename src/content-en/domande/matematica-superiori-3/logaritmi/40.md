What is the domain of $\ln\sqrt{x-5}$?

- $(0,+\infty)$
- $(5,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,5)$
- $[5,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The square root exists when $x-5\geq0$. Its value, however, is the argument of $\ln$ and must be strictly positive. The condition $\sqrt{x-5}>0$ holds exactly when $x>5$. At $x=5$ the square root is defined and is zero, so the outer logarithm is undefined. The domain is $(5,+\infty)$. At $x=6$, by comparison, the argument of $\ln$ is $1$ and the result is zero.
