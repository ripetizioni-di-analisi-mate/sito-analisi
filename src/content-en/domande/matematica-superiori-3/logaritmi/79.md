The inverse of $f(x)=\log_2(\log_3 x)$ can be found by recovering the argument of the outer logarithm first. Which formula gives $f^{-1}(y)$ for $y\in\mathbb R$?

- $3^{2^y}$
- $6^y$
- $3^{2y}$
- $2^{3^y}$

## Explanation

From $y=\log_2(\log_3x)$ we first obtain $\log_3x=2^y$. Exponentiating next in base $3$ gives $x=3^{2^y}$. For every real $y$ this value exceeds $1$, as required by the domain of $f$.
