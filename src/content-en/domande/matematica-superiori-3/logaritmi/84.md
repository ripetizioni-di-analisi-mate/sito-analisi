Which step preserves the solutions of $\log_2(x-1)+\log_2(x-3)=0$?

- $(x-1)+(x-3)=1$ with $x>3$.
- $(x-1)(x-3)=1$ with $x>3$.
- $(x-1)(x-3)=0$ with $x>3$.
- $(x-1)(x-3)=1$ with $x\in\mathbb R$.

## Explanation

The common domain is $x>3$, because $x-3$ and $x-1$ must both be positive. On that domain, the sum is the logarithm of the product. Since $\log_2 1=0$, the equation is equivalent to $(x-1)(x-3)=1$ with $x>3$. Without the constraint, $x=2-\sqrt2$ would also appear, making both original arguments negative. That positive product would arise from two negative factors, where the logarithm product law would not apply.
