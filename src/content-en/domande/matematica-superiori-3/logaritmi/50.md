How does $\log_3 x$ change when its argument goes from $x$ to $3x$, for every $x>0$?

- It increases by $3$.
- It triples.
- It increases by $1$.
- It decreases by $1$.

## Explanation

For $x>0$ the product $3x$ has two positive factors. The product law gives $\log_3(3x)=\log_3 3+\log_3 x=1+\log_3 x$. The difference between the new value and the original one is always $1$. For example, going from $x=3$ to $3x=9$ changes the value from $1$ to $2$. The example separates the additive increase of the logarithm from the multiplication applied to its argument.
