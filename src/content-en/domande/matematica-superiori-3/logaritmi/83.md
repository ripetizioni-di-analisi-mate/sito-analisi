For which values of $k$ does $\ln(x^2+1)=k$ have a solution?

- $k>0$
- $k\leq0$
- $k\in\mathbb R$
- $k\geq0$

## Explanation

Since $x^2+1$ is always positive, rewrite the equation as $x^2+1=e^k$. This gives $x^2=e^k-1$, with at least one real solution when $e^k\geq1$. The exponential increases and $e^0=1$, so $k\geq0$. For $k=0$ only $x=0$ works, while for $k>0$ there are two opposite roots. The graph is symmetric because the arguments at $x$ and $-x$ are equal.
