Which formula for $3\ln x-2\ln y$ holds on the whole domain $x,y>0$?

- $\ln(x^3-y^2)$
- $\ln\dfrac{x^3}{y^2}$
- $\ln\dfrac{3x}{2y}$
- $\ln\dfrac{y^2}{x^3}$

## Explanation

Since $x$ and $y$ are positive, the power law gives $3\ln x=\ln(x^3)$ and $2\ln y=\ln(y^2)$. The difference becomes $\ln\dfrac{x^3}{y^2}$. The second term provides the denominator. The quotient is positive for every allowed pair, so the identity holds throughout the domain. At $x=2$ and $y=4$, the quotient is $\dfrac12$, agreeing with $3\ln2-2\ln4=-\ln2$.
