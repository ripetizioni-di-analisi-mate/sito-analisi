A model $M(t)=M_0q^t$, with $M_0>0$ and $q>0$, predicts $M(0)=40$ and $M(2)=360$. What is the factor $q$?

- $\dfrac13$
- $3$
- $6$
- $9$

## Explanation

At time zero, $q^0=1$, so $M_0=40$. After two periods the ratio to the initial value is $\dfrac{360}{40}=9$, giving $q^2=9$. Since the model requires $q>0$, we get $q=3$. The quantity goes from $40$ to $120$ in one period and from $120$ to $360$ in the second. The ratio $9$ measures growth over two periods, while $3$ is the factor for one period.
