For $x>0$ and $y>0$, which condition is equivalent to $\ln x+\ln y=0$?

- $x+y=1$
- $x=y$
- $xy=0$
- $xy=1$

## Explanation

For $x,y>0$, the sum is $\ln(xy)$. The natural logarithm is zero only at argument $e^0=1$, so the product must be $xy=1$. The condition is also sufficient because substituting $xy=1$ gives $\ln1=0$. For example, $x=2$ and $y=\dfrac12$ satisfy the equation even though the two numbers differ. Equivalently, the two variables are reciprocals because $xy=1$ is equivalent to $y=\dfrac1x$ when $x>0$.
