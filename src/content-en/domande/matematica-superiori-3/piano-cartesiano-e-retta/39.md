What is the slope of a line perpendicular to $y=-\dfrac{2}{3}x+5$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The slopes must multiply to $-1$. Dividing $-1$ by $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ gives $\dfrac{3}{2}$. The sign becomes positive because the given slope is negative and the product must be negative. Check the magnitude by multiplying the slopes to get $\left(-\dfrac23\right)\dfrac32=-1$. Changing just the sign would leave the product equal to $-\dfrac49$.
