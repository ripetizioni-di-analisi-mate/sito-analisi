The circles $x^2+y^2=4$ and $x^2+y^2=36$ bound an annulus. Which circle centred at the origin divides the annulus into two parts of equal area?

- $x^2+y^2=20$
- $x^2+y^2=16$
- $x^2+y^2=10$
- $x^2+y^2=32$

## Explanation

The two given circles are centred at the origin and have radii $2$ and $6$. Let $r$ be the radius of the required circle, with $2<r<6$. The inner part of the annulus lies between the circles of radii $2$ and $r$, so its area is $\pi r^2-4\pi=\pi(r^2-4)$. The outer part lies between the circles of radii $r$ and $6$, therefore its area is $\pi(36-r^2)$.

The two areas are equal if $r^2-4=36-r^2$, that is, if $r^2=20$. Therefore the required circle is $x^2+y^2=20$, with radius $2\sqrt5$. The equation $x^2+y^2=16$ corresponds to the radius $4$, the mean of the two radii; with this radius the inner part has area $12\pi$ and the outer part $20\pi$.
