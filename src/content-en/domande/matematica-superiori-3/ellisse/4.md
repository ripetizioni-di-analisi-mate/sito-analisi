At which points does the line $y=3$ meet $\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{25}=1$?

- $\left(-\dfrac45,3\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac45,3\right)$
- $\left(-\dfrac{12}{5},3\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{12}{5},3\right)$
- $(0,3)$ only
- $(-3,3)$ and $(3,3)$

## Explanation

Substituting the $y$-coordinate gives $\dfrac{x^2}{9}=1-\dfrac9{25}=\dfrac{16}{25}$. Taking square roots of $x^2=\dfrac{144}{25}$ gives the two opposite $x$-coordinates $\pm\dfrac{12}{5}$.
