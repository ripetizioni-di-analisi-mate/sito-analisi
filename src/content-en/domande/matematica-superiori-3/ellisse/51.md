What is the tangent to $\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{16}=1$ at its highest point?

- $x=4$
- $y=-4$
- $y=16$
- $y=4$

## Explanation

The highest point is $(0,4)$. The term in $x$ in the tangent formula is zero, leaving $\dfrac{4y}{16}=1$, or $y=4$.

Substituting $y=4$ gives $\dfrac{x^2}{9}+1=1$. This leaves only $x=0$, confirming the unique contact. A vertical line through $(0,4)$ would have equation $x=0$ and would also meet the lower vertex $(0,-4)$.
