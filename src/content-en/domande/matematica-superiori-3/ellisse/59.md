The transformation $(X,Y)=(2x,3y)$ maps the circle $x^2+y^2=1$ onto the ellipse $\dfrac{X^2}{4}+\dfrac{Y^2}{9}=1$ and every tangent to the circle onto a tangent to the ellipse. A tangent to the circle has slope $-1$. What is the slope of the corresponding tangent to the ellipse?

- $-\dfrac32$
- $-\dfrac23$
- $-1$
- $\dfrac32$

## Explanation

Let $P_1$ and $P_2$ be two points of the tangent to the circle. Since the slope is $-1$, the displacements between the two points satisfy $\Delta y=-\Delta x$. The transformation multiplies $x$-coordinates by $2$ and $y$-coordinates by $3$, so the displacements between the images are $\Delta X=2\Delta x$ and $\Delta Y=3\Delta y=-3\Delta x$. Therefore the slope of the transformed line is
$$
\frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta X}=\frac{-3\Delta x}{2\Delta x}=-\frac32 .
$$
The value $-\dfrac23$ is obtained by swapping the factors of the two coordinates.
