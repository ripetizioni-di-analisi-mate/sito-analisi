At which point does $y$ reach its maximum on $\dfrac{(x+3)^2}{36}+\dfrac{(y-2)^2}{9}=1$?

- $(-3,8)$
- $(3,5)$
- $(-3,5)$
- $(-3,-1)$

## Explanation

To maximise $y$, the vertical term must reach $1$. The horizontal term is then zero, giving $x=-3$. Take $y-2=3$, so $y=5$.

Since the horizontal term is non-negative, the square $(y-2)^2$ is at most $9$. Thus $-3\leq y-2\leq3$. The upper extreme is attained only when $x+3=0$, so the required point is $(-3,5)$.
