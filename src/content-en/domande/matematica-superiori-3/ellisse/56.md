The ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$ meets the line $y=\dfrac34x$. How many intersections are there?

- None.
- Infinitely many.
- Two.
- One.

## Explanation

Substituting into the curve gives $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{x^2}{16}=1$, so $x^2=8$. The two opposite roots give two distinct points.

The horizontal coordinates are $\pm2\sqrt2$, and each gives one vertical coordinate by multiplication by $\dfrac34$. The two points are opposite across the origin because the line passes through the centre and the ellipse has the same symmetry.
