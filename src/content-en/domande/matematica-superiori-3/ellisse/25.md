Both semi-axes of an ellipse are doubled. What happens to the eccentricity?

- It always becomes zero.
- It halves.
- It stays the same.
- It doubles.

## Explanation

The new value of $c$ is $\sqrt{(2a)^2-(2b)^2}=2c$. In $\dfrac{2c}{2a}$ the factor $2$ cancels, so the eccentricity stays the same.

Doubling gives a uniform scaling. For example, if $a=5$ and $c=4$, the transformed lengths are $a=10$ and $c=8$, so $\dfrac45=\dfrac8{10}$. Eccentricity measures a shape ratio rather than the size of the curve.
