How many points of $\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{4}=1$ have $y$-coordinate $-3$?

- Two.
- Four.
- None.
- One.

## Explanation

The vertical semi-axis measures $2$, so the lowest $y$-coordinate on the curve is $-2$. Since $-3<-2$, the line misses the ellipse.

Substitution confirms the geometric conclusion. It would give $\dfrac{x^2}{9}=1-\dfrac94=-\dfrac54$, which is impossible for a real square. The value $y=-2$ would instead give $x=0$, with just one intersection at the bottom vertex.
