When does $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{m}+\dfrac{(y+1)^2}{16}=1$ with $m>0$ have a horizontal major axis?

- When $m>16$.
- When $m<16$.
- When $m>4$.
- When $m=16$.

## Explanation

The two semi-axis lengths are $\sqrt m$ horizontally and $4$ vertically. The first is larger if $\sqrt m>4$, or $m>16$. If $m=16$, the curve is a circle.

For $0<m<16$, the horizontal extent $2\sqrt m$ is shorter than the vertical extent $8$. For $m>16$, the inequality reverses. The numbers being compared are the squares of the semi-axes, $m$ and $16$, both positive.
