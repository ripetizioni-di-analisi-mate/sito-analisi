What is the domain of the function representing the upper half of $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$?

- $\mathbb R$
- $[-4,4]$
- $[-3,3]$
- $(-4,4)$

## Explanation

The formula is $y=3\sqrt{1-\dfrac{x^2}{16}}$. Its radicand is non-negative when $x^2\leq16$. At the endpoints it is zero and the function is defined.

The condition $1-\dfrac{x^2}{16}\geq0$ is equivalent to $|x|\leq4$. This gives $[-4,4]$, with $(-4,0)$ and $(4,0)$ as the endpoints of the upper half. Outside that interval the radicand is negative, so no corresponding real vertical coordinate exists.
