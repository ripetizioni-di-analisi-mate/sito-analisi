For which $k$ does $(k-1)x^2=(k-1)$ hold for every real $x$?

- $1$
- $-1$
- For no value.
- $0$

## Explanation

For $k=1$ both sides are zero for every $x$. For $k\neq1$, dividing by $k-1$ gives $x^2=1$, which holds only for $x=\pm1$. Moving all terms to the left gives $(k-1)(x^2-1)=0$. For it to hold even at $x=0$, where the second factor is $-1$, we need $k-1=0$. With that parameter it is indeed an identity.
