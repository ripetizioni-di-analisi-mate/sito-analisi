Solve $\dfrac{x+5}{x+1}>1$.

- $(-1,+\infty)$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{-1\}$
- $(-\infty,-1)$
- $(-5,-1)$

## Explanation

Subtracting $1$ and using a common denominator gives $\dfrac4{x+1}>0$. The numerator is positive, so $x+1>0$ is required. In the domain, replace $1$ by $\dfrac{x+1}{x+1}$. The difference in the numerator is $(x+5)-(x+1)=4$. The value $x=-1$ is excluded, and to its left the quotient is negative.
