For which value of $a$ does the numerator of $\dfrac{ax-6}{x-3}$ have the same zero as the denominator?

- $3$
- $-2$
- $2$
- $6$

## Explanation

The denominator is zero at $3$, so the numerator must also equal zero at $3$. The equation $3a-6=0$ gives $a=2$. With $a=2$, the numerator is $2(x-3)$ and its zero is exactly $3$. For $x\neq3$, the fraction simplifies to $2$. However, the common zero remains excluded from the original fraction.
