For which $k$ is $(k+1)x^2-3x+2=0$ linear?

- $\dfrac18$
- $0$
- $1$
- $-1$

## Explanation

At $k=-1$ the quadratic term vanishes while the coefficient of $x$ is $-3$, which is nonzero. The remaining equation is the linear equation $-3x+2=0$. Find the value satisfying $k+1=0$. In that case $-3x+2=0$ gives $x=\dfrac23$. For every other $k$, the quadratic coefficient is nonzero, so the degree is two.
