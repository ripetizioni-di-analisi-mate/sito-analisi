Solve $\dfrac{1}{(x-2)^2}>0$.

- $\mathbb R$
- $(-\infty,2)$
- $\mathbb R\setminus\{2\}$
- $(2,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The numerator is positive and the square in the denominator is positive for every $x\neq2$. At $2$ the quotient is undefined. Both intervals on either side of $2$ are in the solution set. Two symmetric points, such as $x=1$ and $x=3$, both give denominator $1$ and hence the same sign. The zero of the denominator separates the rays without any change in sign.
