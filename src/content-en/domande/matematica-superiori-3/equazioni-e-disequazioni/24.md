Suppose $c<0$. What follows about $x^2+bx+c=0$ for every $b\in\mathbb R$?

- The two real roots have opposite signs.
- The discriminant is always negative.
- Both real roots are negative.
- Both real roots are positive.

## Explanation

Since $-4c>0$, the discriminant $b^2-4c$ is positive and the roots are distinct. Their product is $c<0$, so they have opposite signs.
