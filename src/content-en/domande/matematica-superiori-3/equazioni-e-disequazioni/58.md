For which value of $k$ is the solution set of $x^2-2x+k\leq0$ an interval of length $6$?

- $-5$
- $1$
- $-8$
- $10$

## Explanation

Completing the square, the inequality becomes $(x-1)^2\leq1-k$. If $1-k>0$, the solutions are the $x$ with $1-\sqrt{1-k}\leq x\leq1+\sqrt{1-k}$, so they form an interval of length $2\sqrt{1-k}$.

The length is $6$ if $\sqrt{1-k}=3$, that is, if $1-k=9$. Therefore $k=-8$, and indeed $x^2-2x-8=(x-4)(x+2)$ is less than or equal to zero on the interval $[-2,4]$. The value $-5$ is obtained by equating $1-k$ to the length $6$ instead of to the square of half the length.
