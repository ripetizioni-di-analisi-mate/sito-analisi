Solve $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{x+1}<0$.

- $(-1,2)$
- $(-\infty,-1)$
- $(2,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-1]\cup\{2\}$

## Explanation

For $x\neq2$, the numerator is positive. Thus the quotient is negative where $x+1<0$, which means $x<-1$. At $2$ the quotient is zero, and the inequality is strict.
