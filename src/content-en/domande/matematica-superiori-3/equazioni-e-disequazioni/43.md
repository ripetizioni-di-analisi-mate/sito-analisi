For which values of $x$ does $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{x+1}<0$ hold?

- $(-1,2)$
- $(-\infty,-1)$
- $(2,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-1]\cup\{2\}$

## Explanation

For $x\neq2$, the numerator is positive. Thus the quotient is negative where $x+1<0$, which means $x<-1$. At $2$ the quotient is zero, and the inequality is strict. At $x=-2$ the expression is $\dfrac{16}{-1}=-16$, so the left-hand branch is allowed. At $x=-1$ the fraction is undefined. To the right of that point the denominator is positive, so the fraction cannot be negative.
