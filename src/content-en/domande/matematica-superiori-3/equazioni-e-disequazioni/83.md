Which equation has two distinct roots that can be found without evaluating a discriminant?

- $(x-3)^2=-5$
- $(x-3)^2=5$
- $(x-3)^2=0$
- $x-3=5$

## Explanation

A square equal to $5>0$ allows two values for its base, $\sqrt5$ and $-\sqrt5$. The corresponding roots $3+\sqrt5$ and $3-\sqrt5$ are distinct.
