For which $k$ does $x^2+4x+k=0$ have two distinct real roots?

- $k=4$
- $k\leq4$
- $k>4$
- $k<4$

## Explanation

The discriminant is $16-4k=4(4-k)$. The roots are real and distinct when the discriminant is positive, which means $k<4$. The same result follows from $(x+2)^2=4-k$. A square has two distinct real bases when its value is positive. At $k=4$, the square is zero and there is only one root.
