Two functions satisfy $f(x)=2x+1$ on $\mathbb R$ and $g(x)=2x+1$ on $[0,+\infty)$. Which statement holds?

- $g$ is the restriction of $f$ to $[0,+\infty)$.
- The two functions have the same domain.
- $g$ is the inverse of $f$.
- $f$ is the restriction of $g$ to $\mathbb R$.

## Explanation

Each input allowed for $g$ is also allowed for $f$ and gives the same value. The domain of $g$ is a proper subset of the domain of $f$, so $g$ is the restriction. For example, $f(-1)=-1$, while $g$ is undefined at $-1$. The formulas agree on their common inputs. The restriction keeps each pair $(x,2x+1)$ with $x\geq0$ and removes the pairs with negative inputs.
