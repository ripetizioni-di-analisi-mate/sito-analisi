What is the real domain of $f(x)=\sqrt{\dfrac{1-x}{x+2}}$?

- $(-2,1]$
- $[-2,1]$
- $(-\infty,-2)\cup[1,+\infty)$
- $(-2,1)$

## Explanation

The denominator excludes $-2$. The quotient is positive on $(-2,1)$, where both factors are positive. At $x=1$ the quotient is zero, so the square root is also defined there. To the left of $-2$, the numerator is positive and the denominator negative. To the right of $1$, the numerator is negative and the denominator positive. Both outside regions therefore give a negative radicand, while $-2$ is excluded before taking the square root.
