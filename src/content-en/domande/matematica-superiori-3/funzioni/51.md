For $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x-1}$ on $\mathbb R\setminus\{1\}$, what is its inverse?

- $y-1$ for $y\neq0$
- $\dfrac{1}{y}-1$ for $y\neq0$
- $1+\dfrac{1}{y}$ for $y\neq0$
- $\dfrac{1}{y-1}$ for $y\neq1$

## Explanation

Since the numerator is $1$, all outputs are different from $0$. From $y(x-1)=1$, with $y$ different from $0$, we get $x-1=\dfrac{1}{y}$ and hence $x=1+\dfrac{1}{y}$, which is allowed for all $y\neq0$. The result can be checked by substituting the recovered input into $f$. This gives $\dfrac{1}{(1+\frac1y)-1}=\dfrac{1}{\frac1y}=y$. The input excluded by $f$ is $1$, while the output excluded from the range is $0$, so the domains differ.
