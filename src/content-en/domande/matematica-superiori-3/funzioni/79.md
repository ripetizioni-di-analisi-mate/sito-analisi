A function $f$ defined on $\mathbb R$ satisfies $f(f(x))=x$ for every real $x$, and $f(4)=-2$. Which other point certainly lies on the graph of $f$?

- $(-4,2)$
- $(2,-4)$
- $(-2,4)$
- $(-2,-2)$

## Explanation

Setting $x=4$ in the identity gives $f(f(4))=4$. Since $f(4)=-2$, this equality becomes $f(-2)=4$. Therefore the point $(-2,4)$ lies on the graph of $f$.

The identity says that $f$ coincides with its own inverse, so its graph is symmetric about the line $y=x$ and contains $(-2,4)$, the reflection of $(4,-2)$. The point $(2,-4)$ is obtained by changing the sign of the coordinates instead of swapping them.
