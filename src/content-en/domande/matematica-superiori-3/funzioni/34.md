Let $f$ be even and strictly increasing on $[0,+\infty)$. Which inequality always follows?

- $f(-3)<f(-2)$
- $f(0)>f(2)$
- $f(-1)>f(1)$
- $f(-3)>f(-2)$

## Explanation

Strict increase gives $f(3)>f(2)$. By evenness, these values equal $f(-3)$ and $f(-2)$, respectively. Compare the distances of the inputs from zero. The point $-3$ is farther from the origin than $-2$, so on the left branch its output is greater even though $-3<-2$.
