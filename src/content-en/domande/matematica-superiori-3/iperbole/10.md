Find the center of the hyperbola $x^2-y^2-6x-4y+1=0$.

- $(3,-2)$
- $(-3,2)$
- $(3,2)$
- $(6,-4)$

## Explanation

Write $x^2-6x=(x-3)^2-9$ and $-y^2-4y=-(y+2)^2+4$. This gives $(x-3)^2-(y+2)^2=4$, so the center is $(3,-2)$.

In the $y$ completion, the linear term belongs to the group $-(y^2+4y)$, so the square is $(y+2)^2$. At $(3,2)$ that square would equal $16$, whereas it must vanish at the center. The center gives zero on the left of the centered form.
