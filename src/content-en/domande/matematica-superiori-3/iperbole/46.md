What are the foci of $\dfrac{(y-2)^2}{9}-\dfrac{(x+1)^2}{16}=1$?

- $(4,2)$ and $(-6,2)$
- $(-1,5)$ and $(-1,-1)$
- $(1,7)$ and $(1,-3)$
- $(-1,7)$ and $(-1,-3)$

## Explanation

The center is $(-1,2)$ and the transverse axis is vertical. With $c=\sqrt{9+16}=5$, the foci have abscissa $-1$ and ordinates $2\pm5$.

The vertices have ordinates $2\pm3$, while the foci are farther from the center and have ordinates $2\pm5$. The points with ordinates $5$ and $-1$ are therefore the vertices. The positive term contains $y$ and fixes the common abscissa at $-1$.
