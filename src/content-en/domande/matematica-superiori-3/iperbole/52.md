What is the zero of $f(x)=\dfrac{2x-6}{x+1}$?

- $3$
- $-1$
- $6$
- $-3$

## Explanation

The numerator is zero when $2x-6=0$, hence when $x=3$. The denominator there is $4$, so $3$ is a zero.

At $(3,0)$ the ratio is $\dfrac{6-6}{4}=0$. The abscissa $-1$ instead makes the denominator zero, so the function is undefined there. Finding a zero means locating an intersection with the $x$-axis, where the ordinate must be zero.
