What is the abscissa of the right vertex of $\dfrac{(x+2)^2}{9}-\dfrac{(y-5)^2}{4}=1$?

- $3$
- $1$
- $-5$
- $5$

## Explanation

The center has abscissa $-2$ and the transverse semiaxis is $3$. The right vertex therefore has abscissa $-2+3=1$.

Setting $y=5$, the center’s ordinate, gives $(x+2)^2=9$. The abscissas are $1$ and $-5$, so the larger one belongs to the right vertex. The number $3$ measures the horizontal distance from the center and must be added to its abscissa.
