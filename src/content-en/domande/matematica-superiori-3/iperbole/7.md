Find the only common point of $x^2-y^2=4$ and $y=x+2$.

- $(2,0)$
- $(-2,0)$
- $(-2,-4)$
- $(0,2)$

## Explanation

Substituting $y=x+2$ gives $x^2-(x+2)^2=4$, or $-4x-4=4$. Hence $x=-2$ and $y=0$. The quadratic term cancels because the line is parallel to the asymptote $y=x$.

At the point found, the line gives $y=-2+2=0$ and the hyperbola gives $(-2)^2-0^2=4$. The other vertex $(2,0)$ satisfies the hyperbola but misses the line, which has ordinate $4$ at that abscissa. A single intersection from a linear equation is a simple root.
