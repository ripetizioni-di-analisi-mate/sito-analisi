Which expression, with negation written only in front of the individual letters, is equivalent to $\lnot \left(A \land \left(B \lor C\right)\right)$?

- $\lnot A \land \left(\lnot B \land \lnot C\right)$
- $\lnot A \land \left(\lnot B \lor \lnot C\right)$
- $\lnot A \lor \left(\lnot B \land \lnot C\right)$
- $\lnot A \lor \left(\lnot B \lor \lnot C\right)$

## Explanation

By **De Morgan's law** the negation of a conjunction is the disjunction of the negations, so $\lnot \left(A \land \left(B \lor C\right)\right)$ is equivalent to $\lnot A \lor \lnot \left(B \lor C\right)$. By the same law the negation of a disjunction is the conjunction of the negations, hence $\lnot \left(B \lor C\right)$ is equivalent to $\lnot B \land \lnot C$. The required expression is therefore $\lnot A \lor \left(\lnot B \land \lnot C\right)$. The answer $\lnot A \land \left(\lnot B \lor \lnot C\right)$ comes from negating the letters while leaving the connectives unchanged.
