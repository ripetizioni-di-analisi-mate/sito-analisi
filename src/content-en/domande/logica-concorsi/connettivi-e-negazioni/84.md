A company has the rule “If an employee works on Sunday, they receive a day off.” Which of the following statements has the same meaning as the rule? In the answers, “or” is inclusive.

- It never happens that an employee works on Sunday and does not receive a day off
- It never happens that an employee does not work on Sunday and receives a day off
- An employee does not work on Sunday or does not receive a day off
- If an employee does not receive a day off, they work on Sunday

## Explanation

The **implication** “if $A$, then $B$” is false in only one case, when $A$ is true and $B$ is false. Therefore stating the rule is equivalent to stating that this case never occurs, that is, that it never happens that an employee works on Sunday and does not receive the day off. The statement that excludes an employee who does not work on Sunday and receives the day off is instead equivalent to the converse “if they receive a day off, they work on Sunday.” The statement with “or” is false for an employee who works on Sunday and receives the day off, so it excludes exactly a case in which the rule is satisfied. The last statement is false for an employee who neither works on Sunday nor receives the day off, while the rule is true in that case.
