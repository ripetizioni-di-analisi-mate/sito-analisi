Today Ada is at the office and Bruno is not, while Carla's whereabouts are unknown. Which of the following statements is certainly true? In the answers, "or" is inclusive.

- Ada or Carla is at the office
- Ada and Carla are at the office
- Bruno or Carla is at the office
- If Carla is at the office, Bruno is there as well

## Explanation

Let $A$ = "Ada is at the office," which is true, $B$ = "Bruno is at the office," which is false, and $C$ = "Carla is at the office," whose value is unknown. The disjunction $A \lor C$ has a true part $A$, so it is **true** regardless of the value of $C$. The conjunction $A \land C$ and the disjunction $B \lor C$ have the same value as $C$, so neither is certainly true. The implication $C \Rightarrow B$ has a false consequent, so it is true only if Carla is not at the office.
