It is known that $P$ is a tautology, while the truth value of $Q$ is unknown. Which of the following propositions is certainly a tautology?

- $P \lor Q$
- $P \land Q$
- $P \Leftrightarrow Q$
- $Q \Rightarrow \lnot P$

## Explanation

Since $P$ is a tautology, $P$ is true in every case. A disjunction is true when at least one of its two parts is true, so $P \lor Q$ is true in every case, whatever the value of $Q$, and is a **tautology**. Since $P$ is always true, the conjunction $P \land Q$ and the biconditional $P \Leftrightarrow Q$ always have the same truth value as $Q$. Therefore these two propositions are false in the cases where $Q$ is false. The implication $Q \Rightarrow \lnot P$ has the consequent $\lnot P$ always false, so it is false in the cases where $Q$ is true.
