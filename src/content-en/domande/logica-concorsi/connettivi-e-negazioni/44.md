Which of the following propositions is a tautology?

- $\big( (A \Rightarrow B) \land \lnot B \big) \Rightarrow \lnot A$
- $\big( (A \Rightarrow B) \land B \big) \Rightarrow A$
- $\big( (A \Rightarrow B) \land \lnot A \big) \Rightarrow \lnot B$
- $(A \Rightarrow B) \Rightarrow (\lnot A \Rightarrow \lnot B)$

## Explanation

By **equivalence to the contrapositive**, $A \Rightarrow B$ is equivalent to $\lnot B \Rightarrow \lnot A$. When the antecedent $(A \Rightarrow B) \land \lnot B$ is true, $\lnot B \Rightarrow \lnot A$ and $\lnot B$ are true, so $\lnot A$ is also true. Thus the implication $\big( (A \Rightarrow B) \land \lnot B \big) \Rightarrow \lnot A$ is never false and is a **tautology**. The other three propositions are false when $A$ is false and $B$ is true because their antecedent is true and their consequent is false in that case.
