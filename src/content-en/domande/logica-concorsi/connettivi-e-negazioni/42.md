Which of the following propositions is a contradiction, that is, false in every case?

- $(A \Rightarrow B) \land \lnot B$
- $(A \lor B) \land \lnot A$
- $(A \Leftrightarrow B) \land (A \land \lnot B)$
- $A \Leftrightarrow \lnot B$

## Explanation

In proposition $(A \Leftrightarrow B) \land (A \land \lnot B)$ the second part is true only when $A$ is true and $B$ is false. In that case $A$ and $B$ have different values, so $A \Leftrightarrow B$ is false. The two parts are never both true, so their conjunction is a **contradiction**. Each of the other propositions is true in at least one case: $(A \Rightarrow B) \land \lnot B$ when $A$ and $B$ are false, $(A \lor B) \land \lnot A$ when $A$ is false and $B$ is true, and $A \Leftrightarrow \lnot B$ when $A$ is true and $B$ is false.
