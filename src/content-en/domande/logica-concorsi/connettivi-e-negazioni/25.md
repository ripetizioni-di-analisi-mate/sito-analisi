Which of the following statements is equivalent to "If the car has not passed inspection, it stays in the garage"? In the answers, "or" is inclusive.

- The car has not passed inspection or stays in the garage
- The car has passed inspection or stays in the garage
- The car has passed inspection and stays in the garage
- The car has not passed inspection and stays in the garage

## Explanation

Let $R$ = "The car has passed inspection" and $G$ = "The car stays in the garage," so the given statement is $\lnot R \Rightarrow G$. Expressing an **implication as a disjunction** gives the equivalent $\lnot \lnot R \lor G$, and double negation gives $R \lor G$. The statement "The car has not passed inspection or stays in the garage" is $\lnot R \lor G$, which is equivalent to "If the car has passed inspection, it stays in the garage," a different implication. Both conjunctions are false when the car has passed inspection and does not stay in the garage, whereas the given statement is true in that case.
