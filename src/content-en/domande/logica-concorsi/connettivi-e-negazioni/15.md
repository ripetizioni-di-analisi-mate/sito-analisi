The statements "It is not true that Elio and Fede are both present at the meeting" and "Elio or Fede is present at the meeting," where "or" is inclusive, are true. Which of the following statements must be true?

- Elio is present at the meeting
- Neither Elio nor Fede is present at the meeting
- Both Elio and Fede are present at the meeting
- Exactly one of Elio and Fede is present at the meeting

## Explanation

Let $E$ = "Elio is present" and $F$ = "Fede is present." By **De Morgan's laws**, the first statement is equivalent to $\lnot E \lor \lnot F$, so at least one of the two is absent. The second statement says that at least one of them is present. The two statements are both true exactly when one is present and the other is absent, so **exactly one** of them is present. "Elio is present at the meeting" need not be true because the two statements are also true when only Fede is present.
