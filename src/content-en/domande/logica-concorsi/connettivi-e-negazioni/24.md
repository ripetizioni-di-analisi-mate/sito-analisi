What is the negation of the statement "If Tiziana does not find a parking spot, she arrives late"?

- Tiziana does not find a parking spot and does not arrive late
- Tiziana finds a parking spot or arrives late
- Tiziana finds a parking spot and arrives late
- If Tiziana does not find a parking spot, she does not arrive late

## Explanation

Let $P$ = "Tiziana finds a parking spot" and $R$ = "Tiziana arrives late," so the given statement is $\lnot P \Rightarrow R$. To **negate an implication**, assert the antecedent and negate the consequent. This gives $\lnot P \land \lnot R$, that is, "Tiziana does not find a parking spot and does not arrive late." The statement "Tiziana finds a parking spot or arrives late" is $P \lor R$, which is equivalent to the given statement by the rule for expressing an implication as a disjunction. "Tiziana finds a parking spot and arrives late" negates the antecedent instead of the consequent.
