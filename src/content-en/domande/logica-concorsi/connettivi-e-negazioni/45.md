Consider the statements "If Marco is promoted, he celebrates" and "If Marco celebrates, he is promoted." In which of the following cases is the first statement true and the second false?

- Marco is promoted and celebrates
- Marco is not promoted and celebrates
- Marco is promoted and does not celebrate
- Marco is not promoted and does not celebrate

## Explanation

The second statement is an implication, so it is false only when its antecedent "Marco celebrates" is true and its consequent "Marco is promoted" is false. In this case the first statement has a false antecedent and is therefore true. If Marco is promoted and does not celebrate, the first statement is false and the second is true, so that case does not meet the requirement. Marco's celebrating without being promoted is a **counterexample** showing that the converse is not equivalent to the original implication.
