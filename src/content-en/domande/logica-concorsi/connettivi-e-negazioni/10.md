What is the negation of the statement "Ugo has a degree and knows at least one of the two languages, English and French"?

- Ugo does not have a degree and knows neither English nor French
- Ugo does not have a degree or knows neither English nor French
- Ugo does not have a degree or does not know English or does not know French
- Ugo has a degree and knows neither English nor French

## Explanation

Let $L$ = "Ugo has a degree," $I$ = "Ugo knows English," and $F$ = "Ugo knows French," so the given statement is $L \land (I \lor F)$. By **De Morgan's laws**, the negation of a conjunction is the disjunction of the negations, and the negation of $I \lor F$ is $\lnot I \land \lnot F$. Therefore
$$
\lnot \big( L \land (I \lor F) \big) \equiv \lnot L \lor (\lnot I \land \lnot F) .
$$
The statement "Ugo does not have a degree or does not know English or does not know French" is true even when Ugo has a degree and knows only English, a case in which the given statement is true. Consequently, it is not the negation of the given statement.
