Given the implication "if $A$, then $B$," consider its converse, inverse, and contrapositive. Which of the following pairs consists of two equivalent statements, whatever $A$ and $B$ are?

- The implication and its converse
- The converse and the contrapositive
- The inverse and the contrapositive
- The converse and the inverse

## Explanation

The converse is $B \Rightarrow A$ and the inverse is $\lnot A \Rightarrow \lnot B$. The inverse exchanges the antecedent and the consequent of the converse and negates them, so it is the contrapositive of the converse. By **equivalence to the contrapositive**, the converse and inverse are therefore equivalent. The original implication and its converse have different values when $A$ is true and $B$ is false because the first is false and the second is true. The contrapositive is equivalent to the original implication, so it differs from the converse and inverse in this same case.
