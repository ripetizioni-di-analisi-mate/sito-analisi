Which of the following statements is *not* equivalent to "If the technician arrives, the boiler is repaired"?

- The technician does not arrive or the boiler is repaired
- If the boiler is not repaired, the technician does not arrive
- It is not true that the technician arrives and the boiler is not repaired
- If the technician does not arrive, the boiler is not repaired

## Explanation

Let $T$ = "The technician arrives" and $C$ = "The boiler is repaired," so the given statement is $T \Rightarrow C$. Expressing the **implication as a disjunction** gives the equivalent $\lnot T \lor C$, and **equivalence to the contrapositive** gives $\lnot C \Rightarrow \lnot T$. The statement $\lnot (T \land \lnot C)$ negates the negation of $T \Rightarrow C$, so it too is equivalent to the given statement. "If the technician does not arrive, the boiler is not repaired" is the inverse, which is false when the technician does not arrive and the boiler is repaired anyway, a case in which the given statement is true.
