Which proposition must replace $X$ for $(A \land B) \lor X$ to be a tautology?

- $\lnot A \land \lnot B$
- $\lnot A$
- $A \lor B$
- $\lnot A \lor \lnot B$

## Explanation

The disjunction $(A \land B) \lor X$ is false only in the cases where both $A \land B$ and $X$ are false. Therefore the disjunction is a tautology exactly when $X$ is true in every case in which $A \land B$ is false, that is, when at least one of $A$ and $B$ is false. The proposition $\lnot A \lor \lnot B$ is true precisely in these cases, so with $X = \lnot A \lor \lnot B$ the disjunction is a **tautology**. By **De Morgan's laws** the same disjunction can be written $(A \land B) \lor \lnot (A \land B)$, that is, the disjunction of $A \land B$ with its negation. With $X = \lnot A$ the disjunction is false when $A$ is true and $B$ is false, with $X = A \lor B$ when $A$ and $B$ are both false, and with $X = \lnot A \land \lnot B$ when exactly one of the two is true.
