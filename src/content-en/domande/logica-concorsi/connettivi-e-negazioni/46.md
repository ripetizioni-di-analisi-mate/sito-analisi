Which expression separates the two consequences of the rule $A \Rightarrow \left(B \land C\right)$ without changing its meaning?

- $\left(B \land C\right) \Rightarrow A$
- $\left(B \Rightarrow A\right) \land \left(C \Rightarrow A\right)$
- $\left(A \Rightarrow B\right) \land \left(A \Rightarrow C\right)$
- $\left(A \Rightarrow B\right) \lor \left(A \Rightarrow C\right)$

## Explanation

When $A$ is true, the rule requires both $B$ and $C$. Therefore both implications with antecedent $A$ must hold.
