What is the negation of "The gym is open if and only if an instructor is present"?

- The gym is not open and no instructor is present
- Either the gym is open or an instructor is present, but not both
- The gym is not open if and only if no instructor is present
- The gym is open and no instructor is present

## Explanation

A **biconditional** is false when its two parts have different truth values, that is, when only one of them is true. The negation of the given statement asserts exactly this, so it is the **exclusive disjunction** "Either the gym is open or an instructor is present, but not both." The statement "The gym is open and no instructor is present" describes only one of the two cases in which the given statement is false. The statement with "if and only if" and two negations is true exactly when the two parts have the same value, so it is equivalent to the given statement. "The gym is not open and no instructor is present" describes a case in which the given statement is true.
