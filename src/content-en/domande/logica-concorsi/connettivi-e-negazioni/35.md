What is the negation of "Olga's signing is sufficient for the contract to be valid"?

- Olga does not sign and the contract is valid
- If Olga signs, the contract is not valid
- Olga's not signing is sufficient for the contract to be invalid
- Olga signs and the contract is not valid

## Explanation

The expression "$A$ is sufficient for $B$" means $A \Rightarrow B$, so the given statement is "If Olga signs, the contract is valid." By the rule for **negating an implication**, its negation is "Olga signs and the contract is not valid." The statements "If Olga signs, the contract is not valid" and "Olga's not signing is sufficient for the contract to be invalid" are implications. Both can be true when Olga does not sign and the contract is invalid, but the required conjunction is false in that case. "Olga does not sign and the contract is valid" describes a case in which the given statement is true because its antecedent is false.
