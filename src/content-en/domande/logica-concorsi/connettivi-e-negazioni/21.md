Which of the following statements is equivalent to "The fire door is closed if and only if the alarm is off"?

- The fire door is closed and the alarm is off
- Either the fire door is closed or the alarm is off, but not both
- If the fire door is closed, the alarm is off, and if the alarm is off, the fire door is closed
- If the fire door is closed, the alarm is off

## Explanation

Let $P$ = "The fire door is closed" and $S$ = "The alarm is off." The **biconditional** $P \Leftrightarrow S$ is equivalent to the two implications $P \Rightarrow S$ and $S \Rightarrow P$ taken together, which is precisely what the statement with the two implications asserts. "If the fire door is closed, the alarm is off" contains only one of the two implications and is true even with the door open and the alarm off, a case in which the given statement is false. Exclusive "or" has the opposite truth value from the biconditional in every case, so it is its negation. The conjunction is false with the door open and the alarm on, whereas the given statement is true in that case.
