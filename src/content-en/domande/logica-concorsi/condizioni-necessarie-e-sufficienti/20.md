Which statement has the same meaning as “Luca goes to the gym unless he has to work overtime”?

- If Luca does not have to work overtime, he goes to the gym.
- If Luca has to work overtime, he does not go to the gym.
- If Luca does not go to the gym, he does not have to work overtime.
- Luca goes to the gym if and only if he does not have to work overtime.

## Explanation

“$A$ unless $B$” translates as “if not $B$, then $A$.” In the Italian expression “a meno che non,” the word “non” does not negate the condition. With $A$ = “Luca goes to the gym” and $B$ = “Luca has to work overtime,” we get “if Luca does not have to work overtime, he goes to the gym.” The original says nothing about days when he works overtime. Therefore the statement beginning “if Luca has to work overtime” and the one containing “if and only if” both add information. The statement beginning “if Luca does not go to the gym” is equivalent to its own contrapositive, “if Luca has to work overtime, he goes to the gym,” a different implication.
