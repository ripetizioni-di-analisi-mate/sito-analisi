In an association, which statement says that paying dues is necessary and sufficient for having the right to vote?

- “Only members who have paid their dues have the right to vote.”
- “All members who have paid their dues have the right to vote.”
- “Members who have paid their dues have the right to vote, and those without the right to vote have not paid their dues.”
- “All and only those members who have paid their dues have the right to vote.”

## Explanation

A necessary and sufficient condition requires the two implications “if a member has paid, they have the right to vote” and “if a member has the right to vote, they have paid.” In “all and only,” the word *all* gives the first implication and *only* the second. The statement with “only” alone gives just the second implication, and the one with “all” alone gives just the first. In the statement with two parts joined by “and,” the second part is the contrapositive of the first, so it says the same thing. That statement says only that paying is a **sufficient condition**.
