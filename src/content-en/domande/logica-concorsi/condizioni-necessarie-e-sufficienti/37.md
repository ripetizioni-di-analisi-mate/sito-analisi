The statements “Valerio goes to the concert only if he has a ticket” and “Valerio has a ticket only if he buys it by Monday” are true. Which statement must be true?

- If Valerio buys the ticket by Monday, he goes to the concert.
- If Valerio has a ticket, he goes to the concert.
- If Valerio does not go to the concert, he did not buy the ticket by Monday.
- If Valerio does not buy the ticket by Monday, he does not go to the concert.

## Explanation

Each “only if” introduces a **necessary condition**, so the premises translate as “if Valerio goes to the concert, he has a ticket” and “if Valerio has a ticket, he bought it by Monday.” By the **transitive property**, “if Valerio goes to the concert, he bought the ticket by Monday.” Its contrapositive is the statement beginning “if Valerio does not buy the ticket by Monday.” The statements beginning “if Valerio buys the ticket” and “if Valerio has a ticket” treat two conditions that are only necessary as sufficient. The statement beginning “if Valerio does not go to the concert” is the inverse of the conclusion.
