A rail company's policy states, “A delay exceeding $60$ minutes is enough to entitle a passenger to a refund.” Elena's train arrived $40$ minutes late. What can we conclude from the policy?

- Elena is entitled to a refund.
- Whether Elena is entitled to a refund cannot be determined.
- Elena is not entitled to a refund.
- Elena would be entitled to a refund only for a delay exceeding $60$ minutes.

## Explanation

“$A$ is enough for $B$” makes $A$ a **sufficient condition** for $B$, so the policy translates as “if the delay exceeds $60$ minutes, the passenger is entitled to a refund.” For Elena the antecedent is false, and the policy says nothing about shorter delays. Other company rules might allow a refund even for a $40$-minute delay, so the given policy does not determine whether Elena is entitled to one. Concluding that she is not entitled to a refund is the error of **denying the antecedent**. The statement containing “only for a delay exceeding $60$ minutes” treats as necessary a condition that the policy gives as sufficient.
