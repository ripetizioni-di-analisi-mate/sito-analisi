Which statement has the same meaning as “Whenever the director is on site, the meeting takes place in person”?

- If the meeting takes place in person, the director is on site.
- If the meeting does not take place in person, the director is not on site.
- If the director is not on site, the meeting does not take place in person.
- The meeting takes place in person if and only if the director is on site.

## Explanation

“Whenever $A$, $B$” translates as “if $A$, then $B$,” with $A$ = “the director is on site” and $B$ = “the meeting takes place in person.” The implication is equivalent to its **contrapositive**, “if not $B$, then not $A$,” which is the statement beginning “if the meeting does not take place in person.” The statement beginning “if the meeting takes place in person” is the converse, and the one beginning “if the director is not on site” is the inverse. Neither is equivalent to the given statement. “If and only if” adds the converse to the implication.
