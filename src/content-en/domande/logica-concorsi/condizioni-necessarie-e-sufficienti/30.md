Consider this argument: “If a car has an electric motor, it is exempt from the downtown entry charge. Bruno's car is exempt from the downtown entry charge. Therefore, Bruno's car has an electric motor.” Which assessment is correct?

- The argument is valid by modus ponens.
- The argument is valid by modus tollens.
- The argument is invalid because it affirms the consequent.
- The argument is invalid because it denies the antecedent.

## Explanation

The antecedent of the premise is “the car has an electric motor,” and its consequent is “the car is exempt from the downtown entry charge.” The second fact says the consequent is true for Bruno's car, and the argument infers the antecedent. This is **affirming the consequent**, an invalid deduction: Bruno's car might be exempt for another reason, for example because it is an emergency vehicle. Denying the antecedent would start from “the car does not have an electric motor,” which does not appear in the argument.
