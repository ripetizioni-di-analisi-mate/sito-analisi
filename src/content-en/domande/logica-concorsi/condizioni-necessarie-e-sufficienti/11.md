For a positive integer $n$ with at least two digits, what kind of condition is “$n$ is a multiple of $4$” for “the number formed by the last two digits of $n$ is a multiple of $4$”?

- sufficient but not necessary
- necessary but not sufficient
- neither necessary nor sufficient
- necessary and sufficient

## Explanation

Let $r$ be the number formed by the last two digits of $n$ and let $k$ be the number formed by the preceding digits, or zero if there are none. Then $n = 100k + r$. Since $100 = 4 \cdot 25$ is a multiple of $4$, the numbers $n$ and $r$ differ by a multiple of $4$. Thus $n$ is a multiple of $4$ whenever $r$ is, and $r$ is a multiple of $4$ whenever $n$ is. Both implications hold, so the condition is **necessary and sufficient**. For example, $316$ and $16$ are both multiples of $4$, whereas $318$ and $18$ are not.
