Which of the following statements about a positive integer $n$ is true?

- “If $n$ is a multiple of $3$, then $n$ is a multiple of $9$.”
- “If the sum of the digits of $n$ is a multiple of $3$, then $n$ is a multiple of $9$.”
- “If $n$ is a multiple of $9$, then the sum of the digits of $n$ is a multiple of $3$.”
- “If $n$ is a multiple of $3$, then the units digit of $n$ is $3$, $6$, or $9$.”

## Explanation

A number is a multiple of $9$ if and only if the sum of its digits is a multiple of $9$. So if $n$ is a multiple of $9$, its digit sum is a multiple of $9$ and is therefore also a multiple of $3$. The number $12$ is a multiple of $3$, its digit sum is $3$, and $12$ is not a multiple of $9$ and has units digit $2$. Thus $12$ is a **counterexample** to each of the other three implications.
