Use the following definitions. A rhombus is a quadrilateral with four equal sides, a rectangle is a quadrilateral with four right angles, and a square is a quadrilateral that is both a rhombus and a rectangle. For a quadrilateral, in which pair is the first statement a necessary but not sufficient condition for the second?

- “it is a square” and “it is a rectangle”
- “it has four equal sides” and “it is a rhombus”
- “it is a rectangle” and “it is a rhombus”
- “it is a rhombus” and “it is a square”

## Explanation

By definition, every square is also a rhombus, so “it is a rhombus” is **necessary** for “it is a square.” A rhombus with angles of $60°$ and $120°$ does not have four right angles, so it is not a square and the condition is not sufficient. In the square–rectangle pair, the first statement is sufficient: the implication goes in the opposite direction. Having four equal sides is the definition of a rhombus, so in that pair the condition is necessary and sufficient. Finally, “it is a rectangle” is neither necessary nor sufficient for “it is a rhombus”: a rectangle with side lengths $2$ and $5$ is not a rhombus, and a rhombus with angles of $60°$ and $120°$ is not a rectangle.
