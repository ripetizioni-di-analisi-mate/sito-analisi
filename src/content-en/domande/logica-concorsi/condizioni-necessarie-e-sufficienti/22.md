An association's bylaws state, “Membership is required to vote at a meeting.” Which statement follows from the bylaws?

- Being a member is sufficient for voting at a meeting.
- Being a nonmember is necessary for not voting at a meeting.
- Being a nonmember is sufficient for not voting at a meeting.
- Being a member is necessary and sufficient for voting at a meeting.

## Explanation

The bylaws make membership a **necessary condition** for voting, which translates as “if someone votes at a meeting, then they are a member.” Its **contrapositive** is “if someone is not a member, then they do not vote at a meeting.” Here the antecedent “being a nonmember” is sufficient for the consequent “not voting.” Making membership sufficient gives the converse, “if someone is a member, then they vote,” which the bylaws do not state. Making nonmembership necessary gives “if someone does not vote, then they are not a member.” This is the contrapositive of the converse and does not follow for the same reason. A necessary and sufficient condition would require both implications.
