Among plane figures, consider “triangles,” “squares,” and “red figures.” Which description corresponds to the Venn diagram for these three categories?

- The three circles overlap pairwise, with a region common to all three
- The circles for triangles and squares are separate, and the circle for red figures overlaps both and extends outside both
- The circles for triangles and squares are separate, and the circle for red figures overlaps the circle for triangles but is separate from the circle for squares
- The circles for triangles and squares are separate and both lie inside the circle for red figures

## Explanation

A figure cannot be both a triangle and a square, so their circles are **disjoint**. There are red triangles and red squares, so the circle for red figures overlaps both. There are also triangles and squares of other colors, as well as red hexagons, so the circle for red figures contains neither of the other two and extends outside both. A diagram with a region common to all three would represent figures that are both triangles and squares.
