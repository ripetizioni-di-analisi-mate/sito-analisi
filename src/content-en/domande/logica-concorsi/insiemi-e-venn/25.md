Among the positive integers, consider “prime numbers,” “odd numbers,” and “multiples of $9$.” Which description corresponds to the Venn diagram for the three categories?

- The circle for multiples of $9$ lies inside the circle for odd numbers, and the circle for primes overlaps the one for odd numbers but is separate from the one for multiples of $9$
- The three circles overlap pairwise, with a region common to all three
- The circles for primes and for multiples of $9$ are separate, and the circle for odd numbers overlaps part of each
- The circle for primes lies inside the circle for odd numbers, and the circle for multiples of $9$ overlaps the one for odd numbers but is separate from the one for primes

## Explanation

Every multiple of $9$ can be written as $9k = 3 \cdot 3k$, so it is divisible by $3$ and greater than $3$ and is therefore not prime. The circles for primes and for multiples of $9$ are thus **disjoint**. The number $2$ is prime and even, while $3$ is prime and odd, so the circle for primes overlaps the one for odd numbers without being contained in it. Similarly, $18$ is a multiple of $9$ and is even, while $9$ is a multiple of $9$ and is odd. The two descriptions with a circle inside the one for odd numbers overlook either the even prime $2$ or the even multiples of $9$.
