In a group of $60$ tourists, $42$ visited the museum and $35$ visited the castle. What is the smallest possible number who visited only the museum?

- $7$
- $17$
- $35$
- $25$

## Explanation

Let $M$ and $C$ be the sets of tourists who visited the museum and the castle. The number who visited only the museum is $42 - |M \cap C|$, so it is smallest when the intersection is largest. The intersection has at most $35$ elements, attained when everyone who visited the castle also visited the museum. The required minimum is therefore $42 - 35 = 7$. The number $25$ is obtained with the smallest intersection, $42 + 35 - 60 = 17$, and is the largest possible number who visited only the museum. The numbers $17$ and $35$ are the smallest and largest sizes of the intersection, that is, the number who visited both places.
