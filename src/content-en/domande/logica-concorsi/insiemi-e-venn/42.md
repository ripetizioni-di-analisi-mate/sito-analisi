In a group of $30$ people, $18$ speak English and $16$ speak German. The number who speak neither is unknown. Which of the following cannot be the number who speak both languages?

- $4$
- $10$
- $16$
- $3$

## Explanation

Let $I$ and $T$ be the sets of English and German speakers. At most $30$ people speak at least one of the two languages, so $18 + 16 - |I \cap T| \leq 30$, or equivalently $|I \cap T| \geq 4$. All those who speak both are among the $16$ German speakers, so $|I \cap T| \leq 16$. Every integer from $4$ to $16$ is possible. If $3$ people spoke both, however, $18 + 16 - 3 = 31$ would speak at least one language, more than the $30$ people in the group.
