Consider the categories “vehicles,” “bicycles,” and “blue objects.” An object can belong or fail to belong to each of the three categories. There are seven membership combinations in which it belongs to at least one category. How many of the seven combinations correspond to objects that actually exist?

- $4$
- $6$
- $7$
- $5$

## Explanation

Every bicycle is a vehicle, so the two combinations in which an object is a bicycle but not a vehicle are impossible, whether it is blue or some other color. Examples of the other five combinations are a blue bicycle, a red bicycle, a blue car, a red car, and a blue pen. Thus, in the Venn diagram, the circle for bicycles lies inside the one for vehicles, leaving $5$ nonempty regions inside the circles. The number $6$ comes from ruling out only a blue bicycle that is not a vehicle, without also ruling out bicycles of other colors that are not vehicles.
