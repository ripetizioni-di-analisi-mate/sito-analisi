The shop opens at 10\:15. A photograph of its clock is edited by swapping the two hands without changing their directions. Why can the new photograph not show a whole-minute time on an ordinary clock?

- The new hour hand can point exactly at $3$ only at noon
- Two hands can never form an obtuse angle
- The long hand necessarily points exactly at $10$ after the swap
- The new minute hand falls between two minute marks

## Explanation

At 10\:15 the hour hand is at $300^\circ+7.5^\circ=307.5^\circ$. After the swap that is where the minute hand points. Minute marks are $6^\circ$ apart, and $307.5$ is not a multiple of $6$.
