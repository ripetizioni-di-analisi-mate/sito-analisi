At noon the hands of a clock coincide. After how many minutes do they coincide for the third time, not counting the coincidence at noon?

- $196 + \dfrac{4}{11}$ minutes
- $130 + \dfrac{10}{11}$ minutes
- $261 + \dfrac{9}{11}$ minutes
- $166 + \dfrac{2}{13}$ minutes

## Explanation

The minute hand covers $6°$ per minute and the hour hand $0.5°$, so the minute hand gains $5.5°$ per minute on the hour hand. Two consecutive **coincidences** are separated by the time the minute hand needs to gain a full turn, that is $\dfrac{360}{5.5} = \dfrac{720}{11}$ minutes. Therefore the third coincidence after noon occurs after
$$
3 \cdot \frac{720}{11} = \frac{2160}{11} = 196 + \frac{4}{11} \ \text{minutes},
$$
shortly after 15\:16. The value $130 + \dfrac{10}{11}$ minutes counts the coincidence at noon as the first one, and $261 + \dfrac{9}{11}$ minutes corresponds to the fourth coincidence. The value $166 + \dfrac{2}{13}$ minutes comes from adding the speeds of the two hands instead of subtracting them.
