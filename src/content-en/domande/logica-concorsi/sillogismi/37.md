The letters $A$, $B$, and $C$ stand for any three categories. Which argument is *not* valid?

- All $A$ are $B$; no $B$ is $C$; therefore, no $A$ is $C$
- Some $A$ are $B$; all $B$ are $C$; therefore, some $A$ are $C$
- All $A$ are $B$; some $B$ are $C$; therefore, some $A$ are $C$
- All $A$ are $B$; some $C$ are not $B$; therefore, some $C$ are not $A$

## Explanation

In the argument that starts “All $A$ are $B$; some $B$ are $C$,” the member common to $B$ and $C$ can lie outside $A$, so the conclusion “some $A$ are $C$” does not follow. For a **counterexample**, let $A$ be cats, $B$ mammals, and $C$ dogs. All cats are mammals and some mammals are dogs, but no cat is a dog. The other three arguments are valid. In the one with “some $C$ are not $B$,” for example, the member of $C$ outside $B$ is also outside $A$ because $A$ is contained in $B$.
