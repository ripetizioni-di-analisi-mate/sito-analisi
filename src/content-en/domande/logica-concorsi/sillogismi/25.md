Consider the argument “All fish can fly; all animals that can fly have feathers; therefore, all fish have feathers.” Which of the following is true?

- The argument is invalid because its premises are false
- The argument is invalid because its conclusion is false
- The argument is invalid because the middle term appears in the conclusion
- The argument is valid even though its premises are false

## Explanation

A syllogism is **valid** if its conclusion is true in every situation in which its premises are true. This argument has the form “All $A$ are $B$, all $B$ are $C$, so all $A$ are $C$,” which is valid for any choice of categories. Validity concerns the connection between the premises and conclusion, so the fact that the premises and conclusion are false in the real world does not change it. The middle term is the category of animals that can fly, which does not appear in the conclusion.
