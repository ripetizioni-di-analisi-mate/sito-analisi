The argument “All $A$ are $B$; all $C$ are $B$; therefore, all $A$ are $C$” is invalid. Which choice of categories among the positive integers provides a counterexample, making the premises true and the conclusion false?

- $A$ multiples of $4$, $B$ even numbers, $C$ multiples of $2$
- $A$ multiples of $4$, $B$ even numbers, $C$ multiples of $6$
- $A$ multiples of $6$, $B$ multiples of $3$, $C$ even numbers
- $A$ multiples of $3$, $B$ even numbers, $C$ multiples of $4$

## Explanation

If $A$ is the multiples of $4$, $B$ the even numbers, and $C$ the multiples of $6$, the premises are true because multiples of $4$ and multiples of $6$ are even. The conclusion is false because $4$ is a multiple of $4$ but not a multiple of $6$, so this choice is a **counterexample**. With $C$ equal to the multiples of $2$, the conclusion is true, so that choice is not a counterexample. In each of the other two choices a premise is false, because $2$ is even but not a multiple of $3$, and $3$ is an odd multiple of $3$.
