At a pharmacy, “All over-the-counter medications are displayed on the shelves” and “No prescription medication is displayed on the shelves” are true. Which conclusion necessarily follows?

- All medications displayed on the shelves are over-the-counter medications
- Some over-the-counter medications require a prescription
- All medications not displayed on the shelves require a prescription
- No prescription medication is an over-the-counter medication

## Explanation

The first premise places over-the-counter medications in the category of medications on display and the second places prescription medications outside it. A prescription medication is therefore not displayed on the shelves. Since every over-the-counter medication is displayed, a prescription medication cannot be over the counter. Thus, “No prescription medication is an over-the-counter medication” follows. “All medications displayed on the shelves are over-the-counter medications” reverses the first premise, which does not exclude other medications available without a prescription from the shelves.
