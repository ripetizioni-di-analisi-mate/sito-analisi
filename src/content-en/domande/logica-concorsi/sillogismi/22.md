There are $12$ books on a shelf. Consider “All the books on the shelf are bound,” “No book on the shelf is bound,” “Some books on the shelf are bound,” and “Some books on the shelf are not bound.” How many of these four statements are true?

- One or two, depending on the number of bound books
- Two or three, depending on the number of bound books
- Exactly one, regardless of the number of bound books
- Exactly two, regardless of the number of bound books

## Explanation

The four statements form two pairs of **contradictories**. The statement with “all” pairs with the one containing “some … are not,” and the statement with “no” pairs with the affirmative statement containing “some.” In each pair of contradictories, one statement is true and the other false, so exactly two statements are true. For example, with $12$ bound books, the statements with “all” and with “some … are” are true. With $5$ bound books, both statements containing “some” are true.
