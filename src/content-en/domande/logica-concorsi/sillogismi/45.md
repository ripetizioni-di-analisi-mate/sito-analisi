Which pair of premises allows us to conclude “Some employees do not have degrees”?

- “Some employees work part-time” and “No part-time worker has a degree”
- “Some employees do not work part-time” and “All part-time workers have degrees”
- “No employee works part-time” and “All part-time workers have degrees”
- “Some employees work part-time” and “Some part-time workers do not have degrees”

## Explanation

With the premises “Some employees work part-time” and “No part-time worker has a degree,” there is a part-time employee, and this employee does not have a degree, so the conclusion follows. With “Some part-time workers do not have degrees,” the part-time worker without a degree might not be an employee, because **two particular premises** do not yield a statement connecting employees and graduates. In the two pairs containing “All part-time workers have degrees,” the second premise concerns only part-time workers, so every employee who does not work part-time could have a degree.
