At a transportation company, “All the company’s drivers have a category D driver’s license” is true. Mario does not have a category D license, while Sergio does. Which of the following must be true?

- Mario is not a driver for the company
- Sergio is a driver for the company
- Sergio is not a driver for the company
- Neither Mario nor Sergio is a driver for the company

## Explanation

If Mario were a driver for the company, the premise tells us he would have a category D license. Since he does not have that license, he is not a driver for the company. For Sergio the information is insufficient, because the premise says the drivers have category D licenses, without saying that everyone with that license is a driver. Concluding that “Sergio is a driver for the company” is the fallacy of **affirming the consequent**. The opposite conclusion about Sergio does not follow either.
