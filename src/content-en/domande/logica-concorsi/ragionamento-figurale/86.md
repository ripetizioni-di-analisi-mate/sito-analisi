The four corner squares are removed from a square grid four unit squares on each side, leaving the edges they shared with the remaining part. How many squares can be counted in the drawing?

::figura{tipo="linee" segmenti="1,3,1,5;1,3,3,3;1,5,1,7;1,5,3,5;1,7,3,7;3,1,3,3;3,1,5,1;3,3,3,5;3,3,5,3;3,5,3,7;3,5,5,5;3,7,3,9;3,7,5,7;3,9,5,9;5,1,5,3;5,1,7,1;5,3,5,5;5,3,7,3;5,5,5,7;5,5,7,5;5,7,5,9;5,7,7,7;5,9,7,9;7,1,7,3;7,3,7,5;7,3,9,3;7,5,7,7;7,5,9,5;7,7,7,9;7,7,9,7;9,3,9,5;9,5,9,7" titolo="Grid showing the segments to count"}

- $16$
- $12$
- $21$
- $17$

## Explanation

Twelve unit squares remain. Of the original nine squares of side length two, each of the four corner ones loses part of its outline, so five remain. Every square of side length three or four loses at least one corner. The total is therefore $12 + 5 = 17$.
