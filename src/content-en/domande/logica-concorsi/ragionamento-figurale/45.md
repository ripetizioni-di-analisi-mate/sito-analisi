The figure shows a grid of $3 \times 3$ equal small squares with the top right square removed. How many squares of any size can be traced along the lines of the figure?

::figura{tipo="linee" segmenti="1,1,7,1;1,4,10,4;1,7,10,7;1,10,10,10;1,1,1,10;4,1,4,10;7,1,7,10;10,4,10,10" titolo="The grid without its top right square"}

- $8$
- $11$
- $14$
- $12$

## Explanation

Measure side lengths in grid units. The squares of side length $1$ are the remaining individual grid squares, $9 - 1 = 8$ in total. For side length $2$ the complete grid has $4$ squares. Exactly one contains the removed square, so the number of squares of side length $2$ is $3$. The square of side length $3$ would be the entire grid, which is no longer a square because a corner is missing. The total number of squares is therefore $8 + 3 = 11$. Counting only the small squares gives $8$, using the sum of squares for the complete grid gives $14$, and counting all four squares of side length $2$ gives $12$.
