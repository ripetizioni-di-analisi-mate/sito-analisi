The figure shows a triangle with segments joining the midpoints of its sides and a segment from its top vertex to the midpoint of the base. How many triangles are there?

::figura{tipo="linee" segmenti="0,9,10,9;0,9,5,1;10,9,5,1;2.5,5,7.5,5;2.5,5,5,9;7.5,5,5,9;5,1,5,9" titolo="The triangle with its interior segments"}

- $13$
- $6$
- $11$
- $10$

## Explanation

Use **systematic counting** to group the triangles by their number of parts. The smallest parts are $6$ triangles: two at the top and two in the inverted central triangle, split by the vertical segment, and two in the bottom corners. There are $4$ triangles formed from two parts: the entire top triangle, the entire inverted central triangle, and two triangles with the vertical segment as a side and the midpoint of a sloping side as a vertex. The triangles formed from three parts are the two halves of the large triangle, to the left and right of the vertical segment. Finally, count the large triangle itself. This gives $6 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 13$ triangles. Counting only the smallest parts gives $6$. Omitting the two triangles with a side along the vertical segment gives $11$. Excluding the triangles crossed by the vertical segment, namely the top triangle, the central triangle, and the large triangle, gives $10$.
