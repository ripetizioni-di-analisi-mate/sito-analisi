On a die, opposite faces always add up to $7$. Which of the following nets, folded along the common edges, forms a die?

- ::figura{tipo="sviluppo" schema="..x.|xxxx|.x.." celle="lettera:3 | lettera:1 | lettera:6 | lettera:2 | lettera:5 | lettera:4"}
- ::figura{tipo="sviluppo" schema="xx..|.xxx|..x." celle="lettera:1 | lettera:3 | lettera:2 | lettera:4 | lettera:5 | lettera:6"}
- ::figura{tipo="sviluppo" schema="x...|xxxx|...x" celle="lettera:2 | lettera:1 | lettera:3 | lettera:6 | lettera:4 | lettera:5"}
- ::figura{tipo="sviluppo" schema=".x..|xxx.|..xx" celle="lettera:3 | lettera:1 | lettera:2 | lettera:6 | lettera:5 | lettera:4"}

## Explanation

By the **opposite faces** rule, in the net with the row of four faces $1$, $3$, $6$, $4$ the first and third faces of the row are opposite, and so are the second and fourth, so the opposite pairs are $1$ and $6$, $3$ and $4$. The $2$ and the $5$ are left, so they are opposite each other, and in this net all three pairs of opposite faces add up to $7$. The net with the row $1$, $6$, $2$, $5$ has the sum $7$ on adjacent faces of the row, while the opposite faces $1$ and $2$ add up to $3$. In the stepped net that begins with $1$ and $3$, the faces $1$ and $4$ form a Z and are opposite, but their sum is $5$. In the last net the $2$ and the $4$ are opposite, because they form a Z with the $6$ and the $5$, and their sum is $6$.
