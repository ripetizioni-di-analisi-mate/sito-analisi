Which of the following arrangements does *not* fold into a cube when the squares are folded along their common edges?

- ::figura{tipo="sviluppo" schema=".x..|xxx.|..xx" celle="- | - | - | - | - | -"}
- ::figura{tipo="sviluppo" schema="x...|xxxx|..x." celle="- | - | - | - | - | -"}
- ::figura{tipo="sviluppo" schema="x..x|xxxx" celle="- | - | - | - | - | -"}
- ::figura{tipo="sviluppo" schema="xx..|.xxx|..x." celle="- | - | - | - | - | -"}

## Explanation

In the arrangement with four squares in a row and one square above each end, the two extra squares are on the same side. As the row folds around the cube, both squares end up on the same face and the opposite face is uncovered. This is one of the **arrangements that do not fold into a cube**. In the row of four with one square above the first face and one below the third, the extra squares are on opposite sides, so the net folds into a cube. In each of the remaining two nets the row of three squares forms three consecutive walls of the cube, and the other three squares fold onto three different remaining faces, so both nets also form cubes.
