A cantilever of length $3\,\mathrm m$ and a pin-ended column of length $6\,\mathrm m$ have the same cross section and material. Each carries a compressive force of fixed direction. How do the two lowest buckling loads compare?

- The cantilever’s value is $4$ times as large
- The cantilever’s value is smaller by a factor of $4$
- The cantilever’s value is $2$ times as large
- They are equal

## Explanation

The cantilever has effective length factor $K=2$, giving an effective length of $2\cdot3\,\mathrm m=6\,\mathrm m$. The pin-ended column has $K=1$ and effective length $6\,\mathrm m$. Since $EI$ and $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ are the same for both members, their buckling loads $\dfrac{\pi^2EI}{L_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}$ are **equal**. Reflecting the cantilever mode about the fixed end gives the half sine wave of the pin-ended column, so the cantilever mode corresponds to half that wave.
