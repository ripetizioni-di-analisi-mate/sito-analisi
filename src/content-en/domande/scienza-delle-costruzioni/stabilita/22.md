A perfectly straight pin-ended column carries a perfectly concentric compressive load that increases from zero. In the linearized elastic model, what does the graph of load $P$ versus midspan deflection $\delta$ look like?

- $\delta$ increases as $\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{P}{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}}$ from the outset
- $\delta=0$ up to $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$; at $P=P_{\mathrm{cr}}$ any amplitude $\delta$ is admissible
- $\delta$ increases in proportion to $P$ up to $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$
- $\delta=0$ up to $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$, then $\delta$ increases in proportion to $P-P_{\mathrm{cr}}$

## Explanation

For $P<P_{\mathrm{cr}}$, the equation $EIv''+Pv=0$ with $v(0)=v(L)=0$ has only the zero solution, so the deflection remains zero. At $P=P_{\mathrm{cr}}$ there is a solution $v=C\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$ with arbitrary $C$. The diagram therefore consists of the vertical axis $\delta=0$ and, at the load level $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$, a horizontal line representing the **bifurcation**. Gradual growth in deflection by the factor $\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{P}{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}}$ describes a column with an initial imperfection.
