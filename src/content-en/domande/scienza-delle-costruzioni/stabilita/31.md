A very slender pin-ended member ($\lambda=180$) is made of S235 steel. The steel is replaced by S355, which has the same elastic modulus $E$ and a higher yield stress. The cross section, length, and supports remain the same. The Euler buckling load

- increases in proportion to $\dfrac{355}{235}$
- stays the same
- increases in proportion to $\sqrt{\dfrac{355}{235}}$
- decreases because the stronger steel is less ductile

## Explanation

Euler’s formula $P_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2EI}{L_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}$ depends on the elastic modulus, the area moment of inertia, and the effective length. The yield stress does not appear because the buckling load follows from elastic equilibrium of the deflected column. Since $E$, $I$, and $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ are unchanged, the critical load **stays the same**. At $\lambda=180$, the buckling stress $\dfrac{\pi^2E}{\lambda^2}\simeq64\,\mathrm{MPa}$ lies well below both yield stresses, so the elastic prediction applies to both steels.
