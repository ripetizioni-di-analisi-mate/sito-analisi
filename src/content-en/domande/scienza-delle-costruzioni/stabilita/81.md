A pin-ended column has an initial first-mode imperfection. Its midpoint is $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the left of the line joining the pins. As a concentric compressive load increases but remains below the critical load, how does the midpoint move in the elastic model?

- To the right, after returning to the line joining the pins
- To the left, with increasing deflection
- It remains stationary until the critical load is reached
- To the left or to the right with equal probability

## Explanation

Under a load $P<P_{\mathrm{cr}}$, the total deflection is $\dfrac{e_0}{1-\dfrac{P}{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}}$, where $e_0$ is the signed initial imperfection. The factor $\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{P}{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}}$ is positive and greater than $1$, so the deflection has the same sign as $e_0$ and increases in magnitude. The midpoint therefore moves **to the left**, toward the side of the initial imperfection. For a perfect column, either direction of buckling is possible.
