A steel rod of diameter $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $0.5\,\mathrm m$ and a column of the same steel of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $5\,\mathrm m$ are both pinned at their ends. How do the Euler critical stresses of the two members compare?

- The column’s value is $10$ times as large
- They are equal
- The column’s value is $100$ times as large
- The column’s value is smaller by a factor of $10$

## Explanation

A solid circular section has radius of gyration $i=\dfrac d4$. The solid round bar therefore has $i=5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda=\dfrac{500}{5}=100$. The column has $i=50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda=\dfrac{5000}{50}=100$. Since the critical stress $\sigma_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2E}{\lambda^2}$ depends only on the elastic modulus and slenderness ratio, the two critical stresses are **equal**. The critical loads differ because the cross-sectional areas differ by a factor of $100$.
