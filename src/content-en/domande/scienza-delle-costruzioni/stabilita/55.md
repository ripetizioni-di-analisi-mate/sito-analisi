A fixed-fixed column of length $5\,\mathrm m$ with $EI=1000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ can slide axially at one end. With $u=kL$, the buckling equation factors into $\sin\!\left(\dfrac u2\right)=0$ and $\tan\!\left(\dfrac u2\right)=\dfrac u2$. The first positive root of $\tan t=t$ is $t\simeq4.493$. Calculate the **second** critical load.

- $3231\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $808\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $1579\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $6317\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The factor $\sin\!\left(\dfrac u2\right)=0$ has roots $u=2\pi,\,4\pi,\ldots$. The first root gives the lowest buckling load $\dfrac{4\pi^2EI}{L^2}\simeq1579\,\mathrm{kN}$. The factor $\tan\!\left(\dfrac u2\right)=\dfrac u2$ has its first root at $\dfrac u2\simeq4.493$, or $u\simeq8.987$, which is less than $4\pi\simeq12.57$. Thus the second buckling load corresponds to $u\simeq8.987$ and equals $\dfrac{8.987^2\cdot1000}{25}\,\mathrm{kN}\simeq3231\,\mathrm{kN}$. The rule $P_n=n^2P_1$, which would give $6317\,\mathrm{kN}$, applies only to a pin-ended column.
