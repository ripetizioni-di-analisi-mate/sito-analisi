A compression member has $EI_1=600\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ in its lower segment and $EI_2=1500\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ in its upper segment. There is no hinge, point load, or applied couple at the junction. In the buckled shape the curvature immediately below the junction is $2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. What is the curvature on the upper-segment side of the junction?

- $1.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $6.25\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $0.4\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$

## Explanation

The bending moment is continuous at the junction because there is no concentrated couple there. Since $M=-EIv''$, the curvatures on the two sides must satisfy $EI_1v''_1=EI_2v''_2$. The curvature immediately above the junction is therefore $\dfrac{EI_1}{EI_2}$ times that immediately below it. This gives $\dfrac{600}{1500}\cdot2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}=1.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. Thus the curvature jumps, while displacement and rotation remain continuous.
