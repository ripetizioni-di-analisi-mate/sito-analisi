A tubular column has outside diameter $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and inside diameter $60\,\mathrm{mm}$. Compare it with a solid circular column of the same cross-sectional area. The two columns have the same material, length, and end conditions. What is the ratio of $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$ for the tube to $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$ for the solid column? Round to two decimal places.

- $0.47$
- $1.25$
- $1.00$
- $2.12$

## Explanation

Equating the areas gives $100^2-60^2$ for the square of the solid section’s diameter. The ratio of the buckling loads equals the ratio of the area moments of inertia, $\dfrac{100^4-60^4}{(100^2-60^2)^2}$, or approximately $2.12$.
