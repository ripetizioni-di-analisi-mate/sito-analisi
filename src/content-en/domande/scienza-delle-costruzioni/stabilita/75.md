A vertical column of length $6\,\mathrm m$ with $EI=900\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ is laterally pinned at both ends. The base supports the column’s entire weight, which is uniformly distributed at $w$ per unit length. The axial force is therefore $N(x)=w(L-x)$, with $x$ measured from the base. For the trial function $\varphi=\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$, what critical value of $w$ does Rayleigh’s method predict?

- $82.2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $41.1\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $164.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $20.6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

For a variable axial force, the denominator of the quotient becomes $\int_0^LN(x)(\varphi')^2\,dx$. Given $N=w(L-x)$ and $(\varphi')^2=\dfrac{\pi^2}{L^2}\cos^2\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$, the integral is $\dfrac{w\pi^2}{4}$. The bending contribution to the numerator is $\dfrac{\pi^4EI}{2L^3}$. Equating the two terms gives $w_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{2\pi^2EI}{L^3}=\dfrac{2\pi^2\cdot900}{216}\,\mathrm{kN/m}\simeq82.2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The value $41.1\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ would correspond to concentrating the entire weight $wL$ at the top.
