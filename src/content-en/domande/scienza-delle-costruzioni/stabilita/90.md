The graph shows the Euler critical stress as a function of the slenderness ratio for a steel with $E=200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. For which of the four slenderness ratios $60$, $90$, $120$, and $150$ is the critical stress between $100\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $200\,\mathrm{MPa}$?

::grafico{f="200000*pi^2/x^2;200;100" legenda="\sigma_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2E}{\lambda^2};\sigma=200\ \mathrm{MPa};\sigma=100\ \mathrm{MPa}" nomi="λ,σ" xmin=40 xmax=170 ymin=0 ymax=700 titolo="Euler critical stress in MPa as a function of slenderness ratio"}

- $\lambda=60$
- $\lambda=90$
- $\lambda=120$
- $\lambda=150$

## Explanation

The Euler critical stress is $\sigma_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2E}{\lambda^2}$ and decreases as the slenderness ratio increases. For $\lambda=90$, the critical stress is $\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot200\,000}{8100}\,\mathrm{MPa}\simeq244\,\mathrm{MPa}$, above the upper limit of $200\,\mathrm{MPa}$. For $\lambda=120$, it is $\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot200\,000}{14\,400}\,\mathrm{MPa}\simeq137\,\mathrm{MPa}$, which lies between the two limits. For $\lambda=150$, the stress drops to approximately $88\,\mathrm{MPa}$. For $\lambda=60$, it rises to approximately $548\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
