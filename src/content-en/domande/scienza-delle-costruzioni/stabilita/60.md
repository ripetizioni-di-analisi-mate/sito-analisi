Four solid sections each have area $3600\,\mathrm{mm^2}$. They are a square of side $60\,\mathrm{mm}$, a rectangle $45\,\mathrm{mm}\times80\,\mathrm{mm}$, a rectangle $30\,\mathrm{mm}\times120\,\mathrm{mm}$, and a circle. Given the same material, the same length, and pinned ends in both planes, which cross section has the higher first buckling load?

- The circle
- The square
- The rectangle $45\,\mathrm{mm}\times80\,\mathrm{mm}$
- The rectangle $30\,\mathrm{mm}\times120\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

With the same end conditions in both planes, the smallest area moment of inertia governs. The square has $I_{\min}=\dfrac{60^4}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}=1.08\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$. The circular section of area $A$ has $I=\dfrac{A^2}{4\pi}\simeq1.03\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$. The two rectangles have $I_{\min}=\dfrac{80\cdot45^3}{12}\simeq0.61\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$ and $\dfrac{120\cdot30^3}{12}=0.27\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$. The **square** therefore has the highest buckling load.
