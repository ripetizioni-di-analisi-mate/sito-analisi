A vertical rigid bar of length $2\,\mathrm m$ is pinned at its base. A spring of stiffness $k=50\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at the top remains horizontal and resists lateral displacement of the upper end. The spring is unstressed when the bar is vertical. A vertical compressive force $P$ acts at the top and maintains its direction as the bar rotates. Without assuming small rotations, what value of $P$ holds the member in equilibrium at an angle $60^\circ$ to the vertical?

- $100\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $200\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $50\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $86.6\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

When the bar rotates through $\theta$, the upper end moves horizontally by $L\sin\theta$ and the spring exerts a horizontal force $kL\sin\theta$. About the pin, this force has moment arm $L\cos\theta$ and the vertical force $P$ has moment arm $L\sin\theta$. Moment equilibrium gives $kL^2\sin\theta\cos\theta=PL\sin\theta$, so for $\theta\neq0$ we obtain $P=kL\cos\theta$. For $\theta=60^\circ$, the result is $P=50\cdot2\cdot\dfrac12\,\mathrm{kN}=50\,\mathrm{kN}$. At $\theta\to0$ this recovers the critical load $kL=100\,\mathrm{kN}$, and the postbuckling branch is **decreasing**.
