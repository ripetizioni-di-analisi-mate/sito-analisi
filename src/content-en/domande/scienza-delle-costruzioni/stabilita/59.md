An I-section column of length $5\,\mathrm m$ has $I_y=4\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$ about its weak axis and $I_z=30\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$ about the other axis, with $E=210\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Both ends are pinned for bending in either plane. A midspan brace restrains only the displacement associated with bending about $y$. Calculate the first buckling load.

- $332\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2487\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $1326\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $9949\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

For bending about $y$, the brace divides the column into two pin-ended segments, so $L_{\mathrm{eff}}=2500\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $P_{\mathrm{cr},y}=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot210\,000\cdot4\cdot10^6}{2500^2}\,\mathrm N\simeq1326\,\mathrm{kN}$. For bending about $z$, the brace provides no restraint, so $L_{\mathrm{eff}}=5000\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $P_{\mathrm{cr},z}=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot210\,000\cdot30\cdot10^6}{5000^2}\,\mathrm N\simeq2487\,\mathrm{kN}$. The lowest buckling load is the smaller of the two values, $1326\,\mathrm{kN}$. Even with the brace, the weak axis still governs because $4I_y$ is less than $I_z$.
