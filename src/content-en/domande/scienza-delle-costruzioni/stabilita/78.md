An initially straight column of length $4\,\mathrm m$ with $EI=320\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ is laterally restrained at both ends. A compressive force $P=5\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts along a line parallel to the column axis, at the same eccentricity $e=10\,\mathrm{mm}$ and on the same side at both ends. Calculate the midspan deflection of the column centerline.

- $3.33\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $4.14\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $14.1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $2.07\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The buckling load of the column is $\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot320}{16}\,\mathrm{kN}=20\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$, so $P=\dfrac{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}{4}$. With $k=\sqrt{\dfrac{P}{EI}}$ this gives $\dfrac{kL}{2}=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac14}=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. With constant load eccentricity, the midspan deflection is $e\left[\dfrac{1}{\cos\!\left(\dfrac{kL}{2}\right)}-1\right]$. It therefore equals $10\left(\sqrt2-1\right)\,\mathrm{mm}\simeq4.14\,\mathrm{mm}$. The value $3.33\,\mathrm{mm}$ follows from the amplification formula for a sinusoidal initial imperfection, which describes different loading conditions.
