A pin-ended column of length $L=2.5\,\mathrm m$ deflects in its first mode $v(x)=C\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$ with $C=25\,\mathrm{mm}$. To second order, by how much does the axial separation of the ends decrease relative to the straight configuration, neglecting elastic shortening?

- $1.23\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.31\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.62\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

A segment $dx$ inclined at slope $v'$ has axial projection $dx\sqrt{1-(v')^2}\simeq dx\left(1-\dfrac{(v')^2}{2}\right)$. The ends therefore move toward each other by $\Delta=\dfrac12\int_0^L(v')^2\,dx$. Since $v'=\dfrac{C\pi}{L}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$ and the integral of the squared cosine is $\dfrac L2$, the result is $\Delta=\dfrac{\pi^2C^2}{4L}$. Given $C=25\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $L=2500\,\mathrm{mm}$, the result is $\Delta=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot625}{10\,000}\,\mathrm{mm}\simeq0.62\,\mathrm{mm}$.
