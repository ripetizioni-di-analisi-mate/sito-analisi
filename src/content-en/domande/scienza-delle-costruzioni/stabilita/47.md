The column shown has fixed ends at $A$ and $B$, with axial sliding permitted at $B$. An internal hinge at midspan $C$ has no lateral restraint. The length is $6\,\mathrm m$ and the flexural rigidity is $EI=720\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. Calculate the first buckling load.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,3,0;B,6,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,doppio-pendolo,270" cerniere="C" forze="B,-1.2,0,P" quote="A,B,6 m,0.8" titolo="Member fixed at both ends with an internal hinge at midspan"}

- $80\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $20\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $164\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The first mode is symmetric about $C$, where the hinge moves laterally. By symmetry the effective shear at $C$ is zero. The hinge also makes the bending moment zero. Each half thus acts as a cantilever of length $3\,\mathrm m$, fixed at its outer end and free at $C$, with $L_{\mathrm{eff}}=2\cdot3\,\mathrm m=6\,\mathrm m$. Therefore, $P_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot720}{36}\,\mathrm{kN}=20\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$. The antisymmetric mode would keep $C$ stationary, giving two fixed-pinned segments and a much higher buckling load.
