In a load test on a pin-ended member with a small initial first-mode imperfection, the additional midspan deflection $\delta$ is measured relative to the unloaded shape. At $P=40\,\mathrm{kN}$, the graph gives $\delta=2\,\mathrm{mm}$. At $P=80\,\mathrm{kN}$, the graph gives $\delta=6\,\mathrm{mm}$. Using model $\delta=\dfrac{e_0P}{P_{\mathrm{cr}}-P}$, what is the critical load?

- $100\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $120\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $160\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $240\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Rewriting the model as $\delta=\dfrac{\delta}{P}P_{\mathrm{cr}}-e_0$ shows that $\delta$ is a linear function of $\dfrac{\delta}{P}$ with slope $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$. This is the Southwell plot. The two readings give $\dfrac{\delta}{P}=0.05\,\mathrm{mm/kN}$ and $0.075\,\mathrm{mm/kN}$. The slope between the two points is $\dfrac{6-2}{0.075-0.05}\,\mathrm{kN}=160\,\mathrm{kN}$. Thus $P_{\mathrm{cr}}=160\,\mathrm{kN}$, with imperfection $e_0=6\,\mathrm{mm}$. The value $100\,\mathrm{kN}$ would result from treating $\delta$ as the total deflection.
