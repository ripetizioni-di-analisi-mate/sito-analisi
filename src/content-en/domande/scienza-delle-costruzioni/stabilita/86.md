A pin-ended column of length $4\,\mathrm m$ buckles in its first mode with midspan deflection $10\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the magnitude of the end rotation?

- $7.85\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $2.50\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $5.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $2.47\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{rad}$

## Explanation

The first mode is $v=C\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$ with $C=10\,\mathrm{mm}$, since the sine equals $1$ at midspan. For small rotations, the rotation magnitude equals the magnitude of the slope $v'=\dfrac{C\pi}{L}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$. At $x=0$ the cosine equals $1$, giving a rotation of $\dfrac{C\pi}{L}=\dfrac{10\pi}{4000}\,\mathrm{rad}\simeq7.85\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$. The value $5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$ is the slope of the chord from the end to midspan. It is smaller than the slope of the sine curve at the end.
