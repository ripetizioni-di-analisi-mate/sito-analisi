A vertical rigid bar of length $L$ is pinned at its foot and subjected to a fixed-direction vertical force at the top. A horizontal spring, unstrained when the bar is vertical, is moved from height $a$ to $2a<L$ with unchanged stiffness. How does the critical load change in the small-rotation model?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,0,1;D,0,2" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera" forze="B,0,-1.4,P" tratteggi="0,1 1.5,1;0,2 1.5,2" quote="A,C,a,-0.7;C,D,a,-0.7" titolo="Alternative heights for one horizontal spring"}

- It doubles.
- It stays the same.
- It is halved.
- It quadruples.

## Explanation

For a rotation $\theta$, the spring at height $a$ extends by $a\theta$ and supplies moment $ka^2\theta$. Doubling the height doubles both extension and moment arm, so the restoring moment quadruples. The lever arm of the vertical top load remains $L\theta$, so the critical load also quadruples.
