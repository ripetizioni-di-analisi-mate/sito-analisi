For a thin-walled member, the lowest global buckling load $520\,\mathrm{kN}$ and the local buckling load $310\,\mathrm{kN}$ of its most slender wall are calculated separately in the elastic range. Neglecting interaction between the two phenomena, which instability occurs first as the compressive load increases?

- Global buckling of the member at $520\,\mathrm{kN}$
- Global buckling of the member at $310\,\mathrm{kN}$
- Local buckling of the wall at $310\,\mathrm{kN}$
- No buckling before $830\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The two loads correspond to different modes. In global buckling the member centerline bends. In local buckling the wall forms waves while the centerline stays nearly straight. As the force increases, the lower buckling load is reached first. Since $310\,\mathrm{kN}<520\,\mathrm{kN}$, **local buckling** of the wall occurs first. The Euler load for the member as a whole therefore does not fully describe the behavior of this section.
