A pin-ended column of length $4\,\mathrm m$ has $EI=400\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. It carries a concentric compressive force of $400\,\mathrm{kN}$ and has no intermediate lateral restraint. In the conservative elastic model, the straight configuration is

- stable because $400\,\mathrm{kN}$ is not a critical load
- stable because the load is below the second critical load
- unstable
- in neutral equilibrium

## Explanation

The first two buckling loads are $P_1=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot400}{16}\,\mathrm{kN}\simeq246.7\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $P_2=4P_1\simeq987\,\mathrm{kN}$. For the mode shape $v=\alpha\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$, the second variation of the potential energy is proportional to $\alpha^2(P_1-P)$. Since $P=400\,\mathrm{kN}>P_1$, this variation is negative, so the potential energy has no minimum at the straight configuration. The straight equilibrium configuration is therefore **unstable**, even though $400\,\mathrm{kN}$ does not coincide with any buckling load.
