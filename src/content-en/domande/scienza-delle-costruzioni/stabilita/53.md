A cantilever of length $L$ and constant $EI$ is fixed at $x=0$ and buckles in its first mode under a compressive force of fixed direction. The tip displacement is $40\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the transverse displacement at midlength?

- $20.0\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $28.3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $11.7\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $10.0\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The first cantilever mode is $v(x)=C\left[1-\cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{2L}\right)\right]$, satisfying $v(0)=v'(0)=0$ and $v''(L)=0$. At the tip, the argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, giving $v(L)=C=40\,\mathrm{mm}$. At midlength, the argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, giving $v\!\left(\dfrac L2\right)=40\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{mm}\simeq11.7\,\mathrm{mm}$. This is less than half the tip displacement because the deflected shape has a horizontal tangent at the fixed end.
