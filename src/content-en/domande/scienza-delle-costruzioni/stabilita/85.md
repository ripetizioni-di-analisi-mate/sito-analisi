The pin-ended column shown has length $4\,\mathrm m$ and flexural rigidity $EI=320\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. A lateral spring of stiffness $k$ resists displacement at midspan. What is the minimum spring stiffness for which the first buckling load equals the value for a rigid brace at midlength?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,4,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-1,0,P" linee="2,0 2,-0.2 2.13,-0.3 1.87,-0.45 2.13,-0.6 1.87,-0.75 2,-0.85 2,-1.05;1.72,-1.05 2.28,-1.05" etichette="2.45,-0.55,k" quote="A,C,2 m,0.8;C,B,2 m,0.8" titolo="Compression member with a transverse spring at midspan"}

- $80\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $40\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $20\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $160\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

With a rigid brace, the lowest buckling load belongs to the antisymmetric mode, $P_2=\dfrac{4\pi^2EI}{L^2}=80\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$. This mode leaves the midpoint stationary and the spring undeformed. The minimum required stiffness therefore makes the symmetric mode buckle at $P_2$ as well. At $P=P_2$, we have $k_PL=2\pi$ with $k_P=\sqrt{\dfrac{P}{EI}}$. On the left half the mode shape is $v=c_1x+c_3\sin(k_Px)$. Symmetry requires $v'\!\left(\dfrac L2\right)=0$, so $c_1=c_3k_P$, and the effective shear $EIv'''+Pv'$ equals $Pc_1$ throughout this half. The spring balances the effective shear forces from both halves, so $k\,v\!\left(\dfrac L2\right)=2Pc_1$ with $v\!\left(\dfrac L2\right)=c_1\dfrac L2$. It follows that $k=\dfrac{4P_2}{L}=\dfrac{4\cdot80\pi^2}{4}\,\mathrm{kN/m}=80\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$.
