Compare two pin-ended columns with the same buckling load $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$. The first column is straight and carries a compressive load applied with the same eccentricity $e$ at both ends. The second column has a sinusoidal initial imperfection of amplitude $e_0$ and carries a concentric compressive load. Which statement is true?

- In the first case the end moment is $Pe$. In the second it is zero
- The midspan deflections follow the same relation $\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{P}{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}}$
- In the first case deflection becomes unbounded as early as $P=\dfrac{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}{4}$
- The second case has a critical load reduced by a factor of $1-\dfrac{e_0}{L}$

## Explanation

In the first column the force has a moment arm $e$ even at the ends. The moment is therefore $P(e+v)$, and its end value is $Pe$. In the second column the moment arm is the total offset $v$ from the line joining the pins. This offset vanishes at the ends, so both end moments are zero. The secant formula $e\left[\dfrac{1}{\cos\!\left(\dfrac{kL}{2}\right)}-1\right]$ gives the deflection of the first column. The second follows a different amplification law. In both cases the deflection grows without bound as $P$ approaches the same critical value $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$.
