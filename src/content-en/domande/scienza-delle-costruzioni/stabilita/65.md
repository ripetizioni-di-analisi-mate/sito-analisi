A timber stud of length $3\,\mathrm m$ with $E=10\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$ has a rectangular section of width $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $z$ and depth $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $y$. Both ends are pinned in both planes. A panel nailed to the stud along its full length prevents displacement in the $z$ direction at every section. Calculate the first buckling load.

- $36.6\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5.85\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $146\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $23.4\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

A displacement along $z$ would bend the section about its weak axis, $y$. Since the panel prevents this displacement at every section, buckling about $y$ cannot occur. The remaining possibility is bending about $z$, with $I_z=\dfrac{40\cdot100^3}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}\simeq3.33\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$ and $L_{\mathrm{eff}}=3000\,\mathrm{mm}$. This gives $P_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot10\,000\cdot3.33\cdot10^6}{3000^2}\,\mathrm N\simeq36.6\,\mathrm{kN}$. The value $5.85\,\mathrm{kN}$ uses the area moment of inertia about $y$, which would govern without the panel.
