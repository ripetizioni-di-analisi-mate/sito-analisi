In a laboratory test, a nominally straight pin-ended column with a calculated Euler load of $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ already exhibits measurable lateral deflection at $4\,\mathrm{kN}$. The deflection increases more rapidly as the load rises, without a sudden jump at any single load. Which cause is consistent with this behavior?

- A greater effective length than assumed
- An elastic modulus that decreases during the test
- The second mode, which occurs before the first
- A small initial imperfection amplified by compression

## Explanation

For a perfect column, deflection remains zero up to the buckling load, where a branch of deflected equilibrium configurations appears. With an initial first-mode imperfection $e_0$, the total deflection is $\dfrac{e_0}{1-\dfrac{P}{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}}$. This relation gives a deflection from the start of loading that grows increasingly rapidly as the load approaches $P_{\mathrm{cr}}$, without a sudden jump. The observed behavior is therefore consistent with a **small initial imperfection**. A different effective length would only shift the bifurcation load.
