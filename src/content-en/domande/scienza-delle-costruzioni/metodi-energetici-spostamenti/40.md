Two forces act on a linearly elastic cantilever. Force 1 alone produces strain energy $U_1=10\,\mathrm{J}$, and force 2 alone produces strain energy $U_2=40\,\mathrm{J}$. For their two bending moment diagrams, $\displaystyle\int\frac{M_1M_2}{EI}\,dx=15\,\mathrm{J}$. What is the strain energy when the two forces act together?

- $50\,\mathrm{J}$
- $65\,\mathrm{J}$
- $57.5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $80\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

With both forces present, the bending moment is $M_1+M_2$, giving
$$
U=\int\frac{(M_1+M_2)^2}{2EI}\,dx=U_1+U_2+\int\frac{M_1M_2}{EI}\,dx.
$$
The third term is the mutual work appearing in the proof of **Betti’s theorem**.

Thus $U=10+40+15=65\,\mathrm{J}$.

Strain energy is quadratic in the loads. Displacements and internal forces obey superposition, while strain energy also contains a cross term. The value $50\,\mathrm{J}$ omits the cross term.
