An L-shaped frame consists of column $AB$ of height $2\,\mathrm{m}$ fixed at base $A$ and crossbeam $BC$ of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$ rigidly connected at $B$. Both members have $EI=6\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. A downward force of $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $C$. Considering only bending, what is the downward deflection of $C$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,2;C,3,2" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,incastro" forze="C,0,-0.8,P" quote="B,C,3 m,-0.6;A,B,2 m,0.6" titolo="L-shaped frame with a vertical force at the free end"}

- $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $12\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $18\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $9\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Apply **Castigliano’s theorem**, with $P$ conjugate to the downward displacement of $C$.

On the crossbeam, at a distance $s$ from $C$, the moment has magnitude $Ps$. Its contribution is $\dfrac{Pa^3}{3EI}$ with $a=3\,\mathrm{m}$, or $\dfrac{4\cdot27}{3\cdot6\,000}\,\mathrm{m}=6\,\mathrm{mm}$.

The force has the same arm $a$ at every column section, so the column moment has constant magnitude $Pa$. Its contribution is $\dfrac{Pa^2H}{EI}=\dfrac{4\cdot9\cdot2}{6\,000}\,\mathrm{m}=12\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Adding gives $v_C=18\,\mathrm{mm}$. The value $6\,\mathrm{mm}$ includes only the crossbeam contribution, as if the column were rigid.
