A cantilever truss consists of horizontal member $AC$ of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and inclined member $BC$ of length $5\,\mathrm{m}$. The members meet at a pin at $C$ and connect to the wall through stationary pins $A$ and $B$, with $B$ a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ above $A$. Both have $EA=80\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$. Only a downward force of $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $C$. What is the horizontal displacement of $C$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,0;B,0,3" aste="A-C;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera,180;B,cerniera,180" reticolare=1 forze="C,0,-1,P" quote="A,C,4 m,0.6;A,B,3 m,0.6" titolo="Cantilever truss with a vertical force at joint C"}

- $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the right
- $0$
- $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the left
- $3.125\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the left

## Explanation

There is no horizontal load at $C$. To apply **Castigliano’s theorem**, introduce an auxiliary rightward horizontal force $F$ conjugate to the required displacement.

Only the inclined member enters vertical equilibrium at joint $C$. With $\sin\alpha=\dfrac35$, its tensile force is $N_{BC}=\dfrac{5P}{3}=50\,\mathrm{kN}$, independent of $F$. Horizontal equilibrium then gives $N_{AC}=F-\dfrac{4P}{3}$, hence $\dfrac{\partial N_{AC}}{\partial F}=1$ and $\dfrac{\partial N_{BC}}{\partial F}=0$.

Differentiating and then setting $F=0$ gives
$$
u_C=\frac{N_{AC}L_{AC}}{EA}\cdot1=-\frac{40\cdot4}{80\,000}\,\mathrm m=-0.002\,\mathrm m.
$$
The negative sign means that $C$ moves $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ **to the left** because member $AC$ is in compression and shortens. The value $3.125\,\mathrm{mm}$ is the elongation of member $BC$.
