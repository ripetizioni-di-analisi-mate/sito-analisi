A beam is fixed at $A$ and simply supported at $B$. $C$ is the midspan section. A downward force $P$ at $C$ produces an upward reaction $\dfrac{5P}{16}$ at support $B$. In a separate load-free state, support $B$ settles by $8\,\mathrm{mm}$. Apply Betti’s theorem to the two states to find the displacement of $C$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,4,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello" frecce="4.45,0,4.45,-0.7,8 mm" quote="A,C,L/2;C,B,L/2" titolo="Settlement of support B"}

- $2.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ downward
- $2.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ upward
- $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ downward
- $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ downward

## Explanation

Call the state with force $P$ state 1 and the state with settlement state 2. In state 2, the only relevant force is the reaction at $B$, acting through the displacements of state 1. Since point $B$ is stationary in state 1, this work is zero.

In state 1, $P$ does work through the displacement $v_C$ of state 2. The upward reaction $\dfrac{5P}{16}$ also does work through the opposite, downward settlement $8\,\mathrm{mm}$.

By **Betti’s theorem**, these two work contributions sum to zero, so
$$
P\,v_C-\frac{5P}{16}\cdot8\,\mathrm{mm}=0,
\qquad
v_C=2.5\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The positive sign means $C$ moves in the direction of $P$, namely **downward**. Value $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ would correspond to rigid-body rotation about $A$, which the fixed end prevents.
