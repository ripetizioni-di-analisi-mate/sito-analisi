In a linearly elastic structure with stationary supports, a downward force of $8\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$ produces a downward deflection of $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ at $C$. If the only load is instead a downward force of $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$, how far does $B$ move downward?

- $3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $- \dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

By Maxwell's theorem the displacement at $C$ due to a unit force at $B$ equals the displacement at $B$ due to a unit force at $C$. From the first state this coefficient is $\dfrac{3}{8}\,\mathrm{mm/kN}$. Since the structure is linearly elastic, the force of $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$ lowers $B$ by $4\cdot\dfrac38\,\mathrm{mm}=\dfrac32\,\mathrm{mm}$. The value $6\,\mathrm{mm}$ results from inverting the ratio between the two forces.
