A beam of span $4\,\mathrm{m}$ with constant $EI$ is fixed at both ends and carries a uniform downward load $q=6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Use symmetry and Menabrea’s theorem to find the magnitude of the fixed-end bending moments.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,incastro,0" carichi="A,B,0.5,0.5,q" quote="A,B,4 m" titolo="Fixed-fixed beam under a uniform load"}

- $12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

By symmetry each vertical reaction is $\dfrac{qL}{2}$ and the two fixed-end bending moments are equal. Use a simply supported beam as the primary structure and let $X$ be the common value of the end moments.

The moment becomes $M=\dfrac{qx(L-x)}{2}-X$, so $\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial X}=-1$. The **Menabrea** condition requires zero relative rotation, so
$$
\int_0^L\left(\frac{qx(L-x)}{2}-X\right)dx=\frac{qL^3}{12}-XL=0.
$$
Thus $X=\dfrac{qL^2}{12}=\dfrac{6\cdot16}{12}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}=8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the top fibers in tension at both fixed ends. Value $12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ equals $\dfrac{qL^2}{8}$, the maximum bending moment in the simply supported beam.
