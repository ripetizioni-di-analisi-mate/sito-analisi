Three bars of equal axial rigidity $EA$ are pinned to a rigid ceiling and meet at the hinge $D$. The central bar $BD$ is vertical and $2\,\mathrm{m}$ long, while the bars $AD$ and $CD$ are $4\,\mathrm{m}$ long and make angles of $60^\circ$ with the vertical. A vertical force of $50\,\mathrm{kN}$ hangs from $D$. Using Menabrea's condition, with the axial force $X$ of the central bar as the redundant, what is $X$?

::struttura{nodi="A,-3.46,2;B,0,2;C,3.46,2;D,0,0" aste="A-D;B-D;C-D" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera,90;B,cerniera,90;C,cerniera,90" forze="0 -1.1,0,-1.1," etichette="0.75,-0.65,50 kN" quote="B,D,2 m,1.1" titolo="Three bars pinned to the ceiling"}

- $25\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $16.7\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $40\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $33.3\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

By equilibrium of joint $D$ the two inclined bars carry the same axial force $N$, with $X+2N\cos60^\circ=50\,\mathrm{kN}$, so $N=50-X$ in kN. The elastic energy of the three bars is therefore
$$
U=\frac{X^2\cdot2}{2EA}+2\cdot\frac{(50-X)^2\cdot4}{2EA}=\frac{X^2+4(50-X)^2}{EA}.
$$
Menabrea's condition $\dfrac{\mathrm dU}{\mathrm dX}=0$ gives $2X-8(50-X)=0$, so $10X=400$ and $X=40\,\mathrm{kN}$. The inclined bars then carry $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ each. The value $33.3\,\mathrm{kN}$ results from giving the inclined bars the same length as the central bar.
