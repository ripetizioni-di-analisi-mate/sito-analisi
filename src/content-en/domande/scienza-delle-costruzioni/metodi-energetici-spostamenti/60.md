A beam fixed at $A$ and supported at $B$ by a roller with a vertical reaction is statically indeterminate to the first degree. Which of the following primary structures is suitable for applying Menabrea’s theorem?

- A beam with a single pin at $A$ and no support at $B$
- The cantilever fixed at $A$ obtained by removing the roller at $B$
- The beam fixed at both $A$ and $B$
- The beam on two vertical roller reactions at $A$ and $B$

## Explanation

Remove redundant restraints to obtain the primary structure. It must be **statically determinate and stable** because the redundant unknowns act on it as independent loads.

Removing the roller leaves a cantilever that meets both requirements. The unknown is the vertical reaction at $B$.

A beam with only a pin at $A$ can rotate about $A$ and is therefore unstable. A beam on two rollers with vertical reactions is unstable against horizontal translation.

Fixing both ends introduces more restraints than in the original beam. The result is indeterminate and unsuitable as a primary structure.
