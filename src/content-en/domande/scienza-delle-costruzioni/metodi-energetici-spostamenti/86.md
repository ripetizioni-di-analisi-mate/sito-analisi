A stiffness matrix has been assembled for a linearly elastic frame with two joint rotations as coordinates. Its entries include $K_{12}=2\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ and $K_{21}=3\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Which conclusion is correct?

- Joint 2 is stiffer than joint 1
- The structure is unstable
- The result is possible if the two members have different values of $EI$
- The assembly is incorrect because the stiffness matrix must be symmetric

## Explanation

Coefficient $K_{12}$ is the couple at joint 1 due to unit rotation of joint 2, and $K_{21}$ is the couple at joint 2 due to unit rotation of joint 1.

Applying **Betti’s theorem** to these two states gives the mutual works $K_{12}$ and $K_{21}$, which must be equal.

The stiffness matrix is therefore symmetric for any member stiffnesses.

Unequal values indicate an assembly error, such as evaluating coefficient $\dfrac{2EI}{L}$ for the wrong member.
