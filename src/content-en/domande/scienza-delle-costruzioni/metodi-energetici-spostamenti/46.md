For a linearly elastic structure with stationary supports, the flexibility coefficients of two conjugate coordinates are measured in $\mathrm{mm/kN}$. Which of the following can be the flexibility matrix of the structure?

- $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\-1&3\end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\3&1\end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix}-2&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$
- $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$

## Explanation

By **Maxwell’s reciprocity theorem**, $f_{12}=f_{21}$, so the matrix must be symmetric. The matrix with $f_{21}=-1$ does not.

The strain energy $U=\dfrac12\mathbf Q^{T}F\mathbf Q$ is positive for every nonzero load, so the matrix must be positive definite.

A matrix with $-2$ on the diagonal gives negative energy under load $Q_1$ alone. The symmetric matrix with diagonal entries $2$ and $1$ and off-diagonal entry $3$ has determinant $2-9<0$. Some load combination therefore gives negative energy.

Matrix $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$ is symmetric, has positive diagonal entries and determinant $5>0$, and is the only admissible choice.
