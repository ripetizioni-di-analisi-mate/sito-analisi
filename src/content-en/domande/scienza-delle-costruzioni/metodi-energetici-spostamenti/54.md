A cantilever carries only a uniform load. To find its tip deflection, a student adds an auxiliary tip force $F$, immediately sets $F=0$ in the moment expression, and then differentiates the strain energy with respect to $F$. What result does this procedure give?

- The correct deflection, because the order of operations is immaterial
- Half the correct deflection
- The tip rotation instead of the deflection
- Zero deflection, because the energy no longer depends on $F$

## Explanation

By **Castigliano’s theorem**, displacement is the strain energy derivative with respect to the conjugate load.

Setting $F=0$ before differentiation leaves only the uniform load in the moment, and the strain energy no longer contains $F$.

The derivative with respect to $F$ is then zero, and the student obtains zero deflection.

Differentiate the energy, or the moment inside the integral, while $F$ is still present. Set $F=0$ only after differentiation.
