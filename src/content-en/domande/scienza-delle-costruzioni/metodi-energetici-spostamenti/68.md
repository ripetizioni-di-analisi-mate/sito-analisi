A downward force $P_1=10\,\mathrm{kN}$ applied slowly to the tip of a linearly elastic cantilever produces a tip deflection of $4\,\mathrm{mm}$. While $P_1$ remains applied, a second downward force $P_2=5\,\mathrm{kN}$ is added slowly at the same point. What work does $P_1$ perform as $P_2$ is applied?

- $10\,\mathrm{J}$
- $20\,\mathrm{J}$
- $40\,\mathrm{J}$
- $5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

By linearity, $P_2$ alone produces a tip deflection $\dfrac{5}{10}\cdot4=2\,\mathrm{mm}$.

During the second stage $P_1$ remains constant while its point of application moves down by $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. Its work is therefore the full product, without the factor $\dfrac12$.
$$
L_{12}=P_1\cdot0.002\,\mathrm m=10\cdot0.002\,\mathrm{kN\,m}=0.02\,\mathrm{kN\,m}=20\,\mathrm J.
$$
This is the mutual work appearing in **Betti’s theorem**. The energy balance confirms this. The first-stage work $20\,\mathrm J$, the work $5\,\mathrm J$ of $P_2$, and the work $20\,\mathrm J$ of $P_1$ add to $45\,\mathrm J$, the final strain energy $\dfrac12\cdot15\cdot6\,\mathrm{kN\,mm}$ from Clapeyron’s theorem. Value $10\,\mathrm J$ introduces a factor $\dfrac12$ that applies only to a force increasing from zero.
