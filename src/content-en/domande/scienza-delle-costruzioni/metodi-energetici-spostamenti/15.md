A prismatic tie of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$ with $EA=12\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$ is fixed at the left end and free at the right. A uniform axial load $p=4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts to the right along the entire tie. What is the axial strain energy?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" frecce="0.2,0.25,0.6,0.25,;0.8,0.25,1.2,0.25,;1.4,0.25,1.8,0.25,;2.0,0.25,2.4,0.25,;2.6,0.25,3.0,0.25," etichette="1.5,0.5,p" quote="A,B,3 m" titolo="Tie under uniform axial loading"}

- $6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $18\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $12\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

At a section a distance $x$ from the fixed end, the axial force balances the load on the segment to the right. Thus $N(x)=p(L-x)$, with its maximum $pL=12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at the fixed end.

Since $N$ varies along the member, calculate strain energy using the integral
$$
U=\int_0^L\frac{p^2(L-x)^2}{2EA}\,dx=\frac{p^2L^3}{6EA}=\frac{16\cdot27}{6\cdot12\,000}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}.
$$
Therefore, $U=0.006\,\mathrm{kN\,m}=6\,\mathrm{J}$.

Using the maximum axial force over the entire length gives $18\,\mathrm{J}$, while using the mean force $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ gives $4.5\,\mathrm{J}$. Both are incorrect because strain energy depends on the square of $N$, which must be integrated along the member.
