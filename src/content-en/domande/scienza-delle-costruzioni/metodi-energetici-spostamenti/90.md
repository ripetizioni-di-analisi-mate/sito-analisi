A short, deep cantilever of length $1\,\mathrm{m}$ has $EI=4\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ and effective shear rigidity $GA_s=48\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$. It carries a downward tip load of $24\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the strain energy, including the contribution from shear?

- $24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $36\,\mathrm{J}$
- $78\,\mathrm{J}$
- $30\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

At a distance $s$ from the tip, the magnitude of the bending moment is $Ps$. The shear force is $P$ throughout the cantilever.

The bending contribution is
$$
U_M=\int_0^L\frac{P^2s^2}{2EI}\,ds=\frac{P^2L^3}{6EI}=\frac{576}{24\,000}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}=0.024\,\mathrm{kN\,m}.
$$
The shear contribution is
$$
U_T=\frac{P^2L}{2GA_s}=\frac{576}{96\,000}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}=0.006\,\mathrm{kN\,m}.
$$
Thus $U=24+6=30\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $24\,\mathrm{J}$ omits shear, which accounts for one-quarter of the bending energy in this short cantilever. The value $36\,\mathrm{J}$ omits the factor $\dfrac12$ in the shear term.
