A longitudinal slit turns a circular tube of mean radius $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and wall thickness $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ into an open section. It transmits torque $20\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ with free warping. Find the maximum shear stress magnitude using the thin-strip model.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,41.5;0,0,38.5,foro" fori="37,-1.2 44,-1.2 44,1.2 37,1.2" frecce="-52,0,52,0,z;0,-50,0,50,y" etichette="50,10,crack" riferimento=0 titolo="Tube with a longitudinal slit"}

- $0.66\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $26.5\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $8.8\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $79.6\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The slit section is a thin strip of length $2\pi R\simeq251\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $3\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Its torsion constant is $J_t=\dfrac{2\pi R\,t^3}{3}=\dfrac{2\pi\cdot40\cdot27}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4}\simeq2\,262\,\mathrm{mm^4}$.

The maximum shear stress magnitude is $\dfrac{M_tt}{J_t}=\dfrac{20\,000\cdot3}{2\,262}\,\mathrm{MPa}\simeq26.5\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

Bredt's formula for the closed tube would give only $0.66\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The slit therefore increases the stress by a factor of approximately forty.
