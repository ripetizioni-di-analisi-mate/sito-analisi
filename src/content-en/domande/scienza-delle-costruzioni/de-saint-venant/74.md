Two thin-walled tubes have the same wall thickness $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the same midline perimeter $240\,\mathrm{mm}$. One midline is a square of side $60\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the other a rectangle of sides $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $20\,\mathrm{mm}$. Each tube transmits torque $0.72\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Which statement about their mean shear stresses is true?

::struttura{poligoni="-81,-31 -19,-31 -19,31 -81,31;29,-11 131,-11 131,11 29,11" fori="-79,-29 -21,-29 -21,29 -79,29;31,-9 129,-9 129,9 31,9" frecce="-95,0,-5,0,z;-50,-40,-50,42,y" quote="-80 -30,-20 -30,60 mm,9;30 -10,130 -10,100 mm,9" riferimento=0 titolo="Two cells with equal midline perimeters"}

- Both tubes have the same stress because they have the same material area
- The square tube has $50\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the rectangular tube $90\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- The square tube has $90\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the rectangular tube $50\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- The square tube has $25\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the rectangular tube $45\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

Bredt's formula gives mean stress $\overline\tau=\dfrac{M_t}{2A_mt}$. At equal torque and thickness it depends only on the area $A_m$ enclosed by the wall midline.

For the square $A_m=3\,600\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, giving $\overline\tau=\dfrac{0.72\cdot10^6}{2\cdot3\,600\cdot2}\,\mathrm{MPa}=50\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

For the rectangle $A_m=2\,000\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, giving $\overline\tau=\dfrac{0.72\cdot10^6}{2\cdot2\,000\cdot2}\,\mathrm{MPa}=90\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

With the same amount of material, the cell enclosing the larger area transmits the torque at lower stress.
