An isotropic linear elastic circular tube with $\nu>0$ carries concentric tension and has a free lateral surface. What happens to its inner diameter away from the ends?

- It decreases by the same fraction $\nu\varepsilon_x$ as the outer diameter
- It increases because the material moves toward the bore
- It is unchanged because the inner surface is traction-free
- It decreases by a fraction $\varepsilon_x$ equal to the axial strain

## Explanation

Under concentric tension the only nonzero stress is $\sigma_x$, uniform through the wall.

Isotropic Hooke's law therefore gives the same strain $\varepsilon_y=\varepsilon_z=-\nu\varepsilon_x$ in every transverse fiber.

All lengths in the section plane, including the bore circumference, shorten by the fraction $\nu\varepsilon_x$.

The inner diameter therefore **decreases** by the same fraction as the outer diameter.
