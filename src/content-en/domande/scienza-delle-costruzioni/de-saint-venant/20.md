The end face of a prism is a rectangle of width $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $z$ and depth $60\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $y$, with origin at the centroid. A uniform tensile traction of $10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ acts on that face. Which system applied to the same face is statically equivalent to the distribution?

- A tensile force of $24\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $y=10\,\mathrm{mm}$
- Uniform tensile traction of $10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ on the upper half only
- Two tensile forces of $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $y=15\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $y=-5\,\mathrm{mm}$
- Two tensile forces of $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $y=15\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $y=-15\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Two load distributions are statically equivalent if they have the same resultant force and the same resultant moment about a point.

The uniform traction has resultant $10\cdot2\,400\,\mathrm{N}=24\,\mathrm{kN}$ through the centroid, so its moment about $G$ is zero.

The two forces of $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $y=\pm15\,\mathrm{mm}$ also give resultant $24\,\mathrm{kN}$. Their moments cancel about $G$.

The force of $24\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $y=10\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the forces at $y=15\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $y=-5\,\mathrm{mm}$ have a nonzero moment, while loading the upper half alone gives resultant $12\,\mathrm{kN}$.
