Two identical timber boards of width $120\,\mathrm{mm}$ and depth $60\,\mathrm{mm}$ rest one on the other without fasteners and with negligible friction. They bend together under a total bending moment of $3.6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Find the maximum normal stress magnitude.

::struttura{poligoni="-60,0 60,0 60,60 -60,60;-60,-60 60,-60 60,0 -60,0" tratteggi="-68,0 68,0" frecce="-75,0,75,0,z;0,-72,0,74,y" quote="-60 -60,60 -60,120 mm,8;60 0,60 60,60 mm,10;60 -60,60 0,60 mm,10" riferimento=0 titolo="Two stacked boards without connectors"}

- $12.5\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $50\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $6.25\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $25\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

Without connectors, the contact surface transfers no longitudinal force, so the interface shear flow is zero.

Each board therefore bends about its own centroidal axis. Their curvatures and rigidities are equal, so each carries half the moment, $1.8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

The section modulus of one board is $W=\dfrac{120\cdot60^2}{6}\,\mathrm{mm^3}=72\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3}$, giving $|\sigma|_{\max}=\dfrac{1.8\cdot10^6}{72\,000}\,\mathrm{MPa}=25\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

If the boards were bonded, the composite section $120\,\mathrm{mm}\times120\,\mathrm{mm}$ would have $W=288\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3}$ and the maximum stress would be $12.5\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
