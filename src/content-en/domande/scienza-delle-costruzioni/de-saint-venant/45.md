A square section with upward and horizontal centroidal axes $y$ and $z$ is in pure biaxial bending with $M_z\neq0$, $M_y\neq0$, and $\sigma_x=-\dfrac{M_zy}{I_z}+\dfrac{M_yz}{I_y}$. How is the neutral axis oriented relative to the bending moment vector, whose components are $M_y$ along $y$ and $M_z$ along $z$?

- It is perpendicular to the moment vector
- It coincides with axis $z$
- It passes through the centroid and is parallel to the moment vector
- It passes through a corner of the square and is independent of the moment

## Explanation

For the square $I_y=I_z=I$, so the condition $\sigma_x=0$ becomes $-M_zy+M_yz=0$.

The origin satisfies this equation, so the neutral axis passes through the centroid.

Points on the line satisfy $\dfrac{z}{y}=\dfrac{M_z}{M_y}$, so the line is parallel to the vector with components $M_y$ along $y$ and $M_z$ along $z$, the bending moment vector.

If $I_y\neq I_z$, as in a nonsquare rectangle, the two ratios differ and the neutral axis is inclined to the bending moment vector.
