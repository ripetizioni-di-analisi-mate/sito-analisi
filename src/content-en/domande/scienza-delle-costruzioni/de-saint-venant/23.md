A prism has a rectangular section of width $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $z$ and depth $60\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $y$. A tensile force of $48\,\mathrm{kN}$ is distributed uniformly over only the upper half of its end face. Away from that end, what is the normal stress at the bottom fiber?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,7,0,-" poligoni="0,-0.65 7,-0.65 7,0.65 0,0.65" tratteggi="0,0 7,0" forze="0 0.5,-0.9,0;0 0.2,-0.9,0;7 0.5,0.9,0;7 0.2,0.9,0" etichette="3.5,1.05,load on the upper half of each end" titolo="Prism loaded in tension over the upper half of its end faces"}

- $20\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $10\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

By Saint-Venant's principle, away from the end only the statically equivalent loading matters. Its resultant $48\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at the centroid of the upper half, at $y=15\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Transferring the load to the centroid gives $N=48\,\mathrm{kN}$ and a moment of magnitude $48\,000\cdot15\,\mathrm{N\,mm}=720\,000\,\mathrm{N\,mm}$, putting the upper fibers in tension.

With $A=2\,400\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ and $I_z=720\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4}$ the uniform stress is $20\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the bottom-fiber bending stress is $-\dfrac{720\,000\cdot30}{720\,000}\,\mathrm{MPa}=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

At the bottom fiber $\sigma_x=-10\,\mathrm{MPa}$, so it is in **compression** even though the applied force is tensile.
