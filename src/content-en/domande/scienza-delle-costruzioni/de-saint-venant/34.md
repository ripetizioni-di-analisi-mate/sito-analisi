A steel strip of length $1\,000\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ has a width that increases linearly from $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ at one end to $50\,\mathrm{mm}$ at the other. It carries tensile force $40\,\mathrm{kN}$ and has $E=200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Assuming uniform stress $\dfrac{N}{A(x)}$ at each cross section, find its elongation.

- $0.67\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The axial force is $N=40\,\mathrm{kN}$ at every section, and the area is $A(x)=10\left(10+\dfrac{40x}{1\,000}\right)\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, with $x$ in $\mathrm{mm}$.

The strain $\varepsilon_x=\dfrac{N}{EA(x)}$ varies along the strip. Integrating it gives the elongation.
$$
\Delta L=\int_0^{1\,000}\frac{N}{E\,A(x)}\,\mathrm dx=\frac{N\cdot1\,000}{E\cdot10\cdot40}\,\ln\frac{50}{10}.
$$

Therefore $\Delta L=\dfrac{40\,000\cdot1\,000}{200\,000\cdot400}\,\ln5\,\mathrm{mm}\simeq0.80\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Using the mean area $300\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ would give $0.67\,\mathrm{mm}$. The more highly strained narrow region has a larger effect on the integral.
