A composite shaft has a solid aluminum core of radius $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $G_a=25\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$, bonded to a steel tube of radii $30\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $G_s=80\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The shaft transmits a torque of $1.4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. How much torque does the aluminum core carry?

- $622\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $700\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $277\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $100\,\mathrm{N\,m}$

## Explanation

The core and tube are bonded. Their sections rotate together with the same twist per unit length $\vartheta'$.

Each part carries $M_i=G_iJ_i\vartheta'$, so its share of the torque is proportional to its torsional rigidity.

This gives $G_aJ_a=25\,000\cdot\dfrac{\pi\cdot20^4}{2}=2\cdot10^9\pi\,\mathrm{N\,mm^2}$ and $G_sJ_s=80\,000\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{2}(30^4-20^4)=2.6\cdot10^{10}\pi\,\mathrm{N\,mm^2}$.

Thus the core carries $1\,400\cdot\dfrac{2}{2+26}\,\mathrm{N\,m}=100\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Dividing the torque using only the $J$ values and ignoring the shear moduli would give $277\,\mathrm{N\,m}$.
