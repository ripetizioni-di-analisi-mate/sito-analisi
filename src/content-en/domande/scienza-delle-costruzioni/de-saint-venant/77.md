Two channels are joined by two continuous longitudinal welds to form a box section. The cell midline is a rectangle with sides $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $80\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the walls have thickness $5\,\mathrm{mm}$. Each weld can transmit a longitudinal force of $150\,\mathrm N$ per millimeter of beam length. Find the maximum torque in pure torsion.

::struttura{poligoni="-52.5,-42.5 52.5,-42.5 52.5,42.5 -52.5,42.5" fori="-47.5,-37.5 47.5,-37.5 47.5,37.5 -47.5,37.5" tratteggi="-50,-40 50,-40 50,40 -50,40 -50,-40" frecce="-62,0,58,0,z;0,-55,0,55,y" punti="0,40,S;0,-40,S" quote="-50 -40,50 -40,100 mm,12;50 -40,50 40,80 mm,14" riferimento=0 titolo="Welded box section with welds at S and a dashed wall midline"}

- $2.4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $4.8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $2.68\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

In torsion of a closed cell the shear flow $q_t$ is constant around the perimeter. Each weld must therefore transfer force $q_t$ per unit beam length.

The weld limit thus requires $q_t\le150\,\mathrm{N/mm}$.

Bredt's formula gives $M_t=2A_mq_t$, with $A_m=100\cdot80\,\mathrm{mm^2}=8\,000\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ the area enclosed by the midline.

This gives $M_t\le2\cdot8\,000\cdot150\,\mathrm{N\,mm}=2.4\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{N\,mm}=2.4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Using the external area $105\cdot85\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ would overestimate the limiting torque as $2.68\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.
