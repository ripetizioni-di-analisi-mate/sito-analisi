A solid shaft of diameter $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and a tube of radii $25\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $15\,\mathrm{mm}$ have the same cross-sectional area, $400\pi\,\mathrm{mm^2}$. Which statement about their polar second moments of area is true?

- Both have the same $J$ because their areas are equal
- The solid shaft has a larger $J$ because it has no bore
- The tube has $J=170\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}$, more than twice the value for the solid shaft
- The tube has $J=85\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}$, nearly the same as the solid shaft

## Explanation

For the solid shaft $J=\dfrac{\pi R^4}{2}=\dfrac{\pi\cdot20^4}{2}=80\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}$.

For the tube $J=\dfrac{\pi}{2}(25^4-15^4)=\dfrac{\pi}{2}(390\,625-50\,625)=170\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}$.

For the same area the tube places material farther from the axis, and each area element contributes $\rho^2\,\mathrm dA$ to $J$.

The polar second moment of the tube is therefore more than twice that of the solid shaft.
