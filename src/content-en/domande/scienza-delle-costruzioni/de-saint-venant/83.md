A thin steel strip of section $120\,\mathrm{mm}\times5\,\mathrm{mm}$ is cold-formed into an open channel with three walls of midline length $40\,\mathrm{mm}$, without changing its thickness. Under the thin-strip model with free warping, how does the torsion constant change?

- It triples because there are three walls
- It falls to one ninth because each wall is one third as long
- It remains the same, about $5\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- It becomes much larger, as for a closed section

## Explanation

In the thin-strip model $J_t\simeq\displaystyle\sum_i\frac{b_it_i^3}{3}$, so each wall contributes in proportion to its length.

For the original strip $J_t=\dfrac{120\cdot5^3}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4}=5\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4}$.

For the channel $J_t=3\cdot\dfrac{40\cdot5^3}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4}=5\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4}$ because the total wall length remains $120\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Forming the channel therefore leaves the torsion constant unchanged. It remains an open section, so Bredt's formula does not apply.
