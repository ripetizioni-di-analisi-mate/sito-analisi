A beam of length $4\,\mathrm m$ is fixed at $A$, supported at $B$, and carries a uniform downward load of intensity $10\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. A student proposes $V_A=20\,\mathrm{kN}$, $R_B=20\,\mathrm{kN}$, and a zero fixed-end couple. Which statement is true?

- The reactions fail to satisfy vertical force equilibrium
- The reactions satisfy equilibrium but fail to satisfy compatibility
- The reactions satisfy both equilibrium and compatibility
- The reactions satisfy compatibility but fail to satisfy moment equilibrium

## Explanation

The support reactions add to $40\,\mathrm{kN}$, which equals the total load. Taking moments about $A$ gives $20\cdot4-40\cdot2=0$, so equilibrium holds. These reactions, however, belong to a simply supported beam, in which the rotation at section $A$ is $\dfrac{qL^3}{24EI}$. The fixed end requires zero rotation at $A$, so the proposed reactions violate compatibility. The correct solution is $R_B=\dfrac{3qL}{8}=15\,\mathrm{kN}$, $V_A=25\,\mathrm{kN}$, and a fixed-end couple of $20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, which also satisfy equilibrium. An indeterminate structure admits infinitely many sets of reactions satisfying equilibrium. Compatibility determines the actual set.
