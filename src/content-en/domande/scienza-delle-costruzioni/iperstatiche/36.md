A cantilever of length $4\,\mathrm m$ has $EI=20\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ over the $2\,\mathrm m$ nearest the fixed end and $EI=10\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ over the $2\,\mathrm m$ nearest the tip. A downward force of $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at the tip. What is the downward tip deflection?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,4,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" forze="B,0,-1,P" quote="A,C,2 m;C,B,2 m" titolo="Cantilever with two segments of different stiffness"}

- $18\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $32\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $16\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $21.3\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

A downward unit tip force gives $m=-(L-x)$, and the actual moment is $M=-P(L-x)$. Their product is $P(L-x)^2$. Divide it by the flexural rigidity on each segment to obtain
$$
v_B=15\left(\frac{1}{20\,000}\int_0^2(4-x)^2\,\mathrm dx+\frac{1}{10\,000}\int_2^4(4-x)^2\,\mathrm dx\right)\mathrm m=15\left(\frac{56}{60\,000}+\frac{8}{30\,000}\right)\mathrm m .
$$
This gives $v_B=0.018\,\mathrm m=18\,\mathrm{mm}$. The segment near the fixed end is stiffer but contributes more because the moment is larger there.
