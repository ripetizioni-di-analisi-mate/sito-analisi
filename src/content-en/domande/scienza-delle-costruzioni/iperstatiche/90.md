Two identical members of length $5\,\mathrm m$ are pinned to fixed supports $A$ and $B$ at the same elevation and meet at joint $C$, a distance $3\,\mathrm m$ below the supports. Both members undergo a uniform temperature increase of $40\,\mathrm K$, with $\alpha=12\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. No external loads act. How far does $C$ deflect downward?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,8,0;C,4,-3" aste="A-C;B-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera,90;B,cerniera,90" quote="A,B,8 m,3.8" titolo="V-shaped truss with heated members"}

- $2.4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $4\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Apply a downward unit force at $C$. By symmetry, the two members carry the same tensile force $n$. Vertical equilibrium of the joint gives $2n\cdot\dfrac35=1$, so $n=\dfrac56$. With temperature change as the only source of strain, the unit-load method gives
$$
v_C=\sum n\,\alpha\Delta T\,\ell=2\cdot\frac56\cdot12\cdot10^{-6}\cdot40\cdot5\,\mathrm m=4\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The truss is statically determinate, so both members expand freely without developing axial force. The joint displacement exceeds the elongation of either member, $2.4\,\mathrm{mm}$, because the members are inclined to the vertical.
