Beam $A$–$B$–$C$ has an external pin at $A$, rollers with vertical reactions at $B$ and $C$, and an internal hinge $D$ in span $BC$. Which statement is true?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;D,6,0;C,8,0" aste="A-B;B-D;D-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;C,carrello" cerniere="D" quote="A,B,4 m;B,D,2 m;D,C,2 m" titolo="Beam with an internal hinge at D"}

- The beam is statically indeterminate to degree $1$
- The beam is unstable
- The beam is statically determinate, and settlement at $B$ produces no internal forces or moments
- The beam is statically determinate, but settlement at $B$ produces bending moments

## Explanation

The external supports supply $2+1+1=4$ reaction components. The internal hinge adds equation $M_D=0$ to the three equations for overall equilibrium, giving $4$ equations in total. Segment $A$–$D$ is restrained by the pin at $A$ and the roller at $B$. Segment $D$–$C$ is restrained by hinge $D$ and the roller at $C$. The beam is therefore stable and **statically determinate**. In a statically determinate structure, support settlement produces only rigid-body motions of the segments about the hinges, without strain. The reactions and internal forces and moments consequently remain zero.
