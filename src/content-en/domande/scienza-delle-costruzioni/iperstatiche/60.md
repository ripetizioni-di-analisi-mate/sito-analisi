After two vertical supports $B$ and $C$ are removed from a beam, the loads lower those points by $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{mm}$, respectively. Taking forces and displacements positive upward, the flexibility coefficients are $f_{BB}=0.2\,\mathrm{mm/kN}$, $f_{CC}=0.4\,\mathrm{mm/kN}$, and $f_{BC}=f_{CB}=0.1\,\mathrm{mm/kN}$. After both fixed supports are reinstated, what is the reaction at $C$?

- $15.71\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $20\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $-15.71\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The load-induced displacements are $\delta_{0B}=-5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\delta_{0C}=-8\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the stationary supports require both total displacements to vanish. The two compatibility equations are therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
0.2X_B+0.1X_C=5,\\
0.1X_B+0.4X_C=8.
\end{cases}
$$
The determinant is $0.08-0.01=0.07$, so Cramer’s rule gives
$$
X_C=\frac{0.2\cdot8-0.1\cdot5}{0.07}\,\mathrm{kN}=\frac{1.1}{0.07}\,\mathrm{kN}\approx15.71\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$
Omitting the off-diagonal term would give $\dfrac{8}{0.4}=20\,\mathrm{kN}$, overlooking the upward displacement of $C$ caused by the reaction at $B$.
