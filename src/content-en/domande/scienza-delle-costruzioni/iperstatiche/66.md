Continuous beam $A$–$B$–$C$–$D$ has three equal spans of length $6\,\mathrm m$, constant $EI$, and stationary supports. A uniform downward load of intensity $10\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts only on the middle span $BC$. If a positive bending moment puts the bottom fibers in tension, what is the moment at $B$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,12,0;D,18,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;C,carrello;D,carrello" carichi="B,C,0.8,0.8,q" quote="A,B,6 m;B,C,6 m;C,D,6 m" titolo="Load on the middle span only"}

- $-30\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $-22.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $-18\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $-45\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

Insert hinges at $B$ and $C$ and choose $M_B$ and $M_C$ as the redundants. Span $AB$ is unloaded, so only span $BC$ contributes to the load-induced relative rotation at $B$, giving $\dfrac{qL^3}{24EI}$. Multiplying the compatibility equation at $B$ by $EI$ gives
$$
\frac{qL^3}{24}+\frac{2L}{3}M_B+\frac L6M_C=0 .
$$
The load is symmetric about the midpoint of $BC$, so $M_C=M_B$ and the condition reduces to $\dfrac{5L}{6}M_B=-\dfrac{qL^3}{24}$. The result is $M_B=-\dfrac{qL^2}{20}=-18\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, smaller in magnitude than the fixed-ended span value $\dfrac{qL^2}{12}$ because the outer spans allow the interior supports to rotate.
