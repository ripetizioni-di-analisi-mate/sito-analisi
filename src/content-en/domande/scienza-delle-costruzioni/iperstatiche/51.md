A circular shaft of length $5\,\mathrm m$ with constant $GJ$ is restrained against rotation at ends $A$ and $B$. A torque of $20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ acts at section $C$, a distance $2\,\mathrm m$ from $A$. What is the reaction torque at $A$?

- $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

Release rotation at $B$ and take the reaction torque $X$ at $B$ as the redundant. In the primary structure, segment $CB$ is unloaded. The applied torque therefore rotates $B$ through the same angle as $C$, namely $\dfrac{T\,a}{GJ}$. Torque $X$ rotates $B$ in the opposite direction through $\dfrac{XL}{GJ}$. The compatibility condition
$$
\frac{Ta}{GJ}=\frac{XL}{GJ}
$$
gives $X=\dfrac{Ta}{L}=8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Torque equilibrium about the shaft axis then gives the reaction moment $20-8=12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at $A$. The restraint nearer the loaded section takes the larger share of the torque because the shorter segment has greater torsional stiffness.
