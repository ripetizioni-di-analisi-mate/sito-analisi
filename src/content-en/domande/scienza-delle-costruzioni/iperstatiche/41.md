A horizontal beam $4\,\mathrm{m}$ long is clamped at both ends and carries no load. The left clamp stays fixed, while the right one rises by $8\,\mathrm{mm}$. Which rotations must the two clamps undergo so that, under the small-displacement assumption, the beam moves rigidly without bending?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,incastro,0" frecce="4.55,0,4.55,1.05,8 mm" quote="A,B,4 m" titolo="Original positions of both clamps"}

- Left counterclockwise and right clockwise, both by $2\,\mathrm{mrad}$
- Both counterclockwise, by $2\,\mathrm{mrad}$
- Both clockwise, by $2\,\mathrm{mrad}$
- Both zero

## Explanation

The beam does not bend if its deflected shape is straight, that is, if it moves as a rigid body. The line joining the new positions of the two ends makes the angle $\psi=\dfrac{8}{4\,000}=0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ with the original axis, counterclockwise because the right end rises. On a straight deflected shape all sections rotate by the same angle $\psi$, so both clamps must rotate counterclockwise by $2\,\mathrm{mrad}$. If the clamps did not rotate, the settlement would produce end bending moments of magnitude $\dfrac{6EI\delta}{L^2}$, with $\delta=8\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $L=4\,\mathrm{m}$.
