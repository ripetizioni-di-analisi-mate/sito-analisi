A beam has a pin at $A$ and a roller with a vertical reaction at $B$, a distance $4\,\mathrm m$ from $A$. An overhang extends to tip $C$, a distance $6\,\mathrm m$ from $A$. No external loads act. Support $B$ settles by $6\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the vertical displacement of $C$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,6,0" aste="A-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" frecce="4.5,0.12,4.5,-0.32,s" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,2 m" titolo="Overhanging beam with settlement at B"}

- $6\,\mathrm{mm}$ downward
- $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ upward
- $9\,\mathrm{mm}$ downward
- $9\,\mathrm{mm}$ upward

## Explanation

The beam is statically determinate, so settlement produces rigid-body motion without reactions or internal forces and moments. Apply a downward unit force at $C$. Equilibrium gives an upward virtual reaction $R_B^\ast=\dfrac64=1.5$ at $B$. The beam does not deform, so the internal work is zero. The external work includes the term $-R_B^\ast s$ from the virtual reaction acting through the settlement. The virtual work equation therefore gives
$$
1\cdot v_C-1.5\cdot6\,\mathrm{mm}=0,\qquad v_C=9\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
Rigid-body rotation about $A$ gives the same value because each point moves downward in proportion to its distance from $A$.
