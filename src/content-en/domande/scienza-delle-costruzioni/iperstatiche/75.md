A bar of length $2.5\,\mathrm m$ with $EA=100\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $\alpha=12\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ is restrained axially at the left end. A gap of $0.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ separates its right end from a rigid stop. The bar undergoes a uniform temperature increase of $50\,\mathrm K$. What is the final axial force, taking tension as positive?

- $-36\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $-60\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $-24\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The unrestrained bar would expand by $\alpha\Delta T L=12\cdot10^{-6}\cdot50\cdot2\,500\,\mathrm{mm}=1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$, more than the gap, so it reaches the stop. After contact, the total elongation equals the initial gap, giving the compatibility equation
$$
\frac{NL}{EA}+\alpha\Delta T L=0.6\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The bar has axial rigidity $\dfrac{EA}{L}=40\,\mathrm{kN/mm}$, giving $N=-40\cdot(1.5-0.6)\,\mathrm{kN}=-36\,\mathrm{kN}$, a compressive force. Without a gap, the compression would be $EA\alpha\Delta T=60\,\mathrm{kN}$.
