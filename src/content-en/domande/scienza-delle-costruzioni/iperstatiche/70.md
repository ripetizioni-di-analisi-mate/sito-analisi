Continuous beam $A$–$B$–$C$ has spans $AB=4\,\mathrm m$ and $BC=6\,\mathrm m$ and carries no loads. Support $A$ settles by $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and support $C$ by $12\,\mathrm{mm}$. How far must support $B$ settle for the beam to remain free of internal forces and moments?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,10,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;C,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,6 m" titolo="Continuous beam with unequal spans"}

- $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $7\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Without support $B$, the beam is statically determinate. Settlements at $A$ and $C$ move it rigidly onto the chord between the new support positions without bending it. If $B$ settles by the same amount as the chord at that point, the rigid-body motion is compatible with the third support and all settlement-induced reactions remain zero. The downward displacement of the chord varies linearly with position. At $B$, a distance $4\,\mathrm m$ from $A$ along a total length $10\,\mathrm m$,
$$
s_B=2\,\mathrm{mm}+(12-2)\,\mathrm{mm}\cdot\frac{4}{10}=6\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The average settlement $7\,\mathrm{mm}$ would apply only to equal spans.
