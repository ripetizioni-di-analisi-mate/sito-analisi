A cantilever of length $2\,\mathrm m$ carries a downward tip force of $20\,\mathrm{kN}$. The effective shear rigidity of its cross section, already divided by the shear factor, is $\dfrac{GA}{\chi}=400\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$. How much does shear deformation contribute to the downward tip deflection?

- $0.05\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.4\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

When shear deformation is included, the internal work contains the term $\displaystyle\int\frac{\chi\,T\,t}{GA}\,\mathrm dx$, where $t$ is the shear force in the virtual system. The actual tip force produces a constant shear force $T=20\,\mathrm{kN}$ throughout the cantilever. The downward unit tip force likewise produces constant $t=1$. Therefore
$$
v_T=\frac{\chi\,T\,t\,L}{GA}=\frac{20\cdot1\cdot2}{400\,000}\,\mathrm m=10^{-4}\,\mathrm m=0.1\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The shear contribution increases linearly with the span, whereas the bending contribution increases with its cube. Shear deformation is therefore negligible for slender beams.
