An L-shaped frame has a column $AB$ of height $3\,\mathrm m$, fixed at base $A$, and a crossbeam $BC$ of length $2\,\mathrm m$ rigidly connected at $B$. A downward force of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $C$. The members have $EI=9\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. Neglect axial deformation. What is the horizontal displacement of $C$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,3;C,2,3" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,incastro" forze="C,0,-1,P" quote="B,C,2 m,0.6;A,B,3 m,-0.7" titolo="L-shaped frame with a force at the tip of the crossbeam"}

- $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the left
- $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the right
- $6.7\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the right
- $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the right

## Explanation

Apply a horizontal unit force to the right at $C$. This force acts along the crossbeam axis, so its bending moment in $BC$ is zero. In the column the moment has magnitude $s$, where $s$ is the distance from the top $B$. In the column, the actual load produces a constant moment of magnitude $Pa=10\cdot2=20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ that puts the same fibers in tension as the virtual moment. Therefore
$$
u_C=\frac1{EI}\int_0^3 20\,s\,\mathrm ds=\frac{20\cdot9}{2\cdot9\,000}\,\mathrm m=10\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
Their product is positive, so $C$ moves in the direction of the unit force, to the right. Bending of the crossbeam contributes only to the vertical displacement of $C$.
