A beam of span $L$ with constant $EI$ is fixed at both ends and carries vertical loads symmetric about midspan. Cut the beam at midspan and choose the internal forces and moment at that section as redundants. Which statement is true?

- The midspan moment is zero, and the shear force remains to be determined
- The midspan shear force is zero, and the bending moment remains to be determined
- Both the shear force and the bending moment vanish at midspan
- Both the shear force and the bending moment at midspan remain unknown

## Explanation

Under symmetric loading the bending moment is symmetric about midspan. The shear force is its derivative, $T=M'$, and is therefore antisymmetric. At midspan the shear force must equal its own negative, so it vanishes. The bending moment is symmetric and remains unknown. Since the loads are purely vertical, the axial force also vanishes. The only redundant is the midspan moment, found by imposing zero rotation at midspan.
