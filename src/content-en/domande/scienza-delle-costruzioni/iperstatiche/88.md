Continuous beam $A$–$B$–$C$ has two spans of length $4\,\mathrm m$, with $EI=20\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ and stationary bilateral supports. A temperature gradient would produce a constant free curvature $\kappa_T=v_T''=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, with $v$ positive downward. What is the reaction at the middle support?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,8,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;C,carrello" etichette="2,0.45,ΔT_sup;2,-0.45,ΔT_inf;6,0.45,ΔT_sup;6,-0.45,ΔT_inf" quote="A,B,4 m,1;B,C,4 m,1" titolo="Continuous beam with a temperature gradient"}

- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward

## Explanation

Remove support $B$. The simply supported beam of span $2L=8\,\mathrm m$ is statically determinate and can curve freely, so its midpoint rises by $\dfrac{\kappa_T(2L)^2}{8}=8\,\mathrm{mm}$. Support $B$ is stationary, so reaction $X$ must bring the midpoint back to zero elevation. A downward unit force at midspan lowers the midpoint by $\dfrac{(2L)^3}{48EI}=\dfrac{512}{960\,000}\,\mathrm{m/kN}$, so
$$
X=\frac{0.008\cdot960\,000}{512}\,\mathrm{kN}=15\,\mathrm{kN},
$$
directed downward. The result agrees with $\dfrac{3EI\kappa_T}{L}$. It arises entirely from restrained thermal curvature, with no external loads.
