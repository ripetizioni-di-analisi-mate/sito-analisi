A two-hinged parabolic arch has span $12\,\mathrm m$ and rise $3\,\mathrm m$. It carries a downward load of intensity $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ per meter of horizontal projection. Considering only bending deformation, what is the bending moment at the crown?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;P1,2,1.667,-;P2,4,2.667,-;K,6,3;P4,8,2.667,-;P5,10,1.667,-;B,12,0" aste="A-P1;P1-P2;P2-K;K-P4;P4-P5;P5-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" quote="A,B,12 m,0.7" tratteggi="6,0 6,3" etichette="6.4,1.5,f = 3 m" titolo="Two-hinged parabolic arch with crown K"}

- $144\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $-144\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $72\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

Replace one pin with a roller and take the horizontal thrust $H$ as the redundant. The arch moment is then $M=M_0(x)-H\,y(x)$, where $M_0=\dfrac q2x(L-x)$ is the bending moment in a simply supported beam of the same span and $y=\dfrac{4f}{L^2}x(L-x)$ is the arch centerline elevation. These two functions are proportional, so for $H=\dfrac{qL^2}{8f}=48\,\mathrm{kN}$ the bending moment vanishes in every section. With $M=0$ the internal bending work is zero for every virtual system, so the horizontal roller displacement is zero and compatibility holds. The bending moment at the crown is therefore $0$. The parabolic centerline is the **funicular shape** for the uniform load.
