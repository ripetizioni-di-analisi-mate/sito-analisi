A bar with $EA=150\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $\alpha=12\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ is restrained axially at both ends. The temperature change is uniform over each cross section and varies linearly along the axis from $0$ at the left end to $40\,\mathrm K$ at the right end. What is the axial force, taking tension as positive?

- $-72\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $36\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $-18\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $-36\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

With no forces applied along the axis, the axial force is constant. The total elongation is the sum of the elastic and thermal contributions of all differential segments. It must be zero, so
$$
\frac{NL}{EA}+\int_0^L\alpha\,\Delta T(x)\,\mathrm dx=0,\qquad \Delta T(x)=40\,\mathrm K\cdot\frac xL .
$$
The integral equals $\alpha\cdot20\,\mathrm K\cdot L$, so the relevant temperature change is its mean value $20\,\mathrm K$. Therefore
$$
N=-EA\,\alpha\cdot20\,\mathrm K=-150\,000\cdot12\cdot10^{-6}\cdot20\,\mathrm{kN}=-36\,\mathrm{kN},
$$
independent of the bar length.
