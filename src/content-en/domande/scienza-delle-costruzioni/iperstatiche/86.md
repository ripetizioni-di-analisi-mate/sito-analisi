A linearly elastic structure with stationary supports carries a single force $P$ expressed in $\mathrm{kN}$. Its strain energy is $U=5\cdot10^{-4}P^2$ in $\mathrm{kN\,m}$. For $P=12\,\mathrm{kN}$, what is the displacement of the point of application in the direction of $P$?

- $12\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $72\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $24\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

By **Castigliano’s theorem**, displacement in the direction of an applied force is the derivative of the strain energy with respect to that force. Therefore
$$
\delta=\frac{\mathrm dU}{\mathrm dP}=2\cdot5\cdot10^{-4}P=10^{-3}\cdot12\,\mathrm m=12\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
At $P=12\,\mathrm{kN}$, the value of $U$ is $0.072\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. This quantity has units of work, whereas the required displacement has units of length. One example is $\dfrac{P^2L^3}{6EI}$, the strain energy of a cantilever with a tip load, with $L=3\,\mathrm m$ and $EI=9\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$.
