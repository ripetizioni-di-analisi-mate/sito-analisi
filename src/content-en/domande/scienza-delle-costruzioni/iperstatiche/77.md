A horizontal strut of length $2\,\mathrm m$ connects a rigid wall to a flexible element modeled as an initially unloaded axial spring of stiffness $40\,000\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The strut has $EA=80\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $\alpha=12\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ and undergoes a uniform temperature increase of $50\,\mathrm K$. What axial force develops in the strut, taking tension as positive?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,pendolo,0" etichette="2.15,0.18,k;1,0.22,ΔT" quote="A,B,2 m" titolo="Heated strut bearing against an axial spring"}

- $-48\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $-24\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $-12\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The axial force $N$ is constant in the strut. Under compressive force $-N$, the spring shortens by $-\dfrac Nk$. The end of the strut touching the spring moves by the same amount, so compatibility gives
$$
\frac{NL}{EA}+\alpha\Delta T L=-\frac Nk .
$$
The free thermal elongation is $1.2\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$, and the two flexibilities $\dfrac{L}{EA}$ and $\dfrac1k$ both equal $2.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m/kN}$. Therefore
$$
N=-\frac{1.2\cdot10^{-3}}{5\cdot10^{-5}}\,\mathrm{kN}=-24\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$
The spring halves the compressive force $48\,\mathrm{kN}$ that would develop with a stationary end.
