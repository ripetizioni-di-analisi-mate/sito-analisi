Continuous beam $A$–$B$–$C$ has two spans of length $4\,\mathrm m$, with $EI=20\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ and a uniform downward load of intensity $6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ over its full length. How far must the middle support settle for its reaction to vanish?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,8,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;C,carrello" carichi="A,C,0.6,0.6,q" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,4 m" titolo="Continuous beam under a uniform load"}

- $16\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $32\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Remove support $B$ and take its reaction $X$ as the redundant, positive upward. For settlement $s$, compatibility gives $\delta_0+f_{11}X=-s$, so the reaction vanishes when $s=-\delta_0$. The support must settle by the downward midspan deflection of the simply supported beam of span $2L=8\,\mathrm m$ without its middle support. Thus
$$
s=\frac{5q(2L)^4}{384EI}=\frac{5\cdot6\cdot4\,096}{384\cdot20\,000}\,\mathrm m=16\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
With greater settlement, a bilateral support would exert a negative reaction.
