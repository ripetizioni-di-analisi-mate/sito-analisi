Two parallel bars of equal length connect two rigid end plates that remain parallel but may move apart. The first bar has $EA_1=60\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$ and undergoes a temperature increase of $40\,\mathrm K$, with $\alpha=12\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. The second has $EA_2=90\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$ and remains at its initial temperature. With no external forces, what is the tensile force in the second bar?

- $28.8\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $17.28\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $43.2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

With no external forces, equilibrium of either plate requires $N_1+N_2=0$. Since the plates remain parallel, both bars have the same elongation, so
$$
\frac{N_1L}{EA_1}+\alpha\Delta T L=\frac{N_2L}{EA_2}.
$$
Substituting $N_1=-N_2$ gives $N_2\left(\dfrac1{EA_1}+\dfrac1{EA_2}\right)=\alpha\Delta T=4.8\cdot10^{-4}$. Hence
$$
N_2=\frac{4.8\cdot10^{-4}}{\frac{1}{60\,000}+\frac{1}{90\,000}}\,\mathrm{kN}=17.28\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$
The unheated bar is in tension and the heated bar is in compression, with equal force magnitudes. Length cancels from the result because all elongations are proportional to $L$.
