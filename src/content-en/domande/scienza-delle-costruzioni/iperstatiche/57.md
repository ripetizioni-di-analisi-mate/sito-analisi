A symmetric portal frame has columns of height $4\,\mathrm m$, a crossbeam of length $6\,\mathrm m$, rigid joints, constant $EI$, and pinned bases $A$ and $D$. A rightward horizontal force of $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at joint $B$ at the top of the left column. Neglecting axial deformation, what is the horizontal reaction at $D$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,6,4;D,6,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" forze="B,1,0,F" quote="B,C,6 m,-0.6;D,C,4 m,0.7" titolo="Two-hinged portal frame with a horizontal force"}

- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left
- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left
- $0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right

## Explanation

The force acts along the axially inextensible crossbeam. Replacing it with two rightward forces of $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$ and $C$ therefore leaves all reactions and bending moments unchanged. The new loading is antisymmetric about the vertical axis of the frame. The horizontal reactions at $A$ and $D$ consequently have equal magnitudes and point in the same direction. Horizontal equilibrium requires their sum to be $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left, so each is **$15\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left**. The force method gives the same value if a roller is introduced at $D$ and the horizontal reaction at $D$ is the redundant.
