An L-shaped frame has column $AB$ of height $3\,\mathrm m$ fixed at base $A$ and crossbeam $BC$ of length $3\,\mathrm m$, rigidly connected at $B$ and supported at $C$ by a roller with a vertical reaction. The crossbeam carries a uniform downward load of intensity $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. $EI$ is constant. Neglect axial deformation. What is the reaction at $C$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,3;C,3,3" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,incastro;C,carrello" carichi="B,C,0.6,0.6,q" quote="A,B,3 m,-0.7;B,C,3 m,-1.2" titolo="L-shaped frame with a supported crossbeam"}

- $11.25\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $45\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2.25\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Remove the roller and take its reaction $X$ at $C$ as the redundant, positive upward. In the primary structure, the load produces moment $M_0=-\dfrac q2(a-x)^2$ in the crossbeam, with $x$ measured from $B$, and constant moment $-\dfrac{qa^2}{2}=-36\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ in the column. The unit force produces $a-x$ in the crossbeam and constant $a=3\,\mathrm m$ in the column. Using $\mathrm{kN\,m^3}$ and $\mathrm{m^3}$, this gives
$$
EI\,\delta_0=-\frac q2\int_0^a(a-x)^3\,\mathrm dx-\frac{qa^3}{2}\,h=-81-324=-405,\qquad
EI\,f_{11}=\int_0^a(a-x)^2\,\mathrm dx+a^2h=9+27=36 .
$$
Compatibility gives $X=\dfrac{405}{36}=11.25\,\mathrm{kN}$. The column contributes more than the crossbeam to both coefficients because rotation at the column top acts through the lever arm $a$ of the crossbeam.
