A cantilever truss has joints $A$ and $B$ pinned to a vertical wall, with $B$ a distance $3\,\mathrm m$ below $A$. Joint $C$ connects to $A$ through a horizontal member of length $4\,\mathrm m$ and to $B$ through an inclined member of length $5\,\mathrm m$. Both members have $EA=90\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$. A downward force of $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $C$. How far does $C$ deflect downward?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,3;B,0,0;C,4,3" aste="A-C;B-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera,180;B,cerniera,180" forze="C,0,-1,P" quote="A,C,4 m,0.6;B,A,3 m,-0.8" titolo="Two-member cantilever truss"}

- $2.4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $7\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $4.6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $3.5\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Vertical force equilibrium at joint $C$ gives $N_{BC}=-\dfrac53\cdot30=-50\,\mathrm{kN}$ in the inclined member, a compressive force. Horizontal force equilibrium gives $N_{AC}=\dfrac43\cdot30=40\,\mathrm{kN}$ in the horizontal member, a tensile force. Similarly, a downward unit force at $C$ gives $n_{AC}=\dfrac43$ and $n_{BC}=-\dfrac53$. With only axial contributions, the unit-load method gives
$$
v_C=\sum\frac{N\,n\,\ell}{EA}=\frac{40\cdot\frac43\cdot4+50\cdot\frac53\cdot5}{90\,000}\,\mathrm m=\frac{630}{90\,000}\,\mathrm m=7\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
In each member the actual and virtual forces have the same sign, so the two contributions add.
