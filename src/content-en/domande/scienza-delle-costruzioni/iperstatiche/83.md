A beam of depth $h=0.40\,\mathrm m$ with $\alpha=1.2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ is heated by $30\,\mathrm K$ at the top fiber and $10\,\mathrm K$ at the bottom fiber, with a linear temperature variation through its depth. With $v$ positive downward, what is the free thermal curvature $\kappa_T=v_T''$?

- $-6\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $6\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $1.2\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $3\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$

## Explanation

The free strain difference between the top and bottom fibers is $\alpha(\Delta T_{\mathrm{top}}-\Delta T_{\mathrm{bottom}})=1.2\cdot10^{-5}\cdot20=2.4\cdot10^{-4}$. In plane beam theory this difference equals $h\,v_T''$, so
$$
\kappa_T=\frac{\alpha(\Delta T_{\mathrm{top}}-\Delta T_{\mathrm{bottom}})}{h}=\frac{2.4\cdot10^{-4}}{0.40}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}=6\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}.
$$
The sign is positive because the top fiber elongates more than the bottom fiber, and the unrestrained beam curves with its convex side up. The uniform part of the temperature change, $20\,\mathrm K$ at the centroid, produces only axial expansion.
