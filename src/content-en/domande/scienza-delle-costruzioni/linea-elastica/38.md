Four simply supported beams with solid rectangular sections of the same material carry a midspan force. For which beam is the Euler–Bernoulli deflection prediction least reliable?

- Span $6\,\mathrm{m}$, section depth $0.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- Span $5\,\mathrm{m}$, section depth $0.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- Span $9\,\mathrm{m}$, section depth $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$
- Span $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$, section depth $0.6\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The Euler–Bernoulli model neglects shear deformation and gives a good approximation for slender beams. The span-to-depth ratio is $2$ for the beam of length $1.2\,\mathrm m$. For the other beams it is $20$, $12.5$, and $20$. The beam whose span is twice its depth is therefore **short and deep**. In this beam, shear deformation contributes substantially to displacement, and the cross-section rotation differs from the rotation of the tangent to the deflected centerline.
