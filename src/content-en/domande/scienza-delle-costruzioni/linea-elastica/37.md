The beam shown has a pin at $A$, a roller at $B$, and an unloaded overhang $BC$ of length $2\,\mathrm{m}$. Span $AB$ of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a uniform downward load of intensity $6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. If $EI=8\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ is constant, how does end $C$ move?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,6,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0.6,0.6,6 kN/m" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,2 m" titolo="Loaded span with an unloaded overhang"}

- It moves downward by $4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It stays in place because the overhang is unloaded
- It moves upward by $4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It moves upward by $2\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The unloaded overhang has zero bending moment. Its deflected shape is a straight segment continuing the tangent at $B$. The uniform load on the span produces a slope $v'(B)=-\dfrac{qL^3}{24EI}=-\dfrac{6\cdot64}{192\,000}=-2\cdot10^{-3}$ at $B$. The slope is negative because the downward deflection decreases to zero toward the support. The overhang continues with the same slope, giving $v_C=-2\cdot10^{-3}\cdot2\,\mathrm m=-4\,\mathrm{mm}$. Thus $C$ **rises** by $4\,\mathrm{mm}$.
