A simply supported beam of span $5\,\mathrm{m}$ with constant $EI=12\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ carries a downward force of $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ at section $C$, a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from the left support. With $x$ in meters and $v$ positive downward, what is the jump $v'''(C^+)-v'''(C^-)$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="C,0,-0.9,18 kN" quote="A,C,2 m;C,B,3 m" titolo="Point force at an interior section"}

- $1.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$
- $-1.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$
- $7.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$

## Explanation

The downward point force produces a jump $[T]_C=-P$ in shear force. For constant flexural rigidity, $EI\,v'''=-T$, so the third derivative jumps by $[EI\,v''']_C=P$. Therefore, $v'''(C^+)-v'''(C^-)=\dfrac{18}{12\,000}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}=1.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$. The jump depends only on the force and rigidity. Load position and span affect the values of $v'''$ on the two sides.
