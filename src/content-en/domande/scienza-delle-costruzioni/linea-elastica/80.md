A cantilever of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and constant flexural rigidity carries a downward tip force of $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ and a counterclockwise tip couple of $15\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. At what distance from the fixed end is the downward deflection greatest?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0,-;Z,3.9,0,-" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" forze="B,0,-0.9,6 kN" coppie="B,1,15 kN m" quote="A,B,3 m" etichette="3.05,-0.45,B" titolo="Cantilever with a force and a couple at the tip"}

- $3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By superposition, the deflection is
$$
EI\,v(x)=\frac{Px^2(3L-x)}{6}-\frac{\mathcal Mx^2}{2}=x^2(9-x)-7.5x^2=1.5x^2-x^3 .
$$
The derivative $3x-3x^2$ vanishes for $x=0$ and $x=1\,\mathrm m$. At $x=1\,\mathrm m$ the value $EI\,v=0.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m^3}$ is positive, while at the tip $EI\,v(3)=-13.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m^3}$, indicating that the tip rises. The maximum downward deflection therefore occurs a distance $1\,\mathrm m$ from the fixed end.
