A plank with cross section $60\,\mathrm{mm}\times180\,\mathrm{mm}$ acts as a simply supported beam under a specified load. In the first arrangement it stands on edge with the $180\,\mathrm{mm}$ side vertical. In the second it lies flat with the $180\,\mathrm{mm}$ side horizontal. By what factor does the deflection change from the first arrangement to the second?

- $3$
- $27$
- $9$
- $\dfrac19$

## Explanation

Span, load, and Young’s modulus are unchanged, so the deflection is inversely proportional to the second moment of area $I$. On edge, $I_1=\dfrac{60\cdot180^3}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}$. Laid flat, $I_2=\dfrac{180\cdot60^3}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}$. The ratio is $\dfrac{I_1}{I_2}=\left(\dfrac{180}{60}\right)^2=9$. The deflection when laid flat is therefore **nine times** the deflection on edge.
