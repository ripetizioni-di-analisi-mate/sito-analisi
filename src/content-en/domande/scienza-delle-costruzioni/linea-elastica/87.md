A column $AB$ of height $4\,\mathrm{m}$ is fixed at base $A$. An unloaded horizontal cantilever $BC$ of length $2\,\mathrm{m}$ is welded to its top. A horizontal force of $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts to the right at the top $B$ of the column. The column has $EI=16\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. Neglect axial shortening. What is the vertical displacement of the cantilever end $C$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,2,4" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,incastro" forze="B,1,0,6 kN" quote="A,B,4 m,-0.7;B,C,2 m" titolo="Column with a cantilever at the top and a horizontal force"}

- It stays at the same elevation because the force is horizontal
- It moves upward by $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It moves downward by $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It moves downward by $8\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The column acts as a vertical cantilever with a force $H=6\,\mathrm{kN}$ at its top. The top moves to the right, so section $B$ rotates clockwise through an angle of magnitude $\dfrac{Hh^2}{2EI}=\dfrac{6\cdot16}{32\,000}=3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$. Cantilever $BC$ is unloaded and welded to the column, so it remains straight and rotates with section $B$. Since the column does not shorten, $B$ has no vertical displacement. Hence $C$ moves down by $3\cdot10^{-3}\cdot2\,\mathrm m=6\,\mathrm{mm}$. The horizontal displacement of the top $B$ is $\dfrac{Hh^3}{3EI}=8\,\mathrm{mm}$.
