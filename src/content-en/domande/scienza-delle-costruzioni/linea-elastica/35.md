A cantilever of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$ has $EI=8\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ over the first meter from the fixed end. Treat the remaining $2\,\mathrm{m}$ as infinitely rigid. A downward force of $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at the tip. What is the downward tip deflection?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,1,0;B,3,0" poligoni="0,-0.07 1,-0.07 1,0.07 0,0.07;1,-0.16 3,-0.16 3,0.16 1,0.16" vincoli="A,incastro,180" forze="B,0,-0.8,6 kN" etichette="0.5,0.3,EI;2,0.38,rigid" quote="A,C,1 m;C,B,2 m" titolo="Cantilever with a rigid end segment"}

- $6.75\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $4.75\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The rigid segment has zero curvature, so only the first meter contributes to the variable-rigidity deflection integral. With $v(0)=v'(0)=0$ and moment $M(s)=-P(L-s)$, this gives
$$
v(L)=\frac{P}{EI}\int_0^1(3-s)^2\,\mathrm ds=\frac{6}{8\,000}\cdot\frac{19}{3}\,\mathrm m=4.75\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The flexible segment carries the full moment of the force. Even at the end of this segment the moment is $-12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The resulting deflection therefore exceeds the value for a force acting directly at the end of the flexible segment.
