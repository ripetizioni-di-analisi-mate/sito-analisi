A simply supported beam of span $4\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a uniform downward load of intensity $6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The flexural rigidity is $12\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ over the first meter from the left support $A$ and $6\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ throughout the remainder. With $x$ in meters measured from $A$ and $v$ positive downward, what is the jump $v''(1^+)-v''(1^-)$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,1,0;B,4,0" poligoni="0,-0.12 1,-0.12 1,0.12 0,0.12;1,-0.07 4,-0.07 4,0.07 1,0.07" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0.6,0.6,6 kN/m" quote="A,C,1 m;C,B,3 m" titolo="Simply supported beam with a change in cross section"}

- $7.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $-2.25\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $-7.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$

## Explanation

No concentrated force or couple acts at $x=1\,\mathrm m$, so the moment is continuous and equals $M=\dfrac{qx(L-x)}{2}=\dfrac{6\cdot1\cdot3}{2}=9\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Since $v''=-\dfrac{M}{EI}$, the value to the left is $v''(1^-)=-\dfrac{9}{12\,000}=-7.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. The rigidity on the right is half as large, so $v''(1^+)=-\dfrac{9}{6\,000}=-1.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. The jump is therefore $-7.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. Displacement and slope remain continuous because the beam is unbroken.
