A cantilever of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$ with $EI=9\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ carries only a counterclockwise couple of $18\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from the fixed end. How does the free end move?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" coppie="C,1,18 kN m" quote="A,C,2 m;C,B,1 m" titolo="Cantilever with a couple at an interior section"}

- $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ upward
- $9\,\mathrm{mm}$ upward
- $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ downward
- $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ upward

## Explanation

No load acts between the couple and the tip, so the bending moment vanishes there. The jump $[M]=-\mathcal M$ at the couple therefore gives $M=\mathcal M=18\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ between the fixed end and the couple. On this segment $v''=-\dfrac{18}{9\,000}=-2\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. Using $v(0)=v'(0)=0$ gives $v'(2)=-4\cdot10^{-3}$ and $v(2)=-4\,\mathrm{mm}$. Beyond the couple, the deflected centerline is straight with the same slope, so $v(3)=-4\,\mathrm{mm}-4\cdot10^{-3}\cdot1\,\mathrm m=-8\,\mathrm{mm}$. The tip therefore rises by $8\,\mathrm{mm}$.
