A cantilever of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$ with $EI=12\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ carries a uniform downward load of intensity $6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ over the full span and an upward tip force of $12\,\mathrm{kN}$. How does the tip move?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" carichi="A,B,0.6,0.6,6 kN/m" forze="B,0,1,12 kN" quote="A,B,4 m" titolo="Cantilever with a uniform load and an upward tip force"}

- It moves downward by $5.33\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It stays in place because the force equals half the resultant of the distributed load
- It moves upward by $5.33\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It moves downward by $37.33\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Add the two load effects by superposition. The uniform load lowers the tip by $\dfrac{qL^4}{8EI}=\dfrac{6\cdot256}{96\,000}\,\mathrm m=16\,\mathrm{mm}$. The upward force raises it by $\dfrac{PL^3}{3EI}=\dfrac{12\cdot64}{36\,000}\,\mathrm m\simeq21.33\,\mathrm{mm}$. The algebraic sum is $16-21.33=-5.33\,\mathrm{mm}$, so the tip **rises** by $5.33\,\mathrm{mm}$.
