Two cantilevers have the same span $L$ and tip force $P$. Each consists of two segments of length $\dfrac L2$, one with rigidity $EI$ and the other with rigidity $2EI$. In cantilever 1 the segment of rigidity $2EI$ is next to the fixed end. In cantilever 2 it is next to the tip. Which statement about the tip deflections is true?

- Cantilever 2 has the smaller deflection
- Cantilever 1 has the smaller deflection
- The deflections are equal because the moment diagrams are the same
- The deflections are equal because the average rigidity is the same

## Explanation

Both cantilevers are statically determinate and therefore have the same bending moment $M(s)=-P(L-s)$. The tip deflection is $v(L)=P\displaystyle\int_0^L\frac{(L-s)^2}{EI(s)}\,\mathrm ds$, and the weighting factor $(L-s)^2$ is greatest near the fixed end. In cantilever 1, the greater rigidity occurs where the weighting factor is greatest. The calculated deflections are $\dfrac{3PL^3}{16EI}$ for cantilever 1 and $\dfrac{5PL^3}{16EI}$ for cantilever 2, so **cantilever 1** has the smaller deflection.
