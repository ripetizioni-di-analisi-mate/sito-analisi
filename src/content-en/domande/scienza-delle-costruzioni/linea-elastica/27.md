A uniform downward load, distributed over a length of $2\,\mathrm{m}$, moves along a simply supported beam of span $6\,\mathrm{m}$ and constant rigidity. The coordinate $x$ is measured from the left support. In which position of the loaded length is the midspan deflection greatest?

- From $x=0$ to $x=2\,\mathrm{m}$
- From $x=1$ to $x=3\,\mathrm{m}$
- The deflection is the same in every position
- From $x=2$ to $x=4\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By superposition the midspan deflection is the sum of the contributions of the elementary forces $q\,\mathrm dx$, that is $v\left(\dfrac L2\right)=\displaystyle\int q\,\eta(x)\,\mathrm dx$ over the loaded length. Here $\eta(x)$ is the midspan deflection due to a unit force at $x$, which by Maxwell's theorem equals the deflection at $x$ due to a unit force at midspan. This deflected shape is symmetric about midspan and increases from the support up to midspan. Therefore, moving an off-centre loaded length towards the centre replaces smaller ordinates with larger ones. The integral is thus greatest with the loaded length centred at midspan, that is from $x=2$ to $x=4\,\mathrm{m}$.
