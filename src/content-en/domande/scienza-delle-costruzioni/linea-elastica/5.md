A simply supported beam of span $L$ and constant stiffness carries either a central point load or a full-span uniform load. The ratio of midspan deflection to the magnitude of end slope is $\dfrac{L}{3}$ for the point load and $\dfrac{5L}{16}$ for the uniform load. For $L=4\,\mathrm{m}$, the measured deflection is $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and slope is $4\,\mathrm{mrad}$. Which load explains both readings?

- The central point load.
- Both, by adjusting their magnitude.
- The uniform load.
- Neither load.

## Explanation

The measured ratio is $\dfrac{0.005}{0.004}=1.25\,\mathrm{m}$. The uniform load gives $\dfrac{5L}{16}=1.25\,\mathrm{m}$, whereas the central point load gives $\dfrac L3\simeq1.333\,\mathrm{m}$. Changing the magnitude scales both deflection and slope together.
