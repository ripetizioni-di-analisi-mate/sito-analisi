A simply supported beam of span $L$ and constant flexural rigidity carries a downward force $P$ a distance $\dfrac L4$ from the left support and an upward force $P$ a distance $\dfrac L4$ from the right support. What is the midspan deflection?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,1,0;D,3,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="C,0,-0.9,P;D,0,0.9,P" quote="A,C,L/4;C,D,L/2;D,B,L/4" titolo="Two opposing forces at symmetric positions"}

- $\dfrac{11PL^3}{384EI}$ downward
- $\dfrac{11PL^3}{768EI}$ downward
- $\dfrac{PL^3}{48EI}$ upward
- $0$

## Explanation

The load system is antisymmetric about midspan because the forces at mirror-image points act in opposite directions. The problem is linear, so the deflected shape is also antisymmetric, $v(L-x)=-v(x)$. Setting $x=\dfrac L2$ gives $v\left(\dfrac L2\right)=-v\left(\dfrac L2\right)$, so the midspan deflection is **zero**. Superposition gives the same result because each force alone produces a midspan displacement of magnitude $\dfrac{11PL^3}{768EI}$, but the two displacements are opposite.
