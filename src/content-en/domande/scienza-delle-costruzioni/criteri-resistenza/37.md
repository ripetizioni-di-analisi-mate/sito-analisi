In plane stress the two in-plane principal stresses are $\sigma_A\geq\sigma_B$ and the third is zero. For which pair does the Tresca equivalent stress equal $\sigma_A-\sigma_B$?

- $\sigma_A=90\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_B=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_A=90\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_B=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_A=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_B=-90\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_A=\sigma_B=90\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The Tresca equivalent stress is the largest minus the smallest of the three principal stresses, including zero.
If $\sigma_A$ and $\sigma_B$ have opposite signs, zero lies between them, so the extremes are $\sigma_A$ and $\sigma_B$.
With $90\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ the result is therefore $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,T}}=120\,\mathrm{MPa}=\sigma_A-\sigma_B$.
For the pair $(90,\ 30)$, zero is the minimum and the equivalent stress is $90\,\mathrm{MPa}$. For the pair $(-30,\ -90)$, zero is the maximum and the equivalent stress is again $90\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
With $\sigma_A=\sigma_B=90\,\mathrm{MPa}$ the in-plane difference vanishes, yet the equivalent stress is $90\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
