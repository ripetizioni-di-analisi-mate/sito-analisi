At the inner wall of a thick-walled tube, closed at its ends and subjected to internal pressure, the hoop stress is $\sigma_\theta=120\,\mathrm{MPa}$, the axial stress is $\sigma_z=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the radial stress is $\sigma_r=-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The shear stresses on the faces with normals $r$, $\theta$ and $z$ are zero. What is the von Mises equivalent stress?

- $80\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $160\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $80\sqrt6\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $120\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The shear stresses on the three faces are zero, so $\sigma_\theta$, $\sigma_z$ and $\sigma_r$ are the principal stresses, ordered as $\sigma_1=120$, $\sigma_2=40$ and $\sigma_3=-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The differences between the principal stresses are $80$, $80$ and $160\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Therefore
$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=\sqrt{\frac{80^2+80^2+160^2}{2}}\,\mathrm{MPa}=\sqrt{19\,200}\,\mathrm{MPa}=80\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$
The value $160\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is the Tresca equivalent stress, while $80\sqrt6\,\mathrm{MPa}$ results from omitting the factor $\dfrac12$ under the square root.
