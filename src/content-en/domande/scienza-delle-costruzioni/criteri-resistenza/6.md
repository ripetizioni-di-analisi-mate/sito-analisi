For an element in plane stress the known quantities are $\sigma_x=80\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=-10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the in-plane principal stresses, $110\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Which values can the shear stress $\tau_{xy}$ take?

- $75\,\mathrm{MPa}$ or $-75\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $45\,\mathrm{MPa}$ or $-45\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ or $-60\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- Only $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The centre of the Mohr circle is $\dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}=35\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the radius is half the difference of the principal stresses, $R=\dfrac{110-(-40)}{2}=75\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The radius is also $R=\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\right)^2+\tau_{xy}^2}$, with $\dfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}=45\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Therefore $\tau_{xy}^2=75^2-45^2=3\,600\,\mathrm{MPa^2}$, that is $|\tau_{xy}|=60\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The principal stresses depend only on $\tau_{xy}^2$, so the data are consistent with both $\tau_{xy}=60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau_{xy}=-60\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The sign of $\tau_{xy}$ only determines the orientation of the principal directions.
