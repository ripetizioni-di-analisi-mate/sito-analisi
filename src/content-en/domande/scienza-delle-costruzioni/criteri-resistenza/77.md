A thin-walled tube with closed ends has radius-to-thickness ratio $\dfrac{r}{t}=50$. Under internal pressure $p$, the hoop stress is $\dfrac{pr}{t}$, the axial stress is $\dfrac{pr}{2t}$, and the radial stress is negligible. Given $f_y=300\,\mathrm{MPa}$, what pressure brings the tube to the von Mises yield limit?

- $6\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $12\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $4\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $2\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The principal stresses are $50p$, $25p$, and $0$.
The intermediate principal stress is the mean of the other two, so the von Mises equivalent stress is $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot50p=25\sqrt3\,p$.
Setting $25\sqrt3\,p=300\,\mathrm{MPa}$ gives $p=\dfrac{12}{\sqrt3}=4\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$, approximately $6.93\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The value $6\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is the Tresca limiting pressure, for which $50p=300\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
