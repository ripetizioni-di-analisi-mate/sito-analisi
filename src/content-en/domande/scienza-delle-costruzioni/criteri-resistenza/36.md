A brittle material has $f_t=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $f_c=120\,\mathrm{MPa}$. In plane stress the tensile principal stress is $\sigma_A=10\,\mathrm{MPa}$. What compressive principal stress $\sigma_B$ brings the state to the Coulomb–Mohr limit?

- $-90\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-120\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-80\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-110\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The extreme principal stresses are $\sigma_A=10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\sigma_B<0$ and the third is zero, placing this state on the mixed branch.
The Coulomb–Mohr limit is $\dfrac{\sigma_A}{f_t}-\dfrac{\sigma_B}{f_c}=1$, or $\dfrac{10}{40}-\dfrac{\sigma_B}{120}=1$.
It follows that $-\dfrac{\sigma_B}{120}=\dfrac34$, hence $\sigma_B=-90\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The value $-120\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is the Rankine limit, which in this comparison ignores the tensile stress present.
