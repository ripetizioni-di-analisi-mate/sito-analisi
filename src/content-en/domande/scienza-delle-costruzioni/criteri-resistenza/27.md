At a point in a brittle rock with $f_t=10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $f_c=120\,\mathrm{MPa}$, the principal stresses are $(-10,\ -40,\ -90)\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Find the Coulomb–Mohr utilization ratio.

- $\dfrac34$
- $\dfrac23$
- $1$
- $\dfrac74$

## Explanation

All three principal stresses are negative, so the stress state is entirely compressive.
On this branch the Coulomb–Mohr utilization ratio is $-\dfrac{\sigma_3}{f_c}$, giving $\dfrac{90}{120}=\dfrac34$.
Principal stress $\sigma_1=-10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is compressive, so tensile strength $f_t$ does not enter.
The value $\dfrac74$ results from applying the mixed-branch formula to the magnitudes, $\dfrac{10}{10}+\dfrac{90}{120}$.
The value $\dfrac23$ comes from dividing by $f_c$ the difference $\sigma_1-\sigma_3$, as if applying Tresca.
