A cast-iron beam of rectangular section, width $b=100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and depth $h=200\,\mathrm{mm}$, is in uniaxial bending. The material strengths are $f_t=6\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $f_c=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$. What bending moment brings the section to the Rankine limit?

- $4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $26.7\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

At the extreme fibers the stress magnitude is $\dfrac{M}{W}$, using section modulus $W=\dfrac{bh^2}{6}=\dfrac{100\cdot200^2}{6}\simeq6.67\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{mm^3}$.
One extreme fiber is in tension and the other is in compression at the same magnitude. Both states are uniaxial.
Since $f_t<f_c$, the tensile fiber reaches its limit first, at $\dfrac{M}{W}=6\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
Thus $M=6\cdot6.67\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{N\,mm}=4\cdot10^{6}\,\mathrm{N\,mm}=4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.
The value $26.7\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ corresponds to the compressive fiber, which is still well below its limit.
