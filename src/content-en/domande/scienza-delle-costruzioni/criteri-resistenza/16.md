In a Brazilian splitting test, a force $P$ compresses a concrete disk of diameter $D=100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $t=50\,\mathrm{mm}$ along a diameter. At its center the tensile and compressive stresses are $\sigma_x=\dfrac{2P}{\pi Dt}$ and $\sigma_y=-\dfrac{6P}{\pi Dt}$, respectively. Given $f_t=5\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $f_c=60\,\mathrm{MPa}$, what force brings the center to the Rankine failure limit?

- $78.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $39.3\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $157\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The tensile ratio is $\dfrac{2P}{\pi Dt\,f_t}$ and the compressive ratio is $\dfrac{6P}{\pi Dt\,f_c}$.
Since $\dfrac{2}{5}>\dfrac{6}{60}$, tension governs for every value of $P$.
The limit is therefore reached at $\dfrac{2P}{\pi Dt}=5\,\mathrm{MPa}$, or
$$
P=\frac{5\,\pi\cdot100\cdot50}{2}\,\mathrm{N}\simeq39\,270\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
Thus $P\simeq39.3\,\mathrm{kN}$, and the test measures the tensile strength of the concrete.
