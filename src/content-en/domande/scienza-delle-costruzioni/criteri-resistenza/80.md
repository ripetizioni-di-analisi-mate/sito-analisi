The principal stresses at a point in a brittle material are $30\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $0$, and $-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with $f_t=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $f_c=150\,\mathrm{MPa}$. A hydrostatic pressure is added, corresponding to $-50\,\mathrm{MPa}$ in every direction. Find the new Rankine utilization ratio.

- $\dfrac34$
- $\dfrac13$
- $\dfrac35$
- $\dfrac{2}{15}$

## Explanation

The new principal stresses are $(-20,\ -50,\ -90)\,\mathrm{MPa}$, all compressive.
Since $\sigma_1<0$, the tensile term makes no contribution.
The compressive term is $\dfrac{90}{150}=\dfrac35$, so the Rankine ratio is $\dfrac35$.
The initial ratio was $\dfrac{30}{40}=\dfrac34$. The hydrostatic component therefore changes the Rankine ratio while preserving the Tresca and von Mises equivalent stresses.
