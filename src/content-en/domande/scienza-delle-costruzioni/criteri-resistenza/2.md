At a point of a ductile material two principal stresses are $-10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$, while the third one, $s$, is not yet known. For which values of $s$ does the Tresca equivalent stress not exceed $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$?

- $-100\,\mathrm{MPa}\le s\le50\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-40\,\mathrm{MPa}\le s\le-10\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-70\,\mathrm{MPa}\le s\le20\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-60\,\mathrm{MPa}\le s\le60\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The Tresca equivalent stress is the difference between the largest and the smallest principal stress, so it equals the largest magnitude of the differences between two principal stresses. Therefore the condition requires each of the three differences to have magnitude at most $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The known pair gives $|-10-(-40)|=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$, so it satisfies the condition for every $s$. The condition $|s+10|\le60$ gives $-70\le s\le50$, while $|s+40|\le60$ gives $-100\le s\le20$, with stresses in MPa. Both conditions must hold together, hence $-70\,\mathrm{MPa}\le s\le20\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
