At a point in a steel sheet under plane stress there is no engineering shear strain in the $xy$ plane. Strain gauges measure $\varepsilon_x=510\cdot10^{-6}$ and $\varepsilon_y=120\cdot10^{-6}$. The material has $E=200\,\mathrm{GPa}$ and $\nu=0.3$. Find the Tresca equivalent stress.

- $102\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $120\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $24\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

In plane stress Hooke's law gives $\sigma_x=\dfrac{E}{1-\nu^2}(\varepsilon_x+\nu\varepsilon_y)$ and $\sigma_y=\dfrac{E}{1-\nu^2}(\varepsilon_y+\nu\varepsilon_x)$.
Substitution gives $\varepsilon_x+\nu\varepsilon_y=546\cdot10^{-6}$ and $\varepsilon_y+\nu\varepsilon_x=273\cdot10^{-6}$, hence
$$
\sigma_x=\frac{200\,000\cdot546\cdot10^{-6}}{0.91}\,\mathrm{MPa}=120\,\mathrm{MPa},\qquad
\sigma_y=\frac{200\,000\cdot273\cdot10^{-6}}{0.91}\,\mathrm{MPa}=60\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$
Engineering shear strain vanishes, so $\tau_{xy}$ is also zero and the ordered triple is $(120,\ 60,\ 0)\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The Tresca equivalent stress is therefore $120-0=120\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The value $102\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is the product $E\varepsilon_x$, which neglects the Poisson effect.
