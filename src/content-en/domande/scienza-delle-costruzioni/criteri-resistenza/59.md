For the principal stress triple $(90,\ 30,\ -30)\,\mathrm{MPa}$, find the second deviatoric stress invariant $J_2=\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol s^2)}{2}$.

- $10800\,\mathrm{MPa^2}$
- $7200\,\mathrm{MPa^2}$
- $3600\,\mathrm{MPa^2}$
- $1200\,\mathrm{MPa^2}$

## Explanation

The mean stress is $\bar\sigma=\dfrac{90+30-30}{3}=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The principal deviatoric stresses are therefore $60$, $0$, and $-60\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The trace of $\boldsymbol s^2$ is the sum of their squares, $3600+0+3600=7200\,\mathrm{MPa^2}$.
Thus $J_2=\dfrac{7200}{2}=3600\,\mathrm{MPa^2}$. Indeed, $\sqrt{3J_2}=60\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is the von Mises equivalent stress for the triple.
