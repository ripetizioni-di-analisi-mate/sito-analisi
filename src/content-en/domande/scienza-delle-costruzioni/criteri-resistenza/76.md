At a point in a brittle material the principal stresses are $(10,\ 0,\ -90)\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with $f_t=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $f_c=180\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Where does the outer Mohr circle lie relative to the Coulomb–Mohr envelope formed by the common tangents to the circles for the two uniaxial tests?

- It lies inside the envelope without touching it
- It crosses the envelope
- It is tangent to the envelope
- It coincides with the circle of the uniaxial compression test

## Explanation

The state is mixed, so the Coulomb–Mohr utilization ratio is $\dfrac{10}{20}+\dfrac{90}{180}=\dfrac12+\dfrac12=1$.
A ratio of one means that the outer circle radius equals the distance from its center to the common tangent.
The circle with endpoints $10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $-90\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is therefore tangent to the envelope.
The uniaxial compression test circle has endpoints $0$ and $-180\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
