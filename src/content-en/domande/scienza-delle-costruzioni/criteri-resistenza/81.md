A cast-iron bar, made of brittle material with $f_t$ much smaller than $f_c$, is loaded in pure torsion until it fractures. According to Rankine, how is the fracture surface oriented where it meets the lateral surface?

- In a plane perpendicular to the axis, where shear stress is greatest
- Helical, at $45^\circ$ to the axis and perpendicular to the tensile principal stress direction
- In a plane containing the bar axis
- Helically, at $30^\circ$ to the axis

## Explanation

The lateral surface is in pure shear with principal stresses $\tau$, $0$, and $-\tau$.
The tensile and compressive principal directions make an angle $45^\circ$ with the bar axis.
Rankine predicts fracture when $\tau$ reaches $f_t$ because $\dfrac{\tau}{f_t}>\dfrac{\tau}{f_c}$.
The fracture therefore opens perpendicular to the tensile principal direction, following a helix at $45^\circ$ to the axis.
The cross section perpendicular to the axis is the shear failure plane typical of ductile materials.
