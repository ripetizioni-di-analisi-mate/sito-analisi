A brittle material has $f_t=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $f_c=90\,\mathrm{MPa}$. What shear stress brings a pure shear state to the Coulomb–Mohr limit?

- $30\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $15\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $45\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $22.5\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The pure shear principal stresses are $\tau$, $0$, and $-\tau$, placing this state on the mixed branch.
The Coulomb–Mohr utilization ratio is $\tau\left(\dfrac{1}{f_t}+\dfrac{1}{f_c}\right)=\tau\left(\dfrac{1}{30}+\dfrac{1}{90}\right)=\dfrac{4\tau}{90}$.
Setting the ratio to one gives $\tau=\dfrac{90}{4}=22.5\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The value $30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is the Rankine limit, which compares the extreme principal stresses separately.
