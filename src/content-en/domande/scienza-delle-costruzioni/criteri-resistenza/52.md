The principal stresses at a point are $90\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $10\,\mathrm{MPa}$. On which plane does the maximum shear stress act?

- On a face whose normal is parallel to principal direction $2$
- On a face whose normal lies in the plane of principal directions $1$ and $3$, at $45^\circ$ to each
- On a face whose normal lies in the plane of principal directions $1$ and $2$, at $45^\circ$ to each
- On a face whose normal is equally inclined to all three principal directions

## Explanation

The maximum shear stress equals the radius of the outer Mohr circle for the pair $(\sigma_1,\sigma_3)$ and is $\dfrac{90-10}{2}=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
It occurs on a plane whose normal has equal components along $1$ and $3$ and zero component along $2$, making an angle $45^\circ$ with each extreme principal direction.
The face with normal along $2$ is principal, so its shear stress vanishes.
The face at $45^\circ$ between directions $1$ and $2$ has shear stress $\dfrac{90-50}{2}=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The face equally inclined to all three directions has shear stress $\dfrac{1}{3}\sqrt{40^2+40^2+80^2}\simeq32.7\,\mathrm{MPa}$, less than $40\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
