Which of these plane stress states has a tensile principal stress?

- $\sigma_x=\sigma_y=-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\tau_{xy}=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_x=-20\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=-60\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\tau_{xy}=10\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_x=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=-10\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\tau_{xy}=15\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_x=\sigma_y=-20\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\tau_{xy}=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The product of the in-plane principal stresses equal the determinant $\sigma_x\sigma_y-\tau_{xy}^2$.
In all four cases $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_y$ are negative. Their sum is therefore negative and at least one principal stress is compressive.
Thus one principal stress is tensile exactly when the determinant is negative, or $\tau_{xy}^2>\sigma_x\sigma_y$.
With $\sigma_x=\sigma_y=-20\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau_{xy}=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ the result is $900>400$. Indeed, the principal stresses are $-20\pm30$, namely $10\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $-50\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The other three states give $900<1600$, $100<1200$, and $225<300$, so both in-plane principal stresses are compressive.
