A solid circular shaft carries a bending moment $M=2\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ and a torque $T=4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. At the surface the stresses are $\sigma=\dfrac{32M}{\pi d^3}$ and $\tau=\dfrac{16T}{\pi d^3}$. What equivalent bending moment $M_{\mathrm{id}}$ satisfies $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=\dfrac{32M_{\mathrm{id}}}{\pi d^3}$?

- $2\sqrt5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $2\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

At the surface one normal and one shear stress act on the same face, so $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=\sqrt{\sigma^2+3\tau^2}$.
Factoring out $\dfrac{32}{\pi d^3}$ gives
$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=\frac{32}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{M^2+3\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)^2}=\frac{32}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{M^2+\frac{3}{4}T^2}.
$$
Therefore $M_{\mathrm{id}}=\sqrt{4+\dfrac{3}{4}\cdot16}=\sqrt{16}=4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.
The value $2\sqrt5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ is the equivalent bending moment according to Tresca, $\sqrt{M^2+T^2}$.
