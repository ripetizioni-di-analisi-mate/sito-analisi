A state of stress has mean stress $\bar\sigma=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and deviatoric principal stresses $s_1=75\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $s_2=0$ and $s_3=-75\,\mathrm{MPa}$. What are the largest principal stress $\sigma_1$ and the largest shear stress magnitude $\tau_{\max}$?

- $\sigma_1=45\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau_{\max}=75\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_1=75\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau_{\max}=75\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_1=105\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau_{\max}=75\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_1=45\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau_{\max}=150\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

Each principal stress is the sum of the mean stress and the corresponding deviatoric stress, $\sigma_i=\bar\sigma+s_i$. This gives $\sigma_1=45\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_2=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\sigma_3=-105\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The largest shear stress magnitude is the radius of the outer Mohr circle, therefore
$$
\tau_{\max}=\frac{\sigma_1-\sigma_3}{2}=\frac{45+105}{2}\,\mathrm{MPa}=75\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$
The value of $\tau_{\max}$ equals that of the deviator alone, because the hydrostatic part leaves the differences between the principal stresses unchanged. The value $\sigma_1=105\,\mathrm{MPa}$ results from subtracting the mean stress instead of adding it.
