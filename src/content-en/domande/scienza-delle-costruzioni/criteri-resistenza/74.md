A rectangular beam carries bending and shear. Its maximum normal stress at the extreme fiber is $120\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and its maximum shear stress at the neutral axis is $40\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Normal stress varies linearly with distance $y$ from the neutral axis, and shear stress follows the parabolic Jourawski distribution $\tau=\tau_{\max}\left(1-\dfrac{4y^2}{h^2}\right)$. What is the von Mises equivalent stress at distance $y=\dfrac{h}{4}$ from the neutral axis?

- $20\sqrt{21}\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $30\sqrt5\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $30\sqrt7\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The extreme fiber is at distance $\dfrac{h}{2}$. At $y=\dfrac{h}{4}$ the normal stress is half its maximum value, $\sigma=60\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The parabolic shear distribution gives $\tau=40\left(1-\dfrac{4}{16}\right)=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
At the chosen point $\sigma$ and $\tau$ act on the same face. Thus $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=\sqrt{60^2+3\cdot30^2}=\sqrt{6300}=30\sqrt7\,\mathrm{MPa}$, approximately $79.4\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The value $20\sqrt{21}\,\mathrm{MPa}$ results from incorrectly using the maximum shear stress $40\,\mathrm{MPa}$ at this point.
