The principal stresses at a point are $\sigma_1=\sigma_2=60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\sigma_3=-20\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Which statement correctly describes the three Mohr circles?

- Three distinct circles, one for each pair of principal stresses
- A single circle with center $30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and radius $30\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- Two coincident circles with center $20\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and radius $80\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with the third reduced to point $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- Two coincident circles with center $20\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and radius $40\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with the third reduced to point $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The circle for pair $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$ has radius $\dfrac{60-60}{2}=0$ and reduces to point $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ on the $\sigma_n$ axis.
Pairs $(\sigma_1,\sigma_3)$ and $(\sigma_2,\sigma_3)$ have identical endpoints $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $-20\,\mathrm{MPa}$, so their circles coincide.
Their common center is $\dfrac{60+(-20)}{2}=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and their radius is $\dfrac{60-(-20)}{2}=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The proposed radius $80\,\mathrm{MPa}$ equals the difference between endpoints, the diameter of the circle.
The circle with center $30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and radius $30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ has endpoints $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $0$ and therefore omits stress $\sigma_3$.
