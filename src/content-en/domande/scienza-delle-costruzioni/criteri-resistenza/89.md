At a point the only stress components are $\sigma_x=60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\sigma_y=-20\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Find the magnitude of the traction vector on the face with normal $\boldsymbol n=\left(\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ \dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ 0\right)$.

- $20\sqrt5\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $20\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $40\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $40\sqrt2\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The traction vector on the face is the stress tensor multiplied by its normal, $\boldsymbol t=\boldsymbol\sigma\,\boldsymbol n$.
Since only the diagonal entries are nonzero, this gives $\boldsymbol t=\left(60\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ -20\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ 0\right)=\left(30\sqrt2,\ -10\sqrt2,\ 0\right)\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
Its magnitude is therefore $\sqrt{1800+200}=\sqrt{2000}=20\sqrt5\,\mathrm{MPa}$, approximately $44.7\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The normal component is $\boldsymbol t\cdot\boldsymbol n=30-10=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the tangential component is $\sqrt{2000-400}=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The traction magnitude exceeds either.
The value $40\sqrt2\,\mathrm{MPa}$ adds the component magnitudes instead of combining them by the Pythagorean theorem.
