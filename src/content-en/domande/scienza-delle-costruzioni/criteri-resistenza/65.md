A tightened steel bolt has a constant axial preload stress $\sigma=100\,\mathrm{MPa}$. An applied torque then produces shank shear stress $\tau=25\lambda\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with $\lambda$ increasing. Given $f_y=200\,\mathrm{MPa}$, at what value of $\lambda$ is the von Mises yield limit reached?

- $1.84$
- $8$
- $2\sqrt3$
- $4$

## Explanation

In the shank $\sigma$ and $\tau$ act on the same face, so $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=\sqrt{\sigma^2+3\tau^2}$.
Since the preload remains fixed, the yield condition is $100^2+3\cdot(25\lambda)^2=200^2$.
It follows that $1875\lambda^2=30\,000$, giving $\lambda^2=16$ and $\lambda=4$.
The value $1.84$ would result from scaling the preload by the same factor, as for proportional loading.
The value $2\sqrt3$ instead follows from Tresca with $\sqrt{\sigma^2+4\tau^2}$.
