In plane stress, use the normalized in-plane principal stresses $x=\dfrac{\sigma_A}{f_y}$ and $y=\dfrac{\sigma_B}{f_y}$, with zero out-of-plane stress. Which state lies on both the Tresca and von Mises yield boundaries?

- $x=1$, $y=\dfrac12$
- $x=\dfrac12$, $y=-\dfrac12$
- $x=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$, $y=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$
- $x=y=1$

## Explanation

For $x=y=1$ the triple is $(f_y,\ f_y,\ 0)$, so the Tresca utilization ratio is $1-0=1$.
For the same state the von Mises ratio is $\sqrt{x^2-xy+y^2}=\sqrt{1-1+1}=1$.
Thus equibiaxial stress lies on both yield boundaries, as does uniaxial tension, because two principal stresses coincide.
States $\left(1,\ \dfrac12\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac12,\ -\dfrac12\right)$ have Tresca ratio one and von Mises ratio $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so they lie inside the ellipse.
State $\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3},\ -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\right)$ lies on the ellipse, but its Tresca ratio is $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}>1$.
