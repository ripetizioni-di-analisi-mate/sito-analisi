The uniaxial yield strength is $f_y=120\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Which triple of principal stresses lies exactly on the von Mises yield boundary?

- $(120,\ 120,\ 120)\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $(120,\ 60,\ 0)\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $(170,\ 50,\ 50)\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $(60,\ 0,\ -60)\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The triple $(170,\ 50,\ 50)$ is the sum of hydrostatic stress $50\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and uniaxial tension $120\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
Hydrostatic stress leaves the von Mises equivalent stress unchanged, so $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=120\,\mathrm{MPa}=f_y$.
The triple $(120,\ 120,\ 120)$ is purely hydrostatic, so its equivalent stress is zero.
In triples $(120,\ 60,\ 0)$ and $(60,\ 0,\ -60)$ the intermediate principal stress is midway between the extremes. Their equivalent stress is $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot120=60\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$, less than $f_y$.
