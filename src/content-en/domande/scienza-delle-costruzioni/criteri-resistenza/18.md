A plane stress state has $\sigma_x=60\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\tau_{xy}=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$. What angle $\theta$ does the normal to the plane of maximum principal stress make with the $x$ axis? Measure counterclockwise and give an angle between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$.

- $22.5^\circ$
- $45^\circ$
- $67.5^\circ$
- $11.25^\circ$

## Explanation

On a face whose normal is inclined at $\theta$, the normal stress is
$$
\sigma_n=\frac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}+\frac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\cos(2\theta)+\tau_{xy}\sin(2\theta)=40+20\cos(2\theta)+20\sin(2\theta).
$$
The variable term is largest when $\cos(2\theta)$ and $\sin(2\theta)$ are equal and positive, that is, at $2\theta=45^\circ$.
The normal to the plane of greatest principal stress therefore makes an angle $\theta=22.5^\circ$ with the $x$ axis.
At $\theta=67.5^\circ$, $2\theta=135^\circ$. The variable term vanishes, making this a plane of maximum in-plane shear stress.
