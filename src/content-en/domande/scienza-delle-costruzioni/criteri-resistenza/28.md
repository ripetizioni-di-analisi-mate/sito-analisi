The figure shows the Rankine admissible region in the $\sigma_A$–$\sigma_B$ principal stress plane for plane stress with $f_t=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $f_c=120\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Which stress state lies on its boundary?

::grafico{linee="-120,-120 40,-120 40,40 -120,40 -120,-120" xmin=-140 xmax=60 ymin=-140 ymax=60 uguale=1 nomi="a,b" titolo="Rankine admissible region with a = σ_A and b = σ_B in MPa"}

- $\sigma_A=30\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_B=-90\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_A=\sigma_B=-100\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\sigma_A=50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_B=0$
- $\sigma_A=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_B=-60\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The Rankine region is the square in which both principal stresses belong to the interval $[-120,\ 40]\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
In the state with $\sigma_A=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$, the tensile stress equals $f_t$ and $\sigma_B=-60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ lies inside the interval. The state is on the right-hand side of the square.
The state $(30,\ -90)$ has ratio $\max\left(\dfrac{30}{40},\dfrac{90}{120}\right)=\dfrac34$ and lies inside, as does state $(-100,\ -100)$ with ratio $\dfrac56$.
The state $(50,\ 0)$ has ratio $\dfrac{50}{40}>1$ and lies outside.
