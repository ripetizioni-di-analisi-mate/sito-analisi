An I-beam carries bending moment and shear force. The stresses are $\sigma=140\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau=0$ at the outer flange fiber and $\sigma=100\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau=60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ at the flange–web junction. Which statement is true according to von Mises?

- The outer flange fiber is more highly stressed because its normal stress is greatest
- The outer flange fiber is more highly stressed because $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}\simeq117\,\mathrm{MPa}$ at the junction
- Both points have the same von Mises equivalent stress
- The flange–web junction is more highly stressed, with $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}\simeq144\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The outer flange fiber is in uniaxial stress, so $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=140\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
At the junction $\sigma$ and $\tau$ act on the same face, giving $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}=\sqrt{100^2+3\cdot60^2}=\sqrt{20\,800}\simeq144.2\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
The second value is higher, so the flange–web junction is more highly stressed.
The value $117\,\mathrm{MPa}$ follows from $\sqrt{\sigma^2+\tau^2}$, which omits the factor $3$ multiplying the squared shear stress.
