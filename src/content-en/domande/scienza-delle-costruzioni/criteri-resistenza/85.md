The figure shows the von Mises ellipse in the plane of normalized principal stresses $x=\dfrac{\sigma_A}{f_y}$ and $y=\dfrac{\sigma_B}{f_y}$ for plane stress. State $P$ has $x=0.3$ and $y=0.6$, and the loading increases proportionally. What multiplier brings this state to the von Mises yield boundary?

::grafico{curve="cos(t)+sin(t)/sqrt(3),cos(t)-sin(t)/sqrt(3),0,6.2831853" punti="0.3,0.6,pieno,P" xmin=-1.4 xmax=1.4 ymin=-1.4 ymax=1.4 uguale=1 legenda="\mathrm{von\ Mises}" titolo="Von Mises ellipse in normalized coordinates and stress state P"}

- $\dfrac53$
- $\dfrac{10}{9}$
- $\dfrac{100}{27}$
- $\dfrac{10\sqrt3}{9}$

## Explanation

The von Mises utilization ratio for this state is $\sqrt{x^2-xy+y^2}=\sqrt{0.09-0.18+0.36}=\sqrt{0.27}=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{10}$.
Under proportional loading the ratio increases linearly with the multiplier. The limit is therefore reached at $n=\dfrac{10}{3\sqrt3}=\dfrac{10\sqrt3}{9}\simeq1.92$.
The value $\dfrac53$ is the Tresca multiplier because with two positive principal stresses its ratio is $\max(x,y)=0.6$.
The value $\dfrac{100}{27}$ is the reciprocal of $0.27$, the square of the utilization ratio.
