A plane stress state has $\sigma_x=\sigma_y=80\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and an increasing shear stress $\tau_{xy}=\tau>0$. The uniaxial yield strength is $f_y=240\,\mathrm{MPa}$. At what value of $\tau$ is the Tresca yield limit reached?

- $120\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $160\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $80\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $240\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The in-plane circle has center $80\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and radius $\tau$, giving in-plane principal stresses $80+\tau$ and $80-\tau$. The third principal stress is zero.
For $\tau\leq80\,\mathrm{MPa}$ both are nonnegative. The Tresca equivalent stress is therefore $80+\tau\leq160\,\mathrm{MPa}$, below the limit.
For $\tau>80\,\mathrm{MPa}$ the principal stress $80-\tau$ is negative. Zero is intermediate and the equivalent stress is $(80+\tau)-(80-\tau)=2\tau$.
Setting $2\tau=240\,\mathrm{MPa}$ gives $\tau=120\,\mathrm{MPa}$, larger than $80\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and therefore consistent with this case.
The value $160\,\mathrm{MPa}$ comes from using $80+\tau=240$ without checking the sign of $80-\tau$.
