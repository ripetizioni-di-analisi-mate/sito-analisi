The edges of a thin plate restrain in-plane displacement, and the applied load produces a deflection comparable to the thickness. Which effect neglected in linear bending theory becomes significant?

- Compressive membrane forces that increase the deflection
- Transverse shear that eliminates the moments
- Tensile membrane forces due to geometric stretching that stiffen the plate
- No effect, because the linear solution applies at any deflection

## Explanation

A midplane segment that tilts to slope $\partial_xw$ develops a relative elongation of order $(\partial_xw)^2\,\mathrm dx$. If the edges restrain in-plane displacement, this elongation generates membrane tension. On a sloping surface, the tensile forces have vertical components that carry part of the load and reduce the deflection. Linear superposition of membrane and bending solutions therefore applies only for deflections small compared with the thickness.
