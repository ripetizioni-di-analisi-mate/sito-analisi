A simply supported slab has a thickness equal to one-fifth of its span. How does the Kirchhoff–Love deflection compare with the prediction of a model that also accounts for transverse shear deformation?

- It is larger because Kirchhoff–Love theory neglects the shear forces $Q_x$ and $Q_y$
- It is the same because the shear forces can still be found from equilibrium
- It is the same because the deflection depends only on flexural rigidity $D$
- It is smaller because Kirchhoff–Love theory imposes $\gamma_{xz}=\gamma_{yz}=0$

## Explanation

Requiring normals to remain perpendicular to the deflected midsurface imposes zero transverse shear strain, equivalent to infinite shear stiffness. A shear-deformable model adds contributions from $\gamma_{xz}$ and $\gamma_{yz}$ to the bending displacement, so it predicts a larger deflection. Kirchhoff–Love theory also predicts shear resultants $Q_x$ and $Q_y$ from moment equilibrium, but they cause no shear deformation in this theory. With thickness equal to one-fifth of the span, shear deformation is significant. Kirchhoff–Love theory therefore predicts less deflection than the more complete model.
