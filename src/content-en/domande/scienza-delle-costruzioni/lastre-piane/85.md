A slab of length $20\,\mathrm m$, width $b=1.2\,\mathrm m$, and thickness $h=0.3\,\mathrm m$ is supported along its two long edges. Which ratio is used to assess the thin-plate model in transverse bending, and what does it indicate?

- $\dfrac hb=\dfrac14$, so the thin-plate assumption is questionable
- $\dfrac{h}{20\,\mathrm m}=\dfrac{3}{200}$, so the thin-plate model is suitable
- $\dfrac{b}{20\,\mathrm m}=\dfrac{3}{50}$, so the thin-plate model is suitable
- $\dfrac hb=\dfrac14$, so the thin-plate model is suitable

## Explanation

With supports along the long edges, the slope changes in the transverse direction across the width $b=1.2\,\mathrm m$. The relevant ratio is therefore $\dfrac hb=\dfrac{0.3}{1.2}=\dfrac14$. A thickness equal to one-quarter of the support spacing is substantial, so transverse shear deformation, neglected by Kirchhoff–Love theory, can affect the result. The length $20\,\mathrm m$ does not enter the transverse bending calculation.
