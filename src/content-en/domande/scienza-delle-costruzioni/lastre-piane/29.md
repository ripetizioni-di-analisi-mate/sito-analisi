Two strain gauges bonded to the bottom face $z=\dfrac h2$ of a plate of thickness $h=0.2\,\mathrm m$ read $\varepsilon_x=3\cdot10^{-4}$ and $\varepsilon_y=-2\cdot10^{-4}$. The membrane resultants vanish, with $D=5\,\mathrm{MN\,m}$ and $\nu=\dfrac14$. What is $M_x$?

- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $17.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $6.25\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

In the absence of membrane forces, the bottom-face strains are $\varepsilon_x=-\dfrac h2\,w_{,xx}$ and $\varepsilon_y=-\dfrac h2\,w_{,yy}$. Dividing by $-0.1\,\mathrm m$ gives $w_{,xx}=-3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and $w_{,yy}=2\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. The moment–curvature relation then gives
$$
M_x=-D(w_{,xx}+\nu w_{,yy})=-5000\left(-0.003+\frac14\cdot0.002\right)\mathrm{kN}=12.5\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$
The positive sign is consistent with tension in the bottom face along $x$.
