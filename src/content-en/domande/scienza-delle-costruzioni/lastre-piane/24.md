A strip in cylindrical bending is supported along $x=0$ and $x=L$ and carries a uniform load $q$. Its thickness increases linearly from one support to the other, so its flexural rigidity $D$ varies with $x$. Which quantity is the same as for a strip of constant thickness with the same span and loading?

- The midspan deflection
- The slope $w'$ at both supports
- The moment $M_x$ at every section
- None, because the equation $Dw''''=q$ no longer holds

## Explanation

The simply supported strip is statically determinate. Equilibrium alone gives the reactions and bending moment. The bending moment is therefore $M_x(x)=\dfrac{qLx}{2}-\dfrac{qx^2}{2}$ regardless of thickness. The rigidity enters the relation $M_x=-D(x)\,w''$, so curvature, slope, and downward deflection depend on the variation of $D$. For variable $D$, equation $Dw''''=q$ does not hold because differentiating $D(x)\,w''$ twice also introduces derivatives of $D$. Equilibrium then takes the form $\bigl(D\,w''\bigr)''=q$, and the moment is the same as for a strip of constant thickness.
