The end joints of truss member $AB$ initially have positions $A=(0,0)$ and $B=(4,3)$, in meters. The loads produce joint displacements $\mathbf u_A = (2,\,1)\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\mathbf u_B = (6,\,-2)\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the first-order change in member length, taking elongation as positive?

- $5\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $-1.4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $4\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

To first order, the elongation is the projection of the relative displacement onto the unit vector along the undeformed member, $\delta L_{AB} = (\mathbf u_B - \mathbf u_A)\cdot\mathbf e_{AB}$. The relative displacement is $\mathbf u_B - \mathbf u_A = (4,\,-3)\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the unit vector is $\mathbf e_{AB} = \left(\dfrac45,\,\dfrac35\right)$. Therefore
$$
\delta L_{AB} = 4\cdot\frac45 - 3\cdot\frac35 = \frac{16-9}{5}\,\mathrm{mm} = 1.4\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The value $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ is the magnitude of the relative displacement. It includes the component perpendicular to the member, which represents rotation.
