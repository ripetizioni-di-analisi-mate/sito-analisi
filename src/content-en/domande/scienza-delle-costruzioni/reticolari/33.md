Two triangular trusses are joined at their common hinge $B$. The first has vertices $A=(0,0)$, $P=(1,3)$, and $B$, and the second $B$, $Q=(7,5)$, and $C=(8,2)$. Coordinates are in meters. The supports at $A$ and $C$ are fixed pins. For what position of $B$ is the structure unstable?

- $B=(4,4)$
- $B=(4,0)$
- $B=(4,3)$
- $B=(4,1)$

## Explanation

The structure has $j = 5$ joints, $m = 6$ members, and $r = 4$ reaction components, giving $m + r = 10 = 2j$ for every position of $B$. Each triangle is pinned at its external support and can only rotate about that pin. Point $B$ on the first triangle must therefore move perpendicular to $AB$, while the same point on the second triangle must move perpendicular to $CB$. These directions coincide when $A$, $B$, and $C$ are collinear. The triangles can then rotate together with the same displacement at $B$. Line $AC$ has equation $y = \dfrac{x}{4}$. For $x = 4$ this gives $y = 1$, so when $B=(4,1)$ the structure is **unstable**. At the other positions the structure is statically determinate, like a three-hinged arch.
