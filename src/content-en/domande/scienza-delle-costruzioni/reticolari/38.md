Members $AB$ and $BC$ are collinear, with $A=(0,0)$, $B=(2,0)$, and $C=(4,0)$ in meters. Joints $A$ and $C$ are fixed pins. Joint $B$ moves vertically by $v = 20\,\mathrm{mm}$. Using the new distances between joints, how much does each member elongate?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,0;C,4,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,cerniera" reticolare=1 tratteggi="0,0 2,0.4 4,0" quote="A,B,2 m;B,C,2 m" titolo="Intermediate joint between two collinear members"}

- $0\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $20\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The displacement of $B$ is perpendicular to both members. Its projection onto either member axis is zero, and so is the first-order elongation. The new length of $AB$ is $\sqrt{L^2 + v^2}$, with $L = 2000\,\mathrm{mm}$. Since $v$ is small compared with $L$, we have
$$
\sqrt{L^2+v^2} - L \approx \frac{v^2}{2L} = \frac{20^2}{2\cdot 2000}\,\mathrm{mm} = 0.1\,\mathrm{mm},
$$
and the same holds for $BC$. The elongation is second order in the displacement. The two collinear members therefore do not prevent infinitesimal vertical motion of $B$.
