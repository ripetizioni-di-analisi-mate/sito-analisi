A triangular truss has joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(4,3)$, and $C=(0,4)$, with coordinates in meters, and members $AB$, $AC$, and $BC$. It has a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$ whose reaction direction can be chosen. Which direction of the roller reaction makes the structure unstable?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,3;C,0,4" aste="A-B;A-C;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera" reticolare=1 titolo="Triangular truss pinned at $A$, with the orientation of the roller at $B$ to be chosen"}

- Vertical
- Horizontal
- Parallel to the vector $(-3,\,4)$
- Parallel to the vector $(4,\,3)$

## Explanation

The triangle is rigid, so its only possible motions are rigid-body motions. The pin at $A$ prevents translation but permits infinitesimal rotation about $A$, which moves $B$ perpendicular to segment $AB$, along $(-3,\,4)$. The roller prevents this motion if its reaction has a component along $(-3,\,4)$, that is, if its line of action does not pass through $A$. A reaction parallel to $(4,\,3)$ lies on line $AB$ and has zero moment about $A$. Rotation therefore remains possible and the structure is **unstable**. By contrast, the vertical, horizontal, and $(-3,\,4)$-parallel reactions each have a nonzero moment arm about $A$.
