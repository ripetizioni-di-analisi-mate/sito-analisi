The truss shown has joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(6,0)$, $C=(12,0)$, $D=(3,3)$, $E=(9,3)$, and $F=(6,6)$, with coordinates in meters, and members $AB$, $BC$, $AD$, $DF$, $FE$, $EC$, $DB$, $BE$, and $BF$. There is a pin at $A$ and a roller on a horizontal surface at $C$. A downward force of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at joint $D$, and an upward force of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at joint $E$. What is the axial force in vertical member $BF$?

- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ in compression
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension

## Explanation

The geometry is symmetric about line $x = 6$ and the loads are vertical. The horizontal reaction is therefore zero, and the supports act symmetrically. Reflection about line $x = 6$ maps the load at $D$ to a downward load of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $E$, the opposite of the given load there. Reversing the signs in the reflected solution therefore gives equilibrium under the original loads. The solution is unique, so forces in symmetric members have opposite signs. Vertical $BF$ maps onto itself and therefore has $N_{BF} = -N_{BF} = 0$. The same argument gives $N_{FE} = -N_{DF}$. At unloaded joint $F$, horizontal equilibrium gives $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(N_{FE} - N_{DF}) = 0$, so $DF$ and $FE$ are also zero-force members. The vertical is unloaded, as predicted.
