A triangular roof truss with joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(8,0)$, and $C=(4,3)$ (coordinates in meters) is supported at $A$ and $B$ and carries a load at its apex $C$. The axial forces are $N_{AB} = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $N_{AC} = N_{BC} = -25\,\mathrm{kN}$. Which members must be checked for lateral buckling?

- Only for $AB$
- For all three members
- For none, because the members have pinned ends
- For $AC$ and $BC$

## Explanation

A straight member can buckle laterally only in compression, because compression amplifies a small lateral deviation from its straight configuration. Members $AC$ and $BC$ have negative axial forces and act as struts. Each therefore requires a buckling check, as discussed in the chapter on elastic stability. Chord $AB$ is in tension. Tension tends to straighten a member that is displaced laterally. Pinned ends do not prevent buckling. A member pinned at both ends is the reference case for Euler's critical load.
