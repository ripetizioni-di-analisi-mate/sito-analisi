A triangular truss has joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(8,0)$, and $C=(4,3)$, with coordinates in meters, a pin at $A$, and a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. It carries a downward load $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$. An additional force acts at joint $A$ with components $(6,\,-8)\,\mathrm{kN}$, positive to the right and upward. What changes?

- The member forces stay the same, and the reaction at $A$ changes by $(6,\,-8)\,\mathrm{kN}$
- The member forces stay the same, and the reaction at $A$ changes by $(-6,\,8)\,\mathrm{kN}$
- The forces in $AB$ and $AC$ change, and the reaction at $A$ stays the same
- Only the vertical reaction at $B$ changes

## Explanation

The added force acts at the pin joint, so it has zero moment about $A$. The moment equation therefore leaves the roller reaction at $B$ unchanged. The external forces at joints $B$ and $C$ are unchanged. The forces in $AC$, $BC$, and $AB$, found from equilibrium of these two joints, therefore stay the same. The added force now enters the equilibrium equation for joint $A$, so the change in the pin reaction is equal and opposite to that force, namely $(-6,\,8)\,\mathrm{kN}$.
