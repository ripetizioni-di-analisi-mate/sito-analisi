A bracing panel has joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(4,0)$, $C=(4,3)$, and $D=(0,3)$, with coordinates in meters, members $AB$, $BC$, $CD$, and $DA$, a pin at $A$, and a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. Diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ are cables and can carry only tension. A rightward horizontal force of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $D$. Which cable carries load, and what is its tension?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,4,3;D,0,3" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-A" catene="A-C;B-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" reticolare=1 forze="D,1.1,0,10 kN" quote="A,B,4 m;4.55 3,4.55 0,3 m,-0.55" titolo="Panel braced by two cables"}

- Cable $AC$, carrying tension $12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- Cable $BD$, carrying tension $12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- Cable $AC$, carrying tension $10\,\mathrm{kN}$
- Both cables, each carrying tension $6.25\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Assume only one cable is taut and check the sign of the resulting force. With only $AC$ present, the structure is statically determinate. Vertical $DA$ and horizontal chord $DC$ meet at joint $D$, so horizontal equilibrium gives $N_{CD} = -10\,\mathrm{kN}$. The compressed chord pushes $C$ to the right with force $10\,\mathrm{kN}$. At joint $C$ only the cable has a horizontal force component, equal to $-\dfrac45 N_{AC}$ using the unit vector from $C$ to $A$. Thus $10 - \dfrac45 N_{AC} = 0$ and $N_{AC} = 12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, a tensile force that the cable can carry. With only $BD$ present, the same procedure gives $N_{BD} = -12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, a compressive force that a cable cannot carry. Thus $BD$ remains **slack**.
