In the truss shown, supported by a pin at $A$ and a roller at $E$, verticals $BF$ and $DH$ connect bottom-chord joints $B=(3,0)$ and $D=(9,0)$ to top-chord joints $F=(3,3)$ and $H=(9,3)$. When loads act only at $F$, $G$, and $H$, these two verticals carry zero force. A designer proposes removing them to save material. Which statement is true?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,12,0;F,3,3;G,6,3;H,9,3" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;F-G;G-H;A-F;H-E;B-F;C-G;D-H;F-C;C-H" vincoli="A,cerniera;E,carrello" reticolare=1  quote="A,B,3 m,0.9;B,C,3 m,0.9;C,D,3 m,0.9;D,E,3 m,0.9;12.55 3,12.55 0,3 m,-0.55" titolo="Vertical members $BF$ and $DH$ to be removed"}

- The truss becomes unstable because joints $B$ and $D$ can move vertically
- The truss remains statically determinate because the removed members carried zero force
- The truss becomes statically indeterminate
- The truss remains statically determinate, but only for loads on the upper joints

## Explanation

Without the two verticals we have $m + r = 11 + 3 = 14$, while $2j = 16$, so the number of equilibrium equations exceeds the number of unknowns. Joint $B$ remains connected only to collinear members $AB$ and $BC$. An infinitesimal vertical displacement of $B$ therefore leaves their lengths unchanged to first order. The same holds at $D$. The structure therefore has two independent mechanisms and is **unstable**. A member that carries zero force under a particular load can still be necessary for stability. Without $BF$, it would no longer be possible to balance a vertical load at $B$.
