Members $AB$ and $BC$ are collinear, with $A=(0,0)$, $B=(3,0)$, and $C=(6,0)$ in meters. Joints $A$ and $C$ are fixed pins. A downward force of $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at joint $B$. Which statement holds in the initial configuration if the members carry axial force only?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,cerniera" reticolare=1 forze="B,0,-1,6 kN" quote="A,B,3 m,0.9;B,C,3 m,0.9" titolo="Transverse force at the joint between two collinear members"}

- Each member carries tension $3\,\mathrm{kN}$
- The forces in the two members cannot balance the force at $B$
- Only one member carries $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ in compression
- The two members carry equal and opposite axial forces of magnitude $6\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Whatever their axial forces, both members exert forces along the horizontal axis at $B$. The load is therefore the only term in the vertical joint equilibrium equation, and the equation $-6 = 0$ cannot be satisfied. The two columns of the equilibrium matrix associated with $B$ are parallel, so the vertical load lies outside its column space. Indeed, the joint can undergo an infinitesimal vertical displacement without lengthening either member to first order. Equilibrium can be reached only in a deformed configuration after a finite displacement.
