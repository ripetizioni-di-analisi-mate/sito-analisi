An unloaded, statically determinate triangular truss has joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(6,0)$, and $C=(3,4)$ (coordinates in meters), a pin at $A$, and a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. Chord $AB$ undergoes a uniform temperature increase of $40\,\mathrm K$, while the other members remain at their initial temperature. What axial forces develop in the members?

- $AB$ carries compression $N = -EA\,\alpha\,\Delta T$, and the other members carry zero force
- $AC$ and $BC$ in tension, $AB$ in compression
- All three members in compression
- No axial force

## Explanation

The temperature change introduces no external forces, so only member forces and support reactions act on the joints. Since the structure is statically determinate, the unique equilibrium solution for zero load has zero member forces and zero reactions. Member $AB$ is free to undergo the thermal elongation $\alpha\,\Delta T\,L$. The roller moves, and $C$ shifts slightly so that the lengths of $AC$ and $BC$ remain unchanged. The force $-EA\,\alpha\,\Delta T$ would develop if the ends of $AB$ were restrained against moving apart, as can occur in a statically indeterminate structure.
