In a K-truss, joint $M$ is halfway up a vertical member and has no external load. The two portions of the vertical extend upward and downward from the joint, and two diagonals lead to points $(-4,\,3)$ and $(-4,\,-3)$ relative to $M$, with coordinates in meters. The upper diagonal carries a tensile force $15\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the axial force in the lower diagonal?

::struttura{nodi="M,0,0;U,0,3,-;L,0,-3,-;P,-4,3,-;R,-4,-3,-" aste="M-U;M-L;M-P;M-R" etichette="-4.3,3.3,upper diagonal;-4.3,-3.5,lower diagonal" titolo="K-joint at midheight of the vertical member"}

- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ in compression
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ in compression

## Explanation

The two portions of the vertical member do not enter the horizontal equilibrium equation at joint $M$. The diagonals have length $5\,\mathrm m$ and outward unit vectors $\left(-\dfrac45,\,\dfrac35\right)$ and $\left(-\dfrac45,\,-\dfrac35\right)$, both with horizontal component $-\dfrac45$. Horizontal equilibrium gives $-\dfrac45 N_{\mathrm{top}} - \dfrac45 N_{\mathrm{bottom}} = 0$, hence $N_{\mathrm{bottom}} = -N_{\mathrm{top}} = -15\,\mathrm{kN}$. The two diagonals of the K therefore carry equal and opposite member forces, and the lower diagonal is **in compression**.
