In the truss shown, bottom chord $ABCDE$ has joints at horizontal coordinates $0$, $4$, $8$, $12$, and $16\,\mathrm m$, and top chord $FGH$ has $F=(4,3)$, $G=(8,3)$, and $H=(12,3)$. The end members are $AF$ and $HE$, the verticals $BF$, $CG$, and $DH$, and the diagonals $BG$ and $DG$. There is a pin at $A$ and a roller on a horizontal surface at $E$. Each of joints $F$, $G$, and $H$ carries a downward load $12\,\mathrm{kN}$. What are the signs of the forces in diagonals $BG$ and $DG$ and in verticals $BF$ and $DH$?

- Diagonals in compression and vertical members in tension

## Explanation

By symmetry each reaction is $18\,\mathrm{kN}$. Cut through the second panel and isolate the left portion. The reaction of $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ and the load of $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $F$ have an upward resultant $6\,\mathrm{kN}$. Of the cut members, only diagonal $BG$ has a vertical force component. The unit vector from $B$ to $G$ is $\left(\dfrac45,\,\dfrac35\right)$, so $6 + \dfrac35 N_{BG} = 0$ and $N_{BG} = -10\,\mathrm{kN}$. At joint $B$ the chords are horizontal, so vertical equilibrium gives $N_{BF} + \dfrac35 N_{BG} = 0$ and $N_{BF} = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$. By symmetry, $DG$ and $DH$ carry the same forces as $BG$ and $BF$, respectively. When the diagonals slope upward toward midspan, the diagonals are **in compression** and the verticals are **in tension**.
