All members of a statically determinate steel truss with $E = 210\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$ are replaced by aluminum members with the same cross sections and $E = 70\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The loads stay the same, and the displacements remain small. How do the member forces and joint displacements change?

- Both forces and displacements stay the same
- The forces fall to one third of their original values, and the displacements stay the same
- The forces stay the same, and the joint displacements triple
- Both forces and displacements triple

## Explanation

In a statically determinate truss, equilibrium alone determines the member forces. These equations depend on geometry and loading, so the forces are unaffected by the change of material. The elongation of each member is $\Delta L = \dfrac{NL}{EA}$. Reducing $E$ to one-third of its original value therefore triples each elongation. For small displacements, member elongations depend linearly on joint displacements through $\delta L_{AB} = (\mathbf u_B - \mathbf u_A)\cdot\mathbf e_{AB}$. All joint displacements therefore triple as well.
