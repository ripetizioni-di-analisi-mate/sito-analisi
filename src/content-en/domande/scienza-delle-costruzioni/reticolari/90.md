A member of a statically determinate truss has length $4\,\mathrm m$, axial rigidity $EA = 40\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$, and coefficient of thermal expansion $\alpha = 1.2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm K^{-1}$. It carries a tensile force of $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ under the applied loads and simultaneously undergoes a uniform temperature increase of $25\,\mathrm K$. What is its total change in length?

- $2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $3.2\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The total change in length is the sum of the elastic and thermal elongations, so $\Delta L = \dfrac{NL}{EA} + \alpha\,\Delta T\,L$. The elastic elongation is $\dfrac{20\cdot 4000}{40\,000} = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$. The thermal elongation is $1.2\cdot10^{-5}\cdot 25\cdot 4000 = 1.2\,\mathrm{mm}$. Therefore, $\Delta L = 3.2\,\mathrm{mm}$. Because the truss is statically determinate, the temperature increase leaves the axial force imposed by the loads unchanged.
