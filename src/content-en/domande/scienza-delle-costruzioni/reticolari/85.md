A steel diagonal has cross-sectional area $400\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, Young's modulus $E = 200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and axial force $N = -48\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is its longitudinal strain?

- $-6\cdot10^{-4}$
- $6\cdot10^{-4}$
- $-6\cdot10^{-1}$
- $-6\cdot10^{-7}$

## Explanation

The average normal stress is $\sigma = \dfrac NA = \dfrac{-48\,000\,\mathrm N}{400\,\mathrm{mm^2}} = -120\,\mathrm{MPa}$. For a linearly elastic material, the axial strain is $\varepsilon = \dfrac{\sigma}{E}$, giving $\varepsilon = \dfrac{-120}{200\,000} = -6\cdot10^{-4}$. The negative sign indicates shortening, as expected in compression. The value $-6\cdot10^{-7}$ results from leaving the force in kilonewtons, while $-6\cdot10^{-1}$ results from expressing the modulus in gigapascals.
