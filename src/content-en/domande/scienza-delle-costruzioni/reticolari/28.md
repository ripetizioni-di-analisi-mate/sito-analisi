A triangular truss has joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(6,0)$, and $C=(3,3)$, with coordinates in meters, and members $AB$, $AC$, and $BC$. It is supported by three rollers. The reactions at $A$ and $B$ are parallel to vectors $(1,\,-1)$ and $(1,\,1)$, respectively, and the reaction at $C$ is vertical. What infinitesimal motion do the supports permit?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,3,3" aste="A-B;A-C;B-C" vincoli="A,carrello,315;B,carrello,225;C,carrello,90" reticolare=1 titolo="Triangular truss supported on three rollers"}

- Rotation about joint $C$
- Rotation about point $(3,\,-3)$
- Horizontal translation
- No motion, because $m + r = 2j$

## Explanation

The three members form a rigid triangle and $m + r = 3 + 3 = 6 = 2j$, so the count balances. The reaction lines are $y = -x$ through $A$, $y = x - 6$ through $B$, and $x = 3$ through $C$. All pass through point $(3,\,-3)$. An infinitesimal rotation about that point moves each joint perpendicular to the line joining it to the center and hence perpendicular to the line of action of its roller reaction. None of the restraints therefore prevents rotation, and the structure is **unstable**. The rollers at $A$ and $B$ do prevent horizontal translation because their reactions have horizontal components.
