A four-bar linkage without diagonals consists of members $AB$, $BC$, $CD$, and $DA$ and joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(4,0)$, $C=(4,3)$, and $D=(0,3)$. Coordinates are in meters. There is a pin at $A$ and a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. Which set of infinitesimal displacements, with $\mathbf u_A = \mathbf u_B = \mathbf 0$, preserves all member lengths to first order?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,4,3;D,0,3" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-A" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" reticolare=1 quote="A,B,4 m;4.55 3,4.55 0,3 m,-0.55" titolo="Panel without a diagonal"}

- $\mathbf u_C = \mathbf u_D = (0,\,5)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $\mathbf u_C = (5,\,0)\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\mathbf u_D = (-5,\,0)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $\mathbf u_C = (5,\,0)\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\mathbf u_D = \mathbf 0$
- $\mathbf u_C = \mathbf u_D = (5,\,0)\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

For each member, the first-order elongation is the projection of the relative displacement of its ends onto its unit vector. Members $AD$ and $BC$ are vertical, so their elongations are the vertical displacement components at $D$ and $C$, respectively. Chord $CD$ is horizontal, so its elongation is the difference between the horizontal displacement components of $\mathbf u_D$ and $\mathbf u_C$, while $AB$ remains stationary. For $\mathbf u_C = \mathbf u_D = (5,\,0)\,\mathrm{mm}$, the vertical components vanish and the horizontal components are equal. All member lengths are therefore unchanged to first order. This motion is a **mechanism** in which the rectangle becomes a parallelogram. Vertical displacement lengthens the verticals, whereas the other two sets of displacements change the length of $CD$.
