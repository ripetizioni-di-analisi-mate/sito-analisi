The truss shown has joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(3,0)$, $C=(6,0)$, $D=(9,0)$, $E=(3,4)$, and $F=(6,4)$, with coordinates in meters, and members $AB$, $BC$, $CD$, $AE$, $EF$, $FD$, $BE$, $CF$, and $BF$. There is a pin at $A$ and a roller on a horizontal surface at $D$. A vertical load acts at only one joint. For which load positions is $CF$ a zero-force member?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,3,4;F,6,4" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;A-E;E-F;F-D;B-E;C-F;B-F" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello" reticolare=1 quote="A,B,3 m,0.9;B,C,3 m,0.9;C,D,3 m,0.9;9.55 4,9.55 0,4 m,-0.55" titolo="Trapezoidal truss"}

- For a load at any joint other than $C$
- Only for a load at $F$
- Only for a load at $E$ or $F$
- For no position, because $CF$ connects the two chords

## Explanation

Collinear horizontal members $BC$ and $CD$ and vertical $CF$ meet at joint $C$. The vertical equilibrium equation at joint $C$ contains only the force in the vertical and any load applied at $C$. Let $P_C$ be the magnitude of the downward load at $C$. Then $N_{CF} - P_C = 0$, that is, $N_{CF} = P_C$. The vertical is therefore **unloaded** when the load acts at any other joint and carries a tensile force $N_{CF} = P$ when the load acts at $C$.
