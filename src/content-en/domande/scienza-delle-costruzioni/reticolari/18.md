A truss member connects joints $A=(0,0)$ and $B=(4,3)$, whose coordinates are in meters. Its self-weight is $0.8\,\mathrm{kN}$ per meter of member length. In the truss model, replace the weight with equivalent joint loads. What vertical loads should be applied to the two joints?

- $1.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $A$ and $1.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$, downward
- $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $A$ and $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$, downward
- $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $A$ and $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$, downward
- $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $A$ and no load at $B$

## Explanation

The member length is $\sqrt{4^2+3^2} = 5\,\mathrm m$, so its total weight is $0.8\cdot 5 = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$. The weight is uniform along the member, so its resultant passes through the midpoint. The midpoint is the same horizontal distance from both joints. Moment equilibrium of the isolated member therefore assigns half its weight to each end, giving $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $A$ and $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$. The value $1.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ results from multiplying the weight per unit length by the horizontal projection $4\,\mathrm m$, although the specified weight is per unit of member length.
