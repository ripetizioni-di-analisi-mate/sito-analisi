A statically determinate truss consists of a triangle with joints $A=(0,0)$, $B=(6,0)$, and $C=(3,4)$, in meters, a pin at $A$, and a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. A new joint $G$ is added with new connecting members. Which addition keeps the structure stable and statically determinate?

- $G=(9,4)$ connected only to $C$ by one member
- $G=(9,4)$ connected to $A$, $B$, and $C$ by three members
- $G=(9,4)$ connected to $B$ and $C$ by two members
- $G=(9,0)$ connected to $A$ and $B$ by two members

## Explanation

A new joint adds two equilibrium equations, so exactly two new members are needed to maintain $m + r = 2j$. With only one member, joint $G$ can rotate about $C$ and the structure is unstable. With three new members there are more unknowns than equations and the structure is statically indeterminate. For $G=(9,0)$, members $GA$ and $GB$ lie on the same line. An infinitesimal vertical displacement of $G$ therefore leaves their lengths unchanged to first order, and the structure is unstable. For $G=(9,4)$, points $B$, $C$, and $G$ are noncollinear, so triangle $BCG$ is rigid and the structure remains **statically determinate**.
