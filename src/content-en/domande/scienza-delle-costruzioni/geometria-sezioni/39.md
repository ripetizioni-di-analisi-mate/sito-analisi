A thin-walled circular tube has mean radius $R=50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $t=4\,\mathrm{mm}$. Using the thin-wall approximation, find the first moment of its upper half about the horizontal diameter.

::struttura{riferimento=0 cerchi="0,0,52;0,0,48,foro" frecce="0,0,70,0,z;0,0,0,70,y;0,0,-35.36,35.36,R" titolo="Thin-walled tube of mean radius R"}

- $20\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3}$
- $10\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^3}$
- $\dfrac{40\,000}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^3}$
- $10\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3}$

## Explanation

Parameterize the upper half of the wall by angle $\theta$ from $0$ to $\pi$. A wall segment of length $R\,d\theta$ has area $tR\,d\theta$ and elevation $R\sin\theta$. Thus
$$
S=\int_0^{\pi}R\sin\theta\;tR\,d\theta=2R^2t .
$$
With $R=50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $t=4\,\mathrm{mm}$ the result is $S=2\cdot2\,500\cdot4=20\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3}$. The elevation $\dfrac{4R}{3\pi}$ belongs to a solid semicircle, whose area also occupies the region close to the center.
