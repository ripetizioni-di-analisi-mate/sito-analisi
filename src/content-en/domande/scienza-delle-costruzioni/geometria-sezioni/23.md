Relative to centroidal axes $x$ and $y$, a section has $I_x = 12 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$, $I_y = 8 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$, and $I_{xy} = 3 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$, with $I_{xy} = \int xy\,\mathrm{d}A$. Rotate axis $x$ through $45^\circ$, taking counterclockwise angles as positive. Find the second moment of area about the new axis $x'$. Round the coefficient to two decimal places.

- $12.00 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $7.00 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $10.00 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $13.00 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$

## Explanation

The signed distance from the new axis is $y' = -x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta$. Expanding $\int (y')^2\,\mathrm{d}A$ gives $I_x' = I_x\cos^2\theta + I_y\sin^2\theta - 2I_{xy}\sin\theta\cos\theta = 7.00 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$.
