Four solid sections have the same area, $3\,600\,\mathrm{mm^2}$. Which has the largest second moment of area about its horizontal centroidal axis?

- The square of side $60\,\mathrm{mm}$
- The rectangle of width $120\,\mathrm{mm}$ and depth $30\,\mathrm{mm}$
- The circle of area $3\,600\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- The rectangle of width $30\,\mathrm{mm}$ and depth $120\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

For equal areas, the second moment increases as material lies farther from the axis. The values are $\dfrac{30\cdot120^3}{12}=4.32\cdot10^6$ for the tall rectangle, $\dfrac{60^4}{12}=1.08\cdot10^6$ for the square, and $\dfrac{120\cdot30^3}{12}=0.27\cdot10^6$ for the shallow rectangle, in $\mathrm{mm^4}$. For the circle $I=\dfrac{\pi r^4}{4}=\dfrac{A^2}{4\pi}\simeq1.03\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$. The tall rectangle therefore has the largest value because its material extends farthest from the horizontal axis.
