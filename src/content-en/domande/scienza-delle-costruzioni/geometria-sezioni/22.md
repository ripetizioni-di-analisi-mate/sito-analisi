An angle section of thickness $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ has a horizontal bottom leg of width $80\,\mathrm{mm}$ and a vertical left leg of height $120\,\mathrm{mm}$. The dimensions are external. Centroidal axes $x$ and $y$ point to the right and upward. Calculate $I_{xy} = \int xy\,\mathrm{d}A$ to the nearest $\mathrm{mm^4}$.

- $0\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $-972\,632\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $972\,632\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $-1\,945\,263\,\mathrm{mm^4}$

## Explanation

Split the angle into its bottom leg and the vertical portion above it. Each rectangle has zero product of inertia about its centroidal axes parallel to $x$ and $y$. For the two rectangles the parallel-axis contribution is $\dfrac{A_1 A_2}{A_1 + A_2} \times \Delta x \Delta y$. From the bottom leg centroid to the vertical portion centroid, $\Delta x = -35.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\Delta y = 60.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. This gives $-972\,632\,\mathrm{mm^4}$.
