A compression member has principal radii of gyration $\rho_z=80\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\rho_y=50\,\mathrm{mm}$. An intermediate brace halves the effective buckling length about $y$. The effective lengths are $6\,\mathrm{m}$ for buckling about $z$ and $3\,\mathrm{m}$ for buckling about $y$. Which slenderness ratio $\lambda=\dfrac{L_0}{\rho}$ is larger?

- $\lambda_y=60$
- $\lambda_z=75$
- $\lambda_y=120$
- $\lambda_z=37.5$

## Explanation

For buckling about $z$ use $\rho_z$ and the corresponding effective length, giving $\lambda_z=\dfrac{6\,000}{80}=75$. For buckling about $y$, the result is $\lambda_y=\dfrac{3\,000}{50}=60$. The larger slenderness ratio is therefore $\lambda_z=75$ even though $\rho_z$ is the larger radius of gyration. The radii alone identify the larger slenderness ratio only when the two effective lengths are equal.
