A section has $I_x = 9 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$, $I_y = 3 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$, and centroidal product of inertia $I_{xy} = 2 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$, with $I_{xy} = \int xy\,\mathrm{d}A$. Find the angle from $x$ to the principal axis of maximum second moment of area. Taking counterclockwise as positive, give an angle between $-90^\circ$ and $+90^\circ$ to one decimal place.

- $-16.8^\circ$
- $-33.7^\circ$
- $16.8^\circ$
- $73.2^\circ$

## Explanation

$I(\theta)$ is the sum of the mean and $\dfrac{(I_x - I_y)\cos(2\theta)}{2} - I_{xy}\sin(2\theta)$. The maximum occurs when $(\cos 2\theta, \sin 2\theta)$ points in the same direction as $\left(\dfrac{I_x - I_y}{2}, -I_{xy}\right)$. The required angle is $-16.8^\circ$. The other principal direction gives the minimum second moment of area.
