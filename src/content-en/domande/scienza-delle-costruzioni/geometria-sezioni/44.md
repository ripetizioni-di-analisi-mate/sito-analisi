A square lamina of side $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ has a steel lower half and a wooden upper half of much lower density. Which statement about its geometric centroid $G$ and center of mass is true?

::struttura{riferimento=0 poligoni="0,0 100,0 100,100 0,100" linee="0,50 100,50" etichette="50,25,steel;50,75,wood" frecce="0,0,116,0,z;0,0,0,116,y" quote="100 0,100 100,100 mm,9" titolo="Lamina composed of two materials"}

- Both $G$ and the center of mass are at height $50\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $G$ is at height $50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the center of mass is lower
- Both $G$ and the center of mass lie below height $50\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $G$ lies below height $50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the center of mass is at height $50\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The geometric centroid is the area-weighted mean of the coordinates, independent of material. The square is symmetric about the horizontal line at elevation $50\,\mathrm{mm}$, so $G$ lies on that line. The center of mass weights each portion by its mass. The steel half is much heavier than the wooden half, so the center of mass lies below elevation $50\,\mathrm{mm}$.
