A solid semicircular section has radius $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and a horizontal diameter. Find its second moment of area about the diameter.

::struttura{riferimento=0 poligoni="40.00,0.00 38.64,10.35 34.64,20.00 28.28,28.28 20.00,34.64 10.35,38.64 0.00,40.00 -10.35,38.64 -20.00,34.64 -28.28,28.28 -34.64,20.00 -38.64,10.35 -40.00,0.00" frecce="0,0,0,56,y;-56,0,58,0,z" quote="0 0,40 0,40 mm,8" titolo="Semicircle above its diameter"}

- $640\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $160\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $1\,280\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}$
- $320\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}$

## Explanation

A full disk of radius $R$ has diametral second moment $\dfrac{\pi R^4}{4}$. Its horizontal diameter splits it into mirror-image semicircles with equal contributions to the integral of $y^2$. Thus for the semicircle,
$$
I=\frac{\pi R^4}{8}=\frac{2\,560\,000\pi}{8}\,\mathrm{mm^4}=320\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
$$
The axis in question is the diameter, which does not pass through the centroid of the semicircle.
