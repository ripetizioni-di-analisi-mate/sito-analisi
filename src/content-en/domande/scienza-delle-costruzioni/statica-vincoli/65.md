A straight segment over $0\leq x\leq2\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a downward vertical load of intensity $q(x)=3x^2$, in $\mathrm{kN/m}$ with $x$ in meters. At what coordinate does its resultant act?

::grafico{f="3*x^2" xmin=-0.3 xmax=2.4 ymin=-1 ymax=13 titolo="Load intensity $q(x)=3x^2$"}

- $x_Q=1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x_Q=\dfrac{4}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x_Q=0.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x_Q=1.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The resultant is
$$
Q=\int_0^2 3x^2\,\mathrm{d}x=8\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$
The first moment of the load about the origin is
$$
\int_0^2 x\cdot3x^2\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{3\cdot2^4}{4}=12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}.
$$
Thus $x_Q=\dfrac{12}{8}=1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The resultant lies beyond two thirds of the interval because a parabolic load is more concentrated toward $x=2\,\mathrm{m}$ than a triangular load.
