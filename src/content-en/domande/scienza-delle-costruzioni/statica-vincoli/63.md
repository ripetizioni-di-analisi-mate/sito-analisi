A seesaw is a rigid horizontal board supported at its midpoint by a pin, and its weight acts through the pivot. A child of weight $300\,\mathrm{N}$ sits a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left of the pivot. How far to the right must a child of weight $400\,\mathrm{N}$ sit to keep the board horizontal and in equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="S,-2.5,0;O,0,0;D,2.5,0" aste="S-O;O-D" vincoli="O,cerniera" forze="-2 0,0,-1.1,W_1" quote="-2 0,O,2 m,-0.8" titolo="Seesaw on a pivot"}

- $\dfrac{8}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pin is the only support, so the board can rotate about its pivot. Equilibrium requires zero resultant moment of the loads about the pivot.

The first child produces counterclockwise moment $300\cdot2=600\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The second, a distance $d$ to the right, produces clockwise moment $400\,d$.

Moment equilibrium requires $400\,d=600$, giving $d=1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The heavier child sits closer to the pivot.
