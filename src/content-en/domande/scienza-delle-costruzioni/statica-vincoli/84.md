The horizontal bar shown is pinned at $A=(0,0)$ and supported at $B=(6,0)$, with coordinates in meters, by a vertical tension-only cable. It extends to the left of $A$ as far as $D=(-2,0)$. A single downward force acts at coordinate $x$ on the bar. For which positions of the force is the cable in tension?

::struttura{nodi="D,-2,0;A,0,0;B,6,0;K,6,2.5,-" aste="D-A;A-B" catene="B-K" vincoli="A,cerniera" linee="5.4,2.5 6.6,2.5" quote="D,A,2 m,-0.7;A,B,6 m,-0.7" titolo="Bar with a vertical cable and an overhang"}

- For $-2\leq x<0\,\mathrm{m}$
- For every position between $D$ and $B$
- Only for $x=3\,\mathrm{m}$
- For $0<x\leq6\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $P$ denote the load magnitude and $T$ the cable tension, positive when it pulls the beam upward. The pin reactions have zero moment arm about $A$, so moment equilibrium gives $6T-xP=0$.

Thus $T=\dfrac{xP}{6}$, positive only for $x>0$. For $x<0$ equilibrium would require compression, which the cable cannot carry.

The cable is therefore in tension for a force between $A$ and $B$, excluding $A$. With the force on overhang $DA$ the cable goes slack and the bar rotates about $A$.
