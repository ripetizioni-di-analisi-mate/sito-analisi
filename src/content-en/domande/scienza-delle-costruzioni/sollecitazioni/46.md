Horizontal beam $AB$ of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$ has a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$ and carries only vertical loads. The shear force is constant at $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ between $x=0$ and $x=2\,\mathrm{m}$, then decreases linearly to $-8\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $x=6\,\mathrm{m}$. Which loading produces this diagram?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" diagramma="0,0,2,0,4;2,0,6,0,4-3*s" segni=1 titolo="Shear force diagram in kilonewtons"}

- A downward point force $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $x=2\,\mathrm{m}$
- A uniform downward load $2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ over the full span
- A uniform downward load $3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ between $x=2\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x=6\,\mathrm{m}$
- A downward triangular load varying from $0$ to $6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ between $x=2\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x=6\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Between $0$ and $2\,\mathrm{m}$ the shear is constant, so $T'=-q=0$ and the segment is unloaded. At $x=2\,\mathrm{m}$ there is no jump and therefore no concentrated force.

Between $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{m}$ the shear is linear with slope $\dfrac{-8-4}{4}=-3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Since $T'=-q$, the load on that interval is **uniform with intensity $3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$** downward.

A triangular load would give parabolic shear. A uniform load over the whole span would also give linear shear on the first interval. The reactions confirm the result because $V_A=4\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_B=8\,\mathrm{kN}$ balance the resultant $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ a distance $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$.
