A horizontal foundation beam $AB$ of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a downward column load $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ at each end. The soil supplies a uniform upward pressure $10\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ over the full length. The beam is in equilibrium under these loads alone. Find the midspan bending moment, taking bottom-fiber tension as positive.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" forze="A,0,-1.2,30 kN;B,0,-1.2,30 kN" carichi="A,B,0.5,0.5,10 kN/m,90" punti="3,0,S" quote="A,3 0,3 m,0.9;3 0,B,3 m,0.9" titolo="Foundation beam loaded by two columns"}

- $45\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $-45\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $-90\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

The soil pressure has upward resultant $10\cdot 6=60\,\mathrm{kN}$ equal to the sum of the two column loads. The actions are symmetric, so the beam is in equilibrium without supports and the method of sections applies.

Isolate the portion from $A$ to midspan. The downward column load $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ has moment arm $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and gives counterclockwise moment $90\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The upward soil resultant $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ on this segment has arm $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ and gives clockwise moment $45\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

Thus $M_{\mathrm{left}}=90-45=45\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ and, since $M=-M_{\mathrm{left}}$, the result is $M=-45\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The top fibers are in tension as in an inverted simply supported beam, because the distributed load acts upward and the point loads downward.
