The shear diagram shown belongs to a simply supported beam $AB$ of span $8\,\mathrm{m}$. Shear remains at $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ over the first meter, falls linearly to $-3\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $x=5\,\mathrm{m}$, and remains at $-3\,\mathrm{kN}$ as far as support $B$. Use areas under the diagram to find the maximum bending moment.

## Explanation

Moment is zero at support $A$ and increases while shear is positive. Between $x=1$ and $x=5\,\mathrm{m}$ shear is $5-2(x-1)$, vanishing at $x=3.5\,\mathrm{m}$. There $M'=T$ changes from positive to negative, so the moment attains its maximum.

The maximum equals the shear diagram area from $0$ to $3.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The initial rectangle contributes $5\cdot 1=5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ and the following triangle $\dfrac{5\cdot 2.5}{2}=6.25\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, giving
$$
M_{\max}=5+6.25=11.25\,\mathrm{kN\,m}.
$$
The diagram closes as a check. The negative area is $\dfrac{3\cdot 1.5}{2}+3\cdot 3=11.25\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, returning the moment to zero at $B$.
