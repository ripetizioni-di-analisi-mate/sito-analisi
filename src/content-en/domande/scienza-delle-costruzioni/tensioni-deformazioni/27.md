An isotropic sheet in plane stress has $E=200\,\mathrm{GPa}$, $\nu=0.25$, and thermal expansion coefficient $\alpha=12\times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Its restraints impose $\varepsilon_{x}=\varepsilon_{y}=0$ when the temperature changes uniformly by $40\,\mathrm{K}$. Find the normal stress $\sigma_{x}$, taking tension as positive. Round to one decimal place.

- $-96.0\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-128.0\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $128.0\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-192.0\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

Both directions have the same restraint, so $\sigma_{x}=\sigma_{y}=\sigma$. The total strain is $\varepsilon_{x}=\dfrac{(1-\nu)\sigma}{E}+\alpha \Delta T$. Setting it to zero gives $\sigma=-\dfrac{E\alpha \Delta T}{1-\nu}=-128.0\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
