A square element in the $xy$ plane carries a shear stress of $30\,\mathrm{MPa}$ on faces with normal $\pm\mathbf e_x$ and a shear stress of $20\,\mathrm{MPa}$ on faces with normal $\pm\mathbf e_y$, in the directions shown. No other stresses or body forces act. Which statement is true?

::struttura{poligoni="-1,-1 1,-1 1,1 -1,1" frecce="1.15,-0.6,1.15,0.6,30;-1.15,0.6,-1.15,-0.6;-0.6,1.15,0.6,1.15,20;0.6,-1.15,-0.6,-1.15" titolo="Shear stresses on the element faces in MPa"}

- The element is not in moment equilibrium
- The element is in equilibrium if $\sigma_x=\sigma_y$ is added
- The element is in equilibrium because forces on opposite faces cancel
- The element is not in force equilibrium

## Explanation

The shear forces on the two faces normal to $\pm\mathbf e_x$ are equal and opposite, as are those on the faces normal to $\pm\mathbf e_y$. Force equilibrium is therefore satisfied.

The shear forces on the faces normal to $\pm\mathbf e_x$ form a couple, as do those on the faces normal to $\pm\mathbf e_y$. Their moments have opposite senses and magnitudes proportional to $30$ on faces $x$ and to $20$ on faces $y$.

The two magnitudes differ, so the resultant moment is nonzero.

Moment equilibrium requires $\tau_{xy}=\tau_{yx}$, that is, **symmetry of the stress tensor**. This condition fails here.
