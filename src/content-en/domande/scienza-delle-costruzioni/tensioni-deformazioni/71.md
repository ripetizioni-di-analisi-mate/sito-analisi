A steel bar with $\nu=0.3$ and $\alpha=12\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ is fixed at both ends, so $\varepsilon_x=0$, while its lateral faces are free. Its temperature increases uniformly by $50\,\mathrm K$. Find the transverse strain $\varepsilon_y$.

- $180\cdot10^{-6}$
- $420\cdot10^{-6}$
- $600\cdot10^{-6}$
- $780\cdot10^{-6}$

## Explanation

With thermal expansion, Hooke's law becomes $\varepsilon_x=\dfrac{\sigma_x}{E}+\alpha\Delta T$ because the lateral stresses vanish.

Since $\varepsilon_x=0$, the result is $\dfrac{\sigma_x}{E}=-\alpha\Delta T$.

Along $y$ the relation is $\varepsilon_y=-\nu\dfrac{\sigma_x}{E}+\alpha\Delta T=(1+\nu)\alpha\Delta T$.

Thus $\varepsilon_y=1.3\cdot12\cdot10^{-6}\cdot50=780\cdot10^{-6}$. The Poisson effect of axial compression adds to the free thermal expansion.
