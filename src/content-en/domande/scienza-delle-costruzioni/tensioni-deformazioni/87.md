Which of the following stress states has zero von Mises equivalent stress?

- $\sigma_x=\sigma_y=50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_z=0$, with zero shear stresses
- $\sigma_x=50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=-50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with all other components zero
- $\tau_{xy}=50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with all other components zero
- $\sigma_x=\sigma_y=\sigma_z=-50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with zero shear stresses

## Explanation

In terms of the principal stresses, $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2+(\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2+(\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}{2}}$. This vanishes only when all three principal stresses are equal.

In the hydrostatic state all three principal stresses are $-50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, so $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}=0$.

In the equibiaxial state the principal stresses are $50$, $50$, and $0$, giving $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}=50\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

Pure shear and the state with $\pm50\,\mathrm{MPa}$ both give $50\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
