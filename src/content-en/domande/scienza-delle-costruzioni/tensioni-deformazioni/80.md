At a point, $\varepsilon_x=300\cdot10^{-6}$, $\varepsilon_y=-100\cdot10^{-6}$, and $\gamma_{xy}=-300\cdot10^{-6}$. Find the maximum engineering shear strain between two orthogonal in-plane directions.

- $400\cdot10^{-6}$
- $250\cdot10^{-6}$
- $721\cdot10^{-6}$
- $500\cdot10^{-6}$

## Explanation

On Mohr's strain circle the ordinate is $\dfrac{\gamma}{2}$ and the radius is $R=\sqrt{\Big(\dfrac{\varepsilon_x-\varepsilon_y}{2}\Big)^2+\Big(\dfrac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}\Big)^2}$.

The given data yield $R=\sqrt{200^2+150^2}\cdot10^{-6}=250\cdot10^{-6}$.

The maximum of $\dfrac{\gamma}{2}$ is $R$, so $\gamma_{\max}=2R=500\cdot10^{-6}$.

This equals the difference of the two principal strains, $\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2$.
