A sheet of area $200\,\mathrm{mm}\times100\,\mathrm{mm}$ undergoes uniform strains $\varepsilon_x=300\cdot10^{-6}$, $\varepsilon_y=-100\cdot10^{-6}$, and $\gamma_{xy}=0$. Find the first-order change in its area.

- $6\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $8\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{mm^2}$
- $4\,\mathrm{mm^2}$

## Explanation

The side lengths become $200(1+\varepsilon_x)$ and $100(1+\varepsilon_y)$.

Neglecting the product $\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y$, the relative area change is $\dfrac{\Delta A}{A}=\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y=200\cdot10^{-6}$.

Since $A=20\,000\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, this gives $\Delta A=4\,\mathrm{mm^2}$.

Engineering shear strain makes no first-order contribution and in this case is zero anyway.
