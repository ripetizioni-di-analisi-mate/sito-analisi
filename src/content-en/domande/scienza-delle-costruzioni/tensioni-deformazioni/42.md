The stress at a point is hydrostatic with $\boldsymbol\sigma=-40\,\mathbf I\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Find the normal stress and the shear stress magnitude on the face with normal $\mathbf n=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(1,1,1)$.

- $-40\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $0\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-\dfrac{40}{\sqrt3}\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $0\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $0\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $40\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

Cauchy's relation gives the traction $\boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf n=-40\,\mathbf n$.

The traction is therefore parallel to the normal. Its normal component is $\mathbf n\cdot(-40\,\mathbf n)=-40\,\mathrm{MPa}$ because $\mathbf n$ is a unit vector.

The traction has no component perpendicular to $\mathbf n$, so the shear stress vanishes.

This holds for every face. Every direction is therefore principal in a hydrostatic stress state.
