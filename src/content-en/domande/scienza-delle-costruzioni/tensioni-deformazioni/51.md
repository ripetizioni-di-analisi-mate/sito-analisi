A long solid cylinder of isotropic linear elastic material with $E=200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\nu=0.25$ has ends restrained by rigid walls and a uniform pressure of $60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ on its lateral surface. In the interior $\sigma_x=\sigma_y=-60\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\tau_{xy}=0$, and the cylinder is in plane strain with $\varepsilon_z=0$. Find $\varepsilon_x$.

- $-225\cdot10^{-6}$
- $-187.5\cdot10^{-6}$
- $-281.25\cdot10^{-6}$
- $-375\cdot10^{-6}$

## Explanation

Since $\varepsilon_z=0$, the axial stress is $\sigma_z=\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_y)=0.25\cdot(-120)=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

Hooke's law along $x$ therefore gives $\varepsilon_x=\dfrac{\sigma_x-\nu(\sigma_y+\sigma_z)}{E}=\dfrac{-60+0.25\cdot90}{200\,000}$.

Therefore $\varepsilon_x=\dfrac{-37.5}{200\,000}=-187.5\cdot10^{-6}$.

The plane stress expression would impose $\sigma_z=0$ and give $-225\cdot10^{-6}$. The difference comes from compression $\sigma_z$, which reduces the in-plane contraction through the Poisson effect.
