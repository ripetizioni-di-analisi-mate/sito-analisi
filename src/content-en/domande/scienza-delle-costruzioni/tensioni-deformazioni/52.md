A bar of length $L=2\,\mathrm m$ has axial strain $\varepsilon_x(x)=400\cdot10^{-6}\,\dfrac{x}{L}$, with $x$ measured from the left end. Find the elongation.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 4,0 4,0.3 0,0.3" quote="0 0.3,4 0.3,2 m,-0.5" frecce="0,-0.5,1.2,-0.5,x" riferimento=0 titolo="Bar with a varying axial strain"}

- $0.8\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.04\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.4\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Axial strain is the derivative of axial displacement, so the elongation is $\Delta L=\displaystyle\int_0^L\varepsilon_x\,dx$.

Since $\varepsilon_x$ increases linearly from $0$ to $400\cdot10^{-6}$, the integral is $\dfrac{400\cdot10^{-6}\cdot L}{2}$.

With $L=2000\,\mathrm{mm}$ this gives $\Delta L=0.4\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Multiplying the maximum strain by the length gives $0.8\,\mathrm{mm}$, a result valid only for uniform strain.
