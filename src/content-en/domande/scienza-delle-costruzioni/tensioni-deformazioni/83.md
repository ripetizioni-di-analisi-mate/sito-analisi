An isotropic linear elastic sheet in plane stress has $\nu=0.25$. The measured strains are $\varepsilon_x=400\cdot10^{-6}$ and $\varepsilon_y=200\cdot10^{-6}$. Find the through-thickness strain $\varepsilon_z$.

- $-200\cdot10^{-6}$
- $-150\cdot10^{-6}$
- $200\cdot10^{-6}$
- $-600\cdot10^{-6}$

## Explanation

In plane stress $\varepsilon_z=-\dfrac{\nu(\sigma_x+\sigma_y)}{E}$.

Adding the two in-plane relations gives $\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y=\dfrac{(1-\nu)(\sigma_x+\sigma_y)}{E}$.

Thus $\varepsilon_z=-\dfrac{\nu}{1-\nu}(\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y)=-\dfrac{0.25}{0.75}\cdot600\cdot10^{-6}=-200\cdot10^{-6}$.

The product $-\nu(\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y)$ would give $-150\cdot10^{-6}$ by incorrectly equating strains with stresses divided by $E$.
