A thin-walled spherical vessel of mean radius $400\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $6\,\mathrm{mm}$ contains gas at pressure $1.5\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The stress is $\dfrac{pr}{2t}$ in every tangential direction, and radial stress is negligible. Find the maximum shear stress magnitude in the wall over all planes.

- $50\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $25\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $100\,\mathrm{MPa}$
- $0\,\mathrm{MPa}$

## Explanation

The stress in the wall plane is $\dfrac{1.5\cdot400}{2\cdot6}=50\,\mathrm{MPa}$ in every tangential direction.

The Mohr circle for the tangent plane reduces to a point, so the shear stress on these faces vanishes.

In three dimensions the principal stresses are $50$, $50$, and $0\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

The maximum shear stress is therefore $\dfrac{50-0}{2}=25\,\mathrm{MPa}$ on faces inclined at $45^\circ$ to the wall.
