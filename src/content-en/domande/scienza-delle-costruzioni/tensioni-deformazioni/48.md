Displacements are in $10^{-4}\,\mathrm m$ and coordinates are in meters. Which plane displacement field represents infinitesimal rigid-body motion, with all strain components zero?

- $u_x=2+3y$, $u_y=3x$
- $u_x=2-3y$, $u_y=1+3x$
- $u_x=-3y$, $u_y=-3x$
- $u_x=3x$, $u_y=3y$

## Explanation

The strains are $\varepsilon_x=\dfrac{\partial u_x}{\partial x}$, $\varepsilon_y=\dfrac{\partial u_y}{\partial y}$, and $\gamma_{xy}=\dfrac{\partial u_x}{\partial y}+\dfrac{\partial u_y}{\partial x}$.

For $u_x=2-3y$ and $u_y=1+3x$, the result is $\varepsilon_x=\varepsilon_y=0$ and $\gamma_{xy}=-3+3=0$. The displacement therefore combines a translation and an infinitesimal rotation.

For $u_x=-3y$, $u_y=-3x$ and for $u_x=2+3y$, $u_y=3x$, the cross derivatives have the same sign, giving $\gamma_{xy}=\mp6\cdot10^{-4}$.

With $u_x=3x$ and $u_y=3y$ the normal strains are $3\cdot10^{-4}$, so the displacement represents a uniform dilation.
