At a point in an isotropic linear elastic material with $E=200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\nu=0.25$, the normal stresses are $\sigma_x=100\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=-50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\sigma_z=-150\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Find $\varepsilon_x$.

- $250\cdot10^{-6}$
- $500\cdot10^{-6}$
- $750\cdot10^{-6}$
- $562.5\cdot10^{-6}$

## Explanation

Three-dimensional Hooke's law gives $\varepsilon_x=\dfrac{\sigma_x-\nu(\sigma_y+\sigma_z)}{E}$.

The sum of transverse stresses is $-50-150=-200\,\mathrm{MPa}$.

Thus $\varepsilon_x=\dfrac{100+0.25\cdot200}{200\,000}=\dfrac{150}{200\,000}=750\cdot10^{-6}$.

Through the Poisson effect both transverse compressions elongate the element along $x$. Neglecting $\sigma_z$ would give $562.5\cdot10^{-6}$.
