A square element undergoes simple shear $u_x=ay$, $u_y=0$, with $a=4\cdot10^{-4}$. Which statement holds to first order?

- The diagonal along $(1,1)$ elongates with strain $4\cdot10^{-4}$, and the area is unchanged
- $\varepsilon_x=4\cdot10^{-4}$ and the area increases
- The diagonal along $(1,1)$ elongates with strain $2\cdot10^{-4}$, and the area is unchanged
- The diagonal along $(1,1)$ shortens with strain $-2\cdot10^{-4}$, and the area is unchanged

## Explanation

The displacement field gives $\varepsilon_x=\varepsilon_y=0$ and $\gamma_{xy}=a=4\cdot10^{-4}$.

Since $\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y=0$, the area is unchanged to first order.

Along the direction at $45^\circ$ the strain is $\dfrac{\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y+\gamma_{xy}}{2}=2\cdot10^{-4}$, so diagonal $(1,1)$ elongates.

Along the direction at $135^\circ$ the strain is $-2\cdot10^{-4}$, so the other diagonal shortens.
