A satellite in low orbit slowly loses mechanical energy because of friction with the thin atmosphere, while remaining at every instant, to a good approximation, on a circular orbit. What happens to the radius of the orbit and to the magnitude of the velocity?

- The radius decreases and the speed decreases
- The radius stays the same and the speed decreases
- The radius increases and the speed decreases
- The radius decreases and the speed increases

## Explanation

On a circular orbit the mechanical energy is $E = -\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$, which increases with $r$. Friction makes $E$ decrease, so the radius of the orbit decreases.

The orbital speed is $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$, therefore as $r$ decreases the speed increases. In terms of energy, $K = -E$ grows when $E$ decreases.

The increase in kinetic energy is possible because, as the satellite descends, the gravitational force does positive work equal to twice the energy dissipated by friction.
