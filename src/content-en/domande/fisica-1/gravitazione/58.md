Two point masses $m$ and $4m$ are fixed at distance $d$. On the segment joining them there is a single point $P$ where the total gravitational field is zero. What can be said about the gravitational potential at $P$, compared with its value at the other interior points of the segment?

- At $P$ the potential has its maximum value
- At $P$ the potential has its minimum value
- At $P$ the potential is zero
- The potential has the same value at all points of the segment

## Explanation

Take an $x$ axis along the segment. The component of the field along the axis is $g_x = -\dfrac{dV}{dx}$, so at the points where the field is zero the derivative of the potential is zero.

Approaching either mass the potential tends to $-\infty$, because it contains the term $-\dfrac{Gm}{r}$ with $r \to 0$. The potential is continuous at the interior points and tends to $-\infty$ at the endpoints, therefore it has a maximum at an interior point, where the derivative is zero.

The only interior point with zero derivative is $P$, so at $P$ the potential has its maximum value. The direction of the field confirms this: it points from $P$ toward the nearer mass, that is, toward decreasing potential.
