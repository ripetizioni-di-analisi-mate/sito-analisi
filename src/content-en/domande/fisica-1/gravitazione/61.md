Around the same star, a planet $A$ moves on a circular orbit of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{AU}$ and a planet $B$ moves on an elliptical orbit with perihelion at $1.0\,\mathrm{AU}$ and aphelion at $3.0\,\mathrm{AU}$ from the star. Which statement about the periods of revolution is true?

- The period of $B$ is longer, because at aphelion $B$ is farther from the star than $A$
- The period of $B$ is shorter, because at perihelion $B$ is closer to the star than $A$
- The two periods are equal
- The ratio of the periods depends on the masses of the two planets

## Explanation

The perihelion and the aphelion are the ends of the major axis, so the semi-major axis of the orbit of $B$ is $a = \dfrac{1.0 + 3.0}{2}\,\mathrm{AU} = 2.0\,\mathrm{AU}$.

By **Kepler's third law** the ratio $\dfrac{T^2}{a^3}$ is the same for all bodies orbiting the same star, and for a circular orbit the semi-major axis is the radius.

The two planets have the same semi-major axis, therefore they have the same period. The period does not depend on the mass of the planets, because the constant $\dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}$ contains only the mass of the star.
