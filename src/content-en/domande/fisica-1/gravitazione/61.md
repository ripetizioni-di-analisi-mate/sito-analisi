Two satellites orbit the same planet in circular orbits. The first has period $4\,\mathrm{h}$ and orbital radius $R_1$. Given the first orbital radius $R_{1} = 10\,\mathrm{Mm}$ and the second orbital radius $R_{2} = 40\,\mathrm{Mm}$, what is the second satellite's period $T$?

- $16\,\mathrm{h}$
- $64\,\mathrm{h}$
- $32\,\mathrm{h}$
- $48\,\mathrm{h}$

## Explanation

By Kepler's third law, the square of the period is proportional to the cube of orbital radius. In the stated units, the relation is $T = \dfrac{4 \cdot R_{2}^{\dfrac{3}{2}}}{R_{1}^{\dfrac{3}{2}}}$. Substituting the given values yields $32\,\mathrm{h}$.
