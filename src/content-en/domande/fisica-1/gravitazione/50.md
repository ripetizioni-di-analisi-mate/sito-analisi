Two homogeneous lead spheres, of density $\rho = 1.13 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ each, are touching each other. What is the magnitude of the gravitational force between the two spheres? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $3.7 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9.3 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $7.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The mass of each sphere is
$$
m = \rho\,\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = 1.13 \cdot 10^4 \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi \cdot 0.10^3\,\mathrm{kg} = 47\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The spheres are homogeneous, so by the shell theorem each one attracts as if its mass were concentrated at its center. Since the spheres touch, the distance between the centers is $2R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$.

Therefore $F = G\,\dfrac{m^2}{(2R)^2} = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot \dfrac{47.3^2}{0.20^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 3.7 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$. Using the radius $R$ as the distance would give a value four times larger.
