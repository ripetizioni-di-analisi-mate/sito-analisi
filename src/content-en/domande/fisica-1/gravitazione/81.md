A solid uniform sphere of radius $R$ has mass $M$. A spherical cavity of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$, tangent to the surface of the sphere, is hollowed out inside it. A point mass $m$ lies on the line through the centers of the sphere and of the cavity, on the side of the cavity, at distance $2R$ from the center of the sphere. What is the magnitude of the gravitational force that the sphere with the cavity exerts on the point mass?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29;1+cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno,O;1,0,vuoto,C;4,0,pieno,m" segmenti="0,0,4,0" xmin=-2.4 xmax=4.6 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.4 titolo="Sphere of center $O$ and radius $R$ with the cavity of center $C$ and radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$; the point mass $m$ is at distance $2R$ from $O$"}

- $\dfrac{GMm}{4R^2}$
- $\dfrac{7GMm}{32R^2}$
- $\dfrac{GMm}{18R^2}$
- $\dfrac{7GMm}{36R^2}$

## Explanation

The solid sphere consists of the sphere with the cavity and of the small sphere that fills the cavity. By the **superposition principle** the force of the solid sphere is the sum of the forces of these two parts, so the required force is the difference between the force of the solid sphere and that of the small sphere.

The small sphere has $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of the volume of the sphere, therefore it has mass $\dfrac{M}{8}$; its center is at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center $O$, so it is at distance $\dfrac{3R}{2}$ from the point mass. The point mass is outside both spheres, and each of them attracts like a point mass placed at its center.

The two forces have the same direction and the same sense, therefore
$$
F = \frac{GMm}{(2R)^2} - \frac{G\,\dfrac{M}{8}\,m}{\left(\dfrac{3R}{2}\right)^2} = \frac{GMm}{R^2} \left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{18} \right) = \frac{7GMm}{36R^2} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{7GMm}{32R^2}$ is obtained by placing the remaining mass, $\dfrac{7M}{8}$, at the center $O$, but the sphere with the cavity does not have spherical symmetry.
