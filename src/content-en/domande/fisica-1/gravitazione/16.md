A body of mass $m = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at the origin of a Cartesian plane. A mass $m_1 = 3{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at the point $(0{,}30\,\mathrm{m};\ 0)$ and a mass $m_2 = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at the point $(0;\ 0{,}40\,\mathrm{m})$. What is the magnitude of the total gravitational force on the body? ($G = 6{,}67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $7{,}8 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1{,}1 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5{,}6 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4{,}4 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The force due to $m_1$ is directed along the $x$ axis and the force due to $m_2$ along the $y$ axis, so the two forces are perpendicular. Their magnitudes are
$$
F_1 = G\,\frac{2{,}0 \cdot 3{,}0}{0{,}30^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 4{,}45 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N} , \qquad F_2 = G\,\frac{2{,}0 \cdot 4{,}0}{0{,}40^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 3{,}34 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
By the **superposition principle** the total force is the vector sum, and since the forces are perpendicular its magnitude is found with the Pythagorean theorem. We have $F_2 = \dfrac{3}{4} F_1$, therefore $F = \sqrt{F_1^2 + F_2^2} = \dfrac{5}{4} F_1 = 5{,}6 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$. The sum of the magnitudes, $7{,}8 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$, would give the magnitude of the resultant only for collinear forces pointing in the same direction.
