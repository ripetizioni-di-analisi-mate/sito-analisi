A probe launches radially from an isolated spherical body's surface. Neglect atmosphere and rotation. The gravitational parameter is $b=GM$. The initial data are the radius $R = 8\,\mathrm{m}$ and the gravitational parameter $b = 400\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$. Change the radius to $32\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $v$ to its initial value?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$

## Explanation

At the escape threshold, total energy is zero. At the surface, energy per unit mass is $v^2/2-GM/R$. The relation $v = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{b}}{\sqrt{R}}$ gives $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before the change and $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
