With $g_0 = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $R_T = 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the gravitational potential of the Earth at a point on its surface, with the potential zero at infinity?

- $6.25 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $-6.25 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $-3.12 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $-1.25 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$

## Explanation

Outside the Earth and on its surface, the Earth behaves like a point mass placed at its center, so on the surface the potential is $V = -\dfrac{G M_T}{R_T}$.

Since $G M_T = g_0 R_T^2$, one has $V = -g_0 R_T$, therefore
$$
V = -\,9.81 \cdot 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg} = -6.25 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J/kg} .
$$
The potential is negative because it is zero at infinity and increases moving away from the Earth. The value $-1.25 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ is twice as large, that is, $-2 g_0 R_T$, which is the opposite of the square of the escape speed.
