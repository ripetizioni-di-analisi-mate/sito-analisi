The radius of the Earth is $R_T = 6400\,\mathrm{km}$. Using the approximation valid for altitudes small compared with the radius, by what percentage does the gravitational acceleration decrease going up from the surface to the altitude $h = 32\,\mathrm{km}$?

- $0.50\,\%$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\%$
- $99\,\%$
- $1.0\,\%$

## Explanation

By the **variation of g with altitude**, if $h$ is small compared with $R_T$, then $g \approx g_0 \left( 1 - \dfrac{2h}{R_T} \right)$. The relative decrease is therefore $\dfrac{2h}{R_T} = \dfrac{2 \cdot 32}{6400} = 0.010$, that is, $1.0\,\%$. The ratio $\dfrac{h}{R_T}$ is a pure number only if altitude and radius are expressed in the same unit; with $h$ in kilometers and $R_T$ in meters one would obtain a value a thousand times smaller. The value $99\,\%$ is the ratio $\dfrac{g}{g_0}$, that is, the part of $g_0$ that remains.
