Two identical stars, of mass $M = 2.0 \cdot 10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $7.0 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{m}$ each, are initially at rest with their centers at distance $1.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m}$ and move only under their mutual attraction. With what speed does each star move at the instant the two surfaces touch? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $4.4 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.2 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.2 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.1 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The system is isolated and initially at rest, so the total momentum remains zero and at every instant the two stars have velocities equal in magnitude and opposite.

When the surfaces touch, the distance between the centers is $2 \cdot 7.0 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{m} = 1.4 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{m}$. Conservation of energy for the system gives
$$
2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} M v^2 = G M^2 \left( \frac{1}{1.4 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{m}} - \frac{1}{1.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m}} \right) ,
$$
therefore $v = \sqrt{G M \left( \dfrac{1}{1.4 \cdot 10^9} - \dfrac{1}{1.0 \cdot 10^{12}} \right)}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The value $4.4 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by assigning all the kinetic energy to a single star.
