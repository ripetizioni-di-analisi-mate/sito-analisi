The Moon, which has no atmosphere, has radius $R_L = 1.74 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$, and at its surface the gravitational acceleration is $g_L = 1.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. With what speed does a satellite move on a circular orbit grazing the lunar surface?

- $2.37\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $4.13\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $1.68\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $1.19\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

On the grazing orbit the only force is the weight $m g_L$, directed toward the center of the Moon, and it provides the centripetal acceleration $\dfrac{v^2}{R_L}$. By Newton's second law
$$
m g_L = m\,\frac{v^2}{R_L} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{g_L R_L} .
$$
With the data $v = \sqrt{1.62 \cdot 1.74 \cdot 10^6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.68 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.68\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The value $2.37\,\mathrm{km/s}$ is the escape speed, $\sqrt{2}$ times larger; the value $4.13\,\mathrm{km/s}$ is obtained with the gravitational acceleration of the Earth.
