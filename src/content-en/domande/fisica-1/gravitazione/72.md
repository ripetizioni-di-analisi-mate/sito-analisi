A uniform sphere has radius $40\,\mathrm{m}$ and surface gravity $g_0$. Consider an interior point. Given the distance from the center $r = 10\,\mathrm{m}$ and the interior gravitational acceleration $g = \dfrac{1}{100000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the surface gravity $g_{0}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{6250}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3125}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{12500}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{25000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Only the mass inside radius $r$ contributes. It scales as $r^3$, while the distance factor is $1/r^2$. In the stated units, the relation is $g = \dfrac{g_{0} \cdot r}{40}$. Solving for the unknown gives $g_{0} = \dfrac{40 \cdot g}{r}$. Substituting the given values yields $\dfrac{1}{25000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
