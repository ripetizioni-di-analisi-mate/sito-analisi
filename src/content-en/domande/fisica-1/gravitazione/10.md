- $6{,}00 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4{,}26 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1{,}24 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2{,}04 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The only force is the Moon's gravity, which is conservative, so the mechanical energy is conserved between the launch and the highest point, where the speed is zero. Dividing by the mass of the body gives
$$
\frac{1}{2} v_0^2 - \frac{G M_L}{R_L} = -\,\frac{G M_L}{r_{\max}} , \quad\text{cioè}\quad \frac{1}{r_{\max}} = \frac{1}{R_L} - \frac{v_0^2}{2 G M_L} .
$$
With $G M_L = 4{,}90 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ we obtain $\dfrac{1}{r_{\max}} = (5{,}747 - 4{,}080) \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, so $r_{\max} = 6{,}00 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$. The altitude is the distance from the surface, $h = r_{\max} - R_L = 4{,}26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$. The formula $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2 g_L}$ of uniformly accelerated motion gives $1{,}24 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$ and greatly underestimates the altitude, because $g$ decreases as the body rises.
