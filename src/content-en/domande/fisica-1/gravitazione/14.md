Two homogeneous lead spheres, of masses $m_1 = 10\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2 = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, have their centers at a distance $d = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the magnitude of the gravitational force between the two spheres? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $3.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $7.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.3 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.3 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the **field of a homogeneous sphere**, each sphere attracts external points like a point mass placed at its center, so the law of gravitation uses the distance between the centers. In meters the distance is $d = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, and its square is $1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Therefore
$$
F = G\,\frac{m_1 m_2}{d^2} = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot \frac{10 \cdot 0.50}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{N} = 3.3 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
With the distance left in centimeters one would obtain $3.3 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$, ten thousand times less.
