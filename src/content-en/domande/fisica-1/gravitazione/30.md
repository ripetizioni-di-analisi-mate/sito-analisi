A spherical asteroid has mass $1{,}5 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and diameter $10\,\mathrm{km}$. What is the gravitational acceleration at its surface? ($G = 6{,}67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $1{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4{,}0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The radius is half the diameter, $R = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{km} = 5{,}0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m}$. The gravitational acceleration at the surface is $g_0 = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$, with $GM = 6{,}67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 1{,}5 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2} = 1{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ and $R^2 = 2{,}5 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Hence $g_0 = 4{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, about $2500$ times less than on the Earth. Using the diameter instead of the radius would give a value four times smaller.
