A body of mass $m = 100\,\mathrm{kg}$ is brought from the Earth's surface to the altitude $h = R_T$. What is the change in its gravitational potential energy? Use $g_0 = 9{,}81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $R_T = 6{,}37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$.

- $6{,}25 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1{,}56 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $9{,}37 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3{,}12 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The body goes from the distance $R_T$ to the distance $2R_T$ from the center, so the formula for **gravitational potential energy** gives
$$
\Delta U = -\,\frac{G M_T m}{2R_T} + \frac{G M_T m}{R_T} = \frac{G M_T m}{2R_T} .
$$
Since $G M_T = g_0 R_T^2$, we have $\Delta U = \dfrac{m g_0 R_T}{2} = \dfrac{100 \cdot 9{,}81 \cdot 6{,}37 \cdot 10^6}{2}\,\mathrm{J} = 3{,}12 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$. The approximation $m g_0 h$ would give twice as much, $6{,}25 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$, because at this altitude $h$ equals the radius of the Earth and the approximation for small altitudes is not valid.
