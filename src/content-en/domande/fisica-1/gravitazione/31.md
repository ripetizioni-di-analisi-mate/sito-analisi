An astronaut has a mass of $60\,\mathrm{kg}$. At the surface of the Moon the gravitational acceleration is $g_L = 1.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What are her mass and her weight on the Moon?

- Mass $9.9\,\mathrm{kg}$, weight $97\,\mathrm{N}$
- Mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$, weight $589\,\mathrm{N}$
- Mass $9.9\,\mathrm{kg}$, weight $16\,\mathrm{N}$
- Mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$, weight $97\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Mass measures the inertia of the body and does not depend on the location, so on the Moon it is still $60\,\mathrm{kg}$.

Weight is the gravitational force that the Moon exerts on the body at its surface, therefore it equals $m g_L = 60 \cdot 1.62\,\mathrm{N} = 97\,\mathrm{N}$.

On the Earth the same astronaut weighs $60 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 589\,\mathrm{N}$, about six times as much.
