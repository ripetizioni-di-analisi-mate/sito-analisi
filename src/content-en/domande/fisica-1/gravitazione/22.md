Three equal point masses, $m = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ each, are at the vertices of an equilateral triangle with side $\ell = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the gravitational potential energy of the system, zero when the masses are at infinite distance from one another? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $-1.2 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-7.2 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3.6 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.6 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The potential energy of a system of masses is the sum of the contributions of all pairs, each counted once. With three masses there are three pairs, all at distance $\ell$, so $U = -\dfrac{3Gm^2}{\ell}$. With the data
$$
U = -\,\frac{3 \cdot 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 3.0^2}{0.50}\,\mathrm{J} = -3.6 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The energy is negative, and separating the three masses to infinite distance requires supplying the work $3.6 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{J}$.
