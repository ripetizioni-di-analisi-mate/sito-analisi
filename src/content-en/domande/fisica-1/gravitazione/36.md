Two satellites, of mass $m$ and $3m$, move around the Earth in the same circular orbit. How do their periods compare?

- They are equal
- The period of the satellite of mass $3m$ is three times that of the other
- The period of the satellite of mass $3m$ is one third of that of the other
- The period of the satellite of mass $3m$ is $\sqrt{3}$ times that of the other

## Explanation

In the equation of motion $\dfrac{G M_T m}{r^2} = m\,\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ the mass of the satellite appears on both sides and cancels. Hence $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{G M_T}{r}}$ and $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{G M_T}}$ depend only on the mass of the Earth and on the radius of the orbit, and the two periods are **equal**. The satellite of mass $3m$ is subject to a force three times larger, and since its mass is also three times larger its acceleration is the same.
