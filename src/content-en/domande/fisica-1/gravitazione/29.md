A spherical planet has the same mean density as the Earth and twice its radius. What is the gravitational acceleration at its surface, if on the Earth it is $9{,}81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$?

- $78{,}5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $19{,}6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2{,}45\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $39{,}2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

For the same density the mass is proportional to the volume, so with twice the radius the mass is $2^3 = 8$ times that of the Earth. The acceleration at the surface is $g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$, and the doubled radius divides the result by $2^2 = 4$. Therefore $g = \dfrac{8}{4}\,g_0 = 2 g_0 = 19{,}6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. More generally, since $M = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \rho$, we have $g = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi G \rho R$, which for the same density is proportional to the radius.
