A probe goes from the orbit of the Earth, circular with radius $1.00\,\mathrm{AU}$, to the orbit of Mars, circular with radius $1.52\,\mathrm{AU}$, traveling half an ellipse with the Sun at a focus, the perihelion on the Earth's orbit and the aphelion on the orbit of Mars. The Earth's period is $365$ days. How long does the trip take, if only the Sun's attraction acts during the transfer?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29;1.52*cos(t),1.52*sin(t),0,6.29;-0.26+1.26*cos(t),1.2329*sin(t),0,3.1416" punti="0,0,pieno,Sun;1,0,pieno,Earth;-1.52,0,pieno,Mars" xmin=-1.9 xmax=1.9 ymin=-1.7 ymax=1.7 titolo="Circular orbits of the Earth and Mars and half of the transfer ellipse"}

- $258$ days
- $517$ days
- $342$ days
- $213$ days

## Explanation

Perihelion and aphelion are the ends of the major axis, so the semi-major axis of the ellipse is $a = \dfrac{1.00 + 1.52}{2}\,\mathrm{AU} = 1.26\,\mathrm{AU}$.

By Kepler's third law, with $a$ in astronomical units and $T$ in Earth years, $T^2 = a^3$, so the period of the ellipse is $T = 1.26^{\frac{3}{2}}$ years $= 1.41$ years.

By Kepler's second law half of the ellipse, which encloses half of the area, is traveled in half of the period. Therefore the trip takes $0.707$ years, that is, $258$ days. The value $342$ days is obtained by using the radius of the orbit of Mars as the semi-major axis.
