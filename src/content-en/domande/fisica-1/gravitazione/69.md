On the surface of a spherical planet of radius $4.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$ a simple pendulum $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ long performs small oscillations with period $3.00\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the mass of the planet? Rotation is neglected ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$).

- $1.1 \cdot 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^{23}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.7 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^{17}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

For small oscillations the period of the simple pendulum is $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$, so the gravitational acceleration at the surface is
$$
g = \frac{4\pi^2 \ell}{T^2} = \frac{4\pi^2 \cdot 1.00}{3.00^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 4.39\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
At the surface of a spherical planet $g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$, therefore
$$
M = \frac{g R^2}{G} = \frac{4.39 \cdot (4.0 \cdot 10^6)^2}{6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}}\,\mathrm{kg} = 1.1 \cdot 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $2.6 \cdot 10^{17}\,\mathrm{kg}$ is obtained by multiplying by $R$ instead of $R^2$.
