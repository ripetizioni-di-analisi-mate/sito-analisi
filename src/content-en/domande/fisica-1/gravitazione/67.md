An astronaut is on a spherical, homogeneous asteroid of density $\rho = 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, without atmosphere. By jumping, he leaves the ground with speed $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the maximum radius of the asteroid from which the astronaut can escape with a jump? Rotation is neglected ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$).

- $2.7 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The mass of the asteroid is $M = \rho\,\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$, so the escape speed is
$$
v_f = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = R \sqrt{\frac{8\pi G \rho}{3}} ,
$$
which grows in proportion to the radius.

The astronaut escapes if $v_f \leq 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, that is, if
$$
R \leq \frac{2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{\sqrt{\dfrac{8\pi \cdot 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3}{3}}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 1.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $2.7 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds to the condition for a grazing circular orbit, which requires a speed $\sqrt{2}$ times smaller than the escape speed.
