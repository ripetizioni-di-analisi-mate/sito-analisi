The escape speed from the surface of the Moon is $2.37\,\mathrm{km/s}$. A meteorite, initially at rest very far from the Moon, falls onto it attracted only by the Moon, which has no atmosphere. With what speed does it hit the lunar surface?

- $1.68\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $2.37\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $3.35\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $4.74\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

At the start the meteorite is at rest and at a distance that can be considered infinite, so its mechanical energy is zero. The only force is the gravity of the Moon, which is conservative, therefore the mechanical energy stays zero until the impact:
$$
\frac{1}{2} m v^2 - \frac{G M_L m}{R_L} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{\frac{2 G M_L}{R_L}} .
$$
This expression is the escape speed from the lunar surface, so the meteorite arrives at $2.37\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The value $1.68\,\mathrm{km/s}$ is the speed of the grazing circular orbit, which is $\sqrt{2}$ times smaller.
