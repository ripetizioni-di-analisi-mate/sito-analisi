An object is at altitude $h$ above a spherical body of radius $R$. Surface gravity is $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the body's radius $R = 12\,\mathrm{km}$ and the altitude $h = 8\,\mathrm{km}$. Change the body's radius to $16\,\mathrm{km}$ and the altitude to $4\,\mathrm{km}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $g$ minus its initial value?

- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $- \dfrac{875}{72}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $- \dfrac{14}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{14}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Distance from the center increases from $R$ to $R+h$. Gravitational acceleration is inversely proportional to the square of that distance. The relation $g = \dfrac{10 \cdot R^{2}}{\left(R + h\right)^{2}}$ gives $\dfrac{18}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ before the change and $\dfrac{32}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $\dfrac{14}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
