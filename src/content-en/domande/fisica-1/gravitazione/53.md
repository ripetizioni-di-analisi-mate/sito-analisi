What is the gravitational potential energy of the Earth-Moon system, zero when the two bodies are infinitely far apart? Data: $M_T = 5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $M_L = 7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{kg}$, distance between the centers $d = 3.84 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{m}$, $G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$.

- $-3.81 \cdot 10^{28}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $7.62 \cdot 10^{28}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-7.62 \cdot 10^{28}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.99 \cdot 10^{20}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The Earth and the Moon attract each other as point masses placed at their centers, because they are spherically symmetric bodies. The system consists of a single pair, so
$$
U = -\,\frac{G M_T M_L}{d} = -\,\frac{6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 5.97 \cdot 10^{24} \cdot 7.35 \cdot 10^{22}}{3.84 \cdot 10^8}\,\mathrm{J} = -7.62 \cdot 10^{28}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The energy is negative, and $7.62 \cdot 10^{28}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work needed to separate the two bodies while keeping them at rest. The value $-1.99 \cdot 10^{20}$ is obtained by dividing by $d^2$ and is, apart from the sign, the magnitude of the force in newtons.
