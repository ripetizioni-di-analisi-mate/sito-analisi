A planet moves on an elliptical orbit around the Sun, which is considered fixed. About which point does the angular momentum of the planet remain constant during the motion?

- About the center of the ellipse
- About the focus of the ellipse not occupied by the Sun
- About any point, because the orbit is closed
- About the center of the Sun, which occupies a focus of the ellipse

## Explanation

About a fixed point $O$ we have $\dfrac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$, where $\vec{r}$ is the vector from $O$ to the planet. The angular momentum is constant if $\vec{r} \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$ at every instant, that is, if the force is always parallel to $\vec{r}$.

The gravitational force always points toward the center of the Sun, so this condition is satisfied by taking $O$ at the center of the Sun.

About the center of the ellipse or the other focus the line of the force does not pass through the reference point, except at perihelion and aphelion, therefore the angular momentum about those points changes along the orbit.
