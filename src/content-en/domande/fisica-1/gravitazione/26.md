A thin homogeneous spherical shell has mass $M$ and radius $R$. What are the magnitude of the gravitational field $g$ and the potential $V$, zero at infinite distance, at an internal point at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center?

- $g = 0$ and $V = 0$
- $g = \dfrac{4GM}{R^2}$ and $V = -\dfrac{2GM}{R}$
- $g = 0$ and $V = -\dfrac{GM}{R}$
- $g = 0$ and $V = -\dfrac{2GM}{R}$

## Explanation

By the **shell theorem**, inside the shell the potential is constant and equals $-\dfrac{GM}{R}$, the same value it has on the surface. The field is $g_r = -\dfrac{dV}{dr}$, and the derivative of a constant is zero, so inside $g = 0$. The values $\dfrac{4GM}{R^2}$ and $-\dfrac{2GM}{R}$ are obtained by applying to an internal point the formulas valid outside, as if the mass were concentrated at the center.
