A satellite moves on an elliptical orbit around the Earth. At perigee it is $7000\,\mathrm{km}$ from the center of the Earth and has speed $9.92\,\mathrm{km/s}$. At another point $P$ of the orbit it is $10\,000\,\mathrm{km}$ from the center and its velocity makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the position vector. What is the speed of the satellite at $P$?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,O;2,0,pieno,P" segmenti="0,0,2,0" vettori="2,0,2.5,0.866,v" etichette="1,-0.2,r;2.2,0.15,60°" xmin=-0.5 xmax=3 ymin=-0.6 ymax=1.2 titolo="At $P$ the velocity $\vec{v}$ makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the position vector drawn from the center $O$ of the Earth"}

- $6.94\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $8.02\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $13.9\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $11.5\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

The gravitational force points toward the center of the Earth, so the angular momentum about the center is conserved. Its magnitude is $m r v \sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the position vector and the velocity.

At perigee the velocity is perpendicular to the position vector, therefore
$$
r_p v_p = r v \sin 60^\circ \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v = \frac{r_p v_p}{r \sin 60^\circ} = \frac{7000 \cdot 9.92}{10\,000 \cdot 0.866}\,\mathrm{km/s} = 8.02\,\mathrm{km/s} .
$$
The value $6.94\,\mathrm{km/s}$ is obtained by neglecting $\sin 60^\circ$, that is, by treating $P$ as a point where the velocity is perpendicular to the position vector.
