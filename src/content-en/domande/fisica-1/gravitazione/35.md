The Earth moves around the Sun on an orbit taken as circular, of radius $1.50 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{m}$, with period $3.156 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the orbital speed of the Earth?

- $2.99 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.49 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.75 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.97 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

In a circular orbit the motion is uniform, so the speed is the circumference divided by the period:
$$
v = \frac{2\pi r}{T} = \frac{2\pi \cdot 1.50 \cdot 10^{11}}{3.156 \cdot 10^7}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.99 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $4.75 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by dividing the radius by the period, that is, by using the radius instead of the circumference.
