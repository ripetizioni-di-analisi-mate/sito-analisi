What is the mechanical energy of a satellite of mass $500\,\mathrm{kg}$ moving around the Earth on a circular orbit with period $2.00\,\mathrm{h}$? Use $G M_T = 3.98 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$, with the potential energy zero at infinity.

- $-2.47 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.24 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3.63 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.24 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The period in seconds is $T = 7200\,\mathrm{s}$. From the formula for the period on a circular orbit, $T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2 r^3}{G M_T}$, one obtains the radius of the orbit:
$$
r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{G M_T T^2}{4\pi^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3.98 \cdot 10^{14} \cdot 7200^2}{4\pi^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = 8.05 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
By the **energy of a satellite on a circular orbit**, $E = -\dfrac{G M_T m}{2r}$, therefore
$$
E = -\,\frac{3.98 \cdot 10^{14} \cdot 500}{2 \cdot 8.05 \cdot 10^6}\,\mathrm{J} = -1.24 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $-2.47 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the potential energy, which is twice the mechanical energy, while $1.24 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$ is the kinetic energy.
