A satellite of mass $m = 400\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves in a circular orbit of radius $r = 1.00 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m}$ around the Earth, with $G M_T = 3.98 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$. How much work must be supplied to bring it to a circular orbit of radius $2r$?

- $7.96 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3.98 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.59 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.98 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

By the **energy of a satellite in a circular orbit**, $E = -\dfrac{G M_T m}{2r}$, and the work to be supplied is the change in mechanical energy. Going from $r$ to $2r$
$$
L = -\,\frac{G M_T m}{4r} + \frac{G M_T m}{2r} = \frac{G M_T m}{4r} .
$$
With the data, $L = \dfrac{3.98 \cdot 10^{14} \cdot 400}{4 \cdot 1.00 \cdot 10^7}\,\mathrm{J} = 3.98 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$. The change in potential energy alone is twice as much, $7.96 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$, but in the transfer the kinetic energy decreases by $3.98 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$.
