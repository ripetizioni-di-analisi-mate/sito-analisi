The International Space Station moves in a circular orbit at an altitude of about $400\,\mathrm{km}$, where the gravitational acceleration is about $90\,\%$ of its value at the ground. Which statement explains why the astronauts on board float?

- At that altitude the gravitational force of the Earth on the astronauts is zero
- Astronauts and station have the same acceleration $\vec{g}$, so the floor exerts no normal force
- The gravitational force of the Earth is balanced by that of the Moon
- At that altitude the gravitational force of the Earth is about $1\,\%$ of its value at the ground

## Explanation

At an altitude of $400\,\mathrm{km}$ the gravitational force on the astronauts is about $90\,\%$ of their weight at the ground, so the gravitational force is present. The only force acting on the station and on the astronauts is gravity, therefore they have the same acceleration $\vec{g}$ and are in free fall together. As a consequence the floor exerts no normal force, and the apparent weight, which is precisely the normal force from the supporting surface, is zero.
