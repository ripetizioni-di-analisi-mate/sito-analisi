A body of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest on the Earth's surface. What minimum energy must be supplied to it to place it on a circular orbit grazing the surface? The atmosphere and the Earth's rotation are neglected; $g_0 = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $R_T = 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$.

- $6.25 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.12 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.56 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $9.37 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

On the surface the body is at rest, so its mechanical energy is $E_1 = -\dfrac{G M_T m}{R_T}$. By the **energy of a satellite on a circular orbit**, on the grazing orbit $E_2 = -\dfrac{G M_T m}{2R_T}$.

The minimum energy to be supplied is the difference
$$
E_2 - E_1 = \frac{G M_T m}{2R_T} = \frac{m g_0 R_T}{2} ,
$$
where $G M_T = g_0 R_T^2$ has been used. With the data one obtains $\dfrac{1.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 6.37 \cdot 10^6}{2}\,\mathrm{J} = 3.12 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J}$.

The distance from the center does not change, therefore all the energy supplied becomes kinetic energy. The value $6.25 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J}$ is the energy needed to escape from the Earth, which is twice as large.
