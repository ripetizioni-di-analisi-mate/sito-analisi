The Moon, of mass $M_L = 7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{kg}$, has its center at distance $d = 3.84 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center of the Earth, of radius $R_T = 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$. For a mass of $1.00\,\mathrm{kg}$, the Moon's gravitational force at the point of the Earth's surface closest to the Moon is compared with the force the Moon would exert on the same mass at the center of the Earth. By how much do the two magnitudes differ? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $3.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.5 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The point of the surface closest to the Moon is at distance $d - R_T$ from the center of the Moon, while the center of the Earth is at distance $d$. The difference of the magnitudes is therefore
$$
\Delta F = G M_L \left( \frac{1}{(d - R_T)^2} - \frac{1}{d^2} \right) .
$$
Substituting the data, $\dfrac{1}{(3.776 \cdot 10^8)^2} - \dfrac{1}{(3.84 \cdot 10^8)^2} = 2.31 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$, therefore $\Delta F = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 7.35 \cdot 10^{22} \cdot 2.31 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{N} = 1.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N}$.

The difference is about $\dfrac{2 R_T}{d}$ times the force at the center, which is $3.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$; this difference is what produces the tides.
