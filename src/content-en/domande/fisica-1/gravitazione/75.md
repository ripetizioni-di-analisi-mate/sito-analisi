A satellite moves on a circular orbit in the equatorial plane with period $3.00\,\mathrm{h}$, in the same direction as the Earth's rotation. The Earth completes one rotation in $24.0\,\mathrm{h}$. After how long does the satellite pass again over the same point of the equator?

- $3.00\,\mathrm{h}$
- $2.67\,\mathrm{h}$
- $3.43\,\mathrm{h}$
- $27.0\,\mathrm{h}$

## Explanation

The satellite rotates with angular velocity $\omega_s = \dfrac{2\pi}{T_s}$ and the point on the equator with $\omega_T = \dfrac{2\pi}{T_T}$, in the same direction. Relative to the point, the satellite therefore rotates with angular velocity $\omega_s - \omega_T$.

The satellite passes over the point again when the relative angle equals $2\pi$, that is, after the time $t$ such that
$$
\frac{1}{t} = \frac{1}{T_s} - \frac{1}{T_T} = \left( \frac{1}{3.00} - \frac{1}{24.0} \right)\,\mathrm{h^{-1}} = \frac{7}{24}\,\mathrm{h^{-1}} .
$$
Therefore $t = \dfrac{24}{7}\,\mathrm{h} = 3.43\,\mathrm{h}$. The value $2.67\,\mathrm{h}$ is obtained by adding the angular velocities, as for an orbit traveled opposite to the Earth's rotation.
