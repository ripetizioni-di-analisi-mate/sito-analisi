A uniform sphere has radius $40\,\mathrm{m}$ and surface gravity $g_0$. Consider an interior point. The initial data are the surface gravity $g_{0} = \dfrac{1}{25000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the distance from the center $r = 10\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the surface gravity to $\dfrac{1}{12500}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the distance from the center to $5\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $g$ minus its initial value?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $- \dfrac{1}{800000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $- \dfrac{3}{6400000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{100000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Only the mass inside radius $r$ contributes. It scales as $r^3$, while the distance factor is $1/r^2$. The relation $g = \dfrac{g_{0} \cdot r}{40}$ gives $\dfrac{1}{100000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ before the change and $\dfrac{1}{100000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
