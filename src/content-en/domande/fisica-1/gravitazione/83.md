To bring a satellite, at rest on the Earth's surface, onto a circular orbit, it must be given the increase in gravitational potential energy and the orbital kinetic energy. At what height above the surface are the two contributions equal? The atmosphere and the Earth's rotation are neglected; $R_T = 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$.

- $3.19 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.37 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.56 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.27 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

On the circular orbit of radius $r$ the kinetic energy is $K = \dfrac{G M_T m}{2r}$, while the increase in potential energy from the surface is $\Delta U = G M_T m \left( \dfrac{1}{R_T} - \dfrac{1}{r} \right)$.

Setting $\Delta U = K$ and dividing by $G M_T m$ gives
$$
\frac{1}{R_T} - \frac{1}{r} = \frac{1}{2r} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r = \frac{3}{2}\,R_T .
$$
The height is therefore $h = r - R_T = \dfrac{R_T}{2} = 3.19 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $9.56 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$ is the radius of the orbit, measured from the center of the Earth; the value $6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the kinetic energy.
