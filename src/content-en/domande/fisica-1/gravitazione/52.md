At a point $P$ the gravitational potential, zero at infinite distance, is $V(P) = -6.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. What minimum work must be supplied to carry a body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, at rest at $P$, to infinite distance, where it arrives with zero speed?

- $-1.2 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The potential energy of the body at $P$ is $U = m V(P) = 2.0 \cdot (-6.0 \cdot 10^6)\,\mathrm{J} = -1.2 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J}$, while at infinite distance it is zero.

The kinetic energy is zero both at the start and at the end, so the work supplied equals the change in potential energy:
$$
L = U_\infty - U_P = 0 - (-1.2 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J}) = 1.2 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $6.0 \cdot 10^6$ is the work per unit mass, expressed in $\mathrm{J/kg}$.
