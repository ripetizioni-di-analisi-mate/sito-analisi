Inside a homogeneous sphere of mass $M$ and radius $R$ the gravitational potential, zero at infinite distance, is
$$
V(r) = -\,\frac{GM}{2R^3}\,(3R^2 - r^2) ,
$$
where $r$ is the distance from the center. What is the radial component $g_r$ of the gravitational field for $0 \leq r \leq R$?

- $g_r = \dfrac{GM}{R^3}\,r$
- $g_r = -\dfrac{GM}{2R^3}\,r$
- $g_r = -\dfrac{GM}{R^3}\,r$
- $g_r = -\dfrac{GM}{r^2}$

## Explanation

The field is obtained from the potential through $g_r = -\dfrac{dV}{dr}$. Differentiating,
$$
\frac{dV}{dr} = -\,\frac{GM}{2R^3} \cdot (-2r) = \frac{GM}{R^3}\,r ,
$$
so $g_r = -\dfrac{GM}{R^3}\,r$.

The radial component is negative, therefore the field points toward the center, and its magnitude $\dfrac{GM}{R^3}\,r$ agrees with the field of a homogeneous sphere at interior points. The positive sign comes from forgetting the minus sign in the relation between field and potential.
