A moon of a planet moves in a circular orbit of radius $3{,}6 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{m}$ in $27$ days. What is the radius of the circular orbit of a satellite of the same planet with a period of $1{,}0$ day?

- $1{,}3 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4{,}0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6{,}9 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1{,}2 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By **Kepler's third law** for circular orbits, around the same planet $\dfrac{r_1^3}{r_2^3} = \dfrac{T_1^2}{T_2^2}$. The ratio of the periods is $27$, so the ratio of the cubes of the radii is $27^2 = 729$. The ratio of the radii is then $\sqrt[3]{729} = 9$, therefore the required radius is $\dfrac{3{,}6 \cdot 10^8}{9}\,\mathrm{m} = 4{,}0 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m}$.
