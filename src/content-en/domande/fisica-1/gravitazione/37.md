A planet travels its elliptical orbit around the Sun in $12$ years. During a certain time interval the segment joining the Sun to the planet sweeps an area equal to one third of the area enclosed by the orbit. How long is the interval?

- $4.0$ years, on any arc of the orbit
- $4.0$ years only if the arc is symmetric about the perihelion
- Less than $4.0$ years if the arc contains the perihelion
- $6.9$ years, on any arc of the orbit

## Explanation

By **Kepler's second law** the areal velocity is constant, so the area swept by the position vector is proportional to the time taken.

In one period of $12$ years the position vector sweeps the whole area of the ellipse. Therefore one third of the area is swept in one third of the period, that is, in $4.0$ years.

Near the perihelion the planet covers a longer arc in the same time, but the swept area is the same, so the duration does not depend on the arc considered.
