We want a satellite on a circular orbit that stays always above Rome, which lies at $42^\circ$ north latitude. Which statement is true?

- It is possible, if the period equals the rotation period of the Earth
- It is not possible, because the plane of a circular orbit passes through the center of the Earth
- It is possible, if the altitude is about $36\,000\,\mathrm{km}$
- It is not possible, because at that latitude the period would have to exceed one day

## Explanation

The gravitational force always points toward the center of the Earth, so the angular momentum of the satellite about the center is constant and the motion takes place in a plane through the center of the Earth.

Because of the Earth's rotation, a fixed point on the Earth at latitude $42^\circ$ describes a circle whose center is on the Earth's axis but away from the center of the Earth, in a plane that does not pass through the center.

Therefore no orbit can follow Rome. Geostationary orbits lie in the equatorial plane, and the altitude of about $36\,000\,\mathrm{km}$ together with a period of one sidereal day are necessary conditions, not sufficient ones.
