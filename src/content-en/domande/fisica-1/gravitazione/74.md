A uniform sphere has radius $40\,\mathrm{m}$ and surface gravity $g_0$. Consider an interior point. The initial data are the surface gravity $g_{0} = \dfrac{1}{25000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the distance from the center $r = 10\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the distance from the center to $20\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $g$ to its initial value?

- $4$
- $2$
- $8$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Only the mass inside radius $r$ contributes. It scales as $r^3$, while the distance factor is $1/r^2$. The relation $g = \dfrac{g_{0} \cdot r}{40}$ gives $\dfrac{1}{100000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ before the change and $\dfrac{1}{50000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
