A planet has twice the mass and twice the radius of the Earth, from whose surface the escape velocity is $11{,}2\,\mathrm{km/s}$. What is the escape velocity from the surface of the planet?

- $11{,}2\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $15{,}8\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $7{,}92\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $5{,}60\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

The formula for **escape velocity** gives $v_f = \sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}$, which depends on the ratio $\dfrac{M}{R}$. Doubling both the mass and the radius leaves the ratio $\dfrac{M}{R}$ unchanged, so the escape velocity is equal to that of the Earth, $11{,}2\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The gravitational acceleration at the surface, instead, is halved, because $g_0 = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$ contains the square of the radius.
