For a body carried from the Earth's surface to height $h$, the expression $m g_0 h$ overestimates the exact change in gravitational potential energy. At what height is the overestimate $10\%$? ($R_T = 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$)

- $3.19 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.37 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.37 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.01 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The exact change is $\Delta U = G M_T m \left( \dfrac{1}{R_T} - \dfrac{1}{R_T + h} \right) = \dfrac{G M_T m\,h}{R_T (R_T + h)}$. Since $G M_T = g_0 R_T^2$, we have $\Delta U = m g_0 h\,\dfrac{R_T}{R_T + h}$.

The ratio of the approximation to the exact value is therefore
$$
\frac{m g_0 h}{\Delta U} = 1 + \frac{h}{R_T} .
$$
The overestimate is $10\%$ if $\dfrac{h}{R_T} = 0.10$, that is, for $h = 0.10 \cdot 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m} = 6.37 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m}$.
