Two satellites orbit the same planet in circular orbits. The first has period $4\,\mathrm{h}$ and orbital radius $R_1$. Given the second orbital radius $R_{2} = 40\,\mathrm{Mm}$ and the second satellite's period $T = 32\,\mathrm{h}$, what is the first orbital radius $R_{1}$?

- $5\,\mathrm{Mm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{Mm}$
- $10 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{Mm}$
- $20\,\mathrm{Mm}$

## Explanation

By Kepler's third law, the square of the period is proportional to the cube of orbital radius. In the stated units, the relation is $T = \dfrac{4 \cdot R_{2}^{\dfrac{3}{2}}}{R_{1}^{\dfrac{3}{2}}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $R_{1} = \dfrac{2 \cdot \sqrt[3]{2} \cdot R_{2}}{T^{\dfrac{2}{3}}}$. Substituting the given values yields $10\,\mathrm{Mm}$.
