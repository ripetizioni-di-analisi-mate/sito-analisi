Four equal point masses, $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ each, are at the vertices of a square whose diagonals are $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long. What is the gravitational potential at the center of the square, with zero potential at infinite distance? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $0\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $-1.3 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $-2.7 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $-5.3 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$

## Explanation

The center of the square is the midpoint of the diagonals, so it is at a distance $r = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from each vertex. Each mass produces there the potential $-\dfrac{Gm}{r} = -1.33 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. The potential is a scalar quantity, therefore the four contributions add up and $V = 4 \cdot \left( -1.33 \cdot 10^{-10} \right)\,\mathrm{J/kg} = -5.3 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. At the center the field is zero, since it is a vector with contributions that are opposite in pairs, while the potential is negative.
