A thin uniform rod of mass $M$ and length $L$ lies on the $x$ axis, with its ends at the points $x = L$ and $x = 2L$. What is the magnitude of the gravitational field of the rod at the origin?

- $\dfrac{4GM}{9L^2}$
- $\dfrac{GM}{L^2}$
- $\dfrac{GM}{4L^2}$
- $\dfrac{GM}{2L^2}$

## Explanation

The rod is divided into pieces of length $dx$, each of mass $dm = \dfrac{M}{L}\,dx$. The piece located at the point with abscissa $x$ produces at the origin a field of magnitude $\dfrac{G\,dm}{x^2}$, directed toward the piece, that is, in the positive direction of the $x$ axis.

All the contributions have the same direction and the same sense, so by the **superposition principle** the magnitudes add up:
$$
g = \int_L^{2L} \frac{GM}{L}\,\frac{dx}{x^2} = \frac{GM}{L} \left( \frac{1}{L} - \frac{1}{2L} \right) = \frac{GM}{2L^2} .
$$
Concentrating the mass at the center of the rod, at distance $\dfrac{3L}{2}$, would give $\dfrac{4GM}{9L^2}$, which is a smaller value. The rod does not have spherical symmetry, therefore it does not attract like a point mass placed at its center.
