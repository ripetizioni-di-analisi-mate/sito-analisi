A satellite of mass $m$ completes one full turn of a circular orbit of radius $r$ around a planet of mass $M$. What is the work done by the planet's gravitational force during the turn?

- $-\dfrac{GMm}{r}$
- $0$
- $\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$
- $-2\pi\,\dfrac{GMm}{r}$

## Explanation

On a circular orbit the gravitational force points toward the center, while the displacement is tangent to the orbit. The two directions are perpendicular, so on every element $\vec{F} \cdot d\vec{s} = 0$.

Therefore the work is zero, both over a full turn and over any arc of the orbit.

The same result follows from the potential energy: the distance from the center stays equal to $r$, so $U = -\dfrac{GMm}{r}$ does not change and the work $U_{\text{initial}} - U_{\text{final}}$ is zero.
