For the Moon orbiting the Earth and for the Earth orbiting the Sun the ratio $\dfrac{T^2}{r^3}$ is computed, taking both orbits as circular. With $M_S = 1.99 \cdot 10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $M_T = 5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $M_L = 7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{kg}$, which statement is true?

- The Moon's ratio is about $3.3 \cdot 10^5$ times the Earth's ratio
- The two ratios are equal
- The Earth's ratio is about $3.3 \cdot 10^5$ times the Moon's ratio
- The Moon's ratio is about $81$ times the Earth's ratio

## Explanation

For a circular orbit around a central mass $M$ we have $\dfrac{T^2}{r^3} = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}$, so the constant of Kepler's third law depends on the central body.

For the Moon the central body is the Earth, for the Earth it is the Sun, therefore
$$
\frac{\left( \dfrac{T^2}{r^3} \right)_{\text{Moon}}}{\left( \dfrac{T^2}{r^3} \right)_{\text{Earth}}} = \frac{M_S}{M_T} = 3.3 \cdot 10^5 .
$$
The third law with the same constant holds only for bodies orbiting the same central mass; the ratio $81$ between the masses of the Earth and the Moon plays no role, because the mass of the orbiting body does not appear in the constant.
