At a point $P$ a body of mass $m$ is subject to a gravitational force of magnitude $F$, due to a planet. If a body of mass $3m$ is placed at the same point, how does the gravitational field of the planet at $P$ change?

- It becomes three times larger
- It becomes one third
- It stays the same
- It becomes nine times larger

## Explanation

The field at $P$ is $\vec{g}(P) = \dfrac{\vec{F}}{m}$, and the gravitational force is proportional to the mass of the body placed at $P$. With the mass $3m$ the force becomes $3F$, so the ratio of force to mass is still $\dfrac{F}{m}$. Therefore the field **stays the same**, because it depends only on the mass of the planet and on the position of $P$. It is the force that becomes three times larger.
