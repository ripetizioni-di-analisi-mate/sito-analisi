The Moon is $3.84 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center of the Earth and about $1.50 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center of the Sun. With $M_T = 5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $M_S = 1.99 \cdot 10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$, which statement about the gravitational forces exerted on the Moon by the Sun and by the Earth is true?

- The Earth's force is about twice the Sun's force
- The Sun's force is about $850$ times the Earth's force
- The Sun's force is about $3 \cdot 10^5$ times the Earth's force
- The Sun's force is about twice the Earth's force

## Explanation

Both forces act on the same mass $M_L$, so their ratio depends only on the attracting masses and on the distances:
$$
\frac{F_S}{F_T} = \frac{M_S}{M_T} \left( \frac{d_T}{d_S} \right)^2 = 3.33 \cdot 10^5 \cdot \left( \frac{3.84 \cdot 10^8}{1.50 \cdot 10^{11}} \right)^2 = 2.2 .
$$
Therefore the Sun attracts the Moon with a force about twice that of the Earth. The value $850$ is obtained by raising the ratio of the distances to the first power instead of squaring it; the value $3 \cdot 10^5$ is the ratio of the masses alone.
