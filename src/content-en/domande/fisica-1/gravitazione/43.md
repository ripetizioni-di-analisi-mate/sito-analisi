A satellite moves in a circular orbit around the Earth with speed $v = 7{,}0\,\mathrm{km/s}$. What minimum speed must it reach, at the same position, to move away from the Earth indefinitely? The atmosphere and the attraction of other bodies are neglected.

- $14\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $4{,}9\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $9{,}9\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

At the distance $r$ from the center the speed of the circular orbit is $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{G M_T}{r}}$. The body moves away indefinitely if its mechanical energy is at least zero, that is, if $\dfrac{1}{2} m v_f^2 \geq \dfrac{G M_T m}{r}$. Hence the minimum speed is $v_f = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 G M_T}{r}} = \sqrt{2}\,v = 1{,}41 \cdot 7{,}0\,\mathrm{km/s} = 9{,}9\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The value $14\,\mathrm{km/s}$ doubles the speed, while the energy condition doubles its square.
