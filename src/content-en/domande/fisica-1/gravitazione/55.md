Two point masses $M$ and $2M$ are at distance $d$. With the gravitational potential zero at infinite distance, at which points at a finite distance from the masses is the total potential zero?

- At the point of the segment where the total field is zero
- At no point
- At the midpoint of the segment joining the masses
- At the points of the plane perpendicular to the segment through its midpoint

## Explanation

The potential of a point mass $m$ at distance $r$ is $-\dfrac{Gm}{r}$, which is negative for every finite distance.

The total potential is the sum of the two potentials, $V = -\dfrac{GM}{r_1} - \dfrac{2GM}{r_2}$, that is, the sum of two negative numbers, so it is negative at every point.

At the point of the segment where the field is zero the two forces cancel, being vectors of opposite direction; the two potentials, instead, are scalars of the same sign and do not compensate each other.
