At a point $P$ outside a spherical planet, the gravitational field of the planet has magnitude $2.0\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ and the gravitational potential, zero at infinity, is $-8.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. How far from the center of the planet is $P$?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At outside points the planet behaves like a point mass $M$ placed at its center, so at distance $r$ from the center
$$
g = \frac{GM}{r^2} , \qquad V = -\,\frac{GM}{r} .
$$
Dividing the magnitude of the potential by the magnitude of the field, the product $GM$ cancels and one obtains $\dfrac{|V|}{g} = r$.

Therefore $r = \dfrac{8.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}}{2.0\,\mathrm{N/kg}} = 4.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$, since $\mathrm{J/N} = \mathrm{m}$. The value $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by taking the square root of the ratio, which is already a length.
