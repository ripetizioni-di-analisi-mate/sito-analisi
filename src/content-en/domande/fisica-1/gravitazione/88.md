The position vector joining the Sun to a comet sweeps an area of $1.5 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ per second. At perihelion the comet is $7.5 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{m}$ from the Sun. What is its speed at perihelion?

- $20\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $40\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $6.4\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $80\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

The areal velocity is $\dfrac{dA}{dt} = \dfrac{|\vec{L}|}{2m}$. At perihelion the velocity is perpendicular to the position vector, so $|\vec{L}| = m r_p v_p$ and
$$
\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{r_p v_p}{2} .
$$
Therefore $v_p = \dfrac{2}{r_p}\,\dfrac{dA}{dt} = \dfrac{2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^{15}}{7.5 \cdot 10^{10}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{m/s} = 40\,\mathrm{km/s}$.

The value $20\,\mathrm{km/s}$ forgets the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the area of the triangle swept by the position vector.
