The escape speed from the surface of the Moon is $2.37\,\mathrm{km/s}$. A body is launched from the lunar surface with speed $3.0\,\mathrm{km/s}$ in a direction inclined at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Neglecting the rotation of the Moon and the attraction of other bodies, what happens to the body? The Moon has no atmosphere.

- It falls back onto the Moon, because the vertical component of the velocity is smaller than the escape speed
- It goes into a circular orbit around the Moon
- It reaches a maximum distance equal to twice the lunar radius and then falls back
- It moves indefinitely far from the Moon

## Explanation

Gravity is conservative, so the mechanical energy $E = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 - \dfrac{G M_L m}{r}$ depends only on the magnitude of the velocity and on the distance, not on the direction of launch.

Since $3.0\,\mathrm{km/s}$ is larger than the escape speed, the mechanical energy is positive and the motion is **unbound**.

Therefore the body moves indefinitely far away for any outward direction of launch. Comparing only the vertical component with the escape speed is an error, because the kinetic energy depends on the magnitude of the velocity.
