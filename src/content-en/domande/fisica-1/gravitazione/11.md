A satellite of mass $800\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves in a circular orbit of radius $r = 8{,}0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$ around the Earth, with $G M_T = 3{,}98 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$. What is the minimum work needed to bring it to an infinite distance?

- $3{,}98 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1{,}99 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $9{,}95 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1{,}99 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

From the expression for the **energy of a satellite in a circular orbit**, the mechanical energy is $E = -\dfrac{G M_T m}{2r}$. With the data, $E = -\dfrac{3{,}98 \cdot 10^{14} \cdot 800}{2 \cdot 8{,}0 \cdot 10^6}\,\mathrm{J} = -1{,}99 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$. At an infinite distance, with a speed that tends to zero, the mechanical energy is zero, so the minimum work to be supplied is $0 - E = 1{,}99 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $\dfrac{G M_T m}{r} = 3{,}98 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$ neglects the kinetic energy that the satellite already has in orbit.
