Two equal point masses, $M = 50\,\mathrm{kg}$ each, are at the points $(-0{,}30\,\mathrm{m};\ 0)$ and $(0{,}30\,\mathrm{m};\ 0)$. What is the magnitude of the total gravitational field at the point $P = (0;\ 0{,}40\,\mathrm{m})$? ($G = 6{,}67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $2{,}1 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N/kg}$
- $2{,}7 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N/kg}$
- $1{,}6 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N/kg}$
- $1{,}3 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N/kg}$

## Explanation

The distance of each mass from $P$ is $\sqrt{0{,}30^2 + 0{,}40^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 0{,}50\,\mathrm{m}$, so each one produces at $P$ a field of magnitude $g_1 = \dfrac{GM}{0{,}50^2} = 1{,}33 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, directed toward the mass. By symmetry the $x$ components of the two fields are opposite, while the $y$ components are equal and directed toward the origin. Each $y$ component equals $g_1 \cos\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between the field and the $y$ axis and $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{0{,}40}{0{,}50} = 0{,}80$. By the **superposition principle** the total field has magnitude $2 \cdot 0{,}80 \cdot g_1 = 2{,}1 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N/kg}$.
