A planet is a sphere of radius $R$ with uniform density. Along a straight line leaving the center, at which point is the magnitude of the planet's gravitational field maximum?

- At the center of the planet
- At distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from the center
- On the surface of the planet
- The magnitude is the same at all interior points

## Explanation

Inside a homogeneous sphere the field has magnitude $g(r) = \dfrac{GM}{R^3}\,r$, so it grows linearly from $0$ at the center to $\dfrac{GM}{R^2}$ at the surface.

Outside, the magnitude is $\dfrac{GM}{r^2}$, which decreases as $r$ increases.

Therefore the magnitude of the field is maximum for $r = R$, that is, at the surface. At the center the field is zero, because the attractions of the parts of the planet balance each other.
