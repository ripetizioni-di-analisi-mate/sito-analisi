Two stars of equal mass $M = 2.0 \cdot 10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$ move on the same circular orbit about their center of mass, keeping a constant distance $d = 1.5 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{m}$ between their centers. What is the period of revolution? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$; one day is $8.64 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{s}$)

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" punti="-1,0,pieno,M;1,0,pieno,M;0,0,vuoto,C" segmenti="-1,0,1,0" vettori="1,0,1,0.6,v;-1,0,-1,-0.6,v" etichette="0.5,-0.15,d" xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 titolo="The two stars are at the ends of a diameter of the orbit, with center $C$"}

- $259$ days
- $366$ days
- $129$ days
- $183$ days

## Explanation

The stars have the same mass, so the center of mass is at the midpoint and each star moves on a circle of radius $\dfrac{d}{2}$. The force on each star is the attraction of the other, of magnitude $\dfrac{G M^2}{d^2}$.

By Newton's second law, with $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$,
$$
\frac{G M^2}{d^2} = M \omega^2\,\frac{d}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{d^3}{2GM}} .
$$
Substituting, $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{(1.5 \cdot 10^{11})^3}{2 \cdot 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{30}}}\,\mathrm{s} = 2.24 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{s} = 259$ days.

The value $366$ days corresponds to one star orbiting the other, held fixed, with radius $d$.
