A satellite moves on a circular orbit around the Earth, outside the atmosphere; the attraction of the Moon and of the Sun is neglected. In an inertial frame of reference, which forces act on the satellite?

- The gravitational force and the centrifugal force, which balance each other
- The gravitational force and a force tangent to the orbit in the direction of motion
- No force, because the satellite is weightless
- Only the Earth's gravitational force, directed toward the center of the orbit

## Explanation

In an inertial frame forces are due to interactions with other bodies, and the only body interacting with the satellite is the Earth. So only the gravitational force acts on the satellite.

This force points toward the center and provides the centripetal acceleration $\dfrac{v^2}{r}$; since it has no tangential component, the magnitude of the velocity stays constant without any other force.

The centrifugal force appears only in a rotating frame of reference, which is not inertial. Weightlessness means that the satellite is in free fall, while the gravitational force is present.
