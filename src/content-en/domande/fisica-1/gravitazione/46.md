The Moon is about $60$ Earth radii from the center of the Earth. If the Earth's attraction decreases as the inverse square of the distance from the center and the gravitational acceleration at the surface is $g_0 = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what acceleration does the Earth give the Moon?

- $0.164\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.73 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4.54 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8.18 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Outside the Earth the gravitational field has magnitude $\dfrac{G M_T}{r^2}$, so at distance $r$ from the center it equals $g_0 \left( \dfrac{R_T}{r} \right)^2$.

For $r = 60\,R_T$ the factor is $\left( \dfrac{1}{60} \right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{3600}$, therefore
$$
a = \frac{9.81}{3600}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.73 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
This value agrees within $1\%$ with the centripetal acceleration of the Moon obtained from the radius and the period of its orbit, and it is the check by which Newton verified the $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$ dependence.
