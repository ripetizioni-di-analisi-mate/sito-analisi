The engines of a satellite on a circular orbit do a total work $W = 3.0 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$ and bring it onto another circular orbit around the same planet. What is the change in the gravitational potential energy of the satellite? The change in mass due to the fuel is neglected.

- $3.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work of the engines is the only work done by non-conservative forces, so the mechanical energy increases by $\Delta E = W = 3.0 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$.

On every circular orbit $U = 2E$, therefore $\Delta U = 2\,\Delta E = 6.0 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$.

Consequently the kinetic energy changes by $\Delta K = \Delta E - \Delta U = -3.0 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$: the satellite rises to a higher orbit and moves more slowly.
