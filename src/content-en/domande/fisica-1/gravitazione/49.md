A mass $m_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at the origin and a mass $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at the point $P = (3.0\,\mathrm{m};\ 4.0\,\mathrm{m})$. What is the force $\vec{F}_{12}$ exerted by $m_1$ on $m_2$? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

::grafico{uguale=1 nomi="x,y" punti="0,0,pieno,m₁;3,4,pieno,m₂" segmenti="0,0,3,4" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=5 titolo="Positions of the two masses; coordinates are in meters"}

- $(9.6\,\hat{\imath} + 13\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-9.6\,\hat{\imath} - 13\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-4.8\,\hat{\imath} - 6.4\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-1.9\,\hat{\imath} - 2.6\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The distance between the masses is $r = \sqrt{3.0^2 + 4.0^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$, so the magnitude of the force is
$$
F = G\,\frac{2.0 \cdot 3.0}{5.0^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The unit vector from $m_1$ to $m_2$ is $\hat{u}_r = 0.60\,\hat{\imath} + 0.80\,\hat{\jmath}$. The force on $m_2$ is attractive, therefore it points toward $m_1$, that is, opposite to $\hat{u}_r$, so $\vec{F}_{12} = -F\,\hat{u}_r$.

This gives $\vec{F}_{12} = (-9.6\,\hat{\imath} - 13\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{N}$. Multiplying the magnitude by the vector $(3.0;\ 4.0)$, which does not have unit length, would give components five times too large.
