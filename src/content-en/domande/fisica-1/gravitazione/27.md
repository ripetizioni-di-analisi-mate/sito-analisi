A planet is a homogeneous sphere of radius $R$. At what distance from the center, inside the planet, is the magnitude of the gravitational field equal to its value outside, at distance $2R$ from the center?

- $\dfrac{R}{2}$
- $\dfrac{R}{8}$
- $\dfrac{R}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3R}{4}$

## Explanation

Let $g_0$ be the magnitude of the field at the surface. Outside, the field decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r^2}$, so at distance $2R$ it equals $g_0 \left( \dfrac{R}{2R} \right)^2 = \dfrac{g_0}{4}$.

Inside a homogeneous sphere the field grows linearly with the distance from the center, $g(r) = g_0\,\dfrac{r}{R}$.

Setting $g_0\,\dfrac{r}{R} = \dfrac{g_0}{4}$ gives $r = \dfrac{R}{4}$. The distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ would be correct if the outside field decreased as $\dfrac{1}{r}$.
