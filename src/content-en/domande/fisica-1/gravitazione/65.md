The Sun has mass $M_S = 1.99 \cdot 10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$. If it were compressed into a sphere, what radius would it need for the escape speed from its surface, computed with the Newtonian formula, to equal the speed of light $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $2.95 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.48 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.90 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $54.3\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The escape speed from a sphere of mass $M$ and radius $R$ is $v_f = \sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}$. Setting $v_f = c$ and squaring gives $\dfrac{2GM}{R} = c^2$, hence
$$
R = \frac{2 G M_S}{c^2} = \frac{2 \cdot 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 1.99 \cdot 10^{30}}{(3.00 \cdot 10^8)^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.95 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The radius is about $3\,\mathrm{km}$, while the actual radius of the Sun is about $7 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $1.48 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from forgetting the factor $2$ in the escape speed.
