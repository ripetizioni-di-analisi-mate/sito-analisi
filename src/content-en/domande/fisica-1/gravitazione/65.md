Two satellites orbit the same planet in circular orbits. The first has period $4\,\mathrm{h}$ and orbital radius $R_1$. The initial data are the first orbital radius $R_{1} = 10\,\mathrm{Mm}$ and the second orbital radius $R_{2} = 40\,\mathrm{Mm}$. Change the first orbital radius to $20\,\mathrm{Mm}$ and the second orbital radius to $45\,\mathrm{Mm}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $T$ minus its initial value?

- $- \dfrac{175}{4}\,\mathrm{h}$
- $\dfrac{37}{2}\,\mathrm{h}$
- $- \dfrac{37}{2}\,\mathrm{h}$
- $-7\,\mathrm{h}$

## Explanation

By Kepler's third law, the square of the period is proportional to the cube of orbital radius. The relation $T = \dfrac{4 \cdot R_{2}^{\dfrac{3}{2}}}{R_{1}^{\dfrac{3}{2}}}$ gives $32\,\mathrm{h}$ before the change and $\dfrac{27}{2}\,\mathrm{h}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $- \dfrac{37}{2}\,\mathrm{h}$.
