A body moves radially away from a planet without atmosphere and at a certain instant has mechanical energy $E = 0$, with potential energy zero at infinite distance. Which statement is true?

- It reaches arbitrarily large distances and its speed tends to zero
- It reaches arbitrarily large distances and its speed tends to a positive value
- It reaches a maximum distance and then falls back onto the planet
- It moves away with constant speed

## Explanation

The only force is gravity, so the mechanical energy stays zero throughout the motion. For every distance $r$ we then have $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = \dfrac{GMm}{r} > 0$, therefore the speed never vanishes and the body keeps moving away. The motion is **unbound**, and as $r \to \infty$ the kinetic energy $\dfrac{GMm}{r}$ tends to zero. A positive final speed would require $E > 0$, with $v_{\infty} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2E}{m}}$.
