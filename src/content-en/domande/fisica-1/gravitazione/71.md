A uniform sphere has radius $40\,\mathrm{m}$ and surface gravity $g_0$. Consider an interior point. Given the surface gravity $g_{0} = \dfrac{1}{25000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the distance from the center $r = 10\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the interior gravitational acceleration $g$?

- $\dfrac{1}{100000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{50000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{400000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{1600000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Only the mass inside radius $r$ contributes. It scales as $r^3$, while the distance factor is $1/r^2$. In the stated units, the relation is $g = \dfrac{g_{0} \cdot r}{40}$. Substituting the given values yields $\dfrac{1}{100000}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
