A spherical planet of radius $R$ consists of a core of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ with uniform density $2\rho$ and a mantle with uniform density $\rho$. At the surface the gravitational acceleration is $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the gravitational acceleration at the boundary between core and mantle?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29;cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="0,0,2,0;0,0,0,1" punti="0,0,pieno" etichette="1.3,0.15,R;0.15,-0.45,core 2ρ;0,-1.5,mantle ρ" xmin=-2.4 xmax=2.4 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.4 titolo="Core of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ and density $2\rho$, mantle of density $\rho$"}

- $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $17.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8.72\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Let $V = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$ be the volume of the planet. The core has volume $\dfrac{V}{8}$ and mass $M_n = 2\rho\,\dfrac{V}{8} = \dfrac{\rho V}{4}$, while the mantle has mass $\rho\,\dfrac{7V}{8}$. The total mass is therefore $M = \dfrac{9}{8}\,\rho V$.

At the boundary of the core the mantle, which is a set of spherical shells outside the point, gives no field by the **shell theorem**. Therefore
$$
\frac{g\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)}{g_0} = \frac{M_n}{M} \cdot \frac{R^2}{\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^2} = 4\,\frac{M_n}{M} = 4 \cdot \frac{2}{9} = \frac{8}{9} .
$$
This gives $g = \dfrac{8}{9} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 8.72\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The value $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is that of the homogeneous planet, in which the field at half the radius is halved.
