A person of mass $m = 70\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on the Earth's surface. What acceleration does the gravitational force exerted by the person on the Earth give the Earth? Data: $G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$, $R_T = 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$.

- $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.15 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7.33 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

By Newton's third law the person attracts the Earth with a force whose magnitude equals the person's weight, $F = \dfrac{G M_T m}{R_T^2}$.

The acceleration of the Earth is $a = \dfrac{F}{M_T} = \dfrac{G m}{R_T^2}$, so the mass of the Earth cancels and
$$
a = \frac{6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 70}{(6.37 \cdot 10^6)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.15 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The acceleration is not zero, but it is completely negligible because the mass of the Earth is huge compared with that of the person.
