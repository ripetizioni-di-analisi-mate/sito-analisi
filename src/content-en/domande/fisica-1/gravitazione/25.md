A body is dropped from rest from an altitude equal to the Earth's radius, $h = R_T$, neglecting the atmosphere. With what speed does it reach the ground? Use $g_0 = 9{,}81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $R_T = 6{,}37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$.

- $11{,}2\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $7{,}91\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $5{,}59\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $13{,}7\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

The only force is gravity, which is conservative, so the mechanical energy is conserved. Dividing by the mass gives
$$
-\,\frac{G M_T}{2R_T} = \frac{1}{2} v^2 - \frac{G M_T}{R_T} , \quad\text{quindi}\quad v^2 = \frac{G M_T}{R_T} = g_0 R_T .
$$
Therefore $v = \sqrt{9{,}81 \cdot 6{,}37 \cdot 10^6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 7{,}91 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s} = 7{,}91\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The formula $\sqrt{2 g_0 h}$ for a fall with constant $g$ gives $11{,}2\,\mathrm{km/s}$ and overestimates the speed, because along the fall $g$ is smaller than $g_0$.
