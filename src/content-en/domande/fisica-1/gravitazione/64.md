A probe of mass $500\,\mathrm{kg}$, with its engines on, moves along a curved path from a point at distance $2R_T$ from the center of the Earth to a point at distance $3R_T$. What is the work done on the probe by the gravitational force of the Earth alone? Use $g_0 = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $R_T = 6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$.

- $5.21 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3.12 \cdot 10^{10}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-7.81 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-5.21 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The gravitational force is conservative, so its work depends only on the initial and final distances from the center, and not on the shape of the path or on the other forces acting on the probe. Therefore
$$
L = U(2R_T) - U(3R_T) = G M_T m \left( \frac{1}{3R_T} - \frac{1}{2R_T} \right) = -\,\frac{G M_T m}{6 R_T} .
$$
Since $G M_T = g_0 R_T^2$, one has $L = -\dfrac{m g_0 R_T}{6} = -\dfrac{500 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 6.37 \cdot 10^6}{6}\,\mathrm{J} = -5.21 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$.

The work is negative because the probe moves away from the Earth, which attracts it. The value $-7.81 \cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by treating the field $\dfrac{g_0}{4}$ at the starting point as constant along the radial displacement $R_T$.
