Two satellites orbit the same planet in circular orbits. The first has period $4\,\mathrm{h}$ and orbital radius $R_1$. The initial data are the first orbital radius $R_{1} = 10\,\mathrm{Mm}$ and the second orbital radius $R_{2} = 40\,\mathrm{Mm}$. Change the second orbital radius to $90\,\mathrm{Mm}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $T$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{81}{16}$
- $\dfrac{8}{27}$
- $\dfrac{9}{4}$
- $\dfrac{27}{8}$

## Explanation

By Kepler's third law, the square of the period is proportional to the cube of orbital radius. The relation $T = \dfrac{4 \cdot R_{2}^{\dfrac{3}{2}}}{R_{1}^{\dfrac{3}{2}}}$ gives $32\,\mathrm{h}$ before the change and $108\,\mathrm{h}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{27}{8}$.
