A thin homogeneous ring has mass $M$ and radius $R$. What is the magnitude of the ring's gravitational field at the point of its axis at distance $R$ from the center?

- $\dfrac{GM}{2R^2}$
- $\dfrac{GM}{2\sqrt{2}\,R^2}$
- $\dfrac{GM}{R^2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Each mass element $dm$ of the ring is at distance $\sqrt{R^2 + R^2} = \sqrt{2}\,R$ from the point, so it produces a field of magnitude $\dfrac{G\,dm}{2R^2}$, directed toward the element.

By **superposition** the contributions are added. Diametrically opposite elements give equal and opposite components perpendicular to the axis, therefore only the component along the axis remains, which makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the direction of each contribution.

Adding the axial components $\dfrac{G\,dm}{2R^2} \cos 45^\circ$ over the whole ring gives $g = \dfrac{GM}{2R^2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{GM}{2\sqrt{2}\,R^2}$. The value $\dfrac{GM}{2R^2}$ neglects the projection onto the axis.
