## Explanation

Let $x$ be the distance of the point from $m_1$. On the segment the two fields have opposite directions, so the total field is zero when their magnitudes are equal, that is, when
$$
\frac{G m_1}{x^2} = \frac{G m_2}{(d - x)^2} .
$$
Since $x$ and $d - x$ are positive, taking the square root gives $\dfrac{x}{d - x} = \sqrt{\dfrac{m_1}{m_2}} = 2$. Then $x = 2(d - x)$, that is, $x = \dfrac{2}{3}\,d = 0{,}60\,\mathrm{m}$. The point is closer to the smaller mass, because the larger mass must be at twice the distance to produce a field of the same magnitude.
