A spherical, homogeneous asteroid has density $\rho = 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. What is the period of a satellite on a circular orbit grazing the asteroid's surface? ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$)

- $2.3\,\mathrm{h}$
- $1.3\,\mathrm{h}$
- $4.7\,\mathrm{h}$
- $0.37\,\mathrm{h}$

## Explanation

For a circular orbit of radius equal to the radius $R$ of the asteroid the period is $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{R^3}{GM}}$. With $M = \rho\,\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$ we have $\dfrac{R^3}{GM} = \dfrac{3}{4\pi G \rho}$, so the radius cancels and
$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi G \rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{G \rho}} .
$$
Substituting, $T = \sqrt{\dfrac{3\pi}{6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3}}\,\mathrm{s} = 8.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{s} = 2.3\,\mathrm{h}$.

The period of the grazing orbit therefore depends only on the density, not on the size of the asteroid.
