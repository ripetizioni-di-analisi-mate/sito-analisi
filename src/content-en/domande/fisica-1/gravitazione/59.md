A comet passes at distance $r = 1.50 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{m}$ from the Sun with speed $50\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The Sun has mass $M_S = 1.99 \cdot 10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$, and the attraction of the planets is neglected ($G = 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$). What trajectory does the comet follow?

- An ellipse with the Sun at a focus
- A parabola with the Sun at the focus
- A branch of a hyperbola with the Sun at a focus
- A circle with the Sun at the center

## Explanation

The shape of the trajectory depends on the sign of the mechanical energy $E = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 - \dfrac{G M_S m}{r}$, and $E$ is positive if $v$ exceeds $\sqrt{\dfrac{2 G M_S}{r}}$.

At this distance
$$
\sqrt{\frac{2 G M_S}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 1.99 \cdot 10^{30}}{1.50 \cdot 10^{11}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 42.1\,\mathrm{km/s} .
$$
Since $50\,\mathrm{km/s}$ is greater than $42.1\,\mathrm{km/s}$, the mechanical energy is positive and the motion is **unbound**. Therefore the trajectory is a branch of a hyperbola with the Sun at a focus, and the comet does not come back. The ellipse corresponds to $E < 0$ and the parabola to $E = 0$.
