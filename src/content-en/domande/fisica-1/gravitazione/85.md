The Earth, of mass $5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$, moves around the Sun on an orbit taken as circular, of radius $1.50 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{m}$, with speed $2.98 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of its orbital angular momentum about the Sun?

- $2.67 \cdot 10^{40}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- $1.33 \cdot 10^{40}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- $4.47 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- $4.00 \cdot 10^{51}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$

## Explanation

The angular momentum about the Sun is $\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times m\vec{v}$. On a circular orbit the velocity is perpendicular to the position vector, so $|\vec{L}| = m r v$.

Substituting,
$$
|\vec{L}| = 5.97 \cdot 10^{24} \cdot 1.50 \cdot 10^{11} \cdot 2.98 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} = 2.67 \cdot 10^{40}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} .
$$
The value $1.33 \cdot 10^{40}$ is half the angular momentum and, divided by the mass, would give the areal velocity $\dfrac{|\vec{L}|}{2m}$.
