A body is launched from the surface of a planet without atmosphere with speed $v_0 = 10\,\mathrm{km/s}$, and the escape velocity from the planet is $v_f = 6{,}0\,\mathrm{km/s}$. Neglecting the attraction of other bodies, what speed does the body have when it reaches infinite distance?

- $8{,}0\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $4{,}0\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $5{,}7\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy is conserved, and at the surface it equals $E = \dfrac{1}{2} m v_0^2 - \dfrac{GMm}{R}$. Since $\dfrac{GMm}{R} = \dfrac{1}{2} m v_f^2$, we have $E = \dfrac{1}{2} m \left( v_0^2 - v_f^2 \right)$. At infinite distance the potential energy is zero, so $\dfrac{1}{2} m v_{\infty}^2 = E$ and
$$
v_{\infty} = \sqrt{v_0^2 - v_f^2} = \sqrt{100 - 36}\,\mathrm{km/s} = 8{,}0\,\mathrm{km/s} .
$$
The difference $v_0 - v_f = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{km/s}$ subtracts the speeds, while the conservation of energy subtracts their squares.
