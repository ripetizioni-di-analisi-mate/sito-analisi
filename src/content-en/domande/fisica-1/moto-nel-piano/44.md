On an ellipse with semi-axes $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, centered at the origin, a point moves according to $\vec{r}(t) = 3.0\cos t\;\hat{\imath} + 2.0\sin t\;\hat{\jmath}$, with $\vec{r}$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. Which statement about its acceleration is true?

- It points toward the origin and its magnitude is always $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- It is tangent to the trajectory at every point
- It is zero at the four vertices of the ellipse
- It points toward the origin and its magnitude varies between $2.0$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Differentiating twice component by component gives
$$
\vec{a}(t) = -3.0\cos t\;\hat{\imath} - 2.0\sin t\;\hat{\jmath} = -\vec{r}(t) ,
$$
with $\vec{a}$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Hence at every instant the acceleration is opposite to the position vector, that is, it points toward the origin. Its magnitude equals the distance of the point from the origin in meters, which on the ellipse varies between $2.0$ and $3.0$; therefore $|\vec{a}|$ varies between $2.0$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At the vertices the acceleration is $2.0$ or $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, so it is not zero.
