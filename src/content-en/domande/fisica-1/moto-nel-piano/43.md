Rain falls vertically with velocity $8{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the ground, and a car moves horizontally at $6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of the velocity of the rain relative to the car?

- $14\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5{,}3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The velocity of the rain relative to the car is the **relative velocity** $\vec{v}_{P,A} = \vec{v}_P - \vec{v}_A$. The vector $\vec{v}_P$ is vertical and the vector $-\vec{v}_A$ is horizontal, therefore the two vectors are perpendicular. Hence $|\vec{v}_{P,A}| = \sqrt{8{,}0^2 + 6{,}0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. For someone riding in the car the drops fall obliquely, with a horizontal component of $6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ opposite to the motion.
