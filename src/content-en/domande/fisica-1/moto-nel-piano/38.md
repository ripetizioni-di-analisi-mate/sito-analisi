A point moves in uniform circular motion. If the period is halved and the radius stays the same, how does the centripetal acceleration change?

- It doubles
- It is halved
- It becomes four times smaller
- It becomes four times larger

## Explanation

The angular velocity is $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$, therefore halving the period doubles the angular velocity. The centripetal acceleration $a_n = \omega^2 R = \dfrac{4\pi^2 R}{T^2}$ is proportional to the square of $\omega$, so for the same radius it becomes **four times larger**. For example, with $R = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ it goes from $4\pi^2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $T = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ to $16\pi^2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $T = 0.50\,\mathrm{s}$.
