A particle moves along a circle at changing speed. At the point under study the magnitudes of both tangential and normal acceleration are positive. Given the tangential acceleration magnitude $a_{t} = 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the normal acceleration magnitude $a_{n} = 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the total acceleration magnitude $a$?

- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The tangent and normal are perpendicular, so the magnitude is the square root of the sum of the squares of the two components. In the stated units, the relation is $a = \sqrt{a_{n}^{2} + a_{t}^{2}}$. Substituting the given values yields $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
