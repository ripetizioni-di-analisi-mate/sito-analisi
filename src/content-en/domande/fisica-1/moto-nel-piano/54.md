From the top of a straight slope inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal, a ball is thrown horizontally, downhill, at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting air resistance, at what distance from the launch point, measured along the slope, does the ball hit the slope?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1,0 0,0 7,-4.041" segmenti="0,0,3,0" vettori="0,0,1.6,0,v₀" punti="0,0,pieno" etichette="1.25,-0.32,30°" xmin=-1.2 xmax=7.4 ymin=-4.4 ymax=0.8 titolo="The ball leaves the top of the slope horizontally"}

- $11.8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $27.2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With the origin at the launch point, the ball is at $x = v_0\,t$ and $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$, while the points of the slope satisfy $y = -x\tan 30^\circ$. The ball hits the slope when $\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2 = v_0\,t\tan 30^\circ$, that is, at
$$
t = \frac{2v_0\tan 30^\circ}{g} = \frac{2 \cdot 10 \cdot 0.577}{9.81}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.18\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
At that instant the horizontal distance is $x = 11.8\,\mathrm{m}$, so the distance along the slope is $\dfrac{x}{\cos 30^\circ} = \dfrac{11.8}{0.866}\,\mathrm{m} = 13.6\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $11.8\,\mathrm{m}$ is only the horizontal distance.
