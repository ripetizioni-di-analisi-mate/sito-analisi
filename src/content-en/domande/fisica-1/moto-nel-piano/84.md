A boat moves at $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water of a river whose current flows at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The boat goes $1.2\,\mathrm{km}$ upstream and then returns to the starting point. How long does the round trip take?

- $400\,\mathrm{s}$
- $600\,\mathrm{s}$
- $300\,\mathrm{s}$
- $450\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

By the composition of velocities, relative to the bank the boat goes upstream at $6.0 - 2.0 = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and downstream at $6.0 + 2.0 = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. So the total time is
$$
t = \frac{1200\,\mathrm{m}}{4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}} + \frac{1200\,\mathrm{m}}{8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 300\,\mathrm{s} + 150\,\mathrm{s} = 450\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $400\,\mathrm{s}$ is the time in still water. The current lengthens the trip, because the boat spends more time on the slow leg than on the fast one.
