A point moves on a circle, and at a certain instant its acceleration is nonzero and perpendicular to the velocity. Which statement is true at that instant?

- The velocity is zero
- The motion is uniform circular at every instant
- The centripetal acceleration is zero
- The derivative of the speed is zero

## Explanation

The acceleration splits into a tangential component $a_t = \dfrac{dv}{dt}$, parallel to the velocity, and a centripetal component $a_n = \dfrac{v^2}{R}$, perpendicular to the velocity. If the acceleration is perpendicular to the velocity, its tangential component is zero, so at that instant $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = 0$. The acceleration then coincides with the centripetal component, which is nonzero, therefore $v$ is nonzero as well. The information concerns a single instant, so it does not allow us to say that the motion is uniform at other instants.
