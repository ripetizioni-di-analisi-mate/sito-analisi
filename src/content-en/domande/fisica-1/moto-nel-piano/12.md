A point moves with equation of motion $\vec{r}(t) = 4.0\,t\;\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,t^2\;\hat{\jmath}$, with $\vec{r}$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. What is the normal component of the acceleration at time $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $0.308\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.60\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $6.00\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.90\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Differentiating gives $\vec{v} = 4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 6.0\,t\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{a} = 6.0\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore at $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ one has $\vec{v} = (4.0;\ 12)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with $v = \sqrt{160}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 12.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The tangential component is the projection of $\vec{a}$ onto the direction of the velocity, $a_t = \dfrac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{v}}{v} = \dfrac{72}{12.6}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 5.69\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The two components are orthogonal, so by the Pythagorean theorem
$$
a_n = \sqrt{|\vec{a}|^2 - a_t^2} = \sqrt{36 - 32.4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.90\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
