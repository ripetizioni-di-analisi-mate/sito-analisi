A point moves on a circle of radius $0{,}50\,\mathrm{m}$. At a certain instant its angular velocity is $4{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and its angular acceleration is $6{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. What is the magnitude of the acceleration at that instant?

- $8{,}54\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $11{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3{,}61\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5{,}00\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The tangential acceleration is $a_t = \alpha R = 6{,}0 \cdot 0{,}50\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the centripetal acceleration is $a_n = \omega^2 R = 16 \cdot 0{,}50\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 8{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The two components are perpendicular, therefore by the Pythagorean theorem
$$
|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_n^2} = \sqrt{9{,}0 + 64}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = \sqrt{73}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 8{,}54\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The sum $a_t + a_n$ would give the magnitude of the acceleration only if the two components were parallel.
