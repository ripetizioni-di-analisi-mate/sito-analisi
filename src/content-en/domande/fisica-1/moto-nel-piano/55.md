A ball kicked from the ground reaches the highest point of its trajectory $1.2\,\mathrm{s}$ after the kick. Neglecting air resistance, what is the vertical component of the initial velocity?

- $5.89\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $23.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $11.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.06\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The vertical component of the velocity is $v_y(t) = v_{0y} - g\,t$ and it vanishes at the highest point. Hence at $t_H = 1.2\,\mathrm{s}$ we have $v_{0y} = g\,t_H$, therefore
$$
v_{0y} = 9.81 \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m/s} = 11.8\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $23.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from mistaking the rise time for the time of flight, which is twice as long.
