A stone is hurled downward, at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and inclined $30^\circ$ below the horizontal, from the edge of a $25\,\mathrm{m}$ cliff. Neglecting air resistance, at what horizontal distance from the base of the cliff does the stone hit the water?

- $24.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.02\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With the origin on the water at the base of the cliff, the initial vertical component is $v_{0y} = -10\sin 30^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} = -5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the height is $y(t) = 25 - 5.0\,t - 4.905\,t^2$, in meters. The stone hits the water when $y = 0$, and the only positive solution of $4.905\,t^2 + 5.0\,t - 25 = 0$ is
$$
t = \frac{-5.0 + \sqrt{25 + 4 \cdot 4.905 \cdot 25}}{2 \cdot 4.905}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.80\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The horizontal component is $v_{0x} = 10\cos 30^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} = 8.66\,\mathrm{m/s}$, therefore the distance is $8.66 \cdot 1.80\,\mathrm{m} = 15.6\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $24.5\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds to a throw $30^\circ$ upward.
