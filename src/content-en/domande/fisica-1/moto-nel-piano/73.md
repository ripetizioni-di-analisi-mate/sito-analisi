The angular velocity of a wheel varies with time according to $\omega(t) = 2.0\,t^3$, with $\omega$ in $\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $t$ in seconds. What is the angular acceleration at $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $24\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $16\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $12\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

The angular acceleration is the time derivative of the angular velocity, so $\alpha(t) = \dfrac{d\omega}{dt} = 6.0\,t^2$. At $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ we get
$$
\alpha = 6.0 \cdot 2.0^2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 24\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} .
$$
The value $16\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ coincides numerically with $\omega(2.0\,\mathrm{s})$, while $8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ is the ratio $\dfrac{\omega}{t}$, that is, the average angular acceleration between $0$ and $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$.
