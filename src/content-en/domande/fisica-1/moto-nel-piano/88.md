Two stones are thrown at the same instant from two different points, with different initial velocities. Neglecting air resistance, while both are in flight, how does stone $B$ move as seen from stone $A$?

- Along a parabolic arc
- In uniformly accelerated rectilinear motion with acceleration $2g$
- It stays at rest
- In uniform rectilinear motion

## Explanation

The two stones have the same acceleration $\vec{g}$, so their relative velocity $\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A$ has derivative $\vec{g} - \vec{g} = \vec{0}$ and is constant. Therefore the position of $B$ relative to $A$, $\vec{r}_B - \vec{r}_A$, changes linearly in time, and $B$ as seen from $A$ moves in **uniform rectilinear motion**, with velocity $\vec{v}_{0B} - \vec{v}_{0A}$. This result is why a projectile aimed directly at a target released at the instant of firing hits it, provided it reaches the target before the target hits the ground.
