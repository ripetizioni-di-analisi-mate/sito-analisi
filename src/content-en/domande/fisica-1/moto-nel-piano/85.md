Two ships move at constant velocities of the same magnitude, $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$: ship $A$ toward the east, ship $B$ in a direction making $60^\circ$ with east, toward the northeast. What is the magnitude of the velocity of $B$ relative to $A$?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,10,0,A;0,0,5,8.66,B" etichette="2.3,0.9,60°" xmin=-1.5 xmax=11.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=10 uguale=1 nomi="east,north" titolo="Velocities of ships $A$ and $B$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$"}

- $10.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0$
- $14.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The velocity of $B$ relative to $A$ is $\vec{v}_{B,A} = \vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A$. In components, with the $x$ axis toward the east and the $y$ axis toward the north, $\vec{v}_A = (10;\ 0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{v}_B = (5.0;\ 8.66)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so $\vec{v}_{B,A} = (-5.0;\ 8.66)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its magnitude is
$$
|\vec{v}_{B,A}| = \sqrt{5.0^2 + 8.66^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 10.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The triangle formed by $\vec{v}_A$, $\vec{v}_B$ and their difference is equilateral. The value $17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the magnitude of the sum $\vec{v}_A + \vec{v}_B$.
