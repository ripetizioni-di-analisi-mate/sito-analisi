A cyclist rides along a circular bend at constant speed. The initial data are the speed $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the radius of curvature $R = 18\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the speed to $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the radius of curvature to $27\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $a$ minus its initial value?

- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Acceleration points toward the center. Its magnitude is the square of speed divided by the radius. The relation $a = \dfrac{v^{2}}{R}$ gives $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ before the change and $12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
