A point moves in uniform circular motion and at a certain instant passes through the origin with velocity $\vec{v} = (3.0;\ 4.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and acceleration $\vec{a} = (-8.0;\ 6.0)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What are the coordinates of the center of the circle?

- $(2.0;\ -1.5)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(1.5;\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(-2.0;\ 1.5)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(-8.0;\ 6.0)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In uniform circular motion the acceleration is centripetal, that is, directed from the point toward the center, and has magnitude $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$. We have $v = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $a = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, so $R = \dfrac{25}{10}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The center lies at distance $R$ from the origin along the unit vector $\dfrac{\vec{a}}{a} = (-0.80;\ 0.60)$, therefore its coordinates are
$$
2.5 \cdot (-0.80;\ 0.60)\,\mathrm{m} = (-2.0;\ 1.5)\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The point $(1.5;\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{m}$ instead lies along the direction of the velocity, which is tangent to the circle.
