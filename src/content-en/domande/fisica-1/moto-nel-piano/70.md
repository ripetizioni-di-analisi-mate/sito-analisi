A point travels at constant speed $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ around a circle of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the magnitude of its average acceleration over a quarter turn?

- $18.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $16.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0$
- $11.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

In a quarter turn the velocity rotates by $90^\circ$ keeping the magnitude $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the change in velocity has magnitude $|\Delta\vec{v}| = \sqrt{6.0^2 + 6.0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 8.49\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The quarter turn is an arc of length $\dfrac{\pi R}{2} = 3.14\,\mathrm{m}$, traveled in $\Delta t = \dfrac{3.14}{6.0}\,\mathrm{s} = 0.524\,\mathrm{s}$. Therefore
$$
|\vec{a}_m| = \frac{|\Delta\vec{v}|}{\Delta t} = \frac{8.49}{0.524}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 16.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The value is smaller than the instantaneous acceleration $\dfrac{v^2}{R} = 18.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, because the average combines vectors with different directions.
