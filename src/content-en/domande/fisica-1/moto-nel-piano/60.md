A cyclist rides along a circular bend at constant speed. Given the radius of curvature $R = 18\,\mathrm{m}$ and the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration $a = 8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the speed $v$?

- $12 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Acceleration points toward the center. Its magnitude is the square of speed divided by the radius. In the stated units, the relation is $a = \dfrac{v^{2}}{R}$. Solving for the unknown gives $v = \sqrt{R} \cdot \sqrt{a}$. Substituting the given values yields $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
