A boat moves at $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water and must cross a river $60\,\mathrm{m}$ wide, arriving at the point of the opposite bank directly across from the starting point. The current has velocity $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ parallel to the banks. How long does the crossing take?

- $15\,\mathrm{s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{s}$
- $30\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

To arrive directly across from the starting point, the velocity relative to the bank $\vec{v} = \vec{v}\,' + \vec{V}$ must be perpendicular to the banks, therefore the boat heads partly upstream. The vectors $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{V}$ are then perpendicular and $\vec{v}\,'$ is the hypotenuse of the right triangle they form, so $v = \sqrt{v'^2 - V^2} = \sqrt{25 - 9.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The crossing time is therefore $t = \dfrac{60}{4.0}\,\mathrm{s} = 15\,\mathrm{s}$.
