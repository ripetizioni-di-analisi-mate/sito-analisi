A point starts from the origin at $t = 0$ with velocity $\vec{v}_0 = 2.0\,\hat{\jmath}$, in $\mathrm{m/s}$, and its acceleration is $\vec{a}(t) = 6.0\,t\;\hat{\imath}$, in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, with $t$ in seconds. What is the Cartesian equation of the trajectory, with $x$ and $y$ in meters?

- $x = \dfrac{y^3}{8}$
- $x = y^3$
- $x = \dfrac{y^3}{2}$
- $y = \dfrac{x^3}{8}$

## Explanation

Integrating the acceleration with $\vec{v}(0) = 2.0\,\hat{\jmath}$ gives $\vec{v}(t) = 3.0\,t^2\,\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,\hat{\jmath}$. Integrating again with $\vec{r}(0) = \vec{0}$ gives the equations of motion $x(t) = t^3$ and $y(t) = 2.0\,t$. From the second, $t = \dfrac{y}{2}$, and substituting into the first,
$$
x = \left(\frac{y}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{y^3}{8} .
$$
The equation $x = y^3$ comes from substituting $t = y$, that is, from forgetting the factor $2.0$ in the equation for $y$.
