Because of the Earth's rotation about its own axis, a point on the equator moves on a circle of radius $6.37 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m}$ with period $24.0\,\mathrm{h}$. What is its centripetal acceleration?

- $4.37 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8.53 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.37 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5.36 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The motion of the point is uniform circular motion with period $T = 24.0\,\mathrm{h} = 8.64 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{s}$, so the angular velocity is $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T} = 7.27 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. By the formula for the **centripetal acceleration** we therefore have
$$
a_c = \omega^2 R = \frac{4\pi^2 R}{T^2} = \frac{4\pi^2 \cdot 6.37 \cdot 10^6}{(8.64 \cdot 10^4)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.37 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The value $4.37 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is obtained by leaving the period in hours. The centripetal acceleration at the equator is about $0.3\%$ of $g$.
