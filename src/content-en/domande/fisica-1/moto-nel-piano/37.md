What angle do the velocity and the acceleration make at $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$, for a particle located at $\vec{r}(t) = 3.0\,t\;\hat{\imath} + t^3\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $\vec{r}$ in meters and $t$ in seconds?

- $90^\circ$
- $45^\circ$
- $0^\circ$
- $63.4^\circ$

## Explanation

Differentiating twice gives $\vec{v}(t) = 3.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3t^2\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{a}(t) = 6t\,\hat{\jmath}$. At $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ we have $\vec{v} = (3.0;\ 3.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{a} = (0;\ 6.0)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. From the dot product,
$$
\cos\varphi = \frac{\vec{v}\cdot\vec{a}}{v\,a} = \frac{18}{\sqrt{18}\cdot 6.0} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} ,
$$
so the angle is $\varphi = 45^\circ$. Since $\varphi$ is acute, at that instant the speed is increasing.
