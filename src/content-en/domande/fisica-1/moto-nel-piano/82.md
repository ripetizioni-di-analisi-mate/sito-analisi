A target moves in a plane with initial velocity along the $x$ axis and constant acceleration along the $y$ axis. The initial velocity and acceleration point in the positive directions of their respective axes. Given the vertical acceleration $a = 6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the vertical displacement $y = 27\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the elapsed time $t$?

- $6\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\dfrac{3 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The initial vertical velocity is zero, so only the quadratic term of the $y$ position equation contributes. In the stated units, the relation is $y = \dfrac{a \cdot t^{2}}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $t = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{a}}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{s}$.
