Two trains travel on parallel tracks in opposite directions, one at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the other at $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the ground. The second train is $180\,\mathrm{m}$ long. For how long does a passenger seated in the first train see the second one passing by the window?

- $36\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $7.2\,\mathrm{s}$
- $9.0\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

In the frame of the first train, which translates relative to the ground, the velocity of the second train is the relative velocity $\vec{v}_{2,1} = \vec{v}_2 - \vec{v}_1$. The two trains move in opposite directions, so the relative velocity has magnitude $25\,\mathrm{m/s} + 20\,\mathrm{m/s} = 45\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The passenger sees the entire length of the second train go by, therefore
$$
t = \frac{180\,\mathrm{m}}{45\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $36\,\mathrm{s}$ comes from subtracting the speeds, which is correct only for trains moving in the same direction.
