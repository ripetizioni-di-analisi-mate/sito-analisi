Frame $S'$ translates relative to frame $S$ with constant velocity $\vec{V} = 3.0\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s}$, and the two origins coincide at $t = 0$. At that instant a point leaves the origin of $S'$ and moves in $S'$ with constant velocity $\vec{v}\,' = 2.0\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s}$. How far from the origin of $S$ is it at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $12.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $14.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In $S'$ the position of the point is $\vec{r}\,' = 2.0\,t\;\hat{\jmath}$, so at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ we have $\vec{r}\,' = 8.0\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m}$. By the Galilean transformations $\vec{r} = \vec{r}\,' + \vec{V}\,t$, therefore in $S$ the position is $\vec{r} = (12;\ 8.0)\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance from the origin of $S$ is
$$
|\vec{r}| = \sqrt{12^2 + 8.0^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 14.4\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from adding the distances traveled along the two axes, which are perpendicular.
