A swimmer crosses a river swimming perpendicular to the banks with velocity $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water; the current has velocity $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ parallel to the banks. What is the magnitude of the swimmer's velocity relative to the bank?

- $1.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By **velocity addition** the velocity relative to the bank is $\vec{v} = \vec{v}\,' + \vec{V}$, the sum of the velocity relative to the water and the velocity of the current. The two vectors are perpendicular, therefore the magnitude is obtained with the Pythagorean theorem,
$$
v = \sqrt{1.2^2 + 0.50^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{1.69}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.3\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The magnitude of the sum equals the sum of the magnitudes only for parallel vectors with the same direction.
