A stone is thrown from the ground with initial velocity of components $v_{0x} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_{0y} = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting air resistance, what is the magnitude of the velocity when the stone passes through the height of $10\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $7.33\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $21.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.37\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The height of $10\,\mathrm{m}$ can be reached, because the maximum height is $\dfrac{15^2}{2 \cdot 9.81}\,\mathrm{m} = 11.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The square of the magnitude of the initial velocity is $v_0^2 = 5.0^2 + 15^2 = 250\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, therefore by the **speed as a function of height**
$$
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 - 2g\,(y - y_0)} = \sqrt{250 - 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 10}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{53.8}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 7.33\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The stone passes through that height twice, on the way up and on the way down, and in both passages the velocity has this magnitude.
