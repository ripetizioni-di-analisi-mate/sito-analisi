A projectile is launched over level ground at a fixed angle $\theta$. Neglecting air resistance, how does the range change if the launch speed is doubled while the angle is left unchanged?

- It doubles
- It stays the same
- It becomes $\sqrt{2}$ times larger
- It becomes four times larger

## Explanation

The range is $R = \dfrac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$, therefore for a given angle it is proportional to the square of $v_0$. Doubling $v_0$ the square becomes $4\,v_0^2$, so the range **quadruples**. The same result is obtained by observing that both the horizontal component $v_0\cos\theta$ and the time of flight $T = \dfrac{2\,v_0\sin\theta}{g}$ double, and the range is their product.
