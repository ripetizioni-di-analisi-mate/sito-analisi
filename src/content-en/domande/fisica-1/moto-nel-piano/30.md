A railroad car moves along a straight horizontal track and increases its speed with constant acceleration $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ relative to the track. A passenger drops a coin. Neglecting air resistance, what is the magnitude of the acceleration of the coin measured in the frame of the car?

- $9{,}81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $11{,}8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7{,}81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

After it is released, the coin has, in the frame of the track, acceleration $\vec{g}$ pointing downward. The car translates with variable velocity $\vec{V}(t)$, therefore the accelerations measured in the two frames differ by the acceleration of the moving frame, $\vec{a} = \vec{a}\,' + \dfrac{d\vec{V}}{dt}$. With the $x$ axis in the direction of motion of the car and the $y$ axis upward one thus has, in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$,
$$
\vec{a}\,' = \vec{a} - \frac{d\vec{V}}{dt} = -9{,}81\,\hat{\jmath} - 2{,}0\,\hat{\imath} .
$$
The two components are perpendicular, therefore by the Pythagorean theorem $|\vec{a}\,'| = \sqrt{2{,}0^2 + 9{,}81^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 10{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The value $9{,}81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is the one measured in the car when it travels at constant velocity.
