A river $40\,\mathrm{m}$ wide has a current of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and a swimmer moves at $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water. The swimmer chooses the heading so as to be carried downstream as little as possible. How far downstream does the swimmer drift during the crossing?

- $69.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $80.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $46.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0$

## Explanation

If the swimmer heads upstream at an angle $\varphi$ from the perpendicular to the banks, the components of the velocity relative to the bank are $1.0\cos\varphi$ across the river and $2.0 - 1.0\sin\varphi$ downstream. The downstream drift is
$$
d(\varphi) = 40\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \frac{2.0 - \sin\varphi}{\cos\varphi} ,
$$
whose derivative vanishes for $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{1.0}{2.0}$, that is, for $\varphi = 30^\circ$. Hence the minimum drift is $d = 40 \cdot \dfrac{1.5}{0.866}\,\mathrm{m} = 69.3\,\mathrm{m}$. Swimming perpendicular to the banks the drift would be $80.0\,\mathrm{m}$, and since the current is faster than the swimmer no heading leads to the point directly opposite.
