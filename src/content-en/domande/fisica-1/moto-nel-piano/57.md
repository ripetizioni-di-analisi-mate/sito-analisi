A particle moves along a circle at changing speed. At the point under study the magnitudes of both tangential and normal acceleration are positive. The initial data are the tangential acceleration magnitude $a_{t} = 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the normal acceleration magnitude $a_{n} = 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Change the tangential acceleration magnitude to $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $a$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{5 \cdot \sqrt{13}}{26}$
- $\dfrac{10}{7}$
- $\dfrac{2 \cdot \sqrt{13}}{5}$
- $2$

## Explanation

The tangent and normal are perpendicular, so the two components are combined in quadrature. The relation $a = \sqrt{a_{n}^{2} + a_{t}^{2}}$ gives $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ before the change and $2 \cdot \sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{2 \cdot \sqrt{13}}{5}$.
