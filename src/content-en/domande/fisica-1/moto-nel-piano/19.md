A point has constant acceleration $\vec{a} = 2{,}0\,\hat{\imath} - 1{,}0\,\hat{\jmath}$, in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and at time $t = 0$ it has velocity $\vec{v}_0 = 3{,}0\,\hat{\imath} + 4{,}0\,\hat{\jmath}$, in $\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of the velocity at time $t = 3{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $12{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $11{,}4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9{,}06\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6{,}71\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

With constant acceleration $\vec{v}(t) = \vec{v}_0 + \vec{a}\,t$ holds, which in components gives $v_x = (3{,}0 + 2{,}0 \cdot 3{,}0)\,\mathrm{m/s} = 9{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_y = (4{,}0 - 1{,}0 \cdot 3{,}0)\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The magnitude is therefore $v = \sqrt{81 + 1}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{82}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 9{,}06\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ appears only in the position equation, $\vec{r} = \vec{r}_0 + \vec{v}_0\,t + \dfrac{1}{2}\,\vec{a}\,t^2$.
