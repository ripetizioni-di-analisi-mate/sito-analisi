A point starts from the origin with velocity $\vec{v}_0 = 4.0\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ and moves with constant acceleration $\vec{a} = 2.0\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. How far from the origin is it at time $t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $22\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Along the $x$ axis the motion is uniform, because $a_x = 0$, therefore $x = v_{0x}\,t = 12\,\mathrm{m}$. Along the $y$ axis the motion is uniformly accelerated with zero initial velocity, therefore $y = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a_y\,t^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 9.0\,\mathrm{m} = 9.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance from the origin is the magnitude of the position vector, so $|\vec{r}| = \sqrt{12^2 + 9.0^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 15\,\mathrm{m}$.
