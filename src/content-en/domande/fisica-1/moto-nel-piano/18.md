A point moves with equations of motion $x(t) = t^3 - 12\,t$ and $y(t) = 5{,}0\,t$, with $x$ and $y$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. At what time $t > 0$ is the velocity parallel to the $y$ axis?

- $3{,}5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The velocity is parallel to the $y$ axis when its component $v_x$ is zero, since $v_y = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ never vanishes. Differentiating gives $v_x = 3t^2 - 12$, which vanishes for $t^2 = 4$. Therefore the positive time sought is $t = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$. The time $t = \sqrt{12}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 3{,}5\,\mathrm{s}$ is the one at which the coordinate $x$ vanishes, that is, the one at which the point crosses the $y$ axis.
