The valve of a wheel of radius $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rolling without slipping traces a cycloid, with position function $\vec{r}(t) = (t - \sin t)\,\hat{\imath} + (1 - \cos t)\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $\vec{r}$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. What is the radius of curvature of the trajectory at the highest point, reached at $t = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$?

::grafico{curve="t-sin(t),1-cos(t),0,6.2832" punti="3.1416,2,pieno,P" xmin=-0.6 xmax=7 ymin=-0.5 ymax=2.8 uguale=1 titolo="One arch of a cycloid, with lengths in meters; $P$ is the highest point"}

- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Differentiating gives $\vec{v}(t) = (1 - \cos t)\,\hat{\imath} + \sin t\;\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{a}(t) = \sin t\;\hat{\imath} + \cos t\;\hat{\jmath}$. At $t = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$ we have $\vec{v} = 2.0\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{a} = -1.0\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, so the acceleration is perpendicular to the velocity and entirely normal, $a_n = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Therefore
$$
\rho = \frac{v^2}{a_n} = \frac{2.0^2}{1.0}\,\mathrm{m} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The radius of curvature at the highest point is thus twice the height of the cycloid, and four times the radius $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ of the wheel that generates it.
