A particle moves along a circle at changing speed. At the point under study the magnitudes of both tangential and normal acceleration are positive. The initial data are the tangential acceleration magnitude $a_{t} = 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the normal acceleration magnitude $a_{n} = 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Change the tangential acceleration magnitude to $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the normal acceleration magnitude to $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $a$ minus its initial value?

- $-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The tangent and normal are perpendicular, so the two components are combined in quadrature. The relation $a = \sqrt{a_{n}^{2} + a_{t}^{2}}$ gives $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ before the change and $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
