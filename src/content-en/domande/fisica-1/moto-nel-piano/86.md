A projectile is launched from the ground with initial velocity components $v_{0x} > 0$ and $v_{0y} > 0$. A cart moves on the level ground with constant velocity $v_{0x}\,\hat{\imath}$, and at the instant of launch it passes through the launch point. Neglecting air resistance, what trajectory does the projectile follow in the frame of the cart?

- A parabolic arc identical to the one seen from the ground
- A vertical segment, traveled upward and then downward
- A horizontal segment
- An oblique segment, inclined like the launch velocity

## Explanation

The cart translates with constant velocity $\vec{V} = v_{0x}\,\hat{\imath}$, so by the composition of velocities the velocity of the projectile in its frame is $\vec{v}\,' = \vec{v} - \vec{V}$. The horizontal component of $\vec{v}$ stays $v_{0x}$, therefore $v'_x = 0$ at every instant, while $v'_y = v_{0y} - g\,t$. In the frame of the cart the projectile thus moves like a body thrown vertically with speed $v_{0y}$, along a **vertical segment** traveled upward and then downward, and falls back onto the cart.
