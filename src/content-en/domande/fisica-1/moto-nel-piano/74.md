A point starts from rest on a circle and moves with constant, nonzero angular acceleration. What is the direction of its acceleration at the instant it starts?

- It points toward the center
- It is tangent to the circle
- It is zero
- It makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the radius

## Explanation

The acceleration has the tangential component $a_t = \alpha R$ and the centripetal component $a_n = \omega^2 R$. At the instant it starts the point is at rest, so $\omega = 0$ and $a_n = 0$. The tangential component is instead $\alpha R \neq 0$, therefore at that instant the acceleration is **tangent to the circle**. Immediately afterwards $\omega$ grows, and the centripetal component appears as well.
