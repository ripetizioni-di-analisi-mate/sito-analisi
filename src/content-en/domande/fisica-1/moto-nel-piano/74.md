A point moves along a circle with constant angular acceleration, starting from rest. Given the trajectory radius $R = 4\,\mathrm{m}$ and the tangential acceleration magnitude $a_{t} = 12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the angular acceleration $\alpha$?

- $6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

Tangential speed is $R\omega$. Because $R$ is fixed, its time derivative is $R\alpha$. In the stated units, the relation is $a_{t} = R \cdot \alpha$. Solving for the unknown gives $\alpha = \dfrac{a_{t}}{R}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$.
