An airplane flies horizontally at constant velocity and releases a package. Neglecting air resistance, where is the package relative to the airplane during the fall?

- Farther and farther behind the airplane
- Farther and farther ahead of the airplane
- Always directly below the airplane
- Always directly below the release point

## Explanation

At the moment of release the package has the same horizontal velocity as the airplane. Since air resistance is negligible, the only force acting on the package is its weight, so the horizontal component of its velocity stays constant. Hence the airplane and the package cover the same horizontal distance at every instant, and the package is **always directly below the airplane**, while vertically it falls with uniformly accelerated motion. Relative to the ground the package follows a parabolic arc and hits the ground far from the point directly below the release point.
