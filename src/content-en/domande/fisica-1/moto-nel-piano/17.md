A car travels around a circular curve with the speedometer steady at $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Which statement about its acceleration is true?

- It points toward the center of the curve
- It is zero, because the speed is constant
- It is tangent to the trajectory
- It points toward the outside of the curve

## Explanation

The speedometer measures the magnitude of the velocity, which is constant, therefore the tangential acceleration $a_t = \dfrac{dv}{dt}$ is zero. The direction of the velocity changes along the curve, so the acceleration is nonzero. In uniform circular motion the acceleration is the **centripetal acceleration**, directed toward the center of the curve and with magnitude $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$.
