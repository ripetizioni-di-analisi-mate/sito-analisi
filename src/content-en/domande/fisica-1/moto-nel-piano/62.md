A cyclist rides along a circular bend at constant speed. The initial data are the speed $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the radius of curvature $R = 18\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the radius of curvature to $9\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $a$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $1$
- $4$

## Explanation

Acceleration points toward the center. Its magnitude is the square of speed divided by the radius. The relation $a = \dfrac{v^{2}}{R}$ gives $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ before the change and $16\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
