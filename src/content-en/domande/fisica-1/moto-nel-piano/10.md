The shaft of an engine goes from $600$ to $1500$ revolutions per minute in $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$, with constant angular acceleration. What is the angular acceleration?

- $2.50\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $26.2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $94.2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

One revolution per minute corresponds to $\dfrac{2\pi}{60}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, therefore the initial angular velocity is $\omega_0 = 20\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the final one is $\omega = 50\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Since the angular acceleration is constant, $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha\,t$ holds, so
$$
\alpha = \frac{\omega - \omega_0}{t} = \frac{30\pi}{6.0}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 5\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} .
$$
