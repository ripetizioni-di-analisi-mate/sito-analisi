A disk starts from rest and rotates with constant angular acceleration $4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. What is the centripetal acceleration of a point $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis at time $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $1.60\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $12.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.512\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $320\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The disk starts from rest, therefore at time $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the angular velocity is $\omega = \alpha\,t = 8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The **centripetal acceleration** depends only on $\omega$ and on the radius, and equals
$$
a_n = \omega^2 R = 8.0^2 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 12.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The speed of the point is $v = \omega R = 1.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and indeed $\dfrac{v^2}{R} = \dfrac{1.60^2}{0.20}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 12.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
