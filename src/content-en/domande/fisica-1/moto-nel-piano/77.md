A point moves on a circle of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ with angular position $\theta(t) = 4.0\,t - t^2$, with $\theta$ in radians and $t$ in seconds. What is the magnitude of the acceleration at the instant the point stops?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The angular velocity is $\omega(t) = 4.0 - 2.0\,t$, which vanishes at $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and the angular acceleration is $\alpha = -2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ at every instant. At the instant the point stops $\omega = 0$, so the centripetal component $\omega^2 R$ is zero. The tangential component is instead $\alpha R = -4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, therefore the magnitude of the acceleration is $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The point has zero velocity but nonzero acceleration, and indeed immediately afterwards it starts moving in the opposite direction.
