A point $P$ moves counterclockwise around a circle centered at the origin with radius $R = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, at constant speed. At a certain instant it is at $(0;\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the direction of its velocity at that instant?

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,2,pieno,P;0,0,pieno" xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.9 uguale=1 titolo="Circle of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ traveled counterclockwise"}

- That of the unit vector $-\hat{\imath}$
- That of the unit vector $+\hat{\imath}$
- That of the unit vector $-\hat{\jmath}$
- That of the unit vector $+\hat{\jmath}$

## Explanation

The velocity is tangent to the trajectory, so at $P$ it is perpendicular to the radius, which is vertical there; therefore the velocity is horizontal. Moving counterclockwise from the top of the circle, the point moves toward negative $x$, so the velocity points along $-\hat{\imath}$. The same result follows from $\vec{v} = -\omega R\sin\theta\,\hat{\imath} + \omega R\cos\theta\,\hat{\jmath}$ with $\theta = 90^\circ$ and $\omega > 0$. The unit vector $-\hat{\jmath}$ instead gives the direction of the centripetal acceleration.
