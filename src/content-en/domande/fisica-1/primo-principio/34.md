A gas absorbs $1.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of heat and its internal energy increases by $0.40\,\mathrm{kJ}$. What is the work done by the gas?

- $0.80\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-0.80\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-1.6\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

From the **first law** $\Delta U = Q - L$ one obtains $L = Q - \Delta U$. Therefore $L = 1.2\,\mathrm{kJ} - 0.40\,\mathrm{kJ} = 0.80\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The work is positive, hence the gas expands and does work on the surroundings. Of the $1.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$ absorbed, $0.40\,\mathrm{kJ}$ go into internal energy and $0.80\,\mathrm{kJ}$ into work. The value $-0.80\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is the work done on the gas by the surroundings, and $1.6\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is obtained by adding $Q$ and $\Delta U$ instead of subtracting them.
