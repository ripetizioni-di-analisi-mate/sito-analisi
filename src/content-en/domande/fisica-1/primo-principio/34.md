A gas expands and, during the expansion, gives off heat to the surroundings. With the convention $\Delta U = Q - L$, where $Q$ is the heat absorbed by the gas and $L$ the work done by the gas, which statement about the signs is true?

- $L < 0$, $Q < 0$ and $\Delta U < 0$
- $L > 0$, $Q < 0$ and $\Delta U > 0$
- $L > 0$, $Q < 0$ and $\Delta U < 0$
- $L > 0$, $Q < 0$ and the sign of $\Delta U$ depends on the values of $Q$ and $L$

## Explanation

The gas expands, so $dV > 0$ and the work done by the gas is positive, $L > 0$. The gas gives off heat, hence the heat absorbed is negative, $Q < 0$. Then $\Delta U = Q - L$ is the difference between a negative number and a positive number, and it is **negative** whatever the values. The internal energy decreases, because the gas loses energy both as heat and as work.
