An ideal gas occupies $2.00\,\mathrm{L}$ at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and is heated at constant pressure. By how much does its volume increase for each degree Celsius of temperature increase?

- $3.66\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $7.32\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $20.0\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{cm^3}$

## Explanation

By **Gay-Lussac's first law**, at constant pressure $V = V_0\,(1 + \alpha_0\,t_C)$, where $V_0$ is the volume at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $\alpha_0 = \dfrac{1}{273.15}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ is the same for all gases. Hence the volume is a linear function of $t_C$, and each increase of one degree corresponds to the volume increase
$$
\Delta V = \alpha_0\,V_0 = \frac{2.00 \cdot 10^3}{273.15}\,\mathrm{cm^3} \approx 7.32\,\mathrm{cm^3} .
$$
The value $3.66\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ is the increase for a single liter of gas, and $20.0\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ is obtained by assuming that the volume increases by $1\,\%$ per degree.
