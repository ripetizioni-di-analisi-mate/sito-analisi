An ideal gas goes around a cycle made of an isothermal expansion $A \to B$, in which it absorbs $600\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat, an isobaric compression $B \to C$, in which it gives off $500\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat and receives $200\,\mathrm{J}$ of work, and an isochoric process $C \to A$. How much heat does the gas exchange in the process $C \to A$?

- It gives off $100\,\mathrm{J}$
- It absorbs $300\,\mathrm{J}$
- It absorbs $500\,\mathrm{J}$
- It gives off $300\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

On the isotherm $\Delta U_{AB} = 0$, because the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature. On the isobar $Q_{BC} = -500\,\mathrm{J}$ and $L_{BC} = -200\,\mathrm{J}$, so $\Delta U_{BC} = Q_{BC} - L_{BC} = -300\,\mathrm{J}$. In the cycle the sum of the changes in internal energy is zero, hence $\Delta U_{CA} = +300\,\mathrm{J}$. The isochore has zero work, so $Q_{CA} = \Delta U_{CA}$ and the gas **absorbs** $300\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $-100\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by setting the sum of the heats equal to zero, whereas it equals the work of the cycle.
