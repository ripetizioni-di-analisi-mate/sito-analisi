In an ideal calorimeter one places $0{,}10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $0{,}20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ ($c_a = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, $\lambda_f = 3{,}33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$). What is the final state?

- The final temperature is $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and about $50\,\mathrm{g}$ of ice melt
- The final temperature is $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and all the ice melts
- All the ice melts and the final temperature is about $13\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- The final temperature is about $-13\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

By cooling down to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, the water can release at most $Q_{\text{disp}} = 0{,}20 \cdot 4186 \cdot 20\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1{,}67 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. Melting all the ice, instead, requires $Q_{\text{fus}} = 0{,}10 \cdot 3{,}33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} = 3{,}33 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. Since $Q_{\text{disp}} < Q_{\text{fus}}$, the ice melts only partially and the final temperature is $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The melted mass is $\dfrac{1{,}67 \cdot 10^4}{3{,}33 \cdot 10^5}\,\mathrm{kg} \approx 0{,}050\,\mathrm{kg}$, that is, about $50\,\mathrm{g}$. The value $-13\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is obtained by writing the balance with complete melting without first comparing the two amounts of heat, and $13\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ by ignoring the latent heat.
