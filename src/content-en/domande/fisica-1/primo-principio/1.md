At what temperature, expressed in kelvins, do a Celsius thermometer and a Fahrenheit thermometer read the same number?

- $313{,}15\,\mathrm{K}$
- $233{,}15\,\mathrm{K}$
- $345{,}15\,\mathrm{K}$
- $273{,}15\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

The two scales are related by $t_F = \dfrac{9}{5}\,t_C + 32$. The two thermometers read the same number $t$ when $t = \dfrac{9}{5}\,t + 32$, that is, when $-\dfrac{4}{5}\,t = 32$, therefore $t = -40$. Hence the required temperature is $-40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, which in kelvins is $T = -40 + 273{,}15 = 233{,}15\,\mathrm{K}$. The value $313{,}15\,\mathrm{K}$ corresponds to $+40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and $345{,}15\,\mathrm{K}$ is obtained by swapping $\dfrac{9}{5}$ and $\dfrac{5}{9}$ in the conversion formula.
