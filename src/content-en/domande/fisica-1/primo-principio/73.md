A gas expands quasi-statically from a volume of $1.0\,\mathrm{L}$ to a volume of $2.0\,\mathrm{L}$ along a process in which the product $pV^2$ stays constant. The initial pressure is $4.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. How much work does the gas do?

- $200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $277\,\mathrm{J}$
- $400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $100\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Along the process $p = \dfrac{p_i V_i^2}{V^2}$, so the work is
$$
L = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{p_i V_i^2}{V^2}\,dV = p_i V_i^2 \left(\frac{1}{V_i} - \frac{1}{V_f}\right) = p_i V_i \left(1 - \frac{V_i}{V_f}\right) .
$$
With $p_i V_i = 4.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 400\,\mathrm{J}$ and $\dfrac{V_i}{V_f} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ one gets $L = 200\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $277\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work of an isotherm between the same volumes, which in the $(V, p)$ plane lies above this curve because along the isotherm the pressure decreases more slowly.
