An unconstrained bar of uniform material is heated by $50\,\mathrm{K}$. The linear approximation applies. The initial data are the linear expansion coefficient $a = 12\,\mathrm{\mu m/(m\,K)}$ and the initial length $L = 4\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the linear expansion coefficient to $16\,\mathrm{\mu m/(m\,K)}$ and the initial length to $5\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $d$ minus its initial value?

- $\dfrac{8}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $1600\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $\dfrac{16}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $- \dfrac{8}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The extension is $\alpha L\Delta T$. With the coefficient in micrometers per meter per kelvin, divide the result by a thousand to obtain millimeters. The relation $d = \dfrac{L \cdot a}{20}$ gives $\dfrac{12}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$ before the change and $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $\dfrac{8}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$.
