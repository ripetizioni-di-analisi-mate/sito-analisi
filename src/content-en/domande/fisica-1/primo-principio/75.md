A rigid container holds $1.0\,\mathrm{mol}$ of helium and $1.0\,\mathrm{mol}$ of nitrogen, both treated as ideal gases. How much heat is needed to raise the temperature of the mixture by $10\,\mathrm{K}$? ($R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\cdot K)}$)

- $249\,\mathrm{J}$
- $416\,\mathrm{J}$
- $333\,\mathrm{J}$
- $499\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The container is rigid, so the work is zero and the heat absorbed equals the change in internal energy. The internal energy of the mixture is the sum of those of the two gases, with $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$ for helium, which is monatomic, and $c_V = \dfrac{5}{2}R$ for nitrogen, which is diatomic. Therefore
$$
Q = \left(\frac{3}{2} + \frac{5}{2}\right) R\,\Delta T = 4 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{J} \approx 333\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $499\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained with the molar heat capacities at constant pressure, which apply only when the pressure stays constant.
