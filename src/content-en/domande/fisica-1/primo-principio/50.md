Mercury has a density of $13.60 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and a coefficient of volume expansion $\beta = 1.82 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. How does its density change when it is heated to $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

- It increases by about $2.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- It decreases by about $2.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- It decreases by about $82\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- It decreases by about $2.5\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

The mass of the mercury does not change, while the volume becomes $V = V_0\,(1 + \beta\,\Delta T)$, so the density becomes $\rho = \dfrac{\rho_0}{1 + \beta\,\Delta T}$ and decreases. With $\beta\,\Delta T = 1.82 \cdot 10^{-2}$ one gets
$$
\rho = \frac{13.60 \cdot 10^3}{1.0182}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \approx 13.36 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} .
$$
Therefore the density decreases by about $2.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The value $82\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is obtained by using $\dfrac{\beta}{3}$, that is a linear coefficient, instead of the volume coefficient.
