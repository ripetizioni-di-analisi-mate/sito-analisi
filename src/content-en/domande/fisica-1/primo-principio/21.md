A calorimeter has a water equivalent of $100\,\mathrm{g}$ and contains $100\,\mathrm{g}$ of water at $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, in equilibrium with the container. $300\,\mathrm{g}$ of water at $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ are added. What is the equilibrium temperature?

- $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $42\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $34\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

In the heat balance the calorimeter appears as a mass of water of $100\,\mathrm{g}$ at the initial temperature of the liquid it contains, that is, $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Hence there are $200\,\mathrm{g}$ of equivalent water in all at $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and $300\,\mathrm{g}$ at $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The specific heat is the same in all the terms and cancels, therefore
$$
T_e = \frac{200 \cdot 10 + 300 \cdot 50}{200 + 300}\,^\circ\mathrm{C} = \frac{17\,000}{500}\,^\circ\mathrm{C} = 34\,^\circ\mathrm{C} .
$$
The value $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is obtained by ignoring the water equivalent, and $42\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ by assigning to the calorimeter the temperature of the added water.
