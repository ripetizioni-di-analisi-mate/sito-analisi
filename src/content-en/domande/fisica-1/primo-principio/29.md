An ideal gas is heated at constant pressure from $300\,\mathrm{K}$ to $600\,\mathrm{K}$. How does its density change?

- It is halved
- It doubles
- It stays the same
- It drops to one quarter

## Explanation

At constant pressure **Charles's law** $\dfrac{V}{T} = \text{costante}$ holds, therefore the volume doubles together with the absolute temperature. The mass of the gas does not change, and the density is the ratio of the mass to the volume. Hence the density **is halved**.
