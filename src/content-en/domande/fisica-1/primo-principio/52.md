A bucket contains $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water and a cup contains $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$, both at the temperature of $60\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Which statement is true?

- The bucket contains more heat than the cup, because it has a larger mass
- If the bucket and the cup are put in contact, heat flows from the bucket to the cup
- Cooling down to $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, the bucket and the cup give off the same heat, because the temperature change is the same
- Cooling down to $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, the bucket gives off $50$ times the heat given off by the cup

## Explanation

By the law of calorimetry the heat given off on cooling is $m\,c_w\,|\Delta T|$, and the specific heat and the temperature change are the same for the bucket and for the cup. Hence the ratio of the heats given off equals the ratio of the masses, $\dfrac{10}{0.20} = 50$. Heat is the energy that passes from one body to another because of a temperature difference, so a body possesses internal energy and exchanges heat. The bucket and the cup have the same temperature, hence they are in thermal equilibrium and in contact they exchange no heat.
