A gas goes from state $A$ to state $B$ in two ways. In the first process it absorbs $500\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat and does $200\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. In the second it does $350\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. How much heat does it absorb in the second process?

- $500\,\mathrm{J}$
- $150\,\mathrm{J}$
- $650\,\mathrm{J}$
- $850\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

From the first process, by the first law, $\Delta U = Q_1 - L_1 = (500 - 200)\,\mathrm{J} = 300\,\mathrm{J}$. Internal energy is a state function, so in the second process too $\Delta U = 300\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore $Q_2 = \Delta U + L_2 = 300\,\mathrm{J} + 350\,\mathrm{J} = 650\,\mathrm{J}$. The heat changes with the process, while $\Delta U$ stays the same.
