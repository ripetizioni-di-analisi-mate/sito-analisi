$900\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat are supplied to an aluminum block of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ ($c = 900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$) and the same amount to an iron block of mass $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ ($c = 450\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$). Which statement is true?

- The temperature of the aluminum rises twice as much as that of the iron
- The temperature of the iron rises twice as much as that of the aluminum
- The temperatures of the two blocks both rise by $5.0\,\mathrm{K}$
- The temperature of the iron rises four times as much as that of the aluminum

## Explanation

For a given absorbed heat the temperature change is $\Delta T = \dfrac{Q}{C}$, where $C = m\,c$ is the heat capacity. For the aluminum $C = 0.20 \cdot 900\,\mathrm{J/K} = 180\,\mathrm{J/K}$, and for the iron $C = 0.40 \cdot 450\,\mathrm{J/K} = 180\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The heat capacities are equal, therefore the two temperatures rise by the same amount, $\Delta T = \dfrac{900}{180}\,\mathrm{K} = 5.0\,\mathrm{K}$. Iron has half the specific heat and twice the mass, and the two factors compensate each other.
