The label of a snack states an energy content of $250\,\mathrm{kcal}$, where $1\,\mathrm{kcal} = 1000\,\mathrm{cal}$. What is this energy in joules?

- $1.05 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $5.97 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.50 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.05 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

One kilocalorie is $1000\,\mathrm{cal}$, so $250\,\mathrm{kcal} = 2.50 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{cal}$. Since $1\,\mathrm{cal} = 4.186\,\mathrm{J}$, the energy in joules is obtained by multiplying by $4.186$:
$$
E = 2.50 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 4.186\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.05 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $5.97 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by dividing by $4.186$ instead of multiplying, and $1.05 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ by treating kilocalories as calories.
