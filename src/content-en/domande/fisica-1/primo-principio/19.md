How much heat is needed to heat $0{,}50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of copper ($c = 385\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$) from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

- $19{,}3\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $71{,}8\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $30{,}8\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $15{,}4\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

The copper does not change state, therefore the heat follows from the law of calorimetry $Q = m\,c\,\Delta T$. The temperature change is $\Delta T = 80\,\mathrm{K}$, hence
$$
Q = 0{,}50 \cdot 385 \cdot 80\,\mathrm{J} = 1{,}54 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} = 15{,}4\,\mathrm{kJ} .
$$
The value $19{,}3\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is obtained by using the final temperature instead of the temperature change, $71{,}8\,\mathrm{kJ}$ by using the final temperature in kelvins, and $30{,}8\,\mathrm{kJ}$ by ignoring the mass.
