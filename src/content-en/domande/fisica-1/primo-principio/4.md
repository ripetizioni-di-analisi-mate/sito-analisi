An electric kettle with a power of $2000\,\mathrm{W}$ heats $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water from $15\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and all the electrical energy is absorbed by the water ($c_a = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$). How long does it take?

- $314\,\mathrm{s}$
- $178\,\mathrm{s}$
- $267\,\mathrm{s}$
- $361\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The heat absorbed by the water is $Q = m\,c_a\,\Delta T$ with $\Delta T = 85\,\mathrm{K}$, therefore $Q = 1.5 \cdot 4186 \cdot 85\,\mathrm{J} \approx 5.34 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$. The kettle supplies $2000\,\mathrm{J}$ every second, hence the time is
$$
\Delta t = \frac{Q}{P} = \frac{5.34 \cdot 10^5}{2000}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 267\,\mathrm{s} ,
$$
that is, about $4.4$ minutes. The value $314\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained with $\Delta T = 100\,\mathrm{K}$, that is, starting from $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and $178\,\mathrm{s}$ by ignoring the mass of the water.
