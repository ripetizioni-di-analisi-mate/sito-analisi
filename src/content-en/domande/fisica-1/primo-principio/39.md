A rigid container holds $0.50\,\mathrm{mol}$ of a monatomic ideal gas, which is heated from $300\,\mathrm{K}$ to $400\,\mathrm{K}$. How much heat does the gas absorb?

- $1.04 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $416\,\mathrm{J}$
- $624\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.25 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The volume is constant, therefore the work is zero and by the first law $Q = \Delta U = n\,c_V\,\Delta T$, as in every **isochoric process**. For a monatomic gas $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$, hence
$$
Q = 0.50 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 100\,\mathrm{J} \approx 624\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $1.04 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained with $c_p = \dfrac{5}{2}R$, which refers to processes at constant pressure, and $1.25 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ by ignoring the number of moles.
