A gas goes quasi-statically from state $A$ to state $D$ along the path $ABCD$ in the figure, made of an isobaric expansion from $A$ to $B$, an isochoric cooling from $B$ to $C$ and an isobaric compression from $C$ to $D$. With $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, how much work does the gas do along the whole path?

::grafico{vettori="1,3,4,3;4,3,4,1;4,1,2,1" punti="1,3,pieno,A;4,3,pieno,B;4,1,pieno,C;2,1,pieno,D" segmenti="1,0,1,3;2,0,2,1;4,0,4,1;0,3,1,3;0,1,2,1" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=4 nomi="V,p" titolo="Path $ABCD$ with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$"}

- $900\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1100\,\mathrm{J}$
- $200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $700\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Along the isobar $AB$ the gas expands by $3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ at the pressure $3 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, so $L_{AB} = 3 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 900\,\mathrm{J}$. Along the isochore $BC$ the volume does not change, therefore $L_{BC} = 0$. Along the isobar $CD$ the gas is compressed by $2 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ at the pressure $1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, so the work is negative and equals $L_{CD} = -200\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore the work along the whole path is $L = 900\,\mathrm{J} + 0 - 200\,\mathrm{J} = 700\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $1100\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by adding the works without taking the sign into account, and $900\,\mathrm{J}$ by neglecting the compression.
