A gas releases $300\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat to the surroundings while it is being compressed, and the surroundings do $500\,\mathrm{J}$ of work on the gas. What is the change in internal energy of the gas?

- $-200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $800\,\mathrm{J}$
- $200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-800\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In the **first law** $\Delta U = Q - L$, $Q$ is the heat absorbed by the gas and $L$ the work done by the gas. The gas releases heat, therefore $Q = -300\,\mathrm{J}$, and it receives work, therefore $L = -500\,\mathrm{J}$. Hence
$$
\Delta U = -300\,\mathrm{J} - (-500\,\mathrm{J}) = 200\,\mathrm{J} ,
$$
and the internal energy **increases**. The value $-800\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by giving $Q$ the negative sign and $L$ the positive sign, and $800\,\mathrm{J}$ by adding the two values without sign.
