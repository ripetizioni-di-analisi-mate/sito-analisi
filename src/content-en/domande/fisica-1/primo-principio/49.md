An aluminum ring ($\alpha = 2.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$) has an inner diameter of $4.990\,\mathrm{cm}$ at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and must be slipped onto a steel shaft ($\alpha = 1.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$) with a diameter of $5.000\,\mathrm{cm}$ at the same temperature. Ring and shaft are heated together. At what temperature does the inner diameter of the ring become equal to that of the shaft?

- $203\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $107\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $77\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- At no temperature, because the shaft expands too

## Explanation

After a temperature rise $\Delta T$ the two diameters are, in centimeters, $4.990\,(1 + \alpha_{\mathrm{Al}}\,\Delta T)$ and $5.000\,(1 + \alpha_{\mathrm{st}}\,\Delta T)$. Aluminum has the larger coefficient, so the diameter of the ring grows faster and reaches that of the shaft. Setting the two diameters equal gives
$$
\Delta T = \frac{5.000 - 4.990}{4.990 \cdot 2.3 \cdot 10^{-5} - 5.000 \cdot 1.2 \cdot 10^{-5}}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 183\,\mathrm{K} .
$$
Therefore the required temperature is about $20 + 183 = 203\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The value $107\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is obtained by neglecting the expansion of the shaft.
