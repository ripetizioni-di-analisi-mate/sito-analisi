The filament of a light bulb has an area of $5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and emissivity $e = 0.35$, and it radiates a power of $60\,\mathrm{W}$. What is its temperature? ($\sigma = 5.67 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W/(m^2\cdot K^4)}$)

- $2.1 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{K}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{K}$
- $2.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{K}$
- $7.8 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

By the Stefan-Boltzmann law $P = e\,\sigma\,A\,T^4$, so $T^4 = \dfrac{P}{e\,\sigma\,A}$. The denominator is $0.35 \cdot 5.67 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{W/K^4} \approx 9.92 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{W/K^4}$, therefore
$$
T = \left(\frac{60}{9.92 \cdot 10^{-13}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 2.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{K} .
$$
The value $2.1 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{K}$ is obtained by neglecting the emissivity, and $7.8 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{K}$ by taking the square root instead of the fourth root.
