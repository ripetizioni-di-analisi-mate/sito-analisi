A constant-volume gas thermometer reads the pressure $p_0 = 80.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ when immersed in melting ice at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and the pressure $p = 100.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ when immersed in a liquid. Treating the gas as ideal, what is the temperature of the liquid?

- $341\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $68.3\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $-54.6\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $20.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

By Gay-Lussac's second law, at constant volume the pressure of an ideal gas is proportional to the absolute temperature. So $\dfrac{T}{T_0} = \dfrac{p}{p_0}$ with $T_0 = 273.15\,\mathrm{K}$, and
$$
T = 273.15 \cdot \frac{100.0}{80.0}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 341.4\,\mathrm{K} .
$$
Therefore the temperature of the liquid is $t_C = 341.4 - 273.15 \approx 68.3\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The proportionality holds with the temperature in kelvin; the value $-54.6\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is obtained by inverting the ratio of the pressures.
