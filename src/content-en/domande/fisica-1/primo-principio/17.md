An aluminum container ($\alpha_r = 2.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$) is filled to the brim with $1.0\,\mathrm{L}$ of water ($\beta_{\ell} = 2.1 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$). The container and the water are heated together by $40\,\mathrm{K}$. How much water overflows?

- $8.4\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $5.6\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $7.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $11\,\mathrm{cm^3}$

## Explanation

Both the liquid and the container expand, therefore the amount that overflows is the difference between the increase in volume of the water and the increase in capacity of the container. The container expands as if it were filled with aluminum, with volume expansion coefficient $3\alpha_r = 6.9 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Hence, with $V_0 = 1.0\,\mathrm{L} = 1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}$,
$$
\Delta V = (\beta_{\ell} - 3\alpha_r)\,V_0\,\Delta T = 1.41 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 1000 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{cm^3} \approx 5.6\,\mathrm{cm^3} .
$$
The value $8.4\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ is obtained by ignoring the expansion of the container, and $7.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ by using $\alpha_r$ instead of $3\alpha_r$.
