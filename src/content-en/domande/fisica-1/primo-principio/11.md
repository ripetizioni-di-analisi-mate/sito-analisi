$1.00\,\mathrm{mol}$ of ideal gas at a temperature of $27.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is compressed quasi-statically and isothermally from a volume of $20.0\,\mathrm{L}$ to a volume of $10.0\,\mathrm{L}$. What is the work done by the gas?

- $1.73 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-156\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.73 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-751\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In an **isothermal process** the work done by the gas is $L = nRT \ln\dfrac{V_f}{V_i}$, with the temperature in kelvins, $T = 300.15\,\mathrm{K}$. Since $\dfrac{V_f}{V_i} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, we have
$$
L = 1.00 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 300.15 \cdot \ln\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{J} \approx -1.73 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is negative because the gas is compressed. The internal energy does not change, therefore the gas releases about $1.73\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of heat to the surroundings. The value $-156\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained with the temperature in degrees Celsius, and $-751\,\mathrm{J}$ with the base-$10$ logarithm.
