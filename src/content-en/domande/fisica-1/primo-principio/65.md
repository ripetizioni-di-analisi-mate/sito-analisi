A metal sample is immersed in water in an insulated vessel. The metal cools by $30\,\mathrm{K}$ and the water warms by $5\,\mathrm{K}$. Use $c_{\mathrm{acqua}}=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and neglect the vessel. The initial data are the mass of water $m_{a} = \dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the mass of metal $m_{m} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{kg}$. In the second configuration, change the mass of water to $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the mass of metal to $\dfrac{4}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $c$ minus its initial value?

- $-2520\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $70\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $-70\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $-420\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$

## Explanation

Heat lost by the metal equals heat gained by the water. Set $m_m c\cdot30$ equal to $m_a c_{\mathrm{acqua}}\cdot5$. The relation $c = \dfrac{700 \cdot m_{a}}{m_{m}}$ gives $420\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ before the change and $350\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $-70\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
