A $2.0\,\mathrm{L}$ container holds an ideal gas at a pressure of $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, and a $3.0\,\mathrm{L}$ container holds the same gas at a pressure of $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and at the same temperature. A valve connecting the two containers is opened and the temperature stays the same. What pressure is reached at equilibrium?

- $1.8 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.2 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The gas stays in the two containers, so the total number of moles is conserved, $n_1 + n_2 = n_f$. By the equation of state at fixed temperature $n = \dfrac{pV}{RT}$, hence $p_1 V_1 + p_2 V_2 = p_f\,(V_1 + V_2)$ and
$$
p_f = \frac{3.0 \cdot 2.0 + 1.0 \cdot 3.0}{5.0} \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.8 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The final pressure is the average of the initial pressures weighted by the volumes; swapping the weights gives $2.2 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, and the simple average gives $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
