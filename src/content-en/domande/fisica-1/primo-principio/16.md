A steel plate ($\alpha = 1.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$) has a circular hole of diameter $10.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ at a temperature of $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. How does the diameter of the hole change when the plate is heated to $120\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

- It decreases by $0.12\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It increases by $0.24\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It increases by $0.36\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It increases by $0.12\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

A hole in a plate expands as if it were filled with the same material, therefore its diameter follows the law of **linear expansion**. With $\Delta T = 100\,\mathrm{K}$ one obtains
$$
\Delta d = \alpha\,d_0\,\Delta T = 1.2 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 10.0 \cdot 100\,\mathrm{cm} = 0.012\,\mathrm{cm} ,
$$
hence the diameter **increases** by $0.12\,\mathrm{mm}$. The values $0.24\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $0.36\,\mathrm{mm}$ are obtained with the coefficients $2\alpha$ and $3\alpha$, which refer to area and volume.
