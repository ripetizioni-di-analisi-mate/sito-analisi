$1{,}0\,\mathrm{mol}$ of a monatomic ideal gas expands adiabatically and its temperature drops from $500\,\mathrm{K}$ to $300\,\mathrm{K}$. How much work does the gas do?

- $-2{,}5\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $4{,}2\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $2{,}5\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $1{,}7\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

In the **adiabatic process** $Q = 0$, therefore by the first law $L = -\Delta U = n\,c_V\,(T_i - T_f)$. For a monatomic gas $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$, hence
$$
L = 1{,}0 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8{,}314 \cdot 200\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2{,}5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is positive because the gas expands, and the gas does it at the expense of its own internal energy. The value $4{,}2\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is obtained with $c_p$ instead of $c_V$, and $1{,}7\,\mathrm{kJ}$ with the product $nR\,\Delta T$.
