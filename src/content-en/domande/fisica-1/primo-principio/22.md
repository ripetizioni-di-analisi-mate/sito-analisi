$10\,\mathrm{g}$ of steam at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ fall on a hand, condense and cool down to $35\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. How much heat does the steam give to the skin? ($\lambda_v = 2.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, $c_w = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$)

- $2.53 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.26 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.72 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.99 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The steam gives off heat in two stages. In the condensation at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ it releases $m\,\lambda_v = 0.010 \cdot 2.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J} = 2.26 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. Then the water formed cools by $65\,\mathrm{K}$ and releases $m\,c_w\,\Delta T = 0.010 \cdot 4186 \cdot 65\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.72 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore the total heat released is
$$
Q = 2.26 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} + 2.72 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.53 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The same mass of water at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ would release only the second term, about nine times less; this is why a steam burn is more severe than a burn from boiling water.
