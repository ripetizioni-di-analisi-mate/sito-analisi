How much heat is needed to turn $0{,}20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $-40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ into liquid water at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$? (Ice $c_g = 2100\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, water $c_a = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, $\lambda_f = 3{,}33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$.)

- $6{,}66 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1{,}68 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8{,}34 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The process takes place in two stages. The ice warms from $-40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ with its own specific heat and absorbs $m\,c_g\,\Delta T = 0{,}20 \cdot 2100 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{J} = 1{,}68 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. Then it melts at constant temperature and absorbs $m\,\lambda_f = 0{,}20 \cdot 3{,}33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} = 6{,}66 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore the total heat is $Q = 8{,}34 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $1{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using for the ice the specific heat of liquid water.
