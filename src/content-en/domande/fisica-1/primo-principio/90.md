A monatomic ideal gas goes quasi-statically around the rectangular cycle $A \to B \to C \to D \to A$, with $A$ ($1.0\,\mathrm{L}$; $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$), $B$ ($1.0\,\mathrm{L}$; $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$), $C$ ($3.0\,\mathrm{L}$; $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$) and $D$ ($3.0\,\mathrm{L}$; $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$). How much heat does the gas absorb in one cycle, adding only the parts in which the heat exchanged is positive?

- $200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1150\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $950\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

For an ideal gas $nR\,\Delta T = \Delta(pV)$, so on an isochore $Q = n\,c_V\,\Delta T = \dfrac{3}{2}\,V\,\Delta p$ and on an isobar $Q = n\,c_p\,\Delta T = \dfrac{5}{2}\,p\,\Delta V$. On the isochore $AB$ the pressure increases, hence $Q_{AB} = \dfrac{3}{2} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} = 150\,\mathrm{J}$. On the isobar $BC$ the volume increases, hence $Q_{BC} = \dfrac{5}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 1000\,\mathrm{J}$. On the parts $CD$ and $DA$ the temperature decreases and the gas gives off heat, so $Q_{\text{abs}} = 150\,\mathrm{J} + 1000\,\mathrm{J} = 1150\,\mathrm{J}$. The work of the cycle, $200\,\mathrm{J}$, is the difference between the heat absorbed and the $950\,\mathrm{J}$ given off.
