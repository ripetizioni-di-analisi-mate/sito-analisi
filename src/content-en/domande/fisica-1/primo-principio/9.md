A room measures $5{,}0\,\mathrm{m} \times 4{,}0\,\mathrm{m} \times 3{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ and contains air at $1{,}013 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Treating air as an ideal gas with molar mass $29\,\mathrm{g/mol}$, what is the mass of the air in the room?

- $1{,}1 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $7{,}2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $14\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $72\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The volume of the room is $V = 60\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and the absolute temperature is $T = 293{,}15\,\mathrm{K}$. The **equation of state** gives the number of moles
$$
n = \frac{pV}{RT} = \frac{1{,}013 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 60}{8{,}314 \cdot 293{,}15}\,\mathrm{mol} \approx 2{,}49 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{mol} .
$$
The mass is $m = n\,M$ with $M = 0{,}029\,\mathrm{kg/mol}$, therefore $m \approx 2{,}49 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 0{,}029\,\mathrm{kg} \approx 72\,\mathrm{kg}$. The value $1{,}1 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$ is obtained with the temperature in degrees Celsius, and $7{,}2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kg}$ with the molar mass left in grams.
