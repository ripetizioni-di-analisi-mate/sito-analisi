A solid sample is already at its melting temperature. Heating melts it completely, without losses. Given the mass $m = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the heat required to melt the sample $Q = 120000\,\mathrm{J}$, what is the specific latent heat $l$?

- $150\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$
- $300000\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$
- $600\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$
- $300\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$

## Explanation

Temperature remains constant during melting. Heat equals mass times latent heat, converted to joules per kilogram. In the stated units, the relation is $Q = 1000 \cdot l \cdot m$. Solving for the unknown gives $l = \dfrac{Q}{1000 \cdot m}$. Substituting the given values yields $300\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$.
