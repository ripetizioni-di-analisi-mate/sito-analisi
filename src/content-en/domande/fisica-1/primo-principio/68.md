At an atmospheric pressure of $1.013 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of liquid water at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, occupying $1.0\,\mathrm{L}$, boils and turns entirely into steam at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, with a density of $0.60\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. How much work does the water do by expanding against the atmosphere?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.3 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.1 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Vaporization takes place at constant pressure, so the work is $L = p\,(V_f - V_i)$. The steam occupies $V_f = \dfrac{m}{\rho} = \dfrac{1.0}{0.60}\,\mathrm{m^3} \approx 1.67\,\mathrm{m^3}$, while the liquid occupied $1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Therefore
$$
L = 1.013 \cdot 10^5 \cdot (1.67 - 0.001)\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The heat absorbed is $m\,\lambda_v \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$, so only about $7\,\%$ of this heat becomes work and the rest increases the internal energy.
