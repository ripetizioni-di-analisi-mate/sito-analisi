An ideal gas is enclosed in a rigid container with adiabatic walls. A paddle wheel driven from the outside stirs it and does $200\,\mathrm{J}$ of work on the gas. Which statement is true?

- $\Delta U = 0$, because the gas exchanges no heat
- $\Delta U = 200\,\mathrm{J}$ and the temperature of the gas increases
- $\Delta U = -200\,\mathrm{J}$ and the temperature of the gas decreases
- $\Delta U = 0$, because the volume of the gas stays constant

## Explanation

The walls are adiabatic, therefore $Q = 0$. The paddle wheel does $200\,\mathrm{J}$ of work on the gas, hence the work done by the gas is $L = -200\,\mathrm{J}$, even though the volume of the container does not change. By the **first law** $\Delta U = Q - L = 200\,\mathrm{J}$. The internal energy of the ideal gas depends only on the temperature and increases with it, therefore the temperature of the gas increases. Joule's paddle wheel experiment is based on this fact.
