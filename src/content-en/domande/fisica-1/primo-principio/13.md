An ideal gas goes around the triangular cycle $A \to C \to B \to A$, with $A$ ($1{,}00\,\mathrm{L}$; $1{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$), $C$ ($1{,}00\,\mathrm{L}$; $3{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$) and $B$ ($3{,}00\,\mathrm{L}$; $1{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$), and each side is traversed quasi-statically along a segment of the $(V, p)$ plane. What is the total work done by the gas in the cycle?

- $200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work of a quasi-static cycle is, in absolute value, the area enclosed by the cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane. The cycle is a right triangle with legs $V_B - V_A = 2{,}00 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and $p_C - p_A = 2{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, therefore the area is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2{,}00 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 2{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} = 200\,\mathrm{J}$. From $A$ the gas goes up along the isochore to $C$, goes down to the right to $B$ and returns to $A$ along the isobar, hence the cycle is traversed clockwise and the work is **positive**. Therefore $L = 200\,\mathrm{J}$, and since $\Delta U = 0$ the total heat exchanged is also $200\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $400\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the area of the triangle.
