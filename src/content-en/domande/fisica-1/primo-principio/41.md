A mass of dry air rises along the slope of a mountain, and its pressure goes from $1.00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ to $8.00 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The initial temperature of the air is $20.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Treating the air as a diatomic ideal gas ($\gamma = 1.40$) and the expansion as quasi-static and adiabatic, what temperature does the air reach?

- $-5.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $18.8\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $-38.6\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- $1.9\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

In a quasi-static **adiabatic process** $pV^{\gamma}$ is constant. By the equation of state $V = \dfrac{nRT}{p}$, so substituting one finds that $T^{\gamma}\,p^{1 - \gamma}$ is constant as well, that is $T\,p^{\frac{1 - \gamma}{\gamma}}$. Since $\dfrac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma} = \dfrac{0.40}{1.40} = \dfrac{2}{7}$, we get
$$
T_f = T_i \left(\frac{p_f}{p_i}\right)^{\frac{2}{7}} = 293.15 \cdot 0.800^{\frac{2}{7}}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 275.0\,\mathrm{K} .
$$
Therefore the air reaches about $1.9\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and it cools by about $18\,\mathrm{K}$ while rising. The value $-5.0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is obtained by using, with the pressure ratio, the exponent $\gamma - 1$ of the relation between temperature and volume, $-38.6\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ by assuming the temperature proportional to the pressure, and $18.8\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ by applying the formula to the temperature in degrees Celsius.
