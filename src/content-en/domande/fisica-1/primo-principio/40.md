A diatomic ideal gas absorbs $700\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat in a quasi-static process at constant pressure. How much work does it do?

- $200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $500\,\mathrm{J}$
- $280\,\mathrm{J}$
- $700\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In an **isobaric process** $L = nR\,\Delta T$ and $Q = n\,c_p\,\Delta T$, therefore $\dfrac{L}{Q} = \dfrac{R}{c_p}$. For a diatomic gas $c_p = \dfrac{7}{2}R$, hence $\dfrac{L}{Q} = \dfrac{2}{7}$ and $L = \dfrac{2}{7} \cdot 700\,\mathrm{J} = 200\,\mathrm{J}$. The difference, $500\,\mathrm{J}$, is the increase in internal energy. The value $280\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained with the ratio $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of a monatomic gas.
