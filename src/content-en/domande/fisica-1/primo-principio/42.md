An ideal gas is compressed quasi-statically to half its initial volume, starting each time from the same state, once along an isotherm and once along an adiabat. Which statement is true?

- With the adiabat the final pressure is higher and the gas warms up
- With the adiabat the final pressure is lower and the gas cools down
- The two final pressures are equal, because the final volume is the same
- With the adiabat the final pressure is higher and the temperature stays the same

## Explanation

In the isotherm the product $pV$ is constant, therefore the pressure doubles. In the adiabat $pV^{\gamma}$ is constant with $\gamma > 1$, hence the pressure is multiplied by $2^{\gamma} > 2$ and the final pressure is higher. In the adiabat the gas exchanges no heat and receives work, therefore by the first law $\Delta U = -L > 0$ and its temperature increases. Since $\gamma > 1$, in the $(V, p)$ plane the adiabat through a state is steeper than the isotherm through the same state.
