A wall loses $400\,\mathrm{W}$ by conduction. It is replaced by a wall of the same area, twice as thick and made of a material with half the thermal conductivity, with its faces at the same temperatures as before. What power does the new wall lose?

- $400\,\mathrm{W}$
- $100\,\mathrm{W}$
- $200\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1600\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

By **Fourier's law** $P = k\,A\,\dfrac{T_1 - T_2}{d}$, therefore the power is proportional to the conductivity $k$ and inversely proportional to the thickness $d$. Halving $k$ halves the power, and doubling $d$ halves it again. Hence the new wall loses one quarter of the initial power, that is, $100\,\mathrm{W}$. The value $400\,\mathrm{W}$ is obtained by assuming that the two changes compensate each other.
