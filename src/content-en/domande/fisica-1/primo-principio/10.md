$3.00\,\mathrm{mol}$ of a diatomic ideal gas are heated at constant pressure and their temperature increases by $50.0\,\mathrm{K}$. How much heat does the gas absorb?

- $3.12 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.36 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.25 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.87 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In an **isobaric process** the heat absorbed is $Q = n\,c_p\,\Delta T$, and for a diatomic gas $c_p = \dfrac{7}{2}R$. Therefore
$$
Q = 3.00 \cdot \frac{7}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 50.0\,\mathrm{J} \approx 4.36 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The same result follows from the first law, $Q = \Delta U + L$, with $\Delta U = n\,c_V\,\Delta T \approx 3.12 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ and $L = nR\,\Delta T \approx 1.25 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $3.12 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ is the change in internal energy alone, which is obtained by using $c_V$ instead of $c_p$, and $1.87 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by subtracting the work instead of adding it.
