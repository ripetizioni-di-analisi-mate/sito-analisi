Air at a pressure of $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and a temperature of $300\,\mathrm{K}$, treated as a diatomic ideal gas, is compressed adiabatically and quasi-statically to half its volume. Then, at constant volume, it cools until the temperature returns to $300\,\mathrm{K}$. What is the final pressure of the air?

- $2.6 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The final state has the initial temperature and half the initial volume, and the amount of gas has not changed. By the equation of state $p_f V_f = nRT = p_i V_i$, so $p_f = 2\,p_i = 2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, whatever the intermediate processes. Following the two processes gives the same value: after the adiabat $p = 1.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2^{1.4}\,\mathrm{Pa} \approx 2.64 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $T = 300 \cdot 2^{0.4}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 396\,\mathrm{K}$, and the isochore multiplies the pressure by $\dfrac{300}{396}$. The value $2.6 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ neglects the cooling.
