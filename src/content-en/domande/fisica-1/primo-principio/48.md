An ideal gas expands slowly at constant pressure. Work done by the gas is positive. Given the pressure $p = 150\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the work done by the gas $W = 600\,\mathrm{J}$, what is the volume increase $V$?

- $8\,\mathrm{L}$
- $2\,\mathrm{L}$
- $6\,\mathrm{L}$
- $4\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

On the pressure-volume diagram, work is the area of a rectangle of height $p$. One kilopascal-liter equals one joule. In the stated units, the relation is $W = V \cdot p$. Solving for the unknown gives $V = \dfrac{W}{p}$. Substituting the given values yields $4\,\mathrm{L}$.
