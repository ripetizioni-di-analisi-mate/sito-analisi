A steel measuring tape ($\alpha = 1.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$) is calibrated at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. At $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ it is used to measure a distance and the reading is $50.000\,\mathrm{m}$. How does the reading compare with the true length of the distance?

- The reading is larger than the true length by about $12\,\mathrm{mm}$
- The reading is smaller than the true length by about $24\,\mathrm{mm}$
- The reading is smaller than the true length by about $1.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- The reading is smaller than the true length by about $12\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

At $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ the tape has expanded, so the distance between two marks is larger than the marked one. Each stretch that the tape shows as $1\,\mathrm{m}$ is $(1 + \alpha\,\Delta T)\,\mathrm{m}$ long, with $\Delta T = 20\,\mathrm{K}$. Therefore the true length is
$$
L = 50.000 \cdot (1 + 1.2 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 20)\,\mathrm{m} = 50.012\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and the reading is **smaller** than the true length by $12\,\mathrm{mm}$.
