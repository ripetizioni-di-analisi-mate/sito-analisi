A solid sample is already at its melting temperature. Heating melts it completely, without losses. Given the specific latent heat $l = 300\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$ and the heat required to melt the sample $Q = 120000\,\mathrm{J}$, what is the mass $m$?

- $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $400\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Temperature remains constant during melting. Heat equals mass times latent heat, converted to joules per kilogram. In the stated units, the relation is $Q = 1000 \cdot l \cdot m$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{Q}{1000 \cdot l}$. Substituting the given values yields $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$.
