The envelope of a hot-air balloon has a volume of $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and is open at the bottom, so the inside air is at the outside pressure. The outside air is at $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and has a density of $1.25\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, while the inside air is heated to $90\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Treating air as an ideal gas, what total mass of envelope, basket and load can the balloon support?

- $2.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $5.5 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

At the same pressure the density of an ideal gas, $\rho = \dfrac{pM}{RT}$, is inversely proportional to the absolute temperature, so the inside air has density $\rho_i = 1.25 \cdot \dfrac{283.15}{363.15}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \approx 0.975\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The buoyant force is the weight of the $2.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$ of outside air displaced, and the inside air has a mass of about $1.95 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$. Therefore the mass the balloon can support is the difference
$$
m = (1.25 - 0.975) \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg} \approx 5.5 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $2.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$ is obtained by using the temperatures in degrees Celsius.
