A copper pot of mass $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ ($c = 385\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$) contains $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water ($c_w = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$). What is the heat capacity of the pot with the water?

- $2.09 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $7.12 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $2.56 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $4.57 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The pot and the water warm up together, so the heat needed to raise their temperature by $1\,\mathrm{K}$ is the sum of the heats needed for each of them. Therefore the total heat capacity is the sum of the heat capacities $m\,c$ and equals
$$
C = 1.2 \cdot 385\,\mathrm{J/K} + 0.50 \cdot 4186\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 462\,\mathrm{J/K} + 2093\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 2.56 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The value $2.09 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$ neglects the pot, which accounts for almost one fifth of the total.
