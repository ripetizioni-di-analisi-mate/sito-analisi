In an experiment like Joule's, a mass of $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ descends $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant speed and turns a paddle wheel immersed in $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water, in a container with adiabatic walls. The descent is repeated $20$ times, and all the work done by gravity becomes internal energy of the water. By how much does the temperature of the water rise? ($c_w = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$)

- $0.094\,\mathrm{K}$
- $1.9\,\mathrm{K}$
- $7.8\,\mathrm{K}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

The mass descends at constant speed, so in each descent the work done by gravity is $m\,g\,h = 10 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{J} \approx 196\,\mathrm{J}$, and in $20$ descents it is $W \approx 3.92 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$. The container exchanges no heat, hence this energy increases the internal energy of the water as a heat $Q = W$ would. Then
$$
\Delta T = \frac{W}{m_w\,c_w} = \frac{3.92 \cdot 10^3}{0.50 \cdot 4186}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 1.9\,\mathrm{K} .
$$
A single descent would give $0.094\,\mathrm{K}$; the value $7.8\,\mathrm{K}$ is obtained by dividing the work in joules by the heat capacity of the water expressed in calories per kelvin.
