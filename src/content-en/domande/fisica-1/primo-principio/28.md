How many molecules does $1.0\,\mathrm{L}$ of ideal gas contain at a pressure of $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and a temperature of $27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$? ($k_B = 1.38 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J/K}$)

- $2.4 \cdot 10^{22}$
- $2.4 \cdot 10^{25}$
- $2.7 \cdot 10^{23}$
- $2.4 \cdot 10^{19}$

## Explanation

Written with the number of molecules $N$, the **equation of state** is $pV = N k_B T$, with the volume in cubic meters, $V = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, and the temperature in kelvins, $T \approx 300\,\mathrm{K}$. Therefore
$$
N = \frac{pV}{k_B T} = \frac{1.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{1.38 \cdot 10^{-23} \cdot 300} = \frac{100}{4.14 \cdot 10^{-21}} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^{22} .
$$
The value $2.4 \cdot 10^{25}$ is obtained by leaving the volume in liters, $2.4 \cdot 10^{19}$ by converting the liters as if they were cubic centimeters, and $2.7 \cdot 10^{23}$ with the temperature in degrees Celsius.
