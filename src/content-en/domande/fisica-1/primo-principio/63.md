In the $(V, p)$ plane of the figure three equilibrium states $A$, $B$, $C$ of the same amount of ideal gas are marked, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Which ordering of the temperatures is correct?

::grafico{punti="1,4,pieno,A;3,2,pieno,B;5,1,pieno,C" segmenti="1,0,1,4;0,4,1,4;3,0,3,2;0,2,3,2;5,0,5,1;0,1,5,1" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=5 nomi="V,p" titolo="Three states of the same amount of ideal gas, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$"}

- $T_A > T_B > T_C$
- $T_A < T_B < T_C$
- $T_A < T_C < T_B$
- $T_A = T_B = T_C$

## Explanation

By the equation of state $T = \dfrac{pV}{nR}$, and $n$ is the same in the three states, so the temperature is proportional to the product $pV$. From the figure $p_A V_A = 4$, $p_B V_B = 6$ and $p_C V_C = 5$, in units of $10^2\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore $T_A < T_C < T_B$. The state with the highest pressure and the state with the largest volume both have a temperature lower than that of $B$, because the temperature depends on the product of the two variables.
