A pot of water boils at atmospheric pressure on a stove that transfers a power of $1.5\,\mathrm{kW}$ to it. How much water evaporates in $5.0$ minutes? ($\lambda_v = 2.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}$)

- $3.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.1\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The water is already boiling, so its temperature stays at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and all the heat received serves to vaporize it. In $5.0$ minutes, that is $300\,\mathrm{s}$, the stove transfers $Q = P\,\Delta t = 1.5 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 300\,\mathrm{J} = 4.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore the mass evaporated is
$$
m = \frac{Q}{\lambda_v} = \frac{4.5 \cdot 10^5}{2.26 \cdot 10^6}\,\mathrm{kg} \approx 0.20\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $1.4\,\mathrm{kg}$ is obtained with the latent heat of fusion, and $3.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}$ by leaving the time in minutes.
