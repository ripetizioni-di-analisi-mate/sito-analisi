A wall of area $10\,\mathrm{m^2}$ consists of a brick layer $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ thick ($k = 0.60\,\mathrm{W/(m\cdot K)}$) and an insulating panel $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ thick ($k = 0.040\,\mathrm{W/(m\cdot K)}$) placed against the brick. The two outer faces of the wall are at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. In steady state, what power flows through the wall?

- $7.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

In steady state the same power $P$ flows through the two layers, because otherwise energy would accumulate at the contact surface. By Fourier's law each layer has a temperature difference $\Delta T_i = \dfrac{P\,d_i}{k_i\,A}$, and the sum of the two differences is $20\,\mathrm{K}$. So
$$
P = \frac{A\,\Delta T}{\dfrac{d_1}{k_1} + \dfrac{d_2}{k_2}} = \frac{10 \cdot 20}{0.333 + 1.25}\,\mathrm{W} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The value $7.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$ is obtained by adding the powers that each layer alone would transmit with the whole temperature difference, and $1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$ by considering only the insulating panel.
