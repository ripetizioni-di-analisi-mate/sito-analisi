A rigid $20\,\mathrm{L}$ cylinder contains oxygen ($M = 32.0\,\mathrm{g/mol}$) at a pressure of $1.5 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and a temperature of $27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. After use the pressure has dropped to $6.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, at the same temperature. Treating oxygen as an ideal gas, what mass of gas has been drawn off?

- $0.38\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.6\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.23\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Volume and temperature do not change, so by the equation of state the number of moles is proportional to the pressure, and the moles that left are $\Delta n = \dfrac{\Delta p\,V}{RT}$. With $\Delta p = 9.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $V = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and $T = 300.15\,\mathrm{K}$ one gets $\Delta n \approx 7.21\,\mathrm{mol}$. Therefore the mass drawn off is $\Delta m = \Delta n\,M \approx 7.21 \cdot 0.0320\,\mathrm{kg} \approx 0.23\,\mathrm{kg}$. The values $0.38\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ are the masses contained in the cylinder before and after use.
