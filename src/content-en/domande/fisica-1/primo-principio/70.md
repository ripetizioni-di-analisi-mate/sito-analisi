A solid sample is already at its melting temperature. Heating melts it completely, without losses. The initial data are the mass $m = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the specific latent heat $l = 300\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. Change the mass to $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the specific latent heat to $200\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $Q$ minus its initial value?

- $2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $120000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Temperature remains constant during melting. Heat equals mass times latent heat, converted to joules per kilogram. The relation $Q = 1000 \cdot l \cdot m$ gives $120000\,\mathrm{J}$ before the change and $120000\,\mathrm{J}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $0\,\mathrm{J}$.
