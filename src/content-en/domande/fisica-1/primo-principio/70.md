A vertical cylinder contains a gas closed by a piston of mass $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and area $1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, which slides without friction; above the piston there is the atmosphere at $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The gas is heated slowly and the piston rises by $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. How much work does the gas do? ($g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$)

- $1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $20\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The piston rises slowly, so it is in equilibrium at every instant and the pressure of the gas balances the atmospheric pressure and the weight of the piston. Therefore the pressure of the gas is constant and equals
$$
p = p_{\mathrm{atm}} + \frac{m g}{A} = 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} + \frac{20 \cdot 9.81}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{Pa} \approx 1.20 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The volume increases by $\Delta V = A\,h = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, so $L = p\,\Delta V \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$ neglects the weight of the piston, and $20\,\mathrm{J}$ neglects the atmosphere.
