A monatomic ideal gas at a temperature of $400\,\mathrm{K}$ expands adiabatically and quasi-statically until its volume doubles. What is the final temperature?

- $252\,\mathrm{K}$
- $303\,\mathrm{K}$
- $126\,\mathrm{K}$
- $200\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

In a quasi-static **adiabatic process** $TV^{\gamma - 1} = \text{costante}$ holds, and for a monatomic gas $\gamma - 1 = \dfrac{5}{3} - 1 = \dfrac{2}{3}$. Therefore
$$
T_f = T_i \left( \frac{V_i}{V_f} \right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 400 \cdot 2^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,\mathrm{K} \approx \frac{400}{1{,}59}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 252\,\mathrm{K} .
$$
The gas cools because it does work without absorbing heat. The value $303\,\mathrm{K}$ is obtained with the exponent $0{,}40$ of a diatomic gas, $126\,\mathrm{K}$ with the exponent $\gamma$ instead of $\gamma - 1$, and $200\,\mathrm{K}$ by assuming the temperature inversely proportional to the volume.
