An ideal gas occupies $2.0\,\mathrm{L}$ at a pressure of $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and a temperature of $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Without any loss of gas, it is brought to a volume of $1.0\,\mathrm{L}$ and a temperature of $450\,\mathrm{K}$. What pressure does it reach?

- $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The amount of gas does not change, so by the equation of state the ratio $\dfrac{pV}{T} = nR$ has the same value in the two states. It follows that
$$
p_f = p_i\,\frac{V_i}{V_f}\,\frac{T_f}{T_i} = 1.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{450}{300}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
Halving the volume doubles the pressure, and the increase of the absolute temperature multiplies it by a further $1.5$. The value $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ takes only the volume into account, and $1.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ only the temperature.
