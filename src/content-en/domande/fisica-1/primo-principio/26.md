A rigid container holds an ideal gas at $27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and at a pressure of $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. What pressure does the gas reach if it is heated to $177\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

- $1.3 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The volume is constant, therefore the **second law of Gay-Lussac** $\dfrac{p}{T} = \text{constant}$ holds, with temperatures in kelvins. The absolute temperature goes from about $300\,\mathrm{K}$ to about $450\,\mathrm{K}$, that is, it increases by a factor $\dfrac{3}{2}$. Hence the pressure also increases by a factor $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and becomes $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The value $1.3 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is obtained with the ratio of the temperatures in degrees Celsius, and $1.3 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ with the ratio of the temperatures inverted.
