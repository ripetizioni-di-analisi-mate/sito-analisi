An ideal gas goes quasi-statically, counterclockwise in the $(V, p)$ plane, around a rectangular cycle formed by two isobars at $1{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $2{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and two isochores at $2{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$ and $5{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$. What is the total work done by the gas?

- $300\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-300\,\mathrm{J}$
- $600\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work of a quasi-static cycle is, in absolute value, the area enclosed by the cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane. The rectangle has sides $1{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, therefore the area is $1{,}0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 300\,\mathrm{J}$. The cycle is traversed counterclockwise, hence the work is **negative** and $L = -300\,\mathrm{J}$. In this direction the expansion takes place at the lower pressure and the compression at the higher pressure, therefore more work is done on the gas than by the gas. The value $-600\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using the higher pressure instead of the difference of the pressures.
