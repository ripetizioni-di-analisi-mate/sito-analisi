A gas goes quasi-statically around the cycle $ABCDA$ in the figure, with the sides traversed along segments, volumes in liters and pressures in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Which statement is true?

::grafico{vettori="1,1,4,1;4,1,4,2;4,2,1,3;1,3,1,1" area="3-(x-1)/3;1;1;4" punti="1,1,pieno,A;4,1,pieno,B;4,2,pieno,C;1,3,pieno,D" segmenti="1,0,1,1;4,0,4,1" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=4 nomi="V,p" titolo="Cycle $ABCDA$ with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$"}

- The total work done by the gas is $-450\,\mathrm{J}$ and the gas gives off a net $450\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat
- The total work done by the gas is $450\,\mathrm{J}$ and the gas absorbs a net $450\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat
- The total work done by the gas is $-450\,\mathrm{J}$ and the total heat exchanged is zero, because $\Delta U = 0$
- The total work done by the gas is $-900\,\mathrm{J}$ and the gas gives off a net $900\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat

## Explanation

The total work of a quasi-static cycle is, in absolute value, the area enclosed by the cycle. The cycle is a trapezoid with vertical sides $AD$ and $BC$ of lengths $2 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and height $3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, so the area is $\dfrac{(2 + 1) \cdot 10^5}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 450\,\mathrm{J}$. From $A$ the gas moves to the right along the lower side and comes back to the left along the upper side, hence the cycle is traversed counterclockwise and $L = -450\,\mathrm{J}$. Since $\Delta U = 0$ in a cycle, the first law gives $Q_{\text{tot}} = L = -450\,\mathrm{J}$, that is, the gas **gives off** a net $450\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat.
