A gas goes quasi-statically from state $A$ ($1{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$; $1{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$) to state $B$ ($3{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$; $3{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$) along the segment joining them in the $(V, p)$ plane. How much work does the gas do?

- $600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $800\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work of a quasi-static expansion is the area under the curve of the process in the $(V, p)$ plane. Under the segment $AB$ there is a trapezoid with bases $p_A = 1{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $p_B = 3{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and height $V_B - V_A = 2{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Therefore
$$
L = \frac{(1{,}0 + 3{,}0) \cdot 10^5}{2} \cdot 2{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 400\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The values $600\,\mathrm{J}$ and $200\,\mathrm{J}$ are obtained by using only the final pressure or only the initial pressure, as if the process were isobaric, and $800\,\mathrm{J}$ by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the area of the trapezoid.
