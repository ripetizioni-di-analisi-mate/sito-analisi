An ideal gas expands quasi-statically and isothermally and does $800\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. Which statement is true?

- The gas exchanges no heat and $\Delta U = -800\,\mathrm{J}$
- The gas absorbs $800\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat and $\Delta U = 0$
- The gas absorbs $1600\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat and $\Delta U = 800\,\mathrm{J}$
- The gas gives off $800\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat and $\Delta U = 0$

## Explanation

The temperature stays constant and the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature, so $\Delta U = 0$. By the first law $Q = \Delta U + L = 800\,\mathrm{J}$, hence the gas **absorbs** $800\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat. In an isothermal expansion all the heat absorbed becomes work; the situation with $Q = 0$ and $\Delta U = -800\,\mathrm{J}$ is that of an adiabatic expansion.
