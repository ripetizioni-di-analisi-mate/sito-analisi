The same heat $Q$ is supplied to $1.0\,\mathrm{mol}$ of ideal gas in four different situations. In which case does the temperature of the gas rise the most?

- Monatomic gas at constant pressure
- Diatomic gas at constant volume
- Diatomic gas at constant pressure
- Monatomic gas at constant volume

## Explanation

In each case $\Delta T = \dfrac{Q}{n\,c}$, where $c$ is the molar heat capacity of the process, so the temperature rise is larger when $c$ is smaller. The monatomic gas has $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$ and $c_p = \dfrac{5}{2}R$, the diatomic gas has $c_V = \dfrac{5}{2}R$ and $c_p = \dfrac{7}{2}R$. The smallest value is $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$, hence the temperature rises the most in the **monatomic gas at constant volume**. At constant volume the gas does no work, and all the heat increases the internal energy.
