A mass of water at atmospheric pressure is slowly heated from $1\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $3\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. How does its volume change?

- It decreases
- It increases
- It stays constant
- It increases up to $2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and then decreases

## Explanation

Between $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ water contracts when heated, and its density is maximum at about $4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Therefore, going from $1\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $3\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, the density increases. Since the mass stays the same, the volume **decreases**. Above $4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ water expands like other liquids.
