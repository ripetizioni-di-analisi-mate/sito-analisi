An ideal gas goes from state $A$ ($1.0\,\mathrm{L}$; $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$) to state $B$ ($3.0\,\mathrm{L}$; $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$) in two quasi-static ways. The first path is an isobar followed by an isochore, the second is an isochore followed by an isobar. Which statement is true?

- $\Delta U = 0$ along both paths, and the gas absorbs $200\,\mathrm{J}$ along the first and $600\,\mathrm{J}$ along the second
- The gas absorbs the same heat along the two paths, because the initial and final states are the same
- $\Delta U = 600\,\mathrm{J}$ along the first path and $\Delta U = 200\,\mathrm{J}$ along the second
- $\Delta U = 0$ along both paths, and the gas absorbs $600\,\mathrm{J}$ along the first and $200\,\mathrm{J}$ along the second

## Explanation

States $A$ and $B$ have the same product $pV = 300\,\mathrm{J}$, therefore by the equation of state they have the same temperature. The internal energy of the ideal gas depends only on the temperature, hence $\Delta U = 0$ along any path. Along the first path the work is that of the isobar at $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $L_1 = 3.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 600\,\mathrm{J}$, while along the second it is that of the isobar at $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $L_2 = 200\,\mathrm{J}$. By the first law $Q = \Delta U + L = L$, therefore the gas absorbs $600\,\mathrm{J}$ along the first path and $200\,\mathrm{J}$ along the second. Heat and work depend on the process, while $\Delta U$ depends only on the states.
