A body is heated from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. What is its temperature change in kelvins and in degrees Fahrenheit?

- $\Delta T = 303\,\mathrm{K}$ and $\Delta t_F = 54\,^\circ\mathrm{F}$
- $\Delta T = 30\,\mathrm{K}$ and $\Delta t_F = 54\,^\circ\mathrm{F}$
- $\Delta T = 30\,\mathrm{K}$ and $\Delta t_F = 86\,^\circ\mathrm{F}$
- $\Delta T = 303\,\mathrm{K}$ and $\Delta t_F = 86\,^\circ\mathrm{F}$

## Explanation

The kelvin and the degree Celsius have the same size, therefore a temperature difference has the same value on the two scales and $\Delta T = 30\,\mathrm{K}$. On the Fahrenheit scale one hundred degrees Celsius correspond to one hundred eighty degrees, hence one degree Celsius equals $\dfrac{9}{5}$ of a degree Fahrenheit and $\Delta t_F = \dfrac{9}{5} \cdot 30\,^\circ\mathrm{F} = 54\,^\circ\mathrm{F}$. The shift of the zero, $273{,}15$ for kelvins and $32$ for degrees Fahrenheit, cancels in the difference. The values $303\,\mathrm{K}$ and $86\,^\circ\mathrm{F}$ are obtained by converting the difference as if it were a temperature.
