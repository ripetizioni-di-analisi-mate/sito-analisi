A gas is compressed at a constant pressure of $3{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ from a volume of $5{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$ to a volume of $2{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$. What is the work done by the gas?

- $900\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-900\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-9{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-600\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

At constant pressure the work done by the gas is $L = p\,(V_f - V_i)$, with the volumes in cubic meters. The change in volume is $V_f - V_i = -3{,}0\,\mathrm{L} = -3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, therefore
$$
L = 3{,}0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot \left( -3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3} \right)\mathrm{J} = -900\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is **negative** because the gas is compressed and receives work from the outside. The value $-9{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by leaving the volumes in liters, and $-600\,\mathrm{J}$ by using the final volume $2{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$ in place of the change in volume.
