In a room the radiator is installed near the floor. Why does the air near the ceiling also warm up?

- Air is a good conductor, so heat reaches the ceiling by conduction
- The heated air expands, becomes less dense and rises, and its place is taken by colder air
- The heated air becomes denser and pushes the cold air upward
- The radiator emits radiation that is absorbed only by the air near the ceiling

## Explanation

In fluids heat also propagates by **convection**, which involves a transport of matter. The air in contact with the radiator warms up and expands, so its density decreases and the buoyant force makes it rise. Its place is taken by colder air, which in turn warms up, hence a circulation forms in the room that carries warm air toward the ceiling. Conduction through the air contributes little, because air has a very low thermal conductivity.
