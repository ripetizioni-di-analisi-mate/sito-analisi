Nitrogen $\mathrm{N_2}$ ($M = 28.0\,\mathrm{g/mol}$) at room temperature behaves as a diatomic ideal gas. What is its specific heat at constant pressure per unit mass? ($R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\cdot K)}$)

- $29.1\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $1.04 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $742\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$
- $1.04\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$

## Explanation

For a diatomic gas the molar heat capacity at constant pressure is $c_p = \dfrac{7}{2}R \approx 29.1\,\mathrm{J/(mol\cdot K)}$. One mole of nitrogen has a mass of $0.0280\,\mathrm{kg}$, so the heat per kilogram is obtained by dividing by the molar mass:
$$
c = \frac{c_p}{M} = \frac{29.1}{0.0280}\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)} \approx 1.04 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)} .
$$
The value $742\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ is the specific heat at constant volume, obtained from $c_V = \dfrac{5}{2}R$.
