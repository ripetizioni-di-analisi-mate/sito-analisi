An unconstrained bar of uniform material is heated by $50\,\mathrm{K}$. The linear approximation applies. The initial data are the linear expansion coefficient $a = 12\,\mathrm{\mu m/(m\,K)}$ and the initial length $L = 4\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the initial length to $6\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $d$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$
- $3$

## Explanation

The extension is $\alpha L\Delta T$. With the coefficient in micrometers per meter per kelvin, divide the result by a thousand to obtain millimeters. The relation $d = \dfrac{L \cdot a}{20}$ gives $\dfrac{12}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$ before the change and $\dfrac{18}{5}\,\mathrm{mm}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
