A gas goes from the equilibrium state $A$ to the equilibrium state $B$ by two different processes. Which quantity certainly has the same value in the two processes?

- The change in internal energy $\Delta U$
- The heat $Q$ absorbed by the gas
- The work $L$ done by the gas
- The sum $Q + L$

## Explanation

Internal energy is a state function, so $\Delta U = U_B - U_A$ depends only on the initial and final states. The work is the area under the curve of the process in the $(V, p)$ plane, hence it changes with the process, and by the first law $Q = \Delta U + L$ changes as well. Consequently $Q + L = \Delta U + 2L$ also depends on the process; what stays the same is the difference $Q - L$, which is exactly $\Delta U$.
