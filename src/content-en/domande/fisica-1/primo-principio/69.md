A solid sample is already at its melting temperature. Heating melts it completely, without losses. The initial data are the mass $m = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the specific latent heat $l = 300\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. In the second configuration, change the mass to $\dfrac{4}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $Q$ to its initial value?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $1$

## Explanation

Temperature remains constant during melting. Heat equals mass times latent heat, converted to joules per kilogram. The relation $Q = 1000 \cdot l \cdot m$ gives $120000\,\mathrm{J}$ before the change and $240000\,\mathrm{J}$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
