A room that is not airtight contains air at atmospheric pressure, which stays constant because the air can escape through the gaps. The heating brings the air of the room from $15\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Treating air as a diatomic ideal gas, how does the internal energy of the air contained in the room change?

- It stays the same
- It increases by about $3.5\,\%$, like the absolute temperature
- It increases by about $67\,\%$, like the temperature in degrees Celsius
- It decreases, because part of the air escapes

## Explanation

Setting the internal energy at $0\,\mathrm{K}$ equal to zero, the internal energy of $n$ moles of diatomic gas is $U = \dfrac{5}{2}\,nRT$. By the equation of state $nRT = pV$, so the internal energy of the air in the room is $U = \dfrac{5}{2}\,pV$. The pressure and the volume of the room do not change, hence $U$ **stays the same**. The temperature rises, and the number of moles contained decreases in the same proportion because part of the air escapes.
