$2{,}0\,\mathrm{mol}$ of a diatomic ideal gas go from $300\,\mathrm{K}$ to $500\,\mathrm{K}$ through a process whose details are unknown. What is the change in internal energy of the gas?

- $12\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $8{,}3\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $5{,}0\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $3{,}3\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

Internal energy is a state function, and for the ideal gas $\Delta U = n\,c_V\,\Delta T$ holds in *every* process. For a diatomic gas $c_V = \dfrac{5}{2}R$, therefore
$$
\Delta U = 2{,}0 \cdot \frac{5}{2} \cdot 8{,}314 \cdot 200\,\mathrm{J} \approx 8{,}3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $12\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is obtained with $c_p$ instead of $c_V$, $5{,}0\,\mathrm{kJ}$ with the molar heat capacity $\dfrac{3}{2}R$ of a monatomic gas, and $3{,}3\,\mathrm{kJ}$ with the product $nR\,\Delta T$, which is the work of an isobaric process.
