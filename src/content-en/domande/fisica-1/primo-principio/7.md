- $1.30 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Heating the water from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ requires $m\,c_a\,\Delta T = 0.500 \cdot 4186 \cdot 80\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.67 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$. Then evaporating all of it at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ requires $m\,\lambda_v = 0.500 \cdot 2.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J} = 1.13 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$. Hence the total heat is $Q \approx 1.67 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} + 1.13 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.30 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$. Vaporization requires almost seven times the heat needed for the warming. The value $2.43 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained without multiplying $\lambda_v$ by the mass.
