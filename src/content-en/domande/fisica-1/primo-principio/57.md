A substance of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$, initially solid, receives heat at constant pressure. The graph shows its temperature as a function of the heat absorbed. What is the latent heat of fusion of the substance?

::grafico{f="min(40,8+2*x)+max(0,x-56)" xmin=0 xmax=80 ymin=0 ymax=70 nomi="Q,T" punti="16,40,pieno;56,40,pieno" segmenti="16,0,16,40;56,0,56,40;0,40,16,40" etichette="30,44,40 °C" legenda=0 titolo="Temperature $T$ in $^\circ\mathrm{C}$ as a function of the heat absorbed $Q$ in $\mathrm{kJ}$"}

- $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $2.8 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/kg}$

## Explanation

During melting the temperature stays constant, so melting corresponds to the horizontal part of the graph, at $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. This part goes from $Q = 16\,\mathrm{kJ}$ to $Q = 56\,\mathrm{kJ}$, hence the heat absorbed to melt the whole substance is $40\,\mathrm{kJ}$. Since $Q = m\,\lambda_f$,
$$
\lambda_f = \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^4}{0.20}\,\mathrm{J/kg} = 2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg} .
$$
The value $2.8 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ is obtained by using the heat read at the end of the horizontal part, which also includes the heating of the solid.
