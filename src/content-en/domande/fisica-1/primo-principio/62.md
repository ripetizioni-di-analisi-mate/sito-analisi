Two identical rigid containers hold, at the same pressure and the same temperature, one oxygen $\mathrm{O_2}$ ($M = 32.0\,\mathrm{g/mol}$) and the other nitrogen $\mathrm{N_2}$ ($M = 28.0\,\mathrm{g/mol}$), both treated as ideal gases. Which statement is true?

- They contain the same number of molecules and the same mass
- They contain the same number of molecules, and the mass of the oxygen is $\dfrac{8}{7}$ of that of the nitrogen
- The nitrogen container holds more molecules, because nitrogen molecules are lighter
- They contain the same mass, and the number of nitrogen molecules is $\dfrac{8}{7}$ of that of oxygen

## Explanation

By the equation of state $n = \dfrac{pV}{RT}$, and the two gases have the same values of $p$, $V$ and $T$, so they contain the same number of moles and of molecules (**Avogadro's law**). The mass is $m = nM$, hence for the same number of moles the ratio of the masses equals the ratio of the molar masses, $\dfrac{32.0}{28.0} = \dfrac{8}{7}$. The number of molecules is fixed by $p$, $V$ and $T$ and does not depend on the mass of the individual molecules.
