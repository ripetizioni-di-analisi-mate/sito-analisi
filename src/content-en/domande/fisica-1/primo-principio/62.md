A metal sample is immersed in water in an insulated vessel. The metal cools by $30\,\mathrm{K}$ and the water warms by $5\,\mathrm{K}$. Use $c_{\mathrm{water}}=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and neglect the vessel. Given the mass of metal $m_{m} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the metal's specific heat $c = 420\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, what is the mass of water $m_{a}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{20}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{1}{120}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Heat lost by the metal equals heat gained by the water. Set $m_m c\cdot30$ equal to $m_a c_{\mathrm{water}}\cdot5$. In the stated units, the relation is $c = \dfrac{700 \cdot m_{a}}{m_{m}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $m_{a} = \dfrac{c \cdot m_{m}}{700}$. Substituting the given values yields $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{kg}$.
