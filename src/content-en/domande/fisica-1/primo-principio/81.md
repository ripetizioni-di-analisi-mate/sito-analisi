A free diver is at a depth of $10\,\mathrm{m}$ in a lake and has $5.0\,\mathrm{L}$ of air in the lungs. The diver comes up holding the breath, and the temperature of the air in the lungs stays the same. What volume would the air occupy at the surface, where the pressure is $1.013 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$? (Density of water $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$)

- $9.8\,\mathrm{L}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{L}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{L}$
- $4.8\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

By Stevin's law the pressure at a depth of $10\,\mathrm{m}$ is $p_1 = 1.013 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} + 1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{Pa} \approx 1.994 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The temperature and the amount of air do not change, so by Boyle's law $p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2$. Therefore
$$
V_2 = 5.0 \cdot \frac{1.994 \cdot 10^5}{1.013 \cdot 10^5}\,\mathrm{L} \approx 9.8\,\mathrm{L} .
$$
The volume almost doubles, because at $10\,\mathrm{m}$ the pressure is about twice the atmospheric pressure. The value $4.8\,\mathrm{L}$ is obtained by using only the pressure due to the water, without the atmospheric pressure.
