An open jar contains air at atmospheric pressure and at a temperature of $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. It is put in an oven and heated to $200\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and the pressure stays atmospheric. Neglecting the expansion of the jar and treating air as an ideal gas, what fraction of the initial air leaves the jar?

- About $62\,\%$
- About $38\,\%$
- About $90\,\%$
- None, because the pressure stays the same

## Explanation

The volume and the pressure of the air in the jar stay the same, so by the equation of state $n = \dfrac{pV}{RT}$ the number of moles contained is inversely proportional to the absolute temperature. Therefore the ratio between the final and the initial moles is
$$
\frac{n_f}{n_i} = \frac{T_i}{T_f} = \frac{293.15}{473.15} \approx 0.62 .
$$
About $62\,\%$ of the air remains in the jar, so about $38\,\%$ leaves it. The value $90\,\%$ is obtained by using the temperatures in degrees Celsius.
