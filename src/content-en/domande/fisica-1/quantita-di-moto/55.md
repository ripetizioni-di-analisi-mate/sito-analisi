In an Atwood machine two bodies of masses $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hang from the ends of an inextensible string of negligible mass, which passes over a fixed ideal pulley. The system is released from rest. What is the acceleration of the center of mass of the two bodies?

- $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ downward
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ downward
- $0$, because the forces of the string are internal
- $9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ downward

## Explanation

For the Atwood machine the $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ body goes down and the $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ body goes up with acceleration of magnitude
$$
a = \frac{3.0 - 1.0}{3.0 + 1.0}\,g = \frac{g}{2} \approx 4.9\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
With the $y$ axis pointing upward, the accelerations of the two bodies are $-a$ and $+a$, so by the definition of center of mass
$$
a_{cm} = \frac{3.0 \cdot (-a) + 1.0 \cdot a}{4.0} = -\frac{a}{2} = -\frac{g}{4} \approx -2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The result is not zero because the system of the two bodies is also acted on by the external force of the pulley, equal to $2T$; the resultant of the external forces, $4.0\,g - 2T$ downward, equals $9.8\,\mathrm{N}$ and divided by the total mass gives the same value.
