The Earth has mass $5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the Moon $7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{kg}$; the distance between their centers is $3.84 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}$. Treating the two bodies as particles located at their centers, how far from the center of the Earth is the center of mass of the Earth-Moon system?

- $1.9 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.8 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.7 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.7 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Take the $x$ axis with origin at the center of the Earth and pointing toward the Moon, so the Earth has coordinate $0$ and the Moon $D = 3.84 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}$. By the definition of **center of mass**
$$
x_{cm} = \frac{m_L\,D}{M_T + m_L} = \frac{7.35 \cdot 10^{22} \cdot 3.84 \cdot 10^{8}}{6.04 \cdot 10^{24}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 4.7 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The mass of the Earth is about $81$ times that of the Moon, therefore the center of mass is much closer to the center of the Earth. Since the Earth's radius is about $6.4 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$, the center of mass of the system lies inside the Earth.
