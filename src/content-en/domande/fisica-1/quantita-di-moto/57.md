A person on a sled, of total mass $40\,\mathrm{kg}$, is at rest on smooth ice and holds a $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ sack. The person throws the sack horizontally, and the sack moves away from the sled with velocity $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ *relative to the sled*. What is the speed of the sled relative to the ice right after the throw?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Let $V$ be the component of the velocity of the sled relative to the ice along the direction of the throw. The velocity of the sack relative to the ice is the sum of the relative velocity and that of the sled, that is $V + 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The system starts at rest and the external forces are vertical, so the horizontal momentum stays zero:
$$
40\,V + 10\,(V + 5.0) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
V = -\frac{50}{50}\,\mathrm{m/s} = -1.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The sled therefore moves at $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ opposite to the sack, which moves at $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the ice. The value $1.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ would be obtained by treating $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ as a velocity relative to the ice.
