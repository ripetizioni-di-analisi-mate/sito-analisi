A ball of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves on a circle in a horizontal plane with constant speed $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of the change in its momentum over a quarter turn?

- $1.4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$
- $0$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$

## Explanation

The magnitude of the momentum stays $p = m v = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$, but its direction changes: after a quarter turn the velocity has rotated by $90^\circ$. So $\vec{p}_1$ and $\vec{p}_2$ are perpendicular vectors of the same magnitude, and their difference is the hypotenuse of an isosceles right triangle:
$$
|\Delta \vec{p}| = |\vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1| = \sqrt{p^2 + p^2} = \sqrt{2}\,m v \approx 1.4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} .
$$
The change is not zero, because momentum is a vector; by the impulse-momentum theorem this change is the impulse of the centripetal force over the quarter turn.
