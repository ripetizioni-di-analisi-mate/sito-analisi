A truck and a bicycle move along a straight road. Each is stopped by a constant force; the two forces have the same magnitude and act for the same time interval. Which statement about the two vehicles before braking is true?

- They had speeds of equal magnitude
- They had the same kinetic energy
- They cover the same braking distance
- They had momenta of equal magnitude

## Explanation

The two forces are constant, have the same magnitude and act for the same time $\Delta t$, so they produce impulses of equal magnitude $F\,\Delta t$. By the **impulse-momentum theorem** each impulse equals the change in momentum of the vehicle, which goes from $p$ to $0$; therefore the two vehicles had initial momenta of equal magnitude. The masses are different, so the speeds $v = \dfrac{p}{m}$ and the kinetic energies $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$ are different. The braking distances, equal to $\dfrac{v}{2}\,\Delta t$, are also different: the truck, which is slower, covers a shorter one.
