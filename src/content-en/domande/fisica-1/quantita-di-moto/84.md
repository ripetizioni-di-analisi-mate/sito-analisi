On a sheet of smooth ice a $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block slides at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the $x$ axis and strikes a glancing blow on a $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block at rest, which leaves at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a direction at $30^\circ$ from the $x$ axis. Treating the blocks as particles and neglecting friction, what is the speed of the $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block after the collision?

- $1.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Choose the $y$ axis so that the $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block has a negative $y$ component; its momentum is $1.0 \cdot 3.0\,(\cos 30^\circ,\ -\sin 30^\circ) \approx (2.60,\ -1.50)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. By conservation of momentum the $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block has momentum
$$
\vec{p}_1' = (6.0,\ 0) - (2.60,\ -1.50) = (3.40,\ 1.50)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} .
$$
Dividing by the mass gives $\vec{v}_1' \approx (1.70,\ 0.75)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, whose magnitude is $\sqrt{1.70^2 + 0.75^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
