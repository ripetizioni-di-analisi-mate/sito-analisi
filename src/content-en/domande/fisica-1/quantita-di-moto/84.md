Two bodies lie along a track. The first mass is at the origin and the second at positive coordinate $d$. The first mass is $2\,\mathrm{kg}$. Given the second mass $m = 6\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the center-of-mass coordinate $x = 6\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the distance $d$?

- $12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $24\,\mathrm{m}$
- $16\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The center-of-mass coordinate is a mass-weighted average. The first body contributes zero to the numerator. In the stated units, the relation is $x = \dfrac{d \cdot m}{m + 2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $d = \dfrac{x \cdot \left(m + 2\right)}{m}$. Substituting the given values yields $8\,\mathrm{m}$.
