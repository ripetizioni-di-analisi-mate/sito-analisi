A ball reaches a smooth horizontal floor with speed $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at an angle of $45^\circ$ with the floor, and bounces. The coefficient of restitution for the component perpendicular to the floor is $e = 0.50$, and the smooth floor exerts no forces parallel to itself. What angle does the velocity make with the floor right after the bounce?

- $27^\circ$
- $45^\circ$
- $22.5^\circ$
- $63^\circ$

## Explanation

The smooth floor exerts only a perpendicular force, so the horizontal component of the velocity does not change and stays $10 \cos 45^\circ \approx 7.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The vertical component reverses and its magnitude is multiplied by $e$, therefore it becomes $0.50 \cdot 7.1\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ upward. The angle $\alpha$ with the floor satisfies
$$
\tan \alpha = \frac{e\,v \sin 45^\circ}{v \cos 45^\circ} = e = 0.50
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\alpha \approx 27^\circ .
$$
The ball therefore leaves at a shallower angle to the floor than the one at which it arrived.
