A gnat is squashed against the windshield of a moving car. Which statement about the collision is true?

- The force on the gnat has a greater magnitude than the force on the car
- The change in velocity of the car equals that of the gnat
- The impulse received by the car has the same magnitude as the impulse received by the gnat
- The car receives no impulse, because its velocity does not change

## Explanation

By **Newton's third law**, at every instant of the collision the force of the windshield on the gnat and that of the gnat on the windshield have the same magnitude and opposite directions. The two forces act for the same time interval, so their impulses have the same magnitude and opposite directions. By the impulse-momentum theorem the changes in momentum also have the same magnitude; since the mass of the car is enormously larger, its change in velocity is tiny, but not zero.
