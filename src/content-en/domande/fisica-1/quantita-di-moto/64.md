A block of mass $1.98\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest on a smooth horizontal plane and is attached to an ideal spring of spring constant $800\,\mathrm{N/m}$, relaxed, with the other end fixed to a wall. A bullet of mass $20\,\mathrm{g}$, with velocity $300\,\mathrm{m/s}$ directed along the axis of the spring, embeds itself in the block. What is the maximum compression of the spring?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.11\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The collision lasts a very short time, during which the spring is not deformed and exerts no impulsive forces; so momentum is conserved and
$$
V = \frac{m v}{m + M} = \frac{0.020 \cdot 300}{2.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
After the collision the only force doing work is the elastic force, which is conservative, therefore the kinetic energy of the block with the bullet turns into elastic energy: $\dfrac{1}{2}(m + M) V^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} k x^2$. It follows that
$$
x = V \sqrt{\frac{m + M}{k}} = 3.0 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2.0}{800}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.15\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ would be obtained by using the kinetic energy of the bullet, which instead is almost entirely dissipated in the collision.
