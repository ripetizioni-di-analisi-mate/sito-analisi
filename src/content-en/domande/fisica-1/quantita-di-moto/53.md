A diver leaves the platform and, during the flight, performs a series of somersaults while bending and stretching the body. Neglecting air resistance, what trajectory does the diver's center of mass follow until entering the water?

- A spiral curve, because the body rotates
- An arc of a parabola only if the body stays rigid
- A vertical segment
- An arc of a parabola, whatever the motion of the body

## Explanation

During the flight the only external force on the diver is the total weight $M\vec{g}$, while the forces with which the diver bends and stretches the body are internal. By the **theorem of the motion of the center of mass** we have $M\,\vec{a}_{cm} = M\vec{g}$, so the center of mass moves with constant acceleration $\vec{g}$, like a projectile. Therefore the center of mass follows the arc of a parabola determined by the velocity at take-off, regardless of the somersaults and of the changes in the shape of the body.
