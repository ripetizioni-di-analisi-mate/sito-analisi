A rocket is in space, far from any celestial body. The engine is switched on and the gases leave the nozzle toward the left relative to the rocket. In what direction does the thrust on the rocket point?

- Toward the left
- Perpendicular to the direction in which the gases leave
- It has no direction, because in vacuum there is no air for the gases to push on
- Toward the right

## Explanation

In the **equation of variable-mass systems** the thrust is $\vec{u}\,\dfrac{dM}{dt}$, where $\vec{u}$ is the velocity of the gases relative to the rocket. The rocket loses mass, so $\dfrac{dM}{dt} < 0$, and the thrust points opposite to $\vec{u}$; since the gases leave toward the left, the thrust points to the right. The thrust comes from the exchange of momentum between the rocket and the expelled gases, which are part of the system, and for this reason it does not require air.
