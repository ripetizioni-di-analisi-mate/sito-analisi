A rocket in space, with no external forces, travels at $4.0\,\mathrm{km/s}$ relative to an inertial frame and expels gas backward with velocity $2.5\,\mathrm{km/s}$ relative to the rocket. Which statement is true?

- The thrust is zero, because in the inertial frame the gases move forward
- The thrust points backward
- In the inertial frame the gases move in the same direction as the rocket, and the thrust still points forward
- The rocket can no longer accelerate, because its speed exceeds the exhaust speed

## Explanation

In the inertial frame the gases have velocity $4.0 - 2.5 = 1.5\,\mathrm{km/s}$ forward, so they move in the same direction as the rocket. The **equation of variable-mass systems**, however, contains the velocity of the gases *relative to the rocket*, $\vec{u}$, which points backward; since $\dfrac{dM}{dt} < 0$, the thrust $\vec{u}\,\dfrac{dM}{dt}$ points forward. Therefore the rocket keeps accelerating, and the rocket equation holds whatever the initial velocity $v_i$.
