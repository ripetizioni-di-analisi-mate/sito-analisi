On a smooth horizontal plane two pucks, whose masses and initial velocities are known, collide with non-aligned velocities and remain joined. How many scalar equations does conservation of momentum provide, and how many unknowns does the problem have?

- One equation and two unknowns
- Two equations and two unknowns, the components of the common velocity
- Two equations and four unknowns
- Three equations and four unknowns

## Explanation

In the plane conservation of momentum is an equality between vectors, so it is equivalent to two scalar equations, one for each component. The pucks remain joined, therefore after the collision they have the same velocity $\vec{V}$, and the unknowns are the two components $V_x$ and $V_y$. The system of two equations in two unknowns determines $\vec{V}$ without using energy, which is not conserved in this collision. Four unknowns and three equations are instead the case of the elastic collision in the plane, in which the bodies separate.
