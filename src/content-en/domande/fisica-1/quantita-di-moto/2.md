A body of mass $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, initially at rest on a smooth horizontal plane, is pushed by a horizontal force of fixed direction and magnitude $F(t) = 6.0\,t$, with $F$ in newtons and $t$ in seconds, for $0 \leq t \leq 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the speed of the body at the instant $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The weight and the normal force of the plane are vertical and balance each other, so the net force is the horizontal force $F(t)$. The impulse of a variable force is the integral over time:
$$
J = \int_0^{2.0} 6.0\,t\,dt\ \mathrm{N\cdot s} = 3.0\,t^2 \Big|_0^{2.0}\ \mathrm{N\cdot s} = 12\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} .
$$
The body starts from rest, therefore by the **impulse-momentum theorem** $m v = J$ and so $v = \dfrac{12}{4.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The impulse is the area of the triangle with base $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $12\,\mathrm{N}$ under the graph of $F(t)$, that is, half the product $F(2.0\,\mathrm{s}) \cdot \Delta t$.
