A body at rest explodes into three fragments; the external forces during the explosion are neglected. A fragment of $1{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ leaves with velocity $3{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the positive direction of the $x$ axis. A second fragment of $1{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ leaves with velocity $4{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the positive direction of the $y$ axis. What is the speed of the third fragment, of mass $2{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$?

- $3{,}5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0{,}50\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2{,}5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The forces of the explosion are internal, so the total momentum stays zero. Therefore the third fragment has momentum opposite to the sum of the other two, $\vec{p}_3 = -(3{,}0\,\hat{\imath} + 4{,}0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The two components are perpendicular, so the magnitude is $p_3 = \sqrt{3{,}0^2 + 4{,}0^2}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. Dividing by the mass one obtains $v_3 = \dfrac{5{,}0}{2{,}0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2{,}5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
