A projectile is launched from horizontal ground and, neglecting air resistance, would land $120\,\mathrm{m}$ from the launch point. At the highest point it explodes into two fragments. The fragment of mass $m$ is at rest just after the explosion and falls vertically, whereas the fragment of mass $2m$ continues moving. At what distance from the launch point does the fragment of mass $2m$ land?

- $150\,\mathrm{m}$
- $180\,\mathrm{m}$
- $240\,\mathrm{m}$
- $120\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The forces of the explosion are internal and the weight is not impulsive, so just after the explosion the momentum is still horizontal. The light fragment is at rest, therefore the heavy fragment also has a horizontal velocity, and the two fragments, which fall from the same height with zero vertical velocity, land at the same instant. At that instant the center of mass, which follows the original parabola, is at $120\,\mathrm{m}$, and the light fragment is at $60\,\mathrm{m}$, below the highest point. From the definition of the center of mass one obtains
$$
120\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{m \cdot 60\,\mathrm{m} + 2m \cdot x}{3m},
\qquad
x = \frac{360 - 60}{2}\,\mathrm{m} = 150\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
