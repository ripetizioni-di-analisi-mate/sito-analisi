Sand falls vertically, at a rate of $30\,\mathrm{kg}$ per second, onto a horizontal conveyor belt moving at a constant speed of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the sand immediately comes to rest relative to the belt. Neglecting friction in the rollers, what horizontal force must the motor exert on the belt to keep its speed constant?

- $0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $60\,\mathrm{N}$
- $30\,\mathrm{N}$
- $15\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The system formed by the belt and the sand lying on it gains mass at the rate $\dfrac{dM}{dt} = 30\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. The sand arrives with zero horizontal component of velocity, so its velocity relative to the belt is $\vec{u} = -\vec{v}$. By the **equation of variable-mass systems**, along the direction of motion $M\,\dfrac{dv}{dt} = F - v\,\dfrac{dM}{dt}$. The speed is constant, therefore the left-hand side is zero and
$$
F = v\,\frac{dM}{dt} = 2.0 \cdot 30\,\mathrm{N} = 60\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $30\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by equating the power of the motor to the increase in the kinetic energy of the sand, which however is only half of that power, because the other half is dissipated while the sand slides on the belt before coming to rest.
