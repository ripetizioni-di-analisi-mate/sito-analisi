A thin uniform ring has the shape of a circle of radius $R$. Where is its center of mass?

- At any point of the ring, because all its points are equivalent
- At the center of the circle, where there is no material
- The center of mass does not exist, because the center of the circle does not belong to the ring
- At the lowest point of the ring

## Explanation

The ring is uniform and the center of the circle is a center of symmetry of the ring. With the origin at the center, each mass element $dm$ at position $\vec{r}$ has an equal element at position $-\vec{r}$, so the contributions of the two elements to the integral $\displaystyle\int \vec{r}\,dm$ add up to zero. Therefore $\vec{r}_{cm} = \vec{0}$, that is, the center of mass is the center of the circle. The center of mass is defined for every mass distribution and need not belong to the body: in this case it is a point where there is no material.
