A rocket in space ejects gas with a speed of $2.0\,\mathrm{km/s}$ relative to the rocket, at a rate of $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ per second. What is the magnitude of the thrust?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $40\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the **equation of variable-mass systems** the thrust is the term $\vec{u}\,\dfrac{dM}{dt}$, of magnitude $u\,\left|\dfrac{dM}{dt}\right|$. The relative speed must be expressed in meters per second, $u = 2.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the thrust has magnitude $2.0 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 50\,\mathrm{N} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{N}$. The gases leave backward and the mass of the rocket decreases, therefore the thrust points forward.
