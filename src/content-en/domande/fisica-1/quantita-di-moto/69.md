Three carts $A$, $B$ and $C$, of masses $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, are lined up on a smooth horizontal rail; $B$ and $C$ are at rest. Cart $A$ arrives at $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, couples to $B$, and then the pair couples to $C$. How much kinetic energy is dissipated in total in the two collisions?

- $12\,\mathrm{J}$
- $36\,\mathrm{J}$
- $18\,\mathrm{J}$
- $24\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In both collisions momentum is conserved, and it always equals $2.0 \cdot 6.0 = 12\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. At the end the three carts move together, so the final velocity is $\dfrac{12}{6.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, whatever the order of the couplings. The kinetic energy goes from $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 6.0^2 = 36\,\mathrm{J}$ to $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 6.0 \cdot 2.0^2 = 12\,\mathrm{J}$, therefore the dissipated energy is
$$
36\,\mathrm{J} - 12\,\mathrm{J} = 24\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Each collision dissipates $12\,\mathrm{J}$: after the first coupling the velocity is $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the kinetic energy is $24\,\mathrm{J}$.
