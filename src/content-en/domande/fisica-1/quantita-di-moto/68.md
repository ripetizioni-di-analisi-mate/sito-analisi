A block starts from rest at a height of $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and slides down a smooth ramp. At the bottom, on a smooth horizontal stretch, it collides with an identical block at rest and the two remain joined; then they move together up a second smooth ramp. To what height do they rise?

- $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

During the descent mechanical energy is conserved, so at the bottom of the ramp the block has speed $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$. In the perfectly inelastic collision with a block of equal mass momentum is conserved, therefore $m v = 2 m V$ and $V = \dfrac{v}{2}$. During the ascent mechanical energy is again conserved, so
$$
h' = \frac{V^2}{2g} = \frac{1}{4}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{h}{4} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The height is one quarter of the initial one because the collision dissipates half of the kinetic energy and the other half is shared by a double mass.
