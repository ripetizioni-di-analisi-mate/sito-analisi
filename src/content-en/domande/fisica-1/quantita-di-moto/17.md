A stone of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is dropped from rest and falls freely for $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ without hitting the ground. Neglecting air resistance, what is the impulse of the weight during this interval?

- $4.9\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ downward
- $20\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ downward
- $9.8\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ downward
- $9.8\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ upward

## Explanation

The weight is a constant force of magnitude $mg$ pointing downward, so its impulse over the interval $\Delta t$ is $\vec{J} = m\vec{g}\,\Delta t$, pointing downward. Its magnitude is
$$
J = m g\,\Delta t = 0.50 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} \approx 9.8\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} .
$$
The weight is the only force acting on the stone, therefore by the **impulse-momentum theorem** the same value is the change in momentum: after $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the stone has speed $g\,\Delta t \approx 19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and momentum $0.50 \cdot 19.6\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 9.8\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ downward.
