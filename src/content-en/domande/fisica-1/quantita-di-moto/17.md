A force of fixed direction acts on a ball for $4{,}0\,\mathrm{ms}$. Its magnitude increases linearly from $0$ to $400\,\mathrm{N}$ in the first $2{,}0\,\mathrm{ms}$ and returns linearly to $0$ in the next $2{,}0\,\mathrm{ms}$. What is the magnitude of the impulse of the force?

- $1{,}6\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $0{,}40\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $0{,}80\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $8{,}0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$

## Explanation

The force has fixed direction, so the magnitude of the impulse is the area under the graph of $F(t)$. The graph is a triangle with base $4{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $400\,\mathrm{N}$, therefore
$$
J = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 400\,\mathrm{N} = 0{,}80\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} .
$$
The average force over the interval is $\dfrac{J}{\Delta t} = 200\,\mathrm{N}$, half the maximum value.
