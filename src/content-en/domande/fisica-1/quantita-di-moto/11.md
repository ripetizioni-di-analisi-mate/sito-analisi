On a smooth horizontal plane a disk of mass $0{,}20\,\mathrm{kg}$ with velocity $3{,}0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ collides with a disk of mass $0{,}30\,\mathrm{kg}$ with velocity $2{,}0\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the two stick together. What is the magnitude of the common velocity after the collision?

- $2{,}4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1{,}2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0{,}85\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1{,}7\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The external forces are vertical, so momentum in the plane is conserved, component by component. The total momentum is $\vec{P} = (0{,}60\,\hat{\imath} + 0{,}60\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$, therefore the common velocity is
$$
\vec{V} = \frac{\vec{P}}{m_1 + m_2} = (1{,}2\,\hat{\imath} + 1{,}2\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The two components are perpendicular, so the magnitude is computed with the Pythagorean theorem, $V = 1{,}2\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1{,}7\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the velocity makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the $x$ axis.
