A rod of length $L = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ lies on the $x$ axis between $0$ and $L$ and has linear density $\lambda(x) = k x^2$, with $k > 0$. What is the coordinate $x_{cm}$ of its center of mass?

- $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

For a rod with linear density $\lambda(x)$ the center of mass is the ratio of $\displaystyle\int_0^L x\,\lambda(x)\,dx$ to the mass $\displaystyle\int_0^L \lambda(x)\,dx$. In this case
$$
\int_0^L x \cdot k x^2\,dx = \frac{k L^4}{4},
\qquad
\int_0^L k x^2\,dx = \frac{k L^3}{3} ,
$$
so $x_{cm} = \dfrac{3L}{4} = \dfrac{3 \cdot 1.2}{4}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$. The center of mass is closer to the end $x = L$, where the rod is denser. The value $\dfrac{2L}{3} = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds instead to the density $\lambda(x) = k x$.
