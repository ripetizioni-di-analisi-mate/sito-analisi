A ball of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ is launched from level ground with a velocity of magnitude $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, inclined at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal, and falls back to the ground at the same height. Neglecting air resistance, what is the change in the momentum of the ball between the launch and the arrival at the ground?

- $0$, because the ball lands with a velocity of the same magnitude
- $2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ downward
- $3.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ downward
- $4.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ downward

## Explanation

During the flight the only force is the weight, which is vertical, so the horizontal component of the velocity does not change. The ball lands at the launch height, therefore by conservation of mechanical energy it arrives with a velocity of the same magnitude, and the vertical component goes from $v \sin 30^\circ$ upward to $v \sin 30^\circ$ downward. It follows that
$$
|\Delta \vec{p}| = 2\,m v \sin 30^\circ = 2 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 10 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} ,
$$
with $\Delta \vec{p}$ directed downward. By the **impulse-momentum theorem** the same value is the impulse of the weight $m g\,T$ over the time of flight $T = \dfrac{2 v \sin 30^\circ}{g}$. Momentum is a vector, so its change is not zero even though the initial and final magnitudes are equal.
