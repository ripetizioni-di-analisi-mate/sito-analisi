A ping-pong ball collides head-on and elastically with a bowling ball at rest, of much larger mass. Which statement about the motion just after the collision is true?

- The ping-pong ball stops and the bowling ball moves off with the initial velocity of the ping-pong ball
- The ping-pong ball bounces back with a speed almost equal to the initial one and the bowling ball stays almost at rest
- The ping-pong ball and the bowling ball move on together with almost zero velocity
- The bowling ball moves off with almost twice the initial velocity of the ping-pong ball

## Explanation

With the second body at rest, the **elastic collision** gives $v_1' = \dfrac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1$ and $v_2' = \dfrac{2 m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1$. For $m_2$ much larger than $m_1$ the first ratio is close to $-1$ and the second is close to $0$. So the ping-pong ball bounces backward with a speed almost equal to the initial one, and the bowling ball stays almost at rest, as happens for a ball hitting a wall.
