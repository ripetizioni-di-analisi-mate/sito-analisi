Two disks of different masses collide elastically on a smooth horizontal plane, and the collision is not head-on. Which statement about the velocities of the two disks in the center-of-mass frame, right after the collision, is true?

- They are zero, because in the center-of-mass frame the total momentum is zero
- They are equal to the velocities the disks had before the collision in the same frame
- They have the same magnitudes as before the collision and opposite directions to each other, but their direction can change
- They have equal magnitudes, whatever the masses

## Explanation

In the **center-of-mass frame** the total momentum is zero before and after the collision, so $m_1 \vec{v}_1^{\,*} = -m_2 \vec{v}_2^{\,*}$: the two momenta have the same magnitude $p^{*}$ and opposite directions. Therefore the kinetic energy in the center-of-mass frame is
$$
K^{*} = \frac{p^{*2}}{2 m_1} + \frac{p^{*2}}{2 m_2} = \frac{p^{*2}}{2\mu} .
$$
The collision is elastic, so $K$ is conserved, and $\vec{v}_{cm}$ does not change; hence by **König's theorem** $K^{*}$ is conserved as well. It follows that $p^{*}$ has the same value before and after the collision, and each disk keeps the magnitude of its velocity. The direction of the line along which the disks move after the collision depends on the point where they touch, therefore in a collision that is not head-on it changes; the magnitudes of the two velocities are equal to each other only if the masses are equal.
