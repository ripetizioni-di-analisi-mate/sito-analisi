A thin uniform wire is bent at a right angle as in the figure: one segment goes from $(0,\ 0)$ to $(0.60,\ 0)$ and the other from $(0,\ 0)$ to $(0,\ 0.30)$, with coordinates in meters. What are the coordinates of the center of mass of the wire?

::grafico{linee="0.6,0 0,0 0,0.3" punti="0,0,pieno;0.6,0,pieno;0,0.3,pieno" etichette="0.26,-0.06,60 cm;-0.1,0.16,30 cm" xmin=-0.15 xmax=0.75 ymin=-0.12 ymax=0.42 uguale=1 titolo="Uniform wire bent at a right angle: a $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ segment along the $x$ axis and a $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ segment along the $y$ axis (coordinates in meters)"}

- $(0.15,\ 0.075)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(0.20,\ 0.050)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(0.10,\ 0.10)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(0.30,\ 0.15)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The wire is uniform, so the mass of each segment is proportional to its length, and the center of mass of each segment is its midpoint: $(0.30,\ 0)$ for the $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ segment and $(0,\ 0.15)$ for the $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ one. By the **center of mass of a composite body**, with lengths in place of masses,
$$
x_{cm} = \frac{0.60 \cdot 0.30 + 0.30 \cdot 0}{0.90}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
y_{cm} = \frac{0.60 \cdot 0 + 0.30 \cdot 0.15}{0.90}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.050\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The center of mass does not lie on the wire: it is inside the angle formed by the two segments, closer to the longer segment.
