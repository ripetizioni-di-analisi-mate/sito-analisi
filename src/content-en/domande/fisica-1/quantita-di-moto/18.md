In an accident a person of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ goes from $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $0$. Without an airbag the stop lasts $0.050\,\mathrm{s}$, with the airbag it lasts $0.15\,\mathrm{s}$. How does the average force on the person change with the airbag?

- It stays the same, because the change in momentum is the same
- It becomes one third
- It becomes three times larger
- It becomes one ninth

## Explanation

In both cases the change in momentum is the same, $|\Delta p| = 70 \cdot 15\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} = 1050\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The average force is $F_m = \dfrac{|\Delta p|}{\Delta t}$, so for the same $\Delta p$ it is inversely proportional to the duration of the stop. The duration triples, therefore the average force **becomes one third**, from $2.1 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N}$ to $7.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$.
