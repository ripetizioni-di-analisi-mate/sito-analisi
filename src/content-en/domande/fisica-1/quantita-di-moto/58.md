Two skaters initially at rest push apart on frictionless ice. The first skater has mass $50\,\mathrm{kg}$. Given the first skater's speed $u = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the second skater's speed $v = 2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the second skater's mass $m$?

- $150\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $75\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $25\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{100}{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Total momentum remains zero. The final momenta are therefore equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. In the stated units, the relation is $v = \dfrac{50 \cdot u}{m}$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{50 \cdot u}{v}$. Substituting the given values yields $75\,\mathrm{kg}$.
