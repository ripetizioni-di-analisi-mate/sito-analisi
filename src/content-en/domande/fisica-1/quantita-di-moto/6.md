A person of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands at rest on a raft of mass $240\,\mathrm{kg}$, at rest in calm water. The person walks in a straight line for $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the raft and stops. Neglecting the horizontal resistance of the water, how far does the raft move relative to the water?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ in the same direction as the person
- $1.3\,\mathrm{m}$ opposite to the person
- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ opposite to the person
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ opposite to the person

## Explanation

The external forces on the system formed by the person and the raft are vertical and the system starts from rest, so the center of mass stays at rest horizontally. If the raft moves by $d$ in one direction, the person moves by $5.0\,\mathrm{m} - d$ relative to the water in the opposite direction. The position of the center of mass does not change if $60\,(5.0 - d) - 240\,d = 0$, therefore
$$
d = \frac{60 \cdot 5.0}{60 + 240}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The raft therefore moves $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ opposite to the person, who covers $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the water.
