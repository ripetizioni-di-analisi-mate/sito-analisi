Two spheres hang from the same point on strings of the same length and, at equilibrium, touch each other. The sphere of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pulled aside and released from rest at a height of $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ above the equilibrium position; at the lowest point it collides elastically and head-on with the sphere at rest, of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. Neglecting air resistance, to what height does the $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ sphere rise after the collision?

- $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.050\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

During the descent mechanical energy is conserved, so the light sphere reaches the collision with speed $v = \sqrt{2 g h} \approx 2.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$. During the collision the strings are vertical, therefore horizontal momentum is conserved, and for the **one-dimensional elastic collision** with the target at rest
$$
v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v = -\frac{v}{3} .
$$
The light sphere moves back with one third of its speed, and during the rise mechanical energy is conserved again, so
$$
h' = \frac{v_1'^2}{2g} = \frac{1}{9}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{h}{9} = 0.050\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ sphere starts with $v_2' = \dfrac{2}{3}\,v$ and rises to $\dfrac{4}{9}\,h = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$.
