A body collides head-on and elastically with a body at rest. For what mass of the body at rest does the first body give it all its kinetic energy?

- Mass equal to that of the first body
- Mass much greater than that of the first body
- Mass much smaller than that of the first body
- Mass twice that of the first body

## Explanation

With the target at rest, the velocity of the first body after the elastic collision is $v_1' = \dfrac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1$. The first body gives up all its kinetic energy if it stops, that is if $v_1' = 0$, and this happens if and only if $m_2 = m_1$. In that case the target leaves with velocity $v_1$, as in the balls of Newton's cradle. With a much heavier target the first body bounces back with almost the same energy, with a much lighter one it continues almost undisturbed.
