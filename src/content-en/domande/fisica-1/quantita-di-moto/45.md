A rocket in space, with no external forces, starts from rest and ejects gas with a relative velocity of constant magnitude. With a ratio of initial to final mass $\dfrac{M_i}{M_f} = 3$ it reaches the speed $v$. How does the final speed change if, with the same ejection speed, the mass ratio becomes $9$?

- It triples
- It becomes nine times larger
- It stays the same
- It doubles

## Explanation

By the **rocket equation** with $v_i = 0$ the final speed is $v_f = u \ln \dfrac{M_i}{M_f}$. With the ratio $3$ one has $v = u \ln 3$. Since $9 = 3^2$, with the ratio $9$ one has $u \ln 9 = 2\,u \ln 3 = 2v$. Therefore the final speed **doubles**, and to double it the mass ratio must go from $3$ to its square.
