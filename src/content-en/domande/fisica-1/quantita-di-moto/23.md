A circular hole of radius $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, internally tangent to the edge, is cut out of a uniform disk of radius $R = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. At what distance from the center of the disk is the center of mass of the remaining part, and on which side?

- $0.050\,\mathrm{m}$, on the side of the hole
- $0.050\,\mathrm{m}$, on the side opposite the hole
- $0.038\,\mathrm{m}$, on the side opposite the hole
- $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, on the side opposite the hole

## Explanation

The masses of the parts of a uniform plate are proportional to their areas. The hole has radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$, so its area is one quarter of that of the disk. Therefore, if $M$ is the mass of the whole disk, the hole has mass $\dfrac{M}{4}$ and the remaining part $\dfrac{3M}{4}$. The $x$ axis is oriented from the center of the disk toward the center of the hole, which is at $x = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$. The whole disk is made of the remaining part and the hole, and its center of mass is the origin, so
$$
0 = \frac{3M}{4}\,x + \frac{M}{4} \cdot 0.15\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x = -\frac{0.15}{3}\,\mathrm{m} = -0.050\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The minus sign indicates that the center of mass is on the side opposite the hole.
