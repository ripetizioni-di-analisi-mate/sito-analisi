On a billiard table a moving ball collides with an identical ball at rest. After the collision both balls move and their velocities make an angle of $70^\circ$. Treating the balls as particles and neglecting friction during the collision, which statement is true?

- The collision is elastic
- The collision is perfectly inelastic
- The final kinetic energy is greater than the initial one
- The collision is inelastic and the final kinetic energy is less than the initial one

## Explanation

The masses are equal, so dividing the conservation of momentum by $m$ gives $\vec{v}_1 = \vec{v}_1' + \vec{v}_2'$. Taking the dot product of this relation with itself gives
$$
v_1^2 = v_1'^2 + v_2'^2 + 2\,v_1' v_2' \cos 70^\circ .
$$
Since $\cos 70^\circ > 0$, we get $v_1^2 > v_1'^2 + v_2'^2$, that is, the final kinetic energy $\dfrac{1}{2} m\,(v_1'^2 + v_2'^2)$ is less than the initial one. The collision is not elastic, because in an elastic collision between equal masses with the target at rest the final velocities are perpendicular; nor is it perfectly inelastic, because the balls separate. Therefore the collision is **inelastic**.
