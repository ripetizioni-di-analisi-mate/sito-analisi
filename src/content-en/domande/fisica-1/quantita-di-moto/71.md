In a nuclear reactor a neutron collides head-on and elastically with a carbon nucleus at rest, whose mass is $12$ times that of the neutron. What fraction of its initial kinetic energy does the neutron keep after the collision?

- $0.72$
- $0.85$
- $0.28$
- $0.92$

## Explanation

For the one-dimensional elastic collision with the target at rest, the velocity of the neutron after the collision is
$$
v_1' = \frac{m - 12 m}{m + 12 m}\,v_1 = -\frac{11}{13}\,v_1 .
$$
The kinetic energy is proportional to the square of the velocity, so the fraction left to the neutron is
$$
\frac{K_1'}{K_1} = \left(\frac{11}{13}\right)^2 = \frac{121}{169} \approx 0.72 .
$$
The carbon nucleus receives the rest, about $28\,\%$; therefore after a series of collisions the neutron is slowed down.
