A cart of mass $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, at rest, carries at its front an ideal spring bumper of spring constant $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and negligible mass. A second cart, of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, runs into it at $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along a smooth horizontal rail, and the collision is elastic. What is the maximum compression of the spring during the collision?

- $0.23\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At the instant of maximum compression the distance between the carts stops decreasing, so the two carts have the same velocity, which by conservation of momentum is
$$
V = \frac{1.0 \cdot 4.0}{4.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
At that instant the kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 1.0^2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{J}$, while at the start it was $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 4.0^2 = 8.0\,\mathrm{J}$. The spring is ideal, so the difference of $6.0\,\mathrm{J}$ is stored as elastic energy, and
$$
\frac{1}{2} k x^2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{J}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 6.0}{300}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The maximum elastic energy is the kinetic energy in the center-of-mass frame, because the term $\dfrac{1}{2} M v_{cm}^2$ cannot change.
