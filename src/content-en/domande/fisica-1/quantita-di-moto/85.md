A rocket of mass $2.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands vertically at rest on the launch pad. The gases leave downward with velocity $3.0\,\mathrm{km/s}$ relative to the rocket. What mass of gas must it expel per second, at least, for the rocket to lift off?

- $67\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

By the **equation of variable-mass systems**, the thrust points upward and has magnitude $u\,\left|\dfrac{dM}{dt}\right|$. The rocket lifts off if the thrust exceeds the weight $M g$, so the minimum mass flow rate follows from the equality
$$
u\,\left|\frac{dM}{dt}\right| = M g
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\left|\frac{dM}{dt}\right| = \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{5} \cdot 9.81}{3.0 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{kg/s} \approx 6.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{kg/s} .
$$
The exhaust speed must be written in meters per second, because the thrust must come out in newtons.
