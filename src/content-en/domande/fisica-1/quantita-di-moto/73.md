A tennis ball rests on top of a basketball of much greater mass, and the two are dropped together from a height of $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The basketball bounces elastically on the floor and immediately afterward collides elastically with the tennis ball, which is still moving down. Neglecting air and the sizes of the bodies, to what height does the tennis ball rise?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Both bodies reach the bottom with speed $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$. The basketball bounces elastically on the floor and goes back up with speed $v$, while the tennis ball is still moving down with speed $v$. In the frame of the basketball, whose velocity does not change in the collision because of its much greater mass, the tennis ball arrives at $2v$ and bounces back at $2v$; relative to the ground its upward velocity is therefore $2v + v = 3v$. The height reached is then
$$
h' = \frac{(3v)^2}{2g} = 9\,h = 9.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
This is the case of a much heavier target moving toward the light body: the light body bounces back with its relative velocity reversed.
