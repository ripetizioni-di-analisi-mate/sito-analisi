Two carts, $A$ of mass $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $B$ of mass $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$, are held at rest on a smooth horizontal rail $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ apart and are connected by a stretched elastic band of negligible mass. When they are released, the band pulls them together until they touch. Treating the carts as particles, at what point do they touch?

- $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ from the initial position of $A$
- $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ from the initial position of $A$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the initial position of $A$
- At the initial position of $A$

## Explanation

The force of the elastic band is internal to the system of the two carts, while the external forces, that is the weights and the normal forces of the rail, are vertical. By the **theorem of the motion of the center of mass** the center of mass has zero horizontal acceleration, and since it starts at rest it stays at rest. With the $x$ axis from $A$ toward $B$ and the origin at $A$, its position is
$$
x_{cm} = \frac{0.80 \cdot 0 + 0.40 \cdot 0.90}{1.20}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.30\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
When the carts touch they are at the same point, which must be the center of mass; therefore they touch $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the initial position of $A$, the heavier cart.
