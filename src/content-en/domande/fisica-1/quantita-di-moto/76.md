Along a smooth straight horizontal track a cart with velocity $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ catches up with a cart moving ahead of it at $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the same direction, and collides with it elastically. The masses are not known. Right after the collision the first cart moves at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the same direction. What is the velocity of the second cart right after the collision?

- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- It cannot be calculated without knowing the masses

## Explanation

In a one-dimensional **elastic collision** the relative velocity after the collision is the opposite of the one before the collision, that is $v_2' - v_1' = -(v_2 - v_1)$, and this relation does not contain the masses. Before the collision $v_2 - v_1 = (1.0 - 4.0)\,\mathrm{m/s} = -3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
v_2' = v_1' + 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = (2.0 + 3.0)\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Conservation of momentum, $4.0\,m_1 + 1.0\,m_2 = 2.0\,m_1 + 5.0\,m_2$, then gives $m_1 = 2\,m_2$; the masses follow from the data and are not needed to calculate $v_2'$.
