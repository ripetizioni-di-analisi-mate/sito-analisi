On a smooth horizontal track a cart of $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a cart of $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the opposite direction collide head-on and stick together. How much kinetic energy is dissipated in the collision?

- $12\,\mathrm{J}$
- $24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $36\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

With the axis oriented in the direction of the first cart, the velocities are $v_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2 = -3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. By conservation of momentum the common velocity is $V = \dfrac{6.0 - 3.0}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The kinetic energy goes from $K_i = (9.0 + 4.5)\,\mathrm{J} = 13.5\,\mathrm{J}$ to $K_f = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 1.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 1.5\,\mathrm{J}$, so $12\,\mathrm{J}$ are dissipated. The same value is obtained from the formula of the **perfectly inelastic collision**:
$$
|\Delta K| = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,(v_1 - v_2)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2.0}{3.0} \cdot 6.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 12\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
