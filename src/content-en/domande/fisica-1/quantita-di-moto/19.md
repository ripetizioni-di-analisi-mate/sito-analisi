A ball of mass $0{,}40\,\mathrm{kg}$ hits a smooth wall with a speed of $5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with the velocity at an angle of $60^\circ$ to the normal to the wall, and bounces back with a velocity of the same magnitude, at $60^\circ$ from the normal on the other side. What is the magnitude of the impulse received by the ball?

- $3{,}5\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $1{,}0\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $4{,}0\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $2{,}0\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$

## Explanation

The $x$ axis is chosen perpendicular to the wall, pointing outward, and the $y$ axis parallel to the wall. The component $v_y = v \sin 60^\circ$ has the same value before and after the collision, so it does not contribute to the change in momentum. The component $v_x$ goes from $-v \cos 60^\circ$ to $v \cos 60^\circ$, therefore by the **impulse-momentum theorem**
$$
J = 2\,m v \cos 60^\circ = 2 \cdot 0{,}40 \cdot 5{,}0 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} .
$$
The impulse is perpendicular to the wall and points outward.
