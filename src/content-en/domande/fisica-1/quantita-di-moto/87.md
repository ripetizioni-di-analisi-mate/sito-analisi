A cart full of sand slides on smooth horizontal rails at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Through a hole in the bottom the sand falls out vertically relative to the cart, that is with zero horizontal relative velocity. How does the speed of the cart change while the sand leaks out?

- It increases, because the mass of the cart decreases
- It stays $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- It decreases, because the sand carries momentum away
- It increases until the sand runs out, then stays constant

## Explanation

By the **equation of variable-mass systems**, along the horizontal $M\,\dfrac{dv}{dt} = F_{\mathrm{ext},x} + u_x\,\dfrac{dM}{dt}$. The external forces are vertical, so $F_{\mathrm{ext},x} = 0$, and the sand leaves with zero horizontal relative velocity, so $u_x = 0$. Therefore $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = 0$ and the cart keeps moving at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The sand that leaves carries away its own share of momentum, $dM\,v$, and the velocity of the rest of the cart does not change.
