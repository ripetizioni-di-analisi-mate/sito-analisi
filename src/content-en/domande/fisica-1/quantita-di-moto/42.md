Two identical cars of mass $m$, with speeds $v$ in opposite directions, collide head-on and remain joined and at rest. The dissipated energy is compared with that of a single car that hits a rigid wall at speed $v$ and stops. Which statement is true?

- Each car dissipates $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$, as in the collision with the wall at speed $v$
- Each car dissipates four times the energy of the collision with the wall at speed $v$, because the relative speed is $2v$
- The dissipated energy is zero, because the total momentum is zero
- Each car dissipates half the energy of the collision with the wall at speed $v$

## Explanation

In the head-on collision the total momentum is $m v - m v = 0$, so after the collision the joined cars are at rest and all the initial kinetic energy is dissipated. The total dissipated energy is therefore $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = m v^2$, and each car dissipates $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$ of it. The same result follows from the loss in a perfectly inelastic collision with the reduced mass $\mu = \dfrac{m}{2}$ and the relative speed $2v$:
$$
\Delta K = -\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{m}{2}\,(2v)^2 = -m v^2 .
$$
A car that hits the wall at speed $v$ and stops dissipates $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$, so the energy dissipated by each car is the same in the two cases.
