Two bodies $A$ and $B$ have the same kinetic energy, and the mass of $A$ is four times that of $B$. What relation holds between the magnitudes of their momenta?

- $p_A = 4\,p_B$
- $p_A = p_B$
- $p_A = \dfrac{p_B}{2}$
- $p_A = 2\,p_B$

## Explanation

From $K = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m v^2$ and $p = m v$ one obtains $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$, that is, $p = \sqrt{2 m K}$. The kinetic energies are equal, so the momenta are in the ratio of the square roots of the masses and
$$
\frac{p_A}{p_B} = \sqrt{\frac{m_A}{m_B}} = \sqrt{4} = 2 .
$$
Body $A$ therefore has half the speed of $B$ and twice its momentum.
