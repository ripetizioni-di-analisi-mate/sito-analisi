Two bodies lie along a track. The first mass is at the origin and the second at positive coordinate $d$. The first mass is $2\,\mathrm{kg}$. Given the distance $d = 8\,\mathrm{m}$ and the center-of-mass coordinate $x = 6\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the second mass $m$?

- $6\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The center-of-mass coordinate is a mass-weighted average. The first body contributes zero to the numerator. In the stated units, the relation is $x = \dfrac{d \cdot m}{m + 2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{2 \cdot x}{d - x}$. Substituting the given values yields $6\,\mathrm{kg}$.
