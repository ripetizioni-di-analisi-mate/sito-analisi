A hockey puck of mass $0.16\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides east at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on smooth ice. A very short stroke of the stick gives it an impulse of magnitude $0.80\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ directed north. In which direction does the puck move right after the stroke?

- At $31^\circ$ north of east
- At $45^\circ$ north of east
- North, in the direction of the impulse
- At $59^\circ$ north of east

## Explanation

By the **impulse-momentum theorem** $m\,\Delta\vec{v} = \vec{J}$, and the impulse is directed north, so only the north component of the velocity changes. With the $x$ axis pointing east and the $y$ axis pointing north, the component $v_x = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ stays the same, while
$$
v_y = \frac{J}{m} = \frac{0.80}{0.16}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The angle $\alpha$ of the velocity with the east direction satisfies $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{v_y}{v_x} = \dfrac{5.0}{3.0}$, so $\alpha \approx 59^\circ$ north of east. The puck keeps its east component, therefore after the stroke its velocity does not have the direction of the impulse.
