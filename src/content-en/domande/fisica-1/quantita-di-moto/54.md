A grenade in flight explodes into several fragments. The instants just before and just after the explosion are compared, neglecting the impulse of the external forces during the explosion. By König's theorem the kinetic energy is $K = \dfrac{1}{2} M v_{cm}^2 + K^{*}$. Which statement is true?

- The term $\dfrac{1}{2} M v_{cm}^2$ stays the same and $K^{*}$ increases
- The total kinetic energy stays the same, because momentum is conserved
- The term $\dfrac{1}{2} M v_{cm}^2$ increases and $K^{*}$ stays zero
- The velocity of the center of mass increases and $K^{*}$ stays the same

## Explanation

The forces of the explosion are internal and the impulse of the external forces is negligible, so the total momentum is conserved and $\vec{v}_{cm}$ is the same just before and just after. Therefore the term $\dfrac{1}{2} M v_{cm}^2$ does not change. Before the explosion all points of the grenade have the same velocity, so $K^{*} = 0$; afterward the fragments move away from the center of mass and $K^{*} > 0$. The total kinetic energy therefore increases, at the expense of the chemical energy of the explosive, and the whole increase is in the term $K^{*}$.
