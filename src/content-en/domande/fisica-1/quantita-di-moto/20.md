A body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a smooth horizontal plane at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. For $2.5\,\mathrm{s}$ a constant force of $4.0\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on it, horizontal and opposite to the initial velocity. What is the velocity of the body at the end of the interval?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ opposite to the initial direction
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the initial direction
- $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the initial direction
- $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ opposite to the initial direction

## Explanation

The $x$ axis is oriented in the direction of the initial velocity. The force is constant, so its impulse is $J_x = F_x\,\Delta t = -4.0 \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} = -10\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$. By the **impulse-momentum theorem** $m v' = m v + J_x = (6.0 - 10)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} = -4.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$, therefore $v' = -2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The body therefore moves at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ opposite to the initial direction, because the force stops it at the instant $1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ and then accelerates it in the opposite direction.
