A rocket starts from rest in a vertical launch and expels gas with velocity $2.5\,\mathrm{km/s}$ relative to the rocket. The burn lasts $80\,\mathrm{s}$ and at the end the mass is one third of the initial mass. Taking $g$ as constant and neglecting air resistance, what is the speed of the rocket at the end of the burn?

- $2.7\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $3.5\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $4.2\,\mathrm{km/s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{km/s}$

## Explanation

For a vertical launch the term due to the weight, $-g\,t$, is added to the **rocket equation**, where $t$ is the duration of the burn. With $v_i = 0$ and $\dfrac{M_i}{M_f} = 3$ we get
$$
v_f = u \ln \frac{M_i}{M_f} - g\,t = 2500 \ln 3\,\mathrm{m/s} - 9.81 \cdot 80\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx (2747 - 785)\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.0\,\mathrm{km/s} .
$$
The weight reduces the final speed by almost $0.8\,\mathrm{km/s}$ compared with the rocket in space, and the reduction is larger the longer the burn lasts.
