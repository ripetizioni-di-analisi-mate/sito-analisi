Two blocks of masses $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, connected by a spring, lie on a smooth horizontal plane. A horizontal external force of $6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ in the positive direction of the $x$ axis acts on the first, and a horizontal external force of $2.0\,\mathrm{N}$ in the negative direction acts on the second. What is the acceleration of the center of mass?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ in the positive direction of the $x$ axis
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ in the positive direction of the $x$ axis
- It depends on the force exerted by the spring
- $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ in the positive direction of the $x$ axis

## Explanation

By the **center-of-mass theorem** $M\,\vec{a}_{cm} = \vec{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}$, where only the resultant of the external forces appears. The forces of the spring on the two blocks are internal and, by Newton's third law, opposite, so their sum is zero and the acceleration of the center of mass does not depend on them. The weights and the normal forces of the plane are vertical and balance each other, therefore $F_{\mathrm{ext},x} = (6.0 - 2.0)\,\mathrm{N} = 4.0\,\mathrm{N}$. It follows that $a_{cm} = \dfrac{4.0}{4.0}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ in the positive direction of the $x$ axis.
