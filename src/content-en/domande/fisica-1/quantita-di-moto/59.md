A wooden block of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest on a smooth horizontal plane. A $10\,\mathrm{g}$ bullet hits it horizontally at $400\,\mathrm{m/s}$, passes through it and comes out of the opposite side at $100\,\mathrm{m/s}$, in the same direction. With what speed does the block move right afterward?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

While the bullet passes through, the forces between bullet and block are internal and the external forces are vertical, so the horizontal component of momentum is conserved:
$$
m v = m v' + M V
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
V = \frac{m\,(v - v')}{M} = \frac{0.010 \cdot 300}{2.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The block receives the momentum lost by the bullet, which is $m\,(v - v')$ and not $m v$, because the bullet keeps moving after coming out.
