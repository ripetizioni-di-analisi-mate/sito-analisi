A baseball of mass $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ reaches the bat horizontally with a speed of $35\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and is hit back along the same line, in the opposite direction, at $45\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The contact lasts $1.5\,\mathrm{ms}$. Neglecting the weight during the contact, what is the magnitude of the average force exerted by the bat on the ball?

- $8.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The $x$ axis is oriented in the direction in which the ball is hit back, so the initial velocity is $v_1 = -35\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the final one is $v_2 = 45\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The weight during the contact is neglected, therefore by the **impulse-momentum theorem** the impulse of the bat equals the change in momentum:
$$
J = m\,(v_2 - v_1) = 0.15 \cdot 80\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} = 12\,\mathrm{N\cdot s} .
$$
The velocity reverses direction, so the magnitude of its change equals the sum of the two magnitudes. The average force is the ratio of the impulse to the duration of the contact, therefore
$$
F_m = \frac{12\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}}{1.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
