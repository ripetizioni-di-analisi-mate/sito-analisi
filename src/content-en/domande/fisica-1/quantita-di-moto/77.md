A light particle collides elastically with a nucleus at rest of much greater mass and is deflected by $90^\circ$, with its speed practically unchanged. In what direction does the nucleus recoil?

- Along the initial direction of the particle
- At $45^\circ$ from the initial direction of the particle, on the side opposite to the deflection of the particle
- At $45^\circ$ from the initial direction of the particle, on the same side as the deflection of the particle
- Perpendicular to the initial direction of the particle

## Explanation

Take the $x$ axis along the initial velocity of the particle and the $y$ axis in the direction in which the particle is deflected, so the momentum of the particle goes from $(m v,\ 0)$ to $(0,\ m v)$. By conservation of momentum the nucleus receives the momentum lost by the particle:
$$
\vec{p}_N = (m v,\ 0) - (0,\ m v) = (m v,\ -m v) .
$$
The two components have the same magnitude, so the nucleus recoils at $45^\circ$ from the initial direction, on the side of negative $y$, that is on the side opposite to the deflection. Its velocity is very small, because the mass of the nucleus is very large.
