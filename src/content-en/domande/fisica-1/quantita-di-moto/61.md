A cart moves on smooth horizontal rails at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and carries a spring launcher that shoots a ball vertically *relative to the cart*. Neglecting air resistance, where does the ball land?

- Behind the cart, because the cart moves on while the ball rises vertically
- In front of the cart, because the ball also keeps the velocity given by the spring
- Back in the launcher of the cart
- At the point of the rails from which it was launched

## Explanation

The forces of the launcher are internal to the system formed by the cart and the ball, and the external forces, namely the weights and the reaction of the rails, are vertical. So the horizontal component of momentum is conserved, and before the launch it equals $(m + M) \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The ball leaves vertically relative to the cart, therefore right after the launch it has the same horizontal velocity $V$ as the cart, and conservation gives $(m + M)\,V = (m + M) \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, that is $V = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. During the flight the ball is subject only to its weight and the cart only to vertical forces, so both keep the horizontal velocity of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Therefore at every instant the ball is on the vertical through the launcher and lands back in the launcher.
