A railcar of mass $3.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg}$, moving at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a horizontal track, hits a railcar at rest of mass $2.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the two stay coupled. Neglecting friction, what fraction of the initial kinetic energy is dissipated in the coupling?

- $0.60$
- $0.40$
- $0.64$
- $0.24$

## Explanation

The collision is **perfectly inelastic** and the external forces are not impulsive, so momentum is conserved and the common velocity is
$$
V = \frac{m_1 v_1}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 2.0}{5.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.2\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The ratio of the final to the initial kinetic energy is $\dfrac{(m_1 + m_2)\,V^2}{m_1 v_1^2} = \dfrac{5.0 \cdot 1.2^2}{3.0 \cdot 2.0^2} = 0.60$, where the factor $10^{4}$ of the masses cancels. Therefore the fraction $1 - 0.60 = 0.40$ is dissipated, which coincides with $\dfrac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$.
