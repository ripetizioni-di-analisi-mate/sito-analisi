A small ball is dropped from rest from a height of $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$ onto a rigid floor. The coefficient of restitution of the collision is $e = 0.50$. Neglecting air resistance, up to what height does the ball rise after the first bounce?

- $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

During the descent mechanical energy is conserved, so the ball reaches the floor with speed $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$. The floor is at rest, therefore just after the collision the ball has speed $v' = e\,v$. During the rise mechanical energy is again conserved, so
$$
h' = \frac{v'^2}{2g} = e^2 h = 0.25 \cdot 1.6\,\mathrm{m} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The height is reduced by the factor $e^2$, because kinetic energy is proportional to the square of the speed.
