A rocket in space, with no external forces, ejects gas with a speed of $3.0\,\mathrm{km/s}$ relative to the rocket. What ratio $\dfrac{M_i}{M_f}$ of initial to final mass is needed to go from rest to $6.0\,\mathrm{km/s}$?

- $2.0$
- $1.6$
- $7.4$
- $0.14$

## Explanation

By the **rocket equation** $v_f - v_i = u \ln \dfrac{M_i}{M_f}$, with $v_i = 0$. So $\ln \dfrac{M_i}{M_f} = \dfrac{6.0}{3.0} = 2$, and therefore $\dfrac{M_i}{M_f} = e^{2} \approx 7.4$. The number $2$ is the natural logarithm of the mass ratio, and the fuel is the fraction $1 - e^{-2} \approx 0.86$ of the initial mass.
