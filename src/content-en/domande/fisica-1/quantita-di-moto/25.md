Two skaters, $A$ of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $B$ of mass $40\,\mathrm{kg}$, are at rest on an ice rink with negligible friction and push each other. Which statement about the motion just after the push is true?

- $A$ and $B$ move apart with velocities of the same magnitude
- $A$ moves away with a speed $1.5$ times the speed of $B$
- $B$ moves away with a speed $1.5$ times the speed of $A$
- $A$ and $B$ have the same kinetic energy

## Explanation

The forces of the push are internal, and the external forces, namely the weights and the normal forces of the ice, are vertical. So the horizontal component of the momentum is conserved and stays zero, that is, $m_A v_A + m_B v_B = 0$. Therefore the velocities have opposite directions and magnitudes inversely proportional to the masses, with $\dfrac{v_B}{v_A} = \dfrac{60}{40} = 1.5$. The momenta have the same magnitude $p$, so the kinetic energy $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$ is larger for the lighter skater.
