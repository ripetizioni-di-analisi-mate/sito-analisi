A rocket in space, with no external forces, expels gas with a relative velocity of constant magnitude. On which quantities does the increase in the rocket's speed between ignition and engine shutdown depend?

- On how quickly the fuel is burned
- On the duration of the burn
- Only on the exhaust speed and on the ratio of the initial mass to the final mass
- Only on the mass of the fuel, whatever the mass of the empty rocket

## Explanation

By the **rocket equation** the increase in speed is
$$
v_f - v_i = u \ln \frac{M_i}{M_f} ,
$$
so it depends only on the exhaust speed $u$ and on the ratio $\dfrac{M_i}{M_f}$. The burning rate and the burn duration affect the thrust and the acceleration, but not the final speed. For the same fuel the mass of the empty rocket also matters, because it enters $M_f$ and therefore the ratio.
