A ball falls toward the Earth, starting from rest. Neglecting air resistance and the interactions with all other bodies, for which system is the total momentum conserved during the fall?

- The ball alone
- The Earth alone
- No system, because the weight acts
- The system formed by the ball and the Earth

## Explanation

The momentum of the ball increases, because the ball is acted on by its weight, which for the ball is an external force. By the third law the ball attracts the Earth with an equal and opposite force, so the momentum of the Earth changes as well. If the system includes the ball and the Earth, the two forces are internal and the resultant of the external forces is zero; therefore by the **conservation of momentum** the total momentum stays zero. The momenta of the ball and of the Earth have at every instant the same magnitude and opposite directions.
