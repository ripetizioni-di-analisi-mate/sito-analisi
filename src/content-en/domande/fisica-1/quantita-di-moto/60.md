Two skaters initially at rest push apart on frictionless ice. The first skater has mass $50\,\mathrm{kg}$. The initial data are the first skater's speed $u = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the second skater's mass $m = 75\,\mathrm{kg}$. Change the first skater's speed to $\dfrac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the second skater's mass to $90\,\mathrm{kg}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $v$ minus its initial value?

- $\dfrac{57}{140}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\dfrac{18}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $- \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Total momentum remains zero. The final momenta are therefore equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. The relation $v = \dfrac{50 \cdot u}{m}$ gives $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before the change and $\dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
