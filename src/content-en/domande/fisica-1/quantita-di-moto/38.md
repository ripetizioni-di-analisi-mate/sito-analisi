In a ballistic pendulum a bullet of mass $10\,\mathrm{g}$, with horizontal velocity $v$, embeds itself in a block of mass $0.99\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest, hanging from inextensible strings of negligible mass. The block with the bullet rises up to $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ above the initial position. Neglecting air resistance, what is $v$?

- $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $8.8 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.1 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

After the collision the tension of the strings is perpendicular to the velocity and the only force that does work is the weight, so mechanical energy is conserved. Therefore the velocity just after the collision is $V = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.45}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$. During the collision the horizontal component of the momentum is conserved, so $m v = (m + M)\,V$ and
$$
v = \frac{m + M}{m}\,V = \frac{1.00}{0.010} \cdot 2.97\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 3.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
In the collision, which is perfectly inelastic, mechanical energy is not conserved, and $99\,\%$ of the kinetic energy of the bullet is dissipated.
