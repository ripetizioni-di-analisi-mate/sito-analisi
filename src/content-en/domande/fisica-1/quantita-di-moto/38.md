A car of mass $1.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$ collides with a van of mass $1.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest and the two vehicles remain locked together. After the collision they skid together on a horizontal road for $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$ before stopping, with coefficient of kinetic friction $0.60$. What was the speed of the car just before the collision?

- $8.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $13\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $21\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $53\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The problem splits into two stages, and it is solved starting from the second. After the collision the joined vehicles, of mass $M$, stop because of friction, which does the work $-\mu M g d$; by the work-energy theorem $\dfrac{1}{2} M V^2 = \mu M g d$, so
$$
V = \sqrt{2 \mu g d} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 0.60 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 6.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 8.4\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
During the collision friction is not impulsive, therefore momentum is conserved and $m v = (m + M_f)\,V$, where $M_f$ is the mass of the van. It follows that
$$
v = \frac{m + M_f}{m}\,V = \frac{2.5 \cdot 10^{3}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{3}} \cdot 8.4\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 21\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Mechanical energy is not conserved in the collision, so the initial speed of the car cannot be linked directly to the skid.
