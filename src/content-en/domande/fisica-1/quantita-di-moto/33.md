A fisherman of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ jumps off a pier with horizontal velocity $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and lands on a boat of mass $130\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest, remaining standing on it. Neglecting the horizontal resistance of the water, with what speed does the boat move right afterward?

- $1.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The fisherman stays on the boat, so after the landing the two move with the same velocity and the collision is **perfectly inelastic**. The external forces are vertical, therefore the horizontal component of momentum is conserved:
$$
m v = (m + M)\,V
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
V = \frac{70 \cdot 3.0}{70 + 130}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.1\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The denominator contains the total mass, because after the collision the fisherman and the boat move together.
