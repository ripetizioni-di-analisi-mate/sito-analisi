The graph shows the steady-state amplitude $A$ of a driven oscillator, in centimeters, as a function of the driving angular frequency $\Omega$, in $\mathrm{rad/s}$, with the amplitude of the force fixed. What is, to a good approximation, the quality factor of the oscillator?

::grafico{f="16/sqrt((16-x^2)^2+0.25*x^2)" punti="0,1,pieno;3.996,8,pieno" asintoti="x=4" xmin=-0.3 xmax=8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=9.5 nomi="Ω,A" legenda=0 titolo="Amplitude $A$, in centimeters, as a function of the angular frequency $\Omega$, in $\mathrm{rad/s}$; at $\Omega = 0$ it is $1\,\mathrm{cm}$, the maximum is about $8\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

- $8$
- $16$
- $64$
- $4$

## Explanation

For $\Omega \to 0$ the amplitude tends to the static displacement $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$, which from the graph is $1\,\mathrm{cm}$. With small damping the maximum amplitude is $A_{\max} \approx Q\,\dfrac{F_0}{k}$, so
$$
Q \approx A_{\max}\,\frac{k}{F_0} = \frac{8\,\mathrm{cm}}{1\,\mathrm{cm}} = 8 .
$$
The peak is at $\Omega \approx 4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, so $\omega_0 \approx 4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and $Q = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2\gamma}$ gives $\gamma \approx 0.25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, consistent with small damping. The value $16$ is $\dfrac{\omega_0}{\gamma}$, the value $64$ is $Q^2$, and $4$ is the resonance angular frequency read on the horizontal axis.
