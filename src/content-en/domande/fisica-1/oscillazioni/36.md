A simple pendulum of length $1{,}00\,\mathrm{m}$ hangs from the ceiling of an elevator moving up with constant acceleration of magnitude $2{,}45\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed upward. What is the period of small oscillations?

- $2{,}32\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2{,}01\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1{,}79\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4{,}01\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

In the frame of the elevator the particle is subject, in addition to its weight, to the fictitious force $-m\vec{a}$, directed downward. The pendulum therefore behaves as in a gravitational field of magnitude $g + a = 12{,}26\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the period is
$$
T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g + a}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1{,}00}{12{,}26}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1{,}79\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $2{,}32\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained with $g - a$, that is, with the fictitious force in the wrong direction, and the value $2{,}01\,\mathrm{s}$ is the period with the elevator at rest. The value $4{,}01\,\mathrm{s}$ uses the acceleration of the elevator alone instead of $g + a$.
