A body oscillates along the $x$ axis according to the law $x(t) = 0.050\sin\left(4.0\,t + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. What is the velocity component $v_x$ at the instant $t = 0$?

- $v_x = -0.10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $v_x = 0.10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $v_x = -0.17\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $v_x = 0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The velocity is the derivative of the position function, and the derivative of $\sin(4.0\,t + \varphi)$ is $4.0\cos(4.0\,t + \varphi)$, so
$$
v_x(t) = 0.050 \cdot 4.0\cos\left(4.0\,t + \frac{\pi}{3}\right)\mathrm{m/s} = 0.20\cos\left(4.0\,t + \frac{\pi}{3}\right)\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
For $t = 0$ we have $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore $v_x(0) = 0.10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, positive. The value $-0.17\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by applying the formula $v = -A\omega\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$, valid for the position function written with the cosine, to a function written with the sine. The value $0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the maximum speed $A\omega$.
