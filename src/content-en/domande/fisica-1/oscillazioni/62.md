A simple pendulum swings through small angles. Its string is inextensible and has negligible mass. Given gravitational acceleration $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the period $T = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$, what is the pendulum length $L$?

- $5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{5}{4}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{15}{4}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At small angles the linear equation has angular frequency $\sqrt{g/L}$. The period is the reciprocal of frequency. In the stated units, the relation is $T = \dfrac{2 \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{L}}{\sqrt{g}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $L = \dfrac{T^{2} \cdot g}{4 \cdot \pi^{2}}$. Substituting the given values yields $\dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$.
