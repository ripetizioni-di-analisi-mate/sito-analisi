A body of mass $0{,}50\,\mathrm{kg}$, attached to an ideal spring with spring constant $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and with negligible damping, is driven by the force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)$ with $F_0 = 3{,}0\,\mathrm{N}$ and $\Omega = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. What is the amplitude of the particular solution that oscillates at the angular frequency of the driving force?

- $0{,}020\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}015\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}012\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}060\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The natural angular frequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{200}{0{,}50}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. With $\gamma = 0$ and $\Omega \neq \omega_0$ the particular solution has amplitude
$$
A = \frac{F_0}{m\,|\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2|} = \frac{3{,}0}{0{,}50 \cdot (400 - 100)}\,\mathrm{m} = 0{,}020\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0{,}015\,\mathrm{m}$ is the static extension $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$, which is obtained only for $\Omega \to 0$. The value $0{,}012\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained with $\omega_0^2 + \Omega^2$ in the denominator, and the value $0{,}060\,\mathrm{m}$ with $(\omega_0 - \Omega)^2$ instead of $\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2$.
