A driven oscillator has $\omega_0 = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\gamma = 3.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. For what driving angular frequency $\Omega > 0$ is the steady-state amplitude equal to the static displacement $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$?

- $9.06\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $12.8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $14.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $7.07\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

Since $k = m\omega_0^2$, the condition $A(\Omega) = \dfrac{F_0}{m\omega_0^2}$ is equivalent to $(\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\Omega^2 = \omega_0^4$. Expanding the square gives $\Omega^4 - 2\omega_0^2\Omega^2 + 4\gamma^2\Omega^2 = 0$, that is, $\Omega^2(\Omega^2 - 2\omega_0^2 + 4\gamma^2) = 0$. Excluding $\Omega = 0$ leaves
$$
\Omega = \sqrt{2\omega_0^2 - 4\gamma^2} = \sqrt{200 - 36}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 12.8\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The value $9.06\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the resonance angular frequency $\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2}$, where the amplitude is maximum. The values $14.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $7.07\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ are $\sqrt{2}\,\omega_0$ and $\dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$, obtained by neglecting the damping or by confusing the condition with the one for the resonance peak.
