A body attached to an ideal spring oscillates on a smooth horizontal surface with period $T$; at the instant $t = 0$ it is released from rest at the position $x = A$. What fraction of the mechanical energy is kinetic energy at the instant $t = \dfrac{T}{6}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

## Explanation

The body starts from rest at $x = A$, so the position function is $x(t) = A\cos(\omega t)$ and the kinetic energy is $K = E\sin^2(\omega t)$. At the instant $t = \dfrac{T}{6}$ the phase is $\omega t = \dfrac{2\pi}{T} \cdot \dfrac{T}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, therefore
$$
\frac{K}{E} = \sin^2\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{3}{4} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{1}{4}$ is $\cos^2\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, the fraction of potential energy, and the value $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ without the square. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the fraction at the instant $\dfrac{T}{8}$, or the average of $\dfrac{K}{E}$ over one period.
