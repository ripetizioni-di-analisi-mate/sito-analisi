A mass-spring oscillator has spring constant $k = 400\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and amplitude $A = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the kinetic energy of the body when it is at the position $x = 0.060\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $0.720\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.28\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.320\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy is constant and equals $E = \dfrac{1}{2}kA^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 400 \cdot 0.010\,\mathrm{J} = 2.00\,\mathrm{J}$. At the position $x = 0.060\,\mathrm{m}$ the elastic potential energy is $U = \dfrac{1}{2}kx^2 = 200 \cdot 0.0036\,\mathrm{J} = 0.720\,\mathrm{J}$. The kinetic energy is the difference, therefore
$$
K = E - U = 2.00\,\mathrm{J} - 0.720\,\mathrm{J} = 1.28\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $0.720\,\mathrm{J}$ is the potential energy, the value $2.00\,\mathrm{J}$ is the total energy, which is all kinetic only when the body passes through equilibrium, and the value $0.320\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by computing $\dfrac{1}{2}k\,(A - x)^2$.
