A point $P$ moves in uniform circular motion on a circle of radius $0{,}15\,\mathrm{m}$, making $2{,}0$ revolutions per second. The projection of $P$ onto a diameter performs harmonic motion. What is the maximum speed of the projection?

- $1{,}9\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0{,}30\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0{,}94\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3{,}8\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The projection of $P$ onto a diameter performs harmonic motion with amplitude equal to the radius, $A = 0{,}15\,\mathrm{m}$, and angular frequency equal to the angular velocity of $P$. With $2{,}0$ revolutions per second the angular velocity is $\omega = 2\pi \cdot 2{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 4\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The maximum speed of the projection is therefore
$$
v_{\max} = A\omega = 0{,}15 \cdot 4\pi\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1{,}9\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
equal to the speed of $P$ on the circle. The value $0{,}30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by multiplying the radius by the frequency without the factor $2\pi$, the value $0{,}94\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by taking $\omega = 2\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and the value $3{,}8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by using the diameter as the amplitude.
