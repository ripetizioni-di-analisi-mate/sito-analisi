In the steady state of a driven oscillator the velocity also oscillates, with an amplitude that depends on the driving angular frequency. The body has mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, the ideal spring has constant $50\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the viscous force has coefficient $b = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg/s}$; the driving force is $F_0\cos(\Omega t)$ with $F_0 = 0.20\,\mathrm{N}$ and $\Omega$ equal to the natural angular frequency. What is the amplitude of the velocity?

- $0.050\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The natural angular frequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the damping constant is $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m} = 0.40\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. For $\Omega = \omega_0$ the term $(\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2)^2$ vanishes, so the amplitude of the displacement is
$$
A = \frac{F_0}{2m\gamma\,\omega_0} = \frac{0.20}{2 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 10}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.050\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The velocity $\dot{x} = -\Omega A\sin(\Omega t - \delta)$ has amplitude $\Omega A = 0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and since $2m\gamma = b$ this value coincides with $\dfrac{F_0}{b}$. The value $0.050\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the amplitude of the displacement written as a velocity, the value $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is $\Omega^2 A$, and $0.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is $\dfrac{F_0}{2b}$.
