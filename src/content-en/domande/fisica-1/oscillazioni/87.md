An oscillator on a frictionless horizontal surface has mechanical energy $E$ relative to equilibrium. Its speed is measured at equilibrium. Given the mechanical energy $E = 36\,\mathrm{J}$ and the equilibrium-crossing speed $v = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the mass $m$?

- $2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $3\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The elastic energy at equilibrium is zero, so $E$ equals the kinetic energy $mv^2/2$. In the stated units, the relation is $v = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{m}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{2 \cdot E}{v^{2}}$. Substituting the given values yields $2\,\mathrm{kg}$.
