A body of mass $0{,}20\,\mathrm{kg}$, attached to an ideal spring with spring constant $80\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and with negligible damping, is driven by the force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)$ with $F_0 = 1{,}6\,\mathrm{N}$ and $\Omega$ equal to the natural angular frequency. The equation of motion has a particular solution of the form $x_p(t) = C\,t\sin(\Omega t)$, whose amplitude $C\,t$ grows in time. What is this amplitude at the instant $t = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $0{,}80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}020\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The natural angular frequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{80}{0{,}20}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. With $\gamma = 0$ and $\Omega = \omega_0$ the resonance rule of the method of undetermined coefficients gives the particular solution
$$
x_p(t) = \frac{F_0}{2m\omega_0}\,t\sin(\omega_0 t) ,
$$
so the amplitude grows linearly in time and at the instant $t = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$ equals $\dfrac{1{,}6 \cdot 2{,}0}{2 \cdot 0{,}20 \cdot 20}\,\mathrm{m} = 0{,}40\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0{,}80\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained without the factor $2$ in the denominator, and the value $0{,}20\,\mathrm{m}$ by forgetting the factor $t$. The value $0{,}020\,\mathrm{m}$ is the static extension $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$, which corresponds to $\Omega \to 0$.
