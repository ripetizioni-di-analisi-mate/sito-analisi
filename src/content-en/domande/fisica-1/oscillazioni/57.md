The graph shows the elastic potential energy $U(x)$ of a mass-spring oscillator, with $U$ in joules and $x$ in decimeters. The mechanical energy of the oscillator is $4.5\,\mathrm{J}$. What is the amplitude of the oscillations?

::grafico{f="0.5*x^2" punti="1,0.5,pieno" segmenti="1,0,1,0.5;0,0.5,1,0.5" xmin=-3.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=6 nomi="x,U" legenda=0 titolo="Elastic potential energy $U$, in joules, as a function of the position $x$, in decimeters"}

- $2.1\,\mathrm{dm}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{dm}$
- $9.0\,\mathrm{dm}$
- $4.2\,\mathrm{dm}$

## Explanation

The graph is the parabola $U = \dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ and passes through the point $x = 1\,\mathrm{dm}$, $U = 0.5\,\mathrm{J}$, so $U = 0.5\,x^2$ with $x$ in decimeters and $U$ in joules. At the turning points $x = \pm A$ the velocity is zero, therefore all the energy is potential and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}kA^2 = E$. Then
$$
0.5\,A^2 = 4.5 ,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
A = \sqrt{9}\,\mathrm{dm} = 3.0\,\mathrm{dm} .
$$
The value $9.0\,\mathrm{dm}$ is $A^2$, without the square root. The value $4.2\,\mathrm{dm}$ is obtained by solving $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.5\,A^2 = 4.5$, that is, by taking the coefficient of the parabola as the spring constant, and $2.1\,\mathrm{dm}$ is obtained by solving $A^2 = 4.5$.
