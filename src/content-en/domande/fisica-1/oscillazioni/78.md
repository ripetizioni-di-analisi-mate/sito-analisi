The displacement $x$ of an oscillator, in meters, satisfies the equation $2.0\,\ddot{x} + 0.80\,\dot{x} + 50\,x = 0$, with the coefficients in SI units. What is the damping constant $\gamma$?

- $0.40\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$

## Explanation

The equation has the form $m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = 0$ with $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, $b = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ and $k = 50\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Dividing by $m$ gives the form $\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0$, so $2\gamma = \dfrac{b}{m}$ and
$$
\gamma = \frac{b}{2m} = \frac{0.80}{4.0}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 0.20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} .
$$
Since $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is much larger than $\gamma$, the damping is weak. The value $0.40\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is $\dfrac{b}{m}$, without the factor $2$, the value $0.80$ is the coefficient $b$, and $0.10\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is obtained by dividing $b$ by $4m$.
