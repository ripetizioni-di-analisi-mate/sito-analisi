An underdamped oscillator has amplitude envelope $A(t)=A_0 e^{-\beta t}$. The time taken to reduce the amplitude to a fraction of its initial value is measured. Given the decay rate $\beta = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and the final-to-initial amplitude ratio $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$, what is the elapsed time $t$?

- $2 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The final-to-initial amplitude ratio is $a=e^{-\beta t}$. Taking logarithms gives $\beta t=-\ln a$. In the stated units, the relation is $t = - \dfrac{\log{\left(a \right)}}{\beta}$. Substituting the given values yields $2 \cdot \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{s}$.
