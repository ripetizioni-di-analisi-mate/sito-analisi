A rigid body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ oscillates as a physical pendulum about a horizontal axis with respect to which its moment of inertia is $0.60\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$; the center of mass is $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis. What is the reduced length, that is, the length of the simple pendulum that oscillates with the same period?

- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.21\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The period of the physical pendulum is $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{mgd}}$ and that of the simple pendulum is $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$. The two periods are equal if $\ell = \dfrac{I}{md}$, so
$$
\ell_r = \frac{I}{md} = \frac{0.60}{2.0 \cdot 0.25}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.2\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The reduced length is greater than the distance $d$ of the center of mass from the axis, because $I = I_{\mathrm{cm}} + md^2 > md^2$. The value $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ is $\dfrac{I}{m}$, the value $4.8\,\mathrm{m}$ is $\dfrac{I}{md^2}$, and $0.21\,\mathrm{m}$ is the reciprocal of $\dfrac{I}{md^2}$.
