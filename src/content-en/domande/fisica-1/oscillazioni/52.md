A body of mass $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$ and is held by an ideal spring with constant $80\,\mathrm{N/m}$, parallel to the plane and fixed at the top. By how much is the spring stretched, with respect to its natural length, in the equilibrium position?

- $4.9\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $4.2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Along the plane act the component of the weight $mg\sin 30^\circ$, directed downward, and the elastic force $k\,\Delta\ell$, directed upward; the normal force is perpendicular to the plane and has no component along it. At equilibrium the two forces balance, so $k\,\Delta\ell = mg\sin 30^\circ$ and
$$
\Delta\ell = \frac{mg\sin 30^\circ}{k} = \frac{0.40 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50}{80}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.025\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $4.9\,\mathrm{cm}$ is obtained with the whole weight, as for a vertical spring, and $4.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ with the component $mg\cos 30^\circ$, which is perpendicular to the plane. The period of the oscillations about this position is still $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$.
