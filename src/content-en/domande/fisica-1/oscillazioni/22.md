A body attached to an ideal spring on a smooth horizontal plane is displaced by $2{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the equilibrium position and released from rest. The test is repeated displacing it by $4{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Compared with the first test, how do the period and the maximum speed change?

- The period stays the same and the maximum speed doubles
- The period doubles and the maximum speed stays the same
- The period and the maximum speed both double
- The period stays the same and the maximum speed quadruples

## Explanation

The period of the mass-spring oscillator is $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$ and depends only on the mass and the spring constant, so by **isochronism** it stays the same. A body released from rest at the position $x_0$ oscillates with amplitude $A = x_0$, therefore the amplitude doubles. The maximum speed is $v_{\max} = A\omega$ and, since $\omega$ does not change, it doubles as well. The mechanical energy $\dfrac{1}{2}kA^2$ quadruples, since it is proportional to the square of the amplitude.
