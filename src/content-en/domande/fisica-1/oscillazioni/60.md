The graph represents the potential energy $U(x) = \dfrac{x^4}{4} - \dfrac{x^3}{3} - x^2$, with $x$ in meters and $U$ in joules, of a particle constrained to a straight line. What can be said about small oscillations?

::grafico{f="x^4/4-x^3/3-x^2" punti="-1,-0.4167,pieno;2,-2.667,pieno;0,0,vuoto" xmin=-2.2 xmax=3 ymin=-3.4 ymax=2.4 nomi="x,U" legenda=0 titolo="Potential energy $U(x)$, with minima at $x = -1$ and $x = 2$ and a maximum at $x = 0$"}

- The periods of small oscillations about the two minima are equal
- The period of small oscillations about $x = -1$ is shorter than about $x = 2$
- About $x = 0$ the particle also performs small harmonic oscillations
- The period of small oscillations about $x = 2$ is shorter than about $x = -1$

## Explanation

The derivative is $U'(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 2x = x(x + 1)(x - 2)$, so the equilibrium positions are $x = -1$, $x = 0$ and $x = 2$, and the second derivative is $U''(x) = 3x^2 - 2x - 2$. We have $U''(-1) = 3$, $U''(2) = 6$ and $U''(0) = -2$. By the theorem on small oscillations the angular frequency about a minimum is $\sqrt{\dfrac{U''(x_e)}{m}}$, therefore about $x = 2$, where the parabola approximating $U$ is narrower, the angular frequency is larger and the **period is shorter**. At $x = 0$ we have $U'' < 0$, so the equilibrium is unstable and the particle does not oscillate. The angular frequency therefore depends on the curvature of $U$ at the minimum and not on its depth.
