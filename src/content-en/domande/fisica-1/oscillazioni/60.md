A mass attached to an ideal spring oscillates on a frictionless horizontal surface. The initial data are the spring constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the amplitude $A = \dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{m}$. In the second configuration, change the spring constant to $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the amplitude to $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $K$ minus its initial value?

- $-6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $- \dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

At maximum displacement from equilibrium, the oscillator energy is potential. On passing through equilibrium it is entirely kinetic. The relation $K = \dfrac{A^{2} \cdot k}{2}$ gives $9\,\mathrm{J}$ before the change and $6\,\mathrm{J}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $-3\,\mathrm{J}$.
