A body of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, attached to an ideal spring with constant $50\,\mathrm{N/m}$, is subject to a viscous force with coefficient $b = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. With what angular frequency does the body oscillate?

- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $13\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The natural angular frequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{100}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the damping constant is $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m} = 8.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Since $\gamma < \omega_0$, the damping is weak and the body oscillates with angular frequency
$$
\omega_s = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2} = \sqrt{100 - 64}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The value $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the natural angular frequency, which the body would have without damping, and the value $13\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is obtained with the $+$ sign under the square root. The value $2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is obtained by subtracting $\gamma$ from $\omega_0$ without squaring.
