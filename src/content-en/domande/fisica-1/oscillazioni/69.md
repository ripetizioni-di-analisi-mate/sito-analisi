A simple pendulum of length $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is fixed to the roof of a car. The car accelerates along a straight horizontal road with constant acceleration of magnitude $7.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the period of small oscillations about the equilibrium position, in the reference frame of the car?

- $1.53\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.75\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.01\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.81\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

In the reference frame of the car the bob is acted on, besides its weight, by the fictitious force $-m\vec{a}$, horizontal and opposite to the acceleration. The two forces add up to an effective weight $m\vec{g}_{\mathrm{eff}}$, which is inclined with respect to the vertical and has magnitude
$$
g_{\mathrm{eff}} = \sqrt{g^2 + a^2} = \sqrt{9.81^2 + 7.0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 12.1\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The pendulum oscillates about the direction of $\vec{g}_{\mathrm{eff}}$, so $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g_{\mathrm{eff}}}} = 1.81\,\mathrm{s}$. The value $1.53\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by adding the magnitudes $g + a$ as in an elevator, the value $3.75\,\mathrm{s}$ with $g - a$, and $2.01\,\mathrm{s}$ is the period with the car at rest.
