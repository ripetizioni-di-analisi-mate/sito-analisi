A physical pendulum is a rigid body with moment of inertia $I$ about its pivot. It has mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and swings through small angles. Assume $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the moment of inertia $I = 5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and the distance from pivot to center of mass $d = 1\,\mathrm{m}$. In the second configuration, change the moment of inertia to $20\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $T$ to its initial value?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $1$

## Explanation

Linearizing the torque from gravity gives $I\ddot\theta=-mgd\theta$. It follows that $\omega=\sqrt{mgd/I}$. The relation $T = \dfrac{\sqrt{5} \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{I}}{5 \cdot \sqrt{d}}$ gives $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$ before the change and $2 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{s}$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
