A rigid body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ can rotate about a fixed horizontal axis, and its center of mass is $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis. What is the magnitude of the torque of the weight about the axis when the segment joining the axis to the center of mass makes an angle of $10^\circ$ with the vertical?

- $5.8\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $3.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $59\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$

## Explanation

The weight acts at the center of mass, and its vertical line of action is at a distance $d\sin\theta$ from the axis. The magnitude of the torque of the weight is therefore
$$
mgd\sin\theta = 2.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.30 \cdot \sin 10^\circ\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 5.886 \cdot 0.174\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 1.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} .
$$
It is the restoring torque that in a physical pendulum tends to bring the body back to the position $\theta = 0$. The value $5.8\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ is obtained with $\cos\theta$ instead of $\sin\theta$, the value $3.4$ is the magnitude $mg\sin\theta$ of the component of the weight perpendicular to the segment, in newtons, without the lever arm $d$, and $59\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ is obtained by replacing $\sin\theta$ with the angle in degrees.
