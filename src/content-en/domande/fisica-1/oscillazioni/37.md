A uniform rigid rod of mass $m$ and length $L = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ hangs from a horizontal pivot, perpendicular to the rod, passing through a point of the rod at distance $d$ from its center. The moment of inertia about the perpendicular axis through the center is $\dfrac{1}{12}mL^2$. For what value of $d$ is the period of small oscillations a minimum?

- $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By the parallel-axis theorem the moment of inertia about the pivot is $I = \dfrac{1}{12}mL^2 + md^2$, so the period of the physical pendulum is
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1}{g}\left(\frac{L^2}{12\,d} + d\right)} .
$$
The period is a minimum where the function $h(d) = \dfrac{L^2}{12\,d} + d$ is a minimum; its derivative is $h'(d) = 1 - \dfrac{L^2}{12\,d^2}$, which vanishes at $d = \dfrac{L}{\sqrt{12}}$, is negative before and positive after. Therefore $d = \dfrac{1.20}{\sqrt{12}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds to the pivot at one end, which is obtained by neglecting the term $\dfrac{L^2}{12\,d}$; the value $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$ is $\dfrac{L^2}{12}$ without the square root, and $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ is $\dfrac{L}{4}$.
