A uniform rigid rod of mass $m$ and length $L = 1{,}20\,\mathrm{m}$ can rotate without friction about a horizontal pivot, perpendicular to the rod, that passes through a point of the rod $0{,}30\,\mathrm{m}$ from its center. The moment of inertia about the perpendicular axis through the center is $\dfrac{1}{12}mL^2$. What is the period of small oscillations?

- $1{,}27\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1{,}68\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1{,}10\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2{,}54\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The distance of the center of mass from the axis is $d = 0{,}30\,\mathrm{m}$. By the parallel-axis theorem
$$
\frac{I}{m} = \frac{L^2}{12} + d^2 = 0{,}12\,\mathrm{m^2} + 0{,}09\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0{,}21\,\mathrm{m^2} .
$$
The period of the physical pendulum is therefore $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{0{,}21}{9{,}81 \cdot 0{,}30}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1{,}68\,\mathrm{s}$. The value $1{,}27\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained without the parallel-axis term, the value $1{,}10\,\mathrm{s}$ by treating the rod as a simple pendulum of length $d$, and the value $2{,}54\,\mathrm{s}$ by using the moment of inertia $\dfrac{1}{3}mL^2$ about one end.
