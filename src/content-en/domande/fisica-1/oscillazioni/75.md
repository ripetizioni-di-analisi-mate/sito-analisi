A body undergoes harmonic motion about the origin with angular frequency $\omega$ and zero initial velocity. At the instant considered its coordinate is positive. Given the instantaneous coordinate $x = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$ and the acceleration magnitude $a = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the angular frequency $\omega$?

- $5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $25\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $\dfrac{5 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

Harmonic motion satisfies $a_x=-\omega^2 x$. A positive coordinate gives acceleration toward the origin. In the stated units, the relation is $a = \omega^{2} \cdot x$. Solving for the unknown gives $\omega = \dfrac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{x}}$. Substituting the given values yields $5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
