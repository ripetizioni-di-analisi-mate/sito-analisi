A uniform solid sphere of radius $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ hangs from a light inextensible string; the distance between the point of suspension and the center of the sphere is $L = 45\,\mathrm{cm}$. The moment of inertia of the sphere about an axis through its center is $\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$. How does the period of small oscillations compare with that of a simple pendulum of length $L$?

- Equal, because the period of a pendulum does not depend on the mass
- Slightly shorter, because part of the mass of the sphere is closer to the point of suspension
- Slightly longer, because the moment of inertia about the point of suspension exceeds $mL^2$
- About $18\%$ longer, because the moment of inertia about the point of suspension is $\dfrac{7}{5}mL^2$

## Explanation

The sphere on the string is a physical pendulum with $d = L$ and, by the parallel-axis theorem, $I = \dfrac{2}{5}mr^2 + mL^2$. The period is therefore
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgL}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}\,\sqrt{1 + \frac{2r^2}{5L^2}} ,
$$
which exceeds the period $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$ of the simple pendulum by a factor $\sqrt{1.0049} \approx 1.0025$, that is, $1.349\,\mathrm{s}$ versus $1.346\,\mathrm{s}$. The period is therefore **slightly longer**, because of the term $\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$ that the simple-pendulum model neglects. The mass cancels in the formula, so the period does not depend on the mass. The $18\%$ increase, that is, the factor $\sqrt{1.4}$, is obtained by writing $\dfrac{2}{5}mL^2$ instead of $\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$ in the moment of inertia.
