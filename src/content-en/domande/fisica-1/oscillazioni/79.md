With strong damping the position function of an oscillator is the sum of two decreasing exponentials. An oscillator with natural angular frequency $\omega_0 = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and damping constant $\gamma = 5.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is displaced from equilibrium and released from rest. With what constant does the term that determines the motion after a long time decay?

- $9.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$

## Explanation

Since $\gamma > \omega_0$, the damping is strong and the characteristic equation $r^2 + 2\gamma r + \omega_0^2 = 0$, that is, $r^2 + 10r + 9 = 0$, has the real roots
$$
r_{1,2} = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2} = -5 \pm 4 ,
$$
so $r_1 = -1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $r_2 = -9\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The position function is $x(t) = C_1 e^{-t} + C_2 e^{-9t}$, and the term $e^{-9t}$ becomes negligible much sooner than $e^{-t}$. Therefore after a long time the motion is determined by the term that decays with constant $1.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The value $9.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is the constant of the term that dies out first, $5.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is $\gamma$, and $4.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is $\sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}$.
