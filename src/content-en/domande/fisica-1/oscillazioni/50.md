A point in simple harmonic motion passes through the position $x_1 = 0.07\,\mathrm{m}$ with speed $1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and through the position $x_2 = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ with speed $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the angular frequency of the motion?

- $25\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $6.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

At every position the speed satisfies $v^2 = \omega^2(A^2 - x^2)$, so we can write the two equalities $v_1^2 = \omega^2(A^2 - x_1^2)$ and $v_2^2 = \omega^2(A^2 - x_2^2)$. Subtracting the second from the first eliminates the amplitude and leaves $v_1^2 - v_2^2 = \omega^2(x_2^2 - x_1^2)$. Therefore
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{x_2^2 - x_1^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.44 - 1.00}{0.0225 - 0.0049}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = \sqrt{25}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 5.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The value $25$ is $\omega^2$, the value $2.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the ratio $\dfrac{v_1 - v_2}{x_2 - x_1}$ of the differences without squaring, and $6.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is $\dfrac{v_2}{x_2}$, which equals $\omega$ only if the amplitude is ignored.
