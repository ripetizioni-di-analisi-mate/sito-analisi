An oscillator with natural angular frequency $\omega_0 = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and damping constant $\gamma = 6{,}0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is driven by a sinusoidal force with angular frequency $\Omega = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. With what angular frequency does the body oscillate after the transient?

- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $4{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $8{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $5{,}3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the steady state, after the transient the motion is $x(t) = A(\Omega)\cos(\Omega t - \delta)$, so the body oscillates at the angular frequency of the driving force, $\Omega = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, whatever the initial conditions. The angular frequency $\omega_s = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2} = 8{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is that of the transient, which decays as $e^{-\gamma t}$, and $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the natural angular frequency. The value $5{,}3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the resonance angular frequency $\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2}$, at which the amplitude of the steady state would be maximum.
