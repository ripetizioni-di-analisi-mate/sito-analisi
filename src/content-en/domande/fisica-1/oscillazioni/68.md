A physical pendulum is a rigid body with moment of inertia $I$ about its pivot. It has mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$, swings through small angles, and take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the moment of inertia $I = 5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and the period $T = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$, what is the distance from pivot to center of mass $d$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Linearizing the torque from gravity gives $I\ddot\theta=-mgd\theta$. It follows that $\omega=\sqrt{mgd/I}$. In the stated units, the relation is $T = \dfrac{\sqrt{5} \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{I}}{5 \cdot \sqrt{d}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $d = \dfrac{\pi^{2} \cdot I}{5 \cdot T^{2}}$. Substituting the given values yields $1\,\mathrm{m}$.
