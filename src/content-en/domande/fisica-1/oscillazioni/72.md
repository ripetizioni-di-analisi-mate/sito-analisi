A uniform wheel is mounted on a fixed horizontal axle, without friction, passing through its center. The wheel is turned by a small angle and released from rest. Which statement about the subsequent motion is true?

- The wheel oscillates with period $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{mgR}}$, where $R$ is the radius
- The wheel stays at rest in the new position, because the torque of the weight about the axle is zero
- The wheel oscillates like a simple pendulum of length equal to the radius
- The wheel oscillates with the shortest possible period for that body

## Explanation

In the physical pendulum the restoring torque is $-mgd\sin\theta$, where $d$ is the distance of the center of mass from the axis. For a uniform wheel the center of mass is the center, which lies on the axis, so $d = 0$ and the weight has zero torque in every position. The reaction of the axle also has zero torque, therefore by the rotational form of Newton's second law $I\ddot{\theta} = 0$. The wheel released from rest **stays at rest**, in neutral equilibrium, and indeed the formula $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{mgd}}$ with $d \to 0$ gives a period that tends to infinity.
