A body undergoes harmonic motion about the origin with angular frequency $\omega$ and zero initial velocity. At the instant considered its coordinate is positive. The initial data are the angular frequency $\omega = 5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the instantaneous coordinate $x = \dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$. In the second configuration, change the angular frequency to $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $a$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $1$
- $4$
- $2$

## Explanation

Harmonic motion satisfies $a_x=-\omega^2 x$. A positive coordinate gives acceleration toward the origin. The relation $a = \omega^{2} \cdot x$ gives $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ before the change and $40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after it. The ratio is $4$.
