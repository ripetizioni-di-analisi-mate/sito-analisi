A disk with moment of inertia $5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about the axis of a torsion wire with constant $0.080\,\mathrm{N\cdot m/rad}$ performs torsional oscillations with angular amplitude $0.50\,\mathrm{rad}$. What is the maximum magnitude of the angular velocity of the disk?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $0.32\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The torque of the wire is $-\kappa\theta$, so the motion is harmonic with angular frequency
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{I}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.080}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The angle varies as $\theta(t) = \theta_0\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, therefore the angular velocity $\dot{\theta} = -\theta_0\,\omega\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$ has maximum magnitude $\theta_0\,\omega = 0.50 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The value $4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the angular frequency, which describes how fast the phase changes and coincides with the maximum angular velocity only for $\theta_0 = 1\,\mathrm{rad}$. The value $8.0$ is $\theta_0\,\omega^2$, that is, the maximum magnitude of the angular acceleration in $\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, and $0.32\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is obtained with the frequency instead of the angular frequency.
