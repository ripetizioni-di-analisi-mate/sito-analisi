A pendulum clock keeps exact time when the length of its pendulum equals the design length. On a hot day, because of thermal expansion, the length of the pendulum increases by $0.10\%$. What does the clock show after $24$ hours?

- It is about $43\,\mathrm{s}$ fast
- It is about $43\,\mathrm{s}$ slow
- It is about $86\,\mathrm{s}$ slow
- It is about $86\,\mathrm{s}$ fast

## Explanation

The period is $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$, proportional to $\sqrt{\ell}$. If $\ell$ increases by $0.10\%$, the period increases by the factor $\sqrt{1.0010} \approx 1.0005$, that is, by $0.05\%$. Each oscillation therefore lasts longer than intended, and the clock, which counts oscillations, shows less time than has elapsed; therefore it is **slow**. In one day the delay is
$$
86\,400\,\mathrm{s} \cdot 0.0005 \approx 43\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $86\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by assuming the period proportional to $\ell$ instead of $\sqrt{\ell}$.
