An undamped harmonic oscillator has amplitude $A$. Its speed is measured as it passes equilibrium. The initial data are the angular frequency $\omega = 8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the amplitude $A = \dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the angular frequency to $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the amplitude to $\dfrac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $v$ minus its initial value?

- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The sinusoidal velocity has amplitude $\omega A$, attained when displacement is zero. The relation $v = A \cdot \omega$ gives $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before the change and $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
