Two stacked blocks oscillate together on a smooth horizontal surface. The lower block, of mass $M = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$, is attached to an ideal spring with constant $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$; the upper block, of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, is carried along only by static friction, with coefficient $0.40$. What is the maximum amplitude with which the system can oscillate without the upper block slipping?

- $3.9\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2.9\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.98\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $39\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

As long as there is no slipping the two blocks move together, so they oscillate with $\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{M + m} = 100\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$. The upper block is accelerated only by static friction, whose magnitude is at most $\mu_s m g$, therefore its acceleration cannot exceed $\mu_s g$. The maximum acceleration of the harmonic motion is $\omega^2 A$, so the condition is $\omega^2 A \leq \mu_s g$ and
$$
A_{\max} = \frac{\mu_s g}{\omega^2} = \frac{0.40 \cdot 9.81}{100}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.039\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $2.9\,\mathrm{cm}$ is obtained with only the mass $M$ in the angular frequency, $0.98\,\mathrm{cm}$ with only the mass $m$, and $39\,\mathrm{cm}$ by dividing by $\omega$ instead of $\omega^2$.
