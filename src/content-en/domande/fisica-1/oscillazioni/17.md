A particle performs harmonic motion with period $T = 0{,}50\,\mathrm{s}$ along the $x$-axis, about the equilibrium position $x = 0$. What is the component $a_x$ of the acceleration when the particle is at $x = +0{,}040\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $a_x = 6{,}32\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $a_x = -0{,}503\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $a_x = -6{,}32\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $a_x = -0{,}160\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The angular frequency is $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T} = 4\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. In harmonic motion acceleration and position are related by $a = -\omega^2 x$, therefore
$$
a_x = -16\pi^2 \cdot 0{,}040\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = -6{,}32\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The negative sign indicates that the acceleration points toward the equilibrium position, opposite to the displacement. The value $-0{,}503\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is obtained by multiplying by $\omega$ instead of $\omega^2$, and the value $-0{,}160\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ by using the frequency $f = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{Hz}$ instead of the angular frequency.
