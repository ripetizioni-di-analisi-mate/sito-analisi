On a smooth horizontal surface, a mass-spring oscillator reaches a maximum speed of $0.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a maximum acceleration magnitude of $3.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The body has a mass of $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the spring constant?

- $3.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $72\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $9.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{N/m}$

## Explanation

In harmonic motion $v_{\max} = A\omega$ and $a_{\max} = A\omega^2$, so their ratio does not depend on the amplitude and equals
$$
\omega = \frac{a_{\max}}{v_{\max}} = \frac{3.6}{0.60}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
For the mass-spring oscillator $\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$, therefore $k = m\omega^2 = 0.50 \cdot 36\,\mathrm{N/m} = 18\,\mathrm{N/m}$. The value $3.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is $m\omega$, without the square, the value $72\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is $\dfrac{\omega^2}{m}$, and $9.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2$.
