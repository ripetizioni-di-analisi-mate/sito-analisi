An oscillator with critical damping has $\gamma = \omega_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. At the instant $t = 0$ the body is released from rest at the position $x_0 = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Where is it at the instant $t = 0.50\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $x = 7.4\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $x = 3.7\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $x = 5.5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $x = 1.4\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

With critical damping the position function is $x(t) = (C_1 + C_2 t)\,e^{-\gamma t}$. The condition $x(0) = x_0$ gives $C_1 = x_0$; the velocity is $\dot{x}(t) = \bigl(C_2 - \gamma(C_1 + C_2 t)\bigr)e^{-\gamma t}$, so the condition $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ gives $C_2 = \gamma x_0$. Therefore $x(t) = x_0(1 + \gamma t)\,e^{-\gamma t}$ and
$$
x(0.50\,\mathrm{s}) = 10\,\mathrm{cm} \cdot (1 + 1)\,e^{-1} = 7.4\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The value $3.7\,\mathrm{cm}$ is $x_0 e^{-\gamma t}$, which forgets the term $C_2 t$ and therefore the condition on the initial velocity. The value $5.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ is obtained with $C_2 = \dfrac{\gamma x_0}{2}$, and $1.4\,\mathrm{cm}$ is $x_0 e^{-2\gamma t}$.
