The suspension of a car, with its weak damping, has a natural frequency of $1.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The car travels along a road with identical bumps placed every $10\,\mathrm{m}$. At what speed are the vertical oscillations of the car body largest?

- $24\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $54\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $15\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $340\,\mathrm{km/h}$

## Explanation

The bumps act on the car body as a periodic driving force, with period equal to the time $\dfrac{d}{v}$ between two bumps, so with frequency $\dfrac{v}{d}$. The oscillations are largest at resonance, that is, when this frequency is approximately equal to the natural frequency; therefore $v = f_0\,d$ and
$$
v = 1.5\,\mathrm{Hz} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{m} = 15\,\mathrm{m/s} = 54\,\mathrm{km/h} .
$$
The value $24\,\mathrm{km/h}$ is obtained with $\dfrac{d}{f_0}$, the value $15\,\mathrm{km/h}$ forgets the conversion from $\mathrm{m/s}$ to $\mathrm{km/h}$, and $340\,\mathrm{km/h}$ uses the angular frequency $2\pi f_0$ instead of the frequency.
