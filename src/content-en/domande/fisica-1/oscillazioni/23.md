A body of mass $0{,}20\,\mathrm{kg}$, attached to an ideal spring on a smooth horizontal plane, oscillates with period $0{,}50\,\mathrm{s}$. The body is replaced with one of mass $0{,}80\,\mathrm{kg}$, keeping the same spring. What is the new period?

- $2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0{,}25\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0{,}50\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The period of the mass-spring oscillator is $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$, so for fixed $k$ it is proportional to $\sqrt{m}$. The mass becomes four times larger, therefore the period is multiplied by $\sqrt{4} = 2$ and equals $1{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$. The value $2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by assuming the period proportional to the mass, and the value $0{,}25\,\mathrm{s}$ by swapping $m$ and $k$ in the formula. The value $0{,}50\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by applying to the spring a property of the simple pendulum, whose period does not depend on the mass.
