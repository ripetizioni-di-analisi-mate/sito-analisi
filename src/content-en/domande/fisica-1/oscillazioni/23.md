Two ideal springs, with constants $k_1 = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $k_2 = 300\,\mathrm{N/m}$, connect a body of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, resting on a smooth horizontal surface, to two opposite walls: the first spring to the wall on the left, the second to the wall on the right. In the equilibrium position both springs have their natural length. What is the period of the oscillations along the direction of the springs?

- $0.73\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.63\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.31\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.36\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

If the body moves by $x$ to the right, the left spring stretches by $x$ and the right spring is compressed by $x$, so both exert a force to the left. The total force is $F_x = -k_1 x - k_2 x$, therefore the springs are **in parallel**, because they have the same deformation, and the equivalent constant is $k_1 + k_2 = 400\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Then
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k_1 + k_2}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1.0}{400}}\,\mathrm{s} = 0.31\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $0.73\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by treating the springs as if they were in series, with $k = 75\,\mathrm{N/m}$, while $0.63\,\mathrm{s}$ and $0.36\,\mathrm{s}$ are the periods with only one of the two springs.
