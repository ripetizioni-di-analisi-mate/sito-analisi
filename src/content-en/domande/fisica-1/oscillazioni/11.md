In a weakly damped oscillator the amplitude halves in $4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the damping constant $\gamma$?

- $0{,}0866\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $0{,}173\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $0{,}347\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $0{,}125\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$

## Explanation

With weak damping the amplitude decreases as $A_0 e^{-\gamma t}$, so halving in $4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$ is written $e^{-\gamma \cdot 4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Taking logarithms we obtain
$$
\gamma = \frac{\ln 2}{4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}} = 0{,}173\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} .
$$
The value $0{,}0866\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is obtained by imposing the halving on the energy, which decreases as $e^{-2\gamma t}$, and the value $0{,}347\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = \dfrac{2\ln 2}{4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}}$ with the factor $2$ in the numerator instead of the denominator. The value $0{,}125\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ treats the decrease of the amplitude as linear in time.
