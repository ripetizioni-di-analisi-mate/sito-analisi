A body of mass $0{,}50\,\mathrm{kg}$, attached to an ideal spring with constant $32\,\mathrm{N/m}$, is subject to a viscous force with coefficient $b = 10\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. The body is displaced from the equilibrium position and released from rest. Which statement about the motion is true?

- The body is underdamped and oscillates with decreasing amplitude
- The damping is critical, and the body returns to equilibrium without oscillating in the shortest possible time
- The body is overdamped and returns to equilibrium without oscillating
- The body is overdamped and oscillates with decreasing amplitude

## Explanation

The natural angular frequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{32}{0{,}50}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 8{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the damping constant is $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m} = 10\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Since $\gamma > \omega_0$, the body is **overdamped**. The roots of the characteristic equation are then real and negative, so the body returns to equilibrium without oscillating. Critical damping would require $b = 2\sqrt{km} = 8{,}0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$, smaller than the given value. The body would be underdamped if one computes $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2} = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, without the mass in the denominator.
