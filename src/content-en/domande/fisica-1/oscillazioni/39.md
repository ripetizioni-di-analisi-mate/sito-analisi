A torsion pendulum consists of a rigid body, with moment of inertia $I$, hanging from a wire that exerts the restoring torque $M = -\kappa\theta$. The body is set oscillating with an angular amplitude of $1.0\,\mathrm{rad}$. Which statement about the period is true?

- It is $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{\kappa}}$, as for small amplitudes, because the torque is proportional to the angle
- It is longer than $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{\kappa}}$, because the approximation $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ does not hold at $1.0\,\mathrm{rad}$
- It is shorter than $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{\kappa}}$, because the restoring torque increases with the angle
- It is proportional to the amplitude, because in one oscillation the body sweeps a larger angle

## Explanation

Since the torque of the wire is proportional to the angle, the rotational equation of motion gives $I\ddot{\theta} = -\kappa\theta$ without any approximation. The equation has the form $\ddot{\theta} = -\omega^2\theta$ with $\omega^2 = \dfrac{\kappa}{I}$, so by the theorem on the equation of harmonic motion the motion is harmonic for every amplitude and the period is $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{\kappa}}$. The approximation $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ is needed for the simple pendulum and the physical pendulum, where the torque of the weight is proportional to $\sin\theta$. With a larger amplitude the body sweeps a larger angle with a proportionally larger angular velocity, therefore the period does not change.
