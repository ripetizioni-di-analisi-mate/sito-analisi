A mass-spring oscillator oscillates with period $T = 0.80\,\mathrm{s}$. With what period does its kinetic energy vary in time?

- $0.80\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The kinetic energy is $K = \dfrac{1}{2}kA^2\sin^2(\omega t + \varphi)$. Since $\sin^2\alpha = \dfrac{1 - \cos(2\alpha)}{2}$, the function $K(t)$ oscillates with angular frequency $2\omega$, and therefore with period $\dfrac{T}{2} = 0.40\,\mathrm{s}$. The same result can be read from the motion, because the kinetic energy is maximum at every passage through equilibrium and in one period the body passes through equilibrium twice, once in each direction. The value $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$ is the time between a maximum of the kinetic energy and the next maximum of the potential energy.
