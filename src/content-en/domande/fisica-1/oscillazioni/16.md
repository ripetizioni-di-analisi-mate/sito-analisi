A point moves in simple harmonic motion with amplitude $A = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and period $T = 0.50\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its average speed over one period, that is, the distance traveled in one period divided by $T$?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.63\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

In one period the point goes from $x = A$ to $x = -A$ and back to $x = A$, so it travels the distance $4A = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore the average speed is
$$
\frac{4A}{T} = \frac{0.20\,\mathrm{m}}{0.50\,\mathrm{s}} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the average velocity, which is zero because after one period the point is back at its starting position. The value $0.63\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the maximum speed $A\omega = \dfrac{2\pi A}{T}$, reached only when passing through equilibrium, and $0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by counting a single trip, $2A$, over the whole period.
