A particle performs harmonic motion with amplitude $A = 0{,}20\,\mathrm{m}$. At what distance from the equilibrium position is the speed half of its maximum value?

- $0{,}100\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}173\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}141\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}0500\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The speed at the position $x$ is $|v| = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$, and its maximum value is $v_{\max} = \omega A$. The condition $|v| = \dfrac{\omega A}{2}$ is equivalent to $\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = \dfrac{A}{2}$, so $x^2 = \dfrac{3}{4}A^2$ and
$$
|x| = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,A = 0{,}173\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0{,}100\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by assuming that $|v|$ depends linearly on $x$. The value $0{,}141\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, $\dfrac{A}{\sqrt{2}}$, is the position where the kinetic energy is half of its maximum, and there the velocity equals $\dfrac{v_{\max}}{\sqrt{2}}$.
