Two simple pendulums, of lengths $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.64\,\mathrm{m}$, are released from rest at the same instant, at small angles on the same side of the vertical. After how much time are they both back at their starting positions for the first time?

- $10\,\mathrm{s}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.6\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The periods are $T_1 = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{1.00}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s} = 2.01\,\mathrm{s}$ and $T_2 = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{0.64}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.60\,\mathrm{s}$, and their ratio is $\dfrac{T_1}{T_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1.00}{0.64}} = \dfrac{5}{4}$. Each pendulum returns to its starting position after a whole number of its own periods, so the required instant is the smallest time that is a multiple of both. Since $4T_1 = 5T_2$, we have
$$
t = 4T_1 = 4 \cdot 2.01\,\mathrm{s} = 8.0\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $10\,\mathrm{s}$ is $5T_1$, which is not a multiple of $T_2$; the value $0.40\,\mathrm{s}$ is the difference $T_1 - T_2$, and $3.6\,\mathrm{s}$ is the sum $T_1 + T_2$.
