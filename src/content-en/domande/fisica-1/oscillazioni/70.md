A physical pendulum is a rigid body with moment of inertia $I$ about its pivot. It has mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$, swings through small angles, and take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the moment of inertia $I = 5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and the distance from pivot to center of mass $d = 1\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the moment of inertia to $10\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and the distance from pivot to center of mass to $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $T$ minus its initial value?

- $- \pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\sqrt{2} \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Linearizing the torque from gravity gives $I\ddot\theta=-mgd\theta$. It follows that $\omega=\sqrt{mgd/I}$. The relation $T = \dfrac{\sqrt{5} \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{I}}{5 \cdot \sqrt{d}}$ gives $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$ before the change and $2 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{s}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$.
