A simple pendulum swings through small angles. Its string is inextensible and has negligible mass. Given the pendulum length $L = \dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$ and the period $T = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$, what is gravitational acceleration $g$?

- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

At small angles the linear equation has angular frequency $\sqrt{g/L}$. The period is the reciprocal of frequency. In the stated units, the relation is $T = \dfrac{2 \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{L}}{\sqrt{g}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $g = \dfrac{4 \cdot \pi^{2} \cdot L}{T^{2}}$. Substituting the given values yields $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
