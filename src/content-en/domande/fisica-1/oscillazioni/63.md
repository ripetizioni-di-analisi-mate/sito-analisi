A simple pendulum performs small oscillations, with an amplitude of $6^\circ$, in a laboratory on Earth. Which of the following changes alters the period of the oscillations?

- Replacing the bob with one of the same size and four times the mass
- Making the pendulum swing in a vertical plane rotated by $90^\circ$
- Setting the pendulum in motion with a push instead of releasing it from rest, with the same amplitude
- Taking the pendulum to the Moon

## Explanation

For small oscillations the period is $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$, so it depends only on the length of the string and on the gravitational acceleration. On the Moon $g$ is about one sixth of its value on Earth, therefore the period becomes about $\sqrt{6} \approx 2.4$ times longer. The mass cancels in Newton's second law, and the vertical plane in which the oscillation takes place does not enter the equation of motion. The way the pendulum is set in motion determines the amplitude and the initial phase and leaves the angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$ unchanged.
