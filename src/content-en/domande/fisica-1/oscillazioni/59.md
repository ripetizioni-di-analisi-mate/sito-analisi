A mass attached to an ideal spring oscillates on a frictionless horizontal surface. The initial data are the spring constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the amplitude $A = \dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{m}$. In the second configuration, change the amplitude to $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $K$ to its initial value?

- $4$
- $8$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $1$

## Explanation

At maximum displacement from equilibrium, the oscillator energy is potential. On passing through equilibrium it is entirely kinetic. The relation $K = \dfrac{A^{2} \cdot k}{2}$ gives $9\,\mathrm{J}$ before the change and $36\,\mathrm{J}$ after it. The ratio is $4$.
