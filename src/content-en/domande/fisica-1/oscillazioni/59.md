A bullet of mass $10\,\mathrm{g}$, traveling at $200\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the axis of an ideal spring with constant $400\,\mathrm{N/m}$, embeds itself in a block of mass $0.99\,\mathrm{kg}$ attached to the spring. Before the collision the block is at rest on a smooth horizontal surface and the spring is at its natural length. What is the amplitude of the subsequent oscillations?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0050\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.63\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The collision is completely inelastic and short, so momentum is conserved and the velocity of the block with the bullet just after the collision is
$$
V = \frac{m v}{m + M} = \frac{0.010 \cdot 200}{1.00}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The system starts from the equilibrium position with velocity $V$, therefore the amplitude is $A = \dfrac{V}{\omega}$ with $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m + M}} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, that is, $A = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by conserving the kinetic energy of the bullet through the collision as well, whereas the collision dissipates most of it. The value $0.0050\,\mathrm{m}$ is $\dfrac{V}{\omega^2}$ and $0.63\,\mathrm{m}$ is $\dfrac{V}{f}$, with the frequency in place of the angular frequency.
