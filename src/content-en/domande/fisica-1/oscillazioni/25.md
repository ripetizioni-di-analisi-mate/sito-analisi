A body of mass $0{,}50\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from an ideal vertical spring with constant $98\,\mathrm{N/m}$. The body is pulled $3{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the equilibrium position and released from rest. By how much is the spring stretched, with respect to its rest length, when the body is at the lowest point of the oscillation? Use $g = 9{,}8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $3{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $8{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $11\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

At equilibrium the elastic force balances the weight, so the spring is stretched by $\Delta\ell = \dfrac{mg}{k} = \dfrac{0{,}50 \cdot 9{,}8}{98}\,\mathrm{m} = 0{,}050\,\mathrm{m} = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The body oscillates about this position with amplitude $3{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$, because it is released from rest at that distance from equilibrium. At the lowest point the extension is therefore $5{,}0\,\mathrm{cm} + 3{,}0\,\mathrm{cm} = 8{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $3{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$ measures the displacement from equilibrium and the value $5{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$ the extension at equilibrium. The value $11\,\mathrm{cm}$ is obtained by adding the whole excursion $2A$ to the static extension.
