A body attached to an ideal spring with constant $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is immersed in a fluid that exerts a viscous force. The body is displaced by $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the equilibrium position and released from rest; after some time it is practically at rest at the equilibrium position. What is the total work done by the viscous force?

- $+0.25\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-0.25\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-0.50\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

At the start the body is at rest with the spring deformed by $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the initial mechanical energy is entirely elastic and equals
$$
E_i = \frac{1}{2}kx_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 200 \cdot 0.050^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.25\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
At the end the body is at rest at the equilibrium position, therefore $E_f = 0$. The viscous force is the only non-conservative force that does work, so $L_{\text{nc}} = E_f - E_i = -0.25\,\mathrm{J}$, whatever the value of $b$. The work is negative because the viscous force is always opposite to the velocity; the value $0\,\mathrm{J}$ treats the viscous force as conservative, and $-0.50\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained without the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
