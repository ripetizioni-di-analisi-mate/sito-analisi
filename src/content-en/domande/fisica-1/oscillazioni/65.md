A simple pendulum swings through small angles. Its string is inextensible and has negligible mass. The initial data are the pendulum length $L = \dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$ and gravitational acceleration $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Change the pendulum length to $5\,\mathrm{m}$ and gravitational acceleration to $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $T$ minus its initial value?

- $- 2 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- $- \pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

At small angles the linear equation has angular frequency $\sqrt{g/L}$. The period is the reciprocal of frequency. The relation $T = \dfrac{2 \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{L}}{\sqrt{g}}$ gives $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$ before the change and $2 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{s}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$.
