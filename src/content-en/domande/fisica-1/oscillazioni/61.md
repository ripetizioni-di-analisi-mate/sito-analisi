A simple pendulum swings through small angles. Its string is inextensible and has negligible mass. Given the pendulum length $L = \dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$ and gravitational acceleration $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the period $T$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2 \cdot \pi\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

At small angles the linear equation has angular frequency $\sqrt{g/L}$. The period is the reciprocal of frequency. In the stated units, the relation is $T = \dfrac{2 \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{L}}{\sqrt{g}}$. Substituting the given values yields $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$.
