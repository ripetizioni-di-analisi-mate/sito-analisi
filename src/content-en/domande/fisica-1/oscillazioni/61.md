A U-tube of constant cross section contains a column of water of total length $L = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, measured along the tube. If the two free surfaces are displaced by $x$ from the equilibrium position, one upward and the other downward, the potential energy is $U = \rho S g\,x^2$, while the mass of the liquid is $\rho S L$. Neglecting friction, what is the period of small oscillations of the liquid?

- $1.4\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.71\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

All the liquid moves with the same velocity $\dot{x}$, so the kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho S L\,\dot{x}^2$, that of a particle of mass $m = \rho S L$. The potential energy $U = \rho S g\,x^2$ has its minimum at $x = 0$ with $U''(0) = 2\rho S g$, therefore by the theorem on small oscillations
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{2\rho S g}{\rho S L}} = \sqrt{\frac{2g}{L}},
\qquad
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{2g}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.50}{19.62}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.0\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $1.4\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by taking $\rho S g$ as the spring constant, without the factor $2$ of the second derivative. The value $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained with $\dfrac{2L}{g}$ under the square root instead of $\dfrac{L}{2g}$, and $0.71\,\mathrm{s}$ with $4g$ in the denominator.
