An oscillator with $\omega_0 = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\gamma = 0.50\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is driven by the force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)$ with $\Omega = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. In the steady state, how is the displacement $x(t)$ related to the force?

- Lagging by a quarter of a period, that is, with $\delta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- In phase with the force, that is, with $\delta = 0$
- In antiphase, that is, with $\delta = \pi$
- Lagging by an eighth of a period, that is, with $\delta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

In the steady state $x(t) = A\cos(\Omega t - \delta)$, with $\delta \in (0, \pi)$ and $\tan\delta = \dfrac{2\gamma\Omega}{\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2}$. For $\Omega = \omega_0$ the denominator is zero and the numerator $2\gamma\Omega$ is positive, so the cosine of $\delta$ is zero and
$$
\delta = \frac{\pi}{2} .
$$
The displacement therefore lags the force by a quarter of a period, and the velocity $\dot{x}$ is in phase with the force. The phase lag $\delta = 0$ holds in the limit $\Omega \to 0$, and $\delta = \pi$ in the limit $\Omega \to +\infty$.
