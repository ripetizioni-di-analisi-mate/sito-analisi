An oscillator on a frictionless horizontal surface has mechanical energy $E$ relative to equilibrium. Its speed is measured at equilibrium. Given the mechanical energy $E = 36\,\mathrm{J}$ and the mass $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$, what is the equilibrium-crossing speed $v$?

- $3 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The elastic energy at equilibrium is zero, so $E$ equals the kinetic energy $mv^2/2$. In the stated units, the relation is $v = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{m}}$. Substituting the given values yields $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
