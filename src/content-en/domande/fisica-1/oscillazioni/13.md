A body of mass $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$, attached to an ideal spring with constant $100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and subject to a viscous force with $b = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg/s}$, is driven by a force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)$ with $F_0 = 1.0\,\mathrm{N}$. What is the maximum amplitude of the steady state as $\Omega$ varies?

- $0.0100\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.0503\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.100\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The natural angular frequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the damping constant is $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m} = 1.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Since $\gamma < \dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$, the amplitude of the steady state has a maximum, at resonance, which equals
$$
A_{\max} = \frac{F_0}{2m\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}} = \frac{1.0}{0.50 \cdot \sqrt{399}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.100\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0.0100\,\mathrm{m}$ is the static extension $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$, that is, the amplitude as $\Omega \to 0$, which is about $Q = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2\gamma} = 10$ times smaller. The value $0.0503\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained with $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{m}$, and the value $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$ by forgetting the factor $2$ in the denominator.
