A weakly damped oscillator has damping constant $\gamma = 0{,}10\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, much smaller than the natural angular frequency. What fraction of the initial mechanical energy remains after $5{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $0{,}61$
- $0{,}14$
- $0{,}50$
- $0{,}37$

## Explanation

With weak damping and $\gamma$ much smaller than $\omega_0$, the energy is that of a harmonic oscillator with amplitude $A_0 e^{-\gamma t}$, so $E(t) \approx E_0\,e^{-2\gamma t}$. After $5{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$ we have $2\gamma t = 1{,}0$, therefore the remaining fraction is
$$
\frac{E}{E_0} = e^{-1} = 0{,}37 .
$$
The value $0{,}61 = e^{-0{,}5}$ is the fraction of the amplitude that remains, because the amplitude decreases with the constant $\gamma$. The value $0{,}14 = e^{-2}$ is obtained with the constant $4\gamma$, and the value $0{,}50 = 1 - \gamma t$ by assuming that the energy decreases linearly in time.
