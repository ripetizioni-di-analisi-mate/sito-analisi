A body attached to a spring oscillates in a fluid that exerts the viscous force $\vec{F}_v = -b\,\vec{v}$, with $b = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. At the instant it passes through the equilibrium position the body has speed $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the power dissipated by the viscous force at that instant?

- $0.10\,\mathrm{W}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{W}$
- $0.050\,\mathrm{W}$
- $0\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The power of a force is the dot product of the force and the velocity, so for the viscous force it is $\vec{F}_v \cdot \vec{v} = -b\,v^2$. The negative sign means that the force removes energy from the body, therefore the dissipated power is
$$
P = b\,v^2 = 0.40 \cdot 0.50^2\,\mathrm{W} = 0.10\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
It is also the rate at which the mechanical energy decreases, $\dfrac{dE}{dt} = -b\,v^2$. The value $0\,\mathrm{W}$ confuses the viscous force with the elastic force, which is zero at the equilibrium position; the value $0.20$ is the magnitude $bv$ of the force in newtons, and $0.050\,\mathrm{W}$ contains a factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ taken from the kinetic energy.
