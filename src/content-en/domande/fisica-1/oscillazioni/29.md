A mass-spring oscillator has amplitude $A = 0{,}12\,\mathrm{m}$. At what distance from the equilibrium position is the kinetic energy equal to the elastic potential energy?

- $0{,}0600\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}104\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}0849\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0{,}0300\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy is $E = \dfrac{1}{2}kA^2$. If $K = U$, each of the two equals $\dfrac{E}{2}$, so $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}kA^2$ and $x^2 = \dfrac{A^2}{2}$. The required distance is therefore
$$
|x| = \frac{A}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{0{,}12}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0{,}0849\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0{,}0600\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, $\dfrac{A}{2}$, is obtained by assuming the potential energy proportional to $x$ instead of $x^2$, and at that point $U = \dfrac{E}{4}$. At the position $x = 0{,}104\,\mathrm{m}$, instead, $U = \dfrac{3}{4}E$.
