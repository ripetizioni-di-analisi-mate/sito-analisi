In a mass-spring oscillator with $k = 300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and amplitude $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the body moves from the turning point $x = A$ to the equilibrium position $x = 0$. What is the work done by the elastic force on this stretch?

- $-0.24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $+0.24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $+0.48\,\mathrm{J}$
- $+0.12\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The elastic force is conservative, so its work is the opposite of the change in potential energy, $L = U(A) - U(0)$. Therefore
$$
L = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 - 0 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 300 \cdot 0.040^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.24\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is **positive**, because on the stretch from $x = A$ to $x = 0$ the elastic force and the displacement are both directed toward equilibrium; by the work-energy theorem this work is the kinetic energy the body has when passing through $x = 0$. The value $-0.24\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work on the stretch from $x = 0$ to $x = A$, the value $0.48\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained without the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $0.12\,\mathrm{J}$ with a factor $\dfrac{1}{4}$.
