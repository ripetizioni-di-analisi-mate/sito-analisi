An oscillator with natural angular frequency $\omega_0 = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and small damping is driven, in four trials, by sinusoidal forces of equal amplitude $F_0$ and different angular frequencies. In which trial is the steady-state amplitude largest?

- With $\Omega = 5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- With $\Omega = 15\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- With $\Omega = 19\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- With $\Omega = 40\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The steady-state amplitude is $A(\Omega) = \dfrac{F_0}{m\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\Omega^2}}$, so it is larger the smaller the term $|\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2|$, which is the one that matters when damping is small. With $\omega_0^2 = 400$ we have
$$
|\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2| = 375,\ 175,\ 39,\ 1200
$$
for $\Omega = 5$, $15$, $19$, $40\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The smallest value occurs for $\Omega = 19\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, the angular frequency closest to the **resonance** frequency, which with small damping almost coincides with $\omega_0$. The driving force with $\Omega = 40\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ produces the smallest amplitude, because above resonance the amplitude decreases toward zero.
