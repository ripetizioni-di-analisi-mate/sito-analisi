A simple pendulum oscillates with angular amplitude $\theta_0 = 0{,}40\,\mathrm{rad}$. According to the first term of the correction for amplitudes that are not small, by how much is the period longer than the small-oscillation value $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$?

- By $2{,}5\%$
- By $2{,}0\%$
- By $0{,}50\%$
- By $1{,}0\%$

## Explanation

The first term of the correction gives $T_0 \approx 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}\left(1 + \dfrac{\theta_0^2}{16}\right)$, with $\theta_0$ in radians. The relative increase of the period is therefore
$$
\frac{\theta_0^2}{16} = \frac{0{,}16}{16} = 0{,}010 ,
$$
that is, $1{,}0\%$. The value $2{,}5\%$ is obtained with $\dfrac{\theta_0}{16}$, without squaring the amplitude. The values $2{,}0\%$ and $0{,}50\%$ are obtained with $8$ and with $32$ instead of $16$ in the denominator.
