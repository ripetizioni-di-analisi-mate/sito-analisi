A simple pendulum of mass $0{,}20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $0{,}50\,\mathrm{m}$ is released from rest with the string inclined at $60^\circ$ to the vertical. What is the tension in the string at the lowest point of the path?

- $3{,}92\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1{,}96\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0{,}981\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2{,}94\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By conservation of mechanical energy the velocity at the lowest point satisfies $v^2 = 2g\ell\,(1 - \cos 60^\circ) = g\ell$. At the lowest point the weight is directed along the string, opposite to $O$, and the acceleration is centripetal, so $T - mg = m\,\dfrac{v^2}{\ell}$. Therefore
$$
T = mg + m\,\frac{g\ell}{\ell} = 2mg = 2 \cdot 0{,}20 \cdot 9{,}81\,\mathrm{N} = 3{,}92\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $1{,}96\,\mathrm{N}$ is the weight alone, which equals the tension only when the pendulum is at rest, and the value $0{,}981\,\mathrm{N}$ is the tension $mg\cos 60^\circ$ at the starting position. The value $2{,}94\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained with $v^2 = \dfrac{g\ell}{2}$, that is, by forgetting the factor $2$ in the conservation of energy.
