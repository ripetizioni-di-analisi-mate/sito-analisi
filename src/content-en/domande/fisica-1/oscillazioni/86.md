What is the minimum value of the coefficient $b$ of the viscous force for which the steady-state amplitude has no maximum as the driving angular frequency varies? The body has mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ and is attached to an ideal spring with constant $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, and the sinusoidal force driving it has fixed amplitude.

- $14\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $7.1\,\mathrm{kg/s}$

## Explanation

The natural angular frequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. By the resonance theorem the amplitude has a maximum only if $\gamma < \dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$; if $\gamma \geq \dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$ the amplitude decreases as $\Omega$ increases. The required minimum value therefore corresponds to $\gamma = \dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$, and since $b = 2m\gamma$
$$
b = \sqrt{2}\,m\,\omega_0 = \sqrt{2} \cdot 0.50 \cdot 20\,\mathrm{kg/s} = 14\,\mathrm{kg/s} .
$$
The value $20\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ is the coefficient for critical damping, $2m\omega_0$, which is larger than required. The value $10\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ is $m\omega_0$, and $7.1\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ is obtained with $b = m\gamma$ instead of $b = 2m\gamma$.
