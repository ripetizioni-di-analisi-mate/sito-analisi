An oscillator on a frictionless horizontal surface has mechanical energy $E$ relative to equilibrium. Its speed is measured at equilibrium. Given the mass $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the equilibrium-crossing speed $v = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the mechanical energy $E$?

- $72\,\mathrm{J}$
- $36\,\mathrm{J}$
- $18\,\mathrm{J}$
- $54\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The elastic energy at equilibrium is zero, so $E$ equals the kinetic energy $mv^2/2$. In the stated units, the relation is $v = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{m}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $E = \dfrac{m \cdot v^{2}}{2}$. Substituting the given values yields $36\,\mathrm{J}$.
