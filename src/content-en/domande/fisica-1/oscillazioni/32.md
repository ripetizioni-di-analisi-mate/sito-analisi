A particle moves along the $x$-axis with potential energy $U(x) = x^4$, with $x$ in meters and $U$ in joules. Which statement about the position $x = 0$ is true?

- It is a position of unstable equilibrium, because $U''(0) = 0$
- About it the small oscillations are harmonic with zero angular frequency
- It is a position of stable equilibrium, and the theorem on small oscillations does not apply because $U''(0) = 0$
- It is not an equilibrium position, because $U(0) = 0$

## Explanation

The force is $F(x) = -U'(x) = -4x^3$, which vanishes at $x = 0$, so $x = 0$ is an equilibrium position. The function $U$ has an absolute minimum at $x = 0$ and the force points opposite to the displacement, therefore the equilibrium is **stable**. The theorem on small oscillations requires $U''(x_e) > 0$, while $U''(x) = 12x^2$ equals $0$ at $x = 0$, so the theorem does not apply. The oscillations about $x = 0$ are periodic, but the force is proportional to $x^3$ and the equation of motion does not have the form $\ddot{x} = -\omega^2 x$, therefore they are not harmonic.
