A mass oscillates without friction on an ideal vertical spring. The period is measured about equilibrium. The initial data are the mass $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the spring constant $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Change the mass to $9\,\mathrm{kg}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $T$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $3$

## Explanation

Weight shifts the equilibrium, while the change in spring force is $-kx$. Thus $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$ and the period is $2\pi/\omega$. The relation $T = \dfrac{2 \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{m}}{\sqrt{k}}$ gives $\dfrac{2 \cdot \pi}{5}\,\mathrm{s}$ before the change and $\dfrac{3 \cdot \pi}{5}\,\mathrm{s}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
