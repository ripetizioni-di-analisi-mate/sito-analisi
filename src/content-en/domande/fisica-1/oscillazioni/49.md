The position of a point moving along the $x$ axis is $x(t) = \cos(2t) + \cos(3t)$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. Is the motion periodic? If so, what is its period?

- Yes, with period $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- Yes, with period $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}\,\mathrm{s}$
- Yes, with period $2\pi\,\mathrm{s}$
- No, because the motion is not harmonic

## Explanation

The term $\cos(2t)$ has period $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$ and the term $\cos(3t)$ has period $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\,\mathrm{s}$. The sum repeats itself after a time $T$ if $T$ is an integer multiple of both periods, that is, if $T = n\pi = m\,\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ with $n$ and $m$ positive integers. This equality is equivalent to $3n = 2m$, and the smallest pair satisfying it is $n = 2$, $m = 3$. Therefore the motion is periodic with
$$
T = 2\pi\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The motion is not harmonic, because the position function does not have the form $A\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$; the definition of periodic motion only requires position and velocity to repeat after a time $T$. After $\pi\,\mathrm{s}$ the term $\cos(3t)$ has changed sign, and the period $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by adding the two angular frequencies.
