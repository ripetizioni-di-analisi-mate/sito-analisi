A simple pendulum of length $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is released from rest at a small angle $\theta_0$ from the vertical. After how much time is the angle equal to $\dfrac{\theta_0}{2}$ for the first time?

- $0.45\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.22\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.15\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The pendulum starts from rest at the angle $\theta_0$, so its equation of motion is $\theta(t) = \theta_0\cos(\omega t)$, with $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}} = 3.50\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The condition $\theta = \dfrac{\theta_0}{2}$ is equivalent to $\cos(\omega t) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and the first positive solution is $\omega t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. Therefore
$$
t = \frac{\pi}{3\omega} = \frac{T_0}{6} = \frac{1.79\,\mathrm{s}}{6} = 0.30\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $0.45\,\mathrm{s}$ is $\dfrac{T_0}{4}$, the time to reach the vertical; the value $0.22\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by assuming that the angle decreases uniformly in time, and $0.15\,\mathrm{s}$ by solving $\sin(\omega t) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ instead of $\cos(\omega t) = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
