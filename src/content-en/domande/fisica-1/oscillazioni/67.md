A physical pendulum is a rigid body with moment of inertia $I$ about its pivot. It has mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and swings through small angles. Assume $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the distance from pivot to center of mass $d = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ and the period $T = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$, what is the moment of inertia $I$?

- $5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $\dfrac{15}{2}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$

## Explanation

Linearizing the torque from gravity gives $I\ddot\theta=-mgd\theta$. It follows that $\omega=\sqrt{mgd/I}$. In the stated units, the relation is $T = \dfrac{\sqrt{5} \cdot \pi \cdot \sqrt{I}}{5 \cdot \sqrt{d}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $I = \dfrac{5 \cdot T^{2} \cdot d}{\pi^{2}}$. Substituting the given values yields $5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$.
