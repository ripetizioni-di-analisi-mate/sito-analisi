A flat wall of uniform thermal conductivity conducts heat in steady state. Its thickness is $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and the temperature difference across it is $10\,\mathrm{K}$. Given the wall area $A = 3\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and the rate of heat transfer $P = 300\,\mathrm{W}$, what is the thermal conductivity $k$?

- $20\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$
- $2\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$
- $4\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$

## Explanation

The temperature difference divided by thickness is $50\,\mathrm{K/m}$. The conduction law multiplies this by area and conductivity, giving watts. In the stated units, the relation is $P = 50 \cdot A \cdot k$. Solving for the unknown gives $k = \dfrac{P}{50 \cdot A}$. Substituting the given values yields $2\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$.
