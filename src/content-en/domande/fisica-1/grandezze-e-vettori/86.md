A body has variable mass $m(t) = (2.0 + 1.0\,t)\,\mathrm{kg}$ and velocity $\vec{v}(t) = (3.0\,t\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with $t$ in seconds. What is the derivative of the momentum $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$ at the instant $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $(12\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(3.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}$
- $12\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the differentiation rule for the product of a scalar and a vector
$$
\frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = \frac{dm}{dt}\,\vec{v} + m\,\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} .
$$
For $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ we have $\dfrac{dm}{dt} = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$, $\vec{v} = (3.0;\ 4.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $m = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $\dfrac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = (3.0;\ 0)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Therefore
$$
\frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = (3.0;\ 4.0)\,\mathrm{N} + (9.0;\ 0)\,\mathrm{N} = (12;\ 4.0)\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The vector $9.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{N}$ accounts only for the term $m\,\dfrac{d\vec{v}}{dt}$, as if the mass were constant.
