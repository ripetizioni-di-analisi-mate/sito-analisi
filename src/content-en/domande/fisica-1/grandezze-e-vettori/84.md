The force $\vec{F} = (4.0;\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{N}$ is written as the sum of a vector parallel to $\vec{B} = (3;\ 4)$ and a vector perpendicular to $\vec{B}$. What is the vector perpendicular to $\vec{B}$?

- $(2.4;\ 3.2)\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-1.6;\ 1.2)\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(1.6;\ -1.2)\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-8.0;\ -14)\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The unit vector of $\vec{B}$ is $\hat{u} = \dfrac{\vec{B}}{5} = (0.60;\ 0.80)$, and the component of $\vec{F}$ along $\hat{u}$ is
$$
\vec{F} \cdot \hat{u} = (4.0 \cdot 0.60 + 2.0 \cdot 0.80)\,\mathrm{N} = 4.0\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The vector parallel to $\vec{B}$ is therefore $\vec{F}_{\parallel} = 4.0\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \hat{u} = (2.4;\ 3.2)\,\mathrm{N}$. Hence the perpendicular vector is $\vec{F}_{\perp} = \vec{F} - \vec{F}_{\parallel} = (1.6;\ -1.2)\,\mathrm{N}$, and indeed $\vec{F}_{\perp} \cdot \vec{B} = (1.6 \cdot 3 - 1.2 \cdot 4)\,\mathrm{N} = 0$. The vector $(-1.6;\ 1.2)\,\mathrm{N}$ is also perpendicular to $\vec{B}$, but added to $\vec{F}_{\parallel}$ it gives $(0.8;\ 4.4)\,\mathrm{N}$, which differs from $\vec{F}$. The vector $(2.4;\ 3.2)\,\mathrm{N}$ is the parallel part, and $(-8.0;\ -14)\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by multiplying the vector $\vec{B}$ by $4.0$ instead of its unit vector.
