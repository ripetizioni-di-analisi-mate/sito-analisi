A body travels around the perimeter of a square of side $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, with its sides parallel to the $x$- and $y$-axes, and returns to its starting point. Along the way the constant force $\vec{F} = (3.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on it. What is the work done by the force over the whole path?

- $40\,\mathrm{J}$
- $10\,\mathrm{J}$
- $28\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The force is constant, so the work along the path is the sum of the works $\vec{F} \cdot \vec{s}_i$ on the four sides. By the distributive property of the dot product this sum equals $\vec{F} \cdot (\vec{s}_1 + \vec{s}_2 + \vec{s}_3 + \vec{s}_4)$. The body returns to its starting point, therefore the sum of the four displacements is the zero vector, and the total work is $0\,\mathrm{J}$. On each of the two sides parallel to the $x$-axis the work is $\pm 6.0\,\mathrm{J}$, and on the two sides parallel to the $y$-axis it is $\pm 8.0\,\mathrm{J}$, with opposite signs on sides travelled in opposite directions. The value $28\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by taking the work on all four sides as positive, and the value $40\,\mathrm{J}$ by multiplying the magnitude of the force, $5.0\,\mathrm{N}$, by the perimeter.
