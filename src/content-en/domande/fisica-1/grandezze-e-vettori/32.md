A velocity of magnitude $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ forms an angle of $150^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis, measured counterclockwise. What is its component $v_y$?

- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The component along $y$ is $v_y = v\sin\theta$. Since $\sin 150^\circ = \sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$, we obtain $v_y = 12 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The vector lies in the second quadrant, so the component $v_y$ is positive and the component $v_x = 12\cos 150^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx -10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is negative. The value $-10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is therefore the component $v_x$, which is obtained by using the cosine instead of the sine.
