A seesaw consists of a horizontal plank pivoted at its center. A boy of weight $300\,\mathrm{N}$ sits $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pivot, and a girl of weight $450\,\mathrm{N}$ sits on the other side. At what distance from the pivot must the girl sit for the total torque of the two weights about the pivot to be zero?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.67\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The weights are vertical and the plank is horizontal, so the lever arm of each weight about the pivot is the distance of the child from the pivot. The two torques have magnitude $\tau = P\,b$ and opposite senses, because the two children sit on opposite sides of the pivot. The total torque is therefore zero if the two magnitudes are equal, that is, if
$$
300\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m} = 450\,\mathrm{N} \cdot b .
$$
Hence $b = \dfrac{450}{450}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$, and the girl, who is heavier, sits closer to the pivot. The value $2.3\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by swapping the two weights in the ratio, the value $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance that makes the total torque zero only for two equal weights, and the value $0.67\,\mathrm{m}$ is the ratio of the weights without the boy's distance.
