The position of a point, in meters, is $\vec{r}(t) = t^2\,\hat{\imath} + 2t\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $t$ in seconds. What is the difference quotient $\dfrac{\Delta\vec{r}}{\Delta t}$, that is, the average velocity, between the instants $t = 0$ and $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $(4\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(4\,\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(2\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The positions at the given instants are $\vec{r}(0) = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{r}(2\,\mathrm{s}) = (4\,\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$, so $\Delta\vec{r} = (4\,\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$. Dividing by $\Delta t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ gives
$$
\frac{\Delta\vec{r}}{\Delta t} = (2\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The difference quotient has the direction of the chord joining the two positions, and as $\Delta t$ tends to zero it becomes the instantaneous velocity. The instantaneous velocity is $\dfrac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = 2t\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore the vectors $(4\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $2\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ are the instantaneous velocities at the instants $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t = 0$. The vector $(4\,\hat{\imath} + 4\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ has the components of $\Delta\vec{r}$ not divided by $\Delta t$.
