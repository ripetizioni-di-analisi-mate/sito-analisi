A flat plate can rotate about a perpendicular axis through the origin $O$. At its point $P = (2.0;\ 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ it is pulled with the force $\vec{F} = (3.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}$. What is the lever arm, that is, the distance of $O$ from the line of action of the force?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.7\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The torque about $O$ has only the $z$ component, which equals $\tau_z = x F_y - y F_x = 2.0 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 8.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. Since $\tau = F\,b$ and $F = 5.0\,\mathrm{N}$, the lever arm is
$$
b = \frac{\tau}{F} = \frac{8.0}{5.0}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.6\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance of $P$ from $O$, which coincides with the lever arm only for a force perpendicular to $\overrightarrow{OP}$. The value $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained with the cosine of the angle instead of the sine, and $2.7\,\mathrm{m}$ by dividing the torque by $F_x$.
