For the speed with which an object dropped from rest from height $h$ reaches the ground, neglecting air resistance, a student proposes the formula $v = 2\sqrt{g h}$. Which statement is true?

- The formula is homogeneous, and dimensional analysis cannot establish whether the coefficient $2$ is correct
- The formula is not homogeneous, so it is certainly wrong
- The formula is homogeneous, so it is certainly correct
- The formula is homogeneous only if $h$ is measured in meters

## Explanation

The dimensions of the right-hand side are $\sqrt{[L\,T^{-2}]\,[L]} = [L\,T^{-1}]$, that is, those of a speed, and the coefficient $2$ is dimensionless. Therefore the formula is **homogeneous**, whatever unit is chosen for $h$. Dimensional analysis does not provide numerical coefficients, so it cannot tell whether the $2$ is right. Conservation of mechanical energy gives $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$, so the proposed formula is homogeneous but wrong.
