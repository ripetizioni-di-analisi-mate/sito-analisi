Two vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ have the same magnitude $A$, and their sum $\vec{A} + \vec{B}$ also has magnitude $A$. What angle do $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ make?

- $120^\circ$
- $60^\circ$
- $90^\circ$
- $180^\circ$

## Explanation

The square of the magnitude of the sum is $|\vec{A} + \vec{B}|^2 = A^2 + B^2 + 2AB\cos\theta$. With $B = A$ and $|\vec{A} + \vec{B}| = A$ we get $A^2 = 2A^2 + 2A^2\cos\theta$, so $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\theta = 120^\circ$. Indeed, with the tip-to-tail rule, $\vec{A}$, $\vec{B}$ and $\vec{A} + \vec{B}$ form an equilateral triangle, and the angle between $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ is supplementary to the interior angle of $60^\circ$. For $\theta = 60^\circ$ the sum would have magnitude $\sqrt{3}\,A$.
