A crate is dragged $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ across a horizontal floor by a horizontal force of $20\,\mathrm{N}$, while friction exerts on it a force of $8.0\,\mathrm{N}$ opposite to the displacement. The weight and the normal force of the floor also act on the crate. What is the total work done by all the forces acting on the crate?

- $84\,\mathrm{J}$
- $36\,\mathrm{J}$
- $60\,\mathrm{J}$
- $24\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The total work is the sum of the works done by the individual forces. The weight and the normal force are vertical, so they are perpendicular to the displacement and their work is zero. The pulling force is parallel to the displacement and in the same direction, therefore it does the work $20 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{J} = 60\,\mathrm{J}$. Friction points opposite to the displacement, so it forms the angle $\theta = 180^\circ$ with it and does the work $-8.0 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{J} = -24\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore the total work is $L = (60 - 24)\,\mathrm{J} = 36\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $84\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by taking the work of friction as positive too, the value $60\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work of the pulling force alone, and $24\,\mathrm{J}$ is the absolute value of the work of friction.
