The position of a point is $\vec{r}(t) = 1{,}5\,t^2\,\hat{\imath} + \left( 2{,}0\,t^3 - 6{,}0\,t \right)\hat{\jmath}$, with lengths in meters and time in seconds. What is the velocity at the instant $t = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $(3{,}0\,\hat{\imath} + 24\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(6{,}0\,\hat{\imath} + 4{,}0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(6{,}0\,\hat{\imath} + 24\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(6{,}0\,\hat{\imath} + 18\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The velocity is the derivative of the position vector, which is computed component by component, so $\vec{v}(t) = 3{,}0\,t\,\hat{\imath} + \left(6{,}0\,t^2 - 6{,}0\right)\hat{\jmath}$. For $t = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$ we obtain $v_x = 3{,}0 \cdot 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_y = (6{,}0 \cdot 4{,}0 - 6{,}0)\,\mathrm{m/s} = 18\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The vector $(6{,}0\,\hat{\imath} + 24\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by neglecting the derivative of the term $-6{,}0\,t$. The components $3{,}0$ and $24$ are those of the acceleration $\dfrac{d\vec{v}}{dt}$ at the same instant, in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the components $6{,}0$ and $4{,}0$ are those of the position, in meters.
