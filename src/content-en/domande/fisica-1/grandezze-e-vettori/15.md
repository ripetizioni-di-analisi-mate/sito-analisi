A pressure equals $3{,}0\,\mathrm{N/cm^2}$. What is its value in pascals?

- $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The pascal is $1\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1\,\mathrm{N/m^2}$, so the square centimeter in the denominator is expressed in square meters. Since $1\,\mathrm{cm^2} = (10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^2 = 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, we have
$$
3{,}0\,\frac{\mathrm{N}}{\mathrm{cm^2}} = \frac{3{,}0\,\mathrm{N}}{10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}} = 3{,}0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is obtained without squaring the factor of the prefix, the value $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ by multiplying by $10^{-4}$ instead of dividing. The value $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ uses the factor $10^{-6}$ of cubic centimeters.
