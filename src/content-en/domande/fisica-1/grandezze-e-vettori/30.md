A car goes around a bend with velocity of constant magnitude $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At the entrance of the bend it moves east, at the exit it moves north. What is the magnitude of the change in velocity $\Delta\vec{v}$?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $40\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $28\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

With the $x$-axis pointing east and the $y$-axis pointing north, the velocities are $\vec{v}_1 = (20;\ 0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\vec{v}_2 = (0;\ 20)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The change is $\Delta\vec{v} = \vec{v}_2 - \vec{v}_1 = (-20;\ 20)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so its magnitude is $20\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 28\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The change is nonzero because the velocity changes direction, even though its magnitude stays the same. The value $40\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by adding the magnitudes, as if the two velocities had the same direction and opposite orientations.
