A vector $\vec{A}(t)$, measured in meters, has constant magnitude. At a certain instant $\vec{A} = (3.0;\ 4.0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\dfrac{d\vec{A}}{dt} = (a;\ 6.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is $a$?

- $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Since the magnitude of $\vec{A}$ is constant, the derivative is perpendicular to $\vec{A}$, that is, $\vec{A} \cdot \dfrac{d\vec{A}}{dt} = 0$. In components the condition is $3.0\,a + 4.0 \cdot 6.0 = 0$, so $a = -\dfrac{24}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = -8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $-4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by pairing $a$ with the component $A_y$ and $6.0$ with the component $A_x$.
