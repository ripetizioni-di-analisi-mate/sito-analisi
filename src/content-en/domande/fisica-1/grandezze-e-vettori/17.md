In three days a refrigerator uses an energy of $2.5\,\mathrm{kWh}$. What is this energy in joules?

- $9.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $9.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The kilowatt-hour is the energy transferred by a power of $1\,\mathrm{kW}$ in one hour. Since $1\,\mathrm{kW} = 10^{3}\,\mathrm{W}$, $1\,\mathrm{h} = 3600\,\mathrm{s}$ and $1\,\mathrm{W\cdot s} = 1\,\mathrm{J}$, we have
$$
1\,\mathrm{kWh} = 10^{3}\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 3.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Therefore the energy used is $2.5 \cdot 3.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J} = 9.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $9.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by neglecting the prefix kilo, the value $1.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$ by counting $60\,\mathrm{s}$ in an hour.
