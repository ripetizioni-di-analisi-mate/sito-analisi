A point moves along a parabola with position $\vec{r}(t) = t\,\hat{\imath} + t^2\,\hat{\jmath}$, with lengths in meters and time in seconds. What angle does the velocity make with the positive $x$-semiaxis at the instant $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $45.0^\circ$
- $26.6^\circ$
- $76.0^\circ$
- $63.4^\circ$

## Explanation

The velocity is $\vec{v} = \dfrac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = \hat{\imath} + 2t\,\hat{\jmath}$, so for $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$ it equals $(1;\ 2)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since $v_x > 0$, the angle is $\theta = \arctan 2 \approx 63.4^\circ$. The velocity is tangent to the trajectory, and indeed the tangent line to the parabola $y = x^2$ at the point $(1;\ 1)$ has slope $2$. The value $45.0^\circ$ is the angle of the position vector $(1;\ 1)$, and $26.6^\circ$ is obtained by swapping the components.
