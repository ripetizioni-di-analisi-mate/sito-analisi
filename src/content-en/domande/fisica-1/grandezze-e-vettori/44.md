The square of the distance of a moving point from the origin is $\vec{r} \cdot \vec{r}$. If the position, in meters, is $\vec{r}(t) = t\,\hat{\imath} + 2t^2\,\hat{\jmath}$ with $t$ in seconds, what is the time derivative of $\vec{r} \cdot \vec{r}$ at the instant $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $18\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$

## Explanation

By the differentiation rule for the dot product
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\big(\vec{r} \cdot \vec{r}\big) = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} \cdot \vec{r} + \vec{r} \cdot \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = 2\,\vec{r} \cdot \vec{v} .
$$
The velocity is $\vec{v} = \hat{\imath} + 4t\,\hat{\jmath}$, so for $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$ we have $\vec{r} = (1;\ 2)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\vec{v} = (1;\ 4)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Then $\vec{r} \cdot \vec{v} = (1 + 8)\,\mathrm{m^2/s} = 9\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ and the derivative equals $18\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$. The same result is obtained by differentiating $\vec{r} \cdot \vec{r} = t^2 + 4t^4$, which gives $2t + 16t^3$. The value $9\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ forgets the factor $2$, the value $5\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ has the numerical value of $\vec{r} \cdot \vec{r} = 5\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at the same instant.
