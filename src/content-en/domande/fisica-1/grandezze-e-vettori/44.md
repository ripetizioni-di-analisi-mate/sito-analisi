The position of a point is $\vec{r}(t) = 2.0\,t^3\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,t\,\hat{\jmath}$, with lengths in meters and time in seconds. What is the acceleration at the instant $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $6.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $(6.0\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $(12\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $12\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Differentiating component by component gives the velocity $\vec{v}(t) = 6.0\,t^2\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath}$, and differentiating again gives the acceleration $\vec{a}(t) = 12\,t\,\hat{\imath}$. The component $v_y = -4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is constant, so its derivative is zero and the acceleration has zero component along $y$. For $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ we have $\vec{a} = 12\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The vector $(6.0\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ has the components of the velocity at the same instant, and the vector $(12\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is obtained by differentiating only the first component twice.
