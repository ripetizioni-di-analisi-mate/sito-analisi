A point moves along a circle and the magnitude of its velocity $\vec{v}$ is constant. Which statement about the acceleration $\vec{a} = \dfrac{d\vec{v}}{dt}$ is true at every instant?

- $\vec{a}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{v}$
- $\vec{a}$ is zero
- $\vec{a}$ is parallel to $\vec{v}$ with the same orientation
- $\vec{a}$ is parallel to $\vec{v}$ with the opposite orientation

## Explanation

The vector $\vec{v}$ has constant magnitude, so $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{v} = v^2$ is constant and its derivative is zero. By the differentiation rule for the dot product, $\dfrac{d}{dt}(\vec{v} \cdot \vec{v}) = 2\,\vec{v} \cdot \vec{a}$, therefore $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{a} = 0$. The point moves along a circle, so the direction of $\vec{v}$, tangent to the trajectory, changes at every instant and $\vec{a}$ is nonzero. It follows that the acceleration is **perpendicular** to the velocity at every instant.
