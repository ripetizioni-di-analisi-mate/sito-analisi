A material has uniform thermal conductivity. A flat wall transfers heat under steady-state conditions. Its thickness is $0{,}20\,\mathrm{m}$ and the temperature difference across it is $10\,\mathrm{K}$. Given the thermal conductivity $k = 2\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$ and the rate of heat transfer $P = 300\,\mathrm{W}$, what is the wall area $A$?

- $3\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $30\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m^2}$

## Explanation

The temperature difference divided by thickness is $50\,\mathrm{K/m}$. The conduction law multiplies this by area and conductivity, giving watts. In the stated units, the relation is $P = 50 \cdot A \cdot k$. Solving for the unknown gives $A = \dfrac{P}{50 \cdot k}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
