The position of a car starting from rest is described by the law $x = \dfrac{1}{2} a t^2 + c\,t^3$, where $x$ is a length, $a$ an acceleration and $t$ the time. What dimensions must the constant $c$ have for the law to be homogeneous?

- $[L\,T^{-3}]$
- $[L\,T^{-2}]$
- $[L\,T^{3}]$
- $[T^{-3}]$

## Explanation

By the principle of homogeneity every term of the sum has the dimensions of $x$, that is, $[L]$. Therefore $[c]\,[T^{3}] = [L]$, which gives
$$
[c] = [L\,T^{-3}] .
$$
The dimensions $[L\,T^{-2}]$ are those of an acceleration and would make the term $c\,t^3$ a length times a time. The dimensions $[L\,T^{3}]$ are obtained by multiplying by $t^3$ instead of dividing.
