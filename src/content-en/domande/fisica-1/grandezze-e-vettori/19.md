Which of the following pairs consists of two quantities with the same dimensions?

- Force and momentum
- Power and work
- Work and the torque of a force
- Pressure and force

## Explanation

Work is the dot product of a force and a displacement, so it has dimensions $[M\,L\,T^{-2}]\,[L] = [M\,L^{2}\,T^{-2}]$. The magnitude of the torque is $\tau = F\,b$, the product of a force and the lever arm, which is a length, so the torque also has dimensions $[M\,L^{2}\,T^{-2}]$. Both quantities are measured in $\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, and the name joule is not used for the torque because the torque is a vector and work is a scalar. Momentum has dimensions $[M\,L\,T^{-1}]$, different from those of force, $[M\,L\,T^{-2}]$. Power is work divided by a time, so it has an extra factor $[T^{-1}]$ compared with work. Pressure is a force divided by an area and has dimensions $[M\,L^{-1}\,T^{-2}]$.
