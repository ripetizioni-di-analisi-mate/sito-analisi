A hiker walks $5.0\,\mathrm{km}$ east, then $2.0\,\mathrm{km}$ north and finally $1.0\,\mathrm{km}$ west. What is the magnitude of the hiker's total displacement?

- $5.4\,\mathrm{km}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{km}$
- $6.3\,\mathrm{km}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{km}$

## Explanation

The $x$-axis is taken pointing east and the $y$-axis pointing north. The displacements are $(5.0;\ 0)\,\mathrm{km}$, $(0;\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{km}$ and $(-1.0;\ 0)\,\mathrm{km}$, so the total displacement is $\Delta\vec{r} = (4.0;\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{km}$. Its magnitude is
$$
\Delta r = \sqrt{4.0^2 + 2.0^2}\,\mathrm{km} = \sqrt{20}\,\mathrm{km} \approx 4.5\,\mathrm{km} .
$$
The value $8.0\,\mathrm{km}$ is the distance traveled, that is, the sum of the lengths of the three legs. The value $6.3\,\mathrm{km}$ is obtained by adding the westward leg with a positive sign, and the value $5.4\,\mathrm{km}$ by neglecting that leg.
