A cyclist covers $12\,\mathrm{km}$ in $40\,\mathrm{min}$. What is the cyclist's average speed in $\mathrm{m/s}$?

- $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0{,}30\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

In SI units, the distance is $12\,\mathrm{km} = 1{,}2 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m}$ and the time is $40\,\mathrm{min} = 40 \cdot 60\,\mathrm{s} = 2{,}4 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}$. The average speed is therefore
$$
v = \frac{1{,}2 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m}}{2{,}4 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}} = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The number $18$ is the speed expressed in $\mathrm{km/h}$, since $40\,\mathrm{min}$ is $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{h}$. The value $3{,}0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by converting kilometers to meters and leaving the time in minutes, the value $0{,}30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by leaving both quantities in their original units.
