Let $x$ and $x_0$ be lengths, $t$ a time, $v$ and $v_0$ velocities and $a$ an acceleration. Which of the following relations is dimensionally homogeneous?

- $x = x_0 + a\,t$
- $x = x_0 + v_0\,t^2$
- $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\,(x - x_0)$
- $v = v_0 + a\,x$

## Explanation

In the relation $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\,(x - x_0)$ every term has dimensions $[L^2\,T^{-2}]$, since $[a]\,[x] = [L\,T^{-2}]\,[L] = [L^2\,T^{-2}]$ and the factor $2$ is dimensionless. In the relation $x = x_0 + v_0\,t^2$ the term $v_0\,t^2$ has dimensions $[L\,T]$ and is added to a length. The term $a\,x$ has dimensions $[L^2\,T^{-2}]$, different from those of $v$, and the term $a\,t$ has the dimensions of a velocity, different from those of $x$. These three relations therefore do not satisfy the principle of dimensional homogeneity.
