Two nonzero vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ satisfy $|\vec{A} + \vec{B}| = |\vec{A} - \vec{B}|$. Which statement is true?

- $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ are parallel
- $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ have the same magnitude
- $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ are perpendicular
- $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ lie along the same line and point in opposite directions

## Explanation

Squaring the two magnitudes with the dot product gives
$$
A^2 + 2\,\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} + B^2 = A^2 - 2\,\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} + B^2 ,
$$
so $4\,\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = 0$. Since the two vectors are nonzero, the dot product is zero only if they are **perpendicular**. Geometrically, the two diagonals of the parallelogram with sides $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ have the same length, so the parallelogram is a rectangle. Equal magnitudes of $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ correspond instead to perpendicular diagonals.
