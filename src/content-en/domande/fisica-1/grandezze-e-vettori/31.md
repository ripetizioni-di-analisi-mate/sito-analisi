A point moves from the position $\vec{r}_1 = (3.0\,\hat{\imath} - 1.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$ to the position $\vec{r}_2 = (-1.0\,\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the magnitude of the displacement?

- $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.93\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The displacement is $\Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1$, and subtracting component by component gives $\Delta\vec{r} = (-4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$. Its magnitude is $\sqrt{4.0^2 + 3.0^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $2.2\,\mathrm{m}$ is the magnitude of the sum $\vec{r}_1 + \vec{r}_2$, and the value $0.93\,\mathrm{m}$ is the difference between the magnitudes of the two positions, $\sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{m} - \sqrt{5}\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $7.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by adding the absolute values of the components of $\Delta\vec{r}$ instead of applying the Pythagorean theorem.
