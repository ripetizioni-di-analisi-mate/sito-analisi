Two vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ satisfy $\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = 0$, with $\vec{A} \neq \vec{0}$. What can be deduced about $\vec{B}$?

- $\vec{B} = \vec{0}$
- $\vec{B}$ is nonzero and perpendicular to $\vec{A}$
- $\vec{B}$ is nonzero and parallel to $\vec{A}$
- $\vec{B} = -\vec{A}$

## Explanation

The magnitude of the cross product is $AB\sin\theta$ and the dot product is $AB\cos\theta$. Since both are zero, we have $(AB\sin\theta)^2 + (AB\cos\theta)^2 = A^2B^2 = 0$. Since $A \neq 0$, it follows that $B = 0$, that is, $\vec{B} = \vec{0}$. A nonzero vector cannot be both parallel and perpendicular to $\vec{A}$, and $\vec{B} = -\vec{A}$ gives $\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = -A^2 \neq 0$.
