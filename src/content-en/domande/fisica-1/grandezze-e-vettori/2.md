Seawater has a density of $1.03\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the mass of $2.5\,\mathrm{L}$ of seawater?

- $2.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.6\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

A liter is a cubic decimeter, and since $1\,\mathrm{dm} = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ we have $1\,\mathrm{L} = 10^{3}\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. The volume is therefore $V = 2.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, and the mass is
$$
m = \rho V = 1.03\,\mathrm{g/cm^3} \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{cm^3} \approx 2.6 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{g} = 2.6\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The result has two significant figures, like the volume. The value $2.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}$ corresponds to mistaking a liter for a cubic centimeter, the value $2.6 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$ to mistaking a liter for a cubic meter.
