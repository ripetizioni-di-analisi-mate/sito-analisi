The radius of the Earth is about $6.4 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$ and the radius of the hydrogen atom about $5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the order of magnitude of the ratio of the radius of the Earth to that of the atom?

- $10^{-5}$
- $10^{18}$
- $10^{17}$
- $10^{-17}$

## Explanation

Separating the coefficients from the powers of ten gives
$$
\frac{6.4 \cdot 10^{6}}{5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}} = \frac{6.4}{5.3} \cdot 10^{17} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^{17} .
$$
Since $1.2 < \sqrt{10}$, the order of magnitude is $10^{17}$. The value $10^{-5}$ is obtained by adding the exponents instead of subtracting them, the value $10^{-17}$ is the order of the inverse ratio.
