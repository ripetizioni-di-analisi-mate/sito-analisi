Two displacements are perpendicular. The first is horizontal and the second vertical. The initial data are the magnitude of the horizontal displacement $x = 9\,\mathrm{m}$ and the magnitude of the vertical displacement $y = 12\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the magnitude of the vertical displacement to $40\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $R$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{7}{3}$
- $\dfrac{31}{3}$
- $\dfrac{41}{15}$
- $\dfrac{15}{41}$

## Explanation

The components of the resultant are $x$ and $y$. Its magnitude follows from the Pythagorean theorem. The relation $R = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}$ gives $15\,\mathrm{m}$ before the change and $41\,\mathrm{m}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{41}{15}$.
