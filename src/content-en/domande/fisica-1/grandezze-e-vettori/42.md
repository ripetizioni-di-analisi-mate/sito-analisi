A door is opened by pushing with a force perpendicular to the door, first at $0{,}80\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis of the hinges and then at $0{,}40\,\mathrm{m}$, with the same magnitude. How does the magnitude of the torque of the force about the axis of the hinges change?

- It is halved
- It doubles
- It stays the same
- It becomes one quarter

## Explanation

The magnitude of the torque is $\tau = F\,b$, where the lever arm $b$ is the distance of the axis from the line of action of the force. The force is perpendicular to the door, so the lever arm coincides with the distance of the point where the push is applied from the hinges. For the same $F$ the torque is proportional to the lever arm, and since the lever arm goes from $0{,}80\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0{,}40\,\mathrm{m}$, the torque is halved. To obtain the same torque by pushing at $0{,}40\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinges, a force twice as large is needed.
