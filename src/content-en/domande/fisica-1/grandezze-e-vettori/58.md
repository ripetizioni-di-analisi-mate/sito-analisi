A point moves with position $\vec{r}(t) = R\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}$, where $R = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\omega = 3.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. What is the velocity at the instant $t = 0$?

- $6.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.0\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.0\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-18\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The velocity is obtained by differentiating each component, with the chain rule:
$$
\vec{v}(t) = -R\omega\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
For $t = 0$ we have $\sin 0 = 0$ and $\cos 0 = 1$, so $\vec{v}(0) = R\omega\,\hat{\jmath} = 6.0\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The velocity is perpendicular to the position $\vec{r}(0) = 2.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m}$, as it must be for a position vector of constant magnitude. The value $2.0\,\hat{\jmath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ forgets the inner derivative $\omega$, and $-18\,\hat{\imath}$ is the numerical value of the acceleration in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
