Treating the Earth as a sphere of radius $R = 6{,}37 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the order of magnitude of its volume?

- $10^{15}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $10^{20}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $10^{21}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $10^{18}\,\mathrm{m^3}$

## Explanation

The volume of the sphere is $V = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$. Since $6{,}37^3 \approx 258$ and $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi \approx 4{,}19$, we obtain
$$
V \approx 4{,}19 \cdot 258 \cdot 10^{18}\,\mathrm{m^3} \approx 1{,}08 \cdot 10^{21}\,\mathrm{m^3} .
$$
The coefficient $1{,}08$ is less than $\sqrt{10} \approx 3{,}16$, so the order of magnitude is $10^{21}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The order $10^{20}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi$, because $R^3 \approx 2{,}6 \cdot 10^{20}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, and the order $10^{18}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ by cubing only the power of ten. The order $10^{15}$ is that of the surface area $4\pi R^2 \approx 5{,}1 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
