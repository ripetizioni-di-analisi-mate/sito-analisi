$1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water is heated from $300\,\mathrm{K}$ to $360\,\mathrm{K}$ in two ways: by putting it in contact with a single reservoir at $360\,\mathrm{K}$, or with a very dense sequence of reservoirs at temperatures increasing from $300\,\mathrm{K}$ to $360\,\mathrm{K}$. Which statement is true?

- The entropy of the water increases more in the first way, because the heat exchange is irreversible
- The entropy of the universe increases by the same amount in the two ways, because the states of the water are the same
- The entropy of the water increases by the same amount in the two ways, while the entropy of the universe increases by about $66\,\mathrm{J/K}$ in the first way and almost not at all in the second
- The entropy of the universe does not change in the first way and increases in the second

## Explanation

The initial and final states of the water are the same in the two ways, therefore its change in entropy is the same, $m c \ln\dfrac{360}{300} \approx 763\,\mathrm{J/K}$. In the first way the reservoir gives up $m c \cdot 60\,\mathrm{K} \approx 2.51 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$ at $360\,\mathrm{K}$, hence its entropy decreases by about $698\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and $\Delta S_{\text{u}} \approx 66\,\mathrm{J/K}$. In the second way each reservoir gives up heat at a temperature that differs very little from that of the water, therefore each exchange is nearly reversible and $\Delta S_{\text{u}}$ gets closer to zero the denser the sequence.
