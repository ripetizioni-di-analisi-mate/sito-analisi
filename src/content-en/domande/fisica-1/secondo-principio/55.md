A freezer keeps its interior at $-18\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ in a kitchen at $27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and has a coefficient of performance equal to $40\%$ of that of a Carnot refrigerator between the same temperatures. The motor draws a power of $100\,\mathrm{W}$. How much heat does it extract from the interior in one hour?

- $0.82\,\mathrm{MJ}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{MJ}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{MJ}$
- $0.14\,\mathrm{MJ}$

## Explanation

In kelvins the temperatures are $T_2 = 255\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_1 = 300\,\mathrm{K}$, therefore the Carnot coefficient of performance is $\varepsilon_C = \dfrac{255}{45} \approx 5.67$. The coefficient of performance of the freezer is hence $\varepsilon = 0.40 \cdot 5.67 \approx 2.27$. In one hour the motor spends $100\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 3.6 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$, and the heat extracted is
$$
Q_2 = \varepsilon\, |L| \approx 2.27 \cdot 3.6 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} \approx 0.82\,\mathrm{MJ} .
$$
The value $2.0\,\mathrm{MJ}$ corresponds to the Carnot refrigerator, and $1.2\,\mathrm{MJ}$ is the heat released to the kitchen, $Q_2 + |L|$.
