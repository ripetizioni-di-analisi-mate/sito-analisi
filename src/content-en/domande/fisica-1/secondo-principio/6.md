- It is not compatible, because the maximum coefficient of performance is about $6{,}4$

## Explanation

Between $T_2 = 255\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_1 = 295\,\mathrm{K}$ the refrigerator with the maximum coefficient of performance is the Carnot one, with
$$
\varepsilon_C = \frac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2} = \frac{255}{40} \approx 6{,}4 .
$$
From Carnot's theorem it follows that no refrigerator between the same reservoirs has a coefficient of performance greater than $\varepsilon_C$. Since $9{,}0 > 6{,}4$, the claim is **incompatible** with the second law. A coefficient of performance greater than $1$ is instead possible for any refrigerator, because the work input serves only to transfer heat.
