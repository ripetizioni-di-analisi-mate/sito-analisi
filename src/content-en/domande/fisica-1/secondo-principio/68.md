Suppose there is a machine $M$ that in each cycle absorbs $400\,\mathrm{J}$ from a hot reservoir and turns all of it into work. This work drives a refrigerator with coefficient of performance $\varepsilon = 3.0$, which works between the same hot reservoir and a cold reservoir. How much heat does the hot reservoir receive in total in one cycle of the two machines together?

- $1600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2000\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The refrigerator receives the work $|L| = 400\,\mathrm{J}$, therefore it extracts $Q_2 = \varepsilon\,|L| = 1200\,\mathrm{J}$ from the cold reservoir and releases $Q_2 + |L| = 1600\,\mathrm{J}$ to the hot reservoir. In the same cycle the machine $M$ takes $400\,\mathrm{J}$ from the hot reservoir. Hence the hot reservoir receives in total $1600\,\mathrm{J} - 400\,\mathrm{J} = 1200\,\mathrm{J}$, exactly the heat given up by the cold reservoir. The two machines together exchange no work with the outside, so their only result is the transfer of $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ from the cold body to the hot body. Therefore a machine violating the Kelvin-Planck statement would allow a violation of the **Clausius statement**.
