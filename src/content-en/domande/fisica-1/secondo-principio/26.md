A Carnot engine works between $T_1 = 600\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_2 = 300\,\mathrm{K}$. One can raise $T_1$ by $100\,\mathrm{K}$ keeping $T_2$ fixed, or lower $T_2$ by $100\,\mathrm{K}$ keeping $T_1$ fixed. Which statement is true?

- The two changes give the same efficiency, because the difference $T_1 - T_2$ becomes the same
- Lowering $T_2$ gives the higher efficiency, about $0.67$ against about $0.57$
- Raising $T_1$ gives the higher efficiency, about $0.67$ against about $0.57$
- Neither change affects the efficiency, which stays $0.50$

## Explanation

The Carnot efficiency is $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$. Raising $T_1$ to $700\,\mathrm{K}$ gives $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{300}{700} \approx 0.57$. Lowering $T_2$ to $200\,\mathrm{K}$ gives $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{200}{600} \approx 0.67$. The difference $T_1 - T_2$ is $400\,\mathrm{K}$ in both cases, but the efficiency is $\dfrac{T_1 - T_2}{T_1}$, so for the same difference it is larger when $T_1$ is lower, that is in the second case.
