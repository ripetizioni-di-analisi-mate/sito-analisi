A Carnot engine operates between $T_1 = 600\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_2 = 300\,\mathrm{K}$. The temperature of the hot reservoir is raised to $1200\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_2$ is left unchanged. What is the new efficiency?

- $0.75$
- $1.0$
- $0.50$
- $0.25$

## Explanation

The initial efficiency is $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{300}{600} = 0.50$, and after the change it becomes $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{300}{1200} = 0.75$. The efficiency depends on $T_1$ through the ratio $\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$, therefore doubling $T_1$ halves $\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$ and the efficiency goes from $0.50$ to $0.75$. An efficiency equal to $1$ is excluded by the Kelvin-Planck statement.
