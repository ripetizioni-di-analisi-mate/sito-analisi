An ideal gas, in contact with a single reservoir, expands isothermally and reversibly and converts all the heat it absorbs into work. Which statement is true?

- The process violates the Kelvin-Planck statement
- The process violates the first law, because the work cannot equal the heat absorbed
- The process is compatible with the second law, because at the end the volume of the gas has increased
- The process is possible only if the gas releases part of the heat to a second reservoir

## Explanation

In an isothermal process the internal energy of the ideal gas remains constant, therefore by the first law $L = Q$. The **Kelvin-Planck statement** excludes a process whose *only* result is the complete conversion into work of heat taken from a single reservoir. At the end of this process the gas occupies a larger volume, hence the work produced is not the only result and the process satisfies the statement. The statement instead excludes a *cyclic* machine with a single reservoir, because in a cycle the gas would have to return to its initial volume.
