A gas enclosed in a rigid cylinder is cooled by putting it in contact with colder surroundings, and its entropy decreases. Which statement is true?

- The process violates the principle of entropy increase
- The process does not violate the principle of entropy increase, because the gas is not an isolated system
- The process is possible only if the cooling is reversible
- The entropy of the gas stays constant, because the volume does not change

## Explanation

The principle of entropy increase concerns an *isolated* system, which exchanges neither heat nor work with the outside. The gas releases heat to the surroundings, therefore it is not isolated and its entropy can decrease. The isolated system is the universe made of the gas and the surroundings. The surroundings absorb the heat released by the gas at a temperature lower than that of the gas, hence the increase in their entropy exceeds the decrease in that of the gas and the entropy of the universe **increases**.
