Which of the following processes, if it occurred, would be ruled out by the Clausius statement of the second law?

- A refrigerator, powered by an electric motor, extracts heat from the compartment and releases it to the warmer kitchen
- An ice cube melts in a room at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- In an insulated box two blocks in contact, initially at the same temperature, become one hotter and the other colder, with no other effect
- A metal bar conducts heat from its hot end to its cold end

## Explanation

The Clausius statement rules out a process whose *only* result is the transfer of heat from a colder body to a hotter one. In the insulated box, as soon as the temperatures of the two blocks become different, the hotter block would keep receiving heat from the colder one with no other effect. Therefore the process is ruled out. The refrigerator transfers heat from cold to hot, but it receives work from the motor, hence the heat transfer is not the only result. The melting of the ice and the conduction in the bar are transfers of heat from the hotter body to the colder one.
