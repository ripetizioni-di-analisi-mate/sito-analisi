In which of the following processes does the entropy of an ideal gas decrease?

- Reversible isothermal expansion
- Reversible isobaric expansion
- Free expansion into vacuum in an insulated container
- Cooling at constant volume

## Explanation

For the ideal gas $\Delta S = n c_V \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A} + nR \ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$. In the isothermal expansion and in the free expansion the final temperature equals the initial one and the volume increases, therefore $\Delta S > 0$. In the isobaric expansion both the volume and the temperature increase, hence here too $\Delta S > 0$. In the cooling at constant volume the second term is zero and $T_B < T_A$, therefore $\Delta S = n c_V \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A} < 0$.
