An irreversible engine works between a reservoir at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ and a reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. In each cycle it absorbs $900\,\mathrm{J}$ from the hot reservoir, and the entropy of the universe increases by $0.50\,\mathrm{J/K}$. How much heat does it release to the cold reservoir in each cycle?

- $450\,\mathrm{J}$
- $300\,\mathrm{J}$
- $150\,\mathrm{J}$
- $600\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In a cycle the entropy of the working fluid does not change, therefore the change in entropy of the universe is the sum of those of the two reservoirs. The hot reservoir gives up $900\,\mathrm{J}$ at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ and the cold reservoir receives the heat $Q$ at $300\,\mathrm{K}$, hence
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = -\frac{900\,\mathrm{J}}{600\,\mathrm{K}} + \frac{Q}{300\,\mathrm{K}} = 0.50\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
It follows that $\dfrac{Q}{300\,\mathrm{K}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{J/K}$, that is, $Q = 600\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $450\,\mathrm{J}$ is the heat released by a reversible engine, for which $\Delta S_{\text{u}} = 0$, and $150\,\mathrm{J} = 300\,\mathrm{K} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is the work lost compared with the reversible engine.
