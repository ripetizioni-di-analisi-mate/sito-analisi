An ideal gas goes through the Carnot cycle in the figure, with $A \to B$ and $C \to D$ isotherms. In which part does the temperature of the gas decrease without the gas exchanging heat?

::grafico{curve="t<1?(1+(1.5)*(t-0)):(t<2?(2.5+(2.5)*(t-1)):(t<3?(5+(-3)*(t-2)):((2+(-1)*(t-3))))),t<1?8/(1+(1.5)*(t-0)):(t<2?14.74/(2.5+(2.5)*(t-1))^1.6667:(t<3?5.04/(5+(-3)*(t-2)):(8/(2+(-1)*(t-3))^1.6667))),0,4" vettori="1.6,5,1.9,4.21;3.5,1.83,4,1.46;3.8,1.33,3.2,1.57;1.6,3.65,1.4,4.57" punti="1,8,pieno,A;2.5,3.2,pieno,B;5,1.01,pieno,C;2,2.52,pieno,D" legenda=0 xmin=-0.3 xmax=6 ymin=-0.5 ymax=9 nomi="V,p" titolo="Carnot cycle of an ideal gas in the $(V, p)$ plane, in arbitrary units"}

- $A \to B$
- $B \to C$
- $C \to D$
- $D \to A$

## Explanation

Along the isotherms $A \to B$ and $C \to D$ the temperature stays constant and the gas exchanges heat with the reservoirs. The parts $B \to C$ and $D \to A$ are adiabats, in which the gas exchanges no heat. In the adiabatic expansion $B \to C$ the gas does work at the expense of its own internal energy, therefore the temperature drops from $T_1$ to $T_2$. In the adiabatic compression $D \to A$ the temperature rises again instead.
