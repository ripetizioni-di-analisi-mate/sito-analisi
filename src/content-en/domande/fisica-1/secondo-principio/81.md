In the $(S, T)$ plane the curves $a$ and $b$ represent two reversible heatings of the same ideal gas starting from the same state $A$: one at constant volume and one at constant pressure. Which curve represents the heating at constant volume?

::grafico{curve="t,300*exp((t-1)/3),1,4.3;t,300*exp((t-1)/5),1,4.3" punti="1,300,pieno,A" etichette="2.4,640,a;3.6,420,b" legenda=0 xmin=-0.3 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=1000 nomi="S,T" titolo="Two reversible heatings of an ideal gas in the $(S, T)$ plane, with $T$ in kelvins and $S$ in arbitrary units"}

- Curve $a$, because for the same temperature increase the change in entropy is smaller
- Curve $b$, because for the same temperature increase the change in entropy is larger
- Curve $a$, because at constant volume the entropy of the gas does not change
- Curve $b$, because at constant volume the gas absorbs more heat

## Explanation

At constant volume $\Delta S = n c_V \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A}$, at constant pressure $\Delta S = n c_p \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A}$. Since $c_p > c_V$, for the same final temperature the heating at constant pressure has the larger change in entropy. Therefore, for the same height reached, the curve of the isochoric heating moves less to the right and is steeper. Hence the heating at constant volume is represented by curve $a$. At constant pressure the gas absorbs more heat, because it gives part of it back as work of expansion.
