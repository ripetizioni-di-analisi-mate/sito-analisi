One mole of ideal gas, in contact with a reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$, is compressed from $20\,\mathrm{L}$ to $10\,\mathrm{L}$ by a constant external pressure equal to the final pressure of the gas; the initial and final temperatures are $300\,\mathrm{K}$. What is the change in entropy of the universe?

- $-5.76\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $8.31\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $2.55\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The external pressure is $p = \dfrac{RT}{V_B}$, therefore the work received by the gas is $p\,(V_A - V_B) = RT\,\dfrac{V_A - V_B}{V_B} = RT$. The temperature does not change, hence the gas releases the heat $RT$ to the reservoir and the reservoir has $\Delta S_{\text{ser}} = R \approx 8.31\,\mathrm{J/K}$. For the gas $\Delta S_{\text{gas}} = R \ln\dfrac{10}{20} \approx -5.76\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Therefore
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = R\,(1 - \ln 2) \approx 2.55\,\mathrm{J/K} > 0 ,
$$
as required by an irreversible compression. The value zero holds for the reversible isothermal compression.
