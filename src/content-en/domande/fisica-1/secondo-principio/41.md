A copper block of mass $2{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$, with specific heat $c = 385\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, at the temperature of $400\,\mathrm{K}$ is immersed in a lake at $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and reaches equilibrium. What is the change in entropy of the universe?

- $64\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $-35\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $4{,}8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $35\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The block cools down to $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and releases to the lake the heat $mc \cdot 100\,\mathrm{K} = 7{,}7 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. The lake is a reservoir, therefore its change in entropy is $\dfrac{7{,}7 \cdot 10^4}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 257\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The change in entropy of the block is $mc\ln\dfrac{300}{400} \approx -222\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Hence $\Delta S_{\text{u}} \approx (257 - 222)\,\mathrm{J/K} = 35\,\mathrm{J/K}$, positive as required by the **principle of entropy increase**. The value $64\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is obtained by treating the block as a reservoir at $400\,\mathrm{K}$ as well.
