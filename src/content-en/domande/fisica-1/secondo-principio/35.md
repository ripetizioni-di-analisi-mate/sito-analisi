One mole of monatomic ideal gas, with $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$, is heated at constant volume from $300\,\mathrm{K}$ to $600\,\mathrm{K}$. What is its change in entropy?

- $14.4\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $12.5\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $8.64\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $3.75\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

At constant volume the term $nR\ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$ in the formula for the entropy of the ideal gas is zero, therefore $\Delta S = n c_V \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A}$. Hence
$$
\Delta S = 1 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln 2\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 8.64\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The value $14.4\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is obtained by using $c_p = \dfrac{5}{2}R$, which applies to heating at constant pressure.
