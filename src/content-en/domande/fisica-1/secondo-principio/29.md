A real engine operates between $450\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$, has an efficiency equal to $0.60$ times the efficiency of a Carnot engine between the same reservoirs and absorbs $3000\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle. How much work does it produce in one cycle?

- $600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1800\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1200\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The Carnot efficiency between the two reservoirs is $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{300}{450} = \dfrac{1}{3}$. The efficiency of the engine is therefore $\eta = 0.60 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = 0.20$. Hence the work per cycle is $L = 0.20 \cdot 3000\,\mathrm{J} = 600\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $1000\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work of the Carnot engine, while $1800\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using $0.60$ as the efficiency.
