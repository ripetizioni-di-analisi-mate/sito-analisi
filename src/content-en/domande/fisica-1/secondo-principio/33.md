An ideal gas goes from state $A$ to state $B$ through a reversible process, and its entropy increases by $12\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Afterwards the same gas goes from the same state $A$ to the same state $B$ through an irreversible process. What is the change in entropy of the gas in this second case?

- More than $12\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- Less than $12\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $12\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

Entropy is a state function, therefore its change depends only on the initial state and on the final state. The two processes connect the same states $A$ and $B$, hence the change in entropy of the gas is $12\,\mathrm{J/K}$ in both cases. In the irreversible process the Clausius integral changes $\displaystyle\int_A^B \frac{\delta Q}{T}$, which turns out to be less than $12\,\mathrm{J/K}$.
