An ideal gas goes reversibly through a cycle made of an isobaric, an isochoric and an isothermal process, and in each cycle it produces $300\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. Denoting by $\delta Q$ the infinitesimal heat absorbed by the gas at temperature $T$, which pair of values is correct?

- $\displaystyle\oint \delta Q = 300\,\mathrm{J}$ and $\displaystyle\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} = 0$
- $\displaystyle\oint \delta Q = 0$ and $\displaystyle\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} = 0$
- $\displaystyle\oint \delta Q = 300\,\mathrm{J}$ and $\displaystyle\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} < 0$
- $\displaystyle\oint \delta Q = 0$ and $\displaystyle\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} < 0$

## Explanation

In a cycle $\Delta U = 0$, therefore by the first law the net heat absorbed equals the work, $\displaystyle\oint \delta Q = 300\,\mathrm{J}$. Heat depends on the process, hence its integral over a cycle can be nonzero. The cycle is reversible, so the **Clausius inequality** holds with the equality sign and $\displaystyle\oint \dfrac{\delta Q}{T} = 0$. This result expresses the fact that, on reversible processes, $\dfrac{\delta Q}{T}$ is the differential of the entropy, which is a state function.
