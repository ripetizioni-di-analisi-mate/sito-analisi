In an insulated container of negligible heat capacity $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $90\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ are mixed ($c = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$). What is the change in entropy of the universe?

- $-65\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $65\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $4.3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The two masses are equal, therefore the equilibrium temperature is the average, $T_e = 50\,^\circ\mathrm{C} = 323\,\mathrm{K}$. The container is insulated, hence $\Delta S_{\text{u}}$ is the sum of the changes in entropy of the two masses of water, each equal to $m c \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A}$, therefore
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = 4186\left(\ln\frac{323}{363} + \ln\frac{323}{283}\right)\mathrm{J/K} \approx (-489 + 553)\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 65\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The heats exchanged are equal and opposite, but the cold water absorbs heat at lower temperatures, therefore its entropy increases more than that of the hot water decreases. The positive result is in agreement with the **principle of entropy increase**.
