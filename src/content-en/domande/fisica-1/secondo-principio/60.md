A Carnot engine works between reservoirs at $T_1$ and $T_2 < T_1$. What does its cycle look like in the $(S, T)$ plane, with the entropy $S$ on the horizontal axis and the temperature $T$ on the vertical axis?

- A rectangle traversed clockwise
- A rectangle traversed counterclockwise
- A vertical segment, because the cycle is reversible and therefore $\Delta S = 0$
- A closed curve made of two hyperbolas and two vertical segments

## Explanation

The isotherms are horizontal segments, at the heights $T_1$ and $T_2$, and the reversible adiabats are vertical segments, because the entropy is constant along them; therefore the cycle is a rectangle. On the isotherm at $T_1$ the gas absorbs heat, hence its entropy increases and the point moves to the right along the upper side. On the isotherm at $T_2$ the gas releases heat and the point moves back to the left along the lower side. The cycle is therefore traversed **clockwise**, and the enclosed area is the work produced. Over a whole cycle $\Delta S = 0$ because the gas returns to its initial state, but the entropy changes along the isotherms.
