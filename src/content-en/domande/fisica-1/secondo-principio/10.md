Two moles of ideal gas expand isothermally and reversibly at $350\,\mathrm{K}$ from $10\,\mathrm{L}$ to $25\,\mathrm{L}$. How much heat does the gas absorb?

- $-5{,}33\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $5{,}33\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $2{,}32\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $14{,}5\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

In an isothermal process the internal energy of the ideal gas does not change, therefore by the first law $Q = L$. The work of the reversible isothermal process is $nRT \ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$, hence
$$
Q = 2 \cdot 8{,}314 \cdot 350 \cdot \ln 2{,}5\,\mathrm{J} \approx 5{,}33 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} = 5{,}33\,\mathrm{kJ} .
$$
The heat is positive because the gas expands. The same value is obtained from $Q = T\,\Delta S$, with $\Delta S = nR\ln 2{,}5 \approx 15{,}2\,\mathrm{J/K}$; with the base-$10$ logarithm one would instead obtain $2{,}32\,\mathrm{kJ}$.
