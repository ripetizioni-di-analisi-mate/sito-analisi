A thermal power plant produces an electrical power of $600\,\mathrm{MW}$ with efficiency $0.40$ and releases all its waste heat into a river with flow rate $150\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Water has density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and specific heat $4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. By how much does the temperature of the river rise?

- $1.4\,\mathrm{K}$
- $0.96\,\mathrm{K}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{K}$
- $0.57\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

The thermal power absorbed by the plant is $\dfrac{600\,\mathrm{MW}}{0.40} = 1500\,\mathrm{MW}$, therefore the power released into the river is $1500\,\mathrm{MW} - 600\,\mathrm{MW} = 900\,\mathrm{MW}$. Every second $1.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water flow by, hence
$$
\Delta T = \frac{9.0 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{W}}{1.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{kg/s} \cdot 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}} \approx 1.4\,\mathrm{K} .
$$
The value $0.96\,\mathrm{K}$ is obtained by using the electrical power instead of the released power, and $0.57\,\mathrm{K}$ by computing the released power as $600\,\mathrm{MW} \cdot (1 - 0.40)$.
