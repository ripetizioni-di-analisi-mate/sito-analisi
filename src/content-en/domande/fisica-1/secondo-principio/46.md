A gas goes quasi-statically through the cycle $A \to B \to C \to A$ shown in the $(V, p)$ plane, with $A = (1.0\,\mathrm{L};\ 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa})$, $B = (1.0\,\mathrm{L};\ 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa})$ and $C = (4.0\,\mathrm{L};\ 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa})$. How much work does the gas do in one cycle?

::grafico{poligoni="1,1 1,3 4,1" vettori="1,1,1,3;1,3,4,1;4,1,1,1" punti="1,1,pieno,A;1,3,pieno,B;4,1,pieno,C" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5 ymin=-0.5 ymax=4 nomi="V,p" titolo="Cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$"}

- $600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $300\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-300\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work done by the gas in a quasi-static cycle is the area enclosed by the cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane, positive if the cycle is traversed clockwise. The cycle $A \to B \to C \to A$ goes up along the isochore, down along the diagonal and back along the isobar, therefore it is traversed clockwise. The area is that of a right triangle with legs $\Delta V = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and $\Delta p = 2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, hence
$$
L = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} = 300\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $600\,\mathrm{J}$ is the area of the rectangle with the same sides, and $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by leaving the volume in liters.
