A car engine delivers a mechanical power of $25\,\mathrm{kW}$ with efficiency $0.25$. The fuel releases $44\,\mathrm{MJ}$ of heat for each kilogram burned. How many kilograms of fuel does the engine use in one hour?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6.1\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.51\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $8.2\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

In one hour the engine produces the work $L = 25 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 9.0 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J}$. The efficiency is the ratio of the work to the heat absorbed, therefore the heat supplied by the fuel is
$$
Q_{\text{ass}} = \frac{L}{\eta} = \frac{9.0 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J}}{0.25} = 3.6 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Hence the mass burned is $\dfrac{3.6 \cdot 10^8\,\mathrm{J}}{44 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}} \approx 8.2\,\mathrm{kg}$. The value $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is obtained by neglecting the efficiency, and $6.1\,\mathrm{kg}$ corresponds to the heat released to the environment only.
