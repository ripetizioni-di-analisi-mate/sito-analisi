A fluid goes through a quasi-static cycle that in the $(V, p)$ plane is traversed counterclockwise and encloses an area corresponding to $250\,\mathrm{J}$. Which statement is true?

- In each cycle the fluid does $250\,\mathrm{J}$ of work
- The work in one cycle is zero, because the final state coincides with the initial one
- In each cycle the fluid receives $250\,\mathrm{J}$ of work from the outside
- In each cycle the fluid absorbs a net heat of $250\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work done by the fluid in a quasi-static cycle is the area enclosed by the cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane, taken with the positive sign if the cycle is traversed clockwise. The cycle is traversed counterclockwise, therefore $L = -250\,\mathrm{J}$ and the fluid **receives work**. In a cycle $\Delta U = 0$, hence the net heat is also $Q = L = -250\,\mathrm{J}$, and the fluid releases heat. The work depends on the process, therefore in a cycle it can be nonzero even though the final state coincides with the initial one.
