A crate is dragged at constant speed on a floor with friction, in a room at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. All the work of the pulling force becomes heat released to the air of the room, while the crate and the floor return to their initial temperature. Which statement is true?

- The process is ruled out by the Kelvin-Planck statement, because it exchanges heat with a single reservoir
- The process is ruled out by the Clausius statement, because the air warms up
- The process is ruled out by the first law, because work cannot become heat
- The process is possible, because the second law rules out only the opposite process

## Explanation

The Kelvin-Planck statement rules out that the only result be the complete conversion into work of heat taken from a single reservoir. Here the opposite happens, namely the complete conversion of work into heat released to the air, therefore the statement does not rule it out. The first law is satisfied, because the energy released to the air equals the work done. The process is hence **possible**, and it is irreversible, since the air receives the heat $Q$ at temperature $T$ and its entropy increases by $\dfrac{Q}{T} > 0$.
