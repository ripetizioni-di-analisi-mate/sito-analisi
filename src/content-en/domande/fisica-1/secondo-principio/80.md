A rigid, insulated container is divided by a partition into two equal halves: one contains one mole of helium, the other one mole of argon, at the same temperature and the same pressure. The partition is removed and the two gases, treated as ideal gases, mix. What is the change in entropy of the system?

- $0\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $11.5\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $5.76\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $23.1\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The two gases are ideal and have the same temperature, therefore in the mixing they exchange no heat and the temperature stays constant. Each gas goes from volume $V$ to volume $2V$, as in a free expansion, and its entropy increases by $R \ln 2$. Hence
$$
\Delta S = 2 R \ln 2 = 2 \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln 2\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 11.5\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The value $5.76\,\mathrm{J/K}$ accounts for the expansion of one gas only.
