A reversible refrigerator cools $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water from $293\,\mathrm{K}$ to $273\,\mathrm{K}$ ($c = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$) and releases heat to a room that stays at $293\,\mathrm{K}$. How much work does the refrigerator receive in total?

- $6.1\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $84\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{MJ}$

## Explanation

The refrigerator is reversible and in each cycle its entropy does not change, therefore the entropy of the universe stays constant. Hence the increase in entropy of the room, $\dfrac{|Q_1|}{293\,\mathrm{K}}$, equals the decrease in entropy of the water, $m c \ln\dfrac{293}{273}$. Therefore the room receives $|Q_1| = 293 \cdot 4186 \cdot \ln\dfrac{293}{273}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 86.7\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The heat extracted from the water is $Q_2 = m c \cdot 20\,\mathrm{K} \approx 83.7\,\mathrm{kJ}$, and the work is the difference
$$
|L| = |Q_1| - Q_2 \approx 86.7\,\mathrm{kJ} - 83.7\,\mathrm{kJ} \approx 3.0\,\mathrm{kJ} .
$$
The value $6.1\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is obtained by using for the whole cooling the Carnot coefficient of performance between $273\,\mathrm{K}$ and $293\,\mathrm{K}$, which holds only at the end.
