In a closed kitchen, thermally insulated from the outside, a refrigerator is left running with its door open. What happens to the temperature of the kitchen?

- It decreases, because the refrigerator extracts heat from the air
- It stays constant, because the heat extracted from the air returns to it from the rear coils
- It increases, because the kitchen also receives the energy of the work spent by the motor
- It decreases down to the temperature of the compartment and then stays constant

## Explanation

With the door open the refrigerator extracts the heat $Q_2$ from the kitchen air and releases to the same air, from the rear coils, the heat $|Q_1| = Q_2 + |L|$. In each cycle the air therefore receives in total $|Q_1| - Q_2 = |L| > 0$. The kitchen is insulated, hence this energy stays in the air and the temperature of the kitchen **increases**. All the electrical energy drawn by the refrigerator ends up as internal energy of the air.
