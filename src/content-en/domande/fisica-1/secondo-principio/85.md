A $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ sandbag falls from a height of $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and stops on the floor. In the end all the mechanical energy lost is released as heat to the air of the room, at $21\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and the bag and the floor return to their initial temperature. What is the change in entropy of the universe?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $-2.0\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $28\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The mechanical energy lost is $m g h = 20 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{J} \approx 589\,\mathrm{J}$, and the air absorbs it as heat at the temperature of $294\,\mathrm{K}$. The bag and the floor return to their initial thermal state, therefore their entropy does not change. The air behaves as a reservoir, hence
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = \frac{589\,\mathrm{J}}{294\,\mathrm{K}} \approx 2.0\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The value $28\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is obtained by dividing by the temperature in degrees Celsius.
