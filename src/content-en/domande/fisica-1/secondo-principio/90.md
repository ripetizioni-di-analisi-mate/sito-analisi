At low temperature the heat capacity of a solid sample is $C(T) = b\,T^3$, with $b = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J/K^4}$. What is the change in entropy of the sample when it is heated from $10\,\mathrm{K}$ to $20\,\mathrm{K}$?

- $21\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $16.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $7.0\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $5.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

In a reversible heating $\delta Q = C(T)\, dT$, therefore
$$
\Delta S = \int_{10\,\mathrm{K}}^{20\,\mathrm{K}} \frac{b\,T^3}{T}\, dT = \frac{b}{3} \left(20^3 - 10^3\right)\mathrm{J/K} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 7000\,\mathrm{J/K} = 7.0\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The formula $m c \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A}$ holds only for constant heat capacity; using it with the heat capacity at $20\,\mathrm{K}$ would give $16.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The value $21\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the antiderivative of $T^2$.
