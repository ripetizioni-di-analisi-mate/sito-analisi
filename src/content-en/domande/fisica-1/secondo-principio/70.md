Suppose there exists a device $D$ that in each cycle transfers $600\,\mathrm{J}$ from a cold reservoir to a hot reservoir with no other effect. Between the same reservoirs an engine works that in each cycle absorbs $1000\,\mathrm{J}$ from the hot reservoir, produces $400\,\mathrm{J}$ of work and releases $600\,\mathrm{J}$ to the cold reservoir. What is the result of one cycle of the system formed by the engine and $D$?

- The transfer of $600\,\mathrm{J}$ from the cold reservoir to the hot reservoir with no other effect, ruled out by the Clausius statement
- The conversion into work of $400\,\mathrm{J}$ taken from the cold reservoir alone, ruled out by the Kelvin-Planck statement
- The production of $400\,\mathrm{J}$ of work with $600\,\mathrm{J}$ released to the cold reservoir, as in an ordinary engine
- The conversion into work of $400\,\mathrm{J}$ taken from the hot reservoir alone, ruled out by the Kelvin-Planck statement

## Explanation

In one cycle the engine takes $1000\,\mathrm{J}$ from the hot reservoir and $D$ returns $600\,\mathrm{J}$ to it, therefore the hot reservoir gives up $400\,\mathrm{J}$ in total. The cold reservoir receives $600\,\mathrm{J}$ from the engine and gives $600\,\mathrm{J}$ to $D$, hence it undergoes no change. The system produces the work of $400\,\mathrm{J}$, equal to the heat taken from the hot reservoir, and has no other effect. Therefore the only result of the cycle is the complete conversion into work of heat taken from a single reservoir, which is ruled out by the **Kelvin-Planck statement**. A device violating the Clausius statement would thus allow a violation of the Kelvin-Planck statement as well.
