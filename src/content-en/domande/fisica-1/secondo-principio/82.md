One mole of monatomic ideal gas, with $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$, at $400\,\mathrm{K}$ expands isothermally to eight times its initial volume and is then cooled at constant volume. At what final temperature is the overall change in entropy of the gas zero?

- $50\,\mathrm{K}$
- $174\,\mathrm{K}$
- $17.7\,\mathrm{K}$
- $100\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

On the isotherm the entropy increases by $R \ln 8$, in the isochoric cooling it changes by $\dfrac{3}{2} R \ln\dfrac{T_f}{400\,\mathrm{K}}$. The sum is zero if
$$
\ln\frac{T_f}{400\,\mathrm{K}} = -\frac{2}{3} \ln 8 = -2\ln 2 ,
$$
that is $\dfrac{T_f}{400\,\mathrm{K}} = \dfrac{1}{4}$ and $T_f = 100\,\mathrm{K}$. The final state therefore has the same entropy as the initial state, and indeed it satisfies $T V^{\frac{2}{3}} = \text{constant}$, the relation of the reversible adiabat. The value $174\,\mathrm{K}$ is obtained by using $c_p$ instead of $c_V$.
