A refrigerator with coefficient of performance $\varepsilon = 5.0$ extracts $2.5 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$ from its interior. How much heat does it release to the surroundings?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The coefficient of performance is $\varepsilon = \dfrac{Q_2}{|L|}$, therefore the work spent is $|L| = \dfrac{2.5 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}}{5.0} = 5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$. The surroundings receive both the heat extracted from the interior and the equivalent of the work spent, that is, $|Q_1| = Q_2 + |L|$. Hence the heat released to the surroundings is $|Q_1| = 2.5 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} + 5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} = 3.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$.
