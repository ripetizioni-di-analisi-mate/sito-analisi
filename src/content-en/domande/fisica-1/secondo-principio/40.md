A mass of $0{,}50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water vapor condenses at the constant temperature of $373\,\mathrm{K}$, with latent heat of vaporization $\lambda_v = 2{,}26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. What is the change in entropy of the water?

- $3{,}03 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $-6{,}06 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $-3{,}03 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $-1{,}13 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

During condensation the temperature remains constant and the vapor releases the heat $m\lambda_v = 0{,}50 \cdot 2{,}26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J} = 1{,}13 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$. The heat absorbed by the water is therefore $Q = -1{,}13 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$, and
$$
\Delta S = \frac{Q}{T} = -\frac{1{,}13 \cdot 10^6}{373}\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx -3{,}03 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The sign is negative because in condensation the water releases heat. The value $-6{,}06 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is obtained by forgetting the mass, and $-1{,}13 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J/K}$ by using the temperature in degrees Celsius.
