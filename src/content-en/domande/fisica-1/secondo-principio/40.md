A lead bullet of mass $10\,\mathrm{g}$, with specific heat $c = 128\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, travels at $200\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the temperature of $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and stops in a target. Assume that all its kinetic energy becomes internal energy of the bullet and that no heat is exchanged during the impact. What is the change in entropy of the bullet?

- $0.667\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0.537\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0.438\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.010 \cdot 200^2\,\mathrm{J} = 200\,\mathrm{J}$. This energy raises the temperature of the bullet by $\Delta T = \dfrac{200}{0.010 \cdot 128}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 156\,\mathrm{K}$, therefore the final temperature is about $456\,\mathrm{K}$. Entropy is a state function, hence its change is computed along a reversible heating between the same temperatures:
$$
\Delta S = m c \ln\frac{T_B}{T_A} = 1.28 \cdot \ln\frac{456}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 0.537\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The value zero is obtained from the absence of heat exchange, which implies $\Delta S = 0$ only for a reversible process; $0.667\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and $0.438\,\mathrm{J/K}$ are obtained by dividing $200\,\mathrm{J}$ by the initial temperature only or by the final temperature only.
