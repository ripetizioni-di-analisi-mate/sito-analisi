An inventor describes a device that goes through one cycle after another and in each cycle produces $50\,\mathrm{J}$ of work without absorbing heat from any body. Which statement is true?

- The device is ruled out only by the Kelvin-Planck statement
- The device is ruled out by the first law
- The device is possible, if the cycle is reversible
- The device is ruled out only by the Clausius statement

## Explanation

In a cycle the final state coincides with the initial one, therefore $\Delta U = 0$ and the first law gives $L = Q$. The device absorbs no heat, hence $Q = 0$ and the work produced should be zero. A work of $50\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle is therefore ruled out by the **first law**, whatever the way the cycle is traversed. The statements of the second law concern instead machines that satisfy the energy balance.
