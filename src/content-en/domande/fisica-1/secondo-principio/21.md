An inventor proposes a cyclic device that, without receiving work, transfers $500\,\mathrm{J}$ in each cycle from a reservoir at $280\,\mathrm{K}$ to a reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. By how much would the entropy of the universe change in one cycle?

- $-0{,}119\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0{,}119\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $3{,}45\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $-3{,}45\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

In a cycle the entropy of the device does not change, therefore only the changes in entropy of the reservoirs count, each equal to $\dfrac{Q}{T}$. The cold reservoir absorbs $-500\,\mathrm{J}$ and the hot one $+500\,\mathrm{J}$, hence
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = -\frac{500}{280}\,\mathrm{J/K} + \frac{500}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K} = -\frac{500 \cdot 20}{84000}\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx -0{,}119\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The entropy of the universe would decrease, therefore by the **principle of entropy increase** the device cannot exist. In this way the Clausius statement is recovered.
