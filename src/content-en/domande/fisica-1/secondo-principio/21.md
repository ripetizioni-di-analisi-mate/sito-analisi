A ship is proposed whose engine, going through one cycle after another, takes heat only from the ocean water, at constant temperature, and turns all of it into the work that drives the propeller. Which statement is true?

- The engine is possible, because energy is conserved
- The engine is ruled out by the Kelvin-Planck statement
- The engine is ruled out by the first law
- The engine is possible, because the ocean has a practically infinite heat capacity

## Explanation

In each cycle the engine absorbs a heat $Q$ and produces the work $L = Q$, therefore the first law is satisfied. However, the only result of the cycle is the complete conversion into work of heat taken from a single reservoir. Hence the engine is ruled out by the **Kelvin-Planck statement**. The fact that the ocean behaves as a reservoir, with constant temperature, does not change the conclusion, because the statement concerns exactly a single reservoir.
