In a Carnot cycle of an ideal gas, during the isothermal expansion $A \to B$ the volume goes from $V_A$ to $V_B = 3.0\,V_A$. In the following adiabatic expansion $B \to C$ and in the isothermal compression $C \to D$ the gas reaches the volumes $V_C$ and $V_D$. What is the ratio $\dfrac{V_C}{V_D}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $9.0$
- $3^{\gamma}$, with $\gamma = \dfrac{c_p}{c_V}$
- $3.0$

## Explanation

Along the reversible adiabats $B \to C$ and $D \to A$ the relation $T V^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}$ holds, therefore
$$
T_1 V_B^{\gamma - 1} = T_2 V_C^{\gamma - 1} , \qquad T_1 V_A^{\gamma - 1} = T_2 V_D^{\gamma - 1} .
$$
Dividing the first equality by the second, side by side, gives $\left(\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}\right)^{\gamma - 1} = \left(\dfrac{V_C}{V_D}\right)^{\gamma - 1}$. Hence $\dfrac{V_C}{V_D} = \dfrac{V_B}{V_A} = 3.0$, whatever the gas and whatever the temperatures of the reservoirs.
