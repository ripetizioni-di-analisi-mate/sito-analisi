A Carnot heat pump heats a house at $22\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ taking heat from the outside at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and it delivers a thermal power of $5.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ to the house. What is the minimum mechanical power required?

- $4.6 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{W}$
- $3.7 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{W}$
- $6.7 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The temperatures in kelvins are $T_1 = 295\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_2 = 273\,\mathrm{K}$. The Carnot heat pump has the maximum coefficient of performance between the two reservoirs,
$$
\varepsilon_{\text{hp},C} = \frac{T_1}{T_1 - T_2} = \frac{295}{22} \approx 13.4 .
$$
Since the coefficient of performance is the ratio of the heat delivered to the house to the work spent, the minimum mechanical power is $P = \dfrac{5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{W}}{13.4} \approx 3.7 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$. With the temperatures in degrees Celsius the formula would give $\dfrac{22}{22 - 0} = 1$, and therefore a power of $5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{W}$.
