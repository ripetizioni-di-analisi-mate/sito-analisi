An ideal gas goes through a cycle that includes a free expansion into vacuum and other processes with heat exchanges with the surroundings, and at the end it returns to its initial state. Which statement about the changes in entropy over one cycle is true?

- The entropy of the gas increases and that of the surroundings stays constant
- The entropy of the gas stays constant and that of the surroundings increases
- The entropy of the gas and that of the surroundings both stay constant
- The entropy of the gas increases and that of the surroundings decreases by the same amount

## Explanation

Entropy is a state function and the gas returns to its initial state, therefore over one cycle $\Delta S_{\text{gas}} = 0$. The cycle contains a free expansion, hence it is irreversible, and by the **principle of entropy increase** the universe has $\Delta S_{\text{u}} > 0$. Since $\Delta S_{\text{u}} = \Delta S_{\text{gas}} + \Delta S_{\text{amb}}$, the entropy of the surroundings **increases**.
