An ideal gas doubles its volume in two different ways: with a reversible adiabatic expansion, or with a free expansion into vacuum inside a rigid, insulated container. Which statement about the change in entropy of the gas is true?

- It is zero in the reversible adiabatic expansion and positive in the free expansion
- It is zero in both cases, because in both the gas exchanges no heat
- It is positive in both cases, because in both the volume increases
- It is positive in the reversible adiabatic expansion and zero in the free expansion

## Explanation

In the reversible adiabatic process $\delta Q = 0$ in every step and the process is reversible, therefore $\Delta S = 0$. In this case the temperature of the gas decreases, and the decrease in entropy due to the cooling offsets the increase due to the volume. In the free expansion $Q = 0$ and $L = 0$, hence the final temperature equals the initial one. The change in entropy is then computed along a reversible isothermal process between the same states and equals $nR\ln 2 > 0$. A zero heat exchange implies $\Delta S = 0$ only for a reversible process.
