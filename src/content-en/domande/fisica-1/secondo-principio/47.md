An ideal gas goes through the reversible cycle in the figure: isobaric expansion $A \to B$, adiabatic expansion $B \to C$, isobaric compression $C \to D$ and adiabatic compression $D \to A$. In which parts does the gas absorb heat?

::grafico{curve="t,10.556/t^1.4,2,5.384;t,4/t^1.4,1,2.692" vettori="1,4,2,4;5.384,1,2.692,1;3.4,1.903,3.8,1.629;1.9,1.629,1.6,2.072" punti="1,4,pieno,A;2,4,pieno,B;5.384,1,pieno,C;2.692,1,pieno,D" legenda=0 xmin=-0.3 xmax=6.2 ymin=-0.4 ymax=5 nomi="V,p" titolo="Cycle of an ideal gas in the $(V, p)$ plane, in arbitrary units: isobars $AB$ and $CD$, adiabats $BC$ and $DA$"}

- Only in $A \to B$
- In $A \to B$ and in $B \to C$
- Only in $C \to D$
- In $A \to B$ and in $D \to A$

## Explanation

Along the adiabats $B \to C$ and $D \to A$ the gas exchanges no heat. In the isobaric expansion $A \to B$ the volume increases at constant pressure, therefore by the equation of state the temperature rises and the gas absorbs the heat $n c_p (T_B - T_A) > 0$. In the isobaric compression $C \to D$ the temperature decreases, hence the gas releases heat. Therefore the gas absorbs heat **only** in the part $A \to B$.
