Three engines operate between two reservoirs at $500\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$: $R$ is reversible and uses an ideal gas, $R'$ is reversible and uses water vapor, $M$ is irreversible because of friction. Which statement is true?

- $R$ has efficiency $0{,}40$, while the efficiency of $R'$ depends on the properties of the vapor
- All three engines have efficiency $0{,}40$
- $R$ and $R'$ have efficiency $0{,}40$, while $M$ has efficiency less than $0{,}40$
- $M$ exceeds the efficiency $0{,}40$ if it absorbs enough heat in each cycle

## Explanation

By **Carnot's theorem** all reversible engines between the same two reservoirs have the same efficiency, whatever the working fluid. Since the Carnot cycle of the ideal gas is reversible, this efficiency is $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{300}{500} = 0{,}40$, both for $R$ and for $R'$. The theorem also ensures that every other engine between the same reservoirs has efficiency less than or equal to $0{,}40$, with equality only for reversible engines. Therefore $M$, which is irreversible, has efficiency **less** than $0{,}40$, whatever the heat absorbed in one cycle.
