Two Carnot engines are connected in series: the first works between $900\,\mathrm{K}$ and an intermediate temperature $T$, the second absorbs all the heat released by the first at temperature $T$ and works between $T$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$. The two engines produce the same work in each cycle. What is $T$?

- $600\,\mathrm{K}$
- $520\,\mathrm{K}$
- $450\,\mathrm{K}$
- $750\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

Let $Q_1$ be the heat absorbed by the first engine. For reversible engines the heats are proportional to the temperatures, therefore the first releases $Q_1 \dfrac{T}{900}$ and the second releases $Q_1 \dfrac{T}{900} \cdot \dfrac{300}{T} = Q_1 \dfrac{300}{900}$. The two works are hence
$$
L' = Q_1\, \frac{900 - T}{900} , \qquad L'' = Q_1\, \frac{T - 300}{900} .
$$
Setting $L' = L''$ gives $900 - T = T - 300$, that is $T = 600\,\mathrm{K}$. The value $520\,\mathrm{K} \approx \sqrt{900 \cdot 300}\,\mathrm{K}$ is obtained by requiring that the two engines have the same efficiency.
