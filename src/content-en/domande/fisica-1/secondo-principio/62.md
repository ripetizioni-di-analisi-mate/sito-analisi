In a Carnot cycle a monatomic ideal gas, with $\gamma = \dfrac{5}{3}$, ends the isothermal expansion at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ with volume $V_B = 2.0\,\mathrm{L}$ and then expands adiabatically down to $300\,\mathrm{K}$. What is the volume $V_C$ at the end of the adiabatic expansion?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{L}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{L}$
- $5.7\,\mathrm{L}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

Along the reversible adiabat $T V^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}$ holds, with $\gamma - 1 = \dfrac{2}{3}$. Therefore $600 \cdot V_B^{\frac{2}{3}} = 300 \cdot V_C^{\frac{2}{3}}$, that is $\left(\dfrac{V_C}{V_B}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 2$. Hence
$$
V_C = V_B \cdot 2^{\frac{3}{2}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{L} \cdot 2.83 \approx 5.7\,\mathrm{L} .
$$
The value $4.0\,\mathrm{L}$ is obtained by forgetting the exponent, that is, by assuming the product $T V$ constant, and $3.0\,\mathrm{L}$ by using the exponent $\dfrac{1}{\gamma}$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{\gamma - 1}$.
