An inventor claims to have built an engine that operates between $500\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$, absorbs $2000\,\mathrm{J}$ and produces $900\,\mathrm{J}$ in each cycle. Which statement is true?

- The engine can exist, because the work produced is less than the heat absorbed
- The engine cannot exist, because between these reservoirs, absorbing $2000\,\mathrm{J}$, at most $800\,\mathrm{J}$ can be produced
- The engine can exist, because between these reservoirs, absorbing $2000\,\mathrm{J}$, up to $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ can be produced
- The engine can exist, because it releases heat to the cold reservoir and therefore respects the Kelvin-Planck statement

## Explanation

By **Carnot's theorem** the efficiency of an engine between $500\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$ does not exceed $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{300}{500} = 0{,}40$. Therefore, absorbing $2000\,\mathrm{J}$, the maximum work is $0{,}40 \cdot 2000\,\mathrm{J} = 800\,\mathrm{J}$. The claimed engine would have efficiency $\dfrac{900}{2000} = 0{,}45 > 0{,}40$, hence it **cannot exist**. This is confirmed by the balance $\dfrac{Q_1}{T_1} + \dfrac{Q_2}{T_2} = \dfrac{2000}{500}\,\mathrm{J/K} - \dfrac{1100}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 0{,}33\,\mathrm{J/K}$, which is positive and therefore violates the Clausius inequality.
