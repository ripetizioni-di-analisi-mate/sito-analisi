For $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water four processes are considered: melting of ice at $273\,\mathrm{K}$ ($\lambda_f = 3.33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$), heating of the liquid from $273\,\mathrm{K}$ to $373\,\mathrm{K}$ ($c = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$), vaporization at $373\,\mathrm{K}$ ($\lambda_v = 2.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}$), cooling of the liquid from $373\,\mathrm{K}$ to $273\,\mathrm{K}$. In which process does the entropy of the water increase the most?

- In the heating of the liquid
- In the vaporization
- In the melting
- In the cooling of the liquid

## Explanation

In phase changes the temperature is constant, therefore $\Delta S = \dfrac{m\lambda}{T}$. For melting one gets $\dfrac{3.33 \cdot 10^5}{273}\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 1.22 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$, for vaporization $\dfrac{2.26 \cdot 10^6}{373}\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 6.06 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The heating of the liquid gives $m c \ln\dfrac{373}{273} \approx 1.31 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The cooling gives the same value with opposite sign, hence the entropy decreases. Therefore the largest increase occurs in the **vaporization**.
