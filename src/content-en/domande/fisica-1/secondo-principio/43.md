An engine operates between $500\,\mathrm{K}$ and $250\,\mathrm{K}$, absorbs $2000\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle and has efficiency $0{,}30$. How much work does it lose in each cycle compared with a reversible engine that absorbs the same heat between the same reservoirs?

- $600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{J}$
- $800\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The reversible engine has efficiency $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{250}{500} = 0{,}50$, therefore it produces $L_{\text{rev}} = 0{,}50 \cdot 2000\,\mathrm{J} = 1000\,\mathrm{J}$. The engine produces $L = 0{,}30 \cdot 2000\,\mathrm{J} = 600\,\mathrm{J}$, hence the lost work is $1000\,\mathrm{J} - 600\,\mathrm{J} = 400\,\mathrm{J}$. The same value is obtained from $T_2\,\Delta S_{\text{u}}$. The engine releases $1400\,\mathrm{J}$ to the cold reservoir, therefore $\Delta S_{\text{u}} = \left(-\dfrac{2000}{500} + \dfrac{1400}{250}\right)\mathrm{J/K} = 1{,}6\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and $250\,\mathrm{K} \cdot 1{,}6\,\mathrm{J/K} = 400\,\mathrm{J}$.
