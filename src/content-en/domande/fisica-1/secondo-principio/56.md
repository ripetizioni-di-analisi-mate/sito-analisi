A thermal power plant with efficiency $0.40$ produces the electrical energy that runs a heat pump with coefficient of performance $\varepsilon_{\text{pc}} = 3.5$, used to heat a house. For every joule of heat that the power plant absorbs by burning fuel, how many joules of heat does the house receive?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.1\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The efficiency of the power plant is the ratio of the work produced to the heat absorbed, therefore from $1\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat the plant obtains $0.40\,\mathrm{J}$ of electrical energy. The coefficient of performance of the heat pump is the ratio of the heat delivered to the house to the work spent, hence the house receives $3.5 \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{J} = 1.4\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $1.0\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using the coefficient of performance as a refrigerator, $\varepsilon_{\text{pc}} - 1 = 2.5$, and $2.1\,\mathrm{J}$ by multiplying the coefficient of performance by the fraction of heat released by the plant, $0.60$.
