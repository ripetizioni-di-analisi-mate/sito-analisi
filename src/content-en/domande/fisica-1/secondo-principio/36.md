Two moles of ideal gas go from state $A$ to state $B$, which has the same temperature as $A$ and a pressure equal to one third of that of $A$. What is the change in entropy of the gas?

- $-18.3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $18.3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $9.13\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

The change in entropy of the ideal gas can be written $\Delta S = n c_p \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A} - nR \ln\dfrac{p_B}{p_A}$, whatever the process. Since $T_B = T_A$, the first term is zero. Therefore
$$
\Delta S = -2 \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln\frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{J/K} = 2 \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln 3\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 18.3\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The entropy increases because at constant temperature the decrease in pressure corresponds to an increase in volume. The value zero is obtained by considering only the temperature, which does not change.
