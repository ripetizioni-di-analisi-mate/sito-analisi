Two bodies with the same heat capacity $C = 100\,\mathrm{J/K}$, at the temperatures of $400\,\mathrm{K}$ and $200\,\mathrm{K}$, are put in contact in an insulated container until equilibrium. Which statement is true?

- The final temperature is $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and the entropy of the system does not change, because the heat released by the hot body equals the heat absorbed by the cold body
- The final temperature is $\sqrt{400 \cdot 200}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 283\,\mathrm{K}$ and the entropy of the system does not change
- The final temperature is $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and the entropy of the system increases
- The final temperature is $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and the entropy of the hot body increases

## Explanation

The system is isolated and the heat capacities are equal, therefore the heat released by the hot body equals the heat absorbed by the cold body and the final temperature is the average, $300\,\mathrm{K}$. The change in entropy of the system is
$$
\Delta S = C\ln\frac{300}{400} + C\ln\frac{300}{200} = C\ln\frac{9}{8} \approx 11.8\,\mathrm{J/K} > 0 .
$$
The hot body releases heat, hence its entropy decreases. The entropy of the cold body increases by more, because the cold body absorbs the same heat at lower temperatures; therefore the entropy of the system **increases**, as predicted by the principle of entropy increase for an irreversible process of an isolated system.
