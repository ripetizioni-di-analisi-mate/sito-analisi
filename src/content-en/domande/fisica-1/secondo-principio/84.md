One mole of monatomic ideal gas, with $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$, undergoes a reversible process in which the pressure is always proportional to the volume, and the volume doubles. What is the change in entropy of the gas?

- $5.76\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $23.1\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $17.3\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $34.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

Since $p = kV$, the equation of state gives $nRT = pV = kV^2$, therefore the temperature is proportional to the square of the volume. The volume doubles, hence the temperature becomes four times the initial one. The formula for the entropy of the ideal gas gives
$$
\Delta S = n c_V \ln 4 + nR \ln 2 = \left(\frac{3}{2} \cdot 2 + 1\right) R \ln 2 = 4 \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln 2\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 23.1\,\mathrm{J/K} .
$$
The value $5.76\,\mathrm{J/K}$ accounts only for the change in volume, $17.3\,\mathrm{J/K}$ only for the change in temperature, and $34.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is obtained from the pressure form of the formula by adding the term $nR \ln\dfrac{p_B}{p_A}$ instead of subtracting it.
