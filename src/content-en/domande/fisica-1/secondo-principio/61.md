In a Carnot cycle $2.0$ moles of monatomic ideal gas work between $500\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$. How much work does the gas do during the adiabatic expansion?

- $8.31\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $-4.99\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $4.99\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

In the adiabatic expansion the gas exchanges no heat, therefore by the first law $L = -\Delta U$. The internal energy of the monatomic ideal gas is $n c_V T$ with $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$, and the temperature drops from $500\,\mathrm{K}$ to $300\,\mathrm{K}$, hence
$$
L = n c_V (T_1 - T_2) = 2.0 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 200\,\mathrm{J} \approx 4.99\,\mathrm{kJ} .
$$
The work is positive because the gas expands. The value $8.31\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is obtained by using $c_p$ instead of $c_V$.
