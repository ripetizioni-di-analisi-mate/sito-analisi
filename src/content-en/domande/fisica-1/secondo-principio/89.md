A body with constant heat capacity is heated by a reservoir. Its initial temperature is $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Consider the entropy change of the body alone. Given the final body temperature $T = 360\,\mathrm{K}$ and the entropy change $S = - 400 \cdot \log{\left(5 \right)} + 400 \cdot \log{\left(6 \right)}\,\mathrm{J/K}$, what is the heat capacity of the body $C$?

- $- 2000 \cdot \log{\left(5 \right)} + 2000 \cdot \log{\left(6 \right)}\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $800\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $400\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $- 2400 \cdot \log{\left(5 \right)} + 2400 \cdot \log{\left(6 \right)}\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

Calculate the entropy change by integrating $C\,dT/T$ between the initial and final temperatures along an equivalent reversible heating path. In the stated units, the relation is $S = \log{\left(\left(\dfrac{T}{300}\right)^{C} \right)}$. Solving for the unknown gives $C = \dfrac{S}{\log{\left(\dfrac{T}{300} \right)}}$. Substituting the given values yields $400\,\mathrm{J/K}$.
