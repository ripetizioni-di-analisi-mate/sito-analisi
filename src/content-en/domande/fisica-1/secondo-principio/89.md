Two bodies with the same heat capacity $C = 100\,\mathrm{J/K}$ are at $400\,\mathrm{K}$ and $100\,\mathrm{K}$. A reversible engine works between the two bodies until their temperatures become equal. How much work does it produce in total?

- $10\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $15\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $20\,\mathrm{kJ}$

## Explanation

The engine is reversible, therefore the entropy of the universe does not change and the sum of the changes in entropy of the two bodies is zero, that is
$$
C \ln\frac{T_f}{400\,\mathrm{K}} + C \ln\frac{T_f}{100\,\mathrm{K}} = 0 .
$$
Hence $T_f^2 = 400 \cdot 100\,\mathrm{K^2}$ and $T_f = 200\,\mathrm{K}$. The hot body gives up $C \cdot 200\,\mathrm{K} = 20\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and the cold body absorbs $C \cdot 100\,\mathrm{K} = 10\,\mathrm{kJ}$, therefore the work produced is $L = 20\,\mathrm{kJ} - 10\,\mathrm{kJ} = 10\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The value $15\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is the heat the hot body would give up without the engine, down to the temperature of $250\,\mathrm{K}$. The zero value is obtained by taking the final temperature $250\,\mathrm{K}$ also with the engine, in which case the heat given up by the hot body equals the heat absorbed by the cold body.
