An ideal gas goes from state $A$ to state $B$ along two different reversible processes. Along the first it absorbs $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ and the Clausius integral $\displaystyle\int_A^B \frac{\delta Q}{T}$ equals $4.0\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Along the second it absorbs $2000\,\mathrm{J}$. What is the value of the Clausius integral along the second process?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $6.7\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{J/K}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J/K}$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the **Clausius integral over reversible processes**, the integral $\displaystyle\int_A^B \frac{\delta Q}{T}$ has the same value along every reversible process connecting the same two states. Both processes are reversible and go from $A$ to $B$, therefore along the second the integral still equals $4.0\,\mathrm{J/K}$, that is, the change in entropy $S(B) - S(A)$. The heat absorbed is instead different, because it depends on the process. The value $6.7\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is obtained by assuming the integral proportional to the heat absorbed.
