A monatomic ideal gas goes clockwise through a rectangular cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane formed by two isochoric processes at $3{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$ and $6{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$ and by two isobaric processes at $1{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $2{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. What is the efficiency of the cycle?

- $0{,}200$
- $0{,}222$
- $0{,}154$
- $0{,}750$

## Explanation

The work per cycle is the area of the rectangle, $L = (1{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}) \cdot (3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}) = 300\,\mathrm{J}$. The gas absorbs heat in the isochoric process at $3{,}0\,\mathrm{L}$, where the pressure rises, and in the isobaric process at $2{,}0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, where the volume increases. For the monatomic gas, using $pV = nRT$, the two amounts of heat are
$$
Q_{\text{isocora}} = \frac{3}{2}\, V\,\Delta p = 450\,\mathrm{J} , \qquad Q_{\text{isobara}} = \frac{5}{2}\, p\,\Delta V = 1500\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Therefore $Q_{\text{ass}} = 1950\,\mathrm{J}$ and $\eta = \dfrac{300}{1950} = \dfrac{2}{13} \approx 0{,}154$. Without the heat absorbed in the isochoric process one would obtain $0{,}200$.
