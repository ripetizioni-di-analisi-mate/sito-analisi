A system goes through a sequence of cycles exchanging heat with a single reservoir, at temperature $T$. What can be said about the work $L$ done by the system in one cycle?

- $L \leq 0$, with equality only if the cycle is reversible
- $L \geq 0$, with equality only if the cycle is reversible
- $L = 0$ in every case
- The sign of $L$ depends on the fluid going through the cycle

## Explanation

With a single reservoir the **Clausius inequality** reduces to $\dfrac{Q}{T} \leq 0$, where $Q$ is the heat absorbed in one cycle. Since $T > 0$, one has $Q \leq 0$. In a cycle $\Delta U = 0$, therefore the first law gives $L = Q \leq 0$. The system can receive work and release heat, but not produce work, as the Kelvin-Planck statement asserts; the equality sign holds for reversible cycles.
