Two equal point masses are fixed on a rod of negligible mass, on opposite sides of the axis of rotation, which is perpendicular to the rod, and at the same distance $d$ from it. The masses are moved to the distance $2d$ from the axis. How does the moment of inertia of the system about the axis change?

- It doubles
- It stays the same
- It becomes four times as large
- It halves

## Explanation

The moment of inertia of a system of particles is $I = \sum_i m_i R_i^2$, so each mass contributes in proportion to the square of its distance from the axis. Before the displacement $I = 2 m d^2$, and after it $I = 2 m (2d)^2 = 8 m d^2$. Therefore the moment of inertia **becomes four times as large**. Doubling would correspond to contributions proportional to the distance.
