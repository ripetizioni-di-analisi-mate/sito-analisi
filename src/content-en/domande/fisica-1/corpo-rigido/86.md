A load hangs from a rope wrapped around a drum with a fixed axis. The rope does not slip. The drum has radius $0{,}5\,\mathrm{m}$ and the load descends at constant speed. Given the speed of descent $v = 2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the power absorbed by the brake $P = 60\,\mathrm{W}$, what is the magnitude of the braking torque $\tau$?

- $60\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $15\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $30\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $\dfrac{15}{2}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$

## Explanation

Without slipping, $\omega=v/R=2v$. The power magnitude is braking torque times $\omega$. In the stated units, the relation is $P = 2 \cdot \tau \cdot v$. Solving for the unknown gives $\tau = \dfrac{P}{2 \cdot v}$. Substituting the given values yields $15\,\mathrm{N\,m}$.
