A star of radius $7.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{km}$ rotates about itself with a period of $25$ days. At the end of its life it collapses into a neutron star of radius $10\,\mathrm{km}$, without losing mass and without external torques. Treating the star as a uniform sphere before and after the collapse, what is the new rotation period?

- $31\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^{16}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4.4 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The external torque is zero, so the angular momentum is conserved: $I_1\omega_1 = I_2\omega_2$. For a uniform sphere $I = \dfrac{2}{5} M R^2$ and the mass does not change, therefore $R_1^2\,\omega_1 = R_2^2\,\omega_2$; since $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$, the period is proportional to $R^2$. With $T_1 = 25 \cdot 86\,400\,\mathrm{s} = 2.16 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{s}$
$$
T_2 = T_1 \left( \frac{R_2}{R_1} \right)^2 = 2.16 \cdot 10^{6} \cdot \left( \frac{10}{7.0 \cdot 10^{5}} \right)^2\,\mathrm{s} = 4.4 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $31\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by assuming the period proportional to the radius instead of its square.
