A uniform solid cylinder of mass $2{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $5{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a thin ring of mass $1{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ start from rest at the same height on the same inclined plane and roll without slipping. Which statement is true?

- The ring reaches the bottom before the cylinder
- The two bodies reach the bottom together
- Which body arrives first depends on the inclination of the plane
- The cylinder reaches the bottom before the ring

## Explanation

For a body with $I_C = k M R^2$ that rolls without slipping, the acceleration of the center is $a_C = \dfrac{g\sin\beta}{1 + k}$, which depends neither on the mass nor on the radius. The solid cylinder has $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the ring $k = 1$, so at any inclination the acceleration of the cylinder, $\dfrac{2}{3}\,g\sin\beta$, is greater than that of the ring, $\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\sin\beta$. The two bodies cover the same distance starting from rest, therefore **the cylinder reaches the bottom first**.
