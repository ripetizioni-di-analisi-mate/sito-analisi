A figure skater spins on the ice, with negligible friction. By pulling in her arms she halves her moment of inertia about the vertical axis of rotation. How does her kinetic energy change?

- It stays the same
- It quadruples
- It doubles
- It halves

## Explanation

The weight and the reaction of the ice are parallel to the axis, so $L_z = I\omega$ is conserved and, with the moment of inertia halved, the angular velocity doubles. The kinetic energy can be written $K = \dfrac{1}{2} I\omega^2 = \dfrac{L_z^2}{2I}$, so for a given $L_z$ it is inversely proportional to the moment of inertia. Therefore the kinetic energy **doubles**. The increase equals the work of the internal forces exerted by the muscles of the arms. The fourfold value is obtained by squaring the doubled angular velocity without halving $I$.
