Two blocks of masses $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hang from the ends of an inextensible string of negligible mass that passes, without slipping, over a pulley in the shape of a uniform disk of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, free to rotate without friction. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the blocks?

- $2.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.18\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The block of mass $m_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves down and the block of mass $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves up, with accelerations of the same magnitude $a$. The equations of the blocks are $m_1 g - T_1 = m_1 a$ and $T_2 - m_2 g = m_2 a$. For the pulley $(T_1 - T_2) R = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2\,\alpha$, and since $a = \alpha R$ one obtains $T_1 - T_2 = \dfrac{1}{2} M a$. Adding the equations of the blocks and substituting $T_1 - T_2$ gives
$$
a = \frac{(m_1 - m_2)\,g}{m_1 + m_2 + \frac{M}{2}} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 9.81}{9.0}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.18\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
Neglecting the mass of the pulley one would obtain $2.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
