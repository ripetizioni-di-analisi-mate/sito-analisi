A disk with moment of inertia $0{,}40\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ rotates at $6{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about a frictionless vertical axis. A second disk, at rest and with moment of inertia $0{,}20\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about the same axis, is dropped coaxially onto the first, and the two disks end up rotating together. What is the final angular velocity?

- $12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $2{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $3{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $4{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The forces the disks exert on each other are internal, and the weight and the reactions of the axis have zero axial torque, so $L_z$ is conserved. Before the coupling $L_z = I_1\omega_1 = 0{,}40 \cdot 6{,}0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} = 2{,}4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$, and after it $L_z = (I_1 + I_2)\,\omega$. Therefore
$$
\omega = \frac{I_1\omega_1}{I_1 + I_2} = \frac{2{,}4}{0{,}60}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The value $3{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the average of the two angular velocities, which coincides with the result only for disks with the same moment of inertia.
