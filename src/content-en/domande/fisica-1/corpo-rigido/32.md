A small ball of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a smooth horizontal table, tied to a string that passes through a small hole at the center of the table. The ball moves along a circle of radius $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ with a speed of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. By pulling the string from below the table, the radius is reduced to $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the new speed of the ball?

- $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $32\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The tension of the string is always directed toward the hole, so its torque about the hole is zero; the weight and the reaction of the table balance each other. Therefore the angular momentum of the ball about the hole is conserved, and since the velocity is perpendicular to the string, $m v_1 r_1 = m v_2 r_2$. Hence
$$
v_2 = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\,v_1 = \frac{0.40}{0.10} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The kinetic energy increases, because the tension does positive work while the string is being pulled.
