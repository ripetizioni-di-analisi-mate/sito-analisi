A uniform plank of mass $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on a horizontal pier and sticks out beyond the edge, without being fastened. A person of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ must be able to walk to the overhanging end without the plank tipping over. What is the maximum overhang of the plank?

- $0.67\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $x$ be the length of the overhanging part. When the plank is about to tip the only support is the edge of the pier, so torques are taken about the edge. The person at the end has lever arm $x$, while the center of the plank lies on the pier side, at distance $2.0\,\mathrm{m} - x$ from the edge. At the limit the two torques are equal, therefore $60\,x = 20\,(2.0 - x)$ and
$$
x = \frac{40}{80}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0.67\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by taking half the length, $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, as the lever arm of the plank's weight instead of the distance of its center from the edge.
