A motor applies a constant torque of $6.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ to a shaft, which meanwhile makes $5$ complete revolutions. How much work does the motor do?

- $30.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $188\,\mathrm{J}$
- $94.2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.08 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

For a constant torque the work is $W = \tau_z\,(\theta_2 - \theta_1)$, with the angle expressed in radians. One revolution corresponds to $2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$, so $5$ revolutions correspond to $10\pi\,\mathrm{rad} \approx 31.4\,\mathrm{rad}$. Therefore
$$
W = 6.0 \cdot 10\pi\,\mathrm{J} = 188\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $30.0\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using the number of revolutions in place of the angle in radians, $94.2\,\mathrm{J}$ by counting $\pi$ radians per revolution, and $1.08 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$ by using the angle in degrees.
