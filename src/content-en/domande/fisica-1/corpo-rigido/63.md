A skater pulls her arms inward with negligible external torque about her rotation axis. Her initial moment of inertia is $6\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Given the initial angular speed $u = 4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the final angular speed $\omega = 12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, what is the final moment of inertia $I$?

- $2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $18\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$

## Explanation

Angular momentum is conserved, so the product of moment of inertia and angular speed stays the same. In the stated units, the relation is $\omega = \dfrac{6 \cdot u}{I}$. Solving for the unknown gives $I = \dfrac{6 \cdot u}{\omega}$. Substituting the given values yields $2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$.
