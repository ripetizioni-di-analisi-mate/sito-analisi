A frictionless hinge at one end allows a uniform rod of $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ to rotate in a vertical plane. The rod is held still in a horizontal position and then let go. At the instant it is let go, what is the acceleration of its free end?

- $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7.36\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $14.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

At the instant of release the weight, applied at the midpoint, has lever arm $\dfrac{L}{2}$ about the hinge, and the moment of inertia about the hinge is $\dfrac{1}{3} M L^2$. The equation of rotational motion therefore gives
$$
\alpha = \frac{M g\,\dfrac{L}{2}}{\dfrac{1}{3} M L^2} = \frac{3g}{2L} .
$$
The initial angular velocity is zero, so the free end has only tangential acceleration $a = \alpha L = \dfrac{3}{2}\,g = 14.7\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, which does not depend on the length and is greater than $g$. The value $7.36\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is the acceleration of the center of mass.
