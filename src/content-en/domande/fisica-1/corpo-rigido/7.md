A motor applies a constant torque of $12\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ to a wheel, initially at rest, with moment of inertia $0.80\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about the axis; friction is negligible. What is the power delivered by the motor $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after the start?

- $360\,\mathrm{W}$
- $461\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1440\,\mathrm{W}$
- $720\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

By the equation of rotational motion $\alpha = \dfrac{\tau_z}{I} = \dfrac{12}{0.80}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 15\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, so after $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the wheel turns at $\omega = \alpha t = 60\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The instantaneous power of the torque is $P = \tau_z\,\omega = 12 \cdot 60\,\mathrm{W} = 720\,\mathrm{W}$. The value $360\,\mathrm{W}$ is the average power over the first $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$, that is the kinetic energy $1.44 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ divided by the time. The value $1440\,\mathrm{W}$ is obtained by using the angle described, $120\,\mathrm{rad}$, in place of the angular velocity.
