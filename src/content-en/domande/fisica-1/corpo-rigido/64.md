A skater pulls her arms inward with negligible external torque about her rotation axis. Her initial moment of inertia is $6\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. The initial data are the initial angular speed $u = 4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the final moment of inertia $I = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Change the final moment of inertia to $3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $\omega$ to its initial value?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

Angular momentum is conserved, so the product of moment of inertia and angular speed stays the same. The relation $\omega = \dfrac{6 \cdot u}{I}$ gives $12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ before the change and $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
