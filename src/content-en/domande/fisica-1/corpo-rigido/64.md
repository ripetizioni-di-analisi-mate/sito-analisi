A yo-yo, which can be modeled as a uniform solid disk, descends by unwinding an inextensible string of negligible mass wound around its rim; the other end of the string is held still and the string stays vertical. What is the acceleration of the center of the yo-yo?

- $6.54\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.27\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The forces on the yo-yo are the weight, at the center, and the tension $T$ of the string, upward at the rim. The first cardinal equation and the second one about the center of mass give $M g - T = M a$ and $T R = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2\,\alpha$. The string is at rest and does not slip, so $a = \alpha R$, and from the second equation $T = \dfrac{1}{2} M a$. Substituting into the first
$$
a = \frac{2}{3}\,g = 6.54\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The value $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is obtained by neglecting the tension of the string, as if the yo-yo were falling freely.
