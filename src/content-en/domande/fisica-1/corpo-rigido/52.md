A thin-walled cylindrical tube has mass $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$, length $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ and diameter $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. What is its moment of inertia about its own axis of symmetry?

- $0.030\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.24\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.060\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.18\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

In the thin-walled tube all the mass is at the same distance $R$ from the axis, as in a ring, so $I = M R^2$ for any length of the tube. The radius is $R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore
$$
I = 1.5 \cdot 0.20^2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.060\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} .
$$
The value $0.030\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is the moment of inertia of a solid cylinder, $\dfrac{1}{2} M R^2$, and $0.24\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is obtained by using the diameter instead of the radius. The value $0.18\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is obtained by applying the rod formula, $\dfrac{1}{12} M L^2$, to the length of the tube.
