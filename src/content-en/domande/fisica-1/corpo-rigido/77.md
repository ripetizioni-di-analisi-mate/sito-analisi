A rigid body's axis is parallel to an axis through its center of mass. The moment of inertia about the center-of-mass axis is $5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Given the distance between the axes $d = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ and the moment of inertia about the new axis $I = 17\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, what is the mass $m$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{17}{4}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The parallel-axis theorem adds $md^2$ to the center-of-mass value. In the stated units, the relation is $I = d^{2} \cdot m + 5$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{I - 5}{d^{2}}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{kg}$.
