A uniform disk of mass $2{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rotates without friction about its own fixed horizontal axis. An inextensible string of negligible mass, wound around the rim without slipping, supports a block of mass $1{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$. The system starts from rest. What is the speed of the block after it has descended $1{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $3{,}13\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4{,}43\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2{,}56\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2{,}21\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The axis is frictionless and the string does not slip, so the mechanical energy of the system is conserved. The rim of the disk has the same speed $v$ as the string, therefore $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R}$ and the kinetic energy of the disk is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2 \cdot \dfrac{v^2}{R^2} = \dfrac{1}{4} M v^2$, which does not depend on the radius. Conservation of energy gives $m g h = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 + \dfrac{1}{4} M v^2$, so
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 m g h}{m + \frac{M}{2}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1{,}0 \cdot 9{,}81 \cdot 1{,}0}{2{,}0}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3{,}13\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $4{,}43\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the free-fall speed, which is obtained by neglecting the kinetic energy of the disk.
