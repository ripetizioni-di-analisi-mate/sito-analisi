A wheel rolls without slipping on a fixed floor. A short exposure shows an instantaneously stationary point at the bottom. Can the wheel's instantaneous kinetic energy be computed as $\dfrac12 I_P\omega^2$ about that point $P$?

- Yes, using the centre-of-mass inertia directly.
- Yes, using the inertia about the axis through contact parallel to the wheel axis.
- No, because the centre translates.
- No, because the angular speed of the wheel is zero at contact.

## Explanation

The points have the velocities of an instantaneous rotation about the contact. By the parallel-axis theorem $I_P=I_C+mR^2$, so $\dfrac12 I_P\omega^2$ includes $\dfrac12m v_C^2$ because $v_C=R\omega$.
