Two coaxial disks have the same moment of inertia about their common axis, which is vertical and frictionless. The first rotates with angular velocity $\omega_0$ and the second is at rest; the two disks are coupled and rotate together. What fraction of the initial kinetic energy is dissipated in the coupling?

- None
- One quarter
- One half
- Three quarters

## Explanation

The external forces have zero axial torque, so $L_z$ is conserved and from $I\omega_0 = 2I\omega$ it follows that $\omega = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$. The kinetic energy goes from $\dfrac{1}{2} I\omega_0^2$ to $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2I \cdot \dfrac{\omega_0^2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{4} I\omega_0^2$, that is to half of the initial value. Therefore **one half** of the initial kinetic energy is dissipated. The coupling is an inelastic collision between the two disks, and the friction forces between the surfaces in contact do negative work even though their total torque is zero.
