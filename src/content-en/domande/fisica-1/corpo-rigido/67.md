A frisbee, which can be modeled as a uniform disk of mass $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$, is thrown with its center moving at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and spinning at $60\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about its own symmetry axis. What is its total kinetic energy?

- $9.44\,\mathrm{J}$
- $7.50\,\mathrm{J}$
- $11.4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.94\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

By the kinetic energy of a rigid body, which follows from **König's theorem**, the energy is the sum of that of the center of mass and that of the rotation about the axis through the center of mass. The first equals $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.15 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J} = 7.50\,\mathrm{J}$, the second $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.15 \cdot 0.12^2 \cdot 60^2\,\mathrm{J} = 1.94\,\mathrm{J}$, so
$$
K = 7.50\,\mathrm{J} + 1.94\,\mathrm{J} = 9.44\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $7.50\,\mathrm{J}$ neglects the rotation, and $11.4\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using $M R^2$ as the moment of inertia of the disk.
