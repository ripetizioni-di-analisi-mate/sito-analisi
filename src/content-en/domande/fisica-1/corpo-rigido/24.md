A constant tangential force of $20\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied to the rim of a uniform disk of mass $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$, free to rotate without friction about its own fixed axis. What is the angular acceleration of the disk?

- $20\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $40\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $160\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

The force is tangent to the rim, so its lever arm is the radius and the axial torque is $\tau_z = F R = 20 \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The moment of inertia of the disk is $I = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 0.25^2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.125\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. By the equation of rotational motion
$$
\alpha = \frac{\tau_z}{I} = \frac{5.0}{0.125}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 40\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} .
$$
With the moment of inertia $M R^2$ one would obtain $20\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, and dividing the force instead of the torque by $I$ one would obtain $160\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$.
