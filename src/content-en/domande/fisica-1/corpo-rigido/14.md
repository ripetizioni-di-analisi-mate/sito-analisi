A disk starts from rest and rotates about a fixed axis with constant angular acceleration $\alpha = 3{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. What angle does it sweep out in the first $4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $48\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $12\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $6{,}0\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $24\,\mathrm{rad}$

## Explanation

With constant angular acceleration and zero initial angular velocity, $\theta - \theta_0 = \dfrac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$. Therefore the angle swept out is
$$
\theta - \theta_0 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3{,}0 \cdot 4{,}0^2\,\mathrm{rad} = 24\,\mathrm{rad} ,
$$
that is a little less than $4$ revolutions. The value $48\,\mathrm{rad}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $12\,\mathrm{rad}$ is the product $\alpha t$, which gives the final angular velocity in $\mathrm{rad/s}$.
