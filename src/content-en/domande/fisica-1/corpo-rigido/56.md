A rigid body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ has moment of inertia $0.18\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about an axis $a$ that is $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center of mass. What is the moment of inertia about an axis parallel to $a$ that is $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center of mass?

- $0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.26\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.42\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.36\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

The **parallel-axis theorem** relates the moment of inertia about an axis to the one about the parallel axis through the center of mass. From the axis $a$ one therefore gets $I_C = 0.18 - 2.0 \cdot 0.20^2 = 0.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. Then, about the second axis,
$$
I = I_C + M d^2 = 0.10 + 2.0 \cdot 0.40^2 = 0.42\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} .
$$
The value $0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is obtained by applying the theorem starting from the axis $a$, which does not pass through the center of mass.
