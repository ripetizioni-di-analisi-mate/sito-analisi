A wheel of radius $R = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and weight $400\,\mathrm{N}$ rests on the floor, touching the edge $E$ of a step of height $h = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. A horizontal force $F$ applied at the center $C$ pushes the wheel toward the step. What is the minimum value of $F$ that lifts the wheel off the floor?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2+2*sin(t),0,6.29" poligoni="1.6,0 4,0 4,0.8 1.6,0.8" linee="-3,0 4,0" vettori="0,2,1.9,2,F;0,2,0,0.6,mg" segmenti="0,2,1.6,0.8" punti="0,2,pieno,C;1.6,0.8,pieno,E" xmin=-3 xmax=4.2 ymin=-0.4 ymax=4.3 titolo="The wheel of radius $R$ is pushed against the edge $E$ of a step of height $h$"}

- $400\,\mathrm{N}$
- $160\,\mathrm{N}$
- $300\,\mathrm{N}$
- $533\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

At the instant the wheel leaves the floor the reaction of the floor is zero, and the wheel is acted on by $F$, by the weight and by the reaction of the edge. The edge $E$ is chosen as the pole, so its reaction has zero torque. The weight has a lever arm equal to the horizontal distance between $C$ and $E$, that is $\sqrt{R^2 - (R - h)^2} = \sqrt{0.25 - 0.09}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, and the force $F$ has a lever arm equal to the height difference $R - h = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Setting the two torques equal gives
$$
F = \frac{400 \cdot 0.40}{0.30}\,\mathrm{N} = 533\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $300\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by swapping the two lever arms.
