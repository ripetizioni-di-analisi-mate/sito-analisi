A uniform disk oscillates in a vertical plane about a frictionless horizontal axis, perpendicular to the disk and passing through a point of its rim. The disk is replaced by a uniform disk of the same radius and twice the mass, suspended in the same way. How does the period of small oscillations change?

- It doubles
- It stays the same
- It halves
- It becomes $\sqrt{2}$ times larger

## Explanation

For a physical pendulum the angular frequency of small oscillations is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{M g d}{I_O}}$. The center of the disk is at distance $d = R$ from the axis and, by the **parallel-axis theorem**, $I_O = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2 + M R^2 = \dfrac{3}{2} M R^2$. Therefore $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{2g}{3R}}$, because the mass appears both in the torque of the weight and in the moment of inertia and cancels out. Hence the period $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{3R}{2g}}$ **stays the same** with the disk of twice the mass.
