A disk rotates about its own fixed axis with constant angular velocity. Point $A$ of the disk is at distance $r$ from the axis and point $B$ is at distance $2r$. Which statement is true?

- $B$ has twice the speed and four times the centripetal acceleration of $A$
- $B$ has twice the speed and the same centripetal acceleration as $A$
- $B$ has twice the speed and twice the centripetal acceleration of $A$
- $B$ has the same speed and the same centripetal acceleration as $A$

## Explanation

All points of the disk have the same angular velocity $\omega$. For a point at distance $r$ from the axis, $v = \omega r$ and $a_c = \omega^2 r$, so both quantities are proportional to the distance from the axis. Therefore $B$ has speed $2\omega r$ and centripetal acceleration $2\omega^2 r$, twice the values of $A$. The formula $a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$ gives the same result, because $\dfrac{(2v)^2}{2r} = 2\,\dfrac{v^2}{r}$; the factor $4$ is obtained by doubling the speed without doubling the distance.
