A circular track is fixed on a horizontal wheel, free to rotate without friction about its own vertical axis. A toy electric train is on the track, and at the start everything is at rest. The train starts moving counterclockwise, as seen from above. What does the wheel do?

- It rotates clockwise and keeps rotating as long as the train moves
- It stays at rest, because the train and the track exchange internal forces
- It also rotates counterclockwise
- It rotates clockwise only while the train accelerates, then it stops

## Explanation

The only forces acting on the system formed by the train and the wheel are the weights and the reactions of the axis, which have zero axial torque. Therefore the angular momentum of the system about the axis is conserved and stays equal to its initial value, which is zero. The train moving counterclockwise has positive angular momentum, so the wheel must have an equal and opposite angular momentum, that is, rotate clockwise. As long as the train moves its angular momentum is not zero, and neither is that of the wheel.
