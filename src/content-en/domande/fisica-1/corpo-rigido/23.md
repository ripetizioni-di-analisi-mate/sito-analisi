Consider the moments of inertia of a uniform rod of mass $M$ and length $L$ about axes perpendicular to the rod. About which of these axes is the moment of inertia smallest?

- The axis through one end of the rod
- The axis through the point at distance $\dfrac{L}{4}$ from the center
- The moment of inertia is the same about all these axes
- The axis through the center of the rod

## Explanation

The axes perpendicular to the rod are parallel to one another, so the **parallel-axis theorem** applies with respect to the axis through the center of mass, which is the center of the rod. For an axis at distance $d$ from the center, $I = \dfrac{1}{12} M L^2 + M d^2$, which increases with $d$. Therefore the minimum value, $\dfrac{1}{12} M L^2$, occurs for the axis through the center. For the axis at $\dfrac{L}{4}$ from the center $I = \dfrac{7}{48} M L^2$, and for the axis through one end $I = \dfrac{1}{3} M L^2$.
