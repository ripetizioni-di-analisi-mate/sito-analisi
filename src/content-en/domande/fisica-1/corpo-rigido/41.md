A uniform solid sphere starts from rest and rolls without slipping down a rough section, with a drop in height of $1.4\,\mathrm{m}$. At the bottom, it continues up a perfectly smooth ramp. What maximum height does the center of the sphere reach on the ramp, measured from the level of the bottom?

- $1.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

On the way down static friction does no work, so $M g h = \dfrac{7}{10} M v^2$; therefore the translational energy is $\dfrac{1}{2} M v^2 = \dfrac{5}{7} M g h$ and the rotational energy is $\dfrac{2}{7} M g h$. On the smooth ramp the forces acting are the weight, applied at the center, and the normal force, which passes through the center, so the torque about the center is zero and the angular velocity stays constant. Hence only the translational energy turns into potential energy, and
$$
h' = \frac{5}{7}\,h = \frac{5}{7} \cdot 1.4\,\mathrm{m} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $1.4\,\mathrm{m}$ would be correct if the ramp were rough as well and the sphere kept rolling without slipping.
