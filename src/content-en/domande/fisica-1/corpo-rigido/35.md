A thin uniform hoop of radius $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ spins at $20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about its own horizontal axis, with its center at rest. It is set upright on a rough horizontal floor: at first it slides, then friction brings it to pure rolling. What is the speed of the center when the hoop starts rolling without slipping?

- $3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Take as pole a fixed point $O$ of the floor on the line along which the contact point moves. The friction force lies on this line, so its torque about $O$ is zero; the weight and the normal reaction are vertical, opposite and have the same lever arm, therefore their total torque is also zero. Hence the angular momentum about $O$ is conserved. At the start the center is at rest and $L = M R^2\,\omega_0$; in pure rolling $L = M v R + M R^2\,\dfrac{v}{R} = 2 M v R$. So
$$
v = \frac{\omega_0 R}{2} = \frac{20 \cdot 0.35}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.5\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by imposing conservation of energy, which does not hold, because kinetic friction dissipates energy while the hoop slides.
