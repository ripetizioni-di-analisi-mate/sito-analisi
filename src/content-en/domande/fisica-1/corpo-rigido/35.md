A platform in the shape of a uniform disk, of mass $100\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$, rotates without friction about its own vertical axis at $0{,}50\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. A person of mass $50\,\mathrm{kg}$, treated as a point mass, walks from the rim to the center of the platform. What is the final angular velocity?

- $1{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $0{,}25\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $0{,}75\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $0{,}50\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The forces between the person and the platform are internal, and the external forces have zero axial torque, so $L_z$ is conserved. At the start $I_1 = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2 + m R^2 = (200 + 200)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 400\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, while with the person on the axis only the moment of inertia of the platform remains, $I_2 = 200\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. Therefore
$$
\omega_2 = \frac{I_1}{I_2}\,\omega_1 = 2 \cdot 0{,}50\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 1{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
With the moment of inertia $M R^2$ for the platform one would obtain $0{,}75\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and $0{,}25\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is obtained by inverting the ratio of the moments of inertia.
