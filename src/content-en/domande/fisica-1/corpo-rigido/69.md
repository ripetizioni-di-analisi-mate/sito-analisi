A uniform rod of mass $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ rotates about a fixed axis perpendicular to the rod and passing through one of its ends. The free end has speed $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the kinetic energy of the rod?

- $12\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Let $L$ be the length of the rod. The free end is at distance $L$ from the axis, so $\omega = \dfrac{v}{L}$. The moment of inertia about the axis through an end is $\dfrac{1}{3} M L^2$, therefore
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} M L^2 \cdot \frac{v^2}{L^2} = \frac{1}{6} M v^2 = \frac{1.5 \cdot 4.0^2}{6}\,\mathrm{J} = 4.0\,\mathrm{J} ,
$$
and the length of the rod cancels. The value $12\,\mathrm{J}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2} M v^2$, the energy the rod would have if all its points had the speed of the end, and $3.0\,\mathrm{J}$ is the kinetic energy of the center of mass alone, which has speed $\dfrac{v}{2}$. The value $1.0\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using the moment of inertia about the center, $\dfrac{1}{12} M L^2$.
