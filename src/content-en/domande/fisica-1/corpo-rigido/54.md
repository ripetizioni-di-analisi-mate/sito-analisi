A wheel with moment of inertia $I$ starts from rest about a fixed axis. A constant net torque acts on it. The initial data are the magnitude of the net torque $\tau = 12\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, the elapsed time $t = 3\,\mathrm{s}$ and the moment of inertia $I = 6\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Change the elapsed time to $6\,\mathrm{s}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $\theta$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $4$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

Angular acceleration is constant and equals $\tau/I$. Starting from rest gives $\theta=\alpha t^2/2$. The relation $\theta = \dfrac{t^{2} \cdot \tau}{2 \cdot I}$ gives $9\,\mathrm{rad}$ before the change and $36\,\mathrm{rad}$ after it. The ratio is $4$.
