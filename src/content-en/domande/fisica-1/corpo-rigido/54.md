A concentric disk of radius $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ is cut out of a uniform solid disk of mass $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, leaving an annulus. What is the moment of inertia of the annulus about its symmetry axis perpendicular to its plane?

- $0.0800\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.0350\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.0294\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.0547\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

The mass is distributed uniformly over the surface, so the removed part has mass $m = 4.0 \cdot \dfrac{0.15^2}{0.20^2}\,\mathrm{kg} = 2.25\,\mathrm{kg}$. The moment of inertia is additive, therefore that of the annulus is the difference between that of the whole disk and that of the removed disk:
$$
I = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 0.20^2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} - \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.25 \cdot 0.15^2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.0547\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} .
$$
The value $0.0350\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is obtained by applying $\dfrac{1}{2} M R^2$ to the mass of the annulus as if it were a solid disk, and $0.0294\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ by subtracting $m R^2$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{2} m R^2$ for the removed part.
