A door is a uniform rectangular slab of mass $25\,\mathrm{kg}$ and width $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$, which rotates about the vertical axis of its hinges, located along one of its sides. What is the moment of inertia of the door about this axis?

- $6.75\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $1.69\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $20.3\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $10.1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

The slab can be divided into thin horizontal strips, each of which is a rod of length $a = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ rotating about a perpendicular axis through one of its ends. Each strip of mass $dm$ contributes $\dfrac{1}{3}\,a^2\,dm$, so, adding up, the moment of inertia of the door does not depend on its height and equals
$$
I = \frac{1}{3} M a^2 = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 25 \cdot 0.90^2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 6.75\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} .
$$
The value $1.69\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is the moment of inertia about the vertical axis through the center of the door, and $20.3\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is obtained as if all the mass were on the free edge.
