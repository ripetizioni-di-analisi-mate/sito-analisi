A thin ring and a solid disk, both uniform, have the same mass and the same radius and can rotate without friction about their own fixed axis of symmetry. The same constant axial torque is applied to each of them. Which statement is true?

- The two angular accelerations are equal, because mass and radius are equal
- The angular acceleration of the disk is twice that of the ring
- The angular acceleration of the ring is twice that of the disk
- The angular acceleration of the disk is four times that of the ring

## Explanation

Both bodies rotate about a fixed axis, so by the equation of rotational motion $\alpha = \dfrac{\tau_z}{I}$. The ring has moment of inertia $M R^2$ and the disk $\dfrac{1}{2} M R^2$, because in the disk part of the mass is closer to the axis. Therefore, for the same torque, the angular acceleration of the disk, $\dfrac{2\tau_z}{M R^2}$, is twice that of the ring, $\dfrac{\tau_z}{M R^2}$.
