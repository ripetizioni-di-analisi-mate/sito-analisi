A uniform beam of mass $80\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rests horizontally on two supports placed at the ends $A$ and $B$. A person of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on the beam $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. What is the reaction of the support at $A$?

- $564\,\mathrm{N}$
- $907\,\mathrm{N}$
- $736\,\mathrm{N}$
- $515\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

We choose the pole at $B$, so that the reaction $R_B$ has zero torque. The weight of the beam, $784.8\,\mathrm{N}$, is applied at the midpoint, $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $B$; the weight of the person, $686.7\,\mathrm{N}$, is applied $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $B$; the reaction $R_A$ has lever arm $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The balance of torques about $B$ therefore gives
$$
R_A = \frac{784.8 \cdot 3.0 + 686.7 \cdot 4.5}{6.0}\,\mathrm{N} = 907\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $564\,\mathrm{N}$ is the reaction at $B$, which follows from the balance of the vertical forces $R_A + R_B = 1471.5\,\mathrm{N}$, and $515\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by forgetting the weight of the beam.
