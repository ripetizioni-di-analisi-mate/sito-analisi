A rigid body's axis is parallel to an axis through its center of mass. The moment of inertia about the center-of-mass axis is $5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Given the mass $m = 3\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the moment of inertia about the new axis $I = 17\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, what is the distance between the axes $d$?

- $\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{51}}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The parallel-axis theorem adds $md^2$ to the center-of-mass value. In the stated units, the relation is $I = d^{2} \cdot m + 5$. Solving for the unknown gives $d = \dfrac{\sqrt{I - 5}}{\sqrt{m}}$. Substituting the given values yields $2\,\mathrm{m}$.
