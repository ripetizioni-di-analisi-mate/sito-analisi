A wheel with moment of inertia $I$ starts from rest about a fixed axis. A constant net torque acts on it. Given the magnitude of the net torque $\tau = 12\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, the moment of inertia $I = 6\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and the angle swept out $\theta = 9\,\mathrm{rad}$, what is the elapsed time $t$?

- $9\,\mathrm{s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\dfrac{3 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Angular acceleration is constant and equals $\tau/I$. Starting from rest gives $\theta=\alpha t^2/2$. In the stated units, the relation is $\theta = \dfrac{t^{2} \cdot \tau}{2 \cdot I}$. Solving for the unknown gives $t = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{I} \cdot \sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{\tau}}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{s}$.
