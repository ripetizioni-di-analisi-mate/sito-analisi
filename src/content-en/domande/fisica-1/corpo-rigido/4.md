A uniform solid cylinder of mass $8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ rotates without friction about its own horizontal axis. An inextensible string of negligible mass, wound around the cylinder without slipping, supports a block of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the acceleration of the block?

- $1.09\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.577\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Let $m$ be the mass of the block, $M$ and $R$ the mass and the radius of the cylinder, and $T$ the tension of the string. Newton's second law for the block gives $m g - T = m a$, and the equation of rotational motion for the cylinder gives $T R = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2\,\alpha$. The string does not slip, so $a = \alpha R$ and therefore $T = \dfrac{1}{2} M a$. Substituting into the first equation gives
$$
a = \frac{m g}{m + \frac{M}{2}} = \frac{1.0 \cdot 9.81}{1.0 + 4.0}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
With the moment of inertia $M R^2$ in place of $\dfrac{1}{2} M R^2$ one would obtain $1.09\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
