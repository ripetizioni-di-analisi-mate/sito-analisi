A uniform rod $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ long is held vertical, with its lower end resting on a smooth horizontal floor, and is released from rest. The lower end slides on the floor without lifting off. What is the speed of the center of the rod at the instant the rod reaches the floor in horizontal position?

- $3.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.94\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.85\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The forces on the rod are its weight and the normal reaction of the floor, both vertical, so the horizontal component of the velocity of the center stays zero and the center moves down along the vertical. The normal reaction is perpendicular to the velocity of the sliding end, therefore it does no work and mechanical energy is conserved. If $\varphi$ is the angle between the rod and the floor, the center is at height $z_C = \dfrac{L}{2}\sin\varphi$, so its speed is $\dfrac{L}{2}\cos\varphi\,|\omega|$ and, when the rod is horizontal, it equals $v_C = \dfrac{L}{2}\,|\omega|$. Conservation of energy then gives
$$
M g\,\frac{L}{2} = \frac{1}{2} M v_C^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{12} M L^2 \cdot \frac{4 v_C^2}{L^2} = \frac{2}{3} M v_C^2 ,
$$
therefore $v_C = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{3 g L} = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{3 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $3.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by neglecting the rotational kinetic energy, as if the center fell freely from $\dfrac{L}{2}$.
