A spiral spring exerts on a disk a restoring torque $\tau_z = -\kappa\,\theta$, with $\kappa = 0.80\,\mathrm{N\cdot m/rad}$, where $\theta$ is the angle of rotation measured from the rest position. The disk has a moment of inertia of $0.010\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ and rotates without friction. It is turned by half a revolution and released from rest. What is its angular velocity as it passes through the rest position?

- $39.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $22.4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $4.47\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $28.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

Half a revolution corresponds to $\theta_0 = \pi\,\mathrm{rad}$. The work of the spring from $\theta_0$ to $0$ is $W = \displaystyle\int_{\theta_0}^{0} (-\kappa\,\theta)\,d\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\kappa\,\theta_0^2$, and by the work-energy theorem it equals $\dfrac{1}{2} I\omega^2$. So
$$
\omega = \theta_0\,\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{I}} = \pi\,\sqrt{\frac{0.80}{0.010}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 28.1\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The value $22.4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is obtained by computing the work as $\kappa\,\theta_0$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\kappa\,\theta_0^2$, and $4.47\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ by expressing the angle in revolutions instead of radians.
