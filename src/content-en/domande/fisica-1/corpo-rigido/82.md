A front-wheel-drive car accelerates on a horizontal road, and the front wheels roll without slipping. Which statement about the friction force the road exerts on a front wheel is true?

- It is static friction and points opposite to the direction of motion
- It is kinetic friction and points opposite to the direction of motion
- It is zero, because the wheel does not slip
- It is static friction and points in the direction of motion

## Explanation

The motor applies to the wheel a torque that tends to make it spin faster; without friction the contact point would slide backward relative to the road. Friction opposes this sliding, so it points forward, in the direction of motion. The contact point is at rest, therefore the friction is static. The friction forces on the driving wheels are the external horizontal forces on the car pointing forward, and by the first cardinal equation they are the forces that accelerate the car.
