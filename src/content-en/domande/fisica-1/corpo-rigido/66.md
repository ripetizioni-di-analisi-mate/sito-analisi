A motor delivering a constant power of $2.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ sets a flywheel in rotation; the flywheel starts at rest and has moment of inertia $2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about its own axis, and friction is negligible. After how long does the flywheel reach $3000$ rpm?

- $98.7\,\mathrm{s}$
- $49.3\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4.50 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.314\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Friction is negligible, so by the work-energy theorem the work done by the motor equals the final kinetic energy of the flywheel. The final angular velocity is $\omega = \dfrac{3000 \cdot 2\pi}{60}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 100\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 314\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, therefore $K = \dfrac{1}{2} I\omega^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 314^2\,\mathrm{J} = 9.87 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{J}$. The power is constant, so the work done in the time $t$ is $P t$, and
$$
t = \frac{K}{P} = \frac{9.87 \cdot 10^{4}}{2.0 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{s} = 49.3\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $98.7\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the kinetic energy, and $0.314\,\mathrm{s}$ by dividing the angular momentum $I\omega$ by the power instead of the kinetic energy.
