A grinding wheel of radius $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ rotates at a constant angular velocity of $300\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. A piece of metal at rest is pressed against the rim of the wheel with a normal force of $40\,\mathrm{N}$, and the coefficient of kinetic friction between the piece and the wheel is $0.50$. What power must the motor supply to make up for friction?

- $600\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1200\,\mathrm{W}$
- $6000\,\mathrm{W}$
- $300\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The kinetic friction force on the rim of the wheel has magnitude $f = \mu_k N = 0.50 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{N} = 20\,\mathrm{N}$ and is tangent to the rim. Its axial torque has magnitude $f R = 20 \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The angular velocity is constant, so the motor applies an equal and opposite torque, and its power is
$$
P = \tau_z\,\omega = 2.0 \cdot 300\,\mathrm{W} = 600\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The value $1200\,\mathrm{W}$ is obtained by using the normal force instead of the friction force, and $6000\,\mathrm{W}$ by multiplying the friction force by $\omega$ without the radius.
