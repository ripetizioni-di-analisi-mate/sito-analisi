A point mass of $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves in uniform straight-line motion with speed $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the line $y = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ of the $xy$ plane, in the direction of increasing $x$. What is the magnitude of its angular momentum about the origin at the instant it passes through $x = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$
- Zero, because the motion is along a straight line

## Explanation

The magnitude of the angular momentum is $L = r\,m v \sin\varphi$, where $r \sin\varphi$ is the distance of the origin from the line along which the point moves. This distance is $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at every instant, so
$$
L = m v b = 0.50 \cdot 4.0 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s} ,
$$
and the value is the same at every position, consistent with the fact that no forces act on the point. The value $10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$ is obtained by using the distance $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the origin instead of the lever arm.
