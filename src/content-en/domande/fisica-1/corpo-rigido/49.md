A uniform disk rotates about its central axis. A tangential force acts at the rim and friction is negligible. The initial data are the force magnitude $F = 12\,\mathrm{N}$, the mass $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the radius $R = 2\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the radius to $4\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $\alpha$ to its initial value?

- $2$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

The torque is $FR$ and the disk's moment of inertia is $mR^2/2$. Their ratio gives the angular acceleration. The relation $\alpha = \dfrac{2 \cdot F}{R \cdot m}$ gives $3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ before the change and $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
