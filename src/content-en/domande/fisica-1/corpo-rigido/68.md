A uniform cubic crate of mass $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ and edge $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on a horizontal floor. It is tipped over slowly by rotating it about a bottom edge, until it lies on the adjacent face. What is the minimum work required?

- $118\,\mathrm{J}$
- $48.8\,\mathrm{J}$
- $166\,\mathrm{J}$
- $97.5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The potential energy of the crate is $M g z_C$, where $z_C$ is the height of the center of mass. During the slow rotation the center of mass rises until it is vertically above the edge, and from that position the crate tips over by itself, so the minimum work is the increase in potential energy up to that position. At the start the center of mass is at height $\dfrac{a}{2}$; in the critical position it is at a distance from the edge equal to half the diagonal of the face, $\dfrac{a\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and it is vertically above the edge, therefore it is at height $\dfrac{a\sqrt{2}}{2}$. Then
$$
L = M g\,\frac{a\left( \sqrt{2} - 1 \right)}{2} = 40 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \frac{0.60 \cdot 0.414}{2}\,\mathrm{J} = 48.8\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $166\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using the maximum height of the center of mass instead of its increase, and $118\,\mathrm{J}$ by assuming that the center of mass rises by $\dfrac{a}{2}$.
