A uniform rod of mass $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rotates on a smooth horizontal plane about a fixed, frictionless vertical axis through its midpoint, at $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Two small rings of $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ each, threaded on the rod and initially on the axis, slide toward the ends, where they stop against two stoppers. What is the final angular velocity?

- $7.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The external forces are the weights, the reactions of the plane and those of the axis, all with zero axial torque; the forces between the rings and the rod are internal. So the angular momentum about the axis is conserved. At the start $I_1 = \dfrac{1}{12} \cdot 0.60 \cdot 1.0^2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.050\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$; at the end each ring is $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis and $I_2 = 0.050 + 2 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 0.50^2 = 0.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. Therefore
$$
\omega_2 = \frac{I_1}{I_2}\,\omega_1 = \frac{0.050}{0.10} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 5.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$
The value $7.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is obtained by imposing conservation of kinetic energy, which does not hold because the stopping of the rings against the ends is inelastic.
