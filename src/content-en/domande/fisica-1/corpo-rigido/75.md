A flywheel rotates about a fixed axis. A constant net torque acts in the direction of rotation. The initial angular speed is $3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The initial data are the moment of inertia $I = 4\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, the magnitude of the net torque $\tau = 8\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ and the angle swept out $\theta = 5\,\mathrm{rad}$. Change the moment of inertia to $8\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and the magnitude of the net torque to $4\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $K$ minus its initial value?

- $-2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-20\,\mathrm{J}$
- $16\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The constant torque does work $\tau\theta$. The initial kinetic energy $I\omega_0^2/2$ is added to the work. The relation $K = \dfrac{9 \cdot I}{2} + \tau \cdot \theta$ gives $58\,\mathrm{J}$ before the change and $56\,\mathrm{J}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $-2\,\mathrm{J}$.
