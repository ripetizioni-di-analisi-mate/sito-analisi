A disk of diameter $0{,}50\,\mathrm{m}$ starts from rest and rotates about its own fixed axis with constant angular acceleration $\alpha = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of a point on the rim $0{,}50\,\mathrm{s}$ after the start?

- $2{,}00\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1{,}00\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2{,}83\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1{,}41\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The rim is at distance $r = 0{,}25\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis, and after $0{,}50\,\mathrm{s}$ the angular velocity is $\omega = \alpha t = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The tangential acceleration is $a_t = \alpha r = 1{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the centripetal acceleration is $a_c = \omega^2 r = 4{,}0 \cdot 0{,}25\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The two components are perpendicular, so the magnitude of the acceleration is
$$
a = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_c^2} = \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1{,}41\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The value $2{,}00\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is the sum of the magnitudes, which would give the magnitude of the sum only for parallel vectors, and $2{,}83\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is obtained by using the diameter in place of the radius.
