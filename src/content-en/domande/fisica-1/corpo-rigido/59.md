A uniform rod of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a point mass of $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at one end. Among all the axes perpendicular to the rod, what is the minimum moment of inertia of the system?

- $0.333\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.0833\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.208\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.146\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

By the **parallel-axis theorem**, among parallel axes the minimum moment of inertia is the one about the axis through the center of mass. The center of the rod and the point mass have equal masses, so the center of mass lies halfway between them, $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from each. The moment of inertia about this axis is the sum of that of the rod, with the parallel-axis term, and that of the point mass:
$$
I_C = \frac{1}{12} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 1.0^2 + 1.0 \cdot 0.25^2 + 1.0 \cdot 0.25^2 = 0.208\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} .
$$
The value $0.333\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ corresponds to the axis through the center of the rod, and $0.146\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is obtained by forgetting the parallel-axis term of the rod.
