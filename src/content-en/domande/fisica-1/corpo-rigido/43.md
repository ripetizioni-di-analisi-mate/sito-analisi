A rigid bar of negligible mass rests on a fulcrum. On one side, $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ from the fulcrum, a load of mass $30\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs; on the other side, $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ from the fulcrum, a vertical downward force is applied. What must this force be for the bar to stay in equilibrium in a horizontal position?

- $10.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $883\,\mathrm{N}$
- $98.1\,\mathrm{N}$
- $294\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Torques are computed about the fulcrum, so that the reaction of the fulcrum does not appear. The load has weight $F_1 = 30 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 294\,\mathrm{N}$ and lever arm $b_1 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, while the effort force has lever arm $b_2 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. By the **law of the lever** $F_2\,b_2 = F_1\,b_1$, so
$$
F_2 = \frac{294 \cdot 0.40}{1.20}\,\mathrm{N} = 98.1\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $883\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by exchanging the lever arms, and $10.0\,\mathrm{N}$ by using the mass in place of the weight.
