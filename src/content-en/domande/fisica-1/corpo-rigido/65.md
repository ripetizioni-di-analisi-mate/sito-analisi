A skater pulls her arms inward with negligible external torque about her rotation axis. Her initial moment of inertia is $6\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. The initial data are the initial angular speed $u = 4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the final moment of inertia $I = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Change the initial angular speed to $3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the final moment of inertia to $1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $\omega$ minus its initial value?

- $-1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $-6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $- \dfrac{5}{6}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

Angular momentum is conserved, so the product of moment of inertia and angular speed stays the same. The relation $\omega = \dfrac{6 \cdot u}{I}$ gives $12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ before the change and $18\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
