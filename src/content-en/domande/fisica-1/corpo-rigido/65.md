A drum with two grooves rotates without friction about its own horizontal axis and has a moment of inertia of $0.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. A block of $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the groove of radius $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ by a string; a block of $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs on the opposite side from the groove of radius $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The strings are inextensible, of negligible mass and do not slip. What is the angular acceleration of the drum?

- $24.5\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $10.9\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $32.7\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

The weights of the blocks give opposite torques, $4.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.10 = 3.92\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ and $1.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.20 = 1.96\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, so the $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block goes down. The strings do not slip, therefore the blocks have accelerations $a_1 = \alpha r_1$ and $a_2 = \alpha r_2$. The equations of the two blocks and of the drum, $m_1 g - T_1 = m_1 \alpha r_1$, $T_2 - m_2 g = m_2 \alpha r_2$ and $T_1 r_1 - T_2 r_2 = I\alpha$, give
$$
\alpha = \frac{m_1 g r_1 - m_2 g r_2}{I + m_1 r_1^2 + m_2 r_2^2} = \frac{3.92 - 1.96}{0.10 + 0.040 + 0.040}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 10.9\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} .
$$
The value $19.6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ is obtained by neglecting the inertia of the blocks, and $24.5\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ by neglecting that of the drum.
