A uniform disk of mass $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ can rotate without friction about its own fixed axis $O$. Two forces of magnitude $12\,\mathrm{N}$, parallel and opposite in direction, act in the plane of the disk: $\vec{F}_1$ is tangent to the rim and tends to turn the disk counterclockwise, $\vec{F}_2$ is applied $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis and tends to turn it clockwise. What is the angular acceleration of the disk?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="0,0,0,2" vettori="0,2,-1.6,2,F₁;0,1,1.6,1,F₂" punti="0,0,pieno,O" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.6 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.6 titolo="$\vec{F}_1$ is tangent to the rim, $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis; $\vec{F}_2$ is applied $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis, in the opposite direction"}

- $40\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, clockwise
- $40\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, counterclockwise
- $120\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, counterclockwise
- Zero, because the resultant of the two forces is zero

## Explanation

The moment of inertia of the disk about the axis is $I = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1.5 \cdot 0.20^2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.030\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. Taking counterclockwise as positive, the axial torques of the two forces are $+12 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 2.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ and $-12 \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = -1.2\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, so $\tau_z = 1.2\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. By the equation of rotational motion
$$
\alpha = \frac{\tau_z}{I} = \frac{1.2}{0.030}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 40\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} ,
$$
and the positive sign means counterclockwise. The resultant of the two forces is zero, but their lines of action are distinct and the net torque is not zero; therefore the disk accelerates. The value $120\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ is obtained by adding the magnitudes of the two torques without taking their directions into account.
