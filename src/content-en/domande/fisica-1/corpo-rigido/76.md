A rigid body's axis is parallel to an axis through its center of mass. The moment of inertia about the center-of-mass axis is $5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Given the mass $m = 3\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the distance between the axes $d = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the moment of inertia about the new axis $I$?

- $29\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $34\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$
- $17\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$

## Explanation

The parallel-axis theorem adds $md^2$ to the center-of-mass value. In the stated units, the relation is $I = d^{2} \cdot m + 5$. Substituting the given values yields $17\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$.
