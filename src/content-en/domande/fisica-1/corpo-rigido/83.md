The forearm is held horizontal and the hand holds a weight of mass $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $35\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the elbow. The biceps pulls vertically upward and is attached to the forearm $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the elbow. Neglecting the weight of the forearm, what is the force of the biceps?

- $49.1\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.61\,\mathrm{N}$
- $380\,\mathrm{N}$
- $429\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The forearm is in equilibrium and can rotate about the elbow, so the elbow is chosen as the pole, and then the reaction of the elbow has zero torque. The two remaining forces are vertical and have lever arms $0.040\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the balance of torques gives $F \cdot 0.040 = 5.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.35$ and
$$
F = \frac{5.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.35}{0.040}\,\mathrm{N} = 429\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $380\,\mathrm{N}$ is the reaction of the elbow, which then follows from the balance of forces, and $5.61\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by swapping the two lever arms.
