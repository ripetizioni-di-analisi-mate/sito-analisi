A disk rotates about a fixed axis and its angular position is $\theta(t) = 2.0\,t^3 - 1.5\,t^2$, with $\theta$ in radians and $t$ in seconds. What is the angular acceleration of the disk at the instant $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $24\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $18\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $21\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

The angular velocity is the derivative of the angular position with respect to time, so $\omega(t) = 6.0\,t^2 - 3.0\,t$. The angular acceleration is the derivative of the angular velocity, therefore $\alpha(t) = 12\,t - 3.0$. At the instant $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ we then have
$$
\alpha = (12 \cdot 2.0 - 3.0)\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 21\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} .
$$
The value $18$ is the angular velocity $\omega(2.0) = 18\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and the value $10$ is the angular position $\theta(2.0) = 10\,\mathrm{rad}$. The value $24\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ is obtained by differentiating twice only the term $2.0\,t^3$.
