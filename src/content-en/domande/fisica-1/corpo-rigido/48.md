In a laboratory centrifuge the bottom of a test tube is $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis of rotation. What angular velocity is needed for the centripetal acceleration of the bottom of the tube to be $1000$ times the acceleration due to gravity $g$?

- $31.3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $313\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $100\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $9.81 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The bottom of the tube moves on a circle of radius $r = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, so its centripetal acceleration is $a_c = \omega^2 r$. Setting $\omega^2 r = 1000\,g$ gives
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{1000\,g}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{1000 \cdot 9.81}{0.10}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 313\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
$$
which is about $3.0 \cdot 10^3$ revolutions per minute. The value $9.81 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is obtained by forgetting the square root, and $31.3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ by multiplying by $r$ instead of dividing.
