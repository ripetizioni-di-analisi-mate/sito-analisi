A skater pulls her arms inward with negligible external torque about her rotation axis. Her initial moment of inertia is $6\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Given the final moment of inertia $I = 2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ and the final angular speed $\omega = 12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, what is the initial angular speed $u$?

- $36\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

Angular momentum is conserved, so the product of moment of inertia and angular speed stays the same. In the stated units, the relation is $\omega = \dfrac{6 \cdot u}{I}$. Solving for the unknown gives $u = \dfrac{I \cdot \omega}{6}$. Substituting the given values yields $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
