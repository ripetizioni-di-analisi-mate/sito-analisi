A spool consists of a central cylinder of radius $r$, on which a thread is wound, and two side disks of radius $R > r$, which rest on a rough horizontal surface. The thread leaves from the bottom of the central cylinder and is pulled horizontally, and the spool rolls without slipping. What happens?

- The spool rolls opposite to the force and the thread unwinds
- The spool rolls in the direction of the force and the thread winds up
- The spool rolls in the direction of the force and the thread unwinds
- The spool stays at rest, because the torque of the force about the center is balanced by that of friction

## Explanation

The contact point $P$ with the surface is chosen as the pole, so the normal reaction and friction have zero torque; the weight is applied at the center, vertically above $P$, so its torque is also zero. The force is horizontal and its line of action is at height $R - r$ above the surface, therefore its torque about $P$ has magnitude $F(R - r)$ and rotates the spool about $P$ in the sense that carries the center toward the side where the thread is pulled. So the spool rolls in the direction of the force. The center moves forward with speed $\omega R$, while the point where the thread leaves the cylinder, at distance $r$ below the center, moves forward with speed $\omega (R - r)$, which is also the speed of the free end of the thread. The center is faster than the end of the thread, therefore the spool approaches it and the thread winds up.
