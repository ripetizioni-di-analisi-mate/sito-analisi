A belt connects the pulley of a motor, of radius $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, to a second pulley of radius $12\,\mathrm{cm}$, and does not slip on either of them. The motor pulley turns at $1500$ revolutions per minute. At how many revolutions per minute does the second pulley turn?

- $4500$
- $1500$
- $167$
- $500$

## Explanation

The belt does not slip, so the points on the rims of the two pulleys have the same speed as the belt, that is $\omega_1 r_1 = \omega_2 r_2$. Therefore the angular velocity is inversely proportional to the radius, and
$$
\omega_2 = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\,\omega_1 = \frac{4.0}{12} \cdot 1500 \text{ rpm} = 500 \text{ rpm} .
$$
The value $4500$ is obtained by swapping the two radii, and $1500$ by assuming that the two pulleys have the same angular velocity instead of the same rim speed.
