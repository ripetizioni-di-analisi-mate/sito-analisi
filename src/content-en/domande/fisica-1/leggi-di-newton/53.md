A rope of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, uniformly distributed, hangs vertically from the ceiling and supports a body of mass $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at its lower end. The system is at rest. What is the tension of the rope at its midpoint?

- $49.1\,\mathrm{N}$
- $68.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $34.3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $58.9\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The tension is the same at all points only for an ideal string, of negligible mass. This rope has mass, so its tension changes from point to point. Consider the system formed by the hanging body and the lower half of the rope, of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$.

This system is acted on by the total weight $(5.0 + 1.0)\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot g$ and by the tension $T$ exerted by the upper half of the rope at the midpoint. The system is at rest, therefore
$$
T = (5.0\,\mathrm{kg} + 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}) \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 58.9\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The tension increases from bottom to top, from $49.1\,\mathrm{N}$ at the lower end to $68.7\,\mathrm{N}$ at the ceiling.
