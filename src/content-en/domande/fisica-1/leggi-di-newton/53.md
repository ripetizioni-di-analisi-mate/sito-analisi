An elevator accelerates upward and a passenger stands still relative to its cabin. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the mass $m = 60\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the normal force magnitude $N = 720\,\mathrm{N}$, what is the acceleration magnitude $a$?

- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The normal force points up and weight points down. The vertical equation is $N-mg=ma$. In the stated units, the relation is $N = m \cdot \left(a + 10\right)$. Solving for the unknown gives $a = \dfrac{N}{m} - 10$. Substituting the given values yields $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
