A car of mass $1000\,\mathrm{kg}$ passes over the top of a hump, which in that stretch is an arc of a vertical circle of radius $40\,\mathrm{m}$, at a speed of $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the normal force of the road on the car at that point?

- $9.81 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.54 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.19 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.63 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

At the top of the hump the center of the circle lies below the car, so the component of the net force toward the center points downward. The weight points toward the center and the normal force in the opposite direction, therefore
$$
m g - N = m\,\frac{v^2}{r} .
$$
With $\dfrac{v^2}{r} = \dfrac{225}{40}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 5.63\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ one obtains
$$
N = m\left(g - \frac{v^2}{r}\right) = 1000\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 4.19\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 4.19 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The normal force is smaller than the weight, and it would vanish for $v = \sqrt{g r} = 19.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the speed above which the car leaves the road.
