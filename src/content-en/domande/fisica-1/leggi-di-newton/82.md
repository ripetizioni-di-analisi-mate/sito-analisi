An accelerometer consists of a body of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ sliding without friction in a horizontal guide, attached to a spring of constant $k = 40\,\mathrm{N/m}$ fixed to the front of the guide. The device is mounted in a car accelerating on a straight road, and the body is at rest relative to the guide with the spring stretched by $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the acceleration of the car?

- $0.12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $300\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The body is at rest relative to the guide, so it has the same acceleration $a$ as the car. The guide is smooth, therefore the only horizontal force on the body is the elastic force, of magnitude $k x$, pointing forward.

By the second law $k x = m a$, so
$$
a = \frac{k x}{m} = \frac{40\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot 0.015\,\mathrm{m}}{0.20\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The extension is proportional to the acceleration, and for this reason the scale of the device can be calibrated directly in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
