A car takes a flat circular bend. Static friction is the only available horizontal force. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the radius $R = 40\,\mathrm{m}$ and the coefficient of static friction $\mu = \dfrac{2}{5}$. Change the radius to $160\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $v$ to its initial value?

- $1$
- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$

## Explanation

At the threshold of skidding, maximum static friction $\mu mg$ supplies the entire centripetal force $mv^2/R$. The relation $v = \sqrt{10} \cdot \sqrt{R} \cdot \sqrt{\mu}$ gives $4 \cdot \sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before the change and $8 \cdot \sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
