A sled must be moved at constant velocity on a snowy horizontal surface, with kinetic friction. It can be pulled with a rope inclined $30^\circ$ upward from the horizontal, or pushed with a pole inclined $30^\circ$ downward. In which case is the magnitude of the required force smaller?

- When the sled is pushed with the pole
- The magnitude is the same in both cases
- It depends on the mass of the sled
- When the sled is pulled with the rope

## Explanation

In both cases the horizontal component of the force, $F\cos 30^\circ$, must balance the kinetic friction $\mu_d N$, because the velocity is constant. The normal force, however, changes, because the vertical component $F\sin 30^\circ$ of the force has a different direction in the two cases.

With the rope the vertical component points upward, so $N = m g - F\sin 30^\circ$ is smaller than the weight. With the pole it points downward, so $N = m g + F\sin 30^\circ$ is larger than the weight. The friction is proportional to $N$, therefore the required force is smaller **when the sled is pulled with the rope**, for any value of the mass.
