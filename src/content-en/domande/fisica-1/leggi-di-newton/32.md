A block slides down a smooth inclined plane. If the inclination of the plane changes from $30^\circ$ to $60^\circ$, how does the acceleration of the block change?

- It doubles
- It is divided by $\sqrt{3}$
- It stays the same
- It is multiplied by $\sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

On a smooth inclined plane the acceleration is $a = g\sin\alpha$, independent of the mass. For $\alpha = 30^\circ$ we have $a = \dfrac{g}{2}$, while for $\alpha = 60^\circ$ we have $a = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,g$. The ratio of the two accelerations is therefore $\dfrac{\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 30^\circ} = \sqrt{3}$, so the acceleration is multiplied by $\sqrt{3}$ and goes from $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to $8.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The acceleration increases with the angle but is not proportional to the angle, because it depends on its sine.
