A body of mass $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, at rest on a smooth horizontal plane, is pushed from time $t = 0$ by a horizontal force of constant direction and magnitude $F(t) = 6.0\,t$, with $F$ in newtons and $t$ in seconds. What is the speed of the body at time $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The weight and the normal force balance, so the net force is $F(t)$. By the second law the acceleration is
$$
a(t) = \frac{F(t)}{m} = \frac{6.0\,t}{3.0} = 2.0\,t \quad (\mathrm{m/s^2}) .
$$
The acceleration is not constant, therefore the formulas of uniformly accelerated motion do not apply and the velocity is obtained by integrating $a(t)$. Since the body starts from rest,
$$
v(2.0\,\mathrm{s}) = \int_0^{2} 2.0\,t\,dt = \left[\,t^2\,\right]_0^{2} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ would be obtained by using the final acceleration $a(2) = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ over the whole interval.
