An ideal string passes over a fixed ideal pulley and connects two hanging bodies, of masses $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, which move with acceleration of magnitude $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Which statement about the tension in the string is true?

- The tension is greater at the end attached to the $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ body
- The tension equals the weight of the $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ body
- The tension has the same value at the two ends of the string
- The tension equals the weight of the $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ body

## Explanation

For a segment of string of mass $m_f$ the second law gives $T_2 - T_1 = m_f\,a$, and for an ideal string $m_f = 0$, so $T_1 = T_2$ even if the system accelerates. The ideal pulley changes the direction of the string and preserves the magnitude of the tension, therefore the tension has **the same value** at the two ends. For the Atwood machine its value is $T = \dfrac{2\,m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g = 14.7\,\mathrm{N}$, between the two weights $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$ and $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$.
