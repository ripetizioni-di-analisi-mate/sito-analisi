A block slides across a horizontal surface. A horizontal pull speeds it up. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the mass $m = 5\,\mathrm{kg}$, the acceleration magnitude $a = 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu = \dfrac{3}{10}$. Change the coefficient of kinetic friction to $\dfrac{1}{2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $F$ to its initial value?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{5}{3}$
- $\dfrac{7}{5}$
- $\dfrac{5}{7}$

## Explanation

Kinetic friction has magnitude $\mu mg$ and opposes the pull. The net force along the motion is $ma$. The relation $F = m \cdot \left(a + 10 \cdot \mu\right)$ gives $25\,\mathrm{N}$ before the change and $35\,\mathrm{N}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{7}{5}$.
