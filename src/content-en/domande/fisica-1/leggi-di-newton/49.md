A wedge-shaped scale slides without friction down a smooth incline of $30^\circ$, and its platform stays horizontal. A person of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on the platform and moves together with the scale. What does the scale read?

- $687\,\mathrm{N}$
- $515\,\mathrm{N}$
- $595\,\mathrm{N}$
- $172\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The person and the scale slide down the smooth incline together, so they have the acceleration $a = g\sin 30^\circ$ directed down the incline. The vertical component of this acceleration is $a\sin 30^\circ = g\sin^2 30^\circ$, pointing downward.

The scale reads the vertical normal force $N$ of the platform, because the platform is horizontal. Along the vertical the person is acted on by the weight and by $N$, therefore the second law gives $m g - N = m g \sin^2 30^\circ$. It follows that
$$
N = m g\,(1 - \sin^2 30^\circ) = m g \cos^2 30^\circ = 687\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \frac{3}{4} = 515\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The horizontal component of the acceleration is provided by the static friction of the platform, which does not enter the reading.
