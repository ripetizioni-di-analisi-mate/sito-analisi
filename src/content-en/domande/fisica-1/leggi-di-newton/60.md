A block of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pressed against a vertical wall by a horizontal force perpendicular to the wall. The coefficient of static friction between block and wall is $\mu_s = 0.50$. What is the minimum magnitude of the force for which the block stays at rest?

- $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$
- $39.2\,\mathrm{N}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{N}$
- $13.1\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Along the horizontal the applied force $F$ is balanced by the normal force of the wall, so $N = F$. Along the vertical the weight is balanced by the static friction of the wall, pointing upward, therefore $f_s = m g$.

The **law of static friction** requires $f_s \leq \mu_s N$, that is, $m g \leq \mu_s F$. It follows that
$$
F \geq \frac{m g}{\mu_s} = \frac{19.6\,\mathrm{N}}{0.50} = 39.2\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
In this situation the normal force is produced by the applied force and not by the weight, which is parallel to the wall.
