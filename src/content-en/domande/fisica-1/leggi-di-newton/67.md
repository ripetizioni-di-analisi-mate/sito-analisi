A car brakes on a horizontal road. In a first test the wheels keep rolling without slipping on the asphalt, in a second test the wheels lock and skid. For tires on asphalt $\mu_s = 0.80$ and $\mu_d = 0.60$. Which statement is true?

- With locked wheels the deceleration is larger, because the friction is kinetic
- Without locking the wheels the deceleration can be larger, because the friction is static
- The maximum deceleration is the same, because the normal force does not change
- Without locking the wheels no friction force acts, because there is no slipping

## Explanation

If the wheels roll without slipping, the point of the tire in contact with the asphalt is at rest relative to the asphalt, so the friction is static and can reach $\mu_s N = \mu_s m g$. If the wheels are locked, the tire skids and the friction is kinetic, with the value $\mu_d m g$.

Friction is the only horizontal force, therefore the maximum deceleration is $\mu_s g = 7.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ without locking the wheels and $\mu_d g = 5.9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ with locked wheels. Since $\mu_d < \mu_s$, braking **without locking the wheels** can give a larger deceleration, which is the principle of anti-lock braking systems.
