A block is pressed against a vertical wall by a horizontal force and is on the verge of slipping downward. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the coefficient of static friction $\mu = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the horizontal pressing force $F = 60\,\mathrm{N}$, what is the mass $m$?

- $3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The normal force balances the horizontal push. At impending slip, upward static friction equals $\mu F$ and balances the weight. In the stated units, the relation is $F = \dfrac{10 \cdot m}{\mu}$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{F \cdot \mu}{10}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{kg}$.
