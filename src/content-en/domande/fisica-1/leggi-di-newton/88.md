A body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves on a circle of radius $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. At a certain instant its speed is $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the speed is increasing at a rate of $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the magnitude of the net force on the body at that instant?

- $12\,\mathrm{N}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $14.4\,\mathrm{N}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The circular motion is not uniform, so the acceleration has two mutually perpendicular components. The centripetal component is $a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{r} = \dfrac{9.0}{1.5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, while the tangential component is the rate of change of the speed, $a_t = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The two components are perpendicular, therefore the magnitude of the acceleration is $a = \sqrt{6.0^2 + 4.0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 7.21\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. By the second law the net force has magnitude
$$
R = m\,a = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 7.21\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 14.4\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $12\,\mathrm{N}$ is only the centripetal component of the net force.
