On the orbiting space station the mass of the astronauts is measured with a device that applies a known force to the astronaut, seated on a chair of negligible mass, and measures the acceleration. With a force of $40\,\mathrm{N}$ the measured acceleration is $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the mass of the astronaut?

- $20\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $4.1\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $80\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $160\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

In orbit a scale does not measure the weight, but the inertial mass can still be measured with **Newton's second law**, because for a given force the acceleration is inversely proportional to the mass.

The force of the device is the net force on the astronaut in the frame of the station, so $F = m a$. Therefore
$$
m = \frac{F}{a} = \frac{40\,\mathrm{N}}{0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 80\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $4.1\,\mathrm{kg}$ would be obtained by dividing the force by $g$, which plays no role in this measurement.
