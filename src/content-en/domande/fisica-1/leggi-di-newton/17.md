An astronaut weighs $589\,\mathrm{N}$ on Earth, where $g = 9{,}81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How much does the astronaut weigh on the Moon, where $g = 1{,}62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$?

- $589\,\mathrm{N}$
- $97{,}3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $954\,\mathrm{N}$
- $364\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The mass does not depend on the location, while the weight $P = m g$ depends on the local value of $g$. For the same mass the weight is therefore proportional to $g$, so on the Moon
$$
P_L = P_T\,\frac{g_L}{g_T} = 589\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \frac{1{,}62}{9{,}81} = 97{,}3\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The mass of the astronaut is $\dfrac{589\,\mathrm{N}}{9{,}81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 60{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ both on Earth and on the Moon.
