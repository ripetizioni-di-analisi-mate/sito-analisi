An elevator of mass $800\,\mathrm{kg}$ is supported by a cable and starts moving upward with acceleration $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the tension in the cable?

- $8.81 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6.89 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $7.85 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9.60 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The weight, downward, and the tension in the cable, upward, act on the elevator. With the vertical axis pointing upward the second law gives $T - m g = m a$, so
$$
T = m\,(g + a) = 800\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 11.01\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 8.81 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The tension exceeds the weight, $7.85 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$, because the net force has the sense of the acceleration, that is, upward.
