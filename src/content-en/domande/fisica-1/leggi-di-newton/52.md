An elevator accelerates upward and a passenger stands still relative to its cabin. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the acceleration magnitude $a = 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the normal force magnitude $N = 720\,\mathrm{N}$, what is the mass $m$?

- $90\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $120\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $72\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $60\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The normal force points up and weight points down. The vertical equation is $N-mg=ma$. In the stated units, the relation is $N = m \cdot \left(a + 10\right)$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{N}{a + 10}$. Substituting the given values yields $60\,\mathrm{kg}$.
