A passenger stands in a bus traveling on a straight road. The bus brakes suddenly and the passenger lurches forward. Which description, given in the reference frame of the ground, is correct?

- The bus exerts on the passenger a force pointing forward
- During braking the weight of the passenger tilts forward
- The normal force of the floor becomes larger than the weight and pushes the passenger forward
- The passenger tends to keep his velocity, while the bus slows down

## Explanation

The ground is an inertial frame, so the laws of dynamics hold in it without fictitious forces. During braking the friction of the floor slows down the passenger's feet, while no horizontal force acts on the upper part of the body.

By **Newton's first law** the upper part of the body therefore tends to keep its velocity, while the bus and the feet slow down, and the passenger lurches forward relative to the bus. The forward push felt by the passenger is the fictitious force $-m\vec{A}$, which appears only in the frame of the bus, and that frame is not inertial.
