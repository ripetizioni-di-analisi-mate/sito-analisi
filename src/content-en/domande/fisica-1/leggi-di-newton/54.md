An elevator accelerates upward and a passenger stands still relative to its cabin. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the mass $m = 60\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the acceleration magnitude $a = 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Change the acceleration magnitude to $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $N$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{7}{6}$
- $\dfrac{6}{7}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The normal force points up and weight points down. The vertical equation is $N-mg=ma$. The relation $N = m \cdot \left(a + 10\right)$ gives $720\,\mathrm{N}$ before the change and $840\,\mathrm{N}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{7}{6}$.
