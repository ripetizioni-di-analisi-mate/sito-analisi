A block of mass $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest on a horizontal table, with coefficients of friction $\mu_s = 0.40$ and $\mu_d = 0.30$. An ideal string, passing over an ideal pulley fixed to the edge of the table, connects it to a hanging bucket into which sand is slowly poured. What is the mass of the bucket, sand included, above which the block starts moving?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

As long as the block is at rest the bucket is at rest too, so the tension in the string balances the weight of the bucket, $T = m_2 g$. On the block static friction balances the tension, therefore $f_s = m_2 g$, while along the vertical $N = m_1 g$.

The **law of static friction** requires $f_s \leq \mu_s N$, that is, $m_2 g \leq \mu_s m_1 g$. The block therefore remains at rest as long as
$$
m_2 \leq \mu_s\,m_1 = 0.40 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{kg} = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} ,
$$
and starts moving when the mass of the bucket exceeds this value. The value $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ would be obtained with the coefficient of kinetic friction, which comes into play only once the block is already moving.
