A stone, tied to a string, moves in uniform circular motion on a smooth horizontal table around the other end of the string, fixed to a pin. At a certain instant the string breaks. What path does the stone follow right afterward?

- A segment tangent to the circle at the breaking point
- A segment pointing radially outward
- The same circle, with decreasing speed
- A spiral moving away from the pin

## Explanation

After the string breaks, the stone is acted on only by its weight and by the normal force of the table, which balance. The net force is zero, so by **Newton's first law** the stone moves in uniform straight-line motion with the velocity it had at the instant of breaking.

In circular motion the velocity is tangent to the path, therefore the stone moves along **a segment tangent to the circle** at the breaking point. A radial motion outward would require a force pointing outward, and no such force acts in the frame of the table.
