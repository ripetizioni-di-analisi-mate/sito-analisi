An elevator rises with a constant acceleration of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ pointing upward. A passenger drops a ball, and air resistance is negligible. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the ball measured in the reference frame of the elevator?

- $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $11.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

In the frame of the elevator, which translates with acceleration $\vec{A}$ pointing upward, the ball is acted on by its weight $m\vec{g}$ and by the **fictitious force** $-m\vec{A}$, pointing downward. Both forces point downward, so the second law in the frame of the elevator gives
$$
m\,a' = m g + m A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a' = g + A = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} + 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 11.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The same result is reached in the frame of the building, where the ball falls with acceleration $g$ downward while the floor moves toward it with acceleration $A$ upward.
