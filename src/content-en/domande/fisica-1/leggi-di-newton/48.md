A block slides across a horizontal surface. A horizontal pull accelerates it in the direction of motion. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the mass $m = 5\,\mathrm{kg}$, the coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu = \dfrac{3}{10}$ and the magnitude of the pull $F = 25\,\mathrm{N}$, what is the acceleration magnitude $a$?

- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Kinetic friction has magnitude $\mu mg$ and opposes the pull. The net force along the motion is $ma$. In the stated units, the relation is $F = m \cdot \left(a + 10 \cdot \mu\right)$. Solving for the unknown gives $a = \dfrac{F}{m} - 10 \cdot \mu$. Substituting the given values yields $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
