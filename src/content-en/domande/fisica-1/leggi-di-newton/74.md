A block of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, on a smooth horizontal plane, is attached to a horizontal ideal spring of constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, fixed at its other end. The block is displaced so that the spring is stretched by $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and is then released. What is the magnitude of its acceleration at the instant it is released?

- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The weight and the normal force of the plane balance, so the net force is the elastic force. By **Hooke's law** the force has magnitude $k x$, with the extension expressed in meters, $x = 0.050\,\mathrm{m}$.

By the second law
$$
a = \frac{k x}{m} = \frac{200\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{m}}{0.50\,\mathrm{kg}} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s^2} ,
$$
pointing toward the rest position of the spring. The value $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ would be obtained by leaving the extension in centimeters.
