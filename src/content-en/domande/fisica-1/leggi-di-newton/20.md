A truck of mass $6000\,\mathrm{kg}$ collides with a car of mass $1500\,\mathrm{kg}$. At an instant during the collision the car has an acceleration of magnitude $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Neglecting the other horizontal forces, what is the magnitude of the acceleration of the truck at that instant?

- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

At that instant the force that the truck exerts on the car has magnitude $F = m_A a_A = 1500\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3{,}0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$. By **Newton's third law** the car exerts on the truck a force of the same magnitude and opposite direction. Therefore the acceleration of the truck has magnitude $a_C = \dfrac{F}{m_C} = \dfrac{3{,}0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}}{6000\,\mathrm{kg}} = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The two forces have the same magnitude, while the accelerations are inversely proportional to the masses.
