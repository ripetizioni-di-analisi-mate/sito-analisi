A block of mass $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides to the right on a horizontal floor, with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_d = 0.20$. A person pushes it with a horizontal force of $10\,\mathrm{N}$ directed to the left. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the block while it moves to the right?

- $1.37\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.33\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5.30\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Along the vertical $N = m g = 29.4\,\mathrm{N}$, so kinetic friction has magnitude $f_d = \mu_d N = 0.20 \cdot 29.4\,\mathrm{N} = 5.89\,\mathrm{N}$. The block moves to the right, therefore kinetic friction is directed to the left, opposite to the velocity.

The applied force is also directed to the left, so the net force is directed to the left and has magnitude $10\,\mathrm{N} + 5.89\,\mathrm{N} = 15.9\,\mathrm{N}$. By the second law
$$
a = \frac{15.9\,\mathrm{N}}{3.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 5.30\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The direction of kinetic friction depends on the velocity of the block relative to the floor and not on the direction of the applied force, therefore the value $1.37\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, obtained by subtracting friction from the push, corresponds to friction in the wrong direction.
