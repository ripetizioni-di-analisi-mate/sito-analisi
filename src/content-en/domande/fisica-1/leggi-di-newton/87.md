A body of mass $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$, on a smooth horizontal table, is tied to an ideal string and moves on a circle of radius $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ around the other end of the string, fixed to a pin, completing one revolution every $0.50\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the tension of the string?

- $6.03\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.92\,\mathrm{N}$
- $152\,\mathrm{N}$
- $75.8\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The weight and the normal force of the table balance, so the tension of the string is the only horizontal force and provides the **centripetal force**, $T = m\,\omega^2 r$. The body completes one revolution in the period $T_p = 0.50\,\mathrm{s}$, therefore the angular velocity is $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T_p} = 12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

It follows that
$$
T = m\,\omega^2 r = 1.2\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left(12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s}\right)^2 \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{m} = 75.8\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $6.03\,\mathrm{N}$ would be obtained by using $\omega$ instead of $\omega^2$.
