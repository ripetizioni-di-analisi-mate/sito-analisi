A block is launched up an incline of $20^\circ$, with friction coefficients $\mu_s = 0.50$ and $\mu_d = 0.40$, and stops after some distance. What happens after the block has stopped?

- It slides back with acceleration $g\,(\sin 20^\circ - \mu_d \cos 20^\circ)$
- It slides back with acceleration $g \sin 20^\circ$
- It stays at rest only if its mass is large enough
- It stays at rest at the point where it stopped

## Explanation

After stopping the block is at rest, so it stays at rest if static friction can balance the component of the weight along the incline. By the **angle of repose** a body at rest on an incline of angle $\alpha$ stays at rest if and only if $\tan\alpha \leq \mu_s$.

In this case $\tan 20^\circ = 0.364$ and $\mu_s = 0.50$, therefore the condition is satisfied and the block **stays at rest**. The condition does not contain the mass, so the result holds for any mass. The acceleration $g\,(\sin 20^\circ - \mu_d \cos 20^\circ)$ would make sense only with $\tan 20^\circ > \mu_s$, and here it would even be negative.
