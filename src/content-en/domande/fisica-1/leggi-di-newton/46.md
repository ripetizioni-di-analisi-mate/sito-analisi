A person of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands still on a scale at the equator. The Earth rotates with angular velocity $\omega = 7.27 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and the Earth's radius is $R = 6.37 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m}$. By how much is the reading of the scale smaller than the value $m g$ that would be read on a non-rotating Earth with the same value of $g$?

- $4.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.24\,\mathrm{N}$
- $24\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

In the frame attached to the Earth the person is at rest, but the frame rotates, so the **centrifugal force** $m\,\omega^2 R$, pointing outward, also acts on the person. At the equator the centrifugal force is vertical and points upward, therefore equilibrium along the vertical gives $N + m\,\omega^2 R - m g = 0$.

The reading of the scale is thus smaller than $m g$ by
$$
m\,\omega^2 R = 70\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left(7.27 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad/s}\right)^2 \cdot 6.37 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.4\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The correction is about $0.34\%$ of the weight, therefore the ground can be treated as an inertial frame in most problems.
