The graph shows the magnitude $F$ of the force needed to stretch a spring as a function of the extension $x$, with $x$ in centimeters and $F$ in newtons.

::grafico{f="2.5*x" punti="10,25,pieno" segmenti="10,0,10,25;0,25,10,25" etichette="1,26.3,25" nomi="x,F" xmin=0 xmax=13 ymin=0 ymax=32 titolo="Force $F$ in newtons as a function of the extension $x$ in centimeters"}

What is the spring constant of the spring?

- $250\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $25\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{N/m}$

## Explanation

The graph is a straight line through the origin, so the force is proportional to the extension, as **Hooke's law** states, and the spring constant is the slope of the line.

From the marked point a force of $25\,\mathrm{N}$ produces an extension of $10\,\mathrm{cm} = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore
$$
k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{25\,\mathrm{N}}{0.10\,\mathrm{m}} = 250\,\mathrm{N/m} .
$$
The value $2.5$ is the slope read in newtons per centimeter, that is, $2.5\,\mathrm{N/cm}$, which corresponds to $250\,\mathrm{N/m}$.
