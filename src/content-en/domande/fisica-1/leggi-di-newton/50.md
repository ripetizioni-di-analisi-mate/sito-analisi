A smooth inclined plane at $30^\circ$ is fixed to the floor of an elevator that moves up with a constant acceleration of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ directed upward. A block slides down the plane. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the block relative to the plane?

- $5.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

In the frame of the elevator, which translates with acceleration $\vec{A}$ directed upward, the forces on the block are the weight, the normal force and the **fictitious force** $-m\vec{A}$, directed downward. The weight and the fictitious force are both vertical and directed downward, so their sum has magnitude $m\,(g + A)$, like the weight of a body in a place where the gravitational acceleration were $g + A$.

The plane is smooth, therefore along the plane only the component $m\,(g + A)\sin 30^\circ$ of this force acts. Newton's second law in the frame of the elevator then gives
$$
a' = (g + A)\sin 30^\circ = 11.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.500 = 5.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The value $4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ would be obtained by writing the second law in the frame of the elevator without the fictitious force.
