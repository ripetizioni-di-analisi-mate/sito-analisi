Which of the following reference frames, attached to the given body, can be regarded as inertial to the same approximation as the ground?

- A train traveling at constant velocity on a straight track
- A car braking on a straight road
- A merry-go-round rotating with constant angular velocity
- An elevator starting to move upward

## Explanation

A frame that translates with constant velocity relative to an inertial frame is itself inertial, because by the Galilean transformations the accelerations measured in the two frames coincide. The train traveling at constant velocity on a straight track translates with constant velocity relative to the ground, so it is an **inertial frame**.

The braking car and the starting elevator have an acceleration relative to the ground, therefore fictitious forces appear in them. The merry-go-round rotates, so even with constant angular velocity each of its points has a centripetal acceleration and the frame is not inertial.
