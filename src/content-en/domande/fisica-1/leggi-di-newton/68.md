Two blocks, of masses $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, rest in contact on a smooth inclined plane at $30^\circ$, with the $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block higher up. The blocks are released from rest. What is the force that each block exerts on the other during the descent?

- $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$
- $14.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $24.5\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

On a **smooth inclined plane** every body subject only to its weight and the normal force slides down with acceleration $g\sin 30^\circ$, independent of the mass. Treated as a single body, the two blocks therefore slide down with $a = g\sin 30^\circ$.

Along the plane the upper block is acted on by the component of the weight $m_1 g\sin 30^\circ$ and by the force $F$ exerted by the lower block, directed upward. Newton's second law for the upper block gives $m_1 g\sin 30^\circ - F = m_1 g\sin 30^\circ$, therefore $F = 0$.

Each block, left alone, would have the same acceleration, so the two blocks slide down side by side without pushing each other. On a rough plane with different coefficients of friction for the two blocks the contact force would instead be different from zero.
