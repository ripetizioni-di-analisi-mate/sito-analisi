Two blocks of masses $3{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $5{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ are in contact on a frictionless horizontal surface. A horizontal force of $24\,\mathrm{N}$ pushes the $3{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block, which in turn pushes the $5{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block. What is the force that the first block exerts on the second?

- $9{,}0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $24\,\mathrm{N}$
- $15\,\mathrm{N}$
- $12\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The two blocks move together, so they can be treated as a single body of mass $8{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$, for which the contact forces between the blocks are internal. The common acceleration is $a = \dfrac{24\,\mathrm{N}}{8{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 3{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The $5{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block is accelerated only by the push of the first block, therefore by the second law this push is $5{,}0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 15\,\mathrm{N}$.
