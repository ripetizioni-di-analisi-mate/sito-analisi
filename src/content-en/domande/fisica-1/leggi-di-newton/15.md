A hockey puck slides on a horizontal sheet of ice with a speed of $3{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Friction from the ice and air resistance are negligible. Which statement is true?

- The net force on the puck is zero and its velocity stays constant
- A force in the direction of motion acts on the puck and maintains its velocity
- The puck slows down until it stops even without friction
- The normal force from the ice exceeds the weight of the puck

## Explanation

Only the weight and the normal force from the ice act on the puck, and both are vertical. The puck does not move along the vertical, so the two forces have the same magnitude and the net force is zero. By **Newton's first law** the puck therefore moves in uniform rectilinear motion, with a constant speed of $3{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A force would be needed to change the velocity, while for the velocity to stay constant it is enough that the net force be zero.
