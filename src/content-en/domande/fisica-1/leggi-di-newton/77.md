Two blocks $A$ and $B$, of masses $m_A = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_B = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, rest on a smooth horizontal plane and are connected by an ideal spring of constant $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$. A horizontal force of $10\,\mathrm{N}$ pulls block $B$ away from $A$, and the two blocks move with the same acceleration. What is the extension of the spring?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The two blocks have the same acceleration, so they can be treated as a single body of mass $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, for which the elastic force is internal. The common acceleration is therefore $a = \dfrac{10\,\mathrm{N}}{5.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Block $A$ is accelerated only by the force of the spring, so by the second law this force equals $m_A a = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 4.0\,\mathrm{N}$. By Hooke's law
$$
x = \frac{4.0\,\mathrm{N}}{100\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 0.040\,\mathrm{m} = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The spring is stretched by a force smaller than the applied force, because part of the latter accelerates block $B$.
