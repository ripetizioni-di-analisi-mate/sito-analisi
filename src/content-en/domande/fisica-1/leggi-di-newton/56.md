A worker of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ sits on a chair of mass $10\,\mathrm{kg}$. An ideal rope is tied to the chair, runs vertically up to an ideal pulley fixed above, and comes vertically down to the worker's hands. With what force must the worker pull the rope downward to rise at constant velocity?

- $687\,\mathrm{N}$
- $294\,\mathrm{N}$
- $589\,\mathrm{N}$
- $343\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Take as the system the worker together with the chair, of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$. The rope touches the system at two points, the chair and the worker's hands, and at both it pulls upward with the same tension $T$, because rope and pulley are ideal.

The contact forces between the worker and the chair are internal to the system. The system rises at constant velocity, so the net force is zero and
$$
2T = (60\,\mathrm{kg} + 10\,\mathrm{kg})\,g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = \frac{687\,\mathrm{N}}{2} = 343\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
By the third law the worker pulls the rope downward with a force equal to the tension, that is, $343\,\mathrm{N}$.
