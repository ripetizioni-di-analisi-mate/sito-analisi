A crane lifts a load of mass $500\,\mathrm{kg}$ with a vertical cable, at a constant velocity of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ upward. What is the tension of the cable?

- $5.91 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.91 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.91 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The load rises at constant velocity, so its acceleration is zero and by the first law the net force is zero. The load is acted on by its weight, pointing downward, and by the tension of the cable, pointing upward, therefore
$$
T = m g = 500\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 4.91 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value of the velocity does not enter the calculation. The tension exceeds the weight only while the load accelerates upward, for instance during the start.
