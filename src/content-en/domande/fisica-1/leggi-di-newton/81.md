A smooth horizontal platform rotates about a vertical axis with constant angular velocity $\omega = 8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. A block of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to an ideal spring of constant $k = 50\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and rest length $20\,\mathrm{cm}$, whose other end is fixed on the axis. The block rotates together with the platform. How long is the spring?

- $33\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $13\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $56\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The block moves on a circle of radius $L$, equal to the length of the spring, and the only horizontal force is the elastic force, pointing toward the axis. By the **centripetal force** relation the elastic force equals $m\,\omega^2 L$, so
$$
k\,(L - L_0) = m\,\omega^2 L .
$$
The radius appears on both sides, because the required centripetal force grows with the extension. Solving for $L$ gives
$$
L = \frac{k L_0}{k - m\,\omega^2} = \frac{50 \cdot 0.20}{50 - 0.50 \cdot 64}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{10}{18}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.56\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $33\,\mathrm{cm}$ would be obtained by computing the centripetal force with the radius $L_0$ instead of $L$.
