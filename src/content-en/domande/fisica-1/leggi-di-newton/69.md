A block is pressed against a vertical wall by a horizontal force and is on the verge of slipping downward. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the mass $m = 3\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the coefficient of static friction $\mu = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Change the coefficient of static friction to $\dfrac{1}{4}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $F$ to its initial value?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $1$

## Explanation

The normal force balances the horizontal push. At impending slip, upward static friction equals $\mu F$ and balances the weight. The relation $F = \dfrac{10 \cdot m}{\mu}$ gives $60\,\mathrm{N}$ before the change and $120\,\mathrm{N}$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
