A block of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on top of a block of mass $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, which in turn rests on a smooth horizontal floor. The coefficient of static friction between the two blocks is $\mu_s = 0.40$. A horizontal force pulls the lower block. What is the maximum magnitude of the force for which the two blocks move together, without the upper block slipping?

- $15.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.92\,\mathrm{N}$
- $11.8\,\mathrm{N}$
- $39.2\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The upper block is accelerated only by the static friction of the lower block, because the floor does not touch it. Along the vertical $N = m_1 g$, so static friction can reach the value $\mu_s m_1 g$, and the maximum acceleration of the upper block is
$$
a_{\max} = \frac{\mu_s\,m_1 g}{m_1} = \mu_s\,g = 3.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
As long as the blocks move together they can be treated as a single body of mass $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, for which the friction between the blocks is an internal force. Therefore the maximum force is
$$
F_{\max} = (m_1 + m_2)\,\mu_s\,g = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 15.7\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $3.92\,\mathrm{N}$ is the maximum friction on the upper block, which accelerates only that block.
