A block slides across a horizontal surface. A horizontal pull accelerates it in the direction of motion. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the acceleration magnitude $a = 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, the coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu = \dfrac{3}{10}$ and the magnitude of the pull $F = 25\,\mathrm{N}$, what is the mass $m$?

- $10\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{25}{2}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $5\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{25}{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Kinetic friction has magnitude $\mu mg$ and opposes the pull. The net force along the motion is $ma$. In the stated units, the relation is $F = m \cdot \left(a + 10 \cdot \mu\right)$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{F}{a + 10 \cdot \mu}$. Substituting the given values yields $5\,\mathrm{kg}$.
