An elevator accelerates upward and a passenger stands still relative to its cabin. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the mass $m = 60\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the acceleration magnitude $a = 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Change the mass to $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the acceleration magnitude to $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $N$ minus its initial value?

- $50\,\mathrm{N}$
- $150\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-50\,\mathrm{N}$
- $100\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The normal force points up and weight points down. The vertical equation is $N-mg=ma$. The relation $N = m \cdot \left(a + 10\right)$ gives $720\,\mathrm{N}$ before the change and $770\,\mathrm{N}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $50\,\mathrm{N}$.
