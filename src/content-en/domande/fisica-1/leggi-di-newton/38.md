Two ideal springs with spring constants $k_1 = 300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $k_2 = 600\,\mathrm{N/m}$ are connected in series and support a body of mass $4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the total extension at equilibrium?

- $4.36\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $13.1\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $19.6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6.54\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

For **springs in series** the equivalent constant satisfies
$$
\frac{1}{k_{\text{ser}}} = \frac{1}{300\,\mathrm{N/m}} + \frac{1}{600\,\mathrm{N/m}} = \frac{3}{600\,\mathrm{N/m}} ,
$$
so $k_{\text{ser}} = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$. At equilibrium $k_{\text{ser}}\,x = m g$, therefore $x = \dfrac{39.2\,\mathrm{N}}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 0.196\,\mathrm{m} = 19.6\,\mathrm{cm}$. The total extension is greater than the one each spring would have alone, because both springs are stretched by the same force and their extensions add up.
