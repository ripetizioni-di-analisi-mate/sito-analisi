A curve of radius $50\,\mathrm{m}$ has the roadway banked at $20^\circ$ toward the center, and the coefficient of static friction between tires and asphalt is $\mu_s = 0.30$. What is the maximum speed at which a car can take the curve without skidding outward?

- $13.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $19.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

At the maximum speed the car tends to skid outward, so static friction has its maximum value $\mu_s N$ and points along the roadway inward and downward. Along the vertical the net force is zero, along the horizontal it provides the centripetal force, so
$$
\begin{cases}
N\cos\alpha - \mu_s N \sin\alpha = m g \\[4pt]
N\sin\alpha + \mu_s N \cos\alpha = m\,\dfrac{v^2}{r}
\end{cases}
$$
Dividing the second equation by the first eliminates the normal force, therefore
$$
v_{\max}^2 = g r\,\frac{\sin\alpha + \mu_s \cos\alpha}{\cos\alpha - \mu_s \sin\alpha} = 490.5 \cdot \frac{0.624}{0.837}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = 366\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} ,
$$
so $v_{\max} = 19.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Without friction the curve could be taken only at the speed $\sqrt{g r \tan\alpha} = 13.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
