An ideal spring has a rest length of $12.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. When it is stretched by a force of $6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ its length is $15.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. How long is it when it is stretched by a force of $10\,\mathrm{N}$?

- $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $25.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $20.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $17.0\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

By **Hooke's law** the magnitude of the force is proportional to the extension, that is, to the difference between the length of the spring and its rest length. With the force of $6.0\,\mathrm{N}$ the extension is $15.0\,\mathrm{cm} - 12.0\,\mathrm{cm} = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, so
$$
k = \frac{6.0\,\mathrm{N}}{0.030\,\mathrm{m}} = 200\,\mathrm{N/m} .
$$
With the force of $10\,\mathrm{N}$ the extension is $\dfrac{10\,\mathrm{N}}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 0.050\,\mathrm{m} = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, therefore the spring is $12.0\,\mathrm{cm} + 5.0\,\mathrm{cm} = 17.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ long.

The value $25.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ would be obtained by assuming the length, rather than the extension, to be proportional to the force.
