A body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves on a smooth horizontal plane with a velocity of $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ directed east. A constant horizontal force acts on the body for $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and at the end the velocity of the body is $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ directed north. What is the magnitude of the force?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $7.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $10\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The weight and the normal force balance each other, so the net force is the horizontal force. The force is constant, therefore the acceleration is constant and equals $\vec{a} = \dfrac{\Delta\vec{v}}{\Delta t}$, where $\Delta\vec{v}$ is the difference between the velocity vectors.

With the $x$ axis pointing east and the $y$ axis pointing north, the change in velocity has components $\Delta v_x = -3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\Delta v_y = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so its magnitude is
$$
|\Delta\vec{v}| = \sqrt{3.0^2 + 4.0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
Therefore the acceleration has magnitude $\dfrac{5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2.0\,\mathrm{s}} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and, by **Newton's second law**, the force has magnitude $F = m a = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 5.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

The value $1.0\,\mathrm{N}$ would be obtained by using the change in the magnitude of the velocity, $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, instead of the magnitude of the change in the velocity vector.
