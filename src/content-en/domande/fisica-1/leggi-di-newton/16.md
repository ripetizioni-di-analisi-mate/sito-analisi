A car of mass $1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ travels at $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$ on a straight road and brakes to a stop in $4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$, with constant deceleration. What is the magnitude of the net force on the car during braking?

- $2{,}2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3{,}0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1{,}2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6{,}0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The initial speed in SI units is $v_0 = \dfrac{72}{3{,}6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The deceleration is constant, so the magnitude of the acceleration is $a = \dfrac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}} = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. By the second law the net force has magnitude $R = m a = 1200\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 6{,}0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$ and is directed opposite to the velocity.
