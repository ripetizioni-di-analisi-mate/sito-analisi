A disk of mass $2{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$, resting on a frictionless horizontal surface, is acted on by two horizontal forces perpendicular to each other, of magnitudes $6{,}0\,\mathrm{N}$ and $8{,}0\,\mathrm{N}$. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the disk?

- $5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The weight and the normal force from the surface balance each other, so the net force is the vector sum of the two horizontal forces. By the **principle of superposition** the two perpendicular forces have a net force of magnitude $R = \sqrt{6{,}0^2 + 8{,}0^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 10\,\mathrm{N}$. By the second law the acceleration points in the same direction as the net force and has magnitude $a = \dfrac{R}{m} = \dfrac{10\,\mathrm{N}}{2{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 5{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
