A car travels at constant speed around a circular curve on a level road, without skidding. If the speed doubles and the radius of the curve stays the same, how does the required static friction force change?

- It doubles
- It stays the same
- It is halved
- It becomes four times greater

## Explanation

Static friction is the only horizontal force on the car and provides the centripetal force, so $f_s = m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$. The force is proportional to the square of the speed, therefore if $v$ doubles the required force becomes $2^2 = 4$ times greater. If this value exceeds the maximum static friction $\mu_s m g$, the car skids.
