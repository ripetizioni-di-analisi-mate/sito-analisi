A puck slides on a smooth horizontal sheet of ice with a velocity of $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ pointing north. At a certain instant a constant horizontal force pointing east starts to act on the puck. What path does the puck follow from that instant on?

- An arc of a parabola
- A segment pointing east
- A segment pointing northeast
- An arc of a circle

## Explanation

The weight and the normal force of the ice balance, so the net force is the force pointing east. By the second law the acceleration is constant and points east.

Along the north direction the net force is zero, therefore the north component of the velocity remains $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the northward displacement grows in proportion to $t$. Along the east direction the motion is uniformly accelerated from rest, so the eastward displacement grows in proportion to $t^2$. One coordinate proportional to $t$ and the other proportional to $t^2$ describe **an arc of a parabola**, as in projectile motion.

An arc of a circle would require a force always perpendicular to the velocity, that is, a force of varying direction.
