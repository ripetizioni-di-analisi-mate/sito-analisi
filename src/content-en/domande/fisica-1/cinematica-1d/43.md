A boat with its engine off starts with velocity $6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and is slowed down with acceleration $a = -k\,v$, with $k = 0{,}20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. What total distance does it travel, considering the motion for all subsequent times?

- $1{,}2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $90\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In motion with acceleration proportional to velocity the position is $x(t) = x_0 + \dfrac{v_0}{k}\left(1 - e^{-kt}\right)$. As $t \to +\infty$ the term $e^{-kt}$ tends to $0$, so the position tends to $x_0 + \dfrac{v_0}{k}$. The total distance traveled is therefore $\dfrac{v_0}{k} = \dfrac{6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0{,}20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 30\,\mathrm{m}$, even though the velocity does not vanish at any instant.
