A body is slowed down with acceleration $a = -k\,v$, with $k$ a positive constant. If the initial velocity doubles and $k$ stays the same, how does the time needed for the velocity to halve change?

- It doubles
- It halves
- It stays the same
- It becomes four times larger

## Explanation

The velocity is $v(t) = v_0\,e^{-kt}$, so it halves when $e^{-kt} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, at the instant $t = \dfrac{\ln 2}{k}$. This time depends only on $k$ and not on $v_0$, therefore it stays the same. The total distance traveled, $\dfrac{v_0}{k}$, doubles.
