Two cars brake to a stop with the same constant deceleration. The first travels at $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$, the second at $100\,\mathrm{km/h}$. What is the ratio of the braking distance of the second to that of the first?

- $2$
- $\sqrt{2}$
- $8$
- $4$

## Explanation

By the relation between velocity and displacement, with zero final velocity, the braking distance is $d = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,|a|}$. For the same deceleration the braking distance is therefore proportional to the square of the initial velocity. Since the velocity of the second car is twice as large, its braking distance is $2^2 = 4$ times that of the first.
