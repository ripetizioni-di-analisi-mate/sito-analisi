A car travels at constant speed during the reaction time and then brakes at constant acceleration until it stops. Given the reaction time $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$, the initial speed $v = 16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the total stopping distance $d = 48\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the magnitude of the braking acceleration $a$?

- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

During the reaction interval the car covers $vt$. The braking phase obeys $0=v^2-2a\Delta x$, so the two contributions are added. In the stated units, the relation is $d = t \cdot v + \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot a}$. Solving for the unknown gives $a = \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot \left(d - t \cdot v\right)}$. Substituting the given values yields $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
