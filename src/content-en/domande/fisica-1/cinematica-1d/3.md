A cyclist passes a point $O$ at the constant speed of $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A second cyclist starts from $O$ $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ later, in the same direction, at the constant speed of $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long after the first cyclist passes $O$ does the second catch up with him?

- $25\,\mathrm{s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $30\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.2\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Time is measured from the passage of the first cyclist through $O$, with the origin at $O$ and the positive orientation in the direction of motion. The equations of motion are $x_1(t) = 8\,t$ and, for $t \geq 5\,\mathrm{s}$, $x_2(t) = 10\,(t - 5)$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. The second cyclist catches up with the first when $x_1(t) = x_2(t)$, that is, $8\,t = 10\,t - 50$, so at the instant $t = 25\,\mathrm{s}$. At that instant both are $200\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$.
