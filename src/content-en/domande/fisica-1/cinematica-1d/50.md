A car travels on a straight road, always in the same direction, for half of the total time at $40\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and for the other half at $80\,\mathrm{km/h}$. What is the average velocity over the whole trip?

- $53\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $60\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $120\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $20\,\mathrm{km/h}$

## Explanation

Let $T$ be the total time. In the first half the car covers $40\,\mathrm{km/h} \cdot \dfrac{T}{2}$ and in the second $80\,\mathrm{km/h} \cdot \dfrac{T}{2}$, always in the same direction, so the displacement is the sum of the two stretches. Therefore
$$
v_m = \frac{40\,\mathrm{km/h} \cdot \frac{T}{2} + 80\,\mathrm{km/h} \cdot \frac{T}{2}}{T} = \frac{40\,\mathrm{km/h} + 80\,\mathrm{km/h}}{2} = 60\,\mathrm{km/h} .
$$
The value $53\,\mathrm{km/h}$ would be the average velocity if the two stretches had the same length, because then the slow stretch lasts longer.
