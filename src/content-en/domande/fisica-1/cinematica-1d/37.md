A car starts from rest; its acceleration is $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $4{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$ and then $-1{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for the next $2{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$. What velocity does it have at the end of the $6{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $8{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The change in velocity is the signed area under the acceleration graph, $\Delta v = \displaystyle\int a(t)\,dt$. In the first phase the area is $2{,}0 \cdot 4{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 8{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, in the second it is $-1{,}0 \cdot 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s} = -2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The car starts from rest, so at the end its velocity is $8{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s} - 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
