At the instant $t = 0$ a point is at the origin of the $x$-axis with velocity $v_0 = -6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and moves with constant acceleration $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At what instant does it pass through the origin again?

- $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4.2\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The position function is $x(t) = -6.0\,t + \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0\,t^2 = t^2 - 6.0\,t$. The point is at the origin when
$$
t^2 - 6.0\,t = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t\,(t - 6.0) = 0 ,
$$
so, besides the initial instant, it passes through the origin again at the instant $t = 6.0\,\mathrm{s}$. At the instant $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity $-6.0 + 2.0\,t$ vanishes, so at that instant the point reverses its motion, at the position $x = -9.0\,\mathrm{m}$.
