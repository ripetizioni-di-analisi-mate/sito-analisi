An elevator moves upward with positive initial velocity and brakes uniformly until it stops. The initial data are the initial speed $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the magnitude of the deceleration $a = 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Change the initial speed to $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the magnitude of the deceleration to $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $d$ minus its initial value?

- $6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity-displacement equation gives $0=v^2-2ad$. The relation $d = \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot a}$ gives $24\,\mathrm{m}$ before the change and $27\,\mathrm{m}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $3\,\mathrm{m}$.
