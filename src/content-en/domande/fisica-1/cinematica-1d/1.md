A car covers a straight stretch of $30\,\mathrm{km}$ on the way out at the constant speed of $60\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and returns to the starting point at the constant speed of $40\,\mathrm{km/h}$. What is the average velocity $v_m = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ over the whole trip?

- $48\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $10\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $0\,\mathrm{km/h}$

## Explanation

The average velocity is the ratio of the displacement to the time interval, $v_m = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$. The car returns to the starting point, so the overall displacement is $\Delta x = 0$. The trip lasts $\dfrac{30}{60}\,\mathrm{h} + \dfrac{30}{40}\,\mathrm{h} = 1.25\,\mathrm{h}$, therefore the average velocity is $0\,\mathrm{km/h}$. The value $48\,\mathrm{km/h}$ is the average speed, that is, the ratio of the distance traveled, $60\,\mathrm{km}$, to the time taken.
