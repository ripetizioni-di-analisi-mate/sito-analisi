A body moves in a straight line with initial velocity $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and is slowed down with acceleration $a = -k\,v$, with $k = 0.50\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. What distance does it travel while the velocity drops from $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$?

- $40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $55\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In motion with acceleration proportional to velocity one has $v(t) = v_0\,e^{-kt}$ and $x(t) - x_0 = \dfrac{v_0}{k}\left(1 - e^{-kt}\right)$. Since $e^{-kt} = \dfrac{v}{v_0}$, the distance traveled up to the instant at which the velocity equals $v$ is
$$
x - x_0 = \frac{v_0 - v}{k} = \frac{15\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.50\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 30\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The result does not require the time, which is $\dfrac{\ln 4}{k} = 2.77\,\mathrm{s}$.
