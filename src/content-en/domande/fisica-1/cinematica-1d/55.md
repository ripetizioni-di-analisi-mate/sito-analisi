A motorcyclist covers two equal distances along a straight road without stopping. The initial data are the speed over the first stretch $a = 30\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and the speed over the second stretch $b = 60\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Change the speed over the first stretch to $40\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and the speed over the second stretch to $120\,\mathrm{km/h}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $v$ minus its initial value?

- $20\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $10\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $-20\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $35\,\mathrm{km/h}$

## Explanation

The average speed is the total distance divided by the sum of the two travel times. The relation $v = \dfrac{2 \cdot a \cdot b}{a + b}$ gives $40\,\mathrm{km/h}$ before the change and $60\,\mathrm{km/h}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $20\,\mathrm{km/h}$.
