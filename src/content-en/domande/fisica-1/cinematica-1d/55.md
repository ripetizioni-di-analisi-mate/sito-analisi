A car brakes on a straight road with constant deceleration until it stops. Which statement about the braking is true?

- In each second the car covers the same distance
- In each second the speed decreases by the same amount
- The speed is proportional to the distance still to be covered
- The stopping time is proportional to the square of the initial speed

## Explanation

With constant deceleration the speed is $v(t) = v_0 - a\,t$, so in each second it decreases by the same amount $a \cdot 1\,\mathrm{s}$. The distance covered in each second decreases, because the average speed in the successive seconds keeps getting smaller. From the relation $v^2 = 2\,a\,s$, where $s$ is the distance left until the stop, it is the square of the speed that is proportional to $s$. The stopping time $\dfrac{v_0}{a}$ is proportional to the initial speed, while the stopping distance is proportional to its square.
