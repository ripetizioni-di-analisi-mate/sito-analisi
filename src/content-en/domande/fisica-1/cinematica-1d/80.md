The guidance system of a drone starting from rest along a straight line gives it the acceleration $a(t) = 2\,e^{-t}$, with $a$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $t$ in seconds. How does the velocity of the drone behave over very long times?

- It tends to $0$, because the acceleration tends to $0$
- It tends to $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- It tends to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- It grows without bound

## Explanation

The velocity is obtained by integrating the acceleration with the condition $v(0) = 0$, so
$$
v(t) = \int_0^t 2\,e^{-s}\,ds = 2\,\left(1 - e^{-t}\right) .
$$
Since $e^{-t} \to 0$ as $t \to +\infty$, the velocity tends to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The acceleration tends to zero, so the velocity stops increasing appreciably, but it stays positive and increasing. The limit of $a$ concerns the slope of the graph of $v$, while the value of $v$ tends to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
