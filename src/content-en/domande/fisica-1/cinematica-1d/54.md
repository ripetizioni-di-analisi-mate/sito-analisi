A motorcyclist covers two equal distances along a straight road without stopping. The initial data are the speed over the first stretch $a = 30\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and the speed over the second stretch $b = 60\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Change the speed over the second stretch to $90\,\mathrm{km/h}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $v$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{8}{9}$
- $\dfrac{9}{8}$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$

## Explanation

The average speed is the total distance divided by the sum of the two travel times. The relation $v = \dfrac{2 \cdot a \cdot b}{a + b}$ gives $40\,\mathrm{km/h}$ before the change and $45\,\mathrm{km/h}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{9}{8}$.
