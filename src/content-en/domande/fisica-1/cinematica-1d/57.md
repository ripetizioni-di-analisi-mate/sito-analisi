An elevator moves upward with positive initial velocity and brakes uniformly until it stops. Given the magnitude of the deceleration $a = 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the braking distance $d = 24\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the initial speed $v$?

- $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The velocity-displacement equation gives $0=v^2-2ad$. In the stated units, the relation is $d = \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot a}$. Solving for the unknown gives $v = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{d}$. Substituting the given values yields $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
