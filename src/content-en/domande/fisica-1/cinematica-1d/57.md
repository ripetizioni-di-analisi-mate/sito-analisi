A late passenger runs along the track at the constant speed of $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ toward the door of a stationary train. When he is $25\,\mathrm{m}$ from the door, the train starts in the same direction with constant acceleration $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the minimum distance between the passenger and the door?

- $25\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8.7\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Time is measured from the start of the train, with the origin at the position of the passenger at that instant and the axis pointing in the direction of motion. The positions of the passenger and of the door are $x_P(t) = 6.0\,t$ and $x_T(t) = 25 + 0.50\,t^2$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds, so their distance is
$$
d(t) = x_T(t) - x_P(t) = 0.50\,t^2 - 6.0\,t + 25 .
$$
The derivative $d'(t) = t - 6.0$ is the difference between the velocity of the train and that of the passenger, therefore the distance decreases as long as the train is slower and increases afterward. So the distance is minimum at the instant $t = 6.0\,\mathrm{s}$, when the two velocities are equal, and it equals $d(6.0) = 18 - 36 + 25 = 7.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The minimum is positive, therefore the passenger does not reach the door. The value $8.7\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance at the instant when the passenger has covered $25\,\mathrm{m}$, which is not the instant of minimum distance.
