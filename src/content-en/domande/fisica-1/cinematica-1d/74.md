On a straight road the position of a car that is slowing down is described by $x(t) = 10 \ln(1 + t)$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. What is the acceleration at the instant $t = 4\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-0.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Differentiating the position function gives the velocity $v(t) = \dfrac{10}{1 + t}$, and differentiating again gives the acceleration
$$
a(t) = -\frac{10}{(1 + t)^2} .
$$
So $a(4) = -\dfrac{10}{25}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = -0.40\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The negative sign indicates that the point, which moves in the positive direction, is slowing down. The value $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ coincides numerically with the velocity $v(4)$.
