Between the instants $t_1$ and $t_2$ the average velocity of a point in rectilinear motion is zero. Which statement is necessarily true?

- Between $t_1$ and $t_2$ the point stayed at rest
- At the instants $t_1$ and $t_2$ the point occupies the same position
- Between $t_1$ and $t_2$ the distance traveled is zero
- At the instants $t_1$ and $t_2$ the instantaneous velocity is zero

## Explanation

The average velocity is $v_m = \dfrac{x(t_2) - x(t_1)}{t_2 - t_1}$, so $v_m = 0$ is equivalent to $x(t_2) = x(t_1)$. The point may have moved and then come back. For example, a point that moves forward $5\,\mathrm{m}$ and returns to its starting point has zero displacement and distance traveled $10\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore neither the distance traveled nor the velocity at the ends of the interval is necessarily zero.
