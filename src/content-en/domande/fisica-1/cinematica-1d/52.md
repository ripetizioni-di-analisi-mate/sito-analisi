A motorcyclist covers two equal distances along a straight road without stopping. Given the speed over the second stretch $b = 60\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and the average speed $v = 40\,\mathrm{km/h}$, what is the speed over the first stretch $a$?

- $120\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $60\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $20\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$

## Explanation

The average speed is the total distance divided by the sum of the two travel times. In the stated units, the relation is $v = \dfrac{2 \cdot a \cdot b}{a + b}$. Solving for the unknown gives $a = \dfrac{b \cdot v}{2 \cdot b - v}$. Substituting the given values yields $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$.
