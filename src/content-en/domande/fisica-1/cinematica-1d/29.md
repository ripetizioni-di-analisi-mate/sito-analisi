A car travels at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with a constant deceleration of magnitude $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until it stops. What distance does it travel in the first $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ from the start of the braking?

- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $36\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity $v(t) = 12 - 4.0\,t$ vanishes at the instant $t = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$, so the car is at rest before $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ have elapsed. The laws of uniformly accelerated motion describe the braking only up to the stop, and afterward the car remains at rest. Therefore the distance traveled is the braking distance, $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,|a|} = \dfrac{144}{8.0}\,\mathrm{m} = 18\,\mathrm{m}$. Substituting $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ into the equation of motion would give $10\,\mathrm{m}$, the position of a point that turns back after stopping.
