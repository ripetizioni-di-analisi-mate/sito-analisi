A body moving fast in air is slowed down with acceleration $a = -c\,v^2$, with $a$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $v$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$. In which unit is the constant $c$ measured?

- $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$
- $\mathrm{s^{-2}}$

## Explanation

The two sides of the equality have the same unit, so $c\,v^2$ is measured in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Since $v^2$ is measured in $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, the constant $c$ is measured in
$$
\frac{\mathrm{m/s^2}}{\mathrm{m^2/s^2}} = \mathrm{m^{-1}} .
$$
The unit $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is that of the constant $k$ in the law $a = -k\,v$.
