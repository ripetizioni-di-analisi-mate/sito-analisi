A car travels at constant speed during the reaction time and then brakes at constant acceleration until it stops. Given the reaction time $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$, the magnitude of the braking acceleration $a = 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the initial speed $v = 16\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the total stopping distance $d$?

- $32\,\mathrm{m}$
- $80\,\mathrm{m}$
- $48\,\mathrm{m}$
- $96\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

During the reaction interval the car covers $vt$. The braking phase obeys $0=v^2-2a\Delta x$, so the two contributions are added. In the stated units, the relation is $d = t \cdot v + \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot a}$. Substituting the given values yields $48\,\mathrm{m}$.
