A car travels at $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$. The driver sees an obstacle and reacts after $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$, during which the velocity remains constant; then the driver brakes with a constant deceleration of magnitude $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the stopping distance, counted from the instant the driver sees the obstacle?

- $45\,\mathrm{m}$
- $25\,\mathrm{m}$
- $70\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity in SI units is $v_0 = \dfrac{72}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. During the reaction time the motion is uniform, so the car travels $20\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{s} = 20\,\mathrm{m}$. During the braking one uses the relation between velocity and displacement with zero final velocity, therefore the braking distance is $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,|a|} = \dfrac{400}{16}\,\mathrm{m} = 25\,\mathrm{m}$. The stopping distance is the sum of the two stretches, that is, $45\,\mathrm{m}$.
