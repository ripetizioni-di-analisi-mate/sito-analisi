Two stones are dropped from rest from the same height, the second $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after the first. Neglecting air resistance, how does the distance between the two stones vary while both are falling?

- It stays constant
- It increases by the same amount every second
- It increases in proportion to the square of the time
- It decreases with time

## Explanation

With the axis pointing downward and $t$ measured from the release of the first stone, the distances fallen are $\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,(t - 1)^2$, with $t$ in seconds. Their difference is
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,(t - 1)^2 = g\,t - \frac{g}{2} ,
$$
a first-degree function of $t$. Therefore the distance increases by $9.81\,\mathrm{m}$ every second. The reason is that the two stones have the same acceleration, so the difference of their velocities stays constant and equal to $g \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$.
