A car passes at the constant speed of $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in front of a police car at rest. At the same instant the police car starts, in the same direction, with constant acceleration $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. After how much time does the police car catch up with the car?

- $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{s}$
- $7.1\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

With the origin at the starting point of the police car, the position functions are $x_A(t) = 20\,t$ for the car and $x_P(t) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0\,t^2 = 2.0\,t^2$ for the police car. They meet when $x_A(t) = x_P(t)$, that is
$$
20\,t = 2.0\,t^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = 10\,\mathrm{s} ,
$$
besides the solution $t = 0$ of the start. At the instant $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the two velocities are equal, but at that instant the car is still ahead, because up to then it has been faster.
