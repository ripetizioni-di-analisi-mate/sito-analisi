A ball is thrown vertically from the ground. It passes the same height at times $t_1$ and $t_2$ before coming back down. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. Given the upward crossing time $t_{1} = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ and the height of the crossings $h = 60\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the downward crossing time $t_{2}$?

- $6\,\mathrm{s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2 + 2 \cdot \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The two times are the roots of $\dfrac{g}{2}t^2-v_0t+h=0$. Their product is $2h/g$. In the stated units, the relation is $h = 5 \cdot t_{1} \cdot t_{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $t_{2} = \dfrac{h}{5 \cdot t_{1}}$. Substituting the given values yields $6\,\mathrm{s}$.
