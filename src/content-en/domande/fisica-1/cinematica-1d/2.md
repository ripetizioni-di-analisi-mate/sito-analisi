Two trains travel on straight parallel tracks, toward each other. At the initial instant train $A$ is at station $P$ and travels at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$, train $B$ is $3{,}0\,\mathrm{km}$ from $P$ and travels at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At what distance from $P$ do the two trains pass each other?

- $1{,}2\,\mathrm{km}$
- $9{,}0\,\mathrm{km}$
- $1{,}5\,\mathrm{km}$
- $1{,}8\,\mathrm{km}$

## Explanation

The $x$ axis is chosen with the origin at $P$ and the positive direction from $P$ toward $B$. The equations of motion are $x_A(t) = 30\,t$ and $x_B(t) = 3000 - 20\,t$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds; the velocity of $B$ is negative because $B$ moves toward $P$. The trains pass each other when $x_A(t) = x_B(t)$, that is, $30\,t = 3000 - 20\,t$, so at the instant $t = 60\,\mathrm{s}$. At that instant train $A$ is at $x_A = 30 \cdot 60\,\mathrm{m} = 1800\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the trains pass each other $1{,}8\,\mathrm{km}$ from $P$.
