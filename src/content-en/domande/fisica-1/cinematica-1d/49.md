A train $200\,\mathrm{m}$ long crosses a bridge $400\,\mathrm{m}$ long at the constant speed of $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$. How much time elapses between the instant the front of the train enters the bridge and the instant its rear leaves it?

- $30\,\mathrm{s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{s}$
- $8.3\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The speed is $72\,\mathrm{km/h} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. From the entry of the front to the exit of the rear, the front covers the length of the bridge plus the length of the train, that is $600\,\mathrm{m}$. The motion is uniform, so
$$
\Delta t = \frac{600\,\mathrm{m}}{20\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 30\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The value $20\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by considering only the length of the bridge, that is, by treating the train as a point particle, which is not legitimate because its length is comparable to that of the bridge.
