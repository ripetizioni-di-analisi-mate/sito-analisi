An elevator moves upward with positive initial velocity and brakes uniformly until it stops. Given the initial speed $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the magnitude of the deceleration $a = 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the braking distance $d$?

- $36\,\mathrm{m}$
- $48\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $24\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity-displacement equation gives $0=v^2-2ad$. In the stated units, the relation is $d = \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot a}$. Substituting the given values yields $24\,\mathrm{m}$.
