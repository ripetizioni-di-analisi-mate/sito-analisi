A car traveling at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes with constant deceleration until it stops. What is its speed when it has covered half of the braking distance?

- $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $26\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $21\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Let $d$ be the braking distance and $a$ the magnitude of the deceleration. Since the final velocity is zero, the relation between velocity and displacement gives $v_0^2 = 2\,a\,d$. Halfway through the braking distance
$$
v^2 = v_0^2 - 2\,a\,\frac{d}{2} = v_0^2 - \frac{v_0^2}{2} = \frac{v_0^2}{2} ,
$$
so $v = \dfrac{v_0}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{30\,\mathrm{m/s}}{\sqrt{2}} = 21\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The speed $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is reached instead halfway through the braking *time*, when the car has already covered three quarters of the distance.
