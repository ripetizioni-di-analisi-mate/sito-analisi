During a thunderstorm the thunder is heard $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after the lightning is seen. Sound travels in air at $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the travel time of light is negligible. How far away did the lightning strike?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{km}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{km}$
- $4.1\,\mathrm{km}$
- $57\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Light arrives in a negligible time, so the $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$ delay is the time sound takes to cover the distance $d$ from the lightning. Sound travels in uniform motion, therefore
$$
d = v\,\Delta t = 340\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{s} = 2040\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.0\,\mathrm{km} .
$$
Sound covers the path only once, so the time is not divided by two as in the case of an echo.
