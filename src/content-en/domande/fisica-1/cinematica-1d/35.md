A cyclist rides up a climb $6{,}0\,\mathrm{km}$ long at the constant speed of $12\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and rides back down the same road at the constant speed of $36\,\mathrm{km/h}$. What is the average speed over the whole route?

- $24\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $9{,}0\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $18\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- $0\,\mathrm{km/h}$

## Explanation

The climb lasts $\dfrac{6{,}0}{12}\,\mathrm{h} = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{h}$ and the descent $\dfrac{6{,}0}{36}\,\mathrm{h} = \dfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{h}$, so the total time is $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{h}$. The average speed is the ratio of the distance traveled, $12\,\mathrm{km}$, to the time taken, therefore it equals $12 \cdot \dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{km/h} = 18\,\mathrm{km/h}$. The value is smaller than the arithmetic mean of the two speeds, $24\,\mathrm{km/h}$, because the cyclist spends more time on the stretch covered more slowly.
