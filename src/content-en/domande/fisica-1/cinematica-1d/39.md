A point moves along the $x$ axis with acceleration $a(x) = 4x$, with $a$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $x$ in meters. At the position $x = 0$ it has velocity $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What velocity does it have when it reaches the position $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $4.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Between $x = 0$ and $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ the velocity and the acceleration are positive, so the point does not reverse its motion and the velocity can be regarded as a function of the position. Since $a = v\,\dfrac{dv}{dx}$, integrating between $x_0$ and $x$ one obtains
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,v^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,v_0^2 = \int_0^{2} 4\xi\,d\xi = 8\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} .
$$
Therefore $v^2 = 9 + 16 = 25\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ and $v = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $6.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by using the relation of uniformly accelerated motion with the constant acceleration $a(2) = 8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, which does not hold because the acceleration varies with the position.
