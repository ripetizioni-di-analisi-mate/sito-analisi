A subway train starts from rest at a station with constant acceleration $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ up to $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, continues at constant speed and then brakes with constant deceleration of magnitude $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, stopping at the next station, $2.0\,\mathrm{km}$ away. How long does the trip last?

- $130\,\mathrm{s}$
- $100\,\mathrm{s}$
- $160\,\mathrm{s}$
- $70\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The start lasts $\dfrac{20}{1.0}\,\mathrm{s} = 20\,\mathrm{s}$ and the braking $\dfrac{20}{0.50}\,\mathrm{s} = 40\,\mathrm{s}$. In the velocity graph these phases are two triangles of height $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with areas $200\,\mathrm{m}$ and $400\,\mathrm{m}$. So $2000\,\mathrm{m} - 600\,\mathrm{m} = 1400\,\mathrm{m}$ remain at constant speed, covered in $\dfrac{1400}{20}\,\mathrm{s} = 70\,\mathrm{s}$. Therefore the trip lasts $20\,\mathrm{s} + 70\,\mathrm{s} + 40\,\mathrm{s} = 130\,\mathrm{s}$. The value $100\,\mathrm{s}$ is obtained by assuming that the train always travels at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
