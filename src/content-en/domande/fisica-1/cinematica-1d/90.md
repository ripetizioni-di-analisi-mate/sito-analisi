A speedboat shuts off its engine while traveling at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and from that instant the water slows it down with acceleration $a = -c\,v^2$, with $c = 0.010\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. What is the speed of the speedboat $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after the shutdown?

- $7.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The equation $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = -c\,v^2$ is separable, and the velocity stays positive. Separating the variables gives
$$
\int_{v_0}^{v} \frac{du}{u^2} = -c\,t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{v_0} = c\,t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \frac{v_0}{1 + c\,v_0\,t} .
$$
Since $c\,v_0\,t = 0.010 \cdot 20 \cdot 5.0 = 1.0$, one gets $v = \dfrac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2} = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The value $0$ is obtained by treating the initial acceleration $-c\,v_0^2 = -4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ as constant, and $7.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by using the exponential law $v_0\,e^{-c\,v_0\,t}$, which holds for an acceleration proportional to $v$.
