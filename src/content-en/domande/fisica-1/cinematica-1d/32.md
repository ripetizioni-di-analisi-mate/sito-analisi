A stone is thrown vertically upward. Neglecting air resistance, which statement is true at the highest point of the trajectory?

- The velocity and the acceleration are both zero
- The velocity is zero and the acceleration is $g$, directed downward
- The velocity is zero and the acceleration is $g$, directed upward
- The velocity is $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$ and the acceleration is $g$, directed downward

## Explanation

By the law of falling bodies the acceleration of the stone is $g$, directed downward, at every instant of the motion, on the way up as on the way down. At the highest point the velocity changes from positive to negative, so it vanishes. Since the acceleration is different from zero, the velocity keeps changing, and after the highest point the stone falls back with velocity directed downward.
