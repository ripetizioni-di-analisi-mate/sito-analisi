Two cyclists are on a straight road $30\,\mathrm{km}$ apart and ride toward each other at constant speeds of $12\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and $18\,\mathrm{km/h}$. At the instant they start, a fly leaves the first cyclist and flies toward the second at $45\,\mathrm{km/h}$, touches him, flies back at the same speed and keeps doing so until the cyclists meet. What total distance does the fly cover?

- $30\,\mathrm{km}$
- $45\,\mathrm{km}$
- $21.4\,\mathrm{km}$
- $225\,\mathrm{km}$

## Explanation

The cyclists approach each other with relative speed $12\,\mathrm{km/h} + 18\,\mathrm{km/h} = 30\,\mathrm{km/h}$, so they meet after
$$
t = \frac{30\,\mathrm{km}}{30\,\mathrm{km/h}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{h} .
$$
The fly flies during all this time with constant speed, therefore the distance it covers is $45\,\mathrm{km/h} \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{h} = 45\,\mathrm{km}$. The value $21.4\,\mathrm{km}$ is the length of the first flight only, up to the second cyclist, who approaches the fly with relative speed $63\,\mathrm{km/h}$.
