A car accelerates uniformly from $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $5{,}0\,\mathrm{s}$. What distance does it travel in this interval?

- $150\,\mathrm{m}$
- $50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $100\,\mathrm{m}$
- $200\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In uniformly accelerated motion the average velocity is the arithmetic mean of the initial and final velocities, so $v_m = \dfrac{10 + 30}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The car does not reverse its motion, therefore the distance traveled coincides with the displacement, $v_m\,\Delta t = 20\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 5{,}0\,\mathrm{s} = 100\,\mathrm{m}$. The same value is obtained from the equation of motion with $a = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, since $10 \cdot 5{,}0 + \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4{,}0 \cdot 5{,}0^2 = 100$.
