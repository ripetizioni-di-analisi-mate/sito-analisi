During the first $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after start-up a conveyor belt, initially at rest, has acceleration $a(t) = 2t$, with $a$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $t$ in seconds; afterward the speed of the belt stays constant. What distance does a point of the belt cover in the first $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $32\,\mathrm{m}$
- $45\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In the first phase, integrating the acceleration with the condition $v(0) = 0$ gives $v(t) = t^2$, and integrating the velocity with the condition $x(0) = 0$ gives $x(t) = \dfrac{t^3}{3}$. So at the instant $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the point has velocity $9.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and has covered $9.0\,\mathrm{m}$. In the following $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the motion is uniform, therefore the point covers $9.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{s} = 18\,\mathrm{m}$. The total distance is $9.0\,\mathrm{m} + 18\,\mathrm{m} = 27\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $32\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by computing the distance of the first phase as the area of the triangle $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 9.0\,\mathrm{m} = 13.5\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, by assuming that the velocity grows linearly, whereas it grows as $t^2$.
