A toy rocket on a horizontal rail starts from rest, and while its propellant runs out its acceleration is $a(t) = 6 - 2t$, with $a$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $t$ in seconds. At what instant is the velocity of the rocket maximum?

- $6\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The velocity increases as long as the acceleration, which is its derivative, is positive, and decreases when the acceleration becomes negative. The acceleration $6 - 2t$ is positive for $t < 3\,\mathrm{s}$ and negative for $t > 3\,\mathrm{s}$, so the velocity is maximum at the instant $3\,\mathrm{s}$. Indeed $v(t) = 6t - t^2$ equals $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at that instant. At the instant $6\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity is back to zero, and at the instant $0$ it is the acceleration that is maximum.
