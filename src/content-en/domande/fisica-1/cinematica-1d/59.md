An elevator moving upward brakes uniformly until it stops. The initial data are the initial speed $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the magnitude of the acceleration $a = 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Change the initial speed to $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $d$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $4$
- $1$

## Explanation

The velocity-displacement equation gives $0=v^2-2ad$. The relation $d = \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot a}$ gives $24\,\mathrm{m}$ before the change and $6\,\mathrm{m}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{4}$.
