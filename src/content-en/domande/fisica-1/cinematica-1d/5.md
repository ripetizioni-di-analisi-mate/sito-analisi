A train travels at $108\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and brakes with constant deceleration, stopping in $450\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the magnitude of the deceleration?

- $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $13\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $0{,}067\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The initial velocity in SI units is $v_0 = \dfrac{108}{3{,}6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 30\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The braking time is not given, so one uses the relation $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2\,a\,(x - x_0)$. Since the final velocity is zero, one has
$$
0 = (30\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 + 2\,a \cdot 450\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
It follows that $a = -\dfrac{900}{900}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = -1{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, therefore the deceleration has magnitude $1{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
