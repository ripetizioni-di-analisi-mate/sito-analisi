In front of a red light a driver brings his car from $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to a stop, with constant deceleration of magnitude $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What distance does the car cover in the last second before stopping?

- $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $21\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The motion of the last second, run backward in time, is a start from rest with acceleration of magnitude $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, because the position function is symmetric about the stopping instant. So the distance covered is
$$
d = \frac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (1.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The same result follows from the position function $x(t) = 24\,t - 3.0\,t^2$, because the car stops at $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $x(4) - x(3) = 48\,\mathrm{m} - 45\,\mathrm{m} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $21\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance covered in the first second of braking.
