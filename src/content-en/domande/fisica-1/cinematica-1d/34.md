To describe a stone thrown upward from the edge of a well with speed $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, one chooses the vertical $y$-axis pointing downward, with the origin at the launch point and $t = 0$ at the instant of the throw. Neglecting air resistance, what is the equation of motion of the stone, with $y$ in meters and $t$ in seconds?

- $y(t) = 8.0\,t - 4.905\,t^2$
- $y(t) = -8.0\,t - 4.905\,t^2$
- $y(t) = -8.0\,t + 4.905\,t^2$
- $y(t) = 8.0\,t + 4.905\,t^2$

## Explanation

The gravitational acceleration points downward, that is in the direction of the axis, so its component is $+g$. The initial velocity points upward, opposite to the axis, therefore its component is $v_0 = -8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The launch point is the origin, so the law of uniformly accelerated motion gives
$$
y(t) = -8.0\,t + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,t^2 = -8.0\,t + 4.905\,t^2 .
$$
The law $y(t) = 8.0\,t - 4.905\,t^2$ describes the same motion with the axis pointing upward. The two laws give opposite coordinates at every instant, because the two axes have opposite directions.
