From the edge of a roof $15\,\mathrm{m}$ high a stone is thrown vertically upward with velocity $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and then falls back down to the ground. Neglecting air resistance, at what speed does it hit the ground?

- $19{,}9\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $17{,}2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $13{,}9\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $27{,}2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

With the $y$ axis upward and the origin at the ground one has $y_0 = 15\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v_0 = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The relation between velocity and displacement with $a = -g$ gives, on arrival at $y = 0$, $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2g\,y_0$. Therefore
$$
v^2 = 100 + 2 \cdot 9{,}81 \cdot 15 = 394{,}3\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} ,
$$
and the impact speed is $19{,}9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The formula contains $v_0^2$, so the impact speed would be the same for a downward throw at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
