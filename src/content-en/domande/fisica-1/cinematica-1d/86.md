A car travels at constant speed during the reaction time and then brakes at constant acceleration until it stops. Given the magnitude of the braking acceleration $a = 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, the initial speed $v = 16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the total stopping distance $d = 48\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the reaction time $t$?

- $2\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{s}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

During the reaction interval the car covers $vt$. The braking phase obeys $0=v^2-2a\Delta x$, so the two contributions are added. In the stated units, the relation is $d = t \cdot v + \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot a}$. Solving for the unknown gives $t = \dfrac{d}{v} - \dfrac{v}{2 \cdot a}$. Substituting the given values yields $1\,\mathrm{s}$.
