For $t \geq 0$ the velocity graph $v(t)$ of a point in rectilinear motion is a decreasing straight line that crosses the time axis at the instant $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$. Which statement is true?

- At the instant $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ the point reverses direction
- At the instant $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ the point returns to its initial position
- At the instant $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ the acceleration is zero
- After the instant $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ the point slows down

## Explanation

The graph is a decreasing straight line, so the acceleration, which is its slope, is constant and negative. Before $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity is positive and after it is negative, therefore at $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ the point is momentarily at rest and reverses direction. The displacement between $0$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$ is the area of the triangle above the axis, which is positive, so the point is not at its initial position. After $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ velocity and acceleration are both negative, so the magnitude of the velocity increases.
