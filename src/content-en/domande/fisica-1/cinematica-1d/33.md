A ball is dropped from rest from a window $20\,\mathrm{m}$ high. At the same instant, from the ground and on the same vertical line, a second ball is thrown upward with speed $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting air resistance, after how much time do the two balls meet?

- $1.3\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$
- $0.67\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.1\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

With the $y$-axis pointing upward and the origin on the ground, the heights of the two balls are $y_1(t) = 20 - \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$ and $y_2(t) = 15\,t - \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$. The balls meet when $y_1(t) = y_2(t)$, and the terms containing $g$ cancel, so
$$
20 = 15\,t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = 1.3\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The two balls have the same acceleration, therefore their distance decreases in uniform motion at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. They meet about $11\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground, before the first ball reaches the ground.
