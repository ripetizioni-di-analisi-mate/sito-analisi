A student walks to school at constant speed. If he walks at $4.0\,\mathrm{km/h}$ he arrives $5$ minutes after classes begin, if he walks at $6.0\,\mathrm{km/h}$ he arrives $5$ minutes early. How far is the school from home?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{km}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{km}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{km}$
- $0.83\,\mathrm{km}$

## Explanation

Let $d$ be the distance. The travel times at the two speeds are $\dfrac{d}{4.0\,\mathrm{km/h}}$ and $\dfrac{d}{6.0\,\mathrm{km/h}}$, and their difference is $10\,\mathrm{min} = \dfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{h}$. So
$$
\frac{d}{4} - \frac{d}{6} = \frac{1}{6}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{d}{12} = \frac{1}{6}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = 2.0\,\mathrm{km} .
$$
Indeed at $4.0\,\mathrm{km/h}$ it takes $30\,\mathrm{min}$ and at $6.0\,\mathrm{km/h}$ it takes $20$.
