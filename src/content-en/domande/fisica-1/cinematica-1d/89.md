A skydiver falls vertically at $50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ when he opens the parachute. From that instant, with the axis pointing downward, his acceleration is $a = g - k\,v$, and the terminal velocity with the parachute open is $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is his acceleration right after the opening?

- $88\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed downward
- $9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed downward
- $88\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed upward
- $108\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, directed upward

## Explanation

The terminal velocity is the value at which the acceleration vanishes, so $v_L = \dfrac{g}{k}$ and $k = \dfrac{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 1.96\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Right after the opening
$$
a = g - k\,v = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 1.96\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} \cdot 50\,\mathrm{m/s} = -88\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The acceleration is negative with the axis pointing downward, therefore it is directed upward, and the skydiver slows down approaching the terminal velocity. The value $108\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is obtained by adding the two terms instead of subtracting them.
