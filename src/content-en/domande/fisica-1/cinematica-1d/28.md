A body starts from rest with constant acceleration and in the first second travels $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$. What distance does it travel in the third second, that is, between $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t = 3\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Starting from rest the equation of motion is $x(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2$, and the condition $x(1\,\mathrm{s}) = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $a = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. So $x(t) = 2t^2$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. In the third second the body travels $x(3) - x(2) = 18\,\mathrm{m} - 8{,}0\,\mathrm{m} = 10\,\mathrm{m}$. The distances traveled in successive seconds, $2\,\mathrm{m}$, $6\,\mathrm{m}$, $10\,\mathrm{m}$, are in the ratio of the odd numbers $1, 3, 5$.
