A cart starts from rest at the origin at the instant $t = 0$, and from that instant its acceleration grows in proportion to time, $a(t) = b\,t$ with $b > 0$ constant. Which description of the graphs of the velocity $v(t)$ and of the equation of motion $x(t)$, for $t \geq 0$, is correct?

- $v(t)$ is an increasing half-line and $x(t)$ is an arc of a parabola
- $v(t)$ and $x(t)$ are both arcs of a parabola
- $v(t)$ is an arc of a parabola with its vertex at the origin and $x(t)$ is the graph of a third-degree function
- $v(t)$ is an increasing half-line and $x(t)$ is the graph of a third-degree function

## Explanation

Integrating the acceleration with the condition $v(0) = 0$ gives $v(t) = \displaystyle\int_0^t b\,s\,ds = \dfrac{b}{2}\,t^2$, so the velocity graph is an arc of a parabola with its vertex at the origin. Integrating the velocity with the condition $x(0) = 0$ gives $x(t) = \dfrac{b}{6}\,t^3$, therefore the equation of motion is a third-degree function. The half-line for $v(t)$ and the arc of a parabola for $x(t)$ describe instead motion with constant acceleration, in which the velocity is of first degree and the position of second degree.
