During the flight of a stone thrown vertically upward, an observer sees it pass in front of a window on the way up $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after the throw and on the way down $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after the throw. Neglecting air resistance, with what speed was the stone thrown?

- $19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $29.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $39.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

With the $y$-axis pointing upward the height $y(t) = y_0 + v_0\,t - \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$ is a second-degree function of $t$, so its graph is a parabola symmetric about the instant at which the stone reaches the highest point. The stone is at the same height at the instants $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$, therefore it reaches the highest point at the intermediate instant $t_{\max} = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. At that instant the velocity $v(t) = v_0 - g\,t$ vanishes, so
$$
v_0 = g\,t_{\max} = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{s} = 19.6\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $29.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by taking $3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ as the instant of the highest point, whereas it is the instant of the second passage in front of the window.
