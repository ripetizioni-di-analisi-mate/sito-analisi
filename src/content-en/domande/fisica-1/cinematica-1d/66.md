The graph shows the velocity $v(t)$ of a car on a straight road, with $v$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and $t$ in seconds. What is the acceleration between the instants $10\,\mathrm{s}$ and $14\,\mathrm{s}$?

::grafico{f="min(2*x,12,3*(14-x))+0*sqrt(x*(14-x))" punti="6,12,pieno;10,12,pieno;14,0,pieno" segmenti="6,0,6,12;10,0,10,12" xmin=-2 xmax=16 ymin=-2 ymax=16 nomi="t,v" titolo="Velocity $v(t)$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$, with $t$ in seconds"}

- $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The acceleration is the slope of the velocity graph. Between $10\,\mathrm{s}$ and $14\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity goes from $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $0$, so
$$
a = \frac{0 - 12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{14\,\mathrm{s} - 10\,\mathrm{s}} = -3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The sign is negative because the velocity, which is positive, decreases, so the car is braking. The value $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is the acceleration in the first piece, and $-1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is obtained by dividing the change in velocity by the instant $10\,\mathrm{s}$ instead of by the duration of the piece.
