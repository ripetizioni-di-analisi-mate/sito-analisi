Two cars $A$ and $B$ move in uniform motion on the same straight road, and in the $(t, x)$ plane their position–time graphs are two distinct parallel lines. Which statement is true?

- $A$ and $B$ have the same velocity and meet only once
- $A$ and $B$ have the same position at the instant $t = 0$
- $A$ and $B$ have the same velocity and never meet
- $A$ and $B$ have opposite velocities and meet only once

## Explanation

In uniform motion the velocity is the slope of the position–time graph, and two parallel lines have the same slope; so $A$ and $B$ have the same velocity $v$. The equations of motion are $x_A(t) = p + v\,t$ and $x_B(t) = q + v\,t$, with $p \neq q$ because the lines are distinct. The equation $x_A(t) = x_B(t)$ reduces to $p = q$, which is false, therefore the two cars never meet and their distance remains $|p - q|$.
