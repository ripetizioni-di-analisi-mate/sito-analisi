A body starts from rest and moves with acceleration $a = A - k\,v$, with $A = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $k = 1.5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. What is the terminal velocity?

- $9.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The velocity increases and tends to the terminal velocity, that is, to the value for which the acceleration vanishes. Imposing $A - k\,v_L = 0$ one obtains $v_L = \dfrac{A}{k} = \dfrac{6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{1.5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The same value is the limit as $t \to +\infty$ of $v(t) = \dfrac{A}{k}\left(1 - e^{-kt}\right)$.
