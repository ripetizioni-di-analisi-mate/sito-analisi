A land yacht starts from rest on a straight track, pushed by a constant wind and slowed down by the air, and its acceleration is $a = A - k\,v$, with $A$ and $k$ positive constants. Which statement about the motion of the land yacht is true?

- The velocity and the acceleration both increase with time
- The velocity reaches the value $\dfrac{A}{k}$ in a finite time and then stays constant
- The acceleration stays equal to $A$ until the velocity reaches $\dfrac{A}{k}$
- The velocity increases and the acceleration decreases with time

## Explanation

Starting from rest the velocity is $v(t) = \dfrac{A}{k}\left(1 - e^{-kt}\right)$, which increases for every $t > 0$. The acceleration is
$$
a(t) = A - k\,v(t) = A\,e^{-kt} ,
$$
which starts from the value $A$ and decreases toward zero. The velocity tends to the terminal velocity $\dfrac{A}{k}$ without reaching it in a finite time, because $e^{-kt}$ never vanishes.
