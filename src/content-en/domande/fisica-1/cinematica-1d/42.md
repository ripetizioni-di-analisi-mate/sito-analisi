Two points $A$ and $B$ move along the $x$ axis with the same acceleration $a(t)$ at every instant. At the instant $t = 0$ point $A$ is at $x = 0$ and point $B$ at $x = 3\,\mathrm{m}$, and both have velocity $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which statement is true for every $t > 0$?

- The distance between $A$ and $B$ remains equal to $3\,\mathrm{m}$
- The distance between $A$ and $B$ increases with time
- $A$ and $B$ meet after some time
- The equations of motion of $A$ and $B$ coincide

## Explanation

Let $d(t) = x_B(t) - x_A(t)$. Since the two accelerations are equal, the second derivative of $d$ is zero, so its derivative $v_B - v_A$ is constant. At the initial instant $v_B - v_A = 0$, therefore $d$ has zero derivative at every instant and is constant, equal to $d(0) = 3\,\mathrm{m}$. The equations of motion are different, because the initial conditions are different.
