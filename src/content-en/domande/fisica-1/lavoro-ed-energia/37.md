A body of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is dropped from rest from a height of $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ above the free end of a vertical ideal spring of constant $k = 1000\,\mathrm{N/m}$. What is the maximum compression of the spring? Take $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance.

- $8.9\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6.8\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Mechanical energy, the sum of the kinetic energy, the potential energy of the weight and the elastic potential energy, is conserved. At the maximum compression $s$ the body is at rest and has descended by $h + s$ with respect to the initial position, so, with $s$ in meters,
$$
mg\,(h + s) = \frac{1}{2} k s^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 500\,s^2 - 10\,s - 4.0 = 0 .
$$
The positive solution is $s = \dfrac{10 + \sqrt{100 + 8000}}{1000}\,\mathrm{m} = \dfrac{10 + 90}{1000}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. The term $mgs$ is the work of the weight during the compression. The value $\dfrac{mg}{k} = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the compression at the equilibrium position.
