A book rests on a shelf $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ high in a room $3{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ high. Two students compute the gravitational potential energy of the book, one with zero height at the floor and the other with zero height at the ceiling. Which quantity has the same value in the two calculations?

- The potential energy of the book on the shelf
- The change in potential energy when the book falls to the floor
- The sign of the potential energy of the book on the shelf
- The mechanical energy of the book at rest on the shelf

## Explanation

With zero height at the floor the book has $y = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ and $U = mg \cdot 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m} > 0$; with zero height at the ceiling it has $y = -1{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ and $U = -mg \cdot 1{,}0\,\mathrm{m} < 0$. Changing the reference position adds a constant to $U$, so the value and the sign of $U$ change, and so does the mechanical energy $E = K + U$. The **changes** in potential energy do not contain the constant, and in the fall to the floor they equal $-mg \cdot 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ in both calculations.
