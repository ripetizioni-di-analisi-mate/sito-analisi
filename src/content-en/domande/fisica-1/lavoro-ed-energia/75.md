The graph shows the potential energy $U(x)$ of a particle moving along the $x$ axis under the action of the associated conservative force only. At which of the marked points does the force point in the positive direction of the $x$ axis?

::grafico{f="x^3-3*x" punti="-2,-2,pieno,A;-1,2,pieno,B;0,0,pieno,C;1.5,-1.125,pieno,D" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.6 ymin=-4.5 ymax=4.5 nomi="x,U" legenda=0 titolo="Potential energy $U$ as a function of position $x$"}

- At point $A$
- At point $B$
- At point $D$
- At point $C$

## Explanation

The component of the force is $F_x = -\dfrac{dU}{dx}$, so the force points in the positive direction of the $x$ axis where $U$ decreases.

At $A$ the potential energy increases toward the maximum at $B$, so at $A$ the force points in the negative direction. At $B$, which is a maximum, the derivative is zero and the force is zero. At $C$ the graph goes down from the maximum at $B$ toward the minimum, so $U$ decreases and the force points in the **positive direction**. At $D$, to the right of the minimum, $U$ increases and the force again points in the negative direction.
