A ball moves from rest down a frictionless track. The height difference is $h$ and the speed is measured at the bottom. The initial data are gravitational acceleration $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the height difference $h = 5\,\mathrm{m}$. Change gravitational acceleration to $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the height difference to $10\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $v$ minus its initial value?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Gravity does work $mgh$, so $\frac12 mv^2=mgh$. The mass cancels. The relation $v = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{g} \cdot \sqrt{h}$ gives $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before the change and $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
