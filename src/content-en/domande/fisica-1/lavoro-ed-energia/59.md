A block compresses an ideal spring on a frictionless surface and momentarily stops at maximum compression. The initial data are the speed $v = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the spring constant $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the mass $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$. Change the spring constant to $400\,\mathrm{N/m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $x$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$
- $1$

## Explanation

By energy conservation, the initial kinetic energy $\frac12 mv^2$ becomes elastic energy $\frac12 kx^2$. The relation $x = \dfrac{\sqrt{m} \cdot v}{\sqrt{k}}$ gives $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$ before the change and $\dfrac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{m}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
