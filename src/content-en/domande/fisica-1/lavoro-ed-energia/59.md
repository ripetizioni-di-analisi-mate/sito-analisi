A uniform chain of mass $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ lies stretched out on the floor. It is lifted slowly by one end until it hangs completely vertical with the other end just touching the floor. What is the minimum work required?

- $29\,\mathrm{J}$
- $59\,\mathrm{J}$
- $15\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

When the pulled end is at height $y$, a piece of chain of length $y$ and mass $\dfrac{m}{L}\,y$ is lifted. When lifting slowly, the force to be applied equals the weight of the hanging piece, $F(y) = \dfrac{m}{L}\,g\,y$, which grows with $y$.

The work is therefore the integral of a variable force:
$$
L_{\min} = \int_0^L \frac{m}{L}\,g\,y\,dy = \frac{1}{2}\,m g L = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{J} \approx 29\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The result equals the increase in the potential energy of the chain, whose center of mass rises by $\dfrac{L}{2}$. The value $59\,\mathrm{J}$ corresponds to lifting the whole chain by $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$.
