A block of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a rough horizontal surface and, when its speed is $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hits the free end of an ideal horizontal spring with spring constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, initially at its rest length. The coefficient of kinetic friction between block and surface is $0.50$. What is the maximum compression of the spring?

- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.35\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.92\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

During the compression the elastic force, which is conservative, and friction act; friction has magnitude $\mu_d\, mg \approx 9.81\,\mathrm{N}$ and does the work $-\mu_d\, mg\, x$. At the maximum compression $x$ the block is at rest, so by the theorem on the **work of nonconservative forces**
$$
-\mu_d\, mg\, x = \frac{1}{2} k x^2 - \frac{1}{2} m v^2 ,
$$
that is, $100\,x^2 + 9.81\,x - 9.0 = 0$ with $x$ in meters. The positive solution is
$$
x = \frac{-9.81 + \sqrt{9.81^2 + 3600}}{200}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.25\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ is the maximum compression without friction, $v\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$.
