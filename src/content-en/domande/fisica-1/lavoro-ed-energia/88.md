In which of the following situations is the mechanical energy of the body conserved?

- A crate slides down a rough inclined plane at constant velocity
- A parachutist descends at constant velocity
- A small ball swings hanging from an inextensible string, with negligible air resistance
- A car brakes to a stop on a horizontal road

## Explanation

Mechanical energy is conserved when the forces other than the conservative ones do no work.

The bob of the pendulum is acted on by the weight, which is conservative, and by the tension of the string, which is always perpendicular to the velocity and therefore does no work. Hence the mechanical energy of the ball is **conserved**.

The crate and the parachutist descend with constant kinetic energy while the potential energy decreases, so their mechanical energy decreases, because of the negative work of friction and of air resistance. The car loses kinetic energy at constant height, because the brakes and friction do negative work.
