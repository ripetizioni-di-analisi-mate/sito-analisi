A crate of mass $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pulled for $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ on a horizontal floor with a force of $100\,\mathrm{N}$ inclined at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the crate and the floor is $0.50$. How much work does friction do?

- $-3.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-4.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-2.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The crate does not move vertically, so the sum of the vertical components of the forces is zero. The applied force has the upward vertical component $F\sin 30^\circ = 50\,\mathrm{N}$, therefore the normal force is $N = mg - F\sin 30^\circ = (196.2 - 50)\,\mathrm{N} \approx 146\,\mathrm{N}$. Friction has magnitude $\mu_d N \approx 73\,\mathrm{N}$ and is opposite to the displacement, so
$$
L_{\text{fr}} = -\mu_d\,N\,d \approx -0.50 \cdot 146 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{J} \approx -2.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The equality $N = mg$ holds when the applied force is horizontal.
