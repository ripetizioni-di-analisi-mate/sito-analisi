An electric motor that takes in a power of $1.5\,\mathrm{kW}$ stays on for $2.0$ hours. How much energy does it take in, expressed in joules?

- $3.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.1 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The power is constant, so the energy taken in is the product of the power and the time. In SI units the power is $1500\,\mathrm{W}$ and the time is $2.0 \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 7200\,\mathrm{s}$, therefore
$$
E = P\,\Delta t = 1500 \cdot 7200\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.1 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The same result follows from $E = 3.0\,\mathrm{kWh}$, since $1\,\mathrm{kWh} = 3.6 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$.
