A crate slides along a rough horizontal track until it stops. Kinetic friction of constant magnitude is the only force that does work. Given the initial speed $u = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the mass $m = 5\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the stopping distance $d = 10\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the force magnitude $F$?

- $\dfrac{27}{2}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $18\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9\,\mathrm{N}$
- $\dfrac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The work of friction is $-Fd$ and the change in kinetic energy is $-\frac12 mu^2$. In the stated units, the relation is $d = \dfrac{m \cdot u^{2}}{2 \cdot F}$. Solving for the unknown gives $F = \dfrac{m \cdot u^{2}}{2 \cdot d}$. Substituting the given values yields $9\,\mathrm{N}$.
