A stone of mass $m$ is dropped from rest from the height $h_0$, with air resistance negligible. How does its kinetic energy $K$ depend on the height $h$ during the fall?

- $K = mgh$, which increases linearly with $h$
- $K = mg\,(h_0 - h)$, which decreases linearly as $h$ increases
- $K$ is proportional to $(h_0 - h)^2$
- $K$ is constant and equal to $mgh_0$

## Explanation

Only the weight, which is conservative, acts on the stone, so mechanical energy is conserved and always equals $E = m g h_0$, its initial value.

At height $h$ the potential energy is $m g h$, so $K = E - mgh = mg\,(h_0 - h)$. The graph of $K$ as a function of $h$ is therefore a straight line that decreases as $h$ increases, from $m g h_0$ at the ground to zero at the starting height. The quadratic dependence holds for $K$ as a function of time, because the speed grows linearly with time.
