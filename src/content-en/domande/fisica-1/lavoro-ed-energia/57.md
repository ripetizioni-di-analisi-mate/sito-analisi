A cart moves along a horizontal track on the $x$ axis, pushed by an air jet whose force decreases with distance according to $F_x(x) = 10\,e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$, where $x$ is in meters and $F_x$ in newtons. How much work does the jet do while the cart goes from $x = 0$ to $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $20\,\mathrm{J}$
- $13\,\mathrm{J}$
- $7.4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.3\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The force depends on position, so the work is the integral of $F_x$ between $0$ and $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. An antiderivative of $10\,e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$ is $-20\,e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$, so
$$
L = \left[ -20\,e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \right]_0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 20\,\left( 1 - e^{-1} \right)\mathrm{J} \approx 13\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $20\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by taking the force constant and equal to its initial value $10\,\mathrm{N}$, whereas the force decreases along the stretch.
