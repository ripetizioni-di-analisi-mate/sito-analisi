A block compresses an ideal spring on a frictionless surface and momentarily stops at maximum compression. Given the spring constant $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$, the mass $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the maximum compression $x = \dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the speed $v$?

- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\dfrac{3}{25}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\dfrac{3 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By energy conservation, the initial kinetic energy $\frac12 mv^2$ becomes elastic energy $\frac12 kx^2$. In the stated units, the relation is $x = \dfrac{\sqrt{m} \cdot v}{\sqrt{k}}$. Solving for the unknown gives $v = \dfrac{\sqrt{k} \cdot x}{\sqrt{m}}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
