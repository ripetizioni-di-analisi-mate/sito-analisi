A ball of mass $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ is thrown vertically upward at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and, because of air resistance, rises only $18\,\mathrm{m}$ above the launch point. Assuming the magnitude of the air resistance is constant during the ascent, what is its value?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

During the ascent air resistance points downward, opposite to the displacement, and does the work $-f h$. By the theorem on the **work of nonconservative forces** this work equals the change in mechanical energy between the launch and the highest point, where the ball is at rest:
$$
-f h = m g h - \frac{1}{2} m v_0^2 = (0.15 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 18 - 30)\,\mathrm{J} \approx -3.51\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Hence $f = \dfrac{3.51}{18}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.20\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $1.5\,\mathrm{N}$ is the weight of the ball.
