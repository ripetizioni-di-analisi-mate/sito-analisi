A simple pendulum consists of a small ball of mass $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ hanging from an inextensible string of negligible mass. The ball is released from rest with the string taut and horizontal. What is the tension in the string when the ball passes through the lowest point?

- $2.9\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.9\,\mathrm{N}$
- $8.8\,\mathrm{N}$
- $15\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The tension is always perpendicular to the velocity and does no work, so mechanical energy is conserved. From the horizontal position to the lowest point the ball descends by $\ell$, so $mg\ell = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$, that is, $v^2 = 2 g \ell$.

At the lowest point the ball moves along an arc of a circle of radius $\ell$, and the resultant of the tension and the weight is the centripetal force:
$$
T - mg = \frac{m v^2}{\ell} = 2mg
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = 3mg = 3 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \approx 8.8\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The tension does not depend on the length of the string, which cancels. The value $2.9\,\mathrm{N}$ is the weight of the ball, which equals the tension only when the ball is at rest.
