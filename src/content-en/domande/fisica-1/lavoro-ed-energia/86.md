A body speeds up from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to a higher final speed. The net work is positive. Given the final speed $v = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the net work $W = 48\,\mathrm{J}$, what is the mass $m$?

- $\dfrac{8}{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

By the work-energy theorem, net work is final kinetic energy minus initial kinetic energy. In the stated units, the relation is $W = \dfrac{m \cdot \left(v^{2} - 4\right)}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{2 \cdot W}{v^{2} - 4}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{kg}$.
