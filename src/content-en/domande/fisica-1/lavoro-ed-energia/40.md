A block starts from rest at the top of a plane inclined at $30^\circ$ and $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ high, slides down and continues onto a horizontal stretch joined smoothly to the base of the plane. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $0.20$ both on the incline and on the horizontal stretch. What distance does the block travel on the horizontal stretch before stopping?

- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The incline is $\dfrac{h}{\sin\alpha} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long and on it the normal force is $mg\cos\alpha$, so friction does the work $-\mu_d\, mg\cos\alpha \cdot \dfrac{h}{\sin\alpha} = -\mu_d\, mg\,\dfrac{h}{\tan\alpha}$. On the horizontal stretch the normal force is $mg$ and friction does the work $-\mu_d\, mg\, d$.

The block starts and ends at rest, so by the theorem on the **work of nonconservative forces** the sum of the work done by friction equals the change in mechanical energy, $-mgh$:
$$
-\mu_d\, mg\,\frac{h}{\tan\alpha} - \mu_d\, mg\, d = -mgh
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = \frac{h}{\mu_d} - \frac{h}{\tan\alpha} = \left( 10 - 3.46 \right)\mathrm{m} \approx 6.5\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The mass cancels. The value $10\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by neglecting friction on the incline.
