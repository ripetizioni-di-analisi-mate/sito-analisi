A body moves along the $x$-axis, and the component $v_x$ of its velocity varies with time as in the graph. In which time interval is the total work done by the forces acting on the body negative?

::grafico{linee="0,0 4,8 8,8 12,4 16,-4" segmenti="4,0,4,8;8,0,8,8;12,0,12,4;16,0,16,-4" xmin=-2 xmax=18 ymin=-6 ymax=10 nomi="t,vₓ" legenda=0 titolo="Velocity component $v_x$, in meters per second, as a function of time $t$, in seconds"}

- Between $0$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$
- Between $8\,\mathrm{s}$ and $12\,\mathrm{s}$
- Between $12\,\mathrm{s}$ and $16\,\mathrm{s}$
- Between $4\,\mathrm{s}$ and $8\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

By the **work–energy theorem** the total work over an interval equals the change in the kinetic energy $K = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$, which depends only on the magnitude of the velocity.

Between $0$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$ the speed grows from $0$ to $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the total work is positive. Between $4\,\mathrm{s}$ and $8\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity is constant, therefore the total work is zero. Between $8\,\mathrm{s}$ and $12\,\mathrm{s}$ the speed drops from $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the kinetic energy decreases and the total work is **negative**.

Between $12\,\mathrm{s}$ and $16\,\mathrm{s}$ the component $v_x$ goes from $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the speed at the beginning and at the end is the same. Therefore in this interval the final kinetic energy equals the initial one and the total work is zero.
