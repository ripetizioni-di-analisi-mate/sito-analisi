In an Atwood machine two bodies of masses $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hang from the two ends of an inextensible string of negligible mass that passes over a frictionless pulley of negligible mass. The system starts from rest. How fast are the bodies moving when the $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ body has descended $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $4.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The string is inextensible, so at every instant the two bodies have speeds of the same magnitude $v$, and one rises as much as the other descends. The tension has the same magnitude at both ends and does positive work on one body and the opposite work on the other, so the total work of the tension on the system is zero.

Only the weights, which are conservative, do work, and the mechanical energy of the system is conserved. The body of mass $m_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ loses the potential energy $m_1 g h$, the body of mass $m_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ gains $m_2 g h$, and
$$
(m_1 - m_2)\,g h = \frac{1}{2}\,(m_1 + m_2)\,v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(m_1 - m_2)\,g h}{m_1 + m_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.0}{5.0}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $4.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the speed of free fall from $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$.
