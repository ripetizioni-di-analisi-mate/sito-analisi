A block rests against a compressed ideal spring on a smooth horizontal plane; once the spring is released, the block leaves it with speed $v$. With what speed does a block of twice the mass leave the same spring, if the initial compression is doubled?

- $2v$
- $v$
- $2\sqrt{2}\,v$
- $\sqrt{2}\,v$

## Explanation

The sum of the kinetic energy and the elastic potential energy is conserved, so $\dfrac{1}{2} k x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$ and $v = x\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$. Doubling the compression doubles the speed, and doubling the mass divides the speed by $\sqrt{2}$. Therefore the new speed is $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2}}\,v = \sqrt{2}\,v$. In terms of energy, the spring stores four times as much energy, which is converted into the kinetic energy of twice the mass.
