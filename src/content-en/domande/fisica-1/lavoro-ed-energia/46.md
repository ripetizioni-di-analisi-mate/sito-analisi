A stone tied to a cord moves in uniform circular motion with radius $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ on a smooth horizontal plane, with the other end of the cord fixed at the center. The tension in the cord is $15\,\mathrm{N}$. How much work does the tension do while the stone goes through half a revolution?

- $38\,\mathrm{J}$
- $24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-24\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In uniform circular motion the tension points toward the center, while the velocity is tangent to the circle. At every instant the tension is therefore perpendicular to the infinitesimal displacement, and its contribution $\vec{T} \cdot d\vec{r}$ to the work is zero.

Hence the work of the tension is **zero** on any arc of the path, even though the stone moves $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$ from one end of the diameter to the other. The same result follows from the work–energy theorem, since the tension is the only force that could do work and the kinetic energy of the stone stays constant.
