Two bodies, of mass $m$ and $2m$, are at rest on a smooth horizontal surface. The same constant horizontal force $\vec{F}$ acts on each of them over the same straight stretch of length $d$. Which statement about the two bodies at the end of the stretch is true?

- They have the same kinetic energy, and the body of mass $m$ is faster
- They have the same speed, because the force and the stretch are the same
- The body of mass $2m$ has twice the kinetic energy
- The body of mass $m$ has twice the kinetic energy and twice the speed

## Explanation

On each body the weight and the normal force do no work, so the total work is that of the force, $F d$, the same for the two bodies.

By the **work–energy theorem** both bodies, having started from rest, have final kinetic energy $K = F d$. From the relation $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m}}$ it follows that, for equal $K$, the speed is greater for the smaller mass; in particular the body of mass $m$ has a speed $\sqrt{2}$ times that of the body of mass $2m$.
