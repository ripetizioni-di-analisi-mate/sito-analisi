A small ball slides without friction along a track that ends in a loop-the-loop, that is, a circular section of radius $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ in a vertical plane, traveled on the inside. The ball starts from rest. What is the minimum starting height, measured from the lowest point of the loop, for the ball to go around the loop without leaving the track?

- $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At the top of the loop the normal force and the weight both point toward the center, so $N + mg = \dfrac{m v^2}{R}$. The ball stays in contact if $N \geq 0$, so at the top we need $v^2 \geq g R$.

The track is smooth and the normal force does no work, so mechanical energy is conserved. From the starting height $h$ to the top of the loop, at height $2R$,
$$
m g h = m g \cdot 2R + \frac{1}{2} m v^2 \geq 2 m g R + \frac{1}{2} m g R ,
$$
hence $h \geq \dfrac{5}{2} R = 10\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$, that is $2R$, would bring the ball to the top with zero speed, and the ball would leave the track before reaching it.
