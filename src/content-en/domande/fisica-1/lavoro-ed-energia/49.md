A stone of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is dropped from rest, with air resistance negligible. What is the instantaneous power of the weight $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after the fall begins?

- $48\,\mathrm{W}$
- $4.9\,\mathrm{W}$
- $96\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

In free fall from rest the velocity after the time $t$ has magnitude $v = g t = 9.81 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and points downward, like the weight.

The instantaneous power is $P = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$, and since the weight and the velocity are parallel and in the same direction
$$
P = m g\, v = 0.50 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 19.6\,\mathrm{W} \approx 96\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The power grows linearly with time. The value $48\,\mathrm{W}$ is the average power over the first $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, that is, the work of the weight $m g \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} g t^2$ divided by $t$.
