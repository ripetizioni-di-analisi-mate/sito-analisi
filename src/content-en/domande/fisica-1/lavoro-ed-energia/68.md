A bow pushes an arrow of mass $25\,\mathrm{g}$, initially at rest, over a distance of $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with a force that can be taken as constant and equal to $120\,\mathrm{N}$, directed along the displacement. With what speed does the arrow leave the bow? Weight and friction are negligible.

- $69\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $49\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $98\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The force of the bow does the work $L = F d = 120 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{J} = 60\,\mathrm{J}$. By the **work–energy theorem**, since the arrow starts from rest, $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = 60\,\mathrm{J}$.

The mass must be expressed in kilograms, $m = 0.025\,\mathrm{kg}$, so
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 60}{0.025}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{4800}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 69\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $4.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is $v^2$, before taking the square root.
