A body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is hooked to the free end of an ideal vertical spring with spring constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, initially at its rest length. The body is guided slowly downward by hand to the equilibrium position, where it is left at rest. How much work does the hand do?

- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.9\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.96\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-0.96\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

At the equilibrium position the elastic force balances the weight, so the elongation is $x_e = \dfrac{mg}{k} = \dfrac{2.0 \cdot 9.81}{200}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.098\,\mathrm{m}$.

The body starts and ends at rest, so by the work–energy theorem the sum of the work of the weight, of the elastic force and of the hand is zero. The weight does the work $mg\,x_e \approx 1.92\,\mathrm{J}$ and the elastic force does the work $-\dfrac{1}{2} k x_e^2 \approx -0.96\,\mathrm{J}$, so
$$
L_{\text{hand}} = -mg\,x_e + \frac{1}{2} k x_e^2 = -\frac{(mg)^2}{2k} \approx -0.96\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work of the hand is negative, because the hand pushes upward while the body descends.
