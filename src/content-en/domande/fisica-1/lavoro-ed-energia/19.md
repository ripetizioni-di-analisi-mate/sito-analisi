When an ideal spring is stretched from its rest length to an extension of $2{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the elastic force does the work $-0{,}10\,\mathrm{J}$. How much work does the elastic force do when the extension goes from $2{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $4{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$?

- $-0{,}10\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-0{,}20\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-0{,}40\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-0{,}30\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work of the elastic force between the extensions $x_A$ and $x_B$ is $\dfrac{1}{2} k x_A^2 - \dfrac{1}{2} k x_B^2$, so it depends on the squares of the extensions. The given value yields $\dfrac{1}{2} k\,(2{,}0\,\mathrm{cm})^2 = 0{,}10\,\mathrm{J}$. Since $4{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is twice $2{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$, we have $\dfrac{1}{2} k\,(4{,}0\,\mathrm{cm})^2 = 4 \cdot 0{,}10\,\mathrm{J} = 0{,}40\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore the work over the second stretch is $L = 0{,}10\,\mathrm{J} - 0{,}40\,\mathrm{J} = -0{,}30\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $-0{,}40\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work over the whole extension from $0$ to $4{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$.
