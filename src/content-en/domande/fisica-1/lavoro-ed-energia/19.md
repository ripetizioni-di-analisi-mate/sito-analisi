In a bicycle pump with the outlet closed, the piston is pushed slowly toward the bottom. Let $x$ be the distance of the piston from the bottom: the force that must be exerted on the piston points toward the bottom and has magnitude $F(x) = \dfrac{30}{x}$, with $x$ in meters and $F$ in newtons. How much work does this force do while $x$ goes from $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $15\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-21\,\mathrm{J}$
- $21\,\mathrm{J}$
- $30\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The force points toward the bottom, that is, toward decreasing $x$, so its component is $F_x = -\dfrac{30}{x}$. The work of a variable force is the integral of $F_x$ from the initial position to the final position, therefore
$$
L = \int_{0.30}^{0.15} \left( -\frac{30}{x} \right) dx\,\mathrm{J} = 30 \ln\frac{0.30}{0.15}\,\mathrm{J} = 30 \ln 2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 21\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is positive, because the force and the displacement of the piston have the same direction. The value $30\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by multiplying the final force, $200\,\mathrm{N}$, by the displacement of $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, as if the force were constant.
