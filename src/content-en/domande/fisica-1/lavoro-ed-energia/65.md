A body moves along the $x$ axis and its kinetic energy varies with position as in the graph: it increases linearly from $10\,\mathrm{J}$ at $x = 0$ to $40\,\mathrm{J}$ at $x = 6.0\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the component $R_x$ of the net force acting on the body?

::grafico{linee="0,10 6,40" segmenti="6,0,6,40" xmin=-0.8 xmax=7.2 ymin=-5 ymax=45 nomi="x,K" titolo="Kinetic energy $K$, in joules, as a function of position $x$, in meters"}

- $6.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $30\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the **work–energy theorem**, on any stretch from $x$ to $x + \Delta x$ the work of the net force, $R_x\,\Delta x$, equals the change in kinetic energy $\Delta K$. The component of the net force is therefore the slope of the graph of $K$ as a function of $x$.

The graph is a straight line, so the net force is constant and
$$
R_x = \frac{\Delta K}{\Delta x} = \frac{40 - 10}{6.0}\,\mathrm{N} = 5.0\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $6.7\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by dividing the final kinetic energy, instead of its change, by the displacement.
