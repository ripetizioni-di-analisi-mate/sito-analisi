A person of mass $70\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands still on the floor of an elevator that rises at constant speed through $10\,\mathrm{m}$. How much work does the normal force of the floor do on the person?

- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-6.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $7.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The person moves at constant velocity, so the net force is zero and the normal force balances the weight, $N = mg = 70 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \approx 687\,\mathrm{N}$.

The normal force points upward like the displacement, so it does the positive work
$$
L_N = N\,s = 687 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{J} \approx 6.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The weight does the opposite work, $-6.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$, and the total work is zero, as the work–energy theorem requires. The normal force does zero work only when it is perpendicular to the displacement, as for a body sliding on a fixed surface.
