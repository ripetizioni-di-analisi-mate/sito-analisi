A body moves along the $x$-axis from $x = 0$ to $x = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$, subject to a force with component $F_x(x) = 4{,}0 - 2{,}0\,x$, where $x$ is in meters and $F_x$ in newtons. How much work does the force do?

- $8{,}0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-16\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4{,}0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work is the integral of the component of the force between the two positions,
$$
L = \int_0^{4} (4{,}0 - 2{,}0\,x)\,dx\,\mathrm{J} = \left[ 4{,}0\,x - x^2 \right]_0^{4}\,\mathrm{J} = (16 - 16)\,\mathrm{J} = 0\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The force is positive for $x < 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ and negative for $x > 2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$, so the signed area under the graph is made of two triangles of area $4{,}0\,\mathrm{J}$, the first above the $x$-axis and the second below it. The two contributions have opposite signs, therefore the total work is zero. The sum $8{,}0\,\mathrm{J}$ of the areas taken in absolute value neglects the sign of the work over the second stretch.
