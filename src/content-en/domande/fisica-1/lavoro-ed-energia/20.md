A body moves along the $x$ axis from the position $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ to the position $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$, acted on by a force with component $F_x(x) = 2.0\,x$, where $x$ is in meters and $F_x$ in newtons. How much work does the force do?

- $8.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-4.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-8.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work of a variable force is the integral of $F_x$ from the initial position to the final position, so the lower limit is $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and the upper limit is $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
L = \int_{3}^{1} 2x\,dx\,\mathrm{J} = \left[ x^2 \right]_3^1\,\mathrm{J} = (1 - 9)\,\mathrm{J} = -8.0\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The negative sign has a physical meaning, because on the path traveled the force points toward positive $x$ while the body moves toward negative $x$. The force is therefore **resistive** along the whole displacement.
