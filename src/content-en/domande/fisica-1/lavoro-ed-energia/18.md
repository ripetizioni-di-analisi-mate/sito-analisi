An external force stretches an ideal spring with spring constant $k = 400\,\mathrm{N/m}$ very slowly from its natural length by $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. How much work does the external force do?

- $2{,}0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-2{,}0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4{,}0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $20\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The spring is stretched very slowly, so at every instant the external force balances the elastic force and has component $kx$, in the direction of the stretching. Its work is therefore the opposite of the work of the elastic force,
$$
L_{\text{est}} = \int_0^{0{,}10} k x\,dx = \frac{1}{2} k x^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 400 \cdot 0{,}10^2\,\mathrm{J} = 2{,}0\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is positive, because the external force is in the same direction as the displacement, and it equals the elastic potential energy stored in the spring. The elastic force does the work $-2{,}0\,\mathrm{J}$.
