A ball of mass $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ is thrown with a speed of $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $60^\circ$ above the horizontal, with air resistance negligible. How much work does the weight do from the launch to the instant the ball reaches the highest point of its path?

- $-45\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-34\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-11\,\mathrm{J}$
- $34\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The weight is the only force, so by the **work–energy theorem** its work equals the change in the kinetic energy of the ball.

The horizontal component of the velocity stays constant and equals $15\cos 60^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} = 7.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$; at the highest point the vertical component is zero, so the speed is $7.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Therefore
$$
L_P = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.40 \cdot \left( 7.5^2 - 15^2 \right)\mathrm{J} \approx -34\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $-45\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by assuming that the ball is at rest at the highest point, which holds only for a vertical throw.
