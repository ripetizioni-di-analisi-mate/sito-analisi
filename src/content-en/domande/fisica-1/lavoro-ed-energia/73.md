A ball moves from rest down a frictionless track. The height difference is $h$ and the speed is measured at the bottom. Given gravitational acceleration $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the speed $v = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the height difference $h$?

- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{5}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{15}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Gravity does work $mgh$, so $\frac12 mv^2=mgh$. The mass cancels. In the stated units, the relation is $v = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{g} \cdot \sqrt{h}$. Solving for the unknown gives $h = \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot g}$. Substituting the given values yields $5\,\mathrm{m}$.
