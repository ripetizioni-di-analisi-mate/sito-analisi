A body on a smooth horizontal surface is attached to two ideal horizontal springs whose other ends are fixed. Along the $x$ axis the elongation of the first spring, with spring constant $k_1 = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$, is $x$, and that of the second, with spring constant $k_2 = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, is $x - 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. At what position is the body in equilibrium?

- $x = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $x = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The potential energy of the body is the sum of the elastic potential energies of the two springs:
$$
U(x) = \frac{1}{2} k_1 x^2 + \frac{1}{2} k_2\,(x - 0.30)^2 .
$$
An equilibrium position makes the derivative vanish, $U'(x) = k_1 x + k_2\,(x - 0.30) = 0$, so
$$
x = \frac{0.30\,k_2}{k_1 + k_2} = \frac{0.30 \cdot 200}{300}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Since $U'' = k_1 + k_2 > 0$, the equilibrium is stable. The value $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ would be the equilibrium position with two springs of equal constant.
