A ball moves from rest down a frictionless track. The height difference is $h$ and the speed is measured at the bottom. Given gravitational acceleration $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the height difference $h = 5\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the speed $v$?

- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $10 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Gravity does work $mgh$, so $\frac12 mv^2=mgh$. The mass cancels. In the stated units, the relation is $v = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{g} \cdot \sqrt{h}$. Substituting the given values yields $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
