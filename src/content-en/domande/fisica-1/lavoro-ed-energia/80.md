A small cube of negligible size sits at the top of a smooth, fixed hemisphere of radius $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$, whose flat base rests on a horizontal surface. Given a very small push, the cube starts sliding along the surface. At what height above the center of the hemisphere does it leave the surface?

- $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $\theta$ be the angle between the radius through the cube and the vertical; the cube is at height $R\cos\theta$ above the center. The surface is smooth and the normal force does no work, so by conservation of mechanical energy $v^2 = 2gR\,(1 - \cos\theta)$.

As long as the cube is in contact it moves on a circle of radius $R$, and along the radius $mg\cos\theta - N = \dfrac{m v^2}{R}$. The cube leaves the surface when $N = 0$, so
$$
g\cos\theta = 2g\,(1 - \cos\theta)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\cos\theta = \frac{2}{3} .
$$
The height above the center is therefore $\dfrac{2}{3} R = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$, independent of the mass.
