A charged sphere is fixed at the origin and a second charged sphere moves along the $x$ axis from $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ to $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The repulsive force that the first exerts on the second has component $F_x(x) = \dfrac{8.0}{x^2}$, where $x$ is in meters and $F_x$ in newtons. How much work does this force do?

- $6.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-4.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The force depends on position, so the work is computed as the integral of $F_x$ between the initial and the final position. Since an antiderivative of $\dfrac{8}{x^2}$ is $-\dfrac{8}{x}$,
$$
L = \int_1^2 \frac{8}{x^2}\,dx\,\mathrm{J} = \left[ -\frac{8}{x} \right]_1^2\,\mathrm{J} = (-4 + 8)\,\mathrm{J} = 4.0\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is positive, because the repulsive force points along the displacement, which moves the two spheres apart.
