A block slides from rest to the bottom of a ramp. Friction of constant magnitude $12\,\mathrm{N}$ opposes motion over $3\,\mathrm{m}$. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the height difference $h = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ and the final kinetic energy $K = 84\,\mathrm{J}$, what is the mass $m$?

- $\dfrac{12}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{21}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Gravity does work $mgh$ and friction does $-36\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. The initial kinetic energy is zero. In the stated units, the relation is $K = 10 \cdot h \cdot m - 36$. Solving for the unknown gives $m = \dfrac{K + 36}{10 \cdot h}$. Substituting the given values yields $6\,\mathrm{kg}$.
