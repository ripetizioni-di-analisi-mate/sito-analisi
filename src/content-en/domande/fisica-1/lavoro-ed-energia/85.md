A block slides on a smooth horizontal surface at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and enters a rough stretch in which the coefficient of kinetic friction increases with the distance traveled according to $\mu_d = 0.50\,x$, where $x$ is in meters and is measured from the beginning of the stretch. What distance does the block travel on the rough stretch before stopping?

- $0.92\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.46\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The normal force is $mg$, so friction has magnitude $0.50\,x\, mg$, which varies with position, and opposes the displacement. Its work up to the distance $d$ is
$$
L_{\text{fr}} = -\int_0^d 0.50\, m g\, x\,dx = -0.25\, m g\, d^2 .
$$
By the **work–energy theorem**, since the block stops, $-0.25\, m g\, d^2 = -\dfrac{1}{2} m v_0^2$, so
$$
d = \sqrt{\frac{2 v_0^2}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.0}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.4\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $1.8\,\mathrm{m}$ is $d^2$ in square meters, before taking the square root.
