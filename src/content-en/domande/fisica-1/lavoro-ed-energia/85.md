A body speeds up from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to a higher final speed. The net work is positive. Given the mass $m = 3\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the final speed $v = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the net work $W$?

- $54\,\mathrm{J}$
- $24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $48\,\mathrm{J}$
- $96\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

By the work-energy theorem, net work is final kinetic energy minus initial kinetic energy. In the stated units, the relation is $W = \dfrac{m \cdot \left(v^{2} - 4\right)}{2}$. Substituting the given values yields $48\,\mathrm{J}$.
