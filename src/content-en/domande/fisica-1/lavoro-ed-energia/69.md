A package of mass $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is placed at rest on a horizontal conveyor belt moving at the constant speed of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The package slides for a short stretch and then moves together with the belt. In the ground frame, how much work does the friction force exerted by the belt do on the package?

- $-10\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $20\,\mathrm{J}$
- $10\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

In the ground frame the speed of the package goes from zero to $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The weight and the normal force are vertical and the displacement is horizontal, so the only force doing work is the friction of the belt.

By the **work–energy theorem** the work of friction equals the change in the kinetic energy of the package, therefore
$$
L_{\text{att}} = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 2.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 10\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The work is positive, because while the package slides the belt is faster than the package, and friction, which opposes the velocity of the package relative to the belt, points in the direction of motion of the package relative to the ground.
