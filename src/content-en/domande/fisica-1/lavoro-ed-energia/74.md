A ball moves from rest down a frictionless track. The height difference is $h$ and the speed is measured at the bottom. The initial data are gravitational acceleration $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the height difference $h = 5\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the height difference to $20\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $v$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $1$
- $4$

## Explanation

Gravity does work $mgh$, so $\frac12 mv^2=mgh$. The mass cancels. The relation $v = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{g} \cdot \sqrt{h}$ gives $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before the change and $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after it. The ratio is $2$.
