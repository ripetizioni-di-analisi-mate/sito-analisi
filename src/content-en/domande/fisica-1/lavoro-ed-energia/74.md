A pendulum consists of a small ball of mass $m$ fixed at the end of a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$, free to rotate without friction in a vertical plane about its other end. With $\theta$ the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, what are the equilibrium positions and their stability?

- $\theta = 0$ stable and $\theta = \pi$ unstable
- $\theta = 0$ unstable and $\theta = \pi$ stable
- Only $\theta = 0$, stable
- $\theta = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, both unstable

## Explanation

With zero height at the lowest point, the potential energy of the weight is $U = mg\ell\,(1 - \cos\theta)$. As the coordinate along the path we use the arc length $s = \ell\,\theta$, for which $\dfrac{dU}{ds} = mg\sin\theta$ and $\dfrac{d^2 U}{ds^2} = \dfrac{mg}{\ell}\cos\theta$.

The first derivative vanishes for $\theta = 0$ and for $\theta = \pi$; since the rod is rigid, the ball can also stay above the pivot, so $\theta = \pi$ is an equilibrium position too. By the **stability criterion**, at $\theta = 0$ the second derivative equals $\dfrac{mg}{\ell} > 0$ and the equilibrium is stable, while at $\theta = \pi$ it equals $-\dfrac{mg}{\ell} < 0$ and the equilibrium is unstable.
