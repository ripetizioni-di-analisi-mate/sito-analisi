Every minute a pump lifts $1200$ liters of water from a well to a tank located $15\,\mathrm{m}$ higher, and the water comes out with negligible speed. One liter of water has a mass of $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the minimum power the pump must deliver to the water?

- $2.9\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $1.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{kW}$
- $49\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

In one minute the pump brings $m = 1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water to the height $h = 15\,\mathrm{m}$ without increasing its kinetic energy. The minimum work is therefore the increase in the potential energy of the water, $L = mgh = 1200 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 15\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.77 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$.

The **average power** is the work divided by the time interval, which is $60\,\mathrm{s}$, so
$$
P = \frac{mgh}{\Delta t} = \frac{1.77 \cdot 10^5}{60}\,\mathrm{W} \approx 2.9\,\mathrm{kW} .
$$
The value $1.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kW}$ is obtained by dividing by one second instead of one minute.
