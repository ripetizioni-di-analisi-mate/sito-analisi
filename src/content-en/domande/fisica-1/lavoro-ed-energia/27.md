A bullet of mass $20\,\mathrm{g}$ hits a fixed wooden block at a speed of $400\,\mathrm{m/s}$, penetrates it by $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and stops. What is the magnitude of the force, assumed constant, that the wood exerts on the bullet?

- $3.2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The initial kinetic energy of the bullet is $K = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.020 \cdot 400^2\,\mathrm{J} = 1.6 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$. The force of the wood is opposite to the displacement, so it does the work $-F\,d$, and by the **work-energy theorem** $-F\,d = 0 - K$. With $d = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ this gives
$$
F = \frac{K}{d} = \frac{1.6 \cdot 10^3}{0.10}\,\mathrm{N} = 1.6 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The weight of the bullet, about $0.2\,\mathrm{N}$, is negligible compared with this force.
