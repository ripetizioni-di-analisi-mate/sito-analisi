A ring slides along a horizontal rail and is attached to an ideal spring with spring constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and rest length $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, whose other end is fixed at the point $O$, $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the rail. The ring moves from the point $P$, directly below $O$, to the point $Q$, $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from $P$ along the rail. How much work does the elastic force do?

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-0.3,0 0.75,0" segmenti="0,0.4,0,0;0,0.4,0.3,0" punti="0,0.4,pieno,O;0,0,pieno,P;0.3,0,pieno,Q" xmin=-0.4 xmax=0.85 ymin=-0.12 ymax=0.52 uguale=1 titolo="The spring, dashed, goes from the fixed point $O$ to the ring on the horizontal rail, at $P$ at the start and at $Q$ at the end"}

- $-1.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-9.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-3.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work of the elastic force depends only on the initial and final deformations of the spring, even though the ring does not move along the axis of the spring.

At $P$ the spring is $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ long, so it is stretched by $x_A = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. At $Q$ its length is $\sqrt{0.40^2 + 0.30^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, so the elongation is $x_B = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. By the theorem on the **work of the elastic force**
$$
L = \frac{1}{2} k \left( x_A^2 - x_B^2 \right) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 200 \cdot (0.010 - 0.040)\,\mathrm{J} = -3.0\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $-1.0\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using the difference of the elongations, $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, instead of the difference of their squares.
