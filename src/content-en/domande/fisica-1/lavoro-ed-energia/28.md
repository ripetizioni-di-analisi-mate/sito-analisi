A block of mass $2{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$, initially at rest on a horizontal plane, is pushed by a constant horizontal force of $20\,\mathrm{N}$ for $3{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the plane is $0{,}25$. What is the speed of the block at the end of that distance?

- $6{,}7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7{,}7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4{,}8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $8{,}6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The normal force is $mg$, so friction has magnitude $\mu_d\,mg = 0{,}25 \cdot 2{,}0 \cdot 9{,}81\,\mathrm{N} \approx 4{,}9\,\mathrm{N}$ and is opposite to the displacement. The weight and the normal force do no work, therefore the total work is
$$
L_{\text{tot}} = (F - \mu_d\,mg)\,d \approx (20 - 4{,}9) \cdot 3{,}0\,\mathrm{J} \approx 45\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
By the **work-energy theorem** $L_{\text{tot}} = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$, so $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 L_{\text{tot}}}{m}} \approx \sqrt{45}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 6{,}7\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
