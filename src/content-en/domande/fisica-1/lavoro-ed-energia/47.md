A sailboat rounds a buoy along a semicircle of radius $30\,\mathrm{m}$, from the west end to the east end of a diameter. During the maneuver the wind exerts on the boat a constant force of $200\,\mathrm{N}$ pointing east. How much work does the force of the wind do?

- $1.9 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Along a curved path the work is the sum of the contributions $\vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$ of the small stretches. The force is constant, so it can be taken out of the sum, and the sum of the infinitesimal displacements is the overall displacement $\Delta\vec{r}$, therefore $L = \vec{F} \cdot \Delta\vec{r}$.

The overall displacement is the diameter, $60\,\mathrm{m}$ long and pointing east like the force, so
$$
L = 200 \cdot 60\,\mathrm{J} = 1.2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $1.9 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by multiplying the force by the length of the arc, $\pi R$, but the force is not always tangent to the path.
