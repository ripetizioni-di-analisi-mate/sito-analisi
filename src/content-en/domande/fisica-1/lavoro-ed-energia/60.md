A block compresses an ideal spring on a frictionless surface and momentarily stops at maximum compression. The initial data are the speed $v = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the spring constant $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the mass $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$. Change the speed to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the mass to $9\,\mathrm{kg}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $x$ minus its initial value?

- $- \dfrac{5}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{3 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $- \dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By energy conservation, the initial kinetic energy $\frac12 mv^2$ becomes elastic energy $\frac12 kx^2$. The relation $x = \dfrac{\sqrt{m} \cdot v}{\sqrt{k}}$ gives $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$ before the change and $\dfrac{6}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$.
