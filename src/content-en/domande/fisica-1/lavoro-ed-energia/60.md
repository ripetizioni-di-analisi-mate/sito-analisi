A body of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest at $x = 0$ on a smooth horizontal surface. From that instant a horizontal force with component $F_x(x) = 3\,x^2$ acts on it, where $x$ is in meters and $F_x$ in newtons. How fast is the body moving as it passes $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The weight and the normal force are perpendicular to the displacement, so the total work is that of the horizontal force:
$$
L = \int_0^2 3\,x^2\,dx\,\mathrm{J} = \left[ x^3 \right]_0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 8.0\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
By the **work–energy theorem**, since the body starts from rest, $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = 8.0\,\mathrm{J}$, so
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 8.0}{2.0}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.8\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $4.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by using the work $F_x(2) \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m} = 24\,\mathrm{J}$, as if the force were constant.
