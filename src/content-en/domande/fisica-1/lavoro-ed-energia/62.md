A bucket of mass $8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, initially at rest, is lifted vertically by a rope that exerts a constant force of $100\,\mathrm{N}$. How fast is the bucket moving after rising $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $8.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The forces on the bucket are the tension of the rope, pointing upward, and the weight, $mg \approx 78.5\,\mathrm{N}$, pointing downward. The total work over the upward displacement of $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is therefore
$$
L_{\text{tot}} = (100 - 78.5) \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{J} \approx 64.6\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
By the **work–energy theorem**, since the bucket starts from rest, $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = L_{\text{tot}}$, so
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 64.6}{8.0}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $8.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by forgetting the negative work of the weight.
