A horse walking along the bank tows a barge along a straight canal at a constant speed of $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The rope is under a tension of $600\,\mathrm{N}$ and makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the direction of motion of the barge. What power does the tension of the rope deliver to the barge?

- $9.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$
- $4.5 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$
- $7.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

The instantaneous power of a force is the dot product of the force and the velocity of the point on which it acts, $P = \vec{T} \cdot \vec{v} = T\,v\cos\theta$.

The angle between the rope and the velocity of the barge is $\theta = 30^\circ$, so
$$
P = 600 \cdot 1.5 \cdot \cos 30^\circ\,\mathrm{W} \approx 7.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
Only the component of the tension parallel to the motion, $T\cos 30^\circ \approx 520\,\mathrm{N}$, contributes to the power, because the perpendicular component is perpendicular to the velocity.
