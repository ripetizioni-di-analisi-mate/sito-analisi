A ball moves from rest down a frictionless track. The height difference is $h$ and the speed is measured at the bottom. Given the height difference $h = 5\,\mathrm{m}$ and the speed $v = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is gravitational acceleration $g$?

- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Gravity does work $mgh$, so $\frac12 mv^2=mgh$. The mass cancels. In the stated units, the relation is $v = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{g} \cdot \sqrt{h}$. Solving for the unknown gives $g = \dfrac{v^{2}}{2 \cdot h}$. Substituting the given values yields $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
