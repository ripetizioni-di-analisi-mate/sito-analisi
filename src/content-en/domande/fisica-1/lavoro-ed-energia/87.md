A crate of mass $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ is dragged with a horizontal force across a horizontal floor at a constant speed of $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The coefficient of kinetic friction between crate and floor is $0.35$. How much energy does friction dissipate each second?

- $1.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $7.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $69\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The energy dissipated each second is the magnitude of the power of friction. Friction has magnitude $f = \mu_d\, mg = 0.35 \cdot 40 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \approx 137\,\mathrm{N}$ and opposes the velocity.

By the relation between power and velocity
$$
|P| = f\,v = 137 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{W} \approx 69\,\mathrm{W} ,
$$
so friction dissipates about $69\,\mathrm{J}$ per second. The value $1.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$ is the magnitude of the friction force, without the factor of the speed.
