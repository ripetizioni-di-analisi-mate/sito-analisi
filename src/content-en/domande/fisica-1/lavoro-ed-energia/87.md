A body speeds up from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to a higher final speed. The net work is positive. Given the mass $m = 3\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the net work $W = 48\,\mathrm{J}$, what is the final speed $v$?

- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2 + 4 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By the work-energy theorem, net work is final kinetic energy minus initial kinetic energy. In the stated units, the relation is $W = \dfrac{m \cdot \left(v^{2} - 4\right)}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $v = \dfrac{\sqrt{2 \cdot W + 4 \cdot m}}{\sqrt{m}}$. Substituting the given values yields $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
