A car moves at constant velocity on a horizontal road. The air resistance has magnitude proportional to the square of the speed, and the other resistive forces are negligible. How does the power required from the engine change if the speed doubles?

- It doubles
- It becomes eight times as large
- It quadruples
- It stays the same

## Explanation

At constant velocity the driving force balances the resistive force, so it has magnitude $F = c\,v^2$, with $c$ constant. By the relation between **power and velocity**, with force and velocity parallel, $P = F\,v = c\,v^3$. Therefore, when the speed doubles, the power is multiplied by $2^3 = 8$. The driving force is multiplied by $2^2 = 4$ instead.
