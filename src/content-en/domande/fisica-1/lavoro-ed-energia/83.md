A trunk of mass $30\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pushed at constant velocity from the bottom to the top of a rough ramp $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long and inclined at $25^\circ$, by a force parallel to the ramp. The coefficient of kinetic friction between trunk and ramp is $0.40$. How much work does the force pushing the trunk do?

- $6.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $5.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The velocity is constant, so by the **work–energy theorem** the total work of the forces acting on the trunk is zero. The normal force is perpendicular to the displacement and does no work.

The trunk rises by $h = d\sin\alpha$, therefore the weight does the work $-mg\,d\sin\alpha \approx -622\,\mathrm{J}$. The normal force is $mg\cos\alpha$, so friction does the work $-\mu_d\,mg\cos\alpha\,d \approx -533\,\mathrm{J}$. The work of the pushing force is the opposite of the sum of these two works:
$$
L_F = mg\,d\,(\sin\alpha + \mu_d\cos\alpha) = 30 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 5.0 \cdot (0.423 + 0.40 \cdot 0.906)\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $6.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$ is the increase in the potential energy of the trunk, and is obtained by neglecting friction.
