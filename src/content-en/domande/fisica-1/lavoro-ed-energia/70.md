A cart runs along a straight track in the region $x > 0$, and the only force doing work on it derives from the potential energy $U(x) = x + \dfrac{4}{x}$, where $x$ is in meters and $U$ in joules. Near the stable equilibrium position this force can be approximated by that of a spring. What is the spring constant of this equivalent spring?

- $4.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$

## Explanation

The equilibrium positions are the zeros of $U'(x) = 1 - \dfrac{4}{x^2}$, and in the region $x > 0$ the only one is $x_0 = 2\,\mathrm{m}$.

The second derivative is $U''(x) = \dfrac{8}{x^3}$, so $U''(2) = 1\,\mathrm{N/m} > 0$ and, by the **stability criterion**, $x_0 = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ is a position of stable equilibrium. Near $x_0$ the force is $F_x \approx -U''(x_0)\,(x - x_0)$, so the equivalent spring constant is $U''(x_0) = 1.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$.

The value $8.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is the second derivative evaluated at $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$, which is not an equilibrium position.
