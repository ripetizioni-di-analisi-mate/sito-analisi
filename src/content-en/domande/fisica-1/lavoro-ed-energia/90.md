A crate of mass $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ is dragged across a horizontal floor along a semicircle of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, from one end of the diameter to the other. The coefficient of kinetic friction between crate and floor is $0.40$. How much work does friction do?

- $-2.5 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-78\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

On the horizontal floor the normal force is $mg$, so friction has constant magnitude $\mu_d\, mg = 0.40 \cdot 10 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \approx 39.2\,\mathrm{N}$ and at every instant opposes the displacement. Its work is therefore the product of $-\mu_d\, mg$ and the length of the path, $\pi R \approx 6.28\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
L_{\text{fr}} = -\mu_d\, mg\,\pi R \approx -39.2 \cdot 6.28\,\mathrm{J} \approx -2.5 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $-1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work along the diameter, which is $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ long; the difference shows that friction is not a conservative force.
