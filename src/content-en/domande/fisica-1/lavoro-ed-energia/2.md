A block of mass $5{,}0\,\mathrm{kg}$ starts from rest and slides for $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$ down a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$. What is its speed at the end of the slide?

- $6{,}3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3{,}1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5{,}8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4{,}4\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The drop in height is $h = d\sin\alpha = 2{,}0 \cdot \sin 30^\circ\,\mathrm{m} = 1{,}0\,\mathrm{m}$. The normal force is perpendicular to the displacement and does no work, so the total work is that of the weight, $L_P = mgh \approx 49\,\mathrm{J}$. Since the initial speed is zero, the **work-energy theorem** gives $mgh = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$. Therefore
$$
v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9{,}81 \cdot 1{,}0}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 4{,}4\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The mass cancels out and does not affect the result.
