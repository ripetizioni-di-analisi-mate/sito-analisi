A block of mass $5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ starts from rest and slides for $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ down a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$. With what speed does it reach the end of this stretch?

- $6.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The drop in height is $h = d\sin\alpha = 2.0 \cdot \sin 30^\circ\,\mathrm{m} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The normal force is perpendicular to the displacement and does no work, so the total work is that of the weight, $L_P = mgh \approx 49\,\mathrm{J}$. By the **work-energy theorem**, with zero initial speed, $mgh = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$, therefore
$$
v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 4.4\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The mass cancels out and does not affect the result.
