A block slides from rest to the bottom of a ramp. Friction of constant magnitude $12\,\mathrm{N}$ opposes motion over $3\,\mathrm{m}$. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the mass $m = 6\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the final kinetic energy $K = 84\,\mathrm{J}$, what is the height difference $h$?

- $\dfrac{4}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{7}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The work done by gravity is $mgh$, and the work done by friction is $-36\,\mathrm{J}$. The initial kinetic energy is zero. In the stated units, the relation is $K = 10 \cdot h \cdot m - 36$. Solving for the unknown gives $h = \dfrac{K + 36}{10 \cdot m}$. Substituting the given values yields $2\,\mathrm{m}$.
