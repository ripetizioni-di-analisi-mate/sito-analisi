A jumper is tied to an elastic cord whose rest length is $10\,\mathrm{m}$, with the other end fixed to a bridge. Let $x$ be the distance of the jumper below the anchor point: the cord stays slack as long as $x \leq 10\,\mathrm{m}$ and then exerts an upward force of magnitude $k\,(x - 10\,\mathrm{m})$, with $k = 80\,\mathrm{N/m}$, as in the graph. How much work does the cord do while the jumper descends from $x = 0$ to $x = 15\,\mathrm{m}$?

::grafico{linee="0,0 10,0 15,-400" segmenti="15,0,15,-400" xmin=-2 xmax=17 ymin=-480 ymax=120 nomi="x,F" titolo="Component $F_x$ of the force of the cord, in newtons, with the $x$ axis pointing down, as a function of the distance $x$ from the anchor point, in meters"}

- $-9.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

With the $x$ axis pointing downward, the component of the force of the cord is $F_x = 0$ for $x \leq 10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $F_x = -k\,(x - 10)$ for $x > 10\,\mathrm{m}$. The work is the integral of $F_x$ between the initial and the final position, and on the first stretch it is zero.

On the second stretch the cord lengthens from $0$ to $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
L = \int_{10}^{15} -80\,(x - 10)\,dx\,\mathrm{J} = -\frac{1}{2} \cdot 80 \cdot 5.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = -1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} ,
$$
which is the signed area of the triangle below the axis. The work is negative because the force points upward while the jumper descends. The value $-9.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using the whole distance of $15\,\mathrm{m}$ as the elongation.
