A car of mass $1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ travels on a horizontal road at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. From that instant the engine delivers a constant power of $30\,\mathrm{kW}$ to the wheels for $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Neglecting resistive forces, what speed does the car reach?

- $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $23\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $19\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The power is constant, so the work of the driving force in the $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$ is $L = P\,\Delta t = 3.0 \cdot 10^4 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{J} = 1.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$. The other forces do no work, so this is the total work.

The initial kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1200 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J} = 6.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$, and by the **work–energy theorem** the final one is $K = (6.0 + 15) \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} = 2.1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2K}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 2.1 \cdot 10^5}{1200}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 19\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by neglecting the initial kinetic energy.
