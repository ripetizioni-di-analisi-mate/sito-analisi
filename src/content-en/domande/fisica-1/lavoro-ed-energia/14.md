A constant force $\vec{F} = (6{,}0\,\hat{\imath} + 2{,}0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on a point particle that undergoes the straight-line displacement $\vec{s} = (3{,}0\,\hat{\imath} - 4{,}0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$. How much work does the force do?

- $26\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-18\,\mathrm{J}$
- $10\,\mathrm{J}$
- $32\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

For a constant force and a straight-line displacement the work is the dot product $L = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s}$, which in Cartesian components is $F_x\,\Delta x + F_y\,\Delta y$. Therefore
$$
L = \left( 6{,}0 \cdot 3{,}0 + 2{,}0 \cdot (-4{,}0) \right)\mathrm{J} = (18 - 8{,}0)\,\mathrm{J} = 10\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The second term is negative, because the component $F_y$ points in the direction opposite to the component $\Delta y$ of the displacement. The product of the magnitudes $F\,s \approx 32\,\mathrm{J}$ coincides with the work only when force and displacement are parallel and in the same direction.
