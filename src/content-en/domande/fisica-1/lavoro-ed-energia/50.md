The graph shows the power $P$ delivered by a motor during the first $30\,\mathrm{s}$ of operation: in the first $10\,\mathrm{s}$ it increases linearly from $0$ to $4.0\,\mathrm{kW}$, then it stays constant. How much work does the motor do in the $30\,\mathrm{s}$?

::grafico{linee="0,0 10,4 30,4" segmenti="10,0,10,4;30,0,30,4" xmin=-5 xmax=36 ymin=-0.6 ymax=5 nomi="t,P" titolo="Power $P$, in kilowatts, as a function of time $t$, in seconds"}

- $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Since power is the derivative of work with respect to time, the work done in an interval is the integral of the power, that is, the area under the graph of $P$ as a function of $t$.

In the first $10\,\mathrm{s}$ the area is that of the triangle, $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} = 2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. In the following $20\,\mathrm{s}$ it is that of the rectangle, $20 \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} = 8.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. So the total work is $L = 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$.

The value $1.2 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by taking the power equal to $4.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ for the whole $30\,\mathrm{s}$.
