In a model of the bond between two atoms, one atom is fixed and the other, at distance $x > 0$, has potential energy $U(x) = \dfrac{2}{x^2} - \dfrac{4}{x}$, where $x$ is in meters and $U$ in joules; only the associated force acts on the movable atom. The movable atom is at rest at the stable equilibrium position. What is the minimum energy that must be supplied to it so that it can move away indefinitely?

- $2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1\,\mathrm{J}$
- No finite energy is sufficient

## Explanation

The derivative is $U'(x) = -\dfrac{4}{x^3} + \dfrac{4}{x^2} = \dfrac{4\,(x - 1)}{x^3}$, which vanishes only at $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$. Since $U' < 0$ for $x < 1$ and $U' > 0$ for $x > 1$, the position $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ is a minimum of $U$ and therefore a position of **stable equilibrium**, with $U(1) = -2\,\mathrm{J}$.

For $x > 1$ the potential energy increases and tends to $0$ as $x \to +\infty$, remaining negative. The atom can therefore move away indefinitely if and only if its mechanical energy is at least $0$, because then $K = E - U(x) > 0$ at every point to the right of the minimum.

The initial mechanical energy is $U(1) = -2\,\mathrm{J}$, so the minimum energy to supply is $0 - (-2\,\mathrm{J}) = 2\,\mathrm{J}$.
