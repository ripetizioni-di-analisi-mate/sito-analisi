A worker turns a capstan by pushing the end of a bar, which describes a circle of radius $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The worker's force is always tangent to the circle and in the direction of motion, and its magnitude grows in proportion to the angle described, from $200\,\mathrm{N}$ at the start to $400\,\mathrm{N}$ after one full turn. How much work does the worker do in this turn?

- $2.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The force is always tangent to the path and in the direction of the displacement, so on an arc of length $ds$ it does the work $F\,ds$. The arc traveled is $s = R\theta$, therefore the magnitude of the force also grows linearly with $s$, from $200\,\mathrm{N}$ at $s = 0$ to $400\,\mathrm{N}$ at $s = 2\pi R$.

The work is hence the area of the trapezoid under the graph of $F$ as a function of $s$:
$$
L = \int_0^{2\pi R} F\,ds = \frac{200 + 400}{2} \cdot 2\pi \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{J} = 900\pi\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The end of the bar returns to its initial position, but the work is not zero, because the force always points in the direction of motion and is therefore not conservative. The value $3.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by using the final force for the whole turn.
