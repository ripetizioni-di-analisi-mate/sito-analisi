A block slides from rest to the bottom of a ramp. Friction of constant magnitude $12\,\mathrm{N}$ opposes motion over $3\,\mathrm{m}$. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The initial data are the mass $m = 6\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the height difference $h = 2\,\mathrm{m}$. Change the mass to $8\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the height difference to $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $K$ minus its initial value?

- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $84\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Gravity does work $mgh$ and friction does $-36\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. The initial kinetic energy is zero. The relation $K = 10 \cdot h \cdot m - 36$ gives $84\,\mathrm{J}$ before the change and $84\,\mathrm{J}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $0\,\mathrm{J}$.
