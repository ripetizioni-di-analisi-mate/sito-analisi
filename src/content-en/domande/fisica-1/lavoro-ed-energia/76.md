A bead can slide without friction along a straight wire, taken as the $x$-axis, and is acted on by a conservative force with potential energy $U(x) = (x - 2)^4 + 3$, where $x$ is in meters and $U$ in joules. Which statement about the position $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ is true?

- It is a position of unstable equilibrium, because $U''(2) = 0$
- It is an equilibrium position, but its stability cannot be determined
- It is a position of stable equilibrium
- It is not an equilibrium position, because $U''(2) = 0$

## Explanation

The derivative is $U'(x) = 4\,(x - 2)^3$, which vanishes at $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, so $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ is an equilibrium position.

The second derivative is $U''(x) = 12\,(x - 2)^2$, and since $U''(2) = 0$ the **stability criterion** does not apply. One then studies the sign of the force $F_x = -U'(x) = -4\,(x - 2)^3$. For $x > 2\,\mathrm{m}$ the force is negative and for $x < 2\,\mathrm{m}$ it is positive, so near $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ the force always points toward this position.

Therefore the equilibrium at $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ is **stable**. The same result follows from the fact that $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ is the minimum point of $U$, where $U$ equals $3\,\mathrm{J}$.
