A block compresses an ideal spring on a frictionless surface and momentarily stops at maximum compression. Given the speed $v = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the spring constant $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the mass $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$, what is the maximum compression $x$?

- $\dfrac{6}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{3 \cdot \sqrt{2}}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By energy conservation, the initial kinetic energy $\frac12 mv^2$ becomes elastic energy $\frac12 kx^2$. In the stated units, the relation is $x = \dfrac{\sqrt{m} \cdot v}{\sqrt{k}}$. Substituting the given values yields $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$.
