In the $xy$ plane the force $\vec{F} = x\,\hat{\jmath}$ acts, with $F$ in newtons and $x$ in meters. A particle goes from the origin to the point $(2.0\,\mathrm{m};\ 2.0\,\mathrm{m})$, traveling first along the $x$ axis to $(2.0\,\mathrm{m};\ 0)$ and then along the vertical segment to the endpoint. How much work does the force do along this path?

- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work along a path is the sum of the contributions $\vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = F_x\,dx + F_y\,dy = x\,dy$, because $F_x = 0$ and $F_y = x$.

On the first segment $y$ stays equal to zero, so $dy = 0$ and the work is zero. On the second segment $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is constant and $y$ goes from $0$ to $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
L = \int_0^2 2\,dy\,\mathrm{J} = 4.0\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
Along the path that first goes up the $y$ axis, where $x = 0$, and then continues horizontally, the work is instead zero; the work therefore depends on the path, and the force is **not conservative**.
