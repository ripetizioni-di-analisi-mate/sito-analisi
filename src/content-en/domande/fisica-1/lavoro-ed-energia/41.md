A ball of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is dropped from rest from a height of $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and, after bouncing on the floor, rises again to $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting air resistance, how much mechanical energy is dissipated in the bounce?

- $7.4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $9.8\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

At the instant of release and at the highest point after the bounce the ball is at rest, so its mechanical energy is only potential energy. With zero height at the floor the mechanical energy goes from $mgh_1 = 0.50 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{J} \approx 9.8\,\mathrm{J}$ to $mgh_2 = 0.50 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{J} \approx 7.4\,\mathrm{J}$. Air resistance is negligible, therefore the energy dissipated in the bounce is
$$
E_{\text{diss}} = mg\,(h_1 - h_2) = 0.50 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.5\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
