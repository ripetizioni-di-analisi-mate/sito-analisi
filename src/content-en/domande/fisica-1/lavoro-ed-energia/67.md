A skydiver with a total mass of $80\,\mathrm{kg}$ opens the parachute and, while descending $40\,\mathrm{m}$ vertically, slows from $50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How much work does air resistance do over this stretch?

- $-9.9 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-6.7 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-1.3 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The forces on the skydiver are the weight and air resistance. By the **work–energy theorem** the sum of their work equals the change in kinetic energy:
$$
\Delta K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 80 \cdot \left( 6.0^2 - 50^2 \right)\mathrm{J} \approx -9.86 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
During the descent of $40\,\mathrm{m}$ the weight does the positive work $80 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3.14 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$. Therefore air resistance does the work
$$
L_{\text{air}} = \Delta K - L_P \approx (-9.86 - 3.14) \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} \approx -1.3 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $-9.9 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}$ is the change in kinetic energy, which also includes the contribution of the weight.
