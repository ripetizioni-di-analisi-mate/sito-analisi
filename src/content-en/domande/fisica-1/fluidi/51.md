In a hydraulic lift a car of mass $1500\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on the large piston, of area $0.20\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and negligible mass. What gauge pressure must the oil under the piston have to hold the car in equilibrium?

- $7.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $7.4 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.7 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Above the piston act the weight of the car and atmospheric pressure, below it acts the oil; the contribution of atmospheric pressure cancels, so the gauge pressure $p$ of the oil is what counts.
Equilibrium of the piston requires $p A = m g$, therefore
$$
p = \frac{m g}{A} = \frac{1500 \cdot 9.81}{0.20}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 7.4 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $7.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$ divides the mass by the area, without the factor $g$. The value $1.7 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is the absolute pressure, which also includes $p_0$.
