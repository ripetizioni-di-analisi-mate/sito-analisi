A buoy of volume $10\,\mathrm{L}$ and mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is tied with a rope to the bottom of a swimming pool and stays completely under water. What is the tension in the rope, which is vertical and of negligible mass?

- $98\,\mathrm{N}$
- $78\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

On the buoy act the buoyant force upward, the weight downward and the tension of the rope, also downward because the rope pulls toward the bottom.
By **Archimedes' principle** the buoyant force is $S = \rho V g = 1000 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}$, and the weight is $mg = 19.6\,\mathrm{N}$.
The buoy is in equilibrium, so
$$
T = S - m g = (98.1 - 19.6)\,\mathrm{N} = 78\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $98\,\mathrm{N}$ is the buoyant force alone, and $1.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$ adds the weight to the buoyant force instead of subtracting it.
