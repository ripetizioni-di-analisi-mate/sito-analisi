A hot-air balloon has an envelope of volume $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The outside air has density $1.20\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, the hot air inside the envelope $0.95\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The volume of the basket and of the envelope fabric is neglected. What is the maximum total mass of fabric, basket and passengers that the balloon can lift?

- $2.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $4.3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

By **Archimedes' principle** the outside air exerts on the balloon a buoyant force equal to the weight of the displaced outside air, $\rho_o V g$.
The buoyant force must support the weight of the hot air inside, $\rho_i V g$, and the weight $M g$ of fabric, basket and passengers.
The maximum mass is reached when the buoyant force equals the total weight, that is $\rho_o V g = \rho_i V g + M g$, therefore
$$
M = (\rho_o - \rho_i)\, V = (1.20 - 0.95) \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg} = 5.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $2.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$ is the mass of the displaced air, which neglects the weight of the hot air inside the envelope.
