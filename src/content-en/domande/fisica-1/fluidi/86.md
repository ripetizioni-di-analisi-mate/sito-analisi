A sealed tank contains a liquid at rest. The gas pressure above it is greater than atmospheric pressure. Assume $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and a liquid density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Given the depth below the surface $h = 3\,\mathrm{m}$ and the absolute pressure at the submerged point $p = 150\,\mathrm{kPa}$, what is the absolute gas pressure $p_{0}$?

- $180\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $120\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $240\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $60\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

At this density, each meter of depth adds $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The starting pressure is the absolute gas pressure. In the stated units, the relation is $p = 10 \cdot h + p_{0}$. Solving for the unknown gives $p_{0} = - 10 \cdot h + p$. Substituting the given values yields $120\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
