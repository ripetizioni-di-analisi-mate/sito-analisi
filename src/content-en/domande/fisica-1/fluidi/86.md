In a cylindrical pipe water flows with Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re} = 1500$. Which of the following changes, leaving everything else unchanged, takes the flow beyond the limit of the laminar regime?

- Doubling the diameter of the pipe, keeping the same flow rate
- Doubling the average speed of the water
- Halving the average speed of the water
- Replacing the water with a more viscous oil, at the same speed

## Explanation

The **Reynolds number** is $\mathrm{Re} = \dfrac{\rho v D}{\eta}$, and in cylindrical pipes the flow is laminar for $\mathrm{Re}$ less than about $2000$.
Doubling the average speed doubles the Reynolds number, which becomes $3000$, so the flow goes beyond the limit of the laminar regime.
At the same flow rate the average speed is inversely proportional to the area of the cross section, that is, to the square of the diameter, therefore doubling the diameter makes the speed a quarter and halves $\mathrm{Re}$.
Halving the speed halves $\mathrm{Re}$, and a more viscous oil increases $\eta$ in the denominator, so in these cases the Reynolds number decreases.
