A horizontal conical pipe narrows uniformly: the diameter goes from $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ at cross-section $1$ to $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ at cross-section $2$, so at cross-section $M$, halfway, the diameter is $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. At cross-section $1$ the water flows at $0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Taking the speed as uniform over each cross-section, what is the speed at cross-section $M$?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,1 2,1 6,0.5 8,0.5;0,-1 2,-1 6,-0.5 8,-0.5" poligoni="0,1 2,1 6,0.5 8,0.5 8,-0.5 6,-0.5 2,-1 0,-1" segmenti="2,-1,2,1;4,-0.75,4,0.75;6,-0.5,6,0.5" vettori="0.2,0.35,1.2,0.35,v₁" etichette="2,1.3,1;4,1.05,M;6,0.8,2" xmin=0 xmax=8 ymin=-1.4 ymax=1.5 titolo="Conical pipe: the diameter decreases linearly from cross-section $1$ to cross-section $2$"}

- $2.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.51\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By the **continuity equation** the flow rate is the same at every cross-section, $A_1 v_1 = A_M v_M$.
The areas are proportional to the square of the diameters, so
$$
v_M = \left( \frac{d_1}{d_M} \right)^2 v_1 = \left( \frac{4.0}{3.0} \right)^2 \cdot 0.90\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.6\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The diameter varies linearly along the pipe, but the speed does not, because it depends on the inverse of the square of the diameter.
The value $2.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the average of $0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the speed $3.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at cross-section $2$, which would be correct only if the speed varied linearly.
