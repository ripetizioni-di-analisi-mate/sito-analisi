A balloon filled with helium has volume $0.50\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and is in air, of density $1.2\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$; the helium has density $0.18\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. What is the buoyant force that the air exerts on the balloon?

- $0.88\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.60\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.9\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

**Archimedes' principle** applies to every fluid, so also to air. The buoyant force equals the weight of the displaced air and is computed with the density of the air, therefore
$$
S = \rho_{\mathrm{air}}\, V g = 1.2 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 5.9\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The density of the helium is used to compute the weight of the gas contained in the balloon, $0.88\,\mathrm{N}$, which is less than the buoyant force.
