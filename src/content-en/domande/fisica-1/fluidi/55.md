An ideal hydraulic press has pistons at the same height. The large piston has area $80\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Neglect the weights of the pistons. The initial data are the force on the small piston $f = 100\,\mathrm{N}$ and the area of the small piston $A = 10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Change the force on the small piston to $80\,\mathrm{N}$ and the area of the small piston to $5\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $F$ minus its initial value?

- $480\,\mathrm{N}$
- $240\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-480\,\mathrm{N}$
- $- \dfrac{15}{2}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The pressure increase is the same below both pistons. The force ratio therefore equals the area ratio. The relation $F = \dfrac{80 \cdot f}{A}$ gives $800\,\mathrm{N}$ before the change and $1280\,\mathrm{N}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $480\,\mathrm{N}$.
