A closed container full of water has a piston of area $10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ at the top and, on its side, a vertical tube open at the top. With no force on the piston, the water in the tube reaches the height of the lower face of the piston. The piston is pushed down with a force of $20\,\mathrm{N}$. Neglecting the displacement of the piston, by how much does the water rise in the tube above that height?

- $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The force on the piston increases the pressure of the water below it by $\Delta p = \dfrac{F}{A} = \dfrac{20}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
By **Pascal's principle** the same increase is transmitted to every point of the water, including the point of the tube at the height of the piston.
The tube is open, so at equilibrium the water rises in it to a height $h$ such that, by the **hydrostatic pressure law**, $\rho g h = \Delta p$:
$$
h = \frac{\Delta p}{\rho g} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^4}{1000 \cdot 9.81}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ uses as area $1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ instead of $1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and $20\,\mathrm{m}$ forgets the factor $g$.
