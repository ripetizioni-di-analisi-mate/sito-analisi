A viscous liquid is in laminar flow through a horizontal pipe. The pipe is replaced with one of the same length and half the radius, and the flow remains laminar. How does the pressure difference between the ends needed to obtain the same flow rate change?

- It doubles
- It becomes $4$ times greater
- It becomes $16$ times greater
- It becomes $16$ times smaller

## Explanation

By **Poiseuille's law** $Q = \dfrac{\pi R^4\, \Delta p}{8 \eta L}$, so for the same flow rate, viscosity and length the product $R^4\, \Delta p$ stays constant. Halving the radius makes $R^4$ equal to $\dfrac{1}{16}$ of its initial value. Therefore the required pressure difference becomes **16 times greater**.
