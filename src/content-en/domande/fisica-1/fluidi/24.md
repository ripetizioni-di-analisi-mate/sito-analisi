A closed container, completely full of water, has a piston at the top. Pushing the piston increases the pressure of the water just below the piston by $2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$. By how much does the pressure increase at a point on the bottom, $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ lower?

- $2.5 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

By **Pascal's principle** a change in pressure produced at a point of a liquid at rest in a closed container is transmitted unchanged to every point of the liquid. The difference $\rho g h$ between the pressure on the bottom and the pressure below the piston depends only on the difference in height, so it is the same before and after the push. Therefore the pressure on the bottom increases by $2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The value $2.5 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$ adds to the increase the pressure $\rho g h = 4.9 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$ of the column, which was already present before the push.
