A wooden block of volume $2.0\,\mathrm{dm^3}$ and density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is in fresh water. A piece of steel of density $7800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, also immersed, is hung below the block with a string of negligible mass and volume. What mass of steel keeps the block in equilibrium completely immersed?

- $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.92\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.12\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The completely immersed block receives a buoyant force that exceeds its weight by $(\rho_w - \rho_b)\, V_b\, g = (1000 - 600) \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\, g = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot g$.
This excess must be balanced by the apparent weight of the immersed steel, $(\rho_s - \rho_w)\, V_s\, g$.
From equilibrium $(\rho_s - \rho_w)\, V_s = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ we obtain $V_s = \dfrac{0.80}{6800}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 1.18 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, so
$$
m_s = \rho_s V_s = 7800 \cdot 1.18 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{kg} = 0.92\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ neglects the buoyant force that the water exerts on the steel.
