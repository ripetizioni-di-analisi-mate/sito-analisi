Flow in a cylindrical capillary is laminar and satisfies Poiseuille's assumptions. Initially the flow rate is $2\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ and the radius is $1\,\mathrm{mm}$. Length and viscosity stay constant. Given the new-to-old pressure-drop ratio $b = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the new flow rate $Q = 16\,\mathrm{mL/s}$, what is the new radius $r$?

- $1\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $16\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $2\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The Poiseuille flow rate is proportional to the fourth power of radius and to the pressure drop. In the stated units, the relation is $Q = 2 \cdot b \cdot r^{4}$. Solving for the unknown gives $r = \dfrac{2^{\dfrac{3}{4}} \cdot \sqrt[4]{Q}}{2 \cdot \sqrt[4]{b}}$. Substituting the given values yields $2\,\mathrm{mm}$.
