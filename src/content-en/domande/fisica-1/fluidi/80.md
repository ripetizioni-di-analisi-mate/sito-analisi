The wind blows at $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ parallel to the flat roof of a house, of area $1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Under the roof the air is still at atmospheric pressure. Treating air ($\rho = 1.20\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) as an ideal fluid and neglecting differences in height, what is the resultant pressure force on the roof?

- $1.1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.6 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$
- $5.4 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The air flowing above the roof comes from regions where it is at rest at pressure $p_0$, so by **Bernoulli's theorem** the pressure above the roof is $p_0 - \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$.
Under the roof the pressure is $p_0$, therefore the pressure difference is $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1.20 \cdot 30^2\,\mathrm{Pa} = 540\,\mathrm{Pa}$, with the higher pressure underneath.
The resultant force is directed upward and is
$$
F = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 A = 540 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N} = 5.4 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $1.1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{N}$ forgets the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $5.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$ is the pressure difference alone, not multiplied by the area.
