- $2{,}2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2{,}2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0{,}11\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

By **Pascal's principle** the pressure $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1}$ produced on the small piston also acts on the large piston, which therefore supports the force $F_2 = \dfrac{A_2}{A_1}\,F_1$. The areas are in the ratio of the squares of the radii, therefore $\dfrac{A_2}{A_1} = \left( \dfrac{18}{1{,}5} \right)^2 = 144$ and $F_2 = 144 \cdot 150\,\mathrm{N} = 2{,}16 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$. The large piston supports the mass whose weight is $F_2$, so
$$
m = \frac{F_2}{g} = \frac{2{,}16 \cdot 10^4}{9{,}81}\,\mathrm{kg} = 2{,}2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $2{,}2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kg}$ is the number that expresses the force in newtons, not divided by $g$.
