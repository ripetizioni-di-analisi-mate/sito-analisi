A submarine is in equilibrium, completely submerged. At some instant it fills some internal tanks, which previously contained air, with sea water, without changing its external volume. What happens?

- The buoyant force increases, so the submarine goes up
- The weight and the buoyant force increase by the same amount, so the submarine stays in equilibrium
- The weight increases and the buoyant force stays the same, so the submarine goes down
- The buoyant force decreases and the weight stays the same, so the submarine goes down

## Explanation

By **Archimedes' principle** the buoyant force on the completely submerged submarine is $\rho V g$, where $V$ is its external volume.
The external volume does not change, so the buoyant force stays the same.
The water that entered the tanks increases the mass of the submarine, therefore the weight exceeds the buoyant force and the net force is directed downward.
In terms of average density, the submarine becomes denser than sea water, so it goes down.
