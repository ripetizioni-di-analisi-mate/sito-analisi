Two sheets of paper hang vertically and parallel, a few centimeters apart. If one blows into the space between the two sheets, the sheets move closer together. Which statement explains the phenomenon?

- The moving air between the sheets has a lower pressure than the still air outside
- The moving air between the sheets has a higher pressure than the still air outside
- The blown air lowers the density of the air outside the sheets
- The moving air drags the sheets toward the center because of viscosity

## Explanation

By **Bernoulli's theorem**, along a horizontal flow tube the sum $p + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$ is constant.
The blown air comes from the mouth, where it is at rest at a pressure approximately equal to $p_0$, and between the sheets it has acquired a speed $v$, so its pressure is approximately $p_0 - \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$, lower than $p_0$.
On the outer faces of the sheets acts still air at pressure $p_0$.
Therefore on each sheet the resultant pressure force is directed toward the space between the sheets, which move closer together.
