A horizontal pipe carries water. Two taps, at points $1$ and $2$, are connected to the arms of a U-tube manometer containing a liquid of density $1600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ that does not mix with water; above it, in both arms, there is water. The manometer liquid stands $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ higher in the arm connected to point $2$. By how much does the pressure at point $1$ exceed the pressure at point $2$?

- $2.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $7.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Take as reference the height of the surface of the manometer liquid in arm $1$, the lower one; the points at that height in the two arms lie in the same liquid, so they have the same pressure.
In arm $1$ above that height there is only water up to the pipe; in arm $2$ there is first a stretch $\Delta h$ of manometer liquid and then water, and the rest of the water column is the same in the two arms.
By the **hydrostatic pressure law** we therefore have $p_1 + \rho_w g \Delta h = p_2 + \rho_m g \Delta h$, hence
$$
p_1 - p_2 = (\rho_m - \rho_w)\, g \Delta h = 600 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{Pa} = 2.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $7.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$ neglects the water above the manometer liquid and uses the density $\rho_m$ alone.
