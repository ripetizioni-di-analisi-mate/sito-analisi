A buoy is a hollow, closed steel cylinder of mass $24\,\mathrm{kg}$ and external volume $60\,\mathrm{dm^3}$, cavity included. What volume of the buoy stays immersed when it floats in fresh water?

- $3.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $2.4 \cdot 10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $2.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}$

## Explanation

For a hollow body the **condition for flotation** applies with the average density $\dfrac{m}{V}$, where $V$ includes the closed cavity. With $V = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ the average density is $\dfrac{24}{6.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} = 400\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, less than that of water, so the buoy floats with the immersed fraction $\dfrac{400}{1000} = 0.40$. Therefore
$$
V_{\mathrm{imm}} = 0.40 \cdot 6.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 2.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3} .
$$
The same value follows from the balance between buoyant force and weight, $\rho_a V_{\mathrm{imm}}\, g = m g$. The value $3.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ is the volume of the emerged part, and $2.4 \cdot 10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ is obtained by dividing the weight of the buoy instead of its mass by the density of water.
