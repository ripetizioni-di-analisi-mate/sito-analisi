A dam has a vertical rectangular wall. If the level of the water against the dam doubles, how does the resultant force that the water exerts on the dam change?

- It doubles
- It becomes $4$ times greater
- It becomes $8$ times greater
- It stays the same

## Explanation

The force on a vertical rectangular wall of width $w$, wetted up to the height $H$, is $F = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\rho g w H^2$. Doubling $H$ doubles the wetted area $wH$ and also doubles the average gauge pressure $\rho g\,\dfrac{H}{2}$. Therefore the force becomes **4 times greater**.
