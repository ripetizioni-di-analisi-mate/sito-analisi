Two open containers have bottoms of the same area. The first contains water $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ deep, the second contains oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $1.25\,\mathrm{m}$ deep. Which statement about the pressure exerted by the liquid on the bottom is true?

- It is the same in the two containers
- It is greater in the container with water, because water is denser
- It is greater in the container with oil, because the column is taller
- It is greater in the container that holds the larger mass of liquid

## Explanation

By the **hydrostatic pressure law** the gauge pressure on the bottom is $\rho g h$ and depends only on the density and on the depth of the liquid.
For water $\rho h = 1000 \cdot 1.00\,\mathrm{kg/m^2} = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^2}$, and for oil $\rho h = 800 \cdot 1.25\,\mathrm{kg/m^2} = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^2}$.
The two products are equal, so the gauge pressures on the bottom are equal, and so are the absolute pressures, because the same atmospheric pressure acts above both liquids.
The lower density of the oil is exactly compensated by the greater height of the column.
