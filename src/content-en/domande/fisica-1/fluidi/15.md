A brick shaped like a rectangular box, of dimensions $20\,\mathrm{cm} \times 10\,\mathrm{cm} \times 5{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$, rests on the floor on its largest face. How does the pressure it exerts on the floor change if it is placed on its smallest face?

- It stays the same
- It becomes $4$ times smaller
- It becomes $4$ times greater
- It becomes $2$ times greater

## Explanation

In both positions the brick is at rest, so the force perpendicular to the floor is its weight, which does not change. The largest face has area $20 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 200\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, the smallest $10 \cdot 5{,}0\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 50\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Since $p = \dfrac{F_\perp}{A}$ and the area becomes one quarter, the pressure becomes **4 times greater**.
