A mercury barometer reads $4.0\,\mathrm{mmHg}$ less at the top of a tower than at its base. Treat air as a fluid of uniform density $1.20\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and use $1\,\mathrm{mmHg} = 133\,\mathrm{Pa}$. How tall is the tower?

- $0.054\,\mathrm{m}$
- $45\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pressure difference between the base and the top of the tower is $\Delta p = 4.0 \cdot 133\,\mathrm{Pa} = 532\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
Air is treated as a fluid at rest of uniform density, so by the **hydrostatic pressure law** $\Delta p = \rho_{\mathrm{air}}\, g h$.
Therefore the height of the tower is
$$
h = \frac{\Delta p}{\rho_{\mathrm{air}}\, g} = \frac{532}{1.20 \cdot 9.81}\,\mathrm{m} = 45\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $0.054\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by using the density of water instead of that of air, and $4.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{m}$ by forgetting the factor $g$.
