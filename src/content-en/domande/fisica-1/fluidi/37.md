Water rises in steady flow through a vertical pipe of constant cross section. At the lowest point the pressure is $2{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Treating water as an ideal fluid, what is the pressure $5{,}00\,\mathrm{m}$ higher up?

- $2{,}49 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1{,}75 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1{,}51 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The cross section is constant, so by the **continuity equation** the speed is the same at the two points and the terms $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$ of **Bernoulli's theorem** cancel. What remains is $p_1 + \rho g y_1 = p_2 + \rho g y_2$, therefore
$$
p_2 = p_1 - \rho g\,(y_2 - y_1) = 2{,}00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} - 1000 \cdot 9{,}81 \cdot 5{,}00\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1{,}51 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The pressure decreases with height as in a liquid at rest, because the kinetic term is the same at the two points.
