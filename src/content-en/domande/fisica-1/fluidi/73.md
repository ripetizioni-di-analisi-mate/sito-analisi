In a vertical cylindrical tank, of cross-section $0.50\,\mathrm{m^2}$, water enters from a pipe with flow rate $2.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and leaves through a tap with flow rate $0.50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. At what speed does the water level rise?

- $3.0\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{mm/s}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{mm/s}$

## Explanation

The volume of water in the tank increases, every second, by the difference between the incoming and the outgoing flow rates, that is, by $Q = (2.0 - 0.50)\,\mathrm{L/s} = 1.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
The tank has constant cross-section, so an increase in volume $dV$ raises the level by $\dfrac{dV}{A}$, and the rising speed is
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{1.5 \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.50}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm/s} .
$$
The value $4.0\,\mathrm{mm/s}$ considers only the incoming water, and $5.0\,\mathrm{mm/s}$ adds the two flow rates instead of subtracting them.
