Ideal incompressible water flows steadily through a horizontal pipe. It moves at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the wider section and faster in the constriction. Given the water density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the pressure drop from the wider section to the constriction $p = 16000\,\mathrm{Pa}$, what is the speed in the constriction $v$?

- $4 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2 + 4 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

At the same height, Bernoulli's equation conserves the sum of pressure and the kinetic term $\rho v^2/2$. Pressure falls as speed rises. In the stated units, the relation is $p = \dfrac{\rho \cdot \left(v^{2} - 4\right)}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $v = \dfrac{\sqrt{2 \cdot p + 4 \cdot \rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}$. Substituting the given values yields $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
