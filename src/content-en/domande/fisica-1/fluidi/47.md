A U-tube of constant cross section contains mercury ($\rho = 13.6 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$), with both arms open. Water is poured into one of the arms until the water column above the mercury is $27.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ high. By how much does the mercury rise in the other arm with respect to its initial level?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $14\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Consider the height of the interface between water and mercury; at the same height, in the other arm, there is mercury, so the two points have the same pressure.
Above them there are the water column on one side and a mercury column of height $\Delta h$ on the other, therefore by the **hydrostatic pressure law** $\rho_a\, g \cdot 0.272\,\mathrm{m} = \rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\, g\, \Delta h$, that is,
$$
\Delta h = \frac{1000}{13.6 \cdot 10^3} \cdot 27.2\,\mathrm{cm} = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The cross section is constant and mercury is incompressible, so the mercury goes down in the arm with the water by as much as it rises in the other, and the difference in level $\Delta h$ is twice the rise.
Therefore the mercury rises by $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the whole difference in level between the two mercury surfaces.
