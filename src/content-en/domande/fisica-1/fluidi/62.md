A beaker of water rests on a scale, which reads $10.0\,\mathrm{N}$. An aluminum block ($\rho = 2.70 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) of volume $200\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, hanging from a string held by hand, is completely immersed in the water without touching the bottom or the walls, and the water does not overflow. What does the scale read?

- $10.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $12.0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $15.3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $13.3\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The water exerts on the block the buoyant force $S = \rho_w V g = 1000 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 1.96\,\mathrm{N}$, upward.
By Newton's third law the block exerts on the water an equal and opposite force, directed downward, which the water transmits to the bottom of the beaker.
The weight of the block is supported partly by the string and partly by the buoyant force, and it does not load the scale beyond this force.
Therefore the scale reads
$$
10.0\,\mathrm{N} + 1.96\,\mathrm{N} = 12.0\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $15.3\,\mathrm{N}$ adds the whole weight of the block, which is also supported by the string, and $13.3\,\mathrm{N}$ adds the apparent weight, which is the force supported by the string.
