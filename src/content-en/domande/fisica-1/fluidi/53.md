A Cartesian diver is a small inverted test tube, with an air bubble trapped at the top, floating in a closed plastic bottle completely full of water. If the walls of the bottle are squeezed, the diver sinks. Which statement explains this fact?

- The pressure increase is transmitted to all the water, compresses the bubble, water enters the test tube and the average density of the diver exceeds that of water
- The pressure increase acts only near the walls squeezed by the hands and pushes the diver downward
- The pressure increase makes the water denser, so the buoyant force on the diver decreases
- The pressure increase is larger on the upper part of the diver than on the lower part, so the resultant points downward

## Explanation

By **Pascal's principle** the pressure increase produced by squeezing the bottle is transmitted undiminished to every point of the water, including the water in contact with the bubble inside the test tube.
The air of the bubble, which is compressible, reduces its volume, so water enters the test tube.
The mass of the diver with its contents increases while the volume it displaces decreases, therefore its average density grows.
When the average density exceeds that of water the weight exceeds the buoyant force, and by the **condition for flotation** the diver sinks.
Water is incompressible, so its density does not change, and the same pressure increase acts above and below the diver.
