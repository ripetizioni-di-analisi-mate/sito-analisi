An ideal hydraulic press has pistons at the same height. The large piston has area $80\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Neglect the weights of the pistons. Given the force on the small piston $f = 100\,\mathrm{N}$ and the force on the large piston $F = 800\,\mathrm{N}$, what is the area of the small piston $A$?

- $5\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $640\,\mathrm{cm^2}$

## Explanation

The pressure increase is the same below both pistons. The force ratio therefore equals the area ratio. In the stated units, the relation is $F = \dfrac{80 \cdot f}{A}$. Solving for the unknown gives $A = \dfrac{80 \cdot f}{F}$. Substituting the given values yields $10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.
