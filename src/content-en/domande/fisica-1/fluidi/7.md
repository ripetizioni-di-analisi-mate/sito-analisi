A stone hanging from a spring scale weighs $12.0\,\mathrm{N}$ in air and $7.5\,\mathrm{N}$ when it is completely immersed in water. Neglecting the buoyant force of the air, what is the density of the stone?

- $2.7 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $3.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

The difference between the weight in air and the apparent weight is the buoyant force, $S = P - P_{\mathrm{app}} = 4.5\,\mathrm{N}$. The stone is completely immersed, so $S = \rho_a V g$, while $P = \rho V g$; therefore the ratio of the weight to the buoyant force is the ratio of the densities. It follows that
$$
\rho = \frac{P}{S}\,\rho_a = \frac{12.0}{4.5} \cdot 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} = 2.7 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} .
$$
The value $2.6 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is obtained by dividing the weight instead of the mass by the volume $V = 4.6 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.
