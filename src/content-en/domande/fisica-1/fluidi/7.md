An aluminum block ($\rho = 2.70 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) of mass $2.70\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from a spring scale and is immersed in water for half of its volume. What does the spring scale read?

- $21.6\,\mathrm{N}$
- $16.7\,\mathrm{N}$
- $26.5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.91\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The volume of the block is $V = \dfrac{m}{\rho} = \dfrac{2.70}{2.70 \cdot 10^3}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, so the immersed volume is $V_{\mathrm{imm}} = 5.00 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.
By **Archimedes' principle** the buoyant force is $S = \rho_a V_{\mathrm{imm}}\, g = 1000 \cdot 5.00 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 4.91\,\mathrm{N}$.
The block is in equilibrium, therefore the spring scale reads the weight reduced by the buoyant force:
$$
P - S = 2.70 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} - 4.91\,\mathrm{N} = 26.5\,\mathrm{N} - 4.91\,\mathrm{N} = 21.6\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $16.7\,\mathrm{N}$ is the apparent weight with the block completely immersed, and $26.5\,\mathrm{N}$ is the weight in air.
