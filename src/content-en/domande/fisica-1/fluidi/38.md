A gas container is connected to one arm of a U-tube manometer containing water; the other arm is open to the atmosphere. In the open arm the water stands $25.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ higher than in the arm connected to the gas. What is the gauge pressure of the gas?

- $3.34 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.04 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.23 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.45 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Consider the height of the water surface in the arm connected to the gas, where the pressure is that of the gas, $p_g$.
In the open arm, at the same height, there is water under a column of height $h = 0.250\,\mathrm{m}$, so by the **hydrostatic pressure law** the pressure is $p_0 + \rho g h$.
The two points are at the same height in the same liquid, therefore they have the same pressure and
$$
p_g - p_0 = \rho g h = 1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250\,\mathrm{Pa} = 2.45 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $3.34 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$ uses the density of mercury, which is not the liquid in the manometer, and $1.04 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is the absolute pressure of the gas.
