A sealed tank contains a liquid at rest. The gas pressure above it is greater than atmospheric pressure. Assume $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and a liquid density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Given the absolute gas pressure $p_{0} = 120\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the absolute pressure at the submerged point $p = 150\,\mathrm{kPa}$, what is the depth below the surface $h$?

- $3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At this density, each meter of depth adds $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The starting pressure is the absolute gas pressure. In the stated units, the relation is $p = 10 \cdot h + p_{0}$. Solving for the unknown gives $h = \dfrac{p}{10} - \dfrac{p_{0}}{10}$. Substituting the given values yields $3\,\mathrm{m}$.
