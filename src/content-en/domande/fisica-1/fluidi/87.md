A viscous liquid flows in steady laminar flow in a horizontal tube of constant cross-section. How does the pressure vary along the tube, in the direction of flow?

- It stays constant, because the cross-section and the height are constant
- It increases linearly
- It decreases linearly
- It decreases only near the outlet of the tube

## Explanation

By **Poiseuille's law** the flow rate in a stretch of length $L$ is $Q = \dfrac{\pi R^4\, \Delta p}{8 \eta L}$, where $\Delta p$ is the pressure drop over the stretch.
The flow rate and the radius are the same in every stretch of the tube, so $\dfrac{\Delta p}{L} = \dfrac{8 \eta Q}{\pi R^4}$ is the same everywhere.
Therefore the pressure drops by equal amounts over stretches of equal length, that is, linearly in the direction of flow.
A constant pressure is the result of Bernoulli's theorem, which holds for a fluid without viscosity.
