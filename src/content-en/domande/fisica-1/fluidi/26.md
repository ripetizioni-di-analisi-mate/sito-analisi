In an ideal hydraulic press the area of the large piston is $20$ times that of the small piston, and the pistons have negligible mass. The force $F_1$ acts on the small piston and the piston moves down by $s_1$; the large piston supports the force $F_2$ and rises by $s_2$. Which quantity has the same value on the two pistons?

- The magnitude $F$ of the force
- The product $F s$ of the force and the displacement
- The displacement $s$
- The ratio $\dfrac{F}{s}$ of the force to the displacement

## Explanation

By **Pascal's principle** the pressure is the same under the two pistons, so $F_2 = 20\,F_1$. The liquid is incompressible, therefore $A_1 s_1 = A_2 s_2$ and $s_2 = \dfrac{s_1}{20}$. Multiplying the two equations term by term we obtain $F_2 s_2 = F_1 s_1$, so the work is the same on the two pistons. The ratio $\dfrac{F}{s}$, on the other hand, is $400$ times greater on the large piston.
