Ideal incompressible water flows steadily through a horizontal pipe. It moves at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the wider section and faster in the constriction. The initial data are the water density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the speed in the constriction $v = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Change the speed in the constriction to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $p$ to its initial value?

- $\dfrac{4}{9}$
- $\dfrac{3}{8}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}$

## Explanation

At the same height, Bernoulli's equation conserves the sum of pressure and the kinetic term $\rho v^2/2$. Pressure falls as speed rises. The relation $p = \dfrac{\rho \cdot \left(v^{2} - 4\right)}{2}$ gives $16000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ before the change and $6000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{3}{8}$.
