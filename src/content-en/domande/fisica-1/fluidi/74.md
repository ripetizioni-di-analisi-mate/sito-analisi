A shower head has $40$ holes, each of diameter $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, and is fed by a pipe of diameter $1.6\,\mathrm{cm}$ in which water flows at $0.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At what speed does the water come out of the holes?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.32\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

All the water flowing in the pipe comes out of the holes, so by the **continuity equation** the flow rate of the pipe equals the sum of the flow rates of the $40$ holes.
The area of a circle is proportional to the square of the diameter, therefore $\dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}\, v_1 = 40\, \dfrac{\pi d^2}{4}\, v_2$.
It follows that
$$
v_2 = \frac{D^2}{40\, d^2}\, v_1 = \frac{16^2}{40 \cdot 1.0^2} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.1\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
where the diameters are in millimeters.
The value $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ treats the shower head as a single hole, and $0.32\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the ratio of the diameters without squaring it.
