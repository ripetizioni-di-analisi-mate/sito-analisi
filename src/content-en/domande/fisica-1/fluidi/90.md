Two horizontal tubes are connected in series and carry water ($\eta = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$) in laminar flow. The first has radius $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, the second radius $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $40\,\mathrm{cm}$. The pressure difference between the inlet of the first tube and the outlet of the second is $4.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$. What is the flow rate?

- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $7.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

The tubes are in series, so the same flow rate $Q$ passes through them, and the total pressure difference is the sum of the pressure drops in the two tubes.
By **Poiseuille's law** the drop in each tube is $\Delta p_i = \dfrac{8 \eta L_i}{\pi R_i^4}\, Q$, therefore
$$
\Delta p = \frac{8 \eta Q}{\pi} \left( \frac{L_1}{R_1^4} + \frac{L_2}{R_2^4} \right) = \frac{8 \eta Q}{\pi} \left( 1.0 \cdot 10^{11} + 2.5 \cdot 10^{10} \right)\mathrm{m^{-3}} .
$$
It follows that
$$
Q = \frac{\pi \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^2}{8 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1.25 \cdot 10^{11}}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 1.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The value $1.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ considers only the thin tube, and $7.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ adds the flow rates that the two tubes would each have with the whole pressure difference, as if they were in parallel.
