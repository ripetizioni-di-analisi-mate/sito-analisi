A pipeline of diameter $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ carries crude oil of density $850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, which flows with average speed $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the mass flow rate?

- $8.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $0.24\,\mathrm{kg/s}$
- $2.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/s}$

## Explanation

The cross section of the pipeline has radius $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$, so its area is $A = \pi \cdot (0.25)^2\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.196\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
The volumetric **flow rate** is $Q = A v = 0.196 \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.236\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.
The mass flow rate is the product of the density and the volumetric flow rate, therefore
$$
\rho Q = 850 \cdot 0.236\,\mathrm{kg/s} = 2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/s} .
$$
The value $8.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ uses the diameter instead of the radius to compute the area, and $0.24\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ is the numerical value of the volumetric flow rate, without the density.
