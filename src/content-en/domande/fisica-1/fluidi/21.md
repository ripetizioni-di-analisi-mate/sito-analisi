A swimmer breathes through a tube that sticks out above the water surface, so the lungs contain air at atmospheric pressure $p_0 = 1.013 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The chest muscles can expand the lungs only if the gauge pressure of the water on the chest does not exceed $\dfrac{1}{20}$ of $p_0$. What is the maximum depth of the chest below the surface at which the swimmer can still breathe in?

- $10.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.26\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.52\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The gauge pressure of the water at depth $h$ is $\rho g h$, by the **hydrostatic pressure law**.
The condition for breathing in is $\rho g h \leq \dfrac{p_0}{20}$, so the maximum depth is
$$
h = \frac{p_0}{20\, \rho g} = \frac{1.013 \cdot 10^5}{20 \cdot 1000 \cdot 9.81}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.52\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $10.3\,\mathrm{m}$ is the depth at which the gauge pressure equals the whole atmospheric pressure. The value $5.1\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $g$ in the denominator.
