A Torricelli barometer is built with an oil of density $850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ instead of mercury, and the pressure above the column is negligible. How tall is the oil column when the atmospheric pressure is $p_0 = 1{,}013 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$?

- $0{,}760\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10{,}3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8{,}78\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12{,}1\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The pressure above the column is negligible, so by **Stevin's law** $p_0 = \rho g h$. Therefore
$$
h = \frac{p_0}{\rho g} = \frac{1{,}013 \cdot 10^5}{850 \cdot 9{,}81}\,\mathrm{m} = 12{,}1\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The height is inversely proportional to the density, so the oil column is taller than the $10{,}3\,\mathrm{m}$ of a water column. The value $8{,}78\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by multiplying the height of the water column by the ratio of the densities instead of dividing it.
