A cylinder hanging from a spring scale is slowly lowered into a container of water, with its axis vertical: first the base touches the surface, then the cylinder goes in until it is completely immersed, and finally it goes further down without touching the bottom. How does the reading of the spring scale vary as a function of the depth of the base?

- It decreases linearly until the cylinder is fully immersed, then it stays constant
- It decreases linearly during the whole descent
- It stays constant, because the buoyant force does not depend on depth
- It decreases until the cylinder is fully immersed, then it increases again

## Explanation

The spring scale reads the apparent weight $P - S$, where $S = \rho g V_{\mathrm{imm}}$ is the buoyant force.
While the cylinder goes into the water the immersed volume is $V_{\mathrm{imm}} = A z$, where $A$ is the area of the base and $z$ the depth of the base, so the buoyant force grows linearly with $z$ and the reading decreases linearly.
When the cylinder is fully immersed the immersed volume is the whole volume of the cylinder and no longer changes.
Therefore from that moment on the buoyant force and the reading of the spring scale stay constant, at any depth.
