A Pitot tube mounted on a small airplane measures the difference between the pressure at the point where the air comes to rest against the front opening and the pressure of the air flowing past the side openings. The difference is $1.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the air has density $1.20\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. What is the speed of the airplane relative to the air?

- $35\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $50\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

In the frame of the airplane the air arrives with speed $v$ and comes to rest against the front opening, while along the side openings it keeps flowing with speed $v$.
By **Bernoulli's theorem**, at the same height, the pressure at the stagnation point exceeds that of the moving air by $\Delta p = \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$.
Therefore
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\, \Delta p}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^3}{1.20}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 50\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $35\,\mathrm{m/s}$ forgets the factor $2$, and $1.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the density of water instead of that of air.
