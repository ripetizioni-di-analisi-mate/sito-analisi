An oil of density $900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and viscosity $0{,}30\,\mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$ flows with average speed $1{,}5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a pipe of diameter $2{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the Reynolds number?

- $90$
- $45$
- $9{,}0 \cdot 10^3$
- $0{,}011$

## Explanation

The Reynolds number is $\mathrm{Re} = \dfrac{\rho v D}{\eta}$, with the diameter in meters, $D = 0{,}020\,\mathrm{m}$. So
$$
\mathrm{Re} = \frac{900 \cdot 1{,}5 \cdot 0{,}020}{0{,}30} = 90 .
$$
The value is much less than $2000$, therefore the flow is **laminar**. The value $9{,}0 \cdot 10^3$ is obtained by leaving the diameter in centimeters, and $45$ by using the radius instead of the diameter.
