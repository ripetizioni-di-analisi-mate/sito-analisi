Three vessels have bottoms of the same area and contain water up to the same height $h$: a cylinder, a vessel that widens upward and a vessel that narrows upward. Which statement about the pressure of the water on the bottom is true?

- It is greatest in the vessel that widens upward, because it contains more water
- It is greatest in the vessel that narrows upward, because the walls push the water downward
- It is the same in the three vessels
- It is greatest in the cylinder, because it has vertical walls

## Explanation

By **Stevin's law** the pressure on the bottom is $p_0 + \rho g h$, so it depends only on the density of the water and on the depth of the bottom below the free surface. The three vessels contain water up to the same height $h$, therefore the pressure on the bottom is **the same**. The weight of the water contained is different in the three vessels, and the difference between this weight and the force that the water exerts on the bottom is due to the forces that the slanted walls exert on the water.
