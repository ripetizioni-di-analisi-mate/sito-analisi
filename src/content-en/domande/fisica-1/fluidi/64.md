A barge has the shape of a rectangular box with a horizontal bottom of $10.0\,\mathrm{m} \times 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and floats in fresh water. The barge is loaded with $20\,\mathrm{t}$ of goods and stays afloat. By how much does it sink?

- $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.050\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The barge floats both before and after loading, so the buoyant force increases exactly by the added weight, $m g$.
The walls are vertical, therefore if the barge sinks by $\Delta h$ the immersed volume increases by $A\,\Delta h$, with $A = 40\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and the buoyant force increases by $\rho A\,\Delta h\, g$.
From the equality $\rho A\,\Delta h\, g = m g$ we obtain
$$
\Delta h = \frac{m}{\rho A} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^4}{1000 \cdot 40}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ uses as area only the $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ side instead of the area of the bottom.
