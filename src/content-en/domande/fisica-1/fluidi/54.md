An ideal hydraulic press has pistons at the same height. The large piston has area $80\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Neglect the weights of the pistons. The initial data are the force on the small piston $f = 100\,\mathrm{N}$ and the area of the small piston $A = 10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. In the second configuration, change the area of the small piston to $20\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the ratio of the final value of $F$ to its initial value?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$

## Explanation

The pressure increase is the same below both pistons. The force ratio therefore equals the area ratio. The relation $F = \dfrac{80 \cdot f}{A}$ gives $800\,\mathrm{N}$ before the change and $400\,\mathrm{N}$ after it. The ratio is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
