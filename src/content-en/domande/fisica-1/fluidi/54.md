In a hydraulic press the small piston is pushed by a lever. The hand applies a force of $60\,\mathrm{N}$ at the end of the lever, $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the fulcrum, and the piston rod is connected to the lever $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the fulcrum; the forces are perpendicular to the lever. The area of the large piston is $20$ times that of the small piston and the pistons are at the same height. What force can the large piston support?

- $1.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.4 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The lever is in equilibrium, so the moments about the fulcrum of the force of the hand and of the force of the piston are equal, that is, $60 \cdot 50 = F_1 \cdot 10$.
Therefore the small piston is pushed with the force $F_1 = 300\,\mathrm{N}$.
By the **hydraulic press** relation the large piston supports the force
$$
F_2 = \frac{A_2}{A_1}\,F_1 = 20 \cdot 300\,\mathrm{N} = 6.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $1.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$ takes into account only the press, and $3.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N}$ only the lever.
