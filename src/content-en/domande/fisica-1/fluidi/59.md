A cork stopper of density $250\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is held still under water and then released. Considering only the weight and the buoyant force, what is its acceleration at the instant it is released?

- $7.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $29\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $39\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

On the completely immersed stopper act the buoyant force $\rho_w V g$ upward and the weight $\rho_c V g$ downward, where $V$ is the volume of the stopper.
The mass of the stopper is $\rho_c V$, so by Newton's second law the acceleration, directed upward, is
$$
a = \frac{(\rho_w - \rho_c)\, V g}{\rho_c V} = \frac{1000 - 250}{250} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 29\,\mathrm{m/s^2} .
$$
The value $7.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ divides the net force by the mass of the displaced water instead of the mass of the stopper. The value $39\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ considers the buoyant force alone and neglects the weight.
