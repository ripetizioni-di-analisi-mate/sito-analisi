A block of mass $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides down a plane inclined at $30^\circ$ on a film of oil $0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$ thick, of viscosity $0.25\,\mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$; the contact area is $0.10\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The force of the oil on the block is given by the viscosity relation for two parallel plates. What constant speed does the block reach?

- $0.34\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.39\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

At constant speed the net force on the block is zero, so along the plane the component of the weight $m g \sin 30^\circ$ equals the viscous force $\eta A\, \dfrac{v}{d}$.
Therefore
$$
v = \frac{m g \sin 30^\circ\, d}{\eta A} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-4}}{0.25 \cdot 0.10}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $0.34\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the component of the weight perpendicular to the plane, $m g \cos 30^\circ$, and $0.39\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uses the whole weight.
The value $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ leaves the thickness in millimeters.
