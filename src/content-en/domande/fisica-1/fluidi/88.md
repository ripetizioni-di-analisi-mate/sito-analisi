A viscous fluid flows in steady laminar flow in a cylindrical tube. How is the speed of the fluid distributed over the cross-section of the tube?

- It is the same at all points of the cross-section
- It is maximum near the wall and zero on the axis
- It is zero on the axis and at the wall and maximum at half the radius
- It is maximum on the axis and zero at the wall, with a parabolic profile

## Explanation

In a viscous fluid the layer in contact with the wall of the tube is at rest, so at the wall the speed is zero.
Each cylindrical layer is slowed down by the viscous forces of the outer, slower layer and dragged by the inner one, therefore the speed increases moving away from the wall.
The balance between pressure forces and viscous forces on each layer, on which **Poiseuille's law** is based, gives a parabolic profile.
The speed is therefore maximum on the axis of the tube.
