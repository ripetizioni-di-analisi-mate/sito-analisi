A stone is held completely immersed in the water of a lake, first at a depth of $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and then at $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$. How does the buoyant force on it change?

- It stays the same
- At $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ it is $5$ times greater
- At $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ it is greater, because the pressure is greater
- At $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ it is smaller

## Explanation

By **Archimedes' principle** the buoyant force is $\rho_f V_{\mathrm{imm}}\, g$, so it depends only on the density of the water and on the immersed volume. Water is incompressible and the stone is completely immersed in both positions, therefore the buoyant force **stays the same**. At $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ the pressures on the upper and lower faces of the stone are both greater, but their difference depends only on the difference in height between the faces, which does not change.
