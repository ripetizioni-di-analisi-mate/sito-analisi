In a horizontal duct water flows with speed $2{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and pressure $1{,}80 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Further along, the cross-sectional area of the duct is halved. Treating water as an ideal fluid, by how much is the pressure in the narrow section lower than in the wide section?

- $1{,}2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $6{,}0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $8{,}0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3{,}0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

By the **continuity equation**, since the cross section is halved, the speed doubles and in the narrow section it is $v_2 = 4{,}0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The duct is horizontal, therefore **Bernoulli's theorem** gives
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1000 \cdot (16 - 4{,}0)\,\mathrm{Pa} = 6{,}0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The pressure in the narrow section is therefore $1{,}74 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The value $1{,}2 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is obtained without the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the kinetic energy.
