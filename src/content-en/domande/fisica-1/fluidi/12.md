A jet of water flows out horizontally from a small hole in the vertical wall of an open, very wide tank resting on the ground. The hole is $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface of the water and $1.25\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Neglecting air resistance, at what distance from the wall does the jet hit the ground?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

By **Torricelli's theorem** the water leaves the hole with horizontal speed $v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.80}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.96\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
Outside the hole each particle of water moves like a projectile launched horizontally, so it falls $H = 1.25\,\mathrm{m}$ in the time $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2H}{g}} = 0.505\,\mathrm{s}$.
In this time it covers the horizontal distance
$$
x = v\, t = 3.96 \cdot 0.505\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$ uses for the exit speed the height of the hole above the ground instead of its depth below the free surface, and $3.2\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the sum of the two heights.
