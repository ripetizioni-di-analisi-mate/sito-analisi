A horizontal Venturi tube carrying water has cross section $12\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ in the wide section and $4.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ in the constriction; the pressure difference between the two sections is $2.4 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$. What is the flow rate?

- $9.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $1.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $6.6 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$
- $3.1 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$

## Explanation

By the **continuity equation** $v_2 = \dfrac{A_1}{A_2}\,v_1 = 3\,v_1$. The duct is horizontal, so **Bernoulli's theorem** gives $\Delta p = \dfrac{1}{2}\rho\,(9 v_1^2 - v_1^2) = 4 \rho v_1^2$. Therefore
$$
v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta p}{4 \rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{2.4 \cdot 10^3}{4000}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{0.60}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.77\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The flow rate is computed with the wide section and the corresponding speed, so $Q = A_1 v_1 = 12 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.77\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 9.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The value $3.1 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ multiplies the speed in the wide section by the area of the constriction.
