In a Torricelli barometer some air has remained above the mercury column, where the pressure should be negligible. How does this barometer compare with a correctly built one, under the same conditions?

- Its column is higher, so it indicates an atmospheric pressure higher than the true one
- Its column has the same height, because the height depends only on the external pressure
- Its column is lower, so it indicates an atmospheric pressure higher than the true one
- Its column is lower, so it indicates an atmospheric pressure lower than the true one

## Explanation

At the height of the surface in the dish the pressure is $p_0$, and by the **hydrostatic pressure law** $p_0 = p_{\mathrm{air}} + \rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\, g h$, where $p_{\mathrm{air}}$ is the pressure of the air left in the tube.
Since $p_{\mathrm{air}} > 0$, the height $h = \dfrac{p_0 - p_{\mathrm{air}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\, g}$ is smaller than that of the correct barometer.
Whoever reads the barometer computes the pressure as $\rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\, g h$, so they obtain a value lower than the true atmospheric pressure.
