An ice cube floats in a glass of water. When the ice has melted completely, how does the water level in the glass compare with before?

- It is the same as before
- It is higher
- It is lower
- It is higher or lower depending on the shape of the cube

## Explanation

The cube floats, so the buoyant force equals its weight and the displaced water has the same mass $m$ as the ice; the volume of the immersed part is therefore $V_{\mathrm{imm}} = \dfrac{m}{\rho_a}$. On melting, the ice becomes water of mass $m$, which occupies the volume $\dfrac{m}{\rho_a}$. This volume coincides with that of the immersed part of the cube, so the level **stays the same**.
