A cylinder of height $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and base area $0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is held completely immersed in water, with its axis vertical. By how much does the force exerted by the water on the lower base exceed the force on the upper base?

- $19.6\,\mathrm{N}$
- $1.96 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

By the **hydrostatic pressure law** the pressure on the lower base exceeds that on the upper base by $\rho g h$, where $h = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is the difference in depth.
The two bases have the same area $A$, so the difference between the forces is
$$
F_2 - F_1 = \rho g h A = 1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 0.010\,\mathrm{N} = 19.6\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
This difference is the buoyant force, equal to the weight of the water contained in a volume $hA$, and it does not depend on the depth of the cylinder.
The value $1.96 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$ is obtained by leaving the height in centimeters.
