Water, modeled as an ideal incompressible fluid, flows steadily through a horizontal pipe. It moves at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the wider section and faster in the constriction. Given the water density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the speed in the constriction $v = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the pressure drop from the wider section to the constriction $p$?

- $16000\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $32000\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $18000\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $8000\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

At the same height, Bernoulli's equation shows that the sum of the pressure and kinetic terms is constant $\rho v^2/2$. Pressure falls as speed rises. In the stated units, the relation is $p = \dfrac{\rho \cdot \left(v^{2} - 4\right)}{2}$. Substituting the given values yields $16000\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
