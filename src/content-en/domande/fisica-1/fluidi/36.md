A barrel full of water is closed at the top by a horizontal lid of area $0.20\,\mathrm{m^2}$. A thin vertical tube, of cross-section $1.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, is inserted in the lid and filled with water up to $10\,\mathrm{m}$ above the lid; the tube holds about $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water. Above the lid there is air at atmospheric pressure. Neglecting the area occupied by the tube, what is the resultant force exerted by the water and the air on the lid?

- $9.8\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Just below the lid the water is $h = 10\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface in the tube, so by the **hydrostatic pressure law** its pressure is $p_0 + \rho g h$.
Air at pressure $p_0$ acts above the lid, therefore the resultant force is computed with the gauge pressure $\rho g h$ alone, uniform over the whole lid:
$$
F = \rho g h A = 1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 10 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N} = 2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
directed upward.
The force depends on the height of the column and not on the amount of water in the tube, so it is much larger than the weight $9.8\,\mathrm{N}$ of the water contained in the tube. The value $4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$ uses the absolute pressure and neglects the air pressing on the other face of the lid.
