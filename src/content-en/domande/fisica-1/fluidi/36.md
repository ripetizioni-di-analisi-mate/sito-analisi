In a circular duct water flows at $3{,}6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a section of diameter $2{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Further along, the diameter becomes $6{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the speed in the wide section?

- $1{,}2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $32\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0{,}40\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $11\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By the **continuity equation** $A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$. The diameter triples, so the area, which is proportional to the square of the diameter, becomes $9$ times greater. Therefore
$$
v_2 = \frac{v_1}{9} = \frac{3{,}6}{9}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0{,}40\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $1{,}2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by dividing by the ratio of the diameters instead of the ratio of the areas.
