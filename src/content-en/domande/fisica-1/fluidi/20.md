A U-tube contains mercury ($\rho = 13.6 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$). Water is poured into one arm and forms a column $27.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ high above the interface between water and mercury. How far above the height of the interface is the free surface of the mercury in the other arm?

- $25.2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $370\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $27.2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2.00\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The two points at the height of the interface, one in each arm, lie in the same connected mercury, so they have the same pressure. By **Stevin's law** we therefore have $\rho_a h_a = \rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\, h_{\mathrm{Hg}}$, with the heights measured from that level. It follows that
$$
h_{\mathrm{Hg}} = \frac{\rho_a}{\rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}}\,h_a = \frac{27.2}{13.6}\,\mathrm{cm} = 2.00\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The value $25.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the difference in level between the two free surfaces.
