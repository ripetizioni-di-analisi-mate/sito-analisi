A person is standing still. Their feet are $1.30\,\mathrm{m}$ below the heart. Treating blood ($\rho = 1060\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) as a liquid at rest, by how much does the blood pressure in the feet exceed the pressure at the level of the heart?

- $1.38 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.15 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.35 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $6.76 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Blood is treated as an incompressible liquid at rest, so the **hydrostatic pressure law** applies between two points at different heights.
The feet are $h = 1.30\,\mathrm{m}$ lower than the heart, therefore the pressure difference is
$$
\Delta p = \rho g h = 1060 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.30\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.35 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $1.38 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is the product $\rho h$, without the factor $g$. The value $1.15 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ adds atmospheric pressure, which is not part of the requested difference.
