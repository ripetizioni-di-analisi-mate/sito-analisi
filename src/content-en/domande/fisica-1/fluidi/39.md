A duct carries water, treated as an ideal fluid, from a point $1$ to a point $2$ that is $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ higher. At point $1$ the speed is $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the pressure $1.50 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$; at point $2$ the speed is $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the pressure at point $2$?

- $1.24 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.44 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.64 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.76 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

By **Bernoulli's theorem**, with $y_2 - y_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, we have
$$
p_2 = p_1 - \frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) - \rho g\,(y_2 - y_1) .
$$
The kinetic term is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1000 \cdot (16 - 4.0)\,\mathrm{Pa} = 6.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and the height term is $1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.96 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$. So $p_2 = (1.50 - 0.060 - 0.196) \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.24 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The value $1.44 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ neglects the difference in height.
