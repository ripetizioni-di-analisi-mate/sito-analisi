Flow in a cylindrical capillary is laminar and satisfies Poiseuille's assumptions. Initially the flow rate is $2\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ and the radius is $1\,\mathrm{mm}$. Length and viscosity stay constant. Given the new radius $r = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the new-to-old pressure-drop ratio $b = \dfrac{1}{2}$, what is the new flow rate $Q$?

- $16\,\mathrm{mL/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{mL/s}$
- $32\,\mathrm{mL/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{mL/s}$

## Explanation

The Poiseuille flow rate is proportional to the fourth power of radius and to the pressure drop. In the stated units, the relation is $Q = 2 \cdot b \cdot r^{4}$. Substituting the given values yields $16\,\mathrm{mL/s}$.
