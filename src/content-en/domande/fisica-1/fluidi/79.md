A very wide open tank contains water. A small tube connected to the lower part of the tank ends with its opening pointing upward, at a depth $h$ below the free surface, and the water comes out as a vertical jet. Neglecting all friction and air resistance, up to what height does the jet rise?

- Up to halfway between the opening and the free surface
- Up to a height $2h$ above the opening
- Up to the height of the free surface of the water in the tank
- Up to a height that depends on the area of the opening, even if it is small

## Explanation

By **Torricelli's theorem** the water leaves the opening with speed $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$, because the very wide free surface goes down with negligible speed.
In the jet each particle moves upward subject only to its weight, so it rises to the height $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ above the opening.
Substituting the speed gives the height $\dfrac{2 g h}{2 g} = h$.
Therefore the jet reaches exactly the height of the free surface of the tank, as predicted by energy conservation for a fluid without friction.
