A horizontal pipeline $100\,\mathrm{m}$ long, of radius $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, carries an oil of viscosity $0.20\,\mathrm{Pa \cdot s}$ in laminar flow, with flow rate $5.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. What power must the pump supply to maintain the flow, if all the energy supplied is dissipated by viscosity?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$
- $13\,\mathrm{W}$
- $25\,\mathrm{W}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

By **Poiseuille's law** the required pressure difference is
$$
\Delta p = \frac{8 \eta L Q}{\pi R^4} = \frac{8 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 100 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{\pi \cdot (5.0 \cdot 10^{-2})^4}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 4.07 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The pressure forces do the work $\Delta p$ for each unit volume of oil passing through the pipe, and each second the volume $Q$ passes, so the power is
$$
P = \Delta p\, Q = 4.07 \cdot 10^4 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{W} = 2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The value $13\,\mathrm{W}$ is obtained using the diameter instead of the radius, and $25\,\mathrm{W}$ forgetting the factor $8$ in Poiseuille's law.
