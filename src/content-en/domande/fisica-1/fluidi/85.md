A sealed tank contains a liquid at rest. The gas pressure above it is greater than atmospheric pressure. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and liquid density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Given the absolute gas pressure $p_{0} = 120\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the depth below the surface $h = 3\,\mathrm{m}$, what is the absolute pressure at the submerged point $p$?

- $30\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $90\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $150\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $300\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

At this density, each meter of depth adds $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The starting pressure is the absolute gas pressure. In the stated units, the relation is $p = 10 \cdot h + p_{0}$. Substituting the given values yields $150\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
