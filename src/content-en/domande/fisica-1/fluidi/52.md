In a hydraulic press with the pistons at the same height the oil is replaced with water, which is less dense. The force applied to the small piston stays the same. What happens to the force that the large piston can support?

- It decreases in proportion to the density of the liquid
- It increases, because water is less viscous than oil
- It decreases, because water transmits a lower pressure
- It stays the same

## Explanation

By **Pascal's principle** the pressure increase $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1}$ produced by the small piston is transmitted unchanged to every point of the liquid.
The pistons are at the same height, so between them the liquid adds no pressure difference, whatever its density.
Therefore the large piston receives the force $F_2 = \dfrac{A_2}{A_1}\,F_1$, which depends only on the areas and on the applied force.
The density of the liquid would enter the result only if the pistons were at different heights.
