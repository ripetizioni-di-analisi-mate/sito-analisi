An ideal hydraulic press has pistons at the same height. The large piston has area $80\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Neglect the weights of the pistons. Given the area of the small piston $A = 10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and the force on the large piston $F = 800\,\mathrm{N}$, what is the force on the small piston $f$?

- $200\,\mathrm{N}$
- $50\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6400\,\mathrm{N}$
- $100\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The pressure increase is the same below both pistons. The force ratio therefore equals the area ratio. In the stated units, the relation is $F = \dfrac{80 \cdot f}{A}$. Solving for the unknown gives $f = \dfrac{A \cdot F}{80}$. Substituting the given values yields $100\,\mathrm{N}$.
