A homogeneous body with the same density as water is released at rest, completely immersed, at half the depth of a swimming pool. Treating the water and the body as incompressible, what does the body do?

- It stays at rest at the point where it was released
- It rises to the surface and floats with part of its volume out of the water
- It slowly sinks to the bottom
- It rises to the surface, then returns to half the depth and oscillates about that point

## Explanation

The body is completely immersed, so by **Archimedes' principle** it receives the buoyant force $\rho_a V g$, where $V$ is its volume.
Its weight is $\rho_c V g$, and since $\rho_c = \rho_a$ the weight equals the buoyant force.
The water and the body are incompressible, therefore the buoyant force is the same at every depth and the resultant force is zero at every point of the pool.
By the **condition for flotation** the body is thus in equilibrium at any depth and, released at rest, it stays at rest where it is.
