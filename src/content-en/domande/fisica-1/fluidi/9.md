A garden hose of diameter $2{,}0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries water with a flow rate of $0{,}30\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and ends with a nozzle of diameter $0{,}50\,\mathrm{cm}$. With what speed does the water leave the nozzle?

- $3{,}8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $48\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0{,}95\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By the **continuity equation** the flow rate is the same in every cross section, so at the outlet $v_2 = \dfrac{Q}{A_2}$. In SI units the flow rate is $Q = 3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, and the nozzle has radius $2{,}5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore $A_2 = \pi\,(2{,}5 \cdot 10^{-3})^2\,\mathrm{m^2} = 1{,}96 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Then
$$
v_2 = \frac{3{,}0 \cdot 10^{-4}}{1{,}96 \cdot 10^{-5}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 15\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $0{,}95\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the speed in the hose, whose cross section is $16$ times that of the nozzle.
