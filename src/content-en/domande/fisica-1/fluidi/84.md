A sample hanging from a spring scale is fully submerged. Its weight in air is $70\,\mathrm{N}$ and air buoyancy is negligible. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the liquid density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the spring scale reading $F = 40\,\mathrm{N}$, what is the sample volume $V$?

- $\dfrac{1}{250}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{500}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2000}\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{1000}\,\mathrm{m^3}$

## Explanation

The string tension and the buoyant force both point upward. Their sum balances the weight. In the stated units, the relation is $F = - 10 \cdot V \cdot \rho + 70$. Solving for the unknown gives $V = \dfrac{70 - F}{10 \cdot \rho}$. Substituting the given values yields $\dfrac{3}{1000}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.
