A closed pressure tank contains water and, above the water, air at the gauge pressure of $2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$. In the wall, $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ below the surface of the water, there is a small hole from which the water flows out into the atmosphere. The surface of the water is very wide compared with the hole. Treating water as an ideal fluid, at what speed does the water flow out?

- $4.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $11\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

We apply **Bernoulli's theorem** between a point of the surface of the water, where the pressure is $p_0 + p_r$ and the speed is negligible, and a point of the jet just outside the hole, where the pressure is $p_0$.
Taking the height of the hole as zero we obtain $p_0 + p_r + \rho g h = p_0 + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$, so
$$
v = \sqrt{2 \left( g h + \frac{p_r}{\rho} \right)} = \sqrt{2 \cdot \left( 9.81 \cdot 1.0 + \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^4}{1000} \right)}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 7.7\,\mathrm{m/s} .
$$
The value $4.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the speed given by **Torricelli's theorem**, which neglects the overpressure of the air. The value $6.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ takes into account only the overpressure, and $11\,\mathrm{m/s}$ adds the two speeds instead of adding the two energies per unit volume.
