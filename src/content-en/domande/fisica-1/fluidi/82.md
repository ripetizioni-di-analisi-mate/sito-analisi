A sample hanging from a force gauge is fully submerged. Its weight in air is $70\,\mathrm{N}$ and air buoyancy is negligible. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Given the liquid density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the sample volume $V = \dfrac{3}{1000}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, what is the force gauge reading $F$?

- $80\,\mathrm{N}$
- $40\,\mathrm{N}$
- $100\,\mathrm{N}$
- $30\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

String tension and buoyancy both point upward. Their sum balances the weight. In the stated units, the relation is $F = - 10 \cdot V \cdot \rho + 70$. Substituting the given values yields $40\,\mathrm{N}$.
