A siphon of constant cross-section empties a very wide open tank. The highest point $C$ of the tube is $h_1 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the free surface of the water, and the outlet $D$ is $h_2 = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. Treating water as an ideal fluid, what is the gauge pressure at point $C$?

- $-3.92 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $-1.18 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.18 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $-7.85 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

**Bernoulli's theorem** is applied between the free surface, where the pressure is $p_0$ and the speed is negligible, and the outlet $D$, where the pressure is $p_0$, which gives $\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = \rho g h_2$.
The tube has constant cross-section, so by the **continuity equation** the water at $C$ has the same speed $v$ as at $D$.
Between the free surface and point $C$, which is $h_1$ higher, Bernoulli's theorem gives $p_0 = p_C + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g h_1$.
Therefore
$$
p_C - p_0 = -\rho g\,(h_1 + h_2) = -1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.20\,\mathrm{Pa} = -1.18 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $-3.92 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$ neglects the kinetic term, as if the water in the siphon were at rest.
