A wooden block floats in the water of a container placed on the floor of an elevator. The elevator starts upward with constant acceleration. During the acceleration, how does the fraction of the volume of the block immersed in the water change?

- It increases, because the block presses more on the water
- It stays the same
- It decreases, because the buoyant force increases
- It increases in proportion to the acceleration of the elevator

## Explanation

In the reference frame of the elevator, which accelerates upward with acceleration $a$, every body behaves as if the acceleration of gravity were $g + a$.
The apparent weight of the block therefore becomes $\rho_c V (g + a)$.
The water is subject to the same effective gravity, therefore by the **hydrostatic pressure law** the pressures in the water are given by $\rho_a (g + a)$ times the depth, and the buoyant force becomes $\rho_a V_{\mathrm{imm}} (g + a)$.
Equating the buoyant force and the weight the factor $g + a$ cancels, so by the **condition for flotation** the immersed fraction stays $\dfrac{V_{\mathrm{imm}}}{V} = \dfrac{\rho_c}{\rho_a}$.
