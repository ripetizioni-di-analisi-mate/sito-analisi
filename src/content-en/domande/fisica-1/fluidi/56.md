In a hydraulic press filled with oil ($\rho = 900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) the small piston has area $2.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and the large piston $200\,\mathrm{cm^2}$; the large piston is $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ higher than the small piston. The pistons have negligible mass. What force must be applied to the small piston to support a load of $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$ on the large piston?

- $20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $17\,\mathrm{N}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $23\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Under the large piston the gauge pressure of the oil must be $\dfrac{F_2}{A_2} = \dfrac{2.0 \cdot 10^3}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
The small piston is $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ lower, so by the **hydrostatic pressure law** the gauge pressure under it is greater by $\rho g \Delta y = 900 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.50\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.32 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
Therefore
$$
F_1 = A_1 \left( \frac{F_2}{A_2} + \rho g \Delta y \right) = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot \left( 1.0 \cdot 10^5 + 1.32 \cdot 10^4 \right)\mathrm{N} = 23\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
The value $20\,\mathrm{N}$ neglects the difference in height between the pistons, and $17\,\mathrm{N}$ subtracts the term $\rho g \Delta y$ instead of adding it.
