A wooden raft ($\rho = 600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) has volume $0.50\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and floats in fresh water. What is the maximum mass of the load it can carry without ending up completely under water?

- $3.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

In the limiting condition the raft is completely immersed and the buoyant force $\rho_a V g$ balances the weight of the raft plus that of the load. So $\rho_a V g = \rho_{\mathrm{wood}} V g + m g$, and the mass of the load is
$$
m = (\rho_a - \rho_{\mathrm{wood}})\,V = (1000 - 600) \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} = 2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg} .
$$
The value $5.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{kg}$ neglects the weight of the raft, and $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$ is the number that expresses the weight of the load in newtons.
