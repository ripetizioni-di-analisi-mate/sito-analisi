Water comes out of a tap with an opening of diameter $1.20\,\mathrm{cm}$, moving downward with speed $1.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and forms a continuous stream. Neglecting air resistance, what is the diameter of the stream $10.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the tap?

- $0.70\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.91\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $1.58\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Each water particle falls freely, so after a drop $h$ it has speed $v = \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2 g h} = \sqrt{1.00 + 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.100}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.72\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
The stream is a flow tube in steady flow, therefore by the **continuity equation** $d_0^2\, v_0 = d^2\, v$, where $d$ is the diameter.
It follows that
$$
d = d_0 \sqrt{\frac{v_0}{v}} = 1.20 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1.00}{1.72}}\,\mathrm{cm} = 0.91\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The stream becomes thinner because it accelerates. The value $0.70\,\mathrm{cm}$ makes the diameter vary in inverse proportion to the speed, forgetting that the speed is related to the area, that is, to the square of the diameter.
