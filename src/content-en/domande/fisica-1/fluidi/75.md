Water, modeled as an ideal incompressible fluid, flows steadily through a horizontal pipe. It moves at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the wider section and faster in the constriction. The initial data are the water density $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the speed in the constriction $v = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In the second configuration, change the water density to $950\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the speed in the constriction to $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, keeping the other data fixed. What is the final value of $p$ minus its initial value?

- $12500\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $12400\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $9100\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $-12500\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

At the same height, Bernoulli's equation shows that the sum of the pressure and kinetic terms is constant $\rho v^2/2$. Pressure falls as speed rises. The relation $p = \dfrac{\rho \cdot \left(v^{2} - 4\right)}{2}$ gives $16000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ before the change and $28500\,\mathrm{Pa}$ after it. Subtracting the initial value from the final value gives $12500\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
