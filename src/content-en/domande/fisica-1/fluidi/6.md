A homogeneous plastic cylinder floats in water with three quarters of its volume immersed. What fraction of its volume stays immersed if the cylinder floats in an oil of density $900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{27}{40}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{5}{6}$

## Explanation

By the **condition for flotation** the immersed fraction is the ratio of the density of the body to that of the liquid. In water the fraction is $\dfrac{3}{4}$, so the plastic has density $\rho_c = \dfrac{3}{4} \cdot 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} = 750\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. In the oil the immersed fraction is therefore
$$
\frac{V_{\mathrm{imm}}}{V} = \frac{750}{900} = \frac{5}{6} .
$$
The oil is less dense than water, so the immersed fraction is greater in the oil. The fraction $\dfrac{1}{6}$ is the part of the cylinder that stays out of the oil.
