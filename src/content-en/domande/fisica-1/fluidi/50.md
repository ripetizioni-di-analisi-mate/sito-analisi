A U-tube of constant cross-section contains water. Someone blows into one of the arms, and the water surface in that arm goes down by $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ with respect to the initial equilibrium position. What is the gauge pressure of the blown air?

- $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $7.8 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.9 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The cross-section is constant and water is incompressible, so the volume that goes down in one arm rises in the other, and the other surface rises by $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.
The level difference between the two surfaces is therefore $h = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.
At the height of the surface in the arm where one blows the pressure is that of the blown air, and in the other arm, at the same height, it is $p_0 + \rho g h$ by the **hydrostatic pressure law**. Hence
$$
p - p_0 = \rho g h = 1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{Pa} = 3.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $2.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{Pa}$ uses as level difference only the $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ drop.
