In a very wide fire hose the water is practically at rest. At its end there is a nozzle pointing upward, at the same height as the hose, and the jet reaches $20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ above the nozzle. Neglecting friction and air resistance, what gauge pressure does the water in the hose have?

- $1.96 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $9.81 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.97 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.98 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The jet rises by $h = 20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ subject only to its weight, so at the exit of the nozzle the water has a speed $v$ with $\dfrac{1}{2} v^2 = g h$.
**Bernoulli's theorem** is applied between the hose, where the water is at rest at pressure $p$, and the exit of the nozzle, at the same height and at pressure $p_0$, which gives $p = p_0 + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$.
Therefore the gauge pressure in the hose is
$$
p - p_0 = \rho g h = 1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 20.0\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.96 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $2.97 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is the absolute pressure, and $9.81 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is obtained by writing $v^2 = g h$ instead of $v^2 = 2 g h$.
