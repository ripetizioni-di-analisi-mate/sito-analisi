Flow in a cylindrical capillary is laminar and satisfies Poiseuille's assumptions. Initially the flow rate is $2\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ and the radius is $1\,\mathrm{mm}$. Length and viscosity stay constant. Given the new radius $r = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the new flow rate $Q = 16\,\mathrm{mL/s}$, what is the new-to-old pressure-drop ratio $b$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $4$

## Explanation

Poiseuille flow is proportional to the fourth power of radius and to the pressure drop. In the stated units, the relation is $Q = 2 \cdot b \cdot r^{4}$. Solving for the unknown gives $b = \dfrac{Q}{2 \cdot r^{4}}$. Substituting the given values yields $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
