Water, modeled as an ideal incompressible fluid, flows steadily through a horizontal pipe. It moves at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the wider section and faster in the constriction. Given the speed in the constriction $v = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the pressure drop from the wider section to the constriction $p = 16000\,\mathrm{Pa}$, what is the water density $\rho$?

- $\dfrac{8000}{9}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $2000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $500\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

At the same height, Bernoulli's equation shows that the sum of the pressure and kinetic terms is constant $\rho v^2/2$. Pressure falls as speed rises. In the stated units, the relation is $p = \dfrac{\rho \cdot \left(v^{2} - 4\right)}{2}$. Solving for the unknown gives $\rho = \dfrac{2 \cdot p}{v^{2} - 4}$. Substituting the given values yields $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.
