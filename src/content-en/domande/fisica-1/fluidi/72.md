Blood passes from the aorta into an enormous number of capillaries, each much thinner than the aorta, and in the capillaries it flows much more slowly than in the aorta. Which statement explains this fact?

- In a thinner duct the speed of a fluid is always lower
- The total flow rate in the capillaries is much smaller than the flow rate in the aorta
- In the capillaries the pressure is higher, and a higher pressure slows the blood down
- The sum of the cross-sections of the capillaries is much larger than the cross-section of the aorta, and the total flow rate is the same

## Explanation

The blood that crosses the aorta per unit time is distributed among all the capillaries, so the total flow rate in the capillaries equals that in the aorta.
By the **continuity equation** the average speed is the ratio of this flow rate to the total area crossed, that is, the sum of the cross-sections of all the capillaries.
Each capillary is thin, but the capillaries are so numerous that the sum of their cross-sections is much larger than the cross-section of the aorta.
Therefore the speed in the capillaries is much lower.
