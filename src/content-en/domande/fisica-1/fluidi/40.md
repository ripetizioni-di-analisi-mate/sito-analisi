The tube of a Torricelli barometer is tilted with respect to the vertical, without the mercury reaching the closed end; atmospheric pressure does not change. Which quantity stays the same as before?

- The vertical distance between the top of the column and the surface of the mercury in the dish
- The length of the mercury column measured along the tube
- The volume of mercury contained in the tube
- The length of the empty part at the top of the tube

## Explanation

In the tube, above the mercury, the pressure is negligible, and at the height of the surface in the dish the pressure is $p_0$.
By the **hydrostatic pressure law** we therefore have $p_0 = \rho_{\mathrm{Hg}}\, g h$, where $h$ is the difference in height between the two points.
Atmospheric pressure does not change, so the vertical distance $h$ stays the same.
In the tilted tube that vertical distance corresponds to a longer stretch of tube, therefore the length of the column and the volume of mercury in the tube increase, and the empty part becomes shorter.
