A wide open tank has a small hole in its wall. If the height of the water above the hole becomes $4$ times greater, how does the speed at which the water flows out of the hole change?

- It stays the same
- It becomes $4$ times greater
- It doubles
- It becomes $16$ times greater

## Explanation

By **Torricelli's theorem** the outflow speed is $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$, so it is proportional to the square root of the height $h$ of the water above the hole. If the height becomes $4h$, the speed becomes $\sqrt{2 g \cdot 4h} = 2\sqrt{2 g h}$, that is, it **doubles**. The same relation holds in free fall, where the speed gained by falling from a height $h$ is $\sqrt{2 g h}$.
