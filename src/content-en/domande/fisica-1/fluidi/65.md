In a container a layer of oil ($\rho_o = 800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$) lies on top of water. A homogeneous block of density $900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ floats completely immersed in the two liquids, partly in the water and partly in the oil. What fraction of the volume of the block is in the water?

- $0.50$
- $0.90$
- $0.10$
- $0.80$

## Explanation

Let $f$ be the fraction of the volume $V$ in the water; then the fraction $1 - f$ is in the oil.
By **Archimedes' principle** each liquid pushes with the weight of the liquid displaced, so the total buoyant force is $\left[ f \rho_a + (1 - f)\rho_o \right] V g$.
The block floats, therefore the buoyant force equals the weight $\rho_c V g$, that is, $f \rho_a + (1 - f)\rho_o = \rho_c$. It follows that
$$
f = \frac{\rho_c - \rho_o}{\rho_a - \rho_o} = \frac{900 - 800}{1000 - 800} = 0.50 .
$$
The value $0.90$ is the immersed fraction the block would have in water without the oil, and $0.10$ is the fraction that in that case would stay out of the water.
