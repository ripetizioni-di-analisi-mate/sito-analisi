An open container holds a layer of water $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ deep and, on top of it, a layer of oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ that is $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ deep. What is the gauge pressure on the bottom?

- $1.96 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.75 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.35 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.04 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

We apply **Stevin's law** to each layer, starting from the surface of the oil, where the gauge pressure is zero. At the base of the oil layer the gauge pressure is $800 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{Pa} = 785\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Across the water the pressure increases by $1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1962\,\mathrm{Pa}$, so the gauge pressure on the bottom is
$$
785\,\mathrm{Pa} + 1962\,\mathrm{Pa} = 2.75 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $1.04 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is the absolute pressure, which also includes $p_0$.
