The tip of a nail has area $0.50\,\mathrm{mm^2}$. A hammer pushes the nail against a board with a force of $40\,\mathrm{N}$, perpendicular to the board. What pressure does the tip exert on the wood?

- $8.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

One square millimeter is $10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the area of the tip is $A = 0.50 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
The force is perpendicular to the surface, therefore the pressure is the ratio of the force to the area:
$$
p = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{40}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 8.0 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
The value $8.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is obtained by converting square millimeters as if they were millimeters, that is, with the factor $10^{-3}$. The same force spread over a very small area produces a very high pressure, which is why the nail penetrates the wood.
