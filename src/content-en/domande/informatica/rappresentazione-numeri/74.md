A six-bit two’s-complement word has four fractional bits. How many source words can be converted without a change in value to a five-bit two’s-complement format with two fractional bits?

- $32$
- $64$
- $16$
- $8$

## Explanation

If $K$ is the source integer and $J$ the destination integer, equality $\dfrac{K}{16}=\dfrac{J}{4}$ requires $K=4J$. Between $-32$ and $31$, the multiples of four run from $-32$ through $28$. They correspond to the sixteen integers $J$ from $-8$ through $7$, all representable in five bits.
