`p` holds the only starting address of an allocated block. With `bytes > 0` representable as `size_t` and `<stdlib.h>` included, execute `p = realloc(p, bytes);` and suppose the call fails. What is the outcome?

- The block is released and `p` becomes null.
- The block remains alive and its starting address is lost.
- The block changes size and `p` becomes null.
- The block remains alive and `p` retains its address.

## Explanation

With a positive request, failed `realloc` leaves the original block alive. Its return value is `NULL` and is stored directly in `p`. If `p` held the only reference, the program thereby loses the address needed to access or release the block.
