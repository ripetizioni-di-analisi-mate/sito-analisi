A pure recursive function on decreasing natural-number arguments may return zero. Its table is initially all zeros, and only nonzero cells are treated as already computed. Assuming every uncached computation is correct and terminates, what concrete defect remains?

- A state whose result is zero can be recomputed on every request.
- Recursion necessarily becomes infinite at the first zero cell.
- Any positive result becomes zero on the second request.
- The numerical result is necessarily wrong.

## Explanation

Zero plays two roles, a legitimate result and an indicator of an empty cell. After a calculation returning zero the cell is indistinguishable from one never visited. The next result remains correct by the assumption on the function, but the work is repeated. A separate flag can record that a calculation has taken place.
