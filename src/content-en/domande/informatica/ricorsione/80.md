A recursive helper receives a prefix of length $n$ and an initial sum $a$. Its step consumes the last element $v$ and calls the child with $(n-1,a+v)$. The public function starts with $a=0$. Which inductive hypothesis allows the contract to be used for the child as well?

- The child is correct only when its accumulator is zero.
- Every child argument is numerically smaller than the corresponding parent argument.
- The child always returns just the sum of its prefix even with a nonzero accumulator.
- For every admissible accumulator, the child returns that accumulator plus the sum of its prefix.

## Explanation

The length decreases, but the child’s accumulator is $a+v$, which can be nonzero. The hypothesis must cover that value as well. Applying it gives $a+v$ plus the sum of the first $n-1$ elements, namely $a$ plus the sum of the original prefix. The required sums are assumed representable.
