A recursion splits a nonempty string into two nonempty halves and counts maximal runs of identical consecutive characters. Both children have already counted their own runs. When adding the results, when should one be subtracted?

- The halves have at least one character in common.
- The string’s first character equals its last.
- Both halves have the same length.
- The final character of the left half equals the first of the right half.

## Explanation

All runs internal to either half retain their boundaries. Only the last run on the left and the first on the right can become a single run across the cut. This happens exactly when their touching characters match. For example, splitting `abbba` into `ab` and `bba` gives $2+2-1=3$.
