It has been independently proved that, on the four integer inputs 0 through 3, `f(x)` has the exact form `a*x+b`, without overflow. To verify `f(x) == 3*x+2`, the tests observe `f(0) == 2` and `f(3) == 11`. Which conclusion is justified?

- The two tests and the proved affine form determine the results at 1 and 2 as well.
- The two observations alone determine all four values for any function.
- The specification is proved for all `int` values, even outside the domain.
- The slope must be 9 because the expected values differ by 9.

## Explanation

The observation at zero gives `b = 2`. The one at 3 imposes `3*a + 2 = 11`, hence `a = 3`. The conclusion for 1 and 2 uses the prior proof of the affine form and holds on the domain for which that form was established.
