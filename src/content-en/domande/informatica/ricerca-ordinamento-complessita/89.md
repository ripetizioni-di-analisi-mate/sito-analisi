A matrix with $R$ rows and $C$ columns, both positive, is nondecreasing along rows and columns. Search starts in its top-right corner. Move left if the current value is greater than the key, down if it is smaller, and stop on equality. What is the maximum number of cells examined?

- Exactly $RC$ in the worst case even when $R,C>1$
- $R+C-1$
- Exactly $R+C+1$ in the worst case
- $\min(R,C)$ for every matrix

## Explanation

A move left removes the current column from the remaining candidates, and a move down removes the current row. While still inside the matrix there can be at most $C-1$ left moves and $R-1$ downward moves. Counting the starting cell gives $R+C-1$, attainable by following the top edge and then the left edge.
