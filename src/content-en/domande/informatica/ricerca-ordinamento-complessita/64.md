A variant of insertion sort uses binary search for the insertion position within the prefix, then shifts every key after that position by one cell. On $n$ distinct keys initially in decreasing order, which pair best describes the costs?

- $O(n)$ comparisons and $O(1)$ shifts
- $O(n\log n)$ comparisons and $\Theta(n^2)$ shifts
- $O(\log n)$ total comparisons and $O(n)$ shifts
- $\Theta(n^2)$ comparisons and zero shifts

## Explanation

Each search in a prefix of length at most $n$ takes at most a constant multiple of $\log n$ comparisons. The new key must nevertheless enter at the beginning of the prefix. Thus $1+2+\cdots+(n-1)$ records are shifted, or $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$.
