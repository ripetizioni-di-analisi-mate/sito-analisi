We seek membership of $k$ in $n$ unsorted cells with no auxiliary index or prior information about their values. Every cell read so far differs from $k$, but one cell remains unread. Why can absence not yet be certified?

- Because every search requires sorting.
- Two inputs agreeing on every inspected cell can differ only in the unread one and give opposite membership answers.
- Because all cell pairs must be counted separately.
- Because even an empty array always contains $k$.

## Explanation

Construct two arrays that reproduce every observed read. In the unread cell, place $k$ in the first and a different value in the second. The observations agree, but the correct answers are opposite. The absence case therefore requires reading every cell.
