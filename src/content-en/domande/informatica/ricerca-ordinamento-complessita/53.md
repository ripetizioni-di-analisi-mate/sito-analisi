On sorted data, functions `L(x)` and `U(x)` give the first index whose value is respectively at least $x$ and strictly greater than $x$, or the length if none exists. How can we count integers in the closed interval $[a,b]$, where $a\le b$, even when $b$ is `INT_MAX`?

- `L(b) - L(a)`
- `U(b) - U(a)`
- `U(b) - L(a)`
- `L(b + 1) - L(a)` evaluated in `int`

## Explanation

The first eligible entry lies at boundary `L(a)`. The first entry after all eligible ones lies at `U(b)`, so the difference is the length of the range. The upper boundary uses a strict comparison that is valid at `INT_MAX` without forming its successor.
