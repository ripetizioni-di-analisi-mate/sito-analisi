On $n=2^k$ entries, a recursive merge-sort variant splits every non-base run into equal halves. In a call of length $m>1$ it allocates an array of $m$ cells. It keeps that array alive while running the children one at a time and releases it before returning. Excluding the call stack and input, what is the tight peak number of simultaneously live auxiliary cells?

- $\Theta(\log n)$
- $\Theta(n)$
- $\Theta(n^2)$
- $\Theta(n\log n)$

## Explanation

At peak, only allocations on one root-to-leaf path are live. Their sizes are $n,\dfrac{n}{2},\dfrac{n}{4},\ldots,2$, summing to less than $2n$. The root alone allocates $n$ cells. These upper and lower bounds give the same linear order.
