What role does the first comparison play in the guard `j > 0 && a[j - 1] > key`?

- It ends the traversal when the predecessor is larger.
- It makes `a[j - 1]` always equal to `key`.
- It skips reading the predecessor when `j == 0`.
- It eliminates every shift in the prefix.

## Explanation

The first part of the guard tests whether a preceding index exists. If `j` is zero, that part is false. Short-circuit evaluation skips the right operand on that test, so the guard can stop at the left boundary without accessing an out-of-range element.
