Assume `<stdio.h>` is included and the C locale is in use. With precision $p>0$, `%g` considers the exponent that rounding to $p$ significant digits in scientific notation would produce. It uses scientific notation if this exponent is less than $-4$ or at least $p$. Without the `#` flag it removes trailing zeros from the fractional part and drops a decimal point left with no digits after it. What does the call print?

```c
printf("%.3g|%.3g", 1200.0, 12.0);
```

- `1.20e+03|12.0`
- `1.2e+03|12`
- `1200|12`
- `1.2e+03|1.2e+01`

## Explanation

The exponent of $1200$ is $3$, equal to the precision. Scientific notation is selected and `1.20e+03` loses the last zero of its significand. The exponent of $12$ is $1$, so ordinary decimal notation is selected. Three significant digits would give `12.0`, which the format shortens to `12`.
