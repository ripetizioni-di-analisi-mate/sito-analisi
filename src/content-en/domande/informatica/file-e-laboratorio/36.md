After a successful open, a program computes `int bad = fprintf(file, "%d\n", data) < 0 || fclose(file) != 0;`. Suppose `fprintf` returns a negative number. What is `bad` and what happens to the close call?

- `bad` is $1$ and the close is called.
- `bad` is $0$ and the close is skipped.
- `bad` is $0$ and the close is called.
- `bad` is $1$ and the close is skipped.

## Explanation

The comparison to the left of `||` gives $1$. Short-circuit evaluation makes the result true without evaluating the second operand. Thus `bad` is $1$ and closing does not happen through that expression. To close in either case, record its result in a separate statement and then combine both statuses.
