An isosceles right triangle has the legs of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is its maximum principal centroidal moment of inertia $I_1$?

- $54\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $36\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $18\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $72\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

The legs are placed parallel to the axes, with the right angle at the bottom left. Compared to the centroidal axes parallel to the $I_{x_G} = I_{y_G} = \dfrac{6 \cdot 6^3}{36} = 36\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = -\dfrac{6^2 \cdot 6^2}{72} = -18\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ legs. Since $I_{x_G} = I_{y_G}$, the term $\dfrac{I_{x_G} - I_{y_G}}{2}$ is zero and the formula of the principal moments gives
$$
I_{1,2} = 36 \pm \sqrt{0 + 18^2} = 36 \pm 18\,\mathrm{cm^4} .
$$
Therefore $I_1 = 54\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_2 = 18\,\mathrm{cm^4}$; The principal axes are the bisector of the right angle, the triangle's axis of symmetry, and its perpendicular.
