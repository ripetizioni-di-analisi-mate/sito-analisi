Compared to centroidal axes a section has $I_{x_G} = 600\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{y_G} = 200\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = 150\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. What is the radius of Mohr's circle for the moments of area?

- $200\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $350\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $427.20\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $250\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

The center of Mohr's circle with horizontal coordinate $\dfrac{600 + 200}{2} = 400\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, and the radius is the distance of the center from the point $X = (600;\ 150)$, so
$$
R = \sqrt{\left( \frac{600 - 200}{2} \right)^2 + 150^2} = \sqrt{200^2 + 150^2} = 250\,\mathrm{cm^4} .
$$
Therefore the principal moments are $400 \pm 250$, i.e. $650\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $150\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. The value $427.20\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ is obtained by using $I_{x_G} - I_{y_G}$ instead of its half, and $350\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ by adding $200$ and $150$ instead of composing them with the Pythagoras theorem.
