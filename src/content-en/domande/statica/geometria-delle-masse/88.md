A section has product of inertia $I_{x_G y_G} = 50\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ about its own centroidal axes. Pairs of $x'$, $y'$ axes parallel to $x_G$ and $y_G$ are considered, with the origin $O'$ in different positions, given by the coordinates of $O'$ about $G$. For what position of $O'$ does the product of inertia $I_{x'y'}$ still equal $50\,\mathrm{cm^4}$?

- $O' = (3;\ 3)\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $O' = (0;\ 5)\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $O' = (-2;\ 4)\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $O' = (2;\ -2)\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

For the **parallel-axis theorem** $I_{x'y'} = I_{x_G y_G} + A\,x_G'\,y_G'$, where $(x_G', y_G')$ are the coordinates of $G$ with respect to the $x'$, $y'$ axes, i.e. the coordinates of $O'$ with respect to $G$ changed in sign. Since $A > 0$, the parallel-axis term is zero if and only if one of the two coordinates is zero, i.e. if $O'$ lies on one of the centroidal axes. Among the proposed positions this applies only to $O' = (0;\ 5)\,\mathrm{cm}$, which lies on the axis $y_G$. For the other positions the product of the coordinates is different from zero and the product of inertia changes, whatever the area.
