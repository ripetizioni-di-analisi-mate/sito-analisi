The Z-section shown has overall height $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its web and flanges have thickness $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ and each flange has overall length $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, including the web thickness. Where is the centroid?

::struttura{poligoni="-5,-6 1,-6 1,4 5,4 5,6 -1,6 -1,-4 -5,-4" punti="0,0,O" frecce="-5.5,0,7,0,x;0,-7.3,0,7.8,y" quote="-1 6,5 6,6 cm,-0.9;-5 -6,1 -6,6 cm,0.9;-5 6,-5 -6,12 cm,1.2;5 4,5 6,2 cm,0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Z-section of height $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Point $O$ is the center of the web"}

- On the web centerline, $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ above center $O$
- Outside the web, on the side of the upper flange
- It cannot be determined without calculating the first moments of area because the section has no axes of symmetry
- At the center $O$ of the web

## Explanation

The Z-section has no symmetry axes, but it has the **center of symmetry** $O$, because rotating it by $180^\circ$ around $O$ gives the same figure. With the origin at $O$, each element $dA$ at point $(x, y)$ then corresponds to the equal element at point $(-x, -y)$. The two elements give $S_x$ the $y\,dA$ and $-y\,dA$ contributions, and $S_y$ the $x\,dA$ and $-x\,dA$ contributions, the sum of which is nil. Therefore $S_x = S_y = 0$, then $x_G = y_G = 0$ and the centroid coincide with $O$. The same reasoning applies to any figure with a center of symmetry.
