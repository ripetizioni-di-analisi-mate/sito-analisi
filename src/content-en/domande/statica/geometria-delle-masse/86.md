A section of area $50\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ has the radii of gyration centroidal $\rho_{x_G} = 4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\rho_{y_G} = 3\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is its polar moment of area about the centroid?

- $1250\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $250\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $350\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $625\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

For the definition of radius of gyration $I_{x_G} = A\,\rho_{x_G}^2 = 50 \cdot 16 = 800\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{y_G} = A\,\rho_{y_G}^2 = 50 \cdot 9 = 450\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. The polar moment about $G$ is the sum of the moments about the two orthogonal centroidal axes, therefore
$$
I_p = 800 + 450 = A\left( \rho_{x_G}^2 + \rho_{y_G}^2 \right) = 1250\,\mathrm{cm^4} .
$$
The value $250\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ multiplies the area by $\sqrt{\rho_{x_G}^2 + \rho_{y_G}^2} = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$ without elevating it to square; the value $350\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ multiplies the area by the sum of the rays.
