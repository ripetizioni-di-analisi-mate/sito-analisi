For the L-section in the figure, with the horizontal long flange $12\,\mathrm{cm}$, the vertical high flange $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ and both thick $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, the centroidal moments equal $I_{x_G} = 884\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{y_G} = 596\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = -420\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. What is the maximum principal moment of inertia $I_1$?

- $1249.26\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $1160\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $884\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $1184\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

$C = \dfrac{I_{x_G} + I_{y_G}}{2} = 740\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $D = \dfrac{I_{x_G} - I_{y_G}}{2} = 144\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ are placed. By the formula of the principal moments
$$
I_1 = C + \sqrt{D^2 + I_{x_G y_G}^2} = 740 + \sqrt{144^2 + 420^2} = 740 + 444 = 1184\,\mathrm{cm^4} .
$$
The value $1249.26\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ is obtained using $I_{x_G} - I_{y_G}$ instead of its half, and $1160\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ adding to $C$ the product of inertia only. Because $I_{x_G y_G} \neq 0$, the axis $x_G$ is not main, so $884\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ is not the maximum moment.
