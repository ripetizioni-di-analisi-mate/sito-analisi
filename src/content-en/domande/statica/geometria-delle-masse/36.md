An $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ side square has the centroid in the origin and the sides parallel to the $x$ and $y$ axes. What is its second moment of area about a diagonal?

- $54\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $216\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $36\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $108\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

For the square $I_x = I_y = \dfrac{a^4}{12} = \dfrac{1296}{12} = 108\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, and $I_{xy} = 0$ because the axes are of symmetry. In the formula of rotation of the axes with $\alpha = 45^\circ$ the coefficient $\dfrac{I_x - I_y}{2}$ of $\cos 2\alpha$ and the product of inertia are zero, therefore
$$
I_\xi = \frac{I_x + I_y}{2} = 108\,\mathrm{cm^4} .
$$
The same value is obtained with the two triangles in which the diagonal divides the square, each with base $6\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$, height $3\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$ and moment $\dfrac{b h^3}{12} = 54\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ about the diagonal. The value $216\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ is the polar moment, and $36\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ is obtained by using the formula centroidal $\dfrac{b h^3}{36}$ for triangles.
