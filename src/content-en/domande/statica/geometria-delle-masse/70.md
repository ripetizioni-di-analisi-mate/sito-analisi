About the $x_G$ and $y_G$ centroidal axes a section has $I_{x_G} = I_{y_G} = 400\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = -150\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. Which centroidal axis gives the largest second moment of area, and what is its value?

- The axis rotated $45^\circ$ clockwise from $x_G$, with $550\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- The axis rotated counterclockwise by $45^\circ$ from $x_G$, with $550\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- The $x_G$ axis, with $400\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- The axis rotated counterclockwise by $45^\circ$ from $x_G$, with $250\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

Since $I_{x_G} = I_{y_G}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} \neq 0$, the principal axes are the bisectors of the $x_G$ and $y_G$ axes. For the rotation formula of the axes with $\alpha = 45^\circ$ you have $\cos 2\alpha = 0$ and $\sin 2\alpha = 1$, so
$$
I_\xi = 400 - (-150) \cdot 1 = 550\,\mathrm{cm^4} .
$$
For the axis rotated clockwise through $45^\circ$, $\sin 2\alpha = -1$. The same calculation gives the minimum, $400 - 150 = 250\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. The maximum $I_1 = 550\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ therefore belongs to the **axis rotated counterclockwise through $45^\circ$**, which passes through the first and third quadrants of the centroidal coordinate system.
