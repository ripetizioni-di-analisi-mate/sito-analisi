The rectangle in the figure is $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ wide along $x$ and $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ high along $y$, with a vertex at the origin $O$ and the sides on the positive axes. Through what angle are the principal axes of inertia through $O$ rotated from axes $x$ and $y$?

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 4,0 4,2 0,2" punti="0,0,O" frecce="0,0,5.5,0,x;0,0,0,3.5,y" quote="0 0,4 0,4 cm,0.8;4 0,4 2,2 cm,0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangle of dimensions $4 \times 2\,\mathrm{cm}$ with one vertex at the origin"}

- By $22.5^\circ$ clockwise
- Counterclockwise through $22.5^\circ$
- Counterclockwise through $26.57^\circ$, along the diagonal of the rectangle
- They are not rotated because axes $x$ and $y$ are already principal axes

## Explanation

The $x$ and $y$ axes contain two sides, then $I_x = \dfrac{4 \cdot 2^3}{3} = \dfrac{32}{3}\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_y = \dfrac{2 \cdot 4^3}{3} = \dfrac{128}{3}\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. For the rectangle with a vertex in the origin and sides on positive axes $I_{xy} = \dfrac{b^2 h^2}{4} = \dfrac{16 \cdot 4}{4} = 16\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, other than zero, therefore $x$ and $y$ are not principal axes. The formula of the direction of the principal axes gives
$$
\tan 2\alpha_0 = -\frac{2 \cdot 16}{\dfrac{32}{3} - \dfrac{128}{3}} = -\frac{32}{-32} = 1 ,
$$
So $2\alpha_0 = 45^\circ$ and $\alpha_0 = 22.5^\circ$. Therefore the principal axes for $O$ are rotated $22.5^\circ$ counterclockwise, along with their perpendicular tilted $112.5^\circ$. The diagonal, tilted by $26.57^\circ$, passes through the centroid, but about it and its perpendicular to $O$ the product of inertia is different from zero.
