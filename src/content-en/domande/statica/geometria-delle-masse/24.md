The isosceles triangle in the figure has the horizontal base and the $O$ vertex opposite the base. Which principal axes of inertia pass through $O$?

::struttura{poligoni="-3,0 3,0 0,8" punti="0,8,O" quote="-3 0,3 0,6 cm,0.9;3 0,3 8,8 cm,1.2" riferimento=0 titolo="Isosceles triangle of base $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, height $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, and vertex $O$"}

- The vertical and horizontal lines through $O$
- The two lines through $O$ inclined at $45^\circ$ to the vertical
- No pair, because $O$ is not the centroid of the triangle
- Every pair of perpendicular lines through $O$

## Explanation

The vertical line for $O$ is the symmetry axis of the triangle. For the product of inertia and symmetry theorem, if one of the two axes is the figure's axis of symmetry, the product of inertia about the pair of axes is zero, even when the origin of the axes is not the centroid. So the vertical line and the horizontal line for $O$ have $I_{xy} = 0$ and are main inertia axes for $O$. The principal axes exist for each point of the plan; the centroidal ones are a special case. Each couple would only be main if the second moments of area about the two axes were equal, while here the moment about the horizontal line for $O$ equals $\dfrac{b h^3}{4} = 768\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and that about the vertical equals $\dfrac{h b^3}{48} = 36\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.
