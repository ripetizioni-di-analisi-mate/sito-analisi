One section has the radius of gyration $\rho_{x_G} = 3\,\mathrm{cm}$ about the horizontal centroidal axis, and its centroid is $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis $x$, parallel to $x_G$. What is the radius of gyration $\rho_x$ about axis $x$?

- $5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $7\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2.65\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $4\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

For the **parallel-axis theorem** $I_x = I_{x_G} + A\,y_G^2$. For the definition of radius of gyration $I_x = A\,\rho_x^2$ and $I_{x_G} = A\,\rho_{x_G}^2$, then, dividing by area,
$$
\rho_x^2 = \rho_{x_G}^2 + y_G^2 = (9 + 16)\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 25\,\mathrm{cm^2} .
$$
Therefore $\rho_x = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the result does not depend on the cross-sectional area. The value $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ adds the rays instead of their squares, $2.65\,\mathrm{cm}$ subtracts the squares, and $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the only distance of the centroid from the axis.
