The section in the figure is a quarter annulus with the center in the origin, the outer radius of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, the inner radius of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the straight sides on the positive axes $x$ and $y$. What is the product of inertia $I_{xy}$ about axes $x$ and $y$?

## Explanation

The section is the quarter circle of radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ minus the quarter circle of radius $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. For a quarter circle of radius $R$ in the first quadrant the strip at $y$ elevation goes from $x = 0$ to $x = \sqrt{R^2 - y^2}$, so it makes the contribution $y \cdot \dfrac{R^2 - y^2}{2}\,dy$ and
$$
I_{xy} = \int_0^R y\,\frac{R^2 - y^2}{2}\,dy = \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{R^4}{2} - \frac{R^4}{4} \right) = \frac{R^4}{8} .
$$
For the $I_{xy} = \dfrac{6^4 - 4^4}{8} = \dfrac{1296 - 256}{8} = 130\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ additivity. The value is positive because in each element $x > 0$ and $y > 0$. The bisector of the first quadrant is an axis of symmetry, but the $x$ and $y$ axes are not, so the product of inertia about $x$ and $y$ is different from zero. The value $260\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ is obtained with $\dfrac{R^4}{4}$, the formula of the square of the $R$ side.
