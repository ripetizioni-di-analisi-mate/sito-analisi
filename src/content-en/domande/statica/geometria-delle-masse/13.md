A rectangle of width $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ along $x$ and height $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ along $y$ has its centroid at the origin. What is the product of inertia $I_{\xi\eta}$ about centroidal axes $\xi$ and $\eta$ rotated counterclockwise by $60^\circ$ from axes $x$ and $y$?

- $-160\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $0\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $160\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $320\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

The $x$ and $y$ axes are symmetry axes, hence $I_{xy} = 0$, while $I_x = \dfrac{8 \cdot 12^3}{12} = 1152\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_y = \dfrac{12 \cdot 8^3}{12} = 512\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. For axis rotation formula with $2\alpha = 120^\circ$
$$
I_{\xi\eta} = \frac{I_x - I_y}{2}\sin 120^\circ + I_{xy}\cos 120^\circ = 320 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 160\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm^4} .
$$
The rotated axes are not axis of symmetry of the rectangle, so the product of inertia about them is different from zero. The value $320\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ is obtained using $I_x - I_y$ instead of its half.
