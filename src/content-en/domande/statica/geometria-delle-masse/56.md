A circular section of $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ radius has the center at point $(-3, 4)$, with coordinates in $\mathrm{cm}$. What is its product of inertia $I_{xy}$ about axes $x$ and $y$?

- $48\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $0\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $-12\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $-48\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

The centroidal axes of the circle parallel to $x$ and $y$ are symmetry axes, hence $I_{x_G y_G} = 0$. For the **parallel-axis theorem** applied to the product of inertia
$$
I_{xy} = I_{x_G y_G} + A\,x_G\,y_G = 0 + 4\pi \cdot (-3) \cdot 4 = -48\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4} .
$$
In the parallel-axis term the coordinates of the centroid enter with their sign, and the circle lies on the second quadrant, where $x < 0$ and $y > 0$; therefore the product of inertia is negative. The null value is only valid with respect to the centroidal axes.
