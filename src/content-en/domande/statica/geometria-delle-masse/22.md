A section is divided into two parts. About the horizontal line $y = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$, with positive distances upwards, the first moments of area of the two parts equal $40\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and $-64\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. What can be concluded about the location of the section centroid?

- It lies above line $y = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- It lies on line $y = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- It lies above or below the line, depending on the areas of the two parts
- It lies below line $y = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The first moment of area is additive, so the first moment of area of the section about the line is $S_r = 40 - 64 = -24\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. By the theorem on the first moment of area about a line $S_r = A\,d_G$, where $d_G$ is the distance with an $G$ sign from the line. Since $A > 0$ and $S_r < 0$, you have $d_G < 0$, so the centroid stands **below** the line $y = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$. The area only intervenes in the value of $d_G = \dfrac{-24\,\mathrm{cm^3}}{A}$, and the centroid would stand on the line if the sum of the two first moments of area were zero.
