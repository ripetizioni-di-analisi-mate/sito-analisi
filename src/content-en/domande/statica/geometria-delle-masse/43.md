Compared to centroidal axes a section has $I_{x_G} = 500\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{y_G} = 300\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = -80\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. In Mohr's circle of inertia, each $\xi$ axis for $G$ corresponds to the point $(I_\xi, I_{\xi\eta})$, where $\eta$ is the rotated axis of $90^\circ$ counterclockwise about $\xi$. Which point corresponds to axis $y_G$?

- $(300;\ -80)\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $(500;\ 80)\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $(300;\ 80)\,\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $(400;\ 0)\,\mathrm{cm^4}$

## Explanation

The rotated $\xi$ axis of $\alpha$ corresponds to the point $(I_\xi, I_{\xi\eta})$. The axis $y_G$ is obtained by rotating $x_G$ of $90^\circ$ counterclockwise, and for the rotation formula with $2\alpha = 180^\circ$ you have $I_\xi = I_{y_G} = 300\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{\xi\eta} = -I_{x_G y_G} = 80\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. So the axis $y_G$ corresponds to the point $Y = (300;\ 80)$, diametrically opposed to $X = (500;\ -80)$. The point $(400;\ 0)$ is the center of the circle.
