The L-section in the figure has the two long flanges $12\,\mathrm{cm}$, including the edge, and thick $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. In which region does the centroid lie?

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 12,0 12,2 2,2 2,12 0,12" frecce="0,0,14,0,x;0,0,0,14,y" quote="0 0,12 0,12 cm,0.9;0 0,0 12,12 cm,-0.9;12 0,12 2,2 cm,0.8;0 12,2 12,2 cm,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="L-section with legs of length $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and thickness $2\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

- In the horizontal leg
- In the vertical leg
- Outside the section, between the legs
- In the corner square common to both legs

## Explanation

Divide the section into the horizontal flange of $12 \times 2\,\mathrm{cm}$, with area $24\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and centroid $(6, 1)$, and into the vertical part of $2 \times 10\,\mathrm{cm}$, with area $20\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and centroid $(1, 7)$. For the centroid formula of a composite figure
$$
x_G = \frac{24 \cdot 6 + 20 \cdot 1}{44}\,\mathrm{cm} = 3.73\,\mathrm{cm} ,
\qquad
y_G = \frac{24 \cdot 1 + 20 \cdot 7}{44}\,\mathrm{cm} = 3.73\,\mathrm{cm} .
$$
The points in the section have $x \leq 2\,\mathrm{cm}$ or $y \leq 2\,\mathrm{cm}$, while $G$ has both coordinates greater than $2\,\mathrm{cm}$; So the centroid falls **outside the section**, between the two flanges. In open sections such as L and C sections the centroid can stay out of the material.
