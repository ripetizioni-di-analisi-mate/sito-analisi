The section in the figure is a quarter circle of radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, with the center in the origin and the extreme rays on the positive axes $x$ and $y$. Where is the centroid?

- $x_G = y_G = 2.55\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $x_G = y_G = 2\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The bisector of the first quadrant is an axis of symmetry of the figure, so the centroid lies on it and $x_G = y_G$. The quarter circle is half of the semicircle with the diameter on the axis $y$, and the axis $x$ divides that semicircle into two symmetrical halves. The semicircle has first moment of area about the diameter $\dfrac{2r^3}{3} = 144\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, so the quarter circle has $S_y = 72\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Since the area is $A = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 36}{4} = 9\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, you have
$$
x_G = \frac{S_y}{A} = \frac{72}{9\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{8}{\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = 2.55\,\mathrm{cm} = y_G .
$$
It is the same distance $\dfrac{4r}{3\pi}$ as the centroid of the semicircle from the diameter.
