The following four sections all have the area of $36\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Which has the largest second moment of area about its own horizontal centroidal axis?

- The square of side $6\,\mathrm{cm}$
- The rectangle of width $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- The rectangle of width $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- The circle of area $36\,\mathrm{cm^2}$

## Explanation

For a rectangle, $I_{x_G} = \dfrac{b h^3}{12}$, where $h$ is the vertical dimension. The square gives $\dfrac{6 \cdot 6^3}{12} = 108\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. The rectangle of height $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ gives $\dfrac{3 \cdot 12^3}{12} = 432\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, and the one of height $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ gives $\dfrac{12 \cdot 3^3}{12} = 27\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. For the circle, $I_{x_G} = \dfrac{\pi r^4}{4} = \dfrac{A^2}{4\pi} = 103.1\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. The largest value therefore belongs to the **rectangle of width $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $12\,\mathrm{cm}$**. For the same total area, this shape places more material farther from the horizontal axis.
