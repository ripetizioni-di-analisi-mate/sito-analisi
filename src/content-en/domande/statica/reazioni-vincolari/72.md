Beam $AB$ is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ long and has a pin at $A$ and a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. At a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, a vertical force of $8\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. A student draws the reaction $V_A$ downward on the free-body diagram, takes that direction as positive in the equations, and obtains $V_A = -6\,\mathrm{kN}$. What vertical force does the pin exert on the beam?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="1 0,0,-1.2,8 kN" quote="A,1 0,1 m;1 0,B,3 m" titolo="Simply supported beam under a force a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pin"}

- $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward

## Explanation

A reaction’s sign refers to its assumed direction in the free-body diagram. A negative result reverses that assumption. The student took $V_A$ as positive downward, so $V_A = -6\,\mathrm{kN}$ means $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward. Taking moments about $A$ gives $4\,V_B - 8 \cdot 1 = 0$, or $V_B = 2\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward. Using the student’s sign convention in the vertical force balance gives $-V_A + 2 - 8 = 0$, hence $V_A = -6\,\mathrm{kN}$. The value $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the roller reaction.
