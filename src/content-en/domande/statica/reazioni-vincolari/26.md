Beam $AB$ is $6\,\mathrm{m}$ long and has a pin at $A$ and a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. A triangular load begins $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and ends $5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. It increases linearly from $0$ to $q_0 = 6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ over that segment. The rest of the beam is unloaded. What is the reaction $V_B$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="2 0,5 0,0,0.9,q_0" quote="A,2 0,2 m;2 0,5 0,3 m;5 0,B,1 m" titolo="Simply supported beam with a triangular load on an interior segment"}

- $V_B = 5.25\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_B = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_B = 4.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_B = 3\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward

## Explanation

The loaded length is $3\,\mathrm{m}$, so the resultant load is $Q = \dfrac{6 \cdot 3}{2} = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$. It acts at the centroid of the triangle, one third of the loaded length from the end where the intensity is $q_0$. That places the resultant $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from the point located $5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Its distance from $A$ is therefore $4\,\mathrm{m}$. Taking moments about $A$ gives
$$
6\,V_B - 9 \cdot 4 = 0 ,
$$
Thus $V_B = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts upward. The value $3\,\mathrm{kN}$ measures the centroid location from the beginning of the beam instead of the beginning of the loaded segment.
