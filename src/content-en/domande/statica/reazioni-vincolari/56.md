Beam $AB$ is $6\,\mathrm{m}$ long and has pins at $A$ and $B$. At a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, a vertical force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. Which statement about the reactions follows from the equilibrium equations alone?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="2 0,0,-1.5,F" quote="A,2 0,2 m;2 0,B,4 m" titolo="Beam with two pin supports"}

- All four are uniquely determined, with $H_A = H_B = 0$, $V_A = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$, and $V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$
- None of the reactions is uniquely determined because there are more unknowns than equations
- $H_A = H_B = 0$, while $V_A$ and $V_B$ depend on a parameter
- $V_A = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$, while $H_A + H_B = 0$ is the only known relation between $H_A$ and $H_B$

## Explanation

The pins provide four unknown reaction components $H_A$, $V_A$, $H_B$, and $V_B$. Only three planar equilibrium equations are available, so the beam is statically indeterminate. Both horizontal reactions act along the beam axis and have zero moment about $A$ and $B$. Taking moments about $A$ gives $6\,V_B - 12 \cdot 2 = 0$, or $V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$. Taking moments about $B$ gives $V_A = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$. Horizontal force equilibrium gives only $H_A + H_B = 0$, satisfied by any pair of equal and opposite horizontal reactions. Their actual values depend on the deformation of the beam.
