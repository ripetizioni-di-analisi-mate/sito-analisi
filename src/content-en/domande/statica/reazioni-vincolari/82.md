Rigid member $AB$ is $3\,\mathrm{m}$ long and free to move in the plane. At $A$, a vertical force of $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. At $B$, a vertical force of $3\,\mathrm{kN}$ also acts downward. What additional force would bring the member into equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" forze="A,0,-1.4,6 kN;B,0,-0.8,3 kN" quote="A,B,3 m,0.4" titolo="Free member under two vertical forces"}

- An upward force of magnitude $9\,\mathrm{kN}$ at midspan, a distance $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$
- An upward force of magnitude $9\,\mathrm{kN}$ applied a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$
- An upward force of magnitude $9\,\mathrm{kN}$ applied a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$
- An upward force of magnitude $3\,\mathrm{kN}$ applied a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$

## Explanation

The required force $\vec{E}$ must make both the resultant force and resultant moment zero. Vertical force equilibrium gives $E - 6 - 3 = 0$, hence $E = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward. Taking moments about $A$ gives $9\,x - 3 \cdot 3 = 0$, where $x$ is the distance from $A$ to the line of action of $\vec{E}$. Thus $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$. The balancing force acts opposite to the resultant of the two applied forces. By Varignon’s theorem that resultant acts $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, closer to the larger force. The value $2\,\mathrm{m}$ places it closer to the smaller force.
