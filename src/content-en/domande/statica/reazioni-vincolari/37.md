Beam $AB$ is $6\,\mathrm{m}$ long and has only one support, a pin at $A$. At end $B$, a vertical force of $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. At point $C$, a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, an upward force $F$ keeps the beam in equilibrium. What is the vertical pin reaction $V_A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera" forze="B,0,-1,6 kN;C,0,1.3,F" quote="A,C,2 m,0.9;C,B,4 m,0.9" titolo="Beam restrained only by a pin at $A$"}

- $V_A = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_A = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_A = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $V_A = 18\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward

## Explanation

With only the pin, the beam is unstable because it can rotate about $A$. Equilibrium is possible only if the applied loads have zero net moment about $A$. Taking moments about $A$ eliminates the pin reactions and gives
$$
2\,F - 6 \cdot 6 = 0 ,
$$
Thus $F = 18\,\mathrm{kN}$. Vertical force equilibrium then gives $V_A + 18 - 6 = 0$, or $V_A = -12\,\mathrm{kN}$. The pin therefore exerts $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward. The answer $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward omits force $F$ from the vertical balance.
