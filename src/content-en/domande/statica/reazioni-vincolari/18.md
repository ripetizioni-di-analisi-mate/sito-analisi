Horizontal beam $AB$ is $5\,\mathrm{m}$ long and has a pin at $A$ and a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. It carries an inclined force $F$. The equations for moment equilibrium about $A$ and about $B$ have already been written. Which third equation ensures that both the resultant force and the resultant moment on the beam are zero?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="2 0,0.9,-1.2,F" quote="A,2 0,2 m;2 0,B,3 m" titolo="Simply supported beam under an inclined force"}

- Vertical translation
- Horizontal translation
- Moment equilibrium about the midpoint of $AB$
- None, because two moment equations suffice

## Explanation

If the moments about $A$ and $B$ both vanish, shifting the moment center gives $(A - B) \times \vec{R} = \vec{0}$. The resultant $\vec{R}$ must therefore be zero or parallel to the horizontal line $AB$. Horizontal force equilibrium sets the horizontal component of $\vec{R}$ to zero and, together with the two moment equations, gives $\vec{R} = \vec{0}$. Vertical force equilibrium cannot exclude a horizontal resultant because line $AB$ is perpendicular to the vertical. The midpoint of $AB$ lies on the same line as $A$ and $B$, so taking moments there adds no independent condition.
