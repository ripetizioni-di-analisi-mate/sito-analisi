A canopy consists of a horizontal beam $AB$ of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$ pinned to a wall at $A$. A tie rod $BD$ is pinned to the beam end $B$ and to the wall at $D$, a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ above $A$. A uniform load $q = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts over the entire beam. What is the tensile force in the tie rod?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;D,0,3" aste="A-B" catene="B-D" vincoli="A,cerniera,180;D,cerniera,180" carichi="A,B,0.4,0.4,q" quote="A,B,4 m,0.8;A,D,3 m,-0.9" titolo="Canopy pinned to a wall and supported by a tie rod"}

- $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $7.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension

## Explanation

The tie is pinned at both ends and carries no intermediate loads. It therefore exerts a tensile force $S$ along $BD$, toward $D$. Its length is $\sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5\,\mathrm{m}$, so the force has a vertical component $\dfrac{3}{5}\,S$ upward and a horizontal component $\dfrac{4}{5}\,S$ toward the wall. The horizontal component acts along the beam axis and has no moment about $A$. The load resultant $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ has moment arm $2\,\mathrm{m}$, giving
$$
\frac{3}{5}\,S \cdot 4 - 12 \cdot 2 = 0 .
$$
The tie carries a tensile force of $S = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$. The answer $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ treats the entire tie force as vertical. The answer $7.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ uses the cosine $\dfrac{4}{5}$ in place of the sine $\dfrac{3}{5}$.
