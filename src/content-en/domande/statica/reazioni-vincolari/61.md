The L-shaped frame $ACB$ consists of a column $AC$ of height $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and a crossbeam $CB$ of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$, welded together at $C$. Three two-force links support the frame. The link at $A$ is horizontal, and the link at $B$ is vertical. The inclined link at $C$ runs from $C$ toward point $K = (4\,\mathrm{m}, 0)$, directly below $B$ and at the same elevation as $A$. How is the structure classified?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,0,3;B,4,3" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,pendolo,180;B,pendolo,270;C,pendolo,323.13" punti="4,0,K" tratteggi="0,0 4,0;4,3 4,0;0,3 4,0" quote="A,C,3 m,-1.2;C,B,4 m,-1" titolo="L-shaped beam supported by three two-force links"}

- Statically determinate because there are three scalar restraints and the reactions are not parallel
- Unstable because the lines of action of all three reactions pass through the same point $K$
- Unstable because two of the three reactions are parallel
- Statically determinate because the line of action of the link at $C$ does not pass through $A$

## Explanation

Each two-force link supplies a reaction along its axis. The link at $A$ acts along the horizontal line $y = 0$, the link at $B$ along the vertical line $x = 4\,\mathrm{m}$, and the link at $C$ lies on a line through $K$ by construction. All three lines therefore pass through $K$. No reaction appears in the moment equation about $K$, which requires the applied loads to have zero net moment about $K$. A vertical force on the crossbeam to the left of $B$ has a nonzero moment about $K$ and cannot be balanced. Although the three scalar reactions are not parallel, the structure is **unstable**: the links allow infinitesimal rotation about $K$.
