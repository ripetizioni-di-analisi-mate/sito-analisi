The L-shaped frame $ACB$ consists of a column $AC$ of height $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and a crossbeam $CB$ of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$, welded together at $C$. It is pinned at its base $A$. At $B$, a roller bears on a surface whose inclination can be varied. The roller reaction $R_B$ is normal to this surface. For what direction of $R_B$ is the structure unstable?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,0,3;B,4,3" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,cerniera" tratteggi="0,0 4,3" quote="A,C,3 m,-0.8;C,B,4 m,-0.8" titolo="L-shaped beam with an adjustable roller inclination at $B$"}

- With $R_B$ vertical
- With $R_B$ horizontal
- With $R_B$ inclined at angle $\beta$ to the horizontal, with $\tan\beta = \dfrac{4}{3}$
- With $R_B$ inclined at angle $\beta$ to the horizontal, with $\tan\beta = \dfrac{3}{4}$, so that it acts along line $AB$

## Explanation

The pin reactions pass through $A$. Taking moments about $A$ therefore leaves only $R_B$ times the distance from $A$ to its line of action. If $R_B$ acts through $A$, its moment arm vanishes. The moment equation about $A$ then contains no unknown reaction, so the supports cannot balance a load with nonzero moment about $A$. The structure is **unstable** in this case. The line through $B = (4, 3)$ and $A$ is $AB$, inclined to the horizontal with $\tan\beta = \dfrac{3}{4}$. A vertical reaction has moment arm $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and a horizontal reaction has moment arm $3\,\mathrm{m}$. The direction with $\tan\beta = \dfrac{4}{3}$ is perpendicular to $AB$ with moment arm $5\,\mathrm{m}$. Each of these last three arrangements is statically determinate.
