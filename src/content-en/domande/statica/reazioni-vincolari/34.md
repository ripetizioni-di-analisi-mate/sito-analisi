Horizontal cantilever $AB$ is $3\,\mathrm{m}$ long and fixed to a wall at $A$. At its free end $B$, a frictionless pulley of negligible radius supports a rope of negligible weight. The rope holds a weight $W = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ directly below $B$, passes over the pulley, and runs horizontally to the right to a hook $K$ on a second wall at the elevation of the beam axis. What is the horizontal reaction $H_A$ at the fixed support?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;K,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" linee="3,0 3,-1.5;3,0 5,0;5,-0.7 5,0.7" cerchi="3,0,0.12" poligoni="2.8,-1.9 3.2,-1.9 3.2,-1.5 2.8,-1.5" etichette="3.45,-1.7,W" quote="A,B,3 m,-0.9" titolo="Cantilever with a pulley at end $B$ and a taut cable between weight $W$ and hook $K$"}

- $H_A = 0$
- $H_A = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left
- $H_A = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right
- $H_A = 2\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left

## Explanation

The cable has negligible weight and the pulley is frictionless, so both cable segments have tension $W = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$. The vertical segment pulls the pulley downward with force $4\,\mathrm{kN}$. The horizontal segment pulls it toward $K$, to the right, with force $4\,\mathrm{kN}$. The free-body diagram of the cantilever and pulley therefore includes both a vertical and a horizontal force at $B$, each of magnitude $4\,\mathrm{kN}$. Horizontal force equilibrium gives $H_A + 4 = 0$, hence $H_A = -4\,\mathrm{kN}$. The fixed support exerts $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left. The value $0$ accounts for the hanging weight alone and omits the pull of the horizontal cable segment.
