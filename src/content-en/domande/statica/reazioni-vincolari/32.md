A free rigid rod translates in the plane at a constant speed of $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to the right without rotating. A system of in-plane forces is then applied. Its resultant force and its resultant moment about one point are both zero. How does the rod move from that instant onward?

- It stops because the applied forces are in equilibrium
- It slows to a stop because the resultant force is zero
- It continues to translate at constant speed $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ without rotating
- It begins to rotate about its center of mass because the forces act at different points

## Explanation

The resultant force is zero and the moment vanishes about one point, so it vanishes about every point, including the center of mass $G$. Linear momentum balance gives $m\,\vec{a}_G = \vec{0}$, so $G$ keeps its initial speed of $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to the right. Angular momentum balance gives $\dfrac{d\vec{L}_G}{dt} = \vec{0}$. Since the rod initially has no rotation, its angular velocity stays zero and it continues in uniform translation. A balanced force system maintains rest if the body is initially at rest and maintains uniform translation if the body is already translating uniformly.
