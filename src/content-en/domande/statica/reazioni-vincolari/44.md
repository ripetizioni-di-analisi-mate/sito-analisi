Beam $AB$ is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ long, fixed at $A$, and supported by a roller on a horizontal surface at $B$. A downward force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at midspan. What can be determined about the support reactions from the equilibrium equations alone?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello" forze="2 0,0,-1.2,F" quote="A,2 0,2 m;2 0,B,2 m" titolo="Beam fixed at $A$ and supported at $B$"}

- They exist but depend on a parameter that the equations do not determine
- They are uniquely determined, with $V_A = V_B = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $M_A = 0$
- They do not exist because the structure is unstable
- They are uniquely determined, with $V_A = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$, $M_A = 20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, and $V_B = 0$

## Explanation

The fixed support provides three unknown reactions $H_A$, $V_A$, and $M_A$ and the roller provides $V_B$, for a total of four unknowns. The three equilibrium equations are
$$
\begin{cases}
H_A = 0 \\[4pt]
V_A + V_B - 10 = 0 \\[4pt]
M_A + 4\,V_B - 10 \cdot 2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
For each choice of $V_B$ the equations determine $V_A = 10 - V_B$ and $M_A = 20 - 4\,V_B$. One free parameter remains, so the structure is **statically indeterminate**. Both proposed sets of three reaction values satisfy equilibrium. Selecting the physical value of $V_B$ requires information about beam deformation.
