Beam $AB$ is $6\,\mathrm{m}$ long and pinned at $A$. It is supported at $B$ by a two-force link that extends downward and to the left from $B$. Measured upward and to the right, the link has direction cosines $\left(\dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5}\right)$ with respect to the $x$ and $y$ axes. At a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, a vertical force $F = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. At $B$, a horizontal force $H = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts to the left. What is the magnitude of the link reaction, and is the link in tension or compression?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,pendolo,233.13" forze="2 0,0,-1.5,F;B,-1.2,0,H" quote="A,2 0,2 m;2 0,B,4 m" titolo="Beam with a pin support and an inclined link"}

- $R_B = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$, with the link in compression
- $R_B = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$, with the link in tension
- $R_B = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$, with the link in compression
- $R_B = \dfrac{40}{3}\,\mathrm{kN}$, with the link in compression

## Explanation

The two-force link transmits a reaction along its axis. Take $R_B$ as positive upward and to the right, with horizontal component $\dfrac{3}{5}\,R_B$ and vertical component $\dfrac{4}{5}\,R_B$. Force $H$ and the horizontal component of $R_B$ act along the beam axis, so taking moments about $A$ gives
$$
6 \cdot \frac{4}{5}\,R_B - 24 \cdot 2 = 0 ,
$$
or $R_B = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$. The positive result means the link pushes upward on the beam and is **in compression**. Using the entire reaction instead of its vertical component would give $8\,\mathrm{kN}$.
