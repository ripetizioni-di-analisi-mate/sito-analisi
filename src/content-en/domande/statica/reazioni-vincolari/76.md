A planar force system has a horizontal resultant $\vec{R}$ of magnitude $3\,\mathrm{kN}$ directed to the right. Its resultant moment about the origin $O$ is zero. Taking counterclockwise as positive, what is the resultant moment about $O' = (0, 2\,\mathrm{m})$?

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;O',0,2" forze="O,1.5,0,R" punti="0,0,,pieno;0,2,,pieno" quote="O,O',2 m,0.8" titolo="Resultant $\vec{R}$ applied at $O$ and moment reference point $O'$ a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ above $O$"}

- $M_{O'} = 0$, because the moment is independent of the reference point
- $M_{O'} = -6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $M_{O'} = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $M_{O'} = 1.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

Shifting the reference point gives $\vec{M}_{O'} = \vec{M}_O + (O - O') \times \vec{R}$. Here $O - O'$ equals $(0, -2)$ and $\vec{R} = (3, 0)$, so the $z$-component of the cross product is $0 \cdot 0 - (-2) \cdot 3 = 6$. Since $\vec{M}_O = \vec{0}$, the moment is $M_{O'} = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ counterclockwise. The equivalent force of $3\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts to the right along the $x$-axis, which passes $2\,\mathrm{m}$ below $O'$. It therefore has a counterclockwise moment about $O'$. Only a force system with zero resultant has the same moment about every point. This system has a nonzero resultant.
