A simply supported beam spans $6\,\mathrm{m}$, with a pin at the left end $A$ and a roller at the right end $B$. Its vertical load increases quadratically. At a distance $x$ from $A$, measured in meters, the load intensity is $q(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{4}\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. It increases from $0$ at $A$ to $9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$. What is the reaction $V_B$?

::grafico{f="(x>=0 && x<=6) ? x^2/4 : NaN" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=10.5 nomi="x,q" titolo="Load intensity $q(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{4}$ along the beam, varying from $0$ at $A$ to $9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$"}

- $V_B = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_B = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_B = 4.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_B = 13.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward

## Explanation

The resultant of the load is the area below the diagram:
$$
Q = \int_0^6 \frac{x^2}{4}\,dx = \frac{6^3}{12} = 18\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The moment of the load about $A$ equals
$$
\int_0^6 x \cdot \frac{x^2}{4}\,dx = \frac{6^4}{16} = 81\,\mathrm{kN\,m} ,
$$
The resultant therefore acts $\dfrac{81}{18} = 4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Taking moments about $A$ gives $6\,V_B - 81 = 0$, hence $V_B = 13.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward. The value $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ places the resultant at $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the span, as for a triangular load. The value $4.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the reaction at $A$.
