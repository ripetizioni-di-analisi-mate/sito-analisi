Beam $AB$ is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ long. It has a roller at $A$ on a horizontal surface and a roller at $B$ on a surface inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal. The inclined roller reaction points upward and to the left. The only load is a downward force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ whose position can be chosen. For which positions of $F$ is the beam in equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello;B,carrello,315" forze="2 0,0,-1.2,F" quote="A,B,4 m" titolo="Beam on a horizontal roller and an inclined roller"}

- At every position, because the two roller reactions are not parallel
- Only with $F$ applied at $B$
- Only with $F$ applied at $A$
- At no position

## Explanation

With two scalar restraints the beam is unstable, and equilibrium is possible only for particular loads. The roller at $A$ exerts vertical reaction $V_A$. The roller at $B$ exerts $R_B$, with components $\dfrac{R_B}{\sqrt{2}}$ to the left and $\dfrac{R_B}{\sqrt{2}}$ upward. Horizontal equilibrium contains only the horizontal component of $R_B$, so $R_B = 0$. This leaves $V_A = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ and the moment equation about $A$, which gives $10\,x = 0$, where $x$ is the distance from $F$ to $A$. Thus equilibrium is possible **only with $F$ applied at $A$**, where the roller directly balances the force.
