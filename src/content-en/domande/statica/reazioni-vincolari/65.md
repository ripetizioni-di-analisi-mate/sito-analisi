Beam $AB$ is $5\,\mathrm{m}$ long and pinned at $A$. At $B$, a two-force link extends downward and to the left from $B$ at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal, with $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{3}{4}$. The link is to be replaced by a roller at $B$ that gives the same reaction for any loading. How should the roller's supporting surface be oriented?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,pendolo,216.87" quote="A,B,5 m" titolo="Beam with a pin at $A$ and an inclined two-force link at $B$"}

- A horizontal surface
- A surface parallel to the link and inclined at $\alpha$ to the horizontal
- A surface perpendicular to the link and inclined at $90^\circ - \alpha$ to the horizontal
- A vertical surface

## Explanation

At $B$ the link provides a force along its axis. A roller at $B$ would instead exert a reaction normal to its support surface. To have the same line of action, the surface normal must be parallel to the link, so the surface must be perpendicular to it. The link makes an angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal. A perpendicular surface therefore makes an acute angle $90^\circ - \alpha$ with the horizontal, with $\tan(90^\circ - \alpha) = \dfrac{4}{3}$. A surface parallel to the link would give a reaction perpendicular to the link, while a horizontal or vertical surface would give a vertical or horizontal reaction, respectively.
