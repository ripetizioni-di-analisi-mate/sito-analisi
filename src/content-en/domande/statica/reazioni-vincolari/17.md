Horizontal beam $AB$ is $6\,\mathrm{m}$ long and carries arbitrary loads in its plane. Point $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, and point $D$ lies $2\,\mathrm{m}$ above $A$. Which set of three equations ensures that both the resultant force and the resultant moment on the beam are zero?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" punti="3,0,M;0,2,D" quote="A,B,6 m" titolo="Beam $AB$ with midpoint $M$ and point $D$"}

- Moment equations about $A$, $B$, and $D$
- Moment equations about $A$, $B$, and $M$
- Moment equations about $A$ and $B$ and vertical force equilibrium
- Force equilibrium in the horizontal and vertical directions and in the direction at $45^\circ$

## Explanation

Three moment equations establish planar equilibrium if their reference points are noncollinear. Points $A$, $B$, and $D$ meet this condition, so moments about them are equivalent to the usual equilibrium equations. Points $A$, $B$, and $M$ all lie on the beam axis. Zero moments about $A$ and $B$ still allow a resultant parallel to $AB$, that is, horizontal. Vertical force equilibrium cannot rule it out because line $AB$ is perpendicular to the vertical. Three force-balance equations alone impose no condition on the resultant moment.
