An L-shaped steel frame consists of a vertical column of height $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and a horizontal arm of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$, welded together at corner $C$. A pin at the column base $A$ supports the frame, and the tip $B$ of the arm rests on a roller on a horizontal surface. The only load is the frame's self-weight, $g = 2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ per unit length of either member. What is the reaction $V_A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,0,3;B,4,3" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="C,B,0.4,0.4,g" quote="A,C,3 m,-1;C,B,4 m,-1" titolo="L-shaped structure under self-weight $g$, shown on its horizontal arm"}

- $V_A = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_A = 7\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_A = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $V_A = 14\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward

## Explanation

The column weight $2 \cdot 3 = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts vertically along its axis through $A$. The crossbeam weight $2 \cdot 4 = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at the crossbeam midpoint, a horizontal distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from the vertical through $A$. Taking moments about $A$ gives $4\,V_B - 8 \cdot 2 = 0$, or $V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$. Vertical force equilibrium gives
$$
V_A = 6 + 8 - 4 = 10\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
upward. The pin therefore carries the entire column weight. The value $7\,\mathrm{kN}$ divides the total weight equally between the supports, while $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ omits the column weight.
