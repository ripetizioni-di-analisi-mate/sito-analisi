The beam $AB$ shown, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, is fixed at $A$ and has at $B$ a parallel-link support with the vertical links; in the middle acts a vertical force $F$. The unknown reactions are $H_A$, $V_A$, $M_A$, the vertical force $V_B$ and the moment $M_B$ of parallel-link support. Which statement about the beam's equilibrium equations is true?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,doppio-pendolo" forze="3 0,0,-1.5,F" quote="A,3 0,3 m;3 0,B,3 m"}

- They have a unique solution because the beam is stable
- They have no solution because there are more unknowns than equations
- They have infinitely many solutions depending on two parameters
- They have infinitely many solutions depending on one parameter

## Explanation

The beam is a body with simple $s = 3 + 2 = 5$ constraints. Fixed support alone prevents any displacement, hence $l = 0$ and, from the $3n - s = l - i$, $i = s - 3n = 2$ relationship.

With lengths in meters, the equations of horizontal translation, vertical translation and rotation around $A$ are
$$
\begin{cases}
H_A = 0 \\
V_A + V_B - F = 0 \\
M_A + M_B + 6\,V_B - 3\,F = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Since $l = 0$, the three equations are independent and the system has solution for any load. There are five unknowns, so you can assign $V_B$ and $M_B$, and the others are obtained: $V_A = F - V_B$ and $M_A = 3\,F - M_B - 6\,V_B$.

Solutions therefore depend on two parameters, as many as the independent systems of reactions in equilibrium without loads, i.e. $i = 2$.
