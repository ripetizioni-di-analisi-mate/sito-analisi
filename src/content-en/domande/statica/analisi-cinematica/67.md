The beam shown consists of segments $AC$ and $CD$, each of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$. There is a pin support at $A$, an internal parallel-link connection with vertical links at $C$, and a roller on a horizontal surface at $D$. Which segment can move, and what motion can it undergo?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,0;D,8,0" aste="A-C;C-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello" interni="C,doppio-pendolo,90" quote="A,C,4 m;C,D,4 m"}

- Segment $AC$ remains stationary and segment $CD$ translates horizontally
- Both segments rotate by the same angle about $A$
- Segment $AC$ rotates about $A$ and segment $CD$ translates vertically
- None, because the beam is statically determinate

## Explanation

Internal parallel-link connection prevents relative rotation, hence $\varphi_2 = \varphi_1$, and vertical relative displacement; leaves horizontal relative sliding free.

If $AC$ rotated $\varphi_1 \neq 0$ around $A$, $CD$ would also rotate $\varphi_1$, with the center $C_2$ on the vertical through $D$. For the first theorem of the $C_2$ kinematic chains would be on the line through $C_1 = A$ and for $C_{12}$, the point at infinity in the vertical direction, that is, on the vertical through $A$; the two verticals are distinct, so $\varphi_1 = 0$ and $AC$ is stationary.

With $AC$ still, the segment $CD$ has a parallel-link support with the pin-ended links vertical and a roller support with the vertical reaction, parallel to the pin-ended links. Therefore $CD$ can move horizontally, and the beam is unstable with $l = 1$.
