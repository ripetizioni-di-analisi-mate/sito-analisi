A beam has at the end $B$ a two-force link whose axis is tilted by $30^\circ$ on the horizontal and rises to the right. For infinitesimal displacements, what roller at $B$ is equivalent to the link?

- A roller on a surface inclined at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal and descending to the right
- A roller on a surface inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal and rising to the right
- A roller on a surface inclined at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal and rising to the right
- A roller on a horizontal surface

## Explanation

The two-force link prevents $B$ from moving along its axis and leaves the shift free perpendicular to the axis. Roller support prevents normal $B$ from moving to the support surface, so the two constraints impose the same condition when the normal to the plane coincides with the axis of the two-force link, i.e. when the plane is perpendicular to the axis.

The axis lies along unit vector $(\cos 30^\circ, \sin 30^\circ)$. The plane that descends to the right with the inclination of $60^\circ$ has the direction $(\cos 60^\circ, -\sin 60^\circ)$, and the scalar product of the two directions equals $\cos 30^\circ \cos 60^\circ - \sin 30^\circ \sin 60^\circ = 0$. So that plane is perpendicular to the axis.

The inclined plane of $30^\circ$ rising to the right is instead parallel to the axis, and the corresponding roller support would prevent perpendicular displacement to the axis.
