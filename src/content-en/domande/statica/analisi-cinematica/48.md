A planar rigid body is connected to the ground by a pin support at $A$. You want to replace the hinge with two two-force links that impose the same conditions on travel. Which pair of two-force links is equivalent to the pin?

- Two links with parallel axes, one passing through $A$
- Two links with nonparallel axes intersecting at a point other than $A$
- Two links with nonparallel axes, both passing through $A$
- Two links with perpendicular axes, provided one of them is horizontal

## Explanation

The hinge prevents the two components from moving $A$ and leaves rotation about $A$ free. A two-force link prevents the point of attack from moving along its axis, so it requires the center of rotation to stand on the axis.

With two non-parallel axes passing through $A$ the center must stand on both, therefore it is $A$, and the two components of $A$'s displacement along two independent directions are zero. That's the effect of the hinge.

If the axes meet at a point $P \neq A$, the body rotates about $P$ and not around $A$. Two parallel two-force links instead equate to a parallel-link support, which prevents rotation.
