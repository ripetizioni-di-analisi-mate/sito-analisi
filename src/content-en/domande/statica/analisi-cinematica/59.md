The portal frame in the figure has the rigid joints $D$ and $E$ and is resting on the feet $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ on two roller supports that scroll on horizontal planes, so it is unstable. Which applied load on the portal frame can be balanced by the roller reactions?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,0,4;E,6,4;B,6,0" aste="A-D;D-E;E-B" vincoli="A,carrello;B,carrello" quote="D,E,6 m,-0.7;B,E,4 m"}

- A vertical force applied anywhere on the crossbeam
- A horizontal force applied at joint $D$
- A force inclined at $45^\circ$ acting at the midpoint of the crossbeam
- No load, because an unstable structure can never be in equilibrium

## Explanation

The $R_A$ and $R_B$ reactions of the two roller supports are vertical, so they do not appear in the horizontal translation equation. Therefore, the portal frame is in equilibrium only if the horizontal component of the load is zero, and the horizontal force and the inclined force cannot be balanced.

For a vertical $F$ downward force, applied on the crossbeam at the distance $a$ from the vertical line through $A$, the equations of vertical translation and rotation around $A$ are
$$
\begin{cases}
R_A + R_B - F = 0 \\
6\,R_B - F\,a = 0
\end{cases}
$$
with lengths in meters. The system has the solution $R_B = \dfrac{F\,a}{6}$ and $R_A = \dfrac{F\,(6 - a)}{6}$, so the vertical force is balanced in any position. An unstable structure is only in equilibrium for particular loads, and here are the loads without horizontal component.
