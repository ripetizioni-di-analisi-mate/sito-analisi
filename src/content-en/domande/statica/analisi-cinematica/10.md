The four-bar linkage shown consists of moving members $AC$, $CD$, and $DB$, pinned to the ground at $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and joined by internal hinges $C = (0, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $D = (3, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$. Where is the absolute center of rotation $C_2$ of member $CD$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,0,4;D,3,4;B,6,0" aste="A-C;C-D;D-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" cerniere="C;D" quote="A,B,6 m,0.9;A,C,4 m,-0.7;C,D,3 m,-0.7"}

- $C_2 = (0, 8)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_2 = (0, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_2 = (3, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_2 = (3, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The absolute centers of the rods hinged to the ground are $C_1 = A$ and $C_3 = B$, and the relative centers are the internal hinges, $C_{12} = C$ and $C_{23} = D$. For the first theorem of the kinematic chains $C_2$ is aligned with $C_1$ and $C_{12}$, so it lies on the line $AC$, of equation $x = 0$. For the same theorem $C_2$ is aligned with $C_3$ and $C_{23}$, so it lies on the line $BD$, of equation $y = -\dfrac{4}{3}\,(x - 6)$. For $x = 0$ this equation gives $y = 8$, therefore $C_2 = (0, 8)\,\mathrm{m}$. The $(0, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $(3, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$ points are the $C$ and $D$ hinges, i.e. the relative centers.
