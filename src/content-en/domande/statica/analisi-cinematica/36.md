The structure in the figure is formed by two bodies. The body $1$, with the $AP$ and $PR$ rods joined at $P$ by a rigid joint, has a pin support at $A = (0, 0)$; the body $2$, with the $BQ$ and $QS$ rods joined at $Q$ by a rigid node, has a pin support at $B = (8, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. The two bodies are connected only by the pin-ended links $PS$ and $RQ$, with $P = (3, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$, $R = (3, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$, $Q = (5, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $S = (5, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$. How is the structure classified?

- Unstable, with $l = 1$ and $i = 0$
- Unstable and statically indeterminate, with $l = i = 1$
- Statically indeterminate, with $l = 0$ and $i = 1$
- Statically determinate, with $l = i = 0$

## Explanation

You have $n = 2$ and $s = 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6 = 3n$, so just determine if $l = 0$.

Each pin-ended link is an internal two-force link, so the relative center $C_{12}$ lies on the axis of both. The axis $PS$ is the line $y = 7 - x$ and the axis $RQ$ is the line $y = x - 1$, which meet at point $(4, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$. The two pin-ended links thus amount to an internal hinge at $(4, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$, and the structure is a three-hinged arch.

The points $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (8, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $(4, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$ are not collinear, therefore, for the three-hinges arch theorem, the structure is **statically determinate**. It would be unstable and statically indeterminate if the two pin-ended links met at a point in the line $AB$.
