The beam shown is formed by the $GC$ and $CB$ sections, connected by the internal hinge $C$, with $G = (-2, 0)$, $A = (0, 0)$, $C = (4, 0)$ and $B = (8, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. At $A$ and $B$ there are two pin supports and at the end $G$ of the overhang a two-force link with the horizontal axis. What are $l$ and $i$?

::struttura{nodi="G,-2,0;A,0,0;C,4,0;B,8,0" aste="G-A;A-C;C-B" vincoli="G,pendolo,180;A,cerniera;B,cerniera" cerniere="C" quote="G,A,2 m;A,C,4 m;C,B,4 m"}

- $l = 0$, $i = 1$
- $l = 1$, $i = 1$
- $l = 1$, $i = 2$
- $l = 0$, $i = 0$

## Explanation

The bodies are $n = 2$ and the scalar constraints are $s = 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 7$, so $3n - s = -1$.

The $A$, $C$ and $B$ hinges are on the line $y = 0$. Therefore, by the three-hinges arch theorem, the two segments can rotates about $A$ and $B$ and the point $C$ moves vertically. The point $G$ belongs to the segment that rotates about $A$, so it moves vertically, and the two-force link, whose line of action passes through $A$, only prevents horizontal displacement of $G$. Therefore the two-force link does not prevent movement, and $l = 1$.

From the $3n - s = l - i$ relationship you get $i = l + s - 3n = 1 + 7 - 6 = 2$. The $l = 0$, $i = 1$ couple is obtained from the count alone, and the $l = 1$, $i = 1$ couple is that of the beam without the two-force link.
