In the structure in the figure the $AC$ and $CB$ rods form a three-hinged arch, with the pin supports at $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (8, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and the internal hinge at $C = (4, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$. The horizontal $DF$ beam is connected to member $CB$, which is continuous at $D$, by a hinge at point $D = (6, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$, and in $F = (10, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$ is resting on a roller support that moves over a horizontal plane. How is the structure classified?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,4;D,6,2;B,8,0;F,10,2" aste="A-C;C-D;D-B;D-F" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera;F,carrello" cerniere="C;D" quote="A,B,8 m,0.9;D,F,4 m,-0.7"}

- Statically determinate, with $l = i = 0$
- Unstable, with $l = 1$ and $i = 0$
- Statically indeterminate, with $l = 0$ and $i = 1$
- Unstable and statically indeterminate, with $l = i = 1$

## Explanation

The bodies are $n = 3$, i.e. the $AC$, $CB$ rods and the beam $DF$, then $3n = 9$, and the scalar constraints are $s = 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 9$.

The points $A$, $B$ and $C$ are not collinear, therefore, by the three-hinges arch theorem, the arch is stationary. A stationary body behaves like the ground, so the pin at $D$ is, for the beam $DF$, a pin support.

The center of $DF$ should then be $D$ and stand on the vertical through $F$, the line $x = 10\,\mathrm{m}$, which does not pass by $D$. Therefore $DF$ is also firm, $l = 0$ and, from the $3n - s = l - i$, $i = 0$ relationship, so the structure is **statically determinate**.
