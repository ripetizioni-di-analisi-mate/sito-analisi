In the mechanism in the figure the crank $AC$ (body $1$) and the balance $DB$ (body $3$) are connected by the pin-ended link $CD$ (body $2$); pin supports are at $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (8, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and internal hinges in $C = (2, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $D = (6, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$. Using the second theorem of kinematic chains, locate the relative center of rotation $C_{13}$ of the crank and the rocker.

- $C_{13} = \left(\dfrac{8}{3}, \dfrac{16}{3}\right)\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{13} = (10, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{13} = (4, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{13} = (4, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

For the second theorem of kinematic chains the relative centers $C_{12} = C$, $C_{23} = D$ and $C_{13}$ are aligned, so $C_{13}$ lies on line $CD$, of equation $y = 5 - \dfrac{x}{2}$.

For the first theorem, applied to bodies $1$ and $3$, $C_{13}$ lies on the line for absolute centers $C_1 = A$ and $C_3 = B$, i.e. on the axis $y = 0$.

By placing $y = 0$ you get $x = 10$, so $C_{13} = (10, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. The point $\left(\dfrac{8}{3}, \dfrac{16}{3}\right)\mathrm{m}$ is the absolute center $C_2$ of the pin-ended link $CD$, intersection of the $AC$ and $BD$ lines.
