The beam shown is formed by the segments $AC$, $CD$ and $DE$, connected by the internal hinges $C$ and $D$. At $A$ there is a pin support, at $B$ and at $E$ two roller supports that travel on horizontal surfaces. Which description of the possible displacements is correct?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,12,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;E,carrello" cerniere="C;D" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,2 m;C,D,3 m;D,E,3 m"}

- The beam is statically determinate, and no segment moves
- $AC$ rotates about $A$ while $CD$ and $DE$ remain stationary
- $AC$ and $CD$ remain stationary while $DE$ rotates about $D$
- $AC$ remains stationary, $CD$ rotates about $C$, and $DE$ rotates about $E$

## Explanation

You have $n = 3$ and $s = 2 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 8 < 9$, so the beam is unstable.

The segment $AC$ has a pin at $A$ and a well-arranged roller support at $B$, so it is stationary, and with it the point $C$. Segment $CD$ then has the center $C_2 = C$. Segment $DE$ has the center on the vertical through $E$ and, for the first theorem of kinematic chains, on the line through $C_2 = C$ and $C_{23} = D$, i.e. on the axis of the beam; then $C_3 = E$.

The two sections $CD$ and $DE$ therefore rotates about $C$ and $E$, and the point $D$ moves vertically. The segment $DE$ cannot rotates about $D$ with $CD$ stationary, because roller support at $E$ prevents vertical displacement of $E$.
