The beam shown is formed by the $AC$ and $CD$ sections, connected by the internal hinge $C$. At $A$ there is a pin support, and the section $CD$ is supported at $B$ and $D$ on two roller supports that scroll on horizontal planes. How is the beam classified?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,4,0;D,8,0" aste="A-C;C-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;D,carrello" cerniere="C" quote="A,C,2 m;C,B,2 m;B,D,4 m"}

- Unstable, with $l = 1$ and $i = 0$
- Unstable and statically indeterminate, with $l = i = 1$
- Statically determinate, with $l = i = 0$
- Statically indeterminate, with $l = 0$ and $i = 1$

## Explanation

You have $n = 2$ and $s = 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6 = 3n$. Suppose both segments move. The center $C_1$ of section $AC$ is the hinge $A$, and the center $C_2$ of section $CD$ lies on the vertical lines through $B$ and $D$, so it is the point at infinity in the vertical direction. For the first theorem of the kinematic chains $C_1$, $C_2$ and $C_{12} = C$ should be aligned, i.e. the line $AC$ should be vertical, while it is horizontal. If, on the other hand, one of the two sections is stationary, the other has two distinct centers and is also stationary. Therefore $l = 0$, $i = 0$ and the beam is **statically determinate**, although neither segment, considered alone, is statically bound.
