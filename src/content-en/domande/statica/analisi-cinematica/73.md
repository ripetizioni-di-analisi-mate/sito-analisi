In the structure in the figure the body $APQ$ is framed at $A$, with $P = (2, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $Q = (2, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$. The body $RTF$, with $R = (4, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$, $T = (4, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $F = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$, is connected to the first only by two pin-ended links, $PR$ and $QT$. About which point can body $RTF$ rotate?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;P,2,0;Q,2,2;R,4,2;T,4,0;F,6,0" aste="A-P;P-Q;R-T;T-F" catene="P-R;Q-T" vincoli="A,incastro,180" quote="A,P,2 m;P,T,2 m;T,F,2 m"}

- About point $T$
- About point $(3, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$, the midpoint of $PT$
- About point $(3, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$, the midpoint of $QR$
- About point $(3, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$, the intersection of the link axes

## Explanation

The body $APQ$ is stationary, because it is fixed, so each pin-ended link behaves for $RTF$ as a two-force external link, and the center of $RTF$ must stand on the axes of both.

The axis $PR$ is the line $y = x - 2$ and the axis $QT$ is the line $y = 4 - x$. Equalizing results in $x = 3$ and $y = 1$.

Therefore, $RTF$ rotates about the point $(3, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$: two non-parallel pin-ended links between two bodies equal an internal hinge placed in the intersection of their axes. With $n = 2$ and $s = 3 + 1 + 1 = 5$ the structure has $l = 1$ and $i = 0$.
