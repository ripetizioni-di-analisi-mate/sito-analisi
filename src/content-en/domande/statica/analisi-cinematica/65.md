The connecting-handle mechanism in the figure is formed by the $AC$ crank, hinged to the ground at $A = (0, 0)$, and the pin $CD$-ended link, connected by the internal hinge $C = (2, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$. The extreme $D = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ is resting on a roller support that moves over a horizontal plane. Where is the absolute center of rotation $C_2$ of link $CD$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,2;D,6,0" aste="A-C;C-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello" cerniere="C" quote="A,D,6 m,0.9"}

- $C_2 = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_2 = (2, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_2 = (4, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_2 = (6, 6)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The roller $D$ support requires that $C_2$ stand on the line of action of its reaction, the vertical $x = 6$.

The crank rotates about the pin support, then $C_1 = A$, and the relative center is $C_{12} = C$. For the first theorem of the kinematic chains $C_2$ lies on the line through $A$ and $C$, i.e. on the line $y = x$.

Therefore $C_2$ is the intersection of the $x = 6$ and $y = x$ lines, i.e. $C_2 = (6, 6)\,\mathrm{m}$. The point $D$ moves horizontally, perpendicular to $C_2 D$, as the roller support requires.
