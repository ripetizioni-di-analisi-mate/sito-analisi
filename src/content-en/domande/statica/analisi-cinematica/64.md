The frame enclosed in the figure is a rectangle of $6\,\mathrm{m}$ for $4\,\mathrm{m}$ with the vertices $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$, $C = (6, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$ and $D = (0, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$. The upper crossbeam has three internal hinges: in the $D$ and $C$ vertices and in the midpoint $E$. At $A$ there is a pin support and at $B$ a roller support that moves along a horizontal plane. What are $l$ and $i$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,6,4;E,3,4;D,0,4" aste="A-B;B-C;C-E;E-D;D-A" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" cerniere="D;E;C" quote="A,B,6 m,0.9;B,C,4 m"}

- $l = 0$, $i = 0$
- $l = 1$, $i = 0$
- $l = 1$, $i = 1$
- $l = 0$, $i = 1$

## Explanation

The three hinges divide the frame into $n = 3$ bodies: the section $DABC$ (straight and lower crossbeam), the section $DE$ and the section $EC$. The scalar constraints are $s = 2 + 1 + 3 \cdot 2 = 9 = 3n$, then $l = i$.

The body $DABC$ is bound to the ground with a well-arranged hinge and roller support, so it is stationary, and with it the $D$ and $C$ points. The $DE$ and $EC$ sections then form a three-hinged arch with the $D$, $E$, $C$ hinges collinear on the line $y = 4$. So the point $E$ can move vertically and $l = 1$.

The $l = i$ report follows $i = 1$. With three non-collinear hinges the closed loop would be statically determinate.
