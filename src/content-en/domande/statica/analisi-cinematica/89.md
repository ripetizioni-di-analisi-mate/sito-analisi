A straight beam fixed at $A$, at horizontal coordinate $0$, and is resting on two roller supports that travel on horizontal surfaces, at $B$, at horizontal coordinate $6\,\mathrm{m}$, and at $D$, at horizontal coordinate $12\,\mathrm{m}$. To make it statically determinate, two internal hinges are inserted. For which pair of horizontal hinge coordinates is the beam unstable?

- $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and $8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In all cases $n = 3$ and $s = 3 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 9 = 3n$, so only the arrangement of the hinges counts.

With the $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $10\,\mathrm{m}$ hinges the $0$ to $8\,\mathrm{m}$ segment is fixed and stationary, the $8$ to $10\,\mathrm{m}$ segment has no external supports and the $10$ to $12\,\mathrm{m}$ segment only has roller support at $D$. The center of the latter lies on the vertical through $D$ and, for the first theorem of kinematic chains, on the line for the $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $10\,\mathrm{m}$ hinges, i.e. on the axis; so it's $D$, and the two segments rotate about the pin at $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $D$.

With $3$ and $9\,\mathrm{m}$ or $4$ and $8\,\mathrm{m}$ each segment is stationary one after the other. With $2$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$, the $2$ to $4\,\mathrm{m}$ segment could only rotates about the pin at $2\,\mathrm{m}$, moving the hinge vertically into $4\,\mathrm{m}$, while the $4$ to $12\,\mathrm{m}$ segment, resting on two roller supports, could only move horizontally. The two movements are not compatible in the pin at $4\,\mathrm{m}$, so this beam is also statically determinate.
