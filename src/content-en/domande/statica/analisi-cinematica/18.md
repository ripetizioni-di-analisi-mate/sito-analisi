The cantilever $AB$ shown, of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$, is fixed at $A$. You want to replace the fixed support with three two-force links connected to the cantilever at points of the section $AD$, with $D$ a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Which set of three links makes the cantilever statically determinate?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,1,0;B,4,0" aste="A-D;D-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" quote="A,D,1 m;D,B,3 m"}

- Two vertical links at $A$ and $D$ and a horizontal link at $A$
- A vertical link, a horizontal link, and a link inclined at $45^\circ$, all attached at $A$
- Two horizontal links at $A$ and $D$ and a vertical link at $D$
- Three vertical links, at $A$, at $D$, and at the midpoint of $AD$

## Explanation

The cantilever is a body with three scalar constraints, so it is statically determinate if and only if the three lines of action do not pass through the same point, finite or at infinity.

The vertical $x = 0$ and $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ have in common only the point at infinity in the vertical direction, and the horizontal $y = 0$ does not pass through that point. Thus the set of two vertical links and one horizontal link makes the cantilever **statically determinate**.

The three two-force links applied at $A$ have the concurrent lines at $A$, so the cantilever can rotates about $A$. The two horizontal two-force links have the same $y = 0$ line, which passes through $D$ as the vertical through $D$, so the cantilever can rotates about $D$. The three vertical two-force links have parallel lines and leave the horizontal translation free.
