In a portal frame traveled from foot to foot, with the reference fibers inside, the bending moment at the top of the left column is $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the inner fibers in tension. The joint between column and crossbeam is rigid and no concentrated couples act on it. What is the bending moment at the beginning of the crossbeam?

- $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the outer fibers in tension
- $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the inner fibers in tension
- $0$
- $5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the inner fibers in tension

## Explanation

Bending moment is continuous through a rigid joint if the joint carries no applied couple, even when a point force acts there. The moment at the end of the first member then equals the moment at the start of the second. Following the portal frame from one base to the other places the reference fibers on the inside of both members, so the sign also stays the same. At the start of the crossbeam the moment is therefore $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the inner fibers in tension. A hinged joint would have zero bending moment.
