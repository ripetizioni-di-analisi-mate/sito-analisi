The beam $AB$ shown, of length $8\,\mathrm{m}$, has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$ and brings the downward force $F = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $C$, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. In which direction does shear tend to rotate a small element of segment $CB$, and what is the shear force?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,8,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="C,0,-1.6,F" quote="A,C,2 m;C,B,6 m" titolo="Simply supported beam under force $F$ at $C$"}

- Counterclockwise, with $T = -2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- Clockwise, with $T = -2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- Clockwise, with $T = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$
- Counterclockwise, with $T = -6\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Moment equilibrium about $A$ gives $8\,V_B - 8 \cdot 2 = 0$, then $V_B = 2\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_A = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$, both upward. In the segment $CB$, the right side, which contains only $V_B$, and for practical $T = -V_B = -2\,\mathrm{kN}$ rules, is convenient. On the left side of the beam element the shear then acts downward and on the right side upward, then the two forces form a couple **counterclockwise**. By the sign convention, positive shear tends to rotate a beam element clockwise. Indeed, on segment $AC$ we have $T = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ and clockwise rotation.
