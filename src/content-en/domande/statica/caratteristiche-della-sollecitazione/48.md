The cantilever $AB$ shown, of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$, is fixed at $A$ and carries the force $F = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$ directed **upward** in the free end $B$. Which fibers are in tension, and at which section does the bending moment have its greatest absolute value?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" forze="B,0,0.9,F" quote="A,B,3 m" titolo="Cantilever with upward force $F$ at the free end"}

- The upper fibers, with a maximum of $15\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at the fixed end
- The lower fibers, with a maximum of $15\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at the free end
- The lower fibers, with a maximum of $15\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at the fixed end
- The lower fibers, with a maximum of $7.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at midspan

## Explanation

The portion to the right of the section $x$ of the coordinate is used, which contains only $F$. The force is directed upward and stands to the right of the section, so it has counterclockwise moment $5\,(3 - x)$ about it. For the $M = M^{\mathrm{right}}_S$ rule you have therefore $M(x) = 5\,(3 - x)$, positive in each section: **Lower Fibers** are in tension. The moment decreases linearly from $15\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ to fixed support at zero in $B$, so the maximum is at the fixed end section. A downward force would give the same diagram with the upper fibers in tension.
