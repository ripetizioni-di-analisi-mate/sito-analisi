The column $AB$ of a two-story building, $6\,\mathrm{m}$ high and fixed at the base $A$, receives the loads of the attics as vertical forces directed along its axis: $P_1 = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward in the top $B$ and $P_2 = 60\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at section $C$, to $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from the ground. The self-weight of the column is neglected. Which description of the axial force in the column is correct?

- $N = -40\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $CB$ and $N = -60\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $AC$
- $N = -100\,\mathrm{kN}$ is constant throughout the column
- $N = -40\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $AC$ and $N = -100\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $CB$
- $N = -40\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $CB$ and $N = -100\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $AC$

## Explanation

In a section of the segment $CB$ the part above contains only $P_1$, directed along the axis towards the section, then $N = -40\,\mathrm{kN}$, of compression. In a section of the segment $AC$ the part above contains $P_1$ and $P_2$, therefore $N = -(40 + 60) = -100\,\mathrm{kN}$. The two forces are directed along the axis, so the shear and bending moment are zero in each section. The $N$ diagram therefore has an $60\,\mathrm{kN}$ jump at $C$, equal to $P_2$. The value $-60\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $AC$ neglects the load of the upper floor, which comes to the base through the whole column.
