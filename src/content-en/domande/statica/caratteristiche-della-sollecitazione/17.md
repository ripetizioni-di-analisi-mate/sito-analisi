In a horizontal beam unloaded segment the shear equals $T = -3\,\mathrm{kN}$. How does the bending moment vary from left to right along the segment?

- It decreases by $3\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ per meter
- It increases by $3\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ per meter
- It remains constant at $-3\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- It decreases parabolically

## Explanation

In a segment with uniform load $q$ the moment equals $M(x) = M_a + T_a\,(x - a) - \dfrac{q\,(x - a)^2}{2}$. In the $q = 0$ unloaded segment, so the moment is linear and its slope is the shear. Because $T = -3\,\mathrm{kN}$, the moment decreases by $3\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ for each meter traveled to the right. A parabolic variation is only with a distributed load in the segment, and a constant moment only with zero shear.
