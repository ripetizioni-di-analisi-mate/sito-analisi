A simply supported beam of span $L$ carries into an internal $C$ section an $\mathcal{M}$ concentrated couple and no other load. Which statement about the diagrams at $C$ is true?

- The $T$ diagram jumps by $\mathcal{M}$ and the moment is continuous
- The $M$ diagram has a corner and the $T$ diagram has a jump
- Both diagrams have a jump, and the jump in $T$ has magnitude $\dfrac{\mathcal{M}}{L}$
- The $M$ diagram jumps by $\mathcal{M}$ and the shear is continuous

## Explanation

The forces to the left of a section immediately to the right of $C$ are those to the left of a section immediately to the left, plus the couple. The couple has zero resultant, so $N$ and $T$ do not change through $C$. The couple moment equals $\mathcal{M}$ about any reference point, so the bending moment has an $\mathcal{M}$ amplitude jump at $C$. The shear is the same at the two sides, so the two segments of the $M$ diagram have the same slope. The value $\dfrac{\mathcal{M}}{L}$ is the shear produced by the reactions, constant throughout the beam.
