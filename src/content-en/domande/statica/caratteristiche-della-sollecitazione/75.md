The three-hinged arch shown is formed by the straight $AC$ and $CB$ members, connected by the internal hinge $C$ in crown, with a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$; the tie $AB$, connected with hinges in $A$ and $B$, absorbs thrust. $AB$ span is $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $C$ is located $2\,\mathrm{m}$ above the midpoint of $AB$. In $C$ the vertical force $F = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. What is the axial force in the tie?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,2;B,8,0" aste="A-C;C-B" catene="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" cerniere="C" forze="C,0,-1.4,F" quote="A,B,8 m,1;4 0,C,2 m,0.5" titolo="Tied three-hinged arch with a force at the crown"}

- $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $8\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $16\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $8\,\mathrm{kN}$ in compression

## Explanation

The roller support at $B$ gives a vertical reaction and the load is vertical, then $H_A = 0$ and by $V_A = V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ symmetry upward. It isolates the left side, cutting the tie and separating the hinge $C$: $V_A$ and the $N$ axial force of the tie act on it, which pulls $A$ to the right if it is tension. About $C$, $V_A$ has a moment arm of $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and clockwise moment, while the force of the tie has a moment arm of $2\,\mathrm{m}$ and counterclockwise moment. The rotation around $C$ then gives $2N - 4 \cdot 4 = 0$, therefore $N = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$, of **tension**. Without the tie the same force would be the horizontal thrust required of two hinges on the ground.
