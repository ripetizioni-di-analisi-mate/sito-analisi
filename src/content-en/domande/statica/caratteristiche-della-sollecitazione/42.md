At the rigid joint $J$ of a frame only a vertical column, below the joint, and a horizontal beam, to its right, meet; no external load acts on the joint. The column exerts on the joint a vertical force of $7\,\mathrm{kN}$ upwards, a horizontal force of $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right and no couple. What is the axial force in the beam next to the joint?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,-2,-;J,0,0;B,3,0,-" aste="A-J;J-B" forze="J,0,1.1,7 kN;J,1,0,2 kN" titolo="Forces exerted by the column on the joint $J$"}

- $7\,\mathrm{kN}$ compression
- $\sqrt{53}\,\mathrm{kN}$ compression
- $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ compression
- $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ tension

## Explanation

The joint is in equilibrium, so the beam exerts on the joint the force opposite to that of the column, that is $7\,\mathrm{kN}$ downwards and $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left. The axis of the beam is horizontal, therefore the axial force is the horizontal component, of $2\,\mathrm{kN}$, while the vertical component of $7\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the shear force. The horizontal force of $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ that the beam exerts on the joint pushes it to the left, away from the beam, so the beam is **in compression** and the axial force is $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ in compression. The value $\sqrt{53}\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the magnitude of the whole force, which also includes the shear, and the value $7\,\mathrm{kN}$ mistakes the shear force for the axial force.
