A simply supported beam carries over all span a triangular load, zero in one extreme and maximum in the other. What are the polynomial degrees of $T(x)$ and $M(x)$?

- Linear $T$ and quadratic $M$
- Quadratic $T$ and cubic $M$
- Constant $T$ and linear $M$
- Quadratic $T$ and quadratic $M$

## Explanation

The load $q(t)$ is first grade in $t$. The load between $A$ and the $x$ horizontal coordinate section resulted equal to the area of its diagram, $\displaystyle\int_0^x q(t)\,dt$, which is second degree in $x$; the shear differs from $V_A$ by this resultant, so it is second degree. The moment of the same load about the section is $\displaystyle\int_0^x q(t)\,(x - t)\,dt$, which is third grade, so $M(x)$ is third grade as well. The same result follows from $\dfrac{dM}{dx} = T$, because the derivative of a cubic polynomial is second degree.
