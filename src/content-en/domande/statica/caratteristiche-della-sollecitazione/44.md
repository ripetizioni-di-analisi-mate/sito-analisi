The frame shown consists of column $AB$ of height $3\,\mathrm{m}$ fixed at its base $A$, beam $BC$ of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$, and vertical member $CD$ extending downward from $C$ by a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$. Joints $B$ and $C$ are rigid. At the free end $D$ a horizontal force $P = 3\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts to the right. Follow the frame from $A$ to $D$, taking the inner fibers as the reference fibers. Which description of the bending moment in beam $BC$ is correct?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,3;C,4,3;D,4,1" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" vincoli="A,incastro" forze="D,1,0,P" tratteggi="0.12,0 0.12,2.88 3.88,2.88 3.88,1" quote="A,B,3 m,-0.8;B,C,4 m,-0.9;C,D,2 m,-0.8" titolo="Fixed frame with horizontal force $P$ at the free end; dashed lines indicate the reference fibers"}

- Constant $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the outer fibers in tension
- Linear from $0$ at $C$ to $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at $B$
- Constant $9\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the inner fibers in tension
- Constant $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the inner fibers in tension

## Explanation

In a crossbeam section, the following part is used, i.e. the column $CD$, on which only $P$ acts. The force $P$ is horizontal and its line of action passes $2\,\mathrm{m}$ below the axis of the crossbeam, so it has the same moment arm, $2\,\mathrm{m}$, about each section of $BC$. A force to the right applied under the section has counterclockwise moment, therefore $M^{\mathrm{right}}_S = 3 \cdot 2 = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ and, for the $M = M^{\mathrm{right}}_S$ rule, $M = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ throughout the crossbeam. The moment is positive, so the dotted fibers are in tension, which in the crossbeam run from $B$ to $C$ stand at the bottom, inside the frame. The value $9\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ uses the height of the column as its arm.
