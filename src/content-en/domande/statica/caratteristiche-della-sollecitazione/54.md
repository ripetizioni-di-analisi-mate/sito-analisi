The beam $AB$ shown, of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and supported at both ends, carries a triangular load that equals zero in $A$ and $q_0 = 6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ in $B$. How far from $A$ is the bending moment greatest?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0,0.7,q_0" quote="A,B,3 m" titolo="Simply supported beam under a triangular load"}

- $\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.73\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The resultant load is $\dfrac{6 \cdot 3}{2} = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ and passes $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, so moment equilibrium about $B$ gives $V_A = \dfrac{9 \cdot 1}{3} = 3\,\mathrm{kN}$. In the section $x$, the load is $q(x) = 2x$, so the load to the left of the section has has resultant $\dfrac{x \cdot 2x}{2} = x^2$. By the section rules $T(x) = 3 - x^2$, which vanishes for $x = \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$. The moment is maximum where the shear vanishes, so the maximum falls $\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.73\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, moved to the most loaded extreme. The value $2\,\mathrm{m}$ is the position of the resultant, which does not coincide with the section of the maximum.
