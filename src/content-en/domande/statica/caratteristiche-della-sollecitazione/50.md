The beam $AB$ shown, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$. His shear diagram is the one drawn: a segment from $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $A$ to $-6\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $B$, without jumps inside the beam. What load acts on the beam?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,6-2*s" segni=1 quote="A,B,6 m,0.9" titolo="Shear diagram $T$ ($\mathrm{kN}$)"}

- A uniform load of $2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ over the entire span
- A uniform load of $6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ over the entire span
- A downward force of $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at midspan
- A triangular load equal to $4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$

## Explanation

The diagram has no jumps inside the beam, so between $A$ and $B$ no concentrated forces act; a force at midspan would drop the shear suddenly. The shear is linear, so the load is uniform, because for the variation theorem in a segment with uniform load it is $T(x) = T_a - q\,(x - a)$. The slope of the segment is $\dfrac{-6 - 6}{6} = -2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, so $q = 2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ down. The total load $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ is in fact equal to the sum of the reactions, $V_A = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_B = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$. A triangular load would give a second-degree shear.
