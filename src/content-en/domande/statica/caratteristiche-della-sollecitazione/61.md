The beam $AB$ shown, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$. It only carries two couples of $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$: a clockwise applied at $A$ and a counterclockwise applied at $B$. What are the shear force and bending moment at midspan?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" coppie="A,-1,ℳ;B,1,ℳ" quote="A,B,6 m" titolo="Simply supported beam with opposing couples at the ends"}

- $T = 0$, $M = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ with the upper fibers in tension
- $T = 2\,\mathrm{kN}$, $M = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ with the lower fibers in tension
- $T = 0$, $M = 0$
- $T = 0$, $M = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ with the lower fibers in tension

## Explanation

The two couples are equal and opposite, so the load system is balanced on its own and the reactions are zero. For the moment jump theorem, the clockwise couple at $A$, i.e. $\mathcal{M} = -6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, brings the moment from $0$ to $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ immediately to the right of $A$. Between $A$ and $B$ there are no loads and the shear is zero, so the moment remains constant and equals $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ in each section, with the lower fibers in tension. The counterclockwise couple at $B$ brings it back to zero at the end. The beam is then in **pure bending**: $T = 0$ and $M$ constant over all span.
