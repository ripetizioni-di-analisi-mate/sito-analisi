Throughout the central segment $CD$ of a beam the measured shear is zero. It is positive just before $C$ and negative just after $D$. The bending moment is continuous and satisfies $M'=T$. Where does the moment reach a local maximum?

- Only at the midpoint of $CD$.
- Only at $C$.
- At every section from $C$ to $D$.
- The moment is necessarily zero throughout $CD$.

## Explanation

The sign of $T$ determines how $M$ changes. The moment rises before $C$, remains constant on $CD$ and falls after $D$. Zero shear specifies the slope without fixing the value of the moment.
