The beam $AB$ shown, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$ and supported at both ends, carries a trapezoidal load that equals $q_A = 2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ in $A$ and grows linearly up to $q_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ in $B$. What is the shear force at midspan?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0.4,0.8,q" quote="A,B,6 m" titolo="Simply supported beam with a trapezoidal load varying from $2$ to $4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$"}

- $0$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $-1\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

Split the load diagram into a rectangle with resultant $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acting $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and a triangle with resultant $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acting $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Taking moments about $B$ gives $6\,V_A - 12 \cdot 3 - 6 \cdot 2 = 0$, hence $V_A = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$. The load intensity at midspan is $3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. From $A$ to midspan the load diagram is therefore a trapezoid with resultant $\dfrac{(2 + 3) \cdot 3}{2} = 7.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. The shear at midspan is $T = 8 - 7.5 = 0.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. Since it is still positive, the maximum bending moment occurs just to the right of midspan, toward the more heavily loaded end. The answer $0$ applies only to symmetric loading.
