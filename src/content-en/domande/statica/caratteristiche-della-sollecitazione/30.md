The beam $AD$ shown, of length $5\,\mathrm{m}$, has a hinge $A$ and $B$ roller support, at $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, and carries the uniform $q = 4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ load over the entire length, including the $BD$ overhang. How far from $A$ does the bending moment reach its positive maximum?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;D,5,0" aste="A-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,D,0.5,0.5,q" quote="A,B,4 m;B,D,1 m" titolo="Overhanging beam with a uniform load over its entire length"}

- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.875\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.125\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The resultant load, $20\,\mathrm{kN}$, is applied $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, so moment equilibrium about $B$ gives $-4\,V_A + 20 \cdot 1.5 = 0$ and $V_A = 7.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. In the segment $AB$ the shear is $T(x) = 7.5 - 4x$. The moment has the maximum where the shear vanishes, i.e.
$$
x = \frac{7.5}{4} = 1.875\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The load on the overhang reduces $V_A$ compared to that of the beam without overhang, and the maximum is therefore located before the midspan of $AB$, which is at $2\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $3.125\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by dividing by $q$ the reaction $V_B = 12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ instead of $V_A$.
