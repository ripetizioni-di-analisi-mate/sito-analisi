In an $0 < x < 4\,\mathrm{m}$ segment of a horizontal beam only a uniform load acts. The shear equals $T(x) = 6 - 2x$, in $\mathrm{kN}$, and the bending moment at the beginning of the segment is $M(0) = 0$. What is the maximum bending moment in the segment?

- $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $9\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $27\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

For the variation theorem in a segment with uniform load, the $T(x) = 6 - 2x$ shear corresponds to $T_a = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $q = 2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, so the moment is
$$
M(x) = 6x - \frac{2x^2}{2} = 6x - x^2 .
$$
The derivative of $M$ is $T$, so the maximum is in the section where the shear vanishes, $x = 3\,\mathrm{m}$, and equals $M(3) = 18 - 9 = 9\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The value $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ is the moment at the end of the segment, in $x = 4\,\mathrm{m}$, and the value $18\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ is obtained without the end of the load.
