A simply supported beam of span $4\,\mathrm{m}$ can carry the same total load as $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ in two ways: as a uniform $q = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ load over all span, or as a concentrated force of $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at midspan. How do the maximum bending moments in the two cases compare?

- They are equal because the total load is the same
- The maximum moment is twice as large under the uniform load
- The maximum moment is one quarter as large under the uniform load
- The maximum moment is half as large under the uniform load

## Explanation

In both cases the reactions equal $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ and the maximum moment falls at midspan. With concentrated force the left part of the midspan contains only $V_A$, with a moment arm of $2\,\mathrm{m}$, then $M_{\max} = 6 \cdot 2 = 12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. With the uniform load the left side also contains the section of length $2\,\mathrm{m}$ load, resulting in $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $1\,\mathrm{m}$ arm, therefore $M_{\max} = 12 - 6 = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The distributed load then gives the **half** of the maximum moment, as is also shown by the $\dfrac{qL^2}{8}$ and $\dfrac{PL}{4}$ formulas with $P = qL$.
