The frame shown has external hinges $A$ and $E$ at the same elevation, columns of height $4\,\mathrm{m}$, and a beam of length $8\,\mathrm{m}$. A parallel-link connection with horizontal links joins bodies $ABC$ and $CDE$ at midspan. It restrains relative horizontal translation and relative rotation. How is the structure classified?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,4,4;D,8,4;E,8,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E" interni="C,doppio-pendolo,0" vincoli="A,cerniera;E,cerniera" quote="A,B,4 m,0.8;B,C,4 m,-0.8;C,D,4 m,-0.8" titolo="Portal frame with an internal parallel-link connection with horizontal links at $C$"}

- Statically determinate because $s = 3n = 6$
- Kinematically unstable because equal rotations about $A$ and $E$ are compatible with the parallel-link connection
- Statically determinate because the parallel-link connection prevents relative rotation of the two bodies
- Kinematically unstable because $s < 3n$

## Explanation

The $A$ and $E$ hinges and parallel-link support give $s = 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 = 3n$, so counting does not rule out static determinacy. They rotate $ABC$ around $A$ and $CDE$ around $E$ of the same rotation $\varphi$, so the relative rotation is zero. The point $C$ is located $4\,\mathrm{m}$ above both $A$ and $E$, so in the two rotations it has the same horizontal displacement $-4\,\varphi$, while the vertical shifts equal $4\,\varphi$ and $-4\,\varphi$. Parallel-link support to horizontal links leaves the vertical relative translation free, so motion is compatible with all constraints and the structure is **unstable**. With vertical links the vertical relative translation would be prevented and the portal frame would be statically determinate.
