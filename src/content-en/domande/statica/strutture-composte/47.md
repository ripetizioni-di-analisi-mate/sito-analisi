The Gerber beam shown has the segment $AC$ bound by a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$, with the $BC$ overhang, and the segment $CD$ connected in $C$ by an internal hinge. The roller support at $D$ has the inclined support surface of $45°$, so its reaction is directed along the line to $45°$ rising to the left. At the midpoint of segment $CD$, the vertical force $P = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. What is the horizontal reaction $H_A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,6,0;D,10,0" aste="A-C;C-D" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;D,carrello,315" forze="8 0,0,-1.6,P" quote="A,B,4 m,0.8;B,C,2 m,0.8;C,8 0,2 m,0.8;8 0,D,2 m,0.8" titolo="Gerber beam with an inclined roller support at $D$ and force $P = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$"}

- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left
- $H_A = 0$
- $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right

## Explanation

The reaction $R_D$ of the inclined roller support has components of equal intensity, the vertical upward and the horizontal to the left. Moment equilibrium of the $CD$-suspended segment around $C$ gives $4\,R_{Dy} - 20 \cdot 2 = 0$, then $R_{Dy} = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ and the horizontal component equals $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left. Therefore the balance at the horizontal translation of $CD$ requires that in $C$ the segment $AC$ exerts on $CD$ a horizontal force of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right. On the segment $AC$ then acts in $C$ the opposite force, $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left, and the roller support at $B$ does not give horizontal reactions. So $H_A = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$, **to the right**. With a horizontal plane roller support at $D$ you would have $H_A = 0$.
