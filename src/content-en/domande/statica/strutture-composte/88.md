The arch shown has the members $AC$ and $CB$ connected by the hinge $C$ and the springings at the same elevation; the springing $A$ is a hinge, the springing $B$ is a roller support on a horizontal surface, and the springings are connected by the tie $AB$. The arch only carries vertical loads directed downward. Which statement about the tie is correct?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,3;B,8,0" aste="A-C;C-B" catene="A-B" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" titolo="Tied arch with a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$"}

- It is in compression because the loads tend to move the springings closer together
- It is in tension, with a force equal to the thrust of the same arch hinged at both springings and without a tie
- It is unloaded because pin $A$ has no horizontal reaction under vertical loading
- It is in tension, with a force equal to half the resultant of the loads

## Explanation

The roller support only gives a vertical reaction and the loads are vertical, so horizontal force equilibrium of the structure gives $H_A = 0$, and the vertical reactions are those of the equivalent simply supported beam. On the $CB$ half, the tie exerts the horizontal force $-N$ in $B$, with positive in tension $N$, at the same point where the reaction $H_B = -H$ acts in the arch on two hinges. Therefore the auxiliary equation around $C$ is the same in the two cases, and results in $N = H = \dfrac{M_C^0}{f}$. With loads directed down the moment $M_C^0$ of the equivalent beam is positive, so the tie is **in tension** and absorbs the thrust that in the untied arch would give the springings.
