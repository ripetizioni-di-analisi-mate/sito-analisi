The scissor frame shown consists of straight members $AD$ and $BE$, connected by a hinge at their common midpoint $C$. The members are hinged to the ground in $A$ and $B$, at the same elevation and at $4\,\mathrm{m}$ distance; end $D$ is located on the vertical line through $B$, $4\,\mathrm{m}$ higher. In $D$ the vertical force $P = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. What is the vertical reaction $V_A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,2,2;D,4,4;E,0,4" aste="A-C;C-D;B-C;C-E" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="D,0,-1.4,P" quote="A,B,4 m,0.8;B,D,4 m,0.6" titolo="Scissor frame with hinge $C$ at the intersection and force $P = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $D$"}

- $V_A = 0$
- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward

## Explanation

The member $BE$ carries no loads and is only connected by the $B$ and $C$ hinges, so by the theorem on the hinged unloaded member at the two extremes the reaction at $B$ is directed along the line $BC$, inclined by $45°$. Therefore $H_B = -V_B$. Moment equilibrium of the structure about $A$ gives $4\,V_B - 10 \cdot 4 = 0$, i.e. $V_B = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$, and therefore $H_B = -10\,\mathrm{kN}$. Vertical force equilibrium of the structure gives $V_A = 10 - V_B$, therefore **$V_A = 0$**. The reaction at $A$ is horizontal, $H_A = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right: all vertical load drops down along the member $BE$.
