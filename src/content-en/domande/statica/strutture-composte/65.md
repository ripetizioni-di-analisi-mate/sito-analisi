The three-hinged arch shown is formed by the straight $AC$ and $CB$ members, with the springings at the same elevation, the span $12\,\mathrm{m}$ and the crown $C$ at midspan, $4\,\mathrm{m}$ above the springings. On the member $AC$, at $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ horizontally, the vertical force $P_1 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts; on the member $CB$, at $9\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, the vertical force $P_2 = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts; both are directed downward. What is the vertical reaction $V_A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,6,4;B,12,0" aste="A-C;C-B" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="2 1.333,0,-1.4,P_1;9 2,0,-1.4,P_2" quote="A,2 0,2 m,0.8;2 0,6 0,4 m,0.8;6 0,9 0,3 m,0.8;9 0,B,3 m,0.8" titolo="Three-hinged arch with forces $P_1 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $P_2 = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$"}

- $V_A = 6.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $V_A = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $V_A = 7.5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $V_A = 11.5\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The springings are at the same elevation and the loads are vertical, so for the **Theorem on Arch thrust** the vertical reactions coincide with those of the equivalent simply supported beam of span $12\,\mathrm{m}$. Horizontal reactions have zero arm about $B$, so moment equilibrium about $B$ gives
$$
-12\,V_A + 12 \cdot 10 + 6 \cdot 3 = 0 .
$$
So $V_A = \dfrac{138}{12} = 11.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward, regardless of the rise. The value $6.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ is obtained by measuring the arms from $A$ instead of $B$.
