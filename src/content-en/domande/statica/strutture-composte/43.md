The three-hinged arch shown is formed by the straight $AC$ and $CB$ members, with the springings at the same elevation, the span $10\,\mathrm{m}$ and the crown $C$ at midspan, $5\,\mathrm{m}$ above the springings. At $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ the vertical downward force $P = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts on the member $AC$. With the origin at $A$ and coordinates in meters, through which point does the line of action of the reaction at springing $A$ pass?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,5,5;B,10,0" aste="A-C;C-B" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="2 2,0,-1.6,P" quote="A,2 0,2 m,0.8;2 0,5 0,3 m,0.8;5 0,B,5 m,0.8;5 0,C,5 m,0.6" titolo="Three-hinged arch with force $P = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ on half $AC$"}

- $(2,\ 2)$
- $(5,\ 5)$
- $(2,\ 5)$
- $(2,\ 8)$

## Explanation

The $CB$ half is unloaded, so by the theorem on the unloaded member hinged at the two extremes the force transmitting in $C$ is directed along the line $BC$, of equation $y = 10 - x$. On the $AC$ half then act three non-parallel forces: $P$, the reaction at $A$ and the force at $C$. Since three non-parallel forces in equilibrium have concurrent lines of action at one point, the reaction at $A$ passes through the point where the line of action of $P$, i.e. $x = 2$, meets the line of action $BC$, that is, through $(2,\ 8)$. The same point is found with reactions. The equivalent simply supported beam gives $V_A = 16\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$, the line $BC$ is inclined by $45^\circ$, so $H = V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$, and the $\dfrac{V_A}{H} = 4$ ratio is the slope of the line connecting $A$ with $(2,\ 8)$. The point $(2,\ 2)$ is the point of application of $P$, the point $(5,\ 5)$ is the crown.
