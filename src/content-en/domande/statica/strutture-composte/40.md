The three-hinged arch shown is formed by the straight $AC$ and $CB$ members, with the springings at the same elevation, the span $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and the crown $C$ at midspan, $4\,\mathrm{m}$ above the springings. In the crown, the horizontal force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts to the right, applied to the member $AC$. What is the vertical reaction $V_A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,4;B,8,0" aste="A-C;C-B" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="C,1.6,0,F" quote="A,4 0,4 m,0.8;4 0,B,4 m,0.8;4 0,C,4 m,0.6" titolo="Three-hinged arch with horizontal force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at the crown"}

- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $V_A = 0$

## Explanation

External reactions can be calculated with the equations of the whole structure, which contain $F$ at the same point whatever the member is receiving it. The force $F$ is applied to $4\,\mathrm{m}$ of elevation, so moment equilibrium about $A$ gives
$$
8\,V_B - 10 \cdot 4 = 0 ,
$$
i.e. $V_B = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$. The vertical force equilibrium gives $V_A + V_B = 0$, therefore $V_A = -5\,\mathrm{kN}$, i.e. $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ **downward**. The two vertical reactions form a couple that balances the moment of $F$, and for this reason a horizontal force produces non-zero vertical reactions.
