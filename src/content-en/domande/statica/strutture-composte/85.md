A parabolic three-hinged arch and a three-hinged portal frame have the same $L$ span, the pin supports at the same elevation and the internal pin at midspan, at the same $f$ elevation above the pin supports. Both carry the same $q$ load, uniform on the horizontal projection of all span; in the portal frame the load acts on the crossbeam. Which statement about the thrusts is correct?

- The arch develops greater thrust because its curved axis follows the load distribution
- The portal frame has zero thrust because its columns are vertical
- The portal frame develops greater thrust because the entire load acts at the elevation of the internal hinge
- The thrusts are equal, each with magnitude $\dfrac{qL^2}{8f}$

## Explanation

In both structures the support hinges are at the same elevation and the loads are vertical. The theorem for the **horizontal thrust of a three-hinged arch** therefore applies. It allows any shape for the two members, including the straight segments of the portal frame. The thrust is $H = \dfrac{M_C^0}{f}$, where $M_C^0$ is the midspan bending moment in the equivalent simply supported beam. The moment arms of the vertical loads depend only on their horizontal positions. Both structures therefore have the same equivalent beam, with $M_C^0 = \dfrac{qL^2}{8}$, and the same horizontal thrust, $\dfrac{qL^2}{8f}$. The shapes of the members do affect their internal forces and bending moments.
