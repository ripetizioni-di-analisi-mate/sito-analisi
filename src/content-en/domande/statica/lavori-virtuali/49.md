An infinitesimal rigid-body displacement of a plane body has at point $O = (0, 0)$ the components $\delta u_O = 3\varepsilon$ and $\delta v_O = -2\varepsilon$ and the rotation $\delta\varphi = \varepsilon$, positive counterclockwise, with $\varepsilon$ infinitesimal, coordinates and displacements in meters. Where is the center of rotation?

- $(3, 2)$
- $(2, 3)$
- $(-2, -3)$
- $(2, -3)$

## Explanation

The center $C = (x_C, y_C)$ is the point that does not move, and the displacement of each point is written $\delta u = -\delta\varphi\,(y - y_C)$, $\delta v = \delta\varphi\,(x - x_C)$. At point $O$, the first formula gives $3\varepsilon = -\varepsilon\,(0 - y_C)$, then $y_C = 3$. The second gives $-2\varepsilon = \varepsilon\,(0 - x_C)$, then $x_C = 2$. Therefore the center is $C = (2, 3)$. The point $(3, 2)$ is obtained by exchanging the two formulas, and $(-2, -3)$ by changing the sign of both.
