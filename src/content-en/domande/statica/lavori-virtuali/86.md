The beam shown has the pin supports $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (9, 0)$ and the internal hinge $C = (3, 0)$ (coordinates in meters). Which load, acting alone, leaves the structure in equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,3,0;B,9,0" aste="A-C;C-B" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" quote="A,C,3 m,0.9;C,B,6 m,0.9" titolo="Beam with pin supports $A$ and $B$ and internal hinge $C$"}

- A vertical force at $C$
- A couple applied to segment $AC$
- A horizontal force at $C$
- A vertical force applied at the midpoint of $AC$

## Explanation

The two segments rotate about $A$ and $B$, and the three hinges are collinear, so the beam admits a non-zero virtual displacement. The displacement of $C$ is $(0, 3\,\delta\varphi_1)$ as the point of $AC$ and $(0, -6\,\delta\varphi_2)$ as the point of $CB$, so $\delta\varphi_2 = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,\delta\varphi_1$ and $C$ only move vertically. A horizontal force at $C$ performs null work, and by the theorem on the equilibrium of unstable structures the beam is in equilibrium. A vertical force at $C$ or at the midpoint of $AC$ and a couple on the section $AC$ perform a different work from zero for $\delta\varphi_1 \neq 0$.
