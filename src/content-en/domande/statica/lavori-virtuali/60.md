The beam $AB$ shown, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, has a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$ moving along a vertical plane, with the horizontal reaction. At the midpoint, the force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. Which statement is true?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;M,3,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello,0" forze="M,0,-1.5,F" quote="A,M,3 m,0.9;M,B,3 m,0.9" titolo="Beam with a pin at $A$ and a roller on a vertical surface at $B$"}

- It is in equilibrium, with $V_A = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $R_B = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- It is in equilibrium because the three scalar restraints equal the three degrees of freedom
- It is not in equilibrium because $F$ does work during rotation about $A$
- It is in equilibrium because the reactions at frictionless restraints do zero work

## Explanation

The roller support reaction at $B$ is horizontal, so its line of action passes through $A$. The rotation $\delta\varphi$ around $A$ moves $B$ vertically by $6\,\delta\varphi$, parallel to the support surface, so it is compatible with both constraints and the beam is unstable, despite the three scalar constraints. In this rotation the midpoint rises by $3\,\delta\varphi$ and $F$ does the $-10 \cdot 3\,\delta\varphi = -30\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \delta\varphi$ work, other than zero for $\delta\varphi \neq 0$. By the principle of virtual work the beam **is not in equilibrium**. None of the reactions have moment about $A$, so none can balance the moment of $F$.
