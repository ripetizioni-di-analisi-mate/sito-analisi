A planar rigid body makes the infinitesimal displacement identified by the $\delta u_O = \varepsilon$ and $\delta v_O = 2\,\varepsilon$ components of the origin $O = (0, 0)$ displacement and the rotation $\delta\varphi = \varepsilon$, positive counterclockwise, with $\varepsilon$ infinitesimal, coordinates and displacements in meters. At point $P = (1, 4)$ of the body acts the force of components $F_x = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $F_y = -2\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the virtual work of the force?

- $-24\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \varepsilon$
- $2\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \varepsilon$
- $28\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \varepsilon$
- $-12\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \varepsilon$

## Explanation

For infinitesimal rigid-body displacement the point $P$ moves by $\delta u_P = \delta u_O - \delta\varphi\,(y_P - y_O)$ and $\delta v_P = \delta v_O + \delta\varphi\,(x_P - x_O)$. With $P = (1, 4)$ you get $\delta u_P = \varepsilon - 4\,\varepsilon = -3\,\varepsilon$ and $\delta v_P = 2\,\varepsilon + \varepsilon = 3\,\varepsilon$. The virtual work of force is therefore
$$
\delta L = F_x\,\delta u_P + F_y\,\delta v_P = 6 \cdot (-3\,\varepsilon) + (-2) \cdot 3\,\varepsilon = -24\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \varepsilon .
$$
The value $2\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \varepsilon$ is obtained by neglecting rotation, the value $28\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \varepsilon$ with the marks of the inverted rotation terms and the value $-12\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \varepsilon$ by exchanging $x_P$ and $y_P$ with each other.
