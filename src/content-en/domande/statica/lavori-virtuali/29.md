The beam $AB$ shown, of length $L$, is fixed at $A$, resting on a roller support at $B$ and carries the uniform load $q$ over the entire length. You want to calculate the reaction $R_B$ with the principle of virtual work for rigid bodies, removing the roller support. Which statement is true?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0.6,0.6,q" quote="A,B,L,0.9" titolo="Beam fixed at $A$ and supported at $B$ under uniform load $q$"}

- Rotating the beam about $A$ gives $R_B = \dfrac{qL}{2}$
- Removing the roller leaves a statically determinate cantilever that admits only zero virtual displacement, so the principle gives no equation for $R_B$
- The result is $R_B = 0$ because the fixed support alone balances the load
- Translating the beam vertically gives $R_B = qL$

## Explanation

Fixed support exhibits three simple reactions and roller support one, so the beam has four scalar constraints for three degrees of freedom and is once statically indeterminate. Removing the roller support remains the cantilever fixed at $A$, which is statically determinate. Fixed support cancels $A$ displacement and rotation, so cantilever only admits zero virtual displacement and rotation around $A$ is not compatible with the remaining constraints. Consequently the work of $R_B$ and the load is identically null and the principle provides no equation for $R_B$. For a statically indeterminate structure the reactions are not derived from the balance of rigid bodies.
