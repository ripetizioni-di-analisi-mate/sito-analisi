The beam $AB$ shown, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, rests only on a roller at $A$ that moves along a horizontal plane. A $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ acts the force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downwards; At the end $B$ a vertical $V$ force and a horizontal $H$ force apply. Which values of $V$ and $H$ keep the beam in equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;E,4,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello" forze="E,0,-1.5,F" quote="A,E,4 m,0.9;E,B,2 m,0.9" titolo="Beam supported only by a roller at $A$, with $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$"}

- $V = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward and $H = 0$
- $V = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward and arbitrary $H$
- $V = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward and $H = 0$
- $V = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward and $H = 0$

## Explanation

The beam has $3$ degrees of freedom and the roller support removes only one scalar constraint, so it is unstable with degree of kinematic instability $l = 2$. Two linearly independent virtual shifts are horizontal $\delta u$ translation and $\delta\varphi$ rotation around $A$, both compatible with roller support. For the equilibrium theorem of unstable structures the work of loads must be null for each of the two. In the horizontal translation only $H$ works, with the $H\,\delta u$ work, then $H = 0$. In rotation about $A$ the force $F$ does work $-12 \cdot 4\,\delta\varphi$ and the force $V$, positive upwards, the $V \cdot 6\,\delta\varphi$ work, therefore $V = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$, upwards. With these values the roller support exerts the reaction $12 - 8 = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$, upwards. The value $V = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ is obtained by exchanging arms, and the choice of $H$ any neglects the condition given by the translation.
