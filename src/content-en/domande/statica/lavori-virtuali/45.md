The beam $AB$ shown, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, has only a parallel-link support at $A$ with the horizontal links, which prevents horizontal translation and rotation. At $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ the force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. What load, added to $F$, makes equilibrium possible?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,doppio-pendolo,180" forze="C,0,-1.5,F" quote="A,C,2 m,0.9;C,B,4 m,0.9" titolo="Beam restrained only by a parallel-link support at $A$, under force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$"}

- An upward vertical force of magnitude $\dfrac{10}{3}\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$
- An upward vertical force of magnitude $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ applied anywhere on the beam
- A counterclockwise couple of magnitude $20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at $A$
- No load, because the parallel-link support supplies a reaction couple

## Explanation

Parallel-link support leaves only vertical translation free, so the beam is a mechanism and its virtual shifts are the $\delta v$ translation, equal in all points. For the equilibrium theorem of an unstable structure there is equilibrium if and only if the work of loads for $\delta v$ is zero. The work of $F$ is $-10\,\delta v$, and that of a vertical $V$ force upwards is $V\,\delta v$ at whatever point it is applied. Therefore it serves $V = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ upwards, at any point; the resultant moment is balanced by the reactive couple of parallel-link support. A couple does no work for a translation, so on its own it doesn't give the balance.
