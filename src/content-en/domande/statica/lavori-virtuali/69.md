The beam shown is formed by the section $AC$, with a hinge at $A = (0, 0)$ and a roller support at $B = (4, 0)$, and by the section $CD$, connected to $AC$ at $C = (6, 0)$ by an internal parallel-link connection with the horizontal links and resting on a roller support at $D = (9, 0)$ (coordinates in meters). At the horizontal coordinate $7.5\,\mathrm{m}$ point, the force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. Using virtual work, what is reaction $R_B$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,6,0;D,9,0" aste="A-C;C-D" interni="C,doppio-pendolo,0" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;D,carrello" forze="7.5 0,0,-1.5,F" quote="A,B,4 m,0.9;B,C,2 m,0.9;C,7.5 0,1.5 m,0.9;7.5 0,D,1.5 m,0.9" titolo="Beam with an internal parallel-link connection at $C$ and $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$"}

- $4.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward
- $0$
- $13.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, downward
- $4.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, downward

## Explanation

Remove the roller at $B$. Segment $AC$ then rotates about $A$ through angle $\delta\varphi$. Parallel-link support prevents relative rotation, so $CD$ also rotates $\delta\varphi$, and does so around $D$, because roller support at $D$ blocks its vertical displacement; at $C$ the relative vertical scroll remains free. The point of application of $F$ then moves by $\delta\varphi\,(7.5 - 9) = -1.5\,\delta\varphi$, and $B$ by $4\,\delta\varphi$. The principle of virtual work gives
$$
R_B \cdot 4\,\delta\varphi - 12 \cdot (-1.5\,\delta\varphi) = 0 ,
\qquad
R_B = -4.5\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
i.e. $4.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ down. The value $0$ stems from the idea that parallel-link support does not transmit anything to the segment $AC$; Instead, it transmits a moment of $18\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.
