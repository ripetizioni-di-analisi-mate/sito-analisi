The simply supported beam in the figure has $6\,\mathrm{m}$ span, a hinge at $A$ and a roller support at $B$, and brings the downward force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acting $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. A student finds $V_A = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $R_B = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$, both upwards. The beam is considered as a free body, subject to $F$ and these two forces, and given the rigid rotation $\delta\varphi$ around $A$, positive counterclockwise. What is the total virtual work?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" forze="C,0,-1.5,F" reazioni="A,0,1.2,V_A;B,0,1.2,R_B" quote="A,C,2 m,0.9;C,B,4 m,0.9" titolo="Free-body diagram of the beam with the reactions calculated by the student"}

- $0$
- $-24\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \delta\varphi$
- $48\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \delta\varphi$
- $24\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \delta\varphi$

## Explanation

During a rotation about $A$, the point with horizontal coordinate $x$ rises by $\delta\varphi\,x$. The reaction $V_A$ at $A$ therefore does no work. Force $F$ does work $-12 \cdot 2\,\delta\varphi = -24\,\delta\varphi$, and force $R_B$ does work $8 \cdot 6\,\delta\varphi = 48\,\delta\varphi$. The total virtual work is $24\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \cdot \delta\varphi$. The principle of virtual work for a free rigid body requires a force system in equilibrium to do zero work in every rigid-body displacement. The proposed reactions fail this test. The correct values are $V_A = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $R_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$, for which the work is $-24\,\delta\varphi + 24\,\delta\varphi = 0$.
