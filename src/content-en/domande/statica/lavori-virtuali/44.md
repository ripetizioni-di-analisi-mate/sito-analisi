The beam shown is formed by segments $AB$, $BC$ and $CD$, with a pin support at $A = (0, 0)$, the internal hinges $B = (2, 0)$ and $C = (5, 0)$ and a roller support at $D = (8, 0)$ that moves along a horizontal plane (coordinates in meters). In the hinge $B$, the force $P = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. Which statement about the vertical force $F$ required at $C$ for equilibrium is true?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,0;C,5,0;D,8,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello" forze="B,0,-1.4,P" quote="A,B,2 m,0.9;B,C,3 m,0.9;C,D,3 m,0.9" titolo="Three-segment beam under force $P = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ at hinge $B$"}

- No value of $F$ gives equilibrium
- $F = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $F = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- Every value of $F$ gives equilibrium because the forces at the internal hinges do no work

## Explanation

The three segments have nine degrees of freedom and the scalar constraints are seven, so the structure has at least two degrees of kinematic instability. The $\delta v_B$ and $\delta v_C$ vertical shifts of the two internal hinges can be independently chosen: $AB$ rotates about $A$, $CD$ around $D$ and $BC$ follows the two extremes. For the equilibrium theorem of an unstable structure the work of the loads must be null for each of the two independent shifts. With $\delta v_C = 0$ and $\delta v_B \neq 0$ only $P$ does work, $-6\,\delta v_B \neq 0$, so the condition is not met for any $F$ value. A single force at $C$ therefore does not balance the force $P$ at $B$.
