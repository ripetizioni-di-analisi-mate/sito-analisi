The Warren truss shown has base triangles $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $2\,\mathrm{m}$, pin at $A$, roller support at $D$ and one overhang formed by the joints $I$ and $L$. At the $L$ extreme of the overhang the force $P = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. What is the vertical reaction $V_A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;L,12,0;E,1.5,2;G,4.5,2;H,7.5,2;I,10.5,2" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-L;E-G;G-H;H-I;A-E;E-B;B-G;G-C;C-H;H-D;D-I;I-L" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello" forze="L,0,-1.3,P" quote="A,B,3 m,1.2;B,C,3 m,1.2;C,D,3 m,1.2;D,L,3 m,1.2;12 0,12 2,2 m,0.9" titolo="Warren truss with force $P = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at the overhang tip"}

- $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward
- $16\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward

## Explanation

Take moments of the whole truss about roller $D$ to eliminate $V_D$. Reaction $V_A$ has moment arm $9\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left of $D$, and force $P$ has moment arm $3\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right, giving
$$
-9\,V_A - 12 \cdot 3 = 0 .
$$
This gives $V_A = -4\,\mathrm{kN}$, or $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ **downward**. The overhang load tends to rotate the truss about $D$ and lift joint $A$. Vertical equilibrium then gives $V_D = 16\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward for the other reaction. Interchanging the two reactions would give this second value.
