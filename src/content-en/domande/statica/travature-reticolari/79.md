The Pratt truss shown has $n = 8$ joints and $a = 13$ members and it is supported by a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $E$. First the three reactions are calculated with the equilibrium equations of the whole truss, then the axial forces with the method of the joints. How many unused joint equilibrium equations remain available as checks?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,12,0;F,3,4;G,6,4;H,9,4" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;F-G;G-H;A-F;E-H;B-F;C-G;D-H;F-C;H-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;E,carrello" forze="F,0,-1.5,P;G,0,-1.5,P;H,0,-1.5,P" quote="A,B,3 m,1.3;B,C,3 m,1.3;C,D,3 m,1.3;D,E,3 m,1.3;12 0,12 4,4 m,0.9" titolo="Pratt truss with eight joints and thirteen members"}

- $0$
- $3$
- $6$
- $16$

## Explanation

Each joint provides two force equilibrium equations, making $2n = 16$ joint equations in all. With the reactions known, only $a = 13$ member forces remain unknown and $13$ independent equations suffice. This leaves $16 - 13 = 3$ unused equations. They are satisfied automatically because the three equilibrium equations of the whole truss are combinations of the joint equations. The unused equations provide a check, as at the last joint when working from one support toward the other.
