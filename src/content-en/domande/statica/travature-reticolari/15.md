In a truss, an $AB$ member has the curved, circle-arch axis and is connected to the joints $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (4, 3)$, in meters, with ideal hinges. Along the member do not act loads and its weight is negligible. In what direction does the member exert a force on joint $B$?

- Along the tangent to the member axis at point $B$
- Perpendicular to line $AB$
- Vertically, because the member carries no horizontal loads
- Along line $AB$ joining the two pins

## Explanation

Only the end forces at pins $A$ and $B$ act on the member. There are no distributed or intermediate loads, and the ideal pins transmit no couples. For this two-force member, moment equilibrium about $A$ requires the force at $B$ to pass through $A$. Thus the force transmitted between the member and joint $B$ acts along line $AB$, with unit vector $\left(\dfrac{4}{5}, \dfrac{3}{5}\right)$, regardless of the member’s shape. Curvature affects the internal forces and can produce bending moment at an intermediate section, but the end forces still act along the line joining the pins.
