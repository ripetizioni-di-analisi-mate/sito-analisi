At joint $K = (0, 0)$ of a truss only the horizontal member $KA$, with $A = (-4, 0)$, and the member $KB$, with $B = (3, 4)$, in meters, concur. In $K$ the horizontal force $F = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts to the right, directed like the member $KA$ and towards the outside of the member. What are the forces in the two members?

::struttura{nodi="K,0,0;A,-4,0;B,3,4" aste="K-A;K-B" reticolare=1 forze="2.2 0,1.4,0,F" titolo="Joint $K$ with two members and force $F$ acting along $KA$"}

- $N_{KA} = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $N_{KB} = 0$
- $N_{KA} = -9\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $N_{KB} = 0$
- $N_{KA} = 0$ and $N_{KB} = 15\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $N_{KA} = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $N_{KB} = -15\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The unit vectors pointing away from joint $K$ are $(-1, 0)$ for member $KA$ and $\left(\dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5}\right)$ for $KB$. Force $F$ acts along $KA$, so $KB$ is the only member force in the vertical equilibrium equation and its coefficient is $\dfrac{4}{5}$. Equation $\dfrac{4}{5}\,N_{KB} = 0$ gives $N_{KB} = 0$. Horizontal equilibrium then gives $-N_{KA} + 9 = 0$ and hence $N_{KA} = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$. Member $KA$ is in tension because the force pulls the joint away from $A$. Member $KB$ carries zero force, as expected when a load at a two-member joint acts along one of the members.
