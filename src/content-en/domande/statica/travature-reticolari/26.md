The cantilever truss shown has the joints $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (0, 3)$, $C = (4, 3)$ and $D = (4, 0)$ in meters and the members $AB$, $BC$, $AD$, $CD$, $AC$. It is attached to the wall with a hinge $A$ and a roller support at $B$ that moves along the vertical wall. A cable pulls the joint $C$ with the horizontal force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right. What is the force in member $BC$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,3;C,4,3;D,4,0" aste="A-B;B-C;A-D;C-D;A-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera,180;B,carrello,180" forze="5.8 3,1.4,0," etichette="5.2,3.35,F" quote="A,D,4 m,1;0 0,0 3,3 m,-1.4" titolo="Cantilever truss pulled at $C$ by horizontal force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$"}

- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $16\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $0$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)

## Explanation

The roller at $B$ moves along a vertical wall and has a horizontal reaction $H_B$. Taking moments about $A$ includes $H_B$ and $F$, both horizontal at elevation $3\,\mathrm{m}$. The equation $-3\,H_B - 3 \cdot 12 = 0$ gives $H_B = -12\,\mathrm{kN}$, or $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left. Joint $B$ is acted on by vertical member $AB$, horizontal member $BC$, and reaction $H_B$. Horizontal equilibrium at joint $B$ gives $N_{BC} - 12 = 0$, hence $N_{BC} = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$, so member $BC$ is in tension. Force equilibrium of the whole cantilever gives $H_A = 0$ and $V_A = 0$. All other members carry zero force.
