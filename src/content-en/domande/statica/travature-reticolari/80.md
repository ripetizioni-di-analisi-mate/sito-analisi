The three-hinged trussed arch shown is formed by the triangle $ADC$ and the triangle $CEB$, connected only at joint $C$, with $A = (0, 0)$, $D = (3, 0)$, $C = (4, 3)$, $E = (5, 0)$ and $B = (8, 0)$ in meters; it is supported by the external $A$ and $B$ hinges. The only load is the downward force $P = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ at joint $D$. What is the force in member $CB$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,3,0;C,4,3;E,5,0;B,8,0" aste="A-D;D-C;A-C;C-E;E-B;C-B" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="D,0,-1.3,P" quote="A,D,3 m,1.2;D,E,2 m,1.2;E,B,3 m,1.2;8 0,8 3,3 m,0.9" titolo="Three-hinged trussed arch with force $P = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $D$"}

- $0$
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)

## Explanation

At unloaded joint $E$ only the noncollinear members $CE$ and $EB$ meet, so both carry zero force. This leaves member $CB$ and the pin reaction at joint $B$. Equilibrium requires the reaction to act along $CB$, so the right-hand part acts as an inclined strut. Taking moments about $A$ gives $8\,V_B - 24 \cdot 3 = 0$ and hence $V_B = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$. The unit vector for $BC$ is $\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}, \dfrac{3}{5}\right)$, and vertical equilibrium at joint $B$ gives
$$
\frac{3}{5}\,N_{CB} + 9 = 0 ,
$$
Thus $N_{CB} = -15\,\mathrm{kN}$, placing the member **in compression**. The reaction at $B$ has magnitude $15\,\mathrm{kN}$, with $H_B = -12\,\mathrm{kN}$. The value $0$ would assume that the whole right-hand part is force-free merely because it carries no external load.
