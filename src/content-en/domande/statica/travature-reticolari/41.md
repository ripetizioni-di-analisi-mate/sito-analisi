A parallel chord truss must be constructed, supported at the ends and loaded with downward forces, arranged symmetrically in the joints of the top chord, using wooden members for the diagonals and steel members for the verticals. Steel tie rods are thin and can only work with tension. Which arrangement permits the use of tension rods as verticals?

- The Pratt truss, because the verticals carry the loads toward the supports
- The Warren truss without verticals, because successive diagonals alternate between tension and compression
- The Howe truss, because its verticals are in tension or carry zero force and its interior diagonals are in compression
- Any of the three, because the verticals of a simply supported truss are always in tension

## Explanation

The interior diagonals of a Howe truss rise toward midspan. On the left half $V_s > 0$, so section equilibrium puts those diagonals in compression. Vertical joint equilibrium then places the vertical members in tension or gives them zero force. Timber can carry the diagonal compression and steel rods can carry tension in the verticals, making the Howe layout suitable. In a Pratt truss the internal diagonals are in tension and the verticals are in compression or carry zero force, so tension rods are unsuitable for those verticals. A Warren truss without verticals has no such members for the rods.
