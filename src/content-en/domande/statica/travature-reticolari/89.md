The Warren truss shown has the joints $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ on the bottom chord and $E$, $G$, $H$ on the top chord, and the eleven members $AB$, $BC$, $CD$, $EG$, $GH$, $AE$, $EB$, $BG$, $GC$, $CH$, $HD$. Which sequence shows that this is a simple truss, constructed from a triangle by adding one joint at a time with two noncollinear members connected to existing joints?

- Triangle $ABE$, then joints $C$, $G$, $H$, and $D$
- Triangle $ABC$, then joints $E$, $G$, $H$, and $D$
- Triangle $ABE$, then joints $G$, $C$, $H$, and $D$
- Triangle $ABE$, then joints $G$, $H$, $C$, and $D$

## Explanation

Start with triangle $ABE$ and add $G$, $C$, $H$, and $D$ in that order. Joint $G$ connects to $E$ and $B$ through $EG$ and $BG$. Joint $C$ connects to $B$ and $G$ through $BC$ and $GC$. Joint $H$ connects to $G$ and $C$ through $GH$ and $CH$, and joint $D$ to $C$ and $H$ through $CD$ and $HD$. Each new joint connects through two noncollinear members, so the truss is simple, consistent with $a = 11 = 2 \cdot 7 - 3$. Adding $C$ immediately after triangle $ABE$ would leave joint $C$ connected only through $BC$. Adding $H$ immediately after $G$ would similarly leave joint $H$ connected only through $GH$. Joints $A$, $B$, and $C$ are collinear, and there is no member between $A$ and $C$, so $ABC$ is not a triangle of the truss.
