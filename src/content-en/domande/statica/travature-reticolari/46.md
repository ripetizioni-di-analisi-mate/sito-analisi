The Pratt truss shown has four $3\,\mathrm{m}$ panels and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ height, pin at $A$ and roller support at $E$. On the top chord, between the joints $F$ and $H$, a uniformly distributed load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ downward acts. To apply the truss model the load is replaced with concentrated forces in the joints. What force must be applied at joint $G$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,12,0;F,3,4;G,6,4;H,9,4" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;F-G;G-H;A-F;E-H;B-F;C-G;D-H;F-C;H-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;E,carrello" carichi="F,H,0.5,0.5,q" quote="A,B,3 m,1.3;B,C,3 m,1.3;C,D,3 m,1.3;D,E,3 m,1.3;12 0,12 4,4 m,0.9" titolo="Pratt truss with distributed load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ on the top chord between $F$ and $H$"}

- $7.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward

## Explanation

The ideal truss model requires loads to act at its joints. Treat each loaded chord segment as a simply supported beam between its end joints. Segment $FG$, of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$, carries $q \cdot 3 = 15\,\mathrm{kN}$. By symmetry, half goes to $F$ and half to $G$. The same reasoning applies to segment $GH$. Joint $G$ therefore receives half the load from each neighboring segment, giving
$$
F_G = \frac{q \cdot 3}{2} + \frac{q \cdot 3}{2} = 7.5 + 7.5 = 15\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
Joints $F$ and $H$ each receive $7.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. The sum $7.5 + 15 + 7.5 = 30\,\mathrm{kN}$ equals the resultant $q \cdot 6$ of the applied load. Dividing that resultant equally among the three joints would give $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ and would overlook that $F$ and $H$ each border only one loaded segment.
