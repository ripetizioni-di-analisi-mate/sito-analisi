The Howe truss shown has four $3\,\mathrm{m}$ panels and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ height, pin at $A$, roller support at $E$ and three $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ forces down at joints $F$, $G$, $H$ of the top chord. What is the force in diagonal $BG$?

- $12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $16.67\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)

## Explanation

By symmetry, $V_A = V_E = 30\,\mathrm{kN}$. A vertical section between $B$ and $C$ cuts $FG$, $BG$, and $BC$. The two chords are parallel and horizontal, so use vertical equilibrium of the left-hand part, containing joints $A$, $B$, and $F$. Diagonal $BG$ slopes up to the right with $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$. Assuming tension, its force on the left-hand part has the vertical component $\dfrac{4}{5}\,N_{BG}$, giving
$$
30 - 20 + \frac{4}{5}\,N_{BG} = 0 .
$$
This gives $N_{BG} = -12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, so the diagonal is **in compression**, as are the diagonals rising toward midspan in a Howe truss under this loading. The value $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the sum $V_s$ of the vertical forces on the left, before division by $\sin\alpha$.
