In a K-truss the joint $K$ is located in the middle of a vertical vertical, between the $KU$ sections, upward, and $KL$, downward. Two diagonals also extend to the left from the joint: $KP$ with unit vector $\left(-\dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5}\right)$ and $KQ$ with unit vector $\left(-\dfrac{3}{5}, -\dfrac{4}{5}\right)$. No loads are acting in the joint and the diagonal $KP$ is in tension with $N_{KP} = 15\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the force in diagonal $KQ$?

::struttura{nodi="K,0,0;U,0,2;L,0,-2;P,-1.5,2;Q,-1.5,-2" aste="K-U;K-L;K-P;K-Q" reticolare=1 titolo="Joint $K$ of a K-truss"}

- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $0$
- $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)

## Explanation

Members $KU$ and $KL$ are vertical, so only the two diagonals enter the horizontal equilibrium equation at the joint. The equation is
$$
-\frac{3}{5}\,N_{KP} - \frac{3}{5}\,N_{KQ} = 0 ,
$$
This gives $N_{KQ} = -N_{KP} = -15\,\mathrm{kN}$, so diagonal $KQ$ carries **compression** of the same magnitude. The two diagonal forces have opposite horizontal components, while their vertical components both act upward and sum to $\dfrac{4}{5}(15 + 15) = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$. Vertical equilibrium then gives $N_{KL} - N_{KU} = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$, so the forces in the two vertical-member segments differ.
