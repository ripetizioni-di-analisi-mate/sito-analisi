The truss tower shown has the joints $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (3, 0)$, $C = (0, 4)$, $D = (3, 4)$, $E = (0, 8)$ and $F = (3, 8)$ in meters, the members $AB$, $AC$, $BD$, $CD$, $CE$, $DF$, $EF$ and the diagonals $AD$ and $CF$. It rests on a pin at $A$ and on a horizontal-level roller support at $B$. At the top, at joint $E$, the wind exerts the force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right. A horizontal $s$ section at $6\,\mathrm{m}$ elevation cuts the $CE$, $CF$ and $DF$ members. What is the force in diagonal $CF$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,0,4;D,3,4;E,0,8;F,3,8" aste="A-B;A-C;B-D;C-D;A-D;C-E;D-F;E-F;C-F" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="E,1.5,0,F" tratteggi="-0.8,6 3.8,6" etichette="-1.1,6.2,s" quote="A,B,3 m,1.1;3 0,3 4,4 m,0.9;3 4,3 8,4 m,0.9" titolo="Trussed tower with force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $E$ and section $s$"}

- $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)

## Explanation

Cut members $CE$ and $DF$ are vertical and parallel, so the moment center for diagonal $CF$ lies at infinity. Instead, project the forces on the upper free body perpendicular to the two parallel members, in the horizontal direction. Assuming tension, the diagonal force on the upper part acts at $F$ toward $C$ with unit vector $\left(-\dfrac{3}{5}, -\dfrac{4}{5}\right)$. Horizontal equilibrium $12 - \dfrac{3}{5}\,N_{CF} = 0$ gives $N_{CF} = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$, so the diagonal is in tension. Using the vertical component of the unit vector in place of the horizontal one would give $15\,\mathrm{kN}$.
