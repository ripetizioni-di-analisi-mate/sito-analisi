The arched truss shown has the bottom chord $ABCDE$ of span $12\,\mathrm{m}$, with joints each $3\,\mathrm{m}$, and the joints of the top chord $F = (3, 3)$, $G = (6, 4)$ and $H = (9, 3)$, which stand on the $y = \dfrac{x\,(12 - x)}{9}$ parabola; the members are the chords, the end members $AF$ and $HE$, the verticals $BF$, $CG$, $DH$ and the diagonals $FC$ and $HC$. It is supported by a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $E$, and at joints $B$, $C$, $D$ three forces of $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ are hung down. Which statement is true?

- Diagonals $FC$ and $HC$ are in tension, and the force in the bottom chord is greatest at midspan
- Diagonals $FC$ and $HC$ carry zero force, and the bottom chord carries a force of $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ in every panel
- The verticals carry zero force, and the loads reach the supports through the diagonals
- Diagonals $FC$ and $HC$ are in compression, and the bottom chord carries zero force

## Explanation

By symmetry, $V_A = V_E = 30\,\mathrm{kN}$. A vertical section in the second panel cuts $FG$, $FC$, and $BC$. Member $FG$ lies along line $y = 3 + \dfrac{x - 3}{3}$, which intersects the bottom chord at $O = (-6, 0)$, the moment center for the diagonal. The forces to the left have moment $30 \cdot 6 - 20 \cdot 9 = 0$ about $O$, so $N_{FC} = 0$ and, by symmetry, $N_{HC} = 0$. For the bottom chord, take moments about the upper joints to obtain
$$
N_{BC} = \frac{30 \cdot 3}{3} = 30\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
N_{AB} = N_{CD} = N_{DE} = 30\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The top chord follows the funicular shape for the applied loads. The diagonals therefore **carry zero force** and the bottom chord carries **the same axial force** in every segment. The verticals carry tension $20\,\mathrm{kN}$, transferring the loads to the arch.
