The truss shown has six panels of $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ height, pin at $A$ and roller support at $G$, and carries equal forces down into the joints of the top chord. Which truss arrangement is this, and are its interior diagonals in tension or compression?

- Pratt truss with interior diagonals in tension
- Howe truss with interior diagonals in compression
- Warren truss with diagonals alternating between tension and compression
- Howe truss with interior diagonals in tension

## Explanation

Interior diagonals $BI$ and $CL$ rise from the bottom chord toward midspan. $FM$ and $EL$ do the same from the right. Together with the vertical members, this identifies the **Howe** layout. Under symmetric downward loading $V_s > 0$ on the left half, and a diagonal that rises from left to right has $N_d = -\dfrac{V_s}{\sin\alpha} < 0$. The interior diagonals are therefore in compression, as are their counterparts on the right by symmetry. In a Pratt truss the diagonals slope down toward midspan and are in tension. The basic Warren layout has no verticals.
