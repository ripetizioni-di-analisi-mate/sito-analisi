The truss shown has the joints $A = (0, 0)$, $M = (3, 0)$, $B = (6, 0)$ and $C = (3, 4)$ in meters and the members $AM$, $MB$, $AC$, $BC$, $MC$; it is supported by a pin at $A$ and a horizontal roller support at $B$. With the force $P = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ alone down in $C$ the member $MC$ is unloaded. What happens to the truss if member $MC$ is removed?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;M,3,0;B,6,0;C,3,4" aste="A-M;M-B;A-C;B-C;M-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="C,0,-1.4,P" quote="A,M,3 m,1.1;M,B,3 m,1.1;6 0,6 4,4 m,0.9" titolo="Truss showing member $MC$ and force $P = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$"}

- It becomes unstable, with $l = 1$ and $i = 0$, because joint $M$ can move vertically
- It remains statically determinate because the removed member carries zero force
- It becomes statically indeterminate, with $i = 1$
- It remains statically determinate, but the forces in $AC$ and $BC$ change

## Explanation

At unloaded joint $M$, collinear members $AM$ and $MB$ meet $MC$, so $MC$ carries zero force under this loading. Removing $MC$ gives $n = 4$, $a = 4$, and $v = 3$, hence $2n - (a + v) = 1$ and, by $2n = a + v$, $l - i = 1$. Joint $M$ is left connected only to collinear members $AM$ and $MB$. Infinitesimal vertical motion of $M$ does not change their lengths to first order, so $M$ can move vertically. The triangle formed by $AC$, $BC$, and tie $AMB$ remains rigid with statically determinate supports, giving $l = 1$ and $i = 0$. A member that carries zero force for one load case may still be essential under another. Only $MC$ can balance a vertical force at $M$.
