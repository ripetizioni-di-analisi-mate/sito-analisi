A supported truss has horizontal chords at $h = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ distance and carries vertical loads. A vertical section cuts the top chord, the bottom chord and a diagonal starting from the joint $R$ of the top chord. The moment of the external forces to the left of the section about $R$ equals $64\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ clockwise. What is the force in the bottom chord?

- $32\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $128\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $16\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $32\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)

## Explanation

The cut top chord and diagonal intersect at $R$, a distance $h$ from the bottom chord. Use that intersection as the moment center for the bottom-chord force. Assuming tension, the chord acts on the left-hand part horizontally to the right, producing counterclockwise moment $h\,N_{\text{lower}}$ about $R$. The external forces produce clockwise moment $M_R$. Moment equilibrium about $R$ gives $h\,N_{\text{lower}} - M_R = 0$, hence
$$
N_{\text{lower}} = \frac{M_R}{h} = \frac{64}{2}\,\mathrm{kN} = 32\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
and the bottom chord is **in tension**. Multiplying the moment by $h$ instead of dividing would give $128\,\mathrm{kN}$.
