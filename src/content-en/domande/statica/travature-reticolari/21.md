The truss shown is formed by the triangles $ABC$ and $CDE$, connected only at joint $C$, with $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (4, 0)$, $C = (2, 3)$, $D = (6, 3)$ and $E = (4, 6)$ in meters. It is supported by a pin at $A$ and a horizontal-level roller support at $B$. How is the truss classified?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,2,3;D,6,3;E,4,6" aste="A-B;A-C;B-C;C-D;C-E;D-E" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m,1.1" titolo="Triangles $ABC$ and $CDE$ connected at joint $C$"}

- Statically determinate
- Statically indeterminate, with $i = 1$
- Kinematically unstable, with $l = 1$ and $i = 0$
- Kinematically unstable, with $l = 2$ and $i = 0$

## Explanation

There are $n = 5$ joints, $a = 6$ members, and $v = 3$ restraints. The count gives $2n - (a + v) = 10 - 9 = 1$. Since this number equals $l - i$, we have $l \geq 1$. Triangle $ABC$ is rigid and is supported determinately by the pin at $A$ and roller at $B$. Triangle $CDE$ is also rigid, but joint $C$ is its only connection to the rest of the truss and acts as a pin. The triangle can therefore rotate about $C$, giving exactly one mechanism. Thus $l = 1$ and $i = 0$, and the truss is **unstable**.
