The Pratt truss shown has four $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ height panels and it is supported by a hinge $A$ and a hinge $E$. How is the truss classified?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,12,0;F,3,4;G,6,4;H,9,4" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;F-G;G-H;A-F;E-H;B-F;C-G;D-H;F-C;H-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;E,cerniera" quote="A,B,3 m,1.3;B,C,3 m,1.3;C,D,3 m,1.3;D,E,3 m,1.3;12 0,12 4,4 m,0.9" titolo="Pratt truss with pin supports at $A$ and $E$"}

- Statically determinate
- Kinematically unstable, with $l = 1$ and $i = 0$
- Unstable and statically indeterminate, with $l = i = 1$
- Statically indeterminate, with $i = 1$

## Explanation

There are $n = 8$ joints, $a = 13$ members, and $v = 2 + 2 = 4$ external scalar restraints, so $2n - (a + v) = 16 - 17 = -1$ and the count $2n = a + v$ gives $l - i = -1$. Starting from triangle $ABF$, each new joint connects through two noncollinear members. This makes the simple truss rigid. Releasing the horizontal restraint at $E$ leaves a pin at $A$ and a roller at $E$. These supply three independent restraints and the released truss is statically determinate, so $l = 0$. It follows that $i = 1$. The original truss is **statically indeterminate** to degree $1$, with one external redundant.
