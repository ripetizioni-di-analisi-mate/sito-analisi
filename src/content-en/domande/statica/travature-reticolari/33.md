At joint $K$ of a truss, two aligned horizontal members, $KA$ and $KB$, and the vertical member $KC$, which rises from $K$ towards $C$, meet. No load acts at $K$, but the truss rests there on a roller with a horizontal surface, which exerts an upward reaction of $5\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the axial force in member $KC$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;K,2,0;B,4,0;C,2,2" aste="A-K;K-B;K-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="K,carrello" titolo="Joint $K$ on a roller, with two aligned members and a vertical member"}

- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $0$
- It cannot be found without the forces in $KA$ and $KB$
- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)

## Explanation

The roller reaction is an external force applied to the joint, so it enters the equilibrium of $K$ like a load. Members $KA$ and $KB$ are horizontal, so they do not appear in vertical force equilibrium of the joint. A member in tension pulls the joint towards its other end, so the force of $KC$ on the joint is $N_{KC}$ upward, and vertical force equilibrium gives
$$
N_{KC} + 5 = 0 .
$$
Hence $N_{KC} = -5\,\mathrm{kN}$, that is, member $KC$ is in **compression**. The value $0$ is obtained by applying the zero-force member rules without accounting for the roller reaction, which acts along the direction of $KC$.
