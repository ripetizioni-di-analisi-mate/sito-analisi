The truss shown is a Pratt of four $3\,\mathrm{m}$ panels and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ height, with a pin at $A$ and roller support at $E$, in which the second panel has both diagonals $FC$ and $BG$. Which member can be removed to obtain a statically determinate truss?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,12,0;F,3,4;G,6,4;H,9,4" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;F-G;G-H;A-F;E-H;B-F;C-G;D-H;F-C;H-C;B-G" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;E,carrello" quote="A,B,3 m,1.3;B,C,3 m,1.3;C,D,3 m,1.3;D,E,3 m,1.3;12 0,12 4,4 m,0.9" titolo="Pratt truss with both diagonals $FC$ and $BG$ in the second panel"}

- End member $AF$
- Diagonal $BG$
- Vertical $DH$
- Bottom chord $DE$

## Explanation

The counts $n = 8$, $a = 14$, and $v = 3$ give $2n - (a + v) = -1$ and $l - i = -1$, so at least one member is redundant. Panel $BCGF$ has two diagonals and six members joining four joints. Only five are needed to make it rigid, locating the redundancy in that panel. Removing diagonal $BG$ gives a simple Pratt truss with three independent external restraints, so it is **statically determinate**. Removing vertical $DH$ instead leaves joint $D$ connected only by collinear members $CD$ and $DE$, allowing a vertical mechanism while the second panel retains its redundancy. The truss is then unstable and statically indeterminate with $l = i = 1$. Removing $AF$ likewise leaves the rigid part connected to pin $A$ only by $AB$. Removing $DE$ lets $E$ slide on its roller and the truss rotate about $A$.
