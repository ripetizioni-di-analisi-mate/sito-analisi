The truss shown has four $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ height panels, the $AF$ and $EL$ end verticals and the $AG$, $BH$, $HD$, $IE$ diagonals; it is supported by a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $E$. At joint $C$ hangs the downward force $P = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the force in vertical $CH$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,12,0;F,0,4;G,3,4;H,6,4;I,9,4;L,12,4" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;F-G;G-H;H-I;I-L;A-F;B-G;C-H;D-I;E-L;A-G;B-H;H-D;I-E" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;E,carrello" forze="6 -1.6,0,-1.6," etichette="6.3,-1.3,P" quote="A,B,3 m,2.3;B,C,3 m,2.3;C,D,3 m,2.3;D,E,3 m,2.3;12 0,12 4,4 m,0.9" titolo="Truss with end verticals and force $P = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$ suspended at $C$"}

- $40\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $40\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $0$

## Explanation

Two collinear horizontal chord members $BC$ and $CD$ meet the vertical member $CH$ at joint $C$, where the load $P$ is suspended. The chord forces have no vertical component, so vertical equilibrium gives
$$
N_{CH} - 40 = 0 ,
$$
Thus $N_{CH} = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$. The vertical member is **in tension** and transfers the load to joint $H$. The value $0$ comes from applying the zero-force member rule at a joint where an external load acts perpendicular to the collinear members. The rule does not apply under that load.
