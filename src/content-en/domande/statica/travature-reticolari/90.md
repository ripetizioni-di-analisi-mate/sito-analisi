A three-member triangular truss has a base $AB$ of length $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and an apex $C$ at height $3\,\mathrm{m}$ above the midpoint of the base. It is pinned to the ground at both $A$ and $B$. A downward load $P = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at the apex. What can be concluded about the axial force in base member $AB$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,8,0;C,4,3" aste="A-B;A-C;B-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="C,0,-1.4,P" quote="A,B,8 m,1.1;8 0,8 3,3 m,0.9" titolo="Triangular truss with two external pin supports"}

- The force in $AB$ cannot be determined from equilibrium alone
- Member $AB$ carries zero force because the two pins resist the horizontal forces
- Member $AB$ is in tension with $16\,\mathrm{kN}$, as in the pin-and-roller configuration
- Member $AB$ is in compression with $16\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The counts $n = 3$, $a = 3$, and $v = 4$ give $2n - (a + v) = -1$. The triangle is rigid, so $l = 0$ and the truss is statically indeterminate with $i = 1$. The six joint equations contain seven unknowns, three member forces and four reaction components. Vertical equilibrium gives $V_A = V_B = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $N_{AC} = N_{BC} = -20\,\mathrm{kN}$, while horizontal equilibrium at joint $A$ gives
$$
N_{AB} - 16 + H_A = 0 ,
$$
where $H_A$ is unknown. Equilibrium **does not determine** how the strut thrust is shared between the tie and the support pins. Any value of $H_A$ with $H_B = -H_A$ satisfies all the equations. Determining the actual force requires member deformations and displacement compatibility, as studied in mechanics of materials.
