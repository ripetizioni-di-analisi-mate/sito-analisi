The truss shown has the joints $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (3, 0)$, $C = (6, 0)$, $D = (0, 4)$, $E = (3, 4)$ and $F = (6, 4)$ in meters, the chords $AB$, $BC$, $DE$, $EF$, the verticals $AD$, $BE$, $CF$ and the diagonals $DB$, $FB$; it is supported by a pin at $A$ and a horizontal roller support at $C$. At joint $B$ hangs the downward force $P = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the force in vertical $BE$?

- $24\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)

## Explanation

At unloaded joint $E$, vertical member $BE$ meets the collinear chord members $DE$ and $EF$. Only $BE$ enters vertical equilibrium at joint $E$, so the zero-force member rule gives $N_{BE} = 0$. Diagonals $DB$ and $FB$ carry the load hung at $B$, each in tension $15\,\mathrm{kN}$. The tensile value $24\,\mathrm{kN}$ would result from balancing joint $B$ while omitting its two diagonals.
