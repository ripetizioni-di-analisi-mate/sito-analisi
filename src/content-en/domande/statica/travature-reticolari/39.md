The roof truss shown has a span of $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $3\,\mathrm{m}$ height. The tie $AC$ is divided in half by the joint $B$, the $AE$ and $EC$ struts are divided in half by the joints $D$ and $F$, the king post is $BE$ and the diagonal struts are $BD$ and $BF$; the roof truss is supported with a pin at $A$ and a horizontal roller support at $C$. The $s$ vertical section between $D$ and $B$ cuts the $DE$ strut, the diagonal $BD$ strut diagonal strut and the tie $AB$. What is the Ritter point for strut $BD$?

- Joint $A$
- Joint $B$
- Joint $D$
- The point at infinity common to horizontal lines

## Explanation

To isolate a cut-member force, use the intersection of the lines of action of the other two cut members as the moment center. For brace $BD$, those members are strut $DE$ and tie $AB$. Segment $DE$ is part of strut $AE$, so its line passes through $A$. The tie also passes through $A$. The moment center for $BD$ is therefore **joint $A$**. Taking moments about $A$ leaves only the unknown force $N_{BD}$. Joint $B$ is the moment center for strut $DE$ and joint $D$ is the center for tie $AB$.
