A statically determinate truss is reconstructed with all dimensions doubled: the coordinates of each joint are multiplied by $2$, the members and constraints are the same and in the corresponding joints the same loads act. How do the member forces change?

- They stay the same
- They double
- They are halved
- They quadruple

## Explanation

The joint equilibrium equations multiply each axial force by its member’s direction cosines, $\dfrac{x_{B_j} - x_A}{L_{AB_j}}$ and $\dfrac{y_{B_j} - y_A}{L_{AB_j}}$. Doubling all coordinates doubles both the member lengths and their projections, leaving the direction cosines unchanged. The support reactions also remain the same because every moment arm doubles and the common factor $2$ cancels. The joint equations then have the same coefficients and applied loads, so their member forces are unchanged. For these loads the forces depend on the truss geometry through member directions, irrespective of overall scale.
