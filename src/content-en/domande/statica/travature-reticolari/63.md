The tie beam $AC$ of the roof truss shown has length $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and a joint $B$ at its midpoint. Rafters $AE$ and $EC$ rise to the ridge $E = (4, 3)$ and have midpoint joints $D$ and $F$. The king post is $BE$, and the diagonal struts are $BD$ and $BF$. The truss has a pin at $A$ and a roller at $C$ and carries a single downward load $P = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ at the ridge. Which members carry zero force?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,8,0;D,2,1.5;E,4,3;F,6,1.5" aste="A-B;B-C;A-D;D-E;E-F;F-C;B-E;B-D;B-F" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello" forze="E,0,-1.3,P" quote="A,B,4 m,1.1;B,C,4 m,1.1;8 0,8 3,3 m,0.9" titolo="Roof truss with a king post and struts, carrying only force $P = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ at the ridge"}

- Only struts $BD$ and $BF$
- Only king post $BE$
- No member
- Struts $BD$ and $BF$ and king post $BE$

## Explanation

At unloaded joint $D$, collinear strut segments $AD$ and $DE$ meet brace $BD$. By the zero-force member rule, brace $BD$ **carries zero force**. At joint $F$ the same rule eliminates brace $BF$. At unloaded joint $B$ this leaves the collinear tie segments $AB$ and $BC$ and king post $BE$, so the king post also carries zero force. The inclined struts transfer the load to the supports in compression $N_{AD} = N_{DE} = -20\,\mathrm{kN}$, and the tie carries tension $16\,\mathrm{kN}$. The king post can be identified as a zero-force member only after the two braces have been eliminated.
