Two four-panel trusses of $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ height, one with the Pratt scheme and the other with the Howe scheme, both have the pin at $A$, the roller support at $E$ and three forces of $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ down at joints $F$, $G$, $H$ of the top chord. Which statement is true?

- The reactions are the same, and the interior diagonals carry forces of opposite signs in the two trusses
- The reactions differ because the member arrangements differ
- The interior diagonals are in tension in both trusses
- The top chord is in tension in the Howe truss and in compression in the Pratt truss

## Explanation

Only the applied loads and support reactions enter equilibrium of the whole truss. Thus both layouts have the same reactions, $V_A = V_E = 30\,\mathrm{kN}$, and both have $V_s = 30 - 20 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ in the second panel. In the Pratt truss, diagonal $FC$ slopes down to the right and has $N_{FC} = \dfrac{10}{\frac{4}{5}} = 12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. In the Howe truss, diagonal $BG$ slopes up to the right and has $N_{BG} = -12.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. The diagonal forces therefore **have opposite signs**. The top chord is in compression in both trusses because the bending moment from the applied loads is positive throughout the span.
