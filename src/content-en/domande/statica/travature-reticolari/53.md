The Pratt truss shown has four $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ height panels and is supported on three roller supports on a horizontal plane, at joints $A$, $C$ and $E$. For which joint loads is the truss in equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,9,0;E,12,0;F,3,4;G,6,4;H,9,4" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;F-G;G-H;A-F;E-H;B-F;C-G;D-H;F-C;H-C" reticolare=1 vincoli="A,carrello;C,carrello;E,carrello" quote="A,B,3 m,1.3;B,C,3 m,1.3;C,D,3 m,1.3;D,E,3 m,1.3;12 0,12 4,4 m,0.9" titolo="Pratt truss on three roller supports"}

- For any load
- For no load
- Only for loads with zero horizontal resultant
- Only for vertical loads applied at joint $C$

## Explanation

The counts $2n = 16$ and $a + v = 13 + 3 = 16$ satisfy $2n = a + v$. However, all three reactions are vertical. Only applied loads enter horizontal equilibrium of the truss, so their horizontal resultant must be zero. Even for such loads, vertical force and moment equilibrium provide only two equations for three reactions. The truss is **unstable and statically indeterminate**, with $l = i = 1$. It can carry precisely those loads whose horizontal resultant is zero, including vertical loads at any joint.
