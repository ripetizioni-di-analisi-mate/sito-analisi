The roof truss shown has span $8\,\mathrm{m}$, height $3\,\mathrm{m}$, a horizontal tie $ABCDE$ with joints spaced at $2\,\mathrm{m}$, rafters of slope $\dfrac{3}{4}$, king post $CG$, intermediate verticals $BF$ and $DH$, and diagonal struts $FC$ and $HC$. It has a pin at $A$ and a roller at $E$. Downward forces of $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ act at joints $F$, $G$, and $H$. What is the axial force in tie segment $BC$?

- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ (compression)
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)
- $22.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ (tension)

## Explanation

By symmetry, $V_A = V_E = 22.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. Section $s$ cuts members $FG$, $FC$, and $BC$. The lines of action of $FG$ and $FC$ intersect at $F$, at elevation $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. Taking moments about that point isolates the force in tie $BC$. For the left-hand free body, the load at $F$ has zero moment about $F$, $V_A$ has moment arm $2\,\mathrm{m}$, and the assumed tensile force in $BC$ acts horizontally to the right with moment arm $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus
$$
1.5\,N_{BC} - 22.5 \cdot 2 = 0 .
$$
This gives $N_{BC} = 30\,\mathrm{kN}$, so the tie is **in tension**. The value $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ would result from using the full roof-truss height $3\,\mathrm{m}$ instead of the elevation of the moment center.
