In the Pratt truss bridge shown, $18\,\mathrm{m}$ long and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ high, each of the top five joints $H$, $I$, $L$, $M$, $N$ receives $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward; the bridge rests on a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $G$. The internal diagonals are $HC$, $ID$, $MD$, $NE$. Which of them carry the greatest force?

- Diagonals $ID$ and $MD$, near midspan
- All the interior diagonals carry the same force
- None, because the interior diagonals carry zero force
- Diagonals $HC$ and $NE$, near the supports

## Explanation

By symmetry, $V_A = V_G = 25\,\mathrm{kN}$. Cut the diagonal and take vertical equilibrium of the part to the left. This gives $N_d = \dfrac{V_s}{\sin\alpha}$, with $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$ for every diagonal. In the second panel $V_s = 25 - 10 = 15\,\mathrm{kN}$ and in the third $V_s = 25 - 10 - 10 = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$, so
$$
N_{HC} = \frac{15}{\frac{4}{5}} = 18.75\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
N_{ID} = \frac{5}{\frac{4}{5}} = 6.25\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
By symmetry, $N_{NE} = N_{HC}$ and $N_{MD} = N_{ID}$. The largest diagonal forces occur in **$HC$ and $NE$**, near the supports, where the vertical forces to the left of the cut have the largest resultant. The largest chord forces occur at midspan.
