The inclined beam of a flight of stairs has a hinge in the lower extreme $A$ and a roller support in the upper extreme $B$, which moves along a horizontal plane and then gives a vertical reaction. The horizontal projection of the beam is $4\,\mathrm{m}$, the beam is $5\,\mathrm{m}$ long, and the vertical load is $q = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ for each horizontal projection meter. What is the maximum bending moment?

- $12.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $16\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

The load and the two reactions are vertical, so the reactions depend only on horizontal distances and, by symmetry, they equal $V_A = V_B = \dfrac{4 \cdot 4}{2} = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$. The moment in a section also depends only on horizontal distances, which are the arms of vertical forces. Hence, with $x$ measured horizontally by $A$, it equals $M(x) = 8x - 2x^2$, as in a horizontal beam of span $4\,\mathrm{m}$. The maximum is found in $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, where $T = 0$, and equals $\dfrac{q \cdot 4^2}{8} = 8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The value $12.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ is obtained using the length of the beam, $5\,\mathrm{m}$, instead of the horizontal projection.
