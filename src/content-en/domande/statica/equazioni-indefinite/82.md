A beam has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$ and carries only concentrated vertical forces and distributed, cross loads, without couples. What property does the shear diagram always have between $A$ and $B$?

- It vanishes at midspan
- It equals $T(A^+) = -T(B^-)$
- Its area equals the resultant load
- Its signed area is zero

## Explanation

For the integral form $M(B) - M(A) = \displaystyle\int_A^B T\,dx$, and the relationship also applies through concentrated forces, because under them the moment is continuous. supports without applied couples give $M(A) = 0$ and $M(B) = 0$, so the integral of the shear between $A$ and $B$ is zero. The undoing of the shear at midspan and the equality $T(A^+) = -T(B^-)$ are only valid for loads symmetrical with respect at midspan. The resultant of the loads is the $T(A^+) - T(B^-)$ difference between the reactions, not the area of the diagram.
