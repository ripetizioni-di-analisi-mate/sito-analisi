The beam shown has a pin at $A$ and $B$, at $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, a roller support that moves along an inclined plane of $45^\circ$: its reaction is perpendicular to the plane and is directed upward and to the left. The beam carries the uniform load $q = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. What is the axial force in the beam?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello,315" carichi="A,B,0.5,0.5,q" quote="A,B,4 m,1" titolo="Beam with a roller on an inclined surface at $B$ and uniform load $q = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$"}

- $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ in tension
- $0\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kN}$, in compression
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$, in compression

## Explanation

Taking moments about $A$ gives the vertical component of the roller reaction, $V_B = \dfrac{12 \cdot 2}{4} = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$. The reaction makes an angle $45^\circ$ with the vertical, so its horizontal component has the same magnitude and points left, giving $H_B = -6\,\mathrm{kN}$. At end $B$, the jump condition gives $N(4^-) = H_B = -6\,\mathrm{kN}$ because the internal forces outside the beam are zero. There is no distributed axial load, so $\dfrac{dN}{dx} = 0$ and the axial force is $-6\,\mathrm{kN}$ throughout the beam, corresponding to $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ in compression. The value $0$ applies to a roller on a horizontal surface. The value $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ uses the resultant of the applied load in place of reaction $V_B$.
