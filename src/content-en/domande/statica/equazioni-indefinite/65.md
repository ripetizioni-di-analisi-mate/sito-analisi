The cantilever shown is fixed at $A$, has length $4\,\mathrm{m}$, and carries a uniform load $q = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. At end $B$, a tie exerts an upward force $F = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the maximum bending moment?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" carichi="A,B,0.5,0.5,q" forze="B,0,1.1,F" quote="A,B,4 m,0.7" titolo="Cantilever with uniform load $q = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ and tie-rod force $F = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$"}

- $24\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $0\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

Let $s$ be the distance from end $B$ to the section. The portion of the cantilever between the section and $B$ carries the upward tie force and the downward load resultant $3s$. Moment equilibrium gives $M = 6s - \dfrac{3s^2}{2}$. The bending moment is stationary where the shear vanishes. Solving $6 - 3s = 0$ gives $s = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, and $q > 0$ makes this point a maximum. Thus $M_{\max} = 12 - 6 = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with the bottom fibers in tension. At the fixed support, $s = 4\,\mathrm{m}$, the moment is $24 - 24 = 0$. The answer $24\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ uses $\dfrac{qL^2}{2}$ and overlooks the force in the tie.
