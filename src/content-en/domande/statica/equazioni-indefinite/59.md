A beam has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$ and carries a uniform load. You add a vertical $F$ force, downward, applied exactly in the section $B$, above the roller support. What changes?

- The shear decreases by $F$ along the entire beam
- The midspan moment increases by $\dfrac{FL}{4}$
- Both $V_A$ and $V_B$ increase by $\dfrac{F}{2}$
- The increase is $F$ in reaction $V_B$, while the shear and moment along the beam are unchanged

## Explanation

The force has zero moment arm about $B$, so moment equilibrium about $B$ gives the same $V_A$ as before, and the vertical force equilibrium gives $V_B$ increased by $F$. In each inner section the left beam part carries the same loads and the same $V_A$ reaction, so the shear and the moment do not change. At section $B$ the downward force $F$ and the reaction $V_B + F$ up act, so the shear jump at $B$ remains $V_B$. The force passes directly to the constraint.
