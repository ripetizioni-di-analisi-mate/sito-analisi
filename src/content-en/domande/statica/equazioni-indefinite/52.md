To integrate the differential equilibrium equations from the right end of a beam of span $L$, the coordinate $s = L - x$ is used, measured from right to left, and the usual sign convention for $T$ and $M$ is maintained. Which relations hold on a segment with no concentrated loads or couples and no distributed couples?

- $\dfrac{dT}{ds} = -q$ and $\dfrac{dM}{ds} = T$
- $\dfrac{dT}{ds} = q$ and $\dfrac{dM}{ds} = -T$
- $\dfrac{dT}{ds} = -q$ and $\dfrac{dM}{ds} = -T$
- $\dfrac{dT}{ds} = q$ and $\dfrac{dM}{ds} = T$

## Explanation

Because $x = L - s$, you have $\dfrac{dx}{ds} = -1$. For the derivative of the compound function $\dfrac{dT}{ds} = \dfrac{dT}{dx} \cdot \dfrac{dx}{ds} = -\dfrac{dT}{dx}$, then $\dfrac{dT}{ds} = q$. Similarly $\dfrac{dM}{ds} = -\dfrac{dM}{dx} = -T$. Deriving again yields $\dfrac{d^2M}{ds^2} = -\dfrac{dT}{ds} = -q$, so the equation of the second order for the moment remains the same. For example, in a left-mounted cantilever with uniform load $q$ and the free right end, $T(0) = 0$ and $\dfrac{dT}{ds} = q$ give $T = q\,s$, positive.
