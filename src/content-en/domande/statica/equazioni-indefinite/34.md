An $4\,\mathrm{m}$-high vertical column is fixed at base $A$ and is free at top $B$. Its self-weight is a uniform load of $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ for each meter in height, directed downward along the axis, and in $B$ the horizontal force $H = 3\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts. At the base section $A$, what are the magnitudes of the axial force and bending moment?

- $N = -20\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $|M| = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $N = 0$ and $|M| = 12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $N = -20\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $|M| = 12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $N = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $|M| = 12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

With $x$ measured along the upward axis by $A$, the self-weight is an $p = -5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ axial load, because it is directed in the direction of the decreasing $x$. The equation $\dfrac{dN}{dx} = -p = 5$, with the condition $N(4) = 0$ in the top unloaded in the axial direction, gives $N(x) = 5\,(x - 4)$. So $N(0) = -20\,\mathrm{kN}$, i.e. a compressive force. The force $H$ is perpendicular to the axis, so it gives a constant shear of magnitude $3\,\mathrm{kN}$ and a moment that grows linearly from zero in $B$ up to $|M| = 3 \cdot 4 = 12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ in $A$. The self-weight acts along the axis and does not contribute at the moment. The value $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ is obtained by treating $H$ as a distributed load on the height.
