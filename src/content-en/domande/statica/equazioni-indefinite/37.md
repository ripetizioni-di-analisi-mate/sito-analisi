A simply supported beam of span $L = 4\,\mathrm{m}$ carries the parabolic load $q(x) = \dfrac{4q_0\,x\,(L - x)}{L^2}$, null in the supports and equal to $q_0 = 6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at midspan. What is the maximum bending moment?

- $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

By integrating $\dfrac{d^2M}{dx^2} = -q$ twice with $M(0) = 0$ and $M(L) = 0$, a fourth degree moment is obtained, symmetrical about the midspan. By symmetry the shear vanishes at midspan, where the moment is maximum and equals
$$
M_{\max} = \frac{5\,q_0 L^2}{48} = \frac{5 \cdot 6 \cdot 16}{48} = 10\,\mathrm{kN\,m} .
$$
The value $12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ is $\dfrac{q_0 L^2}{8}$ and applies to the uniform $q_0$ load over all span. The value $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ is the maximum moment of a uniform load with the same resultant $\dfrac{2}{3}\,q_0 L = 16\,\mathrm{kN}$, which is distributed differently.
