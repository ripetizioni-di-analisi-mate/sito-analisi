A reinforced concrete beam has a rectangular cross section of width $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $60\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a unit weight of $25\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$. It supports a slab strip of width $3\,\mathrm{m}$ carrying load $4\,\mathrm{kN/m^2}$. What is the uniform load $q$ on the beam, including its self-weight?

- $16.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $8.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $28.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

Multiply the material's weight per unit volume by the cross-sectional area to obtain the beam's self-weight per unit length, $25 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 0.60 = 4.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Each meter of beam carries $3\,\mathrm{m^2}$ of slab, so the slab contributes $4 \cdot 3 = 12\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The total line load is therefore $q = 4.5 + 12 = 16.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. The answer $12\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ omits the beam's self-weight. The answer $8.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ adds the slab load without multiplying by the width of slab supported by the beam.
