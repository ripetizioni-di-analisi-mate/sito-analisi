A simply supported beam of span $L$, with a pin at $A$ ($x = 0$) and roller support at $B$, carries only the parabolic load $q(x) = \dfrac{q_0\,x^2}{L^2}$, zero in $A$ and equal to $q_0$ in $B$. What are the degrees of the polynomials $T(x)$ and $M(x)$?

- Quadratic $T$ and cubic $M$
- Cubic $T$ and quartic $M$
- Linear $T$ and quadratic $M$
- Both $T$ and $M$ are quadratic

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{dT}{dx} = -q$ and the load is second grade, shear is achieved by integrating once and is third grade. Since $\dfrac{dM}{dx} = T$, the moment is obtained by integrating again and is fourth degree. With the conditions $M(0) = 0$ and $M(L) = 0$ is in fact
$$
M(x) = \frac{q_0\,x\,(L^3 - x^3)}{12\,L^2} ,
$$
where the maximum grade term is $-\dfrac{q_0\,x^4}{12\,L^2}$. In general, each integration increases the grade by one, so an $n$ grade load gives an $n + 1$ grade shear and an $n + 2$ grade moment. The second and third grades are those of the triangular load, which is first grade.
