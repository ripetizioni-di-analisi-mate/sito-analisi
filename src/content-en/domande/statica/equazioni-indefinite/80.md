A beam has a pin at $A$ ($x = 0$) and a roller support at $B$ ($x = 6\,\mathrm{m}$) and does not carry distributed loads. His moment diagram is the one shown, with a corner in $C$ ($x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$) and a jump at $D$ ($x = 4\,\mathrm{m}$). What loads act at $C$ and $D$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;D,4,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,2,0,5*s;2,0,4,0,10-s;4,0,6,0,2-s" verso=-1 titolo="Bending moment diagram $M$ ($\mathrm{kN\,m}$)"}

- A downward force at $C$ and a counterclockwise couple at $D$
- A downward force at $C$ and a clockwise couple at $D$
- A counterclockwise couple at $C$ and a downward force at $D$
- A downward force at $C$ and an upward force at $D$

## Explanation

In $C$ the moment is continuous and its slope goes from $\dfrac{10}{2} = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$ to $\dfrac{8 - 10}{2} = -1\,\mathrm{kN}$, so the shear jumps by $-6\,\mathrm{kN}$, and in $C$ it acts a force of $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ down. In $D$ the moment jumps from $8$ to $2\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ and the slope remains $-1\,\mathrm{kN}$, so in $D$ a couple and no force acts. The jump equals $M(D^+) - M(D^-) = -6\,\mathrm{kN\,m} = -\mathcal{M}$, then $\mathcal{M} = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, positive, i.e. counterclockwise. A clockwise couple would produce a jump to higher values.
