A simply supported beam carries a uniform $q$ load over all span and a concentrated $F$ force down into the $C$ inner section. Which statement about the shear diagram near $C$ is true?

- It is continuous at $C$ and has a corner
- It jumps by $-F$ at $C$ and varies linearly on both sides of $C$, with the same slope $-q$
- It jumps by $-F$ at $C$ and its slope changes from $-q$ to $-q - F$
- It jumps by $-F$ at $C$ and is constant to the right of $C$

## Explanation

For the jump rule, under concentrated force the shear goes from $T(C^-)$ to $T(C^+) = T(C^-) - F$. To the left and right of $C$ acts the same load $q$, then in both sections $\dfrac{dT}{dx} = -q$. Therefore the shear diagram is formed by two parallel lines, offset by $F$ in $C$. The corner under the force is a property of the moment diagram, the slope of which is the shear.
