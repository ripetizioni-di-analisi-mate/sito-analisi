A simply supported beam carries only vertical concentrated forces, all directed downwards, and no distributed load or couple. Which statement about the maximum bending moment is true for every arrangement of the forces?

- It is reached under one of the forces
- It is always reached at midspan
- It is reached under the force of largest magnitude
- It is reached under the force nearest to midspan

## Explanation

Between two forces the distributed load is zero, so $\dfrac{dT}{dx} = 0$ and the shear force is constant; since $\dfrac{dM}{dx} = T$, the moment is linear in each segment. The moment diagram is therefore a polygonal line, zero at the supports, with its vertices at the sections of the forces. A polygonal line reaches its maximum at a vertex or at an end, and at the ends the moment is zero, so the maximum is reached **under one of the forces**, the one where the shear passes from a positive value to a negative or zero value. For example, in a $6\,\mathrm{m}$ beam with $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $3.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ one has $V_A = \dfrac{85}{6}\,\mathrm{kN}$, and the shear becomes negative already under the first force. The maximum is therefore under the $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ force, which is not the one nearest to midspan. With $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ at midspan, instead, the shear remains positive after the first force, and the maximum is under the smaller force.
