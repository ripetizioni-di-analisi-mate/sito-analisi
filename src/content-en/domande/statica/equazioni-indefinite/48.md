The equilibrium is written at the rotation of a beam element of length $dx$, subject to uniform load $q$, around the centroid of the right face. The load $q\,dx$ has a moment arm of $\dfrac{dx}{2}$ about this reference point. Why is its moment absent from equation $\dfrac{dM}{dx} = T$?

- Its moment is $\dfrac{q\,dx^2}{2}$, which approaches zero when divided by $dx$
- Its line of action passes through the moment center
- It is equal and opposite to the moment of the shear force $T + dT$
- The load is perpendicular to the beam axis

## Explanation

The moment of loading about the reference point is $q\,dx \cdot \dfrac{dx}{2} = \dfrac{q\,dx^2}{2}$, i.e. an infinitesimal of the second order. The moment equation contains the $dM$ variation, the moment $T\,dx$ of the left face shear and this term. Divide by $dx$, the term of the load becomes $\dfrac{q\,dx}{2}$, which tends to zero by $dx \to 0$. Therefore $\dfrac{dM}{dx} = T$ remains at the limit. The $T + dT$ shear of the right face passes through the reference point and has zero moment.
