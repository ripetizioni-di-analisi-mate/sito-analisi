A simply supported beam in $A$ and $B$, with no couples in the supports, carries only a continuous distributed load $q(x) \geq 0$, of any shape, with no concentrated forces or couples. Which statement is true for every load of this type?

- The bending moment is greatest at midspan
- The shear is positive at every section
- The bending moment is nonnegative at every section
- The shear vanishes at midspan

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{d^2M}{dx^2} = -q \leq 0$, the $M$ function is concave over the entire beam. supports without applied couples give $M(A) = 0$ and $M(B) = 0$. A concave function stands above the string that connects the values to the extremes, and here the string is the zero segment, therefore $M \geq 0$ in each section and the lower fibers are always in tension. The shear instead changes sign, because it equals $V_A > 0$ in $A$ and $-V_B < 0$ in $B$. With the triangular load $q(x) = \dfrac{q_0\,x}{L}$ the shear vanishes in $x = \dfrac{L}{\sqrt{3}}$, so the maximum of the moment is out of the midspan.
