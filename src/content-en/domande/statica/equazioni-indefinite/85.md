For a simply supported beam in $A$ ($x = 0$) and $B$ ($x = 4\,\mathrm{m}$), with the uniform load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, one student got $T(x) = 10 - 2.5x$ ($\mathrm{kN}$) and $M(x) = 10x - 2.5x^2$ ($\mathrm{kN\,m}$). Which statement is true?

- Both expressions are correct because $M(0) = 0$ and $M(4) = 0$
- The shear expression is incorrect because its slope is $-2.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ instead of $-q = -5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- The moment expression is incorrect because it does not vanish at $B$
- The moment expression is incorrect because its second derivative differs from $-q$

## Explanation

The second derivative of the moment is $\dfrac{d^2M}{dx^2} = -5 = -q$, and the moment satisfies $M(0) = 0$ and $M(4) = 40 - 40 = 0$, so the expression of the moment is correct. Its derivative is $\dfrac{dM}{dx} = 10 - 5x$, which is different from the written shear. In fact, the slope of the written shear is $-2.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, while the equation $\dfrac{dT}{dx} = -q$ requires $-5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Therefore the expression of the shear is wrong, and the correct one is $T(x) = 10 - 5x$. The conditions at the supports only check the moment and are not enough to confirm the shear.
