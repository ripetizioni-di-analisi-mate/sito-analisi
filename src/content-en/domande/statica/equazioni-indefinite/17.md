In a segment of beam with no concentrated forces or couples the uniform load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts downward. In the section at coordinate $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ the shear equals $8\,\mathrm{kN}$. What is the shear force at coordinate $x = 3\,\mathrm{m}$ in the same segment?

- $-2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $18\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $3\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $13\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

By the integral form of differential equilibrium equations the variation of the shear between two sections is the opposite of the resultant of the load between them. Between $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x = 3\,\mathrm{m}$ the resultant is $5 \cdot 2 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$, down. Therefore $T(3) = 8 - 10 = -2\,\mathrm{kN}$. The value $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ follows from reversing the sign in equation $\dfrac{dT}{dx} = -q$, the value $3\,\mathrm{kN}$ with only one meter of load.
