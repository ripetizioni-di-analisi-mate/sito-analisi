A simply supported beam of span $L$ carries two equal forces $F$, down, to $\dfrac{L}{3}$ and to $\dfrac{2L}{3}$ from the $A$ mount, and no other load. Which statement about the bending moment is true?

- It is constant between the two forces, where the shear is zero
- It reaches its maximum only at midspan, where the diagram has a horizontal tangent
- It is greatest under the point loads and smaller at midspan
- It increases linearly between the two forces

## Explanation

By $V_A = V_B = F$ symmetry. Between $A$ and the first force the shear equals $F$, and under the force jumps by $-F$, so between the two forces $T = 0$. Since $\dfrac{dM}{dx} = T$, in the middle segment the moment is constant and equals $F \cdot \dfrac{L}{3} = \dfrac{FL}{3}$. The maximum is then reached throughout the segment between the two forces, with the same value under the forces and at midspan.
