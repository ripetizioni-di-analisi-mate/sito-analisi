A lamp of $W = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ weight is hung at point $K$ with two cables fixed to the ceiling, as in the figure. The left cable forms with the horizontal one angle of $30^\circ$ and the right one angle of $60^\circ$. What is the tension in the right-hand cable?

::struttura{nodi="K,0,0;A,-3.46,2;B,1.15,2" catene="K-A;K-B" linee="-4.2,2 2,2" forze="0 -1.2,0,-1.2,W" etichette="-2.55,1.78,30°;0.78,1.72,60°" titolo="Lamp suspended from two inclined cables"}

- $8.66\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5.77\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

On point $K$ the weight and the $T_1$ and $T_2$ pitches of the left and right cables act, three forces concurrent at $K$; their moment about $K$ is zero, so the system is balanced if the resultant is zero:
$$
\begin{cases}
-T_1 \cos 30^\circ + T_2 \cos 60^\circ = 0 \\[4pt]
T_1 \sin 30^\circ + T_2 \sin 60^\circ - 10 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $T_1 = \dfrac{T_2}{\sqrt{3}}$, and substituting in the second gives $\dfrac{T_2}{2\sqrt{3}} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}\,T_2}{2} = 10$, i.e. $\dfrac{2\,T_2}{\sqrt{3}} = 10$. Therefore $T_2 = 5\sqrt{3} = 8.66\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $T_1 = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$. The steepest cable carries the greatest tension; the value $5.77\,\mathrm{kN}$ is that of two symmetric cables at $60^\circ$.
