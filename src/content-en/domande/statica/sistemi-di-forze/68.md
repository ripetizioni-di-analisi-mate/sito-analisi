An $W = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ weight block rests on a smooth inclined plane, which forms an angle of $30^\circ$ with the horizontal, and is held firm by a horizontal $H$ force that pushes it towards the plane. The smooth plane exerts a force perpendicular to the plane on the block, and the three forces are considered to be applied at the same point. What is $H$?

- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $5.77\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $8.66\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $17.32\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The three forces are concurrent, so their moment about the common point is zero, and the system is balanced if the resultant is zero. You project the resultant onto the direction of the inclined plane, because on this direction the force of the plane has component nothing. The weight has the component $W\sin 30^\circ$ along the plane, down the slope, and the horizontal force has the component $H\cos 30^\circ$, up the slope. Therefore
$$
H\cos 30^\circ = W\sin 30^\circ
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
H = W\tan 30^\circ = 5.77\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The value $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ is the weight component along the plane, and $17.32\,\mathrm{kN}$ is obtained by dividing the weight by $\tan 30^\circ$ instead of multiplying it.
