The $AB$ ladder in the figure, $5\,\mathrm{m}$ long and $W = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ weight applied at the midpoint, rests at $A$ on the floor and at $B$ on a smooth vertical wall; $B$ is located at $3\,\mathrm{m}$ horizontally and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ vertically from $A$. The smooth wall exerts a horizontal force at $B$. What is this force if the ladder is in equilibrium?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,4" aste="A-B" linee="-0.8,0 3,0;3,0 3,5" forze="1.5 2,0,-1.2,W" quote="A,3 0,3 m,0.9;3 0,B,4 m,-0.7" titolo="Ladder resting on the floor and against a smooth vertical wall"}

- $6\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $3\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The weight, wall force at $B$ and floor force at $A$, of unknown direction, act on the ladder. The system is balanced, so the moment about $A$ is zero, and the force of the floor does not appear because it is applied at $A$. The force of the wall, of $H$ intensity, is horizontal and directed to the left, with moment arm equal to the elevation of $B$, $4\,\mathrm{m}$; the weight has arm equal to the horizontal distance of the midpoint from $A$, $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore
$$
4H - 1.5 \cdot 8 = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
H = 3\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The value $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ is obtained by applying the weight a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ instead of at the midpoint, and $2.4\,\mathrm{kN}$ using the length of the ladder as an arm of $H$.
