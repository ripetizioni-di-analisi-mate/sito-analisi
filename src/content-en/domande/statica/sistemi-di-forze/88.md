A slab carries a uniform load of $5\,\mathrm{kN/m^2}$ and is supported by parallel beams, with $4\,\mathrm{m}$ wheelbase; each beam carries the load of the slab strip between the two half-carries that separate it from the nearby beams. What distributed load acts on an interior beam per unit beam length?

- $10\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $1.25\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

The slab strip that weighs on an internal beam goes from the center of the left span to that of the right span, so it is as wide as $2 + 2 = 4\,\mathrm{m}$, the wheelbase. A section of beam along $1\,\mathrm{m}$ therefore carries a floor area of $4\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and the load per unit length is
$$
q = 5 \cdot 4 = 20\,\mathrm{kN/m} .
$$
The value $10\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ considers only one half of the strip, which is the load of an edge beam, and $1.25\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ divides by the wheelbase instead of multiplying.
