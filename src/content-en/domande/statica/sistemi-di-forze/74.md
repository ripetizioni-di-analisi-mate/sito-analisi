On one beam acts a load of $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from the extreme $A$. The load is to be replaced by two vertical forces applied at point $C$, at $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, and at point $B$, at $6\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Which forces form a system equivalent to the load?

- $40\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $C$ and $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $B$
- $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $C$ and $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $B$
- $40\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $C$ and $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward at $B$
- $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $C$ and $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $B$

## Explanation

$F_C$ and $F_B$ indicate the two forces, positive downwards. The two systems are equivalent if they have the same resultant and the same moment about $A$:
$$
\begin{cases}
F_C + F_B = 30 \\[4pt]
3F_C + 6F_B = 30 \cdot 2
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting from the second equation three times the first gives $3F_B = -30$, then $F_B = -10\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $F_C = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$. The load lies outside the section $CB$, on the $C$ side, so the force at $C$ exceeds the load and the force at $B$ is **upward**. With $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ down at $B$ the resultant would equal $50\,\mathrm{kN}$.
