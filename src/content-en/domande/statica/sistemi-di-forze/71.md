Four systems of forces are considered, with coordinates in meters. Which of them is equivalent to a single nonzero force?

- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right at $(0, 0)$ and $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward at $(0, 2)$
- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right at $(0, 0)$ and $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left at $(0, 2)$
- $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right at $(0, 0)$ and $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left at $(3, 0)$
- $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward at $(0, 0)$, $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $(2, 0)$, and a counterclockwise couple of magnitude $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$

## Explanation

For the reduction of a plane system, a system is equivalent to only one force not nothing if and only if its result is zero. The system with $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right and $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward has resultant $(5, 5)\,\mathrm{kN}$, so it reduces to a force $7.07\,\mathrm{kN}$; its moment about the origin is zero, therefore the line of action passes through $(0, 0)$. The other three systems has zero resultant. The opposing horizontal forces in $(0, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$ form a couple of $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Opposite forces in $(0, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$ have the same line of action, the axis $x$, and form a null system. In the latter system the two vertical forces form a couple of $-8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, which with the couple of $8\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ still gives the system null.
