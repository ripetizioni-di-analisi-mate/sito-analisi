An $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ intensity force is directed left and up and forms an angle of $60^\circ$ with the horizontal. What are its components $F_x$ and $F_y$?

- $F_x = -10\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = 17.32\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = 17.32\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = -17.32\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = -10\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = -17.32\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The angle the force forms with the axis $x$, measured counterclockwise, is $\alpha = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$. So
$$
F_x = 20\cos 120^\circ = -10\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
F_y = 20\sin 120^\circ = 17.32\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The horizontal component is negative because the force is directed to the left, and the vertical one is positive because the force is directed upwards. The $-17.32\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ components are obtained by exchanging sine and cosine, that is, by measuring the angle from the vertical.
