On the cantilever $AB$, which is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ long and fixed at $A$, the same force as $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ down applies once a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and once a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Which statement about the two applied forces is true?

- They are equivalent because they are the same vector
- They have the same resultant but are not equivalent
- They are equivalent because they have the same line of action
- They are not equivalent because their resultants differ

## Explanation

The two forces are the same vector, so they have the same resultant, $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downwards. However, their lines of action are two different verticals, $1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Therefore the moments about $A$ are different:
$$
M_A = -10 \cdot 1 = -10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
M_A = -10 \cdot 3 = -30\,\mathrm{kN\,m} .
$$
Two systems are equivalent only if they have the same resultant and the same moment about a reference point, so the two forces applied **are not equivalent**: on a rigid body a force counts through its line of action, as well as as as a vector.
