A beam of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a triangular load that decreases linearly from $12\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at its left end $A$ to zero at its right end $B$. What is the moment of the load about $B$?

- $72\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, counterclockwise
- $108\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, counterclockwise
- $-144\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, clockwise
- $144\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, counterclockwise

## Explanation

The resultant is the area of the load diagram, $Q = \dfrac{12 \cdot 6}{2} = 36\,\mathrm{kN}$ down. For the triangular load the line of action stands one third of the way from the extreme where the load is maximum, then a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $B$. The resultant is directed downward and acts to the left of $B$, therefore $M_B = 36 \cdot 4 = 144\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, counterclockwise. The value $72\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ results from placing the resultant one third of the way from $B$, the unloaded end. The value $108\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ results from placing it at midspan.
