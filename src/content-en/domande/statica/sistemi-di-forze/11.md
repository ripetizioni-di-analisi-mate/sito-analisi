- $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $A$ and $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $B$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $A$ and $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $B$
- $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $A$ and $15\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $B$
- $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $A$ and $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward at $B$

## Explanation

$F_A$ and $F_B$ indicate the two forces, positive downwards. The system is equivalent to the load if it has the same resultant and the same moment about $A$:
$$
\begin{cases}
F_A + F_B = 30 \\[4pt]
5\,F_B = 2 \cdot 30
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation gives $F_B = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$, and the first gives then $F_A = 18\,\mathrm{kN}$. The force majeure lies at $A$, because the load is closer to $A$; the forces exchanged give a moment about $A$ of $90\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ instead of $60\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.
