A curved rod has the shape of a semicircumference of center $O$ and radius $r = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, placed above the horizontal diameter. On the outer face acts a uniform pressure $p = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, per unit arch length, perpendicular to the rod and directed towards the center $O$. What is the resultant pressure force?

- $18.85\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $0$
- $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward

## Explanation

$\theta$ indicates the angle that the radius forms with the diameter, from $0$ to $\pi$. The $r\,d\theta$-length arc element receives the force $p\,r\,d\theta$, directed towards $O$, then of $-p\,r\cos\theta\,d\theta$ and $-p\,r\sin\theta\,d\theta$ components. Summing all over the arch
$$
R_x = -p\,r \int_0^{\pi} \cos\theta\,d\theta = 0 ,
\qquad
R_y = -p\,r \int_0^{\pi} \sin\theta\,d\theta = -2\,p\,r .
$$
Therefore the resultant equals $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ down, as much as the pressure for the diameter length. The value $18.85\,\mathrm{kN}$ is $p\,\pi r$, i.e. the sum of the intensities without taking into calculation the directions, and the value $0$ neglects that the vertical components all have the same direction.
