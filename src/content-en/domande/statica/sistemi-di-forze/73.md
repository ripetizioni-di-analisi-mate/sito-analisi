The arm of a tower crane carries the load of $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ to $12\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right of the axis of the tower and has the self-weight of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$, with line of action to $4\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right of the axis. The counterweight is located at $4\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left of the axis. How much must the counterweight weigh for the resultant of the three vertical forces to pass through the tower axis?

- $60\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $70\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $50\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The origin is placed on the axis of the tower and the weight of the counterweight is indicated with $C$. The three forces are vertical and act in the same direction, so their result passes through the axis if and only if their resultant moment about one point of the axis is zero:
$$
12 \cdot (-20) + 4 \cdot (-10) + (-4) \cdot (-C) = 0 .
$$
Therefore $4C = 240 + 40 = 280$ and $C = 70\,\mathrm{kN}$. The value $60\,\mathrm{kN}$ neglects the arm's self-weight, and $50\,\mathrm{kN}$ puts it on the counterweight side.
