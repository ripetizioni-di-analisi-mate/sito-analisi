On the beam $AB$, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, a counterclockwise couple of $10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ acts. Two cases are compared: the couple applied a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and the same couple applied a distance $5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Which statement is true?

- The moment about $A$ is greater in the second case
- The two systems are not equivalent because the moment about $A$ changes
- The two systems are equivalent only with respect to the midpoint of the beam
- The two systems are equivalent

## Explanation

A couple has zero resultant and, by the theorem on the moment of a couple, the same moment as with each reference point. Therefore in both cases the system has $\vec{R} = \vec{0}$ and $M_A = 10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Two systems with the same resultant and the same moment about a reference point are equivalent, so the couple gives the same effects on the rigid body at whatever point it is applied. This applies to equilibrium equations and therefore to the reactions of constraints; the internal efforts of the beam instead depend on where the couple acts.
