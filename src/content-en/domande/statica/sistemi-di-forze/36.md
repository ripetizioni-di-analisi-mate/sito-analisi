The beam $AB$ shown, of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, carries a distributed load down that equals $0$ at $A$, grows linearly up to $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ in the $C$ centerpiece and then decreases linearly down to $0$ at $B$. Which system is equivalent to the load?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,3,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" carichi="A,C,0,1,q;C,B,1,0," quote="A,C,3 m,0.8;C,B,3 m,0.8" titolo="Symmetric triangular load with peak intensity $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at midspan"}

- $48\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $C$
- $24\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward, a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$
- $24\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $C$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $C$

## Explanation

The resultant is the area of the load diagram, a base triangle $6\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, then $Q = \dfrac{6 \cdot 8}{2} = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ down. The diagram is symmetric with respect to the vertical through $C$, so its centroid lies on the vertical one and the line of action of the resultant passes through $C$. The same result is achieved by adding the two triangles: each gives $12\,\mathrm{kN}$, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, and the center of the two forces is $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. The value $48\,\mathrm{kN}$ forgets the $\dfrac{1}{2}$ factor in the area.
