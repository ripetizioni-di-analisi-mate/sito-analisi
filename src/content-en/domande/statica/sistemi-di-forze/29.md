Two vertical forces in opposite directions act on beam $AB$ in the figure, which is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ long: $F_1 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward at $A$ and $F_2 = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward at $B$. Where does the line of action of the resultant pass?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" forze="A,0,-1.5,F_1;B,0,0.8,F_2" quote="A,B,4 m,0.8" titolo="Two vertical forces in opposite directions at the ends of the beam"}

- $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, between $A$ and $B$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, between $A$ and $B$
- $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, on the side opposite to $B$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, on the side opposite to $B$

## Explanation

Taking downward forces as positive gives $F_1 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $F_2 = -4\,\mathrm{kN}$, so the resultant is $12 - 4 = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$, directed downward. Since the sum is not zero, the line of action passes through the **center of parallel forces**; with the abscissa measured from $A$ towards $B$,
$$
x_C = \frac{12 \cdot 0 + (-4) \cdot 4}{12 - 4} = \frac{-16}{8} = -2\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The negative sign means that the center lies $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, on the side opposite to $B$. The center of two opposite forces of different magnitude lies outside the segment joining them, on the side of the larger force. The value $1\,\mathrm{m}$ between $A$ and $B$ is obtained by treating the two forces as acting in the same direction, and $1\,\mathrm{m}$ on the opposite side by dividing by the sum of the magnitudes, $16\,\mathrm{kN}$.
