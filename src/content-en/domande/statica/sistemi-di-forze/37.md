Two parallel forces acting in the same direction, of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $30\,\mathrm{kN}$, are applied at points $(0, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$, in meters. Both forces rotate through the same angle about their respective points of application. Through which point does the line of action of the resultant pass in every position?

- $(1, 0)$
- $(3, 0)$
- $(2, 0)$
- Through no fixed point

## Explanation

The resultant of the two forces passes through the **center of parallel forces**, of coordinates
$$
x_C = \frac{10 \cdot 0 + 30 \cdot 4}{40} = 3\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
y_C = 0 .
$$
The coordinates of the center depend on the intensities and application points, and not on the common direction of forces. Thus, when both forces rotate through the same angle, the line of action of their resultant rotates about $(3, 0)$. The point $(1, 0)$ is obtained by exchanging intensities, and $(2, 0)$ is the midpoint between the two application points.
