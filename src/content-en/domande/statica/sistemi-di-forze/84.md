At one point in a body, the force $F_1 = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right and the force $F_2 = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward act. What force applied at the same point brings the other two forces into equilibrium?

- $F_x = -6\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = -8\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = -8\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = -6\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = -8\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The three forces have the same point of application, so their moment about that point is zero, and the system is balanced if and only if the resultant is zero. The resultant of $F_1$ and $F_2$ has $(6, -8)\,\mathrm{kN}$ components, so the third force is the opposite:
$$
F_x = -6\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
F_y = 8\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ intensity, directed upwards and to the left. The $(6, -8)$ components are those of the resultant itself, which doubles the system instead of balancing it.
