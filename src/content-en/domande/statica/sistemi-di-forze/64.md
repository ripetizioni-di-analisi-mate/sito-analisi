On one plate only a counterclockwise couple of $12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ acts. Which of the following systems brings the given couple into equilibrium when added to it?

- Two opposite forces of magnitude $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ on parallel lines a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ apart, forming a counterclockwise couple
- A force of magnitude $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ whose line of action lies a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from the point where the couple is applied
- Two opposing forces of $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ on parallel lines $3\,\mathrm{m}$ apart, forming a clockwise couple
- A force of magnitude $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ whose line of action lies a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from the point where the couple is applied

## Explanation

The overall system is balanced if the resultant and the resultant moment are zero. The couple has zero resultant, so the added system must have result in nothing; one force nothing does not satisfy this condition, whatever its line of action. The added system must then be a couple, with the moment opposite that given. Two opposing forces of $4\,\mathrm{kN}$ on lines distant $3\,\mathrm{m}$ form a couple of $4 \cdot 3 = 12\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$; therefore the couple **clockwise** thus formed balances the given couple.
