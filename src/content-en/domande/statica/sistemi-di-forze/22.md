The $S_1$ system is formed by the force of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ down applied at point $(2, 0)$, the $S_2$ system by the force of $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ down applied at point $(4, 0)$, with coordinates in meters. Both have $-20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ moment about the origin $O$. Which statement is true?

- The two systems are equivalent because they have the same moment about $O$
- The two systems are not equivalent because their resultants differ
- The two systems are equivalent because they have the same moment about every point
- The two systems are equivalent because each consists of a single vertical force

## Explanation

Two systems are **equivalent** if they have the same resultant and the same moment about a reference point. The resultant of $S_1$ is $(0, -10)\,\mathrm{kN}$ and that of $S_2$ is $(0, -5)\,\mathrm{kN}$, so the first condition is not met and the two systems are not equivalent. The equality of the moments about $O$ is only valid for that reference point: about point $(2, 0)$ the moment of $S_1$ is zero, while the moment of $S_2$ is valid for $2 \cdot (-5) = -10\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.
