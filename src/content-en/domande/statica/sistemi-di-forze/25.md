A beam is formed by two rigid $AC$ and $CB$ segments, connected at $C$ by a hinge. On the section $AC$ there is a load of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward, $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $C$, and on the section $CB$ there is a load of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward, $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $C$. You want to replace the two loads with their resultant $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ down, applied at $C$. Which statement is true?

- The replacement is valid because the two loads and the force at $C$ have the same resultant and the same moment about every point
- The replacement is invalid because the two loads act on different rigid bodies
- The replacement is valid because $C$ is the center of the two parallel forces
- The replacement is valid because the hinge at $C$ transmits moments between the segments

## Explanation

Equivalence between force systems is defined for systems applied to the **same rigid body**. The hinge $C$ leaves the relative rotation of the two segments free, so the beam is not a single rigid body. The load on $AC$ acts only on $AC$ and the load on $CB$ only on $CB$; moving both of them to $C$ changes the forces acting on each segment, and thus in general support reactions. The point $C$ is actually the center of the two parallel forces, and the two forces have the same resultant and the same moment of force at $C$, but these facts allow replacement only on a rigid body. The hinge transmits a force between the two segments and not a moment.
