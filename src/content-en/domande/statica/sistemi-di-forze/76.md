Three vertical downward loads of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ to $1\,\mathrm{m}$, $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ to $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ to $6\,\mathrm{m}$ from the extreme $A$ act on one beam, the center of which is $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ to $A$. Add a fourth load of $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ down applied a distance $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Where is the center of the four loads?

- A distance $3.88\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$
- A distance $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$
- A distance $6\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$
- A distance $5.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$

## Explanation

The three initial loads are equivalent to their resultant, $60\,\mathrm{kN}$ down, applied in their center a distance $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. The fourth load is applied at the same point, so the four loads are equivalent to two forces concurring with the same line of action, and the center remains at $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. The direct calculation confirms this:
$$
x_C = \frac{10 \cdot 1 + 20 \cdot 4 + 30 \cdot 6 + 20 \cdot 4.5}{80} = \frac{360}{80} = 4.5\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The value $3.88\,\mathrm{m}$ is the simple average of the four positions, and $6\,\mathrm{m}$ is obtained by dividing the sum of the moments by the result of the only three initial loads.
