A beam of length $L = 6\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a load $q(x) = q_0\Big( 1 - \dfrac{x}{L} \Big)^2$, where $q_0 = 12\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ and $x$ is measured from the left end $A$. How far from $A$ does the line of action of the resultant pass?

- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

With the $t = \dfrac{x}{L}$ replacement the result is
$$
Q = \int_0^L q(x)\,dx = q_0 L \int_0^1 (1 - t)^2\,dt = \frac{q_0 L}{3} = 24\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The first moment of area of the diagram about $A$ is
$$
\int_0^L x\,q(x)\,dx = q_0 L^2 \int_0^1 t\,(1 - t)^2\,dt = \frac{q_0 L^2}{12} = 36\,\mathrm{kN\,m} .
$$
Therefore $x_Q = \dfrac{36}{24} = 1.5\,\mathrm{m} = \dfrac{L}{4}$. The value $2\,\mathrm{m}$ is the position for the triangular load, which also cancels at $B$ but decreases linearly.
