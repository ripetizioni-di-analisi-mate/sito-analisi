A force of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$, parallel to the vector $(3, 4)$ and with its direction, is applied at point $A = (0, 0)$; an opposite force, of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$, is applied at point $B = (4, 0)$, with the coordinates in meters. What is the moment of the couple formed by the two forces?

- $32\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, clockwise
- $32\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, counterclockwise
- $40\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, clockwise
- $24\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, clockwise

## Explanation

The force at $A$ has $(6, 8)\,\mathrm{kN}$ components and the force at $B$ has $(-6, -8)\,\mathrm{kN}$ components. The moment of a couple is the same as at each reference point, so it is calculated about $A$, where the first force has zero moment:
$$
M = 4 \cdot (-8) - 0 \cdot (-6) = -32\,\mathrm{kN\,m} .
$$
The couple is therefore **clockwise**, of $32\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The moment arm of the couple is the distance between the two lines of action, $d = \dfrac{32}{10} = 3.2\,\mathrm{m}$, less than the distance $AB = 4\,\mathrm{m}$ because the forces are inclined about $AB$; the value $40\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ uses the distance $AB$ as its arm.
