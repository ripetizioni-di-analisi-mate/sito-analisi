A plane system of forces has zero resultant moment about point $A = (0, 0)$ and about point $B = (4, 0)$, with coordinates in meters. What additional condition ensures that the system is in equilibrium?

- The resultant moment is also zero about point $(2, 0)$
- The resultant moment is also zero about point $(6, 0)$
- The resultant moment is also zero about point $(0, 3)$
- The $R_y$ component of the resultant is zero

## Explanation

If the resultant were nothing, the system would be equivalent to only one force with zero moment about $A$ and $B$, then with line of action for $A$ and $B$, i.e. the axis $x$. Therefore the two given conditions only allow for a direct result along the axis $x$, in addition to the balanced system. A force along the axis $x$ has zero moment about each point of the axis and has $R_y = 0$, so conditions on $(2, 0)$, $(6, 0)$ and $R_y$ do not rule it out. The point $(0, 3)$ does not stand on the axis $x$, so a non-nothing force along the axis has about it moment other than zero; the null moment about $(0, 3)$ therefore excludes any resultant non-nothing, and the system is **balanced**. It is the choice of three unaligned reference points for equilibrium equations.
