A cable is stretched between the point $P = (0, 4)$ and the anchor point $Q = (3, 0)$, with the coordinates in meters, and pulls the point $P$ towards $Q$ with a force of $15\,\mathrm{kN}$. What are the components $F_x$ and $F_y$ of the force applied at $P$?

- $F_x = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = -9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = -12\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = -9\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $F_x = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_y = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

A cable transmits a direct force along the cable itself, so the force applied at $P$ has the direction and sense of the vector
$$
\overrightarrow{PQ} = (3 - 0,\; 0 - 4) = (3, -4) ,
$$
which has length $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\,\mathrm{m}$. The force unit vector is therefore $\Big( \dfrac{3}{5}, -\dfrac{4}{5} \Big)$, and multiplying it by the intensity results in
$$
F_x = 15 \cdot \frac{3}{5} = 9\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
F_y = 15 \cdot \Big( -\frac{4}{5} \Big) = -12\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The $(12, -9)$ components are obtained by exchanging the two legs, and the $(-9, 12)$ components correspond to a direct force from $Q$ towards $P$, that is, to a pushing cable.
