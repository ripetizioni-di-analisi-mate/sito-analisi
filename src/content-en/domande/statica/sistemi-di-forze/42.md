Beam $AB$ has length $L = 6\,\mathrm{m}$, with coordinate $x$ measured from end $A$. It carries a downward distributed load $q(x) = q_0 \sin\dfrac{\pi x}{L}$, where $q_0 = 10\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. What is the magnitude of the resultant load?

- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $38.2\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $60\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $19.1\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

By the theorem on **Resultant of a distributed load** the resultant is the area of the load diagram. With the $t = \dfrac{\pi x}{L}$ replacement you get
$$
Q = \int_0^L q_0 \sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx = \frac{q_0 L}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \sin t\,dt = \frac{2\,q_0 L}{\pi} = \frac{120}{\pi}\,\mathrm{kN} = 38.2\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$
The diagram is symmetrical with respect to the middle, so the resultant acts downward at $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. The value $19.1\,\mathrm{kN}$ is obtained by taking equal to $1$ the integral of $\sin t$ on $[0, \pi]$, which equals $2$, and $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ by treating the load as triangular with the maximum in the middle.
