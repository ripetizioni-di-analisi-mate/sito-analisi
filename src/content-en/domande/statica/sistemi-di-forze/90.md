An $5\,\mathrm{m}$ long inclined rod, with horizontal projection of $4\,\mathrm{m}$, has the self-weight of $2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ per unit length of the rod. What uniform vertical load per unit length of horizontal projection has the same resultant?

- $2.5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$
- $3.33\,\mathrm{kN/m}$

## Explanation

Self-weight has resultant $q_s\,\ell = 2 \cdot 5 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$, vertical and applied at the midpoint of the rod. A load $q$ related to the horizontal projection has resultant $q\,a$, also vertical for the midpoint, so the two loads have the same resultant if
$$
4q = 10
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
q = 2.5\,\mathrm{kN/m} .
$$
The load per meter of projection is greater than the weight per meter of rod, because on one meter of projection stands more than one meter of rod. The value $1.6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ is obtained with the ratio of the inverted lengths, and $3.33\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ using the vertical projection of $3\,\mathrm{m}$.
