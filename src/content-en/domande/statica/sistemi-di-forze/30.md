A curved rod has the shape of a quarter of a circumference of center $O = (0, 0)$ and radius $r = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, from point $A = (2, 0)$ to point $B = (0, 2)$, with coordinates in meters. Its self-weight is $3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ per unit arch length. At what distance from the vertical line through $O$ does the line of action of the member's total weight pass?

- $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.85\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

It describes the arch at the angle $\vartheta$ from $0$ to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$: the point of the arch with horizontal coordinate $x = r\cos\vartheta$ and the arch element is $ds = r\,d\vartheta$ long. The total weight is $Q = 3 \cdot \dfrac{\pi r}{2} = 3\pi = 9.42\,\mathrm{kN}$. The weight line of action passes through the **center of parallel forces** of the weights of the arch elements, then
$$
x_Q = \frac{1}{Q}\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} 3\,r\cos\vartheta \cdot r\,d\vartheta = \frac{3r^2}{3\pi} = \frac{12}{3\pi} = \frac{4}{\pi} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
i.e. $\dfrac{2r}{\pi}$. The value $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$ is the horizontal coordinate of the midpoint of the arch, $1\,\mathrm{m}$ is half the radius, as for a straight rod, and $0.85\,\mathrm{m}$ is the horizontal coordinate $\dfrac{4r}{3\pi}$ of the centroid of the full quarter circle.
