The beam shown, of span $6\,\mathrm{m}$, has a pin at $A$ and in $B$ a two-force inclined link of $45^\circ$, with the pin-ended link descending to the left. To trace with Müller–Breslau theorem the influence lines of the horizontal reaction $H_A$, the pin at $A$ is replaced with a roller support that leaves the horizontal displacement free. Where is the center of rotation of the beam in the mechanism?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,pendolo,225" quote="A,B,6 m,0.9" titolo="Beam with a pin at $A$ and an inclined link at $B$"}

- At $B$
- At $A$
- On the vertical through $B$, a distance $6\,\mathrm{m}$ below $B$
- On the vertical through $A$, a distance $6\,\mathrm{m}$ below $A$

## Explanation

The roller support at $A$ exerts a vertical reaction, so the center of rotation lies on the vertical through $A$. The two-force link in $B$ prevents moving along the pin-ended link, so the center also lies on the line of the pin-ended link, which passes through $B$ and is inclined by $45^\circ$. With the origin in $A$ this line has equation $y = x - 6$ and meets vertical $x = 0$ at point $(0, -6)$, so the center is located $6\,\mathrm{m}$ below $A$. By imposing $A$ the horizontal $1$ shift to the right, the rotation equals $-\dfrac{1}{6}$ and the vertical shifts are $-\dfrac{\xi}{6}$, which is the line $H_A$ of influence given by the equilibrium.
