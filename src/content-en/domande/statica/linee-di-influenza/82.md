The Gerber beam shown has suspended beam $AC$ on the left and supporting beam $CD$ on the right. A crowd load of $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ may occupy any combination of segments. What maximum upward reaction must roller $D$ resist?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,3,0;B,5,0;D,9,0" aste="A-C;C-D" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,carrello;B,cerniera;D,carrello" quote="A,C,3 m,0.9;C,B,2 m,0.9;B,D,4 m,0.9" titolo="Gerber beam with suspended beam $AC$ on the left"}

- $6\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $16\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $26\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $32\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

On the supporting beam the influence line for $V_D$ is $\dfrac{\xi - 5}{4}$. It is zero at $B$, positive between $B$ and $D$, where it reaches $1$, and negative on overhang $CB$, with value $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ at $C$. On the suspended segment the line is the segment that goes from $0$ in $A$ to $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ in $C$, so it is negative. For the variable-length distributed load theorem, the maximum is given by loading only the section $BD$, where the area is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 1 = 2\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore $V_{D,\max} = 8 \cdot 2 = 16\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward. With the load along the entire beam you would get $6\,\mathrm{kN}$, because the negative areas equal all $-\dfrac{5}{4}\,\mathrm{m}$, while $26\,\mathrm{kN}$ adds up the negative areas as if they were positive.
