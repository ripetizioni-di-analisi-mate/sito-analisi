In the Gerber beam shown, suspended beam $AC$ rests on a roller at $A$ and is connected at $C$, a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, to supporting beam $CD$. The latter has a pin at $B$, a distance $5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, and a roller at $D$, a distance $8\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. What is the influence line for $V_A$ when $\xi$ is measured from $A$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,5,0;D,8,0" aste="A-C;C-D" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,carrello;B,cerniera;D,carrello" quote="A,C,2 m,0.9;C,B,3 m,0.9;B,D,3 m,0.9" titolo="Gerber beam with suspended beam $AC$ on the left"}

- $\dfrac{2 - \xi}{2}$ on $AC$ and $0$ on $CD$
- $\dfrac{8 - \xi}{8}$ along the entire beam
- $\dfrac{2 - \xi}{2}$ along the entire beam
- $\dfrac{5 - \xi}{5}$ on $AC$ and $0$ on $CD$

## Explanation

For Müller–Breslau's theorem, the roller support at $A$ is removed and $A$ is forced to move vertically $1$. The $CD$ supporting segment retains the pin at $B$ and the roller support at $D$, so it stays still, and the line is zero from $C$ to $D$. The suspended segment revolves about hinge $C$, which does not move, so on $AC$ the line is the segment that goes from $1$ under $A$ to $0$ under $C$, i.e. $\dfrac{2 - \xi}{2}$. The extended segment across the beam would treat the two bodies as one, and $\dfrac{8 - \xi}{8}$ is the line of a simply supported beam in $A$ and $D$.
