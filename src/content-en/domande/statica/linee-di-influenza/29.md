It traces with Müller–Breslau theorem the influence line for the reaction moment $M_A$, positive counterclockwise, of an $3\,\mathrm{m}$-long cantilever, fixed at $A$ on the left and free at the right end. Which mechanism is used, and what influence line does it produce?

- Replace the fixed support with a parallel-link connection having horizontal links and vertical displacement $1$. The result is $\eta_{M_A}(\xi) = 1$
- Replace the fixed support with a pin and impose rotation $\varphi = 1$. The result is $\eta_{M_A}(\xi) = 3 - \xi$
- Replace the fixed support with a pin and impose rotation $\varphi = 1$. The result is $\eta_{M_A}(\xi) = \xi$
- Replace the fixed support with a pin and impose rotation $\varphi = 1$. The result is $\eta_{M_A}(\xi) = -\xi$

## Explanation

The reaction moment is the reaction that prevents rotation, so you replace fixed support with a hinge, which leaves only the rotation around $A$ free. The conjugate displacement of $M_A$ is the rotation $\varphi$, positive counterclockwise, and $\varphi = 1$ is imposed. The coordinate $\xi$ point then rises by $v(\xi) = \varphi\,\xi = \xi$, therefore $\eta_{M_A}(\xi) = \xi$, with the ordinate maximum $3\,\mathrm{m}$ at the free end. Parallel-link support with the horizontal links leaves the vertical displacement free and gives the line of $V_A$, constant and equal to $1$.
