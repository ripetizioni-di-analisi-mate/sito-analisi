The beam shown has a pin at $A$, the roller support at $B$ $5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and the overhang $BC$ of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$; section $D$ is at $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $B$. Müller–Breslau theorem traces the influence line for the bending moment at $D$. What instantaneous motion results?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,5,0;D,6.5,0;C,8,0" aste="A-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" quote="A,B,5 m,0.9;B,D,1.5 m,0.9;D,C,1.5 m,0.9" titolo="Overhanging beam showing section $D$ on the overhang"}

- Segment $AD$ remains stationary and segment $DC$ rotates about $D$. The displacement of $C$ is $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- Segment $AD$ rotates about $A$ and segment $DC$ remains stationary
- Segment $AD$ remains stationary and segment $DC$ rotates about $D$. The displacement of $C$ is $-1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- The entire beam rotates about $B$. The displacement of $C$ is $-3\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

You insert a pin at $D$. The segment $AD$ retains the pin at $A$ and the roller support at $B$, so it is statically constrained and remains stationary: $\varphi_s = 0$. The $\varphi_s - \varphi_d = 1$ condition then gives $\varphi_d = -1$, i.e. the segment $DC$ rotates clockwise around $D$. The displacement of $C$ is therefore $-1 \cdot (8 - 6.5) = -1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The influence line is zero up to $D$ and drops linearly down to $-1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ in $C$, in accordance with the section theorem on one overhang.
