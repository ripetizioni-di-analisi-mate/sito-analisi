In the Gerber beam shown, suspended beam $C_1C_2$ rests on the overhangs of two supporting beams: $AC_1$, with a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$, and $C_2E$, with rollers at $D$ and $E$. A crowd of $10\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ can arrange themselves on any segments. What is the maximum reaction at roller $B$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C_1,8,0;C_2,12,0;D,14,0;E,20,0" aste="A-C_1;C_1-C_2;C_2-E" cerniere="C_1;C_2" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;D,carrello;E,carrello" quote="A,B,6 m,0.9;B,C_1,2 m,0.9;C_1,C_2,4 m,0.9;C_2,D,2 m,0.9;D,E,6 m,0.9" titolo="Gerber beam with suspended beam $C_1C_2$ supported by two overhangs"}

- $30\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $120\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $\dfrac{160}{3}\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $80\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

On the $AC_1$ supporting segment the influence lines of $V_B$ is $\dfrac{\xi}{6}$, which in $C_1$ is $\dfrac{8}{6} = \dfrac{4}{3}$. By removing the roller support at $B$ the beam $C_2E$ remains stationary, so by Müller–Breslau's theorem the line is zero on $C_2E$ and on the suspended segment is the segment ranging from $\dfrac{4}{3}$ in $C_1$ to $0$ in $C_2$. The line is positive from $A$ to $C_2$, so for the variable-length distributed load theorem the maximum is by loading this whole segment. The area equals $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot \dfrac{4}{3} + \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot \dfrac{4}{3} = 8\,\mathrm{m}$, then $V_{B,\max} = 10 \cdot 8 = 80\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward. The value $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ is obtained by loading the span $AB$ only, and $\dfrac{160}{3}\,\mathrm{kN}$ neglects the load on the suspended segment.
