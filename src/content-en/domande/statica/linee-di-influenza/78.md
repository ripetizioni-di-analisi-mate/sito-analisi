A segment of $2\,\mathrm{m}$ of an $6\,\mathrm{m}$ long walkway, hinged at $A$ and resting on an $B$ roller support, can be occupied by a load of $9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, in any position. If section $S$ is a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, what is the greatest shear the load can produce at $S$?

- $9\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $3\,\mathrm{kN}$
- $18\,\mathrm{kN}$

## Explanation

The influence line for shear in $S$ is negative to the left of $S$ and equals $\dfrac{6 - \xi}{6}$ to the right, where it is positive and decreasing. The line is discontinuous in $S$, so the continuity hypothesis of the theorem on the assigned assigned length distributed load is not satisfied, and the position is found by comparing the ordinate: the load should be put immediately to the right of $S$, on the segment from $2\,\mathrm{m}$ to $4\,\mathrm{m}$, where the ordinate are positive and larger. On this segment the area of the line is a trapezoid with bases $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and height $2\,\mathrm{m}$, then $\mathcal{A} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \left(\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{3}\right) \cdot 2 = 1\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore $T_{S,\max} = 9 \cdot 1 = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$. The value $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ multiplies the resultant $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ by the maximum sort $\dfrac{2}{3}$, and $3\,\mathrm{kN}$ corresponds to the load centered on $S$.
