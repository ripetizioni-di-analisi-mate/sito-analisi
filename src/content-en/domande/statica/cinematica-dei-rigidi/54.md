A flat rigid plate rotates $\varphi = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$, counterclockwise, around the center $C = (3;\ 1.5)\,\mathrm{m}$. Which points on the plate have displacements parallel to direction $(1, 1)$?

- The points on the line through $C$ in direction $(1, 1)$, that is, $y = x - 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- The points on the line through $C$ in direction $(1, -1)$, that is, $x + y = 4.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- The points on line $x = 3\,\mathrm{m}$
- The points on line $y = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

For the rotation center existence theorem the displacement of a point $P$ is $\vec{s}_P = \varphi\,\hat{k} \times \overrightarrow{CP}$, i.e. the vector $\overrightarrow{CP}$ rotated by a right angle and multiplied by $\varphi$. If $\overrightarrow{CP} = (a, b)$, you have $\hat{k} \times \overrightarrow{CP} = (-b, a)$, which is parallel to $(1, 1)$ if and only if $-b = a$. So the shift is parallel to $(1, 1)$ if and only if $\overrightarrow{CP}$ is parallel to $(1, -1)$, i.e. perpendicular to the given direction. The points sought therefore form the line through $C$ with the direction $(1, -1)$, of equation $x + y = 3 + 1.5 = 4.5\,\mathrm{m}$. On the line $x = 3\,\mathrm{m}$ the displacements are horizontal, and on the line $y = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ they are vertical.
