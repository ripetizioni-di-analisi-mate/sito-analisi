An $AB$ horizontal beam, $5\,\mathrm{m}$ long and with $A$ to the left of $B$, undergoes an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement in which $A$ moves vertically upwards and $B$ vertically downwards; the modules of the two shifts are not known. What can be concluded about the center of rotation?

- It lies on line $AB$ between $A$ and $B$, at a position that depends on the ratio of the magnitudes
- It is the midpoint of $AB$
- It is the point at infinity in the horizontal direction
- It lies on line $AB$ outside segment $AB$

## Explanation

The center lies on the line through $A$ perpendicular to $\vec{s}_A$ and the line through $B$ perpendicular to $\vec{s}_B$, both of which coincide with the line $AB$. The only directions of the movements therefore say that the center lies on the line $AB$, without fixing its position. Because $A$ goes up and $B$ moves down, the $v = \varphi\,(x - x_C)$ vertical displacement changes sign between $A$ and $B$, so the center is inside the segment. Its position follows from the ratio of the modules, which are proportional to the distances from the center: for example with $|v_A| = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $|v_B| = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$ the center is $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $B$.
