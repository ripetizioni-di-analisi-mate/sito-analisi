A flat rigid plate rotates at a right angle, counterclockwise, around the origin $O = (0, 0)$. After the rotation, what is the position of the point on the plate that was originally at $P = (3, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$?

- $P' = (1, -3)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $P' = (-3, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $P' = (-1, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $P' = (-4, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The displacement is rigid with $O$ stationary and $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, hence $P' = R(\theta)\,P$. Because $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$, you have
$$
R = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
P' = R \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
The new position is therefore $P' = (-1, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$, and the point shift is $P' - P = (-4, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$. As a control, $\overline{OP'} = \sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{m} = \overline{OP}$. For such a large rotation one cannot use the infinitesimal displacement formulas, which are only valid at the first order in the angle.
