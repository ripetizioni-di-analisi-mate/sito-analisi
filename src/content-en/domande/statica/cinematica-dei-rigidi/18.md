Points $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (3, 4)$, in meters, belong to a planar rigid body. In an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement we have $u_A = 5\,\mathrm{mm}$, $v_A = 0$ and $u_B = 1\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is $v_B$?

- $v_B = -3\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $v_B = 4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $v_B = 0$
- $v_B = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

By the theorem on displacements along the line joining two points, $\vec{s}_A$ and $\vec{s}_B$ have the same displacement component along line $AB$, whose unit vector is $\left( \dfrac{3}{5},\ \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$. The component of $\vec{s}_A$ is $5\,\mathrm{mm} \cdot \dfrac{3}{5} = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$, that of $\vec{s}_B$ is $\dfrac{3 \cdot 1\,\mathrm{mm} + 4\,v_B}{5}$. Equalizing the two components results in $3\,\mathrm{mm} + 4\,v_B = 15\,\mathrm{mm}$, then $v_B = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$. The same result follows from the formulas of infinitesimal shifts, because $u_B = u_A - \varphi \cdot 4\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $\varphi = 0.001\,\mathrm{rad}$ and then $v_B = v_A + \varphi \cdot 3\,\mathrm{m} = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$.
