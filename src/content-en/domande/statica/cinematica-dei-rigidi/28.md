A planar rigid body rotates about the center $C = (2, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. The point $P = (2, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$ of the body moves to the right. In which direction does the body rotate, and how does point $Q = (5, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ move?

- Clockwise rotation, with $Q$ moving downward
- Clockwise rotation, with $Q$ moving upward
- Counterclockwise rotation, with $Q$ moving downward
- Counterclockwise rotation, with $Q$ moving upward

## Explanation

For the rotation center existence theorem the horizontal displacement of $P$ is $u_P = -\varphi\,(y_P - y_C) = -3\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi$. Because $P$ moves to the right, $u_P > 0$, then $\varphi < 0$ and the rotation is **clockwise**. The vertical displacement of $Q$ is $v_Q = \varphi\,(x_Q - x_C) = 3\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi$. So $v_Q < 0$ and $Q$ move down.
