The displacement of a rigid plate is the sum of two infinitesimal rotations: a rotation of $0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ counterclockwise around the point $(0, 0)$ and a rotation of $0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ clockwise around the point $(0, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$. How does the plate move?

- A translation of $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the left
- A translation of $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the right
- No displacement because the two rotations cancel
- A rotation of $0.004\,\mathrm{rad}$ about point $(0, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The sum has rotation $0.002 - 0.002 = 0$, so it is a translation, and just calculate the displacement of a point. The point $(0, 0)$ is stationary in the first rotation. In the second, which has $\varphi = -0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ and center $(0, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$, the same point has
$$
u = -\varphi\,(0 - 4000\,\mathrm{mm}) = -8\,\mathrm{mm} ,
\qquad
v = \varphi\,(0 - 0) = 0 .
$$
Therefore, all points in the body move $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the left. Two opposite rotations around distinct centers give a translation perpendicular to the center line, here the vertical line $x = 0$.
