Horizontal beam $AB$ has points $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (6, 0)$, with coordinates in meters. It undergoes an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement with $u_A = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$, $v_A = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $v_B = -9\,\mathrm{mm}$. Where is the center of rotation?

- $C = (1.5;\ 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (-1.5;\ -1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (1.5;\ 1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (1.5;\ -1)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Since $A$ and $B$ have the same vertical coordinate, the infinitesimal rigid-body displacement formula gives $v_B - v_A = \varphi\,(x_B - x_A)$. So $\varphi = \dfrac{(-9 - 3)\,\mathrm{mm}}{6000\,\mathrm{mm}} = -0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$, clockwise. By the theorem of existence of the center of rotation we have $x_C = x_A - \dfrac{v_A}{\varphi} = -\dfrac{3\,\mathrm{mm}}{-0.002} = 1500\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $y_C = y_A + \dfrac{u_A}{\varphi} = \dfrac{2\,\mathrm{mm}}{-0.002} = -1000\,\mathrm{mm}$. The center is therefore $C = (1.5;\ -1)\,\mathrm{m}$, one meter below the beam. The point $(1.5;\ 0)$ of the beam has $v = 0$ and $u = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$, so it moves horizontally.
