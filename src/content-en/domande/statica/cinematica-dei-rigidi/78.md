Two beam segments lie on the $x$ axis: segment $AB$ rotates about the fixed pin $A = (0, 0)$ and segment $BC$ rests at $C = (8, 0)$ on a roller sliding on a horizontal plane. At $B = (4, 0)$ they are connected by an internal double link whose links are inclined at $45^\circ$, parallel to the direction $(1, 1)$ (coordinates in metres). Where is the absolute centre of rotation $C_2$ of segment $BC$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,8,0" aste="A-B;B-C" interni="B,doppio-pendolo,45" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,4 m" titolo="Two segments connected by an internal double link with inclined links"}

- $C_2 = (8, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_2 = (8, 8)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_2 = (8, -8)\,\mathrm{m}$
- At the point at infinity of the direction $(1, 1)$

## Explanation

The fixed pin gives $C_1 = A$. The internal double link prevents the relative rotation and the relative displacement along the links, so it only allows a relative translation perpendicular to the links, and $C_{12}$ is the point at infinity of the direction $(1, 1)$. By the **first theorem of kinematic chains** $C_2$ lies on the line through $C_1$ with the direction of $C_{12}$, that is on the line $y = x$. The roller at $C$ has a vertical reaction, therefore $C_2$ also lies on the vertical $x = 8\,\mathrm{m}$, and hence $C_2 = (8, 8)\,\mathrm{m}$. The point $(8, -8)$ is obtained by taking the direction perpendicular to the links, and the point at infinity of the direction $(1, 1)$ is the relative centre $C_{12}$.
