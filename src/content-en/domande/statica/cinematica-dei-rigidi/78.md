The arch in the figure is formed by the $AC$ (body $1$) and $CB$ (body $2$) rods, connected by the internal hinge $C = (3, 3)$; at $A = (0, 0)$ there is a pin support and at $B = (6, 0)$ a roller support that runs on a plane parallel to the direction $(1, -1)$. The normal at the level of the roller support is parallel to member $AC$. How does member $CB$ move when member $AC$ rotates through $\varphi_1 = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,3,3;B,6,0" aste="A-C;C-B" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello,225" quote="A,3 0,3 m,0.8;3 0,B,3 m,0.8" titolo="Arch with a roller on a surface inclined at $45^\circ$ at point $B$"}

- It translates by $(6, -6)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It translates by $(-6, 6)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It translates by $(-6, 0)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It rotates about $B$

## Explanation

The pin support gives $C_1 = A$ and the internal hinge gives $C_{12} = C$. The normal to the support surface for $B$ has the direction $(1, 1)$, so it is the line $y = x - 6$, and $C_2$ lies on this line; for the **first theorem of kinematic chains** also lies on the $AC$, $y = x$ line. The two lines are parallel, so $C_2$ is the point at infinity of the direction $(1, 1)$ and member $CB$ moves. The displacement is that of $C$ as the point of member $AC$:
$$
u_C = -\varphi_1 \cdot 3\,\mathrm{m} = -6\,\mathrm{mm} ,
\qquad
v_C = \varphi_1 \cdot 3\,\mathrm{m} = 6\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
Member $CB$ then moves $(-6, 6)\,\mathrm{mm}$, up to the left, parallel to the plane of the roller support.
