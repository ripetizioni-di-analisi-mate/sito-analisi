In a kinematic chain the body $1$ rotates of $\varphi_1 = 0.001\,\mathrm{rad}$ around $C_1 = (0, 0)$, while the body $2$ moves with $u = 0$, $v = 4\,\mathrm{mm}$ displacement. Where is the relative center $C_{12}$?

- $C_{12} = (0, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{12} = (-4, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- At infinity in the horizontal direction
- $C_{12} = (4, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The relative center is the point where the displacements of the two bodies coincide. The body $1$ gives $(-\varphi_1\,y,\ \varphi_1\,x)$ displacement at point $(x, y)$, and the body $2$ gives $(0;\ 4\,\mathrm{mm})$ displacement at each point. Equality requires $y = 0$ and $0.001\,x = 4\,\mathrm{mm}$, so $x = 4\,\mathrm{m}$ and $C_{12} = (4, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. The point at infinity in the horizontal direction is the absolute center $C_2$ of the vertical translation, and the three centers $C_1$, $C_{12}$, $C_2$ stand on the line $y = 0$, as the **first theorem of kinematic chains** requires.
