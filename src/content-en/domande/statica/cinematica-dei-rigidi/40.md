Two planar rigid bodies are connected by an internal hinge at point $B$ and an internal two-force link, that is, a pin-ended link hinged to the two bodies, the line of which does not pass through $B$. Which statement about the relative motion of the two bodies is true?

- The two bodies can rotate relative to each other about $B$
- The bodies have no relative motion and behave as a single body
- The bodies can translate relative to each other in the direction of the link
- The relative center $C_{12}$ lies at infinity in the direction of the link

## Explanation

The internal hinge requires that the relative center be $C_{12} = B$. The internal two-force link requires $C_{12}$ to stand on the pin-ended link line. Since $B$ does not stand on this line, no relative motion with relative rotation does not meet the two conditions. A relative translation is also excluded, because the hinge requires that $B$ have the same displacement in the two bodies. Therefore the two **have no relative motions** bodies and behave like one rigid body.
