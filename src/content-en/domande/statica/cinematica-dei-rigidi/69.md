In the beam shown the sections $AB$ (body $1$) and $BC$ (body $2$) are connected by the internal hinge $B$; $A$ is a pin support and $C$ is a horizontal roller support. For $\varphi_1 \neq 0$, what happens to the vertical displacement diagram $v(x)$ at hinge $B$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,5,0" aste="A-B;B-C" cerniere="B" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello" quote="A,B,3 m;B,C,2 m" titolo="Two-body beam with an internal hinge at $B$"}

- It has a jump because the two bodies rotate through different angles
- It is zero because $B$ is a hinge
- It is continuous and has a corner
- It is continuous and has the same slope on both segments

## Explanation

The internal hinge imposes on $B$ the same displacement as the point of the two bodies, so the $v(x)$ diagram is continuous at $B$. For the vertical displacement theorem of a body, the slope of each segment is the rotation of the body. With the origin at $A$ you have $C_1 = A$ and $C_2 = C$, and the **first theorem of kinematic chains** gives $\varphi_1 \cdot 3\,\mathrm{m} = \varphi_2\,(3 - 5)\,\mathrm{m}$, i.e. $\varphi_2 = -\dfrac{3}{2}\varphi_1$. The two slopes are different, so the diagram has an **angled point** at $B$ where it equals $v_B = 3\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1$. A jump of $v$ comes with an internal parallel-link connection at horizontal links, which allows relative vertical sliding.
