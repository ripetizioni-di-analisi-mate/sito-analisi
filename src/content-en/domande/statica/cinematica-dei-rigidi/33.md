In a kinematic chain the body $1$ rotates $\varphi_1 = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ around $C_1 = (0, 0)$ and the body $2$ rotates $\varphi_2 = -0.001\,\mathrm{rad}$ around $C_2 = (6, 0)$, in meters. Where is the relative center $C_{12}$?

- $C_{12} = (4, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{12} = (2, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{12} = (-6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{12} = (3, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

For the **first theorem of kinematic chains** $C_{12}$ it lies on line $C_1 C_2$, i.e. on line $y = 0$. At a point $(x, 0)$ the two bodies give zero horizontal shifts and vertical shifts $\varphi_1\,x$ and $\varphi_2\,(x - 6)$. The relative center is the point where the shifts coincide, hence $0.002\,x = -0.001\,(x - 6)$, from which $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$. The point $C_{12} = (2, 0)$ is internal to the segment $C_1 C_2$, consistent with the opposite-direction rotations.
