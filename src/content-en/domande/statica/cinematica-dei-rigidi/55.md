The horizontal $AB$ beam in the figure, with $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (6, 0)$ in meters, rotates $\varphi = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ around the center $C = (2, -3)\,\mathrm{m}$. Which point on the beam has the smallest displacement magnitude, and what is that magnitude?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" punti="2,-3,C,centro" tratteggi="2,0 2,-3" quote="A,B,6 m,-0.6" titolo="Beam $AB$ and center of rotation $C$ a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ below the beam"}

- Point $A$, with $7.2\,\mathrm{mm}$
- Point $(2, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$, with zero displacement
- Point $(2, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$, with $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- Point $B$, with $10\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

For the rotation center existence theorem each point $P$ has displacement of magnitude $|\varphi|\,\overline{CP}$, so the magnitude is minimal at the point of the beam closest to $C$. The point of the line $y = 0$ closest to $C = (2, -3)$ is the foot of the perpendicular, i.e. $(2, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$, which is distant from $3\,\mathrm{m}$ by $C$. Therefore the minimum magnitude equals $0.002 \cdot 3000\,\mathrm{mm} = 6\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the displacement of that point is horizontal, because it is perpendicular to the vertical segment that connects it with $C$. The center does not belong to the beam, so no point in the beam has zero displacement.
