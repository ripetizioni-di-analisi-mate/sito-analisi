The horizontal beam $AB$, with $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (4, 0)$ in meters, is a rigid body. Which pair of displacements, in millimeters, cannot describe an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement of the beam?

- $\vec{s}_A = (1, 0)$, $\ \vec{s}_B = (1, 4)$
- $\vec{s}_A = (2, -1)$, $\ \vec{s}_B = (2, 3)$
- $\vec{s}_A = (0, 3)$, $\ \vec{s}_B = (0, -1)$
- $\vec{s}_A = (1, 2)$, $\ \vec{s}_B = (3, 2)$

## Explanation

By the theorem on the displacements of two points along the conjunct, $\vec{s}_A$ and $\vec{s}_B$ have the same component along the line $AB$, which here is horizontal, so it must be $u_A = u_B$. The couple with $u_A = 1\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $u_B = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$ would lengthen the beam by $2\,\mathrm{mm}$, so it does not describe a rigid-body displacement. The other three couples have $u_A = u_B$, and each corresponds to the rotation
$$
\varphi = \frac{v_B - v_A}{4000\,\mathrm{mm}} ,
$$
which equals $0.001$, $0.001$ and $-0.001\,\mathrm{rad}$ respectively.
