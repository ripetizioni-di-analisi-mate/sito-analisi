A planar rigid body rotates about a center of its own. The point $P = (3, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$ moves vertically and the point $Q = (3, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$ moves in the direction of the vector $(-4, 3)$. Where is the center of rotation?

- $C = (3, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (0, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (0, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

For the center theorem from the directions of two-point shifts, the center lies on the line through $P$ perpendicular to $\vec{s}_P$ and on the line through $Q$ perpendicular to $\vec{s}_Q$. The displacement of $P$ is vertical, so the first line is the horizontal $y = 0$. The direction perpendicular to $(-4, 3)$ is $(3, 4)$, so the second line is formed by the $(3 + 3t,\ 4 + 4t)$ points. This line meets $y = 0$ by $t = -1$, so the center is $C = (0, 0)$. As a control, $\overrightarrow{CQ} = (3, 4)$ is perpendicular to $(-4, 3)$, because $3 \cdot (-4) + 4 \cdot 3 = 0$.
