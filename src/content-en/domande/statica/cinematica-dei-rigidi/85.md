In a chain of two rigid bodies, the absolute centers $C_1$, $C_2$ and the relative center $C_{12}$ are known, all of which are their own, and the rotations are non-zero. In which of the following cases, with coordinates in meters, do the two bodies rotate in the same direction?

- $C_1 = (0, 0)$, $\ C_2 = (2, 0)$, $\ C_{12} = (6, 0)$
- $C_1 = (0, 0)$, $\ C_2 = (6, 0)$, $\ C_{12} = (2, 0)$
- $C_1 = (0, 0)$, $\ C_2 = (6, 6)$, $\ C_{12} = (3, 3)$
- $C_1 = (0, 5)$, $\ C_2 = (0, 0)$, $\ C_{12} = (0, 2)$

## Explanation

For the **first theorem of kinematic chains** it equals $\varphi_1\,\overrightarrow{C_1 C_{12}} = \varphi_2\,\overrightarrow{C_2 C_{12}}$. Rotations have the same sign if and only if vectors $\overrightarrow{C_1 C_{12}}$ and $\overrightarrow{C_2 C_{12}}$ have the same direction, i.e. if $C_{12}$ is outside the segment $C_1 C_2$. With $C_1 = (0, 0)$, $C_2 = (2, 0)$, $C_{12} = (6, 0)$ the relative center is external, and the $\varphi_1 \cdot 6\,\mathrm{m} = \varphi_2 \cdot 4\,\mathrm{m}$ relationship gives $\varphi_2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\varphi_1$, of the same sign. In the other three cases $C_{12}$ is internal to the segment $C_1 C_2$, so the bodies rotate in opposite directions: for example with $C_1 = (0, 5)$, $C_2 = (0, 0)$, $C_{12} = (0, 2)$ you get $\varphi_2 = -\dfrac{3}{2}\varphi_1$.
