A two-body beam has the pin support $A$ in the axis $x = 0$ and the internal hinge $B$ in $x = 4\,\mathrm{m}$; the body $2 = BC$ reaches up to $x = 7\,\mathrm{m}$. The vertical displacement diagram equals $v = 0.002\,x$ on the body $1 = AB$ and $v = 8\,\mathrm{mm}$, constant, on the body $2$. Which statement about body $2$ is true?

- It rotates about hinge $B$
- It translates vertically, with its center at infinity in the horizontal direction
- It remains stationary
- It translates horizontally

## Explanation

For the vertical displacement theorem of a body, the segment of the diagram relative to the body $2$ has slope $\varphi_2$, so a constant segment corresponds to $\varphi_2 = 0$. The body $2$ does not rotate and its points all have $v = 8\,\mathrm{mm}$, so it moves upwards. The center of a vertical translation is the point at infinity in the horizontal direction. The value $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ coincides with the displacement of $B$ as the point of the body $1$, $0.002 \cdot 4000\,\mathrm{mm}$, as the continuity of the diagram in the hinge requires.
