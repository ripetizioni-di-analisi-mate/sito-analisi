The portal frame shown consists of bodies $1 = AB$, $2 = BC$, and $3 = CD$. Points $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (0, 4)$, $C = (6, 4)$, and $D = (6, 2)$ have coordinates in meters. Points $A$ and $D$ are pin supports, while $B$ and $C$ are internal hinges. Where is the relative center $C_{13}$ of the two columns?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,6,4;D,6,2" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" quote="A,B,4 m,-0.8;B,C,6 m,-0.7;D,C,2 m,0.8" titolo="Portal frame with columns of unequal height"}

- $C_{13} = (-12, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{13} = (6, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C_{13} = (12, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$
- At infinity in the horizontal direction

## Explanation

Hinges give $C_1 = A$, $C_3 = D$, $C_{12} = B$ and $C_{23} = C$. For **first theorem of kinematic chains**, applied to bodies $1$ and $3$, $C_{13}$ lies on the line $C_1 C_3 = AD$, of equation $y = \dfrac{x}{3}$. For the **Second theorem of kinematic chains** $C_{13}$ it also lies on the line $C_{12} C_{23} = BC$, i.e. $y = 4$. The two lines meet in $C_{13} = (12, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$. The lines intersect because columns have different heights, while with two equal columns they would be parallel and $C_{13}$ would be at infinity.
