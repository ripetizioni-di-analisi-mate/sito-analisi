A flat rigid plate rotates about a center of its own $C$. The point $P$ of the plate, distant $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $C$, moves by $4\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the displacement of point $Q$ on the plate, a distance $5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $C$?

- $10\,\mathrm{mm}$, perpendicular to segment $CQ$
- $10\,\mathrm{mm}$, parallel to segment $CQ$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{mm}$, perpendicular to segment $CQ$
- $4\,\mathrm{mm}$, perpendicular to segment $CQ$

## Explanation

By the rotation center existence theorem each point of the plate has a perpendicular shift to the segment that connects it with $C$, of magnitude $|\varphi|$ for distance from $C$. From point $P$ is obtained
$$
|\varphi| = \frac{4\,\mathrm{mm}}{2000\,\mathrm{mm}} = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad} .
$$
Therefore the point $Q$ moves $0.002 \cdot 5000\,\mathrm{mm} = 10\,\mathrm{mm}$, in a direction perpendicular to $CQ$.
