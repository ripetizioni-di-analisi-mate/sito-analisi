In an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement of a plane body the points $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (4, 0)$, in meters, have the same displacement, with $u = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $v = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$. Where is the center of rotation?

- At infinity in the direction of displacement $(2, 3)$
- At infinity in the direction perpendicular to displacement $(2, 3)$
- At the midpoint of segment $AB$
- Nowhere, because this is not a rigid-body displacement

## Explanation

For the rigid-body displacements infinitesimal theorem we have $v_B - v_A = \varphi\,(x_B - x_A) = \varphi \cdot 4\,\mathrm{m}$. Since $v_B = v_A$, the rotation is $\varphi = 0$, so the displacement is an **translation** and all points of the body move by $(2, 3)\,\mathrm{mm}$. A translation has no fixed points, and its center is the point at infinity in the direction perpendicular to the displacements. The center is therefore the point at infinity of direction $(-3, 2)$.
