The points $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (4, 0)$ and $D = (0, 3)$, in meters, are vertices of a rigid plate. In an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement we have $\vec{s}_A = (1, 2)\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\vec{s}_B = (1, 6)\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is $\vec{s}_D$?

- $(4, 2)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $(1, 2)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $(-2, 2)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $(-2, 6)\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

Since $A$ and $B$ have the same vertical coordinate, formula $v = v_A + \varphi\,(x - x_A)$ gives
$$
\varphi = \frac{v_B - v_A}{x_B - x_A} = \frac{(6 - 2)\,\mathrm{mm}}{4000\,\mathrm{mm}} = 0.001\,\mathrm{rad} .
$$
The point $D$ has the same horizontal coordinate as $A$ and an order greater than $3\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
u_D = u_A - \varphi \cdot 3000\,\mathrm{mm} = 1\,\mathrm{mm} - 3\,\mathrm{mm} = -2\,\mathrm{mm} ,
\qquad
v_D = v_A = 2\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
Therefore $\vec{s}_D = (-2, 2)\,\mathrm{mm}$. The result complies with the theorem on the displacements of two points along the conjunct, because $A$ and $D$, on the same vertical, have the same vertical component.
