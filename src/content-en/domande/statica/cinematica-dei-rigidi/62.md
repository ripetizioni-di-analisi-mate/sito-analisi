Two bodies in a kinematic chain rotate through the same angle $\varphi_1 = \varphi_2 = 0.001\,\mathrm{rad}$, the first about center $C_1 = (0, 0)$ and the second about center $C_2 = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. What can be concluded about their relative center $C_{12}$?

- At infinity in the vertical direction
- At midpoint $(3, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- At infinity in the horizontal direction
- At point $C_1$

## Explanation

The displacement of the body $2$ as seen by the body $1$ is $\vec{s}_2 - \vec{s}_1$, with $\varphi_2 - \varphi_1 = 0$ rotation, so it is a translation. At point $C_1$, you have $\vec{s}_1 = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{s}_2 = \varphi_2\,\hat{k} \times \overrightarrow{C_2 C_1} = 0.001 \cdot (0,\ -6000)\,\mathrm{mm}$, so the relative translation is $(0, -6)\,\mathrm{mm}$, vertical. The center of a translation is the point at infinity in the direction perpendicular to the displacements, so $C_{12}$ is the **Misplaced point of horizontal direction**. The result is consistent with the first kinematic chain theorem, because the line $C_1 C_2$ is horizontal.
