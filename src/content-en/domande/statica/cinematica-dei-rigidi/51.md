Two rigid-body infinitesimal displacements apply to a planar rigid body together, both counterclockwise: a rotation of $0.001\,\mathrm{rad}$ around the point $(0, 0)$ and a rotation of $0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ around the point $(3, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. The resulting displacement is the sum of the two. Where is its center of rotation?

- $(1, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(1.5;\ 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(2, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $(6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The rigid-body displacements infinitesimal are linear in parameters, so the sum of the two is still an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement, with rotation $0.001 + 0.002 = 0.003\,\mathrm{rad}$. The two centers stand on the axis $x$, so at the points of the axis both shifts have zero horizontal component. The vertical components, with $x$ in meters, are $0.001\,x$ and $0.002\,(x - 3)$, and their sum cancels by
$$
0.001\,x + 0.002\,(x - 3) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = 2 .
$$
The center of the resulting shift is therefore $(2, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. Its coordinates are the average of the two centers weighed with rotations, and for this reason the center is closer to the center of the major rotation.
