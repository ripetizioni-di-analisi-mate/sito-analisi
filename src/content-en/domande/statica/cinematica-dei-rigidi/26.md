Two horizontal beam sections, the body $1$ on the left and the body $2$ on the right, are connected at section $B$ only by an internal two-force link with the vertical pin-ended link, which joins the point $P$ of the body $1$ and the point $Q$ of the body $2$, on the vertical line through $B$. What can be concluded about the displacements of $P$ and $Q$?

- They have the same horizontal displacement and may have different vertical displacements
- They have the same vertical displacement and may have different horizontal displacements
- They have the same displacement, as at an internal hinge
- They may have different horizontal and vertical displacements

## Explanation

The pin-ended link is a rigid body, therefore, by the theorem on the displacements of two points along the conjunct, its extremes $P$ and $Q$ have displacements with the same component along the line $PQ$. Since the pin-ended link is vertical, you have $v_P = v_Q$. The pin-ended link can rotate instead, and its infinitesimal rotation moves $Q$ about $P$ in a direction perpendicular to $PQ$, i.e. horizontally. Therefore $P$ and $Q$ **have the same vertical displacement and may have different horizontal shifts**. As a result, the vertical displacement diagram of the beam is continuous at section $B$, as in the case of an internal hinge.
