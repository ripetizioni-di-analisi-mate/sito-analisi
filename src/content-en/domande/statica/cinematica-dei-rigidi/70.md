The beam shown is formed by the body $1 = AB$, with a pin support at $A = (0, 0)$, and the body $2 = BC$, connected to the first by the internal hinge $B = (3, 0)$; at $C = (5, 0)$, in meters, the body $2$ is resting on a roller support that moves along an inclined plane of $30^\circ$ on the horizontal. For $\varphi_1 \neq 0$, which statement about point $C$ is true?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,5,0" aste="A-B;B-C" cerniere="B" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello,300" quote="A,B,3 m,-0.7;B,C,2 m,-0.7" titolo="Two-body beam with a roller on a surface inclined at $30^\circ$ at point $C$"}

- $C$ moves along the inclined surface
- $C$ remains stationary, as it does for any nonvertical sliding plane
- $C$ moves vertically
- $C$ moves horizontally

## Explanation

The pin support gives $C_1 = A$ and the internal hinge gives $C_{12} = B$. The roller support only allows $C$ to move along the plane, so $C_2$ lies on the normal plane for $C$; for the **first theorem of kinematic chains** $C_2$ also lies on the line $AB$, i.e. on the axis $y = 0$. The normal passes through $C$, which belongs to the axis, and does not coincide with the axis because the plane is not vertical, so the two lines meet at $C$ and $C_2 = C$. The body $2$ then rotates about $C$, with $\varphi_2 = -\dfrac{3}{2}\varphi_1$, and the point $C$ **Stay still**. The same is true for any non-vertical support surface, because its normal passes through $C$ and does not coincide with the axis.
