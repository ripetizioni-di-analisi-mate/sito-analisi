In a coordinate system where $x$ and $y$ and the displacements $u$ and $v$ are all in millimeters, which displacement field represents an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement?

- $u = 2 + 0.001\,y$, $\ v = 3 + 0.001\,x$
- $u = 2 - 0.001\,y$, $\ v = 3 + 0.001\,x$
- $u = 2 + 0.001\,x$, $\ v = 3 + 0.001\,y$
- $u = 2 - 0.002\,y$, $\ v = 3 + 0.001\,x$

## Explanation

For the rigid-body displacements infinitesimal theorem with $O = (0, 0)$, an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement has the form $u = u_O - \varphi\,y$, $v = v_O + \varphi\,x$. So $u$ does not depend on $x$, $v$ does not depend on $y$, and the coefficient of $y$ in $u$ is the opposite of the coefficient of $x$ in $v$. The $u = 2 - 0.001\,y$, $v = 3 + 0.001\,x$ field has this shape, with $u_O = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$, $v_O = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\varphi = 0.001\,\mathrm{rad}$. The field with $u = 2 + 0.001\,x$ extends the horizontal segments, because it gives their extremes different horizontal shifts. The other two fields have coefficients of $y$ in $u$ and $x$ in $v$ that are not opposite, therefore they deform the body: for example that with $u = 2 + 0.001\,y$ lengthens the segment from $(0, 0)$ to $(1000, 1000)$, whose extremes have displacements that differ by $(1, 1)$.
