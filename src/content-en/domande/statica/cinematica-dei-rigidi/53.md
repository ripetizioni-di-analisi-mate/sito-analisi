The $A = (1, 0)$ and $B = (4, 4)$ points, in meters, belong to a planar rigid body that undergoes an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement with rotation $\varphi = -0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$, clockwise. The displacement of $A$ is not known. What is the difference $\vec{s}_B - \vec{s}_A$?

- $(-8, 6)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $(-6, -8)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $(6, 8)\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $(8, -6)\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

For the rigid-body displacements infinitesimal theorem with $O = A$ we have $\vec{s}_B = \vec{s}_A + \varphi\,\hat{k} \times \overrightarrow{AB}$, so the difference depends only on the rotation and the vector $\overrightarrow{AB} = (3, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$. Product $\hat{k} \times \overrightarrow{AB}$ is the vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$ rotated by a right angle counterclockwise, i.e. $(-4, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore
$$
\vec{s}_B - \vec{s}_A = -0.002 \cdot (-4000,\ 3000)\,\mathrm{mm} = (8, -6)\,\mathrm{mm} .
$$
The difference is perpendicular to $\overrightarrow{AB}$, because $8 \cdot 3 + (-6) \cdot 4 = 0$, as required by the theorem on the displacements of two points along the conjunct.
