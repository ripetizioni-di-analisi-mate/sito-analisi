The triangular $ABD$ plate in the figure, with $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (4, 0)$ and $D = (4, 3)$ in meters, is supported by three two-force links: at $A$ a vertical pin-ended link, at $D$ a horizontal pin-ended link and at $B$ a pin-ended link parallel to the vector $(4, -3)$. Where is the center of rotation of the plate?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;D,4,3" aste="A-B;B-D;D-A" vincoli="A,pendolo;B,pendolo,323.13;D,pendolo,0" quote="A,B,4 m,0.9;B,D,3 m,-0.6" titolo="Triangular plate supported by three links"}

- The plate remains stationary
- $C = (0, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (4, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (2;\ 1.5)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Each two-force link only allows its point to move perpendicular to the pin-ended link, so the center lies on the line of the pin-ended link. The pin-ended link at $A$ gives the line $x = 0$ and the pin-ended link at $D$ gives the line $y = 3$, which meet at point $(0, 3)$. The line of the pin-ended link at $B$ is formed by the points $(4 + 4t,\ -3t)$ and for $t = -1$ passes right through $(0, 3)$. The three lines are therefore concurrent, therefore the **can rotate** plate around $C = (0, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$, while having three scalar constraints. With three straight lines without a common point the slab would stand still.
