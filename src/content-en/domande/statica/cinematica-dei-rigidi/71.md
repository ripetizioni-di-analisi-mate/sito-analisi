A four-bar linkage is formed by the bodies $1 = AB$, $2 = BC$, $3 = CD$, with the pin supports $A$ and $D$ and the internal hinges $B$ and $C$. What geometric condition is necessary and sufficient for the two columns to have the same rotation, $\varphi_3 = \varphi_1$?

- Columns $AB$ and $CD$ have the same length
- Columns $AB$ and $CD$ are parallel
- Crossbeam $BC$ is horizontal
- Crossbeam $BC$ is parallel to line $AD$

## Explanation

The two columns have the same rotation if and only if their relative center $C_{13}$ is at infinity. For the first theorem of kinematic chains, applied to bodies $1$ and $3$, $C_{13}$ lies on line $C_1 C_3 = AD$. For the **Second theorem of kinematic chains** it also lies on the line $C_{12} C_{23} = BC$. The two lines have their own point in common except when parallel, so $\varphi_3 = \varphi_1$ if and only if $BC$ is parallel to $AD$. For example with $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (0, 3)$, $C = (4, 3)$, $D = (4, 1)$ the columns are parallel and the crossbeam is horizontal, but the line $AD$ is inclined, so $C_{13} = (12, 3)\,\mathrm{m}$ is proper and the rotations are different.
