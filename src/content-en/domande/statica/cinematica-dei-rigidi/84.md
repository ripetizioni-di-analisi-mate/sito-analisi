The beam $AE$ (body $1$) and the beam $FG$ (body $2$) in the figure are connected by two cross pin-ended links, $EK$ and $HF$, hinged at the ends; $E = (3, 0)$ and $H = (3, -1)$ are body points $1$, $F = (5, 0)$ and $K = (5, -1)$ body points $2$, in meters. Where is the relative center $C_{12}$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;E,3,0;H,3,-1;F,5,0;K,5,-1;G,8,0" aste="A-E;E-H;F-G;F-K" catene="E-K;H-F" cerniere="E;H;F;K" titolo="Two beams connected by two crossed links"}

- At point $(4, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$
- At infinity in the horizontal direction
- It does not exist because the two bodies have no point in common
- At point $(4;\ -0.5)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Each pin-ended link is an internal two-force link, so $C_{12}$ lies on the line of each pin-ended link. The pin-ended link $EK$ goes from $(3, 0)$ to $(5, -1)$ and has equation $y = -\dfrac{x - 3}{2}$; the pin-ended link $HF$ goes from $(3, -1)$ to $(5, 0)$ and has equation $y = -1 + \dfrac{x - 3}{2}$. Equating the two expressions results in $x - 3 = 1$, then $x = 4\,\mathrm{m}$ and $y = -0.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The relative center is therefore $C_{12} = (4;\ -0.5)\,\mathrm{m}$, the point where pin-ended links cross. For infinitesimal displacements the two pin-ended links therefore amount to an internal hinge at that point, which does not belong to either body.
