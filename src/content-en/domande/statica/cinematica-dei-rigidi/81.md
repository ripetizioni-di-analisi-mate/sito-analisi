In a kinematic chain the $1$ and $2$ bodies rotate about the same absolute center $C_1 = C_2 = (2, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$, with rotations $\varphi_1 = 0.001\,\mathrm{rad}$ and $\varphi_2 = 0.003\,\mathrm{rad}$. Where is the relative center $C_{12}$?

- At point $(2, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- At infinity in the horizontal direction
- At any point on the vertical line through $(2, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- At point $(1, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The relative center is the point where the displacements of the two bodies coincide. Indicated with $C$ the point $(2, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$, the relative displacement of a point $P$ is $\vec{s}_2 - \vec{s}_1 = (\varphi_2 - \varphi_1)\,\hat{k} \times \overrightarrow{CP}$, i.e. a rotation of $0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ around $C$. The center of this rotation is $C$, so $C_{12} = (2, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$, the only point where the displacements of the two bodies are both null and therefore equal. The first theorem of kinematic chains is satisfied, because the three centers coincide.
