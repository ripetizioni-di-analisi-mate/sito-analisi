A flat plate undergoes a transformation that brings each of its $P = (x, y)$ points to the point $P'$, with coordinates in meters. Which of the following transformations is a rigid-body displacement of the form $P' = O' + R(\theta)\,(P - O)$, where $R(\theta)$ is a rotation matrix?

- $P' = (1 - x,\ 2 + y)$
- $P' = (1 + y,\ 2 + x)$
- $P' = (1 - y,\ 2 + x)$
- $P' = (1 - y,\ 2 + 2x)$

## Explanation

With $O = (0, 0)$, $O' = (1, 2)$ and $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ you have $R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $R(\theta)\,(x, y) = (-y, x)$, so the $P' = (1 - y,\ 2 + x)$ transformation is a rigid-body displacement: the plate rotates a right angle counterclockwise and shifts. The $P' = (1 - x,\ 2 + y)$ and $P' = (1 + y,\ 2 + x)$ transformations retain distances, but their linear part has $-1$ determinant matrix, while each rotation matrix has $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$ determinant; therefore they flip the plate, like a symmetry with respect to a line. The $P' = (1 - y,\ 2 + 2x)$ transform does not retain distances, because it brings the $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$ points to $(1, 2)$ and $(1, 4)$, which are $2\,\mathrm{m}$ apart.
