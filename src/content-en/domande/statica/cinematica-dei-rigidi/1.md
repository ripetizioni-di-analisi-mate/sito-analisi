A rigid body undergoes an infinitesimal planar displacement. Point $O = (0, 0)$ has displacement components $u_O = 4\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $v_O = -2\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the body rotates counterclockwise through $\varphi = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$. Where is the center of rotation?

- $C = (-1, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (1, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (2, 1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $C = (1, -2)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The center of rotation is the point with zero displacement. By the theorem on the existence of the center of rotation, its coordinates are $x_C = x_O - \dfrac{v_O}{\varphi}$ and $y_C = y_O + \dfrac{u_O}{\varphi}$. Expressing the displacements in meters gives $\dfrac{v_O}{\varphi} = \dfrac{-0.002\,\mathrm{m}}{0.002} = -1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\dfrac{u_O}{\varphi} = \dfrac{0.004\,\mathrm{m}}{0.002} = 2\,\mathrm{m}$. Hence $x_C = 0 - (-1\,\mathrm{m}) = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $y_C = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, or $C = (1, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$. As a check, at $C$ the displacement components are $u = u_O - \varphi\,y_C = 4\,\mathrm{mm} - 0.002 \cdot 2000\,\mathrm{mm} = 0$ and $v = v_O + \varphi\,x_C = -2\,\mathrm{mm} + 0.002 \cdot 1000\,\mathrm{mm} = 0$.
