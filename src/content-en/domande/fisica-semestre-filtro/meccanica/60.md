A force is always perpendicular to a particle's velocity. What work does it do during the motion?

- Always negative because it changes direction.
- Zero.
- Always positive because the force is present.
- Force magnitude times distance, without an angular factor.

## Explanation

On each infinitesimal section, $dW=\vec F\cdot d\vec r=0$ because displacement and velocity share a direction. The force can change motion direction without changing kinetic energy through its own work. An example is the centripetal resultant in uniform circular motion, where speed remains constant.
