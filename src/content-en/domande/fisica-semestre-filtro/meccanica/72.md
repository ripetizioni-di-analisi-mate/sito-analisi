A body descends along a fixed smooth guide under uniform gravity. Which reasoning justifies mechanical-energy conservation?

- Velocity is necessarily constant.
- Gravity is conservative and normal force does no work.
- Potential energy remains constant.
- Net force is necessarily zero.

## Explanation

The ideal fixed guide's normal force is perpendicular to motion; gravity transfers energy between potential and kinetic forms. Mechanical-energy conservation requires neither zero resultant force nor constant speed. Contact with the guide still needs checking and is assumed valid in the problem’s model.
