The centre of mass of a closed system with constant total mass initially moves at $+2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. External resultant force remains zero while its parts move internally. What is the centre-of-mass velocity afterwards?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- It depends only on internal forces.
- $+2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

## Explanation

For constant total mass, $M\vec a_{\mathrm{CM}}=\sum\vec F_{\mathrm{ext}}=0$. Centre-of-mass velocity keeps its initial value; fixed position would require zero initial velocity. Internal motion does not change this balance. The centre of mass therefore follows a straight line even if individual parts follow different trajectories.
