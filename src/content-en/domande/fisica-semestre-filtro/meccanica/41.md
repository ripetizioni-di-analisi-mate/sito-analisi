A horse pulls a cart and the cart pulls the horse with an equal opposite force. Why does this not prevent the horse–cart system from accelerating?

- The horse's force on the cart is greater than the reaction.
- Newton's third law applies only to stationary bodies.
- Those forces are internal; the ground can exert an external resultant on the system.
- Internal forces add in the same direction.

## Explanation

Forces between horse and cart cancel in the complete-system balance. Centre-of-mass acceleration depends on external forces, including ground contact. Equality of the internal pair remains valid during acceleration. If the cart alone is isolated, however, the horse’s pull becomes an external force to include.
