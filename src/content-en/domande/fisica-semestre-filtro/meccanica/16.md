For the same constant deceleration magnitude, how does doubling initial speed change braking distance alone? Neglect reaction time.

- It halves it.
- It doubles it.
- It quadruples it.
- It leaves it unchanged.

## Explanation

From $s=\dfrac{v_0^2}{2|a|}$, braking distance is proportional to speed squared. A fixed reaction time would add a term linear in $v_0$, but that section is excluded here. Thus a merely linear speed–distance proportion would underestimate braking distance at the higher initial speed.
