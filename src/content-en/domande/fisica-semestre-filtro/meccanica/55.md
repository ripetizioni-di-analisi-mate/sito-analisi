A block slides on a horizontal floor. An extra downward push leaves mass and kinetic coefficient unchanged. How does friction magnitude change while contact persists?

- It becomes static solely because of the downward push.
- It decreases because the extra force is vertical.
- It stays equal because mass is unchanged.
- It increases because normal force increases.

## Explanation

With no vertical acceleration, $N=mg+F_{\downarrow}$. Kinetic friction $\mu_dN$ therefore increases with the push even though the push is vertical. The regime remains kinetic while relative sliding continues. Equal mass does not guarantee equal normal force when other perpendicular forces change.
