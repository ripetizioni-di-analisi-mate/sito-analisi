A refrigerator removes heat from its cold compartment and rejects it into a warmer room. Why does this not violate Clausius’s statement?

- The compartment’s entropy must increase during every cooling process.
- Returning the fluid to its initial state allows the surroundings to be excluded from the balance.
- Heat rejected into the room is less than heat removed from the compartment.
- The transfer requires work, so it is not the sole effect.

## Explanation

Clausius excludes cold-to-hot heat transfer as the sole result. A refrigerator receives work and rejects $Q_h=Q_c+W_{\mathrm{in}}$. Heat delivered to the room therefore exceeds heat removed from the compartment; the full balance includes supplied work.
