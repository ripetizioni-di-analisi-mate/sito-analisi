A wall conducts heat steadily between faces held at different temperatures. Its internal energy does not change. Does this imply that the wall–reservoir whole is in thermal equilibrium?

- No: the wall must necessarily accumulate heat over time.
- No: sustained flow generates entropy without energy accumulation in the wall.
- Yes: constant internal energy always implies thermal equilibrium.
- Yes: equal incoming and outgoing powers cancel all entropy generation.

## Explanation

A steady state requires equal incoming and outgoing powers, leaving the temperature profile unchanged. The finite reservoir temperature difference nevertheless sustains irreversible transfer and total entropy increase. Thermal equilibrium and absence of energy accumulation are different conditions.
