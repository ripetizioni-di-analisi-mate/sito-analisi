An ideal gas slowly loses molecules through a valve while volume and temperature stay constant. What happens to absolute pressure?

- It decreases in proportion to the remaining moles.
- It decreases in proportion to the square of remaining moles.
- It stays constant because temperature is unchanged.
- It increases because remaining molecules have more space.

## Explanation

From $p=\dfrac{nRT}{V}$, fixed $R,T,V$ give $p\propto n$. Matter loss changes the factor held constant in Boyle’s law for a closed sample. Mean kinetic energy remains tied to temperature, while fewer particles collide with the walls.
