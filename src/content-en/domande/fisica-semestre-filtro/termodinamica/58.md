A closed ideal gas with constant molar heat capacities and only volume work expands from the same initial state to the same final volume, once along a reversible isotherm and once along a reversible adiabat. Which final pressure is lower?

- They are equal because final volumes coincide.
- The adiabatic pressure, because temperature falls.
- The isothermal pressure, because its work is zero.
- The isothermal pressure, because heat enters.

## Explanation

At equal $n$ and $V_f$, pressure is proportional to $T_f$. The isotherm retains initial temperature; in reversible adiabatic expansion, internal energy decreases as work is done. With $\gamma>1$, $p\propto V^{-\gamma}$ falls faster than $p\propto V^{-1}$.
