A gas in an adiabatic rigid vessel receives $200\,\mathrm J$ of work from a stirrer. What is $\Delta U$, neglecting final macroscopic energy changes?

- $+200\,\mathrm J$.
- $-200\,\mathrm J$.
- $0\,\mathrm J$ because the vessel is rigid.
- $+400\,\mathrm J$.

## Explanation

Rigidity eliminates volume work, but the stirrer transfers another form of work. With $Q=0$ and work done by the system $W=-200\,\mathrm J$, the first law gives $\Delta U=+200\,\mathrm J$. The volume-work-only assumption is therefore crucial in isochoric formulas.
