A gas is enclosed in a rigid, thermally insulated container. A paddle stirrer, driven from outside, does $200\,\mathrm J$ of work on the gas. The gas is at rest at the beginning and at the end. What is the change $\Delta U$ in its internal energy?

- $+200\,\mathrm J$
- $-200\,\mathrm J$
- $0\,\mathrm J$
- $+400\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The container is thermally insulated, so the gas exchanges no heat and $Q=0$. The container is rigid, therefore the volume does not change and the gas does no volume work. The stirrer, however, does work of another kind on the gas, and with the convention of the theory this work is $W=-200\,\mathrm J$, because it is received by the system. By the first law, then, $\Delta U=Q-W=0-(-200\,\mathrm J)=+200\,\mathrm J$. The value $0\,\mathrm J$ comes from considering only volume work, and the value $+400\,\mathrm J$ from counting the same work once as heat and once as work.
