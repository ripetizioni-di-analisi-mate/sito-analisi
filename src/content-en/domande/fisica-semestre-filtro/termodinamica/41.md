A $0.50\,\mathrm{m^2}$ surface uniformly transmits $100\,\mathrm W$ of heat. What is its heat-flux density?

- $50\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$.
- $200\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$.
- $100\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$.
- $2\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$.

## Explanation

Heat-flux density is power per unit area: $j_Q=\dfrac{P}{A}=\dfrac{100}{0.50}=200\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. Multiplying by area would give different dimensions. Watts describe the entire surface; watts per square metre describe transfer intensity.
