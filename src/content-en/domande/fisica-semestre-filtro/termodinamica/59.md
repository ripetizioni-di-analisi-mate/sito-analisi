Why does temperature increase during reversible adiabatic compression of a closed ideal gas with only volume work and constant, positive molar heat capacity $C_V$?

- Temperature increases because internal energy depends only on volume.
- The gas necessarily absorbs heat from its surroundings.
- Received work increases internal energy.
- Work done by the gas is positive and removes internal energy.

## Explanation

Adiabaticity gives $Q=0$. Compression has $W<0$, so $\Delta U=-W>0$; with $\Delta U=nC_V\Delta T$ and $C_V>0$, $\Delta T>0$. The change results from received work without heat transfer.
