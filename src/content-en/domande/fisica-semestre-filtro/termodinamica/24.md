Identical closed ideal-gas samples with constant molar heat capacities and only volume work start in the same state and undergo the same temperature rise: one at constant volume, the other at constant pressure in a mechanically reversible process. How do heat and internal-energy changes compare?

- Equal $\Delta U$; the isochoric sample absorbs more heat.
- Equal heat; the isobaric sample has greater $\Delta U$.
- Both heat and $\Delta U$ are equal.
- Equal $\Delta U$; the isobaric sample absorbs more heat.

## Explanation

For an ideal gas, $\Delta U=nC_V\Delta T$ regardless of the constraint. During isobaric heating, the gas also does positive work, so $Q=\Delta U+W$ is larger. The samples’ final volumes may differ. Equal final temperatures therefore do not imply that every variable of the final states is equal.
