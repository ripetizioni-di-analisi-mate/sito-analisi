A $2.4\,\mathrm{m}$ measurement is converted exactly to centimetres. What can be stated about precision?

- The result becomes one hundred times more precise because the number grows.
- Changing units does not improve experimental information.
- Conversion always introduces one centimetre of uncertainty.
- Relative uncertainty divides by one hundred because the unit is smaller.

## Explanation

The metre-to-centimetre factor is exact. Numerical value and absolute uncertainty, if known, change by the same ratio, while relative uncertainty stays equal. Writing more digits after conversion creates no new observations. The original instrument’s precision remains the information limit.
