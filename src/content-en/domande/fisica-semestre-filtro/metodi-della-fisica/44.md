Density is intensive. Does that make it impossible to change density by compressing a fixed-mass sample?

- Yes: every intensive quantity is constant in every transformation.
- No: it must always decrease when volume decreases.
- Yes: density depends only on total mass.
- No: reducing volume at fixed mass can increase it.

## Explanation

Intensive means independent of material amount when portions in the same state are compared. Compression changes state and volume. At fixed mass, $\rho=\dfrac mV$ increases when volume decreases. Intensivity is neither a conservation law nor a guarantee of constancy in every process.
