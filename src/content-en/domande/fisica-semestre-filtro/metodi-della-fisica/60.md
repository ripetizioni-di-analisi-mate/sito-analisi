A plane vector has magnitude $5\,\mathrm{m}$ and component $a_x=3\,\mathrm{m}$. With no vertical-direction information, what can be inferred?

- $a_y=2\,\mathrm{m}$ necessarily.
- $a_y=\pm4\,\mathrm{m}$: two directions remain possible.
- $a_y=4\,\mathrm{m}$ necessarily.
- $a_y=\pm\sqrt{34}\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

Pythagoras determines the missing component’s square: $a_y^2=(25-9)\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The square root gives magnitude four but not its sign. Both vectors are symmetric about the horizontal axis. Subtracting magnitudes five and three as if collinear does not reconstruct a perpendicular component.
