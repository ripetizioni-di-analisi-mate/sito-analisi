A homogeneous sample is duplicated in the same state, doubling $m$ and $V$. How does $\dfrac{m^2}{V}$ scale?

- It halves.
- It doubles.
- It quadruples.
- It stays equal because it is a ratio of extensive quantities.

## Explanation

The numerator grows fourfold and denominator twofold, giving an overall factor two. Mass squared is not an extensive quantity: it quadruples, rather than doubling, when the system is duplicated. Having a ratio therefore does not by itself make the expression intensive. Factors would cancel for $\dfrac mV$, but squaring mass changes the conclusion.
