Two forces on a particle are $(4;1)\,\mathrm{N}$ and $(-1;2)\,\mathrm{N}$. Which third force gives zero resultant?

- $(-5;1)\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $(-3;-3)\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $(0;0)\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $(3;3)\,\mathrm{N}$.

## Explanation

The first two forces sum to three newtons on each axis. The third must cancel both components, so it is the opposite resultant. Adding the resultant itself would double the imbalance. Particle equilibrium concerns the vector sum, not the magnitude sum.
