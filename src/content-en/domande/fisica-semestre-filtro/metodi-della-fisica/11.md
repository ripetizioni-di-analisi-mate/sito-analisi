A $5.0\,\mathrm{N}$ force is at $60^\circ$ from positive $x$ in the first quadrant. What are its components?

- $(F_x;F_y)=\left(\dfrac{5\sqrt3}{2};2.5\right)\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $(F_x;F_y)=\left(2.5;\dfrac{5\sqrt3}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $(F_x;F_y)=\left(-2.5;\dfrac{5\sqrt3}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $(F_x;F_y)=(5;0)\,\mathrm{N}$.

## Explanation

The angle is measured from $x$, so the horizontal component uses cosine and the vertical one sine. At sixty degrees the vertical component is larger; the first quadrant requires both positive. Swapping components would describe thirty degrees, while reversing the horizontal sign would move the vector into the second quadrant.
