A thin converging lens has focal length $f=15.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, and a real object is at $p=10.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens. With the convention $q>0$ for a real image, what are the image distance $q$ and the magnification $M$?

- $q=+6.00\,\mathrm{cm}$, $M=-0.600$
- $q=-30.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, $M=+3.00$
- $q=+30.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, $M=-3.00$
- $q=-30.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, $M=-3.00$

## Explanation

The thin lens equation gives
$$
q=\frac{fp}{p-f}=\frac{15.0\cdot10.0}{10.0-15.0}\,\mathrm{cm}=-30.0\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
The magnification is therefore $M=-\dfrac qp=-\dfrac{-30.0}{10.0}=+3.00$. Since $q$ is negative, the image is virtual and forms on the same side as the object. Since $M$ is positive and greater than $1$, the image is upright and magnified, as for every object placed between the lens and the focus. The value $q=+6.00\,\mathrm{cm}$ is obtained by adding $\dfrac1f$ and $\dfrac1p$ instead of subtracting them.
