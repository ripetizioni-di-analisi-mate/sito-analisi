A sample initially contains equal numbers of two independent radionuclides with half-lives $T$ and $2T$. What total fraction remains after time $T$, counting only these initial nuclei?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$.
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- $\dfrac12\left(\dfrac12+\dfrac1{\sqrt2}\right)$.

## Explanation

The first component retains one half; the second retains $2^{-\dfrac{1}{2}}=\dfrac1{\sqrt2}$. Since the initial populations are equal, the total fraction is the average of the surviving fractions, that is $\dfrac12\left(\dfrac12+\dfrac1{\sqrt2}\right)\simeq0.604$. Do not replace the mixture with one average half-life: a sum of different exponentials generally differs from one exponential.
