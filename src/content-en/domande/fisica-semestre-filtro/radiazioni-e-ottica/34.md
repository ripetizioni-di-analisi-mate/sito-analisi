A narrow detector measures lower intensity after a slab because some photons scatter out of the beam. Which conclusion is correct?

- Loss from the direct beam does not necessarily equal energy fully absorbed in the slab.
- Every unrecorded photon deposited all its energy in the slab.
- Scattering cannot contribute to an attenuation coefficient.
- Slab dose follows from the ratio $\dfrac{I}{I_0}$ alone.

## Explanation

A scattered photon can leave the collection geometry while still carrying energy. Narrow-beam attenuation includes removal by scattering as well as absorption. Dose requires actually deposited energy and mass: reduced direct-beam signal alone does not supply both.
