Two photons have equal energy: one comes from an inner-electron transition and the other from a nuclear transition. Which conclusion about the X-ray/gamma-ray distinction is justified?

- Equal energy rules out any distinction based on origin.
- Both must be gamma rays because their energies are equal.
- Their origin can distinguish them even when energies coincide.
- The electronic photon must have longer wavelength even at equal energy.

## Explanation

X rays and gamma rays can overlap in energy; production mechanism is an important physical distinction. Equal energies imply equal frequencies, and both travel at $c$ in vacuum. Electronic transitions are a production mechanism for characteristic X rays.
