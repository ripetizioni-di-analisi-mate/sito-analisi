In a sinusoidal progressive plane wave in vacuum, the electric component is zero at a certain point and time. What does this imply for the wave’s magnetic component there and then?

- It is zero: the fields oscillate in phase.
- It is nonzero because all the wave energy becomes magnetic whenever the electric field vanishes.
- Its value depends only on frequency, not phase.
- It is at its positive maximum: the fields differ by a quarter period.

## Explanation

In the stated model the components are proportional at each instant, with amplitude ratio $c$. Their zeros coincide. Perpendicularity concerns spatial directions, not temporal phase difference; a magnetic maximum at an electric zero would confuse these two properties.
