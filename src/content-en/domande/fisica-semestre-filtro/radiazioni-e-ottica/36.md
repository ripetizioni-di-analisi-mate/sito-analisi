In a $1.00\,\mathrm{cm}$ cuvette, absorbance increases by $0.150$ for each $1.00\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ of solute. What is $\varepsilon$ in $\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}$?

- $0.150\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}$.
- $150\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}$.
- $1500\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}$.
- $6.67\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}$.

## Explanation

The slope against concentration in moles per litre is $\dfrac{0.150}{0.00100}=150\,\mathrm{L/mol}$. It equals $\varepsilon\ell$, giving the stated coefficient for $\ell=1.00\,\mathrm{cm}$. The number $0.150$ would use millimole-based units, not mole-based units; the reciprocal slope is not the coefficient.
