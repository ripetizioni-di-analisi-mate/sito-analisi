Two filters described by independent transmittances at the same frequency give different measured results when their order is reversed. Which assessment is correct?

- The first filter always removes more absolute power, so reversing the filters must change the transmitted fraction.
- The same final result is predicted, so the difference can be ignored without checking the experiment.
- The simple product model is insufficient: an assumption or the measurement must be reconsidered.
- The difference necessarily proves higher incident frequency in the first case.

## Explanation

The independent model predicts $\mathcal T_1\mathcal T_2=\mathcal T_2\mathcal T_1$. A difference requires checking, for example, inter-element reflections, geometry or source stability. The measurement alone identifies no unique cause, and the mathematical operation cannot be changed arbitrarily to fit the result.
