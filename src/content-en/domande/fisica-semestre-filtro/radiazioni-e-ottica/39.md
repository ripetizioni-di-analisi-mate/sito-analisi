For a passive nonluminescent sample, $\dfrac{I}{I_0}=1.08$ is measured, where $I_0$ should be the correct reference at equal geometry. What conclusion is initially justified?

- Sample absorbance must be $+1.08$.
- The attenuation coefficient must be positive and the measurement is still compatible.
- The reading certainly identifies an intrinsic negative coefficient without checking the reference.
- Check the reference and measurement before interpreting the result as attenuation.

## Explanation

In the correctly referenced passive model, $\mathcal T=e^{-\mu x}\le1$ for $\mu\ge0$. The value exceeds one and gives negative absorbance, requiring checks of blank, normalization, geometry and uncertainty. It does not alone demonstrate physical amplification; absorbance and transmittance are also different quantities.
