Two radionuclides give straight lines of $\ln(\dfrac{N}{N_0})$ against time, with slopes $-0.10$ and $-0.20\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}$. How are their half-lives related?

- They cannot be compared without knowing initial populations.
- The half-lives are equal because both lines start at zero.
- The second half-life is twice the first.
- The second half-life is half the first.

## Explanation

The slope is $-\lambda_d$ and $T_{1/2}=\dfrac{\ln2}{\lambda_d}$. Doubling slope magnitude halves half-life. Normalization by $N_0$ removes initial population from the slope; the zero intercept only says that the initial ratio is one.
