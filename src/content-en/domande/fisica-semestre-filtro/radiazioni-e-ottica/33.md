A polychromatic beam crosses a material strongly attenuating one component and weakly attenuating another. Why can one constant coefficient inferred from the first layer fail for subsequent layers?

- Individual component coefficients must increase because the first layer has already been crossed.
- Every unabsorbed photon must halve its energy in each layer.
- The proportions of transmitted components change with thickness.
- The initial coefficient average remains exact even when component weights change.

## Explanation

The more strongly attenuated component is depleted faster, so the beam reaching the next layer differs from the initial beam. Each component may still follow its own exponential, but their sum is generally not one exponential. No continuous energy loss by every transmitted photon need be assumed.
