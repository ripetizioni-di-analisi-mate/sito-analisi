A ray in glass, $n_1=1.50$, reaches an interface with air, $n_2=1.00$, at $60.0^\circ$ from the normal. Which description is correct in the ray model?

- A grazing transmitted ray, because every incidence beyond $41.8^\circ$ gives $90^\circ$.
- Total internal reflection: incidence exceeds about $41.8^\circ$.
- Refraction at $60.0^\circ$ because frequency does not change.
- Refraction at about $35.3^\circ$ from the normal.

## Explanation

The critical angle is $\theta_c=\arcsin(\dfrac{1}{1.50})\simeq41.8^\circ$. At $60^\circ$, Snell would require $\sin\theta_t=1.50\sin60^\circ>1$, so no propagating transmitted ray exists. A grazing ray occurs only at the critical angle, not at all larger incidences; unchanged frequency does not require equal angles.
