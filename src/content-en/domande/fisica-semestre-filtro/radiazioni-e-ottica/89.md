A real object slowly approaches the focus of a converging lens while keeping $p>f$. What happens to real-image distance in the thin-lens model?

- It stays equal to $f$ at every position.
- It increases and tends to infinity as $p$ approaches $f$ from above.
- It decreases to zero.
- It becomes negative before the object reaches the focus.

## Explanation

$q=\dfrac{fp}{p-f}$ has a positive denominator tending to zero while its numerator tends to $f^2$. Distance grows without bound in the ideal model. The change to $q<0$ occurs only after crossing to $p<f$; for very distant objects, instead, $q$ approaches $f$.
