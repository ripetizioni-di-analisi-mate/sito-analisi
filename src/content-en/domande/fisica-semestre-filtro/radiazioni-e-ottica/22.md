Two sources emit equal numbers of photons per second, one at $450\,\mathrm{nm}$ and one at $600\,\mathrm{nm}$ in vacuum. What is $\dfrac{P_{450}}{P_{600}}$?

- $\dfrac{4}{3}$.
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$.
- $\dfrac{16}{9}$.
- $1$.

## Explanation

At equal photon-number rate, $P=\dfrac{\dot N_\gamma hc}{\lambda_0}$, so the ratio is $\dfrac{600}{450}=\dfrac{4}{3}$. Equal counts do not guarantee equal power because photon energies differ. Squaring the wavelength ratio would incorrectly apply the amplitude–intensity law to a different pair of quantities.
