The mean exponential curve stays positive at every finite time. What does this imply for an actual small sample of independent nuclei?

- The sample may already have no survivors: the mean is not every realization’s count.
- The final nucleus becomes stable when left alone.
- At least one nucleus must remain forever in every sample.
- The law necessarily predicts physical fractions of a nucleus in the sample.

## Explanation

A mean value, for example below one, averages integer counts over many realizations. Some samples may be empty while others contain survivors. Positivity of the mean neither stabilizes the last nucleus nor requires every sample to maintain a positive population.
