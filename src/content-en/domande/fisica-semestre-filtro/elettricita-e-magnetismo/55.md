An ideal source maintains $9\,\mathrm V$ across its load. The ohmic load changes from $3\,\Omega$ to $9\,\Omega$. How does supplied current change?

- From $3\,\mathrm A$ to $1\,\mathrm A$.
- From $1\,\mathrm A$ to $3\,\mathrm A$.
- It remains $3\,\mathrm A$.
- From $3\,\mathrm A$ to $9\,\mathrm A$.

## Explanation

An ideal voltage source fixes $\Delta V$, not $I$. Ohm’s law gives initially $\dfrac{9}{3}=3\,\mathrm A$ and then $\dfrac{9}{9}=1\,\mathrm A$. Tripling resistance at unchanged voltage reduces current to one third; supplied current is not conserved between two different circuits.
