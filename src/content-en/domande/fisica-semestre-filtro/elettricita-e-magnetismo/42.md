A positive rod remains near an initially neutral conductor. The conductor is grounded, then disconnected from ground, and finally the rod is removed. What net charge remains on the conductor?

- Negative.
- Necessarily equal in magnitude to the rod’s charge.
- Positive.
- Zero, because the rod never touched it.

## Explanation

During grounding, electrons enter because they are attracted by the rod. Disconnecting ground before removing the rod traps the electron excess, which then redistributes. Rod contact is unnecessary; the magnitude depends on geometry and need not equal that of the external source.
