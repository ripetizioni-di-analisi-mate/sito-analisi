Two unequal resistors necessarily carry the same current because they are in series without intermediate branches. Which quantity need not be equal?

- The voltage drop across the two resistors.
- Current entering and leaving their common node in steady conditions.
- Current magnitude in the two resistors.
- Charge passing through each during the same steady interval.

## Explanation

Charge conservation prevents indefinite accumulation at the node, so current is common. Voltage drops are instead $\Delta V_1=IR_1$ and $\Delta V_2=IR_2$, unequal when resistances differ and current is nonzero. Series does not mean equality of every electrical quantity.
