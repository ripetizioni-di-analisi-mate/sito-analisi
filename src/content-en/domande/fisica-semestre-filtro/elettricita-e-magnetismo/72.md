Voltage across an ideal linear capacitor doubles without changing its geometry or material. What change occurs?

- Doubled capacitance, doubled charge, doubled energy.
- Unchanged capacitance, quadrupled charge, doubled energy.
- Halved capacitance, unchanged charge, doubled energy.
- Unchanged capacitance, doubled charge, quadrupled energy.

## Explanation

Capacitance is a property of geometry and material in the linear regime. From $q=C\Delta V$, charge doubles, while $U_C=\dfrac{C(\Delta V)^2}{2}$ gives a factor four for energy. Defining capacitance as a ratio does not make it vary when charge and voltage increase proportionally together.
