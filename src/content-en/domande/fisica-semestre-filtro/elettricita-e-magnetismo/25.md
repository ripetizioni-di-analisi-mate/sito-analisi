A positive charge enters a uniform electric field with perpendicular velocity. All other forces are negligible. Why does its trajectory not coincide with a field line?

- Electric force is always perpendicular to velocity like magnetic force.
- The field determines acceleration; the initial velocity component perpendicular to it also remains.
- The initial perpendicular velocity component vanishes immediately.
- The field directly determines velocity, which is always parallel to it.

## Explanation

There is no force along the initial velocity direction, so that component remains constant. Along the field, velocity changes under constant acceleration: combining the motions gives a parabola. Field lines represent force directions on positive test charges, not the motion’s initial conditions.
