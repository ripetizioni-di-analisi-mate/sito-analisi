An electron initially moves along a nonzero uniform electric field. With no other forces, what happens before any reversal of motion?

- It slows because acceleration opposes its initial velocity.
- Speed stays constant because electric force does no work.
- Its speed increases because the field follows its velocity.
- It immediately follows a circle at constant speed.

## Explanation

The electron’s charge is negative, so force opposes both the field and its initial velocity. Initial electric work is negative and kinetic energy decreases. If the uniform field persists long enough, velocity reaches zero and then reverses; this reasoning differs from the magnetic-force case.
