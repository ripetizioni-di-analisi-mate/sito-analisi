A closed conducting enclosure is in electrostatic equilibrium and contains an empty charge-free cavity. What effect does an external electrostatic field produce inside the cavity?

- The cavity field remains zero because of shielding.
- The cavity field always equals the external field.
- Shielding necessarily requires direct current in the enclosure.
- The cavity must have zero potential even if the enclosure is not grounded.

## Explanation

Conductor charges redistribute until equilibrium is reached, shielding a closed cavity without internal sources. Potential in the cavity is constant but not necessarily zero. The charge-free condition prevents incorrectly applying the conclusion to a cavity containing its own source.
