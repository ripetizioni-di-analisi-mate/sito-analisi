An ideal capacitor is charged through a resistor from a DC source. After the transient, at constant voltage, which statement is correct?

- Current stays constant because the capacitor maintains voltage.
- The dielectric conducts the same current as an ideal wire.
- Capacitor energy must be zero because current is zero.
- Conduction current in the branch becomes zero and the capacitor can remain charged.

## Explanation

At constant voltage and capacitance, charge no longer changes, so no current is needed in the charging branch. Opposite plate charges and the field between them remain, so energy can stay stored. Transient wire current does not imply free charges crossing an ideal dielectric.
