An ideal nonzero voltage source is connected across a wire with exactly zero resistance. What does the ideal model indicate?

- Current numerically equals voltage regardless of units.
- The source simultaneously maintains nonzero voltage and arbitrary finite current.
- Current is zero because resistance is zero.
- There is no finite-current solution; real resistances are needed to describe the case.

## Explanation

The relation $\Delta V=RI$ with $R=0$ and finite current would require zero voltage, incompatible with the specified ideal source. The limit as resistance tends to zero suggests unbounded current. A real short circuit requires at least internal and connection resistance, omitted from the model.
