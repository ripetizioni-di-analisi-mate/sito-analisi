A resistive metal wire carries a steady direct current. Why cannot the electrostatic-equilibrium rule be used to impose zero internal electric field?

- The wire is not in electrostatic equilibrium: a field sustains transport with dissipation.
- Random thermal motion alone produces drift, even without an applied field.
- Steady current requires every electron to accelerate forever without collisions.
- Voltage is maintained along the wire without any local field.

## Explanation

Steady means that macroscopic quantities do not change with time, not that charges are in electrostatic equilibrium. In an ohmic wire, the field transfers energy to carriers, which interact with the material. Average drift can remain constant while energy is dissipated.
