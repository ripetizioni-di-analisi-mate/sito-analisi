A charge $q=-2\,\mu\mathrm C$ moves from $10\,\mathrm V$ to $30\,\mathrm V$ with no work by other forces. How much does its kinetic energy change?

- It decreases by $40\,\mu\mathrm J$.
- It increases by $40\,\mu\mathrm J$.
- It stays constant because the charge is negative.
- It increases by $80\,\mu\mathrm J$.

## Explanation

Here $\Delta V=20\,\mathrm V$ and $\Delta U=q\Delta V=-40\,\mu\mathrm J$. Therefore $\Delta K=-\Delta U=+40\,\mu\mathrm J$. A negative charge can gain kinetic energy while moving towards higher potential; potential’s sign alone does not determine energy’s sign.
