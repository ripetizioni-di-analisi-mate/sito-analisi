A second resistor is added in parallel with a first resistor already powered by an ideal voltage source. What happens to current through the first resistor?

- It doubles regardless of the new resistor.
- It becomes zero because the branches have equal voltage.
- It remains unchanged; total source current increases.
- It decreases because the original current must split equally.

## Explanation

The first resistor retains its resistance and voltage, so $I_1=\dfrac{\Delta V}{R_1}$ is unchanged. The new branch adds to total current demanded from the ideal source. Dividing a previously fixed total current would apply to a different constraint, not the voltage source specified here.
