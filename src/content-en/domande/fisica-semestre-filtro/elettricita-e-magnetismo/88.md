The flux through a loop of constant resistance $R$ changes by the same amount in two trials. In the second trial the change takes twice as long. Neglecting self-inductance, how does the net charge passing a point of the loop during the change compare?

- It is the same in both trials.
- It is twice as large in the second.
- It is half as large in the second.
- It depends only on the final flux.

## Explanation

Current is the induced electromotive force divided by $R$. Integrating Faraday's law over time gives net charge $-\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{R}$. The result depends on the flux change and resistance.

Theory — [Faraday–Neumann–Lenz law](/en/fisica-semestre-filtro/elettricita-e-magnetismo/teoria#induzione).
