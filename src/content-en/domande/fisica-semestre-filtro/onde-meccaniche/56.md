Why can a solid support longitudinal and transverse elastic waves at different speeds, whereas an ideal-fluid model does not support shear waves?

- A solid also supplies shear restoring forces; different modes involve different elastic moduli.
- The distinction depends only on frequency: at equal frequency both modes always have equal speed.
- The difference is due only to higher solid density, with no role for elastic moduli.
- An ideal fluid resists shear like a solid, but all shear waves are absorbed instantaneously.

## Explanation

Propagation requires inertia and a restoring force for the deformation considered. A solid has shear rigidity; an ideal fluid has no static shear modulus even though it resists compression. Different speeds depend on the relevant moduli together with density, not frequency alone. The absence of a shear mode in this model should not be interpreted as instantaneous absorption of an ordinary wave.
