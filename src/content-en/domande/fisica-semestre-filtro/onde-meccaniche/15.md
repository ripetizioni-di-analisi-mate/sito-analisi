An oscillator crosses the same interior position twice in succession with opposite velocities. Is the interval between those passages necessarily the period?

- No: that time is always exactly one quarter-period.
- Yes, provided the position is not equilibrium.
- Yes: repeating coordinate is sufficient.
- No: one period requires the same state, including velocity.

## Explanation

An oscillator’s state requires position and velocity. Passing through the same position in opposite directions generally corresponds to different phases rather than a complete cycle. At equilibrium the two passages are half a period apart; elsewhere the interval depends on the chosen position.
