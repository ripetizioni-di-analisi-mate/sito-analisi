Two waves in different media have the same wavelength. What can be concluded about their frequencies without knowing their speeds?

- The ratio is $\dfrac{f_2}{f_1}=\dfrac{v_1}{v_2}$.
- They must be equal.
- They cannot be compared: $\dfrac{f_2}{f_1}=\dfrac{v_2}{v_1}$.
- The ratio is $\dfrac{f_2}{f_1}=(\dfrac{v_2}{v_1})^2$.

## Explanation

At a common wavelength, $f=\dfrac{v}{\lambda}$ makes frequency directly proportional to speed in each medium. Without those speeds, the larger frequency cannot be identified. The ratio is neither inverted nor squared; equal wavelength alone does not require equal frequency.
