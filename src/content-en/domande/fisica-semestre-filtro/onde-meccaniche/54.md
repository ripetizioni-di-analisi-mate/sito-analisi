A liquid is denser than a gas. Is that sufficient to conclude that sound travels more slowly in the liquid?

- Yes: all liquids have the same modulus as gases.
- No: density never enters sound speed.
- No: elastic response through the appropriate modulus is also needed.
- Yes: speed depends only on $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt\rho}$.

## Explanation

The formula contains the stiffness-to-density ratio. Greater density increases inertia, but much greater stiffness can dominate and produce higher speed. Comparing different materials does not justify silently holding modulus fixed without supporting information.
