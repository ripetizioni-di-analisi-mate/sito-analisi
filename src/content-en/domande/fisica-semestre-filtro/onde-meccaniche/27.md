A mechanical source vibrates inside a vessel whose medium is ideally removed to leave a vacuum. What prevents sound transmission across the empty space?

- Vacuum rapidly dissipates an already travelling sound wave’s energy as heat.
- The medium’s particles and interactions transmitting the disturbance are absent.
- Without external pressure any source necessarily stops vibrating.
- The lack of reflecting walls also prevents direct propagation.

## Explanation

Sound is a mechanical disturbance transmitted by medium inertia and elastic interactions. In vacuum that medium is absent: this is not a wave undergoing rapid absorption. The source can still vibrate, and direct propagation where a medium exists does not require wall reflections. Any vibration transmitted by the container would instead follow a solid path.
