Two opposite-sign pulses overlap on an ideal linear nondispersive string, making the total profile zero at one instant. What happens after the encounter?

- Both pulses continue in their respective directions with their original shapes.
- They exchange velocities and return to the sources like rigid bodies.
- They merge into a stationary pulse with amplitude equal to the sum of magnitudes.
- Both disappear permanently because displacement was zero.

## Explanation

In a linear medium each solution continues propagating, and their sum describes the total disturbance. Zero displacement at one instant does not require zero medium velocity or eliminate either contribution. The pulses’ later separation is consistent with energy conservation in the ideal system. Shape is preserved by the nondispersive assumption; linearity alone guarantees superposition but does not exclude pulse spreading during propagation.
