A sound rarefaction in air has $p^{\prime}<0$, where $p^{\prime}$ is the departure from equilibrium pressure $p_0$. What does this mean?

- Mean wave flow must reverse direction at every rarefaction.
- Absolute pressure is necessarily negative.
- Absolute pressure $p_0+p^{\prime}$ is below $p_0$ but can remain positive.
- Absolute pressure magnitude is always $|p^{\prime}|$.

## Explanation

The sign refers to a departure, not absolute pressure. In the small-disturbance regime, $|p^{\prime}|$ is much smaller than $p_0$, so their sum stays positive. A rarefaction has pressure and density below equilibrium; this does not reverse frequency or propagation. Perturbation magnitude is not absolute pressure: the equilibrium term is missing.
