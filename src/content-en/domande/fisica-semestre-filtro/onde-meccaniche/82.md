Two equal waves travel in the same direction in the same medium. Relative to one alone, what level increases occur when they are coherent in phase and, respectively, incoherent?

- $3.01\,\mathrm{dB}$ / $6.02\,\mathrm{dB}$.
- $3.01\,\mathrm{dB}$ / $3.01\,\mathrm{dB}$.
- $6.02\,\mathrm{dB}$ / $3.01\,\mathrm{dB}$.
- $6.02\,\mathrm{dB}$ / $6.02\,\mathrm{dB}$.

## Explanation

In the coherent in-phase case, amplitudes add steadily: doubled amplitude gives quadrupled intensity. In the incoherent case the averaged cross term vanishes and intensity doubles. The two distinct increases follow. The direction conditions avoid confusing local standing-wave pressure with net energy flux.
