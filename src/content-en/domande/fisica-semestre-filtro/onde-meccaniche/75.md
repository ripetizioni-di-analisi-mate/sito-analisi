A sensor is very close to a large loudspeaker. Is it always justified to predict a fourfold intensity decrease when distance doubles?

- No: near the source intensity must always increase with distance.
- No: conditions must justify the point-source approximation and the observed field.
- Yes: the law depends only on intensity units.
- Yes: every sound source is isotropic at any distance.

## Explanation

The inverse-square law does not follow from intensity’s definition alone. It requires suitable geometry and negligible effects such as near-field interference, reflection or absorption. An extended source observed very closely does not automatically satisfy the point-source model. This does not imply a universal alternative growth law.
