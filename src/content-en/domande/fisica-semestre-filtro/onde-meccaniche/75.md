According to the inverse-square law, doubling the distance from the source reduces the sound intensity to a quarter. In which situation does this law describe well the sound emitted by a loudspeaker?

- At distances small compared with the size of the loudspeaker.
- At distances large compared with the size of the loudspeaker, outdoors and in a medium that does not absorb.
- At any distance, because the law follows from the definition of intensity.
- In a closed room, where the sound is reflected by the walls.

## Explanation

The law is derived by assuming that the emitted power is spread over spherical surfaces of area $4\pi r^2$, which grow as the square of the distance. This model requires a source that is small compared with the distance and a medium that does not absorb energy. At distances large compared with its size the loudspeaker can be treated as a small source, so outdoors and without absorption the law applies. Close to the loudspeaker the source is not small compared with the distance, so the model is not adequate. In a closed room the sound reflected by the walls adds to the direct sound, so the intensity decreases more slowly than the law predicts.
