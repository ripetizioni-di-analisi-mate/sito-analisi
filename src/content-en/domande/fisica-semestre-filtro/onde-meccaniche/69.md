Two plane travelling waves in different media have the same $p_{\mathrm{rms}}$. In the second medium $\rho v$ is twice as large. How do their intensities compare?

- $I_2=\dfrac{I_1}{2}$.
- $I_2=I_1$.
- $I_2=2I_1$.
- $I_2=\dfrac{I_1}{4}$.

## Explanation

At fixed RMS pressure, $I=\dfrac{p_{\mathrm{rms}}^2}{\rho v}$ is inversely proportional to acoustic impedance. Doubling the denominator halves intensity. Pressure alone does not determine energy flux without medium properties. The square applies to pressure, not impedance.
