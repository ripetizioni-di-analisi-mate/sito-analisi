A graph of sound pressure versus position is sinusoidal. Does this make sound in a fluid a transverse wave?

- No: pressure being scalar is sufficient to classify any wave as longitudinal.
- Yes: transverse means the signal oscillates above and below equilibrium.
- Yes: every crest-and-trough graph represents transverse displacement.
- No: classification compares particle motion with propagation.

## Explanation

The graph’s vertical coordinate is pressure, not the medium’s physical height. In fluid sound, particles move back and forth along propagation: the wave is longitudinal. Neither the graph’s mathematical shape nor frequency changes that geometric comparison. Graph shape and the scalar nature of pressure do not replace comparison of physical directions.
