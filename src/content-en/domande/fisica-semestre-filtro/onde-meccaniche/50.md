Two coherent sources produce an interference pattern in a medium that does not absorb energy. At a point $P$ there is an intensity minimum and at a point $Q$ a maximum. Which statement about the energy of the waves is correct?

- At point $P$ the energy of the waves is destroyed.
- The energy is distributed differently in space and its total amount is conserved.
- At point $Q$ extra energy is created beyond that emitted by the sources.
- At point $P$ all the energy of the waves is converted into heat.

## Explanation

By the superposition principle, the resulting displacement at every point is the sum of the displacements of the two waves. At $P$ the waves arrive in antiphase, so the resulting amplitude is smaller and the intensity is less than the sum of the intensities of the two waves. At $Q$ the waves arrive in phase, so the intensity is greater than that sum. The medium does not absorb energy, so superposition only changes the way the energy is distributed in space. The intensity missing at the minima is therefore found at the maxima, and the total energy remains the energy emitted by the sources.
