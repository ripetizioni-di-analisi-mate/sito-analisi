A rigid body fully submerged in a homogeneous incompressible liquid is lowered further, without contact and remaining at equilibrium. How does buoyancy change?

- It remains unchanged.
- It doubles whenever absolute pressure doubles.
- It decreases because pressure also acts from above.
- It increases in proportion to depth.

## Explanation

Displaced volume and density remain constant, so $F_A=\rho gV$ does not change. Pressures around the surface increase with depth, but the uniform increment does not change their resultant. Rigidity matters: a compressible bubble could instead change volume and buoyancy.
