An ideal horizontal conduit narrows and then returns to its original area. In steady incompressible flow without pumps or turbines, with uniform speed across the sections considered, how do pressures before and after the whole section compare?

- Final pressure is higher because the conduit widens again.
- They are equal.
- Pressure is also constant in the throat.
- Final pressure is always lower solely because of the narrowing.

## Explanation

Initial and final area and flow rate are equal, so speeds are equal. At equal height Bernoulli returns the same pressure. Pressure drops in the throat and then ideally recovers. Permanent loss would require dissipation, excluded by the stated model.
