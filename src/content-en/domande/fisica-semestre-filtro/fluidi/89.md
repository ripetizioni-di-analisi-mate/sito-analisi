In a vertical pipe a Newtonian liquid is at rest, yet bottom pressure exceeds top pressure. Why cannot flow be inferred from the horizontal relation $Q=\dfrac{\Delta p}{R_h}$ alone?

- Gauge pressures at both ends are equal even though absolute pressures differ.
- Gravity must add to the pressure drop, increasing the driving contribution.
- The hydrostatic difference is balanced by the gravitational contribution to the driving balance.
- A static-friction threshold in the liquid must be exceeded to start flow.

## Explanation

Vertically, the change in $p+\rho gz$ matters, not static pressure alone. At hydrostatic equilibrium this sum is constant, so there is no net driving difference. Horizontal Poiseuille omits gravity because heights are equal; transferring it without correction creates a false prediction.
