The same body floats freely first in fresh water and then in a denser liquid, without absorbing either. How do buoyancy and submerged volume change?

- Greater buoyancy and equal submerged volume in the denser liquid.
- Both buoyancy and submerged volume are greater in the denser liquid.
- Equal buoyancy, smaller submerged volume in the denser liquid.
- Smaller buoyancy and greater submerged volume in the denser liquid.

## Explanation

In both equilibria buoyancy equals the same weight. From $\rho_f gV_{\mathrm{sub}}=mg$ follows $V_{\mathrm{sub}}=\dfrac{m}{\rho_f}$: greater density requires less submerged volume. Saying buoyancy increases at fixed volume would describe another situation, not these two freely floating equilibria.
