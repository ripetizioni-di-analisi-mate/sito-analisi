Two open vessels contain the same liquid at rest to the same height above the bottom; one widens upwards and the other is cylindrical. How do bottom pressures compare?

- They are equal if atmospheric pressure is the same.
- It is greater in the vessel holding more liquid.
- They are equal only if volumes are equal.
- It is greater in the vessel with the smaller bottom.

## Explanation

The law $p=p_0+\rho gh$ contains density and depth, not total volume or shape. Bottom pressure is therefore equal. Its force must not be equated with the entire liquid weight: inclined walls can supply vertical components completing equilibrium.
