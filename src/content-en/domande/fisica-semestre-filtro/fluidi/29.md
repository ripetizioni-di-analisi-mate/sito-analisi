A closed empty metal box floats even though the metal is denser than water. Neglecting external air and surface tension, which quantity governs whether it can float?

- Only base area, independently of mass.
- Total mass divided by metal volume alone.
- Total mass divided by the maximum external volume it can displace.
- Only the density of the wall metal.

## Explanation

Buoyancy depends on water excluded by the box, including its sealed cavity, while weight depends on total mass. Average density relative to external volume therefore matters. A closed box can displace enough water before being submerged; if water enters the cavity, the system and balance change.
