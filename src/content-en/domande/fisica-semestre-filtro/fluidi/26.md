In a hydraulic circuit at rest, one piston is $2.0\,\mathrm m$ above the other. Gauge pressure below the lower piston is $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what gauge pressure acts below the upper piston?

- $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $180\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $220\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

## Explanation

Moving upwards lowers pressure by $\rho g\Delta h=20000\,\mathrm{Pa}=20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Thus $p_{\mathrm{upper}}=200-20=180\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Pascal transmits an added change but does not cancel the existing hydrostatic gradient. Equal pressures below pistons require equal heights or negligible corrections.
