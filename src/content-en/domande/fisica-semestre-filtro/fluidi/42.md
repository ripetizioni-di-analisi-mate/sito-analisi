For $4\,\mathrm s$ a deformable segment receives $5\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ and releases $3\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ of incompressible liquid. How does its contained volume change?

- It decreases by $8\,\mathrm{mL}$.
- It does not change because the liquid is incompressible.
- It increases by $32\,\mathrm{mL}$.
- It increases by $8\,\mathrm{mL}$.

## Explanation

The accumulation balance is $\Delta V=(Q_{\mathrm{in}}-Q_{\mathrm{out}})\Delta t=(5-3)\cdot4=8\,\mathrm{mL}$. Incompressibility maintains density, not the volume of a deformable container. Equal inflow and outflow would require no accumulation, which does not hold here.
