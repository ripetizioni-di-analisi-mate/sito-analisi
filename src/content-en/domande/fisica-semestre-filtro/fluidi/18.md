At the same depth in water at rest, two small surfaces have areas $A$ and $2A$ and different orientations. Neglecting depth changes within each, which statement holds for water forces on one side?

- Both forces are vertical because liquid weight is vertical.
- Pressure on the second is doubled and force magnitudes are equal.
- Pressure is equal; the second force has twice the magnitude and each is normal to its own surface.
- Force magnitudes are equal because depth is equal.

## Explanation

Hydrostatic pressure is a scalar depending on depth. Force is a vector: for uniform pressure its magnitude is $pA$ and its direction is normal to the surface. Doubling area doubles magnitude, while changing orientation changes force direction rather than pressure.
