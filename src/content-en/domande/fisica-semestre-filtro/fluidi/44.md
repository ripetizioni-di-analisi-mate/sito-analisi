Water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ rises by $2.0\,\mathrm m$ in steady ideal flow while retaining the same speed along a streamline, without pumps or turbines. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is $p_2-p_1$?

- $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $0\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $-2\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
- $-20\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

## Explanation

Kinetic terms cancel, but gravitational terms do not. Bernoulli gives $p_2-p_1=-\rho g(z_2-z_1)=-20000\,\mathrm{Pa}=-20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Pressure work supports the potential-energy increase. Equal speed does not imply equal pressure when heights differ.
