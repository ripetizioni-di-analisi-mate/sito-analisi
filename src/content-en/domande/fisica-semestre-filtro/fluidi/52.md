A large closed tank contains water, and the air above the free surface is at a pressure $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ higher than atmospheric pressure. A small side hole is so close to the surface that the height difference between them is negligible, and the speed of the surface is negligible. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and an ideal fluid, at what speed does the water leave the hole?

- $\sqrt{20}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq4.47\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $40\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\sqrt{40}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq6.32\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Bernoulli's equation is applied between the free surface and the outlet of the hole. At the surface the speed is negligible, while the jet leaves at atmospheric pressure, so the pressure at the surface exceeds that of the jet by $\Delta p=20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Since the two points are at the same height, the term $\rho gz$ is the same at both points and the pressure difference becomes entirely kinetic energy per unit volume, that is $\Delta p=\dfrac12\rho v^2$. It follows that
$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{2\,\Delta p}{\rho}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot20000}{1000}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=\sqrt{40}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq6.32\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $\sqrt{20}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $\dfrac12$ in the kinetic energy, and the value $40\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by forgetting the square root.
