For a single flat interface at equilibrium with uniform tension, what follows from Young-Laplace?

- Pressure cannot vary with height away from the interface.
- The pressure jump due to curvature is zero.
- Surface tension must be zero.
- Absolute pressure in both phases is zero.

## Explanation

Both principal curvatures vanish, so $\Delta p=\gamma(0+0)=0$. A local difference across the interface vanishes, not absolute pressure. Tension may remain finite and pressures in both phases may vary hydrostatically away from the interface level.
