What does a small Reynolds number in a conduit primarily indicate?

- Flow is certainly fully developed even at the inlet.
- The fluid is necessarily incompressible and Newtonian.
- Viscous effects are relatively important compared with inertial effects.
- Inertial effects dominate over viscous effects.

## Explanation

Reynolds compares inertia with viscosity, and a small value favours laminar flow. It does not establish geometry, profile development, incompressibility or fluid constitutive law. These conditions require separate checks before using Poiseuille or transferring an experimental threshold to a biological vessel.
