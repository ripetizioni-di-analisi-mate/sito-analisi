In an ideal press with negligible hydrostatic differences, the large piston area is $20$ times the small area. The small piston moves $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. How far does the large piston move?

- $0.50\,\mathrm{cm}$.
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$.
- $200\,\mathrm{cm}$.
- $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.

## Explanation

The incompressible liquid conserves transferred volume: $A_1s_1=A_2s_2$. Thus $s_2=\dfrac{s_1}{20}=0.50\,\mathrm{cm}$. Force advantage is paid for by a shorter large-piston stroke. Increasing both force and stroke by the same factor would violate the ideal press work balance.
