A gas of negligible density is connected to one arm of a U-tube manometer, with the other open to atmosphere. The liquid level is lower on the gas side. Which conclusion is correct?

- Gas pressure exceeds atmospheric pressure.
- The direction of the level difference alone determines absolute gas pressure.
- Gas pressure is below atmospheric pressure.
- Gas pressure equals atmospheric pressure.

## Explanation

Higher pressure pushes liquid down in the gas-connected arm. Comparing equal-height points in the same liquid gives $p_{\mathrm{gas}}-p_{\mathrm{atm}}=\rho g\Delta h>0$. The direction gives the sign, but a value requires density, height difference and, for absolute pressure, atmospheric pressure.
