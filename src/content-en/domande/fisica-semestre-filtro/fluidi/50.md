A small hole discharges ideally from a large open reservoir. If the instantaneous height difference between surface and hole quadruples, with other conditions unchanged, how does outflow speed change?

- It doubles.
- It halves.
- It quadruples.
- It increases by a factor of $16$.

## Explanation

Torricelli gives $v=\sqrt{2g\Delta h}$ for an almost stationary surface, common external pressure and negligible losses. The ratio is $\dfrac{v_2}{v_1}=\sqrt4=2$. Potential energy per unit mass grows linearly with height difference, whereas kinetic energy is proportional to squared speed.
