A pipe is replaced by a narrower one, with the same fluid and length, in the Poiseuille regime. It is unknown whether the pump maintains flow or pressure difference. Which conclusion is still justified?

- Pressure difference certainly stays equal because the fluid is the same.
- Resistance increases; flow change requires knowing the pump constraint.
- Flow certainly stays equal by continuity between two different experiments.
- Flow certainly decreases in the same proportion as radius.

## Explanation

Resistance rises as $\dfrac1{r^4}$ regardless of pump control. To infer $Q=\dfrac{\Delta p}{R_h}$, however, the change in $\Delta p$ is needed. Continuity connects sections of the same steady flow; it does not impose equal flow across experiments with different geometries and supply conditions.
