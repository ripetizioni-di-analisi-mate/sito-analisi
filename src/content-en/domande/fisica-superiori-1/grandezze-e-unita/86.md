A $40\,\mathrm{mL}$ measuring cup fills a container with constant cross section and base area $20\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. How much does the level rise with each pour?

- $800\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.002\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The liquid poured in takes up $40\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ because one millilitre is one cubic centimetre. The rise is the height of the layer that this volume forms over the base of $20\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. Compute $\dfrac{40}{20}=2\,\mathrm{cm}$. With a constant cross-section, equal pours always produce the same rise in level.
