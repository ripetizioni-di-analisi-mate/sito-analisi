A road is $4.3\,\mathrm{km}$ long. How many metres long is it?

- $430\,\mathrm m$
- $4300\,\mathrm m$
- $43\,\mathrm m$
- $0.0043\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The prefix kilo stands for $10^3$. The road is a thousand metres long for every kilometre in the given value, including any fraction of a kilometre. Therefore the numerical value is multiplied by a thousand. The length stays the same because the same road is counted in smaller units.

The result is $l=4.3\cdot1000\,\mathrm m=4300\,\mathrm m$.
