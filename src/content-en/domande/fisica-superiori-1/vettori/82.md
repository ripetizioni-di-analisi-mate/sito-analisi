The resultant magnitude is $3\,\mathrm N$ and one vector has magnitude $7\,\mathrm N$. Which magnitude for the other is impossible?

- $4\,\mathrm N$
- $2\,\mathrm N$
- $7\,\mathrm N$
- $10\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

The second magnitude must be at least $7-3=4$ for a resultant of three to be possible. Two is too small.

The largest admissible magnitude of the other vector is $7\,\mathrm N+3\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N$.

With a second magnitude of two, even pointing it in the opposite direction would leave $7-2=5\,\mathrm N$. The requested value of three is therefore unreachable in any direction.
