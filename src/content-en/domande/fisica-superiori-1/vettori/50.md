A route goes four metres east and three north. How much longer is the route than the straight distance from start to finish?

- $7\,\mathrm m$
- $2\,\mathrm m$
- $5\,\mathrm m$
- $3\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The travelled sections total seven metres. Pythagoras gives five for the direct distance, so subtract five from seven.

$4+3-5=2\,\mathrm m$.
