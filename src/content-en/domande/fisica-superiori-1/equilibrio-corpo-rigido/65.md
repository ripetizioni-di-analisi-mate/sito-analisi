Two blocks of equal weight have different base widths. Both centres of mass lie directly over the middle of their bases. A horizontal push acts at the same height on both, and friction prevents sliding. Which base requires a greater push to reach the tipping threshold?

- The wider base
- The narrower base
- They are equal
- The height of the centre of gravity is needed to tell

## Explanation

A wider base increases the lever arm of the weight about the edge. A stronger push is needed to balance the moment of the weight because the push is applied at the same height. With the centre of mass midway across the base, the lever arm is half the width. Doubling that width at fixed weight and height also doubles $F_{\mathrm{rib}}=\dfrac{Pb}{h}$, where $b$ is the weight's lever arm.
