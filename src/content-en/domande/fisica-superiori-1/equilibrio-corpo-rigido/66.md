A light rod has a $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass at its left end, at $x=0$, and a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass at its right end, at $x=4\,\mathrm m$. The masses swap ends. By how much does the centre-of-mass coordinate change, taking rightwards as positive?

- $+1\,\mathrm m$
- $+2\,\mathrm m$
- $-2\,\mathrm m$
- $+4\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The total mass stays at eight kilograms. Swapping moves the six-kilogram mass to the right and the two-kilogram mass the same distance to the left, so the larger mass dominates the change. The weighted mean moves from one metre to three metres, giving a positive change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{G,i}&=\frac{6\cdot0+2\cdot4}{8}\,\mathrm m=1\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
x_{G,f}&=\frac{2\cdot0+6\cdot4}{8}\,\mathrm m=3\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
\Delta x_G&=+2\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$
