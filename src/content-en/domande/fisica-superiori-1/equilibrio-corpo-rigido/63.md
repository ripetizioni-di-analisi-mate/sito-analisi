In a system of total mass $10\,\mathrm{kg}$, a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass slides from $x=-0.20\,\mathrm m$ to $x=1.30\,\mathrm m$ while everything else stays fixed. How much does the coordinate of the centre of mass change?

- $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.26\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The total mass is unchanged. Only the moving mass changes the numerator of the weighted mean, by $2[1.30-(-0.20)]=3\,\mathrm{kg\,m}$. Dividing by $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives a shift of $0.30\,\mathrm m$ to the right. The moving mass is one fifth of the system, so the centre moves one fifth of $1.50\,\mathrm m$.
