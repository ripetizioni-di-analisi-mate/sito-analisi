A system has total mass $12\,\mathrm{kg}$ and centre of mass at $x=2.5\,\mathrm m$. A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass at $x=4\,\mathrm m$ is removed, while all the other masses stay in place. Where is the centre of mass of the remaining system?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The initial first moment of mass is $12\cdot2.5=30\,\mathrm{kg\,m}$. The removed mass contributed $3\cdot4=12\,\mathrm{kg\,m}$, leaving $18\,\mathrm{kg\,m}$ and $9\,\mathrm{kg}$. Their ratio gives $2\,\mathrm m$. The shift to the left is consistent with removing mass to the right of the initial centre.
