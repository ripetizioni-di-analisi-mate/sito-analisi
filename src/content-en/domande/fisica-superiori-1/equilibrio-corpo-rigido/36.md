A horizontal bar of negligible weight rests on a point support underneath it at $O$. On the right there is a $30\,\mathrm N$ weight at $0.60\,\mathrm m$ and an upward pull from a vertical rope at $0.30\,\mathrm m$. The tension can be adjusted. Which description applies to equilibrium?

- The bar can stay at rest with a $30\,\mathrm N$ upward reaction
- The bar can stay at rest with a $60\,\mathrm N$ upward reaction
- The bar can stay at rest with zero reaction
- Equilibrium is impossible because the bar would need a $30\,\mathrm N$ downward reaction

## Explanation

Torque balance requires twice the weight in tension because its moment arm is half as long. At that value the rope exceeds the weight by thirty newtons, so a downward reaction would be needed. A support beneath the bar can only push it upwards.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T\cdot0.30\,\mathrm m&=30\,\mathrm N\cdot0.60\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
T&=60\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
R_y&=30\,\mathrm N-60\,\mathrm N=-30\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$
