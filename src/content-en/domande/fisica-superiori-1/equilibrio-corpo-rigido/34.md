On a horizontal lever of negligible weight, the frictionless pivot lies between the load and the point where the vertical effort acts. Both points of application stay fixed as the pivot moves along the lever towards the load, remaining between them. What happens to the effort needed for equilibrium?

- It increases
- It decreases
- It stays unchanged
- The exact lever arm lengths are needed to tell

## Explanation

The load arm decreases and the effort arm increases. Their ratio falls so the same load needs less effort. If the distance between the point of effort and the load is fixed and the pivot stays between them, moving it towards the load transfers the same length from the load arm to the effort arm.
