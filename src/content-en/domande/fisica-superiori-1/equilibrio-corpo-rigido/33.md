A sensor at the ideal pivot of a horizontal lever of negligible weight reads a $36\,\mathrm N$ upward reaction. Only two downward forces are applied to the lever in equilibrium. The left force is $24\,\mathrm N$ and acts $0.30\,\mathrm m$ from the pivot. How far from the pivot does the right force act?

- $0.60\,\mathrm m$
- $0.10\,\mathrm m$
- $0.15\,\mathrm m$
- $0.30\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

First use the vertical force balance to find the right force, because the sensor measures the sum of the two loads. The pivot reaction has a zero moment arm about the pivot. Equating the load torques shows that the smaller force must act at twice the distance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_2&=(36-24)\,\mathrm N=12\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
b&=\frac{24\,\mathrm N\cdot0.30\,\mathrm m}{12\,\mathrm N}=0.60\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$
