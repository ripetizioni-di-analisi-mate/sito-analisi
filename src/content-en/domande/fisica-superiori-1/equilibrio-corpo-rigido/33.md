A horizontal lever of negligible weight is in equilibrium on an ideal pivot, which exerts an upward reaction of $36\,\mathrm N$ on it. Apart from the pivot reaction, only two downward forces act on the lever. The left force is $24\,\mathrm N$ and acts $0.30\,\mathrm m$ from the pivot. How far from the pivot does the right force act?

- $0.60\,\mathrm m$
- $0.10\,\mathrm m$
- $0.15\,\mathrm m$
- $0.30\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The lever is in equilibrium, so the pivot reaction equals the sum of the two loads, and this gives the right load. The pivot reaction has a zero moment arm about the pivot, so only the two loads enter the balance of torques. The right load is half the left one, so it must act at twice the distance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_2&=(36-24)\,\mathrm N=12\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
b&=\frac{24\,\mathrm N\cdot0.30\,\mathrm m}{12\,\mathrm N}=0.60\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$
