A uniform rod $1.2\,\mathrm m$ long has mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$. A mass of $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from one of its ends. The rod, with the hanging mass, must stay balanced horizontally on a single support. At what distance from the loaded end must the support be placed?

- $0.6\,\mathrm m$
- $0.4\,\mathrm m$
- $0.2\,\mathrm m$
- $0.3\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

A body resting on a single point is in equilibrium when the total weight has zero moment about the support, that is when the support lies on the vertical through the centre of gravity. Positions are measured from the loaded end, so the hanging mass is at $x=0$. The rod is uniform, so in the calculation it counts as a mass of $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ placed at its midpoint, at $x=0.6\,\mathrm m$. The coordinate of the centre of gravity is then
$$
x_G=\frac{4\cdot0+2\cdot0.6}{4+2}\,\mathrm m=\frac{1.2}{6}\,\mathrm m=0.2\,\mathrm m.
$$
The support must therefore be placed $0.2\,\mathrm m$ from the loaded end. The value $0.4\,\mathrm m$ is obtained by placing the mass of the rod at the free end instead of at the midpoint.
