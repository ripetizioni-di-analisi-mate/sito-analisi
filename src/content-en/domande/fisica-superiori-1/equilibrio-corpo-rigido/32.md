A horizontal lever of negligible weight is at rest. On the right of the pivot, a $24\,\mathrm N$ force acts upwards $0.60\,\mathrm m$ from the pivot and a $50\,\mathrm N$ load acts downwards $0.20\,\mathrm m$ from it. What moment must friction at the pivot exert to keep the lever in equilibrium? Take anticlockwise moments as positive.

- $+4.4\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $-24.4\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $-4.4\,\mathrm{N\,m}$

## Explanation

On the right of the pivot, the upward force tends to turn the lever anticlockwise and the load tends to turn it clockwise. Their moments add to $+4.4\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Since the lever is at rest, the frictional moment $M_s$ must cancel this total exactly.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_F+M_P&=(0.60\cdot24-0.20\cdot50)\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
&=+4.4\,\mathrm{N\,m},\\[6pt]
M_s&=-4.4\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$
