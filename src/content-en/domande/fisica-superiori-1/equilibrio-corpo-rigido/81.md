The point where a push acts on a frame lies $0.30\,\mathrm m$ to the right and $0.20\,\mathrm m$ above the pivot. A $20\,\mathrm N$ force can point either horizontally to the right or vertically downwards. Which direction gives the greater magnitude of clockwise torque, and by how much?

- Vertically downwards, by $2\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- Horizontally to the right, by $2\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- Vertically downwards, by $10\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- The two torques have the same magnitude

## Explanation

Both directions produce clockwise torque. The vertical line is thirty centimetres from the pivot, and the horizontal one is twenty centimetres away. At equal force, the longer moment arm gives the greater torque. The difference is the force times the ten-centimetre difference between the moment arms.

$$
|M_{\mathrm v}|-|M_{\mathrm h}|=20\,\mathrm N(0.30-0.20)\,\mathrm m=2\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
$$
