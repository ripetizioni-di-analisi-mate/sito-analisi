A beam of negligible weight rests on A at $x=0$ and B at $x=3\,\mathrm m$. A $50\,\mathrm N$ load acts at $x=4\,\mathrm m$. A downward counterweight is placed at $x=1\,\mathrm m$. What counterweight makes the reaction at A exactly zero at the point of losing contact?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-.2,0 4.27,0;-.15,-.25 0,0 .15,-.25 -.15,-.25;2.85,-.25 3,0 3.15,-.25 2.85,-.25" vettori="1,0,1,-.8,P;4,0,4,-.8,50 N" punti="0,0,pieno,A;3,0,pieno,B" etichette="1,.40,1 m;3.03,.40,3 m;4,.40,4 m" xmin=-.49 xmax=4.66 ymin=-1.42 ymax=.62 titolo="The coordinates are measured from A. The arrows show the direction of each load."}

- $12.5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $50\,\mathrm{N}$
- $25\,\mathrm{N}$
- $100\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

At the threshold the reaction at A is zero and the reaction at B passes through B. The load has a lever arm of $1\,\mathrm m$ to the right and the counterweight a lever arm of $2\,\mathrm m$ to the left. The moment balance $2P=50\cdot1$ gives $P=25\,\mathrm N$. With a lighter counterweight, A would have to pull downwards.
