During hydrostatic weighing, an air bubble sticks to the underside of a rigid sample that is already submerged. At equilibrium the tension in the upper string is $24\,\mathrm N$. The bubble detaches, and at the new equilibrium the tension is $30\,\mathrm N$. What was the volume of the bubble, assuming its weight is negligible? Use $\rho_f=1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ for water.

- $0.06\,\mathrm L$
- $2.4\,\mathrm L$
- $3.0\,\mathrm L$
- $0.60\,\mathrm L$

## Explanation

With the bubble attached, the sample and bubble together displace more water. The tension is $6\,\mathrm N$ lower and the weight of the bubble is negligible, so these $6\,\mathrm N$ are its buoyant force. Each litre of water provides $10\,\mathrm N$ of buoyancy, giving $V_b=\dfrac{6\,\mathrm N}{10\,\mathrm{N/L}}=0.60\,\mathrm L$.
