A shell of mass $9\,\mathrm{kg}$ has an external volume of $12\,\mathrm L$ and a cavity of $4\,\mathrm L$. It is held at rest, fully submerged in still water. The cavity is filled with water connected to the surroundings. When the shell is released, what is the sum of its weight and hydrostatic buoyant force? Use $\rho_f=1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$.

- $10\,\mathrm N$ upwards
- $30\,\mathrm N$ upwards
- $10\,\mathrm N$ downwards
- $40\,\mathrm N$ downwards

## Explanation

Water occupies the cavity, so the shell excludes only $(12-4)\,\mathrm L=8\,\mathrm L$. Buoyancy is $80\,\mathrm N$ and the shell's weight is $90\,\mathrm N$. The net force is $10\,\mathrm N$ downwards. Including the cavity in the displaced volume would give a buoyant force of $120\,\mathrm N$, which would apply to a sealed shell.
