A body at rest under only weight, buoyancy, and tension reads $18\,\mathrm N$ on the scale in water and receives $12\,\mathrm N$ of buoyancy. What does it weigh?

- $30\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6\,\mathrm{N}$
- $18\,\mathrm{N}$
- $12\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Tension and buoyancy both point upwards. Their sum balances downward weight.

$$
P=T+F_A=(18+12)\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N.
$$

Subtracting twelve from eighteen newtons would give less than the tension alone. The weight must exceed each supporting force separately because both act in the same direction.
