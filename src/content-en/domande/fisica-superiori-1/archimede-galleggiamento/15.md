A vessel containing water sits on a scale that reads $6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. A body suspended from a separate support is held still in the water, with no contact with the vessel and no overflow. The water exerts a buoyant force of $18\,\mathrm N$ on the body. What does the scale read with $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$?

- $4.2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $7.8\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $24\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

By Newton's third law, the body pushes down on the water with $18\,\mathrm N$. The weight of the water and vessel is unchanged. The scale must support the extra force, so its reading is higher. The separate support continues to bear the remainder of the body's weight.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta m&=\frac{18\,\mathrm N}{10\,\mathrm{N/kg}}=1.8\,\mathrm{kg},\\[6pt]
m_\mathrm{ind}&=(6.0+1.8)\,\mathrm{kg}=7.8\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$
