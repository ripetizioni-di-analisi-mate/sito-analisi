A freely floating platform has vertical sides and horizontal area $0.5\,\mathrm m^2$. It receives $15\,\mathrm{kg}$ of dry load. In water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, how many centimetres does it go down?

- $0.03\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $0.3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The added load requires fifteen more kilograms of displaced water. The mass determines the extra volume. With vertical sides the added volume is cross-section times the depth increase. The result in metres is three hundredths, equivalent to three centimetres.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta V_i&=\frac{15}{1000}\,\mathrm m^3=0.015\,\mathrm m^3\\[6pt]\Delta h&=\frac{0.015}{0.5}\,\mathrm m=3\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$
