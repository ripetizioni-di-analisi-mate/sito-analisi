A float weighing $22\,\mathrm N$ is held at a fixed height by a vertical line anchored below it. Slowly raising the water level increases its submerged volume. The float can submerge up to $4\,\mathrm L$. The line can withstand at most $8\,\mathrm N$, and each litre of displaced water weighs $10\,\mathrm N$. What submerged volume marks the line’s limit?

- $3.0\,\mathrm L$
- $2.2\,\mathrm L$
- $0.8\,\mathrm L$
- $1.4\,\mathrm L$

## Explanation

The line pulls downwards, in the same direction as the weight. At the limit buoyancy must balance $(22+8)\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N$. In water this means displacing $3.0\,\mathrm L$. A further increase in submerged volume would give more buoyancy and require more tension than the line can withstand.
