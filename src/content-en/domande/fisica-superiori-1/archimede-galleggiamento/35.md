A solid of volume $0.020\,\mathrm m^3$ is weighed at rest, first in air and then fully submerged in water. Its upper string remains taut and the solid stays clear of the container. Air density is $1.2\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, water density is $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. By how much does buoyancy in water exceed the drop in the spring scale reading?

- $0\,\mathrm N$
- $2.4\,\mathrm N$
- $0.24\,\mathrm N$
- $200\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

In air and in water the scale reads the weight minus buoyancy from the relevant fluid. The reading therefore drops by $F_{A,\mathrm{acqua}}-F_{A,\mathrm{aria}}$. To recover buoyancy in water, add the buoyant force from air to the difference. It is $1.2\cdot0.020\cdot10\,\mathrm N=0.24\,\mathrm N$. The true weight cancels when the readings are compared.
