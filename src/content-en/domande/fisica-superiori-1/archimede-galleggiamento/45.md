Two sealed floats have the same total volume and height. The lower half of A has a larger volume than its upper half. In B the upper half has the larger volume. Both float upright in the same liquid with exactly half their height submerged. Which has the greater mean density?

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-2.64,-1 -1.06,-1 -1.06,0 -1.41,0 -1.41,1 -2.29,1 -2.29,0 -2.64,0;1.41,-1 2.29,-1 2.29,0 2.64,0 2.64,1 1.06,1 1.06,0 1.41,0" linee="-3.13,0 -2.64,0;-1.06,0 1.06,0;2.64,0 3.13,0" segmenti="-2.64,0,-1.06,0;1.06,0,2.64,0" etichette="-1.93,1.2,A;1.77,1.2,B" xmin=-3.3 xmax=3.3 ymin=-1.17 ymax=1.43 titolo="The waterline crosses the change in cross-section on both floats."}

- A, because a larger fraction of its volume is submerged
- B, because its upper section is wider
- They have the same mean density because the submerged heights are equal
- The values of the total and submerged heights are needed

## Explanation

The waterline crosses both objects at half their height. The submerged part of A contains more than half its volume, while the submerged part of B contains less than half. In the same liquid, a larger submerged fraction corresponds to a greater mean density.
