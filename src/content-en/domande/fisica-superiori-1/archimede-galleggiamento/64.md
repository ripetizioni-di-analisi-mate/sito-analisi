A pure ice cube of mass $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ floats in a container of fresh water. It melts completely with no spilling or evaporation. Take liquid water to have a constant density of $1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ and neglect buoyancy from air. How does the final water level change once the water is still again?

- It rises because three tenths of a litre are added
- It falls because the ice occupied more space than the liquid it produced
- It falls because the cube no longer displaces water
- It stays at the same level

## Explanation

The floating cube displaces $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water, which has a volume of $0.30\,\mathrm L$. After the ice melts, that same mass of liquid water directly occupies $0.30\,\mathrm L$. It exactly fills the space under the initial level. The total volume of the ice before melting included the part that protruded above the surface.
