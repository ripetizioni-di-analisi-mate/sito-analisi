A calculation for a cord from above a submerged body gives $T=-6\,\mathrm N$ with upward pull assumed. What constraint could keep the same equilibrium?

- The same upper cord exerting a downward push
- An upward reaction from the bottom
- A taut cord to the bottom applying $6\,\mathrm N$
- A second upper string pulling upward

## Explanation

Negative tension signals that the missing force acts opposite to the assumed direction. A line can pull toward its anchor. Anchoring it underneath supplies the downward force required when the body is lighter than the fluid it displaces.

$$
F_A-P=6\,\mathrm N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_{\downarrow}=6\,\mathrm N.
$$
