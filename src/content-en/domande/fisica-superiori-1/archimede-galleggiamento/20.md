A fully submerged solid displaces $6\,\mathrm L$ across the boundary between oil of density $0.75\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ and water of density $1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$, both at rest. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ the buoyant force is $52.5\,\mathrm N$. Neglecting surface tension, what volume of the solid is in water?

- $2\,\mathrm L$
- $4.5\,\mathrm L$
- $5.25\,\mathrm L$
- $3\,\mathrm L$

## Explanation

If all six litres were in oil, the buoyant force would be $45\,\mathrm N$. Each litre transferred to water increases buoyancy by $(1-0.75)\cdot10\,\mathrm{N/L}=2.5\,\mathrm{N/L}$. The extra force is $52.5-45=7.5$ newtons. Dividing by $2.5\,\mathrm{N/L}$ gives three litres in water. The remaining three litres are in oil.
