A float in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ descends $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ when loaded. Its constant horizontal area is $0.8\,\mathrm m^2$. What dry mass was added?

- $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $120\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The volume increase is area times vertical descent. Multiply by density to obtain extra displaced water mass, equal to the added load.

$$
\Delta m=(1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3})(0.015\,\mathrm m)(0.8\,\mathrm m^2)=12\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

The additional volume is twelve litres. In water that is one kilogram displaced per litre. Using one point five directly with the area in square metres would give a volume one hundred times too large.
