An object with an open cavity hangs motionless while fully submerged. Water fills the cavity. In a second trial, a film of negligible mass seals the empty cavity before immersion. The trapped air has negligible mass. The external volume and the water stay the same, and the upper line stays taut in both trials. How do buoyancy and tension change?

- Buoyancy decreases and tension increases
- Buoyancy increases and tension decreases
- Buoyancy and tension both increase
- Buoyancy and tension stay the same

## Explanation

The sealed cavity excludes water, so the displaced volume increases. Buoyancy increases with that volume. The object’s weight stays the same because the film and the air have negligible mass. With more buoyancy, the line needs to support a smaller share of the weight.
