A body is suspended in water by a line from above. What prevents us from always concluding $F_A=P$?

- Tension is another term in the balance
- Buoyancy points down in water
- A submerged body always has zero weight
- Water pressure does not increase with depth

## Explanation

When weight and buoyancy are the only forces, equilibrium gives $F_A=P$. If a taut string also pulls upwards, the balance becomes $F_A+T=P$. Buoyancy is then smaller than the weight by an amount equal to the tension. With a slack string, tension is zero and the two forces balance each other at equilibrium.
