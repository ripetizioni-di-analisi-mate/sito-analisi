A sealed float displaces $12\,\mathrm L$ of oil of density $0.80\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ while floating freely. It is moved into water of density $1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$. How much dry mass must be added so that the liquid reaches the same mark on the float and the submerged volume stays at $12\,\mathrm L$?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $9.6\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

In oil, twelve displaced litres have mass $12\cdot0.80\,\mathrm{kg}=9.6\,\mathrm{kg}$, the float’s original mass. In water the same volume supports $12\,\mathrm{kg}$. Add the difference, $(12-9.6)\,\mathrm{kg}=2.4\,\mathrm{kg}$. The extra weight balances the greater buoyancy at the marked waterline.
