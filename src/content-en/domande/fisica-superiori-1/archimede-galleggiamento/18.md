Two watertight bodies are immersed in one uniform fluid. The second receives $36\,\mathrm N$ of buoyancy and displaces $3\,\mathrm L$ more than the first, which receives $9\,\mathrm N$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is the fluid density?

- $300\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $1200\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $2700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

The first volume is unknown, but subtracting the buoyancy equations uses the change in volume directly. Twenty-seven extra newtons correspond to three extra litres. After converting to cubic metres, divide by volume change and field strength. Neither total body volume is needed.

$$
\rho_f=\frac{36-9}{10\cdot0.003}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$
