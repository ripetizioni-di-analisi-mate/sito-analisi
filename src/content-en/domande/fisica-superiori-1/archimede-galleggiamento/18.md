In a water test, a buoyancy sensor reads $13\,\mathrm N$ when the submerged volume is $1\,\mathrm L$, and $43\,\mathrm N$ when it is $4\,\mathrm L$. Each litre of displaced water weighs $10\,\mathrm N$, and the sensor has a constant zero offset. What does it read at zero submerged volume?

- $0\,\mathrm N$
- $3\,\mathrm N$
- $10\,\mathrm N$
- $-3\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

The first litre produces $10\,\mathrm N$ of buoyancy and the sensor reads $13$. The offset is $+3\,\mathrm N$. Four litres give $40\,\mathrm N$ of buoyancy with the same offset. At zero submerged volume the buoyant force vanishes and the sensor shows only its zero reading.
