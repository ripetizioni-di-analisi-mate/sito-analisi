A stone weighs $30\,\mathrm N$ in air. Hanging from a spring scale and fully submerged in water, at rest and without touching the container, it gives a reading of $20\,\mathrm N$. With $\rho_f=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ for water, $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ and negligible air buoyancy, what is the density of the stone?

- $1500\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $3000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $2000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $300\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

The stone is at rest, so the buoyant force equals the drop in the reading, $F_A=(30-20)\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N$. The stone is fully submerged, so the submerged volume equals its volume and is found by dividing the buoyant force by $\rho_f g$. The mass of the stone is its weight divided by $g$, that is $3\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac{F_A}{\rho_f g}=\frac{10}{1000\cdot10}\,\mathrm{m^3}=0.001\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
\rho&=\frac{m}{V}=\frac{3}{0.001}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=3000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
\end{aligned}
$$
