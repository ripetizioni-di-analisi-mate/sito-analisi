A body in water weighs $100\,\mathrm N$ and receives buoyancy $65\,\mathrm N$. An upper vertical line already carries tension $20\,\mathrm N$. What additional upward force holds the body still at this tension?

- $20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $35\,\mathrm{N}$
- $85\,\mathrm{N}$
- $15\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Buoyancy and upward tension total eighty-five newtons. The downward weight exceeds them by fifteen. Equilibrium needs an upward force of that magnitude. An oblique support would need the same vertical contribution and a separate check along the other axis.

$$
F_{\rm add}=100\,\mathrm N-65\,\mathrm N-20\,\mathrm N=15\,\mathrm N.
$$
