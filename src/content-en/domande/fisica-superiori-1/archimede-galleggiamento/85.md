A boat in a tank of constant horizontal cross-sectional area $0.50\,\mathrm m^2$ carries a stone of mass $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ and volume $2\,\mathrm L$. The stone is dropped into the water and settles fully submerged on the bottom. The boat stays afloat and no water is lost. How does the final water level change if the water density is $1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$?

- It falls by $6\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It falls by $4\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It falls by $10\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It rises by $6\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

While the stone is aboard, the boat displaces five extra litres of water to support its weight. On the bottom, the stone excludes only the two litres of its own volume. This frees three litres of space inside the tank. The water level falls by the thickness of a layer with that volume and the tank's cross-section.

$$
\Delta h=\frac{(5-2)\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m^3}{0.50\,\mathrm m^2}=6\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
