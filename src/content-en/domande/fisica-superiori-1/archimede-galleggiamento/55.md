A float with vertical walls has a horizontal cross-sectional area of $0.03\,\mathrm m^2$. It is pressed down until its submerged height is $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ greater than at equilibrium. It is released from rest while still partly submerged. In water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ with $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is the sum of its weight and hydrostatic buoyant force when released?

- $12\,\mathrm N$ downwards
- $1.2\,\mathrm N$ upwards
- $12\,\mathrm N$ upwards
- $0\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

Weight and buoyancy balanced at the equilibrium position. Pushing the body down increases only buoyancy. The additional volume of water displaced is $0.03\cdot0.04\,\mathrm m^3$, so its weight is $1000\cdot10\cdot0.03\cdot0.04\,\mathrm N=12\,\mathrm N$. On release the weight is unchanged and the net force is the increase in buoyancy, directed upwards.
