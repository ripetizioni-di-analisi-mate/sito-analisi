The graph shows a vertical float's displaced volume as a function of its submerged height. In water of density $1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ the float supports a total mass of $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ including its own mass. What is the equilibrium submerged height?

::grafico{linee="0,0 4,1.2 14,7.2" punti="4,1.2,pieno,A;10,4.8,pieno,B;14,7.2,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=16 ymin=0 ymax=8 nomi="submerged height (cm),displaced volume (L)" titolo="A corresponds to 4 cm and 1.2 L. B corresponds to 10 cm and 4.8 L."}

- $4\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $7\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Supporting three kilograms requires the displacement of three litres of water. Three litres lies halfway between $1.2$ and $4.8\,\mathrm L$. The graph is linear between these values. The height is therefore halfway between $4$ and $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, or $7\,\mathrm{cm}$. The intermediate point lies on the steeper section.
