A line passes through the points $(2;10)$ and $(5;4)$. What slope is obtained if the differences are calculated going from the second point to the first?

- $2$
- $-2$
- $-0.5$
- $5$

## Explanation

Going from the second point to the first, the difference of the $y$-coordinates is $10-4=6$ and that of the $x$-coordinates is $2-5=-3$. The slope is the ratio of the two differences, so
$$
m=\frac{10-4}{2-5}=\frac{6}{-3}=-2 .
$$
Reversing the direction changes the sign of both differences, so the ratio is the same as the one obtained going from the first point to the second. The slope is negative because the $y$-coordinate decreases as the $x$-coordinate increases. The value $-0.5$ comes from the inverted ratio, with the difference of the $x$-coordinates in the numerator.
