Two identical vertical springs are mounted in parallel between a fixed support and a load. Each spring is $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ long at its natural length and $16\,\mathrm{cm}$ long when the load is in equilibrium. One spring is removed and the same load settles on the other. The springs operate in their linear range in both arrangements. How long is the remaining spring now?

- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $16\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $24\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $32\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Initially each spring supports half the weight with an extension of $(16-12)\,\mathrm{cm}=4\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the new equilibrium, the remaining spring supports the full weight, so its tension doubles. By Hooke's law the extension doubles to $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. The natural length is still $12\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the spring is now $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ long.
