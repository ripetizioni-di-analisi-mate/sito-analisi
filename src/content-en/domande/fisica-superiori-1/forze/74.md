A horizontal spring stays within its linear range. Its natural length is $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $k=200\,\mathrm{N/m}$. On a rightward-pointing axis, the end coordinates change from $(0;22)\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $(3;26)\,\mathrm{cm}$, in left-to-right order. By how much does the component of the spring force on the right end change?

- $+2\,\mathrm N$
- $-2\,\mathrm N$
- $-8\,\mathrm N$
- $-6\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

The length is the difference between the two end coordinates. It changes from $22\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $(26-3)\,\mathrm{cm}=23\,\mathrm{cm}$. Since the natural length is fixed, the extension increases by $0.01\,\mathrm m$. The change in the component is

$$
\Delta F_x=-k\Delta x=-200\cdot0.01\,\mathrm N=-2\,\mathrm N.
$$

The right end moves $4\,\mathrm{cm}$, but the left end shares $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ of that displacement. Only the remaining centimetre changes the deformation.
