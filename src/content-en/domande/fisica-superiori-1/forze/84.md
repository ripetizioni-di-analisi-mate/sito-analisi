Two linear springs A and B are in series along the same horizontal axis. A runs from the wall to the junction, and B runs from the junction to the free end. The pull is increased, and the springs are allowed to settle. The free end moves by $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the junction, whose mass is negligible, moves by $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ in the same direction. What is the ratio $\dfrac{k_A}{k_B}$?

- $\dfrac12$
- $3$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

The wall stays fixed, so spring A extends by another $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Both ends of B move, so its additional extension is $(9-3)\,\mathrm{cm}=6\,\mathrm{cm}$. The junction is in equilibrium in both arrangements, so both springs experience the same increase in tension. This gives $\dfrac{k_A}{k_B}=\dfrac{6}{3}=2$.
