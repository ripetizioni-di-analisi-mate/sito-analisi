One end of a spring is fixed. The other starts at $x=-4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and then moves in the positive direction to $x=+6\,\mathrm{cm}$. The deformation $x$ is positive for extension, and the spring remains in its linear range. How far from its starting position must the end move for the elastic force to have its original magnitude and the opposite direction?

- $4\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $8\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $2\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

In the linear range, $F_{e,x}=-kx$. Reversing the force while keeping its magnitude requires the opposite deformation, $x=+4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The end moves four centimetres to reach the natural length and another four to reach the required extension. Its total displacement from the start is $(4-(-4))\,\mathrm{cm}=8\,\mathrm{cm}$.
