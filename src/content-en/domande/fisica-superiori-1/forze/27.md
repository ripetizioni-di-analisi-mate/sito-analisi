For a linear spring, the force magnitude rises from $4$ to $10\,\mathrm N$ during extension. The spring extends a further $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is $k$?

- $700\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $3\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$
- $500\,\mathrm{N/m}$

## Explanation

Apply the law to the differences between measurements. The same spring constant applies in both states, so the change in force is $k$ times the change in extension. The $4\,\mathrm N$ initial load was already present. The $6\,\mathrm N$ increase is the quantity associated with the extra two centimetres.

$$
k=\frac{\Delta F}{\Delta x}=\frac{(10-4)\,\mathrm N}{0.02\,\mathrm m}=300\,\mathrm{N/m}.
$$
