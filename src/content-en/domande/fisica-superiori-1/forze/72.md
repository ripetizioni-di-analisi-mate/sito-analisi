Two pieces have a combined mass of $6\,\mathrm{kg}$. One stays at location A, where $g_A=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, and the other is taken to location B, where $g_B=4\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. They are weighed separately at their respective locations, and the readings add up to $36\,\mathrm{N}$. What is the mass of the piece taken to B?

- $2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $3\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

If both pieces were at A, their combined weight would be $(6)(10)\,\mathrm{N}=60\,\mathrm{N}$. Moving one piece to B reduces the sum of the readings by $(60-36)\,\mathrm{N}=24\,\mathrm{N}$. Each kilogram moved weighs $(10-4)\,\mathrm{N}=6\,\mathrm{N}$ less, so that difference corresponds to $4\,\mathrm{kg}$. This gives $20\,\mathrm{N}$ at A and $16\,\mathrm{N}$ at B.
