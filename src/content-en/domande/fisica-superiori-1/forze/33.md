The $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring in a force gauge is replaced by a $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring with the same natural length. The zero and markings of the old scale stay in place. Both springs operate in their linear range. What force is applied when the gauge reads $8\,\mathrm{N}$ on the old scale?

- $12\,\mathrm{N}$
- $8\,\mathrm{N}$
- $\dfrac{16}{3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The mark records the same position and therefore the same extension from zero. In the old calibration, $8\,\mathrm{N}$ corresponded to $x=\dfrac{8\,\mathrm{N}}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}}=0.04\,\mathrm{m}$. The new spring is stiffer, so reaching that mark requires a pull of $(300\,\mathrm{N/m})(0.04\,\mathrm{m})=12\,\mathrm{N}$.
