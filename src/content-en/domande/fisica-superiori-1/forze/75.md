A spring scale displays kilograms because the measured force is divided by the calibration field strength of $10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. At a location where $g=4\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, a sample hanging at rest from the same scale gives a reading of $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the mass of the sample?

- $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.32\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The spring measures a force. Converting the displayed number back to its force under the old calibration gives $(0.80)(10)\,\mathrm{N}=8\,\mathrm{N}$, the sample's weight at the test location. Dividing that weight by the actual field strength gives the mass, $\dfrac{8\,\mathrm{N}}{4\,\mathrm{N/kg}}=2\,\mathrm{kg}$. The reading on the kilogram scale was smaller because it used a stronger field.
