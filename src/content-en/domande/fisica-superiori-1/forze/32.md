An ideal spring scale ends at the limit of the linear elastic range. Why can a reading beyond full scale not be found simply by extending the line?

- Stiffness is always zero at full scale
- The spring stops exerting any force at the end of the scale
- Force must become negative beyond the scale
- Proportionality must be checked in the new range

## Explanation

A calibration establishes the model over the measured range where it was tested. Beyond the linear limit, changes in slope and permanent deformations are possible.

A measurement at a greater force could give an extension different from the one predicted by the line. Interpreting that reading requires a calibration that actually covers the new range of loads.
