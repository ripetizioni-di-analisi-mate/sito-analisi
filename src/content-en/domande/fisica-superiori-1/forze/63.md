A weighing platform supports all the legs of a small table and reads $42\,\mathrm{N}$. A bag weighing $18\,\mathrm{N}$ is hung from a hook fixed to the underside of the table. The bag is stationary and touches no other surface. What does the platform read now?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-3.5,-0.25 3.5,-0.25 3.5,0 -3.5,0;-2.8,2.9 2.8,2.9 2.8,3.12 -2.8,3.12;-0.56,0.85 0.56,0.85 0.42,1.83 -0.42,1.83" linee="-2.55,0 -2.55,2.9;2.55,0 2.55,2.9;0,2.9 0,2.21;-0.3,1.83 0,2.21 0.3,1.83" etichette="-0.23,3.48,table;-0.18,1.31,bag;-0.43,-0.63,platform" xmin=-3.9 xmax=3.9 ymin=-0.94 ymax=3.82 titolo="The legs rest on the platform. The bag hangs from a hook fixed to the table."}

- $18\,\mathrm{N}$
- $42\,\mathrm{N}$
- $60\,\mathrm{N}$
- $24\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The table and the bag are considered together. The force that the hook exerts on the bag is internal to this system, so it does not appear in the balance of external forces. The external vertical forces are the normal force of the platform, upwards, and the two weights, downwards. Since the system is at rest, the normal force balances the sum of the weights and equals
$$
N=(42+18)\,\mathrm N=60\,\mathrm N.
$$
The platform therefore reads $60\,\mathrm{N}$, even though the bag does not touch it. The value $42\,\mathrm N$ is obtained by ignoring the weight transmitted through the hook.
