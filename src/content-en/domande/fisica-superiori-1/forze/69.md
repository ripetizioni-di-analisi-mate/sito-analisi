Two samples differ in mass by $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$. Their weights lie within the force gauge's scale and are treated as distinguishable if they differ by at least one division, equal to $1\,\mathrm{N}$. The field strengths at locations A and B are $g_A=6\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ and $g_B=3\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, respectively. Where can the two samples be distinguished by this criterion?

- Only at location A
- Only at location B
- At both locations
- At neither location

## Explanation

At a given location the difference in weight is $g$ times the difference in mass. At A it is $(6)(0.25)\,\mathrm{N}=1.5\,\mathrm{N}$, so it exceeds one division. At B it is $(3)(0.25)\,\mathrm{N}=0.75\,\mathrm{N}$, which is less than one division. Only A meets the criterion with the same instrument scale.
