A spring extends by $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ when the calibration force is increased by $3\,\mathrm N$. What force increase gives a further $4.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ extension in the same linear range?

- $4.5\,\mathrm{N}$
- $6\,\mathrm{N}$
- $12\,\mathrm{N}$
- $9\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Changes are directly proportional while the stiffness remains constant. The second increase in length is three times the first, so it requires three times the increase in force.

$$
\Delta F=3\,\mathrm N\cdot\frac{4.5\,\mathrm{cm}}{1.5\,\mathrm{cm}}=9\,\mathrm N.
$$

The initial measurements give a stiffness of $2\,\mathrm{N/cm}$. Multiplying by the additional $4.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ gives an increase of $9\,\mathrm N$. The previous load does not enter this increase.
