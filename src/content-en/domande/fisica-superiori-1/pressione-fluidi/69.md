The scale of a U-tube manometer can distinguish height differences of at least $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. It must detect a pressure difference of $18\,\mathrm{Pa}$ with $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. The two available liquids have densities of $800$ and $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Which gives a readable height difference if capillary effects and the weight of the gas columns are negligible?

- Only the liquid with density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- Only the liquid with density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- Both liquids
- Neither liquid

## Explanation

For a height difference of $2\,\mathrm{mm}$, the smallest detectable pressure difference is $\rho g\Delta h$. It is $16\,\mathrm{Pa}$ for the first liquid and $20\,\mathrm{Pa}$ for the second. The pressure difference of $18\,\mathrm{Pa}$ exceeds the threshold only for the less dense liquid.
