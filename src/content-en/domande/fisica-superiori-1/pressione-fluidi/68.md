Two columns open to the same atmosphere exert the same pressure at the comparison level. The water column is $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ high, with a density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The other column is $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ high. What is its density?

- $250\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $\dfrac{4000}{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $750\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

At the comparison level, the pressure found from either branch is the same. Atmospheric pressure and field strength cancel, so only the products of density and column height are needed. The taller branch is four thirds as high as the other. To compensate, its density must be three quarters of water's density.

$$
\rho_2=\frac{1000\cdot9}{12}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=750\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$
