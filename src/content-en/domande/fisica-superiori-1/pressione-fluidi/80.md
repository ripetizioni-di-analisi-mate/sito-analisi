Two vessels contain the same two liquid layers, with the same thickness of each liquid. The layers are at rest, and an ideal separator of negligible weight keeps them apart while transmitting pressure without external support. Does reversing the order of the layers change the pressure increase from the surface to the bottom?

- Yes, the denser liquid loses its contribution if it is on top
- Yes, it always doubles
- No, because only the lower layer matters
- No, both contributions add in either order

## Explanation

In the stated model, add the two column contributions keeping each density and thickness. Reversing the order leaves the two contributions unchanged, so the bottom increase agrees. Pressures at intermediate depths change because different amounts of the two liquids are traversed at those positions.
