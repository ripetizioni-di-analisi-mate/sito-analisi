Two open, vertical communicating arms have constant cross-sectional areas of $10$ and $30\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The water surfaces start $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the rim of the shorter arm. The other rim is higher. A volume of $100\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of water is poured in slowly. How much water overflows?

- $0\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $40\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $80\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$

## Explanation

The two surfaces rise together until the lower rim is reached. The volume available before overflow is $(10+30)\cdot2\,\mathrm{cm^3}=80\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Once the lower rim is reached the extra water spills out, so $(100-80)\,\mathrm{cm^3}=20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ overflows.
