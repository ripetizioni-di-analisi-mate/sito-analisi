In a real press a constant input force of $60\,\mathrm N$ acts over $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. At the output a constant force of $180\,\mathrm N$ acts over $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. Work is exchanged with the surroundings only at the pistons. At the end of the stroke the press is at rest and has the same mechanical energy as at the start. How much mechanical energy was dissipated?

- $0.6\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $3.6\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $1.2\,\mathrm{J}$.
- $4.8\,\mathrm{J}$.

## Explanation

The forces act along their respective strokes. Convert the strokes to metres to obtain $60\cdot0.08\,\mathrm J=4.8\,\mathrm J$ at the input and $180\cdot0.02\,\mathrm J=3.6\,\mathrm J$ at the output. Since the press has the same final mechanical energy, the difference of $1.2\,\mathrm J$ is dissipated. The increase in force alone does not determine the work.
