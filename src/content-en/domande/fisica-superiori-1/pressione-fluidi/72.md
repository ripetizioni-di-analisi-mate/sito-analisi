Two barometers measure the same atmosphere with $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. In the first, pressure above the column is negligible. The column is $0.80\,\mathrm m$ high and the liquid density is $12500\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. In the second, the liquid density is $15000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the residual pressure at the top is $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$. How high is the second column?

- $0.80\,\mathrm m$
- $0.67\,\mathrm m$
- $0.73\,\mathrm m$
- $0.60\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The first barometer gives an atmospheric pressure of $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In the second, only $90\,\mathrm{kPa}$ must be added to the residual pressure by the column. Its gradient is $150\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$, so its height is $\dfrac{90\,\mathrm{kPa}}{150\,\mathrm{kPa/m}}=0.60\,\mathrm m$.
