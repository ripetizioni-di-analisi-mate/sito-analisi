Gas above a tank's liquid is at $112\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Outside atmosphere is $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the hydrostatic contribution at the bottom is $24\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is bottom pressure relative to atmosphere?

- $12\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $24\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $136\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $36\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

At the surface the gas is already $12\,\mathrm{kPa}$ above atmospheric pressure. The bottom has a further increase of $24\,\mathrm{kPa}$ relative to the gas inside. Add the two differences because they describe successive parts of the comparison. The result is $36\,\mathrm{kPa}$ relative to outside, while the absolute reading would be $136\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

$$
p_{\rm rel}=(112+24-100)\,\mathrm{kPa}=36\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$
