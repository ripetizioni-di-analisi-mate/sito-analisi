A water manometer has one arm open to the atmosphere at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The water surface in the arm connected to the gas is $2.0\,\mathrm m$ lower than in the open arm. With a gradient of $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$, what is the gas's absolute pressure?

- $80\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $120\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

At the surface on the open side the pressure is $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. From there, go down by $2.0\,\mathrm m$ to the level of the surface under the gas. Pressure rises by $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ over that descent, so the gas pressure is above atmospheric pressure. A negative increase would disagree with the lower level.

$$
p_{\rm gas}=100\,\mathrm{kPa}+(10\,\mathrm{kPa/m})(2.0\,\mathrm m)=120\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$
