Two arms contain water with gradient $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$. Surface pressure above A is $3\,\mathrm{kPa}$ greater than above B. What level difference develops?

- $3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{10}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $30\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The higher pressure above A pushes its surface down. Starting at the surface of B and moving down to A's level, the water column must make up $3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At a gradient of $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$, this takes three tenths of a metre. B lies above A and the difference is vertical.

$$
\Delta h=\frac{3\,\mathrm{kPa}}{10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}}=0.3\,\mathrm m.
$$
