A bottom is $15\,\mathrm{kPa}$ above surface pressure. The oil above the water adds $3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The water's hydrostatic gradient is $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$. How thick is it?

- $0.3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

After subtracting the $3\,\mathrm{kPa}$ already produced by the oil, $12\,\mathrm{kPa}$ remains for the water layer. That water adds $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$ per metre, so the layer is just over a metre thick. Dividing the total increase by the water gradient would incorrectly assign the oil's contribution to the water.

$$
h_{\rm acqua}=\frac{15-3}{10}\,\mathrm m=1.2\,\mathrm m.
$$
