A U-tube contains water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. In arm A a $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ column of oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is between the water and a gas. In arm B another gas meets the water surface $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ above the oil–water interface. The liquids are at rest and capillary effects are negligible. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is $p_B-p_A$ between the two gases?

- $-600\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2600\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $600\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1600\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

At the elevation of the interface, pressure has a single value in the connected water. Moving down from gas A through $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ of oil adds $1600\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Moving down from gas B through $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ of water adds $1000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Equating these results shows that B exceeds A by $600\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
