In two communicating vessels, which property lets us compare the pressure at a flat oil–water interface in one arm with the pressure in the water at the same elevation in the other arm? Capillary effects are negligible.

- Oil always having the same density as water
- Equal volumes of liquid
- Pressure continuity at the flat interface and the connected water
- The free surfaces having equal levels for different liquids

## Explanation

Neglecting surface tension effects, pressure is continuous across the flat oil–water boundary. From just below the interface, a point at the same height can be chosen in the other branch because the water is a single connected liquid. Pressure is unchanged in that comparison too.
