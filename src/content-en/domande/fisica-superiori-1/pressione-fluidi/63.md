A circular membrane has uniform pressure difference $500\,\mathrm{Pa}$ across it. Its initial area is $0.02\,\mathrm m^2$. If the radius doubles, what is the new resultant force?

- $10\,\mathrm{N}$
- $20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $40\,\mathrm{N}$
- $80\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The pressure difference is fixed. The new area is four times the old one because it depends on the square of the radius. The resultant force also grows by a factor of four. This distinguishes doubling a length from doubling an area.

$$
F=500\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot(4\cdot0.02\,\mathrm m^2)=40\,\mathrm N.
$$
