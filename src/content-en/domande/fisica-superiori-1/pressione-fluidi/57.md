A press contains water at rest. The lower piston, which has area $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, is pressed by a load of $16\,\mathrm N$. The upper piston, of area $20\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, is $2\,\mathrm m$ higher. The atmosphere is at the same pressure outside both pistons and their weights are negligible. What load can be balanced on the upper piston? Use gradient $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$.

- $40\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $80\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $120\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $20\,\mathrm{N}$.

## Explanation

Under the first piston the pressure exceeds atmospheric pressure by $\dfrac{16}{0.0004}\,\mathrm{Pa}=40\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Moving up $2\,\mathrm m$ subtracts $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$, so the second piston has $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ above atmospheric pressure on its inside face. The balanced load is $20000\cdot0.002\,\mathrm N=40\,\mathrm N$. Using the same net pressure at both pistons would omit the difference in height.
