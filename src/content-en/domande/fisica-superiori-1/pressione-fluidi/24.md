In tank A, the absolute surface pressure is $94\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the hydrostatic gradient is $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$. In tank B, the corresponding values are $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $8\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$. At $2\,\mathrm m$ below the respective surfaces, in which tank is absolute pressure higher and in which is the pressure relative to its own surface higher?

- Both the absolute pressure and the pressure relative to its own surface are higher in A
- Both the absolute pressure and the pressure relative to its own surface are higher in B
- Absolute pressure is higher in A, and pressure relative to its own surface is higher in B
- Absolute pressure is higher in B, and pressure relative to its own surface is higher in A

## Explanation

In A the column adds $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$, so the absolute pressure is $114\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In B the column adds $16\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the absolute pressure reaches $116\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The ranking depends on the reference. For pressures relative to their respective surfaces, compare the hydrostatic increases alone.
