Two vertical communicating branches, with areas of $12$ and $36\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, contain water at the same level. Changing the pressure above the narrow branch makes its surface fall by $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. No water is added or removed. How far does the surface in the wider branch rise?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,2.4 0,0 6,0 6,2.4;1,2.4 1,0.4 3,0.4 3,2.4;0,0.8 1,0.8;3,1.6 6,1.6" segmenti="0,1.4,6,1.4" vettori="0.5,1.35,0.5,0.85;4.5,1.42,4.5,1.58" etichette="0.05,2.1,12 cm²;3.9,2.1,36 cm²;-0.5,1.1,6 cm" xmin=-0.8 xmax=6.3 ymin=-0.15 ymax=2.55 titolo="The dashed line shows the initial level. The liquid falls in the narrow branch and rises in the wider branch."}

- $2\,\mathrm{cm}$.
- $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.
- $8\,\mathrm{cm}$.
- $18\,\mathrm{cm}$.

## Explanation

The narrow branch loses $12\cdot6\,\mathrm{cm^3}=72\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, which fills the new volume in the wider branch. Divide by $36\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ to find the height of $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. The final difference between the surfaces is $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, while the question asks only for the rise from the initial level.
