Density times field is $9$ and $12\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$ in two liquids respectively. What depth in the second gives the same pressure increase as $4\,\mathrm m$ in the first?

- $4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{16}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

In the first liquid, four metres at a gradient of $9\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$ give $36\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In the second, divide the same increase by $12\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$ to obtain three metres. The depth must be smaller because each metre in the second liquid adds more pressure than a metre in the first.

$$
h_2=\frac{9\cdot4}{12}\,\mathrm m=3\,\mathrm m.
$$
