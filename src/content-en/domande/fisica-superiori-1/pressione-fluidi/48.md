In a tank with oil above water, $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ of oil is replaced by the same thickness of water. Total depth and surface pressure are unchanged. The hydrostatic gradients are $8\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$ in oil and $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$ in water. How does the pressure at the bottom change?

- It increases by $2.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- It decreases by $0.50\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- It stays the same
- It increases by $0.50\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The contribution of $0.25\,\mathrm m$ of oil is removed and the contribution of $0.25\,\mathrm m$ of water is added. The other sections retain their original thicknesses. The change is the difference in gradients multiplied by the thickness replaced, $(10-8)\cdot0.25\,\mathrm{kPa}=0.50\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
