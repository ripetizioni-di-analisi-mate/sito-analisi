In a sealed tank, two sensors are fixed at different elevations and remain submerged in the same water. After an intervention both absolute readings increase by $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The initial water level is known and the known hydrostatic gradient has stayed constant. Which additional measurement determines the change in pressure of the gas above the water?

- The horizontal distance between the two sensors
- The difference between the two final absolute readings
- The change recorded by a third fixed sensor that remained submerged
- The final elevation of the water surface

## Explanation

Pressure at each sensor includes the gas pressure and the contribution from the water above it. A rise in the surface level increases the depth of every fixed sensor by the same amount. The final surface elevation gives that contribution. Subtract it from $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ to find the change in gas pressure.
