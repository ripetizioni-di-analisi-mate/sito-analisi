Pressure differs by five kilopascals between two levels. The air pressure above the surface rises by two kilopascals, without changing the liquid or depths. What pressure difference is measured between the levels now?

- It becomes seven kilopascals
- It stays at five kilopascals
- It becomes three kilopascals
- It becomes zero

## Explanation

The surface increase of $2\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is transmitted to both depths. The second value increases by as much as the first, so the shared increment cancels when the readings are subtracted. The difference remains $5\,\mathrm{kPa}$ because density and the vertical distance between the levels stayed the same.
