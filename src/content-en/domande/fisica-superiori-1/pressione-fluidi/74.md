A pressure-versus-depth graph has a corner at a flat interface between two resting liquids. What changes at the corner when capillary effects are negligible?

- Pressure jumps but slope is unchanged
- The slope changes while pressure is continuous
- Pressure and slope both vanish
- Density and slope both remain the same

## Explanation

Going downwards, the second segment starts exactly at the pressure reached at the end of the first. The pressure is therefore continuous across the boundary. However, density times field strength determines the rise per metre. With different densities the line changes gradient, making the corner on the graph.
