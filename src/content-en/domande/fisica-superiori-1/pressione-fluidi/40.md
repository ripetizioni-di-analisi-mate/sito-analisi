In water, one point is $7\,\mathrm m$ below the surface. Another point has $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ lower pressure. If the gradient is $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$, what is the second depth?

- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

A lower pressure in the same liquid means the second point is higher up. At $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$ per metre, a drop of $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ requires going up by $2\,\mathrm m$. The depth decreases from seven to five metres. Adding that distance would give a higher pressure, contrary to the stated reading.

$$
h_2=7\,\mathrm m-\frac{20\,\mathrm{kPa}}{10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}}=5\,\mathrm m.
$$
