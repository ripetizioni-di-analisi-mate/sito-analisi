A $0.5\,\mathrm m$ layer has density $700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and a $1.5\,\mathrm m$ layer has density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. What uniform density gives the same pressure rise over two metres?

- $850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $925\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

Equate the hydrostatic contributions and cancel the shared field. In the average, the taller layer gets more weight.

$$
\rho_{\rm eq}=\frac{700\cdot0.5+1000\cdot1.5}{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=925\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$
