A $0.5\,\mathrm m$ layer has density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and a $1.5\,\mathrm m$ layer has density $1600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. What uniform density gives the same pressure rise over two metres?

- $1100\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $1350\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $1600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

The first layer is one quarter of the total depth and the second is three quarters. To reproduce the same bottom pressure increase, weight each density by that fraction of the height. This gives $1350\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, closer to the density of the layer occupying more of the column.

$$
\rho_{\rm eq}=\frac{600\cdot0.5+1600\cdot1.5}{2}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=1350\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$
