Two pistons of equal area move vertically and are initially at rest in a press filled with water with a gradient of $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$. After an adjustment the lower piston comes to rest $0.10\,\mathrm m$ higher and the pressure underneath it increases by $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The upper piston comes to rest $0.10\,\mathrm m$ lower. How does the pressure just beneath the upper piston change?

- It increases by $2\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- It increases by $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- It increases by $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- It decreases by $2\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Moving up from the lower piston to the upper piston requires subtracting the hydrostatic contribution of the elevation difference. The difference decreases by $0.20\,\mathrm m$, so $2\,\mathrm{kPa}$ less is subtracted. The additional $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the lower piston brings the increase at the upper piston to $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
