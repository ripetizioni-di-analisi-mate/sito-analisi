When measuring deeper in the same uniform liquid with unchanged surface pressure, how does the point move on a graph of absolute pressure against depth?

- Right and downward
- Vertically at the same depth
- Onto a different horizontal line
- Right and upward along the same line

## Explanation

Going deeper puts the point farther to the right because depth increases. The weight of the liquid above also raises pressure, so the point moves upward on the graph. It stays on the same line because density, gravitational field strength and surface pressure are unchanged between readings.
