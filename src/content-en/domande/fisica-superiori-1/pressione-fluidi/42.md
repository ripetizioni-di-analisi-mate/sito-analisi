In the two vessels shown, the surface pressures are $98\,\mathrm{kPa}$ in A and $104\,\mathrm{kPa}$ in B. Their gradients are $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$ and $6\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$, respectively. At what depth below the respective surfaces do the absolute pressures match?

::grafico{f="98+10*x;104+6*x" legenda="p_A;p_B" punti="0,98,pieno,A;0,104,pieno,B" nomi="h (m),p (kPa)" xmin=0 xmax=2.6 ymin=94 ymax=127 titolo="A starts at the lower surface pressure and increases more quickly than B."}

- $0.6\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $1\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

A starts $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ below B. With every metre of depth, the gap closes by $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$, the difference between the gradients. Equality requires $4h=6$ in the graph's units, giving $h=1.5\,\mathrm m$. At that depth both readings are $113\,\mathrm{kPa}$.
