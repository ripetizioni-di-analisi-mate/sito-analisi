Two vessels of different shapes contain water open to the same atmosphere. Point A in the narrow vessel and B in the flared vessel are both $1.35\,\mathrm m$ below the surface. How do their pressures compare?

- A has greater pressure because its vessel is narrow
- They are equal
- B has greater pressure because there is more water in total
- They depend on the weight of the whole vessel

## Explanation

The column determining local pressure is counted vertically from the point to its reference surface. The heights match, as do the liquid and surface pressure. Vessel shape changes the total liquid quantity, but that quantity is not in the local pressure law.

$$
p_A-p_B=\rho g(h_A-h_B)=0.
$$
