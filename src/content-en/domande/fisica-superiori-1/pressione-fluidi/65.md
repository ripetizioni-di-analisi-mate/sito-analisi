Two open arms communicate through the same liquid and one is much wider. In equilibrium, what can differ between the arms?

- The volume held up to the common level
- The elevation of the free surfaces
- The air pressure at the same elevation, assumed shared
- The liquid pressure at the same elevation

## Explanation

The same height can represent different volumes when cross-section areas differ. Pressures at equal elevation in the connected liquid are still equal.
