Two open arms communicate through the same liquid and one is much wider. In equilibrium, what can differ between the arms?

- The volume held up to the common level
- The elevation of the free surfaces
- The air pressure at the same elevation, assumed shared
- The liquid pressure at the same elevation

## Explanation

With the same liquid and atmosphere, the surfaces come to the same level. In a wider branch, that height encloses more volume above the same starting level. The amount of liquid can therefore differ between branches even though the pressure at the same height in the connected liquid is equal.
