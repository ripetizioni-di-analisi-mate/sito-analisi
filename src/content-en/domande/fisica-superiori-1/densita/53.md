An object of external volume $35\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ has a $10\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ cavity. The cavity is opened and filled with liquid of density $1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. How much mass is added, neglecting the displaced air?

- $14\,\mathrm{g}$
- $49\,\mathrm{g}$
- $35\,\mathrm{g}$
- $\dfrac{50}{7}\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The wall is the same before and after filling. Since the removed air has negligible mass, the increase equals the mass of the added liquid. Use the cavity's volume of $10\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ to find $1.4\cdot10=14\,\mathrm{g}$. The external volume would be needed for the mean density after filling.
