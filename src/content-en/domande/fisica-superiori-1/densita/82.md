Two components occupy $25\%$ and $75\%$ of the total volume. The component volumes add. The first component is replaced with an equal volume of a material $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ denser. By how much does the mean density increase?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

With additive volumes, the first component contributes one quarter of the weight in the density average. The second stays the same, so only the first changes the result. Raising its density by $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ across a quarter of the volume changes the mean by $\dfrac14\cdot2=0.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. The relevant weight is the replaced component's share of the volume.
