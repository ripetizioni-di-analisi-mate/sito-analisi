A $6\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ cavity is filled with wall material of density $4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. External volume is $30\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. How much does mean density increase?

- $0.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $24\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.25\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The added material increases mass by twenty-four grams. The external denominator is fixed, so divide the added mass by that volume.

$\frac{6\cdot 4}{30}=0.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
