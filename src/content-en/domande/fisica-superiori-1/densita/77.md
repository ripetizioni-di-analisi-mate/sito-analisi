A rigid shell has external volume $50\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and empty mass $66\,\mathrm{g}$. Filling the cavity with a liquid of density $1.2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ raises the mass to $102\,\mathrm{g}$. Ignoring the mass of the air, what is the density of the wall?

- $3.3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $2.2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.32\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $5.1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The difference $102-66=36\,\mathrm{g}$ is the liquid's mass. Dividing it by the liquid's density gives a cavity volume of $30\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. This leaves $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ for the wall. Its density compares the mass of the empty shell with that volume, so it is $\dfrac{66}{20}=3.3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
