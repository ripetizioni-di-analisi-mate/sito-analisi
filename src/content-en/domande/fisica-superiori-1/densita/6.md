An object has external volume $13\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, an internal cavity of $2\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, and material density $10\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Neglecting the mass of air, what is its mass?

- $130\,\mathrm{g}$
- $110\,\mathrm{g}$
- $20\,\mathrm{g}$
- $1.1\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The external volume includes material and cavity. The cavity contains none of the wall, so subtract its volume from the external volume before multiplying. The stated density is the material's density. With negligible air, multiplying it by the wall volume gives the object's entire mass.

The material volume is $13-2=11\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, so $m=10\cdot11=110\,\mathrm{g}$.
