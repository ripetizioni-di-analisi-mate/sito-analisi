A container reads $98\,\mathrm{g}$ with $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ and $126\,\mathrm{g}$ with $40\,\mathrm{mL}$ of the same liquid. What is the liquid's density?

- $3.15\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $4.9\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $0.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The same container appears in both weighings, so its mass cancels on subtraction. The added mass is $126-98=28\,\mathrm{g}$. Volume increases by $40-20=20\,\mathrm{mL}=20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. The ratio $\dfrac{28}{20}=1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ gives the liquid's density without a separate weighing of the empty container.
