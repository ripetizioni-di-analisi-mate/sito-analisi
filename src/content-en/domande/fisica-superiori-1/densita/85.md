A block consists of $30\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of a material of density $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ and $10\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of a material of density $5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Half the volume of the denser material alone is removed. What is the mean density of the remaining block if the volumes add?

- $\dfrac{17}{7}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac{11}{4}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac{22}{7}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

All $30\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of the first material and $5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of the second remain. Their respective masses are $60$ and $25\,\mathrm{g}$. The remaining mass divided by the remaining volume is $\dfrac{85}{35}=\dfrac{17}{7}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. The mean density falls because cutting reduces the volume share of the denser material.
