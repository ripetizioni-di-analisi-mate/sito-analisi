Two masses of $60\,\mathrm{g}$ consist of materials of density $3$ and $12\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. If their volumes add, what is the mean density?

- $7.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $4.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $15\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The first part occupies $\dfrac{60}{3}=20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, and the second occupies $\dfrac{60}{12}=5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Add the masses and volumes, yielding $\dfrac{120}{25}=4.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. The result is closer to $3$ because the less dense part occupies four fifths of the volume, even though it supplies half the mass.
