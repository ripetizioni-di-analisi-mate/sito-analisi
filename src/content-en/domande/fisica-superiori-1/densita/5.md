A graduated cylinder goes from $38$ to $51\,\mathrm{mL}$ when a nonporous solid of mass $39\,\mathrm{g}$ is fully submerged. What is the solid's density?

- $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac{13}{17}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac{39}{38}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $39\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The final reading includes the original liquid volume and the space occupied by the solid. Subtracting the first reading removes the first contribution and gives the whole solid's volume because it is fully submerged. The displacement in millilitres equals that in cubic centimetres numerically. Divide the solid's mass by the displaced volume.

The displaced volume is $V=51-38=13\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ and the density is $\rho=\dfrac{39}{13}=3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
