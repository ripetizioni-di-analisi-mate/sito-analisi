A $100\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ mixture has density $1.6\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Adding $20\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of another liquid gives density $1.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What was the added liquid's density if volumes are additive?

- $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $9\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $0.1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The initial mixture has mass $1.6\cdot100=160\,\mathrm{g}$. With additive volumes, the new volume is $120\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and its mass is $1.5\cdot120=180\,\mathrm{g}$. The added liquid contributes $20\,\mathrm{g}$ in $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, giving $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. It is less dense than the initial mixture, consistent with the decrease in mean density.
