An object has external volume $28\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, mass $69\,\mathrm{g}$, and material density $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the cavity volume if the mass of air is negligible?

- $5\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $28\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $23\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $51\,\mathrm{cm}^3$

## Explanation

The given mass comes from the wall material because air is neglected. Divide that mass by the material density to find the volume of the wall. The cavity occupies the difference from the outside volume. The two resulting volumes must add up to the reported external volume.

The material volume is $\dfrac{69}{3}=23\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, so the cavity is $28-(23)=5\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.
