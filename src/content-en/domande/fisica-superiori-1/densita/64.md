A homogeneous $80\,\mathrm{mL}$ mixture has density $1.25\,\mathrm{g/mL}$. If $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ is removed, how much mass remains?

- $100\,\mathrm{g}$
- $25\,\mathrm{g}$
- $125\,\mathrm{g}$
- $75\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

Removing $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ from the $80\,\mathrm{mL}$ leaves $60\,\mathrm{mL}$. The mixture is homogeneous, so the remaining liquid still has density $1.25\,\mathrm{g/mL}$. Its mass is $1.25\cdot60=75\,\mathrm{g}$. The initial mass is $100\,\mathrm{g}$ and the removed portion has a mass of $25\,\mathrm{g}$. Their difference confirms the result.
