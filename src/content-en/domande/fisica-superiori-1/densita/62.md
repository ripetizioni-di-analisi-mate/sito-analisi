Two liquids of combined mass $90\,\mathrm{g}$ start with volumes of $40$ and $50\,\mathrm{mL}$. After mixing them without losing mass, the measured volume is $80\,\mathrm{mL}$. What is the mixture density?

- $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac98\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac89\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac9{17}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The final mass is $90\,\mathrm{g}$, and the final volume is the measured $80\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Their ratio is $\dfrac{90}{80}=\dfrac98\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Adding the original volumes would give $90\,\mathrm{mL}$ and a density of $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. That would underestimate density because it uses a volume larger than the actual mixture volume.
