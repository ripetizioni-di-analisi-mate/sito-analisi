A $60\,\mathrm{g}$ sample contains two distinguishable regions. Its total volume is $25\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. What conclusion follows from those two measurements alone?

- Each region has density $2.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- Each region has volume $25\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- Each region has mass $30\,\mathrm{g}$
- The mean density is $2.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

Both measurements describe the entire sample, so their ratio gives the mean density $\dfrac{60}{25}=2.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Finding a region's density would require its own mass and volume. Different distributions of mass and volume between the regions can have the same totals.
