A density of $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ was obtained for a $45\,\mathrm{g}$ sample. If volume was underestimated by $3\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, what corrected density is obtained?

- $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $3.75\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $6\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The initial density comes from a measured volume of $\dfrac{45}{3}=15\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Since the volume was underestimated by $3\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, its corrected value is $18\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Dividing the same mass by the larger volume gives $\dfrac{45}{18}=2.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. The corrected density is lower because the denominator has increased.
