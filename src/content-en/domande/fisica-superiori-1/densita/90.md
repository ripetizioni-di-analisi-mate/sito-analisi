Two shells with equal mass and mean density have equal external volume. If the second uses denser material and air is negligible, what is true of their wall volumes?

- The second has larger wall volume
- The second has smaller wall volume
- Their wall volumes are equal
- Both walls have zero volume

## Explanation

The air has negligible mass, so the two walls have the same mass. For the wall material, $V_m=\dfrac{m}{\rho_m}$. The second shell uses denser material, so it achieves that mass with less wall volume. With the same external volume, it leaves more space for the cavity.
