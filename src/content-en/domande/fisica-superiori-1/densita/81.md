The graph compares the total masses of containers $A$ and $B$ as different liquids are added. The tare masses of $A$ and $B$ are $45$ and $15\,\mathrm{g}$ respectively. At point $E$, how do the masses of the liquids alone compare?

::grafico{f="45+1.25*x;15+2.75*x" xmin=0 xmax=30 ymin=10 ymax=105 punti="20,70,pieno,E" nomi="V (cm³),m (g)" legenda="\mathrm{A};\mathrm{B}" titolo="Point E belongs to both lines."}

- $B$ contains $30\,\mathrm{g}$ more liquid.
- $A$ contains $30\,\mathrm{g}$ more liquid.
- The two liquids have the same mass.
- $B$ contains $60\,\mathrm{g}$ more liquid.

## Explanation

At $E$ the two vertical coordinates agree at $70\,\mathrm{g}$. This is the total mass in each container. Subtract the tare masses to find $70-45=25\,\mathrm{g}$ of liquid in $A$ and $70-15=55\,\mathrm{g}$ in $B$. The difference is $30\,\mathrm{g}$, exactly the difference between the tare masses.
