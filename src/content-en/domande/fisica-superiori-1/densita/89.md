The volume of a solid is measured by immersion in a graduated cylinder. An air bubble of $2\,\mathrm{mL}$ remains trapped in the water together with the solid. The level rises by $8\,\mathrm{mL}$ and the mass of the solid is $18\,\mathrm{g}$. What density of the solid is obtained?

- $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $2.25\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $9\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The rise of $8\,\mathrm{mL}$ in the level also includes the $2\,\mathrm{mL}$ occupied by the bubble. Subtracting the bubble shows that the solid displaces $8-2=6\,\mathrm{mL}$, that is $6\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, because $1\,\mathrm{mL}=1\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. The density is the ratio of the mass to this volume, so
$$
\rho=\frac{18\,\mathrm g}{6\,\mathrm{cm^3}}=3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}.
$$
The value $2.25\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ comes from using the whole rise of $8\,\mathrm{mL}$ as the volume of the solid.
