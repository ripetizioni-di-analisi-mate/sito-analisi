To make $50\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of a $1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ mixture, use liquids at $1$ and $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. With additive volumes, how much of the denser liquid is used?

- $30\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $70\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $25\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $20\,\mathrm{cm}^3$

## Explanation

A $50\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ mixture at $1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ must have mass $70\,\mathrm{g}$. If the entire volume consisted of the less dense liquid, the mass would be $50\,\mathrm{g}$. Each cubic centimetre replaced by the denser liquid adds one gram, so adding twenty grams requires $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of the second liquid.
