A stack of $200$ identical aluminium sheets, laid exactly on top of one another, is $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ high. Each sheet is a square of side $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the density of aluminium is $2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the mass of one sheet?

- $27\,\mathrm{g}$
- $2.7\,\mathrm{g}$
- $540\,\mathrm{g}$
- $0.27\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The height of the stack is the sum of the thicknesses of the $200$ sheets, so the thickness of one sheet is $\dfrac{20\,\mathrm{mm}}{200}=0.1\,\mathrm{mm}=0.01\,\mathrm{cm}$. The sheet is a slab of area $10\cdot10=100\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, so its volume is
$$
V=100\,\mathrm{cm^2}\cdot0.01\,\mathrm{cm}=1\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
$$
The mass is the product of the density and the volume, so $m=2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}\cdot1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=2.7\,\mathrm g$. The value $27\,\mathrm g$ comes from reading the thickness of $0.1\,\mathrm{mm}$ as $0.1\,\mathrm{cm}$, while $540\,\mathrm g$ is the mass of the whole stack.
