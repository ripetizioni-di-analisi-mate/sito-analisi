Two liquids are poured into a mixing vessel. The first has volume $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ and density $1.2\,\mathrm{g/mL}$. The second starts with a volume of $50\,\mathrm{mL}$ and has density $1.8\,\mathrm{g/mL}$. Some of the second liquid is left in its original container. The transferred liquids have a combined mass of $96\,\mathrm{g}$. What volume is left behind?

- $10\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $15\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $18\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $40\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

Before pouring, the liquids have a combined mass of $1.2\cdot20+1.8\cdot50=114\,\mathrm{g}$. The difference from the $96\,\mathrm{g}$ that reached the mixing vessel is $18\,\mathrm{g}$ of the second liquid. Dividing this mass by its density gives $\dfrac{18}{1.8}=10\,\mathrm{mL}$. The calculation uses the mass left behind, so the final volume of the mixture is irrelevant.
