Mix $90\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of liquid with density $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ and $10\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of liquid with density $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Assuming additive volumes, what is the mixture density?

- $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac{10}{11}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

Each liquid's mass is the product of its density and volume. With no loss, add the two masses. The additivity assumption allows the volumes to be added for the denominator. The first liquid occupies more space, so the ratio should be closer to its density.

Therefore $\rho=\dfrac{90+20}{90+10}=1.1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
