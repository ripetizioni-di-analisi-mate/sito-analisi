Mix $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of a liquid of density $1.2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ and $30\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of a liquid of density $1.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ without losing mass. The measured final density is $1.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the percentage volume change relative to the sum of the initial volumes?

- $-4\,\%$
- $+2\,\%$
- $0\,\%$
- $+4\,\%$

## Explanation

The masses add to $24+54=78\,\mathrm{g}$. The final density gives the actual volume $\dfrac{78}{1.5}=52\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. The reference for the change is $20+30=50\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. The extra $2\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ is $\dfrac{2}{50}=4\,\%$ of the reference, so the change is positive.
