A balance reads $120\,\mathrm{g}$ for a glass containing $50\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of liquid. The glass alone has mass $70\,\mathrm{g}$. By how much is the density overestimated if the total mass is used in place of the liquid’s mass?

- $2.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac{5}{7}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $\dfrac{12}{7}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

## Explanation

The mass of $120\,\mathrm{g}$ includes the $70\,\mathrm{g}$ glass. The liquid alone therefore has mass $50\,\mathrm{g}$. Dividing the two masses by the same volume gives $2.4$ and $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ respectively. The overestimate is their difference, $1.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$, which is the tare divided by the liquid's volume.
