Two bodies each have mass $96\,\mathrm g$. Their densities are $8$ and $16\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the volume of the denser body?

- $6\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $12\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $24\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $48\,\mathrm{cm}^3$

## Explanation

At equal mass, the denser sample contains more mass in each cubic centimetre. It needs fewer cubic centimetres to contain the given mass. Use the greater density in the denominator of $V=\dfrac{m}{\rho}$, and check that the inverse product recovers the mass.

For the denser body, $V=\dfrac{96}{16}=6\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.
