Mass stays unchanged during mixing, but the final volume exceeds the initial total. How is density calculated with additive volumes biased?

- It is too low
- It is correct
- It is too high
- It becomes zero

## Explanation

The total mass used in the ratio is the same in both calculations. If the actual volume exceeds the sum of the initial volumes, assuming additivity gives a smaller denominator. For positive mass, dividing by a smaller number increases the ratio. The calculated density therefore exceeds the actual density.
