An intervention narrows each centred interval without shifting its centre. What can be said about two intervals that were initially disjoint?

- They touch at an endpoint
- They remain disjoint
- They always overlap
- Their union becomes a single interval

## Explanation

Each new interval contains only values from its original interval because its centre stays fixed and its radius decreases. A value shared by the two narrower intervals would also have belonged to both original intervals. That is impossible because the originals were disjoint. They therefore remain separate after the change.
