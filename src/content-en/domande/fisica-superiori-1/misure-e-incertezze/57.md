An exact mass of $24\,\mathrm g$ has a measured volume of $(6.0\pm\Delta V)\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. Density must be at least $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ for every allowed volume. What is the maximum permitted $\Delta V$?

- $2\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $3\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $4\,\mathrm{cm}^3$
- $8\,\mathrm{cm}^3$

## Explanation

At fixed mass, minimum density occurs at maximum volume. To satisfy $\dfrac{24\,\mathrm g}{V_{\max}}\geq3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ we need $V_{\max}\leq8\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. The upper volume endpoint is $6.0+\Delta V$ in cubic centimetres, so $\Delta V\leq2\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. With this uncertainty all allowed volumes are positive, and at maximum volume the density reaches the limit exactly.
