To measure the thickness of a coin, $20$ identical coins are stacked and the height of the stack is measured as $(36.0\pm0.5)\,\mathrm{mm}$. What is the absolute uncertainty of the thickness of one coin?

- $0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.025\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.05\,\mathrm{mm}$
- $0.014\,\mathrm{mm}$

## Explanation

The thickness of one coin is the height of the stack divided by the exact number $20$. Therefore the central value and the absolute uncertainty are both divided by $20$, and the thickness is
$$
s=\left(\frac{36.0}{20}\pm\frac{0.5}{20}\right)\mathrm{mm}=(1.800\pm0.025)\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
The relative uncertainty stays that of the stack, $\dfrac{0.5}{36.0}\simeq1.4\,\%$, because the number $20$ has no uncertainty. The value $0.014\,\mathrm{mm}$ comes from taking this relative uncertainty as an absolute uncertainty, while $0.05\,\mathrm{mm}$ is obtained by dividing by $20$ the width of the interval instead of its radius.
