The denominator of a ratio lies in $[-0.1;0.3]$. Why is linear propagation around the centre problematic?

- Every denominator is exactly one
- Any ratio with an interval is dimensionless
- The interval includes zero and denominators arbitrarily close to zero
- The upper bound is too small to be measured

## Explanation

Division by zero is undefined. Near zero the reciprocal can grow without bound, making a small linear correction inadequate.
