The denominator of a ratio with a fixed positive numerator lies in $[-0.1;0.3]$. Why is linear propagation around the centre problematic?

- Every denominator is exactly one
- Any ratio with an interval is dimensionless
- The interval includes zero and denominators arbitrarily close to zero
- The upper bound is too small to be measured

## Explanation

The interval contains zero, where the quotient is undefined. It also allows positive or negative values arbitrarily close to zero, at which the magnitude of the quotient grows without a finite bound. A linear approximation around the centre would only describe local changes and would miss the behaviour near zero.
