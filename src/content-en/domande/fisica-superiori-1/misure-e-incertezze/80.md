Two similar scales each have twenty intervals. One goes from zero to $10\,\mathrm N$ and the other from zero to $50\,\mathrm N$. How do their divisions compare?

- Each division is worth one newton
- Divisions of the second are five times smaller
- The resolutions match because the numbers of marks are equal
- Each division of the second is worth five first-scale divisions

## Explanation

The first dial spreads $10\,\mathrm N$ over twenty intervals, and the second spreads $50\,\mathrm N$ over twenty. Their divisions are therefore $0.5\,\mathrm N$ and $2.5\,\mathrm N$. The ratio is $5$. The number of intervals is the same, so increasing the range also increases how much each division represents.
