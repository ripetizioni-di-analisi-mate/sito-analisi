A vertical wall is perfectly smooth. A body is pushed into it perpendicularly with no cords. What follows for static equilibrium at positive weight?

- A sufficiently large push can support the weight
- The wall's normal reaction turns vertical
- Friction reaches the weight even with zero coefficient
- The wall provides no vertical support

## Explanation

A smooth wall exerts only a horizontal normal reaction. No upward force remains to balance the weight.

The horizontal push can be balanced by the normal force, so the first balance is possible. The second leaves a downward resultant equal in magnitude to the weight. An additional vertical force would be needed.
