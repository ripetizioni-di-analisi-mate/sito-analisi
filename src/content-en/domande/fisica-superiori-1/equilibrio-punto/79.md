With two ropes at different slopes, why can't their tension magnitudes generally be set equal?

- Equilibrium always forces a common magnitude
- Both tensions have only a zero vertical component
- Horizontal components must cancel and their projections depend on the angles
- The steeper rope must have zero force

## Explanation

Horizontal balance uses each tension times its own cosine. Different angles generally give different factors, so equal component magnitudes do not imply equal tension magnitudes.

The ratio of the magnitudes follows directly from the inverse ratio of the cosines measured from the horizontal. Vertical equilibrium then sets the scale of the two tensions.
