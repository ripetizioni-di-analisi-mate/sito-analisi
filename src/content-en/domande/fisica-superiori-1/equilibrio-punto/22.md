A ring on a smooth horizontal plane is subject to a force of $12\,\mathrm N$ eastwards and one of $8\,\mathrm N$ westwards. A device can add a force of fixed magnitude $5\,\mathrm N$ in any direction in the plane. Can it balance the forces on the ring by choosing its direction?

- Yes, by pointing the force due west
- No, the balancing force must have magnitude $4\,\mathrm N$ and point west
- Yes, by choosing an angled direction with a westward component of $4\,\mathrm N$
- Yes, by pointing the force north, perpendicular to the resultant

## Explanation

The two given forces leave a resultant of $(12-8)\,\mathrm N=4\,\mathrm N$ eastwards. Cancelling it requires $4\,\mathrm N$ westwards and a zero north–south component. If the $5\,\mathrm N$ force is aimed to cancel only the east–west component, a north–south component of magnitude $\sqrt{5^2-4^2}\,\mathrm N=3\,\mathrm N$ remains.
