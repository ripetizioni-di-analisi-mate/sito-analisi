A ring resting on a smooth horizontal plane is subject to a force of $12\,\mathrm N$ eastwards and a force of $8\,\mathrm N$ westwards. A third force, parallel to the plane and of magnitude $5\,\mathrm N$, is applied to the ring. Is there a direction of the third force for which the ring is in equilibrium?

- Yes, if the third force points west
- No, because the balancing force must have magnitude $4\,\mathrm N$ and point west
- Yes, if the third force is oblique and has a westward component of $4\,\mathrm N$
- Yes, if the third force points north, perpendicular to the resultant

## Explanation

The resultant of the two given forces is $(12-8)\,\mathrm N=4\,\mathrm N$ and points east. The ring is in equilibrium only if the third force is the opposite of this resultant, that is, if it has magnitude $4\,\mathrm N$ and points west. The third force has magnitude $5\,\mathrm N$, so it does not coincide with the balancing force in any direction. For example, if its westward component is $4\,\mathrm N$, its north-south component has magnitude $\sqrt{5^2-4^2}\,\mathrm N=3\,\mathrm N$, therefore the total force on the ring is not zero.
