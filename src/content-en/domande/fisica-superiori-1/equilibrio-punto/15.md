The forces on a ring have a resultant $(12;-20)\,\mathrm N$. An inclined rope pulling up and left and a vertical rope pulling upwards are added. The magnitudes of the horizontal and vertical components of the inclined rope are $\dfrac35 T$ and $\dfrac45 T$. What is the tension in the vertical rope at equilibrium?

- $16\,\mathrm N$
- $20\,\mathrm N$
- $4\,\mathrm N$
- $8\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

Only the inclined rope can cancel the $12\,\mathrm N$ horizontal component of the resultant. Thus $\dfrac35 T=12\,\mathrm N$, giving $T=20\,\mathrm N$. Its vertical component is $\dfrac45\cdot20\,\mathrm N=16\,\mathrm N$. To provide the $20\,\mathrm N$ required for vertical equilibrium, the upright rope must supply the remaining $4\,\mathrm N$.
