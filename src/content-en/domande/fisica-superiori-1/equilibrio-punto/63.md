A $30\,\mathrm N$ load is held by an inclined rope pulling up and left and a horizontal rope pulling to the right. The horizontal and vertical fractions of the inclined tension are $\dfrac45$ and $\dfrac35$. What is the tension in the horizontal rope?

- $50\,\mathrm N$
- $30\,\mathrm N$
- $40\,\mathrm N$
- $24\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

The horizontal rope supplies no vertical support. The inclined rope must therefore provide the entire vertical component, so $\dfrac35 T=30\,\mathrm N$ and $T=50\,\mathrm N$. It pulls leftwards with $\dfrac45\cdot50\,\mathrm N=40\,\mathrm N$. To hold the load at rest, the other rope must pull rightwards with that same magnitude.
