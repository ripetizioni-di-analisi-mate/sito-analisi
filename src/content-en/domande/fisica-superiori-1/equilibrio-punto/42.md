A block is at rest on a smooth ramp, held by a rope parallel to the ramp. The tension in the rope has the same magnitude as the normal force exerted by the surface. What can be concluded about the height and the horizontal base of the ramp?

- The height is twice the base.
- The height equals the base.
- The height is half the base.
- The comparison depends on the block's mass.

## Explanation

Let $\alpha$ be the angle between the ramp and the horizontal. The block is in equilibrium, so along the axis parallel to the surface the tension balances $P_\parallel$ and equals $T=P\sin\alpha$. Along the perpendicular axis the normal force balances $P_\perp$, therefore $N=P\cos\alpha$. Since $T=N$, it follows that $\sin\alpha=\cos\alpha$, that is $\dfrac{h}{\ell}=\dfrac{b}{\ell}$. Hence the height of the ramp equals its base, whatever the mass of the block.
