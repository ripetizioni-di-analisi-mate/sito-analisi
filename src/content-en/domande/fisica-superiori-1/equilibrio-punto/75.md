A block weighing $20\,\mathrm N$ is pressed against a wall by a $30\,\mathrm N$ horizontal push. The coefficient of static friction is $0.40$. A vertical rope pulls upwards with $40\,\mathrm N$. With the horizontal push fixed, what is the smallest reduction in tension that allows the block to rest?

- $12\,\mathrm N$
- $20\,\mathrm N$
- $8\,\mathrm N$
- $32\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

The horizontal push sets the normal force to $30\,\mathrm N$ and the friction limit to $12\,\mathrm N$. The rope pulls upwards more strongly than the weight pulls downwards, so the required friction points downwards. At the limit the rope can reach $20\,\mathrm N+12\,\mathrm N=32\,\mathrm N$. From the initial $40\,\mathrm N$, the smallest reduction is $8\,\mathrm N$.
