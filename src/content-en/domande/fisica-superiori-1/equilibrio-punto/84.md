A body is assumed at rest on a ramp with $P_\parallel=20\,\mathrm N$, $N=30\,\mathrm N$, and $\mu_s=0.5$. An applied $4\,\mathrm N$ force acts uphill. Is rest admissible?

- Yes, because the downslope weight is smaller than the normal reaction
- Yes, because only $4\,\mathrm N$ of friction is required
- Yes, because static friction grows to any required magnitude
- No, the required $16\,\mathrm N$ exceeds the $15\,\mathrm N$ limit

## Explanation

The uphill force cancels part of the downslope weight. This still leaves sixteen newtons for friction to balance. The available limit is lower by one newton. Even with this small difference, the stated exact values fail the inequality required for the static model.

$$
\begin{aligned}f_{\mathrm{rich}}&=(20-4)\,\mathrm N=16\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]f_{\max}&=0.5\cdot30\,\mathrm N=15\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$
