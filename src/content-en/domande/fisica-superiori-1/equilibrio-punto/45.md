A block is at rest on a smooth ramp, held by a rope parallel to the ramp. A force of $12\,\mathrm N$, perpendicular to the ramp and directed outwards, also acts on the block, and the normal force from the surface is $20\,\mathrm N$. For what value of the perpendicular force does the normal force become zero, at the limit of contact?

- $32\,\mathrm N$
- $8\,\mathrm N$
- $20\,\mathrm N$
- $44\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

Along the axis perpendicular to the surface act the normal force and the force $F$, both directed outwards, and the component $P_\perp$ of the weight, directed towards the surface. Equilibrium along this axis gives $N+F=P_\perp$, so $P_\perp=(20+12)\,\mathrm N=32\,\mathrm N$. At the limit of contact $N=0$, therefore the force $F$ must balance $P_\perp$ on its own. Hence the normal force becomes zero for $F=32\,\mathrm N$.
