A calculated normal reaction uses only the parallel weight component and equals the rope tension on a ramp. How can this result be checked?

- Add weight and tension directly without projections
- Write the perpendicular balance and use the normal weight component
- Always set the normal reaction equal to tension
- Choose the rope as the positive axis and do nothing else

## Explanation

The parallel rope is in the along-ramp equation. The normal reaction belongs on the other axis. The results coincide only in particular configurations.

With no other applied forces, $T=P\sin\alpha$ and $N=P\cos\alpha$. They are equal at $45^\circ$, where sine and cosine coincide. The equality does not hold at an arbitrary slope angle.
