A block weighing $100\,\mathrm N$ is stationary on a smooth slope whose height, base and inclined length are in the ratio $3:4:5$. A horizontal force of $50\,\mathrm N$ pushes it towards the higher side. A rope parallel to the slope pulls it uphill. What are, in that order, the magnitudes of the normal force and the tension?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 4,0 4,3 0,0" vettori="2,1.5,3.15,1.5,F;2,1.5,2,-0.8,P" punti="2,1.5,pieno" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5 ymin=-1.2 ymax=3.6 titolo="The push F and the weight P are shown. Useful calculation axes are parallel and perpendicular to the slope."}

- $(80;10)\,\mathrm N$
- $(50;20)\,\mathrm N$
- $(110;20)\,\mathrm N$
- $(110;60)\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

Weight acts with $80\,\mathrm N$ into the slope and $60\,\mathrm N$ downhill. The horizontal force adds $50\cdot\dfrac35\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N$ into the slope and $50\cdot\dfrac45\,\mathrm N=40\,\mathrm N$ uphill. The normal force therefore balances $110\,\mathrm N$, and the rope supplies the remaining $20\,\mathrm N$ uphill.
