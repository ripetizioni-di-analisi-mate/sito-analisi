Two static trials use the same block and surface. In the first, the normal force is $20\,\mathrm N$ and the static friction magnitude is $6\,\mathrm N$. In the second, the normal force is $40\,\mathrm N$ and the friction magnitude is $8\,\mathrm N$. What can be inferred about the coefficient of static friction $\mu_s$?

- It is at least $0.30$.
- It is exactly $0.30$.
- It is at most $0.20$.
- It is exactly $0.25$.

## Explanation

Every measured static friction force must satisfy $|f|\leq\mu_sN$. The first trial requires $\mu_s\geq\dfrac6{20}=0.30$, and the second requires $\mu_s\geq\dfrac8{40}=0.20$. Both conditions must hold, so the lower bound is $0.30$. To determine an exact value, we would need to know whether a trial reached the sliding threshold.
