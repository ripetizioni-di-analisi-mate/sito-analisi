A smooth ramp has sloping length $10\,\mathrm m$. A rope parallel to the ramp needs tension $27\,\mathrm N$ to hold a $90\,\mathrm N$ load. How high is the ramp?

- $7\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{10}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $27\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

A smooth ramp supplies no friction. Along the slope, the rope balances the component of weight, making tension divided by weight the sine. Geometrically the same ratio is height divided by the sloping length. Multiply by ten metres to get the height, which must be less than the sloping length.

$$
\sin\alpha=\frac{T}{P}=\frac{27}{90},\qquad h=(10\,\mathrm m)\frac{27}{90}=3\,\mathrm m.
$$
