A parallel rope holds a block on a smooth ramp of positive height. To estimate the ratio of tension to weight, a student divides the height of the ramp by its horizontal base. What effect does this have on the calculated tension?

- The calculated tension is exact for every ramp.
- The tension is underestimated because the sloping side is longer than the base.
- The estimate always gives zero because the base is horizontal.
- The tension is overestimated because the sloping side is longer than the base.

## Explanation

The balance along the ramp requires $T=P\dfrac{h}{L}$, where $L$ is the sloping side. For a ramp of positive height that side is longer than the base. Using the base in the denominator increases the ratio and gives too much tension. The height and base together give $L$ by Pythagoras.
