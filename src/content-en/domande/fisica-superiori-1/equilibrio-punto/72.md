A parcel rests on a box on a table. In the first trial a string pulls upwards on the parcel with $4\,\mathrm N$. In the second trial the same upward force is applied to the box. The bodies are at rest and both contacts are maintained. From the first trial to the second, how do the normal force from the table on the box and that from the box on the parcel change?

- The table’s normal force increases by $4\,\mathrm N$ and the other stays the same.
- The table’s normal force drops by $4\,\mathrm N$ and the other increases by $4\,\mathrm N$.
- The table’s normal force stays the same and the other drops by $4\,\mathrm N$.
- The table’s normal force stays the same and the other increases by $4\,\mathrm N$.

## Explanation

For the box and parcel together, the weights and the external upward force are the same in both trials, so the table's normal force stays the same. For the parcel alone, the string supplies no support in the second trial. The box must then provide all of the parcel's upward support, which is $4\,\mathrm N$ more.
