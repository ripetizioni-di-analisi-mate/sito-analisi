A body weighing $15\,\mathrm N$ hangs from a vertical spring with $k=150\,\mathrm{N/m}$. A stop beneath the body prevents the extension from exceeding $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. When the body touches it, the stop can exert an upward reaction. At equilibrium, what upward forces are exerted by the spring and the stop, respectively?

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-0.7,3.1 0.7,3.1;0,3.1 0,2.8 -0.22,2.65 0.22,2.48 -0.22,2.32 0.22,2.16 -0.22,2 0.22,1.84 0,1.7 0,1.5;-0.8,0.52 0.8,0.52" poligoni="-0.32,0.98 0.32,0.98 0.32,1.5 -0.32,1.5" vettori="0.57,0.98,0.57,0.52" xmin=-1.35 xmax=1.52 ymin=0.15 ymax=3.45 titolo="Diagram not to scale. The stop limits how far the body can move downwards."}

- $(15;0)\,\mathrm N$
- $(0;15)\,\mathrm N$
- $(9;6)\,\mathrm N$
- $(6;9)\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

Without the stop, the extension would be $\dfrac{15}{150}\,\mathrm m=0.10\,\mathrm m$, exceeding the limit. The body therefore touches the stop, and the spring stretched by $0.06\,\mathrm m$ pulls upwards with $9\,\mathrm N$. The stop supplies the remaining $6\,\mathrm N$ needed to balance the weight. A positive normal force confirms contact.
