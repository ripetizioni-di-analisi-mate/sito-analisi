In the simple linear model the predictor values are $1,2,3,4$, and the errors, which are independent, have variance $\sigma^2=4$. What is the covariance between the mean of the responses $\bar Y$ and the random slope $B$?

- $1$
- $0$
- $\dfrac45$
- $2$

## Explanation

The errors enter $\bar Y$ with coefficients $\dfrac14$ and $B$ with coefficients $\dfrac{x_i-\bar x}{S_{xx}}$. Here $\bar x=2.5$ and $S_{xx}=2.25+0.25+0.25+2.25=5$, so the second coefficients are $-0.3$, $-0.1$, $0.1$, $0.3$. Different errors are independent, so the covariance is $\sigma^2$ times the sum of the products of the coefficients, $4\cdot\dfrac14\cdot(-0.3-0.1+0.1+0.3)=0$. The result holds for any set of predictor values, because $\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)=0$. The value $1$ is the variance $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}$ of $\bar Y$, and the value $\dfrac45$ is the variance $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{S_{xx}}$ of $B$.
