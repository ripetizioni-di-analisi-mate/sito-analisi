Two samples have the same Pearson correlation. Only the second contains a point with a predictor value far from the others. Which check distinguishes the role of this point in the two fits?

- Conclude that the point is influential because the correlations agree.
- Conclude that the two lines are equally sensitive because $r$ is the same.
- Change all measurement units to make the means agree.
- Compare the line for the second sample with the line fitted after removing the point.

## Explanation

Correlation summarizes linear association over the whole sample. A point far out on the predictor axis may have high leverage, but its influence also depends on its position relative to the line. To measure its effect, fit the line to the second sample with and without the point. Comparing the resulting slopes and intercepts quantifies how much the fit depends on that observation.
