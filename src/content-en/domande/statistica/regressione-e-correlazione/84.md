A line is constrained to pass through the origin and fitted by least squares. Which step in the proof of $SST=SSR+SSE$ using a centered total sum of squares might fail?

- The identity $e_i=y_i-\hat y_i$.
- The square of a residual is nonnegative.
- The free intercept guarantees that $\sum_i e_i$ vanishes.
- The calculation of the observed mean $\bar y$.

## Explanation

With a freely fitted intercept, differentiating the criterion with respect to it imposes $\sum_i e_i=0$. Requiring the line to pass through the origin removes this normal equation. The residuals can then have a nonzero sum. Expanding the squares about the mean gives a cross term containing $\sum_i e_i$, which may no longer vanish as the centered decomposition requires.
