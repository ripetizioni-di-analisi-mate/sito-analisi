In a regression with an intercept, $n=14$, $SSE=48$, and $S_{xx}=64$. Calculate $SE(b)$.

- $\dfrac14$
- $\dfrac12$
- $2$
- $\dfrac1{16}$

## Explanation

First estimate the spread of the errors. Estimating the intercept and slope leaves $14-2=12$ residual degrees of freedom. This gives $s_e^2=\dfrac{48}{12}=4$ and then $s_e=2$. The standard error of the slope divides this standard deviation by the square root of $S_{xx}$, yielding $SE(b)=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{64}}=\dfrac14$. The two denominators reflect the residual degrees of freedom and predictor spread, respectively.
