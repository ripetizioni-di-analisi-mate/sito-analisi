A point has a predictor value far from all others and lies exactly on the line fitted without it. What can happen when it is included?

- It can have high leverage and leave the fitted line unchanged.
- Its residual must be larger than all other residuals.
- It must reverse the sign of the slope.
- Its leverage is zero because it lies on the line.

## Explanation

Leverage increases with the distance from the predictor mean, so an outlying horizontal position can give high leverage. The existing line predicts the new point's response exactly, adding zero to the sum of squares. Any other line has at least as large a sum on the original data and adds a nonnegative square. The previous line therefore remains a minimizer.
