In a regression with an intercept the sample residuals have mean exactly zero. What does this establish about the model errors?

- It shows that the errors are independent and normal.
- It shows that the variance is constant at every predictor value.
- It shows that each individual error is zero.
- It cannot establish normality on its own because it already follows from the normal equations.

## Explanation

With a freely fitted intercept, the derivative of the sum of squares with respect to the intercept is $-2\sum_i e_i$. At the minimum it vanishes, so the residuals have mean zero by construction. Normality concerns the true errors, whose distributions may differ while still yielding centered residuals. The identity is a property of the fit and by itself provides no evidence about the shape of the error distribution.
