On three new observations a regression model produces a sum of squared errors of $15$. The constant prediction equal to the mean of the three responses gives $10$. What conclusion do these numbers justify?

- The model’s mean squared error exceeds that of the constant prediction by $50\%$ and the correlation must be negative
- On these data the model’s mean squared error exceeds that of the constant prediction by $50\%$, and the sign of the predictor–response correlation remains undetermined
- The model reduces mean squared error by one third compared with the mean
- The correlation is zero because the constant prediction is more accurate

## Explanation

Both sums are divided by the same number of observations, so the ratio of the mean squared errors is $15/10=1.5$. Sums of squared errors alone do not reveal whether responses rise or fall as the predictor increases.
