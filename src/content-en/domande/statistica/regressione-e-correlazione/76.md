Two predictor values are equally distant from $\bar x$, one to its right and the other to its left. In the same model, how do the mean response interval widths compare?

- The interval on the right is wider because the predictor value is larger.
- The interval on the left is wider because the signed distance is negative.
- The widths are equal because the weight depends on the squared distance.
- The widths are equal only when the estimated slope is zero.

## Explanation

The half-widths of the mean-response intervals are proportional to the square root of $h_0=\dfrac1n+\dfrac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}$. The two predictor values have opposite deviations from $\bar x$ and equal squared deviations, so their weights are equal. The same model and level give the same residual standard deviation and quantile, so the widths are equal.
