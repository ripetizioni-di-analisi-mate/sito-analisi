A population is strongly skewed with known standard deviation. A normal interval for the mean is based on five independent observations. Which assessment is correct?

- Knowing $\sigma$ guarantees exact coverage under every distribution
- The confidence level equals the proportion of observations inside the interval
- Exact coverage of the normal formula requires a normally distributed sample mean. With five observations, the approximation needs justification
- The margin must use five chi-square degrees of freedom

## Explanation

Knowing $\sigma$ gives the standard error of the mean, but standardization adjusts only location and scale. Using normal quantiles also requires normality of the standardized mean or an adequate normal approximation. Five observations from a highly skewed population do not, by sample size alone, justify that approximation.
