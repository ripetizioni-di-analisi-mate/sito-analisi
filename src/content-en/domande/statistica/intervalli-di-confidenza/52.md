A large sample comes from a markedly nonnormal population. Which statement about the chi-square interval for $\sigma^2$ is correct?

- Asymptotic normality of the mean makes every function of the data chi-square distributed
- The chi-square distribution is exact provided that $n>30$
- The sample variance always has an exact normal distribution
- The exact chi-square distribution of the pivot depends on normally distributed observations

## Explanation

The central limit theorem for the mean concerns the distribution of standardized $\bar X$. The chi-square interval for variance relies on the distribution of $\dfrac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2}$. This quantity has a chi-square distribution with $n-1$ degrees of freedom when the observations are normal. The central limit theorem says nothing about its distribution, so a large sample does not make it chi-square. If values far from the mean are more probable in the population than under the normal distribution, the coverage of the chi-square interval stays below the level even for large $n$.
