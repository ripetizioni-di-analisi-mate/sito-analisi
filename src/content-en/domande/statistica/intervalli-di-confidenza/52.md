A large sample comes from a markedly nonnormal population. Does the central limit theorem for the mean make the chi-square interval for $\sigma^2$ exact?

- Asymptotic normality of the mean makes every function of the data chi-square distributed
- The chi-square distribution is exact provided that $n>30$
- The sample variance always has an exact normal distribution
- The exact chi-square distribution of the pivot depends on normally distributed observations

## Explanation

The central limit theorem for the mean concerns the distribution of standardized $\bar X$. The chi-square interval for variance relies on the distribution of $\dfrac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2}$. The normal model supplies its exact distribution. A large sample can support other approximations, but the theorem for the mean does not establish an exact chi-square distribution for the variance pivot.
