A survey was planned with $p_*=\dfrac12$, $z=2$, and Wald margin $0.10$, hence with $n=100$. After the data are collected, the observed proportion is $\widehat p=0.10$. What Wald margin is actually obtained?

- $0.06$
- $0.10$
- $0.03$
- $0.18$

## Explanation

The Wald margin is $z\sqrt{\dfrac{\widehat p(1-\widehat p)}{n}}=2\sqrt{\dfrac{0.10\cdot0.90}{100}}=2\sqrt{0.0009}=2\cdot0.03=0.06$. The value $0.10$ is the design margin, computed with $p_*=\dfrac12$, which maximizes the product $p(1-p)$. Since $0.10\cdot0.90=0.09$ is less than $0.25$, the margin obtained is smaller than the design margin, as the conservative choice ensures.
