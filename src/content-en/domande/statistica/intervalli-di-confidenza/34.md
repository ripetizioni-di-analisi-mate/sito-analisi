For a normal sample, $\sigma$ is replaced by $S$ in the pivot for the mean. Which fact establishes its exact t distribution?

- The sample variance equals the population variance with probability one
- The mean and the sample variance are independent in every population
- The mean standardized using $\sigma$ is independent of the normalized sample variance
- The sample contains equal numbers of observations above and below its mean

## Explanation

For a normal sample, the mean standardized using $\sigma$ has a standard normal distribution. Also, $(n-1)S^2$ divided by $\sigma^2$ has a chi-square distribution with $n-1$ degrees of freedom and is independent of the sample mean. Replacing $\sigma$ with $S$ divides the normal numerator by the square root of the chi-square variable divided by its degrees of freedom. The resulting ratio has a t distribution.
