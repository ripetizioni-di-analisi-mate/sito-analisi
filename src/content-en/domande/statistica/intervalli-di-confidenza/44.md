An interval for a proportion contains the values of $p$ for which $n(\widehat p-p)^2\leq z^2p(1-p)$. Which construction is this?

- The Wald interval, replacing $p$ with $\widehat p$ in the variance
- The exact binomial interval obtained from the tails
- A t interval for the mean of two samples
- The Wilson interval, with the standard error computed under each value of $p$

## Explanation

Dividing by $n$ and taking square roots, the inequality is equivalent to $|\widehat p-p|\leq z\sqrt{\dfrac{p(1-p)}{n}}$. The standard error on the right is computed with the value $p$ being assessed, not with $\widehat p$ as in the Wald interval. The exact binomial interval instead uses the binomial probabilities of the two tails, with no normal quantile. Collecting terms in $p$ gives a quadratic inequality, and the values between its two roots form the Wilson interval.
