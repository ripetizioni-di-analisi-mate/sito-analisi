For a normal sample with known standard deviation, the sample mean centered at $\mu$ is divided by its standard error. Which property makes this ratio a pivot?

- The ratio always equals zero
- The standard normal distribution is the same for every value of $\mu$
- The ratio equals $\mu$ for every sample
- The ratio contains only observable quantities

## Explanation

The mean of independent normal observations has expectation $\mu$ and standard deviation $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}$. Subtracting $\mu$ centers it at zero, and dividing by its standard error gives variance one. The ratio contains the parameter but always has a standard normal distribution. We can therefore choose its quantiles before knowing $\mu$.
