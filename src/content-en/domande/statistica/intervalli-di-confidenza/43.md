For a Wilson interval with $n=36$, $K=18$, and $z=2$, which expression gives its half-width?

- $\dfrac16$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{40}}$
- $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{40}}$
- $\dfrac1{20}$

## Explanation

With $K=18$ in $36$ trials, $\widehat p=\dfrac12$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $n$, the Wilson half-width can be written $h=\dfrac{z}{n+z^2}\sqrt{n\widehat p(1-\widehat p)+\dfrac{z^2}{4}}$. Here $n+z^2=36+4=40$ and the quantity under the square root is $36\cdot\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac12+\dfrac{2^2}{4}=9+1=10$. Thus the half-width is $\dfrac{2}{40}\sqrt{10}=\dfrac{2\sqrt{10}}{40}$. Since $\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}$, the same quantity can be written as $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{40}}$.
