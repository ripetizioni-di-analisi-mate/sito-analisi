An independent sample of $6$ units from a normal population has $s^2=6$. Use $\chi^2_{5,0.95}=11.07$ and $\chi^2_{5,0.05}=1.145$ to find the lower endpoint of the $90\,\%$ confidence interval for the variance $\sigma^2$. Round to two decimal places.

- $2.71$
- $1.65$
- $26.20$
- $3.25$

## Explanation

Under the normal model the pivot for the variance is $\dfrac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2}$. Its observed numerator is $(6-1)\cdot6=30$. Since the variance and quantile are positive, $\dfrac{30}{\sigma^2}\leq11.07$ is equivalent to $\sigma^2\geq\dfrac{30}{11.07}$. The larger quantile therefore gives the lower endpoint, approximately $2.71$.
