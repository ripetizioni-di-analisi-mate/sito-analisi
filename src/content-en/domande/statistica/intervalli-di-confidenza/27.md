A laboratory makes $9$ weighings of a reference sample whose true mass is $100\,\mathrm{g}$. The weighings are normal and independent, with known standard deviation $\sigma=3\,\mathrm{g}$, and their mean is $101.8\,\mathrm{g}$. Which statement about the $95\,\%$ interval for the mean, with $z=1.96$, is correct?

- The interval is $[99.84,103.76]$ and contains the true mass
- The interval is $[101.15,102.45]$ and excludes the true mass
- The interval is $[95.92,107.68]$ and contains the true mass
- The interval is $[100.16,103.45]$ and excludes the true mass

## Explanation

The standard error of the mean is $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt9}=1\,\mathrm{g}$, so the margin is $1.96\cdot1=1.96\,\mathrm{g}$. The interval runs from $101.8-1.96=99.84$ to $101.8+1.96=103.76$. Since $99.84\leq100\leq103.76$, the interval contains the true mass and its coverage indicator equals $1$. Dividing $\sigma$ by $n$ instead of $\sqrt n$ would give the interval $[101.15,102.45]$, which is too narrow; using $\sigma$ in place of the standard error would give $[95.92,107.68]$; using the $90\,\%$ quantile $1.645$ would give $[100.16,103.45]$.
