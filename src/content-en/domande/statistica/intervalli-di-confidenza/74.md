When estimating a proportion with a specified margin and no pilot data, why is $p_*=\dfrac12$ the conservative choice for sample-size planning?

- It guarantees that the observed proportion is exactly $\dfrac12$
- It maximizes the product $p_*(1-p_*)$ over $[0,1]$
- It minimizes the required sample size among all pilot values
- It makes the product $p_*(1-p_*)$ equal to zero

## Explanation

For fixed $n$ the normal margin increases with $p_*(1-p_*)$ because this product is under a square root. Rewriting it as $\dfrac14-(p_*-\dfrac12)^2$ shows that its maximum on $[0,1]$ is $\dfrac14$, attained at $p_*=\dfrac12$. Planning for this value gives the most conservative sample size for the anticipated margin.
