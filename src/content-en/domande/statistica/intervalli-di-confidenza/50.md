Let $D$ be the sum of squared deviations and $a$ the lower chi-square quantile used in the pivot. Which inequality is equivalent to $a\leq\dfrac{D}{\sigma^2}$ when $a>0$ and $\sigma^2>0$?

- $\sigma^2\geq\dfrac{D}{a}$
- $\sigma^2\geq\dfrac{a}{D}$
- $\sigma^2\leq\dfrac{D}{a}$
- $\sigma^2\leq aD$

## Explanation

The denominator $\sigma^2$ is positive. Multiplying both sides of $a\leq\dfrac{D}{\sigma^2}$ by $\sigma^2$ preserves the inequality and gives $a\sigma^2\leq D$. Since $a$ is also positive, dividing by $a$ gives $\sigma^2\leq\dfrac{D}{a}$. Thus the lower inequality on the pivot gives an upper bound for the variance.
