In a relative frequency density histogram, two classes have the same height. The second is twice as wide as the first. How do their relative frequencies compare?

- The first frequency is twice the second
- The frequencies are equal
- The total number of observations is needed to compare their frequencies
- The second frequency is twice the first

## Explanation

Let $b$ be the first class width and $h$ the common height. The areas are $bh$ and $(2b)h=2bh$. Since areas represent relative frequencies, the second frequency is twice the first. Equal heights indicate equal densities, or proportions per unit width.
