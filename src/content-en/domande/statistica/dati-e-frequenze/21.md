A stopwatch records durations to the nearest whole second. What distinguishes the physical duration from the recorded value?

- The duration is modeled as continuous and the recorded value is rounded
- The duration must already be an integer before recording
- Both are nominal because they appear in a database
- The duration is nominal and the recorded value is ordinal

## Explanation

A physical duration might be $2.4\,\mathrm{s}$ or $2.6\,\mathrm{s}$. Rounding to the nearest second, the stopwatch records them as $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $3\,\mathrm{s}$. The measured quantity is continuous, while the recorded values are discrete. Two distinct durations, such as $2.1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2.4\,\mathrm{s}$, can consequently produce the same reading.
