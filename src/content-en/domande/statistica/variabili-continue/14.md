A component has an exponential lifetime with mean $6\,\mathrm{h}$ and has already lasted $10\,\mathrm{h}$. Calculate the probability that it lasts at least another $3\,\mathrm{h}$.

- $e^{-\frac{13}{6}}$
- $e^{-\frac12}$
- $1-e^{-\frac12}$
- $e^{-\frac53}$

## Explanation

The three hours are additional time, so we need the probability of lasting beyond thirteen hours given survival beyond ten. For an exponential distribution with mean six hours, the tail at $t$ hours is $e^{-\frac t6}$. Surviving thirteen hours implies surviving ten, so divide the former tail probability by the latter.

$$
\frac{e^{-\frac{13}{6}}}{e^{-\frac{10}{6}}}=e^{-\frac36}=e^{-\frac12}.
$$

The elapsed time cancels, as the memoryless property predicts.
