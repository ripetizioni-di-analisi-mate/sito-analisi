Let $X$ be a time in seconds with standard deviation $30\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the variance of the same duration expressed in minutes?

- $\dfrac12\,\mathrm{min}^2$
- $15\,\mathrm{min}^2$
- $\dfrac14\,\mathrm{min}^2$
- $30\,\mathrm{min}^2$

## Explanation

To convert seconds to minutes, divide each duration by $60$. Deviations from the mean scale by the same conversion factor, so the standard deviation changes from $30\,\mathrm{s}$ to $\dfrac{30}{60}\,\mathrm{min}=\dfrac12\,\mathrm{min}$. The variance is the square of the standard deviation.

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X\text{ in minutes})
=\left(\frac12\,\mathrm{min}\right)^2=\frac14\,\mathrm{min}^2.
$$

Converting variance therefore requires the square of the conversion factor for individual values.
