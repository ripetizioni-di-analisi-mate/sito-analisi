Let $X$ have density $2x$ on $(0,1)$ and zero elsewhere, and let $Y=X^2$. What is the density of $Y$ for $0<y<1$?

- $2y$
- $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt y}$
- $1$
- $4y^3$

## Explanation

The function $x^2$ is strictly increasing on $(0,1)$ and maps the interval $(0,1)$ onto itself. Its inverse is $x=\sqrt y$, with derivative $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt y}$. The formula for a monotone transformation gives

$$
f_Y(y)=f_X(\sqrt y)\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt y}=2\sqrt y\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt y}=1,\qquad 0<y<1.
$$

The density of $Y$ is constant, so $Y$ is uniform on $(0,1)$. The value $2y$ comes from forgetting the factor given by the derivative of the inverse.
