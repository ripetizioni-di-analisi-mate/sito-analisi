The density equals $1$ on $(0,1)$ and zero elsewhere. Why does $P(2<X<3)=0$ hold?

- The density is constant on $(0,1)$.
- The interval is disjoint from the support.
- The specified interval has length one.
- The density equals one somewhere.

## Explanation

The probability of the interval $(2,3)$ is the integral of the density over it. The support lies between zero and one, so no part of the specified interval lies in the support. The density is zero throughout the integration interval and the area is zero. An interval can have positive length and zero probability when it lies entirely outside the region of positive density.
