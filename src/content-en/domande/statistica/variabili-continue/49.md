The rate of an exponential distribution changes from $\lambda$ to $2\lambda$. How does the median change?

- It is halved.
- It doubles.
- It stays the same.
- It is divided by four.

## Explanation

The median $m$ satisfies $P(T>m)=\dfrac12$. The tail formula gives $e^{-\lambda m}=\dfrac12$. Taking logarithms yields $\lambda m=\log 2$, so $m=\dfrac{\log2}{\lambda}$. When the rate doubles, the numerator remains $\log2$ while the denominator becomes $2\lambda$. The new median is $\dfrac{\log2}{2\lambda}=\dfrac m2$. It takes half as long to reach the same cumulative probability.
