A continuous cumulative distribution function equals $\dfrac{x^2}{4}$ for $0<x<2$. What is the density at $x=1$ on the differentiable segment?

- $\dfrac14$
- $1$
- $\dfrac12$
- $2$

## Explanation

The density on the differentiable segment is the derivative of the cumulative distribution function, measuring how fast probability accumulates. Differentiate $\dfrac{x^2}{4}$ before substituting $x=1$.

$$
f_X(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{x^2}{4}\right)=\frac{x}{2},
\qquad f_X(1)=\frac12.
$$

The cumulative distribution function at the same point is $\dfrac14$ and measures the area accumulated up to $1$.
