The time $X$, in hours, that a student would need to complete a test is uniformly distributed on $(0,2)$. The test lasts at most one hour, so a student who has not finished hands in at the deadline and the hand-in time is $Y=\min(X,1)$. Which statement about the distribution of $Y$ is true?

- $Y$ has density $\dfrac12$ on $(0,1)$ and zero elsewhere
- $P(Y=1)=\dfrac12$ and $Y$ has no density
- $Y$ has density $1$ on $(0,1)$ and zero elsewhere
- $P(Y=1)=0$

## Explanation

The student hands in at the deadline when $X\ge1$, so the event $\{Y=1\}$ coincides with the event $\{1\le X<2\}$. Since $X$ is uniform on $(0,2)$, this event has probability $\dfrac{2-1}{2}=\dfrac12$, so $P(Y=1)=\dfrac12$. By the theorem on the probability at a single point, a variable with a density assigns probability zero to every single value, hence $Y$ **has no density**. The answer $P(Y=1)=0$ comes from applying that theorem to $Y$, which does not satisfy its hypothesis. A density $\dfrac12$ on $(0,1)$ would instead give total area $\dfrac12$, and a density $1$ on $(0,1)$ would give $P(Y<1)=1$, whereas $P(Y<1)=P(X<1)=\dfrac12$.
