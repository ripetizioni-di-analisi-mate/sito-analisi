A measurement $X$ is uniform on $(0,2)$. A program stores $Y=\min(X,1)$. Which observation shows that the distribution of $Y$ cannot be described solely by an ordinary density?

- $Y$ takes no negative values
- $P(Y=1)=1/2$
- $P(Y<1)=1/2$
- $Y$ has a finite upper bound

## Explanation

All inputs with $X\ge1$ map to the single output $Y=1$. They occupy half of the uniform interval, so that point has mass $1/2$. A distribution described only by a density assigns zero probability to every single point.
