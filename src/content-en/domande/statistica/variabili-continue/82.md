Let $X\sim U(0,2)$ and $Y=X^2$. What is the density of $Y$ for $0<y<4$?

- $\dfrac1{2\sqrt y}$
- $\dfrac1{4\sqrt y}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt y}{2}$
- $\dfrac14$

## Explanation

Squaring is increasing on the support of $X$. For $0<y<4$, the condition $X^2\le y$ is therefore equivalent to $X\le\sqrt y$. On the original scale the contributing interval has length $\sqrt y$ and the support has length two. Their ratio gives the cumulative distribution function. Differentiate it to obtain the density.

$$
F_Y(y)=\frac{\sqrt y}{2},\qquad
f_Y(y)=\frac{1}{4\sqrt y}.
$$

The first factor $\dfrac12$ comes from the support length. The other comes from differentiating the square root.
