The function $f$ equals $\dfrac34$ on $(0,1)$ and $-\dfrac14$ on $(1,2)$. Which property prevents it from being a density even after multiplication by a positive constant?

- It has two constant pieces.
- It is discontinuous at $1$.
- It takes negative values on an interval.
- It is less than $1$ on $(0,1)$.

## Explanation

A density must give nonnegative area on every interval because the area represents a probability. On $(1,2)$ this function is identically $-\dfrac14$. After multiplication by a positive constant $c$, the integral over the interval would be $-\dfrac c4$, still negative. A factor can make the total area one, but the sign on $(1,2)$ still violates the definition of a density.
