For the transformation $U=X+Y$, $V=X-Y$, what is the inverse?

- $x=\dfrac{u+v}{2},\,y=\dfrac{u-v}{2}$
- $x=u+v,\,y=u-v$
- $x=\dfrac{u-v}{2},\,y=\dfrac{u+v}{2}$
- $x=\dfrac u2,\,y=\dfrac v2$

## Explanation

Adding $u=x+y$ and $v=x-y$ eliminates the terms in $y$ and gives $u+v=2x$. Subtracting the second equation from the first eliminates the terms in $x$ and gives $u-v=2y$. Dividing both equations by two gives the inverse, $x=\dfrac{u+v}{2}$ and $y=\dfrac{u-v}{2}$. Substituting these values into $x+y$ and $x-y$ returns $u$ and $v$, verifying the result.
