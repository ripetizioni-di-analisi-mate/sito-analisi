Given $\operatorname{Var}(X)=5$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=3$, calculate $\operatorname{Cov}(X,X-2Y)$.

- $-1$
- $2$
- $8$
- $11$

## Explanation

Covariance is linear in its second argument, so $\operatorname{Cov}(X,X-2Y)=\operatorname{Cov}(X,X)-2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$. The first term is the variance of $X$, namely $5$, because its two centered factors coincide. Substituting $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=3$ gives $5-2\cdot3=-1$. The coefficient $-2$ appears to the first power here. Its square would appear in the variance of $-2Y$, which involves the same centered variable twice.
