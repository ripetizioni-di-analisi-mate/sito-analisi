If $\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X-Y)$ and the second moments are finite, which conclusion follows?

- The variables are always independent
- The variances are equal
- The covariance is zero
- The means are equal

## Explanation

The individual variance terms are the same for the sum and the difference. Their respective cross terms are $2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$ and $-2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$. Subtracting the variances leaves $4\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$. The difference is given as zero, so the covariance is zero. For arbitrary joint distributions with positive variances, this establishes zero linear correlation.
