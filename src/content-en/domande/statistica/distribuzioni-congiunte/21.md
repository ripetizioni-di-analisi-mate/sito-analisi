If $F_{X,Y}(a,b)=0$ and $F_{X,Y}(c,b)>0$ for $a<c$, which event must have positive probability?

- $X\le a,\,Y>b$
- $a<X\le c,\,Y\le b$
- $X>c,\,Y\le b$
- $X\le a,\,Y\le b$

## Explanation

The event $\{X\le c,Y\le b\}$ splits into two disjoint parts, one with $X\le a$ and the other with $a<X\le c$. Both retain the constraint $Y\le b$. The second part has probability $F_{X,Y}(c,b)-F_{X,Y}(a,b)=F_{X,Y}(c,b)>0$. Fixing $Y=b$ would select a smaller portion of the event. Any positive mass at $X=a$ belongs to the first part and is subtracted to exclude the left boundary.
