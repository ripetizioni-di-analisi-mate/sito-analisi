A population is split into two groups. The two group means are identical but their within-group variances differ. Which quantity can be determined without knowing the group proportions?

- The between-group variance component is zero
- The total variance is the arithmetic mean of the two variances
- The total variance equals the larger variance
- The within-group standard deviations are equal

## Explanation

The conditional mean given the group is constant. Its variance, the between-group component, is therefore zero for all group weights. The within-group component is a weighted average of the variances and depends on the weights.
