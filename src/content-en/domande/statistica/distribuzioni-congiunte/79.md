A pair is uniform on the square $(-1,1)^2$. Why does the transformation $u=x^2$, $v=y$ require two interior branches for $0<u<1$?

- The pairs $(\sqrt u,v)$ and $(-\sqrt u,v)$ have the same image
- The determinant of the transformation always vanishes
- The image has probability zero
- The value $v$ always has two preimages

## Explanation

For $0<u<1$ the equation $x^2=u$ has two solutions, $\sqrt u$ and $-\sqrt u$, both inside $(-1,1)$. The second equation fixes $y=v$ in both cases. These are distinct preimages on the branches $x>0$ and $x<0$. Both branches therefore contribute to the transformed density, and their contributions must be added.
