What information is already sufficient to calculate $E[X^2]$ from a joint distribution?

- The marginal distribution of $X$
- The marginal distribution of $Y$
- The value $E[X]$ alone
- The value $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$ alone

## Explanation

The function $X^2$ depends only on the first coordinate. All pairs with the same $X$ therefore contribute the same squared value. Add their masses first to obtain the marginal distribution of $X$. The expectation is $\sum_x x^2p_X(x)$ in the discrete case, with the corresponding integral when a density exists.
