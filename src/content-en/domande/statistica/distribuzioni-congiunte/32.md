The pair $(X,Y)$ has density $f_{X,Y}(x,y)=x+y$ on the square $0<x<1$, $0<y<1$ and zero elsewhere. Calculate $P\left(Y>\dfrac12\ \middle|\ X=\dfrac12\right)$.

- $\dfrac12$
- $\dfrac38$
- $\dfrac34$
- $\dfrac58$

## Explanation

The marginal density of $X$ at $x=\dfrac12$ is obtained by integrating the vertical cross section:
$$
f_X\left(\frac12\right)=\int_0^1\left(\frac12+y\right)dy=\frac12+\frac12=1.
$$
The conditional density is therefore $f_{Y\mid X}\left(y\ \middle|\ \dfrac12\right)=\dfrac{\frac12+y}{1}=\dfrac12+y$ for $0<y<1$. It is not uniform, because it increases with $y$. The required probability is
$$
\int_{1/2}^1\left(\frac12+y\right)dy=\left[\frac y2+\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_{1/2}^1=1-\frac38=\frac58.
$$
The value $\dfrac38$ is the probability of the part $0<y<\dfrac12$, and $\dfrac12$ would result from a constant conditional density.
