A density on the unit square is proportional to $1+xy$. Which comparison could rule out independence?

- The marginal distributions of $X$ and $Y$ coincide
- The square has area one
- The density integrates to one
- The density of $Y$ given $X=x$ varies with $x$

## Explanation

Write $f(x,y)=c(1+xy)$ on the square and integrate with respect to $y$ to obtain $f_X(x)=c(1+\dfrac x2)$. The conditional density is therefore $\dfrac{1+xy}{1+\frac x2}$. Its coefficient of $y$ varies with $x$ throughout $(0,1)$. Under independence this density would equal the same marginal density of $Y$ almost everywhere. Its variation rules out independence.
