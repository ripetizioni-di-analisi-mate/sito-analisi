For $X,Y\in\{0,1\}$, with $p_{ij}=P(X=i,\ Y=j)$, the joint masses are $p_{00}=\dfrac1{10}$, $p_{01}=\dfrac2{10}$, and $p_{10}=\dfrac4{10}$. Calculate $P(X=1)$.

- $\dfrac3{10}$
- $\dfrac7{10}$
- $\dfrac6{10}$
- $\dfrac4{10}$

## Explanation

The marginal distribution of $X$ includes all pairs with the specified first coordinate, so for $X=1$ we need $p_{10}$ and $p_{11}$. The three given masses sum to $\dfrac7{10}$. All four masses sum to one, so $p_{11}=1-\dfrac7{10}=\dfrac3{10}$. This gives $P(X=1)=\dfrac4{10}+\dfrac3{10}=\dfrac7{10}$. Using only mass $p_{10}$ would count just those cases where $Y=0$ also holds.
