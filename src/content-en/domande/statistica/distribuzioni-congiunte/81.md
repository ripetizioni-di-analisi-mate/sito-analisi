If $u=x$ and $v=y-x$, what multiplicative factor does the Jacobian of the inverse introduce?

- $2$
- $1$
- $\dfrac12$
- $0$

## Explanation

The inverse is given by $x=u$ and $y=u+v$. Its derivative matrix with respect to $(u,v)$ has rows $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$. The matrix is triangular, so its determinant is the product of its diagonal entries, $1\cdot1=1$. The multiplier is therefore one. The transformation shears the domain and preserves area. For each fixed $u$, the inverse adds $u$ to coordinate $v$.
