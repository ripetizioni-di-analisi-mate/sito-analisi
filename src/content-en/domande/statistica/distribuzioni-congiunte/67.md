If $X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(2)$ and $Y\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(3)$ are independent, what is $P(X+Y=0)$?

- $e^{-6}$
- $e^{-2}+e^{-3}$
- $1-e^{-5}$
- $e^{-5}$

## Explanation

Poisson variables take nonnegative integer values. Their sum is zero precisely when both variables are zero. A Poisson distribution with parameter $\lambda$ assigns probability $e^{-\lambda}$ to zero. By independence, multiplying $e^{-2}$ and $e^{-3}$ gives $e^{-5}$. This computes just the mass at the origin. Its complement would give the probability of a positive sum.
