Given $P(A\mid B)=0.7$, suppose that groups $B$ and $B^c$ both have positive probability. Which value is determined by this information alone?

- $P(A\mid B^c)=0.3$.
- $P(A^c\mid B)=0.3$.
- $P(B\mid A)=0.7$.
- $P(A)=0.7$.

## Explanation

Within group $B$ each outcome belongs either to $A$ or to its complement. The two proportions therefore sum to one and $P(A^c\mid B)=1-0.7=0.3$. Finding $P(A\mid B^c)$ would require the proportion of $A$ outside $B$. The intersection $P(A\cap B)$ would also require an additional value, the probability of $B$, because it equals $0.7P(B)$.
