A batch of $10$ parts contains $3$ defective parts. Two parts are taken without replacement. What is the probability that at least one of the two parts is defective?

- $\dfrac{1}{15}$
- $\dfrac{7}{15}$
- $\dfrac{8}{15}$
- $\dfrac{51}{100}$

## Explanation

The event “at least one defective” has as its complement the event “no defective,” that is, two nondefective parts. The first part is nondefective with probability $\dfrac{7}{10}$. After a nondefective part, $6$ nondefective parts remain among $9$, so by the multiplication rule two nondefective parts have probability $\dfrac{7}{10}\cdot\dfrac69=\dfrac{7}{15}$. By the complement rule, the required probability is $1-\dfrac{7}{15}=\dfrac{8}{15}$. The value $\dfrac{51}{100}=1-\left(\dfrac{7}{10}\right)^2$ would be correct with replacement, because the second draw would then again be from $7$ nondefective parts out of $10$.
