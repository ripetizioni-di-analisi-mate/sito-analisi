Events $A$ and $B^c$ are independent. What follows about $A$ and $B$?

- They are complementary.
- They are dependent unless $P(A)=P(B)$.
- They are disjoint.
- They are independent.

## Explanation

Event $A$ is the disjoint union of $A\cap B$ and $A\cap B^c$. Since $A$ and $B^c$ are independent, the second term has probability $P(A)P(B^c)$. Subtracting it from $P(A)$ gives $P(A\cap B)=P(A)[1-P(B^c)]=P(A)P(B)$. This is precisely the condition for independence of $A$ and $B$. The proof uses products of probabilities and also holds when one event has probability zero.
