Two fair dice are rolled independently. Let $A$ be the event “the first die shows $6$” and $B$ the event “the sum is $12$.” What follows?

- $P(A\mid B)=1$ and the events are dependent.
- $P(A\mid B)=1$ and the events are independent.
- $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{1}{6}$ and the events are independent.
- $P(A\mid B)=0$ because the events are disjoint.

## Explanation

A sum of $12$ requires both dice to show $6$. Every outcome of $B$ therefore belongs to $A$ and $P(A\mid B)=1$. The marginal probability of $A$ is $\dfrac16$. Since $B$ has positive probability, the difference between the conditional and marginal probabilities proves dependence. Independence of the individual dice concerns their separate results, while $B$ involves both dice.
