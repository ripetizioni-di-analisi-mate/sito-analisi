Suppose $P(A)=\dfrac{2}{5}$, $P(B)=\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $P(A\cup B)=\dfrac{7}{10}$. What is $P(A\mid B)$?

- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4}{7}$

## Explanation

The union formula subtracts the intersection because adding $P(A)$ and $P(B)$ counts shared outcomes twice. Solving for the intersection probability gives $P(A\cap B)=\dfrac25+\dfrac12-\dfrac7{10}=\dfrac15$. Condition $B$ has probability $\dfrac12$, so $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{\dfrac15}{\dfrac12}=\dfrac25$. In this example the proportion of $A$ among outcomes in $B$ equals its marginal probability, so the events are independent.
