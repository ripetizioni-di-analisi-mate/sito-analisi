Two distinct positions are chosen at random from four. Let $A$ be the event “position $1$ was chosen” and $B$ the event “at least one of positions $1$ and $2$ was chosen.” What is $P(A\mid B)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$

## Explanation

Choosing two distinct positions from four gives $\binom42=6$ equally likely pairs. Condition $B$ excludes only $\{3,4\}$, leaving five pairs. Of these, $\{1,2\}$, $\{1,3\}$ and $\{1,4\}$ contain position $1$. This gives $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac35$. Before conditioning, the share of pairs containing $1$ is $\dfrac36=\dfrac12$. Excluding a pair without $1$ reduces the denominator while leaving the numerator fixed.
