Four independent attempts are made, each with success probability $\dfrac{1}{5}$. Given that the first two failed, what is the probability of at least one success in the remaining two?

- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{25}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{9}{25}$

## Explanation

Independence leaves the success probabilities of the remaining two attempts unchanged after the observed failures. Each new attempt fails with probability $\dfrac45$, and the two outcomes are independent, so two failures have probability $\left(\dfrac45\right)^2=\dfrac{16}{25}$. At least one success is the complement and has probability $1-\dfrac{16}{25}=\dfrac9{25}$. The condition already fixes the first two attempts.
