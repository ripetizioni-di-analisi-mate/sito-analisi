Events $A$ and $B$ are independent with probabilities $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$. What is the probability that exactly one occurs?

- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{5}{6}$

## Explanation

Exactly one event occurs in the two disjoint cases $A\cap B^c$ and $A^c\cap B$. Since $A$ and $B$ are independent, probabilities still multiply when one event is replaced by its complement. The two probabilities are $\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac23=\dfrac13$ and $\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac13=\dfrac16$ and sum to $\dfrac12$. The case in which both occur belongs to the union $A\cup B$, but is excluded here.
