Failure $A$ in a machine has probability $\dfrac{1}{5}$. When $A$ occurs, failure $B$ follows with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. In the absence of $A$, failure $B$ has probability $\dfrac{1}{4}$. What is the probability that exactly one of the two failures occurs?

- $\dfrac{3}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$

## Explanation

Exactly one failure occurs on $A\cap B^c$ or $A^c\cap B$. The first case has probability $\dfrac15\cdot\left(1-\dfrac12\right)=\dfrac1{10}$ and the second $\dfrac45\cdot\dfrac14=\dfrac15$. The first requires $A$ and the second excludes it, so the cases are disjoint and their probabilities sum to $\dfrac3{10}$. Each rate for failure $B$ must be applied within the condition for which it is given.
