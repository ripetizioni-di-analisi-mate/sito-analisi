Two events $A$ and $B$ satisfy $P(A\cap B)=P(A)$, with $P(A)>0$ and $P(B)<1$. Can they be independent?

- No, because independence would require $P(B)=1$.
- Yes, because $P(A\mid B)=P(A)$ by assumption.
- It depends on $P(A\cup B)$.
- Yes, because the intersection has positive probability.

## Explanation

The equality $P(A\cap B)=P(A)$ implies $P(B\mid A)=1$ because $P(A)>0$. Under independence this conditional probability would instead equal $P(B)$. The given value $P(B)<1$ rules out equality, so the events are dependent. In terms of intersections, the outcomes in $A$ lying outside $B$ have probability zero because $A$ has the same probability as its part in $B$.
