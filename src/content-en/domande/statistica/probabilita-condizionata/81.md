Of $200$ orders, $80$ are international. A total of $24$ orders were returned, including $12$ international orders. How does the probability that an order is international change after we learn that it was returned?

- It stays at $40\%$.
- It decreases from $40\%$ to $15\%$.
- It increases from $40\%$ to $50\%$.
- It decreases from $50\%$ to $40\%$.

## Explanation

Before the return is observed, $80$ orders out of $200$ are international, giving $\dfrac25=40\%$. After it is observed, the reference group contains the $24$ returns, of which $12$ are international. The new share is $\dfrac{12}{24}=\dfrac12=50\%$, an increase of ten percentage points. The ratio $\dfrac{12}{80}=15\%$ would give the probability of a return among international orders, reversing the conditioning.
