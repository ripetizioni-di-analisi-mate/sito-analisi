In a probability model, $A$ and $B$ are independent. Given $P(A)=\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $P(A\cap B)=\dfrac{1}{6}$, calculate $P(B)$.

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{9}$

## Explanation

By independence, the probability of the intersection is the product of the marginal probabilities. Substituting the data gives $\dfrac16=\dfrac23 P(B)$. Since $\dfrac23$ is positive, division yields $P(B)=\dfrac16\cdot\dfrac32=\dfrac14$. The same ratio $\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(A)}$ is the probability of $B$ within $A$. Under independence it equals the probability of $B$ in the whole space.
