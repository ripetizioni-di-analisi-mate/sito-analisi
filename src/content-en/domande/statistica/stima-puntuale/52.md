An exponential rate is estimated by $\dfrac{n}{\sum_iX_i}$. If $n=5$ and the true rate is $8$, what is its bias?

- $10$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{8}{5}$
- $-2$

## Explanation

The sum $A$ of $n$ independent exponential waiting times of rate $\lambda$ has a gamma distribution with shape $n$ and rate $\lambda$, with density $\dfrac{\lambda^na^{n-1}e^{-\lambda a}}{(n-1)!}$ for $a>0$. Integrating $\dfrac1a$ against this density gives, for $n>1$, $E\left[\dfrac1A\right]=\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1}$, which equals $\dfrac{8}{4}=2$ for $n=5$ and $\lambda=8$. The estimator multiplies $\dfrac1A$ by five, giving expectation $5\cdot2=\dfrac54\cdot8=10$. Subtracting the true parameter yields bias $10-8=2$. The estimator overestimates the rate on average.
