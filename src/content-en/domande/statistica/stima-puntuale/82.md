Independent Poisson counts have means $\lambda t_i$ and known exposures $t_1=1$ and $t_2=3$. The observed counts are $2$ and $6$. Up to an additive constant the log-likelihood is $8\log\lambda-4\lambda$. What estimate is obtained for $\lambda$?

- $4$
- $8$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The given log-likelihood uses the total number of events $2+6=8$ and total exposure $1+3=4$. Differentiating with respect to $\lambda>0$ gives $\dfrac8\lambda-4$. Setting it to zero gives $\lambda=2$. The derivative is positive before two and negative afterward. The maximum is the ratio of total events to total exposure, the estimated count per unit of exposure.
