In an exponential model with a strictly positive rate, all observed times are zero. What happens to the first-moment equation?

- It has a unique solution at $\lambda=0$
- It has no solution among finite positive rates
- It has a unique solution at $\lambda=1$
- Every positive rate satisfies it

## Explanation

The theoretical first moment of an exponential distribution of rate $\lambda$ is $\dfrac1\lambda$. With all observed times equal to zero, the moment equation becomes $\dfrac1\lambda=0$. The reciprocal of any finite positive rate is strictly positive. It approaches zero as $\lambda$ increases without bound, so the equation has no solution in the stated parameter space.
