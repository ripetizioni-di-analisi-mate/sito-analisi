An exponential model has rate $\lambda>0$ and density $\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$, including at $x=0$. All observed times are zero. Which statement about the log-likelihood is correct?

- It has its maximum at $\lambda=0$
- It grows without bound when $\lambda\to+\infty$
- It is constant for every $\lambda>0$
- It has its maximum at $\lambda=1$

## Explanation

When all observations are zero, the density at each observation equals $\lambda$. The likelihood is $L(\lambda)=\lambda^n$ and the log-likelihood is $n\log\lambda$. Its derivative $\dfrac n\lambda$ is positive at every admissible rate, and $\ell(\lambda)$ increases without bound as $\lambda\to+\infty$. No finite positive rate attains a maximum.
