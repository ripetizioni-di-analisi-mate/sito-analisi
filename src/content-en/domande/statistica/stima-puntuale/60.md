For normal data with known variance and observed mean $4$, the only candidate parameter values are $\mu=3$ and $\mu=5$. How do their likelihoods compare?

- The likelihood at $\mu=3$ is greater
- One likelihood is zero
- They are equal
- The likelihood at $\mu=5$ is greater

## Explanation

With known variance the part of the log-likelihood depending on $\mu$ is proportional to $-(\mu-\bar x)^2$. The candidates $3$ and $5$ are each one unit from $\bar x=4$ and give the same squared distance and log-likelihood. Their likelihoods are equal because equal distances on either side of the mean have the same effect.
