For two candidate parameters the likelihoods are $\ell(\theta_1)=-6$ and $\ell(\theta_2)=-9$. Which candidate has higher likelihood for the observed data?

- $\theta_1$
- It depends on the number of observations
- $\theta_2$
- They have equal likelihoods

## Explanation

The log-likelihood is the logarithm of the likelihood wherever the latter is positive. Since the logarithm is strictly increasing, $-6>-9$ implies $L(\theta_1)>L(\theta_2)$. Equivalently, the ratio of the two likelihoods is $e^{-6-(-9)}=e^3>1$. Selection depends on their ordering, which negative logarithm values do not reverse.
