A variable is uniformly distributed on the interval $[\theta,2\theta]$, whose right endpoint is twice the left one, with $\theta>0$ unknown and positive density also at the endpoints. In three independent observations the smallest value is $6$ and the largest is $10$. Which value of $\theta$ maximizes the likelihood?

- $6$
- $10$
- $3$
- $5$

## Explanation

The support $[\theta,2\theta]$ has length $\theta$, so each observation has density $\dfrac1\theta$ if it lies in the support and density zero otherwise. All observations lie in the support when the lower endpoint does not exceed the minimum $6$ and the upper endpoint is not smaller than the maximum $10$:
$$
\begin{cases}
\theta\le6\\
2\theta\ge10
\end{cases}
$$
The system is equivalent to $5\le\theta\le6$, so on this interval the likelihood is $L(\theta)=\theta^{-3}$ and outside it is zero. Since $\theta^{-3}$ decreases as $\theta$ increases, the maximum occurs at the smallest admissible value, hence $\widehat\theta=5$. The answer $6$ comes from using only the condition on the minimum, which gives the largest admissible value.
