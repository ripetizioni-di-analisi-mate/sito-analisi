A measurement $X$ has mean $\theta$ and variance $1$. Under what condition does $U=\dfrac X2$ have smaller MSE than $X$?

- $\lvert\theta\rvert<1$
- $\theta\in\mathbb R$
- $\lvert\theta\rvert>\sqrt3$
- $\lvert\theta\rvert<\sqrt3$

## Explanation

For $X$ the mean squared error is $1$ because its expectation equals $\theta$. For $U$ the expectation is $\dfrac\theta2$, the bias is $-\dfrac\theta2$, and the variance is $\dfrac14$, so $MSE_\theta(U)=\dfrac{1+\theta^2}{4}$. Requiring this to be strictly less than $1$ gives $\theta^2<3$, or $\lvert\theta\rvert<\sqrt3$. The two errors are equal at the endpoints.
