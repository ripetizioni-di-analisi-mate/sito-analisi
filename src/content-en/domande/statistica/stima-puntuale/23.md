A measurement $X$ has mean $\theta$ and variance $1$. For exactly which values of $\theta$ does $U=\dfrac X2$ have smaller MSE than $X$?

- $\lvert\theta\rvert<1$
- $\theta\in\mathbb R$
- $\lvert\theta\rvert>\sqrt3$
- $\lvert\theta\rvert<\sqrt3$

## Explanation

For $X$ the mean squared error is $1$ because its expectation equals $\theta$. For $U$ the expectation is $\dfrac\theta2$, the bias is $-\dfrac\theta2$, and the variance is $\dfrac14$, so $MSE_\theta(U)=\dfrac{1+\theta^2}{4}$. Requiring this to be strictly less than $1$ gives $\theta^2<3$, or $\lvert\theta\rvert<\sqrt3$. The two errors are equal at the endpoints. The condition $\lvert\theta\rvert<1$ is enough for $U$ to have smaller MSE, but it leaves out values such as $\theta=1.5$, for which $MSE_\theta(U)=\dfrac{3.25}{4}<1$.
