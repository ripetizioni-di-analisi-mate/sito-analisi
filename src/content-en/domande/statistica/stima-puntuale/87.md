A log-likelihood is twice differentiable on an open interval. It satisfies $\ell'(\theta_0)=0$ and $\ell''(\theta)<0$ throughout the interval. What follows?

- Every point is a maximizer
- $\theta_0$ is the only maximizer in the interval
- $\theta_0$ is a local minimizer
- The supremum can occur only at a boundary

## Explanation

The second derivative is negative throughout the interval, so $\ell'$ strictly decreases. Since $\ell'(\theta_0)=0$, $\ell'$ is positive to the left of $\theta_0$ and negative to its right. The function increases before that point and decreases afterward, making $\theta_0$ the unique maximizer on the whole interval. The sign argument holds globally and establishes uniqueness.
