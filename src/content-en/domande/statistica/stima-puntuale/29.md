The standard deviation is estimated by taking the square root of the unbiased estimator $S^2$. Which inequality holds for its expectation?

- $E[S]=E[S^2]$
- $E[S]\ge\sigma$ for every population
- $E[S]\le\sigma$
- $E[S]=\sigma^2$

## Explanation

The square root is concave on nonnegative values. Applying Jensen's inequality to $S^2$ gives $E[\sqrt{S^2}]\le\sqrt{E[S^2]}$. Since $S^2$ is unbiased for $\sigma^2$, the right-hand side equals $\sqrt{\sigma^2}=\sigma$ and the left-hand side is $E[S]$. Equality is possible when $S^2$ is almost surely constant. Taking a square root generally does not preserve unbiasedness of a variance estimator.
