The standard deviation is estimated by taking the square root of the unbiased estimator $S^2$. Which inequality holds for its expectation?

- $E[S]=E[S^2]$
- $E[S]\ge\sigma$ for every population
- $E[S]\le\sigma$
- $E[S]=\sigma^2$

## Explanation

The variance of $S$ is nonnegative, so $\operatorname{Var}(S)=E[S^2]-E[S]^2\ge0$ and therefore $E[S]^2\le E[S^2]$. Since $S^2$ is unbiased for $\sigma^2$, $E[S^2]=\sigma^2$, so $E[S]^2\le\sigma^2$ and, because $E[S]\ge0$, $E[S]\le\sigma$. Equality holds only when $\operatorname{Var}(S)=0$, that is, when $S$ always takes the same value. The square root of an unbiased variance estimator therefore has negative bias unless it is constant.
