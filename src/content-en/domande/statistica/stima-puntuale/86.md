Two independent Poisson counts with parameter $\lambda$ are $2$ and $0$. What is the ratio of the likelihood at $\lambda=2$ to the likelihood at $\lambda=1$?

- $4$
- $2e^{-2}$
- $4e^{-2}$
- $e^{-2}$

## Explanation

The product of the Poisson masses is $L(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}\dfrac{\lambda^2}{2!}\cdot e^{-\lambda}\dfrac{\lambda^0}{0!}=\dfrac{e^{-2\lambda}\lambda^2}{2}$. The denominator $2$ cancels in the ratio, so $\dfrac{L(2)}{L(1)}=\dfrac{e^{-4}\cdot4}{e^{-2}\cdot1}=4e^{-2}\approx0.54$. The ratio is less than $1$, so the data have higher probability with $\lambda=1$, which is also the maximum likelihood estimate $\bar x=\dfrac{2+0}{2}=1$.
