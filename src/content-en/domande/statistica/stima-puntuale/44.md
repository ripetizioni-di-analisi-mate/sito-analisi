All observed trials are failures and $p\in(0,1)$ is assumed. What happens to the Bernoulli likelihood?

- Its supremum is approached as $p\to0^+$, and there is no maximum
- It is maximized at $1$
- It is constant on $(0,1)$
- It is maximized at $0$

## Explanation

If every trial is a failure, the likelihood for $n$ trials is $(1-p)^n$. It decreases strictly as $p$ increases on $0<p<1$ and approaches $1$ as $p$ tends to zero from the right. Zero is excluded, so $1$ is the supremum and no admissible parameter attains the maximum.
