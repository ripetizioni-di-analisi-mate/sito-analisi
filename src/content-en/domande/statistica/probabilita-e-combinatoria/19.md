Two fair dice are rolled independently and the smaller of their two values is recorded. Which comparison shows that the six possible values of the minimum are not equally likely?

- Swapping the dice always changes the minimum.
- The minimum is $6$ in just one pair and $5$ in three equally likely pairs.
- Fair dice give their faces different probabilities.
- The sample space of ordered pairs contains only doubles.

## Explanation

The ordered pairs for the two dice are equally likely. For the minimum to be $6$, both dice must show $6$, so the probability is $\dfrac1{36}$. A minimum of $5$ occurs with $(5,5)$, $(5,6)$ and $(6,5)$, whose combined probability is $\dfrac3{36}$. These two different probabilities already show that grouping pairs by their minimum produces outcomes with different probabilities.
