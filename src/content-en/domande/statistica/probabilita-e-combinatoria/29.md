For two events $A$ and $B$, which event describes “exactly one of $A$ and $B$ occurs”?

- $(A\cup B)^c$
- $(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)$
- $A\cup B$
- $A\cap B$

## Explanation

If only $A$ occurs, the outcome belongs to the difference $A\setminus B$. If only $B$ occurs, it belongs to the difference $B\setminus A$. The two regions are disjoint and cover all ways to meet the condition. Their union is the symmetric difference. The intersection $A\cap B$ instead contains the outcomes in which both events occur.
