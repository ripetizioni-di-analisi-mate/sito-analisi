In a die roll, event $A$ is “the result is even” and $B$ is “the result is odd.” Which property distinguishes these events from an arbitrary pair of mutually exclusive events?

- Their union is the whole sample space.
- They cannot both occur.
- Their intersection is empty.
- Their probabilities add to give the probability of their union.

## Explanation

Every face of the die is either odd or even and none is both. Consequently, $A\cap B=\varnothing$ and $A\cup B=\Omega$. The first property holds for every pair of mutually exclusive events. The second also says that the events exhaust the space, making them complementary. For example, “rolling $1$” and “rolling $2$” are mutually exclusive and leave out the other four faces.
