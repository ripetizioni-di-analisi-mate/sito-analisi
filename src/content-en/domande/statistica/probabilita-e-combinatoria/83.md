A string contains two copies of $+$ and two copies of $-$, indistinguishable within each type. Which description avoids counting the same string twice?

- Arrange the four symbols as if they were all distinct.
- Freely choose a position twice for the symbol $+$.
- Choose an ordered pair of distinct positions for the two $+$ symbols and count both orders separately.
- Choose the two positions for the $+$ symbols and fill the remaining positions with $-$.

## Explanation

Two distinct positions among the four completely determine where the $+$ symbols occur. The two remaining positions must then contain the $-$ symbols. Since the two copies of $+$ are indistinguishable, choosing either position first gives the same string. Count the two-position subsets to obtain $\binom42=6$ strings.
