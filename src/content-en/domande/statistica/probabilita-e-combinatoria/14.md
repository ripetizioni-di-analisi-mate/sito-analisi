Two cards are drawn uniformly at random without replacement from a deck of ten distinct cards, four of them special. Calculate the probability that at least one of the two cards is special.

- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{8}{15}$

## Explanation

The complement of “at least one special card” requires two ordinary cards. There are $\binom62=15$ pairs of ordinary cards among $\binom{10}{2}=45$ pairs in total. Every pair is equally likely under uniform sampling without replacement because each has two possible draw orders. The required probability is therefore $1-\dfrac{15}{45}=\dfrac23$, which includes the case in which both cards are special.
