Two of eight distinct parts are faulty. Two parts are sampled uniformly without replacement and the first is known to be faulty. A colleague says the chance that the second is also faulty is still $2/8$. Which count corrects that prediction?

- Seven equally likely parts remain and only one is faulty
- Seven parts remain and two are faulty
- Eight parts remain and one is faulty
- Six good parts and no faulty part remain

## Explanation

The first outcome removes one faulty part from the lot. The seven possible second parts consist of six good ones and one faulty one, giving conditional probability $1/7$.
