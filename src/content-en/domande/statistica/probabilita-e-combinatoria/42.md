Let $A$ and $B$ be events with $P(A)=\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $P(B)=\dfrac{1}{2}$. What is the smallest possible value of $P(A\cap B)$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$

## Explanation

The individual probabilities sum to $\dfrac35+\dfrac12=\dfrac{11}{10}$. Since the union can have probability at most $1$, the overlap must have probability at least $\dfrac{11}{10}-1=\dfrac1{10}$. The bound is attained by assigning the regions $A$ only, $B$ only and the intersection probabilities $\dfrac12$, $\dfrac25$ and $\dfrac1{10}$, respectively. These probabilities sum to $1$ and give both specified event probabilities.
