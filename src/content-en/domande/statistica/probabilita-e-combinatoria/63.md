Consider the first and second entries of an ordered pair of integers. Let $A$ be the event “the integers are equal” and $B$ the event “the first is strictly greater than the second.” How are $A$ and $B$ related?

- They are complementary in the space of all ordered pairs of integers.
- They are mutually exclusive.
- Their intersection contains all ordered pairs of integers.
- One is contained in the other.

## Explanation

An ordered pair belongs to $A$ when its entries are equal. To belong to $B$, its first entry must be strictly greater than its second. Equality rules out strict inequality, so $A\cap B=\varnothing$ and the events are mutually exclusive. The pair $(1,2)$ belongs to neither. Their union therefore does not cover every ordered pair, and the events are not complementary.
