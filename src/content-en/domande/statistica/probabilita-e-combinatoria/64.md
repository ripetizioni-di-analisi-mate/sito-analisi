A route from X to Z goes through either Y or W. There are two roads from X to Y and three from Y to Z, four roads from X to W and one from W to Z. Each route follows only the two stated legs. How many routes are there?

- $12$
- $24$
- $6$
- $10$

## Explanation

A route through Y combines one of the two roads from X to Y with one of the three from Y to Z, giving $2\cdot3=6$ routes through Y. Going through W gives $4\cdot1=4$ routes. The intermediate stop distinguishes the two sets of routes, so the counts add to $6+4=10$.
