Two balls are drawn uniformly at random without replacement from an urn with two red and three white balls. Why may unordered pairs of balls be treated as equally likely outcomes?

- The three possible color pairs are equally likely.
- Each pair of distinct balls corresponds to two equally likely ordered draws.
- Replacement allows the same ball to be drawn again.
- Each distinct pair corresponds to only one ordered draw.

## Explanation

The balls can be distinguished even when they have the same color. A particular ordered sequence of two balls has probability $\dfrac15\cdot\dfrac14=\dfrac1{20}$. Every unordered pair corresponds to two mutually exclusive sequences, obtained by swapping the first and second balls. Each pair therefore has probability $2\cdot\dfrac1{20}=\dfrac1{10}$, the same for every pair.
