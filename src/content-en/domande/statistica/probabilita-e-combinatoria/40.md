Let $A=\{2,4\}$ and $B=\{2,4,6\}$. Which equality follows from the inclusion of $A$ in $B$?

- $A\setminus B=A$
- $A\cap B=\varnothing$
- $B\setminus A=B$
- $A\cup B=B$

## Explanation

The union contains the elements that belong to at least one set. The numbers $2$ and $4$ belong to both, while $6$ belongs to $B$. Every element of $A$ already belongs to $B$, so the union is $\{2,4,6\}=B$. The same inclusion implies that the intersection, the part common to both, is $A$.
