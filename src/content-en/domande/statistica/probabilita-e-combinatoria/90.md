Two events $A$ and $B$ have probabilities $0.45$ and $0.30$. Given that $P(A\cap B)=0.30$, what follows about their probabilities?

- $P((A\cup B)^c)=0.25$
- $P(A\setminus B)=0$
- $P(B\setminus A)=0$
- $P(A\cup B)=0.75$

## Explanation

Event $B$ is the disjoint union of $A\cap B$ and $B\setminus A$. By additivity, the probability of the second part is $P(B)-P(A\cap B)=0.30-0.30=0$. This conclusion concerns probability. In a space that allows outcomes of zero probability, $B\setminus A$ can have probability zero even if it contains some outcomes.
