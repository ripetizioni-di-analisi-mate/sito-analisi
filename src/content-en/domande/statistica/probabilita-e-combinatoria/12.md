Of $30$ students, $12$ take course A, $10$ take B and $8$ take C. The intersections $A\cap B$, $A\cap C$ and $B\cap C$ contain $4$, $3$ and $2$ students, respectively. One student takes all three courses. How many students take at least one course?

- $22$
- $20$
- $21$
- $30$

## Explanation

Adding the three individual enrollment counts gives $12+10+8=30$. A student who takes two courses appears twice, so subtract the three pairwise intersections, totaling $4+3+2=9$. This removes even the last occurrence of the student taking all three courses, so add that student back once. The result is $30-9+1=22$ students.
