A fair die is rolled. Only if the result is even is a fair coin tossed independently. The entire sequence is recorded. Which statement about the probabilities of individual outcomes is correct?

- The outcome “$2$ then heads” has the same probability as “$3$.”
- All recorded outcomes are equally likely.
- The outcome “$1$” is twice as likely as “$2$ then heads.”
- The outcome “$1$” is half as likely as “$2$ then heads.”

## Explanation

When the die shows $1$ the experiment ends, so the outcome “$1$” has the probability of face $1$ of a fair die, $\dfrac16$. Face $2$ also has probability $\dfrac16$, and after a $2$ the coin is tossed. The event “a $2$ comes up” is therefore the union of the two mutually exclusive outcomes “$2$ then heads” and “$2$ then tails,” and by additivity their probabilities sum to $\dfrac16$. The coin is fair and tossed independently of the die, so the two outcomes have the same probability, namely $\dfrac1{12}$ each. The outcome “$1$” is thus twice as likely as “$2$ then heads.”
