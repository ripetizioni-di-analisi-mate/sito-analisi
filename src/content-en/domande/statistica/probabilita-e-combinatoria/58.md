Four distinct objects are arranged in a row. In how many arrangements is A first or B last?

- $10$
- $8$
- $12$
- $6$

## Explanation

With A first, the other three objects can be arranged in $3!=6$ ways. The same count applies with B last. Rows satisfying both restrictions have been counted twice. They have two free positions for two objects, giving $2!=2$ such rows. The inclusion–exclusion formula gives $6+6-2=10$ rows.
