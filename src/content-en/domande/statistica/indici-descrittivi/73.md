Two observations $a<b$ have sample standard deviation $s=3$. What is their distance $b-a$?

- $6$
- $3$
- $3\sqrt2$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt2}{2}$

## Explanation

The mean of the two observations is $\dfrac{a+b}{2}$, so the deviations are $-\dfrac{b-a}{2}$ and $\dfrac{b-a}{2}$. The sum of their squares is $D=2\cdot\dfrac{(b-a)^2}{4}=\dfrac{(b-a)^2}{2}$. With $n=2$ the corrected sample variance divides by $n-1=1$, so $s^2=\dfrac{(b-a)^2}{2}=9$. This gives $(b-a)^2=18$, and therefore $b-a=\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2$.
