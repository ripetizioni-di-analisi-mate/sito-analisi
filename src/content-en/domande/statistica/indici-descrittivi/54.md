Two batches of metal bars have the same standard deviation of the lengths. The bars in the first batch have mean length $250\,\mathrm{cm}$ and coefficient of variation $4\,\%$, those in the second have mean length $200\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the coefficient of variation of the lengths in the second batch?

- $4\,\%$
- $3.2\,\%$
- $5\,\%$
- $10\,\%$

## Explanation

The coefficient of variation is the ratio of the standard deviation to the mean, so the standard deviation of the first batch is the product of the coefficient of variation and the mean. Since $4\,\%=0.04$, we get $s=0.04\cdot250\,\mathrm{cm}=10\,\mathrm{cm}$. The second batch has the same standard deviation and mean $200\,\mathrm{cm}$, so its coefficient of variation is $\dfrac{10\,\mathrm{cm}}{200\,\mathrm{cm}}=0.05$, that is, $5\,\%$. The coefficient is larger than that of the first batch because the same spread is compared with a smaller mean.
