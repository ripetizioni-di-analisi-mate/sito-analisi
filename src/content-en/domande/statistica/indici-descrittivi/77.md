For observations with mean $10$, which pair of centers gives the same sum of squared deviations?

- $7$ and $13$.
- $5$ and $13$.
- $8$ and $13$.
- $10$ and $13$.

## Explanation

The sum of squares about $c$ is the sum of squares about the mean plus $n(c-\bar x)^2$. Equal totals therefore require centers equidistant from the mean $10$. The centers $7$ and $13$ differ from it by $-3$ and $3$, both with square $9$. Thus only the magnitude of the shift matters, not its direction.
