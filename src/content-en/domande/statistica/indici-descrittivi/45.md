Two thermometers give sets of readings in degrees Celsius with positive means. Why can the ratio $\dfrac{s}{\bar x}$ change when the same readings are converted to kelvins?

- The conversion shifts the mean without changing the standard deviation.
- The conversion changes only the standard deviation.
- The conversion multiplies the mean and standard deviation by the same factor.
- The standard deviation becomes a variance.

## Explanation

Conversion to kelvins adds $273.15$ to every value and to the mean. Subtracting the new mean cancels the increment, leaving deviations unchanged, so $s$ does not change. The ratio, however, goes from $\dfrac{s}{\bar x}$ to $\dfrac{s}{\bar x+273.15}$. Its increased denominator explains why shifting the scale's zero can change the coefficient of variation.
