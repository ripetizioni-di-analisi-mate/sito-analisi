Let $X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(1)$. How are the masses at $0$, $1$ and $2$ related?

- $P(X=0)>P(X=1)>P(X=2)$
- $P(X=0)=P(X=1)>P(X=2)$
- $P(X=0)<P(X=1)<P(X=2)$
- $P(X=0)=P(X=1)=P(X=2)$

## Explanation

With parameter $\lambda=1$, the mass at $k$ simplifies to $\dfrac{e^{-1}}{k!}$ because $1^k=1$. The factorials $0!$ and $1!$ both equal one, so $P(X=0)=P(X=1)=e^{-1}$. Since $2!=2$, the mass at $2$ is $\dfrac12e^{-1}$, which is strictly smaller. The factorials increase from $k=2$ onward, so the maximum mass occurs at $0$ and $1$.
