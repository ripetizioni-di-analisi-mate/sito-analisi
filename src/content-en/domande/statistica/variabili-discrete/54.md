Let $X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(3)$. What is $P(X>1)$?

- $1-3e^{-3}$
- $4e^{-3}$
- $1-4e^{-3}$
- $1-e^{-3}$

## Explanation

Because $X$ counts events, exceeding one means observing at least two. The complement contains only counts $0$ and $1$. The Poisson formula gives $P(X=0)=e^{-3}$ and $P(X=1)=3e^{-3}$. Their sum is $4e^{-3}$, so $P(X>1)=1-4e^{-3}$. Subtracting only the mass at zero would include the case of one event.
