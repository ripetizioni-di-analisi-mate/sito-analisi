The only information about a count over a particular time period is that its expected value is $5$. What can be concluded from this fact alone?

- The expected value of the count, but not its distribution.
- The count has a Poisson distribution with parameter $5$.
- The count has a binomial distribution with five trials.
- The count is always $5$.

## Explanation

The expected value is the mean number of arrivals in the period, and different distributions have the same expected value. For example, a count always equal to $5$ has mean $5$ and zero variance. A Poisson count with parameter $5$ also has mean $5$ but variance $5$. The given information therefore fixes the mean and leaves the choice of distribution open.
