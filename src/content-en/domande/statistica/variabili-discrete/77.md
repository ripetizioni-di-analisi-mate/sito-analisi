An archive of forty applications contains nine incomplete applications. Six distinct applications are selected uniformly. Which model gives the exact probability that at least one is incomplete?

- Binomial with six trials and probability $\dfrac9{40}$.
- Geometric with probability $\dfrac9{40}$.
- Poisson with parameter $\dfrac{54}{40}$.
- Hypergeometric with population size $40$, group of interest size $9$ and sample size $6$.

## Explanation

The six applications are distinct and chosen uniformly, so the sample is drawn without replacement. The number $X$ of incomplete applications is hypergeometric with population size $N=40$, group of interest size $R=9$ and sample size $n=6$. Subtract the probability of no incomplete applications to calculate the required probability, giving $P(X\ge1)=1-\dfrac{\binom{31}{6}}{\binom{40}{6}}$ because $31$ applications are complete.
