Let $X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(5,p)$ with $0<p<1$. What is the ratio $\dfrac{P(X=1)}{P(X=0)}$?

- $\dfrac{5p}{1-p}$
- $\dfrac{p}{1-p}$
- $5p$
- $\dfrac{1-p}{5p}$

## Explanation

Zero successes requires five failures, so $P(X=0)=(1-p)^5$. For exactly one success there are five possible positions for the successful trial, giving $P(X=1)=5p(1-p)^4$. In the ratio of the two masses the common factor $(1-p)^4$ cancels because $p<1$. This leaves $\dfrac{5p}{1-p}$. The factor five counts the possible positions of the only success.
