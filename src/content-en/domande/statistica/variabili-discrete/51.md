In independent trials with success probability $\dfrac12$, which event is equivalent to “the third success occurs on the fifth trial”?

- Exactly three successes in the first four trials and success on the fifth.
- Exactly two successes in the first four trials and success on the fifth.
- Exactly three successes in the first five trials, in any order.
- Exactly two successes in the first four trials and failure on the fifth.

## Explanation

For the third success to occur on trial five, the count immediately beforehand must be two. If it were already three, the third success would have occurred earlier. If it were at most one, a single additional trial would be insufficient. We therefore need exactly two successes in the first four trials and a fifth-trial success to raise the count from two to three.
