Let $X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(4)$. What is the ratio $\dfrac{P(X=3)}{P(X=2)}$?

- $2$
- $\dfrac34$
- $\dfrac43$
- $4$

## Explanation

The mass at $3$ is $P(X=3)=e^{-4}\dfrac{4^3}{3!}$, while the mass at $2$ is $P(X=2)=e^{-4}\dfrac{4^2}{2!}$. The factors $e^{-4}$ and $4^2$ cancel in their ratio. The factorial ratio gives $\dfrac{2!}{3!}=\dfrac13$, so the result is $\dfrac43$. The mass at $3$ is therefore greater than the mass at $2$.
