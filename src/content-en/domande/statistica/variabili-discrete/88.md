A uniform sample of two parts is drawn without replacement from five parts, two of them marked. How many distinct samples contain exactly one marked part?

- $10$
- $3$
- $2$
- $6$

## Explanation

The required sample contains one marked part and one unmarked part. There are $\binom21=2$ choices for the marked part and $\binom31=3$ for the unmarked one. Each combination determines a distinct pair because the marking identifies the two roles. The number of samples is $2\cdot3=6$. Reversing the order of the two parts leaves the sample unchanged.
