A binomial variable $X$ has $E[X]=4$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)=3$. How many trials are there?

- $8$
- $16$
- $12$
- $4$

## Explanation

A binomial variable satisfies $E[X]=np$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)=np(1-p)$. Since the mean is positive, dividing the second equation by the first cancels $np$ and gives $1-p=\dfrac34$. Hence $p=\dfrac14$. Substituting this parameter in the mean gives $n\cdot\dfrac14=4$, or $n=16$ trials. This value also yields the stated variance because $4\cdot\dfrac34=3$.
