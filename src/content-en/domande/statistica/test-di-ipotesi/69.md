A researcher wants to test for an increase but chooses the lower tail after observing a negative statistic because it gives a smaller p-value. Which part of the procedure changes?

- The variance calculated from the data.
- The observed value of the statistic.
- The rule for choosing the rejection region, and hence its calibration under the null hypothesis.
- The sample size.

## Explanation

The upper-tailed alternative specifies a right-hand critical region in advance. Switching to the left tail whenever the statistic is negative also permits rejection for very small values. This changes the set of samples leading to rejection and hence the rejection probability under $H_0$. The original calibration applies to the upper tail alone.
