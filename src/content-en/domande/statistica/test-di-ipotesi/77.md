A two-sided test of a normal mean switches from a known standard deviation to a standard deviation estimated from a small sample. Which comparison changes?

- A chi-square quantile with $n$ degrees of freedom is always used.
- The rejection region necessarily becomes one-sided.
- The unstandardized observed mean is compared with the p-value.
- A t quantile with $n-1$ degrees of freedom replaces the normal quantile.

## Explanation

With known standard deviation the denominator is fixed and the statistic is standard normal under $H_0$. Estimating the standard deviation makes the denominator vary across samples. For a normal sample the new ratio has Student's t distribution with $n-1$ degrees of freedom. The test remains two-sided, so at level $\alpha$ the absolute-value comparison uses the t quantile of order $1-\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.
