A test specifies $Z$ before the data are collected and subsequently gives $z_{\mathrm{obs}}=2.1$. Which description is correct?

- $Z$ is a statistic and $2.1$ is its value for the observed sample.
- $Z$ is the population mean.
- $2.1$ is the critical quantile for every alternative.
- $2.1$ is the significance level of the test.

## Explanation

Before observation, $Z$ is a function of the random variables in the sample. Another sample may give a different result, so $Z$ has a distribution under the null model. Substituting the observations gives the number $z_{\mathrm{obs}}=2.1$, which can be compared with the quantiles of the distribution of $Z$.
