Two designs for an upper-tailed z test have the same $\sigma$, significance level, and sample size. The second targets a larger increase. How does the calculated power change?

- It decreases because the individual significance level is lower.
- It increases because the alternative distribution of the mean shifts to the right relative to the same threshold.
- It can be compared only after the data are observed.
- It stays the same because the threshold is unchanged.

## Explanation

The rejection threshold is calculated at the null boundary and is the same because level, standard deviation, and sample size coincide. A larger true increase moves the center of the alternative distribution of the sample mean to the right without changing its standard error. The probability that the sample mean exceeds the fixed threshold therefore increases. This probability is the power.
