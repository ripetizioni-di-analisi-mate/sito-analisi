Given known population standard deviation $6$, how many independent observations are needed for a standard error of the mean of $0.5$?

- $36$
- $144$
- $72$
- $12$

## Explanation

Independence gives standard error $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}$ for the mean. Substituting the known standard deviation and desired standard error gives $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt n}=0.5$. Hence $\sqrt n=\dfrac6{0.5}=12$ and $n=12^2=144$. Since the relation involves the square root of the sample size, reducing the standard error by a factor of twelve requires a sample size of twelve squared.
