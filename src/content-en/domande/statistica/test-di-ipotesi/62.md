The hypothesis $H_0:\mu=10$ is tested in a normal population with unknown variance. Why is the null hypothesis composite when the model also includes the variance?

- The sample variance is a function of the data.
- The alternative can be two-sided.
- The same mean is compatible with several normal distributions with different variances.
- The observed mean is unknown at the design stage.

## Explanation

The normal model has two parameters, mean and variance. The hypothesis $H_0:\mu=10$ fixes the mean and allows every positive variance. For example, $N(10,1)$ and $N(10,4)$ both satisfy $H_0$ and give the observations different dispersions. The null hypothesis is composite because it allows more than one data distribution.
