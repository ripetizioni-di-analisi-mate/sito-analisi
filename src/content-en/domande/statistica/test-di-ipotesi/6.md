In $60$ independent trials the observed counts in three categories are $27$, $23$, and $10$. The null hypothesis assigns them probabilities $\dfrac12$, $\dfrac13$, and $\dfrac16$. Calculate Pearson's statistic.

- $0.45$
- $18$
- $0.3$
- $0.75$

## Explanation

Multiply $60$ by the three null category probabilities to obtain expected counts $30$, $20$, and $10$. The observed-minus-expected differences are $-3$, $3$, and $0$. Pearson's statistic divides each squared difference by its corresponding expected count, giving $Q=\dfrac{9}{30}+\dfrac{9}{20}+0=0.75$. The two differences of magnitude $3$ contribute different amounts because their expected counts differ.
