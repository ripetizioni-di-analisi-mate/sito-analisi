An upper-tailed variance test with $n=10$ and $\sigma_0^2=9$ rejects when $Q_\sigma\ge16.919$. What is the corresponding threshold for the unbiased sample variance $S^2$?

- $1.88$
- $16.919$
- $152.271$
- $9$

## Explanation

The statistic is $Q_\sigma=\dfrac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma_0^2}$. To convert a threshold for $Q_\sigma$ into a threshold for $S^2$, multiply by the positive factor $\dfrac{\sigma_0^2}{n-1}=\dfrac99=1$. The direction of the inequality is preserved and the required threshold is $16.919$. The numerical agreement of the standardized and variance-scale thresholds follows from the specific values of $n$ and $\sigma_0^2$.
