Two independent samples have different standard deviations and sample sizes. Which terms are added to obtain the squared Welch standard error?

- $s_1^2$ and $s_2^2$.
- $\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_2}$ and $\dfrac{s_2^2}{n_1}$.
- $\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1}$ and $\dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2}$.
- $\dfrac{s_1}{n_1}$ and $\dfrac{s_2}{n_2}$.

## Explanation

The variance of a group mean is the variance of individual observations divided by their number in the group. Welch's method substitutes estimates to obtain $\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1}$ and $\dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2}$. Independence makes the covariance zero, so the variance of the difference is the sum of the two terms. Its square root is the estimated standard error.
