A goodness-of-fit test has four categories with positive expected counts. The first two have observed counts $12,8$ and expected counts $10,10$. The other counts match their expectations. What happens to Pearson’s statistic for these data if the first two categories are merged?

- It rises from zero to $0.8$
- It stays $0.8$
- It falls from $0.8$ to zero
- It rises to $4$

## Explanation

The separate cells contribute $2^2/10+(-2)^2/10=0.8$. In the merged cell the observed and expected counts are both $20$, so the opposite deviations cancel before being squared.
