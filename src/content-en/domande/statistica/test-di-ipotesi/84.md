In a goodness-of-fit test, two categories have the same positive absolute difference between the observed and expected counts. The first has the smaller expected count. Which category contributes more to Pearson's statistic?

- The first, because the same squared deviation is divided by a smaller expected count.
- The signs of the two deviations must be known.
- The two categories make equal contributions.
- The second, because its expected count is larger.

## Explanation

A category contributes $\dfrac{(O-E)^2}{E}$. The two deviations have equal magnitudes, so their squared numerators agree despite opposite signs. For a positive common magnitude, dividing that square by the smaller expected count gives the larger contribution. For example, with deviation $3$ and expected counts $9$ and $18$, the contributions are $1$ and $\dfrac12$, respectively.
