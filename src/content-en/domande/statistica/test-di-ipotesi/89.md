Ten test statistics are calculated from the same data and may be dependent. Why does Bonferroni control still apply?

- The correction automatically makes the statistics independent.
- The probability of at least one error is always the product of the individual error probabilities.
- Dependence implies that all null hypotheses are false.
- The probability bound for a union applies even to dependent events.

## Explanation

Let $A_j$ be the event that test $j$ rejects its own true null hypothesis. At least one false rejection is the union of these events. The probability of the union is at most the sum of the individual probabilities because summing may count an outcome several times. The bound thus applies to dependent statistics as well and justifies per-test level $\dfrac{\alpha}{10}$.
