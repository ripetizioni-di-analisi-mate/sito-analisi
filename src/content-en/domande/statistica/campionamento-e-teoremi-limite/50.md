For i.i.d. measurements with mean $12$ and finite variance, which statement expresses the weak law of large numbers?

- The variance of an individual measurement tends to zero
- Each $\bar X_n$ has a normal distribution
- For every $\varepsilon>0$, $P(|\bar X_n-12|>\varepsilon)$ tends to zero
- For every $n$, $P(\bar X_n=12)=1$

## Explanation

At fixed tolerance $\varepsilon>0$, the mean has variance $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}$ and Chebyshev's inequality gives $P(|\bar X_n-12|>\varepsilon)\le\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2}$. The right-hand side tends to zero because only $n$ increases. Thus the probability of the deviation tends to zero for every fixed tolerance. This is convergence in probability to $12$.
