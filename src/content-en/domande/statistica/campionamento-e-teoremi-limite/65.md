A population has zero variance and mean $5$. An i.i.d. sample is drawn. Which statement about the sample mean is correct?

- The mean equals $5$ with probability one for every $n$
- The mean has positive standard error for every $n$
- The mean converges in probability to $0$
- The standardized mean has a standard normal distribution

## Explanation

The variance is the expectation of the nonnegative variable $(X-5)^2$. If its expectation is zero, the squared deviation equals zero with probability one. Hence each observation equals $5$ with probability one, as does their mean at every $n$. Consequently, the standard error of the mean is also $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}=0$. The standardized mean is undefined, because it has $\sigma=0$ in the denominator, and the central limit theorem requires a positive variance.
