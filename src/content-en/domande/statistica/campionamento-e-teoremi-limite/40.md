Nonnegative consumption has mean $3$ and the threshold is $2$. Combining Markov's inequality with the general upper bound on a probability, what upper bound can be given for the probability of exceeding the threshold?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

For nonnegative consumption, Markov's inequality gives upper bound $\dfrac{E[Y]}2=\dfrac32$ at threshold $2$. The same bound applies to strictly exceeding that threshold because $\{Y>2\}\subseteq\{Y\ge2\}$. The general probability bound $P(Y>2)\le1$ also applies. The smaller of the two bounds is $1$.
