Design A selects a pair without replacement from a finite population. Design B draws the first unit at random from the same population and selects the second at random among the units that are not adjacent to the first in the list. Which condition justifies using the finite population correction?

- The two draws must give the same result
- Each unit must have a distinct value
- The first draw must have positive finite variance
- All unordered pairs must be equally likely

## Explanation

The finite population correction assumes simple random sampling without replacement. For a sample of two, every pair of units must have the same probability. In design B, pairs of units adjacent in the list are never drawn, so they have probability zero, whereas the other pairs have positive probability. Sampling without replacement alone does not establish the design the formula requires.
