The number of i.i.d. measurements from the same population increases from $36$ to $144$. What is the ratio of the new standard error to the original one?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

Both samples come from the same population, so their observations share the same standard deviation $\sigma$. The factor $\sigma$ cancels in the ratio of standard errors, leaving $\dfrac{\sqrt{36}}{\sqrt{144}}=\dfrac6{12}=\dfrac12$. The sample size quadruples, while the factor in the denominator of the standard error only doubles.
