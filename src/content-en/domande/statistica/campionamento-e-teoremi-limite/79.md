In a simple random sample of $10$ units without replacement from $37$, what factor multiplies the standard error under sampling with replacement when the population variance uses divisor $N$?

- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac2{\sqrt3}$
- $\sqrt{\dfrac{27}{37}}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$

## Explanation

The ratio of the variances of the sample mean without and with replacement is $\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}$. Here its value is $\dfrac{37-10}{37-1}=\dfrac{27}{36}=\dfrac34$. Standard errors are the square roots of the respective variances, so their ratio is $\sqrt{\dfrac34}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$. The factor $\dfrac34$ applies to the variance.
