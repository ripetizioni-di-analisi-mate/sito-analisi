For an i.i.d. normal sample of $21$ observations, how many degrees of freedom does $\dfrac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2}$ have?

- $22$
- $21$
- $19$
- $20$

## Explanation

The $21$ deviations from the sample mean sum to zero. Once $20$ of them are known, the remaining deviation is determined. For a normal sample the standardized sum of squares can be represented as the sum of $20$ squared independent standard normal variables. The statistic therefore has $21-1=20$ degrees of freedom.
