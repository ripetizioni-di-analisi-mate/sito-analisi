An i.i.d. sample comes from a skewed population with finite mean and positive finite variance. What does the central limit theorem imply?

- The standardized mean converges in probability to $0$
- The cumulative distribution function of the standardized mean converges to $\Phi$
- The mean is exactly normal for every $n$
- The population becomes normal for large $n$

## Explanation

The observations are i.i.d. with positive finite variance, so the central limit theorem applies. Subtract $\mu$ from the sample mean and divide by $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}$. The cumulative distribution function of the resulting statistic converges to $\Phi$. Skewness concerns the individual measurements and may persist for every $n$.
