The tolerance changes from $a$ to $3a$ while the variable and its center remain fixed. How does Chebyshev's ratio of the variance to the squared tolerance change?

- It is divided by $9$
- It is divided by $3$
- It is multiplied by $9$
- It is multiplied by $3$

## Explanation

For a variable with variance $\sigma^2$ and tolerance $a>0$, Chebyshev's ratio is $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{a^2}$. Changing the tolerance to $3a$ leaves the numerator unchanged and makes the denominator $(3a)^2=9a^2$. The new ratio is therefore one-ninth of the original ratio because the threshold is squared.
