In $30$ i.i.d. Bernoulli trials with $p=0.01$, which fact indicates that the normal approximation to the count may be inaccurate?

- The count has zero variance
- The expected count is $30$
- The trials are dependent
- The probability mass at zero is approximately $0.74$

## Explanation

For $K=0$ to occur, all $30$ trials must fail. Independence gives probability $(1-0.01)^{30}=0.99^{30}\approx0.74$. Almost three-quarters of the probability lies at the boundary of the support. The expected number of successes is also $np=0.3$. This concentration at one value makes a symmetric normal density a poor approximation.
