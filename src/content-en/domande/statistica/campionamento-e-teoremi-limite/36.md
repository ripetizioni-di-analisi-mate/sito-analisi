For $10$ i.i.d. observations from a normal population with positive variance, which two statistics are independent?

- The mean $\bar X_{10}$ and the sum of the observations
- The mean $\bar X_{10}$ and twice that mean
- The variance $S^2$ and its square root $S$
- The mean $\bar X_{10}$ and the sample variance $S^2$

## Explanation

For a normal sample with $n\ge2$, the theorem on the t statistic states that the mean $\bar X_n$ and the sample variance $S^2$ are independent; here $n=10$. In the other three pairs one statistic is a function of the other: the sum equals $10\bar X_{10}$, twice the mean equals $2\bar X_{10}$, and $S$ is the square root of $S^2$. Knowing the first therefore determines the second, so the two are not independent. The independence of the mean and the sample variance holds because the sample is normal.
