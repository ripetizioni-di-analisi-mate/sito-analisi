For i.i.d. nonnormal measurements with positive finite variance, which statement distinguishes the weak law from the central limit theorem?

- The individual observations converge in distribution to $N(0,1)$
- The mean converges in distribution to $N(0,1)$ without standardization
- The mean converges in probability to $\mu$ and the standardized mean converges in distribution to $N(0,1)$
- The standardized mean converges in probability to $\mu$

## Explanation

The weak law considers the sample mean in its original units and says deviations greater than any fixed tolerance become unlikely. The central limit theorem considers $Z_n=\dfrac{\bar X_n-\mu}{\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}}$ and gives its limiting distribution, $N(0,1)$. The conclusions concern two different quantities formed from the same sample.
