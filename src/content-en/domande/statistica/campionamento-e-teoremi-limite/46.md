The mean and variance of $X$ are known and finite. Why can Chebyshev's inequality be applied even if $X$ takes negative values?

- Markov's inequality is applied to the squared deviation
- It is sufficient for the variance to be smaller than the mean
- The mean makes all the deviations positive
- Markov's inequality is applied directly to $X$

## Explanation

For tolerance $a>0$, the event $|X-\mu|\ge a$ is equivalent to $(X-\mu)^2\ge a^2$. Markov's inequality applies because $(X-\mu)^2$ is nonnegative even when $X$ can take negative values. Its expectation is $\operatorname{Var}(X)$. This gives Chebyshev's bound $\dfrac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{a^2}$.
