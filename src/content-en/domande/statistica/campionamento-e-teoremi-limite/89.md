A student sorts the numerical data in a sample before calculating the mean. Which property explains why the mean stays the same?

- Sorting makes the observations independent
- Permuting the observations leaves their sum unchanged
- Sorting turns the mean into a median
- The mean depends only on the maximum

## Explanation

Sorting the sample permutes the observation indices. Each measurement still appears exactly once, so the commutative law of addition leaves the sum unchanged. The number of observations is still $n$, so dividing the sum by the sample size gives the same result. This holds for every permutation and applies to the observed sample mean.
