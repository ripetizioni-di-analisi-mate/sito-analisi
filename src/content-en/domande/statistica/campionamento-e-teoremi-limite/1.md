An i.i.d. sample has size $49$, and each observation has standard deviation $14\,\mathrm{g}$. Calculate the standard error of the sample mean.

- $\dfrac27\,\mathrm{g}$
- $4\,\mathrm{g}$
- $2\,\mathrm{g}$
- $14\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

Independence gives the sum of the $49$ observations variance $49\cdot14^2$. Dividing the sum by $49$ to form the mean divides its variance by $49^2$, giving $\dfrac{14^2}{49}=4\,\mathrm{g}^2$. Its square root is the standard error $2\,\mathrm{g}$, which measures the spread of means across repeated samples of the same size.
