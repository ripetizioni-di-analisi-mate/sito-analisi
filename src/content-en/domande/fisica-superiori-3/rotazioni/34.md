At the end of a horizontal lever $0.30\,\mathrm m$ long, a $20\,\mathrm N$ force points $30^\circ$ upwards from the outward radius. A second force is applied vertically downwards halfway along the lever. What magnitude cancels the net torque about the pivot?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 3,0" segmenti="3,0,5,0" curve="3+.72*cos(t),.72*sin(t),0,.52359878" punti="0,0,pieno,O;3,0,pieno,A" vettori="3,0,5.598,1.5,F" etichette="3.95,.21,30°" xmin=-1 xmax=6.5 ymin=-1 ymax=3 titolo="The force makes thirty degrees with the arm r"}

Neglect the lever’s own weight.

- $10\,\mathrm N$
- $20\,\mathrm N$
- $5\,\mathrm N$
- $40\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

The first torque comes from the ten-newton vertical component applied at the tip. The second force is already perpendicular to the lever and gives an opposing torque. Its moment arm is half as long, so equal torque magnitudes require a force of twice ten newtons.

$$
F_2=\frac{0.30\cdot20\sin30^\circ}{0.15}=20\,\mathrm N.
$$
