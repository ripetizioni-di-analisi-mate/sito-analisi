A constant net torque of $+5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ produces $\alpha=+20\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ about a rotor’s fixed axis. Find the moment of inertia about that axis.

- $100\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

The torque and angular acceleration refer to the same axis. The inertia is the factor that turns angular acceleration into net torque. Divide five by twenty to find it. The result means that each radian per second squared of acceleration requires a quarter of a newton metre.

$$
I=\frac{M}{\alpha}=\frac5{20}=0.25\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.
$$
