A disc has inertia $0.20\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about its central axis. A $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ point mass can be fixed at different distances from the axis. At what distance should it be fixed for a total inertia of $0.29\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$?

- $0.30\,\mathrm m$
- $0.09\,\mathrm m$
- $\sqrt{0.29}\,\mathrm m$
- $0.60\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

Subtract the disc’s existing contribution from the target moment of inertia. The difference is the contribution available to the added mass. Because this contribution is proportional to the square of the distance, the distance is the positive square root of the difference divided by the mass.

$$
r=\sqrt{\frac{I-I_d}{m_p}}=\sqrt{\frac{0.29-0.20}{1.0}}=0.30\,\mathrm m.
$$
