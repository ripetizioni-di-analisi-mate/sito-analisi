A $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ rotor has a moment of inertia of $0.175\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about the axis through its centre of mass. It is mounted about a parallel axis $0.10\,\mathrm m$ from the central one. What is its moment of inertia about the new axis?

- $0.060\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.175\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.235\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.115\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

The new axis is parallel to the one through the centre of mass. The total mass times the square of the ten-centimetre separation is added to the central inertia. The distance must be expressed in metres for the result to be in kilogram metres squared.

$$
\begin{aligned}I&=I_{CM}+md^2\\[6pt]&=0.175+6\cdot(0.10)^2\\[6pt]&=0.235\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.\end{aligned}
$$
