Why can static friction provide a torque about the wheel’s centre while doing zero work in rolling without slipping on a fixed ramp?

- Its point of application is momentarily stationary
- The friction coefficient is always zero
- The force always passes through the centre
- Mass cancels the friction work

## Explanation

Friction has a moment arm about the centre of the wheel and can therefore change its rotation. Its work depends on the motion of the point of application. For rolling without slipping on a fixed incline that point is instantaneously at rest, so force times velocity is zero.
