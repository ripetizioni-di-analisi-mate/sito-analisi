A motor applies a constant torque of $+3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ to a rotor of inertia $0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. An encoder measures a linear increase from $3$ to $5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ in $2\,\mathrm s$. What is the signed resistive torque if the motor and the resistance are the only sources of torque?

- $-2.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$

## Explanation

The two readings give the slope of angular velocity and therefore the acceleration. Multiplying by the inertia gives the net torque. This is smaller than the torque supplied by the motor, so the resistive torque is negative and equals the difference between the two values.

$$
\begin{aligned}\alpha&=\frac{5-3}{2}=1\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}\\[6pt]M_{\mathrm{r}}&=I\alpha-M_{\mathrm{m}}\\[6pt]&=0.50\cdot1-3=-2.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}.\end{aligned}
$$
