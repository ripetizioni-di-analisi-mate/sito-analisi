A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ plate has a moment of inertia of $0.24\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ about a perpendicular axle $0.20\,\mathrm m$ from its centre of mass. A parallel axle is $0.10\,\mathrm m$ from the centre on the opposite side. What is its moment of inertia about the second axle?

- $0.51\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.27\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.15\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.12\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

Both axes are parallel to the central one, so the parallel-axis theorem can be used twice. First subtract the shift term for the first axle to find the central inertia. Then add the term for the second separation. Each axle uses its distance from the centre.

$$
\begin{aligned}I_{CM}&=0.24-3(0.20)^2=0.12\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}\\[6pt]I_2&=0.12+3(0.10)^2=0.15\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.\end{aligned}
$$
