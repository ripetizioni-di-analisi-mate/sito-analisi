The centre of a wheel moves rightwards at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over a stationary surface. At the lowest point, rotation gives a velocity of $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to the left relative to the centre. What is the velocity of the bottom point relative to the surface, taking rightwards as positive?

- $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The two velocity contributions at the bottom lie along the same line and point in opposite directions. The negative contribution of the rotation is added to the positive centre velocity. The second contribution is larger here, so the bottom point slips to the left relative to the surface.

$$
v_P=v_{CM}-|\omega|R=2-3=-1\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
