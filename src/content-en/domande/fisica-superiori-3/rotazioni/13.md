A flywheel starts with $4.4\,\mathrm J$ of kinetic energy. The net torque is $+2\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ over the first $4\,\mathrm{rad}$ and $-1.3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ over the next $2\,\mathrm{rad}$, all in the positive direction. What is the final kinetic energy?

- $9.8\,\mathrm J$
- $12.4\,\mathrm J$
- $15.0\,\mathrm J$
- $5.4\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The angular displacement is positive in both intervals. The first torque does positive work and the second removes energy. The signed sum of the two amounts of work is the change in kinetic energy. To find the final energy, add the energy already stored in the flywheel.

$$
\begin{aligned}W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=2\cdot4-1.3\cdot2=5.4\,\mathrm J\\[6pt]K_f&=4.4\,\mathrm J+5.4\,\mathrm J=9.8\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$
