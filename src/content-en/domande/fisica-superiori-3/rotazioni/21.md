Two discs start from rest with constant angular accelerations of $4$ and $9\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. The first takes $6\,\mathrm s$ to reach a reference mark. How long does the second take to turn through the same angle?

- $4\,\mathrm s$
- $\dfrac{8}{3}\,\mathrm s$
- $9\,\mathrm s$
- $6\,\mathrm s$

## Explanation

For each disc the angle swept from rest is proportional to acceleration and to the square of the time. Reaching the same angle requires equal products. The second takes less time because it accelerates more. The time ratio is the square root of the inverse acceleration ratio.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac12\alpha_1t_1^2&=\frac12\alpha_2t_2^2\\[6pt]t_2&=6\sqrt{\frac49}=4\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$
