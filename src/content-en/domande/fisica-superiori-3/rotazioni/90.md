A disc starts from rest and rolls down a ramp without slipping or dissipating energy. A block starts from rest and slides without friction through the same vertical drop. Why does the disc’s centre reach a smaller speed?

- The disc loses all energy to friction
- Some of the disc's energy goes into rotation
- The radius cancels gravity
- The block gains no kinetic energy

## Explanation

For the same drop, the block and disc gain the same kinetic energy per unit mass. The block gains it all as translation. The disc stores a fraction of it in rotation, leaving a smaller share for the speed of the centre.
