A flywheel has a moment of inertia of $0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ on its own and carries two point masses of $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ each. They start $0.50\,\mathrm m$ from the axis as the wheel turns at $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. They move inwards symmetrically with zero external torque. At what radius are they when the flywheel reaches $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$?

- $0.0625\,\mathrm m$
- $0.25\,\mathrm m$
- $0.75\,\mathrm m$
- $0.40\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

Initial inertia includes the flywheel and both masses. Conservation of angular momentum gives the combined final inertia. The fixed flywheel contribution is subtracted. The contribution left comes from both point masses and allows the squared distance from the axis to be calculated.

$$
\begin{aligned}I_i&=0.50+2\cdot0.50\cdot0.50^2=0.75\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}\\[6pt]I_f&=0.75\frac68=0.5625\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}\\[6pt]r_f&=\sqrt{\frac{0.5625-0.50}{2\cdot0.50}}=0.25\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$
