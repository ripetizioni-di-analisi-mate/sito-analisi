A rotor of radius $0.25\,\mathrm m$ and inertia $0.40\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ has a fixed axis. At the leftmost point, a $32\,\mathrm N$ force makes a $30^\circ$ angle with the outward radius and has a downward vertical component. At the rightmost point, a $4\,\mathrm N$ tangential force points downwards. With anticlockwise rotation positive, what is the angular acceleration $\alpha$?

- $12.5\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $-7.5\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$
- $7.5\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$

## Explanation

On the left only the vertical component gives a torque, and it tends to turn the disc anticlockwise. The force on the right produces a clockwise torque. The net torque is the difference of the two contributions, and dividing it by the inertia about the same axis gives $\alpha$.

$$
\begin{aligned}M_z&=0.25\left(32\sin30^\circ-4\right)=3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}\\[6pt]\alpha&=\frac{3}{0.40}=7.5\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}.\end{aligned}
$$
