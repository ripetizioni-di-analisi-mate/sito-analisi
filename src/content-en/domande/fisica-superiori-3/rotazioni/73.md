A pulley accelerates with negligible inertia and axle friction. The only torques along its axis come from tensions on opposite sides, tangent to the same circle. Why are the tension magnitudes almost equal?

- Their torques have a negligible sum
- The angular acceleration must be zero
- Each tension produces zero torque
- Axle friction balances the difference between the tensions

## Explanation

For a pulley of negligible inertia, the product of inertia and angular acceleration is negligible even while it accelerates. With a frictionless axle, the tension torques are the only ones left. They have opposite signs and the same moment arm, so their negligible sum makes the tensions approximately equal.
