A thin ring of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ starts from rest and rolls without slipping down a vertical drop of $1.6\,\mathrm m$. Air resistance removes $14\,\mathrm J$ of mechanical energy. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what speed does the centre reach?

- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\sqrt{18}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\sqrt7\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Gravity releases thirty-two joules and resistance removes fourteen. For a ring, translational and rotational energies are equal as long as the rolling constraint holds. Their sum is the mass times the speed squared. The speed is the positive square root.

$$
\begin{aligned}K_f&=2\cdot10\cdot1.6-14=18\,\mathrm J\\[6pt]v_f&=\sqrt{\frac{K_f}{m}}=\sqrt{\frac{18}{2}}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$
