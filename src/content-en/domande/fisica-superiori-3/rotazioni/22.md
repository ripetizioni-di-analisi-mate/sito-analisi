A disc starts with angular velocity $-2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and accelerates uniformly for $4\,\mathrm s$. Its net angular displacement is $+6\,\mathrm{rad}$. What is its signed final angular velocity?

- $3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $-5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

Mean angular velocity is net angular displacement divided by time. At constant acceleration it is also the arithmetic mean of the initial and final velocities. The final value must therefore lie as far above the mean as the initial value lies below it. Its positive sign implies reversal.

$$
\begin{aligned}\overline\omega&=\frac64=1.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}\\[6pt]\omega_f&=2\overline\omega-\omega_0=3-(-2)=5\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\end{aligned}
$$
