An axially symmetric body rolls without slipping on a plane. Its rotational kinetic energy about the centre is one quarter of its total kinetic energy. What is the ratio $\dfrac{I}{mR^2}$, where $I$ is the moment of inertia about the axis through the centre?

- $\dfrac13$
- $\dfrac14$
- $\dfrac34$
- $3$

## Explanation

The total kinetic energy is the sum of the translational energy $\dfrac12 mv^2$ and the rotational energy $\dfrac12 I\omega^2$. Since rotation holds one quarter of it, translation holds three quarters, so the rotational energy is one third of the translational energy. In rolling without slipping $v=\omega R$, therefore the ratio of the two energies is
$$
\frac{\frac12 I\omega^2}{\frac12 mv^2}=\frac{I\omega^2}{m\omega^2R^2}=\frac{I}{mR^2}.
$$
It follows that $\dfrac{I}{mR^2}=\dfrac13$. The value $\dfrac14$ comes from comparing the rotational energy with the total energy instead of the translational energy.
