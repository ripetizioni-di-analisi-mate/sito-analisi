A rotor of inertia $I=0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ initially turns at $+8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Its only axial torque is a braking torque of $-3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. What is the angular velocity after it turns a further $4\,\mathrm{rad}$ in the positive direction?

- $2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $-4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The brake removes twelve joules because torque and angular displacement have opposite signs. Four of the initial sixteen joules remain. Taking the square root converts that energy to angular velocity. The sign is positive because the rotor completes the interval before stopping.

$$
\begin{aligned}K_f&=\frac12\cdot0.50\cdot8^2-3\cdot4=4\,\mathrm J\\[6pt]\omega_f&=+\sqrt{\frac{2K_f}{I}}=4\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\end{aligned}
$$
