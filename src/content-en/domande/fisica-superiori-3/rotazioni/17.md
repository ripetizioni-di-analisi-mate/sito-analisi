An encoder records $\theta=0$, $3$ and $8\,\mathrm{rad}$ at $t=0$, $1$ and $2\,\mathrm s$. If angular acceleration is constant, what is the angular velocity at $t=0$?

- $2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The two averages, three and five radians per second, are the values at the interval midpoints because acceleration is constant. Their change over one second gives the acceleration. To return to the start, subtract the half-second increase from the first average.

$$
\begin{aligned}\alpha&=\frac{5-3}{1.5-0.5}=2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}\\[6pt]\omega_0&=3-2\cdot0.5=2\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\end{aligned}
$$
