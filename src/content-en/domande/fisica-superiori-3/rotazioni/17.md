The angular position of a wheel is $\theta=0$, $3$ and $8\,\mathrm{rad}$ at times $t=0$, $1$ and $2\,\mathrm s$. If angular acceleration is constant, what is the angular velocity at $t=0$?

- $2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The average angular velocities over the two intervals are $3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Since the acceleration is constant, each equals the angular velocity at the midpoint of its interval, that is at $t=0.5\,\mathrm s$ and $t=1.5\,\mathrm s$. The change between these two instants gives the angular acceleration. To return to $t=0$, the increase in angular velocity over half a second is therefore subtracted from the first average.

$$
\begin{aligned}\alpha&=\frac{5-3}{1.5-0.5}=2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}\\[6pt]\omega_0&=3-2\cdot0.5=2\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\end{aligned}
$$
