A pulley of radius $0.20\,\mathrm m$ has downward tangential tensions of $12\,\mathrm N$ on the left and $7\,\mathrm N$ on the right. The tangential acceleration of the rim has magnitude $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the moment of inertia if axle friction is negligible?

- $0.02\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

The difference of the tensions multiplied by the radius gives the net torque magnitude. The measured acceleration is linear. Divide it by the radius to get the angular acceleration. The ratio of net torque to angular acceleration then gives the moment of inertia about the axle.

$$
\begin{aligned}|M|&=(12-7)\cdot0.20=1\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}\\[6pt]|\alpha|&=\frac{2}{0.20}=10\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}\\[6pt]I&=\frac{1}{10}=0.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.\end{aligned}
$$
