A velocity–time plot is a rising straight line. Another inertial observer adds the same constant to every velocity. How does the graph change?

- It rotates around the origin.
- It shifts vertically with unchanged slope.
- The slope becomes zero.
- It shifts horizontally with an always unchanged intercept.

## Explanation

A constant added to the ordinates shifts every point by the same amount. Differences between ordinates stay unchanged, so the slope representing acceleration is unchanged. The intercept depends on the frame and may even become negative. The graph still rises linearly because each pair of velocities keeps the same difference over the same time interval.
