A moving walkway advances at $2.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Between two images taken $2.0\,\mathrm s$ apart, a person walking on it moves $1.30\,\mathrm m$ backwards relative to a mark fixed to the walkway. What is the person's mean ground-velocity component, taking the direction of the walkway as positive?

- $2.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.65\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.95\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-1.95\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The two images give a relative displacement of $-1.30\,\mathrm m$. The mean velocity in the walkway frame is therefore $-\dfrac{1.30}{2.0}\,\mathrm{m/s}=-0.65\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The walkway adds $2.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the ground, giving $+1.95\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The person moves forward across the room while moving backwards away from the mark.
