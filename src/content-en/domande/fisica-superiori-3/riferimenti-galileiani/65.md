A kayak travels $60\,\mathrm m$ downstream and then returns to its starting point. Its speed through the water is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on both legs and the current is $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does the entire trip take?

- $40\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $30\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $20\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $15\,\mathrm{s}$.

## Explanation

Downstream the current adds, giving ten seconds. Upstream it reduces the ground speed, giving thirty seconds. Add the times of the two legs.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\dfrac{60}{4+2}+\dfrac{60}{4-2}\\[4pt]
&= 40\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

An arithmetic mean of the two speeds would overlook that the slower leg lasts longer.
