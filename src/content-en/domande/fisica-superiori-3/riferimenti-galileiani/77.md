Two probes have ground-frame accelerations $+3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ on the same axis. A third observer translates at constant velocity. What acceleration of the first relative to the second also holds for the moving observer?

- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

## Explanation

Each probe has the same acceleration in both inertial frames. For their separation, subtract the accelerations because their positions are subtracted at every instant.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{\mathrm{rel}}&=a_1-a_2=3-(-2)\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The positive sign refers to the first probe as seen from the second. Swapping the probes preserves the magnitude of five and makes the component negative.
