A rower points perpendicular to the banks relative to the water. An observer measures a drift of $18\,\mathrm m$ in $12\,\mathrm s$. What is the uniform current speed?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

## Explanation

The rower's contribution is entirely transverse. All ground drift comes from the current, so divide the downstream distance by the observed time.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=\dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{18}{12}\\[4pt]
&= \dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

With a tilted heading, the rower would also contribute an along-bank component. In that case the drift alone would measure the sum of the two contributions.
