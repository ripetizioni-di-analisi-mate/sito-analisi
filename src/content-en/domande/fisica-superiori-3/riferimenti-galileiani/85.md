A $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ person stands still relative to a lift's scale, which reads $450\,\mathrm N$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and upwards positive, what is the lift acceleration?

- $-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $-9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

## Explanation

The normal force is below the weight. The net force therefore points down, and the acceleration has the same direction while contact with the scale is maintained.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\dfrac{N-mg}{m}=\dfrac{450-500}{50}\\[4pt]
&= -1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The net force is $(450-500)\,\mathrm N=-50\,\mathrm N$, so $a=-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The sign gives the direction of the velocity change.
