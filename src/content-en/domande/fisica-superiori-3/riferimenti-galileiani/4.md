A runner at $v_A=8.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ chases another at $v_B=3.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, initially $d=45\,\mathrm m$ ahead. Both velocities are constant and in the same direction. Find the time to catch up, rounded to the nearest hundredth of a second.

- $5.49\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $3.91\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $-9.18\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $9.18\,\mathrm{s}$.

## Explanation

The pursuer closes the gap at the rate $v_A-v_B$. Divide the initial gap by this closing velocity. Up to rounding, substituting the time into the two position equations gives the same coordinate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\dfrac{d}{v_A-v_B}\\[4pt]&=\dfrac{45}{4.9}\,\mathrm s\simeq9.18\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

In nine seconds the pursuer gains $44.1\,\mathrm m$, still less than the initial gap. The calculated time is therefore a little more than nine seconds.
