A laboratory and a nonrotating carriage measure the accelerations of two bodies at the same instant with the same positive directions. For the first they measure $\vec a_1=(3,1)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $\vec a'_1=(1,2)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. For the second the laboratory measures $\vec a_2=(-1,4)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What must the carriage measure for the second body?

- $(1,3)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $(-1,4)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $(-3,5)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $(-3,3)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The first body gives the carriage acceleration as $\vec a_1-\vec a'_1=(2,-1)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. In pure translation the same correction applies to every body at a common instant. For the second body, subtracting $(2,-1)$ from $(-1,4)$ gives $(-3,5)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The vertical component increases because a negative value is subtracted.
