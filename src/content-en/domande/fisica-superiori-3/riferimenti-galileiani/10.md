A train maintains the same velocity. A passenger walks along the aisle towards the front and then towards the rear at the same speed relative to the carriage. Taking the train's direction as positive, the station measures $18.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $16.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for the passenger. What is the train's velocity relative to the ground?

- $14.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $17.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $34.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

If the train has velocity $u$ and the passenger walks at relative speed $w$, the station measures $u+w$ on the outward walk and $u-w$ on the return. Adding the readings gives $2u$ because the relative contributions cancel. Halving $18.4+16.0$ gives $17.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Half their difference would give the walking speed inside the carriage.
