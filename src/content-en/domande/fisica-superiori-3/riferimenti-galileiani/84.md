A person of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ stays on a scale in a lift that is moving up and slowing down with acceleration of magnitude $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The scale shows $54\,\mathrm{kg}$ and obtains that number by dividing the force on its platform by $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Which interpretation is consistent?

- The acceleration is upwards because the lift is rising.
- The mass has become $54\,\mathrm{kg}$ for all observers.
- The normal force is $600\,\mathrm N$ and the display requires a calibration error.
- The normal force is $540\,\mathrm N$ and the mass remains $60\,\mathrm{kg}$.

## Explanation

The lift is moving up, but its upward velocity is decreasing. Taking upwards as positive, the ground-frame equation is $N-mg=m(-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2})$, so $N=540\,\mathrm N$. The force on the scale has the same magnitude as the normal force. Dividing by the calibration value of gravity gives the displayed number, while the person's mass stays the same.
