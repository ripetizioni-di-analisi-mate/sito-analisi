Which observation can tell a passenger the train's constant velocity relative to the station?

- Measure the time a body takes to fall between two marks inside the train.
- Measure the time taken to pass two fixed signs a known distance apart.
- Let a free puck slide on the smooth carriage floor and measure its acceleration.
- Repeat a mass measurement on the same scales in the carriage.

## Explanation

The signs are stationary relative to the station. Their separation and the elapsed time between passing them give the relative velocity. Entirely internal measurements obey the same laws in inertial frames. The result is relative to the station because the markers belong to that frame. The time an object takes to fall between two points inside the carriage instead depends on the conditions of the fall.
