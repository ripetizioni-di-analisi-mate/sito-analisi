Two points are photographed at the same instant. Their coordinates in $S'$ are $-2$ and $5\,\mathrm m$. What can an observer in $S$ with parallel, aligned axes say?

- The second point is seven metres ahead of the first.
- The second point is five metres ahead of the first.
- The second point is seven metres behind the first.
- The separation depends on the origins' velocity.

## Explanation

The same $X(t)$ is added to each position. Subtraction cancels the common term. This gives $5-(-2)=7\,\mathrm m$ with the same sign in both frames. The simultaneous photographs allow a single origin position to be used. With photographs at different times, the common term could vary and subtracting the coordinates would not measure the same separation.
