A carriage has constant acceleration $-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. A horizontally free object starts at rest in the carriage. What is its relative displacement after $2\,\mathrm s$?

- $-8\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $16\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $8\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $4\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

The real horizontal force is zero, so the object's ground velocity stays constant. The braking frame sees positive relative acceleration. Starting from zero relative velocity, use the quadratic displacement law.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a'&=0-(-4)=4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[4pt]\Delta x'&=\dfrac{1}{2}\,4\cdot2^2\\[4pt]
&= 8\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

Its positive sign is opposite to the carriage acceleration, as expected for a body free to move horizontally.
