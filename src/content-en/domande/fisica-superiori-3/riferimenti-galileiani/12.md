At $t=0$ runner $A$ is $45.6\,\mathrm m$ behind $B$ and runs at $8.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. $B$ runs in the same direction at $5.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for the first $2.0\,\mathrm s$, then changes to $3.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At what time, to the nearest hundredth of a second, does $A$ catch $B$?

- $7.62\,\mathrm{s}$
- $5.30\,\mathrm{s}$
- $15.2\,\mathrm{s}$
- $9.62\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

During the first two seconds the gap closes by $(8.6-5.6)\cdot2.0\,\mathrm m=6.0\,\mathrm m$. There are $39.6\,\mathrm m$ to go, so the meeting occurs in the second section. The new closing rate is $(8.6-3.4)\,\mathrm{m/s}=5.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The meeting time is the sum of the initial two seconds and the time needed to close the remaining gap. Measured from the original zero, the time is $2.0\,\mathrm s+\dfrac{39.6}{5.2}\,\mathrm s\simeq9.62\,\mathrm s$.
