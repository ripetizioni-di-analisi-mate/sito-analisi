A body changes ground velocity from $3$ to $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $4\,\mathrm s$. A second observer translates at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the same axis. What mean acceleration does the second observer measure?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

## Explanation

Subtract the same frame velocity at the start and at the end. The velocity change stays twelve metres per second even though the sign of the initial velocity changes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline a'&=\dfrac{(15-6)-(3-6)}{4}\\[4pt]
&= 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The two moving-frame readings are $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Their difference is $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Dividing by $4\,\mathrm s$ again gives $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, even though the initial velocities measured by the two observers have opposite signs.
