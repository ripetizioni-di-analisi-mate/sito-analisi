Two trolleys modelled as points move along straight parallel lanes separated by $1\,\mathrm m$ across the lanes. At a given instant they have the same coordinate measured along the lanes. What follows about whether they meet in space?

- They meet because they are at the same point.
- They meet only if their velocities also agree.
- They are side by side at different positions.
- They meet because the transverse distance vanishes.

## Explanation

Agreement along the lanes puts the trolleys at the same position on the longitudinal axis. Their transverse coordinates still differ, so the separation between them is $1\,\mathrm m$. For two points in the plane to meet, their second coordinates must also agree, regardless of their velocities.
