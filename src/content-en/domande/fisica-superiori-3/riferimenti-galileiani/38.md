A ball has speed $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to a carriage, and the carriage speed is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What information is missing to determine the ground speed uniquely?

- The ball's mass.
- The angle between the velocities.
- The initial coordinate of the carriage.
- The scale of the shared clock.

## Explanation

The sum is a vector. Parallel velocities in the same direction give magnitude seven, while opposite velocities give one. The ground speed therefore depends on their relative direction.
