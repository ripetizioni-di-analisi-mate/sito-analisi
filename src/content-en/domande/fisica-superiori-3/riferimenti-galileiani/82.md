A carriage accelerates right at $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ without rotating. An observer inside measures relative acceleration $-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for a body. What acceleration does a ground observer measure with the same positive direction?

- $-8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

## Explanation

To return to the ground frame, add the acceleration of the moving origin to the relative acceleration. The result is still negative and its magnitude is smaller than the onboard reading.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=a'+A=-5+3\\[4pt]
&= -2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The signed sum is $(-5+3)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The carriage accelerates to the right, so the leftward acceleration measured inside is larger.
