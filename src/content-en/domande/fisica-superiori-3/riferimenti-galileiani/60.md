A boat travelling at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water crosses an $80\,\mathrm m$ river with a $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ current. How long does the crossing take if it must land directly opposite?

- $8\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $20\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $\dfrac{40}{3}\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $10\,\mathrm{s}$.

## Explanation

A six-metres-per-second upstream component cancels the current. The remaining transverse projection is eight metres per second, so use that component to divide the width.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v'_y&=\sqrt{10^2-6^2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=8\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[4pt]t&=\dfrac{80}{8}\\[4pt]
&= 10\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$
