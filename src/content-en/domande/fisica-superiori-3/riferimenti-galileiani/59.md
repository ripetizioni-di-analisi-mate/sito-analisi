A motor produces a speed relative to the water equal to the current speed. What happens if the pilot decides to cancel the drift exactly?

- The transverse component equals the current speed.
- The crossing time is minimal.
- The entire speed is used upstream and the transverse component is zero.
- The boat still crosses and lands downstream.

## Explanation

Cancelling $u$ requires $v'_x=-u$. With fixed speed $v_b=u$, the identity $v_b^2=(v'_x)^2+(v'_y)^2$ leaves only $v'_y=0$. With all the velocity directed against the current, the boat stays at the same position relative to the bank. If the pilot reserves part of the velocity for crossing, cancellation along the bank is incomplete and drift returns.
