A river is $45.0\,\mathrm m$ wide. The boat maintains a constant velocity relative to the water, perpendicular to the banks, and the uniform current is parallel to the banks. When it has travelled $30.0\,\mathrm m$ across the river, the boat is already $10.8\,\mathrm m$ downstream from its starting point. What is the drift distance on arrival?

- $16.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $45.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10.8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Both ground-velocity components are constant. The two displacements therefore grow in the same ratio because each is proportional to time. That ratio stays the same from the observed point to the opposite bank. For the full crossing the drift is $10.8\cdot\dfrac{45.0}{30.0}\,\mathrm m=16.2\,\mathrm m$. The $5.4\,\mathrm m$ added after the observation represents only the final section.
