A moving frame has axes parallel to and in the same direction as those of the ground, and it translates only along $x$. In the ground frame its origin has $y$-coordinate $Y=4\,\mathrm m$ and a drop has $y$-coordinate $y=11\,\mathrm m$. What is the $y$-coordinate of the drop in the moving frame?

- $11\,\mathrm m$
- $7\,\mathrm m$
- $15\,\mathrm m$
- $4\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

With parallel axes in the same direction, each relative coordinate is the difference between the ground coordinate of the point and that of the moving origin, so $y'=y-Y$. The frame translates only along $x$, therefore the $y$-coordinate of its origin stays $Y=4\,\mathrm m$ at every instant. It follows that
$$
y'=(11-4)\,\mathrm m=7\,\mathrm m.
$$
The value $11\,\mathrm m$ comes from ignoring the offset between the $y$-coordinates of the two origins, and $15\,\mathrm m$ from adding the two coordinates instead of subtracting them.
