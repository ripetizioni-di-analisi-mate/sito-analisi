Two observers put the zeros of their axes at different points. The second translates at constant velocity to the right. When is the formula $x=x'+ut$ exact?

- When measuring one second after departure.
- As soon as the relative velocity has a positive component.
- When the object under study is at the moving origin.
- When time zero is chosen at the instant the origins pass each other.

## Explanation

In general, $X=X_0+ut$. The formula without $X_0$ holds when $X(0)=0$, so the two origins coincide at the chosen initial instant.
