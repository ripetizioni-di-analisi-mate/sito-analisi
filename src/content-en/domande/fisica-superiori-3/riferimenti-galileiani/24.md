Two frames lie along the same oriented line. The moving origin travels right at constant velocity relative to the fixed one. Which choice of time zero makes $x=x'+ut$ hold?

- When measuring one second after departure.
- As soon as the relative velocity has a positive component.
- When the object under study is at the moving origin.
- When time zero is chosen at the instant the origins pass each other.

## Explanation

In general, $X=X_0+ut$. The formula without $X_0$ holds when $X(0)=0$, so the two origins coincide at the chosen initial instant. The instant when the moving origin passes the fixed origin can be chosen as the new time zero. With that choice in both descriptions, the intercept disappears while the frame velocity stays the same.
