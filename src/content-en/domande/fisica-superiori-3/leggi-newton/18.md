At the same nonzero net force, mass doubles. How does acceleration change?

- Opposite direction and same magnitude.
- Same direction and half the magnitude.
- Same direction and twice the magnitude.
- It vanishes.

## Explanation

The net force divided by mass gives acceleration. The denominator doubles and stays positive, so direction is unchanged. If the original acceleration was $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ eastwards, at unchanged resultant it becomes $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ eastwards. The comparison holds at equal net force.
