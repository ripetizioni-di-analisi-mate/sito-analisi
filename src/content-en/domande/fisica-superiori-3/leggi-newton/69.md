In a massless model, a taut straight string links two bodies. Why do its ends have equal tension magnitudes?

- A nonzero resultant on the massless string would be inconsistent with finite acceleration.
- Each end necessarily exerts the string's weight.
- The tensions must equal the two masses.
- The third law applies to the two ends as though they were one external body.

## Explanation

Isolate the actions at the ends of the ideal string. Their sum must vanish in its negligible-mass balance, so the magnitudes agree.
