Masses of $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ hang from the ends of a cord passing over a pulley. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, the heavier mass descends with acceleration of magnitude $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The tension is $22\,\mathrm N$ in the branch of the lighter mass and $27\,\mathrm N$ in the branch of the heavier mass. Which model of the pulley is consistent with these data?

- An ideal pulley and equal tensions
- A pulley with appreciable rotational inertia
- Static equilibrium of both masses
- A completely slack cord on both sides

## Explanation

The lighter mass accelerates upwards, so the tension in its branch exceeds its weight by $2\,\mathrm N$. The heavier mass accelerates downwards, so the tension in its branch is $3\,\mathrm N$ less than its weight.

$$
T_1=2(10+1)\,\mathrm N=22\,\mathrm N,\qquad T_2=3(10-1)\,\mathrm N=27\,\mathrm N.
$$

The two tensions are different, so they exert a nonzero net torque on the pulley. In an ideal pulley, which has no inertia, the tensions in the two branches are equal, so the data describe a pulley with appreciable rotational inertia.
