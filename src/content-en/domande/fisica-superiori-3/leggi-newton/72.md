An ideal Atwood machine has hanging masses of $2$ and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$. A mass is added to the lighter side so that the $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ side still descends with acceleration at most $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the minimum mass to add, with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $\dfrac{5}{11}\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The added load reduces the weight imbalance and increases the total mass. Acceleration falls as more mass is added to the lighter side. The smallest addition reaches exactly one metre per second squared. At that value the descending side is still heavier.

$$
a=g\frac{1\,\mathrm{kg}-\Delta m}{5\,\mathrm{kg}+\Delta m}=1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta m=\frac{5}{11}\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
