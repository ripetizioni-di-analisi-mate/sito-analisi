In an ideal Atwood machine, $2$ and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ masses hang on either side. At $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the acceleration magnitude?

::grafico{assi=0 curve="cos(t),2+sin(t),0,3.141592654" segmenti="-1,2,-1,0;1,2,1,-0.5" punti="-1,0,pieno;1,-0.5,pieno" vettori="-1,0,-1,0.7,T;1,-0.5,1,0.2,T" etichette="-1.35,-0.25,2;1.35,-0.7,3" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=3.4 titolo="The two branches exert equal tensions on the masses"}

- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

## Explanation

The three-kilogram mass falls and the two-kilogram mass rises. Taking each direction of motion as positive, the weight difference provides ten newtons to accelerate both. Dividing by five kilograms gives two. This is below $g$, as expected when one mass resists the other's fall.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=g\frac{m_2-m_1}{m_1+m_2}=10\frac{3-2}{3+2}\\[4pt]
&= 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
