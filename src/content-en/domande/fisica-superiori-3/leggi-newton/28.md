A stationary book on a table has weight and normal force of equal magnitude. Why are they not a third-law pair?

- They point in opposite directions.
- They occur simultaneously.
- They have the same magnitude.
- Both act on the book.

## Explanation

The reciprocal forces in one interaction act on two different bodies. Weight and normal force act externally on the same book through different interactions. Find the reaction to the normal in the table’s diagram, where it receives the book’s pressure. For the reaction to weight, inspect Earth’s diagram instead.
