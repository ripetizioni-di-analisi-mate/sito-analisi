A stationary book on a table has weight and normal force of equal magnitude. Why are they not a third-law pair?

- They point in opposite directions.
- They occur simultaneously.
- They have the same magnitude.
- Both act on the book.

## Explanation

The reciprocal forces in one interaction act on two different bodies. Weight and normal force act externally on the same book through different interactions.
