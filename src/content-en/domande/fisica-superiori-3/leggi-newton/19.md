An object instantaneously has $v=0$ but a nonzero resultant. What does Newton's second law imply?

- Acceleration is zero because the object is at rest at that instant.
- The resultant must be parallel to the zero velocity.
- Acceleration is nonzero and agrees with the resultant.
- The mass must become zero there.

## Explanation

The relationship concerns change in velocity, rather than its value. A nonzero resultant can initiate motion from an instant of rest. For example, at the highest point of a vertical throw the velocity is zero while gravity still produces downward acceleration.
