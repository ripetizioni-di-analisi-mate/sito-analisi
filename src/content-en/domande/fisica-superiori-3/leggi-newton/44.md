A lift moves up and its speed rises uniformly from $1$ to $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $2\,\mathrm s$. Next, its speed drops uniformly from $3$ to $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $1\,\mathrm s$ while the lift still moves up. A $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ person stands on the scale throughout. Find $N_1-N_2$.

- $50\,\mathrm{N}$
- $100\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-150\,\mathrm{N}$
- $150\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

With an upward axis the first segment has positive slope and the second has negative slope. The person’s weight contributes equally to both normal forces and cancels in their difference. The acceleration contributions remain even though the lift is moving up in both segments.

$$
\begin{aligned}a_1&=\frac{3-1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=1\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\qquad a_2=\frac{1-3}{1}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\N_1-N_2&=m(a_1-a_2)=50(1-(-2))\,\mathrm N=150\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$
