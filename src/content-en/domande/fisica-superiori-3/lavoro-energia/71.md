A crate–floor system initially has $80\,\mathrm J$ of total kinetic energy and ends with $44\,\mathrm J$. Its height is unchanged. An external actuator does $12\,\mathrm J$ of work on the system and there is no heat exchange with the surroundings. By how much does the system's internal energy rise?

- $24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $36\,\mathrm{J}$
- $48\,\mathrm{J}$
- $12\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Total energy increases by $12\,\mathrm J$ through external work. The mechanical part falls by $36\,\mathrm J$, so internal energy must rise by $(12+36)\,\mathrm J=48\,\mathrm J$. Crate–floor friction is internal to the system. Heating accounts for both the loss of kinetic energy and the actuator's input.
