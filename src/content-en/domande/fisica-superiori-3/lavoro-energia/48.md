A person carries an $8\,\mathrm{kg}$ bucket horizontally for $10\,\mathrm m$ and then lifts it by $3\,\mathrm m$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, by how much does the gravitational potential energy of the bucket change?

- $800\,\mathrm J$
- $120\,\mathrm J$
- $1040\,\mathrm J$
- $240\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

Near the Earth's surface the gravitational potential energy depends only on height, $U=mgh$. Along the horizontal stretch the height of the bucket does not change, so that stretch does not contribute to the change. Along the vertical stretch the bucket rises by $3\,\mathrm m$, therefore
$$
\Delta U=mg\,\Delta h=8\cdot10\cdot3\,\mathrm J=240\,\mathrm J.
$$
The value $1040\,\mathrm J$ comes from multiplying the weight by the length of the whole path, $13\,\mathrm m$, and $800\,\mathrm J$ from using only the horizontal stretch.
