A crate stays at rest on the horizontal floor of a lift while the lift rises $1.5\,\mathrm m$ relative to the laboratory. The normal force from the floor is constant at $180\,\mathrm N$. How much work does it do on the crate in the laboratory frame?

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-1,0 1,0 1,1 -1,1" linee="-1.75,0 1.75,0" vettori="0,.05,0,1.68,N;1.35,.15,1.35,1.28,s" xmin=-1.78 xmax=1.86 ymin=-.22 ymax=1.85 titolo="Displacement and the normal force are shown in the laboratory frame."}

- $-270\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $120\,\mathrm{J}$
- $270\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The normal force is perpendicular to the floor, so it points vertically upwards here. In the laboratory, the crate also moves upwards. The work is $180\cdot1.5\,\mathrm J=270\,\mathrm J$. A moving constraint can transfer energy because the contact point moves along the force.
