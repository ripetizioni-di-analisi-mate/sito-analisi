An object moves at $21.6\,\mathrm{km/h}$. In the kinetic energy formula the number $21.6$ is mistakenly substituted for the speed, as though it were already in metres per second, while the mass is correctly expressed in kilograms. By what factor is the kinetic energy overestimated?

- $12.96$
- $3.60$
- $1.80$
- $0.077$

## Explanation

The correct conversion is $21.6\,\mathrm{km/h}=6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The number substituted is therefore $3.6$ times the correct speed. Since kinetic energy depends on the square of the speed, the value obtained is $3.6^2=12.96$ times the correct one. The mass does not change the factor, because it has the same value in both calculations.
