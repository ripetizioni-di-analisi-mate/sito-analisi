A real elastic element is stretched and then returned to its initial length at negligible speed. The area under the loading branch of the force–extension graph is $6.0\,\mathrm J$. The area under the unloading branch is $4.5\,\mathrm J$. Stored elastic energy returns to its initial value. How much mechanical energy is dissipated in the cycle?

::grafico{linee="0,0 4,300;0,0 2,75 4,300" area="75*x;37.5*x;0;2|75*x;112.5*x-150;2;4" punti="0,0,pieno;2,75,pieno;4,300,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=4.5 ymin=0 ymax=325 nomi="x (cm),F (N)" titolo="The upper branch is followed during loading and the other during unloading."}

- $10.5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.5\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Stretching supplies $6.0\,\mathrm J$ and unloading returns $4.5\,\mathrm J$. Since final configuration and velocity agree with their initial values, the difference $(6.0-4.5)\,\mathrm J=1.5\,\mathrm J$ is the mechanical loss over the cycle. On the graph it is the area between the branches, which coincide for an ideal spring.
