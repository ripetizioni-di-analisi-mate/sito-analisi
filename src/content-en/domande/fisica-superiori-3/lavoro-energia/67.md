A mass descends without friction from $5$ to $3\,\mathrm m$ while its speed changes from $2$ to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which value of $g$ fits the model?

- $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Compare the kinetic energy gain with the loss in potential energy. The ratio keeps the difference of squared speeds and the vertical drop.

$$
g=\dfrac{v_f^2-v_i^2}{2(h_i-h_f)}=\dfrac{4^2-2^2}{2(5-3)}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

The kinetic energy increase per kilogram is $6\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. It must match what gravity supplies per kilogram over the two-metre descent. The assumption of negligible friction is essential to the comparison.
