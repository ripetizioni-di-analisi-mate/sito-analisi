An ideal spring is already under load. During a further quasi-static extension of $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the applied force rises linearly from $60$ to $100\,\mathrm N$. How much does the elastic energy increase?

::grafico{linee="0,60 4,100" area="60+10*x;0;0;4" punti="0,60,pieno;4,100,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=4.6 ymin=0 ymax=112 nomi="Δx (cm),F (N)" titolo="Δx measures the extra extension from the start of the test."}

- $3.2\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.8\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The work done by the applied force comes from the trapezoid between the two extensions. The mean force is $(60+100)/2\,\mathrm N=80\,\mathrm N$. Multiplying by $0.040\,\mathrm m$ gives $3.2\,\mathrm J$. A quasi-static test has negligible change in kinetic energy. The area gives the energy gain from the initial loaded state.
