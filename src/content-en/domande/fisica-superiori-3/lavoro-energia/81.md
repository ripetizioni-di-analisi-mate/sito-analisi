Over $6.0\,\mathrm s$, a machine's useful power increases linearly from $100$ to $400\,\mathrm W$. Using the power–time profile, how much useful energy is transferred?

::grafico{linee="0,100 6,400" area="100+50*x;0;0;6" punti="0,100,pieno;6,400,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=6.6 ymin=0 ymax=450 nomi="t (s),P (W)" titolo="Power varies linearly over the six seconds."}

- $1500\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1800\,\mathrm{J}$
- $250\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The transferred energy is the area of the trapezoid beneath the graph. A linear profile has mean power $(100+400)/2\,\mathrm W=250\,\mathrm W$. Multiplying by $6.0\,\mathrm s$ gives $1500\,\mathrm J$. Using the final value alone would give the energy delivered by a four-hundred-watt machine operating at that level throughout.
