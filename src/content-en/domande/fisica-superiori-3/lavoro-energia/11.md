A vehicle brakes on level ground under a net force of constant magnitude. In the first $4.0\,\mathrm m$ its speed falls from $8.0$ to $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. If the same force acts until it stops, how many more metres does it travel? Round to two decimal places.

- $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9.14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5.14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Distance is proportional to the loss of kinetic energy. The first four metres consume energy proportional to $8^2-6^2=28$. Energy proportional to $6^2=36$ remains. The remaining distance is $4.0\cdot\dfrac{36}{28}\,\mathrm m\simeq5.14\,\mathrm m$. Both energy changes correspond to the same braking force.
