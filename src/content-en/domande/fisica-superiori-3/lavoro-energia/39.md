A body loses half its kinetic energy between the start and end of a segment. If its initial speed is $14\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is its exit speed?

- $19.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4.95\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

At constant mass, halving the energy halves the squared speed. Take a square root to find the ratio of speeds.

$$
v_f=\dfrac{v_i}{\sqrt2}=\dfrac{14}{\sqrt2}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq9.90\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

The result is about seventy-one per cent of the initial speed. An exit speed of $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ would leave one quarter of the energy instead of half.
