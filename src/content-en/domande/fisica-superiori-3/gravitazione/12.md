A probe with period $2300\,\mathrm s$ orbits a planet of parameter $\mu_1$. A second probe has twice the semimajor axis and orbits another planet with $\mu_2=\dfrac89\mu_1$. What is the second probe’s period?

- $4.6\cdot10^{3}\,\mathrm s$
- $2.3\cdot10^{3}\,\mathrm s$
- $2.07\cdot10^{4}\,\mathrm s$
- $6.9\cdot10^{3}\,\mathrm s$

## Explanation

In the squared period ratio, doubling the semimajor axis contributes a factor of eight. The gravitational parameter is in the denominator, so multiply by the inverse parameter ratio. The product is nine. Taking the square root gives a period three times as long.

$$
\frac{T_2}{T_1}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{a_2}{a_1}\right)^3\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}}=\sqrt{8\cdot\frac98}=3,\qquad T_2=6.9\cdot10^3\,\mathrm s.
$$
