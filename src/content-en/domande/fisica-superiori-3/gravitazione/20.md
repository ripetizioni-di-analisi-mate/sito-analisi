At the surface of a spherical planet the field has magnitude $12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At an altitude of $6000\,\mathrm{km}$ it falls to $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the planet’s radius?

- $3000\,\mathrm{km}$
- $12000\,\mathrm{km}$
- $24000\,\mathrm{km}$
- $6000\,\mathrm{km}$

## Explanation

The field at the given height is a quarter of the surface field. The inverse-square law makes the distance from the centre twice the surface radius. This distance includes both the radius and the height. For it to be twice the radius, the height must equal the radius.

$$
\frac{3}{12}=\left(\frac{R}{R+6000\,\mathrm{km}}\right)^2,\qquad R=6000\,\mathrm{km}.
$$
