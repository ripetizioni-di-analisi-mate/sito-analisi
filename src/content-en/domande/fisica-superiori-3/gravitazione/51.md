A probe in a circular orbit has total energy $-10\,\mathrm{MJ}$. A first manoeuvre adds $6\,\mathrm{MJ}$. How much energy must a second manoeuvre supply so that the probe can reach infinity with $2\,\mathrm{MJ}$ of kinetic energy and no further work?

- $4\,\mathrm{MJ}$
- $8\,\mathrm{MJ}$
- $6\,\mathrm{MJ}$
- $12\,\mathrm{MJ}$

## Explanation

After the first manoeuvre, total energy remains negative at minus four megajoules. Potential energy is zero at infinity, so the target total equals the two megajoules of residual kinetic energy. The second manoeuvre must make up the difference between that value and the one already reached.

$$
W_2=2-(-10+6)=6\,\mathrm{MJ}.
$$
