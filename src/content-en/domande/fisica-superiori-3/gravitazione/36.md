A satellite in a circular orbit travels at $5000\,\mathrm{m/s}$. An ideal impulse points along its velocity and leaves the position unchanged during the manoeuvre. By how much must its speed increase to reach the escape threshold exactly?

- $5000\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5000\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2500\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5000(\sqrt2-1)\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

At that radius the required final speed is the circular speed times the square root of two. The satellite already has the initial speed and the impulse increases it along the same direction. The requested amount is the difference between the two speeds, about two thousand and seventy metres per second.

$$
\Delta v=v_{\mathrm{fuga}}-v_c=5000(\sqrt2-1)\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
