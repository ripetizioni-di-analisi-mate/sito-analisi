Two point masses $m_1=160\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2=290\,\mathrm{kg}$ are separated by $r=1.6\,\mathrm m$. Find the gravitational force magnitude. Take $G=6.67\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}$. Give the result in newtons to three significant figures.

- $1.21\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$
- $2.41788\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$
- $6.04469\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm N$
- $-1.20894\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

The gravitational law uses the product of the masses divided by the squared separation of their centres. Here the mass product is forty-six thousand four hundred kilograms squared. Divide by the separation squared and then multiply by G. The result is of the order of one millionth of a newton.

$$
F=6.67\cdot10^{-11}\frac{160\cdot290}{1.6^2}\simeq1.21\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N.
$$
