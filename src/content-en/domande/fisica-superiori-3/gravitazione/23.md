For a satellite of negligible mass in a circular orbit around a spherical planet, the orbital radius, the period and hence the gravitational parameter $GM$ of the planet are known. Which quantity can be calculated only if the radius of the planet is also known?

- The speed of the satellite along its orbit
- The centripetal acceleration of the satellite
- The gravitational acceleration at the surface of the planet
- The potential energy per unit mass on the orbit, with zero at infinity

## Explanation

From the orbital radius $r$ and the period $T$ one calculates the orbital speed $v=\dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$. The centripetal acceleration $\dfrac{v^2}{r}=\dfrac{GM}{r^2}$ and the potential energy per unit mass on the orbit, $-\dfrac{GM}{r}$, also depend only on $GM$ and $r$. The field at the surface is instead $g_0=\dfrac{GM}{R^2}$, where $R$ is the radius of the planet, which differs from the orbital radius. Therefore the gravitational acceleration at the surface is the only quantity that requires the measurement of $R$.
