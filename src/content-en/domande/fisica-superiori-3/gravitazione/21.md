A person of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on a scale without moving relative to the vehicle. Relative to the planet, the vehicle accelerates vertically upwards at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the scale exerts an upward force of $360\,\mathrm N$. What is the magnitude of the local gravitational field?

- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The scale pushes upwards on the person and gravity pulls downwards. Their net force must produce the vehicle’s acceleration because the person stays on the scale. The reading per unit mass therefore includes the local field and the acceleration. Subtract the acceleration to find the field.

$$
g=\frac{N}{m}-a=\frac{360}{60}-2=4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
