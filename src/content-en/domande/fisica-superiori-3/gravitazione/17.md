A $200\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass alone produces a force of $2.0\,\mu\mathrm N$ on a test mass. A second source is added on the same line and side, at twice the distance from the test mass. The total force rises to $2.5\,\mu\mathrm N$. What is the second mass?

- $200\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $50\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $100\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $400\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The second force points in the same direction and adds half a micronewton, a quarter of the first force. Since the source is twice as far away, its force denominator is already four times larger. The two sources must therefore have equal mass to give that ratio.

$$
\frac{F_2}{F_1}=\frac{2.5-2.0}{2.0}=\frac14=\frac{m_2}{200}\cdot\frac14.
$$
