In $3\,\mathrm s$ a drone travels $9\,\mathrm m$ east and then $12\,\mathrm m$ south. By how much does its mean speed along the route exceed the magnitude of its mean velocity?

- $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Mean speed uses all the metres travelled, while the magnitude of mean velocity uses the segment from start to finish. The two perpendicular legs total twenty-one metres. The direct segment is fifteen metres by Pythagoras. Divide each length by three and take the difference.

$$
\frac{9+12}{3}-\frac{\sqrt{9^2+12^2}}{3}=(7-5)\,\mathrm{m/s}=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
