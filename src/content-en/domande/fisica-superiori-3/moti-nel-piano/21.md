The nonzero velocity of an object is tangent to its smooth path. When is its nonzero acceleration also tangent to the path?

- When the normal component vanishes.
- Whenever the curve is not a straight line.
- When speed is constant on a circle.
- Whenever velocity is nonzero.

## Explanation

Tangential acceleration describes changing speed. If direction also changes, a normal component tilts acceleration away from the tangent. For example, motion speeding up on a straight line accelerates along that line. On a circular curve travelled at constant speed the tangential component is zero and acceleration is entirely normal.
