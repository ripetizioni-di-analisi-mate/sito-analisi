An object travels around a first bend of radius $12\,\mathrm m$ at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The second bend has radius $3\,\mathrm m$. What is the highest speed on the second bend that does not exceed the first centripetal acceleration?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The maximum occurs when the two centripetal accelerations agree. The first bend thus sets the limit. The second radius is one quarter as large, so the squared speed must be one quarter as large. Taking the root gives half the speed on the first curve.

$$
v_{2,\max}=6\sqrt{\frac3{12}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
