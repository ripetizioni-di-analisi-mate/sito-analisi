A point moves uniformly around a circle of radius $2\,\mathrm{m}$ at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of its average acceleration over a quarter turn?

- $\dfrac{16\sqrt2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{8\sqrt2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{16}{\pi}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The velocities at the ends of a quarter turn are perpendicular and each has magnitude $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Their difference has magnitude $4\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since $\omega=2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, the interval takes $\dfrac\pi4\,\mathrm{s}$. The result is $\dfrac{16\sqrt2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, lower than the instantaneous magnitude of $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
