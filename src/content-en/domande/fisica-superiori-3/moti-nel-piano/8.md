In circular motion the tangential acceleration has magnitude $a_t=5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the centripetal acceleration has magnitude $a_c=6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Which value best approximates the magnitude of the resultant acceleration?

- $11\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $7.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The tangent is perpendicular to the radius. Combine the component magnitudes using the Pythagorean theorem. The magnitude squared is $5^2+6^2=61$ in the corresponding units. The square root is about $7.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. A magnitude of eleven would require both contributions to point along the same line and in the same direction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\sqrt{a_t^2+a_c^2}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 7.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
