Two points of a rotor turning with constant angular velocity $\omega$ are at distances $r$ and $\dfrac{r}{2}$ from the axis. How does the tangential speed of the second point compare with that of the first?

- It is twice as large
- It is half as large
- It is a quarter as large
- It is the same

## Explanation

All points of a rigid rotor turn through the same angle in the same time, so they have the same angular velocity $\omega$. The tangential speed of a point at distance $r$ from the axis is $v_t=r\omega$, so at fixed $\omega$ it is proportional to the radius. It follows that the point at distance $\dfrac r2$ has speed $\dfrac r2\,\omega=\dfrac{v_t}{2}$, that is half that of the first point. The same result can be read from the paths, because in one period the second point travels along a circle half as long.
