A disk rotates uniformly anticlockwise. Two pictures taken $0.30\,\mathrm{s}$ apart show a mark first to the right of the centre and then at the diametrically opposite point. Between the two pictures the mark passes through its initial position exactly once. What is the angular speed, rounded to two decimal places?

- $31.42\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $10.47\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $20.94\,\mathrm{rad/s}$
- $52.36\,\mathrm{rad/s}$

## Explanation

The mark passes once through its initial position, so after the first picture the disk completes one full revolution. To reach the opposite point the mark then covers another half turn. The total angle is therefore $3\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$, and the angular speed is $\omega=\dfrac{3\pi}{0.30}\,\mathrm{rad/s}=10\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}\approx31.42\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The two pictures alone would determine the angle covered only up to a whole number of turns.
