A point is at the top of a circle of radius $9\,\mathrm{m}$ and moves around it clockwise. With $x$ to the right and $y$ upwards, its acceleration is $\vec a=(-3;-4)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What are the tangential component, taken along the direction of the velocity, and the speed?

- $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $+3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

At the top of the circle the clockwise motion points to the right, so the tangential component along the velocity is $a_x=-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The centre lies below, so the centripetal component has magnitude $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. From $a_c=\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ the speed is $v=\sqrt{4\cdot9}\,\mathrm{m/s}=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The tangential component is negative, so the point is slowing down.
