Why does $R=\dfrac{v_0^2\sin(2\theta)}{g}$ not directly apply when landing is below launch level?

- Horizontal velocity becomes zero.
- The launch angle must become a right angle.
- Weight reverses during motion.
- Impact time is not the time of return to launch height.

## Explanation

The formula uses $t_f=\dfrac{2v_{0y}}g$, which solves $y(t)=y_0$. With a different landing height, solve the vertical equation for that height. When the ground is lower, the projectile returns to launch height first and then continues downwards. During that additional time it keeps the same horizontal velocity and covers more horizontal distance.
