For accelerated motion, when does the mean of the initial and final velocities always give the average velocity?

- When initial velocity is positive.
- When final time exceeds one second.
- When acceleration is constant throughout the interval.
- When the object does not cross the origin.

## Explanation

At constant acceleration, the velocity–time graph is straight. Displacement per unit time is the mean height of the trapezium under the line. With a linear velocity law, both endpoint values have equal weight in the area. If velocity follows segments of different slopes, the endpoint readings alone generally do not determine the area.
