A ball is launched straight upwards at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting air resistance and using $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what maximum height does it gain?

- $22.5\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $11.25\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $15\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $7.5\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

At maximum height the vertical velocity passes through zero at one instant. Use the time-free equation with acceleration $-g$, giving $0=v_0^2-2gH$. The rise is $\dfrac{15^2}{2\cdot10}\,\mathrm{m}=11.25\,\mathrm{m}$. It is measured relative to launch regardless of the starting height above the ground.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H&=\frac{v_0^2}{2g}=\frac{15^2}{2\cdot10}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 11.25\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
