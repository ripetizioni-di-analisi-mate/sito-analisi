If initial speed doubles at constant deceleration magnitude, how does braking distance change, excluding reaction?

- It doubles.
- It is unchanged.
- It halves.
- It quadruples.

## Explanation

When stopping, $s=\dfrac{v_0^2}{2d}$. At fixed $d$, distance depends on the square of initial speed, so the factor is four. On the velocity–time graph, the triangle's height at the start doubles. Since the loss per second stays the same, the braking time doubles as well, making the area four times as large.
