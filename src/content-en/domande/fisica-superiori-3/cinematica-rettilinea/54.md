An object starts at rest with constant acceleration $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How far does it travel only between $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $1\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $5\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $9\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $4\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

The position formula from the start applies at both instants. The distance between them is nine minus four metres. Using one second in the from-rest formula would lose the velocity already reached at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$. The endpoint velocities, $4$ and $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, provide a check. Their average over one second gives $5\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\tfrac12\cdot2(3^2-2^2)\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
