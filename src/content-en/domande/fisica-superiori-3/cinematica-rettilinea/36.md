A body moving along a straight line has velocity $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$, $11\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$ and $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=6\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its average acceleration between the first and the last reading?

- $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The average acceleration over an interval is the change in velocity divided by the duration of the interval, so it depends only on the first and the last reading. Between $t=2\,\mathrm s$ and $t=6\,\mathrm s$ the velocity goes from $5$ to $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$, therefore
$$
a_m=\frac{9-5}{6-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The reading at $t=5\,\mathrm s$ gives the average accelerations of the two stages, which are $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ over $3\,\mathrm s$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ over $1\,\mathrm s$. The value $0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ comes from the arithmetic mean of these two numbers, which ignores the different durations of the stages. Weighting each acceleration by the duration of its stage gives again $\dfrac{2\cdot3-2\cdot1}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
