A vehicle travelling at $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ continues for $1\,\mathrm s$ during reaction, then brakes at constant deceleration magnitude $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its total stopping distance?

::grafico{segmenti="0,18,1,18;1,18,7,0" punti="1,18,pieno;7,0,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=7.5 ymin=0 ymax=20 nomi="t,v" titolo="Total distance includes a rectangle and a triangle"}

- $72\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $54\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $18\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $126\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

During reaction the speed is steady and covers eighteen metres. During braking it falls from eighteen to zero in six seconds: the velocity–time triangle has area fifty-four metres. The two areas add because velocity remains along the initial direction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=v_0t_r+\frac{v_0^2}{2d}=18+\frac{18^2}{2\cdot3}\\[4pt]
&= 72\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
