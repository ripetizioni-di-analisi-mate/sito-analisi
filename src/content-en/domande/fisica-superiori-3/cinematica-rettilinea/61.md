In a stopping model, two trolleys hold their initial speeds for $1\,\mathrm{s}$ and then brake with acceleration magnitude $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Their initial speeds are $10$ and $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ respectively. What is the ratio of the second total stopping distance to the first?

- $\dfrac{24}{7}$
- $2$
- $3$
- $4$

## Explanation

The constant-speed legs cover $10$ and $20\,\mathrm{m}$. The braking distances are $\dfrac{10^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m}=25\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\dfrac{20^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m}=100\,\mathrm{m}$. The ratio is $\dfrac{120}{35}=\dfrac{24}{7}$. The factor of four applies to braking alone, while the initial legs double.
