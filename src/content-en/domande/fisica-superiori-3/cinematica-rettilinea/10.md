In an object's velocity–time graph, a straight line begins at $+4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=0$, crosses the axis at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$ and reaches $-6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$. What distance does the object travel in the first $5\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $13\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $25\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The two triangular areas represent travel in opposite directions. The first gives $\dfrac{2\cdot4}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=4\,\mathrm{m}$. The second has magnitude $\dfrac{3\cdot6}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=9\,\mathrm{m}$. Adding the magnitudes gives the distance, $13\,\mathrm{m}$. Subtracting one area would instead give the signed displacement.
