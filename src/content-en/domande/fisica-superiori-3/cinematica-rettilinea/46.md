A particle starts at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and accelerates at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ over a displacement of $4\,\mathrm{m}$. What is the final velocity if it stays in the positive direction?

- $-5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

## Explanation

Displacement is known, so $v^2=v_0^2+2a\Delta x$ avoids finding time. Use the positive square root, as required by the direction of motion. The calculation gives $v^2=(9+16)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}=25\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ and thus $v=+5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The positive sign also agrees with positive initial velocity and acceleration in the same direction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=+\sqrt{3^2+2\cdot2\cdot4}\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
