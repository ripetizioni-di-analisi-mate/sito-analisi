Two cars begin braking at the same speed with constant decelerations. The first has twice the deceleration magnitude of the second. How do their braking times and distances compare?

- Both are halved for the first car.
- Only time is halved for the first car.
- Only distance is halved for the first car.
- Distance is quartered and time doubled for the first car.

## Explanation

At fixed initial speed, both $\dfrac{v_0}{d}$ and $\dfrac{v_0^2}{2d}$ are inversely proportional to the magnitude of deceleration. With twice the deceleration, the first car loses the same speed in half the time. The graphs have the same initial heights, so halving the base halves the area.
