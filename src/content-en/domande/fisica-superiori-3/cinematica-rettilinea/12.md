An object with constant acceleration is photographed at $t=0$, $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$. Its coordinates are $1\,\mathrm{m}$, $9\,\mathrm{m}$ and $29\,\mathrm{m}$, respectively. What is its signed acceleration along the axis?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The displacements over the two-second intervals are $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $20\,\mathrm{m}$. The average velocities are $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With constant acceleration they equal the velocities at the midpoint times, $1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $3\,\mathrm{s}$. The acceleration is therefore $\dfrac{10-4}{3-1}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
