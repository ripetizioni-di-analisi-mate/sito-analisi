The origin is shifted five metres in the positive direction. How does a particle's constant-velocity equation change?

- Every coordinate increases by five metres and the slope stays the same.
- Every coordinate falls by five metres and the slope stays the same.
- The slope decreases by five metres per second.
- The new equation always has zero intercept.

## Explanation

The coordinate measures signed distance from the new origin. Subtracting the same constant from every value leaves their differences, and therefore velocity, unchanged.
