A vehicle is travelling at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ when the driver sees an obstacle $20\,\mathrm{m}$ away. Its speed remains constant for a reaction time of $1\,\mathrm{s}$. What minimum magnitude of constant braking acceleration is needed to stop by the obstacle?

- $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

The vehicle covers $10\cdot1=10\,\mathrm{m}$ during the reaction, leaving $10\,\mathrm{m}$ to brake. At the limiting value the vehicle reaches the obstacle with zero speed. The equation without time uses acceleration $-d$, so $0=10^2-2d\cdot10$. This gives $d=5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Weaker braking would require more distance.
