With fixed reaction time, which part of stopping distance increases linearly with initial speed?

- Braking distance at fixed deceleration.
- Both parts.
- Distance travelled before braking.
- Neither part.

## Explanation

During reaction the vehicle proceeds steadily, so $s_r=v_0t_r$. Braking distance grows as the square of initial speed. Doubling speed doubles the height of the reaction rectangle while leaving its base unchanged. At fixed deceleration, braking increases both the height and the base of its triangle.
