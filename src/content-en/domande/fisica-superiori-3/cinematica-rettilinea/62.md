A car at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes without delay and stops in $50\,\mathrm m$. What is the magnitude of the constant deceleration?

- $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $\frac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

## Explanation

The time-free equation relates zero final velocity to the drop in $v^2$. Divide the square of the initial speed by twice the distance to obtain the magnitude of deceleration. The result implies five seconds of braking at mean speed $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving $10\cdot5=50\,\mathrm m$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{20^2}{2\cdot50}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
