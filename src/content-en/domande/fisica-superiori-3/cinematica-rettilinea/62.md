A trolley travels at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for an unknown delay, then brakes with constant acceleration of magnitude $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. It covers $42\,\mathrm{m}$ from the start of the delay until it stops. How long is the delay?

- $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $3.5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

Braking from $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to zero takes $\dfrac{12^2}{2\cdot3}\,\mathrm{m}=24\,\mathrm{m}$. The remaining $18\,\mathrm{m}$ are travelled at the initial speed. Divide by $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to obtain a delay of $1.5\,\mathrm{s}$. Dividing speed by the deceleration would give the four seconds of braking.
