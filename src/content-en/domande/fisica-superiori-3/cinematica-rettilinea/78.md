A stone is dropped and reaches the ground at a speed of $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. What was its initial height above the ground?

- $10\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $40\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $200\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

Time is unknown, but the link between squared speed and displacement gives the height. Take downwards as positive, so both $g$ and $h$ enter positively. With zero initial velocity this gives $h=\dfrac{20^2}{2\cdot10}\,\mathrm{m}=20\,\mathrm{m}$. The result is positive because it represents a vertical distance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=\frac{v_f^2}{2g}=\frac{20^2}{2\cdot10}\\[4pt]
&= 20\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
