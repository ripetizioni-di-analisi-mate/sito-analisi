Velocity increases from $5$ to $11\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How long does the increase take?

- $2\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $\frac{11}{3}\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $6\,\mathrm{s}$.
- $3\,\mathrm{s}$.

## Explanation

Acceleration means each second adds three metres per second. An increase of six takes two one-second intervals. The ratio $\dfrac{11-5}{3}$ is $2\,\mathrm{s}$. Multiply two seconds by the acceleration and add the initial velocity to recover $11\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{11-5}{3}\\[4pt]
&= 2\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$
