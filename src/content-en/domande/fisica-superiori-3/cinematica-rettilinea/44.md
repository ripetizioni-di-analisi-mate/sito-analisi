In a constant-acceleration stage, velocity rises from $2$ to $14\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over $6\,\mathrm s$. What distance is covered?

- $84\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $36\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $96\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $48\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

The velocity–time graph is a straight line above the axis. Its trapezium area uses the mean of the two endpoint velocities as its average height. This is $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and covers forty-eight metres in six seconds.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\frac{v_0+v_f}{2}t=\frac{2+14}{2}\cdot6\\[4pt]
&= 48\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
