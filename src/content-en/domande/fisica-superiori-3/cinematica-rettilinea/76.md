Two objects are in free flight under the same uniform gravity. One has just been released and the other is already moving downwards. How does the difference between their velocities change while both remain in flight?

- It instantly becomes zero.
- It grows by $g$ every second.
- It always falls to zero.
- It remains constant.

## Explanation

Over the same interval both gain the same signed change in velocity. Subtracting the velocities cancels that change. The velocity difference that already existed at the second release therefore stays the same. The positions meanwhile separate at a constant relative velocity because that difference is nonzero.
