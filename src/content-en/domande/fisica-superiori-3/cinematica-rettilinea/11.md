A cyclist rides $120\,\mathrm{m}$ in a straight line at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and returns to her starting point along the same route without stopping. If her average speed over the full trip is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is her constant speed on the return leg?

- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The total distance, $240\,\mathrm{m}$, divided by the average speed gives $60\,\mathrm{s}$. The first leg takes $\dfrac{120}{6}\,\mathrm{s}=20\,\mathrm{s}$, leaving $40\,\mathrm{s}$ for the return. Dividing the $120\,\mathrm{m}$ return distance by that time gives $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The total displacement is zero.
