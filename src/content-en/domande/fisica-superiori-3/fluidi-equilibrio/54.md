A straight tube $2\,\mathrm m$ long, filled with still water, is inclined to the horizontal. The pressure at end $B$ exceeds the pressure at end $A$ by $5\,\mathrm{kPa}$. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the sine of the angle between the tube and the horizontal?

- $0.50$
- $0.25$
- $0.97$
- $0.025$

## Explanation

By Stevin's law the pressure difference between two points of a liquid at rest depends only on their height difference, $\Delta p=\rho g\,\Delta h$. Therefore end $B$ lies below $A$ by
$$
\Delta h=\frac{\Delta p}{\rho g}=\frac{5000}{1000\cdot10}\,\mathrm m=0.50\,\mathrm m.
$$
The tube is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose vertical leg is the height difference, so $\sin\alpha=\dfrac{0.50}{2}=0.25$. The value $0.50$ comes from taking the height difference as the sine without dividing it by the length of the tube, and $0.97$ is the cosine of the same angle.
