A barge in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ stays level as it is loaded. Above its initial waterline, its hull has a cross-sectional area of $2\,\mathrm{m^2}$ in the first $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $3\,\mathrm{m^2}$ in the next $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. How much mass must be added for the waterline on the hull to rise by the full $5\,\mathrm{cm}$?

- $100\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $150\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $130\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $90\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Cross-sectional area changes with height, so add the volumes of the two newly submerged portions. The lower one adds forty litres and the upper one ninety. The increase in supported mass equals the additional mass of displaced water. Add the two contributions before multiplying by density.

$$
\Delta m=\rho(2\cdot0.02+3\cdot0.03)\,\mathrm{m^3}=130\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
