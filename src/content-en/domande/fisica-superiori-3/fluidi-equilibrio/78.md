Which practical loss makes a hydraulic press output less work than it receives?

- A large area in the ideal model
- Piston friction
- Using the same liquid on both sides
- Choosing joules as the unit

## Explanation

When a piston slides, friction opposes the motion. Part of the input work goes into overcoming that force and may heat the device instead of moving the load. Area ratio changes force and travel even in an ideal press, while friction reduces useful output energy.
