A floating body has two sections each $0.20\,\mathrm m$ high. The lower section has horizontal area $0.10\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and the upper section $0.20\,\mathrm{m^2}$. In water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, the bottom is $0.30\,\mathrm m$ below the free surface. What is the body’s mean density, rounded to the nearest kilogram per cubic metre?

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-.65,-.3 .65,-.3 .65,-.1 .92,-.1 .92,.1 -.92,.1 -.92,-.1 -.65,-.1" linee="-1.8,0 -.92,0;.92,0 1.8,0" segmenti="-.92,0,.92,0" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-.5 ymax=.34 titolo="The waterline crosses the upper section of the stepped body."}

- $667\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $750\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $333\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $500\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

The whole lower section and half of the upper section are submerged. The submerged volume is $(0.20\cdot0.10+0.10\cdot0.20)\,\mathrm{m^3}=0.04\,\mathrm{m^3}$ out of $0.06\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Weight balances buoyancy, giving $\rho_c=1000\cdot\dfrac{0.04}{0.06}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\simeq667\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Using the fraction of height would give the wrong density because the cross-section changes.
