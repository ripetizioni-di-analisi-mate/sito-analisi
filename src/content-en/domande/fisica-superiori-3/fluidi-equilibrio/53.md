An open tank with vertical walls is split by a thin partition into two compartments with the same base area. In the two compartments the water reaches $0.80\,\mathrm m$ and $0.20\,\mathrm m$ above the bottom. The partition is removed and the water is left to come back to rest. By how much does the pressure decrease at a point on the bottom of the compartment that had the higher level? Use $\rho g=10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$.

- $3\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $5\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

Once the partition is removed, the water forms a single connected liquid, and at rest its free surface settles at the same height in both compartments. The volume of water is conserved and the two base areas are equal, so the final level is the mean of the two initial levels, $h=\dfrac{0.80+0.20}{2}\,\mathrm m=0.50\,\mathrm m$. By Stevin's law the pressure on the bottom depends only on the height of the water above it, therefore in the compartment that had the higher level it decreases by
$$
\rho g\,\Delta h=10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}\cdot0.30\,\mathrm m=3\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$
The value $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ corresponds to a level dropping all the way to $0.20\,\mathrm m$, while $5\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $8\,\mathrm{kPa}$ are the gauge pressures on the bottom in the final and in the initial state.
