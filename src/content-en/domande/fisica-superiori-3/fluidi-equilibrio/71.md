The gas taps are above the manometer liquid surfaces. Which assumption lets the height difference be found using only the liquid's density?

- The liquid is in turbulent motion
- Gravity differs between the two arms
- The gas-column weights are negligible or already accounted for
- Both gases must always be atmospheric

## Explanation

The height difference balances the pressure difference at the surfaces of the manometer liquid. If the gas taps are at other heights, pressure may vary along each gas column. A calculation using only the liquid density assumes these changes are negligible or already included in the values used.
