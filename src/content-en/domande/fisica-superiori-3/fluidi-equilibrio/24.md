One liquid has three times the density of another. At what depth in the second liquid is the gauge pressure the same as at $2\,\mathrm m$ in the first?

- $12\,\mathrm m$
- $3\,\mathrm m$
- $-6\,\mathrm m$
- $6\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

For the same gravity, equal excess pressures mean equal products of density and depth. If the first fluid has density $3\rho$, its product is $3\rho\cdot2\,\mathrm m$. In the second fluid of density $\rho$, the depth must be $6\,\mathrm m$. Greater depth compensates for the lower density.
