One liquid has three times the density of another. At what depth in the second liquid is the gauge pressure the same as at $2\,\mathrm m$ in the first?

- $12\,\mathrm m$
- $3\,\mathrm m$
- $-6\,\mathrm m$
- $6\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The same gauge pressure requires the same density–depth product. Depth is inversely related to density for this comparison.

$$
h_2=\frac{\rho_1h_1}{\rho_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}h_2&=3 \cdot 2\\[4pt]&=6\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$
