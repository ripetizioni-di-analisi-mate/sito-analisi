A pure ice cube floats in a glass of fresh water. Ignore evaporation, changes in water density, buoyancy from the air and surface tension. After the ice has fully melted, how does the water level compare with its initial level?

- It rises because some ice was originally above the water
- It falls because liquid water is denser than ice
- It depends on how much of the cube was above the surface
- It stays at the same height

## Explanation

While floating, the ice displaces a mass of water equal to its own mass. Melting adds exactly that mass of water. At the same density, the meltwater occupies the same volume that the cube excluded below the surface. The occupied volume below the surface therefore stays constant.
