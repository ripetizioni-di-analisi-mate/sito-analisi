A spring scale reads $40\,\mathrm N$ for a block suspended in air and $30\,\mathrm N$ when it is fully submerged in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Neglecting air buoyancy, what is the block’s mean density?

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-.7,-.65 .7,-.65 .7,.65 -.7,.65" linee="-2,1 2,1;0,.65 0,2.45" vettori="-.35,0,-.35,.5,F_A;0,0,0,-2,P;.33,0,.33,1.5,T" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-2.5 ymax=2.8 titolo="Force diagram for the measurement in water."}

- $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $750\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $3000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $4000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

## Explanation

The reading falls by $40-30=10\,\mathrm N$, the weight of the displaced water. The block weighs four times as much. Because it is fully submerged, the block and the displaced water have the same volume. Their weight ratio is their density ratio, so the block has $\rho_c=4000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.
