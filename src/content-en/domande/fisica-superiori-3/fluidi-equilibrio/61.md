Two points in the same connected homogeneous liquid at rest are at the same height. What follows?

- Their pressures agree
- They differ if the vessel cross sections differ
- The nearer wall has higher pressure
- Both have zero pressure

## Explanation

At equal height, a path that descends and rises through the same liquid has zero net change in height. Hydrostatic contributions cancel even if the connection runs along the bottom. This reasoning requires the liquid to be stationary, homogeneous and continuous along the path.
