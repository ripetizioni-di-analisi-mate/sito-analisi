In a closed tank, the absolute pressure of the gas above the oil is held at $130\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The tank bottom can withstand at most $160\,\mathrm{kPa}$ absolute. The oil is at rest, its density is $900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the maximum depth of the bottom below the oil surface, rounded to the nearest centimetre.

- $1.67\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.33\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4.44\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The gas already accounts for most of the pressure limit. The oil column can add only the remaining thirty kilopascals. Convert that difference to pascals, then divide by the pressure increase per metre. A deeper oil level would exceed the limit.

$$
h_{\max}=\frac{(160-130)\cdot1000}{900\cdot10}\,\mathrm m=\frac{10}{3}\,\mathrm m\simeq3.33\,\mathrm m.
$$
