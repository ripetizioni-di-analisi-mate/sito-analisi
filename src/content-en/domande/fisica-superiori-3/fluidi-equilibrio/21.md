The graph shows the pressure increase $\Delta p$ as a function of depth $h$ in two superposed liquid layers. At point I, at the interface, the increase is $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at $0.50\,\mathrm m$. At point B, at the bottom, it is $14\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at $1.50\,\mathrm m$. At what depth is the pressure increase $9\,\mathrm{kPa}$?

::grafico{linee="0,0 .5,4 1.5,14" punti=".5,4,pieno,I;1.5,14,pieno,B" xmin=0 xmax=1.8 ymin=0 ymax=16 nomi="h (m),Δp (kPa)" titolo="I marks the interface and B the bottom."}

- $1.00\,\mathrm m$
- $0.50\,\mathrm m$
- $0.90\,\mathrm m$
- $1.125\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The value $9\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is larger than the value at the interface, so the required depth lies in the lower layer. In that layer the pressure increases by $\dfrac{(14-4)\,\mathrm{kPa}}{(1.50-0.50)\,\mathrm m}=10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$. Adding $5\,\mathrm{kPa}$ to the $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the interface therefore requires another $0.50\,\mathrm m$ below the interface. The required depth is thus $1.00\,\mathrm m$ below the free surface.
