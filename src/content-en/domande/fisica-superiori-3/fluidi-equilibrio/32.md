A hydraulic press contains incompressible oil. The smaller piston of area $0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$ moves down by $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. The larger piston of area $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$ rises only $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ because some oil leaks out. Both chambers stay full. How many litres of oil are lost during the stroke?

::grafico{assi=0 linee="0,1.6 0,0 4,0 4,1.6;.6,1.6 .6,.45 1.4,.45 1.4,1.6;0,1.2 .6,1.2;1.4,1.2 4,1.2" vettori=".3,1.85,.3,1.35,s₁;2.6,1.4,2.6,1.504167,s₂" xmin=-.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-.3 ymax=2.1 titolo="The arrows show the two measured displacements."}

- $1.2\,\mathrm L$
- $1.0\,\mathrm L$
- $2.2\,\mathrm L$
- $0.20\,\mathrm L$

## Explanation

The smaller piston displaces $0.010\cdot0.12\,\mathrm{m^3}=1.2\,\mathrm L$. The larger one receives only $0.040\cdot0.025\,\mathrm{m^3}=1.0\,\mathrm L$. Since the oil is incompressible and both chambers remain full, whatever does not reach the large piston leaves through the leak. The difference is $0.20\,\mathrm L$.
