An open vessel contains oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ above water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The total liquid depth is $1.50\,\mathrm m$ and the gauge pressure at the bottom is $13800\,\mathrm{Pa}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, how thick is the oil layer?

- $0.12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.38\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

An all-water column would give $15000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Replacing one metre of water with one metre of oil reduces the reading by $(1000-800)\cdot10\,\mathrm{Pa}=2000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The observed reduction is $1200\,\mathrm{Pa}$, so the oil thickness is $\dfrac{1200}{2000}\,\mathrm m=0.60\,\mathrm m$. The remaining $0.90\,\mathrm m$ is water.
