The same body hangs from a spring scale, first fully submerged in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and then fully submerged in oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The readings are $20.0\,\mathrm N$ and $22.4\,\mathrm N$. What is its weight in air, neglecting air buoyancy?

- $32\,\mathrm N$
- $24.8\,\mathrm N$
- $22.4\,\mathrm N$
- $12\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

Oil buoyancy is eighty per cent of water buoyancy at the same displaced volume. The difference between readings, $2.4\,\mathrm N$, is therefore twenty per cent of the buoyant force in water. That force is $12\,\mathrm N$. Adding it to the $20.0\,\mathrm N$ reading gives a weight of $32\,\mathrm N$.
