A $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ float is partly above the surface and held by a vertical line anchored to the bottom. The line tension is $3\,\mathrm N$ and the float is stationary. In water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and negligible air buoyancy, what is the submerged volume?

- $0.0003\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $0.0009\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $0.0015\,\mathrm{m^3}$
- $0.0006\,\mathrm{m^3}$

## Explanation

The line pulls towards the bottom, in the same direction as weight. At equilibrium the buoyant force must be $(6+3)\,\mathrm N=9\,\mathrm N$. Submerged volume is $\dfrac{9}{1000\cdot10}\,\mathrm{m^3}=0.0009\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The float displaces extra water because the line adds a downward force.
