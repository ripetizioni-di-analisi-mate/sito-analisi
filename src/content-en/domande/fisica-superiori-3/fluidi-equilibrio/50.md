In a water U-tube manometer the right level is $0.20\,\mathrm m$ higher than the left. The pressure on the right branch is raised by $3.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ while the left branch pressure is held fixed. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the new signed height difference $h_D-h_S$, right minus left?

::grafico{assi=0 linee="0,.7 0,0 1,0 1,.7;.2,.7 .2,.2 .8,.2 .8,.7;0,.3 .2,.3;.8,.5 1,.5" segmenti=".2,.3,.8,.3" vettori=".1,.65,.1,.34,p_S;.9,.65,.9,.52,p_D" xmin=-.3 xmax=1.4 ymin=-.1 ymax=.88 titolo="Initial liquid levels in the two branches."}

- $+0.10\,\mathrm m$
- $-0.10\,\mathrm m$
- $+0.50\,\mathrm m$
- $-0.50\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

Initially $p_S-p_D=\rho g(h_D-h_S)=2000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Raising the right pressure by $3000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ makes this difference $-1000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Dividing by $10000\,\mathrm{Pa/m}$ gives $h_D-h_S=-0.10\,\mathrm m$. The sign places the right level lower, consistent with higher pressure on that arm.
