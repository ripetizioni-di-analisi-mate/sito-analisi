Why does the equal-pressure rule need correction for pistons at different heights?

- The liquid volume doubles
- There is a hydrostatic pressure difference
- Density must be negative
- The smaller piston must always be higher

## Explanation

The lower point in the liquid has internal pressure higher by $\rho g\Delta z$ than the upper point. This term accounts for the weight of the liquid column between the pistons. Before converting pressures to loads, include this difference and subtract each piston's external pressure.
