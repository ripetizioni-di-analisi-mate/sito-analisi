Why cannot a measured flow rate always be predicted by Poiseuille's law as diameter grows at fixed pressure?

- The regime can cease to be laminar
- Inlet pressure always vanishes
- A wider tube always prevents flow
- Mass ceases to be conserved

## Explanation

Enlarging the pipe can change Reynolds number. The fourth-power law assumes laminar flow, which must be checked.
