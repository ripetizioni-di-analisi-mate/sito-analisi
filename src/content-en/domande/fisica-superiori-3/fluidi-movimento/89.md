Why cannot a measured flow rate always be predicted by Poiseuille's law as diameter grows at a fixed pressure difference?

- The regime can cease to be laminar
- Inlet pressure always vanishes
- A wider tube always prevents flow
- Mass ceases to be conserved

## Explanation

At a fixed pressure drop, a larger diameter can increase both mean speed and Reynolds number. The fourth-power prediction holds in the laminar regime specified by Poiseuille. As flow becomes turbulent the pressure drop–flow rate relationship changes, so the model must be reassessed.
