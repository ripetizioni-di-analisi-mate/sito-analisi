What speed at the higher pressure tap is consistent with these measurements? Ideal water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ flows steadily along a streamline without pumps. At point 1, $p_1=135\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $v_1=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At point 2, which is $0.95\,\mathrm m$ higher, the pressure is $p_2=139\,\mathrm{kPa}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, express the answer in metres per second.

- $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Both pressure and height increased downstream. The kinetic term must decrease by the sum of the other two increases. The rise uses $9.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and pressure uses $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The kinetic term falls from eighteen kilopascals to four and a half. Taking the positive square root gives the required speed.

$$
v_2=\sqrt{\frac{2(18-9.5-4)\cdot1000}{1000}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
