What is the usual effect of dissipation on pressure recovery downstream of a throat?

- They reduce recovery compared with the ideal case
- They always increase recovery
- They leave the energy balance unaffected
- They necessarily stop all flow

## Explanation

Beyond the throat, slowing tends to recover static pressure. Dissipation reduces the mechanical energy available for recovery. Even if the pipe returns to the original area and height, restoring the speed, pressure can stay below the value measured before the contraction.
