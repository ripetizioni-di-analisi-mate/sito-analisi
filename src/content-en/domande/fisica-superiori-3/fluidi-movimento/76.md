Why can “more speed means less pressure” fail when comparing two different elevations?

- Density is always negative
- The equation requires zero difference
- A higher point always has zero speed
- The gravity term may offset the kinetic change

## Explanation

Increasing speed raises the kinetic term. If the fluid also descends, the gravitational term drops and can offset that change. With a large enough descent, both speed and pressure rise. Comparing the pressures thus requires the balance of those two terms.
