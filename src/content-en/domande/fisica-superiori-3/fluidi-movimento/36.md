In a large pressurised tank the difference between surface and outlet pressures is the same in two measurements. At a depth of $0.40\,\mathrm m$ the measured speed is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and at a depth of $1.40\,\mathrm m$ it is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Ignoring losses and using the same liquid, what value of $g$ follows?

- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Pressure contributes the same amount in both measurements. Write the squared speed for each depth and subtract to remove that term. The difference of the squared speeds equals twice gravity times the depth difference. This gives $g$ without a separate measurement of gauge pressure.

$$
g=\frac{6^2-4^2}{2(1.40-0.40)}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
