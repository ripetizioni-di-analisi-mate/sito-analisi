A Newtonian liquid undergoes Poiseuille flow through a horizontal pipe of constant radius. Three taps mark two sections in series, $2\,\mathrm m$ and $4\,\mathrm m$ long. The measured pressure drop from the first tap to the last is $12\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the drop across the second section?

- $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $12\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The flow rate is the same through both sections. With the same viscosity and radius, Poiseuille's law gives a constant pressure drop per metre. The second section makes up four of the six metres, so it accounts for two thirds of the total drop. Equally spaced taps would give equal drops.

$$
\Delta p_2=12\,\mathrm{kPa}\cdot\frac{4}{2+4}=8\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$
