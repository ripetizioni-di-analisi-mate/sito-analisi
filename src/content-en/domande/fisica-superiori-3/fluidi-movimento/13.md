A large closed tank discharges water into the air through a small hole $0.30\,\mathrm m$ below the free surface. The jet emerges at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Here $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, the level is nearly constant and losses are negligible. By how many pascals does free-surface pressure exceed atmospheric pressure?

- $9500\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $12500\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $15500\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3000\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The jet's kinetic term requires $12500\,\mathrm{Pa}$ of energy per unit volume. The depth already supplies $3000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The gas above the free surface must supply the difference. Opening the tank at the same depth would bring the ideal speed down to $\sqrt6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}p_{\mathrm s}-p_{\mathrm{atm}}&=\frac{\rho v^2}{2}-\rho gh\\&=(12500-3000)\,\mathrm{Pa}=9500\,\mathrm{Pa}.\end{aligned}
$$
