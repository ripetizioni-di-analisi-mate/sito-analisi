A circular pipe with Poiseuille flow is replaced by one of twice the radius. Length, viscosity and flow rate must remain the same, and both pipes stay in the same regime. What fraction of the original pressure drop is needed in the new pipe?

- $\frac1{16}$
- $\frac14$
- $\frac12$
- $16$

## Explanation

::grafico{f="1/x^4" punti="1,1,pieno;2,.0625,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,.0625" etichette="1.74,.22,1/16" xmin=.94 xmax=2.2 ymin=0 ymax=1.45 nomi="r/r₁,Δp/Δp₁" titolo="At the same flow rate, pressure drop varies inversely with radius to the fourth power."}

Keeping the flow rate fixed requires the product of radius to the fourth power and pressure drop to stay fixed. Doubling the radius multiplies the fourth power by sixteen. The required pressure drop must fall by the same factor. At the original pressure drop the new pipe would carry much more liquid per second.

$$
\frac{\Delta p_2}{\Delta p_1}=\left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^4=\frac1{16}.
$$
