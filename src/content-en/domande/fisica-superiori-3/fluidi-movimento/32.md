An ideal horizontal Venturi carries water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. A U-tube manometer connected at the inlet and throat contains a liquid of density $1800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The interfaces differ in height by $0.50\,\mathrm m$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $p_1-p_2=(\rho_m-\rho)g\Delta h$. The inlet speed is $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the throat speed.

- $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Because water occupies the connecting arms above the denser liquid, the reading uses the difference of the two densities. The manometer gives a drop of four kilopascals. In the horizontal section it raises the square of the speed by eight in SI units. Add the square of the inlet speed before taking a square root.

$$
\begin{aligned}p_1-p_2&=(1800-1000)\cdot10\cdot0.50\,\mathrm{Pa}=4000\,\mathrm{Pa},\\ v_2&=\sqrt{1^2+\frac{2\cdot4000}{1000}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$
