Water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ flows steadily through an ideal horizontal contraction with no pumps. The speed at the narrower section is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The upstream speed is smaller but unknown. What is the upper bound on the static pressure drop in this model?

- $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $8\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $16\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The pressure drop balances the gain in kinetic energy per volume. The largest gain occurs in the limit as upstream speed approaches zero. A positive upstream speed subtracts from this upper bound. A reading of nine kilopascals would be inconsistent with the stated data in the ideal model.

$$
\frac12\rho v_2^2=\frac{1000}{2}\cdot4^2\,\mathrm{Pa}=8\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$
