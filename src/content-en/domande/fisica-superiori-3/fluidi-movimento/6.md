Ideal water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ flows steadily along a streamline. Section 1 has $p_1=200000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $v_1=1.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Section 2 is higher by $h=1.1\,\mathrm m$ and has $v_2=2.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With no pumps or losses find $p_2$. Take $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Choose the closest value.

- $3.74818\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.87\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $93704.5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $-1.87409\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The water rises and speeds up over the same section. The rise uses $10791\,\mathrm{Pa}$ of the pressure term and the speed increase uses another $1800\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Both effects have the same sign. Subtract their sum from the two hundred thousand pascals at point 1.

$$
p_2=(200000-10791-1800)\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq1.87\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$
