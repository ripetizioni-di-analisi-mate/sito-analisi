A sphere moves rightward at $0.012\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in an initially stationary liquid. With the same liquid now flowing, the sphere travels rightward at $0.005\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and drag has half its original magnitude but points right. Assume Stokes flow in both cases. Taking rightward as positive, what is the liquid's velocity?

- $-0.001\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.006\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.011\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $0.017\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Drag is proportional to the relative speed. Halving the force lowers that speed to six thousandths of a metre per second. Since drag now points right, the sphere's velocity relative to the fluid points left. The liquid therefore moves rightward faster than the sphere.

$$
v_{\mathrm{liq}}=v_{\mathrm s}-v_{\mathrm{rel}}=[0.005-(-0.006)]\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.011\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
