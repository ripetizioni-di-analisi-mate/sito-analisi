There is Poiseuille flow in a circular pipe of area $0.004\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The centreline speed is $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Using the centreline speed across the entire section gives $8\,\mathrm{L/s}$. By how many litres per second does that estimate exceed the flow rate for the laminar profile?

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-2,1.25 2.25,1.25;-2,-1.25 2.25,-1.25" vettori="-1.53,-.83,-.42,-.83;-1.53,-.42,.24,-.42;-1.53,0,.47,0,vₘₐₓ;-1.53,.42,.24,.42;-1.53,.83,-.42,.83" unicolore=1 xmin=-2.35 xmax=2.5 ymin=-1.58 ymax=1.64 titolo="The velocity profile has its greatest speed on the axis."}

- $2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $12\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

The shorter arrows near the wall show that the centreline reading is the maximum. In the Poiseuille profile the mean speed is half the maximum, so multiplying by area gives four litres per second. Subtracting that from the estimated eight gives the requested overestimate.

$$
\begin{aligned}Q&=(0.004\cdot1)\,\mathrm{m^3/s}=4\,\mathrm{L/s},\\ Q_{\mathrm{stima}}-Q&=(8-4)\,\mathrm{L/s}=4\,\mathrm{L/s}.\end{aligned}
$$
