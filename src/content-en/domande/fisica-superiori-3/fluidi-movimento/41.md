In steady ideal water flow with no pumps, the upstream pressure is $140\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the speed is $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The downstream section on the same streamline has speed $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and its pressure must stay at or above $115\,\mathrm{kPa}$. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the maximum rise in height?

::grafico{assi=0 linee="0,0 2,0 4,1.4 6,1.4" vettori=".28,.3,1.19,.3,v_1;4.21,1.72,6.03,1.72,v_2" segmenti="1.45,0,6.65,0;5.93,1.4,6.65,1.4;6.6,0,6.6,1.4" etichette="6.84,.64,h;1.18,-.41,1;5.04,1.01,2" xmin=-.42 xmax=7.34 ymin=-.8 ymax=2.42 titolo="The downstream section is higher and has a greater mean speed. The arrows are schematic."}

- $1.9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3.1\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The largest allowed pressure drop is $25\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The increase in the kinetic term uses $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$, leaving $19\,\mathrm{kPa}$ for the rise. Dividing that allowance by the specific weight of water gives the maximum height change. A larger rise would take downstream pressure below the minimum.

$$
h_{\max}=\frac{(140-115-6)\,\mathrm{kPa}}{10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}}=1.9\,\mathrm m.
$$
