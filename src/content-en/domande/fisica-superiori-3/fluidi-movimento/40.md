Two separate horizontal pipes carry water in steady ideal flow. In the first pipe, measurements give $120\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In the second pipe, at the same height and with the same pressure reference, the speed is $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What can be concluded about the pressure in the second pipe?

- $112\,\mathrm{kPa}$ because the speed is higher.
- $128\,\mathrm{kPa}$ because the speed is higher.
- $120\,\mathrm{kPa}$ because the heights agree.
- It cannot be found without pressure or energy information for the second flow.

## Explanation

In the first pipe, pressure and speed fix the Bernoulli value along the streamline. The second pipe is independent and may have a different value. Equal heights and a shared pressure reference let us compare two pressure measurements, but a single speed cannot give the missing reading.
