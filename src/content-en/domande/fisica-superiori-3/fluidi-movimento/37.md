A large open tank discharges water through a small hole $0.80\,\mathrm m$ below the free surface. The effective area of the jet is $2.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and negligible losses, what is the flow rate in litres per second?

- $0.80\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $3.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $0.57\,\mathrm{L/s}$
- $8.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$

## Explanation

By Torricelli's law the speed of the jet is $v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{2\cdot10\cdot0.80}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since one square centimetre is $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the effective area is $2.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The flow rate is the product of the effective area and the speed of the jet, so
$$
Q=2.0\cdot10^{-4}\cdot4.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}=8.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}.
$$
One cubic metre holds $1000$ litres, therefore the flow rate is $0.80\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The value $3.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$ comes from using $2gh$ instead of its square root, and $8.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from converting square centimetres with the factor $10^{-3}$.
