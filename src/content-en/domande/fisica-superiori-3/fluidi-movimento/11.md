A gas pipe in steady flow has one leak and no branches. Upstream measurements are $20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and density $1.2\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Downstream measurements give $18\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $1.0\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. How many grams per second escape through the leak?

- $2\,\mathrm{g/s}$
- $18\,\mathrm{g/s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{g/s}$
- $24\,\mathrm{g/s}$

## Explanation

At each section, use the local density to convert volume per second into mass per second. Twenty-four grams per second enter and eighteen continue downstream. In steady flow the missing mass escapes through the leak. The difference between the readings in litres per second does not give the mass loss.

$$
\dot m_{\mathrm f}=[1.2\cdot0.020-1.0\cdot0.018]\,\mathrm{kg/s}=6\,\mathrm{g/s}.
$$
