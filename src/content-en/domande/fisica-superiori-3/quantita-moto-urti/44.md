A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ platform and a $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ block on it are initially at rest on a frictionless surface. The platform launches the block horizontally. The block moves at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the platform. What is the platform's speed in the laboratory frame?

- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

If the platform recoils at speed $v_r$, the block's ground speed is $10-v_r$. Equating the momentum magnitudes gives the recoil speed.

$$
v_r=\frac{m_bu_{\mathrm{rel}}}{m_b+m_p},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_r&=1 \cdot 10 \cdot \frac{1}{1 + 4}\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

The ten metres per second is a difference between velocities measured along the same axis. The block travels more slowly relative to the floor because the platform recoils.
