Two carts of equal mass move on a straight track. Before the collision the first travels at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the second is at rest. The system is isolated and no source supplies energy during the collision. Which pair of final velocities $(v_1;v_2)$ is impossible, even though it conserves momentum?

- $(-1;5)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(2;2)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(0;4)\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $(1;3)\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

With equal masses, conservation of momentum requires $v_1+v_2=4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and all the proposed pairs satisfy this condition. The total kinetic energy is $\dfrac12 m\left(v_1^2+v_2^2\right)$, so for equal masses it is enough to compare the sum of the squares of the velocities. Before the collision this sum is $16\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, while afterwards it is $8$ for $(2;2)$, $16$ for $(0;4)$, $10$ for $(1;3)$ and $26$ for $(-1;5)$. Without energy sources the kinetic energy cannot increase, therefore the pair $(-1;5)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is **impossible**.
