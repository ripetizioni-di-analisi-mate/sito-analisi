A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ body at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ catches and sticks to a $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ body travelling at $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the same direction. How much kinetic energy is lost on an ideal track?

- $30\,\mathrm J$
- $7.5\,\mathrm J$
- $15\,\mathrm J$
- $-15\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

Both bodies contribute to initial kinetic energy and momentum. Momentum gives the shared speed of three metres per second used in the final energy.

$$
E_{\mathrm{loss}}=K_i-\frac12(m_1+m_2)v_f^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}E_{\mathrm{loss}}&=\frac{2\cdot6^2+3\cdot1^2}{2}\,\mathrm J-\frac{(2+3)\cdot3^2}{2}\,\mathrm J\\[4pt]&=(37.5-22.5)\,\mathrm J\\[4pt]&=15\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

The initially faster mass slows down while the other speeds up. The comparison is between the total energies. Counting only the first mass would count energy transferred to the second as dissipation.
