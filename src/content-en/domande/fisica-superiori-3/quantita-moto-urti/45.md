Masses of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ are at $0$ and $4\,\mathrm m$. What is the coordinate of their centre of mass?

- $3\,\mathrm m$
- $6\,\mathrm m$
- $1.5\,\mathrm m$
- $-3\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The four-metre coordinate has three times the weight of the other. The result is one metre to the left of the heavier mass and three metres from the lighter one.

$$
x_{CM}=\frac{\sum_jm_jx_j}{\sum_jm_j},\qquad
\begin{aligned}x_{CM}&=\frac{2 \cdot 0 + 6 \cdot 4}{2 + 6}\\[4pt]&=3\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

As a check, three metres from the lighter mass and one metre from the heavier mass give equal mass–distance products.
