A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass is at $-4\,\mathrm m$ and a second mass at $6\,\mathrm m$. The centre of mass is at $x=0$. What is the second mass?

- $4\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

The mean coordinate is zero when the numerator is zero. Equating the two product magnitudes gives a smaller mass on the more distant side.

$$
m_2=-\frac{m_1x_1}{x_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}m_2&=3 \cdot 4 \cdot \frac{1}{6}\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

The masses contribute with opposite signs to the numerator. The body farther from the origin needs less mass for the weighted terms to cancel.
