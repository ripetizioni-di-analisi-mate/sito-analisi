A force increases linearly from zero to $40\,\mathrm N$ in $0.20\,\mathrm s$ and keeps its direction. Find the impulse magnitude.

::grafico{linee="0,0 200,40" area=".2*x;0;0;200" xmin=0 xmax=250 ymin=0 ymax=50 nomi="t (ms),F (N)" titolo="Impulse is the triangular area below the graph"}

- $8\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$
- $-4\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$

## Explanation

The force–time graph is a triangle. The time-average force is half the final value.

$$
J=\frac12 F_{\mathrm{max}}\Delta t,\qquad
\begin{aligned}J&=40 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}.\end{aligned}
$$

The average force is half its final value. Using forty newtons over the whole interval would double the area by treating the rising segment as a rectangle.
