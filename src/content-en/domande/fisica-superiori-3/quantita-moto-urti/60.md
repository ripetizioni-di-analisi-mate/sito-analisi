A moving $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass hits and sticks to a stationary $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass. What fraction of the initial kinetic energy remains after the isolated collision?

- $0.5$
- $0.125$
- $-0.25$
- $0.25$

## Explanation

The final motion carries the initial momentum but has greater mass. At fixed momentum, kinetic energy is inversely proportional to the moving mass.

$$
K_f/K_i=\frac{m_1}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}K_f/K_i&=\frac{2}{2 + 6}\\[4pt]&=0.25.\end{aligned}
$$

The stationary body is three times as massive as the first. The same momentum is shared by four times the initial moving mass, so both the final speed and total kinetic energy are one quarter of their initial values.
