A mass $m_1=5\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at $x_1=0$, and a mass $m_2=6\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at $x_2=4\,\mathrm m$. Which value best approximates the centre-of-mass coordinate?

- $1.82\,\mathrm m$
- $2.18\,\mathrm m$
- $2.00\,\mathrm m$
- $4.80\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

Weight the coordinates by their masses. The first coordinate is zero, and the weighted mean lies between the two bodies.

$$
x_{CM}=\frac{m_1x_1+m_2x_2}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}x_{CM}&=\frac{5 \cdot 0 + 6 \cdot 4}{5 + 6}\\[4pt]&\simeq2.18\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

The point lies just beyond the halfway mark. The masses differ by only one kilogram, so their centre is close to the midpoint, slightly nearer the six-kilogram mass.
