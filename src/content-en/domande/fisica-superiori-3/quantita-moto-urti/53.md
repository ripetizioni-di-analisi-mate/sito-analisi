A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ collides elastically in one dimension with a $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart at $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the second cart's final velocity?

- $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $14\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $-7\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Initial momentum is ten kilogram metres per second, and relative separation after impact is six metres per second. Solving both constraints gives the second velocity.

$$
v_2=\frac{2m_1u_1+(m_2-m_1)u_2}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_2&=\frac{\left(1 - 3\right) \cdot \left(-2\right) + 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4}{1 + 3}\\[4pt]&=7\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

Since the second initial velocity is negative, the approach speed is six metres per second. After the elastic collision the second cart therefore separates from the first at the same relative speed.
