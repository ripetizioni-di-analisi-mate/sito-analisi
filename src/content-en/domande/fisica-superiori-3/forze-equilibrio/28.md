A stationary mass extends the same spring on two planets. What determines the ratio of extensions?

- The natural-length ratio of two different springs.
- The square of the acceleration ratio.
- The rotation-period ratio of the planets.
- The ratio of local gravitational accelerations.

## Explanation

In the ratio from $x=mg/k$, both the mass and the shared spring constant cancel. Only the ratio of the two values of $g$ remains. If one planet has twice the field strength, the same mass puts twice the weight on the support and the static extension doubles, provided the spring stays in its linear range.
