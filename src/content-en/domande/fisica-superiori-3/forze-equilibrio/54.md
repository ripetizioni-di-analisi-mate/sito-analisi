For a $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ block on a slope with $\sin\theta=0.6$, $\cos\theta=0.8$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the downslope component of weight.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 segmenti="0,0,4,0;4,0,4,3;0,0,4,3" vettori="2.4,1.8,2.4,-0.7,P;2.4,1.8,1.2,0.9,P_p;2.4,1.8,3.6,0.2,P_n" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1 ymax=3.4 titolo="The parallel projection points down the slope"}

- $40\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $30\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $50\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $10\,\mathrm{N}$.

## Explanation

Weight is fifty newtons and points vertically. In its projection triangle, the downslope component is opposite the angle between the vertical and the inward normal. Use the sine to obtain thirty newtons. The cosine would give forty newtons along the normal to the plane.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_\parallel&=mg\sin\theta=5\cdot10\cdot0.6\\[4pt]
&= 30\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
