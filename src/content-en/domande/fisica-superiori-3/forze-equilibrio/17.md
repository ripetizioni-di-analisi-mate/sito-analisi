A $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring is held in equilibrium first by $6$ and then by $15\,\mathrm N$. By how much does the extension increase?

- $0.03\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $0.02\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$.
- $0.07\,\mathrm{m}$.

## Explanation

Both extensions are measured from the same natural state. Apply Hooke's law in both equilibria and subtract, leaving a nine-newton increase in the numerator. The first extension is $0.02\,\mathrm m$ and the second $0.05\,\mathrm m$, a difference of three centimetres.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=\frac{\Delta F}{k}=\frac{15-6}{300}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 0.03\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
