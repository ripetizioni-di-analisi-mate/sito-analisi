A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ block slides down a slope with $\sin\theta=3/5$ and $\cos\theta=4/5$. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_d=0.25$, $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the acceleration is $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ downhill. Besides weight, the normal force and friction, an additional force acts parallel to the slope. What is its component with downhill positive?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 4,3" segmenti="0,0,4,0;4,0,4,3" vettori="2.6,2.23,1.65,1.5175,a" xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.7 titolo="The arrow shows the direction of the observed acceleration."}

- $8\,\mathrm{N}$
- $16\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0\,\mathrm{N}$
- $-8\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

Along the slope, weight and friction give a resultant of $(24-8)\,\mathrm N=16\,\mathrm N$ downhill. The measured acceleration requires $ma=8\,\mathrm N$. The additional force is $(8-16)\,\mathrm N=-8\,\mathrm N$, so it points uphill. The block still accelerates downhill because its net force remains positive.
