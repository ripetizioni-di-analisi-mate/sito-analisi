A body slides down a slope with $\sin\theta=3/5$, $\cos\theta=4/5$ and $\mu_d=0.25$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find acceleration signed downslope.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 segmenti="0,0,4,0;4,0,4,3;0,0,4,3" punti="4,3,pieno" xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.7 titolo="The slope triangle has legs 4 and 3 and hypotenuse 5"}

- $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

## Explanation

Weight contributes six metres per second squared down the slope. Opposing friction depends on the normal force through the cosine and reduces acceleration by two. The positive result means downslope speed grows. Mass cancels from the slope balance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=10\left(\frac35-0.25\cdot\frac45\right)\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
