An ideal lever of negligible weight experiences $F_1=35\,\mathrm N$ with moment arm $b_1=0.36\,\mathrm m$. A second force produces the opposite torque with moment arm $b_2=0.66\,\mathrm m$. Which value is closest to the force $F_2$ that gives rotational equilibrium?

- $9.55\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $38.2\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $28.65\,\mathrm{N}$.
- $19.1\,\mathrm{N}$.

## Explanation

The torques have opposite signs. For rotational equilibrium their magnitudes must be equal, so $F_1b_1=F_2b_2$. The second force has a longer lever arm, so its magnitude has to be smaller. The ratio is $0.36/0.66$. This does not give the pivot reaction, which comes from force balance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_2&=\frac{F_1b_1}{b_2}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 19.1\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
