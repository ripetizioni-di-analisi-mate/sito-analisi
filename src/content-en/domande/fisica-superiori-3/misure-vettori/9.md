A coating uses $18\,\mathrm g$ of product per $100\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. How many kilograms are needed to coat $1\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at the same application rate?

- $1.8\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.18\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $18\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $180\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

One metre measures $100\,\mathrm{cm}$, so one square metre contains $100^2\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. This gives $\dfrac{100^2}{100}=100$ portions of $100\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The required mass is $18\cdot100=1800\,\mathrm g=1.8\,\mathrm{kg}$. Using the linear factor $100$ directly for the area would count only one portion and underestimate the quantity. The result is also the mass per square metre of coating. Doubling the area at the same coverage rate would double the amount of product.
