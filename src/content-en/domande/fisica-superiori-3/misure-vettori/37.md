A ratio has an exact numerator of $1.2\,\mathrm m$ and a denominator measured as $(0.2\pm0.3)\,\mathrm s$. Every value of the denominator in the interval is allowed except zero. What can be guaranteed about the magnitude of the ratio?

- It has no finite upper bound.
- It is at most $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- It is at most $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- It is always equal to $2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

## Explanation

The interval runs from $-0.1$ to $0.5\,\mathrm s$. It contains arbitrarily small positive denominators. For example, a denominator of $0.01\,\mathrm s$ gives $120\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Choosing values closer to zero makes the magnitude grow without bound. A first-order percentage estimate would require an interval well away from zero.
