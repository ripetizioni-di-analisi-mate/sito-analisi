A scale weighs a vessel holding the same liquid at two fill levels. At $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ the total mass is $28\,\mathrm g$ and at $60\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ it is $60\,\mathrm g$. What is the mass of the empty vessel?

- $8\,\mathrm{g}$
- $12\,\mathrm{g}$
- $16\,\mathrm{g}$
- $28\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

Subtracting the readings cancels the tare. The slope gives the density $\dfrac{60-28}{60-20}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}=0.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. At $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ the liquid alone therefore has mass $0.8\cdot20=16\,\mathrm g$. The difference $28-16=12\,\mathrm g$ is the intercept of the relation between liquid volume and total mass. A check uses the second reading. At $60\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ the liquid alone has a mass of $48\,\mathrm g$. Adding the container's $12\,\mathrm g$ recovers the total reading.
