A boat travels along a channel at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water. The current moves at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the opposite direction relative to the bank. How is the boat’s velocity relative to the bank described?

- The resultant points with the flow.
- The resultant is perpendicular to both vectors.
- The resultant keeps the larger vector's orientation and is smaller in magnitude than both.
- The resultant is larger in magnitude than both.

## Explanation

With both vectors on the same line in opposite directions, the sum is their signed difference. Taking the larger vector's direction as positive gives $5-3=2$. The speed relative to the bank is therefore $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. It points along the boat's velocity relative to the water because the weaker current cancels only part of that velocity.
