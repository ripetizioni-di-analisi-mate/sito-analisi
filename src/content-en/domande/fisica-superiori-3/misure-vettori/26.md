A closed hollow object has an external volume of $100\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and a mass of $108\,\mathrm g$. It consists of one material of density $2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. The contents of the cavity have negligible mass. What is the volume of the cavity?

- $40\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $60\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $100\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$

## Explanation

The measured mass comes from the material, so its volume is $\dfrac{108}{2.7}\,\mathrm{cm^3}=40\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. The external volume also includes the cavity. Subtracting the volume occupied by the material leaves $60\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ empty. Applying the material density to all $100\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ would assign mass to the cavity as well.
