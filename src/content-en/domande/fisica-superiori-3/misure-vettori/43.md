A balance always shows $2\,\mathrm g$ too much. What does repeating the same measurement do without correcting its tare?

- It removes the systematic offset from the mean.
- It reverses the offset in alternate readings.
- It leaves the systematic offset in the mean.
- It turns the fixed offset into the half-range.

## Explanation

Every reading includes the same additive component. After adding the readings and dividing by their number, the mean is also larger by exactly that component. A mass of $80\,\mathrm g$ read each time as $82\,\mathrm g$ would still have a mean of $82\,\mathrm g$. Estimating the common offset requires a reading against a known reference, for example with the balance empty.
