The empty container gives a scale reading of $80\,\mathrm{g}$. Holding $45\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of liquid, the reading becomes $170\,\mathrm{g}$. What is the liquid's density?

::grafico{f="2*x+80" punti="0,80,pieno;45,170,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=50 ymin=0 ymax=210 nomi="V,m" titolo="The line joins the empty and filled readings"}

- $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
- $\frac{34}{9}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
- $\frac{16}{9}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
- $\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

## Explanation

The scale measures the mass of the entire container and liquid. To isolate the mass of the liquid, subtract the empty reading. Density is the slope joining the two readings as a function of volume. The zero-volume point represents the tare.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m_{\mathrm{tot}}-m_{\mathrm{tara}}}{V}\\[4pt]&=\frac{170-80}{45}\\[4pt]
&= 2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$
