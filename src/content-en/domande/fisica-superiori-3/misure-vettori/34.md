Every linear dimension of a replica made of the same material is tripled. How does its mass change?

- It increases by a factor of three.
- It increases by a factor of twenty-seven.
- It increases by a factor of nine.
- It is unchanged.

## Explanation

Each of the three linear dimensions contributes a factor of three to volume, so volume scales as $3^3$. At constant density, mass scales in the same way. Consider a cubic sample. Three lengths of the original cube fit along each axis of the copy. The volume of the copy therefore contains twenty-seven original cubes.
