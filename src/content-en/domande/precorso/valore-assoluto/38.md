What are the solutions of the inequality $|2x - 1| < x + 2$?

- $x < 3$
- $-3 < x < \dfrac{1}{3}$
- $x < -\dfrac{1}{3} \ \vee \ x > 3$
- $-\dfrac{1}{3} < x < 3$

## Explanation

The inequality $|A| < B$ is equivalent to the system of the two conditions $A < B$ and $A > -B$, even when $B$ contains the variable:
$$
\begin{cases}
2x - 1 < x + 2 \\
2x - 1 > -x - 2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < 3 \\
x > -\dfrac{1}{3}
\end{cases}
$$
The solutions are the **intersection** of the two half-lines, that is, $-\dfrac{1}{3} < x < 3$. The endpoints are excluded, because at each of them the two sides are equal.
