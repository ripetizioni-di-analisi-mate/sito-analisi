Which statement about the equation $|x| + |x - 4| = 4$ is true?

- The only solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 4$
- The equation has no solutions
- The solutions are all $x$ with $0 \leq x \leq 4$
- The only solution is $x = 2$

## Explanation

The arguments vanish at $0$ and at $4$. In the interval $0 \leq x < 4$ we have $|x| = x$ and $|x - 4| = 4 - x$, so the left-hand side equals $x + (4 - x) = 4$ and the equation is satisfied by every $x$ in the interval. For $x < 0$ the equation becomes $4 - 2x = 4$, that is, $x = 0$, which does not satisfy $x < 0$. For $x \geq 4$ it becomes $2x - 4 = 4$, that is, $x = 4$, which belongs to the interval. The solutions are therefore **all** $x$ with $0 \leq x \leq 4$, that is, the points whose distances from $0$ and from $4$ add up to $4$.
