What are the solutions of the inequality $|x - 2| - |x| > 1$?

- $x < 0$
- $0 \leq x < \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x > \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x < \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The arguments vanish at $0$ and at $2$, so the real line is divided into three intervals:
$$
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
(2 - x) - (-x) > 1
\end{cases}
\ \cup \
\begin{cases}
0 \leq x < 2 \\
(2 - x) - x > 1
\end{cases}
\ \cup \
\begin{cases}
x \geq 2 \\
(x - 2) - x > 1
\end{cases}
$$
In the first interval the left-hand side equals $2$ and the inequality $2 > 1$ is true, so every $x < 0$ is a solution. In the second interval we obtain $2 - 2x > 1$, that is, $x < \dfrac{1}{2}$, which together with the interval condition gives $0 \leq x < \dfrac{1}{2}$. In the third interval the left-hand side equals $-2$ and the inequality $-2 > 1$ is false. The union of the results is $x < \dfrac{1}{2}$.
