For which real numbers $x$ does the equality $|x - 3| = 3 - x$ hold?

- $x \leq 3$
- $x \geq 3$
- $x < 3$
- For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

The right-hand side is the opposite of the argument, because $3 - x = -(x - 3)$. By the definition of absolute value, $|x - 3| = -(x - 3)$ when $x - 3 < 0$, that is, for $x < 3$. At $x = 3$ both sides equal $0$, so $x = 3$ also satisfies the equality. For $x > 3$, instead, $|x - 3|$ is positive while $3 - x$ is negative, therefore the equality holds exactly for $x \leq 3$.
