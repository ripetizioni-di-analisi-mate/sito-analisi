The equation $|x - 1| - |x - 3| = 2$ is satisfied by which real numbers?

- Only $x = 3$
- All $x \geq 3$
- All $x$ with $1 \leq x \leq 3$
- No real number

## Explanation

The arguments vanish at $1$ and at $3$. For $x < 1$ the equation becomes $(1 - x) - (3 - x) = 2$, that is, $-2 = 2$, which is false. For $1 \leq x < 3$ it becomes $(x - 1) - (3 - x) = 2$, that is, $2x - 4 = 2$, which gives $x = 3$, outside the interval. For $x \geq 3$ it becomes $(x - 1) - (x - 3) = 2$, that is, $2 = 2$, which is true for every $x$ in the interval. Therefore the solutions are all $x \geq 3$.
