What are the solutions of the equation $|x - 1| + |x + 3| = 6$?

- $x = -4 \ \vee \ x = 2$
- $x = -2 \ \vee \ x = 4$
- $x = 2$
- $x = -4$

## Explanation

The arguments vanish at $-3$ and at $1$, so the real line is divided into three intervals:
$$
\begin{cases}
x < -3 \\
-2x - 2 = 6
\end{cases}
\ \cup \
\begin{cases}
-3 \leq x < 1 \\
4 = 6
\end{cases}
\ \cup \
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
2x + 2 = 6
\end{cases}
$$
In the first interval we obtain $x = -4$, which is admissible since $-4 < -3$. In the middle interval the equality $4 = 6$ is false, so there are no solutions. In the third interval we obtain $x = 2$, which is admissible since $2 \geq 1$. The solutions are therefore $x = -4$ and $x = 2$.
