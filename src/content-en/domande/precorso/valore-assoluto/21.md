What are the solutions of the equation $|x^2 - 2x| = 3$?

- $x = 3$
- $x = -3 \ \vee \ x = 1$
- $x = -1 \ \vee \ x = 1 \ \vee \ x = 3$
- $x = -1 \ \vee \ x = 3$

## Explanation

Since $3 > 0$, the equation is equivalent to $x^2 - 2x = 3 \ \vee \ x^2 - 2x = -3$. The first is written $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$, that is, $(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0$, and gives $x = 3$ or $x = -1$. The second is written $x^2 - 2x + 3 = 0$ and has discriminant $\Delta = 4 - 12 = -8 < 0$, so it has no real solutions. The solutions are therefore $x = -1$ and $x = 3$.
