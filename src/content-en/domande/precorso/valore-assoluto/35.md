What are the solutions of the inequality $|4 - x| \geq 1$?

- $3 \leq x \leq 5$
- $x < 3 \ \vee \ x > 5$
- $x \leq -5 \ \vee \ x \geq -3$
- $x \leq 3 \ \vee \ x \geq 5$

## Explanation

Since $1 > 0$, the inequality is equivalent to $4 - x \leq -1 \ \vee \ 4 - x \geq 1$. The first gives $-x \leq -5$, that is, $x \geq 5$; the second gives $-x \geq -3$, that is, $x \leq 3$. The solutions are the **union** of the two half-lines, with the endpoints included because the inequality is non-strict. Since $|4 - x| = |x - 4|$ is the distance of $x$ from $4$, these are the points at distance at least $1$ from $4$.
