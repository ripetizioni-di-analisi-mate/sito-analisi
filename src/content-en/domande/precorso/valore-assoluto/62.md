The equation $\left|\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 1}\right| = 1$ makes sense for $x \neq -1$. What can be said about its solutions?

- The equation has no solutions
- Every $x \neq -1$ is a solution
- The only solution is $x = 0$
- The solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 1$

## Explanation

Since $1 > 0$, the equation is equivalent to $\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 1} = 1 \ \vee \ \dfrac{x - 1}{x + 1} = -1$. Multiplying by $x + 1 \neq 0$, the first becomes $x - 1 = x + 1$, that is, $-1 = 1$, which is false. The second becomes $x - 1 = -x - 1$, that is, $2x = 0$, so $x = 0$, which satisfies the condition $x \neq -1$. Hence the only solution is $x = 0$.
