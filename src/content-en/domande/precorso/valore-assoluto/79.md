The figure shows the graphs of the functions $f(x) = |x|$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{x}{2} + 3$. For which $x$ does $|x| < \dfrac{x}{2} + 3$ hold?

::grafico{f="abs(x);x/2+3" xmin=-5 xmax=8 ymin=-1 ymax=8}

- $-2 < x < 6$
- Every $x \in \mathbb{R}$
- $x < -2 \ \vee \ x > 6$
- $-2 \leq x \leq 6$

## Explanation

The solutions are the abscissas of the points where the graph of $f$ lies *below* that of $g$. The inequality $|A| < B$ is equivalent to the system of the two conditions $A < B$ and $A > -B$:
$$
\begin{cases}
x < \dfrac{x}{2} + 3 \\
x > -\dfrac{x}{2} - 3
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < 6 \\
x > -2
\end{cases}
$$
The two conditions must hold together, so the solutions are $-2 < x < 6$. The endpoints are excluded, because at $x = -2$ and at $x = 6$ the two sides are equal and the two graphs meet.
