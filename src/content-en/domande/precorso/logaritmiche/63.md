What is the smallest integer $n$ such that $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n} < 0.001$?

- $n = 10$
- $n = 9$
- $n = 3$
- $n = 6$

## Explanation

We apply the logarithm in base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ to both sides. The base lies between $0$ and $1$, so the direction is reversed and we obtain $n > \log_{\frac{1}{2}} 0.001 = \log_2 1000$. Since $2^{9} = 512$ and $2^{10} = 1024$, we have $2^{9} < 1000 < 2^{10}$, therefore $9 < \log_2 1000 < 10$. Hence the smallest integer greater than $\log_2 1000$ is $n = 10$, and indeed $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{10} = \dfrac{1}{1024} < 0.001$.
