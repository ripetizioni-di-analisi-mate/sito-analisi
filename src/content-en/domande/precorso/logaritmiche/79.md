Do the equations $\log_3 \bigl(x(x + 2)\bigr) = 1$ and $\log_3 x + \log_3 (x + 2) = 1$ have the same solutions?

- No, the first has the solutions $x = 1$ and $x = -3$, the second has the single solution $x = 1$
- Yes, both have the solutions $x = 1$ and $x = -3$
- Yes, both have the single solution $x = 1$
- No, the first has the single solution $x = 1$, the second has the solutions $x = 1$ and $x = -3$

## Explanation

In the first equation there is a single argument, $x(x + 2)$, so the domain restriction is $x(x + 2) > 0$, that is $x < -2$ or $x > 0$. By the definition of logarithm the first equation is equivalent to $x(x + 2) = 3$, that is $x^{2} + 2x - 3 = 0$, which factors as $(x + 3)(x - 1) = 0$. Both values $x = -3$ and $x = 1$ satisfy the condition, therefore the first equation has two solutions. In the second equation the arguments are $x$ and $x + 2$, and the domain restrictions form the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\
x + 2 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff x > 0 .
$$
By the property of the logarithm of a product the second equation leads to the same algebraic equation, but the value $x = -3$ does not satisfy the condition $x > 0$. Hence the second equation has the single solution $x = 1$.
