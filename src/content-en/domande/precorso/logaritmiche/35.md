What are the solutions of the inequality $\log_{\frac{1}{2}} (x - 1) > 2$?

- $x > \dfrac{5}{4}$
- $1 < x < \dfrac{5}{4}$
- $x < \dfrac{5}{4}$
- $1 < x < 5$

## Explanation

The number $2$ is written as a logarithm in base $\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is $2 = \log_{\frac{1}{2}} \dfrac{1}{4}$, because $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. The base lies between $0$ and $1$, so the direction **is reversed** and we obtain $x - 1 < \dfrac{1}{4}$. The domain condition does not follow from this inequality, so it must be written in the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x - 1 < \dfrac{1}{4} \\
x - 1 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x < \dfrac{5}{4} \\
x > 1
\end{cases}
$$
The solutions are therefore $1 < x < \dfrac{5}{4}$.
