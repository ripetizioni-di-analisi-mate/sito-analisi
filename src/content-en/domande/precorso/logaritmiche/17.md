Which transformation takes the graph of $y = \log_2 x$ to the graph of $y = \log_2 (4x)$?

- A translation of $2$ units upward
- A translation of $4$ units upward
- A translation of $4$ units to the left
- A translation of $2$ units downward

## Explanation

Both functions are defined for $x > 0$. For these values the property of the logarithm of a product applies, so $\log_2 (4x) = \log_2 4 + \log_2 x = 2 + \log_2 x$. Therefore the point $(x, \log_2 x)$ of the first graph corresponds to the point $(x, 2 + \log_2 x)$ of the second, with the same abscissa and the ordinate increased by $2$. Hence the graph of $y = \log_2 (4x)$ is obtained from that of $y = \log_2 x$ by a translation of $2$ units upward. The translation of $4$ units to the left instead gives the graph of $y = \log_2 (x + 4)$.
