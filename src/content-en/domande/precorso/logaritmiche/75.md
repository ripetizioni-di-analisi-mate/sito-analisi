In the equation $\log_x (2x + 3) = 2$ the unknown also appears in the base of the logarithm. What are the solutions?

- $x = 3$ and $x = -1$
- $x = -1$
- $x = 1$ and $x = -3$
- $x = 3$

## Explanation

The base must be positive and different from $1$ and the argument must be positive, so the domain restrictions form the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\
x \neq 1 \\
2x + 3 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\
x \neq 1
\end{cases}
$$
By the definition of logarithm the equation is equivalent to $x^{2} = 2x + 3$, that is $x^{2} - 2x - 3 = 0$, which factors as $(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0$. The solutions are $x = 3$ and $x = -1$; the value $x = -1$ is negative and cannot be the base of a logarithm. Hence the only solution is $x = 3$.
