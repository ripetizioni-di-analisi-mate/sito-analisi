What must $x$ be for $\log_3 \bigl(2^{x} + 1\bigr) = 2$ to hold?

- $x = 0$
- $x = \log_2 5$
- $x = 8$
- $x = 3$

## Explanation

The argument $2^{x} + 1$ is positive for every $x$, so there are no domain restrictions. By the definition of logarithm the equation is equivalent to $2^{x} + 1 = 3^{2} = 9$, that is $2^{x} = 8$. Since $8 = 2^{3}$, we obtain $x = 3$.
