For which value of $x$ is the sum $\log_2 x + \log_x 2$ equal to $2$?

- $x = 1$
- $x = 2$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x = 2$
- $x = 4$

## Explanation

The base $x$ of the second logarithm must be positive and different from $1$, so the domain restrictions form the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\
x \neq 1
\end{cases}
$$
By the change of base formula, $\log_x 2 = \dfrac{\log_2 2}{\log_2 x} = \dfrac{1}{\log_2 x}$. With the substitution $t = \log_2 x$, where $t \neq 0$, the equation becomes $t + \dfrac{1}{t} = 2$, that is $t^{2} - 2t + 1 = 0$, which has the single solution $t = 1$. By the definition of logarithm, $x = 2^{1} = 2$, which satisfies the domain restrictions.
