For which $x$ is the logarithm of the sum, $\log_5 (x + 4)$, equal to the sum of the logarithms $\log_5 x + \log_5 4$?

- For every $x > 0$
- Only for $x = 4$
- Only for $x = \dfrac{4}{3}$
- For no value of $x$

## Explanation

The domain restrictions form the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 4 > 0 \\
x > 0
\end{cases}
\iff x > 0 .
$$
By the property of the logarithm of a product the right-hand side becomes $\log_5 (4x)$, and by the injectivity of the logarithmic function the equation is equivalent to $x + 4 = 4x$. Hence $x = \dfrac{4}{3}$, which satisfies the condition $x > 0$. Therefore the equality between $\log_5 (x + 4)$ and $\log_5 x + \log_5 4$ holds only for this value of $x$.
