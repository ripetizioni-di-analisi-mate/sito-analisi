For which value of the real number $k$ does the equation $\log_3 (x^{2} + 1) = k$ have exactly one solution?

- $k = 0$
- $k = 1$
- $k = -1$
- For no value of $k$

## Explanation

The argument $x^{2} + 1$ is positive for every $x$, so there are no domain restrictions. By the definition of logarithm the equation is equivalent to $x^{2} + 1 = 3^{k}$, that is to $x^{2} = 3^{k} - 1$. If $3^{k} - 1 > 0$, that is $k > 0$, there are two opposite solutions; if $3^{k} - 1 < 0$, that is $k < 0$, there are no solutions. For $k = 0$ we have $x^{2} = 0$, therefore the equation has the single solution $x = 0$.
