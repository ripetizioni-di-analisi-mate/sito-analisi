For $x > 0$, $y > 0$ and $z > 0$, which logarithm equals $\log_3 x - 2\log_3 y + \dfrac{1}{2}\log_3 z$?

- $\log_3 \dfrac{x\sqrt{z}}{y^{2}}$
- $\log_3 \dfrac{x\sqrt{z}}{2y}$
- $\log_3 \dfrac{x}{y^{2}\sqrt{z}}$
- $\log_3 \bigl(x - y^{2} + \sqrt{z}\bigr)$

## Explanation

The arguments are positive, so the properties of logarithms apply. By the power property, $2\log_3 y = \log_3 (y^{2})$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}\log_3 z = \log_3 \sqrt{z}$. The difference of two logarithms is the logarithm of the quotient and the sum is the logarithm of the product, therefore
$$
\log_3 x - \log_3 (y^{2}) + \log_3 \sqrt{z} = \log_3 \frac{x\sqrt{z}}{y^{2}} .
$$
The coefficient $2$ becomes the exponent of $y$, and the coefficient $\dfrac{1}{2}$ becomes the square root of $z$.
