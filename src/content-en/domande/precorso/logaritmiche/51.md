Which equality holds for every $a > 0$ with $a \neq 1$ and for every $x > 0$, $y > 0$?

- $\log_a (x + y) = \log_a x + \log_a y$
- $\log_a (xy) = \log_a x \cdot \log_a y$
- $\log_a (x^{3}) = 3\log_a x$
- $\log_a \dfrac{x}{y} = \dfrac{\log_a x}{\log_a y}$

## Explanation

By the power property, $\log_a (x^{p}) = p \log_a x$ for every real exponent $p$, so $\log_a (x^{3}) = 3 \log_a x$ for every $x > 0$. The logarithm of a product is the *sum* of the logarithms and the logarithm of a quotient is the *difference* of the logarithms. There is no general formula for the logarithm of a sum; for example, with $a = 2$ and $x = y = 1$ we have $\log_2 2 = 1$, while $\log_2 1 + \log_2 1 = 0$.
