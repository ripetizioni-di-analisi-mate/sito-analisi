What are the solutions of the equation $\log_2 \bigl((x - 1)^{2}\bigr) = 2$?

- $x = 3$
- $x = 1 + \sqrt{2}$ and $x = 1 - \sqrt{2}$
- $x = -1$
- $x = 3$ and $x = -1$

## Explanation

The argument $(x - 1)^{2}$ is positive for every $x \neq 1$, so the domain condition is $x \neq 1$. By the definition of logarithm the equation is equivalent to $(x - 1)^{2} = 2^{2} = 4$, that is to $x - 1 = 2$ or $x - 1 = -2$. This gives $x = 3$ and $x = -1$, both different from $1$, therefore **they are solutions**. Rewriting the equation as $2 \log_2 (x - 1) = 2$ would require $x - 1 > 0$ and would lose the solution $x = -1$.
