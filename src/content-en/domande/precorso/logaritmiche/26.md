For which $x > 0$ does the graph of $y = \log_2 x$ lie above the graph of $y = \log_3 x$?

- $0 < x < 1$
- $x > 1$
- For every $x > 0$
- For no $x > 0$

## Explanation

By the change of base formula $\log_2 x = \dfrac{\log_3 x}{\log_3 2}$, so
$$
\log_2 x - \log_3 x = \log_3 x \left( \frac{1}{\log_3 2} - 1 \right) .
$$
Since $1 < 2 < 3$ and the function $\log_3 x$ is increasing, we have $0 < \log_3 2 < 1$, therefore $\dfrac{1}{\log_3 2} > 1$ and the second factor is positive. The difference then has the same sign as $\log_3 x$, which is positive for $x > 1$, zero for $x = 1$ and negative for $0 < x < 1$. Hence the graph of $y = \log_2 x$ lies above that of $y = \log_3 x$ for $x > 1$, and the two graphs meet at the point $(1, 0)$.
