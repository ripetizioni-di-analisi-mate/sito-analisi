With the substitution $t = \log_3 x$, which of the following equations becomes $t^{2} - t - 6 = 0$?

- $(\log_3 x)^{2} - \log_3 x - 6 = 0$
- $\log_3 (x^{2}) - \log_3 x - 6 = 0$
- $\log_3 (x^{2} - x - 6) = 0$
- $(\log_3 x)^{2} - \log_3 (6x) = 0$

## Explanation

With $t = \log_3 x$ the square $(\log_3 x)^{2}$ becomes $t^{2}$, therefore the equation $(\log_3 x)^{2} - \log_3 x - 6 = 0$ becomes $t^{2} - t - 6 = 0$. The logarithm of the square of $x$ instead gives a first-degree term in $t$, because for $x > 0$ the power property gives $\log_3 (x^{2}) = 2 \log_3 x = 2t$; so $\log_3 (x^{2}) - \log_3 x - 6 = 0$ becomes $t - 6 = 0$. By the property of the logarithm of a product $\log_3 (6x) = \log_3 6 + t$, so $(\log_3 x)^{2} - \log_3 (6x) = 0$ becomes $t^{2} - t - \log_3 6 = 0$. In the equation $\log_3 (x^{2} - x - 6) = 0$ the argument of the logarithm is a trinomial, therefore the equation does not contain $\log_3 x$ and the substitution does not turn it into an equation in $t$.
