At which points does the parabola $y = 2x^2 - x - 3$ intersect the $x$-axis?

- $(1, 0)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}, 0\right)$
- $(-2, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$
- $(-4, 0)$ and $(6, 0)$
- $(-1, 0)$ and $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, 0\right)$

## Explanation

The intersections with the $x$-axis are the solutions of the equation $2x^2 - x - 3 = 0$. The discriminant is $\Delta = (-1)^2 - 4 \cdot 2 \cdot (-3) = 1 + 24 = 25$, which is positive, therefore there are two intersections. With the quadratic formula we obtain
$$
x = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{25}}{2 \cdot 2} = \frac{1 \pm 5}{4} ,
$$
that is $x = \dfrac{6}{4} = \dfrac{3}{2}$ or $x = -\dfrac{4}{4} = -1$. So the parabola intersects the $x$-axis at the points $(-1, 0)$ and $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, 0\right)$.
