The hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - y^2 = 1$ has exactly one of the following properties. Which one?

- It meets the $y$-axis at the points $(0, \pm 1)$
- None of its points has $x$-coordinate between $-2$ and $2$, endpoints excluded
- Its asymptotes are the lines $y = \pm 2x$
- It meets the $x$-axis at the points $(\pm 4, 0)$

## Explanation

From the equation we get $\dfrac{x^2}{4} = 1 + y^2 \geq 1$, so every point of the hyperbola satisfies $x^2 \geq 4$, that is, $x \leq -2$ or $x \geq 2$. Therefore no point has $x$-coordinate strictly between $-2$ and $2$, and the two branches lie to the left of $x = -2$ and to the right of $x = 2$. With $a = 2$ and $b = 1$ the vertices are $(\pm 2, 0)$ and the asymptotes are $y = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}x$. Setting $x = 0$ we obtain $y^2 = -1$, so the hyperbola does not meet the $y$-axis.
