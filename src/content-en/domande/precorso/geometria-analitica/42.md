What is the equation in standard form of the hyperbola with vertices $(\pm 4, 0)$ and asymptotes $y = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}x$?

- $\dfrac{x^2}{16} - \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{16} - \dfrac{y^2}{64} = 1$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{2} = 1$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$

## Explanation

The vertices $(\pm 4, 0)$ lie on the $x$-axis, therefore the hyperbola has equation $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ with $a = 4$. The asymptotes are the lines $y = \pm\dfrac{b}{a}x$, so $\dfrac{b}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is $b = 2$. The equation contains the squares of the semi-axes, $a^2 = 16$ and $b^2 = 4$. Therefore the hyperbola has equation $\dfrac{x^2}{16} - \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$.
