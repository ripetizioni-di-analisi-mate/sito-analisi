The parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 2x + k$ are tangent for exactly one value of $k$. Which one?

- $k = 1$
- $k = -2$
- $k = -1$
- $k = 2$

## Explanation

The $x$-coordinates of the common points are the solutions of $x^2 = 2x + k$, that is, of $x^2 - 2x - k = 0$. The line is tangent to the parabola if and only if this equation has exactly one solution, that is, if and only if its discriminant is zero. The discriminant is $\Delta = 4 + 4k$, so $\Delta = 0$ for $k = -1$. For this value the equation becomes $(x - 1)^2 = 0$, and the point of tangency is $(1, 1)$.
