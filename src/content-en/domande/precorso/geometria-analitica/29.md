At which points does the line $y = x + 2$ intersect the parabola $y = x^2$?

- $(-2, 4)$ and $(1, 1)$
- $(2, 4)$ and $(-1, 1)$
- $(2, 4)$ and $(1, 1)$
- $(2, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$

## Explanation

The intersection points satisfy both equations, therefore their abscissas are the solutions of $x^2 = x + 2$, that is of $x^2 - x - 2 = 0$. Since $x^2 - x - 2 = (x - 2)(x + 1)$, the solutions are $x = 2$ and $x = -1$. The ordinates are obtained by substituting into the equation of the line: for $x = 2$ we get $y = 4$ and for $x = -1$ we get $y = 1$. So the intersection points are $(2, 4)$ and $(-1, 1)$.
