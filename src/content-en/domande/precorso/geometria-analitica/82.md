The ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ passes through the points $(2, 0)$ and $\left(1, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$. What is its equation?

- $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{2} = 1$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{y^2}{3} = 1$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{3} = 1$
- $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$

## Explanation

Substituting the point $(2, 0)$ into the equation we obtain $\dfrac{4}{a^2} = 1$, so $a^2 = 4$. Then substituting the point $\left(1, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$ we obtain
$$
\frac{1}{4} + \frac{9}{4b^2} = 1 ,
$$
so $\dfrac{9}{4b^2} = \dfrac{3}{4}$, and therefore $b^2 = 3$. The ellipse has equation $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{3} = 1$.
