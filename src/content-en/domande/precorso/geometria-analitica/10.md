What are the asymptotes of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{16} - \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$?

- $y = \pm\dfrac{4}{3}x$
- $y = \pm\dfrac{3}{4}x$
- $y = \pm\dfrac{9}{16}x$
- $y = \pm\dfrac{5}{4}x$

## Explanation

From the equation we read $a^2 = 16$ and $b^2 = 9$, therefore $a = 4$ and $b = 3$. The asymptotes of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ are the lines $y = \pm\dfrac{b}{a}x$, which are the diagonals of the rectangle bounded by the lines $x = \pm a$ and $y = \pm b$. The ratio $\dfrac{b}{a}$ is formed with the semi-axes, that is with the square roots of the denominators $16$ and $9$. Therefore the asymptotes are $y = \pm\dfrac{3}{4}x$.
