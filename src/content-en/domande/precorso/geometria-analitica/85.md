Which of the following are the coordinates of a point of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{9} - \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$?

- $(3, 2)$
- $(0, 2)$
- $(3\sqrt{2}, 2)$
- $(9, 0)$

## Explanation

A point lies on the hyperbola if and only if its coordinates satisfy the equation. For $(3\sqrt{2}, 2)$ the left-hand side equals $\dfrac{18}{9} - \dfrac{4}{4} = 2 - 1 = 1$, so the point lies on the hyperbola. For $(3, 2)$ the left-hand side equals $1 - 1 = 0$, for $(0, 2)$ it equals $-1$ and for $(9, 0)$ it equals $9$. The point $(3, 2)$ is a vertex of the rectangle with sides $x = \pm 3$ and $y = \pm 2$, and it lies on the asymptote $y = \dfrac{2}{3}x$.
