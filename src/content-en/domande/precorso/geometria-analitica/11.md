What does the equation $x^2 + y^2 + 4x - 2y + 1 = 0$ represent?

- The circle with center $(-2, 1)$ and radius $2$
- The circle with center $(2, -1)$ and radius $2$
- The circle with center $(-2, 1)$ and radius $4$
- The circle with center $(-2, 1)$ and radius $\sqrt{6}$

## Explanation

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are equal and the term $xy$ does not appear, therefore the equation represents a circle, provided that after the reduction the right-hand side turns out to be positive. Since $x^2 + 4x = (x + 2)^2 - 4$ and $y^2 - 2y = (y - 1)^2 - 1$, the equation becomes
$$
(x + 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 4 + 1 - 1 = 4 .
$$
The right-hand side is positive, so it is a **circle** with center $(-2, 1)$ and radius $\sqrt{4} = 2$.
