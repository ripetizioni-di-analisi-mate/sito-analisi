Which of the following equations represents a circle concentric with the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 2y = 0$?

- $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 2y + 5 = 0$
- $x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 2y = 0$
- $x^2 + y^2 - 3x + y = 0$
- $x^2 + 2y^2 - 6x + 2y = 0$

## Explanation

Two circles are concentric if and only if they have the same center. The center of the circle $x^2 + y^2 + ax + by + c = 0$ is $\left(-\dfrac{a}{2}, -\dfrac{b}{2}\right)$, so it depends only on $a$ and $b$, and the given circle has center $(3, -1)$. The equation $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 2y + 5 = 0$ has the same $a$ and $b$, and completing the squares it becomes $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 5$, with a positive right-hand side. Therefore it represents a circle with center $(3, -1)$, concentric with the given one. The equations $x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 2y = 0$ and $x^2 + y^2 - 3x + y = 0$ have centers $(-3, 1)$ and $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, while $x^2 + 2y^2 - 6x + 2y = 0$ does not represent a circle, because the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are different.
