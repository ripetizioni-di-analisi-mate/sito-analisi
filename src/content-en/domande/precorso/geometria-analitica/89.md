For which value of $b > 0$ are the asymptotes of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ perpendicular?

- $4$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $1$
- $2$

## Explanation

With $a = 2$ the asymptotes are the lines $y = \dfrac{b}{2}x$ and $y = -\dfrac{b}{2}x$. Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if the product of the slopes is $-1$, so we need $\dfrac{b}{2} \cdot \left(-\dfrac{b}{2}\right) = -1$, that is, $b^2 = 4$. Since $b > 0$, we obtain $b = 2$, and the hyperbola is $x^2 - y^2 = 4$, with asymptotes $y = \pm x$.
