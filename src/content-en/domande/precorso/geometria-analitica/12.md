The equation $4x^2 - y^2 - 8x - 4y - 4 = 0$ represents a hyperbola. What are its vertices?

- $(1, 0)$ and $(1, -4)$
- $(-1, -2)$ and $(3, -2)$
- $(0, -2)$ and $(2, -2)$
- $(0, 2)$ and $(2, 2)$

## Explanation

We factor $4$ out of the terms in $x$ and $-1$ out of the terms in $y$, then complete the squares:
$$
\begin{aligned}
4(x^2 - 2x) - (y^2 + 4y) - 4 &= 0 \\[4pt]
4(x - 1)^2 - 4 - (y + 2)^2 + 4 - 4 &= 0 \\[4pt]
4(x - 1)^2 - (y + 2)^2 &= 4 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Dividing by $4$ we obtain the standard form $(x - 1)^2 - \dfrac{(y + 2)^2}{4} = 1$, therefore the hyperbola has center $(1, -2)$, semi-axes $a = 1$ and $b = 2$, and the positive term is the one in $x$. So the vertices lie on the horizontal line $y = -2$, at distance $a = 1$ from the center, and they are $(0, -2)$ and $(2, -2)$.
