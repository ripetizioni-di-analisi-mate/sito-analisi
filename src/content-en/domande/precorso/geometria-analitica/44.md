What does the equation $x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 4y + 5 = 0$ represent?

- The circle with center $(-1, 2)$ and radius $\sqrt{5}$
- Only the point $(-1, 2)$
- The circle with center $(1, -2)$ and radius $\sqrt{5}$
- No point of the plane

## Explanation

Completing the squares we have $x^2 + 2x = (x + 1)^2 - 1$ and $y^2 - 4y = (y - 2)^2 - 4$, therefore the equation becomes
$$
(x + 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 1 + 4 - 5 = 0 .
$$
A sum of two squares is zero if and only if both squares are zero, that is if and only if
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 = 0 \\
y - 2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Therefore the equation is satisfied only by the point $(-1, 2)$ and represents a **degenerate case**.
