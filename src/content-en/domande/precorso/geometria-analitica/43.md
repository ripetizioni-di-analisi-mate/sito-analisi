The two asymptotes of the hyperbola $\dfrac{(x - 1)^2}{4} - \dfrac{(y + 2)^2}{9} = 1$ meet at a point. Which point is it, and what slopes do the asymptotes have?

- The point $(0, 0)$; slopes $\pm\dfrac{3}{2}$
- The point $(1, -2)$; slopes $\pm\dfrac{2}{3}$
- The point $(-1, 2)$; slopes $\pm\dfrac{3}{2}$
- The point $(1, -2)$; slopes $\pm\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The equation is that of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$ with $x - 1$ and $y + 2$ in place of $x$ and $y$, so it represents the translated standard hyperbola, with center $(1, -2)$. The standard hyperbola has $a = 2$, $b = 3$ and asymptotes $y = \pm\dfrac{3}{2}x$, which meet at the origin. The translation carries the origin to the center $(1, -2)$ and preserves the slopes. Therefore the asymptotes are $y + 2 = \pm\dfrac{3}{2}(x - 1)$, they meet at the point $(1, -2)$ and have slopes $\pm\dfrac{3}{2}$.
