Which of the following lines is perpendicular to the line $2x + 3y - 1 = 0$?

- $3x - 2y + 5 = 0$
- $2x + 3y + 5 = 0$
- $3x + 2y + 1 = 0$
- $2x - 3y = 0$

## Explanation

The given line has slope $m_1 = -\dfrac{a}{b} = -\dfrac{2}{3}$. Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if $m_1 \, m_2 = -1$, therefore the required line has slope $m_2 = \dfrac{3}{2}$. The line $3x - 2y + 5 = 0$ has slope $-\dfrac{3}{-2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$, so it is perpendicular to the given line. The line $2x + 3y + 5 = 0$ has slope $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ and is parallel to the given line; the lines $3x + 2y + 1 = 0$ and $2x - 3y = 0$ have slopes $-\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$, and their product with $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ is different from $-1$.
