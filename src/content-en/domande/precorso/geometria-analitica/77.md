How are the circles $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ and $(x - 3)^2 + y^2 = 1$ positioned with respect to each other?

- They intersect at two points
- Each lies outside the other, with no common points
- They are externally tangent
- They are internally tangent

## Explanation

The first circle has center $O(0, 0)$ and radius $2$, the second has center $C(3, 0)$ and radius $1$. The distance between the centers is $\overline{OC} = 3$, equal to the sum of the radii $2 + 1$. So the two circles have in common only the point of the segment $OC$ at distance $2$ from $O$ and $1$ from $C$, that is, $(2, 0)$, and each lies outside the other. Therefore the circles are **externally tangent** at $(2, 0)$.
