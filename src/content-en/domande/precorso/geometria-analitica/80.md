At which points does the ellipse $\dfrac{(x + 1)^2}{9} + \dfrac{(y - 2)^2}{4} = 1$ meet the line $y = 2$?

- $(-2, 2)$ and $(4, 2)$
- $(-10, 2)$ and $(8, 2)$
- $(-3, 2)$ and $(1, 2)$
- $(-4, 2)$ and $(2, 2)$

## Explanation

Substituting $y = 2$ into the equation we obtain $\dfrac{(x + 1)^2}{9} = 1$, that is, $(x + 1)^2 = 9$. So $x + 1 = 3$ or $x + 1 = -3$, that is, $x = 2$ or $x = -4$. Therefore the intersection points are $(-4, 2)$ and $(2, 2)$. The line $y = 2$ passes through the center $(-1, 2)$ of the ellipse, and the two points found are the vertices on the horizontal axis, at distance $\sqrt{9} = 3$ from the center.
