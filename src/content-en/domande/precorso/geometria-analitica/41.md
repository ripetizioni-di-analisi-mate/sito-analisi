What are the vertices of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - y^2 = -1$?

- $(\pm 2, 0)$
- $(0, \pm 2)$
- $(\pm 1, 0)$
- $(0, \pm 1)$

## Explanation

The equation has the form $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = -1$ with $a^2 = 4$ and $b^2 = 1$, therefore it represents a hyperbola with foci on the $y$-axis. Setting $y = 0$ we obtain $\dfrac{x^2}{4} = -1$, which has no real solutions, so the hyperbola does not intersect the $x$-axis. Setting $x = 0$ we obtain $-y^2 = -1$, that is $y = \pm 1$. Therefore the vertices are $(0, \pm 1)$, that is $(0, \pm b)$.
