Do the lines $r\colon y = 2x + 1$ and $s\colon 4x - 2y + 5 = 0$ have any points in common?

- Infinitely many, because they coincide
- Exactly one, and the lines are perpendicular
- Exactly one, and the lines are not perpendicular
- None, because they are parallel and distinct

## Explanation

Solving the equation of $s$ for $y$ we obtain $2y = 4x + 5$, that is, $y = 2x + \dfrac{5}{2}$. So $r$ and $s$ have the same slope $2$, and therefore they are parallel. The $y$-intercepts are $1$ and $\dfrac{5}{2}$, which are different, so the lines are distinct. Two parallel and distinct lines **have no points in common**.
