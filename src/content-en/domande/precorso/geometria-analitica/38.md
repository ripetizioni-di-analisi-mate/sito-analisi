A square with sides parallel to the coordinate axes is inscribed in the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{12} = 1$, that is, its four vertices lie on the ellipse. What is the length of its side?

- $\sqrt{3}$
- $2\sqrt{3}$
- $4$
- $2\sqrt{6}$

## Explanation

The ellipse is symmetric with respect to both axes, so the vertices of an inscribed rectangle with sides parallel to the axes are the points $(\pm t, \pm s)$, with $t > 0$ and $s > 0$. The sides have lengths $2t$ and $2s$, therefore the rectangle is a square if and only if $s = t$, that is, if the vertex $(t, t)$ lies on the bisector $y = x$. Requiring that $(t, t)$ lie on the ellipse we obtain
$$
\frac{t^2}{4} + \frac{t^2}{12} = 1 ,
$$
that is, $\dfrac{4t^2}{12} = 1$, so $t^2 = 3$ and $t = \sqrt{3}$. Therefore the side of the square has length $2t = 2\sqrt{3}$.
