For which values of $x$ is the expression $\sqrt{\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 2}}$ defined?

- $x < -2 \ \vee \ x \geq 1$
- $x \leq -2 \ \vee \ x \geq 1$
- $-2 < x \leq 1$
- $x \geq 1$

## Explanation

The root is defined when the radicand is not negative, so we solve the inequality $\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 2} \geq 0$. The numerator is positive for $x > 1$ and the denominator is positive for $x > -2$. The quotient is positive when the numerator and the denominator have the same sign, that is, for $x < -2$ or for $x > 1$, and it equals $0$ for $x = 1$. The value $x = -2$ is excluded, because it makes the denominator zero. Therefore the expression is defined for $x < -2$ or $x \geq 1$.
