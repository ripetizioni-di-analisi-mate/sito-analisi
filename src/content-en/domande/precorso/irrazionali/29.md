What are the solutions of the inequality $\sqrt{4 - x} \leq 1$?

- $x \geq 3$
- $3 \leq x \leq 4$
- $x \leq 3$
- $3 < x < 4$

## Explanation

The inequality has the form $\sqrt{A} \leq B$ with $B = 1$. The condition $1 \geq 0$ is true for every $x$, so the system used to solve the inequality reduces to
$$
\begin{cases}
4 - x \geq 0 \\
4 - x \leq 1
\end{cases}
$$
The first condition gives $x \leq 4$. The second gives $-x \leq -3$, and changing the sign of both sides reverses the direction, so $x \geq 3$. The endpoints are included, because at $x = 4$ the root equals $0$ and at $x = 3$ it equals $1$. Therefore the solution is $3 \leq x \leq 4$.
