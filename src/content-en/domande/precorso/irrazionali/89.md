Taking the factor $x^2$ out of the root, which expression is equal to $\sqrt{x^2 - x^3}$ on its whole domain?

- $x\sqrt{1 - x}$
- $x^2\sqrt{1 - x}$
- $-x\sqrt{1 - x}$
- $|x|\sqrt{1 - x}$

## Explanation

The radicand factors as $x^2 - x^3 = x^2(1 - x)$. Since $x^2 \geq 0$, the radicand is nonnegative for $1 - x \geq 0$ or for $x = 0$, so the domain is $x \leq 1$. On the domain the factors $x^2$ and $1 - x$ are nonnegative, therefore $\sqrt{x^2(1 - x)} = \sqrt{x^2} \cdot \sqrt{1 - x}$. For every real number we have $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$, so $\sqrt{x^2 - x^3} = |x|\sqrt{1 - x}$. The domain also contains negative numbers; for example, at $x = -3$ we have $\sqrt{9 + 27} = 6 = |-3| \cdot \sqrt{4}$, while $x\sqrt{1 - x}$ equals $-6$. For $0 < x < 1$, instead, the expression $-x\sqrt{1 - x}$ is negative and cannot be equal to a root.
