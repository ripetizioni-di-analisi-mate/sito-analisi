The equation $x - 5\sqrt{x} + 6 = 0$ is solved with the substitution $t = \sqrt{x}$. What are its solutions?

- $x = \sqrt{2} \ \vee \ x = \sqrt{3}$
- $x = 2 \ \vee \ x = 3$
- $x = 4 \ \vee \ x = 9$
- $x = 9$

## Explanation

The root is defined for $x \geq 0$, and for these values we have $x = (\sqrt{x})^2 = t^2$ with $t \geq 0$. The equation becomes $t^2 - 5t + 6 = 0$, that is, $(t - 2)(t - 3) = 0$, with solutions $t = 2$ and $t = 3$. Both are nonnegative, so they are accepted. From $\sqrt{x} = 2$ it follows that $x = 4$, and from $\sqrt{x} = 3$ it follows that $x = 9$. Therefore the solutions are $x = 4$ and $x = 9$.
