At which points does the expression $\sqrt{x^2 - 4}$ take strictly positive values?

- $x < -2 \ \vee \ x > 2$
- $x \leq -2 \ \vee \ x \geq 2$
- $x > 2$
- $-2 < x < 2$

## Explanation

The root is defined where $x^2 - 4 \geq 0$, and it is positive where the radicand is positive, because $\sqrt{A} = 0$ only for $A = 0$. So we solve $x^2 - 4 > 0$, that is, $(x - 2)(x + 2) > 0$. The coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, therefore the trinomial is positive outside the interval between its roots. The solution is $x < -2$ or $x > 2$; at $x = \pm 2$ the root exists and equals $0$.
