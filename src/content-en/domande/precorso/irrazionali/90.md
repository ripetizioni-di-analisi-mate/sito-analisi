For which values of $k$ does the equation $\sqrt{9 - x^2} = k$ have exactly two solutions?

- $0 < k < 3$
- $0 \leq k < 3$
- $-3 < k < 3$
- $0 \leq k \leq 3$

## Explanation

The left-hand side is nonnegative, so for $k < 0$ the equation has no solutions. For $k \geq 0$ the equation is equivalent to $9 - x^2 = k^2$, that is, $x^2 = 9 - k^2$. For $0 \leq k < 3$ the number $9 - k^2$ is positive, so the solutions are the two values $x = -\sqrt{9 - k^2}$ and $x = \sqrt{9 - k^2}$. For $k = 3$ there is the single solution $x = 0$, and for $k > 3$ there are no solutions. Therefore the equation has two solutions for $0 \leq k < 3$; for $k = 0$ they are $x = -3$ and $x = 3$, the endpoints of the semicircle.
