For which values of $x$ does the product $\sqrt{x} \cdot \sqrt{x + 3}$ equal $2$?

- $x = -4$
- $x = -4 \ \vee \ x = 1$
- $x = -1 \ \vee \ x = 4$
- $x = 1$

## Explanation

The product is defined where both roots are defined, that is, for $x \geq 0$, and for these values we have $\sqrt{x} \cdot \sqrt{x + 3} = \sqrt{x(x + 3)}$. The equation becomes $\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} = 2$, which, with a positive right-hand side, is equivalent to $x^2 + 3x = 4$. The equation $x^2 + 3x - 4 = 0$ factors as $(x + 4)(x - 1) = 0$, with solutions $x = -4$ and $x = 1$. The value $x = -4$ does not satisfy $x \geq 0$, because $\sqrt{-4}$ is not defined, so it is discarded. Therefore the only solution is $x = 1$, and indeed $\sqrt{1} \cdot \sqrt{4} = 2$.
