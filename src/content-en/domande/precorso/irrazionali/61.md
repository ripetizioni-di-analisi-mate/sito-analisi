For which $x$ does the function $f(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}{x - 1}$ satisfy $f(x) \geq 0$?

- $1 < x \leq 2$
- $x = -2 \ \vee \ 1 < x \leq 2$
- $x = -2 \ \vee \ 1 \leq x \leq 2$
- $x > 1$

## Explanation

The function is defined where $4 - x^2 \geq 0$ and $x \neq 1$, that is, for $-2 \leq x \leq 2$ with $x \neq 1$. The numerator is nonnegative and equals $0$ only at $x = -2$ and at $x = 2$. At these two points $f$ equals $0$, so the inequality is satisfied. At the other points of the domain the numerator is positive, therefore $f$ has the sign of $x - 1$ and is positive for $1 < x < 2$. Hence $f(x) \geq 0$ for $x = -2$ or $1 < x \leq 2$.
