At which abscissas do the graphs of $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = \sqrt[3]{x}$ intersect, that is, for which $x$ do we have $\sqrt{x} = \sqrt[3]{x}$?

- $x = 1$
- $x = 0 \ \vee \ x = 1$
- $x = -1 \ \vee \ x = 0 \ \vee \ x = 1$
- $x = 0$

## Explanation

The square root requires $x \geq 0$, while the cube root is defined for every $x$; so the equation makes sense for $x \geq 0$. For $x \geq 0$ both sides are nonnegative, therefore raising to the sixth power preserves the equality and gives $x^3 = x^2$. Hence $x^2(x - 1) = 0$, which has solutions $x = 0$ and $x = 1$, both nonnegative. The two graphs therefore intersect at the points $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 1)$.
