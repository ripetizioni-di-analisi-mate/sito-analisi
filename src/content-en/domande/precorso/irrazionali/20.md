What are the solutions of the equation $\sqrt{2x - 3} = 1 - x$?

- $x = 2$
- The equation has no solutions
- $x = 1$
- $x = -2$

## Explanation

The equation has the form $\sqrt{A} = B$, so it is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
1 - x \geq 0 \\
2x - 3 = (1 - x)^2
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation becomes $x^2 - 4x + 4 = 0$, that is, $(x - 2)^2 = 0$, with the single root $x = 2$. For $x = 2$ the right-hand side equals $-1 < 0$, so the condition $1 - x \geq 0$ is not satisfied and the value is discarded. Therefore the equation **has no solutions**. The same conclusion follows from the observation that the root is defined only for $x \geq \dfrac{3}{2}$, while the right-hand side is nonnegative only for $x \leq 1$.
