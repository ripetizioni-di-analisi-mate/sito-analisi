The two roots in the equation $\sqrt{2x - 1} = \sqrt{1 - 2x}$ have opposite radicands. Which real number satisfies the equation?

- $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- No real number
- $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x = 0$

## Explanation

The equation has the form $\sqrt{A} = \sqrt{B}$, so it is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x - 1 \geq 0 \\
2x - 1 = 1 - 2x
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation gives $4x = 2$, that is, $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. For this value both radicands equal $0$, so the condition is satisfied. Therefore the solution is $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which is also the only point where both roots are defined.
