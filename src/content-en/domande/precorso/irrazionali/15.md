For which values of $x$ is the expression $\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{4 - x}$ defined?

- $x \geq -1$
- $x \leq 4$
- $-1 < x < 4$
- $-1 \leq x \leq 4$

## Explanation

The sum is defined where both roots are defined, so the two radicands must be nonnegative at the same time.
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 \geq 0 \\
4 - x \geq 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first condition gives $x \geq -1$ and the second gives $x \leq 4$. The endpoints are included, because at $x = -1$ and at $x = 4$ one of the two radicands equals $0$ and its root exists. Therefore the expression is defined for $-1 \leq x \leq 4$.
