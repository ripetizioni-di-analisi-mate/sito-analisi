At which points is the square root of $x$ greater than $x$, that is, $\sqrt{x} > x$?

- $x < 1$
- $0 \leq x < 1$
- $x > 1$
- $0 < x < 1$

## Explanation

The root is defined for $x \geq 0$, and for these values the right-hand side $x$ is nonnegative. So only the system with a nonnegative right-hand side matters, and in it squaring preserves the direction.
$$
x > x^2
$$
The inequality $x^2 - x < 0$, that is, $x(x - 1) < 0$, is satisfied for $0 < x < 1$. At $x = 0$ and at $x = 1$ the two sides are equal, so the endpoints are excluded. Therefore the solution is $0 < x < 1$; for example $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{4}} = \dfrac{1}{2} > \dfrac{1}{4}$.
