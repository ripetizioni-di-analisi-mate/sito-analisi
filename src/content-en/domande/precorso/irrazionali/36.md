What are the solutions of the inequality $\sqrt{x^2 - 1} > x + 1$?

- $x \leq -1$
- $x \leq -1 \ \vee \ x \geq 1$
- $x < -1$
- $x > -1$

## Explanation

The inequality has the form $\sqrt{A} > B$, so it is equivalent to the union of two systems.
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 < 0 \\
x^2 - 1 \geq 0
\end{cases}
\ \cup \
\begin{cases}
x + 1 \geq 0 \\
x^2 - 1 > (x + 1)^2
\end{cases}
$$
For $x < -1$ we have $x^2 > 1$, so the first system gives $x < -1$. In the second, expanding $(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$, the inequality becomes $2x + 2 < 0$, that is, $x < -1$, which is incompatible with $x \geq -1$; so the second system has no solutions. The **union** of the two systems gives $x < -1$. At $x = -1$ both sides equal $0$, therefore the endpoint is excluded.
