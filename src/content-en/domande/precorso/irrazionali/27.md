Squaring both sides of the equation $\sqrt{x^2 + 3x - 1} = \sqrt{2x + 5}$ gives $x^2 + x - 6 = 0$, which has roots $x = 2$ and $x = -3$. Which statement is true?

- Both values are solutions of the equation
- Only $x = 2$ is a solution, because for $x = -3$ the radicands are negative
- Only $x = -3$ is a solution, because for $x = 2$ the radicands are different
- Neither value is a solution of the equation

## Explanation

The equation has the form $\sqrt{A} = \sqrt{B}$, so it is equivalent to the system formed by $x^2 + 3x - 1 = 2x + 5$ and the domain condition $2x + 5 \geq 0$. For $x = 2$ we have $2x + 5 = 9$ and $x^2 + 3x - 1 = 4 + 6 - 1 = 9$, so $x = 2$ is a solution. For $x = -3$ we have $2x + 5 = -1 < 0$, and the other radicand also equals $9 - 9 - 1 = -1$; so the condition is not satisfied and the roots are not defined. Therefore the equation has the **only solution** $x = 2$.
