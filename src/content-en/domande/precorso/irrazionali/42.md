For which real numbers does the expression $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{x - 3}}{x + 1}$ make sense?

- $x \neq -1$
- $x \geq 3$
- $x > -1$
- $x \neq 1$

## Explanation

The cube root, like every root of odd index, is defined for every real number, so the numerator imposes no conditions. The denominator must be different from $0$, therefore $x + 1 \neq 0$, that is, $x \neq -1$. Hence the expression makes sense for every $x \neq -1$; for example, for $x = 2$ it equals $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{-1}}{3} = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.
