What are the solutions of the inequality $\sqrt[3]{2x + 1} > -1$?

- $x > 0$
- $x > -1$
- $x \geq -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- Every real number $x$

## Explanation

The cube root is defined for every real number, so the inequality has no domain conditions. Since the function $y = x^3$ is increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$, cubing preserves the direction.
$$
2x + 1 > (-1)^3 = -1
$$
Therefore $2x > -2$, that is, $x > -1$. The condition $x \geq -\dfrac{1}{2}$ must not be imposed, because for $-1 < x < -\dfrac{1}{2}$ the radicand is negative and the cube root exists; for example, for $x = -\dfrac{3}{4}$ the radicand equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and its cube root is greater than $-1$.
