Where does the function $f(x) = (x + 3)\sqrt[3]{x - 1}$ take nonpositive values?

- $x = 1$
- $x \leq -3 \ \vee \ x \geq 1$
- $-3 \leq x \leq 1$
- $-1 \leq x \leq 3$

## Explanation

The cube root is defined for every real number, so $f$ is also defined for every $x$. Since cubing preserves order and $\sqrt[3]{0} = 0$, the cube root has the same sign as the radicand; therefore $\sqrt[3]{x - 1}$ is negative for $x < 1$, equals $0$ at $x = 1$ and is positive for $x > 1$. The factor $x + 3$ is negative for $x < -3$ and positive for $x > -3$. A product of two factors is negative where the factors have opposite signs, that is, for $-3 < x < 1$, and it equals $0$ at $x = -3$ and at $x = 1$. Hence $f(x) \leq 0$ for $-3 \leq x \leq 1$; for example $f(0) = 3 \cdot \sqrt[3]{-1} = -3$.
