Among the numbers $x \geq 0$, for which ones does the equality $\sqrt{x + 2} = \sqrt{x} + \sqrt{2}$ hold?

- Only for $x = 0$
- For every $x \geq 0$
- For no $x \geq 0$
- Only for $x = 2$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 0$ both sides are nonnegative, so squaring preserves the equality. The square of the right-hand side contains the cross term $2\sqrt{x}\,\sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{2x}$.
$$
x + 2 = x + 2\sqrt{2x} + 2
$$
Simplifying gives $2\sqrt{2x} = 0$, so $2x = 0$ and $x = 0$. Therefore the equality holds only for $x = 0$; for example, for $x = 2$ the left-hand side equals $2$ and the right-hand side equals $2\sqrt{2}$.
