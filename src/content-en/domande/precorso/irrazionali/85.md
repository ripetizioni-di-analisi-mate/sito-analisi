At which points is the cube root $\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 2}}$ less than $1$?

- $x \geq 1$
- $x < -2$
- $x > -2$
- Every $x \neq -2$

## Explanation

The cube root is defined for every real number, so it suffices that the radicand is defined, that is, $x \neq -2$. Cubing preserves the direction, therefore the inequality is equivalent to $\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 2} < 1$. Moving $1$ to the left-hand side we obtain
$$
\frac{x - 1}{x + 2} - 1 = \frac{x - 1 - (x + 2)}{x + 2} = \frac{-3}{x + 2} < 0 .
$$
The numerator is negative, so the quotient is negative where the denominator is positive, that is, for $x > -2$. Therefore the solution is $x > -2$. For $x < -2$ the radicand is greater than $1$; for example, at $x = -5$ it equals $2$ and $\sqrt[3]{2} > 1$.
