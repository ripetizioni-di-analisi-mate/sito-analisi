What are the solutions of the inequality $\sqrt{x^2 + x - 2} > 2$?

- $-3 < x < 2$
- $x < -3 \ \vee \ x > 2$
- $x > 2$
- $x \leq -2 \ \vee \ x \geq 1$

## Explanation

The inequality has the form $\sqrt{A} > B$ with $B = 2$. The system with $B < 0$ requires $2 < 0$, which is false, so only the system with $B \geq 0$ remains, in which squaring preserves the direction.
$$
x^2 + x - 2 > 4
$$
The inequality becomes $x^2 + x - 6 > 0$, that is, $(x + 3)(x - 2) > 0$, which is satisfied for $x < -3$ or $x > 2$. The domain condition need not be added, because it follows from $x^2 + x - 2 > 4$. Therefore the solutions are $x < -3$ or $x > 2$.
