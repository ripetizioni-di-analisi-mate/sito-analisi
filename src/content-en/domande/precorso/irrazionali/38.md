For which $x$ is the quotient $\dfrac{\sqrt{x + 3}}{\sqrt{x - 1}}$ greater than $2$?

- $x < \dfrac{7}{3}$
- $1 < x < \dfrac{7}{3}$
- $1 < x < 5$
- $1 \leq x < \dfrac{7}{3}$

## Explanation

The quotient is defined where both roots exist and the denominator is different from $0$, that is, for $x + 3 \geq 0$ and $x - 1 > 0$; so the domain is $x > 1$. For $x > 1$ the denominator $\sqrt{x - 1}$ is positive, therefore multiplying by it preserves the direction and the inequality is equivalent to $\sqrt{x + 3} > 2\sqrt{x - 1}$. Both sides are nonnegative, so squaring preserves the direction; the square of the right-hand side is $4(x - 1)$, because both the coefficient $2$ and the root are squared.
$$
x + 3 > 4x - 4
$$
The inequality becomes $3x < 7$, that is, $x < \dfrac{7}{3}$. Therefore the solution is $1 < x < \dfrac{7}{3}$; for example, at $x = 2$ the quotient equals $\sqrt{5} > 2$.
