The inequality $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} > 2$ has the root in the denominator. What is its solution set?

- $0 < x < \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $0 \leq x < \dfrac{1}{4}$
- $x > \dfrac{1}{4}$
- $0 < x < \dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

The left-hand side is defined for $x > 0$, because the root requires $x \geq 0$ and the denominator must be different from $0$. For $x > 0$ both sides are positive, so multiplying by the positive number $\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{2}$ the inequality is equivalent to $\sqrt{x} < \dfrac{1}{2}$. With both sides nonnegative, squaring preserves the direction, therefore $x < \dfrac{1}{4}$. Hence the solution is $0 < x < \dfrac{1}{4}$.
