Which of the following transformations takes the graph of $y = \sin x$ to the graph of $y = \cos x$?

- Translation by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the right
- Translation by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the left
- Translation by $\pi$ to the left
- Reflection in the $y$-axis

## Explanation

For $a > 0$ the graph of $y = f(x + a)$ is the graph of $y = f(x)$ translated by $a$ to the left.

By the sine addition formula
$$
\sin\left(x + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \sin x\cos\frac{\pi}{2} + \cos x\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = \cos x .
$$
Therefore the graph of the cosine is the graph of the sine translated by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the left.

The translation by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the right gives instead $y = \sin\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = -\cos x$, the translation by $\pi$ to the left gives $y = \sin(x + \pi) = -\sin x$, and the reflection in the $y$-axis gives $y = \sin(-x) = -\sin x$.
