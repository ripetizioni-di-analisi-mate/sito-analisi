Let $\alpha$ be the angle in the interval $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ with $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$. What are the solutions of the equation $\sin x = -\dfrac{3}{5}$, written in terms of $\alpha$?

- $x = \alpha + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi - \alpha + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = -\alpha + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi + \alpha + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pm\alpha + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = -\alpha + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi - \alpha + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Since $\sin(-x) = -\sin x$, the angle $-\alpha$ has sine $-\dfrac{3}{5}$.

The solutions of the elementary equation are therefore $x = -\alpha + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi - (-\alpha) + 2n\pi = \pi + \alpha + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Indeed, by the related-angle formulas, $\sin(\pi + \alpha) = -\sin\alpha = -\dfrac{3}{5}$.

The angles $\alpha$ and $\pi - \alpha$ have sine $\dfrac{3}{5}$, so they solve the equation $\sin x = \dfrac{3}{5}$. The family $\pm\alpha + 2n\pi$ has the form of the solutions of an equation in cosine and contains $\alpha$, whose sine is positive.
