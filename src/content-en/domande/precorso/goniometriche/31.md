What are the solutions of the equation $\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \cos x$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

By the identities for related angles, we have $\cos x = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x\right)$, so the equation becomes $\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x\right)$. The two alternatives are
$$
x + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{2} - x + 2n\pi \quad \text{oppure} \quad x + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{2} + x + 2n\pi .
$$
From the first $2x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, that is, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$. The second is equivalent to $2n\pi = -\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, which is not satisfied by any integer $n$.

Therefore the solutions are $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
