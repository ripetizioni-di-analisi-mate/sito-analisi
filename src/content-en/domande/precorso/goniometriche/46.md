The point $P$ in the figure lies on the unit circle and has coordinates $\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$. Which angle $x \in [0, 2\pi)$ determines $P$?

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" uguale=1 xmin=-1.4 xmax=1.4 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 segmenti="-0.5,0,-0.5,-0.866;0,-0.866,-0.5,-0.866;0,0,-0.5,-0.866" punti="-0.5,-0.866,pieno,P" legenda=0 titolo="The point $P$ on the unit circle"}

- $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The point $P$ has negative $x$- and $y$-coordinates, so it lies in the third quadrant and the angle is between $\pi$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$.

The point symmetric to $P$ with respect to the origin is $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$, which corresponds to the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, because $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Since the point symmetric to the point of angle $\alpha$ with respect to the origin is the point of angle $\pi + \alpha$, the required angle is $x = \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.

The angle $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ determines the point $\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, whose coordinates are swapped.
