Which of the following equalities holds for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$?

- $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\cos^2 x$
- $\sin 2x = 2\sin x$
- $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$
- $\sin x + \cos x = 1$

## Explanation

By the double-angle formula $\cos 2x = \cos^2 x - \sin^2 x$, and substituting $\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x$ gives $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$ for every $x$.

The other three equalities do not hold for every $x$. For $x = 0$ one has $\cos 0 = 1$, while $1 - 2\cos^2 0 = -1$; for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ one has $\sin\pi = 0$, while $2\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 2$; for $x = \pi$ one has $\sin\pi + \cos\pi = -1$.

The equality $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\cos^2 x$ has the opposite sign of the correct one, $\cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x - 1$.
