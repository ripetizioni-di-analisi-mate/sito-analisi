- $x = \arcsin\dfrac{3}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi - \arcsin\dfrac{3}{4} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \arcsin\dfrac{4}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi - \arcsin\dfrac{4}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- The equation has no solutions
- $x = -\arcsin\dfrac{3}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi + \arcsin\dfrac{3}{4} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Dividing both sides by $3$, the equation becomes $\sin x = \dfrac{4}{3}$.

The sine of an angle is the $y$-coordinate of a point of the unit circle, which has radius $1$, so $-1 \leq \sin x \leq 1$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

Since $\dfrac{4}{3} > 1$, no angle has sine equal to $\dfrac{4}{3}$, and therefore **the equation has no solutions**. For the same reason the expression $\arcsin\dfrac{4}{3}$ is not defined.
