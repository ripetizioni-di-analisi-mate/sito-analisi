For which values of the real number $k$ does the equation $\sin x = k$ have exactly one solution in the interval $[0, \pi)$?

- $k = 0$ or $k = 1$
- Only $k = 1$
- $0 \leq k \leq 1$
- $-1 \leq k \leq 1$

## Explanation

For $0 \leq x < \pi$ the point on the unit circle has nonnegative $y$-coordinate, so for $k < 0$ the equation has no solutions in the interval. For $k > 1$ it has no solutions in any interval.

For $0 < k < 1$ the solutions in the first turn are $\alpha$ and $\pi - \alpha$, with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and both belong to $[0, \pi)$. So in this case there are two solutions.

For $k = 1$ the two angles $\alpha$ and $\pi - \alpha$ coincide with $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which is the only solution. For $k = 0$ the solutions in $[0, \pi]$ are $0$ and $\pi$, but $\pi$ does not belong to the interval, so only $x = 0$ remains.

Therefore the equation has exactly one solution for $k = 0$ or $k = 1$.
