Setting $t = \tan x$, the equation $\tan^2 x - (1 + \sqrt{3})\tan x + \sqrt{3} = 0$ becomes quadratic in $t$. Which $x$ solve it?

- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Setting $t = \tan x$, the equation $t^2 - (1 + \sqrt{3})t + \sqrt{3} = 0$ has sum of the roots $1 + \sqrt{3}$ and product $\sqrt{3}$, so its roots are $t = 1$ and $t = \sqrt{3}$.

The tangent takes every real value, so both roots are acceptable. From $\tan x = 1$ it follows that $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$, and from $\tan x = \sqrt{3}$ it follows that $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$.

The step of both families is $n\pi$, because the tangent has period $\pi$. The angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ has tangent $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$.
