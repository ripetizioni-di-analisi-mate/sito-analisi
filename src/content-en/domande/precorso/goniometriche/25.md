What are the solutions of the equation $\sin x = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$?

- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Since $\sin(-x) = -\sin x$, the angle $\alpha = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ has sine equal to $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

The other point of the unit circle with the same ordinate corresponds to the angle $\pi - \alpha = \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$.

The sine has period $2\pi$, so the solutions are $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. The family $x = \pm\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ is that of the equation $\cos x = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
