For which $x \in \mathbb{R}$ is the sine equal to $-1$?

- $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pi + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The sine of $x$ is the $y$-coordinate of the point on the unit circle determined by $x$, and the $y$-coordinate equals $-1$ only at the point $(0, -1)$. This point corresponds to the angle $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, so the solutions are $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

In this case the two families $\alpha + 2n\pi$ and $\pi - \alpha + 2n\pi$ coincide, because with $\alpha = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ one has $\pi - \alpha = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which differs from $\alpha$ by $2\pi$.

The family with step $n\pi$ also contains $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, where the sine equals $1$. At the points $x = \pi + 2n\pi$ it is the cosine that equals $-1$.
