An angle $x$ has tangent $3$. What is $\sin 2x$?

- $\dfrac{3}{10}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{9}{10}$

## Explanation

Since $\tan x$ is defined, $\cos x \neq 0$, and dividing the fundamental identity by $\cos^2 x$ gives $\tan^2 x + 1 = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$. Therefore $\cos^2 x = \dfrac{1}{1 + 9} = \dfrac{1}{10}$.

From the double-angle formula and from $\sin x = \tan x \cos x$ it follows that
$$
\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x = 2\tan x\cos^2 x = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \frac{1}{10} = \frac{3}{5} .
$$
The result does not depend on the quadrant. Indeed, an angle with positive tangent lies in the first or in the third quadrant, and in both sine and cosine have the same sign, so their product is positive.

The value $\dfrac{3}{10}$ is $\sin x\cos x$, that is, it comes from forgetting the factor $2$ in the double-angle formula; the value $\dfrac{9}{10}$ is $\sin^2 x$.
