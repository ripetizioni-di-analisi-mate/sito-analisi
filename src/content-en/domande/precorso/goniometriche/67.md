Using the fundamental identity and the substitution $t = \cos x$, which equation does $\sin^2 x + \cos x + 1 = 0$ become?

- $t^2 + t = 0$
- $t^2 - t - 1 = 0$
- $t^2 + t + 2 = 0$
- $t^2 - t - 2 = 0$

## Explanation

From the fundamental identity $\sin^2 x = 1 - \cos^2 x$, so the equation becomes $1 - t^2 + t + 1 = 0$, that is, $-t^2 + t + 2 = 0$. Multiplying by $-1$ gives $t^2 - t - 2 = 0$.

The roots are $t = 2$ and $t = -1$. The root $t = 2$ must be discarded, because the cosine does not exceed $1$, and from $\cos x = -1$ it follows that $x = \pi + 2n\pi$.

The equation $t^2 + t = 0$ comes from writing $\sin^2 x = \cos^2 x - 1$, with the wrong sign.
