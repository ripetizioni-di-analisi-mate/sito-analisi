What are the solutions of the equation $\sin^2 x + 2\sin x\cos x - 3\cos^2 x = 0$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = \arctan 3 + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = \arctan 3 + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = -\arctan 3 + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The equation is homogeneous of second degree in sine and cosine. If $\cos x = 0$, then $\sin^2 x = 1$ and the left-hand side equals $1 \neq 0$, so one can divide by $\cos^2 x$:
$$
\tan^2 x + 2\tan x - 3 = 0 .
$$
Setting $t = \tan x$, we have $t^2 + 2t - 3 = (t - 1)(t + 3)$, so the roots are $t = 1$ and $t = -3$.

From $\tan x = 1$ it follows that $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$. From $\tan x = -3$ it follows that $x = \arctan(-3) + n\pi = -\arctan 3 + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
