- $x = n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{n\pi}{4}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Two angles have the same cosine if and only if they coincide or are opposite, up to multiples of $2\pi$. Hence $\cos 3x = \cos x$ is equivalent to
$$
3x = x + 2n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad 3x = -x + 2n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$
The first alternative gives $2x = 2n\pi$, that is, $x = n\pi$; the second gives $4x = 2n\pi$, that is, $x = \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$.

Every integer multiple of $\pi$ is also an integer multiple of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the first family is contained in the second and the solutions are $x = \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
