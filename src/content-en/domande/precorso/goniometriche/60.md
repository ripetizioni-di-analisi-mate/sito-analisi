We want to write $\sqrt{3}\sin x + \cos x = 2\sin(x + \varphi)$ for every $x$, with $0 \leq \varphi < 2\pi$. What is $\varphi$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$

## Explanation

In the auxiliary angle method with $a = \sqrt{3}$ and $b = 1$ one has $R = \sqrt{3 + 1} = 2$, and the angle $\varphi$ is determined by the system
$$
\begin{cases}
\cos\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[8pt]
\sin\varphi = \dfrac{1}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
Cosine and sine are both positive, so $\varphi$ lies in the first quadrant, and from the table of special values $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

The angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ has cosine $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and sine $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and corresponds to the expression $\sin x + \sqrt{3}\cos x$.
