For which $x$ is the product $\sin x\cos x$ equal to zero?

- $x = n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

A product is zero if and only if at least one factor is zero. The sine vanishes for $x = n\pi$ and the cosine for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$.

The union of the two families consists of the integer multiples of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which determine the points $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(-1, 0)$ and $(0, -1)$ of the unit circle. Therefore the solutions are $x = \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

At the points $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$ the product equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$ or $-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
