- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{2n\pi}{3}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{12} + \dfrac{2n\pi}{3}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{12} + \dfrac{2n\pi}{3}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Two angles have the same sine if and only if they coincide or are supplementary, up to multiples of $2\pi$. Hence the equation is equivalent to
$$
2x = x + \frac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad 2x = \pi - x - \frac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$
The first alternative gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$. The second gives $3x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$, and dividing the term $2n\pi$ by $3$ as well yields $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{2n\pi}{3}$.

For $n$ a multiple of $3$ the second family gives exactly $\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2m\pi$, with $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, so the first family is contained in the second and the solutions are $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{2n\pi}{3}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
