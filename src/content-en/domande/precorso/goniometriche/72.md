Where is the fraction $\dfrac{1}{2\sin x - 1}$ defined?

- $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x \neq \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ and $x \neq \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ and $x \neq \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The fraction is defined when the denominator is different from $0$, that is, for $\sin x \neq \dfrac{1}{2}$.

The angles with the same sine as $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ are $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and its supplement $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, up to multiples of $2\pi$. So the equation $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ has the solutions $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$.

Therefore the domain consists of the real numbers different from all these values. The angle $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ has sine $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, so it does not make the denominator zero.
