What are the solutions of the equation $\cos\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = 0$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The cosine equals $0$ at the points $(0, 1)$ and $(0, -1)$ of the unit circle, which are half a turn apart and correspond to the angles $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$.

So the equation is equivalent to $x - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$. Adding $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to both sides gives
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{\pi}{6} + n\pi = \frac{2\pi}{3} + n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$
The result $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$ is obtained by subtracting $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ instead of adding it.
