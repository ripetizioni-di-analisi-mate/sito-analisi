How many solutions does the equation $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ have in the interval $[0, 4\pi]$?

- $2$
- $0$
- $4$
- $1$

## Explanation

Since $0 < \dfrac{1}{3} < 1$, in each period the line $y = \dfrac{1}{3}$ meets the sine curve at two points. In the first period they are the angles $\alpha = \arcsin\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\pi - \alpha$.

The interval $[0, 4\pi]$ contains two periods, and at the endpoints the sine equals $0$, so the endpoints are not solutions. The solutions are therefore $\alpha$, $\pi - \alpha$, $\alpha + 2\pi$ and $3\pi - \alpha$, four in all.

The angle $\alpha$ exists because $\dfrac{1}{3}$ lies between $-1$ and $1$, even though it does not appear in the table of special values.
