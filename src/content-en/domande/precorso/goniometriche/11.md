- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < x < \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- $0 \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ or $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} \leq x < 2\pi$
- $0 \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ or $\dfrac{7\pi}{4} \leq x < 2\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \leq x \leq \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

On $[0, 2\pi)$ the equation $\sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ has the two solutions $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.

The two corresponding points divide the unit circle into two arcs. The upper arc, from $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ to $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, consists of the points with $y$-coordinate greater than $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

The inequality is non-strict, so the endpoints $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, where the sine equals exactly $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, are also solutions. Therefore the solutions on $[0, 2\pi)$ are $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \leq x \leq \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.
