What are the solutions of the equation $\sin 2x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

First solve the elementary equation in the angle $2x$:
$$
2x = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad 2x = \frac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$
Dividing all terms by $2$, including $2n\pi$, gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$.

The families with step $2n\pi$ exclude, for example, $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, which is a solution because $\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{3} = \sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
