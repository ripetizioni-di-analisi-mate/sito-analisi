What are the solutions of the equation $2\sin^2 x + \sin x = 0$?

- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = n\pi$, $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = n\pi$, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = 2n\pi$, $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Factoring out $\sin x$, the equation becomes $\sin x\,(2\sin x + 1) = 0$, and a product is zero if and only if at least one factor is zero.

The sine equals $0$ at the points $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$ of the unit circle, so $\sin x = 0$ gives $x = n\pi$.

From $2\sin x + 1 = 0$ it follows that $\sin x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, with the angles $\alpha = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\pi - \alpha = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$. Therefore the solutions are $x = n\pi$, $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$. Dividing by $\sin x$ would lose the solutions $x = n\pi$.
