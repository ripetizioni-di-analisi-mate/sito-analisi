What are the solutions of the equation $\cos 3x = \cos\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{8} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Two angles have the same cosine if and only if they coincide or are negatives of each other, up to multiples of $2\pi$. So the equation is equivalent to
$$
3x = x + \frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi \quad \text{oppure} \quad 3x = -x - \frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$
From the first $2x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$, that is, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$. From the second $4x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$, that is, $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$.

The second family does not contain $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, because $\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi}{8} = \dfrac{3\pi}{8}$ is not a multiple of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the solutions are given by both families.
