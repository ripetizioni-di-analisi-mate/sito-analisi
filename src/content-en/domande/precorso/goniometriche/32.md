In the interval $[0, 2\pi)$, which angles satisfy $\sin 2x = \sin x$?

- $\left\{\dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{5\pi}{3}\right\}$
- $\left\{0, \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \pi\right\}$
- $\left\{0, \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \pi, \dfrac{5\pi}{3}\right\}$
- $\left\{0, \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \pi, \dfrac{11\pi}{6}\right\}$

## Explanation

With the double-angle formula the equation becomes $2\sin x\cos x - \sin x = 0$, and factoring out $\sin x$ gives
$$
\sin x\,(2\cos x - 1) = 0 .
$$
A product is zero if and only if at least one factor is zero. In $[0, 2\pi)$ the sine equals $0$ for $x = 0$ and $x = \pi$, while $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$.

Therefore the solutions are $0$, $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\pi$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$. Dividing by $\sin x$ would lose the solutions $0$ and $\pi$.
