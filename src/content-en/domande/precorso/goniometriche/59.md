In $[0, \pi]$, which angles satisfy $4\sin^2 x = 3$?

- $\left\{\dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right\}$
- $\left\{\dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \dfrac{4\pi}{3}, \dfrac{5\pi}{3}\right\}$
- $\left\{\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right\}$
- $\left\{\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right\}$

## Explanation

The equation is equivalent to $\sin^2 x = \dfrac{3}{4}$, that is, to $\sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ or $\sin x = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

For $0 \leq x \leq \pi$ the sine is greater than or equal to $0$, so the second equation has no solutions in the interval. The first one has the solutions $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

The angles $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$ solve the equation, but they do not belong to the interval $[0, \pi]$.
