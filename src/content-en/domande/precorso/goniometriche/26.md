What are the solutions of the equation $\sqrt{3}\tan x + 1 = 0$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The equation is equivalent to $\tan x = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

From the table of special values $\tan\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$. Since $\sin(-x) = -\sin x$ and $\cos(-x) = \cos x$, we have $\tan(-x) = -\tan x$, so $\alpha = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ has tangent $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

The tangent has period $\pi$, so the solutions are $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. The family with step $2n\pi$ excludes, for example, $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, which is a solution.
