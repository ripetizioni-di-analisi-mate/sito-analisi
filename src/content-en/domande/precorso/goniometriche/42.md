What are the solutions on $[0, 2\pi)$ of the inequality $\cos x \leq \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$?

- $0 \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ or $\dfrac{11\pi}{6} \leq x < 2\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6} \leq x \leq \dfrac{11\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6} < x < \dfrac{11\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6} \leq x \leq \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$

## Explanation

On $[0, 2\pi)$ the equation $\cos x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ has the solutions $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $2\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{11\pi}{6}$.

The two points divide the unit circle into two arcs. The points with abscissa less than $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ form the arc that goes from $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$ passing through the angle $\pi$.

The inequality is non-strict, so the endpoints are also solutions. Therefore the solutions on $[0, 2\pi)$ are $\dfrac{\pi}{6} \leq x \leq \dfrac{11\pi}{6}$.
