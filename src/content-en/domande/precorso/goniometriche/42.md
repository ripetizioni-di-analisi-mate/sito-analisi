For which $x \in [0, 2\pi)$ does the inequality $\sin x > \cos x$ hold?

- $0 \leq x < \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ or $\dfrac{5\pi}{4} < x < 2\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < x < \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \leq x \leq \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

Sine and cosine are equal at the points of the unit circle lying on the bisector $y = x$, that is, in $[0, 2\pi)$, for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$. These two points divide the circle into two arcs.

The inequality $\sin x > \cos x$ says that the point has $y$-coordinate greater than its $x$-coordinate, that is, it lies above the bisector $y = x$. Above the bisector lies the arc from $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ to $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ through $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\pi$.

Therefore the solutions are $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, with the endpoints excluded because the inequality is strict.
