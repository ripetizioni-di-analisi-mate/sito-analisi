What are the solutions of the equation $2\sin^2 x + 3\cos x - 3 = 0$?

- $x = n\pi$ or $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = 2n\pi$ or $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pi + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = 2n\pi$ or $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

By the Pythagorean identity $\sin^2 x = 1 - \cos^2 x$, so the equation becomes $2 - 2\cos^2 x + 3\cos x - 3 = 0$, that is,
$$
2\cos^2 x - 3\cos x + 1 = 0 .
$$
Setting $t = \cos x$, the roots of $2t^2 - 3t + 1 = 0$ are $t = \dfrac{3 \pm 1}{4}$, that is, $t = 1$ and $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$, both **acceptable**.

The cosine equals $1$ only at the point $(1, 0)$, so $\cos x = 1$ gives $x = 2n\pi$. From $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ it follows that $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
