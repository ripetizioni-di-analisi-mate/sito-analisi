What is the simplified form of $\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) - \sin\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$?

- $\sqrt{3}\cos x$
- $\cos x$
- $\sqrt{3}\sin x$
- $0$

## Explanation

By the sine addition and subtraction formulas
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\left(x + \frac{\pi}{3}\right) &= \sin x\cos\frac{\pi}{3} + \cos x\sin\frac{\pi}{3}, \\[6pt]
\sin\left(x - \frac{\pi}{3}\right) &= \sin x\cos\frac{\pi}{3} - \cos x\sin\frac{\pi}{3} .
\end{aligned}
$$
In the difference the terms with $\sin x$ cancel, so what remains is $2\cos x\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = 2 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x = \sqrt{3}\cos x$.

The result $\cos x$ comes from using $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which is instead the value of $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
