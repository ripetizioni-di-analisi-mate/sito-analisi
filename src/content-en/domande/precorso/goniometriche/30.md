For which $x$ does $\cos x = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{5}$ hold?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{5} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{5} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{5} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{4\pi}{5} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{5} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Two angles have the same cosine if and only if they coincide or are opposite, up to multiples of $2\pi$. So the equation is equivalent to
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{5} + 2n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad x = -\frac{\pi}{5} + 2n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} ,
$$
and the two families are written together as $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{5} + 2n\pi$.

The angle $\dfrac{4\pi}{5}$ is supplementary to $\dfrac{\pi}{5}$, so it has the same sine and the opposite cosine, because $\cos(\pi - x) = -\cos x$. The family with step $n\pi$ contains $\dfrac{6\pi}{5}$, where the cosine equals $-\cos\dfrac{\pi}{5}$.
