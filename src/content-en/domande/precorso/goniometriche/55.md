If $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{4}$, what is $\cos 2x$?

- $\dfrac{7}{8}$
- $-\dfrac{7}{8}$
- $\dfrac{15}{16}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The double-angle formula in the form $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$ contains only the sine, so
$$
\cos 2x = 1 - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{16} = \frac{7}{8} .
$$
The result does not depend on the quadrant of $x$, because the formula involves $\sin^2 x$.

The value $-\dfrac{7}{8}$ comes from the form $1 - 2\cos^2 x$, which has the wrong sign, and $\dfrac{15}{16}$ is $1 - \sin^2 x$, that is, $\cos^2 x$.
