- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

Moving $\sqrt{3}$ to the right-hand side and dividing by $2$, the equation becomes $\cos x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

From the table of special values, the angle $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ has cosine equal to $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

The points of the unit circle with $x$-coordinate $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ are symmetric with respect to the $x$-axis, so they correspond to the angles $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$.

Since the cosine has period $2\pi$, the solutions are $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
