What are the solutions of the equation $\cos 2x + \cos x = 0$?

- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = 2n\pi$ or $x = \pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

With the double-angle formula $\cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x - 1$ the equation contains only the cosine and becomes
$$
2\cos^2 x + \cos x - 1 = 0 .
$$
Setting $t = \cos x$, the roots of $2t^2 + t - 1 = 0$ are $t = \dfrac{-1 \pm 3}{4}$, that is, $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $t = -1$, both acceptable.

From $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ it follows that $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$. The cosine equals $-1$ only at the point $(-1, 0)$, so $\cos x = -1$ gives $x = \pi + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
