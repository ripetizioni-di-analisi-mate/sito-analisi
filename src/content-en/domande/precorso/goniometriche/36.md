How many angles in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ solve the equation $2\sin^2 x - \sin x - 1 = 0$?

- $3$
- $4$
- $2$
- $1$

## Explanation

Setting $t = \sin x$, the equation becomes $2t^2 - t - 1 = 0$, with the roots $t = \dfrac{1 \pm 3}{4}$, that is, $t = 1$ and $t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Both are acceptable, because they lie between $-1$ and $1$.

The sine equals $1$ only at the point $(0, 1)$ of the unit circle, so in $[0, 2\pi)$ the equation $\sin x = 1$ has the single solution $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

The equation $\sin x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ has the two solutions $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$ in $[0, 2\pi)$. Therefore the angles that solve the equation are $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$, that is, $3$.

The number $4$ comes from giving two solutions to the equation $\sin x = 1$ as well, whereas it determines a single point of the circle.
