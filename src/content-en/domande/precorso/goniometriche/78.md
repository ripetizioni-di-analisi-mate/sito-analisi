The figure shows the graph of a function $y = A\sin(kx)$, with $A > 0$ and $k > 0$. The graph passes through the origin, has a maximum at the point $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}, 3\right)$ and meets the $x$-axis again at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. What are $A$ and $k$?

::grafico{f="3*sin(2*x)" xmin=-3.3 xmax=3.3 ymin=-3.8 ymax=3.8 segmenti="0.7854,0,0.7854,3;0,3,0.7854,3" punti="0.7854,3,pieno;1.5708,0,pieno" etichette="0.45,-0.6,π/4;1.75,0.45,π/2" legenda=0 titolo="The graph of $y = A\sin(kx)$"}

- $A = 3$ and $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $A = 3$ and $k = 1$
- $A = 6$ and $k = 2$
- $A = 3$ and $k = 2$

## Explanation

The maximum value of $A\sin(kx)$ is $A$, because the sine is at most $1$, so the maximum $3$ gives $A = 3$.

Between the consecutive zeros $0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ there is a single maximum, so the interval $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$ is half a period and the period is $\pi$. Since the period of $A\sin(kx)$ is $\dfrac{2\pi}{k}$, from $\dfrac{2\pi}{k} = \pi$ it follows that $k = 2$.

The value $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$ comes from setting the period equal to $2\pi k$ instead of $\dfrac{2\pi}{k}$, and $k = 1$ from giving the sine period $\pi$. With $A = 6$ the amplitude is confused with the distance between maximum and minimum.
