What is $\cos\left(-\dfrac{10\pi}{3}\right)$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Since $\cos(-x) = \cos x$, one has $\cos\left(-\dfrac{10\pi}{3}\right) = \cos\dfrac{10\pi}{3}$.

The cosine has period $2\pi$ and $\dfrac{10\pi}{3} = 2\pi + \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, so $\cos\dfrac{10\pi}{3} = \cos\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.

The angle $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} = \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ lies in the third quadrant, and from the formula $\cos(\pi + x) = -\cos x$ it follows that $\cos\dfrac{4\pi}{3} = -\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
