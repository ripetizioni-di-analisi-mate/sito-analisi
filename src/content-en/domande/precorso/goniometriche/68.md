In the equation $2\sin^2 x - \sin x\cos x - \cos^2 x = 0$, are the $x$ with $\cos x = 0$ solutions?

- No, because at them the left-hand side equals $2$
- Yes, because every term contains $\cos x$
- No, because at them the left-hand side equals $-1$
- Yes, but only those with $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$

## Explanation

If $\cos x = 0$, the fundamental identity gives $\sin^2 x = 1$. Substituting, the left-hand side equals $2 \cdot 1 - 0 - 0 = 2$, which is different from $0$.

So the points with $\cos x = 0$ are not solutions, and the equation can be divided by $\cos^2 x$ without losing solutions. This gives $2\tan^2 x - \tan x - 1 = 0$, with the roots $\tan x = 1$ and $\tan x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The term $2\sin^2 x$ does not contain the cosine, so it does not vanish when $\cos x = 0$.
