- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = -\arctan\dfrac{1}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = \arctan 3 + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = \arctan\dfrac{1}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \arctan\dfrac{1}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The equation is homogeneous of second degree in sine and cosine. If $\cos x = 0$, then $\sin^2 x = 1$ and the left-hand side equals $3 \neq 0$, so the points with $\cos x = 0$ are not solutions and one can divide by $\cos^2 x$:
$$
3\tan^2 x - 4\tan x + 1 = 0 .
$$
Setting $t = \tan x$, the roots of $3t^2 - 4t + 1 = 0$ are $t = \dfrac{4 \pm 2}{6}$, that is, $t = 1$ and $t = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

The tangent has period $\pi$, so from $\tan x = 1$ it follows that $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ and from $\tan x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ it follows that $x = \arctan\dfrac{1}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
