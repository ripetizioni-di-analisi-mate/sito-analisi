An angle $x$ satisfies $\sin x + \cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. What is $\sin 2x$?

- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{8}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Squaring both sides gives
$$
\sin^2 x + 2\sin x\cos x + \cos^2 x = \frac{1}{4} .
$$
By the fundamental identity $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$, and by the double-angle formula $2\sin x\cos x = \sin 2x$. Therefore $1 + \sin 2x = \dfrac{1}{4}$, that is, $\sin 2x = -\dfrac{3}{4}$.

The value $-\dfrac{3}{8}$ is $\sin x\cos x$, and it comes from writing $\sin 2x = \sin x\cos x$, without the factor $2$.
