The point $P$ of the unit circle is determined by the angle $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$. The line $OP$, where $O$ is the origin, meets the line tangent to the circle at $(1, 0)$ at the point $T$. What is the $y$-coordinate of $T$?

- $-1$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\sqrt{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

The angle $\dfrac{5\pi}{4} = \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ lies in the third quadrant, and by the related-angle formulas $P = \left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$.

The coordinates of $P$ are equal, so the line $OP$ is the bisector $y = x$. The line tangent to the circle at $(1, 0)$ is $x = 1$, so $T = (1, 1)$ and the $y$-coordinate of $T$ is $1$, that is, $\tan\dfrac{5\pi}{4} = 1$.

The point $T$ has positive $y$-coordinate even though $P$ lies in the third quadrant, because $T$ and $P$ lie on the line $OP$ on opposite sides of the origin.

The value $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ is the $y$-coordinate of $P$, and $\sqrt{2}$ is the length of the segment $OT$.
