- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

From the table of special values, the angle $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ has sine equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

On the unit circle there are two points with $y$-coordinate $\dfrac{1}{2}$, symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis, and they correspond to the angles $\alpha$ and $\pi - \alpha = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

The sine has period $2\pi$, so $2n\pi$ is added to each of the two angles. The solutions are therefore
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$
The family $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ belongs to the equation $\cos x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and indeed $\sin\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
