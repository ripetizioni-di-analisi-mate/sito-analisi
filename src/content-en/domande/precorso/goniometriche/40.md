What are the solutions of the equation $\sqrt{3}\sin x - \cos x = 1$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

In the equation $a\sin x + b\cos x = c$ we have $a = \sqrt{3}$ and $b = -1$, so $R = \sqrt{3 + 1} = 2$. The angle with $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin\varphi = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

With the auxiliary angle method the equation becomes $2\sin\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = 1$, that is, $\sin\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore
$$
x - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad x - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$
Adding $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ gives the solutions $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \pi + 2n\pi$.
