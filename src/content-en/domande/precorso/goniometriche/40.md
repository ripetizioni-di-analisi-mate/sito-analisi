What are the solutions of the equation $\sin x\cos x - \sqrt{3}\cos^2 x = 0$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Explanation

The equation is homogeneous of second degree in sine and cosine, with the coefficient of $\sin^2 x$ equal to $0$. Therefore the points with $\cos x = 0$ are solutions, and factoring out $\cos x$ gives
$$
\cos x\,(\sin x - \sqrt{3}\cos x) = 0 .
$$
From $\cos x = 0$ it follows that $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$.

If $\cos x = 0$, then $\sin x = \pm 1$ and the second factor does not vanish, so in the second factor one can divide by $\cos x$. This gives $\tan x = \sqrt{3}$, that is, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Dividing the original equation by $\cos^2 x$ would have left only $\tan x = \sqrt{3}$, and therefore would have lost the solutions $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$.
