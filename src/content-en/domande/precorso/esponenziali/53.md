Which transformation takes the graph of $y = 3^{x}$ to the graph of $y = \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{x}$?

- The reflection across the $x$-axis
- The reflection across the line $y = x$
- The reflection through the origin
- The reflection across the $y$-axis

## Explanation

By the laws of exponents $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{x} = 3^{-x}$, so the value of $y = \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{x}$ at $x$ equals the value of $y = 3^{x}$ at $-x$. Therefore the point $(x_0, y_0)$ of the graph of $y = 3^{x}$ corresponds to the point $(-x_0, y_0)$ of the second graph, and the two graphs are **symmetric** with respect to the $y$-axis. The reflection across the $x$-axis would lead to the graph of $y = -3^{x}$, the reflection across the line $y = x$ to the graph of $y = \log_3 x$, and the reflection through the origin to the graph of $y = -3^{-x}$.
