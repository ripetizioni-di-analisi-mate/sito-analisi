For which values of the real parameter $k$ is the inequality $9^{x} - 2 \cdot 3^{x+1} + k \geq 0$ satisfied for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$?

- $k \geq 9$
- $k > 9$
- $k \geq 1$
- $k \geq 0$

## Explanation

Since $9^{x} = \left(3^{x}\right)^{2}$ and $3^{x+1} = 3 \cdot 3^{x}$, with the substitution $t = 3^{x}$ the inequality becomes $t^{2} - 6t + k \geq 0$. As $x$ varies in $\mathbb{R}$, the variable $t$ takes every positive value, so the inequality in $t$ must hold for every $t > 0$. Since $t^{2} - 6t + k = (t - 3)^{2} + k - 9$, the minimum value of the trinomial for $t > 0$ is $k - 9$, attained at $t = 3$. Therefore the inequality holds for every $t > 0$ if and only if $k - 9 \geq 0$, that is, for $k \geq 9$.
