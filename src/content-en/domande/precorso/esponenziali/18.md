How many solutions does the equation $2^{x} = 3 - x$ have?

- Exactly one, $x = 1$
- Exactly one, $x = 2$
- Two
- None

## Explanation

The equation is equivalent to $2^{x} + x - 3 = 0$. The function $2^{x}$ is strictly increasing, because the base $2$ is greater than $1$, and $x - 3$ is strictly increasing as well. Then the function $2^{x} + x - 3$, a sum of two strictly increasing functions, is strictly increasing, so it vanishes at most at one point. For $x = 1$ we have $2^{1} = 2$ and $3 - 1 = 2$, therefore $x = 1$ is a solution. Hence the equation has **exactly one solution**, $x = 1$.
