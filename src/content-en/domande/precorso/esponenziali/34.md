The equation $2^{x} - 3 \cdot 2^{\frac{x}{2}} + 2 = 0$ has two real solutions. Which ones?

- $x = 0$ and $x = 2$
- $x = 0$ and $x = 1$
- $x = 1$ and $x = 2$
- $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

By the power-of-a-power rule $2^{x} = \left(2^{\frac{x}{2}}\right)^{2}$, so with the substitution $t = 2^{\frac{x}{2}}$, $t > 0$, the equation becomes $t^{2} - 3t + 2 = 0$. Its solutions are $t = 1$ and $t = 2$, both positive and therefore acceptable. From $2^{\frac{x}{2}} = 1 = 2^{0}$ it follows that $\dfrac{x}{2} = 0$, that is, $x = 0$. From $2^{\frac{x}{2}} = 2$ it follows that $\dfrac{x}{2} = 1$, that is, $x = 2$.
