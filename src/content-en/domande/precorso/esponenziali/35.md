What are the solutions of the equation $\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^{x} - 3 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x} - 4 = 0$?

- $x = 2$
- $x = 4$
- $x = -2$
- $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

Since $\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^{x} = \left(\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}\right)^{2}$, with the substitution $t = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$, $t > 0$, the equation becomes $t^{2} - 3t - 4 = 0$. We have $t^{2} - 3t - 4 = (t - 4)(t + 1)$, so $t = 4$ or $t = -1$, and the value $t = -1$ is discarded. What remains is $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x} = 4$. Since $4 = 2^{2} = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-2}$, by the **equality of powers with the same base** the solution is $x = -2$.
