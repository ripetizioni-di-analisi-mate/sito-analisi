Which statement about the function $f(x) = \left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^{x}$ is true?

- The graph passes through $\left(0, \dfrac{1}{5}\right)$ and $f$ is decreasing
- The graph passes through $(0, 1)$ and $f$ is increasing
- The graph passes through $(1, 0)$ and $f$ is decreasing
- The graph passes through $(0, 1)$ and $f$ is decreasing

## Explanation

Since $a^{0} = 1$ for every base, we have $f(0) = \left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^{0} = 1$, so the graph passes through the point $(0, 1)$. The point $(1, 0)$ does not belong to the graph, because $f(1) = \dfrac{1}{5}$. The base $\dfrac{1}{5}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, therefore the function is **strictly decreasing**.
