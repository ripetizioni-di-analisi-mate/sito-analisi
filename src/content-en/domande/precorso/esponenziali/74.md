What are the zeros of the function $f(x) = x \cdot 2^{x} - 4x$?

- $x = 2$
- $x = 0$ and $x = 4$
- $x = 0$
- $x = 0$ and $x = 2$

## Explanation

Factoring out the common factor $x$, the equation becomes $x\left(2^{x} - 4\right) = 0$. By the zero-product property, $x = 0$ or $2^{x} - 4 = 0$. The second equation can be written as $2^{x} = 2^{2}$ and, by the equality of powers with the same base, gives $x = 2$. Therefore the solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.
