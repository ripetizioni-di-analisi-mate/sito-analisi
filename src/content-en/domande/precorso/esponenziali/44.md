What are the solutions of the inequality $\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^{x} - 5 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x} + 4 < 0$?

- $0 < x < 2$
- $-2 < x < 0$
- $1 < x < 4$
- $x < -2$ or $x > 0$

## Explanation

Since $\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^{x} = \left(\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}\right)^{2}$, with the substitution $t = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$, $t > 0$, the inequality becomes $t^{2} - 5t + 4 < 0$. The trinomial has roots $t = 1$ and $t = 4$ and is negative between the roots, so $1 < t < 4$, an interval already contained in $t > 0$. Since $1 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{0}$ and $4 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-2}$, we return to the variable $x$ with
$$
\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{0} < \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} < \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-2} .
$$
The base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, therefore the direction **is reversed** and the solutions are $-2 < x < 0$.
