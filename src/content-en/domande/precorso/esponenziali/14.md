For which values of the real parameter $k$ does the equation $2^{x} = k - 3$ have at least one solution?

- $k > 3$
- $k \geq 3$
- $k > 0$
- $k \neq 3$

## Explanation

Since $2^{x} > 0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the equation can have solutions only if the right-hand side is positive, that is, if $k - 3 > 0$. In this case the equation has the solution $x = \log_2 (k - 3)$. For $k = 3$ the right-hand side equals $0$ and the equation $2^{x} = 0$ has no solutions, therefore the value $k = 3$ is excluded. Hence the equation has a solution for $k > 3$.
