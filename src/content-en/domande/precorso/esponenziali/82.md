The equation $4^{x} - 2^{x+1} + k = 0$ depends on the real parameter $k$. For which $k$ does it have two distinct solutions?

- $k < 1$
- $0 < k < 1$
- $0 < k < \dfrac{1}{4}$
- $0 \leq k < 1$

## Explanation

Since $4^{x} = \left(2^{x}\right)^{2}$ and $2^{x+1} = 2 \cdot 2^{x}$, with the substitution $t = 2^{x}$, $t > 0$, the equation becomes $t^{2} - 2t + k = 0$. Each positive value of $t$ corresponds to exactly one $x$, so the exponential equation has two distinct solutions if and only if the equation in $t$ has two distinct positive solutions. The discriminant is $4 - 4k$, and the sum of the roots is $2 > 0$, therefore the two roots are positive if and only if their product $k$ is positive. The conditions to be satisfied are therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
4 - 4k > 0 \\
k > 0
\end{cases}
$$
and they give $0 < k < 1$.
