Which values of $x$ make the expression $2^{2x+1} - 5 \cdot 2^{x} + 2$ equal to zero?

- $x = 2$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x = \log_2 \dfrac{5 - \sqrt{17}}{2}$ and $x = \log_2 \dfrac{5 + \sqrt{17}}{2}$
- $x = -1$ and $x = 1$
- The equation has no solutions

## Explanation

By the laws of exponents $2^{2x+1} = 2 \cdot 2^{2x} = 2 \left(2^{x}\right)^{2}$. With the substitution $t = 2^{x}$, $t > 0$, the equation becomes
$$
2t^{2} - 5t + 2 = 0 ,
$$
which has discriminant $25 - 16 = 9$ and solutions $t = \dfrac{5 \pm 3}{4}$, that is, $t = 2$ and $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Both are positive and therefore acceptable. From $2^{x} = 2$ it follows that $x = 1$, and from $2^{x} = \dfrac{1}{2} = 2^{-1}$ it follows that $x = -1$.
