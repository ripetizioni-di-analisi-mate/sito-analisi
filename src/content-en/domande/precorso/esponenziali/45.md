What are the solutions of the inequality $2^{x} + 2^{-x} < 2$?

- $x < 0$
- $x \neq 0$
- $x > 0$
- The inequality has no solutions

## Explanation

With the substitution $t = 2^{x}$, $t > 0$, since $2^{-x} = \dfrac{1}{t}$, the inequality becomes $t + \dfrac{1}{t} < 2$. Multiplying both sides by $t$, which is positive, preserves the direction and gives $t^{2} - 2t + 1 < 0$, that is, $(t - 1)^{2} < 0$. A square is always greater than or equal to $0$, so no value of $t$ satisfies the inequality. Therefore the inequality **has no solutions**.
