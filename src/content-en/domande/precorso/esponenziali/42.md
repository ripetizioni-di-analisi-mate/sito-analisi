What are the solutions of the inequality $4^{x} - 2^{x} - 2 > 0$?

- $x > 1$
- $x < -1$ or $x > 1$
- $x < 0$ or $x > 1$
- $x > 2$

## Explanation

Since $4^{x} = (2^{x})^{2}$, with the substitution $t = 2^{x}$, $t > 0$, the inequality becomes $t^{2} - t - 2 > 0$. The trinomial has roots $t = -1$ and $t = 2$ and is positive outside the roots, so $t < -1$ or $t > 2$. No value $t < -1$ satisfies $t > 0$, therefore the intersection with $t > 0$ is $t > 2$. From $2^{x} > 2$, with base greater than $1$, we get $x > 1$.
