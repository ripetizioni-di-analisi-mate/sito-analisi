At which points does the graph of $f(x) = 2^{x} - 4$ intersect the coordinate axes?

- $(0, -4)$ and $(2, 0)$
- $(0, 1)$ and $(2, 0)$
- $(0, -3)$ and $(2, 0)$
- $(0, -3)$ and $(4, 0)$

## Explanation

The intersection with the $y$-axis has abscissa $0$, and $f(0) = 2^{0} - 4 = 1 - 4 = -3$, so it is the point $(0, -3)$. The intersection with the $x$-axis has ordinate $0$, so its abscissa solves $2^{x} - 4 = 0$, that is, $2^{x} = 2^{2}$, which gives $x = 2$. Therefore the intersection points are $(0, -3)$ and $(2, 0)$.
