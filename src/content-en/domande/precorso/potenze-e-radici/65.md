The sum $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2}} + \sqrt{8}$ can be reduced to a single term. Which number is it equal to?

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{17}{2}}$
- $3\sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{4}$

## Explanation

By the property of the root of a quotient $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, and rationalizing we obtain $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. Since $8 = 4 \cdot 2$, the factor $\sqrt{4} = 2$ is taken out, and $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$. The two terms are like radicals, so their coefficients are added, and
$$
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 2\sqrt{2} = \left(\frac{1}{2} + 2\right)\sqrt{2} = \frac{5\sqrt{2}}{2} .
$$
A sum of roots is in general different from the root of the sum, therefore the result is different from $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2} + 8} = \sqrt{\dfrac{17}{2}}$.
