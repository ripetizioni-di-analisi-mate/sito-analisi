The figure shows the graphs of the functions $y = a^{x}$ and $y = b^{x}$, where $a$ and $b$ are positive bases different from $1$. What can be deduced about the two bases?

::grafico{f="2^x;0.4^x" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=8 legenda="y = a^x;y = b^x" punti="0,1,pieno" titolo="The graphs of $y = a^x$ and of $y = b^x$, which meet at the point $(0, 1)$"}

- $0 < a < 1$ and $b > 1$
- $a > 1$ and $b > 1$
- $a > 1$ and $0 < b < 1$
- $0 < a < 1$ and $0 < b < 1$

## Explanation

The graph of $y = a^{x}$ rises from left to right, so the power $a^{x}$ increases as the exponent increases, and this happens for a base $a > 1$. The graph of $y = b^{x}$ falls, therefore $b^{x}$ decreases as the exponent increases, and this happens for a base $0 < b < 1$.

Both graphs pass through the point $(0, 1)$, because $a^{0} = b^{0} = 1$, and lie above the $x$-axis, because a power with positive base is positive.
