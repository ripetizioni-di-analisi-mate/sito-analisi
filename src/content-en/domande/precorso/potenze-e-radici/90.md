The property $\sqrt{a}\,\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$ holds for $a, b \geq 0$. For which real $x$ are both sides of the equality $\sqrt{x} \cdot \sqrt{x - 1} = \sqrt{x(x - 1)}$ defined and equal?

- $x \leq 0$ or $x \geq 1$
- $x \geq 1$
- For every real $x$
- $x > 1$

## Explanation

The left-hand side requires both roots to exist, so
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
x - 1 \geq 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x \geq 1 .
$$
For $x \geq 1$ the numbers $x$ and $x - 1$ are greater than or equal to zero, therefore the property applies and the two sides are equal; for $x = 1$ both equal $0$.

The right-hand side also exists for $x \leq 0$, where $x(x - 1) \geq 0$, but at those points the left-hand side is not defined. For example, for $x = -1$ the root $\sqrt{(-1)(-2)} = \sqrt{2}$ exists, while $\sqrt{-1}$ and $\sqrt{-2}$ do not exist in $\mathbb{R}$. Hence the equality holds for $x \geq 1$.
