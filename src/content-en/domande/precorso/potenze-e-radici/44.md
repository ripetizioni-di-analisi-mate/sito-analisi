Which relation holds between $2\sqrt{3}$ and $3\sqrt{2}$?

- $2\sqrt{3} > 3\sqrt{2}$, because $\sqrt{3} > \sqrt{2}$
- $2\sqrt{3} = 3\sqrt{2}$, because $2 \cdot 3 = 3 \cdot 2$
- $2\sqrt{3} < 3\sqrt{2}$, because $2\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{12}$ and $3\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{18}$
- $2\sqrt{3} > 3\sqrt{2}$, because $2\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{12}$ and $3\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{6}$

## Explanation

For $k, a \geq 0$ the property $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a} \, \sqrt{b}$ gives $k\sqrt{a} = \sqrt{k^2} \, \sqrt{a} = \sqrt{k^2 a}$, so a positive coefficient is brought under the square root sign by squaring it. Therefore $2\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} = \sqrt{12}$ and $3\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{9 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{18}$. Roots with the same index are compared through their radicands, and $12 < 18$, so $2\sqrt{3} < 3\sqrt{2}$.

Comparing only the roots $\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{2}$ neglects the coefficients $2$ and $3$, which are different.
