- $\sqrt{6} + 2$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - 2}{2}$
- $\sqrt{6} - 2$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - 2}{5}$

## Explanation

The denominator is the binomial $\sqrt{6} + 2$, so the numerator and the denominator are multiplied by the **conjugate binomial** $\sqrt{6} - 2$. The product in the denominator is a sum times a difference, therefore it equals the difference of the squares
$$
\left(\sqrt{6} + 2\right)\left(\sqrt{6} - 2\right) = 6 - 4 = 2 .
$$
Then
$$
\frac{2}{\sqrt{6} + 2} = \frac{2\left(\sqrt{6} - 2\right)}{2} = \sqrt{6} - 2 .
$$
The second square is $2^2 = 4$; with $2$ in place of $4$ the denominator would equal $4$ and the result would be $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - 2}{2}$.
