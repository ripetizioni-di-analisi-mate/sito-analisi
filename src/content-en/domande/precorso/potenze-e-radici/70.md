For $x \geq 0$ the difference $\sqrt{x + 1} - \sqrt{x}$ can be written as a fraction, by multiplying and dividing by the conjugate binomial. Which fraction is obtained?

- $\dfrac{2x + 1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x}}$
- $\dfrac{-1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2x + 1}}$

## Explanation

We multiply and divide by the conjugate binomial $\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x}$, which is positive. The product of a difference and the sum of the same terms is the difference of squares, so
$$
\left(\sqrt{x + 1} - \sqrt{x}\right)\left(\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x}\right) = (x + 1) - x = 1 .
$$
Therefore
$$
\sqrt{x + 1} - \sqrt{x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x}} .
$$
This form moves the roots to the denominator and is useful in limits involving differences of roots.
