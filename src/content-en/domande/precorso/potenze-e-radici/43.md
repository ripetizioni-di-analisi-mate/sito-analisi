Which of the following inequalities is true?

- $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-2} < \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}$
- $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{5} < \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{3}$
- $3^{-2} > 2^{-2}$
- $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} > \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}$

## Explanation

With base $0 < a < 1$ the power decreases as the exponent increases. Since $5 > 3$, it follows that $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^5 < \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^3$, so this inequality is **true**.

For the same reason $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-2} = 4$ is greater than $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$, and $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} < \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}$, because $\dfrac{1}{2} > \dfrac{1}{4}$. With a negative exponent the inequality between the bases is reversed, therefore $3^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{9}$ is less than $2^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
