For $x \geq 0$ and $x \neq 1$, the fraction $\dfrac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x} - 1}$ can be simplified. Which expression is it equal to?

- $\sqrt{x} - 1$
- $\sqrt{x} + 1$
- $x + 1$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 1}$

## Explanation

For $x \geq 0$ we have $x = \left(\sqrt{x}\right)^{2}$, so the numerator is a difference of squares and factors as $x - 1 = \left(\sqrt{x} - 1\right)\left(\sqrt{x} + 1\right)$. Since $x \neq 1$, the factor $\sqrt{x} - 1$ is different from zero and cancels, therefore
$$
\frac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x} - 1} = \frac{\left(\sqrt{x} - 1\right)\left(\sqrt{x} + 1\right)}{\sqrt{x} - 1} = \sqrt{x} + 1 .
$$
The same result is reached by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate binomial $\sqrt{x} + 1$, because $\left(\sqrt{x} - 1\right)\left(\sqrt{x} + 1\right) = x - 1$.
