Let $p = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$ and $q = \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}$, two powers with different bases and exponents. Which relation holds between $p$ and $q$?

- $p < q$
- $p = q$
- $p > q$
- $q < 1 < p$

## Explanation

The two powers are brought to the same exponent by raising them to the sixth power, because $6$ is the least common multiple of $3$ and $2$. By the power of a power
$$
p^{6} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2} = \frac{1}{4}, \qquad q^{6} = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{3} = \frac{1}{27} .
$$
The numbers $p$ and $q$ are positive, and by the comparison of powers with the same positive exponent they are in the same order as their sixth powers. Since $\dfrac{1}{4} > \dfrac{1}{27}$, it follows that $p > q$.

Both numbers are less than $1$, because they are powers with positive exponent of bases less than $1$.
