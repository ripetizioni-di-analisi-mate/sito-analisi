For which real $x$ is the expression $\dfrac{\sqrt[4]{x + 2}}{x - 1}$ defined?

- $x > -2$ and $x \neq 1$
- $x \geq -2$
- $x \geq -2$ and $x \neq 1$
- $x \neq 1$

## Explanation

The root $\sqrt[4]{x + 2}$ has an even index, so it requires $x + 2 \geq 0$. The denominator must be nonzero, therefore $x - 1 \neq 0$. The two conditions must hold together, so they form a system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2 \geq 0 \\
x - 1 \neq 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x \geq -2 \ \text{e} \ x \neq 1 .
$$
The endpoint $-2$ is included, because for $x = -2$ the numerator equals $\sqrt[4]{0} = 0$ and the denominator equals $-3$.
