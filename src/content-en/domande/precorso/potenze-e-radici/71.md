We look for the real number $k$ for which the fraction $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k} - 2}$ is equal to $\sqrt{k} + 2$. What is $k$?

- $3$
- $-3$
- $25$
- $5$

## Explanation

For $k \geq 0$ and $k \neq 4$ both sides are defined, and the equality is equivalent to $\left(\sqrt{k} - 2\right)\left(\sqrt{k} + 2\right) = 1$. The product of a binomial and its conjugate is the difference of squares, so $\left(\sqrt{k} - 2\right)\left(\sqrt{k} + 2\right) = k - 4$. Hence $k - 4 = 1$, that is, $k = 5$, which satisfies the conditions $k \geq 0$ and $k \neq 4$.

Subtracting the squares in reverse order one would get $4 - k = 1$, that is, $k = 3$, and with the sum of the squares one would get $k + 4 = 1$.
