Which of the following expressions is defined for every real $x$?

- $\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}$
- $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1}$
- $\sqrt[4]{x^{3}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{x}}$

## Explanation

For every real $x$ we have $x^{2} \geq 0$, so $x^{2} + 1 \geq 1$. The radicand of $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1}$ is therefore positive for every $x$, and the square root is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

The other three expressions are undefined at some point at least. For $x = 0$ the radicand $x^{2} - 1$ equals $-1$, so $\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}$ is not defined, because the square root requires the radicand to be greater than or equal to zero. For $x = -1$ the radicand $x^{3}$ equals $-1$, and the root $\sqrt[4]{x^{3}}$ has an even index, therefore it is not defined. The cube root $\sqrt[3]{x}$ exists for every real $x$ and equals $0$ for $x = 0$, so at that point the fraction $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{x}}$ has a zero denominator.
