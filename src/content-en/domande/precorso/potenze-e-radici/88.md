The figure shows the graph of a function, symmetric with respect to the line $x = 1$, with vertex at the point $(1, 0)$ and passing through $(-1, 2)$ and $(3, 2)$. Which function is represented?

::grafico{f="abs(x-1)" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=5 uguale=1 legenda=0 punti="1,0,pieno;-1,2,pieno;3,2,pieno" titolo="The graph of the function, with vertex at $(1, 0)$"}

- $y = x - 1$
- $y = \left(\sqrt{x - 1}\right)^{2}$
- $y = \sqrt{x^{2}} - 1$
- $y = \sqrt{(x - 1)^{2}}$

## Explanation

By the square root of a square $\sqrt{(x - 1)^{2}} = |x - 1|$, which equals $x - 1$ for $x \geq 1$ and $1 - x$ for $x < 1$. The graph therefore consists of two half-lines starting from the point $(1, 0)$, and it passes through $(-1, 2)$ and $(3, 2)$, like the one in the figure.

The function $y = \left(\sqrt{x - 1}\right)^{2}$ is defined only for $x \geq 1$, where it coincides with $y = x - 1$, while $y = \sqrt{x^{2}} - 1 = |x| - 1$ has its vertex at the point $(0, -1)$.
