What is the value of $\sqrt{\left(1 - \sqrt{3}\right)^2}$?

- $\sqrt{3} - 1$
- $1 - \sqrt{3}$
- $1 + \sqrt{3}$
- $4 - 2\sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

For the square root of a square we have $\sqrt{a^2} = |a|$, so $\sqrt{\left(1 - \sqrt{3}\right)^2} = \left|1 - \sqrt{3}\right|$. Since $\sqrt{3} > 1$, the number $1 - \sqrt{3}$ is negative, therefore its absolute value is the opposite $\sqrt{3} - 1$.

The number $1 - \sqrt{3}$ is negative, while the square root is by definition greater than or equal to zero; so $1 - \sqrt{3}$ is ruled out as the value of the expression.
