For which value of $x$ does the equality $\sqrt[3]{4} = 2^{x}$ hold?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

Since $4 = 2^2$, we have $\sqrt[3]{4} = \sqrt[3]{2^2}$. By the definition of a power with rational exponent, $\sqrt[n]{a^m} = a^{\frac{m}{n}}$, so $\sqrt[3]{2^2} = 2^{\frac{2}{3}}$. Therefore the equality holds for $x = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

In the fraction of the exponent the numerator is the exponent of the radicand and the denominator is the index of the root; swapping them would give $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
