What is the rationalized form of $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{8}}$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt[4]{8}}{8}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt[4]{8}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt[4]{2}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt[4]{2}}{8}$

## Explanation

Since $8 = 2^3$, the denominator is $\sqrt[4]{2^3}$. The factor that completes the fourth power under the root is $\sqrt[4]{2}$, because $\sqrt[4]{2^3} \cdot \sqrt[4]{2} = \sqrt[4]{2^4} = 2$. Hence
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{8}} = \frac{\sqrt[4]{2}}{\sqrt[4]{2^3} \cdot \sqrt[4]{2}} = \frac{\sqrt[4]{2}}{2} .
$$
Multiplying by $\sqrt[4]{8}$ the denominator would become $\sqrt[4]{64}$, which is still irrational, because $64$ is not the fourth power of an integer.
