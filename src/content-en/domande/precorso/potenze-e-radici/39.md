What is the rationalized form of $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} - 1}$?

- $3 + \sqrt{3}$
- $\dfrac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3 - \sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{4}$

## Explanation

The denominator is the binomial $\sqrt{3} - 1$, so numerator and denominator are multiplied by the **conjugate binomial** $\sqrt{3} + 1$. The product in the denominator is a sum times a difference, therefore it equals the difference of squares $\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^2 - 1^2 = 3 - 1 = 2$. In the numerator $\sqrt{3}\left(\sqrt{3} + 1\right) = 3 + \sqrt{3}$, so
$$
\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \frac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{2} .
$$
With the sum $3 + 1$ in place of the difference of squares the denominator would be $4$.
