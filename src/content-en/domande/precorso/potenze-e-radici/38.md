What is the rationalized form of $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}}$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}}{4}$
- $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\left(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}\right)}{5}$
- $\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

The denominator is the binomial $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$, so numerator and denominator are multiplied by the **conjugate binomial** $\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}$. The product in the denominator is a sum times a difference, therefore it equals the difference of squares
$$
\left(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}\right)\left(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}\right) = 7 - 3 = 4 .
$$
The factor $4$ in the denominator cancels with the numerator, so
$$
\frac{4}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}} = \frac{4\left(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}\right)}{4} = \sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3} .
$$
With the sum $7 + 3$ in place of the difference of squares the denominator would be $10$, giving $\dfrac{2\left(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}\right)}{5}$.
