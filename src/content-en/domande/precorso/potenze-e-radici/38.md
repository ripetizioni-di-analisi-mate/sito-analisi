To rationalize $\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt{2} + 1}$, numerator and denominator are multiplied by the conjugate $3\sqrt{2} - 1$. What is the value of the new denominator $\left(3\sqrt{2} + 1\right)\left(3\sqrt{2} - 1\right)$?

- $19$
- $5$
- $17$
- $-17$

## Explanation

The product is a sum times a difference of the same terms, so it equals the difference of squares $\left(3\sqrt{2}\right)^{2} - 1^{2}$. The power of a product is the product of the powers, therefore $\left(3\sqrt{2}\right)^{2} = 9 \cdot 2 = 18$. Hence
$$
\left(3\sqrt{2} + 1\right)\left(3\sqrt{2} - 1\right) = 18 - 1 = 17 .
$$
Squaring only $\sqrt{2}$ one would get $3 \cdot 2 - 1 = 5$, and with the sum of the squares one would get $19$.
