What is the rationalized form of $\dfrac{4}{3\sqrt{2}}$?

- $\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}$
- $2\sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$

## Explanation

The denominator contains the single root $\sqrt{2}$, so numerator and denominator are multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$. Since $\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2$, the denominator becomes $3 \cdot 2 = 6$, and we obtain
$$
\frac{4}{3\sqrt{2}} = \frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2}} = \frac{4\sqrt{2}}{6} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} .
$$
The coefficient $3$ stays in the denominator; neglecting it would give $2\sqrt{2}$.
