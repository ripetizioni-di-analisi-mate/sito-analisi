The fraction $\dfrac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{\sqrt{5} - 1}$ also has a binomial with roots in the numerator. What is its rationalized form?

- $\dfrac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

Numerator and denominator are multiplied by the conjugate binomial of the denominator, $\sqrt{5} + 1$. The denominator becomes the difference of squares $5 - 1 = 4$, while the numerator becomes the square of a binomial, $\left(\sqrt{5} + 1\right)^{2} = 5 + 2\sqrt{5} + 1 = 6 + 2\sqrt{5}$. Therefore
$$
\frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{\sqrt{5} - 1} = \frac{6 + 2\sqrt{5}}{4} = \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2} .
$$
The square of the binomial contains the cross term $2\sqrt{5}$; neglecting it one would get $\dfrac{6}{4} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
