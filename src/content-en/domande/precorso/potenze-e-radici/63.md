The nested radical $\sqrt{3 + 2\sqrt{2}}$ can be written without nested roots. Which number is it equal to?

- $1 + \sqrt{2}$
- $\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}$
- $1 + 2\sqrt{2}$
- $2 + \sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The square root of a positive number is the unique positive number whose square is the radicand. By the square of a binomial
$$
\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\right)^{2} = 1 + 2\sqrt{2} + 2 = 3 + 2\sqrt{2} ,
$$
and $1 + \sqrt{2}$ is positive, so $\sqrt{3 + 2\sqrt{2}} = 1 + \sqrt{2}$.

The other numbers have squares different from the radicand; for example $\left(\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}\right)^{2} = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}$.
