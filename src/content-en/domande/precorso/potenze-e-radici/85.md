In the expression $\sqrt{-x}$ there is a minus sign in front of the variable. For which real $x$ is the root defined?

- $x \geq 0$
- $x < 0$
- $x \leq 0$
- For no real $x$

## Explanation

The square root has an even index, so it requires the radicand to be greater than or equal to zero, that is, $-x \geq 0$. Multiplying by $-1$ reverses the inequality, therefore $x \leq 0$.

The radicand $-x$ is positive for negative $x$; for example, for $x = -9$ we have $\sqrt{9} = 3$. For $x = 0$ the root equals $\sqrt{0} = 0$, so the endpoint $0$ is included.
