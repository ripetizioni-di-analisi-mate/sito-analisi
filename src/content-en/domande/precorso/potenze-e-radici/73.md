The number $\sqrt{2} - 1$ is the result of rationalizing one of the following fractions. Which one?

- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} - 1}$
- $\dfrac{1}{1 - \sqrt{2}}$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} + 1}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2} + 1}$

## Explanation

We rationalize $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} + 1}$ by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate binomial $\sqrt{2} - 1$. The denominator becomes the difference of squares $2 - 1 = 1$, so $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} + 1} = \sqrt{2} - 1$.

With the same procedure $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} - 1} = \sqrt{2} + 1$ and $\dfrac{1}{1 - \sqrt{2}} = -\left(\sqrt{2} + 1\right)$, while $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2} + 1} = \sqrt{2}\left(\sqrt{2} - 1\right) = 2 - \sqrt{2}$.
