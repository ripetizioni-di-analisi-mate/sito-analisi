The radicand of $\sqrt{\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 2}}$ is a fraction with the variable also in the denominator. For which real $x$ is the root defined?

- $x \leq -2$ or $x \geq 1$
- $x \geq 1$
- $-2 < x \leq 1$
- $x < -2$ or $x \geq 1$

## Explanation

The square root requires the radicand to be greater than or equal to zero, and the fraction requires the denominator to be different from zero. The numerator $x - 1$ is positive for $x > 1$ and the denominator $x + 2$ is positive for $x > -2$, so the fraction is positive for $x < -2$ or $x > 1$, where numerator and denominator have the same sign. The fraction vanishes for $x = 1$, which is therefore included, while $x = -2$ makes the denominator zero and is excluded.

Therefore the root is defined for $x < -2$ or $x \geq 1$.
