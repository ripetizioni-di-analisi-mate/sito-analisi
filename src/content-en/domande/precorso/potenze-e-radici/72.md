After rationalizing the first term, which value is obtained for the expression $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}} - \sqrt{12}$?

- $2 - 2\sqrt{3}$
- $0$
- $4\sqrt{3}$
- $-2\sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

The first term has a single root in the denominator, so numerator and denominator are multiplied by $\sqrt{3}$, and we obtain $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{6\sqrt{3}}{3} = 2\sqrt{3}$. Since $12 = 4 \cdot 3$, the factor $\sqrt{4} = 2$ is taken out, therefore $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$. The two terms are equal, so the expression equals
$$
2\sqrt{3} - 2\sqrt{3} = 0 .
$$
In the fraction $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}}$ the $6$ is outside the radical sign and the $3$ is under it, therefore the two numbers cannot be simplified with each other.
