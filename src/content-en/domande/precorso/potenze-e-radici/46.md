In scientific notation a number is written as $k \cdot 10^{m}$, with $1 \leq k < 10$ and $m$ an integer. How is the product $\left(4 \cdot 10^{-3}\right) \cdot \left(5 \cdot 10^{7}\right)$ written in scientific notation?

- $2 \cdot 10^{4}$
- $2 \cdot 10^{11}$
- $2 \cdot 10^{5}$
- $2 \cdot 10^{-20}$

## Explanation

The coefficients are multiplied together and the powers of $10$ are multiplied together. The coefficients give $4 \cdot 5 = 20$. By the product of powers with the same base the exponents are added, each with its own sign, so $10^{-3} \cdot 10^{7} = 10^{4}$. The product is therefore $20 \cdot 10^{4}$, and since $20 = 2 \cdot 10$ we obtain
$$
20 \cdot 10^{4} = 2 \cdot 10^{1} \cdot 10^{4} = 2 \cdot 10^{5} .
$$
The coefficient $20$ is greater than $10$, so the expression $2 \cdot 10^{4}$ leaves out the factor $10$ contained in $20$.
