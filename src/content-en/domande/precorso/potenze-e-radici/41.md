What is the value of $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} + 1}$, written with rationalized denominators?

- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} - 1$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} + 1$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - 1$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2} - 1}{2}$

## Explanation

The first term has a single root in the denominator, so numerator and denominator are multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$, and we obtain $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The second has the binomial $\sqrt{2} + 1$ in the denominator, therefore it is multiplied by the **conjugate binomial** $\sqrt{2} - 1$. Since $\left(\sqrt{2} + 1\right)\left(\sqrt{2} - 1\right) = 2 - 1 = 1$, we have $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} + 1} = \sqrt{2} - 1$. The sum of the two terms is therefore
$$
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \sqrt{2} - 1 = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} - 1 .
$$
