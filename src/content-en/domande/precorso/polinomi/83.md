Which of the following equalities describes the division of $x^3 - 4x + 1$ by $x - 2$, with the correct quotient and remainder?

- $x^3 - 4x + 1 = (x - 2)\left( x^2 + 2x - 4 \right) + 4x - 7$
- $x^3 - 4x + 1 = (x - 2)\left( x^2 + 2x \right) + 1$
- $x^3 - 4x + 1 = (x - 2)\left( x^2 - 2x \right) + 1$
- $x^3 - 4x + 1 = x\left( x^2 - 4 \right) + 1$

## Explanation

**Synthetic division**, on the coefficients $1, 0, -4, 1$ and with $c = 2$, gives the row of results $1, 2, 0, 1$. The quotient is therefore $x^2 + 2x$ and the remainder is $1$, that is, $x^3 - 4x + 1 = (x - 2)\left( x^2 + 2x \right) + 1$. The equality with the remainder $4x - 7$ holds for every $x$, but that remainder has degree $1$, equal to the degree of the divisor, therefore it is not the remainder of the division. The equality $x^3 - 4x + 1 = x\left( x^2 - 4 \right) + 1$ is true and describes the division by $x$.
