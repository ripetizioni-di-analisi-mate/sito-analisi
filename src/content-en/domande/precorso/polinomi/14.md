For which values of $a$ and $b$ does the equality $(x - 1)\left( x^2 + ax + b \right) = x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + 2$ hold for every real $x$?

- $a = 3$ and $b = -2$
- $a = 1$ and $b = 2$
- $a = 3$ and $b = 2$
- $a = -3$ and $b = -2$

## Explanation

Expanding the left-hand side gives
$$
(x - 1)\left( x^2 + ax + b \right) = x^3 + (a - 1)\,x^2 + (b - a)\,x - b .
$$
By the **identity principle for polynomials** the coefficients of the terms of the same degree are equal, so
$$
\begin{cases}
a - 1 = 2 \\[2pt]
b - a = -5 \\[2pt]
-b = 2
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives $a = 3$ and the third gives $b = -2$. The two values also satisfy the second equation, since $-2 - 3 = -5$, therefore $a = 3$ and $b = -2$.
