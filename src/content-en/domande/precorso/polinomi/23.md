What is the complete factorization of $x^6 - 1$?

- $(x - 1)(x + 1)\left( x^2 + 2x + 1 \right)\left( x^2 - 2x + 1 \right)$
- $(x - 1)(x + 1)\left( x^2 + x + 1 \right)\left( x^2 - x + 1 \right)$
- $(x - 1)(x + 1)\left( x^2 + 1 \right)^2$
- $\left( x^2 - 1 \right)\left( x^4 + 1 \right)$

## Explanation

Since $x^6 = \left( x^3 \right)^2$, the **difference of squares** gives $x^6 - 1 = \left( x^3 - 1 \right)\left( x^3 + 1 \right)$. By the **difference and sum of cubes**, with $A = x$ and $B = 1$, we have
$$
x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)\left( x^2 + x + 1 \right), \qquad x^3 + 1 = (x + 1)\left( x^2 - x + 1 \right) .
$$
The factors $x^2 + x + 1$ and $x^2 - x + 1$ are quadratics that are not perfect squares and have no real roots, therefore the complete factorization is $(x - 1)(x + 1)\left( x^2 + x + 1 \right)\left( x^2 - x + 1 \right)$.
