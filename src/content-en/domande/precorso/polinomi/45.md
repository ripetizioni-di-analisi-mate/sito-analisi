For which integer values of $k$ does the polynomial $P(x) = x^3 + kx + 2$ have at least one rational root?

- $k = -5$, $k = -3$, $k = 3$ or $k = 5$
- For every integer $k$
- $k = -1$, $k = 3$ or $k = 5$
- $k = -5$, $k = -3$ or $k = 1$

## Explanation

For integer $k$ the polynomial has integer coefficients, leading coefficient $1$ and constant term $2$. By the criterion for **finding rational roots**, its rational roots are therefore integers dividing $2$, that is, they lie among $\pm 1$ and $\pm 2$. At these candidates the polynomial takes the values $P(1) = 3 + k$, $P(-1) = 1 - k$, $P(2) = 10 + 2k$ and $P(-2) = -6 - 2k$. These values vanish for $k = -3$, $k = 1$, $k = -5$ and $k = -3$ respectively. Therefore $P$ has a rational root if and only if $k = -5$, $k = -3$ or $k = 1$; for $k = 0$, for example, the polynomial $x^3 + 2$ has none.
