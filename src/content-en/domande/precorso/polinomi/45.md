What is the complete factorization of $x^4 - 2x^3 - x^2 + 2x$?

- $x\,(x + 2)(x - 1)(x + 1)$
- $(x - 2)(x - 1)(x + 1)$
- $x\,(x - 2)\left( x^2 + 1 \right)$
- $x\,(x - 2)(x - 1)(x + 1)$

## Explanation

**Factoring out the common factor** $x$ gives $x\left( x^3 - 2x^2 - x + 2 \right)$. In the third-degree factor we group the first two terms and the last two, and **factoring by grouping** gives $x^2 (x - 2) - (x - 2) = (x - 2)\left( x^2 - 1 \right)$. The factor $x^2 - 1$ is a difference of squares, so $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$. Therefore $x^4 - 2x^3 - x^2 + 2x = x\,(x - 2)(x - 1)(x + 1)$.
