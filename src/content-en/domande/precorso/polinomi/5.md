- $Q(x) = x^2 - 6x + 13$ and $R = -20$
- $Q(x) = x^2 - 2x - 3$ and $R = 0$
- $Q(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 - 3x$ and $R = 0$
- $Q(x) = x^2 + 2x - 3$ and $R = 0$

## Explanation

The first term of the quotient is $\dfrac{x^3}{x} = x^2$, and subtracting $x^2 (x - 2) = x^3 - 2x^2$ from the dividend leaves $-2x^2 + x + 6$. The next term is $\dfrac{-2x^2}{x} = -2x$, and subtracting $-2x\,(x - 2) = -2x^2 + 4x$ leaves $-3x + 6$. The last term is $\dfrac{-3x}{x} = -3$, and subtracting $-3\,(x - 2) = -3x + 6$ leaves $0$. Therefore the quotient is $Q(x) = x^2 - 2x - 3$, of degree $2$, and the remainder is $R = 0$, so the division is **exact**. The result is consistent with the **remainder theorem**, since $P(2) = 8 - 16 + 2 + 6 = 0$.
