A polynomial $P(x)$ is known to satisfy $P(3) = 0$ and $P(-1) = 5$. Which statement is true?

- $x + 3$ divides $P$, and the remainder of the division of $P$ by $x - 1$ is $5$
- $x - 3$ divides $P$, and $x + 1$ also divides $P$
- $x - 3$ divides $P$, and the remainder of the division of $P$ by $x + 1$ is $-5$
- $x - 3$ divides $P$, and the remainder of the division of $P$ by $x + 1$ is $5$

## Explanation

By **Ruffini's theorem**, $x - 3$ divides $P$ if and only if $P(3) = 0$, and this condition is satisfied. The binomial $x + 1$ is written $x - (-1)$, so by the **remainder theorem** the remainder of the division of $P$ by $x + 1$ is $P(-1) = 5$. Since this remainder is different from $0$, the binomial $x + 1$ does not divide $P$.
