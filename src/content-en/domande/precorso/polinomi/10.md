What is the complete factorization of $3x^3 - 2x^2 - 3x + 2$?

- $(x - 1)(x + 1)(3x + 2)$
- $(x - 1)(x + 1)\left( x - \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$
- $(3x - 2)\left( x^2 + 1 \right)$
- $(x - 1)(x + 1)(3x - 2)$

## Explanation

Grouping the first two terms and the last two, **factoring by grouping** gives
$$
3x^3 - 2x^2 - 3x + 2 = x^2 (3x - 2) - (3x - 2) = (3x - 2)\left( x^2 - 1 \right) .
$$
The factor $x^2 - 1$ is a difference of squares, so $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$ and the complete factorization is $(x - 1)(x + 1)(3x - 2)$. The result agrees with the search for rational roots, since the roots $1$, $-1$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$ appear among the candidates $\pm 1$, $\pm 2$, $\pm\dfrac{1}{3}$, $\pm\dfrac{2}{3}$. The product $(x - 1)(x + 1)\left( x - \dfrac{2}{3} \right)$ has the same roots but leading coefficient $1$, therefore it is different from the given polynomial, which has leading coefficient $3$.
