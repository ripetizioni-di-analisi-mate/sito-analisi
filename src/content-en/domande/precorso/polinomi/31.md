Let $P(x) = (x + 2)^3 (x - 1)^2 (x - 4)$. At which roots does the graph of $P$ touch the $x$-axis without crossing it?

- Only at $x = -2$
- Only at $x = 1$
- At $x = -2$ and at $x = 1$
- Only at $x = 4$

## Explanation

The graph of a polynomial crosses the $x$-axis at the roots of odd **multiplicity** and touches it without crossing at those of even multiplicity. The roots of $P$ are $-2$ with multiplicity $3$, $1$ with multiplicity $2$ and $4$ with multiplicity $1$. Therefore the graph touches the axis without crossing it *only* at $x = 1$. Indeed the factor $(x + 2)^3 (x - 4)$ equals $27 \cdot (-3) = -81$ at $x = 1$, so it is negative near $1$, while $(x - 1)^2$ is positive on both sides; consequently $P$ is negative both to the left and to the right of $1$.
