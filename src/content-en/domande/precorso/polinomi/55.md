Dividing a polynomial $P(x)$ by $x^2 + 1$ gives the quotient $x - 3$ and the remainder $2x + 1$. What is $P(x)$?

- $x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 2$
- $x^3 - 3x^2 + x - 3$
- $x^3 - 3x^2 - x - 4$
- $3x^2 - 5x - 2$

## Explanation

By the **theorem on division with remainder** the dividend is $P(x) = B(x)\,Q(x) + R(x)$. The product of the divisor and the quotient is $(x^2 + 1)(x - 3) = x^3 - 3x^2 + x - 3$. Adding the remainder we get $P(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + x - 3 + 2x + 1 = x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 2$. The remainder $2x + 1$ has degree $1 < 2$, so it is compatible with the divisor $x^2 + 1$.
