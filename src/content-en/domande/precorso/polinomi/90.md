The constants $A$ and $B$ are such that $A(x + 1) + B(x - 2) = 5x - 1$ for every real $x$. What are they?

- $A = 2$ and $B = 3$
- $A = 3$ and $B = 2$
- $A = 3$ and $B = -2$
- $A = 1$ and $B = 4$

## Explanation

Expanding the left-hand side gives $(A + B)\,x + A - 2B$. By the **identity principle** the coefficients of $x$ and the constant terms of the two sides coincide, so
$$
\begin{cases}
A + B = 5 \\[2pt]
A - 2B = -1
\end{cases}
$$
Subtracting the second equation from the first gives $3B = 6$, that is, $B = 2$, and hence $A = 3$. Indeed $3(x + 1) + 2(x - 2) = 5x - 1$.
