Which polynomial of positive degree divides both $x^2 - 9$ and $x^2 - 6x + 9$?

- $x - 3$
- $(x - 3)^2$
- $x + 3$
- $x^2 - 9$

## Explanation

By the **difference of squares** we have $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$. The trinomial $x^2 - 6x + 9$ is the **square of a binomial** $(x - 3)^2$, since $x^2$ and $9$ are the squares of $x$ and $3$ and $-6x = -2 \cdot x \cdot 3$. The factor $x - 3$ appears in both factorizations, so it divides the two polynomials. The binomial $x + 3$ does not divide $(x - 3)^2$, which equals $36$ at $x = -3$. The polynomial $(x - 3)^2$ does not divide $x^2 - 9$, since in $(x - 3)(x + 3)$ the root $3$ is simple. Finally $x^2 - 9$ and $(x - 3)^2$ have the same degree and the same leading coefficient and are different, therefore neither divides the other.
