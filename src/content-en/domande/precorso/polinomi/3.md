What is the factorization of $27x^3 - 1$?

- $(3x - 1)\left( 9x^2 - 3x + 1 \right)$
- $(3x - 1)\left( 3x^2 + 3x + 1 \right)$
- $(3x - 1)\left( 9x^2 + 3x + 1 \right)$
- $(3x - 1)\left( 9x^2 + 6x + 1 \right)$

## Explanation

Since $27x^3 = (3x)^3$ and $1 = 1^3$, the **difference of cubes** $A^3 - B^3 = (A - B)\left( A^2 + AB + B^2 \right)$ applies with $A = 3x$ and $B = 1$. The second factor is $A^2 + AB + B^2 = 9x^2 + 3x + 1$, so
$$
27x^3 - 1 = (3x - 1)\left( 9x^2 + 3x + 1 \right) .
$$
The false square contains the product $AB = 3x$, while the square of $3x + 1$ would contain the double product $6x$. The discriminant of $9x^2 + 3x + 1$ is $9 - 36 = -27 < 0$, therefore this factor has no real roots and the factorization is complete.
