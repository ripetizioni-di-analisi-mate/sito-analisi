A polynomial $A(x)$ of degree $5$ is divided by a polynomial $B(x)$ of degree $2$. Which statement about the quotient $Q(x)$ and the remainder $R(x)$ is true?

- $\deg Q = 3$, and $R(x) = 0$ or $\deg R \leq 1$
- $\deg Q = 3$ and $\deg R = 1$
- $\deg Q = 3$, and $R(x) = 0$ or $\deg R \leq 2$
- $\deg Q = 7$, and $R(x) = 0$ or $\deg R \leq 1$

## Explanation

By the theorem on **division with remainder**, $A(x) = B(x)\,Q(x) + R(x)$, with $R(x) = 0$ or $\deg R < \deg B = 2$, that is, $\deg R \leq 1$. The remainder therefore has degree less than $5$, so the degree-$5$ term of $A$ comes from the product $B\,Q$ and $\deg (BQ) = 5$. In a product the degrees add up, so $2 + \deg Q = 5$ and $\deg Q = 3$. The remainder may also be constant or zero, as in the division of $x^5$ by $x^2$, which has remainder $0$.
