We divide $x + 3$ by $x^2 + 1$. What are the quotient and the remainder?

- $Q(x) = x + 3$ and $R(x) = 0$
- $Q(x) = 1$ and $R(x) = -x^2 + x + 2$
- The division cannot be carried out
- $Q(x) = 0$ and $R(x) = x + 3$

## Explanation

The quotient and the remainder are the polynomials $Q$ and $R$ such that $x + 3 = \left( x^2 + 1 \right) Q(x) + R(x)$, with $R(x) = 0$ or $\deg R < 2$. The choice $Q(x) = 0$ and $R(x) = x + 3$ satisfies both conditions, since $\deg (x + 3) = 1 < 2$. By the **uniqueness** of quotient and remainder, the division therefore gives $Q(x) = 0$ and $R(x) = x + 3$. In general, when the dividend has lower degree than the divisor, the remainder coincides with the dividend.
