Which of the following polynomials is divisible by $x^2 - 1$?

- $x^3 + x^2 + x + 1$
- $x^4 - 2x^2 + x$
- $x^4 - 2x^2 - 1$
- $x^5 - x^3 + 2x^2 - 2$

## Explanation

Since $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$, a polynomial divisible by $x^2 - 1$ is divisible both by $x - 1$ and by $x + 1$, so by the **factor theorem** it vanishes at $1$ and at $-1$. The polynomial $x^5 - x^3 + 2x^2 - 2$ vanishes at both points, and indeed $x^5 - x^3 + 2x^2 - 2 = x^3 \left( x^2 - 1 \right) + 2\left( x^2 - 1 \right) = \left( x^2 - 1 \right)\left( x^3 + 2 \right)$. The polynomial $x^3 + x^2 + x + 1$ equals $4$ at $1$, $x^4 - 2x^2 + x$ equals $-2$ at $-1$ and $x^4 - 2x^2 - 1$ equals $-2$ at $1$, so none of them is divisible by $x^2 - 1$.
