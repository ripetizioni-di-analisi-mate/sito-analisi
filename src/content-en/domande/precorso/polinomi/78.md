How does $x^4 + 4$ factor into factors with real coefficients?

- $\left( x^2 + 2 \right)^2$
- $\left( x^2 - 2 \right)\left( x^2 + 2 \right)$
- It does not factor, because it is a sum of squares
- $\left( x^2 - 2x + 2 \right)\left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right)$

## Explanation

Adding and subtracting $4x^2$, the polynomial becomes
$$
x^4 + 4 = x^4 + 4x^2 + 4 - 4x^2 = \left( x^2 + 2 \right)^2 - (2x)^2 .
$$
It is a **difference of squares** with $A = x^2 + 2$ and $B = 2x$, so $x^4 + 4 = \left( x^2 + 2 - 2x \right)\left( x^2 + 2 + 2x \right)$. The two factors have no real roots, since $x^2 - 2x + 2 = (x - 1)^2 + 1$ and $x^2 + 2x + 2 = (x + 1)^2 + 1$, therefore the factorization is complete. The fact that $x^4 + 4$ has no real roots rules out only the linear factors.
