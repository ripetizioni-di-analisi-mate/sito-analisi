A polynomial of degree $4$ has the double root $1$ and the simple root $-2$. How many other distinct real roots does it have?

- $1$
- $2$
- $0$
- $3$

## Explanation

Since $1$ is a double root and $-2$ is a simple root, the polynomial can be written $P(x) = (x - 1)^2 (x + 2)\,Q(x)$, with $Q(1) \neq 0$ and $Q(-2) \neq 0$ by the definition of multiplicity. In a product the degrees add up, therefore $\deg Q = 4 - 3 = 1$. A first-degree polynomial vanishes at exactly one real number, which is different from $1$ and from $-2$. Hence the polynomial has exactly **one** other real root, which is simple.
