What is the complete factorization of $x^4 - 81$?

- $(x - 3)(x + 3)\left( x^2 - 9 \right)$
- $(x - 3)(x + 3)\left( x^2 + 9 \right)$
- $(x - 9)(x + 9)\left( x^2 + 9 \right)$
- $(x - 3)(x + 3)\left( x^2 + 3 \right)$

## Explanation

Since $x^4 = \left( x^2 \right)^2$ and $81 = 9^2$, the **difference of squares** with $A = x^2$ and $B = 9$ gives $x^4 - 81 = \left( x^2 - 9 \right)\left( x^2 + 9 \right)$. The factor $x^2 - 9$ is in turn a difference of squares, with $A = x$ and $B = 3$, so $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$. The factor $x^2 + 9$ is a sum of squares and satisfies $x^2 + 9 \geq 9 > 0$ for every $x$, therefore it has no real roots and cannot be factored into first-degree factors. The complete factorization is hence $(x - 3)(x + 3)\left( x^2 + 9 \right)$.
