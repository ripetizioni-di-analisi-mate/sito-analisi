- $(x + 4)\left( x^2 - 3 \right)$
- $(x + 4)\left( x^2 + 3 \right)$
- $(x + 3)\left( x^2 + 4 \right)$
- $(x - 4)\left( x^2 + 3 \right)$

## Explanation

We group the first two terms and the last two, and factor $x^2$ out of the first group and $3$ out of the second, obtaining
$$
x^3 + 4x^2 + 3x + 12 = x^2 (x + 4) + 3\,(x + 4) .
$$
The two groups have the common factor $x + 4$, so **factoring by grouping** gives $(x + 4)\left( x^2 + 3 \right)$. Since $x^2 + 3 \geq 3 > 0$ for every $x$, the factor $x^2 + 3$ has no real roots, therefore the factorization is complete.
