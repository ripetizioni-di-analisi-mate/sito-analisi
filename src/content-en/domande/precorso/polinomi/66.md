The polynomial $x^5 - 4x^3$ has the root $0$. With what multiplicity?

- $1$
- $5$
- $3$
- $2$

## Explanation

**Factoring out the common factor** $x^3$ gives $x^5 - 4x^3 = x^3 \left( x^2 - 4 \right)$. The factor $Q(x) = x^2 - 4$ satisfies $Q(0) = -4 \neq 0$, so by the definition of multiplicity the root $0$ has multiplicity $3$. Indeed $x^5 - 4x^3 = x^3 (x - 2)(x + 2)$, and the other two roots $2$ and $-2$ are simple.
