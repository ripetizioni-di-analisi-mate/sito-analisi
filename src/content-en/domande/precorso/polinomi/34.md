For which integer values of $k$ can the trinomial $x^2 + kx + 6$ be written as a product $(x + a)(x + b)$ with integers $a$ and $b$?

- $k = \pm 1$ or $k = \pm 5$
- $k = \pm 2$ or $k = \pm 3$
- $k = \pm 5$ or $k = \pm 7$
- $k = \pm 1$, $k = \pm 5$ or $k = \pm 7$

## Explanation

Since $(x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)\,x + ab$, by the **identity principle** the trinomial can be written in this form if and only if
$$
\begin{cases}
a + b = k \\[2pt]
ab = 6
\end{cases}
$$
The pairs of integers with product $6$ are $1$ and $6$, $2$ and $3$, $-1$ and $-6$, $-2$ and $-3$, whose sums are $7$, $5$, $-7$ and $-5$. Therefore the **monic quadratic trinomial** factors with integer factors for $k = \pm 5$ or $k = \pm 7$. The pairs $1$ and $-6$, $-1$ and $6$, $2$ and $-3$, $-2$ and $3$ have product $-6$, so their sums $\pm 5$ and $\pm 1$ concern the trinomial $x^2 + kx - 6$.
