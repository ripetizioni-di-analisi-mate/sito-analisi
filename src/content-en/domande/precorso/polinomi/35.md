What are the real roots of $P(x) = 4x^3 - 3x - 1$ and their multiplicities?

- $1$ simple and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ double
- $1$ simple and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ double
- $1$ double and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ simple
- $1$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, all simple

## Explanation

Since $P(1) = 4 - 3 - 1 = 0$, the number $1$ is a root. **Synthetic division** with $c = 1$, on the coefficients $4, 0, -3, -1$, gives the quotient $4x^2 + 4x + 1$ with remainder $0$. The quotient is the square of a binomial $(2x + 1)^2$, so $P(x) = (x - 1)(2x + 1)^2$. The factor $2x + 1$ vanishes at $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and appears squared, therefore $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ is a **double** root. The root $1$ is simple, since $(2 \cdot 1 + 1)^2 = 9 \neq 0$.
