By grouping the terms suitably, how does $x^2 - y^2 + 3x - 3y$ factor?

- $(x - y)(x + y - 3)$
- $(x + y)(x - y + 3)$
- $(x - y)(x + y)(x + 3)$
- $(x - y)(x + y + 3)$

## Explanation

The first group $x^2 - y^2$ is a **difference of squares** and equals $(x - y)(x + y)$. From the second group we factor out $3$, so $3x - 3y = 3\,(x - y)$. The two groups have the common factor $x - y$, which is factored out in turn:
$$
x^2 - y^2 + 3x - 3y = (x - y)(x + y) + 3\,(x - y) = (x - y)(x + y + 3) .
$$
