- $Q(x) = 2x^2 + x - 1$ and $R = 0$
- $Q(x) = 2x^2 + x - 5$ and $R = -6$
- $Q(x) = 2x^2 + x - 5$ and $R = 4$
- $Q(x) = 2x^2 + x - 5$ and $R = 2x + 4$

## Explanation

The first term of the quotient is $\dfrac{2x^4}{x^2} = 2x^2$, and subtracting $2x^2\left( x^2 + 1 \right) = 2x^4 + 2x^2$ from the dividend leaves $x^3 - 5x^2 + x - 1$. The next term is $\dfrac{x^3}{x^2} = x$, and subtracting $x\left( x^2 + 1 \right) = x^3 + x$ leaves $-5x^2 - 1$. The last term is $\dfrac{-5x^2}{x^2} = -5$, and subtracting $-5\left( x^2 + 1 \right) = -5x^2 - 5$ leaves $-1 - (-5) = 4$. The remainder $4$ has degree $0$, less than the degree $2$ of the divisor, therefore the algorithm ends with $Q(x) = 2x^2 + x - 5$ and $R = 4$. Indeed
$$
\left( x^2 + 1 \right)\left( 2x^2 + x - 5 \right) + 4 = 2x^4 + x^3 - 3x^2 + x - 1 .
$$
