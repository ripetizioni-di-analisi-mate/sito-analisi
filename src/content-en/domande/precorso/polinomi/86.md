The polynomial $\left( x^2 - 1 \right)^2 - (x + 1)^2$ is the difference of two squares. What is its complete factorization?

- $\left( x^2 - x - 2 \right)\left( x^2 + x \right)$
- $x\,(x - 2)(x + 1)^2$
- $x\,(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$
- $x\,(x - 2)(x + 1)$

## Explanation

The **difference of squares** $A^2 - B^2 = (A - B)(A + B)$ with $A = x^2 - 1$ and $B = x + 1$ gives
$$
\left( x^2 - 1 \right)^2 - (x + 1)^2 = \left( x^2 - 1 - x - 1 \right)\left( x^2 - 1 + x + 1 \right) = \left( x^2 - x - 2 \right)\left( x^2 + x \right) .
$$
The factor $x^2 - x - 2$ is a **monic quadratic trinomial**, since $(-2) + 1 = -1$ and $(-2) \cdot 1 = -2$, so $x^2 - x - 2 = (x - 2)(x + 1)$. Factoring $x$ out of $x^2 + x$ gives $x\,(x + 1)$. Therefore the polynomial is equal to $x\,(x - 2)(x + 1)^2$, in which every factor has degree $1$.
