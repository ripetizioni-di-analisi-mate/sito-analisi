From a square of side $x + 3$, with $x > 0$, a square of side $x$ sharing a vertex with the first one is removed, as in the figure. What is the area of the shaded part?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-1.2 xmax=5.6 ymin=-1 ymax=5.6 poligoni="2,0 5,0 5,5 0,5 0,2 2,2" linee="0,0 2,0 2,2 0,2 0,0;0,2 0,5 5,5 5,0 2,0" etichette="1,-0.45,x;3.5,-0.45,3;-0.45,1,x;-0.45,3.5,3" titolo="The square of side $x + 3$ without the square of side $x$"}

- $6x + 9$
- $9$
- $3x + 9$
- $x^2 + 6x + 9$

## Explanation

The area of the shaded part is the difference between the area of the large square and that of the removed square, that is, $(x + 3)^2 - x^2$. By the **square of a binomial** we have $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$, where $6x$ is twice the product $2 \cdot x \cdot 3$. Subtracting $x^2$, the area is $6x + 9$. The same result can be read on the figure, since the shaded part consists of two rectangles with sides $x$ and $3$ and a square of side $3$.
