The figure shows the graph of a third-degree polynomial with leading coefficient $1$. Which polynomial is it?

::grafico{f="(x+2)*(x-1)^2" xmin=-3 xmax=2.3 ymin=-3 ymax=6 legenda=0 punti="-2,0,pieno;1,0,pieno;0,2,pieno" titolo="The graph of the polynomial, with the points of x-coordinate $-2$, $0$ and $1$"}

- $(x + 2)^2 (x - 1)$
- $(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$
- $(x - 2)(x + 1)^2$
- $(x + 2)(x - 1)(x + 1)$

## Explanation

The graph meets the $x$-axis at $-2$ and at $1$, so the real roots are $-2$ and $1$. At $-2$ the graph crosses the axis, therefore $-2$ has odd multiplicity; at $1$ it touches the axis without crossing it, therefore $1$ has even multiplicity. The degree is $3$, so the multiplicities are $1$ and $2$, and with leading coefficient $1$ the polynomial is $(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$. Its value at $0$ is $2 \cdot (-1)^2 = 2$, the y-coordinate of the point of the graph on the $y$-axis.
