The polynomial $A(x) = 2x^4 - 3x^3 + 5x - 7$ is divided by the monomial $x^2$. What is the remainder?

- $-7$
- $-3x^3 + 5x - 7$
- $5x - 7$
- $0$

## Explanation

The terms $2x^4$ and $-3x^3$ are multiples of $x^2$, so $A(x) = x^2\left( 2x^2 - 3x \right) + 5x - 7$. The polynomial $5x - 7$ has degree $1$, less than the degree $2$ of the divisor. By the uniqueness of quotient and remainder in **division with remainder**, the quotient is $2x^2 - 3x$ and the remainder is $5x - 7$. The number $-7 = A(0)$ is instead the remainder of the division by $x$, which has degree $1$.
