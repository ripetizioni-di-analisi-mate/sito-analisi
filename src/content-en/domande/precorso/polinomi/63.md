The polynomial $P$ has the graph shown in the figure. What is the remainder of the division of $P(x)$ by $x + 1$?

::grafico{f="x^3-3*x+1" xmin=-2.4 xmax=2.4 ymin=-3 ymax=5 legenda=0 punti="-1,3,pieno;1,-1,pieno" segmenti="-1,0,-1,3;-1,3,0,3;1,0,1,-1;1,-1,0,-1" titolo="The graph of $y = P(x)$ with the points of x-coordinate $-1$ and $1$"}

- $3$
- $-1$
- $1$
- $0$

## Explanation

The divisor $x + 1$ vanishes at $x = -1$. By the **remainder theorem** the remainder of the division is $P(-1)$, that is, the y-coordinate of the point of the graph with x-coordinate $-1$. From the graph we read $P(-1) = 3$, so the remainder is $3$. The y-coordinate $P(1) = -1$ is the remainder of the division by $x - 1$.
