Which of the following expressions is a complete factorization with real coefficients?

- $(x + 1)\left( x^2 - 1 \right)$
- $(x - 1)\left( x^2 + x + 1 \right)$
- $x\left( x^2 - 5x + 6 \right)$
- $(x - 2)\left( x^3 + 8 \right)$

## Explanation

A factorization is complete when every factor has degree $1$ or is quadratic with no real roots. The factor $x^2 + x + 1$ satisfies $x^2 + x + 1 = \left( x + \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^2 + \dfrac{3}{4} > 0$ for every $x$, so it has no real roots, and the factorization $(x - 1)\left( x^2 + x + 1 \right)$ is **complete**. The other expressions contain a factor that can still be factored, since $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$, $x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)$ and $x^3 + 8 = (x + 2)\left( x^2 - 2x + 4 \right)$.
