A polynomial $P(x)$ divided by $x - 1$ gives remainder $3$, and divided by $x + 1$ gives remainder $5$. What is the remainder of the division of $P(x)$ by $x^2 - 1$?

- $-x + 4$
- $x + 4$
- $8$
- $15$

## Explanation

The divisor $x^2 - 1$ has degree $2$, so the remainder has degree at most $1$ and is written $R(x) = ax + b$. The equality $P(x) = (x^2 - 1)\,Q(x) + ax + b$, evaluated at $x = 1$ and at $x = -1$, where $x^2 - 1$ vanishes, gives $P(1) = a + b$ and $P(-1) = -a + b$. By the **remainder theorem** $P(1) = 3$ and $P(-1) = 5$, therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
a + b = 3 \\[2pt]
-a + b = 5
\end{cases}
$$
Adding the two equations gives $2b = 8$, hence $b = 4$ and $a = -1$. The remainder is $R(x) = -x + 4$.
