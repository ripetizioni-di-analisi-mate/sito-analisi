What are the quotient and the remainder of the division of $2x^3 - x^2 + 3$ by $2x + 1$?

- $Q(x) = 2x^2 - 2x + 1$ and $R = \dfrac{5}{2}$
- $Q(x) = x^2 - x + \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $R = \dfrac{7}{2}$
- $Q(x) = x^2 - x + \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $R = \dfrac{5}{2}$
- $Q(x) = x^2 - x + 1$ and $R = 2$

## Explanation

Accounting for the missing degree, the dividend is written $2x^3 - x^2 + 0x + 3$. The first term of the quotient is $\dfrac{2x^3}{2x} = x^2$, and subtracting $x^2 (2x + 1) = 2x^3 + x^2$ leaves $-2x^2 + 3$. The next term is $\dfrac{-2x^2}{2x} = -x$, and subtracting $-x\,(2x + 1) = -2x^2 - x$ leaves $x + 3$. The last term is $\dfrac{x}{2x} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and subtracting $\dfrac{1}{2}(2x + 1) = x + \dfrac{1}{2}$ leaves $\dfrac{5}{2}$. Therefore $Q(x) = x^2 - x + \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $R = \dfrac{5}{2}$.
