For which values of $a$ and $b$ is the polynomial $P(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + bx - 6$ divisible both by $x - 1$ and by $x + 2$?

- $a = 4$ and $b = 1$
- $a = 1$ and $b = 4$
- $a = -6$ and $b = 11$
- $a = 4$ and $b = -1$

## Explanation

By the **factor theorem**, $x - 1$ and $x + 2$ divide $P$ if and only if $P(1) = 0$ and $P(-2) = 0$. Since $P(1) = a + b - 5$ and $P(-2) = 4a - 2b - 14$, we obtain the system
$$
\begin{cases}
a + b = 5 \\[2pt]
4a - 2b = 14
\end{cases}
$$
Dividing the second equation by $2$ gives $2a - b = 7$, and adding it to the first gives $3a = 12$, so $a = 4$ and $b = 1$. Indeed $x^3 + 4x^2 + x - 6 = (x - 1)(x + 2)(x + 3)$.
