The polynomial $x^3 + ax^2 + bx + 2$, with $a$ and $b$ real, divided by $x^2 + 2$ gives remainder $3x - 4$. What are $a$ and $b$?

- $a = -3$ and $b = 5$
- $a = 3$ and $b = 5$
- $a = 3$ and $b = 1$
- $a = 1$ and $b = 3$

## Explanation

In the division algorithm the first term of the quotient is $x$, and subtracting $x\left( x^2 + 2 \right)$ leaves $ax^2 + (b - 2)x + 2$. The next term is $a$, and subtracting $a\left( x^2 + 2 \right)$ leaves $(b - 2)x + 2 - 2a$, of degree less than $2$. This is the remainder, which must coincide with $3x - 4$ for every $x$; by the **identity principle**
$$
\begin{cases}
b - 2 = 3 \\[2pt]
2 - 2a = -4
\end{cases}
$$
Therefore $b = 5$ and $a = 3$.
