The polynomial $P(x) = x^3 + ax + b$ has a double root at $1$. What are $a$ and $b$?

- $a = -1$ and $b = 0$
- $a = -3$ and $b = -2$
- $a = -3$ and $b = 2$
- $a = 2$ and $b = -3$

## Explanation

**Synthetic division** with $c = 1$, on the coefficients $1, 0, a, b$, gives the quotient $x^2 + x + 1 + a$ and the remainder $1 + a + b$. The number $1$ is a root if the remainder is zero, that is, $1 + a + b = 0$. The root is at least double if the quotient also vanishes at $1$, that is, $3 + a = 0$, so $a = -3$ and then $b = 2$. Then $P(x) = x^3 - 3x + 2 = (x - 1)^2 (x + 2)$, and the root $1$ is exactly double, since $x + 2$ equals $3 \neq 0$ at $x = 1$.
