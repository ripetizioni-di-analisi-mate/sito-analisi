The divisor $B(x) = x^3 + x$ has degree $3$. What are the quotient and the remainder of the division of $A(x) = x^5 + 2x^3 - x + 1$ by $B(x)$?

- $Q(x) = x^2 + 1$ and $R(x) = -2x + 1$
- $Q(x) = x^2 + 2$ and $R(x) = -x^3 - 3x + 1$
- $Q(x) = x^2 + 3$ and $R(x) = -4x + 1$
- $Q(x) = x^2 + 1$ and $R(x) = -x + 1$

## Explanation

The first term of the quotient is $\dfrac{x^5}{x^3} = x^2$, and subtracting $x^2\left( x^3 + x \right) = x^5 + x^3$ from the dividend leaves $x^3 - x + 1$. This partial remainder has degree $3$, equal to the degree of the divisor, so the algorithm continues. The next term is $\dfrac{x^3}{x^3} = 1$, and subtracting $x^3 + x$ leaves $-2x + 1$. The new remainder has degree $1 < 3$, therefore the division stops with $Q(x) = x^2 + 1$ and $R(x) = -2x + 1$. The expression with $R(x) = -x^3 - 3x + 1$ satisfies the equality $A = BQ + R$, but that remainder has degree $3$ and hence is not the remainder of the division.
