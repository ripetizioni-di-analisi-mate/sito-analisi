What are the quotient and the remainder of the division of $x^3 - 2x + 4$ by $x^2 + 2x - 1$?

- $Q(x) = x - 2$ and $R(x) = -5x + 2$
- $Q(x) = x - 4$ and $R(x) = 9x$
- $Q(x) = x$ and $R(x) = -2x^2 - x + 4$
- $Q(x) = x - 2$ and $R(x) = 3x + 2$

## Explanation

Accounting for the missing degree, the dividend is written $x^3 + 0x^2 - 2x + 4$. The first term of the quotient is $\dfrac{x^3}{x^2} = x$, and subtracting $x\left( x^2 + 2x - 1 \right) = x^3 + 2x^2 - x$ leaves $-2x^2 - x + 4$. This partial remainder has degree $2$, equal to the degree of the divisor, so the algorithm continues with the term $\dfrac{-2x^2}{x^2} = -2$. Subtracting $-2\left( x^2 + 2x - 1 \right) = -2x^2 - 4x + 2$ leaves $3x + 2$, which has degree $1$, less than $2$. Therefore $Q(x) = x - 2$ and $R(x) = 3x + 2$.
