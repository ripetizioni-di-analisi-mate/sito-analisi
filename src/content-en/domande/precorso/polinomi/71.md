The factorization $x^3 + 27 = (x + 3)\left( x^2 - 3x + 9 \right)$ holds. Which statement about the real roots of $x^3 + 27$ is true?

- The only real root is $3$
- It has three distinct real roots
- The only real root is $-3$
- It has the two real roots $-3$ and $3$

## Explanation

The factor $x + 3$ vanishes at $x = -3$. The second factor is **a quadratic that is not a perfect square**, and completing the square it is written
$$
x^2 - 3x + 9 = \left( x - \frac{3}{2} \right)^2 + \frac{27}{4} ,
$$
so it is at least $\dfrac{27}{4}$ for every $x$ and has no real roots. A product vanishes if and only if one of its factors vanishes, therefore the only real root of $x^3 + 27$ is $-3$.
