Which constant $k$ makes the equality $x^2 + 6x + 2 = (x + 3)^2 + k$ true for every real $x$?

- $k = 7$
- $k = 11$
- $k = -1$
- $k = -7$

## Explanation

By the **square of a binomial** $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$, so $(x + 3)^2 + k = x^2 + 6x + 9 + k$. The terms in $x^2$ and in $x$ of the two sides already coincide. By the identity principle the constant terms must coincide as well, that is, $9 + k = 2$, therefore $k = -7$. Writing $x^2 + 6x + 2 = (x + 3)^2 - 7$ is **completing the square** of the trinomial.
