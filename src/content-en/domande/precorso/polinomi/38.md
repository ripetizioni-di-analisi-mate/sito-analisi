Which of the following numbers is a root of $P(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 3x + 2$?

- $1$
- $-2$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The four numbers are candidates in **finding rational roots**, since the numerators divide the constant term $2$ and the denominators divide the leading coefficient $2$. The value of $P$ at $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is
$$
P\left( \frac{1}{2} \right) = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{3}{4} - \frac{3}{2} + 2 = 0 ,
$$
therefore $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is a **root** of $P$. At the other candidates the polynomial takes the values $P(1) = -2$, $P(-2) = -20$ and $P\left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right) = \dfrac{5}{2}$, all different from $0$.
