Viewed as a trinomial in the variable $x$, the trinomial $x^2 - 5xy + 6y^2$ is equal to which product?

- $(x + 2y)(x + 3y)$
- $(x - y)(x - 6y)$
- $(x - 2y)(x - 3y)$
- $(x + y)(x - 6y)$

## Explanation

As a trinomial in $x$, the polynomial has the form $x^2 + sx + p$ with $s = -5y$ and $p = 6y^2$. Two monomials with sum $-5y$ and product $6y^2$ are $-2y$ and $-3y$, since $-2y - 3y = -5y$ and $(-2y)(-3y) = 6y^2$. By the **monic quadratic trinomial** rule we therefore have $x^2 - 5xy + 6y^2 = (x - 2y)(x - 3y)$. The pairs $2y$ and $3y$, $-y$ and $-6y$, $y$ and $-6y$ satisfy only one of the two conditions.
