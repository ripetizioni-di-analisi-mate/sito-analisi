Which statement about the polynomial $P(x) = x^3 + x + 1$ is true?

- $P$ has the root $1$
- $P$ has the root $-1$
- $P$ has no rational roots
- $P$ has no real roots

## Explanation

The leading coefficient and the constant term are both $1$, so by **finding rational roots** the only candidates are $1$ and $-1$. Since $P(1) = 3$ and $P(-1) = -1$, neither candidate is a root, therefore $P$ **has no rational roots**. The polynomial does have a real root between $-1$ and $0$, since $P(-1) = -1 < 0$, $P(0) = 1 > 0$ and the graph of a polynomial is a continuous curve. The existence of this root follows from the intermediate zero theorem, found in the chapter on limits and continuity of Mathematical Analysis 1.
