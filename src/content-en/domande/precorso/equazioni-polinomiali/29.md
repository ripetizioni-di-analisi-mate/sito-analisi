What are the real solutions of the equation $4x^4 - 5x^2 + 1 = 0$?

- $x = \pm 1$ and $x = \pm \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x = \pm 1$ and $x = \pm \dfrac{1}{4}$
- $x = 1$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{4}$
- $x = \pm 1$

## Explanation

The equation is **biquadratic**. With the substitution $t = x^2$ it becomes $4t^2 - 5t + 1 = 0$, which has discriminant $\Delta = 25 - 16 = 9$ and roots $t = \dfrac{5 \pm 3}{8}$, that is, $t = 1$ and $t = \dfrac{1}{4}$. Both values are positive, so each of them gives two solutions. From the equation $x^2 = 1$ it follows that $x = \pm 1$, and from the equation $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$ it follows that $x = \pm \dfrac{1}{2}$.
