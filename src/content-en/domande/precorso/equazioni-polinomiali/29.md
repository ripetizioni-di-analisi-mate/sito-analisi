In the equation $x^6 - 9x^3 + 8 = 0$ the unknown appears only through the powers $x^6$ and $x^3$. What are its real solutions?

- $x = \pm 1$ and $x = \pm 2$
- $x = 1$ and $x = 8$
- $x = -1$ and $x = -2$
- $x = 1$ and $x = 2$

## Explanation

Since $x^6 = (x^3)^2$, the substitution $t = x^3$ turns the equation into $t^2 - 9t + 8 = 0$. Two numbers with sum $9$ and product $8$ are $1$ and $8$, so $t = 1$ or $t = 8$. The equation $x^3 - 1 = 0$ factors as $(x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1) = 0$, and the trinomial has $\Delta = -3 < 0$, so the only real solution is $x = 1$. In the same way $x^3 - 8 = (x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4)$, with $\Delta = -12 < 0$, so $x^3 = 8$ has only the solution $x = 2$. The real solutions are therefore $x = 1$ and $x = 2$.
