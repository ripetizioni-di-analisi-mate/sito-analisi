A student divides both sides of the equation $2x = 6x$ by $x$, obtains $2 = 6$ and concludes that the equation has no solutions. Which statement is correct?

- The conclusion is wrong, because $x = 0$ is a solution
- The conclusion is correct, because $2 = 6$ is false
- The equation is an identity
- The equation has the solution $x = 3$

## Explanation

The second equivalence principle allows us to divide both sides only by a nonzero quantity, and $x$ can be zero. Moving $6x$ to the left-hand side gives $-4x = 0$, so $x = 0$. For $x = 0$ both sides equal $0$, so the equation has the unique solution $x = 0$ and **is not impossible**. Dividing by $x$ removed exactly this value.
