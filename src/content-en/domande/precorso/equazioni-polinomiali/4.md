What are the solutions of the equation $9x^2 - 6x + 1 = 0$?

- $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ (double)
- $x = 3$ (double)
- $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ (double)
- $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

The discriminant is $\Delta = 36 - 36 = 0$, so the equation has one **double real solution**, given by
$$
x = -\frac{b}{2a} = \frac{6}{18} = \frac{1}{3} .
$$
The same result is obtained by observing that the trinomial is the square $9x^2 - 6x + 1 = (3x - 1)^2$, which vanishes only for $3x - 1 = 0$, that is, for $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$.
