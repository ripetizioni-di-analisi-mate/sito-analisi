Taking the domain restriction into account, for which $x$ is the fraction $\dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ positive?

- $x > -1$
- $x < -1$
- $-1 < x < 1$ or $x > 1$
- $-1 \leq x < 1$ or $x > 1$

## Explanation

The domain restriction is $x \neq 1$. For $x \neq 1$ the fraction can be simplified, and we obtain
$$
\frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = \frac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x - 1} = x + 1 .
$$
The condition $\dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} > 0$ is therefore equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 > 0 \\[2pt]
x \neq 1
\end{cases}
$$
whose solutions are $-1 < x < 1$ or $x > 1$. The value $x = 1$ satisfies $x + 1 > 0$, but it makes the denominator zero and is therefore excluded.
