The equation $\dfrac{x^2}{3} - \dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{1}{6}$ has fractional coefficients. What are its solutions?

- $x = \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt{17}}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt{17}}{4}$
- $x = \dfrac{-3 \pm \sqrt{17}}{4}$
- $x = 1$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The least common multiple of the denominators is $6$. Multiplying every term on both sides by $6$ gives $2x^2 - 3x = 1$, that is, $2x^2 - 3x - 1 = 0$. The coefficients are $a = 2$, $b = -3$, $c = -1$, so the discriminant is $\Delta = 9 + 8 = 17$, where the term $-4ac = 8$ is positive because $a$ and $c$ have opposite signs. Since $\Delta > 0$, by the **quadratic formula** the solutions are
$$
x = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{17}}{4} .
$$
