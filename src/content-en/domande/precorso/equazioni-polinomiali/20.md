What are the solutions of the equation $x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$?

- $x = 4 \pm 2\sqrt{3}$
- $x = 2 \pm \sqrt{5}$
- $x = -2 \pm \sqrt{3}$
- $x = 2 \pm \sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

The coefficients are $a = 1$, $b = -4$, $c = 1$, so the discriminant is $\Delta = 16 - 4 = 12 > 0$ and there are two real solutions. By the **quadratic formula** we have
$$
x = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{12}}{2} .
$$
Since $\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} = 2\sqrt{3}$, the numerator is $4 \pm 2\sqrt{3}$. Dividing each term by $2$ we obtain the solutions $x = 2 \pm \sqrt{3}$.
