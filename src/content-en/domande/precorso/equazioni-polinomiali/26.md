For which $x$ is the product $(x - 2)(x + 1)$ equal to $4$?

- $x = 2$ and $x = -1$
- $x = 6$ and $x = 3$
- $x = 3$ and $x = -2$
- $x = -3$ and $x = 2$

## Explanation

The **zero product property** concerns a product equal to zero, so it does not apply directly to $(x - 2)(x + 1) = 4$. Expanding the product and moving $4$ to the left-hand side gives $x^2 - x - 2 - 4 = 0$, that is, $x^2 - x - 6 = 0$. Two numbers with sum $1$ and product $-6$ are $3$ and $-2$, so $x^2 - x - 6 = (x - 3)(x + 2)$. By the zero product property the solutions are $x = 3$ and $x = -2$; indeed $(3 - 2)(3 + 1) = 4$ and $(-2 - 2)(-2 + 1) = 4$.
