How many distinct real solutions does the equation $x^4 - 2x^2 = 0$ have?

- Two
- Three
- Four
- One

## Explanation

With the substitution $t = x^2$ the **biquadratic** equation becomes $t^2 - 2t = 0$, that is, $t\,(t - 2) = 0$, with solutions $t = 0$ and $t = 2$. From the equation $x^2 = 2$ we get the two solutions $x = \pm\sqrt{2}$. From the equation $x^2 = 0$ we get only $x = 0$, which is a double solution, since $x^4 - 2x^2 = x^2\,(x^2 - 2)$. Therefore there are three distinct real solutions, namely $-\sqrt{2}$, $0$ and $\sqrt{2}$.
