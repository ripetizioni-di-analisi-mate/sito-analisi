What can be said about the equation $(x + 2)(x - 3) = x^2 + 4$?

- It is linear and has the unique solution $x = -10$
- It is quadratic and has two distinct real solutions
- It is linear and has the unique solution $x = 10$
- It has no solutions

## Explanation

Expanding the product gives $x^2 - x - 6 = x^2 + 4$. Subtracting $x^2$ from both sides cancels the second-degree terms and leaves $-x - 6 = 4$, so the equation is **linear**. From $-x = 10$ it follows that $x = -10$, because the coefficient of $x$ is negative. Indeed for $x = -10$ both sides equal $104$.
