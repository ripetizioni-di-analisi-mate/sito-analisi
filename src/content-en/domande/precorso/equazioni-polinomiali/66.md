For which real numbers $x \neq 0$ is the reciprocal $\dfrac{1}{x}$ greater than $x$?

- $-1 < x < 0$ or $0 < x < 1$
- $-1 < x < 0$ or $x > 1$
- $x \leq -1$ or $0 < x \leq 1$
- $x < -1$ or $0 < x < 1$

## Explanation

The condition is the rational inequality $\dfrac{1}{x} > x$, with the domain restriction $x \neq 0$. Moving $x$ to the left-hand side and writing everything over the same denominator gives
$$
\frac{1 - x^2}{x} > 0 .
$$
The numerator $1 - x^2$ has roots $-1$ and $1$ and a negative coefficient of $x^2$, so it is positive for $-1 < x < 1$ and negative for $x < -1$ and for $x > 1$. The denominator $x$ is positive for $x > 0$. From the **sign chart** the quotient is positive for $x < -1$ and for $0 < x < 1$, negative for $-1 < x < 0$ and for $x > 1$. The direction $>$ excludes the zeros $-1$ and $1$ of the numerator, so the solutions are $x < -1$ or $0 < x < 1$. The inequality $1 > x^2$, obtained by multiplying both sides by $x$ without knowing its sign, is also satisfied by $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, whose reciprocal $-2$ is however less than $-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
