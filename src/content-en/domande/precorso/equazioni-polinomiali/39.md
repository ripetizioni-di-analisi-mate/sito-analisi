At which points does the polynomial $P(x) = (x - 3)^3 (x + 1)^2$ change sign?

- Only at $x = -1$
- Only at $x = 3$
- At $x = -1$ and at $x = 3$
- At no point

## Explanation

By the **sign of a factor raised to a power**, a polynomial changes sign at the roots of odd multiplicity and keeps its sign at those of even multiplicity. The root $3$ has multiplicity $3$, which is odd, so the polynomial changes sign at $x = 3$. The root $-1$ has multiplicity $2$, so the factor $(x + 1)^2$ is positive for $x \neq -1$ and the polynomial keeps its sign at $x = -1$. Therefore $P$ changes sign only at $x = 3$, where it goes from negative to positive.
