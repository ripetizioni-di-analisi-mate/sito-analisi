For which real numbers $x$ do we have $\sqrt{2}\,x - 2 < x$?

- $x > 2\sqrt{2} + 2$
- $x < 2\sqrt{2} - 2$
- $x > -2\sqrt{2} - 2$
- $x < 2\sqrt{2} + 2$

## Explanation

Moving $x$ to the left-hand side and $-2$ to the right-hand side gives $(\sqrt{2} - 1)\,x < 2$. The coefficient $\sqrt{2} - 1$ is positive, because $\sqrt{2} > 1$, so dividing by it preserves the direction and
$$
x < \frac{2}{\sqrt{2} - 1} = \frac{2\,(\sqrt{2} + 1)}{(\sqrt{2} - 1)(\sqrt{2} + 1)} = 2\sqrt{2} + 2 .
$$
The last step rationalizes the denominator, since $(\sqrt{2} - 1)(\sqrt{2} + 1) = 2 - 1 = 1$.
