A cyclist covers $60$ km at constant speed. If he rode $5$ km/h faster, he would take one hour less. What is his speed?

- $15$ km/h
- $20$ km/h
- $30$ km/h
- $12$ km/h

## Explanation

Letting $v$ be the speed in km/h, with $v > 0$, the cyclist takes $\dfrac{60}{v}$ hours, while at speed $v + 5$ he would take $\dfrac{60}{v + 5}$ hours. The condition is the rational equation $\dfrac{60}{v} - \dfrac{60}{v + 5} = 1$, with the domain restrictions $v \neq 0$ and $v \neq -5$. Multiplying both sides by $v\,(v + 5)$ gives $60\,(v + 5) - 60v = v\,(v + 5)$, that is, $v^2 + 5v - 300 = 0$. The discriminant is $\Delta = 25 + 1200 = 1225 = 35^2$, so by the **quadratic formula** $v = \dfrac{-5 \pm 35}{2}$, that is, $v = 15$ or $v = -20$. A travel speed is positive, so $v = -20$ is discarded and the cyclist rides at $15$ km/h. Indeed he takes $4$ hours at $15$ km/h and $3$ hours at $20$ km/h.
