At which points does the product $(x^2 + x + 1)(x^2 - 4)$ vanish?

- Only at $x = 2$
- At $x = \pm 2$ and at $x = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$
- At no point
- Only at $x = 2$ and at $x = -2$

## Explanation

By the **zero product property** the product is zero if and only if at least one factor is zero. The trinomial $x^2 + x + 1$ has discriminant $\Delta = 1 - 4 = -3 < 0$, so it is not zero for any real $x$. The binomial $x^2 - 4$ is zero when $x^2 = 4$, that is, for $x = 2$ and for $x = -2$. Therefore the product vanishes only at $x = \pm 2$.
