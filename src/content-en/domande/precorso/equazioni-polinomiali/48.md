When is the product $(x^2 + 1)(x - 3)$ negative?

- $x > 3$
- $x \leq 3$
- $x < -1$ or $1 < x < 3$
- $x < 3$

## Explanation

The factor $x^2 + 1$ is positive for every $x$, because it is the sum of a square and a positive number. The sign of the product is therefore the sign of $x - 3$. Since $x - 3 < 0$ for $x < 3$, the product is **negative** for $x < 3$. At $x = 3$ the product equals zero, which is not negative.
