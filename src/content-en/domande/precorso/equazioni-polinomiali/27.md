Which real numbers satisfy the equation $(x^2 - 2x)^2 - 2\,(x^2 - 2x) - 3 = 0$?

- $x = -1$, $x = 1$, $x = 3$
- $x = -1$ and $x = 3$
- $x = \pm\sqrt{3}$ and $x = \pm 1$
- $x = 1 \pm \sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

With the substitution $t = x^2 - 2x$ the equation becomes $t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0$, which has solutions $t = 3$ and $t = -1$. For $t = 3$ we solve $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$, which has solutions $x = 3$ and $x = -1$. For $t = -1$ we solve $x^2 - 2x + 1 = 0$, that is, $(x - 1)^2 = 0$, which has the double solution $x = 1$. Therefore the real solutions are $x = -1$, $x = 1$ and $x = 3$.
