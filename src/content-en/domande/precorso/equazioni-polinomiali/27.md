What are the solutions of the equation $2x^3 - 3x^2 - 3x + 2 = 0$?

- $x = -2$, $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, $x = 1$
- $x = 2$, $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, $x = 1$
- $x = 2$, $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $x = -1$
- $x = 2$, $x = 1$, $x = -1$

## Explanation

Let $P(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 3x + 2$. Since $P(2) = 16 - 12 - 6 + 2 = 0$, the binomial $x - 2$ divides $P$, and **synthetic division** gives $P(x) = (x - 2)(2x^2 + x - 1)$. The trinomial $2x^2 + x - 1$ has discriminant $\Delta = 1 + 8 = 9$ and roots $x = \dfrac{-1 \pm 3}{4}$, that is, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-1$. By the **zero product property** the solutions are $x = 2$, $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $x = -1$.
