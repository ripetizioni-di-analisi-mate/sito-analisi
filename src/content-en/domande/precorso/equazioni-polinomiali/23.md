What are the sum and the product of the solutions of the equation $2x^2 - 6x - 5 = 0$?

- Sum $-3$, product $-\dfrac{5}{2}$
- Sum $3$, product $-5$
- Sum $3$, product $-\dfrac{5}{2}$
- Sum $6$, product $-5$

## Explanation

The discriminant is $\Delta = 36 + 40 = 76 > 0$, so the real solutions $x_1$ and $x_2$ exist. By the theorem on the **sum and product of the roots**, with $a = 2$, $b = -6$, $c = -5$, we have
$$
x_1 + x_2 = -\frac{b}{a} = \frac{6}{2} = 3 , \qquad x_1 \cdot x_2 = \frac{c}{a} = -\frac{5}{2} .
$$
Both formulas involve division by the coefficient $a = 2$ of $x^2$.
