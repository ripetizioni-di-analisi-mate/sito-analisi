Increasing the edge of a cube by $1$ cm increases its volume by $37\ \mathrm{cm}^3$. How long is the edge of the original cube?

- $4$ cm
- $2\sqrt{3}$ cm
- No length, because the problem has no solutions
- $3$ cm

## Explanation

Letting $x$ be the length in centimeters of the original edge, with $x > 0$, the condition is the equation $(x + 1)^3 - x^3 = 37$. By the expansion of the cube of a binomial $(x + 1)^3 = x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 1$, so the terms $x^3$ cancel and the equation becomes $3x^2 + 3x + 1 = 37$, which is quadratic. Moving $37$ to the left-hand side and dividing by $3$ gives $x^2 + x - 12 = 0$. Two numbers with sum $-1$ and product $-12$ are $3$ and $-4$, so the solutions are $x = 3$ and $x = -4$. A length is positive, so $x = -4$ is discarded and the edge is $3$ cm long. Indeed $4^3 - 3^3 = 64 - 27 = 37$.
