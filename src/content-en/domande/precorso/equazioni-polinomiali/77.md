The roots of the trinomial $x^2 - 2x - 1$ are irrational. For which $x$ is the trinomial negative?

- $1 - \sqrt{2} < x < 1 + \sqrt{2}$
- $x < 1 - \sqrt{2}$ or $x > 1 + \sqrt{2}$
- $-1 - \sqrt{2} < x < -1 + \sqrt{2}$
- $2 - 2\sqrt{2} < x < 2 + 2\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

The discriminant is $\Delta = 4 + 4 = 8$, so the roots are
$$
x = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{8}}{2} = \frac{2 \pm 2\sqrt{2}}{2} = 1 \pm \sqrt{2} .
$$
The coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, so by the **sign of the quadratic trinomial** the trinomial is negative between the roots. The solutions are therefore $1 - \sqrt{2} < x < 1 + \sqrt{2}$.
