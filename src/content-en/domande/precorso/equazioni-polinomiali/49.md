One faucet alone fills a tub in $3$ hours; when a second faucet is also opened, the tub fills in $2$ hours. How many hours would the second faucet alone take to fill it?

- $1$ hour
- $6$ hours
- $5$ hours
- $\dfrac{6}{5}$ hours

## Explanation

In one hour the first faucet fills $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the tub, the second fills $\dfrac{1}{x}$, if $x$ is its time in hours, and the two together fill $\dfrac{1}{2}$. This gives the rational equation $\dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, with the domain restriction $x \neq 0$. Moving $\dfrac{1}{3}$ to the right-hand side gives $\dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{6}$, so $x = 6$. This value satisfies the domain restriction, so the second faucet alone takes $6$ hours.
