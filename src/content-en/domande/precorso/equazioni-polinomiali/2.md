What are the solutions of the equation $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$?

- $x = 1$ and $x = -6$
- $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $x = -3$
- $x = 3$ and $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $x = -1$ and $x = -\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The coefficients are $a = 2$, $b = 5$, $c = -3$, so the discriminant is $\Delta = 25 + 24 = 49 > 0$ and there are two real solutions. By the **quadratic formula**, with denominator $2a = 4$, we have
$$
x = \frac{-5 \pm 7}{4} .
$$
Therefore the solutions are $x = \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $x = -\dfrac{12}{4} = -3$.
