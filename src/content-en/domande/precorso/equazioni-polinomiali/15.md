- $x = 3$ and $x = -1$
- $x = 3$
- $x = -3$
- $x = 1 \pm \sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

Since $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$, the **domain conditions** are
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 \neq 0 \\[2pt]
x - 1 \neq 0
\end{cases}
$$
that is, $x \neq -1$ and $x \neq 1$. Moving every term to the left-hand side and reducing to the common denominator $(x - 1)(x + 1)$, the numerator becomes $x\,(x - 1) - (x + 1) - 2 = x^2 - 2x - 3 = (x - 3)(x + 1)$. By the **zero of a fraction** theorem the solutions are the zeros of the numerator that satisfy the domain conditions. The numerator vanishes at $x = 3$ and at $x = -1$; the value $-1$ makes a denominator vanish and is discarded, so the only solution is $x = 3$.
