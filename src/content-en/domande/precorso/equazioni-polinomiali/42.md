What is the domain of the equation $\dfrac{1}{x^2 - 4x} + \dfrac{2}{x^2 + 1} = 3$?

- The real numbers different from $0$ and $4$
- The real numbers different from $0$, $4$, $1$ and $-1$
- The real numbers different from $4$
- The real numbers different from $2$ and $-2$

## Explanation

The **domain restrictions** require every denominator to be nonzero. The first denominator factors as $x^2 - 4x = x\,(x - 4)$, which vanishes at $x = 0$ and at $x = 4$. The second denominator $x^2 + 1$ is the sum of a square and $1$, so it is positive for every $x$ and imposes no condition. Therefore the domain is the set of real numbers different from $0$ and $4$.
