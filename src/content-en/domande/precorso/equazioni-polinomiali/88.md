As the real parameter $a$ varies, which statement about the solutions of the inequality $(a - 1)\,x > 2$ is true?

- For $a > 1$ they are $x > \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$, for $a < 1$ they are $x < \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$, for $a = 1$ they are all real numbers
- For every $a \neq 1$ they are $x > \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$, for $a = 1$ there are none
- For $a > 1$ they are $x > \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$, for $a < 1$ they are $x < \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$, for $a = 1$ there are none
- For $a > 1$ they are $x < \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$, for $a < 1$ they are $x > \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$, for $a = 1$ there are none

## Explanation

For $a > 1$ the coefficient $a - 1$ is positive and dividing by it preserves the direction, so $x > \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$. For $a < 1$ the coefficient is negative and dividing by it reverses the direction, so $x < \dfrac{2}{a - 1}$. For $a = 1$ the inequality becomes $0 \cdot x > 2$, that is, $0 > 2$, which is false for every $x$, so there are no solutions.
