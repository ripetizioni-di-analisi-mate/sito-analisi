The figure shows the graph of a cubic polynomial $P(x)$, which meets the $x$-axis only at the points with $x$-coordinates $-2$ and $1$. Which of the following can be the expression of $P(x)$?

::grafico{f="(x+2)*(x-1)^2" xmin=-3 xmax=2.5 ymin=-5 ymax=6 punti="-2,0,pieno;1,0,pieno" legenda="y = P(x)"}

- $(x + 2)^2 (x - 1)$
- $-(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$
- $(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$
- $(x - 2)(x + 1)^2$

## Explanation

At $x = -2$ the graph crosses the $x$-axis and at $x = 1$ it touches it without crossing. By the theorem on the **sign of a factor raised to a power**, a polynomial changes sign at the roots of odd multiplicity and keeps its sign at those of even multiplicity, so $-2$ is a simple root and $1$ is a double root. The polynomial $(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$ has these roots and is positive for $x > -2$ with $x \neq 1$, as in the graph; for example it equals $2$ at $x = 0$. The polynomial $-(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$ has the same roots but the opposite sign.
