- Sufficient and not necessary
- Necessary and not sufficient
- Necessary and sufficient
- Neither necessary nor sufficient

## Explanation

Let $p$ be the condition "$n$ is a multiple of $9$" and $q$ the condition "$n$ is a multiple of $3$".

Every multiple of $9$ can be written as $9k = 3 \cdot 3k$ with $k$ natural, so it is a multiple of $3$. Therefore the implication $p \Rightarrow q$ is true and $p$ is a **sufficient condition** for $q$.

The converse implication $q \Rightarrow p$ is false, because $n = 6$ is a multiple of $3$ and the equation $6 = 9k$ has no natural solutions. Consequently "being a multiple of $9$" is a sufficient and not necessary condition for "being a multiple of $3$".
