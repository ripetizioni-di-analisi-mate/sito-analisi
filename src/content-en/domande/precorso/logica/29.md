For an integer $n$, of which proposition is the condition "$n$ ends with the digit $0$" a sufficient but not necessary condition?

- $n$ is a multiple of $10$
- $n$ is a multiple of $100$
- $n$ is a multiple of $5$
- $n$ is odd

## Explanation

An integer that ends with the digit $0$ can be written as $n = 10k = 5 \cdot 2k$ with $k$ an integer, so it is a multiple of $5$. Therefore the condition is **sufficient** for "$n$ is a multiple of $5$".

The condition is not necessary, because $15$ is a multiple of $5$ and ends with the digit $5$.

An integer ends with $0$ if and only if it is a multiple of $10$, so for "$n$ is a multiple of $10$" the condition is necessary and sufficient. For "$n$ is a multiple of $100$" the condition is only necessary, because $10$ ends with $0$ and is not a multiple of $100$. Every integer that ends with $0$ is even, therefore the condition is not sufficient for "$n$ is odd".
