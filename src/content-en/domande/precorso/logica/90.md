We want to prove that among any four integers two have a difference divisible by $3$. Which argument proves this for every choice?

- There are three possible remainders and four integers, so a remainder repeats.
- Checking the four consecutive integers $1,2,3,4$ suffices.
- For a contradiction, one starts by assuming there is a pair whose difference is divisible by $3$.
- At least one of the four integers must be a multiple of $3$.

## Explanation

Division by three gives possible remainders $0,1,2$. Four integers cannot occupy four distinct classes. The difference of two integers with the same remainder is a multiple of three.
