The implication "if $a + b \geq 10$, then $a \geq 5$ or $b \geq 5$", with $a$ and $b$ real, is proved through its contrapositive. Which implication must be verified?

- If $a < 5$ and $b < 5$, then $a + b < 10$
- If $a < 5$ or $b < 5$, then $a + b < 10$
- If $a \geq 5$ or $b \geq 5$, then $a + b \geq 10$
- If $a + b < 10$, then $a < 5$ and $b < 5$

## Explanation

The contrapositive of $p \Rightarrow q$ is $\lnot q \Rightarrow \lnot p$. Here $p$ is $a + b \geq 10$ and $q$ is $a \geq 5 \lor b \geq 5$.

By De Morgan's laws $\lnot q$ is $a < 5 \land b < 5$, and $\lnot p$ is $a + b < 10$. Therefore the contrapositive is "if $a < 5$ and $b < 5$, then $a + b < 10$", which is verified by adding the two inequalities.

The implication with "or" in the hypothesis does not negate $q$ correctly and is false for $a = 1$, $b = 20$. The implication "if $a \geq 5$ or $b \geq 5$, then $a + b \geq 10$" is the converse, and the last one negates hypothesis and conclusion without swapping them.
