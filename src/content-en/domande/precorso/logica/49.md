For a triangle, being isosceles is a necessary condition for being equilateral. Which triangle shows that being isosceles is not a sufficient condition for being equilateral?

- The triangle with sides $5$, $5$, $5$
- The triangle with sides $3$, $4$, $5$
- The triangle with sides $5$, $5$, $8$
- The triangle with sides $4$, $5$, $6$

## Explanation

The condition "isosceles" is not sufficient for "equilateral" if there exists an isosceles triangle that is not equilateral.

The triangle with sides $5$, $5$, $8$ exists, because $5 + 5 > 8$, and it has two equal sides, so it is isosceles. The third side differs from the other two, therefore the triangle **is not equilateral** and shows that the condition is not sufficient.

The triangle with sides $5$, $5$, $5$ is isosceles and equilateral, so it is consistent with sufficiency. The triangles with sides $3$, $4$, $5$ and $4$, $5$, $6$ have all sides different, therefore they are not isosceles and say nothing about the sufficiency of the condition.
