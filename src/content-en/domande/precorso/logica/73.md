It is stated that an equation has at most two real solutions. In which case is this statement false?

- When the equation has at least three real solutions
- When the equation has at least two real solutions
- When the equation has no real solutions
- When the equation has exactly two real solutions

## Explanation

Denoting by $s$ the number of real solutions, the statement "at most two solutions" means $s \leq 2$.

The statement is false when its negation $s > 2$ is true. Since $s$ is a natural number, $s > 2$ is equivalent to $s \geq 3$, that is, the equation has **at least three real solutions**.

With no solutions or with exactly two solutions $s \leq 2$ holds, so the statement is true. The condition "at least two solutions" includes the case $s = 2$, in which the statement is true.
