In the proof by contradiction that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational one writes $\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{m}{n}$ with $m$ and $n$ positive integers with no common factors, and obtains $m^2 = 2n^2$. Which conclusion follows immediately from this equality?

- $n$ is even
- $m = 2n$
- $m$ and $n$ are odd
- $m$ is even

## Explanation

The number $2n^2$ is twice an integer, so $m^2$ is even.

The square of an odd number is odd, because $(2a + 1)^2 = 2(2a^2 + 2a) + 1$. Therefore, if $m$ were odd, $m^2$ would also be odd, and so **$m$ is even**.

The parity of $n$ is obtained only at the next step, by substituting $m = 2k$. The equality $m = 2n$ would give $4n^2 = 2n^2$, that is, $n = 0$, which is excluded.
