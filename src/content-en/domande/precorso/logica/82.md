Both $p\Rightarrow q$ and $r\Rightarrow q$ are true. Which conclusion holds for every choice of the three truth values?

- $(p\lor r)\Rightarrow q$
- $q\Rightarrow(p\lor r)$, taking the converse of $(p\lor r)\Rightarrow q$ to be its contrapositive.
- $p\Rightarrow r$
- $q\Rightarrow(p\land r)$

## Explanation

When at least one of $p$ and $r$ is true, one of the assumptions guarantees $q$. If both are false, the proposed implication has a false antecedent. Neither assumption requires $p$ or $r$ to be true whenever $q$ is true.
