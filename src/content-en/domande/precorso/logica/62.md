To prove that the proposition $\exists x \in \mathbb{R} : x^2 + 4 = 2x$ is false, which reasoning is correct?

- Find a real $x$ for which $x^2 + 4 \neq 2x$
- Check that $x = 0$ does not satisfy the equality
- Show that $x^2 - 2x + 4 > 0$ for every real $x$
- Find a real $x$ for which $x^2 + 4 = 2x$

## Explanation

An existential proposition is false when its negation $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} : x^2 + 4 \neq 2x$, which is a universal proposition, is true. To prove it, an argument valid for every real $x$ is needed.

We have $x^2 - 2x + 4 = (x - 1)^2 + 3 \geq 3$, so $x^2 - 2x + 4 > 0$ for every real $x$. Therefore $x^2 + 4 \neq 2x$ for every $x$, and the original proposition is **false**.

A single value that does not satisfy the equality, such as $x = 0$, does not exclude that another value satisfies it. Finding an $x$ that satisfies the equality would instead prove that the proposition is true.
