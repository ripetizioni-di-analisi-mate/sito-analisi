One proves directly that, if $n$ is odd, then $n^2 - 1$ is a multiple of $4$. Setting $n = 2a + 1$ with $a$ an integer, which expression for $n^2 - 1$ is obtained?

- $4a^2$
- $4a^2 + 2a$
- $4a^2 + 4a + 2$
- $4(a^2 + a)$

## Explanation

Squaring the binomial gives $(2a + 1)^2 = 4a^2 + 4a + 1$. Subtracting $1$ we obtain
$$
n^2 - 1 = 4a^2 + 4a = 4(a^2 + a) .
$$
Since $a^2 + a$ is an integer, $n^2 - 1$ is a **multiple of $4$**.

The expression $4a^2$ is obtained by neglecting the cross term $2 \cdot 2a \cdot 1 = 4a$, and the expression $4a^2 + 4a + 2$ is obtained by adding $1$ instead of subtracting it.
