Between the proposition $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} : x^2 \geq 1$ and its negation, which one is true?

- The proposition is true, because for $x = 1$ we have $x^2 = 1 \geq 1$
- The negation is true, because for $x = 0$ we have $x^2 = 0 < 1$
- The proposition is true, because $x^2 \geq 0$ for every real $x$
- The negation is true, because for $x = -1$ we have $x^2 < 1$

## Explanation

Negation swaps $\forall$ with $\exists$ and negates the predicate, so the negation of $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} : x^2 \geq 1$ is $\exists x \in \mathbb{R} : x^2 < 1$.

For $x = 0$ we have $x^2 = 0 < 1$, therefore $x = 0$ satisfies the predicate and the negation is **true**. Consequently the original proposition is false, and $x = 0$ is a counterexample to it.

The value $x = 1$ satisfies $x^2 \geq 1$, but a universal proposition requires the predicate to hold for *every* real $x$. The value $x = -1$ is not a counterexample, because $x^2 = 1 \geq 1$.
