For every integer $m$ the implication "if $m$ is a multiple of $9$, then the sum of the digits of $m$ is a multiple of $9$" is true. The sum of the digits of an integer $m$ equals $20$. What can be deduced from the implication?

- $m$ is a multiple of $9$
- Nothing can be deduced about $m$ from the implication
- $m$ is not a multiple of $9$
- $m$ is a multiple of $2$

## Explanation

The implication is equivalent to its **contrapositive**, that is, to "if the sum of the digits of $m$ is not a multiple of $9$, then $m$ is not a multiple of $9$".

The number $20$ is not a multiple of $9$, so the hypothesis of the contrapositive is true. Therefore **$m$ is not a multiple of $9$**.

The implication says nothing about divisibility by $2$. For example $992$ and $389$ both have digit sum $20$, and the first is even while the second is odd.
