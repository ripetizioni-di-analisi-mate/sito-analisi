The equation $x^2 + bx + c = 0$, with $b$ and $c$ real, has two distinct real solutions if and only if $b^2 - 4c > 0$. Which of the following conditions on $b$ and $c$ guarantees two distinct real solutions without being necessary?

- $b^2 - 4c \geq 0$
- $c < 0$
- $b^2 - 4c > 0$
- $b > 0$

## Explanation

If $c < 0$, then $-4c > 0$ and so $b^2 - 4c > b^2 \geq 0$. Therefore the discriminant is positive and the condition $c < 0$ is **sufficient**.

The condition is not necessary, because for $b = 3$ and $c = 1$ we have $b^2 - 4c = 5 > 0$ with $c > 0$.

The condition $b^2 - 4c > 0$ is necessary and sufficient, so it does not answer the question. The condition $b^2 - 4c \geq 0$ is only necessary, because for $b = 2$ and $c = 1$ the discriminant is zero and the equation has the single solution $x = -1$. The condition $b > 0$ is not sufficient, because for $b = 1$ and $c = 1$ the discriminant equals $-3$.
