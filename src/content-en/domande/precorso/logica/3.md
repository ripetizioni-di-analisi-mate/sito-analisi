What is the negation of the proposition $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} : x \geq 0 \ \land \ x^2 \geq 0$?

- $\exists x \in \mathbb{R} : x < 0 \ \land \ x^2 < 0$
- $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} : x < 0 \ \lor \ x^2 < 0$
- $\exists x \in \mathbb{R} : x < 0 \ \lor \ x^2 < 0$
- $\exists x \in \mathbb{R} : x \leq 0 \ \lor \ x^2 \leq 0$

## Explanation

By the **negation of quantifiers** the symbol $\forall$ becomes $\exists$ and the predicate $x \geq 0 \land x^2 \geq 0$ is negated.

The predicate is a conjunction, so by **De Morgan's laws** its negation is the disjunction of the negations of the two parts. The negation of $x \geq 0$ is $x < 0$ and the negation of $x^2 \geq 0$ is $x^2 < 0$, therefore the required negation is
$$
\exists x \in \mathbb{R} : x < 0 \ \lor \ x^2 < 0 .
$$
This proposition is true, because for $x = -1$ the first alternative $x < 0$ is satisfied.
