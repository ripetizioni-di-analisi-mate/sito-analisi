In which case is the proposition $\lnot (p \lor \lnot q)$ true?

- $p$ false and $q$ true
- $p$ true and $q$ false
- $p$ and $q$ both false
- $p$ and $q$ both true

## Explanation

By **De Morgan's laws** the negation of a disjunction is the conjunction of the negations, so $\lnot (p \lor \lnot q) \equiv \lnot p \land \lnot (\lnot q)$.

Double negation gives back the original proposition, therefore $\lnot (\lnot q)$ has the same truth table as $q$ and
$$
\lnot (p \lor \lnot q) \equiv \lnot p \land q .
$$
A conjunction is true only when both parts are true, so the proposition is true exactly when $p$ is **false** and $q$ is **true**.
