The implication "if $x = 3$, then $x^2 = 9$" is true for every real $x$. Which statement about its converse is correct?

- The converse is true, because it is equivalent to the original implication
- The converse is true, because $3^2 = 9$
- The converse is false, because for $x = 0$ we have $x^2 \neq 9$
- The converse is false, because for $x = -3$ we have $x^2 = 9$ and $x \neq 3$

## Explanation

The converse is obtained by swapping hypothesis and conclusion, so it is "if $x^2 = 9$, then $x = 3$".

For $x = -3$ the hypothesis is true, because $(-3)^2 = 9$, and the conclusion is false. Therefore the converse is **false**.

For $x = 0$ the hypothesis $x^2 = 9$ is false, so for $x = 0$ the converse is true and $x = 0$ is not a counterexample. The equivalence holds between an implication and its contrapositive, while the converse can have a different truth value.
