Let $n$ be a natural number. What is the negation of the implication "if $n$ is a multiple of $6$, then $n$ is a multiple of $4$"?

- $n$ is a multiple of $6$ and $n$ is not a multiple of $4$
- If $n$ is a multiple of $6$, then $n$ is not a multiple of $4$
- If $n$ is not a multiple of $6$, then $n$ is not a multiple of $4$
- $n$ is not a multiple of $6$ and $n$ is a multiple of $4$

## Explanation

Let $p$ be the proposition "$n$ is a multiple of $6$" and $q$ the proposition "$n$ is a multiple of $4$".

By the **negation of an implication** we have $\lnot (p \Rightarrow q) \equiv p \land \lnot q$, so the negation of an implication is a conjunction. Therefore the required negation is "$n$ is a multiple of $6$ and $n$ is not a multiple of $4$".

For $n = 6$ this conjunction is true, because $6$ is a multiple of $6$ and leaves remainder $2$ when divided by $4$. Consequently, for $n = 6$ the original implication is false.
