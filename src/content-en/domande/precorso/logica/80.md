An alleged proof that $2 = 1$ starts from two equal real numbers $a = b$, with $b \neq 0$, and writes in succession
$$
a^2 = ab, \quad a^2 - b^2 = ab - b^2, \quad (a + b)(a - b) = b(a - b), \quad a + b = b, \quad 2b = b .
$$
Which step is not legitimate?

- Multiplying both sides of $a = b$ by $a$
- Subtracting $b^2$ from both sides of $a^2 = ab$
- Dividing both sides by $a - b$
- Factoring $a^2 - b^2$ as the product $(a + b)(a - b)$

## Explanation

Both sides of an equality can be divided only by a nonzero number.

By hypothesis $a = b$, so $a - b = 0$. Therefore the step from $(a + b)(a - b) = b(a - b)$ to $a + b = b$ divides by zero and **is not legitimate**. Indeed the equality $(a + b) \cdot 0 = b \cdot 0$ is true whatever $a + b$ and $b$ are.

Multiplying by $a$, subtracting $b^2$ and factoring the difference of squares are correct steps.
