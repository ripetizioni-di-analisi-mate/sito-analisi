What is the negation of the proposition $\exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 = 10$, and what is its truth value?

- $\exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 \neq 10$, and it is true
- $\forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 \neq 10$, and it is false
- $\forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 < 10$, and it is true
- $\forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 \neq 10$, and it is true

## Explanation

Negation swaps $\exists$ with $\forall$ and negates the predicate, so the negation of $\exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 = 10$ is $\forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 \neq 10$. The negation of $n^2 = 10$ is $n^2 \neq 10$, which includes both $n^2 < 10$ and $n^2 > 10$.

Since the square of a natural number increases as the number increases, for $n \leq 3$ we have $n^2 \leq 9$ and for $n \geq 4$ we have $n^2 \geq 16$. Hence no natural number has square equal to $10$, so the negation is **true**.

The number $\sqrt{10}$ satisfies $n^2 = 10$, but it lies between $3$ and $4$ and is therefore not a natural number. Consequently the original proposition is false.
