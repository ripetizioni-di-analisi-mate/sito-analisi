What is the negation of the proposition "$\forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 + n$ is even", and what is its truth value?

- $\exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 + n$ is odd, and it is true
- $\forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 + n$ is odd, and it is false
- $\exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 + n$ is even, and it is false
- $\exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 + n$ is odd, and it is false

## Explanation

Negation swaps $\forall$ with $\exists$ and negates the predicate. The negation of "$n^2 + n$ is even" is "$n^2 + n$ is odd", so the negation is "$\exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n^2 + n$ is odd".

We have $n^2 + n = n(n + 1)$, the product of two consecutive natural numbers. One of the two factors is even, so $n^2 + n$ is even for every natural $n$.

Therefore the original proposition is true and its negation is **false**.
