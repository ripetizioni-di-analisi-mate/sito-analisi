Let $A$ be a subset of $\mathbb{R}$. What is the negation of the proposition $\exists M \in \mathbb{R} \ \forall x \in A : x \leq M$?

- $\forall M \in \mathbb{R} \ \exists x \in A : x \geq M$
- $\exists M \in \mathbb{R} \ \forall x \in A : x > M$
- $\forall x \in A \ \exists M \in \mathbb{R} : x > M$
- $\forall M \in \mathbb{R} \ \exists x \in A : x > M$

## Explanation

With two quantifiers we proceed from the outside in, so $\exists M$ becomes $\forall M$ and $\forall x$ becomes $\exists x$, in the same order.

Then the predicate is negated, and the negation of $x \leq M$ is $x > M$. Therefore the required negation is
$$
\forall M \in \mathbb{R} \ \exists x \in A : x > M .
$$
The original proposition states that there exists a real number greater than or equal to all the elements of $A$. The negation states that for every real number $M$ there exists an element of $A$ greater than $M$.
