A function $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is strictly increasing if
$$
\forall x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R} : x_1 < x_2 \Rightarrow f(x_1) < f(x_2) .
$$
Which proposition states that $f$ is not strictly increasing?

- $\exists x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R} : x_1 < x_2 \Rightarrow f(x_1) \geq f(x_2)$
- $\forall x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R} : x_1 < x_2 \Rightarrow f(x_1) \geq f(x_2)$
- $\exists x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R} : x_1 < x_2 \land f(x_1) > f(x_2)$
- $\exists x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R} : x_1 < x_2 \land f(x_1) \geq f(x_2)$

## Explanation

By the **negation of quantifiers**, the symbol $\forall$ becomes $\exists$ and the predicate is negated; the predicate is the implication $x_1 < x_2 \Rightarrow f(x_1) < f(x_2)$.

The negation of an implication is the conjunction of the hypothesis with the negation of the conclusion. The negation of $f(x_1) < f(x_2)$ is $f(x_1) \geq f(x_2)$, so the required proposition is
$$
\exists x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R} : x_1 < x_2 \land f(x_1) \geq f(x_2) .
$$
In words, there exist two points $x_1 < x_2$ at which the value $f(x_2)$ is less than or equal to $f(x_1)$. The condition $f(x_1) > f(x_2)$ excludes the case $f(x_1) = f(x_2)$, which is already enough to negate strict increase, as happens for a constant function.
