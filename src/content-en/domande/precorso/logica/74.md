Which implication is equivalent to $\lnot q \Rightarrow p$?

- $p \Rightarrow \lnot q$
- $q \Rightarrow \lnot p$
- $p \land \lnot q$
- $\lnot p \Rightarrow q$

## Explanation

The **contrapositive** of an implication is obtained by swapping hypothesis and conclusion and negating both, and it is equivalent to the original implication.

For $\lnot q \Rightarrow p$ the negation of the conclusion is $\lnot p$ and the negation of the hypothesis is $\lnot (\lnot q)$, that is, $q$. Therefore the contrapositive is $\lnot p \Rightarrow q$.

The implication $p \Rightarrow \lnot q$ is the converse and $q \Rightarrow \lnot p$ negates hypothesis and conclusion without swapping them. For $p$ and $q$ both true these two implications are false, while $\lnot q \Rightarrow p$ is true, so they are not equivalent to $\lnot q \Rightarrow p$. For $p$ false and $q$ true the conjunction $p \land \lnot q$ is false and $\lnot q \Rightarrow p$ is true.
