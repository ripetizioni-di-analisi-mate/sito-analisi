To prove that the sum of an even integer and an odd integer is odd, the two integers are written as $2k$ and $2k + 1$, with $k$ an integer. Which change makes the proof valid for every pair consisting of an even and an odd integer?

- Require $k > 0$
- Write the two integers as $2k$ and $2h + 1$, with $k$ and $h$ integers
- Check the property for $k = 1$
- Write the two integers as $2k + 1$ and $2k$

## Explanation

With the same $k$ the numbers $2k$ and $2k + 1$ are consecutive, so this notation describes only pairs such as $4$ and $5$ and does not include pairs such as $4$ and $7$.

An arbitrary even integer and an arbitrary odd integer are instead written as $m = 2k$ and $n = 2h + 1$, with $k$ and $h$ independent integers. Then $m + n = 2k + 2h + 1 = 2(k + h) + 1$. Since $k + h$ is an integer, the sum is **odd** for every pair.

Requiring $k > 0$ or swapping the order of the two numbers leaves the numbers consecutive, and checking $k = 1$ tests a single case. Therefore none of these changes proves the statement for every pair.
