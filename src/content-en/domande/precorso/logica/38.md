What is the negation of the proposition $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} \ \exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n > x$?

- $\exists x \in \mathbb{R} \ \forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n < x$
- $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} \ \exists n \in \mathbb{N} : n \leq x$
- $\exists x \in \mathbb{R} \ \forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n \leq x$
- $\exists n \in \mathbb{N} \ \forall x \in \mathbb{R} : n \leq x$

## Explanation

With two quantifiers we proceed from the outside in, so $\forall x$ becomes $\exists x$ and $\exists n$ becomes $\forall n$, in the same order.

Then the predicate is negated, and the negation of $n > x$ is $n \leq x$. Therefore the required negation is
$$
\exists x \in \mathbb{R} \ \forall n \in \mathbb{N} : n \leq x ,
$$
which states that there exists a real number greater than or equal to every natural number.

The case $n = x$ makes the inequality $n > x$ false, so the negation contains the non-strict inequality $n \leq x$.
