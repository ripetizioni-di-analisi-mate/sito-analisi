For which set $D$ is $\forall x\in D\ \exists y\in D:\ y>x$ true even though replacing $>$ by $<$ makes the statement false?

- $\mathbb Z$
- $\{-2,-1,0\}$, because when $x=-2$ one can choose $y=-1$.
- $\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$
- $\{0\}$

## Explanation

Every natural number $x$ has a successor $x+1$, but $0$ has no smaller member. The integers are unbounded in both directions. Each of the other two sets has a greatest element.
