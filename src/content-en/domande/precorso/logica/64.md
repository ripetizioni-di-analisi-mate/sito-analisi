For real $x$, in which of the following pairs of conditions is the first a necessary and sufficient condition for the second?

- $x^2 = 9$ and $x = 3$
- $x > 0$ and $x > 1$
- $x \geq 1$ and $x > 1$
- $x^3 = -27$ and $x = -3$

## Explanation

The first condition is necessary and sufficient for the second when each implies the other.

The cube takes each real value only once and $(-3)^3 = -27$, so $x^3 = -27 \Leftrightarrow x = -3$. Therefore in the pair $x^3 = -27$ and $x = -3$ the first condition is **necessary and sufficient** for the second.

The equality $x^2 = 9$ is also satisfied by $x = -3$, so it is not sufficient for $x = 3$. The conditions $x > 0$ and $x \geq 1$ are satisfied by $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and by $x = 1$ respectively, which do not satisfy $x > 1$, therefore they are only necessary.
