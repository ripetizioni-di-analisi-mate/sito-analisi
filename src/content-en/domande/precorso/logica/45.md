Which statement about the proposition "for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the number $n^2 + n + 41$ is prime" is correct?

- It is false, and $n = 40$ is a counterexample
- It is true, because it holds for $n = 1$, $n = 2$ and $n = 3$
- It is false, and $n = 1$ is a counterexample
- It is true, because $41$ is a prime number

## Explanation

For $n = 40$ we have
$$
40^2 + 40 + 41 = 40 \cdot 41 + 41 = 41 \cdot 41 = 1681 ,
$$
which is not prime. So $n = 40$ is a **counterexample** and the proposition is false.

For $n = 1$ we obtain $43$, which is prime, therefore $n = 1$ is not a counterexample.

Checking a few values of $n$ does not prove a universal proposition, because an argument valid for every $n$ is needed.
