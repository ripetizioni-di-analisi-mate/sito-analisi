We want to prove by contradiction that, for every integer $n$, if $3n + 2$ is odd then $n$ is odd. From which assumption does the proof start?

- $3n + 2$ is even and $n$ is even
- $3n + 2$ is even and $n$ is odd
- $3n + 2$ is odd and $n$ is even
- $3n + 2$ is odd and $n$ is odd

## Explanation

To prove $p \Rightarrow q$ by contradiction one assumes $p \land \lnot q$, which is the negation of the implication. Here one therefore assumes that $3n + 2$ is odd and that $n$ is even.

From $n = 2a$ with $a$ an integer it follows that $3n + 2 = 6a + 2 = 2(3a + 1)$, which is even. This contradicts the hypothesis that $3n + 2$ is odd.

Therefore the assumption is false and the implication is **true**.
