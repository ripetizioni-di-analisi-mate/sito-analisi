For how many natural numbers $n$ with $1 \leq n \leq 40$ is the proposition "$n$ is a perfect square or $n$ is a multiple of $4$" true?

- $3$
- $16$
- $13$
- $27$

## Explanation

The perfect squares between $1$ and $40$ are $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36$, that is, $6$ numbers. The multiples of $4$ are $4, 8, 12, \dots, 40$, that is, $10$ numbers.

A disjunction is true when at least one of its two parts is true, so its truth set is the union of the two lists. The numbers $4$, $16$ and $36$ appear in both lists and are counted only once in the union. Therefore the proposition is true for $6 + 10 - 3 = 13$ numbers.

The sum $6 + 10 = 16$ counts the numbers $4$, $16$ and $36$ twice. The value $3$ counts only the numbers for which both parts are true, that is, the truth set of the conjunction, and the value $27$ counts the numbers for which the disjunction is false.
