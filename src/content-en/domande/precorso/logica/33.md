For natural $n$, which of the following is a necessary condition for $n$ to be a multiple of $10$?

- $n$ is a multiple of $20$
- $n$ is even
- $n$ is a multiple of $100$
- $n$ ends in the digit $5$

## Explanation

A condition $q$ is necessary for $p$ when the implication $p \Rightarrow q$ holds.

If $n$ is a multiple of $10$, then $n = 10k = 2 \cdot 5k$, so $n$ is even. Therefore "$n$ is even" is a **necessary** condition for $n$ to be a multiple of $10$.

The number $n = 10$ is a multiple of $10$, is not a multiple of $20$ or of $100$, and ends in the digit $0$. So the other three conditions are not necessary; being a multiple of $20$ or of $100$ is instead a sufficient condition.
