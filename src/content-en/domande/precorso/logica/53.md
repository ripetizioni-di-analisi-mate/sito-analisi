For two propositions $p$ and $q$, the compound proposition $p \land (p \lor q)$ is equivalent to a simpler proposition. Which one?

- $q$
- $p$
- $p \land q$
- $p \lor q$

## Explanation

If $p$ is true, the disjunction $p \lor q$ is also true, so $p \land (p \lor q)$ is the conjunction of two true propositions and is true.

If $p$ is false, the conjunction $p \land (p \lor q)$ has a false part, therefore it is false whatever $q$ is.

The compound proposition thus has in every row of the truth table the same value as $p$, that is, $p \land (p \lor q) \equiv p$.

For $p$ true and $q$ false the compound proposition is true, while $q$ and $p \land q$ are false. For $p$ false and $q$ true the compound proposition is false, while $p \lor q$ is true.
