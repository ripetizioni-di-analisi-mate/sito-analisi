The proposition $\forall \varepsilon > 0 \ \exists n \in \mathbb{N}, \ n \geq 1 : \dfrac{1}{n} < \varepsilon$ is true. For $\varepsilon = \dfrac{1}{50}$, which of the following values of $n$ satisfies the inequality?

- $n = 10$
- $n = 40$
- $n = 50$
- $n = 100$

## Explanation

For $\varepsilon = \dfrac{1}{50}$ we look for $n \geq 1$ with $\dfrac{1}{n} < \dfrac{1}{50}$. For positive numbers this inequality is equivalent to $n > 50$.

Therefore $n = 100$ satisfies the inequality, because $\dfrac{1}{100} < \dfrac{1}{50}$.

For $n = 50$ we have $\dfrac{1}{n} = \varepsilon$, so the strict inequality does not hold. For $n = 40$ and for $n = 10$ we have $\dfrac{1}{n} > \dfrac{1}{50}$.
