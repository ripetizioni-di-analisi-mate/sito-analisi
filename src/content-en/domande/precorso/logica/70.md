A real number $M$ is the maximum of a set $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ if
$$
M \in A \ \land \ \forall x \in A : x \leq M .
$$
Which proposition states that $M$ is not the maximum of $A$?

- $M \notin A \ \land \ \exists x \in A : x > M$
- $M \notin A \ \lor \ \forall x \in A : x > M$
- $M \notin A \ \lor \ \exists x \in A : x > M$
- $M \notin A \ \lor \ \exists x \in A : x \geq M$

## Explanation

The definition is the conjunction of $M \in A$ and of $\forall x \in A : x \leq M$. By **De Morgan's laws** its negation is the disjunction of the negations of the two parts.

The negation of $M \in A$ is $M \notin A$. By the **negation of quantifiers** the negation of $\forall x \in A : x \leq M$ is $\exists x \in A : x > M$. Therefore $M$ is not the maximum of $A$ if and only if
$$
M \notin A \ \lor \ \exists x \in A : x > M .
$$
For example $M = 1$ is not the maximum of $A = [0, 1)$, because $1 \notin A$, even though every element of $A$ is less than $1$. In this case the conjunction with $\land$ is false, so it does not describe all the cases in which $M$ is not the maximum.

The proposition with $x \geq M$ is true for every $M \in A$, because it is enough to choose $x = M$, therefore it is true also when $M$ is the maximum. The proposition with $\forall x \in A : x > M$ is false for $A = \{0, 2\}$ and $M = 0$, even though $M$ is not the maximum, because $2 > 0$.
