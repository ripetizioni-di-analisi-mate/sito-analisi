Which of the following propositions is true?

- $\exists y \in \mathbb{R} \ \forall x \in \mathbb{R} : x + y = 0$
- $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} \ \exists y \in \mathbb{R} : x + y = 0$
- $\exists y \in \mathbb{R} \ \forall x \in \mathbb{R} : x \cdot y = 1$
- $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} \ \exists y \in \mathbb{R} : x \cdot y = 1$

## Explanation

In the proposition $\forall x \ \exists y$ the number $y$ may depend on $x$. For every real $x$ the number $y = -x$ satisfies $x + y = 0$, so $\forall x \in \mathbb{R} \ \exists y \in \mathbb{R} : x + y = 0$ is **true**.

In the proposition $\exists y \ \forall x$ a single $y$ is needed for all $x$. For $x = 0$ the equation $x + y = 0$ requires $y = 0$ and for $x = 1$ it requires $y = -1$, therefore $\exists y \in \mathbb{R} \ \forall x \in \mathbb{R} : x + y = 0$ is false.

For $x = 0$ we have $x \cdot y = 0 \neq 1$ whatever $y$ is, so the two propositions with $x \cdot y = 1$ are both false.
