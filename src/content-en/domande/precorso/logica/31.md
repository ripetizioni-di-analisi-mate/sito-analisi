For natural $n$, what kind of condition is "$n$ is divisible by $2$ and by $3$" for "$n$ is divisible by $6$"?

- Sufficient and not necessary
- Necessary and not sufficient
- Necessary and sufficient
- Neither necessary nor sufficient

## Explanation

If $n$ is divisible by $6$, then $n = 6k = 2 \cdot 3k = 3 \cdot 2k$ with $k$ natural, so $n$ is divisible by $2$ and by $3$. Therefore the condition is necessary.

If $n$ is divisible by $2$ and by $3$, then $n = 3m$ with $m$ natural. Since the product of two odd numbers is odd and $3m$ is even, the factor $m$ is even, that is, $m = 2k$ with $k$ natural. Therefore $n = 6k$ is divisible by $6$, and the condition is also sufficient.

Both implications hold, therefore the condition is **necessary and sufficient**.
