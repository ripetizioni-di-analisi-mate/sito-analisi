For a natural number $n$, divisibility by $2$ and by $3$ is a necessary and sufficient condition for divisibility by $6$. Which of the following analogous equivalences is false?

- $n$ is divisible by $2$ and by $5$ if and only if it is divisible by $10$
- $n$ is divisible by $3$ and by $5$ if and only if it is divisible by $15$
- $n$ is divisible by $3$ and by $4$ if and only if it is divisible by $12$
- $n$ is divisible by $2$ and by $4$ if and only if it is divisible by $8$

## Explanation

If $n$ is divisible by $8$, then $n = 8k = 2 \cdot 4k = 4 \cdot 2k$, so it is divisible by $2$ and by $4$. The converse implication is false, because $n = 4$ is divisible by $2$ and by $4$ and is not divisible by $8$. Therefore the equivalence with $2$, $4$ and $8$ is **false**.

The factor $2$ already appears in the factorization of $4$, so divisibility by $2$ and by $4$ is equivalent to divisibility by $4$ alone.

In the other three equivalences the two divisors have no common prime factors. Then a number divisible by both contains in its factorization the prime factors of both, and therefore it is divisible by their product.
