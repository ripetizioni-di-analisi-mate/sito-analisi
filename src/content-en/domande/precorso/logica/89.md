One proves by contradiction that $\log_2 3$ is irrational. Assuming $\log_2 3 = \dfrac{m}{n}$ with $m$ and $n$ positive integers, one obtains $2^m = 3^n$. Why is this equality a contradiction?

- $2^m$ is even and $3^n$ is odd
- $2^m$ and $3^n$ are both even
- $m$ and $n$ would have to be equal
- $\log_2 3$ is less than $1$

## Explanation

From $\log_2 3 = \dfrac{m}{n}$ it follows that $2^{\frac{m}{n}} = 3$, and raising to the power $n$ gives $2^m = 3^n$. Since $\log_2 3 > 0$, the numbers $m$ and $n$ can be chosen positive.

With $m \geq 1$ the number $2^m$ is even. The number $3^n$ is a product of odd factors, so it is odd. An even number cannot be equal to an odd number, therefore the equality is a **contradiction** and $\log_2 3$ is irrational.

The number $3^n$ is odd, so it is not true that the two numbers are both even. No step requires $m = n$, and $\log_2 3 > 1$, because $3 > 2$.
