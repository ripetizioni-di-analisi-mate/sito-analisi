Let $n$ be a natural number. What is the negation of the proposition "$n$ is even and $n > 10$"?

- $n$ is odd and $n \leq 10$
- $n$ is odd or $n \leq 10$
- $n$ is odd or $n < 10$
- $n$ is even or $n \leq 10$

## Explanation

The proposition is the conjunction of "$n$ is even" and "$n > 10$", so by **De Morgan's laws** its negation is the disjunction of the negations of the two parts.

A natural number that is not even is odd, and the negation of $n > 10$ is $n \leq 10$. Therefore the negation is "$n$ is odd or $n \leq 10$".

For $n = 4$ the original proposition is false, because $4 \leq 10$. The proposition "$n$ is odd and $n \leq 10$" is also false for $n = 4$, because $4$ is even, so it is not the required negation.
