For which real values of $x$ does the predicate $p(x)$ given by $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ become a true proposition?

- $x = 2$ and $x = 3$
- $x = -2$ and $x = -3$
- $x = 1$ and $x = 6$
- Only $x = 2$

## Explanation

The predicate becomes a proposition when a value is assigned to $x$, and the resulting proposition is true exactly for the values that satisfy the equation.

The trinomial factors as $x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)$, because $2 + 3 = 5$ and $2 \cdot 3 = 6$. A product is zero if and only if at least one of the factors is zero, so $p(x)$ is **true** for $x = 2$ and for $x = 3$.

For every other real value of $x$ the proposition $p(x)$ is false. For example, for $x = 1$ we have $1 - 5 + 6 = 2 \neq 0$.
