We look for a compound proposition that is true when exactly one of $p$ and $q$ is true, and false in the other cases. Which proposition has this property?

- $p \lor q$
- $\lnot (p \land q)$
- $(p \land \lnot q) \lor (\lnot p \land q)$
- $(p \lor \lnot q) \land (\lnot p \lor q)$

## Explanation

Exactly one of $p$ and $q$ is true in two cases, namely $p$ true and $q$ false, or $p$ false and $q$ true. The first case is described by $p \land \lnot q$ and the second by $\lnot p \land q$.

It is enough that one of the two cases occurs, so the required proposition is the disjunction $(p \land \lnot q) \lor (\lnot p \land q)$.

The disjunction $p \lor q$ is true also when $p$ and $q$ are both true, and $\lnot (p \land q)$ is true also when they are both false. The proposition $(p \lor \lnot q) \land (\lnot p \lor q)$ is true precisely when $p$ and $q$ have the same truth value.
