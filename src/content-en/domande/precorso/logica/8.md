What is the contrapositive of the implication "if $n$ is a multiple of $8$, then $n$ is a multiple of $4$", with $n$ natural?

- If $n$ is a multiple of $4$, then $n$ is a multiple of $8$
- If $n$ is not a multiple of $8$, then $n$ is not a multiple of $4$
- $n$ is a multiple of $8$ and $n$ is not a multiple of $4$
- If $n$ is not a multiple of $4$, then $n$ is not a multiple of $8$

## Explanation

Let $p$ be the proposition "$n$ is a multiple of $8$" and $q$ the proposition "$n$ is a multiple of $4$". The contrapositive of $p \Rightarrow q$ is obtained by swapping hypothesis and conclusion and negating both, so it is $\lnot q \Rightarrow \lnot p$.

In the given case we obtain "if $n$ is not a multiple of $4$, then $n$ is not a multiple of $8$". The original implication is true, because $8k = 4 \cdot 2k$, therefore the contrapositive, which is equivalent to it, is also **true**.

The converse "if $n$ is a multiple of $4$, then $n$ is a multiple of $8$" only swaps hypothesis and conclusion and is false, because $12$ is a multiple of $4$ and $12 = 8 \cdot 1 + 4$ leaves remainder $4$ when divided by $8$.
