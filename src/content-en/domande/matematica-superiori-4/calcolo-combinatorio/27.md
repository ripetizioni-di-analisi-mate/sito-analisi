How many $3$-letter strings, whether or not they form words, can be made from the $21$ letters of the Italian alphabet if the letters must all be different?

- $9261$
- $1330$
- $63$
- $7980$

## Explanation

Each string is an ordered sequence of $3$ distinct letters, so it is an ordered selection without repetition from $21$ objects taken $3$ at a time. By the formula for such selections, the number of strings is $D_{21,3} = 21 \cdot 20 \cdot 19 = 7980$. The number $21^3 = 9261$ counts strings in which letters may repeat.
