An association with $15$ members elects a president, a secretary, and a treasurer, all different people. In how many ways can the three offices be assigned?

- $455$
- $3375$
- $45$
- $2730$

## Explanation

The offices are distinct, so it matters which member holds each office. Each choice is an ordered selection without repetition from $15$ objects taken $3$ at a time. The president can be chosen in $15$ ways, the secretary from the $14$ remaining members, and the treasurer from the $13$ still remaining. There are therefore $D_{15,3} = 15 \cdot 14 \cdot 13 = 2730$ choices. The number $\dbinom{15}{3} = 455$ counts groups of three members without assigning offices.
