For which natural number $n \geq 3$ does $D_{n,3} = 6 \, D_{n,2}$ hold?

- $n = 6$
- $n = 8$
- $n = 7$
- $n = 9$

## Explanation

By the counting formula for ordered selections without repetition, $D_{n,3} = n \, (n - 1)(n - 2)$ and $D_{n,2} = n \, (n - 1)$. The equation therefore becomes $n \, (n - 1)(n - 2) = 6 \, n \, (n - 1)$. Since $n \geq 3$, the product $n \, (n - 1)$ differs from $0$, so dividing both sides by it gives $n - 2 = 6$. Therefore $n = 8$.
