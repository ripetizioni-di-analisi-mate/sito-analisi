In how many ways can $6$ identical pieces of candy be distributed among $3$ children if a child may receive none?

- $28$
- $729$
- $216$
- $20$

## Explanation

A distribution is determined by how many pieces each child receives, so it amounts to choosing $6$ times from $3$ children, allowing repetition and disregarding order. This is a combination with repetition from $3$ objects taken $6$ at a time, and the number of combinations is
$$
C'_{3,6} = \binom{3 + 6 - 1}{6} = \binom{8}{6} = \binom{8}{2} = 28 .
$$
The number $3^6 = 729$ counts distributions of $6$ distinct pieces of candy.
