From city $A$ to city $B$ there are $3$ roads. From $B$ to city $C$ there are $4$ roads, and from $A$ to $C$ there are $2$ direct roads that do not pass through $B$. How many routes lead from $A$ to $C$?

- $9$
- $14$
- $24$
- $12$

## Explanation

Routes passing through $B$ have two stages, with $3$ choices for the stretch from $A$ to $B$ and $4$ choices for the stretch from $B$ to $C$. By the fundamental counting principle there are $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ such routes. There are $2$ direct routes, all different from the preceding ones. By the addition principle the number of routes is therefore $12 + 2 = 14$.
