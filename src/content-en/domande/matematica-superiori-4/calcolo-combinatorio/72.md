For which natural number $n \geq 2$ does $\dbinom{n}{1} + \dbinom{n}{2} = 21$ hold?

- $n = 6$
- $n = 7$
- $n = 5$
- $n = 20$

## Explanation

By Pascal’s identity, $\dbinom{n}{1} + \dbinom{n}{2} = \dbinom{n + 1}{2}$, so the equation becomes $\dfrac{(n + 1) \, n}{2} = 21$. This gives $n^2 + n - 42 = 0$, whose solutions are $n = 6$ and $n = -7$. Since $n \geq 2$, we have $n = 6$, and indeed $6 + 15 = 21$.
