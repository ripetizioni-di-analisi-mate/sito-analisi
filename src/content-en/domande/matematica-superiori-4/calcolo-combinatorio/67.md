From $9$ people, a president and $2$ council members are chosen, with the two council members having the same role. In how many ways can the choice be made?

- $504$
- $84$
- $324$
- $252$

## Explanation

The president can be chosen in $9$ ways. The two council members form an unordered group drawn from the $8$ remaining people, so they can be chosen in $\dbinom{8}{2} = 28$ ways. By the fundamental counting principle there are $9 \cdot 28 = 252$ choices. The number $D_{9,3} = 504$ counts every choice twice, since it distinguishes the two council members.
