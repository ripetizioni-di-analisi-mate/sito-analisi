What are the solutions of the inequality $\cos x > -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$?

- $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} < x < \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$
- $0 \leq x < \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ or $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} < x < 2\pi$
- $0 \leq x < \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ or $\dfrac{7\pi}{6} < x < 2\pi$
- $-\dfrac{5\pi}{6} < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$

## Explanation

In $[0, 2\pi)$, the solutions of the equation $\cos x = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ are $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, in the second and third quadrants. The cosine is less than or equal to $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ only for $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} \leq x \leq \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, where the points on the circle are close to $(-1, 0)$. Therefore, in the specified interval the solutions are $0 \leq x < \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ or $\dfrac{7\pi}{6} < x < 2\pi$. The expression $-\dfrac{5\pi}{6} < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ describes the solutions in $[-\pi, \pi)$, and its negative values do not belong to $[0, 2\pi)$.
