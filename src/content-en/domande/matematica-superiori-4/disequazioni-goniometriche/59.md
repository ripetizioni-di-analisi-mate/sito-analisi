With $X = \cos x$ and $Y = \sin x$, the inequality $\sin x - \cos x > 1$ becomes $Y - X > 1$. At which points does the line $Y - X = 1$ intersect the circle $X^2 + Y^2 = 1$?

- $(0, -1)$ and $(1, 0)$
- $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 0)$
- $(0, 1)$ and $(-1, 0)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$ and $\left( \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$

## Explanation

The equation of the line gives $Y = 1 + X$, and substitution into the equation of the circle gives $X^2 + (1 + X)^2 = 1$. Expanding gives $2X^2 + 2X = 0$, that is, $2X\,(X + 1) = 0$, with solutions $X = 0$ and $X = -1$. For $X = 0$ we have $Y = 1$, and for $X = -1$ we have $Y = 0$. The points are therefore $(0, 1)$ and $(-1, 0)$, corresponding to the angles $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\pi$. The origin does not satisfy $Y - X > 1$, so the solutions of the inequality are the angles on the arc on the opposite side of the line from the origin, that is, $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi < x < \pi + 2k\pi$.
