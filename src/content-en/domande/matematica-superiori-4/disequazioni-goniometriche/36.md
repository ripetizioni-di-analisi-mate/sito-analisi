What are the solutions of the inequality $\cos\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) > 0$?

- $-\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$
- $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{7\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

Set $t = x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The solutions of the inequality $\cos t > 0$ are $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi < t < \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, since cosine is positive in the first and fourth quadrants. Adding $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ to all three parts gives $-\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$. For $x = 0$ we have $\cos\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} > 0$, and indeed $0$ lies in the interval for $k = 0$.
