What are the solutions of the inequality $(\sin x - 1)(2\cos x - 1) \geq 0$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \dfrac{5\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

The factor $\sin x - 1$ is negative for $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$ and zero for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. Where this factor is negative, the product is greater than or equal to $0$ when $2\cos x - 1 \leq 0$, that is, for $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \dfrac{5\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. The points $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, where the product equals $0$, are solutions and already belong to this interval. The solutions are therefore $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \dfrac{5\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$.
