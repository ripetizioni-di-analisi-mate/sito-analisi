A switch starts off and follows the signal $R(x)=\dfrac{\sin x}{2+\cos x}$ as $x$ increases from $0$ to $2\pi$. It switches on when $R(x)>\dfrac13$, switches off when $R(x)<-\dfrac13$, and otherwise keeps its current state. What are its states at $x=\pi$ and $x=2\pi$, in that order?

- On, then off.
- Off, then on.
- On at both times.
- Off at both times.

## Explanation

The denominator is always positive. On the first half-turn the signal is nonnegative and reaches $\dfrac12$ at $\pi/2$, so the switch is on at $\pi$. On the second half-turn the signal is nonpositive and reaches $-\dfrac12$ at $3\pi/2$. The switch stays off through $2\pi$, even though the signal returns to zero.
