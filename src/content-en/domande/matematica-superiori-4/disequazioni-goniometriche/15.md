Which of the following numbers is a solution of the inequality $\cos x < -\dfrac{1}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

We have $\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and since $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} > \dfrac{1}{2}$ it follows that $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} < -\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ is a solution. In contrast, $\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, which is not less than $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. The values $\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\cos\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{6} \right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ are positive. The solutions of the inequality are $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{4\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$, and $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ belongs to the interval for $k = 0$.
