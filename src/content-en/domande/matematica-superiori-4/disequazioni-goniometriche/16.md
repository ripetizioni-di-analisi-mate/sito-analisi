In $[0, 2\pi)$, the inequality $\sin x > \dfrac{1}{2}$ has solutions $\dfrac{\pi}{6} < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. Which of the following intervals lies within the solution set on $\mathbb{R}$?

- $\left( \dfrac{7\pi}{6}, \dfrac{11\pi}{6} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{13\pi}{6}, \dfrac{17\pi}{6} \right)$
- $\left( -\dfrac{5\pi}{6}, -\dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$
- $\left( \dfrac{19\pi}{6}, \dfrac{23\pi}{6} \right)$

## Explanation

Sine has period $2\pi$, so by periodicity of the solution set, the solutions on $\mathbb{R}$ are $\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$. Setting $k = 1$ gives the interval $\left( \dfrac{13\pi}{6}, \dfrac{17\pi}{6} \right)$. The intervals $\left( \dfrac{7\pi}{6}, \dfrac{11\pi}{6} \right)$ and $\left( \dfrac{19\pi}{6}, \dfrac{23\pi}{6} \right)$ are obtained by shifting the endpoints by $\pi$ and $3\pi$, respectively, and sine is negative on them. Sine is also negative on $\left( -\dfrac{5\pi}{6}, -\dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$ because these angles lie in the third and fourth quadrants.
