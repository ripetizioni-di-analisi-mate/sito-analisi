None of the angles $x=\dfrac{k\pi}{3}$, with $k=0,1,\ldots,5$, satisfies the inequality $\sin x>0.9$. Can one conclude that the inequality has no solutions in $[0,2\pi)$?

- Yes, because at no multiple of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ does sine exceed $0.9$.
- Yes, because sine has period $2\pi$.
- No, because $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ satisfy it.
- No, because $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2}=1>0.9$.

## Explanation

At the angles $\dfrac{k\pi}{3}$ sine equals $0$ or $\pm\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, and $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\approx0.866$ is less than $0.9$; so none of these angles satisfies the inequality. The angle $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is not among those considered and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2}=1$, so $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is a solution. On the unit circle the points with ordinate greater than $0.9$ form an arc around $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the solutions in $[0,2\pi)$ are the $x$ with $\arcsin 0.9<x<\pi-\arcsin 0.9$. The inequality therefore **has solutions**.
