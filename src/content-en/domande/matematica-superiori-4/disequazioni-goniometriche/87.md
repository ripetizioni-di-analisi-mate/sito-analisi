Why is $\sqrt{\sin^2 x}$ defined for every real input, whereas $\sqrt{\sin x}$ may fail to be defined?

- Sine is always positive.
- Square roots allow negative radicands in the reals.
- Squaring removes all zeros.
- The first radicand is always nonnegative.

## Explanation

The square of any real number lies in $[0,+\infty)$. Sine on its own also takes negative values.
