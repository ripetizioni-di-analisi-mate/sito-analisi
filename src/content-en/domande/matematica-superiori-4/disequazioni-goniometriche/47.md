The polynomial $P(t)$ is positive only for $t\in(-2,-1)\cup(1,3)$. Does the inequality $P(\sin x)>0$ have real solutions?

- No, because neither interval has points in common with $[-1,1]$.
- Yes, all angles with sine $1$.
- Yes, all angles with sine $-1$.
- Yes, because the intervals contain infinitely many real numbers.

## Explanation

With the substitution $t=\sin x$ the inequality becomes $P(t)>0$, with the condition $-1\leq t\leq 1$, because sine takes only values in $[-1,1]$. The intervals $(-2,-1)$ and $(1,3)$ are open, so they do not contain the endpoints $-1$ and $1$ and have no points in common with $[-1,1]$. Hence no value of $\sin x$ makes the polynomial positive, and the inequality $P(\sin x)>0$ **has no solutions**.
