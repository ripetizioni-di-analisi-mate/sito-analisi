Which of the following numbers is a solution of the inequality $\cos^2 x > \cos x$?

- $0$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

For $x = \pi$, we have $\cos^2 x = 1$ and $\cos x = -1$, and $1 > -1$, so $\pi$ is a solution. For $x = 0$ the inequality becomes $1 > 1$, for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ it becomes $\dfrac{1}{4} > \dfrac{1}{2}$, and for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ it becomes $0 > 0$. None of the three inequalities is true. In general, the inequality can be written as $\cos x\,(\cos x - 1) > 0$. Since $\cos x - 1 \leq 0$, the product is positive only for $\cos x < 0$, that is, for $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$.
