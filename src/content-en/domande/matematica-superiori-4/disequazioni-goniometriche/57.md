By the auxiliary-angle identity, which expression equals $\sin x - \cos x$ for every real $x$?

- $\sqrt{2}\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$
- $2\sin\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$
- $\sqrt{2}\sin\left( x - \dfrac{3\pi}{4} \right)$
- $\sqrt{2}\sin\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$

## Explanation

We have $a = 1$ and $b = -1$, so $r = \sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$. The angle $\varphi$ satisfies $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $\sin\varphi = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, so it lies in the fourth quadrant and equals $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Hence $\sin x - \cos x = \sqrt{2}\sin\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$. For $x = 0$, the left-hand side equals $-1$ and the right-hand side equals $\sqrt{2}\sin\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = -1$, as expected.
