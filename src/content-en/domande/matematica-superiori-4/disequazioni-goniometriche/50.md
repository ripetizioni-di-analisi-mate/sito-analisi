On $0<x<\pi$ we want to divide $\sin^2x\le \sin x$ by $\sin x$. Why does the division preserve equivalence on this interval?

- The divisor is always one.
- The divisor is strictly positive throughout.
- A squared term permits any division.
- Sine changes sign once in the interval.

## Explanation

Sine is positive on the open upper semicircle. Division keeps the same direction and loses no zeros of the divisor.
