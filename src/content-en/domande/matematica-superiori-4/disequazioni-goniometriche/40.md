How many disjoint intervals form the set of solutions of the inequality $\sin 2x > 0$ that lie in $[0, 2\pi]$?

- $1$
- $2$
- $4$
- $3$

## Explanation

With $t = 2x$, the inequality $\sin t > 0$ gives $2k\pi < 2x < \pi + 2k\pi$, that is, $k\pi < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. In $[0, 2\pi]$, we obtain the interval $\left( 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ for $k = 0$ and the interval $\left( \pi, \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right)$ for $k = 1$. With $k = 2$, the interval $\left( 2\pi, \dfrac{5\pi}{2} \right)$ has no points in common with $[0, 2\pi]$, so the number of intervals is $2$.
