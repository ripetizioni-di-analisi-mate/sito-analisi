What are the solutions of the inequality $\sin x < \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{7\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

Since $\arcsin\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, the line $y = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ intersects the unit circle at the points corresponding to $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. The points below the line form the arc starting at $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, passing through $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, and reaching the point corresponding to $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ after a full turn. In the expression with $k$, the left endpoint must be less than the right endpoint, so the endpoint $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is increased by $2\pi$ to obtain $\dfrac{7\pi}{3}$. The expression $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$, on the other hand, describes the empty set.
