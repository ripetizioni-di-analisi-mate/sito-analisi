What is the solution of the equation $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{x + 1} = 4^{x}$?

- $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$
- $x = -1$
- $x = 1$

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{1}{2} = 2^{-1}$ and $4 = 2^2$, the equation can be written $2^{-(x + 1)} = 2^{2x}$. Equating the exponents gives $-x - 1 = 2x$, so $-3x = 1$. Therefore the solution is $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.
