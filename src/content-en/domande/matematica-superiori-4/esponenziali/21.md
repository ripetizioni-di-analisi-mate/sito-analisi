Why is the expression $(-8)^{\frac{1}{3}}$ undefined as a power with a rational exponent?

- The number $\sqrt[3]{-8}$ does not exist in the real numbers
- The exponent $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is less than $1$
- A power with a negative base is always negative
- The equivalent fraction $\dfrac{2}{6}$ would give $\sqrt[6]{64} = 2$ instead of $-2$

## Explanation

The number $\sqrt[3]{-8}$ exists and equals $-2$ because $(-2)^3 = -8$. The value of a power with a rational exponent, however, must depend only on the rational number, regardless of the fraction used to write it. The expression $\dfrac{2}{6}$, equal to $\dfrac{1}{3}$, would give $\sqrt[6]{(-8)^2} = \sqrt[6]{64} = 2$. The two results differ, so the definition requires a positive base.
