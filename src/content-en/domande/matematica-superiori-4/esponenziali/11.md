At what point does the graph of the function $f(x) = 3^{x + 1} - 1$ intersect the $x$-axis?

- $(1, 0)$
- $(-1, 0)$
- $(0, 2)$
- $(0, 0)$

## Explanation

Points on the $x$-axis have vertical coordinate $0$, so solve the equation $3^{x + 1} - 1 = 0$. It is equivalent to $3^{x + 1} = 1 = 3^0$, so $x + 1 = 0$, that is, $x = -1$. Hence the graph intersects the $x$-axis at $(-1, 0)$. The point $(0, 2)$ is the intersection with the $y$-axis because $f(0) = 3 - 1 = 2$.
