How many real solutions does the equation $4^x + 3 \cdot 2^x + 2 = 0$ have?

- $0$
- $1$
- $2$
- $4$

## Explanation

With $t = 2^x$, $t > 0$, the equation becomes $t^2 + 3t + 2 = 0$, that is, $(t + 1)(t + 2) = 0$. The solutions $t = -1$ and $t = -2$ are both negative and therefore inadmissible. Hence the equation **has no solutions** in the real numbers. The same result follows because the left-hand side is the sum of three positive terms.
