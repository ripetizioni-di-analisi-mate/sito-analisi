How many real solutions does the equation $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^x = x$ have?

- $1$
- $0$
- $2$
- $3$

## Explanation

The left-hand side is a decreasing function and the right-hand side is increasing, so their graphs intersect at most once. For $x = 0$ we have $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^0 = 1 > 0$, whereas for $x = 1$ we have $\dfrac{1}{2} < 1$. The difference between the two sides changes sign between $0$ and $1$, so the graphs intersect at a point with horizontal coordinate between $0$ and $1$. Therefore the equation has **exactly one solution**.
