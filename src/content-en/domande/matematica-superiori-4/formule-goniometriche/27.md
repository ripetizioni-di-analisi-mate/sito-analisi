What is $\cos 70^\circ\cos 50^\circ - \sin 70^\circ\sin 50^\circ$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

## Explanation

The expression has the form $\cos\alpha\cos\beta - \sin\alpha\sin\beta$, so by the **cosine sum** formula it equals $\cos(70^\circ + 50^\circ) = \cos 120^\circ$. The angle $120^\circ$ lies in the second quadrant and is supplementary to $60^\circ$, so
$$
\cos 120^\circ = -\cos 60^\circ = -\frac{1}{2} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $\cos 60^\circ$ and results from overlooking the sign of cosine in the second quadrant.
