Given $\tan\alpha = 2$, what is $\cos 2\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-3$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$

## Explanation

Write $\cos 2\alpha = \cos^2\alpha - \sin^2\alpha$ and divide by $\cos^2\alpha + \sin^2\alpha = 1$. Dividing the numerator and denominator by $\cos^2\alpha$, which differs from $0$ because the tangent is defined, gives
$$
\cos 2\alpha = \frac{1 - \tan^2\alpha}{1 + \tan^2\alpha} = \frac{1 - 4}{1 + 4} = -\frac{3}{5} .
$$
The value $-3$ results from using only the numerator $1 - \tan^2\alpha$, and $\dfrac{4}{5}$ is the value of $\sin 2\alpha = \dfrac{2\tan\alpha}{1 + \tan^2\alpha}$.
