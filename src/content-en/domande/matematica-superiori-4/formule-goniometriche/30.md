Given that $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \alpha < \pi$, what is $\cos 2\alpha$?

- $-\dfrac{7}{9}$
- $\dfrac{7}{9}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{8}{9}$

## Explanation

By the **double-angle formula** for cosine, $\cos 2\alpha = 1 - 2\sin^2\alpha$, which involves only the sine of $\alpha$. Substituting $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$ gives $\cos 2\alpha = 1 - 2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{9} = \dfrac{7}{9}$. Since $\alpha$ lies in the second quadrant, $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$, and its square $\cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{8}{9}$ is positive. Therefore the formula $\cos 2\alpha = \cos^2\alpha - \sin^2\alpha$ also gives $\dfrac{8}{9} - \dfrac{1}{9} = \dfrac{7}{9}$. The value $-\dfrac{7}{9}$ comes from giving $\cos 2\alpha$ the sign of $\cos\alpha$, and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ comes from writing $1 - 2\sin\alpha$ instead of $1 - 2\sin^2\alpha$.
