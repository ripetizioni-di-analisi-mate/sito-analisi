For which values of $x$ does the identity $\dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{\sin 2x} = \tan x$ hold?

- $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$
- $x \neq k\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $x \neq k\pi$
- $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

By the **double-angle formulas**, the left-hand side is $\dfrac{2\sin^2 x}{2\sin x\cos x}$, which simplifies to $\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \tan x$ wherever it is defined. The left-hand side requires $\sin 2x \neq 0$, that is, $2x \neq k\pi$, so $x \neq k\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. This condition also excludes the values $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, where tangent is undefined. Therefore the identity holds for $x \neq k\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. For $x = k\pi$ the right-hand side equals $0$ but the left-hand side is undefined, so these values must be excluded.
