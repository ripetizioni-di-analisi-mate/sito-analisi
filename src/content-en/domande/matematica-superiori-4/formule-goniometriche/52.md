Given $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, what is $2\sin x + \cos x$?

- $-1$
- $1$
- $-\dfrac{11}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{7}{5}$

## Explanation

The **tangent half-angle substitution** with $t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ gives $1 + t^2 = \dfrac{5}{4}$, so
$$
\sin x = \frac{-1}{\dfrac{5}{4}} = -\frac{4}{5} , \qquad \cos x = \frac{\dfrac{3}{4}}{\dfrac{5}{4}} = \frac{3}{5} .
$$
Therefore $2\sin x + \cos x = -\dfrac{8}{5} + \dfrac{3}{5} = -1$. The value $-\dfrac{11}{5}$ results from taking $\cos x$ to be negative, and $1$ from taking $\sin x$ to be positive.
