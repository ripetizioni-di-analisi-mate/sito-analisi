Which of the following equalities holds for every real $\alpha$?

- $\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} + \alpha \right) = -\sin\alpha$
- $\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} + \alpha \right) = \sin\alpha$
- $\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} + \alpha \right) = -\cos\alpha$
- $\sin(\pi + \alpha) = \sin\alpha$

## Explanation

By the **cosine sum** formula,
$$
\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{2} + \alpha \right) = \cos\frac{\pi}{2}\cos\alpha - \sin\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha = 0 \cdot \cos\alpha - 1 \cdot \sin\alpha = -\sin\alpha .
$$
The sine sum formula gives $\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} + \alpha \right) = \cos\alpha$ and $\sin(\pi + \alpha) = -\sin\alpha$ instead. Therefore the other three equalities fail, for example at $\alpha = 0$ or $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
