In a triangle, two angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are acute, with $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\sin\beta = \dfrac{5}{13}$. What is the cosine of the third angle $\gamma$?

- $\dfrac{33}{65}$
- $-\dfrac{63}{65}$
- $-\dfrac{33}{65}$
- $\dfrac{63}{65}$

## Explanation

The angles are acute, so $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$ and $\cos\beta = \dfrac{12}{13}$. Since $\gamma = \pi - (\alpha + \beta)$, we have $\cos\gamma = -\cos(\alpha + \beta)$, and by the **cosine sum** formula,
$$
\cos\gamma = -\left( \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{12}{13} - \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{13} \right) = -\frac{48 - 15}{65} = -\frac{33}{65} .
$$
The cosine is negative, so $\gamma$ is obtuse. The value $\dfrac{33}{65}$ is $\cos(\alpha + \beta)$ and comes from omitting the step that takes the supplementary angle.
