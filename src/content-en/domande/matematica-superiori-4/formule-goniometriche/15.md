What is the value of $\sin 75^\circ$ when calculated as $\sin(45^\circ + 30^\circ)$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2} + 1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{2}$

## Explanation

By the **sine sum** formula,
$$
\sin 75^\circ = \sin 45^\circ\cos 30^\circ + \cos 45^\circ\sin 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{2} + 1}{2}$ is the sum $\sin 45^\circ + \sin 30^\circ$, which differs from the sine of the sum. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{2}$ exceeds $1$, so it cannot be the sine of an angle.
