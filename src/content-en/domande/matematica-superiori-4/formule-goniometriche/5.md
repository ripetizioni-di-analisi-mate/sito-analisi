Given $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ with $\pi < \alpha < \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, what is $\tan 2\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{7}$
- $-\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{7}$
- $4\sqrt{2}$
- $-\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{9}$

## Explanation

The angle $\alpha$ lies in the third quadrant, where sine is negative, so $\sin\alpha = -\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{9}} = -\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$. The **double-angle formulas** give $\sin 2\alpha = 2 \cdot \left( -\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} \right)\left( -\dfrac{1}{3} \right) = \dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{9}$ and $\cos 2\alpha = 2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{9} - 1 = -\dfrac{7}{9}$. Therefore
$$
\tan 2\alpha = \frac{\sin 2\alpha}{\cos 2\alpha} = \frac{\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{9}}{-\dfrac{7}{9}} = -\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{7} .
$$
A positive value of $\sin\alpha$ would give the opposite result, $\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{7}$.
