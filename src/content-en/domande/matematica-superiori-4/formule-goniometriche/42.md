Given $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{7}{25}$ with $\pi < \alpha < \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, what is $\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$?

- $-\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$

## Explanation

Dividing by $2$ throughout the condition $\pi < \alpha < \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ gives $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \dfrac{\alpha}{2} < \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, so the half-angle lies in the second quadrant, where sine is positive. By the **half-angle formula**,
$$
\sin\frac{\alpha}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{1 + \dfrac{7}{25}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{16}{25}} = \frac{4}{5} .
$$
The value $-\dfrac{4}{5}$ results from choosing the sign based on the quadrant of $\alpha$, where sine is negative. The value $\dfrac{3}{5}$ results from taking $1 - \cos\alpha$ to be $1 - \dfrac{7}{25}$, thus overlooking the sign of $\cos\alpha$.
