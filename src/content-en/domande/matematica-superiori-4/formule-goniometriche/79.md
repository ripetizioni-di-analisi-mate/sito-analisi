For all real $\alpha$ and $\beta$, which expression equals $\sin(\alpha + \beta)\sin(\alpha - \beta)$?

- $\sin^2\alpha - \sin^2\beta$
- $\sin^2\alpha + \sin^2\beta$
- $\sin^2\alpha - \cos^2\beta$
- $\cos^2\alpha - \sin^2\beta$

## Explanation

By the **sine sum and difference** formulas, the product is
$$
(\sin\alpha\cos\beta + \cos\alpha\sin\beta)(\sin\alpha\cos\beta - \cos\alpha\sin\beta) = \sin^2\alpha\cos^2\beta - \cos^2\alpha\sin^2\beta .
$$
Substituting $\cos^2\beta = 1 - \sin^2\beta$ and $\cos^2\alpha = 1 - \sin^2\alpha$ gives $\sin^2\alpha - \sin^2\alpha\sin^2\beta - \sin^2\beta + \sin^2\alpha\sin^2\beta$. The terms containing $\sin^2\alpha\sin^2\beta$ cancel, so the product equals $\sin^2\alpha - \sin^2\beta$.
