Which of the following equalities is an equation but not an identity?

- $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$
- $\sin^2 x = \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$
- $\cos^4 x - \sin^4 x = \cos 2x$
- $\cos 2x = 2\cos x - 1$

## Explanation

The equalities $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$ and $\sin^2 x = \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$ are the double-angle and power-reduction formulas, so they hold for every $x$. The equality $\cos^4 x - \sin^4 x = \cos 2x$ also holds for every $x$ because the left-hand side is $\left( \cos^2 x - \sin^2 x \right)\left( \cos^2 x + \sin^2 x \right)$. However, the equality $\cos 2x = 2\cos x - 1$ fails at $x = \pi$, where the left-hand side equals $1$ and the right-hand side equals $-3$. It is therefore an **equation**. The correct formula contains $2\cos^2 x$.
