The position of a point as a function of time is $s(t) = 2\sin(\omega t) - 2\cos(\omega t)$. What are the amplitude and initial phase of the simple harmonic motion when written in the form $A\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$?

- Amplitude $4$, phase $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- Amplitude $2\sqrt{2}$, phase $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- Amplitude $2$, phase $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- Amplitude $2\sqrt{2}$, phase $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

With $a = 2$ and $b = -2$ the amplitude is $r = \sqrt{4 + 4} = 2\sqrt{2}$. The **auxiliary angle** satisfies $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{2}{2\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\sin\varphi = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so it lies in the fourth quadrant and $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Therefore
$$
s(t) = 2\sqrt{2}\sin\left( \omega t - \frac{\pi}{4} \right) .
$$
The amplitude $4$ is the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients, which differs from $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.
