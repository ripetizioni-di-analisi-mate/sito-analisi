Given $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} = -2$, what is $\cos x$?

- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$

## Explanation

The **tangent half-angle substitution** with $t = \tan\dfrac{x}{2} = -2$ gives $t^2 = 4$, so
$$
\cos x = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2} = \frac{1 - 4}{1 + 4} = -\frac{3}{5} .
$$
The square of $t$ is independent of the sign of $t$, so the cosine also equals $-\dfrac{3}{5}$ for $t = 2$. The value $-\dfrac{4}{5}$ is $\sin x = \dfrac{2t}{1 + t^2}$ instead.
