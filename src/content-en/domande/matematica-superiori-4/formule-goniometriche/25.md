Given $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$ and $\cos\beta = \dfrac{5}{13}$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are acute angles, what is $\sin(\alpha + \beta)$?

- $\dfrac{16}{65}$
- $\dfrac{56}{65}$
- $-\dfrac{33}{65}$
- $\dfrac{63}{65}$

## Explanation

The angles are acute, so $\cos\alpha = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{16}{25}} = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\sin\beta = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{25}{169}} = \dfrac{12}{13}$. By the **sine sum** formula,
$$
\sin(\alpha + \beta) = \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{13} + \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{12}{13} = \frac{20}{65} + \frac{36}{65} = \frac{56}{65} .
$$
The value $-\dfrac{33}{65}$ is $\cos(\alpha + \beta)$, and $\dfrac{63}{65}$ is $\cos(\alpha - \beta)$.
