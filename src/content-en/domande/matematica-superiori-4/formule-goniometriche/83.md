Given $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$ with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, what is $\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{3} - \alpha \right)$?

- $\dfrac{4\sqrt{3} - 3}{10}$
- $\dfrac{4\sqrt{3} + 3}{10}$
- $\dfrac{3\sqrt{3} - 4}{10}$
- $\dfrac{4 - 3\sqrt{3}}{10}$

## Explanation

The angle $\alpha$ lies in the first quadrant, so $\cos\alpha = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{9}{25}} = \dfrac{4}{5}$. By the **sine difference** formula,
$$
\sin\left( \frac{\pi}{3} - \alpha \right) = \sin\frac{\pi}{3}\cos\alpha - \cos\frac{\pi}{3}\sin\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{5} - \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{5} = \frac{4\sqrt{3} - 3}{10} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{4\sqrt{3} + 3}{10}$ is $\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{3} + \alpha \right)$, and $\dfrac{3\sqrt{3} - 4}{10}$ results from interchanging $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$.
