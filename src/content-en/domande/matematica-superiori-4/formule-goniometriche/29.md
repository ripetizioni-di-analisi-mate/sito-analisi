Given $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$ with $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \alpha < \pi$, what is $\sin 2\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{9}$
- $-\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{9}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{9}$

## Explanation

The angle $\alpha$ lies in the second quadrant, where cosine is negative, so $\cos\alpha = -\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{9}} = -\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$. By the sine **double-angle formula**,
$$
\sin 2\alpha = 2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{3} \cdot \left( -\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} \right) = -\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{9} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is twice the sine, which differs from the sine of twice the angle, and $-\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{9}$ results from omitting the factor $2$.
