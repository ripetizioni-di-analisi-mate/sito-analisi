What is $2\cos^2\dfrac{5\pi}{12} - 1$?

- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

By the **double-angle formula** $\cos 2\alpha = 2\cos^2\alpha - 1$ with $\alpha = \dfrac{5\pi}{12}$, the expression equals $\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. The angle $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ is supplementary to $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, so
$$
\cos\frac{5\pi}{6} = -\cos\frac{\pi}{6} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ results from overlooking that $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ lies in the second quadrant.
