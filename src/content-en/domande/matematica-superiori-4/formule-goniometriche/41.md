Given $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{8}$ with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, what is $\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$?

- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{9}{16}$

## Explanation

The condition $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ gives $0 < \dfrac{\alpha}{2} < \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, so the half-angle lies in the first quadrant and its cosine is positive. By the **half-angle formula**,
$$
\cos\frac{\alpha}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{1 + \dfrac{1}{8}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{9}{16}} = \frac{3}{4} .
$$
The number $\dfrac{9}{16}$ is the square of the result, and $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{4}$ is the value of $\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.
