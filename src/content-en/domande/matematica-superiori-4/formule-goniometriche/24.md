Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be two acute angles with $\tan\alpha = 3$ and $\tan\beta = \dfrac{1}{3}$. Which statement is true?

- $\alpha + \beta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \dfrac{10}{3}$
- $\alpha + \beta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\tan(\alpha + \beta) = 0$

## Explanation

The product of the tangents is $3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = 1$, so the denominator $1 - \tan\alpha\tan\beta$ in the tangent sum formula is zero. The cosine sum formula gives $\cos(\alpha + \beta) = \cos\alpha\cos\beta\,(1 - \tan\alpha\tan\beta) = 0$. The angles are acute, so $0 < \alpha + \beta < \pi$, and the only angle in this interval with cosine zero is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Therefore $\alpha + \beta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the tangent of $\alpha + \beta$ is undefined. The value $\dfrac{10}{3}$ is the sum of the two tangents.
