How can $-\sqrt{3}\sin x - \cos x$ be written in the form $r\sin(x + \varphi)$?

- $2\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$
- $2\sin\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$
- $2\sin\left( x + \dfrac{7\pi}{6} \right)$
- $2\sin\left( x + \dfrac{5\pi}{6} \right)$

## Explanation

With $a = -\sqrt{3}$ and $b = -1$ we have $r = \sqrt{3 + 1} = 2$, and the **auxiliary angle** satisfies $\cos\varphi = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin\varphi = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The point $\left( -\sqrt{3}, -1 \right)$ lies in the third quadrant, so $\varphi = \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$. Therefore
$$
-\sqrt{3}\sin x - \cos x = 2\sin\left( x + \frac{7\pi}{6} \right) .
$$
The angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ is given by $\arctan\dfrac{b}{a}$ and must be corrected by adding $\pi$ since $a < 0$.
