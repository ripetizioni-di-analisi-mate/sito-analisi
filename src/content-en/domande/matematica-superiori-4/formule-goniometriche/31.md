Given $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$, what is $\tan 2\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

By the tangent **double-angle formula**,
$$
\tan 2\alpha = \frac{2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{9}} = \frac{\dfrac{2}{3}}{\dfrac{8}{9}} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{8} = \frac{3}{4} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is twice the tangent, and $\dfrac{3}{5}$ results from using $1 + \tan^2\alpha$ in the denominator.
