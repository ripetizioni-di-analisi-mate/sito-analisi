Given $\tan\alpha = 3$ and $\tan\beta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, what is $\tan(\alpha + \beta)$?

- $-7$
- $7$
- $1$
- $\dfrac{7}{5}$

## Explanation

By the **tangent sum** formula,
$$
\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \frac{3 + \dfrac{1}{2}}{1 - 3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}} = \frac{\dfrac{7}{2}}{-\dfrac{1}{2}} = -7 .
$$
The denominator is negative because the product of the tangents exceeds $1$. The value $\dfrac{7}{5}$ results from using $1 + \tan\alpha\tan\beta$ in the denominator, and $1$ is the tangent of the difference $\alpha - \beta$.
