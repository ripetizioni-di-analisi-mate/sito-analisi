What is $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{8}\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{8}$?

- $\dfrac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{2 - \sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

By the first of **Werner's formulas**, with $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{8}$ and $\beta = \dfrac{3\pi}{8}$ we have $\alpha + \beta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\alpha - \beta = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, so
$$
\sin\frac{\pi}{8}\cos\frac{3\pi}{8} = \frac{1}{2}\left( \sin\frac{\pi}{2} + \sin\left( -\frac{\pi}{4} \right) \right) = \frac{1}{2}\left( 1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) = \frac{2 - \sqrt{2}}{4} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{4}$ results from using $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ instead of $\sin\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$ and overlooking that sine is odd.
