Given $\cos 2\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$ with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, what is $\tan\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$

## Explanation

By the **power-reduction formulas**, $\sin^2\alpha = \dfrac{1 - \cos 2\alpha}{2} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{1 + \cos 2\alpha}{2} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. Therefore
$$
\tan^2\alpha = \frac{\sin^2\alpha}{\cos^2\alpha} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
The angle $\alpha$ is acute, so the tangent is positive and $\tan\alpha = \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is the square of the tangent, and $\sqrt{2}$ is the reciprocal of the result.
