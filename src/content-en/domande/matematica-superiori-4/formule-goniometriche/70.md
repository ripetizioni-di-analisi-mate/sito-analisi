At which point in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ does the function $f(x) = \cos x - \sqrt{3}\sin x$ attain its minimum?

- $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

With $a = -\sqrt{3}$ and $b = 1$ we have $r = 2$, $\cos\varphi = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the **auxiliary angle** is $\varphi = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $f(x) = 2\sin\left( x + \dfrac{5\pi}{6} \right)$. The minimum $-2$ occurs when $x + \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, that is, at $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. Indeed,
$$
f\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right) = -\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = -2 .
$$
At the point $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$ the function instead attains its maximum $2$.
