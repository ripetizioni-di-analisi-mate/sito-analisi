The difference $\cos a-\cos b$ must vanish when $a=b$. Which product form makes this immediate?

- $2\cos (\dfrac{a+b}{2})\cos (\dfrac{a-b}{2})$
- $2\sin (\dfrac{a+b}{2})\cos (\dfrac{a-b}{2})$
- $-2\cos (\dfrac{a+b}{2})\cos (\dfrac{a-b}{2})$
- $-2\sin (\dfrac{a+b}{2})\sin (\dfrac{a-b}{2})$

## Explanation

For equal angles the half-difference is zero, so its sine makes the product vanish. The negative sign comes from the cosine difference formula.
