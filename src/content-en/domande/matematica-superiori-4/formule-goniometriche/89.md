For every real $x$, which expression equals $\sin x + \sin\left( x + \dfrac{2\pi}{3} \right) + \sin\left( x + \dfrac{4\pi}{3} \right)$?

- $\sin x$
- $3\sin x$
- $-\sin x$
- $0$

## Explanation

By the **sum-to-product formula** for a sum of sines, with half-sum $x + \pi$ and half-difference $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$,
$$
\sin\left( x + \frac{2\pi}{3} \right) + \sin\left( x + \frac{4\pi}{3} \right) = 2\sin(x + \pi)\cos\frac{\pi}{3} = 2 \cdot (-\sin x) \cdot \frac{1}{2} = -\sin x .
$$
Adding the first term $\sin x$ gives $0$ for every $x$. The result $\sin x$ comes from using $\sin(x + \pi) = \sin x$ and overlooking the minus sign.
