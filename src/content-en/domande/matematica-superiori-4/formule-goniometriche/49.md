Let $t = \tan\dfrac{x}{2}$, with $x \neq \pi + 2k\pi$. How can $\sin x + \cos x$ be expressed in terms of $t$?

- $\dfrac{1 - 2t - t^2}{1 + t^2}$
- $\dfrac{1 + 2t + t^2}{1 + t^2}$
- $\dfrac{1 + 2t - t^2}{1 - t^2}$
- $\dfrac{1 + 2t - t^2}{1 + t^2}$

## Explanation

The **tangent half-angle substitution** gives $\sin x = \dfrac{2t}{1 + t^2}$ and $\cos x = \dfrac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}$. The two fractions have the same denominator, so add their numerators:
$$
\sin x + \cos x = \frac{2t + 1 - t^2}{1 + t^2} = \frac{1 + 2t - t^2}{1 + t^2} .
$$
The expression containing $1 + 2t + t^2$ in the numerator results from using the wrong sign before $t^2$ in the cosine formula. The expression containing $1 - t^2$ in the denominator uses the denominator of the formula for $\tan x$.
