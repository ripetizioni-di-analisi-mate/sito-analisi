Given $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, what is $\cos x$?

- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{5}{4}$

## Explanation

The **tangent half-angle substitution** with $t = \dfrac{1}{3}$ gives $t^2 = \dfrac{1}{9}$, so
$$
\cos x = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2} = \frac{\dfrac{8}{9}}{\dfrac{10}{9}} = \frac{4}{5} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{3}{5}$ is $\sin x = \dfrac{2t}{1 + t^2}$, and $\dfrac{5}{4}$ results from interchanging the numerator and denominator in the formula. A number greater than $1$ cannot be a cosine.
