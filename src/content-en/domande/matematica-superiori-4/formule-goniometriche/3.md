Given $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{12}{13}$ with $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \alpha < \pi$ and $\cos\beta = \dfrac{3}{5}$ with $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \beta < 2\pi$, what is $\sin(\alpha - \beta)$?

- $\dfrac{16}{65}$
- $\dfrac{56}{65}$
- $\dfrac{33}{65}$
- $-\dfrac{16}{65}$

## Explanation

The angle $\alpha$ lies in the second quadrant, so $\cos\alpha = -\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{144}{169}} = -\dfrac{5}{13}$. The angle $\beta$ lies in the fourth quadrant, so $\sin\beta = -\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{9}{25}} = -\dfrac{4}{5}$. By the **sine difference** formula,
$$
\sin(\alpha - \beta) = \frac{12}{13} \cdot \frac{3}{5} - \left( -\frac{5}{13} \right)\left( -\frac{4}{5} \right) = \frac{36}{65} - \frac{20}{65} = \frac{16}{65} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{56}{65}$ results from using only one of the two negative signs.
