- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$

## Explanation

The angle $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ lies in the second quadrant, so $\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. By the **cosine sum** formula,
$$
\cos\frac{13\pi}{12} = \cos\frac{3\pi}{4}\cos\frac{\pi}{3} - \sin\frac{3\pi}{4}\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = -\frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} .
$$
The result is negative, as expected since $\dfrac{13\pi}{12}$ lies in the third quadrant. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4}$ results from putting a $+$ sign before the product of the sines, as in the difference formula.
