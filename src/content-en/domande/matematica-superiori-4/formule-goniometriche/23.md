Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be two acute angles with $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\tan\beta = \dfrac{1}{3}$. What is $\alpha + \beta$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$

## Explanation

By the **tangent sum** formula,
$$
\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \frac{\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3}} = \frac{\dfrac{5}{6}}{\dfrac{5}{6}} = 1 .
$$
The angles are acute, so $0 < \alpha + \beta < \pi$. The only angle in this interval with tangent $1$ is $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, hence $\alpha + \beta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
