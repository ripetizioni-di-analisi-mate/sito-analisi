For $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, which expression equals $\dfrac{2\tan x}{1 + \tan^2 x}$?

- $\tan 2x$
- $\cos 2x$
- $2\sin x$
- $\sin 2x$

## Explanation

Since $1 + \tan^2 x = \dfrac{\cos^2 x + \sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$, we have
$$
\frac{2\tan x}{1 + \tan^2 x} = 2 \cdot \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} \cdot \cos^2 x = 2\sin x\cos x .
$$
By the sine **double-angle formula**, the expression therefore equals $\sin 2x$. The formula for $\tan 2x$ instead has $1 - \tan^2 x$ in the denominator.
