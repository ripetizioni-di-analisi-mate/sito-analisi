For values of $x$ with $\sin x \neq 0$ and $\cos x \neq 0$, which expression equals $\dfrac{\sin 2x}{\sin x} - \dfrac{\cos 2x}{\cos x}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{\sin x}$
- $\cos x$
- $\dfrac{1}{\cos x}$
- $2\cos x - 1$

## Explanation

By the sine **double-angle formula**, the first term is $\dfrac{2\sin x\cos x}{\sin x} = 2\cos x$. Bringing the terms to the common denominator $\cos x$ gives
$$
2\cos x - \frac{\cos 2x}{\cos x} = \frac{2\cos^2 x - \cos 2x}{\cos x} .
$$
Since $\cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x - 1$, the numerator equals $1$, so the expression equals $\dfrac{1}{\cos x}$. The result $2\cos x - 1$ comes from canceling $\cos 2x$ with $\cos x$ as though it were $2\cos x$.
