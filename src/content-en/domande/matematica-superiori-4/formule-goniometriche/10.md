What is $\cos 75^\circ + \cos 15^\circ$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{4}$
- $1$

## Explanation

In the **sum-to-product formula** for a sum of cosines, half the sum of the angles is $45^\circ$ and half their difference is $30^\circ$, so
$$
\cos 75^\circ + \cos 15^\circ = 2\cos 45^\circ\cos 30^\circ = 2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{4}$ results from omitting the factor $2$ in the formula. Adding the values from the table gives the same result, $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4} + \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$.
