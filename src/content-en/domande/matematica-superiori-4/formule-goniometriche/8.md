Given $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{7}{9}$ with $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$, what is $\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$?

- $-\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$

## Explanation

Dividing by $2$ throughout the condition $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$ gives $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} < \dfrac{\alpha}{2} < \pi$, so the half-angle lies in the second quadrant, where cosine is negative. By the **half-angle formula**,
$$
\cos\frac{\alpha}{2} = -\sqrt{\frac{1 + \dfrac{7}{9}}{2}} = -\sqrt{\frac{8}{9}} = -\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$ results from choosing the sign based on the quadrant of $\alpha$ instead of the quadrant of $\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$, and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is the value of $\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.
