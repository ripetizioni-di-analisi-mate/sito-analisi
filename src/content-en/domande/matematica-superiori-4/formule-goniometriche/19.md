Given $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$ with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\cos\beta = -\dfrac{4}{5}$ with $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \beta < \pi$, what is $\cos(\alpha - \beta)$?

- $-\dfrac{7}{25}$
- $-1$
- $\dfrac{7}{25}$
- $-\dfrac{24}{25}$

## Explanation

The angle $\alpha$ lies in the first quadrant, so $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$. The angle $\beta$ lies in the second quadrant, so $\sin\beta = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{16}{25}} = \dfrac{3}{5}$. By the **cosine difference** formula,
$$
\cos(\alpha - \beta) = \frac{4}{5} \cdot \left( -\frac{4}{5} \right) + \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5} = -\frac{16}{25} + \frac{9}{25} = -\frac{7}{25} .
$$
The value $-1$ results from taking $\sin\beta$ to be negative, and $-\dfrac{24}{25}$ is the value of $\sin(\alpha - \beta)$.
