Given $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{4}{5}$, what is $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $-3$
- $3$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

By the **tangent half-angle** formula, which applies because $\cos\alpha \neq -1$,
$$
\tan\frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{\sin\alpha}{1 + \cos\alpha} = \frac{\dfrac{3}{5}}{1 - \dfrac{4}{5}} = \frac{\dfrac{3}{5}}{\dfrac{1}{5}} = 3 .
$$
The formula has no square roots, so it gives the sign directly. The value $\dfrac{1}{3}$ results from using $1 - \cos\alpha$ in the denominator, which belongs to the other formula, $\dfrac{1 - \cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha}$.
