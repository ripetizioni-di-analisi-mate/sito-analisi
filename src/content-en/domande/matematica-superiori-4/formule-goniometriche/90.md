Given $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$ with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, what is $\sin 4\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{168}{625}$
- $\dfrac{336}{625}$
- $-\dfrac{336}{625}$
- $-\dfrac{527}{625}$

## Explanation

The angle $\alpha$ is acute, so $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$. The **double-angle formulas** give $\sin 2\alpha = 2 \cdot \dfrac{3}{5} \cdot \dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{24}{25}$ and $\cos 2\alpha = 1 - 2 \cdot \dfrac{9}{25} = \dfrac{7}{25}$. Applying the double-angle formula again to the angle $2\alpha$ gives
$$
\sin 4\alpha = 2\sin 2\alpha\cos 2\alpha = 2 \cdot \frac{24}{25} \cdot \frac{7}{25} = \frac{336}{625} .
$$
The value $-\dfrac{527}{625}$ is $\cos 4\alpha$, and $\dfrac{168}{625}$ results from omitting the factor $2$ in the last application of the double-angle formula.
