Solve the equation $\sin x - \cos x = 1$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ using the substitution $t = \tan\dfrac{x}{2}$ and checking the excluded value separately. What are the solutions?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\pi$
- Only $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- Only $\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

With the **tangent half-angle substitution**, the equation becomes $\dfrac{2t - 1 + t^2}{1 + t^2} = 1$, that is, $2t - 1 + t^2 = 1 + t^2$, giving $t = 1$. The value $t = \tan\dfrac{x}{2} = 1$ gives $\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, that is, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The substitution excludes $x = \pi$, which must be checked separately, and we have $\sin\pi - \cos\pi = 0 + 1 = 1$. Therefore the solutions are $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\pi$. At $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, by contrast, we have $\sin x - \cos x = -1$.
