Given $\sin\alpha + \cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, what is $\sin 2\alpha$?

- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{8}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

Squaring both sides gives $\sin^2\alpha + 2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{1}{4}$. By the Pythagorean identity and the sine **double-angle formula**, the left-hand side equals $1 + \sin 2\alpha$, so
$$
1 + \sin 2\alpha = \frac{1}{4} , \qquad \sin 2\alpha = -\frac{3}{4} .
$$
The value $-\dfrac{3}{8}$ is the product $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha$, that is, half of $\sin 2\alpha$.
