What is $\sin 50^\circ\cos 20^\circ - \cos 50^\circ\sin 20^\circ$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

## Explanation

The expression has the form $\sin\alpha\cos\beta - \cos\alpha\sin\beta$ with $\alpha = 50^\circ$ and $\beta = 20^\circ$. By the **sine difference** formula, it therefore equals
$$
\sin(50^\circ - 20^\circ) = \sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ results from reading the expression as a cosine, that is, $\cos 30^\circ$.
