Given $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$ with $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, what is $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$?

- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

The angle $\alpha$ lies in the first quadrant, so $\sin\alpha = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{9}{25}} = \dfrac{4}{5}$. By the **tangent half-angle** formula,
$$
\tan\frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha} = \frac{\dfrac{2}{5}}{\dfrac{4}{5}} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{1}{4}$ is the ratio $\dfrac{1 - \cos\alpha}{1 + \cos\alpha}$, the square of $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$, and $2$ is the reciprocal of the result.
