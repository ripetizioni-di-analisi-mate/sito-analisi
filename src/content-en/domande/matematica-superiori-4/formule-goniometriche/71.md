What is the maximum value of the function $f(x) = \sin x\cos x + \cos^2 x$?

- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $1$
- $1 + \sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{1 + \sqrt{2}}{2}$

## Explanation

The sine **double-angle formula** gives $\sin x\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x$, and the **power-reduction formula** gives $\cos^2 x = \dfrac{1 + \cos 2x}{2}$. Therefore
$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\left( \sin 2x + \cos 2x \right) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sin\left( 2x + \frac{\pi}{4} \right) ,
$$
where the auxiliary angle was used. Sine is at most $1$, so the maximum of $f$ is $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \dfrac{1 + \sqrt{2}}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{3}{2}$ results from adding the maxima of the two terms, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $1$, which occur at different points.
