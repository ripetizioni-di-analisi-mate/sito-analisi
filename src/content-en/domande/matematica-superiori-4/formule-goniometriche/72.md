What horizontal shift gives the graph of $f(x) = \cos x - \sin x$ from the graph of $y = \sqrt{2}\sin x$?

- To the right by $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- To the left by $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- To the left by $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- To the right by $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

With $a = -1$ and $b = 1$ we have $r = \sqrt{2}$, $\cos\varphi = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The point $(-1, 1)$ lies in the second quadrant, so the **auxiliary angle** is $\varphi = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ and
$$
f(x) = \sqrt{2}\sin\left( x + \frac{3\pi}{4} \right) .
$$
Replacing $x$ by $x + \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ shifts the graph to the left by $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$. A shift of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ to the left gives the graph of $\sin x + \cos x$ instead.
