Given $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{4}$, what is $\sin 3\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{11}{16}$
- $\dfrac{13}{16}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The triple-angle formula, obtained from the addition and double-angle formulas, gives $\sin 3\alpha = 3\sin\alpha - 4\sin^3\alpha$. Since $\sin^3\alpha = \dfrac{1}{64}$,
$$
\sin 3\alpha = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{4} - 4 \cdot \frac{1}{64} = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{16} = \frac{11}{16} .
$$
The value $\dfrac{3}{4}$ is three times the sine, and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ results from squaring $\dfrac{1}{4}$ instead of cubing it. The result is independent of the quadrant of $\alpha$ because the formula contains only $\sin\alpha$.
