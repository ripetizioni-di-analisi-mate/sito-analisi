Given $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ with $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$, what is $\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

## Explanation

The condition $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$ gives $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} < \dfrac{\alpha}{2} < \pi$, so the half-angle lies in the second quadrant, where cosine is negative. By the **half-angle formula**,
$$
\cos\frac{\alpha}{2} = -\sqrt{\frac{1 + \dfrac{1}{2}}{2}} = -\sqrt{\frac{3}{4}} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} .
$$
The known values provide a check, since $\alpha = \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$ and $\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ results from choosing the sign based on the quadrant of $\alpha$.
