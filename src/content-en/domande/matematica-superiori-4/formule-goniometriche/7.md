Applying the half-angle formula to the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, what value do we obtain for $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{12}$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{3}}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2 - \sqrt{3}}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}}}{2}$

## Explanation

The angle $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$ lies in the first quadrant, so the sine **half-angle formula** takes the sign $+$. Since $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$,
$$
\sin\frac{\pi}{12} = \sqrt{\frac{1 - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 - \sqrt{3}}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}}}{2} .
$$
The number $\dfrac{2 - \sqrt{3}}{4}$ is the square of $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{12}$, and $\dfrac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{3}}}{2}$ is the value of $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{12}$. The result equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4}$ because the two numbers are positive and have the same square $\dfrac{2 - \sqrt{3}}{4}$.
