Given that $\cos 3\alpha = 4\cos^3\alpha - 3\cos\alpha$ for every real $\alpha$ and that $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$, what is $\cos 3\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{23}{27}$
- $-\dfrac{23}{27}$
- $-\dfrac{5}{9}$
- $\dfrac{31}{27}$

## Explanation

Substitute $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$ into the triple-angle formula. Since $\cos^3\alpha = \dfrac{1}{27}$,
$$
\cos 3\alpha = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{27} - 3 \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{27} - 1 = -\frac{23}{27} .
$$
The value $-\dfrac{5}{9}$ results from squaring $\dfrac{1}{3}$ instead of cubing it. The value $\dfrac{31}{27}$ is greater than $1$, so it cannot be the cosine of an angle.
