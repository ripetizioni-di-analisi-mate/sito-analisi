What is $\cos^4\dfrac{\pi}{8} - \sin^4\dfrac{\pi}{8}$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{4}$

## Explanation

The left-hand side is a difference of squares, so
$$
\cos^4\frac{\pi}{8} - \sin^4\frac{\pi}{8} = \left( \cos^2\frac{\pi}{8} - \sin^2\frac{\pi}{8} \right)\left( \cos^2\frac{\pi}{8} + \sin^2\frac{\pi}{8} \right) .
$$
The second factor equals $1$ by the Pythagorean identity, and the first equals $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ by the cosine **double-angle formula**. Therefore the expression equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{4}$ is $\cos^2\dfrac{\pi}{8}$.
