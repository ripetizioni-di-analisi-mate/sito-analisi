Which of the following equalities holds for every real $x$?

- $\sin 2x\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}\left( \sin 3x - \sin x \right)$
- $\sin 2x\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}\left( \cos 3x + \cos x \right)$
- $\sin 2x\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}\left( \sin 3x + \sin x \right)$
- $\sin 2x\cos x = \sin 3x + \sin x$

## Explanation

By the first of **Werner's formulas**, $\sin\alpha\cos\beta = \dfrac{1}{2}\big( \sin(\alpha + \beta) + \sin(\alpha - \beta) \big)$. With $\alpha = 2x$ and $\beta = x$ we have $\alpha + \beta = 3x$ and $\alpha - \beta = x$, so
$$
\sin 2x\cos x = \frac{1}{2}\left( \sin 3x + \sin x \right) .
$$
The equality without the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ fails, for example, at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, where the left-hand side equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + \sin\dfrac{\pi}{4} = \sqrt{2}$.
