For which values of $x$ does the identity $\dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{1 + \cos 2x} = \tan^2 x$ hold?

- $x \neq k\pi$
- $x \neq k\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- For every real $x$
- $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$

## Explanation

The left-hand side is defined when $\cos 2x \neq -1$, that is, when $2x \neq \pi + 2k\pi$, hence for $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. The right-hand side is defined for the same values. By the **double-angle formulas**, $1 - \cos 2x = 2\sin^2 x$ and $1 + \cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x$, so the left-hand side equals $\dfrac{\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \tan^2 x$. Therefore the identity holds for $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. At $x = k\pi$ both sides equal $0$.
