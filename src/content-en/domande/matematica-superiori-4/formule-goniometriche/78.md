The identity $\dfrac{\sin 2x}{1+\cos 2x}=\tan x$ holds. At which points are neither the left-hand side nor the right-hand side defined?

- $x=k\pi$
- $x=\dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- At no point.
- $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}+k\pi$

## Explanation

By the double-angle formula $\cos 2x=2\cos^2x-1$, so the denominator is $1+\cos 2x=2\cos^2x$. The denominator vanishes if and only if $\cos x=0$, that is, for $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}+k\pi$, with $k\in\mathbb{Z}$. At the same points the tangent is not defined, because $\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$. Therefore both sides are undefined exactly at $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}+k\pi$, and the identity holds at all other points.
