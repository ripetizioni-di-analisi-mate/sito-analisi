How can $\sin x + \sqrt{3}\cos x$ be written in the form $r\sin(x + \varphi)$?

- $2\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$
- $2\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$
- $2\sin\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$
- $4\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$

## Explanation

With $a = 1$ and $b = \sqrt{3}$ we have $r = \sqrt{1 + 3} = 2$. The **auxiliary angle** satisfies $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. Therefore
$$
\sin x + \sqrt{3}\cos x = 2\sin\left( x + \frac{\pi}{3} \right) .
$$
The angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ results from interchanging the roles of $a$ and $b$ and corresponds to the expression $\sqrt{3}\sin x + \cos x$. The coefficient $4$ is $a^2 + b^2$ without the square root.
