How many zeros does the function $f(x) = \sin 4x - \sin 2x$ have in the interval $[0, \pi)$?

- $3$
- $4$
- $2$
- $6$

## Explanation

The **sum-to-product formula** for a difference of sines, with half-sum $3x$ and half-difference $x$, gives $f(x) = 2\cos 3x\sin x$. The product is zero when $\cos 3x = 0$ or $\sin x = 0$. The first condition gives $3x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, that is, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, which in $[0, \pi)$ gives $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. The second gives $x = 0$ in the interval. Therefore the number of zeros is $4$. The number $3$ results from omitting the factor $\sin x$.
