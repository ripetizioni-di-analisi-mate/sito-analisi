A force of $40\,\mathrm{N}$ makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the $x$-axis. What is its component along the $y$-axis?

- $20\,\mathrm{N}$
- $40 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $10 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$
- $20 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The components are the legs of the right triangle whose hypotenuse represents the force, and component $F_y$ is opposite angle $\theta$ made by the force with the $x$-axis. By the first right-triangle theorem, $F_y = F\sin\theta = 40 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$. The value $20\,\mathrm{N}$ is the component $F_x = F\cos\theta$.
