Triangle $ABC$ has a right angle at $A$, hypotenuse $a = 20$, and $\gamma = 30^\circ$. What is the length of leg $b$?

- $10$
- $20 \sqrt{3}$
- $\dfrac{40 \sqrt{3}}{3}$
- $10 \sqrt{3}$

## Explanation

Leg $b = CA$ has one endpoint at vertex $C$, so it is adjacent to angle $\gamma$. By the first right-triangle theorem, a leg equals the hypotenuse times the cosine of the adjacent acute angle, hence $b = a\cos\gamma$. This gives $b = 20 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 10\sqrt{3}$. The value $10$ is the length of leg $c = a\sin\gamma$, opposite $\gamma$.
