A triangle is inscribed in a circle of radius $7$, and two of its angles measure $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. How long is the side opposite the $45^\circ$ angle?

- $7 \sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{7 \sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $7$
- $14$

## Explanation

By the law of sines, each side equals the diameter of the circumcircle times the sine of the opposite angle. The diameter is $14$, so the required side has length $14\sin 45^\circ = 14 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 7\sqrt{2}$. The value $7$ is the length of the side opposite the $30^\circ$ angle.
