In triangle $ABC$, we have $a = 4$, $b = 6$, and $\alpha = 30^\circ$. What are the possible lengths of side $c$?

- $3\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}$ only
- $3\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}$ or $3\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{7}$
- $3\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{47}$ only
- $6\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}$ or $6\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{7}$

## Explanation

By the law of cosines, $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos\alpha$, that is, $16 = 36 + c^2 - 12c \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. This yields the quadratic equation $c^2 - 6\sqrt{3}\,c + 20 = 0$, for which one-quarter of the discriminant is $27 - 20 = 7$. Its solutions are $c = 3\sqrt{3} \pm \sqrt{7}$, both positive since $3\sqrt{3} > \sqrt{7}$. The two solutions correspond to the two triangles in the ambiguous case, consistent with $h = 6\sin 30^\circ = 3 < a < b$.
