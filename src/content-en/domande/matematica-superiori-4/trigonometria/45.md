A circle has radius $R$. Why does the chord formula for a **central** angle $\theta$ use the sine of $\dfrac{\theta}{2}$?

- Sine is defined only for half-angles.
- The perpendicular to the chord bisects the apex angle of the isosceles triangle formed by the radii.
- The arc always has twice the chord's length.
- A radius bisects every inscribed angle.

## Explanation

The two radii are equal sides. The perpendicular from the centre to the midpoint creates two right triangles, each with half-chord $R \sin (\dfrac{\theta}{2})$.
