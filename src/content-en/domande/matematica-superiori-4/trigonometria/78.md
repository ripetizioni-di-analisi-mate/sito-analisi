An observer stands on the roof of a building of height $20\,\mathrm{m}$. The observer sees the top of a tower at an angle of elevation of $45^\circ$ and its base at an angle of depression of $30^\circ$. How tall is the tower?

- $20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\left( 1 + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \right)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\left( 1 + \sqrt{3} \right)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $d$ be the horizontal distance between the building and the tower. In the right triangle whose legs are the height of the building and $d$, the second right-triangle theorem gives $20 = d\tan 30^\circ$, so $d = 20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$. The part of the tower above the observer has height $d\tan 45^\circ = 20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore the tower has height $20 + 20\sqrt{3} = 20\left( 1 + \sqrt{3} \right)\,\mathrm{m} \approx 54.64\,\mathrm{m}$.
