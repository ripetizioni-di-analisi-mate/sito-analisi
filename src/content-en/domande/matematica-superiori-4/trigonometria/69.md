How many triangles $ABC$ exist with $a = 5$, $b = 10$, and $\alpha = 30^\circ$?

- Exactly one, with a right angle at $B$
- Two
- None
- Exactly one, with obtuse $\beta$

## Explanation

This is the ambiguous case with acute $\alpha$. The distance from $C$ to the line containing side $c$ is $h = b\sin\alpha = 10 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 5$, so $a = h$. In this case the circle with center $C$ and radius $a$ is tangent to the line, and **exactly one triangle** exists, with a right angle at $B$. The law of sines confirms the result, since $\sin\beta = \dfrac{10 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}}{5} = 1$.
