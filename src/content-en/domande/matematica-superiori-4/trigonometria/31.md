Triangle $ABC$ has a right angle at $A$, hypotenuse $a = 10$, and $\beta = 30^\circ$. What is the length of the altitude to the hypotenuse?

- $\dfrac{5}{2}$
- $5 \sqrt{3}$
- $5$
- $\dfrac{5 \sqrt{3}}{2}$

## Explanation

By the first right-triangle theorem, the leg adjacent to $\beta$ is $c = a\cos\beta = 10 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}$. Let $H$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$. Triangle $ABH$ has a right angle at $H$, hypotenuse $c$, and angle $\beta$ at $B$. By the same theorem, $AH = c\sin\beta = 5\sqrt{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Dividing the product of the legs by the hypotenuse gives the same value, $\dfrac{5 \cdot 5\sqrt{3}}{10}$.
