Triangle $ABP$ is inscribed in a semicircle with diameter $AB = 2r$, and $x = \widehat{PAB}$, with $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. For which value of $x$ is $AP = \sqrt{3}\,PB$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{12}$

## Explanation

Triangle $ABP$ has a right angle at $P$ because the angle at $P$ intercepts a semicircle. By the first right-triangle theorem, $AP = 2r\cos x$ and $PB = 2r\sin x$. The condition becomes $\cos x = \sqrt{3}\sin x$, that is, $\tan x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, since within the restrictions $\cos x \neq 0$. Therefore $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The value $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ corresponds to the condition $PB = \sqrt{3}\,AP$.
