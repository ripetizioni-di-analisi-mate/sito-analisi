An isosceles triangle has equal sides of length $1$ and vertex angle $x$, with $0 < x < \pi$. For what value of $x$ does its base have length $\sqrt{2}$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

By the law of cosines, the square of the base is $1 + 1 - 2\cos x = 2 - 2\cos x$. The condition $2 - 2\cos x = 2$ is equivalent to $\cos x = 0$. On the interval $0 < x < \pi$, the only solution is $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and the triangle is an isosceles right triangle.
