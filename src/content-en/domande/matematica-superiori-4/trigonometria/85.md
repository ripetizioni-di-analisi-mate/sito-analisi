A triangle has sides of lengths $4$ and $6$ and included angle $x$, with $0 < x < \pi$. For which values of $x$ does its area equal $6\sqrt{3}$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ or $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ only
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ only

## Explanation

By the area formula using two sides and the included angle, $S(x) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 6 \sin x = 12\sin x$. The condition $12\sin x = 6\sqrt{3}$ is equivalent to $\sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. On the interval $0 < x < \pi$, the solutions are $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, and both satisfy the restrictions.
