The base of a tree is $20\,\mathrm{m}$ from an observer on level ground, and the top is seen at an angle of elevation of $60^\circ$. How tall is the tree if the observer's height is neglected?

- $\dfrac{20 \sqrt{3}}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The observer, the base, and the top of the tree form a triangle with a right angle at the base. The height is the leg opposite the angle of elevation and the distance is the adjacent leg. By the second right-triangle theorem, $h = 20\tan 60^\circ = 20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 34.64\,\mathrm{m}$.
