Three segments measure $a=6$, $b=4$ and $c=1.8$. We want to find the angle $\gamma$ opposite $c$ with the law of cosines, that is, with $\cos\gamma=\dfrac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}$. What do we obtain?

- $\gamma$ is a very small acute angle.
- No angle has the cosine obtained, so the three segments do not form a triangle.
- $\gamma$ is an obtuse angle.
- $\gamma$ is a right angle.

## Explanation

Substituting the measures gives $\cos\gamma=\dfrac{36+16-3.24}{48}=\dfrac{48.76}{48}\approx 1.016$. The cosine of an angle takes only values between $-1$ and $1$, so no angle has cosine $1.016$. Therefore the three segments do not form a triangle. Indeed $c=1.8$ is less than $a-b=2$, and the triangle inequality is not satisfied.
