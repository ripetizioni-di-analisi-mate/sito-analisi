Two stations are separated by $d$. The target makes two small angles with their baseline, both close to zero. Why is its distance from either station sensitive to angle errors when using the sine rule?

- The sine of the third angle exceeds one.
- The baseline must vanish.
- One divides by the sine of the third angle, which is near zero.
- The third angle is necessarily a right angle.

## Explanation

The third angle is close to $\pi$. Its sine is small, so small bearing errors can produce substantial changes in the quotient used for distance.
