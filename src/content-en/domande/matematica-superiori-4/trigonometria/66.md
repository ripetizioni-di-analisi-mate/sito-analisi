In a triangle, $\alpha = 40^\circ$, $\beta = 60^\circ$, and side $c = 10$ are known. How can side $a$ be found?

- Apply the law of cosines with angle $\alpha$
- Apply the first right-triangle theorem
- The given data do not determine the triangle
- Calculate $\gamma = 80^\circ$ and apply the law of sines

## Explanation

One side and two angles form the first case for solving triangles, and the triangle is unique. The third angle is $\gamma = 180^\circ - 40^\circ - 60^\circ = 80^\circ$, opposite the known side $c$. The law of sines then gives $a = \dfrac{c\sin\alpha}{\sin\gamma} = \dfrac{10\sin 40^\circ}{\sin 80^\circ} \approx 6.53$. Using the law of cosines with angle $\alpha$ would require sides $b$ and $c$, and $b$ is unknown.
