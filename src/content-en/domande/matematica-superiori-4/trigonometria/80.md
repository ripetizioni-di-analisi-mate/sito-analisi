Two observers on level ground are on opposite sides of a tower, in line with its base and $100\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. They see the top at angles of elevation of $45^\circ$ and $30^\circ$. How tall is the tower?

- $50\left( \sqrt{3} + 1 \right)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $50\,\mathrm{m}$
- $50\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $100\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right)\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Let $h$ be the height. By the second right-triangle theorem, the observers' distances from the base are $\dfrac{h}{\tan 45^\circ} = h$ and $\dfrac{h}{\tan 30^\circ} = \sqrt{3}\,h$. They are on opposite sides, so the two distances add up to $100\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, $h\left( 1 + \sqrt{3} \right) = 100$. Hence $h = \dfrac{100}{1 + \sqrt{3}} = 50\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right)\,\mathrm{m} \approx 36.60\,\mathrm{m}$.
