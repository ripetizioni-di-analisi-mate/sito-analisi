## Explanation

Two sides and angle $\alpha$ opposite $a$ are known, so this is the ambiguous case. The distance from $C$ to the line containing side $c$ is $h = b\sin\alpha = 4 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\sqrt{3} \approx 3.46$. Angle $\alpha$ is acute and $a = 3 < h$, so the circle with center $C$ and radius $a$ does not meet the line, and **no triangle exists**. The law of sines confirms this result, since it would give $\sin\beta = \dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} > 1$.
