A straight ramp of length $12\,\mathrm{m}$ rises a vertical distance of $3\,\mathrm{m}$. What is its angle of inclination above the horizontal, to the nearest hundredth of a degree?

- $14.04^\circ$
- $75.52^\circ$
- $18.43^\circ$
- $14.48^\circ$

## Explanation

The ramp is the hypotenuse of a right triangle in which the vertical rise is the leg opposite the angle of inclination $\theta$. By the first right-triangle theorem, $3 = 12\sin\theta$, so $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{4}$. The angle is acute, hence $\theta = \arcsin\dfrac{1}{4} \approx 14.48^\circ$. The value $14.04^\circ$ comes from using the tangent, treating the ramp as if it were the horizontal leg.
