What is the sum $1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 3 + 3 \cdot 4 + \dots + 10 \cdot 11$?

- $1320$
- $220$
- $440$
- $385$

## Explanation

For every $n \geq 1$, the formula $1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 3 + \dots + n(n + 1) = \dfrac{n(n + 1)(n + 2)}{3}$ holds, as can be proved by induction. The last term is $10 \cdot 11$, so the sum corresponds to $n = 10$ and equals $\dfrac{10 \cdot 11 \cdot 12}{3} = 440$. Writing each term as $k^2 + k$ gives the same value, because the sum of the squares from $1$ to $10$ is $385$ and the sum of the numbers from $1$ to $10$ is $55$.
