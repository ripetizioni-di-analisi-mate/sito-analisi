Which of the following sequences is an arithmetic progression?

- $a_n = n^2 - n$
- $a_n = 3 - 2n$
- $a_n = 2 \cdot 3^n$
- $a_n = \dfrac{n + 1}{n}$

## Explanation

For $a_n = 3 - 2n$, the difference $a_{n+1} - a_n = 3 - 2(n + 1) - 3 + 2n = -2$ is the same for every $n$, so the sequence is an arithmetic progression with common difference $-2$. For $n^2 - n$, the difference equals $2n$ and depends on $n$. The sequence $2 \cdot 3^n$ is a geometric progression with common ratio $3$. For $\dfrac{n + 1}{n}$, the differences between the first few terms, $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-\dfrac{1}{6}$, are unequal.
