We want to prove by induction that $5^n - 1$ is divisible by $4$ for every $n \geq 1$. Which equality allows us to deduce the inductive step from the inductive hypothesis?

- $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\left( 5^n - 1 \right) + 1$
- $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\left( 5^n - 1 \right) - 4$
- $5^{n+1} - 1 = \left( 5^n - 1 \right) + 4$
- $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\left( 5^n - 1 \right) + 4$

## Explanation

Write $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5 \cdot 5^n - 5 + 4$ by subtracting and adding $4$, then factor $5$ out of the first two terms. This gives $5^{n+1} - 1 = 5\left( 5^n - 1 \right) + 4$. By the inductive hypothesis, $5^n - 1 = 4m$ for some integer $m$, so $5^{n+1} - 1 = 20m + 4 = 4\,(5m + 1)$. Thus $5^{n+1} - 1$ is also divisible by $4$.
