For the sequence $a_n = \dfrac{n}{2n + 1}$, what is the difference $a_{n+1} - a_n$?

- $-\dfrac{1}{(2n + 1)(2n + 3)}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2n + 3}$
- $\dfrac{2}{(2n + 1)(2n + 3)}$
- $\dfrac{1}{(2n + 1)(2n + 3)}$

## Explanation

We have $a_{n+1} = \dfrac{n + 1}{2n + 3}$. Bring the two fractions to the common denominator $(2n + 1)(2n + 3)$. The numerator equals $(n + 1)(2n + 1) - n\,(2n + 3) = 2n^2 + 3n + 1 - 2n^2 - 3n = 1$. Thus $a_{n+1} - a_n = \dfrac{1}{(2n + 1)(2n + 3)}$, which is positive for every $n$, so the sequence is strictly increasing.
