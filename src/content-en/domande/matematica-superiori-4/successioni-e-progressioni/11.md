For what value of $k$ are $k$, $k + 4$, and $k + 16$, in that order, consecutive terms of a geometric progression?

- $k = 2$
- $k = -2$
- $k = 4$
- $k = 1$

## Explanation

Three numbers different from $0$ are consecutive terms of a geometric progression when the square of the middle term equals the product of the other two. Thus we require $(k + 4)^2 = k\,(k + 16)$, that is, $k^2 + 8k + 16 = k^2 + 16k$. This gives $8k = 16$, so $k = 2$. The terms are $2, 6, 18$, with common ratio $3$.
