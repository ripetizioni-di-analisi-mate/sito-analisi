For the sequence $a_n = \dfrac{2n - 1}{n + 1}$, with $n \geq 1$, what is the difference $a_{n+1} - a_n$?

- $\dfrac{3}{(n + 1)(n + 2)}$
- $\dfrac{1}{(n + 1)(n + 2)}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{(n + 1)(n + 2)}$
- $\dfrac{3}{n + 2}$

## Explanation

Write $a_{n+1} = \dfrac{2(n + 1) - 1}{n + 2} = \dfrac{2n + 1}{n + 2}$ and bring the two fractions to the common denominator $(n + 1)(n + 2)$. The numerator equals $(2n + 1)(n + 1) - (2n - 1)(n + 2) = 2n^2 + 3n + 1 - \left( 2n^2 + 3n - 2 \right) = 3$, so
$$
a_{n+1} - a_n = \frac{3}{(n + 1)(n + 2)} .
$$
The difference is positive for every $n \geq 1$, so the sequence is **strictly increasing**.
