An equilateral triangle $T_1$ has side length $6\ \mathrm{cm}$. Joining the midpoints of its sides gives the equilateral triangle $T_2$. The construction is repeated on $T_2$, then on $T_3$, and so on. What is the sum of the areas of all the triangles?

- $12\sqrt{3}\ \mathrm{cm^2}$
- $18\sqrt{3}\ \mathrm{cm^2}$
- $9\sqrt{3}\ \mathrm{cm^2}$
- $48\sqrt{3}\ \mathrm{cm^2}$

## Explanation

The area of $T_1$ is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 6^2 = 9\sqrt{3}\ \mathrm{cm^2}$. Each side is half as long as the preceding side by the midpoint theorem, so each area is $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the preceding one. The areas therefore form a geometric progression with $a_1 = 9\sqrt{3}$ and $q = \dfrac{1}{4}$, and their sum is
$$
S = \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{1 - \frac{1}{4}} = 12\sqrt{3}\ \mathrm{cm^2} .
$$
