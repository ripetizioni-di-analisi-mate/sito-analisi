Given the sequence $a_n = \dfrac{n^2 + 1}{2n}$, what is $a_3$?

- $\dfrac{10}{3}$
- $\dfrac{5}{3}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{5}{4}$

## Explanation

To calculate the term, substitute $n = 3$ into the formula for the general term. The numerator is $3^2 + 1 = 10$ and the denominator is $2 \cdot 3 = 6$, so $a_3 = \dfrac{10}{6} = \dfrac{5}{3}$.
