What is the general term of the sequence $\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{3}{4}, -\dfrac{4}{5}, \ldots$ when the index starts at $1$?

- $a_n = (-1)^{n+1}\,\dfrac{n}{n + 1}$
- $a_n = (-1)^{n}\,\dfrac{n}{n + 1}$
- $a_n = (-1)^{n+1}\,\dfrac{n + 1}{n}$
- $a_n = (-1)^{n+1}\,\dfrac{n}{n + 2}$

## Explanation

The absolute value of the term in position $n$ has numerator $n$ and denominator $n + 1$. The sign is positive for odd $n$ and negative for even $n$. The factor $(-1)^{n+1}$ equals $1$ for odd $n$ and $-1$ for even $n$. Thus $a_n = (-1)^{n+1}\,\dfrac{n}{n + 1}$. For example, $a_2 = -\dfrac{2}{3}$.
