Starting with a square of side length $8$, construct a new square by joining the midpoints of its sides. Repeat the construction on the new square, and so on. What is the area of the fourth square?

- $8$
- $16$
- $4$
- $1$

## Explanation

If a square has side length $\ell$, the side of the next square is the hypotenuse of an isosceles right triangle with legs of length $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, so it has length $\dfrac{\ell}{\sqrt{2}}$. Thus the areas form a geometric progression with first term $64$ and common ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The area of the fourth square is therefore $64 \cdot \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^3 = 8$.
