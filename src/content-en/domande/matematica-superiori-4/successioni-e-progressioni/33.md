What is the smallest natural number $n_0$ such that the inequality $2^n > n^2$ holds for every $n \geq n_0$?

- $5$
- $4$
- $1$
- $3$

## Explanation

For $n = 1$ we have $2 > 1$, but for $n = 2, 3, 4$ the inequality fails because $4 = 4$, $8 < 9$, and $16 = 16$. For $n = 5$ we have $32 > 25$, so the base case holds for $n_0 = 5$. The inductive step holds for $n \geq 5$ because $2^n > n^2$ implies $2^{n+1} > 2n^2$, and $2n^2 \geq (n + 1)^2$ for $n \geq 3$. By the principle of induction, the inequality holds for every $n \geq 5$.
