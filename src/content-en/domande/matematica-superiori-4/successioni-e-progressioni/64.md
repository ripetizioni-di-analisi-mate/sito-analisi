Why can $S_n=a_1(1-q^n)/(1-q)$ not be used directly for $q=1$, and what is $S_n$ in that case?

- The denominator is zero and $S_n=na_1$.
- The numerator is zero and $S_n=0$.
- The sequence alternates and $S_n=a_1$.
- The finite sum always diverges.

## Explanation

When $q=1$, every term equals $a_1$. The sum of $n$ copies is $na_1$. Deriving the quotient formula involves division by $1-q$ and requires $q\ne1$.
