For what value of $k$ are $k + 2$, $2k$, and $4k - 5$, in that order, consecutive terms of an arithmetic progression?

- $k = -3$
- $k = 7$
- $k = 1$
- $k = 3$

## Explanation

Three numbers are consecutive terms of an arithmetic progression if and only if the middle term is the arithmetic mean of the other two. Thus we require $2 \cdot 2k = (k + 2) + (4k - 5)$, that is, $4k = 5k - 3$. This gives $k = 3$, and the terms are $5, 6, 7$.
