Every expression $0e^{i\theta}$ represents the complex number zero. Why does this fail to give an argument for zero, even after selecting an interval of length $2\pi$?

- Zero has modulus one.
- The zero vector determines no direction.
- The interval must have length $\pi$ for all numbers.
- No complex exponential is defined for $\theta=0$.

## Explanation

The argument gives the direction of the ray from the origin through a nonzero point. When that point is the origin, every $\theta$ produces the same point and the vector determines no ray.
