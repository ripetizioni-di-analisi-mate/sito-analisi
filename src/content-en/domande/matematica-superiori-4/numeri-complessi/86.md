What are the solutions in $\mathbb{C}$ of the equation $z^2 + 6z + 13 = 0$?

- $z = 3 \pm 2i$
- $z = -3 \pm 4i$
- $z = -6 \pm 4i$
- $z = -3 \pm 2i$

## Explanation

The discriminant is $\Delta = 36 - 52 = -16$, which is negative. Thus the solutions are complex conjugates, with $\sqrt{-\Delta} = 4$:
$$
z_{1,2} = \frac{-6 \pm 4i}{2} = -3 \pm 2i .
$$
In the complex plane, the two solutions are reflections of one another across the real axis because they are conjugates.
