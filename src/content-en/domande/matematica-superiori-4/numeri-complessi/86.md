A cubic polynomial with real coefficients has zeros $2+i$ and $2-i$. What can be said about its third zero, counted with multiplicity?

- It must be zero.
- It must be $2$.
- It is nonzero and purely imaginary.
- It is real.

## Explanation

The product of the two conjugate factors is $(z-2)^2+1$, with real coefficients. Dividing the cubic by this factor leaves a real linear polynomial with a real root.
