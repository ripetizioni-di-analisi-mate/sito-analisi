What figure do the fifth roots of $32i$ form in the complex plane?

- The vertices of a regular pentagon inscribed in the circle of radius $2$
- The vertices of a regular pentagon inscribed in the circle of radius $32$
- The vertices of a regular pentagon inscribed in the circle of radius $\dfrac{32}{5}$
- Five points on the circle of radius $2$ separated by angles of $\dfrac{\pi}{5}$

## Explanation

The number $32i$ has modulus $32$, so its fifth roots all have modulus $\sqrt[5]{32} = 2$ and lie on the circle with center $O$ and radius $2$. The arguments of consecutive roots differ by $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$, so two consecutive roots form an angle of $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$ at the origin. The five roots are therefore the vertices of a **regular pentagon** inscribed in the circle of radius $2$.
