For which integer $n$, with $1 \leq n \leq 6$, is the number $\left( \cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)^n$ purely imaginary?

- $n = 6$
- $n = 2$
- $n = 4$
- $n = 3$

## Explanation

By De Moivre's formula, the power has modulus $1$ and argument $\dfrac{n\pi}{6}$. A number of modulus $1$ is purely imaginary when the cosine of its argument is zero, that is, when the argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ or $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ up to multiples of $2\pi$. For $1 \leq n \leq 6$, the argument $\dfrac{n\pi}{6}$ ranges from $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to $\pi$ and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ only for $n = 3$. Thus $n = 3$, and the power equals $i$.
