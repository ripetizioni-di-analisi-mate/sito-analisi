Someone computes $1/(3+4i)$ by replacing the denominator with its modulus $5$. Which observation already shows that $\dfrac{1}{5}$ is wrong?

- The reciprocal must have modulus $5$.
- Multiplying $\dfrac{1}{5}$ by $3+4i$ gives a nonzero imaginary part.
- Every reciprocal is real.
- The denominator has modulus $7$.

## Explanation

A reciprocal multiplied by the original number must equal $1$. Here the product would be $\dfrac{3}{5}+(\dfrac{4}{5})i$, whose imaginary term rules out equality.
