What is the argument in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ of the quotient $\dfrac{i}{1 + \sqrt{3}\,i}$?

- $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The number $i$ has argument $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The number $1 + \sqrt{3}\,i$ has modulus $2$, and the cosine and sine of its argument are $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so its argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. An argument of a quotient is the argument of the numerator minus the argument of the denominator, so it equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
