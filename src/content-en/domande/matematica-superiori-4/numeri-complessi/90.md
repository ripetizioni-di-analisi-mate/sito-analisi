What are the solutions in $\mathbb{C}$ of the equation $z^4 + 3z^2 - 4 = 0$?

- $z = \pm 1$ and $z = \pm 2i$
- $z = \pm 1$ and $z = \pm 2$
- $z = \pm i$ and $z = \pm 2$
- Only $z = \pm 1$

## Explanation

Set $t = z^2$ to obtain the equation $t^2 + 3t - 4 = 0$, which has solutions $t = 1$ and $t = -4$. The equation $z^2 = 1$ gives $z = \pm 1$. The equation $z^2 = -4$ gives $z = \pm 2i$ because $(2i)^2 = (-2i)^2 = -4$. Thus the equation has the four solutions $\pm 1$ and $\pm 2i$, as predicted by the fundamental theorem of algebra for an equation of degree $4$.
