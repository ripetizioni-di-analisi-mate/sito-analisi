Which set can be **exactly** the set of square roots of one nonzero complex number?

- $\{1+2i,1-2i\}$
- $\{1+2i,2+i\}$
- $\{1+2i\}$
- $\{1+2i,-1-2i\}$

## Explanation

From $u^2=v^2$ one obtains $(u-v)(u+v)=0$. Two distinct roots are therefore opposites, and a nonzero number has exactly two square roots.
