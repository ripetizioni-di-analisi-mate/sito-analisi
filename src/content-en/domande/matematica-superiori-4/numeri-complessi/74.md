The fourth roots of a complex number with argument $\theta$ have arguments $\dfrac{\theta}{4}+k\alpha$, with $k=0,1,2,3$. What is $\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $2\pi$

## Explanation

A complex number with argument $\theta$ also has as arguments all the values $\theta+2k\pi$, with $k$ an integer. A fourth root has as argument one of these values divided by $4$, so its argument is $\dfrac{\theta+2k\pi}{4}=\dfrac{\theta}{4}+\dfrac{k\pi}{2}$. Therefore $\alpha=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and the four roots are the vertices of a square.
