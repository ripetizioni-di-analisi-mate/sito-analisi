What is the argument in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ of the product $(1 + i)\left( -\sqrt{3} + i \right)$?

- $\dfrac{7\pi}{12}$
- $\dfrac{13\pi}{12}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{24}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$

## Explanation

The number $1 + i$ has argument $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The number $-\sqrt{3} + i$ has modulus $2$, and the cosine and sine of its argument are $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so its argument is $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. An argument of a product is the sum of the arguments, so it equals $\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = \dfrac{3\pi + 10\pi}{12} = \dfrac{13\pi}{12}$. This angle already lies in $[0, 2\pi)$.
