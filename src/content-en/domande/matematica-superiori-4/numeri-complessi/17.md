For which real values of $k$ is the number $z = (k^2 - 4) + (k + 2)\,i$ purely imaginary and nonzero?

- $k = \pm 2$
- $k = -2$
- $k = 2$
- $k = 4$

## Explanation

A number is purely imaginary when its real part is zero and its imaginary part is different from $0$. Thus we must have
$$
\begin{cases}
k^2 - 4 = 0 \\
k + 2 \neq 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first condition gives $k = 2$ or $k = -2$, and the second excludes $k = -2$, for which $z = 0$. Thus the only value is $k = 2$, for which $z = 4i$.
