What is the trigonometric form of the number $-3 + 3i$?

- $3\sqrt{2}\left( \cos\dfrac{\pi}{4} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$
- $3\sqrt{2}\left( \cos\dfrac{7\pi}{4} + i\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{4} \right)$
- $3\left( \cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + i\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} \right)$
- $3\sqrt{2}\left( \cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + i\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} \right)$

## Explanation

The modulus is $\sqrt{9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{2}$. The argument satisfies $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{3}{3\sqrt{2}} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so it lies in the second quadrant. The second-quadrant angle with reference angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$. Thus $-3 + 3i = 3\sqrt{2}\left( \cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + i\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} \right)$.
