For what real value of $k$ is the quotient $\dfrac{k + i}{1 + i}$ purely imaginary?

- $k = -1$
- $k = 1$
- $k = 0$
- $k = -2$

## Explanation

Multiply the numerator and denominator by $1 - i$, the conjugate of the denominator. The denominator becomes $(1 + i)(1 - i) = 2$, and the numerator becomes $(k + i)(1 - i) = k - ki + i - i^2 = (k + 1) + (1 - k)\,i$. The quotient is purely imaginary when its real part $\dfrac{k + 1}{2}$ is zero and its imaginary part $\dfrac{1 - k}{2}$ is different from $0$. The first condition gives $k = -1$, which also satisfies the second. Thus $k = -1$, and the quotient is $i$.
