What is the value of $\left( \sqrt{3} + i \right)^6$?

- $64$
- $64i$
- $-64$
- $-8$

## Explanation

The number $\sqrt{3} + i$ has modulus $\sqrt{3 + 1} = 2$, and the cosine and sine of its argument are $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so its argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. By De Moivre's formula, the sixth power has modulus $2^6 = 64$ and argument $6 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \pi$. Since $\cos\pi = -1$ and $\sin\pi = 0$, we obtain $\left( \sqrt{3} + i \right)^6 = -64$.
