The number $z$ has modulus $2$ and argument $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Which transformation of the complex plane takes the point representing a number $w \neq 0$ to the point representing $z w$?

- A rotation through $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ about the origin, with unchanged modulus
- A rotation through $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ about the origin and a dilation by a factor of $2$
- A rotation through $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ about the origin and a dilation by a factor of $2$
- A rotation through $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ about the origin and a dilation by a factor of $4$

## Explanation

By the product rule in trigonometric form, $z w$ has modulus $|z| \cdot |w| = 2\,|w|$ and an argument equal to the argument of $w$ plus $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Increasing the argument by $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ rotates the vector through $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ counterclockwise about the origin. Multiplying the modulus by $2$ scales the vector by a factor of $2$. Thus multiplication by $z$ is a rotation through $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ followed by a dilation by a factor of $2$.
