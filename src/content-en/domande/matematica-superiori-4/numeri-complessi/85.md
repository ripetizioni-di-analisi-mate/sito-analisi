For which values of $\theta$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ does the equality $e^{i\theta} = e^{-i\theta}$ hold?

- Only $\theta = 0$
- $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\theta = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$
- $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$
- For every $\theta$

## Explanation

By Euler's formula, $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ and $e^{-i\theta} = \cos\theta - i\sin\theta$, because the cosine is even and the sine is odd. The two numbers have the same real part, so they are equal if and only if $\sin\theta = -\sin\theta$, that is, $\sin\theta = 0$. In $[0, 2\pi)$, this happens for $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$, that is, when $e^{i\theta}$ is real and equals its own conjugate.
