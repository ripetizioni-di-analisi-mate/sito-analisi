What are the solutions of the equation $4\sin x = 1$?

- $x = \pm\arcsin\dfrac{1}{4} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \arcsin\dfrac{1}{4} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \pi - \arcsin\dfrac{1}{4} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \arcsin\dfrac{1}{4} + k\pi$
- $x = \arcsin\dfrac{1}{4} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \pi + \arcsin\dfrac{1}{4} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

The equation is equivalent to $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{4}$, and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ lies between $-1$ and $1$, so the equation has solutions. The value $\dfrac{1}{4}$ is not the sine of a special angle, so let $\alpha = \arcsin\dfrac{1}{4}$. By the theorem on the basic sine equation, the solutions are $x = \alpha + 2k\pi$ or $x = \pi - \alpha + 2k\pi$. The angle $\alpha$ lies in $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, so the first family contains an angle in the first quadrant and the second contains an angle in the second quadrant.
