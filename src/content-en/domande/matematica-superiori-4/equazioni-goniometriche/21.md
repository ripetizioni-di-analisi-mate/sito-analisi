Let $\alpha=\arcsin(-0.2)$, which is a negative angle. One solution of the equation $\sin x=-0.2$ in $[0,2\pi)$ is $\alpha+2\pi$. What is the other one?

- $-\alpha$
- $\pi-\alpha$
- $\pi+\alpha$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\alpha$

## Explanation

The line $y=-0.2$ meets the unit circle at two points symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis, one in the fourth quadrant and one in the third. The angle $\alpha+2\pi$ corresponds to the point in the fourth quadrant. Since $\sin(\pi-\alpha)=\sin\alpha=-0.2$, the angle $\pi-\alpha$ corresponds to the symmetric point. Moreover $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}<\alpha<0$, so $\pi<\pi-\alpha<\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ and the angle belongs to $[0,2\pi)$. The other solution is therefore $\pi-\alpha$.
