What are the solutions of the equation $\sin (5x) - \sin x = 0$?

- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{k\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

Using the sum-to-product identity for the difference of two sines, with $p = 5x$ and $q = x$, gives $\dfrac{p + q}{2} = 3x$ and $\dfrac{p - q}{2} = 2x$, so the equation becomes $2\cos(3x)\sin(2x) = 0$. The factor $\sin(2x)$ vanishes for $2x = k\pi$, that is, for $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$. The factor $\cos(3x)$ vanishes for $3x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, that is, for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{k\pi}{3}$. The solutions are the union of the two families.
