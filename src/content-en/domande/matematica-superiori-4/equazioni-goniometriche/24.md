Which solutions of the equation $\sqrt{2}\sin x + 1 = 0$ lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

The equation is equivalent to $\sin x = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so $\alpha = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The solutions are $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi = \dfrac{5\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$. The number $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ does not belong to $[0, 2\pi)$, whereas for $k = 1$ the first family gives $\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$. The second family gives $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ for $k = 0$.
