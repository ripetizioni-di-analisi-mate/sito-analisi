How many solutions does the equation $\sin (4x) = \cos (2x)$ have in the interval $[0, \pi)$?

- $4$
- $3$
- $2$
- $6$

## Explanation

By the double-angle identity, $\sin(4x) = 2\sin(2x)\cos(2x)$, so the equation becomes $\cos(2x)\,\big(2\sin(2x) - 1\big) = 0$. The factor $\cos(2x)$ vanishes for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$, which in $[0, \pi)$ gives $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$. The factor $2\sin(2x) - 1$ vanishes for $2x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $2x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$, that is, for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{12} + k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{12} + k\pi$. In $[0, \pi)$, these values are $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$, so there are four solutions.
