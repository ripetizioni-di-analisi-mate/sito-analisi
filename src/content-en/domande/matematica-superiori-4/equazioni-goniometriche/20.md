On the interval $\left[\dfrac{\pi}{3},\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right]$ the function $\tan x$ takes values of opposite sign at the endpoints. Why can one not conclude from this that the equation $\tan x=0$ has a solution in the interval?

- The tangent is negative on the whole interval.
- The interval contains two zeros of the tangent.
- Both endpoints are zeros of the tangent.
- The tangent is not defined at $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which belongs to the interval.

## Explanation

At the endpoints $\tan\dfrac{\pi}{3}=\sqrt3>0$ and $\tan\dfrac{2\pi}{3}=-\sqrt3<0$. The tangent is not defined at $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and goes from positive to negative values across the vertical asymptote $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the change of sign is not due to a zero. The zeros of the tangent are the angles $x=k\pi$, and none of them lies between $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. Therefore the equation $\tan x=0$ **has no solutions** in the interval.
