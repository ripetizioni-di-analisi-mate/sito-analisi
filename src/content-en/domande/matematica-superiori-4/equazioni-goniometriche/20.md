What are the solutions of the equation $2\sin x - \sqrt{3} = 0$?

- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{4\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

Isolating the sine gives $\sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Since $\arcsin\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, the first family is $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. The second is $x = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. The two angles lie in the first and second quadrants, where sine is positive.
