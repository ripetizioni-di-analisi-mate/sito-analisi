Which solutions of the equation $\sin (2x) = \cos x$ lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

By the double-angle identity, the equation becomes $2\sin x\cos x - \cos x = 0$, that is, $\cos x\,(2\sin x - 1) = 0$. The zero-product property gives $\cos x = 0$ or $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. In $[0, 2\pi)$, the cosine is zero at $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, and the sine equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$ at $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. There are therefore four solutions in the interval.
