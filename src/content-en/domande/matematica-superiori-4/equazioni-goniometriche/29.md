What are the solutions of the equation $2\cos x - \sqrt{2} = 0$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

Isolating the cosine gives $\cos x = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. Since $\arccos\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, the basic cosine equation gives the solutions $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$. The second family consists of the negatives of the angles in the first because $\cos(-\alpha) = \cos\alpha$. In contrast, the cosine of $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ is $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
