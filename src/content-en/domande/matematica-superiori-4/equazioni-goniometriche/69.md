What are the solutions of the equation $\sqrt{3}\sin x + \cos x = 0$?

- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

The equation is homogeneous of degree one. Points with $\cos x = 0$ are not solutions because at those points $\sin x = \pm 1$ and the left-hand side equals $\pm\sqrt{3}$. Dividing by $\cos x$ gives $\sqrt{3}\tan x + 1 = 0$, that is, $\tan x = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$. The solutions are therefore $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$.
