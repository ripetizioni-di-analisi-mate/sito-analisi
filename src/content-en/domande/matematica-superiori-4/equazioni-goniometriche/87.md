Which solutions of the equation $\sin\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ lie in the interval $[0, 4\pi]$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{7\pi}{3}$, and $\dfrac{11\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$, and $\dfrac{13\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

We have $\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$. Multiplying by $2$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 4k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{3} + 4k\pi$, so the period of the solutions is $4\pi$. In $[0, 4\pi]$, each family gives only one value, for $k = 0$, because setting $k = 1$ gives a value greater than $4\pi$ and setting $k = -1$ gives a negative number.
