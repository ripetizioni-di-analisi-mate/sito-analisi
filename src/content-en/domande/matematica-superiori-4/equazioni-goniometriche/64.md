What are the solutions of the equation $\sin^2 x - \cos^2 x = \dfrac{1}{2}$?

- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$

## Explanation

By the double-angle identity, $\cos(2x) = \cos^2 x - \sin^2 x$, so the left-hand side equals $-\cos(2x)$ and the equation becomes $\cos(2x) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $\arccos\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, we have $2x = \pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. Dividing both terms by $2$ gives $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$.
