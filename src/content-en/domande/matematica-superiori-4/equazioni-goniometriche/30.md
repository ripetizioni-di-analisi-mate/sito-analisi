Squaring $\sin x=-\cos x$ gives $\sin^2x=\cos^2x$. Which candidate in $[0,\pi)$ is introduced by squaring?

- $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $0$

## Explanation

The squared equation admits $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$. At the first angle sine and cosine are both positive, so the sign required by the original equation is missing.
