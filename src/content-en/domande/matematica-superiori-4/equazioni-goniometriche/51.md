What are the solutions of the equation $\tan^2 x - 2\tan x - 3 = 0$?

- $x = \arctan 3 + k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$
- $x = \arctan 3 + k\pi$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$
- $x = -\arctan 3 + k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$
- $x = \arctan 3 + 2k\pi$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

Setting $t = \tan x$ gives $t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0$, that is, $(t - 3)(t + 1) = 0$. The solutions $t = 3$ and $t = -1$ are both admissible because tangent takes every real value. The equation $\tan x = 3$ gives $x = \arctan 3 + k\pi$, and the equation $\tan x = -1$ gives $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$.
