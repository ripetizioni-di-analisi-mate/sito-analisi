What are the solutions of the equation $\sin (2x) = \sin x$?

- $x = 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$
- $x = 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

By the theorem for angles with equal sines, $2x = x + 2k\pi$ or $2x = \pi - x + 2k\pi$. The first equation gives $x = 2k\pi$. The second gives $3x = \pi + 2k\pi$, and dividing both terms by $3$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$. The family $x = 2k\pi$ is not contained in the second because $0$ cannot be obtained from $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$ with an integer $k$. The solutions are therefore the union of the two families.
