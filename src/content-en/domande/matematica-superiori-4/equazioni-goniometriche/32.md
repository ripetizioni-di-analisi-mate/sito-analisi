Which solutions of the equation $2\cos x + \sqrt{3} = 0$ lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$
- $-\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$

## Explanation

The equation is equivalent to $\cos x = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and $\arccos\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. The solutions are therefore $x = \pm\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$. The family with the plus sign gives $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ for $k = 0$. The number $-\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ does not belong to the interval, so choose $k = 1$ in the family with the minus sign to obtain $-\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2\pi = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$.
