What are the solutions of the equation $2\sin x\cos x + \cos^2 x = 0$?

- $x = -\arctan\dfrac{1}{2} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ or $x = -\arctan 2 + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ or $x = -\arctan\dfrac{1}{2} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ or $x = \arctan\dfrac{1}{2} + k\pi$

## Explanation

The coefficient of $\sin^2 x$ is $0$, so points with $\cos x = 0$ are solutions and dividing by $\cos^2 x$ would discard them. Factoring out $\cos x$ gives $\cos x\,(2\sin x + \cos x) = 0$. The first factor gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. The second factor is homogeneous of degree one, and dividing by $\cos x$ gives $\tan x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, $x = -\arctan\dfrac{1}{2} + k\pi$.
