A program records a zero only if the values on either side have opposite signs. Why can it miss the solution $x=0$ of $1-\cos x=0$?

- Cosine is undefined at zero.
- The function has a jump at zero.
- The function is positive on both sides sufficiently close to zero.
- $1-\cos0$ equals two.

## Explanation

Cosine attains its maximum of one at zero. For $0<|x|<\pi$ it is less than one, so the left-hand side is positive on both sides of the zero and the sign test does not detect it.
