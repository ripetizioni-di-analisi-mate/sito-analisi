Which solutions of the equation $\sin (2x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ lie in the interval $[0, \pi]$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$

## Explanation

We have $2x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $2x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$, so $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$. In $[0, \pi]$, each family gives only one value, for $k = 0$. Indeed, setting $k = 1$ gives $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, both greater than $\pi$.
