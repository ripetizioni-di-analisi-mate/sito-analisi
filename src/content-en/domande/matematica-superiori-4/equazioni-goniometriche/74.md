Which solutions of the equation $\cos x - \sqrt{3}\sin x = 1$ lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$?

- $0$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- Only the value $0$
- $0$ and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

We have $a = -\sqrt{3}$, $b = 1$, and $r = 2$, so the auxiliary angle satisfies $\cos\varphi = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, $\varphi = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. The equation becomes $\sin\left(x + \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, hence $x + \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $x + \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$. This gives $x = -\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $x = 2k\pi$. In $[0, 2\pi)$, the first family gives $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ for $k = 1$, and the second gives $0$.
