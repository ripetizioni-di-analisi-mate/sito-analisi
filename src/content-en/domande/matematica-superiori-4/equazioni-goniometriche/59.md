What are the solutions of the equation $\sin x + \sin (3x) = 0$?

- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- $x = k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{4}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$

## Explanation

Using the sum-to-product identity for the sum of two sines, with $p = 3x$ and $q = x$, gives $\dfrac{p + q}{2} = 2x$ and $\dfrac{p - q}{2} = x$. The equation therefore becomes $2\sin(2x)\cos x = 0$. The factor $\sin(2x)$ vanishes for $2x = k\pi$, that is, for $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$. The factor $\cos x$ vanishes for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. These values already belong to the preceding family, so the solutions are $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$.
