Which solutions of the equation $2\sin^2 x + \sin x - 1 = 0$ lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

Setting $t = \sin x$ gives $2t^2 + t - 1 = 0$, which has $\Delta = 1 + 8 = 9$, so $t = \dfrac{-1 \pm 3}{4}$. The solutions are $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $t = -1$, both admissible. In $[0, 2\pi)$, the equation $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ gives $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, and the equation $\sin x = -1$ gives $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$.
