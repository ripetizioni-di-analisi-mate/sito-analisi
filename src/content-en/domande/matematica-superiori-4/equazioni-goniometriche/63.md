A curve is given by $y=(\sin x-a)^2$ with $-1<a<1$. How does it meet the horizontal axis at each zero?

- It always crosses upwards.
- It touches the axis and stays on the same side.
- It always crosses downwards.
- It has a hole in its domain.

## Explanation

The curve has positive height on either side of each solution of $\sin x=a$ because the factor is squared. Its sign therefore stays the same across the zero.
