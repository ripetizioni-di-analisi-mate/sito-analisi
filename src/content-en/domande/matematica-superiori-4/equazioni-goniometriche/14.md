- The pairs $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$
- Only the pair $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$
- The pairs $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$
- Only the pair $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$

## Explanation

The second equation gives $y = \pi - x$, and the identity for supplementary angles gives $\sin y = \sin(\pi - x) = \sin x$. The first equation becomes $2\sin x = 1$, that is, $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. In $[0, \pi]$, this gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. The corresponding values $y = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $y = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ also belong to $[0, \pi]$, so there are two solution pairs.
