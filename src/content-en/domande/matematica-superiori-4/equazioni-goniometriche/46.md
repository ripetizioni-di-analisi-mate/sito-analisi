Using identities for related angles, which of the following is equivalent to the equation $\sin (3x) = -\sin x$?

- $\sin (3x) = \sin (\pi - x)$
- $\sin (-3x) = \sin (-x)$
- $\sin (3x) = \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x\right)$
- $\sin (3x) = \sin (-x)$

## Explanation

By the negative-angle identity, $-\sin x = \sin(-x)$, so the equation becomes $\sin(3x) = \sin(-x)$. The other three equations are equivalent to $\sin(3x) = \sin x$, since $\sin(\pi - x) = \sin x$, $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \sin x$, and $\sin(-3x) = \sin(-x)$ is equivalent to $-\sin(3x) = -\sin x$. The equation $\sin(3x) = \sin x$ has different solutions from the given equation. For example, at $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ we have $\sin(3x) = \sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so $\sin(3x) \neq -\sin x$.
