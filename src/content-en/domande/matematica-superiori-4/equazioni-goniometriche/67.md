What are the solutions of the equation $\sqrt{3}\sin x - \cos x = 1$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \pi + 2k\pi$
- $x = 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

We have $a = \sqrt{3}$, $b = -1$, and $r = 2$, so the auxiliary angle satisfies $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin\varphi = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The equation becomes $\sin\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, hence $x - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $x - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$. This gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \pi + 2k\pi$. Indeed, for $x = \pi$ we have $\sqrt{3} \cdot 0 - (-1) = 1$.
