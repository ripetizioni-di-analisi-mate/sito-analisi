What are the solutions of the equation $\cos (2x) + 5\sin x - 3 = 0$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

With the double-angle identity $\cos(2x) = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$, the equation becomes $1 - 2\sin^2 x + 5\sin x - 3 = 0$, that is, $2\sin^2 x - 5\sin x + 2 = 0$. Setting $t = \sin x$ gives $\Delta = 25 - 16 = 9$, so $t = \dfrac{5 \pm 3}{4}$, that is, $t = 2$ or $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $t = 2$ is inadmissible because the sine cannot exceed $1$. This leaves $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$.
