What are the solutions of the equation $\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4} - x\right)$?

- $x = 2k\pi$
- $x = k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

By the theorem for angles with equal sines, $x + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{4} - x + 2k\pi$ or $x + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{4} + x + 2k\pi$. The first equation gives $2x = 2k\pi$, that is, $x = k\pi$. The second gives $0 = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, which is false for every integer $k$, so it yields no solutions. The solutions are therefore $x = k\pi$.
