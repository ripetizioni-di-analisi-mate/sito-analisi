A program searches for every intersection of $y=\cos x$ with $y=x$ on $[0,\pi/2]$. Which argument determines the number of intersections without approximating their coordinates?

- Cosine is always nonnegative, so the curves never meet.
- The difference $\cos x-x$ is continuous, strictly decreasing, and changes sign between the endpoints.
- The periodicity of cosine gives infinitely many intersections on this interval.
- Cosine has different values at the two endpoints, so there are two intersections.

## Explanation

The difference equals $1$ at $0$ and $-\pi/2$ at $\pi/2$. Continuity guarantees a zero between them. As $x$ increases, cosine decreases and a larger number is subtracted, so the difference crosses zero only once.
