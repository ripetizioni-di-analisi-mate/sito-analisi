Which argument determines how many solutions the equation $\cos x=x$ has on the interval $\left[0,\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$?

- Cosine is non-negative on the interval, so the equation has no solutions.
- The difference $\cos x-x$ is continuous, strictly decreasing and changes sign between the endpoints.
- The periodicity of cosine implies infinitely many solutions on the interval.
- Cosine takes different values at the endpoints, so there are two solutions.

## Explanation

Let $f(x)=\cos x-x$. We have $f(0)=1>0$ and $f\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)=-\dfrac{\pi}{2}<0$, and since $f$ is continuous it vanishes at least once on the interval. On the interval $\cos x$ is decreasing and $-x$ is strictly decreasing, so $f$ is strictly decreasing. A strictly decreasing function vanishes at most once, so the equation has **exactly one** solution.
