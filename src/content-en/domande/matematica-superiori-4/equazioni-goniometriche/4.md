- $x = k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- $x = k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

By the theorem for equations of the form $\cos f(x) = \cos g(x)$, we have $3x = x + 2k\pi$ or $3x = -x + 2k\pi$. The first equation gives $2x = 2k\pi$, that is, $x = k\pi$. The second gives $4x = 2k\pi$, that is, $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$. Every integer multiple of $\pi$ is also an integer multiple of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the first family is contained in the second, and the solutions are $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$.
