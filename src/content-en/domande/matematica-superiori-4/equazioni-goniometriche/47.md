What are the solutions of the equation $\cos (3x) = -\cos x$?

- $x = \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

By the identity $-\cos\alpha = \cos(\pi - \alpha)$, the equation becomes $\cos(3x) = \cos(\pi - x)$. The first family gives $3x = \pi - x + 2k\pi$, that is, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$. The second gives $3x = -\pi + x + 2k\pi$, that is, $2x = -\pi + 2k\pi$ and $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, which can also be written as $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. The value $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ does not belong to the first family, so the solutions are the union of the two families.
