What are the solutions of the equation $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} + 1 = 0$?

- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

The tangent of $\dfrac{x}{2}$ is defined for $\dfrac{x}{2} \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, that is, for $x \neq \pi + 2k\pi$. The equation is equivalent to $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} = -1$, and since $\arctan(-1) = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, it follows that $\dfrac{x}{2} = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$. Multiplying both terms by $2$ gives $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, so the period of the solutions is $2\pi$. These values satisfy the domain restrictions because the tangent of $\dfrac{x}{2}$ equals $-1$ at those points.
