What are the solutions of the equation $\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \pi + 2k\pi$
- $x = 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$
- $x = 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = 2k\pi$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

Setting $t = x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ gives the equation $\sin t = \dfrac{1}{2}$, whose solutions are $t = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $t = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$. Subtracting $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ in the first family gives $x = 2k\pi$. The second gives $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} - \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$.
