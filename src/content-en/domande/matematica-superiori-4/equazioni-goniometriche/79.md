What are the solutions of the equation $\sin x\cos x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$

## Explanation

By the double-angle identity, $\sin x\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}\sin(2x)$, so the equation becomes $\sin(2x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. We have $2x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ or $2x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. Dividing both terms by $2$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$.
