What are the solutions of the equation $\sin x = \cos (3x)$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{8} + 2k\pi$ or $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

Since $\cos(3x) = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - 3x\right)$, the equation becomes $\sin x = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - 3x\right)$. The first family gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} - 3x + 2k\pi$, that is, $4x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$ and $x = \dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$. The second gives $x = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 3x + 2k\pi$, that is, $-2x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$ and $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} - k\pi$, which can also be written as $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$. The solutions are the union of the two families.
