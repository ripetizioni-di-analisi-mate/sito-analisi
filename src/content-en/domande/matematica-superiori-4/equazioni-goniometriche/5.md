- $\dfrac{2\pi}{9}$, $\dfrac{8\pi}{9}$, $\dfrac{14\pi}{9}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{9}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{2\pi}{9}$, $\dfrac{8\pi}{9}$, $\dfrac{14\pi}{9}$

## Explanation

By the theorem for angles with equal sines, $2x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} = x + 2k\pi$ or $2x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \pi - x + 2k\pi$. The first equation gives $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$, which in $[0, 2\pi)$ gives only $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$, for $k = 1$. The second gives $3x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$, that is, $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{9} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$. With $k = 0, 1, 2$, the values are $\dfrac{2\pi}{9}$, $\dfrac{8\pi}{9}$, $\dfrac{14\pi}{9}$, whereas with $k = 3$ the result exceeds $2\pi$.
