What are the solutions of the equation $\sin x + \cos x = \sqrt{2}$?

- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$
- $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$
- $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$

## Explanation

We have $a = b = 1$ and $r = \sqrt{2}$, so the auxiliary angle is $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and the equation becomes $\sqrt{2}\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \sqrt{2}$. Therefore $\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = 1$, and by the special-case formula for sine, $x + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. Subtracting $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$.
