Under $t=\cos x$ an equation becomes $(t-1)(t-2)=0$. Which addition to the equation in $t$ makes it equivalent to solving for $x\in[0,\pi]$?

- Impose $-1\leq t\leq1$.
- Impose only $t>0$.
- Impose $-\pi\leq t\leq\pi$.
- Allow every real $t$.

## Explanation

On $[0,\pi]$, cosine takes each value from $-1$ to $1$ exactly once. The restriction removes $t=2$, while $t=1$ corresponds to $x=0$.
