For $x,y>0$, Luca claims that $x^{\ln y}=y^{\ln x}$. Which step verifies the identity?

- The two bases are always equal.
- The natural logarithms of both sides are $\ln x\cdot\ln y$.
- Both exponents must be positive.
- The rule $\ln(x+y)=\ln x+\ln y$ holds.

## Explanation

Both sides are positive. The logarithm-of-a-power rule gives the same product of logarithms in opposite orders. The logarithm is one-to-one, so the original values are equal.
