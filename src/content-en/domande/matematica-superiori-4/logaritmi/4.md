- $(-\infty, -3) \cup [1, +\infty)$

## Explanation

The logarithm is defined when its argument is strictly positive, so solve the inequality $\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 3} > 0$.

The numerator is positive for $x > 1$ and the denominator is positive for $x > -3$. The quotient is positive when the numerator and denominator have the same sign, that is, for $x < -3$ or $x > 1$.

The value $x = 1$ is excluded because it makes the argument zero and $\ln 0$ is undefined. Therefore $D = (-\infty, -3) \cup (1, +\infty)$.
