Let $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{\ln x}$. What does $f(x)$ approach as $x$ approaches $1$ from the left and as $x$ approaches $1$ from the right?

- $+\infty$ from both sides
- $-\infty$ from the left and $+\infty$ from the right
- $0$ from both sides
- $+\infty$ from the left and $-\infty$ from the right

## Explanation

As $x$ approaches $1$, the denominator $\ln x$ approaches $\ln 1=0$, so the absolute value of $f(x)$ grows without bound. For $0<x<1$ we have $\ln x<0$, therefore from the left $f(x)$ is negative and approaches $-\infty$. For $x>1$ we have $\ln x>0$, therefore from the right $f(x)$ is positive and approaches $+\infty$.
