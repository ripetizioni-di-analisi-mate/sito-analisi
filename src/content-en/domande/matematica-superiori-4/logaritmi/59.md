What is the domain of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\ln (x - 2)}$?

- $(2, +\infty)$
- $(2, 3) \cup (3, +\infty)$
- $(3, +\infty)$
- $(2, 3)$

## Explanation

The logarithm argument must be positive and the denominator must differ from $0$:
$$
\begin{cases}
x - 2 > 0 \\[4pt]
\ln (x - 2) \neq 0 .
\end{cases}
$$
Since the only number whose logarithm is zero is $1$, the second condition is equivalent to $x - 2 \neq 1$, that is, $x \neq 3$. Therefore the domain is $(2, 3) \cup (3, +\infty)$.
