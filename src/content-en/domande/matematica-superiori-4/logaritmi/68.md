Why does $\log _a x=\dfrac{1}{\log _x a}$ also require $x\ne 1$, even when $a>1$?

- On the right $x$ is a logarithm base.
- The logarithm of one is never defined on the left.
- Powers with base $a$ can never equal one.
- One has two logarithms to base $a$.

## Explanation

At $x=1$ the left side would equal zero. The right side, however, uses base one, which admits no logarithm as an inverse function, so the identity does not apply there.
