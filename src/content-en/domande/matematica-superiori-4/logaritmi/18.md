Let $p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_n$ be numbers with $0<p_i\le 1$ and let $P=p_1p_2\cdots p_n$. Which of the following quantities is a strictly decreasing function of $P$?

- $\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln p_i$
- $-\prod_{i=1}^{n}\ln p_i$
- $\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_i$
- $-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln p_i$

## Explanation

By the product rule for logarithms, $\ln P=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln p_i$, so $-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln p_i=-\ln P$. Since the natural logarithm is strictly increasing, $-\ln P$ is a strictly decreasing function of $P$. The sum $\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln p_i$ instead equals $\ln P$, so it increases with $P$. The quantities $\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_i$ and $-\prod_{i=1}^{n}\ln p_i$ do not depend on $P$ alone. For example, with $n=2$, the pairs $\left(1,\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ give the same product $\dfrac{1}{4}$, but different sums and different values of $-\ln p_1\ln p_2$.
