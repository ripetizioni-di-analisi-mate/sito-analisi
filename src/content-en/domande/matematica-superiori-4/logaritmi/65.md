What are the solutions of the equation $\log_3 \left( x^2 - 1 \right) = \log_3 (x + 1) + 1$?

- $x = 2$
- $x = -1$ and $x = 4$
- $x = 4$
- $x = -1$

## Explanation

The domain conditions must hold together and give
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 - 1 > 0 \\[4pt]
x + 1 > 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad x > 1 .
$$
Write $1 = \log_3 3$; by the product rule for logarithms the right-hand side becomes $\log_3 \big( 3(x + 1) \big)$. Two logarithms in the same base are equal if and only if their arguments are equal, so $x^2 - 1 = 3(x + 1)$. Since $x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)$, the equation becomes $(x + 1)(x - 4) = 0$, hence $x = -1$ or $x = 4$. Only $x = 4$ satisfies the condition $x > 1$, so the equation has the single solution $x = 4$. The value $x = 2$ is obtained by neglecting the term $1$ on the right-hand side.
