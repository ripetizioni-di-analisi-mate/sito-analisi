In the equation $5^{2x}-5^x-6=0$ the substitution $t=5^x$ leads to the only admissible root $t=3$. What is the solution $x$, and in which interval does it lie?

- $x=\dfrac{\ln 3}{\ln 5}$, with $0<x<1$
- $x=\dfrac{\ln 5}{\ln 3}$, with $0<x<1$
- $x=\ln \dfrac{3}{5}$, with $x<0$
- $x=\ln 15$, with $x>1$

## Explanation

The solution comes from the equation $5^x=3$. Since both sides are positive, we take the natural logarithm and, by the power rule for logarithms, we obtain $x\ln 5=\ln 3$. Since $\ln 5\neq 0$, dividing gives $x=\dfrac{\ln 3}{\ln 5}\approx 0.683$. Moreover $5^0<3<5^1$ and the function $5^x$ is increasing, so the solution satisfies $0<x<1$.
