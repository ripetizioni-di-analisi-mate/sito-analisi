At what point does the graph of $y = \ln (x - 1) + 2$ meet the $x$-axis?

- $x = 1 - e^{-2}$
- $x = 1 + e^{-2}$
- $x = 1 + e^2$
- $x = 3$

## Explanation

The intersection with the $x$-axis has vertical coordinate $0$, so solve $\ln (x - 1) + 2 = 0$, that is, $\ln (x - 1) = -2$.

By the definition of a logarithm, $x - 1 = e^{-2}$, so $x = 1 + e^{-2}$.

The value satisfies the domain condition $x > 1$ because $e^{-2} > 0$.
