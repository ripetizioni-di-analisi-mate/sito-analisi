Which of the following expressions, written with common logarithms, equals $\ln x$ for every $x>0$?

- $\log_{10}x+\log_{10}e$
- $\dfrac{\log_{10}x}{\log_{10}e}$
- $\dfrac{\log_{10}e}{\log_{10}x}$
- $\log_{10}x\cdot\log_{10}e$

## Explanation

Applying the change-of-base formula from base $e$ to base $10$ gives $\ln x=\log_e x=\dfrac{\log_{10}x}{\log_{10}e}$. The formula holds for every $x>0$, because $e$ is an admissible base and therefore $\log_{10}e\neq 0$. The sum $\log_{10}x+\log_{10}e$ instead equals $\log_{10}(ex)$. The product $\log_{10}x\cdot\log_{10}e$ is about $0.434\log_{10}x$, whereas $\ln x$ is about $2.303\log_{10}x$, so the two expressions are different.
