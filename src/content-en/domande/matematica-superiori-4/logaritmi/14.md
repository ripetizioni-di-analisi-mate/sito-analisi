An earthquake's magnitude and the energy $E$ it releases, in joules, are related by $\log_{10} E = 4.8 + 1.5\, M$. Two earthquakes have magnitudes $6.2$ and $4.2$. How many times as much energy does the first release as the second?

- $10^2$
- $10^{1.5}$
- $10^3$
- $10^{7.8}$

## Explanation

Write the relation for each earthquake and subtract corresponding sides of the equalities. The term $4.8$ cancels, giving
$$
\log_{10} E_1 - \log_{10} E_2 = 1.5 \cdot (6.2 - 4.2) = 3 .
$$
By the quotient rule for logarithms, the left-hand side is $\log_{10} \dfrac{E_1}{E_2}$, so $\dfrac{E_1}{E_2} = 10^3$. Therefore the first earthquake releases $1000$ times as much energy as the second.
