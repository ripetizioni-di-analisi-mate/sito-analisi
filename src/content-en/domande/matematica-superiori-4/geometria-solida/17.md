Points $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$ do not lie in the same plane. How many distinct planes pass through three of these points?

- $1$
- $6$
- $4$
- $12$

## Explanation

If three of the four points were collinear, their line and the fourth point would determine a plane containing all four, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus each triple consists of noncollinear points and determines exactly one plane. There are $4$ triples, namely $ABC$, $ABD$, $ACD$, and $BCD$. Two different triples cannot determine the same plane because that plane would contain all four points. Therefore there are $4$ distinct planes.
