Two similar pyramids have heights $4$ and $6$, and the first has volume $32$. What is the volume of the second?

- $48$
- $72$
- $96$
- $108$

## Explanation

The scale factor is the ratio of corresponding lengths, namely $k = \dfrac{6}{4} = \dfrac{3}{2}$. The volumes of similar solids are in the ratio $k^3 = \dfrac{27}{8}$. Therefore the volume of the second pyramid is $32 \cdot \dfrac{27}{8} = 108$. The value $72$ comes from using $k^2$, which is the ratio of the areas.
