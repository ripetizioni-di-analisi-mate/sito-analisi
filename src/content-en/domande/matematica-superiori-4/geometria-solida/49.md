A convex polyhedron has only triangular faces. Can it have exactly $7$ faces?

- Yes, provided it has seven vertices.
- Yes, any number of triangles closes a solid.
- No, only tetrahedra have triangular faces.
- No, since the total face-edge incidences must be even.

## Explanation

Each edge belongs to two faces. Seven triangles would yield $3\cdot 7=21$ incidences, which cannot be paired up.
