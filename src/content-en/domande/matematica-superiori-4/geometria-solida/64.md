A chord of a sphere lies in a cross-sectional plane. Why can the chord not be longer than the diameter of the section circle even if the sphere's diameter is greater?

- Every chord must pass through the center of the sphere.
- Its endpoints lie on the same section circle.
- The plane must be tangent.
- All cross-sectional diameters are equal.

## Explanation

Both endpoints are in the plane and on the sphere's surface. They therefore lie on the boundary of the section, whose maximum possible point-to-point distance is its own diameter.
