Cavalieri's principle is used to compare a hemisphere of radius $5$ with a cylinder of radius $5$ and height $5$ from which a cone has been removed, both resting on the same plane. What is the cross-sectional area of the cylinder with the conical cavity when cut by a parallel plane at a distance of $3$ from the base plane?

- $16\pi$
- $9\pi$
- $25\pi$
- $34\pi$

## Explanation

The solid with the cavity is formed by removing a cone from a cylinder of radius $5$ and height $5$. The apex of the removed cone is the center of the lower base. The apex lies in the base plane, and the radius of the cone equals its height, so at a distance of $3$ from the apex, the cross section of the cone has radius $3$. The cross section of the cylinder with the conical cavity is therefore an annulus with radii $5$ and $3$ and area $25\pi - 9\pi = 16\pi$. The cross section of the hemisphere has the same area, since it is a disk of radius $\sqrt{25 - 9} = 4$.
