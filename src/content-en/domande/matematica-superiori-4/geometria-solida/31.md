A dihedral angle is cut by a plane parallel to its edge and distinct from both face planes. If the cutting plane meets both faces, why do the two intersection lines fail to measure the dihedral angle?

- They are both parallel to the edge.
- They are always perpendicular to each other.
- They are two circles.
- The dihedral angle must become right.

## Explanation

Each face plane contains the direction of the edge, as does the cutting plane. Both traces are parallel. The angular measure is obtained from a section perpendicular to the edge.
