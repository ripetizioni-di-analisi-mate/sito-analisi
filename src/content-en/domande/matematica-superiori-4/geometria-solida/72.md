A pyramid and a prism have bases of equal area in the same plane and have the same height. Which statement is true?

- The two solids have equal volumes by Cavalieri's principle
- The volume of the pyramid is half the volume of the prism
- The volume of the pyramid is one-third of the volume of the prism
- Every plane parallel to the bases cuts the two solids in cross sections of equal area

## Explanation

The prism has volume $B \cdot h$ and the pyramid has volume $\dfrac{1}{3}\,B \cdot h$, so the volume of the pyramid is one-third that of the prism. All cross sections of the prism parallel to the base have area $B$, whereas at a distance of $x$ from the apex, the cross section of the pyramid has area $B \left( \dfrac{x}{h} \right)^2$, which is less than $B$ for $x < h$. Therefore the cross sections have unequal areas, the hypothesis of Cavalieri's principle does not hold, and the two solids have unequal volumes.
