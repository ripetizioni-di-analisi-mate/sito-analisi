Segment $PH$ is perpendicular to plane $\alpha$, with $H$ in $\alpha$. Line $r$ in $\alpha$ does not pass through $H$, and $A$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $H$ to $r$. Let $B$ be a point on $r$ other than $A$. Which angle is a right angle?

- $\widehat{PAB}$
- $\widehat{PAH}$
- $\widehat{APH}$
- $\widehat{ABP}$

## Explanation

The hypotheses of the **three perpendiculars theorem** hold because $PH$ is perpendicular to $\alpha$ and $HA$ is perpendicular to $r$. Therefore $PA$ is perpendicular to line $r$, and since $B$ lies on $r$, angle $\widehat{PAB}$ is a right angle. Triangle $PHA$ has a right angle at $H$, so its angles $\widehat{PAH}$ and $\widehat{APH}$ are acute. Angle $\widehat{ABP}$ is also acute because it belongs to triangle $PAB$, which has a right angle at $A$.
