From a deck of $40$ Neapolitan cards, draw $2$ cards together. What is the probability that both belong to the suit of coins?

- $\dfrac{1}{16}$
- $\dfrac{3}{52}$
- $\dfrac{3}{26}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

The possible outcomes are pairs of cards, numbering $\dbinom{40}{2} = 780$. The favorable outcomes are pairs of cards in the suit of coins, numbering $\dbinom{10}{2} = 45$. Therefore $P = \dfrac{45}{780} = \dfrac{3}{52}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{16} = \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4}$ corresponds to two draws with replacement.
