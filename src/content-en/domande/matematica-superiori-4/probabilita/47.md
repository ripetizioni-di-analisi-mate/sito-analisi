Two events $A,B$ are disjoint, with $P(A)=0.4$ and $P(B)=0$. Can they also be independent?

- No, disjoint events are always dependent.
- Yes, but only if $P(A)=1$.
- No, because $P(A\mid B)=1$.
- Yes, since both $P(A\cap B)$ and $P(A)P(B)$ equal zero.

## Explanation

The product definition applies even when probabilities are zero. The intersection is empty and the probability product is zero, so the independence equality holds.
