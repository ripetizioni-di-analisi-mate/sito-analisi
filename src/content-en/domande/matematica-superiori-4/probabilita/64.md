A fair die is rolled three times. What is the probability of obtaining at least one $6$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{125}{216}$
- $\dfrac{91}{216}$
- $\dfrac{25}{72}$

## Explanation

The complement is “no $6$ in the three rolls.” On each roll the probability of a result other than $6$ is $\dfrac{5}{6}$, and the rolls are independent, so $P(\text{no } 6) = \left( \dfrac{5}{6} \right)^3 = \dfrac{125}{216}$. By the complement rule, $P = 1 - \dfrac{125}{216} = \dfrac{91}{216}$. The sum $\dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ counts sequences with two or three occurrences of $6$ more than once, since rolling $6$ on different rolls gives events that can occur together.
