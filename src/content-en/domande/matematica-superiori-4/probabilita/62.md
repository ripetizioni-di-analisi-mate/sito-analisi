Two independent fair coins are tossed. Let $A$ be “first coin heads”, $B$ “second coin heads” and $C$ “the coins agree”. Which statement is true?

- They are pairwise independent but not jointly independent.
- They are jointly independent.
- $A$ and $B$ are disjoint.
- $C$ has probability one.

## Explanation

Each event has probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and each pairwise intersection has probability $\dfrac{1}{4}$. The triple intersection is just heads-heads, with probability $\dfrac{1}{4}$, different from $(\dfrac{1}{2})^3$.
