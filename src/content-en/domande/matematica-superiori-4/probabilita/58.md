For two events $A$ and $B$ with $P(A) > 0$, we know that $P(A \cap B) = P(A)$. Which statement is true?

- $P(A \mid B) = 1$
- $P(B \mid A) = P(B)$
- $P(B \mid A) = 1$
- $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive

## Explanation

By the definition of conditional probability, $P(B \mid A) = \dfrac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)}$, and here the numerator equals the denominator, so $P(B \mid A) = 1$. For example, on a roll of a die with $A = \{ 6 \}$ and $B = \{ 2, 4, 6 \}$, we have $A \cap B = A$, and given that $6$ was rolled, the number is certainly even. In the same example $P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $P(B) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the equalities $P(A \mid B) = 1$ and $P(B \mid A) = P(B)$ are false in general.
