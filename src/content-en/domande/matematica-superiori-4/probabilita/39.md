Let $A$ and $B$ be events with $P(B) > 0$ and $P(A \mid B) = P(A)$. Which statement is true?

- $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive
- $A$ and $B$ are independent
- $A$ is a subset of $B$
- $P(A \cap B) = 0$

## Explanation

By the definition of conditional probability, $P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$. Multiplying both sides by $P(B)$ in the equation $P(A \mid B) = P(A)$ gives $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)$, which is the definition of independent events. For example, on a roll of a die, the events $\{ 2, 4, 6 \}$ and $\{ 1, 2 \}$ are independent and their intersection $\{ 2 \}$ is nonempty.
