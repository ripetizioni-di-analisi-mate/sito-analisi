A batch has $4$ good and $2$ faulty items. For an inspection of two items without replacement, a student estimates the chance of two good items as $(\dfrac{4}{6})^2$. How does this estimate compare with the correct probability?

- It overestimates it by $\dfrac{2}{45}$.
- It underestimates it by $\dfrac{2}{45}$.
- The two probabilities agree.
- It overestimates it by $\dfrac{1}{6}$.

## Explanation

The with-replacement estimate is $\dfrac{4}{9}$. Without replacement, after one good item there are three good items out of five, so the probability is $(\dfrac{4}{6})(\dfrac{3}{5})=\dfrac{2}{5}$. The difference is $\dfrac{4}{9}-\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{2}{45}$.
