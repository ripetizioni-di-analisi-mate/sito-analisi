A disease affects $2\%$ of the population. A test is positive for $90\%$ of people with the disease and $5\%$ of people without it. A randomly selected person tests positive. What is the probability that the person has the disease?

- $\dfrac{9}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{50}$
- $\dfrac{18}{67}$
- $\dfrac{9}{500}$

## Explanation

By the law of total probability, the probability of a positive test is
$$
P(T) = 0.02 \cdot 0.9 + 0.98 \cdot 0.05 = 0.018 + 0.049 = 0.067 .
$$
By Bayes’ theorem,
$$
P(M \mid T) = \frac{0.018}{0.067} = \frac{18}{67} \approx 0.27 .
$$
The probability is low because there are many more people without the disease than with it, and positive tests among people without the disease outnumber positive tests among people with the disease.
