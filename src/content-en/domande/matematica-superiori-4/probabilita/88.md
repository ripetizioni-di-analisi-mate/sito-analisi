In five independent trials with success probability $p=\dfrac{1}{3}$, how do the probabilities of exactly one and exactly two successes compare?

- They are equal.
- The second is twice the first.
- The first is twice the second.
- The second is three times the first.

## Explanation

The ratio of the second probability to the first is $(\binom52/\binom51)\,p/(1-p)$. Substitution gives $(\dfrac{10}{5})(\dfrac{1}{2})=1$, so the probabilities are equal.
