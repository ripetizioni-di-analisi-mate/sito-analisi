- $1$
- $\dfrac{5}{6}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$

## Explanation

There are $6$ equally likely outcomes. The prime numbers from $1$ to $6$ are $2$, $3$, $5$ and the even numbers are $2$, $4$, $6$, so the required event is the union $\{ 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 \}$. By the addition rule,
$$
P = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{3}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6} ,
$$
where $\dfrac{1}{6}$ is the probability of the intersection $\{ 2 \}$. The same result follows by observing that the only unfavorable outcome is $1$.
