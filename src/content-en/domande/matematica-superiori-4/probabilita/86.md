The events $B_1$ and $B_2$ form a partition, with $P(B_1) = 0.3$ and $P(B_2) = 0.7$. An event $A$ satisfies $P(A \mid B_1) = 0.9$ and $P(A \mid B_2) = 0.1$. Which statement is true?

- $P(B_1 \mid A) = 0.3$
- $P(B_1 \mid A)$ is greater than $P(B_1)$
- $P(B_1 \mid A) = 0.9$
- $P(B_1 \mid A)$ is less than $P(B_1)$

## Explanation

By the law of total probability, $P(A) = 0.3 \cdot 0.9 + 0.7 \cdot 0.1 = 0.27 + 0.07 = 0.34$. By Bayes’ theorem,
$$
P(B_1 \mid A) = \frac{0.27}{0.34} = \frac{27}{34} \approx 0.79 ,
$$
which exceeds the prior probability $P(B_1) = 0.3$. Event $A$ is much more likely in case $B_1$ than in case $B_2$, so learning that $A$ occurred increases the probability of $B_1$.
