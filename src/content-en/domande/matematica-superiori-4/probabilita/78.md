An urn contains $3$ red balls and $2$ blue balls. Draw two without replacement. Let $R_2$ be the event “the second ball is red.” Which statement is true?

- $P(R_2) = \dfrac{1}{2}$
- $P(R_2) = \dfrac{3}{10}$
- $P(R_2)$ cannot be calculated without knowing the color of the first ball
- $P(R_2) = \dfrac{3}{5}$

## Explanation

The first ball is either red or blue, and the two cases form a partition. By the law of total probability,
$$
P(R_2) = \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{4} + \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{10} + \frac{3}{10} = \frac{3}{5} .
$$
The result equals the probability that the first ball is red, since without information on the first draw the second position is equivalent to the first.
