In which of the following situations can the Bernoulli model be used to find the probability of $k$ successes?

- Draw $5$ cards without replacement and count the aces
- Draw with replacement from an urn until a red ball appears
- Roll $4$ dice and find the sum of the numbers
- Roll a die $10$ times and count how often $6$ occurs

## Explanation

The Bernoulli model requires a fixed number $n$ of independent trials, each with two possible outcomes and the same probability $p$ of success. In the $10$ rolls of the die, the trials are independent and rolling $6$ counts as a success and always has probability $\dfrac{1}{6}$. In draws without replacement, the probability of an ace changes from draw to draw, so the trials are not independent. When drawing until the first red ball, the number of trials is not fixed, and the sum of the dice is not a count of successes.
