Two cards are drawn one after the other without replacement from a deck of $40$ Neapolitan cards. What is the probability that both are aces?

- $\dfrac{1}{100}$
- $\dfrac{1}{65}$
- $\dfrac{1}{130}$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}$

## Explanation

The first card is an ace with probability $\dfrac{4}{40}$. If the first is an ace, $3$ aces remain among $39$ cards, so the probability that the second is an ace given that the first is an ace equals $\dfrac{3}{39}$. By the multiplication rule,
$$
P = \frac{4}{40} \cdot \frac{3}{39} = \frac{12}{1560} = \frac{1}{130} .
$$
