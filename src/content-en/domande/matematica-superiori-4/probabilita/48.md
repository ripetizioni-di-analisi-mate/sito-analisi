An observation establishes $P(A\mid B)=0.8$, with $P(B)>0$. Which extra quantity by itself is always sufficient to find $P(A\cap B)$?

- $P(B)$
- $P(A)$
- $P(A^c)$
- $P(A\mid B^c)$ when defined

## Explanation

The product rule gives $P(A\cap B)=P(A\mid B)P(B)$. Knowing $P(B)$ converts the proportion within $B$ into a proportion of the whole population.
