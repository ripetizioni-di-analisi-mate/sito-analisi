On a roll of a die, which of the following collections of events forms a partition of the sample space?

- $\{ 1, 2 \}$, $\{ 2, 3 \}$, $\{ 4, 5, 6 \}$
- $\{ 1, 2 \}$, $\{ 4, 5, 6 \}$
- $\{ 1 \}$, $\{ 2, 3 \}$, $\{ 4, 5, 6 \}$
- $\{ 1, 3, 5 \}$, $\{ 2, 4, 6 \}$, $\varnothing$

## Explanation

A partition consists of events with positive probabilities that are pairwise mutually exclusive and whose union equals $\Omega$. The events $\{ 1 \}$, $\{ 2, 3 \}$, and $\{ 4, 5, 6 \}$ satisfy all three conditions. In the collection containing $\{ 1, 2 \}$ and $\{ 2, 3 \}$, outcome $2$ belongs to two events. In the collection $\{ 1, 2 \}$, $\{ 4, 5, 6 \}$, outcome $3$ belongs to none. The empty set has probability $0$, so it cannot be part of a partition.
