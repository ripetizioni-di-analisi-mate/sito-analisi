What is $\sin^2\dfrac{\pi}{6} + \cos^2\dfrac{\pi}{3}$?

- $1$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

The special-angle table gives $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the sum is $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The first Pythagorean identity applies to the sine and cosine of the same angle. Here the angles differ, so the sum differs from $1$.
