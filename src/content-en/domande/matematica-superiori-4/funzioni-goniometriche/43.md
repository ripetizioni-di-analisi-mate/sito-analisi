Given $\tan\alpha = 2$, what is $\sin^2\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$

## Explanation

By the second Pythagorean identity, $\cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{1}{1 + \tan^2\alpha} = \dfrac{1}{5}$. By the first Pythagorean identity, $\sin^2\alpha = 1 - \cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$. The square of the sine is independent of the sign of the sine, so the result holds in whichever quadrant contains $\alpha$.
