What is $\arcsin\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$?

- $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The arcsine of $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is the angle in $\left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$ whose sine is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The special-angle table gives $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ belongs to the interval, so $\arcsin\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ also has sine $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ but is not the arcsine because it exceeds $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
