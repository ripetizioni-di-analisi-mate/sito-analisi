Why does the principal branch of $\arcsin$ include both negative and positive angles?

- Sine on $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ is one-to-one and takes every value from $-1$ to $1$.
- Sine is always positive on that interval.
- Sine has period $\pi$.
- The inverse function must have all real numbers as its range.

## Explanation

An inverse requires a branch on which each output comes from exactly one angle. On this interval sine increases from $-1$ to $1$, covering negative outputs as well.
