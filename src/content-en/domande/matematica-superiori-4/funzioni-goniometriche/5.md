Given that $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{3}{4}$ and $\pi < \alpha < \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, what is $\sin\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{3}{4}$

## Explanation

By the second Pythagorean identity,
$$
\cos^2\alpha = \frac{1}{1 + \tan^2\alpha} = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{9}{16}} = \frac{16}{25} .
$$
In the third quadrant the cosine is negative, so $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{4}{5}$. Since $\sin\alpha = \tan\alpha \cdot \cos\alpha$, we obtain $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{4} \cdot \left( -\dfrac{4}{5} \right) = -\dfrac{3}{5}$, which is negative as expected in the third quadrant.
