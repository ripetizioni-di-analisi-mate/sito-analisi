In a right triangle the hypotenuse has length $10\ \text{cm}$ and one acute angle measures $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. What is the length of the leg opposite this angle?

- $5\sqrt{3}\ \text{cm}$
- $\dfrac{10\sqrt{3}}{3}\ \text{cm}$
- $5\sqrt{2}\ \text{cm}$
- $5\ \text{cm}$

## Explanation

In a right triangle with hypotenuse $1$, the length of the leg opposite an acute angle equals the sine of the angle. Therefore, when the hypotenuse is $10$, the opposite leg is $10$ times the sine. Its length is thus $10 \cdot \sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} = 10 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 5\ \text{cm}$. The number $5\sqrt{3}$ is the length of the adjacent leg, $10\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
