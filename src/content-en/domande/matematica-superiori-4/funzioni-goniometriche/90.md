For the vectors $(x,y)=(1,1)$ and $(x,y)=(-1,-1)$ the expression $\arctan\dfrac{y}{x}$ takes the same value. Why?

- Both have positive components.
- The ratio $\dfrac{y}{x}$ equals $1$ for both, even though the vectors point in opposite directions.
- The two vectors are equal.
- The tangent has period $2\pi$.

## Explanation

For both vectors the ratio of the components is $\dfrac{y}{x}=1$, so the expression equals $\arctan 1=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ in both cases. The arctangent takes only values between $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so it does not distinguish a vector from its opposite. The angle that the vector $(-1,-1)$ makes with the positive $x$-axis is instead $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, and finding it requires the signs of the individual components.
