What shift gives the graph of $y = \cos x$ starting from the graph of $y = \sin x$?

- A shift of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the right
- A shift of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the left
- A shift of $\pi$ to the left
- A shift of $\pi$ to the right

## Explanation

The complementary-angle identities give $\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = \cos x$ for every $x$. The graph of $y = \sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ is the sine graph shifted $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the left, since the value that sine takes at $x_0$ is now attained at $x_0 - \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. For example, the maximum of sine at $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ corresponds to the maximum of cosine at $0$. A shift of $\pi$, in either direction, instead gives the graph of $y = -\sin x$.
