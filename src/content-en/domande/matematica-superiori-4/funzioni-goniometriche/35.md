Given that $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$, what is $\sin\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$
- $-\dfrac{8}{9}$

## Explanation

By the first Pythagorean identity, $\sin^2\alpha = 1 - \dfrac{1}{9} = \dfrac{8}{9}$. The angle lies in the fourth quadrant, where sine is negative, so
$$
\sin\alpha = -\sqrt{\frac{8}{9}} = -\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} .
$$
The number $-\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$ results from subtracting from $1$ the cosine itself instead of its square.
