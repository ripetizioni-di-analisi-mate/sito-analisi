What is $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} \cdot \cos\dfrac{\pi}{6}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## Explanation

The special-angle table gives $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. These values coincide because the angles are complementary. Therefore the product is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{3}{4}$. The number $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$ results from mistakenly using $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
