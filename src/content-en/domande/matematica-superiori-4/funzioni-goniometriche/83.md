What is $\arccos\left( -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$?

- $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$

## Explanation

The arccosine is the angle in $[0, \pi]$ with the given cosine. By the supplementary-angle identity, $\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = -\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ belongs to $[0, \pi]$. Therefore $\arccos\left( -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, in agreement with the relation $\arccos(-x) = \pi - \arccos x$. The angle $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ also has cosine $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ but is not the arccosine because it exceeds $\pi$.
