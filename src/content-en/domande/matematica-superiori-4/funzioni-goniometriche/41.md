Given $\tan\alpha = 3$, what is $\dfrac{\sin\alpha + \cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha - \cos\alpha}$?

- $2$
- $-2$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $4$

## Explanation

Since $\tan\alpha = 3$, the cosine differs from $0$, so we can divide the numerator and denominator by $\cos\alpha$. This gives
$$
\frac{\tan\alpha + 1}{\tan\alpha - 1} = \frac{3 + 1}{3 - 1} = 2 .
$$
The result does not depend on the quadrant of $\alpha$ because the expression depends only on the tangent.
