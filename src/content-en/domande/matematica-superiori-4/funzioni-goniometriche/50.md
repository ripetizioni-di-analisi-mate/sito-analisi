What is $\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$?

- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

## Explanation

The angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ is supplementary to $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, that is, $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. By the supplementary-angle identity, $\cos(\pi - \alpha) = -\cos\alpha$, so $\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = -\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The negative sign agrees with the second quadrant, where cosine is negative.
