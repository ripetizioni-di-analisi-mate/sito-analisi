What is $\dfrac{\sin\frac{\pi}{4}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{3}}$?

- $\sqrt{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

## Explanation

The special-angle table gives $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Dividing by $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $2$, so the quotient equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot 2 = \sqrt{2}$. The number $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$ results from using $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ in place of $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
