A mass oscillates according to $x(t) = 6\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t \right)$, with $x$ in centimeters and $t$ in seconds. What is its position at time $t = \dfrac{4}{3}\ \text{s}$?

- $3\sqrt{3}\ \text{cm}$
- $3\ \text{cm}$
- $-3\ \text{cm}$
- $6\ \text{cm}$

## Explanation

Substitute the time into the equation of motion. The argument of the cosine is $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \cdot \dfrac{4}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so
$$
x\left( \frac{4}{3} \right) = 6\cos\frac{\pi}{3} = 6 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 3\ \text{cm} .
$$
The number $3\sqrt{3}$ results from using sine instead of cosine.
