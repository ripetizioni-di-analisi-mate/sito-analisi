What are the zeros of the function $y = \cos x$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$?

- $0$, $\pi$, and $2\pi$
- Only $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- $\pi$ and $2\pi$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$

## Explanation

The zeros of cosine occur at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, where the corresponding point on the unit circle lies on the $y$-axis. For $k = 0$ and $k = 1$ we obtain $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, which belong to the interval. For $k = -1$ and $k = 2$ we obtain $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{2}$, which lie outside it. The points $0$, $\pi$, and $2\pi$ are the zeros of sine instead.
