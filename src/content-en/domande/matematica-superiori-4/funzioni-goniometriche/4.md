Given that $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \alpha < \pi$, what is $\tan\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$

## Explanation

By the first Pythagorean identity, $\cos^2\alpha = 1 - \dfrac{4}{9} = \dfrac{5}{9}$. The angle lies in the second quadrant, where cosine is negative, so $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$. Therefore
$$
\tan\alpha = \frac{\frac{2}{3}}{-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} = -\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5} .
$$
The negative sign is the sign expected for the tangent in the second quadrant.
