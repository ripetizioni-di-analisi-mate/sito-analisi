Which angle $\alpha$, with $0 \leq \alpha < 2\pi$, satisfies both $\sin\alpha = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\cos\alpha < 0$?

- $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
- $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$

## Explanation

The sine and cosine are both negative, so the point corresponding to $\alpha$ lies in the third quadrant. The first-quadrant angle with sine $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, and the identity for angles that differ by $\pi$ gives $\sin\left( \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore $\alpha = \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$. The angle $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$ also has sine $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, and its cosine is positive because the angle lies in the fourth quadrant.
