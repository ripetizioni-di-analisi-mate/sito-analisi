Given that $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, what is $\cos\alpha$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$
- $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$

## Explanation

By the second Pythagorean identity,
$$
\cos^2\alpha = \frac{1}{1 + \tan^2\alpha} = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{4}} = \frac{4}{5} .
$$
The cosine is positive in the first quadrant, so $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}$. The number $\dfrac{4}{5}$ is the square of the cosine, and $\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$ is the sine of $\alpha$.
