What shift gives the graph of $y = \sin\left( 2x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$ starting from the graph of $y = \sin(2x)$?

- A shift of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to the left
- A shift of $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to the left
- A shift of $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to the right
- A shift of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to the right

## Explanation

Factor the coefficient of $x$ out of the argument, $2x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} = 2\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$. Therefore $\sin\left( 2x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = g\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$, with $g(x) = \sin(2x)$. The phase shift is $-\dfrac{\varphi}{\omega} = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, so the graph is that of $y = \sin(2x)$ shifted $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to the left.
