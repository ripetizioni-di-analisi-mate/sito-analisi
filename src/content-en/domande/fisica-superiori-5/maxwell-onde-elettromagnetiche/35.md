A diagram of a charged capacitor marks its positive and negative plates. A resistor connects them and the charge is decreasing. Which arrow should represent the displacement current in the gap?

- Parallel to the plates
- From the positive plate to the negative plate, along the electric field
- It has no direction, because the displacement current is zero during the discharge
- From the negative plate to the positive plate, opposite to the electric field

## Explanation

Between the plates the electric field points from the positive plate to the negative plate and has magnitude $E = \dfrac{q}{\varepsilon_0 A}$.
During the discharge the charge $q$ decreases, so the flux $\Phi_E$ through a surface oriented along the field also decreases.
Therefore $i_s = \varepsilon_0 \dfrac{d\Phi_E}{dt}$ is negative with respect to that orientation, and the displacement current points **from the negative plate to the positive plate**.
This direction is consistent with the circuit: in the resistor the current flows from the positive plate to the negative plate, and between the plates the displacement current closes the path.
