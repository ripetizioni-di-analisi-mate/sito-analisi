A very long solenoid of radius $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ contains a uniform magnetic field that increases at a constant rate $\dfrac{dB}{dt} = 1.2\,\mathrm{T/s}$. The magnetic field outside is zero. Calculate the magnitude of the induced electric field at a distance $r = 8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis.

- $6.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $4.8\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $1.2\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $3.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

The circulation is taken around a circle of radius $r$, but only the disk of radius $a$ contributes magnetic flux. Symmetry gives $E(2\pi r)=\pi a^2\left|\dfrac{dB}{dt}\right|$. Substituting both lengths in metres gives the stated value.
