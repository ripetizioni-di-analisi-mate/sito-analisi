An off-axis magnetic probe sits between the circular plates of a capacitor in which $E(t)=E_0\sin(\omega t)$ is uniform and normal to the plates. At what points in the electric cycle does it record peaks of $\lvert B\rvert$?

- At the instants when the electric field equals $\dfrac{E_0}{2}$
- At the instants when the electric field is zero
- At every instant, because the magnetic field is constant
- At the instants when the electric field has maximum magnitude

## Explanation

By the Ampère-Maxwell law, at the distance $r$ from the axis $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0\, r}{2}\,\left|\dfrac{dE}{dt}\right|$, so the magnetic field depends on the rate of change of $E$, not on its value.
Since $\dfrac{dE}{dt} = \omega E_0 \cos(\omega t)$, the magnitude of $B$ is largest when $|\cos(\omega t)| = 1$.
At those instants $\sin(\omega t) = 0$, therefore the magnetic field is largest **when the electric field is zero**.
Hence the two fields oscillate a quarter of a period out of phase.
