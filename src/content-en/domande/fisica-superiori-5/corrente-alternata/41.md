At the source frequency a series $RLC$ circuit has $R = 24\,\Omega$, $X_L = 50\,\Omega$ and $X_C = 40\,\Omega$, with the inductor and the capacitor connected one after the other. The source has rms voltage $52\,\mathrm{V}$. What rms voltage does a voltmeter read when connected across the section that contains only the inductor and the capacitor?

- $52\,\mathrm{V}$
- $180\,\mathrm{V}$
- $48\,\mathrm{V}$
- $20\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The impedance of the circuit is $Z = \sqrt{24^2 + (50 - 40)^2}\,\Omega = 26\,\Omega$, so the rms current is $I_{\text{eff}} = \dfrac{52}{26}\,\mathrm{A} = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$.

The voltages across the inductor and the capacitor are in antiphase, so the voltage across the pair has rms value $(X_L - X_C)\,I_{\text{eff}}$. Therefore it equals $10 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{V} = 20\,\mathrm{V}$, while the rms voltages across the two components are $100\,\mathrm{V}$ and $80\,\mathrm{V}$.
