The voltage across a source is $v(t) = 170\sin(120\pi\,t)\,\mathrm{V}$, with $t$ in seconds. What is the voltage at time $t = \dfrac{1}{360}\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $147\,\mathrm{V}$
- $170\,\mathrm{V}$
- $85\,\mathrm{V}$
- $120\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

At time $t = \dfrac{1}{360}\,\mathrm{s}$ the argument of the sine is $120\pi \cdot \dfrac{1}{360} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

Since $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, the voltage is $v = 170 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 147\,\mathrm{V}$. The value $\dfrac{170}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 120\,\mathrm{V}$ is the rms value, which does not depend on the instant.
