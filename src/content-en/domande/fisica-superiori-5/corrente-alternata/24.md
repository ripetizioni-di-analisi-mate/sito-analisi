In an ideal $LC$ circuit with natural angular frequency $\omega_0 = 1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, at time $t = 0$ the capacitor has charge $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ and the circuit carries the current $4.0\,\mathrm{mA}$. What is the maximum value of the charge of the capacitor during the oscillations?

- $7.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $4.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $5.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

The charge is maximum when the current is zero, and at that instant all the energy is in the capacitor. By conservation of energy
$$
\frac{Q_{\max}^2}{2C} = \frac{q_0^2}{2C} + \frac{1}{2} L i_0^2 .
$$
Multiplying by $2C$ and using $LC = \dfrac{1}{\omega_0^2}$ gives $Q_{\max}^2 = q_0^2 + \dfrac{i_0^2}{\omega_0^2}$. Since $\dfrac{i_0}{\omega_0} = \dfrac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{C} = 4.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$, we get $Q_{\max} = \sqrt{3.0^2 + 4.0^2}\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 5.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$.
