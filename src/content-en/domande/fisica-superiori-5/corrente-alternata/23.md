In an ideal $LC$ circuit the charge of the capacitor oscillates with frequency $f_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kHz}$. With what frequency does the energy stored in the capacitor oscillate?

- It does not oscillate, because energy is conserved
- $1.0\,\mathrm{kHz}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{kHz}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{kHz}$

## Explanation

The charge is $q(t) = Q_0 \cos(\omega_0 t)$, so the energy of the capacitor is
$$
U_C = \frac{q^2}{2C} = \frac{Q_0^2}{2C}\cos^2(\omega_0 t) = \frac{Q_0^2}{4C}\left( 1 + \cos(2\omega_0 t) \right) .
$$
The energy of the capacitor therefore oscillates with angular frequency $2\omega_0$, that is, with frequency $2f_0 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kHz}$. The sum of the energies of the capacitor and of the inductor is conserved, while each of the two varies in time.
