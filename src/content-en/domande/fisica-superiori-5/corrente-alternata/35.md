The figure shows the voltage $v(t)$ across an ideal component and the current $i(t)$ through it, in arbitrary units. Which component is it?

::grafico{f="1.5*sin(x);-cos(x)" xmin=-0.5 xmax=13 ymin=-1.9 ymax=2.1 nomi="ωt," legenda="v(t);i(t)" titolo="Voltage $v(t)$ and current $i(t)$ as functions of $\omega t$"}

- A resistor and a capacitor in series
- An inductor
- A capacitor
- A resistor

## Explanation

The voltage reaches its maximum at $\omega t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the current at $\omega t = \pi$, that is, a quarter of a period later. The current therefore lags the voltage by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

A phase difference of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ with the current lagging is that of an ideal **inductor**. In a capacitor the current leads by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, in a resistor it is in phase, and in a resistor in series with a capacitor it leads by an angle less than $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
