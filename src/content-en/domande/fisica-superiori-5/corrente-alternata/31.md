The voltage across an ideal inductor of inductance $L = 0.25\,\mathrm{H}$ is $v(t) = 100\sin(200\,t)\,\mathrm{V}$, with $t$ in seconds. What is the sinusoidal steady-state current if its DC component is zero?

- $i(t) = 0.32\sin\left(200\,t - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{A}$
- $i(t) = 2.0\sin\left(200\,t + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{A}$
- $i(t) = 2.0\sin\left(200\,t - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{A}$
- $i(t) = 2.0\sin(200\,t)\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

The coefficient of $t$ in the expression of $v(t)$ is the angular frequency, $\omega = 200\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, so the inductive reactance is $X_L = \omega L = 50\,\Omega$.

The amplitude of the current is $I_0 = \dfrac{V_0}{X_L} = \dfrac{100}{50}\,\mathrm{A} = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. In an ideal inductor the current lags the voltage by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, therefore $i(t) = 2.0\sin\left(200\,t - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{A}$.
