Immediately after a series RLC circuit is closed its current is still zero. The $2.0\,\mu\mathrm F$ capacitor was charged to $20\,\mathrm V$, the inductance is $0.50\,\mathrm H$, and the resistance is $100\,\Omega$. What is the magnitude of the initial slope of the current graph?

- $0.20\,\mathrm{A/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{A/s}$
- $40\,\mathrm{A/s}$
- $0\,\mathrm{A/s}$

## Explanation

By the loop rule, at every instant $L\,\dfrac{di}{dt} + R\,i + \dfrac{q}{C} = 0$.

At the instant $t = 0$ the current is zero, so the potential drop across the resistor is zero and the capacitor voltage, $20\,\mathrm{V}$, lies entirely across the inductor. Therefore
$$
\left| \frac{di}{dt} \right| = \frac{20}{0.50}\,\mathrm{A/s} = 40\,\mathrm{A/s} .
$$
At that instant the current is zero while its derivative is nonzero, and the result depends neither on the resistance nor on the capacitance.
