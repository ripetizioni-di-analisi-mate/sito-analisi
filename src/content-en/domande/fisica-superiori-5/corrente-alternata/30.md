A circuit is supplied by the voltage $v(t) = V_0\sin\left(\omega t + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$ and carries the current $i(t) = I_0\cos\left(\omega t - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$. What is the power factor, and does the current lead or lag the voltage?

- $\cos\varphi = 0.50$, current leading
- $\cos\varphi = 0.50$, current lagging
- $\cos\varphi \approx 0.87$, current lagging
- $\cos\varphi \approx 0.87$, current leading

## Explanation

To compare the phases, the two quantities are written with the same function. Since $\cos\alpha = \sin\left(\alpha + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, the current is $i(t) = I_0\sin\left(\omega t + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$.

The phase of the current exceeds that of the voltage by $\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, so the current **leads** by $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The power factor is therefore $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.87$.
