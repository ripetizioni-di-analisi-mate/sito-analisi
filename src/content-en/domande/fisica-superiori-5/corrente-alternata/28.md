The periodic voltage in the figure, with period $2\,\mathrm{ms}$, equals $12\,\mathrm{V}$ during half of each period and $0$ during the other half. What is its rms value?

::grafico{linee="0,12 1,12 1,0 2,0 2,12 3,12 3,0 4,0" xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.6 ymin=-2 ymax=14.5 nomi="t,v" legenda=0 titolo="Voltage $v(t)$, in volts, as a function of time $t$, in milliseconds"}

- $12\,\mathrm{V}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $4.2\,\mathrm{V}$
- $8.5\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The rms value is the square root of the mean value of $v^2$ over one period.

The square of the voltage equals $144\,\mathrm{V^2}$ for half a period and $0$ for the other half, so its mean value is $72\,\mathrm{V^2}$. Therefore
$$
V_{\text{eff}} = \sqrt{72}\,\mathrm{V} = 6\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 8.5\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The mean value of the voltage, $6.0\,\mathrm{V}$, differs from the rms value, because the rms value is computed from the mean of the square.
