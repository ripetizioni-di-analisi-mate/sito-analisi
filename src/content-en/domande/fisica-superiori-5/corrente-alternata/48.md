A motor connected to the grid, with rms voltage $230\,\mathrm{V}$ and frequency $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$, absorbs the average power $2.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ with power factor $0.80$ and is an inductive load. What capacitance must be connected in parallel with the motor to bring the power factor of the whole to $1$?

- $90\,\mu\mathrm{F}$
- $72\,\mu\mathrm{F}$
- $150\,\mu\mathrm{F}$
- $120\,\mu\mathrm{F}$

## Explanation

The rms current of the motor is $I = \dfrac{P}{V_{\text{eff}}\cos\varphi} = \dfrac{2000}{230 \cdot 0.80}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 10.9\,\mathrm{A}$, lagging the voltage by $\varphi$. Since $\sin\varphi = 0.60$, its component lagging by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is $I\sin\varphi \approx 6.5\,\mathrm{A}$.

The capacitor carries a current leading by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, with rms value $\omega C V_{\text{eff}}$, and the power factor of the whole equals $1$ when this current compensates the lagging component. Therefore
$$
C = \frac{I\sin\varphi}{\omega V_{\text{eff}}} = \frac{6.5}{100\pi \cdot 230}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 90\,\mu\mathrm{F} .
$$
