Nearly all atoms in a hydrogen sample are in the ground state. Why can the $2\to3$ Balmer absorption line be very weak even when the lamp emits at the required frequency?

- The photon must already occupy level $n=3$
- Few atoms occupy the initial level $n=2$
- Visible photons have zero charge
- The final level must already be populated in every atom

## Explanation

To absorb a photon on the $2\to3$ line an atom must start at $n=2$. If few atoms occupy that state, only a small fraction of the light is absorbed.
