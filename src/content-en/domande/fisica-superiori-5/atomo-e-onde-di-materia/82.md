A detector divides a box into bins much wider than the spacing between neighbouring maxima of $|\psi_n|^2$, with $n$ very large. Why can the data look like those for a classical particle travelling at constant speed?

- All nodes move to the walls
- Each bin averages over many density oscillations
- Energy becomes exactly continuous at any finite $n$
- The state automatically falls to $n=1$

## Explanation

A wide bin contains many nearby maxima and minima. The density averaged over many periods is nearly uniform, as in the classical distribution along a path traversed at constant speed.
