A ball and an electron have the same position spread. For the same lower bound on $\Delta p_x$, why is the bound on $\Delta v_x$ practically imperceptible for the ball?

- Position and velocity have the same units for a ball
- The velocity spread is obtained by dividing by a much larger mass
- Balls have zero momentum by definition
- Planck's constant depends on mass

## Explanation

In the nonrelativistic regime $p_x=mv_x$ gives $\Delta v_x=\dfrac{\Delta p_x}{m}$. The same momentum bound corresponds to a much smaller minimum velocity spread when $m$ is large.
