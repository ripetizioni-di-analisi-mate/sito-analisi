A particle in a box is in the level $n=2$, for which the centre of the box is a node of the wave function. What is the probability of finding the particle in a small interval of non-zero width centred on the centre of the box?

- It equals $1$, because the interval is at the centre of the box
- It is zero for any width of the interval
- It is negative, because the interval contains a node
- It is positive, because the interval contains points where $|\psi|^2>0$

## Explanation

In the level $n=2$ the wave function is $\psi_2(x)=A\sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi x}{L}\right)$, which vanishes at $x=\dfrac{L}{2}$. The density $|\psi_2|^2$ is therefore zero at the centre of the box and positive at the other points of the interval. The probability of finding the particle in the interval is the sum of the contributions $|\psi|^2\,\Delta x$ of its points, so it is **positive**. A probability is never negative, and it equals $1$ only for the whole box.
