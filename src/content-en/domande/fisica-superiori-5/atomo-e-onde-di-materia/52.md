An interferometer splits a coherent particle beam into two paths with equal amplitudes. At recombination one path is longer by $\dfrac{3\lambda}{2}$, with no other phase shifts. What happens at that point?

- A maximum because the amplitudes are equal
- A maximum because the difference exceeds one wavelength
- A minimum from destructive interference
- A doubling of frequency

## Explanation

The phase difference is $2\pi\cdot\dfrac{3\lambda}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\lambda}=3\pi$, equivalent to $\pi$. Equal amplitudes add out of phase and cancel.
