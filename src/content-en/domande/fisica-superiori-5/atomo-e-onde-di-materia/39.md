A model atom has levels at $-8$, $-5$, and $-2\,\mathrm{eV}$. All transitions between them are allowed in the gas. Why are there only two distinct spectral frequencies?

- Two distinct transitions have the same $3\,\mathrm{eV}$ gap
- A positive level is required for emission
- The lowest level gives zero frequency for every transition
- The longest direct transition always emits two photons

## Explanation

The two adjacent gaps are both $3\,\mathrm{eV}$, while the gap between the outer levels is $6\,\mathrm{eV}$. The two $3\,\mathrm{eV}$ transitions coincide at the same frequency.
