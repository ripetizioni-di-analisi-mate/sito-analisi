An electron enters a region where its potential energy increases but remains below its total energy. In the nonrelativistic regime, how does its local wavelength change?

- It is fixed by mass alone
- It becomes zero as soon as the potential changes
- It decreases because the kinetic energy decreases
- It increases because the kinetic energy decreases

## Explanation

At constant total energy $K=E-U$ decreases. Thus $p=\sqrt{2mK}$ decreases and $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}$ increases.
