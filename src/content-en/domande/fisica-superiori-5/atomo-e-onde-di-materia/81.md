The wave function $\psi$ of a particle satisfies $\int|\psi|^2\,dx=9$, so it is not normalised. By what positive factor must $\psi$ be multiplied to obtain a normalised wave function?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $9$
- $3$
- $\dfrac{1}{9}$

## Explanation

A wave function is normalised when the integral of $|\psi|^2$ over the whole axis equals $1$. If $\psi$ is multiplied by a constant $c>0$, the integral of the squared magnitude becomes $9c^2$. Setting $9c^2=1$ gives $c^2=\dfrac{1}{9}$, so the positive factor is $c=\dfrac{1}{3}$.
