A hydrogen atom goes from $n=2$ to $n=6$ by interacting with a single photon. What happens to the photon?

- It changes direction without changing energy, and the atom remains at the initial level.
- It is absorbed.
- It is emitted.
- The transition always produces ionization.

## Explanation

The higher quantum number corresponds to the higher energy. The photon must supply the energy difference, so it is absorbed.
