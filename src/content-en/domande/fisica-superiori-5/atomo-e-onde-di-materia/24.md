In a model atom the only accessible excited level below the ionization threshold is $5\,\mathrm{eV}$ above the ground state. The threshold is $9\,\mathrm{eV}$. Neglecting line widths and collisions, what happens to a $6\,\mathrm{eV}$ photon incident on a ground-state atom?

- It is fully absorbed in the $5\,\mathrm{eV}$ transition
- It ionizes the atom and releases $1\,\mathrm{eV}$
- It cannot be absorbed to excite or ionize the atom
- It excites the atom and remains as a $1\,\mathrm{eV}$ photon in the absorption event

## Explanation

Absorption of a single photon transfers its full energy. Here $6\,\mathrm{eV}$ does not match the bound-state gap and is below the continuum threshold.
