The position of an electron along the $x$-axis is known with an uncertainty $\Delta x=1.0\,\mathrm{nm}$. With $h=6.63\cdot10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$ and $m_e=9.11\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, what is the minimum uncertainty in the component $v_x$ of its velocity?

- $1.2\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $3.6\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.8\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5.8\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

By the **uncertainty principle**, $\Delta x\cdot\Delta p_x\geq\dfrac{h}{4\pi}$. Since $\Delta p_x=m_e\,\Delta v_x$, it follows that $\Delta v_x\geq\dfrac{h}{4\pi\,m_e\,\Delta x}$. Substituting the data gives $\Delta v_x\geq\dfrac{6.63\cdot10^{-34}}{4\pi\cdot9.11\cdot10^{-31}\cdot1.0\cdot10^{-9}}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx5.8\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Therefore the minimum uncertainty in the component $v_x$ of the velocity is **$5.8\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{m/s}$**.
