A particle with rest energy $8\,\mathrm{MeV}$ is proposed to decay in vacuum into two stable particles with rest energies $5$ and $4\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Could it do so if it moves very fast in the lab?

- Yes, a lab energy exceeding $9\,\mathrm{MeV}$ suffices.
- Yes, because masses can be added as vectors.
- Yes, if the products fly in opposite directions.
- No, at least $1\,\mathrm{MeV}$ is missing in the parent frame.

## Explanation

An allowed decay must also work in the original particle's rest frame. The minimum product energy there is $9\,\mathrm{MeV}$, exceeding the available energy.
