A photon with energy $7$ MeV is alone in a vacuum. Why can it not produce a pair of massive particles on its own?

- The final total charge would have to be positive.
- A photon in a vacuum always has an energy below one electronvolt.
- The energy–momentum invariant would change.
- The momentum of the photon is zero.

## Explanation

For the initial photon, $E^2-(pc)^2=0$. For the two-particle system this invariant is positive, so energy and momentum cannot both be conserved.
