A particle of mass $m$ has total energy $E$ and momentum of magnitude $p$. Which expression equals its kinetic energy $K=E-mc^2$?

- $K=\dfrac{p^2c^2}{E-mc^2}$
- $K=\dfrac{p^2c^2}{E+mc^2}$
- $K=E^2-m^2c^4$
- $K=\dfrac{E+mc^2}{p^2c^2}$

## Explanation

From the relation $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$ we get $E^2-m^2c^4=p^2c^2$, that is, $(E-mc^2)(E+mc^2)=p^2c^2$. Since $E+mc^2$ is positive, both sides can be divided by $E+mc^2$, which gives $K=E-mc^2=\dfrac{p^2c^2}{E+mc^2}$. The expression $E^2-m^2c^4$ equals $p^2c^2$, so it is the square of an energy and not the kinetic energy. For $v\ll c$ we have $E+mc^2\approx 2mc^2$, so this form gives $K\approx\dfrac{p^2}{2m}$, which is the classical kinetic energy.
