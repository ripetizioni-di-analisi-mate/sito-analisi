A free atom at rest emits just one photon. Why is the photon energy slightly less than the difference between the initial and final atomic rest energies?

- The emitted photon is stationary relative to the atom.
- Part of the difference supplies the atom's recoil kinetic energy.
- The photon has the atom's rest mass.
- Energy is conserved only on average in a decay.

## Explanation

Initial momentum is zero. The photon carries momentum, so the atom acquires the opposite momentum and its recoil energy enters the energy balance.
