A stationary $\Lambda$ with rest energy $1115.7\,\mathrm{MeV}$ decays into a proton ($938.3\,\mathrm{MeV}$ at rest) and a pion ($139.6\,\mathrm{MeV}$ at rest). Their final momentum magnitudes are equal. Which kinetic-energy pair, in the order proton then pion, is consistent with both conservation laws?

- $5.4\,\mathrm{MeV},\ 37.8\,\mathrm{MeV}$
- $32.4\,\mathrm{MeV},\ 5.4\,\mathrm{MeV}$
- $5.4\,\mathrm{MeV},\ 32.4\,\mathrm{MeV}$
- $18.9\,\mathrm{MeV},\ 18.9\,\mathrm{MeV}$

## Explanation

The kinetic energies sum to $1115.7-938.3-139.6=37.8\,\mathrm{MeV}$. At the same $p$, kinetic energy $K=\sqrt{p^2c^2+m^2c^4}-mc^2$ is greater for the lighter particle. Only one pair with the required sum assigns the larger share to the pion.
