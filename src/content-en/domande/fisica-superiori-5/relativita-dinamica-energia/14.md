A clock at an altitude of $1000\,\mathrm m$ gains on one at sea level. In the weak-field limit the fractional rate difference is $\dfrac{gh}{c^2}$. How large is the gain in $3.15\cdot10^7\,\mathrm s$ with $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$?

- $1.1\cdot10^{-13}\,\mathrm s$
- $3.4\,\mu\mathrm s$
- $0.11\,\mu\mathrm s$
- $3.4\,\mathrm{ms}$

## Explanation

The fractional rate difference is about $1.09\cdot10^{-13}$. Multiplying that number by the duration of the year yields a time gain of about $3.4\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$.
