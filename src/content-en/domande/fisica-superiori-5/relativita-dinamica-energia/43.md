A beam hits a stationary target. In another setup two identical beams collide head-on with equal and opposite momenta. Why can a larger fraction of the total energy become new rest mass in the second setup?

- The beam kinetic energies cancel.
- The beams have zero rest mass.
- The lab is the centre-of-momentum frame and no overall kinetic energy must be retained.
- Momentum need not be conserved in the second setup.

## Explanation

With a fixed target the final system must retain the initial momentum. In the symmetric case that momentum is zero and all lab energy is centre-of-momentum energy.
