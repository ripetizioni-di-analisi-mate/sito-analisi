Separating a nucleus into free nucleons at rest relative to each other far apart requires $24\,\mathrm{MeV}$. How does the nucleus mass compare with the sum of the nucleon masses?

- The nucleus exceeds the sum by $24\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$.
- The masses are equal.
- The sum minus the mass of the nucleus is $24\,\mathrm{MeV}/c$.
- The sum minus the mass of the nucleus is $24\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$.

## Explanation

External energy increases the system's final rest energy. The bound state therefore has a mass smaller by $\dfrac{B}{c^2}$.
