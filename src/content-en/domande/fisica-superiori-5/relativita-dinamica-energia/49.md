A photon produces an electron–positron pair near a nucleus. Why can the nucleus enable the process even though it remains the same species?

- It accelerates the photon beyond $c$.
- It can take up recoil momentum.
- It must supply the pair's net charge.
- It makes both lepton energies zero.

## Explanation

The nucleus adds a body to the energy and momentum balance. Its large mass lets it take recoil with relatively little kinetic energy.
