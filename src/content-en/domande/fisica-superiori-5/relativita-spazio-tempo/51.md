One book uses $s^2=c^2\Delta t^2-\Delta x^2$ and another uses the opposite sign. If the separation is timelike, which statement holds in both conventions?

- A frame exists in which the events occur at the same place.
- The events can be simultaneous.
- No subluminal signal can connect them.
- The quantity written as $s^2$ is always positive.

## Explanation

A timelike separation satisfies $\lvert\Delta x\rvert<c\lvert\Delta t\rvert$. A boost with $v=\dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ puts both events at the same position, irrespective of the interval-sign convention.
