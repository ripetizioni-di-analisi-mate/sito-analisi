An observer describes motion by $x'=x-ut$ and $t'=t$. Which measurement of a light pulse travelling along $+x$ shows the limitation of these transformations when $u$ is appreciable?

- Light always stops in the new frame.
- The new frame still measures $c$, whereas the transformation gives $c-u$.
- The arrival time is infinite.
- The frames always assign opposite directions to the light.

## Explanation

Differentiating the Galilean rule gives $\dfrac{dx'}{dt}=c-u$. The light-speed postulate requires the moving inertial observer to measure $c$ as well.
