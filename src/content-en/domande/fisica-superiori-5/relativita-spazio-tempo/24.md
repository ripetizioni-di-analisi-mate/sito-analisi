An interferometer in vacuum splits light into two equal perpendicular arms. In special relativity, should slowly rotating it through $90^\circ$ in an ideal inertial lab change the difference in round-trip times after it settles?

- Yes, the east arm always measures $c+v$.
- Yes, one time must halve.
- Yes, because frequency depends on absolute orientation.
- No, each arm takes $\dfrac{2L}{c}$ regardless of orientation.

## Explanation

In the apparatus frame the arms have equal lengths and light in vacuum is isotropic. Rotating the whole apparatus leaves the two round-trip times equal.
