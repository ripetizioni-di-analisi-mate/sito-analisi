A satellite carries a spectral-line source. In the detector frame the observed light travels exactly perpendicular to the source velocity. The observed frequency is $f=\dfrac{f_0}{\gamma}$. Why is there still a shift at zero radial velocity?

- Light travels through vacuum at $\dfrac{c}{\gamma}$.
- The rate of the source clock is time-dilated in the detector frame.
- The distance is zero.
- The atom's rest wavelength contracts in every direction.

## Explanation

For transverse emission in the stated frame there is no first-order radial Doppler term. The moving source still contributes the time factor $\dfrac{1}{\gamma}$.
