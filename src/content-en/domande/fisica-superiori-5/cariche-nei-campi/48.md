A proton starts from rest in a uniform field $E = 5.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and travels $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ in the direction of the field. What is its kinetic energy at the end of the stretch?

- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $3.2 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $8.0 \cdot 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The electric force $e\vec{E}$ is constant and parallel to the displacement, so its work, denoted by $W$ because the letter $L$ is reserved for inductance, is $W = eE\,\Delta x$.

By the work-energy theorem, starting from rest, $K = W = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 0.020\,\mathrm{J} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{J}$.

The result does not depend on the mass: an electron traveling the same stretch opposite to the field acquires the same kinetic energy.
