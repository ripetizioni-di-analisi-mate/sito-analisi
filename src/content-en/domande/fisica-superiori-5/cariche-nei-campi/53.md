A proton and an electron enter from the same direction, one at a time, into a region of length $\ell$ with a uniform transverse electric field. They have the same initial nonrelativistic kinetic energy and both reach the exit. How do their sideways displacements at the exit compare?

- The electron is deflected about $1800$ times more than the proton, in the opposite sense
- The deflections are equal in magnitude and in the same sense
- The deflections have the same magnitude and opposite senses
- The proton is deflected about $1800$ times more than the electron, in the opposite sense

## Explanation

The deflection at the exit is $y_u = \dfrac{|q|\,E\,\ell^2}{2mv_0^2}$. Since $mv_0^2 = 2K$, it can be written as $y_u = \dfrac{|q|\,E\,\ell^2}{4K}$, which does not contain the mass.

The proton and the electron have charges of equal magnitude $e$ and the same kinetic energy $K$, so the deflections have the **same magnitude**.

The charges have opposite signs, therefore the forces $q\vec{E}$ have opposite senses and the two particles are deflected in opposite senses.
