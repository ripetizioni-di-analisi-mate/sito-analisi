An electron starts from rest and moves through a potential difference $\Delta V = 250\,\mathrm{V}$. Calculate its final speed. ($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

- $3.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.1\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9.4\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.3\cdot10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The electron gains kinetic energy $e\Delta V$. The classical expression is appropriate because the result is about three percent of the speed of light. $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 e\,\Delta V}{m_e}} \approx 9.4 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
