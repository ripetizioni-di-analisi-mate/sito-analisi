An electron enters a parallel-plate capacitor exactly halfway between the plates, which are $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart, with an initial velocity parallel to the plates. If the plates did not stop it, the electron would leave the capacitor deflected by $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ from its initial direction. What happens to the electron?

- It returns to its initial direction before leaving the capacitor
- It hits one of the plates before leaving the capacitor
- It leaves the capacitor deflected by $5\,\mathrm{mm}$
- It stops halfway between the plates

## Explanation

The electron starts $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ from each plate. Inside the capacitor the electric force is constant and perpendicular to the plates, so the deflection increases continuously during the crossing. Since the deflection at the exit would be $5\,\mathrm{mm}$, it reaches $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ before the exit. Therefore the electron **hits the positive plate** before leaving the capacitor.
