A charged particle moves in a helix in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ directed along the $z$ axis. A uniform electric field parallel to $\vec{B}$ is switched on, which accelerates the particle in the direction in which it advances along the axis of the helix. The weight is negligible. What happens to the helix in the following turns?

- The radius and the pitch stay constant
- The radius stays constant and the pitch increases
- The period decreases and the radius stays constant
- The radius increases and the pitch stays constant

## Explanation

The electric force is parallel to the $z$ axis, so it changes only the component $v_z$ of the velocity, which increases.

The magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity and does no work, and the electric force has no components in the $xy$ plane; therefore the magnitude $v_\perp$ of the velocity perpendicular to the field stays constant. The radius $r = \dfrac{m v_\perp}{|q|\,B}$ and the period $T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{|q|\,B}$ therefore stay constant.

The pitch is the displacement along the axis in one period, and in each turn the particle advances with a larger $v_z$ than in the previous one. Consequently the radius stays constant and the **pitch increases** turn after turn.
