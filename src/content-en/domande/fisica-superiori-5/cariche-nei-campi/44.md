A charged particle enters a uniform electric field perpendicular to its initial velocity $\vec{v}_0$. The field region extends a distance $\ell$ along that initial direction. In the nonrelativistic regime the particle emerges deflected and then travels straight. Where does the backward extension of its outgoing line meet the line of its initial velocity?

- At the exit of the region
- At the entrance of the region
- At distance $\dfrac{\ell}{3}$ from the entrance
- At distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from the entrance, at the midpoint of the region

## Explanation

At the exit the particle is deflected by $y_u = \dfrac{a\,\ell^2}{2v_0^2}$ and its velocity forms with the initial direction the angle $\alpha$ with $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{a\,\ell}{v_0^2}$.

The outgoing line returns to the initial axis after a backward stretch equal to $\dfrac{y_u}{\tan\alpha} = \dfrac{a\,\ell^2}{2v_0^2} \cdot \dfrac{v_0^2}{a\,\ell} = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$.

Therefore the particle seems to come from the **midpoint** of the region, for any value of the field, the charge and the speed.
