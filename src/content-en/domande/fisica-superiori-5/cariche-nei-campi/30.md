A copper strip, in which the charge carriers are electrons, carries the current $I$ to the right and is placed in a uniform magnetic field out of the page, perpendicular to the strip. Which edge of the strip is at the higher potential in the steady state?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,-1 6,-1 6,1 0,1" linee="-1.5,0 0,0;6,0 7.4,0" vettori="-1.4,0.3,-0.3,0.3,I" etichette="2.4,1.25,upper;2.4,-1.55,lower;0.5,1.75,⊙;2,1.75,⊙;3.5,1.75,⊙;5,1.75,⊙;0.5,-2.25,⊙;2,-2.25,⊙;3.5,-2.25,⊙;5,-2.25,⊙" xmin=-1.7 xmax=7.6 ymin=-2.5 ymax=2.1 titolo="Strip carrying the current $I$ to the right in a uniform magnetic field out of the page"}

- The upper edge
- It depends on the magnitude of the current
- The lower edge
- The two edges are at the same potential

## Explanation

The electrons move opposite to the current, that is, to the left, with $\vec{v}_d = -v_d\,\hat{\imath}$.

With the field $\vec{B} = B\,\hat{k}$ the magnetic force on an electron is
$$
\vec{F} = -e\,\vec{v}_d \times \vec{B} = e\,v_d B\,(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k}) = -e\,v_d B\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
pointing downward. The electrons therefore accumulate on the lower edge, which becomes negatively charged, while the upper edge is left with a positive charge. The Hall field thus points from the upper edge to the lower one, and the **upper edge** is at the higher potential, whatever the magnitude of the current.
