An electron beam travels straight between charged plates and magnetic poles. Both fields are then turned off. Why does the second straight passage fail to measure the speed?

- The radius directly gives charge
- Electrons lose mass without a field
- Switched-off fields create a constant force
- Without fields any straight-line speed is possible

## Explanation

The method $v=E/B$ requires balanced nonzero forces. With both fields off, the initial velocity can have any magnitude and the track remains straight.
