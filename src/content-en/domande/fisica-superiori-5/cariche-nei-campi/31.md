A Hall probe, always carrying the same current, reads a voltage of $3.0\,\mathrm{mV}$ in a reference field of $0.20\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the strip. Placed perpendicular to an unknown field, it reads $7.5\,\mathrm{mV}$. What is the unknown field?

- $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.080\,\mathrm{T}$
- $0.32\,\mathrm{T}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

By the **Hall effect** the voltage is $\Delta V_H = \dfrac{I B}{n\,|q|\,s}$. The current, the carrier density and the thickness do not change, so the voltage is proportional to the field.

The ratio of the two voltages equals the ratio of the two fields, therefore
$$
B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T} \cdot \frac{7.5\,\mathrm{mV}}{3.0\,\mathrm{mV}} = 0.50\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
The value $0.080\,\mathrm{T}$ is obtained by inverting the ratio of the voltages.
