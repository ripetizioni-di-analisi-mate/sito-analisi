The current $i(t) = I_0 \sin(\omega t)$ flows in an ideal inductor. At which instants is the magnitude of the self-induced emf maximum?

- At the instants when the current is maximum in magnitude
- At the instants when the magnitude of the current is $\dfrac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}}$
- The magnitude of the emf is the same at every instant
- At the instants when the current is zero

## Explanation

By the **self-induced emf** formula $\mathcal{E}_L = -L\,\dfrac{di}{dt} = -L I_0 \omega \cos(\omega t)$. The magnitude is maximum when $|\cos(\omega t)| = 1$, that is, when $\sin(\omega t) = 0$. At the same instants the current is zero, therefore the self-induced emf is maximum in magnitude when the current vanishes. When the current is maximum in magnitude its derivative is zero, and hence the emf is zero as well.
