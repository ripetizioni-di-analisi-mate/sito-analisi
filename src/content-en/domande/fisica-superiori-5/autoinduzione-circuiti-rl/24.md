In a series RL circuit, closed at the instant $t = 0$ with zero current, the inductance is $0.50\,\mathrm{H}$ and the steady-state current is $4.0\,\mathrm{A}$. How much energy is stored in the inductor after a time equal to the time constant?

- $1.6\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.54\,\mathrm{J}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{J}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

When the RL circuit is closed, at the instant $t = \tau$ the current is $I \left( 1 - e^{-1} \right) = 4.0 \cdot 0.632\,\mathrm{A} \approx 2.53\,\mathrm{A}$. The **energy stored in the inductor** is $\dfrac{1}{2} L i^2$, so
$$
U = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.50 \cdot 2.53^2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.6\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The steady-state energy would be $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.50 \cdot 4.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 4.0\,\mathrm{J}$. The value $2.5\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by multiplying the steady-state energy by $1 - e^{-1}$, without squaring the factor of the current.
