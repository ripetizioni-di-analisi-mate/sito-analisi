In an ideal inductor of inductance $10\,\mathrm{mH}$ the current varies in time as shown in the figure. What is the magnitude of the self-induced emf while the current rises and while it falls, in this order?

::grafico{linee="0,0 2,3 3,0" punti="2,3,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,3" nomi="t,i" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.8 titolo="Current $i$ in the inductor, in amperes, as a function of the time $t$, in milliseconds"}

- $0\,\mathrm{V}$ and $30\,\mathrm{V}$
- $15\,\mathrm{V}$ and $15\,\mathrm{V}$
- $30\,\mathrm{V}$ and $15\,\mathrm{V}$
- $15\,\mathrm{V}$ and $30\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The self-induced emf is $\mathcal{E}_L = -L\,\dfrac{di}{dt}$, so it is constant on each straight portion of the graph. While rising, the current increases by $3\,\mathrm{A}$ in $2\,\mathrm{ms}$, therefore $\left| \dfrac{di}{dt} \right| = 1.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{A/s}$ and $|\mathcal{E}_L| = 0.010 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V} = 15\,\mathrm{V}$. While falling, the current decreases by $3\,\mathrm{A}$ in $1\,\mathrm{ms}$, so $|\mathcal{E}_L| = 0.010 \cdot 3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V} = 30\,\mathrm{V}$. In the two portions the emf has opposite signs, because the current first increases and then decreases.
