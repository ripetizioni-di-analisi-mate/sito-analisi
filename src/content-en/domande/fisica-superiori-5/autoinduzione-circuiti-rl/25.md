Two rigid, stationary coils are close to each other. When the current $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ flows in coil $A$, the flux linked with coil $B$ is $6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Wb}$. If instead no current flows in coil $A$ and the current $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ flows in coil $B$, what is the flux linked with coil $A$ due to coil $B$?

- It cannot be computed without knowing the number of turns and the shape of the two coils
- $2.4 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $1.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb}$

## Explanation

By the definition of **mutual inductance** the flux linked with $B$ due to the current in $A$ is $M i_A$, so $M = \dfrac{6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}}{2.0}\,\mathrm{H} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{H}$. By the reciprocity property the flux linked with $A$ due to the current in $B$ is $M i_B$, with the same constant $M$. Therefore
$$
\Phi_A = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{Wb} = 1.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} .
$$
The shape and the number of turns of the coils are already contained in the value of $M$, which is enough for the calculation.
