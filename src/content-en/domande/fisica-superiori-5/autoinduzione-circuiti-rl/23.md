In vacuum, what magnitude must an electric field have for its energy density to equal that of a magnetic field of $0.10\,\mathrm{T}$?

- $3.3 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $3.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $0.10\,\mathrm{V/m}$
- $9.0 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{V/m}$

## Explanation

The energy density of the electric field is $\dfrac{1}{2} \varepsilon_0 E^2$, that of the magnetic field is $\dfrac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$. Equating the two expressions gives $E^2 = \dfrac{B^2}{\varepsilon_0 \mu_0}$. Since $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_0 \mu_0}} = c$, one has
$$
E = c B = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{V/m} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
A magnetic field of $0.10\,\mathrm{T}$, easy to obtain in the laboratory, therefore contains the same energy per unit volume as an electric field much stronger than the dielectric strength of air.
