An ideal source of $10\,\mathrm{V}$ is connected in series with a resistor $R_1 = 5.0\,\Omega$; after $R_1$ the circuit splits into two branches in parallel, one with a resistor $R_2 = 20\,\Omega$ and the other with an ideal inductor. The switch is closed at the instant $t = 0$, when all currents are zero. What current does the source deliver just after closing?

- $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$
- $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$
- $0\,\mathrm{A}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{A}$

## Explanation

The current in an inductor cannot change with a jump, because the self-induced emf stays finite; so just after closing the current in the inductor is still zero. At that instant all the current flows through $R_1$ and $R_2$, which are then in series. Therefore
$$
i(0^+) = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R_1 + R_2} = \frac{10}{25}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.40\,\mathrm{A} .
$$
The value $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ is the steady-state current, when the ideal inductor has zero voltage across it and short-circuits $R_2$.
