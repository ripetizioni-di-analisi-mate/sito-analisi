Two ideal inductors, of inductance $30\,\mathrm{mH}$ and $60\,\mathrm{mH}$, are connected in parallel and placed far from each other, so that mutual induction is negligible. What is the equivalent inductance?

- $45\,\mathrm{mH}$
- $10\,\mathrm{mH}$
- $20\,\mathrm{mH}$
- $90\,\mathrm{mH}$

## Explanation

The two inductors in parallel have the same potential difference $v$ across them, so $\dfrac{di_1}{dt} = \dfrac{v}{L_1}$ and $\dfrac{di_2}{dt} = \dfrac{v}{L_2}$. The total current is $i = i_1 + i_2$, therefore
$$
\frac{di}{dt} = v \left( \frac{1}{L_1} + \frac{1}{L_2} \right) .
$$
The equivalent inductance satisfies $\dfrac{di}{dt} = \dfrac{v}{L}$, so $\dfrac{1}{L} = \dfrac{1}{L_1} + \dfrac{1}{L_2}$ and $L = \dfrac{30 \cdot 60}{30 + 60}\,\mathrm{mH} = 20\,\mathrm{mH}$. Inductors without mutual induction combine like resistors, and $90\,\mathrm{mH}$ is the inductance of the series connection.
