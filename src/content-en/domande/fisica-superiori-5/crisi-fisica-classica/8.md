A metal of work function $W_0 = 2.25\,\mathrm{eV}$ is illuminated by light of wavelength $\lambda = 400\,\mathrm{nm}$. Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons in electronvolts. Use $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$.

- $0.86\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $2.25\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $3.11\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $5.36\,\mathrm{eV}$

## Explanation

Light of wavelength $400\,\mathrm{nm}$ supplies about $3.11\,\mathrm{eV}$ per photon with the stated constants. After allowing for the work function, the largest kinetic energy is the difference $3.11-2.25=0.86\,\mathrm{eV}$.
