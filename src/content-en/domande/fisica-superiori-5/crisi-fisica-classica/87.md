A grating of spacing $d=2.0\,\mu\mathrm m$ sends the first-order maximum of unknown light to an angle with $\sin\theta=0.25$. If the light is directed onto a metal with $W=2.0\,\mathrm{eV}$, what is predicted? Use $hc=1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}$ and normal incidence on the grating.

- Emission with $K_{\max}\simeq2.48\,\mathrm{eV}$.
- Emission with $K_{\max}\simeq0.48\,\mathrm{eV}$.
- No emission because the photon energy is less than $W$.
- Emission with $K_{\max}\simeq4.48\,\mathrm{eV}$.

## Explanation

At first order $\lambda=d\sin\theta=0.50\,\mu\mathrm m=500\,\mathrm{nm}$. Each photon has energy $2.48\,\mathrm{eV}$. Subtracting the work function leaves $0.48\,\mathrm{eV}$ for the most energetic electrons.
