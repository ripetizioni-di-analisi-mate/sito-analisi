A macroscopic spring oscillates at $2\,\mathrm{Hz}$ with energy $0.1\,\mathrm J$. Using $h\simeq6.6\cdot10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$, why does its energy appear continuous?

- The spacing $hf$ is tiny relative to the total energy and experimental resolution.
- A macroscopic oscillator has only two levels.
- The spring has zero frequency.
- Planck's constant vanishes for heavy objects.

## Explanation

The spacing is about $1.3\cdot10^{-33}\,\mathrm J$. Compared with one tenth of a joule the levels are so close that an ordinary measurement cannot distinguish them.
