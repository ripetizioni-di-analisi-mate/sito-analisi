A surface is heated from $27^\circ\mathrm C$ to $327^\circ\mathrm C$ at fixed area and emissivity. Why would using the ratio $\dfrac{327}{27}$ in Stefan–Boltzmann's law give the wrong answer?

- Emissivity diverges at all positive temperatures.
- The law only applies to surfaces at zero degrees.
- The law uses only Celsius differences.
- The law uses kelvin; the temperature ratio is about $\dfrac{600}{300}=2$.

## Explanation

The law uses absolute zero as its reference. Adding about $273$ gives $300$ and $600\,\mathrm K$, which are then raised to the fourth power.
