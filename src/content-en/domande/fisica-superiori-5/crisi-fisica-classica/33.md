One instrument reports $B_\lambda$ per nanometre and another reports $B_f$ per hertz for the same source. Does simply setting $f=\dfrac{c}{\lambda_{\max}}$ convert the wavelength-density peak into the frequency-density peak?

- Yes, frequency is linear in wavelength.
- No, equal wavelength bins correspond to unequal frequency bins.
- No, frequency and wavelength are unrelated.
- Yes, every density keeps its numerical value.

## Explanation

Band energy is preserved, so $B_f\lvert df\rvert=B_\lambda\lvert d\lambda\rvert$. The bin-width conversion factor varies across the spectrum and can shift the density peak.
