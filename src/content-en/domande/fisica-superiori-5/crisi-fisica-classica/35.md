A black plate peaks at $1\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. Another plate at the same temperature has emissivity that suppresses near infrared almost entirely. Can its peak be used as if it were a blackbody to infer the same temperature reliably?

- Yes, all surfaces have the same spectrum at equal temperature.
- No, wavelength-dependent emissivity can shift the measured peak.
- Yes, if their areas are identical.
- No, because only vacuum has a temperature.

## Explanation

The second plate's spectrum is the blackbody spectrum times its spectral emissivity. A factor varying with wavelength changes the curve's shape.
