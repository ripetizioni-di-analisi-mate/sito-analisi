A lamp illuminates a photocathode. To stop the electrons the collector is made negative relative to the cathode. Why does this polarity impede travel towards the collector?

- The collector's magnetic field cancels the light.
- The electron's rest mass doubles.
- For a negative charge, potential energy rises on moving to lower electric potential.
- The voltage lowers each photon's energy before absorption.

## Explanation

We have $\Delta U=q\Delta V$. On travel to the collector $q<0$ and $\Delta V<0$, so $\Delta U>0$ at the expense of kinetic energy.
