A calcium photocathode with work function $2.90\,\mathrm{eV}$ receives $300\,\mathrm{nm}$ light. Taking $h=6.63\cdot10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$, $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$, what is the magnitude of the stopping potential?

- $1.24\,\mathrm V$
- $2.90\,\mathrm V$
- $7.04\,\mathrm V$
- $4.14\,\mathrm V$

## Explanation

The photon carries $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}\simeq4.14\,\mathrm{eV}$, leaving about $1.24\,\mathrm{eV}$ for the fastest electrons. The electrical barrier must supply the same energy per electron to stop them, which requires $1.24\,\mathrm V$.
