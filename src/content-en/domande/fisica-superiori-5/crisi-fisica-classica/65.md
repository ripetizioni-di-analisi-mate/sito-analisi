An initially stationary free electron cannot absorb a single photon and remain the sole product. What distinguishes it from a bound electron in a photocathode?

- Photons incident on a metal have no momentum.
- Energy is not conserved in a metal.
- A free electron has a different charge.
- In a metal the material can also take part in momentum conservation.

## Explanation

In vacuum the lone final electron cannot satisfy both conservation laws. The solid system includes ions and the lattice as well as the emitted electron.
