A metal’s photoelectric threshold energy is $5$ eV. It is illuminated by photons of energy $2$ eV under conditions in which each electron absorbs a single photon. What happens if the intensity is doubled?

- No photoelectric emission is observed.
- Electrons emerge with twice the kinetic energy.
- The work function is halved.
- Electrons emerge with the same positive energy as in the initial case.

## Explanation

Each photon still has less energy than the work function. The intensity changes the number of photons incident per unit time, while the energy of each photon stays the same.
