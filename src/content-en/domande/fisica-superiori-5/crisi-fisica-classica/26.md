A spectrometer rejects all wavelengths above $2\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. Can it directly measure by Wien's law the temperature of a body whose actual peak lies at $4\,\mathrm{\mu m}$?

- Yes, the $2\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ cutoff automatically becomes the true peak.
- Yes, Wien's law uses only the total area.
- No, Wien's law only applies in the ultraviolet.
- No, the peak lies outside its measured range.

## Explanation

The law uses the peak of the complete spectrum per unit wavelength. An instrumental cutoff can hide that peak.
