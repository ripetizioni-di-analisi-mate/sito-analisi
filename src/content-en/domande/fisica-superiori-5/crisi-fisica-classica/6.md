Determine how many red-light photons with $\lambda = 620\,\mathrm{nm}$ have a total energy of $1.0\,\mathrm{J}$. Use $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$ and $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

- $3.1\cdot10^{18}$
- $3.2\cdot10^{-19}$
- $3.1\cdot10^{15}$
- $1.6\cdot10^{19}$

## Explanation

One red photon carries $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}\simeq3.2\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm J$. One joule is the sum of all the photon energies, so the number is the reciprocal of that energy expressed in joules.
