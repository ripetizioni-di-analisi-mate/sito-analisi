In a photoelectric experiment the frequency of the light illuminating a metal is gradually increased, and the threshold frequency is taken to be the one at which the first photoelectrons appear. Besides the selected light, the lamp emits a small ultraviolet component with a frequency above the threshold of the metal. What error does this component cause in the measurement of the threshold?

- The measured threshold is higher than the true one.
- The measured threshold equals the true one, because the ultraviolet component is weak.
- The measured threshold is lower than the true one.
- The ultraviolet component increases the work function of the metal.

## Explanation

The ultraviolet photons have a frequency above the threshold, so they eject electrons even while the selected light is still below the threshold. Above the threshold frequency emission occurs even with very weak light, so a small ultraviolet component is enough to make photoelectrons appear. It follows that the first photoelectrons appear at a selected frequency below the true threshold, and the **measured threshold is lower** than the true one. The work function depends only on the metal and does not change.
