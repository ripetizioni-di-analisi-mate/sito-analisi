A rectangular conducting loop, with its plane vertical, falls through a region bounded by two horizontal planes containing a uniform horizontal magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the loop; the region is taller than the loop. Neglecting air resistance, which statement about the acceleration of the loop is true?

- It is less than $g$ during entry, during exit and also when the loop is entirely inside the region.
- It equals $g$ in every phase, because the magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity.
- During entry it is less than $g$, during exit it is greater than $g$, and it equals $g$ when the loop is entirely inside the region.
- It is less than $g$ during entry and during exit, and it equals $g$ when the loop is entirely inside the region.

## Explanation

During entry the flux through the loop increases, and during exit it decreases; in both cases an induced current flows. By **Lenz's law** the magnetic force on the induced current opposes, in both cases, the motion that produces the change in flux, so it points upward and the acceleration is less than $g$. When the loop is entirely inside the region the flux is constant, therefore the current is zero, only the weight acts on the loop and the acceleration equals $g$. The force on the induced current acts on the horizontal side immersed in the field and is directed along the vertical, that is, along the velocity.
