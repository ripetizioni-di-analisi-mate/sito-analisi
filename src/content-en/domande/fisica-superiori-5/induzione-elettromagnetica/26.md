Two straight conducting rails, in the same plane, meet at the point $O$ and form an angle of $45^\circ$. A conducting rod, perpendicular to the first rail and always in contact with both, starts from $O$ at time $t = 0$ and moves along the first rail with constant velocity $0.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A uniform magnetic field of $0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ is perpendicular to the plane of the rails. What is the magnitude of the emf induced in the triangular circuit at time $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $0.20\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.57\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

At time $t$ the rod is at distance $x = v t$ from $O$, and the part of the rod between the rails has length $x \tan 45^\circ = x$. The circuit is an isosceles right triangle of area $\dfrac{x^2}{2}$, so the flux is $\Phi_B = \dfrac{B x^2}{2}$. By the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** the magnitude of the emf is
$$
|\mathcal{E}| = \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = B x v = B v^2 t = 0.40 \cdot 0.50^2 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{V} = 0.40\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The same value is obtained from the motional emf $B \ell v$ with the length $\ell = x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ of the part of the rod between the rails. The value $0.20\,\mathrm{V}$ is the average emf between $0$ and $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$, that is $\dfrac{\Phi_B}{t}$, and $0.57\,\mathrm{V}$ is obtained by using the length of the second rail, $x\sqrt{2}$, instead of that of the rod.
