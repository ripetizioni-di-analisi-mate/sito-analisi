An electromagnetic brake slows a rod on frictionless horizontal rails. The circuit closes through $R=0.10\,\Omega$. The rod has mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $0.20\,\mathrm m$, perpendicular to its motion and to a uniform $0.50\,\mathrm T$ field normal to the rails. With initial speed $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and no further driving force, what total distance does it cover? You may use $v(t)=v_0e^{-t/\tau}$ and neglect self-inductance and other resistances.

- $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.75\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The magnetic braking force has magnitude $\dfrac{B^2 \ell^2 v}{R}$, so the velocity decreases as $v_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$ with $\tau = \dfrac{m R}{B^2 \ell^2}$. With the data one has
$$
\tau = \frac{0.20 \cdot 0.10}{0.50^2 \cdot 0.20^2}\,\mathrm{s} = 2.0\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The distance traveled is the integral of the velocity from $0$ to $+\infty$, that is, $v_0 \tau = 1.5 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The rod stops only asymptotically, but the total distance is finite.
