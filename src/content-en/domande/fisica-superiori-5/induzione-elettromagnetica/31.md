An infinitely long straight solenoid has its axis perpendicular to the page; inside it the magnetic field is uniform, pointing out of the page and increasing in time, while outside it is zero. An electron is at rest outside the solenoid, at a point to the right of the axis. In which direction is the force acting on the electron?

- The force is zero, because the magnetic field is zero outside the solenoid
- Toward the axis of the solenoid
- Downward
- Upward

## Explanation

The electron is at rest, so the magnetic force is zero and only the induced electric field acts on it, which is nonzero outside the solenoid. By symmetry the lines of the induced field are circles coaxial with the solenoid. The circle through the electron is oriented counterclockwise, so that the normal points out of the page; the outward flux increases, therefore by the **integral form of Faraday's law** the circulation of the electric field in the counterclockwise direction is negative. The induced field therefore circulates clockwise, and at the point to the right of the axis it points downward. The charge of the electron is negative, so the force $-e\vec{E}$ points **upward**.
