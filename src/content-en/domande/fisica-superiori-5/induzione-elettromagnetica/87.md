A solenoid confines varying magnetic flux inside itself. Two external paths between the same points pass on opposite sides and together surround the solenoid. Why can their electric-field integrals differ?

- Different charges must occupy the endpoints in each trial
- Every external electric field is always zero
- The closed contour formed by the paths links changing flux
- The difference depends only on wire mass

## Explanation

The difference of the directed integrals is the circulation around the closed contour. By Faraday's law it can be nonzero even when magnetic field along the wires vanishes.
