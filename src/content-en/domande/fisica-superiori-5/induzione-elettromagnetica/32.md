A conducting ring of radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ surrounds, coaxially, a long solenoid of radius $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Inside the solenoid the magnetic field is uniform and increases by $4.0\,\mathrm{T}$ per second, while outside it is zero. What is the magnitude of the emf induced in the ring?

- $0.13\,\mathrm{V}$
- $25\,\mathrm{mV}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{mV}$
- $5.0\,\mathrm{mV}$

## Explanation

The magnetic field is nonzero only in the cross section of the solenoid, so the flux through the disk bounded by the ring is $\Phi_B = B \pi a^2$, with $a$ the radius of the solenoid. By the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** the magnitude of the emf is
$$
|\mathcal{E}| = \pi a^2 \left| \frac{dB}{dt} \right| = \pi \cdot 0.020^2 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{V} \approx 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The emf does not depend on the radius of the ring, provided the ring encloses the solenoid. The value $0.13\,\mathrm{V}$ is obtained by using the area of the ring, as if the field filled the whole disk.
