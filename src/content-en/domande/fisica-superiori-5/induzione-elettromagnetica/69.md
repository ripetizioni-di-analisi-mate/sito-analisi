An eddy-current brake acts on a conducting wheel through a localized static field. Can it hold an already stationary wheel against a constant applied torque?

- Yes, braking is strongest at zero speed
- No, at rest there is no motion-induced flux change
- Yes, if the wheel is pure copper
- Yes, if the wheel is circular

## Explanation

At zero speed a steady external field induces no new currents. Once transients decay, inductive braking torque vanishes.
