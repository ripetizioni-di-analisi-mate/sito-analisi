In a circular region of radius $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, the magnetic field is uniform, perpendicular to the plane of the region, and increasing at a constant rate of $0.30\,\mathrm{T/s}$. Calculate the magnitude of the induced electric field at a distance of $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center.

- $9.0\,\mathrm{mV/m}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{mV/m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{mV/m}$
- $3.0\,\mathrm{mV/m}$

## Explanation

The measurement point is inside the region. Symmetry gives a uniform tangential electric field around the circle, and the flux involves the disk of that radius. $E = \dfrac{r}{2}\,\dfrac{dB}{dt} = 9.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V/m}$.
