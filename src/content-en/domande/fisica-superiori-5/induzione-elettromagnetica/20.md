A conducting bar $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ long rotates with angular velocity $30\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ about an axis through its midpoint, in a plane perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the potential difference between the two ends of the bar?

- $0.60\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

Each half of the bar is a segment of length $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ rotating about one of its ends, so between the center and each end there is the emf $\dfrac{1}{2} B \omega \left( \dfrac{\ell}{2} \right)^2 = 0.30\,\mathrm{V}$. The two halves are symmetric with respect to the center, therefore the Lorentz force pushes the positive charges from the center toward both ends, or from both ends toward the center. The two ends are hence at the same potential, and the potential difference between them is **zero**. The value $0.30\,\mathrm{V}$ is the potential difference between the center and one end, and $1.2\,\mathrm{V}$ is the emf of a bar rotating about one end.
