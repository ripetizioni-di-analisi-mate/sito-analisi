An elastic circular loop is perpendicular to a uniform, constant magnetic field of $0.40\,\mathrm{T}$. Its radius decreases at a constant rate of $2.0\,\mathrm{cm/s}$. What is the magnitude of the induced emf at the instant when the radius is $10\,\mathrm{cm}$?

- $5.0\,\mathrm{mV}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{mV}$
- $50\,\mathrm{mV}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{mV}$

## Explanation

The flux linkage is $\Phi_B = B \pi r^2$, and only the radius varies in it. Differentiating with respect to time gives $\dfrac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = 2\pi r B\,\dfrac{dr}{dt}$. By the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** the magnitude of the emf is therefore
$$
|\mathcal{E}| = 2\pi \cdot 0.10 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 0.020\,\mathrm{V} \approx 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The value $2.5\,\mathrm{mV}$ is obtained by forgetting the factor $2$ in the derivative of $r^2$.
