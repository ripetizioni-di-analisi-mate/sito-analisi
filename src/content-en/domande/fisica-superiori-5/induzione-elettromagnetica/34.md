A straight conducting rod $60\,\mathrm{cm}$ long translates with constant velocity $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a plane perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$. The rod forms an angle of $30^\circ$ with the direction of the velocity. What is the magnitude of the emf between the ends of the rod?

- $1.0\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.60\,\mathrm{V}$
- $1.2\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The charges of the rod are subject to the Lorentz force per unit charge $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$. Since $\vec{B}$ is perpendicular to the plane of motion, the vector $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ lies in that plane, is perpendicular to the velocity and has magnitude $v B$. The emf is the work per unit charge along the rod, so only the component of $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ parallel to the rod counts. The rod forms $30^\circ$ with the velocity, therefore it forms $60^\circ$ with $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$, and
$$
|\mathcal{E}| = v B \ell \cos 60^\circ = v B \ell \sin 30^\circ = 4.0 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 0.60 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{V} = 0.60\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The value $1.2\,\mathrm{V}$ is the **motional emf** of a rod perpendicular to the velocity, and $1.0\,\mathrm{V}$ is obtained with the cosine of the angle between the rod and the velocity.
