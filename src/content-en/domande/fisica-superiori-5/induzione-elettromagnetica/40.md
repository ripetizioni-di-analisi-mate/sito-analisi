A peak voltmeter will measure the signal from a stationary search coil consisting of a single turn of area $0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The normal field varies sinusoidally between $-0.50$ and $+0.50\,\mathrm T$ at $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. What peak voltage magnitude must the meter be able to read?

- $6.3\,\mathrm{V}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{V}$
- $3.1\,\mathrm{V}$
- $2.2\,\mathrm{V}$

## Explanation

The flux linkage is $\Phi_B(t) = A B_0 \sin(\omega t)$, because the loop is at rest and perpendicular to the field. By the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** the emf is $\mathcal{E}(t) = -A B_0 \omega \cos(\omega t)$, so its maximum magnitude is $A B_0 \omega$. With $\omega = 2\pi \cdot 50\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 314\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ one obtains
$$
\mathcal{E}_{\max} = 0.020 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 314\,\mathrm{V} \approx 3.1\,\mathrm{V} .
$$
The value $0.50\,\mathrm{V}$ corresponds to using the frequency instead of the angular frequency, and $2.2\,\mathrm{V}$ is the rms value.
