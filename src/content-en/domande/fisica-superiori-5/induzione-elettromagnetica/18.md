A square loop of side $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ is made of a wire with a resistance of $0.050\,\Omega$ per meter of length. The loop is perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field that increases at a constant rate of $0.30\,\mathrm{T/s}$. Neglecting self-induction, what power is dissipated in the loop by the Joule effect?

- $0.23\,\mathrm{mW}$
- $0.45\,\mathrm{mW}$
- $1.8\,\mathrm{mW}$
- $0.90\,\mathrm{mW}$

## Explanation

The wire is as long as the perimeter, $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, so the resistance of the loop is $R = 0.40 \cdot 0.050\,\Omega = 0.020\,\Omega$. By the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** the magnitude of the emf is $A\,\dfrac{dB}{dt} = 0.010 \cdot 0.30\,\mathrm{V} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V}$. The dissipated power is therefore
$$
P = \frac{\mathcal{E}^2}{R} = \frac{\left( 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \right)^2}{0.020}\,\mathrm{W} = 4.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{W} .
$$
The value $1.8\,\mathrm{mW}$ is obtained by using the length of a single side instead of the perimeter.
