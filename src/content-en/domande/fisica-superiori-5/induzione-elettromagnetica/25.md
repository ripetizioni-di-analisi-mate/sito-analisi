A rod of mass $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$, launched with velocity $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on two frictionless horizontal rails closed by a resistor, in a magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the rails, slows down with time constant $\tau$. Neglecting the other resistances and self-induction, how much heat is produced in the resistor from the instant of launch to the instant $t = \tau$?

- $1.8\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.1\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0.24\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The power of the braking force is equal in magnitude to the power dissipated in the resistor, so the heat produced equals the kinetic energy lost by the rod. The velocity decreases as $v_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$, therefore at the instant $\tau$ it is $\dfrac{v_0}{e}$ and the kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2} m v_0^2\,e^{-2}$. The heat is thus
$$
Q = \frac{1}{2} m v_0^2 \left( 1 - e^{-2} \right) = 1.8 \cdot 0.865\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.6\,\mathrm{J} .
$$
The value $1.1\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained by applying the factor $1 - e^{-1}$, which holds for the velocity, to the kinetic energy, which is proportional to the square of the velocity.
