Two students calculate the magnetic flux linked by the same ring. One uses the flat disk spanning the ring, the other a curved cap with the same boundary and consistent orientation. Why must they get the same result even in a nonuniform field?

- The two surfaces always have the same area
- The field strength must be the same on both surfaces
- The field must be tangent to the cap everywhere
- The net magnetic flux through the closed surface formed from the two surfaces is zero

## Explanation

Joining the cap to the oppositely oriented disk gives a closed surface. Gauss’s law for magnetism makes its net flux zero. The two fluxes computed with the same boundary orientation are therefore equal.
