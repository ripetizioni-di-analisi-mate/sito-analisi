A rectangular loop $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ wide hangs vertically from a balance, with its lower side immersed in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$, perpendicular to the plane of the loop, and its upper side outside the field. The current in the loop is $I = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the magnitude of the total magnetic force on the loop?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,-1 2,-1 2,3 0,3 0,-1;-1.4,0.6 3.4,0.6" vettori="0.6,3.3,1.4,3.3,I" etichette="0.6,-1.45,10 cm;-1.1,0,⊗;0.65,0,⊗;2.6,0,⊗;-1.1,-1.3,⊗;2.6,-1.3,⊗;-1.3,1.3,B = 0;2.6,1.3,B = 0" xmin=-1.8 xmax=3.6 ymin=-1.9 ymax=3.8 titolo="Loop with its lower side in the region of uniform field into the page, below the horizontal line, and its upper side outside the field"}

- $0.10\,\mathrm{N}$
- Zero, because the loop is closed
- $0.40\,\mathrm{N}$
- $0.20\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

The total force is zero for a closed loop entirely immersed in a uniform field; here instead the field acts only on the lower part of the loop, and the contributions of the sides must be added one by one.

The two vertical sides have segments of equal length in the field carrying opposite currents, therefore they experience opposite horizontal forces, which cancel. The upper side is outside the field and experiences no force.

What remains is the force on the lower side, perpendicular to the field, which by the **force on a straight wire** is
$$
F = I \ell B = 5.0 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{N} = 0.20\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
and is vertical. The current balance is based on this principle.
