A horizontal straight wire, with mass per unit length $\lambda = 0.040\,\mathrm{kg/m}$, hangs from two light insulating threads in a uniform vertical magnetic field $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$. When the current $I = 1.0\,\mathrm{A}$ flows in the wire, the threads tilt and the wire stays in equilibrium displaced sideways. What angle do the threads make with the vertical? Use $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $27^\circ$
- $79^\circ$
- $63^\circ$
- $31^\circ$

## Explanation

The wire is horizontal and the field vertical, so on a segment of length $\ell$ there acts the horizontal magnetic force $I \ell B$, perpendicular to the wire. The weight $\lambda \ell g$ acts on the same segment.

At equilibrium the tension of the threads balances the sum of the weight and the magnetic force, therefore the threads are aligned with this sum. The angle $\theta$ with the vertical thus satisfies
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{I \ell B}{\lambda \ell g} = \frac{1.0 \cdot 0.20}{0.040 \cdot 9.81} \approx 0.51 ,
$$
and $\theta \approx 27^\circ$. The value $63^\circ$ is the angle with the horizontal.
