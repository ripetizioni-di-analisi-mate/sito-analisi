An ion passes through the same point twice, with nonzero velocities that are not parallel to each other. In both trials the magnetic force is exactly zero. What follows about the field at that point if it stayed constant?

- The field points along the sum of the velocities.
- The magnetic field is zero.
- The field may have any magnitude and direction.
- The field is perpendicular to the plane of the two velocities.

## Explanation

For a nonzero charge, $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B=0$ requires that the field be parallel to the velocity or zero. A nonzero vector cannot be parallel to two nonparallel directions.
