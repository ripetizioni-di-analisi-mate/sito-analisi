A single-loop motor is stationary with the loop normal already parallel to the field. Why do angularly offset windings help it start?

- Every loop always remains stationary
- All windings become resistance-free
- Another winding can provide torque when the first provides zero
- Rotor mass becomes zero

## Explanation

The first loop has torque $\mu B\sin 0=0$. An offset loop normal can make an angle other than zero or $180^\circ$ and produce starting torque.
