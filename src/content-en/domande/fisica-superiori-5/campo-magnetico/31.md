A thin insulating ring of radius $r = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the charge $Q = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ uniformly distributed and rotates about its own axis with angular velocity $\omega = 50\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. What is the magnitude of its magnetic moment?

- $3.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$
- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$

## Explanation

In one period $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$ all the charge of the ring passes once through each point of the circle, so the ring is equivalent to a loop carrying the current $I = \dfrac{Q}{T} = \dfrac{Q\,\omega}{2\pi}$.

The enclosed area is $\pi r^2$, therefore
$$
\mu = I\,\pi r^2 = \frac{Q\,\omega\, r^2}{2} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 50 \cdot 0.10^2}{2}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} .
$$
The value $1.6 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$ is obtained by using the area $r^2$ without the factor $\pi$, and the value $3.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$ by using $Q\,\omega$ as the current.
