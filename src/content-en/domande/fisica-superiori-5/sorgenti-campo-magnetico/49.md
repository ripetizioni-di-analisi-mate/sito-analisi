An iron bar is brought to the temperature of $900\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and, kept at that temperature, is inserted into a solenoid carrying a current. How does the iron behave?

- As a ferromagnet with a relative permeability of a few thousand
- As a diamagnet: it is magnetized opposite to the field
- As a ferromagnet, and it stays magnetized when the current is switched off
- As a paramagnet: it is weakly magnetized and the magnetization vanishes when the current is switched off

## Explanation

The Curie temperature of iron is about $770\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and above this temperature a ferromagnet becomes a paramagnet.

At $900\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ thermal agitation prevents the atomic magnetic moments from staying aligned in domains, so the moments align only partly with the field.

Therefore the iron behaves as a **paramagnet**: the susceptibility is positive and small, and without an external field the magnetization is zero.
