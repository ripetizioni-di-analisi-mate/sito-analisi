An infinite straight wire carries the current $8.0\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the line integral $\displaystyle\int \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l}$ of its field along a quarter of a circle centered on the wire, in a plane perpendicular to the wire, traversed in the direction of the field lines?

- $1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$
- $2.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$

## Explanation

Along a circle centered on the wire the field is tangent and has constant magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$, so the integral equals the magnitude of the field times the length of the arc.

The quarter circle has length $\dfrac{\pi r}{2}$, therefore
$$
\int \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} \cdot \frac{\pi r}{2} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4} = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 8.0}{4}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} .
$$
The result does not depend on the radius, because the magnitude of the field decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r}$ while the length of the arc grows as $r$.
