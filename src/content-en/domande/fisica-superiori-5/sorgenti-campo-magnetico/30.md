Two identical circular loops of radius $R$ are coaxial and placed at some distance from each other; they carry currents of equal magnitude in opposite directions. What can be said about the magnetic field at the point of the axis equidistant from the two loops?

- It is directed along the axis and is twice the field of a single loop at that point
- It equals the field at the center of a single loop
- It is perpendicular to the axis
- It is zero

## Explanation

The point lies on the axis of both loops, at the same distance $z$ from the two centers.

By the field of a circular loop on its axis, each loop produces at that point a field directed along the axis, of magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2\,(R^2 + z^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$, the same for the two loops.

The currents circulate in opposite directions, so by the right-hand rule the two fields have opposite directions. Therefore the field at the midpoint is **zero**.
