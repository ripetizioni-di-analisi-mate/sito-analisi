A semi-infinite solenoid, that is, very long and with only one end at a finite distance, has $2000$ turns per meter carrying the current $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the magnetic field at the point of the axis lying in the plane of the end?

- $2.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $1.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $6.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

An infinite solenoid can be regarded as the union of two identical semi-infinite solenoids placed side by side at the plane of the end.

The second solenoid is the image of the first in the reflection across that plane, which leaves the sense of rotation of the currents unchanged; hence at the point of the axis lying in the plane the two solenoids produce the same axial field.

By the superposition principle the sum of the two fields is the field of the infinite solenoid, $\mu_0 n I$. Therefore at the end of the semi-infinite solenoid
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 n I}{2} = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2000 \cdot 1.0}{2}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
