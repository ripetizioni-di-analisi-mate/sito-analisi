A square loop of side $L = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ lies in the $xy$ plane, with a vertex at the origin and two sides on the positive half-axes. The magnetic field is $\vec{B} = B_0\,\dfrac{x}{L}\,\hat{k}$, with $B_0 = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$. What is the flux of the field through the loop, oriented with the normal $\hat{k}$?

- $8.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb}$
- $4.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$

## Explanation

The field varies with $x$, so the loop is divided into strips parallel to the $y$ axis, of width $dx$ and area $L\,dx$, on each of which the field is constant.

The flux is
$$
\Phi_B = \int_0^L B_0\,\frac{x}{L}\,L\,dx = B_0\,\frac{L^2}{2} = 0.40 \cdot \frac{0.20^2}{2}\,\mathrm{Wb} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} .
$$
The result equals the flux of a uniform field equal to the mean value $\dfrac{B_0}{2}$, because the field grows linearly with $x$.
