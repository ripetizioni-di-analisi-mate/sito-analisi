A coaxial cable has an inner conductor of radius $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and an outer conductor with radii $4.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$; the two conductors carry the current $9.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in opposite directions, uniformly distributed over the cross sections. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field $4.2\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis?

- $2.9 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $4.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $3.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$
- $7.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$

## Explanation

The coaxial circle of radius $r = 4.2\,\mathrm{mm}$ lies inside the outer conductor, so it encloses the whole inner current and the part of the outer current that crosses the annulus between $b = 4.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $r$.

The outer current is uniform, therefore that part equals
$$
I\,\frac{r^2 - b^2}{c^2 - b^2} = 9.0 \cdot \frac{17.64 - 16}{25 - 16}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 1.64\,\mathrm{A} ,
$$
and the enclosed current is $9.0\,\mathrm{A} - 1.64\,\mathrm{A} \approx 7.36\,\mathrm{A}$.

By Ampère's law
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}}{2\pi r} = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 7.36}{4.2 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} .
$$
