A drop of water has volume $0.05\,\mathrm{mL}$. How many drops are needed to fill a $1\,\mathrm{dm^3}$ container?

- $2000$
- $20\,000$
- $20$
- $2\cdot10^{7}$

## Explanation

A cubic decimeter is a liter, so $1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=1\,\mathrm{L}=1000\,\mathrm{mL}$. The number of drops is the volume of the container divided by the volume of one drop, both expressed in the same unit, so
$$
N=\frac{1000\,\mathrm{mL}}{0.05\,\mathrm{mL}}=20\,000.
$$
Milliliters cancel, leaving a dimensionless number. The value $2000$ comes from using $1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=100\,\mathrm{mL}$, the area conversion factor. The value $20$ comes from confusing a cubic decimeter with a milliliter. The value $2\cdot10^{7}$ comes from using $1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=10^{6}\,\mathrm{mL}$, the number of cubic millimeters.
