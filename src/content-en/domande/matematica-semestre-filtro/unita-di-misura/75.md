In which situation does $c_1V_1=c_2V_2$ apply to the molar concentration of a solute during dilution?

- When half of the initial solute reacts.
- When more solute is added without changing the volume.
- When some solute is lost through precipitation and only the dissolved solute is counted.
- When only solvent is added and the amount of the solute in question is conserved.

## Explanation

The product $c_1V_1$ is the amount of solute before dilution, and $c_2V_2$ is its amount afterward. Therefore $c_1V_1=c_2V_2$ holds when the amount of solute is conserved, as it is when only solvent is added. If half of the solute reacts, its final amount is half its initial amount, so $c_2V_2=\dfrac{1}{2}c_1V_1$. If more solute is added or some precipitates, the dissolved amount changes and the two products differ.
