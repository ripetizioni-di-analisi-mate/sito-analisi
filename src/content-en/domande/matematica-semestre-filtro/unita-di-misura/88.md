A solution has initial volume $50\,\mathrm{mL}$. The problem specifies that $150\,\mathrm{mL}$ of solvent is added. The volumes are additive. Which volume belongs in the denominator of the final concentration?

- $150\,\mathrm{mL}$, the volume of added solvent alone.
- $100\,\mathrm{mL}$, the difference between the two volumes.
- $200\,\mathrm{mL}$, the total volume after the addition.
- $50\,\mathrm{mL}$, the volume before the addition.

## Explanation

The final concentration refers to the entire solution obtained after the addition. Since volumes are additive, the final volume is
$$
V=50\,\mathrm{mL}+150\,\mathrm{mL}=200\,\mathrm{mL},
$$
and this is the denominator. The $150\,\mathrm{mL}$ is just the added solvent, which contains no solute on its own. The $50\,\mathrm{mL}$ is the volume before the addition. The phrase “add $150\,\mathrm{mL}$” differs from “dilute to $150\,\mathrm{mL}$,” which would specify the final volume.
