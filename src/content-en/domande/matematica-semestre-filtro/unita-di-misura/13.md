Dilute $10\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a solution at $0.75\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ to $50\,\mathrm{mL}$. Then dilute $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ of the resulting homogeneous solution to $100\,\mathrm{mL}$. The solute is conserved during each dilution. Calculate the final molar concentration.

- $0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.075\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.03\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.30\,\mathrm{mol/L}$

## Explanation

Each dilution conserves the solute in the sample taken, so multiply the concentration by the ratio of sample volume to final volume. The first dilution gives $c_2=0.75\cdot\dfrac{10}{50}\,\mathrm{mol/L}=0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. The second starts with $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ at this concentration, so
$$
c_3=0.15\cdot\frac{20}{100}\,\mathrm{mol/L}=0.03\,\mathrm{mol/L}.
$$
The value $0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ is the concentration after the first dilution alone. The value $0.075\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ comes from using a single ratio, $\dfrac{10}{100}$, between the first sample volume and the final volume of the last dilution.
