The period $P$ of a simple pendulum depends on its string length $\ell$ and the acceleration due to gravity $g$ according to the relation $P=C\,\ell^{a}g^{b}$, where $C$ is dimensionless. Which exponents make the relation dimensionally consistent?

- $a=\dfrac{1}{2},\ b=\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $a=\dfrac{1}{2},\ b=-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $a=1,\ b=-1$
- $a=-\dfrac{1}{2},\ b=\dfrac{1}{2}$

## Explanation

The right-hand side has dimension $L^{a}\left(LT^{-2}\right)^{b}=L^{a+b}T^{-2b}$ and must match the dimension $T$ of the period. Equating the exponents of $L$ and $T$ gives the conditions
$$
\begin{cases}a+b=0\\-2b=1\end{cases}
$$
which must hold simultaneously. The second condition gives $b=-\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $a=\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the period is proportional to $\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$. The exponents $a=1$ and $b=-1$ give dimension $T^{2}$. The exponents $a=-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $b=\dfrac{1}{2}$ give $T^{-1}$.
