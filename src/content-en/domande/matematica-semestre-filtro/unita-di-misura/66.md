A container holds $1\,\mathrm{L}$. A pump removes liquid at $12\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ for $20\,\mathrm{s}$ and then at $5\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ for $12\,\mathrm{s}$. How much liquid remains?

- $300\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $700\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $940\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $456\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

The volume removed in each interval is the flow rate times the duration. The volumes are $12\,\mathrm{mL/s}\cdot20\,\mathrm{s}=240\,\mathrm{mL}$ and $5\,\mathrm{mL/s}\cdot12\,\mathrm{s}=60\,\mathrm{mL}$, so the total removed is $300\,\mathrm{mL}$. Since $1\,\mathrm{L}=1000\,\mathrm{mL}$, the liquid remaining is
$$
1000\,\mathrm{mL}-300\,\mathrm{mL}=700\,\mathrm{mL}.
$$
The value $300\,\mathrm{mL}$ is the volume removed. The value $940\,\mathrm{mL}$ comes from considering only the second interval. The value $456\,\mathrm{mL}$ comes from subtracting from $1000\,\mathrm{mL}$ the product of the sum of the flow rates and the sum of the times, $17\cdot32=544$.
