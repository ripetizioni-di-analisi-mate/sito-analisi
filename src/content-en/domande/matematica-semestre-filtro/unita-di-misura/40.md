A blood test reports a glucose concentration of $90\,\mathrm{mg/dL}$. What is this concentration in grams per liter?

- $9\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $0.09\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $0.9\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $900\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

One milligram equals $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{g}$ and one deciliter equals $10^{-1}\,\mathrm{L}$. Substituting for both units in the quotient gives
$$
90\,\frac{\mathrm{mg}}{\mathrm{dL}}=90\cdot\frac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{g}}{10^{-1}\,\mathrm{L}}=90\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{g/L}=0.9\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The value $0.09\,\mathrm{g/L}$ comes from converting milligrams but treating a deciliter as one liter. The value $900\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is the concentration in milligrams per liter, with the numerator left unconverted. The value $9\,\mathrm{g/L}$ comes from applying the factor $10^{-1}$ to the numerator.
