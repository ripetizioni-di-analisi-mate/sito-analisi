A $100\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample at a concentration of $1.5\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ is diluted to a final volume of $500\,\mathrm{mL}$. The solute is conserved. Calculate the final concentration.

- $0.3\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $7.5\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.03\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{mol/L}$

## Explanation

During dilution only solvent is added and the solute is conserved, so $c_1V_1=c_2V_2$. It follows that
$$
c_2=c_1\frac{V_1}{V_2}=1.5\,\mathrm{mol/L}\cdot\frac{100\,\mathrm{mL}}{500\,\mathrm{mL}}=0.3\,\mathrm{mol/L}.
$$
The volume ratio is dimensionless, so both volumes can remain in milliliters. The value $7.5\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ comes from inverting the volume ratio, which would imply that adding solvent increases the concentration.
