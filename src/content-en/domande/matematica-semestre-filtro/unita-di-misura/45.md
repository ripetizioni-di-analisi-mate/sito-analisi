A solution has mass concentration $15\,\mathrm{g/L}$. The solute has molar mass $60\,\mathrm{g/mol}$. What is the molar concentration?

- $4\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $900\,\mathrm{mol/L}$

## Explanation

The mass concentration gives the number of grams of solute in one liter, and the molar mass gives the number of grams in one mole. Dividing the first by the second gives moles per liter, so
$$
c=\frac{c_m}{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{15\,\mathrm{g/L}}{60\,\mathrm{g/mol}}=0.25\,\mathrm{mol/L}.
$$
Grams cancel, leaving moles per liter. The value $4\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ comes from inverting the quotient, giving $\dfrac{60}{15}$. The value $900\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ comes from multiplying the two quantities.
