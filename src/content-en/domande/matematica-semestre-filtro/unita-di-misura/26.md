A solution contains $108\,\mathrm{mg}$ in $9\,\mathrm{mL}$. What is its concentration in $\mathrm{g/L}$?

- $0.012$
- $12000$
- $12$
- $108$

## Explanation

Mass concentration is the ratio of solute mass to volume, so $\dfrac{108\,\mathrm{mg}}{9\,\mathrm{mL}}=12\,\mathrm{mg/mL}$. Since $1\,\mathrm{mg}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{g}$ and $1\,\mathrm{mL}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{L}$, we have
$$
12\,\frac{\mathrm{mg}}{\mathrm{mL}}=12\cdot\frac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{g}}{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{L}}=12\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The two factors of $10^{-3}$ cancel, so the numerical value stays the same. The value $0.012$ comes from converting only milligrams. The value $12000$ comes from converting only milliliters.
