A homogeneous solution of volume $2\,\mathrm{L}$ has mass concentration $3\,\mathrm{g/L}$. Only solvent evaporates until the volume reaches $1.5\,\mathrm{L}$, and the solute remains dissolved. What is the final concentration?

- $4\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $2.25\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $3\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

Only solvent evaporates, so the mass of solute remains constant. Initially the mass of solute is $m=3\,\mathrm{g/L}\cdot2\,\mathrm{L}=6\,\mathrm{g}$. After evaporation the volume is $1.5\,\mathrm{L}$, so
$$
c_m=\frac{6\,\mathrm{g}}{1.5\,\mathrm{L}}=4\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The concentration increases because the same mass of solute is contained in a smaller volume. The value $2.25\,\mathrm{g/L}$ comes from inverting the volume ratio, giving $3\cdot\dfrac{1.5}{2}$. The value $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$ comes from dividing the mass by one liter instead of the final volume.
