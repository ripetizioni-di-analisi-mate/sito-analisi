A cell has surface area $50\,\mu\mathrm{m}^2$. What is its surface area in square meters?

- $5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $5\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $5\cdot10^{-17}\,\mathrm{m^2}$
- $5\cdot10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m^2}$

## Explanation

One micrometer equals $10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$. Since area is the product of two lengths, the linear factor is squared, so $1\,\mu\mathrm{m}^2=(10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m})^2=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Therefore
$$
50\,\mu\mathrm{m}^2=50\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{m^2}=5\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^2}.
$$
The value $5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ comes from using the linear factor $10^{-6}$ without squaring it. The value $5\cdot10^{-17}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ comes from cubing the factor, which applies to volumes. The value $5\cdot10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ comes from adjusting the exponent in the wrong direction when writing $50=5\cdot10$.
