A solution at $0.60\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ is to be used to prepare $150\,\mathrm{mL}$ at $0.20\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ by adding only solvent. What volume of the initial solution should be taken?

- $30\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $100\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $450\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $50\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

Only solvent is added, so the amount of solute in the sample equals the amount in the final solution and $c_1V_1=c_2V_2$. Solving for the sample volume gives
$$
V_1=\frac{c_2V_2}{c_1}=\frac{0.20\cdot150}{0.60}\,\mathrm{mL}=50\,\mathrm{mL}.
$$
The concentration falls to one-third of its original value, so the sample volume is one-third of the final volume. The value $450\,\mathrm{mL}$ comes from inverting the concentration ratio. The value $100\,\mathrm{mL}$ would be the volume of solvent to add to the $50\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample if the volumes were additive.
