If a mass of $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ and a volume of $6\,\mathrm{L}$ are used to calculate a density in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, by what factor should the numerical value of the volume in liters be multiplied?

- $1000$
- $0.001$
- $1$
- $0.01$

## Explanation

To obtain density in kilograms per cubic meter, the volume must be expressed in cubic meters. A liter is a cubic decimeter and $1\,\mathrm{dm}=10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m}$, so $1\,\mathrm{L}=(10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m})^3=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Therefore multiply the number of liters by $0.001$, giving $6\,\mathrm{L}=0.006\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The factor $1000$ applies to the reverse conversion, from cubic meters to liters. The factor $0.01$ comes from treating the liter as one hundredth of a cubic meter.
