A rectangle has a fixed area. One side is expressed in a unit one-tenth the size of the original unit, keeping the physical length of the side the same. What changes?

- The numerical value of that side is multiplied by one hundred.
- The numerical value of that side is multiplied by ten.
- The physical area is multiplied by ten.
- The physical dimension of the side becomes that of an area.

## Explanation

The side is a length, so its numerical value changes according to the linear factor between the two units. If the new unit is one-tenth the size of the old one, each old unit contains ten new ones, so the numerical value of the side increases by a factor of ten, as in $3\,\mathrm{cm}=30\,\mathrm{mm}$. The factor of one hundred applies to areas, specifically to the numerical value of the area in the corresponding square units. The physical quantities, the side length and the area, stay the same.
