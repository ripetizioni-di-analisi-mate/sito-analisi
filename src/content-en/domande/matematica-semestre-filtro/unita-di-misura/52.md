Which relation explains why $1\,\mathrm{mL}$ and $1\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ represent the same volume?

- $1\,\mathrm{L}=1\,\mathrm{m^3}$.
- $1\,\mathrm{L}=1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.
- $1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=10\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.
- $1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=100\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.

## Explanation

One decimeter equals $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, so a cubic decimeter equals $(10\,\mathrm{cm})^3=1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. A liter is a cubic decimeter, so $1\,\mathrm{L}=1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Dividing both sides by one thousand gives $1\,\mathrm{mL}=1\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ because a milliliter is one thousandth of a liter. The equalities with $10\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and $100\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ use the linear and area factors in place of the cube. The equality $1\,\mathrm{L}=1\,\mathrm{m^3}$ is false because a cubic meter contains one thousand liters.
