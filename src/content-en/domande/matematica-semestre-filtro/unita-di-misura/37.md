The kinetic energy of an object of mass $m$ and speed $v$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. If mass, length, and time are denoted by $M,L,T$, what is the dimension of kinetic energy?

- $ML^2T^{-2}$
- $MLT^{-2}$
- $ML^2T^{-1}$
- $MLT^{-1}$

## Explanation

The coefficient $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is dimensionless and makes no contribution to the dimension. Velocity has dimension $LT^{-1}$, so its square has dimension $L^2T^{-2}$. Therefore
$$
\left[\frac{1}{2}mv^2\right]=M\cdot\left(LT^{-1}\right)^2=ML^2T^{-2}.
$$
The answer $ML^2T^{-1}$ comes from squaring only the length. The answer $MLT^{-1}$ is the dimension of $mv$, without squaring the velocity. The answer $MLT^{-2}$ is the dimension of force.
