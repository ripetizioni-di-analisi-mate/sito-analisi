What is the unit of the product ($5\,\mathrm{m/s}$) · ($8\,\mathrm{s}$)?

- $\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\mathrm{s/m}$
- $\mathrm{m/s}$
- $\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

When multiplying two quantities, multiply both their numerical values and their units, so
$$
5\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}\cdot8\,\mathrm{s}=40\,\frac{\mathrm{m}\cdot\mathrm{s}}{\mathrm{s}}=40\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
Seconds appear once in the numerator and once in the denominator, so they cancel and meters remain. The result is a length, the distance traveled in $8\,\mathrm{s}$ at a constant speed of $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The unit $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ would result from dividing speed by time instead of multiplying.
