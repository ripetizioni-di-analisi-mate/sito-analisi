A container holds water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the pressure increase due to the water column at a depth of $50\,\mathrm{cm}$?

- $5\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $500\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $5\cdot10^{4}\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $5000\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

The hydrostatic pressure increase is $\rho gh$. With density in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and acceleration in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, the product is in pascals if the height difference is also in meters, so $h=50\,\mathrm{cm}=0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Therefore
$$
\rho gh=1000\cdot10\cdot0.50\,\mathrm{Pa}=5000\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$
The value $5\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ comes from substituting $h=50$ without converting centimeters. The value $500\,\mathrm{Pa}$ comes from the conversion $50\,\mathrm{cm}=0.05\,\mathrm{m}$, which is off by a factor of ten.
