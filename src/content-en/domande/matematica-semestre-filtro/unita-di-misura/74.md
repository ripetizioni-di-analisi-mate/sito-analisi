How many grams of a solute with molar mass $50\,\mathrm{g/mol}$ are needed to prepare $1.5\,\mathrm{L}$ of solution at $0.04\,\mathrm{mol/L}$?

- $2\,\mathrm{g}$
- $0.06\,\mathrm{g}$
- $3\,\mathrm{g}$
- $30\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The required amount of solute is the concentration times the volume, so $n=0.04\,\mathrm{mol/L}\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{L}=0.06\,\mathrm{mol}$. The mass is obtained by multiplying the amount of substance by the molar mass, giving
$$
m=0.06\,\mathrm{mol}\cdot50\,\mathrm{g/mol}=3\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
The value $0.06\,\mathrm{g}$ is the amount of substance with the wrong unit, obtained without using the molar mass. The value $2\,\mathrm{g}$ comes from multiplying by $50$ only the concentration, without accounting for the volume.
