During an experiment, the temperature of a sample changes from $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $35\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$. What is the temperature change in kelvins?

- $15\,\mathrm{K}$
- $288.15\,\mathrm{K}$
- $308.15\,\mathrm{K}$
- $293.15\,\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

Each temperature in kelvins is obtained by adding $273.15$ to its value in degrees Celsius. When taking the difference, the term $273.15$ occurs in both values and cancels, so
$$
\Delta T=(35+273.15)\,\mathrm{K}-(20+273.15)\,\mathrm{K}=15\,\mathrm{K}.
$$
A change of one degree Celsius therefore equals a change of one kelvin. The value $288.15\,\mathrm{K}$ comes from adding $273.15$ to the difference as if it were a temperature reading. The values $293.15\,\mathrm{K}$ and $308.15\,\mathrm{K}$ are the initial and final temperatures.
