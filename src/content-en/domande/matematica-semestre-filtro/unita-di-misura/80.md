A cube has edge length $0.4\,\mathrm{dm}$. What is its volume in milliliters?

- $64\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $4\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $16\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $400\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

Since $1\,\mathrm{dm}=10\,\mathrm{cm}$, the edge length is $0.4\,\mathrm{dm}=4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The cube’s volume is the cube of its edge length, so
$$
V=(4\,\mathrm{cm})^3=64\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
$$
A milliliter equals one cubic centimeter, so the volume is $64\,\mathrm{mL}$. The value $16\,\mathrm{mL}$ comes from squaring the edge length, which gives the area of one face. The value $4\,\mathrm{mL}$ is the numerical value of the edge length in centimeters. The value $400\,\mathrm{mL}$ comes from applying the factor $1000$ to the edge length instead of the volume.
