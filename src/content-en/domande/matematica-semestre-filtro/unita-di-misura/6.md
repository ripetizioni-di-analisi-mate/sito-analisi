A solution contains $0.3\,\mathrm{mol}$ in a final volume of $500\,\mathrm{mL}$. Calculate the concentration in $\mathrm{mol/L}$.

- $0.0006\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $6\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $\dfrac{5}{3}\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.6\,\mathrm{mol/L}$

## Explanation

Molar concentration is the amount of solute in moles divided by the final volume of solution in liters. Since $500\,\mathrm{mL}=0.500\,\mathrm{L}$, we have
$$
c=\frac{0.3\,\mathrm{mol}}{0.500\,\mathrm{L}}=0.6\,\mathrm{mol/L}.
$$
The value $0.0006\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ comes from dividing by $500$ without converting milliliters to liters. The value $\dfrac{5}{3}\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ comes from inverting the ratio and dividing volume by amount of substance.
