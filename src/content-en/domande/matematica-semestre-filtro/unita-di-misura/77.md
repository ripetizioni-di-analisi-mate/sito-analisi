A liquid of density $1.2\,\mathrm{g/mL}$ and another of density $0.8\,\mathrm{g/mL}$ each occupy $50\,\mathrm{mL}$. By how many grams do their masses differ?

- $0.4\,\mathrm{g}$
- $40\,\mathrm{g}$
- $20\,\mathrm{g}$
- $100\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The mass of each liquid is its density times its volume. We have $m_1=1.2\,\mathrm{g/mL}\cdot50\,\mathrm{mL}=60\,\mathrm{g}$ and $m_2=0.8\,\mathrm{g/mL}\cdot50\,\mathrm{mL}=40\,\mathrm{g}$, so
$$
m_1-m_2=60\,\mathrm{g}-40\,\mathrm{g}=20\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
Since the volume is the same for both, $50\cdot(1.2-0.8)\,\mathrm{g}$ gives the same result. The value $0.4\,\mathrm{g}$ is the difference in densities without multiplication by the volume. The value $100\,\mathrm{g}$ is the sum of the two masses.
