A homogeneous solution has mass concentration $12\,\mathrm{g/L}$. How many grams of solute are present in $250\,\mathrm{mL}$?

- $3\,\mathrm{g}$
- $0.3\,\mathrm{g}$
- $12\,\mathrm{g}$
- $48\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

Express the volume in liters, the unit in the denominator of the concentration, so $250\,\mathrm{mL}=0.250\,\mathrm{L}$. The solute mass is concentration times volume, so
$$
m=12\,\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\mathrm{L}}\cdot0.250\,\mathrm{L}=3\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
Liters cancel and grams remain. The value $48\,\mathrm{g}$ comes from dividing the concentration by the volume. The value $0.3\,\mathrm{g}$ comes from the conversion $250\,\mathrm{mL}=0.025\,\mathrm{L}$, which is off by a factor of ten. The value $12\,\mathrm{g}$ is the mass contained in one full liter.
