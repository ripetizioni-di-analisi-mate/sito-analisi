A rectangular box has internal dimensions $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, $3\,\mathrm{cm}$, and $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. How many milliliters does it hold when full?

- $9\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $24\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $240\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

The internal volume of the box is the product of the three dimensions, so
$$
V=2\cdot3\cdot4\,\mathrm{cm^3}=24\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
$$
A liter is a cubic decimeter, or $1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, and a milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, so $1\,\mathrm{mL}=1\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. It follows that the container holds $24\,\mathrm{mL}$. The value $9\,\mathrm{mL}$ comes from adding the three dimensions instead of multiplying. The values $2.4\,\mathrm{mL}$ and $240\,\mathrm{mL}$ come from incorrectly introducing a factor of ten between cubic centimeters and milliliters.
