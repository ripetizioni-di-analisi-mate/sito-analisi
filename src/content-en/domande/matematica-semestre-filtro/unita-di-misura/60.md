The prescribed dose of a drug is $5\,\mathrm{mg}$ per kilogram of body mass, and the patient has a mass of $70\,\mathrm{kg}$. The drug is available in a solution at $25\,\mathrm{mg/mL}$. How many milliliters of solution contain the dose?

- $2.8\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $0.014\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $14\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $8750\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

The mass of the drug is the dose per kilogram times the patient’s mass, so $m=5\,\mathrm{mg/kg}\cdot70\,\mathrm{kg}=350\,\mathrm{mg}$. The volume of solution containing this mass is the drug mass divided by the concentration, so
$$
V=\frac{350\,\mathrm{mg}}{25\,\mathrm{mg/mL}}=14\,\mathrm{mL}.
$$
Milligrams cancel and milliliters remain. The value $2.8\,\mathrm{mL}$ comes from dividing the patient’s mass by the concentration without calculating the dose. The value $0.014\,\mathrm{mL}$ is the correct volume in liters labeled with the wrong unit.
