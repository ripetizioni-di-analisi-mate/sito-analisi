A $5\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample of a solution at $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is brought to $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ by adding only water. How many milligrams of solute does each milliliter of the resulting solution contain?

- $0.002\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $40\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $8\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $2\,\mathrm{mg}$

## Explanation

The initial sample has volume $5\,\mathrm{mL}=0.005\,\mathrm{L}$, so it contains $8\,\mathrm{g/L}\cdot0.005\,\mathrm{L}=0.04\,\mathrm{g}=40\,\mathrm{mg}$ of solute. The added water does not change the mass of the solute, so the same $40\,\mathrm{mg}$ are spread over $20\,\mathrm{mL}$.
$$
\frac{40\,\mathrm{mg}}{20\,\mathrm{mL}}=2\,\mathrm{mg/mL}.
$$
Therefore each milliliter of the resulting solution contains $2\,\mathrm{mg}$ of solute. The value $40\,\mathrm{mg}$ is the mass of solute in the whole solution, and $8\,\mathrm{mg}$ comes from ignoring the dilution, because $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is equivalent to $8\,\mathrm{mg/mL}$. The value $0.002\,\mathrm{mg}$ comes from writing in milligrams the number $0.002$ that expresses the concentration $2\,\mathrm{g/L}$ in grams per milliliter.
