A $5\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample of solution is diluted with water to $20\,\mathrm{mL}$. The initial concentration is $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$. What is the final concentration in grams per liter?

- $0.5$
- $32$
- $8$
- $2$

## Explanation

Only water is added during dilution, so the solute mass is conserved and $c_1V_1=c_2V_2$. The volume increases from $5\,\mathrm{mL}$ to $20\,\mathrm{mL}$, a factor of four, so
$$
c_2=8\,\mathrm{g/L}\cdot\frac{5}{20}=2\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The volume ratio is dimensionless, so the volumes can remain in milliliters. The value $32$ comes from inverting the ratio, which would increase the concentration. The value $0.5$ comes from dividing twice by $4$.
