Consider the formulas $v=at$ and $v=2at$ for an object starting from rest. What can dimensional analysis alone establish?

- Only the first has the dimension of velocity.
- Both have the dimension of velocity.
- Only the second has the dimension of velocity.
- Both are physically correct under all conditions.

## Explanation

Velocity has dimension $LT^{-1}$. The product $at$ has dimension $LT^{-2}\cdot T=LT^{-1}$, so $v=at$ is dimensionally consistent. The factor $2$ is dimensionless and leaves the dimension unchanged, so $v=2at$ also passes the check. Dimensional analysis therefore provides a necessary condition and leaves numerical coefficients undetermined. For an object starting from rest at constant acceleration, $v=at$ holds, and the coefficient $1$ follows from the equation of motion.
