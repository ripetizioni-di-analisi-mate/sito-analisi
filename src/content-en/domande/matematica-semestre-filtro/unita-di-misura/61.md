In $3\,\mathrm{s}$ an object’s speed increases from $36$ to $54\,\mathrm{km/h}$ as it moves in the same direction. What is its average acceleration?

- $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{5}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Average acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time interval. Express velocity in meters per second to obtain the result in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The change is $54-36=18\,\mathrm{km/h}$, which divided by $3.6$ gives $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Therefore
$$
a=\frac{5\,\mathrm{m/s}}{3\,\mathrm{s}}=\frac{5}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The value $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ comes from dividing $18$ by $3$ without converting. The value $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ comes from inverting the quotient. The value $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is the change in velocity before dividing by time.
