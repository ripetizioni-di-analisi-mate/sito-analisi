If mass, length, and time are denoted by $M,L,T$, what is the dimension of $\text{mass}\cdot\dfrac{\text{length}}{\text{time}^2}$?

- $MLT^{-1}$
- $MLT^{-2}$
- $ML^2T^{-2}$
- $ML^{-1}T^{-2}$

## Explanation

Dimensions combine according to the rules for powers. Mass contributes $M$, length in the numerator contributes $L$, and time squared in the denominator contributes $T^{-2}$, so
$$
M\cdot\frac{L}{T^{2}}=MLT^{-2}.
$$
This is the dimension of force, the product of mass and acceleration. The answer $MLT^{-1}$ overlooks the exponent $2$ on time and gives the dimension of mass times velocity. The answer $ML^{-1}T^{-2}$ puts length in the denominator.
