Volumetric flow rate is calculated by multiplying the cross-sectional area by the average velocity component perpendicular to it. Why does the result have dimension $L^3T^{-1}$?

- Area has dimension $L^3$ and velocity has dimension $T^{-1}$.
- Velocity has dimension $LT$ and time cancels.
- Area has dimension $L^2$ and velocity has dimension $LT^{-1}$.
- The product of area and velocity is a length.

## Explanation

When two quantities are multiplied, their dimensions multiply and the exponents of each common base dimension add. Area has dimension $L^2$ and velocity has dimension $LT^{-1}$, so
$$
[A\,v]=L^{2}\cdot LT^{-1}=L^{3}T^{-1}.
$$
The result is a volume divided by a time, the dimension of flow rate. Assigning dimensions $L^3$ to area and $T^{-1}$ to velocity would give the same product, but those are the dimensions of volume and frequency.
