A volume of $200\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a solution at $0.30\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ is mixed with $50\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a solution of the same solute at $0.80\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. Volumes are additive and the solute is conserved. What is the final concentration?

- $0.55\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $1.10\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{mol/L}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{mol/L}$

## Explanation

The amount of solute in the mixture is the sum of the amounts in the two solutions. With volumes in liters, we have $n=0.200\cdot0.30\,\mathrm{mol}+0.050\cdot0.80\,\mathrm{mol}=0.100\,\mathrm{mol}$. Since volumes are additive, the final volume is $0.250\,\mathrm{L}$, so
$$
c=\frac{0.100\,\mathrm{mol}}{0.250\,\mathrm{L}}=0.40\,\mathrm{mol/L}.
$$
The value $0.55\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ is the simple average of the two concentrations, which ignores the different volumes of the portions. The value $0.50\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ comes from dividing only by the volume of the first solution.
