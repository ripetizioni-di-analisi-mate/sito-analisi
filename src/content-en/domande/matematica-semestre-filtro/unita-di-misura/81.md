An acceleration is expressed in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$. By what factor should its numerical value be multiplied to express it in $\mathrm{cm/s^2}$?

- $10^4$
- $10^{-2}$
- $1$
- $100$

## Explanation

The seconds squared in the denominator stay the same, so only the length unit in the numerator is converted. Since $1\,\mathrm{m}=100\,\mathrm{cm}$, we have
$$
1\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s^2}}=100\,\frac{\mathrm{cm}}{\mathrm{s^2}},
$$
so multiply the numerical value by $100$. The meter occurs to the first power, so the linear factor also remains to the first power. The factor $10^{4}$ comes from applying the time exponent $2$ to the length as well. The factor $10^{-2}$ applies to the reverse conversion.
