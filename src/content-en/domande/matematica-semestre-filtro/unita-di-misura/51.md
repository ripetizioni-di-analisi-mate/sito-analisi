A full pipe has cross-sectional area $12\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The fluid velocity is uniform and perpendicular to the cross section, with magnitude $25\,\mathrm{cm/s}$. What is the flow rate in milliliters per second?

- $300\,\mathrm{mL/s}$
- $30\,\mathrm{mL/s}$
- $37\,\mathrm{mL/s}$
- $3000\,\mathrm{mL/s}$

## Explanation

When the velocity is uniform and perpendicular to the cross section, the flow rate is the area times the speed, so
$$
Q=A\,v=12\,\mathrm{cm^2}\cdot25\,\mathrm{cm/s}=300\,\mathrm{cm^3/s}.
$$
Multiplying the units gives cubic centimeters per second, a volume divided by a time. Since $1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=1\,\mathrm{mL}$, the flow rate is $300\,\mathrm{mL/s}$. The value $37\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ comes from adding area and speed, which have different dimensions. The values $30\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ and $3000\,\mathrm{mL/s}$ come from incorrectly introducing a factor of ten between cubic centimeters and milliliters.
