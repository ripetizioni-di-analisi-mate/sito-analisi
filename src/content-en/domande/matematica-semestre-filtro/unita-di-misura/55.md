A cyclist covers $1.2\,\mathrm{km}$ in $5\,\mathrm{min}$ and then another $600\,\mathrm{m}$ in $1\,\mathrm{min}$, traveling in the same direction throughout. What is the average velocity over the entire trip, in meters per second?

- $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $300\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Average velocity is total displacement divided by total time. The displacement is $1200\,\mathrm{m}+600\,\mathrm{m}=1800\,\mathrm{m}$ and the time is $6\,\mathrm{min}=360\,\mathrm{s}$, so
$$
v=\frac{1800\,\mathrm{m}}{360\,\mathrm{s}}=5\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the arithmetic mean of the speeds on the two legs, $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which ignores the different durations of the legs. The value $300\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from dividing by $6$ without converting minutes to seconds. The number $18$ is the average speed in kilometers per hour.
