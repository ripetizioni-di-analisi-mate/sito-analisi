A solute is dissolved in water. Which volume is needed to calculate the mass concentration of the resulting solution?

- The volume of water measured before the solute was dissolved.
- The volume of the solute alone.
- The final volume of the solution.
- The difference between the final volume and the initial volume of water.

## Explanation

Mass concentration is defined as $c_m=\dfrac{m_{\mathrm{s}}}{V}$, where $m_{\mathrm{s}}$ is the mass of solute and $V$ is the final volume of the solution. When the solute dissolves, the volume of the solution generally differs from the original volume of water. Therefore use the volume measured for the resulting solution. The initial volume of water, the volume of the solute alone, and the difference between the volumes each describe only part of the system.
