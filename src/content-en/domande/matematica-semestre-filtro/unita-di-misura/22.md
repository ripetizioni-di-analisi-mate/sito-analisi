A $500\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample of solution has mass $540\,\mathrm{g}$ and contains $20\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute. What is the density of the solution in grams per liter?

- $1080\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $40\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $1.08\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $540\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

The density of the solution is the total mass of the solution divided by its volume. Since $500\,\mathrm{mL}=0.500\,\mathrm{L}$, we have
$$
\rho=\frac{540\,\mathrm{g}}{0.500\,\mathrm{L}}=1080\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The value $40\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is the mass concentration of the solute, which has the same unit but includes only the solute mass in the numerator. The value $1.08\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is the density in grams per milliliter, with the volume left in milliliters. The value $540\,\mathrm{g/L}$ comes from treating the mass of the sample as the mass in one liter instead of half a liter.
