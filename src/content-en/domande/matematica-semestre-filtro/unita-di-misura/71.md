An experiment lasts $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of an hour and then continues for another $90\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its total duration in seconds?

- $8100\,\mathrm{s}$
- $2790\,\mathrm{s}$
- $4590\,\mathrm{s}$
- $135\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

One hour equals $60\,\mathrm{min}$, so three-quarters of an hour equals $\dfrac{3}{4}\cdot60\,\mathrm{min}=45\,\mathrm{min}$. Each minute equals $60\,\mathrm{s}$, so $45\,\mathrm{min}=2700\,\mathrm{s}$. Adding the second part of the experiment gives
$$
2700\,\mathrm{s}+90\,\mathrm{s}=2790\,\mathrm{s}.
$$
The value $8100\,\mathrm{s}$ comes from reading the duration of the second part as $90$ minutes. The value $135\,\mathrm{s}$ comes from adding $45$ minutes and $90$ seconds as if they had the same unit.
