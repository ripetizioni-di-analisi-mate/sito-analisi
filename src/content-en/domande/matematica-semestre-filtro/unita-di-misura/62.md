A blood sample contains $5\cdot10^{6}$ red blood cells per cubic millimeter. How many red blood cells are there in $5\,\mathrm{L}$ of blood at the same concentration?

- $2.5\cdot10^{10}$
- $2.5\cdot10^{7}$
- $2.5\cdot10^{16}$
- $2.5\cdot10^{13}$

## Explanation

A liter is a cubic decimeter and a decimeter equals $100\,\mathrm{mm}$, so $1\,\mathrm{L}=(10^{2}\,\mathrm{mm})^3=10^{6}\,\mathrm{mm^3}$. Therefore $5\,\mathrm{L}=5\cdot10^{6}\,\mathrm{mm^3}$, and the number of red blood cells, the concentration times the volume, is
$$
5\cdot10^{6}\cdot5\cdot10^{6}=25\cdot10^{12}=2.5\cdot10^{13}.
$$
The value $2.5\cdot10^{10}$ comes from using $1\,\mathrm{L}=10^{3}\,\mathrm{mm^3}$, which confuses a cubic millimeter with a milliliter. The value $2.5\cdot10^{7}$ comes from multiplying by $5$ without converting liters.
