In the formula $z(t)=z_0e^{-\lambda t}$, $t$ is measured in seconds and the exponent must be dimensionless. What unit can $\lambda$ have?

- $\mathrm{s}$
- $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$
- $\mathrm{s^{-2}}$
- $\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The argument of an exponential is dimensionless, so the product $\lambda t$ is dimensionless. Since $t$ is measured in seconds, $\lambda$ has the reciprocal unit, $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, and indeed $\mathrm{s^{-1}}\cdot\mathrm{s}=1$. With $\lambda$ in seconds, the product would have unit $\mathrm{s^2}$. With $\lambda$ in $\mathrm{s^{-2}}$, the unit $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ would remain. The unit $\mathrm{m/s}$ would leave a meter in the exponent that no other unit could cancel.
