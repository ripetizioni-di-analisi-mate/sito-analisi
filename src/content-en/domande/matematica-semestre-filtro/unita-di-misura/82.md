A car travels $20\,\mathrm{km}$ on one liter of fuel. How many liters does it use per $100\,\mathrm{km}$?

- $0.2\,\mathrm{L}$
- $2000\,\mathrm{L}$
- $5\,\mathrm{L}$
- $20\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

Fuel consumption per kilometer is the reciprocal of the distance traveled per liter, so it is $\dfrac{1\,\mathrm{L}}{20\,\mathrm{km}}=0.05\,\mathrm{L/km}$. Over $100\,\mathrm{km}$, the consumption is multiplied by one hundred, giving
$$
0.05\,\frac{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{km}}\cdot100\,\mathrm{km}=5\,\mathrm{L}.
$$
Kilometers cancel and liters remain. The value $2000\,\mathrm{L}$ comes from multiplying the distance per liter by $100$ without first taking its reciprocal. The value $0.2\,\mathrm{L}$ comes from the inverted ratio $\dfrac{20}{100}$. The value $20\,\mathrm{L}$ comes from confusing distance per unit of fuel with fuel consumption per unit of distance.
