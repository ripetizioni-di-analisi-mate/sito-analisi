Which unit can replace $\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ in a density measurement without changing its numerical value?

- $\mathrm{kg/L}$
- $\mathrm{g/L}$
- $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $\mathrm{mg/L}$

## Explanation

Since $1\,\mathrm{kg}=10^{3}\,\mathrm{g}$ and $1\,\mathrm{L}=10^{3}\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, we have
$$
1\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{L}}=\frac{10^{3}\,\mathrm{g}}{10^{3}\,\mathrm{cm^3}}=1\,\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\mathrm{cm^3}}.
$$
The two factors of one thousand cancel, so the numerical value stays the same. For example, water has density $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}=1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$. By comparison, $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ equals $1000\,\mathrm{g/L}$ and $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, so conversion to either of those units multiplies the numerical value by one thousand. Conversion to $\mathrm{mg/L}$ multiplies it by one million.
