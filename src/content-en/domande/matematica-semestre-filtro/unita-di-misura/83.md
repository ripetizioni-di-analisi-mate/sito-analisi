A volume of $15\,\mathrm{mL}$ is taken from a homogeneous solution at $0.4\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. Only solvent is added, without loss of solute, to give a final volume of $60\,\mathrm{mL}$. How many moles of solute are in the entire resulting solution?

- $0.0015\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $0.024\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $6\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $0.006\,\mathrm{mol}$

## Explanation

Only solvent is added during dilution, so the final solution contains the same number of moles of solute as the sample. With the volume in liters, $15\,\mathrm{mL}=0.015\,\mathrm{L}$, so
$$
n=0.4\,\mathrm{mol/L}\cdot0.015\,\mathrm{L}=0.006\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$
The final volume $60\,\mathrm{mL}$ determines the final concentration and leaves the amount of solute unchanged. The value $0.024\,\mathrm{mol}$ comes from multiplying the initial concentration by the final volume. The value $0.0015\,\mathrm{mol}$ comes from using the final concentration, $0.1\,\mathrm{mol/L}$, with the sample volume.
