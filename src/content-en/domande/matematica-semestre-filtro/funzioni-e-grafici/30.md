The total mass of a container and its liquid is $M(V)=20+2V$, with $M$ in grams and $V$ in milliliters. If the volume changes from $10\,\mathrm{mL}$ to $20\,\mathrm{mL}$, by what factor is the total mass multiplied?

- $2$
- $1.5$
- $3$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

The two total masses are $M(10)=20+20=40\,\mathrm{g}$ and $M(20)=20+40=60\,\mathrm{g}$. The factor is their ratio, $\dfrac{60}{40}=1.5$. The total mass does not double because the relationship is affine and the intercept $20\,\mathrm{g}$, the container’s mass, is independent of the volume. Only the liquid mass doubles, from $20\,\mathrm{g}$ to $40\,\mathrm{g}$. The factor $2$ treats total mass as directly proportional to volume, while $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is the reciprocal ratio.
