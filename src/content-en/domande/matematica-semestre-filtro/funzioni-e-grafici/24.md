For $5\,\mathrm{s}$, an object’s velocity remains between $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and its velocity–time graph is nonlinear. What bounds can we place on its displacement?

- Between $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $7\,\mathrm{m}$
- Between $15\,\mathrm{m}$ and $35\,\mathrm{m}$
- Between $\dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\dfrac{7}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- Between $25\,\mathrm{m}$ and $35\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The region under the velocity–time graph contains the rectangle of height $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and is contained in the rectangle of height $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$, both with base $5\,\mathrm{s}$. The rectangles have areas $(3\,\mathrm{m/s})(5\,\mathrm{s})=15\,\mathrm{m}$ and $(7\,\mathrm{m/s})(5\,\mathrm{s})=35\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus the displacement lies between $15\,\mathrm{m}$ and $35\,\mathrm{m}$ even without knowing the shape of the graph. The bounds $3$ and $7\,\mathrm{m}$ confuse velocities with displacements.
