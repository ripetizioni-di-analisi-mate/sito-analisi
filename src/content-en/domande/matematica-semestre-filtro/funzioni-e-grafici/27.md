An object’s velocity decreases linearly from $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to zero in $4\,\mathrm{s}$. It then remains at rest for another $4\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its average velocity over the full $8\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

While the object slows down, the region under the velocity–time graph is a triangle with base $4\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the displacement is $\dfrac{(4\,\mathrm{s})(8\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=16\,\mathrm{m}$. While the object is at rest, the graph coincides with the axis and the displacement is zero. The average velocity is the total displacement divided by the total time:
$$
v_m=\frac{16\,\mathrm{m}}{8\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the average velocity during braking alone because it excludes the time spent at rest.
