Two liquids have densities $0.8\,\mathrm{g/mL}$ and $1.2\,\mathrm{g/mL}$. Equal masses of the two liquids are taken. What is the ratio of the volume of the first to the volume of the second?

- $1.5$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $0.4$
- $2.25$

## Explanation

The volume is $V=\dfrac{m}{\rho}$, so at fixed mass the volume is inversely proportional to the density. The common mass cancels in the ratio:
$$
\frac{V_1}{V_2}=\frac{\frac{m}{\rho_1}}{\frac{m}{\rho_2}}=\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{1.2}{0.8}=1.5.
$$
Thus the less dense liquid occupies a greater volume. The value $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is the density ratio, which would give the mass ratio at fixed volume. The value $0.4$ is the difference between the densities.
