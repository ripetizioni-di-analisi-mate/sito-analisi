A fixed amount of gas at constant temperature satisfies $p=\dfrac{120}{V}$, with $p$ in kilopascals and $V$ in liters. By how much does the pressure decrease as the volume goes from $2$ to $3\,\mathrm{L}$, and by how much as it goes from $3$ to $4\,\mathrm{L}$?

- $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$
- $60\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $40\,\mathrm{kPa}$

## Explanation

The pressures are $p(2)=60\,\mathrm{kPa}$, $p(3)=40\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and $p(4)=30\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The decreases are therefore $60-40=20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ in the first step and $40-30=10\,\mathrm{kPa}$ in the second. Equal increases in volume produce different decreases in pressure because the slope changes along the curve for an inverse proportion. The pair of equal decreases treats the relationship as affine. The pair $60$ and $40$ gives the pressures themselves instead of their differences.
