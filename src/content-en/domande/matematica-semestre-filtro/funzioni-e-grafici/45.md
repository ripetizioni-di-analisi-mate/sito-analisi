The temperature of a cooling liquid is described by the model $T(t)=20+60\cdot0.9^{\,t}$, with $T$ in degrees Celsius and $t$ in minutes, obtained from measurements taken between $t=0$ and $t=8\,\mathrm{min}$. Which statement about the value $T(10)$ is correct?

- The value $T(10)$ is an extrapolation outside the domain of the model.
- The value $T(10)$ is reliable, because the formula is defined for every $t$.
- The value $T(10)$ is confirmed by the measurements.
- The domain of the model is the interval $[0,10]$.

## Explanation

The model has been compared with measurements only for $0\le t\le 8$, so its domain is the interval $[0,8]$, in minutes, even though the formula can be evaluated for every real number $t$. Since $t=10$ does not belong to this interval, the value $T(10)$ is an extrapolation. The fact that the formula returns a number for $t=10$ concerns the calculation and does not ensure that the law also holds at ten minutes. The measurements stop at eight minutes, so the interval $[0,10]$ is not the domain of the model.
