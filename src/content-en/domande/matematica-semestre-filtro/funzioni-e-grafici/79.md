On a velocity–time graph, $v$ varies linearly. Its value is $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at time $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at time $t=8\,\mathrm{s}$. When does it reach zero?

- $4\,\mathrm{s}$
- $6\,\mathrm{s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{s}$
- $8\,\mathrm{s}$

## Explanation

The slope of the graph is
$$
a=\frac{2-(-4)}{8-2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=\frac{6}{6}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
Starting from $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$, the velocity must increase by $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to reach zero. At this slope that takes $4\,\mathrm{s}$, so the velocity reaches zero at $t=2+4=6\,\mathrm{s}$. The value $4\,\mathrm{s}$ is only the elapsed time and omits the starting time. The value $5\,\mathrm{s}$ is the midpoint of the interval, which would be the zero crossing only if the endpoint velocities were opposites.
