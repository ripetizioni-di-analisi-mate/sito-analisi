An object moves at $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $11\,\mathrm{s}$, then at $-8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $12\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its signed displacement, in meters?

- $-184$
- $-8$
- $184$
- $8$

## Explanation

The displacement is the sum of the signed areas on the velocity–time graph. The first interval contributes $(8\,\mathrm{m/s})(11\,\mathrm{s})=88\,\mathrm{m}$ and the second contributes $(-8\,\mathrm{m/s})(12\,\mathrm{s})=-96\,\mathrm{m}$. The displacement is therefore $88-96=-8\,\mathrm{m}$, and the object ends up $8\,\mathrm{m}$ behind its starting position. The value $184$ is the distance traveled, the sum of the absolute areas. The value $8$ has the wrong sign.
