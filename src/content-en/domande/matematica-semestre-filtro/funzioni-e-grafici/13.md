Between $0$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity varies linearly from $6$ to $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate the displacement.

- $8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $16\,\mathrm{m}$
- $-8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity varies linearly, so the average velocity over the interval is the arithmetic mean of the endpoint values:
$$
v_m=\frac{6\,\mathrm{m/s}+(-2\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The displacement is therefore $(2\,\mathrm{m/s})(4\,\mathrm{s})=8\,\mathrm{m}$. Signed areas give the same result. The velocity is zero at $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$, so the triangle above the axis has area $9\,\mathrm{m}$, the triangle below it has area $1\,\mathrm{m}$, and $9-1=8$. The value $10\,\mathrm{m}$ adds the magnitudes of the two areas, giving the distance traveled.
