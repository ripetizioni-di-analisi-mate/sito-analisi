At time zero an object is at position $5\,\mathrm{m}$ with velocity $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its velocity decreases linearly to zero in $3\,\mathrm{s}$. Where is the object at $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $23\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The displacement is the area under the velocity–time graph, here a triangle with base $3\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$:
$$
\Delta s=\frac{(3\,\mathrm{s})(6\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=9\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The final position is the initial position plus the displacement, so $5\,\mathrm{m}+9\,\mathrm{m}=14\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $9\,\mathrm{m}$ mistakes the displacement for the position. The values $18\,\mathrm{m}$ and $23\,\mathrm{m}$ use the area of the rectangle $(3\,\mathrm{s})(6\,\mathrm{m/s})$ instead of that of the triangle.
