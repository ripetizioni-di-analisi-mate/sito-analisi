An object travels $30\,\mathrm{m}$ at a constant velocity of $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and then another $30\,\mathrm{m}$ in the same direction at a constant velocity of $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is its average velocity over the whole trip?

- $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

Average velocity is total displacement divided by total time. The two parts of the trip take $\dfrac{30\,\mathrm{m}}{3\,\mathrm{m/s}}=10\,\mathrm{s}$ and $\dfrac{30\,\mathrm{m}}{6\,\mathrm{m/s}}=5\,\mathrm{s}$. Therefore
$$
v_m=\frac{60\,\mathrm{m}}{15\,\mathrm{s}}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The value $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the arithmetic mean of the two velocities. This would equal the average velocity if the two parts of the trip took the same time. Here the slower part takes twice as long and therefore has greater weight.
