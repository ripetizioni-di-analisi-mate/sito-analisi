Three models all give $6$ at $x=3$. Their formulas are $y=2x$, $y=\dfrac{18}{x}$, and $y=\dfrac{2}{3}x^2$. A second measurement gives $y=12$ at $x=6$. Which model fits both measurements?

- $y=\dfrac{18}{x}$
- $y=\dfrac{2}{3}x^2$
- All three models.
- $y=2x$

## Explanation

At $x=6$ the three models predict different values. The direct proportion gives $2\cdot 6=12$, the inverse proportion gives $\dfrac{18}{6}=3$, and the quadratic model gives $\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot 36=24$. Only the prediction of $y=2x$ matches the measurement $12$. The measurement at $x=3$ alone cannot distinguish the models because each coefficient can be found from that one point. Doubling $x$ quadruples $y$ in the quadratic model and halves it in the inverse model, so the second measurement distinguishes them.
