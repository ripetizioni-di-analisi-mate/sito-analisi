On an object’s position–time graph, the position remains at $4\,\mathrm{m}$ for $3\,\mathrm{s}$. What does the area of the rectangle between the graph and the time axis represent?

- A displacement of $12\,\mathrm{m}$.
- An average velocity of $\dfrac{4}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- A distance traveled of $12\,\mathrm{m}$.
- A quantity of $12\,\mathrm{m\cdot s}$. The displacement is zero.

## Explanation

The area has units of vertical coordinate times horizontal coordinate, or $\mathrm{m\cdot s}$. The rectangle therefore has area $(4\,\mathrm{m})(3\,\mathrm{s})=12\,\mathrm{m\cdot s}$, which does not have units of length. The displacement is the final minus the initial position, $4\,\mathrm{m}-4\,\mathrm{m}=0$, because the object is at rest. The answers containing $12\,\mathrm{m}$ interpret the area on a position–time graph as if it were the area on a velocity–time graph.
