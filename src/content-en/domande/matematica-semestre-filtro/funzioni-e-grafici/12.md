An object’s velocity is $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the average acceleration between these times.

- $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
- $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Explanation

Average acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the elapsed time. The change is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}-(-2\,\mathrm{m/s})=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the elapsed time is $5\,\mathrm{s}-2\,\mathrm{s}=3\,\mathrm{s}$, so
$$
a_m=\frac{6\,\mathrm{m/s}}{3\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
The value $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ comes from treating $-2$ as $+2$, that is, calculating $\dfrac{4-2}{3}$. The value $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ omits division by the elapsed time, while $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ reverses the order of subtraction.
