A car brakes with constant acceleration until it stops. With the same acceleration and twice the initial speed, by what factor is the braking distance multiplied?

- $2$
- $8$
- $4$
- $\sqrt{2}$

## Explanation

On the velocity–time graph of braking, the region under the graph is a triangle with height equal to the initial speed $v_0$ and base equal to the stopping time $t_a$. With the same acceleration, the slope stays the same, so $t_a=\dfrac{v_0}{|a|}$ is proportional to $v_0$. The braking distance is the area of the triangle,
$$
\Delta s=\frac{1}{2}\,v_0\,t_a=\frac{v_0^2}{2|a|},
$$
so doubling $v_0$ multiplies the braking distance by $2^2=4$. The factor $2$ accounts only for the doubling of the height and overlooks the doubling of the base.
