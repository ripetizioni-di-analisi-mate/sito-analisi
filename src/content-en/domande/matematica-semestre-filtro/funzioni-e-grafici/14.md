Between $0$ and $6\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity varies linearly from $-3$ to $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate the distance traveled.

- $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m}$
- $15\,\mathrm{m}$
- $27\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity increases by $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $6\,\mathrm{s}$, so the slope is $\dfrac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $v(t)=-3+\dfrac{3}{2}t$. The velocity is zero when $\dfrac{3}{2}t=3$, that is, at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$. The unsigned areas of the two triangles are
$$
S_-=\frac{(2\,\mathrm{s})(3\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=3\,\mathrm{m},\qquad S_+=\frac{(4\,\mathrm{s})(6\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=12\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The distance traveled is their sum, $3+12=15\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $9\,\mathrm{m}$ is the displacement $12-3$ obtained from the signed areas.
