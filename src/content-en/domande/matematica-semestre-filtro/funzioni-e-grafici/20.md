A container of unknown mass holds a homogeneous liquid. With $10\,\mathrm{mL}$ of liquid the scale reads $30\,\mathrm{g}$, and with $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ it reads $50\,\mathrm{g}$. What is the density of the liquid?

- $3\,\mathrm{g/mL}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{g/mL}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{g/mL}$
- $2\,\mathrm{g/mL}$

## Explanation

The total mass is $M(V)=M_0+\rho V$, where $M_0$ is the mass of the container. When we subtract the two readings, $M_0$ cancels, so the density is the ratio of the changes:
$$
\rho=\frac{50\,\mathrm{g}-30\,\mathrm{g}}{20\,\mathrm{mL}-10\,\mathrm{mL}}=2\,\mathrm{g/mL}.
$$
It also follows that $M_0=30\,\mathrm{g}-20\,\mathrm{g}=10\,\mathrm{g}$. The values $3$ and $2.5\,\mathrm{g/mL}$ are ratios of total mass, including the container, to volume. The value $0.5$ is the reciprocal ratio $\dfrac{10}{20}$.
