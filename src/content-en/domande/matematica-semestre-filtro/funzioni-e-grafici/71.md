On a straight segment of a velocity–time graph, $v$ rises from $-5$ to $-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What happens to the speed?

- It increases from $1$ to $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- It stays the same because the direction does not change.
- It becomes zero at the end of the segment.
- It decreases from $5$ to $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

## Explanation

Speed is the absolute value of velocity. It is $|-5\,\mathrm{m/s}|=5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the start of the segment and $|-1\,\mathrm{m/s}|=1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the end, so it decreases from $5$ to $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The velocity stays negative throughout the segment, so it never reaches zero. The change in velocity is $+4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the average acceleration is positive, but the object slows down. The answer claiming that the speed increases confuses an increase in the signed value with an increase in its magnitude.
