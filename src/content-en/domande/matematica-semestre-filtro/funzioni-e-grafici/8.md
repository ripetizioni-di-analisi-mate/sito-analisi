The velocity increases linearly from zero to $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $5\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the displacement.

- $40\,\mathrm{m}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\dfrac{8}{5}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The velocity increases linearly from zero, so the region under the velocity–time graph is a triangle. Its base is the elapsed time $5\,\mathrm{s}$ and its height is the final velocity $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Therefore
$$
\Delta s=\frac{(5\,\mathrm{s})(8\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=20\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The value $40\,\mathrm{m}$ is the area of the rectangle and results from omitting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{8}{5}$ is velocity divided by time, which here gives the acceleration, measured in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
