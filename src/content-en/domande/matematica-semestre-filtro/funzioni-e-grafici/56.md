A homogeneous liquid has a mass of $18\,\mathrm{g}$ for a volume of $15\,\mathrm{mL}$. What volume does a mass of $30\,\mathrm{g}$ occupy at the same density?

- $20\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $27\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $36\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $25\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

The density of the liquid is
$$
\rho=\frac{18\,\mathrm{g}}{15\,\mathrm{mL}}=1.2\,\mathrm{g/mL}.
$$
At constant density the volume is $V=\dfrac{m}{\rho}$, so $30\,\mathrm{g}$ gives $V=\dfrac{30}{1.2}\,\mathrm{mL}=25\,\mathrm{mL}$. The proportion $V=15\cdot\dfrac{30}{18}\,\mathrm{mL}$ gives the same value. The value $36\,\mathrm{mL}$ multiplies the mass by the density instead of dividing. The value $27\,\mathrm{mL}$ adds the mass difference $12$ to the volume as if grams and milliliters were the same unit.
