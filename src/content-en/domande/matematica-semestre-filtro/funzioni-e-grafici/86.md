A position–time graph consists of three successive straight segments. Their slopes are $4$, $3$, and $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, respectively. How does the velocity change from one segment to the next?

- It is positive on all three segments and decreases from one segment to the next.
- It becomes negative on the last segment.
- It stays constant because the position increases.
- It increases because the position increases.

## Explanation

On a position–time graph the slope of each segment is the velocity on that segment. The slopes $4$, $3$, and $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ are all positive, so the object always moves in the positive direction. Their values decrease from one segment to the next, so the velocity stays positive and becomes progressively smaller. The position continues to increase, but more and more slowly. The answers that infer constant or increasing velocity from increasing position confuse the position with the slope of its graph.
