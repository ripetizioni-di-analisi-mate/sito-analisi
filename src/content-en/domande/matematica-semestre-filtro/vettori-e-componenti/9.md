A force of magnitude $10\,\mathrm{N}$ makes an angle of $150^\circ$ measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis. Calculate its two Cartesian components.

- $(-5\sqrt{3},5)\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(5\sqrt{3},5)\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-5,-5\sqrt{3})\,\mathrm{N}$
- $(-5\sqrt{3},-5)\,\mathrm{N}$

## Explanation

With the angle $\theta$ measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis, the components are $F_x=F\cos\theta$ and $F_y=F\sin\theta$. Since $\cos150^\circ=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and $\sin150^\circ=\dfrac12$, we have
$$
F_x=10\,\mathrm{N}\cdot\left(-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)=-5\sqrt3\,\mathrm{N},\qquad F_y=10\,\mathrm{N}\cdot\frac12=5\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
The vector lies in the second quadrant, so the $x$ component is negative and the $y$ component positive. The vector $(5\sqrt3,5)\,\mathrm{N}$ makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the $x$ axis.
