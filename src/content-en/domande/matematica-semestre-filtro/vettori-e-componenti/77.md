Two nonzero vectors are parallel and point in opposite directions. What is their cross product?

- A vector pointing in the same direction as the first.
- The zero vector.
- A positive scalar.
- A vector whose magnitude is the product of the two magnitudes.

## Explanation

The magnitude of the cross product is $|\vec A||\vec B|\sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the vectors. Two vectors pointing in opposite directions make an angle of $180^\circ$, and $\sin180^\circ=0$. Their cross product therefore has magnitude zero and is the zero vector. The same holds for vectors pointing in the same direction, with $\theta=0^\circ$. The magnitude of the cross product equals the product of the magnitudes only for perpendicular vectors.
