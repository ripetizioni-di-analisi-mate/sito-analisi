A force points in the same direction as the vector from the origin to its point of application. What is its torque about the origin?

- Its magnitude is the product of the magnitudes and is nonzero.
- It always points upward.
- It is zero.
- It is maximal at every distance.

## Explanation

The torque about the origin is $\vec M=\vec r\times\vec F$, where $\vec r$ is the position vector of the point of application. The force points in the same direction as $\vec r$, so the angle between the vectors is $0^\circ$ and the torque magnitude is
$$
|\vec r|\,|\vec F|\sin0^\circ=0.
$$
Indeed, the line of action of the force passes through the origin, so the moment arm is zero. A torque whose magnitude is the product of the magnitudes corresponds to a force perpendicular to $\vec r$.
