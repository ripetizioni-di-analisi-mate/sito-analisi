A person walks $6\,\mathrm{m}$ east, then $8\,\mathrm{m}$ north, then $6\,\mathrm{m}$ west. What are the distance traveled and the magnitude of the displacement, respectively?

- $20\,\mathrm{m}$ and $8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$ and $20\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m}$ and $8\,\mathrm{m}$
- $20\,\mathrm{m}$ and $10\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The distance traveled is the sum of the lengths of the three segments, $6+8+6=20\,\mathrm{m}$. To find the displacement, choose axes pointing east and north and add the components of the three segments:
$$
(6,0)+(0,8)+(-6,0)=(0,8)\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The displacement magnitude is therefore $8\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $10\,\mathrm{m}$ is the displacement magnitude after the first two segments, and $20\,\mathrm{m}$ uses the path length as the displacement magnitude.
