The vectors $\vec u=(4,1)$ and $\vec v=(3,2)$ span a parallelogram. What happens to its area when $\vec v$ is replaced by $\vec v-2\vec u$?

- It stays the same
- It doubles
- It halves
- It becomes zero

## Explanation

In the plane, the area of the parallelogram spanned by two vectors is the absolute value of the $z$ component of their cross product, that is, $|u_xv_y-u_yv_x|$. For $\vec u=(4,1)$ and $\vec v=(3,2)$ this gives $|4\cdot2-1\cdot3|=5$. The new vector is $\vec v-2\vec u=(3-8,\,2-2)=(-5,0)$, so the new area is $|4\cdot0-1\cdot(-5)|=5$. The area therefore **stays the same**. The result holds for every pair of vectors, because a vector parallel to the base $\vec u$ is subtracted from $\vec v$ and the height relative to that base does not change.
