Two forces act along the same line. The first has magnitude $5\,\mathrm{N}$ and points east. The second has magnitude $3\,\mathrm{N}$ and points west. Which rule determines the direction of the resultant?

- Add the magnitudes and use the direction of the second force.
- Subtract the magnitudes and use the direction of the larger force.
- Choose a direction perpendicular to the two forces.
- Conclude that the resultant is zero because the directions are opposite.

## Explanation

Choose east as the positive direction. The components of the two forces are $+5\,\mathrm{N}$ and $-3\,\mathrm{N}$, so the resultant has component
$$
5\,\mathrm{N}+(-3\,\mathrm{N})=2\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
The component is positive, so the resultant points east, in the direction of the larger force. Its magnitude is the difference of the magnitudes. The resultant would be zero only if the two force magnitudes were also equal.
