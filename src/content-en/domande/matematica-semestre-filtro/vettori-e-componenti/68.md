Two displacements have magnitudes $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $5\,\mathrm{m}$ and can point in any direction. Which value *cannot* be the magnitude of their resultant?

- $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- $5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $8\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The squared magnitude of the resultant depends on the angle $\theta$ between the displacements:
$$
|\vec a+\vec b|^2=(9+25+30\cos\theta)\,\mathrm{m^2}.
$$
The cosine ranges from $-1$ to $1$, so the square ranges from $4\,\mathrm{m^2}$ to $64\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The magnitude ranges from $2\,\mathrm{m}$ for opposite directions to $8\,\mathrm{m}$ for the same direction. The value $9\,\mathrm{m}$ exceeds the sum of the magnitudes and is therefore impossible. The value $5\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds to $\cos\theta=-0.3$.
