For $\vec A=(1,2,0)$ and $\vec B=(0,1,1)$, a student writes $\vec A\times\vec B=(2,1,1)$. Which check shows that this result is incorrect?

- The magnitude of $(2,1,1)$ differs from $1$.
- The dot product of $(2,1,1)$ with $\vec A$ is $4$, which is nonzero.
- The $z$ component of $(2,1,1)$ is positive.
- The magnitude of $(2,1,1)$ is less than $|\vec A|\,|\vec B|$.

## Explanation

The cross product is perpendicular to both factors, so its dot products with $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ must be zero. For the student's vector, $(2,1,1)\cdot(1,2,0)=2+2=4$, so the result is incorrect. The correct calculation gives
$$
\vec A\times\vec B=(2\cdot1-0\cdot1,\;0\cdot0-1\cdot1,\;1\cdot1-2\cdot0)=(2,-1,1).
$$
The other properties also hold for the correct result, whose magnitude is $\sqrt6$, different from $1$ and less than $\sqrt5\cdot\sqrt2$.
