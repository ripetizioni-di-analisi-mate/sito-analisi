Two nonzero vectors point in the same direction. How is the magnitude of their sum found?

- By always subtracting the smaller magnitude from the larger.
- By multiplying their magnitudes.
- By taking the square root of the sum of their squares.
- By adding their magnitudes.

## Explanation

Two nonzero vectors pointing in the same direction have the same unit vector $\hat e$, so write them as $\vec A=|\vec A|\,\hat e$ and $\vec B=|\vec B|\,\hat e$. Their sum is
$$
\vec A+\vec B=\bigl(|\vec A|+|\vec B|\bigr)\,\hat e,
$$
and the coefficient is positive, so the magnitude of the sum is $|\vec A|+|\vec B|$. The square root of the sum of the squared magnitudes gives the magnitude of the sum for perpendicular vectors, and the difference of the magnitudes gives it for vectors pointing in opposite directions.
