What angle does $\left(-\sqrt3,\,-1\right)$ make when measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis? Give an angle from $0^\circ$ inclusive to $360^\circ$ exclusive.

- $30^\circ$
- $210^\circ$
- $150^\circ$
- $330^\circ$

## Explanation

The magnitude of the vector is $\sqrt{(-\sqrt3)^2+(-1)^2}=2$, so its angle $\theta$ satisfies
$$
\cos\theta=-\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\qquad \sin\theta=-\frac12.
$$
The cosine and sine are both negative, so the vector lies in the third quadrant and $\theta=180^\circ+30^\circ=210^\circ$. The ratio $\dfrac{A_y}{A_x}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$ is the tangent of both $30^\circ$ and $210^\circ$, so it cannot determine the angle on its own. The value $30^\circ$ comes from ignoring the signs of the components.
