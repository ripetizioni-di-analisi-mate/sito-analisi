Do the arrows from $(0,0)$ to $(2,1)$ and from $(3,-1)$ to $(5,0)$ represent the same free vector?

- Yes, because both have components $(2,1)$.
- No, because their tails are at different points.
- No, because the second arrow is horizontal.
- Yes, because both start at $(0,0)$.

## Explanation

The components of the free vector represented by an arrow are the coordinates of its head minus those of its tail. The first arrow gives $(2-0,\;1-0)=(2,1)$ and the second gives
$$
\bigl(5-3,\;0-(-1)\bigr)=(2,1).
$$
The components agree, so the arrows have the same length, are parallel, and point in the same direction. They therefore represent the same free vector. Their tails are at different points, and translating an arrow leaves its free vector unchanged.
