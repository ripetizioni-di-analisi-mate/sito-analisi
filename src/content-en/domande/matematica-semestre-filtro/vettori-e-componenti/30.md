The order of the two vectors spanning a parallelogram is reversed. What happens to its area?

- It changes sign because the columns of the determinant are interchanged.
- It doubles because both orientations are counted.
- It becomes zero because the order of the sides is reversed.
- It stays the same because the absolute value of the determinant is taken.

## Explanation

The area of the parallelogram spanned by $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ is $|\vec A\times\vec B|$, which in the plane equals $|A_xB_y-A_yB_x|$. The number $A_xB_y-A_yB_x$ is the determinant of the matrix whose columns are the components of the two vectors. Reversing their order gives $B_xA_y-B_yA_x=-(A_xB_y-A_yB_x)$, so the determinant changes sign. Its absolute value is unchanged, so the area stays the same. The sign change describes the reversal of the direction of the cross product $\vec B\times\vec A$.
