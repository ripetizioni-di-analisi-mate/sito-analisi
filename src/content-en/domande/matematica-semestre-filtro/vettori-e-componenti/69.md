The point of application of a force lies on the positive $x$ axis. The force points in the positive $y$ direction. In a right-handed coordinate system, which way does the torque about the origin point?

- In the negative $z$ direction.
- In the positive $z$ direction.
- In the positive $y$ direction.
- In the negative $x$ direction.

## Explanation

The torque about the origin is $\vec M=\vec r\times\vec F$. The position vector points in the direction of $\hat\imath$ and the force in the direction of $\hat\jmath$, so $\vec r=r\,\hat\imath$ and $\vec F=F\,\hat\jmath$ with $r>0$ and $F>0$. Therefore
$$
\vec M=rF\,(\hat\imath\times\hat\jmath)=rF\,\hat k,
$$
and the torque points in the positive $z$ direction. Viewed from the positive side of the $z$ axis, the force tends to turn the object counterclockwise, in agreement with the right-hand rule.
