In a right-handed Cartesian coordinate system, a force $\vec F=(2,-1,3)\,\mathrm N$ is applied at the point with position vector $\vec r=(1,2,0)\,\mathrm m$ relative to the pivot $O$. What is the torque $\vec M_O=\vec r\times\vec F$?

- $(-6,3,5)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $(6,-3,-5)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $(6,3,-5)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$
- $(6,-3,3)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$

## Explanation

The torque about $O$ is the cross product $\vec r\times\vec F$, whose components are $r_yF_z-r_zF_y$, $r_zF_x-r_xF_z$ and $r_xF_y-r_yF_x$. With $\vec r=(1,2,0)\,\mathrm m$ and $\vec F=(2,-1,3)\,\mathrm N$ this gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_x&=2\cdot3-0\cdot(-1)=6\\[6pt]
M_y&=0\cdot2-1\cdot3=-3\\[6pt]
M_z&=1\cdot(-1)-2\cdot2=-5.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore $\vec M_O=(6,-3,-5)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The vector $(-6,3,5)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ is the product in the order $\vec F\times\vec r$, which points the opposite way. The vector $(6,3,-5)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ comes from swapping the two terms of the $y$ component, while $(6,-3,3)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ comes from adding the two terms of the $z$ component instead of subtracting them.
