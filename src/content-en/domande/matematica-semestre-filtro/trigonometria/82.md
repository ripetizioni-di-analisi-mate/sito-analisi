A vertical pole of height $3\sqrt3\,\mathrm m$ casts a shadow on level ground. The Sun’s rays make an angle of $60^\circ$ with the ground. How long is the shadow?

- $3\sqrt3\,\mathrm m$
- $3\,\mathrm m$
- $9\,\mathrm m$
- $6\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

Relative to the $60^\circ$ angle that the rays make with the ground, the pole is the opposite leg and the shadow $\ell$ is the adjacent leg. Thus $\tan60^\circ=\dfrac{h}{\ell}$, giving
$$
\ell=\frac{3\sqrt3}{\tan60^\circ}\,\mathrm m=\frac{3\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}\,\mathrm m=3\,\mathrm m.
$$
The value $9\,\mathrm m$ comes from multiplying the height by $\tan60^\circ$ instead of dividing. The value $6\,\mathrm m$ is the distance along a ray from the top of the pole to the tip of the shadow.
