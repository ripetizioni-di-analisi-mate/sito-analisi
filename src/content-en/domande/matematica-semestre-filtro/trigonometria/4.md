A right triangle has a hypotenuse of length $14\,\mathrm{m}$ and an angle of $30^\circ$. Calculate the length of the leg opposite this angle.

- $14\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7\,\mathrm{m}$
- $28\,\mathrm{m}$
- $7\sqrt3\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

The **sine** of an acute angle is the ratio of the opposite leg to the hypotenuse, so the opposite leg equals the hypotenuse times the sine. Since $\sin30^\circ=\dfrac12$, we obtain
$$
a=14\sin30^\circ\,\mathrm{m}=14\cdot\frac12\,\mathrm{m}=7\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The value $7\sqrt3\,\mathrm{m}$ is the adjacent leg, found by using cosine in place of sine. Dividing by the sine instead of multiplying gives $28\,\mathrm{m}$.
