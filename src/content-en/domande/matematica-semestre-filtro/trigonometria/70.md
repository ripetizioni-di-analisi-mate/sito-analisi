Two arcs have the same positive length. The second circle has three times the radius of the first. How do the two central angles compare?

- The second angle is one-third of the first.
- The second angle is three times the first.
- The angles are equal.
- The second angle is one-ninth of the first.

## Explanation

For a fixed arc length $s$, the angle in radians is $\theta=\dfrac{s}{r}$. If the radius becomes $3r$, the angle becomes
$$
\frac{s}{3r}=\frac13\cdot\frac{s}{r},
$$
so the second angle is **one-third** of the first. On the larger circle the same arc length covers a smaller fraction of a turn. Treating the angle as proportional to the radius gives three times the angle, and squaring the radius ratio gives one-ninth.
