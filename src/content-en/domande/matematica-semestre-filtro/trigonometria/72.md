Two forces of magnitude $12\,\mathrm N$ have directions $30^\circ$ and $150^\circ$ measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis. What is the vertical component of their sum?

- $12\,\mathrm N$
- $0\,\mathrm N$
- $6\,\mathrm N$
- $12\sqrt3\,\mathrm N$

## Explanation

The vertical component of each force is $F\sin\theta$. The sines are $\sin30^\circ=\dfrac12$ and $\sin150^\circ=\sin(180^\circ-30^\circ)=\dfrac12$, so each force contributes $12\cdot\dfrac12\,\mathrm N=6\,\mathrm N$ upward. The components of the sum are the sums of the corresponding components, so
$$
F_y=6\,\mathrm N+6\,\mathrm N=12\,\mathrm N.
$$
The value $0\,\mathrm N$ is the horizontal component of the sum, which is zero because $\cos150^\circ=-\cos30^\circ$.
