An angle $\theta$ lies in the second quadrant and satisfies $\cos\theta=-\dfrac{3}{5}$. Calculate $\sin\theta$.

- $\dfrac45$
- $-\dfrac45$
- $\dfrac{16}{25}$
- $\dfrac35$

## Explanation

The identity $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ gives
$$
\sin^2\theta=1-\left(-\frac35\right)^2=1-\frac{9}{25}=\frac{16}{25}.
$$
Thus $\sin\theta=\dfrac45$ or $\sin\theta=-\dfrac45$. The vertical coordinate is positive in the second quadrant, so $\sin\theta=\dfrac45$. Choosing the wrong sign gives $-\dfrac45$, while $\dfrac{16}{25}$ is the square of the sine before taking the square root.
