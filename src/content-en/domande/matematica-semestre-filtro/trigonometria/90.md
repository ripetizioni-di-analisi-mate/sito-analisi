From a point above level ground, a marker on the ground is seen at an angle of depression of $30^\circ$. The vertical distance is $6\sqrt3\,\mathrm m$. What is the horizontal distance?

- $6\,\mathrm m$
- $18\,\mathrm m$
- $12\,\mathrm m$
- $6\sqrt3\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The angle of depression is measured downward from the horizontal, so the vertical distance is the leg opposite the $30^\circ$ angle in the right triangle and the horizontal distance is the adjacent leg. Therefore,
$$
d=\frac{6\sqrt3}{\tan30^\circ}\,\mathrm m=6\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt3\,\mathrm m=18\,\mathrm m.
$$
The value $6\,\mathrm m$ comes from multiplying by $\tan30^\circ$ instead of dividing. The value $12\,\mathrm m$ comes from dividing by $\cos30^\circ$ instead of $\tan30^\circ$.
