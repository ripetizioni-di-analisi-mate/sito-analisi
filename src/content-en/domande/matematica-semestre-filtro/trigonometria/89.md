In a right triangle, the leg adjacent to $\theta$ has length $15\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the angle satisfies $\cos\theta=\dfrac{3}{5}$. Calculate the length of the opposite leg.

- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $9\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $25\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Cosine is the adjacent leg divided by the hypotenuse, so the hypotenuse is
$$
c=\frac{15}{\frac35}\,\mathrm{cm}=15\cdot\frac53\,\mathrm{cm}=25\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
By the Pythagorean theorem, the opposite leg is $\sqrt{25^2-15^2}\,\mathrm{cm}=\sqrt{400}\,\mathrm{cm}=20\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ comes from multiplying the leg length by $\dfrac35$ instead of dividing, and $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the length of the hypotenuse.
