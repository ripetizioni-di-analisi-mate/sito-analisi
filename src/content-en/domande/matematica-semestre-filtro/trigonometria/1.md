A right triangle has legs of lengths $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate its hypotenuse.

- $17\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $169\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $13\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $7\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

By the **Pythagorean theorem**, the square of the hypotenuse is the sum of the squares of the legs, so
$$
c=\sqrt{5^2+12^2}\,\mathrm{cm}=\sqrt{25+144}\,\mathrm{cm}=\sqrt{169}\,\mathrm{cm}=13\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
Omitting the square root gives $169\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $17\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the sum of the legs. It always exceeds the hypotenuse because each side of a triangle is shorter than the sum of the other two.
