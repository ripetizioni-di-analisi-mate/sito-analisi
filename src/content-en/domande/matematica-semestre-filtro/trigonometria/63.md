A ray starts in the direction $45^\circ$ measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis and rotates counterclockwise through $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ radians. What is its final direction in the interval $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$?

- $105^\circ$
- $150^\circ$
- $225^\circ$
- $195^\circ$

## Explanation

Expressed in degrees, the rotation is
$$
\frac{5\pi}{6}\cdot\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}=150^\circ.
$$
The rotation is counterclockwise, so add it to the initial direction to obtain $45^\circ+150^\circ=195^\circ$, which already lies in $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$. The value $150^\circ$ is the rotation alone, without the initial direction. Subtracting gives $105^\circ$, which treats the rotation as clockwise.
