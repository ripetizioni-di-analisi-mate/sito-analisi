An observer’s eyes are $1.5\,\mathrm m$ above the ground. The top of a pole a horizontal distance $12\,\mathrm m$ away is seen at an angle of elevation of $45^\circ$. The ground is level. How tall is the pole?

- $12\,\mathrm m$
- $10.5\,\mathrm m$
- $13.5\,\mathrm m$
- $12\sqrt2\,\mathrm m$

## Explanation

The vertex of the angle of elevation is at the observer’s eyes, so the leg opposite the $45^\circ$ angle is the rise from eye level to the top. Since $\tan45^\circ=1$, the rise is
$$
12\tan45^\circ\,\mathrm m=12\,\mathrm m.
$$
The eyes are $1.5\,\mathrm m$ above the ground, so the pole is $12\,\mathrm m+1.5\,\mathrm m=13.5\,\mathrm m$ tall. Omitting the eye height gives $12\,\mathrm m$, and subtracting it instead of adding it gives $10.5\,\mathrm m$.
