An equilateral triangle has side length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is its altitude?

- $5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5\sqrt2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The altitude divides the equilateral triangle into two right triangles with hypotenuse $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and shorter leg equal to half the side, $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. By the **Pythagorean theorem**,
$$
h=\sqrt{10^2-5^2}\,\mathrm{cm}=\sqrt{75}\,\mathrm{cm}=5\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
The same result follows from $h=10\sin60^\circ\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $10\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}$ comes from multiplying the side length by $\sqrt3$ instead of $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, and the value $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ comes from using $\sin30^\circ$.
