A segment of length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ starts at the origin and makes an angle of $50^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis. The line $r$ passes through the origin and makes an angle of $20^\circ$ with the same axis, measured in the same direction. What is the length of the orthogonal projection of the segment onto $r$?

- $5\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\cos70^\circ\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\cos50^\circ\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

Choose an axis directed along $r$. The segment makes an angle of $50^\circ-20^\circ=30^\circ$ with this axis, so its orthogonal projection onto $r$ is the component along the axis, its length times the cosine of the angle:
$$
10\cos30^\circ\,\mathrm{cm}=10\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}=5\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
Adding the angles instead of subtracting them gives $10\cos70^\circ\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value $10\cos50^\circ\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the projection on the $x$-axis, and using sine gives $5\,\mathrm{cm}$.
