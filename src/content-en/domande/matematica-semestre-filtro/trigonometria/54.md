If $r>0$ is the radius and $s$ is the length of an arc of the circle, how can the radian measure of the central angle be found directly?

- $\dfrac{r}{s}$
- $sr$
- $\dfrac{s}{2\pi r}$
- $\dfrac{s}{r}$

## Explanation

By definition, the radian measure of a central angle is the ratio of arc length to radius, $\theta=\dfrac{s}{r}$. One radian is the angle subtending an arc as long as the radius. The ratio $\dfrac{s}{2\pi r}$ is the fraction of a turn, and multiplying by $2\pi$ again gives $\dfrac{s}{r}$. The ratio $\dfrac{r}{s}$ is the reciprocal of the required measure, and the product $sr$ has units of length squared.
