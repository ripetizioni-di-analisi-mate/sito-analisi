Which pair could represent $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ for an angle in the second quadrant?

- $\left(\dfrac{8}{17},\dfrac{15}{17}\right)$
- $\left(-\dfrac{8}{17},\dfrac{15}{17}\right)$
- $\left(-\dfrac{8}{17},\dfrac{9}{17}\right)$
- $\left(-\dfrac{15}{17},-\dfrac{8}{17}\right)$

## Explanation

In the second quadrant the cosine, the horizontal coordinate, is negative, and the sine, the vertical coordinate, is positive. The point is also on the unit circle, so $\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$ must hold. The pair $\left(-\dfrac{8}{17},\dfrac{15}{17}\right)$ satisfies $\dfrac{64}{289}+\dfrac{225}{289}=1$ and has the required signs. The pair $\left(-\dfrac{8}{17},\dfrac{9}{17}\right)$ has the right signs, but its squared coordinates sum to $\dfrac{145}{289}$. The pair $\left(\dfrac{8}{17},\dfrac{15}{17}\right)$ lies in the first quadrant.
