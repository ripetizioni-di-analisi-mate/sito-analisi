A right triangle has a hypotenuse of length $20\,\mathrm{cm}$. A second right triangle has legs twice as long as the corresponding legs of the first. What is the length of the second hypotenuse?

- $20\sqrt2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $80\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $20\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $40\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

If the legs become $2a$ and $2b$, the square of the new hypotenuse is
$$
(2a)^2+(2b)^2=4(a^2+b^2)=4\cdot20^2\,\mathrm{cm^2}.
$$
Taking the square root gives $2\cdot20\,\mathrm{cm}=40\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the hypotenuse also doubles. The same follows from similarity because every side of the second triangle is twice as long as the corresponding side of the first. The value $80\,\mathrm{cm}$ comes from multiplying by $4$ without taking the square root, and $20\sqrt2\,\mathrm{cm}$ comes from multiplying by $\sqrt2$.
