Calculate $\cos405^\circ$.

- $-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$
- $\dfrac12$

## Explanation

The measures $405^\circ$ and $405^\circ-360^\circ=45^\circ$ differ by one full turn, so they identify the same point on the unit circle. Cosine and sine repeat every $360^\circ$, so
$$
\cos405^\circ=\cos45^\circ=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}.
$$
The value $-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$ comes from subtracting $180^\circ$ instead of $360^\circ$. The resulting angle $225^\circ$ points in another direction, in the third quadrant.
