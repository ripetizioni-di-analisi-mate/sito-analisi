An observer’s eyes are level with the base of a vertical tower. The observer sees its top at an angle of elevation of $45^\circ$. After the observer moves $6\,\mathrm{m}$ farther away along the horizontal line through the base, the angle is $30^\circ$. How tall is the tower?

- $6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3(\sqrt3+1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3(\sqrt3-1)\,\mathrm{m}$
- $6\sqrt3\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

At an angle of elevation of $45^\circ$ the tangent is $1$, so the initial distance from the base equals the height $h$. After the observer moves back, the distance is $h+6$, so
$$
\frac{h}{h+6}=\tan30^\circ=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}.
$$
Cross-multiplying gives $\sqrt3\,h=h+6$, or $h(\sqrt3-1)=6$. Rationalizing gives
$$
h=\frac{6}{\sqrt3-1}\,\mathrm m=\frac{6(\sqrt3+1)}{2}\,\mathrm m=3(\sqrt3+1)\,\mathrm m.
$$
The value $3(\sqrt3-1)\,\mathrm m$ results from a sign error when rationalizing.
