A point $P=(-4,3)$ lies on a circle centered at the origin. If $\beta$ is the direction of $\overrightarrow{OP}$, what is $\cos\beta$?

- $-\dfrac45$
- $\dfrac45$
- $-\dfrac35$
- $\dfrac34$

## Explanation

The radius of the circle is the distance of $P$ from the origin, or
$$
r=\sqrt{(-4)^2+3^2}=\sqrt{25}=5.
$$
The cosine of the direction angle is the horizontal coordinate divided by the radius, so $\cos\beta=-\dfrac45$. Its negative sign agrees with the second-quadrant location of $P$. The value $\dfrac34$ is the ratio of the unsigned coordinates, while $-\dfrac35$ reverses the roles of the horizontal and vertical coordinates.
