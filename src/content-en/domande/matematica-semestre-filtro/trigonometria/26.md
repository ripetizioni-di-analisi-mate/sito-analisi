A square has side length $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the length of its diagonal?

- $12\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $72\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $3\sqrt2\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $6\sqrt2\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The diagonal divides the square into two isosceles right triangles with legs of length $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. By the **Pythagorean theorem**,
$$
d=\sqrt{6^2+6^2}\,\mathrm{cm}=\sqrt{72}\,\mathrm{cm}=6\sqrt2\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
The acute angles in these triangles measure $45^\circ$, and indeed $\dfrac{6}{6\sqrt2}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}=\cos45^\circ$. Omitting the square root gives $72\,\mathrm{cm}$, and adding the two side lengths gives $12\,\mathrm{cm}$.
