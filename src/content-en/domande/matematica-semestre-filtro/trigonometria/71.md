Calculate $\sin(-30^\circ)$ using the symmetry of the unit circle.

- $\dfrac12$
- $-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$
- $-\dfrac12$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$

## Explanation

The angles $30^\circ$ and $-30^\circ$ have the same magnitude and are measured in opposite directions, so their points on the unit circle are reflections across the $x$-axis. This reflection preserves the horizontal coordinate and reverses the sign of the vertical coordinate, so
$$
\sin(-30^\circ)=-\sin30^\circ=-\frac12.
$$
The value $\dfrac12$ comes from overlooking the change in sign, and $-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ comes from using $\sin60^\circ$ instead of $\sin30^\circ$.
