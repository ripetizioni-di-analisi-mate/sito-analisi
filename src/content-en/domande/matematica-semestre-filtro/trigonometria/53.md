Two acute angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are complementary and satisfy $\beta=2\alpha$. What is $\cos\beta$?

- $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$
- $\dfrac12$
- $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$
- $1$

## Explanation

Complementary angles sum to $90^\circ$. Substituting $\beta=2\alpha$ gives $\alpha+2\alpha=90^\circ$, so $\alpha=30^\circ$ and $\beta=60^\circ$. Therefore,
$$
\cos\beta=\cos60^\circ=\frac12.
$$
The value $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ equals $\cos30^\circ$, the cosine of $\alpha$ instead of $\beta$. The value $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$ corresponds to two equal angles of $45^\circ$, which would ignore the condition $\beta=2\alpha$.
