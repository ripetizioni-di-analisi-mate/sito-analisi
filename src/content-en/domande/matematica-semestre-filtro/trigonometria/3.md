Express $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ radians in degrees.

- $60^\circ$
- $120^\circ$
- $30^\circ$
- $240^\circ$

## Explanation

Since an angle of $\pi$ radians corresponds to $180^\circ$, multiply by $\dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi}$ to convert radians to degrees. The factor $\pi$ cancels, leaving
$$
\theta=\frac{\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}=\frac{180^\circ}{3}=60^\circ.
$$
The value $120^\circ$ comes from equating $\pi$ to $360^\circ$, a full turn instead of half a turn. The value $30^\circ$ corresponds to $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
