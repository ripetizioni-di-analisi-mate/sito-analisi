A segment of length $L$ has orthogonal projections of lengths $p$ and $q$ on two perpendicular coordinate axes. Which equation holds for every inclination?

- $p+q=L$
- $pq=L^2$
- $p^2+q^2=L^2$
- $(p-q)^2=L^2$

## Explanation

If the segment is inclined to both axes, its projections are the legs of a right triangle whose hypotenuse is the segment. The **Pythagorean theorem** therefore gives $p^2+q^2=L^2$. If the segment is parallel to one axis, one projection has length $L$ and the other $0$, and the equation still holds. The equation $p+q=L$ confuses the sum of the legs with the hypotenuse. Indeed, for $L=5$, $p=3$, and $q=4$, we have $p+q=7$.
