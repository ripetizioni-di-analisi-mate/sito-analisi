A rotation of $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ radians counterclockwise is followed by one of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ radians clockwise. What is the net directed rotation, taking counterclockwise as positive?

- $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad}$
- $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\,\mathrm{rad}$

## Explanation

Counterclockwise is positive, so the clockwise rotation through $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ has measure $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The net rotation is the signed sum
$$
\frac{5\pi}{6}-\frac{\pi}{3}=\frac{5\pi}{6}-\frac{2\pi}{6}=\frac{3\pi}{6}=\frac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad}.
$$
Adding the rotations as though both were counterclockwise gives $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad}$. The value $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad}$ has the opposite sign.
