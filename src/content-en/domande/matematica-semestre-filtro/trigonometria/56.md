A wheel of radius $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ undergoes a directed rotation of $-270^\circ$, taking counterclockwise as positive. How far does a point on the rim travel?

- $-30\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $5400\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $10\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$
- $30\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$

## Explanation

The distance traveled depends on the absolute value of the angle swept out, expressed in radians. Since $270\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{180}=\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, we have
$$
s=20\cdot\frac{3\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}=30\pi\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
The value $-30\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$ retains the sign of the rotation, whereas a length is positive. The value $10\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$ corresponds to $90^\circ$, the counterclockwise rotation reaching the same final position. Using degrees in the formula gives $5400\,\mathrm{cm}$.
