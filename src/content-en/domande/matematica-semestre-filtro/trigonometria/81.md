One rectangle has sides of lengths $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Another has sides of lengths $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. Which diagonal is longer?

- The diagonal of the second rectangle.
- The diagonal of the first rectangle.
- The two diagonals are equal.
- The comparison depends on the unit used.

## Explanation

Each diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are the sides of the rectangle. By the Pythagorean theorem, the squares of the diagonals are
$$
5^2+10^2=125\,\mathrm{cm^2},\qquad 7^2+8^2=113\,\mathrm{cm^2}.
$$
The lengths are positive, so the diagonal with the larger square is longer: the diagonal of the **first rectangle**. The diagonals appear equal if the sums of the side lengths are compared, since these are $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ in both rectangles.
