Two points are at the same positive height above an observer and have distinct acute angles of elevation. Which is farther away horizontally?

- The one with the larger angle of elevation.
- They are the same horizontal distance away.
- The one with the smaller angle of elevation.
- The distance is independent of the angle.

## Explanation

Let $h$ be the rise and $d$ the horizontal distance. The right triangle gives $\tan\alpha=\dfrac hd$, so $d=\dfrac{h}{\tan\alpha}$. The rise is the same for both points. Tangent increases with an acute angle, so the smaller angle has the smaller tangent and the greater horizontal distance. For example, with $h=10\,\mathrm m$ the distance is $d=10\,\mathrm m$ at $45^\circ$ and $d=10\sqrt3\,\mathrm m$ at $30^\circ$.
