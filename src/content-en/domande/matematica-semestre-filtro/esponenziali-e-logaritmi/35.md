Two sound levels are $30$ and $50\,\mathrm{dB}$ relative to the same reference. How is the intensity at $40\,\mathrm{dB}$ related to the intensities at the two endpoints?

- It is their arithmetic mean.
- It is their geometric mean.
- It is their sum.
- It is their product.

## Explanation

Relative to the same reference, the levels $30$, $40$, and $50\,\mathrm{dB}$ correspond to the ratios $\dfrac{I}{I_0}=10^3$, $10^4$, and $10^5$. Since

$$
\sqrt{10^3\cdot10^5}=\sqrt{10^8}=10^4,
$$

the intensity at $40\,\mathrm{dB}$ is the geometric mean of the other two. The level is proportional to the logarithm, so the arithmetic mean of the levels corresponds to the geometric mean of the intensities. The arithmetic mean of the intensities, $5.05\cdot10^{4}I_0$, would give approximately $47\,\mathrm{dB}$.
