Determine the real domain of $\log_2\!\left(\sqrt{x-2}\right)$.

- $[2,+\infty)$
- $(2,+\infty)$
- $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2\}$
- $(-\infty,2)$

## Explanation

The function $\sqrt{x-2}$ is defined for $x\ge2$, and the logarithm also requires a strictly positive argument. Both conditions must hold.

$$
\begin{cases}
x-2\ge0\\
\sqrt{x-2}>0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x>2.
$$

The interval $[2,+\infty)$ includes $x=2$, where the square root equals zero and the logarithm is undefined. The set $\mathbb R\setminus\{2\}$ allows values of $x$ below two, for which the square root is undefined over the reals.
