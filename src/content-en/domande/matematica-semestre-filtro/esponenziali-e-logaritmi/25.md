Solve $\log_2(2-x)\le 1$ over the real numbers.

- $[0,2)$
- $(-\infty,0]$
- $[0,2]$
- $(0,2)$

## Explanation

The domain requires $2-x>0$, or $x<2$. Since $1=\log_2 2$ and the base-two logarithm is strictly increasing, the inequality between the arguments has the same direction as the inequality between the logarithms. Both conditions must hold.

$$
\begin{cases}
2-x>0\\
2-x\le2
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad 0\le x<2.
$$

The interval $[0,2]$ includes $x=2$, where the argument is zero. The interval $(-\infty,0]$ reverses the inequality.
