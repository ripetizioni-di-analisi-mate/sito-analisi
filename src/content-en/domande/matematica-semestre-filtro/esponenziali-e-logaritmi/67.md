Solve $\log_2(x+2)=2+\log_2(x)$.

- $x=2$
- $x=-\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $x=4$
- $x=\dfrac{2}{3}$

## Explanation

The domain requires both $x+2>0$ and $x>0$, which together are equivalent to $x>0$. Since $2=\log_2 4$, the product rule allows us to write the right-hand side as a single logarithm.

$$
\log_2(x+2)=\log_2(4x),\qquad x+2=4x,\qquad x=\frac23.
$$

The value $\dfrac23$ is positive and therefore admissible. The value $x=2$ comes from $x+2=2x$, that is, from writing $2=\log_2 2$. The value $-\dfrac23$ results from a sign error and is outside the domain.
