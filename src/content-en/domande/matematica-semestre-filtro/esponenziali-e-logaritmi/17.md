An integer between $1$ and $70$ must be identified by asking yes-or-no questions. Each question splits the numbers still possible into two groups as equal in size as possible. How many questions are needed, in the worst case, to identify the number?

- $35$
- $6$
- $7$
- $8$

## Explanation

With $k$ yes-or-no answers at most $2^k$ cases can be distinguished. Since $2^6=64<70$, six questions are not enough to distinguish all the numbers. With splits into two groups as equal as possible, in the worst case the numbers still possible are successively $70$, $35$, $18$, $9$, $5$, $3$, $2$, $1$, so a single number remains after seven questions. The number of questions is therefore the smallest integer greater than or equal to $\log_2 70\approx6.13$, that is $7$.
