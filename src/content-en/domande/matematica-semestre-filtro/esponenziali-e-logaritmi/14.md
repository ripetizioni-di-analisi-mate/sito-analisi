A quantity undergoing exponential decay decreases from $240\,\mathrm{mg}$ to $15\,\mathrm{mg}$ in $12\,\mathrm h$. Determine its half-life.

- $4\,\mathrm{h}$
- $6\,\mathrm{h}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}\,\mathrm{h}$
- $3\,\mathrm{h}$

## Explanation

The ratio of final to initial quantity is a power of one-half.

$$
\frac{15}{240}=\frac{1}{16}=\left(\frac12\right)^4.
$$

Thus four half-lives have elapsed in $12\,\mathrm h$, and each lasts $\dfrac{12\,\mathrm h}{4}=3\,\mathrm h$. The value $6\,\mathrm h$ counts two half-lives instead of four. The value $\dfrac34\,\mathrm h$ results from dividing $12$ by the factor $16$ instead of by the number of half-lives.
