To recover $x$ from $y=\log_2(x^2-9)$, the branch $x<-3$ is selected. What is the inverse formula on that branch?

- $x=-\sqrt{2^y-9}$
- $x=-\sqrt{2^y+9}$
- $x=-2^{y/2}-3$
- $x=\sqrt{2^y+9}$

## Explanation

The definition of a logarithm gives $x^2-9=2^y$, hence $x^2=2^y+9$. There are two square roots, but only the negative one belongs to the chosen branch. Its absolute value exceeds three for every real $y$.
