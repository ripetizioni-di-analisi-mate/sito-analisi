Determine the real domain of $\log_2(x^2-9)$.

- $(-3,3)$
- $(-\infty,-3)\cup(3,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-3]\cup[3,+\infty)$
- $(3,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The argument of the logarithm must be strictly positive, so solve $x^2-9>0$. Since $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$, the product is positive when the two factors have the same sign, that is, outside the interval between the roots $-3$ and $3$.

$$
x<-3\quad\text{or}\quad x>3.
$$

Intervals with closed endpoints include $x=\pm3$, where the argument is zero. The interval $(-3,3)$ is the set on which the argument is negative. The interval $(3,+\infty)$ omits the negative solutions.
