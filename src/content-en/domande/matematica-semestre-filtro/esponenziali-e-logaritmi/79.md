A model $Q(t)=Q_0a^t$, with $a>0$, holds for $-3\le t\le3$, where $t$ is the numerical value of time in hours. The measured values are $Q(0)=12$ and $Q(3)=18$ units. Calculate $Q(-3)$.

- $8$ units
- $6$ units
- $9$ units
- $27$ units

## Explanation

In the exponential model, equal intervals give the same factor. From $t=0$ to $t=3$ the factor is $\dfrac{18}{12}=\dfrac32$, so from $t=-3$ to $t=0$ the quantity is also multiplied by $\dfrac32$.

$$
\frac32\,Q(-3)=12,\qquad Q(-3)=12\cdot\frac23=8\ \text{units}.
$$

The value $6$ units comes from a linear model, subtracting the increase of $6$ units from $12$. The value $27$ units multiplies $18$ by $\dfrac32$, extending the model toward $t=6$.
