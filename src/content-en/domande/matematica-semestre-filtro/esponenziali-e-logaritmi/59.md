An initial quantity of $80\,\mathrm{mg}$ decays exponentially with a half-life of $4\,\mathrm{h}$. How many milligrams remain after $2\,\mathrm{h}$?

- $40\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $60\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $20\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{mg}$
- $40\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{mg}$

## Explanation

The exponential model also applies to fractions of a half-life, and $2\,\mathrm h$ is half the half-life. The factor is therefore $\left(\dfrac12\right)^{\frac{2}{4}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$.

$$
N=\frac{80}{\sqrt2}\,\mathrm{mg}=40\sqrt2\,\mathrm{mg}\approx56.6\,\mathrm{mg}.
$$

The value $60\,\mathrm{mg}$ assumes a linear decrease and subtracts half the $40\,\mathrm{mg}$ lost over a full half-life. The value $40\,\mathrm{mg}$ corresponds to a full half-life. The value $20\sqrt2\,\mathrm{mg}$ is half the correct result.
