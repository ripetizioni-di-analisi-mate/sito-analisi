In an idealized model, consider $10\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a dilute solution of a strong monoprotic acid with a pH of two. Water is added while the amount of solute stays constant. Assume complete dissociation and a negligible contribution from water. What final volume corresponds to a pH of four?

- $100\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $1\,\mathrm{L}$
- $20\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $4\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

Going from pH $2$ to pH $4$ means that the hydronium concentration changes by a factor of

$$
\frac{c_{\mathrm f}}{c_{\mathrm i}}=10^{-(4-2)}=\frac1{100}.
$$

With complete dissociation and a fixed amount of solute, the concentration is inversely proportional to the volume. Therefore the final volume is one hundred times the initial volume, or $100\cdot10\,\mathrm{mL}=1000\,\mathrm{mL}=1\,\mathrm L$. The value $100\,\mathrm{mL}$ corresponds to a tenfold dilution, which brings the pH to $3$. The value $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ doubles the volume because the pH doubles.
