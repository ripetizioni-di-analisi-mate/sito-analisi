The graph of $y=3^x$ contains the point $(2,9)$. Which point, obtained by reflecting this point, belongs to the graph of $y=\left(\dfrac13\right)^x$?

- $(9,2)$
- $(-2,9)$
- $(2,-9)$
- $(-2,-9)$

## Explanation

Since $\left(\dfrac13\right)^x=3^{-x}$, the graph of $y=\left(\dfrac13\right)^x$ is the reflection of the graph of $y=3^x$ across the vertical axis. The point $(2,9)$ therefore becomes $(-2,9)$, and the direct check gives $\left(\dfrac13\right)^{-2}=3^2=9$. The point $(9,2)$ is obtained by interchanging the coordinates, reflecting across the line bisecting the first quadrant, which gives the graph of the logarithm. Points with vertical coordinate $-9$ do not belong to the graph because an exponential function takes only positive values.
