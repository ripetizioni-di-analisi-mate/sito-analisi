A student removes the logarithms from $\log_2(x-1)=\log_2(3-x)$ and obtains $x=2$. Which property makes that step valid on the allowed inputs?

- Both expressions inside the logarithms are positive for every real number.
- The base-two logarithm turns sums into products.
- All values of the base-two logarithm are positive.
- The base-two logarithm takes different values at different positive inputs.

## Explanation

The inputs are both positive for $1<x<3$. On this interval equal logarithms require equal inputs because the logarithm is strictly increasing. Solving $x-1=3-x$ gives $2$, which is in the domain.
