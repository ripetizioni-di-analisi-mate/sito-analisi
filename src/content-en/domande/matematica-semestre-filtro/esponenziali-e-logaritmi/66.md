Determine the real domain of $\ln\left(\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}\right)$.

- $(1,+\infty)$
- $(-2,1)$
- $(-\infty,-2]\cup(1,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,-2)\cup(1,+\infty)$

## Explanation

The argument of the logarithm must be strictly positive, so examine the sign of the quotient. The numerator $x-1$ is positive for $x>1$ and the denominator $x+2$ is positive for $x>-2$. The quotient is positive when the two have the same sign.

$$
\frac{x-1}{x+2}>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x<-2\ \text{or}\ x>1.
$$

The value $x=-2$ is excluded because it makes the denominator zero. The interval $(-2,1)$ is the set where the quotient is negative. The interval $(1,+\infty)$ considers only the case in which both terms are positive.
