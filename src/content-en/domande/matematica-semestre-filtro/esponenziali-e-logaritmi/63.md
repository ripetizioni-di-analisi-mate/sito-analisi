For all $x>0$ and $y>0$, which expression equals $\log_{10}(x)+\log_{10}(y)$?

- $\log_{10}(x+y)$
- $\log_{10}(xy)$
- $\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)$
- $\log_{10}(x^y)$

## Explanation

For $x>0$ and $y>0$, set $u=\log_{10}x$ and $v=\log_{10}y$, so that $x=10^u$ and $y=10^v$. The product equals $xy=10^{u+v}$, so

$$
\log_{10}(xy)=u+v=\log_{10}x+\log_{10}y.
$$

The expression $\log_{10}(x+y)$ is different, as seen in the case $x=1$, $y=9$, where it equals $1$ while the sum of the logarithms is $\log_{10}9$. The quotient $\dfrac{x}{y}$ corresponds to the difference of the logarithms.
