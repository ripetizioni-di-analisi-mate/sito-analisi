Solve $\log_3(x-1)+\log_3(x-3)=1$.

- $x=0$
- $x=4$
- $x=0$ or $x=4$
- $x=3$

## Explanation

Both arguments must be positive, so we need both $x>1$ and $x>3$, which together are equivalent to $x>3$. On this domain the sum of the logarithms is the logarithm of their product, and $1=\log_3 3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)(x-3)&=3,\\[6pt]
x^2-4x&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The roots are $x=0$ and $x=4$, and only $x=4$ lies in the domain. The answer with both roots overlooks the domain, since the arguments are negative for $x=0$.
