Let $S$, $B$ and $R$ be real numbers with $R>0$. For which values of $S$ and $B$ is the expression $\log_{10}\dfrac{S-B}{R}$ defined?

- $S\ne B$
- $S>B+R$
- $S\ge B$
- $S>B$

## Explanation

The logarithm is defined only for strictly positive arguments, so $\dfrac{S-B}{R}>0$ is required. Since $R>0$, the fraction has the same sign as its numerator, so the condition is equivalent to $S-B>0$, that is $S>B$. The condition $S\ge B$ also includes $S=B$, for which the argument is zero. The condition $S>B+R$ is equivalent to $\dfrac{S-B}{R}>1$ and only ensures that the logarithm is positive, whereas for $B<S\le B+R$ the logarithm is defined and its value is negative or zero.
