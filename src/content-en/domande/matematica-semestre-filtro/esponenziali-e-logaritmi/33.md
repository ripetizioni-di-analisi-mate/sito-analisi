A positive quantity follows $Q(t)=Q_0e^{\lambda t}$. The measurements are $Q=5$ units at $t=0$ and $Q=20$ units at $t=2\,\mathrm h$. What is $\lambda$?

- $\ln 4\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}$
- $\ln 2\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}$
- $7.5\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}$
- $2\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}$

## Explanation

Dividing the measurements eliminates $Q_0$ and leaves an exponential that depends on the time between them.

$$
\begin{aligned}
e^{\lambda\cdot2\,\mathrm h}&=\frac{20}{5}=4,\\[6pt]
\lambda&=\frac{\ln 4}{2\,\mathrm h}=\frac{2\ln 2}{2\,\mathrm h}=\ln 2\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The quantity therefore doubles every hour. The value $\ln 4\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}$ omits division by the length of the interval. The value $7.5$ equals $\dfrac{20-5}{2}$, the hourly change in a linear model. The value $2\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}$ divides the factor $4$ by two hours without taking a logarithm.
