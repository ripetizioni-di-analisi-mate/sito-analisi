A quantity decays exponentially with a half-life of $4\,\mathrm h$. Using $\log_{10}2\approx0.30$, estimate how long it takes for one-tenth of the initial quantity to remain.

- $13.3\,\mathrm h$
- $40\,\mathrm h$
- $1.2\,\mathrm h$
- $20\,\mathrm h$

## Explanation

After time $t$ the fraction remaining is $\left(\dfrac12\right)^{\frac{t}{4\,\mathrm h}}$. Set it equal to $\dfrac1{10}$ and take the base-ten logarithm of both sides.

$$
-\frac{t}{4\,\mathrm h}\log_{10}2=-1,\qquad t=\frac{4\,\mathrm h}{\log_{10}2}\approx\frac{4\,\mathrm h}{0.30}\approx13.3\,\mathrm h.
$$

The result is consistent, since after $12\,\mathrm h$ the remaining fraction is $\dfrac18$ and after $16\,\mathrm h$ it is $\dfrac1{16}$. The value $40\,\mathrm h$ counts ten half-lives. The value $1.2\,\mathrm h$ multiplies by $\log_{10}2$ instead of dividing.
