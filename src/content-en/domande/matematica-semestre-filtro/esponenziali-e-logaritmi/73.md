For $a>0$, $a\ne1$, and $u>0$, express $\log_{a^2}(u)$ in terms of $\log_a(u)$.

- $\dfrac{1}{2}\log_a(u)$
- $2\log_a(u)$
- $\left(\log_a(u)\right)^2$
- $-\log_a(u)$

## Explanation

Use the change-of-base formula to express the logarithm to base $a^2$ in terms of logarithms to base $a$.

$$
\log_{a^2}u=\frac{\log_a u}{\log_a(a^2)}=\frac{\log_a u}{2}.
$$

The denominator equals $2$ by the power rule, since $\log_a(a^2)=2\log_a a=2$. The value $2\log_a u$ results from confusing $\log_{a^2}u$ with $\log_a(u^2)$. For example, with $a=2$ and $u=16$ we have $\log_4 16=2$, whereas $\log_2 16=4$.
