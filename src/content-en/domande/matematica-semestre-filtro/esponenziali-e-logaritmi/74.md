Two samples of equal initial mass decay exponentially. Their half-lives are $4\,\mathrm{h}$ and $6\,\mathrm{h}$, respectively. After $12\,\mathrm{h}$, what is the ratio of the first mass to the second?

- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $2$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$

## Explanation

Over $12\,\mathrm h$ the first sample undergoes $\dfrac{12}{4}=3$ half-lives and the second undergoes $\dfrac{12}{6}=2$. The initial masses are equal, so they cancel in the ratio.

$$
\frac{m_1}{m_2}=\frac{\left(\frac12\right)^3}{\left(\frac12\right)^2}=\frac12.
$$

The value $\dfrac23$ comes from dividing the half-lives. The value $2$ is the ratio of the second mass to the first. The value $\dfrac14$ corresponds to a difference of two half-lives instead of one.
