A positive quantity follows $Q(t)=Q_0a^t$, with $a>0$, $a\ne1$, and $t$ the numerical value of time in a fixed unit. Which quantity, when plotted against time, gives a straight line through the origin?

- $\dfrac{Q(t)}{Q_0}$
- $Q(t)-Q_0$
- $\dfrac{Q_0}{Q(t)}$
- $\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{Q(t)}{Q_0}\right)$

## Explanation

The ratio to the initial value is $\dfrac{Q(t)}{Q_0}=a^t$. Taking the base-ten logarithm and applying the power rule gives

$$
\log_{10}\left(\frac{Q(t)}{Q_0}\right)=t\log_{10}a,
$$

which is a linear function of $t$ with a constant term of zero, that is, a straight line through the origin with slope $\log_{10}a$. The ratio $\dfrac{Q(t)}{Q_0}$ and its reciprocal are exponential functions of $t$. The difference $Q(t)-Q_0$ is zero at $t=0$ but is not linear.
