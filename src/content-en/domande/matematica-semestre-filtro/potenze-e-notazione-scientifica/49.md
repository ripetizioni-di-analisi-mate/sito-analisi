A positive length is written as $a\cdot10^n\,\mathrm m$ with $1\le a<10$. To express it in micrometers, keep $a$ unchanged. How does the exponent change?

- It becomes $n-6$.
- It becomes $n+6$.
- It remains $n$.
- It becomes $6n$.

## Explanation

One micrometer is $10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$, so one meter contains $10^{6}$ micrometers and the numerical value is multiplied by $10^6$.
$$
a\cdot10^{n}\,\mathrm{m}=a\cdot10^{n}\cdot10^{6}\,\mu\mathrm{m}=a\cdot10^{n+6}\,\mu\mathrm{m}.
$$
The coefficient $a$ remains between $1$ and $10$, so the exponent becomes $n+6$. The exponent $n-6$ corresponds to converting in the opposite direction. The exponent $6n$ comes from multiplying the exponents instead of adding them.
