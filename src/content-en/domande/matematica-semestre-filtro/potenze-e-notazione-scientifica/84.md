What is the value of $\dfrac{(0.04)^2}{(0.2)^3}$?

- $0.02$
- $0.2$
- $2$
- $0.8$

## Explanation

Write the two decimals in scientific notation as $0.04=4\cdot10^{-2}$ and $0.2=2\cdot10^{-1}$, then raise both the coefficients and the powers of ten to the indicated powers.
$$
\frac{(0.04)^2}{(0.2)^3}=\frac{16\cdot10^{-4}}{8\cdot10^{-3}}=2\cdot10^{-4-(-3)}=2\cdot10^{-1}=0.2.
$$
The value $2$ comes from evaluating $-4+3$ as $0$, an error of one in the difference of the exponents. The value $0.02$ comes from making an error of one in the opposite direction.
