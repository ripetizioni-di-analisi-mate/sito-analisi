A homogeneous solution has mass concentration $1.2\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{g/L}$. Calculate the mass of solute in $2.50\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{L}$ of solution. Express the result in scientific notation to two significant figures.

- $3.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{g}$
- $3.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{g}$
- $4.8\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{g}$
- $3.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The mass concentration $c_m=\dfrac{m}{V}$ gives $m=c_mV$. Multiply the coefficients, add the exponents, and cancel the liters.
$$
m=(1.2\cdot2.50)\cdot10^{-2+(-3)}\,\mathrm{g}=3.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
The concentration has two significant figures and the volume has three, so the result has two. The answers with exponents $-4$ and $-6$ are each off by one in the sum $-2+(-3)$.
