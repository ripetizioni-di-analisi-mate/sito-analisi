A disk has area $9\pi\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Use $A=\pi r^2$ to find its radius.

- $9\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

From $A=\pi r^2$ we obtain $r^2=\dfrac{A}{\pi}=9\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The radius is positive, so take the nonnegative square root and halve the even exponent $-6$.
$$
r=\sqrt{9\cdot10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{m}=3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The value $9\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$ is $r^2$ before taking the root. The value $3\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from taking the square root of the coefficient alone. The value $9\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from halving only the exponent.
