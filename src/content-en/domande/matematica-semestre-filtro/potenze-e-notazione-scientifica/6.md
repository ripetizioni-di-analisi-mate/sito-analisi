A square has an area of $16\cdot10^{-6}$ square units. Which value does $\sqrt{16\cdot10^{-6}}$ give for its positive side length?

- $4\cdot10^{-6}$
- $16\cdot10^{-3}$
- $4\cdot10^{-3}$
- $-4\cdot10^{-3}$

## Explanation

The radicand is positive and the exponent $-6$ is even, so take the square roots of the coefficient and of the power of ten separately.
$$
\sqrt{16\cdot10^{-6}}=\sqrt{16}\cdot\sqrt{10^{-6}}=4\cdot10^{-3}.
$$
The root of $10^{-6}$ is $10^{-3}$ because $\left(10^{-3}\right)^2=10^{-6}$. The value $4\cdot10^{-6}$ comes from taking the root of the coefficient alone. The value $-4\cdot10^{-3}$ is ruled out because a square root is nonnegative by definition.
