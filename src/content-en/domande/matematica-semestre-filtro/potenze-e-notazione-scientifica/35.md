The area $A$ of a square sample is expressed in $\mathrm{cm^2}$. What unit must the side length have when calculated as $\sqrt A$?

- $\mathrm{cm^2}$
- $\mathrm{cm^4}$
- $\mathrm{cm}$
- $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$

## Explanation

For a square with side length $\ell$, we have $A=\ell^2$, so $\ell=\sqrt{A}$ because the side length is positive. Take the square root of both the numerical value and the unit.
$$
\sqrt{\mathrm{cm^2}}=\left(\mathrm{cm^2}\right)^{\frac12}=\mathrm{cm}.
$$
The side length is therefore expressed in centimeters, a unit of length. The unit $\mathrm{cm^2}$ comes from taking the square root only of the numerical value. The unit $\mathrm{cm^4}$ comes from confusing taking the square root with squaring.
