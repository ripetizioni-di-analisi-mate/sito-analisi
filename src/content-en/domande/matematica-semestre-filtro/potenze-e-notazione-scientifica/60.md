A table calculates speed as $v=d/t$ and stores times $t$ in milliseconds. Why is the factor converting the numerical value of $1/t$ from $\mathrm{ms}^{-1}$ to $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ greater than one?

- One event per millisecond is one thousand events per second.
- One millisecond lasts one thousand seconds.
- Taking a reciprocal makes every number greater than one.
- The prefix milli always denotes a factor of $10^3$ in the original unit.

## Explanation

Since $1\,\mathrm{ms}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}$, taking reciprocals gives $1\,\mathrm{ms}^{-1}=10^3\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$. The exponent changes sign because the unit of time occurs in the denominator.
