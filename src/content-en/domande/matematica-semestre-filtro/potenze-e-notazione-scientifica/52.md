Two positive readings $a$ and $b$ are expressed numerically in the same unit. A spreadsheet must add their reciprocals. Which expression is valid for all such readings?

- $a^{-1}+b^{-1}=(a+b)^{-1}$
- $a^{-1}+b^{-1}=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}$
- $a^{-1}+b^{-1}=(ab)^{-1}$
- $a^{-1}+b^{-1}=\dfrac{a+b}{ab}$

## Explanation

The reciprocals are $a^{-1}=\dfrac1a$ and $b^{-1}=\dfrac1b$. Since $a$ and $b$ are positive, their product $ab$ is a nonzero common denominator.
$$
\frac1a+\frac1b=\frac{b}{ab}+\frac{a}{ab}=\frac{a+b}{ab}.
$$
The expression $(a+b)^{-1}$ comes from distributing the exponent $-1$ over a sum, although the rule applies only to products. For example, when $a=b=1$ the sum of the reciprocals is $2$, whereas $(a+b)^{-1}=\dfrac12$.
