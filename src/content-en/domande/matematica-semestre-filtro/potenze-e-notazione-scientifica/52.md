Let $a$ and $b$ be positive real numbers. Which equality holds for every choice of $a$ and $b$?

- $a^{-1}+b^{-1}=(a+b)^{-1}$
- $a^{-1}+b^{-1}=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}$
- $a^{-1}+b^{-1}=(ab)^{-1}$
- $a^{-1}+b^{-1}=\dfrac{a+b}{ab}$

## Explanation

The reciprocals are $a^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{a}$ and $b^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{b}$. Since $a$ and $b$ are positive, the product $ab$ is a nonzero common denominator, so
$$
\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}=\frac{b}{ab}+\frac{a}{ab}=\frac{a+b}{ab}.
$$
The equality with $(a+b)^{-1}$ comes from distributing the exponent $-1$ over a sum, whereas this rule holds only for products. For example, when $a=b=1$ the sum of the reciprocals is $2$, whereas $(a+b)^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.
