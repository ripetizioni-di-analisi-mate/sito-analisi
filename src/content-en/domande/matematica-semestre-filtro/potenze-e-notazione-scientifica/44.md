What is the set of all real numbers $x$ satisfying $\sqrt{x^2}=-x$?

- $[0,+\infty)$
- $(-\infty,0)$
- $(-\infty,0]$
- $\mathbb{R}$

## Explanation

For every real $x$, we have $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$, so the equation is equivalent to $|x|=-x$. By the definition of absolute value, $|x|=-x$ when $x\le0$. For $x>0$, however, $|x|=x$, and the equality $x=-x$ would give $x=0$, contradicting $x>0$. Thus the solution set is $(-\infty,0]$. The interval $(-\infty,0)$ excludes zero, which satisfies the equation because $\sqrt{0}=0=-0$.
