Why can the product $2^3\cdot5^3$ be written as a power of ten?

- The bases add, so the product is $(2+5)^3$.
- The two factors have the same exponent, so the product is $(2\cdot5)^3$.
- The exponents multiply, so the product is $10^9$.
- The exponents add, so the product is $10^6$.

## Explanation

The product of two powers with the same exponent is the power of the product of the bases with that exponent, that is $a^n\cdot b^n=(a\cdot b)^n$. Since both factors have exponent $3$, $2^3\cdot5^3=(2\cdot5)^3=10^3$. The expression $(2+5)^3=343$ gives a value different from $2^3\cdot5^3=8\cdot125=1000$. Exponents add only in a product of powers with the same base, so $10^6$ is wrong.
