Why does normalized scientific notation $a\cdot10^n$, with $1\le|a|<10$ and integer $n$, require a special case for zero?

- Zero can be obtained only by taking the coefficient $a=1$.
- An exponent of $n=0$ always makes the product zero.
- With that coefficient restriction, both factors are nonzero.
- A negative power of ten is equal to zero.

## Explanation

An integer power of ten is always positive. Obtaining zero would therefore require $a=0$, which the normalization rule excludes. Zero is recorded separately. Without the rule, $0\cdot10^n$ would represent zero for every exponent.
