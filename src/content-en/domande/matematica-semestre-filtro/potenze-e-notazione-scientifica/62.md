An ideal pendulum swinging through a small angle has period $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$. If its length $L$ is multiplied by $9$ and $g$ stays constant, by what factor is the period multiplied?

- $9$
- $\dfrac13$
- $81$
- $3$

## Explanation

The period is proportional to $\sqrt{L}$ because $2\pi$ and $g$ remain constant. If the length becomes $9L$, the new period is
$$
T'=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{9L}{g}}=\sqrt9\cdot2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}=3T.
$$
The factor $9$ comes out of the square root as $\sqrt9=3$, so the period triples. The value $9$ comes from ignoring the square root. The value $81$ comes from squaring the factor instead of taking its square root. The value $\dfrac13$ comes from reversing the proportionality.
