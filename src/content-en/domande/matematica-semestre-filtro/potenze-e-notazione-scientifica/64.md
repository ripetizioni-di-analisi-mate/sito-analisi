For two positive inputs $a$ and $b$, a model calls for $\sqrt{a+b}$. One step uses $\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ instead. How can you show that the two results differ?

- The square of $\sqrt{a+b}$ equals $a^2+b^2$.
- The square of $\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ always equals $a+b$.
- The extra term in the square of $\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ is negative.
- The square of $\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ contains the additional positive term $2\sqrt{ab}$.

## Explanation

The square of $\sqrt{a+b}$ equals $a+b$. Expand the square of $\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ using the formula for the square of a binomial.
$$
\left(\sqrt a+\sqrt b\right)^2=a+2\sqrt{ab}+b.
$$
Since $a>0$ and $b>0$, the term $2\sqrt{ab}$ is strictly positive, so the two squares differ. Two positive numbers with different squares are different, so $\sqrt a+\sqrt b>\sqrt{a+b}$. For example, when $a=b=1$, the two values are $2$ and $\sqrt2$.
