By how much does $(2+3)^2$ exceed $2^2+3^2$?

- $0$
- $12$
- $6$
- $18$

## Explanation

For the square of a sum, add first and then square, so $(2+3)^2=5^2=25$. The sum of the squares is $2^2+3^2=4+9=13$, so the difference is $25-13=12$. The expansion $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$ shows that the extra term is twice the product, $2\cdot2\cdot3=12$. The value $0$ comes from distributing the exponent over the sum. The value $6$ comes from forgetting the factor $2$ in twice the product.
