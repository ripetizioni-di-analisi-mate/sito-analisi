Let $x>0$ and let $z=\dfrac{1}{x}$. How is $x^2$ written in terms of $z$?

- $z^{-2}$
- $z^{\frac{1}{2}}$
- $-z^2$
- $2z^{-1}$

## Explanation

From $z=\dfrac{1}{x}$ it follows that $x=\dfrac{1}{z}=z^{-1}$. Squaring and applying the power of a power rule gives $x^2=\left(z^{-1}\right)^2=z^{-2}$. The expression $-z^2$ comes from reading the minus sign in the exponent as a change of sign. Since $z>0$, it is negative, whereas $x^2$ is positive, so the two expressions are different.
