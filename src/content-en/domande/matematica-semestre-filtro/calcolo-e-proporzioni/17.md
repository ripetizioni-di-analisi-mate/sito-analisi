A container holds $\dfrac{5}{6}\,\mathrm{L}$ of liquid. As many full $\dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{L}$ portions as possible are poured. How much liquid is left?

- $\dfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{L}$
- $\dfrac{7}{12}\,\mathrm{L}$
- $\dfrac{1}{12}\,\mathrm{L}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

The number of portions is the ratio of the two volumes.
$$
\frac{5}{6}:\frac{1}{4}=\frac{5}{6}\cdot4=\frac{10}{3}=3+\frac{1}{3}.
$$
Thus $3$ whole portions can be filled. They require $\dfrac{3}{4}\,\mathrm{L}$, so the volume left over is
$$
\frac{5}{6}\,\mathrm{L}-\frac{3}{4}\,\mathrm{L}=\frac{10}{12}\,\mathrm{L}-\frac{9}{12}\,\mathrm{L}=\frac{1}{12}\,\mathrm{L}.
$$
The value $\dfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{L}$ treats the fractional part of the quotient as a number of liters. It is actually a fraction of a portion and corresponds to $\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{L}=\dfrac{1}{12}\,\mathrm{L}$.
