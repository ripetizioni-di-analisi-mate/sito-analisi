To $0.4\,\mathrm{L}$ of a solution at $12\,\mathrm{g/L}$, $0.2\,\mathrm{L}$ of pure water is added. The volumes are additive. What is the final concentration?

- $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $10\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $12\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

Adding pure water leaves the solute mass unchanged at $12\,\mathrm{g/L}\cdot0.4\,\mathrm{L}=4.8\,\mathrm{g}$. The volumes are additive, so the final volume is $0.4\,\mathrm{L}+0.2\,\mathrm{L}=0.6\,\mathrm{L}$.
$$
c_f=\frac{4.8\,\mathrm{g}}{0.6\,\mathrm{L}}=8\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The concentration is multiplied by $\dfrac{0.4}{0.6}=\dfrac{2}{3}$. The value $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is the arithmetic mean of $12$ and $0$, which gives equal weight to different volumes.
