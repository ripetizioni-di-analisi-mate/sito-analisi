A homogeneous solution contains $8\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute in $400\,\mathrm{mL}$. How many grams of solute does a $150\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample contain?

- $6\,\mathrm{g}$
- $\frac{64}{3}\,\mathrm{g}$
- $3\,\mathrm{g}$
- $\frac{3}{10}\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The solution is homogeneous, so the ratio of solute mass to volume is the same in every sample. The sample has $\dfrac{150}{400}=\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the total volume, so it contains $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the solute mass.
$$
m=8\,\mathrm{g}\cdot\frac{150}{400}=8\,\mathrm{g}\cdot\frac{3}{8}=3\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
Reversing the volume ratio, that is, multiplying by $\dfrac{400}{150}$, gives $\dfrac{64}{3}\,\mathrm{g}$.
