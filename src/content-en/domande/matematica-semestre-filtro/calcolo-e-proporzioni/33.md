Two solutions of the same solute are mixed. The two initial volumes and their respective concentrations in $\mathrm{g/L}$ are known. The solute is conserved, but the volume contracts during mixing. What information is needed to calculate the final concentration?

- The actual volume of the mixture.
- The sum of the initial volumes, calculated a second time.
- The volume of the more concentrated initial solution.
- The arithmetic mean of the two concentrations.

## Explanation

The solute is conserved, so its final mass is the sum of the initial solute masses, each equal to concentration times volume. The final concentration is this mass divided by the volume of the mixture. Since the volume contracts, the volume of the mixture is less than the sum of the initial volumes and must be determined separately. The arithmetic mean of the concentrations ignores both the volumes and the contraction.
