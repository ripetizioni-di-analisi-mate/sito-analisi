A $10\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample of a solution at $200\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is diluted with water to a volume of $50\,\mathrm{mL}$. Then $10\,\mathrm{mL}$ of the new solution is diluted with water to a volume of $100\,\mathrm{mL}$. What is the final concentration?

- $4\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $40\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $20\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $\dfrac{40}{3}\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

In each dilution the mass of solute in the sample stays the same, so the concentration is multiplied by the ratio of the volume sampled to the final volume.
$$
c_1=200\cdot\frac{10}{50}\,\mathrm{g/L}=40\,\mathrm{g/L},\qquad c_2=40\cdot\frac{10}{100}\,\mathrm{g/L}=4\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The overall factor is $\dfrac{1}{5}\cdot\dfrac{1}{10}=\dfrac{1}{50}$. The value $40\,\mathrm{g/L}$ corresponds to the first dilution alone, while $20\,\mathrm{g/L}$ applies only the second. Dividing by the sum $5+10$ of the two factors instead of their product gives $\dfrac{40}{3}\,\mathrm{g/L}$.
