Mix $200\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a solution at $3\,\mathrm{g/L}$ with $100\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a solution of the same solute at $9\,\mathrm{g/L}$. The volumes are additive and the solute is conserved. Calculate the final concentration.

- $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $4\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $10\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

The solute is conserved and the volumes are additive, so the final concentration is the average of the two concentrations weighted by their respective volumes, expressed in liters.
$$
c_f=\frac{0.2\cdot3+0.1\cdot9}{0.2+0.1}\,\mathrm{g/L}=\frac{1.5}{0.3}\,\mathrm{g/L}=5\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The numerator $1.5\,\mathrm{g}$ is the total solute mass. The value $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is the arithmetic mean of the concentrations, which gives equal weight to different volumes.
