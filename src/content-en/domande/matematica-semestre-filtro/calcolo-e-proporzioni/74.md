Positive volumes of two solutions of the same solute at $2\,\mathrm{g/L}$ and $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$ are mixed. The solute is conserved and the volumes are additive. Which concentration is impossible?

- $7\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $3\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $4\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

The final concentration is the average of the two concentrations weighted by their positive volumes. A weighted average of two distinct values with positive weights lies strictly between them, so
$$
2\,\mathrm{g/L}<c_f<6\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
Therefore $7\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is impossible because it exceeds the higher concentration. The values $3$, $4$, and $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$ can be obtained with suitable volume ratios. For example, equal volumes give $4\,\mathrm{g/L}$.
