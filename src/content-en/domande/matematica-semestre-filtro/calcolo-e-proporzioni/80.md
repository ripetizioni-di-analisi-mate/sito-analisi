The volume of a solution at $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$ decreases by $20\%$ because only the solvent evaporates. The solute remains dissolved. What is the new concentration?

- $4.8\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $7.5\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $7.2\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

The solute remains dissolved, so its mass is unchanged. The volume falls to $80\%$ of its initial value. At fixed solute mass, concentration is inversely proportional to volume, so divide by $0.80$.
$$
c'=\frac{6\,\mathrm{g/L}}{0.80}=7.5\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
For example, $6\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute is initially contained in $1\,\mathrm{L}$ and finally in $0.8\,\mathrm{L}$. Increasing the concentration by $20\%$ gives $7.2\,\mathrm{g/L}$, while multiplying it by $0.80$ gives $4.8\,\mathrm{g/L}$.
