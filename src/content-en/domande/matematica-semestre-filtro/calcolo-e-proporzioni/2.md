Calculate $\dfrac{5}{6}:\dfrac{1}{3}$.

- $\frac{5}{4}$
- $\frac{5}{2}$
- $\frac{7}{2}$
- $-\frac{5}{2}$

## Explanation

The divisor $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is nonzero, so dividing by $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal, $3$.
$$
\frac{5}{6}:\frac{1}{3}=\frac{5}{6}\cdot3=\frac{15}{6}=\frac{5}{2}.
$$
The result is greater than $\dfrac{5}{6}$, as always occurs when a positive number is divided by a number between $0$ and $1$. The value $-\dfrac{5}{2}$ has the wrong sign because the dividend and divisor are both positive.
