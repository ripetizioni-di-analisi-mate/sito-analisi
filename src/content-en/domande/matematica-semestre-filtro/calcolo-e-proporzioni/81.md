At $2$, $4$, and $6\,\mathrm{min}$ after a liquid transfer begins, the measured volumes transferred are $6$, $12$, and $18\,\mathrm{L}$, respectively. Which conclusion follows from these measurements alone?

- The measurements prove that the instantaneous flow rate is constant throughout the transfer.
- The measurements are consistent only with inverse proportionality because time is increasing.
- The measurements are consistent with direct proportionality because the ratio of volume to time is constant at the observed points.
- The fact that volume and time both increase is sufficient to conclude that they are directly proportional.

## Explanation

At the three times, the ratio of volume to time is
$$
\frac{6}{2}=\frac{12}{4}=\frac{18}{6}=3\,\mathrm{L/min},
$$
so all three points lie on the line $V=3t$ through the origin and are consistent with direct proportionality. Three measurements do not describe what happens between observations, so they cannot show that the flow rate is always constant. The relation $V=3+2t$ is also increasing, yet it does not represent direct proportionality.
