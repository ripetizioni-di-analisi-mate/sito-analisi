Two samples start with the same positive mass. The first sample’s mass is reduced to $20\%$ of its initial value. The second sample’s mass is reduced by $20\%$ of its initial value. Which sample retains more mass?

- The first, because it retains $80\%$ while the second retains $20\%$.
- The second, because it retains $80\%$ while the first retains $20\%$.
- Both retain $20\%$ of their initial mass.
- Both retain $80\%$ of their initial mass.

## Explanation

Reducing *to* $20\%$ specifies the fraction remaining, so the first sample retains $20\%$ of its initial mass. Reducing *by* $20\%$ specifies the fraction removed, so the second sample’s mass is multiplied by $1-\dfrac{20}{100}=0.80$ and it retains $80\%$. Therefore the second sample retains more mass. For example, with an initial mass of $100\,\mathrm{g}$, the first sample retains $20\,\mathrm{g}$ and the second retains $80\,\mathrm{g}$.
