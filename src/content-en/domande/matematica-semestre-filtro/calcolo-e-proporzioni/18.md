Mix $300\,\mathrm{g}$ of a solution containing $10\%$ solute by mass with $100\,\mathrm{g}$ of a solution of the same solute at $30\%$ by mass. What is the mass percentage of solute in the mixture?

- $20\%$
- $25\%$
- $40\%$
- $15\%$

## Explanation

The solute masses add, as do the sample masses, so the final percentage is the average of the two percentages weighted by the sample masses. There is $30\,\mathrm{g}+30\,\mathrm{g}=60\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute in $400\,\mathrm{g}$ of mixture.
$$
\frac{300\cdot0.10+100\cdot0.30}{300+100}=\frac{60\,\mathrm{g}}{400\,\mathrm{g}}=15\%.
$$
The value $20\%$ is the arithmetic mean, which gives equal weight to different masses. Interchanging the weights of the two samples gives $25\%$, while adding the percentages gives $40\%$.
