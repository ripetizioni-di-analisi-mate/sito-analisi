One-third of the contents is removed from an initially full container. One-third of what remains is then removed. How do the two volumes removed compare?

- The volumes are equal because the fraction is one-third both times.
- The second is larger because the remaining volume is two-thirds of the initial volume.
- The comparison depends on the initial volume in the container.
- The second is smaller because the second one-third applies to a smaller volume.

## Explanation

Let $V$ be the initial volume. The first withdrawal is $\dfrac{1}{3}V=\dfrac{3}{9}V$ and leaves $\dfrac{2}{3}V$ in the container. The second withdrawal is one-third of this remainder, so it is
$$
\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}V=\frac{2}{9}V.
$$
Since $\dfrac{2}{9}<\dfrac{3}{9}$, the second withdrawal is smaller for every positive initial volume. The fraction removed is the same, but the base for the second withdrawal is smaller.
