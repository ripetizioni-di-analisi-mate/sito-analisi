Positive volumes of two solutions of the same solute at $2\,\mathrm{g/L}$ and $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$ are mixed. The solute is conserved and the volumes are additive. When does the final concentration exceed $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$?

- When the volumes are equal.
- When the volume of the solution at $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is greater.
- When the volume of the solution at $2\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is greater.
- Always, for any positive volumes.

## Explanation

The value $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is the midpoint between $2\,\mathrm{g/L}$ and $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$, the concentration obtained with equal volumes. Let $q$ be the fraction of the final volume supplied by the solution at $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$. The concentration in grams per liter is
$$
c_f=2(1-q)+8q=2+6q.
$$
Thus $c_f>5$ if and only if $q>\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, if the volume of the solution at $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is greater than that of the other solution.
