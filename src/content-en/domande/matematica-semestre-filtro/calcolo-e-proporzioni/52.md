One solution contains $5\%$ solute by mass, and another contains $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$ of the same solute. What information allows the two concentrations to be compared in grams per liter?

- The density of the pure solute.
- The density of the solution whose concentration is given as a mass percentage.
- The density of the solution whose concentration is already given in grams per liter.
- Only the solute mass in the solution whose concentration is given as a mass percentage.

## Explanation

A concentration of $5\%$ by mass means $5\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute per $100\,\mathrm{g}$ of solution, whereas $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$ gives grams of solute per liter of solution. Converting the first concentration to the second form requires the mass of one liter of the solution, which can be found from the density of the solution labeled with the mass percentage. For example, at a density of $1.02\,\mathrm{kg/L}$, one liter has a mass of $1020\,\mathrm{g}$ and contains $51\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute.
