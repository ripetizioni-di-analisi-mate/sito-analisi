A mass of $100\,\mathrm{g}$ increases by $20\%$, and then $10\,\mathrm{g}$ is added. In a second trial the addition comes before the percent increase. By how many grams does the final mass in the second trial exceed that in the first?

- $0$
- $10$
- $2$
- $12$

## Explanation

The percent increase multiplies the mass present at that point, while the fixed addition adds $10\,\mathrm{g}$ to it. The first trial gives $100\cdot1.20+10=130\,\mathrm{g}$. In the second trial, the addition takes place first, so the added $10\,\mathrm{g}$ is also multiplied by $1.20$.
$$
(100+10)\cdot1.20=132\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
The difference is therefore $132-130=2\,\mathrm{g}$, or $20\%$ of the added $10\,\mathrm{g}$.
