A sensor takes several readings. The same additive error of $2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ makes every reading too high. How does the mean change when all the readings are corrected?

- It decreases by $2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ divided by the number of readings.
- It decreases by $2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ times the number of readings.
- It stays the same because all readings receive the same correction.
- It decreases by $2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ regardless of the number of readings.

## Explanation

The mean is the sum $S$ of the readings divided by their number $n$. Correcting every reading subtracts $2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ from each term, so the sum decreases by $2n$ degrees.
$$
\frac{S-2n}{n}=\frac{S}{n}-2.
$$
The mean therefore decreases by $2\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ regardless of the number of readings. Multiplying or dividing the correction by $n$ overlooks the fact that $n$ appears both in the decrease in the sum and in the divisor.
