One-quarter of the volume of a homogeneous solution is removed, and the remaining liquid is kept. How does the concentration change?

- It decreases by 25%.
- It increases by 25%.
- It falls to one-quarter of the initial value.
- It stays the same.

## Explanation

The solution is homogeneous, so every portion has the same concentration. The withdrawal takes one-quarter of the volume $V$ and one-quarter of the solute mass $m$. Thus the remaining liquid contains $\dfrac{3}{4}m$ of solute in $\dfrac{3}{4}V$.
$$
c=\frac{\frac{3}{4}m}{\frac{3}{4}V}=\frac{m}{V}.
$$
The concentration therefore stays the same. A decrease would attribute the withdrawal to the solute alone, while an increase would attribute it to the solvent alone.
