Two solutions of the same solute at $2\,\mathrm{g/L}$ and $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$ are mixed. The volumes are additive and the solute is conserved. The mixture has a volume of $300\,\mathrm{mL}$ and a concentration of $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$. Calculate the volume of the solution at $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$.

- $100\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $150\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $225\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $200\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

Let $v$ be the volume in liters of the solution at $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$. The other solution then has volume $0.3-v$. The solute mass in the mixture is the sum of the solute masses in the two samples.
$$
2(0.3-v)+8v=6\cdot0.3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0.6+6v=1.8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v=0.2.
$$
Thus the mixture requires $200\,\mathrm{mL}$ of the solution at $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$ and $100\,\mathrm{mL}$ of the other solution. Interchanging the fractions gives $100\,\mathrm{mL}$. Taking $\dfrac{6}{8}$ of the total volume gives $225\,\mathrm{mL}$, which ignores the solute in the less concentrated solution.
