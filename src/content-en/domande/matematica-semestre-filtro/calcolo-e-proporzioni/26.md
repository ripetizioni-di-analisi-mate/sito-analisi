Two scales each overestimate the mass of their reference sample by $2\,\mathrm{g}$. The first sample has a reference mass of $40\,\mathrm{g}$ and the second $80\,\mathrm{g}$. How do the relative errors compare?

- The first scale has the greater relative error because its reference mass is smaller.
- The relative errors are equal because the errors in grams are equal.
- The second scale has the greater relative error because its reference mass is larger.
- Divide $2\,\mathrm{g}$ by the sum of the two reference masses to find the relative error.

## Explanation

The relative error is the error divided by the reference mass for the same scale.
$$
\frac{2\,\mathrm{g}}{40\,\mathrm{g}}=\frac{1}{20}=5\%,\qquad \frac{2\,\mathrm{g}}{80\,\mathrm{g}}=\frac{1}{40}=2.5\%.
$$
The numerators are equal and positive, so the greater ratio is the one with the smaller denominator, corresponding to the first scale. Equal errors in grams give different relative errors because the reference masses differ.
