An alloy contains only copper and zinc. Zinc makes up $35\%$ of the mass of the alloy, and the copper has a mass of $260\,\mathrm{g}$. What is the mass of the alloy?

- $400\,\mathrm{g}$
- $351\,\mathrm{g}$
- $\dfrac{5200}{7}\,\mathrm{g}$
- $140\,\mathrm{g}$

## Explanation

The alloy contains only two substances, so their mass percentages sum to $100\%$ and copper makes up $65\%$ of the total. Let $m$ be the mass of the alloy. Then $0.65\,m=260\,\mathrm{g}$, so
$$
m=\frac{260\,\mathrm{g}}{0.65}=400\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
The zinc has a mass of $400\,\mathrm{g}-260\,\mathrm{g}=140\,\mathrm{g}$. Assigning the zinc percentage to copper gives $\dfrac{5200}{7}\,\mathrm{g}$, while adding $35\%$ to the copper mass gives $351\,\mathrm{g}$.
