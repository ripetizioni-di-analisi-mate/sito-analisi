Two containers hold different amounts of water. One-quarter of the water in each container is removed. What fraction of the initial combined volume is removed?

- One-half, because two quarters are removed.
- One-eighth, because one-quarter is divided between two containers.
- One-quarter, because the two withdrawals take the same fraction of their respective volumes.
- The fraction depends on how much the two initial volumes differ.

## Explanation

Let $V_1$ and $V_2$ be the initial volumes, so the initial combined volume is $V_1+V_2$. The total volume removed is
$$
\frac{V_1}{4}+\frac{V_2}{4}=\frac{V_1+V_2}{4}.
$$
Therefore one-quarter of the combined volume is removed, whatever the initial volumes. This holds because each withdrawal takes one-quarter of its own base. Adding the two fractions $\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{4}$ would give one-half, as if the withdrawals both referred to a single container.
