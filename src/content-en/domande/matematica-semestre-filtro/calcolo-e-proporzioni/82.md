A $200\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample of a solution is diluted with pure water to a volume of $250\,\mathrm{mL}$. How does the solute concentration change?

- It decreases by $25\%$.
- It stays the same.
- It decreases by $80\%$.
- It decreases by $20\%$.

## Explanation

The added water contains no solute, so the solute mass stays the same. At fixed mass the concentration is inversely proportional to volume, so multiply it by
$$
\frac{200\,\mathrm{mL}}{250\,\mathrm{mL}}=\frac{4}{5}=0.80.
$$
The factor $0.80$ corresponds to a decrease of $20\%$. The value $25\%$ is the relative increase in volume, which differs from the relative decrease in concentration. Treating the multiplicative factor as the percentage removed gives $80\%$.
