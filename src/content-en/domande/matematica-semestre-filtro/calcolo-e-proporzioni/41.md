Calculate $\dfrac{5}{6}-\left(\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$.

- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{7}{12}$

## Explanation

First find the sum of $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ using the common denominator $12$.
$$
\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{4}=\frac{4}{12}+\frac{3}{12}=\frac{7}{12}.
$$
Then subtract this sum from $\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{10}{12}$, giving $\dfrac{10-7}{12}=\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{1}{4}$. Subtracting $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and adding $\dfrac{1}{4}$ gives $\dfrac{3}{4}$, which changes the sign of only the first term in $\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}$.
