Solve $\dfrac1{x-1}+\dfrac1{x+1}=1$ over the real numbers.

- $\{1-\sqrt2,1+\sqrt2\}$
- $\{-1,1\}$
- $\{-1-\sqrt2,-1+\sqrt2\}$
- $\{1+\sqrt2\}$

## Explanation

The domain excludes $x=1$ and $x=-1$. Multiplying both sides by $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$ gives
$$
(x+1)+(x-1)=x^2-1,\qquad x^2-2x-1=0,\qquad x=1\pm\sqrt2 .
$$
Neither root equals $\pm1$, so both are solutions. The set $\{-1,1\}$ consists of the values excluded from the domain. The set $\{1+\sqrt2\}$ omits the negative root, which is admissible.
