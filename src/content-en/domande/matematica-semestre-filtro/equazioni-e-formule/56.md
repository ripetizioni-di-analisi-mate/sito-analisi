The period of an ideal oscillation is $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$, with $\ell,g>0$. With $g$ fixed, by what factor must $\ell$ change to double $T$?

- $2$
- $\sqrt{2}$
- $4$
- $\dfrac12$

## Explanation

Divide by $2\pi$ and square both sides. Both are positive, so this operation preserves equivalence.
$$
\ell=g\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^2 .
$$
With $g$ fixed, the length is proportional to $T^2$. If the period becomes $2T$, the length is multiplied by $2^2=4$. The factor $2$ comes from treating $\ell$ as proportional to $T$, while $\sqrt2$ is the factor by which $T$ is multiplied when $\ell$ doubles.
