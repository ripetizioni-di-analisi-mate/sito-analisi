What condition is necessary and sufficient for $F=k\ell$ to determine a unique real $k$ for every real value of $F$?

- $\ell\ne0$
- $F\ne0$
- $F=0$
- $\ell=0$

## Explanation

The unknown is $k$, and its coefficient is $\ell$. If $\ell\ne0$, dividing both sides by $\ell$ gives the unique value
$$
k=\frac{F}{\ell}.
$$
If $\ell=0$, the equation becomes $F=0$. For $F=0$, every $k$ satisfies it. For $F\ne0$, no $k$ satisfies it. The condition $\ell\ne0$ is therefore both *necessary* and *sufficient*. The condition $F\ne0$ is insufficient because there is no solution when $\ell=0$.
