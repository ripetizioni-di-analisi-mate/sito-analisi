Two resistors $R_1=4\,\Omega$ and $R_2$ in series are connected across voltage $V=12\,\mathrm{V}$ and carry current $I=2\,\mathrm{A}$. Given $V=I(R_1+R_2)$, what is $R_2$?

- $6\,\Omega$
- $2\,\Omega$
- $10\,\Omega$
- $4\,\Omega$

## Explanation

The current is positive, so divide both sides by $I$ and then subtract $R_1$.
$$
R_1+R_2=\frac{V}{I}=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{2\,\mathrm{A}}=6\,\Omega,\qquad R_2=6\,\Omega-4\,\Omega=2\,\Omega.
$$
Indeed, $2\cdot(4+2)=12$. The value $6\,\Omega$ is the total resistance rather than the resistance of the second resistor. The value $4\,\Omega$ comes from $V-IR_1=12-8$ without subsequently dividing by $I$, while $10\,\Omega$ comes from adding $R_1$ instead of subtracting it.
