Two solutions of the same solute, with concentrations of $2\,\mathrm{g/L}$ and $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$, are mixed. Their volumes are additive, and the solute is conserved. The final volume is $0.6\,\mathrm{L}$ and the final concentration is $5.5\,\mathrm{g/L}$. Calculate the volume of the second solution.

- $0.25\,\mathrm{L}$
- $0.4125\,\mathrm{L}$
- $0.35\,\mathrm{L}$
- $0.45\,\mathrm{L}$

## Explanation

Let $V_2$ be the numerical value of the second volume in liters. Since the volumes are additive, the first volume is $0.6-V_2$. The final solute mass is $5.5\cdot0.6=3.3\,\mathrm{g}$, so the solute balance gives
$$
2(0.6-V_2)+8V_2=3.3,\qquad 6V_2=2.1,\qquad V_2=0.35 .
$$
The value $0.25\,\mathrm{L}$ is the volume of the first solution, while $0.4125\,\mathrm{L}$ results from attributing all the solute to the second solution.
