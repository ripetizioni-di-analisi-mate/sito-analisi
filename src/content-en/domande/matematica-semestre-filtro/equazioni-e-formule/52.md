Solve $\dfrac{x+3}{x-2}=\dfrac{5}{x-2}$ over the real numbers.

- $x=2$
- $x=8$
- $x=-3$
- The equation has no solutions

## Explanation

The denominator is zero at $x=2$, so the domain is $x\ne2$. On this domain, $x-2$ is nonzero, so both sides can be multiplied by $x-2$.
$$
x+3=5,\qquad x=2 .
$$
The original domain excludes the only candidate, so the equation **has no solutions**. The answer $x=2$ overlooks the restriction on the denominator. The value $8$ comes from adding $3$ to $5$ instead of subtracting it.
