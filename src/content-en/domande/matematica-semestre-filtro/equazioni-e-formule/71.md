The height in meters of a ball thrown upward is $h(t)=1+4t-5t^2$, with $t$ in seconds. Does the ball reach a height of $3\,\mathrm{m}$?

- No, because the discriminant of $h(t)=3$ is negative.
- Yes, at time $t=0.4\,\mathrm{s}$.
- Yes, at two distinct times.
- Yes, at exactly one time, because the discriminant is zero.

## Explanation

Set $h(t)=3$ and move all terms to the right.
$$
5t^2-4t+2=0,\qquad \Delta=16-40=-24 .
$$
The discriminant is negative, so the equation has no real solutions and the ball **does not reach** $3\,\mathrm{m}$. Indeed, the maximum height occurs at $t=0.4\,\mathrm{s}$ and equals $h(0.4)=1+1.6-0.8=1.8\,\mathrm{m}$. At time $0.4\,\mathrm{s}$, the ball is at its highest point rather than at height $3\,\mathrm{m}$.
