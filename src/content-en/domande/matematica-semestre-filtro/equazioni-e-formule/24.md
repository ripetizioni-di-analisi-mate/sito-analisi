Solve $\dfrac{3}{x-2}+\dfrac{1}{x+2}=\dfrac{8}{x^2-4}$ over the real numbers.

- $x=3$
- $x=0$
- $x=1$
- The equation has no solutions

## Explanation

The domain excludes $x=2$ and $x=-2$. Since $x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)$, multiply both sides by $x^2-4$. The factor $x+2$ remains in the first term and the factor $x-2$ in the second.
$$
3(x+2)+(x-2)=8,\qquad 4x+4=8,\qquad x=1 .
$$
The value $1$ belongs to the domain. The value $3$ results from interchanging the two factors and writing $3(x-2)+(x+2)=8$.
