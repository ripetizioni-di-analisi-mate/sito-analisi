What is the real solution set of $\sqrt{x+2}=x$?

- $\{-1,2\}$
- $\{2\}$
- $\{-1\}$
- $\{0,2\}$

## Explanation

The square root is nonnegative, so a solution must satisfy $x\ge0$. Squaring both sides gives an equation that may have extra candidate solutions.
$$
x+2=x^2,\qquad x^2-x-2=0,\qquad (x-2)(x+1)=0 .
$$
At $x=2$, both sides equal $2$. At $x=-1$, the left side equals $1$ and the right side equals $-1$, so $-1$ is an extraneous solution. The set $\{-1,2\}$ comes from failing to check the candidates after squaring.
