The period of an ideal pendulum is $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$. With $T=2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $g=\pi^2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the length $\ell$?

- $1\,\mathrm{m}$
- $4\,\mathrm{m}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- $\pi\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Divide by $2\pi$ and square both sides, which are positive. Then multiply by $g$.
$$
\frac{T^2}{4\pi^2}=\frac{\ell}{g},\qquad \ell=\frac{gT^2}{4\pi^2}=\frac{\pi^2\cdot4}{4\pi^2}\,\mathrm{m}=1\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
The value $4\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from dividing by $\pi^2$ instead of $(2\pi)^2=4\pi^2$, omitting the square of the factor $2$. The value $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ comes from squaring $2\pi$ without squaring $T$, while $\pi\,\mathrm{m}$ leaves both unsquared.
