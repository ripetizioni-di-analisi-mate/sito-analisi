A volume $50\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a solution at concentration $2\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ is diluted with water to a concentration of $0.5\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. The amount of solute is conserved, so $c_1V_1=c_2V_2$. What is the final volume $V_2$?

- $12.5\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $150\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $200\,\mathrm{mL}$
- $2000\,\mathrm{mL}$

## Explanation

Since $c_2>0$, divide both sides by $c_2$.
$$
V_2=\frac{c_1V_1}{c_2}=\frac{2\cdot50}{0.5}\,\mathrm{mL}=200\,\mathrm{mL}.
$$
The concentration units cancel, leaving milliliters. Dilution lowers the concentration, so the volume must increase. The value $150\,\mathrm{mL}$ gives the volume of water added rather than the final volume. The value $12.5\,\mathrm{mL}$ comes from interchanging $c_1$ and $c_2$.
