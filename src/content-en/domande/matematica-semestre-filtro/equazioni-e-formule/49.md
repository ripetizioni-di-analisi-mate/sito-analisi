Which equation results from multiplying both sides of $\dfrac{x+1}{6}-\dfrac{x-2}{4}=1$ by the least common multiple of the denominators?

- $2(x+1)-3(x-2)=1$
- $3(x+1)-2(x-2)=12$
- $2(x+1)-3x-6=12$
- $2(x+1)-3(x-2)=12$

## Explanation

The least common multiple of $6$ and $4$ is $12$. Since $12=6\cdot2$ and $12=4\cdot3$, multiply the first numerator by $2$, the second by $3$, and the right side by $12$ as well.
$$
2(x+1)-3(x-2)=12 .
$$
The equation with right side $1$ results from omitting the factor $12$ in the term written without a fraction. The equation with $-3x-6$ results from changing the sign of only the first term of $x-2$.
