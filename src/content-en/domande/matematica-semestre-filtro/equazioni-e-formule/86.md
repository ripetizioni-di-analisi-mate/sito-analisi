A volume $0.4\,\mathrm{L}$ of a solution at concentration $3\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is mixed with $0.1\,\mathrm{L}$ of a solution of the same solute at concentration $13\,\mathrm{g/L}$. The volumes are additive, and the solute is conserved. What is the final concentration?

- $8\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $11\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $2.5\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

The solute mass is the sum of the masses of solute in the two solutions, and the final volume is $0.5\,\mathrm{L}$.
$$
m=3\cdot0.4+13\cdot0.1=1.2+1.3=2.5\,\mathrm{g},\qquad C=\frac{2.5\,\mathrm{g}}{0.5\,\mathrm{L}}=5\,\mathrm{g/L}.
$$
The value $8$ is the unweighted mean of $3$ and $13$, which overlooks the different volumes. The value $11$ interchanges the two volumes. The value $2.5$ is the solute mass in grams before division by the volume.
