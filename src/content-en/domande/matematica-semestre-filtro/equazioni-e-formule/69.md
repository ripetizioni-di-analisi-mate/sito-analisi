In the relation $Q=mc(T_f-T_i)$, the measured values are $Q=0$ and $T_f=T_i$, with $c>0$. What does the equation tell us about the mass $m>0$?

- The mass remains undetermined.
- The mass is zero.
- The mass equals $c$.
- No positive mass satisfies the equation.

## Explanation

Since $T_f=T_i$, the temperature change is zero and the right side is zero for every mass.
$$
0=m\cdot c\cdot0 .
$$
The equality holds for every $m>0$, so the data do not determine the mass. The rearranged formula $m=\dfrac{Q}{c(T_f-T_i)}$ cannot be used because the divisor is zero. No mass would satisfy the equation if the measured value were $Q\ne0$ with the same zero temperature change.
