A car brakes with constant acceleration $a=-2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. After $4\,\mathrm{s}$ of braking its speed is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Using the model $v=v_0+at$, what is the speed $v_0$ at the start of braking?

- $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $8.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

In the model $v=v_0+at$, $v_0$ is isolated by subtracting $at$ from both sides, so $v_0=v-at$. The acceleration is negative because the car slows down, so the term $at$ equals $(-2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2})\cdot(4\,\mathrm{s})=-10\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
$$
v_0=6\,\mathrm{m/s}-(-10\,\mathrm{m/s})=16\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
The initial speed is greater than the final speed, as happens when a car brakes. The value $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from adding $at$ to $v$ instead of subtracting it, while $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is only the decrease in speed during braking. The value $8.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from subtracting the acceleration without multiplying it by the time.
