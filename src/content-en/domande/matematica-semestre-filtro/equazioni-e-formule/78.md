A solution at concentration $2\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is mixed with one at $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$. The volumes are additive, and the solute is conserved. Which final concentration is impossible?

- $3\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $7\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $4\,\mathrm{g/L}$
- $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$

## Explanation

With nonnegative volumes $V_1$ and $V_2$ and with $V=V_1+V_2>0$, the solute balance gives the final concentration as a weighted mean.
$$
C=\frac{2V_1+6V_2}{V_1+V_2}.
$$
Since the weights $\dfrac{V_1}{V}$ and $\dfrac{V_2}{V}$ lie between zero and one and sum to $1$, the concentration $C$ lies between $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{g/L}$. Thus $7\,\mathrm{g/L}$ is **impossible**, while $3$, $4$, and $5\,\mathrm{g/L}$ can be obtained by choosing suitable volume ratios.
