What is the minimum value of $x^2-4x+5$ as $x$ ranges over the real numbers?

- $1$
- $2$
- $5$
- $-1$

## Explanation

Complete the square by writing $x^2-4x$ as $(x-2)^2-4$.
$$
x^2-4x+5=(x-2)^2-4+5=(x-2)^2+1 .
$$
The square of a real number is nonnegative, so the expression is at least $1$, attaining exactly $1$ at $x=2$. Thus the minimum is $1$, consistent with the discriminant $16-20=-4$, which rules out real zeros. The value $2$ is the point where the minimum is attained. The value $-1$ comes from using the wrong sign for the constant, $(x-2)^2-1$.
