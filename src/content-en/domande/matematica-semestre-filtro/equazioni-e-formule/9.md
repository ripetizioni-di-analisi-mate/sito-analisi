Solve $\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}=x+3$ over the real numbers.

- $\mathbb{R}$
- $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{3\}$
- $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-3\}$
- $\{3\}$

## Explanation

The denominator is zero at $x=3$, so the domain is $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{3\}$. Since $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$, for $x\ne3$ we can cancel the factor $x-3$.
$$
\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3 .
$$
The resulting equality holds for every $x$ in the domain, so the solution set is $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{3\}$. The answer $\mathbb{R}$ overlooks that the left side is undefined at $x=3$.
