A gas is compressed adiabatically and receives $75\,\mathrm{J}$ of work from its surroundings. What is its internal-energy change?

- $+75\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-75\,\mathrm{J}$
- $+150\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-150\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Adiabatic means $Q=0$. Taking work as positive when done by the gas, a compression receiving $75\,\mathrm{J}$ has $W=-75\,\mathrm{J}$. The first law therefore gives $\Delta U=0-(-75)=75\,\mathrm{J}$. No heat exchange does not prevent internal energy from increasing.
