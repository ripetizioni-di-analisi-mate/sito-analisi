A motor lifts a $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass through $3\,\mathrm{m}$ in $4\,\mathrm{s}$ at constant velocity. Neglecting losses, what is average power? Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

- $600\,\mathrm{W}$
- $50\,\mathrm{W}$
- $150\,\mathrm{W}$
- $60\,\mathrm{W}$
- $200\,\mathrm{W}$

## Explanation

Constant velocity leaves kinetic energy unchanged. The motor’s work therefore increases potential energy by $mgh=20\cdot10\cdot3=600\,\mathrm{J}$. Average power is work divided by time, $\dfrac{600}{4}=150\,\mathrm{W}$. The value $600$ represents energy, not power.
