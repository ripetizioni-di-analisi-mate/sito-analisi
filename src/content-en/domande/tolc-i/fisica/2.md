A body starts from rest with constant acceleration $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What distance does it travel in the first $3\,\mathrm{s}$?

- $6\,\mathrm{m}$
- $9\,\mathrm{m}$
- $18\,\mathrm{m}$
- $3\,\mathrm{m}$
- $12\,\mathrm{m}$

## Explanation

Initial velocity is zero, so displacement is $s=\dfrac12at^2$. Substitution gives $\dfrac12\cdot2\cdot3^2=9\,\mathrm{m}$. Acceleration keeps the motion in the positive direction, so distance and displacement coincide. The product $at$ would instead give a velocity, not a distance.
