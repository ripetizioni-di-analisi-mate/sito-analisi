Two identical conducting spheres initially carry $+6\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ and $-2\,\mu\mathrm{C}$. They touch and are then separated, with no charge exchanged externally and far from other bodies. What is the final charge on each sphere?

- $+4\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $+8\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $+2\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $-2\,\mu\mathrm{C}$
- $0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$

## Explanation

Total charge is conserved and equals $6-2=4\,\mu\mathrm{C}$. During contact, charges redistribute until equilibrium; for identical spheres in symmetrical conditions, the final charge divides equally. Each therefore carries $\dfrac42=2\,\mu\mathrm{C}$. Adding the initial magnitudes would give a total charge that is not conserved.
