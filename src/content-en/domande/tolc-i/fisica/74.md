Two wires of the same material and temperature have equal length. The second has twice the cross-sectional area. Given $R=\dfrac{\rho_e\ell}{A}$, how do their resistances compare?

- The second is twice the first
- They are equal
- The second is four times greater
- The second is half the first
- The second is one quarter

## Explanation

Material and temperature fix resistivity $\rho_e$, while length is equal. Only inverse dependence on area remains, $\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}=\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}=\dfrac12$. A larger cross section allows greater current for the same potential difference.
