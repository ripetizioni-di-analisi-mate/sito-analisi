A group of students interviews one hundred people leaving a station during a single rainy afternoon. Seventy say they used public transport that day. The report specifies the place, time, and conditions of data collection; it does not present the respondents as a random sample of all residents.

Discussing the results, the students distinguish describing the people encountered from estimating citywide habits. The location favors encounters with transport users, while rain may have changed travel choices; the report does not measure how much either factor contributed. For a later study, it proposes interviews at different places and times under a plan defined in advance. A journalist writes the headline “Seventy percent of residents travel by public transport every day”: the students challenge both its extension to all residents and its shift from the observed day to an everyday habit.

To whom does the survey’s seventy percent directly refer?

- The one hundred people interviewed that afternoon
- All city residents
- All commuters throughout the year
- Everyone who uses the station
- Only the student interviewers

## Explanation

Seventy responses out of one hundred describe the group actually interviewed. The text excludes presenting it as a random resident sample. Extending the result to all station users would still go beyond those encountered on that occasion.
