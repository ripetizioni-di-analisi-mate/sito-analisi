“We had the only ticket for the last train,” Ada recounts. She later adds, “I say ‘we had’ because there were two of us, but there was one piece of card in my pocket.”

Which ambiguity does the clarification resolve?

- The plural form certifies that the ticket entitled both people to travel
- The pronoun distinguishes who paid for the ticket from who kept it
- The plural refers to the people involved without multiplying the tickets
- The phrase “last train” indicates the two had already taken different trains
- The account attributes the card first to Ada and then to a different passenger

## Explanation

The speaker relates two people to a single piece of card. The first-person plural marks a shared experience and does not establish how many tickets there are.
