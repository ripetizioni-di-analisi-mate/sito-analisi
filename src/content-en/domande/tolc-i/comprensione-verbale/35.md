An editorial team’s manual distinguishes submitting a draft from its approval. The author uploads the text by Monday; the reviewer then has two working days to request changes. If none are requested, the draft is considered approved when that period expires. If corrections are requested, the two-day count restarts when the new version is uploaded. An automated message confirms only that the file has arrived.

The coordinator notes that this system prevents a receipt from being confused with a judgment on the content. She also explains that the deadline is a maximum: explicit approval may arrive earlier. However, the manual sets no maximum duration for the whole process, because revision may require more than one resubmission. Authors must therefore keep the dates of their versions, not just the date of the first submission.

Keeping the dates of the versions mainly serves to:

- Prove that no draft contained errors
- Replace the reviewer’s reading
- Automatically advance approval
- Eliminate every possible request for changes
- Reconstruct when each deadline period begins

## Explanation

The recommendation follows from the count restarting with every new version. Dates allow those stages to be reconstructed. They do not certify the quality of the texts or change the conditions under which a draft is approved.
