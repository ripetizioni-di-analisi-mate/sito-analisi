For three months, a municipal library extended its opening hours until ten in the evening. The final report records an increase in evening entries, while those in the morning remained stable. The counters record every entry: a person entering twice is therefore counted twice. The report measures neither time spent reading nor the distinction between new and established users.

The management considers the experiment useful for finding out whether the evening hours are used. Before deciding on a permanent extension, it wants to compare the cost of the additional hours with visits and collect information about the activities undertaken. Some visitors used the tables for group work, while others consulted books. Increased entry numbers are therefore an observed result; increased individual reading remains a question to investigate.

Which title best represents the whole passage?

- More individual reading thanks to evening opening
- The library has chosen permanent opening hours
- New users replace established visitors
- More evening entries, further evidence before a final decision
- More entries both morning and evening

## Explanation

The passage combines an observed result with a decision that remains open. The chosen title preserves both without quantifying an unstated increase or confusing entries with readers. The alternatives attribute the increase to individual reading, present the decision as final, replace established visitors with new users, or extend the increase to the morning. None of these steps is supported by the report.
