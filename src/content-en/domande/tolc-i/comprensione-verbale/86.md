A research group uses a model to estimate three buildings’ water consumption in the following month. The table gives a central estimate and a plausible interval under the conditions considered. The endpoints are not presented as absolutely impossible to exceed.

| Building | Central estimate, cubic meters | Plausible interval, cubic meters |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| A | 100 | 90–110 |
| B | 105 | 80–130 |
| C | 95 | 92–98 |

The report explains that a higher central value does not imply that actual consumption will certainly be higher. The intervals describe model uncertainty, not extra quantities to add to the estimates. If building usage hours change substantially, the forecasts must be updated. After the observed month, comparison with measured consumption will help assess the model; one error alone does not prove that all its predictions are useless.

What can be stated when comparing B and A?

- B has a higher central estimate, but this does not make its actual consumption certainly higher
- A will certainly consume more than B
- B will certainly consume more than A
- Actual consumption will necessarily be equal
- B has both a lower central estimate and a smaller interval width

## Explanation

B’s central estimate is 105 against 100, but the intervals overlap and the report rules out a certain ordering of actual values. Confusing estimate rankings with certainty about outcomes erases the stated uncertainty.
