A laboratory offers two optional courses. Its report distinguishes enrollment numbers from the number of students completing every required activity. Each enrolled student appears in only one row.

| Course | Enrolled | Completions |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Short | 40 | 30 |
| Extended | 100 | 60 |

The director observes that the extended course produces more completions in absolute terms, while the short course has a higher proportion relative to its enrollment. She adds that students chose their course independently and that the programs demand different commitments. The table describes observed outcomes but does not prove that moving the same students between courses would improve results. Assessing that effect would require a comparison designed to distinguish the course’s influence from the characteristics of those choosing it.

Which comparison respects both measures in the table?

- Extended has more completions; short has a higher completion proportion
- Extended has fewer completions but a higher proportion
- Short has more completions and a higher proportion
- The courses have equal numbers of completions
- The proportions are equal because both exceed half

## Explanation

Sixty completions exceed thirty, but 60 out of 100 is 60%, below 30 out of 40, or 75%. The numerator and ratio answer different questions. Both exceeding half of enrollment does not make the proportions equal.
