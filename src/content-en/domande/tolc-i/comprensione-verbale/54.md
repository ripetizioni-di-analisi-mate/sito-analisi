On each outing a tester measures the same tyre pressure three times without changing it. The values are close. A later check finds that the gauge always reads slightly above the reference.

Why did agreement among the first readings fail to reveal the fault?

- Similarity among readings guarantees the reference has the same fault
- A mean of three readings removes even an offset consistently in the same direction
- A tyre at constant pressure necessarily makes the instrument accurate
- An error shared by all readings can leave them close together
- Each repeated measurement automatically corrects the gauge

## Explanation

Repetition checks how readings vary among themselves. An external comparison reveals the shared error in the same direction that all three may contain.
