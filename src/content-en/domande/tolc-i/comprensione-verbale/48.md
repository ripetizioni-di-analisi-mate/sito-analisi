A complaints chart shows columns 2 and 8 centimetres high for 102 and 108 complaints. Its vertical scale starts at 100. A reader concludes that complaints quadrupled.

Why does the height ratio not equal the count ratio?

- One must add one hundred only to the second column before taking the ratio
- The height ratio directly expresses the percentage increase in the counts
- The heights measure only the excess above 100
- Starting at 100 makes the scale a percentage scale
- The unequal column lengths prove that the scale changes between the two dates

## Explanation

There are two units from 100 to 102 and eight from 100 to 108. Those excesses quadruple, while the full counts move from 102 to 108.
