A catalog describes two containers. The first is “made with recycled material”: some of its input material comes from recovering previous products. The second is “recyclable”: its material has characteristics permitting recycling through a suitable process. The specification explains that the two adjectives concern different aspects and may also apply to the same product.

A note adds that technical recyclability does not guarantee that the container will actually be collected and processed where it is used. This also depends on available services and correct disposal. The specification does not state which container has the lower overall environmental impact: that comparison would require information about production, transport, use, and end of life. Readers are therefore asked to interpret the qualifications according to what they specify, without turning one material property into a complete judgment about every consequence of the product.

Can a container be both recycled and recyclable?

- No, because the first qualification necessarily describes the end of all use
- Yes, the properties concern different aspects
- Only if local recycling has already been completed
- No, because recycled means without any possibility of recovery
- Only if the product has zero environmental impact

## Explanation

The catalog explicitly states that both adjectives may apply together. Some material may come from recovery while retaining characteristics suitable for future processing. The passage contains no rule making the properties mutually exclusive.
