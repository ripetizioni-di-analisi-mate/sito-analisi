“The comparison alone does not establish that cycling improves well-being,” the authors write. They then suggest that people already in good health may find it easier to choose cycling.

How are the two sentences related?

- The first describes an effect that the second quantifies
- The first rules out the possibility mentioned in the second
- The second presents prior health as an already demonstrated consequence of cycling
- The second proposes an alternative explanation that justifies the caution in the first
- The second supplies the experimental test requested in the first

## Explanation

The second sentence places good health before the transport choice. That sequence could produce the association even without the effect suggested in the first.
