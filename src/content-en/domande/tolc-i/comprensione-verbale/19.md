The workshop paper announces “the valley’s first wooden bridge designed by students”. An editor shortens this to “the valley’s first bridge”. There has long been a stone footbridge built by local residents.

Why does the shortened wording change the claim?

- It retains the limits of the distinction because the footbridge is made of stone
- It restricts the distinction to bridges built before the footbridge
- It attributes the footbridge to the new bridge’s designers
- It extends a first within a limited category to all bridges in the valley
- It shifts the distinction from order of construction to bridge length

## Explanation

“Wooden” and “designed by students” limit what competes for the distinction. Once both are dropped, the old footbridge enters the comparison.
