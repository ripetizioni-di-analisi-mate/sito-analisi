What is the solution set of $2\cos^2x-\cos x-1=0$ in the interval $[0,2\pi)$?

- $\left\{\dfrac{2\pi}{3},\dfrac{4\pi}{3},2\pi\right\}$
- $\left\{0,\dfrac{2\pi}{3},\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\right\}$
- $\left\{\dfrac\pi3,\pi,\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\right\}$
- $\left\{\dfrac{2\pi}{3},\pi,\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\right\}$
- $\left\{0,\dfrac\pi3,\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\right\}$

## Explanation

Setting $t=\cos x$, the equation becomes $2t^2-t-1=0$, whose solutions are $t=\dfrac{1\pm3}{4}$, that is, $t=1$ and $t=-\dfrac12$. The equation $\cos x=1$ has the single solution $x=0$ in $[0,2\pi)$, because $2\pi$ is excluded from the interval. The cosine equals $-\dfrac12$ in the second and third quadrants, with reference angle $\dfrac\pi3$, so $x=\pi-\dfrac\pi3=\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ or $x=\pi+\dfrac\pi3=\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$. Therefore the solution set is $\left\{0,\dfrac{2\pi}{3},\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\right\}$. The set $\left\{\dfrac\pi3,\pi,\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\right\}$ corresponds to the roots $t=\dfrac12$ and $t=-1$, which are obtained with the wrong sign of the term $-t$.
