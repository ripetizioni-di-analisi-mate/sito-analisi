Five observations have mean $10$ and descriptive variance $6$, computed with divisor $N=5$. A sixth observation equal to $10$ is added. What is the descriptive variance of the six observations, with divisor $N=6$?

- $6$
- $5$
- $\dfrac{36}{5}$
- $\dfrac{25}{6}$
- $\sqrt5$

## Explanation

The new observation equals the mean, so the mean of the six observations remains $10$. Since the variance of the five observations is $6$, the sum of their squared deviations from the mean is $5\cdot6=30$. The deviation of the sixth observation is $10-10=0$, so the sum of the squared deviations remains $30$. The new variance is therefore $\dfrac{30}{6}=5$. The value $6$ is obtained by overlooking that the divisor changes from $5$ to $6$, while $\sqrt5$ is the standard deviation of the six observations.
