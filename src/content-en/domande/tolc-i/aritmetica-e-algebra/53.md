In a sign table for $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{x+1}$, a student reverses the sign at both $-1$ and $2$. What do the values immediately to the left and right of $2$ show?

- The quotient is negative on both sides and zero at $2$
- The sign goes from positive to negative at $2$
- The quotient is positive on both sides and zero at $2$
- The sign goes from negative to positive at $2$
- The quotient is undefined at $2$

## Explanation

Near $2$ the denominator $x+1$ is positive. The squared numerator is positive for $x\ne2$ and zero at $2$. A zero of even multiplicity does not produce a sign change.
