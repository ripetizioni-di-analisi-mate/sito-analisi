A reading $L=\dfrac{x+2}{x-1}$ is compared with the reference $1$ for $x\ne1$. Which expression for the difference $L-1$ shows that the reading never equals the reference?

- $\dfrac3{x+1}$
- $\dfrac3{x-1}$
- $\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}$
- $\dfrac1{x-1}$
- $0$

## Explanation

Write $1$ as $\dfrac{x-1}{x-1}$. The numerator of the difference is then $(x+2)-(x-1)=3$. Its constant nonzero numerator guarantees that the difference never vanishes anywhere in the domain.
