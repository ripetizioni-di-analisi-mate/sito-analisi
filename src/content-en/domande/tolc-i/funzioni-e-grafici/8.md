For $a,b>0$, which identity always holds?

- $\ln(a+b)=\ln a+\ln b$
- $\ln(ab)=\ln a\ln b$
- $\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b$
- $\ln\dfrac ab=\ln b-\ln a$
- $\ln(a^2)=\ln a+2$

## Explanation

The logarithm of a product of positive factors is the sum of their logarithms. Indeed, writing $a=e^u$ and $b=e^v$ gives $ab=e^{u+v}$ and hence $\ln(ab)=u+v$. The assumptions $a,b>0$ ensure that both logarithms are defined. A sum of arguments has no analogous property.
