Restrict $f(x)=x^2$ to the domain $(-\infty,0]$. What is its inverse?

- $f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt x$, $x\ge0$
- $f^{-1}(x)=-\sqrt{-x}$, $x\le0$
- $f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac1{x^2}$, $x\ne0$
- $f^{-1}(x)=x^2$, $x\le0$
- $f^{-1}(x)=-\sqrt x$, $x\ge0$

## Explanation

The range of squaring on the negative half-axis is $[0,+\infty)$, which becomes the inverse’s domain. The relation $y=x^2$ formally gives two signs, but the original domain requires $x\le0$. Therefore choose $x=-\sqrt y$. The inverse is $-\sqrt x$ for $x\ge0$.
