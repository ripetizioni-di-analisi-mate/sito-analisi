What is the inverse of $f(x)=3^x$, considered from $\mathbb R$ to $(0,+\infty)$?

- $f^{-1}(x)=\log_3x$, $x>0$
- $f^{-1}(x)=3^{-x}$, $x\in\mathbb R$
- $f^{-1}(x)=3x$, $x\in\mathbb R$
- $f^{-1}(x)=\log_3x$, $x\in\mathbb R$
- $f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt[3]x$, $x\in\mathbb R$

## Explanation

From $y=3^x$, we obtain $x=\log_3y$. The inverse must therefore apply the base-$3$ logarithm to its input. Its domain is the exponential function’s range, namely $(0,+\infty)$. The power $3^{-x}$ is instead the reciprocal of $3^x$ and does not exchange the roles of input and image.
