An indicator equals $L=\log_{10}I$ for $I>0$. If $I$ is multiplied by $100$, how does $L$ change?

- It increases by $100$.
- It always doubles.
- It increases by $10$.
- It decreases by $2$.
- It increases by $2$.

## Explanation

The new value is $\log_{10}(100I)$. The product property gives $\log_{10}100+\log_{10}I=2+L$. The indicator therefore increases by $2$, regardless of the initial value of $I$. Multiplying the measured quantity corresponds to adding in its logarithmic representation.
