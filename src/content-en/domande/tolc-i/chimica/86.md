Why can a small molecule such as ethanol mix with water, whereas a similarly sized nonpolar hydrocarbon is much less soluble?

- Ethanol's OH group can form favorable interactions and hydrogen bonds with water
- Ethanol necessarily turns into sodium ions
- Hydrocarbons have no mass
- Carbon always prevents solubility
- Water dissolves ionic substances only

## Explanation

Ethanol's hydroxyl group enables polar interactions and hydrogen bonding with the solvent. A nonpolar hydrocarbon does not provide the same interactions. Solubility depends on the balance between initial and new interactions, so carbon's presence alone cannot decide it. Neutral molecular substances can also be highly water-soluble.
