Two pure samples of the same compound contain $3\,\mathrm g$ and $9\,\mathrm g$ of carbon, respectively. The first contains $8\,\mathrm g$ of oxygen. How much oxygen does the second contain?

- $24\,\mathrm g$
- $8\,\mathrm g$
- $16\,\mathrm g$
- $27\,\mathrm g$
- $72\,\mathrm g$

## Explanation

A pure compound keeps the mass ratio between its elements. The carbon mass in the second sample is three times that in the first because $\dfrac93=3$. Oxygen mass must therefore also triple, giving $3\cdot8=24\,\mathrm g$. The proportion applies because the samples are the same compound, not merely the same elements.
