In the reaction $2\mathrm{H_2}+\mathrm{O_2}\to2\mathrm{H_2O}$, $3\,\mathrm{mol}$ of oxygen react completely with excess hydrogen. How many moles of water form?

- $3\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $9\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $6\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $2\,\mathrm{mol}$

## Explanation

The balanced equation associates one mole of oxygen with two moles of water. Hence $n(\mathrm{H_2O})=3\cdot2=6\,\mathrm{mol}$. Six moles of hydrogen are also needed; the statement “in excess” guarantees their availability. The coefficient ratio concerns amounts in moles, not the reactant masses directly.
