Compare $1\,\mathrm{mol}$ of helium atoms and $1\,\mathrm{mol}$ of methane molecules. Which statement is correct?

- The samples necessarily have the same mass
- Methane has four times as many molecules as helium particles
- Moles cannot compare particle counts
- Helium contains five atoms per particle
- The specified entities are equal in number, but methane contains five times as many atoms

## Explanation

One mole contains the same number of specified entities. Each helium entity is one atom, whereas each methane molecule contains one carbon and four hydrogens, hence five atoms. The stated particle counts therefore match, but methane's total atom count is five times greater. The molar masses remain different.
