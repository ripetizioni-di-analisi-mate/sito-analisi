In $\mathrm{NH_3+H_2O\rightleftharpoons NH_4^++OH^-}$, why is ammonia a Brønsted base?

- It donates a proton to water
- It donates an electron to water
- Its formula already contains a hydroxide ion
- It converts nitrogen into oxygen
- It accepts a proton from water

## Explanation

Ammonia changes from $\mathrm{NH_3}$ to $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$, so it gains hydrogen with charge $+1$, namely a proton. Water donates it and becomes $\mathrm{OH^-}$. The Brønsted definition concerns this proton transfer; a base can therefore produce hydroxide without containing an OH group in its initial formula.
