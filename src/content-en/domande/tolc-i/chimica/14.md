The reaction $2\mathrm{Mg}+\mathrm{O_2}\to2\mathrm{MgO}$ produces $10\,\mathrm g$ of MgO. With $M(\mathrm{MgO})=40\,\mathrm{g/mol}$, how many moles of oxygen were consumed?

- $0.25\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $2\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $0.125\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $5\,\mathrm{mol}$

## Explanation

The product mass corresponds to $n(\mathrm{MgO})=\dfrac{10}{40}=0.25\,\mathrm{mol}$. The equation requires one mole of $\mathrm{O_2}$ for every two moles of MgO, so $n(\mathrm{O_2})=\dfrac{0.25}{2}=0.125\,\mathrm{mol}$. The factor of two arises from diatomic oxygen and is already represented by the balanced coefficients.
