Which pair has the same electron count? Given $Z_{\mathrm{Na}}=11$, $Z_{\mathrm{Mg}}=12$ and $Z_{\mathrm F}=9$.

- $\mathrm{Na}$ and $\mathrm{F^-}$
- $\mathrm{Na^+}$ and $\mathrm{F^-}$
- $\mathrm{Mg^+}$ and $\mathrm{F}$
- $\mathrm{Na^+}$ and $\mathrm{Mg^+}$
- $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{Na}$

## Explanation

Neutral sodium has eleven electrons and its positive ion has lost one, so $\mathrm{Na^+}$ has ten. Neutral fluorine has nine and its anion has gained one, so $\mathrm{F^-}$ also has ten. The species are isoelectronic but remain different elements because their nuclei have different proton numbers.
