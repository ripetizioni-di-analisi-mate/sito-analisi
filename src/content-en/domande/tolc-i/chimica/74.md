Sodium and potassium both tend to form $+1$ cations. Which shared feature explains this similarity?

- The same total electron count
- The same neutron count
- Identical atomic mass
- One electron in the outermost shell
- The same periodic-table period

## Explanation

Sodium and potassium belong to the alkali-metal group and have one outer electron. Losing it produces a cation with charge $+1$. Total electron counts and masses differ because the elements belong to different periods. Chemical similarity depends primarily on valence configuration.
