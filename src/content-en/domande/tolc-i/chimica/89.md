A $50\,\mathrm g$ sample contains $80\%$ by mass $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$ and inert impurities. With $M(\mathrm{CaCO_3})=100\,\mathrm{g/mol}$, how many moles of carbonate can react?

- $0.50\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $0.80\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $4.0\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $0.40\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $40\,\mathrm{mol}$

## Explanation

The actual reactant mass is $50\cdot0.80=40\,\mathrm g$. Dividing by molar mass gives $n=\dfrac{40}{100}=0.40\,\mathrm{mol}$. The impurities are stated to be inert and do not contribute carbonate. Using all fifty grams would overestimate available moles by twenty-five percent relative to the actual amount.
