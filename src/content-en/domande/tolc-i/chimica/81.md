The $\mathrm{CO_2}$ molecule is linear and its C=O bonds are polar. Why is the overall dipole zero?

- The two equal contributions point in opposite directions
- Polar bonds have no electrons
- Carbon and oxygen always have the same electronegativity
- The contributions are perpendicular and therefore sum to zero
- The molecule contains only separated ions

## Explanation

Molecular polarity depends on the vector sum of bond contributions. In symmetric linear O=C=O, the contributions have equal magnitudes and opposite directions, so they cancel. Each individual C=O bond remains polar. Electronegativity alone, without geometry, cannot establish overall polarity.
