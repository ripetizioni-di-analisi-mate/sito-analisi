During melting of a pure substance at constant pressure, heat is supplied and temperature remains constant while solid and liquid coexist. What happens to the supplied energy?

- It disappears because temperature does not rise
- It necessarily turns into new elements
- It only increases the substance's mass
- It always lowers the system's internal energy
- It contributes to converting the solid phase into liquid

## Explanation

During a phase change, heat changes particle organization and interactions. Temperature can therefore remain constant while the liquid fraction changes. Energy does not disappear: it is associated with the phase transformation. This distinguishes supplied heat from temperature, which alone does not measure all the system's energy.
