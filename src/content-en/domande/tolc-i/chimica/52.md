Two equal-volume containers hold helium and nitrogen, both ideal, at the same temperature and pressure. Which quantity is necessarily equal?

- Total mass
- Amount of substance
- Mass of one particle
- Total number of atoms
- Density

## Explanation

The ideal gas law gives $n=\dfrac{pV}{RT}$. Since pressure, volume and temperature match, the amounts and particle counts also match. A helium particle is one atom, whereas a nitrogen molecule contains two atoms. Masses and densities differ because molar masses differ.
