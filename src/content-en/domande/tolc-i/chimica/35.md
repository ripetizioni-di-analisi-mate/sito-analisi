Why can solid copper conduct electric current without melting?

- All its nuclei move freely through the wire
- It consists of charged water molecules
- Each atom continuously changes into another element
- Positive and negative ions migrate as in a salt solution
- It has mobile delocalized electrons

## Explanation

In metallic bonding, some electrons are delocalized and can respond to an electric field by moving through the material. Nuclei remain at lattice positions, apart from thermal vibrations. Charge transport in a metal differs from that in an electrolyte solution, where positive and negative ions move.
