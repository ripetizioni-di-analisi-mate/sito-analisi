Why does water have a higher boiling point than $\mathrm{H_2S}$ at the same pressure, despite its lower molar mass?

- Liquid water is a metal
- Strong hydrogen bonds form between water molecules
- Water has greater molar mass by definition
- Boiling requires breaking all water nuclei
- $\mathrm{H_2S}$ has no electrons

## Explanation

Vaporization requires separating molecules that attract each other. Hydrogen bonds between water molecules increase the energy needed for this separation beyond what molar masses alone would suggest. Boiling mainly concerns intermolecular interactions; water molecules enter the vapor while retaining their covalent O–H bonds.
