A bond costs $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and pays $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one. Its effective annual yield is $10\%$. What total payment in year two would account for its only remaining cash flow after year one?

- $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1089\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $900\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The price equals the sum of the two cash flows discounted at $10\%$. The first receipt is worth $\dfrac{110}{1.1}=100$ euros today, so the second must be worth $1000-100=900$ euros today. Accumulating this value to year two gives
$$
F_2=900\cdot1.1^2=1089\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $900$ is the present value of the cash flow rather than its amount at payment. The value $1210$ compounds the entire price and ignores the first receipt.
