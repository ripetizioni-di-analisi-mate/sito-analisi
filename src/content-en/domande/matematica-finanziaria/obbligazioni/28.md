Two bonds have the same redemption payment in two years. The first also pays a coupon of $33\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $36.30\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at maturity. At an effective annual yield of $10\%$, by how much does the first bond's price exceed the second's?

- $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $66\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $30\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $69.3\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The redemption payments are identical in amount and date, so the price difference is the present value of the first bond's coupons alone. At $10\%$ this gives
$$
\frac{33}{1.1}+\frac{36.3}{1.21}=30+30=60\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $69.3$ adds the coupons without discounting, while $30$ euros counts the present value of only one coupon.
