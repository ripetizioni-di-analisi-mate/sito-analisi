A bond pays a coupon of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and, in two years, the last coupon of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ together with the redemption of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The one-year and two-year discount factors are $0.9$ and $0.8$. What is the bond worth today?

- $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1070\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $970\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The price is the sum of the present values of the payments, each discounted with the factor of its own date. The one-year payment is worth $100\cdot0.9=90\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The two-year payment is $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and is worth $1100\cdot0.8=880\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The price is therefore
$$
P=90+880=970\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ adds the nominal amounts and $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounts only the redemption. The value $1070\,\mathrm{EUR}$ swaps the two discount factors.
