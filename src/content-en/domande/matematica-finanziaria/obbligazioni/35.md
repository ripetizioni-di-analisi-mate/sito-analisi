A bond costs $470\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and promises $84\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one and $576\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two. Which effective annual rate equates the price to the discounted cash flows?

- $20\%$
- $10\%$
- $12\%$
- $25\%$

## Explanation

The yield to maturity is the rate $i$ satisfying $470=\dfrac{84}{1+i}+\dfrac{576}{(1+i)^2}$. At $i=20\%$ the first payment is worth $\dfrac{84}{1.2}=70$ euros today and the second $\dfrac{576}{1.44}=400$ euros, totaling $470$ euros. Present value is strictly decreasing in the rate, so the solution is unique and the yield to maturity is $20\%$. At $10\%$, for example, present value is approximately $552.40$ euros, above the price.
