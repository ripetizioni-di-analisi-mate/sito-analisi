On the first payment date a portfolio receives principal of $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ from a zero-coupon bond and $40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ from a coupon bond. On the second date it receives $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of principal and another $40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ from the coupon bond. How much larger is the second cash flow than the first?

- $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $540\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $260\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $340\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The aggregate cash flow on a date includes every receipt on that date. The portfolio receives $200+40=240$ euros on the first date and $500+40=540$ euros on the second, giving a difference of
$$
540-240=300\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The coupons are equal on the two dates and therefore cancel in the difference. The value $340$ counts a coupon only on the second date.
