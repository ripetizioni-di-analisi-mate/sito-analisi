Immediately after a coupon payment, a bond redeemable at par pays annual coupons of $3\%$ of face value. The required annual yield is $5\%$. How does the bond trade?

- At par.
- Above par only if at least two years remain.
- Above par.
- Below par.

## Explanation

Immediately after a coupon payment, a bond redeemable at par satisfies $P-N=(C-iN)a_{\overline n|i}$ with $a_{\overline n|i}>0$. The coupon rate per period $\dfrac{C}{N}=3\%$ is below the yield $i=5\%$, so $C-iN<0$ and $P<N$. The bond therefore trades below par regardless of the number of remaining coupons. With one coupon, for example, it costs $\dfrac{103}{1.05}\approx98.10$ per $100$ of face value.
