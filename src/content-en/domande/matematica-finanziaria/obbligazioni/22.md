A zero-coupon bond matures in eighteen months. Why does its price at an effective annual yield involve the exponent $\dfrac{3}{2}$?

- Because the annual rate is multiplied by three.
- Because its term is one and a half years.
- Because three coupons are paid over two years.
- Because an intermediate maturity requires a nominal rate.

## Explanation

Under compound interest with the exponential convention, the exponent of $(1+y)^T$ is the term measured in the time unit of the rate. The rate is annual and eighteen months equals $\dfrac{18}{12}=\dfrac{3}{2}$ years, so the price is $P=\dfrac{M}{(1+y)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$. The exponent is independent of coupons and nominal rates because a zero-coupon bond has a single payment.
