A bond trades above face value, and its contractual redemption payment also exceeds face value. What can be concluded about the coupon rate and the yield?

- The coupon rate is below the yield.
- The coupon rate equals the yield.
- The coupon rate exceeds the yield.
- The price relative to face value alone does not determine the comparison.

## Explanation

For redemption value $M$ different from face value, $P-M=(C-iM)a_{\overline n|i}$ holds. The sign of $C-iM$ therefore depends on how $P$ compares with $M$, which is unspecified. With $N=1000$, $M=1050$, one payment date, and $i=5\%$, coupon $C=20$ gives $P\approx1019.05$ and coupon $C=100$ gives $P\approx1095.24$. In both cases $P>N$, but the coupon rate is below the yield in the first case and above it in the second.
