Two portfolios have the same price today. The first pays $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years, the second $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year, with no other cash flows. Both cost $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. Which has the higher annual yield to maturity?

- The comparison depends on the number of bonds held.
- The two yields to maturity are equal.
- The second portfolio, whose payment is in one year.
- The first portfolio, whose payment is in two years.

## Explanation

For a single payment, the yield to maturity $i$ satisfies $(1+i)^T=\dfrac{1210}{1000}=1.21$. For the first portfolio $T=2$, so $1+i=\sqrt{1.21}=1.1$ and the yield to maturity is $10\%$. For the second $T=1$, so the yield to maturity is $21\%$. The second portfolio therefore has the higher yield because it achieves the same growth factor in half the time.
