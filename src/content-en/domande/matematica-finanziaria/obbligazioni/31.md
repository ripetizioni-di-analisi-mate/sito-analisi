A contract pays coupons of $20$, $40$, and $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ over three years and repays principal in the last year. Which method gives its price at a constant compound rate?

- Discount the principal repayment for a single period.
- Replace every coupon by the average coupon.
- Discount each cash flow from its own payment date.
- Discount the sum of the three coupons for one year.

## Explanation

At a compound rate $i$, the price is the sum of the present values of the individual payments, each discounted over its own term:
$$
P=\frac{20}{1+i}+\frac{40}{(1+i)^2}+\frac{60+M}{(1+i)^3}.
$$
The coupons differ, so the level annuity formula does not apply. Replacing them with their average gives a higher price because it moves $20$ euros from the third payment date to the first, where they are discounted less.
