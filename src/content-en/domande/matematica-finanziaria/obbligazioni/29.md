A bond pays $40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ per year and repays $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years with the final coupon. At an effective annual yield of $10\%$, how much would today's price rise if the redemption payment increased by $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$?

- $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $133.1\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The coupons and original principal payment are unchanged, so the price change is the present value of the additional $121$ euros alone. The redemption payment is due in two years, so the increase is
$$
\frac{121}{1.1^2}=\frac{121}{1.21}=100\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $110$ discounts for only one year, $121$ ignores discounting, and $133.1$ compounds instead of discounting.
