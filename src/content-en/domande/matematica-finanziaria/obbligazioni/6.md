A bond costs $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and pays $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one and $363\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two. Find its effective annual yield to maturity.

- $10\%$
- $5\%$
- $18.25\%$
- $20\%$

## Explanation

The yield to maturity $i$ satisfies $400=\dfrac{110}{1+i}+\dfrac{363}{(1+i)^2}$. At $i=10\%$, the cash flows are worth $\dfrac{110}{1.1}=100$ euros and $\dfrac{363}{1.21}=300$ euros today, totaling $400$ euros. Present value is strictly decreasing in the rate, so the unique yield to maturity is $10\%$. The value $18.25\%$ is the total gain $\dfrac{473-400}{400}$, with neither discounting nor conversion to an annual rate.
