On a day without a coupon payment the dirty price of a bond rises by $0.10\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The coupon for the period is $72\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the accrued interest builds up linearly over $180$ actual days. How does the clean price change on the same day?

- It rises by $0.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
- It falls by $0.30\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
- It rises by $0.10\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
- It falls by $0.40\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

## Explanation

With linear accrual every day of the period adds the same fraction of the coupon to the accrued interest, so in one day the accrued interest rises by $\dfrac{72}{180}=0.40\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The dirty price is the sum of the clean price and the accrued interest, therefore the change in the clean price is the change in the dirty price minus that of the accrued interest. This gives $0.10-0.40=-0.30\,\mathrm{EUR}$, that is, the clean price falls by $0.30\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The value $-0.40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ ignores the rise of the dirty price, while $+0.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ adds the two changes.
