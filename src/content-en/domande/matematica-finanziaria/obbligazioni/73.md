A two-year bond with face value $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ pays annual coupons of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and is bought at par at a yield of $10\%$. After one year, immediately after the first coupon, the market yield has risen to $25\%$ and the bond is sold. What is the holding-period return for the year?

- $10\%$
- $-12\%$
- $2\%$
- $-2\%$

## Explanation

At the sale a single payment of $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ remains, due in one year, so the sale price at $25\%$ is $\dfrac{1100}{1.25}=880\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The sale takes place immediately after the coupon, therefore the final account includes the price of $880\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the coupon of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The holding-period return is
$$
h=\frac{880+100-1000}{1000}=-2\%.
$$
The coupon covers only part of the price drop caused by the rise in the yield. The value $10\%$ is the yield to maturity at purchase and $-12\%$ ignores the coupon.
