An investor holds equal numbers of units of two bonds. The first has twice the unit price of the second. What are their market-value weights?

- $\dfrac{3}{4}$ for the first and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ for the second.
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$ for the first and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ for the second.
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$ for the first and $\dfrac{2}{3}$ for the second.
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$ each.

## Explanation

A bond's market-value weight equals the holding's value divided by total portfolio value. With $x$ units of each bond and unit prices $2p$ and $p$, the holdings are worth $2px$ and $px$, totaling $3px$. The first bond therefore has weight $\dfrac{2px}{3px}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ and the second $\dfrac{1}{3}$. Equal weights of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ count units and ignore prices.
