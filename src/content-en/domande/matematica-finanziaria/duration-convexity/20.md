A portfolio of two discounted cash flows is worth $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$. A present value of $20\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is moved from year two to year five. By how much does duration increase?

- $0.1\,\mathrm{years}$
- $0.3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

The shift changes the date to which the present value is assigned but not the amount, so the price stays at $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The numerator $\sum_k t_kA_k$ increases by $20\cdot(5-2)=60$. Duration is that numerator divided by the price, so its increase is
$$
\Delta D=\frac{60}{200}=0.3\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
The value $3\,\mathrm{years}$ is the unweighted interval between the dates. The value $0.1\,\mathrm{years}$ is the share $\dfrac{20}{200}$ moved, without the factor $5-2$.
