The central price and two prices at yields $h$ from the center are known. Which numerator gives the centered second difference?

- $2P(y)-P(y+h)-P(y-h)$.
- $P(y+h)-P(y-h)$.
- $P(y+h)+P(y-h)$.
- $P(y+h)-2P(y)+P(y-h)$.

## Explanation

Taylor expansion gives $P(y\pm h)=P(y)\pm hP'(y)+\dfrac{h^2}{2}P''(y)+\dots$. In the sum $P(y+h)+P(y-h)$ the linear terms cancel, leaving $2P(y)+h^2P''(y)+\dots$. Subtracting $2P(y)$ also removes the constant term, so the numerator is
$$
P(y+h)-2P(y)+P(y-h)\approx h^2P''(y).
$$
Dividing by $h^2$ estimates $P''(y)$. The numerator $2P(y)-P(y+h)-P(y-h)$ has the opposite sign and would give negative convexity, while $P(y+h)-P(y-h)$ is used for the first derivative.
