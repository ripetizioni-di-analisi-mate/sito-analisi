Two bonds start at different yields. In which scenario can their individual modified durations be combined directly with market-value weights to estimate the total percentage price change?

- When the portfolio holds equal quantities of the two bonds.
- When both bonds have the same number of coupons.
- When both yields rise by 10 percent of their respective initial values.
- When both yields undergo the same absolute change.

## Explanation

To first order the portfolio's relative change is $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}\approx-\sum_j\omega_jD_{\mathrm{mod},j}\Delta y_j$. If each $\Delta y_j$ equals $\Delta y$, the common shift can be factored out, leaving $-\bigl(\sum_j\omega_jD_{\mathrm{mod},j}\bigr)\Delta y$. The two yields therefore need **the same absolute change**. A rise of $10\%$ relative to each starting value produces different absolute changes when initial yields differ.
