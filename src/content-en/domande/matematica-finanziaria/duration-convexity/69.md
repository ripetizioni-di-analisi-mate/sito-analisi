Two positions with positive modified durations face a rise in one yield and a fall in the other. What determines the sign of the net first-order change?

- The longer of the two maturities.
- Each position's value times its modified duration times its yield shift.
- The initial portfolio yield alone.
- The simple average of the modified durations alone.

## Explanation

To first order, the portfolio change is $\Delta V\approx-\sum_jP_jD_{\mathrm{mod},j}\Delta y_j$. Opposite yield shifts give price changes of opposite signs, so the net sign depends on the products $P_jD_{\mathrm{mod},j}|\Delta y_j|$. For example, $300\cdot2\cdot0.01=6$ and $600\cdot2\cdot0.005=6$ cancel. Average duration ignores the position values and shift sizes.
