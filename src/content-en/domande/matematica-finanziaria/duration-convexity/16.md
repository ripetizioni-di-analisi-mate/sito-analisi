A coupon in year two accounts for $30\%$ of the price. The only other payment is principal in year four. What is the Macaulay duration?

- $3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2.6\,\mathrm{years}$
- $4\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3.4\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

Since the weights sum to one, the year-four payment accounts for $70\%$. Macaulay duration weights the payment times by their shares of the price, so
$$
D=2\cdot0.3+4\cdot0.7=0.6+2.8=3.4\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
The value $2.6\,\mathrm{years}$ reverses the weights. The value $3\,\mathrm{years}$ is the unweighted mean, and $4\,\mathrm{years}$ is final maturity.
