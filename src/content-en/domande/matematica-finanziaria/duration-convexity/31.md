For a bond with positive future cash flows, the price is $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and its derivative with respect to annual yield is $-600$. Which interpretation of modified duration is correct?

- The price falls by approximately $3\,\mathrm{EUR}$ for a one-percentage-point increase in yield.
- The price falls by approximately $3\%$ for a one-percentage-point increase in yield.
- The price rises by approximately $3\%$ for a one-percentage-point increase in yield.
- Modified duration equals the price divided by its derivative.

## Explanation

Modified duration is $D_{\mathrm{mod}}=-\dfrac{P'}{P}=\dfrac{600}{200}=3$. A one-percentage-point yield increase means $\Delta y=0.01$, so the estimated relative change is
$$
\frac{\Delta P}{P}\approx-3\cdot0.01=-3\%.
$$
The price therefore falls by approximately $3\%$. The loss in euros is $200\cdot0.03=6\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so interpreting the result as approximately $3\,\mathrm{EUR}$ confuses a relative change with a change in euros.
