A bond makes two positive payments on different dates. A second bond pays twice each amount on the same dates. At the same yield, how does Macaulay duration change?

- It stays the same.
- It is halved.
- It doubles.
- It increases by one year.

## Explanation

Doubling every payment doubles each present value $A_k=C_kv^{t_k}$ and hence the price $P=\sum_kA_k$. The weights $w_k=\dfrac{A_k}{P}$ are unchanged. Macaulay duration $D=\sum_kt_kw_k$ depends only on dates and weights, so it **stays the same**. The answer that duration doubles confuses the weighted mean of payment times with the price, which does double.
