Taylor's formula is applied to the price of a bond with positive convexity. How do the second-order and first-order price estimates compare when the yield falls?

- The second-order price is higher.
- The second-order price is lower.
- The comparison depends on the absolute value of duration.
- The estimates coincide for every yield decrease.

## Explanation

The second-order estimate equals the first-order estimate plus $P\,\dfrac{\mathcal C}{2}(\Delta y)^2$. With positive convexity, that term is positive for every $\Delta y\neq0$ because $(\Delta y)^2>0$ for either direction of shift. When the yield falls, the second-order estimate is therefore **higher**. The opposite answer assigns the quadratic term the sign of $\Delta y$, even though the yield change is squared.
