A zero-coupon bond pays $625\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Its effective annual yield rises from $20\%$ to $25\%$. Calculate the exact new price.

- $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $625\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $390\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The payment of $625\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is still due in two years, so discount it for two years at the new yield to obtain the exact price. Since $1.25^2=1.5625$, this gives
$$
P=\frac{625}{1.5625}=400\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
At the initial yield of $20\%$ the price was $\dfrac{625}{1.44}\approx434.03\,\mathrm{EUR}$. This price is unnecessary for exact repricing. The value $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounts for just one year, while $625\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the undiscounted redemption payment.
