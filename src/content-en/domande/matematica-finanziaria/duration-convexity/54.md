The quadratic correction for a shock of $40$ basis points is $0.80\,\mathrm{EUR}$. What is it for a shock of $20$ basis points at the same initial yield?

- $1.6\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $3.2\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0.4\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

At a fixed initial yield, the quadratic correction is $P\,\dfrac{\mathcal C}{2}(\Delta y)^2$, proportional to the square of the shift. Moving from $40$ to $20$ basis points halves the shift and quarters its square. Thus
$$
\frac{0.80}{4}=0.20\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $0.40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ halves the correction as if it were linear in the shift, while $3.20\,\mathrm{EUR}$ multiplies it by four.
