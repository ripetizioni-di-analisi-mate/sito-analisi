A bond is worth $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at yield $y$. At yields $y-0.0001$ and $y+0.0001$ it is worth $1000.45$ and $999.55\,\mathrm{EUR}$ respectively. What is the DV01 estimated with the central finite difference?

- $0.90\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $4.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0.45\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $4500\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The central finite difference estimates the derivative of the price,
$$
P'(y)\approx\frac{999.55-1000.45}{0.0002}=-4500.
$$
The DV01 is the estimated loss for a one-basis-point rise, that is $P\,D_{\mathrm{mod}}\,10^{-4}$. Since $P\,D_{\mathrm{mod}}=-P'(y)$, the DV01 is $4500\cdot10^{-4}=0.45\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The value $0.90\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the difference between the two prices, which corresponds to a shift of two basis points. The value $4500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the derivative with its sign changed, before multiplying by $10^{-4}$.
