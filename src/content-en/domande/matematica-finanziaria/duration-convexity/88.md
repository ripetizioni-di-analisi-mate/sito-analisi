The central price is strictly below the average price at two yields symmetrically placed around the central yield. If the central price is positive, what is the sign of convexity estimated by the centered finite difference?

- Positive.
- Zero.
- Negative.
- It depends on which outer price is higher.

## Explanation

The numerator of the second difference can be written as
$$
P(y+h)+P(y-h)-2P(y)=2\left[\frac{P(y+h)+P(y-h)}{2}-P(y)\right].
$$
By assumption the central price is below the average of the outer prices, so the numerator is positive. The denominator $h^2P(y)$ is positive, making the estimated convexity **positive**. The sign is independent of which outer price is higher.
