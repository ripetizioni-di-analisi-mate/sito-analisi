A perpetuity pays $25\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the end of each year. Find its Macaulay duration at an effective annual yield of $10\%$.

- $1\,\mathrm{year}$
- $10\,\mathrm{years}$
- $11\,\mathrm{years}$
- $25\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

For a perpetuity payable in arrears, the price is $P=\dfrac{R}{y}$ with derivative $P'=-\dfrac{R}{y^2}$. Modified duration is therefore $-\dfrac{P'}{P}=\dfrac1y=10$. Since $D=(1+y)D_{\mathrm{mod}}$, Macaulay duration is
$$
D=\frac{1+y}{y}=\frac{1.1}{0.1}=11\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
The payment $25\,\mathrm{EUR}$ cancels in the ratio and does not affect the result. The value $10\,\mathrm{years}$ is modified duration before multiplication by $1+y$.
