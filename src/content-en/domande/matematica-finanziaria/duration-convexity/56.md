A two-year zero-coupon bond experiences a large increase in yield. Which operation gives its exact new price?

- Subtract modified duration from the initial price.
- Discount the initial price for two years at the old yield.
- Discount the redemption payment for two years at the new yield.
- Add the convexity adjustment to the initial price.

## Explanation

A zero-coupon bond has price $C(1+y)^{-T}$, with redemption payment $C$ and maturity $T$ fixed by contract. Its exact new price is the redemption payment discounted for two years at the new yield. Duration and convexity give local approximations, and for a large shift higher-order terms can make the error appreciable. Discounting the initial price at the old rate ignores the new yield.
