A zero-coupon bond maturing in two years is worth $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at an effective annual yield of $25\%$. The yield rises by five percentage points. What is the convexity adjustment in the second-order estimate of the new price?

- $3.84\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $3\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1.28\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1.92\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

For a zero-coupon bond maturing at $T$ the convexity is $\mathcal C=\dfrac{T(T+1)}{(1+y)^2}$, so with $T=2$ and $y=0.25$ it equals $\dfrac{6}{1.5625}=3.84$. The convexity adjustment in euros is $P\,\dfrac{\mathcal C}{2}(\Delta y)^2$, with $\Delta y=0.05$. Therefore
$$
400\cdot\frac{3.84}{2}\cdot0.05^2=1.92\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $3.84\,\mathrm{EUR}$ omits the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, while $3\,\mathrm{EUR}$ uses $T(T+1)=6$ without dividing by $(1+y)^2$. The value $1.28\,\mathrm{EUR}$ uses $T^2$ instead of $T(T+1)$.
