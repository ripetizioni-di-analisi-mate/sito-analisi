A zero-coupon bond is valued at an effective annual yield of $-20\%$. The available maturities are whole numbers of years and the modified duration of the position must not exceed $4$. What is the longest admissible maturity?

- $4\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $5\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

For a zero-coupon bond maturing at $T$ the Macaulay duration is $T$, so the modified duration is $\dfrac{T}{1+y}=\dfrac{T}{0.8}$. The constraint $\dfrac{T}{0.8}\leq4$ is equivalent to $T\leq3.2$. Since the maturity is a whole number of years, the longest admissible one is $3$ years. The value $4$ years confuses modified duration with maturity, while $5$ years comes from the division $\dfrac{4}{0.8}$ instead of the product $4\cdot0.8$.
