A bond pays $66\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $48.40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Calculate its Macaulay duration at an effective annual yield of $10\%$.

- $1.6\,\mathrm{years}$
- $1.4\,\mathrm{years}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

At $10\%$ the present values are $A_1=\dfrac{66}{1.1}=60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $A_2=\dfrac{48.40}{1.1^2}=40\,\mathrm{EUR}$, giving a price of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Their respective shares of the price are $0.6$ and $0.4$. Macaulay duration is the average time to payment weighted by these shares, so
$$
D=1\cdot0.6+2\cdot0.4=1.4\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
Reversing the weights gives $1.6\,\mathrm{years}$, while $1.5\,\mathrm{years}$ is the unweighted average of the two times.
