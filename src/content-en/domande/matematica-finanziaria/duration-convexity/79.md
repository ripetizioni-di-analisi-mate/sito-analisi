The effective annual yield of a perpetuity payable in arrears changes from $5\%$ to $10\%$. How does modified duration change?

- It increases by five years.
- It doubles.
- It stays the same.
- It is halved.

## Explanation

A perpetuity payable in arrears has modified duration $D_{\mathrm{mod}}=\dfrac1y$. At $5\%$ this equals $\dfrac{1}{0.05}=20$ and at $10\%$ it equals $\dfrac{1}{0.10}=10$, so modified duration **is halved**. Macaulay duration $\dfrac{1+y}{y}$ moves from $21$ to $11\,\mathrm{years}$ and is not halved. The other answers overlook the inverse relationship between modified duration and yield.
