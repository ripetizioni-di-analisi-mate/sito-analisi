At an initial yield of $10\%$, a one-year zero-coupon bond is worth $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. If the yield rises to $25\%$, what is its exact new price?

- $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $80\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $85\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $88\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The bond is worth $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at $10\%$, so the redemption payment in one year is $100\cdot1.1=110\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Discounting this payment at the new yield gives the exact price:
$$
P=\frac{110}{1.25}=88\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $80\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounts the initial price instead of principal. The value $85\,\mathrm{EUR}$ subtracts the $15$-percentage-point increase from the price, and $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the undiscounted redemption payment.
