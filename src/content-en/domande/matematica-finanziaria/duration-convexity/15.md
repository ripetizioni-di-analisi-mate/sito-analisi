A bond pays $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $146.41\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in four years. Its effective annual yield is $10\%$. What is its Macaulay duration?

- $2.5\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2\,\mathrm{years}$
- $4\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

At $10\%$ the present values are $A_1=\dfrac{110}{1.1}=100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $A_2=\dfrac{146.41}{1.1^4}=\dfrac{146.41}{1.4641}=100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Both payments therefore have the same weight, $\dfrac12$. Macaulay duration is the simple mean of the two times,
$$
D=\frac{1+4}{2}=2.5\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
The value $4\,\mathrm{years}$ is the final maturity. It equals duration only for a bond that makes a single payment.
