A bond with face value $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ matures in three years, pays an annual coupon of $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and is redeemed at par. At an effective annual yield of $10\%$ its price is $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. What is its Macaulay duration, rounded to two decimal places?

- $2.74\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2.49\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

At $10\%$ the present values of the three payments are $\dfrac{10}{1.1}\approx9.09$, $\dfrac{10}{1.1^2}\approx8.26$ and $\dfrac{110}{1.1^3}\approx82.64\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The Macaulay duration is the average of the times weighted by these present values, divided by the price, so
$$
D=\frac{1\cdot9.09+2\cdot8.26+3\cdot82.64}{100}\approx2.74\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
The duration is shorter than the maturity because the two coupons also have a positive weight. The value $2.49\,\mathrm{years}$ is the modified duration, obtained by dividing by $1.1$, and $2\,\mathrm{years}$ is the simple average of the three dates.
