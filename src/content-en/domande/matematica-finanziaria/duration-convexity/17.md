Three payments at $1$, $2$, and $4$ years have present values of $20$, $30$, and $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$, respectively. What is their Macaulay duration?

- $2.6\,\mathrm{years}$
- $4\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2.8\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2.33\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

The price is the sum of the present values, $20+30+50=100\,\mathrm{EUR}$, giving weights of $0.2$, $0.3$, and $0.5$. Macaulay duration is the weighted mean of the times, so
$$
D=\frac{1\cdot20+2\cdot30+4\cdot50}{100}=\frac{280}{100}=2.8\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
The value $2.33\,\mathrm{years}$ is the arithmetic mean $\dfrac{1+2+4}{3}$, which ignores present values. The value $4\,\mathrm{years}$ is final maturity.
