A bond makes positive payments in one and four years. What happens to Macaulay duration when the effective annual yield increases?

- It increases.
- It stays the same.
- It decreases.
- It immediately becomes one year.

## Explanation

The derivative of a price weight is $w'_k=\dfrac{D-t_k}{1+y}\,w_k$. Duration lies between $1$ and $4$ years. As $y$ rises, the four-year payment loses weight and the one-year payment gains weight. The average time therefore moves toward one year because
$$
D'(y)=-\frac{\sum_k w_k(t_k-D)^2}{1+y}<0.
$$
Duration **decreases** but remains above one year because both weights are positive.
