At yield $y$ a bond is worth $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. At yields $y-0.02$ and $y+0.02$ it is worth $112$ and $90\,\mathrm{EUR}$ respectively. Using the derivatives estimated by central finite differences, what price change does the second-order approximation give for a four-percentage-point rise in the yield?

- $-22\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-14\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-26\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-18\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

With $h=0.02$ the estimated first derivative is $\dfrac{90-112}{0.04}=-550$ and the estimated second derivative is $\dfrac{112-2\cdot100+90}{0.0004}=5000$. The second-order Taylor expansion with $\Delta y=0.04$ therefore gives
$$
\Delta P\approx-550\cdot0.04+\frac{5000}{2}\cdot0.04^2=-22+4=-18\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $-22\,\mathrm{EUR}$ stops at first order and $-14\,\mathrm{EUR}$ omits the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $-26\,\mathrm{EUR}$ subtracts the quadratic adjustment instead of adding it.
