A cash flow has an IRR of $10\%$. All amounts are tripled and all dates stay the same. What happens to NPV and the IRR?

- NPV triples at every rate, and $10\%$ remains an IRR.
- NPV stays the same and the IRR triples.
- The rate $10\%$ is an IRR only if all receipts occur together.
- NPV and the IRR both triple.

## Explanation

Every NPV term is proportional to its cash flow amount, so tripling all amounts gives $3\operatorname{NPV}(i)$ at every rate. At $10\%$ the old NPV is zero, so the new NPV is also $3\cdot0=0$ and $10\%$ remains an IRR. The dates and discount factors stay the same, and the IRR does not triple.
