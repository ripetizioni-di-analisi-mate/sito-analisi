Each of two projects has an initial outlay followed only by receipts. What assumption guarantees a unique crossover rate?

- The first IRR is positive.
- Both projects have the same initial cost.
- Both projects are conventional, whatever the form of $B-A$.
- The cash flow $B-A$ has an initial outlay followed by nonnegative receipts, at least one of which is positive.

## Explanation

A crossover rate makes $\operatorname{NPV}_{B-A}$ zero, so it is an IRR of the incremental cash flow. The uniqueness theorem applies to $B-A$ if this cash flow has an initial outlay followed by nonnegative receipts, at least one positive. Conditions on the individual projects do not suffice because subtracting two conventional cash flow streams can produce more than one sign change. Equal initial costs would make the incremental cash flow zero at time $0$.
