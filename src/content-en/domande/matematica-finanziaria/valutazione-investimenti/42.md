A project's successive cash flows have signs $-,+,-$. What can be concluded from these signs alone?

- The IRR is always unique and positive.
- The full cash flow stream is needed to determine the number of admissible IRRs.
- NPV is always positive.
- There are always exactly two IRRs.

## Explanation

With signs $-,+,-$, a rise in the rate reduces the absolute present value of the final payment and also reduces the value of the intermediate receipt. NPV need not be monotonic, so the signs alone do not determine the number of roots. For example, the cash flow $(-100,230,-132)$ has IRRs $10\%$ and $20\%$, while $(-100,180,-100)$ has none. The full cash flow stream determines the number of IRRs.
