A conventional project replaces a zero receipt in year two with a positive one. All other cash flows stay the same. How does the IRR change?

- It must become zero.
- It decreases.
- It stays the same.
- It increases.

## Explanation

Let $i^*$ be the original IRR. At $i^*$ the new NPV equals the old NPV, zero, plus the positive present value of the extra receipt, so it is positive. The new cash flow is still conventional and its NPV strictly decreasing. The new NPV therefore vanishes at a rate above $i^*$, so the IRR increases.
