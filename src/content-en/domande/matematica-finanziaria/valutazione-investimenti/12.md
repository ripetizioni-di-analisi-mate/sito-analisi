Switching from project $A$ to $B$ requires an extra $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and brings in an extra $460\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. At an annual rate of $20\%$, which project has the higher NPV?

- The NPVs are equal.
- Project $A$.
- It depends on their individual IRRs.
- Project $B$.

## Explanation

The incremental NPV of $B$ relative to $A$ is $-400+\dfrac{460}{1+i}$. It decreases with $i$ and vanishes at $1+i=\dfrac{460}{400}=1.15$, or $15\%$. At $20\%$ we have
$$
-400+\frac{460}{1.2}\approx-16.67\,\mathrm{EUR}<0,
$$
so $\operatorname{NPV}_B<\operatorname{NPV}_A$ and project $A$ has the higher NPV. The ranking follows from the sign of incremental NPV without using the individual IRRs.
