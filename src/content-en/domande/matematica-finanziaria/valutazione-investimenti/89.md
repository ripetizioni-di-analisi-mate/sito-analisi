A project costs $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and pays $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Without calculating the exact IRR, what follows if NPV is negative at $10\%$ and positive at zero?

- The IRR is below $0\%$.
- The IRR lies between $0\%$ and $10\%$.
- The IRR is exactly $10\%$.
- The IRR is above $10\%$.

## Explanation

The cash flow has an initial outlay followed by a positive receipt, so it is conventional and NPV is continuous and strictly decreasing. At a zero rate NPV is $-110+121=11\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and at $10\%$ it is $-110+100=-10\,\mathrm{EUR}$. By the intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity, the unique IRR lies between $0\%$ and $10\%$. Its exact value is $\sqrt{1.1}-1\approx4.88\%$.
