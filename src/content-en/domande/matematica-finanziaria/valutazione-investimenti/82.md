At an annual rate of $10\%$, the NPV of $B$ exceeds that of $A$ by $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Which single change to the cash flows of $B$ restores equal NPVs?

- An additional receipt of $11\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year.
- An additional receipt of $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today.
- An additional payment of $11\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year.
- An additional payment of $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year.

## Explanation

To restore equality, the NPV of $B$ must decrease by $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so the additional payment must have a present value of $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$. A payment of $11\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year is worth $\dfrac{11}{1.1}=10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and meets the condition. A payment of $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year is worth only $\dfrac{10}{1.1}\approx9.09\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today, while a receipt would widen the gap.
