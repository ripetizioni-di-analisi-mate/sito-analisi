At an annual rate of $20\%$, a project costing $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ with a receipt of $288\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years breaks even. If the valuation rate changes to $10\%$, what is the sign of NPV?

- It depends on the initial capital.
- Negative.
- It remains zero.
- Positive.

## Explanation

At $20\%$ the NPV is zero because $\dfrac{288}{1.2^2}=\dfrac{288}{1.44}=200\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so $20\%$ is the IRR. The project is conventional, so NPV is strictly decreasing and positive for every rate below the IRR. Since $10\%<20\%$, the NPV at $10\%$ is positive and equals $\dfrac{288}{1.21}-200\approx38.02\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
