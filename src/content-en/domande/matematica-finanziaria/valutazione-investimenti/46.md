At $10\%$, project $A$ costs $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and pays $264\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year, while project $B$ costs $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and pays $473\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. Which project has the higher NPV?

- Project $A$.
- It depends on the unknown crossover rate.
- Their NPVs are equal.
- Project $B$.

## Explanation

At $10\%$ the two NPVs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}_A&=-200+\frac{264}{1.1}=-200+240=40\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
\operatorname{NPV}_B&=-400+\frac{473}{1.1}=-400+430=30\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $40>30$, project $A$ has the higher NPV. Project $B$ pays more in absolute terms but also requires twice the initial outlay. The given rate allows direct calculation of both NPVs without using the crossover rate.
