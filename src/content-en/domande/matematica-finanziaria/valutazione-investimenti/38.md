A cash flow has NPV $-(11v-10)^2$, where $v=(1+i)^{-1}$. What happens at a rate of $10\%$?

- NPV changes from positive to negative.
- NPV is zero and stays negative on both sides.
- NPV is positive.
- NPV changes from negative to positive.

## Explanation

At $10\%$ we have $v=\dfrac{1}{1.1}=\dfrac{10}{11}$, so $11v-10=0$ and NPV is zero. At every other admissible rate $11v-10\neq0$, so the square is positive and its negative, the NPV, is negative. Thus $10\%$ is an IRR at which NPV keeps the same sign, staying negative on both sides.
