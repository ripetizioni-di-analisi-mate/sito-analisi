A project with an NPV of $40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at $10\%$ loses an expected receipt of $24.20\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. What is its new NPV?

- $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $18\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $20\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $15.8\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Removing a future receipt reduces NPV by its present value. At $10\%$, the two-year receipt is worth
$$
\frac{24.20}{1.1^2}=\frac{24.20}{1.21}=20\,\mathrm{EUR},
$$
today, so the new NPV is $40-20=20\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The answer $15.8\,\mathrm{EUR}$ subtracts the nominal amount, while $18\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounts it for only one year.
