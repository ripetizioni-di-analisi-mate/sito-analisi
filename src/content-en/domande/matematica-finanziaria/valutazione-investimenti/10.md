An investment has cash flows $-100$, $160$ and $-100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at annual dates $0,1,2$. How many admissible annual IRRs does it have?

- One positive IRR.
- None.
- Two positive IRRs.
- One negative IRR.

## Explanation

Let $v=(1+i)^{-1}$. The NPV is $-100+160v-100v^2$. Completing the square gives
$$
-100+160v-100v^2=-100(v-0.8)^2-36.
$$
The first term is nonpositive, so NPV is at most $-36\,\mathrm{EUR}$ for every $v>0$. No admissible rate therefore makes NPV zero. The cash flow has no IRR even though its sign changes twice.
