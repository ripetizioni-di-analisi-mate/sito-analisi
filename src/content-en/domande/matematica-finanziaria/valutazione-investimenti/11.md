A project costs $100$ euros today and provides a single receipt in two years. An annual IRR of at least $10\%$ is required even if the actual receipt is $20\%$ lower than expected. What is the smallest expected receipt?

- $96.80$ euros
- $121$ euros
- $151.25$ euros
- $169$ euros

## Explanation

With a single receipt in two years, the IRR is at least $10\%$ when the receipt is at least $100\cdot1.1^2=121$ euros. In the unfavorable case the actual receipt is $0.8X$, where $X$ is the expected receipt, so $0.8X\geq121$ must hold. Therefore $X\geq\dfrac{121}{0.8}=151.25$, and the smallest expected receipt is $151.25$ euros. The value of $121$ euros ignores the reduction, while $96.80$ euros applies the reduction to $121$ euros instead of dividing by $0.8$. The value of $169$ euros adds the reduction to the required rate and accumulates at $30\%$.
