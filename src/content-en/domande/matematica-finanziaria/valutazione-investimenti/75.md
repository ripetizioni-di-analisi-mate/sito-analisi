What can bring cumulative discounted cash flows back below the initial cost after they first reach it?

- A final cost after the receipts.
- An intermediate period with no cash flows.
- Reaching the threshold exactly, with only receipts to follow.
- A new positive receipt.

## Explanation

Cumulative discounted cash flows fall only when a payment occurs, since its present value enters with a negative sign. A final cost after the receipts can therefore bring the cumulative amount below the initial cost again. A positive receipt increases it, and a date with no cash flow leaves it unchanged. In those cases it remains at or above the cost once the threshold has been reached.
