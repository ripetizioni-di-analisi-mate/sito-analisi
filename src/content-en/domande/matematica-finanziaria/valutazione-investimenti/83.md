A project costs $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Its annual receipts change from $330$ and $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$ to $220$ and $242\,\mathrm{EUR}$. How does NPV change at $10\%$?

- It stays the same.
- It increases by $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
- It increases by $11\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
- It decreases by $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

## Explanation

The cost stays the same, so only the receipts affect the change in NPV. The first year's receipt falls by $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$, worth $\dfrac{110}{1.1}=100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. The second year's receipt rises by $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$, worth $\dfrac{121}{1.21}=100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. The two present-value changes offset, so NPV stays the same. The answer $11\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the nominal change.
