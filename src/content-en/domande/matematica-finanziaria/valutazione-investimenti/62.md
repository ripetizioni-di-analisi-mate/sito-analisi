A loan requires one repayment of $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years and has an effective annual cost of $10\%$. How much does the borrower actually receive at time zero?

- $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $980.1\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The effective annual cost of $10\%$ makes the borrower's NPV zero, so the amount received equals the present value of the repayment. The repayment falls due in two years, giving net proceeds of
$$
\frac{1210}{1.1^2}=\frac{1210}{1.21}=1000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The answer $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounts the repayment for only one year, while $980.1\,\mathrm{EUR}$ multiplies by $0.9^2$ instead of dividing by $1.1^2$.
