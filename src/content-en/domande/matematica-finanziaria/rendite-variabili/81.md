An account earning an effective annual rate of $10\%$ receives an initial deposit $C_0$ and year-end deposits of $200$, $300$, and $400$ euros at times $1$, $2$, and $3$. What value of $C_0$ gives a balance of $1637.50$ euros at time $3$?

- $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $665.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $554.09\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $426.97\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

At time $3$ the three deposits are worth
$$
200\cdot1.1^2+300\cdot1.1+400=242+330+400=972\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The initial deposit must therefore provide $1637.50-972=665.50$ euros at time $3$. Since it earns interest for three years, $C_0=\dfrac{665.50}{1.1^3}=500$ euros. The value of $665.50$ euros neglects the accumulation of $C_0$. The value of $554.09$ euros uses the nominal sum of the deposits, and $426.97$ euros accumulates each for one extra year.
