A continuous stream pays at a rate of $c(t)=160e^{(\log 2)t}$ euros per year. What is its exact present value over one year at zero force of interest?

- $\dfrac{160}{\log{\left(2 \right)}}\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $\dfrac{320}{\log{\left(2 \right)}}\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $160 \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $\dfrac{80}{\log{\left(2 \right)}}\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

With zero force of interest the discount factor is $1$ and present value is the integral of the payment rate alone. An antiderivative of $160e^{(\log 2)t}$ is $\dfrac{160}{\log 2}e^{(\log 2)t}$, giving
$$
V_0=\frac{160}{\log 2}\left(e^{\log 2}-e^{0}\right)=\frac{160}{\log 2}(2-1)=\frac{160}{\log 2}\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value of $\dfrac{320}{\log 2}$ euros uses only the upper endpoint. The value of $160\log 2$ euros multiplies by $\log 2$, as in differentiation.
