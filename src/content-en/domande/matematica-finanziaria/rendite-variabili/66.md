A continuous perpetuity pays at a rate of $c(t)=240e^{-0.03t}$ euros per year at a constant annual force of interest of $3\%$. What is its present value?

- $4000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $8000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $2000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $240\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

With payment rate $c_0e^{\gamma t}$ and $\gamma=-0.03$, the discounted payment rate is $240e^{-0.03t}e^{-0.03t}=240e^{-0.06t}$. Since $\delta=0.03>\gamma=-0.03$, the integral converges and
$$
V_0=\frac{240}{0.03-(-0.03)}=\frac{240}{0.06}=4000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The exponents add, so the discounted payment rate declines at an instantaneous annual rate of $6\%$. The value of $8000$ euros treats the payment rate as constant and divides by the force of interest alone.
