A payment rate grows continuously at an instantaneous annual rate of $4\%$ and the constant annual force of interest is $7\%$. How does the discounted payment rate $c(t)e^{-\delta t}$ change?

- It decreases exponentially at an instantaneous annual rate of $3\%$.
- It increases exponentially at an instantaneous annual rate of $11\%$.
- It increases exponentially at an instantaneous annual rate of $3\%$.
- It stays constant.

## Explanation

The payment rate is $c(t)=c_0e^{0.04t}$ and the discount factor $e^{-0.07t}$. Their product has an exponent equal to the sum of the exponents, giving
$$
c(t)e^{-\delta t}=c_0e^{(0.04-0.07)t}=c_0e^{-0.03t}.
$$
The discounted payment rate therefore declines at an instantaneous annual rate of $3\%$. The value $11\%$ adds the force to the growth rate instead of subtracting it.
