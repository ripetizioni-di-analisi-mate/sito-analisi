An annuity with payments in arithmetic progression pays $100$ euros at time $2$ and $150$ euros at time $3$. What is its present value at an effective annual rate of $25\%$?

- $176\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $112.64\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $140.80\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The discount factor is $v=\dfrac{1}{1.25}=0.8$. The payments fall at times $2$ and $3$ and have factors $v^2=0.64$ and $v^3=0.512$, giving
$$
V_0=100\cdot0.64+150\cdot0.512=64+76.80=140.80\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value of $176$ euros places the payments at times $1$ and $2$, ignoring the deferral. The value of $112.64$ euros discounts for one year too many, and $250$ euros is the nominal sum.
