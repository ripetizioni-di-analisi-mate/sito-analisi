A continuous perpetuity has initial payment rate $180\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$, instantaneous growth rate $\gamma=0.02\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$, and force of interest $\delta=0.05\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. What is its present value?

- $3600\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $9000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $6000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $2571.43\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The discounted payment rate is $180e^{0.02t}e^{-0.05t}=180e^{-0.03t}$. Since $\delta=0.05>\gamma=0.02$, the integral over $[0,+\infty)$ converges and
$$
V_0=\frac{c_0}{\delta-\gamma}=\frac{180}{0.03}=6000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The discounted payment rate therefore declines at an instantaneous annual rate of $3\%$. The value of $3600$ euros divides by the force alone, $9000$ euros divides by the growth rate, and $2571.43$ euros divides by the sum $\delta+\gamma$.
