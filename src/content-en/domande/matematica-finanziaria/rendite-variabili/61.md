A continuous annuity has discounted payment density $c_0e^{-kt}$, with $c_0>0$ and $k>0$. A manager wants the horizon $T$ by which half of the perpetuity's total present value is received. What is $T$?

- $\dfrac1{2k}$
- $k\ln2$
- $\dfrac{\ln2}{k}$
- $\dfrac2k$

## Explanation

The present value of payments after $T$, as a fraction of the total present value, is $\dfrac{\int_T^\infty c_0e^{-kt}\,dt}{\int_0^\infty c_0e^{-kt}\,dt}=e^{-kT}$. If half is received by $T$, half remains afterward, so $e^{-kT}=\dfrac12$.
