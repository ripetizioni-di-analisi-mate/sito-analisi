A continuous perpetuity pays $c(t)=240e^{0.03t}$ euros per year. Find its present value at a constant annual force of interest of $\delta=0.07\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$.

- $2400\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $8000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $6000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $3428.57\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The discounted payment rate is $240e^{0.03t}e^{-0.07t}=240e^{-0.04t}$. Since $\delta=0.07>\gamma=0.03$, the integral over $[0,+\infty)$ converges and
$$
V_0=\int_0^{+\infty}240e^{-0.04t}\,dt=\frac{240}{0.07-0.03}=6000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The discounted payment rate therefore declines at an instantaneous annual rate of $4\%$. The value of $3428.57$ euros divides by the force alone, ignoring growth. The value of $8000$ euros divides by the growth rate instead of the difference $\delta-\gamma$.
