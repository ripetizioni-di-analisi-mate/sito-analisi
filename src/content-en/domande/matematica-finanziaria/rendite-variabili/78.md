Let $\delta(t)=a+bt$, with time measured in years. What units must $b$ have for the discount exponent to be dimensionless?

- $\mathrm{year}^{-1}$
- $\mathrm{year}$
- It is dimensionless.
- $\mathrm{year}^{-2}$

## Explanation

The discount exponent $\int_0^T\delta(u)\,du$ must be dimensionless. Since $du$ is measured in years, the force is measured in $\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. In the expression $a+bt$ the term $bt$ must also be measured in $\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. Time $t$ is measured in years, so $b$ has units of $\mathrm{year}^{-2}$. The unit $\mathrm{year}^{-1}$ belongs to $a$.
