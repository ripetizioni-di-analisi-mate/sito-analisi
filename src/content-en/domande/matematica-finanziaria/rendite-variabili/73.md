An affine force of interest rises from $0.02$ to $0.06$ over three years. Which constant force gives the same three-year discount factor?

- $0.02\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$
- $0.06\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$
- $0.12\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$
- $0.04\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$

## Explanation

The affine force of interest equals $0.02+\dfrac{0.04}{3}t$ for $0\leq t\leq3$. Its integral over three years is
$$
\int_0^3\left(0.02+\frac{0.04}{3}t\right)dt=0.06+0.06=0.12 .
$$
A constant force $\bar\delta$ produces the same discount factor if $3\bar\delta=0.12$, so $\bar\delta=0.04\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$, the average of the endpoint values. The value $0.12$ is the integral before division by the three-year term.
