A continuous stream pays $300\log 2$ euros per year from today to time $2$. The annual force of interest is $\log 2$ in the first year and zero in the second. What is its exact present value?

- $150 + 300 \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $150 \log{\left(2 \right)} + 150\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $150 \log{\left(2 \right)} + 300\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $300 \log{\left(2 \right)}\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

During the first year the discount factor is $2^{-t}$, so the first interval contributes
$$
\int_0^1 300\log 2\cdot2^{-t}\,dt=300\left(1-\frac12\right)=150\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
In the second year the force is zero and the discount factor stays at $\dfrac12$. The second interval therefore contributes $\dfrac12\cdot300\log 2=150\log 2$ euros, giving $V_0=150+150\log 2$ euros in total. The value of $150+300\log 2$ euros omits the factor $\dfrac12$ on the second interval.
