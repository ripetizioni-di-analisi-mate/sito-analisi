A perpetuity has payment growth rate $g=-8\%$ and effective interest rate $i=-3\%$ per period. Its first payment is one period from now. Which conclusion is correct?

- The perpetuity diverges because the interest rate is negative.
- The perpetuity has a finite present value.
- All payments have the same present value.
- Each payment has a higher present value than its predecessor.

## Explanation

The ratio of consecutive present values is
$$
\rho=\frac{1+g}{1+i}=\frac{0.92}{0.97}<1 .
$$
The convergence condition $i>g$ holds because $-0.03>-0.08$, so the perpetuity has finite present value $\dfrac{R_1}{i-g}=\dfrac{R_1}{0.05}$. The negative sign of $i$ does not by itself determine convergence, which depends on the comparison between $i$ and $g$. Since $\rho<1$, each discounted payment is smaller than the preceding one.
