A perpetuity with positive payments in arrears grows by $3\%$ annually. The effective annual rate is $3\%$. How do the partial sums of the first $n$ discounted payments behave?

- They stay constant.
- They grow without bound in proportion to $n$.
- They converge to the first payment amount.
- They decrease toward zero.

## Explanation

Payment $k$ equals $R_1\cdot1.03^{k-1}$ and is discounted by $1.03^{-k}$, so its present value is $\dfrac{R_1}{1.03}$ for every $k$. The first $n$ present values sum to $\dfrac{nR_1}{1.03}$, increasing in proportion to $n$. Since $i=g$, the convergence condition $i>g$ fails and the perpetuity has no finite value.
