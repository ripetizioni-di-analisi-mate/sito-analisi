Two continuous perpetuities have the same instantaneous annual growth rate $2\%$ and constant annual force of interest $6\%$. The initial payment rate of the first is $500\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$. What initial payment rate makes the second worth three times as much?

- $500\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$
- $750\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$
- $1500\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$
- $2500\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$

## Explanation

Holding the rates fixed gives $V_0=\dfrac{c_0}{\delta-\gamma}=\dfrac{c_0}{0.04}$, proportional to the initial payment rate. The first annuity is worth $\dfrac{500}{0.04}=12500$ euros and the second must be worth $37500$ euros. Its initial payment rate must therefore be $37500\cdot0.04=1500$ euros per year, three times $500$ euros per year. At $750$ euros per year the second annuity would be worth $18750$ euros, just one and a half times the first.
