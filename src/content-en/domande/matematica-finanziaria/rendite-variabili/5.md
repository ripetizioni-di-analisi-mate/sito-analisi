A continuous cash flow pays at a constant rate of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$ from today to time $1$. What is its present value at an effective annual interest rate of $25\%$?

- $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $896.28\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1120.36\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The given rate is effective and annual, so the equivalent constant force of interest in the integral is $\delta=\log 1.25$. The present value of a constant payment rate over one year is
$$
V_0=\frac{1000\left(1-e^{-\delta}\right)}{\delta}=\frac{1000\,(1-0.8)}{\log 1.25}\approx896.28\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value of $800$ euros corresponds to a single payment of $1000$ euros at year-end. The value of $1120.36$ euros is the accumulated value at time $1$, namely $\dfrac{1000\left(e^{\delta}-1\right)}{\delta}$. The value of $1000$ euros ignores discounting.
