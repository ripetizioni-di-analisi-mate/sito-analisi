The accumulated value of the initial deposit alone exceeds a savings plan's exact target. All accumulation factors are positive. Which adjustment can make the plan meet its target?

- Make a withdrawal or reduce the initial deposit.
- Add at least one positive deposit.
- Add the same positive deposit at every payment date.
- Keep the schedule with all subsequent deposits equal to zero.

## Explanation

The savings equation is $K=C_0q^n+\sum_kR_kq^{n-t_k}$, with positive factors $q^{n-t_k}$. Since $C_0q^n>K$, every positive deposit increases the balance and moves it further from $K$. To reach $K$ exactly, a negative contribution, or withdrawal, is needed unless the initial deposit is reduced. With only zero deposits the balance remains $C_0q^n$, which already exceeds $K$.
