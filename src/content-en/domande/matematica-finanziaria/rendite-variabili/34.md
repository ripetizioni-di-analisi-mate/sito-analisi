A lease provides for ten annual rents paid at the end of each year. The first rent is $1000$ euros and each subsequent rent increases by $3\%$. The effective annual rate is $5\%$ and $\left(\dfrac{1.03}{1.05}\right)^{10}\approx0.82505$. What is the present value of the rents, rounded to the nearest euro?

- $7722\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $8748\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $11464\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $50000\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The rents form an ordinary geometric annuity with $R_1=1000$ euros, $g=3\%$ and $i=5\%$. Since $i\neq g$, the present value is
$$
V_0=\frac{R_1}{i-g}\left(1-\rho^{10}\right),\qquad \rho=\frac{1.03}{1.05}.
$$
Substituting the data gives $V_0=\dfrac{1000}{0.02}\left(1-0.82505\right)=50000\cdot0.17495\approx8748$ euros. The value of $7722$ euros treats the rents as constant and ignores the growth. The value of $11464$ euros is the nominal sum of the rents, while $50000$ euros is the value of the growing perpetuity with the same first payment.
