An irregular annuity is worth $1000$ euros today. At the same effective annual rate of $10\%$, every payment is postponed by two years. What is the new present value?

- $\dfrac{10000}{11}\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $\dfrac{100000}{121}\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Delaying every payment by two years replaces $v^{t_k}$ by $v^{t_k+2}=v^2v^{t_k}$ in each term. The new present value is the old one multiplied by $v^2$, giving
$$
\frac{1000}{1.1^2}=\frac{100000}{121}\approx826.45\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value of $\dfrac{10000}{11}$ euros delays the payments by just one year. The value of $1210$ euros multiplies by $q^2$ instead of $v^2$.
