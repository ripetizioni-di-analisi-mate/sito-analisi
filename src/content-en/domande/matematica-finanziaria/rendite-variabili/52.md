A continuous stream pays $200\log 2$ euros per year from today at a constant annual force of interest $\log 2$. How many years must it run for its present value to equal $150$ euros?

- $2\,\mathrm{years}$
- $1.08\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $4\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

A constant payment rate $c$ on $[0,T]$ gives $V_0=\dfrac{c\left(1-e^{-\delta T}\right)}{\delta}$. With $c=200\log 2$ and $\delta=\log 2$ this becomes $V_0=200\left(1-2^{-T}\right)$. Setting $200\left(1-2^{-T}\right)=150$ gives $2^{-T}=\dfrac14$, hence $T=2$ years. The answer of $1.08$ years ignores discounting by solving $200\log 2\cdot T=150$. At $3$ years the present value would be $175$ euros, and at $4$ years it would be $187.50$ euros.
