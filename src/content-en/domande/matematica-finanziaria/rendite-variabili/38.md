An annuity pays $121$ euros at time $2$ and makes a second payment, $10\%$ larger, at time $3$. What is its present value at an effective annual rate of $10\%$?

- $220\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $242\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The first payment is worth $\dfrac{121}{1.1^2}=100$ euros today. The second is $121\cdot1.1=133.10$ euros at time $3$, worth $\dfrac{133.10}{1.1^3}=100$ euros today. Growth offsets the extra year's discounting, giving $V_0=200$ euros. The value of $220$ euros refers to time $1$ and $242$ euros refers to time $2$.
