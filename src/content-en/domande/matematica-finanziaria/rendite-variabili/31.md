Two arithmetic annuities payable in arrears have at least two payments, the same number of payments, and the same positive first payment. The second has a larger increment $d$. At a common valuation rate, which comparison holds?

- Their present values are equal because the first payment is the same.
- The second schedule has the higher present value.
- The second schedule has the lower present value at every positive rate.
- The second schedule has the higher present value only at a zero rate.

## Explanation

The first payment is the same in both schedules. At payment $k$ the second schedule exceeds the first by $(k-1)\Delta d$, with $\Delta d>0$. The difference is zero for $k=1$ and positive for $k\geq2$. Since every discount factor $v^k$ is positive at every rate greater than $-1$, the difference in present values is positive. The second schedule therefore has the higher value at every admissible rate.
