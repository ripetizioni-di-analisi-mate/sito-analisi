A perpetual stream pays at a rate of $c(t)=c_0e^{\gamma t}$ with $c_0>0$ and has force of interest $\delta=\gamma$. What happens to its present value over an infinite horizon?

- It equals $c_0$.
- It equals zero.
- It equals the initial payment rate divided by $\gamma$.
- It diverges to $+\infty$.

## Explanation

With $\delta=\gamma$, the discounted payment rate $c_0e^{\gamma t}e^{-\gamma t}=c_0$ is a positive constant. Integrating to time $T$ gives $c_0T$, which approaches $+\infty$ as $T\to+\infty$. The present value over an infinite horizon therefore diverges. The value $\dfrac{c_0}{\gamma}$ uses the constant-payment-rate formula with force $\gamma$, which does not apply because the payment rate grows.
