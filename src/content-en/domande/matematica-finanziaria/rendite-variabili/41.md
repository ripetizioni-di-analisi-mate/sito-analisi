The first payment of a geometric annuity decreases with all dates and the growth factor held fixed. How does its present value change at the same interest rate?

- It stays the same because the growth rate is unchanged.
- It decreases only for a single-payment annuity.
- It increases if the annuity is deferred.
- It decreases in the same proportion as the first payment.

## Explanation

In a geometric annuity payment $k$ equals $R_1(1+g)^{k-1}$, so every payment is proportional to the first. Its present value
$$
V_0=\sum_{k=1}^n R_1(1+g)^{k-1}v^{t_k}
$$
is also proportional to $R_1$ because the factors $(1+g)^{k-1}$ and $v^{t_k}$ depend only on growth, interest, and the payment dates, all held fixed. The present value therefore falls in the same proportion as the first payment.
