A finite geometric annuity satisfies $i<g$. In the formula $V_0=\dfrac{R_1}{i-g}(1-\rho^n)$, the ratio of consecutive present values is $\rho=\dfrac{1+g}{1+i}$. What are the signs of $i-g$ and $1-\rho^n$?

- Both are negative.
- The first is negative and the second positive.
- The first is positive and the second negative.
- Both are positive.

## Explanation

From $i<g$ we obtain $i-g<0$. Since $1+i>0$ and $1+g>1+i$, the ratio $\rho=\dfrac{1+g}{1+i}$ exceeds $1$. Hence $\rho^n>1$ for every $n\geq1$, implying $1-\rho^n<0$. Both factors are negative, so their ratio is positive, as the present value of an annuity with positive payments must be.
