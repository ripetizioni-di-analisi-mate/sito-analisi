An arithmetic annuity makes six end-of-period payments. The first is $90$ euros and the last $190$ euros. What is the present value at a zero rate?

- $540\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $740\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1140\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $840\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

At a zero rate $v=1$, so present value equals the nominal sum. The six payments form an arithmetic sequence. Their sum is the number of payments times the average of the first and last, giving
$$
V_0=6\cdot\frac{90+190}{2}=6\cdot140=840\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value of $540$ euros counts the first payment six times. The value of $1140$ euros counts the last payment six times.
