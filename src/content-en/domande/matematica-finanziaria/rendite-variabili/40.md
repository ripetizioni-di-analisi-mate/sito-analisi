The ratio $\rho$ of consecutive present values in a finite geometric annuity exceeds $1$. How are its growth rate $g$ and valuation rate $i$ related if both exceed $-1$?

- $g=i$
- $g<i$
- $g>i$
- $g=i-1$

## Explanation

The ratio of consecutive present values is $\rho=(1+g)v=\dfrac{1+g}{1+i}$. Since $i>-1$, the denominator $1+i$ is positive. Multiplying $\rho>1$ by $1+i$ gives $1+g>1+i$, and hence $g>i$. With $g<i$ we would have $\rho<1$ and the present values would decrease, while $g=i$ would give $\rho=1$.
