The effective compound interest rate is negative. How does a positive receivable's value change when it is discounted to a date before maturity?

- It increases because the accumulation factor in the denominator is less than one.
- It stays the same because the amount is positive.
- It increases because it is multiplied by the accumulation factor.
- It decreases because discounting always reduces a receivable.

## Explanation

If $-1<i<0$, the annual factor satisfies $0<q<1$. To move an amount from time $t$ to an earlier time $s$, divide by $q^{t-s}$, which is less than one because $t-s>0$. Dividing a positive receipt by a number between $0$ and $1$ increases its value. Its discounted value therefore rises. At a positive interest rate discounting would reduce it.
