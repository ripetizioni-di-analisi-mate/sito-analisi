What does it mean for an accumulation law to be multiplicative and time-dependent?

- The factor depends only on duration and the product over consecutive intervals differs from the direct factor.
- The direct factor is the sum of the factors over consecutive intervals.
- The value of a principal is unchanged at every date.
- Factors multiply over consecutive intervals and may depend on the dates as well as the duration.

## Explanation

The law is multiplicative if $r(s,u)=r(s,t)\,r(t,u)$ for every $s\le t\le u$, meaning factors multiply over consecutive intervals. Under a time-homogeneous law intervals of equal length share a factor, such as $q^{t-s}$ under compound interest. A multiplicative law can depend on dates, as when rates vary from year to year. Its factors still multiply, although intervals of equal length may have different factors.
