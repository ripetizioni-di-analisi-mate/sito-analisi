A contract requires an outflow today and provides a positive refund later. At a positive compound interest rate, how does the refund enter the present cost?

- Subtract its nominal amount regardless of its date.
- Add its present value as an expense.
- Subtract its present value.
- Subtract its accumulated value at the future date.

## Explanation

The refund is a receipt for the person paying for the contract, so it reduces the cost. Value every cash flow today. A refund $R$ at time $t$ has present value $Rq^{-t}$ and must be subtracted. At a positive rate $Rq^{-t}<R$. Subtracting the nominal amount $R$ ignores discounting, while subtracting the accumulated value moves it in the wrong direction.
