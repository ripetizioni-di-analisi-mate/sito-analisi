Two positive debts have different due dates. Under compound interest at a nonzero rate, what property puts the equivalent average due date strictly between the two dates?

- The compound rate eliminates the factors attached to individual amounts.
- The replacement payment is smaller than either original debt.
- The equivalent factor is a weighted mean of the factors, with both weights positive.
- The equivalent average due date is always the simple arithmetic mean of the dates.

## Explanation

At the equivalent average due date the discount factor is $q^{-T}=w_1q^{-t_1}+w_2q^{-t_2}$ with weights $w_k=\dfrac{C_k}{C_1+C_2}$. Both debts are positive, so both weights are positive and sum to one. A weighted mean of two distinct factors with positive weights lies strictly between them. Since $t\mapsto q^{-t}$ is strictly monotonic for $i\ne0$, the date $T$ also lies strictly between $t_1$ and $t_2$.
