Under compound interest at a nonzero rate, both amounts in a two-receivable schedule are doubled. How does their equivalent average due date change?

- It doubles along with the receivables.
- It stays the same because the weights are unchanged.
- It halves because the present value doubles.
- It moves closer to the due date of the larger receivable.

## Explanation

At the equivalent average due date the discount factor is $q^{-T}=\sum_kw_kq^{-t_k}$ with weights $w_k=\dfrac{C_k}{S}$. Doubling all amounts doubles every $C_k$ and the sum $S$, leaving each weight unchanged. The factor $q^{-T}$ and the date $T$ therefore remain the same. Both the present value and the replacement payment double.
