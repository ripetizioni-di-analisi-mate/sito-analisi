Under compound interest an equivalent due date lies between years two and three. Settlement must occur exactly at the end of year three while preserving present value. At a positive rate, how must the replacement payment change?

- Multiply it by the discount factor for the additional time.
- Decrease it.
- Increase it.
- Leave it unchanged.

## Explanation

At a positive rate the present value $Xq^{-T}$ decreases as the due date increases. Moving the payment from its equivalent due date $T$ to year three preserves present value if the new amount $X'$ satisfies $X'q^{-3}=Xq^{-T}$, or
$$
X'=Xq^{3-T}.
$$
Since $3-T>0$ and $q>1$, we have $X'>X$, so the payment must increase. Multiplying it by the discount factor $q^{-(3-T)}$ would decrease it.
