Two schedules have the same nominal total. The first requires the full amount now and the second in one year. At a positive compound interest rate, which costs the borrower less?

- They are equivalent because the nominal totals are equal.
- The second, because its payment is in the future.
- The second only if the interest rate is negative.
- The first, because the payment is immediate.

## Explanation

Let $S$ be the common nominal amount. The first schedule pays everything today and costs $S$. The second pays $S$ in one year, with present cost $\dfrac{S}{q}$. At a positive rate $q>1$ and hence $\dfrac{S}{q}<S$. The second costs the borrower less. Although the nominal totals are equal, the different dates give different present values.
