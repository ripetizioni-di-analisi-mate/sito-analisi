Two plans A and B are equivalent at an annual compound rate of $10\%$. Plan A gains a receipt of $55\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and a payment of $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Which immediate cash flow, added to plan B, preserves the equivalence?

- A payment of $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
- A receipt of $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
- A payment of $66\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
- A receipt of $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

## Explanation

Under compound interest, equivalence at one date holds at every date, so the additions can be compared at time $0$. The addition to plan A is worth $\dfrac{55}{1.1}-\dfrac{121}{1.1^2}=50-100=-50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. Since the two plans had the same value before the changes, the value of plan B must also fall by $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Therefore an immediate payment of $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is added to plan B. The value $66\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the difference between the nominal amounts without discounting, while the receipt of $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$ adds the two present values instead of subtracting them.
