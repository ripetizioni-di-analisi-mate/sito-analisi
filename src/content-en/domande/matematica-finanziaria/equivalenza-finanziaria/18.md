An investor is owed $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and owes $360\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. What is the net present value at an annual compound interest rate of $20\%$?

- $-50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-60\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

With $q=1.2$ the receivable is worth $\dfrac{300}{1.2}=250$ euros today and the liability $\dfrac{360}{1.2^2}=\dfrac{360}{1.44}=250$ euros. The liability is negative for the investor, giving a net value of $250-250=0$ euros. The value of $-50$ euros discounts the liability for only one year because $250-300=-50$. The value of $-60$ euros is the difference between the nominal amounts.
