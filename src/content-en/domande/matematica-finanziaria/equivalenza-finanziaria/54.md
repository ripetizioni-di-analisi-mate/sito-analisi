A purchase can be paid with $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today or with $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years, and the two options are equivalent at an annual compound rate of $10\%$. If the valuation rate falls to $5\%$, by how much does the present cost of the deferred payment exceed that of the immediate payment?

- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $105\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $48.75\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $76.19\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The equivalence at $10\%$ comes from the equality $605\cdot1.1^{-2}=500$, which does not hold at a different rate. At $5\%$ the immediate payment still costs $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$, because it falls on the valuation date. The deferred payment is instead worth
$$
\frac{605}{1.05^2}=\frac{605}{1.1025}\approx548.75\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
Therefore the present cost of the deferred payment exceeds the other by about $48.75\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The value $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$ extends the equivalence to every rate, $105\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the difference between the nominal amounts and $76.19\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounts for one year only.
