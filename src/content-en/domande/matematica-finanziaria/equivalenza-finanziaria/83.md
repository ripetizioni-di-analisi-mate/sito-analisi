For a multiplicative accumulation law, $r(1,3)$ and $r(1,5)$ are known. Which factor isolates the interval from time $3$ to time $5$?

- $r(1,3)r(1,5)$
- $\dfrac{r(1,5)}{r(1,3)}$
- $r(1,5)-r(1,3)$
- $\dfrac{r(1,3)}{r(1,5)}$

## Explanation

By multiplicativity, accumulation from $1$ to $5$ can be split into the intervals from $1$ to $3$ and from $3$ to $5$, giving $r(1,5)=r(1,3)\,r(3,5)$. Dividing by $r(1,3)>0$ yields
$$
r(3,5)=\frac{r(1,5)}{r(1,3)}.
$$
The reciprocal ratio $\dfrac{r(1,3)}{r(1,5)}$ is the discount factor from $5$ to $3$, while the product $r(1,3)\,r(1,5)$ counts the interval from $1$ to $3$ twice.
