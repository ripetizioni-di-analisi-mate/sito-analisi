A single payment replacing two debts is due after both original payment dates. At a positive compound interest rate, how does it compare with their nominal sum?

- It is equal because both amounts are positive.
- It is larger because both debts are accumulated.
- It is smaller because both debts are discounted.
- It is larger only if the two debts are equal.

## Explanation

The single payment is due after both original debts, so accumulate each debt to its date $T$. With $q>1$ and exponent $T-t_k>0$, the factor $q^{T-t_k}$ exceeds one, so each term $C_kq^{T-t_k}$ exceeds its nominal amount $C_k$. The single payment therefore exceeds the sum of the positive nominal amounts. They would be equal only at a zero rate.
