A payment today and a payment in two years are equivalent at time $2$ under simple interest at a positive rate. Which missing property prevents us from inferring equivalence at time $1$?

- Multiplicativity of the accumulation factors.
- The existence of a factor equal to one on zero-length intervals.
- The ability to add amounts referring to the same date.
- Positivity of the factor on every interval.

## Explanation

Under simple interest the factor from $s$ to $t$ is $1+i(t-s)$. For two years it is $1+2i$, while multiplying the successive one-year factors gives $(1+i)^2=1+2i+i^2$. For $i>0$ the results differ, so the law is not multiplicative. Preserving equivalence across dates requires multiplicativity, and equivalence at time $2$ therefore need not hold at time $1$.
