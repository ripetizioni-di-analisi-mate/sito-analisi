Two equal receipts have different due dates. At a positive compound interest rate, why does the arithmetic mean of the dates fall after the equivalent average due date?

- The discount factor is strictly convex and decreasing.
- The arithmetic mean uses weights different from the financial weights here.
- The replacement payment is smaller than the nominal sum.
- The discount factor is affine and increasing.

## Explanation

The function $t\mapsto q^{-t}$ has second derivative $(\log q)^2q^{-t}>0$ and is strictly convex. By Jensen's inequality the mean of its values exceeds its value at the mean date, giving $q^{-T}>q^{-\bar t}$. At a positive interest rate the discount factor is decreasing, so $T<\bar t$. Equal payment amounts give equal weights in both calculations, leaving convexity as the source of the difference.
