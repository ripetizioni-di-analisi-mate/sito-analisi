Two schedules are equivalent under compound interest at $10\%$. One pays everything today and the other everything in one year. How does raising the rate affect their present values relative to one another?

- Both present values increase in the same proportion.
- They remain equivalent because interest is still compounded.
- The future payment has a lower present value than the immediate payment.
- The future payment has a higher present value than the immediate payment.

## Explanation

The immediate payment has discount factor $q^0=1$ and its present value is independent of the rate. The payment $C$ due in a year is worth $\dfrac{C}{q}$ today, which falls as $q$ increases. At $10\%$ the two values coincide. At a higher rate the future payment has a lower present value than the immediate one. The two are equivalent only at $10\%$.
