Under compound interest at a constant rate, two schedules are worth $A$ and $B$ at the same time $\tau$, with $A>B$. How do their values compare at time $\tau+1$?

- The second is worth more if the rate is positive.
- The second is worth more if the rate is negative.
- Both equal the mean of the two values.
- The first is still worth more.

## Explanation

Under compound interest at a constant rate, moving any value from time $\tau$ to time $\tau+1$ multiplies it by $q$. Since $i>-1$, the factor $q$ is positive even at a negative rate. Multiplying $A>B$ by $q>0$ gives $Aq>Bq$, so the first schedule is still worth more. The ordering is preserved at every admissible rate, positive or negative.
