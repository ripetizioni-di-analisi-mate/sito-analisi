Two receivables of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in three years are replaced by their nominal sum of $210\,\mathrm{EUR}$. At an annual compound rate of $10\%$, what is the average financial due date?

- $2\,\mathrm{years}$
- $0\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

The average due date $T$ is the date at which $210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ has the same present value as the two receivables, that is $210\cdot1.1^{-T}=100\cdot1.1^{-1}+110\cdot1.1^{-3}$. Since $110=100\cdot1.1$, the first term equals $110\cdot1.1^{-2}$ and the second equals $100\cdot1.1^{-2}$. The right-hand side is therefore $(110+100)\cdot1.1^{-2}=210\cdot1.1^{-2}$, so $1.1^{-T}=1.1^{-2}$ and the average due date is $T=2$ years. The value $-2$ years comes from a sign error in the exponent, while $0$ years is obtained by comparing nominal amounts without discounting them. The value $3$ years is the last due date, which the average due date of positive amounts cannot reach.
