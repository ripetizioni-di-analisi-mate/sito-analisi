Two schedules are worth $480\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $420\,\mathrm{EUR}$, respectively, at time $2$. At an annual compound interest rate of $20\%$, what is the value of A minus B at time $1$?

- $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $40\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $72\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Under compound interest the difference between two schedules can be carried to a new date as a single amount because both values use the same factor. At time $2$ the difference is $480-420=60$ euros. One year earlier it is worth
$$
\frac{60}{1.2}=50.
$$
The required difference is $50$ euros. The value of $60$ euros leaves it at its original date, while $72$ euros accumulates the difference instead of discounting it.
