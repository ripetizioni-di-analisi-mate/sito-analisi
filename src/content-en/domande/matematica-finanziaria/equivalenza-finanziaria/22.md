At a simple annual interest rate of $20\%$, an outflow of $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and one of $700\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years are equivalent at the two-year due date. What is the value of the second outflow at time $1$, expressed as a positive cost to the nearest cent?

- $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $583.33\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $840\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Under simple interest the factor between dates $s$ and $t$ is $1+0.2(t-s)$. To value the second outflow at the earlier date, move it from time $2$ to time $1$ by dividing by the one-year factor. This gives
$$
\frac{700}{1+0.2}=\frac{700}{1.2}\approx583.33.
$$
The amount $600$ euros is the first outflow valued at time $1$. The two values differ because under simple interest equivalence at time $2$ need not hold at another date.
