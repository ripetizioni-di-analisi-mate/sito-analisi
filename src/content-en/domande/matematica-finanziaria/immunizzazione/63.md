Two portfolios satisfy Redington's conditions for the same liability. Their respective quadratic coefficients are $1000$ and $1400$. What does the second-order approximation predict for the same nonzero shock?

- The comparison depends on the sign of the shock.
- The second portfolio has the larger estimated surplus.
- The estimated surpluses are equal.
- The first portfolio has the larger estimated surplus.

## Explanation

Under Redington's conditions the surplus and its first derivative vanish, so the second-order estimate is $k\,h^2$, where $k$ is the quadratic coefficient. For the same shock $h\ne0$ the factor $h^2$ is positive and common to both portfolios. Comparing $1000h^2$ and $1400h^2$ shows that the second portfolio has a **larger** estimated surplus for either sign of the shock.
