The initial force of interest is zero. A portfolio receives $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ and $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $5$. Find the horizon at which the derivative of the horizon value with respect to the force of interest is zero.

- $4\,\mathrm{years}$
- $5\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

The derivative of the horizon value is $W_H'=(H-D)W_H$ and $W_H>0$, so it vanishes exactly when $H=D$. At zero force of interest, all discount factors equal $1$. The weights in the duration calculation are therefore proportional to the nominal amounts, and
$$
D=\frac{200\cdot1+600\cdot5}{200+600}=\frac{3200}{800}=4\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
The answer $3\,\mathrm{years}$ is the simple average of the two maturities, which ignores the amounts.
