After the shock, the effective annual rate stays at $20\%$. One receipt of $240\,\mathrm{EUR}$ occurs exactly at the target time $2$. Another receipt of $432\,\mathrm{EUR}$ falls due at time $4$. Calculate the value at time $2$.

- $540\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $645.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $672\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

At $20\%$, value the cash flows at time $H=2$. The receipt of $240\,\mathrm{EUR}$ falls exactly at the horizon, so include its nominal amount. Discount the receipt at time $4$ for two years to get $\dfrac{432}{1.2^2}=\dfrac{432}{1.44}=300\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The horizon value is therefore $240+300=540\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The answer $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounts the second receipt for only one year, while $645.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ also accumulates the first receipt for two years.
