At an effective annual rate of $10\%$, an asset pays $550\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and a liability requires $363\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Calculate the initial surplus.

- $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $913\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $170\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Find the present value of each amount by dividing by $1.1^t$, where $t$ is its maturity in years. The asset is therefore worth $\dfrac{550}{1.1}=500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the liability $\dfrac{363}{1.1^2}=\dfrac{363}{1.21}=300\,\mathrm{EUR}$, giving a surplus of $S=500-300=200\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The answer $170\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounts the liability for only one year, while $913\,\mathrm{EUR}$ adds the nominal amounts.
