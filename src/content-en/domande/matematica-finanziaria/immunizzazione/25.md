Receipts at times $1$ and $4$ have present values of $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$, respectively. Calculate the Macaulay duration of the assets.

- $2.5\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3\,\mathrm{years}$
- $4\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

Macaulay duration is the present-value-weighted average of the maturities. The total present value is $200+400=600\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so the weights are $\dfrac{200}{600}=\dfrac13$ and $\dfrac{400}{600}=\dfrac23$. Therefore
$$
D_A=\frac13\cdot1+\frac23\cdot4=\frac{1+8}{3}=3\,\mathrm{years}.
$$
The answer $2.5\,\mathrm{years}$ is the simple average of the maturities and ignores the present values.
