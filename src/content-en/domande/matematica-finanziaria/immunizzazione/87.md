The initial curve gives zero surplus. The only nonzero net present values are $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ and $-400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $3$. What relative change in the discount factor at time $3$ offsets a rise of $2\%$ in the discount factor at time $1$?

- $0\%$
- $6\%$
- $-2\%$
- $2\%$

## Explanation

The positive value at time $1$ rises by $2\%$, an increase of $400\cdot0.02=8\,\mathrm{EUR}$. For the surplus to remain zero, the value at time $3$, currently $-400\,\mathrm{EUR}$, must decrease by $8\,\mathrm{EUR}$ to $-408\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Its discount factor must therefore rise by $\dfrac{8}{400}=0.02$, or $2\%$. The answer $-2\%$ ignores the negative sign of the cash flow at time $3$.
