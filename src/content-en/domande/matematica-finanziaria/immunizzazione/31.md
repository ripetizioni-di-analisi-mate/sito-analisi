An asset portfolio has a present value of $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and a duration of $4$ years. A liability has a duration of $6$ years. What present value must the liability have to match the sensitivities to the force of interest?

- $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1800\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The present-value sensitivities to the force of interest are $-A\,D_A$ and $-L\,D_L$, so the derivative of the surplus vanishes when $A\,D_A=L\,D_L$. With the data given, the condition is $1200\cdot4=L_0\cdot6$, hence
$$
L_0=\frac{4800}{6}=800\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The answer $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ matches present values but leaves a nonzero derivative, while $1800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ multiplies by the inverted ratio $\dfrac64$.
