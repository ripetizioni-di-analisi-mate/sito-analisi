A portfolio has a duration of $5$ years at the initial force of interest. The horizon is $3$ years. In the flat-rate model with a permanent shock, what is the sign of the derivative of horizon value with respect to the force of interest?

- Negative.
- Zero.
- Positive.
- It depends on the convexity of the portfolio.

## Explanation

In the flat-force model with a permanent shock, $W_H'(\delta)=(H-D)W_H(\delta)$ holds. The horizon value $W_H$ is positive because the cash flows are positive, while $H-D=3-5=-2$ is negative. The derivative is therefore **negative**. A rise in the force of interest reduces the horizon value. Convexity enters only at second order.
