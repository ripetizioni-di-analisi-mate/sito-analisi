A portfolio's initial surplus and first derivative are zero, and its second derivative is positive. What follows for a sufficiently small nonzero parallel shift in the force of interest?

- The surplus is zero for shifts in either direction.
- The surplus is positive only when the force of interest rises.
- The surplus is positive only when the force of interest falls.
- The surplus is positive for shifts in either direction.

## Explanation

With $S_0=S'_0=0$, the Taylor expansion of the surplus is $S(\delta_0+h)=\dfrac12S''_0h^2+o(h^2)$. Since $S''_0>0$ and $h^2>0$ for $h\ne0$, the quadratic term is positive. For sufficiently small $|h|$ the remainder is smaller in absolute value than the quadratic term, so the surplus is **positive** in both directions. These are Redington's conditions, and $\delta_0$ is a strict local minimum.
