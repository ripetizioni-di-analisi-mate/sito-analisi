At an effective annual rate of $10\%$, a liability requires $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $2$. Receipts of $440\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ are already expected. Calculate the present value of the additional asset needed to bring the surplus to zero.

- $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $660\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $770\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The liability is worth $\dfrac{1210}{1.1^2}=\dfrac{1210}{1.21}=1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and the expected receipts are worth $\dfrac{440}{1.1}=400\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The surplus is zero when assets and liabilities have equal values, so the additional asset must be worth $1000-400=600\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The answer $770\,\mathrm{EUR}$ uses nominal amounts, $1210-440$, while $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ ignores the receipts already expected.
