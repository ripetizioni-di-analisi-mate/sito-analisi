A portfolio pays $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ and $480\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $3$. After the shock, the effective annual rate stays at $20\%$. Calculate the portfolio value at the horizon $H=2$.

- $840\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $760\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $936\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $650\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

With a flat rate of $20\%$, value each cash flow at time $H=2$. The payment at time $1$ precedes the horizon and earns interest for one year, giving $300\cdot1.2=360\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The payment at time $3$ follows the horizon and must be discounted for one year, giving $\dfrac{480}{1.2}=400\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The horizon value is therefore $360+400=760\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The answer $840\,\mathrm{EUR}$ adds the second cash flow without discounting it.
