A firm must pay $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. At an effective annual rate of $10\%$ it covers the commitment with two zero-coupon bonds that repay $550\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $665.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in three years. Immediately after the purchase the rate falls to $0\%$ and stays constant. What is the surplus?

- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $5.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-5.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $215.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

At $10\%$ the two bonds are worth $\dfrac{550}{1.1}=500$ and $\dfrac{665.50}{1.1^3}=500\,\mathrm{EUR}$, while the liability is worth $\dfrac{1210}{1.1^2}=1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The assets therefore have the same value as the liability and a duration of $\dfrac{1+3}{2}=2$ years, equal to that of the liability. The asset maturities are spread around the liability date, so their convexity is larger and the Redington conditions are satisfied. At a zero rate every amount is worth its nominal value, therefore
$$
S=550+665.50-1210=5.50\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $215.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ revalues only the assets at $0\%$ and leaves the liability at $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
