Two positive cash flows mature in $2$ and $7$ years. The common rate rises while the nominal amounts stay fixed. What happens to duration?

- It increases.
- It decreases.
- It rises to the sum of the two maturities.
- It stays the same.

## Explanation

When the force of interest rises by $h>0$, the present value of a cash flow with maturity $t$ is multiplied by $e^{-ht}$. This factor is smaller for $t=7$ than for $t=2$, so the longer cash flow loses a larger fraction of its value. The weight of the $7$-year maturity consequently falls and duration, the weighted average of the two maturities, **decreases**.
