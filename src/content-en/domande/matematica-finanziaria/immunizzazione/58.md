The asset payments due at times $1$ and $5$ have respective present values of $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The liability due at time $3$ has a present value of $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$. What is the coefficient of $h^2$ in the expansion of the surplus with respect to the force of interest?

- $800\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $5200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $1600\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$

## Explanation

The assets are worth $200+200=400\,\mathrm{EUR}$, as is the liability, and their duration is $\dfrac{200\cdot1+200\cdot5}{400}=3$ years, the same as the liability duration. Hence $S_0=S'_0=0$ and the second derivative is
$$
S''(\delta_0)=200\cdot1^2+200\cdot5^2-400\cdot3^2=5200-3600=1600 .
$$
The coefficient of $h^2$ is $\dfrac12S''(\delta_0)=800\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$. The answer $1600$ omits the factor $\dfrac12$, while $5200$ accounts only for the assets.
