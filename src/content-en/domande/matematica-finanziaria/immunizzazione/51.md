A portfolio with positive cash flows has duration $D$ and convexity $D^2$ with respect to the force of interest. What must be true of its payment dates?

- The longest maturity is twice the shortest.
- The dates form an arithmetic progression with a positive common difference.
- Two maturities are equally far from duration.
- All receipts fall on the same date.

## Explanation

For the force of interest, $C=\sigma^2+D^2$ holds, where $\sigma^2$ is the present-value-weighted variance of the maturities. If $C=D^2$, the variance is zero. All weights are positive, so every deviation $t_k-D$ must be zero. All receipts therefore have the same maturity $D$. Two distinct maturities equally far from duration would give positive variance and convexity greater than $D^2$.
