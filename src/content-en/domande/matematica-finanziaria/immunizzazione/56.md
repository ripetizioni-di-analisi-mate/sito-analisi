A surplus satisfies $S_0=S'_0=0$ and $S''_0<0$. What happens under sufficiently small nonzero parallel shifts in the force of interest?

- It becomes positive in either direction.
- It becomes positive only if the force of interest rises.
- It remains zero in either direction.
- It becomes negative in either direction.

## Explanation

With $S_0=S'_0=0$, the Taylor expansion of the surplus is $S(\delta_0+h)=\dfrac12S''_0h^2+o(h^2)$. Since $S''_0<0$, the quadratic term is negative for every $h\ne0$, and for small $|h|$ the remainder cannot change its sign. The surplus therefore becomes **negative** in either direction and $\delta_0$ is a strict local maximum. This reverses the curvature in Redington's conditions.
