Two portfolios have the same positive present value and the same duration. Portfolio A has greater convexity with respect to the force of interest. How does the comparison change when a common effective annual rate is used as the parameter?

- The ordering reverses.
- Both convexities are zero.
- A also has greater convexity with respect to the effective rate.
- The convexities become equal.

## Explanation

Let $C_1>C_2$ be the convexities of portfolios A and B with respect to the force of interest, and let $D$ be their common duration. Convexity with respect to the effective rate is $\dfrac{C+D}{(1+i)^2}$, so the difference between A and B is
$$
\frac{(C_1+D)-(C_2+D)}{(1+i)^2}=\frac{C_1-C_2}{(1+i)^2}.
$$
The denominator is positive, so the difference has the sign of $C_1-C_2$ and A still has greater convexity.
