A single positive receipt occurs one year before the horizon. After the shock, the same flat force of interest applies until the horizon. How does the value at the target date change if the force of interest rises?

- It stays the same because the nominal amount is certain.
- It rises because the payment is reinvested until the horizon.
- It falls because the payment is discounted to the horizon.
- It falls because duration is positive.

## Explanation

A receipt of $c$ at time $t=H-1$ has target-date value $W_H=c\,e^{\delta(H-t)}=c\,e^{\delta}$, with a positive exponent. This is the accumulated value of the receipt reinvested for one year until the horizon. If the force of interest rises, the factor $e^{\delta}$ increases, so the target-date value **increases**. Only payments after the horizon require discounting.
