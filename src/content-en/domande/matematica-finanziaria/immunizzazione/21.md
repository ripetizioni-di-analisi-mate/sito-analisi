A receipt of $484\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $2$ funds a payment of $440\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$. Calculate the new surplus if the effective annual rate falls from $10\%$ to zero.

- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-44\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $84\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $44\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

At $10\%$ the present values are $\dfrac{484}{1.21}=400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $\dfrac{440}{1.1}=400\,\mathrm{EUR}$, giving an initial surplus of zero. If the rate falls to zero, each discount factor equals $1$ and the present values equal the nominal amounts. The new surplus is therefore $484-440=44\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The answer $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the surplus at the original rate, while $84\,\mathrm{EUR}$ combines the nominal receipt with the debt discounted at $10\%$.
