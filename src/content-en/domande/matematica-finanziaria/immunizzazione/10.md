Assets and liabilities are each worth $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the initial force of interest. Their durations are equal, and their convexities are $20$ and $17\,\mathrm{years}^2$, respectively. Find the coefficient of $h^2$ in the expansion of the surplus for a shift $h$ in the force of interest.

- $2400\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $3\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$

## Explanation

Equal initial values and equal durations give $S_0=S'_0=0$, and the second derivative is
$$
S''(\delta_0)=L_0(C_A-C_L)=800(20-17)=2400\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2 .
$$
The coefficient of $h^2$ in the Taylor expansion is $\dfrac12S''(\delta_0)$, which equals $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$. The answer $2400$ is the second derivative without the factor $\dfrac12$, while $3$ is the difference in convexities without the common present value.
