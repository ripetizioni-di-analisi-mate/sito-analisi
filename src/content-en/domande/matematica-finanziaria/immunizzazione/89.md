The net present values at times $1$, $2$ and $3$ are $250$, $-500$ and $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Calculate the new surplus if only the three-year discount factor changes, falling by $4\%$ in relative terms.

- $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-20\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Only the three-year discount factor changes, so the first two values remain $250$ and $-500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The third falls by $4\%$ to $250\cdot0.96=240\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The new surplus is therefore
$$
250-500+240=-10\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The initial surplus was $250-500+250=0$. The answer $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ has the opposite sign, while $-20\,\mathrm{EUR}$ counts the decrease twice.
