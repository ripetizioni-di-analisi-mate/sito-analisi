One year passes between valuations. The flat force of interest and the holdings remain constant, and no payment falls due. How do the present-value weights of the remaining cash flows change?

- The weights of the longer cash flows necessarily fall.
- They stay the same.
- They all rise in the same proportion.
- They all become equal to one another.

## Explanation

If the force of interest stays constant and no payment falls due, after one year each remaining present value equals its old value times $e^{\delta}$. The total value is also multiplied by $e^{\delta}$. Each weight is the ratio of an individual value to the total, so the factor $e^{\delta}$ cancels and the weights **stay the same**. All remaining maturities decrease by one year.
