The initial surplus and its derivative with respect to a parallel shift are zero. Why can the surplus change to first order when only one node of the curve moves?

- The node sensitivities can cancel in the sum without vanishing individually.
- Each node's derivative depends only on the number of securities.
- Duration is the sum of the nominal amounts.
- Every node derivative must be zero, so the first-order change always vanishes.

## Explanation

With a distinct force of interest $\delta_t$ at each node, the derivative of the surplus with respect to $\delta_t$ is $-tN_te^{-\delta_tt}$. A parallel shift changes all the forces of interest together, so its derivative is the sum of the node sensitivities. That sum can vanish even if some terms are positive and others negative. When only one node moves, the sensitivity at that maturity remains and is generally nonzero.
