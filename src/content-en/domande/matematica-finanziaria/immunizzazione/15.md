A receivable of $1250\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is due in two years and a debt of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is due in one year. At an effective annual rate of $25\%$, what is the surplus today?

- $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

At $25\%$, the one-year discount factor is $\dfrac{1}{1.25}$. The receivable is due in two years and is worth $\dfrac{1250}{1.25^2}=\dfrac{1250}{1.5625}=800\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The debt is due in one year and is worth $\dfrac{1000}{1.25}=800\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The surplus is therefore $800-800=0\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The answer $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the difference between the nominal amounts, while $-200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ compares the nominal debt with the present value of the receivable.
