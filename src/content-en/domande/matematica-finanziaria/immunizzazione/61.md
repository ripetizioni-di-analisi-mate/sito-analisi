At the initial force of interest a portfolio has zero surplus and a positive first derivative. For a very small rise in the force of interest, which effect dominates?

- The surplus remains zero to first order.
- The second derivative must be positive.
- The surplus falls below its initial value.
- The surplus rises above its initial value.

## Explanation

With $S(\delta_0)=0$, the Taylor expansion of the surplus is $S(\delta_0+h)=S'(\delta_0)h+o(h)$. For a sufficiently small increase $h>0$, the linear term $S'(\delta_0)h$ is positive and the higher-order terms are small relative to it. The surplus therefore **increases** from its initial value of zero. The sign of the second derivative determines the local direction only when the first derivative vanishes.
