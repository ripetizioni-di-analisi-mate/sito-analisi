A calculation gives $S_0=S'_0=S''_0=0$. What can be concluded about the sign of the surplus near the initial force of interest?

- The surplus is identically zero.
- The surplus is negative throughout a neighborhood except at its center.
- More information about the surplus is needed.
- The surplus is positive throughout a neighborhood.

## Explanation

With $S_0=S'_0=S''_0=0$, all terms through second order in the Taylor expansion vanish, so the sign depends on higher derivatives. A leading cubic term gives opposite signs on the two sides, as in $S(\delta_0+h)=(e^{-h}-1)^3$. If instead the cash flows match date by date, the surplus is identically zero. The given data therefore do not determine the sign, and more information about the surplus is needed.
