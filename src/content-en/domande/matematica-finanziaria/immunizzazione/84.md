The net cash flow at maturity $2$ has a present value of $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$, and the one at maturity $4$ has a present value of $-400\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The first discount factor rises by $10\%$ and the second falls by $5\%$, both in relative terms. Calculate the new surplus.

- $-150\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-100\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The present value of each net cash flow changes in the same proportion as its discount factor. The two-year value becomes $300\cdot1.10=330\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the four-year value becomes $-400\cdot0.95=-380\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The new surplus is therefore
$$
330-380=-50\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The answer $-100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the surplus before the shock, while $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ has the opposite sign.
