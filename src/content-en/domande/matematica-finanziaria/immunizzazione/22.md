A receivable due in $T>0$ years has already been discounted at the positive force of interest $\delta_0$. The force of interest rises by $h>0$. By what factor does the original present value change?

- $e^{-hT}$
- $e^{hT}$
- $e^{-(\delta_0+h)T}$
- $e^{-hT-1}$

## Explanation

Let $c$ be the amount receivable. At the force of interest $\delta_0$ its present value is $c\,e^{-\delta_0T}$, and at the new force of interest it is $c\,e^{-(\delta_0+h)T}$. The ratio of the new value to the old value is therefore
$$
\frac{e^{-(\delta_0+h)T}}{e^{-\delta_0T}}=e^{-hT}.
$$
Since $h>0$ and $T>0$, the factor is less than $1$ and the value falls. The expression $e^{-(\delta_0+h)T}$ is the new discount factor, which applies to the amount $c$ due at maturity rather than to its original present value.
