The initial force of interest is zero. A portfolio pays $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in five years. For a three-year horizon, calculate the second derivative of the horizon value with respect to the force of interest.

- $1600\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $800\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $400\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$

## Explanation

The horizon value is $W_3(\delta)=200e^{2\delta}+200e^{-2\delta}$. Differentiating twice multiplies each term by the square of the coefficient in the exponent, $(\pm2)^2=4$. At zero force of interest this gives
$$
W_3''(0)=200\cdot4+200\cdot4=1600\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2 .
$$
The answer $0$ assigns opposite signs to the two contributions. Both are positive because the distances from the horizon enter as squares.
