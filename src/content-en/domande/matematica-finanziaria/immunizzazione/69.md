At a horizon of $4$ years a portfolio is worth $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the initial force of interest and has an initial duration of $2.5$ years. Calculate the derivative of horizon value with respect to the force of interest for an immediate, permanent shock.

- $2000\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$
- $3200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$
- $-1200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$
- $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$

## Explanation

In the flat-force model with an immediate, permanent shock, the derivative of the horizon value is $W_H'=(H-D)W_H$. With $H=4$ years, $D=2.5$ years and $W_H=800\,\mathrm{EUR}$, this gives
$$
W_H'=(4-2.5)\cdot800=1.5\cdot800=1200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}.
$$
The derivative is positive because duration is shorter than the horizon. The answer $-1200$ uses $D-H$ instead of $H-D$, while $3200$ multiplies by $H$ alone.
