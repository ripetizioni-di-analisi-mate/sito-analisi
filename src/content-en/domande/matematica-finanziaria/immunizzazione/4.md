At an effective annual rate of $25\%$, assets are worth $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and have a Macaulay duration of $3$ years. Liabilities are worth $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and have a Macaulay duration of $4$ years. Calculate the derivative of the surplus in euros with respect to the effective rate.

- $5120\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1280\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-1280\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1600\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

With respect to the effective rate $i$, the derivative of a present value is $A'(i)=-\dfrac{A\,D_A}{1+i}$, where $\dfrac{D_A}{1+i}$ is the modified duration. Therefore
$$
S'(i)=\frac{-A\,D_A+L\,D_L}{1+i}=\frac{-800\cdot3+1000\cdot4}{1.25}=\frac{1600}{1.25}=1280\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The answer $1600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ omits the factor $\dfrac{1}{1+i}$, while $-1280\,\mathrm{EUR}$ reverses the signs of both sensitivities.
