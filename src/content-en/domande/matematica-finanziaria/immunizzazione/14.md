The surplus on the reference curve is zero, and the year-four net cash flow has a present value of $-500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Its discount factor increases by $2\%$ in relative terms. Calculate the new surplus if all other factors remain the same.

- $-510\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-10\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The present value of a net cash flow equals its amount times the discount factor for its date, so it is proportional to that factor. When the year-four factor rises by $2\%$, the cash flow's present value becomes $-500\cdot1.02=-510\,\mathrm{EUR}$, a decrease of $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The other present values are unchanged and the initial surplus was zero, so the new surplus is $-10\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The answer $-510\,\mathrm{EUR}$ gives the new present value of the year-four cash flow alone.
