A portfolio consists of one zero-coupon bond maturing exactly at the horizon. How does a parallel shift in the flat force of interest affect its horizon value?

- The value is stable only to first order.
- The value falls as the force of interest rises.
- The value stays equal to the nominal redemption payment.
- The value rises as the force of interest rises.

## Explanation

The horizon value of a cash flow $c$ at time $t$ is $c\,e^{\delta(H-t)}$. When maturity equals the horizon, the exponent is $\delta(H-H)=0$ and the factor is $1$ for any force of interest. The horizon value therefore equals the nominal redemption payment at every order and for shocks of any size. This is the simplest case in which duration equals the horizon.
