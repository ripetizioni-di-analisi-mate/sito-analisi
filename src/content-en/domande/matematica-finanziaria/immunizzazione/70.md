At zero force of interest a portfolio pays $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ and $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $3$. Calculate the derivative of its value at horizon $2$ with respect to the force of interest.

- $-1000\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$
- $-200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$
- $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$
- $200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$

## Explanation

The horizon value is $W_2(\delta)=\sum_k c_ke^{\delta(2-t_k)}$, and the derivative of each term with respect to $\delta=0$ is $c_k(2-t_k)$. The first cash flow contributes $400(2-1)=400$ and the second contributes $600(2-3)=-600$. Therefore
$$
W_2'(0)=400-600=-200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}.
$$
The answer $200$ reverses the signs by using $t_k-H$ in place of $H-t_k$.
