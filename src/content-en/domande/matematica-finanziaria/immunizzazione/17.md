A portfolio has $350\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in cash today and will receive $242\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. It must pay $660\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. At an effective annual rate of $10\%$, how much additional cash today would give a surplus of zero?

- $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $68\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The available cash is worth $350\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today, and the future receipt is worth $\dfrac{242}{1.1^2}=\dfrac{242}{1.21}=200\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The payment is worth $\dfrac{660}{1.1}=600\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Total assets are therefore $550\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the surplus is $550-600=-50\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Additional cash of $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ brings the surplus to zero.

Using the nominal amounts gives $68\,\mathrm{EUR}$, since $660-350-242$.
