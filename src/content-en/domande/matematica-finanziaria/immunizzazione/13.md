At time $2$ the zero-coupon bonds in a portfolio have original maturity dates $3$ and $7$. The shorter bond holding is already worth $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The remaining liabilities are worth $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and have a remaining duration of $3$ years. How much more should be invested in the shorter bond to reach its target value?

- $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

At time $2$ the bonds have $3-2=1$ and $7-2=5$ years left to maturity. The remaining liability duration of $3$ years is midway between $1$ and $5$, so the target fraction in the shorter bond is $\dfrac{5-3}{5-1}=\dfrac12$. Its target value is therefore $1000\cdot\dfrac12=500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Since the existing holding is worth $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$, buy an additional $500-300=200\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The answer $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ gives the target value before subtracting the existing holding.
