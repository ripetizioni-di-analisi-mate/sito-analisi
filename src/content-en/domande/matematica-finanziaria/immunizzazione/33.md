At the initial force of interest the derivative of the surplus is $-1500\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$. Find the first-order change in the surplus if the force of interest falls by $0.002\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$.

- $30\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-30\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-3\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $3\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The first-order change in the surplus is $\Delta S\approx S'(\delta_0)h$. A decrease of $0.002\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$ in the force of interest corresponds to $h=-0.002$, so
$$
\Delta S\approx(-1500)\cdot(-0.002)=3\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The product of two negative numbers is positive, so the surplus increases. The answer $-3\,\mathrm{EUR}$ takes $h$ as positive, while $30\,\mathrm{EUR}$ misplaces the decimal point by one position.
