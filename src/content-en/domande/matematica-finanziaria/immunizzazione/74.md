A portfolio has two receipts on opposite sides of the horizon, and its initial duration equals the horizon. If reinvestment rates also vary from date to date after the shock, what follows from the initial data alone?

- Evaluating sensitivity to the new path of rates requires knowing that path.
- The cash flows cancel at every date.
- The hedge depends only on the longest maturity.
- The hedge holds for every possible path of rates.

## Explanation

The formula $W_H'=(H-D)W_H$ assumes that the same force of interest applies immediately after the shock to every transfer of value, both the pricing of remaining securities and the reinvestment of receipts. If reinvestment rates then change from date to date, the horizon value depends on the actual factor for each transfer. The condition $D=H$ is therefore insufficient on its own. The path of rates is needed.
