A liability has a present value of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and a duration of $4$ years. The available instruments are two zero-coupon bonds maturing in $2$ and $6$ years, short sales are not allowed and at most $40\%$ of the capital may be invested in the six-year bond. Investing the whole $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$, what is the largest duration that can be obtained?

- $4\,\mathrm{years}$
- $4.4\,\mathrm{years}$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{years}$
- $3.6\,\mathrm{years}$

## Explanation

Let $w$ be the share of the capital invested in the six-year bond. The duration of the portfolio is the average of the maturities weighted by the amounts invested, so $D=2(1-w)+6w=2+4w$. This function increases with $w$, therefore the maximum is reached at the largest permitted share, $w=0.4$, and equals $D=2+4\cdot0.4=3.6$ years. Matching the duration of $4$ years would require $w=0.5$, which the constraint rules out. The value $4.4$ years corresponds to $60\%$ in the long bond, while $2.4$ years counts only the contribution of the long bond.
