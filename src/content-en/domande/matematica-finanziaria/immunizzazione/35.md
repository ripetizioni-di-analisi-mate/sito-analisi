A debt has a present value of $700\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and a duration of $2$ years. Two available zero-coupon bonds mature in $1$ and $8$ years. How much should be invested in the shorter bond?

- $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $350\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $700\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

For two zero-coupon bonds with maturities $T_1=1$ and $T_2=8$, matching value and duration gives the following fraction in the shorter bond.
$$
w_1=\frac{T_2-D_L}{T_2-T_1}=\frac{8-2}{8-1}=\frac67 .
$$
Thus $700\cdot\dfrac67=600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is invested in the shorter bond and the remaining $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in the longer bond. The answer $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the holding in the longer bond, while $350\,\mathrm{EUR}$ splits the investment equally without accounting for duration.
