A liability has a duration of $9$ years. The only assets available mature in $3$ and $6$ years and pay only at maturity. Why is it impossible to match the liability duration using purchases alone?

- A weighted average with nonnegative weights cannot exceed six years.
- The weighted average always equals three years.
- The liability must have zero value.
- Convexity must be negative.

## Explanation

A zero-coupon bond's duration equals its maturity, so the bonds have durations of $3$ and $6$ years. With purchases alone the weights $w_1,w_2$ are nonnegative and sum to $1$. The portfolio duration $3w_1+6w_2$ therefore lies between $3$ and $6$ years. The liability duration of $9$ years lies outside this interval and can be reached only by selling the shorter bond short.
