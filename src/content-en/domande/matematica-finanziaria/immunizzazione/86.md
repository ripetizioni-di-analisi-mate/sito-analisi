The surplus is zero because asset and liability payments match on every date. The yield curve tilts, and both sides still use the same discount factors. What happens to the surplus?

- It becomes positive because durations increase.
- It remains zero.
- It remains zero only to first order.
- It becomes negative because the weights change.

## Explanation

Receipts equal payments on every date, so the net cash flow is $N_t=0$ for every $t$. The surplus is the sum of the terms $N_t$ multiplied by each date's discount factor, common to assets and liabilities. Each term therefore stays zero for any discount factors, even when the yield curve tilts, and the surplus **remains zero** exactly, at every order.
