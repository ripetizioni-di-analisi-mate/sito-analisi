A liability is worth $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and has a duration of $4$ years. Two zero-coupon bonds mature in $1$ and $5$ years. How much should be invested in the longer bond to match both value and duration?

- $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $450\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $360\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The duration of each zero-coupon bond equals its maturity, so portfolio duration is the average of the maturities weighted by the fractions invested. For a portfolio worth $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ with a duration of $4$ years, the fraction invested in the longer bond is
$$
w_2=\frac{D_L-T_1}{T_2-T_1}=\frac{4-1}{5-1}=\frac34 .
$$
The amount invested in the longer bond is therefore $600\cdot\dfrac34=450\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The answer $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the amount invested in the shorter bond.
