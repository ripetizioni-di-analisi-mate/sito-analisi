A single liability payment due at time $5$ has a present value of $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Calculate the second derivative of its value with respect to the force of interest.

- $25\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $2500\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $-12500\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$
- $12500\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$

## Explanation

The present value of the liability is $L(\delta)=\ell\,e^{-5\delta}$, with $L(\delta_0)=500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Differentiating twice with respect to $\delta$ introduces the factor $-5$ twice, so $L''(\delta)=25\,\ell\,e^{-5\delta}=25\,L(\delta)$. Therefore
$$
L''(\delta_0)=25\cdot500=12500\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2 .
$$
The answer $-12500$ retains the sign of the first derivative, while $2500$ multiplies by $5$ instead of $5^2$.
