An annuity due makes at least two positive payments, the first of $120$ euros. A price of $100$ euros is proposed. Which conclusion follows?

- There is a positive implied rate because the price is below the first installment.
- No finite rate greater than $-100\%$ makes the cash flows equivalent.
- The implied rate is negative.
- The implied rate is zero.

## Explanation

Today's payment enters the value at $120$ euros whatever the rate. The other positive payments have positive present value for every $i>-1$, so the annuity is worth more than $120$ euros at every admissible rate. Since the price of $100$ euros is below $120$ euros, no rate makes the cash flows equivalent. A solution exists only at prices above the initial payment $R$.
