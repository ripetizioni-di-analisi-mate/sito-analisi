Deposits of $100$ euros are made at the end of each of the next three years into an account earning an effective annual rate of $20\%$. After the third deposit the balance stays in the account for two more years, with no further deposits or withdrawals. What is the balance at the end of the fifth year?

- $364$ euros
- $524.16$ euros
- $432$ euros
- $628.99$ euros

## Explanation

Immediately after the third deposit the balance is the accumulated value of the ordinary annuity,
$$
100\,s_{\overline3|0.2}=100\left(1.2^2+1.2+1\right)=364\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
This balance earns interest for two more years, so at the end of the fifth year it is worth $364\cdot1.2^2=364\cdot1.44=524.16$ euros. The value of $364$ euros ignores the two years after the last deposit. The value of $432$ euros accumulates the nominal sum of the deposits for two years, without the interest earned before year three. The value of $628.99$ euros treats the deposits as made at the start of each year.
