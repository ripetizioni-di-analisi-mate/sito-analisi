A given principal is repaid with the same number of installments at a fixed positive rate. How does the installment change if payments move from the end to the beginning of each period?

- Multiply the payment by $q$.
- Divide the payment by $q$.
- Divide the payment by the number of payment dates.
- The payment stays the same.

## Explanation

For a fixed principal $C$, the payment in arrears is $R=\dfrac{C}{a_{\overline n|i}}$ and the payment in advance is $R'=\dfrac{C}{\ddot a_{\overline n|i}}$. Since $\ddot a_{\overline n|i}=q\,a_{\overline n|i}$, we have $R'=\dfrac Rq$. At a positive rate $q>1$, so the payment in advance is smaller. Multiplying the annuity factor in the denominator by $q$ divides the payment by the same factor.
