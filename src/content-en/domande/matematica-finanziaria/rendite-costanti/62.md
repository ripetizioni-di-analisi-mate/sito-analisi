A finite ordinary annuity pays $100$ euros per year and is valued at an effective annual rate of $10\%$. What amount is a strict upper bound on its present value for any finite number of payments?

- $0$ euros.
- $1000$ euros.
- $900$ euros.
- $100$ euros.

## Explanation

A perpetuity with the same payment is worth $\dfrac{100}{0.1}=1000$ euros. The finite schedule contains only the first $n$ payments. The perpetuity also includes those from time $n+1$ onward, worth $1000\,v^n>0$. Therefore $100\,a_{\overline n|0.1}=1000(1-v^n)<1000$ euros for every $n$. Since $v^n\to0$, no number smaller than $1000$ is an upper bound for every $n$.
