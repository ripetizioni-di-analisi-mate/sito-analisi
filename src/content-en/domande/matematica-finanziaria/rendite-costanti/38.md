Given $i>0$ and at least three payments, how can the accumulated value at time $n$ of an ordinary annuity be found from its present value $V(0)$?

- $qV(0)$
- $q^{n-1}V(0)$
- $q^n V(0)$
- $v^n V(0)$

## Explanation

The present value $V(0)=Ra_{\overline n|i}$ refers to time zero. To value it at time $n$, accumulate it for $n$ periods, giving
$$
M=q^nV(0)=Rq^na_{\overline n|i}=Rs_{\overline n|i}.
$$
The exponent is the distance between the valuation dates, zero and $n$. The expression $q^{n-1}V(0)$ gives the value at time $n-1$, one period before the last installment.
