A schedule has seven equal deposits at a zero interest rate. How does the accumulated value immediately after the final deposit compare with their present value?

- The accumulated value equals eight payments because the opening balance is included.
- The present value is less than the accumulated value because it precedes the last deposit.
- The accumulated value equals six payments because the first deposit earns no interest.
- The two values are equal and each equals seven payments.

## Explanation

At a zero interest rate, $q=v=1$, so every discount and accumulation factor equals one. The present value is $Ra_{\overline7|0}=7R$ and the accumulated value immediately after the last deposit is $Rs_{\overline7|0}=7R$. The two are equal. In general $M=q^nV(0)$, and with $q=1$ they coincide for any number of deposits.
