The price equation for an ordinary annuity is written as a polynomial in $v$. Which root can represent an admissible interest rate?

- The root $v=0$.
- A strictly positive real root.
- A strictly negative real root.
- A complex root of modulus below one.

## Explanation

For an interest rate $i>-1$, the accumulation factor $1+i$ is positive. The discount factor $v=\dfrac{1}{1+i}$ is therefore strictly positive and real. Conversely, each $v>0$ corresponds to the rate $i=\dfrac1v-1>-1$. Only a strictly positive real root thus represents an admissible rate. The root $v=0$ represents no finite rate.
