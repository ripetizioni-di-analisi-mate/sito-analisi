A perpetuity pays $30$ euros each year beginning next year. What happens to its present value at a zero interest rate?

- The partial sums converge to $300$ euros.
- The partial sums grow without bound.
- The present value is $30$ euros.
- The present value is zero.

## Explanation

At a zero rate, $v=1$, so each payment is worth $30$ euros today. The first $n$ payments are worth $30n$ euros in total. Since $30n\to+\infty$ as $n\to\infty$, the partial sums grow without bound and the perpetuity has no finite present value. The formula $\dfrac Ri$ requires $i>0$, which fails here. The value of $300$ euros corresponds to $\dfrac{30}{0.1}$, a rate of $10\%$.
