An annuity due costs less than the corresponding ordinary annuity. Both have the same positive payment and the same positive number of payments. What is the sign of the interest rate?

- Positive.
- The sign depends on the number of payments.
- Zero.
- Negative.

## Explanation

The annuity-due and ordinary annuity factors satisfy $\ddot a_{\overline n|i}=q\,a_{\overline n|i}$, so the ratio of their prices is $q=1+i$. The annuity due has the lower price, giving $1+i<1$ and hence $i<0$. The rate is therefore negative. At a zero rate the prices would be equal, and at a positive rate the annuity due would cost more for any number of payments.
