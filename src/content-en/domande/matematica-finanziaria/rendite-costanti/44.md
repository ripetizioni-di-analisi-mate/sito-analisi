Two otherwise identical ordinary annuities have first payments in one and three years, respectively. The first is worth $1210$ euros today. What is the second worth at an effective annual interest rate of $10\%$?

- $1210$ euros
- $1100$ euros
- $1000$ euros
- $1464.1$ euros

## Explanation

The two annuities have identical payments but the first begins at time $1$ and the second at time $3$. Each payment of the second annuity falls two periods after its counterpart. Multiplying the present value by $v^2$ therefore gives
$$
1210\cdot\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^2=1000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value of $1100$ euros discounts for just one period, while $1464.10$ euros accumulates instead of discounting.
