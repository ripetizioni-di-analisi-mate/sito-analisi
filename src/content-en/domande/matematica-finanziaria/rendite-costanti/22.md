Let $A=v+v^2+v^3+v^4$ with $0<v<1$. What remains after subtracting $vA$ from $A$?

- $v-v^5$
- $1-v^4$
- $v-v^4$
- $v^2-v^5$

## Explanation

Multiplying $A$ by $v$ increases every exponent by one, giving $vA=v^2+v^3+v^4+v^5$. In the difference $A-vA$ the powers from $v^2$ through $v^4$ occur in both sums and cancel, leaving $A-vA=v-v^5$. The expression $v-v^4$ overlooks the term $v^5$ in $vA$. The subtraction gives the formula $a_{\overline n|i}=\dfrac{1-v^n}{i}$.
