A perpetuity pays $90$ euros annually starting at the end of year one. By how much does its present value fall when the effective annual rate rises from $5\%$ to $6\%$?

- $90$ euros
- $180$ euros
- $150$ euros
- $300$ euros

## Explanation

A perpetuity payable in arrears is worth $\dfrac Ri$. At $5\%$ it is worth $\dfrac{90}{0.05}=1800$ euros, and at $6\%$ it is worth $\dfrac{90}{0.06}=1500$ euros, a decrease of $1800-1500=300$ euros. A higher rate reduces each factor $v^k$, and those reductions add up across infinitely many payments.
