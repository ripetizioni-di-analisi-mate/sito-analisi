At a negative interest rate greater than $-100\%$, how do the individual discounted payments of a perpetuity with positive payments behave?

- They grow as the due dates become more distant.
- They become negative after the first due date.
- They decrease toward zero.
- They all equal the payment amount.

## Explanation

For $-1<i<0$, we have $0<1+i<1$ and consequently $v=\dfrac{1}{1+i}>1$. The contribution of the payment at time $k$ is $Rv^k$, which is positive because $R>0$ and $v>0$. Since $v>1$, the sequence $v^k$ is increasing and more distant payments contribute more. The partial sums therefore diverge, and the perpetuity has no finite present value.
