After two years of repayments a loan has an outstanding balance of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$. For the following year only, the rate falls from $6\%$ to $2\%$, while the installment stays at $240\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Compared with the original schedule, by how much does the principal component of this installment increase?

- $20\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $40\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $9.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The interest component of the third year is computed on the outstanding balance of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$. At $6\%$ it is $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so in the original schedule the principal component is $240-60=180\,\mathrm{EUR}$. At $2\%$ the interest component is $20\,\mathrm{EUR}$, therefore the principal component becomes $240-20=220\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The principal component thus increases by $220-180=40\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The value $9.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ applies the four-point reduction to the installment instead of the balance.
