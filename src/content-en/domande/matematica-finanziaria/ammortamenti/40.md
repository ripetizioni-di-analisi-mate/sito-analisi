In an equal-principal loan at $6\%$, the installments form a decreasing arithmetic sequence. What accounts for the constant decrease?

- The opening balance falls by the same principal portion each period.
- The interest rate falls by the same number of percentage points at each payment.
- Each principal portion decreases by the same amount.
- The balance remains equal to the original principal until maturity.

## Explanation

The equal-principal installment is $R_k=C+iD_{k-1}$. The principal portion $C$ is constant, so the opening balance falls by $C$ at every due date. Interest $iD_{k-1}$ consequently falls by $iC$ per period, and the installment decreases by that same amount. The interest rate stays fixed at $6\%$ and the principal portions are constant, so the fixed decrease comes entirely from the falling balance.
