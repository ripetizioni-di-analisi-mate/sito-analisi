Two level-payment loans have the same principal and positive rate. The second adds two final installments at the same payment frequency. How does its installment change?

- It decreases.
- It increases.
- It stays the same.
- It depends on the day of the first payment, even though both schedules start paying after one period.

## Explanation

The level installment is $R=\dfrac{D_0}{a_{\overline n|i}}$. Adding two payment dates changes the annuity factor to $a_{\overline{n+2}|i}=a_{\overline n|i}+v^{n+1}+v^{n+2}$, with two additional positive terms. The principal is unchanged, so the installment decreases. For example, at a zero rate and with $D_0=1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$, extending the term from four payments to six reduces the installment from $300$ to $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
