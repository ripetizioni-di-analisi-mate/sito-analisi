An amortization schedule has been split into interest and principal portions. Which equality holds at a zero valuation rate?

- The principal-only value equals the sum of the principal portions.
- The interest-only value is always zero.
- The principal-only value equals the sum of the full installments.
- The interest-only value always equals the original principal.

## Explanation

At a zero valuation rate $(1+j)^{-k}=1$ for every $k$, so $N(0)=\sum C_k$ and $U(0)=\sum I_k$. The principal-only value is therefore the sum of the principal portions, equal to the original loan when the loan is fully repaid. The interest-only value is the sum of all interest, which is positive at a positive contractual rate.
