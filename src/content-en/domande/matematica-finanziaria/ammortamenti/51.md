A separate sinking fund supporting a loan's final principal repayment will receive one fewer deposit than planned. What happens at maturity if all other amounts stay the same?

- The lender reduces the principal owed by the same amount.
- The fund reaches the same balance because its rate is fixed.
- The fund accumulates less, and the principal owed to the lender remains the amount agreed.
- Interest already paid is transferred to the fund.

## Explanation

The final fund balance is the sum of all deposits accumulated to maturity. Omitting a deposit removes its entire accumulated value, so the fund falls short of its target. The separate loan contract fixes the principal owed to the lender. At maturity the borrower must repay the full principal and make up the shortfall from other resources.
