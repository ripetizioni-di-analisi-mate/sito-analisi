A loan with positive future installments has a market value equal to its contractual balance on the same date. What follows about the constant valuation and contractual rates?

- The market rate is higher.
- The market rate is lower.
- Equality is possible only when both rates are zero.
- They are equal.

## Explanation

The market value $V_k(j)$ decreases strictly in $j$ because the remaining payments are positive and every factor $(1+j)^{k-h}$ has a negative exponent. For $j=i$ the value is the contractual balance $D_k$. A strictly decreasing function takes the value $D_k$ only once, so equality requires $j=i$. At a higher market rate the value would be below $D_k$.
