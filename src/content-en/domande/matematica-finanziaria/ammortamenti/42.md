The original principal of an equal-principal loan doubles with the term and rate held fixed. What happens to its installments?

- The first doubles and all the others stay the same.
- The principal portions double and the interest portions stay the same.
- They all increase by the same fixed amount, even at a positive rate.
- Each doubles.

## Explanation

In the equal-principal loan both $C=\dfrac{D_0}{n}$ and $D_{k-1}=D_0-(k-1)C$ are proportional to $D_0$. Interest $iD_{k-1}$ is also proportional to $D_0$ because the rate is fixed. Each installment $R_k=C+iD_{k-1}$ therefore doubles. For example, with $n=2$ and $i=5\%$, a loan of $2000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ has installments $1100$ and $1050\,\mathrm{EUR}$, while a loan of $4000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ has installments $2200$ and $2100\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
