An equal-principal loan has $n\geq2$ installments and a positive interest rate. Which equality holds immediately after the penultimate installment?

- $D_{n-1}=R_n$.
- $D_{n-1}=D_0$.
- $D_{n-1}=C_n$.
- $D_{n-1}=0$ at every interest rate.

## Explanation

After $n-1$ installments of an equal-principal loan, the balance is $D_{n-1}=D_0-(n-1)C=C$. Since $C_n=C$, we have $D_{n-1}=C_n$. The final installment is $R_n=C+iC$. At a positive interest rate it exceeds the balance $D_{n-1}$ by the last period's interest. Thus $D_{n-1}=R_n$ holds only at a zero rate. For example, with $D_0=3000\,\mathrm{EUR}$, $n=3$, and $i=5\%$, we have $D_2=1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $R_3=1050\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
