An equal-principal loan and a level-payment loan have the same principal, positive rate, and number of installments, greater than one. Which outstanding balance is smaller immediately after the first installment?

- The level-payment loan.
- The equal-principal loan.
- The two outstanding balances are equal.
- The comparison depends on the first interest portion.

## Explanation

In the equal-principal loan the first principal portion is $\dfrac{D_0}{n}$. In the level-payment loan the principal portions sum to $D_0$ and increase strictly, so the first is below their mean $\dfrac{D_0}{n}$. The equal-principal loan therefore repays more principal with the first installment and has the smaller balance afterward. For example, for $3000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at $5\%$ over three payments, the balances are $2000$ and approximately $2048.37\,\mathrm{EUR}$.
