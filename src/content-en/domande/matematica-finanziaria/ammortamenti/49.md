An interest-only loan repays its principal at maturity and runs for at least two periods. The borrower pays interest and makes a level sinking-fund deposit at each period-end. The loan and fund have the same positive rate. Which quantity agrees with its counterpart in a level-payment loan with the same principal and term?

- The total periodic outlay for interest and the sinking-fund deposit.
- The interest paid to the lender at each individual due date.
- The debt owed to the lender after each payment.
- The amount of each deposit into the fund.

## Explanation

The periodic outlay is $iD_0+\dfrac{D_0}{s_{\overline n|i}}$. Since $s_{\overline n|i}=q^na_{\overline n|i}$ and $i\,s_{\overline n|i}=q^n-1$, this gives
$$
iD_0+\frac{D_0}{s_{\overline n|i}}=\frac{D_0q^n}{s_{\overline n|i}}=\frac{D_0}{a_{\overline n|i}},
$$
which is the installment of a level-payment loan. Interest paid to the lender and outstanding balances differ, because the principal owed under the sinking-fund arrangement remains $D_0$.
