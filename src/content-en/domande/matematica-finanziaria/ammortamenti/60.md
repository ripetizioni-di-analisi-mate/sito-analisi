Why do the retrospective and prospective balances agree when the contractual rate is used and the loan is fully repaid at maturity?

- Both use the current market rate.
- Both follow from the original equation of value for the principal and all installments, carried to the balance date.
- The present value of each installment equals its accumulated value.
- The undiscounted installments always sum to the original principal.

## Explanation

At inception, the equation of value at the contractual rate is $D_0=\sum_{h=1}^nR_hq^{-h}$. Multiplying by $q^k$ carries the equation to time $k$. Separating past and future payments gives
$$
D_0q^k=\sum_{h=1}^kR_hq^{k-h}+\sum_{h=k+1}^nR_hq^{k-h}.
$$
Subtract the first sum. On the left, $D_0q^k-\sum_{h=1}^kR_hq^{k-h}$ is the retrospective balance. On the right, the sum of discounted future installments is the prospective balance. The two methods therefore give the same result.
