A bank increases the interest rate on a level-payment loan immediately after the third installment, keeping the final date fixed. Why does the installment increase for the same outstanding balance?

- The original amount advanced increases.
- All installments already paid are recalculated retroactively.
- The sum of the new discount factors increases.
- The present-value factor for the remaining installments decreases.

## Explanation

The new installment is $R=\dfrac{D_3}{a_{\overline m|j}}$ with $m$ installments remaining. As the rate increases each discount factor $(1+j)^{-h}$ falls, reducing the annuity factor $a_{\overline m|j}$. With the same outstanding balance $D_3$, the installment rises. The revision applies only to subsequent periods, leaving past payments and the original principal unchanged.
