Two deposits with equal principal earn simple interest at $6\%$ per year for eight months and $8\%$ per year for six months, respectively. How do their accumulated values compare?

- The deposit earning $8\%$ has the higher accumulated value.
- They are equal.
- The comparison depends on the initial principal.
- The eight-month deposit has the higher accumulated value.

## Explanation

The simple-interest accumulation factor is $1+it$. With equal principal, only the product $it$ matters. For the first deposit, $it=0.06\cdot\dfrac{8}{12}=0.04$. For the second, $it=0.08\cdot\dfrac{6}{12}=0.04$. Both factors equal $1.04$, so the accumulated values agree for any initial principal. The shorter term offsets the second deposit's higher rate.
