A deposit of $3000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ earns $10\%$ simple annual interest. After one year its full balance is reinvested for one more year at the same rate. By how much does the final balance exceed that of a two-year simple-interest deposit with the same initial principal?

- $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $0\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $30\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The two-year simple-interest factor is $1+0.10\cdot2=1.20$, giving a balance of $3600\,\mathrm{EUR}$. With renewal, the first year's balance of $3300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is reinvested and grows to $3300\cdot1.10=3630\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in the second year. The difference is $30\,\mathrm{EUR}$, the second year's interest on the $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ earned in year one. The answer $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is one year's interest on the original principal.
