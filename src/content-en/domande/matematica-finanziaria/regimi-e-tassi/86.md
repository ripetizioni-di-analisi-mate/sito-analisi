A continuous accumulation factor depends only on the term, equals $1$ for a zero term and satisfies $r(s+t)=r(s)r(t)$. If $r(1)=0.9$, what is $r\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$?

- $0.45$
- $\sqrt{0.9}$
- $0.81$
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{0.9}}$

## Explanation

Setting $s=t=\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the multiplicative property gives
$$
r(1)=r\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2=0.9.
$$
The accumulation factor is positive, so $r\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=\sqrt{0.9}\approx0.9487$. The answer $0.45$ halves the factor instead of taking its square root. The answer $0.81$ is the two-year factor. The answer $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{0.9}}$ corresponds to the term $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
