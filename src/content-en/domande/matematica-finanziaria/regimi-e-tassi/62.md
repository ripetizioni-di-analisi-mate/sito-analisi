An effective six-month compound rate of $10\%$ is equivalent to a twelve-month rate of $21\%$. Under exponential compounding for fractional periods, how do their three-month factors compare?

- They are equal and both are $\sqrt{1.21}$.
- They are equal and both are $\sqrt{1.1}$.
- They are equal and both are $1.05$.
- The factor obtained from the annual rate is larger.

## Explanation

Three months is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a six-month period and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a year. Under exponential compounding the first rate gives the factor $1.1^{\frac{1}{2}}$ and the second gives $1.21^{\frac{1}{4}}$. Since $1.21=1.1^2$, we have
$$
1.21^{\frac{1}{4}}=\left(1.1^2\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}=1.1^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{1.1}.
$$
Equivalent rates give the same factor for every term. The answer $1.05$ is a simple-interest factor, while $\sqrt{1.21}$ is the six-month factor.
