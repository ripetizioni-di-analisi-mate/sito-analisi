The annual interest rate under rational discount is fixed and positive. How does the equivalent annual commercial discount rate change as the term increases?

- It stays constant.
- It decreases.
- It always becomes negative.
- It increases.

## Explanation

For an annual interest rate $i$ under rational discount and a term $t$, the equivalent commercial discount rate is
$$
d=\frac{i}{1+it}.
$$
With $i>0$ fixed, the numerator is constant and the denominator $1+it$ increases with $t$, so $d$ decreases. For example, with $i=10\%$ the equivalent rate is $d\approx9.52\%$ for six months and $d\approx9.09\%$ for one year. The equivalent rate stays positive for every term.
