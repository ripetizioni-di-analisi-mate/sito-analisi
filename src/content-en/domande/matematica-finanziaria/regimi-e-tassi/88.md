A contract has force of interest $\delta(u)=0.02u$. Its accumulation factor between two dates is obtained by integrating the force of interest over that interval. Why can one-year periods with different starting dates have different factors?

- The integral depends on the starting date as well as the length of the period.
- A positive exponential makes every factor equal to one.
- Intervals of equal length always have equal factors.
- The integrals over consecutive intervals sum to zero.

## Explanation

The accumulation factor from $a$ to $b$ is $\exp\left(\int_a^b0.02u\,du\right)=\exp\left(0.01\left(b^2-a^2\right)\right)$. From $0$ to $1$ the exponent is $0.01$, and from $1$ to $2$ it is $0.03$. The force of interest increases over time, so the integral depends on the starting date as well as the term. The law is therefore not time-homogeneous, and equal-length intervals can have different factors.
