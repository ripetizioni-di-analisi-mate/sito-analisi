A contract has a positive effective annual compound rate. How does the equivalent nominal annual rate compounded semiannually compare with the effective annual rate?

- The nominal rate is higher.
- It depends on the principal.
- The two rates are equal.
- The nominal rate is lower.

## Explanation

Let $i_2>0$ be the effective six-month rate. The nominal annual rate compounded twice a year is $j^{(2)}=2i_2$, and the effective annual rate is
$$
i=(1+i_2)^2-1=2i_2+i_2^2.
$$
Since $i_2^2>0$, we have $i>j^{(2)}$, so the nominal rate is lower. For example, an effective annual rate of $21\%$ corresponds to a nominal annual rate of $20\%$ compounded semiannually. This relationship between the rates is independent of the principal.
