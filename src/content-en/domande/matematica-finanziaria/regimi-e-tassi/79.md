A positive, time-varying force of interest produces the same two-year growth as a constant force of interest. Which quantity must be the same under both models?

- The force of interest at the end of year two.
- The initial force of interest.
- The force of interest at the end of year one.
- The integral of the force of interest over both years.

## Explanation

The two-year accumulation factor is $\exp\left(\int_0^2\delta(u)\,du\right)$. The exponential function is strictly increasing and therefore one-to-one. Two factors are equal exactly when their integrals are equal. The equivalent constant force of interest is therefore $\dfrac{1}{2}\int_0^2\delta(u)\,du$, the average of the time-varying force over the two years. The force at a single instant measures only the relative growth rate at that instant.
