Two receivables are discounted for the same positive term at the same positive commercial discount rate. One has twice the maturity value of the other. How do the discounts compare?

- The discount on the larger receivable is half as much.
- The discounts are equal.
- The discount on the larger receivable is twice as much.
- The discount on the larger receivable is four times as much.

## Explanation

The commercial discount is $D=Mdt$. Both receivables have the same rate $d$ and term $t$, so they share the factor $dt$ and the discount is proportional to the maturity value. If the second receivable is worth $2M$, its discount is $2Mdt$, twice as much. The proceeds also double because $V=M(1-dt)$ is proportional to $M$.
