A simple-interest deposit has the same value at the outset and after six months. If the initial principal is positive, what must the rate be?

- $-100\%$
- $6\%$
- Any positive rate.
- $0\%$

## Explanation

The six-month accumulated value is $M=C(1+it)$, with $t=\dfrac{1}{2}$. The equality $M=C$ is equivalent to $Cit=0$. Since $C>0$ and $t>0$, this product vanishes only if $i=0$. A positive rate would give $M>C$. At $i=-100\%$ the factor is $1-\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the accumulated value is half the principal.
