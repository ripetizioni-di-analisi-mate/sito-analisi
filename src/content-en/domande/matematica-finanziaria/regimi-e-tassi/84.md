Two periods have positive lengths $s$ and $t$. At the same simple interest rate $i$, how much does the factor obtained by reinvesting after the first period exceed the direct simple-interest factor?

- $i(s+t)$
- $ist$
- $i^2(s+t)$
- $i^2st$

## Explanation

Reinvestment applies the simple-interest factor $1+is$ and then applies $1+it$ to the resulting balance, so
$$
(1+is)(1+it)=1+i(s+t)+i^2st.
$$
The direct simple-interest factor for the whole term $s+t$ is $1+i(s+t)$. The difference is therefore $i^2st$, the interest earned in the second period on the first period's interest $is$. With $i\neq0$ the difference is positive, so the simple-interest law is not multiplicative.
