A deposit under compound interest has an effective rate of $44\%$ in the first year and $-19\%$ in the second. Which constant effective annual rate gives the same accumulated value after two years?

- $12.5\%$
- $16.64\%$
- $25\%$
- $8\%$

## Explanation

Under compound interest the two-year factor is the product of the two annual factors, so it equals $1.44\cdot0.81=1.1664$. A constant rate $i$ gives the same accumulated value if $(1+i)^2=1.1664$. Since $1.1664=1.08^2$ and the annual factor $1+i$ is positive, we get $1+i=1.08$. The constant effective annual rate is therefore $8\%$. The value $12.5\%$ is the arithmetic mean of the two rates and ignores compounding, while $16.64\%$ is the rate over the whole two-year period. The value $25\%$ adds the two annual rates.
