Simple interest is calculated for $36$ actual days. With the same principal and positive rate, what happens to the interest when the day-count basis changes from $360$ to $365$?

- It stays the same.
- It increases.
- It decreases.
- It becomes negative.

## Explanation

On a $B$-day basis the term in years is $\dfrac{36}{B}$ and the interest is $I=Ci\cdot\dfrac{36}{B}$. The numerator stays at $36$ while the denominator changes from $360$ to $365$, so the term falls from $\dfrac{36}{360}=0.1$ to approximately $0.0986$. Since $C>0$ and $i>0$, interest is proportional to the term and therefore decreases. All factors remain positive, so the interest is still positive.
