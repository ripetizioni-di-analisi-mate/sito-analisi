Two deposits use the same annual rate of $10\%$. One earns simple interest, the other compound interest with exponential compounding for fractional periods. Which has the greater six-month factor?

- The factors are equal.
- It depends on the initial principal.
- The simple-interest factor.
- The compound-interest factor.

## Explanation

With $t=\dfrac{1}{2}$ the simple-interest factor is $1+0.10\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}=1.05$ and the compound-interest factor is $\sqrt{1.10}$. Both are positive, so their squares can be compared. Since $1.05^2=1.1025>1.10$, we have $1.05>\sqrt{1.10}\approx1.0488$. The simple-interest factor is therefore larger, as it is for every term strictly between zero and one period. The initial principal multiplies both factors and does not affect the comparison.
