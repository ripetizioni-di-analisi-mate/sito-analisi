A discount of $120\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is deducted from a receivable with a one-year maturity value of $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$. What is the cost as a fraction of the proceeds received today?

- $\dfrac{1}{9}$
- $\dfrac{1}{10}$
- $\dfrac{1}{11}$
- $\dfrac{9}{10}$

## Explanation

The discount is $10\%$ of the maturity value, leaving proceeds of $1200-120=1080\,\mathrm{EUR}$. To express the cost relative to the proceeds, divide by the amount actually received. This gives
$$
\frac{120}{1080}=\frac{1}{9}.
$$
The answer $\dfrac{1}{10}=\dfrac{120}{1200}$ expresses the discount relative to the maturity value. The answer $\dfrac{9}{10}$ is the discount factor $1-dt$, while $\dfrac{1}{11}$ divides $120$ by $1320$.
