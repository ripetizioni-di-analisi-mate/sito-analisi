The effective six-month compound rate is $33.1\%$. What is the equivalent effective four-month rate?

- $15.37\%$
- $10\%$
- $53.56\%$
- $21\%$

## Explanation

Six months is not a multiple of four months, so the two rates are compared over a year, which contains two six-month periods and three four-month periods. Under compound interest the rates are equivalent if they give the same annual factor, that is, if $(1+i_3)^3=1.331^2$. Since $1.331=1.1^3$, the annual factor is $1.1^6$ and therefore $1+i_3=1.1^2=1.21$. The equivalent effective four-month rate is thus $21\%$. The value $15.37\%$ is the quarterly rate, obtained from the square root of the six-month factor, while $10\%$ takes the cube root of the six-month factor as if six months contained three four-month periods. The value $53.56\%$ raises the six-month factor to the power $\dfrac{3}{2}$ instead of $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
