At an annual commercial discount rate of $20\%$, for which terms does a positive receivable have a strictly positive present value?

- $0\leq t<5$ years.
- $0\leq t<0.2$ years.
- $0\leq t\leq5$ years.
- $t>5$ years.

## Explanation

Under commercial discount the present value is $V=M(1-dt)$, with $M>0$. Thus $V>0$ exactly when $1-0.20t>0$. Solving gives $t<\dfrac{1}{0.20}=5$ years and, with $t\geq0$, the interval is $0\leq t<5$. The endpoint $t=5$ is excluded because the factor and the proceeds are zero there. The answer $0.2$ is the rate itself rather than the limiting term.
