Two one-year deposits offer a nominal annual rate of $12\%$ compounded monthly and an effective annual rate of $12.5\%$, respectively. For the same principal, which produces the higher balance after one year?

- The offer at an effective annual rate of $12.5\%$.
- The balances are equal.
- The offer at a nominal annual rate of $12\%$ compounded monthly.
- It depends on the principal invested.

## Explanation

A nominal annual rate of $12\%$ compounded monthly gives an effective monthly rate of $1\%$, so the annual factor is
$$
1.01^{12}\approx1.126825.
$$
The nominal-rate offer therefore has an effective annual rate of approximately $12.68\%$, above $12.5\%$. Comparing accumulation factors gives the same conclusion for every positive principal. Directly comparing $12\%$ and $12.5\%$ treats the nominal rate as an effective rate and reverses the result.
