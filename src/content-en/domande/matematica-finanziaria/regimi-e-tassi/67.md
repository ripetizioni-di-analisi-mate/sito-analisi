A principal of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ earns a nominal annual rate of $20\%$ compounded semiannually. How much interest does it earn in the first six months?

- $210\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

With semiannual compounding the effective six-month rate is $i_2=\dfrac{j^{(2)}}{2}=\dfrac{20\%}{2}=10\%$. In the first six months this rate applies to the initial principal, giving interest of $1000\cdot0.10=100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The answer $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ applies the entire nominal annual rate for just six months. The answer $210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the interest for the whole year, $1000\cdot\left(1.1^2-1\right)$, while $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ divides the nominal rate by four.
