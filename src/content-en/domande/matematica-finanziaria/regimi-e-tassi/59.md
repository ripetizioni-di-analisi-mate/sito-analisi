When are two rates quoted for different periods equivalent under compound interest?

- The two rates differ by a whole number of percentage points.
- They have the same numerical value as percentages.
- Rate times term is the same for every term.
- They give the same accumulation factor over a common term.

## Explanation

Rates quoted for different periods can have different numerical values and still be equivalent. By definition, applying them to the same principal for their respective periods must give the same final balance on a common date, or equivalently the same factor over that term. For example, $5\%$ every six months and $10.25\%$ annually are equivalent because $1.05^2=1.1025$. Equality of rate times term is the criterion under simple interest.
