Which simple-interest law $r(t)=1+it$, positive for all $t\geq0$, is multiplicative for every term?

- The one with $i=1$.
- The one with $i=10\%$.
- The one with any $i>0$.
- The one with $i=0$.

## Explanation

The product of the simple-interest factors is $(1+is)(1+it)=1+i(s+t)+i^2st$. Multiplicativity requires it to equal $1+i(s+t)$ for all $s,t\geq0$, which means $i^2st=0$ for every pair of terms. Setting $s=t=1$ gives $i^2=0$, so $i=0$. With $i=0$ the factor equals $1$ for every term and the law is multiplicative.
