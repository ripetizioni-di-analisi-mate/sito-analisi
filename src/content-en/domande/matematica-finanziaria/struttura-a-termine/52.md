If $v(0,T)=e^{-0.04T}$, how do the continuously compounded spot rates and average forward rates compare?

- The forward rates are twice the spot rates.
- The spot rates rise with maturity and the forward rates stay constant.
- The forward rates are zero because the rate is constant.
- They all equal $4\%$.

## Explanation

The logarithm of the discount factor is $\log v(0,T)=-0.04T$, so the continuously compounded spot rate is
$$
z(T)=-\frac{\log v(0,T)}{T}=0.04
$$
at every maturity. Between dates $s<t$ the average continuously compounded forward rate is $\dfrac{-0.04s+0.04t}{t-s}=0.04$. The spot and forward rates therefore all equal $4\%$. A log discount factor linear in maturity corresponds to a constant force of interest.
