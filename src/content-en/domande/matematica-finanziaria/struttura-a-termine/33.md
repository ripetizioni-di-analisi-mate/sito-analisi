If the one- and two-year spot rates are equal and positive, how do the discount factors compare?

- The two factors are equal.
- The two-year factor is twice the one-year factor.
- The one-year factor is smaller than the two-year factor.
- The two-year factor is smaller than the one-year factor.

## Explanation

At the same spot rate $s>0$, $v(0,1)=(1+s)^{-1}$ and $v(0,2)=(1+s)^{-2}=v(0,1)^2$ hold. Since $0<v(0,1)<1$, its square is smaller than the number itself, so the two-year factor is smaller. For example, at $s=5\%$ we obtain $v(0,1)\approx0.9524$ and $v(0,2)\approx0.9070$. Discounting a later euro over more years gives it a lower present value.
