Why does bootstrapping often start with the shortest-maturity bond, which has a single payment?

- Its price always equals its redemption amount.
- Its pricing equation has just one unknown and determines the first factor.
- Longer bonds always depend on only one factor.
- The first factor makes all subsequent prices unnecessary.

## Explanation

A zero-coupon bond maturing at $t_1$ depends on one discount factor because $P=C_1\,v(0,t_1)$. This immediately gives $v(0,t_1)=\dfrac{P}{C_1}$. The next bond pays at $t_1$ and $t_2$ and thus introduces another unknown. Subtracting the known contribution for $t_1$ leaves only one unknown. The procedure then repeats at later maturities.
