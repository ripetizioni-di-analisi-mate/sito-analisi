Two instruments have identical redemption payments. The first can be bought for $90\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the second sold short for $92\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Each order costs $0.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$. How much cash is left today after these trades?

- $2\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1.5\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $3\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Because the redemption payments match, net cash flow at maturity is zero. Today the short sale brings in $92\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the purchase costs $90$. Two orders incur fees of $2\cdot0.50=1\,\mathrm{EUR}$, leaving
$$
92-90-1=1\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $2\,\mathrm{EUR}$ ignores commissions, while $1.5\,\mathrm{EUR}$ counts only one fee.
