Bond $A$ pays $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one. Bond $B$ pays $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one and $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two. How many units of $A$ must be added to one unit of $B$ to receive $35\,\mathrm{EUR}$ on the first date?

- $0.5$
- $0.7$
- $0.2$
- $-0.5$

## Explanation

One unit of $B$ pays $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one. Reaching $35\,\mathrm{EUR}$ requires another $25$. Each unit of $A$ supplies $50$, so
$$
x_A=\frac{35-10}{50}=0.5 .
$$
The value $0.7=\dfrac{35}{50}$ ignores the year-one payment from $B$. The value $-0.5$ has the opposite sign. The year-two payment of $B$ does not enter the first-date condition.
