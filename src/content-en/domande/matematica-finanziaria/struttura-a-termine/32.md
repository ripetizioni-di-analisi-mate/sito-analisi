Two zero-coupon bonds pay different amounts with certainty on the same date and are priced from the same curve. What spot rates do they imply?

- Different rates if their face values differ.
- Equal rates only if they trade at par.
- The same rate.
- The same rate only if the prices are identical.

## Explanation

For a zero-coupon bond the discount factor is the price divided by the redemption amount. Both prices follow the same curve and thus give the same factor $v(0,T)$ at the common maturity. The spot rate depends only on that factor and maturity because $s_T=v(0,T)^{-\frac{1}{T}}-1$. The bonds therefore imply **the same rate** even though their face values and prices differ.
