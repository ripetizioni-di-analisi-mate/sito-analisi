One- and two-year unit zero-coupon bonds cost $0.97$ and $0.9\,\mathrm{EUR}$. What is the cost of a portfolio paying $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one and $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two?

- $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $145.5\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $142\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $135\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Each unit bond pays one euro at maturity. The holdings are therefore $100$ one-year unit bonds and $50$ two-year unit bonds. The cost is
$$
100\cdot0.97+50\cdot0.9=97+45=142\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the nominal sum. The value $145.5\,\mathrm{EUR}$ applies the one-year factor to both payments, while $135\,\mathrm{EUR}$ applies the two-year factor to both.
