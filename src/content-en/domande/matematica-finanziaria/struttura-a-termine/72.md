Two bonds pay the same positive amount, one entirely in year one and the other entirely in year three. The positive discount factors are strictly decreasing. Which bond is worth more today?

- The second.
- They have equal value.
- The first.
- The comparison depends on face value even though it is the same for both.

## Explanation

Both pay $N>0$, so their values are $N\,v(0,1)$ and $N\,v(0,3)$. Since the factors are strictly decreasing, $v(0,1)>v(0,3)$. Multiplication by $N>0$ preserves the inequality, so **the first bond** is worth more. Only the factors affect the comparison because the nominal amounts are equal.
