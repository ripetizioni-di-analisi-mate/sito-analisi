For a positive differentiable discount curve, which expression gives the instantaneous forward rate at time $T$?

- The derivative of $v(0,T)$.
- The logarithm of $v(0,T)$.
- The derivative of $\log v(0,T)$.
- The derivative of $-\log v(0,T)$.

## Explanation

The average continuously compounded forward rate between $T$ and $T+h$ is
$$
\frac{-\log v(0,T+h)+\log v(0,T)}{h},
$$
the difference quotient of $-\log v(0,T)$. As $h$ tends to zero, the quotient tends to the derivative. The instantaneous forward rate is therefore $g(T)=-\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dT}\log v(0,T)$. Differentiating $\log v(0,T)$ without the minus sign gives the negative of the forward rate.
