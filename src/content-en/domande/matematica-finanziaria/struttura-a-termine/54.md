The instantaneous forward rate is strictly positive throughout an interval. How does the discount factor vary on that interval?

- It decreases.
- It increases.
- It remains constant.
- It must exceed one.

## Explanation

The instantaneous forward rate is $g(T)=-\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dT}\log v(0,T)$. If $g>0$ throughout the interval, the derivative of $\log v(0,T)$ is negative, so $\log v(0,T)$ decreases. Since $v(0,T)=e^{\log v(0,T)}$ and the exponential function is increasing, the discount factor also **decreases** over that interval. A euro payable later therefore costs less today.
