A strategy costs zero today, requires a payment in year one, and generates a larger receipt in year two. Without further trades, does it meet the chapter's definition of arbitrage?

- Yes, because the sum of the future cash flows is positive.
- Yes, because the initial cost is zero.
- Yes, provided both payments are certain.
- No, because the year-one cash flow is negative.

## Explanation

Arbitrage requires nonpositive initial cost, nonnegative future cash flows at *every* date, and at least one strictly positive gain. This strategy costs zero but requires a payment in year one, giving a negative cash flow. It fails the second condition and **is not an arbitrage**. A positive sum of future cash flows does not resolve the need to fund that intermediate payment.
