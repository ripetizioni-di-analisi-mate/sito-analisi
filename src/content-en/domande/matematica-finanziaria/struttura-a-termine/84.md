A two-year bond pays $10$ and $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and costs $108.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$. A one-year bond pays $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and costs $96\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Fractional and negative holdings are allowed. What does it cost to isolate the payment of $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two?

- $99\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $108.6\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $118.2\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $96\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

To isolate the final amount $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$, buy one two-year bond and offset its year-one coupon of $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The one-year bond pays $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so selling $0.1$ units short brings in $0.1\cdot96=9.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. The net cost is therefore
$$
108.60-9.60=99\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $118.2\,\mathrm{EUR}$ buys one tenth of the one-year bond instead of selling it, while $108.6\,\mathrm{EUR}$ leaves the coupon in place.
