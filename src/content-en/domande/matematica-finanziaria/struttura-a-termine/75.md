Instrument $X$ pays $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and costs $95\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Instrument $Y$ pays $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ on the same date and costs $192\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Without transaction costs, short one unit of $Y$ and buy enough units of $X$ to cover the redemption obligation. What initial receipt results?

- $2\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $97\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $-2\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Instrument $Y$ pays $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at maturity, so two units of $X$ are needed to cover the short position. With two units of $X$ the net future cash flow is zero. Today's receipt is
$$
192-2\cdot95=2\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $97\,\mathrm{EUR}$ buys only one unit of $X$, leaving $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ uncovered at maturity. The value $1\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the price difference per $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of redemption payment.
