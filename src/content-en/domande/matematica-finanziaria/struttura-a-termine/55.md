A zero-coupon bond paying $100$ euros in one year has a price between $94$ and $96$ euros. A bond paying $10$ euros in one year and $100$ euros in two years costs $99$ euros. In which interval does the discount factor $v(0,2)$ lie?

- $[0.94,\,0.96]$
- $[0.894,\,0.896]$
- $[0.89,\,0.99]$
- $[0.813,\,0.815]$

## Explanation

The price of the zero-coupon bond is $100\,v(0,1)$, so the one-year factor lies between $0.94$ and $0.96$. The bond price satisfies $10\,v(0,1)+100\,v(0,2)=99$, hence
$$
v(0,2)=\frac{99-10\,v(0,1)}{100}.
$$
This expression decreases as $v(0,1)$ increases, so $v(0,1)=0.96$ gives the lower endpoint $0.894$ and $v(0,1)=0.94$ gives the upper endpoint $0.896$. The factor $v(0,2)$ therefore lies in the interval $[0.894,\,0.896]$. The interval $[0.89,\,0.99]$ lets $v(0,1)$ range from $0$ to $1$ instead of within the observed bounds, while $[0.813,\,0.815]$ divides by $110$ as if coupon and principal were both due in year two.
