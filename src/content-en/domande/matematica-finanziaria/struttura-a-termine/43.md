Today's forward rate for the second year is $4\%$. What can be concluded about the spot rate observed at the start of that year?

- It must be exactly $4\%$.
- It may differ from today's forward rate.
- It must exceed $4\%$.
- It must be twice the forward rate.

## Explanation

Today's discount factors give the forward rate, $1+f_{1,2}=\dfrac{v(0,1)}{v(0,2)}$. The one-year spot rate at the start of year two depends on prices observed then. If prices change in the meantime, that spot rate **can differ** from $4\%$. The forward rate is the rate that can be locked in today for that year using zero-coupon bonds.
