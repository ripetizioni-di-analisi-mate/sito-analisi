A bond pays $20\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one and $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two. Its price is $109.40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $v(0,2)=0.9$ is known. What one-year discount factor does the price imply?

- $0.9$
- $0.194$
- $0.97$
- $1.094$

## Explanation

The price is $20\,v(0,1)+100\,v(0,2)$. The second payment is worth $100\cdot0.9=90\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today, leaving $109.40-90=19.40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ as the contribution from year one. Hence
$$
v(0,1)=\frac{19.40}{20}=0.97 .
$$
The value $1.094$ divides the whole price by the face value of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The value $0.194$ divides the residual by $100$ rather than by the year-one payment.
