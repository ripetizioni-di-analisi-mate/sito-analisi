An investment costs $125\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and pays $50$ and $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in years $1$ and $2$. Calculate its net present value using discount factors $0.98$ and $0.9$.

- $25\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $14\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $18\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $139\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

The initial outlay occurs on the valuation date, when the discount factor is one, so it enters with a negative sign and no discounting. The two receipts are worth $50\cdot0.98=49\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $100\cdot0.9=90\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. Hence
$$
-125+49+90=14\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $139\,\mathrm{EUR}$ omits the initial outlay, while $25\,\mathrm{EUR}$ adds the nominal cash flows. The value $18\,\mathrm{EUR}$ interchanges the discount factors for the two dates.
