A one-year zero-coupon bond pays $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and costs $95\,\mathrm{EUR}$. A bond paying $10$ and $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in years one and two costs $108\,\mathrm{EUR}$. How much does it cost to replicate cash flows of $(60,110)$ euros on those dates?

- $108\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $155.5\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $165\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $203\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Only the two-year bond pays $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two, so one unit is needed. It also pays $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one. The zero-coupon bond must cover the remaining $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$, requiring $0.5$ units. The cost is
$$
108+0.5\cdot95=155.50\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The value $165\,\mathrm{EUR}$ ignores the two-year bond's coupon and uses $0.6$ units of the zero-coupon bond. The value $203\,\mathrm{EUR}$ uses a whole unit of the zero-coupon bond.
