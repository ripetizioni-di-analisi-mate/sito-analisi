The two-year discount factor exceeds the one-year factor. Cash can be held at no cost from year one to year two, and both unit zero-coupon bonds can be bought or sold short without costs. Which trade exploits the difference?

- Buy the two-year bond and sell the one-year bond.
- Buy both bonds and hold both redemption payments.
- Buy the one-year bond, hold its proceeds, and short the two-year bond.
- Sell both bonds without covering the amounts due at maturity.

## Explanation

Since $v(0,2)>v(0,1)$, selling the two-year bond short raises more today than the cost of the one-year bond. The one-year bond pays one euro at year one, which can be held at no cost until year two. That euro covers the short position's obligation. The initial receipt is $v(0,2)-v(0,1)>0$ and net future cash flows are zero, so the trade is an arbitrage.
