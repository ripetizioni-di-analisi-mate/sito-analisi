For a bond paying coupons and principal on different dates, what does yield to maturity represent?

- The spot rate at final maturity for any curve.
- The single rate that reproduces the bond price.
- The arithmetic mean of the spot rates on the curve.
- The annual coupon divided by face value.

## Explanation

Yield to maturity is the rate $i$ that equates the price to the sum of the cash flows discounted at that one rate, $P=\sum_k C_k(1+i)^{-t_k}$. Spot rates are specific to their respective maturities. Yield to maturity therefore depends on the coupons and principal payment and generally differs from both the final spot rate and the average spot rate. The annual coupon divided by face value is the coupon rate.
