A bond pays $20\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in years one and two and can be sold short for $111\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Tradable discount factors are $0.95$ and $0.9$. Without costs, how much does selling the bond short and buying its replicating cash flows bring in immediately?

- $-2\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $1\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $2\,\mathrm{EUR}$
- $109\,\mathrm{EUR}$

## Explanation

Replication requires $20$ one-year unit zero-coupon bonds and $100$ two-year unit bonds, costing
$$
20\cdot0.95+100\cdot0.9=19+90=109\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$
The short sale brings in $111\,\mathrm{EUR}$, and the replicating payments cover the obligations, canceling all future cash flows. The immediate receipt is $111-109=2\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The value $109\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the replication cost, and $-2\,\mathrm{EUR}$ has the opposite sign.
