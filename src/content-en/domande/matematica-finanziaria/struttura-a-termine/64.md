A portfolio receives $100$ euros in one year and pays $100$ euros in two years. Both discount factors $v(0,1)$ and $v(0,2)$ increase by the same amount $\varepsilon$ and remain positive. How does the net present value of the portfolio change?

- It rises by $200\varepsilon$.
- It rises by $100\varepsilon$.
- It falls by $100\varepsilon$.
- It is unchanged.

## Explanation

Before the change the net present value of the portfolio is $100\,v(0,1)-100\,v(0,2)$. After the change it becomes
$$
100\left(v(0,1)+\varepsilon\right)-100\left(v(0,2)+\varepsilon\right)=100\,v(0,1)-100\,v(0,2),
$$
because the receipt and the payment have the same amount and the two terms $100\varepsilon$ appear with opposite signs. Therefore the net present value is unchanged. The answer $200\varepsilon$ adds the two effects without taking into account that the second cash flow is a payment.
