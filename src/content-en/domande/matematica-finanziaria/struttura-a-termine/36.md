The one-year discount factor is $0.96$ and the effective annual forward rate between years one and two lies between $0\%$ and $20\%$, inclusive. Between which prices can a zero-coupon bond paying $100$ euros at time $2$ lie?

- From $96$ to $115.20$ euros.
- From $76.80$ to $96$ euros.
- From $83.33$ to $100$ euros.
- From $80$ to $96$ euros.

## Explanation

The two-year factor is obtained from the one-year factor and the forward rate, $v(0,2)=\dfrac{v(0,1)}{1+f_{1,2}}$. The price of the zero-coupon bond is therefore $P=\dfrac{100\cdot0.96}{1+f_{1,2}}=\dfrac{96}{1+f_{1,2}}$, which decreases as the forward rate increases. With $f_{1,2}=0\%$ we get the maximum price of $96$ euros, and with $f_{1,2}=20\%$ the minimum price $\dfrac{96}{1.2}=80$ euros. The price can therefore lie from $80$ to $96$ euros. The value of $76.80$ euros multiplies by $0.8$ instead of dividing by $1.2$, while $115.20$ euros accumulates instead of discounting.
