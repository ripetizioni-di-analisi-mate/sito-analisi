Two planets receive the same solar energy per unit area. Is that information sufficient to conclude that their average temperatures are equal?

- Yes, because incident radiation is always absorbed entirely
- Yes, provided the two planets have the same radius
- No, because higher albedo always means greater absorption
- No: albedo and atmospheric properties also matter
- Yes, provided they complete a rotation in the same time

## Explanation

Incoming energy need not equal absorbed energy: some is reflected. The atmosphere also changes radiative exchanges, especially in the infrared. Equal incident energy alone does not determine equal equilibrium temperature.
