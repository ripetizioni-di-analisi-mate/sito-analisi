The illuminated part of the Moon is visible even when the Sun is below the observer’s horizon. Where does visible moonlight mainly come from?

- Nuclear fusion in the lunar core
- Sunlight reflected by the lunar surface
- Mainly the Moon’s thermal emission in the visible band
- Mainly reflected Earthlight even in the directly sunlit portion
- Atmospheric processes in a dense lunar atmosphere

## Explanation

The Sun can illuminate the Moon even when it is not directly visible to the observer on Earth. The lunar surface reflects some of that light towards us. The Moon does not produce its own visible light through nuclear fusion like a star.
