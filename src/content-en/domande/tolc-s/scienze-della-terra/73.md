Why does a full moon not produce a lunar eclipse every month?

- Earth stops casting a shadow during most months
- A full moon always lies between the Sun and Earth
- The lunar orbital plane is tilted and the Moon often passes outside Earth’s shadow
- Sunlight passes completely through Earth
- The lunar orbit has no geometric relation to Earth

## Explanation

Full moon places the Moon on the opposite side of Earth from the Sun, but does not ensure exact alignment. Orbital inclination often takes it above or below the shadow. An eclipse also requires a suitable position near an orbital node.
