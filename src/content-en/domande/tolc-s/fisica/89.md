A gas follows the quasistatic cycle $A\to B\to C\to D\to A$ shown. The $(V,p)$ coordinates are $A=(1\,\mathrm{L},100\,\mathrm{kPa})$, $B=(3\,\mathrm{L},100\,\mathrm{kPa})$, $C=(3\,\mathrm{L},200\,\mathrm{kPa})$, and $D=(1\,\mathrm{L},200\,\mathrm{kPa})$. What is the net work done by the gas?

::grafico{linee="1,100 3,100 3,200 1,200 1,100" vettori="1.4,100,2.4,100;3,130,3,180;2.4,200,1.4,200;1,180,1,130" punti="1,100,pieno,A;3,100,pieno,B;3,200,pieno,C;1,200,pieno,D" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=250 nomi="V,p" titolo="Volume V in liters and pressure p in kPa; arrows show the counterclockwise cycle direction"}

- $+200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $+600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $0\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

On the vertical segments volume does not change, so work is zero. During expansion, $W_{AB}=100\cdot2=200\,\mathrm{J}$; during compression, $W_{CD}=200\cdot(-2)=-400\,\mathrm{J}$, using $1\,\mathrm{kPa}\cdot1\,\mathrm{L}=1\,\mathrm{J}$. Total work is $200-400=-200\,\mathrm{J}$. Counterclockwise traversal means net work is done on the gas: returning to the initial state makes $\Delta U$ zero, but not necessarily work.
