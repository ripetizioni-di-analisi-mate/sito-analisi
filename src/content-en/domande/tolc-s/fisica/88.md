An ideal gas expands isothermally and does $100\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. How much heat does it absorb?

- $0\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-100\,\mathrm{J}$
- $100\,\mathrm{J}$
- $200\,\mathrm{J}$
- $50\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

For an ideal gas, internal energy depends only on temperature. In an isothermal process $\Delta U=0$, so the first law $\Delta U=Q-W$ requires $Q=W=100\,\mathrm{J}$. The gas absorbs heat while doing work even though temperature stays constant.
