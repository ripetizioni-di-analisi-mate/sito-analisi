A population remains near 500 individuals for years. After a lasting increase in resources it reaches a new equilibrium near 800. Which interpretation fits the carrying-capacity model?

- Carrying capacity always equals chromosome number
- The first equilibrium proved that births and deaths were absent
- Carrying capacity depends on environmental conditions
- Each species has a universal unchanging carrying capacity
- The increase necessarily proves a new species

## Explanation

Carrying capacity represents population size sustainable by the resources and conditions of a context. A lasting increase in resources can raise the level around which the population stabilizes. Numerical equilibrium permits compensating births and deaths; it does not imply no turnover. Demographic change alone does not demonstrate speciation.
