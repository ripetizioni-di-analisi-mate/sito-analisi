If intestinal villi flatten while intestinal length remains unchanged, which structural consequence reduces absorption?

- The number of alveoli necessarily increases
- The available contact surface decreases
- Every pancreatic enzyme disappears
- Blood stops containing water
- The intestine becomes nervous tissue

## Explanation

Villi are projections of the intestinal mucosa covered by epithelium that increase surface area compared with a smooth tube of the same length. Flattening reduces the area available to transfer nutrients from the lumen into the organism. Total length alone therefore does not describe exchange capacity. The observation does not imply the loss of all digestive enzymes: digestion and absorption are distinct processes.
