With complete dominance of $A$, a dominant-phenotype individual is crossed with $aa$. At least one $aa$ offspring is born. Excluding mutations and errors, what is the dominant parent’s genotype?

- $Aa$
- $AA$
- $aa$
- It has no alleles at the locus
- It must be haploid

## Explanation

The $aa$ offspring received one $a$ allele from each parent. The dominant parent must therefore carry $a$, but also carry $A$ to show the dominant phenotype: its genotype is $Aa$. An $AA$ parent would always transmit $A$ and, with $aa$, produce only $Aa$. The recessive offspring is enough to exclude this under the stated assumptions.
