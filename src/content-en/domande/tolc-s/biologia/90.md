A eukaryotic gene contains exons and introns. Both occur in its initial transcript, whereas the mature mRNA lacks introns and has joined exons. Which process explains the difference?

- Semiconservative DNA replication
- Translation of introns into a new chromosome
- Removal of introns from the DNA of every cell
- Crossing-over between homologous chromosomes
- Splicing of the RNA transcript

## Explanation

Splicing removes introns from the transcript and joins the exons retained in mature RNA. The comparison concerns two stages of RNA processing, not loss of sequences from DNA. Translation subsequently uses mRNA to build a polypeptide; replication and crossing-over instead act on genetic material with different functions.
