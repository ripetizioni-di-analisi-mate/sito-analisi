In a tree, A and B share a more recent common ancestor than either shares with C. Which conclusion follows?

- A necessarily descends from the current species B
- C must be A’s living ancestor
- A and B are more closely related to each other
- A and B must look identical
- C is a less evolved species

## Explanation

Phylogenetic relatedness is assessed by the recency of the common ancestor. A and B share more recent evolutionary history and are therefore closer to each other than to C. A common ancestor does not imply that one current species produced the other. The tree describes historical branches, not a ranking of progress or a requirement for identical appearance.
