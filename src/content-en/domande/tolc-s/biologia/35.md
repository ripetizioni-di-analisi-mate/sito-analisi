A bacterial cell replicates its chromosome and separates into two daughter cells. Why is this process not described as nuclear mitosis?

- The bacterium contains no genetic material
- Bacterial division never requires replication
- Both daughter cells must be gametes
- Every bacterial division includes two meioses
- The bacterium has no membrane-bounded nucleus and divides by binary fission

## Explanation

Bacteria have prokaryotic organization and lack an envelope-bounded nucleus. Binary fission includes replication and segregation of genetic material but not eukaryotic nuclear mitosis. Absence of mitosis does not mean absence of DNA or inability to transmit it. Gamete terminology must not automatically be applied to this form of cell reproduction.
