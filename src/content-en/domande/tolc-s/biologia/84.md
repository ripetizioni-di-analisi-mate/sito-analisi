Complete hydrolysis of a triglyceride breaks the bonds joining its backbone to three acid chains. Which main products result?

- Glucose and three nucleotides
- Ribose and three amino acids
- One nitrogenous base and three phosphates
- Glycerol and three fatty acids
- Cholesterol and three glucose molecules

## Explanation

A triglyceride consists of glycerol esterified with three fatty acids. Hydrolysis of the three ester bonds therefore releases glycerol and fatty acids. It does not produce protein or nucleic-acid monomers because the starting molecule is a lipid with a different structure. The word triglyceride indicates the connection of three chains to glycerol.
