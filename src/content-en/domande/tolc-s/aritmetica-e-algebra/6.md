To turn $\sqrt{x+1}=x-1$ into an equivalent equation, you want to square both sides while retaining a single condition covering the domain and sign. Which condition is necessary on the right-hand side and also sufficient for this step?

- $x\ge1$
- $x\le1$
- $x\ne1$
- $x\ge-1$
- $x>1$

## Explanation

The principal square root is nonnegative, so the right-hand side must satisfy $x-1\ge0$. This already ensures $x+1\ge0$. On $[1,+\infty)$ both sides are nonnegative, making squaring an equivalent transformation.
