Let $u$, $v$, $c$ be real numbers with $0<u<v$ and $c>0$. Where does the fraction $\dfrac{u+c}{v+c}$ lie?

- It equals $\dfrac{u}{v}$
- It is strictly greater than $1$
- It is strictly less than $\dfrac{u}{v}$
- It lies strictly between $\dfrac{u}{v}$ and $1$
- It equals $1$

## Explanation

Since $u<v$, also $u+c<v+c$; the denominator $v+c$ is positive, so $\dfrac{u+c}{v+c}<1$. Moreover
$$
\frac{u+c}{v+c}-\frac{u}{v}=\frac{v(u+c)-u(v+c)}{v(v+c)}=\frac{c(v-u)}{v(v+c)} .
$$
The numerator and the denominator of this fraction are positive, therefore the difference is positive and $\dfrac{u+c}{v+c}>\dfrac{u}{v}$. Hence the fraction $\dfrac{u+c}{v+c}$ lies strictly between $\dfrac{u}{v}$ and $1$.
