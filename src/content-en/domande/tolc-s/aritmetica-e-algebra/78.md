What is the form of $\dfrac6{\sqrt3}$ with a rational denominator?

- $\sqrt6$
- $3\sqrt3$
- $2\sqrt3$
- $6\sqrt3$
- $\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{3}$

## Explanation

Multiply numerator and denominator by the nonzero number $\sqrt3$. This gives $\dfrac{6\sqrt3}{3}$ because $\sqrt3\sqrt3=3$. Simplifying the numerical factor gives $2\sqrt3$. The result is equivalent to the original expression, but its denominator no longer contains a radical.
