A formula contains $\sqrt{u+v}$ with $u,v\ge0$. When can it be replaced by $\sqrt u+\sqrt v$ without changing its value?

- Whenever $u+v=1$
- Only when $u=v=1$
- When at least one of $u,v$ is zero
- For every pair of perfect squares
- Whenever $u=v$

## Explanation

Both sides are nonnegative. Comparing their squares gives $u+v=u+v+2\sqrt{uv}$. Equality holds precisely when $uv=0$.
