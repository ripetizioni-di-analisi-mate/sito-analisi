How is $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}\to Cu}$ completed by adding electrons only?

- $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}+2e^-\to Cu}$
- $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}\to Cu+2e^-}$
- $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}+e^-\to Cu}$
- $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}\to Cu+e^-}$
- $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}+2e^+\to Cu}$

## Explanation

The ion starts with charge $+2$ and becomes a neutral atom. It must therefore gain two electrons, each with charge $-1$, so the sum on the left is $+2-2=0$. Electrons belong among the reactants and the process is reduction. Placing them on the right would give different total charges on the two sides.
