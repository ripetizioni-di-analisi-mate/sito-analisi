At a given temperature, a salt's solubility is $20\,\mathrm g$ per $100\,\mathrm g$ of water. A $28\,\mathrm g$ amount of salt is added to $100\,\mathrm g$ of water. How much solid remains at equilibrium?

- $8\,\mathrm g$
- $20\,\mathrm g$
- $28\,\mathrm g$
- $0\,\mathrm g$
- $48\,\mathrm g$

## Explanation

The water amount matches the reference amount, so at most twenty grams of salt can dissolve. The remaining $28-20=8\,\mathrm g$ stay solid. The solution contains $120\,\mathrm g$ of water and dissolved salt, while the whole system weighs $128\,\mathrm g$. The solid at the bottom must be distinguished from the liquid phase.
