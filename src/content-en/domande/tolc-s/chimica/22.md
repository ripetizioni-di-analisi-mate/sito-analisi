For $\mathrm{N_2+3H_2\to2NH_3}$, $2\,\mathrm{mol}$ of nitrogen and $3\,\mathrm{mol}$ of hydrogen are introduced. Which reactant limits the maximum amount of ammonia?

- Nitrogen, because it initially has fewer moles
- Hydrogen, because its initial amount divided by its coefficient is smaller
- Both, because there are two reactants
- Nitrogen, because its molar mass is greater
- Neither, because all reactants are gases

## Explanation

Compare the amounts divided by their coefficients: $\dfrac21=2$ for nitrogen and $\dfrac33=1$ for hydrogen. The smaller ratio belongs to hydrogen, which is consumed first. One mole of nitrogen reacts with the three moles of hydrogen; the other mole of nitrogen remains. Comparing initial amounts alone would be insufficient.
