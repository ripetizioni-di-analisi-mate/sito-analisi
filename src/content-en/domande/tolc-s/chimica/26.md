A saturated solution is in contact with crystals of a nonvolatile solute. A small amount of water evaporates at constant temperature and equilibrium is restored. What happens to the liquid-phase concentration?

- It remains at saturation while more solute crystallizes
- It necessarily doubles
- It becomes zero
- It decreases because the solid absorbs all solute
- It increases without limit while liquid remains

## Explanation

At constant temperature, equilibrium concentration in contact with the solid is fixed by solubility. Evaporation reduces the available solvent, so some solute enters the solid phase. The remaining solution returns to its saturation concentration. The solute balance must include both dissolved and crystallized portions.
