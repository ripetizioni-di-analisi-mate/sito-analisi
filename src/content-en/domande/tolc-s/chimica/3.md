How many moles of water molecules are present in $36\,\mathrm g$? Take $M(\mathrm{H_2O})=18\,\mathrm{g/mol}$.

- $18\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $2\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $648\,\mathrm{mol}$
- $36\,\mathrm{mol}$

## Explanation

Molar mass states that each mole of molecules has a mass of $18\,\mathrm g$. Dividing the available mass by the mass of one mole gives $n=\dfrac{36}{18}=2\,\mathrm{mol}$. The result counts water molecules; the total amount of atoms would be six moles because each molecule contains three atoms.
