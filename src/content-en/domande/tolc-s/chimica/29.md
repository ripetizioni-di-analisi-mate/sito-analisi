Two samples contain $16\,\mathrm g$ of $\mathrm{O_2}$ and $14\,\mathrm g$ of $\mathrm{N_2}$. Their molar masses are $32$ and $28\,\mathrm{g/mol}$, respectively. Which comparison is correct?

- Oxygen contains twice as many molecules
- Nitrogen contains twice as many molecules
- Molecular count is proportional to mass alone
- They contain the same number of molecules
- Volumes are needed to compare molecular counts

## Explanation

The amounts are $\dfrac{16}{32}=0.50\,\mathrm{mol}$ and $\dfrac{14}{28}=0.50\,\mathrm{mol}$. Since one mole always contains the same number of specified entities, the molecular counts are equal. The masses differ because an oxygen molecule has greater mass than a nitrogen molecule, not because its sample contains more molecules.
