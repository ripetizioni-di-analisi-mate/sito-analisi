Commenting on the well-being survey, a reader writes that failure to prove a benefit from cycling establishes that cycling is useless. The authors had called the data insufficient to decide about causation.

Which step in the reader’s reasoning is unwarranted?

- Mistaking a voluntary answer for a clinical examination conducted on site
- Assuming the researchers chose transport for the commuters
- Extending a group’s average rating to individual cyclists’ scores
- Treating absence of evidence for a benefit as evidence of no effect
- Distinguishing a bicycle from other means of transport

## Explanation

If the evidence is insufficient to decide, both an effect and its absence remain possible. The reader picks the second possibility without additional evidence.
