An agricultural survey publishes two rows, “land examined” and “land under cultivation”. Its introduction says the first includes cultivated fields and uncultivated meadows visited by surveyors. In one village 12 hectares were examined and 8 of the 12 cultivated.

Why would adding 12 and 8 overstate the area examined?

- All twelve hectares were necessarily visited twice
- The eight hectares represent the portion unvisited by the surveyors
- The eight cultivated hectares are already included in the twelve
- The second row concerns only land in another village
- Meadows are excluded from the first row

## Explanation

“Eight of the twelve” makes the second quantity part of the first. Adding would count the cultivated portion twice.
