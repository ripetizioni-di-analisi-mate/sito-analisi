During a visit to neighborhood fountains, the guide reads a municipal register and then recounts a resident’s memory. The register dates one fountain’s closure to 1978; the resident recalls using it during a summer associated with 1980. The guide presents the discrepancy instead of immediately choosing one date.

The guide explains that the register documents an administrative decision, whereas the memory concerns a personal experience. An approved closure might not coincide with the water actually stopping; the remembered date might also be imprecise. To clarify the sequence, the guide proposes looking for work orders and dated photographs. The account does not put every source on the same footing for every question: it asks what each can establish and what checks are needed before turning a discrepancy into an accusation of falsehood.

Which scenario could reconcile the two accounts without correcting the dates?

- The fountain permanently stopped supplying water in 1978
- The 1978 decision was implemented after the summer of 1980
- The resident used the fountain only before 1978
- The register concerns physical implementation in 1978
- The remembered date was corrected to 1977

## Explanation

The text distinguishes the decision from its implementation. If water remained available after the resolution, both the administrative date and the remembered experience could be correct. This is a compatible scenario rather than an established fact: work orders would help verify it.
