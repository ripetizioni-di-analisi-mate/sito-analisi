In the novel, a ship’s voyage is told by two voices. A sailor describes maneuvers on deck as they occur. Many years later, a passenger remembers the same voyage, experienced as a child. Recalling the stormy night, she remembers a closed door and the sound of water; the sailor instead recounts repairs made outside.

The accounts do not agree in every detail. The commentator observes that this difference does not justify immediately declaring either narrator unreliable: their viewpoints and temporal distances differ. The reader thus reconstructs the event partly through what each voice could not see. The novel leaves the comparison of perspectives to the reader without introducing a third voice to settle every discrepancy definitively.

Which difference between the voices is explicit?

- One narrates as events occur; the other remembers years later
- Both speak from the deck during the storm
- The sailor was a child
- The passenger carried out the exterior repairs
- A third voice corrects every date

## Explanation

The first paragraph distinguishes narration of maneuvers as they happen from the passenger’s later memory. This temporal difference accompanies different observation locations. The other options swap roles or introduce a third voice that the passage excludes.
