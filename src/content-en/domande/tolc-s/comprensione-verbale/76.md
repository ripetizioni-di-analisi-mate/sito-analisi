During a school council meeting, a committee proposes starting lessons ten minutes earlier the following year. The minutes record a discussion about transport and postpone the vote until the next meeting, after an opinion is obtained from the bus operator. Meanwhile, schedules remain unchanged.

The student newspaper publishes the headline “Lessons start ten minutes earlier from Monday.” The editors receive a report and compare the headline with the minutes. A correction clarifies that a proposal was presented, not a change approved, and that the period discussed was the following year. It does not say that the earlier start will certainly be rejected: the outcome depends on a future vote. The editors retain the original article in the archive, adding a visible link to the correction so that both the error and its correction remain identifiable.

What is the change’s status at the end of the described meeting?

- It is a proposal still awaiting a vote
- It is already approved and active
- It has been definitively rejected
- It applies only to buses
- It has become compulsory for every school

## Explanation

The minutes postpone the vote and keep schedules unchanged. The proposal exists but does not yet have a decision’s outcome. Postponement amounts to neither approval nor definitive rejection.
