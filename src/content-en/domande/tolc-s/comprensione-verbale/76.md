In a digital archive “find location” originally meant the nearest village. From 2010 it means the precise position on the ground. The curator retains the old definition with earlier entries and adds a dated note about the change. The data in those entries have not been collected again.

Why is it important to keep the earlier definition visible?

- To prevent a village name in an earlier entry being read as a precise position measured on the ground
- To make the modern definition apply retroactively to every entry
- To turn all recent data into mere village names
- To suggest that all earlier positions were measured again
- To prove that the finds physically moved in 2010

## Explanation

The newer meaning would give earlier data a precision they do not possess. The definition and date allow each entry to be read under the practice used when it was created.
