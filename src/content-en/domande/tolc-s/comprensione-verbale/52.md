In a teaching experiment, two groups of seeds from the same packet are randomly assigned to identical containers. They receive the same quantity of water and remain at the same temperature. Group A is exposed to light for eight hours per day, group B for four. After one week, more sprouts are observed in group A.

The teacher considers the result an indication of an effect of lighting duration under the experiment’s conditions. She does not present it as a rule applying to every species or every quantity of light. She also proposes repeating the experiment with new seeds: replication allows the stability of the observed difference to be checked. A pupil suggests changing both light and water; the teacher explains that this would make any change harder to attribute to just one of those factors.

Which wording respects the stated scope of the result?

- Every species always sprouts more as lighting increases
- Under the experiment’s conditions, longer lighting is associated with more sprouts
- Water never affects germination
- Four hours of light prevent all sprouting
- Temperature alone determines the outcome

## Explanation

The correct wording preserves the restriction to the studied conditions and the observed comparison. The text explicitly rejects generalizing to every species or quantity of light. It does not say B had zero sprouts and does not study variation in water or temperature.
