For one month a ferry trials a new timetable. The report compares the number of crossings made with the number planned in the new timetable. A councillor instead wants to know whether passengers wait less than under the previous timetable.

Why does the report’s comparison not answer the councillor’s question?

- It measures individual times but not the number of crossings
- It concerns only passengers’ self-reported satisfaction
- It already proves that waiting fell in proportion to the planned crossings
- It measures fulfilment of the new timetable, not waiting times in both periods
- It directly compares old and new timetables for every passenger

## Explanation

A crossing can run as planned yet leave a long wait for someone arriving just after it. The question requires comparable waiting times before and after the change.
