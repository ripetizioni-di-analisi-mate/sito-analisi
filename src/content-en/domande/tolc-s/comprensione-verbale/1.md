During the three-month trial of evening opening, the counter at the library entrance recorded more entries. Some of those who enter are people who step out briefly and then return, and the counter does not distinguish one person from another.

Which situation would be enough to explain a rise in entries while the number of visitors stays the same?

- The same people return more often in the evening
- Readers change tables more often while remaining inside
- The number of distinct readers rises while return visits decline
- Many new readers arrive and enter once
- Those already inside stay longer without passing the counter again

## Explanation

Each return is counted as a new entry, while the number of people stays the same. A longer visit alone adds no entries, and the arrival of new readers changes the number of visitors. Therefore repeated returns by the same people are the situation that explains the rise.
