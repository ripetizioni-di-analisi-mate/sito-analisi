Two pamphlets say the greenhouse opened in 1946 and both use the odd spelling “Gardne” for “Garden”. At the end of the second, the editor thanks the first pamphlet, “from which I copied the report”.

When evaluating the opening date, how should the two pamphlets be treated?

- As proof that the greenhouse did not open that year
- As two independent direct observations
- As two written attestations linked to the same source
- As proof that the first copied the second
- As independent dates merely because the pamphlets are separate objects

## Explanation

The shared spelling suggests dependence and the acknowledgement explicitly confirms it. Agreement on the date therefore does not constitute a second independent confirmation.
