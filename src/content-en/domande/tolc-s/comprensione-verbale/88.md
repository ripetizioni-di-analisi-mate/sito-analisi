A bulletin forecasts “possible fog in one of the three valleys” without identifying which. One valley stays clear. A reader claims to have disproved the forecast for the whole area.

Which aspect of the bulletin makes that observation insufficient?

- One clear valley forces the other two to be foggy
- The text guarantees fog in all three valleys
- The text does not assign the possibility of fog specifically to the observed valley
- The text places possible fog specifically in the observed valley at a later time
- The forecast assigns fog to the observed valley only if the other two are clear

## Explanation

The bulletin leaves location open and speaks of a possibility. Clear skies in just one valley are compatible with it even before the conditions in the others are known.
