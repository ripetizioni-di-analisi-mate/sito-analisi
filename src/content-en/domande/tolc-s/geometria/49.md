Two solid hemispheres of radius $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ are joined at their bases. What is the resulting solid’s volume?

- $18\pi\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $12\pi\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $72\pi\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $36\pi\,\mathrm{cm^3}$
- $27\pi\,\mathrm{cm^3}$

## Explanation

The two hemispheres, joined without overlapping volumes, form a sphere of radius $3$. Its volume is therefore $V=\dfrac43\pi r^3=\dfrac43\pi\cdot27=36\pi\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Their common base surface lies inside the solid and has zero volume; no additional volume term is subtracted.
