A point $P$ lies inside a convex angle and is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ from each of its two boundary lines. What follows?

- P is the angle’s vertex
- P lies on the internal angle bisector
- The angle must measure 60 degrees
- P is 3 cm from the vertex
- The angle must be a right angle

## Explanation

Distances from a point to lines are measured along perpendiculars. Inside an angle, the locus of points equally distant from its sides is the internal angle bisector. Thus $P$ lies on it. The common value $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ determines neither the angle size nor, by itself, the distance from $P$ to the vertex.
