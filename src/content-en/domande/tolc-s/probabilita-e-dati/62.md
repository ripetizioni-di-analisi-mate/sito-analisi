Let $P(A)>0$, $P(B)>0$ and $P(A\mid B)=P(B\mid A)>0$. Which conclusion is necessary?

- A and B are independent
- $P(A)=P(B)$
- A and B are disjoint
- $P(A)+P(B)=1$
- A and B are the same event

## Explanation

The conditional probabilities are $\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$ and $\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(A)}$. Their common numerator is positive, so equal ratios require equal denominators. Hence $P(A)=P(B)$, without requiring independence or identical events.
