A nonempty numerical list has median $m$. One observation is selected uniformly, and $X$ is its value. Which inequality always holds?

- $P(X\le m)<\dfrac12$
- $P(X<m)\ge\dfrac12$
- $P(X>m)\ge\dfrac12$
- $P(X\le m)\ge\dfrac12$
- $P(X=m)=0$

## Explanation

At least half of the sorted positions are no greater than the median, for both even and odd list sizes. The required probability is therefore at least $\dfrac12$. Strict inequalities lack the same guarantee: if all observations equal $m$, none is strictly below or above it.
