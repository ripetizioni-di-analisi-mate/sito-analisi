In the model of a fair six-sided die numbered from $1$ to $6$, let $B$ be the event “the result is $7$.” What happens when applying the formula $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$?

- The result is always zero
- The result is always one
- The result is always $\dfrac16$
- The formula is undefined because the denominator is zero
- The result is negative

## Explanation

Event B is impossible in the stated model, so $P(B)=0$. Its intersection with any event A is also empty, but the ratio would take the form $\dfrac00$, which is undefined. The elementary conditional-probability formula explicitly requires the conditioning event to have positive probability.
