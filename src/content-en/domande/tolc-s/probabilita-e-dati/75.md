An urn contains at least one red ball and at least one blue ball. Two uniform draws are made without replacement. How does the probability that the second is red, given that the first is red, compare with the initial probability of red?

- It always increases
- It always stays the same
- It always becomes zero
- It always becomes one
- It always decreases

## Explanation

Let $r$ be the number of red balls and $b$ the number of blue balls, both positive. Initially the probability is $\dfrac r{r+b}$; after a red it becomes $\dfrac{r-1}{r+b-1}$. The first ratio exceeds the second by $\dfrac{b}{(r+b)(r+b-1)}>0$. The probability decreases and becomes zero only if there was initially one red ball.
