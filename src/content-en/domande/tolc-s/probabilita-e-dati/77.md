A diagnostic test is positive in $90\%$ of the people who have a certain disease. Can this information alone determine the probability that a person with a positive test has the disease?

- Yes, it always equals $90\%$
- No: the prevalence of the disease and the rate of positives among healthy people are needed
- Yes, it always equals $10\%$
- Yes, it always equals $1$
- Yes, it always equals $0$

## Explanation

Denoting by $M$ the event “the person has the disease”, the given fact specifies $P(+\mid M)$, whereas the question asks for $P(M\mid +)$. If the disease has probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the test is never positive among healthy people, then $P(M\mid +)=1$. If instead the disease has probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the test is positive in $90\%$ of healthy people, then $P(M\mid +)=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Both situations satisfy the original fact, so the required probability is not determined.
