A detector gives a positive signal for $90\%$ of samples possessing a characteristic. Can this fact alone determine the probability that a positive sample possesses the characteristic?

- Yes, it always equals $90\%$
- No: information about prevalence and positives without the characteristic is needed
- Yes, it always equals $10\%$
- Yes, it always equals $1$
- Yes, it always equals $0$

## Explanation

The given fact specifies $P(+\mid C)$, whereas the question asks for $P(C\mid +)$. If the characteristic has probability $\dfrac12$, a zero false-positive rate gives $P(C\mid +)=1$, while a $90\%$ false-positive rate gives $\dfrac12$. Both models satisfy the original fact, so there is no unique answer.
