An integer from $1$ to $20$ is selected uniformly. Let $A=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ and $B=\{4,5,6,7,8\}$. What is the probability that exactly one of $A$ and $B$ occurs?

- $\dfrac25$
- $\dfrac{11}{20}$
- $\dfrac14$
- $\dfrac3{20}$
- $\dfrac12$

## Explanation

The outcomes belonging only to A are $1,2,3$; those belonging only to B are $7,8$. The values $4,5,6$ belong to both and must be excluded. There are therefore five favorable outcomes out of twenty, with probability $\dfrac5{20}=\dfrac14$. The ordinary union would include the intersection too.
