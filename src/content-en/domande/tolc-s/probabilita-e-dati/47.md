Two events satisfy $A\subseteq B$, $P(A)>0$, and $P(B)<1$. Which statement is necessarily true?

- A and B are independent
- A and B are not independent
- A and B are disjoint
- $P(A)>P(B)$
- $P(A\cup B)=1$

## Explanation

Inclusion gives $A\cap B=A$, so $P(A\cap B)=P(A)$. Because $P(A)>0$ and $P(B)<1$, the product $P(A)P(B)$ is strictly smaller than $P(A)$. It therefore cannot equal the intersection probability. The assumptions exclude precisely the degenerate cases that could allow independence.
