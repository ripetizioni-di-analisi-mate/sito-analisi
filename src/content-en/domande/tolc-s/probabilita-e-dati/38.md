A website collects opinions on a proposal through a survey that anyone may choose to answer. Which observation is justified?

- A voluntary sample is always representative
- One million responses removes every selection bias
- Respondents may not represent the population to which the result is to be extended
- Nonrespondents certainly have the same opinions as respondents
- Percentages among respondents cannot be calculated

## Explanation

Voluntary participation may depend on interest in the subject or strength of opinion. The observed group may therefore differ in composition from the intended population. Valid percentages can be calculated for respondents, but automatically extending them to everyone is unjustified. Sample size alone does not correct this selection mechanism.
