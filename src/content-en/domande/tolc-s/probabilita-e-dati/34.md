Two fair coins are tossed independently. Only cases with at least one head are retained. In this conditional space, are “first coin heads” and “second coin heads” still independent?

- Yes, because the tosses were independent before selection
- Yes, because the three remaining cases are equally likely
- No, because the events are disjoint
- No: the joint probability is $\dfrac13$, different from $\left(\dfrac23\right)^2$
- The conditional probabilities cannot be calculated

## Explanation

HH, HT and TH remain, each with conditional probability $\dfrac13$. Each heads event has probability $\dfrac23$, but their intersection HH has probability $\dfrac13$, not $\dfrac49$. Selecting cases through a shared event can therefore change the original independence.
