The function $f(x)=|\sin x|$ is defined on $\mathbb R$. What is the smallest number $T>0$ such that $f(x+T)=f(x)$ for every real $x$?

- $2\pi$
- $\dfrac\pi2$
- $\pi$
- $4\pi$
- It does not exist, because $f$ is not periodic

## Explanation

For every real $x$ we have $\sin(x+\pi)=-\sin x$, so $|\sin(x+\pi)|=|\sin x|$ and $\pi$ is a period of $f$. We have $f(0)=0$, and for $0<x<\pi$ the sine is positive, so $f(x)>0$. Therefore no number $T$ with $0<T<\pi$ is a period, because it would give $f(T)=f(0)=0$. The smallest positive period is therefore $\pi$. The value $2\pi$ is the fundamental period of $\sin x$ and is also a period of $f$, but not the smallest one.
