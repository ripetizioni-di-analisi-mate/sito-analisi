On a $3\times3$ board, in how many ways can two indistinguishable rooks occupy different squares without sharing a row or column?

- $36$
- $9$
- $12$
- $72$
- $18$

## Explanation

The first rook can occupy $9$ squares. The second has $2$ other rows and $2$ other columns available, giving $4$ squares. However, $9\cdot4=36$ distinguishes which rook was placed first. Since the rooks are indistinguishable, each arrangement is counted twice, giving $18$.
