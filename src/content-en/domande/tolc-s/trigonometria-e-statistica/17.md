The point of the unit circle associated with the angle $x$ has coordinates $(\cos x,\sin x)$. Which expression gives, for every real $x$, the ordinate of the point associated with the angle $x+\dfrac\pi2$?

- $-\cos x$
- $\cos x$
- $\sin x$
- $-\sin x$
- $\sin x+\dfrac\pi2$

## Explanation

The ordinate of the point associated with the angle $x+\dfrac\pi2$ is $\sin\left(x+\dfrac\pi2\right)$. By the sine addition formula, $\sin\left(x+\dfrac\pi2\right)=\sin x\cos\dfrac\pi2+\cos x\sin\dfrac\pi2$. Since $\cos\dfrac\pi2=0$ and $\sin\dfrac\pi2=1$, the ordinate equals $\cos x$. The expression $-\sin x$ is instead the abscissa of the new point, that is $\cos\left(x+\dfrac\pi2\right)$, while $\sin x+\dfrac\pi2$ comes from adding $\dfrac\pi2$ to the value of the sine instead of to the angle.
