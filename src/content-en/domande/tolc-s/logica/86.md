In a row, A is next to B and B is next to C. The three people are distinct. Which conclusion is correct?

- B is between A and C, which are not next to one another
- A must be next to C
- A and C are the same person
- All three occupy the same position
- No arrangement is possible

## Explanation

In a row, each position has at most two neighbors, one on each side. Since A and C are distinct and both next to B, they occupy B’s opposite sides. The local orders are ABC or CBA. In either case B separates A from C; “being next to” is not transitive.
