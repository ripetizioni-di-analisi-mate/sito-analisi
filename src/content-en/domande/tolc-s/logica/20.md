“If road A is open, road B is also open” holds. Which case obeys the rule but refutes its converse?

- A open and B closed
- A and B both open
- A and B both closed
- No case can do so
- A closed and B open

## Explanation

The converse is B open $\Rightarrow$ A open. Refuting it requires B open and A closed. This case obeys the original rule because its antecedent, A open, is false. It therefore gives a concrete counterexample to equivalence between an implication and its converse.
