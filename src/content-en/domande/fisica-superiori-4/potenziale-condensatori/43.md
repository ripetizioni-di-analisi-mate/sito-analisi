A positive charge of $+2e$ goes from $50$ to $20\,\mathrm V$ with initial kinetic energy $10\,\mathrm{eV}$. Under the electric force alone, what is its final kinetic energy?

- $60\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $40\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $70\,\mathrm{eV}$
- $100\,\mathrm{eV}$

## Explanation

Under the electric force alone the change in kinetic energy equals the electric work, so $K_f-K_i=q(V_i-V_f)$. The potential drop is $30\,\mathrm V$ and the charge is $2e$, so the work is $2e\cdot30\,\mathrm V=60\,\mathrm{eV}$. We get
$$
K_f=K_i+2e(V_i-V_f)=10\,\mathrm{eV}+60\,\mathrm{eV}=70\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$
The value $60\,\mathrm{eV}$ is the work alone, without the initial kinetic energy, and $40\,\mathrm{eV}$ comes from taking a charge $e$.
