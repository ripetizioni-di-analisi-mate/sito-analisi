A proton starts at rest and crosses a potential difference of $500\,\mathrm V$ in the direction in which the electric force does positive work. With $m_p=1.67\cdot10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$, what is its final speed?

- $3.1\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $2.2\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $1.3\cdot10^{7}\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- $9.6\cdot10^{10}\,\mathrm{m/s}$

## Explanation

The proton has charge $e$ and starts at rest, so its final kinetic energy is $K_f=e\cdot500\,\mathrm V=500\,\mathrm{eV}=500\cdot1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm J=8.0\cdot10^{-17}\,\mathrm J$. From $K_f=\dfrac12m_pv^2$ it follows that
$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{2K_f}{m_p}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot8.0\cdot10^{-17}}{1.67\cdot10^{-27}}}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq3.1\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
This speed is about one thousandth of the speed of light, so the classical formula is justified. The value $2.2\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes from forgetting the factor $2$, the value $1.3\cdot10^{7}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from using the electron mass and the value $9.6\cdot10^{10}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from leaving out the square root.
