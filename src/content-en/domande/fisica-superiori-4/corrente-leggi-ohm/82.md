A resistor immersed in water delivers a power of $840\,\mathrm{W}$ to the water for $50\,\mathrm{s}$. Neglecting losses and with $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, what mass of water is heated by $20\,\mathrm{K}$?

- $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

Since losses are negligible, the water receives all the energy $P\,\Delta t$ delivered by the resistor. This energy equals the heat $mc\,\Delta T$ needed for the heating, so the mass is obtained by dividing the energy by $c\,\Delta T$.

$$
m=\frac{P\,\Delta t}{c\,\Delta T}=\frac{840\cdot50}{4200\cdot20}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.50\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$
