A load draws $2.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ at $200\,\mathrm V$ and is supplied by a line whose two conductors together have a resistance of $0.50\,\Omega$. How much power is dissipated in the line by Joule heating?

- $5.0\,\mathrm W$
- $50\,\mathrm W$
- $100\,\mathrm W$
- $80\,\mathrm{kW}$

## Explanation

The current in the load, which is the same as in the line, is $I=\dfrac{P}{V}=\dfrac{2.0\cdot10^3\,\mathrm W}{200\,\mathrm V}=10\,\mathrm A$. A resistor $R$ carrying current $I$ dissipates power $RI^2$, and the $0.50\,\Omega$ resistance already includes both conductors. Hence
$$
P_{\mathrm{line}}=0.50\,\Omega\cdot\left(10\,\mathrm A\right)^2=50\,\mathrm W.
$$
The value $80\,\mathrm{kW}$ comes from using $\dfrac{V^2}{R}$ with the load voltage, which is not the voltage across the line, and the value $5.0\,\mathrm W$ from the product $RI$ without the square.
