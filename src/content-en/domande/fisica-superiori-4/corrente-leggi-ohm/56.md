A wire measures $10\,\Omega$ at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $12\,\Omega$ at $60\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$. What is $\alpha$ referred to $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

- $5.0\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $5.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$
- $2.0\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$

## Explanation

Divide the resistance rise by the reference resistance and then by the temperature rise. The result is the relative change per kelvin.

$$
\alpha=\frac{12-10}{10(60-20)}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}=5.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}.
$$
