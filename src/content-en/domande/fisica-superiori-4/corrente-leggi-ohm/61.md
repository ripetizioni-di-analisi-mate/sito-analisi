A linear $R(T)$ characteristic passes through $(20\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C},8\,\Omega)$ and $(70\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C},10\,\Omega)$. By how much does $R$ rise per kelvin?

- $0.0050\,\Omega/\mathrm{K}$
- $0.25\,\Omega/\mathrm{K}$
- $0.040\,\Omega/\mathrm{K}$
- $25\,\Omega/\mathrm{K}$

## Explanation

This is an absolute change, so divide the resistance increase by the temperature difference. The relative coefficient $\alpha$ would also require dividing by the reference resistance.

$$
\frac{\Delta R}{\Delta T}=\frac{10-8}{70-20}\,\Omega/\mathrm{K}=0.040\,\Omega/\mathrm{K}.
$$
