In a room the sound intensity level is $80\,\mathrm{dB}$, with reference $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. A listener's eardrum has an area of $0.50\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and is struck perpendicularly by the wave. What sound power does it receive?

- $5.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm W$
- $5.0\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm W$
- $5.0\cdot10^{-13}\,\mathrm W$
- $5.0\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm W$

## Explanation

From the definition of the level, $\dfrac{I}{I_0}=10^{\frac{80}{10}}=10^8$, so $I=10^8\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. Intensity is the power per unit area of a surface perpendicular to the propagation, so the power received is $P=IA$. Since $1\,\mathrm{cm^2}=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the area is $A=0.50\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. We get
$$
P=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}\cdot0.50\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}=5.0\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm W.
$$
The value $5.0\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm W$ comes from converting $1\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ into $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and $5.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm W$ from leaving the area in square centimetres.
