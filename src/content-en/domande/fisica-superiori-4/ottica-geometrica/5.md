A converging lens with focal length $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ cannot project onto a screen the image of a slide placed $9.6\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens, wherever the screen is placed. Which of the following changes makes it possible to project a sharp image?

- Moving the slide to $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens.
- Bringing the slide closer, to $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens.
- Placing the slide exactly at the focal point.
- Covering half of the lens with a piece of card.

## Explanation

For a real object the lens equation $\dfrac{1}{q}=\dfrac{1}{f}-\dfrac{1}{p}$ gives $q>0$, that is a real image, only if $p>f$. With the slide at $9.6\,\mathrm{cm}$ we have $p<f$, so the image is virtual and no screen position can capture it. At $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ we get $\dfrac{1}{q}=\left(\dfrac{1}{20}-\dfrac{1}{30}\right)\mathrm{cm^{-1}}=\dfrac{1}{60}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, so the image is real and forms $60\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens. At $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ the image is still virtual and at the focal point the rays emerge parallel, while covering half of the lens only reduces the brightness. Hence the slide must be **moved to $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens**.
