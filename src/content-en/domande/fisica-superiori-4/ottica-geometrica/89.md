Two fixed rays strike the same point on a plane mirror. Their incoming directions make angles of $25^\circ$ and $35^\circ$ on opposite sides of the normal. How should the normal rotate in the plane of the rays to make their reflection angles equal?

- By $5^\circ$ towards the ray initially at $25^\circ$.
- By $10^\circ$ towards the ray initially at $35^\circ$.
- By $30^\circ$ towards the ray initially at $25^\circ$.
- By $5^\circ$ towards the ray initially at $35^\circ$.

## Explanation

The reflection angles are equal when the incidence angles are equal. The normal must bisect the total angle of $60^\circ$. Rotating it by $5^\circ$ towards the more distant ray gives angles of $25^\circ+5^\circ$ and $35^\circ-5^\circ$, both $30^\circ$.
