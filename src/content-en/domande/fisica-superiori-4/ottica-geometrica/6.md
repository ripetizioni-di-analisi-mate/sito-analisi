A short-sighted eye sees objects sharply up to a distance of $40\,\mathrm{cm}$. Neglecting the distance between lens and eye, what power must the corrective lens have so that the eye sees distant objects sharply?

- $-2.5\,\mathrm D$
- $+2.5\,\mathrm D$
- $-0.40\,\mathrm D$
- $-40\,\mathrm D$

## Explanation

For a distant object $\dfrac{1}{p}\simeq0$, and the lens must form a virtual image of it at the farthest point the eye sees sharply, so $q=-0.40\,\mathrm m$. The lens equation then gives $\dfrac{1}{f}=\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{q}\simeq-\dfrac{1}{0.40\,\mathrm m}$. The power is the reciprocal of the focal length in metres, so $D=-2.5\,\mathrm D$ and the lens is diverging. The value $-0.40\,\mathrm D$ confuses the focal length with the power, and $-40\,\mathrm D$ comes from using the distance in centimetres.
