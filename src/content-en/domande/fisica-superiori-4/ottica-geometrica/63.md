Two coins are at depths of $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ in a liquid of index $1.50$ and $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ in another liquid of index $2.00$. Both surfaces are viewed from air nearly along the normal. How do the apparent depths compare?

- The first is greater.
- The second is greater.
- They are equal.
- Both appear at the surface.

## Explanation

For the first coin $d'\simeq\dfrac{9}{1.50}\,\mathrm{cm}=6\,\mathrm{cm}$. For the second $d'\simeq\dfrac{12}{2.00}\,\mathrm{cm}=6\,\mathrm{cm}$. The higher index compensates for the greater actual depth.
