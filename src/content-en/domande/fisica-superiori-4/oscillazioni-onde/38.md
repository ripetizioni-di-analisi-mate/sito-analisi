A simple pendulum is released from rest at $60^\circ$ from vertical. Which assumption of $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$ needs checking?

- The constancy of $g$ over time
- Cancellation of mass
- The approximation of $\sin\theta$ by $\theta$ in radians
- The direction of tension toward the pivot

## Explanation

The formula $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell}{g}}$ is obtained by replacing $\sin\theta$ with $\theta$ in the restoring law, and this replacement is accurate only for small amplitudes. For $\theta=60^\circ$,
$$
\sin 60^\circ=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\simeq0.866,\qquad \theta=\frac{\pi}{3}\,\mathrm{rad}\simeq1.047\,\mathrm{rad}.
$$
The two values differ by about $0.18$, so the linear restoring law does not accurately describe the oscillation. Therefore the assumption that needs checking is the approximation of $\sin\theta$ by $\theta$.
