A string fixed at both ends has two consecutive standing-wave modes at frequencies $150\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $200\,\mathrm{Hz}$. What is its fundamental frequency?

- $150\,\mathrm{Hz}$
- $25\,\mathrm{Hz}$
- $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$
- $175\,\mathrm{Hz}$

## Explanation

For a string fixed at both ends the frequencies of the standing-wave modes are $f_n=nf_1$, with $n$ a positive integer. Two consecutive modes correspond to $n$ and $n+1$, so their frequency difference is $f_{n+1}-f_n=f_1$. Therefore $f_1=200\,\mathrm{Hz}-150\,\mathrm{Hz}=50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The result agrees with the data, because $150\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $200\,\mathrm{Hz}$ are the third and fourth harmonics of $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The value $150\,\mathrm{Hz}$ comes from taking the lower of the two modes as the fundamental, but the ratio $\dfrac{200}{150}$ is not an integer.
