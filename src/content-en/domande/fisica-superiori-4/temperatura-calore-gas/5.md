A mass $m=0.216\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is to become water at $t_f=30\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Assume $\ell_f=3.34\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, with no heat loss. How much total energy is required?

- $9.92\cdot10^4\,\mathrm J$
- $2.71\cdot10^4\,\mathrm J$
- $4.59\cdot10^5\,\mathrm J$
- $7.21\cdot10^4\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The ice melts and the resulting water then warms by $30\,\mathrm K$. Add the two contributions to get $Q=0.216(334\,000+4180\cdot30)\,\mathrm J=99\,230.4\,\mathrm J$. The latent heat contribution is about $72.1\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and alone would leave the resulting water at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$.
