The layer on the warm side of a two-layer wall has thermal resistance $0.30\,\mathrm{K/W}$, and the other has $0.10\,\mathrm{K/W}$. The outer faces are at $40$ and $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. What is the steady-state temperature at the interface?

- $25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $30\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$
- $35\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$

## Explanation

The warm-side layer has three quarters of the total resistance, so three quarters of the $20\,\mathrm K$ temperature difference, or $15\,\mathrm K$, falls across it. The interface is therefore at $40-15=25$ degrees Celsius.
