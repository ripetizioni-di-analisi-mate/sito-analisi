An ideal gas consists of diatomic molecules. Why can it not in general be assigned the same internal energy $\dfrac32nRT$ as the monatomic model?

- Internal energy is zero in every ideal gas.
- Absolute temperature always has negative values.
- Rotations and vibrations can contribute energy.
- The molecules do not translate.

## Explanation

The monatomic model counts translational energy alone. In a diatomic gas some internal motions can store energy, depending on temperature, so the translational contribution alone is not always the total.
