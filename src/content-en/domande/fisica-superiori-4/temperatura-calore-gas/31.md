A copper cylinder with heat capacity $150\,\mathrm{J/K}$, at $90\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, is placed on an iron plate with heat capacity $1350\,\mathrm{J/K}$, at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The two bodies are insulated from the surroundings and do not change phase. How much heat flows from the copper to the iron until equilibrium is reached?

- $10\,500\,\mathrm J$
- $5250\,\mathrm J$
- $9450\,\mathrm J$
- $47\,250\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The common temperature follows from the calorimetric balance, which expresses it as the mean of the temperatures weighted by the heat capacities, so
$$
t_f=\frac{150\cdot90+1350\cdot20}{150+1350}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=\frac{40\,500}{1500}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=27\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$
The heat released by the copper is its heat capacity times its drop in temperature, so $Q=150\,\mathrm{J/K}\cdot(90-27)\,\mathrm K=9450\,\mathrm J$. The iron absorbs $1350\,\mathrm{J/K}\cdot(27-20)\,\mathrm K=9450\,\mathrm J$, the same amount, as insulation requires. The value $10\,500\,\mathrm J$ comes from assuming that the copper cools down to $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, while $5250\,\mathrm J$ and $47\,250\,\mathrm J$ are obtained by taking the arithmetic mean $55\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ as the final temperature for the copper and for the iron.
