A pure substance is heated at constant pressure with constant power delivered to it. While in a single phase, its temperature rises faster after melting than before. What does this imply about the specific heat capacities of the two phases?

- The liquid's specific heat capacity is lower.
- They are equal because the mass is unchanged.
- The liquid's specific heat capacity is higher.
- The only conclusion is that the latent heat is smaller.

## Explanation

Within a single phase the slope of temperature versus time is $\dfrac{P}{mc}$. Since $P$ and $m$ are unchanged, the steeper segment corresponds to lower $c$.
