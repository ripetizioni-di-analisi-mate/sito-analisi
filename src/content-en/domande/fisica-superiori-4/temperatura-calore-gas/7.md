A fixed amount of ideal gas goes from $p_1=1.68\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $V_1=0.012\,\mathrm{m^3}$, and $T_1=300\,\mathrm K$ to $V_2=0.018\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and $T_2=360\,\mathrm K$. What is $p_2$?

- $1.34\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $3.02\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $1.12\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$
- $2.02\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$

## Explanation

Since $\dfrac{pV}{T}=nR$ is constant, $p_2=p_1\dfrac{V_1}{V_2}\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}=1.68\cdot10^5\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{6}{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}=134\,400\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Volume rises by a factor of $1.5$ and temperature by a factor of $1.2$, so pressure decreases.
