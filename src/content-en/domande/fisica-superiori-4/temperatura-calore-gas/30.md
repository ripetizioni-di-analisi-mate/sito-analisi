A $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ sample receives $900\,\mathrm J$ and warms by $5\,\mathrm K$ without changing phase. What is its specific heat capacity?

- $225\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $4500\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$
- $180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$

## Explanation

The ratio $\dfrac Q{\Delta T}=\dfrac{900}{5}\,\mathrm{J/K}=180\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is the heat capacity of the sample. Dividing by its mass gives $c=\dfrac{180}{0.20}\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.
