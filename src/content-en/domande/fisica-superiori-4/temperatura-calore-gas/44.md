Water at $40\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ mixes with ice at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ in an insulated vessel of negligible heat capacity. If there is $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice, what mass of warm water is just sufficient to melt it? Use $\ell_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$ and $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.

- About $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- About $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- About $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- About $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$.

## Explanation

At the threshold, the warm water releases all the heat available as it cools to $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, namely $m_hc\cdot40$. Equate this to $0.10\cdot334\,000\,\mathrm J$ to obtain $m_h=\dfrac{33\,400}{4180\cdot40}\,\mathrm{kg}\simeq0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$.
