A lamp emits two close spectral lines with wavelengths $500.0\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $500.5\,\mathrm{nm}$. The beam illuminates, at normal incidence, a $0.80\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide region of a grating with $500$ lines per millimetre. What is the first order in which the two lines are resolved according to the Rayleigh criterion?

- The first.
- The second.
- The third.
- No order of the grating.

## Explanation

The number of illuminated lines is $N=0.80\,\mathrm{mm}\cdot500\,\mathrm{mm^{-1}}=400$. The ratio of the wavelength to the separation of the two lines is $\dfrac{\lambda}{\Delta\lambda}=\dfrac{500.0}{0.5}=1000$. The lines are resolved in order $m$ if $mN\ge1000$, that is if $m\ge\dfrac{1000}{400}=2.5$, so the first useful order is $m=3$. This order exists, because the spacing is $d=\dfrac{1}{500}\,\mathrm{mm}=2.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ and $3\lambda=1.5\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ is less than $d$. Therefore the two lines are first resolved in the **third** order.
