In a material with $n=1.60$, $\lambda=375\,\mathrm{nm}$ is measured. What is the wavelength in vacuum?

- $\lambda_0=600\,\mathrm{nm}$.
- $\lambda_0=234\,\mathrm{nm}$.
- $\lambda_0=375\,\mathrm{nm}$.
- $\lambda_0=960\,\mathrm{nm}$.

## Explanation

Frequency conservation gives $\lambda_0=n\lambda$. Thus $\lambda_0=1.60\cdot375\,\mathrm{nm}=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The wavelength increases along with speed on entering vacuum.
