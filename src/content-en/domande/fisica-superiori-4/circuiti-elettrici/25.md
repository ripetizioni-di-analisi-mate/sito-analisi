Three equal $6\,\Omega$ resistors are available, and all three must be connected between the same two terminals. Which connection gives an equivalent resistance of $9\,\Omega$?

- All three in series.
- One in series with the parallel combination of the other two.
- Two in series, with that series combination in parallel with the third.
- All three in parallel.

## Explanation

Two $6\,\Omega$ resistors in parallel have equivalent resistance $\dfrac{6\cdot6}{6+6}\,\Omega=3\,\Omega$. Resistances in series add, so the third resistor placed in series with that parallel pair gives $6\,\Omega+3\,\Omega=9\,\Omega$. The other connections give different values: the three resistors in series give $18\,\Omega$ and in parallel $2\,\Omega$. Two resistors in series, equal to $12\,\Omega$, in parallel with the third give $\dfrac{12\cdot6}{12+6}\,\Omega=4\,\Omega$. Therefore $9\,\Omega$ is obtained with **one resistor in series with the parallel combination of the other two**.
