An ideal $9\,\mathrm V$ battery powers a divider with $R_1=3\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $R_2=6\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$. What load in parallel with $R_2$ lowers the output voltage to $3\,\mathrm V$?

- $1.5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$.
- $3\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$.
- $2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$.
- $6\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$.

## Explanation

The six volts across the upper resistor determine the total current. The lower combination must therefore be one and a half kilohms. The parallel formula then gives a two-kilohm load.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm p}&=\frac{3\,\mathrm V}{6\,\mathrm V}(3\,\mathrm{k\Omega})
             =1.5\,\mathrm{k\Omega},\\[4pt]
\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm L}}&=
\left(\frac{1}{1.5}-\frac{1}{6}\right)\,(\mathrm{k\Omega})^{-1}
=\frac{1}{2}\,(\mathrm{k\Omega})^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$
