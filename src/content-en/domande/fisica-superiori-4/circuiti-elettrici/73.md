The charging current of an initially uncharged capacitor starts at $I_0$. If $\tau=0.50\,\mathrm s$, when does it reach $\dfrac{I_0}{2}$?

- $0.5\,\mathrm s$.
- $(2\ln 2)\,\mathrm s$.
- $\ln 2\,\mathrm s$.
- $\dfrac{\ln 2}{2}\,\mathrm s$.

## Explanation

The current is proportional to the voltage still across the resistor. It follows an exponential decay, so the half-life is shorter than one time constant.

$$
\frac{I(t)}{I_0}=e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}=\frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=\tau\ln 2=\frac{\ln 2}{2}\,\mathrm s.
$$
