A monatomic ideal gas goes from $(2\,\mathrm{L},100\,\mathrm{kPa})$ to $(4\,\mathrm{L},200\,\mathrm{kPa})$. It first goes to $(2\,\mathrm{L},200\,\mathrm{kPa})$ and then expands quasistatically at constant pressure. How much heat does it receive along the path?

- $900\,\mathrm{J}$
- $400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1300\,\mathrm{J}$
- $1100\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

The increase in internal energy depends on the endpoint products $pV$. The work instead comes from the horizontal segment at $200$ kilopascals. Their sum gives the heat.

$$
Q=\frac32(200\cdot4-100\cdot2)\,\mathrm{J}+200(4-2)\,\mathrm{J}=1300\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
