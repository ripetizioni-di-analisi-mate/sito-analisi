A monatomic ideal gas sample expands quasistatically at constant pressure and does $160\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. How much does its internal energy increase?

- $160\,\mathrm{J}$
- $240\,\mathrm{J}$
- $400\,\mathrm{J}$
- $80\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Constant pressure gives $W=nR\Delta T$. For a monatomic ideal gas the change in internal energy is three halves of that same amount. It is positive because heating accompanies expansion at constant pressure.

$$
\Delta U=\frac32 nR\Delta T=\frac32 W=240\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
