An ideal gas starts from state $A$, where $nRT_A=1000\,\mathrm J$, and goes through a three-stage cycle: a reversible isothermal expansion to twice the volume, a quasistatic compression at constant pressure back to the initial volume, and heating at constant volume that brings it back to $A$. With $\ln2\simeq0.693$, what is the net work done by the gas in the cycle?

- $693\,\mathrm J$
- $193\,\mathrm J$
- $-307\,\mathrm J$
- $1193\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

In the isothermal expansion the gas does work $W_1=nRT_A\ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}=1000\,\mathrm J\cdot0.693=693\,\mathrm J$. At the end of the isotherm the temperature is still $T_A$, so $p_BV_B=nRT_A=1000\,\mathrm J$. The compression takes place at pressure $p_B$ and reduces the volume from $V_B$ to $V_A=\dfrac{V_B}{2}$, therefore
$$
W_2=p_B\left(V_A-V_B\right)=-\frac{p_BV_B}{2}=-500\,\mathrm J.
$$
In the constant-volume stage the work is zero, so the net work is $W_{\mathrm{tot}}=693\,\mathrm J-500\,\mathrm J=193\,\mathrm J$. The value $-307\,\mathrm J$ comes from computing the compression with the initial pressure $p_A=2p_B$, and $693\,\mathrm J$ is the work of the isotherm alone.
