A gas follows a clockwise rectangular cycle between $100$ and $312\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and between $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{L}$. What is the net work?

::grafico{linee="2,100 2,312 6,312 6,100 2,100" vettori="2,160,2,245;3,312,5,312;6,245,6,160;5,100,3,100" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=400 nomi="V,p (kPa)" titolo="The arrows indicate the clockwise direction of the cycle. Volume is in liters."}

- $W=1248\,\mathrm{J}$
- $W=1648\,\mathrm{J}$
- $W=848\,\mathrm{J}$
- $W=-848\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Expansion at higher pressure does more work in magnitude than compression at lower pressure. The difference equals the area of the rectangle, the product of the two coordinate differences.

$$
W_{\mathrm{tot}}=(312-100)(6-2)\,\mathrm{J}=848\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
