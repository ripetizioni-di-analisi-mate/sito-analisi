A gas does $240\,\mathrm{J}$ in an expansion at $160\,\mathrm{kPa}$. It then returns to the initial volume by quasistatic compression at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$, joined to the expansion by an isochoric stage. What is the total work?

- $150\,\mathrm{J}$
- $240\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-90\,\mathrm{J}$
- $90\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Compression traverses the same volume interval in reverse. The connecting stage at constant volume makes no contribution to the work.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=\frac{240}{160}\,\mathrm{L}=1.5\,\mathrm{L}, \\[6pt]
W&=240\,\mathrm{J}-100\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{J}=90\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$
