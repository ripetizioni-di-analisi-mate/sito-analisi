A vertical cylinder contains a gas and is closed by a piston of mass $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ and area $50\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, which slides without friction; above the piston is the atmosphere at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The gas is heated slowly and the piston rises by $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, how much work does the gas do?

- $20\,\mathrm J$
- $4.0\,\mathrm J$
- $2.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$
- $24\,\mathrm J$

## Explanation

The piston rises slowly, so at every instant it is in equilibrium and the force of the gas balances the weight of the piston and the force of the atmosphere. Therefore the pressure of the gas stays constant and equals
$$
p=p_{\mathrm{atm}}+\frac{mg}{A}=100\,\mathrm{kPa}+\frac{10\cdot10\,\mathrm N}{5.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}}=100\,\mathrm{kPa}+20\,\mathrm{kPa}=120\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$
The volume increases by $\Delta V=A\,\Delta h=5.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}\cdot0.040\,\mathrm m=2.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Since the pressure is constant, the work done by the gas is $W=p\,\Delta V=1.20\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot2.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}=24\,\mathrm J$. The value $20\,\mathrm J$ accounts only for the atmospheric pressure, the value $4.0\,\mathrm J$ only for lifting the piston, and the value $2.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$ comes from using the displacement in centimetres.
