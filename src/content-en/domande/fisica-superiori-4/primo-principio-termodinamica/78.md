A gas quasistatically follows $A\to B\to C\to A$ along straight segments, with $A=(1\,\mathrm{L},100\,\mathrm{kPa})$, $B=(1\,\mathrm{L},300\,\mathrm{kPa})$, and $C=(4\,\mathrm{L},100\,\mathrm{kPa})$. What is the net work?

::grafico{linee="1,100 1,300 4,100 1,100" vettori="1,164,1,263;1.75,250,3.25,150;3.25,100,1.75,100" punti="1,100,pieno,A;1,300,pieno,B;4,100,pieno,C" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=380 nomi="V,p (kPa)" titolo="The cycle has a vertical, a sloping, and a horizontal segment. Volume is in liters."}

- $300\,\mathrm{J}$
- $600\,\mathrm{J}$
- $900\,\mathrm{J}$
- $-300\,\mathrm{J}$

## Explanation

Expansion along the diagonal occurs at a higher average pressure than horizontal compression. Subtracting the respective areas gives the triangle enclosed by the clockwise cycle.

$$
W_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac12(4-1)(300-100)\,\mathrm{J}=300\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
