A solid melts at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Its specific latent heat is $150\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. How much mass melts when the entropy of the material increases by $100\,\mathrm{J/K}$ at that temperature?

- $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$
- $2\,\mathrm{kg}$

## Explanation

During melting at constant temperature, the transition heat is temperature times entropy change. To find mass, divide the heat by the amount required for one kilogram.

$$
\begin{gathered}
Q=300\cdot100\,\mathrm{J}=30000\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
m=\frac{30000\,\mathrm{J}}{150000\,\mathrm{J/kg}}=0.20\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{gathered}
$$
