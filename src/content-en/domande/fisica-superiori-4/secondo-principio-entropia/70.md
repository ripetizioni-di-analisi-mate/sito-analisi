In a thermally insulated container water is stirred by paddles driven by a motor. The paddle work is dissipated and the water comes to rest at a higher temperature. How does the entropy of the water change?

- It stays the same because the container is thermally insulated.
- It increases even though no heat is exchanged.
- It decreases because the paddles do work on the water.
- It does not depend on the final temperature because the process is irreversible.

## Explanation

The paddle work is dissipated in the water and increases its internal energy, so the water reaches an equilibrium state at a higher temperature. Entropy is a state function, so its change is calculated along a reversible heating between the same states and equals $\Delta S=mc\ln\dfrac{T_f}{T_i}$. Since $T_f>T_i$, the change is positive. The heat exchanged with the surroundings is zero, so the increase in entropy is due to the irreversibility of the dissipation.
