An insulated device heats water with motor-driven paddles. Paddle work is dissipated and the water comes to rest at a higher temperature. How does the entropy of the water change?

- It stays the same because the container is thermally insulated.
- It increases even though no heat is exchanged.
- It decreases because the paddles do work on the water.
- It does not depend on the final temperature because the process is irreversible.

## Explanation

Work is dissipated in the water and increases its internal energy. The water returns to rest at a higher temperature, so its entropy rises. Thermal insulation is consistent with the increase in entropy. Reversible heating can be used to calculate the state change.
