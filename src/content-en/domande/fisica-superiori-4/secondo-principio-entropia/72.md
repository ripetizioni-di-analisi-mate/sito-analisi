An ideal gas with $C_V=\dfrac32R$ reaches one-eighth of its initial volume and four times its initial absolute temperature. How does its entropy change?

- It increases because the temperature rises.
- It decreases because the volume decreases.
- It cannot be determined without knowing the path.
- It stays the same.

## Explanation

For the change between states, heating contributes positively and compression negatively. After including the molar heat capacity factor, the two logarithms cancel exactly.

$$
\frac{\Delta S}{nR}=\frac32\ln4+\ln\frac18=3\ln2-3\ln2=0.
$$
