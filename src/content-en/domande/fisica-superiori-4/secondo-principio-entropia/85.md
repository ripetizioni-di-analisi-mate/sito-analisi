At the melting temperature part of a solid melts reversibly. Why does its entropy change even though the temperature is constant?

- The material absorbs latent heat and changes phase.
- Entropy does not change as long as the temperature stays constant.
- The material rejects latent heat during melting.
- The change is determined solely by the increase in absolute temperature.

## Explanation

During melting the liquid fraction changes. The material absorbs latent heat $m\ell_f$ at the equilibrium temperature $T$, so its entropy increases by $\dfrac{m\ell_f}{T}$.
