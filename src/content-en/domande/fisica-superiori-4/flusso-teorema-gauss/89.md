An insulating sphere with charge uniform in its volume and an isolated conducting sphere at equilibrium have the same radius $R$ and the same total positive charge. They are studied separately. At which distances, excluding the surface $r=R$, do the field magnitudes differ?

- At $0<r<R$, inside the spheres.
- Only at $r>R$.
- Only at $r=0$.
- At every $r>R$ and also at the center.

## Explanation

Outside, each Gaussian sphere encloses the same total charge. Inside the metal the field is zero, while in the insulating volume a sphere of positive inner radius encloses part of the charge and the field is nonzero.
