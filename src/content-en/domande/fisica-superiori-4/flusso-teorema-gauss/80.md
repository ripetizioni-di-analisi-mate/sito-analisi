A point charge moves from the center of a spherical cavity to an off-center point, staying inside a cavity in a conductor at equilibrium. What does Gauss's law fix about the wall?

- The density must remain uniform.
- Every point on the wall keeps its own density.
- Total induced charge becomes zero.
- Total induced charge stays the same.

## Explanation

The field is zero in the metal, so flux through a Gaussian surface around the cavity is zero. Total wall charge remains the negative of the point charge. Moving the charge breaks symmetry, so the local density cannot be found from that balance alone.
