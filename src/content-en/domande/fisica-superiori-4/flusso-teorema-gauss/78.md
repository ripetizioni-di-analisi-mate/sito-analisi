A neutral conductor contains two closed cavities, one with a charge of $+7\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the other with $-3\,\mathrm{nC}$. What is the total charge on the conductor's outer surface?

- $-4\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $4\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $10\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $0\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

In equilibrium the field is zero at every point of the metal, so a Gaussian surface lying entirely in the metal around a cavity encloses zero net charge. Therefore the wall of the cavity with $+7\,\mathrm{nC}$ carries $-7\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the wall of the cavity with $-3\,\mathrm{nC}$ carries $+3\,\mathrm{nC}$. The two walls carry $-4\,\mathrm{nC}$ together. Since the conductor is neutral, the outer surface carries the opposite charge, that is $4\,\mathrm{nC}$. The value $-4\,\mathrm{nC}$ is the charge on the cavity walls.
