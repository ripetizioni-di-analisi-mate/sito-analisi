A closed cavity in a grounded conductor contains a charge of $+7\,\mathrm{nC}$. There are no other external charges. The ground connection is removed first, then the cavity is opened, the charge is taken far away without touching the metal, and the cavity is closed again. How much charge remains on the conductor's outer surface at equilibrium?

- $+7\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-7\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $0\,\mathrm{nC}$
- $-14\,\mathrm{nC}$

## Explanation

While grounded, the inner wall carries $-7\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the outer surface is uncharged. Disconnecting isolates a conductor with net charge $-7\,\mathrm{nC}$. Once the cavity is empty its wall no longer carries induced charge, so the entire net charge moves to the outer surface.

Theory — [Field in the metal and just outside it](/en/fisica-superiori-4/flusso-teorema-gauss/teoria#conduttore).
