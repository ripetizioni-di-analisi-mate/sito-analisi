A closed surface encloses net charge $+4\,\mathrm{nC}$. A charge of $-9\,\mathrm{nC}$ moves from outside to inside. With $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$, which value is closest to the new net outward flux?

- $565\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$
- $-565\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$
- $-1020\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$
- $452\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$

## Explanation

By Gauss's theorem, the outward flux through a closed surface is $\Phi_E=\dfrac{Q_{\mathrm{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$, where $Q_{\mathrm{enc}}$ is the net enclosed charge. After the new charge enters, the enclosed charge is the signed sum $Q_{\mathrm{enc}}=(4-9)\,\mathrm{nC}=-5\,\mathrm{nC}$. Hence
$$
\Phi_E=\frac{-5\cdot10^{-9}}{8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}\simeq-565\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}.
$$
The negative sign means that the net flux is inward. The value $-1020\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$ comes from using only the charge that entered, and $452\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$ from using only the initial charge.
