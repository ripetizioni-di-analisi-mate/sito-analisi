Two open surfaces share a boundary and together bound a charge-free volume. First orient both with normals outward from that volume. How are their fluxes related?

- Both are necessarily zero.
- Their sum is zero.
- Both must be positive.
- The greater flux is always through the surface of greater area.

## Explanation

The two surfaces make up the closed boundary. Gauss's law gives zero sum because there is no enclosed charge. The individual fluxes are opposite and can be nonzero.
