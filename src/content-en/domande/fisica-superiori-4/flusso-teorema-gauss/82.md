A static internal field is measured along a metal segment, and current flows. Why does the rule of zero internal field not apply?

- Conductors contain no mobile charges.
- The segment is not in electrostatic equilibrium.
- The vacuum constant is zero in the metal.
- The rule holds only for fields that change with time.

## Explanation

The field exerts a force on mobile carriers and maintains the current. A time-independent field can accompany steady motion of charge. Electrostatic equilibrium requires that there be no such motion.
