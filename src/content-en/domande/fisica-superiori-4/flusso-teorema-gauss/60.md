The field is measured close to the end of a finite wire. Why may the infinite-wire formula be inaccurate there?

- Gauss's law applies only to infinite wires.
- A finite distribution produces no electric field.
- The vacuum permittivity changes with position.
- The finite wire is not invariant under translations along its axis.

## Explanation

Near an end, moving along the wire changes the charge distribution seen from the point. Translational symmetry is lost, so the field cannot be assumed identical over the whole curved side.
