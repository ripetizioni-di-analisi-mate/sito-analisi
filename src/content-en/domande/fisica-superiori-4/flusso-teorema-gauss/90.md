An insulating sphere with a fixed, spherically symmetric charge distribution is enclosed by a concentric Gaussian surface. A point charge is added just outside the Gaussian surface. Which method gives the resulting field at a point on the surface?

- Divide total flux by area to obtain the local field.
- Vector-add the spherical field and the exterior charge's field.
- Apply Gauss's law counting both charges as enclosed.
- Discard any contribution produced by the exterior charge.

## Explanation

Find the field of the spherical distribution alone using Gauss's law. Then add the exterior point charge's Coulomb field as a vector. The total flux remains that from the inner distribution, but the normal component is no longer constant over the surface.
