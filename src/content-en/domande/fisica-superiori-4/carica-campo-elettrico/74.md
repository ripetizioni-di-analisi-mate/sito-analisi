A dipole is in angular equilibrium with $\vec p$ opposite to $\vec E$. If it is displaced slightly and released, does the torque bring it back toward that configuration?

- No, the torque tends to align $\vec p$ with $\vec E$.
- The torque stays zero after the displacement.
- Yes, the torque tends to move the vectors apart until they are opposite.
- Yes, because a parallel dipole is always unstable.

## Explanation

At $180^\circ$ the torque is zero. After a displacement, the moment arm is nonzero and the rotation tends toward alignment, so antiparallel equilibrium is unstable.
