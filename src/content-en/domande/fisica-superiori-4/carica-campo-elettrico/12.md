A charge $q=-2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is placed at a point $P$ where the electric field has components $(E_x,E_y)=(300,400)\,\mathrm{N/C}$. What is the vertical force component $F_y$, taking upward as positive?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,P" vettori="0,0,1.5,2,E" segmenti="0,2,1.5,2;1.5,0,1.5,2" xmin=-2.5 xmax=3.3 ymin=-2.4 ymax=3.2 titolo="The field components at P are 300 and 400 N/C"}

- $+0.80\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $-0.80\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $-0.60\,\mathrm{\mu N}$
- $+0.60\,\mathrm{\mu N}$

## Explanation

The force on a charge in an electric field is $\vec F=q\vec E$, so each force component is the charge times the corresponding field component. Since $q$ is negative, the force points opposite to the field. The vertical component is therefore
$$
F_y=qE_y=\left(-2.0\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm C\right)\cdot400\,\mathrm{N/C}=-8.0\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm N=-0.80\,\mathrm{\mu N}.
$$
The value $-0.60\,\mathrm{\mu N}$ is the horizontal component $qE_x$, while the positive values come from ignoring the sign of the charge.
