## Exercise 1

A beam of electrons accelerated through a voltage $V = 54\,\mathrm{V}$ is normally incident on the surface of a crystal whose rows of atoms are spaced $d = 0.215\,\mathrm{nm}$ apart. Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of the electrons and the angle to the surface normal at which the first diffraction maximum occurs. Use $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, and $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$.

$$
\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m_e e V}} ,
\qquad
d \sin\theta = \lambda
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\sin\theta = \frac{\lambda}{d}
$$
$$
\lambda = \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 54}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.67 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\sin\theta = \frac{1.67 \cdot 10^{-10}}{2.15 \cdot 10^{-10}} \approx 0.777
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\theta \approx 51^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An electron accelerated from rest gains kinetic energy $eV$. Since $v \ll c$, its momentum is $\sqrt{2 m_e e V}$. By **de Broglie’s hypothesis**, its wavelength is $\dfrac{h}{p}$, or $0.167\,\mathrm{nm}$.

The rows of atoms at the surface scatter the electrons like the slits of a grating. Waves scattered by neighboring rows at angle $\theta$ have a path difference $d \sin\theta$ and interfere constructively when this difference equals one wavelength. The ratio $\dfrac{\lambda}{d}$ is less than $1$, so the angle exists and is about $51^\circ$. This agrees with the angle of $50^\circ$ measured by **Davisson and Germer** using a nickel crystal with this particular spacing between rows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a diffraction experiment, electrons are normally incident on a crystal with rows of atoms spaced $d = 0.30\,\mathrm{nm}$ apart. The first maximum is observed at $\theta = 30^\circ$ to the normal. Calculate the electron wavelength and the voltage through which the electrons were accelerated from rest. Explain whether a second-order maximum can also be observed. Use $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, and $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 unicolore=1 linee="-1,0 7,0" vettori="2,3.5,2,0.15;4,3.5,4,0.15;2,0,3.5,2.6;4,0,5.5,2.6" segmenti="2,0.15,2,3.2;4,0,2.5,0.866" punti="0,0,pieno;2,0,pieno;4,0,pieno;6,0,pieno" etichette="2.2,2.4,θ;2.9,-0.45,d;1.2,3.1,e⁻" xmin=-1.5 xmax=7.5 ymin=-1 ymax=3.8 titolo="Electrons normally incident on the crystal surface and scattered at an angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ to the normal. The spacing between two rows of atoms is $d$"}

$$
d \sin\theta = \lambda ,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m_e e V}}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
V = \frac{h^2}{2 m_e e \lambda^2}
$$
$$
\lambda = 0.30\,\mathrm{nm} \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 0.30\,\mathrm{nm} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 0.15\,\mathrm{nm}
$$
$$
V = \frac{\left( 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \right)^2}{2 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot \left( 1.5 \cdot 10^{-10} \right)^2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 67\,\mathrm{V}
$$
$$
d \sin\theta_2 = 2\lambda
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\sin\theta_2 = \frac{2 \cdot 0.15}{0.30} = 1
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\theta_2 = 90^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first maximum occurs when the path difference $d \sin\theta$ equals one wavelength, so $\lambda = d \sin 30^\circ = 0.15\,\mathrm{nm}$. To find the voltage, square the relation $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2 m_e e V}}$ and solve for $V$. The result is approximately $67\,\mathrm{V}$.

A second-order maximum requires a path difference of $2\lambda = 0.30\,\mathrm{nm}$, so $\sin\theta_2 = 1$. The angle is therefore $90^\circ$. Electrons scattered in that direction travel along the surface, so the second-order maximum **is not observed** at this voltage. At a higher voltage the wavelength decreases, $\sin\theta_2$ becomes less than $1$, and the second-order maximum appears at an angle smaller than $90^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Electrons accelerated through $100\,\mathrm{V}$ pass through two parallel slits separated by $d = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ and reach a screen a distance $L = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ away. Calculate the spacing between adjacent bright fringes using the small-angle approximation. Use $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, and $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$.

$$
\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m_e e V}} ,
\qquad
\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}
$$
$$
\lambda = \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 100}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.23 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta y = \frac{1.23 \cdot 10^{-10} \cdot 1.5}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 9.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m} = 92\,\mu\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The electrons have de Broglie wavelength $\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2 m_e e V}}$, which for $100\,\mathrm{V}$ equals $0.123\,\mathrm{nm}$. In Young’s experiment with light, bright fringes form where $d \sin\theta = k \lambda$, and for small angles $\sin\theta \approx \tan\theta = \dfrac{y}{L}$. The spacing of neighboring fringes is therefore $\dfrac{\lambda L}{d}$. The same relation applies to electrons, using their de Broglie wavelength.

The fringe spacing is about $92\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, a measurable distance. The corresponding angle is $\dfrac{\lambda}{d} \approx 6 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad}$, so the small-angle approximation is justified. Each electron reaches the screen at a single point. Fringes appear after many electrons arrive, and the density of the points is proportional to $|\psi|^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

In a hydrogen atom, the electron is in the Bohr orbit with $n = 2$. Calculate the orbital radius, the electron’s speed, and its de Broglie wavelength. Verify that the circumference contains exactly two wavelengths. Use $r_1 = 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, and $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$.

$$
r_2 = 4 r_1 = 4 \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.12 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{2 h}{2\pi m_e r_2} = \frac{2 \cdot 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{2\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 2.12 \cdot 10^{-10}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.09 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\lambda_2 = \frac{h}{m_e v_2} = \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 1.093 \cdot 10^{6}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 6.66 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
2\pi r_2 = 2\pi \cdot 2.12 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.33 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{2\pi r_2}{\lambda_2} \approx \frac{1.33 \cdot 10^{-9}}{6.66 \cdot 10^{-10}} = 2.0
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="(3+0.45*sin(2*t))*cos(t),(3+0.45*sin(2*t))*sin(t),0,6.29;3*cos(t),3*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 legenda="\text{de Broglie wave};\text{orbit } r_2" titolo="On orbit $n = 2$, the circumference $2\pi r_2$ contains two de Broglie wavelengths of the electron and the wave is a standing wave"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The radius of orbit $n = 2$ is $4 r_1$, from the formula for the radii of the Bohr orbits. The speed follows from the second postulate, $m_e v_2 r_2 = 2 \dfrac{h}{2\pi}$, and is about $1.09 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, half of $v_1$. This speed is much smaller than $c$, so the momentum is $m_e v_2$ and the de Broglie wavelength is $\dfrac{h}{m_e v_2} \approx 0.666\,\mathrm{nm}$.

The circumference of the orbit is $1.33\,\mathrm{nm}$, and its ratio to the wavelength is $2$. The electron wave therefore matches up with itself after two wavelengths and forms a **standing wave**, as predicted by the relation between Bohr orbits and standing waves. The result is independent of rounding because the condition $2\pi r_n = n \lambda_n$ is equivalent to Bohr’s second postulate.

</div>
