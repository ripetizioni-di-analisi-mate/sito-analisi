## Exercise 1

An electron is confined to a one-dimensional box of width $L = 0.40\,\mathrm{nm}$. Calculate the first three energy levels in electronvolts and the wavelength of the photon emitted in a transition from level $n = 2$ to level $n = 1$. Use $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$.

$$
E_n = \frac{n^2 h^2}{8 m_e L^2} = n^2 E_1 ,
\qquad
E_{\text{photon}} = E_2 - E_1 = 3 E_1 ,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{h c}{E_{\text{photon}}}
$$
$$
E_1 = \frac{\left( 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \right)^2}{8 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot \left( 4.0 \cdot 10^{-10} \right)^2}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3.77 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.36\,\mathrm{eV}
$$
$$
E_2 = 4 E_1 \approx 9.42\,\mathrm{eV} ,
\qquad
E_3 = 9 E_1 \approx 21.2\,\mathrm{eV}
$$
$$
\lambda = \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{3 \cdot 3.77 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m} = 176\,\mathrm{nm}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 linee="0,0 0,23;4,0 4,23;0,2.36 4,2.36;0,9.42 4,9.42;0,21.2 4,21.2" vettori="2,9.42,2,2.36,hf" etichette="4.2,2.0,n = 1   2.36 eV;4.2,9.1,n = 2   9.42 eV;4.2,20.9,n = 3   21.2 eV;1.7,-1.5,L" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7.5 ymin=-2.5 ymax=24 titolo="Energy levels of an electron in a box of width $L = 0.40\,\mathrm{nm}$. The levels increase as $n^2$, and the transition from $n = 2$ to $n = 1$ emits a photon of energy $3 E_1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The walls of the box are impenetrable, so the electron’s de Broglie wave is a standing wave with nodes at the walls. According to the expression for the **energy levels of a particle in a box**, the energy takes only the values $E_n = \dfrac{n^2 h^2}{8 m_e L^2}$. First calculate $E_1$ in joules, then convert it to electronvolts by dividing by $1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}$. The next levels are obtained by multiplying $E_1$ by $n^2$, namely by $4$ and by $9$.

In the transition from $n = 2$ to $n = 1$, the emitted photon’s energy is the difference $4 E_1 - E_1 = 3 E_1 \approx 7.07\,\mathrm{eV}$. The wavelength is $h c$ divided by this energy, or about $176\,\mathrm{nm}$, in the ultraviolet. The energies obtained are a few electronvolts, like those of electrons in atoms, because the box is comparable in width to an atom.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron in a one-dimensional box goes from level $n = 2$ to level $n = 1$ and emits a photon of wavelength $\lambda = 500\,\mathrm{nm}$. Find the width $L$ of the box. Use $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$, and $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\frac{h c}{\lambda} = E_2 - E_1 = \frac{4 h^2}{8 m_e L^2} - \frac{h^2}{8 m_e L^2} = \frac{3 h^2}{8 m_e L^2}
$$
$$
L^2 = \frac{3 h^2 \lambda}{8 m_e h c} = \frac{3 h \lambda}{8 m_e c}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
L = \sqrt{\frac{3 h \lambda}{8 m_e c}}
$$
$$
L = \sqrt{\frac{3 \cdot 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 5.00 \cdot 10^{-7}}{8 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 6.7 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.67\,\mathrm{nm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The emitted photon’s energy, $\dfrac{h c}{\lambda}$, equals the difference between levels $n = 2$ and $n = 1$. From the expression for the **energy levels in a box**, this difference is $\dfrac{3 h^2}{8 m_e L^2}$. Solve for $L^2$ by multiplying both sides by $L^2 \lambda$ and dividing by $h c$. One of the two factors of $h$ cancels. Taking the square root gives $L \approx 0.67\,\mathrm{nm}$.

Compare this result with the preceding exercise. The box is wider than the $0.40\,\mathrm{nm}$ box, so the energy levels are closer together and the emitted photon has a longer wavelength, in the visible range. This provides a simple model for some long molecules in which electrons move along a chain of atoms and absorb visible light.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A proton is confined to a region of width $L = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}$, comparable to the size of a nucleus, modeled as a one-dimensional box. Calculate the lowest energy level in $\mathrm{MeV}$ and the wavelength of the photon emitted in the transition from level $n = 2$ to level $n = 1$. Identify the region of the spectrum to which the photon belongs. Use $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$.

$$
E_1 = \frac{h^2}{8 m_p L^2} = \frac{\left( 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \right)^2}{8 \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot \left( 1.0 \cdot 10^{-14} \right)^2}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3.29 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.1\,\mathrm{MeV}
$$
$$
E_{\text{photon}} = 3 E_1 \approx 9.87 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 6.2\,\mathrm{MeV}
$$
$$
\lambda = \frac{h c}{E_{\text{photon}}} = \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{9.87 \cdot 10^{-13}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.0 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the formula for the **energy levels in a box** with the proton mass and $L = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}$. The lowest level is about $2.1\,\mathrm{MeV}$, a million times greater than typical energy levels for electrons in atoms. The difference is mainly due to the box being narrower by a factor of about $10^{4}$, since its width is squared in the denominator, and partly to the mass being greater by a factor of about $1830$.

The proton has kinetic energy of approximately $2\,\mathrm{MeV}$ compared with a rest energy of about $940\,\mathrm{MeV}$, so $v \ll c$ and the classical formula $E = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$ applies. In the transition from $n = 2$ to $n = 1$, the photon has energy $3 E_1 \approx 6.2\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and a wavelength of about $2.0 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m}$, shorter than $0.01\,\mathrm{nm}$. It is therefore a **gamma ray**, consistent with the fact that nuclear processes emit gamma rays.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A small ball of mass $m = 0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves back and forth between two walls $L = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ apart at speed $v = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Describe the system as a particle in a box. Calculate the lowest energy level, the quantum number $n$ corresponding to the ball’s kinetic energy, and the energy difference between that level and the next. Explain why the energy quantization is not observed. Use $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$.

$$
E_1 = \frac{h^2}{8 m L^2} = \frac{\left( 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \right)^2}{8 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 1.0^2}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 5.5 \cdot 10^{-67}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.10 \cdot 1.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.050\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
n^2 E_1 = K
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
n = \sqrt{\frac{K}{E_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.050}{5.5 \cdot 10^{-67}}} \approx 3.0 \cdot 10^{32}
$$
$$
E_{n+1} - E_n = \left( (n + 1)^2 - n^2 \right) E_1 = (2n + 1)\, E_1 \approx 2 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{32} \cdot 5.5 \cdot 10^{-67}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3.3 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ball moves freely between the walls, so the formula for the **energy levels in a box** applies with $m = 0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $L = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The lowest energy level is about $5.5 \cdot 10^{-67}\,\mathrm{J}$, an extremely small value. The ball’s energy is entirely kinetic. Equating it to $n^2 E_1$ gives a quantum number of order $3 \cdot 10^{32}$.

The spacing between neighboring levels follows by expanding $(n + 1)^2 - n^2 = 2n + 1$. Since $n$ is enormous, the spacing is approximately $2n E_1 \approx 3.3 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J}$. This difference is about $10^{-32}$ times the energy of the ball, so no measurement can distinguish adjacent levels and the energy appears continuous. For macroscopic objects, energy quantization **is not observable**, and quantum mechanics gives the same results as classical mechanics.

</div>
