## Exercise 1

In a hydrogen atom, the electron occupies the Bohr orbit with $n = 1$ and radius $r_1 = 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the magnitude of the Coulomb force between the electron and the proton, the centripetal acceleration, and the speed of the electron. Then verify that the product $m_e v r_1$ equals $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$. Use $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}$, $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, and $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="0,2,1.4,2,v;0,2,0,1.0,F" punti="0,0,pieno,p;0,2,pieno,e" segmenti="0,0,1.41,-1.41" etichette="0.85,-0.45,r₁" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.6 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.6 titolo="The electron moves around the proton in an orbit of radius $r_1$. The Coulomb force $\vec{F}$ is directed toward the center and is perpendicular to the velocity $\vec{v}$"}

$$
F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{e^2}{r_1^2} ,
\qquad
a = \frac{F}{m_e} ,
\qquad
a = \frac{v^2}{r_1}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
v = \sqrt{a\, r_1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{\left( 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \right)^2}{\left( 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11} \right)^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 8.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
a &= \frac{8.19 \cdot 10^{-8}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 9.0 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \\[6pt]
v &= \sqrt{8.99 \cdot 10^{22} \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.2 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
m_e v r_1 = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 2.18 \cdot 10^{6} \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s} \approx 1.05 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s} ,
\qquad
\frac{h}{2\pi} = \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s} \approx 1.05 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The proton and electron have charges $+e$ and $-e$. They therefore attract each other by the Coulomb force. The force on the electron has magnitude $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \dfrac{e^2}{r_1^2}$ and points toward the proton. In the Bohr model the electron moves in a circular orbit at constant speed, so the Coulomb force provides the centripetal force. By Newton’s second law, the acceleration is the force divided by the electron’s mass and is approximately $9.0 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

In uniform circular motion the centripetal acceleration is $\dfrac{v^2}{r_1}$, so the speed is the square root of the product $a\, r_1$, about $2.2 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. This is less than $1\%$ of the speed of light, so the use of classical mechanics is justified. Finally, the product $m_e v r_1$ agrees with $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$ within rounding error. Thus the orbit satisfies **Bohr’s second postulate** with $n = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate the radius, speed, and orbital period of the electron in the $n = 3$ Bohr orbit of hydrogen, given the Bohr radius $r_1 = 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$. Use $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$.

$$
r_n = n^2 r_1 ,
\qquad
m_e v_n r_n = n \frac{h}{2\pi}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
v_n = \frac{n h}{2\pi m_e r_n} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi r_n}{v_n}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_3 &= 9 \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 4.8 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
v_3 &= \frac{3 \cdot 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{2\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 4.77 \cdot 10^{-10}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 7.3 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 4.77 \cdot 10^{-10}}{7.29 \cdot 10^{5}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 4.1 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From the formula for the **radii of the Bohr orbits**, the radius of orbit $n$ is $n^2 r_1$. For $n = 3$, the radius is therefore nine times the Bohr radius. The speed follows from the second postulate, $m_e v_n r_n = n \dfrac{h}{2\pi}$, by dividing both sides by $m_e r_n$.

The result is about one-third of the speed in orbit $n = 1$, which equals $2.2 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, in agreement with $v_n = \dfrac{v_1}{n}$. The period is the time taken to cover the circumference $2\pi r_3$ at speed $v_3$. It is about $4.1 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{s}$, so the electron makes about $2.4 \cdot 10^{14}$ revolutions per second.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The electron in a hydrogen atom has total energy $E = -0.544\,\mathrm{eV}$. Find the quantum number $n$ of its orbit, the orbital radius, the kinetic and potential energies of the electron, and its speed. Use $r_1 = 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, and $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$.

$$
E_n = -\frac{13.6\,\mathrm{eV}}{n^2}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
n^2 = \frac{13.6}{0.544} = 25
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
n = 5
$$
$$
r_5 = 25\, r_1 = 25 \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
K = -E = 0.544\,\mathrm{eV} ,
\qquad
U = 2E = -1.09\,\mathrm{eV}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} m_e v^2
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2K}{m_e}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 0.544 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 4.37 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

According to the expression for the **energy levels** of hydrogen, the energy in orbit $n$ is $-\dfrac{13.6\,\mathrm{eV}}{n^2}$. Setting this equal to the given energy gives $n^2 = 25$, so the electron is in orbit $n = 5$, with radius $25 r_1$.

In the Bohr model the kinetic energy is the negative of the total energy, and the potential energy is twice the total energy. These relations follow from the equation of circular motion with the Coulomb force. Therefore $K = 0.544\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $U = -1.09\,\mathrm{eV}$, and their sum $K + U$ gives $E = -0.544\,\mathrm{eV}$. To calculate the speed, convert the kinetic energy to joules and solve $K = \dfrac{1}{2} m_e v^2$ for speed. The result, $4.37 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, is one-fifth of $v_1 \approx 2.2 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, as predicted by $v_n = \dfrac{v_1}{n}$.

</div>
