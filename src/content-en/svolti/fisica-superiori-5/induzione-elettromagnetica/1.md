## Exercise 1

A square loop with side length $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and resistance $0.20\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ is perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field. The field magnitude increases uniformly from $0.15\,\mathrm{T}$ to $0.45\,\mathrm{T}$ in $50\,\mathrm{ms}$. Calculate the initial and final flux linkage, the induced emf, and the induced current.

$$
\Phi_B = B S , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}_m| = \frac{|\Delta\Phi_B|}{\Delta t} , \qquad
i = \frac{|\mathcal{E}_m|}{R}
$$
$$
|\mathcal{E}_m| = \frac{(B_2 - B_1)\,S}{\Delta t} , \qquad
i = \frac{(B_2 - B_1)\,S}{R\,\Delta t}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 0.12^2\,\mathrm{m^2} = 1.44 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
\Phi_1 &= 0.15 \cdot 1.44 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 2.2 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
\Phi_2 &= 0.45 \cdot 1.44 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 6.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}_m| &= \frac{0.30 \cdot 1.44 \cdot 10^{-2}}{0.050}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 8.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i &= \frac{8.64 \cdot 10^{-2}}{0.20}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.43\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is uniform and perpendicular to the loop, so the angle between the field and the normal is $0^\circ$ and the **flux linkage** is the product $B S$. The area of the loop is the square of the side length in meters, $0.12^2\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

The flux changes because the field magnitude changes while the area remains constant. The change in flux is therefore $(B_2 - B_1)\,S$. By **Faraday’s law**, the magnitude of the average emf is this change divided by the time interval, which must be expressed in seconds, $50\,\mathrm{ms} = 0.050\,\mathrm{s}$. The field increases uniformly, so the emf is constant throughout the interval and equals the average emf.

The loop is closed and has resistance $0.20\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. By Ohm’s law, the induced current is the emf divided by the resistance, or about $0.43\,\mathrm{A}$. The current flows only during the $50\,\mathrm{ms}$ in which the field changes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A coil of $150$ circular turns of radius $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is in a uniform magnetic field of $0.25\,\mathrm{T}$. The axis of the coil makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the field.

a) Calculate the flux linkage of the coil.

b) The field decreases uniformly to zero in $0.12\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the magnitude of the induced emf.

c) Instead of dropping to zero, the field reverses direction and returns to a magnitude of $0.25\,\mathrm{T}$ in the same time interval of $0.12\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the magnitude of the induced emf.

$$
\Phi_B = N B\,\pi r^2 \cos\theta , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}_m| = \frac{|\Delta\Phi_B|}{\Delta t}
$$
$$
\text{b)}\ \ \Delta\Phi_B = 0 - \Phi_B = -\Phi_B , \qquad
\text{c)}\ \ \Delta\Phi_B = -\Phi_B - \Phi_B = -2\,\Phi_B
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_B &= 150 \cdot 0.25 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.030^2 \cdot \cos 30^\circ\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 9.2 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}_b| &= \frac{9.18 \cdot 10^{-2}}{0.12}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 0.77\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}_c| &= \frac{2 \cdot 9.18 \cdot 10^{-2}}{0.12}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 1.5\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis of the coil is perpendicular to the plane of the turns and therefore points along the normal. The angle between the field and the normal is thus $\theta = 30^\circ$. The flux linkage of a coil of $N$ turns is $N$ times the flux through one turn, and each turn has area $\pi r^2$.

In part b), the final flux is zero, so the magnitude of the change in flux equals the initial flux. By **Faraday’s law**, the magnitude of the emf is $\dfrac{9.18 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}}{0.12\,\mathrm{s}} \approx 0.77\,\mathrm{V}$.

In part c), the field reverses direction. The angle between the new field and the normal is $180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$, and $\cos 150^\circ = -\cos 30^\circ$, so the final flux is $-\Phi_B$. The flux change is therefore $-2\,\Phi_B$, twice as large as in part b), and the emf is also **twice as large**, about $1.5\,\mathrm{V}$. Reversing the field produces a larger emf than reducing it to zero in the same time.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A flexible conducting ring of resistance $0.40\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ is perpendicular to a uniform, constant magnetic field of $0.80\,\mathrm{T}$. The ring is drawn inward so that its radius changes from $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ in $0.25\,\mathrm{s}$, while its shape remains circular. Calculate the average induced emf and average induced current, and determine whether the field produced inside the ring by the induced current points in the same direction as the external field or in the opposite direction.

$$
\Phi_B = B\,\pi r^2 , \qquad
\Delta\Phi_B = B\,\pi \left( r_2^2 - r_1^2 \right) , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}_m| = \frac{|\Delta\Phi_B|}{\Delta t} , \qquad
i_m = \frac{|\mathcal{E}_m|}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi \left( r_1^2 - r_2^2 \right) &= \pi \left( 0.10^2 - 0.060^2 \right)\,\mathrm{m^2} \approx 2.01 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
|\Delta\Phi_B| &= 0.80 \cdot 2.01 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 1.61 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}_m| &= \frac{1.61 \cdot 10^{-2}}{0.25}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 6.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i_m &= \frac{6.43 \cdot 10^{-2}}{0.40}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.16\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In this exercise the field is constant, and the flux changes because the **area** of the ring changes. The field is perpendicular to the ring, so the flux is $B\,\pi r^2$ and its change is the product of the field and the change in area. The final area is smaller than the initial area, so the flux decreases.

By **Faraday’s law**, the magnitude of the average emf is the magnitude of the change in flux divided by $0.25\,\mathrm{s}$, or about $64\,\mathrm{mV}$. We use the average emf because the problem does not specify how the radius changes from instant to instant. Dividing by the resistance gives the average current, about $0.16\,\mathrm{A}$.

The flux decreases. By **Lenz’s law**, the induced current therefore produces a field inside the ring that opposes this decrease. The induced field thus points **in the same direction** as the external field.

</div>
