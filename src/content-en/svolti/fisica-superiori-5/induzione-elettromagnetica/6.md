## Exercise 1

A square loop of side $\ell = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and resistance $R = 0.050\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ moves to the right at constant speed $v = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and enters a region of width $d = 25\,\mathrm{cm}$. The region contains a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ directed into the page and perpendicular to the loop. Outside the region the field is zero. The right side of the loop begins to enter the region at $t = 0$.

a) Calculate the emf and the current while the loop is entering the region, and state the direction of the current.

b) Plot the current as a function of time from $t = 0$ until the loop has completely left the region.

c) Calculate the energy dissipated in the loop during its entire passage through the region.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="3,0 8,0 8,3 3,3" linee="1.4,0.5 3.4,0.5 3.4,2.5 1.4,2.5 1.4,0.5" vettori="3.6,1.5,4.8,1.5,v;3.2,2.5,1.9,2.5,i" etichette="3.4,0.2,⊗;4.4,0.2,⊗;5.4,0.2,⊗;6.4,0.2,⊗;7.4,0.2,⊗;4.4,2.6,⊗;5.4,2.6,⊗;6.4,2.6,⊗;7.4,2.6,⊗;5.4,1.4,⊗;6.4,1.4,⊗;7.4,1.4,⊗;1.6,1.4,ℓ;5.2,3.25,d;7.1,3.25,B ⊗" xmin=0.5 xmax=8.5 ymin=-0.3 ymax=3.6 titolo="The loop of side $\ell$ moves with velocity $\vec{v}$ into a region of width $d$. The field points into the page ($\otimes$). As the loop enters, the induced current $i$ flows counterclockwise"}

$$
\mathcal{E} = B \ell v , \qquad
i = \frac{B \ell v}{R} , \qquad
t_{\text{entry}} = \frac{\ell}{v}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E} &= 0.40 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{V} = 0.10\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i &= \frac{0.10}{0.050}\,\mathrm{A} = 2.0\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
t_{\text{entry}} &= \frac{0.10}{2.5}\,\mathrm{s} = 0.040\,\mathrm{s} = 40\,\mathrm{ms}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
i(t) =
\begin{cases}
2.0\,\mathrm{A} \ \text{(counterclockwise)} & \text{if } 0 < t < 40\,\mathrm{ms} \\[6pt]
0 & \text{if } 40\,\mathrm{ms} < t < 100\,\mathrm{ms} \\[6pt]
2.0\,\mathrm{A} \ \text{(clockwise)} & \text{if } 100\,\mathrm{ms} < t < 140\,\mathrm{ms}
\end{cases}
$$

::grafico{f="x<0||x>14?0:(x<4?2:(x<10?0:-2))" xmin=-2 xmax=16 ymin=-3 ymax=3 nomi="t,i" legenda="i(t)" titolo="Current in the loop as a function of time $t$ in hundredths of a second, with counterclockwise current taken as positive. The current is $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ on entry, zero when the entire loop is in the field, and $-2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ on exit"}

$$
Q = 2\,R\,i^2\,t_{\text{entry}} = 2 \cdot 0.050 \cdot 2.0^2 \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{J} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As the loop enters, only its right side is in the field. This side has length $\ell$, moves with velocity $v$, and is perpendicular to both the velocity and the field. The formula for the **emf across a moving rod** therefore gives $\mathcal{E} = B \ell v = 0.10\,\mathrm{V}$. The change in flux gives the same result because the area of the loop inside the field increases by $\ell v$ per second. The current is $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Entry takes the time needed for the loop to travel one side length, $40\,\mathrm{ms}$.

On entry, the flux into the page increases. By **Lenz’s law**, the induced field inside the loop therefore points out of the page and the current flows counterclockwise.

When the loop is entirely in the field, the flux is constant and the current is zero. This stage lasts until the right side reaches the opposite boundary of the region, having traveled a distance $d = 25\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the entry point: $\dfrac{0.25}{2.5}\,\mathrm{s} = 100\,\mathrm{ms}$. Then the loop begins to leave and the flux into the page decreases. The induced field points into the page and the current flows clockwise with the same magnitude as on entry, because now the left side is moving in the field. Exit also takes $40\,\mathrm{ms}$ and ends at $140\,\mathrm{ms}$.

Current flows only during entry and exit, for a total of $80\,\mathrm{ms}$, so the energy dissipated by Joule heating is $R i^2$ multiplied by $2 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{ms}$, giving $16\,\mathrm{mJ}$. The velocity is constant, so this energy equals the work done by the external force pushing the loop.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular coil of $20$ turns has sides of lengths $\ell = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $w = 8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a total resistance of $R = 0.50\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. It is pushed at constant speed $v = 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the direction of side $w$ into a very large region where the magnetic field is uniform, has magnitude $0.60\,\mathrm{T}$, and is perpendicular to the plane of the coil.

a) Calculate the emf and the current during entry.

b) Calculate the force required to maintain constant velocity and the work it does during entry.

c) Use two methods to calculate the charge that passes through the coil during entry.

$$
\mathcal{E} = N B \ell v , \qquad
I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R} , \qquad
F = N I \ell B , \qquad
W = F w
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E} &= 20 \cdot 0.60 \cdot 0.050 \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{V} = 0.48\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{0.48}{0.50}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.96\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
F &= 20 \cdot 0.96 \cdot 0.050 \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.58\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
W &= 0.576 \cdot 0.080\,\mathrm{J} \approx 4.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
q = I\,\frac{w}{v} = 0.96 \cdot \frac{0.080}{0.80}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 9.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{C} , \qquad
q = \frac{N B \ell w}{R} = \frac{20 \cdot 0.60 \cdot 0.050 \cdot 0.080}{0.50}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 9.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{C}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As the coil enters, the side of length $\ell$, perpendicular to the velocity, is in the field. In each turn this side produces an emf $B \ell v$. The $N$ turns are in series, so the emf of the coil is $N B \ell v = 0.48\,\mathrm{V}$. The current is $0.96\,\mathrm{A}$.

The side of each turn within the field experiences a magnetic force $I \ell B$, so the force on the coil is $N I \ell B \approx 0.58\,\mathrm{N}$. By Lenz’s law this force opposes entry. The velocity is constant, so the external force has the same magnitude. Entry is complete when the coil has traveled the side length $w$, so the work $W$ done by the external force is $F w \approx 46\,\mathrm{mJ}$. The **energy balance** requires this work to equal the energy dissipated in the coil, and indeed $R I^2 \cdot \dfrac{w}{v} = 0.50 \cdot 0.96^2 \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{J} \approx 46\,\mathrm{mJ}$.

First calculate the charge by multiplying the constant current by the time taken to enter the field, $\dfrac{w}{v} = 0.10\,\mathrm{s}$. Then use the formula for the **charge passing through the circuit**. The flux linkage goes from zero to $N B \ell w$, so the charge is $\dfrac{N B \ell w}{R}$. Both approaches give the same value, about $96\,\mathrm{mC}$.

</div>
