## Exercise 1

In a cylindrical region of radius $a = 8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the magnetic field is uniform and parallel to the axis and increases at a constant rate $\dfrac{dB}{dt} = 0.50\,\mathrm{T/s}$.

a) Calculate the magnitude of the induced electric field at a distance of $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis and at the edge of the region.

b) A copper ring of radius $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and resistance $2.0\,\mathrm{m\Omega}$ is coaxial with this region. Calculate the emf induced in the ring, the current, and the power dissipated.

$$
E \cdot 2\pi r = \pi r^2\,\frac{dB}{dt}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E = \frac{r}{2}\,\frac{dB}{dt} \qquad (r \leq a)
$$
$$
\mathcal{E} = \Gamma(\vec{E}) = \pi r^2\,\frac{dB}{dt} , \qquad
i = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R} , \qquad
P = \frac{\mathcal{E}^2}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E(4.0\,\mathrm{cm}) &= \frac{0.040}{2} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{V/m} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
E(a) &= \frac{0.080}{2} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{V/m} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
\mathcal{E} &= \pi \cdot 0.040^2 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{V} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i &= \frac{2.51 \cdot 10^{-3}}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 1.3\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
P &= \frac{\left( 2.51 \cdot 10^{-3} \right)^2}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{W} \approx 3.2 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The magnetic field is uniform in a circular region and the configuration is symmetric about its axis. The **induced electric field** lines are therefore circles centered on this axis. The field is tangent to each circle and has constant magnitude along it. Its circulation around the circle of radius $r$ is therefore $E \cdot 2\pi r$. The entire disk of radius $r \leq a$ lies inside the region, so the flux through it is $B\,\pi r^2$ and changes at the rate $\pi r^2\,\dfrac{dB}{dt}$. By the Faraday–Neumann–Lenz law, the magnitudes of the circulation and this rate are equal, giving $E = \dfrac{r}{2}\,\dfrac{dB}{dt}$.

The induced field is proportional to the distance from the axis, so at $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ its magnitude is $10\,\mathrm{mV/m}$. At the edge, twice as far from the axis, it is twice as large, $20\,\mathrm{mV/m}$.

The emf in the ring is the circulation of the induced field around the ring, $\pi r^2\,\dfrac{dB}{dt} \approx 2.5\,\mathrm{mV}$. Express the resistance in ohms, $2.0\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. The current is then about $1.3\,\mathrm{A}$ and the power dissipated is about $3.2\,\mathrm{mW}$. An emf of a few millivolts produces a current of more than one ampere because the resistance of the copper is very low. For the same reason, **eddy currents** in solid pieces of metal are large.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A square loop of side $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and resistance $0.40\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ is at rest in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the loop and directed into the page. The field magnitude varies over time as shown in the graph. It increases uniformly from $0$ to $0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ between $t = 0$ and $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, remains constant until $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and decreases uniformly to zero between $t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

::grafico{f="x<0?NaN:(x<=2?2*x:(x<=4?4:(x<=5?4-4*(x-4):0)))" punti="2,4,pieno;4,4,pieno" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=5 nomi="t,B" legenda="B(t)" titolo="Field magnitude $B$ in tenths of a tesla as a function of time $t$ in seconds"}

1. Calculate the maximum flux through the loop and the emf and induced current in each of the three stages. Determine the direction of the current and plot $i(t)$.
2. Calculate the charge that passes through the loop during the first and third stages.
3. Calculate the energy dissipated in the loop between $t = 0$ and $t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}$.
4. The wire in the loop can carry a maximum current of $25\,\mathrm{mA}$. Determine the minimum duration of the stage in which the field decreases, assuming that it still decreases uniformly from $0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ to zero.

**Part 1**

$$
\Phi_{\max} = B_{\max} S , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}| = \frac{|\Delta\Phi_B|}{\Delta t} , \qquad
i = \frac{|\mathcal{E}|}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 0.20^2\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.040\,\mathrm{m^2} , \qquad
\Phi_{\max} = 0.40 \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{Wb} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
\text{phase 1:} &\quad |\mathcal{E}_1| = \frac{1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}}{2.0}\,\mathrm{V} = 8.0\,\mathrm{mV} , \quad i_1 = \frac{8.0\,\mathrm{mV}}{0.40\,\mathrm{\Omega}} = 20\,\mathrm{mA} \ \text{(counterclockwise)} \\[6pt]
\text{phase 2:} &\quad \mathcal{E}_2 = 0 , \quad i_2 = 0 \\[6pt]
\text{phase 3:} &\quad |\mathcal{E}_3| = \frac{1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}}{1.0}\,\mathrm{V} = 16\,\mathrm{mV} , \quad i_3 = \frac{16\,\mathrm{mV}}{0.40\,\mathrm{\Omega}} = 40\,\mathrm{mA} \ \text{(clockwise)}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x<0||x>5?0:(x<2?20:(x<4?0:-40))" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6 ymin=-60 ymax=40 nomi="t,i" legenda="i(t)" titolo="Induced current $i$ in $\mathrm{mA}$ as a function of time $t$ in seconds, with counterclockwise current taken as positive. The current is $20\,\mathrm{mA}$ in the first stage, zero in the second, and $-40\,\mathrm{mA}$ in the third"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is uniform and perpendicular to the loop, so the flux linkage is $B S$. Its maximum value, reached when $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$, is $1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Wb}$.

The field changes uniformly during each stage, so the emf is constant. By **Faraday’s law**, its magnitude equals the magnitude of the change in flux divided by the duration of the stage. In the first stage the flux increases by $\Phi_{\max}$ in $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, giving an emf of $8.0\,\mathrm{mV}$. In the second the flux is constant and the emf is zero. In the third the flux decreases by $\Phi_{\max}$ in $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$, half the time, so the magnitude of the emf is twice as large, $16\,\mathrm{mV}$. Divide by $0.40\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ to obtain the currents.

Apply **Lenz’s law** to find the direction. In the first stage the flux into the page increases, so the induced field points out of the page and the current flows counterclockwise. In the third stage the flux into the page decreases, so the induced field points into the page, in the same direction as the external field, and the current flows clockwise. In the graph of $i(t)$, the current in the third stage therefore has the opposite sign and twice the magnitude of the current in the first stage.

</div>

**Part 2**

$$
q = \frac{|\Delta\Phi_B|}{R} , \qquad
q_1 = q_3 = \frac{\Phi_{\max}}{R} = \frac{1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}}{0.40}\,\mathrm{C} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{C}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In each stage the flux changes in only one direction, so the formula for the **charge passing through the circuit** applies. The change in flux has the same magnitude, $\Phi_{\max}$, in the first and third stages, so the charges have the same magnitude, $40\,\mathrm{mC}$, but flow in opposite directions. The current provides a check: $20\,\mathrm{mA} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{s} = 40\,\mathrm{mC}$ and $40\,\mathrm{mA} \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{s} = 40\,\mathrm{mC}$. In the third stage the current is twice as large but lasts half as long, so the same amount of charge passes.

</div>

**Part 3**

$$
W_{\text{diss}} = R\,i_1^2\,\Delta t_1 + R\,i_3^2\,\Delta t_3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\text{diss}} &= 0.40 \cdot \left( 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \right)^2 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{J} + 0.40 \cdot \left( 4.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \right)^2 \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&= 3.2 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J} + 6.4 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&= 9.6 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The current is constant during each stage, so the energy dissipated by Joule heating in a stage is the power $R i^2$ multiplied by the duration of the stage. Let $W_{\text{diss}}$ denote the energy. In the second stage the current is zero and no energy is dissipated. The third stage dissipates twice as much energy as the first even though it lasts half as long, because power is proportional to the *square* of the current and the current has doubled. The total energy is $0.96\,\mathrm{mJ}$.

</div>

**Part 4**

$$
i = \frac{\Phi_{\max}}{R\,\Delta t} \leq i_{\max}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta t \geq \frac{\Phi_{\max}}{R\,i_{\max}}
$$
$$
\Delta t_{\min} = \frac{1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}}{0.40 \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.6\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If the field decreases uniformly from $0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ to zero over the interval $\Delta t$, the current is constant and equals $\dfrac{\Phi_{\max}}{R\,\Delta t}$. The current is inversely proportional to the time taken for the decrease, so the faster the field decreases, the larger the current. The condition $i \leq 25\,\mathrm{mA}$ is therefore equivalent to $\Delta t \geq 1.6\,\mathrm{s}$.

If the decrease takes $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$, as stated in the problem, the current is $40\,\mathrm{mA}$, which exceeds $25\,\mathrm{mA}$. The wire could not carry this current during that stage. In the first stage, by contrast, the current is $20\,\mathrm{mA}$, below the limit. The decrease must take **at least** $1.6\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
