## Exercise 1

Two horizontal conducting rails a distance $\ell = 40\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart are connected by a resistor $R = 2.0\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. A uniform magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ is perpendicular to the plane of the rails and points out of the page. A conducting rod perpendicular to the rails moves to the right with constant speed $v = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The resistances of the rails and rod are negligible. Calculate the induced emf and the current, and find the direction of the current in the rod.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,2.4 7,2.4;0,0 7,0;0,2.4 0,1.9 0.3,1.8 -0.3,1.6 0.3,1.4 -0.3,1.2 0.3,1.0 -0.3,0.8 0.3,0.6 0,0.5 0,0" poligoni="3.92,-0.3 4.08,-0.3 4.08,2.7 3.92,2.7" vettori="4.2,1.8,5.4,1.8,v;3.8,0.6,2.6,0.6,F;1.6,2.7,2.8,2.7,I;4.25,1.5,4.25,0.9" etichette="-0.8,1.2,R;1.2,1.7,⊙;2.4,1.7,⊙;1.2,0.4,⊙;5.6,0.6,⊙;6.4,1.7,⊙;6.4,0.6,⊙;4.4,1.15,I;4.15,-0.45,ℓ;6.4,2.8,B ⊙" xmin=-1.2 xmax=7.2 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.2 titolo="A rod moving to the right in a field directed out of the page ($\odot$). The current $I$ flows down the rod and clockwise around the circuit, and the magnetic force $\vec{F}$ on the rod points to the left"}

$$
\mathcal{E} = B \ell v , \qquad
I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R} = \frac{B \ell v}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E} &= 0.50 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{V} = 0.60\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{0.60}{2.0}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.30\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod, its velocity, and the field are mutually perpendicular, and the velocity is constant, so the formula for the **emf across a moving rod** applies. Before substituting, convert the length to meters, $\ell = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. The emf is $0.60\,\mathrm{V}$ and, since the resistance of the circuit is $2.0\,\mathrm{\Omega}$, the current is $0.30\,\mathrm{A}$.

To find the direction, consider the Lorentz force on a positive charge in the rod. The velocity points to the right and the field points out of the page. With the fingers of the right hand pointing along $\vec{v}$ and curling toward $\vec{B}$, the cross product $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ points toward the bottom of the page. Positive charges are pushed downward, so the current flows **down** the rod and clockwise around the circuit.

**Lenz’s law** confirms the result. The circuit area increases, so the outward flux increases and the induced field inside the circuit points into the page. A clockwise current produces precisely this inward field.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rod of length $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ slides without friction on two rails connected by a $1.2\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ resistor. A uniform magnetic field of $0.80\,\mathrm{T}$ is perpendicular to the plane of the rails and points into the page. An external force keeps the rod moving to the right at a constant speed of $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The resistances of the rails and rod are negligible.

a) Calculate the induced emf and the current.

b) Calculate the magnitude of the external force and state its direction.

c) Verify that the power supplied by the external force equals the power dissipated in the resistor.

$$
\mathcal{E} = B \ell v , \qquad
I = \frac{B \ell v}{R} , \qquad
F_{\text{ext}} = F_m = I \ell B , \qquad
P_{\text{mech}} = F_{\text{ext}}\,v , \qquad
P_{\text{diss}} = R I^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E} &= 0.80 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{V} = 1.2\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{1.2}{1.2}\,\mathrm{A} = 1.0\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
F_{\text{ext}} &= 1.0 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{N} = 0.24\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
P_{\text{mech}} &= 0.24 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{W} = 1.2\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
P_{\text{diss}} &= 1.2 \cdot 1.0^2\,\mathrm{W} = 1.2\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From the formula for the **emf across a moving rod**, the emf is $B \ell v = 1.2\,\mathrm{V}$ and the current is $1.0\,\mathrm{A}$. The field points into the page and the rod moves to the right, so the cross product $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ points toward the top of the page. The current therefore flows up the rod and counterclockwise around the circuit.

The rod is a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field, so it experiences the force $I\,\vec{\ell} \times \vec{B}$, with $\vec{\ell}$ pointing upward along the current. By the right-hand rule this force points to the left, opposing the motion as Lenz’s law predicts. Its magnitude is $I \ell B = 0.24\,\mathrm{N}$. The velocity is constant, so the net force is zero and the external force has magnitude $0.24\,\mathrm{N}$ and points **to the right**.

The power supplied by a force parallel to the velocity is the product $F v$, which is $1.2\,\mathrm{W}$. The power dissipated in the resistor by Joule heating is $R I^2 = 1.2\,\mathrm{W}$. The two powers are equal, as the **energy balance** requires, because the work done by the external force becomes electrical energy and then heat in the resistor.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rod of resistance $r = 0.10\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ and length $\ell = 50\,\mathrm{cm}$ slides at constant speed $v = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along two rails of negligible resistance connected by a resistor $R = 0.40\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. The magnetic field is uniform, has magnitude $0.20\,\mathrm{T}$, and is perpendicular to the plane of the rails. Friction is negligible.

a) Calculate the current and the potential difference across the resistor.

b) Calculate the power supplied by the external force that maintains the constant velocity and the power dissipated in the resistor and in the rod.

$$
\mathcal{E} = B \ell v , \qquad
I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R + r} , \qquad
\Delta V_R = R I , \qquad
F = I \ell B , \qquad
P_{\text{mech}} = F v
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E} &= 0.20 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{V} = 0.25\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{0.25}{0.40 + 0.10}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.50\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_R &= 0.40 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{V} = 0.20\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
F &= 0.50 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N} = 0.050\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
P_{\text{mech}} &= 0.050 \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{W} = 0.125\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
P_R &= 0.40 \cdot 0.50^2\,\mathrm{W} = 0.10\,\mathrm{W} , \qquad
P_r = 0.10 \cdot 0.50^2\,\mathrm{W} = 0.025\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The moving rod acts as a source of emf $B \ell v = 0.25\,\mathrm{V}$ with internal resistance $r$. The same current flows through the resistor and the rod, so they are in series and the total circuit resistance is $R + r = 0.50\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. The current is therefore $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$.

The potential difference across the resistor is $R I = 0.20\,\mathrm{V}$, which is less than the emf. The difference, $0.05\,\mathrm{V}$, is the voltage drop $r I$ inside the rod, as in a real voltage source.

The magnetic force on the rod has magnitude $I \ell B = 0.050\,\mathrm{N}$. The external force balances it because the velocity is constant. The power supplied by the external force is $0.125\,\mathrm{W}$. Of this power, $0.10\,\mathrm{W}$ is dissipated in the resistor and $0.025\,\mathrm{W}$ in the rod. Their sum, $0.125\,\mathrm{W}$, equals the mechanical power, in agreement with the **energy balance**.

</div>
