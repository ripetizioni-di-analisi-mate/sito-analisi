## Exercise 1

A circular loop lies in the plane of the page in a uniform magnetic field directed into the page ($\otimes$). As viewed by someone looking at the page, determine the direction of the induced current in the loop when the field magnitude
a) increases;
b) decreases.

$$
\text{a)}\ \ \Phi_B \text{ into the page increases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{B}_{\text{ind}} \text{ out of the page } (\odot)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{counterclockwise}
$$
$$
\text{b)}\ \ \Phi_B \text{ into the page decreases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{B}_{\text{ind}} \text{ into the page } (\otimes)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{clockwise}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply **Lenz’s law** in three steps, determining in order how the flux changes, which way the induced field points, and which way the current flows.

In part a), the field into the page increases, so the flux into the page through the loop increases. The field produced by the induced current opposes this increase, so it points out of the page inside the loop. With the right thumb pointing toward the observer, out of the page, the fingers curl counterclockwise. The induced current therefore flows **counterclockwise**.

In part b), the flux into the page decreases. The induced field opposes this decrease, so it points into the page, in the same direction as the external field. With the thumb pointing into the page, the fingers curl clockwise, so the current flows **clockwise**. The current directions in the two cases are opposite because the changes in flux are opposite, even though the external field points in the same direction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bar magnet lies on the axis of a conducting ring with its south pole facing the ring. The magnet is moved away from the ring along the axis. Determine the direction of the induced current in the ring as viewed from the magnet, and explain whether the ring attracts or repels the magnet.

$$
\Phi_B \text{ decreases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{B}_{\text{ind}} \text{ in the same direction as } \vec{B}_{\text{magnet}} \text{ (toward the magnet)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{counterclockwise}
$$
$$
\text{side of the ring facing the magnet} = \text{north pole}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{attraction}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Magnetic field lines leave a magnet’s north pole and enter its south pole. Near the south pole the field therefore points toward the magnet, so it passes through the ring in the direction of the magnet. As the magnet moves away, the field at the ring becomes weaker and the flux **decreases**.

By **Lenz’s law**, the induced field opposes the decrease, so inside the ring it points in the same direction as the magnet’s field, toward the magnet. An observer on the magnet’s side sees this field pointing toward them. With the right thumb pointing toward the observer, the fingers curl counterclockwise, so the induced current flows **counterclockwise**.

The induced field emerges from the side of the ring facing the magnet, so that side acts as a north pole. A north pole and a south pole attract, so the ring **attracts** the magnet and opposes its motion away from the ring. This result is consistent with conservation of energy because work must be done to move the magnet away, and this work supplies the energy of the induced current.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A very long, straight wire lies in the plane of the page and carries an increasing current $I$ to the right. A rectangular loop lies above the wire in the same plane, with two of its sides parallel to the wire. Determine the direction of the induced current in the loop and explain whether the wire attracts or repels the loop.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 7,0;2,1 5,1 5,2.6 2,2.6 2,1" vettori="0.5,-0.3,1.9,-0.3,I;3,2.6,4.2,2.6,i;4.2,1,3,1;5,2.1,5,1.5;2,1.5,2,2.1" etichette="0.8,1.6,⊙;6.2,1.6,⊙;0.8,0.5,⊙;6.2,0.5,⊙;0.8,2.7,⊙;6.2,2.7,⊙;3.2,1.7,⊗ B′;5.9,3.1,B ⊙" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.4 titolo="Above the wire carrying current $I$, the field points out of the page ($\odot$). When $I$ increases, a current $i$ flows clockwise in the loop and produces a field $\vec{B}\,'$ into the page inside the loop"}

$$
\vec{B}_{\text{wire}} \text{ above the wire: out of the page} , \qquad
I \text{ increases} \ \Longrightarrow\ \Phi_B \text{ out of the page increases}
$$
$$
\vec{B}_{\text{ind}} \text{ into the page}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{clockwise}
$$
$$
\text{near side: current opposite to } I \ \Longrightarrow\ \text{repulsion (dominates)} , \qquad
\text{far side: current in the same direction} \ \Longrightarrow\ \text{attraction}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the **right-hand rule** to find the field of the straight wire. With the thumb pointing in the direction of the current, to the right, the fingers curl out of the page above the wire and into the page below it. The field therefore points out of the page at the loop. The field of a wire is proportional to its current, so as $I$ increases, the outward flux through the loop increases.

By **Lenz’s law**, the induced field inside the loop opposes the increase in outward flux and therefore points into the page. With the thumb pointing into the page, the fingers curl clockwise, so the induced current flows **clockwise**: to the right along the top of the loop and to the left along the bottom.

Two parallel wires attract if their currents flow in the same direction and repel if the currents flow in opposite directions, as discussed in the chapter on sources of magnetic fields. The current on the lower side of the loop flows opposite to $I$, so this side is repelled. The current on the upper side flows in the same direction, so this side is attracted. The field is stronger closer to the wire, so the force on the lower side is greater and the loop is **repelled**. By moving away, the loop reduces the increase in flux, in accordance with Lenz’s law.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A circular loop of radius $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and resistance $0.10\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ lies in the plane of the page in a uniform magnetic field directed out of the page. The magnitude of the field decreases uniformly from $0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ to $0.10\,\mathrm{T}$ in $0.30\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the magnitude of the induced current and determine its direction.

$$
\Phi_B = B\,\pi r^2 , \qquad
i = \frac{1}{R}\,\frac{|\Delta\Phi_B|}{\Delta t} = \frac{(B_1 - B_2)\,\pi r^2}{R\,\Delta t}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_1 &= 0.40 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.050^2\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 3.14 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
\Phi_2 &= 0.10 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.050^2\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 0.79 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}| &= \frac{(3.14 - 0.79) \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.30}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 7.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i &= \frac{7.85 \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.10}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 7.9 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\Phi_B \text{ out of the page decreases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{B}_{\text{ind}} \text{ out of the page}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{counterclockwise}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is uniform and perpendicular to the loop, so the flux is $B\,\pi r^2$. The field decreases uniformly, so the emf is constant. By **Faraday’s law**, its magnitude is given by the magnitude of the change in flux divided by the time interval. This gives about $7.9\,\mathrm{mV}$, and Ohm’s law then gives an induced current of about $79\,\mathrm{mA}$.

The outward flux decreases. By **Lenz’s law**, the induced field opposes this decrease, so it points out of the page inside the loop. With the thumb pointing toward the observer, the fingers curl counterclockwise, so the current flows **counterclockwise**.

</div>
