## Exercise 1

An electron enters a uniform magnetic field $B = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$ with a velocity of magnitude $v = 4.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at an angle $\varphi = 60^\circ$ to the direction of the field. Calculate the radius of its helical path, its period, and its pitch. ($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
v_\perp = v \sin\varphi ,
\qquad
v_\parallel = v \cos\varphi ,
\qquad
r = \frac{m_e v_\perp}{e B} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi m_e}{e B} ,
\qquad
p = v_\parallel\, T
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_\perp &= 4.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot \sin 60^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 3.46 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_\parallel = 4.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot \cos 60^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
r &= \frac{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 3.46 \cdot 10^6}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 9.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.79 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
p &= 2.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 1.79 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is neither perpendicular nor parallel to the field, so resolve it into two components. The component parallel to $\vec{B}$ contributes no magnetic force because its cross product with $\vec{B}$ is zero. Motion along the field is therefore at constant velocity $v_\parallel$. The perpendicular component produces a force perpendicular to the velocity, giving uniform circular motion in the plane perpendicular to the field. Combining the two motions gives **helical motion**: the path is a helix.

Use only the perpendicular component to calculate the helix radius, which is approximately $9.9\,\mathrm{mm}$. The period is that of the circular motion and is independent of speed. In one period the electron advances a distance $v_\parallel\, T$ along the field, so the pitch is about $3.6\,\mathrm{cm}$, just under four times the radius.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A proton of speed $v = 5.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ moves in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.10\,\mathrm{T}$ along a helix of radius $r = 2.61\,\mathrm{cm}$.

1. Determine the angle between the proton’s velocity and the field.
2. Calculate the pitch of the helix.
3. Explain what would happen if the proton moved at the same speed parallel to the field.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
r = \frac{m_p v_\perp}{e B}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_\perp = \frac{e B r}{m_p} ,
\qquad
\sin\varphi = \frac{v_\perp}{v}
$$
$$
v_\perp = \frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.10 \cdot 0.0261}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.50 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
\sin\varphi = \frac{2.50 \cdot 10^5}{5.0 \cdot 10^5} = 0.50
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 30^\circ
$$
$$
v_\parallel = v \cos\varphi \approx 4.33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi m_p}{e B} = \frac{2\pi \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.10}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 6.56 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
p = v_\parallel\, T = 4.33 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 6.56 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.28\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radius formula for **helical motion** contains only the velocity component perpendicular to the field, so the radius determines $v_\perp$. The ratio of $v_\perp$ to the speed is the sine of the angle between $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{B}$. The sine is $0.50$, so the angle is $30^\circ$. The sine is also $0.50$ for $150^\circ$, which corresponds to a proton traveling in the opposite direction along the field. The radius and pitch are the same. Only the direction of travel along the axis changes.

The parallel component is $v\cos 30^\circ$, and the period depends only on the mass-to-charge ratio and the field strength. The pitch is the parallel speed times the period, approximately $28\,\mathrm{cm}$. This is more than ten times the radius because the velocity is nearly aligned with the field.

If the velocity were parallel to the field, the angle would be zero and the Lorentz force would vanish, as in the discussion of **velocity parallel to the field**. The proton would then travel in a straight line at constant speed. The helix would reduce to a straight line with zero radius.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A solar-wind electron with kinetic energy $K = 1.0\,\mathrm{keV}$ reaches a region where Earth’s magnetic field can be considered uniform, with magnitude $B = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$. Its velocity makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the field lines.

1. Calculate the speed of the electron and verify that classical mechanics can be used.
2. Calculate the radius and pitch of the helix.
3. Estimate how many revolutions the electron makes while traveling $100\,\mathrm{km}$ along a field line, and explain why the electron is said to follow Earth’s magnetic field lines.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$)

$$
K = 1.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2K}{m_e}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.6 \cdot 10^{-16}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.87 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v}{c} = \frac{1.87 \cdot 10^7}{3.00 \cdot 10^8} \approx 0.062
$$
$$
v_\perp = v_\parallel = v \cos 45^\circ \approx 1.33 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \frac{m_e v_\perp}{e B} = \frac{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 1.33 \cdot 10^7}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.5\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{2\pi m_e}{e B} = \frac{2\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 7.2 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
p &= v_\parallel\, T = 1.33 \cdot 10^7 \cdot 7.16 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 9.5\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
n = \frac{1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m}}{9.5\,\mathrm{m}} \approx 1.1 \cdot 10^4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert the kinetic energy to joules and find the speed from $K = \dfrac{1}{2} m_e v^2$. It is approximately $6\,\%$ of the speed of light, so classical mechanics is a good approximation.

At an angle of $45^\circ$ the two velocity components are equal because $\sin 45^\circ = \cos 45^\circ$. For **helical motion**, the radius is calculated using $v_\perp$ and is about $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The period for this electron depends only on the field strength and is about $0.72\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. The pitch is $v_\parallel\, T \approx 9.5\,\mathrm{m}$.

Over $100\,\mathrm{km}$, the electron completes one revolution per pitch, or about eleven thousand revolutions. The helix has a radius of one and a half meters, whereas Earth’s field changes appreciably only over distances of hundreds of kilometers. The electron therefore stays very close to the same field line and follows its course, as do the particles in the **Van Allen belts** and those that produce auroras near the poles.

</div>
