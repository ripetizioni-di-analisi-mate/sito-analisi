## Exercise 1

A beam of electrons starting from rest is accelerated through a potential difference $\Delta V_a = 500\,\mathrm{V}$ and enters the space between two parallel plates separated by $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. A uniform magnetic field $B = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$ is also present, perpendicular to both the electric field and the velocity of the beam. The voltage across the plates is adjusted, and the beam is observed to be undeflected when the voltage is $\Delta V_p = 265\,\mathrm{V}$.

1. Determine the electron speed.
2. Using the accelerating voltage as well, find the ratio $\dfrac{e}{m_e}$.
3. The electric field is switched off. Calculate the orbital radius of the electrons in the magnetic field alone.
4. A proton accelerated from rest through the same voltage $\Delta V_a$ enters the same region with the same fields. Explain whether it passes through without deflection.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$)

$$
E = \frac{\Delta V_p}{d} = \frac{265}{0.020}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 1.325 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V/m} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{E}{B} = \frac{1.325 \cdot 10^4}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
e\,\Delta V_a = \frac{1}{2} m_e v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{v^2}{2\,\Delta V_a} = \frac{\left( 1.325 \cdot 10^7 \right)^2}{2 \cdot 500}\,\mathrm{C/kg} \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C/kg}
$$
$$
r = \frac{m_e v}{e B} = \frac{v}{\dfrac{e}{m_e}\, B} = \frac{1.325 \cdot 10^7}{1.756 \cdot 10^{11} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 7.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_p = \sqrt{\frac{2 e\,\Delta V_a}{m_p}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 500}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 3.1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s} < \frac{E}{B}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The electric field between the plates is uniform and has magnitude $\dfrac{\Delta V_p}{d}$. The beam is undeflected, so the crossed fields act as a **velocity selector** and the electrons have speed $\dfrac{E}{B}$, about $1.3 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$. This speed is approximately $4\,\%$ of $c$, so classical mechanics applies.

The electrons reached this speed by accelerating from rest through $\Delta V_a$. The work–energy theorem gives $e\,\Delta V_a = \dfrac{1}{2} m_e v^2$. Dividing both sides by $m_e\,\Delta V_a$ gives $\dfrac{e}{m_e} = \dfrac{v^2}{2\,\Delta V_a}$. The value, $1.76 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C/kg}$, agrees with the value obtained from the physical constants. This method differs from Thomson’s because it uses the accelerating voltage in place of the deflection, but it measures the same quantity.

In the magnetic field alone, the velocity is perpendicular to $\vec{B}$. By the result for **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field**, the radius is $\dfrac{m_e v}{eB}$. Dividing the numerator and denominator by $m_e$ introduces the ratio just measured into the calculation, giving approximately $7.5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

A proton accelerated through the same voltage gains the same energy, $500\,\mathrm{eV}$, but has about $1800$ times the mass. Its speed is therefore lower by a factor of about $43$ and is $3.1 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its electric force, of magnitude $eE$, is as strong as that on the electron, while its magnetic force, of magnitude $ev_pB$, is much weaker. The forces oppose each other, as they do for any charge in the selector, but the electric force is stronger. The proton **is deflected** toward the negative plate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A laboratory uses a mass spectrometer to study a magnesium sample. Ions $\mathrm{Mg}^+$ of charge $e$ start from rest, accelerate through a potential difference $\Delta V$, and enter a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.30\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to their velocity. After half a revolution they strike the plate a distance $D$ from the slit. For the most abundant isotope, the measurement is repeated at different voltages, and the values of $D^2$ are plotted against $\Delta V$.

| $\Delta V$ ($\mathrm{kV}$) | $0.50$ | $1.0$ | $1.5$ | $2.0$ | $2.5$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $D^2$ ($10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$) | $1.10$ | $2.22$ | $3.31$ | $4.43$ | $5.54$ |

::grafico{f="2.214*x" punti="0.5,1.10,pieno;1,2.22,pieno;1.5,3.31,pieno;2,4.43,pieno;2.5,5.54,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=3 ymin=0 ymax=7 nomi="ΔV,D²" legenda="D^2 = k\,\Delta V" titolo="Measured values of $D^2$ (in $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$) as a function of the accelerating voltage $\Delta V$ (in $\mathrm{kV}$). The points lie on a straight line through the origin"}

1. Derive the relation between $D^2$ and $\Delta V$ and explain why the points lie on a straight line through the origin.
2. From the slope of the line, determine the ion mass in kilograms and in atomic mass units, and find the mass number of the isotope.
3. For $\Delta V = 2.0\,\mathrm{kV}$, calculate the speed of the ions and the time they take to travel from the slit to the plate.
4. Magnesium also contains the isotope with mass number $26$. For $\Delta V = 2.0\,\mathrm{kV}$, calculate the distance between the impact points of the two isotopes.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $1\,\mathrm{u} = 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
e\,\Delta V = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 ,
\qquad
\frac{D}{2} = r = \frac{m v}{e B}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D^2 = \frac{8 m}{e B^2}\, \Delta V = k\, \Delta V
$$
$$
k = \frac{4.43 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}}{2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V}} \approx 2.21 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/V}
$$
$$
m = \frac{k\, e B^2}{8} = \frac{2.21 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot (0.30)^2}{8}\,\mathrm{kg} \approx 3.98 \cdot 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg} ,
\qquad
\frac{m}{1\,\mathrm{u}} = \frac{3.98 \cdot 10^{-26}}{1.66 \cdot 10^{-27}} \approx 24
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \sqrt{\frac{2 e\,\Delta V}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3}{3.98 \cdot 10^{-26}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.27 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
t &= \frac{T}{2} = \frac{\pi m}{e B} = \frac{\pi \cdot 3.98 \cdot 10^{-26}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.30}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
D_{24} = \sqrt{4.43 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.2105\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
D_{26} = D_{24} \sqrt{\frac{26}{24}} \approx 0.2191\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
D_{26} - D_{24} \approx 8.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During acceleration, the electric field does work $e\,\Delta V$ on the ion, so the work–energy theorem gives $v^2 = \dfrac{2e\,\Delta V}{m}$. In the magnetic field the ion completes half a revolution and, in the **mass spectrometer**, strikes the plate at a distance $D = 2r = \dfrac{2mv}{eB}$. Squaring gives $D^2 = \dfrac{4m^2v^2}{e^2B^2}$. Substituting for $v^2$ gives $D^2 = \dfrac{8m}{eB^2}\,\Delta V$. The mass, charge, and field are fixed, so $D^2$ is **directly proportional** to $\Delta V$, and the graph is a straight line through the origin with slope $k = \dfrac{8m}{eB^2}$.

Read the slope from the graph by dividing the vertical coordinate of a point on the line by its horizontal coordinate. The point at $\Delta V = 2.0\,\mathrm{kV}$ lies on the line that best fits the data and gives $k \approx 2.21 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/V}$ after expressing $D^2$ in square meters and $\Delta V$ in volts. The other points give values that differ by less than $1\,\%$. The relation $k = \dfrac{8m}{eB^2}$ gives a mass of approximately $24\,\mathrm{u}$. The most abundant isotope is therefore **magnesium-24**.

With $\Delta V = 2.0\,\mathrm{kV}$, the formula for an accelerated particle gives a speed of about $127\,\mathrm{km/s}$, much smaller than $c$. The time from the slit to the plate is half the period. By the result for the **period and frequency of circular motion**, it is independent of the voltage and is approximately $2.6\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.

For the most abundant isotope at $\Delta V = 2.0\,\mathrm{kV}$, the table gives $D^2 = 4.43 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so $D_{24} \approx 21.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. At fixed voltage, charge, and field, $D$ is proportional to $\sqrt{m}$, that is, to the square root of the mass number. Magnesium-26 therefore lands at a distance $D_{24}\sqrt{\dfrac{26}{24}} \approx 21.9\,\mathrm{cm}$. The two impact points are approximately $8.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart, separating the **isotopes** on the plate.

</div>
