## Exercise 1

In a velocity selector, two plates separated by $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ with a potential difference $\Delta V = 400\,\mathrm{V}$ produce the electric field. The magnetic field, perpendicular to the electric field, has magnitude $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$.

1. Determine the speed of particles that pass through the selector without deflection.
2. A proton enters the selector at speed $v' = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate the net force on it at the entrance and explain which way it is deflected.
3. Determine the magnetic field that, with the same electric field, would allow protons with speed $5.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to pass through.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$)

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,1.2 6,1.2;0,-1.2 6,-1.2" segmenti="-1.4,0,7,0" vettori="-1.2,0,0,0,v;3,0,3,0.9,qvB;3,0,3,-0.9,qE" punti="3,0,pieno" etichette="0.6,1.4,+;1.8,1.4,+;3,1.4,+;4.2,1.4,+;5.4,1.4,+;0.6,-1.7,−;1.8,-1.7,−;3,-1.7,−;4.2,-1.7,−;5.4,-1.7,−;0.63,0.48,⊗;0.63,-0.82,⊗;1.73,0.48,⊗;1.73,-0.82,⊗;4.43,0.48,⊗;4.43,-0.82,⊗;5.43,0.48,⊗;5.43,-0.82,⊗;3.2,-0.4,p" xmin=-1.6 xmax=7.4 ymin=-2.1 ymax=1.9 titolo="A proton in the selector with a magnetic field directed into the page ($\otimes$). The electric field points from the positive plate to the negative plate, so the electric force is downward and the magnetic Lorentz force is upward"}

$$
E = \frac{\Delta V}{d} = \frac{400}{0.010}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V/m} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{E}{B} = \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^4}{0.20}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
F_B = e v' B = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N} = 9.6 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_E = e E = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N} = 6.4 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F = F_B - F_E = 3.2 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
B' = \frac{E}{v''} = \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^4}{5.0 \cdot 10^5}\,\mathrm{T} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{T}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The electric field between the plates is uniform and has magnitude $\dfrac{\Delta V}{d}$. In a **velocity selector** the electric and magnetic forces point in opposite directions. The particle continues in a straight line if their magnitudes are equal, that is, if $eE = evB$. The charge cancels, leaving $v = \dfrac{E}{B} = 2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The proton’s speed, $v' = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, exceeds the selected speed. The magnetic force, which is proportional to speed, is therefore greater than the electric force, which is independent of speed. The two forces act along the same line in opposite directions, so the net force has magnitude $F_B - F_E = 3.2 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N}$ and points in the direction of the magnetic force. Apply the right-hand rule in the diagram: point your thumb to the right along $\vec{v}$ and your index finger into the page along $\vec{B}$. Your middle finger points upward. The charge is positive, so the proton is deflected **toward the positive plate**.

To select a different speed with the same electric field, change the magnetic field. The condition $v = \dfrac{E}{B}$ gives $B = \dfrac{E}{v}$. A higher speed thus requires a weaker magnetic field, $0.080\,\mathrm{T}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an apparatus similar to Thomson’s, an electron beam crosses a region of length $L = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ containing an electric field $E = 1.5 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and a perpendicular magnetic field $B = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$. At these field strengths the beam is undeflected. When the magnetic field is turned off, the beam leaves the region with a displacement $y = 5.3\,\mathrm{mm}$.

1. Calculate the speed of the electrons.
2. Determine the ratio $\dfrac{e}{m_e}$ and compare it with the value obtained from the physical constants.
3. Determine the potential difference that accelerated the electrons from rest.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
v = \frac{E}{B} = \frac{1.5 \cdot 10^4}{6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.5 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
y = \frac{e E L^2}{2 m_e v^2} = \frac{e}{m_e} \cdot \frac{B^2 L^2}{2E}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{2 y E}{B^2 L^2}
$$
$$
\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{2 \cdot 5.3 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^4}{\left( 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \right)^2 \cdot (0.050)^2}\,\mathrm{C/kg} \approx 1.77 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C/kg}
$$
$$
\frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}\,\mathrm{C/kg} \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C/kg}
$$
$$
e\,\Delta V = \frac{1}{2} m_e v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta V = \frac{m_e v^2}{2e} = \frac{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot \left( 2.5 \cdot 10^7 \right)^2}{2 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With both fields on, the beam is undeflected, so the region acts as a **velocity selector**, and the electron speed is $\dfrac{E}{B} = 2.5 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The selector also works for negative charges because for an electron both the electric and magnetic forces reverse direction and still oppose one another.

With the electric field alone, the electrons enter perpendicular to the field. The displacement at the exit is given by the formula for **deflection in a uniform electric field**. Substitute $v^2 = \dfrac{E^2}{B^2}$ into this formula and solve for $\dfrac{e}{m_e}$, as in the **measurement of the electron’s charge-to-mass ratio**. The resulting value, $1.77 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C/kg}$, differs from the value obtained from the physical constants by less than $1\,\%$. This difference is consistent with the precision of the displacement measurement on the screen.

The electrons gained their speed by accelerating from rest through a potential difference, so the work–energy theorem gives $e\,\Delta V = \dfrac{1}{2} m_e v^2$, yielding approximately $1.8\,\mathrm{kV}$. The speed of the electrons is about $8\,\%$ of $c$, so the classical formulas apply.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the apparatus from the preceding exercise, with $E = 1.5 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and $B = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$, the electric field is switched off. Only the magnetic field remains on, and it now extends over a larger region. The electrons follow a circular arc of radius $r = 23.7\,\mathrm{cm}$.

1. Explain why the electrons still have the speed selected by the crossed fields and derive the formula for $\dfrac{e}{m_e}$ in terms of $E$, $B$, and $r$.
2. Calculate $\dfrac{e}{m_e}$.
3. Explain what could be concluded if, with the same fields, hydrogen ions $\mathrm{H}^+$ selected at the same speed followed a circle with a radius approximately $1800$ times as large.

$$
v = \frac{E}{B} ,
\qquad
r = \frac{m_e v}{e B} = \frac{m_e E}{e B^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{E}{B^2 r}
$$
$$
\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{1.5 \cdot 10^4}{\left( 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \right)^2 \cdot 0.237}\,\mathrm{C/kg} \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C/kg}
$$
$$
\frac{r_{\mathrm{H}}}{r_e} = \frac{m_{\mathrm{H}}}{q_{\mathrm{H}}} \cdot \frac{e}{m_e} \approx 1800
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The magnetic field does not alter the speed because the Lorentz force does no work. The electrons come from the same source with the same accelerating voltage, so they have the speed $\dfrac{E}{B}$ that the beam had when it was undeflected. In the magnetic field alone, the velocity is perpendicular to $\vec{B}$. From the result for **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field**, the radius is $\dfrac{m_e v}{eB}$. Substitute $v = \dfrac{E}{B}$ and solve for the ratio, as in the example on the **charge-to-mass ratio from the orbital radius**.

The value obtained, $1.76 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C/kg}$, agrees with the result of the preceding exercise to the stated number of significant figures, so the two methods give the same result.

For a given speed and field strength, the radius is proportional to the ratio $\dfrac{m}{q}$. If the hydrogen ion follows a circle with a radius $1800$ times as large, its ratio $\dfrac{m}{q}$ is $1800$ times that of the electron. Assuming the ion’s charge has the same magnitude $e$, its mass would be about $1800$ times that of the electron. This is the conclusion of **Thomson’s experiment**: the particles in cathode rays are much lighter than the lightest atom.

</div>
