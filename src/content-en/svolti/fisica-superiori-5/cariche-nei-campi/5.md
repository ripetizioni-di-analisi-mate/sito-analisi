## Exercise 1

In a mass spectrometer, sodium ions $^{23}\mathrm{Na}^+$ of charge $e$ and mass $m = 23\,\mathrm{u}$ start from rest, accelerate through a potential difference $\Delta V = 2.0\,\mathrm{kV}$, and enter a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.25\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to their velocity.

1. Calculate the speed at which the ions enter the magnetic field.
2. Determine how far from the slit the ions strike the plate.
3. Calculate the time the ions take to travel from the slit to the plate.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $1\,\mathrm{u} = 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
m = 23 \cdot 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg} = 3.818 \cdot 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 e\,\Delta V}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3}{3.818 \cdot 10^{-26}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.29 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
r = \frac{m v}{e B} = \frac{3.818 \cdot 10^{-26} \cdot 1.295 \cdot 10^5}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.25}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.124\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
D = 2r \approx 0.25\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
t = \frac{T}{2} = \frac{\pi m}{e B} = \frac{\pi \cdot 3.818 \cdot 10^{-26}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.25}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 3.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ion’s mass in kilograms is found by multiplying the mass number by the atomic mass unit. As the ion accelerates, the electric field does work $e\,\Delta V$ on it. The ion starts from rest, so the expression for a **particle accelerated between two plates** gives a speed of $\sqrt{\dfrac{2e\,\Delta V}{m}}$, approximately $130\,\mathrm{km/s}$.

In the magnetic field the velocity is perpendicular to $\vec{B}$, so the ion follows a circle of radius $\dfrac{mv}{eB}$, about $12.4\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the **mass spectrometer**, the ion completes half a revolution and strikes the plate at the point diametrically opposite the slit, approximately $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ away. Direct calculation using $D = \dfrac{2}{B}\sqrt{\dfrac{2m\,\Delta V}{e}}$ gives the same value.

Half a revolution takes half the period, $\dfrac{\pi m}{eB}$, about $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. As a check, the length of the semicircular path is $\pi r \approx 0.388\,\mathrm{m}$. Dividing this by the speed gives the same time.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a mass spectrometer, ions of a noble gas, all with charge $e$, are accelerated through $\Delta V = 1.0\,\mathrm{kV}$ and enter a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.30\,\mathrm{T}$. The most abundant isotope strikes the plate $D = 19.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the slit, and a less abundant isotope strikes a little closer to the slit.

1. Determine the ion mass of the most abundant isotope, in kilograms and in atomic mass units. Identify the gas, given that it is neon, argon, or krypton.
2. The less abundant isotope has mass number $36$. Calculate how far from the slit it strikes the plate and the distance between the two impact points.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $1\,\mathrm{u} = 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
m = \frac{e B^2 D^2}{8\,\Delta V} = \frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot (0.30)^2 \cdot (0.192)^2}{8 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^3}\,\mathrm{kg} \approx 6.64 \cdot 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
\frac{m}{1\,\mathrm{u}} = \frac{6.636 \cdot 10^{-26}}{1.66 \cdot 10^{-27}} \approx 40
$$
$$
D = \frac{2}{B}\sqrt{\frac{2 m\,\Delta V}{e}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{D_{36}}{D_{40}} = \sqrt{\frac{36}{40}} ,
\qquad
D_{36} = 19.2\,\mathrm{cm} \cdot \sqrt{0.90} \approx 18.2\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
D_{40} - D_{36} \approx 19.2\,\mathrm{cm} - 18.2\,\mathrm{cm} \approx 1.0\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a **mass spectrometer**, the distance from the slit to the impact point is $D = 2r$, and the radius formula gives $m = \dfrac{eB^2D^2}{8\,\Delta V}$. Substituting the data gives a mass of approximately $40\,\mathrm{u}$. The atomic masses of the three gases are approximately $20\,\mathrm{u}$ for neon, $40\,\mathrm{u}$ for argon, and $84\,\mathrm{u}$ for krypton. The gas is therefore **argon**, and the most abundant isotope is argon-40.

The voltage, field strength, and charge are the same for both isotopes, so $D$ is proportional to $\sqrt{m}$, and the mass is proportional to the mass number. The ratio of the distances is therefore $\sqrt{\dfrac{36}{40}}$, and argon-36 strikes the plate approximately $18.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the slit. The two impact points are about $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart, a readily measurable distance on the plate. This allows the spectrometer to distinguish **isotopes**, which have the same chemical properties but different masses.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A velocity selector with $E = 3.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and $B = 0.10\,\mathrm{T}$ is placed before a mass spectrometer. The ions leaving the selector enter a second region with a uniform magnetic field $B' = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to their velocity. The beam contains ions $^{12}\mathrm{C}^+$ and $^{13}\mathrm{C}^+$, each with charge $e$ and with masses $12\,\mathrm{u}$ and $13\,\mathrm{u}$, respectively.

1. Calculate the speed of the ions entering the second region.
2. Calculate the radius of the semicircular path for each isotope and the distance between their impact points on the plate.
3. Explain why the distance from the slit is directly proportional to mass in this spectrometer.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $1\,\mathrm{u} = 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="1.5-1.5*cos(t),1.5*sin(t),0,3.1416;1.625-1.625*cos(t),1.625*sin(t),0,3.1416" legenda=0 linee="-0.6,0 -0.15,0;0.15,0 3.8,0;-0.8,-2.6 -0.8,-0.6;0.8,-2.6 0.8,-0.6" vettori="0,-2.4,0,-0.15,v" punti="3,0,pieno;3.25,0,pieno;0,0,vuoto,O" segmenti="1.5,0,1.5,1.5" etichette="1.6,0.75,r₁₂;-2.0,-1.6,selector;-0.97,1.03,⊙;0.53,0.43,⊙;2.33,0.43,⊙;3.23,1.33,⊙;1.43,2.03,⊙;2.6,-0.45,¹²C;3.3,-0.45,¹³C" xmin=-2.4 xmax=4 ymin=-2.8 ymax=2.4 titolo="Mass spectrometer with a velocity selector. The ions leave the selector with $v = \dfrac{E}{B}$ and pass through $O$ into the field $B'$, which points out of the page. The radius is proportional to mass"}

$$
v = \frac{E}{B} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^4}{0.10}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{12} &= \frac{m_{12}\, v}{e B'} = \frac{12 \cdot 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^5}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 7.47 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
r_{13} &= \frac{13 \cdot 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^5}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 8.09 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
2\,(r_{13} - r_{12}) = \frac{2\,(m_{13} - m_{12})\, v}{e B'} = \frac{2 \cdot 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^5}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Only ions with $v = \dfrac{E}{B}$ pass straight through the **velocity selector**, regardless of their mass, so both isotopes enter the second region with $3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

In the second region the velocity is perpendicular to $\vec{B}'$. By the result for circular motion in a uniform magnetic field, each isotope follows a semicircle of radius $\dfrac{mv}{eB'}$. The radii are about $7.47\,\mathrm{cm}$ for carbon-12 and $8.09\,\mathrm{cm}$ for carbon-13. Each impact point is two radii from the slit, so the separation of the points is twice the difference between the radii, approximately $1.2\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate the radius difference using the mass difference, $1\,\mathrm{u}$, to avoid subtracting two nearly equal numbers.

The speed, charge, and field $B'$ are the same for all ions. Thus, in the expression $r = \dfrac{mv}{eB'}$, only the mass varies, and the radius is **directly proportional** to the mass. Without a selector, as in the spectrometer in the preceding exercise, the speed depends on the mass and the radius is proportional to $\sqrt{m}$. The direct proportionality in a **spectrometer with a velocity selector** makes the distances on the plate proportional to the masses.

</div>
