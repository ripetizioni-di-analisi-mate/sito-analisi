## Exercise 1

A proton travels at speed $v = 3.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ directed into the page and perpendicular to its velocity. Calculate the orbital radius, period, and frequency, and determine the direction of rotation. ($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" legenda=0 vettori="0,-2,1.4,-2,v;0,-2,0,-0.9,F;1.73,1,1.48,1.43" segmenti="0,0,-1.414,1.414" punti="0,-2,pieno;0,0,pieno,C" etichette="-0.6,-2.45,p;-1.0,0.5,r;-3.17,-3.17,⊗;-3.17,-1.67,⊗;-3.17,-0.17,⊗;-3.17,1.33,⊗;-3.17,2.53,⊗;-1.67,-3.17,⊗;-0.17,1.33,⊗;-0.17,2.53,⊗;1.33,-0.17,⊗;1.33,2.53,⊗;2.83,-3.17,⊗;2.83,-1.67,⊗;2.83,-0.17,⊗;2.83,1.33,⊗;2.83,2.53,⊗;1.33,-3.17,⊗" xmin=-3.6 xmax=3.6 ymin=-3.5 ymax=3 titolo="In a field directed into the page ($\otimes$), the proton travels counterclockwise around the circle. At the lowest point the Lorentz force points toward the center $C$"}

$$
e v B = m_p\,\frac{v^2}{r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r = \frac{m_p v}{e B} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi m_p}{e B} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{1}{T}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \frac{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^6}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 6.3 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
f &= \frac{1}{1.31 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s}} \approx 7.6 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The proton experiences only the Lorentz force, and its velocity is perpendicular to the field. From the result for **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field**, the proton moves in a circle, with the Lorentz force of magnitude $evB$ acting as the centripetal force. Equating $evB$ and $m_p\,\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ and dividing by $v$ gives the radius, about $6.3\,\mathrm{cm}$. The period is the time needed to travel around the circumference, $\dfrac{2\pi r}{v}$. When the radius is substituted, the speed cancels. The frequency is the reciprocal of the period.

To find the direction of rotation, consider the lowest point on the path, where the force must point upward, toward the center. By the **right-hand rule**, with the thumb pointing to the right along $\vec{v}$ and the fingers pointing into the page along $\vec{B}$, the palm faces upward. The proton has positive charge, so the force points upward if the proton moves to the right at the lowest point. The proton therefore rotates **counterclockwise**, as predicted by the result on the direction of rotation for a positive charge in a field directed into the page.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron initially at rest is accelerated through a potential difference $\Delta V = 150\,\mathrm{V}$ and enters a uniform magnetic field $B = 1.2 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to its velocity. Calculate the electron’s speed, its orbital radius, and the period of its motion. ($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
e\,\Delta V = \frac{1}{2} m_e v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 e\,\Delta V}{m_e}} ,
\qquad
r = \frac{m_e v}{e B} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi m_e}{e B}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 150}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 7.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
r &= \frac{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 7.26 \cdot 10^6}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.2 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 3.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.2 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 3.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem has two stages. In the first stage the electron is accelerated by the electric field, which does work $e\,\Delta V$. The electron starts from rest, so by the work–energy theorem $e\,\Delta V = \dfrac{1}{2} m_e v^2$, giving the formula for a **particle accelerated between two plates**. The speed is about $2.4\,\%$ of $c$, so classical mechanics applies.

In the second stage the velocity is perpendicular to the uniform magnetic field. By the result for **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field**, the electron follows a circle of radius $\dfrac{m_e v}{eB}$, about $3.4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The formula for the orbital radius of an accelerated particle, $r = \dfrac{1}{B}\sqrt{\dfrac{2 m_e\,\Delta V}{e}}$, gives the same result without requiring the speed to be calculated. The period depends only on the mass-to-charge ratio and the field strength, and is about $30\,\mathrm{ns}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a laboratory tube, electrons with kinetic energy $K = 2.0\,\mathrm{keV}$ follow a circle of radius $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to their velocity.

1. Determine the magnitude of the magnetic field.
2. Calculate the time it takes an electron to complete half a revolution.
3. Explain how the radius and the time found in the preceding part change if the kinetic energy of the electrons doubles.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
K = 2.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} = 3.2 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
m_e v = \sqrt{2 m_e K}
$$
$$
r = \frac{m_e v}{e B} = \frac{\sqrt{2 m_e K}}{e B}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B = \frac{\sqrt{2 m_e K}}{e r}
$$
$$
B = \frac{\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 3.2 \cdot 10^{-16}}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{T} = \frac{2.41 \cdot 10^{-23}}{8.0 \cdot 10^{-21}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
t = \frac{T}{2} = \frac{\pi m_e}{e B} = \frac{\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 3.02 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 5.9 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
K' = 2K
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r' = \frac{\sqrt{2 m_e \cdot 2K}}{e B} = \sqrt{2}\, r \approx 7.1\,\mathrm{cm} ,
\qquad
t' = t
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The energy is given in kiloelectronvolts and must be converted to joules. The formula $K = \dfrac{1}{2} m_e v^2$ gives $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m_e}}$, so the momentum is $m_e v = \sqrt{2 m_e K}$. Substituting the momentum into the formula for **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field** and solving for $B$ gives a field of about $3.0\,\mathrm{mT}$.

Half a revolution takes half a period. By the result for the **period and frequency of circular motion**, the period is $\dfrac{2\pi m_e}{eB}$. The required time is therefore $\dfrac{\pi m_e}{eB}$, about $5.9\,\mathrm{ns}$.

The radius is proportional to $\sqrt{K}$, so doubling the energy increases the radius by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ to approximately $7.1\,\mathrm{cm}$. The period is independent of speed, so the time for half a revolution **remains the same**: the faster electron travels a longer semicircle in the same time. The speed of the $2.0\,\mathrm{keV}$ electrons is $\sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m_e}} \approx 2.7 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$, about $9\,\%$ of $c$, so the classical treatment is still a reasonable approximation.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A proton and an alpha particle (the latter with charge $2e$ and mass $4 m_p$) start from rest and are accelerated through the same potential difference $\Delta V = 2.0\,\mathrm{kV}$. They then enter a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to their velocity.

1. Calculate the orbital radius of each particle and the ratio of the radii.
2. Calculate the period for each particle.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
r = \frac{1}{B}\sqrt{\frac{2 m\,\Delta V}{|q|}} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi m}{|q|\,B}
$$
$$
\frac{r_\alpha}{r_p} = \sqrt{\frac{m_\alpha}{m_p} \cdot \frac{e}{2e}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{2}} = \sqrt{2} ,
\qquad
\frac{T_\alpha}{T_p} = \frac{m_\alpha}{m_p} \cdot \frac{e}{2e} = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_p &= \frac{1}{0.40}\sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^3}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
r_\alpha = \sqrt{2}\, r_p \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T_p &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.40}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
T_\alpha = 2\, T_p \approx 3.3 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both particles are accelerated from rest and then enter the field perpendicular to $\vec{B}$, so the formula for the **orbital radius of an accelerated particle** gives $\dfrac{1}{B}\sqrt{\dfrac{2 m\,\Delta V}{|q|}}$. The field and voltage are the same, so the ratio of the radii depends only on the ratio $\dfrac{m}{|q|}$ under the square root. For the alpha particle, $\dfrac{m}{|q|}$ is twice the value for the proton, so its radius is a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ larger. The two radii are about $1.6\,\mathrm{cm}$ for the proton and $2.3\,\mathrm{cm}$ for the alpha particle.

The period is proportional to $\dfrac{m}{|q|}$ with no square root, so the alpha particle takes **twice** as long. This is consistent with the speeds. The alpha particle has twice the energy, $4.0\,\mathrm{keV}$, but four times the mass, so its speed is lower by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$, while the circumference is $\sqrt{2}$ times as long. The time for one revolution therefore increases by a factor of $\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2$.

</div>
