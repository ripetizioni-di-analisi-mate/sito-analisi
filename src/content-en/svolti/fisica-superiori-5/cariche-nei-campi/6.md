## Exercise 1

A cyclotron of radius $R = 45\,\mathrm{cm}$ with magnetic field $B = 0.80\,\mathrm{T}$ accelerates protons.

1. Calculate the frequency at which the alternating voltage across the two electrodes must operate.
2. Calculate the maximum proton speed and verify that it is small compared with $c$.
3. Calculate the maximum kinetic energy in joules and in megaelectronvolts.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$)

$$
f = \frac{e B}{2\pi m_p} = \frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.80}{2\pi \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}\,\mathrm{Hz} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \frac{e B R}{m_p} = \frac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.80 \cdot 0.45}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 3.4 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
\frac{v_{\max}}{c} \approx 0.11
$$
$$
K_{\max} = \frac{1}{2} m_p v_{\max}^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot \left( 3.449 \cdot 10^7 \right)^2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 9.9 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_{\max} = \frac{9.93 \cdot 10^{-13}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{eV} \approx 6.2 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{eV} = 6.2\,\mathrm{MeV}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a **cyclotron**, the proton travels along semicircles inside the electrodes, and the time for half a revolution is independent of speed. For the proton to accelerate each time it crosses the gap, the voltage must reverse every half revolution. The alternating voltage must therefore oscillate at the cyclotron frequency $\dfrac{eB}{2\pi m_p}$, approximately $12\,\mathrm{MHz}$.

The proton exits when its path reaches the cyclotron radius $R$. The radius formula $R = \dfrac{m_p v_{\max}}{eB}$ gives the maximum speed, approximately $3.4 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$, or $11\,\%$ of $c$. At this speed relativistic corrections are of the order of $1\,\%$, so the classical treatment gives acceptable results to two significant figures.

The maximum kinetic energy can be calculated from the classical formula or obtained directly from $K_{\max} = \dfrac{e^2B^2R^2}{2m_p}$, as in the theorem on the **maximum energy in a cyclotron**. Dividing by $1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}$ gives approximately $6.2\,\mathrm{MeV}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the cyclotron in the preceding exercise ($R = 45\,\mathrm{cm}$, $B = 0.80\,\mathrm{T}$), the peak voltage across the electrodes is $\Delta V = 25\,\mathrm{kV}$. Assume that each proton always crosses the gap when the voltage magnitude is at its maximum.

1. Calculate the energy a proton gains in one revolution and the number of revolutions it needs to reach maximum energy.
2. Calculate the time the proton spends in the cyclotron.
3. Explain what would change if the voltage were $50\,\mathrm{kV}$.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
\Delta K_{\text{rev}} = 2 e\,\Delta V = 2 \cdot 25\,\mathrm{keV} = 50\,\mathrm{keV}
$$
$$
n = \frac{K_{\max}}{\Delta K_{\text{rev}}} = \frac{6.21 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{eV}}{5.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{eV}} \approx 124
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi m_p}{e B} = \frac{1}{f} \approx 8.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
t = n\, T \approx 124 \cdot 8.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In one revolution the proton crosses the gap twice, and each time the electric field gives it energy $e\,\Delta V$, as for a **particle accelerated between two plates**. With the voltage in volts and charge $e$, the energy gained in electronvolts is numerically equal to the voltage in volts, so one revolution supplies $50\,\mathrm{keV}$. To reach $6.2\,\mathrm{MeV}$, approximately $124$ revolutions are needed.

Every revolution takes the same amount of time, the period $T$, because the result for the **period and frequency of circular motion** shows that the period is independent of speed. The total time is therefore $n\,T$, about $10\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. In this time the proton travels along a spiral more than two hundred meters long, even though the cyclotron radius is only $45\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The maximum energy depends only on $B$, $R$, and the particle, *not* on the voltage. With $50\,\mathrm{kV}$, the final energy would still be $6.2\,\mathrm{MeV}$, but each revolution would supply $100\,\mathrm{keV}$. The number of revolutions would be halved to about $62$, and the time in the cyclotron would be about $5\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cyclotron is to accelerate protons to kinetic energy $K = 5.0\,\mathrm{MeV}$ using an alternating-voltage generator of frequency $f = 15\,\mathrm{MHz}$.

1. Determine the required magnetic field.
2. Determine the minimum electrode radius.
3. Explain whether the same cyclotron with the same magnetic field can accelerate alpha particles (charge $2e$, mass $4 m_p$) without changing the frequency.

($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$)

$$
f = \frac{e B}{2\pi m_p}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B = \frac{2\pi m_p f}{e} = \frac{2\pi \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^7}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 0.98\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
K = 5.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2K}{m_p}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 8.0 \cdot 10^{-13}}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 3.10 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
R = \frac{m_p v}{e B} = \frac{v}{2\pi f} = \frac{3.095 \cdot 10^7}{2\pi \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^7}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.33\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
f_\alpha = \frac{2e B}{2\pi \cdot 4 m_p} = \frac{1}{2}\, f = 7.5\,\mathrm{MHz}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The voltage must oscillate at the cyclotron frequency for protons, so the frequency formula gives the magnetic field, approximately $0.98\,\mathrm{T}$.

The final semicircle has the radius of the electrodes and corresponds to the final speed. Find this speed from the kinetic energy converted to joules. It is about $10\,\%$ of $c$, so classical mechanics applies. Since $\dfrac{eB}{m_p} = 2\pi f$, the radius formula can be written as $R = \dfrac{v}{2\pi f}$, giving approximately $33\,\mathrm{cm}$.

For an alpha particle the ratio $\dfrac{|q|}{m}$ is half that for a proton, so in the same field its cyclotron frequency is half as high, $7.5\,\mathrm{MHz}$. With the generator operating at $15\,\mathrm{MHz}$, each half revolution of the alpha particle lasts one full voltage cycle. The field in the gap therefore points in the same direction at every crossing. The particle crosses the gap in alternating directions, however, so it gains and loses energy on alternate crossings. The cyclotron **cannot** accelerate alpha particles without a change in frequency or field strength. For example, keeping the same generator would require twice the field strength, about $2.0\,\mathrm{T}$.

</div>
