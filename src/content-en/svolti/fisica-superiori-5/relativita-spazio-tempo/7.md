## Exercise 1

A shuttle leaves a space base at the origin of the base frame $S$ at time $t = 0$ and travels along the $x$-axis with constant velocity $v = 0.60\,c$. At time $t_1$ such that $c\,t_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{km}$, the base sends a light signal toward the shuttle.

- In the Minkowski diagram for the base, with $x$ and $ct$ in kilometers, draw the worldlines of the shuttle and the signal.
- Determine where and when the signal reaches the shuttle in the base frame.
- Calculate the time shown at that instant by the onboard clock, which was started at departure.

Use $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\text{shuttle: } ct = \frac{x}{\beta} = \frac{x}{0.60} ,
\qquad
\text{signal: } ct = 2.0\,\mathrm{km} + x
$$
$$
\frac{x}{0.60} = 2.0\,\mathrm{km} + x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x \left( \frac{5}{3} - 1 \right) = 2.0\,\mathrm{km}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = 3.0\,\mathrm{km} ,
\qquad
ct = 5.0\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
t = \frac{5.0 \cdot 10^{3}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 16.7\,\mu\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
c\, \Delta t_0 = \sqrt{(c\,t)^2 - x^2} = \sqrt{5.0^2 - 3.0^2}\,\mathrm{km} = 4.0\,\mathrm{km} ,
\qquad
\Delta t_0 = \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^{3}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 13.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{f="x/0.6;2+x" punti="3,5,pieno,P;0,2,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=8 uguale=1 nomi="x,ct" legenda="\text{shuttle: } ct = \dfrac{5}{3}\,x;\text{signal: } ct = 2 + x" titolo="Minkowski diagram for the base, in kilometers. The worldlines of the shuttle and the light signal intersect at event $P$, with $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{km}$ and $ct = 5.0\,\mathrm{km}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The shuttle leaves the origin at constant velocity, so its position is $x = vt$. Multiplying and dividing by $c$ gives $x = \beta\, ct$, or $ct = \dfrac{x}{\beta}$. In the **Minkowski diagram**, its worldline is therefore a straight line through the origin with slope $\dfrac{1}{0.60} = \dfrac{5}{3}$, steeper than a light ray’s worldline. The signal leaves $x = 0$ when $ct = 2.0\,\mathrm{km}$ and moves at speed $c$ in the positive $x$-direction, so its worldline is a straight line inclined at $45^\circ$ with equation $ct = 2.0\,\mathrm{km} + x$.

The signal reaches the shuttle at the event common to both worldlines. To find it, equate the two expressions for $ct$. This gives $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{km}$ and $ct = 5.0\,\mathrm{km}$. Thus, in the base frame, the signal arrives **3.0 km from the base, about 16.7 μs after the shuttle leaves**.

The onboard clock is present at departure, at the origin, and at the arrival of the signal, event $P$. It therefore measures the **proper time** between the two events, which can be obtained from the invariant interval. The result is $c\,\Delta t_0 = \sqrt{(c\,t)^2 - x^2} = 4.0\,\mathrm{km}$, or $\Delta t_0 \approx 13.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. As a check, for $\beta = 0.60$ we have $\gamma = 1.25$, and time dilation gives $\dfrac{16.7\,\mu\mathrm{s}}{1.25} \approx 13.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the Minkowski diagram of frame $S$, with $x$ and $ct$ in kilometers, draw the axes of a frame $S'$ moving at velocity $v = 0.50\,c$, with the origins coinciding at $t = t' = 0$. Consider the events $O$ at the origin, $A$ with $x = 4.0\,\mathrm{km}$ and $ct = 2.0\,\mathrm{km}$, and $B$ with $x = 4.0\,\mathrm{km}$ and $ct = 1.0\,\mathrm{km}$.

- Show that $A$ lies on the $x'$-axis and explain what this means.
- Calculate the time $t'_B$ of event $B$ in frame $S'$ and decide which of $O$ and $B$ occurs first in $S'$.
- Explain why the time order of $O$ and $B$ differs in the two frames.

Use $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\text{axis } x' : \ ct = \beta\, x = 0.50 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{km} = 2.0\,\mathrm{km} = c\, t_A
$$
$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.50^2}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 1.155
$$
$$
c\, t'_B = \gamma \left( c\, t_B - \beta\, x_B \right) = 1.155 \cdot (1.0 - 0.50 \cdot 4.0)\,\mathrm{km} \approx -1.15\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
t'_B = \frac{-1.15 \cdot 10^{3}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx -3.8\,\mu\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
(\Delta s_{OB})^2 = \left( 1.0^2 - 4.0^2 \right) \mathrm{km^2} = -15\,\mathrm{km^2} < 0
$$

::grafico{f="2*x;0.5*x" segmenti="0,0,5,5;4,1,4,0" punti="4,2,pieno,A;4,1,pieno,B" etichette="2.15,4.8,ct′;4.55,2.5,x′" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=5 uguale=1 nomi="x,ct" legenda="\text{axis } ct'\text{: } ct = 2x;\text{axis } x'\text{: } ct = 0.5\,x" titolo="Axes of frame $S'$ for $\beta = 0.5$ in the diagram of $S$, in kilometers. Event $A$ lies on the $x'$-axis, so it is simultaneous with the origin in $S'$. Event $B$ lies below the $x'$-axis, so in $S'$ it precedes the origin"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The $x'$-axis consists of events with $t' = 0$. From the Lorentz transformations, $t' = \gamma \left( t - \dfrac{v\,x}{c^2} \right)$ vanishes when $ct = \beta\, x$, so the $x'$-axis is the line $ct = 0.50\,x$. At $x = 4.0\,\mathrm{km}$ this line gives $ct = 2.0\,\mathrm{km}$, precisely the coordinate of $A$. Thus $A$ has $t'_A = 0$ and is **simultaneous with the origin in frame $S'$**. In $S$, however, it occurs later, at $t_A = \dfrac{2.0\,\mathrm{km}}{c} \approx 6.7\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.

For event $B$, multiply the time transformation by $c$ so that all quantities are in kilometers. This gives $c\,t' = \gamma\,(c\,t - \beta\,x)$, yielding $c\,t'_B \approx -1.15\,\mathrm{km}$, or $t'_B \approx -3.8\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. The time is negative, so in $S'$ event $B$ **occurs before** the origin event $O$, whereas in $S$ it occurs later because $t_B > 0$. In the diagram, $B$ lies below the $x'$-axis, on the side containing events with $t' < 0$.

The interval between $O$ and $B$ is negative and therefore spacelike, so $B$ lies in the elsewhere region of $O$. For events in the **elsewhere region**, the time order depends on the frame of reference and no signal can travel from one event to the other. The reversal of their order therefore does not violate causality. For event $A$, which has the same horizontal coordinate, we have $(\Delta s)^2 = 4.0 - 16 = -12\,\mathrm{km^2}$, also spacelike.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a Minkowski diagram of frame $S$ drawn with the same scale on both axes, the $ct'$-axis of a frame $S'$ makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the $ct$-axis. Determine the velocity of $S'$ relative to $S$ and the Lorentz factor. Event $P$ lies on the $ct'$-axis, with $c\,t = 6.0\,\mathrm{km}$. Calculate its horizontal coordinate $x$ in $S$ and its time $t'$ in $S'$. Use $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\tan \alpha = \beta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta = \tan 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 0.577 ,
\qquad
v \approx 1.73 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{3}}} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} \approx 1.22
$$
$$
x_P = \beta\, c\, t_P = 0.577 \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{km} \approx 3.46\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
c\, t'_P = \sqrt{(c\, t_P)^2 - x_P^2} = \sqrt{36 - 12}\,\mathrm{km} = \sqrt{24}\,\mathrm{km} \approx 4.90\,\mathrm{km} ,
\qquad
t'_P = \frac{4.90 \cdot 10^{3}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 16.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $ct'$-axis is the worldline of the origin $O'$, which moves at velocity $v$. In the diagram this line has equation $x = \beta\, ct$, so it makes an angle with the $ct$-axis given by $\alpha$, with $\tan \alpha = \dfrac{x}{ct} = \beta$. For $\alpha = 30^\circ$ this gives $\beta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, and $S'$ moves at a speed of **approximately** $1.73 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Since $\beta^2 = \dfrac{1}{3}$, the Lorentz factor is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}} \approx 1.22$.

Event $P$ lies on the $ct'$-axis, so its horizontal coordinate in $S$ is $x_P = \beta\, c\,t_P \approx 3.46\,\mathrm{km}$. In $S'$ it occurs at the origin $O'$, with $x'_P = 0$. The **invariant interval** gives $(c\,t'_P)^2 - 0 = (c\,t_P)^2 - x_P^2$, and since $x_P^2 = \dfrac{36}{3}\,\mathrm{km^2} = 12\,\mathrm{km^2}$, we obtain $c\,t'_P = \sqrt{24}\,\mathrm{km}$, or $t'_P \approx 16.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.

The result is the proper time for the origin $O'$ between time $0$ and event $P$. As a check, time dilation gives $t_P = \gamma\, t'_P$, and indeed $1.22 \cdot 4.90\,\mathrm{km} \approx 6.0\,\mathrm{km}$.

</div>
