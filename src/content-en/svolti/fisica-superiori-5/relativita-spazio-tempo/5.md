## Exercise 1

Frame $S'$ moves relative to $S$ at velocity $v = 0.80\,c$ along the $x$-axis. Their origins coincide at time $t = t' = 0$. In $S$, an event has coordinates $x = 600\,\mathrm{m}$ and $t = 3.0\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. Calculate its coordinates in $S'$ and verify that the interval between the event and the origin is the same in both frames. Use $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.80^2}} = \frac{5}{3} ,
\qquad
v = 2.40 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x' = \gamma \left( x - v t \right) = \frac{5}{3} \left( 600 - 2.40 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \right) \mathrm{m} = \frac{5}{3} \cdot (-120)\,\mathrm{m} = -200\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
t' = \gamma \left( t - \frac{v\, x}{c^2} \right) = \frac{5}{3} \left( 3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} - \frac{0.80 \cdot 600}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}} \right) \mathrm{s} = \frac{5}{3} \cdot 1.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
(c\, t)^2 - x^2 = 900^2 - 600^2 = 4.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
(c\, t')^2 - x'^2 = 700^2 - 200^2 = 4.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two frames have the orientation used in the **Lorentz transformations**, so substitute the event coordinates into the formulas. With $\beta = 0.80$, the Lorentz factor is $\dfrac{1}{0.60} = \dfrac{5}{3}$. To calculate $x'$, the product $vt$ is $720\,\mathrm{m}$, so $x - vt = -120\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x' = -200\,\mathrm{m}$. The minus sign means that in $S'$ the event takes place behind the origin $O'$, which by time $t$ has already passed the point $x = 600\,\mathrm{m}$.

To calculate $t'$, use $\dfrac{v\, x}{c^2} = \dfrac{\beta\, x}{c}$, which equals $\dfrac{0.80 \cdot 600}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.6\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. The difference $t - \dfrac{v\, x}{c^2}$ is therefore $1.4\,\mu\mathrm{s}$, and multiplying by $\dfrac{5}{3}$ gives $t' \approx 2.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.

At $0$ the shared origin of the two frames is an event with coordinates zero in both. Thus the interval between the origin and the given event can be calculated from the event coordinates alone. In $S$ we have $c\,t = 900\,\mathrm{m}$, and in $S'$ we have $c\,t' = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot \dfrac{7}{3} \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m} = 700\,\mathrm{m}$. The two differences of squares both equal $4.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, as required by the **invariant interval**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On a straight runway, two lamps separated by $900\,\mathrm{m}$ light up at the same instant in the runway frame $S$. Lamp $A$ is at the origin and lamp $B$ is at $x = 900\,\mathrm{m}$. A rocket travels along the runway from $A$ toward $B$ at velocity $v = 0.60\,c$. Calculate the time and distance between the two flashes in the rocket frame $S'$ and determine which lamp lights up first in that frame. Use $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\Delta t = 0 ,
\qquad
\Delta x = x_B - x_A = 900\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.60^2}} = 1.25
$$
$$
\Delta t' = \gamma \left( \Delta t - \frac{v\, \Delta x}{c^2} \right) = -1.25 \cdot \frac{0.60 \cdot 900}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} = -2.25 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\Delta x' = \gamma \left( \Delta x - v\, \Delta t \right) = 1.25 \cdot 900\,\mathrm{m} = 1125\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In $S$ the two flashes are simultaneous. The Lorentz transformations are linear and therefore apply to the **coordinate differences** between the two events, with $\Delta t = 0$ and $\Delta x = 900\,\mathrm{m}$.

The time interval in $S'$ is $\Delta t' = t'_B - t'_A \approx -2.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. Because $\Delta t'$ is negative, $t'_B < t'_A$, so in the rocket frame **lamp $B$ lights up first**, the lamp toward which the rocket is moving. Two events simultaneous in $S$ are not simultaneous in $S'$, as required by the **relativity of simultaneity**.

The distance in $S'$ is $1125\,\mathrm{m}$, greater than $900\,\mathrm{m}$. This is consistent with length contraction. The two events are simultaneous in $S$, and $900\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance between the lamps at rest in $S$. In $S'$ the events are not simultaneous: $1125\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance between two events at different times, whereas the runway length measured by the rocket is $\dfrac{900\,\mathrm{m}}{1.25} = 720\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two events in frame $S$ are separated by $\Delta x = 1500\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\Delta t = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.

- Determine whether a signal emitted at the first event can reach the location of the second before that event occurs.
- Determine the velocity of a frame $S'$ moving along the $x$-axis in which the two events are simultaneous.
- Calculate their spatial separation in $S'$.

Use $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
c\, \Delta t = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m} = 600\,\mathrm{m} < \Delta x = 1500\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta t' = \gamma \left( \Delta t - \frac{v\, \Delta x}{c^2} \right) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \frac{c^2\, \Delta t}{\Delta x}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta = \frac{c\, \Delta t}{\Delta x} = \frac{600}{1500} = 0.40
$$
$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.40^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.84}} \approx 1.091
$$
$$
\Delta x' = \gamma \left( \Delta x - v\, \Delta t \right) = 1.091 \cdot (1500 - 0.40 \cdot 600)\,\mathrm{m} = 1.091 \cdot 1260\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.37 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the time $\Delta t$, light travels $c\,\Delta t = 600\,\mathrm{m}$, less than the distance $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ separating the two locations. Even a light signal, the fastest possible signal, emitted at the first event cannot reach the second location in time. Thus **no signal can connect the two events**. The interval between them is spacelike.

Require the two events to be simultaneous in $S'$, so that $\Delta t' = 0$. Since $\gamma$ is nonzero, the expression $\Delta t - \dfrac{v\, \Delta x}{c^2}$ must vanish. This gives $v$. Dividing by $c$ gives $\beta = \dfrac{c\,\Delta t}{\Delta x} = 0.40$, which is less than $1$ precisely because $c\,\Delta t < \Delta x$. The required frame therefore moves at velocity $0.40\,c = 1.2 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

In frame $S'$ the spatial separation between the events is found by transforming $\Delta x$ using $v\,\Delta t = \beta\, c\,\Delta t = 0.40 \cdot 600\,\mathrm{m} = 240\,\mathrm{m}$. The result is **approximately** $1.37\,\mathrm{km}$. As a check, for a spacelike interval the separation in the frame in which the events are simultaneous is $\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 - (c\, \Delta t)^2} = \sqrt{1500^2 - 600^2}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1375\,\mathrm{m}$, the same value.

</div>
