## Exercise 1

An infinitely long straight copper wire with a circular cross section of radius $R = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries current $I = 12\,\mathrm{A}$ distributed uniformly over its cross section.

1. Calculate the magnetic field at the surface of the wire, at a distance $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis, and at a distance $6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis.
2. The field at a distance $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the axis also occurs at a point outside the wire. Find the distance of that point from the axis.

($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
B(r) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\mu_0 I\, r}{2\pi R^2} & 0 \leq r \leq R \\[10pt]
\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} & r \geq R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B(R) &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 12}{3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{T} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(1.5\,\mathrm{mm}) &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 12 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^{-3}}{\left( 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \right)^2}\,\mathrm{T} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(6.0\,\mathrm{mm}) &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 12}{6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{T} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{\mu_0 I\, r_i}{2\pi R^2} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r_e}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r_e = \frac{R^2}{r_i} = \frac{(3.0\,\mathrm{mm})^2}{1.0\,\mathrm{mm}} = 9.0\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wire has cylindrical symmetry and the current density is uniform, so the result derived using **Ampère’s law** for the field inside and outside a thick wire applies. At the surface, both expressions give the same value, $8.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$. The point at $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ is inside, halfway from the axis to the surface. The internal field is proportional to $r$, so it is half the surface field there. The point at $6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ is outside, at twice the radius. The external field is inversely proportional to $r$, so the field there is also half the surface field.

For the second question, let $r_i = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ denote the distance inside the wire and $r_e$ the unknown distance outside it. Set the two expressions for the field equal. Canceling $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}$ leaves $\dfrac{r_i}{R^2} = \dfrac{1}{r_e}$, giving $r_e = \dfrac{R^2}{r_i} = 9.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. As in the first part, the distances to two points with the same field magnitude, one inside and one outside, have product $R^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A toroid consists of $N = 800$ turns wound on a plastic ring of inner radius $a = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and outer radius $b = 9.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The turns carry current $I = 0.50\,\mathrm{A}$. Calculate the magnetic field at the inner and outer edges of the ring and at a point $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis of the toroid. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
B(r) = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r} \qquad \text{for } a < r < b
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B(a) &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 800 \cdot 0.50}{0.060}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(b) &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 800 \cdot 0.50}{0.090}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
B(3.0\,\mathrm{cm}) = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field of the toroid follows from **Ampère’s law** applied to a circle centered on its axis. Within the ring, each turn passes once through the disk of radius $r$, so the enclosed current is $N I = 400\,\mathrm{A}$ and the field is $\dfrac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r}$. The field is inversely proportional to $r$, so it is greater at the inner edge. The ratio of the two values is $\dfrac{b}{a} = 1.5$.

The point at a distance $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis lies in the central hole. No turn passes through the disk bounded by the circle of radius $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ centered on the axis, so the enclosed current is zero. By symmetry, the field has the same magnitude all around the circle and is tangent to it. Thus zero circulation implies that the field is **zero**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A coaxial cable consists of an inner wire of radius $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and a coaxial outer conducting tube of inner radius $a = 4.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and outer radius $b = 6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The inner wire carries current $I = 20\,\mathrm{A}$ in one direction, and the tube carries the same current in the opposite direction. The current in the tube is distributed uniformly over its cross section. Calculate the magnetic field at distances $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, $5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
B \cdot 2\pi r = \mu_0\, I_{\text{enc}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B = \frac{\mu_0\, I_{\text{enc}}}{2\pi r}
$$
$$
r = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm} : \quad I_{\text{enc}} = 20\,\mathrm{A} ,
\qquad
B = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 20}{3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
r = 5.0\,\mathrm{mm} : \quad I_{\text{enc}} = I - I\,\frac{r^2 - a^2}{b^2 - a^2} = 20\,\mathrm{A} - 20 \cdot \frac{25 - 16}{36 - 16}\,\mathrm{A} = 20\,\mathrm{A} - 9.0\,\mathrm{A} = 11\,\mathrm{A}
$$
$$
B = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 11}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{T} = 4.4 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
r = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm} : \quad I_{\text{enc}} = 20\,\mathrm{A} - 20\,\mathrm{A} = 0 ,
\qquad
B = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cable has cylindrical symmetry, so the field is tangent to circles centered on the axis and has the same magnitude at every point on a given circle. **Ampère’s law** applied to the circle of radius $r$ gives $B \cdot 2\pi r = \mu_0\, I_{\text{enc}}$. It remains to determine the enclosed current in each case, counting current in the inner wire as positive.

At $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, the circle lies between the wire and the tube and encloses the entire current of the wire, $20\,\mathrm{A}$. At $5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, the circle lies within the wall of the tube and encloses both the wire’s current and the portion of the tube’s current flowing in the annular region between $a$ and $r$. The current density in the tube is uniform, so the ratio of this portion to the tube’s total current equals the ratio of area $\pi\,(r^2 - a^2)$ to area $\pi\,(b^2 - a^2)$. With the radii in millimeters, the ratio is $\dfrac{9}{20}$ and this portion is $9.0\,\mathrm{A}$. It flows opposite to the current in the wire, so the enclosed current is $11\,\mathrm{A}$. At $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the circle lies outside the cable and encloses both opposite currents, whose sum is zero.

The field outside the cable is therefore **zero**. This is why coaxial cables can carry current without producing magnetic fields in the surrounding space.

</div>
