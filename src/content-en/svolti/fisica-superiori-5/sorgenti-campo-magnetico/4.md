## Exercise 1

A circular loop of radius $R = 8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries current $I$.

1. Find the value of $I$ for which the magnetic field at the center of the loop has magnitude $3.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$.
2. Calculate the field at the center of a flat coil of $25$ turns of the same radius, carrying the same current.

($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{2 R B}{\mu_0}
$$
$$
I = \frac{2 \cdot 0.080 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-5}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{A} = \frac{4.8 \cdot 10^{-6}}{1.257 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 3.8\,\mathrm{A}
$$
$$
B_{\text{coil}} = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2R} = N B = 25 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} = 7.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the formula for the **field at the center of a circular loop** and solve for current by multiplying both sides by $2R$ and dividing by $\mu_0$. The radius must be expressed in meters. The value of $4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}$ is approximately $1.257 \cdot 10^{-6}$, giving a current of approximately $3.8\,\mathrm{A}$.

The $N$ turns in a flat coil are superimposed, so they all have the same center and each produces the same field there. By the superposition principle, the $25$ fields, all pointing in the same direction, add. The coil’s field is $7.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$, $25$ times the field of a single loop.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solenoid of length $\ell = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ consists of $N = 600$ turns carrying current $I = 1.5\,\mathrm{A}$.

1. Calculate the magnetic field inside the solenoid.
2. A second solenoid carrying a current of $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ must produce an internal field of $1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{T}$. Calculate the number of turns per unit length needed and the wire diameter if the turns are wound side by side in a single layer.

($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
B = \mu_0\,\frac{N}{\ell}\,I = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot \frac{600}{0.30} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{T} = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 3000\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.8 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
B' = \mu_0\, n' I'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
n' = \frac{B'}{\mu_0 I'} = \frac{1.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}} \approx 4.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}
$$
$$
\text{diameter} = \frac{1}{n'} = \frac{1}{3979}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.25\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Assume that the length of the solenoid is much greater than the radius of its turns. Under these conditions the internal field is uniform and equals $B = \mu_0\, n I$, with $n = \dfrac{N}{\ell} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{3}$ turns per meter. The product $n I$ is $3000\,\mathrm{A/m}$, yielding a field of about $3.8 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$.

For the second solenoid, solve the same formula for $n'$ by dividing both sides by $\mu_0 I'$. About $4.0 \cdot 10^{3}$ turns per meter are needed. If the turns are wound side by side in a single layer, each occupies an axial length equal to the wire diameter, and $n'$ turns fit in one meter. The wire diameter is therefore $\dfrac{1}{n'}$, or about $0.25\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular loop of radius $R = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries a counterclockwise current $I_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. An infinitely long straight wire lies in the plane of the loop, at a distance $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the loop’s center $O$, and carries current $I_1 = 10\,\mathrm{A}$ in the direction shown.

1. Calculate the magnetic field at $O$.
2. Determine the magnitude and direction of the current the wire must carry for the field at $O$ to be zero.

($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,O" curve="1.25*cos(t),1.25*sin(t),0,6.29" legenda=0 linee="2.5,-1.6 2.5,1.6;0.150,0.000 0.139,0.057 0.106,0.106 0.057,0.139 0.000,0.150 -0.057,0.139 -0.106,0.106 -0.139,0.057 -0.150,0.000 -0.139,-0.057 -0.106,-0.106 -0.057,-0.139 -0.000,-0.150 0.057,-0.139 0.106,-0.106 0.139,-0.057 0.150,-0.000" vettori="0.884,-0.884,1.2,-0.5,I₂;2.5,-0.6,2.5,0.6,I₁" segmenti="0,0,2.5,0;0,0,-0.884,0.884" etichette="1.9,0.22,10 cm;-0.7,0.3,R" xmin=-1.8 xmax=3.4 ymin=-1.8 ymax=1.8 titolo="The loop carrying counterclockwise current $I_2$ and the straight wire carrying current $I_1$ lie in the same plane. At the center $O$, both fields point out of the page ($\odot$)"}

$$
B_s = \frac{\mu_0 I_2}{2R} = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0}{2 \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
B_f = \frac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi r} = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 10}{0.10}\,\mathrm{T} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
B = B_s + B_f \approx 4.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
\frac{\mu_0 I_1'}{2\pi r} = \frac{\mu_0 I_2}{2R}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I_1' = \frac{\pi r I_2}{R} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.10 \cdot 2.0}{0.050}\,\mathrm{A} = 4\pi\,\mathrm{A} \approx 13\,\mathrm{A}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **superposition principle**, the field at $O$ is the sum of the loop’s field and the wire’s field. The first follows from the formula for the field at the center of a loop, and the second from the Biot–Savart law at distance $r = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Apply the right-hand rule twice to determine the directions. Current flows counterclockwise around the loop, so curling your fingers in that direction points your thumb out of the page. The loop’s field at $O$ therefore points out of the page. The wire carries current upward, and $O$ is to its left. With your thumb pointing upward, your fingers on the left side of the wire point out of the page, so the wire’s field at $O$ also points out of the page. The fields point in the same direction, so their magnitudes add. At $O$ the field has magnitude $4.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ and points out of the page.

For the field at $O$ to be zero, the field of the wire must point into the page and equal the loop’s field in magnitude. Reversing the current reverses the wire’s field, so the current in the wire must flow **downward**. Equating the magnitudes and canceling $\mu_0$ gives $I_1' = \dfrac{\pi r I_2}{R} = 4\pi\,\mathrm{A}$, or approximately $13\,\mathrm{A}$.

</div>
