## Exercise 1

In a laboratory, a Hall probe measures the magnetic field at the center of an empty solenoid of length $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ as the current varies. The results are shown in the table.

| $I$ ($\mathrm{A}$) | $0.50$ | $1.0$ | $1.5$ | $2.0$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $B$ ($\mathrm{mT}$) | $0.94$ | $1.89$ | $2.83$ | $3.77$ |

1. Explain why the data agree with the formula for the field of a solenoid.
2. Find the number of turns per meter and the total number of turns.
3. With a current of $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$, an iron core is inserted into the solenoid and a field of $B = 0.45\,\mathrm{T}$ is measured. Calculate the relative magnetic permeability of iron under these conditions.

($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

::grafico{f="1.885*x" punti="0.5,0.94,pieno;1,1.89,pieno;1.5,2.83,pieno;2,3.77,pieno" nomi="I,B" legenda="B = k I" xmin=0 xmax=2.5 ymin=0 ymax=4.5 titolo="Field measured at the center of the solenoid ($B$ in $\mathrm{mT}$) as a function of current ($I$ in $\mathrm{A}$). The points lie on a straight line through the origin"}

$$
\frac{B}{I} = \frac{0.94}{0.50} = \frac{1.89}{1.0} = \frac{2.83}{1.5} = \frac{3.77}{2.0} \approx 1.885\,\mathrm{mT/A}
$$
$$
B = \mu_0\, n I
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k = \mu_0\, n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
n = \frac{k}{\mu_0} = \frac{1.885 \cdot 10^{-3}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}} \approx 1.50 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}
$$
$$
N = n\,\ell = 1.50 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 0.40 = 600
$$
$$
B_0 = \mu_0\, n I = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 1500 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.77 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} ,
\qquad
\mu_r = \frac{B}{B_0} = \frac{0.45}{3.77 \cdot 10^{-4}} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a long solenoid, the formula $B = \mu_0\, n I$ predicts that the field is **directly proportional** to the current because $\mu_0$ and $n$ are constant. For each of the four measurements, the ratio $\dfrac{B}{I}$ is approximately $1.885\,\mathrm{mT/A}$, so the points lie on a straight line through the origin, as predicted.

The slope of the line is $k = \mu_0\, n$. Express $k$ in $\mathrm{T/A}$ to obtain $1.885 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T/A}$. Dividing by $\mu_0$ then gives $1.50 \cdot 10^{3}$ turns per meter. The solenoid has length $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, so its total number of turns is $600$.

With current $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$, the empty solenoid would produce $B_0 \approx 3.8 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$. By the definition of **relative magnetic permeability**, $\mu_r$ is the ratio of the field with the core to the field in a vacuum. It is approximately $1.2 \cdot 10^{3}$, a value much greater than $1$, as expected for a ferromagnetic material.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A very long straight wire carries a constant current $I$. A probe measures the magnetic field at several distances $r$ from the wire. The results are shown in the table.

| $r$ ($\mathrm{cm}$) | $2.5$ | $3.0$ | $4.0$ | $5.0$ | $8.0$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $B$ ($\mu\mathrm{T}$) | $120$ | $100$ | $75$ | $60$ | $37.5$ |

1. Show that the data agree with the Biot–Savart law.
2. Determine the current in the wire.
3. A second wire is placed parallel to the first at a distance $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from it and carries current $I_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the opposite direction. Find the point on the line perpendicular to both wires where the total field is zero.
4. Calculate the force per unit length between the wires and the circulation of the field around a circle enclosing both wires, traversed in the direction of the field lines of the first wire.

($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

::grafico{f="30/x" punti="2.5,12,pieno;3,10,pieno;4,7.5,pieno;5,6,pieno;8,3.75,pieno" nomi="r,B" legenda=0 xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=14 titolo="Field of the wire as a function of distance, with $r$ in $\mathrm{cm}$ and $B$ in units of $10\,\mu\mathrm{T}$. The points lie on one branch of a hyperbola because the product $B r$ is constant"}

$$
B\,r = 120 \cdot 2.5 = 100 \cdot 3.0 = 75 \cdot 4.0 = 60 \cdot 5.0 = 37.5 \cdot 8.0 = 300\,\mu\mathrm{T\cdot cm} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}
$$
$$
B\,r = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{2\pi\,B\,r}{\mu_0} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{2 \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{A} = 15\,\mathrm{A}
$$
$$
\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi x} = \frac{\mu_0 I_2}{2\pi\,(x - d)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I\,(x - d) = I_2\,x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{I\,d}{I - I_2} = \frac{15 \cdot 6.0}{15 - 5.0}\,\mathrm{cm} = 9.0\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\frac{F}{\ell} = \frac{\mu_0 I I_2}{2\pi d} = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 15 \cdot 5.0}{0.060}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 2.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
\Gamma(\vec{B}) = \mu_0\,(I - I_2) = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Biot–Savart law predicts that $B$ is inversely proportional to $r$, meaning that the product $B\,r$ is constant and equals $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}$. For all five measurements, the product is $300\,\mu\mathrm{T\cdot cm}$. The data are therefore **consistent** with the law, and the points lie on one branch of a hyperbola. To convert the product to SI units, multiply by $10^{-6}$ and by $10^{-2}$ to obtain $3.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$. Dividing by $\dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi} = 2 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$ gives the current, $15\,\mathrm{A}$.

The two wires carry currents in opposite directions. By the right-hand rule, the fields between the wires point in the same direction and add, so they cannot cancel. Outside the wires, the fields are opposite. They can cancel only on the side of the wire carrying the smaller current, because the shorter distance to the second wire compensates for the stronger field of the first wire. Let $x$ be the distance from the first wire to a point beyond the second, so that the distance from the second wire is $x - d$. Equating the magnitudes gives $x = 9.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The zero-field point is therefore $9.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the first wire and $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the second, on the side away from the first. As a check, $\dfrac{15}{9.0} = \dfrac{5.0}{3.0}$.

The force between the wires follows from the formula for the **force between two parallel wires**. It is **repulsive** because the currents are opposite. The circle encloses both currents. It is traversed in the direction of the field lines of the first wire, so $I$ is counted as positive and $I_2$, which flows in the opposite direction, as negative. By **Ampère’s law**, the circulation is $\mu_0\,(15 - 5.0)\,\mathrm{A}$, or approximately $1.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$, whatever the radius of the circle.

</div>
