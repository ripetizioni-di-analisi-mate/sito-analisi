## Exercise 1

The two conductors in a cable are straight, parallel, and a distance $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. They carry currents $I_1 = 12\,\mathrm{A}$ and $I_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in opposite directions. Calculate the force per unit length between the conductors and the force on a section of length $\ell = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. State whether the force is attractive or repulsive. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
F = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2\,\ell}{2\pi d}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{F}{\ell} = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}
$$
$$
\frac{F}{\ell} = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 12 \cdot 5.0}{0.020}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
F = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{N} = 9.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The separation of the conductors is much smaller than their length. They can therefore be treated as infinitely long wires, and the formula for the **force between two parallel wires** applies. Dividing by $\ell$ gives the force per unit length, independent of the length of the section considered. Multiplying by $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ gives the force on the section, $9.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N}$.

The currents flow in opposite directions, so the force is **repulsive**. Each conductor is pushed away from the other. The forces on the two conductors have the same magnitude even though the currents differ, because the currents enter the formula as a product and Newton’s third law requires the forces to be equal and opposite.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three infinitely long, straight, parallel wires lie in the same plane and are equally spaced, with a distance $d = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ between adjacent wires. Wire $2$ is in the middle. Wires $1$ and $2$ carry currents $I_1 = 10\,\mathrm{A}$ and $I_2 = 20\,\mathrm{A}$ upward, and wire $3$ carries current $I_3 = 10\,\mathrm{A}$ downward. Calculate the net force per unit length on each wire. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
f_{ij} = \frac{\mu_0 I_i I_j}{2\pi\,d_{ij}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{12} &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 10 \cdot 20}{0.050}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N/m} \quad \text{attractive} \\[6pt]
f_{23} &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 20 \cdot 10}{0.050}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N/m} \quad \text{repulsive} \\[6pt]
f_{13} &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 10 \cdot 10}{0.10}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N/m} \quad \text{repulsive}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{F_1}{\ell} &= f_{12} - f_{13} = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N/m} \quad \text{to the right} \\[6pt]
\frac{F_2}{\ell} &= f_{12} + f_{23} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N/m} \quad \text{to the left} \\[6pt]
\frac{F_3}{\ell} &= f_{23} + f_{13} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N/m} \quad \text{to the right}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,4.2;2,0 2,4.2;4,0 4,4.2" vettori="0,0.4,0,1.3,I₁;2,0.4,2,1.3,I₂;4,1.3,4,0.4,I₃;0,2.6,0.6,2.6,F₁;2,3.3,0.4,3.3,F₂;4,2.6,5,2.6,F₃" segmenti="0,3.9,2,3.9;2,3.9,4,3.9" punti="0,2.6,pieno;2,3.3,pieno;4,2.6,pieno" etichette="0.9,4.05,d;2.9,4.05,d" xmin=-0.8 xmax=5.6 ymin=-0.2 ymax=4.4 titolo="Wires $1$ and $2$ carry currents in the same direction, and wire $3$ carries current in the opposite direction. The net forces per unit length are $6.0 \cdot 10^{-4}$, $1.6 \cdot 10^{-3}$, and $1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N/m}$, and their sum is zero"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each pair of wires interacts according to the formula for the **force between two parallel wires**. The force on each wire is the sum of the forces due to the other two. Let $f_{ij}$ denote the magnitude of the force per unit length between wires $i$ and $j$. Wires $1$ and $3$ are a distance $2d = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. Currents $I_1$ and $I_2$ flow in the same direction, so wires $1$ and $2$ attract. Current $I_3$ flows opposite to the other two, so wire $3$ repels both wire $1$ and wire $2$.

Wire $1$ is attracted to the right by wire $2$ and repelled to the left by wire $3$. The attraction is stronger, so the net force points to the right. Wire $2$ is attracted to the left by wire $1$ and repelled to the left by wire $3$, so the two forces add. Wire $3$ is repelled to the right by both other wires.

As a check, take forces to the right as positive. This gives $6.0 \cdot 10^{-4} - 1.6 \cdot 10^{-3} + 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} = 0$. The sum is zero, as Newton’s third law requires for the internal forces of a system.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A long straight wire is fixed horizontally on a table. A second wire of length $\ell = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ and mass $m = 0.10\,\mathrm{g}$ is parallel to and above it. It can move only vertically along two frictionless guides. The wires carry the same current $I$ in opposite directions.

1. Find the value of $I$ for which the upper wire remains in equilibrium at a distance $d = 4.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the lower wire.
2. Find the equilibrium separation if the current is halved.

($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$, $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$)

$$
\frac{\mu_0 I^2\,\ell}{2\pi d} = m g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi d\,m g}{\mu_0\,\ell}}
$$
$$
I = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 9.81}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 0.30}}\,\mathrm{A} = \sqrt{\frac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 10^{-4}}{0.60 \cdot 10^{-7}}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 8.1\,\mathrm{A}
$$
$$
d = \frac{\mu_0 I^2\,\ell}{2\pi\,m g}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d' = \frac{\mu_0 \left( \dfrac{I}{2} \right)^2 \ell}{2\pi\,m g} = \frac{d}{4} = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The upper wire is acted on by its weight, directed downward, and by the magnetic force due to the lower wire. The currents are opposite, so by the formula for the **force between two parallel wires** the magnetic force is repulsive and points upward. The frictionless guides exert only horizontal forces. Thus at equilibrium the magnetic force equals the weight $m g$. Distance $d$ is much smaller than the length of the wire, so the formula for infinitely long wires applies. Solving gives $I^2$ and then $I$. The mass must be expressed in kilograms, $0.10\,\mathrm{g} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{kg}$. The required current is $8.1\,\mathrm{A}$.

The equilibrium equation also gives $d = \dfrac{\mu_0 I^2\,\ell}{2\pi\,m g}$, so the equilibrium separation is proportional to $I^2$. When the current is halved, $I^2$ falls to one-quarter of its original value. The new equilibrium separation is therefore one-quarter of the previous separation, $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The upper wire descends until the repulsive force, which increases as the separation decreases, once again balances the weight.

</div>
