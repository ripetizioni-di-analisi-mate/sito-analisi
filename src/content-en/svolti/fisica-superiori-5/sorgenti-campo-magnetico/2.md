## Exercise 1

Two infinitely long, straight, parallel wires perpendicular to the page are $d = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. Wire $1$ carries current $I_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{A}$ out of the page ($\odot$), and wire $2$ carries current $I_2 = 4.0\,\mathrm{A}$ into the page ($\otimes$). Calculate the magnetic field at the midpoint $M$ of the segment joining the wires. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi r} ,
\qquad
B_2 = \frac{\mu_0 I_2}{2\pi r} ,
\qquad
r = \frac{d}{2} = 0.050\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_1 &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 6.0}{0.050}\,\mathrm{T} = 2.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_2 &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 4.0}{0.050}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
B = B_1 + B_2 = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **superposition principle**, the field at $M$ is the vector sum of the fields of the two wires. Each field follows from the Biot–Savart law at distance $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.

To determine the directions, place wire $1$ on the left and wire $2$ on the right. Current $I_1$ flows out of the page, so by the right-hand rule its field lines run counterclockwise. At $M$, to the right of wire $1$, field $\vec{B}_1$ points up. Current $I_2$ flows into the page, so its field lines run clockwise. At $M$, to the left of wire $2$, field $\vec{B}_2$ also points up. The fields point in the same direction, so their magnitudes add. At $M$ the field has magnitude $4.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ and is perpendicular to the line joining the wires. Between two wires carrying opposite currents, the fields always point in the same direction and add.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two infinitely long, straight, parallel wires are $d = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart and carry currents $I_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{A}$ and $I_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the same direction. Find the point on the line joining the wires where the magnetic field is zero.

$$
B_1 = B_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi x} = \frac{\mu_0 I_2}{2\pi\,(d - x)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{I_1}{x} = \frac{I_2}{d - x}
$$
$$
I_1\,(d - x) = I_2\,x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{I_1\,d}{I_1 + I_2}
$$
$$
x = \frac{6.0 \cdot 10}{6.0 + 2.0}\,\mathrm{cm} = 7.5\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The currents flow in the same direction, for example, both out of the page. By the right-hand rule, at a point between the wires the field of wire $1$ and the field of wire $2$ point in opposite directions. Beyond the ends of the segment they point in the same direction. The total field can therefore vanish only between the wires, at the point where the two magnitudes are equal.

Let $x$ be the distance from the point to wire $1$, so that its distance from wire $2$ is $d - x$. Equating the field magnitudes cancels the factor $\dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi}$. Cross-multiplying gives a linear equation whose solution is $x = 7.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The zero-field point is therefore $7.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from wire $1$ and $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from wire $2$, closer to the wire carrying the smaller current. As a check, $\dfrac{6.0}{7.5} = \dfrac{2.0}{2.5} = 0.80$, so the two field magnitudes are equal.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two infinitely long straight wires perpendicular to the page pass through points $1$ and $2$, which are $8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. Their currents, $I_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ and $I_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$, both flow out of the page. Point $P$ lies a distance $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from wire $1$ on a line perpendicular to the line joining the wires. This perpendicular passes through wire $1$. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the magnetic field at $P$. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,1;8,0,pieno,2;0,6,pieno,P" linee="0.250,0.000 0.231,0.096 0.177,0.177 0.096,0.231 0.000,0.250 -0.096,0.231 -0.177,0.177 -0.231,0.096 -0.250,0.000 -0.231,-0.096 -0.177,-0.177 -0.096,-0.231 -0.000,-0.250 0.096,-0.231 0.177,-0.177 0.231,-0.096 0.250,-0.000;8.250,0.000 8.231,0.096 8.177,0.177 8.096,0.231 8.000,0.250 7.904,0.231 7.823,0.177 7.769,0.096 7.750,0.000 7.769,-0.096 7.823,-0.177 7.904,-0.231 8.000,-0.250 8.096,-0.231 8.177,-0.177 8.231,-0.096 8.250,-0.000" segmenti="0,0,0,6;8,0,0,6" vettori="0,6,-2.5,6,B₁;0,6,-1.5,4,B₂;0,6,-4,4,B" etichette="-0.9,3,6 cm;4.6,3.5,10 cm;4,-0.7,8 cm" xmin=-5 xmax=9.5 ymin=-1.2 ymax=7.2 titolo="Both currents flow out of the page ($\odot$). At $P$, field $\vec{B}_1$ is perpendicular to the line to wire $1$, and field $\vec{B}_2$ is perpendicular to the line to wire $2$. The total field $\vec{B}$ is their vector sum"}

$$
r_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm} ,
\qquad
r_2 = \sqrt{8.0^2 + 6.0^2}\,\mathrm{cm} = 10\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_1 &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 3.0}{0.060}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_2 &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 5.0}{0.10}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\vec{B}_1 = (-1.0;\ 0) \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} ,
\qquad
\vec{B}_2 = B_2 \left( -\frac{6.0}{10};\ -\frac{8.0}{10} \right) = (-0.60;\ -0.80) \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
\vec{B} = \vec{B}_1 + \vec{B}_2 = (-1.6;\ -0.80) \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
B = \sqrt{1.6^2 + 0.80^2} \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} ,
\qquad
\tan\alpha = \frac{0.80}{1.6} = 0.50
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha \approx 27^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose a coordinate system with its origin at wire $1$, its $x$-axis toward wire $2$, and its $y$-axis toward $P$. Wire $2$ is then at $(8.0;\ 0)$ and $P$ at $(0;\ 6.0)$, with coordinates in centimeters. By the Pythagorean theorem, the distance from $P$ to wire $2$ is $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. The Biot–Savart law gives a magnitude of $1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ for each field.

The currents flow out of the page, so the field lines of each wire are counterclockwise circles. At $P$, each field is tangent to the circle passing through $P$, hence perpendicular to the radius from its wire to $P$. The radius from wire $1$ to $P$ points vertically upward. Thus $\vec{B}_1$ is horizontal and, because the field runs counterclockwise, points to the left. The radius from wire $2$ to $P$ points in the direction of the unit vector $\left( -\dfrac{8.0}{10};\ \dfrac{6.0}{10} \right)$. Rotating it by $90^\circ$ counterclockwise gives the unit vector $\left( -\dfrac{6.0}{10};\ -\dfrac{8.0}{10} \right)$, which points in the direction of $\vec{B}_2$.

By the **superposition principle**, we add the components. The total field has magnitude $1.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ and points down and to the left. Its angle with the negative $x$-axis is $\alpha \approx 27^\circ$.

</div>
