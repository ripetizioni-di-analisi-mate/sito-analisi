## Exercise 1

An infinitely long straight wire carries current $I = 20\,\mathrm{A}$. Calculate the magnetic field at a distance $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire and the circulation of the field around a circle of radius $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ centered on the wire in a plane perpendicular to it. Traverse the circle in the direction of the field lines. Compare the result with Ampère’s law. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 20}{0.050}\,\mathrm{T} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
\Gamma(\vec{B}) = B \cdot 2\pi r = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 2\pi \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}
$$
$$
\mu_0 I = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field follows from the Biot–Savart law. Along the circle centered on the wire, the field is tangent to the path, points in the direction of traversal, and has the same magnitude everywhere because all points are a distance $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire. Thus each product $\vec{B} \cdot \Delta\vec{\ell}$ equals $B\,\Delta\ell$, and the **circulation** is $B$ times the circumference, $2.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$.

The circle encloses only current $I$, counted as positive because the path follows the field lines. **Ampère’s law** therefore predicts a circulation $\mu_0 I$, which has the same value. The value would also be the same for a circle of a different radius or a closed path of any shape winding once around the wire.

</div>

Calculate the circulation of the magnetic field around each closed path $\gamma_1$, $\gamma_2$, and $\gamma_3$ shown in the figure, traversed counterclockwise. $$
\Gamma(\vec{B}) = \mu_0 \sum I_{\text{enc}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{\gamma_1} &= \mu_0\,(I_1 - I_2) = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot (4.0 - 7.0)\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \approx -3.8 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \\[6pt]
\Gamma_{\gamma_2} &= \mu_0\,(-I_2 + I_3) = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot (-7.0 + 2.0)\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \approx -6.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \\[6pt]
\Gamma_{\gamma_3} &= \mu_0\,(I_1 - I_2 + I_3) = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot (-1.0)\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \approx -1.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply **Ampère’s law** to each path. The paths are traversed counterclockwise, so by the right-hand rule currents out of the page count as positive, and currents into the page count as negative.

Path $\gamma_1$ encloses wires $1$ and $2$, giving a net enclosed current of $4.0 - 7.0 = -3.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Path $\gamma_2$ encloses wires $2$ and $3$, giving $-5.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Path $\gamma_3$ encloses all three wires, giving $-1.0\,\mathrm{A}$. The circulations are all **negative** because in each case the inward current $I_2$ exceeds the outward current. A wire outside a path does not enter the expression for its circulation, even though that wire’s field is nonzero at points along the path.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An infinitely long straight wire perpendicular to the page carries current $I = 10\,\mathrm{A}$ out of the page. A closed path consists of a circular arc of radius $r_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ subtending an angle $90^\circ$ and centered on the wire, a concentric arc of radius $r_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ subtending the same angle, and two radial segments joining the endpoints of the arcs. The path is traversed counterclockwise, so the outer arc is traversed in the direction of the field lines. Calculate the contribution of each segment to the circulation and the total circulation. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
B_1 = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 10}{0.020}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} ,
\qquad
B_2 = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 10}{0.050}\,\mathrm{T} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$
$$
\ell_1 = \frac{\pi}{2}\,r_1 \approx 3.14 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\ell_2 = \frac{\pi}{2}\,r_2 \approx 7.85 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{outer arc} &: \quad +B_2\,\ell_2 \approx +3.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \\[6pt]
\text{inner arc} &: \quad -B_1\,\ell_1 \approx -3.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m} \\[6pt]
\text{radial sections} &: \quad 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\Gamma(\vec{B}) = B_2\,\ell_2 - B_1\,\ell_1 = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r_2} \cdot \frac{\pi r_2}{2} - \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r_1} \cdot \frac{\pi r_1}{2} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4} - \frac{\mu_0 I}{4} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wire’s field lines are circles centered on the wire, running counterclockwise because the current flows out of the page. On each arc, the field is therefore tangent and has constant magnitude, given by the Biot–Savart law. The length of an arc subtending $90^\circ$ is one-quarter of the circumference, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,r$.

The outer arc is traversed in the direction of the field and therefore contributes $+B_2\,\ell_2$. To return to the starting point, the inner arc is traversed opposite to the field and contributes $-B_1\,\ell_1$. On the radial segments, the field is perpendicular to the displacement, so their contribution is zero. The field on the inner arc is stronger, but the arc is shorter by the same factor. The two contributions therefore have the same magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4}$.

The total circulation is **zero**. This is the result predicted by Ampère’s law, because the wire lies outside the closed path and the path encloses no current.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A closed path in the plane of the page, traversed counterclockwise, encloses two straight wires perpendicular to the page. The first wire carries current $I_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ out of the page. Measurements with a sensor give a circulation $\Gamma(\vec{B}) = -3.77 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m}$ for the magnetic field around the path. Determine the magnitude and direction of the current in the second wire. ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$)

$$
\Gamma(\vec{B}) = \mu_0\,(I_1 + I_2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I_1 + I_2 = \frac{\Gamma(\vec{B})}{\mu_0}
$$
$$
I_1 + I_2 = \frac{-3.77 \cdot 10^{-6}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx -3.0\,\mathrm{A}
$$
$$
I_2 = -3.0\,\mathrm{A} - 5.0\,\mathrm{A} = -8.0\,\mathrm{A}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By **Ampère’s law**, the circulation equals $\mu_0$ times the sum of the enclosed currents. Let $I_2$ be the signed current in the second wire. It is positive if it flows out of the page and negative if it flows into the page because the path is traversed counterclockwise. Dividing the circulation by $\mu_0$ gives the sum of the enclosed currents, $-3.0\,\mathrm{A}$.

Subtracting the known current $I_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ gives $I_2 = -8.0\,\mathrm{A}$. The negative sign indicates that the current in the second wire **flows into the page**, and its magnitude is $8.0\,\mathrm{A}$. The circulation is negative because the inward current is greater than the outward current.

</div>
