## Exercise 1

An electron has kinetic energy $K = 2.00\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Calculate its total energy, its momentum in $\mathrm{MeV}/c$ and in $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$, and its speed in units of $c$. Use $m_e c^2 = 0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $1\,\mathrm{MeV}/c = 5.33 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$.

$$
E = K + m_e c^2 ,
\qquad
p c = \sqrt{E^2 - m_e^2 c^4} ,
\qquad
\beta = \frac{p c}{E}
$$
$$
E = 2.00 + 0.511 = 2.511\,\mathrm{MeV}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
p c &= \sqrt{2.511^2 - 0.511^2}\,\mathrm{MeV} = \sqrt{6.305 - 0.261}\,\mathrm{MeV} \approx 2.46\,\mathrm{MeV} \\[6pt]
p &\approx 2.46\,\mathrm{MeV}/c = 2.46 \cdot 5.33 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 1.31 \cdot 10^{-21}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \\[6pt]
\beta &= \frac{2.458}{2.511} \approx 0.979
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of relativistic kinetic energy, the total energy is the sum of the kinetic and rest energies. From the **energy–momentum relation** $E^2 = p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4$, solve for $p^2 c^2 = E^2 - m^2 c^4$ and take the positive square root. The product $pc$ is obtained in $\mathrm{MeV}$, so $p$ is expressed in $\mathrm{MeV}/c$.

The ratio $\dfrac{pc}{E}$ equals $\beta$ and gives the speed. This yields $\beta \approx 0.979$, so the electron moves at nearly $98\%$ of the speed of light. This method avoids calculating first $\gamma$ and then $\beta$. Using the Lorentz factor $\gamma = \dfrac{E}{m_e c^2} \approx 4.91$ gives the same value of $\beta$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A proton in an accelerator has momentum $p = 1.00 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{MeV}/c$. Calculate its total energy, kinetic energy, speed in units of $c$, and Lorentz factor. Use $m_p c^2 = 938\,\mathrm{MeV}$.

$$
E = \sqrt{p^2 c^2 + m_p^2 c^4} = \sqrt{1000^2 + 938^2}\,\mathrm{MeV} \approx 1371\,\mathrm{MeV}
$$
$$
K = E - m_p c^2 \approx 1371 - 938 = 433\,\mathrm{MeV}
$$
$$
\beta = \frac{p c}{E} = \frac{1000}{1371} \approx 0.729 ,
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{E}{m_p c^2} = \frac{1371}{938} \approx 1.46
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The momentum is given in $\mathrm{MeV}/c$, so $pc = 1000\,\mathrm{MeV}$, and the **energy–momentum relation** immediately gives the total energy. In this relation $E$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $pc$ and $m_p c^2$. The legs are nearly equal, so the hypotenuse is about $\sqrt{2}$ times either leg.

The kinetic energy is the difference between the total and rest energies. The speed follows from the ratio $\dfrac{pc}{E}$. The Lorentz factor is the ratio of total energy to rest energy because $E = \gamma\, m_p c^2$. As a check, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.729^2}} \approx 1.46$, in agreement with the value found above.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A detector measures the kinetic energy $K = 100\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and the momentum $p = 176\,\mathrm{MeV}/c$ of an unknown particle. Determine its mass in $\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$ and identify it from the following candidates: electron ($0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$), muon ($105.7\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$), charged pion ($139.6\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$), or proton ($938\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$).

$$
E = K + m c^2 ,
\qquad
E^2 = p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4
$$
$$
\left( K + m c^2 \right)^2 = p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
K^2 + 2 K m c^2 = p^2 c^2
$$
$$
m c^2 = \frac{p^2 c^2 - K^2}{2K}
$$
$$
m c^2 = \frac{176^2 - 100^2}{2 \cdot 100}\,\mathrm{MeV} = \frac{30\,976 - 10\,000}{200}\,\mathrm{MeV} \approx 105\,\mathrm{MeV}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown is the mass, which appears both in the total energy $E = K + m c^2$ and in the energy–momentum relation. Substitute the first expression into the second and expand the square to obtain $\left( K + m c^2 \right)^2 = K^2 + 2 K m c^2 + m^2 c^4$. The term $m^2 c^4$ appears on both sides and cancels, leaving a linear equation in $m c^2$.

Isolate $m c^2$ and substitute the data to obtain approximately $105\,\mathrm{MeV}$. The mass is therefore about $105\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$, identifying the particle as a **muon**. Given the uncertainty in measurements reported to three significant figures, the value is consistent with $105.7\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$ and far from the masses of the other candidates.

</div>
