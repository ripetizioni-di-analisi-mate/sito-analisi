## Exercise 1

An electron moves at speed $v = 0.600\,c$. Calculate its Lorentz factor, momentum, rest energy, total energy, and kinetic energy. Express the kinetic energy in $\mathrm{MeV}$. Use $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1\,\mathrm{MeV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J}$.

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}} ,
\qquad
p = \gamma\, m_e v ,
\qquad
E_0 = m_e c^2 ,
\qquad
E = \gamma\, m_e c^2 ,
\qquad
K = E - E_0
$$
$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.600^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.640}} = \frac{1}{0.800} = 1.25
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 1.25 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 1.80 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 2.05 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \\[6pt]
E_0 &= 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot \left( 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \right)^2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 8.20 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
E &= 1.25 \cdot 8.20 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.02 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
K &= (1.25 - 1) \cdot 8.20 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.05 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
K &= \frac{2.05 \cdot 10^{-14}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-13}}\,\mathrm{MeV} \approx 0.128\,\mathrm{MeV}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First calculate the **Lorentz factor**, which appears in all the other formulas. With $\beta = 0.600$ we have $\beta^2 = 0.360$ and $1 - \beta^2 = 0.640$, whose square root is $0.800$. Hence $\gamma = 1.25$.

The electron's speed is $v = 0.600 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.80 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its relativistic momentum is the product $\gamma\, m_e v$, so it is $1.25$ times its classical momentum $m_e v \approx 1.64 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$.

The rest energy $m_e c^2$ is independent of speed. The total energy is $\gamma$ times the rest energy, and the kinetic energy is the difference between the two, $(\gamma - 1)\, m_e c^2 = 0.25\, m_e c^2$. To convert from joules to $\mathrm{MeV}$, divide by $1.60 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J/MeV}$, giving $K \approx 0.128\,\mathrm{MeV}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A proton in a beam moves at speed $v = 0.950\,c$. Calculate the ratio of its relativistic momentum to its classical momentum $m_p v$, the value of the momentum in $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$, and its total and kinetic energies in $\mathrm{MeV}$. Use $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $m_p c^2 = 938\,\mathrm{MeV}$, and $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\frac{p}{m_p v} = \frac{\gamma\, m_p v}{m_p v} = \gamma ,
\qquad
E = \gamma\, m_p c^2 ,
\qquad
K = (\gamma - 1)\, m_p c^2
$$
$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.950^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.0975}} \approx 3.20
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 3.20 \cdot 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 2.85 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 1.52 \cdot 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \\[6pt]
E &= 3.20 \cdot 938\,\mathrm{MeV} \approx 3.00 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{MeV} \\[6pt]
K &= (3.20 - 1) \cdot 938\,\mathrm{MeV} \approx 2.07 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{MeV}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the ratio of relativistic to classical momentum, the factors $m_p$ and $v$ cancel, so the ratio equals $\gamma$. With $\beta = 0.950$ we have $1 - \beta^2 = 1 - 0.9025 = 0.0975$, whose square root is about $0.312$. Hence $\gamma \approx 3.20$. The relativistic momentum is therefore **more than three times** the classical momentum.

The speed is $v = 0.950 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.85 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Multiplying by $\gamma\, m_p$ gives the momentum. The rest energy of the proton is given in $\mathrm{MeV}$, so its total and kinetic energies can be calculated directly in $\mathrm{MeV}$ without converting to joules. The kinetic energy, about $2.07 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{MeV}$, is more than twice the rest energy. The classical formula $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$ would give about $423\,\mathrm{MeV}$, a substantially different value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the speed at which a particle's total energy is twice its rest energy. For an electron moving at this speed, calculate its kinetic energy and its momentum, expressing the momentum in $\mathrm{MeV}/c$ and in $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. Use $m_e c^2 = 0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1\,\mathrm{MeV}/c = 5.33 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$.

$$
E = 2 E_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\gamma\, m c^2 = 2\, m c^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\gamma = 2
$$
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}} = 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
1 - \beta^2 = \frac{1}{4}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866
$$
$$
v = 0.866 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.60 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
K = (\gamma - 1)\, m_e c^2 = m_e c^2 = 0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= \gamma\, m_e v = \gamma \beta\, \frac{m_e c^2}{c} = 2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot 0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}/c \approx 0.885\,\mathrm{MeV}/c \\[6pt]
p &= 0.885 \cdot 5.33 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 4.72 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The total energy is $\gamma$ times the rest energy, so the condition $E = 2 E_0$ is equivalent to $\gamma = 2$. This condition is independent of mass and therefore applies to every particle. Squaring the equation $\sqrt{1 - \beta^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ gives $1 - \beta^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$, or $\beta^2 = \dfrac{3}{4}$. Since $\beta$ is positive, $\beta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and the speed is approximately $0.866\,c$.

With $\gamma = 2$ the kinetic energy $(\gamma - 1)\, m_e c^2$ equals the rest energy, so for an electron it is $0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$. For the momentum, write $m_e v = \beta\, m_e c = \beta\, \dfrac{m_e c^2}{c}$. The rest energy then appears in $\mathrm{MeV}$ and the result is expressed directly in $\mathrm{MeV}/c$. Multiplying by $5.33 \cdot 10^{-22}$ converts the result to SI units.

</div>
