## Exercise 1

The electric field of an electromagnetic wave traveling in a vacuum along the $x$ axis is $E(x,t) = 30 \sin\left( 2.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,x - 6.0 \cdot 10^{15}\,t \right)$, with $E$ in $\mathrm{V/m}$, $x$ in meters, and $t$ in seconds. Determine the wavelength, frequency, and wave speed, and write the expression for the magnetic field. Identify the band of the spectrum to which the wave belongs ($c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

$$
E(x,t) = E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t) ,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{2\pi}{k} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{\omega}{k} ,
\qquad
B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}
$$
$$
E_0 = 30\,\mathrm{V/m} ,
\qquad
k = 2.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{rad/m} ,
\qquad
\omega = 6.0 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{2\pi}{2.0 \cdot 10^{7}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 3.1 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
f &= \frac{6.0 \cdot 10^{15}}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{Hz} \approx 9.5 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
v &= \frac{6.0 \cdot 10^{15}}{2.0 \cdot 10^{7}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
B_0 &= \frac{30}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
B(x,t) = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-7} \sin\left( 2.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,x - 6.0 \cdot 10^{15}\,t \right)
\qquad (B \text{ in } \mathrm{T})
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare the given expression with the general form of a **sinusoidal plane wave**, $E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)$. The coefficient multiplying the sine is the amplitude. The coefficient of $x$ is the wave number $k$, and the negative of the coefficient of $t$ is the angular frequency $\omega$. The definitions $k = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}$ and $\omega = 2\pi f$ give the wavelength and frequency.

The speed is the ratio $\dfrac{\omega}{k}$ and equals $3.0 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, as required for an electromagnetic wave in a vacuum. The minus sign before $\omega t$ indicates that the wave travels in the positive $x$ direction.

In the wave, the magnetic field oscillates in phase with the electric field, so the sine has the same argument. The relation $E = cB$ gives a magnetic field amplitude $\dfrac{E_0}{c} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}$. The wavelength, about $310\,\mathrm{nm}$, is less than $380\,\mathrm{nm}$, placing the wave in the **ultraviolet** band.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An antenna emits a sinusoidal electromagnetic wave of period $T = 4.0\,\mathrm{ns}$ that travels in a vacuum in the positive $x$ direction. The electric field has amplitude $E_0 = 3.0\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and at time $t = 0$ is $E(x,0) = E_0 \sin(kx)$. Calculate the frequency, wavelength, angular frequency, wave number, and magnetic field amplitude. Write the expression for $E(x,t)$ and plot the electric field against $x$ at times $t = 0$ and $t = \dfrac{T}{4}$ ($c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

$$
f = \frac{1}{T} ,
\qquad
\lambda = c\,T ,
\qquad
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} ,
\qquad
k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} ,
\qquad
B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{1}{4.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}} = 2.5 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
\lambda &= 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.2\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\omega &= \frac{2\pi}{4.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
k &= \frac{2\pi}{1.2}\,\mathrm{rad/m} \approx 5.2\,\mathrm{rad/m} \\[6pt]
B_0 &= \frac{3.0}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
E(x,t) = 3.0 \sin\left( 5.2\,x - 1.6 \cdot 10^{9}\,t \right)
\qquad (E \text{ in } \mathrm{V/m})
$$

::grafico{f="3*sin(pi*x/6);-3*cos(pi*x/6)" xmin=0 xmax=24 ymin=-4 ymax=4 nomi="x,E" legenda="t = 0;t = \tfrac{T}{4}" titolo="Electric field in $\mathrm{V/m}$ as a function of $x$ in decimeters. In time $\dfrac{T}{4}$ the profile moves a distance $\dfrac{\lambda}{4} = 3\,\mathrm{dm}$ to the right"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The frequency is the reciprocal of the period. In one period, the wave advances one wavelength, so $\lambda = c\,T = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$. The same result follows from $\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f}$. The angular frequency and wave number follow from their definitions, and the magnetic field amplitude from the relation $E = cB$.

The wave travels in the positive $x$ direction and at $t = 0$ its field is $E_0 \sin(kx)$, so its expression is $E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)$ with the values just found. At time $t = \dfrac{T}{4}$, we have $\omega t = \dfrac{2\pi}{T} \cdot \dfrac{T}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the field becomes $E_0 \sin\left( kx - \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = -E_0 \cos(kx)$.

In the graph, the wavelength is $12\,\mathrm{dm}$. Each crest of the second curve is displaced $3\,\mathrm{dm}$ to the right of the corresponding crest of the first because, in time $\dfrac{T}{4}$, the wave has traveled a distance $c \cdot \dfrac{T}{4} = \dfrac{\lambda}{4}$ in the direction of propagation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A helium–neon laser emits red light of wavelength $\lambda = 633\,\mathrm{nm}$ that travels in a vacuum as a sinusoidal plane wave with electric field amplitude $E_0 = 2.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{V/m}$. Calculate the wave number, angular frequency, and magnetic field amplitude. Then calculate the distance between a point where the electric field is at its maximum and the nearest point where the field is zero at the same instant. Also find the time at a fixed point between a field maximum and the next zero ($c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

$$
k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} ,
\qquad
\omega = c\,k = \frac{2\pi c}{\lambda} ,
\qquad
B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c} ,
\qquad
\Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{4} ,
\qquad
\Delta t = \frac{T}{4} = \frac{\lambda}{4c}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
k &= \frac{2\pi}{6.33 \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{rad/m} \approx 9.93 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{rad/m} \\[6pt]
\omega &= 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 9.93 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 2.98 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
B_0 &= \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{3}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 6.7 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
\Delta x &= \frac{633\,\mathrm{nm}}{4} \approx 158\,\mathrm{nm} \\[6pt]
\Delta t &= \frac{6.33 \cdot 10^{-7}}{4 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 5.3 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wave number follows from its definition, with the wavelength in meters. The **relationship between wavelength and frequency** gives $c = \dfrac{\omega}{k}$, so the angular frequency is the product $c\,k$. The magnetic field amplitude follows from $E = cB$.

At a fixed instant, the field is $E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)$, a sinusoidal function of $x$ with period $\lambda$. A maximum of the sine function and its nearest zero are one quarter of a period apart, so the required distance is $\dfrac{\lambda}{4} \approx 158\,\mathrm{nm}$. Similarly, at a fixed point, the field is a sinusoidal function of $t$ with period $T$, so the time between a maximum and the next zero is $\dfrac{T}{4}$. Since $T = \dfrac{\lambda}{c}$, this time is about $5.3 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{s}$. Instruments cannot follow oscillations this fast. For that reason, light detectors measure only the average intensity of the wave.

</div>
