## Exercise 1

A uniform magnetic field in a circular region of radius $R = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ points out of the page, perpendicular to the region. The field decreases uniformly from $0.60\,\mathrm{T}$ to zero in $0.30\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the circulation of the induced electric field around the boundary and the field magnitude at the boundary. Determine the direction of the induced field lines.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 unicolore=1 poligoni="2.000,0.000 1.932,0.518 1.732,1.000 1.414,1.414 1.000,1.732 0.518,1.932 0.000,2.000 -0.518,1.932 -1.000,1.732 -1.414,1.414 -1.732,1.000 -1.932,0.518 -2.000,0.000 -1.932,-0.518 -1.732,-1.000 -1.414,-1.414 -1.000,-1.732 -0.518,-1.932 0.000,-2.000 0.518,-1.932 1.000,-1.732 1.414,-1.414 1.732,-1.000 1.932,-0.518" vettori="0.7,2,-0.7,2,E;-0.7,-2,0.7,-2;2,-0.7,2,0.7;-2,0.7,-2,-0.7" segmenti="0,0,1.414,-1.414" etichette="-0.1,-0.05,⊙;-0.95,0.75,⊙;0.75,0.75,⊙;-0.95,-0.95,⊙;-0.1,-1.2,⊙;0.9,-0.45,R;2.25,1.6,γ" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2.7 ymax=2.7 legenda=0 titolo="The outward field $\vec{B}$ decreases. The induced electric field is tangent to the circle $\gamma$ of radius $R$ and points counterclockwise"}

$$
\Gamma(\vec{E}) = -\frac{d\Phi(\vec{B})}{dt} ,
\qquad
\Phi(\vec{B}) = B\,\pi R^2
$$
$$
\Gamma(\vec{E}) = -\pi R^2 \frac{dB}{dt} ,
\qquad
\Gamma(\vec{E}) = 2\pi R\,E
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E = \frac{R}{2} \left| \frac{dB}{dt} \right|
$$
$$
\frac{dB}{dt} = \frac{0 - 0.60\,\mathrm{T}}{0.30\,\mathrm{s}} = -2.0\,\mathrm{T/s}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\vec{E}) &= \pi \cdot (0.050\,\mathrm{m})^2 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{T/s} \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
E &= \frac{0.050\,\mathrm{m}}{2} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{T/s} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V/m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Orient the circle $\gamma$ counterclockwise. By the right-hand rule, the area vector $\Delta\vec{S}$ of the enclosed surface then points out of the page, as does $\vec{B}$. The field is uniform and perpendicular to the region, so the flux is the product $B\,\pi R^2$, and its derivative is $\pi R^2 \dfrac{dB}{dt}$.

The field decreases uniformly, so its rate of change is constant and equals $-2.0\,\mathrm{T/s}$. By **Faraday's law**, the circulation is the negative of the flux derivative, so it is positive and is about $1.6 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V}$. The positive sign indicates that the induced field points along $\gamma$ in the chosen direction, **counterclockwise**. Lenz's law gives the same direction. The outward flux decreases, and a counterclockwise electric field would drive a current around a loop that produces an outward magnetic field inside the loop, opposing the decrease.

The region is a disk with a uniform field, so it is symmetric about its center. The induced field is therefore tangent to $\gamma$ and has the same magnitude everywhere on it, so the circulation is the product $2\pi R\,E$. Equating the two expressions and canceling $\pi R$ gives $E = \dfrac{R}{2} \left| \dfrac{dB}{dt} \right| = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V/m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A very long straight solenoid has radius $a = 2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Inside, its uniform magnetic field increases at a constant rate $\dfrac{dB}{dt} = 0.60\,\mathrm{T/s}$. Outside, the magnetic field is zero. Calculate the magnitude of the induced electric field at distances $r_1 = 1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $r_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis. Then calculate the force on an electron at rest at a distance $r_1$ from the axis and its acceleration ($e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$).

$$
2\pi r\,E = \left| \frac{d\Phi(\vec{B})}{dt} \right|
\qquad
\begin{cases}
\Phi(\vec{B}) = \pi r^2 B & \text{if } r \leq a \\[4pt]
\Phi(\vec{B}) = \pi a^2 B & \text{if } r \geq a
\end{cases}
$$
$$
E_1 = \frac{r_1}{2} \frac{dB}{dt} ,
\qquad
E_2 = \frac{a^2}{2 r_2} \frac{dB}{dt} ,
\qquad
F = e E_1 ,
\qquad
a_e = \frac{F}{m_e}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_1 &= \frac{0.015\,\mathrm{m}}{2} \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{T/s} = 4.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
E_2 &= \frac{(0.025\,\mathrm{m})^2}{2 \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{m}} \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{T/s} \approx 3.8 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
F &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C} \cdot 4.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 7.2 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
a_e &= \frac{7.2 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{N}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}} \approx 7.9 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The solenoid is very long, so the situation is symmetric about the axis. The induced field lines are therefore circles centered on the axis. The field is tangent to each circle and has constant magnitude along it. Thus the circulation around a circle of radius $r$ is $2\pi r\,E$. By **Faraday's law**, its magnitude equals the magnitude of the rate of change of magnetic flux.

The point at distance $r_1 = 1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ is inside the solenoid because $r_1 < a$. The flux through the disk of radius $r_1$ is $\pi r_1^2 B$. Dividing by $2\pi r_1$ gives $E_1 = \dfrac{r_1}{2} \dfrac{dB}{dt}$. The point at distance $r_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is outside. Since the magnetic field vanishes outside, the flux through the disk of radius $r_2$ is just the flux through the solenoid's cross section, $\pi a^2 B$. Dividing by $2\pi r_2$ gives $E_2$. The induced electric field is therefore nonzero **even where the magnetic field vanishes**.

The electron is at rest, so the magnetic Lorentz force on it is zero and only the electric force $e E_1$ acts, tangent to the circle. By Newton's second law, its acceleration is the force divided by the mass, about $7.9 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The value is large because the electron mass is very small.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A very long straight solenoid has $n = 2000$ turns per meter and radius $a = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The current through it increases at a constant rate $\dfrac{di}{dt} = 50\,\mathrm{A/s}$. A conducting ring of radius $r = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and resistance $R = 0.020\,\Omega$ surrounds the solenoid and shares its axis. Neglecting the self-inductance of the ring, calculate the rate of change of the field in the solenoid, the emf and current induced in the ring, and the magnitude of the induced electric field along the ring ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$).

$$
B = \mu_0 n\, i
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{dB}{dt} = \mu_0 n \frac{di}{dt}
$$
$$
|\mathcal{E}| = \left| \frac{d\Phi(\vec{B})}{dt} \right| = \pi a^2 \frac{dB}{dt} ,
\qquad
i_{\text{ind}} = \frac{|\mathcal{E}|}{R} ,
\qquad
E = \frac{|\mathcal{E}|}{2\pi r}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dB}{dt} &= 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2000 \cdot 50\,\mathrm{T/s} \approx 0.126\,\mathrm{T/s} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}| &= \pi \cdot (0.030\,\mathrm{m})^2 \cdot 0.126\,\mathrm{T/s} \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i_{\text{ind}} &= \frac{3.55 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V}}{0.020\,\Omega} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
E &= \frac{3.55 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V}}{2\pi \cdot 0.060\,\mathrm{m}} \approx 9.4 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V/m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field inside a very long solenoid is uniform and equals $B = \mu_0 n\, i$, as discussed in the chapter on sources of magnetic fields. It is proportional to the current, so its time derivative is $\mu_0 n$ times the time derivative of the current, or approximately $0.126\,\mathrm{T/s}$.

The ring has a larger radius than the solenoid, and the magnetic field vanishes outside the solenoid. Thus the flux linked with the ring is just the flux through the solenoid's cross section, $\pi a^2 B$. By **Faraday's law**, the induced emf has magnitude $\pi a^2 \dfrac{dB}{dt} \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V}$. By Ohm's law, the induced current is the emf divided by the resistance.

The magnetic field is zero at the ring, yet the electrons start to move. The force responsible comes from the **induced electric field**, which exists even outside the solenoid. The emf is the circulation of this field around the ring. By the symmetry of the solenoid, the field is tangent to the ring and has constant magnitude along it. Thus $|\mathcal{E}| = 2\pi r\,E$, and dividing by the ring's circumference gives $E \approx 9.4 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V/m}$.

</div>
