## Exercise 1

An FM radio station transmits at frequency $f = 104\,\mathrm{MHz}$. Calculate the wavelength and period of the emitted waves and the time they take to reach a receiver $30\,\mathrm{km}$ from the antenna ($c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

$$
c = \lambda f
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda = \frac{c}{f} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{f} ,
\qquad
\Delta t = \frac{d}{c}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.04 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{Hz}} \approx 2.88\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{1}{1.04 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{Hz}} \approx 9.62 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\Delta t &= \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Radio waves are electromagnetic waves and travel in air at a speed very close to $c$. The **relationship between wavelength and frequency** gives $\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f}$. Before substituting, express the frequency in hertz, $104\,\mathrm{MHz} = 1.04 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The wavelength is about $2.9\,\mathrm{m}$, placing the wave in the radio band of the electromagnetic spectrum.

The period is the reciprocal of the frequency, about $9.6\,\mathrm{ns}$. The signal travels at constant speed $c$, so the time to travel $30\,\mathrm{km}$ is the distance divided by $c$, or one tenth of a millisecond.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A blue light wave in a vacuum has wavelength $\lambda = 480\,\mathrm{nm}$ and electric field amplitude $E_0 = 36\,\mathrm{V/m}$. Calculate the frequency, period, and magnetic field amplitude of the wave. Give the direction of the magnetic field at an instant when the wave travels east and the electric field points upward ($c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

$$
f = \frac{c}{\lambda} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{\lambda}{c} ,
\qquad
B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4.80 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}} = 6.25 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{4.80 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
B_0 &= \frac{36\,\mathrm{V/m}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 1.2 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **relationship between wavelength and frequency**, the frequency is $\dfrac{c}{\lambda}$, with the wavelength expressed in meters, $480\,\mathrm{nm} = 4.80 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}$. The period is the reciprocal of the frequency. The value of $f$ lies between $4.0 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $7.9 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$, as expected for visible light.

In the wave, the field magnitudes satisfy $E = cB$ at every instant, so the amplitudes also satisfy $E_0 = c B_0$, and the magnetic field amplitude is $1.2 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}$. This is about one four-hundredth of Earth's magnetic field.

To find the direction, note that $\vec{B}$ is perpendicular to both $\vec{E}$ and the propagation direction. It is therefore horizontal and lies along the north–south line. Choose its direction so that $\vec{E} \times \vec{B}$ points east. Applying the right-hand rule with the thumb up along $\vec{E}$ and the palm facing east along $\vec{E} \times \vec{B}$, the other fingers point **south**. At this instant, the magnetic field therefore points south.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A radar sends a microwave pulse of frequency $f = 3.0\,\mathrm{GHz}$ toward an airplane. The echo reflected by the airplane returns to the radar $120\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ after emission. Calculate the microwave wavelength and the distance from the radar to the airplane, neglecting the effect of air on the wave speed ($c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

$$
\lambda = \frac{c}{f} ,
\qquad
2d = c\,\Delta t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = \frac{c\,\Delta t}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}}{3.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\mathrm{Hz}} = 0.10\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
d &= \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 1.20 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}}{2} = 1.8 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **relationship between wavelength and frequency** gives a wavelength of $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, typical of the microwave band. The pulse travels from the radar to the airplane and back, so in time $\Delta t$ it covers twice the distance $d$. Electromagnetic waves travel at the constant speed $c$, so $2d = c\,\Delta t$. Dividing by $2$ gives $d = 18\,\mathrm{km}$. Radar uses the fact that electromagnetic waves have a known speed independent of frequency.

</div>
