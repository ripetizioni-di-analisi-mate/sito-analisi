## Exercise 1

A parallel-plate capacitor in a vacuum has circular plates of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ separated by a distance $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. It is connected to an alternating voltage source $\Delta V(t) = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$, with $V_0 = 100\,\mathrm{V}$ and frequency $f = 1.0\,\mathrm{MHz}$. Neglect edge effects ($\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)}$, $\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

1. Calculate the electric field amplitude between the plates and the displacement current amplitude.
2. Calculate the magnetic field amplitude at the edge of the plates.
3. Compare the maximum magnetic energy density at the edge with the maximum electric energy density between the plates.
4. Explain why the electric field between the plates can be treated as uniform at each instant by comparing the capacitor dimensions with the wavelength at the source frequency.

$$
E_0 = \frac{V_0}{d} ,
\qquad
i_s(t) = \varepsilon_0 \pi R^2 \frac{dE}{dt} = \varepsilon_0 \pi R^2 E_0\, \omega \cos(\omega t)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
i_{s,0} = \varepsilon_0 \pi R^2 E_0\, \omega
$$
$$
B_0(R) = \frac{\mu_0\, i_{s,0}}{2\pi R} ,
\qquad
u_{E,\max} = \frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_0 E_0^2 ,
\qquad
u_{B,\max} = \frac{B_0(R)^2}{2\mu_0} ,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{c}{f}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_0 &= \frac{100\,\mathrm{V}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
i_{s,0} &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot \pi \cdot 0.10^2 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{5} \cdot 2\pi \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.17\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
B_0(R) &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 0.175}{2\pi \cdot 0.10}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.5 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
u_{E,\max} &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot \left( 1.0 \cdot 10^{5} \right)^2\,\mathrm{J/m^3} \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{J/m^3} \\[6pt]
u_{B,\max} &= \frac{\left( 3.49 \cdot 10^{-7} \right)^2}{2 \cdot 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{J/m^3} \approx 4.9 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J/m^3} \\[6pt]
\frac{u_{B,\max}}{u_{E,\max}} &\approx 1.1 \cdot 10^{-6} \\[6pt]
\lambda &= \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{Hz}} = 300\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Question 1.** Edge effects are negligible, so at each instant the field between the plates is uniform and equals $E = \dfrac{\Delta V}{d}$. Its amplitude is $\dfrac{V_0}{d} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m}$. The flux through the plate cross section is $E\,\pi R^2$. By the definition of **displacement current**, multiply its derivative by $\varepsilon_0$. The derivative of $E_0 \sin(\omega t)$ is $E_0\, \omega \cos(\omega t)$, and the maximum value of the cosine is $1$. The displacement current amplitude is therefore $\varepsilon_0 \pi R^2 E_0\, \omega \approx 0.17\,\mathrm{A}$, with $\omega = 2\pi f$.

**Question 2.** By the symmetry of the circular plates, the magnetic field lines are circles centered on the same axis. Applying the **Ampère–Maxwell law** to the circle of radius $R$, which encloses the entire displacement current, gives $2\pi R\,B = \mu_0\, i_s$. The magnetic field is thus proportional to $i_s$. Substituting the displacement current amplitude gives a field amplitude of about $3.5 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}$.

**Question 3.** Calculate the two energy densities using $\dfrac{1}{2} \varepsilon_0 E^2$ and $\dfrac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$ and the maximum field values. The ratio is about one millionth, so nearly all the energy stored in the capacitor is electric field energy. In an electromagnetic wave, the two energy densities are equal. The difference arises because the capacitor dimensions are much smaller than the wavelength at the source frequency.

**Question 4.** At a frequency of $1.0\,\mathrm{MHz}$, an electromagnetic wave has wavelength $300\,\mathrm{m}$. The time for a signal to cross the plates, whose dimension is $20\,\mathrm{cm}$, is about $\dfrac{0.20}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 7 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{s}$, much shorter than the period $T = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}$. Therefore, at any instant the voltage is the same at all points on a given plate. The field between the plates can thus be treated as uniform, as in a capacitor with constant charge.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A radio station transmits at frequency $f = 90\,\mathrm{MHz}$ using an antenna that radiates average power $P = 2.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ uniformly in all directions. A receiver is at a distance $r = 3.0\,\mathrm{km}$, and the air absorbs no energy ($\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

1. The receiver's tuning circuit is an $LC$ circuit with inductance $L = 2.5\,\mu\mathrm{H}$. Calculate the capacitance needed to tune to the station and the wavelength of the received wave.
2. Calculate the wave intensity at the receiver and the electric and magnetic field amplitudes.
3. The receiver's antenna is a circular loop of radius $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ whose plane is perpendicular to the magnetic field of the wave. Calculate the maximum emf induced in the loop.
4. Explain what happens to the induced emf if the loop is rotated until the wave's magnetic field is parallel to its plane.

$$
f = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{L C}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C = \frac{1}{4\pi^2 f^2 L} ,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{c}{f}
$$
$$
I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2} ,
\qquad
I = \frac{1}{2}\, c\,\varepsilon_0 E_0^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E_0 = \sqrt{\frac{2I}{c\,\varepsilon_0}} ,
\qquad
B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}
$$
$$
\Phi(\vec{B}) = \pi a^2 B_0 \sin(\omega t)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|\mathcal{E}|_{\max} = \omega\, \pi a^2 B_0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{1}{4\pi^2 \cdot \left( 9.0 \cdot 10^{7} \right)^2 \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F} \\[6pt]
\lambda &= \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{9.0 \cdot 10^{7}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 3.3\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{3}}{4\pi \cdot \left( 3.0 \cdot 10^{3} \right)^2}\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \\[6pt]
E_0 &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.77 \cdot 10^{-5}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 0.12\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
B_0 &= \frac{0.115}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.8 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}|_{\max} &= 2\pi \cdot 9.0 \cdot 10^{7} \cdot \pi \cdot 0.020^2 \cdot 3.85 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 2.7 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Question 1.** The receiver selects the station when the resonant frequency of the $LC$ circuit, $\dfrac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{LC}}$, equals the wave frequency, as discussed in the chapter on alternating current and transformers. Squaring both sides of the equation and solving gives $C = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2 f^2 L} \approx 1.3\,\mathrm{pF}$. The wavelength follows from $c = \lambda f$ and is about $3.3\,\mathrm{m}$.

**Question 2.** The antenna emits uniformly and the air absorbs no energy, so the formula for the **intensity of a point source** applies. From the intensity, use $I = \dfrac{1}{2}\, c\,\varepsilon_0 E_0^2$ to find the electric field amplitude and $E = cB$ to find the magnetic field amplitude. The magnetic field of the wave, about $3.8 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{T}$, is a hundred thousand times weaker than Earth's magnetic field. The loop can still detect it because Earth's field is constant and induces no emf, whereas the field of the wave oscillates at a very high frequency.

**Question 3.** The loop radius $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is much smaller than the wavelength, so at each instant the wave's magnetic field can be treated as uniform over the loop. The field is perpendicular to the loop plane, so the flux is the product $\pi a^2 B$, with $B = B_0 \sin(\omega t)$ at the position of the loop. By **Faraday's law**, the emf is the negative of the flux derivative, giving $-\omega\, \pi a^2 B_0 \cos(\omega t)$. Its maximum value is $\omega\, \pi a^2 B_0 \approx 0.27\,\mathrm{mV}$, with $\omega = 2\pi f$.

**Question 4.** If the magnetic field is parallel to the plane of the loop, the dot product $\vec{B} \cdot \Delta\vec{S}$ is zero on every surface element because $\Delta\vec{S}$ is perpendicular to the plane. Thus the flux is zero at every instant, its derivative is zero, and the induced emf is **zero**. The signal is therefore received only if the wave's magnetic field passes through the loop. At intermediate orientations, the maximum emf is $\omega\, \pi a^2 B_0 \cos\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between $\vec{B}$ and the normal to the loop.

</div>
