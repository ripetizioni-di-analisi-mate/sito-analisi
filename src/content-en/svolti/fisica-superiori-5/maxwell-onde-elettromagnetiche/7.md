## Exercise 1

A beam of unpolarized light with intensity $I_0 = 60\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ passes through two polarizing filters whose transmission axes make an angle of $45^\circ$. Calculate the light intensity after each filter. Then determine the angle between the two axes that gives a final intensity of $7.5\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.

$$
I_1 = \frac{I_0}{2} ,
\qquad
I_2 = I_1 \cos^2\theta = \frac{I_0}{2} \cos^2\theta
$$
$$
\cos^2\theta' = \frac{I_2'}{I_1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta' = \arccos \sqrt{\frac{I_2'}{I_1}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1 &= \frac{60\,\mathrm{W/m^2}}{2} = 30\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \\[6pt]
I_2 &= 30\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \cdot \cos^2 45^\circ = 30\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 15\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \\[6pt]
\theta' &= \arccos \sqrt{\frac{7.5}{30}} = \arccos \frac{1}{2} = 60^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The light striking the first filter is unpolarized, so the filter transmits half the intensity regardless of its orientation. The light emerging from it is polarized along the first filter's axis. Thus the light incident on the second filter is polarized, and **Malus's law** applies with the angle $\theta = 45^\circ$ between the polarization direction and the second filter's axis. Since $\cos 45^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, we have $\cos^2 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the final intensity is $15\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.

For the second question, solve Malus's law for the angle. After the first filter the intensity is still $30\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, so $\cos^2\theta' = \dfrac{7.5}{30} = \dfrac{1}{4}$ and $\cos\theta' = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The acute angle with this cosine is $\theta' = 60^\circ$. An angle larger than $45^\circ$ transmits less light, as expected.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A linearly polarized beam of intensity $I_0 = 500\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ passes through two filters. The first filter's axis makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the beam's polarization direction. The second axis is rotated a further $40^\circ$ from the first in the same direction. Calculate the intensity after each filter and the rms electric fields before the first and after the second, treating the light as a sinusoidal wave in a vacuum ($c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)}$).

$$
I_1 = I_0 \cos^2\theta_1 ,
\qquad
I_2 = I_1 \cos^2\theta_2 ,
\qquad
E_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{I}{c\,\varepsilon_0}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1 &= 500\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \cdot \cos^2 45^\circ = 250\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \\[6pt]
I_2 &= 250\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \cdot \cos^2 40^\circ \approx 250 \cdot 0.587\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \approx 147\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \\[6pt]
E_{\text{rms},0} &= \sqrt{\frac{500}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 434\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
E_{\text{rms},2} &= \sqrt{\frac{147}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 235\,\mathrm{V/m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The incident light is polarized, so **Malus's law** applies to the first filter with an angle of $45^\circ$, and the intensity is halved. Light emerging from the first filter is polarized along its axis. The appropriate angle for the second filter is therefore $40^\circ$, the angle between the axes rather than the angle to the initial polarization. With $\cos 40^\circ \approx 0.766$, we have $\cos^2 40^\circ \approx 0.587$ and the final intensity is about $147\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.

The rms field values follow from $I = c\,\varepsilon_0 E_{\text{rms}}^2$. As a check, the ratio of the outgoing to the incoming field is $\cos 45^\circ \cdot \cos 40^\circ \approx 0.707 \cdot 0.766 \approx 0.54$ because each filter transmits the field component along its own axis. Indeed, $\dfrac{235}{434} \approx 0.54$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two polarizing filters with perpendicular axes receive unpolarized light of intensity $I_0 = 320\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. A third filter is inserted between them with its axis at an angle $\theta$ to the first axis. Calculate the final intensity without the third filter and with it for $\theta = 30^\circ$. Write the final intensity as a function of $\theta$ and determine the angle at which it is greatest and the maximum value.

$$
I_1 = \frac{I_0}{2} ,
\qquad
I_2 = I_1 \cos^2\theta ,
\qquad
I_3 = I_2 \cos^2\left( 90^\circ - \theta \right) = I_2 \sin^2\theta
$$
$$
I_3(\theta) = \frac{I_0}{2} \cos^2\theta \sin^2\theta = \frac{I_0}{8} \sin^2(2\theta)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{\text{without}} &= \frac{I_0}{2} \cos^2 90^\circ = 0 \\[6pt]
I_3(30^\circ) &= 160\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \cdot \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = 30\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \\[6pt]
I_{3,\max} &= \frac{320\,\mathrm{W/m^2}}{8} = 40\,\mathrm{W/m^2}
\qquad \text{for } \theta = 45^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,40*sin(2*t*pi/180)^2,0,90" punti="30,30,pieno;45,40,pieno" segmenti="45,0,45,40;30,0,30,30" xmin=0 xmax=100 ymin=0 ymax=50 nomi="θ,I" legenda=0 titolo="Intensity in $\mathrm{W/m^2}$ transmitted by three filters as a function of the middle filter angle $\theta$ in degrees. It is zero at $\theta = 0^\circ$ and $\theta = 90^\circ$ and greatest at $\theta = 45^\circ$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Without the middle filter, the light leaves the first filter with intensity $\dfrac{I_0}{2}$, polarized along its axis. The second filter's axis is perpendicular to this direction, so **Malus's law** with $\theta = 90^\circ$ gives **zero** final intensity.

With the middle filter inserted, the light passes through three filters. The first halves the intensity. The middle filter makes an angle $\theta$ with the first axis, so it multiplies the intensity by $\cos^2\theta$. The last filter makes an angle $90^\circ - \theta$ with the middle one and multiplies the intensity by $\cos^2\left( 90^\circ - \theta \right) = \sin^2\theta$. For $\theta = 30^\circ$, we have $\cos^2 30^\circ = \dfrac{3}{4}$ and $\sin^2 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{4}$, so $I_3 = 30\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. The additional filter allows light through a pair of filters that previously blocked it completely because it changes the polarization direction between the crossed filters.

The double-angle identity gives $\sin(2\theta) = 2 \sin\theta \cos\theta$, so $\cos^2\theta \sin^2\theta = \dfrac{1}{4} \sin^2(2\theta)$ and the final intensity is $\dfrac{I_0}{8} \sin^2(2\theta)$. The maximum value of $\sin^2(2\theta)$ is $1$. For $0^\circ \leq \theta \leq 90^\circ$, this occurs when $2\theta = 90^\circ$, that is, $\theta = 45^\circ$. The maximum intensity is therefore $\dfrac{I_0}{8} = 40\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.

</div>
