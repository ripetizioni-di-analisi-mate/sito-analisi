## Exercise 1

A parallel-plate capacitor in a vacuum has circular plates of radius $R = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and charges at a constant current $i = 0.80\,\mathrm{A}$. Neglecting edge effects, calculate the magnetic field magnitude in the region between the planes of the plates at distances $r_1 = 2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $r_2 = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis. Plot the field as a function of distance ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$).

$$
\Gamma(\vec{B}) = \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi(\vec{E})}{dt} ,
\qquad
\Gamma(\vec{B}) = 2\pi r\,B ,
\qquad
\varepsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi(\vec{E})}{dt} =
\begin{cases}
i\,\dfrac{r^2}{R^2} & \text{if } r \leq R \\[10pt]
i & \text{if } r \geq R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
B(r_1) = \frac{\mu_0\, i\, r_1}{2\pi R^2} ,
\qquad
B(r_2) = \frac{\mu_0\, i}{2\pi r_2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B(r_1) &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 0.80 \cdot 0.025}{2\pi \cdot 0.050^2}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(r_2) &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 0.80}{2\pi \cdot 0.10}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="min(0.64*x,16/x)" punti="2.5,1.6,pieno;5,3.2,pieno;10,1.6,pieno" segmenti="5,0,5,3.2" xmin=0 xmax=20 ymin=0 ymax=4 nomi="r,B" legenda=0 titolo="Magnetic field between the planes of the plates, in $\mu\mathrm{T}$, as a function of distance $r$ from the axis in $\mathrm{cm}$. It increases linearly up to $R = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, where its value is $3.2\,\mu\mathrm{T}$, then decreases as $\dfrac{1}{r}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The electric field between the plates is uniform and increasing, and there are no conduction currents. By the symmetry of the circular plates, the magnetic field lines are circles centered on their common axis. On each circle, $\vec{B}$ is tangent and has constant magnitude. Thus the circulation around a circle of radius $r$ is $2\pi r\,B$, and the **Ampère–Maxwell law** applies without a conduction current term.

The displacement current through the entire cross section of the plates equals the current in the wires, $i$. As the electric field is uniform, the fraction of the total displacement current passing through a disk of radius $r_1 = 2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ inside the plate radius is $\dfrac{\pi r_1^2}{\pi R^2}$. Equating the two sides gives $2\pi r_1 B = \mu_0\, i\, \dfrac{r_1^2}{R^2}$. Dividing by $2\pi r_1$ gives $B(r_1) = 1.6\,\mu\mathrm{T}$.

The point at distance $r_2 = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ lies outside the plate radius, where the electric field is zero. Thus the flux through the disk of radius $r_2$ includes all the flux between the plates, and the enclosed displacement current is $i$. The magnetic field is the same as that of a straight wire carrying current $i$ and again has magnitude $1.6\,\mu\mathrm{T}$. The two values are equal because $r_1 = \dfrac{R}{2}$ and $r_2 = 2R$. Inside the plate radius, the field is proportional to $r$, so halfway to the edge it is half its value at the edge. Outside, the field is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r}$, so at twice the radius it is again half its value at the edge.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Between the circular plates of a parallel-plate capacitor of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$, the uniform electric field increases at a constant rate $\dfrac{dE}{dt} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)}$. Neglecting edge effects, calculate the displacement current between the plates and the magnitude of the magnetic field at distances $r_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, $R$, and $r_2 = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis. Then verify that inside the plate radius $B = \dfrac{r}{2c^2} \dfrac{dE}{dt}$ holds ($\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)}$, $\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$).

$$
i_s = \varepsilon_0 \pi R^2 \frac{dE}{dt}
$$
$$
2\pi r\,B = \mu_0 \varepsilon_0\, \pi r^2 \frac{dE}{dt}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B = \frac{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0\, r}{2} \frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{r}{2c^2} \frac{dE}{dt}
\qquad (r \leq R)
$$
$$
2\pi r\,B = \mu_0 \varepsilon_0\, \pi R^2 \frac{dE}{dt}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B = \frac{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0 R^2}{2r} \frac{dE}{dt}
\qquad (r \geq R)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
i_s &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot \pi \cdot 0.10^2 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.28\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
B(r_1) &= \frac{0.050}{2 \cdot \left( 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \right)^2} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 2.8 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(R) &= \frac{0.10}{2 \cdot \left( 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \right)^2} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 5.6 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(r_2) &= \frac{0.10^2}{2 \cdot 0.20 \cdot \left( 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \right)^2} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 2.8 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field between the plates is uniform, so the flux through the full cross section is $E\,\pi R^2$. By the definition of **displacement current**, multiply its derivative by $\varepsilon_0$ to obtain about $0.28\,\mathrm{A}$.

By the symmetry of the circular plates, the field lines of $\vec{B}$ are circles centered on their common axis, and the circulation around one of them is $2\pi r\,B$. Apply the **Ampère–Maxwell law** with no conduction current. If $r \leq R$, the electric flux through the disk of radius $r$ is $E\,\pi r^2$. Equating the two sides and dividing by $2\pi r$ gives $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0\, r}{2} \dfrac{dE}{dt}$. The speed of electromagnetic waves satisfies $c^2 = \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_0 \mu_0}$, so $\mu_0 \varepsilon_0 = \dfrac{1}{c^2}$, giving the required formula. If $r \geq R$, the flux is just the flux between the plates, $E\,\pi R^2$, because outside the plate radius the electric field is zero.

The strongest magnetic field, at the edge of the plates, is about $0.56\,\mu\mathrm{T}$ even though the electric field increases very rapidly. The small factor $\dfrac{1}{c^2}$ makes the magnetic fields produced by changing electric fields weak. For this reason, Maxwell introduced displacement current on theoretical grounds before there was any direct experimental verification.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A parallel-plate capacitor in a vacuum has circular plates of radius $R = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and charges at a constant current. In the region between the plates, at a distance $r = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis, the measured magnetic field has magnitude $B = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}$. Neglecting edge effects, determine the charging current, the rate of increase of the electric field between the plates, and the distance from the axis outside the plate radius at which the magnetic field again has magnitude $2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}$ ($\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)}$, $\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$).

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0\, i\, r}{2\pi R^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
i = \frac{2\pi R^2 B}{\mu_0\, r} ,
\qquad
\frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{i}{\varepsilon_0 \pi R^2}
$$
$$
\frac{\mu_0\, i}{2\pi r'} = B
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r' = \frac{\mu_0\, i}{2\pi B} = \frac{R^2}{r}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
i &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 0.060^2 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 0.040}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.90\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\frac{dE}{dt} &= \frac{0.90}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot \pi \cdot 0.060^2}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)} \approx 9.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)} \\[6pt]
r' &= \frac{(6.0\,\mathrm{cm})^2}{4.0\,\mathrm{cm}} = 9.0\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The measurement point is inside the plate radius because $r < R$. Applying the **Ampère–Maxwell law** to a circle of radius $r$ centered on the axis gives a field $B = \dfrac{\mu_0\, i\, r}{2\pi R^2}$, as in exercise 1. Here $B$ is known and $i$ is required, so multiply both sides by $2\pi R^2$ and divide by $\mu_0 r$. The charging current is $0.90\,\mathrm{A}$.

The displacement current between the plates equals the charging current and is given by $\varepsilon_0 \pi R^2 \dfrac{dE}{dt}$. Dividing $i$ by $\varepsilon_0 \pi R^2$ gives the rate of increase of the electric field, about $9.0 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)}$.

Outside the plate radius, the field is the same as that of a straight wire carrying a current $i$, namely $\dfrac{\mu_0\, i}{2\pi r'}$. Setting this equal to $B$ and substituting the expression for $i$ found earlier gives $r' = \dfrac{R^2}{r} = 9.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, which is greater than $R$ as required. As a check, with $i = 0.90\,\mathrm{A}$ the field at the edge is $\dfrac{\mu_0\, i}{2\pi R} = 3.0\,\mu\mathrm{T}$, greater than both values of $2.0\,\mu\mathrm{T}$ because the field is greatest at the edge.

</div>
