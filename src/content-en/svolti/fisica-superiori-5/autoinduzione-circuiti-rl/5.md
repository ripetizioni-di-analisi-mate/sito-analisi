## Exercise 1

An ideal coil with zero initial current is connected in series with a resistor and an ideal source of emf $\mathcal{E} = 8.0\,\mathrm{V}$. The graph shows the measured current, in amperes, as a function of time, in milliseconds. It gives the steady-state current, $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$, and shows that the tangent at the origin meets the line $i = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ at the horizontal coordinate $5.0\,\mathrm{ms}$. Determine the resistance, the inductance, and the current at $5.0\,\mathrm{ms}$.

::grafico{f="2*(1-exp(-x/5))+0*sqrt(x)" asintoti="y=2" segmenti="0,0,5,2;5,0,5,2" punti="5,1.264,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=27 ymin=-1 ymax=3 nomi="t,i" legenda=0 titolo="Measured current in amperes, with time in milliseconds. The tangent at the origin meets the line $i = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ at time $5.0\,\mathrm{ms}$"}

$$
I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{I} , \qquad
\tau = \frac{L}{R}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L = R\,\tau , \qquad
i(\tau) = I \left( 1 - e^{-1} \right)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \frac{8.0\,\mathrm{V}}{2.0\,\mathrm{A}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega} \\[6pt]
L &= 4.0 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{H} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
i(5.0\,\mathrm{ms}) &= 2.0 \cdot 0.632\,\mathrm{A} = 1.3\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The steady-state current in an RL circuit is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R}$ because the self-induced emf is zero in steady state. The value read from the graph therefore gives $R = 4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega}$.

From the **meaning of the time constant**, the tangent to the graph at the origin intersects the steady-state current line at the horizontal coordinate $\tau$. The initial slope is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{L}$, so a straight line with this slope reaches $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R}$ after a time $\dfrac{L}{R}$. The graph therefore gives $\tau = 5.0\,\mathrm{ms}$, and the inductance is $L = R\,\tau = 20\,\mathrm{mH}$.

At $t = \tau$ the current reaches $63\,\%$ of its steady-state value, or $1.3\,\mathrm{A}$, as indicated by the highlighted point. This provides a second way to find $\tau$ from the graph: locate the time when the current reaches $63\,\%$ of its final value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an experiment, a coil initially carrying a current is connected across a resistor. A sensor connected to a computer measures the current. The total loop resistance, including the coil resistance, is $10\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. The measured current shown on the graph is $6.0\,\mathrm{A}$ at $0$ and $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ at $14\,\mathrm{ms}$. Determine the time constant and the inductance of the coil. Calculate the predicted current at $40\,\mathrm{ms}$.

::grafico{f="6*exp(-x*log(2)/14)+0*sqrt(x)" punti="0,6,pieno;14,3,pieno;28,1.5,pieno;42,0.75,pieno" segmenti="14,0,14,3;0,3,14,3;28,0,28,1.5" xmin=-7 xmax=63 ymin=-1 ymax=7 nomi="t,i" legenda=0 titolo="Measured current in amperes, with time in milliseconds. The current falls by half every $14\,\mathrm{ms}$"}

$$
e^{-\frac{t_{\frac{1}{2}}}{\tau}} = \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tau = \frac{t_{\frac{1}{2}}}{\ln 2} , \qquad
L = R\,\tau , \qquad
i(t) = I_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &= \frac{14\,\mathrm{ms}}{0.693} = 20.2\,\mathrm{ms} \approx 20\,\mathrm{ms} \\[6pt]
L &= 10 \cdot 0.0202\,\mathrm{H} = 0.20\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
i(40\,\mathrm{ms}) &= 6.0 \cdot e^{-\frac{40}{20.2}}\,\mathrm{A} = 6.0 \cdot e^{-1.98}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.83\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coil discharges through the resistor, so the current follows the expression for the **current when an RL circuit is switched off**. The graph shows that the current falls from $6.0\,\mathrm{A}$ to $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in $14\,\mathrm{ms}$ and then to $1.5\,\mathrm{A}$ after another $14\,\mathrm{ms}$. The time it takes to fall by half is therefore $t_{\frac{1}{2}} = 14\,\mathrm{ms}$, the same on each interval, as with any exponential decay.

Setting the exponential factor equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and taking logarithms gives $t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \tau \ln 2$. Dividing by $\ln 2$ yields the time constant, approximately $20\,\mathrm{ms}$. The total loop resistance is $10\,\mathrm{\Omega}$, so the inductance is $L = R\,\tau = 0.20\,\mathrm{H}$.

To predict the current, substitute $t = 40\,\mathrm{ms}$ into the current equation, which gives $0.83\,\mathrm{A}$. This value is consistent with the graph because $40\,\mathrm{ms}$ is just under three half-lives, and dividing $6.0\,\mathrm{A}$ by $2^3$ gives $0.75\,\mathrm{A}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An oscilloscope measures the voltage across an ideal inductor in an RL circuit with an ideal source. The circuit is switched on at $t = 0$. The voltage is $6.0\,\mathrm{V}$ immediately after switch-on and $2.2\,\mathrm{V}$ at $10\,\mathrm{ms}$. The steady-state current is $1.5\,\mathrm{A}$. Explain why the voltage across the inductor decreases with time. Determine the source emf, the time constant, the resistance, and the inductance.

$$
V_L(t) = L\,\frac{di}{dt} = \mathcal{E}\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} , \qquad
V_L(0) = \mathcal{E} , \qquad
\frac{V_L(t_1)}{V_L(0)} = e^{-\frac{t_1}{\tau}} , \qquad
R = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{I} , \qquad
L = R\,\tau
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E} &= 6.0\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad
\frac{2.2}{6.0} = 0.367 \approx e^{-1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tau = 10\,\mathrm{ms} \\[6pt]
R &= \frac{6.0\,\mathrm{V}}{1.5\,\mathrm{A}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega} , \qquad
L = 4.0 \cdot 0.010\,\mathrm{H} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{H}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="6*exp(-x/10)+0*sqrt(x)" punti="0,6,pieno;10,2.207,pieno" segmenti="10,0,10,2.207" xmin=-6 xmax=54 ymin=-1 ymax=7 nomi="t,V" legenda="V_L = 6.0\,e^{-\frac{t}{10}}" titolo="Voltage across the inductor in volts, with time in milliseconds. At $\tau = 10\,\mathrm{ms}$ the voltage is $6.0 \cdot e^{-1}\,\mathrm{V} = 2.2\,\mathrm{V}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the RL circuit equation, the source emf equals the sum of the voltages across the resistor and the inductor. Just after the switch closes, the current is zero, so the voltage across the resistor is zero and the full emf appears across the inductor. The oscilloscope therefore gives $\mathcal{E} = 6.0\,\mathrm{V}$ directly. The current then rises, increasing the voltage $R\,i$ across the resistor. The sum of the two voltages stays at $\mathcal{E}$, so the voltage across the inductor decreases. In steady state the current is constant and the inductor voltage is zero.

From the expression for the **current when an RL circuit is switched on**, the current derivative is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{L}\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$. Multiplication by $L$ then gives $V_L = \mathcal{E}\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$. The ratio of the voltage at $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ to the initial voltage is $0.367$, which agrees with $e^{-1}$ to the precision of the data. Thus $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ is one time constant.

The steady-state current is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R}$, giving $R = 4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega}$, and the inductance is $L = R\,\tau = 40\,\mathrm{mH}$. Just after the switch closes, the current rises at the rate $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{L} = 150\,\mathrm{A/s}$.

</div>
