## Exercise 1

An air-core solenoid of length $\ell = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ has $N = 600$ turns of radius $r = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and carries a current $I = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Treat the solenoid as long compared with the radius of the turns ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$). Calculate the internal magnetic field, the flux through one turn, the flux linkage of the entire solenoid, and the inductance.

$$
B = \mu_0\,\frac{N}{\ell}\,I , \qquad
S = \pi r^2 , \qquad
\Phi_1 = B S , \qquad
\Phi_B = N B S , \qquad
L = \frac{\Phi_B}{I} = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 S}{\ell}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B &= 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot \frac{600}{0.30} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{T} = 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
S &= \pi \cdot 0.020^2\,\mathrm{m^2} = 1.26 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
\Phi_1 &= 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1.26 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} = 6.32 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
\Phi_B &= 600 \cdot 6.32 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb} = 3.79 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
L &= \frac{3.79 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb}}{2.0\,\mathrm{A}} = 1.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{H}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The solenoid is $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ long and its turns have radius $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, so its length is large compared with the radius. The internal field is therefore uniform, parallel to the axis, and has magnitude $\mu_0\,\dfrac{N}{\ell}\,I$, approximately $5.0\,\mathrm{mT}$.

The field is uniform and perpendicular to the plane of each turn, so the flux through one turn is the product $B S$. The same flux passes through all $N$ turns, so the flux linkage of the solenoid is $N$ times the flux through one turn. Dividing this flux linkage by the current gives the **inductance**, approximately $1.9\,\mathrm{mH}$.

The formula for the **inductance of a solenoid** gives the same value because $\dfrac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 600^2 \cdot 1.26 \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.30}\,\mathrm{H} = 1.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{H}$. The current does not appear in this formula, since the inductance depends only on the geometry of the solenoid.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The solenoid in the preceding exercise has inductance $L_0 = 1.9\,\mathrm{mH}$. Explain how its inductance changes and calculate the new value in each of the following cases, considered separately:
- The number of turns doubles while the length stays the same.
- The length doubles while the number of turns stays the same.
- The radius of the turns doubles while the solenoid remains long compared with the radius.

$$
L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 \pi r^2}{\ell}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
N \to 2N: \quad L_a &= \frac{\mu_0 (2N)^2 \pi r^2}{\ell} = 4\,L_0 = 7.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
\ell \to 2\ell: \quad L_b &= \frac{\mu_0 N^2 \pi r^2}{2\ell} = \frac{L_0}{2} = 9.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
r \to 2r: \quad L_c &= \frac{\mu_0 N^2 \pi (2r)^2}{\ell} = 4\,L_0 = 7.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{H}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the formula for the **inductance of a solenoid**, write the area of a turn as $\pi r^2$ to show all the geometric quantities explicitly. In each case, substitute the changed quantity and compare the result with $L_0$.

The number of turns appears squared, so doubling $N$ multiplies the inductance by $2^2 = 4$. There are two physical reasons for this. With twice as many turns over the same length, the internal field doubles, and the flux from this doubled field is summed over twice as many turns.

The length appears in the denominator, so doubling $\ell$ with the same number of turns halves the inductance. The turns are farther apart and the internal field is halved. The radius appears squared because the area of one turn is proportional to $r^2$. Doubling the radius therefore quadruples the area and the flux, so the inductance is **four times** as large.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An air-core solenoid is to have length $\ell = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$, turn radius $r = 1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$, and inductance $L = 5.0\,\mathrm{mH}$ ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$). Determine the required number of turns and the length of wire needed to wind them. Then calculate how many turns would give the same inductance with an iron core of relative magnetic permeability $\mu_r = 400$.

$$
L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 \pi r^2}{\ell}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N = \sqrt{\frac{L\,\ell}{\mu_0 \pi r^2}} , \qquad
\ell_{\text{wire}} = N \cdot 2\pi r , \qquad
N' = \sqrt{\frac{L\,\ell}{\mu_r \mu_0 \pi r^2}} = \frac{N}{\sqrt{\mu_r}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
N &= \sqrt{\frac{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 0.20}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot \pi \cdot 0.015^2}} = \sqrt{1.13 \cdot 10^6} \approx 1.06 \cdot 10^3 \\[6pt]
\ell_{\text{wire}} &= 1.06 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 2\pi \cdot 0.015\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.0 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
N' &= \frac{1.06 \cdot 10^3}{\sqrt{400}} = \frac{1.06 \cdot 10^3}{20} \approx 53
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the inductance is known and the number of turns is unknown. In the formula for the **inductance of a solenoid**, multiply both sides by $\ell$ and divide by $\mu_0 \pi r^2$ to isolate $N^2$, then take the square root. About $1060$ turns are required. Each turn has circumference $2\pi r$, so the length of wire is the number of turns times $2\pi r$, approximately $100\,\mathrm{m}$.

With an iron core of relative permeability $\mu_r$, the internal field and the flux are greater by a factor of $\mu_r$, and the inductance formula includes a factor of $\mu_r$. Consequently, in the formula for $N'$ the denominator under the square root is multiplied by $\mu_r$ and the required number of turns is divided by $\sqrt{\mu_r} = 20$. About $53$ turns suffice, requiring approximately $5\,\mathrm{m}$ of wire. This is an estimate because the relative permeability of iron depends on the field.

</div>
