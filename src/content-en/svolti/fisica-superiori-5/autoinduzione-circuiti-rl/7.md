## Exercise 1

Two nearby coils have mutual inductance $M = 3.0\,\mathrm{mH}$. The second coil forms a closed circuit of total resistance $R_2 = 0.50\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. In the first coil, the current increases uniformly from $0$ to $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in $0.10\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the emf induced in the second coil, the current that flows through it, and the charge that passes through it during the increase. Neglect self-induction in the second coil.

$$
\mathcal{E}_2 = -M\,\frac{\Delta i_1}{\Delta t} , \qquad
i_2 = \frac{|\mathcal{E}_2|}{R_2} , \qquad
q = i_2\,\Delta t
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta i_1}{\Delta t} &= \frac{5.0\,\mathrm{A}}{0.10\,\mathrm{s}} = 50\,\mathrm{A/s} , \qquad
\mathcal{E}_2 = -3.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 50\,\mathrm{V} = -0.15\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i_2 &= \frac{0.15\,\mathrm{V}}{0.50\,\mathrm{\Omega}} = 0.30\,\mathrm{A} , \qquad
q = 0.30 \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{C} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The current in the first coil rises uniformly, so its derivative is constant and equals $50\,\mathrm{A/s}$. By the definition of **mutual inductance**, the emf induced in the second coil is $-M$ times this rate, giving $-0.15\,\mathrm{V}$. By Lenz's law, the minus sign indicates that the induced current produces a field that opposes the increase in flux due to the first coil.

Self-induction in the second coil is negligible, so the induced current follows Ohm's law and equals $0.30\,\mathrm{A}$, remaining constant throughout the increase. The charge is the current times the time, $30\,\mathrm{mC}$. The same result follows from the formula for induced charge in the chapter on electromagnetic induction, $q = \dfrac{\Delta\Phi_{21}}{R_2} = \dfrac{M\,\Delta i_1}{R_2} = \dfrac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 5.0}{0.50}\,\mathrm{C} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{C}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A long air-core solenoid with $1500$ turns per meter has a coaxial coil at its center with $150$ turns of radius $1.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$). Calculate the mutual inductance. The solenoid current decreases uniformly by $60\,\mathrm{A}$ each second. Calculate the emf induced in the coil and determine the direction of the induced current relative to the current in the solenoid.

::grafico{assi=0 linee="0,1.4 7,1.4;0,-1.4 7,-1.4;2.6,0.6 4.4,0.6;2.6,-0.6 4.4,-0.6" vettori="0.3,0,2.1,0,B₁;4.9,0,6.7,0" etichette="0.25,1.4,⊙;0.75,1.4,⊙;1.25,1.4,⊙;1.75,1.4,⊙;2.25,1.4,⊙;2.75,1.4,⊙;3.25,1.4,⊙;3.75,1.4,⊙;4.25,1.4,⊙;4.75,1.4,⊙;5.25,1.4,⊙;5.75,1.4,⊙;6.25,1.4,⊙;6.75,1.4,⊙;0.25,-1.4,⊗;0.75,-1.4,⊗;1.25,-1.4,⊗;1.75,-1.4,⊗;2.25,-1.4,⊗;2.75,-1.4,⊗;3.25,-1.4,⊗;3.75,-1.4,⊗;4.25,-1.4,⊗;4.75,-1.4,⊗;5.25,-1.4,⊗;5.75,-1.4,⊗;6.25,-1.4,⊗;6.75,-1.4,⊗;3.5,0.15,coil" unicolore=1 xmin=-0.5 xmax=7.5 ymin=-2 ymax=2 uguale=1 titolo="Cross section of the solenoid with the coaxial coil at its center. The solenoid's field $\vec{B}_1$ passes through every turn of the coil"}

$$
M = \mu_0\,n_1 N_2\,\pi r_2^2 , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}_2| = M \left| \frac{\Delta i_1}{\Delta t} \right|
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 1500 \cdot 150 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.012^2\,\mathrm{H} = 1.28 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}_2| &= 1.28 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 60\,\mathrm{V} = 7.7 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coil is at the center of a long solenoid, where the solenoid's field is uniform and parallel to its axis. The expression for the **mutual inductance of two coaxial solenoids** therefore applies, with $n_1 = \dfrac{N_1}{\ell_1} = 1500\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and $S_2 = \pi r_2^2$. The mutual inductance is about $0.13\,\mathrm{mH}$. Multiplying by the rate of change of current gives an emf of about $7.7\,\mathrm{mV}$.

As the current in the solenoid decreases, the field $\vec{B}_1$ and the flux through the coil also decrease. By **Lenz's law**, the induced current produces a field in the coil that points in the same direction as $\vec{B}_1$ and opposes the decrease in flux. By the right-hand rule, a field pointing in the same direction as $\vec{B}_1$ is produced by a current flowing in the same direction as the solenoid current. The induced current therefore flows in the **same direction** as the current in the solenoid.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cardboard tube of length $\ell = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ and circular cross section of radius $r = 1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ has two windings extending over its entire length, one on top of the other. The first has $N_1 = 900$ turns and the second has $N_2 = 60$ turns. Assume the tube is long compared with its radius and the external field is negligible ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$). Calculate the inductance of the first winding and the mutual inductance. The current in the first winding increases by $25\,\mathrm{A}$ each second. Calculate the magnitudes of the self-induced emf in the first winding and the emf induced in the second. Verify that their ratio equals the ratio of their numbers of turns.

$$
L_1 = \frac{\mu_0 N_1^2 S}{\ell} , \qquad
M = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 S}{\ell} , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}_1| = L_1\,\frac{di_1}{dt} , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}_2| = M\,\frac{di_1}{dt} , \qquad
\frac{|\mathcal{E}_2|}{|\mathcal{E}_1|} = \frac{M}{L_1} = \frac{N_2}{N_1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= \pi \cdot 0.015^2\,\mathrm{m^2} = 7.07 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
L_1 &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 900^2 \cdot 7.07 \cdot 10^{-4}}{0.30}\,\mathrm{H} = 2.40 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
M &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 900 \cdot 60 \cdot 7.07 \cdot 10^{-4}}{0.30}\,\mathrm{H} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}_1| &= 2.40 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 25\,\mathrm{V} = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}_2| = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 25\,\mathrm{V} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
\frac{|\mathcal{E}_2|}{|\mathcal{E}_1|} &= \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{6.0 \cdot 10^{-2}} = \frac{1}{15} , \qquad
\frac{N_2}{N_1} = \frac{60}{900} = \frac{1}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first winding is a solenoid that is long compared with its radius, so its inductance follows from the formula for the **inductance of a solenoid**. The current $i_1$ produces a uniform internal field $B_1 = \mu_0\,\dfrac{N_1}{\ell}\,i_1$. All the turns of the second winding have the same area $S$ and the same field passes through them, so their flux linkage is $N_2 B_1 S$. Dividing by $i_1$ gives the **mutual inductance** $M = \dfrac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 S}{\ell}$, the same formula as for coaxial solenoids with $S_2 = S$.

The self-induced emf in the first winding is $L_1$ times the rate of change of $i_1$. The emf induced in the second is $M$ times the same rate. Their ratio is therefore $\dfrac{M}{L_1}$, and all factors in the two formulas cancel except $\dfrac{N_2}{N_1}$. The reason is that the same flux passes through every turn of both windings, so the emf in each winding is proportional to its number of turns.

The numerical values confirm the ratio $\dfrac{1}{15}$. This is the operating principle of the **transformer**, discussed in the chapter on alternating current and transformers.

</div>
