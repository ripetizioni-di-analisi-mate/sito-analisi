## Exercise 1

An RL circuit with $\mathcal{E} = 18\,\mathrm{V}$, $R = 4.5\,\mathrm{\Omega}$, and $L = 36\,\mathrm{mH}$ carries its steady-state current. At time $t = 0$ a changeover switch disconnects the source and connects the inductor across the resistor. Calculate the initial current, the time constant, the current at $t_1 = 12\,\mathrm{ms}$, and the time it takes for the current to fall by half. Determine the self-induced emf immediately after switch-off and explain its direction.

$$
I_0 = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R} , \qquad
\tau = \frac{L}{R} , \qquad
i(t) = I_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} , \qquad
t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \tau \ln 2 , \qquad
\mathcal{E}_L(0) = -L \left( \frac{di}{dt} \right)_{t = 0} = L\,\frac{I_0}{\tau} = R\,I_0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_0 &= \frac{18\,\mathrm{V}}{4.5\,\mathrm{\Omega}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{A} , \qquad
\tau = \frac{36 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{H}}{4.5\,\mathrm{\Omega}} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
i(t_1) &= 4.0 \cdot e^{-\frac{12}{8.0}}\,\mathrm{A} = 4.0 \cdot e^{-1.5}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.89\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
t_{\frac{1}{2}} &= 8.0 \cdot 0.693\,\mathrm{ms} = 5.5\,\mathrm{ms} \\[6pt]
\mathcal{E}_L(0) &= 4.5 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{V} = 18\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="4*exp(-x/8)+0*sqrt(x)" segmenti="12,0,12,0.8925;5.545,0,5.545,2;0,2,5.545,2" punti="12,0.8925,pieno;5.545,2,pieno" xmin=-5 xmax=40 ymin=-1 ymax=5 nomi="t,i" legenda="i = 4.0\,e^{-\frac{t}{8.0}}" titolo="Current after switching off, in amperes, with time in milliseconds. It falls by half after $5.5\,\mathrm{ms}$ and is $0.89\,\mathrm{A}$ at time $12\,\mathrm{ms}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Before the source is disconnected the circuit is in steady state, so the current is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R} = 4.0\,\mathrm{A}$. After switch-off the inductor and the same resistor still form a closed loop. The expression for the **current when an RL circuit is switched off** therefore applies, with time constant $\dfrac{L}{R} = 8.0\,\mathrm{ms}$. At $12\,\mathrm{ms}$, or after $1.5$ time constants, the current is $0.89\,\mathrm{A}$.

To find the time it takes for the current to fall by half, set $e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Taking logarithms gives $\dfrac{t}{\tau} = \ln 2$, so the current falls by half in $5.5\,\mathrm{ms}$ and halves again after each additional $5.5\,\mathrm{ms}$.

The initial derivative of the current is $-\dfrac{I_0}{\tau} = -500\,\mathrm{A/s}$. Multiplying it by $-L$ gives $18\,\mathrm{V}$. The self-induced emf is positive and therefore acts in the same direction as the current. By **Lenz's law**, the emf opposes the decrease in current. Its value equals the emf of the disconnected source because the initial current is unchanged and flows through the same resistor $R$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electromagnet consists of a coil with $L = 0.20\,\mathrm{H}$ and resistance $R_b = 4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega}$, powered by an ideal $12\,\mathrm{V}$ source. Once steady state is reached, the source is disconnected, leaving only a voltmeter of resistance $R_V = 196\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ connected across the coil. Calculate the steady-state current and the voltmeter reading just after disconnection. Determine the time constant for the current decay, the current after $2.0\,\mathrm{ms}$, and the energy dissipated over the entire transient.

$$
I_0 = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R_b} , \qquad
V_0 = R_V I_0 , \qquad
\tau' = \frac{L}{R_b + R_V} , \qquad
i(t) = I_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau'}} , \qquad
U = \frac{1}{2} L I_0^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_0 &= \frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{A} , \qquad
V_0 = 196 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{V} = 588\,\mathrm{V} \approx 5.9 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
\tau' &= \frac{0.20\,\mathrm{H}}{4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega} + 196\,\mathrm{\Omega}} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
i(2.0\,\mathrm{ms}) &= 3.0 \cdot e^{-2}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.41\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
U &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.20 \cdot 3.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.90\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before disconnection, the coil and the voltmeter are connected in parallel across the ideal source, so the voltage across the coil is $12\,\mathrm{V}$. In steady state the self-induced emf is zero, so the coil current depends only on its resistance and equals $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$. When the source is disconnected, the current in the coil cannot drop abruptly to zero. Its only remaining path is through the voltmeter. The current through the voltmeter at that instant is therefore $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ and the meter reads $R_V I_0 \approx 590\,\mathrm{V}$, nearly fifty times the source emf. This is the **switch-off voltage spike**.

The current flows around the loop formed by the coil and the voltmeter, with a total resistance of $R_b + R_V = 200\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. The time constant is therefore $\dfrac{L}{200\,\mathrm{\Omega}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{ms}$, much shorter than the time constant $\dfrac{L}{R_b} = 50\,\mathrm{ms}$ of the circuit with the source connected. After $2.0\,\mathrm{ms}$, or $2\tau'$, the current has already fallen to $0.41\,\mathrm{A}$.

During the transient, the **energy stored in the inductor** is converted to heat in the resistances of the coil and the voltmeter. The total energy dissipated is therefore the initial energy of the inductor, $0.90\,\mathrm{J}$. A meter that is not rated for such a high voltage can be damaged. For this reason, circuits with large inductances are switched off through a protective resistor.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The graph shows the current measured in a circuit consisting of an ideal inductor of inductance $L = 0.15\,\mathrm{H}$ connected across a resistor after the source is disconnected at $t = 0$. The current is $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ at $t = 0$ and $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$ at $t = 30\,\mathrm{ms}$. Determine the time constant, the resistance of the resistor, and the current at $t = 60\,\mathrm{ms}$.

::grafico{f="2*exp(-x*log(4)/30)+0*sqrt(x)" punti="0,2,pieno;30,0.5,pieno" segmenti="30,0,30,0.5;0,0.5,30,0.5" xmin=-10 xmax=90 ymin=-1 ymax=3 nomi="t,i" legenda=0 titolo="Measured current in amperes, with time in milliseconds. The current is $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ at $0$ and $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$ at $30\,\mathrm{ms}$"}

$$
I_0\,e^{-\frac{t_1}{\tau}} = i_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{t_1}{\tau} = \ln \frac{I_0}{i_1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tau = \frac{t_1}{\ln \dfrac{I_0}{i_1}} , \qquad
R = \frac{L}{\tau}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &= \frac{30\,\mathrm{ms}}{\ln 4} = \frac{30\,\mathrm{ms}}{1.386} = 21.6\,\mathrm{ms} \approx 22\,\mathrm{ms} \\[6pt]
R &= \frac{0.15\,\mathrm{H}}{0.0216\,\mathrm{s}} = 6.9\,\mathrm{\Omega} \\[6pt]
i(60\,\mathrm{ms}) &= 2.0 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^2\,\mathrm{A} = 0.13\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The current decays according to the expression for the **current when an RL circuit is switched off**, $i(t) = I_0\,e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$, with $I_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. At $t_1 = 30\,\mathrm{ms}$ the current must equal $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$. Dividing by $I_0$ gives $e^{-\frac{t_1}{\tau}} = \dfrac{1}{4}$, and taking logarithms gives $\dfrac{t_1}{\tau} = \ln 4$. It follows that $\tau = 21.6\,\mathrm{ms}$.

The time constant is the ratio $\dfrac{L}{R}$, so the resistance is $\dfrac{L}{\tau} = 6.9\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. To find the current at $60\,\mathrm{ms}$, the value of $\tau$ is not needed. In each interval of $30\,\mathrm{ms}$ the current falls to one-quarter of its previous value because $e^{-\frac{t_1 + t_2}{\tau}} = e^{-\frac{t_1}{\tau}} \cdot e^{-\frac{t_2}{\tau}}$. After two intervals, the current is $2.0 \cdot \dfrac{1}{16}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.125\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.13\,\mathrm{A}$.

</div>
