## Exercise 1

A horizontal metal rod of length $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and mass $12\,\mathrm{g}$ is in a uniform horizontal magnetic field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the rod. Calculate the current required for the magnetic force to balance the rod's weight. Explain which way the current must flow if the field points into the page and the rod lies horizontally in the plane of the page. Use $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
F = I \ell B ,
\qquad
P = m g ,
\qquad
I \ell B = m g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{m g}{\ell B}
$$
$$
I = \frac{0.012 \cdot 9.81}{0.20 \cdot 0.40}\,\mathrm{A} = \frac{0.118}{0.080}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 1.5\,\mathrm{A}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is subject to its weight, directed vertically downward, and to the magnetic force. The rod is perpendicular to the field. By the law for the **force on a straight wire**, the magnetic force has magnitude $I \ell B$ and is perpendicular to both the rod and the field, so it is vertical. The rod remains in equilibrium if the magnetic force points up and has the same magnitude as the weight. Solving $I \ell B = m g$ gives $I \approx 1.5\,\mathrm{A}$, with the mass in kilograms and the length in meters.

For the direction, apply the **right-hand rule** with your fingers pointing into the page along the field. Your palm must face up, in the direction of the required force. This happens when your thumb points to the right. Therefore, the current must flow **to the right** along the rod. With the current flowing to the left, the magnetic force would point down and add to the weight.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A horizontal rod of length $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ and mass $30\,\mathrm{g}$ hangs from two identical vertical springs, each with spring constant $k = 5.0\,\mathrm{N/m}$. It is in a uniform horizontal magnetic field $B = 0.30\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the rod.

- Calculate the extension of each spring when no current flows through the rod.
- A current $I = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ flows through the rod in the direction that makes the magnetic force point upward. Calculate the new extension.
- Calculate the extension when the current has the same magnitude but flows in the opposite direction.

Use $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
2 k x_0 = m g ,
\qquad
2 k x_1 + I \ell B = m g ,
\qquad
2 k x_2 = m g + I \ell B
$$
$$
x_0 = \frac{m g}{2k} ,
\qquad
x_1 = \frac{m g - I \ell B}{2k} ,
\qquad
x_2 = \frac{m g + I \ell B}{2k}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
m g &= 0.030 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.294\,\mathrm{N} ,
\qquad
I \ell B = 2.0 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 0.30\,\mathrm{N} = 0.15\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
x_0 &= \frac{0.294}{10}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.9\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
x_1 &= \frac{0.294 - 0.15}{10}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.4\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
x_2 &= \frac{0.294 + 0.15}{10}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 4.4\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With no current, the forces on the rod are its weight and the forces exerted by the two springs. By Hooke's law, each spring stretched by $x$ exerts an upward spring force $k x$. The two springs are identical, so their total force is $2 k x$. At equilibrium, the total spring force equals the weight, so $x_0 = \dfrac{m g}{2k} \approx 2.9\,\mathrm{cm}$.

When current flows, the magnetic force also acts. The rod is perpendicular to the field, so by the law for the **force on a straight wire**, the magnetic force has magnitude $I \ell B = 0.15\,\mathrm{N}$ and is vertical. When it points upward, it supports part of the weight, leaving only the difference $m g - I \ell B$ for the springs to support. The extension therefore decreases to about $1.4\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Reversing the current also reverses the magnetic force. It now points downward and adds to the weight. The springs must then support $m g + I \ell B$, and the extension increases to about $4.4\,\mathrm{cm}$. As the results show, in both cases the extension differs from $x_0$ by the same amount, $\dfrac{I \ell B}{2k} = 1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two parallel metal rails separated by a distance $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ lie in a plane inclined at $20^\circ$ to the horizontal. A rod of mass $50\,\mathrm{g}$ rests across the rails, perpendicular to them, and can slide without friction. The system is in a uniform upward vertical magnetic field of magnitude $B = 0.30\,\mathrm{T}$. A power supply connected to the rails drives a current $I$ through the rod. Calculate the value of $I$ for which the rod remains at rest and the force that the rails exert on the rod. Use $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 5,0 5,1.82 0,0" vettori="3,1.09,3,-0.41,mg;3,1.09,4.41,1.09,F;3,1.09,2.49,2.5,N" punti="3,1.09,pieno" etichette="1.1,0.12,20°;3.2,1.35,⊗" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5.5 ymin=-0.7 ymax=2.8 titolo="Vertical cross section along the rails. The rod ($\otimes$, current into the page) is subject to its weight $m\vec{g}$, the horizontal magnetic force $\vec{F}$, and the force $\vec{N}$ exerted by the rails perpendicular to the incline"}

$$
F = I \ell B \quad \text{(horizontal)}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
F \cos 20^\circ = m g \sin 20^\circ \\[4pt]
N = m g \cos 20^\circ + F \sin 20^\circ
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{m g \tan 20^\circ}{\ell B} ,
\qquad
N = \frac{m g}{\cos 20^\circ}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{0.050 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.364}{0.25 \cdot 0.30}\,\mathrm{A} = \frac{0.178}{0.075}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 2.4\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
N &= \frac{0.050 \cdot 9.81}{0.940}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.52\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is horizontal and the field is vertical, so they are perpendicular. By the law for the **force on a straight wire**, the magnetic force has magnitude $I \ell B$ and is perpendicular to both the rod and the vertical field. It is therefore *horizontal*, in the vertical plane containing the direction of the rails. Choose the current direction so that the force points toward the uphill end. In the diagram the current flows into the page. By the **right-hand rule**, with the thumb pointing into the page and the fingers pointing up, the palm faces right, toward the uphill end.

The rod is subject to its weight, the magnetic force, and the force exerted by the rails. The last force is perpendicular to the plane because friction is negligible. Resolve the forces into components along and perpendicular to the incline. Along the plane, the component of the weight, $m g \sin 20^\circ$, points downhill, and the component of the magnetic force, $F \cos 20^\circ$, points uphill. Perpendicular to the plane, both forces press the rod against the rails with components $m g \cos 20^\circ$ and $F \sin 20^\circ$, which are balanced by the force $N$ exerted by the rails. Both equilibrium conditions must hold at the same time, giving the system of equations above.

The first equation gives $F = m g \tan 20^\circ$ and hence $I = \dfrac{m g \tan 20^\circ}{\ell B} \approx 2.4\,\mathrm{A}$. Substituting $F$ into the second gives $N = m g \cos 20^\circ + m g \dfrac{\sin^2 20^\circ}{\cos 20^\circ} = \dfrac{m g}{\cos 20^\circ}$, since $\cos^2 20^\circ + \sin^2 20^\circ = 1$. The rails therefore exert a force of about $0.52\,\mathrm{N}$, **greater than the weight** $m g \approx 0.49\,\mathrm{N}$, because one component of the magnetic force presses the rod against the plane.

</div>
