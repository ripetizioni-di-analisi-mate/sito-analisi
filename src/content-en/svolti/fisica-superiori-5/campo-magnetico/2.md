## Exercise 1

A proton moves to the right in the plane of the page with velocity $v = 4.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.25\,\mathrm{T}$ directed out of the page. Calculate the magnitude of the Lorentz force on the proton and its acceleration. Determine the force direction and compare the force with the proton's weight. Use $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$, $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,1.8,0,v;0,0,0,-1.5,F" punti="0,0,pieno" etichette="-0.5,0.3,+e;-2.4,-1.6,⊙;-2.4,-0.5,⊙;-2.4,0.9,⊙;-2.4,2,⊙;-1.3,-1.6,⊙;-1.3,-0.5,⊙;-1.3,0.9,⊙;-1.3,2,⊙;1,-1.6,⊙;1,0.9,⊙;1,2,⊙;2.3,-1.6,⊙;2.3,-0.5,⊙;2.3,0.9,⊙;2.3,2,⊙" xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-2 ymax=2.4 titolo="Proton traveling to the right with velocity $\vec{v}$ in a field pointing out of the page ($\odot$). The Lorentz force points downward"}

$$
F = e v B ,
\qquad
a = \frac{F}{m_p} ,
\qquad
P = m_p g
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{N} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
a &= \frac{1.6 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}} \approx 9.6 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \\[6pt]
P &= 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{F}{P} = \frac{1.6 \cdot 10^{-14}}{1.6 \cdot 10^{-26}} \approx 1 \cdot 10^{12}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity lies in the plane of the page and the field is perpendicular to the page, so the angle between $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{B}$ is $90^\circ$. The **Lorentz force** therefore has its maximum magnitude, $F = |q|\,vB = evB$, which equals $1.6 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}$. By Newton's second law, the acceleration is the force divided by the mass, about $9.6 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

The proton has positive charge, so the force points in the direction of $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$. To apply the **right-hand rule**, point your thumb to the right and your fingers out of the page toward you. Your palm faces down. The force is therefore **directed downward**, perpendicular to the velocity.

The proton's weight is about $1.6 \cdot 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{N}$, roughly one trillionth of the magnetic force. For this reason, the weight is neglected when describing the motion of charged particles in magnetic fields.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron with velocity $v = 3.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ enters a region of uniform magnetic field $B = 2.0\,\mathrm{mT}$. Its velocity makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the field. Calculate the magnitude of the force on the electron and its acceleration. Explain what would change for a proton with the same velocity. Use $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$ and $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
F = e v B \sin\alpha ,
\qquad
a = \frac{F}{m_e}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \sin 60^\circ\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
&= 9.6 \cdot 10^{-16} \cdot 0.866\,\mathrm{N} \approx 8.3 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
a &= \frac{8.3 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{N}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}} \approx 9.1 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **Lorentz force** has magnitude $|q|\,vB\sin\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between the velocity and the field. Express the field in teslas, $2.0\,\mathrm{mT} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$, and use $\sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866$. This gives $F \approx 8.3 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{N}$. Dividing by the electron mass gives an acceleration of about $9.1 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

A proton with the same velocity has charge $+e$, equal in absolute value to the electron's charge. It therefore experiences a force of **the same magnitude** in **the opposite direction**, since for a negative charge the force points opposite to the direction given by the right-hand rule. The proton's mass is about $1800$ times the electron's mass, so its acceleration is smaller by a factor of about $1800$ and equals $\dfrac{8.3 \cdot 10^{-16}}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 5.0 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An alpha particle, consisting of two protons and two neutrons, has charge $q = 2e$ and mass $m = 6.64 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$. It moves perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$ and experiences a Lorentz force of magnitude $3.2 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}$.

- Calculate the particle's speed.
- Calculate its kinetic energy in joules and in electron volts.
- Explain how the kinetic energy changes as the particle moves through the field if the Lorentz force is the only force acting on it.

Use $e = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$ and $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$.

$$
F = q v B
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \frac{F}{q B} = \frac{F}{2 e B} ,
\qquad
K = \frac{1}{2} m v^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{3.2 \cdot 10^{-14}}{2 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.40}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \frac{3.2 \cdot 10^{-14}}{1.28 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
K &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6.64 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot \left( 2.5 \cdot 10^5 \right)^2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.1 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
K &= \frac{2.1 \cdot 10^{-16}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{eV} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{eV}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is perpendicular to the field, so the **Lorentz force** has magnitude $F = qvB$, with $q = 2e$. Dividing both sides by $qB$ gives $v = \dfrac{F}{2eB} = 2.5 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. This speed is about one thousandth of the speed of light, so the classical formula can be used for the kinetic energy.

The formula $K = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$ gives $K \approx 2.1 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{J}$. To convert to electron volts, divide by $1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J/eV}$. The result is about $1.3\,\mathrm{keV}$.

The Lorentz force is always perpendicular to the velocity. Thus, by the theorem on **zero work by the Lorentz force**, it does no work. By the work–energy theorem, the change in $K$ equals the work. The kinetic energy therefore **remains constant**, as does the speed. The field changes only the direction of motion.

</div>
