## Exercise 1

The coil of a DC electric motor has $200$ turns, each of area $12\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. It carries a current $I = 1.5\,\mathrm{A}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$. The commutator reverses the current every half turn.

- Calculate the maximum torque on the coil and the torque when the normal to the coil makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the field.
- Calculate the average torque over one revolution, assuming that the coil rotates at constant angular speed.

$$
M(\theta) = N I S B\,|\sin\theta| ,
\qquad
M_{\max} = N I S B
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{\max} &= 200 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 12 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 0.144\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \approx 0.14\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \\[6pt]
M(30^\circ) &= 0.144 \cdot \sin 30^\circ\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 0.072\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{\text{avg}}
&= \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^{\pi} M_{\max} \sin\theta\,d\theta \\[6pt]
&= \frac{M_{\max}}{\pi} \Big[ -\cos\theta \Big]_0^{\pi} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{M_{\max}}{\pi} \cdot (1 + 1) = \frac{2}{\pi}\,M_{\max} \approx 0.092\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="14.4*abs(sin(x));9.17" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-2 ymax=18 nomi="θ,M" legenda="M = 14.4\,|\sin\theta|;M_{\text{avg}} = 9.2" etichette="2.95,-1.3,π;6.1,-1.3,2π" titolo="Torque on the motor coil, in units of $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, as a function of angle. With the commutator, the torque always acts in the same direction and its average is $\dfrac{2}{\pi}$ of the maximum"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The magnetic moment of the coil has magnitude $N I S$, so the torque has magnitude $N I S B \sin\theta$. The commutator reverses the current every half turn at the instant when the torque would change direction. The torque therefore always acts in the same direction, and its magnitude is $N I S B\,|\sin\theta|$. Express the area in square meters, $12\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 12 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The maximum torque is $0.144\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. At $\theta = 30^\circ$, the sine is $0.50$, so the torque is half its maximum value, or $0.072\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

The coil rotates at constant angular speed, so the angle $\theta$ is proportional to time and the time average equals the average over $\theta$. The function $|\sin\theta|$ repeats every half turn, so its average over one revolution is the average of $\sin\theta$ between $0$ and $\pi$. The average value of a function on an interval is its integral divided by the length of the interval, as discussed in the chapter on definite integrals in fifth-year high school mathematics. An antiderivative of $\sin\theta$ is $-\cos\theta$, and the difference between its values at $\pi$ and $0$ is $1 - (-1) = 2$. Thus the average torque is $\dfrac{2}{\pi}\,M_{\max}$, about $64\%$ of the maximum, or $0.092\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A moving-coil ammeter has a coil of $150$ turns, each of area $2.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, in a radial magnetic field of magnitude $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$. The spiral spring has torsion constant $k = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m/rad}$, and the pointer reaches full scale at a deflection of $90^\circ$. Calculate the full-scale current and the pointer deflection when the current is $0.30\,\mathrm{mA}$.

$$
N I S B = k \varphi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{k \varphi}{N S B} ,
\qquad
\varphi = \frac{N S B}{k}\,I
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
N S B &= 150 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{T\cdot m^2} = 6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m^2} \\[6pt]
I_{\text{fs}} &= \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2}}{6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 7.9 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\varphi &= \frac{6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{3.0 \cdot 10^{-6}} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad} = 0.60\,\mathrm{rad} \approx 34^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **moving-coil ammeter**, the field is radial, so the plane of the coil always contains the field direction. The angle between $\vec{\mu}$ and $\vec{B}$ remains $90^\circ$, and the magnetic torque equals $N I S B$ at every position. The pointer comes to rest when this torque equals the restoring torque of the spring, $k \varphi$, with the angle in radians.

The full-scale deflection is $90^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad}$. Solving the torque balance for the current gives $I_{\text{fs}} \approx 0.79\,\mathrm{mA}$. For a current of $0.30\,\mathrm{mA}$, the angle is $0.60\,\mathrm{rad}$. Multiplying by $\dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi}$ gives about $34^\circ$. Since the angle is proportional to the current, a proportion also checks the result: $0.30\,\mathrm{mA}$ is about $38\%$ of the full-scale current, and $38\%$ of $90^\circ$ is about $34^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A moving-coil ammeter is to have a full-scale current $1.0\,\mathrm{mA}$ corresponding to a deflection of $90^\circ$. The available components are a spiral spring with torsion constant $k = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m/rad}$, a magnet producing a radial field of $0.25\,\mathrm{T}$, and a frame of area $2.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ on which to wind the coil. Calculate the number of turns required and the pointer deflection for a current of $0.40\,\mathrm{mA}$.

$$
N I_{\text{fs}} S B = k \varphi_{\text{fs}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N = \frac{k \varphi_{\text{fs}}}{I_{\text{fs}} S B}
$$
$$
N = \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.25} = \frac{6.28 \cdot 10^{-6}}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-8}} \approx 126
$$
$$
\varphi = \frac{I}{I_{\text{fs}}}\,\varphi_{\text{fs}} = \frac{0.40}{1.0} \cdot 90^\circ = 36^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At full scale, the magnetic torque, $N I_{\text{fs}} S B$, equals the spring torque, $k \varphi_{\text{fs}}$ with $\varphi_{\text{fs}} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad}$. The only unknown in this equation is $N$. Solving for it gives approximately $125.7$. The number of turns must be an integer, so $126$ turns are required. With this value, the full-scale current is $1.0\,\mathrm{mA}$ to the precision of the given data.

The angle is proportional to the current because $\varphi = \dfrac{N S B}{k}\,I$ and the coefficient $\dfrac{N S B}{k}$ is constant. Thus the ratio of two angles equals the ratio of the corresponding currents, and a current of $0.40\,\mathrm{mA}$, equal to $40\%$ of full scale, deflects the pointer by $40\%$ of $90^\circ$, or $36^\circ$.

</div>
