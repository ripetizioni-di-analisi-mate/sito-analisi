## Exercise 1

A rigid rectangular loop with sides $a = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $b = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries a current $I = 3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ and can rotate about an axis through its center parallel to the sides of length $a$. The loop is in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the axis.

- Calculate the force on each side of length $a$.
- Calculate the torque when the field is parallel to the plane of the loop.
- Calculate the moment arm of the couple and the torque when the normal to the loop makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the field.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-0.75,1.3 0.75,-1.3" vettori="-0.75,1.3,-0.75,2.3,F;0.75,-1.3,0.75,-2.3,−F;0,0,1.21,0.7,n̂;-2.6,-2.3,-1.2,-2.3,B" segmenti="0,0,1.6,0;-0.75,1.3,-0.75,-0.4;0.75,-1.3,0.75,0.4;-0.75,-0.4,0.75,-0.4" punti="0,0,pieno" etichette="-1.25,1.2,⊙;0.95,-1.45,⊗;0.62,0.06,θ;-0.25,-0.75,b sin θ" xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.6 titolo="Loop viewed along the axis with $\theta = 30^\circ$. The forces on the sides of length $a$ are perpendicular to $\vec{B}$, and the distance between their lines of action is $b \sin\theta$"}

$$
F = I a B ,
\qquad
M = F \cdot b \sin\theta = I a b B \sin\theta = I S B \sin\theta
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 3.0 \cdot 0.060 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N} = 9.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
M_{90^\circ} &= 9.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 3.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \\[6pt]
b \sin 30^\circ &= 0.040 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
M_{30^\circ} &= 9.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 1.8 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sides of length $a$ are parallel to the axis and therefore perpendicular to the field. By the law for the **force on a straight wire**, each experiences a force of magnitude $I a B = 9.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N}$ perpendicular to the field. The current flows in opposite directions along the two sides, so the two forces are opposite and form a couple. The forces on the sides of length $b$ are parallel to the axis and have the same line of action, so they contribute no torque.

When the field is parallel to the plane of the loop, the normal $\hat{n}$ is perpendicular to the field, $\theta = 90^\circ$. The lines of action of the two forces are then separated by the entire length $b$, and the torque of the couple is $F b = 3.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, its maximum value $I S B$.

With $\theta = 30^\circ$, the forces have the same magnitude as before because the sides $a$ remain perpendicular to the field. What changes is the moment arm. It is the projection of side $b$ onto the field direction, $b \sin\theta = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, as shown in the diagram. The torque is halved and equals $1.8 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The same value follows from the formula for the **torque on a rectangular loop**, $I S B \sin 30^\circ$, with $S = 24\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The torque rotates the loop in the direction that reduces $\theta$, turning $\hat{n}$ toward the field.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A flat coil of $50$ circular turns of radius $r = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries a current $I = 0.40\,\mathrm{A}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$. The normal to the coil makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the field. Calculate the magnetic moment of the coil, the torque acting on it, and the maximum torque. Identify the position in which the coil is in stable equilibrium.

$$
\mu = N I \pi r^2 ,
\qquad
M = \mu B \sin\theta ,
\qquad
M_{\max} = \mu B
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu &= 50 \cdot 0.40 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.020^2\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} \\[6pt]
M &= 2.51 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 0.20 \cdot \sin 60^\circ\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \\[6pt]
M_{\max} &= 2.51 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \approx 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The turns are circular, and the **magnetic moment** formula applies to a plane loop of any shape. Here the area of one turn is the area of a circle, $\pi r^2$. The $50$ turns are identical and carry the same current, so their moments add and $\mu = N I \pi r^2 \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2}$.

The torque has magnitude $\mu B \sin\theta$. With $\theta = 60^\circ$, we have $\sin\theta \approx 0.866$ and $M \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The maximum occurs at $\theta = 90^\circ$, when the field is parallel to the plane of the coil, and is about $5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

The torque always turns $\vec{\mu}$ toward $\vec{B}$. Thus the coil is in **stable equilibrium** when $\vec{\mu}$ points in the same direction as the field, or $\theta = 0^\circ$. In that position, the field is perpendicular to the plane of the coil and the torque is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A coil of $100$ square turns, each of area $8.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, is in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.15\,\mathrm{T}$. As the coil rotates, the maximum measured torque is $6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. Calculate the current in the coil and the angles between the normal to the coil and the field for which the torque is $3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

$$
M_{\max} = N I S B
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{M_{\max}}{N S B} ,
\qquad
\sin\theta = \frac{M}{M_{\max}}
$$
$$
I = \frac{6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{100 \cdot 8.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.15}\,\mathrm{A} = \frac{6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{1.2 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.50\,\mathrm{A}
$$
$$
\sin\theta = \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 0.50
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 30^\circ
\quad \text{or} \quad
\theta = 150^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The magnetic moment of the coil is $\mu = N I S$ because the turns are identical and carry the same current. The torque $\mu B \sin\theta$ is greatest at $\sin\theta = 1$, so $M_{\max} = N I S B$. Express the area in square meters, $8.0\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Solving for $I$ gives $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$.

The torque can be written as $M = M_{\max} \sin\theta$, so the value $3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$, half the maximum, corresponds to $\sin\theta = 0.50$. Between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, the two angles with this sine are $30^\circ$ and $150^\circ$. In the first case, $\vec{\mu}$ makes an acute angle with the field and in the second an obtuse angle. In both cases, the torque turns the coil toward the position $\theta = 0^\circ$.

</div>
