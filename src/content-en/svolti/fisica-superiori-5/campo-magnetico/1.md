## Exercise 1

A straight wire of length $\ell = 40\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries a current $I = 2.5\,\mathrm{A}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.30\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the wire. The wire lies in the plane of the page, with the current flowing to the right and the field pointing into the page. Calculate the magnitude of the force on the wire and determine the direction in which it acts.

$$
F = I \ell B \sin\alpha ,
\qquad
\alpha = 90^\circ
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F = I \ell B
$$
$$
F = 2.5\,\mathrm{A} \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.30\,\mathrm{T} = 0.30\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is uniform and the wire is straight, so the law for the **force on a straight wire**, $F = I \ell B \sin\alpha$, applies. The field points into the page and the wire lies in the plane of the page. Thus the wire is perpendicular to the field, $\alpha = 90^\circ$, and $\sin\alpha = 1$. The force therefore has its maximum magnitude $I \ell B$. The units are consistent because $1\,\mathrm{A} \cdot 1\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 1\,\mathrm{T} = 1\,\mathrm{N}$ by the definition of the tesla, and the length must be expressed in meters, $40\,\mathrm{cm} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$.

The force is perpendicular to the wire and to the field, so it lies in the plane of the page perpendicular to the wire. To apply the **right-hand rule**, point your thumb to the right along the current and your fingers into the page along the field. Your palm then faces up, so the force of $0.30\,\mathrm{N}$ is **directed upward** in the plane of the page.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A straight wire of length $80\,\mathrm{cm}$ carrying a current $I = 4.0\,\mathrm{A}$ makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.050\,\mathrm{T}$.

- Calculate the magnitude of the force on the wire.
- Calculate the maximum force obtainable by rotating the wire while keeping its length and the current fixed.
- Determine the angles between the wire and the field for which the force is $0.12\,\mathrm{N}$.

$$
F = I \ell B \sin\alpha ,
\qquad
F_{\max} = I \ell B ,
\qquad
\sin\alpha = \frac{F}{I \ell B}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 4.0 \cdot 0.80 \cdot 0.050 \cdot \sin 30^\circ\,\mathrm{N} = 0.16 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N} = 0.080\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_{\max} &= 4.0 \cdot 0.80 \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{N} = 0.16\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\sin\alpha = \frac{0.12\,\mathrm{N}}{0.16\,\mathrm{N}} = 0.75
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha \approx 49^\circ
\quad \text{or} \quad
\alpha \approx 180^\circ - 49^\circ = 131^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the law for the **force on a straight wire**, the force magnitude depends on the angle $\alpha$ between the wire and the field through the factor $\sin\alpha$. With $\alpha = 30^\circ$, we have $\sin\alpha = 0.50$, so the force is half the product $I \ell B = 0.16\,\mathrm{N}$, or $0.080\,\mathrm{N}$.

The sine never exceeds $1$ and equals $1$ when $\alpha = 90^\circ$. The force is therefore greatest when the wire is perpendicular to the field. Its maximum value is $F_{\max} = I \ell B = 0.16\,\mathrm{N}$.

For the last question, solve the same law for $\sin\alpha$ by dividing the specified force by $I \ell B$. This gives $\sin\alpha = 0.75$. Between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, there are two angles with this sine because $\sin(180^\circ - \alpha) = \sin\alpha$. A calculator gives $\arcsin 0.75 \approx 48.6^\circ$, so the angles are approximately $49^\circ$ and $131^\circ$. The force has the same magnitude in both cases, and the right-hand rule gives its direction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An L-shaped wire lies in the plane of the page. The first segment $PA$, of length $30\,\mathrm{cm}$, is horizontal and carries current to the right. The second segment $AQ$, of length $40\,\mathrm{cm}$, is vertical and carries current upward. The wire carries a current $I = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$ directed into the page. Calculate the force on each segment and the total force on the wire. Compare the result with the force on a straight wire from $P$ to $Q$ carrying the same current.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 3,0 3,4" segmenti="0,0,3,4" vettori="1.5,0,1.5,1.5,F₁;3,2,1,2,F₂;0.4,-0.35,1.4,-0.35,I;3.35,0.6,3.35,1.6,I" punti="0,0,pieno,P;3,0,pieno,A;3,4,pieno,Q" etichette="-0.9,-0.6,⊗;-0.9,1.4,⊗;-0.9,3.4,⊗;0.6,3.4,⊗;2.2,-0.6,⊗;4.1,-0.6,⊗;4.1,1.4,⊗;4.1,3.4,⊗;1.9,3.4,⊗" xmin=-1.3 xmax=4.6 ymin=-1 ymax=4.5 titolo="L-shaped wire in a field directed into the page ($\otimes$). The force $\vec{F}_1$ on the horizontal segment points upward, and the force $\vec{F}_2$ on the vertical segment points to the left"}

$$
F_1 = I \ell_1 B ,
\qquad
F_2 = I \ell_2 B ,
\qquad
F = \sqrt{F_1^2 + F_2^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1 &= 5.0 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N} = 0.30\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_2 &= 5.0 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N} = 0.40\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F &= \sqrt{0.30^2 + 0.40^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 0.50\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\overline{PQ} = \sqrt{0.30^2 + 0.40^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
I \cdot \overline{PQ} \cdot B = 5.0 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N} = 0.50\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both segments are straight and perpendicular to the field, which points into the page. By the law for the **force on a straight wire**, the force on each segment therefore has magnitude $I \ell B$. This gives $F_1 = 0.30\,\mathrm{N}$ and $F_2 = 0.40\,\mathrm{N}$.

Apply the **right-hand rule**, keeping the fingers pointed into the page. For segment $PA$, the thumb points right and the palm faces up, so $\vec{F}_1$ points upward. For segment $AQ$, the thumb points up and the palm faces left, so $\vec{F}_2$ points to the left.

The forces are perpendicular, so the Pythagorean theorem gives a total force of magnitude $0.50\,\mathrm{N}$. The total force points upward and to the left. In the plane of the page, the line in the direction of this force has slope $\dfrac{0.30}{-0.40} = -\dfrac{3}{4}$, whereas segment $PQ$ has slope $\dfrac{0.40}{0.30} = \dfrac{4}{3}$. The product of the slopes is $-1$, so the total force is perpendicular to $PQ$.

Segment $PQ$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs of lengths $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $40\,\mathrm{cm}$, so its length is $50\,\mathrm{cm}$. A straight wire from $P$ to $Q$ perpendicular to the field would experience a force $I \cdot \overline{PQ} \cdot B = 0.50\,\mathrm{N}$ perpendicular to $PQ$. The forces are identical. Thus in a uniform field the bent wire experiences **the same force** as the straight wire joining its ends.

</div>
