## Exercise 1

An electric motor connected to the power grid, with $V_{\text{eff}} = 230\,\mathrm{V}$ and $f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}$, draws an rms current of $5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ that lags the voltage by $30^\circ$. Calculate the average power drawn. Model the motor as a series combination of a resistor and an inductor and determine $R$ and $L$.

$$
P = V_{\text{eff}}\, I_{\text{eff}} \cos\varphi ,
\qquad
Z = \frac{V_{\text{eff}}}{I_{\text{eff}}} ,
\qquad
R = Z \cos\varphi ,
\qquad
X_L = Z \sin\varphi ,
\qquad
L = \frac{X_L}{2\pi f}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= 230\,\mathrm{V} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{A} \cdot \cos 30^\circ \approx 996\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
Z &= \frac{230\,\mathrm{V}}{5.0\,\mathrm{A}} = 46\,\Omega \\[6pt]
R &= 46\,\Omega \cdot \cos 30^\circ \approx 39.8\,\Omega ,
\qquad
X_L = 46\,\Omega \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 23\,\Omega \\[6pt]
L &= \frac{23\,\Omega}{2\pi \cdot 50\,\mathrm{Hz}} \approx 0.073\,\mathrm{H}
\end{aligned}
$$

The hypotenuse $Z = 46\,\Omega$ makes an angle $\varphi = 30^\circ$ with the leg $R$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The average power depends on the phase difference as well as on the rms values, so apply the result on **average power in an AC circuit** with $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866$. The product $V_{\text{eff}} I_{\text{eff}} = 1150\,\mathrm{W}$ exceeds the average power because the power factor is less than $1$.

In the series $RL$ circuit the impedance is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $R$ and $X_L$. The angle between the hypotenuse and the leg $R$ is $\varphi$ because $\tan\varphi = \dfrac{X_L}{R}$. Right-triangle trigonometry therefore gives $R = Z\cos\varphi$ and $X_L = Z\sin\varphi$. We can check that $R\,I_{\text{eff}}^2 = 39.8 \cdot 25\,\mathrm{W} \approx 995\,\mathrm{W}$, equal to the average power within rounding, in agreement with the definition of **power factor**: all the average power is dissipated in the resistance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A series $RLC$ circuit with $R = 80\,\Omega$, $X_L = 100\,\Omega$, and $X_C = 40\,\Omega$ is supplied by a source of rms voltage $200\,\mathrm{V}$. Calculate the impedance, rms current, power factor, and average power. Verify that the average power equals the power dissipated in the resistor.

$$
Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} ,
\qquad
I_{\text{eff}} = \frac{V_{\text{eff}}}{Z} ,
\qquad
\cos\varphi = \frac{R}{Z} ,
\qquad
P = V_{\text{eff}}\, I_{\text{eff}} \cos\varphi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z &= \sqrt{80^2 + 60^2}\,\Omega = 100\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{200\,\mathrm{V}}{100\,\Omega} = 2.0\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\cos\varphi &= \frac{80}{100} = 0.80 \\[6pt]
P &= 200\,\mathrm{V} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{A} \cdot 0.80 = 320\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
R\, I_{\text{eff}}^2 &= 80\,\Omega \cdot (2.0\,\mathrm{A})^2 = 320\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference between the reactances is $60\,\Omega$. With $R = 80\,\Omega$, the sides of the impedance triangle are proportional to $3$, $4$, and $5$, so the impedance is $100\,\Omega$. In the series $RLC$ circuit the power factor is $\dfrac{R}{Z}$, by the definition of **power factor**, giving $0.80$. The corresponding phase difference is $\varphi = \arctan \dfrac{60}{80} \approx 37^\circ$, with the current lagging because $X_L > X_C$.

The average power, calculated using the result on **average power in an AC circuit**, equals $R\,I_{\text{eff}}^2$. The inductor and capacitor store energy and return it to the source during each period, so they draw no net power on average. All the average power is dissipated in the resistor.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A workshop draws an average power of $3.0\,\mathrm{kW}$ from the grid at $V_{\text{eff}} = 230\,\mathrm{V}$. The line connecting it to the meter has a total resistance of $0.50\,\Omega$. Calculate the rms line current and the power dissipated in the line when the workshop’s power factor is $0.60$ and when it is $0.90$.

$$
I_{\text{eff}} = \frac{P}{V_{\text{eff}} \cos\varphi} ,
\qquad
P_{\text{line}} = R_\ell\, I_{\text{eff}}^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{with } \cos\varphi = 0.60 ,
\quad
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{3000\,\mathrm{W}}{230\,\mathrm{V} \cdot 0.60} \approx 21.7\,\mathrm{A} ,
\qquad
P_{\text{line}} = 0.50\,\Omega \cdot (21.7\,\mathrm{A})^2 \approx 236\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
\text{with } \cos\varphi = 0.90 ,
\quad
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{3000\,\mathrm{W}}{230\,\mathrm{V} \cdot 0.90} \approx 14.5\,\mathrm{A} ,
\qquad
P_{\text{line}} = 0.50\,\Omega \cdot (14.5\,\mathrm{A})^2 \approx 105\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The average power is fixed, so solving the formula for **average power in an AC circuit** for the rms current requires dividing by $V_{\text{eff}} \cos\varphi$. With a lower power factor the current is higher, because part of the current only exchanges energy with the motor inductors and does not contribute to average power.

The line behaves as a resistor carrying the same current and therefore dissipates power $R_\ell\, I_{\text{eff}}^2$ through Joule heating. The power lost is inversely proportional to $\cos^2\varphi$. Increasing the power factor from $0.60$ to $0.90$ reduces the loss by a factor of $\left( \dfrac{0.90}{0.60} \right)^2 = 2.25$, from about $236\,\mathrm{W}$ to about $105\,\mathrm{W}$. This is why the utility asks for a power factor close to $1$.

</div>
