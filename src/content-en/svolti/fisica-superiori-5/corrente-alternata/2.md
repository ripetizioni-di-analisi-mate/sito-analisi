## Exercise 1

The voltage across a resistor of resistance $R = 50\,\Omega$ is $v(t) = 325 \sin(314\,t)$, with voltage in volts and time in seconds. Determine the frequency, rms voltage, current amplitude, rms current, and average power dissipated in the resistor.

$$
f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} ,
\qquad
V_{\text{eff}} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} ,
\qquad
I_0 = \frac{V_0}{R} ,
\qquad
I_{\text{eff}} = \frac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}} ,
\qquad
P = V_{\text{eff}}\, I_{\text{eff}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{314}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{Hz} \approx 50\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
V_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{325}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 230\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
I_0 &= \frac{325\,\mathrm{V}}{50\,\Omega} = 6.5\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{6.5}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 4.6\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
P &= 230\,\mathrm{V} \cdot 4.6\,\mathrm{A} \approx 1.06 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The voltage has the form $V_0 \sin(\omega t)$. Comparison with the given expression gives the amplitude $V_0 = 325\,\mathrm{V}$ and angular frequency $\omega = 314\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The frequency is the angular frequency divided by $2\pi$ and equals the grid frequency. By the result on the **rms value of a sinusoidal quantity**, the rms voltage is the amplitude divided by $\sqrt{2}$, giving about $230\,\mathrm{V}$, the grid voltage.

The current in the resistor is in phase with the voltage and has amplitude $\dfrac{V_0}{R}$, by the result for a purely resistive circuit. The average power is calculated using rms values and the same formula as for direct current, by the definition of rms value. The expression $\dfrac{V_{\text{eff}}^2}{R} = \dfrac{230^2}{50}\,\mathrm{W} \approx 1.06\,\mathrm{kW}$ gives the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A hair dryer operates at a power of $1800\,\mathrm{W}$ when connected to the grid at $V_{\text{eff}} = 230\,\mathrm{V}$. Treat it as a resistor and calculate its rms current, peak current, and resistance. Also calculate the energy consumed in $15$ minutes of operation, in kilowatt-hours.

$$
I_{\text{eff}} = \frac{P}{V_{\text{eff}}} ,
\qquad
I_0 = \sqrt{2}\, I_{\text{eff}} ,
\qquad
R = \frac{V_{\text{eff}}^2}{P} ,
\qquad
E = P\,\Delta t
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{1800\,\mathrm{W}}{230\,\mathrm{V}} \approx 7.83\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
I_0 &= \sqrt{2} \cdot 7.83\,\mathrm{A} \approx 11.1\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
R &= \frac{(230\,\mathrm{V})^2}{1800\,\mathrm{W}} \approx 29.4\,\Omega \\[6pt]
E &= 1.8\,\mathrm{kW} \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{h} = 0.45\,\mathrm{kWh}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The power rating of an appliance is its average power, and the grid voltage of $230\,\mathrm{V}$ is an rms value. For a resistor, voltage and current are in phase, so the average power is the product $V_{\text{eff}} I_{\text{eff}}$. Solving this relation gives the rms current. Multiplying by $\sqrt{2}$ gives the peak value, by the result on the **rms value of a sinusoidal quantity**.

The resistance follows from $P = \dfrac{V_{\text{eff}}^2}{R}$, obtained by substituting $I_{\text{eff}} = \dfrac{V_{\text{eff}}}{R}$ into the power formula. Energy is the product of average power and time. Expressing power in kilowatts and time in hours, $15$ minutes is $0.25\,\mathrm{h}$, giving an energy of $0.45\,\mathrm{kWh}$, equivalent to $1.62 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $20\,\Omega$ resistor is connected first to a battery at a DC voltage of $12\,\mathrm{V}$, then to a sinusoidal AC voltage source with amplitude $V_0 = 12\,\mathrm{V}$. Calculate the power dissipated in each case and explain why the two values differ. Determine the amplitude the AC voltage must have to give the same average power as the battery.

$$
P_{\text{dc}} = \frac{V^2}{R} ,
\qquad
P_{\text{ac}} = \frac{V_{\text{eff}}^2}{R} = \frac{V_0^2}{2R} ,
\qquad
V_{\text{eff}} = V
\;\Longrightarrow\;
V_0 = \sqrt{2}\, V
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\text{dc}} &= \frac{(12\,\mathrm{V})^2}{20\,\Omega} = 7.2\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
P_{\text{ac}} &= \frac{(12\,\mathrm{V})^2}{2 \cdot 20\,\Omega} = 3.6\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
V_0 &= \sqrt{2} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{V} \approx 17\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="7.2*sin(x)^2" asintoti="y=7.2;y=3.6" xmin=0 xmax=13 ymin=-1 ymax=9 nomi="ωt,p" legenda="p(t)" titolo="Instantaneous power in watts in the resistor on an AC supply with $V_0 = 12\,\mathrm{V}$. The power oscillates between $0$ and $7.2\,\mathrm{W}$ and has an average value of $3.6\,\mathrm{W}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

With the battery, the voltage stays at $12\,\mathrm{V}$ and the power is $\dfrac{V^2}{R}$. The AC source voltage reaches $12\,\mathrm{V}$ only at its peaks, and its magnitude is smaller at other times. The instantaneous power $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$ therefore oscillates between $0$ and $7.2\,\mathrm{W}$. Its average value is half its maximum because the average of $\sin^2(\omega t)$ over one period is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The average AC power is therefore **half** the power supplied by the battery.

To obtain the same average power, the rms voltage must equal the battery voltage, by the definition of **rms value**. Since $V_{\text{eff}} = \dfrac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}}$, the amplitude must be $\sqrt{2} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{V} \approx 17\,\mathrm{V}$.

</div>
