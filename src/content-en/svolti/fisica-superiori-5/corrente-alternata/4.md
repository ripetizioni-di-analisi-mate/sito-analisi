## Exercise 1

A $40\,\Omega$ resistor and an ideal $0.10\,\mathrm{H}$ inductor are connected in series to a source of rms voltage $120\,\mathrm{V}$ and frequency $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Calculate the impedance, the rms current, the phase difference between voltage and current, and the rms voltage across each component.

$$
X_L = 2\pi f L ,
\qquad
Z = \sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2} ,
\qquad
I_{\text{eff}} = \frac{V_{\text{eff}}}{Z} ,
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{X_L}{R} ,
\qquad
V_R = R\, I_{\text{eff}} ,
\quad
V_L = X_L I_{\text{eff}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_L &= 2\pi \cdot 50 \cdot 0.10\,\Omega \approx 31.4\,\Omega \\[6pt]
Z &= \sqrt{40^2 + 31.4^2}\,\Omega \approx 50.9\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{120\,\mathrm{V}}{50.9\,\Omega} \approx 2.36\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\varphi &= \arctan \frac{31.4}{40} \approx 38^\circ \\[6pt]
V_R &= 40 \cdot 2.36\,\mathrm{V} \approx 94\,\mathrm{V} ,
\qquad
V_L = 31.4 \cdot 2.36\,\mathrm{V} \approx 74\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circuit contains no capacitors, so we apply the result on the **impedance of a series RLC circuit** with $X_C = 0$. The impedance is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $R$ and $X_L$, and the rms current is the rms voltage divided by the impedance.

The phase difference is positive, so the current **lags** the source voltage by about $38^\circ$ and the circuit is inductive. The voltages across the two components are $90^\circ$ out of phase because the resistor voltage is in phase with the current and the inductor voltage leads the current by $90^\circ$. For this reason their arithmetic sum, $168\,\mathrm{V}$, exceeds the source voltage, while the square root of the sum of their squares is $\sqrt{94^2 + 74^2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 120\,\mathrm{V}$, as required by the phasor diagram.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $120\,\Omega$ resistor and a $15\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor are connected in series to the power grid, with $V_{\text{eff}} = 230\,\mathrm{V}$ and $f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Calculate the impedance, rms current, phase difference, and rms voltage across each component. Explain whether the current leads or lags the voltage.

$$
X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi f C} ,
\qquad
Z = \sqrt{R^2 + X_C^2} ,
\qquad
\tan\varphi = -\frac{X_C}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_C &= \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot 50 \cdot 15 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\Omega \approx 212\,\Omega \\[6pt]
Z &= \sqrt{120^2 + 212^2}\,\Omega \approx 244\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{230\,\mathrm{V}}{244\,\Omega} \approx 0.943\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\varphi &= -\arctan \frac{212}{120} \approx -61^\circ \\[6pt]
V_R &= 120 \cdot 0.943\,\mathrm{V} \approx 113\,\mathrm{V} ,
\qquad
V_C = 212 \cdot 0.943\,\mathrm{V} \approx 200\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circuit contains no inductors, so in the result on the **impedance of a series RLC circuit** we set $X_L = 0$, and the difference $X_L - X_C$ becomes $-X_C$. The phase difference is negative, so the current **leads** the voltage by about $61^\circ$. The circuit is capacitive, as expected when the capacitive reactance exceeds the inductive reactance.

The rms voltages add arithmetically to about $313\,\mathrm{V}$, greater than the source voltage of $230\,\mathrm{V}$. The component voltages are $90^\circ$ out of phase and add as perpendicular vectors, so $\sqrt{113^2 + 200^2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 230\,\mathrm{V}$, in agreement with the source voltage.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A series $RLC$ circuit has $R = 50\,\Omega$, $L = 0.30\,\mathrm{H}$, and $C = 40\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ and is supplied with voltage $v(t) = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$, with $V_{\text{eff}} = 100\,\mathrm{V}$ and $f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

1. Calculate the reactances, impedance, and rms current.
2. Determine the phase difference and write the expression for $i(t)$.
3. Calculate the rms voltage across each of the three components and draw the phasor diagram.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_L &= 2\pi \cdot 50 \cdot 0.30\,\Omega \approx 94.2\,\Omega \\[6pt]
X_C &= \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot 50 \cdot 40 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\Omega \approx 79.6\,\Omega \\[6pt]
Z &= \sqrt{50^2 + (94.2 - 79.6)^2}\,\Omega = \sqrt{50^2 + 14.7^2}\,\Omega \approx 52.1\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{100\,\mathrm{V}}{52.1\,\Omega} \approx 1.92\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\varphi &= \arctan \frac{14.7}{50} \approx 0.29\,\mathrm{rad} \approx 16^\circ \\[6pt]
I_0 &= \sqrt{2} \cdot 1.92\,\mathrm{A} \approx 2.71\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
i(t) &\approx 2.71 \sin(314\,t - 0.29)\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
V_R = 50 \cdot 1.92\,\mathrm{V} \approx 96\,\mathrm{V} ,
\qquad
V_L = 94.2 \cdot 1.92\,\mathrm{V} \approx 181\,\mathrm{V} ,
\qquad
V_C = 79.6 \cdot 1.92\,\mathrm{V} \approx 153\,\mathrm{V}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,1.92,0,VR;0,0,0,3.62,VL;0,0,0,-3.05,VC;0,0,1.92,0.57,V" segmenti="1.92,0,1.92,0.57;0,0.57,1.92,0.57" etichette="0.55,0.12,φ" xmin=-1.6 xmax=3.6 ymin=-3.6 ymax=4.2 titolo="Rms voltage phasors in units of $50\,\mathrm{V}$: $V_R \approx 96\,\mathrm{V}$, $V_L \approx 181\,\mathrm{V}$, and $V_C \approx 153\,\mathrm{V}$. Their sum $V = 100\,\mathrm{V}$ makes an angle $\varphi \approx 16^\circ$ with the current"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate the reactances at the source frequency, using $\omega = 2\pi \cdot 50\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 314\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Then apply the result on the **impedance of a series RLC circuit**. The difference between the reactances is $14.7\,\Omega$, much smaller than $R$, so the impedance is only slightly greater than the resistance.

The inductive reactance exceeds the capacitive reactance, so $\varphi > 0$ and the current lags the voltage. The circuit is inductive. In the theorem the current is $I_0 \sin(\omega t)$ and the voltage is $V_0 \sin(\omega t + \varphi)$. Here the voltage has zero initial phase, so the initial phase of the current is $-\varphi$, and the current expression contains $\omega t - 0.29$. The angle $\varphi$ must be expressed in radians because $\omega t$ is in radians.

The rms voltages are obtained by multiplying the rms current by $R$, $X_L$, and $X_C$, respectively. In the diagram the current lies along the horizontal axis. The phasor $V_R$ points in the same direction, $V_L$ points up, and $V_C$ points down, as in the observation on voltage and current phasors in the three circuits. The sum of $V_L$ and $V_C$ is a vertical vector of magnitude $181 - 153 = 28\,\mathrm{V}$. Together with $V_R$ it forms a right triangle whose hypotenuse is $\sqrt{96^2 + 28^2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 100\,\mathrm{V}$, the source voltage.

</div>
