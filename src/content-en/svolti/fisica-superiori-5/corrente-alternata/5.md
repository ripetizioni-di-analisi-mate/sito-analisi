## Exercise 1

A series $RLC$ circuit has $R = 25\,\Omega$, $L = 50\,\mathrm{mH}$, and $C = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ and is supplied by a source of rms voltage $10\,\mathrm{V}$ with adjustable frequency. Calculate the resonant frequency, rms current at resonance, and rms voltages across the inductor and capacitor at resonance.

$$
\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} ,
\qquad
f_0 = \frac{\omega_0}{2\pi} ,
\qquad
I_{\text{eff}} = \frac{V_{\text{eff}}}{R} ,
\qquad
V_L = V_C = \omega_0 L\, I_{\text{eff}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega_0 &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1.0 \cdot 10^{-7}}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 3.16 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
f_0 &= \frac{3.16 \cdot 10^3}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{Hz} \approx 503\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{10\,\mathrm{V}}{25\,\Omega} = 0.40\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
X_L &= 3.16 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\Omega \approx 158\,\Omega ,
\qquad
V_L = V_C = 158 \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{V} \approx 63\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the result on **resonance in a series RLC circuit**, the rms current is greatest at the angular frequency $\omega_0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$, where the two reactances are equal. Express the inductance in henries, $50\,\mathrm{mH} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{H}$, and the capacitance in farads. At resonance the impedance equals the resistance, so the rms current is $\dfrac{V_{\text{eff}}}{R}$.

At resonance $X_L = X_C$, so the rms voltages across the inductor and capacitor are equal. We can also verify that $X_C = \dfrac{1}{3.16 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\Omega \approx 158\,\Omega$. Each voltage is approximately $63\,\mathrm{V}$, more than six times the source voltage. The two voltages are in opposite phase and cancel at every instant, so the entire source voltage appears across the resistor.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The tuning circuit of a radio consists of a $0.25\,\mathrm{mH}$ inductor and a capacitor whose capacitance can be varied between $30\,\mathrm{pF}$ and $300\,\mathrm{pF}$. Determine the range of frequencies the radio can receive. Then calculate the capacitance required to receive a station broadcasting at $1.0\,\mathrm{MHz}$.

$$
f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} ,
\qquad
C = \frac{1}{4\pi^2 f_0^2 L}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{\max} &= \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2.5 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-11}}}\,\mathrm{Hz} \approx 1.84 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
f_{\min} &= \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2.5 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-10}}}\,\mathrm{Hz} \approx 5.81 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
C &= \frac{1}{4\pi^2 \cdot \left( 1.0 \cdot 10^6 \right)^2 \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 1.0 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F} = 100\,\mathrm{pF}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radio receives the station whose frequency equals the resonant frequency of the circuit because only at that frequency is the current large. This is the application described in the result on **resonance in a series RLC circuit**. The relevant unit prefixes are given by $1\,\mathrm{mH} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{H}$ and $1\,\mathrm{pF} = 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F}$.

The resonant frequency is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{C}$, so the highest frequency corresponds to the smallest capacitance and the lowest frequency to the largest capacitance. The capacitance varies by a factor of $10$, so the frequency varies by a factor of $\sqrt{10} \approx 3.16$. The radio can therefore receive frequencies from about $581\,\mathrm{kHz}$ to about $1.84\,\mathrm{MHz}$. To calculate the capacitance, square the formula for $f_0$ and solve for $C$. The frequency $1.0\,\mathrm{MHz}$ lies within the tuning range and, as expected, the calculated capacitance, $100\,\mathrm{pF}$, lies between $30\,\mathrm{pF}$ and $300\,\mathrm{pF}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A series $RLC$ circuit with $C = 5.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is supplied at rms voltage $3.2\,\mathrm{V}$ and variable angular frequency. The graph shows the measured rms current as a function of angular frequency. Read the resonant angular frequency and peak of the rms current from the graph. Then calculate the resistance and inductance of the circuit and the rms current at angular frequency $\omega = 1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

::grafico{f="3200/sqrt(400+(50*x-200/x)^2)+0*sqrt(x)" punti="2,160,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,160;0,160,2,160" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=-20 ymax=180 nomi="ω,I" legenda="I_{\text{eff}}(\omega)" titolo="Rms current in milliamperes as a function of angular frequency, with $\omega$ in $10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$"}

$$
\omega_0 = 2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
I_{\max} = 160\,\mathrm{mA}
$$
$$
R = \frac{V_{\text{eff}}}{I_{\max}} ,
\qquad
\omega_0^2 = \frac{1}{LC}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
L = \frac{1}{\omega_0^2 C} ,
\qquad
I_{\text{eff}} = \frac{V_{\text{eff}}}{\sqrt{R^2 + \left( \omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C} \right)^2}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \frac{3.2\,\mathrm{V}}{0.160\,\mathrm{A}} = 20\,\Omega \\[6pt]
L &= \frac{1}{\left( 2.0 \cdot 10^3 \right)^2 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{H} = \frac{1}{20}\,\mathrm{H} = 0.050\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
X_L &= 1.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 0.050\,\Omega = 50\,\Omega ,
\qquad
X_C = \frac{1}{1.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\Omega = 200\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{\text{eff}} &= \frac{3.2\,\mathrm{V}}{\sqrt{20^2 + 150^2}\,\Omega} \approx \frac{3.2}{151}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 21\,\mathrm{mA}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The curve reaches its peak at angular frequency $2.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, where the rms current is $160\,\mathrm{mA}$. By the result on **resonance in a series RLC circuit**, the peak occurs at the resonant angular frequency, where the impedance equals $R$. The resistance is therefore the rms voltage divided by the maximum rms current.

Squaring $\omega_0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$ gives $\omega_0^2 = \dfrac{1}{LC}$, from which we obtain $L$. At $\omega = 1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, half the resonant angular frequency, the capacitive reactance exceeds the inductive reactance, so the circuit is capacitive. The difference in magnitude between the reactances, $150\,\Omega$, is much larger than $R$, and the current falls to about $21\,\mathrm{mA}$, consistent with the graph.

</div>
