## Exercise 1

A coil of $200$ turns, each of area $0.015\,\mathrm{m^2}$, rotates at $3000$ revolutions per minute in a uniform magnetic field of $0.080\,\mathrm{T}$ about an axis perpendicular to the field. At time $t = 0$ the plane of the turns is perpendicular to the field. Calculate the frequency and angular frequency of the induced emf, its peak value, and its value at time $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{ms}$.

$$
f = \frac{n_{\text{rev}}}{\Delta t} ,
\qquad
\omega = 2\pi f ,
\qquad
\mathcal{E}(t) = N B S \omega \sin(\omega t) ,
\qquad
\mathcal{E}_0 = N B S \omega
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{3000}{60\,\mathrm{s}} = 50\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
\omega &= 2\pi \cdot 50\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 314\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
\mathcal{E}_0 &= 200 \cdot 0.080\,\mathrm{T} \cdot 0.015\,\mathrm{m^2} \cdot 314\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 75.4\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
\mathcal{E}(1.0\,\mathrm{ms}) &= 75.4\,\mathrm{V} \cdot \sin\left( 314 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3} \right) = 75.4\,\mathrm{V} \cdot \sin(0.314) \approx 23.3\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coil makes $3000$ revolutions in one minute, or $60\,\mathrm{s}$, so it makes $50$ revolutions per second. The emf completes one oscillation per revolution, so its frequency equals the rotational frequency, $f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}$, which is also the grid frequency. The angular frequency is $\omega = 2\pi f$.

At time $t = 0$ the plane of the turns is perpendicular to the field, so the normal to the turns is parallel to $\vec{B}$. This is the initial condition for the result on the **electromotive force of an alternator**. The field is uniform and perpendicular to the axis of rotation, so the result applies and gives $\mathcal{E}(t) = N B S \omega \sin(\omega t)$. The peak value is the product $N B S \omega$.

At time $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{ms}$ the argument of the sine is $\omega t = 0.314\,\mathrm{rad}$, one twentieth of a revolution. Evaluating the sine with a calculator set to radians gives approximately $0.309$. The emf at that instant is therefore about $23.3\,\mathrm{V}$, roughly one third of its peak value, because the coil has rotated only $18^\circ$ from the position where the emf is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A laboratory alternator has a coil of $120$ turns, each of area $0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$, rotating in a uniform magnetic field of $0.15\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the axis of rotation. Determine the angular speed, frequency, and number of revolutions per minute required to produce an emf with peak value $50\,\mathrm{V}$.

$$
\mathcal{E}_0 = N B S \omega
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{N B S} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} ,
\qquad
n_{\text{rpm}} = 60\,\mathrm{s} \cdot f
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega &= \frac{50\,\mathrm{V}}{120 \cdot 0.15\,\mathrm{T} \cdot 0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}} = \frac{50}{0.36}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 139\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
f &= \frac{139}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{Hz} \approx 22.1\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
n_{\text{rpm}} &= 60 \cdot 22.1 \approx 1.33 \cdot 10^3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is uniform and perpendicular to the axis of rotation, so the result on the **electromotive force of an alternator** applies, and the peak emf is $\mathcal{E}_0 = N B S \omega$. The unknown in this formula is $\omega$. To solve for it, divide both sides by the product $N B S$. The product is $120 \cdot 0.15 \cdot 0.020 = 0.36$, measured in $\mathrm{T \cdot m^2}$, or webers.

The units agree because, by Faraday’s law of induction, one volt is one weber per second, so $\dfrac{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{Wb}} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Dividing the angular frequency by $2\pi$ gives the frequency in revolutions per second. Multiplying by $60$ gives the number of revolutions per minute, about $1330$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The graph shows the emf measured across a coil of $200$ turns, each of area $5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, rotating at constant angular speed in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the axis of rotation. Read the peak emf and its period from the graph. Then calculate the angular frequency, the magnitude of the magnetic field, and the maximum flux linkage of the coil, and write the expression for $\Phi_B(t)$.

::grafico{f="30*sin(2*pi*x/40)" segmenti="0,30,10,30;40,0,40,-8" punti="10,30,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=100 ymin=-50 ymax=50 nomi="t,ℰ" legenda="\mathcal{E}(t)" titolo="Emf across the coil in volts, with time $t$ in milliseconds"}

$$
\mathcal{E}_0 = 30\,\mathrm{V} ,
\qquad
T = 40\,\mathrm{ms}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{E}_0 = N B S \omega
\;\Longrightarrow\;
B = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{N S \omega} ,
\qquad
\Phi_0 = N B S = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{\omega}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega &= \frac{2\pi}{0.040\,\mathrm{s}} \approx 157\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
B &= \frac{30\,\mathrm{V}}{200 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2} \cdot 157\,\mathrm{rad/s}} \approx 0.19\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
\Phi_0 &= \frac{30\,\mathrm{V}}{157\,\mathrm{rad/s}} \approx 0.19\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
\Phi_B(t) &\approx 0.19 \cos(157\,t)\,\mathrm{Wb}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Read the peak value from the vertical axis at the crest, reached at time $t = 10\,\mathrm{ms}$. It is $30\,\mathrm{V}$. The curve starts at zero at time $t = 0$ and crosses zero again on the way up at time $t = 40\,\mathrm{ms}$, after one crest and one trough. The period is therefore $T = 40\,\mathrm{ms}$, and the angular frequency is $\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$.

The emf has the form $\mathcal{E}_0 \sin(\omega t)$, so at time $t = 0$ the normal to the turns is parallel to the field, as in the result on the **electromotive force of an alternator**, and $\mathcal{E}_0 = N B S \omega$ holds. Dividing by $N S \omega$ gives $B$. The value of $N S$ is $200 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$, which is why the numerical value of $B$ coincides with that of the maximum flux.

The flux linkage is $N B S \cos(\omega t)$, as in the proof of the theorem, so its peak value $\Phi_0 = N B S$ can also be found as $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}_0}{\omega}$. The flux is greatest at time $t = 0$, when the emf is zero, in agreement with the observation on **maximum flux and zero emf**.

</div>
