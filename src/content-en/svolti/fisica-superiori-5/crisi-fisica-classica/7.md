## Exercise 1

X-rays of wavelength $\lambda = 71.0\,\mathrm{pm}$ are scattered by a graphite block, and the scattered radiation is observed at $\theta = 90^\circ$. Calculate the electron Compton wavelength, the wavelength of the scattered radiation, the relative change in wavelength, and the kinetic energy gained by the electron. Use $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$.

$$
\lambda_C = \frac{h}{m_e c} ,
\qquad
\lambda' = \lambda + \lambda_C (1 - \cos\theta) ,
\qquad
K = \frac{h c}{\lambda} - \frac{h c}{\lambda'}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_C &= \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.43 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.43\,\mathrm{pm} \\[6pt]
\lambda' &= 71.0\,\mathrm{pm} + 2.43\,\mathrm{pm} \cdot (1 - 0) \approx 73.4\,\mathrm{pm} \\[6pt]
\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda} &= \frac{2.43}{71.0} \approx 3.4\% \\[6pt]
\frac{h c}{\lambda} &= \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{7.10 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{eV} \approx 1.751 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{eV} \\[6pt]
\frac{h c}{\lambda'} &= \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{7.343 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{eV} \approx 1.693 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{eV} \\[6pt]
K &\approx 1.751 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{eV} - 1.693 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{eV} \approx 5.8 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{eV}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The X-ray photons have energy of about $17.5\,\mathrm{keV}$, far greater than the energy binding the outer electrons to the carbon atoms. These electrons can therefore be treated as free and initially at rest, and the **Compton scattering formula** applies. The constant $\dfrac{h}{m_e c}$ is the Compton wavelength, about $2.43\,\mathrm{pm}$.

At $\theta = 90^\circ$ we have $\cos\theta = 0$, so the wavelength increases by exactly $\lambda_C$ to about $73.4\,\mathrm{pm}$. The relative change is about $3.4\%$, large enough to measure with an X-ray spectrometer.

Energy is conserved in the collision, so the electron’s kinetic energy equals the difference between the incident and scattered photon energies. The energies, calculated using $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, are close together, so four digits are needed to obtain their difference to two significant figures. The electron gains about $5.8 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mathrm{eV}$, just over $3\%$ of the photon’s energy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A gamma-ray photon of wavelength $\lambda = 10.0\,\mathrm{pm}$ collides head-on with a free electron initially at rest and is scattered backward, with $\theta = 180^\circ$. Calculate the wavelength of the scattered photon, the initial and final photon energies in kiloelectronvolts, the electron’s kinetic energy, and the magnitude of its momentum. Use $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="-4,0.4,-0.2,0.4,λ;-0.2,-0.4,-3,-0.4,λ′;0.2,0,3.4,0,e⁻" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=-4.4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=1 legenda=0 titolo="Backscattering. The photon reverses direction and the electron recoils in the direction of the incident photon"}

$$
\lambda' = \lambda + \lambda_C (1 - \cos 180^\circ) = \lambda + 2\lambda_C ,
\qquad
K = \frac{h c}{\lambda} - \frac{h c}{\lambda'} ,
\qquad
p_e = \frac{h}{\lambda} + \frac{h}{\lambda'}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda' &= 10.0\,\mathrm{pm} + 2 \cdot 2.43\,\mathrm{pm} \approx 14.9\,\mathrm{pm} \\[6pt]
E &= \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{1.00 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{eV} \approx 124\,\mathrm{keV} \\[6pt]
E' &= \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34} \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{1.485 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\mathrm{eV} \approx 83.7\,\mathrm{keV} \\[6pt]
K &\approx 124.3\,\mathrm{keV} - 83.7\,\mathrm{keV} \approx 40.6\,\mathrm{keV} \\[6pt]
p_e &= \left( \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{1.00 \cdot 10^{-11}} + \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{1.485 \cdot 10^{-11}} \right) \mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 1.11 \cdot 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At $\theta = 180^\circ$, we have $1 - \cos\theta = 2$, so the **Compton scattering formula** gives the largest possible shift, $2\lambda_C \approx 4.85\,\mathrm{pm}$, and a scattered wavelength of about $14.9\,\mathrm{pm}$. For such a short initial wavelength, the change is nearly $50\%$. The photon loses one third of its energy, which falls from about $124\,\mathrm{keV}$ to about $83.7\,\mathrm{keV}$. By conservation of energy the electron gains the difference, about $40.6\,\mathrm{keV}$.

Take the positive axis along the direction of the incident photon. Before the collision the total momentum is $\dfrac{h}{\lambda}$. After the collision the photon has momentum $-\dfrac{h}{\lambda'}$ because it travels backward. By **conservation of momentum**, $\dfrac{h}{\lambda} = -\dfrac{h}{\lambda'} + p_e$, so the electron has momentum $\dfrac{h}{\lambda} + \dfrac{h}{\lambda'}$, directed along the path of the incident photon.

The electron’s kinetic energy is about $8\%$ of its rest energy, $m_e c^2 \approx 512\,\mathrm{keV}$, so its speed is not negligible compared with $c$. Its momentum is therefore found using conservation of momentum instead of the classical formula $m_e v$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An X-ray photon of wavelength $\lambda = 40.0\,\mathrm{pm}$ strikes a free electron initially at rest and scatters at angle $\theta = 60^\circ$. Calculate the scattered photon’s wavelength, the components and magnitude of the electron’s momentum after the collision, the angle $\varphi$ between the electron’s direction and that of the incident photon, and the electron’s kinetic energy. Use $h = 6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}$, $m_e = 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,2.49,0,p;0,0,1.21,2.09,p′;0,0,1.28,-2.09,pₑ" segmenti="1.21,2.09,2.49,0;1.28,-2.09,2.49,0" curve="0.45*cos(t),0.45*sin(t),0,1.047;0.55*cos(t),0.55*sin(t),-1.021,0" etichette="0.62,0.3,θ;0.7,-0.3,φ" xmin=-1.6 xmax=4.2 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.4 legenda=0 titolo="Conservation of momentum in a Compton collision. The incident photon momentum $\vec{p}$ is the vector sum of the scattered photon momentum $\vec{p}\,'$ and the electron momentum $\vec{p}_e$"}

$$
\lambda' = \lambda + \lambda_C (1 - \cos\theta) ,
\qquad
\begin{cases}
p_{e,x} = \dfrac{h}{\lambda} - \dfrac{h}{\lambda'} \cos\theta \\[10pt]
p_{e,y} = -\dfrac{h}{\lambda'} \sin\theta
\end{cases}
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{|p_{e,y}|}{p_{e,x}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda' &= 40.0\,\mathrm{pm} + 2.426\,\mathrm{pm} \cdot (1 - 0.500) \approx 41.2\,\mathrm{pm} \\[6pt]
\frac{h}{\lambda} &= \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{4.00 \cdot 10^{-11}}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 1.658 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \\[6pt]
\frac{h}{\lambda'} &= \frac{6.63 \cdot 10^{-34}}{4.121 \cdot 10^{-11}}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 1.609 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \\[6pt]
p_{e,x} &= \left( 1.658 - 1.609 \cdot 0.500 \right) \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx 8.53 \cdot 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \\[6pt]
p_{e,y} &= -1.609 \cdot 10^{-23} \cdot 0.866\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \approx -1.39 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \\[6pt]
p_e &= \sqrt{p_{e,x}^2 + p_{e,y}^2} \approx 1.63 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s} \\[6pt]
\varphi &= \arctan \frac{1.393}{0.853} \approx 58.5^\circ \\[6pt]
K &= \frac{h c}{\lambda} - \frac{h c}{\lambda'} = c \left( \frac{h}{\lambda} - \frac{h}{\lambda'} \right) \approx 3.00 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 4.88 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.46 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 915\,\mathrm{eV}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With $\cos 60^\circ = 0.500$, the **Compton scattering formula** gives a wavelength increase of half the Compton wavelength, about $1.21\,\mathrm{pm}$, so $\lambda' \approx 41.2\,\mathrm{pm}$.

Take the $x$-axis along the incident photon’s direction and the $y$-axis toward the side on which the photon scatters. Before the collision the electron is at rest and the total momentum is the photon’s, $\dfrac{h}{\lambda}$ along $x$. After the collision the scattered photon has components $\dfrac{h}{\lambda'} \cos\theta$ and $\dfrac{h}{\lambda'} \sin\theta$. By **conservation of momentum**, the electron momentum equals the initial momentum minus the scattered photon momentum, component by component. Both component equations hold simultaneously and form the system above. The $y$-component is negative, so the electron recoils on the opposite side of the $x$-axis from the scattered photon.

The Pythagorean theorem gives a magnitude of about $1.63 \cdot 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The tangent of the angle $\varphi$ below the $x$-axis is the absolute value of the $y$-component divided by the $x$-component, so $\varphi \approx 58.5^\circ$.

By conservation of energy, the kinetic energy of the electron equals the energy lost by the photon. Photon energy equals $c$ times photon momentum, so multiply the difference $\dfrac{h}{\lambda} - \dfrac{h}{\lambda'} \approx 4.88 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ by $c$ to obtain about $915\,\mathrm{eV}$. As a check, the electron’s speed is about $\dfrac{p_e}{m_e} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, or $6\%$ of $c$, and the classical formula $\dfrac{p_e^2}{2 m_e}$ gives a value differing by less than $1\%$.

</div>
