## Exercise 1

The spectrum of star A peaks at $\lambda_A = 290\,\mathrm{nm}$, and that of star B at $\lambda_B = 725\,\mathrm{nm}$. Treat both stars as blackbodies. Determine their surface temperatures and the ratio of the power emitted by $1\,\mathrm{m^2}$ of the surface of A to the power emitted by $1\,\mathrm{m^2}$ of the surface of B. Wien’s constant is $2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\cdot K}$.

$$
\lambda_{\max}\, T = 2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\cdot K}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = \frac{2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\cdot K}}{\lambda_{\max}}
$$
$$
\frac{P_A}{P_B} = \frac{\sigma A T_A^4}{\sigma A T_B^4} = \left( \frac{T_A}{T_B} \right)^4 = \left( \frac{\lambda_B}{\lambda_A} \right)^4
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_A &= \frac{2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}}{2.90 \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{K} = 1.00 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{K} \\[6pt]
T_B &= \frac{2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}}{7.25 \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{K} = 4.00 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{K} \\[6pt]
\frac{P_A}{P_B} &= \left( \frac{725}{290} \right)^4 = 2.5^4 \approx 39
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The stars are treated as blackbodies, so **Wien’s law** relates the peak wavelength to temperature. Solving the law for $T$ requires dividing Wien’s constant by the wavelength expressed in meters: $290\,\mathrm{nm} = 2.90 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}$ and $725\,\mathrm{nm} = 7.25 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}$. Star A, whose spectrum peaks in the ultraviolet, appears blue. Star B, whose spectrum peaks at the boundary of the red and infrared regions, appears red.

By the **Stefan–Boltzmann law**, the power emitted by a surface of area $A$ is $\sigma A T^4$. The two areas are both $1\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so $\sigma$ and $A$ cancel in the ratio, leaving the fourth power of the temperature ratio. Since $T$ is inversely proportional to $\lambda_{\max}$, the ratio $\dfrac{T_A}{T_B}$ equals $\dfrac{\lambda_B}{\lambda_A} = 2.5$. Each square meter of star A therefore emits about **39 times** the power emitted by a square meter of star B.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The Sun has radius $R_S = 6.96 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m}$ and surface temperature $T = 5800\,\mathrm{K}$ and is $d = 1.50 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{m}$ from Earth. Treating the Sun as a blackbody, calculate its total power output, the intensity of solar radiation at Earth’s distance, and the wavelength at which the emission peaks. Use $\sigma = 5.67 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W/(m^2\cdot K^4)}$ and Wien’s constant $2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\cdot K}$.

$$
P = \sigma \cdot 4\pi R_S^2 \cdot T^4 ,
\qquad
I = \frac{P}{4\pi d^2} ,
\qquad
\lambda_{\max} = \frac{2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\cdot K}}{T}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= 5.67 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot 4\pi \cdot \left( 6.96 \cdot 10^{8} \right)^2 \cdot 5800^4\,\mathrm{W} \approx 3.91 \cdot 10^{26}\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{3.91 \cdot 10^{26}}{4\pi \cdot \left( 1.50 \cdot 10^{11} \right)^2}\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \approx 1.38 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \\[6pt]
\lambda_{\max} &= \frac{2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}}{5800}\,\mathrm{m} = 5.00 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m} = 500\,\mathrm{nm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Sun is a sphere, so its emitting surface area is $4\pi R_S^2$. By the **Stefan–Boltzmann law**, the power emitted is the product of $\sigma$, the surface area, and the fourth power of temperature. In this calculation, $5800^4 \approx 1.13 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{K^4}$ and $4\pi R_S^2 \approx 6.09 \cdot 10^{18}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

The emitted power spreads uniformly over progressively larger spheres centered on the Sun and is not absorbed along its path through space. At distance $d$ the sphere has area $4\pi d^2$, so the intensity is the power divided by this area. The result, about $1.38\,\mathrm{kW/m^2}$, agrees with the value measured by satellites outside the atmosphere, $1.36\,\mathrm{kW/m^2}$, to within $2\%$.

**Wien’s law** places the peak at $500\,\mathrm{nm}$, in the blue-green region near the center of the visible spectrum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A person’s skin is at temperature $T_p = 306\,\mathrm{K}$ and has surface area $A = 1.8\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The walls of the room are at $T_a = 293\,\mathrm{K}$. Treating the skin as a blackbody, calculate the power the person emits, the power absorbed from radiation from the walls, the net power lost by radiation, and the energy lost in one hour. Finally, determine the wavelength at which the skin’s emission peaks. Use $\sigma = 5.67 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W/(m^2\cdot K^4)}$ and Wien’s constant $2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\cdot K}$.

$$
P_e = \sigma A T_p^4 ,
\qquad
P_a = \sigma A T_a^4 ,
\qquad
P_{\text{net}} = \sigma A \left( T_p^4 - T_a^4 \right) ,
\qquad
E = P_{\text{net}}\, \Delta t
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_e &= 5.67 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot 1.8 \cdot 306^4\,\mathrm{W} \approx 895\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
P_a &= 5.67 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot 1.8 \cdot 293^4\,\mathrm{W} \approx 752\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
P_{\text{net}} &= 895\,\mathrm{W} - 752\,\mathrm{W} \approx 143\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
E &= 143 \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{J} \approx 5.1 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
\lambda_{\max} &= \frac{2.90 \cdot 10^{-3}}{306}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 9.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The skin is treated as a blackbody at temperature $T_p$, so its emitted power is given by the **Stefan–Boltzmann law** with $T_p$. A blackbody absorbs all the radiation it receives. The walls, at temperature $T_a$, fill the room with thermal radiation, of which the skin absorbs power $\sigma A T_a^4$. This equals the power the skin would emit at the temperature of the walls, when emission and absorption would balance.

The power lost is the difference between emitted and absorbed power, about $143\,\mathrm{W}$. In one hour, or $3600\,\mathrm{s}$, the energy lost is the power times the time, about $5.1 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$. The net power is comparable to the metabolic power of a resting person, about $100\,\mathrm{W}$. Clothing lowers the temperature of the outer surface and thus reduces the actual heat loss.

By **Wien’s law**, the skin’s emission peaks at about $9.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, in the infrared. This is why thermal cameras can detect the body’s radiation even though the unaided eye cannot see it.

</div>
