## Exercise 1

A circle has circumference $18\pi$ cm. What area does it enclose?

- A) $324\pi\ \text{cm}^2$
- B) $9\pi\ \text{cm}^2$
- C) $81\pi\ \text{cm}^2$
- D) $36\pi\ \text{cm}^2$
- E) $18\pi\ \text{cm}^2$

$$
2\pi r = 18\pi
\iff
r = 9,
\qquad
A = \pi r^2 = 81\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From the **circumference** $C = 2\pi r$, divide by $2\pi$ to obtain a radius of $r = 9$ cm. The area of the circle is then $\pi \cdot 9^2 = 81\pi\ \text{cm}^2$. Choice A uses the diameter $18$ in place of the radius, and choice B gives the radius without squaring it. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sector has radius $10$ cm and a central angle of $72^\circ$. What is its area?

- A) $4\pi\ \text{cm}^2$
- B) $100\pi\ \text{cm}^2$
- C) $72\pi\ \text{cm}^2$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **area of a sector** is the fraction $\dfrac{\alpha}{360^\circ}$ of the area of the full circle. Since $\dfrac{72}{360} = \dfrac{1}{5}$ and the circle has area $100\pi\ \text{cm}^2$, the sector has area $20\pi\ \text{cm}^2$. Choice A gives the arc length, $\dfrac{1}{5} \cdot 20\pi = 4\pi$, a length rather than an area. Choice B gives the area of the full circle. The correct answer is **D**.

## Exercise 3

A bicycle wheel has diameter $50$ cm. Using $\pi \approx 3.14$, how many complete revolutions does the wheel make over $1.57$ km if it rolls without slipping?

- A) $1000$
- B) $500$
- C) $2000$
- D) $100$
- E) $10\,000$

$$
C = \pi d = 3.14 \cdot 50 = 157\ \text{cm},
\qquad
1.57\ \text{km} = 157\,000\ \text{cm}
$$
$$
\frac{157\,000}{157} = 1000
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With no slipping, the wheel travels one circumference per revolution, or $\pi d = 157$ cm. The total distance in centimeters is $157\,000$ cm, so the number of revolutions is $1000$. Choice B uses $2\pi d$ in place of $\pi d$, confusing the diameter with the radius in the formula $2\pi r$. Choice C uses $\pi r$. Choices D and E introduce an erroneous factor of $10$ in converting kilometers to centimeters. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
