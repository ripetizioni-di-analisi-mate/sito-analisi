## Exercise 1

A trapezoid has bases of $14$ cm and $8$ cm and a height of $5$ cm. What is its area?

- A) $110\ \text{cm}^2$
- B) $70\ \text{cm}^2$
- C) $27\ \text{cm}^2$
- D) $55\ \text{cm}^2$
- E) $40\ \text{cm}^2$

$$
A = \frac{(B + b) \cdot h}{2} = \frac{(14 + 8) \cdot 5}{2} = \frac{110}{2} = 55
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find the **area of a trapezoid**, multiply the sum of its bases by the height and divide by $2$. The area is $55\ \text{cm}^2$. Choice A omits division by $2$. Choices B and E use just one base, as if the figure were a rectangle. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular office floor measuring $6$ m by $4.5$ m is covered with square tiles of side length $30$ cm, with no waste. How many tiles are needed?

- A) $300$
- B) $90$
- C) $30$
- D) $3000$
- E) $70$

$$
A_{\text{floor}} = 600\ \text{cm} \cdot 450\ \text{cm} = 270\,000\ \text{cm}^2
$$
$$
A_{\text{tile}} = 30 \cdot 30 = 900\ \text{cm}^2,
\qquad
\frac{270\,000}{900} = 300
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of tiles is the floor area divided by the area of one tile, with both areas measured in the same units. In centimeters the floor measures $600$ cm by $450$ cm, so it has area $270\,000\ \text{cm}^2$, and each tile covers $900\ \text{cm}^2$. Thus $300$ tiles are needed. As a check, the $6$ m side fits $20$ tiles, the $4.5$ m side fits $15$, and $20 \cdot 15 = 300$. Choice B divides the floor area of $27\ \text{m}^2$ by the tile side length of $0.3$ m instead of the tile area. Choice E divides the perimeter by the side length. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rhombus has a perimeter of $52$ cm and a diagonal of $24$ cm. What is its area?

- A) $240\ \text{cm}^2$
- B) $156\ \text{cm}^2$
- C) $60\ \text{cm}^2$
- D) $312\ \text{cm}^2$
- E) $120\ \text{cm}^2$

$$
l = \frac{52}{4} = 13,
\qquad
\frac{d}{2} = \sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5
$$
$$
d = 10,
\qquad
A = \frac{24 \cdot 10}{2} = 120
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-6,0 0,2.5 6,0 0,-2.5" segmenti="-6,0,6,0;0,-2.5,0,2.5" etichette="2.8,0.25,12;0.2,1.1,5;3.2,1.7,13" xmin=-6.6 xmax=6.6 ymin=-3 ymax=3 titolo="The diagonals of the rhombus are perpendicular and bisect each other, so each $13$ cm side is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with a leg of length $12$ cm"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A rhombus has four equal sides, so its side length is $13$ cm. Its diagonals are perpendicular and bisect each other. The side is therefore the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are half the diagonals. One half-diagonal is $12$ cm, so by the **Pythagorean theorem**, the other is $5$ cm, as in the triple $(5, 12, 13)$. The second diagonal is therefore $10$ cm, and the area is $\dfrac{D \cdot d}{2} = 120\ \text{cm}^2$. Choice A omits division by $2$, and choice C multiplies the two half-diagonals. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
