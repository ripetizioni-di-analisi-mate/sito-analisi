## Exercise 1

A coffee roaster mixes $8$ kg of coffee priced at $12$ euros per kilogram with $2$ kg of coffee priced at $17$ euros per kilogram. What is the price per kilogram of the blend?

- A) $14.50$ euros
- B) $16$ euros
- C) $13$ euros
- D) $13.50$ euros
- E) $12.50$ euros

$$
p = \frac{8 \cdot 12 + 2 \cdot 17}{8 + 2} = \frac{96 + 34}{10} = \frac{130}{10} = 13
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **price per unit of a mixture** is the total cost divided by the total quantity. The coffee costs $130$ euros in all for $10$ kg, so the blend costs $13$ euros per kilogram. Option A takes the arithmetic mean of the two prices and ignores the quantities. Option B switches the quantities of the two kinds of coffee. Since the blend contains more of the less expensive coffee, its price is closer to $12$ euros than to $17$ euros. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

You have $6$ liters of a solution that is $40\%$ alcohol. How much water must you add to obtain a solution that is $15\%$ alcohol?

- A) $16$ liters
- B) $25$ liters
- C) $3.6$ liters
- D) $10$ liters
- E) $12$ liters

$$
0.40 \cdot 6 = 2.4\ \text{l of alcohol}
$$
$$
V_2 = \frac{2.4}{0.15} = 16\ \text{l},
\qquad
16 - 6 = 10\ \text{l}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During **dilution**, the amount of alcohol stays the same. It is $40\%$ of $6$ liters, or $2.4$ liters. In the new solution, these $2.4$ liters must make up $15\%$ of the total, so the final volume is $16$ liters. The amount of water added is the difference between the final and initial volumes, or $10$ liters. Option A gives the final volume instead of the amount of water added. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A merchant wants to prepare $50$ kg of a tea blend priced at $4.20$ euros per kilogram by mixing tea priced at $3$ euros per kilogram with tea priced at $6$ euros per kilogram. How many kilograms of the tea priced at $3$ euros per kilogram are needed?

- A) $20$
- B) $25$
- C) $35$
- D) $40$
- E) $30$

$$
q_1 : q_2 = (6 - 4.20) : (4.20 - 3) = 1.8 : 1.2 = 3 : 2
$$
$$
\frac{50}{3 + 2} = 10,
\qquad
q_1 = 3 \cdot 10 = 30\ \text{kg}
$$

::grafico{linee="2.5,0 6.5,0" punti="3,0,pieno;4.2,0,pieno;6,0,pieno" segmenti="3,0.6,4.2,0.6;4.2,0.6,6,0.6;3,0.3,3,0.9;4.2,0.3,4.2,0.9;6,0.3,6,0.9" etichette="2.7,-0.8,3 €;3.9,-0.8,4.20 €;5.7,-0.8,6 €;3.2,1.2,120 cents;4.8,1.2,180 cents;2.6,-1.6,30 kg;5.7,-1.6,20 kg" assi=0 xmin=2.3 xmax=6.7 ymin=-2.2 ymax=2 titolo="Prices of the two teas and their blend. The differences from the blend price, $1.20$ euros and $1.80$ euros, are in the inverse ratio of the quantities, $30$ kg and $20$ kg."}

<div class="spiegazione">

To find the **quantities to mix for a given price**, use the fact that they are inversely proportional to how far each price is from $4.20$ euros. The differences are $1.80$ euros for the more expensive tea and $1.20$ euros for the cheaper tea, so the ratio of cheaper tea to more expensive tea is $3 : 2$. Divide $50$ kg into $5$ parts of $10$ kg each. The tea priced at $3$ euros per kilogram accounts for $3$ parts, or $30$ kg. To check, $\dfrac{30 \cdot 3 + 20 \cdot 6}{50} = \dfrac{210}{50} = 4.20$. Option A switches the two quantities. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
