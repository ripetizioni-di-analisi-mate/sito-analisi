## Exercise 1

A plot of land has an area of $0.45$ hectares. What is its area in square meters?

- A) $45\ \text{m}^2$
- B) $450\ \text{m}^2$
- C) $45\,000\ \text{m}^2$
- D) $4500\ \text{m}^2$
- E) $4.5\ \text{m}^2$

$$
1\ \text{ha} = 10\,000\ \text{m}^2
$$
$$
0.45 \cdot 10\,000 = 4500\ \text{m}^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A hectare is the area of a square with sides of $100$ m, so it equals $100 \cdot 100 = 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$. To **convert the area**, multiply $0.45$ by $10\,000$, moving the decimal point four places to the right to obtain $4500\ \text{m}^2$. Option A uses a factor of $10$ for each step instead of $100$, giving $1\ \text{ha} = 100\ \text{m}^2$. Option B treats a hectare as $1000\ \text{m}^2$. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular aquarium has inside dimensions of $120$ cm, $80$ cm, and $50$ cm. How much water does it hold when filled to the brim?

- A) $48$ liters
- B) $4800$ liters
- C) $0.48$ liters
- D) $48\,000$ liters
- E) $480$ liters

$$
120\ \text{cm} = 12\ \text{dm},
\qquad
80\ \text{cm} = 8\ \text{dm},
\qquad
50\ \text{cm} = 5\ \text{dm}
$$
$$
V = 12 \cdot 8 \cdot 5 = 480\ \text{dm}^3 = 480\ \text{l}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 12,0 12,5 0,5 0,0;0,5 4,8 16,8 12,5;16,8 16,3 12,0" segmenti="0,0,4,3;4,3,16,3;4,3,4,8" etichette="4,-1,120 cm = 12 dm;14.3,1.2,80 cm = 8 dm;-6.5,2,50 cm = 5 dm" assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-7 xmax=18 ymin=-2 ymax=9 titolo="An aquarium with edge lengths of $12$ dm, $8$ dm, and $5$ dm has a volume of $12 \cdot 8 \cdot 5 = 480\ \text{dm}^3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $1\ \text{l} = 1\ \text{dm}^3$, measuring the dimensions in decimeters gives a volume in cubic decimeters with the same numerical value as the capacity in liters. The product of the three dimensions is $480\ \text{dm}^3$, so the aquarium holds $480$ liters. Working in centimeters gives $480\,000\ \text{cm}^3$. Dividing by $100$ or $10\,000$ instead of $1000$ gives options B and A, respectively. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular tank measures $2.5$ m by $1.2$ m by $0.8$ m on the inside and is full of water. How many $20$-liter containers can you fill by emptying the tank completely?

- A) $12$
- B) $120$
- C) $1200$
- D) $2400$
- E) $240$

$$
V = 2.5 \cdot 1.2 \cdot 0.8 = 2.4\ \text{m}^3 = 2400\ \text{l}
$$
$$
\frac{2400}{20} = 120
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tank's volume is the product of its three dimensions, or $2.4\ \text{m}^3$. Since $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{l}$, the tank holds $2400$ liters, so dividing by the capacity of a $20$-liter container gives $\dfrac{2400}{20} = 120$ containers. Option D gives the capacity in liters instead of the number of containers. Options A and C differ from the result by a factor of $10$. They result from converting $\text{m}^3$ to liters using $100$ or $10\,000$. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
