## Exercise 1

A car travels $195$ km in $2$ hours and $30$ minutes. What is its average speed?

- A) $65$ km/h
- B) $78$ km/h
- C) $97.5$ km/h
- D) $75$ km/h
- E) $84.8$ km/h

$$
2\ \text{h}\ 30\ \text{min} = 2.5\ \text{h}
$$
$$
v = \frac{195}{2.5} = 78\ \text{km/h}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Average speed** is distance divided by time. Here the time must be expressed in decimal hours. Thirty minutes is half an hour, so the time is $2.5$ h and the speed is $78$ km/h. Option E treats $2$ hours and $30$ minutes as $2.3$ h, since $\dfrac{195}{2.3} \approx 84.8$. Options A and C divide by $3$ hours and $2$ hours, respectively. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cyclist travels $32$ km in $2$ hours. At the same average speed, how far does the cyclist travel in $2$ hours and $15$ minutes?

- A) $34.4$ km
- B) $32$ km
- C) $30$ km
- D) $36$ km
- E) $40$ km

$$
v = \frac{32}{2} = 16\ \text{km/h}
$$
$$
2\ \text{h}\ 15\ \text{min} = 2.25\ \text{h},
\qquad
s = 16 \cdot 2.25 = 36\ \text{km}
$$

At $16$ km/h, the cyclist covers $32$ km in $2$ h and $36$ km in $2.25$ h."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The cyclist's average speed is $16$ km/h. Fifteen minutes is a quarter of an hour, or $0.25$ h, so the new time is $2.25$ h. Multiplying speed by time gives a distance of $36$ km. The time exceeds $2$ hours, so the distance must exceed $32$ km. This rules out options B and C. Option A treats $2$ hours and $15$ minutes as $2.15$ h. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An athlete runs $100$ meters in $12.5$ seconds. What is the athlete's average speed in km/h?

- A) $28.8$ km/h
- B) $8$ km/h
- C) $2.22$ km/h
- D) $36$ km/h
- E) $24$ km/h

$$
v = \frac{100}{12.5} = 8\ \text{m/s}
$$
$$
8 \cdot 3.6 = 28.8\ \text{km/h}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With distance in meters and time in seconds, the speed is $8$ m/s. To **convert from m/s to km/h**, multiply by $3.6$. The speed is therefore $28.8$ km/h. Option B gives the value in m/s before conversion, and option C divides by $3.6$ instead of multiplying. The numerical value in km/h is always greater than the value in m/s for a runner in motion, which rules out B and C. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
