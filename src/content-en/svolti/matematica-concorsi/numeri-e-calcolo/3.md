## Exercise 1

Which of the following numbers is divisible by $9$?

- A) $1\,236$
- B) $5\,412$
- C) $7\,371$
- D) $8\,124$
- E) $6\,303$

$$
1 + 2 + 3 + 6 = 12,
\qquad
5 + 4 + 1 + 2 = 12,
\qquad
7 + 3 + 7 + 1 = 18
$$
$$
8 + 1 + 2 + 4 = 15,
\qquad
6 + 3 + 0 + 3 = 12
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **divisibility test for 9**, a number is divisible by $9$ if its digit sum is divisible by $9$. The only digit sum divisible by $9$ is $18$, so the required number is $7\,371$, and indeed $7\,371 = 9 \cdot 819$. The other sums, $12$ and $15$, are divisible by $3$, so all the proposed numbers are divisible by $3$. Confusing the two tests makes it impossible to choose. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which of the following numbers is prime?

- A) $91$
- B) $87$
- C) $119$
- D) $133$
- E) $103$

$$
91 = 7 \cdot 13,
\qquad
87 = 3 \cdot 29,
\qquad
119 = 7 \cdot 17,
\qquad
133 = 7 \cdot 19
$$
$$
10^2 = 100 < 103 < 121 = 11^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To determine whether a number less than $121$ is **prime**, it suffices to test divisibility by $2$, $3$, $5$, and $7$ because $11^2 = 121$. The digits of $87$ add up to $15$, so it is divisible by $3$, while $91$, $119$, and $133$ are divisible by $7$. The number $103$ is odd, has a digit sum of $4$, does not end in $0$ or $5$, and when divided by $7$ leaves a remainder of $5$, so it is prime. The numbers $91$, $119$, and $133$ may appear prime because none is divisible by $2$, $3$, or $5$. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A room needs $180$ chairs arranged in rows with the same number of chairs in each row. There must be at least $2$ rows and at least $2$ chairs per row. In how many different ways can the number of chairs per row be chosen?

- A) $18$
- B) $16$
- C) $8$
- D) $12$
- E) $9$

$$
180 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5
$$
$$
(2 + 1) \cdot (2 + 1) \cdot (1 + 1) = 18,
\qquad
18 - 2 = 16
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of chairs per row must divide $180$ because all rows have the same size. The **prime factorization** $180 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$ gives $3 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 18$ divisors. Exclude $180$, which would give just one row, and $1$, which would give only one chair per row. This leaves $16$ possible choices. Choice A counts all the divisors without taking the stated conditions into account, and choice C adds $2 + 1$, $2 + 1$, and $1 + 1$ instead of multiplying them. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
