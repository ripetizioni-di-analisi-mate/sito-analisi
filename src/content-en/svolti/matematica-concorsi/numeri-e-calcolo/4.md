## Exercise 1

Three bus routes leave from the same terminal. The first has departures every $12$ minutes, the second every $15$ minutes, and the third every $20$ minutes. Buses on all three routes leave together at 7\:00. When do they next leave together?

- A) 7\:47
- B) 7\:20
- C) 9\:00
- D) 8\:00
- E) 7\:30

$$
12 = 2^2 \cdot 3,
\qquad
15 = 3 \cdot 5,
\qquad
20 = 2^2 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm}(12, 15, 20) = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 60
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Departures on the first route occur at multiples of $12$ minutes, those on the second at multiples of $15$, and those on the third at multiples of $20$. The three routes have a simultaneous departure after a number of minutes that is a common multiple of all three intervals. The first such time is given by the **least common multiple**, namely $60$ minutes. The buses therefore leave together again at 8\:00. Choice A adds the three intervals, and choice B uses only the longest interval. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A carpenter has three boards measuring $210$ cm, $252$ cm, and $294$ cm. The carpenter cuts them into equal pieces of the greatest possible length with no waste. How many pieces are there in all?

- A) $42$
- B) $15$
- C) $18$
- D) $54$
- E) $36$

$$
210 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7,
\qquad
252 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7,
\qquad
294 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7^2
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(210, 252, 294) = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 42
$$
$$
\frac{210}{42} + \frac{252}{42} + \frac{294}{42} = 5 + 6 + 7 = 18
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The length of one piece must divide all three lengths exactly and be as large as possible, so it is the **greatest common divisor** of the three lengths. Using the common prime factors with their smallest exponents gives a GCD of $42$ cm. The three boards yield $5$, $6$, and $7$ pieces, respectively, giving $18$ pieces in all. Choice A gives the length of one piece instead of the number of pieces. Choice D uses a length of $14$ cm, which is a common divisor but not the greatest. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A farm collects more than $50$ but fewer than $100$ eggs. If the eggs are packed in cartons of $4$, $6$, or $9$, one egg is left over each time. How many eggs are there?

- A) $37$
- B) $85$
- C) $72$
- D) $109$
- E) $73$

$$
\operatorname{mcm}(4, 6, 9) = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 = 36
$$
$$
36 + 1 = 37,
\qquad
72 + 1 = 73,
\qquad
108 + 1 = 109
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If one egg is left over each time, removing one egg gives a number divisible by $4$, $6$, and $9$. It must therefore be a multiple of their **least common multiple** $36$. The possible numbers of eggs are therefore $37$, $73$, $109$, and so on, and the only one between $50$ and $100$ is $73$. Choice C is the multiple of $36$ without the extra egg. The number $85$ in choice B when divided by $4$ or $6$ leaves a remainder of $1$, but when divided by $9$ leaves a remainder of $4$. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
