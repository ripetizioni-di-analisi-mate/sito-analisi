## Exercise 1

How many three-digit numbers contain the digit $5$ at least once?

- A) $271$
- B) $252$
- C) $300$
- D) $648$
- E) $243$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{all} &= 9 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 900 \\[6pt]
\text{without } 5 &= 8 \cdot 9 \cdot 9 = 648 \\[6pt]
\text{with at least one } 5 &= 900 - 648 = 252
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use **counting by complements** because it is easier to count the numbers without the digit $5$. There are $900$ three-digit numbers since the first digit ranges from $1$ to $9$. If the digit $5$ is excluded, the first digit has $8$ choices, from $1$ to $9$ except $5$. Each remaining digit has $9$ choices, from $0$ to $9$ except $5$. There are $648$ such numbers, so $900 - 648 = 252$ numbers contain at least one $5$. Option A, $1000 - 729$, includes sequences starting with $0$, which are not three-digit numbers. Option D counts the complementary outcomes. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A six-sided die is rolled three times and the sequence of results is recorded. How many sequences contain at least one $6$?

- A) $108$
- B) $125$
- C) $91$
- D) $36$
- E) $216$

$$
\begin{aligned}
6^3 - 5^3 &= 216 - 125 \\[6pt]
&= 91
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Sequences of three rolls are **ordered selections with repetition** from $6$ possible results, making $3$ choices for $3$ rolls. There are $6^3 = 216$ sequences. A sequence with no $6$ has $5$ choices for each roll, so there are $5^3 = 125$ such sequences. By **counting with complements**, there are $216 - 125 = 91$ sequences with at least one $6$. Option A, $3 \cdot 36 = 108$, counts the $6$ in each of the three positions and adds the counts, counting sequences with two or three occurrences of $6$ more than once. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A department has $6$ engineers and $4$ administrative officials. A group of $4$ people is chosen. How many groups contain at least one administrative official?

- A) $195$
- B) $210$
- C) $336$
- D) $15$
- E) $80$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\binom{10}{4} - \binom{6}{4}
&= 210 - 15 \\[6pt]
&= 195
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{tipo="incluso" zone="a" nomi="groups,engineers only" elementi="a:195;ab:15" titolo="Of the $\dbinom{10}{4} = 210$ possible groups, $15$ consist only of engineers and $210 - 15 = 195$ include at least one official."}

<div class="spiegazione">

There are $\dbinom{10}{4} = \dfrac{10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7}{24} = 210$ groups of $4$ people chosen from $10$. Groups without officials consist only of engineers, and there are $\dbinom{6}{4} = \dbinom{6}{2} = 15$ of them. By **counting with complements**, $210 - 15 = 195$ groups include at least one official. Option C, $4 \cdot \dbinom{9}{3} = 336$, chooses an official first and then the other three people. This counts groups with two or more officials more than once. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
