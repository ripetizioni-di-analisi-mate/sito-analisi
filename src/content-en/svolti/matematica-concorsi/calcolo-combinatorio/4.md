## Exercise 1

An association with $10$ members must elect a president, a vice president, and a treasurer. No member may hold two offices. In how many ways can the three offices be assigned?

- A) $120$
- B) $1000$
- C) $30$
- D) $90$
- E) $720$

$$
D_{10,3} = 10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 = 720
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three offices are different, so exchanging the offices of two members produces a different assignment and order matters. Because a member cannot hold two offices, repetition is not allowed. Count the **ordered selections without repetition** of $3$ of the $10$ members for $3$ offices, giving $10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 = 720$. Option A, $120 = C_{10,3}$, counts groups of three without assigning offices. Option B, $10^3$, allows the same member to hold multiple offices. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Using the digits $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $5$, and $6$, how many three-digit numbers with distinct digits can be formed?

- A) $216$
- B) $120$
- C) $20$
- D) $18$
- E) $720$

$$
D_{6,3} = 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 = 120
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Digit order matters because $123$ and $321$ are different numbers, and the question rules out repeated digits. Count the **ordered selections without repetition** of $3$ of the $6$ digits for $3$ positions. The available digits exclude $0$, so there is no restriction on the first digit. There are $6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 = 120$ numbers. Option A, $6^3 = 216$, allows repeated digits. Option C, $C_{6,3} = 20$, ignores order. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

How many four-digit numbers with all digits different can be formed using the digits from $0$ to $9$?

- A) $5040$
- B) $3024$
- C) $9000$
- D) $4536$
- E) $10\,000$

$$
\begin{aligned}
9 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7
&= 81 \cdot 56 \\[6pt]
&= 4536
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.5 xmax=12.5 ymin=-0.6 ymax=2.8 poligoni="0,0 2,0 2,1.5 0,1.5;3.2,0 5.2,0 5.2,1.5 3.2,1.5;6.4,0 8.4,0 8.4,1.5 6.4,1.5;9.6,0 11.6,0 11.6,1.5 9.6,1.5" etichette="0.6,0.6,9;0.1,1.95,1st digit;3.8,0.6,9;3.3,1.95,2nd digit;7,0.6,8;6.5,1.95,3rd digit;10.2,0.6,7;9.7,1.95,4th digit" titolo="Choices for the four digits. There are $9$ choices for the first because it cannot be $0$, then $9$, $8$, and $7$ choices, giving $9 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 4536$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

A four-digit number cannot start with $0$, so the first digit is chosen from the $9$ digits $1$ through $9$. There are $9$ choices left for the second digit because $0$ is now allowed and one digit has already been used. There are $8$ remaining choices for the third digit and $7$ for the fourth. The **multiplication principle** gives $9 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 4536$ numbers. Option A, $D_{10,4} = 5040$, includes sequences starting with $0$. There are $9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 504$ such sequences, and indeed $5040 - 504 = 4536$. Option C, $9000$, counts all four-digit numbers, including those with repeated digits. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
