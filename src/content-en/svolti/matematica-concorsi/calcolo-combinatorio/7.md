## Exercise 1

A candidate must answer $3$ questions chosen from the $7$ provided. The order of the answers does not matter. In how many ways can the questions be chosen?

- A) $35$
- B) $210$
- C) $343$
- D) $21$
- E) $70$

$$
\binom{7}{3} = \frac{7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = \frac{210}{6} = 35
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The question states that order does not matter, and a question cannot be chosen twice. Count the **combinations without repetition** of $3$ of the $7$ questions, with $3$ in each selection. There are $\dbinom{7}{3} = 35$ choices. Option B, $D_{7,3} = 210$, counts ordered choices. Option D, $\dbinom{7}{2} = 21$, chooses two questions instead of three. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An office has $6$ clerks and $5$ officials. A working group must include $2$ clerks and $2$ officials. How many different groups can be formed?

- A) $330$
- B) $25$
- C) $600$
- D) $75$
- E) $150$

$$
\binom{6}{2} \cdot \binom{5}{2} = 15 \cdot 10 = 150
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Forming a group requires two independent choices, one for the clerks and one for the officials. Order does not matter in either choice. The clerks can be chosen in $\dbinom{6}{2} = 15$ ways and the officials in $\dbinom{5}{2} = 10$ ways. By the **multiplication principle**, multiply these counts to get $150$ groups. Option B, $15 + 10$, adds the counts instead of multiplying them. Option A, $\dbinom{11}{4} = 330$, ignores the requirement of $2$ clerks and $2$ officials. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

From a group of $5$ men and $4$ women, a committee of $3$ is chosen. How many committees contain at least $2$ women?

- A) $30$
- B) $84$
- C) $4$
- D) $34$
- E) $42$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\binom{4}{2} \binom{5}{1} + \binom{4}{3} \binom{5}{0}
&= 6 \cdot 5 + 4 \cdot 1 \\[6pt]
&= 30 + 4 \\[6pt]
&= 34
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

“At least $2$ women” covers two disjoint cases, exactly $2$ women or exactly $3$ women. In the first case, choose $2$ of the $4$ women and one of the $5$ men, giving $6 \cdot 5 = 30$ committees. In the second case, choose $3$ of the $4$ women, giving $4$ committees. By the **addition principle**, there are $30 + 4 = 34$ committees. Option A counts only the first case and option C only the second. Option B, $\dbinom{9}{3}$, counts all committees. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
