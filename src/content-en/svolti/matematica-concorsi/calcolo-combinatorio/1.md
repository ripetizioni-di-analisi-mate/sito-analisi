## Exercise 1

A café offers $4$ kinds of sandwiches, $3$ drinks, and $2$ desserts. A meal consists of a sandwich, a drink, and a dessert. How many different meals can be put together?

- A) $9$
- B) $24$
- C) $12$
- D) $18$
- E) $36$

$$
4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choosing a meal involves three successive choices, one for the sandwich, one for the drink, and one for the dessert. By the **multiplication principle**, multiply the numbers of choices at the three stages to get $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 24$ meals. Option A, $9$, is the sum $4 + 3 + 2$, which counts the ways to choose just one item from all the products. Option C, $12$, leaves out the dessert. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a professional development course, an employee must choose just one module from $3$ in computer science, $4$ in English, and $7$ in administrative law. How many choices are there?

- A) $7$
- B) $12$
- C) $84$
- D) $14$
- E) $28$

$$
3 + 4 + 7 = 14
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The employee makes *a single* choice, and the modules fall into three nonoverlapping groups. By the **addition principle**, there are $3 + 4 + 7 = 14$ choices. Option C, $84 = 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 7$, counts the ways to choose one module in each subject, which would involve three successive choices. The question asks for just one. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A company cafeteria offers $3$ first courses, including one fish dish, and $4$ main courses, including two fish dishes. A meal consists of a first course and a main course. Anyone who chooses the fish first course must also choose a fish main course. How many different meals are possible?

- A) $10$
- B) $12$
- C) $8$
- D) $6$
- E) $14$

$$
\underbrace{2 \cdot 4}_{\text{non-fish first course}} + \underbrace{1 \cdot 2}_{\text{fish first course}} = 8 + 2 = 10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of available main courses depends on the first course, so the multiplication principle cannot be applied to the entire meal in one step. Split the meals into two disjoint cases. Each of the $2$ non-fish first courses can be paired with any of $4$ main courses, giving $2 \cdot 4 = 8$ meals. The fish first course can be paired with one of $2$ fish main courses, giving $1 \cdot 2 = 2$ meals. By the **addition principle**, there are $8 + 2 = 10$ meals, as the **tree diagram** shows. Option B, $12 = 3 \cdot 4$, ignores the restriction on the fish first course. Option C counts only the first case. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
