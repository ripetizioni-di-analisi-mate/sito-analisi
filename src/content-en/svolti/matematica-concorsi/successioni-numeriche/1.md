## Exercise 1

What is the next term in the sequence $5, 12, 19, 26, 33, \dots$?

- A) $39$
- B) $40$
- C) $41$
- D) $47$
- E) $38$

$$
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
5 & & 12 & & 19 & & 26 & & 33 \\
& +7 & & +7 & & +7 & & +7 &
\end{array}
$$
$$
33 + 7 = 40
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **differences** between consecutive terms all equal $7$, so each term is the previous one plus $7$. The term following $33$ is therefore $40$. Choice D, $47$, is the term after $40$ and comes from adding $7$ twice. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the next term in the sequence $1, 3, 7, 13, 21, \dots$?

- A) $29$
- B) $42$
- C) $31$
- D) $33$
- E) $30$

$$
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
1 & & 3 & & 7 & & 13 & & 21 \\
& 2 & & 4 & & 6 & & 8 & \\
& & 2 & & 2 & & 2 & &
\end{array}
$$
$$
8 + 2 = 10,
\qquad
21 + 10 = 31
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-x+1" punti="1,1,pieno;2,3,pieno;3,7,pieno;4,13,pieno;5,21,pieno;6,31,vuoto" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-5 ymax=35 nomi="n,aₙ" titolo="The terms $1, 3, 7, 13, 21$ and the required term $31$ (open circle). The differences $2, 4, 6, 8, 10$ increase by $2$ at each step, so the points do not lie on a straight line"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The differences between consecutive terms are $2, 4, 6, 8$, so they are not constant. The **second differences**, however, all equal $2$, so the next difference is $8 + 2 = 10$ and the next term is $21 + 10 = 31$. Choice A, $29$, repeats the last difference $8$ without increasing it. Choice B, $42$, doubles the last term, but the doubling rule already fails between $1$ and $3$. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An office records the numbers of cases closed in six consecutive months: $20, 23, 29, ?, 50, 65$. The numbers follow a consistent rule. What is the missing number?

- A) $35$
- B) $32$
- C) $41$
- D) $39$
- E) $38$

$$
23 - 20 = 3,
\qquad
29 - 23 = 6,
\qquad
65 - 50 = 15
$$
$$
29 + 9 = 38,
\qquad
38 + 12 = 50
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The known differences are $3$ and $6$ at the beginning and $15$ at the end, all multiples of $3$. If the differences are the consecutive multiples $3, 6, 9, 12, 15$, the missing number is $29 + 9 = 38$. Checking all the terms confirms the rule because $38 + 12 = 50$ and $50 + 15 = 65$. Choice A, $35$, repeats the difference $6$, which would give $41$ as the next term instead of $50$. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
