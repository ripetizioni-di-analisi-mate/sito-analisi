## Exercise 1

A candidate scored $6$, $7$, $5$, and $8$ on the first four tests. What score is needed on the fifth test for a mean of $7$ across all five?

- A) $7.5$
- B) $8$
- C) $7$
- D) $8.5$
- E) $9$

::grafico{poligoni="0.7,0 0.7,6 1.3,6 1.3,0;1.7,0 1.7,7 2.3,7 2.3,0;2.7,0 2.7,5 3.3,5 3.3,0;3.7,0 3.7,8 4.3,8 4.3,0" segmenti="0.4,7,5.6,7" etichette="5.2,7.5,target mean 7" xmin=0 xmax=6.6 ymin=0 ymax=10 nomi="p,v" legenda=0 titolo="Scores on the five tests. The first four are $6$, $7$, $5$, and $8$, and the target mean across all five is $7$"}


$$
5 \cdot 7 = 35,
\qquad
6 + 7 + 5 + 8 = 26
$$
$$
35 - 26 = 9
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the relationship between the **sum of the values and the mean**, five scores with mean $7$ must add up to $35$. The four existing scores add up to $26$, so the fifth score must be $35 - 26 = 9$. As a check, $\dfrac{6 + 7 + 5 + 8 + 9}{5} = 7$. Choice A takes the mean of the first tests, $6.5$, and finds the score that averages with $6.5$ to give $7$, as if there were only two tests. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An office has $9$ employees with a mean age of $40$ years. A new employee is hired, and the mean age of the $10$ employees becomes $42$ years. How old is the new employee?

- A) $42$ years
- B) $58$ years
- C) $60$ years
- D) $62$ years
- E) $82$ years

$$
9 \cdot 40 = 360,
\qquad
10 \cdot 42 = 420
$$
$$
420 - 360 = 60
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Work with the sums of the ages. Before the new hire, the sum is $9 \cdot 40 = 360$ years. Afterward, it is $10 \cdot 42 = 420$ years, so the new employee is $420 - 360 = 60$ years old. The result must be greater than $42$, since adding an observation raises the mean only if that observation exceeds the mean. Choice B adds $2$ years for each of the $9$ employees to the old mean $40$. Choice D adds $2$ years for each of the $10$ employees to the new mean $42$. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The mean of $8$ numbers is $15$. Two equal numbers are removed, and the mean of the remaining $6$ numbers is $14$. What is the value of each number removed?

- A) $1$
- B) $36$
- C) $16$
- D) $18$
- E) $17$

$$
8 \cdot 15 = 120,
\qquad
6 \cdot 14 = 84
$$
$$
120 - 84 = 36,
\qquad
\frac{36}{2} = 18
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum of the $8$ numbers is $120$, and the sum of the remaining $6$ is $84$, so the two numbers removed add up to $36$. Since they are equal, each is $18$. The result exceeds $15$, consistent with the fact that removing values above the mean lowers the mean. Choice B is the sum of the two numbers, and choice A is the difference between the two means. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
