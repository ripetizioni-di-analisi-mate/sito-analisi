## Exercise 1

A fair coin is flipped and a fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability of getting tails on the coin and a number less than $3$ on the die?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- B) $\dfrac{5}{6}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{12}$
- D) $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- E) $\dfrac{2}{3}$

$$
P(C) = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
P(\text{die} < 3) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
P = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coin does not affect the die, so the two events are independent and the **multiplication rule** applies. The numbers less than $3$ are $1$ and $2$. The second event therefore has probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$, giving a product of $\dfrac{1}{6}$. As a check, among the $12$ coin-and-die outcomes, the favorable pairs are $(C, 1)$ and $(C, 2)$, where C denotes tails. This gives $2$ out of $12$. Option B adds the two probabilities. Option C counts just one favorable number on the die. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A fair six-sided die is rolled $3$ times. What is the probability of rolling a number greater than $2$ on all three rolls?

- A) $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- B) $\dfrac{4}{9}$
- C) $\dfrac{19}{27}$
- D) $\dfrac{8}{27}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{27}$

$$
P(\text{number} > 2) = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}
$$
$$
P = \left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{3} = \frac{8}{27}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On any one roll, the numbers greater than $2$ are $3$, $4$, $5$, and $6$, so the probability is $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. The three rolls are independent. By the **multiplication rule**, the probability that the event occurs on all three rolls is $\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{3} = \dfrac{8}{27}$. Option A gives the probability for one roll, and option B covers only two rolls. Option C gives the probability of the complement, namely rolling $1$ or $2$ at least once. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An employee takes two buses on different routes to work. The first is on time with probability $0.6$ and the second with probability $0.5$, independently of the first. What is the probability that exactly one bus is on time?

- A) $0.3$
- B) $0.8$
- C) $0.2$
- D) $0.7$
- E) $0.5$

$$
P(\text{only the first}) = 0.6 \cdot 0.5 = 0.3
$$
$$
P(\text{only the second}) = 0.4 \cdot 0.5 = 0.2
$$
$$
P(\text{exactly one}) = 0.3 + 0.2 = 0.5
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 10,0 10,10 0,10 0,0;6,0 6,10;0,5 10,5" poligoni="0,5 6,5 6,10 0,10;6,0 10,0 10,5 6,5" etichette="3,-0.8,first on time;8,-0.8,first late;-2.2,2.5,on time;-2.2,7.5,late;3,7.5,0.3;8,2.5,0.2" xmin=-4.5 xmax=11 ymin=-2 ymax=11 titolo="The square represents all outcomes. Its base is split into $0.6$ and $0.4$ for the first bus, and its height into $0.5$ and $0.5$ for the second. The two shaded areas, $0.6 \cdot 0.5 = 0.3$ and $0.4 \cdot 0.5 = 0.2$, show the outcomes in which exactly one bus is on time."}

<div class="spiegazione">

There are two ways for exactly one bus to be on time. The first can be on time and the second late, or the first can be late and the second on time. The delays are independent, so the **multiplication rule** gives the probability of each case as a product. The first bus is late with probability $1 - 0.6 = 0.4$ and the second with probability $1 - 0.5 = 0.5$. The two cases are mutually exclusive, so add their probabilities, $0.3$ and $0.2$, to get $0.5$. Option A counts only the first case. Option B gives the probability that *at least* one bus is on time, which is $1 - 0.4 \cdot 0.5 = 0.8$. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
