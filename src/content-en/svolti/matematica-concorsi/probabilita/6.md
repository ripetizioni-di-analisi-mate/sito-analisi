## Exercise 1

An urn contains $5$ white balls and $3$ black balls. One ball is drawn, returned to the urn, and then a second ball is drawn. What is the probability that both are black?

- A) $\dfrac{3}{28}$
- B) $\dfrac{9}{64}$
- C) $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- D) $\dfrac{3}{8}$
- E) $\dfrac{15}{64}$

$$
P(\text{two black}) = \frac{3}{8} \cdot \frac{3}{8} = \frac{9}{64}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first ball is returned to the urn, so on the second draw $3$ of the $8$ balls are still black. The two draws are independent. By the multiplication rule for **drawing with replacement**, the probability is $\left( \dfrac{3}{8} \right)^{2} = \dfrac{9}{64}$. Option A gives the probability *without* replacement, $\dfrac{3}{8} \cdot \dfrac{2}{7}$. Option C adds the two probabilities, and option E gives the probability of drawing a white ball followed by a black one. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two balls are drawn successively from the same urn, which holds $5$ white balls and $3$ black balls. The first ball is not returned. What is the probability that the two balls are different colors?

- A) $\dfrac{15}{56}$
- B) $\dfrac{15}{64}$
- C) $\dfrac{15}{28}$
- D) $\dfrac{15}{32}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{2}$

$$
P(BN) = \frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{3}{7} = \frac{15}{56},
\qquad
P(NB) = \frac{3}{8} \cdot \frac{5}{7} = \frac{15}{56}
$$
$$
P(\text{different colors}) = \frac{15}{56} + \frac{15}{56} = \frac{30}{56} = \frac{15}{28}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The balls have different colors if the first is white and the second black, or the first is black and the second white. When **drawing without replacement**, calculate the second probability using the $7$ remaining balls. After a white ball is drawn, $3$ of the remaining $7$ balls are black. After a black ball is drawn, $5$ of the remaining $7$ are white. Each path therefore has probability $\dfrac{15}{56}$. The paths are mutually exclusive, so their probabilities add to $\dfrac{15}{28}$. Option A counts just one order and option D gives the result with replacement. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An office drawer contains $12$ USB flash drives, $3$ of which are defective. An employee randomly takes two drives one after the other without returning the first to the drawer. What is the probability that neither drive is defective?

- A) $\dfrac{9}{16}$
- B) $\dfrac{1}{22}$
- C) $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- D) $\dfrac{5}{11}$
- E) $\dfrac{6}{11}$

$$
P(\text{none defective}) = \frac{9}{12} \cdot \frac{8}{11} = \frac{72}{132} = \frac{6}{11}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are $12 - 3 = 9$ working drives. The first works with probability $\dfrac{9}{12}$. If it works, $8$ of the $11$ drives left in the drawer work, so the second works with probability $\dfrac{8}{11}$. By the **general multiplication rule**, the probability that both work is $\dfrac{9}{12} \cdot \dfrac{8}{11} = \dfrac{6}{11}$. Option A gives the result with replacement, $\left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{2}$. Option B gives the probability that both are defective. Option D gives the probability of the complement, that at least one drive is defective. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
