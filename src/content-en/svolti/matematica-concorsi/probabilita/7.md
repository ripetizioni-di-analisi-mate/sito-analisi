## Exercise 1

A fair six-sided die is rolled and the result is known to be odd. What is the probability that the number is less than $4$?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- B) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- D) $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- E) $\dfrac{3}{4}$

$$
\text{remaining possible outcomes} = \{1, 3, 5\},
\qquad
\text{favorable outcomes} = \{1, 3\}
$$
$$
P(\text{less than } 4 \mid \text{odd}) = \frac{2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number is known to be odd, so to calculate the **conditional probability**, restrict the possible outcomes to $1$, $3$, and $5$. Of these, the numbers less than $4$ are $1$ and $3$, so the probability is $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Option A gives the probability that the number is odd *and* less than $4$, using all $6$ faces. Option B gives the probability that the number is less than $4$ without any additional information. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two fair six-sided dice are rolled and their sum is known to be $8$. What is the probability that at least one die shows $6$?

- A) $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- B) $\dfrac{1}{18}$
- C) $\dfrac{11}{36}$
- D) $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{5}$

$$
\text{sum } 8:\ (2, 6),\ (3, 5),\ (4, 4),\ (5, 3),\ (6, 2)
$$
$$
P(\text{at least one } 6 \mid \text{sum } 8) = \frac{2}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The condition “the sum is $8$” restricts the possible outcomes to the $5$ ordered pairs with sum $8$. They are equally likely because all $36$ pairs are equally likely. Among them, the pairs containing at least one $6$ are $(2, 6)$ and $(6, 2)$, so the **conditional probability** is $\dfrac{2}{5}$. Option B gives the probability that the sum is $8$ *and* a $6$ appears, using all $36$ pairs. Option C gives the probability of at least one $6$ without the condition. Option E counts only the pair with $6$ on the second die. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An agency holds a competitive exam for $120$ candidates. Of these, $64$ pass the written exam, and $48$ of those candidates also pass the oral exam. A candidate is chosen at random from those who passed the written exam. What is the probability that the candidate also passed the oral exam?

- A) $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- B) $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- C) $\dfrac{8}{15}$
- D) $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{4}$

$$
P(\text{oral exam} \mid \text{written exam}) = \frac{48}{64} = \frac{3}{4}
$$

::venn{tipo="incluso" zone="ab" nomi="wr.,oral" universo=1 elementi="a:16;ab:48;u:56" titolo="The outer circle (wr.) represents those who passed the written exam and the inner circle those who passed the oral exam. Of the $120$ candidates, $48$ passed both exams, $16$ passed only the written exam, and $56$ did not pass the written exam. The $16 + 48 = 64$ who passed the written exam are the possible outcomes for the conditional probability."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The candidate is chosen from the $64$ who passed the written exam, so there are $64$ possible outcomes, not $120$. The $48$ who also passed the oral exam are the favorable outcomes, giving a **conditional probability** of $\dfrac{48}{64} = \dfrac{3}{4}$. Option A divides by all $120$ candidates, giving the probability that a candidate chosen from the full group passed both exams. Option C gives the probability that a candidate from the full group passed the written exam. Option E gives the probability of *not* passing the oral exam among those who passed the written exam. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
