## Exercise 1

An urn contains $4$ red balls, $6$ white balls, and $5$ yellow balls that feel identical. One ball is drawn at random. What is the probability that it is white?

- A) $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- B) $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- D) $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{15}$

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = 4 + 6 + 5 = 15
$$
$$
P(\text{white}) = \frac{6}{15} = \frac{2}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The balls feel identical, so each of the $15$ balls is equally likely to be drawn. The $6$ white balls are the favorable outcomes, giving a probability of $\dfrac{6}{15} = \dfrac{2}{5}$. Option B divides the number of white balls by the number of balls that are *not* white, giving the ratio $6 : 9$ instead of the probability. Option C treats the three colors as equally likely, although there are different numbers of balls of each color. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One card is drawn from a well-shuffled Italian deck of $40$ cards. What is the probability that it is from the suit of swords and is not a face card?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- B) $\dfrac{3}{40}$
- C) $\dfrac{7}{10}$
- D) $\dfrac{3}{10}$
- E) $\dfrac{7}{40}$

$$
\text{swords} = 10,
\qquad
\text{face cards in the suit of swords} = 3,
\qquad
10 - 3 = 7
$$
$$
P = \frac{7}{40}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An Italian deck has $10$ cards per suit. The face cards in each suit are the jack, knight, and king. There are therefore $10 - 3 = 7$ cards in the suit of swords that are not face cards, from the ace through the $7$. The deck has $40$ cards, so the probability is $\dfrac{7}{40}$. Option A gives the probability of drawing any card from the suit of swords. Option C gives the probability of drawing any card that is not a face card, regardless of suit. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An office has $80$ case files numbered from $1$ to $80$. The manager chooses one at random for review. What is the probability that its number is a multiple of $6$?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- B) $\dfrac{13}{80}$
- C) $\dfrac{3}{20}$
- D) $\dfrac{7}{40}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{80}$

$$
\frac{80}{6} = 13.33\ldots
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{multiples of } 6:\ 6,\ 12,\ \ldots,\ 78 \quad (13 \text{ numbers})
$$
$$
P = \frac{13}{80}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The manager selects a file at random, so the $80$ files are equally likely and the **classical definition of probability** applies. The multiples of $6$ no greater than $80$ run from $6 \cdot 1$ and $6 \cdot 2$ through $6 \cdot 13 = 78$, since $6 \cdot 14 = 84$ exceeds $80$. There are $13$ favorable outcomes, so the probability is $\dfrac{13}{80}$. Option A would be correct if the number of files were a multiple of $6$, such as $78$ or $84$. With $80$ files, $\dfrac{1}{6}$ is only an approximation. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
