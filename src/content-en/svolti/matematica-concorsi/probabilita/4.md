## Exercise 1

A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling $1$ or an even number?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- B) $\dfrac{1}{12}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- D) $\dfrac{5}{6}$
- E) $\dfrac{2}{3}$

$$
P(1) + P(\text{even}) = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{3}{6} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $1$ is odd, so “roll $1$” and “roll an even number” have no favorable outcomes in common. The events are mutually exclusive. By the **addition rule**, add their probabilities to get $\dfrac{2}{3}$. As a check, the favorable outcomes are $1$, $2$, $4$, and $6$, giving $4$ out of $6$. Option B multiplies the two probabilities, as one would for “$A$ and $B$” if the events were independent. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One card is drawn from a well-shuffled French-suited deck of $52$ cards. What is the probability that it is red or a face card?

- A) $\dfrac{19}{26}$
- B) $\dfrac{8}{13}$
- C) $\dfrac{3}{13}$
- D) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- E) $\dfrac{3}{26}$

$$
P(\text{red}) = \frac{26}{52},
\qquad
P(\text{face card}) = \frac{12}{52},
\qquad
P(\text{red face card}) = \frac{6}{52}
$$
$$
P(\text{red or a face card}) = \frac{26 + 12 - 6}{52} = \frac{32}{52} = \frac{8}{13}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $26$ hearts and diamonds are red. The jacks, queens, and kings of the four suits make $12$ face cards. The two events overlap because the $6$ face cards in hearts and diamonds are both red and face cards. By the **general addition rule**, subtract these $6$ cards, which would otherwise be counted twice, to get $32$ favorable outcomes. The probability is $\dfrac{32}{52} = \dfrac{8}{13}$. Option A adds $26$ and $12$ without subtracting the overlap. Option E gives the probability of a red face card. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An agency employs $60$ people. Of these, $24$ know English, $18$ know French, and $9$ know both languages. One employee is chosen at random. What is the probability that this employee knows at least one of the two languages?

- A) $\dfrac{7}{10}$
- B) $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- C) $\dfrac{11}{20}$
- D) $\dfrac{9}{20}$
- E) $\dfrac{3}{20}$

$$
24 + 18 - 9 = 33
$$
$$
P = \frac{33}{60} = \frac{11}{20}
$$

::venn{zone="a,ab,b" nomi="English,French" universo=1 elementi="a:15;ab:9;b:9;u:27" titolo="Of the $60$ employees, $15$ know only English, $9$ know both languages, $9$ know only French, and $27$ know neither."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The $9$ employees who know both languages are included among the $24$ who know English and among the $18$ who know French. By the **general addition rule**, subtract them once. Thus $24 + 18 - 9 = 33$ employees know at least one language. The diagram confirms the count since $15 + 9 + 9 = 33$. The probability is $\dfrac{33}{60} = \dfrac{11}{20}$. Option A counts the $9$ employees in the overlap twice. Option D gives the probability that the employee does *not* know either language. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>
