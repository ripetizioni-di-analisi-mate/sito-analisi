## Exercise 1

A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a number greater than $4$?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- B) $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- D) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{4}$

$$
\text{favorable outcomes} = \{5, 6\},
\qquad
P = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The die is fair, so its six faces are equally likely and the **classical definition of probability** applies. The only numbers greater than $4$ are $5$ and $6$, since $4$ is not greater than itself. There are $2$ favorable outcomes out of $6$, so the probability is $\dfrac{1}{3}$. Option A includes $4$, answering the question “greater than or equal to $4$.” The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. What is the probability that the two numbers add up to $8$?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{7}$
- B) $\dfrac{5}{36}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{11}$
- D) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{9}$

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = 6 \cdot 6 = 36
$$
$$
\text{favorable outcomes}:\ (2, 6),\ (3, 5),\ (4, 4),\ (5, 3),\ (6, 2)
$$
$$
P = \frac{5}{36}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $36$ ordered pairs are equally likely. The first number is the result on the first die and the second is the result on the second die. Five pairs have a sum of $8$, since $(2, 6)$ and $(6, 2)$ are distinct outcomes, while $(4, 4)$ counts only once. The probability is therefore $\dfrac{5}{36}$. Option C treats the $11$ sums from $2$ to $12$ as equally likely. Option A counts unordered pairs, giving $3$ favorable outcomes out of $21$. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Each week, an office manager flips a fair coin to assign the Saturday shift. Luca works if the coin lands heads and Marta works if it lands tails. What is the probability that Luca works exactly two Saturday shifts in $4$ weeks?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- B) $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- D) $\dfrac{3}{8}$
- E) $\dfrac{1}{16}$

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = 2^4 = 16
$$
$$
TTCC,\quad TCTC,\quad TCCT,\quad CTTC,\quad CTCT,\quad CCTT
$$
$$
P = \frac{6}{16} = \frac{3}{8}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Luca works exactly two shifts when exactly two of the $4$ flips are heads. Each outcome is an ordered sequence of four flips. Each flip has $2$ possible outcomes, so there are $2^4 = 16$ equally likely sequences. Exactly $6$ of them contain two heads. Choose the $2$ positions for the heads from the $4$ positions available, which gives $C_{4,2} = 6$, as shown in the chapter on combinatorics. The probability is therefore $\dfrac{6}{16} = \dfrac{3}{8}$. Option A assumes that two heads in four flips has probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$ because two is “half” of four. Option C treats the $5$ possible numbers of heads, from $0$ to $4$, as equally likely. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
