## Exercise 1

An urn holds $5$ red balls and $4$ green balls. Two balls are drawn at the same time. What is the probability that both are green?

- A) $\dfrac{16}{81}$
- B) $\dfrac{4}{9}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- D) $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- E) $\dfrac{5}{18}$

$$
C_{9,2} = \binom{9}{2} = \frac{9 \cdot 8}{2} = 36,
\qquad
C_{4,2} = \binom{4}{2} = \frac{4 \cdot 3}{2} = 6
$$
$$
P(\text{two green}) = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The balls are drawn together, so order does not matter. Count the outcomes using combinations, as in the chapter on combinatorics. There are $C_{9,2} = 36$ possible pairs, and $C_{4,2} = 6$ consist of two green balls. The probability is therefore $\dfrac{1}{6}$. The multiplication rule gives the same result, $\dfrac{4}{9} \cdot \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{1}{6}$. Option A gives the result with replacement. Option E gives the probability that both balls are red. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An office employs $10$ people, $6$ men and $4$ women. A committee of $3$ is chosen at random. What is the probability that it includes exactly $2$ women?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{30}$
- B) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- C) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- D) $\dfrac{5}{6}$
- E) $\dfrac{3}{10}$

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = C_{10,3} = \frac{10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 120
$$
$$
\text{favorable outcomes} = C_{4,2} \cdot C_{6,1} = 6 \cdot 6 = 36
$$
$$
P = \frac{36}{120} = \frac{3}{10}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Order does not matter on a committee, so the number of possible committees is given by the combination $C_{10,3} = 120$. To form a committee with exactly $2$ women, choose $2$ of the $4$ women in $C_{4,2} = 6$ ways and $1$ man from the $6$ men in $6$ ways. By the multiplication principle, there are $6 \cdot 6 = 36$ favorable outcomes, so the probability is $\dfrac{36}{120} = \dfrac{3}{10}$. Option C gives the probability of exactly $1$ woman, since $C_{4,1} \cdot C_{6,2} = 4 \cdot 15 = 60$ and $\dfrac{60}{120} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Option D gives the probability of at least one woman. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An archive access code has $4$ digits chosen from the $10$ digits $0$ through $9$, and digits may repeat. If a code is chosen at random, what is the probability that all $4$ digits are different?

- A) $0.729$
- B) $0.0504$
- C) $0.496$
- D) $0.504$
- E) $0.021$

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = 10^4 = 10\,000
$$
$$
\text{favorable outcomes} = D_{10,4} = 10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 5040
$$
$$
P = \frac{5040}{10\,000} = 0.504
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The order of the digits matters and digits can repeat, so the total number of codes is the number of ordered selections with repetition, $10^4 = 10\,000$. The codes with distinct digits are the ordered selections without repetition, $D_{10,4}$. There are $10$ choices for the first digit, $9$ for the second, $8$ for the third, and $7$ for the fourth. The probability is therefore $\dfrac{5040}{10\,000} = 0.504$, or about $50\%$. Option B is off by a factor of $10$ in the division. Option C gives the probability of the complement. Option E uses combinations, $C_{10,4} = 210$, which disregard order. Option A, $0.9^3$, gives the probability that none of the last three digits matches the first. That condition alone does not make all four digits different. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
