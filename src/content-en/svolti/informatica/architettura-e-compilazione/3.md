## Exercise 1

In the instructional accumulator model, `PC` starts at $0$. Initially, $M[12]=9$ and $M[13]=-4$. Trace the accumulator after each instruction and give the final contents of $M[14]$.

```text
0  LOAD 12
1  ADD 13
2  STORE 14
3  HALT
```

| Current address | `A` after the step | `PC` after the step |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 9 | 1 |
| 1 | 5 | 2 |
| 2 | 5 | 3 |
| 3 | 5 | 3 |

The final contents are $M[14]=5$.

<div class="spiegazione">

`LOAD 12` replaces the contents of `A` with the data in cell $12$. Then `ADD 13` adds $-4$, taking the accumulator from $9$ to $5$. `STORE 14` transfers the data to cell $14$ and preserves the value of `A`. The halt leaves that final state in place.

As a check, start at the only write to $M[14]$ and trace back to the instructions that produce the value written. That value depends on $M[12]+M[13]$. Since no instruction modifies those cells, the sum is $9+(-4)=5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Run the following program from an initial `PC` of $0$. Initially, $M[21]=3$ and $M[23]=7$. Determine the final $M[23]$ in both cases $M[20]=0$ and $M[20]=-2$.

```text
0  LOAD 20
1  JZ 4
2  ADD 21
3  STORE 23
4  HALT
```

| $M[20]$ | Addresses executed | Final $M[23]$ |
|---:|---|---:|
| $0$ | $0,1,4$ | $7$ |
| $-2$ | $0,1,2,3,4$ | $1$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

In each case, the machine loads $M[20]$ before testing the condition. The zero value takes the branch, so execution goes directly to `HALT` and cell $23$ stays at $7$. The value $-2$ makes the condition false. The counter advances to the addition, which brings `A` to $-2+3=1$. The store writes that value.

To check both answers, look for `STORE 23` in the address sequences. It appears only in the second sequence. The value written comes from cell $20$ and the only addition, so the two results agree with the table.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the instructional accumulator model, `PC` starts at $0$. Initially, $M[20]=3$, $M[21]=-1$, $M[22]=5$, $M[23]=0$ and $M[24]=0$. Determine the final contents of $M[20]$ and $M[23]$ and the number of instructions executed, including `HALT`. Explain the role of the instructions at addresses $7$ and $8$ and generalize the count to the case $M[20]=n$, with $n$ an integer and $n\geq0$.
```text
0  LOAD 20
1  JZ 9
2  ADD 21
3  STORE 20
4  LOAD 23
5  ADD 22
6  STORE 23
7  LOAD 24
8  JZ 0
9  HALT
```

| Pass through address $1$ | `A` | Effect of `JZ 9` | $M[23]$ after the round |
|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | 3 | continues to $2$ | 5 |
| 2 | 2 | continues to $2$ | 10 |
| 3 | 1 | continues to $2$ | 15 |
| 4 | 0 | jumps to $9$ | 15 |

At the end, $M[20]=0$ and $M[23]=15$. The number of instructions executed is $3\cdot9+3=30$. With $M[20]=n$, the machine executes $9n+3$ instructions and $M[23]=5n$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each pass through address $0$ loads the counter $M[20]$ and tests it against zero. If the counter is nonzero, instructions $2$ and $3$ decrease it by one and instructions $4$ to $6$ add $M[22]=5$ to the sum in $M[23]$. Instruction $7$ loads cell $24$, which contains zero and is never written. Therefore `JZ 0` always finds the accumulator equal to zero and sets `PC` back to $0$. The pair at addresses $7$ and $8$ thus implements an **unconditional jump**, which the model does not have among its instructions.

A complete round executes the nine instructions from $0$ to $8$. The counter starts at $3$ and decreases by one each round, so there are three rounds and on the fourth load `A` is zero. In that last pass only `LOAD 20`, `JZ 9` and `HALT` are executed. With $M[20]=n$ there are $n$ rounds, giving $9n+3$ instructions and $n$ additions of $5$. For $n=0$ the machine executes three instructions and $M[23]$ stays zero.

The hypothesis $n\geq0$ guarantees termination. With a negative initial value, the counter decreases by one each round and never reaches zero, so the machine with mathematical integers repeats the loop forever.

</div>
