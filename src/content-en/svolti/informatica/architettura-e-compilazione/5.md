## Exercise 1

A sequence of $12$ accesses has $10$ measured hits. A hit takes $3\,\mathrm{ns}$. A miss takes $27\,\mathrm{ns}$ in total. Compute the mean access time.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{total time} &= 10\cdot3+2\cdot27=84\,\mathrm{ns}\\[6pt]
\bar{t} &= \frac{84}{12}\,\mathrm{ns}=7\,\mathrm{ns}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are $12-10=2$ misses. Since $27\,\mathrm{ns}$ is the entire time for a miss, the sum uses $27$ for each of those two accesses. Divide the total time by the number of accesses so that each access has the same weight in the mean.

To check, express the miss time as $3+24\,\mathrm{ns}$, with $3\,\mathrm{ns}$ for checking the cache and another $24\,\mathrm{ns}$ to finish. Every access pays $3\,\mathrm{ns}$ and the miss fraction is $\dfrac{2}{12}$. The penalty formula gives $3+\dfrac{2}{12}\cdot24=7\,\mathrm{ns}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A two-level model uses $2\,\mathrm{ns}$ to check the cache. Each miss takes an extra $36\,\mathrm{ns}$. A sample of $80$ accesses has an exact mean time of $11\,\mathrm{ns}$. Find the number of hits in the sample.

$$
\begin{aligned}
11 &= 2+(1-h)36\\[6pt]
1-h &= \frac{9}{36}=\frac{1}{4}\\[6pt]
80h &= 80\cdot\frac{3}{4}=60 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first $2\,\mathrm{ns}$ apply to every access. Subtracting them from the mean leaves $9\,\mathrm{ns}$, the average penalty spread across all accesses. Since a single miss incurs $36\,\mathrm{ns}$ of extra time, the miss fraction is $\dfrac{9}{36}$. The remaining accesses are hits.

To check, the $60$ hits take $120\,\mathrm{ns}$ in total and the $20$ misses take $20\cdot38=760\,\mathrm{ns}$. Dividing the total of $880\,\mathrm{ns}$ by $80$ gives the stated mean.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In configuration A a hit takes $1\,\mathrm{ns}$, a miss takes $41\,\mathrm{ns}$ in total and the hit fraction is $0.9$. In configuration B checking the cache takes $2\,\mathrm{ns}$ and a miss takes $42\,\mathrm{ns}$ in total. Find the minimum hit fraction for which B has a mean time no greater than that of A. For a sample of $400$ accesses, find the minimum number of hits that meets the same condition.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar{t}_A &= 0.9\cdot1+0.1\cdot41=0.9+4.1=5\,\mathrm{ns}\\[6pt]
P_B &= 42-2=40\,\mathrm{ns}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2+(1-h)\,40 &\leq 5\\[6pt]
1-h &\leq \frac{3}{40}\\[6pt]
h &\geq \frac{37}{40}=0.925\\[6pt]
400h &\geq 370 .
\end{aligned}
$$

B needs a hit fraction of at least $0.925$, that is, at least $370$ hits out of $400$ accesses.

<div class="spiegazione">

For A the problem gives the total time of a miss, so the mean weights $1\,\mathrm{ns}$ on the fraction $0.9$ of the accesses and $41\,\mathrm{ns}$ on the fraction $0.1$. For B the form $t_h+(1-h)P$ is used, which requires the *extra* time of a miss. Since every access in B spends $2\,\mathrm{ns}$ checking the cache, the penalty is $42-2=40\,\mathrm{ns}$.

The inequality between the means is solved by subtracting $2$ and dividing by $40$. The divisor is positive, so the direction of the inequality is preserved. The condition $h\geq\dfrac{37}{40}$ concerns a fraction. In the sample the number of hits is an integer $k$ with $\dfrac{k}{400}\geq\dfrac{37}{40}$, hence $k\geq370$.

As a check, with $370$ hits the total time is $370\cdot2+30\cdot42=2000\,\mathrm{ns}$ and the mean is $5\,\mathrm{ns}$, equal to that of A. With $369$ hits the total rises to $738+31\cdot42=2040\,\mathrm{ns}$, so the mean of $5.1\,\mathrm{ns}$ exceeds that of A. The value $370$ is therefore the minimum.

</div>
