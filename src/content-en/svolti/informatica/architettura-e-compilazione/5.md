## Exercise 1

A sequence of $12$ accesses has $10$ measured hits. A hit takes $3\,\mathrm{ns}$. A miss takes $27\,\mathrm{ns}$ in total. Compute the mean access time.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{total time} &= 10\cdot3+2\cdot27=84\,\mathrm{ns}\\[6pt]
\bar{t} &= \frac{84}{12}\,\mathrm{ns}=7\,\mathrm{ns}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are $12-10=2$ misses. Since $27\,\mathrm{ns}$ is the entire time for a miss, the sum uses $27$ for each of those two accesses. Divide the total time by the number of accesses so that each access has the same weight in the mean.

To check, express the miss time as $3+24\,\mathrm{ns}$, with $3\,\mathrm{ns}$ for checking the cache and another $24\,\mathrm{ns}$ to finish. Every access pays $3\,\mathrm{ns}$ and the miss fraction is $\dfrac{2}{12}$. The penalty formula gives $3+\dfrac{2}{12}\cdot24=7\,\mathrm{ns}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A two-level model uses $2\,\mathrm{ns}$ to check the cache. Each miss takes an extra $36\,\mathrm{ns}$. A sample of $80$ accesses has an exact mean time of $11\,\mathrm{ns}$. Find the number of hits in the sample.

$$
\begin{aligned}
11 &= 2+(1-h)36\\[6pt]
1-h &= \frac{9}{36}=\frac{1}{4}\\[6pt]
80h &= 80\cdot\frac{3}{4}=60 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first $2\,\mathrm{ns}$ apply to every access. Subtracting them from the mean leaves $9\,\mathrm{ns}$, the average penalty spread across all accesses. Since a single miss incurs $36\,\mathrm{ns}$ of extra time, the miss fraction is $\dfrac{9}{36}$. The remaining accesses are hits.

To check, the $60$ hits take $120\,\mathrm{ns}$ in total and the $20$ misses take $20\cdot38=760\,\mathrm{ns}$. Dividing the total of $880\,\mathrm{ns}$ by $80$ gives the stated mean.

</div>
