## Exercise 1

Compute the C17 quotient and remainder for the `int` dividend and divisor shown in the figure.

![The dividend is minus 29 and the divisor is 6.](/immagini/informatica/tipi-ed-espressioni/dati-svolto-en.png)

The exact ratio lies between $-5$ and $-4$. Truncating towards zero gives the quotient, and the remainder follows from the identity.
$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= -4,\\[6pt]
r &= -29-6\cdot(-4)=-5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The magnitude $29$ lies between four and five times the divisor $6$. The ratio has negative sign because only the dividend is negative. The first integer reached by moving from the ratio towards zero is $-4$, so this is the quotient. The quotient uses the multiple $-24$ of the divisor. The remainder measures the contribution needed to reach $-29$, so it is $-5$. The operands and the quotient lie in the range of `int` on every C17 implementation.

To check the sign and magnitude of the remainder, start from the absolute value of the dividend. We have $29=24+5$ with $0\leq 5<6$. Restoring the sign of the dividend gives contributions of $-24$ and $-5$. Their sum recovers the input $-29$, so this remainder satisfies the required bounds.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the values of `q` and `r`.

```c
int a = 47;
int b = -9;
int q = a / b;
int r = a % b;
```

Since $45\leq 47<54$, truncating the negative ratio gives $q=-5$.
$$
r=47-(-9)\cdot(-5)=47-45=2 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The dividend is positive and the divisor is negative, so the ratio is negative. Five multiples of the absolute value of the divisor give $45$. Six would give $54$, beyond the dividend. The ratio thus belongs to the open interval between $-6$ and $-5$, and truncation towards zero produces $-5$. The product of divisor and quotient is positive and equals $45$. Two units of the dividend remain, so the remainder is positive.

To verify the result, start with the pair $q=-5$, $r=2$. The identity gives $(-9)\cdot(-5)+2=47$. The condition $|2|<9$ holds and the sign of the remainder matches the sign of the dividend. Using $q=-6$ would give a remainder of $-7$, opposite in sign to the dividend, so truncation towards zero rules out that pair.

</div>
