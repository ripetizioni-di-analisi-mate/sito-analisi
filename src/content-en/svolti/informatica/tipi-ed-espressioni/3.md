## Exercise 1

Compute `value` and identify which of the two divisions is performed in type `int`. Assume a C17 Annex F implementation in round-to-nearest mode.

```c
double value = (double)(17 / 8) + (double)17 / 8;
```

$$
\begin{aligned}
17 &=8\cdot 2+1,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{value} &=2+\left(2+\dfrac{1}{8}\right)=4.125.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the left addend, the operand of the cast is the result of `17 / 8`. Both constants have type `int`, so division gives the truncated quotient $2$. The cast converts $2$ to an exact `double`. In the other addend, the operand of the cast is `17` and the right operand of division is `8`. Since the left operand is already `double`, the arithmetic conversions convert the other operand to `double` as well. The ratio retains the fractional contribution of one eighth. The two addends now share a type and their sum is exactly representable.

As a check, subtract the first addend $2$ from the intermediate total. This gives $2.125$. Multiplying it by $8$ gives $17$, so the second division preserved the whole dividend. In the first addend, $2\cdot8=16$ and the residual $1$ is less than $8$, as required by truncation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the value of `units`. Assume a C17 Annex F implementation in round-to-nearest mode.

```c
double difference = -19.75 + 7.5;
int units = (int)difference;
```

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{difference} &= -19.75 + 7.5=-12.25,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{units} &=-12 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two real constants have type `double` and exactly represent fractions with denominators that are powers of two. Addition produces $-12.25$, between $-13$ and $-12$. The cast to `int` truncates towards zero. In this interval, moving towards zero retains $-12$ and discards the fractional part of a unit. Since $-12$ lies in the range of `int`, the conversion is defined.

To check the intermediate value, express both addends in quarters. The numerators are $-79$ and $30$, so the sum is $\dfrac{-49}{4}$. Dividing $49$ by $4$ gives quotient $12$ and remainder $1$. The starting value is negative, so truncation gives $-12$. This check starts from integers and confirms the floating-point calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The fragment computes the mean of four integer measurements with sum $-11$ and two candidates for the nearest integer. Find `mean`, `truncated`, `wrong` and `nearest` and determine which candidate is correct. Assume a C17 Annex F implementation in round-to-nearest mode.
```c
int total = -11;
int count = 4;
double mean = (double)total / count;
int truncated = total / count;
int wrong = (int)(mean + 0.5);
int nearest = (int)(mean - 0.5);
```

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{mean} &= -\frac{11}{4}=-2.75,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{truncated} &= -2,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{wrong} &= \operatorname{trunc}(-2.25)=-2,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{nearest} &= \operatorname{trunc}(-3.25)=-3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the third line the cast converts `total` before the division, and the arithmetic conversions also convert `count` to `double`. The ratio has denominator $4$, a power of two, so `mean` is exactly $-2.75$. On the fourth line both operands are `int` and the division truncates towards zero, so `truncated` is $-2$.

The integer nearest to $-2.75$ is $-3$, because its distance from $-3$ is $0.25$ and its distance from $-2$ is $0.75$. The sum `mean + 0.5` is $-2.25$ and the conversion to `int` truncates it towards zero, so `wrong` is $-2$. For a negative value truncation towards zero rounds up, so $0.5$ is subtracted before the conversion. The difference is $-3.25$ and the conversion gives $-3$, so `nearest` is the **correct** candidate.

The formula for `nearest` gives the nearest integer for every `mean` less than or equal to zero, and it takes a value halfway between two integers, such as $-2.5$, to the integer farther from zero. For a positive value truncation coincides with rounding down, so the nearest integer is given by the formula for `wrong`. For $2.75$, in fact, `(int)(mean - 0.5)` is $2$. A mean of arbitrary sign therefore requires a choice based on the sign, as in `mean < 0 ? (int)(mean - 0.5) : (int)(mean + 0.5)`. Both formulas assume a sum with $0.5$ that is exact in `double`, as for the multiples of a quarter considered here, and a result representable in `int`.

</div>
