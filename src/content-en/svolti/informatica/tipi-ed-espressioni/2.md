## Exercise 1

Find the initial value of `total` and state how the expression is grouped.

```c
int total = 28 - 4 * 5 + 9 % 4;
```

The expression groups as `(28 - (4 * 5)) + (9 % 4)`.
$$
\begin{aligned}
9 &= 4\cdot 2+1,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{total} &= (28-20)+1=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplication and remainder have higher precedence than addition and subtraction. They therefore form the intermediate values $20$ and $1$. Precedence determines the grouping of the expression's nodes, and the two subcalculations can be evaluated in any order because they use constants. Subtraction and addition associate to the left, so `28 - 20` first gives $8$, and the unit from the remainder is then added. The entire expression has type `int` because all operands are small integer constants without a suffix.

As a check, subtract the computed remainder from the result $9$. This gives $8$, and adding the product takes us back to $28$. The remainder also passes the independent check $9-1=8=4\cdot 2$ with $1<4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find `total` and the final value of `n`.

```c
int n = 8;
int before = n++;
int after = ++n;
int total = before + 10 * after;
```

| After the declaration | `n` | `before` | `after` |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 8 |  |  |
| Second | 9 | 8 |  |
| Third | 10 | 8 | 10 |

The value of `total` is $8+10\cdot 10=108$ and `n` is $10$.

<div class="spiegazione">

On the second line, post-increment supplies the previous value $8$ to the initializer of `before`. At the end of the full expression, the update to `n` is complete, so the third line starts from $9$. Pre-increment increases `n` to $10$ and supplies that value to `after`. The two objects store separate results, and the fourth line only reads the copies.

To check, replace the second line with a declaration that copies `n` into `before` followed by `n = n + 1;`. Replace the third line with the increment of `n` before the copy into `after`. The two rewrites produce the pair $(8,10)$ and add two units to the initial value $8$. This again gives `total` equal to $108$.

</div>
