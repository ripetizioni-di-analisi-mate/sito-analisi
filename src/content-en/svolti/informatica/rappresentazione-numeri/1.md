## Exercise 1

Find the decimal value of the binary word $1100101$ shown in the diagram.

![Seven positions with bits 1 1 0 0 1 0 1 and weights 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1.](/immagini/informatica/rappresentazione-numeri/schema-conversione-en.png)

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1100101)_2
  &= 2^6+2^5+2^2+2^0 \\[6pt]
  &= 64+32+4+1 \\[6pt]
  &= 101 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagram assigns index $0$ to the rightmost bit. There are seven bits, so the leftmost bit has index $6$ and weight $64$. The bits equal to $1$ have indices $6$, $5$, $2$ and $0$, so the sum includes the four corresponding weights. The other weights are multiplied by zero.

As a check, evaluate the word one prefix at a time, starting at the left. Appending a digit doubles the previous value and adds the new bit. This gives the sequence
$$
1,\quad 3,\quad 6,\quad 12,\quad 25,\quad 50,\quad 101 .
$$
The final step gives $2\cdot 50+1=101$, so prefix evaluation confirms the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the base-ten value of $(30214)_5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(30214)_5
  &= 3\cdot 5^4+0\cdot 5^3+2\cdot 5^2+1\cdot 5+4 \\[6pt]
  &= 1875+50+5+4 \\[6pt]
  &= 1934 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base is five, so the weights of adjacent positions differ by a factor of $5$. Starting at the right, the weights are $1$, $5$, $25$, $125$ and $625$. The digit $0$ occupies the position with weight $125$, so that position contributes zero. The digit $3$ occupies the position with weight $625$ and counts three units of that weight, so it contributes $1875$.

To verify the calculation, append the digits in order and multiply the value of the prefix by $5$ at each step. The digits $3$ and $0$ give $15$. Appending $2$ gives $15\cdot5+2=77$ and appending $1$ gives $77\cdot5+1=386$. The final step gives $386\cdot5+4=1934$, matching the weighted sum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the base $b$ for which $(132)_b$ has the base-ten value $56$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(132)_b &= b^2+3b+2 = 56 \\[6pt]
b^2+3b-54 &= 0 \\[6pt]
b &= \frac{-3\pm\sqrt{9+216}}{2}=\frac{-3\pm 15}{2} .
\end{aligned}
$$

The required base is $b=6$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The digit $2$ has index $0$ and weight $1$, the digit $3$ has weight $b$ and the digit $1$ has weight $b^2$. The value of the numeral is therefore the polynomial $b^2+3b+2$, and the given condition becomes a quadratic equation in $b$. The discriminant is $225=15^2$, so the roots are $6$ and $-9$. A base is an integer greater than or equal to $2$, so the negative root is discarded. Every digit must also be less than the base. The largest digit is $3$, so the admissible bases are $4$ and above, and $b=6$ satisfies this condition as well.

For $b>0$ the polynomial increases with $b$, so no other base gives the same value. As a check, evaluate $(132)_6$ one prefix at a time. After the digit $1$ the value is $1$, the digit $3$ gives $1\cdot6+3=9$ and the digit $2$ gives $9\cdot6+2=56$.

</div>
