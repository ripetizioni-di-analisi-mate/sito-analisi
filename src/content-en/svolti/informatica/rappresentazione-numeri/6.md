## Exercise 1

An adder keeps the five least significant bits of its result. Add the words $01111$ and $00001$. Interpret the result as unsigned and in two’s complement. For each format, state whether overflow occurs.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(01111)_2+(00001)_2 &= (10000)_2 \\[6pt]
U(10000) &= 2^4=16 \\
S(10000) &= -2^4=-16 .
\end{aligned}
$$

The unsigned result is $16$ without overflow. The two’s complement result is $-16$ with overflow.

<div class="spiegazione">

Adding the bits of weight $1$ generates a carry because $1+1$ equals $2$. The carry propagates through the next three bits of the first operand, all equal to $1$. It then sets the next bit to $1$ at index $4$. The word $10000$ fits in the five available positions.

Check the result by comparing the exact sum $15+1=16$ with the two ranges. The unsigned format includes $16$ because its maximum is $31$. The two’s complement maximum is $15$, so the exact sum exceeds it. The stored word has sign bit $1$, and its signed interpretation is $-16$. Signed overflow occurs here without a carry out of the fifth bit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the sum of $-13$ and $-7$ in six-bit two’s complement. Give the carry out of the sixth position and determine whether signed overflow occurs.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^6-13 &= 51=(110011)_2 \\
2^6-7  &= 57=(111001)_2 \\[6pt]
51+57 &= 108 = 64+44 \\
44 &= (101100)_2 \\[6pt]
44-64 &= -20 .
\end{aligned}
$$

The carry out of the sixth position is $1$. The signed result is $-20$, which is representable.

<div class="spiegazione">

Encode the negative operands by adding $2^6=64$ to each value. The adder treats the words as sequences of bits and keeps the remainder of the sum of their codes on division by $64$. The quotient on division by $64$ is one, so the carry is $1$. The six retained bits hold the code $44$. Its most significant bit is $1$, so decoding it as signed gives $44-64=-20$.

Checking with the original integers gives $-13-7=-20$. Since $-32\leq -20\leq 31$, the exact result is within the range of the format. In this case the carry is $1$ and the signed sum is exact.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The values $-12$ and $-9$ are added in five-bit two’s complement. Compare the eight-bit word obtained by sign-extending the result with the word obtained by sign-extending both operands before addition.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-12 &\longmapsto 10100 \\
-9 &\longmapsto 10111 \\[6pt]
20+23 &= 32+11 \\
11 &\longmapsto 01011\longmapsto 00001011 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
10100 &\longmapsto 11110100 \\
10111 &\longmapsto 11110111 \\[6pt]
244+247 &= 256+235 \\
235 &= (11101011)_2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Sign-extending the result gives $00001011$, with value $11$. Sign-extending the operands first gives $11101011$, with value $-21$.

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first calculation, adding the five-bit words gives remainder $11$ modulo $32$. The most significant bit of $01011$ is zero, so sign extension adds three zeros and preserves the value $11$ already stored in the word. In the second calculation, both operands have sign bit $1$, so sign extension adds three ones to each word and preserves the values $-12$ and $-9$. With eight bits, the remainder of the sum of the codes is $235$, which decodes to $235-256=-21$.

Check both results against $-12-9=-21$. The exact sum is below the five-bit minimum of $-16$, so the first calculation has already lost the information needed to recover the exact value. With eight bits, $-128\leq-21\leq127$, so the second calculation represents the exact sum.

</div>
