## Exercise 1

Read the eight-bit word $10110111$ as an unsigned integer and as a two’s complement integer. Use the weights shown in the diagram.
![The word 10110111 with weight 128 in the first position for unsigned decoding and weight −128 for two’s complement. The other weights are the same.](/immagini/informatica/rappresentazione-numeri/pesi-con-segno-en.png)

$$
\begin{aligned}
U &= 128+32+16+4+2+1=183 \\[6pt]
S &= -128+32+16+4+2+1=-73 .
\end{aligned}
$$

The word represents $183$ as an unsigned integer and $-73$ in two’s complement.

<div class="spiegazione">

The bits equal to $1$ select the weights to add. The most significant bit is $1$, so it contributes $2^7=128$ when read as unsigned and $-2^7=-128$ when read in two’s complement. The other positions have the same weights in both interpretations, so their contributions always add up to $55$.

Check the signed value by encoding $-73$ again. With eight bits, $2^8-73=183$, so the encoding of $-73$ is the unsigned word with value $183$. That word is $10110111$, so the two interpretations agree with the encoding.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Encode $-46$ in seven-bit two’s complement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-64 &\leq -46\leq 63 \\[6pt]
2^7-46 &= 128-46=82 \\[6pt]
82 &= 64+16+2=(1010010)_2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

The required word is $1010010$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The seven-bit range runs from $-2^6$ to $2^6-1$, so it contains $-46$. To encode this negative integer, find its nonnegative remainder modulo $2^7$. Adding $128$ gives $82$. The terms $64$, $16$ and $2$ set the bits at indices $6$, $4$ and $1$, respectively.

To verify the word, apply the signed weights directly. The bit at index $6$ is $1$ and contributes $-64$, while the two set bits to its right contribute $16$ and $2$. This gives $-64+16+2=-46$, the required value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the minimum number of bits needed to represent both $-65$ and $63$ in two’s complement. Encode both values at that width.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_7 &= \{-64,\ldots,63\} \\
I_8 &= \{-128,\ldots,127\} \\[6pt]
2^8-65 &= 191=(10111111)_2 \\
63 &= (00111111)_2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

The minimum is **eight bits**. The words are $10111111$ for $-65$ and $00111111$ for $63$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $I_n$ be the set of integers representable in $n$-bit two’s complement. The seven-bit minimum is $-64$, so $-65$ requires more bits. The range grows with $n$, so narrower widths are also insufficient. The eight-bit range contains both integers, so eight is the minimum width. For the positive integer, use its eight-bit binary representation. For the negative integer, encode the remainder $191$.

As a check, the last six bits in both words equal $1$ and together contribute $63$. The bit at index $6$ is $0$ in both cases. The most significant bit adds $-128$ in the first word and zero in the second. The weighted sums give $-65$ and $63$, respectively.

</div>
