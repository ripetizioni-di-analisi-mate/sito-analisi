## Exercise 1

Write the decimal integer $39$ in base two using the fewest possible digits.

$$
\begin{aligned}
39 &= 2\cdot 19+1 \\
19 &= 2\cdot 9+1 \\
9 &= 2\cdot 4+1 \\
4 &= 2\cdot 2+0 \\
2 &= 2\cdot 1+0 \\
1 &= 2\cdot 0+1 .
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
39=(100111)_2 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first division gives a remainder of $1$. It occupies the binary units position, so it is the rightmost digit. The quotient $19$ gives the value of the remaining digits with their weights divided by $2$. The next division therefore extracts the digit of weight $2$, and each further division extracts the digit at the next weight. The process stops when the quotient reaches $0$.

Write the remainders starting with the last one, since it is the most significant bit. The first bit of the resulting word is $1$, so all six digits are needed in the shortest representation. Summing the weights of the four bits equal to $1$ gives $32+4+2+1=39$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Encode the decimal integer $190$ as a ten-bit unsigned binary word.

$$
\begin{aligned}
190 &= 2\cdot 95+0 \\
95 &= 2\cdot 47+1 \\
47 &= 2\cdot 23+1 \\
23 &= 2\cdot 11+1 \\
11 &= 2\cdot 5+1 \\
5 &= 2\cdot 2+1 \\
2 &= 2\cdot 1+0 \\
1 &= 2\cdot 0+1 .
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(10111110)_2=(0010111110)_2=190 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $190$ is even, the first remainder is $0$ and the least significant bit is $0$. Reading the remainders from the bottom up gives the eight-bit word $10111110$. The problem specifies a ten-bit word, so two more positions are needed. Set the bits at indices $9$ and $8$, to the left of the digits already obtained, to zero. The existing bits keep their weights.

Check the unsigned value by summing the powers of two at the positions where the bit is set. The bits equal to $1$ in the ten-bit word have indices $7$, $5$, $4$, $3$, $2$ and $1$. Their weights add up to $128+32+16+8+4+2=190$, as required.

</div>
