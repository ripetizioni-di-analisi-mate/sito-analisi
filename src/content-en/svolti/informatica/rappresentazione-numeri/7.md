## Exercise 1

Find the finite binary expansion of $0.8125$ by multiplying repeatedly by $2$.

| Starting fraction | Product with $2$ | Next bit | Remaining fraction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0.8125$ | $1.625$ | $1$ | $0.625$ |
| $0.625$ | $1.25$ | $1$ | $0.25$ |
| $0.25$ | $0.5$ | $0$ | $0.5$ |
| $0.5$ | $1$ | $1$ | $0$ |

$$
0.8125=(0.1101)_2 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying by $2$ shifts the binary expansion of the fraction one place to the left. The digit that crosses the binary point becomes the integer part of the product. In the first row, for example, the integer part of $1.625$ gives the first bit $1$, and the fractional part $0.625$ contains the bits still to be found. The remaining rows apply the same procedure to the fractional part. When it reaches zero, every remaining bit is zero.

Check the expansion using the fractional weights. The bits equal to $1$ occupy positions with weights $2^{-1}$, $2^{-2}$ and $2^{-4}$. Their sum is $\dfrac{8+4+1}{16}=\dfrac{13}{16}=0.8125$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Encode $-2.375$ in a seven-bit fixed-point format with four fractional bits. The underlying integer uses two’s complement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= -2.375\cdot 2^4=-38 \\
-64 &\leq m \leq 63 \\[6pt]
2^7+m &= 128-38=90 \\
90 &= (1011010)_2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

The required word is $1011010$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The represented value is $m\,2^{-4}$, where $m$ is the signed integer encoded by the word. Each unit of $m$ contributes $\dfrac{1}{16}$ to the represented value. Multiplying the specified value by $16$ therefore gives $m=-38$. The product is an integer and lies within the seven-bit two’s complement range, so the encoding is exact. To obtain the word, write its nonnegative remainder modulo $128$ in binary.

As a check, apply the fixed-point weights directly. In the word $1011010$, the sign bit has weight $-2^{6-4}=-4$ and the other bits equal to $1$ have weights $2^0$, $2^{-1}$ and $2^{-3}$. The sum is $-4+1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{8}=-2.375$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether $\dfrac{13}{48}$ can be represented exactly in binary fixed point with a finite number of bits. In an unsigned six-bit format with four fractional bits, find the word that minimizes the absolute error and compute that error.

$$
\begin{aligned}
48 &= 2^4\cdot 3 \\[6pt]
\frac{4}{16} &= \frac{12}{48}<\frac{13}{48} \\[6pt]
\frac{5}{16} &= \frac{15}{48}>\frac{13}{48} .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{13}{48}-\frac{12}{48} &= \frac{1}{48} \\[6pt]
\frac{15}{48}-\frac{13}{48} &= \frac{2}{48} .
\end{aligned}
$$

The value requires an infinite binary expansion. In the specified format, the closest word is $000100$, with absolute error $\dfrac{1}{48}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction is in lowest terms and its denominator has a factor of $3$. A finite binary fraction has a reduced denominator that divides a power of $2$, so the given value requires infinitely many fractional bits. In the specified format, consecutive values are $\dfrac{1}{16}$ apart. The closest candidates to compare correspond to the scaled integers $4$ and $5$. Every other value is farther away on one side or the other. The value encoded by $4$ is closer, so represent the integer $4$ in six bits.

Decode the word $000100$ using the weight of its only set bit, which is $2^{2-4}=\dfrac{1}{4}$. The absolute difference $\left\lvert\dfrac{13}{48}-\dfrac{1}{4}\right\rvert$ is $\dfrac{1}{48}$.

</div>
