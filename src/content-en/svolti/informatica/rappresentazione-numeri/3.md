## Exercise 1

Convert the binary number $(11010110011)_2$ to base sixteen.

$$
(11010110011)_2=(0110\;1011\;0011)_2 .
$$

| Four-bit group | Value | Hexadecimal digit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $0110$ | $4+2=6$ | $6$ |
| $1011$ | $8+2+1=11$ | $B$ |
| $0011$ | $2+1=3$ | $3$ |

$$
(11010110011)_2=(6B3)_{16} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $16=2^4$, one group of four bits occupies one position in a hexadecimal number. Start at the bit with weight $1$ and form groups of four from the right. The leftmost group has three bits, so add a leading zero to give it four positions. The total value stays the same because the new bit contributes zero.

To verify the conversion, compute the value in base ten in two ways. The hexadecimal number gives $6\cdot256+11\cdot16+3=1715$. In the binary word, the bits equal to $1$ contribute $1024+512+128+32+16+2+1=1715$. The matching values verify the grouping.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write $(F09)_{16}$ as a twelve-bit word.

| Hexadecimal digit | Value | Four-bit group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $F$ | $15$ | $1111$ |
| $0$ | $0$ | $0000$ |
| $9$ | $9$ | $1001$ |

$$
(F09)_{16}=(1111\;0000\;1001)_2 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The digit $F$ has value $15$, so every weight from $8$ to $1$ contributes in its group. The digit $9$ has value $8+1$, so the outer positions in its group have bits equal to $1$. The middle digit $0$ requires four zeros because its group occupies the hexadecimal position with weight $16$. Placing the three groups in the order of the original digits gives the required twelve bits.

Reading the hexadecimal number gives $15\cdot256+0\cdot16+9=3849$. In the twelve-bit word, the group $1111$ contributes $2048+1024+512+256$, and $1001$ contributes $8+1$. Their sum is again $3849$, so the bit positions are correct.

</div>
