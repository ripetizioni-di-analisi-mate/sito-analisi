## Exercise 1

A stream supplies `2589` followed by a newline. The program makes two successful calls to `fgets(line, 4, file)`. Give the strings in order and say which one contains the delimiter.

The first string is `"258"`. The second is `"9\n"`, which contains the delimiter.

<div class="spiegazione">

Capacity $4$ leaves exactly three positions for input characters and one for the terminator. The first call stops after `8` because of the capacity limit. The second starts at `9` and stops at the delimiter. These are two fragments of a single physical line. As a check, concatenating the fragments reconstructs the four original digits and their newline without loss or duplication.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The file supplies the sequence `A\nZ\n\n`. A loop reads with `fgetc` and separately counts letters and newlines. Find both counts and the number of successful character reads. The full read completes without errors.

The input contains the letters `A` and `Z` and three newlines. There are $2+3=5$ successful reads.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each successful call produces one character. The empty line contributes its own delimiter. Only `A` and `Z` increment the letter counter. The call after the final five characters reports end-of-file and does not count as a successful read. Check by enumerating the sequence positions. The letters occur first and third, and the delimiters second, fourth and fifth. No other characters remain to count.

</div>
