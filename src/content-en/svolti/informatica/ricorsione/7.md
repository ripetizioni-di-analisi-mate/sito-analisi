## Exercise 1

A function with integer parameter $n\geq0$ returns immediately if $n\leq1$. For $n>1$ it makes one call with argument $\lfloor\dfrac{n}{2}\rfloor$ and waits for the result. For $n=25$, compute the total number of invocations and the greatest number of active logical records.

$$
25\longrightarrow12\longrightarrow6\longrightarrow3\longrightarrow1.
$$

Both counts equal $5$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The integer quotient gives a single next argument. Starting at $25$, the first value that meets the base case is $1$. The initial call and four children along the path give five invocations. At the moment the base is reached, each ancestor is still waiting for a result. All invocations are therefore active together at that point.

As a check, $2^4\leq25<2^5$. Dividing by two four times gives a quotient of one. After three divisions the quotient is still $3$. Counting the initial argument and four reductions gives the same depth.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A function with integer parameter $n\geq0$ stops at the base $n=0$. For $n>0$ it makes three calls with argument $n-1$ in sequence. Compute the total invocation count and depth for $n=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{total nodes} &= 1+3+9+27=40\\[6pt]
\text{depth} &= 4 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root is at the first level. Each of its three children is the parent of three more nodes, so there are nine invocations at the third level. Each invocation there has argument $1$ and produces three base calls, so the fourth level has twenty-seven nodes. On each individual path, the arguments are $3$, $2$, $1$ and $0$. Sibling calls run in sequence, so they enter the total count at separate times.

To check the total, start at $N(0)=1$. The rule $N(n)=1+3N(n-1)$ gives $4$, $13$ and $40$ in order. The same trace with one child at a time increases depth by one per level.

</div>
