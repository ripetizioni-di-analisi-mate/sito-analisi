## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with bed slope $i = 0.0005$ and $k_s = 75\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ must carry a discharge of $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in uniform flow, as shown. Find the normal depth, mean velocity and Froude number.

::schema{nodi="S,0,1.12;T,4,1.12" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 4,0 4,1.12 0,1.12" solidi="-1.2,1.7 0,1.7 0,0 4,0 4,1.7 5.2,1.7 5.2,-0.7 -1.2,-0.7" pelo="0,4,1.12,p_a = 0" quote="2 0,2 1.12,h_0,0;0 -0.7,4 -0.7,4.00 m,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel with $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, $i = 0.0005$ and $k_s = 75\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, showing the depth $h_0$ needed to carry $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

$$
\frac{Q}{k_s\,b\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}} = \frac{6.00}{75 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 0.02236} = 0.894
$$
$$
h = \left(\frac{Q}{k_s\,b\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{3}{5}}\left(\frac{b + 2h}{b}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}} = 0.894^{\frac{3}{5}}\left(\frac{4 + 2h}{4}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}}
$$

| Trial | $h$ used on the right ($\mathrm{m}$) | Resulting $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial | $-$ | $0.935$ |
| 1 | $0.935$ | $1.090$ |
| 2 | $1.090$ | $1.113$ |
| 3 | $1.113$ | $1.116$ |
| 4 | $1.116$ | $1.117$ |

The last two results differ by one millimeter. Stop iterating and take $h_0 = 1.12\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
A = 4.00 \cdot 1.117 = 4.47\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
C = 4.00 + 2 \cdot 1.117 = 6.23\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
R = \frac{4.47}{6.23} = 0.717\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{6.00}{4.47} = 1.34\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{1.34}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.117}} = 0.406
$$

Check the calculation by starting from a different initial estimate, $h = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Successive iterations give $1.234\,\mathrm{m}$, $1.133\,\mathrm{m}$, $1.119\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.117\,\mathrm{m}$, the same result. The discharge calculated using $h_0 = 1.117\,\mathrm{m}$ is
$$
Q = 75 \cdot 4.47 \cdot 0.717^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.02236 = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Normal depth enters both the flow area and the hydraulic radius with different exponents, so equation $Q(h_0) = Q$ has no closed-form solution. To obtain the iteration formula, isolate $h$ in the term raised to the power $\dfrac{5}{3}$, leaving the correction involving the wetted perimeter on the right.

For an initial estimate, assume a very wide channel, so that $R \simeq h$. The correction factor is then $1$, giving $h = 0.894^{\frac{3}{5}} = 0.935\,\mathrm{m}$ directly. This underestimates the actual depth because omitting the wetted lengths of the sidewalls underestimates resistance.

At each iteration, substitute the preceding depth on the right. The correction decreases rapidly because $\left(\dfrac{b + 2h}{b}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}}$ changes only slightly with $h$ when the channel is wide relative to the water depth.

Stop when successive estimates differ by one millimeter. This tolerance is more than adequate for a water depth exceeding a meter.

In uniform flow $Fr = 0.406$, so the flow is **subcritical** and the channel has a mild bed slope at this discharge.

Repeat the iteration from a substantially different initial estimate. It converges to the same depth, which reproduces the specified discharge in the Gauckler–Strickler equation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The trapezoidal channel shown has $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, side slope $m = 1.5$, bed slope $i = 0.0009$ and $k_s = 45\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. Find the normal depth at a discharge of $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and classify the flow.

::schema{nodi="S,-2.2,1.49;T,5.2,1.49" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 3,0 5.2,1.49 -2.2,1.49" solidi="-4.6,2 -3,2 0,0 3,0 6,2 7.6,2 7.6,-0.7 -4.6,-0.7" pelo="-2.2,5.2,1.49,p_a = 0" quote="1.5 0,1.5 1.49,h_0,0;0 -0.7,3 -0.7,3.00 m,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal channel with $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, side slope $m = 1.5$, $i = 0.0009$ and $k_s = 45\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$"}

$$
Q(h) = k_s\,A(h)\,R(h)^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}} ,
\qquad
A = (3 + 1.5\,h)\,h ,
\qquad
C = 3 + 2h\sqrt{3.25}
$$

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $C$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $Q(h)$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $1.20$ | $5.76$ | $7.33$ | $0.786$ | $6.62$ |
| $1.40$ | $7.14$ | $8.05$ | $0.887$ | $8.90$ |
| $1.50$ | $7.88$ | $8.41$ | $0.937$ | $10.18$ |
| $1.49$ | $7.80$ | $8.37$ | $0.932$ | $10.04$ |

Stop when the discharge error is less than $1\,\%$, giving $h_0 = 1.49\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
v = \frac{10.0}{7.80} = 1.28\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
B = 3.00 + 2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 1.49 = 7.47\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_m = \frac{7.80}{7.47} = 1.044\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Fr = \frac{1.28}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.044}} = 0.401
$$

Check using the Chézy equation:
$$
\chi = 45 \cdot 0.932^{\frac{1}{6}} = 45 \cdot 0.988 = 44.5\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}/s}
$$
$$
v = \chi\sqrt{R\,i} = 44.5 \cdot \sqrt{0.932 \cdot 0.0009} = 44.5 \cdot 0.0290 = 1.29\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Q = 7.80 \cdot 1.29 = 10.1\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a trapezoidal section the flow area contains a quadratic term in depth, whereas wetted perimeter varies linearly with depth. A convenient approach is to calculate the discharge for several trial depths.

The discharge increases with depth, so the trial depths bracket the solution. At $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ the discharge is too small and at $h = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ it is too large. Choose the next trial within this interval to narrow the bracket.

At $h = 1.49\,\mathrm{m}$ the calculated discharge is $10.04\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, differing from the specified discharge by $0.4\,\%$. This is within the stated tolerance, so the iteration stops.

The Froude number uses hydraulic depth $1.044\,\mathrm{m}$. It is smaller than the water depth $1.49\,\mathrm{m}$ because the top width is $7.47\,\mathrm{m}$ and the bottom width is only $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The result is $Fr = 0.401$, so the flow is **subcritical**.

Recalculate the velocity from the Chézy equation, using coefficient $\chi$ obtained from $k_s$. The resulting discharge agrees with the table to within rounding.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The circular drain shown, of diameter $D = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ with $i = 0.0012$ and $k_s = 80\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, must carry $Q = 0.800\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in uniform flow. Find the water depth, relative depth of filling and Froude number.

::schema{nodi="S,-0.6,0;T,0.6,0" nomi=0 cerchi="0,0,0.6" quote="S,T,1.20 m,-0.75" riferimento=0 titolo="Circular drain of diameter $D = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ with $i = 0.0012$ and $k_s = 80\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, showing the depth needed to carry $Q = 0.800\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

$$
\cos\frac{\theta}{2} = 1 - \frac{2h}{D} ,
\qquad
A = \frac{D^2}{8}\,(\theta - \sin\theta) ,
\qquad
C = \frac{D\,\theta}{2}
$$

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $\theta$ ($\mathrm{rad}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $C$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $Q(h)$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0.50$ | $2.807$ | $0.446$ | $1.684$ | $0.265$ | $0.510$ |
| $0.60$ | $3.142$ | $0.565$ | $1.885$ | $0.300$ | $0.702$ |
| $0.70$ | $3.476$ | $0.685$ | $2.086$ | $0.328$ | $0.903$ |
| $0.65$ | $3.308$ | $0.625$ | $1.985$ | $0.315$ | $0.803$ |

The discharge error is $0.4\,\%$, so $h_0 = 0.65\,\mathrm{m}$ and the relative depth of filling is $\dfrac{h_0}{D} = 0.54$.

$$
v = \frac{0.800}{0.625} = 1.28\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
B = 1.20 \cdot \sin 1.654 = 1.196\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_m = \frac{0.625}{1.196} = 0.523\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Fr = \frac{1.28}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.523}} = 0.565
$$

Check against the full-pipe section:
$$
A_p = \frac{\pi \cdot 1.20^2}{4} = 1.131\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
R_p = \frac{1.20}{4} = 0.300\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Q_p = 80 \cdot 1.131 \cdot 0.300^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.0346 = 1.40\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$
The specified discharge is $57\,\%$ of $Q_p$ at a relative depth of $54\,\%$. These results are consistent.

<div class="spiegazione">

A circular section requires an additional step because depth enters the geometric formulas through the central angle $\theta$. For each trial depth, find the angle first and then the flow area and wetted perimeter.

At $h = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ the pipe is half full, the central angle is exactly $\pi$ and the hydraulic radius is $\dfrac{D}{4}$, the same as for the full pipe. This trial gives a reference discharge of $0.702\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, below the specified value.

The trials at $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$ bracket the solution. The intermediate value $0.65\,\mathrm{m}$ gives a discharge within $0.4\,\%$ of the specified discharge, meeting the stated tolerance.

The relative depth $0.54$ is acceptable for a sewer, leaving space above the water for ventilation. The velocity $1.28\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is sufficient to prevent deposits.

The Froude number is $0.565$, so the flow is **subcritical**. At this degree of filling the hydraulic depth $0.523\,\mathrm{m}$ is smaller than the water depth. Above the pipe center, the circular section narrows toward the free surface.

Compare with the full-pipe discharge. The discharge ratio and relative depth are close, as expected near half-full conditions.

</div>
