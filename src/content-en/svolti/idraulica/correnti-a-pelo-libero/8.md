## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a depth of $h_1 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The bed rises by $\Delta z = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ at the step shown. Find the depth downstream of the step, the change in water-surface elevation and the greatest step the flow can pass. Neglect head loss through the transition.

::schema{nomi=0 acqua="0,0 4,0 4,0.4 9,0.4 9,2.93 0,3" solidi="0,-1 9,-1 9,0.4 4,0.4 4,0 0,0" linee="0,3 9,2.93" tratteggi="4,0 9.8,0" quote="2.7 0,2.7 3,1.50 m,0;9.6 0,9.6 0.4,Δz = 0.20 m,0.5;6.5 0.4,6.5 2.93,h_2,0" frecce="0.5,1.5,1.5,1.5,v_1" riferimento=0 titolo="Upward bed step of $\Delta z = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ in a channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carrying $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

$$
q = \frac{8.00}{4.00} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s} ,
\qquad
v_1 = \frac{2.00}{1.50} = 1.333\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{1.333}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.50}} = 0.348
$$
$$
E_1 = h_1 + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 1.50 + \frac{1.778}{19.62} = 1.50 + 0.0906 = 1.591\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{4.00}{9.81}} = 0.742\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
E_{\min} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 0.742 = 1.112\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
E_2 = E_1 - \Delta z = 1.591 - 0.20 = 1.391\,\mathrm{m} > E_{\min}
$$
$$
h^3 - 1.391\,h^2 + \frac{q^2}{2g} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h^3 - 1.391\,h^2 + 0.2039 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h_2 = 1.263\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{downstream free-surface elevation} = h_2 + \Delta z = 1.263 + 0.200 = 1.463\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{drop} = 1.500 - 1.463 = 0.037\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta z_{\max} = E_1 - E_{\min} = 1.591 - 1.112 = 0.479\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check:
$$
v_2 = \frac{2.00}{1.263} = 1.584\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
E_2 = 1.263 + \frac{2.509}{19.62} = 1.263 + 0.128 = 1.391\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Fr_2 = \frac{1.584}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.263}} = 0.450 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The step is short enough for head loss to be neglected, so total head is conserved between the two sections. Since $H = z_f + E$ and the bed rises by $\Delta z$, the specific energy decreases downstream by the same amount.

The upstream flow has $Fr_1 = 0.348 < 1$ and is **subcritical**. First check that the flow can pass over the step. The available downstream specific energy, $1.391\,\mathrm{m}$, exceeds the minimum $1.112\,\mathrm{m}$ required for the discharge, so the step causes no change in upstream conditions.

The cubic has two positive roots, $1.263\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.471\,\mathrm{m}$. Choose the first because the approaching flow is subcritical and there is no head loss. With no transition through critical flow, the depth must remain above $h_c$ downstream.

The depth decreases by $0.237\,\mathrm{m}$ while the bed rises by $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a drop of $0.037\,\mathrm{m}$ in the water-surface elevation. With subcritical flow an upward step lowers the surface. The specific energy decreases, and since $\dfrac{dE}{dh} > 0$, the water depth decreases as well.

The greatest step height the flow can pass is the difference between the available and minimum specific energies, $0.479\,\mathrm{m}$. A higher step would prevent the specified discharge from passing at the available energy, causing the upstream water level to rise until sufficient energy was available.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The same channel, of width $b_1 = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and carrying $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ with $h_1 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$, contracts to width $b_2 = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ without a change in bed elevation, as shown in plan. Find the water depth in the contraction and the minimum width that can pass the discharge.

::schema{nodi="S,0,0;T,0,4;U,11,0.75;V,11,3.25" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 5,0 6.5,0.75 11,0.75 11,3.25 6.5,3.25 5,4 0,4" solidi="-0.6,-1 11.6,-1 11.6,0.75 6.5,0.75 5,0 0,0 0,-0.6;-0.6,5 11.6,5 11.6,3.25 6.5,3.25 5,4 0,4 0,4.6" frecce="1.2,2,3,2,v_1;7.8,2,9.6,2,v_2" quote="S,T,4.00 m,-0.7;U,V,2.50 m,0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of a contraction from $b_1 = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $b_2 = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with no change in bed elevation"}

$$
E_1 = 1.591\,\mathrm{m} \quad \text{(calculated in Exercise 1)} ,
\qquad
E_2 = E_1 = 1.591\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
q_2 = \frac{8.00}{2.50} = 3.20\,\mathrm{m^2/s} ,
\qquad
h_{c2} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3.20^2}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{1.0438} = 1.014\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
E_{\min,2} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 1.014 = 1.522\,\mathrm{m} < E_2 = 1.591\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h^3 - 1.591\,h^2 + \frac{q_2^2}{2g} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h^3 - 1.591\,h^2 + 0.5219 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h_2 = 1.264\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{drop in free-surface elevation} = 1.500 - 1.264 = 0.236\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Minimum width. The limiting condition is critical flow in the contraction, or $E_{\min} = E_1$:
$$
h_c = \frac{2}{3}\,E_1 = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 1.591 = 1.061\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
q_{\max} = \sqrt{g\,h_c^3} = \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.194} = 3.423\,\mathrm{m^2/s}
$$
$$
b_{\min} = \frac{Q}{q_{\max}} = \frac{8.00}{3.423} = 2.34\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check:
$$
v_2 = \frac{3.20}{1.264} = 2.532\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
E_2 = 1.264 + \frac{6.411}{19.62} = 1.264 + 0.327 = 1.591\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Fr_2 = \frac{2.532}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.264}} = 0.719 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bed elevation is constant, so specific energy is conserved between the wide and contracted sections. The discharge per unit width increases from $2.00$ to $3.20\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$.

Critical depth depends on $q$. In the contraction it increases to $1.014\,\mathrm{m}$, raising the minimum specific energy to $1.522\,\mathrm{m}$. The available energy, $1.591\,\mathrm{m}$, allows the discharge to pass with a margin of $0.069\,\mathrm{m}$.

Choose the root above critical depth. The approaching flow is subcritical and does not pass through critical flow. The depth drops from $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1.264\,\mathrm{m}$. Since the bed is horizontal, the free surface drops by the same amount.

For the minimum width, set the available specific energy in the contraction equal to its minimum value. Use $E_{\min} = \dfrac{3}{2}h_c$ to obtain $h_c = \dfrac{2}{3}E_1$, then use $q^2 = g\,h_c^3$ to find the maximum discharge per unit width and hence the width. This gives $b_{\min} = 2.34\,\mathrm{m}$. Any narrower section would raise the upstream water level.

Recalculating the specific energy in the contraction gives $1.591\,\mathrm{m}$. The Froude number is $0.719$, so the flow remains **subcritical**, although closer to critical conditions than upstream.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a depth of $h_1 = 0.400\,\mathrm{m}$. The bed rises by $\Delta z = 0.300\,\mathrm{m}$ at the step shown. Find the depth downstream of the step and the change in water-surface elevation. Compare the result with Exercise 1.

::schema{nomi=0 acqua="0,0 4,0 4,0.6 9,0.6 9,1.55 0,0.8" solidi="0,-0.8 9,-0.8 9,0.6 4,0.6 4,0 0,0" linee="0,0.8 9,1.55" tratteggi="4,0 9.8,0" quote="2.7 0,2.7 0.8,0.400 m,0;9.6 0,9.6 0.6,Δz = 0.300 m,0.5;6.5 0.6,6.5 1.55,h_2,0" frecce="0.4,0.35,1.4,0.35,v_1" riferimento=0 titolo="Upward bed step of $\Delta z = 0.300\,\mathrm{m}$ in a channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carrying $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in supercritical flow"}

$$
q = \frac{6.00}{3.00} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s} ,
\qquad
v_1 = \frac{2.00}{0.400} = 5.00\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{5.00}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.400}} = 2.52
$$
$$
E_1 = 0.400 + \frac{25.0}{19.62} = 0.400 + 1.274 = 1.674\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
E_{\min} = 1.112\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
E_2 = 1.674 - 0.300 = 1.374\,\mathrm{m} > E_{\min}
$$
$$
h^3 - 1.374\,h^2 + 0.2039 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h_2 = 0.477\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{downstream free-surface elevation} = 0.477 + 0.300 = 0.777\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\text{rise} = 0.777 - 0.400 = 0.377\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check:
$$
v_2 = \frac{2.00}{0.477} = 4.193\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
E_2 = 0.477 + \frac{17.58}{19.62} = 0.477 + 0.896 = 1.373\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Fr_2 = \frac{4.193}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.477}} = 1.94 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The approaching flow has $Fr_1 = 2.52 > 1$ and is **supercritical**. The discharge per unit width is the same as in Exercise 1, so the critical depth is again $0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ and exceeds the water depth.

The upstream specific energy is $1.674\,\mathrm{m}$. Although the water is much shallower, this exceeds the value in Exercise 1 because the kinetic term is large in supercritical flow. Beyond the step, $1.374\,\mathrm{m}$ remains, still above the minimum, so the flow passes.

Of the two positive roots, choose the one below critical depth, $0.477\,\mathrm{m}$. With no head loss and no transition through critical flow, the flow remains supercritical.

The water depth increases by $0.077\,\mathrm{m}$ and the bed rises by $0.300\,\mathrm{m}$, so the free surface rises by $0.377\,\mathrm{m}$. This reverses the behavior in Exercise 1. In supercritical flow $\dfrac{dE}{dh} < 0$, so a decrease in specific energy increases the water depth.

Recalculating the downstream specific energy gives $1.374\,\mathrm{m}$ within rounding error. The Froude number is $1.94$, so the flow remains **supercritical**.

</div>
