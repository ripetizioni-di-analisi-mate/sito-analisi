## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, as shown. Find the critical depth, critical velocity and minimum specific energy.

::schema{nodi="S,0,0.74;T,3,0.74" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 3,0 3,0.74 0,0.74" solidi="-0.9,1.4 0,1.4 0,0 3,0 3,1.4 3.9,1.4 3.9,-0.6 -0.9,-0.6" pelo="0,3,0.74,p_a = 0" quote="1.5 0,1.5 0.74,h_c,0;0 -0.6,3 -0.6,3.00 m,0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel with $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carrying $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, with critical depth to be determined"}

$$
q = \frac{Q}{b} = \frac{6.00}{3.00} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}
$$
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{4.00}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{0.4078} = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A_c = 3.00 \cdot 0.742 = 2.226\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
v_c = \frac{6.00}{2.226} = 2.70\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
E_{\min} = \frac{3}{2}\,h_c = 1.5 \cdot 0.742 = 1.11\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check using the Froude number:
$$
Fr = \frac{v_c}{\sqrt{g\,h_c}} = \frac{2.70}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.742}} = \frac{2.70}{2.70} = 1.00
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a rectangular channel, critical depth depends only on discharge per unit width. First divide the discharge by the bed width.

Equation $h_c = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{q^2}{g}}$ follows from the condition $Q^2 B = g A^3$ with $A = b h$ and $B = b$. The result $0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ is independent of slope and roughness because critical depth depends on the discharge and the cross-sectional geometry.

The minimum specific energy is one and a half times the critical depth. At critical flow the velocity head equals half the water depth. Carrying $6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ therefore requires at least $1.11\,\mathrm{m}$ of specific energy at every section.

Using the critical depth and velocity in the Froude number gives **exactly $1$**, as required by the definition of critical flow.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The trapezoidal channel shown has bottom width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and side slope $m = 1$ and carries $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Find the critical depth and minimum specific energy.

::schema{nodi="S,-0.99,0.99;T,2.99,0.99" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 2,0 2.99,0.99 -0.99,0.99" solidi="-2.5,1.5 -1.5,1.5 0,0 2,0 3.5,1.5 4.5,1.5 4.5,-0.6 -2.5,-0.6" pelo="-0.99,2.99,0.99,p_a = 0" quote="1 0,1 0.99,h_c,0;0 -0.6,2 -0.6,2.00 m,0.45;S,T,B,-0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal channel with $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and side slope $m = 1$ carrying $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

$$
A = (2 + h)\,h ,
\qquad
B = 2 + 2h ,
\qquad
\text{critical-flow condition:} \quad \frac{Q^2 B}{g\,A^3} = 1
$$

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $B$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $\dfrac{Q^2 B}{g A^3}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0.90$ | $2.61$ | $3.80$ | $1.394$ |
| $1.00$ | $3.00$ | $4.00$ | $0.967$ |
| $0.99$ | $2.960$ | $3.98$ | $1.001$ |

$$
h_c = 0.990\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
A_c = 2.960\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
v_c = \frac{8.00}{2.960} = 2.70\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
E_{\min} = h_c + \frac{v_c^2}{2g} = 0.990 + \frac{2.70^2}{19.62} = 0.990 + 0.372 = 1.362\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the minimum by evaluating the specific energy at two neighboring depths. Both values are higher:
$$
E(0.90) = 0.90 + \frac{64.0}{19.62 \cdot 2.61^2} = 0.90 + 0.479 = 1.379\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
E(1.10) = 1.10 + \frac{64.0}{19.62 \cdot 3.41^2} = 1.10 + 0.280 = 1.380\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There is no explicit expression for critical depth in a trapezoidal section because the condition $Q^2 B = g A^3$ is a fifth-degree equation in $h$. Evaluate the ratio $\dfrac{Q^2 B}{g A^3}$ at trial depths. Its critical value is $Fr^2$, and it decreases as depth increases.

At $h = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ the ratio exceeds $1$, so the flow would be supercritical and the depth below critical. At $h = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the ratio is below $1$, so the depth is above critical. The solution lies between these trial values.

At the trial depth $h = 0.99\,\mathrm{m}$ the ratio is $1.001$, giving a critical depth of $h_c = 0.990\,\mathrm{m}$ to three significant figures.

Find the minimum specific energy by substituting the critical depth in its definition. The relation $E_{\min} = \dfrac{3}{2}h_c$ only applies to a rectangular section. Here the full calculation gives a ratio of $E_{\min}$ to $h_c$ of $1.376$, different from $1.5$.

Calculate the specific energy at two depths, one below and one above the critical depth. Both give a value greater than $E_{\min}$, confirming a **minimum** at the calculated point.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The triangular channel shown has side slopes $m = 1.5$ and carries $Q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Derive the relation for the triangular section, then calculate its critical depth and minimum specific energy.

::schema{nodi="S,-1.44,0.96;T,1.44,0.96" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 1.44,0.96 -1.44,0.96" solidi="-3,1.5 -2.25,1.5 0,0 2.25,1.5 3,1.5 3,-0.5 -3,-0.5" pelo="-1.44,1.44,0.96,p_a = 0" quote="0 0,0 0.96,h_c,0.9;S,T,B,-0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Triangular channel with side slope $m = 1.5$ carrying $Q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

$$
A = m\,h^2 ,
\qquad
B = 2m\,h
$$
$$
Q^2\,B = g\,A^3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Q^2 \cdot 2m\,h_c = g\,m^3\,h_c^6
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h_c^5 = \frac{2\,Q^2}{g\,m^2}
$$
$$
h_c = \sqrt[5]{\frac{2 \cdot 9.00}{9.81 \cdot 2.25}} = \sqrt[5]{\frac{18.0}{22.07}} = \sqrt[5]{0.8154} = 0.960\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A_c = 1.5 \cdot 0.960^2 = 1.382\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
v_c = \frac{3.00}{1.382} = 2.17\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
E_{\min} = h_c + \frac{v_c^2}{2g} = 0.960 + \frac{4.709}{19.62} = 0.960 + 0.240 = 1.200\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check using the Froude number:
$$
B_c = 2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 0.960 = 2.880\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_m = \frac{1.382}{2.880} = 0.480\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Fr = \frac{2.17}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.480}} = \frac{2.17}{2.17} = 1.00
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A triangular channel has zero bed width. The flow area is that of a triangle with base $2 m h$ and height $h$, giving $A = m h^2$. The free-surface width is $B = 2 m h$.

Substitution in the critical-flow condition gives an equation with depth raised to the power $1$ on the left and $6$ on the right. Simplification gives $h_c^5 = \dfrac{2 Q^2}{g m^2}$, an explicit formula valid for any triangular section.

The critical depth is $0.960\,\mathrm{m}$. It depends on the side slope because a larger $m$ gives a wider cross section and a greater flow area at the same depth, reducing the critical depth.

For critical flow in a triangular section the hydraulic depth is $\dfrac{A_c}{B_c} = \dfrac{h_c}{2}$, the velocity head is $\dfrac{h_c}{4}$, and the minimum specific energy is $\dfrac{5}{4}h_c$. The numerical result confirms this relation because $1.25 \cdot 0.960 = 1.200\,\mathrm{m}$.

Calculate the Froude number using hydraulic depth rather than water depth. The result **$1$** confirms critical flow.

</div>
