## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, bed slope $i = 0.001$ and roughness coefficient $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ carries uniform flow at a depth of $h_0 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the mean velocity, discharge and Froude number.

::schema{nodi="S,0,1;T,3,1" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 3,0 3,1 0,1" solidi="-0.9,1.5 0,1.5 0,0 3,0 3,1.5 3.9,1.5 3.9,-0.6 -0.9,-0.6" pelo="0,3,1,p_a = 0" quote="1.5 0,1.5 1,1.00 m,0;0 -0.6,3 -0.6,3.00 m,0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel with $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, $h_0 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, $i = 0.001$ and $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$"}

$$
A = 3.00 \cdot 1.00 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
C = 3.00 + 2 \cdot 1.00 = 5.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
R = \frac{3.00}{5.00} = 0.600\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = k_s\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}} = 70 \cdot 0.600^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.001^{\frac{1}{2}} = 70 \cdot 0.711 \cdot 0.0316 = 1.57\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = A\,v = 3.00 \cdot 1.57 = 4.72\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
B = 3.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_m = \frac{3.00}{3.00} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{v}{\sqrt{g\,h_m}} = \frac{1.57}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.00}} = 0.503
$$

Check using the Chézy equation:
$$
\chi = k_s\,R^{\frac{1}{6}} = 70 \cdot 0.600^{\frac{1}{6}} = 70 \cdot 0.918 = 64.3\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}/s}
$$
$$
v = \chi\sqrt{R\,i} = 64.3 \cdot \sqrt{0.600 \cdot 0.001} = 64.3 \cdot 0.0245 = 1.57\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The flow is uniform, so the energy gradient equals the bed slope. Substitute $J = i = 0.001$ in the Gauckler–Strickler equation. The usual assumptions apply: the fluid is incompressible, the flow is steady, pressure varies hydrostatically across the section, and the correction coefficient is $\alpha = 1$.

Calculate the cross-sectional properties before applying the resistance equation, in which the hydraulic radius is raised to the power $\dfrac{2}{3}$. The wetted perimeter includes the bed and both walls. It excludes the free surface.

The velocity is $1.57\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the discharge is $4.72\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Both are of the expected order of magnitude for an artificial channel of this size with a slope of one in a thousand.

In a rectangular section the hydraulic depth equals the water depth, giving Froude number $0.503$. Since $Fr < 1$, the flow is **subcritical**.

For a check, express the same resistance using the Chézy equation. Obtain its coefficient $\chi$ from $k_s$ using $\chi = k_s R^{\frac{1}{6}}$. The resulting velocity agrees with the previous calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An earthen trapezoidal channel has bottom width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, side slope $m = 1$, bed slope $i = 0.0008$ and roughness coefficient $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. The normal depth is $h_0 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the discharge and classify the flow.

::schema{nodi="S,-1.2,1.2;T,3.2,1.2" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 2,0 3.2,1.2 -1.2,1.2" solidi="-3,1.7 -1.7,1.7 0,0 2,0 3.7,1.7 5,1.7 5,-0.6 -3,-0.6" pelo="-1.2,3.2,1.2,p_a = 0" quote="1 0,1 1.2,1.20 m,0;0 -0.6,2 -0.6,2.00 m,0.45;S,T,B,-0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal channel with $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, side slope $m = 1$, $h_0 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, $i = 0.0008$ and $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$"}

$$
A = (2.00 + 1 \cdot 1.20) \cdot 1.20 = 3.20 \cdot 1.20 = 3.84\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
C = 2.00 + 2 \cdot 1.20 \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2.00 + 3.394 = 5.394\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
R = \frac{3.84}{5.394} = 0.712\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = 50 \cdot 0.712^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.0008^{\frac{1}{2}} = 50 \cdot 0.797 \cdot 0.0283 = 1.13\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 3.84 \cdot 1.13 = 4.33\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
B = 2.00 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 1.20 = 4.40\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_m = \frac{3.84}{4.40} = 0.873\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Fr = \frac{1.13}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.873}} = \frac{1.13}{2.93} = 0.385
$$

Check using the force balance for uniform flow. The wall shear stress is
$$
\tau_0 = \gamma\,R\,i = 9810 \cdot 0.712 \cdot 0.0008 = 5.59\,\mathrm{Pa} ,
$$
and, over a reach of length $1\,\mathrm{m}$, the resisting force $\tau_0\,C = 5.59 \cdot 5.394 = 30.1\,\mathrm{N}$ equals the downslope component of the weight $\gamma\,A\,i = 9810 \cdot 3.84 \cdot 0.0008 = 30.1\,\mathrm{N}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The banks make an angle of $45^\circ$ because the side slope is $1$. Each bank therefore has a wetted length of $h\sqrt{2} = 1.697\,\mathrm{m}$, and the wetted perimeter is slightly greater than the bed width plus twice the water depth.

The coefficient $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ is typical of a regular earthen channel, which is rougher than concrete. At the same hydraulic radius and slope, the velocity is consequently lower than in the preceding exercise.

The discharge is $4.33\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and the velocity is $1.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$. This is acceptable in an earthen channel, where velocities above about $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ can erode the bed.

The hydraulic depth $0.873\,\mathrm{m}$ is less than the water depth because the free surface is wider than the bed. The Froude number is $0.385$, so flow is **subcritical**.

Check the force balance underlying the resistance equation. In a channel reach carrying uniform flow, the component of water weight along the bed balances the boundary shear force. Their equality confirms the values of $A$, $C$ and $R$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular drain of diameter $D = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $i = 0.002$ and $k_s = 80\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ carries uniform flow at a water depth of $h_0 = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the discharge, reporting it also in liters per second, and the Froude number.

::schema{nodi="P,-0.49,0.1;Q,0.49,0.1" nomi=0 cerchi="0,0,0.5" acqua="-0.490,0.100 -0.499,-0.026 -0.477,-0.150 -0.424,-0.265 -0.344,-0.363 -0.242,-0.437 -0.125,-0.484 -0.000,-0.500 0.125,-0.484 0.242,-0.437 0.344,-0.363 0.424,-0.265 0.477,-0.150 0.499,-0.026 0.490,0.100" linee="-0.49,0.1 0.49,0.1" quote="0 -0.5,0 0.1,0.60 m,-0.8;P,Q,B,-0.75" etichette="0.05,-0.22,θ" frecce="0,0,-0.475,0.097,;0,0,0.475,0.097," riferimento=0 titolo="Circular drain of diameter $D = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with water depth $h_0 = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\cos\frac{\theta}{2} = 1 - \frac{2 \cdot 0.60}{1.00} = -0.20 ,
\qquad
\frac{\theta}{2} = 1.772\,\mathrm{rad} ,
\qquad
\theta = 3.544\,\mathrm{rad} = 203.1^\circ
$$
$$
A = \frac{1.00^2}{8}\,\big(3.544 - (-0.392)\big) = 0.125 \cdot 3.936 = 0.492\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
C = \frac{1.00 \cdot 3.544}{2} = 1.772\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
R = \frac{0.492}{1.772} = 0.278\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = 80 \cdot 0.278^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.002^{\frac{1}{2}} = 80 \cdot 0.426 \cdot 0.0447 = 1.52\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 0.492 \cdot 1.52 = 0.749\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 749\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
B = 1.00 \cdot \sin 1.772 = 0.980\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_m = \frac{0.492}{0.980} = 0.502\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{1.52}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.502}} = 0.686
$$

Compare with the full section. For the full pipe $A_p = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = 0.785\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $R_p = \dfrac{D}{4} = 0.250\,\mathrm{m}$, giving
$$
Q_p = 80 \cdot 0.785 \cdot 0.250^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.0447 = 1.11\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
and the calculated discharge equals $67\,\%$ of $Q_p$ at a relative depth of $60\,\%$. This is consistent with the hydraulic radius being greater at this depth than in the full pipe.

<div class="spiegazione">

The relative depth is $\dfrac{h_0}{D} = 0.60$, so the water surface is above the center of the pipe. The cosine of the half-angle is negative and the central angle $\theta$ exceeds $\pi$.

The sine of $\theta = 3.544\,\mathrm{rad}$ is $-0.392$, so the difference $\theta - \sin\theta$ in the area formula becomes a sum. Ignoring the sign would underestimate the area.

The discharge is $0.749\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $749\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The mean velocity of $1.52\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is suitable for a sewer, which must keep settleable solids moving.

The Froude number is $0.686$ and the flow is **subcritical**. At this depth the hydraulic depth $0.502\,\mathrm{m}$ is appreciably smaller than the water depth. The circular section narrows near the free surface.

Compare the result with the full-pipe discharge. The discharge ratio $\dfrac{Q}{Q_p} = 0.67$ exceeds the area ratio $0.63$ because, at a relative depth of $60\,\%$, the hydraulic radius is greater than for the full pipe.

</div>
