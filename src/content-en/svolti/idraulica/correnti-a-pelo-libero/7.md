## Exercise 1

Design a lined rectangular channel to carry $Q = 5.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in uniform flow on bed slope $i = 0.001$ with $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, using the most efficient hydraulic section shown.

::schema{nodi="S,0,1.245;T,2.49,1.245" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 2.49,0 2.49,1.245 0,1.245" solidi="-0.8,1.8 0,1.8 0,0 2.49,0 2.49,1.8 3.29,1.8 3.29,-0.6 -0.8,-0.6" pelo="0,2.49,1.245,p_a = 0" quote="1.245 0,1.245 1.245,h,0;0 -0.6,2.49 -0.6,b = 2h,0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Most efficient rectangular section, with width twice the water depth"}

$$
b = 2h ,
\qquad
A = 2h^2 ,
\qquad
C = 4h ,
\qquad
R = \frac{h}{2}
$$
$$
Q = k_s\,A\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}} = k_s \cdot 2h^2 \cdot \left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} i^{\frac{1}{2}} = k_s\,2^{\frac{1}{3}}\,h^{\frac{8}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}
$$
$$
h^{\frac{8}{3}} = \frac{Q}{k_s\,2^{\frac{1}{3}}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}} = \frac{5.00}{70 \cdot 1.2599 \cdot 0.03162} = \frac{5.00}{2.789} = 1.793
$$
$$
h = 1.793^{\frac{3}{8}} = 1.245\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
b = 2h = 2.490\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A = 2 \cdot 1.245^2 = 3.100\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
R = 0.6225\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{5.00}{3.100} = 1.61\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{1.61}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.245}} = 0.462
$$

Check against a rectangular section with the same area and $b = 4h$:
$$
4h^2 = 3.100 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h = 0.880\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
b = 3.521\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
C = 3.521 + 1.761 = 5.282\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
R = 0.587\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Q = 70 \cdot 3.100 \cdot 0.587^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.03162 = 4.81\,\mathrm{m^3/s} < 5.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the most efficient rectangular section the width is twice the water depth, and the hydraulic radius is half the depth. These relations follow by minimizing the wetted perimeter at a specified flow area.

Substitute $b = 2h$ in the Gauckler–Strickler equation. The sole unknown is water depth, raised to the power $\dfrac{8}{3}$. Raising both sides to the power $\dfrac{3}{8}$ gives an explicit solution.

This gives $h = 1.245\,\mathrm{m}$ and $b = 2.490\,\mathrm{m}$. In practice choose rounded dimensions, such as $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, and check that the banks provide the specified freeboard above the water surface.

The velocity is $1.61\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the Froude number is $0.462$, so uniform flow is **subcritical**. This is desirable in a supply canal, where supercritical flow is avoided to prevent hydraulic jumps.

Compare the design with a wider, shallower rectangle of the same area. The wetted perimeter increases from $4.98\,\mathrm{m}$ to $5.28\,\mathrm{m}$, the hydraulic radius falls, and the resulting discharge $4.81\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ is below the requirement. The section with $b = 2h$ is therefore the most efficient.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular channel must have flow area $A = 4.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$, bed slope $i = 0.0009$ and $k_s = 65\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. Find the discharges for four sections with that area and different widths, and identify the most efficient section. The diagram shows the first three sections.

::schema{nodi="S,0,0.5;T,8,0.5" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 8,0 8,0.5 0,0.5;9.5,0 13.5,0 13.5,1 9.5,1;15,0 17.83,0 17.83,1.414 15,1.414" solidi="-0.6,1 0,1 0,0 8,0 8,1 8.6,1 8.6,-0.5 -0.6,-0.5;8.9,1.5 9.5,1.5 9.5,0 13.5,0 13.5,1.5 14.1,1.5 14.1,-0.5 8.9,-0.5;14.4,1.9 15,1.9 15,0 17.83,0 17.83,1.9 18.43,1.9 18.43,-0.5 14.4,-0.5" etichette="4,-1.2,b = 8.00 m;11.5,-1.2,b = 4.00 m;16.4,-1.2,b = 2.83 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Three rectangular sections of area $4.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$. As the width decreases, the wetted perimeter first decreases and then increases."}

| $b$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $C$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $8.00$ | $0.500$ | $9.00$ | $0.444$ | $4.54$ |
| $4.00$ | $1.000$ | $6.00$ | $0.667$ | $5.95$ |
| $2.83$ | $1.414$ | $5.66$ | $0.707$ | $6.19$ |
| $1.00$ | $4.000$ | $9.00$ | $0.444$ | $4.54$ |

$$
C(h) = \frac{A}{h} + 2h ,
\qquad
C'(h) = -\frac{A}{h^2} + 2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h = \sqrt{\frac{A}{2}} = \sqrt{2.00} = 1.414\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
b = \frac{4.00}{1.414} = 2.828\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check. The minimum wetted perimeter is
$$
C_{\min} = \frac{A}{h} + 2h = 2.828 + 2.828 = 5.657\,\mathrm{m} = 2\sqrt{2A} = 2\sqrt{8.00} = 5.657\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
and the ratio $\dfrac{b}{h} = \dfrac{2.828}{1.414} = 2.00$ confirms the condition $b = 2h$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The four sections have the same flow area and therefore require the same excavation volume. Their discharges depend only on the wetted perimeter, through $Q = k_s\,A^{\frac{5}{3}}\,C^{-2/3}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}$ in the Gauckler–Strickler equation.

The first and last rows of the table have the same wetted perimeter, $9.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and hence the same discharge. The wide, shallow section and the narrow, deep section are equally inefficient for opposite reasons.

Between these cases the wetted perimeter reaches a minimum. Setting its derivative to zero gives $h = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{2}} = 1.414\,\mathrm{m}$, or $b = 2h$. The corresponding discharge, $6.19\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, exceeds all the other values.

The section with $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $5.95\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $96\,\%$ of the maximum. Near the optimum, discharge changes little, so rounding the design dimensions causes no appreciable loss of efficiency.

The check confirms that the minimum wetted perimeter is $2\sqrt{2A}$, obtained by substituting $h = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{2}}$ in $C(h)$, and that the width-to-depth ratio is $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Design the most efficient earthen trapezoidal channel with banks inclined at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal, corresponding to side slope $m = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, to carry $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ with $i = 0.0006$ and $k_s = 45\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$. The cross section is shown.

::schema{nodi="S,-1.175,2.035;T,3.525,2.035" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 2.35,0 3.525,2.035 -1.175,2.035" solidi="-3,2.5 -1.44,2.5 0,0 2.35,0 3.79,2.5 5.35,2.5 5.35,-0.8 -3,-0.8" pelo="-1.175,3.525,2.035,p_a = 0" quote="1.175 0,1.175 2.035,h,0;0 -0.8,2.35 -0.8,b = 1.155h,0.5;S,T,B,-0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Most efficient trapezoidal section with sides at $60^\circ$, forming half a regular hexagon"}

$$
m = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = 0.5774 ,
\qquad
b = 2h\left(\sqrt{1 + m^2} - m\right) = 2h\,(1.1547 - 0.5774) = 1.1547\,h
$$
$$
A = (b + m\,h)\,h = (1.1547 + 0.5774)\,h^2 = \sqrt{3}\,h^2 ,
\qquad
R = \frac{h}{2}
$$
$$
Q = k_s\,\sqrt{3}\,h^2\left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} i^{\frac{1}{2}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h^{\frac{8}{3}} = \frac{Q\,2^{\frac{2}{3}}}{k_s\,\sqrt{3}\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}} = \frac{8.00 \cdot 1.5874}{45 \cdot 1.7321 \cdot 0.02449} = \frac{12.70}{1.909} = 6.652
$$
$$
h = 6.652^{\frac{3}{8}} = 2.035\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
b = 1.1547 \cdot 2.035 = 2.350\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A = \sqrt{3} \cdot 2.035^2 = 7.173\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
B = b + 2m\,h = 2.350 + 2.350 = 4.700\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{8.00}{7.173} = 1.12\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Check. The wetted perimeter is
$$
C = b + 2h\sqrt{1 + m^2} = 2.350 + 2 \cdot 2.035 \cdot 1.1547 = 2.350 + 4.700 = 7.050\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so $R = \dfrac{7.173}{7.050} = 1.017\,\mathrm{m}$, or $\dfrac{h}{2} = 1.018\,\mathrm{m}$, as predicted. Substitution in the Gauckler–Strickler equation gives
$$
Q = 45 \cdot 7.173 \cdot 1.017^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 0.02449 = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a trapezoidal section with a specified side slope, the bed width that minimizes the wetted perimeter is $b = 2h\left(\sqrt{1 + m^2} - m\right)$. The hydraulic radius is again half the water depth, as in the rectangular case.

With $m = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ the banks make an angle of $60^\circ$ to the horizontal and $b = 1.1547\,h$. The section is half a regular hexagon. This trapezoidal shape minimizes the wetted perimeter even when the side slope can be varied.

The area simplifies to $A = \sqrt{3}\,h^2$, leaving only the unknown $h$ in the Gauckler–Strickler equation, raised to the power $\dfrac{8}{3}$. Solve as for the rectangular section.

The design dimensions are $h = 2.04\,\mathrm{m}$ and $b = 2.35\,\mathrm{m}$, with free-surface width $4.70\,\mathrm{m}$. The velocity is $1.12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is suitable for an earthen channel.

For a check, use the general formula for wetted perimeter. It gives a hydraulic radius equal to half the water depth within rounding and reproduces the specified discharge.

</div>
