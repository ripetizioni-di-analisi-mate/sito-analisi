## Exercise 1

The rectangular channel shown has width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, roughness coefficient $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.002$ and carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Find its critical slope, classify the bed slope, and check your conclusion using the normal depth.

::schema{nodi="M,0,1.53;N,10,0.93" nomi=0 acqua="0,0.6 10,0 10,0.93 0,1.53" solidi="0,0.6 10,0 10,-0.7 0,-0.7" linee="0,1.53 10,0.93" quote="5 0.3,5 1.23,h_0,0.5" etichette="2.4,1.75,free surface;6.6,-0.45,bed slope i = 0.002" riferimento=0 titolo="Uniform flow in a rectangular channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, $i = 0.002$ and $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$"}

$$
q = \frac{6.00}{3.00} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s} ,
\qquad
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{4.00}{9.81}} = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A_c = 3.00 \cdot 0.742 = 2.225\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
C_c = 3.00 + 2 \cdot 0.742 = 4.483\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
R_c = \frac{2.225}{4.483} = 0.496\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_c = \frac{6.00}{2.225} = 2.697\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
i_c = \frac{v_c^2}{k_s^2\,R_c^{\frac{4}{3}}} = \frac{2.697^2}{70^2 \cdot 0.3929} = \frac{7.274}{1925} = 0.00378
$$

Since $i = 0.002 < i_c = 0.00378$, the bed has a **mild slope**.

Check using normal depth. Successive iterations give $0.764\,\mathrm{m}$, $0.901\,\mathrm{m}$, $0.923\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.926\,\mathrm{m}$, so $h_0 = 0.926\,\mathrm{m}$ and
$$
A = 3.00 \cdot 0.926 = 2.778\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{6.00}{2.778} = 2.16\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{2.16}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.926}} = 0.716 .
$$
The results $h_0 = 0.926\,\mathrm{m} > h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ and $Fr < 1$ agree with the slope classification.

<div class="spiegazione">

The critical slope is the bed slope at which normal depth equals critical depth. First find the critical depth from discharge and width, then find the slope that produces this depth in uniform flow.

Calculate the area, wetted perimeter, hydraulic radius and velocity at critical depth, because the Gauckler–Strickler equation must be evaluated at the critical section.

The critical slope is $0.00378$. A change in discharge alters the critical depth and hence $i_c$. The same channel can therefore have a mild slope for one discharge and a steep slope for another.

The bed slope is smaller than the critical slope, so normal depth exceeds critical depth. The bed has a **mild slope** and uniform flow is subcritical.

As a check, the rectangular-channel iteration gives a normal depth of $0.926\,\mathrm{m}$, above critical depth. The corresponding Froude number is $0.716$, below $1$ as expected.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular channel of the same type, of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 60\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, carries $Q = 5.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ on a bed slope of $i = 0.010$, as shown. Classify the bed slope and find the normal depth.

::schema{nodi="M,0,1.35;N,10,0.35" nomi=0 acqua="0,0.75 10,-0.25 10,0.35 0,1.35" solidi="0,0.75 10,-0.25 10,-0.9 0,-0.9" linee="0,1.35 10,0.35" quote="5 0.25,5 0.85,h_0,0.5" etichette="2.4,1.6,free surface;6.4,-0.75,bed slope i = 0.010" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel of width $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with $Q = 5.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, $i = 0.010$ and $k_s = 60\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$"}

$$
q = \frac{5.00}{2.50} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s} ,
\qquad
h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A_c = 2.50 \cdot 0.742 = 1.854\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
C_c = 2.50 + 1.483 = 3.983\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
R_c = \frac{1.854}{3.983} = 0.465\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_c = \frac{5.00}{1.854} = 2.697\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
i_c = \frac{2.697^2}{3600 \cdot 0.3607} = \frac{7.274}{1299} = 0.00560
$$

Since $i = 0.010 > i_c = 0.00560$, the bed has a **steep slope**.

$$
\frac{Q}{k_s\,b\,i^{\frac{1}{2}}} = \frac{5.00}{60 \cdot 2.50 \cdot 0.100} = 0.333
$$
Successive iterations of $h = 0.333^{\frac{3}{5}}\left(\dfrac{2.50 + 2h}{2.50}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}}$ give $0.517\,\mathrm{m}$, $0.594\,\mathrm{m}$, $0.604\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.606\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus $h_0 = 0.606\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check:
$$
A = 2.50 \cdot 0.606 = 1.515\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{5.00}{1.515} = 3.30\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{3.30}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.606}} = 1.35 .
$$
The results $h_0 = 0.606\,\mathrm{m} < h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ and $Fr > 1$ agree with the slope classification.

<div class="spiegazione">

The discharge per unit width is the same as in the preceding exercise. The critical depth is therefore again $0.742\,\mathrm{m}$. It depends on $q$, with no separate dependence on $Q$ and $b$.

The channel is narrower than in the preceding exercise, giving a smaller critical hydraulic radius. The roughness coefficient is also smaller. Both effects increase the critical slope, which is $0.00560$.

Because the bed slope exceeds the critical slope, normal depth at the given discharge is less than critical depth. The channel therefore has a **steep slope**, and its uniform flow is supercritical.

The normal-depth calculation confirms the classification, giving a Froude number of $1.35$. A velocity of $3.30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ requires an abrasion-resistant lining because it would erode an earthen bed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The trapezoidal channel shown has $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, side slope $m = 1.5$, $k_s = 40\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.005$ and carries $Q = 7.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Calculate the critical slope, classify the bed slope and check the result using the normal depth.

::schema{nodi="S,-1.47,0.98;T,3.47,0.98" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 2,0 3.47,0.98 -1.47,0.98" solidi="-3.4,1.5 -2.25,1.5 0,0 2,0 4.25,1.5 5.4,1.5 5.4,-0.6 -3.4,-0.6" pelo="-1.47,3.47,0.98,p_a = 0" quote="1 0,1 0.98,h_0,0;0 -0.6,2 -0.6,2.00 m,0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal channel with $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, side slope $m = 1.5$, $Q = 7.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, $i = 0.005$ and $k_s = 40\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$"}

Critical depth from the condition $Q^2 B = g A^3$, with $A = (2 + 1.5\,h)h$ and $B = 2 + 3h$:

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $B$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $\dfrac{Q^2 B}{g A^3}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0.80$ | $2.560$ | $4.40$ | $1.310$ |
| $0.90$ | $3.015$ | $4.70$ | $0.857$ |
| $0.86$ | $2.829$ | $4.58$ | $1.010$ |

$$
h_c = 0.862\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
A_c = 2.840\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
C_c = 2.00 + 2 \cdot 0.862 \cdot \sqrt{3.25} = 5.109\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
R_c = \frac{2.840}{5.109} = 0.556\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
v_c = \frac{7.00}{2.840} = 2.465\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
i_c = \frac{v_c^2}{k_s^2\,R_c^{\frac{4}{3}}} = \frac{2.465^2}{40^2 \cdot 0.4571} = \frac{6.076}{731.4} = 0.00831
$$

Since $i = 0.005 < i_c = 0.00831$, the bed has a **mild slope**.

Check using normal depth:

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $Q(h)$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0.90$ | $3.015$ | $0.575$ | $5.90$ |
| $1.00$ | $3.500$ | $0.624$ | $7.23$ |
| $0.98$ | $3.401$ | $0.615$ | $6.95$ |

Interpolation between the last two trials gives $h_0 = 0.983\,\mathrm{m} > h_c = 0.862\,\mathrm{m}$, with $v = 2.05\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $Fr = 0.788 < 1$, consistent with the slope classification.

<div class="spiegazione">

For a trapezoidal section, find critical depth by evaluating the ratio $\dfrac{Q^2 B}{g A^3}$, whose critical value is $Fr^2$. The trial depth $0.86\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $1.010$, placing critical depth just above the trial value, at $0.862\,\mathrm{m}$ to three significant figures.

Find the critical slope by applying the Gauckler–Strickler equation at the critical section. The coefficient $k_s = 40\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ is typical of an earthen channel. Its rough walls require a greater slope to achieve critical flow, so $i_c$ is larger than for the lined channels in the preceding exercises.

The bed slope is about three fifths of the critical slope, so the channel has a **mild slope**.

As a check, trial depths give a normal depth of $0.983\,\mathrm{m}$, above critical depth. The Froude number is $0.788$, below $1$. The slope comparison and the depth comparison give the same classification.

</div>
