## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ carries water at a depth of $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the flow area, wetted perimeter, hydraulic radius, top width and hydraulic depth.

::schema{nodi="S,0,1.2;T,2.5,1.2" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 2.5,0 2.5,1.2 0,1.2" solidi="-0.8,1.7 0,1.7 0,0 2.5,0 2.5,1.7 3.3,1.7 3.3,-0.6 -0.8,-0.6" pelo="0,2.5,1.2,p_a = 0" quote="1.25 0,1.25 1.2,1.20 m,0;0 -0.6,2.5 -0.6,2.50 m,0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular section with $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
A = b\,h = 2.50 \cdot 1.20 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
C = b + 2h = 2.50 + 2 \cdot 1.20 = 4.90\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
R = \frac{A}{C} = \frac{3.00}{4.90} = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
B = b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_m = \frac{A}{B} = \frac{3.00}{2.50} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the wetted perimeter by subtracting the free-surface width from the perimeter of the rectangle:
$$
C = 2\,(b + h) - b = 2\,(2.50 + 1.20) - 2.50 = 7.40 - 2.50 = 4.90\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The flow area is the area of the rectangle occupied by water, equal to its width times the water depth.

The wetted perimeter is the length of boundary in contact with water. It includes the bed and both sidewalls up to the free surface. The free surface is excluded because the shear stress acting there is negligible.

The hydraulic radius is $0.612\,\mathrm{m}$, about half the water depth. It is less than $h$ because the sidewalls add perimeter without adding area. In a channel very wide compared with its depth, their contribution becomes negligible and the hydraulic radius approaches the water depth.

In a rectangular section, the top width equals the bottom width. Its **hydraulic depth** $h_m = \dfrac{A}{B}$ therefore equals the water depth. This equality is specific to rectangular sections.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A trapezoidal channel has bottom width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and side slope $m = 1.5$, giving a horizontal run of $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ for every meter of vertical rise. The water depth is $h = 1.40\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Determine the geometric properties of the flow section.

::schema{nodi="S,-2.1,1.4;T,5.1,1.4" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 3,0 5.1,1.4 -2.1,1.4" solidi="-4.5,1.9 -2.85,1.9 0,0 3,0 5.85,1.9 7.5,1.9 7.5,-0.7 -4.5,-0.7" pelo="-2.1,5.1,1.4,p_a = 0" quote="1.5 0,1.5 1.4,1.40 m,0;0 -0.7,3 -0.7,3.00 m,0.5;S,T,B,-0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal section with $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, $h = 1.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and side slope $m = 1.5$"}

$$
A = (b + m\,h)\,h = (3.00 + 1.5 \cdot 1.40) \cdot 1.40 = 5.10 \cdot 1.40 = 7.14\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
C = b + 2h\sqrt{1 + m^2} = 3.00 + 2 \cdot 1.40 \cdot \sqrt{1 + 2.25} = 3.00 + 2.80 \cdot 1.803 = 8.05\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
R = \frac{A}{C} = \frac{7.14}{8.05} = 0.887\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
B = b + 2m\,h = 3.00 + 2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 1.40 = 7.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_m = \frac{A}{B} = \frac{7.14}{7.20} = 0.992\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the area using the trapezoid with parallel sides $b$ and $B$:
$$
A = \frac{b + B}{2}\,h = \frac{3.00 + 7.20}{2} \cdot 1.40 = 5.10 \cdot 1.40 = 7.14\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Divide the flow section into a rectangle of width $b$ and height $h$ and two right triangles with legs $m h$ and $h$. Their combined area is $b h + 2 \cdot \dfrac{m h \cdot h}{2} = (b + m h)h$, the formula used above.

Each sloping bank is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $m h = 2.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h = 1.40\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a wetted length of $h\sqrt{1 + m^2} = 2.52\,\mathrm{m}$. The two banks and the channel bed form the wetted perimeter.

The top width exceeds the bottom width by $2 m h$, the combined horizontal projection of the two banks. The **hydraulic depth** $h_m = 0.992\,\mathrm{m}$ is therefore less than the water depth $1.40\,\mathrm{m}$. For the same maximum depth, the trapezoidal section holds less water than a rectangle as wide as its free surface.

The check uses the trapezoidal area formula with a separately calculated top width, so it verifies both $A$ and $B$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular sewer of diameter $D = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ runs partly full at a water depth of $h = 0.42\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the central angle subtended by the free surface and the geometric properties of the flow section.

::schema{nodi="P,-0.572,-0.18;Q,0.572,-0.18" nomi=0 cerchi="0,0,0.6" acqua="-0.572,-0.180 -0.531,-0.280 -0.472,-0.371 -0.397,-0.450 -0.310,-0.514 -0.212,-0.561 -0.108,-0.590 -0.000,-0.600 0.108,-0.590 0.212,-0.561 0.310,-0.514 0.397,-0.450 0.472,-0.371 0.531,-0.280 0.572,-0.180" linee="-0.572,-0.18 0.572,-0.18" quote="0 -0.6,0 -0.18,0.42 m,-0.9;P,Q,B,-0.6" etichette="0.06,-0.3,θ" frecce="0,0,-0.555,-0.175,;0,0,0.555,-0.175," riferimento=0 titolo="Circular section of diameter $D = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and water depth $h = 0.42\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\cos\frac{\theta}{2} = 1 - \frac{2h}{D} = 1 - \frac{2 \cdot 0.42}{1.20} = 1 - 0.70 = 0.30
$$
$$
\frac{\theta}{2} = \arccos 0.30 = 1.266\,\mathrm{rad} ,
\qquad
\theta = 2.532\,\mathrm{rad} = 145.1^\circ
$$
$$
A = \frac{D^2}{8}\,(\theta - \sin\theta) = \frac{1.44}{8}\,(2.532 - 0.572) = 0.180 \cdot 1.960 = 0.353\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
C = \frac{D\,\theta}{2} = \frac{1.20 \cdot 2.532}{2} = 1.519\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
R = \frac{A}{C} = \frac{0.353}{1.519} = 0.232\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
B = D\sin\frac{\theta}{2} = 1.20 \cdot 0.954 = 1.145\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_m = \frac{A}{B} = \frac{0.353}{1.145} = 0.308\,\mathrm{m}
$$

To check the flow area, subtract the dry circular segment subtending angle $2\pi - \theta = 3.751\,\mathrm{rad}$ from the area of the full circle:
$$
A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} - \frac{D^2}{8}\,\big[(2\pi - \theta) - \sin(2\pi - \theta)\big] = 1.131 - 0.180 \cdot (3.751 + 0.572) = 1.131 - 0.778 = 0.353\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle $\theta$ is the central angle subtended by the free-surface chord, measured through the wetted part of the section. To obtain $\cos\dfrac{\theta}{2} = 1 - \dfrac{2h}{D}$, note that the distance from the center to the water surface is $\dfrac{D}{2} - h$, equal to the radius times the cosine of half the central angle.

The relative depth is $\dfrac{h}{D} = 0.35$, so the water depth is just over one third of the diameter. The central angle is therefore less than $\pi$, and the flow area is less than half the area of the circle, which is $0.565\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

The wetted perimeter is the arc length subtended by $\theta$ and equals $\dfrac{D\theta}{2}$. The free-surface chord is excluded. The hydraulic radius is $0.232\,\mathrm{m}$, about half the water depth.

In the check, the dry circular segment subtends the remaining angle $2\pi - \theta$. Its sine is $-\sin\theta$, explaining the change of sign in the formula. The wet and dry segment areas add to the full circle area, confirming the result.

</div>
