## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a depth of $h_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Classify the flow and find the alternate depth.

::schema{nodi="S,0,2;T,4,2" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 4,0 4,2 0,2" solidi="-1.2,2.6 0,2.6 0,0 4,0 4,2.6 5.2,2.6 5.2,-0.7 -1.2,-0.7" pelo="0,4,2,p_a = 0" quote="2 0,2 2,2.00 m,0;0 -0.7,4 -0.7,4.00 m,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel with $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and $h_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
q = \frac{10.0}{4.00} = 2.50\,\mathrm{m^2/s} ,
\qquad
v_1 = \frac{q}{h_1} = \frac{2.50}{2.00} = 1.25\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Fr_1 = \frac{v_1}{\sqrt{g\,h_1}} = \frac{1.25}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 2.00}} = \frac{1.25}{4.43} = 0.282
$$
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.25}{9.81}} = 0.860\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
E = h_1 + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 2.00 + \frac{1.5625}{19.62} = 2.00 + 0.0797 = 2.080\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h^3 - E\,h^2 + \frac{q^2}{2g} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h^3 - 2.080\,h^2 + 0.3186 = 0
$$
$$
h' = 0.441\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check. At the section with depth $h'$,
$$
v' = \frac{2.50}{0.441} = 5.67\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
E' = 0.441 + \frac{32.15}{19.62} = 0.441 + 1.639 = 2.080\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which equals the specified flow's specific energy, and
$$
Fr' = \frac{5.67}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.441}} = 2.73 > 1 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a rectangular section the hydraulic depth equals the water depth, so the Froude number follows directly from $h_1$. Here $Fr_1 = 0.282 < 1$ and the flow is **subcritical**. Comparing the water depth with the critical depth gives the same conclusion because $h_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m} > h_c = 0.860\,\mathrm{m}$.

Write $E = h + \dfrac{q^2}{2 g h^2}$ and multiply both sides by $h^2$ to obtain the equation for alternate depths. The resulting cubic has two positive roots, corresponding to the two depths with the same specific energy, and a negative root with no physical significance.

One root is the specified depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, which was used to calculate $E$. The other is $h' = 0.441\,\mathrm{m}$, below critical depth, and therefore corresponds to supercritical flow.

The two alternate depths carry the same discharge at the same specific energy. One corresponds to deep, slow flow and the other to shallow, fast flow. The flow can change from one state to the other at a transition such as a sluice gate.

Using the calculated alternate depth reproduces the specific energy $2.080\,\mathrm{m}$. Its Froude number is $2.73$, confirming that the alternate flow is **supercritical**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At a sluice gate in a rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, a discharge of $Q = 7.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ emerges at a depth of $h_1 = 0.500\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Classify the flow and find the alternate depth.

::schema{nodi="A,3,0.5;B,3,2.6" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 10,0 10,0.5 3,0.5 3,2.2 0,2.2" solidi="0,-0.6 10,-0.6 10,0 0,0;2.85,0.5 3.15,0.5 3.15,3 2.85,3" pelo="0,2.9,2.2,;3.1,10,0.5," frecce="7,0.25,9,0.25,v_1" quote="5 0,5 0.5,0.50 m,-0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Flow under a sluice gate in a channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, with discharge $Q = 7.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at depth $h_1 = 0.500\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream"}

$$
q = \frac{7.50}{3.00} = 2.50\,\mathrm{m^2/s} ,
\qquad
v_1 = \frac{2.50}{0.500} = 5.00\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Fr_1 = \frac{5.00}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.500}} = \frac{5.00}{2.215} = 2.26
$$
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.25}{9.81}} = 0.860\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
E = 0.500 + \frac{25.0}{19.62} = 0.500 + 1.274 = 1.774\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h^3 - 1.774\,h^2 + 0.3186 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h'' = 1.658\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check:
$$
v'' = \frac{2.50}{1.658} = 1.508\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
E'' = 1.658 + \frac{2.274}{19.62} = 1.658 + 0.116 = 1.774\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Fr'' = \frac{1.508}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.658}} = 0.374 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The water is shallow and the velocity high downstream of the gate. This gives $Fr_1 = 2.26 > 1$, so the flow is **supercritical**. The critical-depth comparison gives the same result because $h_1 = 0.500\,\mathrm{m} < h_c = 0.860\,\mathrm{m}$.

The specific energy is $1.774\,\mathrm{m}$, more than three times the water depth. In this supercritical flow the velocity head exceeds the pressure head. In the subcritical flow of the preceding exercise the reverse was true.

The alternate depth is the larger root of the cubic, $1.658\,\mathrm{m}$, above critical depth. This is the depth at which the same discharge could flow subcritically with the same specific energy.

An actual transition from supercritical to subcritical flow takes place through a hydraulic jump, which dissipates some energy. The actual depth downstream of the jump is therefore smaller than the alternate depth calculated here. The chapter on steady flow profiles and hydraulic jumps covers this transition.

The alternate depth reproduces the specific energy $1.774\,\mathrm{m}$. Its Froude number is $0.374$, so the corresponding flow would be **subcritical**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The trapezoidal channel shown has $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and side slope $m = 1$ and carries $Q = 9.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a water depth of $h = 1.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Classify the flow, determine the wave speed of small disturbances and find their upstream and downstream propagation velocities relative to the banks.

::schema{nodi="S,-1.3,1.3;T,3.8,1.3" nomi=0 acqua="0,0 2.5,0 3.8,1.3 -1.3,1.3" solidi="-3.2,1.8 -1.8,1.8 0,0 2.5,0 4.3,1.8 5.7,1.8 5.7,-0.6 -3.2,-0.6" pelo="-1.3,3.8,1.3,p_a = 0" quote="1.25 0,1.25 1.3,1.30 m,0;0 -0.6,2.5 -0.6,2.50 m,0.45;S,T,B,-0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal channel with $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, side slope $m = 1$, $h = 1.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and $Q = 9.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

$$
A = (2.50 + 1.30) \cdot 1.30 = 4.94\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
B = 2.50 + 2 \cdot 1.30 = 5.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_m = \frac{4.94}{5.10} = 0.969\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{9.00}{4.94} = 1.82\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
c = \sqrt{g\,h_m} = \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.969} = 3.08\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Fr = \frac{v}{c} = \frac{1.82}{3.08} = 0.591
$$
$$
c - v = 3.08 - 1.82 = 1.26\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
c + v = 3.08 + 1.82 = 4.90\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Check using the form of the Froude number based on flow area and surface width:
$$
Fr^2 = \frac{Q^2 B}{g\,A^3} = \frac{81.0 \cdot 5.10}{9.81 \cdot 4.94^3} = \frac{413.1}{1183} = 0.349 ,
\qquad
Fr = 0.591
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a trapezoidal section, calculate the Froude number using the hydraulic depth $h_m = \dfrac{A}{B}$, here $0.969\,\mathrm{m}$, compared with a water depth of $1.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Using the water depth would underestimate the Froude number and could misclassify a flow close to critical.

Small disturbances travel at $c = \sqrt{g\,h_m} = 3.08\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water, faster than the flow velocity of $1.82\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Thus $Fr = 0.591 < 1$ and the flow is **subcritical**.

A disturbance travels in both directions at speed $c$ relative to the water. Relative to the banks it travels upstream at $c - v = 1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and downstream at $c + v = 4.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The upstream propagation speed is positive, so a downstream change in level, for example at a sluice gate, can travel against the flow and influence upstream sections.

As a check, recalculate the Froude number using $Fr^2 = \dfrac{Q^2 B}{g A^3}$, which does not require separate calculations of velocity and hydraulic depth. It gives the same value, $0.591$.

</div>
