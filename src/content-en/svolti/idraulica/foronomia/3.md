## Exercise 1

In a laboratory test, a constant-level tank discharges to the atmosphere through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ in a thin vertical wall. Its centroid is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, as shown. The measured discharge is $Q = 7.50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the measured jet diameter at the vena contracta is $D_c = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$. Calculate the three orifice coefficients and the head loss through the orifice.

::schema{solidi="-0.3,-0.4 4.3,-0.4 4.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,3.4 -0.3,3.4;4,0 4.3,0 4.3,0.74 4,0.74;4,1.06 4.3,1.06 4.3,3.4 4,3.4" acqua="0,0 4,0 4,3 0,3;4,0.74 4.6,0.78 5.8,0.78 5.8,1.02 4.6,1.02 4,1.06" pelo="0,4,3,free surface" tratteggi="4.6,0.5 4.6,1.3" quote="4 0.9,4 3,2.00 m,-0.7" etichette="5,1.6,vena contracta;6.2,0.9,D_c" titolo="Test on a circular orifice of diameter $D = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ under head $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The discharge and the diameter of the vena contracta are measured"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.050^2}{4} = 1.963 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_c = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.040^2}{4} = 1.257 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
C_c = \frac{A_c}{A} = \left(\frac{D_c}{D}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{40}{50}\right)^2 = 0.640
$$
$$
\sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.00} = 6.264\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\mu = \frac{Q}{A\,\sqrt{2 g h}} = \frac{0.00750}{1.963 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 6.264} = \frac{0.00750}{0.01230} = 0.610
$$
$$
C_v = \frac{\mu}{C_c} = \frac{0.610}{0.640} = 0.953
$$
$$
\Delta H = \left(1 - C_v^2\right) h = \left(1 - 0.908\right) \cdot 2.00 = 0.184\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check using the mean speed at the vena contracta obtained from the measured discharge.
$$
v_c = \frac{Q}{A_c} = \frac{0.00750}{1.257 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 5.97\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
C_v = \frac{v_c}{\sqrt{2 g h}} = \frac{5.97}{6.264} = 0.953
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The test supplies two independent measurements, the discharge and the contracted jet diameter. The three coefficients satisfy $\mu = C_c C_v$, so these two measurements determine all three.

The contraction coefficient is the ratio of the area at the vena contracta to the orifice area. Both sections are circular, so it equals the square of the diameter ratio, $C_c = 0.640$. The individual areas need not be calculated.

Solving the orifice equation for the discharge coefficient gives the ratio of measured discharge to the ideal discharge without contraction or dissipation, $A\sqrt{2gh}$. The result $\mu = 0.610$ agrees with tabulated values for a sharp-edged orifice in a thin wall. The relation between the three coefficients then gives the velocity coefficient $C_v = 0.953$.

Apply Bernoulli’s equation between the free surface and the vena contracta, where gauge pressure is zero. The upstream total head is $h$ and the head at the vena contracta is $\dfrac{v_c^2}{2g} = C_v^2 h$. Their difference is the head loss $\left(1 - C_v^2\right)h = 0.184\,\mathrm{m}$, or **$9.2\%$ of the available head**.

For the check, divide the measured discharge by the measured contracted area, then compare that speed with $\sqrt{2gh}$. This gives $C_v = 0.953$ independently of $\mu$ and $C_c$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A constant-level tank discharges to the atmosphere through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$ in a thin vertical wall, with head $h = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ at its centroid. Relative to the center of the vena contracta, a point on the jet axis lies $x = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ horizontally downstream and $y = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ below it, as shown. The measured discharge is $Q = 10.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Calculate the three orifice coefficients and the contracted jet diameter.

::schema{solidi="-3.3,-0.3 0.3,-0.3 0.3,0 -3.3,0;-3.3,0 -3,0 -3,5.2 -3.3,5.2;0,0 0.3,0 0.3,2.88 0,2.88;0,3.12 0.3,3.12 0.3,5.2 0,5.2" acqua="-3,0 0,0 0,4.8 -3,4.8" pelo="-3,0,4.8,free surface" linee="0,3 0.5,2.9625 1,2.85 1.5,2.6625 2,2.4 2.4,2.136" punti="2,2.4,P" tratteggi="0,2.4 2,2.4;2,2.4 2,3" quote="0 3,0 4.8,1.80 m,-0.6;0 3,2 3,2.00 m,-0.4;2 3,2 2.4,0.60 m,-0.4" titolo="Jet issuing from an orifice under head $h = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$. Point $P$ is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ horizontally and $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ vertically from the vena contracta"}

$$
v_c = x\,\sqrt{\frac{g}{2\,y}} = 2.00 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{9.81}{2 \cdot 0.60}} = 2.00 \cdot \sqrt{8.175} = 2.00 \cdot 2.859 = 5.718\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.80} = 5.943\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
C_v = \frac{5.718}{5.943} = 0.962
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.060^2}{4} = 2.827 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
\mu = \frac{Q}{A\sqrt{2 g h}} = \frac{0.0101}{2.827 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 5.943} = 0.601
$$
$$
C_c = \frac{\mu}{C_v} = \frac{0.601}{0.962} = 0.625,
\qquad
D_c = D\sqrt{C_c} = 60 \cdot \sqrt{0.625} = 47.4\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

Check the head balance between the free surface and the vena contracta.
$$
\frac{v_c^2}{2g} = \frac{5.718^2}{19.62} = 1.667\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h - \frac{v_c^2}{2g} = 1.800 - 1.667 = 0.133\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\left(1 - C_v^2\right) h = \left(1 - 0.926\right) \cdot 1.80 = 0.133\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Beyond the vena contracta the jet moves under gravity alone and follows a parabola. Its horizontal velocity is constant at $v_c$ and its vertical acceleration is constant. Eliminating time from the two equations gives $y = \dfrac{g\,x^2}{2\,v_c^2}$, hence $v_c = x\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2y}}$.

The measured coordinates give $v_c = 5.718\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Comparison with the theoretical speed $\sqrt{2gh} = 5.943\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives $C_v = 0.962$. This measurement requires neither the discharge nor the jet cross-sectional shape.

The measured flow rate gives the discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.601$. Using $\mu = C_c C_v$ then gives $C_c = 0.625$. The area ratio gives the contracted jet diameter $D_c = D\sqrt{C_c} = 47.4\,\mathrm{mm}$, about one-fifth smaller than the orifice diameter.

Check the energy balance. The velocity head at the vena contracta is $1.667\,\mathrm{m}$, so the head loss from the free surface to that section is $0.133\,\mathrm{m}$. The same result follows from $\left(1 - C_v^2\right)h$ by the definition of the velocity coefficient. The loss is positive and less than the available head, as required.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular orifice of dimensions $100 \times 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ has its longer side horizontal and its centroid $1.25\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface of a constant-level tank. To determine its discharge coefficient, the jet is collected in a calibrated vessel, as shown. In $2$ minutes the vessel collects $1.81\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Calculate the discharge coefficient and check the small-orifice approximation.

::schema{solidi="-0.3,0.7 3.3,0.7 3.3,1 -0.3,1;-0.3,1 0,1 0,3.7 -0.3,3.7;3,1 3.3,1 3.3,1.12 3,1.12;3,1.38 3.3,1.38 3.3,3.7 3,3.7;4,-1.6 4.3,-1.6 4.3,0.2 4,0.2;5.9,-1.6 6.2,-1.6 6.2,0.2 5.9,0.2;4,-1.9 6.2,-1.9 6.2,-1.6 4,-1.6" acqua="0,1 3,1 3,3.35 0,3.35;3,1.12 3.6,1.18 4.4,0.7 4.85,-0.6 4.5,-0.6 4.1,0.5 3.5,1.32 3,1.38;4.3,-1.6 5.9,-1.6 5.9,-0.6 4.3,-0.6" pelo="0,3,3.35,free surface;4.3,5.9,-0.6," quote="3 1.25,3 3.35,1.25 m,-0.7" etichette="5.6,0.6,calibrated vessel" titolo="Volumetric measurement of discharge through a rectangular orifice of dimensions $100 \times 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ under head $1.25\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
Q = \frac{V}{t} = \frac{1.81}{120} = 0.01508\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 15.1\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
A = 0.100 \cdot 0.050 = 5.00 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
\sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.25} = 4.952\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\mu = \frac{Q}{A\sqrt{2 g h}} = \frac{0.01508}{5.00 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 4.952} = \frac{0.01508}{0.02476} = 0.609
$$

Check using the large-orifice formula with the same data and the edge depths $h_1 = 1.225\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_2 = 1.275\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
Q = \frac{2}{3}\,\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\left(h_2^{\frac{3}{2}} - h_1^{\frac{3}{2}}\right) = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 0.609 \cdot 0.100 \cdot 4.429 \cdot \left(1.4397 - 1.3558\right)
$$
$$
Q = 0.1798 \cdot 0.0839 = 0.01508\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the collected volume by the collection time gives the mean discharge. Because the tank level is constant, this is also the instantaneous discharge, $Q = 15.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

The discharge coefficient is the measured discharge divided by $A\sqrt{2gh}$, evaluated with the geometric orifice area and the head at its centroid. The result $\mu = 0.609$ is typical of a sharp-edged orifice in a thin wall.

The small-orifice approximation used the centroid head throughout the opening. To check it, take the opening height $50\,\mathrm{mm}$, giving top and bottom edge depths $1.225\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.275\,\mathrm{m}$. Integration over horizontal strips gives the same discharge to four digits. The difference between the two formulas is approximately $\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{24}$ for $\varepsilon = \dfrac{0.050}{2 \cdot 1.25} = 0.02$, less than two parts in one hundred thousand. The **small-orifice approximation is justified**.

</div>
