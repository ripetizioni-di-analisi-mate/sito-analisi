## Exercise 1

A vertical sluice gate in a rectangular channel of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ is raised $a = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bed. The upstream depth is $h = 2.20\,\mathrm{m}$, and the gate discharges freely downstream, as shown. Its coefficients are $\mu = 0.57$ and $C_c = 0.61$. Calculate the discharge, the depth at the vena contracta, and the Froude number at that section.

::schema{solidi="0,-0.35 6,-0.35 6,0 0,0;2.9,0.4 3.1,0.4 3.1,2.9 2.9,2.9" acqua="0,0 2.9,0 2.9,2.2 0,2.2;2.9,0 3.1,0.4 3.5,0.27 6,0.244 6,0 3.1,0" pelo="0,2.9,2.2,;4,5.6,0.244," tratteggi="3.5,0 3.5,0.75" frecce="3.9,0.12,4.7,0.12,v_c" quote="0 0,0 2.2,2.20 m,-0.5;3 0,3 0.4,0.40 m,-0.45" etichette="4.4,0.55,a_c" titolo="Flat gate raised by $a = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ in a channel of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, with upstream water depth $h = 2.20\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
Q = \mu\,b\,a\,\sqrt{2 g h} = 0.57 \cdot 2.50 \cdot 0.40 \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.20}
$$
$$
\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.20} = \sqrt{43.16} = 6.571\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
Q = 0.57 \cdot 1.00 \cdot 6.571 = 3.75\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
a_c = C_c\,a = 0.61 \cdot 0.40 = 0.244\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
v_c = \frac{Q}{b\,a_c} = \frac{3.75}{2.50 \cdot 0.244} = 6.14\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Fr_c = \frac{v_c}{\sqrt{g\,a_c}} = \frac{6.14}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.244}} = \frac{6.14}{1.547} = 3.97
$$

Check the total-head balance between the upstream section and the vena contracta.
$$
v_m = \frac{Q}{b\,h} = \frac{3.75}{2.50 \cdot 2.20} = 0.681\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
H_m = 2.20 + \frac{0.681^2}{19.62} = 2.224\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_c = 0.244 + \frac{6.14^2}{19.62} = 0.244 + 1.921 = 2.165\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta H = 2.224 - 2.165 = 0.059\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gate leaves a rectangular opening of area $b\,a = 1.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ entirely below the upstream surface. On the downstream side the surface stays below the lower edge of the gate, so the discharge is **free** and is calculated using the upstream head.

The discharge is $Q = 3.75\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Downstream of the gate the jet contracts vertically to depth $a_c = C_c a = 0.244\,\mathrm{m}$. Continuity gives the velocity by dividing the discharge by the contracted area $b\,a_c$, yielding $6.14\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The Froude number at the vena contracta is $Fr_c = 3.97$. It exceeds one, so the flow immediately downstream of the gate is **supercritical**, as expected for free discharge. A hydraulic jump connects it to the downstream flow.

Compare the total heads at the two sections. The upstream head is $2.224\,\mathrm{m}$, the sum of the water depth and the small but finite approach velocity head. At the vena contracta it is $2.165\,\mathrm{m}$. The head loss $0.059\,\mathrm{m}$ is positive in the flow direction and amounts to $2.6\%$ of the available head, consistent with flow beneath a vertical sluice gate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two tanks are connected by a circular orifice of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ in their dividing wall. It is fully submerged on both sides, as shown. The upstream water surface is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the orifice centroid and the downstream surface is $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above it. Both levels are constant. The discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.60$ and the contraction coefficient is $C_c = 0.62$. Calculate the discharge.

::schema{solidi="-0.35,-0.4 9.35,-0.4 9.35,0 -0.35,0;-0.35,0 0,0 0,5 -0.35,5;9,0 9.35,0 9.35,5 9,5;4.4,0 4.6,0 4.6,0.85 4.4,0.85;4.4,1.15 4.6,1.15 4.6,5 4.4,5" acqua="0,0 4.4,0 4.4,4 0,4;4.6,0 9,0 9,2.5 4.6,2.5;4.4,0.85 4.6,0.85 4.6,1.15 4.4,1.15" pelo="0,4.4,4,;4.6,9,2.5," tratteggi="4.6,4 7.6,4" quote="7 2.5,7 4,1.50 m,0.6;4.4 1,4.4 4,4.00 m,-0.6;4.6 1,4.6 2.5,2.50 m,0.6" titolo="Circular submerged orifice of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ between two tanks whose free surfaces are $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above its centroid"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.200^2}{4} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
\Delta h = 4.00 - 2.50 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.50} = 5.425\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta h} = 0.60 \cdot 0.0314 \cdot 5.425 = 0.1023\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 102\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check using the velocity coefficient obtained from the discharge and the contracted jet area.
$$
A_c = C_c\,A = 0.62 \cdot 0.0314 = 0.0195\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
v_c = \frac{Q}{A_c} = \frac{0.1023}{0.0195} = 5.25\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
C_v = \frac{v_c}{\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta h}} = \frac{5.25}{5.425} = 0.968 < 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The orifice is under water on both sides and therefore operates under **submerged discharge**. The jet at the vena contracta is subject to the hydrostatic pressure of the downstream tank. Applying Bernoulli’s equation from the upstream free surface to the vena contracta subtracts the two piezometric heads, leaving the elevation difference between the free surfaces.

The driving head is therefore $\Delta h = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The depth $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of the orifice below the upstream free surface alone would apply to free discharge. The actual discharge is $Q = 102\,\mathrm{L/s}$. If the same orifice discharged to the atmosphere under head $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, it would pass $167\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

Check consistency with the assumed coefficients. Dividing discharge by the contracted jet area gives the speed $5.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the vena contracta. Its ratio to the theoretical speed $\sqrt{2g\Delta h}$ gives $C_v = 0.968$, less than one as required and close to tabulated values for a thin-wall orifice.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vertical sluice gate in a rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is raised $a = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bed. The upstream depth is $2.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and the downstream depth is $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$, so the gate discharges under submerged conditions, as shown. Its discharge coefficient based on the water-level difference is $\mu = 0.60$. Determine the discharge and the head loss across the gate.

::schema{solidi="0,-0.35 7,-0.35 7,0 0,0;3.4,0.5 3.6,0.5 3.6,3.4 3.4,3.4" acqua="0,0 3.4,0 3.4,2.8 0,2.8;3.6,0 7,0 7,1.8 3.6,1.8;3.4,0 3.6,0 3.6,0.5 3.4,0.5" pelo="0,3.4,2.8,;3.6,7,1.8," tratteggi="3.6,2.8 5.8,2.8" quote="5 1.8,5 2.8,1.00 m,0.55;0 0,0 2.8,2.80 m,-0.5;7 0,7 1.8,1.80 m,0.5;3.5 0,3.5 0.5,0.50 m,-0.45" titolo="Submerged gate with water depths $2.80\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream and $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream and an opening of height $a = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\Delta h = 2.80 - 1.80 = 1.00\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = b\,a = 3.00 \cdot 0.50 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta h} = 0.60 \cdot 1.50 \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.00} = 0.90 \cdot 4.429 = 3.99\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
v_m = \frac{Q}{b \cdot 2.80} = \frac{3.99}{8.40} = 0.475\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_v = \frac{Q}{b \cdot 1.80} = \frac{3.99}{5.40} = 0.738\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
H_m = 2.80 + \frac{0.475^2}{19.62} = 2.811\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
H_v = 1.80 + \frac{0.738^2}{19.62} = 1.828\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta H = H_m - H_v = 2.811 - 1.828 = 0.983\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check by comparing the difference in free-surface elevations with the head loss.
$$
\Delta h - \Delta H = 1.000 - 0.983 = 0.017\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\frac{v_v^2 - v_m^2}{2g} = \frac{0.545 - 0.225}{19.62} = 0.016\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The downstream surface is above the lower edge of the gate, so the opening is submerged and the driving head equals the difference $\Delta h = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ between the free-surface elevations. The discharge is $Q = 3.99\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

Use continuity to find the mean velocities upstream and downstream by dividing discharge by the corresponding flow areas. Both speeds are below $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ because the channel sections are much larger than the gate opening. Taking the bed as the datum, the total head at each section is the sum of water depth and velocity head.

The head loss $\Delta H = 0.983\,\mathrm{m}$ accounts for nearly all the water-level difference. Flow accelerates through the opening, then expands abruptly into the relatively still downstream water, dissipating nearly all the kinetic energy it gained.

Check the balance by subtracting head loss from the water-level difference. The result must equal the downstream increase in velocity head. The two values, $0.017\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.016\,\mathrm{m}$, agree to within the rounding of the velocities to three decimal places.

</div>
