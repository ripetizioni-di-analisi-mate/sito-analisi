## Exercise 1

A sharp-crested rectangular weir is installed in a channel. The downstream view shows its horizontal crest of width $b = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and its ventilated nappe. The measured upstream head is $h = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ and the discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.42$. Calculate the discharge.

::schema{solidi="0,0 1.5,0 1.5,3 0,3;4.5,0 6,0 6,3 4.5,3;1.5,0 4.5,0 4.5,1.6 1.5,1.6" acqua="1.5,1.6 4.5,1.6 4.5,2.225 1.5,2.225" pelo="1.5,4.5,2.225," quote="1.5 1.6,1.5 2.225,0.25 m,-0.45;1.5 1.6,4.5 1.6,1.20 m,-0.9" titolo="Downstream view of a sharp-crested rectangular weir with crest width $b = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and head $h = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.25} = \sqrt{4.905} = 2.21\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = \mu\,b\,h\,\sqrt{2 g h} = 0.42 \cdot 1.20 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 2.21 = 0.279\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

Check using the integral form of the same equation, with $\mu' = \dfrac{3}{2}\,\mu = 0.63$.
$$
Q = \frac{2}{3}\,\mu'\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\;h^{\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 0.63 \cdot 1.20 \cdot 4.429 \cdot 0.25^{\frac{3}{2}} = 0.42 \cdot 1.20 \cdot 4.429 \cdot 0.125 = 0.279\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The weir is sharp-crested and its nappe is ventilated. For the given weir, discharge therefore depends only on head and can be calculated using **Bazin’s formula**. The head $h$ is the height of the water surface above the crest, measured far enough upstream to avoid drawdown near the weir.

The equation uses the crest width, the head, and the theoretical speed $\sqrt{2gh}$ corresponding to that head. The coefficient $\mu = 0.42$ accounts for nappe contraction and energy dissipation and applies for heads between $0.05$ and $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$. The discharge is $Q = 0.279\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

As a check, integrate the flow through horizontal strips between the crest and the free surface. This gives $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\mu' b \sqrt{2g}\,h^{\frac{3}{2}}$, the expression from which Bazin’s formula follows. Since $\mu = \dfrac{2}{3}\mu'$, the two expressions are equivalent and give the same numerical result. The mean speed above the crest, $\dfrac{Q}{b\,h} = \dfrac{0.279}{1.20 \cdot 0.25} = 0.93\,\mathrm{m/s}$, also has a reasonable magnitude for channel flow.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sharp-crested Thomson V-notch weir in a laboratory channel has included angle $\theta = 90^\circ$ and coefficient $\mu = 0.60$, as shown in the downstream view. The head measured above the vertex is $h = 0.18\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the discharge and the relative discharge error caused by a head reading error of $1\,\mathrm{mm}$.

::schema{solidi="0,0 3,0 3,0.6 0.6,3 0,3;3,0 6,0 6,3 5.4,3 3,0.6" acqua="3,0.6 4.8,2.4 1.2,2.4" pelo="1.2,4.8,2.4," tratteggi="-0.5,0.6 3,0.6;-0.5,2.4 1.2,2.4" quote="0 0.6,0 2.4,0.18 m,-0.55" etichette="3,1.05,θ = 90°" titolo="Downstream view of a Thomson V-notch weir with head $h = 0.18\,\mathrm{m}$ measured above the vertex"}

$$
Q = \frac{8}{15}\,\mu\,\tan\frac{\theta}{2}\,\sqrt{2g}\;h^{\frac{5}{2}}
$$
$$
\tan\frac{90^\circ}{2} = 1,
\qquad
\frac{8}{15} \cdot 0.60 \cdot 1 \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81} = 0.32 \cdot 4.429 = 1.417
$$
$$
h^{\frac{5}{2}} = 0.18^2 \cdot \sqrt{0.18} = 0.0324 \cdot 0.4243 = 0.01375\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}}
$$
$$
Q = 1.417 \cdot 0.01375 = 0.0195\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 19.5\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
\frac{dQ}{Q} = \frac{5}{2}\,\frac{dh}{h} = 2.5 \cdot \frac{0.001}{0.18} = 0.0139 = 1.4\%
$$

Check using the practical Thomson formula for a $90^\circ$ notch, $Q = 1.42\,h^{\frac{5}{2}}$.
$$
Q = 1.42 \cdot 0.01375 = 0.0195\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The width of the V-notch increases with elevation, so discharge varies as the $\dfrac{5}{2}$ power of the head rather than the $\dfrac{3}{2}$ power. With $\theta = 90^\circ$, the tangent of the half-angle is $1$. The numerical coefficient reduces to $\dfrac{8}{15}\,\mu\sqrt{2g} = 1.417$, giving the value $1.42$ used in the practical formula.

Evaluate the power $h^{\frac{5}{2}}$ as $h^2\sqrt{h}$ to obtain $0.0324 \cdot 0.4243 = 0.01375\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}}$. The discharge is $Q = 19.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

Differentiating $Q = k\,h^{\frac{5}{2}}$ gives the relative error $\dfrac{dQ}{Q} = \dfrac{5}{2}\dfrac{dh}{h}$. A one-millimeter error in a head of $180\,\mathrm{mm}$ therefore produces a discharge error of **$1.4\%$**. The exponent amplifies errors in the head, so the gauge must be read carefully.

For a check, use the practical formula $Q = 1.42\,h^{\frac{5}{2}}$, obtained from the general expression for $\theta = 90^\circ$ and $\mu = 0.60$. It gives the same discharge.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sharp-crested weir is to measure a discharge $Q = 85\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Two instruments are available. The rectangular weir shown has crest width $b = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\mu = 0.42$. The Thomson V-notch weir has $\theta = 90^\circ$ and $\mu = 0.60$. Calculate the head for each and determine which yields the smaller discharge error if head can be read to within $1\,\mathrm{mm}$.

::schema{solidi="0,0 1.5,0 1.5,3 0,3;4.5,0 6,0 6,3 4.5,3;1.5,0 4.5,0 4.5,1.6 1.5,1.6" acqua="1.5,1.6 4.5,1.6 4.5,2.4 1.5,2.4" pelo="1.5,4.5,2.4," quote="1.5 1.6,1.5 2.4,h,-0.45;1.5 1.6,4.5 1.6,0.80 m,-1.1" titolo="Rectangular weir with crest width $b = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and unknown head $h$"}

$$
Q = \mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\;h^{\frac{3}{2}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h = \left(\frac{Q}{\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}
$$
$$
\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g} = 0.42 \cdot 0.80 \cdot 4.429 = 1.488\,\mathrm{m^{3/2}/s}
$$
$$
h_r = \left(\frac{0.085}{1.488}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = \left(0.05713\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 0.148\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{8}{15}\,\mu\sqrt{2g} = 1.417\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}/s},
\qquad
h_t = \left(\frac{0.085}{1.417}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}} = \left(0.05998\right)^{\frac{2}{5}} = 0.324\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\left(\frac{dQ}{Q}\right)_r = \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{0.001}{0.148} = 1.0\%,
\qquad
\left(\frac{dQ}{Q}\right)_t = \frac{5}{2} \cdot \frac{0.001}{0.324} = 0.77\%
$$

Check by substituting each head into its corresponding discharge equation.
$$
1.488 \cdot 0.148^{\frac{3}{2}} = 1.488 \cdot 0.05694 = 0.0847\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
1.417 \cdot 0.324^{\frac{5}{2}} = 1.417 \cdot 0.05977 = 0.0847\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the discharge is known and the head is required. Both weir equations have the form $Q = k\,h^{n}$, giving $h = \left(\dfrac{Q}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}$, with $n = \dfrac{3}{2}$ for the rectangular weir and $n = \dfrac{5}{2}$ for the V-notch weir.

For the rectangular weir the constant is $k = \mu b \sqrt{2g} = 1.488$ and the head is $h_r = 0.148\,\mathrm{m}$. For the V-notch weir the constant is $k = \dfrac{8}{15}\mu\sqrt{2g} = 1.417$ and the head is $h_t = 0.324\,\mathrm{m}$, more than twice as great.

The relative discharge error is $n\,\dfrac{dh}{h}$. Although the V-notch weir has a larger exponent, it operates at more than twice the head. The larger head is the stronger effect, giving an error of **$0.77\%$** compared with $1.0\%$ for the rectangular weir. The V-notch weir is therefore preferable for small discharges.

Substituting each head into its own discharge formula gives $0.0847\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in both cases. This reproduces the initial $85\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to within the rounding of the heads to three decimal places.

</div>
