## Exercise 1

A tank of constant rectangular plan dimensions $3.00 \times 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ drains through a circular bottom orifice of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\mu = 0.61$, as shown. There is no inflow. The initial water surface is $h_0 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the orifice centroid. Find the time for complete drainage and the time for the initial head to fall by half.

::schema{solidi="-0.3,0 0,0 0,4.2 -0.3,4.2;3,0 3.3,0 3.3,4.2 3,4.2;-0.3,-0.3 1.3,-0.3 1.3,0 -0.3,0;1.7,-0.3 3.3,-0.3 3.3,0 1.7,0" acqua="0,0 3,0 3,3 0,3;1.3,0 1.7,0 1.64,-1.4 1.36,-1.4" pelo="0,3,3,free surface" quote="3 0,3 3,3.00 m,0.6" etichette="2.5,-1.0,D = 100 mm" titolo="Prismatic tank of plan dimensions $3.00 \times 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with a bottom orifice and an initial water level of $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\Omega = 3.00 \cdot 4.00 = 12.0\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.100^2}{4} = 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
\mu\,A\,\sqrt{2g} = 0.61 \cdot 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 4.429 = 0.02122\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}
$$
$$
t_{\mathrm{tot}} = \frac{2\,\Omega\sqrt{h_0}}{\mu A \sqrt{2g}} = \frac{2 \cdot 12.0 \cdot \sqrt{3.00}}{0.02122} = \frac{41.57}{0.02122} = 1959\,\mathrm{s} = 32.6\,\mathrm{min}
$$
$$
t_{1/2} = \frac{2\,\Omega\left(\sqrt{3.00} - \sqrt{1.50}\right)}{\mu A \sqrt{2g}} = \frac{24.0 \cdot \left(1.7321 - 1.2247\right)}{0.02122} = \frac{12.18}{0.02122} = 574\,\mathrm{s} = 9.6\,\mathrm{min}
$$

Check using the average discharge.
$$
Q_0 = \mu A \sqrt{2 g h_0} = 0.02122 \cdot 1.7321 = 0.03676\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
\frac{Q_0}{2} = 0.01838\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_m = \frac{\Omega\,h_0}{t_{\mathrm{tot}}} = \frac{12.0 \cdot 3.00}{1959} = \frac{36.0}{1959} = 0.01838\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Without inflow, the water level and discharge both decrease. The orifice area is less than one-thousandth of the tank’s plan area. The surface therefore falls slowly enough for the orifice formula to give the discharge at each instant using the instantaneous head.

The factor $\mu A \sqrt{2g} = 0.02122\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}$ occurs throughout the draining-time equations, so calculate it first. Multiplying it by $\sqrt{h}$ gives the instantaneous discharge.

The complete draining time is $t_{\mathrm{tot}} = 1959\,\mathrm{s}$, or **$32.6$ minutes**. The head falls by half in $574\,\mathrm{s}$, or $9.6$ minutes. The first meter and a half drains in less than one-third of the total time because the early head and discharge are greater. The ratio of the two times is $1 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = 0.293$, independent of the problem data.

Compare two independent evaluations of average discharge. The initial volume $36.0\,\mathrm{m^3}$ divided by the draining time gives $0.01838\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, exactly half the initial discharge. This agrees with the relation between the mean and endpoint discharges for $h_1 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A circular cylindrical tank of diameter $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ has a circular side orifice of diameter $D = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\mu = 0.60$. The orifice centroid lies $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor, as shown. With no inflow, how long does the water level take to fall from $2.90\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor?

::schema{solidi="-0.3,-0.3 3.3,-0.3 3.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,3.4 -0.3,3.4;3,0 3.3,0 3.3,0.37 3,0.37;3,0.63 3.3,0.63 3.3,3.4 3,3.4" acqua="0,0 3,0 3,2.9 0,2.9;3,0.37 3.5,0.41 4.4,0.41 4.4,0.59 3.5,0.59 3,0.63" pelo="0,3,2.9,initial level" tratteggi="0,1 3,1" quote="3 0.5,3 2.9,2.40 m,-0.6;0 0,0 0.5,0.50 m,-0.5;0 0,0 1,1.00 m,-1.5" etichette="1.5,1.15,final level" titolo="Cylindrical tank of diameter $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with a wall orifice $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. The level falls from $2.90\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\Omega = \frac{\pi \cdot 3.00^2}{4} = 7.069\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.080^2}{4} = 5.027 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
h_0 = 2.90 - 0.50 = 2.40\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_1 = 1.00 - 0.50 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\mu A \sqrt{2g} = 0.60 \cdot 5.027 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 4.429 = 0.01336\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}
$$
$$
t = \frac{2 \cdot 7.069 \cdot \left(\sqrt{2.40} - \sqrt{0.50}\right)}{0.01336} = \frac{14.14 \cdot \left(1.5492 - 0.7071\right)}{0.01336} = \frac{11.90}{0.01336} = 891\,\mathrm{s} = 14.9\,\mathrm{min}
$$

Check using the average discharge and the initial and final discharges.
$$
V = \Omega\,(2.90 - 1.00) = 7.069 \cdot 1.90 = 13.43\,\mathrm{m^3},
\qquad
Q_m = \frac{13.43}{891} = 0.01507\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_0 = 0.01336 \cdot 1.5492 = 0.02070\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
Q_1 = 0.01336 \cdot 0.7071 = 0.00945\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
\frac{Q_0 + Q_1}{2} = 0.01507\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The orifice is in the sidewall. Measure the head from its centroid to the water surface by subtracting $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from the given water levels above the floor. This gives $h_0 = 2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_1 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The draining formula applies while the opening remains wholly underwater, with the surface above its top edge. The final level is $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the centroid, so this condition is **satisfied**.

The cylindrical tank has the same plan area at every elevation, so the constant-area draining formula applies. The time is $t = 891\,\mathrm{s}$, or $14.9$ minutes.

Check the mean discharge against the endpoint values. The discharged volume is the plan area times the fall in level, $13.43\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Dividing it by the elapsed time gives $0.01507\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, equal to the arithmetic mean of the initial and final discharges as predicted.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A tank of constant plan area $\Omega = 20.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ has a circular bottom orifice of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\mu = 0.60$ and receives a steady inflow $Q_e = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$, as shown. The initial surface is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the orifice centroid. Determine whether the level rises or falls, find its equilibrium value, and calculate the time for the level to fall to $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

::schema{solidi="-0.3,0 0,0 0,4.6 -0.3,4.6;3,0 3.3,0 3.3,4.6 3,4.6;-0.3,-0.3 1.3,-0.3 1.3,0 -0.3,0;1.7,-0.3 3.3,-0.3 3.3,0 1.7,0" acqua="0,0 3,0 3,4 0,4;1.3,0 1.7,0 1.64,-1.4 1.36,-1.4;0.6,4 0.9,4 0.9,5.4 0.6,5.4" pelo="0,3,4,free surface" tratteggi="0,3 3,3" frecce="0.75,5.9,0.75,5.2,Q_e" quote="3 0,3 4,4.00 m,0.6;3 0,3 3,3.00 m,1.6" etichette="1.5,-1.75,D = 150 mm" titolo="Tank receiving a constant inflow $Q_e = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$, with a bottom orifice of diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.150^2}{4} = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
k = \mu A \sqrt{2g} = 0.60 \cdot 0.01767 \cdot 4.429 = 0.04696\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}
$$
$$
Q_u(4.00) = k\sqrt{h_0} = 0.04696 \cdot 2.00 = 0.0939\,\mathrm{m^3/s} > Q_e = 0.040\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
k\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{eq}}} = Q_e
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h_{\mathrm{eq}} = \left(\frac{Q_e}{k}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{0.040}{0.04696}\right)^2 = 0.8517^2 = 0.725\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Omega\,\frac{dh}{dt} = Q_e - k\sqrt{h}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = \int_{h_1}^{h_0} \frac{\Omega\;dh}{k\sqrt{h} - Q_e}
$$
$$
t = \frac{2\,\Omega}{k}\left(\sqrt{h_0} - \sqrt{h_1}\right) + \frac{2\,\Omega\,Q_e}{k^2}\,\ln\frac{k\sqrt{h_0} - Q_e}{k\sqrt{h_1} - Q_e}
$$
$$
\frac{2\,\Omega}{k} = \frac{40.0}{0.04696} = 851.7\,\mathrm{s/m^{1/2}},
\qquad
\frac{2\,\Omega\,Q_e}{k^2} = \frac{1.600}{0.002206} = 725.4\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\frac{k\sqrt{h_0} - Q_e}{k\sqrt{h_1} - Q_e} = \frac{0.09393 - 0.04000}{0.08134 - 0.04000} = \frac{0.05393}{0.04135} = 1.3044,
\qquad
\ln 1.3044 = 0.2657
$$
$$
t = 851.7 \cdot \left(2.0000 - 1.7321\right) + 725.4 \cdot 0.2657 = 228.2 + 192.7 = 421\,\mathrm{s} = 7.0\,\mathrm{min}
$$

Compare with the time the same tank would take without an inflow.
$$
t_0 = \frac{2\,\Omega}{k}\left(\sqrt{h_0} - \sqrt{h_1}\right) = 228\,\mathrm{s} < 421\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume balance now includes the inflow. During time $dt$ the tank receives $Q_e\,dt$ and discharges $k\sqrt{h}\,dt$, so the water level satisfies $\Omega\,\dfrac{dh}{dt} = Q_e - k\sqrt{h}$.

At the initial level, the outflow $93.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$ exceeds the inflow $40\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The right-hand side is negative, so the **water level falls**. The equilibrium level is $h_{\mathrm{eq}} = 0.725\,\mathrm{m}$, where inflow and outflow are equal. The tank approaches this level without ever draining completely.

The equation is separable. Substituting $u = \sqrt{h}$, with $dh = 2u\,du$, gives the integrand $\dfrac{2\,\Omega\,u}{k\,u - Q_e}$. It splits into a constant plus a fraction with constant numerator, so its integral contains a linear term and a logarithmic term.

The elapsed time is $t = 421\,\mathrm{s}$, or $7.0$ minutes.

Without inflow the time would consist only of the linear term, $228\,\mathrm{s}$. Inflow slows the fall in level, so the actual time must be longer. The positive logarithmic term gives the extra time due to the inflow.

</div>
