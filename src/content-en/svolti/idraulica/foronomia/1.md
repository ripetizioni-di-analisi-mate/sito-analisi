## Exercise 1

A constant-level tank discharges to the atmosphere through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ in a thin vertical wall. Its center is $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$ below the surface, as shown. Given discharge coefficient $\mu = 0.61$ and contraction coefficient $C_c = 0.63$, find the discharge.

::schema{solidi="-0.3,-0.4 4.3,-0.4 4.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,4 -0.3,4;4,0 4.3,0 4.3,0.26 4,0.26;4,0.54 4.3,0.54 4.3,4 4,4" acqua="0,0 4,0 4,3.6 0,3.6;4,0.26 4.5,0.3 5.6,0.3 5.6,0.5 4.5,0.5 4,0.54" pelo="0,4,3.6,free surface" quote="4 0.4,4 3.6,3.20 m,-0.7" titolo="Constant-head tank with a circular orifice of diameter $D = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ in a thin vertical wall"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.080^2}{4} = 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
\sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.20} = \sqrt{62.78} = 7.92\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g h} = 0.61 \cdot 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 7.92 = 0.0243\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 24.3\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check using the area and velocity at the vena contracta:
$$
C_v = \frac{\mu}{C_c} = \frac{0.61}{0.63} = 0.968
$$
$$
A_c = C_c\,A = 0.63 \cdot 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3} = 3.17 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
v_c = C_v\sqrt{2 g h} = 0.968 \cdot 7.92 = 7.67\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = A_c\,v_c = 3.17 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 7.67 = 0.0243\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant tank level gives steady flow and maintains a head of $h = 3.20\,\mathrm{m}$ at the orifice. The opening has height $80\,\mathrm{mm}$ under a centroid head of $3.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Their ratio is only $0.025$, justifying the **small-orifice approximation**.

The ideal velocity $\sqrt{2gh}$ at the vena contracta follows from the head at the orifice centroid. The depth of the lower edge would give a different velocity. The discharge coefficient $\mu$ accounts for both the velocity reduction and the contraction of the jet. The area where the liquid reaches $v_c$ is smaller than the orifice itself.

The discharge is $Q = 24.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

An independent calculation separates contraction and velocity losses. The relation $\mu = C_c C_v$ gives $C_v = 0.968$, from which the jet area and speed at the vena contracta follow. Their product recovers the same discharge because $A_c v_c = C_c A \cdot C_v \sqrt{2gh} = \mu A \sqrt{2gh}$. The contracted jet diameter is $D\sqrt{C_c} = 63.5\,\mathrm{mm}$, giving Reynolds number $\dfrac{7.67 \cdot 0.0635}{10^{-6}} = 4.9 \cdot 10^5$ at that section. The flow is fully turbulent, consistent with the tabulated coefficients used here.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A closed tank contains water beneath air at gauge pressure $p_0 = 20\,\mathrm{kPa}$. A square orifice of side $\ell = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$ in the vertical wall is centered $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, as shown. The orifice discharges to the atmosphere with $\mu = 0.60$. Find the discharge.

::schema{solidi="-0.3,-0.4 4.3,-0.4 4.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,4.2 -0.3,4.2;4,0 4.3,0 4.3,0.26 4,0.26;4,0.54 4.3,0.54 4.3,4.2 4,4.2;-0.3,4.2 4.3,4.2 4.3,4.5 -0.3,4.5" acqua="0,0 4,0 4,1.9 0,1.9;4,0.26 4.5,0.3 5.6,0.3 5.6,0.5 4.5,0.5 4,0.54" pelo="0,4,1.9,free surface" quote="4 0.4,4 1.9,1.50 m,-0.7" etichette="2,3.1,air at p_0 = 20 kPa" titolo="Closed tank with pressurized air above the free surface and a square orifice of side $\ell = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A = \ell^2 = 0.060^2 = 3.60 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
h = \frac{p_0}{\gamma} + 1.50 = \frac{20}{9.81} + 1.50 = 2.04 + 1.50 = 3.54\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.54} = 8.33\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g h} = 0.60 \cdot 3.60 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 8.33 = 0.0180\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 18.0\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the pressure at the orifice centroid:
$$
p_G = p_0 + \gamma \cdot 1.50 = 20 + 9.81 \cdot 1.50 = 34.7\,\mathrm{kPa},
\qquad
\frac{p_G}{\gamma} = \frac{34.7}{9.81} = 3.54\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressurized air supplies additional head equal to its gauge pressure expressed as a water-column height. Apply Bernoulli’s equation from the free surface to the vena contracta. The upstream total head is $\dfrac{p_0}{\gamma} + 1.50$ instead of $1.50$ because the gauge pressure at the free surface is positive.

The pressure $20\,\mathrm{kPa}$ corresponds to a water column of height $\dfrac{20}{9.81} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m}$. The effective head at the orifice is therefore $3.54\,\mathrm{m}$, more than twice its geometric depth. The discharge is $Q = 18.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. With the tank open to the atmosphere, it would be $0.60 \cdot 3.60 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.50} = 11.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

Check the effective head by applying the hydrostatic pressure law from the free surface, where the gauge pressure is $p_0$, to the orifice centroid. Dividing the resulting gauge pressure by the specific weight gives $3.54\,\mathrm{m}$, the head used in the discharge calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two vertically spaced circular orifices in a vertical wall of a constant-level tank both have diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\mu = 0.61$. Their centers are $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, as shown. Find their combined discharge and the common head at which the pair would deliver the same total discharge.

::schema{solidi="-0.3,-0.4 4.3,-0.4 4.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,5.4 -0.3,5.4;4,0 4.3,0 4.3,0.36 4,0.36;4,0.64 4.3,0.64 4.3,2.86 4,2.86;4,3.14 4.3,3.14 4.3,5.4 4,5.4" acqua="0,0 4,0 4,5 0,5;4,0.36 4.5,0.4 5.7,0.4 5.7,0.6 4.5,0.6 4,0.64;4,2.86 4.5,2.9 5.7,2.9 5.7,3.1 4.5,3.1 4,3.14" pelo="0,4,5,free surface" quote="4 3,4 5,2.00 m,-0.7;4 0.5,4 5,4.50 m,-1.7" titolo="Two circular orifices of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ in the same wall operate under heads $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.100^2}{4} = 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q_1 = \mu A \sqrt{2 g h_1} = 0.61 \cdot 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.00} = 0.61 \cdot 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 6.26 = 0.0300\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_2 = \mu A \sqrt{2 g h_2} = 0.61 \cdot 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 4.50} = 0.61 \cdot 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 9.40 = 0.0450\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q = Q_1 + Q_2 = 0.0300 + 0.0450 = 0.0750\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 75.0\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
h_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{2g}\left(\frac{Q}{2\,\mu\,A}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{19.62}\left(\frac{0.0750}{2 \cdot 0.61 \cdot 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3}}\right)^2 = \frac{7.830^2}{19.62} = 3.13\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the combined discharge by assigning the mean head $h_m = 3.25\,\mathrm{m}$ to both orifices:
$$
2\,\mu A \sqrt{2 g h_m} = 2 \cdot 0.61 \cdot 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.25} = 0.0765\,\mathrm{m^3/s} > 0.0750\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two orifices discharge independently to the atmosphere, and the tank level stays constant. Calculate the flow through each orifice using its own centroid head and add the two discharges.

The deeper orifice discharges more water because it has more head. The discharge ratio is $\sqrt{\dfrac{4.50}{2.00}} = 1.50$ rather than $\dfrac{4.50}{2.00} = 2.25$ because discharge varies with the square root of head. Together the orifices discharge $Q = 75.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

Find the equivalent head by setting the discharge from two identical orifices at that head equal to the combined discharge, $Q = 2\,\mu A \sqrt{2 g h_{\mathrm{eq}}}$. This gives $h_{\mathrm{eq}} = 3.13\,\mathrm{m}$, less than the arithmetic mean $3.25\,\mathrm{m}$ of the two actual heads.

Giving both orifices the mean head would produce $76.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$, which exceeds the actual discharge $75.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The square-root function is concave, so the mean of the square roots is smaller than the square root of the mean. Adding the discharges at their individual heads gives the correct total, while using the mean head **overestimates the discharge**.

</div>
