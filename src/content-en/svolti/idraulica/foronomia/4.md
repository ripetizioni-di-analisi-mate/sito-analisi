## Exercise 1

A rectangular orifice of width $b = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $a = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is cut in a vertical reservoir wall. Its top edge is $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, as shown, and its discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.62$. Calculate the discharge using the large-orifice formula and compare it with the small-orifice estimate.

::schema{solidi="-3.3,-0.3 0.3,-0.3 0.3,0 -3.3,0;-3.3,0 -3,0 -3,3 -3.3,3;0,0 0.3,0 0.3,0.6 0,0.6;0,1.8 0.3,1.8 0.3,3 0,3" acqua="-3,0 0,0 0,2.6 -3,2.6;0,0.6 0.9,0.68 1.8,0.7 1.8,1.72 0.9,1.74 0,1.8" pelo="-3,0,2.6,free surface" quote="0 1.8,0 2.6,0.80 m,-0.5;0 0.6,0 2.6,2.00 m,-1.3" titolo="Large rectangular orifice of width $b = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $a = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, with its upper edge $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface"}

$$
h_1 = 0.80\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_2 = h_1 + a = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Q = \frac{2}{3}\,\mu\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\left(h_2^{\frac{3}{2}} - h_1^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)
$$
$$
\frac{2}{3} \cdot 0.62 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 4.429 = 2.746\,\mathrm{m^{3/2}/s},
\qquad
h_2^{\frac{3}{2}} = 2.828\,\mathrm{m^{3/2}},
\qquad
h_1^{\frac{3}{2}} = 0.7155\,\mathrm{m^{3/2}}
$$
$$
Q = 2.746 \cdot \left(2.828 - 0.7155\right) = 2.746 \cdot 2.113 = 5.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
h_G = \frac{h_1 + h_2}{2} = 1.40\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = b\,a = 1.80\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q_p = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g h_G} = 0.62 \cdot 1.80 \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.40} = 0.62 \cdot 1.80 \cdot 5.241 = 5.85\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

Check by adding the discharges through two horizontal strips, each of height $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
Q_{0.8 \div 1.4} = 2.746 \cdot \left(1.4^{\frac{3}{2}} - 0.8^{\frac{3}{2}}\right) = 2.746 \cdot \left(1.6565 - 0.7155\right) = 2.584\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_{1.4 \div 2.0} = 2.746 \cdot \left(2.828 - 1.6565\right) = 3.218\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
2.584 + 3.218 = 5.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The opening height $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is comparable to the head at its centroid, $1.40\,\mathrm{m}$. Consequently the theoretical speed $\sqrt{2gy}$ varies appreciably from the top edge to the bottom, and the opening is treated as a **large orifice**.

The formula integrates the contributions of horizontal strips between the two edge depths. The top edge depth and opening height are given, so the bottom edge depth is $h_2 = h_1 + a$. Evaluate the constant factor $\dfrac{2}{3}\mu b \sqrt{2g}$ once to obtain $2.746$.

The discharge is $Q = 5.80\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The small-orifice formula using the centroid head gives $5.85\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, an overestimate of $0.79\%$. This difference is close to the estimate $\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{24}$ for $\varepsilon = \dfrac{a}{2 h_G} = 0.429$, namely $0.77\%$.

The check uses the additivity of the integral. Divide the opening into two strips of height $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$, apply the same formula to each, and add their discharges. This reproduces the single-integral result. The lower strip carries more water because it is under a greater head.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular bottom outlet of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $a = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is set into a vertical tank wall, with its lower edge at the floor, $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, as shown. Its discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.60$. Find the discharge and the head at which the small-orifice formula would give the same value.

::schema{solidi="-3.3,-0.3 0.3,-0.3 0.3,0 -3.3,0;-3.3,0 -3,0 -3,3.4 -3.3,3.4;0,1 0.3,1 0.3,3.4 0,3.4" acqua="-3,0 0,0 0,3 -3,3;0,0 1,0 1.8,0 1.8,0.92 1,0.94 0,1" pelo="-3,0,3,free surface" quote="0 1,0 3,2.00 m,-0.6;0 0,0 3,3.00 m,-1.4" titolo="Bottom gate of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $a = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, with the channel bottom $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface"}

$$
h_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_2 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{2}{3} \cdot 0.60 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 4.429 = 3.544\,\mathrm{m^{3/2}/s}
$$
$$
Q = 3.544 \cdot \left(3.00^{\frac{3}{2}} - 2.00^{\frac{3}{2}}\right) = 3.544 \cdot \left(5.196 - 2.828\right) = 3.544 \cdot 2.368 = 8.39\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
h_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{2g}\left(\frac{Q}{\mu\,A}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{19.62}\left(\frac{8.39}{0.60 \cdot 2.00}\right)^2 = \frac{6.992^2}{19.62} = 2.49\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check by adding the discharges of two strips, each of height $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
Q_{2.0 \div 2.5} = 3.544 \cdot \left(3.953 - 2.828\right) = 3.98\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
Q_{2.5 \div 3.0} = 3.544 \cdot \left(5.196 - 3.953\right) = 4.41\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
3.98 + 4.41 = 8.39\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The outlet extends down to the floor, so its bottom edge depth is the floor depth $h_2 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and its top edge depth is $h_2 - a = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The coefficient $\mu = 0.60$ accounts for the suppression of contraction along the floor.

The constant factor is $3.544$, giving a discharge of $Q = 8.39\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

To find the equivalent head, invert the small-orifice formula for the head that gives the actual discharge at the same area. The result $h_{\mathrm{eq}} = 2.49\,\mathrm{m}$ is slightly less than the centroid head $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The opening height is only two-fifths of the centroid head, so $\varepsilon = 0.20$ and the difference between the two discharge formulas is $0.17\%$. The small-orifice formula would be **acceptable** for an engineering estimate.

Dividing the opening into two strips of height $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and adding their discharges gives $8.39\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ again.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular orifice of width $b = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $a = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ is cut in a vertical tank wall with its top edge $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, as shown. Its discharge coefficient is $\mu = 0.61$. Find its discharge and the error from using the small-orifice formula.

::schema{solidi="-3.3,-0.3 0.3,-0.3 0.3,0 -3.3,0;-3.3,0 -3,0 -3,3 -3.3,3;0,0 0.3,0 0.3,0.4 0,0.4;0,1.9 0.3,1.9 0.3,3 0,3" acqua="-3,0 0,0 0,2.2 -3,2.2;0,0.4 0.9,0.5 1.8,0.54 1.8,1.82 0.9,1.85 0,1.9" pelo="-3,0,2.2,free surface" quote="0 1.9,0 2.2,0.30 m,-0.5;0 0.4,0 2.2,1.80 m,-1.3" titolo="Rectangular orifice of width $b = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $a = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$, with its upper edge $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface"}

$$
h_1 = 0.30\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_2 = 1.80\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_G = 1.05\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = 1.80\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
\frac{2}{3} \cdot 0.61 \cdot 1.20 \cdot 4.429 = 2.162\,\mathrm{m^{3/2}/s}
$$
$$
Q = 2.162 \cdot \left(1.80^{\frac{3}{2}} - 0.30^{\frac{3}{2}}\right) = 2.162 \cdot \left(2.415 - 0.1643\right) = 2.162 \cdot 2.251 = 4.86\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_p = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2 g h_G} = 0.61 \cdot 1.80 \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.05} = 0.61 \cdot 1.80 \cdot 4.539 = 4.98\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
\frac{Q_p - Q}{Q_p} = \frac{4.98 - 4.86}{4.98} = 0.024 = 2.4\%
$$

Check by adding the discharges through three horizontal strips, each of height $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{0.3 \div 0.8} &= 2.162 \cdot \left(0.7155 - 0.1643\right) = 1.19\,\mathrm{m^3/s} \\[6pt]
Q_{0.8 \div 1.3} &= 2.162 \cdot \left(1.4823 - 0.7155\right) = 1.66\,\mathrm{m^3/s} \\[6pt]
Q_{1.3 \div 1.8} &= 2.162 \cdot \left(2.4150 - 1.4823\right) = 2.02\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
1.19 + 1.66 + 2.02 = 4.87\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The opening height is nearly one and a half times the head at the centroid, giving the largest discrepancy between the formulas in these examples. The bottom edge depth is $h_2 = h_1 + a = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$, and the centroid head is the mean edge depth $1.05\,\mathrm{m}$.

The constant factor is $2.162$, giving a discharge of $Q = 4.86\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The small-orifice approximation gives $4.98\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, a difference of **$2.4\%$**. This is consistent with the estimate $\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{24} = 2.1\%$ for $\varepsilon = \dfrac{1.50}{2 \cdot 1.05} = 0.714$ once higher-order terms are included.

For the check, divide the opening into three strips of height $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and add their discharges. The sum $4.87\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ reproduces the result within the rounding of the individual discharges to three decimal places. Successively deeper strips carry more water, in proportion to the differences between their edge depths raised to the power $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>
