## Exercise 1

Two tanks of constant plan areas $\Omega_1 = 15.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\Omega_2 = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ communicate through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 120\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\mu = 0.61$ in the dividing wall, as shown. It stays submerged on both sides throughout the transfer. The initial difference between free-surface elevations is $\Delta_0 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the time for the difference to decrease to $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, the time for the levels to equalize, and the volume transferred.

::schema{solidi="-0.35,-0.4 8.35,-0.4 8.35,0 -0.35,0;-0.35,0 0,0 0,4.4 -0.35,4.4;8,0 8.35,0 8.35,4.4 8,4.4;3.9,0 4.1,0 4.1,0.85 3.9,0.85;3.9,1.15 4.1,1.15 4.1,4.4 3.9,4.4" acqua="0,0 3.9,0 3.9,3.4 0,3.4;4.1,0 8,0 8,1.4 4.1,1.4;3.9,0.85 4.1,0.85 4.1,1.15 3.9,1.15" pelo="0,3.9,3.4,;4.1,8,1.4," tratteggi="4.1,3.4 6.6,3.4" quote="6 1.4,6 3.4,2.00 m,0.6" etichette="1.9,1.8,Ω_1 = 15.0 m²;6,0.6,Ω_2 = 10.0 m²" titolo="Two tanks connected by a submerged orifice of diameter $D = 120\,\mathrm{mm}$, with an initial difference in water level of $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.120^2}{4} = 0.01131\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
k = \mu A \sqrt{2g} = 0.61 \cdot 0.01131 \cdot 4.429 = 0.03056\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}
$$
$$
c = k\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_1} + \frac{1}{\Omega_2}\right) = 0.03056 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{15.0} + \frac{1}{10.0}\right) = 0.03056 \cdot 0.1667 = 5.093 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}/s}
$$
$$
t_1 = \frac{2\left(\sqrt{\Delta_0} - \sqrt{\Delta_1}\right)}{c} = \frac{2\left(1.4142 - 0.7071\right)}{5.093 \cdot 10^{-3}} = \frac{1.4142}{5.093 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 278\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
t_2 = \frac{2\sqrt{\Delta_0}}{c} = \frac{2.8284}{5.093 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 555\,\mathrm{s} = 9.3\,\mathrm{min}
$$
$$
V = \frac{\Omega_1\,\Omega_2}{\Omega_1 + \Omega_2}\,\Delta_0 = \frac{15.0 \cdot 10.0}{25.0} \cdot 2.00 = 6.00 \cdot 2.00 = 12.0\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

Check using the changes in water level in both tanks.
$$
\frac{V}{\Omega_1} = \frac{12.0}{15.0} = 0.80\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\frac{V}{\Omega_2} = \frac{12.0}{10.0} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
0.80 + 1.20 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta_0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Because the orifice is submerged, discharge depends on the difference between the two water levels. The first level falls while the second rises, so the difference decreases by $\dfrac{Q\,dt}{\Omega_1} + \dfrac{Q\,dt}{\Omega_2}$ during the interval $dt$.

Collecting the constant factors in $c = k\left(\dfrac{1}{\Omega_1} + \dfrac{1}{\Omega_2}\right)$ gives the equation $-\dfrac{d\Delta}{dt} = c\sqrt{\Delta}$, of the same form as the tank-drainage equation. The transfer time depends on the difference between the square roots of the initial and final heads.

The level difference falls to one-quarter of its initial value in $278\,\mathrm{s}$ and reaches zero in $555\,\mathrm{s}$, or $9.3$ minutes. The time ratio is $1 - \sqrt{\dfrac{0.50}{2.00}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ because time is proportional to the difference between the square roots of the heads. Thus **three-quarters of the original level difference disappears in half the total time**.

The transferred volume follows without integration from the condition that the final levels are equal. If the first level falls by $\dfrac{V}{\Omega_1}$ and the second rises by $\dfrac{V}{\Omega_2}$, their combined movement must equal the original level difference. This gives $V = 12.0\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

The check confirms that condition. The first level falls by $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and the second rises by $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Their sum is the original level difference $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A tank of constant plan area $\Omega = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ discharges into a constant-level channel through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\mu = 0.60$, submerged on both sides as shown. The initial difference between the tank and channel levels is $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$. How long does it take for that difference to decrease to $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$?

::schema{solidi="-0.35,-0.4 8.35,-0.4 8.35,0 -0.35,0;-0.35,0 0,0 0,4 -0.35,4;3.9,0 4.1,0 4.1,0.85 3.9,0.85;3.9,1.15 4.1,1.15 4.1,4 3.9,4" acqua="0,0 3.9,0 3.9,3.2 0,3.2;4.1,0 8.35,0 8.35,1.4 4.1,1.4;3.9,0.85 4.1,0.85 4.1,1.15 3.9,1.15" pelo="0,3.9,3.2,;4.1,8,1.4,channel" tratteggi="4.1,3.2 6.6,3.2" quote="6 1.4,6 3.2,1.80 m,0.6" etichette="1.9,1.8,Ω = 8.00 m²" titolo="Tank discharging into a constant-level channel through a submerged orifice of diameter $D = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.080^2}{4} = 5.027 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
k = 0.60 \cdot 5.027 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 4.429 = 0.01336\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}
$$
$$
c = \frac{k}{\Omega} = \frac{0.01336}{8.00} = 1.670 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}/s}
$$
$$
t = \frac{2\left(\sqrt{1.80} - \sqrt{0.20}\right)}{c} = \frac{2\left(1.3416 - 0.4472\right)}{1.670 \cdot 10^{-3}} = \frac{1.7889}{1.670 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 1071\,\mathrm{s} = 17.9\,\mathrm{min}
$$

Check using the average discharge and the initial and final discharges.
$$
V = \Omega\left(1.80 - 0.20\right) = 8.00 \cdot 1.60 = 12.8\,\mathrm{m^3},
\qquad
Q_m = \frac{12.8}{1071} = 0.01195\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_0 = k\sqrt{1.80} = 0.01793\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
Q_1 = k\sqrt{0.20} = 0.00597\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
\frac{Q_0 + Q_1}{2} = 0.01195\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A channel held at constant level behaves like a receiving tank of infinite plan area. The term $\dfrac{1}{\Omega_2}$ in the transfer equation vanishes, leaving $c = \dfrac{k}{\Omega}$. The result has the form of the draining equation with the water-level difference as the driving head.

The difference falls from $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ in $1071\,\mathrm{s}$, or $17.9$ minutes. Full equalization would take $\dfrac{2\sqrt{1.80}}{c} = 1607\,\mathrm{s}$. The final decrease from $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ to zero takes one-third of the total time because discharge approaches zero with the head difference.

Compare the average discharge with the endpoint discharges. The volume leaving the tank is the plan area multiplied by the fall in water level. Since the channel level is constant, this fall equals the decrease in head difference. Dividing the volume by time gives $0.01195\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, the arithmetic mean of the initial and final discharges.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two tanks of constant plan areas $\Omega_1 = 12.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\Omega_2 = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ have floors at the same elevation. They communicate through a circular orifice of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\mu = 0.60$, whose centroid lies $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor, as shown. Initially the water depth in the first tank is $3.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and the second tank is empty. Assume free discharge until the water in the second tank reaches the orifice centroid, then submerged discharge. Find the time for the water-level difference to reach $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and the water level in each tank at that instant.

::schema{solidi="-0.35,-0.4 8.35,-0.4 8.35,0 -0.35,0;-0.35,0 0,0 0,4.4 -0.35,4.4;8,0 8.35,0 8.35,4.4 8,4.4;3.9,0 4.1,0 4.1,0.45 3.9,0.45;3.9,0.75 4.1,0.75 4.1,4.4 3.9,4.4" acqua="0,0 3.9,0 3.9,3.6 0,3.6;3.9,0.45 4.4,0.49 5.1,0.3 5.4,0 4.8,0 4.6,0.25 4.2,0.68 3.9,0.75" pelo="0,3.9,3.6," quote="3.9 0.6,3.9 3.6,3.00 m,-0.6;0 0,0 0.6,0.60 m,-0.6" etichette="1.9,1.8,Ω_1 = 12.0 m²;6.6,1.8,Ω_2 = 6.00 m² (empty)" titolo="Transfer between two tanks through an orifice $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. The second tank is initially empty"}

First stage. The orifice discharges freely while its head falls from $h = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to $h = 2.70\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.100^2}{4} = 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
k = 0.60 \cdot 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 4.429 = 0.02087\,\mathrm{m^{5/2}/s}
$$
$$
V_1 = \Omega_2 \cdot 0.60 = 6.00 \cdot 0.60 = 3.60\,\mathrm{m^3},
\qquad
\frac{V_1}{\Omega_1} = \frac{3.60}{12.0} = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
t_1 = \frac{2\,\Omega_1\left(\sqrt{3.00} - \sqrt{2.70}\right)}{k} = \frac{24.0 \cdot \left(1.7321 - 1.6432\right)}{0.02087} = \frac{2.133}{0.02087} = 102\,\mathrm{s}
$$

Second stage. The orifice discharges under submerged conditions as the level difference falls from $\Delta_0 = 2.70\,\mathrm{m}$ to $\Delta_1 = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
c = k\left(\frac{1}{12.0} + \frac{1}{6.00}\right) = \frac{0.02087}{4.00} = 5.218 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{1/2}/s}
$$
$$
t_2 = \frac{2\left(\sqrt{2.70} - \sqrt{0.30}\right)}{c} = \frac{2\left(1.6432 - 0.5477\right)}{5.218 \cdot 10^{-3}} = \frac{2.191}{5.218 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 420\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
t = t_1 + t_2 = 102 + 420 = 522\,\mathrm{s} = 8.7\,\mathrm{min}
$$
$$
V_2 = \frac{\Omega_1\Omega_2}{\Omega_1 + \Omega_2}\left(\Delta_0 - \Delta_1\right) = \frac{12.0 \cdot 6.00}{18.0} \cdot 2.40 = 4.00 \cdot 2.40 = 9.60\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
h_1^{\mathrm{fin}} = 3.60 - 0.30 - \frac{9.60}{12.0} = 3.60 - 0.30 - 0.80 = 2.50\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_2^{\mathrm{fin}} = 0.60 + \frac{9.60}{6.00} = 2.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the final level difference and the average discharge in the second stage.
$$
h_1^{\mathrm{fin}} - h_2^{\mathrm{fin}} = 2.50 - 2.20 = 0.30\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta_1
$$
$$
\frac{V_2}{t_2} = \frac{9.60}{420} = 0.02286\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
\frac{k\sqrt{2.70} + k\sqrt{0.30}}{2} = \frac{0.03430 + 0.01143}{2} = 0.02286\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The transfer has two stages. Initially the second tank is empty and the orifice discharges to the atmosphere. Later the downstream side is submerged as well.

In the first stage the driving head is the height of the first free surface above the orifice centroid, initially $3.60 - 0.60 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The stage ends when the second tank has received $V_1 = 3.60\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and its surface reaches the centroid. By then the first level has fallen by $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and the head has decreased to $2.70\,\mathrm{m}$. The draining formula gives $t_1 = 102\,\mathrm{s}$.

In the second stage the orifice is submerged, so the driving head equals the difference between the free-surface elevations. At the beginning of this stage the levels are $3.30\,\mathrm{m}$ in the first tank and $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ in the second, so $\Delta_0 = 2.70\,\mathrm{m}$. The transfer formula gives $t_2 = 420\,\mathrm{s}$, for a total time of $8.7$ minutes.

Use the second-stage transfer volume $V_2 = 9.60\,\mathrm{m^3}$ to find the final levels. The first tank falls a further $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ to $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, and the second rises by $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$ to $2.20\,\mathrm{m}$.

There are two checks. The final levels differ by the specified $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, confirming consistency of the volumes and level changes. Also, the volume transferred in the second stage divided by its duration equals the arithmetic mean of its endpoint discharges, as required when discharge is proportional to the square root of the head difference.

</div>
