## Exercise 1

In the junction shown, pipe $1$ has diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and supplies a discharge of $Q_1 = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Pipe $2$ has diameter $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries an outflow of $Q_2 = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The other outlet, pipe $3$, has diameter $D_3 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$. Calculate the discharge in pipe $3$ and the mean velocity in each pipe.

::schema{nomi=0 nodi="N,0,0;A,-3.4,0;B,3,1.8;C,3,-1.8" condotte="A-N,0.8,D_1 = 300 mm;N-B,0.65,D_2 = 250 mm;N-C,0.55,D_3 = 200 mm" frecce="-3.0,0,-1.8,0,Q_1 = 120 L/s;2.1,1.26,3.1,1.86,Q_2 = 70 L/s;2.1,-1.26,3.1,-1.86,Q_3 = ?" riferimento=0 titolo="Junction with one incoming pipe and two outgoing pipes"}

$$
Q_1 = Q_2 + Q_3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Q_3 = Q_1 - Q_2 = 120 - 70 = 50\,\mathrm{L/s} = 0.0500\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.300)^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.250)^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_3 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.200)^2}{4} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.120}{0.0707}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{0.0700}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.43\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_3 = \frac{0.0500}{0.0314}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.59\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Check the volume balance over one minute.
$$
Q_1\,\Delta t = 0.120 \cdot 60 = 7.20\,\mathrm{m^3},
\qquad
(Q_2 + Q_3)\,\Delta t = (0.0700 + 0.0500) \cdot 60 = 7.20\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fluid is incompressible, the flow is steady, and no water accumulates at the junction. The inflow therefore equals the sum of the outflows. The only unknown in the balance is $Q_3$, obtained by subtraction.

Calculate the mean velocity in each branch by dividing its discharge by its cross-sectional area. Branch $3$ carries a smaller discharge than branch $2$ but also has a smaller diameter. Its mean velocity is **higher** because velocity depends on the ratio of discharge to area.

Over one minute, $7.20\,\mathrm{m^3}$ enters the junction and an equal volume leaves. This checks the discharge balance and confirms that the volume of water within the junction stays constant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two pipes carrying discharges $Q_1 = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $Q_2 = 45\,\mathrm{L/s}$ meet at a junction. A single pipe of diameter $D_3 = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries the outflow, as shown. Calculate the discharge and mean velocity in the outlet pipe and the volume it carries in half an hour.

::schema{nomi=0 nodi="N,0,0;A,-3.2,1.6;B,-3.2,-1.6;C,3.6,0" condotte="A-N,0.6,Q_1 = 60 L/s;B-N,0.55,Q_2 = 45 L/s;N-C,0.9,D_3 = 400 mm" frecce="-2.56,1.28,-1.56,0.78;-2.56,-1.28,-1.56,-0.78;2.1,0,3.3,0,Q_3 = ?" riferimento=0 titolo="Junction with two incoming pipes and one outgoing pipe"}

$$
Q_3 = Q_1 + Q_2 = 60 + 45 = 105\,\mathrm{L/s} = 0.105\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
A_3 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.400)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.126\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_3 = \frac{Q_3}{A_3} = \frac{0.105}{0.126}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.836\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
V = Q_3\,\Delta t = 0.105 \cdot 1800\,\mathrm{m^3} = 189\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

Check the volumes supplied by the two inlet branches.
$$
V = (Q_1 + Q_2)\,\Delta t = (0.0600 + 0.0450) \cdot 1800\,\mathrm{m^3} = 108 + 81 = 189\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two branches enter the junction and one leaves, so the downstream discharge equals the sum of the two upstream discharges.

Dividing $Q_3$ by the outlet area gives a mean velocity of $0.836\,\mathrm{m/s}$, low but reasonable for a large-diameter pipe.

The volume passing in half an hour, or $1800\,\mathrm{s}$, equals discharge times duration. As a check, the volumes $108\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and $81\,\mathrm{m^3}$ delivered by the inlet branches over the same interval add up to the outlet volume $189\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pipe delivers discharge $Q = 250\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to the junction shown. Three pipes of diameters $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$, $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $D_3 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ leave the junction, all carrying flow at the same mean velocity. Calculate the common mean velocity and the discharge in each branch.

::schema{nomi=0 nodi="N,0,0;A,-3.4,0;B,3.2,2.0;C,3.6,0;D,3.2,-2.0" condotte="A-N,0.9;N-B,0.75,D_1 = 300 mm;N-C,0.6,D_2 = 250 mm;N-D,0.5,D_3 = 200 mm" frecce="-3.0,0,-1.8,0,Q = 250 L/s" riferimento=0 titolo="Flow dividing among three pipes with the same mean velocity"}

$$
Q = Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3 = v\,A_1 + v\,A_2 + v\,A_3 = v\,(A_1 + A_2 + A_3)
$$
$$
A_1 + A_2 + A_3 = (0.0707 + 0.0491 + 0.0314)\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.1512\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A_1 + A_2 + A_3} = \frac{0.250}{0.1512}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.65\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q_1 = v\,A_1 = 1.65 \cdot 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.117\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 117\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
Q_2 = v\,A_2 = 1.65 \cdot 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0812\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 81.2\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
Q_3 = v\,A_3 = 1.65 \cdot 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0519\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 51.9\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the sum of the three branch discharges.
$$
Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3 = 117 + 81.2 + 51.9 = 250.1\,\mathrm{L/s} \approx 250\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three branch discharges are unknown, so the junction balance alone is insufficient. The common mean velocity supplies the two additional conditions needed, since each discharge can be written as $Q_i = v\,A_i$ with the same value of $v$.

Substituting into the junction balance and factoring out $v$ gives one equation in one unknown. Total discharge is the common velocity times the sum of the three areas. This gives a velocity of $1.65\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

Multiplying the common velocity by each branch area gives its discharge. Discharge is thus divided in proportion to the areas, or to the squares of the diameters. To three significant figures, the three branch discharges add up to the upstream discharge $250\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

</div>
