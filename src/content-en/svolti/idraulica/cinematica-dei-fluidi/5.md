## Exercise 1

Oil of kinematic viscosity $\nu = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ flows under laminar conditions through the circular pipe of diameter $D = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ shown in the figure. At a distance $r$ from the centerline, the velocity is $u = u_{\max}\left(1 - \dfrac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right)$, where $u_{\max} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $r_0$ is the pipe radius. Calculate the discharge, mean velocity, and correction factors $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Verify the flow regime.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,5,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.0,D = 100 mm" frecce="2,0,3.5,0,u_max;2,0.125,3.41,0.125;2,0.25,3.13,0.25;2,0.375,2.66,0.375;2,-0.125,3.41,-0.125;2,-0.25,3.13,-0.25;2,-0.375,2.66,-0.375" linee="2,-0.5 2.66,-0.375 3.13,-0.25 3.41,-0.125 3.5,0 3.41,0.125 3.13,0.25 2.66,0.375 2,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Parabolic velocity distribution in laminar flow"}

$$
r_0 = \frac{D}{2} = 0.0500\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = \pi r_0^2 = 7.85 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = \int_0^{r_0} u_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right) 2\pi r\,dr
= 2\pi u_{\max}\left[\frac{r^2}{2} - \frac{r^4}{4 r_0^2}\right]_0^{r_0}
= \frac{\pi r_0^2 u_{\max}}{2}
$$
$$
Q = \frac{7.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1.20}{2}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 4.71 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 4.71\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{u_{\max}}{2} = 0.600\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{1}{A\,v^3}\int_0^{r_0} u^3\,2\pi r\,dr
= \frac{2\pi u_{\max}^3}{\pi r_0^2 \left(\dfrac{u_{\max}}{2}\right)^{\!3}} \int_0^{r_0} \left(1 - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right)^{\!3} r\,dr
= \frac{16}{r_0^2} \cdot \frac{r_0^2}{8} = 2
$$
$$
\beta = \frac{1}{A\,v^2}\int_0^{r_0} u^2\,2\pi r\,dr
= \frac{2\pi u_{\max}^2}{\pi r_0^2 \left(\dfrac{u_{\max}}{2}\right)^{\!2}} \int_0^{r_0} \left(1 - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right)^{\!2} r\,dr
= \frac{8}{r_0^2} \cdot \frac{r_0^2}{6} = \frac{4}{3} = 1.33
$$

Check the flow regime using the Reynolds number.
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu} = \frac{0.600 \cdot 0.100}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}} = 600 < 2000
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity depends only on distance from the centerline. Take an annular area element of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$. Its area is $dA = 2\pi r\,dr$, and the velocity around it is constant at $u(r)$.

Evaluating the discharge integral with the antiderivative shown gives $\dfrac{\pi r_0^2 u_{\max}}{2}$, one-half the cross-sectional area times the maximum velocity. Dividing by the area gives $v = \dfrac{u_{\max}}{2}$. For this laminar flow, the **mean velocity is half the centerline velocity**.

The two correction factors use the integrals of $u^3$ and $u^2$. Both can be evaluated using the substitution $s = 1 - \dfrac{r^2}{r_0^2}$, which gives $\displaystyle\int_0^{r_0} \left(1 - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right)^{3} r\,dr = \frac{r_0^2}{8}$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^{r_0} \left(1 - \frac{r^2}{r_0^2}\right)^{2} r\,dr = \frac{r_0^2}{6}$. The resulting values $\alpha = 2$ and $\beta = \dfrac{4}{3}$ are independent of radius and maximum velocity, so they apply to laminar flow with this profile in any circular pipe.

The result $Re = 600$ confirms the original assumption of laminar flow, so the parabolic velocity profile is appropriate. With water at the same velocity and diameter, the Reynolds number would be a hundred times greater and the turbulent velocity profile would be flatter.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The rectangular channel shown has width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and water depth $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The vertical velocity profile is $u = u_{\max}\left(\dfrac{y}{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}$, where $y$ is distance above the bed and $u_{\max} = 1.40\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the surface velocity. Calculate the mean velocity, discharge, and correction factors $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

::schema{acqua="0,0 5,0 5,1.2 0,1.2" solidi="-0.3,-0.45 5.3,-0.45 5.3,0 -0.3,0" pelo="0,5,1.2" frecce="1.2,0.15,2.05,0.15;1.2,0.3,2.15,0.3;1.2,0.6,2.27,0.6;1.2,0.9,2.34,0.9;1.2,1.2,2.4,1.2,u_max" linee="1.2,0 2.05,0.15 2.15,0.3 2.27,0.6 2.34,0.9 2.4,1.2" quote="0 0,0 1.2,h = 1.20 m,-0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical velocity profile in a channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
v = \frac{1}{h}\int_0^h u_{\max}\left(\frac{y}{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}} dy
= \frac{u_{\max}}{h}\left[\frac{6}{7}\,\frac{y^{\frac{7}{6}}}{h^{\frac{1}{6}}}\right]_0^h
= \frac{6}{7}\,u_{\max}
$$
$$
v = \frac{6}{7} \cdot 1.40\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A = b\,h = 3.00 \cdot 1.20\,\mathrm{m^2} = 3.60\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
Q = A\,v = 3.60 \cdot 1.20\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 4.32\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{1}{h\,v^3}\int_0^h u^3\,dy
= \frac{u_{\max}^3}{h\,v^3}\int_0^h \left(\frac{y}{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} dy
= \frac{u_{\max}^3}{v^3} \cdot \frac{2}{3}
= \left(\frac{7}{6}\right)^{\!3} \cdot \frac{2}{3}
= \frac{343}{324} = 1.06
$$
$$
\beta = \frac{1}{h\,v^2}\int_0^h u^2\,dy
= \frac{u_{\max}^2}{v^2} \cdot \frac{3}{4}
= \left(\frac{7}{6}\right)^{\!2} \cdot \frac{3}{4}
= \frac{49}{48} = 1.02
$$

Check the discharge by integrating over the cross section.
$$
Q = b\int_0^h u\,dy = 3.00 \cdot 1.40 \cdot \frac{6}{7} \cdot 1.20\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 4.32\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The channel is wide and velocity depends only on distance above the bed. Choose a horizontal area element of width $b$ and height $dy$. All integrals then reduce to integration with respect to $y$ over the water depth.

The mean velocity is the depth average of the velocity profile. Applying the power rule to the integral of $\left(\dfrac{y}{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}$ gives $\dfrac{6}{7}h$. The mean velocity is therefore $\dfrac{6}{7}$ of the surface value, or $1.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

For the two correction factors, the exponents become $\dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, so the integrals give $\dfrac{2}{3}h$ and $\dfrac{3}{4}h$. The ratio $\dfrac{u_{\max}}{v}$ equals $\dfrac{7}{6}$. The results $\alpha = 1.06$ and $\beta = 1.02$ lie within the ranges measured for turbulent flows and justify the approximation $\alpha = \beta = 1$ used in pipe calculations.

Integrating the local velocity directly over the cross section gives the same discharge as multiplying area by mean velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a rectangular channel of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and depth $h = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, velocity is measured at the midpoint of each of five layers of thickness $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. From bed to surface the readings are $0.70$, $0.95$, $1.10$, $1.20$, and $1.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate the discharge, mean velocity, and correction factors $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

::schema{acqua="0,0 3.5,0 3.5,1 0,1" solidi="-0.25,-0.35 3.75,-0.35 3.75,0 -0.25,0" pelo="0,3.5,1" tratteggi="0,0.2 0.45,0.2;0,0.4 0.45,0.4;0,0.6 0.45,0.6;0,0.8 0.45,0.8" frecce="0.5,0.1,1.62,0.1,0.70 m/s;0.5,0.3,2.02,0.3,0.95 m/s;0.5,0.5,2.26,0.5,1.10 m/s;0.5,0.7,2.42,0.7,1.20 m/s;0.5,0.9,2.50,0.9,1.25 m/s" quote="0 0,0 1,h = 1.00 m,-0.35" riferimento=0 titolo="Velocities measured at the centers of five layers of thickness $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\Delta A = b\,\Delta y = 2.00 \cdot 0.200\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.400\,\mathrm{m^2}
\quad \text{for each layer}
$$
$$
Q = \sum_{i=1}^{5} u_i\,\Delta A = 0.400 \cdot (0.70 + 0.95 + 1.10 + 1.20 + 1.25)\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.400 \cdot 5.20 = 2.08\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
A = b\,h = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{2.08}{2.00}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.04\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{5} u_i^3 = 0.343 + 0.857 + 1.331 + 1.728 + 1.953 = 6.212\,\mathrm{m^3/s^3}
$$
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{5} u_i^2 = 0.490 + 0.903 + 1.210 + 1.440 + 1.563 = 5.605\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{\sum u_i^3\,\Delta A}{A\,v^3} = \frac{6.212}{5 \cdot (1.04)^3} = \frac{6.212}{5.624} = 1.10
$$
$$
\beta = \frac{\sum u_i^2\,\Delta A}{A\,v^2} = \frac{5.605}{5 \cdot (1.04)^2} = \frac{5.605}{5.408} = 1.04
$$

Check the mean of velocity cubed.
$$
\alpha\,v^3 = 1.10 \cdot 1.125 = 1.24\,\mathrm{m^3/s^3},
\qquad
\frac{1}{5}\sum u_i^3 = \frac{6.212}{5} = 1.24\,\mathrm{m^3/s^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All layers have the same thickness and hence the same area $\Delta A = 0.400\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Take each measured velocity as constant over the layer in which it was recorded. The discharge is the sum of the products $u_i\,\Delta A$, or the area of one layer times the sum of the five velocities.

Dividing the discharge by the entire flow area gives the mean velocity. Because the layers have equal areas, it is also the arithmetic mean $1.04\,\mathrm{m/s}$ of the five measured velocities.

In the correction-factor integrals, the constant area $\Delta A$ cancels against $A = 5\,\Delta A$. What remains is the mean of the cubed velocities divided by the cube of the mean velocity, or the mean of the squared velocities divided by the square of the mean velocity, respectively. The results $\alpha = 1.10$ and $\beta = 1.04$ exceed $1$, as for every nonuniform profile. They also exceed those in the preceding exercise because the near-bed velocity here differs more from the mean.

As a check, $\alpha\,v^3$ equals the mean of the cubed velocities, as the definition of $\alpha$ requires.

</div>
