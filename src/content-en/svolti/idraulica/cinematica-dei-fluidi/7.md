## Exercise 1

The air duct shown changes from diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$. At the first section the air density is $\rho_1 = 1.20\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the mean velocity is $v_1 = 12.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At the second section the density is $\rho_2 = 1.60\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Calculate the mass flow rate, the mean velocity at the second section, and the volumetric flow rates at both sections.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3.8,0,-;D,7.2,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.2,D_1 = 300 mm;C-D,1.0,D_2 = 250 mm" linee="3,0.6 3.8,0.5;3,-0.6 3.8,-0.5" frecce="1.2,0,2.4,0,v_1;4.9,0,6.1,0,v_2" etichette="1.0,-0.95,ρ_1 = 1.20 kg/m³;5.6,-0.9,ρ_2 = 1.60 kg/m³" riferimento=0 titolo="Air duct with $v_1 = 12.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the first cross section"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.300)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.250)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q_m = \rho_1 A_1 v_1 = 1.20 \cdot 0.0707 \cdot 12.0\,\mathrm{kg/s} = 1.02\,\mathrm{kg/s}
$$
$$
\rho_1 A_1 v_1 = \rho_2 A_2 v_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_2 = \frac{Q_m}{\rho_2 A_2} = \frac{1.02}{1.60 \cdot 0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 13.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q_1 = A_1 v_1 = 0.0707 \cdot 12.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.848\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_2 = A_2 v_2 = 0.0491 \cdot 13.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.636\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

Check the result using the density and area ratios.
$$
v_2 = v_1 \cdot \frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2} \cdot \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^{\!2} = 12.0 \cdot \frac{1.20}{1.60} \cdot \left(\frac{300}{250}\right)^{\!2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 12.0 \cdot 0.750 \cdot 1.44 = 13.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density changes between the sections, so the conserved quantity is the **mass flow rate** $\rho\,A\,v$. The volumetric flow rate changes. Calculate the mass flow rate at the first section, where density, area, and velocity are all known.

Divide the mass flow rate by the product of density and area at the second section to obtain $13.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The contraction tends to increase velocity, while the higher density tends to decrease it. The contraction has the greater effect because the area ratio is $1.44$ and the density ratio is $0.750$.

The volumetric flow rates $0.848\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and $0.636\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ correspond to the same mass passing each second because the denser air occupies less volume. Using the product of the density and area ratios gives the same velocity and checks the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The duct shown has constant diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries a gas at mass flow rate $Q_m = 0.800\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. At section $1$ its density is $\rho_1 = 1.00\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. At section $2$, after compression, its density is $\rho_2 = 2.50\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Calculate the mean velocity and volumetric flow rate at each section.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,8,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.0,D = 200 mm" tratteggi="2,-0.55 2,0.55;6,-0.55 6,0.55" frecce="2.3,0,3.5,0,v_1;6.3,0,7.1,0,v_2" etichette="2,0.85,1;6,0.85,2;2.4,-0.9,ρ_1 = 1.00 kg/m³;6.4,-0.9,ρ_2 = 2.50 kg/m³" riferimento=0 titolo="Constant-diameter duct carrying a gas that is being compressed"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.200)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q_m = \rho_1 A v_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_1 = \frac{Q_m}{\rho_1 A} = \frac{0.800}{1.00 \cdot 0.0314}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 25.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{Q_m}{\rho_2 A} = \frac{0.800}{2.50 \cdot 0.0314}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 10.2\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q_1 = \frac{Q_m}{\rho_1} = \frac{0.800}{1.00}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.800\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
Q_2 = \frac{Q_m}{\rho_2} = \frac{0.800}{2.50}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.320\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

Check the ratio of the two velocities.
$$
\frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{2.50}{1.00} = 2.50,
\qquad
\frac{25.5}{10.2} = 2.50
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two sections have equal areas, so the area cancels in $\rho_1 A v_1 = \rho_2 A v_2$, leaving $\rho_1 v_1 = \rho_2 v_2$. Velocity is therefore inversely proportional to density.

At section $1$, divide the mass flow rate by the product of density and area to obtain the velocity. At section $2$ the density is 2.5 times as large, so the velocity is the first value divided by 2.5, giving $10.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

Divide the mass flow rate by the density at each section to obtain the two volumetric flow rates. The value after compression is less than half the upstream value. Equating volumetric flow rates would give an incorrect balance because gas volume changes along the duct, while the mass passing each section per second remains $0.800\,\mathrm{kg}$.

The calculated ratio confirms that velocity varies inversely with density.

</div>
