## Exercise 1

The rectangular tank shown has plan dimensions $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ by $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$. It receives an inflow of $Q_e = 15\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and has an outflow of $Q_u = 9\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Find the rate at which the free surface rises and the time required for the level to rise by $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$.

::schema{acqua="0,0 5,0 5,2.2 0,2.2" solidi="-0.3,-0.3 5.3,-0.3 5.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,4.2 -0.3,4.2;5,0 5.3,0 5.3,0.65 5,0.65;5,0.95 5.3,0.95 5.3,4.2 5,4.2" pelo="0,5,2.2" tratteggi="0,3.4 5,3.4" nodi="P,-1.8,3.7,-;R,1.2,3.7,-;S,5,0.8,-;T,8.8,0.8,-" condotte="P-R,0.35;S-T,0.3" frecce="-1.0,3.7,0.2,3.7,Q_e = 15 L/s;6.0,0.8,7.2,0.8,Q_u = 9 L/s" quote="0 2.2,0 3.4,1.20 m,-0.75" riferimento=0 titolo="Tank with inflow $15\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and outflow $9\,\mathrm{L/s}$"}

$$
A = 5.00 \cdot 4.00\,\mathrm{m^2} = 20.0\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
A\,\frac{dh}{dt} = Q_e - Q_u
$$
$$
\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{Q_e - Q_u}{A} = \frac{0.0150 - 0.00900}{20.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.00 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.08\,\mathrm{m/h}
$$
$$
t = \frac{\Delta h}{\dfrac{dh}{dt}} = \frac{1.20}{3.00 \cdot 10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{s} = 4000\,\mathrm{s} = 1\,\mathrm{h}\;6\,\mathrm{min}\;40\,\mathrm{s}
$$

Check the volume accumulated in the tank.
$$
(Q_e - Q_u)\,t = 0.00600 \cdot 4000\,\mathrm{m^3} = 24.0\,\mathrm{m^3},
\qquad
A\,\Delta h = 20.0 \cdot 1.20\,\mathrm{m^3} = 24.0\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The level changes with time, so the flow is unsteady. Inflow exceeds outflow and their difference accumulates in the tank. Over a time interval $dt$, the accumulated volume $A\,dh$ equals the inflow volume less the outflow volume, $(Q_e - Q_u)\,dt$.

The rectangular tank has the same surface area $20.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at every level, so the rate of rise is constant. Dividing the net inflow by the area gives $3.00 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, just over one meter per hour.

Since the rate of rise is constant, divide the required rise by this rate to obtain the filling time. The net volume that enters during $4000\,\mathrm{s}$ equals the volume of the added water layer of depth $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, confirming the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The tank shown has a rectangular plan area $A = 30.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$. It receives an inflow of $Q_e = 20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ while a pump withdraws $Q_u = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The initial water depth is $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the time required for the water depth to fall to $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the volume pumped out during that time.

::schema{acqua="0,0 5,0 5,2.5 0,2.5" solidi="-0.3,-0.3 5.3,-0.3 5.3,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,4.2 -0.3,4.2;5,0 5.3,0 5.3,0.85 5,0.85;5,1.15 5.3,1.15 5.3,4.2 5,4.2" pelo="0,5,2.5" tratteggi="0,1 5,1" nodi="P,-1.8,3.7,-;R,1.2,3.7,-;S,5,1,-;T,9.0,1,-" condotte="P-R,0.35;S-T,0.3" pompa="6.0,1,P" frecce="-1.0,3.7,0.2,3.7,Q_e = 20 L/s;6.9,1,8.1,1,Q_u = 50 L/s" quote="0 1,0 2.5,1.50 m,-0.75" riferimento=0 titolo="Tank emptied by a pump as the level falls from $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
A\,\frac{dh}{dt} = Q_e - Q_u = 0.0200 - 0.0500 = -0.0300\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{-0.0300}{30.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = -1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Delta h = 1.00 - 2.50 = -1.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
t = \frac{\Delta h}{\dfrac{dh}{dt}} = \frac{-1.50}{-1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1500\,\mathrm{s} = 25\,\mathrm{min}
$$
$$
V_u = Q_u\,t = 0.0500 \cdot 1500\,\mathrm{m^3} = 75.0\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

Check the tank volume balance.
$$
V_u = V_e + A\,|\Delta h| = 0.0200 \cdot 1500 + 30.0 \cdot 1.50 = 30.0 + 45.0 = 75.0\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The outflow exceeds the inflow, so the net inflow is negative and the level falls. The volume balance is the same as in the previous case, but the time derivative of the level now has the opposite sign.

Divide the change in level by its rate of change to find the time. Both are negative, so the time is positive, as **it must be**. A drop of $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ takes $1500\,\mathrm{s}$, or twenty-five minutes.

Multiplying the pump discharge by the elapsed time gives the volume withdrawn, $75.0\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The volume balance provides an independent check. Part of the withdrawn water entered the tank during this interval, $30.0\,\mathrm{m^3}$, and the rest came from the decrease in stored volume, $45.0\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The tank shown has length $L = 20.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and a trapezoidal cross section. The bottom width is $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the side slopes are $1:1$, corresponding to an angle of $45^\circ$. The tank is initially empty and receives a net inflow of $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Calculate the time required to reach depth $h_f = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$.

::schema{acqua="0,0 4,0 5.5,1.5 -1.5,1.5" solidi="0,0 -2,2 -3.2,2 -3.2,-1.1 7.2,-1.1 7.2,2 6,2 4,0" pelo="-1.5,5.5,1.5" nodi="P,-4.6,2.7,-;R,-1.0,2.7,-" condotte="P-R,0.3" frecce="-3.4,2.7,-2.2,2.7,Q = 40 L/s" quote="0 0,4 0,b = 4.00 m,0.4" riferimento=0 titolo="Trapezoidal tank with side slope $1:1$. The level rises to $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
B(h) = b + 2h,
\qquad
A_s(h) = L\,B(h) = 20.0\,(4.00 + 2h)
$$
$$
A_s(h)\,\frac{dh}{dt} = Q
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
20.0\,(4.00 + 2h)\,dh = Q\,dt
$$
$$
\int_0^{1.50} 20.0\,(4.00 + 2h)\,dh = \int_0^{t} Q\,dt'
$$
$$
20.0\,\Big[\,4.00\,h + h^2\,\Big]_0^{1.50} = 20.0\,(6.00 + 2.25) = 165\,\mathrm{m^3} = Q\,t
$$
$$
t = \frac{165}{0.0400}\,\mathrm{s} = 4125\,\mathrm{s} = 1\,\mathrm{h}\;8\,\mathrm{min}\;45\,\mathrm{s}
$$

Check the volume using the geometry of a trapezoidal prism.
$$
V = L \cdot \frac{(b + B(h_f))\,h_f}{2} = 20.0 \cdot \frac{(4.00 + 7.00) \cdot 1.50}{2}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 20.0 \cdot 8.25 = 165\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sides slope outward, so the free-surface area increases with water depth. At depth $h$ the surface width is $b + 2h$ because each bank extends horizontally by $h$ at the side slope $1:1$. The free-surface area is therefore $L\,(b + 2h)$.

The volume balance is still $A_s(h)\,dh = Q\,dt$, but $A_s$ now depends on $h$, so the water level rises more slowly as the tank fills. Separate variables and integrate the left side from $0$ to $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and the right side from $0$ to $t$.

At the final level, the integral on the left is the **volume of water in the tank**, $165\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Since the net inflow is constant, the integral on the right is the incoming volume $Q\,t$. Equating the two gives the filling time.

The same volume can be calculated by multiplying the tank length by the area of the trapezoid with parallel sides $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $7.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. This again gives $165\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

</div>
