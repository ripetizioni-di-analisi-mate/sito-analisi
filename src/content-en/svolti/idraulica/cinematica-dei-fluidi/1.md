## Exercise 1

The full circular pipe shown has diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 45\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. Calculate the section area, mean speed, mass flow rate, and volume conveyed in one hour.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,7,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.0,D = 200 mm" frecce="1.5,0,3.0,0,Q = 45 L/s" tratteggi="5.0,-0.55 5.0,0.55" riferimento=0 titolo="Pressurized circular pipe. The dashed section is the cross section used in the calculation"}

$$
Q = 45\,\mathrm{L/s} = 45 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0450\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.200)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.0450}{0.0314}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.43\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q_m = \rho\,Q = 1000 \cdot 0.0450\,\mathrm{kg/s} = 45.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}
$$
$$
V = Q\,\Delta t = 0.0450 \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{m^3} = 162\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

Check the Reynolds number:
$$
Re = \frac{v\,D}{\nu} = \frac{1.43 \cdot 0.200}{10^{-6}} = 2.86 \cdot 10^5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert the discharge from liters per second to $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ before combining it with an area in $\mathrm{m^2}$, using $1\,\mathrm{L} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

The pipe runs full, so the flow occupies the entire circle of diameter $D$ and area $\dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$. In a straight cylindrical pipe the streamlines are parallel to the pipe axis. Velocity is therefore normal to the cross section, and the **mean velocity** is the discharge divided by the area.

The calculated velocity $1.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$ lies in the usual range for water pipes, from $0.5$ to $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Because the water density is uniform across the section, the **mass flow rate** equals density times volumetric flow rate. Since the discharge is constant in time, the volume conveyed in one hour is the discharge times the duration.

The Reynolds number $2.86 \cdot 10^5$ indicates **turbulent** flow, the usual regime in water mains of this size. It is consistent with the relatively flat velocity profile assumed when using a representative mean velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The reservoir supplies a pipe that must carry $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at a mean speed no greater than $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The available standard diameters are $100$, $150$, $200$, $250$, $300$, $400$, and $500\,\mathrm{mm}$. Select the pipe size and find the actual mean speed.

::schema{acqua="0,0.8 2.4,0.8 2.4,3.6 0,3.6" solidi="-0.25,0.55 0,0.55 0,3.8 -0.25,3.8;-0.25,0.55 2.65,0.55 2.65,0.8 -0.25,0.8;2.4,0.8 2.65,0.8 2.65,1.55 2.4,1.55;2.4,2.05 2.65,2.05 2.65,3.8 2.4,3.8" pelo="0,2.4,3.6" nodi="A,2.4,1.8,-;B,9,1.8,-" condotte="A-B,0.5,D = ?" frecce="3.2,1.8,4.6,1.8,Q" riferimento=0 titolo="Supply pipeline with $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The diameter is selected from standard sizes"}

$$
Q = 0.120\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
A \geq \frac{Q}{v_{\max}} = \frac{0.120}{1.50}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0800\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
D \geq \sqrt{\frac{4A}{\pi}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 \cdot 0.0800}{\pi}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.319\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
D = 400\,\mathrm{mm},
\qquad
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.400)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.126\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.120}{0.126}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.955\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Test the next smaller standard pipe diameter:
$$
D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}:
\qquad
v = \frac{0.120}{0.0707}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s} > 1.50\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean velocity is $\dfrac{Q}{A}$. At the specified discharge, the velocity limit therefore sets a lower bound on flow area. Any area smaller than $0.0800\,\mathrm{m^2}$ would exceed the allowable velocity.

Solving $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$ gives $D = \sqrt{\dfrac{4A}{\pi}}$ and hence the minimum diameter $0.319\,\mathrm{m}$. Select the first available standard size above this value, namely $400\,\mathrm{mm}$.

With this diameter, the actual velocity is $0.955\,\mathrm{m/s}$, below the limit. The requirement is **satisfied**. The next smaller diameter, $300\,\mathrm{mm}$, would exceed the allowable velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The pump discharge pipe shown has diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries water at mass flow rate $Q_m = 110\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. Find the volumetric flow rate, mean speed, and volume pumped in $8$ hours of continuous operation.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,7,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.1,D = 250 mm" frecce="1.5,0,3.0,0,Q_m = 110 kg/s" riferimento=0 titolo="Discharge pipe in an installation for which the mass flow rate is given"}

$$
Q = \frac{Q_m}{\rho} = \frac{110}{1000}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.110\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 110\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.250)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.110}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.24\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
V = Q\,\Delta t = 0.110 \cdot 8 \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{m^3} = 3168\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

Check the mass balance over $8$ hours:
$$
M = Q_m\,\Delta t = 110 \cdot 28\,800\,\mathrm{kg} = 3.168 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{kg} = \rho\,V = 1000 \cdot 3168\,\mathrm{kg}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With uniform density across the section, $Q_m = \rho\,Q$ relates mass and volumetric flow rates. Dividing by $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ gives $Q = 0.110\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. For water, the numerical values in liters per second and kilograms per second coincide because one liter of water has a mass of one kilogram.

The mean velocity $2.24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is acceptable for a pump discharge pipe, where higher velocities than in a transmission main allow a smaller diameter.

Multiply the discharge by the operating time in seconds to find the volume pumped. A duration of $8$ hours is $28\,800\,\mathrm{s}$. Repeating the calculation with mass gives the same result, **as expected** for a fluid of constant density.

</div>
