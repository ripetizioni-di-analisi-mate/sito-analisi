## Exercise 1

A pipe of diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries flow at a mean velocity of $v_1 = 0.700\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and narrows to diameter $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, as shown. Calculate the discharge and the mean velocity in the narrower section.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3.8,0,-;D,7.5,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.2,D_1 = 300 mm;C-D,0.6,D_2 = 150 mm" linee="3,0.6 3.8,0.3;3,-0.6 3.8,-0.3" frecce="1.7,0,2.9,0,v_1;5.0,0,6.2,0,v_2" tratteggi="0.6,-0.65 0.6,0.65;6.9,-0.35 6.9,0.35" etichette="0.6,-0.95,1;6.9,-0.65,2" riferimento=0 titolo="Contraction between sections $1$ and $2$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.300)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.150)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0177\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = A_1 v_1 = 0.0707 \cdot 0.700\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0495\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 49.5\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_2 = \frac{A_1}{A_2}\,v_1 = \frac{0.0707}{0.0177} \cdot 0.700\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.80\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Check the result using the diameter ratio.
$$
v_2 = v_1 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^{\!2} = 0.700 \cdot \left(\frac{300}{150}\right)^{\!2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.700 \cdot 4 = 2.80\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fluid is incompressible and the flow is steady, so the **continuity equation** gives the same discharge at both sections. Calculate it at section $1$, where both area and velocity are known.

From $A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$, obtain $v_2$ by dividing the discharge by the area at section $2$. The area ratio is $4$ because area varies with the square of diameter. The velocity in the narrower section is therefore four times the upstream velocity.

The diameter-ratio check gives the same result without calculating the areas separately. Both velocities lie in the usual range for pipe flow.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The pipe shown consists of three reaches in series with diameters $D_1 = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$, $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $D_3 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$, carrying discharge $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Calculate the mean velocity in each reach and identify where it is greatest.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,2.6,0,-;C,3.2,0,-;D,5.4,0,-;E,6.0,0,-;F,8.6,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.2,D_1 = 400 mm;C-D,0.75,D_2 = 250 mm;E-F,0.9,D_3 = 300 mm" linee="2.6,0.6 3.2,0.375;2.6,-0.6 3.2,-0.375;5.4,0.375 6.0,0.45;5.4,-0.375 6.0,-0.45" frecce="0.6,0,1.6,0,Q" riferimento=0 titolo="Three pipe sections in series carrying the same flow rate $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$"}

$$
Q = 0.120\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.400)^2}{4} = 0.126\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.250)^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_3 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.300)^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.120}{0.126}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.955\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{0.120}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.44\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_3 = \frac{0.120}{0.0707}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Check the product of area and velocity in each reach.
$$
A_1 v_1 = 0.126 \cdot 0.955 = 0.120\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
A_2 v_2 = 0.0491 \cdot 2.44 = 0.120\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
A_3 v_3 = 0.0707 \cdot 1.70 = 0.120\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three reaches form a single streamtube with no intervening branches, so the discharge is the same in all three. Divide that discharge by the area of each reach to find its mean velocity.

Velocity is **highest in the reach with the smallest diameter**, the second reach, where it equals $2.44\,\mathrm{m/s}$. It is lowest in the first reach, which has the largest area. The velocities are inversely proportional to the areas, and the areas are proportional to the squares of the diameters.

Multiplying each area by the velocity found for that reach recovers $0.120\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ in all three cases, matching the given discharge.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The pipe shown has diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$, carries a discharge of $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and ends in a nozzle that produces a jet of velocity $v_2 = 8.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate the mean velocity in the pipe and the nozzle exit diameter.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3.2,0,-;C,4.4,0,-;D,5.2,0,-" condotte="A-B,1.2,D_1 = 300 mm" linee="3.2,0.6 4.4,0.17;3.2,-0.6 4.4,-0.17;4.4,0.17 5.2,0.17;4.4,-0.17 5.2,-0.17" acqua="5.2,0.17 7.6,0.17 7.6,-0.17 5.2,-0.17" frecce="1.2,0,2.4,0,v_1;6.1,0,7.4,0,v_2" etichette="4.9,0.52,D_2" riferimento=0 titolo="Pipe terminating in a nozzle from which the jet emerges with $v_2 = 8.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$"}

$$
Q = 0.0800\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.300)^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{Q}{A_1} = \frac{0.0800}{0.0707}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.13\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A_2 = \frac{Q}{v_2} = \frac{0.0800}{8.00}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0100\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
D_2 = \sqrt{\frac{4 A_2}{\pi}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 \cdot 0.0100}{\pi}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.113\,\mathrm{m} = 113\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

Check the discharge in the jet.
$$
Q = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.113)^2}{4} \cdot 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0100 \cdot 8.00 = 0.0800\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All the flow entering the nozzle leaves in the jet because the nozzle forms an unbranched streamtube. The continuity equation therefore relates the exit area to the jet velocity.

Divide the discharge by the pipe area to obtain the mean pipe velocity. At the nozzle exit the area is unknown. Solving $Q = A_2 v_2$ gives $A_2 = \dfrac{Q}{v_2}$, and the diameter then follows from $D_2 = \sqrt{\dfrac{4A_2}{\pi}}$.

The exit diameter is $113\,\mathrm{mm}$, just over one-third of the pipe diameter. The velocity increases by a factor of about seven, equal to the ratio of the areas. Using the calculated diameter and the jet velocity recovers the specified discharge $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

</div>
