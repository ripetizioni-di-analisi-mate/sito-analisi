## Exercise 1

A two-dimensional incompressible flow has velocity field $\vec{v} = (k\,x,\;-k\,y)$, with $k = 0.500\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, coordinates in meters, and velocities in $\mathrm{m/s}$. Verify that the field satisfies the continuity equation, find the streamlines, and calculate the velocity at point $P = (2.00;\,1.00)$.

$$
\operatorname{div}\vec{v} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(k\,x) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-k\,y) = k - k = 0
$$
$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{v_y}{v_x} = \frac{-k\,y}{k\,x} = -\frac{y}{x}
$$
$$
\frac{dy}{y} = -\frac{dx}{x}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ln|y| = -\ln|x| + c
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x\,y = \mathrm{constant}
$$
$$
v_x(P) = 0.500 \cdot 2.00\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_y(P) = -0.500 \cdot 1.00\,\mathrm{m/s} = -0.500\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}(P)| = \sqrt{(1.00)^2 + (0.500)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.12\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Check that the velocity is tangent to the streamline through $P$.
$$
x\,y = 2.00
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = \frac{2.00}{x},
\qquad
\frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{x = 2.00} = -\frac{2.00}{(2.00)^2} = -0.500 = \frac{v_y(P)}{v_x(P)} = \frac{-0.500}{1.00}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Divergence is the sum of the partial derivatives of the velocity components with respect to their corresponding coordinates. Here those derivatives are $k$ and $-k$. Their sum is zero, so the field **is consistent** with incompressible flow.

A streamline is tangent to the velocity vector everywhere, so its slope is $\dfrac{v_y}{v_x}$. Separating variables and integrating gives $\ln|y| + \ln|x| = c$. The product $x\,y$ is therefore constant on any given streamline. The curves are rectangular hyperbolas whose asymptotes are the coordinate axes.

Substitute the coordinates of $P$ into the field to obtain the velocity components. The $y$ component is negative, so a particle passing through $P$ moves downward and to the right, as shown.

The derivative of the streamline through $P$ agrees with the ratio of the velocity components there. The velocity is therefore tangent to the calculated streamline.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The horizontal velocity component in a two-dimensional incompressible flow is $v_x = 2k\,x\,y$, with $k = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^{-1}s^{-1}}$. Determine the vertical component $v_y$ given that it vanishes on the $x$ axis. Find the streamlines and calculate the velocity at point $P = (3.00;\,2.00)$.

$$
\operatorname{div}\vec{v} = \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2k\,x\,y) = -2k\,y
$$
$$
v_y = \int (-2k\,y)\,dy = -k\,y^2 + f(x),
\qquad
v_y(x,\,0) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; f(x) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_y = -k\,y^2
$$
$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-k\,y^2}{2k\,x\,y} = -\frac{y}{2x}
$$
$$
\frac{dy}{y} = -\frac{dx}{2x}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ln|y| = -\frac{1}{2}\ln|x| + c
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = \frac{c'}{\sqrt{x}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x\,y^2 = \mathrm{constant}
$$
$$
v_x(P) = 2 \cdot 0.100 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 2.00\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_y(P) = -0.100 \cdot (2.00)^2\,\mathrm{m/s} = -0.400\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}(P)| = \sqrt{(1.20)^2 + (0.400)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Check the streamline through $P$.
$$
x\,y^2 = 3.00 \cdot (2.00)^2 = 12.0,
\qquad
y = \sqrt{\frac{12.0}{x}},
\qquad
\frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{x = 3.00} = -\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{12.0}{x^3}}\bigg|_{x = 3.00} = -0.333 = \frac{-0.400}{1.20}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Local continuity relates the derivatives of the two velocity components and can therefore be used to solve for $v_y$. The derivative of $v_y$ with respect to $y$ must be the negative of the derivative of $v_x$ with respect to $x$. The latter derivative equals $2k\,y$.

Integrating with respect to $y$ gives $v_y = -k\,y^2 + f(x)$. Here $f$ depends only on $x$, since differentiating a function of $x$ with respect to $y$ gives zero. The condition $v_y = 0$ on the $x$ axis, where $y = 0$, requires $f(x) = 0$ for every $x$. Thus $v_y = -k\,y^2$.

The streamline equation is again separable. The factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ introduced on integration gives the square root, so the streamlines are the curves $x\,y^2 = \mathrm{constant}$.

Differentiate the equation of the streamline through $P$ and compare its slope with the ratio of the velocity components at that point. Both give $-0.333$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A two-dimensional incompressible flow has velocity field $\vec{v} = \big(k\,(1 + t)\,x,\;-k\,(1 + t)\,y\big)$, with $k = 0.200\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, coordinates in meters, and time in seconds. Determine whether the flow is steady, verify the continuity equation, and find the position at time $t = 2.00\,\mathrm{s}$ of the particle that is at $P_0 = (1.00;\,2.00)$ at time $t = 0$.

$$
\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial t} = k\,x \neq 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{unsteady flow}
$$
$$
\operatorname{div}\vec{v} = k\,(1 + t) - k\,(1 + t) = 0
\qquad \text{for each } t
$$
$$
\frac{dx}{dt} = k\,(1 + t)\,x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{dx}{x} = k\,(1 + t)\,dt
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ln\frac{x}{x_0} = k\left(t + \frac{t^2}{2}\right)
$$
$$
x(t) = x_0\,e^{\,k\left(t + \frac{t^2}{2}\right)},
\qquad
y(t) = y_0\,e^{-k\left(t + \frac{t^2}{2}\right)}
$$
$$
k\left(t + \frac{t^2}{2}\right)\bigg|_{t = 2.00} = 0.200 \cdot (2.00 + 2.00) = 0.800
$$
$$
x(2.00) = 1.00 \cdot e^{\,0.800}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.23\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
y(2.00) = 2.00 \cdot e^{-0.800}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.899\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the product of the two coordinates.
$$
x(t)\,y(t) = x_0 y_0\,e^{\,k\left(t + \frac{t^2}{2}\right)}e^{-k\left(t + \frac{t^2}{2}\right)} = x_0 y_0 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
2.23 \cdot 0.899 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,2/t,0.42,5" unicolore=1 vettori="1,2,1.2,1.6,v a t = 0;2.23,0.9,3.56,0.36,v a t = 2 s" punti="1,2,pieno,P₀;2.23,0.9,pieno,P(2 s)" uguale=1 xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=4 nomi="x,y" titolo="Path of the particle starting at $P_0$ on the streamline $x\,y = 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At a fixed point, velocity depends on time through the factor $1 + t$. The time derivative is therefore nonzero in general and the flow is **unsteady**.

Calculate divergence as in the preceding exercises. The factor $k\,(1+t)$ enters the two derivatives with opposite signs, so their sum is zero at every instant, as incompressibility requires.

The pathline follows from the initial-value problem $\dfrac{d\vec{x}}{dt} = \vec{v}(\vec{x}, t)$ with the specified initial position. The coordinate equations are independent because the first contains only the coordinate $x$ and the second only $y$. Both are separable, and integrating the time-dependent side gives $k\left(t + \dfrac{t^2}{2}\right)$.

The product $x\,y$ retains its initial value $2.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the particle moves along the hyperbola $x\,y = 2$. These same hyperbolas form the streamlines at each instant because the factor $1 + t$ cancels in the ratio $\dfrac{v_y}{v_x}$. The flow is unsteady, yet the velocity direction at each point stays fixed, so the pathline here coincides with a streamline.

</div>
