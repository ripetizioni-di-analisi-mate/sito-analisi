## Exercise 1

The horizontal pipe shown changes from diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$. At section $1$ the gauge pressure is $p_1 = 250\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting head loss, find the two speeds and the pressure at section $2$.

::schema{acqua="0,0.65 4.6,0.65 4.6,0.33 11,0.33 11,-0.33 4.6,-0.33 4.6,-0.65 0,-0.65" linee="0,0.65 4.6,0.65 4.6,0.33 11,0.33;0,-0.65 4.6,-0.65 4.6,-0.33 11,-0.33" tratteggi="0,0 11,0" frecce="1,0,2.4,0,Q" etichette="2.6,-1.05,1;7.8,-0.75,2;2.2,1.05,D_1 = 200 mm;7.4,0.75,D_2 = 100 mm" punti="2.6,0,,pieno;7.8,0,,pieno" riferimento=0 titolo="Contraction in a horizontal pipe with $p_1 = 250\,\mathrm{kPa}$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.200)^2}{4} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.100)^2}{4} = 0.00785\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.040}{0.0314} = 1.273\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.040}{0.00785} = 5.093\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{1.621}{19.62} = 0.083\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\frac{v_2^2}{2g} = \frac{25.94}{19.62} = 1.322\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
Q = A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 \\[4pt]
z_1 + \dfrac{p_1}{\gamma} + \dfrac{v_1^2}{2g} = z_2 + \dfrac{p_2}{\gamma} + \dfrac{v_2^2}{2g}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = \frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} - \frac{v_2^2}{2g}
= 25.48 + 0.083 - 1.322 = 24.24\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_2 = \gamma \cdot 24.24 = 9.81 \cdot 24.24 = 238\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check using the pressure form of Bernoulli’s equation:
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = 500 \cdot (25.94 - 1.621) = 12\,160\,\mathrm{Pa} = 12.16\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
p_2 = 250 - 12.16 = 238\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two sections lie in a one-dimensional stream. For steady flow of an ideal fluid, apply continuity and **Bernoulli’s equation** with $\alpha = 1$. Solve these equations together. Continuity gives the two velocities, and the energy equation then gives the unknown pressure.

Halving the diameter reduces the area to one quarter and increases the speed by a factor of four. Velocity head varies with the square of the speed, so it is sixteen times greater in the narrow section than in the wide section.

The pipe is horizontal, so $z_1 = z_2$ and the elevation terms cancel. Bernoulli’s equation then gives the same sum of pressure head and velocity head at both sections. The increase in velocity head therefore requires an equal decrease in pressure head, $1.239\,\mathrm{m}$.

The pressure in the narrow section, $238\,\mathrm{kPa}$, is **lower** than in the wide section. The hydraulic grade line therefore steps down at the contraction. The energy grade line stays horizontal because head loss is neglected.

To check the result, multiply the three head terms by $\gamma$ to write the balance in pressure units. The static pressure drop equals the increase in dynamic pressure, giving the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The pipe shown rises from section $1$ of diameter $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and elevation $z_1 = 0$ to section $2$ of diameter $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and elevation $z_2 = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The discharge is $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the gauge pressure at $1$ is $p_1 = 320\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting losses, find the pressure at $2$.

::schema{nodi="A,1,0.8,-;B,4,0.8,-;C,8,5,-;D,11,5,-" condotte="A-B,0.7;B-C,0.5;C-D,0.5" linee="0,0 12,0" frecce="1.2,0.8,2.2,0.8,Q" punti="3.2,0.8,1,pieno;9.6,5,2,pieno" etichette="2,1.6,D_1 = 250 mm;8.4,5.8,D_2 = 150 mm;3,-0.7,elevation datum" quote="10.6 0,10.6 5,5.00 m,0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Diameter reduction in a rising pipe with $p_1 = 320\,\mathrm{kPa}$"}

$$
A_1 = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = 0.0177\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.060}{0.0491} = 1.222\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.060}{0.0177} = 3.395\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{1.494}{19.62} = 0.076\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\frac{v_2^2}{2g} = \frac{11.53}{19.62} = 0.588\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = z_1 + \frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} - z_2 - \frac{v_2^2}{2g}
$$
$$
\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = 0 + 32.62 + 0.076 - 5.00 - 0.588 = 27.11\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_2 = 9.81 \cdot 27.11 = 266\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check the total head at both sections:
$$
H_1 = 0 + 32.62 + 0.076 = 32.70\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
H_2 = 5.00 + 27.11 + 0.588 = 32.70\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take the centerline at section $1$ as the datum, giving $z_1 = 0$ and $z_2 = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Moving the datum leaves the result unchanged because only the elevation difference enters Bernoulli’s equation.

Divide the discharge by the respective areas to find the velocities. Total head at section $1$ is $32.70\,\mathrm{m}$. Since head loss is neglected, section $2$ has the same total head.

At section $2$ the elevation and velocity head are known. Subtracting them from total head gives the pressure head, $27.11\,\mathrm{m}$. Multiplying by $\gamma$ gives the pressure, $266\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

Two effects lower the pressure. The flow rises by $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and accelerates as the pipe narrows. The elevation change accounts for $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of water head, while the acceleration accounts for $0.512\,\mathrm{m}$. Thus almost the entire pressure drop comes from the rise in elevation.

Recalculate total head at both sections using the value found for $p_2$. The results agree, as required for this ideal flow.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A converging nozzle at the end of the horizontal pipe shown has upstream diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and outlet diameter $D_2 = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharges to the atmosphere. At upstream section $1$ the gauge pressure is $p_1 = 180\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting head loss, find the discharge and jet speed.

::schema{acqua="0,0.75 6,0.75 7.4,0.25 9.6,0.25 9.6,-0.25 7.4,-0.25 6,-0.75 0,-0.75;9.6,0.25 12,0.25 12,-0.25 9.6,-0.25" linee="0,0.75 6,0.75 7.4,0.25 9.6,0.25;0,-0.75 6,-0.75 7.4,-0.25 9.6,-0.25" tratteggi="0,0 12,0" frecce="1,0,2.4,0,Q;10.2,0,11.6,0,v_2" etichette="3.4,-1.15,1;8.6,-0.65,2;3,1.15,D_1 = 150 mm;8.2,0.75,D_2 = 50 mm" punti="3.4,0,,pieno;8.6,0,,pieno" riferimento=0 titolo="Converging nozzle discharging to the atmosphere, with $p_1 = 180\,\mathrm{kPa}$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.150)^2}{4} = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.050)^2}{4} = 0.001963\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\frac{150}{50}\right)^2 = 9
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_2 = 9\,v_1
$$
$$
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{v_2^2}{2g}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{(9 v_1)^2 - v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{80\,v_1^2}{2g}
$$
$$
v_1^2 = \frac{2g}{80} \cdot \frac{p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{19.62}{80} \cdot 18.35 = 4.50\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \sqrt{4.50} = 2.12\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = 9 \cdot 2.12 = 19.09\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = A_1 v_1 = 0.01767 \cdot 2.12 = 0.0375\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 37.5\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the velocity heads at both sections:
$$
\frac{v_2^2}{2g} - \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{364.5 - 4.50}{19.62} = 18.35\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{p_1}{\gamma}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The nozzle discharges to the atmosphere, so the gauge pressure at the outlet is zero. The pipe is horizontal, leaving only the pressure-head and velocity-head terms in Bernoulli’s equation.

The **continuity equation** relates the two velocities. Their ratio is the inverse ratio of the cross-sectional areas, or the square of the corresponding diameter ratio, $9$. Substituting $v_2 = 9 v_1$ into Bernoulli’s equation leaves one unknown.

The available upstream pressure head, $18.35\,\mathrm{m}$, is converted into an increase in velocity head equal to $80$ times the upstream velocity head. This gives $v_1$. Multiplying by the area then gives the discharge, **$37.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$**.

The jet speed is $19.09\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The nozzle converts pressure energy in the pipe into kinetic energy in the jet, the operating principle behind fire hose nozzles and impulse turbines.

As a check, subtract the two velocity heads. Their difference equals the upstream pressure head, confirming that the calculated values satisfy Bernoulli’s equation.

</div>
