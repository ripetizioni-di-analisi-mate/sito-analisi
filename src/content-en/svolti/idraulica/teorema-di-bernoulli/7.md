## Exercise 1

The horizontal pipe shown has diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and centerline elevation $20.00\,\mathrm{m}$. It connects reservoir $A$ with surface elevation $30.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to reservoir $B$ and carries discharge $Q = 90\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The entrance loss is $0.5\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, and the two pipe reaches of lengths $300\,\mathrm{m}$ and $200\,\mathrm{m}$ both have energy gradient $J = 0.00900$. Find the total heads and pressures at $1$, $M$, and $2$, and the surface elevation in reservoir $B$.

::schema{acqua="0.2,0.6 2.4,0.6 2.4,10 0.2,10;15,0.6 18.4,0.6 18.4,6.8 15,6.8" solidi="0,0 2.6,0 2.6,0.6 0,0.6;0,0 0.2,0 0.2,10.6 0,10.6;2.4,0.6 2.6,0.6 2.6,3.65 2.4,3.65;2.4,4.35 2.6,4.35 2.6,10.6 2.4,10.6;14.8,0 18.6,0 18.6,0.6 14.8,0.6;14.8,0.6 15,0.6 15,3.65 14.8,3.65;14.8,4.35 15,4.35 15,7.4 14.8,7.4;18.4,0 18.6,0 18.6,7.4 18.4,7.4" pelo="0.2,2.4,10;15,18.4,6.8" condotte="2.6 4-15 4,0.7" punti="3,4,1,pieno;10,4,M,pieno;14.6,4,2,pieno" quote="2.6 2.8,10 2.8,300 m,0;10 2.8,15 2.8,200 m,0;4.6 4,4.6 10,10.00 m,0" etichette="1.3,10.5,A;16.3,7.3,B;12.2,4.9,D = 250 mm;7,4.9,centerline at 20.00 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Pipe connecting two reservoirs. The free surface of $A$ is at elevation $30.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the pipe centerline at $20.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.250)^2}{4} = 0.04909\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
v = \frac{0.090}{0.04909} = 1.833\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{3.360}{19.62} = 0.171\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{imb}} = 0.5 \cdot 0.171 = 0.086\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta H_{1,M} = J L_1 = 0.00900 \cdot 300 = 2.70\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta H_{M,2} = J L_2 = 0.00900 \cdot 200 = 1.80\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta H_{\mathrm{sb}} = \frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.171\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_1 &= 30.00 - 0.086 = 29.91\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
H_M &= 29.91 - 2.70 = 27.21\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
H_2 &= 27.21 - 1.80 = 25.41\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
h = H - \frac{v^2}{2g}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
h_1 = 29.74\,\mathrm{m},
\quad
h_M = 27.04\,\mathrm{m},
\quad
h_2 = 25.24\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = h - 20.00
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} = 9.74\,\mathrm{m},
\quad
\frac{p_M}{\gamma} = 7.04\,\mathrm{m},
\quad
\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = 5.24\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_1 = 95.6\,\mathrm{kPa},
\qquad
p_M = 69.1\,\mathrm{kPa},
\qquad
p_2 = 51.4\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
z_B = H_2 - \frac{v^2}{2g} = 25.41 - 0.171 = 25.24\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the overall head balance between the two free surfaces:
$$
0.086 + 2.70 + 1.80 + 0.171 = 4.76\,\mathrm{m}
= 30.00 - 25.24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diameter is constant. The given discharge therefore yields the same velocity and velocity head at every section. The hydraulic and energy grade lines run parallel with a vertical separation of $0.171\,\mathrm{m}$.

The energy grade line starts at $30.00\,\mathrm{m}$, the free-surface elevation of $A$, and drops as losses accumulate. The entrance dissipates $0.5\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ over a very short distance, producing a step in the line. Along the two straight reaches the friction loss is proportional to length, so the line descends at the constant hydraulic gradient $J$.

Subtract velocity head from total head to obtain piezometric head. Then subtract the pipe centerline elevation to obtain pressure head. The pipe is horizontal, so this elevation is $20.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at every section. All gauge pressures are positive and decrease downstream.

At the outlet into reservoir $B$ the water comes to rest and loses its entire velocity head. The free surface of $B$ therefore lies at **$25.24\,\mathrm{m}$**, or $0.171\,\mathrm{m}$ below the energy grade line at section $2$, precisely level with the hydraulic grade line at that section.

As a check, add the four head losses. Their sum equals the elevation difference between the two free surfaces.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The pipe shown connects reservoir $A$, whose surface is at $40.00\,\mathrm{m}$, to reservoir $B$. The first reach has diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L_1 = 300\,\mathrm{m}$, and energy gradient $J_1 = 0.0130$. The second has diameter $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, length $L_2 = 200\,\mathrm{m}$, and energy gradient $J_2 = 0.0570$. The discharge is $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Neglecting local losses at the entrance and diameter change, find the change in hydraulic grade at the junction and the surface elevation in reservoir $B$.

::schema{acqua="0.2,0.6 2.4,0.6 2.4,10 0.2,10;16,0.6 19.4,0.6 19.4,4.4 16,4.4" solidi="0,0 2.6,0 2.6,0.6 0,0.6;0,0 0.2,0 0.2,10.6 0,10.6;2.4,0.6 2.6,0.6 2.6,3.6 2.4,3.6;2.4,4.4 2.6,4.4 2.6,10.6 2.4,10.6;15.8,0 19.6,0 19.6,0.6 15.8,0.6;15.8,0.6 16,0.6 16,3.7 15.8,3.7;15.8,4.3 16,4.3 16,5 15.8,5;19.4,0 19.6,0 19.6,5 19.4,5" pelo="0.2,2.4,10;16,19.4,4.4" condotte="2.6 4-10 4,0.8;10 4-16 4,0.6" punti="10,4,G,pieno" quote="2.6 2.6,10 2.6,L_1 = 300 m,0;10 2.6,16 2.6,L_2 = 200 m,0" etichette="1.3,10.5,A;17.3,5.4,B;6,5.4,D_1 = 200 mm;13,5.2,D_2 = 150 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Two pipe sections of different diameters joined at $G$"}

$$
A_1 = 0.03142\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.050}{0.03142} = 1.592\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.050}{0.01767} = 2.829\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{2.534}{19.62} = 0.129\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\frac{v_2^2}{2g} = \frac{8.006}{19.62} = 0.408\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta H_1 = J_1 L_1 = 0.0130 \cdot 300 = 3.90\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta H_2 = J_2 L_2 = 0.0570 \cdot 200 = 11.40\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_G = 40.00 - 3.90 = 36.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_G^- = 36.10 - 0.129 = 35.97\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_G^+ = 36.10 - 0.408 = 35.69\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{drop} = h_G^- - h_G^+ = \frac{v_2^2}{2g} - \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 0.408 - 0.129 = 0.279\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 = 36.10 - 11.40 = 24.70\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
z_B = H_2 - \frac{v_2^2}{2g} = 24.70 - 0.408 = 24.29\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the elevation difference between the free surfaces:
$$
40.00 - 24.29 = 15.71\,\mathrm{m}
= 3.90 + 11.40 + 0.408
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The reaches carry the same discharge but have different areas. The flow is faster in the second reach, where the velocity head is about three times that in the first.

At junction $G$ the total head is continuous because the minor loss at the change of diameter is neglected. Just upstream of the junction, piezometric head is $H_G$ minus the velocity head in the first reach. Just downstream it is $H_G$ minus the velocity head in the second. The hydraulic grade line therefore **steps down** by $0.279\,\mathrm{m}$, the difference between the two velocity heads.

The energy grade line descends continuously, with gradients $J_1$ in the first reach and $J_2$ in the second. The second gradient is more than four times the first because the diameter is smaller and the velocity higher.

The free surface in reservoir $B$ is at **$24.29\,\mathrm{m}$**, level with the hydraulic grade line at the last pipe section. The required difference between reservoir elevations is $15.71\,\mathrm{m}$. More than two thirds is lost in the second reach, even though its length is only two thirds that of the first.

Check the free-surface elevation difference against the sum of the losses. Include the outlet loss, which equals the velocity head in the second reach.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The pipe shown, of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, connects two constant-level reservoirs. Each of its two reaches has length $250\,\mathrm{m}$ and energy gradient $J = 0.0150$. A partly closed valve at midlength has loss coefficient $\xi = 8$. The entrance loss is $0.5\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ and the entire velocity head is lost at the outlet. Given discharge $Q = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$, find the required difference in reservoir surface elevations and the total heads at the pipe sections.

::schema{acqua="0.2,3.4 2.4,3.4 2.4,9 0.2,9;16,0.6 19.4,0.6 19.4,3.4 16,3.4" solidi="0,2.8 2.6,2.8 2.6,3.4 0,3.4;0,2.8 0.2,2.8 0.2,9.6 0,9.6;2.4,3.4 2.6,3.4 2.6,5.65 2.4,5.65;2.4,6.35 2.6,6.35 2.6,9.6 2.4,9.6;15.8,0 19.6,0 19.6,0.6 15.8,0.6;15.8,0.6 16,0.6 16,2.65 15.8,2.65;15.8,3.35 16,3.35 16,4 15.8,4;19.4,0 19.6,0 19.6,4 19.4,4" pelo="0.2,2.4,9;16,19.4,3.4" condotte="2.6 6-16 3,0.7" punti="3,5.86,1,pieno;9.3,4.5,V,pieno;15.6,3.14,2,pieno" linee="9,5.3 9.6,3.7;9,3.7 9.6,5.3" quote="17.6 3.4,17.6 9,Δz,0.9" etichette="1.3,9.5,A;18.2,4.2,B;8.8,6.1,valve ξ = 8;5.4,6.6,250 m;12.4,5,250 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Pipe with a valve at its midpoint, dividing two sections of length $250\,\mathrm{m}$ with $J = 0.0150$"}

$$
A = 0.03142\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
v = \frac{0.070}{0.03142} = 2.228\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{4.964}{19.62} = 0.253\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta H_{\mathrm{imb}} &= 0.5 \cdot 0.253 = 0.127\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\Delta H_{\mathrm{segment}} &= J L = 0.0150 \cdot 250 = 3.75\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\Delta H_{\mathrm{val}} &= 8 \cdot 0.253 = 2.024\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\Delta H_{\mathrm{sb}} &= 0.253\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\Delta z = 0.127 + 3.75 + 2.024 + 3.75 + 0.253 = 9.90\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Total heads with the upstream free surface as the datum.
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_1 &= 0 - 0.127 = -0.13\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
H_V^- &= -0.13 - 3.75 = -3.88\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
H_V^+ &= -3.88 - 2.024 = -5.90\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
H_2 &= -5.90 - 3.75 = -9.65\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

Check the elevation of the downstream free surface:
$$
H_2 - \frac{v^2}{2g} = -9.65 - 0.253 = -9.90\,\mathrm{m} = -\Delta z
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In both reservoirs the velocity is negligible and gauge pressure is zero. The difference in free-surface elevations therefore equals the sum of all losses. Calculate them in order at the entrance, along the first reach, at the valve, along the second reach, and at the outlet.

Friction loss equals hydraulic gradient times reach length, giving $3.75\,\mathrm{m}$ in each reach. For each minor loss, multiply the loss coefficient by the velocity head. The required elevation difference is **$9.90\,\mathrm{m}$**.

The partially closed valve dissipates $2.02\,\mathrm{m}$, over one fifth of the available head, in a negligible length of pipe. At the valve the energy grade line drops vertically. Along the two reaches it descends at the same gradient $J$. Since the diameter is constant, the hydraulic grade line stays parallel to the energy grade line.

Taking the upstream free surface as datum gives an initial total head of zero. Subtract each loss in sequence to find the total heads along the pipe. The head at the final section is $-9.65\,\mathrm{m}$.

Subtract the velocity head dissipated at the outlet from the total head at the last section. This gives the downstream free-surface elevation, $-9.90\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the upstream surface.

</div>
