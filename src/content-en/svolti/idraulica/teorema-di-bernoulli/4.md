## Exercise 1

A Venturi meter in the horizontal pipe shown has upstream diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and throat diameter $D_2 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$. Two piezometers at those sections show a level difference $\Delta h = 0.85\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting head loss, find the discharge and both section speeds.

::schema{acqua="0,0.6 3,0.6 4.4,0.3 6,0.3 7.4,0.6 11,0.6 11,-0.6 7.4,-0.6 6,-0.3 4.4,-0.3 3,-0.6 0,-0.6" linee="0,0.6 3,0.6 4.4,0.3 6,0.3 7.4,0.6 11,0.6;0,-0.6 3,-0.6 4.4,-0.3 6,-0.3 7.4,-0.6 11,-0.6;1.5,0.6 1.5,4 2.1,4 2.1,0.6;5,0.3 5,3.3 5.6,3.3 5.6,0.3" tratteggi="0,0 11,0;2.1,4 7.2,4;5.6,3.3 7.2,3.3" frecce="0.3,0,1.2,0,Q" etichette="1.8,-0.95,1;5.3,-0.95,2;1.8,-1.5,D_1 = 200 mm;5.3,-1.5,D_2 = 100 mm;8.8,3.5,Δh = 0.85 m" quote="6.9 3.3,6.9 4,,0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Venturi meter with two piezometers, $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $D_2 = 100\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.200)^2}{4} = 0.03142\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.100)^2}{4} = 0.007854\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
\frac{A_2}{A_1} = \left(\frac{100}{200}\right)^2 = 0.250
$$
$$
Q = \frac{A_2}{\sqrt{1 - \left(\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}\right)^2}}\,\sqrt{2 g\,\Delta h}
= \frac{0.007854}{\sqrt{1 - 0.0625}}\,\sqrt{19.62 \cdot 0.85}
$$
$$
Q = \frac{0.007854}{0.9682} \cdot \sqrt{16.68} = 0.008112 \cdot 4.084 = 0.0331\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 33.1\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{Q}{A_1} = \frac{0.0331}{0.03142} = 1.054\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{Q}{A_2} = \frac{0.0331}{0.007854} = 4.218\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Use the velocities to calculate the difference between the piezometer levels:
$$
\frac{v_2^2 - v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{17.79 - 1.111}{19.62} = \frac{16.68}{19.62} = 0.850\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta h
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The piezometers measure piezometric head upstream and at the throat, so their level difference is $\Delta h = h_1 - h_2$. Because the pipe is horizontal, this is also the difference in pressure head.

The **Venturi meter** equation follows by applying continuity and Bernoulli’s equation between the two sections. The area ratio equals the square of the diameter ratio, $0.250$. The denominator $\sqrt{1 - 0.0625}$ therefore equals $0.9682$. It changes the result by just over three percent compared with neglecting the upstream velocity head.

The discharge is **$33.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$**. Dividing it by the respective areas gives the velocities. The throat has one quarter of the upstream area, so its velocity is four times as high.

As a check, use the calculated velocities to find the piezometer level difference. The difference in velocity head is $0.850\,\mathrm{m}$, matching the measured value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The Venturi meter shown is installed in a horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and has throat diameter $D_2 = 125\,\mathrm{mm}$. A differential mercury manometer shows a level difference $\Delta = 180\,\mathrm{mm}$. Mercury has specific gravity $13.6$. Neglecting head loss, find the discharge and pressure difference.

::schema{acqua="0,0.75 3,0.75 4.4,0.37 6,0.37 7.4,0.75 11,0.75 11,-0.75 7.4,-0.75 6,-0.37 4.4,-0.37 3,-0.75 0,-0.75" linee="0,0.75 3,0.75 4.4,0.37 6,0.37 7.4,0.75 11,0.75;0,-0.75 3,-0.75 4.4,-0.37 6,-0.37 7.4,-0.75 11,-0.75;1.8,-0.75 1.8,-3.2 5.2,-3.2 5.2,-0.37;1.55,-2.3 2.05,-2.3;4.95,-1.6 5.45,-1.6" tratteggi="0,0 11,0" frecce="0.3,0,1.2,0,Q" etichette="1.35,-1.2,1;5.65,-0.9,2;1.8,1.45,D_1 = 250 mm;5.2,1.05,D_2 = 125 mm;3.1,-3.6,mercury;8,-1.9,Δ = 180 mm" quote="6.3 -2.3,6.3 -1.6,,0.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Venturi meter with a differential mercury manometer"}

$$
\Delta h = h_1 - h_2 = \Delta\left(\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma} - 1\right) = 0.180 \cdot (13.6 - 1) = 0.180 \cdot 12.6 = 2.268\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.250)^2}{4} = 0.04909\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.125)^2}{4} = 0.01227\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
\frac{A_2}{A_1} = \left(\frac{125}{250}\right)^2 = 0.250
$$
$$
Q = \frac{0.01227}{\sqrt{1 - 0.0625}}\,\sqrt{19.62 \cdot 2.268}
= \frac{0.01227}{0.9682} \cdot \sqrt{44.50}
$$
$$
Q = 0.012673 \cdot 6.671 = 0.0845\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 84.5\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \gamma\,\Delta h = 9.81 \cdot 2.268 = 22.2\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check the difference in dynamic pressure:
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.0845}{0.04909} = 1.722\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.0845}{0.01227} = 6.889\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\rho\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = 500 \cdot (47.46 - 2.965) = 22\,250\,\mathrm{Pa} = 22.2\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mercury is denser than water and does not mix with it. Multiply the difference between the two mercury levels by $\dfrac{\gamma_m}{\gamma} - 1$, which is $12.6$ for mercury, to obtain the difference in piezometric head. A mercury level difference of $180\,\mathrm{mm}$ therefore corresponds to a water column of $2.268\,\mathrm{m}$.

A dense manometer fluid keeps the instrument compact. Measuring the same head difference with two water piezometers would require tubes more than two meters tall.

The Venturi meter equation gives a discharge of **$84.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$**. Since the pipe is horizontal, the two sections are at the same elevation. Multiply $\Delta h$ by the specific weight of water to find the pressure difference.

The difference between the dynamic pressures calculated from the two velocities provides an independent check. It gives the same pressure difference.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The Venturi meter shown is installed in an inclined pipe of diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$. Its throat, of diameter $D_2 = 75\,\mathrm{mm}$, lies $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the upstream section. The differential mercury manometer reads $\Delta = 120\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the gauge pressure at section $1$ is $p_1 = 160\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting losses, find the discharge and throat pressure.

::schema{nodi="A,0.6,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,6,2.4,-;D,8.4,2.4,-" condotte="A-B,0.6;B-C,0.4;C-D,0.4" linee="0,-1.2 9.4,-1.2" punti="2.2,0,1,pieno;7.4,2.4,2,pieno" etichette="1.4,0.9,D_1 = 150 mm;6.4,3.2,D_2 = 75 mm;4,-0.75,differential mercury manometer: Δ = 120 mm" quote="7.4 0,7.4 2.4,1.20 m,0.9" frecce="0.9,0,1.7,0,Q" riferimento=0 titolo="Venturi meter on an inclined pipe, with the throat $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ higher"}

$$
\Delta h = h_1 - h_2 = 0.120 \cdot 12.6 = 1.512\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.150)^2}{4} = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.075)^2}{4} = 0.004418\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = \frac{0.004418}{0.9682}\,\sqrt{19.62 \cdot 1.512}
= 0.004563 \cdot 5.447 = 0.0249\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 24.9\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
h_1 = z_1 + \frac{p_1}{\gamma} = 0 + \frac{160}{9.81} = 16.31\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_2 = h_1 - \Delta h = 16.31 - 1.51 = 14.80\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p_2}{\gamma} = h_2 - z_2 = 14.80 - 1.20 = 13.60\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
p_2 = 9.81 \cdot 13.60 = 133\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check the total head at both sections:
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.0249}{0.01767} = 1.406\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.0249}{0.004418} = 5.625\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
H_1 = 16.31 + \frac{1.977}{19.62} = 16.31 + 0.101 = 16.41\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 = 1.20 + 13.60 + \frac{31.64}{19.62} = 1.20 + 13.60 + 1.613 = 16.41\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The differential manometer measures the difference in piezometric head between its taps for any pipe inclination or tap elevations. Multiply the reading by $12.6$ to convert it to meters of water. This head difference goes directly into the Venturi meter equation.

The discharge is **$24.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$**. To find the throat pressure, first calculate piezometric head at section $1$ using its centerline as the datum. Subtract $\Delta h$ to obtain $h_2$. Subtract the throat elevation $z_2 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ to obtain the throat pressure head.

The throat pressure is $133\,\mathrm{kPa}$. It is lower than the upstream pressure because the flow both accelerates and rises to a higher elevation.

As a check, calculate total head at both sections. Both values are $16.41\,\mathrm{m}$, as Bernoulli’s equation requires when losses are negligible.

</div>
