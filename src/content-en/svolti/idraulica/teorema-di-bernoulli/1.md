## Exercise 1

A steel pipe of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries discharge $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$. At the section shown its centerline is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the datum and the gauge pressure is $p = 150\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Calculate the mean speed, velocity head, pressure head, piezometric head, and total head.

::schema{nodi="A,1,3,-;B,10,3,-" condotte="A-B,0.6" linee="0,0 11,0;5.5,3.3 5.5,4.35" cerchi="5.5,4.7,0.35" frecce="2,3,3.6,3,Q" etichette="7.2,4.7,p = 150 kPa;8.8,3.7,D = 200 mm;2,0.35,datum" quote="5.5 0,5.5 3,4.00 m,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Pipe cross section at the pressure gauge, with $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s} = 0.060\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.200)^2}{4} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.060}{0.0314} = 1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{(1.91)^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = \frac{3.648}{19.62} = 0.186\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = \frac{150}{9.81} = 15.29\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h = z + \frac{p}{\gamma} = 4.00 + 15.29 = 19.29\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H = h + \frac{v^2}{2g} = 19.29 + 0.186 = 19.48\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check total head using its pressure form:
$$
\gamma H = p + \gamma z + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = 150\,000 + 9810 \cdot 4.00 + 500 \cdot 3.648 = 191\,064\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
H = \frac{191\,064}{9810} = 19.48\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discharge is given in liters per second. Convert it to the SI units used for the other quantities with $1\,\mathrm{L/s} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

The cross section is a circle of diameter $D$. Dividing the discharge by its area gives a mean velocity of $1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}$, within the usual range for a pipe flowing under pressure.

The **velocity head**, calculated from mean speed using $\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$, is $0.186\,\mathrm{m}$. The **pressure head** is the gauge pressure divided by the specific weight of water, $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$. With pressure in kilopascals and specific weight in kilonewtons per cubic meter, their ratio is in meters.

Adding the elevation and pressure head gives the piezometric head, so the hydraulic grade line lies $19.29\,\mathrm{m}$ above the datum. Adding the velocity head gives total head. The energy grade line is therefore at elevation $19.48\,\mathrm{m}$, a distance $0.186\,\mathrm{m}$ higher.

An independent calculation expresses all three head terms in pressure units by multiplying them by $\gamma$. Add the static pressure, elevation term, and dynamic pressure, then divide by $\gamma$. This recovers the same total head and checks all three terms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The pipe shown carries discharge $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$. At section $1$ its diameter is $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$, centerline elevation $z_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and gauge pressure $p_1 = 280\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At section $2$ the corresponding values are $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$, $z_2 = 6.50\,\mathrm{m}$, and $p_2 = 210\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Find the two total heads, head loss, and direction of flow.

::schema{nodi="A,1,1.7,-;B,5,1.7,-;C,8,5.4,-;D,12,5.4,-" condotte="A-B,0.7;B-C,0.55;C-D,0.55" linee="0,0 13,0" etichette="2.6,2.5,D_1;9.4,6.1,D_2" quote="2 0,2 1.7,2.00 m,-0.9;11.4 0,11.4 5.4,6.50 m,0.9" punti="3.4,1.7,1,pieno;10.4,5.4,2,pieno" riferimento=0 titolo="Two pipe sections showing the elevations of their centerlines"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.250)^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.150)^2}{4} = 0.0177\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.050}{0.0491} = 1.019\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.050}{0.0177} = 2.829\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{1.038}{19.62} = 0.053\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\frac{v_2^2}{2g} = \frac{8.006}{19.62} = 0.408\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_1 &= z_1 + \frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 2.00 + \frac{280}{9.81} + 0.053 \\[6pt]
&= 2.00 + 28.542 + 0.053 = 30.595\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_2 &= z_2 + \frac{p_2}{\gamma} + \frac{v_2^2}{2g} = 6.50 + \frac{210}{9.81} + 0.408 \\[6pt]
&= 6.50 + 21.407 + 0.408 = 28.315\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\Delta H = H_1 - H_2 = 30.595 - 28.315 = 2.28\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the discharge using continuity at both sections:
$$
v_1 A_1 = 1.019 \cdot 0.0491 = 0.0500\,\mathrm{m^3/s},
\qquad
v_2 A_2 = 2.829 \cdot 0.0177 = 0.0500\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two diameters differ, but the **continuity equation** requires the same discharge in both sections. Velocity is therefore higher in the narrower section. Velocity head varies with the square of velocity. From $250$ to $150\,\mathrm{mm}$ the velocity nearly triples and the velocity head increases about eightfold.

Add the three head terms at each section using the same horizontal datum. Total head at section $1$ exceeds total head at section $2$ by $2.28\,\mathrm{m}$.

In real flow, viscous stresses dissipate energy, so total head decreases in the direction of flow. Since $H_1 > H_2$, the water flows uphill **from section $1$ to section $2$**, losing head $2.28\,\mathrm{m}$.

Section $2$ is $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ higher than section $1$ and is at lower pressure. The water still flows uphill because the direction depends on the difference in total head.

Multiplying each calculated velocity by its section area gives the same flow rate, equal to the specified discharge.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The pipe shown has diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and centerline elevation $z = 12.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Water in a piezometer rises $3.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the centerline. The discharge is $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Find the gauge pressure, piezometric head, and total head, and the elevation a Pitot tube at the same section would indicate.

::schema{nodi="A,1,5,-;B,10,5,-" condotte="A-B,0.75" linee="0,0 11,0;4.7,5.3 4.7,6.6;5.3,5.3 5.3,6.6;8,6.9 8,4.9 7.2,4.9;7.4,6.9 7.4,5.1 7.2,5.1" frecce="1.8,5,3.4,5,Q" etichette="3,6.8,piezometer;8.6,7.1,Pitot;6.4,0.35,datum;9.4,5.9,D = 300 mm" quote="5 5,5 6.6,3.40 m,0.8;2.4 0,2.4 5,12.00 m,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Piezometer and Pitot tube connected to a pipe of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = 3.40\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
p = \gamma \cdot 3.40 = 9.81 \cdot 3.40 = 33.4\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.300)^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
v = \frac{0.120}{0.0707} = 1.698\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{2.882}{19.62} = 0.147\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h = z + \frac{p}{\gamma} = 12.00 + 3.40 = 15.40\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H = h + \frac{v^2}{2g} = 15.40 + 0.147 = 15.55\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The water level in the Pitot tube is at elevation $H = 15.55\,\mathrm{m}$, or $3.55\,\mathrm{m}$ above the pipe centerline.

Check the Reynolds number:
$$
Re = \frac{v D}{\nu} = \frac{1.698 \cdot 0.300}{10^{-6}} = 5.09 \cdot 10^5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The piezometer is open to the atmosphere and contains water at rest, so its water level gives the **piezometric head** at the section. The column height above the centerline is the pressure head. Multiplying by the specific weight gives gauge pressure.

Add the column height to the pipe centerline elevation to obtain the piezometric head. The hydraulic grade line is thus $15.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the datum. The total head also includes the velocity head calculated from mean velocity.

The Pitot opening faces upstream, so the level in the tube exceeds the piezometer level by the velocity head $0.147\,\mathrm{m}$. Its elevation $15.55\,\mathrm{m}$ above the datum gives the **total head** at the section.

The Reynolds number $5.09 \cdot 10^5$ is well above $4000$, so the flow is turbulent. The kinetic energy correction factor is then close to $1$. Using mean velocity to calculate velocity head is consistent with the assumption $\alpha = 1$.

</div>
