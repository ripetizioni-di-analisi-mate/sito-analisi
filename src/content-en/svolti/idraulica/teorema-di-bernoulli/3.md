## Exercise 1

The open constant-level tank shown discharges through a short converging nozzle of diameter $D = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$ whose axis is $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface. Neglecting head loss, find the exit speed and discharge.

::schema{acqua="0.2,0.5 5,0.5 5,7.5 0.2,7.5;5,1.1 6.1,1.1 6.1,1.7 5,1.7" solidi="0,0 5.2,0 5.2,0.5 0,0.5;0,0 0.2,0 0.2,8 0,8;5,0.5 5.2,0.5 5.2,1.1 5,1.1;5,1.7 5.2,1.7 5.2,8 5,8" pelo="0.2,5,7.5" linee="5.2,1.1 6.1,1.25 6.1,1.55 5.2,1.7" frecce="6.3,1.4,7.6,1.4,v" quote="2.6 1.4,2.6 7.5,4.50 m,0" etichette="7,2.1,D = 60 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Constant-head tank with a converging nozzle of diameter $D = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 4.50} = \sqrt{88.29} = 9.40\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.060)^2}{4} = 0.002827\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = A v = 0.002827 \cdot 9.40 = 0.0266\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 26.6\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the head balance between the free surface and the outlet:
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{(9.40)^2}{19.62} = \frac{88.36}{19.62} = 4.50\,\mathrm{m} = h
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take the nozzle centerline as the datum and apply **Bernoulli’s equation** between a point on the free surface and the outlet. At the free surface the elevation is $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$, the gauge pressure is zero because the surface is open to the atmosphere, and the velocity is negligible because the tank area is much larger than the opening.

At the outlet the elevation is zero and the jet is exposed to the atmosphere, so the gauge pressure is also zero. Only two of the six terms remain in the balance. The entire available head $h$ becomes velocity head, giving Torricelli’s formula $v = \sqrt{2gh}$.

Multiplying the speed by the outlet area gives a discharge of **$26.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$**. The converging nozzle guides the jet to the outlet, where the jet fills the opening. At a sharp-edged orifice in a thin wall, the jet would contract and the actual discharge would be smaller.

Compute the velocity head from the calculated speed. It equals $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$, the depth of the outlet below the free surface, as required by the energy balance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The tank shown is kept at constant level. A short horizontal converging nozzle in its sidewall is $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the water surface and $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above ground. Neglecting head loss and air resistance, find the exit speed and the horizontal distance from the wall at which the jet reaches the ground.

::schema{acqua="0.2,4.8 4,4.8 4,8.5 0.2,8.5;4,5.7 4.6,5.7 4.6,6.3 4,6.3" solidi="-0.6,4.3 9,4.3 9,4.8 -0.6,4.8;0,4.8 0.2,4.8 0.2,9 0,9;4,4.8 4.2,4.8 4.2,5.7 4,5.7;4,6.3 4.2,6.3 4.2,9 4,9" pelo="0.2,4,8.5" tratteggi="4.6,6 8.06,6 8.06,4.8" linee="4.6,6 5.5,5.92 6.3,5.71 7,5.42 7.5,5.16 8.06,4.8" frecce="4.8,6.6,6.2,6.6,v" quote="2.2 6,2.2 8.5,2.50 m,0;8.06 4.8,8.06 6,1.20 m,0.9;4.6 4.3,8.06 4.3,x,1" riferimento=0 titolo="Jet emerging from a converging nozzle $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface and $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground"}

$$
v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.50} = \sqrt{49.05} = 7.00\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Let $a = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ be the height of the opening above the ground. The vertical motion of the jet then gives

$$
a = \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t = \sqrt{\frac{2 a}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.20}{9.81}} = \sqrt{0.2446} = 0.495\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = v\,t = 7.00 \cdot 0.495 = 3.46\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the jet range using the expression that depends only on the two heights:
$$
x = v\,t = \sqrt{2gh}\,\sqrt{\frac{2a}{g}} = 2\sqrt{h\,a} = 2\sqrt{2.50 \cdot 1.20} = 2\sqrt{3.00} = 3.46\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply Bernoulli’s equation between the free surface and the outlet, where the gauge pressure is zero. Torricelli’s formula gives an exit speed of $7.00\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

After the jet leaves the nozzle, the walls no longer guide it and gravity is its only acceleration. Each particle follows the path of a projectile launched horizontally. Its horizontal speed stays at $v$. Its initial vertical speed is zero and its vertical acceleration is $g$.

Set the vertical drop equal to $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ to find the flight time, $0.495\,\mathrm{s}$. The jet travels a horizontal distance of **$3.46\,\mathrm{m}$** in this time.

Substituting the expressions for speed and flight time gives $x = 2\sqrt{h\,a}$. Gravity cancels, so the horizontal range depends only on the depth of the opening below the water surface and its height above the ground.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The closed tank shown contains water beneath air at gauge pressure $p_0 = 50\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The water level stays constant and a nozzle of diameter $D = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$ discharges from depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting head loss, find the exit speed and discharge.

::schema{acqua="0.2,0.5 4.6,0.5 4.6,5 0.2,5;4.6,1.6 5.4,1.6 5.4,2.2 4.6,2.2" solidi="0,0 4.8,0 4.8,0.5 0,0.5;0,0 0.2,0 0.2,7 0,7;4.6,0.5 4.8,0.5 4.8,1.6 4.6,1.6;4.6,2.2 4.8,2.2 4.8,7 4.6,7;0,6.5 4.8,6.5 4.8,7 0,7" pelo="0.2,4.6,5" linee="4.8,1.6 5.4,1.7 5.4,2.1 4.8,2.2" frecce="5.6,1.9,6.8,1.9,v" quote="2.4 1.9,2.4 5,2.00 m,0" etichette="2.4,5.8,air at 50 kPa;6.2,2.6,D = 40 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Pressurized tank with a nozzle $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface"}

$$
\frac{p_0}{\gamma} = \frac{50}{9.81} = 5.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H = \frac{p_0}{\gamma} + h = 5.10 + 2.00 = 7.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2 g H} = \sqrt{19.62 \cdot 7.10} = \sqrt{139.3} = 11.80\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.040)^2}{4} = 0.001257\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = A v = 0.001257 \cdot 11.80 = 0.0148\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 14.8\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check total head at the outlet:
$$
H_U = 0 + 0 + \frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{139.2}{19.62} = 7.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the nozzle centerline as the datum. The free surface is at elevation $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, its velocity is negligible, and its gauge pressure equals that of the air above it, $50\,\mathrm{kPa}$. This pressure corresponds to a head of $5.10\,\mathrm{m}$.

The total head at the free surface is therefore $7.10\,\mathrm{m}$. Pressurizing the air has the same effect as raising the water level by $5.10\,\mathrm{m}$. At the outlet the elevation is zero and the jet is exposed to the atmosphere, so all the available head becomes velocity head.

The exit speed is **$11.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$** and the discharge is $14.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The speed is nearly twice that from an open tank with the same depth above the outlet, because pressurization increases the available head from $2.00$ to $7.10\,\mathrm{m}$.

Recalculate the outlet total head from the exit speed. It equals the total head at the free surface, as it must when losses are neglected.

</div>
