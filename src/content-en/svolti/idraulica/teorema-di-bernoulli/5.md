## Exercise 1

The pipe shown has diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and a piezometer and Pitot tube at the same section. Water rises $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above the centerline in the piezometer and $2.62\,\mathrm{m}$ in the Pitot tube. Assuming uniform velocity across the section, find the speed and discharge.

::schema{nodi="A,1,2.6,-;B,10,2.6,-" condotte="A-B,0.6" linee="3.7,2.9 3.7,5.6;4.3,2.9 4.3,5.6;7.3,5.95 7.3,2.5 6.5,2.5;6.7,5.95 6.7,2.7 6.5,2.7" frecce="1.4,2.6,2.8,2.6,Q" etichette="2.5,5.9,piezometer;7.9,6.3,Pitot;9.4,4.4,D = 200 mm" quote="3.7 2.6,3.7 5.6,2.40 m,-0.9;7.3 2.6,7.3 5.95,2.62 m,0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Piezometer and Pitot tube at the same pipe section"}

$$
\Delta = 2.62 - 2.40 = 0.22\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2 g\,\Delta} = \sqrt{19.62 \cdot 0.22} = \sqrt{4.316} = 2.08\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.200)^2}{4} = 0.03142\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = A v = 0.03142 \cdot 2.08 = 0.0653\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 65.3\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the dynamic pressure:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = 500 \cdot 4.326 = 2163\,\mathrm{Pa} = 2.16\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
\gamma\,\Delta = 9.81 \cdot 0.22 = 2.16\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The piezometer tap is flush with the wall and perpendicular to the flow. Its water level therefore indicates the **piezometric head** at the section. The Pitot opening faces upstream, so its water level indicates the **total head**.

The level difference gives the velocity head, $0.22\,\mathrm{m}$. Solving $\dfrac{v^2}{2g} = \Delta$ for $v$ gives $2.08\,\mathrm{m/s}$, a typical velocity for flow in a pressurized pipe.

A Pitot tube measures the local velocity at its opening. With velocity assumed uniform across the section, this is also the mean velocity. Multiplying by area gives the discharge, **$65.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$**. For a more precise measurement, move the probe across the section and integrate the measured velocity profile.

Compare the pressure difference between the stagnation point and the undisturbed stream in two ways. The dynamic pressure calculated from the speed must equal the product $\gamma\,\Delta$ obtained from the instrument reading.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the rectangular channel shown, width is $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and depth is $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. A Pitot tube shows a water level $0.28\,\mathrm{m}$ above the free surface. Assuming uniform velocity, find the speed, discharge, and Froude number, and classify the flow as subcritical or supercritical.

::schema{acqua="0,0 12,0 12,3 0,3" solidi="-0.5,-0.7 12.5,-0.7 12.5,0 -0.5,0" pelo="0,12,3" linee="7.6,3.8 7.6,1 6.4,1;7,3.8 7,1.3 6.4,1.3" frecce="1.4,2,3.2,2,v" quote="9.6 3,9.6 3.8,0.28 m,-0.9;5.4 0,5.4 3,1.20 m,-0.8" etichette="8.4,4.1,Pitot" riferimento=0 titolo="Pitot tube immersed in channel flow with water depth $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
v = \sqrt{2 g\,\Delta} = \sqrt{19.62 \cdot 0.28} = \sqrt{5.494} = 2.34\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A = b\,h = 2.00 \cdot 1.20 = 2.40\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = A v = 2.40 \cdot 2.34 = 5.62\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
h_m = \frac{A}{B} = \frac{2.40}{2.00} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Fr = \frac{v}{\sqrt{g\,h_m}} = \frac{2.34}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.20}} = \frac{2.34}{3.431} = 0.683
$$

Calculate the velocity head from the velocity:
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{5.476}{19.62} = 0.279\,\mathrm{m} \simeq \Delta
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The channel free surface is open to the atmosphere, so its gauge pressure is zero and its elevation gives the piezometric head. The upstream-facing Pitot opening measures total head. The rise above the free surface therefore gives the velocity head.

The speed is $2.34\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Multiply by the flow area to obtain discharge. In this rectangular section the area equals the channel width times the water depth, giving **$5.62\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$**.

Calculate the Froude number using the hydraulic depth $h_m = \dfrac{A}{B}$, which equals the water depth in a rectangular channel. The result is $Fr = 0.683 < 1$, so the flow is **subcritical**.

Recalculate velocity head from the computed speed and compare it with the measured rise. The values agree to the precision of the speed, which was rounded to three significant figures.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The pipe shown has diameter $D = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$. A Pitot-static tube connects to a differential mercury manometer reading a level difference $\Delta = 35\,\mathrm{mm}$. The mercury specific gravity is $13.6$. Assuming uniform velocity, find the speed, discharge, and Reynolds number.

::schema{nodi="A,1,4,-;B,10,4,-" condotte="A-B,0.55" linee="5.4,3.85 6,3.85 6,7.4;5.4,4.28 5.4,7.4;4.6,7.4 7,7.4 7,8.6 4.6,8.6 4.6,7.4" frecce="1.4,4,2.4,4,Q" etichette="8.8,8,differential manometer;8.8,7.3,mercury: Δ = 35 mm;8.6,4.8,D = 150 mm;6.6,3.2,Pitot–static tube" riferimento=0 titolo="Pitot-static tube with a differential mercury manometer"}

$$
\Delta h = \Delta\left(\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma} - 1\right) = 0.035 \cdot 12.6 = 0.441\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2 g\,\Delta h} = \sqrt{19.62 \cdot 0.441} = \sqrt{8.653} = 2.94\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot (0.150)^2}{4} = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = A v = 0.01767 \cdot 2.94 = 0.0520\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 52.0\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
Re = \frac{v D}{\nu} = \frac{2.94 \cdot 0.150}{10^{-6}} = 4.41 \cdot 10^5
$$

Check the pressure difference indicated by the manometer:
$$
\gamma_m = 13.6 \cdot 9.81 = 133.4\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}
$$
$$
(\gamma_m - \gamma)\,\Delta = (133.4 - 9.81) \cdot 0.035 = 4.33\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = 500 \cdot 8.644 = 4322\,\mathrm{Pa} = 4.32\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A Pitot-static tube combines a forward-facing port that senses total head with side ports that sense piezometric head. The two sets of ports connect to opposite sides of the differential manometer, so its reading measures velocity head directly after conversion to water head.

With mercury as the manometer fluid, multiply the level difference by $\dfrac{\gamma_m}{\gamma} - 1 = 12.6$ to convert it to meters of water. A mercury reading of $35\,\mathrm{mm}$ corresponds to $0.441\,\mathrm{m}$ of water.

The speed is $2.94\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the discharge is **$52.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$**. The Reynolds number is $4.41 \cdot 10^5$, indicating turbulent flow. The kinetic energy correction factor is therefore close to $1$, so a uniform velocity profile is an acceptable approximation for estimating discharge.

The manometer reading corresponds to a pressure difference of $(\gamma_m - \gamma)\,\Delta$. Comparing this with the dynamic pressure calculated from the velocity gives agreement within rounding.

</div>
