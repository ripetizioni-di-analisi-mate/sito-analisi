## Exercise 1

A circular gate of diameter $D = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ closes an opening in a vertical wall, with its center at depth $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the resultant force and the distance from the gate center down to the center of pressure.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0.5;B,0,1.3" aste="A-B" acqua="0,0 4.5,0 4.5,3.4 0,3.4" solidi="-0.35,-0.7 5,-0.7 5,0 -0.35,0;-0.35,0 0,0 0,0.5 -0.35,0.5;-0.35,1.3 0,1.3 0,3.8 -0.35,3.8" pelo="0,4.5,3.4,p_g = 0" pressioni="B,A,0.76,1.05,γ·h" punti="0,0.9,G,baricentro" quote="0 0.9,0 3.4,2.50 m,-1.8;0 0.5,0 1.3,D = 0.80 m,2.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Section of the vertical wall showing the circular opening centered at depth $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

::schema{solidi="-1.6,0 1.6,0 1.6,3.4 -1.6,3.4" cerchi="0,0.9,0.4,foro" pelo="-1.6,1.6,3.4,p_g = 0" punti="0,0.9,G,baricentro" quote="0 0.9,0 3.4,2.50 m,0.95;-0.4 0.9,0.4 0.9,D = 0.80 m,0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Front view of the wall. The gate is the circle of diameter $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. The wall is vertical, so $\sin\alpha = 1$ and distances measured down the wall equal vertical depths.

$$
A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.80^2}{4} = 0.5027\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 2.50 \cdot 0.5027 = 12.33\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{\pi D^4}{64} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.80^4}{64} = 0.02011\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
e = \frac{I_G}{y_G\,A} = \frac{0.02011}{2.50 \cdot 0.5027} = 0.0160\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y_C = 2.50 + 0.0160 = 2.516\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The force $S = 12.3\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts horizontally. The center of pressure is $16.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ below the gate center on the vertical diameter.

Check using the simplified formula for a circle. Substituting $I_G = \dfrac{\pi D^4}{64}$ and $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4}$ into the expression for eccentricity gives
$$
e = \frac{\pi D^4}{64}\cdot\frac{4}{\pi D^2 y_G} = \frac{D^2}{16\,y_G} = \frac{0.64}{16 \cdot 2.50} = 0.0160\,\mathrm{m} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Because the gate is vertical, distances measured down the gate equal vertical depths, giving $y_G = h_G = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$.

The vertical diameter is an axis of symmetry parallel to the direction of increasing pressure. The product of inertia about the centroidal axes is therefore zero, and the center of pressure lies on that diameter.

The offset is only $16\,\mathrm{mm}$, or $2\%$ of the diameter. The gate is small compared with its depth, so the pressure varies little across it and the pressure diagram is nearly rectangular. Even this small offset matters when sizing the operating mechanism because the moment about the center is $S\,e = 12.33 \cdot 0.0160 = 0.197\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

The check derives the general circular-gate expression $e = \dfrac{D^2}{16\,y_G}$ and recovers the same offset.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A circular gate of diameter $D = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ is mounted in a wall inclined at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal. Its center is at depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the hydrostatic force and the location of the center of pressure.

::schema{nodi="A,0.2,0.4804;B,0.8,1.5196" aste="A-B" acqua="-2.6,0 -0.0774,0 1.6547,3 -2.6,3" solidi="-3,-0.8 3.2,-0.8 3.2,3.4 1.8856,3.4 -0.0774,0 -3,0" pelo="-2.6,1.6547,3,p_g = 0" pressioni="A,B,1,0.59,p" punti="0.5,1,G,baricentro" quote="0.5 1,0.5 3,2.00 m,-1.6;A,B,D = 1.20 m,0.7" etichette="-0.35,0.08,α" riferimento=0 titolo="Circular gate of diameter $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ in a wall inclined at $60^\circ$, with its center at depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. Use gauge pressures and $\sin 60^\circ = 0.8660$.

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 1.20^2}{4} = 1.1310\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
y_G = \frac{h_G}{\sin 60^\circ} = \frac{2.00}{0.8660} = 2.3094\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 1.1310 = 22.19\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
e = \frac{D^2}{16\,y_G} = \frac{1.44}{16 \cdot 2.3094} = 0.0390\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y_C = 2.3094 + 0.0390 = 2.3484\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_C = y_C\,\sin 60^\circ = 2.034\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The force $S = 22.2\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts normal to the gate. The center of pressure lies $39.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ down the plane from its center, at vertical depth $2.034\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check by calculating the second moment of area explicitly. It is $I_G = \dfrac{\pi \cdot 1.20^4}{64} = 0.10179\,\mathrm{m^4}$, so
$$
e = \frac{I_G}{y_G\,A} = \frac{0.10179}{2.3094 \cdot 1.1310} = 0.0390\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
in agreement with the simplified formula.

<div class="spiegazione">

The force magnitude depends on the vertical depth $h_G = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of the center. The eccentricity instead depends on its coordinate $y_G = 2.31\,\mathrm{m}$ along the gate, since distance along an inclined plane exceeds the corresponding vertical depth.

The eccentricity is more than twice that in the preceding exercise despite similar depths. The quantity $e$ varies with diameter squared. Doubling $D$ at fixed $y_G$ therefore multiplies the eccentricity by four.

Using the circle’s second moment of area in the general formula confirms the eccentricity obtained from the simplified expression.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular gate of diameter $D = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ is mounted in a vertical wall between two reservoirs. Its center is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the upstream surface and $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the downstream surface, as shown. Find the net force and its point of application.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0.25;B,0,1.75" aste="A-B" acqua="-2.8,0 0,0 0,4 -2.8,4;0,0 2.8,0 2.8,2 0,2" solidi="-3.2,-0.8 3.2,-0.8 3.2,0 -3.2,0;-0.15,0 0.15,0 0.15,0.25 -0.15,0.25;-0.15,1.75 0.15,1.75 0.15,4.4 -0.15,4.4" pelo="-2.8,-1.2,4,upstream;1.2,2.8,2,downstream" pressioni="A,B,1.5,0.9,γ·h_1;B,A,0.1,0.7,γ·h_2" punti="0,1,G,baricentro" quote="0 1,0 4,3.00 m,-2.3;0 0.25,0 1.75,D = 1.50 m,1.3" riferimento=0 titolo="Circular gate between two basins. Its center lies $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the upstream free surface and $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the downstream free surface"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity on each side. Use gauge pressures. The hydrostatic head plane in each basin coincides with its free surface.

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 1.50^2}{4} = 1.7671\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
I_G = \frac{\pi \cdot 1.50^4}{64} = 0.24850\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
S_1 = 9.81 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 1.7671 = 52.01\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
e_1 = \frac{D^2}{16 \cdot 3.00} = 0.04688\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S_2 = 9.81 \cdot 1.00 \cdot 1.7671 = 17.34\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
e_2 = \frac{D^2}{16 \cdot 1.00} = 0.14063\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S = S_1 - S_2 = 52.01 - 17.34 = 34.67\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Moments of the two pressure forces about the gate center, taking the downward offset as positive:
$$
S_1 e_1 - S_2 e_2 = 52.01 \cdot 0.04688 - 17.34 \cdot 0.14063 = 2.438 - 2.438 = 0
$$
$$
e = \frac{S_1 e_1 - S_2 e_2}{S} = 0
$$

The net force $34.7\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downstream **through the center** of the gate.

Check the pressure moments about the centroid. On a vertical plane the moment about the horizontal centroidal axis is $\gamma\,I_G$, independent of depth. Each face contributes a moment of magnitude $9.81 \cdot 0.24850 = 2.438\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The moments act in opposite directions and cancel.

<div class="spiegazione">

The gate center lies at a different depth below each water surface, so the forces differ in magnitude. On each face the center of pressure lies below the gate center. The downstream offset is three times the upstream offset because $e$ varies inversely with $y_G$ and the downstream depth is one-third of the upstream depth.

The moment of each pressure force about the gate center is $S\,e = \gamma\,y_G\,A \cdot \dfrac{I_G}{y_G A} = \gamma\,I_G$. Depth cancels, leaving equal and opposite moments. Their sum is zero, so the resultant acts through the geometric center.

This holds for any pair of water levels as long as both faces remain completely submerged. The operating mechanism of a fully submerged circular gate therefore has no moment to resist from the difference in water levels.

</div>
