## Exercise 1

A vertical rectangular gate of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $h = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ closes an opening extending from the free surface to the bottom, as shown. Find the hydrostatic force and the depth of the center of pressure.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,3" aste="A-B" acqua="0,0 4.5,0 4.5,3 0,3" solidi="-0.5,-0.7 5,-0.7 5,0 -0.5,0" pelo="0,4.5,3,p_g = 0" pressioni="B,A,0,1.5,γ·h" quote="0 0,0 3,3.00 m,-1.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical gate of height $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, with its upper edge at the free surface"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. Use gauge pressures and $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$. The hydrostatic head plane coincides with the free surface.

$$
h_G = \frac{h}{2} = \frac{3.00}{2} = 1.50\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = b\,h = 2.00 \cdot 3.00 = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 6.00 = 88.29\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{b\,h^3}{12} = \frac{2.00 \cdot 3.00^3}{12} = 4.50\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
y_C = y_G + \frac{I_G}{y_G\,A} = 1.50 + \frac{4.50}{1.50 \cdot 6.00} = 1.50 + 0.50 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The hydrostatic force $S = 88.3\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts horizontally from the water toward the gate. Its line of action lies at depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, or $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom.

Check using the pressure diagram. The bottom pressure is $p = \gamma h = 9.81 \cdot 3.00 = 29.43\,\mathrm{kPa}$, so the triangular diagram has area $\dfrac{29.43 \cdot 3.00}{2} = 44.15\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Multiplying by the width gives the force $2.00 \cdot 44.15 = 88.3\,\mathrm{kN}$. The centroid of the triangle is at depth $\dfrac{2h}{3} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The gate is vertical, so the centroid’s vertical depth equals its distance below the free surface measured along the gate, $y_G = h_G = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The force follows directly from $S = \gamma h_G A$ by multiplying the pressure at the centroid, $\gamma h_G = 14.72\,\mathrm{kPa}$, by the surface area, $6.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

The center-of-pressure formula uses the second moment of area about the horizontal centroidal axis. For a rectangle, this is $\dfrac{b\,h^3}{12}$, with $h$ measured in the direction of changing pressure. The center of pressure lies $\dfrac{I_G}{y_G A} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the centroid.

The center of pressure lies **below** the centroid, two-thirds of the gate height down from the free surface. Pressure increases with depth, so the lower part of the gate carries more of the load. The pressure diagram confirms both results. For a surface of constant width, the force equals the width times the area of the diagram and acts through its centroid.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A vertical rectangular gate of width $b = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ closes an opening between depths $a_1 = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and $a_2 = 3.20\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the force and the location of the center of pressure relative to the lower edge.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,2" aste="A-B" acqua="0,0 4.5,0 4.5,3.2 0,3.2" solidi="-0.6,-0.7 5,-0.7 5,0 -0.6,0;-0.25,2 0.25,2 0.25,3.6 -0.25,3.6" pelo="0,4.5,3.2,p_g = 0" pressioni="B,A,0.6,1.6,γ·h" quote="0 0,0 2,2.00 m,-1.6;0 2,0 3.2,1.20 m,-1.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Opening of height $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with its upper edge at depth $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. Use gauge pressures and $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$.

$$
\ell = a_2 - a_1 = 3.20 - 1.20 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = b\,\ell = 1.50 \cdot 2.00 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
h_G = \frac{a_1 + a_2}{2} = \frac{1.20 + 3.20}{2} = 2.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 2.20 \cdot 3.00 = 64.75\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{b\,\ell^3}{12} = \frac{1.50 \cdot 2.00^3}{12} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
y_C = 2.20 + \frac{1.00}{2.20 \cdot 3.00} = 2.20 + 0.1515 = 2.3515\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
a_2 - y_C = 3.20 - 2.3515 = 0.849\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The resultant force $S = 64.7\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at depth $2.35\,\mathrm{m}$, or $0.849\,\mathrm{m}$ above the gate’s lower edge.

Divide the trapezoidal pressure diagram into a rectangle and a triangle. The edge pressures are $p_1 = 9.81 \cdot 1.20 = 11.77\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_2 = 9.81 \cdot 3.20 = 31.39\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The rectangle of height $p_1$ contributes a force $S_r = 1.50 \cdot 11.77 \cdot 2.00 = 35.32\,\mathrm{kN}$ acting at depth $2.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The triangle contributes $S_t = 1.50 \cdot \dfrac{19.62 \cdot 2.00}{2} = 29.43\,\mathrm{kN}$ acting at $1.20 + \dfrac{2}{3}\cdot 2.00 = 2.533\,\mathrm{m}$. The combined force is $64.75\,\mathrm{kN}$, and its line of action is at $\dfrac{35.32 \cdot 2.20 + 29.43 \cdot 2.533}{64.75} = 2.351\,\mathrm{m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The gate’s upper edge is submerged, so the pressure diagram is a **trapezoid**. Formula $S = \gamma h_G A$ still applies because its derivation uses only the depth of the centroid, regardless of the upper edge’s position relative to the free surface.

Use the second moment of area about the gate’s centroidal axis, $\dfrac{b\,\ell^3}{12}$, where $\ell = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is the height of the opening itself. The center of pressure lies $0.152\,\mathrm{m}$ below the centroid. This offset is much smaller than in the preceding example because $y_G$ appears in the denominator. As the depth increases, the pressure diagram approaches a rectangle and the center of pressure approaches the centroid.

Splitting the trapezoid into a rectangle and a triangle gives two shapes of known area and centroid. Combining their contributions recovers both the force and its line of action.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vertical rectangular gate of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on the bottom, separating basins with water depths $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream and $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream, as shown. Find the net force and the height of its line of action above the bottom.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,2;D,0,1.5,-" aste="A-B" acqua="-2.6,0 0,0 0,4 -2.6,4;0,0 2.6,0 2.6,1.5 0,1.5" solidi="-3,-0.7 3,-0.7 3,0 -3,0;-0.15,2 0.15,2 0.15,4.4 -0.15,4.4" pelo="-2.6,-1.2,4,upstream;1.2,2.6,1.5,downstream" pressioni="A,B,1.5,0.75,γ·h_1;D,A,0,0.56,γ·h_2" quote="0 0,0 2,2.00 m,-2.3" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate of height $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with water depths $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream and $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity on each side. Use gauge pressures. The hydrostatic head plane in each basin coincides with its free surface.

Upstream force on the gate between depths $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
h_{G1} = \frac{2.00 + 4.00}{2} = 3.00\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = 2.50 \cdot 2.00 = 5.00\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
S_1 = 9.81 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 5.00 = 147.15\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{2.50 \cdot 2.00^3}{12} = 1.667\,\mathrm{m^4},
\qquad
y_{C1} = 3.00 + \frac{1.667}{3.00 \cdot 5.00} = 3.111\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
z_1 = 4.00 - 3.111 = 0.889\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Downstream force on the wetted portion, which extends $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom:
$$
S_2 = \gamma\,\frac{b\,h_2^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{2.50 \cdot 1.50^2}{2} = 27.59\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
z_2 = \frac{h_2}{3} = 0.500\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Balance horizontal forces and take moments about the foot of the gate to find the net force and its line of action:
$$
S = S_1 - S_2 = 147.15 - 27.59 = 119.56\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
z = \frac{S_1 z_1 - S_2 z_2}{S} = \frac{147.15 \cdot 0.889 - 27.59 \cdot 0.500}{119.56} = \frac{130.82 - 13.80}{119.56} = 0.979\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The net force $119.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downstream along a line $0.979\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom.

Check using the net pressure diagram. Let $z$ denote elevation above the bottom. The net pressure is constant at $\gamma(4.00 - z) - \gamma(1.50 - z) = 9.81 \cdot 2.50 = 24.53\,\mathrm{kPa}$ for $0 \leq z \leq 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$. It equals $\gamma(4.00 - z)$ for $1.50 \leq z \leq 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, decreasing from $24.53$ to $19.62\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The rectangle contributes $2.50 \cdot 24.53 \cdot 1.50 = 91.97\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $z = 0.750\,\mathrm{m}$ and the trapezoid contributes $2.50 \cdot \dfrac{24.53 + 19.62}{2} \cdot 0.50 = 27.59\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $z = 1.741\,\mathrm{m}$. Their sum is $119.56\,\mathrm{kN}$, acting at the combined centroid $\dfrac{91.97 \cdot 0.750 + 27.59 \cdot 1.741}{119.56} = 0.979\,\mathrm{m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each face has its own hydrostatic head plane, so calculate the two forces separately. The entire upstream face is wet. Its top edge is at depth $4.00 - 2.00 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, placing its centroid at depth $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$. On the downstream side, the water reaches only $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. The wetted part starts at the free surface and has a triangular pressure distribution. Its resultant $\gamma\,\dfrac{b\,h^2}{2}$ acts one-third of the wetted height above the bottom.

The horizontal forces act in opposite directions, so the net force is their difference. Its moment about the foot of the gate must equal the algebraic sum of their moments, giving $S\,z = S_1 z_1 - S_2 z_2$.

The net force $119.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts **downstream**, $0.979\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom. This is higher than the line of action of the upstream force alone because subtracting the downstream force, which acts near the bottom, shifts the resultant upward. The net pressure diagram confirms the result. Where both sides are wet, the two pressures vary linearly with the same slope, so their difference is constant.

</div>
