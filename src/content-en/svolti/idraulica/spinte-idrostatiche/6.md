## Exercise 1

A concrete retaining wall of specific weight $\gamma_c = 24\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ has a vertical upstream face, height $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and base width $B = 2.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The water depth is $3.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and the base friction coefficient is $f = 0.55$, as shown. Check overturning and sliding and locate the resultant on the foundation plane.

::schema{solidi="-3.2,-0.9 5,-0.9 5,0 -3.2,0;0,0 2.2,0 2.2,4 0,4" acqua="-3,0 0,0 0,3.5 -3,3.5" pelo="-3,-0.5,3.5,p_g = 0" pressioni="0 0,0 3.5,1.2,0,γ·h" forze="1.1 2,0,-1.6,W" quote="0 0,2.2 0,B = 2.20 m,1.5;2.2 0,2.2 4,4.00 m,1;-3 0,-3 3.5,3.50 m,-0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical-faced retaining wall of height $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $2.20\,\mathrm{m}$, with water depth $3.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Analyze a unit length of wall. Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. The impermeable foundation prevents uplift. Measure horizontal coordinate $x$ from the upstream heel.

Wall weight and water force:
$$
W = \gamma_c\,H\,B = 24 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 2.20 = 211.2\,\mathrm{kN/m},
\qquad
x_W = \frac{B}{2} = 1.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,\frac{h^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{3.50^2}{2} = 60.09\,\mathrm{kN/m},
\qquad
z_S = \frac{h}{3} = 1.1667\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check stability against overturning about the downstream toe, at coordinate $x = B$:
$$
M_{\text{stabilizing}} = W\,(B - x_W) = 211.2 \cdot 1.10 = 232.3\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m}
$$
$$
M_{\text{overturning}} = S\,z_S = 60.09 \cdot 1.1667 = 70.10\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m}
$$
$$
F_r = \frac{232.3}{70.10} = 3.31 \geq 1.5
$$

Check stability against sliding:
$$
N = W = 211.2\,\mathrm{kN/m},
\qquad
F_s = \frac{f\,N}{S} = \frac{0.55 \cdot 211.2}{60.09} = 1.93 \geq 1.3
$$

Locate the resultant on the base by taking moments about the heel:
$$
x_R = \frac{W\,x_W + S\,z_S}{N} = \frac{211.2 \cdot 1.10 + 70.10}{211.2} = \frac{302.4}{211.2} = 1.432\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
e = \left|x_R - \frac{B}{2}\right| = |1.432 - 1.100| = 0.332\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\frac{B}{6} = 0.367\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Both stability checks are satisfied, with $F_r = 3.31$ and $F_s = 1.93$. The resultant crosses the base $1.432\,\mathrm{m}$ from the heel. Its eccentricity $0.332\,\mathrm{m}$ is less than $\dfrac{B}{6} = 0.367\,\mathrm{m}$, placing it **within the middle third**.

Check by taking moments about the downstream toe. The vertical resultant $N$ at the calculated location has moment $N\,(B - x_R) = 211.2 \cdot 0.768 = 162.2\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m}$ about the toe, equal to the difference $M_{\text{restoring}} - M_{\text{overturning}} = 232.3 - 70.1 = 162.2\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Analyze a one-meter length of wall, so all forces are expressed per unit length. The wall's self-weight per unit length is its specific weight times its cross-sectional area. For a rectangular section its line of action passes through the midpoint of the base.

The upstream face is vertical, so the hydrostatic force acts horizontally. The free surface meets the top of the wetted face, giving a triangular pressure distribution. The line of action lies one-third of the wetted height above the base.

For overturning, take moments about the downstream toe, the point about which the wall would rotate. The weight has arm $B - x_W$ and provides a stabilizing moment. The water force has arm $z_S$ and produces an overturning moment. For sliding, the resistance is friction $f\,N$, where $N$ is the total vertical load on the foundation. Here that load equals the wall weight.

Locate the resultant by equating its moment about the heel to the sum of the moments of all the forces. An eccentricity smaller than $\dfrac{B}{6}$ keeps the entire base in compression against the soil.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The triangular gravity dam shown has its upstream heel at $x = 0$, crest at horizontal coordinate $x = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and elevation $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and downstream toe at $x = 3.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The concrete has specific weight $\gamma_c = 24\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$, the water depth is $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and the friction coefficient is $f = 0.60$. Neglecting uplift, check overturning and sliding.

::schema{nodi="T,0,0;K,1.5,5,-;P,3.5,0" aste="T-K;K-P;T-P" acqua="-3,0 0,0 1.2,4 -3,4" solidi="-3.4,-0.9 6,-0.9 6,0 -3.4,0" pelo="-3,-0.3,4,p_g = 0" forze="0.4 1.3333,1.5,0,S_x;0.4 1.3333,0,-1.2;1.6667 1.6667,0,-1.8" etichette="-0.05,2.8,S_z;1.33,2.95,W" quote="T,P,B = 3.50 m,1.5;-3 0,-3 4,4.00 m,-0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Gravity dam of triangular cross section with a sloping upstream face, height $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and base width $3.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Work per unit length of the dam. Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. Neglect uplift and measure the horizontal coordinate $x$ from the heel.

The upstream face is the straight line joining $(0;\,0)$ and $(1.50;\,5.00)$. At elevation $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, its horizontal coordinate is $x = 1.50 \cdot \dfrac{4.00}{5.00} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$.

Dam weight:
$$
A_d = \frac{3.50 \cdot 5.00}{2} = 8.75\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
W = 24 \cdot 8.75 = 210.0\,\mathrm{kN/m}
$$
$$
x_W = \frac{0 + 1.50 + 3.50}{3} = 1.6667\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Force components. The horizontal component acts on the vertical projection of the wetted face, which has height $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and its top edge at the free surface. The vertical component equals the weight of the water above the face. Per unit length of the dam, the cross section of that water is the triangle with vertices $(0;\,0)$, $(0;\,4.00)$, and $(1.20;\,4.00)$:
$$
S_x = \gamma\,\frac{h^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{4.00^2}{2} = 78.48\,\mathrm{kN/m},
\qquad
z_{Sx} = \frac{4.00}{3} = 1.3333\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A_p = \frac{1.20 \cdot 4.00}{2} = 2.40\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
S_z = 9.81 \cdot 2.40 = 23.54\,\mathrm{kN/m},
\qquad
x_{Sz} = \frac{1.20}{3} = 0.400\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check stability against overturning about the downstream toe:
$$
M_{\text{stabilizing}} = 210.0 \cdot (3.50 - 1.6667) + 23.54 \cdot (3.50 - 0.400) = 385.0 + 72.99 = 458.0\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m}
$$
$$
M_{\text{overturning}} = 78.48 \cdot 1.3333 = 104.6\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m},
\qquad
F_r = \frac{458.0}{104.6} = 4.38
$$

Check stability against sliding:
$$
N = W + S_z = 210.0 + 23.54 = 233.5\,\mathrm{kN/m},
\qquad
F_s = \frac{0.60 \cdot 233.5}{78.48} = 1.79
$$

Both stability checks are satisfied, with factors of safety $F_r = 4.38$ and $F_s = 1.79$.

Check the location of the resultant by taking moments about the heel:
$$
x_R = \frac{210.0 \cdot 1.6667 + 23.54 \cdot 0.400 + 78.48 \cdot 1.3333}{233.5} = \frac{350.0 + 9.42 + 104.6}{233.5} = 1.987\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
with an eccentricity $|1.987 - 1.750| = 0.237\,\mathrm{m}$ smaller than $\dfrac{B}{6} = 0.583\,\mathrm{m}$. The resultant is inside the middle third, consistent with the two high factors of safety.

<div class="spiegazione">

The upstream face is inclined, so the hydrostatic force also has a vertical component. Its magnitude equals the weight of the water between the wetted face and the free surface. Per unit length of the dam, this water occupies a right triangle in cross section. Its vertical leg at the heel has height $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and its horizontal leg along the free surface has length $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The centroid lies one-third of the base length from the vertical leg, a distance $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$ from the heel.

The vertical component of the water force is **stabilizing**. It acts downward near the heel, so its moment about the downstream toe has the same sign as the moment of the dam’s weight. It also increases $N$ and hence the friction available at the base. Inclining the upstream face therefore improves stability against both overturning and sliding.

A triangle’s centroid has a horizontal coordinate equal to the mean of the three vertex coordinates. For the dam cross section, this gives $x_W = 1.667\,\mathrm{m}$.

Calculating where the resultant crosses the base gives an eccentricity well below the limit, consistent with the high factor of safety against overturning.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the preceding dam, include triangular uplift varying from $\gamma h$ at the upstream heel to zero at the downstream toe. Repeat both stability checks and assess the effect of drains that reduce the diagram ordinates to one third, as shown.

::schema{nodi="T,0,0;K,1.5,5,-;P,3.5,0" aste="T-K;K-P;T-P" acqua="-3,0 0,0 1.2,4 -3,4" solidi="-3.4,-0.9 6,-0.9 6,0 -3.4,0" pelo="-3,-0.3,4,p_g = 0" pressioni="T,P,1.1,0,U,-1" forze="0.4 1.3333,1.5,0,S_x;0.4 1.3333,0,-1.2;1.6667 1.6667,0,-1.8" etichette="-0.05,2.8,S_z;1.33,2.95,W" quote="T,P,B = 3.50 m,1.9" riferimento=0 titolo="The same dam with the triangular distribution of uplift pressure $U$ beneath its base"}

Use the same assumptions as in the preceding exercise and add a triangular uplift pressure distribution beneath the base.

$$
U = \frac{\gamma\,h\,B}{2} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 3.50}{2} = 68.67\,\mathrm{kN/m},
\qquad
x_U = \frac{B}{3} = 1.1667\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check overturning. The stabilizing moment is unchanged from the preceding exercise. Uplift acts upward between the heel and downstream toe and gives an additional overturning moment:
$$
M_{\text{overturning}} = 104.6 + 68.67 \cdot (3.50 - 1.1667) = 104.6 + 160.2 = 264.8\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m}
$$
$$
F_r = \frac{458.0}{264.8} = 1.73
$$

Check stability against sliding:
$$
N = W + S_z - U = 210.0 + 23.54 - 68.67 = 164.9\,\mathrm{kN/m}
$$
$$
F_s = \frac{0.60 \cdot 164.9}{78.48} = 1.26
$$

Locate the resultant:
$$
x_R = \frac{350.0 + 9.42 - 68.67 \cdot 1.1667 + 104.6}{164.9} = \frac{383.9}{164.9} = 2.329\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
e = |2.329 - 1.750| = 0.579\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\frac{B}{6} = 0.583\,\mathrm{m}
$$

With full uplift, the factor of safety against overturning falls to $1.73$ and that against sliding falls to $1.26$. The resultant approaches the edge of the middle third.

Drainage reduces the uplift pressure at every point to one-third of its original value. The resulting force is $U' = \dfrac{68.67}{3} = 22.89\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ with the same moment arm, so
$$
M_{\text{overturning}} = 104.6 + 22.89 \cdot 2.3333 = 158.0\,\mathrm{kN\,m/m},
\qquad
F_r = \frac{458.0}{158.0} = 2.90
$$
$$
N = 210.0 + 23.54 - 22.89 = 210.7\,\mathrm{kN/m},
\qquad
F_s = \frac{0.60 \cdot 210.7}{78.48} = 1.61
$$

Drainage restores the two factors to $2.90$ and $1.61$, both above the required values.

Find where the resultant acts with the drains in service:
$$
x_R = \frac{350.0 + 9.42 - 22.89 \cdot 1.1667 + 104.6}{210.7} = \frac{437.3}{210.7} = 2.076\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
with eccentricity $0.326\,\mathrm{m}$, well inside the middle third, consistent with the higher factors of safety.

<div class="spiegazione">

Uplift is the resultant of seepage pressures beneath the foundation. The assumed pressure is $\gamma h = 39.24\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at the heel, where full upstream head acts, and zero at the downstream toe, where water emerges to the atmosphere. For a unit length of dam, the force equals the area of the triangular pressure diagram and acts one-third of the base width from the heel.

Uplift reduces stability against both overturning and sliding. Its upward force has an overturning moment about the toe with arm $2.333\,\mathrm{m}$. It also reduces the effective vertical foundation load $N$ and the friction available at the base. The factor of safety against sliding therefore falls from $1.79$ to $1.26$.

With $F_s = 1.26$, the factor of safety against sliding falls below the reference value $1.3$. The design would need either a wider section or reduced uplift. Vertical drains near the heel collect seepage water and discharge it downstream, lowering foundation pressure. Reducing the pressure diagram to one-third restores vertical load $45.8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, bringing both factors of safety above their limits.

Check the resultant in both cases. Under full uplift it lies $0.579\,\mathrm{m}$ from the base centerline, practically at the limit $\dfrac{B}{6}$. Drainage reduces this offset to $0.326\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
