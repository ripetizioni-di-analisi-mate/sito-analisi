## Exercise 1

A rectangular gate of width $b = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and downslope length $L = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ lies in a wall inclined at $\alpha = 30^\circ$ to the horizontal. Its upper edge is at depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the hydrostatic force and the location of the center of pressure in the plane of the gate.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0.4;B,1.732,1.4" aste="A-B" acqua="-2.6,0 -0.6928,0 3.464,2.4 -2.6,2.4" solidi="-3,-0.8 4.3,-0.8 4.3,2.7 3.983,2.7 -0.6928,0 -3,0" pelo="-2.6,3.464,2.4,p_g = 0" pressioni="A,B,1.2,0.6,p" quote="A,B,2.00 m,0.9;1.732 1.4,1.732 2.4,1.00 m,-1.9" etichette="-0.52,0.1,α" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate in a wall inclined at $30^\circ$, with its upper edge at depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. Measure $y$ along the gate from its intersection with the hydrostatic head plane.

$$
y_1 = \frac{1.00}{\sin 30^\circ} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
y_2 = y_1 + L = 4.00\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
y_G = \frac{2.00 + 4.00}{2} = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_G = y_G\,\sin 30^\circ = 3.00 \cdot 0.500 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = b\,L = 1.50 \cdot 2.00 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 3.00 = 44.15\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{b\,L^3}{12} = \frac{1.50 \cdot 2.00^3}{12} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
y_C = y_G + \frac{I_G}{y_G\,A} = 3.00 + \frac{1.00}{3.00 \cdot 3.00} = 3.111\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y_2 - y_C = 4.00 - 3.111 = 0.889\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_C = y_C\,\sin 30^\circ = 1.556\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The force $S = 44.1\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts normal to the gate. The center of pressure lies $0.889\,\mathrm{m}$ from its lower edge, measured along the gate, at vertical depth $1.556\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check the pressure diagram along the gate. The edge pressures $p_1 = 9.81 \cdot 1.00 = 9.81\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_2 = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 = 19.62\,\mathrm{kPa}$ give a trapezoid of area $\dfrac{9.81 + 19.62}{2}\cdot 2.00 = 29.43\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ and hence a force $1.50 \cdot 29.43 = 44.15\,\mathrm{kN}$. The centroid of the trapezoid lies $\dfrac{L}{3}\cdot\dfrac{2p_2 + p_1}{p_2 + p_1} = \dfrac{2.00}{3}\cdot\dfrac{49.05}{29.43} = 1.111\,\mathrm{m}$ from the upper edge, giving $y_C = 2.00 + 1.111 = 3.111\,\mathrm{m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Measure the coordinate $y$ along the gate from the intersection of its plane with the hydrostatic head plane. The top edge is at vertical depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$, corresponding to coordinate $y_1 = \dfrac{1.00}{\sin 30^\circ} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Since $\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$, distances measured along the slope are twice the corresponding vertical depths.

The force magnitude depends only on the vertical centroid depth, so calculate $S = \gamma h_G A$ as for a vertical gate. Inclination enters the center-of-pressure calculation because formula $y_C = y_G + \dfrac{I_G}{y_G A}$ uses the coordinate $y$ along the gate, requiring $y_G = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ in place of $h_G = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$.

Use the rectangle’s second moment of area about the centroidal axis parallel to the sides of length $b$. The other dimension $L$ is measured down the slope. The center of pressure lies $0.111\,\mathrm{m}$ **below** the centroid along the gate. Since the width is constant, the pressure-diagram check applies and confirms both results.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A square gate of side $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ lies in a wall inclined at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal. Its top edge is at depth $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the force and its horizontal and vertical components.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0.3515;B,0.8485,1.2" aste="A-B" acqua="-2.4,0 -0.3515,0 1.6485,2 -2.4,2" solidi="-2.8,-0.7 3,-0.7 3,2.3 1.9485,2.3 -0.3515,0 -2.8,0" pelo="-2.4,1.6485,2,p_g = 0" pressioni="A,B,1.1,0.534,p" quote="A,B,1.20 m,0.8;0.8485 1.2,0.8485 2,0.80 m,-1.5" etichette="-0.25,0.08,α" riferimento=0 titolo="Square gate in a wall inclined at $45^\circ$, with its upper edge at depth $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. Use gauge pressures and $\sin 45^\circ = \cos 45^\circ = 0.7071$.

$$
h_G = 0.80 + \frac{L}{2}\,\sin 45^\circ = 0.80 + 0.60 \cdot 0.7071 = 1.2243\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y_G = \frac{h_G}{\sin 45^\circ} = \frac{1.2243}{0.7071} = 1.7314\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = 1.20^2 = 1.44\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,h_G\,A = 9.81 \cdot 1.2243 \cdot 1.44 = 17.29\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{1.20 \cdot 1.20^3}{12} = 0.1728\,\mathrm{m^4},
\qquad
y_C = 1.7314 + \frac{0.1728}{1.7314 \cdot 1.44} = 1.8007\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y_C - y_1 = 1.8007 - \frac{0.80}{0.7071} = 1.8007 - 1.1314 = 0.669\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S_x = S\,\sin 45^\circ = 17.29 \cdot 0.7071 = 12.23\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
S_z = S\,\cos 45^\circ = 12.23\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

The force $S = 17.3\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts normal to the gate, and the center of pressure lies $0.669\,\mathrm{m}$ from the upper edge along the gate. The horizontal component $S_x = 12.2\,\mathrm{kN}$ points toward the wall, and the vertical component $S_z = 12.2\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward.

Check the components using the vertical projection and the volume of water above the gate. The projection is a rectangle of width $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $1.20 \cdot 0.7071 = 0.8485\,\mathrm{m}$ between depths $0.80$ and $1.6485\,\mathrm{m}$. Its centroid is at depth $1.2243\,\mathrm{m}$, giving $S_x = 9.81 \cdot 1.2243 \cdot 1.20 \cdot 0.8485 = 12.23\,\mathrm{kN}$. The horizontal projection, of width $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and depth $0.8485\,\mathrm{m}$, forms the base of the overlying volume. Its mean height is $1.2243\,\mathrm{m}$, giving $S_z = 9.81 \cdot 1.20 \cdot 0.8485 \cdot 1.2243 = 12.23\,\mathrm{kN}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Add the vertical drop along half the gate to the upper-edge depth to find the centroid depth $\dfrac{L}{2}\sin 45^\circ$. Dividing $h_G$ by $\sin 45^\circ$ gives the coordinate $y_G$ along the gate because depth and distance along the plane satisfy $h = y \sin\alpha$.

Resolve the force normal to the gate into components. The normal to a plane inclined at $45^\circ$ makes angle $45^\circ$ with the vertical, so the horizontal and vertical components are equal in magnitude. The vertical component acts downward because the water lies above the gate.

The general rules for a submerged surface give an independent check. Its horizontal force equals the pressure force on its vertical projection. Its vertical force equals the weight of the overlying water volume. For this plane surface both agree with the components of the force calculated directly.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The upstream wall of a tank is inclined at angle $\alpha$ with $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{4}{3}$, equivalent to $\sin\alpha = 0.8$ and $\cos\alpha = 0.6$. A rectangular gate of width $b = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and downslope length $L = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ is installed with its upper edge at depth $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the force, center of pressure, and horizontal and vertical force components.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0.2;B,1.5,2.2" aste="A-B" acqua="-3.2,0 -0.15,0 2.7,3.8 -3.2,3.8" solidi="-3.6,-0.8 4.8,-0.8 4.8,4.2 3,4.2 -0.15,0 -3.6,0" pelo="-3.2,2.7,3.8,p_g = 0" pressioni="A,B,1.3,0.578,p" quote="A,B,2.50 m,1;1.5 2.2,1.5 3.8,1.60 m,-0.8" etichette="-0.02,0.06,α" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate in the upstream wall with $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{4}{3}$ and its upper edge at depth $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. Use gauge pressures and $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$.

$$
y_1 = \frac{1.60}{0.8} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
y_2 = 2.00 + 2.50 = 4.50\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
y_G = 3.25\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_G = 3.25 \cdot 0.8 = 2.60\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = 1.80 \cdot 2.50 = 4.50\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
S = 9.81 \cdot 2.60 \cdot 4.50 = 114.78\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{1.80 \cdot 2.50^3}{12} = 2.3438\,\mathrm{m^4},
\qquad
y_C = 3.25 + \frac{2.3438}{3.25 \cdot 4.50} = 3.410\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y_2 - y_C = 4.50 - 3.410 = 1.090\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
h_C = 3.410 \cdot 0.8 = 2.728\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S_x = S\,\sin\alpha = 114.78 \cdot 0.8 = 91.82\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
S_z = S\,\cos\alpha = 114.78 \cdot 0.6 = 68.87\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

The water force $S = 115\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts normal to the gate. The center of pressure lies $1.090\,\mathrm{m}$ from the lower edge along the gate, at vertical depth $2.728\,\mathrm{m}$. The horizontal force $S_x = 91.8\,\mathrm{kN}$ points toward the wall and the vertical force $S_z = 68.9\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward.

Check the components. The vertical projection is $2.50 \cdot 0.8 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ high by $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ wide, between depths $1.60$ and $3.60\,\mathrm{m}$. Its centroid depth is $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$, its area is $3.60\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and the resulting horizontal force is $9.81 \cdot 2.60 \cdot 3.60 = 91.82\,\mathrm{kN}$. The horizontal projection is $1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ wide by $2.50 \cdot 0.6 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ deep. The water above it has mean height $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$, giving $V_p = 1.80 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 2.60 = 7.02\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and $S_z = 9.81 \cdot 7.02 = 68.87\,\mathrm{kN}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the right triangle with legs $4$ and $3$ and hypotenuse $5$ to find $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$. It gives $\sin\alpha = 0.8$ and $\cos\alpha = 0.6$. The gate length along the slope is $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, with a vertical drop $2.50 \cdot 0.8 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Its lower edge is thus at depth $3.60\,\mathrm{m}$.

Use the vertical centroid depth $h_G = 2.60\,\mathrm{m}$ for the force magnitude and the coordinate $y_G = 3.25\,\mathrm{m}$ along the gate for the center of pressure. Interchanging the two would give the wrong offset below the centroid.

Resolve the force horizontally and vertically using $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$. The normal to the gate makes the same angle $\alpha$ with the vertical as the gate makes with the horizontal. Because the water rests above the gate, the vertical component acts **downward**.

The vertical projection and the volume of water above the gate independently confirm both force components. The latter volume is bounded by the gate, the hydrostatic head plane, and the vertical surfaces through the perimeter. Its base is the horizontal projection and its mean height equals the vertical depth of the gate centroid.

</div>
