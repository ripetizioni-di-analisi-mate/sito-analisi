## Exercise 1

The vertical rectangular gate shown, of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $h = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, is hinged along its upper edge at the free surface. A horizontal force $F$ at its lower edge holds it closed. Find $F$ and the hinge reaction.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,3" aste="A-B" cerniere="B" acqua="0,0 4.5,0 4.5,3 0,3" solidi="-0.5,-0.7 5,-0.7 5,0 -0.5,0" pelo="0,4.5,3,p_g = 0" reazioni="0 0,1.4,0,F" quote="0 0,0 3,3.00 m,-1.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate hinged at its upper edge and held by the force $F$ at its lower edge"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. The hinge is frictionless, and the gate’s weight is negligible compared with the hydrostatic force.

$$
S = \gamma\,\frac{b\,h^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{2.00 \cdot 3.00^2}{2} = 88.29\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
y_C = \frac{2h}{3} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Balance moments about hinge $B$ and forces in the horizontal direction:
$$
\begin{cases}
F\,h - S\,y_C = 0 \\[6pt]
H_B + F - S = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
F = \frac{S\,y_C}{h} = \frac{88.29 \cdot 2.00}{3.00} = 58.86\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
H_B = S - F = 88.29 - 58.86 = 29.43\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

The holding force $F = 58.9\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts horizontally toward the water, as does the hinge reaction $29.4\,\mathrm{kN}$.

Check by integrating the moment. At depth $y$, a strip of height $dy$ experiences force $\gamma\,y\,b\,dy$. Its moment arm about the hinge is $y$, so
$$
M_B = \int_0^h \gamma\,b\,y^2\,dy = \gamma\,b\,\frac{h^3}{3} = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot \frac{27}{3} = 176.58\,\mathrm{kN\,m} ,
$$
giving $F = \dfrac{176.58}{3.00} = 58.86\,\mathrm{kN}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The gate is a rigid body under the water force, the force $F$, and the hinge reaction. The frictionless hinge transmits no couple. Taking moments about $B$ eliminates the hinge reaction because its line of action passes through $B$. The equation gives $F$ directly.

Since the hinge lies at the free surface, the moment arm of the water force is the center-of-pressure depth $\dfrac{2h}{3} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The moment arm of force $F$ is the full gate height $h$.

The holding force supplies **two-thirds** of the resistance to the water force. The hinge supplies the remaining third. Because pressure is greatest near the bottom of the gate, the lower restraint carries the larger reaction.

Integrating the moments of the elemental pressure forces about the hinge gives the same moment directly, without first locating the center of pressure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A vertical rectangular gate of width $b = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ closes an opening between depths $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and is hinged along its lower edge. A horizontal force $F$ applied at its upper edge holds it closed, as shown. Find $F$.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,2" aste="A-B" cerniere="A" acqua="0,0 4.5,0 4.5,3 0,3" solidi="-0.6,-0.7 5,-0.7 5,0 -0.6,0;-0.25,2 0.25,2 0.25,3.4 -0.25,3.4" pelo="0,4.5,3,p_g = 0" reazioni="0 2,1.4,0,F" quote="0 0,0 2,2.00 m,-1.6;0 2,0 3,1.00 m,-1.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate of height $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ hinged at its lower edge, with its upper edge at depth $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. Neglect the weight of the gate and friction in the hinge.

$$
\ell = 3.00 - 1.00 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A = 1.50 \cdot 2.00 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
h_G = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 3.00 = 58.86\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{1.50 \cdot 2.00^3}{12} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m^4},
\qquad
y_C = 2.00 + \frac{1.00}{2.00 \cdot 3.00} = 2.1667\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The force has the following moment arm about the hinge at depth $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
d = 3.00 - 2.1667 = 0.8333\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
F = \frac{S\,d}{\ell} = \frac{58.86 \cdot 0.8333}{2.00} = 24.53\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

The holding force $F = 24.5\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts horizontally toward the water.

Check by integrating the moment. Let $s$ be the distance upward from the hinge. The depth is $3.00 - s$, giving a moment about the hinge of
$$
M = \int_0^2 \gamma\,b\,(3.00 - s)\,s\,ds = \gamma\,b\left[\frac{3\,s^2}{2} - \frac{s^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = 9.81 \cdot 1.50 \cdot \left(6 - \frac{8}{3}\right) = 49.05\,\mathrm{kN\,m} ,
$$
giving $F = \dfrac{49.05}{2.00} = 24.53\,\mathrm{kN}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The upper gate edge is submerged, so the pressure diagram is trapezoidal. Use the general center-of-pressure formula with $y_G = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and with $I_G$ calculated for the opening height $\ell = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

Because the hinge is at the bottom, the moment arm $3.00 - 2.1667 = 0.833\,\mathrm{m}$ is the distance upward from the hinge to the center of pressure. The force acts close to this hinge, so its moment is small. The required holding force at the opposite edge is only slightly more than two-fifths of the water force.

Integrating the moments of the elemental forces about the hinge confirms moment $49.05\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ and hence holding force $F$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular gate of diameter $D = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ in a vertical wall is hinged along its horizontal diameter, with its center at depth $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the force and the operating moment needed to keep it closed. How does the moment change if the gate is moved to depth $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$?

::schema{solidi="-1.8,0 1.8,0 1.8,2.9 -1.8,2.9" cerchi="0,0.9,0.5,foro" pelo="-1.8,1.8,2.9,p_g = 0" punti="0,0.9,G,baricentro" tratteggi="-0.75,0.9 0.75,0.9" quote="0 0.9,0 2.9,2.00 m,1.1;-0.5 0.9,0.5 0.9,D = 1.00 m,0.75" riferimento=0 titolo="Front view of a circular gate hinged along the horizontal diameter shown by the dashed line"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. The wall is vertical. Neglect the gate’s weight and friction.

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 1.00^2}{4} = 0.7854\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
I_G = \frac{\pi \cdot 1.00^4}{64} = 0.049087\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
S = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 0.7854 = 15.41\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
e = \frac{D^2}{16\,y_G} = \frac{1.00}{16 \cdot 2.00} = 0.03125\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
M = S\,e = 15.41 \cdot 0.03125 = 0.4815\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$

At depth $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$, the calculation gives $S = 9.81 \cdot 5.00 \cdot 0.7854 = 38.52\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $e = \dfrac{1.00}{16 \cdot 5.00} = 0.0125\,\mathrm{m}$, so
$$
M = 38.52 \cdot 0.0125 = 0.4815\,\mathrm{kN\,m} .
$$

The water force is $15.4\,\mathrm{kN}$ and the required holding moment is $0.482\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Lowering the gate to depth $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ increases the force to $38.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, while the moment **stays the same**.

Check by taking the moment of the pressure distribution about the centroid. For a vertical surface this moment is $\gamma\,I_G$, so
$$
M = 9.81 \cdot 0.049087 = 0.4815\,\mathrm{kN\,m} ,
$$
which is independent of depth, as both calculated values confirm.

<div class="spiegazione">

The hinge passes through the gate centroid. The force therefore has a moment arm equal to the center-of-pressure eccentricity $e$, giving a holding moment $S\,e$.

In the product $S\,e$, the force increases in proportion to depth while its eccentricity decreases in the same proportion, because $y_G$ appears in the denominator of $e = \dfrac{I_G}{y_G A}$. The resulting moment $\gamma\,I_G$ depends only on the liquid’s specific weight and the gate geometry.

The operating mechanism for a circular gate hinged along a diameter can be sized once for all submergence depths. Increasing depth does increase the resultant force, so the hinge reaction and the stresses in the gate itself increase.

</div>
