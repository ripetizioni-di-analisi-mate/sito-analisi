## Exercise 1

A steel block of volume $V = 0.015\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and density $\rho_s = 7850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is suspended from a spring scale and completely submerged in water, as shown. Find its weight in air, the buoyant force, and the scale reading.

::schema{acqua="-2.5,0 2.5,0 2.5,2.6 -2.5,2.6" solidi="-2.9,-0.7 2.9,-0.7 2.9,0 -2.9,0;-2.9,0 -2.5,0 -2.5,3.2 -2.9,3.2;2.5,0 2.9,0 2.9,3.2 2.5,3.2" poligoni="-0.45,0.8 0.45,0.8 0.45,1.6 -0.45,1.6" linee="0,1.6 0,3.5" pelo="-2.5,-1.1,2.6,p_g = 0" forze="-0.22 0.55,0,-0.8,P" reazioni="0.22 1.55,0,0.8,S" etichette="0.22,3.1,T" riferimento=0 titolo="Submerged block suspended by a cord. The force gauge reads the difference between the weight and the buoyant force"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and a stationary, fully submerged body. Use $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
P = \rho_s\,g\,V = 7850 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.015 = 1155\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,V = 9810 \cdot 0.015 = 147\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Balance the vertical forces on the block: its weight, the buoyant force, and the string tension $T$.
$$
T + S - P = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
T = P - S = 1155 - 147 = 1008\,\mathrm{N}
$$

The block weighs $1155\,\mathrm{N}$ in air, the buoyant force is $147\,\mathrm{N}$, and the force gauge reads $1008\,\mathrm{N}$, its **apparent weight** in water.

Check using the density difference. The apparent weight can be written directly as
$$
T = (\rho_s - \rho)\,g\,V = (7850 - 1000) \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.015 = 1008\,\mathrm{N} ,
$$
equal to the weight of a body of the same volume and density $6850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By Archimedes’ principle, buoyancy equals the weight of the displaced liquid. It depends on the submerged volume and the liquid’s specific weight, regardless of the body material. A fully submerged wooden block of the same volume would therefore experience the same buoyant force $147\,\mathrm{N}$ as the steel block.

Since the block is stationary, the three vertical forces sum to zero. The force gauge reads the string tension, which equals the block’s weight less the buoyant force.

The reduction in the reading is $13\%$ of the weight in air, equal to the ratio of water density to steel density. Factoring out the volume in the check shows that the apparent weight is the weight of a body whose density equals the difference between the two densities.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rock sample weighs $G = 250\,\mathrm{N}$ in air and has apparent weight $G' = 160\,\mathrm{N}$ when completely submerged in water, as shown. Find its volume and density.

::schema{acqua="-2.5,0 2.5,0 2.5,2.6 -2.5,2.6" solidi="-2.9,-0.7 2.9,-0.7 2.9,0 -2.9,0;-2.9,0 -2.5,0 -2.5,3.2 -2.9,3.2;2.5,0 2.9,0 2.9,3.2 2.5,3.2" poligoni="-0.4,0.9 0.4,0.9 0.4,1.7 -0.4,1.7" linee="0,1.7 0,3.5" pelo="-2.5,-1,2.6,p_g = 0" etichette="0.25,3.1,G' = 160 N" riferimento=0 titolo="Hydrostatic weighing. The specimen weighs $250\,\mathrm{N}$ in air and $160\,\mathrm{N}$ when submerged"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. The body is nonporous, fully submerged, and stationary. Use $\gamma = 9810\,\mathrm{N/m^3}$.

$$
S = G - G' = 250 - 160 = 90\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
V = \frac{S}{\gamma} = \frac{90}{9810} = 9.174 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 9.17\,\mathrm{L}
$$
$$
\rho_s = \frac{G}{g\,V} = \frac{250}{9.81 \cdot 9.174 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 2778\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

The sample has volume $9.17\,\mathrm{L}$ and density $2780\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

Check using the ratio of the two forces. Dividing the weight in air by the buoyant force gives
$$
\frac{G}{G - G'} = \frac{\rho_s\,g\,V}{\rho\,g\,V} = \frac{\rho_s}{\rho} = \frac{250}{90} = 2.778 ,
$$
giving $\rho_s = 2.778 \cdot 1000 = 2778\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ directly, without first calculating the volume.

<div class="spiegazione">

The block’s weight is unchanged on immersion, and the scale force balances weight and buoyancy. The difference between the weight in air and apparent weight in water is therefore the buoyant force. Divide this by the specific weight of water to find the volume.

Once the volume is known, use the weight in air to find the density. The result $2780\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is typical of a dense rock such as granite.

This method, **hydrostatic weighing**, measures the volume of an irregular object using just two weight readings. The ratio of the weight in air to buoyancy equals the ratio of the two densities, so the readings also give the object’s density directly.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wooden cube of side $a = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and density $\rho_l = 650\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is held completely underwater by a vertical cable anchored to the bottom, as shown. Find the cable tension and the draft the cube would have if the cable were cut.

::schema{acqua="-2.5,0 2.5,0 2.5,3 -2.5,3" solidi="-2.9,-0.7 2.9,-0.7 2.9,0 -2.9,0;-2.9,0 -2.5,0 -2.5,3.5 -2.9,3.5;2.5,0 2.9,0 2.9,3.5 2.5,3.5" poligoni="-0.55,1.6 0.55,1.6 0.55,2.7 -0.55,2.7" catene="N1-N2" nodi="N1,0,1.6,-;N2,0,0,-" pelo="-2.5,-1,3,p_g = 0" forze="-0.25 1.4,0,-0.9,P" reazioni="0.25 2.65,0,0.9,S" etichette="0.18,0.75,T" riferimento=0 titolo="Wooden cube of side $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ held underwater by a cable anchored to the bottom"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and a stationary, fully submerged cube. Neglect the cable’s weight and use $\rho = 1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

$$
V = a^3 = 0.50^3 = 0.125\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
P = \rho_l\,g\,V = 650 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.125 = 797.1\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
S = \gamma\,V = 9810 \cdot 0.125 = 1226.3\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Balance vertical forces on the cube:
$$
S - P - T = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
T = 1226.3 - 797.1 = 429.2\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Once the cable is cut, the cube floats and the flotation condition gives its draft $d$:
$$
\gamma\,a^2\,d = P
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
d = \frac{797.1}{9810 \cdot 0.25} = 0.325\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The cable tension is $T = 429\,\mathrm{N}$. After the cable is cut, the cube rises and settles with $0.325\,\mathrm{m}$ submerged and $0.175\,\mathrm{m}$ above the water.

Check using the densities. The cable tension is $T = (\rho - \rho_l)\,g\,V = 350 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.125 = 429.2\,\mathrm{N}$ and the submerged depth is $d = \dfrac{\rho_l}{\rho}\,a = 0.650 \cdot 0.50 = 0.325\,\mathrm{m}$, in agreement with the calculated values.

<div class="spiegazione">

The fully submerged cube displaces $0.125\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of water, giving buoyancy $1226\,\mathrm{N}$. Its weight is that of $0.125\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of wood, or $797\,\mathrm{N}$. Because wood is less dense than water, buoyancy exceeds weight. The cable must pull downward on the cube and is in tension.

After the cable is cut, the cube rises until the buoyancy of the remaining submerged volume balances its weight. The submerged fraction equals the density ratio $0.650$, leaving $0.325\,\mathrm{m}$ of the cube below the surface.

Expressing both results in terms of the densities alone confirms the two calculated values.

</div>
