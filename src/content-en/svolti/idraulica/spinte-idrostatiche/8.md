## Exercise 1

A rectangular pontoon of length $L = 8.00\,\mathrm{m}$, width $B = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and height $H = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ has empty weight $P = 120\,\mathrm{kN}$, as shown. Find its empty draft and freeboard and the maximum load it can carry without exceeding draft $0.90\,\mathrm{m}$.

::schema{acqua="-3.4,0 3.4,0 3.4,2 -3.4,2" solidi="-3.8,-0.7 3.8,-0.7 3.8,0 -3.8,0" poligoni="-1.5,1.49 1.5,1.49 1.5,2.69 -1.5,2.69" pelo="2.85,3.4,2,p_g = 0" quote="-1.5 1.49,-1.5 2,d,-0.9;1.5 1.49,1.5 2.69,H = 1.20 m,1;-1.5 2.69,1.5 2.69,B = 3.00 m,-0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Cross section of the pontoon of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. The pontoon has uniform cross section and a horizontal bottom. Use $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$.

Apply the flotation condition to the unloaded pontoon:
$$
\gamma\,L\,B\,d = P
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
d = \frac{P}{\gamma\,L\,B} = \frac{120}{9.81 \cdot 8.00 \cdot 3.00} = \frac{120}{235.44} = 0.5097\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H - d = 1.20 - 0.510 = 0.690\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Find the maximum load consistent with draft $d_{\max} = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
\Delta P = \gamma\,L\,B\,(d_{\max} - d) = 235.44 \cdot (0.900 - 0.5097) = 235.44 \cdot 0.3903 = 91.90\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

The unloaded pontoon has draft $0.510\,\mathrm{m}$ and freeboard $0.690\,\mathrm{m}$. It can carry a load of $91.9\,\mathrm{kN}$, or approximately $9.37\,\mathrm{t}$.

Check the total weight at the limiting draft. At $d_{\max} = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ the displaced volume is $8.00 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 0.900 = 21.60\,\mathrm{m^3}$, supporting weight $9.81 \cdot 21.60 = 211.9\,\mathrm{kN}$. Subtracting the unloaded weight gives payload $211.9 - 120 = 91.9\,\mathrm{kN}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By Archimedes’ principle, buoyancy equals the weight of displaced water. For the pontoon to float, this must equal its weight. The rectangular section and horizontal bottom give a displaced volume equal to the waterplane area $L\,B$ times the draft $d$, making the flotation equation linear in $d$.

The waterplane area is $24.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$. A weight increment $9.81 \cdot 24.0 = 235.4\,\mathrm{kN}$ therefore increases draft by one meter, so each additional metric ton lowers the pontoon by about $42\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Freeboard is the height of the pontoon side above the waterline, which keeps waves from coming aboard. It is $0.690\,\mathrm{m}$ when the pontoon is unloaded and falls to $0.300\,\mathrm{m}$ at maximum load.

Subtracting the unloaded weight from the total weight supported at the limiting draft confirms the payload.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The same pontoon has loaded draft $d = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and a center of gravity $0.95\,\mathrm{m}$ above the keel, as shown. Find the metacentric radius about the longitudinal axis, the metacenter location, and the metacentric height. Is the equilibrium stable?

::schema{acqua="-3.4,0 3.4,0 3.4,2 -3.4,2" solidi="-3.8,-0.7 3.8,-0.7 3.8,0 -3.8,0" poligoni="-1.5,1.2 1.5,1.2 1.5,2.4 -1.5,2.4" tratteggi="0,1.2 0,3.2" punti="0,2.15,G,baricentro" pelo="2.85,3.4,2,p_g = 0" quote="-1.5 1.2,-1.5 2,d = 0.80 m,-0.9;1.5 1.2,1.5 2.15,0.95 m,1" riferimento=0 titolo="Pontoon with draft $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and center of gravity $G$ at a height $0.95\,\mathrm{m}$ above the keel"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and a uniform rectangular cross section. Consider a small rotation about an axis in the waterplane.

$$
V_c = L\,B\,d = 8.00 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 0.80 = 19.20\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
I = \frac{L\,B^3}{12} = \frac{8.00 \cdot 3.00^3}{12} = 18.00\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
r = \overline{CM} = \frac{I}{V_c} = \frac{18.00}{19.20} = 0.9375\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Locate the center of buoyancy and metacenter above the keel:
$$
z_C = \frac{d}{2} = 0.400\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
z_M = z_C + r = 0.400 + 0.9375 = 1.3375\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\overline{GM} = z_M - z_G = 1.3375 - 0.950 = 0.3875\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The metacentric radius is $0.938\,\mathrm{m}$. The metacenter lies $1.338\,\mathrm{m}$ above the keel and the metacentric height is $0.388\,\mathrm{m}$. Since the metacenter is **above** the center of gravity, equilibrium is stable.

Check the transverse axis. The waterplane’s second moment about that axis is $\dfrac{B\,L^3}{12} = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 8.00^3}{12} = 128.0\,\mathrm{m^4}$, giving $r = \dfrac{128.0}{19.20} = 6.667\,\mathrm{m}$ and $z_M = 7.067\,\mathrm{m}$. This metacenter is much higher. Rotation about the longitudinal axis governs stability because $I$ is smallest for that axis.

<div class="spiegazione">

The metacentric radius is the height of the metacenter above the center of buoyancy. It equals $\dfrac{I}{V_c}$, where $I$ is the waterplane’s second moment of area about the axis of rotation. The waterplane is a rectangle $8.00 \times 3.00$. For roll about the longitudinal axis, that axis is parallel to the long sides, so the width $B$ is cubed in the second moment of area.

The center of buoyancy is the centroid of the displaced volume. For a rectangular box, it is halfway up the draft, $0.400\,\mathrm{m}$ above the keel.

Equilibrium is stable because $\overline{CM} = 0.938\,\mathrm{m}$ exceeds $\overline{CG} = 0.950 - 0.400 = 0.550\,\mathrm{m}$. After a small rotation, weight and buoyancy form a righting couple. The metacentric height $\overline{GM} = 0.388\,\mathrm{m}$ sets the rate at which the righting arm grows with heel angle.

The check about the other axis gives a metacentric radius seven times larger because the waterplane’s second moment scales with the cube of the dimension perpendicular to the axis. Check stability about the axis with the smallest $I$, the axis parallel to the longer dimension.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wooden beam of square cross section, side $a = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, length $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and density $\rho_l = 700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is placed in water with its axis and one face horizontal, as shown. Determine whether this floating orientation is stable.

::schema{acqua="-2.6,0 2.6,0 2.6,2 -2.6,2" solidi="-3,-0.6 3,-0.6 3,0 -3,0" poligoni="-0.75,0.95 0.75,0.95 0.75,2.45 -0.75,2.45" pelo="1,2.6,2,p_g = 0" quote="-0.75 0.95,-0.75 2.45,a = 0.30 m,-0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Cross section of a square beam of side $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and density $700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. The beam has a uniform cross section and floats with one face horizontal. Consider a small rotation about its longitudinal axis.

Find the draft from the flotation condition:
$$
d = \frac{\rho_l}{\rho}\,a = \frac{700}{1000} \cdot 0.30 = 0.210\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
V_c = L\,a\,d = 2.00 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 0.210 = 0.1260\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
I = \frac{L\,a^3}{12} = \frac{2.00 \cdot 0.30^3}{12} = 0.004500\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
r = \frac{I}{V_c} = \frac{0.004500}{0.1260} = 0.03571\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Locate the center of buoyancy, center of gravity, and metacenter above the bottom face:
$$
z_C = \frac{d}{2} = 0.1050\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
z_G = \frac{a}{2} = 0.1500\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
z_M = 0.1050 + 0.03571 = 0.14071\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\overline{GM} = 0.14071 - 0.1500 = -0.00929\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The negative metacentric height places the metacenter below the center of gravity. Equilibrium in this orientation is unstable, so the beam rolls to a stable orientation with an edge downward.

Check using the general stability condition. Setting $k = \dfrac{\rho_l}{\rho}$ gives $d = k\,a$, so the condition $\overline{CM} > \overline{CG}$ becomes
$$
\frac{a^2}{12\,k\,a} > \frac{a - k\,a}{2}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
6\,k^2 - 6\,k + 1 > 0 .
$$
The quadratic vanishes at $k = \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt{3}}{6}$, giving roots $k = 0.2113$ and $k = 0.7887$, and is negative between them. For $k = 0.700$ it gives $6 \cdot 0.49 - 6 \cdot 0.70 + 1 = -0.26 < 0$, consistent with the negative metacentric height found above.

<div class="spiegazione">

The submerged fraction equals the ratio of wood density to water density. Floating equilibrium therefore gives a submerged depth $d = 0.210\,\mathrm{m}$ for a beam of side $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.

The beam’s center of gravity lies at midheight, $0.150\,\mathrm{m}$ above its bottom face. Its center of buoyancy is halfway up the draft, at $0.105\,\mathrm{m}$. Their separation $0.045\,\mathrm{m}$ exceeds the metacentric radius $0.0357\,\mathrm{m}$, placing the metacenter below the center of gravity.

The cross section is narrow relative to its draft. Because the waterplane’s second moment scales with width cubed, the metacentric radius is small. A deeply immersed beam with square cross section has little transverse stability in this orientation. It rolls until a diagonal is vertical and its waterplane is wider.

Expressing the stability condition in terms of the density ratio alone shows that a square beam floats stably with a horizontal face only if $k < 0.211$ or $k > 0.789$. The wood with density ratio $k = 0.700$ lies between the two roots, confirming the result.

</div>
