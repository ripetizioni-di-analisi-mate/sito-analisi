## Exercise 1

A quarter-cylindrical fillet of radius $R = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ joins a vertical wall to the bottom. The water depth is $H = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the force components and resultant magnitude.

::schema{nodi="P0,0,1.5,-;P1,0.051,1.112,-;P2,0.201,0.75,-;P3,0.439,0.439,-;P4,0.75,0.201,-;P5,1.112,0.051,-;P6,1.5,0,-" aste="P0-P1;P1-P2;P2-P3;P3-P4;P4-P5;P5-P6" acqua="0,4 0,1.5 0.051,1.112 0.201,0.75 0.439,0.439 0.75,0.201 1.112,0.051 1.5,0 5.5,0 5.5,4" solidi="-0.8,-0.7 5.5,-0.7 5.5,0 1.5,0 1.112,0.051 0.75,0.201 0.439,0.439 0.201,0.75 0.051,1.112 0,1.5 0,4.4 -0.8,4.4" pelo="0,5.5,4,p_g = 0" punti="1.5,1.5,O,centro" quote="1.5 0,1.5 4,H = 4.00 m,2.2;1.5 1.5,0 1.5,R = 1.50 m,0.55" riferimento=0 titolo="Cylindrical transition of radius $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ between the vertical wall and the bottom, with water depth $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity. Use gauge pressures and $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$.

Find the horizontal force using the curved face’s vertical projection, a rectangle of height $R$ and width $b$ extending from depth $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ to depth $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
A_x = b\,R = 2.00 \cdot 1.50 = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
h_G = \frac{2.50 + 4.00}{2} = 3.25\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
S_x = \gamma\,h_G\,A_x = 9.81 \cdot 3.25 \cdot 3.00 = 95.65\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{b\,R^3}{12} = \frac{2.00 \cdot 1.50^3}{12} = 0.5625\,\mathrm{m^4},
\qquad
y_{Cx} = 3.25 + \frac{0.5625}{3.25 \cdot 3.00} = 3.308\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Find the vertical force from the volume bounded by the curved face, the hydrostatic head plane, and the vertical surfaces through its perimeter:
$$
\frac{V_p}{b} = R\,(H - R) + \frac{\pi R^2}{4} = 1.50 \cdot 2.50 + \frac{\pi \cdot 1.50^2}{4} = 3.750 + 1.767 = 5.517\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
V_p = 2.00 \cdot 5.517 = 11.034\,\mathrm{m^3},
\qquad
S_z = \gamma\,V_p = 9.81 \cdot 11.034 = 108.25\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Combine the components to obtain the resultant:
$$
S = \sqrt{S_x^2 + S_z^2} = \sqrt{95.65^2 + 108.25^2} = 144.45\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{S_z}{S_x} = \frac{108.25}{95.65} = 1.132,
\qquad
\theta = 48.5^\circ
$$

The horizontal component $95.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ points toward the wall and acts at depth $3.31\,\mathrm{m}$. The vertical component $108\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts **downward** because the water lies above the curved face. The resultant has magnitude $144\,\mathrm{kN}$ and makes angle $48.5^\circ$ with the horizontal.

Check both components independently. For the horizontal force, the edge pressures $24.53$ and $39.24\,\mathrm{kPa}$ give a pressure diagram with area $\dfrac{24.53 + 39.24}{2}\cdot 1.50 = 47.82\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ on the vertical projection and resultant $2.00 \cdot 47.82 = 95.65\,\mathrm{kN}$. For the vertical force, subtract the solid between the arc and the bottom from the prism with base $R \times H$. The solid has cross-sectional area $R^2 - \dfrac{\pi R^2}{4} = 2.250 - 1.767 = 0.483\,\mathrm{m^2}$ per unit width:
$$
\frac{V_p}{b} = R\,H - \left(R^2 - \frac{\pi R^2}{4}\right) = 6.000 - 0.483 = 5.517\,\mathrm{m^2} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The elemental forces on a curved face are not parallel, so calculate the resultant through its components. For the horizontal component, project the face onto a vertical plane. The resulting rectangle has height $1.50\,\mathrm{m}$, the vertical extent of the curved face. The plane-surface formulas give both the force and the center of pressure on that rectangle.

The vertical force equals the weight of the water above the curved face. Project each point of the face vertically up to the hydrostatic head plane. The resulting prism has width $b$. Its cross section comprises the rectangle $R \times (H - R)$ above the highest point of the arc plus the quarter circle between the arc and that elevation. The water overlies the face, so its force acts downward.

The resultant has magnitude $144\,\mathrm{kN}$. Because the surface is cylindrical, its line of action passes through the cylinder axis $O$. The pressure-diagram area independently confirms the horizontal component, and calculating the overlying water volume by subtraction confirms the vertical component.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The radial gate shown has radius $R = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Pin $O$ is at the free surface, the upstream depth is $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and the gate rests on the bed directly below $O$. Find the force components and resultant, and verify that the line of action passes through the pin.

::schema{nodi="Q0,0,3,-;Q1,0.102,2.224,-;Q2,0.402,1.5,-;Q3,0.879,0.879,-;Q4,1.5,0.402,-;Q5,2.224,0.102,-;Q6,3,0,-" aste="Q0-Q1;Q1-Q2;Q2-Q3;Q3-Q4;Q4-Q5;Q5-Q6" acqua="-3.5,0 3,0 2.224,0.102 1.5,0.402 0.879,0.879 0.402,1.5 0.102,2.224 0,3 -3.5,3" solidi="-4.2,-0.8 4.8,-0.8 4.8,0 -4.2,0" pelo="-3.5,-0.5,3,p_g = 0" punti="3,3,O,centro" tratteggi="0.879,0.879 3,3" quote="-3.5 0,-3.5 3,3.00 m,-0.7;3 3,0 3,R = 3.00 m,0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Radial gate of radius $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with its pivot $O$ at the free surface"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. The downstream side of the gate is dry.

Calculate the horizontal force on the vertical projection, a rectangle of height $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with its upper edge at the free surface:
$$
S_x = \gamma\,\frac{b\,h^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{2.50 \cdot 3.00^2}{2} = 110.36\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
h_{Cx} = \frac{2h}{3} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$

For the vertical component, the region bounded by the gate, the hydrostatic head plane, and the vertical surfaces through the perimeter is a quarter cylinder. Water lies beneath the gate, so this volume is hypothetical and the force acts upward:
$$
V_p = b\,\frac{\pi R^2}{4} = 2.50 \cdot \frac{\pi \cdot 3.00^2}{4} = 17.671\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
S_z = \gamma\,V_p = 9.81 \cdot 17.671 = 173.36\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
x_{Cz} = \frac{4R}{3\pi} = \frac{4 \cdot 3.00}{3\pi} = 1.2732\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Combine the components to obtain the resultant:
$$
S = \sqrt{110.36^2 + 173.36^2} = 205.5\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
\tan\theta = \frac{173.36}{110.36} = 1.571,
\qquad
\theta = 57.5^\circ
$$

The horizontal component $110\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downstream and the vertical component $173\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts upward. Their resultant has magnitude $206\,\mathrm{kN}$ and makes angle $57.5^\circ$ with the horizontal.

Check by taking moments about the pin. The horizontal force acts $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below $O$, and the vertical force acts at a horizontal distance $1.2732\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$, so
$$
M_O = S_x \cdot 2.00 - S_z \cdot 1.2732 = 220.73 - 220.73 = 0 ,
$$
so the resultant passes through the pin.

<div class="spiegazione">

The gate is a cylindrical surface of radius $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with its pivot at the free surface. The wetted surface is exactly a quarter cylinder. Its upper end is on the free surface, horizontally $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$. Its lower end is on the bottom, vertically $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$.

The vertical projection is a rectangle extending to the free surface. Its horizontal force is therefore that of a flat gate under triangular pressure loading, acting at two-thirds of the water depth below the surface.

The volume between the gate and the free surface is a quarter cylinder. Water is upstream, on **the opposite side** of the gate from that volume. The volume is hypothetical and the vertical force acts upward, tending to lift the gate. The centroid of a quarter circle is at distance $\dfrac{4R}{3\pi}$ from the center along each bounding radius. This gives the horizontal moment arm of $S_z$ about the pin.

The component moments about the pin sum to zero, as required for this cylindrical surface. Every elemental pressure force acts normal to the surface, along a radius through the axis. The operating mechanism therefore need not resist any moment from the hydrostatic force.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A horizontal cylinder of radius $R = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and length $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ rests on a channel bed, holding back water of depth $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$ upstream. The downstream side is dry, as shown. Find the force components, their resultant, and the minimum cylinder weight required to maintain contact with the bed.

::schema{cerchi="0,0.8,0.8" acqua="-3,0 0,0 -0.4,0.107 -0.693,0.4 -0.8,0.8 -0.693,1.2 -0.4,1.493 0,1.6 -3,1.6" solidi="-3.4,-0.8 2.6,-0.8 2.6,0 -3.4,0" pelo="-3,-0.2,1.6,p_g = 0" punti="0,0.8,O,centro" quote="-3 0,-3 1.6,1.60 m,-0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Cylinder of radius $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and length $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ resting on the bottom, with upstream water depth $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible liquid at rest under gravity and use gauge pressures. Only the upstream half of the cylinder is wet, from its bottom contact line to its highest point at the free surface.

Calculate the horizontal force on the vertical projection, a rectangle of height $2R = 1.60\,\mathrm{m}$ with its upper edge at the free surface:
$$
S_x = \gamma\,\frac{b\,(2R)^2}{2} = 9.81 \cdot \frac{2.00 \cdot 1.60^2}{2} = 25.11\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
z_{Cx} = \frac{2R}{3} = 0.533\,\mathrm{m}
$$

For the vertical component, divide the wetted half into an upper quarter, with water above it, and a lower quarter, with the cylinder itself above it:
$$
\frac{V_{p,\text{upper}}}{b} = R^2 - \frac{\pi R^2}{4} = 0.64000 - 0.50265 = 0.13735\,\mathrm{m^2}
\quad \text{(downward)}
$$
$$
\frac{V_{p,\text{lower}}}{b} = R^2 + \frac{\pi R^2}{4} = 0.64000 + 0.50265 = 1.14265\,\mathrm{m^2}
\quad \text{(upward)}
$$
$$
S_z = \gamma\,b\left(1.14265 - 0.13735\right) = 9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 1.00531 = 19.72\,\mathrm{kN}
\quad \text{upward}
$$

Combine the components and check whether the cylinder remains on the bottom:
$$
S = \sqrt{25.11^2 + 19.72^2} = 31.93\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
\tan\theta = \frac{19.72}{25.11} = 0.785,
\qquad
\theta = 38.1^\circ
$$
$$
G \geq S_z = 19.7\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

The horizontal force $25.1\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downstream and the vertical force $19.7\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts upward. Their resultant $31.9\,\mathrm{kN}$ makes angle $38.1^\circ$ with the horizontal and passes through the cylinder axis. The cylinder stays on the bottom if its weight is at least $19.7\,\mathrm{kN}$, or $2.01\,\mathrm{t}$.

Check using Archimedes’ principle. The difference between the two fluid volumes is
$$
\left(R^2 + \frac{\pi R^2}{4}\right) - \left(R^2 - \frac{\pi R^2}{4}\right) = \frac{\pi R^2}{2} = 1.00531\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
$$
which is the semicircle’s area. The vertical force therefore equals the weight $9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 1.00531 = 19.72\,\mathrm{kN}$ of the water that would occupy the submerged half of the cylinder, confirming the result.

<div class="spiegazione">

The upstream half of the cylinder is wet, the downstream side is dry, and the bottom contact is along the lowest line of the cylinder. The vertical projection is a rectangle one diameter high with its upper edge at the free surface. Its triangular pressure diagram gives a horizontal force acting one-third of the diameter above the bottom.

The two quarter-cylinders give opposing vertical forces. Water fills the space above the upper quarter up to the free surface, so its weight gives a downward force. The cylinder occupies the space above the lower quarter, so this larger volume is hypothetical and its force acts upward. The net vertical force therefore acts **upward**.

The difference in the volumes has a semicircular cross section, giving the buoyant force on half the cylinder. The cylinder’s weight must resist that force. If the force exceeds the weight, the cylinder lifts and water flows underneath it.

Subtracting the two overlying volumes leaves the submerged half of the cylinder. Applying Archimedes’ principle to that volume confirms the vertical force.

</div>
