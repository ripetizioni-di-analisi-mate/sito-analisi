## Exercise 1

A horizontal pipe expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water, as shown. Find the head loss, the loss coefficient $\xi$ based on upstream velocity, and the pressure change between the two sections.

::schema{nodi="A,0,2,-;B,5,2,-;C,12,2,-" condotte="A-B,0.9;B-C,1.5" frecce="1.6,2,3.0,2,v_1;8.0,2,9.4,2,v_2" etichette="5.0,3.3,1;9.0,3.3,2;2.2,0.95,D_1 = 150 mm;9.6,0.75,D_2 = 250 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Sudden expansion in a horizontal pipe"}

The fluid is incompressible, the flow is steady, and all pressures are gauge pressures. At section $1$ the pressure $p_1$ acts over the entire area $A_2$.

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.150^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.04909\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.040}{0.01767}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.26\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.040}{0.04909}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.815\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Delta H = \frac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(2.26 - 0.815)^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{2.098}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.107\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\xi = \left( 1 - \frac{A_1}{A_2} \right)^2 = \left( 1 - \frac{0.01767}{0.04909} \right)^2 = (1 - 0.360)^2 = 0.410
$$

Apply the energy equation with head loss and $z_1 = z_2$ to obtain the pressure change:
$$
\frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{2g} - \Delta H = 0.261 - 0.0338 - 0.107\,\mathrm{m} = 0.120\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_2 - p_1 = 9810 \cdot 0.120\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.18\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check the pressure difference independently by applying the axial momentum equation, which gives
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \rho\,v_2\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 0.815 \cdot (0.815 - 2.26)\,\mathrm{Pa} = -1.18\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
$$
so $p_2 - p_1 = 1.18\,\mathrm{kPa}$, in agreement with the energy balance.

<div class="spiegazione">

Continuity gives the same discharge at both sections, so velocity is inversely proportional to area. As the diameter increases from $150$ to $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, the area nearly triples and the mean velocity falls from $2.26$ to $0.815\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The **Borda–Carnot head loss** is proportional to the square of the velocity difference and equals $0.107\,\mathrm{m}$. Dividing by the upstream velocity head gives $\xi = 0.410$. The expansion therefore dissipates $41\,\%$ of the upstream velocity head.

Pressure **rises** across the expansion because the water slows and converts some of its velocity head into pressure head. The pressure head rises by $0.120\,\mathrm{m}$, less than the decrease in velocity head, $0.227\,\mathrm{m}$. The difference is the head loss.

The axial momentum balance provides a second calculation of the pressure difference from the forces acting on the water. Its agreement with the independent energy balance **confirms** the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A horizontal pipe contracts abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries $Q = 50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water, as shown. The contraction coefficient is $C_c = 0.62$. Find the velocity at the vena contracta, the head loss, the coefficient $\xi$ based on downstream velocity, and the pressure change.

::schema{nodi="A,0,2,-;B,5,2,-;C,12,2,-" condotte="A-B,1.5;B-C,0.9" tratteggi="5,2.75 5.9,2.35 7.0,2.35 8.3,2.45;5,1.25 5.9,1.65 7.0,1.65 8.3,1.55" frecce="1.6,2,3.0,2,v_1;9.6,2,11.0,2,v_2" etichette="2.2,0.60,D_1 = 300 mm;9.8,0.90,D_2 = 150 mm;6.4,3.1,vena contracta c" riferimento=0 titolo="Sudden contraction showing the vena contracta"}

$$
A_1 = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
A_2 = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.050}{0.0707}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.707\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.050}{0.01767}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.83\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
A_c = C_c\,A_2 = 0.62 \cdot 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.01096\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
v_c = \frac{v_2}{C_c} = \frac{2.83}{0.62}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.56\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

The loss occurs as the flow expands from the vena contracta to section $2$, so the Borda–Carnot relation applies between those two sections and gives
$$
\Delta H = \frac{(v_c - v_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(4.56 - 2.83)^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{2.993}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.153\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\xi = \left( \frac{1}{C_c} - 1 \right)^2 = \left( \frac{1}{0.62} - 1 \right)^2 = 0.613^2 = 0.376
$$
$$
\frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{2g} - \Delta H = 0.0255 - 0.408 - 0.153\,\mathrm{m} = -0.536\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_2 - p_1 = -9810 \cdot 0.536\,\mathrm{Pa} = -5.26\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check the result by treating the contraction in two stages. Between section $1$ and the vena contracta, the flow accelerates with negligible losses, so Bernoulli’s equation gives
$$
\frac{p_c - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{v_1^2 - v_c^2}{2g} = 0.0255 - 1.061\,\mathrm{m} = -1.036\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Between the vena contracta and section $2$, the momentum equation gives
$$
\frac{p_2 - p_c}{\gamma} = \frac{v_2\,(v_c - v_2)}{g} = \frac{2.83 \cdot 1.73}{9.81}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.499\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Adding the two changes gives $\dfrac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = -1.036 + 0.499 = -0.537\,\mathrm{m}$, in agreement with the earlier result.

<div class="spiegazione">

In a sudden contraction, the flow separates at the sharp edge and initially occupies only part of the smaller pipe. It reaches its minimum area and maximum speed at the **vena contracta**. The contraction coefficient relates its area to the downstream pipe area, giving $v_c = \dfrac{v_2}{C_c}$.

From section $1$ to the vena contracta the flow accelerates. The streamlines remain smooth and dissipation is small. From the vena contracta to section $2$ the flow undergoes a sudden expansion, so the **Borda–Carnot equation** applies. The head loss is $0.153\,\mathrm{m}$, or $37.6\,\%$ of the downstream velocity head.

Pressure **drops** by $5.26\,\mathrm{kPa}$ for two reasons. The flow accelerates, converting pressure head to velocity head. It then dissipates part of its total head in the recirculating eddies as the jet expands.

Check the result in two stages. Apply Bernoulli’s equation with negligible loss to the accelerating flow, then use the momentum equation for the expansion. Adding the two pressure changes reproduces the direct calculation and **confirms** the result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water and must be connected to a pipe of diameter $D_3 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$. Compare the head loss for a single expansion with that for two successive expansions, first to $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and then to $D_3$, as shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,2,-;B,4,2,-;C,8,2,-;E,12,2,-" condotte="A-B,0.8;B-C,1.0;C-E,1.2" frecce="1.2,2,2.4,2,v_1" etichette="1.8,1.00,D_1 = 200 mm;5.6,0.90,D_2 = 250 mm;9.6,0.80,D_3 = 300 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Two successive expansions from $200$ to $300\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A_1 = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
A_2 = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
A_3 = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.060}{0.0314}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.91\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.060}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.22\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_3 = \frac{0.060}{0.0707}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.849\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

For a single expansion from $D_1$ to $D_3$, the head loss is
$$
\Delta H_a = \frac{(v_1 - v_3)^2}{2g} = \frac{(1.91 - 0.849)^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{1.126}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0574\,\mathrm{m}
$$

For two successive expansions, the head loss is
$$
\Delta H_b = \frac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g} + \frac{(v_2 - v_3)^2}{2g} = \frac{0.473}{19.62} + \frac{0.139}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0241 + 0.0071\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0312\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta H_b}{\Delta H_a} = \frac{0.0312}{0.0574} = 0.544
$$

Check the head losses using coefficients based on the upstream velocity at each expansion. This gives
$$
\xi_a = \left( 1 - \frac{A_1}{A_3} \right)^2 = (1 - 0.444)^2 = 0.309
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\Delta H_a = 0.309 \cdot \frac{1.91^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0574\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
$$
\xi_1 = \left( 1 - \frac{A_1}{A_2} \right)^2 = (1 - 0.640)^2 = 0.130 ,
\qquad
\xi_2 = \left( 1 - \frac{A_2}{A_3} \right)^2 = (1 - 0.694)^2 = 0.0934 ,
$$
$$
\Delta H_b = 0.130 \cdot 0.186 + 0.0934 \cdot 0.0761\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0242 + 0.0071\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0313\,\mathrm{m} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Continuity gives the three velocities. The Borda–Carnot loss is proportional to the square of the velocity drop, so dividing that drop into two steps reduces the sum of the squares. For the single expansion the squared drop is $(1.91 - 0.849)^2 = 1.126\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. For the two expansions the sum is $0.473 + 0.139 = 0.612\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$.

The two expansions together dissipate $54.4\,\%$ of the head lost in the single expansion, nearly halving the loss. In the limit of infinitely many infinitesimal steps, the Borda–Carnot loss vanishes. This is why a gradual **conical diffuser** is used when little head is available. A real diffuser still has wall-friction losses and can also have separation losses if its opening angle is large.

The first expansion contributes more head loss than the second because its upstream velocity is higher and velocity head increases with the square of velocity.

As a check, use the coefficients $\xi$ for the individual expansions, each referenced to its own upstream velocity. The losses agree with those calculated from the velocity drops, **confirming** the comparison.

</div>
