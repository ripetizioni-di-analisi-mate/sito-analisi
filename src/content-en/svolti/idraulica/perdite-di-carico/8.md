## Exercise 1

A cast-iron pipe of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 500\,\mathrm{m}$, with $\varepsilon = 0.3\,\mathrm{mm}$, carries $Q = 70\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. Find the energy gradient, wall shear stress and total tangential force that the water exerts on the wall over the length shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,1,-;B,12,1,-" condotte="A-B,0.5" frecce="2.6,1.3,1.6,1.3,τ_0;2.6,0.7,1.6,0.7,;9.0,1.3,8.0,1.3,;9.0,0.7,8.0,0.7,;5.2,1,6.6,1,Q" etichette="4.0,0.0,D = 250 mm · ε = 0.3 mm;0.3,1.9,1;11.4,1.9,2" quote="A,B,L = 500 m,1.4" riferimento=0 titolo="Pipe between sections $1$ and $2$ showing the shear stress exerted by the wall on the fluid"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{0.070}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.43\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
R = \frac{D}{4} = 0.0625\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Re = \frac{1.43 \cdot 0.250}{10^{-6}} = 3.57 \cdot 10^5 ,
\qquad
\frac{\varepsilon}{D} = 1.20 \cdot 10^{-3}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lambda = 0.0213
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.43^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.104\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
J = 0.0213 \cdot \frac{0.104}{0.250} = 0.00881 ,
\qquad
\Delta H = 0.00881 \cdot 500\,\mathrm{m} = 4.41\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\tau_0 = \gamma\,R\,J = 9810 \cdot 0.0625 \cdot 0.00881\,\mathrm{Pa} = 5.40\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$

The wetted wall area of this pipe section is $C\,L = \pi D L$, so
$$
F = \tau_0\,\pi\,D\,L = 5.40 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.250 \cdot 500\,\mathrm{N} = 5.40 \cdot 393\,\mathrm{N} = 2.12\,\mathrm{kN} .
$$

The force balance on the fluid in the pipe provides a check. The pressure drop is
$$
\Delta p = \gamma\,\Delta H = 9810 \cdot 4.41\,\mathrm{Pa} = 43.3\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
$$
and the net pressure force on the two end sections is
$$
\Delta p\,A = 43\,300 \cdot 0.0491\,\mathrm{N} = 2.13\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
which equals the tangential force found above.

<div class="spiegazione">

First find $\lambda$ from the Colebrook–White equation using $Re = 3.57 \cdot 10^5$ and $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 1.20 \cdot 10^{-3}$. The Darcy–Weisbach equation then gives the energy gradient.

The wall shear relation $\tau_0 = \gamma R J$ gives $5.40\,\mathrm{Pa}$, slightly more than one twenty-thousandth of an atmosphere. Wall shear stress is small but acts over a large surface.

The total force is the wall shear stress times the wetted wall area, $\pi D L = 393\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving $2.12\,\mathrm{kN}$. By Newton’s third law, the fluid exerts an equal and opposite force on the pipe in the direction of flow. The pipe anchors must carry this load.

An independent check uses the momentum balance for the whole pipe segment. In uniform flow the resultant force on the segment is zero, so the force due to the pressure drop, $\Delta p\,A$, balances the tangential force exerted by the wall. The two values agree within rounding, **confirming the result**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a cast-iron pipe of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\varepsilon = 0.6\,\mathrm{mm}$, the measured wall shear stress is $\tau_0 = 4.50\,\mathrm{Pa}$, as shown. Find the energy gradient, Darcy friction factor, mean velocity and discharge.

::schema{nodi="A,0,1,-;B,12,1,-" condotte="A-B,0.55" frecce="2.8,1.33,1.8,1.33,τ_0 = 4.50 Pa;2.8,0.67,1.8,0.67,;5.6,1,7.0,1,v" etichette="7.4,0.0,D = 300 mm · ε = 0.6 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Pipe with measured wall shear stress"}

The wall shear relation with $R = \dfrac{D}{4} = 0.0750\,\mathrm{m}$ gives
$$
J = \frac{\tau_0}{\gamma\,R} = \frac{4.50}{9810 \cdot 0.0750} = \frac{4.50}{736} = 0.00612
$$

The definition of the Darcy friction factor, $\tau_0 = \dfrac{\lambda}{8}\rho v^2$, gives
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{8\,\tau_0}{\rho\,\lambda}} = \sqrt{\frac{8 \cdot 4.50}{1000\,\lambda}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.0360}{\lambda}} ,
$$
and since $\lambda$ depends on velocity, solve by iteration using $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{D} = 2.00 \cdot 10^{-3}$.

| Trial | Assumed $\lambda$ | $v$ in m/s | $Re$ | Calculated $\lambda$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $0.02500$ | $1.200$ | $3.60 \cdot 10^5$ | $0.02390$ |
| 2 | $0.02390$ | $1.227$ | $3.68 \cdot 10^5$ | $0.02390$ |

$$
v = 1.23\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
A = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
Q = 1.23 \cdot 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0868\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 86.8\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

As a check, substituting $\lambda = 0.0239$ and $v = 1.227\,\mathrm{m/s}$ into the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives
$$
J = \lambda\,\frac{1}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.0239 \cdot \frac{1}{0.300} \cdot \frac{1.506}{19.62} = 0.00612 ,
$$
which agrees with the energy gradient found from wall shear stress.

<div class="spiegazione">

Wall shear stress and energy gradient are related by $\tau_0 = \gamma R J$. This follows from the force balance on a length of fluid in the pipe and is independent of the flow regime. The energy gradient can therefore be found directly without first calculating velocity.

Determining velocity requires the Darcy friction factor, since $\tau_0 = \dfrac{\lambda}{8}\rho v^2$. Solve the implicit relation by iteration as before. Here $\lambda$ enters only through the relation between $\tau_0$ and $v$ because the energy gradient is already known.

The mean velocity is $1.23\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the discharge is $86.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$, both typical for a $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ cast-iron pipe in normal operation.

As a check, recalculate the energy gradient using the Darcy–Weisbach equation with the velocity and friction factor just obtained. This reproduces the gradient found from wall shear stress, so the two results are **consistent**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A steel pipe of diameter $D = 350\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $L = 400\,\mathrm{m}$, with $\varepsilon = 0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$, carries $Q = 180\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water, as shown. Find the wall shear stress, shear stress at a distance $\dfrac{D}{4}$ from the centerline, total tangential force on the pipe section, and friction velocity $u_* = \sqrt{\dfrac{\tau_0}{\rho}}$.

::schema{nodi="A,0,1,-;B,12,1,-" condotte="A-B,0.7" frecce="2.8,1.4,1.8,1.4,τ_0;2.8,0.6,1.8,0.6,;8.4,1.4,7.4,1.4,;8.4,0.6,7.4,0.6,;4.6,1,6.0,1,Q" tratteggi="4.0,1.175 10.0,1.175;4.0,0.825 10.0,0.825" etichette="10.9,1.175,r = D/4;5.0,-0.15,D = 350 mm · ε = 0.2 mm" quote="A,B,L = 400 m,1.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Pipe section showing shear stress at the wall and at a distance $\dfrac{D}{4}$ from the centerline"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.350^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.0962\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
v = \frac{0.180}{0.0962}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.87\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
R = 0.0875\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Re = \frac{1.87 \cdot 0.350}{10^{-6}} = 6.55 \cdot 10^5 ,
\qquad
\frac{\varepsilon}{D} = 5.71 \cdot 10^{-4}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lambda = 0.0179
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.87^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.178\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
J = 0.0179 \cdot \frac{0.178}{0.350} = 0.00912 ,
\qquad
\Delta H = 3.65\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\tau_0 = \gamma\,R\,J = 9810 \cdot 0.0875 \cdot 0.00912\,\mathrm{Pa} = 7.83\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$

Shear stress increases linearly from the centerline to the wall, and $\dfrac{D}{4}$ is half the pipe radius, so
$$
\tau\!\left( \frac{D}{4} \right) = \tau_0\,\frac{2r}{D} = \frac{\tau_0}{2} = 3.92\,\mathrm{Pa} .
$$
$$
F = \tau_0\,\pi\,D\,L = 7.83 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.350 \cdot 400\,\mathrm{N} = 3.44\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
u_* = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_0}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{7.83}{1000}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.0885\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

The Darcy friction factor gives an independent check on friction velocity. The relation $\tau_0 = \dfrac{\lambda}{8}\rho v^2$ implies
$$
\frac{u_*}{v} = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{8}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.0179}{8}} = 0.0473
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
u_* = 0.0473 \cdot 1.87\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.0885\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
$$
in agreement with the earlier value. The pressure drop also provides a check on the tangential force, giving $\gamma \Delta H A = 9810 \cdot 3.65 \cdot 0.0962 = 3.44\,\mathrm{kN}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate the energy gradient as before. This large-diameter steel pipe has low relative roughness, giving a friction factor of $0.0179$.

The wall shear stress is $7.83\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Shear stress increases linearly with distance from the centerline, where it is zero. At a distance $\dfrac{D}{4}$ from the centerline, halfway to the wall, the stress is therefore half of $\tau_0$. This linear distribution follows from the force balance on a concentric cylinder of fluid and holds in any flow regime.

The **friction velocity** $u_*$ has the dimensions of velocity and measures the intensity of wall shear. Its ratio to the mean velocity depends only on the Darcy friction factor. Here that ratio is $0.0473$, so $u_*$ is about one twentieth of $v$. Friction velocity also enters the roughness criterion through the group $\dfrac{u_*\varepsilon}{\nu}$, which here is $\dfrac{0.0885 \cdot 2 \cdot 10^{-4}}{10^{-6}} = 17.7$. The pipe is in the transitionally rough regime.

Check the friction velocity from the ratio $\sqrt{\dfrac{\lambda}{8}}$ and the tangential force from the pressure drop. The values agree with the direct calculations, **confirming** both results.

</div>
