## Exercise 1

A horizontal $180^\circ$ pipe bend of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries discharge $Q = 90\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 220\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the local head loss in the bend is $\Delta H = \xi\,\dfrac{v^2}{2g}$ with $\xi = 0.50$. Find the force exerted by the water on the bend shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3,1.8,-;D,0,1.8,-" condotte="A-B-C-D,0.5" frecce="0.1,0,0.9,0,Q" tratteggi="0.4,-0.5 0.4,2.3" etichette="0.25,0.55,1;0.25,1.35,2;1.6,0.9,D = 250 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of a horizontal $180^\circ$ bend"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
v = \frac{0.090}{0.0491} = 1.83\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.83^2}{19.62} = 0.171\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\Delta H = 0.50 \cdot 0.171 = 0.0857\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_2 = p_1 - \gamma\,\Delta H = 220 - 9.81 \cdot 0.0857 = 219.2\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Take $x$ along the inlet velocity. The outlet velocity points in the opposite direction and its axial component is $-v$. The outlet pressure force acts in the $+x$ direction because the inward normal at the outlet points against the outgoing flow.

$$
\rho\,Q\,(-v - v) = p_1 A + p_2 A + R_x
$$
$$
2\,\rho\,Q\,v = 2 \cdot 1000 \cdot 0.090 \cdot 1.83 = 330\,\mathrm{N} = 0.330\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
(p_1 + p_2)\,A = (220 + 219.2) \cdot 0.0491 = 21.56\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
R_x = -2\,\rho\,Q\,v - (p_1 + p_2)\,A = -0.330 - 21.56 = -21.89\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
F = 21.9\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check that the difference in total head equals the head loss.
$$
H_1 - H_2 = \frac{p_1 - p_2}{\gamma} = \frac{220 - 219.2}{9.81} = 0.0857\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta H
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the $180^\circ$ bend the inlet and outlet sections are parallel and face opposite flow directions. The two pressure forces, $\vec{\Pi}_1$ and $\vec{\Pi}_2$, therefore act **in the same direction** and add. For given pipe diameter and pressure, a $180^\circ$ bend thus carries the greatest resultant of the bends considered.

Both momentum-flux magnitudes also contribute because the velocity reverses, with change $-2v$. This gives the expression $F = 2\rho Q v + (p_1 + p_2)A$.

The local loss affects the force calculation through the downstream pressure. The sections have equal diameter and elevation, so Bernoulli’s equation gives $p_2 = p_1 - \gamma \Delta H$, lower than $0.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

The force on the bend is $21.9\,\mathrm{kN}$ **in the direction of the incoming velocity**. It tends to push the bend away from the two straight pipe sections.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A water jet of diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ enters a stationary vane and leaves at angle $\theta = 120^\circ$ to the inlet direction. Neglect vane friction, so the outlet speed equals the inlet speed. Find the components, magnitude, and direction of the force exerted on the vane shown.

::schema{acqua="0,1 3.6,1 3.6,1.4 0,1.4;3.60,1.00 3.88,1.03 4.14,1.13 4.37,1.28 4.56,1.48 4.70,1.72 4.78,1.99 4.80,2.27 4.75,2.54 4.64,2.80 4.29,2.60 4.37,2.43 4.40,2.25 4.39,2.06 4.33,1.88 4.24,1.72 4.11,1.59 3.96,1.49 3.78,1.42 3.60,1.40" solidi="3.54,1.00 3.84,1.02 4.12,1.12 4.37,1.28 4.57,1.50 4.72,1.76 4.79,2.05 4.79,2.35 4.72,2.64 4.57,2.91 4.80,3.07 4.98,2.75 5.07,2.39 5.07,2.02 4.98,1.66 4.80,1.34 4.55,1.07 4.24,0.87 3.89,0.75 3.52,0.72" frecce="0.5,1.2,1.7,1.2,v = 20 m/s;4.52,2.61,3.97,3.57,v" etichette="1.2,1.65,d = 50 mm;5.4,2.9,θ = 120°" riferimento=0 titolo="Stationary vane deflecting a jet through $\theta = 120^\circ$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.050^2}{4} = 0.001963\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
Q = 0.001963 \cdot 20 = 0.03927\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 39.3\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,v = 1000 \cdot 0.03927 \cdot 20 = 785\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Take $x$ along the incoming jet. The free jet is at atmospheric pressure at the flow sections, and its weight is neglected. In the first set of equations the transverse reaction on the water is positive upward. In the second set the transverse force on the vane is given as the magnitude of its downward component.

$$
\begin{cases}
R_x = \rho\,Q\,v\,(\cos\theta - 1) \\[4pt]
R_y = \rho\,Q\,v\,\sin\theta
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_x &= \rho\,Q\,v\,(1 - \cos 120^\circ) = 785 \cdot (1 + 0.500) = 1178\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_y &= \rho\,Q\,v\,\sin 120^\circ = 785 \cdot 0.866 = 680\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
F = \sqrt{1178^2 + 680^2} = 1360\,\mathrm{N}
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{680}{1178} = 0.577
\;\Rightarrow\;
\varphi = 30^\circ
$$

Check using the expression for the resultant force on a vane.
$$
F = 2\,\rho\,Q\,v\,\sin\frac{\theta}{2} = 2 \cdot 785 \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 1360\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The free jet has zero gauge pressure at its flow sections. The integral momentum equation therefore equates the change in momentum flux to the force exerted by the vane on the water.

With negligible vane friction and zero gauge pressure at inlet and outlet, Bernoulli’s equation gives equal velocity heads and hence equal speeds. Only the direction changes, through the angle $\theta$.

The force on the vane is $1360\,\mathrm{N}$ at an angle of $\varphi = 30^\circ$ below the incoming jet axis. The vane force points opposite to the upward deflection of the water and bisects the angle between the incoming velocity and the reverse of the outgoing velocity. The relation $\varphi = 90^\circ - \dfrac{\theta}{2}$ is the same as for a pipe elbow, with the pressure terms omitted here.

The force component along the jet, $1178\,\mathrm{N}$, is one and a half times the force $785\,\mathrm{N}$ on a plate normal to the jet. Deflection sends some momentum back upstream, increasing the force beyond that required merely to stop the forward momentum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A water jet of diameter $d = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v_1 = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a stationary vane and turns back through $180^\circ$. Friction reduces its outlet speed to $v_2 = 0.90\,v_1$. Find the force of the jet on the vane shown and compare it with the frictionless value.

::schema{acqua="0,1.05 3.6,1.05 3.6,1.45 0,1.45;3.6,1.45 4.1,1.5 4.5,1.75 4.75,2.15 4.8,2.6 4.75,3.05 4.5,3.45 4.1,3.7 3.6,3.75 3.6,3.35 4.0,3.3 4.3,3.1 4.45,2.8 4.45,2.4 4.3,2.1 4.0,1.9 3.6,1.85;0.3,3.35 3.6,3.35 3.6,3.75 0.3,3.75" solidi="3.6,0.95 4.25,1.0 4.75,1.35 5.1,1.9 5.2,2.6 5.1,3.3 4.75,3.85 4.25,4.2 3.6,4.25 3.6,3.85 4.1,3.8 4.5,3.5 4.75,3.05 4.8,2.6 4.75,2.15 4.5,1.7 4.1,1.4 3.6,1.35" frecce="0.5,1.25,1.7,1.25,v_1 = 25 m/s;2.6,3.55,1.4,3.55,v_2" etichette="1.2,1.7,d = 40 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Vane that turns the jet back, with friction reducing its exit speed"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.040^2}{4} = 0.001257\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
Q = 0.001257 \cdot 25 = 0.03142\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 31.4\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
v_2 = 0.90 \cdot 25 = 22.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Take $x$ along the incoming jet. The outlet velocity component is $-v_2$.

$$
\rho\,Q\,(-v_2 - v_1) = R_x
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
R_x = -\rho\,Q\,(v_1 + v_2)
$$
$$
F = \rho\,Q\,(v_1 + v_2) = 1000 \cdot 0.03142 \cdot (25 + 22.5) = 1492\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Compare with the frictionless case, where $v_2 = v_1$.
$$
F_0 = 2\,\rho\,Q\,v_1 = 2 \cdot 1000 \cdot 0.03142 \cdot 25 = 1571\,\mathrm{N}
\qquad
\frac{F}{F_0} = \frac{1 + 0.90}{2} = 0.95
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With a deflection of $180^\circ$, the inlet and outlet velocities point in opposite directions. The change in momentum flux is $-\rho Q (v_1 + v_2)$, so the two magnitudes add, as in a $180^\circ$ pipe bend.

Friction dissipates some velocity head along the vane, leaving a lower outlet speed. The discharge is unchanged by continuity, so the outgoing sheet must become thicker as its speed decreases.

The force on the vane is $1492\,\mathrm{N}$ in the incoming jet direction. Without friction it would be $1571\,\mathrm{N}$. Reducing the outlet speed by ten percent reduces the force by five percent because the outlet speed originally contributes half of the sum $v_1 + v_2$.

The ratio $\dfrac{F}{F_0} = \dfrac{1 + k}{2}$ applies for any value of the coefficient $k = \dfrac{v_2}{v_1}$ and gives the reduction in force due to friction directly.

</div>
