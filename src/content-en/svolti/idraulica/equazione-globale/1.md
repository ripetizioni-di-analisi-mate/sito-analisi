## Exercise 1

A horizontal $90^\circ$ pipe elbow of diameter $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries discharge $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 250\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Head loss in the elbow is neglected, so the outlet pressure equals the inlet pressure. Find the fluid force on the elbow shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3.2,0,-;C,3.2,3.2,-" condotte="A-B-C,0.6" tratteggi="0.9,-0.6 0.9,0.6;2.6,2.2 3.8,2.2" frecce="0.1,0,1,0,Q" etichette="0.72,0.95,1;3.98,2.2,2;0.2,-0.95,D = 300 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of a horizontal $90^\circ$ elbow, with the control volume between sections $1$ and $2$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.300^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.120}{0.0707} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Take the $x$ axis along the inlet velocity and the $y$ axis along the outlet velocity. Assume steady incompressible flow with $\beta = 1$. Use gauge pressures, neglect losses, and take the elbow to lie in a horizontal plane.

$$
\begin{cases}
\rho\,Q\,(0 - v) = p_1 A + R_x \\[4pt]
\rho\,Q\,(v - 0) = -p_2 A + R_y
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,v = 1000 \cdot 0.120 \cdot 1.70 = 204\,\mathrm{N} = 0.204\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
p_1 A = p_2 A = 250 \cdot 0.0707 = 17.67\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= -\rho Q v - p_1 A = -0.204 - 17.67 = -17.87\,\mathrm{kN} \\[6pt]
R_y &= \rho Q v + p_2 A = 0.204 + 17.67 = 17.87\,\mathrm{kN}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
F_x = 17.87\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F_y = -17.87\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
F = \sqrt{17.87^2 + 17.87^2} = 25.3\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check using the formula for a constant-diameter elbow:
$$
F = 2\,(\rho Q v + p A)\,\sin\frac{\theta}{2} = 2 \cdot 17.87 \cdot \sin 45^\circ = 25.3\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose sections in the straight runs immediately upstream and downstream of the bend. There the streamlines are parallel and pressure is distributed hydrostatically. Inside the bend, the streamlines curve and the same pressure-force calculation would not apply.

Pressure acts inward on the control volume. At section $1$, the inward normal points with the flow, so $\vec{\Pi}_1$ acts along $+x$. At section $2$, the inward normal opposes the flow, so $\vec{\Pi}_2$ acts along $-y$. The elbow is horizontal. Weight is perpendicular to its plane and drops out of both component equations.

With constant diameter, continuity gives the same velocity magnitude $1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at both sections. The elevations are equal and losses are negligible, so Bernoulli’s equation gives $p_2 = p_1$.

The two component equations give $\vec{R}$, the force of the elbow on the water. The force of the water on the elbow is $-\vec{R}$. In the diagram its components are $17.87\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right and $17.87\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward. It has magnitude $25.3\,\mathrm{kN}$ and points outward along the **angle bisector**. The thrust block must resist this force.

The term $\rho Q v = 0.204\,\mathrm{kN}$ is less than 1.5 percent of $pA = 17.67\,\mathrm{kN}$. Under these conditions, the force on a pressurized pipe elbow is governed almost entirely by its operating pressure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A horizontal elbow of diameter $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ turns the flow through $\theta = 60^\circ$. The discharge is $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and negligible head loss gives gauge pressure $p = 320\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at both sections. Find the components and magnitude of the fluid force on the elbow shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,5,3.46,-" condotte="A-B-C,0.55" tratteggi="0.8,-0.6 0.8,0.6;3.9,2.75 4.9,2.15" frecce="0.1,0,0.9,0,Q" etichette="0.62,0.9,1;5.1,2.1,2;2.1,1.5,θ = 60°;0.2,-0.95,D = 250 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of an elbow deflecting the flow through $\theta = 60^\circ$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
v = \frac{0.100}{0.0491} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\rho Q v = 1000 \cdot 0.100 \cdot 2.04 = 204\,\mathrm{N} = 0.204\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
p A = 320 \cdot 0.0491 = 15.71\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Take the $x$ axis along the inlet velocity. The outlet velocity components are $(v\cos\theta,\;v\sin\theta)$.

$$
\begin{cases}
\rho Q\,(v\cos\theta - v) = pA - pA\cos\theta + R_x \\[4pt]
\rho Q\,v\sin\theta = -pA\sin\theta + R_y
\end{cases}
$$
$$
K = \rho Q v + pA = 0.204 + 15.71 = 15.91\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= -K\,(1 - \cos 60^\circ) = -15.91 \cdot 0.500 = -7.96\,\mathrm{kN} \\[6pt]
R_y &= K\,\sin 60^\circ = 15.91 \cdot 0.866 = 13.78\,\mathrm{kN}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
F_x = 7.96\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F_y = -13.78\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F = \sqrt{7.96^2 + 13.78^2} = 15.9\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check the elbow force using the elbow formula and the angle bisector:
$$
F = 2 K \sin\frac{\theta}{2} = 2 \cdot 15.91 \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 15.9\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{13.78}{7.96} = 1.73
\;\Rightarrow\;
\varphi = 60^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the same momentum balance as in the preceding exercise. Here the outlet velocity is oblique to the inlet velocity, so resolve it into components $v\cos\theta$ and $v\sin\theta$. The pressure force at the outlet points into the control volume, giving components $-pA\cos\theta$ and $-pA\sin\theta$.

The terms $\rho Q v$ and $pA$ always appear as a sum. Group them into the constant $K$ before substituting numerical values.

The force on the elbow has magnitude $15.9\,\mathrm{kN}$. Its components are $7.96\,\mathrm{kN}$ along the inlet flow and $13.78\,\mathrm{kN}$ opposite the direction of deflection. It makes an angle $\varphi = 60^\circ$ with the inlet axis, equal to $90^\circ - \dfrac{\theta}{2}$. This is the bisector between the two sections, directed toward the outside of the bend.

At the same diameter, discharge, and pressure, a bend through $60^\circ$ carries force $15.9\,\mathrm{kN}$, whereas a bend through $90^\circ$ would carry $22.5\,\mathrm{kN}$. The ratio of the forces is $\dfrac{\sin 30^\circ}{\sin 45^\circ}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $90^\circ$ elbow of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ turns horizontal flow vertically upward. The discharge is $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$, the upstream gauge pressure $p_1 = 180\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the outlet is $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above the inlet. The elbow contains $W = 0.020\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of water. Neglecting losses, find the fluid force on the elbow shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,3,3,-" condotte="A-B-C,0.55" tratteggi="0.9,-0.6 0.9,0.6;2.4,2.1 3.6,2.1" quote="0.9 0,0.9 2.1,0.60 m,-1.1" frecce="0.1,0,0.8,0,Q" etichette="0.7,0.85,1;3.75,2.1,2;0.2,-0.95,D = 200 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="An $90^\circ$ elbow in a vertical plane containing $W = 0.020\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of water between its two end sections"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.200^2}{4} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
v = \frac{0.060}{0.0314} = 1.91\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
p_2 = p_1 - \gamma\,\Delta z = 180 - 9.81 \cdot 0.60 = 174.1\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
\rho Q v = 1000 \cdot 0.060 \cdot 1.91 = 115\,\mathrm{N} = 0.115\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = p_1 A = 180 \cdot 0.0314 = 5.655\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = p_2 A = 174.1 \cdot 0.0314 = 5.471\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
G = -\gamma\,W = -9.81 \cdot 0.020 = -0.196\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Take the $x$ axis horizontally in the direction of the inlet velocity and the $z$ axis vertically upward.

$$
\begin{cases}
\rho Q\,(0 - v) = \Pi_1 + R_x \\[4pt]
\rho Q\,(v - 0) = -\Pi_2 + G + R_z
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= -0.115 - 5.655 = -5.770\,\mathrm{kN} \\[6pt]
R_z &= 0.115 + 5.471 + 0.196 = 5.782\,\mathrm{kN}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
F_x = 5.770\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F_z = -5.782\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F = \sqrt{5.770^2 + 5.782^2} = 8.17\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check total head at both sections:
$$
H_1 = \frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{180}{9.81} + \frac{1.91^2}{19.62} = 18.349 + 0.186 = 18.535\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 = \Delta z + \frac{p_2}{\gamma} + \frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.600 + 17.749 + 0.186 = 18.535\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The elbow lies in a vertical plane, so the weight of the water in the control volume contributes to the vertical momentum balance. Weight acts downward. Its component along $z$ is negative and enters the equation as $G = -\gamma W$.

The two sections have different elevations. The diameter is constant, so the velocity heads cancel from the lossless Bernoulli equation and $z_1 + \dfrac{p_1}{\gamma} = z_2 + \dfrac{p_2}{\gamma}$. This gives $p_2 = p_1 - \gamma \Delta z$, an outlet pressure $5.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$ below the inlet pressure.

The force on the elbow is $8.17\,\mathrm{kN}$. Its horizontal component $5.770\,\mathrm{kN}$ points with the inlet flow, and its vertical component $5.782\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts **downward**. The anchor therefore loads the ground in compression and must also resist horizontal sliding.

Omitting the water weight would give $8.03\,\mathrm{kN}$, an error of 1.70 percent. Its contribution is small here but can be significant in large vertical elbows because the volume scales with the cube of the characteristic dimension.

With negligible losses, total head must be the same at both sections. Their pressure heads differ, but adding elevation and velocity head gives the same total $18.535\,\mathrm{m}$ at each section.

</div>
